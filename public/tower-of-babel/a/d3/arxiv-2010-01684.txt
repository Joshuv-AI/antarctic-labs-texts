Abstract
In this paper, we study the mean square error (MSE) and the bit error rate (BER) performance of the box-relaxation decoder in massive multiple-input-multiple-output (MIMO) systems under the assumptions of imperfect channel state information (CSI) and receive-side channel correlation. Our analysis assumes that the number of transmit and receive antennas ( n , and ​ m n,\text{and}\ m ) grow simultaneously large while their ratio remains fixed. For simplicity of the analysis, we consider binary phase shift keying (BPSK) modulated signals. The asymptotic approximations of the MSE and BER enable us to derive the optimal power allocation scheme under MSE/BER minimization. Numerical simulations suggest that the asymptotic approximations are accurate even for small n n and m m . They also show the important role of the box constraint in mitigating the so called double descent phenomenon.

II-A

Channel Estimation

The channel matrix
{\boldsymbol{A}}
needs to be estimated prior to decoding the received data signal. Letting
\widehat{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
to denote the estimate of the channel matrix,
in this work, we consider linear minimum mean square error (LMMSE) estimate which is given by
[
37
]

\displaystyle\widehat{{\boldsymbol{A}}}=\sqrt{\frac{n}{\rho_{p}}}{\boldsymbol{R}}\left({\boldsymbol{R}}+\frac{n}{T_{p}\rho_{p}}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m}\right)^{-1}{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{p}{\boldsymbol{X}}_{p}^{\top},

(3)

where
{\boldsymbol{Y}}_{p}=\sqrt{\frac{\rho_{p}}{n}}{\boldsymbol{A}}{\boldsymbol{X}}_{p}+{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times T_{p}}
is the received signal corresponding to the
training

{\boldsymbol{X}}_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times T_{p}}
is the matrix of transmitted orthogonal pilot symbols, and
{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times T_{p}}
is an additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) matrix with
\mathbb{E}[{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{p}{\boldsymbol{Z}}_{p}^{\top}]=T_{p}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m}
.
According to
[
37
,
21
]
, the
k^{th}
column (for all
k\leq n
) of
\widehat{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
is distributed as

\mathcal{N}({\bf{0}},{\boldsymbol{R}}_{\widehat{{\boldsymbol{A}}}})

with a covariance matrix
{\boldsymbol{R}}_{\widehat{{\boldsymbol{A}}}}
that is given by

{\boldsymbol{R}}_{\widehat{A}}={\boldsymbol{R}}\left({\boldsymbol{R}}+\frac{n}{T_{p}\rho_{p}}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{m}\right)^{-1}{\boldsymbol{R}}.

Note that the pilots energy
T_{p}\rho_{p}
controls the quality of the estimation. In fact, as
T_{p}\rho_{p}\to\infty
,
\widehat{{\boldsymbol{A}}}\to{\boldsymbol{A}}
which corresponds to the perfect CSI case.
By invoking the orthogonality principle of the LMMSE estimator, it can be shown that the
k^{th}

\hbox{\boldmath$\Delta$}=\widehat{{\boldsymbol{A}}}-{\boldsymbol{A}}
follows the distribution
\mathcal{N}({\bf{0}},{\boldsymbol{R}}_{\Delta})
with a covariance matrix

\mathbf{R}_{\Delta}={\boldsymbol{R}}-{\boldsymbol{R}}_{\small{\widehat{A}}}.

Also, from the orthogonality principle of the LMMSE, one can show that
\widehat{{\boldsymbol{A}}}
and
\Delta
are statistically independent.

II-B

Symbol Detection via BRO

Since modern massive MIMO systems are equipped with a large number of antennas, the computational complexity of optimum algorithms, such as the maximum-likelihood (ML) and sphere decoders, increases exponentially as the problem size grows. Many heuristic low-complexity detection algorithms are thus
proposed such as zero-forcing (ZF), linear minimum mean square error (LMMSE) and lattice reduction.
To obtain a reasonable computational complexity, one popular heuristic that is used in this paper is the so called box-relaxation optimization (BRO) decoder
[
23
,
24
]
, which is a natural convex relaxation of the optimum ML decoder. In this decoder, the discrete set
\{\pm 1\}^{n}
is relaxed to the convex set
[-1,+1]^{n}
, and now the signal can be recovered via efficient convex optimization followed by hard thresholding. The BRO decoder has been shown to outperform conventional decoders such ZF decoder as we will explain in the next sections.

The BRO decoder consists of two steps. The first step involves solving a convex quadratic optimization with linear constraints. The output of this optimization is hard-thresholded in the second step to produce the desired binary solution. Formally, the algorithm produces an
estimate
{\boldsymbol{x}}^{*}
of
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}
given as

\displaystyle\widehat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}:=\argmin_{-1\leq{\boldsymbol{x}}\leq 1}\frac{1}{n}\|{\boldsymbol{y}}-\sqrt{\frac{\rho_{d}}{n}}\widehat{\boldsymbol{A}}{\boldsymbol{x}}\|^{2},

(4a)

\displaystyle{{\boldsymbol{x}}}^{*}:={\rm{sign}}(\widehat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}),

(4b)

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the
\ell_{2}
-norm of a vector and the
\mathrm{sign}(\cdot)
function returns the
sign of its input and acts element-wise on vector inputs.

We consider the following two metrics to evaluate the performance of the BRO decoder:

Mean Square Error
: This metric is concerned with the evaluation of performance of the first step (estimation step) of the decoder in (
4a
). Formally, the mean square error (MSE) is defined as

\mathrm{MSE}:=\frac{1}{n}\|\widehat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}-{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\|^{2}.

(5)

Bit Error Rate
: For the second step (detection step) in (
4b
), we evaluate the performance of the decoder by the bit error rate (BER), defined as

{\rm{BER}}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{\{{x}^{*}_{i}\neq x_{0,i}\}},

(6)

where
\mathbbm{1}_{\{\cdot\}}
denotes the indicator function.

V-A

Technical Tool: Convex Gaussian Min-max Theorem

The CGMT allows us to replace the analysis of a generally hard optimization problem with a simplified auxiliary optimization problem. In this subsection, we recall the statement of the CGMT, and we refer the interested reader to
[
27
,
26
]
for the complete technical details.
In order to summarize the essential ideas, we consider the following two Gaussian processes:

\displaystyle X_{{{\boldsymbol{w}}},{{\boldsymbol{u}}}}

\displaystyle:={{\boldsymbol{u}}}^{\top}{{\boldsymbol{G}}{\boldsymbol{w}}}+\psi({{\boldsymbol{w}}},{{\boldsymbol{u}}}),

\displaystyle Y_{{{\boldsymbol{w}}},{{\boldsymbol{u}}}}

\displaystyle:=\|{{\boldsymbol{w}}}\|{{\boldsymbol{g}}}^{\top}{{\boldsymbol{u}}}+\|{{\boldsymbol{u}}}\|_{2}{{\boldsymbol{h}}}^{\top}{{\boldsymbol{w}}}+\psi({{\boldsymbol{w}}},{{\boldsymbol{u}}}),

where
{\boldsymbol{G}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and
{\boldsymbol{h}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are all assumed to have iid standard Gaussian entries. Further let
\mathcal{S}_{w}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
\mathcal{S}_{u}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be convex and compact sets, and
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is convex-concave continuous on
\mathcal{S}_{w}\times\mathcal{S}_{u}
, possibly random but independent of
{\boldsymbol{G}}
.
For these two processes, define the following random min-max optimization problems, which we refer to as the primary
optimization (PO) problem and the auxiliary optimization (AO):

\displaystyle\Phi^{n}({\boldsymbol{G}}):=\underset{{\boldsymbol{w}}\in\mathcal{S}_{w}}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \underset{{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathcal{S}_{u}}{\operatorname{\max}}\ X_{{{\boldsymbol{w}}},{{\boldsymbol{u}}}},

(11a)

\displaystyle\phi^{n}({\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}}):=\underset{{\boldsymbol{w}}\in\mathcal{S}_{w}}{\operatorname{\min}}\ \underset{{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathcal{S}_{u}}{\operatorname{\max}}\ Y_{{{\boldsymbol{w}}},{{\boldsymbol{u}}}}.

(11b)

Then, the CGMT relates the above problems as shown in the next theorem, the proof of which can be found in
[
26
]
.

Theorem 3

(CGMT
[
26
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be any arbitrary open subset of
\mathcal{S}_{w}
, and
\mathcal{S}^{c}=\mathcal{S}_{w}\setminus\mathcal{S}
. Denote
\phi_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}^{n}({\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})
the optimal cost of the optimization in (
11b
) when the minimization over
{\boldsymbol{w}}
is constrained over
{\boldsymbol{w}}\in\mathcal{S}^{c}
. Suppose that there exist constants
\overline{\phi}
and
\overline{\phi}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}
such that (i)
\phi^{n}({\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})\to\overline{\phi}
in probability, (ii)
\phi_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}^{n}({\boldsymbol{g}},{\boldsymbol{h}})\to\overline{\phi}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}
in probability, and (iii)
\overline{\phi}<\overline{\phi}_{\mathcal{S}^{c}}
.
Then,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}[{\boldsymbol{w}}_{\Phi}\in\mathcal{S}]=1
, where
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{\Phi}
is a minimizer of (

).

It is not difficult to see that the conditions (i), (ii) and (iii) regarding the optimal cost of the AO imply that its solution
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{\phi}
satisfies:
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}[{\boldsymbol{w}}_{\phi}\in\mathcal{S}]=1
.
The non-trivial and powerful part of the CGMT is that the same conclusion holds true for the optimal solution

of the PO
[

]
.

VI
Conclusion

In this work, we derived precise characterization of the asymptotic behavior of the BRO decoder under the assumptions of imperfect CSI and receiver-side correlation. We used the MSE and BER as performance metrics of the decoder. Then, we derived the optimal power allocation between pilot and data symbols using the presented asymptotic results. Simulation results validate our theoretical analysis even for small dimensions of the problem. They also show that the BRO decoder can mitigate the double descent effect encountered with other conventional decoders such as the ZF decoder. For simplicity of the analysis, BPSK signals are used but our results can be extended to higher order modulation schemes such as
M
-PAM and is left for future work. Possible future work includes studying the fully correlated MIMO systems (Kronecker correlation), and analyzing the regularized version of the BRO decoder.

An imitation model based on the majority
Hsin-Lun Li

Abstract
. The voter model consists of a set of agents whose opinion is a binary variable. At each time step, an agent along with a social neighbor is selected and the agent imitates the social neighbor at the next time step. In this paper, we study a variant of the voter model: an imitation model based on the majority. The agent imitates the selected social neighbor if and only if the number of neighbors following the opinion of the selected social neighbor is more than that following the agent. We study circumstances under which a consensus can not be achieved and the probability of consensus on a finite connected social graph.

1.
Introduction

The voter model consists of a set of agents whose initial opinion is a binary variable
s=\pm 1
. At each time step, an agent
i
along with one of its neighbors
j
is selected and agent
i
imitates agent
j
at the next time step. The voter model differs from the Deffuant model and the Hegselmann-Krause model studied in
[
4
,
2
,
1
,
3
,
5
]
. It has no confidence threshold in opinions. In this paper, we consider a variant of the voter model: an imitation model based on the majority. The mechanism is as follows. Given a finite set of agents, say
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. Let
x_{i}(t)
be the opinion of agent
i
at time
t
and
x_{i}(0),i\in[n]
are binary random variables with state space

S=\{\pm 1\}\quad\hbox{and}\quad P(x_{i}(0)=1)=p.

At each time step, an agent
i
along with one of its neighbors
j
is selected and agent
i
imitates agent
j
at the next time step if and only if the number of social neighbors of agent
i
following the opinion of agent
j
is more than that following the opinion of agent
i
. Interpreting in a graph, each vertex stands for an agent and an edge connecting two vertices indicates the corresponding agents are social neighbors. Let
G=([n],E)
be a connected social graph with vertex set and edge set
[n]
and
E
, let
N_{i}=\{k\in[n]:k\neq i\ \hbox{and}\ (i,k)\in E\}
be the collection of all social neighbors of agent
i
excluding itself, and let
k_{t}
be the vertex selected to imitate the selected social neighbor or not at time
t
, which is a random variable with state space
[n]
. The selected social neighbor of vertex
k_{t}
is of state space
N_{k_{t}}
. Interpreting the mechanism in math,

\displaystyle x_{k_{t}}(t)\neq x_{k_{t}}(t+1)\ \hbox{if and only if}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\card(\{j\in N_{k_{t}}:x_{j}(t)=x_{k_{t}}(t+1)\})>\card(\{j\in N_{k_{t}}:x_{j}(t)=x_{k_{t}}(t)\})

for the cardinality of a set
S
denoted by
\card(S)
.

2.
Main results

Unlike the voter model on a finite connected social graph, the imitation model based on the majority does not always achieve a consensus. Let
\mathscr{C}
be the collection of all sample points leading to a consensus, i.e.,

\mathscr{C}=\{\max_{i,j\in[n]}(x_{i}(T)-x_{j}(T))=0\}.

Theorem 1
.

We have
P(\mathscr{C})=1
if
G
is complete and
P(\mathscr{C})\geq 1-2p(1-p)|E|
for all social graphs
G
.

A tree is a connected graph with the least edges. The social graph
G
is assumed connected. Therefore,
G
is a tree if and only if the number of edges in
G
is
n-1
.

Corollary 2
.

We have
P(\mathscr{C})\geq 1/2|E|
if
p\leq 1/2|E|
. In particular,
P(\mathscr{C})\geq\frac{1}{2(n-1)}
if
p\leq\frac{1}{2(n-1)}
and
G
is a tree.

3.
The model

Lemma 3
.

Let
Z_{t}=\sum_{i,j\in[n]}\mathbbm{1}\{x_{i}(t)\neq x_{j}(t)\}\vee\mathbbm{1}\{(i,j)\notin E\}
. Then,
Z_{t}
is nonincreasing with respect to
t
. In particular,

\displaystyle Z_{t}-Z_{t+1}=2\big(\card(\{i\in N_{k_{t}}:\ x_{i}(t)=x_{k_{t}}(t+1)\})

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt-\card(\{i\in N_{k_{t}}:x_{i}(t)=x_{k_{t}}(t)\})\big).

Proof.

For all
t\geq 0
, let
k=k_{t}
,
x_{i}=x_{i}(t)
and
x_{i}^{\star}=x(t+1)
for all
i\in[n]
. Then,

\displaystyle Z_{t}-Z_{t+1}=\sum_{i,j\in[n]}\big(\mathbbm{1}\{x_{i}\neq x_{j}\}\vee\mathbbm{1}\{(i,j)\notin E\}-\mathbbm{1}\{x_{i}^{\star}\neq x_{j}^{\star}\}\vee\mathbbm{1}\{(i,j)\notin E\}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=2\sum_{i\in[n]-\{k\}}\big(\mathbbm{1}\{x_{i}\neq x_{k}\}\vee\mathbbm{1}\{(i,k)\notin E\}-\mathbbm{1}\{x_{i}\neq x_{k}^{\star}\}\vee\mathbbm{1}\{(i,k)\notin E\}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=2\sum_{i\in N_{k}}\big(\mathbbm{1}\{x_{i}\neq x_{k}\}-\mathbbm{1}\{x_{i}\neq x_{k}^{\star}\}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=2\big(\card(\{i\in N_{k}:\ x_{i}=x_{k}^{\star}\})-\card(\{i\in N_{k}:x_{i}=x_{k}\})\big).

∎

Lemma 4

(Uniform convergence)
.

We have
x_{i}(t)=x_{i}(t+1)
for all
i\in[n]
after some finite time almost surely.

Proof.

(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a supermartingale uniformly bounded in
L^{1}
, i.e.,
E|Z_{t}|\leq E|Z_{0}|<\infty
for all
t\geq 0
. By the martingale convergence theorem,
Z_{t}
converges almost surely to
Z_{\infty}\in L^{1}
. It follows that

\lim_{t\to\infty}(Z_{t}-Z_{t+1})=Z_{\infty}-Z_{\infty}=0.

Since
Z_{t}-Z_{t+1}\in\mathbf{N}
,
Z_{t}=Z_{t+1}
after some finite time. Therefore,
x_{i}(t)=x_{i}(t+1)
for all
i\in[n]
after some finite time.

∎

Let
T
be the earliest time that no agents imitate the other social neighbors, i.e.,

T=\inf\{t\geq 0:x_{i}(s)=x_{i}(s+1)\ \hbox{for all}\ i\in[n]\ \hbox{and}\ s\geq t\}.

It follows from Lemma
4
that
T
is almost surely finite. Via the second Borel-Cantelli lemma, a vertex along with each social neighbor is selected infinitely many times, therefore engendering the following lemma.

Lemma 5
.

We have

\card(\{j\in N_{i}:x_{j}(T)=-x_{i}(T)\})\leq\card(\{j\in N_{i}:x_{j}(T)=x_{i}(T)\})

for all
i\in[n]
.

Lemma 6
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

a consensus can be achieved,

(2)

x_{i}(T)=x_{j}(T)
for all
i\in[n]
and
j\in N_{i}
, and

(3)

Z_{T}=n(n-1)-2|E|
.

Proof.

It is clear that
1
implies
2
. For
2
\implies
3
, observe that

\displaystyle Z_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\sum_{j\in N_{i}}\mathbbm{1}\{x_{i}(t)\neq x_{j}(t)\}+\sum_{j\in N_{i}^{c}-\{i\}}1\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\in N_{i}}\mathbbm{1}\{x_{i}(t)\neq x_{j}(t)\}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(n-\deg_{G}(i)-1)

(1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\in N_{i}}\mathbbm{1}\{x_{i}(t)\neq x_{j}(t)\}+n(n-1)-2|E|

where
\deg_{G}(i)
is the degree of vertex

in
G
and (
1
) follows the handshaking lemma. Hence,
Z_{T}=n(n-1)-2|E|.
For
3
\implies
1
, (
1
) implies

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\in N_{i}}\mathbbm{1}\{x_{i}(T)\neq x_{j}(T)\}=0,\ \hbox{therefore}\ x_{i}(T)=x_{j}(T)

for all
i\in[n]
and
j\in N_{i}
. Due to the connectedness of

, there is an
i,\ j
-path

G
for all vertices
i
and

. Since any two adjacent vertices on

x_{i}(T)=x_{j}(T)

Hence, a consensus can be achieved.
∎

Lemma 7
.

G
complete implies a consensus achieved eventually almost surely.

Proof.

Assume that this is not the case. By Lemma

, there are
i\in[n]

Lemma 8
.

Let
G
be a cycle or a path with
n\geq 4
. Then, a consensus can be achieved if and only if no four consecutive vertices correspond to opinions
1,\ 1,\ -1,\ -1
at the initial time.

Proof.

(\implies)
Assume that this is not the case. Without loss of generality, consider the cycle
1\rightarrow 2\rightarrow\ldots\rightarrow n\rightarrow 1
with
x_{1}(0)=1
,
x_{2}(0)=1
,
x_{3}(0)=-1
and
x_{4}(0)=-1
. We claim by induction that vertices 1, 2, 3 and 4 remain unchanged in opinion. It is true for
t=1
regardless of what vertex along with one of its neighbors is selected. If this is true for
t
, then this is true for
t+1
. Therefore,
x_{1}(t)=1
,
x_{2}(t)=1
,
x_{3}(t)=-1
and
x_{4}(t)=-1
for all
t\geq 0.
This is also true if
G
is a path. So a consensus can not be achieved, a contradiction.

(\impliedby)
Assume that this is not the case. Following Lemma
6
, consider the cycle
1\rightarrow 2\rightarrow\ldots\rightarrow n\rightarrow 1
with
x_{1}(T)=1
and
x_{2}(T)=-1
. Then,
x_{3}(T)=-1
and
x_{n}(T)=-1
, contradicting
x_{1}(T)=1
. Considering the path by removing edge
(1,n)
from
G
, still contradict
x_{1}(T)=1.

∎

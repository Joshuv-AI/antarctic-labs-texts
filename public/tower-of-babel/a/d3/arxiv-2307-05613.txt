Space-Time Fresnel Prism Preprint: APS/123-QED
Zhiyu Li, Xikui Ma, Amir Bahrami, Zoé-Lise Deck-Léger, Christophe Caloz

Abstract
Space-time modulation-based metamaterials have recently spurred considerable interest, owing to the fundamental addition of the time dimension to the medium parameters, and resulting novel properties and potential applications. However, the implementation of most related structures – e.g., involving step, slab or gradient discontinuities – has been hindered by the impossible requirement of infinitely or prohibitively large device sizes. We provide here a solution to this issue, consisting in a space-time transposition of the conventional Fresnel prism, whereby a copy of the target modulation is periodically re-injected at the input of a Fresnel-reduced finite structure, so as to provide the same anharmonic and nonreciprocal frequency conversion as the target space-time interface in the case of a modulation step. This concept, which may readily extend to slab or gradient modulations, as well as accelerated profiles for space-time chirping operations, may pave the way for the practical development of a wide range of novel microwave and optical space-time systems.

VIII
Conclusion and Discussion

We have presented the concept of space-time Fresnel prisms to address the challenge of size reduction in anharmonic and nonreciprocal frequency conversion provided by aperiodic-modulation space-time systems. Such structures may be implemented in various technologies, including switched transmission lines
Ran et al. 2015
;
Bahrami and Caloz 2021
;
Moussa et al. 2023
, nonlinear anisotropic perturbations
Philbin et al. 2008
;
Sloan et al. 2022
, semiconductor free-carrier injection
Gaafar et al. 2019
, and various other acoustic, electronic, mechanical, plasmonic and optical modulations
Caloz et al. 2022
, with the usual single modulation interface being replaced by two independent-velocity and

time-periodic
modulation interfaces. The proposed Fresnel prism structures, whose uniform (flat) space-time interfaces provide uniform space-time frequency transitions, may be readily extended to Fresnel lens structures, with accelerated (curved) space-time interfaces
Bahrami et al. 2023b
providing unprecedented type of space-time chirping functionalities.

(7a)

XIII
Phase-Matching Formulas for the Two Prisms

Figure
10
shows space-time diagrams of Prism I and Prism II with relevant information to derive their phase matching design formulas. We selected here as the excitation a traveling-wave harmonic plane wave of electric field
E_{\mathrm{i}}=E_{0}\exp(-i\phi_{\mathrm{i}})
, with the phase
\phi_{\mathrm{i}}(z,ct)=\omega_{\mathrm{i}}t-k_{\mathrm{i}}z
, but the forthcoming derivations may be extended to (modulated or unmodulated) pulse excitations of different shapes.

Figure 10:
Space-time diagrams to derive the phase continuity design formulas for (a) Prism I and (b) Prism II.

XIII.1
Prism I [Eq. (
2
)]

To derive the size and temporal periods of Prism I [Fig.
10
(a)] for phase continuity, we may utilize the phase tracing method
Kong 1990
;
Lurie et al. 2009
, tracking back the transmission phase from a given space plane to the incidence space plane (
z=0
), for simplifying the derivations. We find

\phi_{\mathrm{s}}(z_{\mathrm{L}},ct_{3})=\phi(z_{\mathrm{f}},ct_{2})=\phi(0,ct_{0})\quad\textrm{and}\quad\phi_{\mathrm{e}}(z_{\mathrm{L}},ct_{4})=\phi(z_{\mathrm{i}},ct_{2})=\phi(0,ct_{1}).

(9)

Successively substituting the last equalities of Eqs. (
9
) into the phase continuity condition
\phi_{\mathrm{e}}-\phi_{\mathrm{s}}=2\pi q
(
q\in\mathbb{N}
) and setting the related phase difference to the corresponding phase difference for the incident wave,
\phi_{\mathrm{i}}(z,ct)=\omega_{\mathrm{i}}t-k_{\mathrm{i}}z
, in the plane
z=0
, we obtain

\phi_{\mathrm{e}}-\phi_{\mathrm{s}}=\phi(0,ct_{1})-\phi(0,ct_{0})=2\pi q=\omega_{\mathrm{i}}(t_{1}-t_{0}),

(10a)

so that

t_{1}-t_{0}=2\pi q/\omega_{\mathrm{i}}.

(10b)

We obtain then the sought-after size and temporal period of Prism I using space-time geometrical relations with Eq. (
10b
)

d_{\mathrm{I}}=z_{\mathrm{f}}-z_{\mathrm{i}}=(t_{1}-t_{0})c/n_{1}=q\frac{\lambda_{0}}{n_{1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{\mathrm{I}}=\frac{d_{\mathrm{I}}}{v_{\mathrm{m}}}=q\frac{cT_{0}}{n_{1}v_{\mathrm{m}}},

(11)

where
\lambda_{0}=2\pi c/\omega_{\mathrm{i}}
and
T_{0}=2\pi/\omega_{\mathrm{i}}
are the free-space incident wavelength and period, respectively.

XIII.2
Prism II [Eq. (
1
)]

For Prism II [Fig.
10
(b)], we use again the phase tracing method but this time applied to the silence intervals, which yields

\phi_{\mathrm{s}}(z_{\mathrm{L}},ct_{7})=\phi(z_{\mathrm{f}},ct_{6})=\phi(0,ct^{-}_{1})

(12a)

and

\phi_{\mathrm{e}}(z_{\mathrm{L}},ct_{4})=\phi(z_{\mathrm{i}},ct_{2})=\phi(0,ct^{+}_{1}),

(12b)

where
t^{\mp}_{1}=t_{1}\mp\delta t
with
\delta t\rightarrow 0
. Equation (
12

\phi_{\mathrm{e}}=\phi_{\mathrm{s}}
is automatically satisfied in Prism II.

For a given prism size
d_{\mathrm{II}}
, the temporal period is given by

\tau_{\mathrm{II}}=t_{2}-0=(t_{6}-0)-(t_{5}-0)=\left(\frac{1}{v_{\mathrm{m}}}-\frac{1}{v_{\mathrm{m}^{\prime}}}\right)d_{\mathrm{II}}.

(13)

(16a)

,

\Delta t_{\mathrm{s}}=\tau_{\mathrm{I}}-\frac{d_{\mathrm{I}}}{c/n_{2}}=\left(\frac{1}{v_{\mathrm{m}}}-\frac{1}{c/n_{\mathrm{2}}}\right)d_{\mathrm{I}}.

(16c)

and the Target Moving Interface

We shall compare here the implementation size of interconnected Prism II [Fig.
3
] with that of the target moving interface [left panel of Fig.
1
(b)] for a finite number of sub-prisms,
N
, for the switched-delay-line scheme.

Interconnected Prism II [Fig.
11
(b)], composed of
N
sub-prisms and involving
N-1
transmission line sections, has the extra delay-line size
(N-1)d_{\mathrm{c}}
compared to the initial Prism II size,
d_{\mathrm{II}}
[Fig.
2
(b)]. The total size of the interconnected Prism II is then obtained as

D_{\mathrm{II}}=d_{\mathrm{II}}+(N-1)d_{\mathrm{c}},

(21)

with
d_{\mathrm{c}}
given in Eq. (
18

N

(22)

\tau_{\mathrm{II}}
and
\Delta t^{\prime}

13
) and (

).

\gamma=\frac{D_{\mathrm{II}}}{D}=\frac{v_{\mathrm{m}^{\prime}}(c/n_{1}-v_{\mathrm{m}})+(N-1)(c/n_{1}-c/n_{2})(v_{\mathrm{m}^{\prime}}-v_{\mathrm{m}})}{(c/n_{1}-v_{\mathrm{m}})[Nv_{\mathrm{m}^{\prime}}-(N-1)v_{\mathrm{m}}]},

18

21

\gamma=\frac{Nn_{2}/n_{1}-(N-1)}{Nn_{2}/n_{1}-(N-1)\beta n_{2}}.

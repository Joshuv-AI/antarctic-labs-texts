Rates for maps and flows in a deterministic multidimensional weak invariance principle
Nicolò Paviato

Abstract
We present the first rates of convergence to an N N -dimensional Brownian motion when N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 for discrete and continuous time dynamical systems. Additionally, we provide the first rates for continuous time in any dimension. Our results hold for nonuniformly hyperbolic and expanding systems, such as Axiom A flows, suspensions over a Young tower with exponential tails, and some classes of intermittent solenoids.

Setup and main results

2.1
Metrics for probability measures

Let
(S,d_{S})
be a separable metric space with Borel
\sigma
-algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
, and denote with
\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
the set of Borel probability measures on
S
.
Let
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
; following
[
17
]
, we have the following metrics on
\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
,

•

1-Wasserstein
(or Kantorovich)

\textstyle\mathcal{W}(\mu,\nu)=\sup_{f\in\Lip_{1}}\bigl|\int_{S}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu-\int_{S}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\nu\bigr|,

where
\Lip_{1}=\{f\colon S\to\mathbb{R}:|f(x)-f(y)|\leq d_{S}(x,y)\}
.

•

Prokhorov
(or Lévy-Prokhorov)

\textstyle\Pi(\mu,\nu)=\inf\{\varepsilon>0:\mu(B)\leq\nu(B^{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon\text{ for all }B\in{\mathcal{B}}\},

where

B^{\varepsilon}=\bigcup_{x\in B}\{y\in S\colon d_{S}(x,y)<\varepsilon\}.

If
X
and
Y
are
S
-valued random elements with respectively laws
\mu
and
\nu
, we write
\Pi(X,Y)=\Pi(\mu,\nu)
and
\mathcal{W}(X,Y)=\mathcal{W}(\mu,\nu)
.
Denoting with
X_{n}\to_{w}X
the weak convergence of the sequence of laws of
(X_{n})_{n\geq 1}
to the law of
X
, we have that
\mathcal{W}(X_{n},X)\to 0
implies
X_{n}\to_{w}X
.
The distance
\Pi
metrizes weak convergence on
\mathcal{M}_{1}(S)
and the same is true for
\mathcal{W}
under the extra assumption
\diam(S)<\infty
.

We recall that
\Pi(X,Y)\leq\sqrt{\mathcal{W}(X,Y)}
(see
[
17
, Theorem 2]
) and that, if
X
and
Y
are defined on a common probability space, then

\Pi(X,Y)\leq|d_{S}(X,Y)|_{\infty}
.
This estimate follows from the definition of
\Pi
,
noting that

\mathbb{P}(d_{S}(X,Y)>\varepsilon)\leq\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
implies that

\Pi(X,Y)\leq\varepsilon
.

2.2
Nonuniformly expanding maps

Let
(X,d)
be a bounded metric space with a Borel probability measure
\rho
and suppose that

T\colon X\to X

is a nonsingular map
(
\rho(T^{-1}E)=0
if and only if
\rho(E)=0
for all Borel sets

E\subset X
). Assume that
\rho
is ergodic.

We suppose that there exists a measurable
Y\subset X
with
\rho(Y)>0
and
\{Y_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
an at most countable measurable partition of
Y
. Let
\tau\colon Y\to\mathbb{Z}^{+}
be an integrable function with constant values
\tau_{j}\geq 1
on partition elements
Y_{j}
. We assume that
T^{\tau(y)}y\in Y
for all
y\in Y
and define
F\colon Y\to Y
as
Fy=T^{\tau(y)}y
.

The dynamical system
(X,T,\rho)
is said to be a
nonuniformly expanding map
if there are constants
\lambda>1
,
\eta\in(0,1]
,
C\geq 1
, such that for each
j\geq 1
and
y,y^{\prime}\in Y_{j}
,

1.

\left.\kern-1.2ptF\vphantom{|}\right|_{Y_{j}}\colon Y_{j}\to Y
is a measure-theoretic bijection;

2.

d(Fy,Fy^{\prime})\geq\lambda d(y,y^{\prime})
;

3.

d(T^{\ell}y,T^{\ell}y^{\prime})\leq Cd(Fy,Fy^{\prime})
for all
0\leq\ell\leq\tau_{j}-1
;

4.

\zeta=\left.\kern-1.2ptd\rho\vphantom{|}\right|_{Y}/(\left.\kern-1.2ptd\rho\vphantom{|}\right|_{Y}\circ F)
satisfies

|\log\zeta(y)-\log\zeta(y^{\prime})|\leq Cd(Fy,Fy^{\prime})^{\eta}
.

We say that
T
is nonuniformly expanding of
order

p\in[1,\infty]
if the
return time

\tau
lies in
L^{p}(Y)
.
A map

satisfying
1

2

4
is a (full-branched) Gibbs-Markov map as in
[
1
]
.
It is standard that there exists a unique
\rho
-absolutely continuous ergodic (and mixing)

-invariant probability measure
\mu_{X}
on
X
(see for example
[
44
, Theorem 1]
).

The LSV map mentioned in the introduction is an example of nonuniformly expanding map of order
p
for every
p\in[1,1/\gamma)
(see
[
2
, Subsection 2.5.2]
).

\textstyle d(\Psi_{t}x,\Psi_{t}y)\leq Kd(x,y).

Let

Proposition 3.3

Definition 3.4
.

A sequence of integrable

-valued random variables

-measurable,

.

Remark 3.5

If

is a RMDS with
\sigma
-algebras
{\mathcal{G}}_{k}

the sequence

,

, with filtration
\mathcal{A}_{k}={\mathcal{G}}_{n-k}
, is a martingale. Namely,

is
\mathcal{A}_{k}
measurable and
{\mathbb{E}}[M_{n}(k+1)|\mathcal{A}_{k}]=M_{n}(k)

We now recall a classical result connecting martingale theory with measure-preserving systems. It justifies the use of the terminology "martingale part" for

, and it will be employed in the proofs of the rates for both maps and semiflows.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(\Lambda,T,{\mathcal{G}},\mu)
be measure-preserving system with transfer operator

, then the sequence

with
\sigma
-algebras

We have that

is

-measurable for all

\textstyle\mathbb{E}[v\circ T^{n}|T^{-(n+1)}{\mathcal{G}}]={\mathbb{E}}[v|T^{-1}{\mathcal{G}}]\circ T^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}[v|T^{-1}{\mathcal{G}}]=(Pv)\circ T,

We now show some estimates for i.i.d. random variables and Brownian motion.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of identically distributed real random variables, defined on the same probability space.
If
a=\mathbb{E}[e^{\xi_{1}}]<\infty

\mathbb{E}[\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\xi_{k}]\leq\log(na)

Proof.

We have that

\textstyle\mathbb{E}[\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\xi_{k}]\leq\log\mathbb{E}[e^{\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\xi_{k}}]\leq\log(na).

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
W\colon[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a centred Brownian motion with covariance
\Sigma
.
Then
\mathbb{E}[e^{\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|W(t)|}]<\infty
.

Proof.

Since
\Sigma
is symmetric and positive semidefinite, there exists an orthogonal
N\times N
matrix
O
such that

O\Sigma O^{T}=\diag(\sigma_{1}^{2},\dots,\sigma_{N}^{2})
, with

\sigma_{i}^{2}\geq 0
.
Then,

is a centred Brownian motion with covariance

O\Sigma O^{T}
, and for all
1\leq i\leq N
the real-valued processes
(OW)_{i}
are independent centred Brownian motions with variances
\sigma_{i}^{2}
. Let
\bar{\sigma}=\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\sigma^{2}_{i}
. If
\bar{\sigma}=0
, then both
OW
and
W
are the constant zero process and the proof is finished.
If
\bar{\sigma}>0
, we use standard Gaussian estimates and get that for every
1\leq i\leq N
there exists
C_{i}>0
such that for all

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathbb{P}\bigl(\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|(OW(t))_{i}|>s\bigr)\leq C_{i}\exp\bigl(-s^{2}/(2\bar{\sigma})\bigr).

Writing

\xi=\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|OW(t)|
,

\hat{C}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}C_{i}
,
and

, we get

\textstyle\mathbb{P}(\xi>s)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}\bigl(\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|OW_{i}(t)|>s/N\bigr)\leq\hat{C}\exp(-s^{2}/c)

and

\mathbb{P}(e^{\xi}>s)=\mathbb{P}(\xi>\log s)\leq\hat{C}\exp\bigl(-(\log s)^{2}/c\bigr).

By a change of variable

\textstyle\mathbb{E}[e^{\xi}]=\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{P}(e^{\xi}>s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(e^{\xi}>s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq 1+\hat{C}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x^{2}/c}e^{x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

By orthogonality,

|O^{T}x|=|x|
for all

. Hence,

|W(t)|=|O^{T}OW(t)|=|OW(t)|

for every

. Therefore,

\mathbb{E}[e^{\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|W(t)|}]=\mathbb{E}[e^{\xi}]<\infty
.

4
Martingale-coboundary decompositions for semiflows

In this section we recall how the semiflows defined in Subsection
2.4
can be modelled by suspensions over a Gibbs-Markov map with an unbounded roof function.
We show that Hölder observables on the ambient space lift to regular functions on the suspension, for which we get two new decompositions in the style of Gordin
[
18
]
.
This follows and extends the approach of
[
23
]
to continuous time.

We inform the reader that sometimes in our arguments it may be necessary to diminish the parameter
\eta\in(0,1]
; such a change does not create any issue because of the inclusion of Hölder spaces.

4.1
Gibbs-Markov semiflows

Let
\Psi_{t}\colon M\to M
be a nonuniformly expanding semiflow of order
p\in[2,\infty]
as in Subsection
2.4
. Hence, there exist
\eta\in(0,1]
, a set
X\subset M
with a Borel probability measure
\rho
and a first return function
r\in C^{\eta}(X,\mathbb{R})
with
\inf r\geq 1
.
Since the map
T=\Psi_{r}\colon X\to X
, is nonuniformly expanding, following Subsection
2.2
there are
a subset
Y\subset X
with
\rho(Y)>0
and a measurable partition
\{Y_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
, a return time
\tau\in L^{p}(Y,\rho)
, and a map
F=T^{\tau}\colon Y\to Y
such that conditions
1
-
4
are satisfied.
It is a standard result that there is a unique absolutely continuous ergodic
F
-invariant probability measure
\mu
on
Y
and

d\mu/(d\left.\kern-1.2pt\rho\vphantom{|}\right|_{Y})
is bounded, and hence
\tau\in L^{p}(Y,\mu)
.

Let
\varphi\colon Y\to[1,\infty)
be defined as
\varphi(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{\tau(y)-1}r(T^{j}y)
. It lies in
L^{p}(Y,\mu)
because
\varphi\leq|r|_{\infty}\tau
.
Define the suspension

Y^{\varphi}=\{(y,u)\in Y\times[0,\infty):u\in[0,\varphi(y)]\}/\sim

where
(y,\varphi(y))\sim(Fy,0)
.
The suspension semiflow
F_{t}:Y^{\varphi}\to Y^{\varphi}
is given by

F_{t}(y,u)=(y,u+t)
computed modulo identifications.

There is a semiconjugacy

\pi_{M}\colon Y^{\varphi}\to M
between
F_{t}
and
\Psi_{t}
, defined as
\pi_{M}(y,u)=\Psi_{u}y
. We have the ergodic
F_{t}
-invariant probability measure

\mu^{\varphi}=(\mu\times{\rm Lebesgue})/\bar{\varphi}
,
where
\bar{\varphi}=\int_{Y}\varphi\,d\mu
. Moreover, it is easy to see that
\mu_{M}=(\pi_{M})_{*}\mu^{\varphi}
is exactly the ergodic
\Psi_{t}
-invariant probability measure on
M
, defined in Subsection
2.4
.

Proposition 4.1
.

There is
C>0
such that

\textstyle|\varphi(y)-\varphi(y^{\prime})|\leq C(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)d(Fy,Fy^{\prime})^{\eta},

(4.1)

for every
j\geq 1
and
y,y^{\prime}\in Y_{j}
.
Moreover, if

then

\textstyle\sum_{j}\mu(Y_{j})(\textstyle\sup_{Y_{j}}\varphi^{p})<\infty.

(4.2)

Proof.

Recall that
\tau
is constant on partition elements.
Using that
r\in C^{\eta}(X,\mathbb{R})
,

\textstyle|\varphi(y)-\varphi(y^{\prime})|\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{\tau(y)-1}|r(T^{\ell}y)-r(T^{\ell}y^{\prime})|\leq|r|_{\eta}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\tau(y)-1}d(T^{\ell}y,T^{\ell}y^{\prime})^{\eta},

for each

and

3
from Subsection
2.2
yields that there is

\textstyle|\varphi(y)-\varphi(y^{\prime})|\leq C\tau(y)d(Fy,Fy^{\prime})^{\eta}.

By

and the definition of

, we get
\left.\kern-1.2pt\tau\vphantom{|}\right|_{Y_{j}}\leq\left.\kern-1.2pt\varphi\vphantom{|}\right|_{Y_{j}}

. Equation (

By (
4.1
), we get

\sup_{Y_{j}}\varphi-\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi\leq C\diam(Y)^{\eta}(\textstyle\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)
.
Hence, there exists

such that

\sup_{Y_{j}}\varphi\leq K\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi

for all

. So,

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{j}\mu(Y_{j})(\textstyle\sup_{Y_{j}}\varphi^{p})\leq K^{p}\sum_{j}\mu(Y_{j})({\textstyle\inf_{Y_{j}}}\varphi^{p})\leq K^{p}|\varphi|_{p}^{p}<\infty.

Since

is Gibbs-Markov and

), we say as in

that

is a

.
We define the space

with norm

similarly to Definition

.

Definition 4.2

(Function space on
Y^{\varphi}
)
.

Let
\eta\in(0,1]
,
N\geq 1
, and define for

the set
Y^{\varphi}_{j}=\{(y,u)\in Y^{\varphi}:y\in Y_{j}\}
.
For

\|v\|_{\eta}=|v|_{\infty}+|v|_{\eta},\qquad|v|_{\eta}=\sup_{j\geq 1}\sup_{(y,u),(y^{\prime},u)\in Y_{j}^{\varphi},\ y\neq y^{\prime}}\frac{|v(y,u)-v(y^{\prime},u)|}{(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)d(Fy,Fy^{\prime})^{\eta}}.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let

w=v\circ\pi_{M}\in\mathcal{F}^{\eta^{2}}(Y^{\varphi},\mathbb{R}^{N})
and there exists

such that

\|w\|_{\eta^{2}}\leq C\|v\|_{\eta}

Proof.

We clearly have that
|w|_{\infty}\leq|v|_{\infty}
.
Hence, we are left to show that
|w|_{\eta^{2}}\ll\|v\|_{\eta}
.
Let
j\geq 1
and
(y,u),(y^{\prime},u)\in Y_{j}^{\varphi}
. We have

\textstyle|w(y,u)-w(y^{\prime},u)|=|v(\Psi_{u}y)-v(\Psi_{u}y^{\prime})|\leq|v|_{\eta}d(\Psi_{u}y,\Psi_{u}y^{\prime})^{\eta}.

(4.3)

For
r\in C^{\eta}(X,\mathbb{R})
and

, we write
r_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}r\circ T^{j}
.
Let

So,

and

. By (

(dependent on

\textstyle d(\Psi_{u}y,\Psi_{u}y^{\prime})=d(\Psi_{E(y)}(\Psi_{r_{n}(y)}y),\Psi_{E(y)}(\Psi_{r_{n}(y)}y^{\prime}))\leq Kd(\Psi_{r_{n}(y)}y,\Psi_{r_{n}(y)}y^{\prime}).

\displaystyle\textstyle d(\Psi_{r_{n}(y)}y,\Psi_{r_{n}(y)}y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\textstyle\leq d(\Psi_{r_{n}(y)}y,\Psi_{r_{n}(y^{\prime})}y^{\prime})+d(\Psi_{r_{n}(y^{\prime})}y^{\prime},\Psi_{r_{n}(y)}y^{\prime})

\displaystyle\textstyle=d(T^{n}y,T^{n}y^{\prime})+d(\Psi_{r_{n}(y^{\prime})}y^{\prime},\Psi_{r_{n}(y)}y^{\prime})

\textstyle d(T^{n}y,T^{n}y^{\prime})\ll d(Fy,Fy^{\prime})\leq\diam(M)^{1-\eta}d(Fy,Fy^{\prime})^{\eta}.

\textstyle|r_{n}(y^{\prime})-r_{n}(y)|\leq|r|_{\eta}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}d(T^{j}y,T^{j}y^{\prime})^{\eta}\ll nd(Fy,Fy^{\prime})^{\eta}.

\textstyle d(\Psi_{u}y,\Psi_{u}y^{\prime})\leq C(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)d(Fy,Fy^{\prime})^{\eta}.

\textstyle|w(y,u)-w(y^{\prime},u)|\leq C|v|_{\eta}(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)^{\eta}d(Fy,Fy^{\prime})^{\eta^{2}}.

\textstyle g(y)\leq C\mu(Y_{j})\qquad\text{and}\qquad|g(y)-g(y^{\prime})|\leq C\mu(Y_{j})d(Fy,Fy^{\prime})^{\eta},

4.2

.

has mean zero.

\textstyle\int_{Y}Pv^{\prime}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{Y}v^{\prime}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{Y}\int_{0}^{\varphi(y)}v(y,u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu=\bar{\varphi}\int_{Y^{\varphi}}v\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{\varphi}=0.

Proposition 4.5
.

There exists

\begin{split}|v^{\prime}(y)-v^{\prime}(y^{\prime})|\leq&\textstyle\int_{0}^{\varphi(y)}|v(y,u)-v(y^{\prime},u)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u+\int_{\varphi(y)}^{\varphi(y^{\prime})}|v(y^{\prime},u)|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\\
\ll&\textstyle\bigl(|v|_{\eta}(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)(\sup_{Y_{j}}\varphi)+|v|_{\infty}(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)\bigr)d(Fy,Fy^{\prime})^{\eta}\\
\leq&\|v\|_{\eta}(\textstyle\sup_{Y_{j}}\varphi^{2})d(Fy,Fy^{\prime})^{\eta}.\end{split}

, with preimages

\displaystyle\textstyle|(Pv^{\prime})(y)-(Pv^{\prime})(y^{\prime})|\leq

Proposition 4.7
.

Let

. Then

(L_{1}v)(y,u)=\begin{cases}v(y,u-1)&u\in[1,\varphi(y))\\

is the unique preimage of
y
under
\left.\kern-1.2ptF\vphantom{|}\right|_{Y_{j}}

Proof.

Let

By definition of

and
\mu^{\varphi}
, using the substitution

\begin{split}\textstyle\int_{Y^{\varphi}}L_{1}(\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\leq u<\varphi-1\}}v)w\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{\varphi}&\textstyle=\bar{\varphi}^{-1}\int_{Y}\int_{0}^{\varphi(y)}\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\leq u<\varphi(y)-1\}}v(y,u)w(y,u+1)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\\
&\textstyle=\int_{Y^{\varphi}}\mathbbm{1}_{\{1\leq u<\varphi(y)\}}v(y,u-1)w(y,u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{\varphi}.\end{split}

(4.8)

Next, let us focus on

\mathbbm{1}_{\{\varphi-1\leq u<\varphi\}}v
.
By the substitution

,

\begin{split}\textstyle\int_{Y^{\varphi}}L_{1}(\mathbbm{1}_{\{\varphi-1\leq u<\varphi\}}v)w\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{\varphi}&\textstyle=\bar{\varphi}^{-1}\int_{Y}\int_{\varphi(y)-1}^{\varphi(y)}v(y,u)w(Fy,u+1-\varphi(y))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\\
&\textstyle=\bar{\varphi}^{-1}\int_{Y}\int_{0}^{1}v(y,u-1+\varphi(y))w(Fy,u)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu.\end{split}

Write

\tilde{v}_{u}(y)=v(y,u-1+\varphi(y))

and

\begin{split}\textstyle\int_{Y^{\varphi}}L_{1}(\mathbbm{1}_{\{\varphi-1\leq u<\varphi\}}v)w\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu^{\varphi}&\textstyle=\bar{\varphi}^{-1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{Y}\tilde{v}_{u}(w^{u}\circ F)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\\
&\textstyle=\bar{\varphi}^{-1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{Y}(P\tilde{v}_{u})w^{u}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\\

(4.9)

\displaystyle\textstyle=\mathbbm{1}_{\{1\leq u<\varphi\}}v(y,u-1)+\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\leq u<1\}}(P\tilde{v}_{u})(y).

The proof is completed by the pointwise formula for

.

Proposition 4.8

\displaystyle\textstyle\ll\|v\|_{\eta}^{2}\sum_{j}\mu(Y_{j})(\sup_{Y_{j}}\varphi^{2})\ d(Fy_{j},Fy^{\prime}_{j})^{\eta}

\displaystyle\textstyle\ll\|v\|^{2}_{\eta}d(y,y^{\prime})^{\eta}.

We conclude that

Proposition 4.17
.

There exists

such that

\textstyle\bigl|\int_{0}^{n}\breve{v}\circ F_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\breve{v}\circ F_{j}\bigr|_{\infty}\leq C\|v\|_{\eta}^{2},

Proof.

Define

\textstyle\bigl|\int_{0}^{n}(U_{1}L_{1}(mm^{T}))(F_{s}(y,u))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s-\sum_{j=0}^{N_{n-1}(y,u)}\alpha(F_{j}y)\bigr|\leq|\alpha(y)|+|\alpha(F^{N_{n}(y,u)}y)|\leq 2|\alpha|_{\infty},

(4.18)

4.17

\displaystyle\textstyle=\bigl|\int_{0}^{n}(U_{1}L_{1}(mm^{T}))\circ F_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s-\sum_{j=0}^{{n-1}}(U_{1}L_{1}(mm^{T}))\circ F_{j}\bigr|_{\infty}

where the last inequality is true by (

6
Nonuniformly hyperbolic flows

In the previous sections we showed how to derive rates in the WIP for certain non-invertible systems using martingale techniques and Gordin’s method. However, these approaches are not applicable directly in the invertible setting. Therefore, in this section we illustrate how the theorems outlined in Section
2
remain valid for a class of invertible systems.

Following the arguments of
[
3
, Remark 6.2(a)]
, Theorems
2.3
and
2.5
are still valid for a class of maps that are nonuniformly hyperbolic in the sense of Young
[
43
,
44
]
, namely they display polynomial tails with inducing time in
L^{p}
, and have an exponential contraction along stable leaves. Such conditions are satisfied if the Young tower displays exponential tails.
However, the matter of passing the rates from semiflows to flows is more delicate than in the discrete setting. In the following, we show how Theorems
2.8
and
2.9
are satisfied for a class of nonuniformly hyperbolic flows that display an exponential contraction along stable leaves.

6.1
Setup

Let
(M,d)
be a bounded metric space and let
\Psi_{t}\colon M\to M
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, be a flow, so
\Psi_{0}=\Id
and
\Psi_{t+s}=\Psi_{t}\circ\Psi_{s}
,

. As in Subsection
2.4
, we assume Lipschitz continuous dependence on initial conditions, on initial conditions (
2.3
) and Lipschitz continuity in time (
2.4
).
Our main assumption is that
\Psi_{t}
can be modelled by a suspension with Hölder continuous first return time, where the induced map is nonuniformly hyperbolic in the sense of Young
[
43
]
.
Here follows a detailed list of our assumptions.

•

There exists
X\subset M
and a function
r\colon X\to[1,\infty)
,
r(x)=\inf\{t>0:\Psi_{t}x\in X\}
, such that
T\colon X\to X
,
Tx=\Psi_{r(x)}x
is nonuniformly hyperbolic. We suppose that there is
\eta_{0}\in(0,1]
such that
|r|_{\eta_{0}}=\sup_{x\neq x^{\prime}}|r(x)-r(x^{\prime})|/d(x,x^{\prime})^{\eta_{0}}<\infty
.

•

There is a Borel probability measure
m
on
X
, which is preserved by
T
.

•

There exists a measurable
Y\subset X
,
m(Y)>0
, with an (at most countable) measurable partition
\{Y_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
and a function
\tau\colon Y\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
that is constant on partition elements, such that
T^{\tau(y)}y\in Y
for all
y\in Y
. We define
F\colon Y\to Y
as

Fy=T^{\tau(y)}y
.

•

Let
p\in[2,\infty]
. We have an
F
-invariant probability measure
\mu
on
Y
such that

\tau\in L^{p}(Y,\mu)
.

•

There is a partition of
Y
consisting of stable leaves
\mathcal{W}^{s}
of
F
, which is a refinement of
\{Y_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
. For
y\in Y
, let
\mathcal{W}^{s}(y)
denote the stable leaf containing
y
. The stable leaves are invariant under

, that is
F(\mathcal{W}^{s}(y))\subset\mathcal{W}^{s}(Fy)
.

•

We have the quotient space
\overline{Y}=Y/\sim
, where
y\sim y^{\prime}
if
y\in\mathcal{W}^{s}(y^{\prime})
, with projection
\overline{\pi}\colon Y\to\overline{Y}
. We can also quotient the map

into
\overline{F}\colon\overline{Y}\to\overline{Y}
with invariant probability measure

.

•

There is a partition
\{\overline{Y}_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}
of
\overline{Y}
such that
Y_{j}=\overline{\pi}^{-1}(\overline{Y}_{j})
. The separation time

is defined as the infimum of

such that
\overline{F}^{n}y
and
\overline{F}^{n}y^{\prime}
belong to different partition elements.
The function
\overline{F}
is a full-branch Gibbs-Markov map and it separates trajectories:

if and only if

. We extend the separation time to

by

.

•

Let
\widetilde{Y}\subset Y
be such that it intersects every stable leaf only once, that is for every
y\in Y
the set
\mathcal{W}^{s}(y)\cap\widetilde{Y}
contains exactly one element, and for

let
\widetilde{Y}_{j}=\widetilde{Y}\cap Y_{j}
. Let
\pi\colon Y\to\widetilde{Y}
be the associated projection, so that
\pi y\in\mathcal{W}^{s}(y)\cap\widetilde{Y}
and
\pi y=\pi y^{\prime}
if and only if
y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}^{s}(y)
.

For
n\geq 0
and
y\in Y
, we let

to be the unique integer such that

\textstyle\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\tau\circ F^{j}(y)\leq n<\sum_{j=0}^{N}\tau\circ F^{j}(y).

(6.1)

To conclude our list of assumptions on
T
, we include some estimates.

•

Suppose there exist
C>0
and

such that

\textstyle d(T^{n}y,T^{n}y^{\prime})\leq C\bigl(\gamma^{n}d(y,y^{\prime})+\gamma^{s(y,y^{\prime})-\beta_{n}(y)}\bigr),

(6.2)

for any

and
y,y^{\prime}\in Y
.

•

Finally, we assume that

d(T^{n}y,T^{n}y^{\prime})\leq C\gamma^{s(y,y^{\prime})-\beta_{n}(y)},

for all
n\geq 0
and
y,y^{\prime}\in\widetilde{Y}
.

In particular, by (

) we have contraction of

and

with

.

Definition 6.1
.

Remark 6.2

Define

If

Theorem 6.3

The remainder of this section is dedicated to proving Theorem

Secondly, we establish that a lift of a Hölder observable

We start by presenting a couple of estimates. We fix

. Since
h
is constant along stable leaves,
it is sufficient to prove that there exists
K>0
such that
|h(y,u)-h(y^{\prime},u)|\leq K(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)\theta^{s(y,y^{\prime})}
for all
(y,u),(y^{\prime},u)\in Y^{\varphi}
such that
y,y^{\prime}\in\widetilde{Y}_{j}
.

For all
j\geq 1
, define
L_{j}=\gamma(1-\gamma)/(C\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)
and fix
y,y^{\prime}\in\widetilde{Y}_{j}
such that
\theta^{s(y,y^{\prime})}\leq L_{j}/2
(the case
\theta^{s(y,y^{\prime})}>L_{j}/2
is trivial). Write
w=v\circ\pi_{M}
and let
u\in[0,\min\{\varphi(y),\varphi(y^{\prime})\}]
. We deal separately with the two addends of
h=w\circ\pi+H
, where
H
is from equation (
6.12
). For the first one, we see by point
1

that

\textstyle|w(\pi y,u)-w(\pi y^{\prime},u)|\ll(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)\gamma^{s(\pi y,\pi y^{\prime})}\leq(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)\theta^{s(y,y^{\prime})}.

Let us deal with
H
.
For
\widehat{N}=\lfloor s(y,y^{\prime})/2\rfloor
, we write

\textstyle|H(y,u)-H(y^{\prime},u)|\leq A_{1}(y,y^{\prime})+A_{2}(y,y^{\prime})+B(y)+B(y^{\prime}),

where

\displaystyle\textstyle A_{1}(y,y^{\prime})=\sum_{n=0}^{\widehat{N}-1}|w(F_{n+1}(\pi y,u))-w(F_{n+1}(\pi y^{\prime},u))|,

\displaystyle\textstyle A_{2}(y,y^{\prime})=\sum_{n=0}^{\widehat{N}-1}|w(F_{n}(\pi(F_{1}(y,u))))-w(F_{n}(\pi(F_{1}(y^{\prime},u))))|,

\displaystyle\textstyle B(y)=\sum_{n=\widehat{N}}^{\infty}|w(F_{n+1}(\pi(y,u)))-w(F_{n}(\pi(F_{1}(y,u))))|.

Let us focus on
B
. By
\inf\varphi\geq 1
, we have

M_{1+u}(y)=M_{1+u}(\pi y)=m\in\{0,1\}
. Using (
6.13
),

F_{1}(\pi(y,u))=(F^{m}\pi y,u^{\prime})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\pi(F_{1}(y,u))=(\pi F^{m}y,u^{\prime}),

where
u^{\prime}
is either
u
or
u+1-\varphi(y)
(depending on
m
).
Since
F^{m}\pi y
and
\pi F^{m}y
belong to the same stable leaf,
point
2
yields,

\displaystyle\textstyle B(y)

\displaystyle\textstyle=\sum_{n=\widehat{N}}^{\infty}\bigl|v\circ\pi_{M}(F_{n}(\pi F^{m}y,u^{\prime}))-v\circ\pi_{M}(F_{n}(F^{m}\pi y,u^{\prime}))\bigr|

\displaystyle\textstyle\leq|v|_{\eta}\sum_{n=\widehat{N}}^{\infty}d\bigl(\pi_{M}\circ F_{n}(\pi F^{m}y,u^{\prime})-\pi_{M}\circ F_{n}(F^{m}\pi y,u^{\prime})\bigr)^{\eta}

\displaystyle\textstyle\ll\sum_{n=\widehat{N}}^{\infty}(\gamma^{\eta})^{n+u^{\prime}}\leq\sum_{n=\widehat{N}}^{\infty}(\gamma^{\eta})^{n}=(\gamma^{\eta})^{\widehat{N}}/(1-\gamma^{\eta}).

By our choice of
\widehat{N}
, we get
B(y)\ll(\gamma^{\eta})^{\widehat{N}}\ll(\gamma^{\eta/2})^{s(y,y^{\prime})}=\theta^{s(y,y^{\prime})}
.

Le us deal with
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
. Note that for all

n=0,\dots,\widehat{N}+1
,
equation (
6.11
) yields

\begin{split}|\varphi_{n}(y)-\varphi_{n}(y^{\prime})|&\leq C(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\gamma^{s(F^{i}y,F^{i}y^{\prime})}\leq C(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)\sum_{i=0}^{\widehat{N}}\gamma^{s(y,y^{\prime})-i}\\
&\leq\frac{C(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)}{1-\gamma}\gamma^{s(y,y^{\prime})-\widehat{N}}\leq\frac{C(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)}{(1-\gamma)\gamma}\gamma^{s(y,y^{\prime})/2}\leq L_{j}^{-1}\theta^{s(y,y^{\prime})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{\inf\varphi}{2}.\end{split}

Hence, for

n=0,\dots,\widehat{N}
, the intervals
[\varphi_{n}(y),\varphi_{n+1}(y)]
and
[\varphi_{n}(y^{\prime}),\varphi_{n+1}(y^{\prime})]
have the initial points and endpoints closer than
(\inf\varphi)/2
.
Let
k=M_{n+u}(y)
and
k^{\prime}=M_{n+u}(y^{\prime})
; by
\inf\varphi\geq 1
it follows that
k,k^{\prime}\leq n\leq\widehat{N}
. Moreover, by

n+u\in[\varphi_{k}(y),\varphi_{k+1}(y)]\cap[\varphi_{k^{\prime}}(y^{\prime}),\varphi_{k^{\prime}+1}(y^{\prime})]

we have that
|k-k^{\prime}|\leq 1
.

For
n=1,\dots,\widehat{N}
, let
a_{1}(y,y^{\prime})=|w(F_{n}(\pi y,u))-w(F_{n}(\pi y^{\prime},u))|
. By (
6.13
),

\begin{split}\textstyle a_{1}(y,y^{\prime})=|w(F^{k}\pi y,n+u-\varphi_{k}(y))-w(F^{k^{\prime}}\pi y^{\prime},n+u-\varphi_{k^{\prime}}(y^{\prime}))|.\end{split}

For
\ell=0,\dots,\widehat{N}-1
, let
a_{2}(y,y^{\prime})=|w(F_{\ell}(\pi(F_{1}(y,u))))-w(F_{\ell}(\pi(F_{1}(y^{\prime},u))))|
and define

\begin{split}&k_{1}=M_{1+u}(y)\qquad k_{2}=M_{\ell+u+1-\varphi_{k_{1}}(y)}(F^{k_{1}}y)\\
&k^{\prime}_{1}=M_{1+u}(y^{\prime})\qquad k^{\prime}_{2}=M_{\ell+u+1-\varphi_{k^{\prime}_{1}}(y^{\prime})}(F^{k^{\prime}_{1}}y^{\prime}).\end{split}

Writing
n=\ell+1\in\{1,\dots,\widehat{N}\}
, we have by (
6.13
) that

a_{2}(y,y^{\prime})=|w(F^{k_{2}}\pi F^{k_{1}}y,n+u-\varphi_{k}(y))-w(F^{k^{\prime}_{2}}\pi F^{k^{\prime}_{1}}y^{\prime},n+u-\varphi_{k^{\prime}}(y^{\prime}))|.

Note that
k_{1}+k_{2}=k=M_{n+u}(y)
and
k^{\prime}_{1}+k^{\prime}_{2}=k^{\prime}=M_{n+u}(y^{\prime})
.
We assume without loss that
k\geq k^{\prime}
.

We claim for
i=1,2
that

\textstyle a_{i}(y,y^{\prime})\ll(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)\gamma^{s(F^{k}y,F^{k}y^{\prime})}+|\varphi_{k}(y)-\varphi_{k}(y^{\prime})|^{\eta}.

(6.14)

Assuming the claim and by equation (
6.11
), we can bound

\begin{split}\textstyle a_{i}(y,y^{\prime})&\textstyle\ll(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)\bigl\{\gamma^{s(y,y^{\prime})-k}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}(\gamma^{\eta})^{s(F^{\ell}y,F^{\ell}y^{\prime})}\bigr\}\\
&\textstyle=(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)\bigl\{\gamma^{s(y,y^{\prime})-k}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}(\gamma^{\eta})^{s(y,y^{\prime})-\ell}\bigr\}\\
&\textstyle=(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)\bigl(1+\gamma^{\eta}/(1-\gamma^{\eta})\bigr)(\gamma^{\eta})^{s(y,y^{\prime})-k}.\end{split}

By
k\leq n
,we have
a_{i}(y,y^{\prime})\ll(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)(\gamma^{\eta})^{s(y,y^{\prime})-n}
and hence

(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)^{-1}A_{i}(y,y^{\prime})\ll\sum_{n=1}^{\widehat{N}}(\gamma^{\eta})^{s(y,y^{\prime})-n}\leq\frac{(\gamma^{\eta})^{s(y,y^{\prime})-\widehat{N}}}{1-\gamma^{\eta}}\ll(\gamma^{\eta})^{s(y,y^{\prime})/2}=\theta^{s(y,y^{\prime})}.

The main statement is proven by combining the estimates for
A_{1},A_{2}
and
B
.

Let us show the claim for
a_{1}(y,y^{\prime})
.
By
|k-k^{\prime}|\leq 1
, we have to deal with two cases. If
k=k^{\prime}
, equation (
6.14
) follows from point
1
.
If
k=k^{\prime}+1
, using again point
1
and the identifications on
Y^{\varphi}
,

\begin{split}a_{1}(y,y^{\prime})&\leq|w(F^{k^{\prime}}\pi y^{\prime},n+u-\varphi_{k^{\prime}}(y^{\prime}))-w(F^{k^{\prime}}\pi y^{\prime},\varphi(F^{k^{\prime}}y^{\prime}))|\\
&\quad+|w(F^{k^{\prime}+1}\pi y^{\prime},0)-w(F^{k^{\prime}+1}\pi y,n+u-\varphi_{k^{\prime}+1}(y))|\\
&\textstyle\leq C(\inf_{Y_{j}}\varphi)\gamma^{s(F^{k^{\prime}+1}\pi y,F^{k^{\prime}+1}\pi y^{\prime})}\\
&\quad+(\varphi(F^{k^{\prime}}y^{\prime})-n-u+\varphi_{k^{\prime}}(y^{\prime}))^{\eta}+(n+u-\varphi_{k^{\prime}+1}(y))^{\eta}.\end{split}

(6.15)

Note that on the last line of (
6.15
) the quantities inside the brackets are positive. Hence, equation (
6.14
) follows by the general inequality
\alpha^{\eta}+\beta^{\eta}\leq 2(\alpha+\beta)^{\eta}
for all
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
.

To deal with
a_{2}(y,y^{\prime})
, we use the identity
s(F^{k_{2}}\pi F^{k_{1}}y,F^{k^{\prime}_{2}}\pi F^{k^{\prime}_{1}}y^{\prime})=s(F^{k}y,F^{k^{\prime}}y^{\prime})
, and the fact that
\varphi
is constant on stable leaves.
Hence, the claim is proven analogously to
a_{1}(y,y^{\prime})
: if
k=k^{\prime}
, equation (
6.14
) follows by point
1
; if
k=k^{\prime}+1
, we conclude by reasoning as in (
6.15
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.3
.

For
v\in C^{\eta}(M,\mathbb{R}^{N})
, let
w=v\circ\pi_{M}
.
By Proposition
6.14
, there exist functions
h,\chi\colon Y^{\varphi}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that
w=h+\chi-\chi\circ F_{1}
with
\chi\in L^{\infty}(Y^{\varphi},\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Let

\overline{h}\colon\overline{Y}^{\overline{\varphi}}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
be the projection of
h
via
\overline{\pi}
.
For
t\in[0,1]
, we define the sequence

\textstyle\overline{W}_{n}(t)=n^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{nt}\overline{h}\circ\overline{F}_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

on the probability space
(\overline{Y}^{\overline{\varphi}},\overline{\mu}^{\overline{\varphi}})
. The first step of the proof is to show that

\mathcal{W}(W_{n},\overline{W}_{n})\ll n^{-1/2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Pi(W_{n},\overline{W}_{n})\ll n^{-1/2}.

Define on the space
(Y^{\varphi},\mu^{\varphi})
the sequences

\textstyle W_{n}^{\prime}(t)=n^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{nt}w\circ F_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\qquad\text{and}\qquad\textstyle W_{n}^{\prime\prime}(t)=n^{-1/2}\int_{0}^{nt}h\circ F_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Note that
W_{n}^{\prime}(t)=W_{n}(t)\circ\pi_{M}
and
\overline{W}_{n}=W^{\prime\prime}_{n}\circ\overline{\pi}
. Since
\pi_{M}
and
\overline{\pi}
are measure-preserving, we have
W_{n}=_{d}W_{n}^{\prime}
and
\overline{W}_{n}=_{d}W^{\prime\prime}_{n}
.
For
x>1
, we get by a change of variables

\textstyle\int_{0}^{x}(\chi-\chi\circ F_{1})\circ F_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{x}\chi\circ F_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s-\int_{1}^{x+1}\chi\circ F_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{1}\chi\circ F_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s-\int_{x}^{x+1}\chi\circ F_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

which implies that

|\int_{0}^{x}(\chi-\chi\circ F_{1})\circ F_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s|\leq 2|\chi|_{\infty}
, for all
x>0
.
So,

\textstyle\bigl|\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|W^{\prime}(t)-W^{\prime\prime}(t)|\bigr|_{\infty}=n^{-1/2}\bigl|\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\bigl|\int_{0}^{nt}(\chi-\chi\circ F_{1})\circ F_{s}\bigr|\bigr|_{\infty}\leq 2n^{-1/2}|\chi|_{\infty}.

For the Wasserstein metric, we get

\textstyle\mathcal{W}(W_{n},\overline{W}_{n})=\mathcal{W}(W^{\prime}_{n},W^{\prime\prime}_{n})\leq\mathbb{E}[\sup_{t\in[0,1]}|W^{\prime}_{n}(t)-W^{\prime\prime}_{n}(t)|]\ll n^{-1/2}.

Since
\Pi
is bounded by the infinity norm,

\Pi(W_{n},\overline{W}_{n})=\Pi(W^{\prime}_{n},W^{\prime\prime}_{n})\ll n^{-1/2}
.
Hence, we are left to estimate the rates for
\overline{W}_{n}
.

Using Remarks
6.13
and
6.16
, we see that
\overline{F}_{t}
is a Gibbs-Markov semiflow and the observable
\overline{h}
belongs to the functional space of Definition
4.2
.
So, the sequence
\overline{W}_{n}
coincides with
\widehat{W}_{n}
of (
5.1
). Hence, the rates for

\mathcal{W}(\overline{W}_{n},W)
and
\Pi(\overline{W}_{n},W)
can be deduced as in the proofs of Theorem
2.8
and
2.9
(which can be found in Section
5
).
∎

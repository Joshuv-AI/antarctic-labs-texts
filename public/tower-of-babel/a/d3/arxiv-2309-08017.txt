Diffeomorphism covariance of the canonical Barbero-Immirzi-Holst triad theory
Donald Salisbury

Abstract
The vanishing phase space generator of the full four-dimensional diffeomorphism-related symmetry group in the context of the Barbero-Immirz-Holst Lagrangian is derived directly for the first time from Noether’s second theorem. It’s applicability in the construction of classical diffeomorphism invariants is reviewed.



1
Introduction

What I identify as the Barbero-Immirzi-Holst model serves as a foundation for today’s canonical approach to loop quantum gravity. I will derive in this article a new analysis of the underlying four-dimensional spacetime diffeomorphism-related classical canonical symmetry. I will derive the canonical symmetry generators directly from the vanishing charge that follows from Emmy Noether’s second theorem, in a manner similar to the first such derivation presented for conventional canonical gravity in
[
Salisbury et al., 2022
]
. The focus will be on a reformulated ADM approach that incorporates densitied triads. And I will argue that the extension of this analysis to the new triad approach to gravity as proposed by
[
Barbero, 1995
]
,
[
Immirzi, 1997
]
, and
[
Holst, 1996
]
is almost trivial. As is well known, in order to achieve the results of canonically generated variations of spacetime coordinates it is necessary to supplement the variations of phase space variables under diffeomorphims with related triad gauge transformations. I conclude with an overview of a technique for introducing intrinsic coordinates as gauge conditions, and employing the full diffeomorphism generator to construct invariant temporal evolution in a manner related to Rovelli’s relative observables
[
Rovelli, 2002
]
. This lays the foundations for an eventual application in loop quantum gravity.

2
Derivation of canonical Hamiltonian

I use the ADM Lagrangian as rewritten using triad variables.

{\cal L}_{ADM}=Nt\left({}^{3}\!R+K_{ab}K^{ab}-\left(K^{a}_{a}\right)^{2}\right)=Nt\left({}^{3}\!R+K_{ab}e^{ac}e^{bd}K_{cd}-\left(e^{ab}K_{ab}\right)^{2}\right),

(2.1)

where

K_{ab}=\frac{1}{2N}\left(g_{ab,0}-N^{c}g_{ab,c}-g_{ca}N^{c}_{,b}-g_{cb}N^{c}_{,a}\right)=\frac{1}{2N}\left(g_{ab,0}-2g_{c(a}N^{c}_{|b)}\right).

(2.2)

The variable
t
is the determinant of the spatial metic
g_{ab}
, with
e^{ab}
its inverse. The variable
N
is the lapse while
N^{a}
represents the metric shift functions.
{}^{3}\!R
is the tree-dimensional curvature scalar.

The first task is to specialize to tetrads with the choice
E^{\mu}_{0}=n^{\mu}=\delta^{\mu}_{0}N^{-1}-\delta^{\mu}_{a}N^{-1}N^{a}
. This tetrad is orthogonal to the constant time hypersurface. The covariant metric is

g_{\mu\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}-N^{2}+N^{c}N^{d}g_{cd}&g_{ac}N^{c}\\
g_{bd}N^{d}&g_{ab}\end{pmatrix},

(2.3)

with the contravariant metric

g^{\mu\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}-1/N^{2}&N^{a}/N^{2}\\
N^{b}/N^{2}&e^{ab}-N^{a}N^{b}/N^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(2.4)

We then choose the remaining tetrads to be tangential to the constant time hypersurface. Thus the full set of contravariant tetrads (with the upper index representing the row and the lower index representing the column) is

E^{\mu}_{I}=\begin{pmatrix}N^{-1}&0\\
-N^{-1}N^{a}&T^{a}_{i}\end{pmatrix}

(2.5)

with the corresponding covariant set

e^{I}_{\mu}=\begin{pmatrix}N&0\\
t^{i}_{a}N^{a}&t^{i}_{a}\end{pmatrix}

(2.6)

We shall, however, employ as independent triad variables
\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{T}}\smash{T}}\limits^{\;{}_{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{a}_{i}:=tT^{a}_{i}
where
t:=\det\left(t^{i}_{a}\right)
. Furthermore, rather than choosing the lapse
N
as an independent configuration variable we work with
\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/:=t^{-1}N
. So for the following we will need

t_{,\mu}=tt^{i}_{a,\mu}T^{a}_{i}=\left(t^{i}_{a}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i}\right)_{,\mu}-t^{i}_{a}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i,\mu},

(2.7)

so we find that

t_{,\mu}=\frac{1}{2}t^{i}_{a}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i,\mu},

(2.8)

t^{i}_{a,\mu}=2t^{-1}t^{[i}_{a}t^{j]}_{b}\tilde{T}^{b}_{j,\mu},

(2.9)

and

T^{a}_{i,\mu}=-\frac{1}{2}t^{-2}t^{j}_{b}\tilde{T}^{b}_{j,\mu}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i}+t^{-1}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i,\mu}.

(2.10)

Now define the canonical momentum

\displaystyle p^{l}_{e}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\cal L}_{ADM}}{\partial\tilde{T}^{e}_{l,0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2Nt\left(e^{ac}e^{bd}-e^{ab}e^{cd}\right)K_{cd}\frac{\partial K_{ab}}{\partial\tilde{T}^{e}_{l,0}}.

So I need

2Nt\frac{\partial K_{ab}}{\partial\tilde{T}^{e}_{l,0}}=g_{ab}t^{l}_{e}-2t^{l}_{(a}g_{b)e}.

(2.12)

Therefore

p^{l}_{e}=-2T^{d}_{l}K_{ed},

(2.13)

from which we deduce that

p^{i}_{a}t^{i}_{b}=-2K_{ab}.

(2.14)

So I can write the Lagrangian immediately in terms of the canonical momenta.

To obtain the canonical Hamiltonian
{\cal H}_{c}
I must now focus on
p^{i}_{a}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i,0}
which I want to write in terms of the momenta. I have

p^{i}_{a}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i,0}=-2K_{ab}T^{b}_{i}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i,0}

(2.15)

I will rewrite this in terms of derivatives of
t^{j}_{c}
. So consider first

\displaystyle\tilde{T}^{a}_{i,0}=\left(tT^{a}_{i}\right)_{,0}=t_{,0}T^{a}_{i}+tT^{a}_{i,0}=tt^{j}_{c,0}T^{c}_{j}T^{a}_{i}-tT^{c}_{i}T^{a}_{j}t^{j}_{c,0},

(2.16)

and I therefore have

p^{i}_{a}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i,0}=-tK_{ab}\left(e^{ab}e^{cd}-e^{bc}e^{ad}\right)g_{cd,0}

(2.17)

But

g_{cd,0}=2NK_{cd}+2g_{e(c}N^{e}_{|d},

(2.18)

so I conclude finally that

p^{i}_{a}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i,0}=-2tK_{ab}\left(e^{ab}e^{cd}-e^{bc}e^{ad}\right)\left(NK_{cd}+g_{e(c}N^{e}_{|d}\right)

(2.19)

I thereby obtain the expression for the canonical Hamiltonian

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{i}_{a}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i,0}-{\cal L}_{ADM}

(2.20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/\left(-{}^{3}\!R+K_{ab}e^{ac}e^{bd}K_{cd}-\left(e^{ab}K_{ab}\right)^{2}\right)+2t\left(-K^{a}_{a}e^{cd}+K^{cd}\right)g_{ec}N^{e}_{|d}

For later use I need to rewrite the canonical Hamiltonian in terms of
p^{i}_{a}
using
K_{ab}=-\frac{1}{2}p^{i}_{a}t^{i}_{b}
, which, implies that

K_{ab}e^{ac}e^{bd}K_{cd}=\frac{1}{4}p^{i}_{a}t^{i}_{b}p^{j}_{c}t^{j}_{d}e^{ac}e^{bd}=p^{i}_{a}p^{i}_{b}e^{ab},

(2.21)

and

e^{ab}K_{ab}e^{cd}K_{cd}=\frac{1}{4}p^{i}_{a}T^{a}_{i}p^{j}_{b}T^{b}_{j}.

(2.22)

So the canonical Hamitonian becomes

{\cal H}_{c}=\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/\left(-{}^{3}\!R+\frac{1}{4}p^{i}_{a}p^{i}_{b}e^{ab}-\frac{1}{4}p^{i}_{a}T^{a}_{i}p^{j}_{b}T^{b}_{j}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left(p^{i}_{a}T^{a}_{i}e^{cd}-p^{i}_{a}T^{d}_{i}e^{ac}\right)tg_{e(c}N^{e}_{|d)}

(2.23)

(It is straightforward to check that this does deliver an almost correct expression for the time rate of change of the densitized triad - lacking, as we shall see shortly, the arbitrary triad gauge rotations),

\displaystyle\tilde{T}^{e}_{l,0}=\frac{\partial{\cal H}_{c}}{\partial p^{l}_{e}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/\left(e^{ac}e^{bd}-e^{ab}e^{cd}\right)K_{cd}\frac{1}{2}\delta^{e}_{a}t^{l}_{b}+tg_{f(c}N^{f}_{|d)}\left(e^{ab}e^{cd}-e^{ac}e^{bd}\right)\frac{1}{2}\delta^{e}_{a}t^{l}_{b}

(2.24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/\left(e^{ec}T^{d}_{l}-e^{eb}e^{cd}\right)K_{cd}+\frac{1}{2}tg_{f(c}N^{f}_{|d)}\left(T^{e}_{l}e^{cd}-e^{ec}T^{d}_{l}\right)

It is important to recognize here that the ADM Lagrangian does not depend on the antisymmetrized linear combination of velocities

\tilde{T}^{a[i}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{t}}\smash{t}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{j]}_{a}
, and as a consequence we will obtain a corresponding primary constraint, with a corresponding addition to the Hamiltonian generator of time evolution. Rosenfeld had indeed in
[
Rosenfeld, 1930
]

[
Rosenfeld, 2017
]
considered a tetrad version of general relativity in which analogous constraints appeared and, although he did not explicitly construct the corresponding extended Hamiltonian, it was shown in
[
Salisbury and Sundermeyer, 2017
]
that he could easily have applied his new techniques to do so. I will next derive the relevant primary constraint by applying Noether’s second theorem.

3
Noether charges

First there is a vanishing charge that arises from the invariance of the ADM action under triad rotations

\delta_{\eta}T^{a}_{i}=\epsilon^{ijk}\tilde{T}^{a}_{j}\eta_{k},

(3.1)

where the
\eta_{k}
are arbitrary spacetime functions.
Following Noether’s second theorem, conserved charge arises as follows. The variation of the action is

0=\delta_{\eta}\int d^{4}\!x{\cal L}_{ADM}=\int d^{4}\!x\left[\left(\frac{\delta{\cal L}_{ADM}}{\delta\tilde{T}^{a}_{i}}\right)\delta_{\eta}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i}+\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}_{ADM}}{\partial\tilde{T}^{a}_{j,\mu}}\epsilon^{ijk}\tilde{T}^{a}_{j}\eta_{k}\right)_{,\mu}\right]

(3.2)

When the field equations are satisfied we thus obtain, letting the variations vanish at spatial infinity, the conserved charge

C_{\eta}=\int d^{3}xp^{i}_{a}\epsilon^{ijk}\tilde{T}^{a}_{j}\eta_{k}.

(3.3)

But since
\eta_{k}
can vary arbitrarily with time we deduce the existence of constraints

0={\cal H}^{k}:=\epsilon^{ijk}p^{i}_{a}\tilde{T}^{a}_{j}.

(3.4)

The additional constraints that arise from the invariance of the action under spacetime diffeomorphisms will require a bit more work to derive. I will derive the vanishing Noether charge diffeomorphism-related generator following the procedure that was applied in the conventional metric case in
[
Salisbury et al., 2022
]
. It should be noted here that this procedure was applied to tetrad-based general relativity by Rosenfeld in 1930. And as observed in
[
Salisbury and Sundermeyer, 2017
]
he did not complete the derivation of the canonical generators that I will shortly find, very likely because he recognized that he could not express them exclusively in terms of canonical variables. In other words he did not recognize, as first observed in
[
Pons et al., 1997
]
, that the variations were not projectable under the Legendre transformation to phase space.

Under an infinitesimal diffeomorphism
x^{\prime\mu}=x^{\mu}-\epsilon^{\mu}
, the scalar density
{\cal L}_{ADM}
transforms as

\bar{\delta}{\cal L}_{ADM}=\left({\cal L}_{ADM}\epsilon^{\mu}\right)_{,\mu},

(3.5)

where the
\bar{\delta}
variation is actually the Lie derivative
{\cal L}_{\epsilon}
. I will shortly work out the corresponding field variations. But first I will derive the corresponding vanishing Noether charges noting that when the field equations are satisfied, and letting
\epsilon^{a}\rightarrow 0
at spatial infinity,

\displaystyle\int d^{4}\!x\bar{\delta}{\cal L}_{ADM}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}\!x\left.\left(\frac{\partial{\cal L}_{ADM}}{\partial\tilde{T}^{a}_{i,0}}\bar{\delta}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i}+\frac{\partial{\cal L}_{ADM}}{\partial\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/_{,0}}\bar{\delta}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/+\frac{\partial{\cal L}_{ADM}}{\partial N^{a}_{,0}}\bar{\delta}N^{a}\right)\right|^{x^{0}_{f}}_{x^{0}_{i}}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}\!x\left.{\cal L}_{ADM}\epsilon^{0}\right|^{x^{0}_{f}}_{x^{0}_{i}}

So again taking into account that the time dependence of
\epsilon^{\mu}
is arbitrary we derive the corresponding vanishing Noether charges

C_{\epsilon}=\int d^{3}\!x\mathfrak{C}_{\epsilon}

(3.7)

with vanishing charge density

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\cal L}_{ADM}}{\partial\tilde{T}^{a}_{i,0}}\bar{\delta}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i}+\frac{\partial{\cal L}_{ADM}}{\partial\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/_{,0}}\bar{\delta}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/+\frac{\partial{\cal L}_{ADM}}{\partial N^{a}_{,0}}\bar{\delta}N^{a}-{\cal L}_{ADM}\epsilon^{0}

(3.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{i}_{a}\bar{\delta}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i}+\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{P}}\smash{P}}\limits^{\;{}_{\approx}}\!\hbox{}\/\bar{\delta}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/+\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{P}}\smash{P}}\limits^{\;{}_{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/_{a}\bar{\delta}N^{a}-{\cal L}_{ADM}\epsilon^{0}

We recognize, of course, that the momenta
\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{P}}\smash{P}}\limits^{\;{}_{\approx}}\!\hbox{}\/
and
\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{P}}\smash{P}}\limits^{\;{}_{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/_{a}
are primary constraints.

The next step is to determine the variations under
x^{\prime\mu}=x^{\mu}-\epsilon^{\mu}
. We must bear in mind that the variations of the triads must yield vectors that remain tangent to the fixed time hypersurface. And furthermore the varied
n^{\mu}=\delta^{\mu}_{0}N^{-1}-\delta^{\mu}_{a}N^{-1}N^{a}
must be perpendicular to this new hypersurface. The resulting variations are

\bar{\delta}N=N\epsilon^{0}_{,0}-NN^{a}\epsilon^{0}_{,a}+N\epsilon^{0}_{,0}+N_{,a}\epsilon^{a},

(3.9)

and

\bar{\delta}N^{a}=N^{a}\epsilon^{0}_{,0}-(N^{2}e^{ab}+N^{a}N^{b})\epsilon^{0}_{,b}+\epsilon^{a}_{,0}-N^{b}\epsilon^{a}_{,b}+N^{a}_{,0}\epsilon^{0}+N^{a}_{,b}\epsilon^{b}.

(3.10)

To determine the variation of
\tilde{T}^{a}_{i}
I refer to the variation of the spatial components of the metric. I have

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}g_{ab}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}t^{i}_{a}t^{i}_{b}+t^{i}_{a}\bar{\delta}t^{i}_{b}

(3.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{i}_{a,\mu}\epsilon^{\mu}t^{i}_{b}+t^{i}_{a}t^{i}_{b,\mu}\epsilon^{\mu}+t^{i}_{c}N^{c}\epsilon^{\mu}_{,a}t^{i}_{b}+t^{i}_{c}\epsilon^{c}_{,a}t^{i}_{b}+t^{i}_{a}t^{i}_{c}N^{c}\epsilon^{0}_{,b}+t^{i}_{a}t^{i}_{c}\epsilon^{c}_{,b}.

So I find

\bar{\delta}t^{i}_{a}=t^{i}_{a,\mu}\epsilon^{\mu}+t^{i}_{b}N^{b}\epsilon^{0}_{,a}+t^{i}_{b}\epsilon^{b}_{,a}

(3.12)

Next I calculate
\bar{\delta}T^{a}_{i}
using

\bar{\delta}t^{i}_{a}T^{a}_{j}=-t^{i}_{a}\bar{\delta}T^{a}_{j},

(3.13)

which implies

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}T^{b}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bar{\delta}t^{i}_{a}T^{a}_{j}T^{b}_{i}=-\left(t^{i}_{a,\mu}\epsilon^{\mu}+t^{i}_{c}N^{c}\epsilon^{0}_{,a}+t^{i}_{c}\epsilon^{c}_{,a}\right)T^{a}_{j}T^{b}_{i}

(3.14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{b}_{j,\mu}\epsilon^{\mu}-N^{b}T^{a}_{j}\epsilon^{0}_{,a}-\epsilon^{b}_{,a}T^{a}_{j}.

Now to get
\bar{\delta}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i}
I need

\bar{\delta}t=t\bar{\delta}t^{i}_{a}T^{a}_{i}=t\left(t^{i}_{a,\mu}\epsilon^{\mu}+t^{i}_{b}N^{b}\epsilon^{0}_{,a}+t^{i}_{b}\epsilon^{b}_{,a}\right)T^{a}_{i},

(3.15)

which implies

\bar{\delta}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i}=\bar{\delta}tT^{a}_{i}+t\bar{\delta}T^{a}_{i}=\tilde{T}^{a}_{,\mu}\epsilon^{\mu}+N^{b}\epsilon^{0}_{,b}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i}+\epsilon^{b}_{,b}\tilde{T}^{a}_{i}-N^{a}\tilde{T}^{c}_{i}\epsilon^{0}_{,c}-\epsilon^{a}_{,c}\tilde{T}^{c}_{i}

(3.16)

Finally, we also find that

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/\left(\frac{1}{2}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{t}}\smash{t}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{i}_{a}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{T}}\smash{T}}\limits^{\;{}_{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{a}_{i,\mu}\epsilon^{\mu}+\epsilon^{a}_{,a}+\epsilon^{0}_{,a}N^{a}\right)

(3.17)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/\epsilon^{0}_{,0}-\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/N^{a}\epsilon^{0}_{,a}+\left(\frac{1}{2}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{t}}\smash{t}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{i}_{a}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{T}}\smash{T}}\limits^{\;{}_{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{a}_{i,0}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/+\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/_{,0}\right)\epsilon^{0}+\left(\frac{1}{2}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{t}}\smash{t}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{i}_{a}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{T}}\smash{T}}\limits^{\;{}_{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{a}_{i,b}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/+\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/_{,b}\right)\epsilon^{b}

As noted originally in
[
Pons et al., 1997
]
with regard to Hilbert action, the variations of the lapse and shift are not projectable under the Legendre transformation to phase space due to the dependence on their time derivatives and the unique means of eliminating these terms in spacetime diffeomorphisms is to require a metric dependence which I rewrite in the form
\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{n}}\smash{n}}\limits^{\;{}_{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{\mu}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}
, where

\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{n}}\smash{n}}\limits^{\;{}_{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{\mu}:=tn^{\mu}=\left(\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/\right)^{-1}\left(\delta^{\mu}_{0}-\delta^{\mu}_{a}N^{a}\right).

(3.18)

The general infinitesimal spacetime coordinate variation is therefore

\epsilon^{\mu}=tn^{\mu}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}+\delta^{\mu}_{a}\xi^{a}.

(3.19)

It should be noted here that this requirement results in a loss of the original spacetime diffeomorphism Lie algebra. The most striking change is a forced dependence on the underlying spatial metric, leading to what has become known as the Bergmann Komar group. A detailed history of this development can be found in
[
Salisbury, 2020
]
and
[
Salisbury, 2022
]
.

Taking this required metric dependence into account, the resulting variations are

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}N

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{\xi}^{0}-N^{a}\xi^{0}_{,a}+\xi^{a}N_{,a}=\left(t\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}\right)_{,0}-N^{a}\left(t\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}\right)_{,a}+\xi^{a}N_{,a}

(3.20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{,0}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}+t\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}_{,0}-N^{a}t_{,a}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}-N^{a}t\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}_{,a}+\xi^{a}N_{,a}

so

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}t^{-1}N+t^{-1}\bar{\delta}N

(3.21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-t^{-2}\bar{\delta}tN+t^{-1}\left(t_{,0}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}+t\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}_{,0}-N^{a}t_{,a}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}-N^{a}t\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}_{,a}+\xi^{a}N_{,a}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-t^{-2}\bar{\delta}tN+t^{-1}\left(tt^{i}_{a,0}T^{a}_{i}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}+t\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}_{,0}-N^{a}tt^{i}_{b,a}T^{b}_{i}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}-N^{a}t\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}_{,a}+\xi^{a}N_{,a}\right)

To continue I need

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}t^{i}_{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{i}_{a,\mu}\epsilon^{\mu}+t^{i}_{b}N^{b}\epsilon^{0}_{,a}+t^{i}_{b}\epsilon^{b}_{,a}

(3.22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N^{-1}t^{i}_{a,0}\xi^{0}-N^{-1}t^{i}_{a,b}N^{b}\xi^{0}+t^{i}_{a,b}\xi^{b}+t^{i}_{b}N^{b}\left(N^{-1}\xi^{0}\right)_{,a}+t^{i}_{b}\left(-N^{-1}N^{b}\xi^{0}+\xi^{b}\right)_{,a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N^{-1}t^{i}_{a,0}\xi^{0}-N^{-1}t^{i}_{a,b}N^{b}\xi^{0}+t^{i}_{a,b}\xi^{b}+t^{i}_{b}\left(-N^{-1}N^{b}_{,a}\xi^{0}+\xi^{b}_{,a}\right)

I use this to calculate

\displaystyle-t^{-2}N\bar{\delta}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-t^{-1}N\bar{\delta}t^{i}_{a}T^{a}_{i}=-t^{-1}T^{a}_{i}\left(t^{i}_{a,0}\xi^{0}-t^{i}_{a,b}N^{b}\xi^{0}+Nt^{i}_{a,b}\xi^{b}+t^{i}_{b}\left(-N^{b}_{,a}\xi^{0}+N\xi^{b}_{,a}\right)\right)

Combining terms I get

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-t^{-1}T^{a}_{i}\left(t^{i}_{a,0}\xi^{0}-t^{i}_{a,b}N^{b}\xi^{0}+Nt^{i}_{a,b}\xi^{b}+t^{i}_{b}\left(-N^{b}_{,a}\xi^{0}+N\xi^{b}_{,a}\right)\right)

(3.24)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle t^{-1}\left(tt^{i}_{a,0}T^{a}_{i}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}+t\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}_{,0}-N^{a}tt^{i}_{b,a}T^{b}_{i}\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}-N^{a}t\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{\xi}}\smash{\xi}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/^{0}_{,a}+\xi^{a}N_{,a}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-t^{-1}T^{a}_{i}\left(Nt^{i}_{a,b}\xi^{b}+t^{i}_{b}\left(-N^{b}_{,a}\xi^{0}+N\xi^{b}_{,a}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+

The canonical Hamiltonian

It must be stressed that the above diffeomorphism generator differs in an essential manner from the conventional temporal evolution generator. This takes the form

H=\int d^{3}\!x\left(\hbox{}\!\mathop{\vphantom{{N}}\smash{N}}\limits_{{}^{\sim}}\!\hbox{}\/{\cal H}^{\prime}_{0}+N^{a}{\cal H}_{a}+\Omega^{k}{\cal H}_{k}\right).

C_{\xi\eta}

6
Extension to the Barbero-Immirzi-Holst model

The Holst addition to the Lagrangian is

{\cal L}_{H}=\frac{1}{4\gamma}NtE^{\mu}_{I}E^{\nu}_{J}{}^{4}\!R^{IJ}_{\mu\nu}

(6.1)

It is introduced with what has become known as the Barbero-Immirzi parameter
\gamma
. The curvature is expressed in terms of the Ricci rotation coefficients,

{}^{4}\!R^{IJ}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\Omega^{IJ}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\Omega^{IJ}_{\mu}+\Omega^{IM}_{\mu}\Omega_{\nu M}{}^{J}-\Omega^{IM}_{\nu}\Omega_{\mu M}{}^{J}.

(6.2)

It is of course well known that this Lagrangian vanishes when, as I shall assume, the torsion vanishes. The outcome for my specific use is that the new canonical momentum
p^{\gamma i}_{a}
is obtained through a canonical transformation of
p^{i}_{a}
, i.e.

p^{\gamma i}_{a}=p^{i}_{a}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{-1}\epsilon^{ijk}\omega^{jk}_{a}

(6.3)

It follows that we need only make this substitution for
p^{i}_{a}
in our Noether generator (
4.7
) to obtain the spacetime diffeomorphism-related symmetry generator in the Barbero-Immirzi-Holst model!

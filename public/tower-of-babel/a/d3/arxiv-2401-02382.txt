Hilbert modular forms & Galois representations

1
Introduction

The notion of a Hilbert modular form originates from the unpublished work of David Hilbert and the habilitation thesis of his doctoral student, Otto Blumenthal
Blu 03
. The theory gained prominence after development of the theory of several complex variables. Shimura laid the foundation for the arithmetic theory of Hilbert modular forms
Shi 78
, and Hida provided a framework for the Hecke theory
Hid 88
.

The Taniyama-Shimura-Weil conjecture states that every elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
is modular. The conjecture was proved by Wiles for semistable elliptic curves, thereby proving Fermat’s last theorem, and the full theorem was proved by Breuil, Conrad, Diamond and Taylor.

Motivated by the success of Wiles’ proof, Darmon
Dar 00
initiated a program to tackle the Diophantine equation

x^{p}+y^{q}=z^{r}

(1.1)

where
p
,
q
and
r
are primes and
p
is odd. Darmon’s approach begins by associating certain Frey representations to putative solutions to Equation
1.1
which correspond to certain abelian varieties of
GL(2)
-type over totally real fields. The next step in Darmon’s program is to establish the modularity of these abelian varieties. Here,
modularity
refers to the existence of an appropriate Hilbert modular form whose associated Galois representation corresponds to the abelian variety. The following conjecture generalizes the Taniyama-Shimura-Weil conjecture, and asserts that abelian varieties of
GL(2)
-type over totally real fields are modular.

Conjecture 1

If
A
is an abelian variety of
GL(2,E)
-type over a number field
K
of conductor
\mathfrak{n}
, then there exists a Hilbert modular form
f
over
K
of weight
2
and level
\mathfrak{n}
such that

\rho_{f,\lambda}\cong\rho_{A,\lambda}

for all primes
\lambda
of
E
.

Here
\rho_{f,\lambda}
(respectively,
\rho_{A,\lambda}
) are compatible systems of
\lambda
-adic representations of
K
associated to the Hilbert modular form
f
(respectively, abelian variety
A
).

The conjecture has been proved for elliptic curves over certain classes of number fields. For more details, we refer the reader to the article
Elliptic curves over totally real fields: A survey
by Bidisha Roy and Lalit Vaishya in this proceedings. The recent work of Billerey, et al.
BCDF 23
implements and extends aspects of Darmon’s program to solve several cases of the the generalized Fermat equation
1.1
.

The purpose of this article is to introduce the theory of Hilbert modular forms and Galois representations, and describe what it means to attach a compatible system of Galois representations
\rho_{f,\lambda}
to a Hilbert modular form
f
. The organization of this article is as follows. In Section
2
, we discuss the theory of Hilbert modular forms in the classical language of holomorphic functions on
n
-copies of the hyperbolic plane. In Section
3
, we re-introduce Hilbert modular forms in the adelic formalism, allowing for a smooth treatment of their Hecke theory and
L
-functions. In Section
4
, we give an introduction to Galois representations and their
L
-functions. We conclude this article with a discussion of the landmark result, Theorem
4.3
, which asserts the existence of a compatible system of Galois representations associated to a Hilbert modular form.

2
Hilbert modular forms (classical formulation)

This section is adapted from Chapter 2 of
The 1-2-3 of Modular Forms
by Jan Hendrik Bruinier
BGHZ 08
and Chapter 1 of Paul Garrett’s
Holomorphic Hilbert Modular Forms

Gar 89
.

2.1
Hilbert modular group

Let
F
be a totally real field of degree
n
over
\mathbb{Q}
. Write
\mathcal{O}_{F}
for the ring of integers in
F
. The
Hilbert modular group

\Gamma_{F}:=SL(2,\mathcal{O}_{F})

embeds into
SL(2,\mathbb{R})^{n}
via the
n
real embeddings
F\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, denoted
x\mapsto x^{(j)}
for
j=1,\cdots,n
. There is an action
\Gamma_{F}\curvearrowright\mathbb{H}^{n}
of the Hilbert modular group on
n
copies of the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\Im(z)>0\}
given by embedding
\Gamma_{F}
into
SL(2,\mathbb{R})^{n}
and letting each copy of
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
act on the corresponding copy of
\mathbb{H}
via linear fractional transformations
z\mapsto(az+b)/(cz+d)
.

In this section, we will be concerned with subgroups of
SL(2,F)
which are
commensurable
with the Hilbert modular group, i.e., subgroups
\Gamma\subset SL(2,F)
such that
\Gamma\cap\Gamma_{F}
has finite index in both
\Gamma
and
\Gamma_{F}
. For each ideal
\mathfrak{n}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, the
principal congruence subgroup of level
\mathfrak{n}

\Gamma_{F}(\mathfrak{n}):=\ker(\Gamma_{F}\rightarrow SL(2,\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{n}))

is an example of a commensurable subgroup. A
congruence subgroup
is a subgroup of
\Gamma_{F}
which contains a principal congruence subgroup. All finite index subgroups of the Hilbert modular group of a totally real number field of degree greater than 1 are congruence subgroups
Ser 70
.

2.2
Hilbert modular varieties

Throughout this section,
\Gamma
will denote a congruence subgroup of
\Gamma_{F}
. The group
\Gamma
acts discretely on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. The quotient

Y(\Gamma):=\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{n}

is a non-compact Hausdorff topological space, which we refer to as a
Hilbert modular variety
. If the image of
\Gamma
in
PSL(2,F)
is torsion free, then the quotient map
\mathbb{H}^{n}\rightarrow Y(\Gamma)
is a covering projection and the Hilbert modular variety
Y(\Gamma)
is naturally an
n
-dimensional complex manifold. In general,
\mathbb{H}^{n}\rightarrow Y(\Gamma)
is a branched covering projection, with finitely many branch points in
Y(\Gamma)
.

Since the measure
\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{dx_{j}dy_{j}}{y_{j}^{2}}
is invariant under the action
SL(2,\mathbb{R})^{n}\curvearrowright\mathbb{H}^{n}
it descends to a well-defined measure on
Y(\Gamma)
. It can be shown that the
Y(\Gamma)
has finite volume with respect to this measure
(
Gar 89
, pg. 46)
.

Hilbert modular varieties are non-compact but can be compactified by adding a finite number of cusps. A
cusp
of
\Gamma
is an equivalence class of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(F):=F\cup\{\infty\}
under the action of
\Gamma
. The cusps of
\Gamma_{F}
are in one to one correspondence with ideal classes of
F
. Indeed, the map

\displaystyle\varphi:\Gamma_{F}\backslash\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(F)

\displaystyle[\alpha:\beta]

\displaystyle\mapsto\alpha\mathcal{O}_{F}+\beta\mathcal{O}_{F}

is a bijection
(
Gar 89
, pg. 7)
. Consequently, any subgroup
\Gamma\subset SL(2,F)
commensurable with the Hilbert modular group has finitely many cusps.

2.3
Bailey-Borel compactification

Consider

(\mathbb{H}^{n})^{*}:=\mathbb{H}^{n}\cup\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)=\mathbb{H}^{n}\cup F\cup\{\infty\}.

It can be equipped with an appropriate topology which extends that of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. We describe this topology by specifying a neighborhood basis for each point of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)
.
A neighborhood basis for the point at infinity is given by the sets

N_{C}:=\{\infty\}\cup\{z\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\mid{\textstyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}}\Im(z_{j})>C\}

as
C>0
ranges over all positive reals. A neighborhood basis for a point
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)
is given by
\{\gamma(N_{C})\}_{C>0}
where
\gamma\in\Gamma
is any group element such that
\gamma(\infty)=\alpha
. Such an element
\gamma
always exists as
SL(2,F)
acts transitively on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)
via linear fractional transformations
[\alpha:\beta]\mapsto[a\alpha+b\beta:c\alpha+d\beta]
.

The
Baily-Borel compactification

X(\Gamma)
of the Hilbert modular variety
Y(\Gamma)
is the quotient space defined as

X(\Gamma):=\Gamma\backslash(\mathbb{H}^{n})^{*}.

Here the action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(\mathbb{H}^{n})^{*}
is given by letting
\Gamma
act on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
\mathbb{P}^{1}(F)
separately, as described above. The Hilbert modular variety
Y(\Gamma)
is included in its Baily-Borel compactification
X(\Gamma)
with complement being the finite set of cusps of
\Gamma
. In contrast to
Y(\Gamma)
being non-compact, the space
X(\Gamma)
is a compact Hausdorff topological space. When
n>1
, even if
Y(\Gamma)
is a complex manifold, the space
X(\Gamma)
can never be given the structure of a complex manifold. This is because no neighborhood of a cusp in
X(\Gamma)
is homeomorphic to an open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
when
n>1

(
Fre 90
, pg. 30)
. Instead,
X(\Gamma)
can be can be given the structure of a normal complex space and also, remarkably, the structure of a projective algebraic variety
(
Fre 90
, Ch. 2, Sect. 4)
. Consequently, Hilbert modular varieties can be seen as quasi-projective varieties.

2.4
Example:
\Gamma=\Gamma_{F}[\mathfrak{n}]
, where
F=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
and
\mathfrak{n}=2\mathcal{O}_{F}

When
n=2
, we refer to Hilbert modular varieties as
Hilbert modular surfaces
.

Consider the case when
F=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
is the real quadratic field of smallest positive discriminant. The principal congruence subgroup of level two,
\Gamma=\Gamma_{F}[\mathfrak{n}]
,
\mathfrak{n}=2\mathcal{O}_{F}
, has torsion-free image in
PSL(2,F)
. Thus the Hilbert modular surface
Y(\Gamma)
is a complex surface. Note
F
has class number one and
\Gamma
has index four in
\Gamma_{F}
. The group
\Gamma
has five cusps, represented by
0,1,\infty,\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2},\frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}
. The Bailey-Borel compactification
X(\Gamma)
is no longer a complex manifold, but can be embedded into complex projective space as a projective algebraic variety. Indeed, consider the
(weight two) Eisenstein series
defined by

E_{0}(z_{1},z_{2}):=\lim_{s\searrow 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c,d\in\mathcal{O}_{F}\\
c\mathcal{O}_{F}+d\mathcal{O}_{F}=\mathcal{O}_{F}\\
c\equiv 0,d\equiv 1\mod\mathfrak{n}\end{subarray}}^{\prime}\frac{\left|c^{(1)}z_{1}+d^{(1)}\right|^{-s}\left|c^{(2)}z_{2}+d^{(2)}\right|^{-s}}{(c^{(1)}z_{1}+d^{(1)})^{2}(c^{(2)}z_{2}+d^{(2)})^{2}}

(2.1)

where the dash indicates that the summation is modulo the group
\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times 3}
of units
\varepsilon\equiv 1\mod\mathfrak{n}
. The Eisenstein series
E_{0}(z_{1},z_{2})
converges and vanishes at all cusps of
\Gamma
except for
\infty

(
Fre 90
, pg. 173)
. There are four other Eisenstein series
E_{1},E_{2},E_{3},E_{4}
associated to the other four cusps in a similar way. The mapping
X(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{4}(C)
given by

(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto[E_{0}(z_{1},z_{2}):E_{1}(z_{1},z_{2}):E_{2}(z_{1},z_{2}):E_{3}(z_{1},z_{2}):E_{4}(z_{1},z_{2})]

is well-defined and embeds
X(\Gamma)
into
\mathbb{P}^{4}(\mathbb{C})
as
Klein’s icosahedral surface
, the projective algebraic variety in
\mathbb{P}^{4}(\mathbb{C})
defined by the equations

\sigma_{2}(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})=\sigma_{4}(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4})=0

where
\sigma_{i}
is the
i
’th symmetric polynomial in five variables
Hir 76
.

2.5
Hilbert modular forms

Let
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. For any function
f:\mathbb{H}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and any
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL(2,F)
, denote by
f|_{\mathbf{k}}\gamma
the function

f|_{\mathbf{k}}\gamma:\mathbb{H}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\quad\text{given by}\quad z\mapsto\prod_{j=1}^{n}(c^{(j)}z_{j}+d^{(j)})^{-k_{j}}f(\gamma z).

For
n>1
, a
Hilbert modular form
for
\Gamma
of weight
\mathbf{k}
is a holomorphic function
f:\mathbb{H}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
which obeys the transformation law
f|_{\mathbf{k}}\gamma=f
for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
. In particular, if
\Gamma=\Gamma_{F}
then
f(z+a)=f(z)
for each
a\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
. The
translation module

\mathfrak{t}=\mathfrak{t}_{\Gamma}
for
\Gamma
is the set of translations in
\Gamma
, i.e.,

\mathfrak{t}_{\Gamma}:=\{a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\exists\gamma\in\Gamma\text{ such that }\gamma(z)=z+a\}.

It is a lattice of rank
n
. It is clear that
f(z+a)=f(z)
for all
a\in\mathfrak{t}_{\Gamma}
. This fact allows us to obtain a
Fourier expansion
for
f
, viz.,

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}}A(\nu)e^{2\pi i\langle\nu,z\rangle},

(2.2)

\displaystyle A(\nu)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\operatorname{covol}(\mathfrak{t})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathfrak{t}}f(x+iy)e^{-2\pi i\langle\nu,x+iy\rangle}\prod_{j=1}^{n}dx_{j},

(2.3)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the usual inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
is the
dual lattice

\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}:=\{\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\langle\nu,a\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for all }a\in\mathfrak{t}\}.

The number
A(\nu)
is called the
\nu
’th
Fourier coefficient
for
f
. The fact that the above formula for
A(\nu)
, Equation
2.3
, is independent of the choice of
y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
follows from the holomorphicity of
f
. The
Koecher principle
states that
A(\nu)=0
unless
\nu
is totally positive or zero
(
Gar 89
, pg. 10)
.

The above expansion, Equation
2.2
, is the Fourier expansion of
f
at
\infty
. We may also obtain a Fourier expansion for
f
at other cusps of
\Gamma
. Indeed, if
\kappa\in F
is another cusp of
\Gamma
, there exists some
\gamma\in SL(2,F)
for which
\gamma\kappa=\infty
. It may be seen that
\gamma\Gamma\gamma^{-1}
is still commensurable with the Hilbert modular group and that
f|_{\mathbf{k}}\gamma
is a Hilbert modular form for
\gamma\Gamma\gamma^{-1}
. The Fourier expansion of
f|_{\mathbf{k}}\gamma
at
\infty
will be considered as a Fourier expansion of
f
at
\kappa
, though it is not unique in that it depends on the choice of
\gamma
. Nevertheless, whether or not the constant term in this Fourier expansion is zero is independent of the choice of
\gamma
. We say
f

vanishes at the cusp

\kappa
if the constant term of any Fourier expansion at
\kappa
is
0
. We say
f
is a
cusp form
for
\Gamma
if
f
vanishes at all cusps of
\Gamma
.

2.6
Finite dimensionality

Let
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be a vector of weights. We write
M_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
to denote the vector space of Hilbert modular forms for
\Gamma
of weight
\mathbf{k}
, and
S_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
to denote the subspace of cusp forms. The codimension of
S_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
in
M_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
is bounded by the number of cusps of
\Gamma
. Indeed, if
\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{h}
are the distinct cusps of
\Gamma
, and
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{h}\in SL(2,F)
are fixed elements such that
\gamma_{j}\kappa_{j}=\infty
, we may define a linear map
M_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{h}
with kernel
S_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
given by
f\mapsto(c_{\gamma_{j}}(f))_{j=1}^{h}
where
c_{\gamma_{j}}(f)
is the constant term in the Fourier expansion of
f|_{\mathbf{k}}\gamma_{j}
at
\infty
. Thus, to show finite dimensionality of these vector spaces, it suffices to show
S_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
is finite dimensional. It is also true that when
\mathbf{k}
is not parallel, i.e.
\mathbf{k}\neq k(1,\cdots,1)
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
M_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)=S_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
. This is a consequence of the Koecher principle
(
Gar 89
, pg. 10)
. Moreover, if
\mathbf{k}\neq 0
and any of its entries are non-positive, then
M_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)=\{0\}

(
vdG 87
, pg. 18)
. Note,
M_{0}(\Gamma)=\mathbf{C}
consists of constant functions.

For any
f,g\in S_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
, the
Petersson inner product

\langle f,g\rangle
is defined as

\langle f,g\rangle:=\int_{\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(z)\overline{g(z)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}^{k_{j}}\frac{dx_{j}dy_{j}}{y_{j}^{2}}.

(2.4)

This defines an inner product on
S_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
and makes
S_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
into a Hilbert space. The finite dimensionality of
S_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
follows from existence of a uniform constant
A
such that
||f||_{\infty}\leq A||f||_{2}
, where
||f||_{\infty}
is the
L^{\infty}
-norm on
S_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
given by
||f||_{\infty}:=\max_{z\in\mathbb{H}^{n}}|f(z)|\prod_{j=1}^{n}y^{k_{j}}
, and
||f||_{2}
is the
L^{2}
-norm on
S_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
coming from the Petersson inner product
(
Fre 90
, pg. 68)
. Indeed, making the constant
A
effective would yield the upper bound on dimension
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}S_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)\leq A^{2}\cdot\operatorname{vol}(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{n})
.

2.7
Eisenstein series

For any
2\times 2
matrix
\gamma
, write
a_{\gamma},b_{\gamma},c_{\gamma},d_{\gamma}
to denote the entries of
\gamma
so that

\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a_{\gamma}&b_{\gamma}\\
c_{\gamma}&d_{\gamma}\end{pmatrix}.

Let
\mathbf{k}=k(1,\cdots,1)
where
2<k\in\mathbf{Z}
is even. Let
\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{F}
be a congruence subgroup. As stated in Section
2.1
, when
n\geq 2
this is equivalent to being of finite index in
\Gamma_{F}
. We generally follow Paul Garrett’s
Holomorphic Hilbert Modular Forms

Gar 89
in defining Eisenstein series for
\Gamma
. For each
\alpha\in SL(2,F)
define an
Eisenstein series of weight
\mathbf{k}
for
\Gamma
by

E_{\alpha}(z):=\sum_{\gamma\in S_{\alpha}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(c_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma}^{(j)}z_{j}+d_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma}^{(j)})^{-k}

(2.5)

where
S_{\alpha}
is a set of representatives for
(\alpha P\alpha^{-1}\cap\Gamma)\backslash\Gamma
. Here,
P
denotes the group of upper triangular matrices in
SL(2,F)
. The series defining
E_{\alpha}
does not depend on the choice of representatives
S_{\alpha}
. Indeed, if
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
represent the same left coset in
(\alpha P\alpha^{-1}\cap\Gamma)\backslash\Gamma
, so that
\gamma^{\prime}=\alpha p\alpha^{-1}\gamma
for some
p\in P
satisfying
\alpha p\alpha^{-1}\in\Gamma
, then

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}(c_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}}^{(j)}z_{j}+d_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}}^{(j)})^{-k}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{m}(d_{p}^{(j)}c_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma}^{(j)}z_{j}+d_{p}^{(j)}d_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma}^{(j)})^{-k}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Nm}^{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}(d_{p})^{-k}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{n}(c_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma}^{(j)}z_{j}+d_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma}^{(j)})^{-k},

and moreover
\mathrm{Nm}^{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}(d_{p})^{-k}=1
as
d_{p}
–being an eigenvalue of a matrix in
\Gamma_{F}
–is a unit in
\mathcal{O}_{F}
and
k
is even.

Recall, a series is
absolutely convergent
if the sum of absolute values of its terms is convergent. The Eisenstein series defined in Equation
2.5
are absolutely convergent, since
k>2

(
Gar 89
, pg. 34)
. This allows us to freely rearrange the terms in the series defining the Eisenstein series and observe that
E_{\alpha}|_{\mathbf{k}}\delta=E_{\alpha}
for any
\delta\in\Gamma
. Indeed, if
\delta=\begin{pmatrix}e&f\\
g&h\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma
, then right multiplication by
\delta
induces a permutation on the right cosets in
(\alpha P\alpha^{-1}\cap\Gamma)\backslash\Gamma
, so that, if
S_{\alpha}
is a set of coset representatives for
(\alpha P\alpha^{-1}\cap\Gamma)\backslash\Gamma
, then so is
S_{\alpha}\delta:=\{\gamma\delta\mid\gamma\in S_{\alpha}\}
. Hence,

\displaystyle(E_{\alpha}|_{\mathbf{k}}\delta)(z)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(g^{(j)}z_{j}+h^{(j)})^{-k}\sum_{\gamma}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(c_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma}^{(j)}\left(\frac{e^{(j)}z_{j}+f^{(j)}}{g^{(j)}z_{j}+h^{(j)}}\right)+d_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma}^{(j)}\right)^{-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in S_{\alpha}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}((c_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma}^{(j)}e^{(j)}+d_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma}^{(j)}g^{(j)})z_{j}+c_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma}^{(j)}f^{(j)}+d_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma}^{(j)}h^{(j)})^{-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in S_{\alpha}}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(c_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma\delta}^{(j)}z_{j}+d_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma\delta}^{(j)})^{-k}\quad\text{where }\begin{pmatrix}a_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma\delta}&b_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma\delta}\\
c_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma\delta}&d_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma\delta}\end{pmatrix}=\alpha^{-1}\gamma\delta

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in S_{\alpha}\delta}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(c_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}}^{(j)}z_{j}+d_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}}^{(j)})^{-k}=E_{\alpha}(z).

Thus, Eisenstein series are examples of Hilbert modular forms, and
E_{\alpha}\in M_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
. Write
E_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
to denote the subspace of
M_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
spanned by Eisenstein series. Notice that
E_{\alpha}
is a constant multiple of
E_{\beta}
if
\Gamma\alpha P=\Gamma\beta P
. Indeed, taking
S_{\alpha}
to be a set of coset representatives for
(\alpha P\alpha^{-1}\cap\Gamma)\backslash\Gamma
, we see that if
\beta=\delta\alpha p
for some
\delta\in\Gamma
and
p\in P
then
\delta S_{\alpha}\delta^{-1}:=\{\delta\gamma\delta^{-1}\mid\gamma\in S_{\alpha}\}
is a set of coset representatives for
(\beta P\beta^{-1}\cap\Gamma)\backslash\Gamma
and

\displaystyle E_{\beta}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in\delta S_{\alpha}\delta^{-1}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(c_{\beta^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}}^{(j)}z_{j}+d_{\beta^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}}^{(j)})^{-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in S_{\alpha}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(c_{(\delta\alpha p)^{-1}(\delta\gamma\delta^{-1})}^{(j)}z_{j}+d_{(\delta\alpha p)^{-1}(\delta\gamma\delta^{-1})}^{(j)})^{-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in S_{\alpha}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(c_{p^{-1}\alpha^{-1}\gamma\delta^{-1}}^{(j)}z_{j}+d_{p^{-1}\alpha^{-1}\gamma\delta^{-1}}^{(j)})^{-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in S_{\alpha}\delta^{-1}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(c_{p^{-1}\alpha^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}}^{(j)}z_{j}+d_{p^{-1}\alpha^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}}^{(j)})^{-k}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Nm}^{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}(d_{p^{-1}})^{-k}\cdot\sum_{\gamma^{\prime}\in S_{\alpha}\delta^{-1}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(c_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}}^{(j)}z_{j}+d_{\alpha^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}}^{(j)})^{-k}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Nm}^{F}_{\mathbb{Q}}(d_{p^{-1}})^{-k}\cdot E_{\alpha}(z).

Let
\{\delta_{i}\}
be a set of representatives for
\Gamma\backslash SL(2,F)/P
. It can be shown that
\{\kappa_{i}:=\delta_{i}\infty\}
is a complete set of representatives for the cusps of
\Gamma

(
Gar 89
, pg. 8)
. Moreover, it can be shown that the Eisenstein series
E_{\alpha}
vanishes at all cusps of
\Gamma
except for the unique cusp corresponding to the double coset
\Gamma\alpha P
where it does not vanish
(
Gar 89
, pg. 34)
. It follows that the Eisenstein series
\{E_{\delta_{i}}\}
form a basis for
E_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
, and
E_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
has dimension equal to the number of cusps of
\Gamma
. Moreover, given any Hilbert modular form
f\in M_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
, one can subtract off a suitable linear combination
E
of the Eisenstein series
\{E_{\delta_{i}}\}
so that
f-E\in S_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
is a cusp form. So we see,
M_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)=E_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)\oplus S_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
. Finally, this direct sum decomposition is orthogonal with respect to the Petersson inner product
(
Gar 89
, pg. 51)
.

The Eisenstein series
E_{0}
defined in Equation
2.1
is also a Hilbert modular form. It can be shown that
E_{0}\in S_{(2,2)}(\Gamma)
for
\Gamma=\Gamma_{F}(\mathfrak{n})
where
F=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
and
\mathfrak{n}=2\mathcal{O}_{F}

(
Fre 90
, pg.65 & pg. 160)
. Note that the formula defining Eisenstein series in Equation
2.5
does not converge absolutely for weight
\mathbf{k}=(2,\cdots,2)
. This is why Eisenstein series of weight
\mathbf{k}=(2,\cdots,2)
are defined using the so-called “Hecke trick” or
Hecke regularization
, which involves analytic continuation in an auxiliary parameter
s\in\mathbb{C},\Re(s)>0
, followed by evaluation at
s=0

(
Fre 90
, pg. 65 & Chapter III, §4)
. It is a phenomenon that only appears when
n>1
that the functions obtained in this way are holomorphic.

2.8
Poincare series

Let
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
where
k_{j}>2
for all
j=1,\cdots,n
. Let
\Gamma\subset\Gamma_{F}
be a congruence subgroup. For each
\nu\in\mathfrak{t}_{\Gamma}^{\vee}
define the
\nu
’th
Poincare series of weight
\mathbf{k}
for
\Gamma
by

P_{\nu}(z)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(c_{\gamma}^{(j)}z_{j}+d_{\gamma}^{(j)})^{-k_{j}}e^{2\pi i\langle\nu,\gamma z\rangle}\quad\text{where}\quad\Gamma_{\infty}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\mid x\in\mathfrak{t}_{\Gamma}\right\}.

One can show that the Poincare series is absolutely convergent and converges uniformly in compact subsets of
\mathbb{H}^{n}

(
Gar 89
, pg. 52)
. We check that
P_{\nu}
is a Hilbert modular form of weight
\mathbf{k}
; for any
\delta\in\Gamma
, observe

\displaystyle(P_{\nu}|_{\mathbf{k}}\delta)(z)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(c^{(j)}_{\delta}z_{j}+d^{(j)}_{\delta})^{-k_{j}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(c^{(j)}_{\gamma}\left(\frac{a^{(j)}_{\delta}z_{j}+b^{(j)}_{\delta}}{c^{(j)}_{\delta}z_{j}+d^{(j)}_{\delta}}\right)+d^{(j)}_{\gamma}\right)^{-k_{j}}e^{2\pi i\langle\nu,\gamma z\rangle}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(c^{(j)}_{\gamma\delta}z_{j}+d^{(j)}_{\gamma\delta})^{-k_{j}}e^{2\pi i\langle\nu,\gamma z\rangle}=P_{\nu}(z).

Moreover,
P_{\nu}
vanishes at each cusp of
\Gamma

(
Gar 89
, pg. 52)
. Thus
P_{\nu}\in S_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
is a cusp form.

It can be shown that the Poincare series span cusp forms. Indeed, if
f\in S_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
is an arbitrary cusp form with Fourier expansion

f(z)=\sum_{\nu\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}}A(\nu)e^{2\pi i\langle\nu,z\rangle}

then

\langle f,P_{\nu}\rangle=A(\nu)\cdot\operatorname{covol}(\mathfrak{t}_{\Gamma})\prod_{j=1}^{n}(4\pi v_{j})^{1-k_{j}}(k_{j}-2)!\quad\cite[cite]{(\@@bibref{AuthorsPhrase1Year}{garrett:1989}{\@@citephrase{, }}{}, \text{pg. 53})}.

It follows that the only
f
in the orthogonal complement of the subspace of
S_{\mathbf{k}}(\Gamma)
spanned by the Poincare series are those
f
with all Fourier coefficients zero, i.e.
f
is the zero function.

3
Hilbert modular forms (adelic formulation)

3.1
Preliminaries

In this section, we gather some preliminary material on valuations, places and adeles of a global field. For more details, see Cassels and Frohlich
CF 67
.

3.1.1
Adeles

We begin with the definition of
restricted product
of topological spaces. Let
\{X_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
be a collection of topological spaces. Let
Y_{\alpha}\subset X_{\alpha}
be open in
X_{\alpha}
for all but finitely many
\alpha\in I
. Define

X:=\{(x_{\alpha})\ |\ x_{\alpha}\in X_{\alpha}\text{ for all }\alpha\in I,\text{ and }x_{\alpha}\in Y_{\alpha}\text{ for all but finitely many }\alpha\in I\}.

We define a topology on
X
by taking a basis of open sets to be of the form
\prod U_{\alpha}
where
U_{\alpha}\subset X_{\alpha}
is open for all
\alpha
and
U_{\alpha}=Y_{\alpha}
for almost all
\alpha
. The topological space
X
is called the
restricted product
of
\{X_{\alpha}\}
with respect to
Y_{\alpha}
.
We note the following properties of the restricted product:

1.

If
S
is a finite subset of
\alpha
’s including those for which
Y_{\alpha}
is not defined, let

\begin{split}X_{S}&:=\{(x_{\alpha})\ |\ x_{\alpha}\in Y_{\alpha}\text{ for }\alpha\notin S\}\\
&\cong\prod_{\alpha\in S}X_{\alpha}\prod_{\alpha\notin S}Y_{\alpha}.\end{split}

X_{S}
is open in
X
and the induced topology is same as the product topology.

2.

If
X_{\alpha}
’s are locally compact, and
Y_{\alpha}
’s are compact, then
X
is locally compact.

3.

Suppose that
X_{\alpha}
’s are measure spaces with measure
\mu_{\alpha}
satisfying
\mu_{\alpha}(Y_{\alpha})=1
whenever
Y_{\alpha}
is defined. Then we define the measure on
X
by letting measurable sets to be of the form
\prod M_{\alpha}
, where
M_{\alpha}\subset X_{\alpha}
has finite
\mu_{\alpha}
-measure and
M_{\alpha}=Y_{\alpha}
for almost all
\alpha
, and

\mu(\prod M_{\alpha}):=\prod\mu_{\alpha}(M_{\alpha}).

Now, let
F
be a global field, i.e., a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
or a finite extension of the function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(t)
. In the following, we will define a
place
for
F
assuming it is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.

Definition 1
.

A
valuation
on
F
is a function
v:F\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that:

1.

v(\alpha)=0
if and only if
\alpha=0
.

2.

v(\alpha\beta)=v(\alpha)v(\beta).

3.

\exists\ C
such that
v(1+\alpha)\leq C
whenever
v(\alpha)\leq 1
.

If we can take
C=1
in property 3, then
v
is said to be non-archimedean. Otherwise, it is said to be archimedean. Two valuations
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are said to be equivalent if and only if
v_{1}=v_{2}^{c}
for some
c>0
. The valuation
v
is said to be discrete if
\text{log}\circ v
has discrete image in
\mathbb{R}
.

Let
v
be a nonarchimedean valuation on
F
. Let
\mathcal{O}:=\{\alpha\in F\ |\ v(\alpha)\leq 1\}
be the valuation ring,
\mathcal{O}^{\times}:=\{\alpha\in F\ |\ v(\alpha)=1\}
be the group of units,
\mathfrak{p}:=\{\alpha\in F\ |\ v(\alpha)<1\}
be the unique maximal ideal, and
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}
be the residue field. Assume
v
is discrete. Then,
\mathfrak{p}
is a principal ideal
(\pi)
, where
\pi
is a fixed uniformizer. (A necessary and sufficient condition for a nonarchimedean valuation to be discrete is that
\mathfrak{p}
is a principal ideal.) The valuation
v
is said to be normalized if
v(\pi)=1/|\mathcal{O}_{v}/\mathfrak{p}|
. A valuation induces a metric, and hence a topology, on
F
. Let
F_{v}
be the completion of the metric induced by
v
on
F
. Then, the valuation
v
on
F
can be extended to
F_{v}
. The valuation ring
O_{v}
of a complete field
F_{v}
is compact.

For an archimedean valuation
v
, if the corresponding completion of
F
is
\mathbb{R}
, we say
v
is normalized if it is equal to the ordinary absolute value on
\mathbb{R}
. If the completion is
\mathbb{C}
, we say it is normalized if it is equal to the square of the ordinary absolute value on
\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 2
.

A
place
of
F
is an equivalence class of valuations on
F
. By a
finite place
(resp.
infinite place
) of
F
, we mean that the corresponding valuation is nonarchimedean (resp. archimedean).

Every place contains a unique normalized valuation.

Definition 3
.

The
finite adeles
of
F
, denoted by
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\infty}
, is defined to be the restricted product of the completions
F_{v}
with respect to
\mathcal{O}_{v}
where
v
is a finite place of
F
. The
infinite adeles
of
F
, denoted by
\mathbb{A}_{F,\infty}
is defined to be
F\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}=\prod_{v|\infty}F_{v}
, where the product is over the infinite places of
F
. The
adeles
of
F
, denoted by
\mathbb{A}_{F}
, is defined to be

\mathbb{A}_{F}:=\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\infty}\times\mathbb{A}_{F,\infty}.

\mathbb{A}_{F}
is a topological ring under component-wise addition and multiplication.
From property 2 of the the definition of restricted product and the fact that the valuation ring
\mathcal{O}_{v}
is compact, it follows that
\mathbb{A}_{F}
is locally compact. This implies, in particular, that the additive group of
\mathbb{A}_{F}
has an invariant Haar measure. The field
F
embeds into
\mathbb{A}_{F}
under the diagonal map
x\mapsto(x,...,x,...)
and the image is called the set of principal adeles.

Let
K
be a finite extension of
F
. Then,

\mathbb{A}_{K}\cong\mathbb{A}_{F}\otimes_{F}K

both algebraically and topologically. Lastly, we have the following important result.

Theorem 3.1

(
CF 67
)

The field
F
is a discrete subgroup of
\mathbb{A}_{F}
and the quotient
\mathbb{A}_{F}/F
is compact. Further, the quotient measure induced on
\mathbb{A}_{F}/F
is finite.

Definition 4
.

The units of the ring
\mathbb{A}_{F}
are called the
ideles
of
F
. They form a group under multiplication which is denoted by
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{*}
. The topology on
\mathbb{A}_{F}^{*}
comes from embedding it in
\mathbb{A}_{F}\times\mathbb{A}_{F}
by the map
x\mapsto(x,x^{-1})
.

When
F=\mathbb{Q}
, we write
\mathbb{A}
in place of
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Similar notation is employed for the finite and infinite parts.

3.2
Definitions

We primarily follow the exposition of Jarvis
Jar 97
and Hida
Hid 88
. Let
F
be a fixed totally real field
F
of degree
d
over
\mathbb{Q}
. We will define the adelic version of the space of Hilbert modular forms for
F
of weight
k=(k_{1},...,k_{d})
and level
\mathfrak{n}
(an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
).

Let
I
denote the set of embeddings of
F
in
\mathbb{R}
. Let
G
be the linear algebraic group
\mathrm{Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{GL}_{2})
. This implies that if
A
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra, then the collection of
A
-points of
G
is
G(A)=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(A\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}F)
. Hence,
G(\mathbb{A})=G(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})
and similarly for the finite and infinite parts of the adele ring.

Let
t=(1,1,...,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}
. We say
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}
is
parallel
if and only if
m\in\mathbb{Z}\cdot t
and
m
is parallel to
n
if and only if
m-n\in\mathbb{Z}\cdot t
. We fix
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}_{\geq 1}
such that

k_{1}\equiv k_{2}\equiv...\equiv k_{d}\text{ (mod 2)}.

We define
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}_{\geq 0}
such that
v_{\tau}=0
for some
\tau\in I
and
k+2v
is parallel, and choose
w
parallel to
v+k
. Such a
v
is uniquely defined. We assume
w=v+k-t
and let
\hat{w}=k-w
.

Let
f:G(\mathbb{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
u=u^{\infty}u_{\infty}\in G(\mathbb{A}^{\infty})\times G(\mathbb{A}_{\infty})_{+}
. Let
z_{0}:=(i,i,...,i)\in\mathbb{H}^{d}
. For
x=\left(\begin{bmatrix}a_{\tau}&b_{\tau}\\
c_{\tau}&d_{\tau}\end{bmatrix}\right)_{\tau\in I}\in G(\mathbb{A}_{\infty})=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})^{d}
, we define the automorphy factor

j(x,z):=(c_{\tau}z_{\tau}+d_{\tau})_{\tau\in I}.

We define

(f|_{k,w}u)(x):=j(u_{\infty},z_{0})^{-k}\mathrm{det}(u_{\infty})^{w}f(xu^{-1}).

(3.1)

Let
U
be an open compact subgroup of
G(\mathbb{A}^{\infty})
. We consider the following conditions on
f:G(\mathbb{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
.

1.

f=f|_{k,w}u
for all
u\in UC_{\infty,+}
, where
C_{\infty,+}
is the stabilizer of
z_{0}=(i,i,...,i)\in\mathbb{H}^{d}
in
G(\mathbb{A}_{\infty})_{+}
which is identified with
\mathbb{R}^{\times}SO_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

2.

f(ax)=f(x)
for all
a\in G(\mathbb{Q})=\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)
.

3.

For each
z\in\mathbb{H}^{d}
, choose
u_{\infty}\in G_{\infty,+}
such that
u_{\infty}(z_{0})=z
. For
f
satisfying the above conditions define
f_{x}:\mathbb{H}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

f_{x}(z):=j(u_{\infty},z_{0})^{k}\mathrm{det}(u_{\infty})^{-w}f(xu_{\infty})

(3.2)

for all
x\in G(\mathbb{A}^{\infty})
. (
f_{x}
is independent of the choice of
u_{\infty}
.) We require
f_{x}
to be holomorphic with respect to each component
z_{\tau}
.

4.

We impose the condition:

\int_{\mathbb{A}_{F}/F}f\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&a\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}x\right)dx=0

for all
x\in G(\mathbb{A})
and for each additive Haar measure on
\mathbb{A}_{F}/F
.

5.

When
F=\mathbb{Q}
, we impose the extra condition that
|\text{Im}(z)^{k/2}f_{x}(z)|
is uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{H}
for all
x\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
.

We define the space of
modular forms

M_{k,w}(U)
to be the space of functions
f:G(\mathbb{A})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfying conditions 1,2 and 3 (and 5 when
F=\mathbb{Q}
), and the space of
cusp forms

S_{k,w}(U)
to be the space of functions
f
in
M_{k,w}(U)
which also satisfy condition 4.

We define the following adelic analogues of the classical group
\Gamma_{1}(N)
. Let
U_{0}=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{q}})
, where
\mathfrak{q}
runs over the finite primes of
F
. Let
\mathfrak{n}
be an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Define

U_{1}(\mathfrak{n}):=\biggl\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in U_{0}\ |\ c\in\mathfrak{n},a-1\in\mathfrak{n}\biggr\},

V_{1}(\mathfrak{n}):=\biggl\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in U_{0}\ |\ c\in\mathfrak{n},d-1\in\mathfrak{n}\biggr\}.

We denote
S_{k,w}(\mathfrak{n})=S_{k,w}(U_{1}(\mathfrak{n}))
and
S^{*}_{k,w}(\mathfrak{n})=S_{k,\hat{w}}(V_{1}(\mathfrak{n}))
. These are the
cusp forms of level
\mathfrak{n}
.

We will now describe the relation of the above definition of Hilbert modular forms with the definition given in Section
2.5
.

We define a congruence subgroup
\Gamma
of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)\cap G(\mathbb{A}^{\infty})G(\mathbb{A}_{\infty})_{+}
to be a subgroup which contains

\Gamma_{\mathfrak{n}}:=\{\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})\ |\ \gamma-I_{2}\in\mathfrak{n}M_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})\}

for some integral ideal
\mathfrak{n}
and such that
\Gamma/(\Gamma\cap F)
is commensurable with
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{F})/\{\pm 1\}
. For
f:\mathbb{H}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
\gamma\in G(\mathbb{A}_{\infty})
we define

(f||_{k,w}\gamma)(z):=j(\gamma,z)^{-k}\mathrm{det}(\gamma)^{w}f(\gamma(z)).

(3.3)

For
\Gamma
a congruence subgroup as above, we consider the space of modular forms
M_{k,w}(\Gamma)
to be the space of functions
f:\mathbb{H}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

f=f||_{k,w}\gamma
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

2.

f
is holomorphic with respect to each
z_{\tau}
.

If
f\in M_{k,w}(\Gamma)
, then
f
has a Fourier expansion

f(z)=\sum_{\xi}a(\xi,f)e_{F}(\xi z),

where
a(\xi,f)\in\mathbb{C}
are the Fourier coefficients and
e_{F}(\xi z)=\mathrm{exp}(2\pi i\sum_{i=1}^{d}\xi^{\tau_{i}}z_{i})
. Here
\xi
runs over all the totally positive elements of a lattice in
F
and zero.
If
f
vanishes at all the cusps of
\Gamma
, then
f
is called a cusp form and the space of such forms is denoted by
S_{k,w}(\Gamma)
. (See Section
2.5
.)

Let
h
be the class number of
F
. By the strong approximation theorem, for any finite set of places
S
, we can choose elements
t_{1},t_{2},...,t_{h}\in\mathbb{A}_{F}^{*}
such that
t_{i}^{\infty}\in\prod_{\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{O}_{F,\mathfrak{q}}
, where
\mathfrak{q}
runs over the finite primes of
F
, and
t_{1}\mathcal{O}_{F},t_{2}\mathcal{O}_{F},...,t_{h}\mathcal{O}_{F}
form a complete system of representatives of the ideal classes, and
(t_{i})_{S}=1
. Here
t\mathcal{O}_{F}
is the fractional ideal of
F
corresponding to
t\in\mathbb{A}_{F}^{*}
. We will take
S
to be a set of places which include all the infinite places of
F
and finite places which divide the level
\mathfrak{n}
.

Let

x_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}t_{i}&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},\hskip 34.1433ptx_{i}^{-\iota}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&t_{i}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}.

Let
E
be the set of totally positive units of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. We define

\Gamma_{i}(\mathfrak{n}):=x_{i}^{-\iota}EU_{1}(\mathfrak{n})G(\mathbb{A}_{\infty})_{+}x_{i}^{\iota}\cap G(\mathbb{Q})=x_{i}EV_{1}(\mathfrak{n})G(\mathbb{A}_{\infty})_{+}x_{i}^{-1}\cap G(\mathbb{Q}).

Then, we have the following bijections:

\begin{split}S_{k,w}(\mathfrak{n})&\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{h}S_{k,w}(\Gamma_{i}(\mathfrak{n})),\\
S^{*}_{k,w}(\mathfrak{n})&\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{h}S_{k,\hat{w}}(\Gamma_{i}(\mathfrak{n})).\end{split}

(3.4)

In the second bijection,
f
maps to
(f_{x_{i}})
and the inverse map is
(f_{1},f_{2},...,f_{h})\mapsto f
where
f
is given by

f(\alpha x_{i}\gamma)=(f_{i}||_{k,w}\gamma_{\infty})(z_{0})

(3.5)

for all
\alpha\in G(\mathbb{Q}),\gamma\in U_{1}(\mathfrak{n})G(\mathbb{A}_{\infty})_{+}
. Here we use the decomposition

G(\mathbb{A})=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{h}G(\mathbb{Q})x_{i}U_{1}(\mathfrak{n})G(\mathbb{A}_{\infty})_{+}.

3.3
Hecke theory, Fourier expansion and eigenfunctions

We now define the Hecke algebras for
S_{k,w}(\mathfrak{n})
and
S_{k,w}^{*}(\mathfrak{n})
.

Let
U
and
U^{\prime}
be open compact subgroups of
G(\mathbb{A}^{\infty})
. Let
x\in G(\mathbb{A}^{\infty})
. We define the operator

\begin{split}[UxU^{\prime}]:M_{k,w}(U)\rightarrow M_{k,w^{\prime}}(U^{\prime})\\
f\mapsto\sum_{i}f|_{k,w}x_{i}\end{split}

where we decompose
UxU^{\prime}=\bigsqcup Ux_{i}
.

Now, let
U=U_{1}(\mathfrak{n})
. For a prime
\mathfrak{q}
of
F
, we define

T_{\mathfrak{q}}:=\biggl[U\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&\pi_{\mathfrak{q}}\end{pmatrix}U\biggr],

where
\pi_{\mathfrak{q}}
is an element of
\mathbb{A}^{\infty}
which has 1 at every finite place, except at the place corresponding to
\mathfrak{q}
where it is a uniformizer for
\mathfrak{q}
. For
\mathfrak{a}
, a fractional ideal of
F
coprime to
\mathfrak{n}
, we define

S_{\mathfrak{a}}:=\biggl[U\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&0\\
0&\alpha\end{pmatrix}U\biggr],

where
\alpha=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}}\pi_{\mathfrak{q}}^{v_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathfrak{a})}
.

The
Hecke algebra

\mathbb{T}_{k,w}(\mathfrak{n})
is defined to be the
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra in
\mathrm{End}(S_{k,w}(\mathfrak{n}))
generated by
T_{\mathfrak{q}}
for all primes
\mathfrak{q}
of
F
and
S_{\mathfrak{a}}
for all integral ideals
\mathfrak{a}
of
F
coprime to
\mathfrak{n}
.

Lemma 1

(
Jar 97
)

There is a natural isomorphism

\begin{split}S_{k,w}(U)\cong S_{k,\hat{w}}(U^{\iota})\\
f(x)\mapsto f^{*}(x)=f(x^{-\iota}),\end{split}

where
\iota
denotes the main involution on
\text{GL}_{2}
, given by
xx^{\iota}=\mathrm{det}(x)
. We also have the relation

(f|[UxU])^{*}=f^{*}|[U^{\iota}x^{-\iota}U^{\iota}]

(3.6)

for all
x\in U
.

Lemma
1
allows us to define the Hecke algebra
\mathbb{T}_{k,w}^{*}(\mathfrak{n})
on
S_{k,w}^{*}(\mathfrak{n})
as the
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra generated by
T_{\mathfrak{q}}
and
S_{\mathfrak{a}}
in
\text{End}(S_{k,w}^{*}(\mathfrak{n}))
which is compatible with equation
3.6
.

Lastly, we define the operator
<\mathfrak{a}>
on
M_{k,w}^{*}(\mathfrak{n})
using the identity

S_{\mathfrak{a}}=N(\mathfrak{a})^{[2w-k]}<\mathfrak{a}>,

where, if

, we write
r=[r]\cdot t
.

We will now consider the notions of
q
-expansion, integrality and congruence of modular forms.

We identify the
q
-expansion of a modular form
f\in M_{k,w}^{*}(\mathfrak{n})
with the
h
Fourier expansions of the corresponding
f_{i}\in M_{k,\hat{w}}(\Gamma_{i}(\mathfrak{n}))
as in

. So, the

-expansion will have

Since
\Gamma_{i}(\mathfrak{n})
contains elements of the form
\begin{bmatrix}1&b\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}
for all
b\in t_{i}\mathcal{O}_{F}
, we see that the translation module for
\Gamma_{i}(\mathfrak{n})
is
t_{i}\mathcal{O}_{F}
, embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
via the
n
real embeddings of
F
. Thus the dual lattice for this translation module is
t_{i}^{-1}\mathfrak{d}^{-1}
embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
\mathfrak{d}
is the
different ideal
for
F
. So,
f_{i}
has a Fourier expansion of the form

f_{i}(z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi\in t_{i}^{-1}\mathfrak{d}^{-1}\\
\xi\gg 0\end{subarray}}a_{i}(\xi)e_{F}(\xi z).

(3.7)

Let
\Phi(v)
denote the subfield of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
generated by

We define

Let

4
Galois representations

Galois representations are continuous representations of the absolute Galois group of a field
K
in a vector space over
\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. In this section, we discuss basic notions related to Galois representations and explain what it means for Galois representations to be attached to Hilbert modular forms.

4.1
Preliminaries

We first give an overview of some preliminary notions related to Galois representations. Our discussion follows the exposition in Chapter 9 of
A First course in Modular forms
by Fred Diamond and Jerry Shurman
DS 05
.

4.1.1
Frobenius elements

Let
F/\mathbb{Q}
be a Galois number field. If
p\in\mathbb{Z}
is any prime, then

p\mathcal{O}_{F}=(\mathfrak{p}_{1}...\mathfrak{p}_{g})^{e},

for some prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}_{1},...,\mathfrak{p}_{g}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. The numbers
e
and
g
are called the
ramification index
and
decomposition index
respectively. The
residue fields

\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}:=\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
are all isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{p^{f}}
for some
f
, called the
inertial degree
. Also,
efg=[F:\mathbb{Q}]
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime in
\mathcal{O}_{F}
dividing
p
. The
decomposition group

D_{\mathfrak{p}}
of
\mathfrak{p}
is defined to be the subgroup of Gal
(F/\mathbb{Q})
which fixes
\mathfrak{p}
, i.e.,

D_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\{\sigma\in\text{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})\ |\ \mathfrak{p}^{\sigma}=\mathfrak{p}\}.

The decomposition group has order
ef
. (Hence, it has index equal to
g
in the Galois group). Since,
D_{\mathfrak{p}}
fixes
\mathfrak{p}
, it acts on the residue field
F_{\mathfrak{p}}
. We get a group homomorphism
D_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightarrow\text{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{p})
which is, in fact, surjective. The kernel of the map
D_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightarrow\text{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{p})
is defined to be the
inertia group
of
\mathfrak{p}
, denoted by
I_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

The order of
I_{\mathfrak{p}}
is equal to
e
. Thus,
p
is unramified if and only if
I_{\mathfrak{p}}
is trivial.

The quotient map
D_{\mathfrak{p}}/I_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightarrow\text{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{p})
is an isomorphism. We note that
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{F}_{p})
is cyclic generated by the Frobenius automorphism
\sigma_{p}
, where
\sigma_{p}:x\mapsto x^{p}
. Then, we have the following definition.

Definition 7
.

A Frobenius element
\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}
of
\mathrm{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})
is any representative in
D_{\mathfrak{p}}
whose coset in
D_{\mathfrak{p}}/I_{\mathfrak{p}}
maps to
\sigma_{p}
. In other words, a Frobenius element of
\mathrm{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})
is any element
\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})
such that

x^{\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\equiv x^{p}\ \ (\mathrm{mod}\ \ \mathfrak{p}),

for all
x\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
.

Note that, if
p
is unramified, the Frobenius element
\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, associated to
\mathfrak{p}
, is unique. Moreover, since
F
is Galois, if
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
are any two primes dividing
p
, then there is a
\sigma\in\text{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})
which sends
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
to
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
. Hence, the subgroups
D_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}
and
D_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}
are conjugate in
\text{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})
, which implies that the respective Frobenius elements
\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}
and
\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}_{2}}
are also conjugate. We have the following weak version of the Chebotarev density theorem about Frobenius elements which generalizes the theorem of Dirichlet on primes in arithmetic progression.

Theorem 4.1

(
DS 05
)

Let
F/\mathbb{Q}
be a Galois extension. Then, every element of
\mathrm{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})
takes the form
\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}
for infinitely many maximal ideals
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathcal{O}_{F}
.

Remark 1
.

Frobenius defined these Frobenius elements while investigating questions about splitting of primes in field extensions.

4.1.2
Gal(
\overline{K}/K
)

Let
K
be a field and write
\overline{K}
to denote its separable closure. We review the profinite group structure of the absolute Galois group
G_{K}:=\text{Gal}(\overline{K}/K
).

Let
I
be an indexing set with a partial order
\leq
. Suppose
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a collection of finite groups, equipped with the discrete topology, and
\phi_{ji}:G_{j}\rightarrow G_{i}
for
i\leq j
are homomorphisms satisfying
\phi_{ii}=\
identity on
G_{i}
and
\phi_{ki}\circ\phi_{jk}=\phi_{ji},\ \ \forall i\leq k\leq j
. The collection
(\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I},\{\phi_{ji}\})
is called an
inverse system of groups
. The
inverse limit

\varprojlim G_{i}
of the inverse system
(\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I},\{\phi_{ji}\})
is defined to be the topological group

\varprojlim G_{i}:=\Big\{(g_{i})_{i\in I}\in\prod_{i\in I}G_{i}\ \ |\ \ \phi_{ji}(g_{j})=g_{i}\ \ \forall i\leq j\Big\},

with underlying topology given by the relative product topology. A topological group
G
is called a
profinite group
if it is isomorphic to the inverse limit of some inverse system of groups. It is a consequence of the Tychonoff’s theorem that all profinite groups are compact.

The prototypical example of a profinite group is the group of
p
-adic integers
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. It can be shown that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is (isomorphic to) the inverse limit of
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z},
for
n\geq 1
with natural projections from
\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z}
when
m\leq n
.

Let
K/F
be a Galois extension. We form an inverse system of groups Gal
(L/F)
for finite, Galois subextensions
F\subset L\subset K
with the natural projections
\text{Gal}(L_{2}/F)\rightarrow\text{Gal}(L_{1}/F)
whenever
L_{1}\subset L_{2}
. Then,
\text{Gal}(K/F)\cong\varprojlim\text{Gal}(L_{i}/F)
. The induced topology on
\text{Gal}(K/F)
via this isomorphism is called the
Krull topology
. The
fundamental theorem of Galois theory
(for infinite extensions) states that there is an order-reversing bijection between closed subgroups
H
of
\text{Gal}(K/F)
and subextensions
F\subset L\subset K
. Under this bijection, a closed subgroup
H
corresponds to the fixed field of
H
, and a subextension
L/F
corresponds to the Galois group
\text{Gal}(K/L)
. Moreover, open normal subgroups of
\text{Gal}(K/F)
correspond, under this bijection, to finite, Galois subextensions. It is a fact that open normal subgroups form a neighborhood basis at the identity for a profinite group.

We now carry over the discussion of Frobenius elements in Section
4.1.1
to the setting of
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}
, where
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
is a fixed algebraic closure of
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
denote the ring of algebraic integers in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. For
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\mathfrak{p}
be a maximal ideal of
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
lying over
p
. Let
\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
be an algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, which we identify with the quotient
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}/\mathfrak{p}
. There is a natural reduction map
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. The
decomposition group

D_{\mathfrak{p}}
is defined as

D_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\{\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}\ |\ \mathfrak{p}^{\sigma}=\mathfrak{p}\}.

The
inertia group

I_{\mathfrak{p}}
is defined as the kernel of the group homomorphism
D_{\mathfrak{p}}\rightarrow G_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
. This map is, in fact, surjective. Any pre-image
\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}
of the Frobenius automorphism of
G_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
in
D_{\mathfrak{p}}
is called an
absolute Frobenius element
for the extension
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}
. A Frobenius element is only defined up to the inertia group. We also have that

\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}_{F}}=\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}|_{F},

where
F
is any Galois number field and
\mathfrak{p}_{F}=\mathfrak{p}\cap F
. Lastly, if
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
are two maximal ideals lying over
p
, then the corresponding absolute Frobenius elements are conjugate in
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Then, by Theorem
4.1
and the definition of Krull topology on
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2

Let
S
be a set consisting of all but finitely many primes in
\mathbb{Z}
. Then, the set of absolute Frobenius elements
\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}
corresponding to a family of maximal ideals
\mathfrak{p}
lying over the primes in
S
forms a dense subset of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

4.2
Galois representations

4.2.1
l
-adic Galois representations

To understand more about the absolute Galois group
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
, we study its representations. One of the main motivations behind considering Galois representations is that they are a bridge between analytic objects such as modular forms and geometric objects such as elliptic curves.

Definition 8
.

Let
K
be either
\mathbb{C}
or a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{l}
with the
l
-adic topology, where
l
is a prime number. A Galois representation is a continuous group homomorphism
\rho:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\text{GL}(V)
, where
V
is a finite-dimensional
K
-vector space.

When
K=\mathbb{C}
, the representation is generally called an
Artin representation
and when,
K
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{l}
, it is called an
l
-adic Galois representation
.

Examples of 1-dimensional Artin representations come from Dirichlet characters. Indeed, the composition of maps

G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{N})/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*},

where the first map is restriction, second map is the isomorphism
(m_{a}:\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{a})\mapsto a
and the third is a primitive Dirichlet character
\chi
, gives a continuous homomorphism
\rho_{\chi}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{*}
. This has finite image. More generally, any continuous homomorphism from
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
into
\mathbb{C}^{*}
has finite image and factors through
\text{Gal}(F/\mathbb{Q})
for some Galois number field contained in a cyclotomic field.

To capture the infinite structure of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
, one considers the
l
-adic cyclotomic character
. Let
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{l^{\infty}})
denote the field obtained by adjoining all the
l
-power roots of unity with
\mathbb{Q}
. Then, we have the following isomorphism

\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{l^{\infty}})/\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{l}^{*}

by taking inverse limits on both sides of the isomorphism
\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{l^{n}})/\mathbb{Q})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/l^{n}\mathbb{Z})^{*}
. By pre-composing the isomorphism with the restriction

G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\text{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{l^{\infty}})/\mathbb{Q}),

we get a continuous homomorphism
\chi_{l}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{l}^{*}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{l}^{*}
, which is called the
l
-adic cyclotomic character of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
. As stated in
(
DS 05
, eq. 9.13)
, we have

\chi_{l}(\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}})=p\text{ \ \ for\ \ }p\neq l,

and for any choice of
\mathfrak{p}
and
\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, thus showing that the image of
\chi_{l}
is infinite.

Since
\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is only defined up to inertia group, we need to impose the condition
I_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset\text{ker}(\rho)
to get a well-defined notion of values of the representation
\rho
at the Frobenius elements. This condition only depends on the underlying prime
p
and the similarity class of
\rho
because the inertia groups for different primes lying over
p
are conjugate and
\text{ker}(\rho)
is a normal subgroup. Furthermore, the characteristic polynomial of
\rho(\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}})
only depends on
p
because the characteristic polynomial is invariant under conjugation. By Theorem
4.2
, the values
\rho(\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}})
determine
\rho
completely. This discussion motivates the following definition.

Definition 9
.

Let
\rho
be a Galois representation and
p\in\mathbb{Z}
be a prime. Then
\rho
is said to be unramified at
p
if
I_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset\mathrm{ker}(\rho)
for any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
lying over
p
.

One can see that
\rho
being unramified at
p
translates to the condition that
p
is unramified in the field extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
corresponding to the closed subgroup
\text{ker}(\rho)
by Galois theory.

We remark that the Artin representation
\rho_{\chi}
which was defined using the primitive Dirichlet character
\chi
is unramified at all
p
not dividing
N
and the
l
-adic cyclotomic character
\chi_{l}
is unramified at all
p\neq l
.

4.3
L
-functions of Galois representations

In this section, we discuss the construction of
L
-functions attached to Galois representations of a number field
F
. In the case of
L
-functions attached to
Artin representations
i.e.
Artin
L
-functions
, our reference is
Algebraic Number theory
by Jürgen Neukirch
Neu 99
, and in the case of
l
-adic representations, we follow
Abelian
l
-adic representations and Elliptic curves
by J. P. Serre
Ser 89

L
-functions at the non-archimedean places.

4.3.1
Artin
L
-functions

Let
\rho:\text{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)\rightarrow\text{GL}(V)\cong\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be an Artin representation and let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
F
. Then,
\rho
factors through some finite Galois extension
K/F
. Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a prime of
K
dividing
\mathfrak{p}
and let
I_{\mathfrak{P}}
be the inertia subgroup corresponding to
\mathfrak{P}
. We can choose a Frobenius element
\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{P}}\in\text{Gal}(K/F)
. (It is uniquely defined if
\mathfrak{p}
is unramified in
K
i.e. if
I_{\mathfrak{P}}
is trivial.)

We recall that
\rho
is unramified at the prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
F
if
I_{\mathfrak{P}}\subset\text{ker}(\rho)
. In this case,
\rho(\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{P}})
is well-defined. Let
V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}
be the subspace of
V
consisting of elements fixed under the action of
I_{\mathfrak{P}}
. It coincides with
V
if
\rho
is unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
.

We let
P_{\mathfrak{p},\rho}(T)
denote the characteristic polynomial of
\rho^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}(\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{P}})\in\mathbb{C}[T]
, where
\rho^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}
is the restriction of
\rho
to the subspace
V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}
. The polynomial
P_{\mathfrak{p},\rho}(T)
is well-defined because if
\rho
is ramified at
\mathfrak{p}
, the inertia subgroup
I_{\mathfrak{P}}
acts trivially on
V^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}
, and hence
\rho^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}(\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{P}})
is well-defined. Also,
P_{\mathfrak{p},\rho}(T)
is independent of the choice of
\mathfrak{P}
dividing
\mathfrak{p}
because the different Frobenius elements corresponding to different primes
\mathfrak{P}
dividing
\mathfrak{p}
are conjugate to each other, and hence their characteristic polynomials are all the same.

Definition 10
.

The local
L
-factor corresponding to a prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
F
is defined to be

L_{\mathfrak{p}}(\rho,T):=\frac{1}{P_{\mathfrak{p},\rho}(T)}=\frac{1}{\text{det}(I-\rho^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}(\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{P}})T)}.

(4.1)

Definition 11
.

The
L
-function associated to
\rho
is defined to be the Euler product

L(\rho,s):=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}L_{\mathfrak{p}}(\rho,N(\mathfrak{p})^{-s})=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\frac{1}{\text{det}(I-\rho^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}(\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{P}})N(\mathfrak{p})^{-s})}.

(4.2)

We also note that

\text{det}(I-\rho^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}(\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{P}})N(\mathfrak{p})^{-s})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\epsilon_{i}N(\mathfrak{p})^{-s}),

(4.3)

where the
\epsilon_{i}
’s are the eigenvalues of
\rho^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}(\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{P}})
. They are roots of unity because
\rho^{I_{\mathfrak{P}}}(\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{P}})
has finite order in
\text{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. This defines
L(\rho,s)
as an analytic function in the half-plane
\text{Re}(s)>1
. The
Artin conjecture
states that
L(\rho,s)
has an analytic continuation to the whole of complex plane for any non-trivial, irreducible
\rho
.

4.3.2
L
-functions associated to
l
-adic representations

Let
F
be a number field, and let
\Sigma_{F}
denote the set of finite places of
F
. Let
\rho:\text{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)\rightarrow\text{GL}(V)
be an
l
-adic representation of
F
over a finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}_{l}
-vector space
V
. Suppose
\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma_{F}
is unramified with respect to
\rho
. As before, set

P_{\mathfrak{p},\rho}(T):=\text{det}(I-\rho(\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{P}})T).

Definition 12
.

The representation
\rho
is said to be rational (resp. integral) if there exists a finite set
S\subset\Sigma_{F}
such that

1.

any element of
\Sigma_{F}\backslash S
is unramified with respect to
\rho
, and

2.

if
\mathfrak{p}\notin S
, the coefficients of
P_{\mathfrak{p},\rho}(T)
belong to
\mathbb{Q}
(resp. to
\mathbb{Z}
).

Definition 13
.

Let
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
be
l
-adic and
l^{\prime}
-adic representations of
F
respectively. Assume
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
are rational. Then
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
are said to be
compatible
if there exists a finite
S\subset\Sigma_{K}
such that
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}
are unramified outside of
S
and
P_{\mathfrak{p},\rho}(T)=P_{\mathfrak{p},\rho^{\prime}}(T)
for
\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma_{K}\backslash S
.

Definition 14
.

For each prime
l
, let
\rho_{l}
be a rational
l
-adic representation of
F
. The system
\rho=\{\rho_{l}\}
is said to be compatible if
\rho_{l}
and
\rho_{l^{\prime}}
are compatible for any two primes
l
and
l^{\prime}
. The system
\rho=\{\rho_{l}\}
is said to be
strictly compatible
if there exists a finite
S\subset\Sigma_{K}
such that the following conditions are satisfied.

1.

Let
S_{l}:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma_{K}\ |\ \mathfrak{p}\text{ divides }l\}
. Then, for all
\mathfrak{p}\notin S\cup S_{l}
,
\rho_{l}
is unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
and
P_{\mathfrak{p},\rho_{l}}(T)
has rational coefficients.

2.

P_{\mathfrak{p},\rho_{l}}(T)=P_{\mathfrak{p},\rho_{l^{\prime}}}(T)
for all
\mathfrak{p}\notin S\cup S_{l}\cup S_{l^{\prime}}
.

The smallest such set
S
is called the
exceptional set
of the system
\rho=\{\rho_{l}\}
.

We will now define the
L
-function attached to a strictly compatible system of
l
-adic representations. Let
\rho=\{\rho_{l}\}
be a strictly compatible system of rational
l
-adic representations of a number field
F
with exceptional set
S\subset\Sigma_{F}
. For
\mathfrak{p}\notin S
, let
l
be a prime
\neq p
where
p
is the rational prime below
\mathfrak{p}
. Since
\rho_{l}
is unramified at
\mathfrak{p}
, and the polynomial
P_{\mathfrak{p},\rho_{l}}(T)
has rational coefficients and is independent of the choice of
l
, we denote it simply by
P_{\mathfrak{p},\rho}(T)
.

Definition 15
.

The
L
-function (at the finite unramified places) attached to
\rho
is defined to be the Euler product

L(\rho,s):=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\frac{1}{P_{\mathfrak{p},\rho}(N(\mathfrak{p})^{-s})}.

(4.4)

As an example, take
\chi
to be the system of
1
-dimensional
l
-adic cyclotomic characters
\chi_{l}
. Note,
\chi_{l}
is ramified outside the set
\{l\}
, and
\chi_{l}(\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}})=p
for any
l\neq p
and for any choice of
\text{Frob}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and prime
\mathfrak{p}
dividing
p
. Hence, we see that
\chi=\{\chi_{l}\}
is a strictly compatible system of
1
-dimensional integral
l
-adic representations of
\mathbb{Q}
with the exceptional set
S
as the empty set. The
L
-function attached to
\{\chi_{l}\}
is

L(\chi,s)=\prod_{p}\frac{1}{(1-p^{1-s})}=\zeta(s-1).

One can similarly generalize the above definitions to the case of
K
-rational
\lambda
-adic representations, where
K
is a number field and
\lambda
is a prime of
K
, by replacing the instances of
\mathbb{Q}
by
K
and
l
by
\lambda
.

4.4
Attaching Galois representations to Hilbert modular forms

We now state the main theorem which asserts the existence of a Galois representation associated to a Hilbert cuspidal eigenform.

Theorem 4.3

(
Jar 97
)

Let
f\in S_{k,w}^{*}(\mathfrak{n};K)
be a
K
-rational Hilbert cuspidal eigenform, where
K
is a number field containing
\Phi(v)
and satisfying the Hida condition (see definition
5
). Let
\theta_{f}(T)
denote the eigenvalue of the Hecke operator
T
acting on
f
. Let
\lambda
be a prime of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
lying above a prime
l\in\mathbb{Z}
and let
\mathcal{O}_{K,\lambda}
denote the completion of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
at the prime
\lambda
. Then there exists a continuous representation

\rho_{\lambda}:\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{K,\lambda})

which is unramified outside
\mathfrak{n}l
such that if
\mathfrak{q}
is a prime of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
not dividing
\mathfrak{n}l
, then

\mathrm{tr\ }\rho_{\lambda}(\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{q}})=\theta_{f}(T_{\mathfrak{q}})

(4.5)

and

\mathrm{det\ }\rho_{\lambda}(\mathrm{Frob}_{\mathfrak{q}})=\theta_{f}(S_{\mathfrak{q}})N(\mathfrak{q}).

(4.6)

Note that Equation
4.5
and Equation
4.6
imply that
\rho=\{\rho_{\lambda}\}
is a strictly compatible system of
K
-rational Galois representations with exceptional set contained in
\{\mathfrak{p}\in\Sigma_{F}\mid\mathfrak{p}\text{ divides }\mathfrak{n}\}
.

The theorem for
d=1
, i.e.
F=\mathbb{Q}
, was proved by Deligne
Del 71
and Deligne-Serre
DS 74
. Carayol
Car 86
proved it for odd
d
whenever the weights are all at least 2, and Taylor
Tay 89
and Blasius-Rogwaski
BR 89
proved it for even
d
and all weights at least 2. When all the weights are equal to one, it was proved by Rogawski-Tunnell
RT 83
and Ohta
Oht 84
. The last remaining case when some of the weights are equal to one was proved by Jarvis
Jar 97
.

The method used by Taylor
Tay 89
and Jarvis
Jar 97
is that of congruences. Taylor constructs congruences between the given form and a form of different suitable level, and then uses Wiles’ method of ‘pseudo-representations’
Wil 88
to construct the desired representations. Jarvis constructs congruences between forms of partial weight one and forms with higher weight thereby reducing the problem to the case where the theorem holds.

Characterisations for the depletion of reactant in a one-dimensional dynamic combustion model
Siran Li, Jianing Yang

Abstract
. In this paper, a novel observation is made on a one-dimensional compressible Navier–Stokes model for the dynamic combustion of a reacting mixture of γ \gamma -law gases ( γ > 1 \gamma>1 ) with discontinuous Arrhenius reaction rate function, on both bounded and unbounded domains. We show that the mass fraction of the reactant (denoted as Z Z ) satisfies a weighted gradient estimate Z y / Z ∈ L t ∞ ​ L y 2 Z_{y}/\penalty\sqrt{Z}\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{y} , provided that at time zero the density is Lipschitz continuous and bounded strictly away from zero and infinity. Consequently, the graph of Z Z cannot form cusps or corners near the points where the reactant in the combustion process is completely depleted at any instant, and the entropy of Z Z is bounded from above. The key ingredient of the proof is a new estimate based on the Fisher information, first exploited by [ 7 , 2 ] with applications to PDEs in chemorepulsion and thermoelasticity. Along the way, we also establish a Lipschitz estimate for the density.

As is often the case for 1D compressible fluid dynamics, the Lagrangian formulation of Eq. (

0.4.
Main theorem and consequences

This note aims at providing a novel estimate for the mass fraction of reactant,
i.e.
, the function
Z
in Eq. (
0.1
) or Eq. (
0.7
). As a consequence, we show that
Z
cannot be merely Hölder or Lipschitz near its nodal set (
a.k.a.
zero locus)
Z^{-1}{\{0\}}
at any instant, so the reactant cannot be depleted abruptly. This, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, is among the very first refined characterisations of solutions to the 1D dynamic combustion model — Eqs. (
0.7
)
\&
(
0.4
) — apart from those obtained via more conventional
L^{2}
-based energy estimates.

Our main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 0.3
.

Let
Z
be the mass fraction of the reactant in a generalised solution to the 1D dynamic combustion model Eqs. (
0.7
)
\&
(
0.4
) in
[0,T]\times\Omega
, where
T>0
and
\Omega=[0,1]
or
\mathbb{R}
. Suppose the initial data satisfy, in addition to the conditions in Eq. (
0.4
), that

(v_{0}(y),u_{0}(y),\theta_{0}(y),Z_{0}(y))\in\left[H^{1}(\Omega)\right]^{4}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{0}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega).

Then we have the estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\{\int_{\Omega}\frac{\left|Z_{y}\right|^{2}}{Z}\,{\rm d}y\right\}\leq\Lambda<\infty,

where
\Lambda
is a uniform constant as in Notation
0.4
below.

Notation 0.4
.

We reserve the symbol
\Lambda
for positive constants depending only on the physical parameters
a
,
q
,
m_{0}
,
M_{0}
,
D
,
K
,
\mu
, and
\nu
; the lifespan

; the

-norm of the discontinuous reaction rate function
\phi
; as well as the natural norm of the initial data

\left\|\big(v_{0},u_{0},\theta_{0},Z_{0}\big)\right\|_{[H^{1}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)]\times[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{3}}.

It may change from line to line.

By Definition

Z\in L^{\infty}\big(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)\big)

Remark 0.5

By virtue of the Aubin–Lions lemma,

Under the above provision, we readily deduce the following from Theorem

1.2.
Uniform bounds on

For the proof of Theorem

The following result shows that the Lipschitzness of

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
T>0
and
\Omega=[0,1]
. Suppose that the initial data
(v_{0},u_{0},\theta_{0},Z_{0})
satisfy the conditions in Theorem

as in Notation
0.4
such that

\displaystyle\left\|v_{y}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}\leq\Lambda.

Proposition
1.1
and Theorem
1.5
together imply that

v\in L^{\infty}\big(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\big).

The essential tool needed for the proof, which has already appeared in
[
3
]
, is an integral representation formula for
v
(Eq. (
1.4
) below) discovered by Kazhikhov
[
18
]
. We shall only sketch the arguments here, with emphasis on the dependency of constants. Special care is taken to show that the Lipschitz estimate of
v
requires only the Lipschitz bounds for the initial specific volume
v_{0}
, but
not
for
u_{0}
,
Z_{0}
,
\theta_{0}
; the
H^{1}
-bounds for the later three variables would suffice, as in Definition
0.2
of the generalised solutions.

Meanwhile, to avoid undesired complications that may blur the main ideas of the proof, we focus on the case of bounded domain
\Omega=[0,1]
. Theorem
1.5
for

is also valid, but we postpone its proof to §
4
towards the end of the paper.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We first introduce some notations, most of which are taken from
[
3
]
. Denote by
\alpha_{0}
,
\beta_{0}
the roots
y_{0}
of the transcendental equation

\displaystyle y_{0}-1-\log y_{0}={\mathcal{E}}_{1},

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{1}:=\frac{1}{\min\left\{1,a\right\}}\left\{{\mathcal{E}}_{0}+q\int_{0}^{1}Z_{0}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right\}.

Clearly
\alpha_{0}
,
\beta_{0}
are positive numbers (as
q>0
for combustion reaction).

Throughout this proof, we consider the
regularised
system as in §
1.1
, with
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
(see Eq. (
1.1
)) in lieu of
\phi
. For notational convenience, we shall drop the superscipt
ε
in the remaining parts of the proof, namely that

(v_{0},u_{0},\theta_{0},Z_{0})\equiv(v^{\varepsilon}_{0},u^{\varepsilon}_{0},\theta^{\varepsilon}_{0},Z^{\varepsilon}_{0})\quad\text{ and }\quad(v,u,\theta,Z)\equiv(v^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon},\theta^{\varepsilon},Z^{\varepsilon}).

The process for passage of limits is the same as in Chen
[
3
]
, so all we need to complete the proof is to obtain an
L^{\infty}
-bound for
v_{y}

uniform in
\varepsilon
and independent of higher-order norms
. See the following paragraph for details.

Under this provision, we may infer from Notation
0.4
that the constants
\Lambda
are allowed to depend only on the physical parameters
a
,
q
,
D
,
K
,
\mu
, and
\nu
; the constants
m_{0}
,
M_{0}
,
\alpha_{0}
,
\beta_{0}
,
{\mathcal{E}}_{0}
,
\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
T
; as well as the natural norms
\left\|\big(v_{0},u_{0},\theta_{0},Z_{0}\big)\right\|_{[H^{1}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)]\times[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{3}}
of the initial data. It, in particular, is independent of the regularisation parameter
\varepsilon
and the Hölder norms
\|v_{0}\|_{1+\sigma}
,
\|(u_{0},\theta_{0},Z_{0})\|_{2+\sigma}
. As a consequence,
\Lambda
is persistent under the limit
\varepsilon\searrow 0
passing from the classical solution to the regularised system to the generalised solution to the original system (
0.4
)
\&
(
0.7
). Especially, the remark ensuing Proposition
1.3
explains why the dependence on
\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}
works here.

Note that the Lemma
1.4
gives us that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}a(v-1-\log v)\,\mathrm{d}y\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{0}+q\int_{0}^{1}Z_{0}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(\theta-1-\log\theta)\,\mathrm{d}y\leq{\mathcal{E}}_{0}+q\int_{0}^{1}Z_{0}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Thus, there exists at least one point
y_{\star}(t)\in[0,1]
such that

\alpha_{0}\leq v\big(t,y_{\star}(t)\big),\,\theta\big(t,y_{\star}(t)\big)\leq\beta_{0}.

In what follows, we shall adapt the arguments in Kazhikhov
[
18
]
to derive an explicit representation formula for
v=v(t,y)
. Let us rewrite the first equality in Eq. (
0.7
) as

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\log v(t,y)=\frac{u_{y}(t,y)}{v(t,y)}

and integrate it over time. We thus obtain

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\left[\log v(t,y)-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{a\theta(t,y)}{\mu v(t,y)}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right]=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\log v_{0}(y)+\frac{1}{\mu}[u(t,y)-u_{0}(y)].

Integrating the above equality from
y_{\star}(t)
to
y
and taking exponential, we deduce that

(1.4)

A(t)v(t,y)=\frac{1+\frac{a}{\mu}\int_{0}^{t}\theta(\tau,y)A(\tau)B(\tau,y)\,\mathrm{d}\tau}{B(t,y)},

where

A(t)=v_{0}\big(y_{\star}(t)\big)\exp\left\{\frac{a}{\mu}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\theta}{v}\big(\tau,y_{\star}(t)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right\}

and

B(t,y)=\frac{1}{v_{0}(y)v\big(t,y_{\star}(t)\big)}\exp\left\{\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{y_{\star}(t)}^{y}[u_{0}(\xi)-u(t,\xi)]\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right\}.

Note that for any
t
, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives us

\left|\int_{y_{\star}(t)}^{y}u(t,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right|\leq\|u(t,\bullet)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sqrt{2{\mathcal{E}}_{1}}.

This together with Eq. (
1.2
) implies

(1.5)

0<\Lambda^{-1}\leq B(t,y)\leq\Lambda<\infty\qquad\text{for all }(t,y)\in Q_{T},

where
\Lambda
is the constant in Notation
0.4
. Meanwhile, integrating Eq. (
1.4
) over the spatial variable
y\in\Omega=(0,1)
and using the first identity in Lemma
1.4
, we arrive at the integral inequality

0<\Lambda^{-1}\leq A(t)\leq\Lambda+\Lambda\int_{0}^{t}A(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Hence, Grönwall’s inequality implies
A(t)\leq\Lambda.
The constant
\Lambda
may depend on the lifespan
T
.

We may now deduce from the representation formula (
1.4
) an explicit expression for
v_{y}(t,y)
. Indeed, by chain rule and the fundamental theorem of calculus, we compute that

(1.6)

\displaystyle v_{y}(t,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\frac{a}{\mu}\int_{0}^{t}\theta(\tau,y)A(\tau)B(\tau,y)\,\mathrm{d}\tau}{A(t)}\left[-\frac{B_{y}(t,y)}{B^{2}(t,y)}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{A(t)B(t,y)}{\int_{0}^{t}\frac{a}{\mu}A(\tau)\big[\theta_{y}(\tau,y)B(\tau,y)+\theta(\tau,y)B_{y}(\tau,y)\big]\,\mathrm{d}\tau},

and

B_{y}(t,y)=B(t,y)\left[-\frac{v_{0y}(y)}{v_{0}(y)}+\frac{1}{\mu}\big(u_{0}(y)-u(t,y)\big)\right].

Here
v_{0y}=\frac{\partial v_{0}}{\partial y}
. Thus, by the
L^{\infty}
-bound for
u
(see Proposition
1.3
), the assumptions on the initial data in the statement of Theorem
1.5
(in particular, the strictly positive lower bound for
v_{0}
and its Lipschitz upper bound), and estimate (
1.5
) above, we deduce that

(1.7)

|B_{y}(t,y)|\leq\Lambda\qquad\text{for all }(t,y)\in Q_{T}.

It is crucial that the above expression for
B_{y}
does not involve any derivative of
u
or
u_{0}
.

Also observe the following simple bound: for any
t\in[0,T]
and
y\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{y}(\tau,y)\mathrm{d}\tau\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{y}\theta_{yy}(\tau,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\tau\right|

(1.8)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\theta_{yy}(\tau,\bullet)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\leq\Lambda.

We used here the fundamental theorem of calculus, the boundary condition for
\theta
at
y=0
, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, as well as Proposition
1.3
.

Therefore, putting together the bounds in Eqs. (
1.5
), (
1.7
),
\&
(
1.2
) and substituting them into Eq. (
1.6
), we readily conclude that

|v_{y}(t,y)|\leq\Lambda\qquad\text{for all }(t,y)\in Q_{T}.

This completes the proof. ∎

Note that the Lipschitzness of
v
is equivalent to that of
\rho=v^{-1}
. This is because

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v(t,y_{1})}-\frac{1}{v(t,y_{2})}=\frac{v(t,y_{2})-v(t,y_{1})}{v(t,y_{1})v(t,y_{2})}\qquad\text{for any $t\in[0,T]$ and $y_{1},y_{2}\in\Omega$},

and
v
is strictly bounded below by a positive constant (see Proposition
1.3
).

2.
An inequality based on Fisher information

Now let us introduce the key inequality in this work. It was first discovered in the recent preprint
[
7
]
by Cieślak–Fuest–Hajduk–Sierżȩga, which relates the Hessian of the square-root of a positive function to the dissipation of Fisher information along heat flow.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
-\infty\leq a<b\leq\infty
, and let
\Psi
be a positive
C^{2}
-function on
]a,b[
such that
\Psi_{x}(a)=\Psi_{x}(b)=0
. Then it holds that

\int_{a}^{b}\left[(\Psi^{1/2})_{xx}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{13}{8}\int_{a}^{b}\Psi\left[(\log\Psi)_{xx}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

When
a=-\infty
, the condition
\Psi_{x}(a)=0
is understood as
\lim_{y\searrow-\infty}\Psi_{x}(y)=0
; similarly for the case
b=\infty
. For convenience of the readers, we shall sketch a proof below. We essentially follow
[
7
]
and do not claim any originality for our arguments. In
[
7
]
the inequality is established only for finite
a
and
b
, but the same proof carries over to the general case without much alterations. Of course, if either
a
or
b
is infinite, the inequality is effective only when
\int_{a}^{b}\Psi\left[(\log\Psi)_{xx}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty
.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

For
0<\Psi\in C^{2}(]a,b[)
, one has

\displaystyle\left[(\Psi^{1/2})_{xx}\right]^{2}=\left(\frac{\Psi_{xx}}{2\Psi^{1/2}}-\frac{\Psi_{x}^{2}}{4\Psi^{3/2}}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\Psi_{xx}}{\Psi^{1/2}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Psi_{x}^{2}}{\Psi^{3/2}}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\Psi\left[(\log\Psi)_{xx}\right]^{2}=\Psi\left(\frac{\Psi_{xx}}{\Psi}-\frac{\Psi_{x}^{2}}{\Psi^{2}}\right)^{2}=\left(\frac{\Psi_{xx}}{\Psi^{1/2}}-\frac{\Psi_{x}^{2}}{\Psi^{3/2}}\right)^{2}.

Completing the square and using the inequality
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left[(\Psi^{1/2})_{xx}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\Psi_{xx}}{\Psi^{1/2}}-\frac{\Psi_{x}^{2}}{\Psi^{3/2}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Psi_{x}^{2}}{\Psi^{3/2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\left[2\left(\frac{\Psi_{xx}}{\Psi^{1/2}}-\frac{\Psi_{x}^{2}}{\Psi^{3/2}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\Psi_{x}^{2}}{\Psi^{3/2}}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Psi\left[(\log\Psi)_{xx}\right]^{2}+\frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{\Psi_{x}^{2}}{\Psi^{3/2}}\right)^{2}.

To conclude, one invokes the following inequality attribute in
[
7
]
to Winkler
[
39
]
. In full generality, it reads:

Lemma

(Bernis-type inequality)
.

Let
h\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be a positive function, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a smooth Euclidean domain. Set
\Theta(s):=\int_{1}^{s}\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{h(\sigma)}
for
s>0
. Then, for any positive function
\varphi\in C^{2}\big(\bar{\Omega}\big)
with
\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\nu}=0
on
\partial\Omega
, it holds that

\int_{\Omega}\frac{h^{\prime}(\varphi)}{h^{3}(\varphi)}\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{4}\,{\rm d}x\leq(2+\sqrt{N})^{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{h(\varphi)}{h^{\prime}(\varphi)}\left|D^{2}\Theta(\varphi)\right|^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

Applying the above inequality to
\Psi=\varphi
,
h={\rm Id}
,
N=1
, and integrating the previous pointwise inequality over space, we arrive at

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left[(\Psi^{1/2})_{xx}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{13}{8}\int_{0}^{1}\Psi\left[(\log\Psi)_{xx}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

which is what we want to prove. ∎

In passing, we note that the reverse inequality is immediate:

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\Psi\left[(\log\Psi)_{xx}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 4\int_{a}^{b}\left[(\Psi^{1/2})_{xx}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

In fact, one has the pointwise identity

\displaystyle\left(\Psi^{1/2}\right)_{xx}-\frac{1}{2}\Psi^{1/2}\left(\log\Psi\right)_{xx}=\frac{1}{4}\frac{\left(\Psi_{x}\right)^{2}}{\Psi^{3/2}}

for
\Psi
as in Lemma
2.1
.

3.
Weighted gradient estimate for reactant fraction on bounded domain

In this section, we prove Theorem
0.3
for
\Omega=[0,1]
.

Proof of Theorem
0.3
for bounded domain.

As in the proof for Theorem
1.5
, let us consider the regularised system —
i.e.
, with
\Phi\equiv\Phi_{\varepsilon}
in place of
\phi
— and we systematically drop the superscripts
ε
in our variables.

It suffices to seek a uniform-in-
\varepsilon
bound for
\frac{Z_{y}}{\sqrt{Z}}
in
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{y}
by
\Lambda
(see Notation
0.4
). Then the theorem follows from a standard passage of limit process, which can be directly adapted from
[
3
]
. To avoid potential dangers of dividing by zero, we introduce the new variable

(3.1)

X:=Z+\delta\equiv Z^{\epsilon}+\delta\qquad\text{ for }\varepsilon,\delta>0,

and look for uniform estimates independent of both the regularisation parameter
\varepsilon
and the “shift” parameter
\delta
. Say
\delta<100^{-1}
.

Note that
X
satisfies the PDE

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &X_{t}+K\Phi(\theta)X-K\Phi(\theta)\delta=\left(\frac{D}{v^{2}}X_{y}\right)_{y}\qquad\text{in }Q_{T},\\
&X_{0}(y)=Z_{0}(y)+\delta\qquad\text{at }t=0,\\
&X_{y}=0\qquad\text{on }\partial\Omega=\{0,1\}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
X_{0}
is the initial data
X|_{t=0}
. Dividing
X^{1/2}
on both sides of Eq. (
3.2
), we obtain the evolution equation for
X^{1/2}
:

2\frac{\partial}{\partial t}X^{1/2}+K\Phi(\theta)X^{1/2}-\frac{K\Phi(\theta)\delta}{X^{1/2}}=\frac{(\frac{D}{v^{2}}X_{y})_{y}}{X^{1/2}}.

As
X
is strictly positive and has the same regularity as
Z
, the above PDE is understood in the classical sense.

We deduce by integrating over
\Omega=[0,1]
that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{X_{y}^{2}}{X}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad=4\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{0}^{1}(X^{1/2})_{y}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad=8\int_{0}^{1}(X^{1/2})_{y}(X^{1/2})_{ty}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad=4\int_{0}^{1}(X^{1/2})_{y}\left[\left(\frac{D}{v^{2}}X_{y}\right)_{y}\frac{1}{X^{1/2}}-K\Phi(\theta)X^{1/2}\right]_{y}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad=4\int_{0}^{1}(X^{1/2})_{y}\left[2\frac{D}{v^{2}}(X^{1/2})_{yy}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{D}{v^{2}}\frac{X_{y}^{2}}{X^{3/2}}+\left(\frac{D}{v^{2}}\right)_{y}\frac{X_{y}}{X^{1/2}}+K\Phi(\theta)\left(\frac{\delta}{X^{1/2}}-X^{1/2}\right)\right]_{y}\,\mathrm{d}y.

The penultimate equality follows from the PDE for
X^{1/2}
.

Let us write

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{X_{y}^{2}}{X}\,\mathrm{d}y=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}

where

\displaystyle I_{1}:=4\int_{0}^{1}(X^{1/2})_{y}\left[2\frac{D}{v^{2}}(X^{1/2})_{yy}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{D}{v^{2}}\frac{X_{y}^{2}}{X^{3/2}}\right]_{y}\,\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle I_{2}:=4\int_{0}^{1}(X^{1/2})_{y}\left[\left(\frac{D}{v^{2}}\right)_{y}\frac{X_{y}}{X^{1/2}}\right]_{y}\,\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle I_{3}:=4\int_{0}^{1}(X^{1/2})_{y}\left[K\Phi(\theta)\left(\frac{\delta}{X^{1/2}}-X^{1/2}\right)\right]_{y}\,\mathrm{d}y.

In what follows, we shall repetitively argue by integration by parts, employing the boundary conditions
X_{y}(t,0)=X_{y}(t,1)=0
as in Eq. (
0.4
).

For
I_{1}
, we compute that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=-8\int_{0}^{1}\left[(X^{1/2})_{yy}\right]^{2}\frac{D}{v^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}y+8\int_{0}^{1}(X^{1/2})_{y}\left(\frac{D}{v^{2}}\right)_{y}\frac{(X^{1/2})_{y}^{2}}{X^{1/2}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\qquad+8\int_{0}^{1}(X^{1/2})_{y}\frac{D}{v^{2}}\left[\frac{2(X^{1/2})_{y}(X^{1/2})_{yy}}{X^{1/2}}-\frac{(X^{1/2})_{y}^{3}}{X}\right]\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=-8\int_{0}^{1}X\frac{D}{v^{2}}\left[(\log X^{1/2})_{yy}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y+8\int_{0}^{1}(X^{1/2})_{y}\left(\frac{D}{v^{2}}\right)_{y}\frac{(X^{1/2})_{y}^{2}}{X^{1/2}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=-2\int_{0}^{1}X\frac{D}{v^{2}}\left[(\log X)_{yy}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{D}{v^{2}}\right)_{y}\frac{\left(X_{y}\right)^{3}}{X^{2}}\,{\rm d}y.

Hence,

(3.4)

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{-2\int_{0}^{1}\frac{D}{v^{2}}X\left[(\log X)_{yy}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y}_{=:\,[{\rm GOOD}]}\underbrace{-2\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{D}{v^{2}}\right)_{y}X_{y}(\log X)_{yy}\,\mathrm{d}y}_{=:\,[{\rm BAD}]}.

The
[{\rm GOOD}]
term is of favourable sign. For the
[{\rm BAD}]
term, one may resort to Young’s inequality to deduce, for arbitrarily small
\eta>0
, that

\displaystyle\big|[{\rm BAD}]\big|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{D^{1/2}X^{1/2}}{v}\left[\log(X^{1/2})\right]_{yy}\cdot\frac{X_{y}}{X^{1/2}}\frac{(D/v^{2})_{y}}{D^{1/2}/\penalty v}\,{\rm d}y\right|

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq\eta\int_{0}^{1}\frac{D}{v^{2}}X\left[\left(\log X\right)_{yy}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y+C_{\eta}D\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(X_{y})^{2}}{X}\frac{(v_{y})^{2}}{v^{4}}\,{\rm d}y.

Here
C_{\eta}\sim\eta^{-1}
is a positive constant. For the second term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
3
), we have
v\geq m_{0}>0
by Proposition
1.3
and
|v_{y}|\leq\Lambda
by Theorem
1.5
, both uniformly in spacetime. Thus, recalling Notation
0.4
, one may continue Eq. (
3
) by

(3.6)

\big|[{\rm BAD}]\big|\leq\eta\int_{0}^{1}\frac{D}{v^{2}}X\left[\left(\log X\right)_{yy}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y+C_{\eta}\Lambda\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(X_{y})^{2}}{X}\,{\rm d}y.

Now let us turn to
I_{3}
. Integration by parts yields that

\displaystyle I_{3}=4\int_{0}^{1}(X^{1/2})_{yy}K\Phi(\theta)\left[X^{1/2}-\frac{\delta}{X^{1/2}}\right]\,\mathrm{d}y.

Thus, for any
\eta^{\prime}>0
we have that

\displaystyle\left|I_{3}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\eta^{\prime}\int_{0}^{1}\left[(X^{1/2})_{yy}\right]^{2}\,{\rm d}y+C_{\eta^{\prime}}K^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi^{2}(\theta)\left[X^{1/2}-\frac{\delta}{X^{1/2}}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{13\eta^{\prime}}{8}\int_{0}^{1}X\left[\left(\log X\right)_{yy}\right]^{2}\,{\rm d}y+C_{\eta^{\prime}}\Lambda\int_{0}^{1}\left[X^{1/2}-\frac{\delta}{X^{1/2}}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{13\eta^{\prime}(M_{0})^{2}}{8D}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{D}{v^{2}}X\left[\left(\log X\right)_{yy}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y+C_{\eta^{\prime}}\Lambda\int_{0}^{1}\left[X^{1/2}-\frac{\delta}{X^{1/2}}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y,

where
C_{\eta^{\prime}}\sim(\eta^{\prime})^{-1}
. In the above, we used the Young’s inequality, Lemma
2.1
, and the upper bounds for
v
and
\theta
in Proposition
1.3
. Recall Notation
0.4
for the constant
\Lambda
.

To proceed, simply notice that since
0\leq Z\leq 1
(see Proposition
1.3
;
Z\equiv Z^{\varepsilon}
is the
C^{1}
-regularised variable as before), one has
\delta\leq X\leq 1+\delta
. This implies that
0\leq 1-\frac{\delta}{X}\leq\frac{1}{1+\delta}<1
, and hence we have a bound uniform in
\delta
:

\displaystyle 0\leq\left[X^{1/2}-\frac{\delta}{X^{1/2}}\right]^{2}\leq{1+\delta}\leq 1.01

whenever
\delta\in\left[0,100^{-1}\right]
. Therefore, choosing
\eta^{\prime}
via

\displaystyle\eta=\frac{13\eta^{\prime}(M_{0})^{2}}{8D}

where
\eta
is as in Eq. (
3.6
), we arrive at the bound

(3.7)

\displaystyle|I_{3}|\leq\eta\int_{0}^{1}\frac{D}{v^{2}}X\left[\left(\log X\right)_{yy}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y+C_{\eta}\Lambda.

Here
\eta>0
is arbitrary; the constants
C_{\eta}\sim\eta^{-1}
and
\Lambda
are both independent of
\delta
and
\varepsilon
.

Now, substituting the estimates (
3.4
), (
3.6
), and (
3.7
) into Eq. (
3.3
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{X_{y}^{2}}{X}\,\mathrm{d}y+2(1-\eta)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{D}{v^{2}}X\left[(\log X)_{yy}\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq C_{\eta}\Lambda\left\{1+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{X_{y}^{2}}{X}\,\mathrm{d}y\right\}.

Fix any
0<\eta<1
and hence
C_{\eta}
. Since
\Lambda
is independent of both the “shift” and regularisation parameters
\delta
and
\varepsilon
, we can now conclude the proof —
i.e.
, recover the
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{y}
-bound for
\frac{Z_{y}}{\sqrt{Z}}
with
Z
non-regularised — from Grönwall’s lemma. ∎

4.
Gradient estimate for reactant fraction on unbounded domains

In this section we prove Theorem
0.3
for the unbounded domain case. For this purpose, we need the analogue of Theorem
1.5
, namely the uniform Lipschitz bound for
v
, on
\Omega=\mathbb{R}
. To avoid unnecessary repetitions with the bounded domain case, the parallel arguments are safely omitted. We shall only highlight the essential differences from the developments in §
3
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Theorem
1.5
remains valid when
\Omega=\mathbb{R}
. That is, for any given
T>0
, we have
\left\|v_{y}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{T})}\leq\Lambda
. The constant
\Lambda
is as in Notation
0.4
.

The proof is based on arguments by Jiang in
[
15
]
, which relies on the localisation lemma
à la
Kazhikhov
[
19
]
. See also Li
[
22
]
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Throughout the proof we denote
I_{k}:=[k,k+1]
for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Consider the cutoff function
\chi=\chi_{k}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
defined by

\chi(y)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1\qquad\text{for }y\leq k,\\
&k+1-y\qquad\text{for }k\leq y\leq k+1,\\
&0\qquad\text{for }y\geq k+1.\end{aligned}\right.

Multiply the equation

u_{t}+\left(\frac{a\theta}{v}\right)_{y}=\left(\frac{\mu u_{y}}{v}\right)_{y}

by
\chi
to obtain that

\chi(y)u_{t}(t,y)=\left[\left(\frac{\mu u_{y}}{v}-\frac{a\theta}{v}\right)(t,y)\chi(y)\right]_{y}-\chi^{\prime}(y)\left(\frac{\mu u_{y}}{v}-\frac{a\theta}{v}\right)(t,y).

Then, integrating the above equality over
[0,t]\times]y,\infty[
and taking exponential, we deduce that

\displaystyle v(t,y)\exp\left\{-\frac{a}{\mu}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\theta}{v}(\tau,y)\mathrm{d}\tau\right\}=v_{0}(y)P(t)Q(t,y),

where

P(t):=\exp\left\{\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{k}^{k+1}\left(\frac{\mu u_{y}}{v}-\frac{a\theta}{v}\right)(\tau,\xi)\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}\xi\right\},

Q(t,y):=v_{0}(y)\exp\left\{\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{y}^{\infty}[u_{0}(\xi)-u(t,\xi)]\chi(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi\right\}.

One may infer the following from the above identity:

\displaystyle v(t,y)=Q(t,y)P(t)+\frac{a}{\mu}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{Q(t,y)P(t)}{Q(\tau,y)P(\tau)}\theta(\tau,y)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\qquad\text{for any }y\in I_{k},\,t\geq 0.

Moreover, the bounds below are established by Jiang
[
15
]
:

(4.1)

0\leq P(t)\leq\Lambda\exp\left\{-t/\Lambda\right\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{P(t)}{P(\tau)}\leq\Lambda\exp\left\{-(t-\tau)/\Lambda\right\}.

Now, straightforward computation yields that

\displaystyle v_{y}(t,y)

\displaystyle=Q_{y}(t,y)P(t)+\frac{a}{\mu}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{P(t)}{P(\tau)}\frac{Q_{y}(t,y)\theta(\tau,y)+Q(t,y)\theta_{y}(\tau,y)}{Q(\tau,y)}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

(4.2)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{a}{\mu}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{P(t)}{P(\tau)}\frac{Q(t,y)\theta(\tau,y)Q_{y}(\tau,y)}{Q^{2}(\tau,y)}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

where

\displaystyle Q_{y}(t,y)

\displaystyle=v_{0y}(y)\exp\left\{\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{y}^{\infty}[u_{0}(\xi)-u(t,\xi)]\chi(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad+v_{0}(y)\left[\frac{1}{\mu}[u(t,y)-u_{0}(y)](k+1-y)\right]\exp\left\{\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{y}^{\infty}[u_{0}(\xi)-u(t,\xi)]\chi(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right\}.

The above expressions are well defined, for it is assumed that
v_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, as well as that
u(t,\bullet)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
for every
t\in[0,T]
.

We are ready to conclude. Indeed, Eq. (
4.1
) gives uniform bounds on
P(t)
and
P(t)/\penalty P(\tau)
. Also, in view of the expressions for
Q
and
Q_{y}
above, the
L^{\infty}_{t,x}
-bound for
(u,\theta)
and the
L^{2}_{t}H^{2}_{x}
-bound (hence the
L^{2}_{t}C^{0,1/2}_{x}
-bound by Sobolev–Morrey embedding) for
\theta
— proved in Proposition
1.3
— as well as the assumptions on
v_{0}
, also imply that
|Q_{y}|\leq\Lambda
and
0<\Lambda^{-1}\leq Q\leq\Lambda<\infty
uniformly in spacetime. The constants
\Lambda
follow the same rule of Notation
0.4
. Most importantly, all these above bounds are uniform in
k
. Substituting these bounds into the representation formula (
4
) for
v_{y}
, we complete the proof.
∎

Sketch of proof of Theorem
0.3
for unbounded domain.

The arguments in §
3
almost carry over verbatim, with the understanding of the boundary condition for
Z
as
\lim\limits_{|y|\rightarrow\infty}Z_{y}(t,y)=0
. Indeed, consider
X=Z^{\varepsilon}+\delta
as in §
3
. The following holds for the case
\Omega=\mathbb{R}
in place of Eq. (
3.3
):

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{X_{y}^{2}}{X}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=8\left(\sqrt{X}\right)_{yy}\left(\sqrt{X}\right)_{y}\frac{D}{v^{2}}\bigg|_{-\infty}^{\infty}-4\left(\sqrt{X}\right)_{y}K\Phi(\theta)\sqrt{X}\bigg|_{-\infty}^{\infty}+8\left(\sqrt{X}\right)_{y}^{2}\left(\frac{D}{v^{2}}\right)_{y}\bigg|_{-\infty}^{\infty}

\displaystyle-2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}X\frac{D}{v^{2}}\left[\left(\log X\right)_{yy}\right]^{2}\mathrm{d}y-2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{D}{v^{2}}\right)_{y}X_{y}\left(\log X\right)_{yy}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+4\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\sqrt{X}\right)_{yy}K\Phi(\theta)\sqrt{X}\,\mathrm{d}y.

This identity holds pointwise in
t
. By virtue of the boundary conditions, the first three terms on the right-hand side are equal to zero. The control for the remaining terms is exactly the same as for the case
\Omega=[0,1]
, which relies essentially on Theorems
4.1
and Lemma
1.3
. ∎

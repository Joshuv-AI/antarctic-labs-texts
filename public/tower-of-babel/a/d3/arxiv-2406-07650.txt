1.
Introduction

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
z_{0}\in\Omega
. For
u\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, we say that
u

vanishes to order
N
in the
L^{p}
sense at
z_{0}
if

(1.1)

\lim_{r\to 0}r^{-N}\int_{B(z_{0},r)}|u|^{p}=0.

We say that
u

vanishes to infinite order in the
L^{p}
sense at
z_{0}
if (
1.1
) holds for all
N
.

The following notation will be used throughout the paper.

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1,\quad\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{q},\quad 1\leq p\leq 2.

The purpose of this paper is to establish a strong unique continuation property (SUCP) for the differential inequality
|\overline{\partial}u|\leq V|u|
, where the potential is locally in
L^{q}
for suitable
q
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a connected open subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. Let
2(2n)^{2}-1\leq q\leq\infty
and set
p=\frac{2q}{q+1}
,
p^{\prime}=\frac{2q}{q-1}
so that
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{q}
(in particular
p=2
when
q=\infty
). Then the differential inequality
|\overline{\partial}u|\leq V|u|
has the following strong unique continuation property:

(1)

Let
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
for
m\geq 1
, and let
|\overline{\partial}u|\leq V|u|
with
V\in L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, for some
2(2n)^{2}-1\leq q<\infty
.
If
u\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,p}(\Omega)
where
p=\frac{2q}{q+1}\in(1,2)
, and
u
vanishes to infinite order in the
L^{p^{\prime}}
sense at some
z_{0}\in\Omega
, then
u
is
0
in
\Omega
.

(2)

Let
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
and let
|\overline{\partial}u|\leq V|u|
with
V\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
. If
u\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,2}(\Omega)
, and
u
vanishes to infinite order in the
L^{2}
sense at some
z_{0}\in\Omega
, then
u
is
0
in
\Omega
.

Our proof for part (i) and (ii) are very different. Part (i) is proved using the Carleman inequality method, whereas part (ii) relies on a theorem of Gong and Rosay
[
GR07
]
, which states that for a continuous function
f:\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
that is not identically zero, if
|\overline{\partial}f|\leq C|f|
for some positive constant
C
, then the zero set of
f
is a complex analytic set and thus has real dimension at most
2n-2
. For (i) we can prove the SUCP for any complex vector-valued functions, due to the fact that Carleman inequality works equally well for scalar-valued and vector-valued functions. For (ii) however, we can only prove for
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions, since there is currently no vector-valued analog for the theorem of Gong and Rosay. We note that part (ii) is not new and actually contained in a more general result proved recently by Pan and Zhang
[
PZ24
, Theorem 5.1]
, where they assume that
V
takes the form
\frac{C}{|z|}
, however we decide to include our proof here since it is slightly simpler (due to our stronger assumption
V\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
).

When the source domain is 1-dimensional (i.e.
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
), Pan and Zhang
[
PZ23
]
proved that SUCP holds for
|\overline{\partial}u|\leq V|u|
with
V\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, for functions
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
with
u\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
. The
L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}
condition is optimal, as SUCP fails if
V\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for any
p<2
, by the example
u=e^{-|x|^{-\varepsilon}}
with suitably chosen
\varepsilon
. In fact, the same example also shows that for
u:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
with
n\geq 2
, SUCP fails if
V\in L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}
for any
q<2n
.

Inequalities involving the
\overline{\partial}
operator arise naturally in complex analysis and geometry. In their study of boundary uniqueness of holomorphic mappings between smooth hypersurfaces, Bell and Lempert
[
BL90
]
showed that given
u:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
, and
u
satisfies
|\overline{\partial}u|\leq C|u|
for some
C>0
, if
u
vanishes to infinite order (all its derivatives vanish) at
0
, then
u
vanish identically in a neighborhood of
0
.
For applications of unique continuation of
\overline{\partial}
inequalities in
J
holomorphic curves and other related problems in almost complex analysis, the reader may refer to
[
Ros10
]
.

For our proof we follow the method of Wolff
[
Wol90
]
, who considered a similar SUCP problem for the differential inequality
|\Delta u|\leq V|\nabla u|
. We started out by constructing a kernel
L_{\nu}(z,\zeta)
which is a
(n,n-1)
form on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
, so that

|z|^{-\nu}u(z)=\int L_{\nu}(z,\zeta)\wedge|\zeta|^{-\nu}\overline{\partial}u(\zeta),\quad u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}).

We then osculate the weight
|z|^{-\nu}=e^{\nu\log\frac{1}{|z|}}
to
e^{\nu\psi(\log\frac{1}{|z|})}
, where
\psi
is a strictly convex function, and obtain the following

e^{\nu\psi(\sigma)}u(z)=\int P_{\nu}(z,\zeta)e^{\nu\psi(\tau)}\wedge\overline{\partial}u(\zeta),\quad\sigma=\log\frac{1}{|z|},\quad\tau=\log\frac{1}{|\zeta|}.

This allows us to localize in the radial variable to intervals of logarithmic length
\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and obtain a Carleman inequality of the form:

(1.2)

\|e^{\nu\psi(\sigma)}u\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(A(\psi^{-1}\gamma))}\leq C_{n}\nu\min\{|\gamma|,\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}\|e^{\nu\psi(\sigma)}\overline{\partial}u\|_{L^{p}(B(0,1))}.

Here
\gamma\subset\mathbb{R}
is an interval in the radial variable and
A(\psi^{-1}\gamma)
is the annulus
\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\sigma(z)=\log(1/|z|)\in\psi^{-1}(\gamma)\}
.
Note that for fixed
\gamma
, the quantity
\nu\min\{|\gamma|,\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}
blows up as
\nu\to\infty
, and hence (
1.2
) is not considered a true Carleman inequality and one cannot apply the standard argument to derive unique continuation property directly from (
1.2
). Instead, we use the following key insight of Wolff: for each
\nu
, there exists a collection of disjoint radial intervals
\gamma_{j}
such that
\left|e^{\nu\psi(\sigma)}V|u|\right|^{p}
is concentrated on
A^{-1}(\gamma_{j})
for each
j
, while
\gamma_{j}
-s are
“collectively small" on the scale of
\nu^{-1}
. One can then apply (
1.2
) on each such annulus and take the sum to get a true Carleman inequality with a constant that does not blow up with
\nu
, from which unique continuation easily follows.

Since
\overline{\partial}
is a first-order differential operator, and in view of the counterexample above, it is natural to conjecture that
L^{2n}
(or perhaps
L^{2n+\varepsilon}
) is the optimal condition on
V
for SUCP to hold (See also Wolff
[
Wol93
]
for a discussion of related unique continuation problems for second-order elliptic operators.) We list this as an open problem.

.

Let
\Omega
be a connected open subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}

. Set

and

so that
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2n}
. Suppose
u\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and satisfies
|\overline{\partial}u|\leq V|u|
, with

. If
u
vanishes to infinite order in the
L^{p^{\prime}}
sense at some point

, does
u
vanish identically?

Another question is whether Theorem
1.2
(ii) still holds for vector-valued functions.

Question 1.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a connected open subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and let
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
, for
m\geq 2
. Suppose
u\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
and satisfies
|\overline{\partial}u|\leq V|u|
, with
V\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
. If
u
vanishes to infinite order in the
L^{2}
sense at some point
z_{0}\in\Omega
, does
u
vanish identically?

We remark that if
u
is smooth, then the infinite order vanishing of
u
at
z_{0}
means that
|u(z)|\leq C|z-z_{0}|^{N}
for any
N>0
, or equivalently, all the derivatives of
u
vanish at
z_{0}
.
In the recent preprint
[
PZ24
]
, Pan and Zhang showed that if
u:\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
with
u\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
,
|\overline{\partial}u|\leq V|u|
for some

V\in L^{2n}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, and
u
vanishes to infinite order at
z_{0}
, then
u
is
0
. Furthermore, they proved that the same result holds for vector (
\mathbb{C}^{m}
) valued smooth functions if
V\in L^{2n+\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
. In the proof of both these results, the smoothness assumption of
u
is used to extend the flatness of
u
into its restrictions along complex one-dimensional radial directions, so then one only needs to solve a 1-dimensional SUCP problem.

We now fix some notations used in the paper.
We denote by
C^{\infty}_{c}(D)
the space of
C^{\infty}
functions with compact support in
D
.
We write
x\lesssim y
to mean that
x\leq Cy
for some constant
C
independent of
x
and
y
, and we write
x\approx y
if
x\lesssim y
and
y\lesssim x
. By
D^{l}
we mean a differential operator of order
l
:
D^{l}_{z}g(z)=\partial{}_{z_{i}}^{\alpha_{i}}\partial{}_{\overline{z}_{j}}^{\beta_{j}}g(z)
,
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j}\beta_{j}=l
.

2.
Taylor expansion of the fundamental solution

For
z,\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
\zeta\neq 0
, denote

r=\frac{|z|}{|\zeta|},\quad\theta=\theta_{z\zeta}=\angle_{z0\zeta}\in[0,\pi].

For a fixed
\zeta
, we denote by
T^{N}_{z}f(z,\zeta)
the degree
N
Taylor polynomial of
f(\cdot,\zeta)
at
z=0
. Similarly, for fixed
\theta
, we let
T^{N}_{r}g(r,\theta)
be the degree
N
Taylor polynomial of
g(\cdot,\theta)
at
r=0
. We write
d=2n
, and for all the computations we will assume
n\geq 2
and
d\geq 3
.

First we need a fact about the Taylor expansion of the Newtonian potential. The result is used in
[
Wol90
]
and we shall provide the details.

Proposition 2.1
.

Fix
\zeta\neq 0
. We have the following Taylor expansion

\frac{1}{|z-\zeta|^{d-2}}=\frac{1}{|\zeta|^{d-2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}P^{(\frac{d-2}{2})}_{m}(\cos\theta)r^{m},

where
P_{m}^{(\frac{d-2}{2})}
is an ultraspherical polynomial of degree
m
such that
|P^{(\frac{d-2}{2})}_{m}(\cos\theta)|\leq m^{d-3}
.

Proof.

Denote
\lambda=\frac{d-2}{2}
. We have

\frac{1}{|z-\zeta|^{d-2}}=\frac{1}{(|z-\zeta|^{2})^{\lambda}}=\frac{1}{\left(|z|^{2}-2|z||\zeta|\cos\theta+|\zeta|^{2}\right)^{\lambda}}=\frac{1}{|\zeta|^{d-2}\left(1-2r\cos\theta+r^{2}\right)^{\lambda}}.

Expand
(1-2\tau\mu+\mu^{2})^{-\lambda}
as a power series of
r
at
r=0
to get

(2.1)

(1-2\tau\mu+\mu^{2})^{-\lambda}=P^{(\lambda)}_{0}(\cos\theta)+P^{(\lambda)}_{1}(\cos\theta)r+P^{(\lambda)}_{2}(\cos\theta)r^{2}+\cdots.

On the other hand we can write

(1-2\tau\mu+\mu^{2})^{-\lambda}=(1-e^{i\theta}r)^{-\lambda}(1-e^{-i\theta}r)^{-\lambda}

Expanding
1-e^{i\theta}r
and
1-e^{i\theta}r
in binomial series, we get

\displaystyle(1-e^{i\theta}r)^{\lambda}=\alpha^{(\lambda)}_{0}+\alpha^{(\lambda)}_{1}e^{i\theta}r+\alpha^{(\lambda)}_{2}e^{2i\theta}r^{2}+\cdots,\quad(1-e^{-i\theta}r)^{\lambda}=\alpha^{(\lambda)}_{0}+\alpha^{(\lambda)}_{1}e^{-i\theta}r+\alpha^{(\lambda)}_{2}e^{-2i\theta}r^{2}+\cdots.

Hence

(2.2)

(1-e^{i\theta}r)^{\lambda}=\alpha^{2}+(2\alpha_{0}\alpha_{1}\cos\theta)r+(2\alpha_{0}\alpha_{2}\cos 2\theta+\alpha_{1}^{2})r^{2}+(2\alpha_{0}\alpha_{3}\cos 3\theta+2\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\cos\theta)r^{3}+\cdots.

Comparing the coefficients in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we get
the following formula for
P^{(\lambda)}_{k}
:

P^{(\lambda)}_{k}=2\alpha_{0}\alpha_{k}\cos k\theta+2\alpha_{1}\alpha_{k-1}\cos(k-2)\theta+2\alpha_{2}\alpha_{k-2}\cos(k-4)\theta+\cdots,

the last term being
\alpha^{2}_{\frac{k}{2}}
if
k
is even, and
2\alpha_{\frac{k-1}{2}}\alpha_{\frac{k+1}{2}}\cos\theta
if
k
is odd. Now the binomial coefficients
\alpha^{(\lambda)}_{k}
are given by (cf.
[
Sze75
, p. 93]
)

\alpha^{(\lambda)}_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}k+\lambda-1\\
k\end{pmatrix},\quad k=0,1,2,\dots.

If
\lambda>0
(which is true in our case since
\lambda=\frac{d-2}{2}>0
when
d\geq 3
), then
\alpha^{(\lambda)}_{k}
are all positive. Now
P^{(\lambda)}_{k}
is a trig polynomial in
\cos\theta
(i.e. a linear combination of
\cos\theta,\cos 2\theta,\dots)
with positive coefficients, thus it must achieve its maximum at
\cos 0=1
:

|P_{k}^{\lambda}(\cos\theta)|\leq P_{k}^{\lambda}(1)=\begin{pmatrix}k+2\lambda-1\\
k\end{pmatrix}\approx k^{2\lambda-1}=k^{d-3},

where we use the formula for
P_{k}^{\lambda}(1)
from
[
Sze75
, p. 80]
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

For each non-negative integer
N
, there exists a
(n,n-1)
form
I^{N}(z,\zeta)
such that

|z|^{-N}u(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}I^{N}(z,\zeta)|\zeta|^{-N+d-1}\wedge\overline{\partial}u(\zeta),\quad u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}),

where
I^{N}(z,\zeta)=c_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta)\widehat{d\overline{\zeta}_{j}}
and

(2.3)

I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta)=c_{d}h_{j1}(z,\zeta)r^{-N}\left(g(r,\theta)-T_{r}^{N-1}[g](r,\theta)\right)+c_{d}h_{j2}(z)r^{-(N-1)}\left(g(r,\theta)-T_{r}^{N-2}[g](r,\theta)\right).

The functions
g,h_{j1},h_{j2}
are given by

\displaystyle g(r,\theta)=\left[(r-e^{i\theta})(r-e^{-i\theta})\right]^{-\frac{d}{2}};

\displaystyle h_{j1}(z,\zeta)=-\frac{1}{|\zeta|}\left(\overline{\zeta}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\overline{z}_{j}}{|z|^{2}}\left(z\cdot\overline{\zeta}+\overline{z}\cdot\zeta\right)\right)-\frac{|z|^{2}+|\zeta|^{2}-|z-\zeta|^{2}}{2|z||\zeta|}\cdot\frac{\overline{z}_{j}}{2|z|};\quad h_{j2}(z)=\frac{\overline{z}_{j}}{2|z|}.

In particular, the
h_{j1}
and
h_{j2}
are homogeneous in
(z,\zeta)
of degree
0
.

Proof.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
. We can write

u(z)=\int_{\Omega}B(z,\zeta)\wedge\overline{\partial}u(\zeta),

where
B(z,\zeta)
is the Bochner-Martinelli formula given by

B(z,\zeta)=c_{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}B_{j}(z,\zeta)\widehat{d\overline{\zeta}_{j}},\quad c_{n}=\frac{(n-1)!}{(2\pi i)^{n}},\quad B_{j}(z,\zeta)=\frac{\overline{\zeta_{j}-z_{j}}}{|\zeta-z|^{2n}}

for
(z,\zeta)\in(\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n})\setminus\{\zeta=z\}
.
Here
\widehat{d\overline{\zeta}_{j}}:=d\zeta_{j}\wedge\left(\bigwedge_{k\neq j}d\overline{\zeta}_{k}\wedge d\zeta_{k}\right)
.

Given a non-negative integer
N
and a fixed
\zeta\neq 0
, let
P_{j}^{N}(\cdot,\zeta)
be the degree
N
Taylor polynomial of
B_{j}(z,\zeta)
at
z=0
, and let

P^{N}(z,\zeta)=c_{2n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}P_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta)\widehat{d\overline{\zeta}_{j}}
. We have
\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}B(z,\zeta)=0
for
\zeta\neq z
, or

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial{}_{\overline{\zeta}_{j}}B_{j}(z,\zeta)=0,\quad\zeta\neq z.

Since

D^{\beta}_{\zeta}(D_{z}^{\alpha}B_{j}(0,\zeta))=(D^{\beta}_{\zeta}D_{z}^{\alpha}B_{j})(0,\zeta)=(D_{z}^{\alpha}D^{\beta}_{\zeta}B_{j})(0,\zeta),

we have for
\zeta\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial{}_{\overline{\zeta}_{j}}P_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial{}_{\overline{\zeta}_{j}}\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N}\frac{D^{\alpha}_{z}B_{j}(0,\zeta)}{\alpha!}z^{\alpha}\overline{z}^{\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq N}\left(\left[D_{z}^{\alpha}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial{}_{\overline{\zeta}_{j}}B_{j}(z,\zeta)\right](0,\zeta)\right)z^{\alpha}\overline{z}^{\beta}=0,

i.e.
\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}P^{N}(z,\zeta)=0
, for any
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\zeta\in\operatorname{supp}u
. Using Stokes theorem, we can write

u(z)=\int_{\Omega}\left[B(z,\zeta)-P_{N-1}(z,\zeta)\right]\wedge\overline{\partial}u(\zeta)=c_{2n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}\left[B_{j}(z,\zeta)-P_{j}^{N-1}(z,\zeta)\right]\widehat{d\overline{\zeta}_{j}}\wedge\overline{\partial}u(\zeta).

Define

I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta)=\left(\frac{|\zeta|}{|z|}\right)^{N}|\zeta|^{{d-1}}\left(B_{j}(z,\zeta)-P_{j}^{N-1}(z,\zeta)\right),

and
I^{N}(z,\zeta)=c_{d}\sum_{j=1}^{n}I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta)\widehat{d\overline{\zeta}_{j}}
.
Then we can write

|z|^{-N}u(z)=\int_{\Omega}I^{N}(z,\zeta)|\zeta|^{-(N+d-1)}\wedge\overline{\partial}u(\zeta).

We now write
B_{j}=\frac{\overline{\zeta_{j}-z_{j}}}{|\zeta-z|^{d}}=c_{d}\partial{}_{z_{j}}\left(\frac{1}{|\zeta-z|^{d-2}}\right)
, for
c_{d}=\frac{2-d}{2}
and

|\zeta|^{d-2}B_{j}(z,\zeta)=c_{d}\partial{}_{z_{j}}\left(\frac{|\zeta|^{d-2}}{|\zeta-z|^{d-2}}\right)=c_{d}\partial{}_{z_{j}}\left(\frac{|\zeta-z|}{|\zeta|}\right)^{-(d-2)}.

Since
|\zeta|^{d-2}P_{j}^{N-1}(\cdot,\zeta)
is the degree
N-1
Taylor polynomial of
|\zeta|^{d-2}B_{j}(\cdot,\zeta)
at
z=0
, by the previous line we have

|\zeta|^{d-2}P_{j}^{N-1}=c_{d}\partial{}_{z_{j}}T^{N}_{z}\left[\left(\frac{|\zeta-z|}{|\zeta|}\right)^{-(d-2)}\right].

Now

\left(\frac{|\zeta-z|}{|\zeta|}\right)^{-(d-2)}=\left(\frac{|\zeta-z|^{2}}{|\zeta|^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}=\left[(r-e^{i\theta})(r-e^{-i\theta})\right]^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}:=f(r,\theta).

Combining the above expression, we get

(2.4)

\displaystyle I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta)

\displaystyle=r^{-N}|\zeta|\left[|\zeta|^{d-2}B_{j}(z,\zeta)-|\zeta|^{d-2}P_{j}^{N-1}(z,\zeta)\right]

\displaystyle=c_{d}r^{-N}|\zeta|\left[\partial{}_{z_{j}}\left(f(r,\theta)-T^{N}_{r}f(r,\theta)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=c_{d}r^{-N}|\zeta|\left[(\partial{}_{z_{j}}r)\partial{}_{r}\left(f(r,\theta)-T^{N}_{r}f(r,\theta)\right)+(\partial{}_{z_{j}}\theta)\partial{}_{\theta}\left(f(r,\theta)-T^{N}_{r}f(r,\theta)\right)\right].

Here
T^{N}_{r}f(r,\theta)
denotes the degree
N
Taylor polynomial of
f(\cdot,\theta)
at
r=0
.

Now we have

\displaystyle\partial{}_{r}f(r,\theta)

\displaystyle=\partial{}_{r}\left[(r-e^{i\theta})(r-e^{-i\theta})\right]^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{d-2}{2}\left[(r-e^{i\theta})(r-e^{-i\theta})\right]^{-\frac{d}{2}}\cdot\partial{}_{r}\left(r^{2}-2r\cos\theta+1\right)

\displaystyle=(2-d)(r-\cos\theta)g(r,\theta),

where we denote

g(r,\theta)=\left[(r-e^{i\theta})(r-e^{-i\theta})\right]^{-\frac{d}{2}}.

Similarly, we get

\displaystyle\partial{}_{\theta}f(r,\theta)=(2-d)(r\sin\theta)g(r,\theta).

The corresponding Taylor expansions are

\displaystyle\partial{}_{r}T^{N}_{r}f(r,\theta)

\displaystyle=T^{N-1}_{r}\partial{}_{r}f(r,\theta)

\displaystyle=(2-d)T^{N-1}_{r}\left[(r-\cos\theta)g(r,\theta)\right]

\displaystyle=(2-d)\left[rT^{N-2}_{r}g(r,\theta)-\cos\theta\cdot T^{N-1}_{r}g(r,\theta)\right],

and

\displaystyle\partial{}_{\theta}T^{N}_{r}f(r,\theta)

\displaystyle=T^{N}_{r}[\partial{}_{\theta}f](r,\theta)

\displaystyle=T^{N}_{r}[(2-d)(r\sin\theta)g(r,\theta)](r,\theta)

\displaystyle=(2-d)(r\sin\theta)\cdot T^{N-1}_{r}[g](r,\theta).

Plug the expressions into (
2.4
) to get

(2.5)

\displaystyle I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta)

\displaystyle=c_{d}r^{-N}|\zeta|\left[(\partial{}_{z_{j}}r)\left(\partial{}_{r}f(r,\theta)-\partial{}_{r}T^{N}_{r}f(r,\theta)\right)+(\partial{}_{z_{j}}\theta)\left(\partial{}_{\theta}f(r,\theta)-\partial{}_{\theta}T^{N}_{r}f(r,\theta)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=(2-d)r^{-N}|\zeta|(\partial{}_{z_{j}}r)\left\{r[g(r,\theta)-T^{N-2}_{r}[g](r,\theta)]-\cos\theta\cdot[g(r,\theta)-T^{N-1}_{r}[g](r,\theta)]\right\}

\displaystyle+(2-d)r^{-N}|\zeta|(\partial{}_{z_{j}}\theta)\left\{(r\sin\theta)\left(g(r,\theta)-T^{N-1}_{r}[g](r,\theta)\right)\right\}.

Next we compute
\partial{}_{z_{j}}r
and
\partial{}_{z_{j}}\theta
. We have

\displaystyle\partial{}_{z_{j}}r=\partial{}_{z_{j}}\left(\frac{|z|}{|\zeta|}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\overline{z}_{j}}{|\zeta||z|}.

To compute
\partial{}_{z_{j}}\theta
, we use the law of cosine

|z-\zeta|^{2}=|z|^{2}+|\zeta|^{2}-2|z||\zeta|\cos\theta.

Hence

\cos\theta=\frac{|z|^{2}+|\zeta|^{2}-|z-\zeta|^{2}}{2|z||\zeta|}=\frac{z\cdot\overline{\zeta}+\zeta\cdot\overline{z}}{2|z||\zeta|},

where we used
|z-\zeta|^{2}=|z|^{2}+|\zeta|^{2}-(z\cdot\overline{\zeta}+\zeta\cdot\overline{z})
. Take
\partial{}_{z_{j}}
on both sides of the first equality above to get

\displaystyle(-\sin\theta)\partial{}_{z_{j}}\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial{}_{z_{j}}\left(\frac{|z|}{|\zeta|}+\frac{|\zeta|}{|z|}-\frac{|z-\zeta|^{2}}{|z||\zeta|}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\partial{}_{z_{j}}\left(r+\frac{1}{r}-\frac{|z-\zeta|^{2}}{|z||\zeta|}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial{}_{z_{j}}\left(r+\frac{1}{r}-r-\frac{1}{r}+\frac{z\cdot\overline{\zeta}+\zeta\cdot\overline{z}}{|z||\zeta|}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\partial{}_{z_{j}}\left(\frac{z\cdot\overline{\zeta}+\zeta\cdot\overline{z}}{|z||\zeta|}\right).

Further computation gives

\displaystyle(r\sin\theta)(\partial{}_{z_{j}}\theta)=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{|z|}{|\zeta|}\left(\frac{\overline{\zeta}_{j}}{|z||\zeta|}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\overline{z}_{j}}{|z|^{3}|\zeta|}\left(z\cdot\overline{\zeta}+\overline{z}\cdot\zeta\right)\right).

The last expression is in
O(|\zeta|^{-1})
as
\zeta\to 0
.
In view of (
2.5
) and the above computations, we can write

\displaystyle I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta)

\displaystyle=c_{d}h_{j1}(z,\zeta)r^{-N}\left(g(r,\theta)-T^{N-1}_{r}[g](r,\theta)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+c_{d}h_{j2}(z,\zeta)r^{-(N-1)}\left(g(r,\theta)-T^{N-2}_{r}[g](r,\theta)\right)

where

\displaystyle h_{j1}(z,\zeta)

\displaystyle:=|\zeta|\left[r\sin\theta(\partial{}_{z_{j}}\theta)-\cos\theta(\partial{}_{z_{j}}r)\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{|\zeta|}\left(\overline{\zeta}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\overline{z}_{j}}{|z|^{2}}\left(z\cdot\overline{\zeta}+\overline{z}\cdot\zeta\right)\right)-\frac{z\cdot\overline{\zeta}+\zeta\cdot\overline{z}}{2|z||\zeta|}\cdot\frac{\overline{z}_{j}}{2|z|};

and

\displaystyle h_{j2}(z,\zeta):=|\zeta|\partial{}_{z_{j}}r=\frac{1}{2}|\zeta|\frac{\overline{z_{j}}}{|\zeta||z|}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\overline{z}_{j}}{|z|}.\qed

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
z,\zeta\neq 0
. There exists constant
C_{d,k}>0
such that

(1)

\left|D_{z}^{k}\left[I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta)-|\zeta|^{d-1}\left(\frac{|\zeta|}{|z|}\right)^{N}B_{j}(z,\zeta)\right]\right|\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-1+k}|\zeta|^{-k},\quad\text{when $|z-\zeta|<\frac{|\zeta|}{2N}$}.

(2)

\left|D_{z}^{k}I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta)\right|\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-2+k}\min\{N,|1-r|^{-1}\}|z|^{-k},\quad\text{when $|z-\zeta|>\frac{|\zeta|}{2N}$}.

Proof.

(i) We first reduce the proof to the case
|\zeta|=1
.
Denote
\widetilde{\zeta}=\lambda\zeta
,
\widetilde{z}=\lambda z
, with
\lambda=|\zeta|^{-1}
. Then
|\widetilde{\zeta}|=1
. Suppose the estimate holds for
\widetilde{\zeta}
and
\widetilde{z}
:

\left|D_{\widetilde{z}}^{k}\left[I_{j}^{N}(\widetilde{z},\widetilde{\zeta})-|\widetilde{\zeta}|^{d-1}\left(|\widetilde{\zeta}||\widetilde{z}|^{-1}\right)^{N}B_{j}(\widetilde{z},\widetilde{\zeta})\right]\right|\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-1+k},\quad|\widetilde{z}-\widetilde{\zeta}|<\frac{|\widetilde{\zeta}|}{2N}.

The left-hand side is equal to

\displaystyle\left|D_{\widetilde{z}}^{k}\left[|\widetilde{\zeta}|^{d-1}\left(|\widetilde{\zeta}||\widetilde{z}|^{-1}\right)^{N}P_{j}^{N-1}(\widetilde{z},\widetilde{\zeta})\right]\right|

\displaystyle=\lambda^{d-1-k}\lambda^{-(d-1)}\ \left|D_{z}^{k}\left[|\zeta|^{d}\left(|\zeta||z|^{-1}\right)^{N}P_{j}^{N-1}(z,\zeta)\right]\right|

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-k}\left|D_{z}^{k}\left[|\zeta|^{d}\left(|\zeta||z|^{-1}\right)^{N-1}P_{j}^{N-1}(z,\zeta)\right]\right|.

Thus

\left|D_{z}^{k}\left[I_{j}^{N-1}(\widetilde{z},\widetilde{\zeta})-|\widetilde{\zeta}|^{d}\left(|\widetilde{\zeta}||\widetilde{z}|^{-1}\right)^{N-1}B_{j}(\widetilde{z},\widetilde{\zeta})\right]\right|\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-1+k}\lambda^{k}=N^{d-1+k}|\zeta|^{-k},

which proves the reduction.

We show that for
|\zeta_{0}|=1
,

\left|D_{z}^{k}\left[I_{j}^{N}-|z|^{-N}B_{j}(z,\zeta_{0})\right]\right|\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-1+k},\quad\text{when $|z-\zeta_{0}|<\frac{1}{2N}$}.

Write

(2.6)

\left|D_{z}^{k}\left[|z|^{N}\left(I_{j}^{N}-|z|^{-N}B_{j}(z,\zeta_{0})\right)\right]\right|=|D_{z}^{k}P_{j}^{N-1}(z,\zeta_{0})|.

By Proposition
2.1
, we can expand the Newtonian potential using zonal harmonics:

\frac{1}{|\zeta_{0}-z|^{d-2}}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}Z_{l}(z),\quad|z|<1,

where
Z_{l}
satisfies the estimates
|Z_{l}(z)|\lesssim l^{d-3}|z|^{l}
. Since
P_{j}^{N-1}(\zeta_{0},\cdot)
is the degree
N-1
Taylor polynomial of
B_{j}(\zeta_{0},\cdot)=c_{d}\partial{}_{z_{j}}\frac{1}{|\zeta_{0}-z|^{d-2}}
at
z=0
, we can write
P_{j}^{N-1}(\zeta_{0},\cdot)
as

P_{j}^{N-1}(\zeta_{0},\cdot)=T^{N-1}\left(\partial{}_{z_{j}}\frac{1}{|\zeta_{0}-z|^{d-2}}\right)=\partial{}_{z_{j}}T^{N}\left(\frac{1}{|\zeta_{0}-z|^{d-2}}\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{N}\partial{}_{z_{j}}Z_{m}(z).

Since each
Z_{m}
is harmonic, we get (cf.
[
HL11
, Proprosition 1.13]

|D^{k}Z_{m}(z)|\leq\frac{C_{d,k}}{r^{k}}\sup_{B_{r}(z)}|Z_{m}|,\quad C_{d,k}=e^{k}e^{k-1}k!.

Choose
r=|z|/m\neq 0
. Then
|D^{k}Z_{m}|\leq C_{d,k}m^{d-3+k}|z|^{m-k}
. Thus

\left|D_{z}^{k}P_{j}^{N-1}(z,\zeta_{0})\right|\leq\sum_{m=1}^{N}|D_{z}^{k+1}Z_{m}(z)|\leq C_{d,k}\sum_{m=1}^{N}m^{d-2+k}|z|^{m-k-1}\leq N^{k}|z|^{-k}\sum_{m=1}^{N}m^{d-2}|z|^{m-1}.

In view of (
2.6
) and the product rule, we have

(2.7)

\left|D_{z}^{k}\left[I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta_{0})-|z|^{-N}B_{j}(z,\zeta_{0})\right]\right|\leq C_{d,k}N^{k}|z|^{-k-N}\sum_{m=1}^{N}m^{d-2}|z|^{m-1}.

For
|z-\zeta_{0}|<\frac{1}{2N}
, we have
|z|^{-k-N+m-1}\leq C_{d,k}
. Hence

(2.8)

\left|D_{z}^{k}\left[I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta_{0})-|z|^{-N}B_{j}(z,\zeta_{0})\right]\right|\leq C_{d,k}N^{k}\sum_{m=1}^{N}m^{d-2}\leq N^{d-1+k}.

The proof of (i) is complete.

Next we prove (ii). Again we first show that the proof can be reduced to the case
|\zeta|=1
. Suppose the estimate holds for
\widetilde{\zeta}=\lambda\zeta,\widetilde{z}=\lambda z
, for
\lambda=|\zeta|^{-1}
. For
|z-\zeta|\geq\frac{|\zeta|}{2N}
, we have
|\widetilde{z}-\widetilde{\zeta}|\geq\frac{|\widetilde{\zeta}|}{2N}=\frac{1}{2N}
, and

(2.9)

\left|D_{\widetilde{z}}^{k}I_{j}^{N}(\widetilde{z},\widetilde{\zeta})\right|\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-2+k}\min\{N,|1-r|^{-1}\}|\widetilde{z}|^{-k}.

We have

\displaystyle I_{j}^{N}(\widetilde{z},\widetilde{\zeta})

\displaystyle=|\widetilde{\zeta}|^{d-1}\left(\frac{|\widetilde{\zeta}|}{|\widetilde{z}|}\right)^{N}\left(B_{j}(\widetilde{z},\widetilde{\zeta})-P_{j}^{N-1}(\widetilde{z},\widetilde{\zeta})\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{(d-1)-(d-1)}|\zeta|^{d-1}\left(\frac{|\zeta|}{|z|}\right)^{N}\left(B_{j}(z,\zeta)-P_{j}^{N-1}(z,\zeta)\right)

\displaystyle=I_{j}^{N}(\zeta,z).

Thus

|D_{\widetilde{z}}^{k}I_{j}^{N}(\widetilde{z},\widetilde{\zeta})|=\lambda^{-k}|D_{z}^{k}I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta)|.

Together with (
2.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle|D_{z}^{k}I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{d,k}\lambda^{k}N^{d-2+k}\min\{N,|1-r|^{-1}\}\lambda^{-k}|z|^{-k}

\displaystyle=C_{d,k}N^{d-2+k}\min\{N,|1-r|^{-1}\}|z|^{-k},

which proves the reduction.

We now fix
\zeta_{0}
with
|\zeta_{0}|=1
and show that

|D_{z}^{k}I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta_{0})|\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-2+k}\min\{N,|1-r|^{-1}\}|z|^{-k},\quad r=|z|,\quad|z-\zeta_{0}|>\frac{1}{2N}.

If
|z-\zeta_{0}|>\frac{1}{2N}
and
|z|\in(1-\frac{1}{N},1+\frac{1}{N})
, (i.e.
|1-|z||^{-1}>N
), the same argument as above shows that (
2.8
) holds:

(2.10)

\left|D_{z}^{k}\left[I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta_{0})-|z|^{-N}B_{j}(z,\zeta_{0})\right]\right|\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-1+k}\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-1+k}|z|^{-k}.

The last inequality holds since
|z|\lesssim 1
.
Suppose
|z-\zeta_{0}|>\frac{1}{2N}
and
|z|>1-\frac{1}{N}
. We have

\displaystyle\left|D_{z}^{k}\left[|z|^{-N}B_{j}(z,\zeta_{0})\right]\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k}\nabla^{k_{1}}_{z}\left(|z|^{-N}\right)D_{z}^{k_{2}}\left(\frac{\overline{(\zeta_{0})_{j}-z_{j}}}{|z-\zeta_{0}|^{d}}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{d,k}N^{k_{1}}|z|^{-N-k_{1}}|z-\zeta_{0}|^{-(d-1)-k_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d,k}N^{k_{1}}|z|^{-k_{1}}|z-\zeta_{0}|^{-(d-1)-k_{2}},

where in the last step we used
|z|>1-\frac{1}{N}
and thus
|z|^{-N}\leq(1-\frac{1}{N})^{-N}
.
By the condition
|z-\zeta_{0}|>\frac{1}{2N}
, we have

|z-\zeta_{0}|^{-(d-1)-k_{2}}\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-1+k_{2}}|z|^{-k_{2}}.

One can check the above inequality by considering the two cases:
|z|\geq 2|\zeta_{0}|=2
and
|z|\leq 2
.
Therefore
\left|D_{z}^{k}\left[|z|^{-N}B_{j}(z,\zeta_{0})\right]\right|\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-1+k}|z|^{-k}
.

On the other hand, since
|z-\zeta_{0}|\geq\left|1-|z|\right|
, by similar reasoning as above,

|z-\zeta_{0}|^{-(d-1)-k_{2}}\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-2+k_{2}}|1-|z||^{-1}|z|^{-k_{2}},

which implies that
\left|D_{z}^{k}\left[|z|^{-N}B_{j}(z,\zeta_{0})\right]\right|\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-2+k}|1-|z||^{-1}|z|^{-k}
. Hence

(2.11)

\left|D_{z}^{k}\left[|z|^{-N}B_{j}(z,\zeta_{0})\right]\right|\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-2+k}\min\{N,|1-|z||^{-1}\}|z|^{-k}

when
|z-\zeta_{0}|>\frac{1}{2N}
and
|z|>1-\frac{1}{N}
.

Combining (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), and using the fact that
N<|1-|z||^{-1}
for
|z|\in(1-\frac{1}{N},1+\frac{1}{N})
, we have

(2.12)

|D_{z}^{k}I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta_{0})|\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-2+k}\min\{N,|1-|z||^{-1}\}|z|^{-k},\>|z-\zeta_{0}|>\frac{1}{2N},\>|z|\in\left(1-\frac{1}{N},1+\frac{1}{N}\right).

When
|z|>1+\frac{1}{N}
, estimate (
2.11
) still holds. In this case we have
(|z|-1)^{-1}\leq N
, thus (
2.11
) reads

(2.13)

\left|D_{z}^{k}\left[|z|^{-N}B_{j}(z,\zeta_{0})\right]\right|\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-2+k}(|z|-1)^{-1}|z|^{-k}.

From (
2.7
), we get

\displaystyle\left|D_{z}^{k}\left[I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta_{0})-|z|^{-N}B_{j}(z,\zeta_{0})\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{d,k}N^{k}|z|^{-k-N}\sum_{m=1}^{N}m^{d-2}|z|^{m-1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-2+k}|z|^{-k-N}\sum_{m=1}^{N}|z|^{m-1}.

By the geometric sum formula, we get

\sum_{m=1}^{N}|z|^{m-1}=(|z|-1)^{-1}(|z|^{N}-1)
, hence the last expression is bounded by

C_{d,k}N^{d-2+k}(|z|-1)^{-1}(|z|^{N}-1)|z|^{-k-N}\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-2+k}(|z|-1)^{-1}|z|^{-k},\quad|z|>1+\frac{1}{N}.

Thus
\left|D_{z}^{k}\left[I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta_{0})-|z|^{-N}B_{j}(z,\zeta_{0})\right]\right|\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-2+k}(|z|-1)^{-1}|z|^{-k}
.
Together with (
2.13
), we get

(2.14)

|D_{z}^{k}I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta_{0})|\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-2+k}(|z|-1)^{-1}|z|^{-k},\quad|z-\zeta_{0}|>\frac{1}{2N}\>\text{and}\>|z|>1+\frac{1}{N}.

Finally, suppose
|z|<1-\frac{1}{N}
. We have

\displaystyle|z|^{N}I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta_{0})

\displaystyle=B_{j}(z,\zeta_{0})-P_{N-1}^{j}(z,\zeta_{0})=c_{d}\partial{}_{z_{j}}\left(\frac{1}{|z-\zeta_{0}|^{d-2}}-\sum_{m\leq N}Z_{m}\right)=c_{d}\partial{}_{z_{j}}\left(\sum_{m\geq N+1}Z_{m}\right).

By summing up a geometric series:

\displaystyle\left|D^{k}_{z}(|z|^{N}I^{N}_{j}(z,\zeta_{0}))\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{d,k}\sum_{m\geq N+1}|D_{z}^{k+1}Z_{m}|\leq C_{d,k}\sum_{m\geq N+1}m^{d-3+(k+1)}|z|^{m-k-1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-2+k}|z|^{N-k}(1-|z|)^{-1}.

Using the product rule, we have

|D^{k}_{z}I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta_{0})|\leq C_{d,k}N^{d-2+k}(1-|z|)^{-1}|z|^{-k}.

The proof is now complete.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
. Then

|a-e^{i\theta}|\gtrsim|a-1|+|\sin\theta|.

Proof.

The proof can be done by considering two cases.
We have

|a-e^{i\theta}|^{2}=(a-\cos\theta)^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\geq\frac{1}{2}\left[|a-\cos\theta)|+|\sin\theta|\right]^{2}.

Hence
|a-e^{i\theta}|\gtrsim|a-\cos\theta|+|\sin\theta|
.
If
|a-\cos\theta|>\frac{1}{2}|a-1|
, we are done. Suppose
|a-\cos\theta|\leq\frac{1}{2}|a-1|
. Then
|a-|\cos\theta||\leq|a-\cos\theta|\leq\frac{1}{2}|a-1|
and

\displaystyle|\sin\theta|\geq\sin^{2}\theta=1-\cos^{2}\theta\geq 1-|\cos\theta|\geq|1-a|-|a-|\cos\theta||\geq\frac{1}{2}|a-1|,

which implies that
|a-e^{i\theta}|\gtrsim|\sin\theta|\gtrsim(|\sin\theta|+|a-1|)
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
g(r,\theta):=\left[(r-e^{i\theta})(r-e^{-i\theta})\right]^{-\frac{d}{2}}
. If
|\sin\theta|\gtrsim\frac{1}{N}
, then we can write

(2.15)

r^{-N}\left(g(r,\theta)-T^{N-1}[g](r,\theta)\right)=\operatorname{Re}\left[a(r,\theta)e^{iN\theta}\right]

for suitable complex-valued function
a
satisfying

(2.16)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial{}^{i}}{\partial{}r^{i}}\frac{\partial{}^{k}}{\partial{}\theta^{k}}a(r,\theta)\right|\leq C_{d,i,k}N^{\frac{d}{2}-1}|\sin\theta|^{-\frac{d}{2}-k}(|\sin\theta|+|1-r|)^{-1-i}.

Proof.

Fix
\theta
, we complexify
g
in the
r
variable.
The function
g^{\theta}(w):=[(w-e^{i\theta})(w-e^{-i\theta})]^{-\frac{d}{2}}
is analytic in
\mathbb{C}

except for poles at
e^{\pm i\theta}
.
For
w\in\mathbb{C}
, we denote
R_{N}(w):=g^{\theta}(w)-T_{N-1}(g)(w)
.
By the Cauchy integral formula and the residue theorem, we have, for small
|w|
and
N\geq 2
,

\displaystyle w^{-N}R_{N}(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=1/2}\frac{R_{N}(\zeta)}{\zeta^{N}(\zeta-w)}\,d\zeta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=1/2}\frac{g(\zeta)}{\zeta^{N}(\zeta-w)}\,d\zeta

\displaystyle=-\left(\underset{\zeta=e^{i\theta}}{\operatorname{Res}}+\underset{\zeta=e^{-i\theta}}{\operatorname{Res}}\right)\left[\zeta^{-N}g(\zeta)(\zeta-w)^{-1}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\underset{L\to\infty}{\lim}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=L}\zeta^{-N}g(\zeta)(\zeta-w)^{-1}\,d\zeta.

The limit is
0
for the last term, so

w^{-N}R_{N}(w)=-\left(\underset{\zeta=e^{i\theta}}{\operatorname{Res}}+\underset{\zeta=e^{-i\theta}}{\operatorname{Res}}\right)\left[\zeta^{-N}(\zeta-w)^{-1}g(\zeta)\right].

By analytic continuation the above holds for all
w
.

Write

\zeta^{-N}(\zeta-w)^{-1}g(\zeta)=\zeta^{-N}(\zeta-w)^{-1}\left[(\zeta-e^{i\theta})(\zeta-e^{-i\theta})\right]^{-\frac{d}{2}}.

The residues can be computed by taking
\eta=\frac{d}{2}-1
derivatives:

\displaystyle\underset{\zeta=e^{i\theta}}{\operatorname{Res}}=\left.\frac{d^{\eta}}{d\zeta^{\eta}}\right|_{\zeta=e^{i\theta}}\zeta^{-N}(\zeta-w)^{-1}\left[(\zeta-e^{-i\theta})\right]^{-\frac{d}{2}}

\displaystyle\underset{\zeta=e^{-i\theta}}{\operatorname{Res}}=\left.\frac{d^{\eta}}{d\zeta^{\eta}}\right|_{\zeta=e^{-i\theta}}\zeta^{-N}(\zeta-w)^{-1}\left[(\zeta-e^{i\theta})\right]^{-\frac{d}{2}}.

Hence we can write

r^{-N}R_{N}(r)=\operatorname{Re}\left(e^{iN\theta}a(r,\theta)\right)

where
a(r,\theta)
is a finite sum of terms of the form

a_{lpm}(e^{-i\theta}-r)^{-1-l}(\sin\theta)^{-\frac{d}{2}-p},

a_{lpm}
being constants with
|a_{lpm}|\approx N^{m}
and
l+p+m=d/2-1
. Since
|\sin\theta|\gtrsim N^{-1}
by assumption, and also
|r-e^{-i\theta}|\gtrsim|\sin\theta|

(Lemma
2.4
), the worst term is the term with
m=\frac{d}{2}-1
. Thus

(2.17)

\displaystyle|a(r,\theta)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}N^{\frac{d}{2}-1}|\sin\theta|^{-\frac{d}{2}}|e^{-i\theta}-r|^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d}N^{\frac{d}{2}-1}|\sin\theta|^{-\frac{d}{2}}(|\sin\theta|+|1-r|)^{-1}.

Similarly we can estimate
\frac{\partial{}^{i}}{\partial{}r^{i}}\frac{\partial{}^{k}}{\partial{}\theta^{k}}a(r,\theta)
. Each
r
derivative produces a factor of
(e^{-i\theta}-r)^{-1}
, while each
\theta
derivative produces a factor at worst a factor of
|\sin\theta|^{-1}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial{}^{i}}{\partial{}r^{i}}\frac{\partial{}^{k}}{\partial{}\theta^{k}}a(r,\theta)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{d,i,k}N^{\frac{d}{2}-1}|\sin\theta|^{-\frac{d}{2}-k}|e^{-i\theta}-r|^{-1-i}

\displaystyle\leq C_{d,i,k}N^{\frac{d}{2}-1}|\sin\theta|^{-\frac{d}{2}-k}(|\sin\theta|+|1-r|)^{-1-i}.\qed

The following result follows immediately from Proposition
2.2
and Proposition
2.5
.

Proof.

The form of

) and (

2.19

h_{1,j}(z,\zeta)=-\frac{1}{|\zeta|}\left(\overline{\zeta}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\overline{z}_{j}}{|z|^{2}}\left(z\cdot\overline{\zeta}+\overline{z}\cdot\zeta\right)\right)-\frac{z\cdot\overline{\zeta}+\zeta\cdot\overline{z}}{2|z||\zeta|}\cdot\frac{\overline{z}_{j}}{2|z|};\quad h_{2,j}(z)=\frac{\overline{z}_{j}}{2|z|}.

Writing in polar coordinates:

, where

\displaystyle h_{1,j}(s\xi,t\zeta)

\displaystyle=-\left(\overline{w_{j}(\eta)}-\frac{1}{2}\overline{w_{j}(\xi)}\left(w(\xi)\cdot\overline{w(\eta)}+w(\xi)\cdot\overline{w(\eta)}\right)\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left[w(\xi)\cdot\overline{w(\zeta)}\right]\overline{w_{j}(\xi)}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\left[\overline{w(\xi)}\cdot w(\zeta)\right]\overline{w_{j}(\xi)}

and

Then the estimate follows by (

|D_{\xi}^{\alpha}w(\xi)|\leq 1,\quad|D_{\eta}^{\beta}w(\eta)|\leq 1,\quad\forall\>\alpha,\beta.\qed

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
. We have

|z|^{-N}u(z)=\int_{\Omega}I^{N}(z,\zeta)|\zeta|^{-(N+d-1)}\wedge\overline{\partial}u(\zeta).

Furthermore,

(i)
If
|\zeta-z|<\frac{|\zeta|}{2N}
, then

|I^{N}(z,\zeta)|\leq C_{d}|\zeta|^{d-1}|\zeta-z|^{-(d-1)}.

(ii)
If
|\zeta-z|\geq\frac{|\zeta|}{2N}
, then

|I^{N}(z,\zeta)|\leq C_{d}N^{d-2}\min\{N,|1-r|^{-1}\}.

Proof.

(i) We have
I^{N}(z,\zeta)=c_{d}\sum_{j=1}^{n}I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta)\widehat{d\overline{\zeta}_{j}}
. For each
I_{j}^{N}
we have

(2.20)

|I_{j}^{N}(\zeta,z)|\leq\left|I_{j}^{N}(z,\zeta)-|\zeta|^{d-1}\left(\frac{|\zeta|}{|z|}\right)^{N}B_{j}(z,\zeta)\right|+|\zeta|^{d-1}\left(\frac{|\zeta|}{|z|}\right)^{N}\left|B_{j}(z,\zeta)\right|.

By Proposition
2.3
(i), the first term on the right is bounded by
C_{d}N^{d-1}
. The assumption

implies that

N^{d-1}|\zeta|\lesssim|\zeta|^{d-1}|\zeta-z|^{-(d-1)}.

To estimate the last term in (

), notice that

|\zeta-z|<\frac{|\zeta|}{2N}\iff|\widetilde{\zeta}-\widetilde{z}|<\frac{1}{2N},\quad\widetilde{\zeta}=\lambda\zeta,\;\widetilde{z}=\lambda z,\;\lambda=|\zeta|^{-1}.

Since
|\widetilde{\zeta}|=1
, the above condition implies that

|\widetilde{z}|^{-1}\leq\frac{1}{1-(2N)^{-1}}=\frac{2N}{2N-1}
. Thus

|\widetilde{z}|^{-N}\leq\frac{(2N)^{N}}{(2N-1)^{N}}=\left(\frac{2N-1}{2N}\right)^{N}=\left[\left(1-\frac{1}{2N}\right)^{N}\right]^{-1}\leq C.

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|\zeta|}{|z|}\right)^{N}|\zeta|^{d-1}\left|B_{j}(z,\zeta)\right|

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{|\widetilde{\zeta}|}{|\widetilde{z}|}\right)^{N}|\zeta|^{d-1}\left|B_{j}(z,\zeta)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-(d-1)}\lambda^{d-1}\left|B_{j}(\widetilde{z},\widetilde{\zeta})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\widetilde{\zeta}-\widetilde{z}|^{-(d-1)}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-(d-1)}|\zeta-z|^{-(d-1)}=|\zeta|^{d-1}|\zeta-z|^{-(d-1)}.

(ii)
The estimate follows immediately from Proposition
2.3
(ii).

4.

Fix
p
and
q
with
\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{q}
. For
z,\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
we let
s=|z|
,
t=|\zeta|
,
\sigma=\log(1/s)

\tau=\log(1/t)

\gamma\subset\mathbb{R}

\gamma_{\ast}=\{s\in\mathbb{R}:\log(1/s)\in\gamma\}

A(\gamma)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|\in\gamma_{\ast}\}

|\gamma|^{\prime}=\min\{\gamma,\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}

Let

be increasing and convex. We follow Wolff and osculate the weight

to

.

Proposition 4.1
.

The following formula holds

(4.1)

e^{\nu\psi(\sigma)}u(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}P_{\nu}(z,\zeta)e^{\nu\psi(\tau)}\wedge\overline{\partial}u(\zeta),\quad u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}),

where
P_{\nu}(z,\zeta):=e^{-\nu(\psi(\tau)-\psi(\sigma)-\psi^{\prime}(\sigma)(\tau-\sigma))}L_{\nu\psi^{\prime}(\sigma)}(z,\zeta)
.

Proof.

Since
e^{\nu\psi^{\prime}(\sigma)\sigma}=|z|^{-\nu\psi^{\prime}(\sigma)}
, we get

\displaystyle e^{\nu\psi(\sigma)}u(z)

\displaystyle=e^{\nu(\psi(\sigma)-\psi^{\prime}(\sigma)\sigma)}|z|^{-\nu\psi^{\prime}(\sigma)}u(z)

\displaystyle=\int e^{\nu(\psi(\sigma)-\psi^{\prime}(\sigma)\sigma)}L_{\nu\psi^{\prime}(\sigma)}(z,\zeta)|\zeta|^{-\nu\psi^{\prime}(\sigma)}\wedge\overline{\partial}u(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int e^{\nu(\psi(\sigma)-\psi^{\prime}(\sigma)\sigma)}L_{\nu\psi^{\prime}(\sigma)}(z,\zeta)e^{\nu(\psi^{\prime}(\sigma)\tau-\psi(\tau))}\wedge e^{\nu\psi(\tau)}\overline{\partial}u(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int e^{\nu(\psi(\sigma)-\psi(\tau)+\psi^{\prime}(\sigma)(\tau-\sigma))}L_{\nu\psi^{\prime}(\sigma)}\wedge e^{\nu\psi(\tau)}\overline{\partial}u(\zeta).\qed

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\lambda>0
,
\rho\geq 0

We assume that

Case 1:

\displaystyle I\leq\lambda^{-q^{\prime}}\int_{\gamma}e^{-\rho q^{\prime}x^{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-q^{\prime}}\left(\int_{\gamma\times\gamma}e^{-\rho q^{\prime}(x^{2}+y^{2})}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-q^{\prime}}\left(\int_{B(0,\sqrt{2}a)}e^{-\rho q^{\prime}r^{2}}\,r\,dr\,d\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\approx\lambda^{-q^{\prime}}\left(\int_{0}^{\sqrt{2}a}e^{-\rho q^{\prime}u}\,du\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

Case 2:

\displaystyle I\leq\lambda^{-q^{\prime}}\int_{\gamma}e^{-\rho q^{\prime}x^{2}}\,dx\leq\lambda^{-q^{\prime}}|\gamma|.

The following lemma of Wolff is the key to deal with the blow-up of the constant in Carleman inequality.

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose
\mu
is a positive measure with faster than exponential decay

(4.6)

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\log\mu\{x:|x|>T\}=-\infty.

Define

. Suppose

. Then there are disjoint intervals

and numbers

such that (with
C
a positive universal constant)

(1)

\mu_{k_{j}}(I_{j})\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\mu_{k_{j}}\|
;

(2)

\sum|I_{j}|^{-1}\geq CN
.

Remark 4.6
.

Condition (
4.6
) means that for any constant
C>0
,

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{CT}\log\mu\{x:|x|>T\}=-\infty.

Taking exponential on both sides we get

\lim_{T\to\infty}\mu\{x:|x|>T\}^{\frac{1}{CT}}=0.

In other words, for each
M>0
, we have
\mu\{x:|x|>T\}\leq M^{-CT}
for all

. This explains the meaning of “faster than exponential decay".

Proposition 4.7
.

Let

\operatorname{supp}f\in B(0,1)

(4.7)

where both sides of the equation are in

Proof.

First, suppose

is supported away from

\psi_{\varepsilon}
be the standard mollifier. Then for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small,
\psi_{\varepsilon}\ast f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B(0,1)\setminus\{0\})

Recall that

P_{\nu}(z,\zeta)=e^{-\nu\Delta_{2}\psi(\sigma,\tau)}L_{\nu}(z,\zeta)=e^{-\nu\Delta_{2}\psi(\sigma,\tau)}(|z||\zeta|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}+\rho}I^{N}(z,\zeta),

where
\Delta_{2}\psi(\sigma,\tau)=\psi(\tau)-\psi(\sigma)-\psi^{\prime}(\sigma)(\tau-\sigma)
. Since
\psi_{\varepsilon}\ast\overline{\partial}f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus\{0\})
and
\psi^{\prime}\leq C
, it follows that

e^{\nu\psi(\tau)-\nu\Delta_{2}\psi(\sigma,\tau)}(|z||\zeta|)^{-\frac{d-1}{2}+\rho}
is uniformly bounded on
\operatorname{supp}(\psi_{\varepsilon}\ast\overline{\partial}f)
.
Let
\varepsilon\to 0
on both sides of (
4.8
). On the left we have
\|\psi_{\varepsilon}f-f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}\to 0
. Now,

I^{N}(z,\zeta)=|z|^{-N}|\zeta|^{N+d-1}\left(B(z,\zeta)-P^{N-1}(z,\zeta)\right).

Let
g_{\varepsilon}=\overline{\partial}f\ast\psi_{\varepsilon}-\overline{\partial}f
. Then

g_{\varepsilon}
is
L^{p}
with compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus\{0\}
and
\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}\to 0

|B_{j}(z,\zeta)|=\left|\frac{\overline{\zeta_{j}-z_{j}}}{|\zeta-z|^{2n}}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{|\zeta-z|^{2n-1}}

\left\|\int P_{\nu}(\cdot,\zeta)e^{\nu\psi(\tau)}\wedge g_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{p}(B(0,1))}\lesssim\left\|\int\frac{1}{|\zeta-\cdot|^{2n-1}}|g_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)|\right\|_{L^{p}(B(0,1))}+\left\|\int|P^{N-1}(\zeta,z)||g_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)|\right\|_{L^{p}(B(0,1))}.

For the first integral on the right, we can write it as
\left\|\frac{1}{|\zeta-\cdot|^{2n-1}}\ast g_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{p}(B(0,1))}
and apply Young’s inequality to obtain

\left\|\frac{1}{|\zeta-\cdot|^{2n-1}}\ast g_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{p}(B(0,1))}\lesssim\left\|\frac{1}{|\zeta|^{2n-1}}\right\|_{L^{1}(B(0,R))}\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}\longrightarrow 0,\quad\text{as $\varepsilon\to 0$},

where

is some sufficiently large ball.

For the last term in (

is the degree

This finishes the proof for the case

Since

First, we show that

\psi(\log(|z|^{-1}))\leq\psi(\log(2^{j}))\leq C^{\prime}\log 2^{j}

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,1)}e^{p\nu\psi(\sigma)}|f|^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 0}\int_{2^{-j}\leq|z|\leq 2^{-(j-1)}}e^{p\nu\psi(\log(|z|^{-1}))}|f(z)|^{p}

where in the last step we use the

to make the sum converge.

To finish the proof we show that

at

.
Write

By Proposition

\|g^{1}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B(0,1))}\leq C_{\nu}\|e^{\nu\psi(\tau)}(\overline{\partial}\chi_{\varepsilon})f\|_{L^{p}(B(0,1))}.

Using

, we get

\|g^{1}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B(0,1))}\lesssim\varepsilon^{-C\nu}\|f\|_{L^{p}(B(0,2\varepsilon))}\to 0\quad\text{as $\varepsilon\to 0$}.

We now show that

in

. By Proposition

, we have

\left\|\int P_{\nu}(\cdot,\zeta)e^{\nu\psi(\tau)}\wedge(\chi_{\varepsilon}\overline{\partial}f-\overline{\partial}f)\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B(0,1))}\leq C_{\nu}\|e^{\nu\psi(\sigma)}(\chi_{\varepsilon}\overline{\partial}f-\overline{\partial}f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}.

Since

when

by the dominated convergence theorem. This shows that

in

. Together with

, this implies that
g_{\varepsilon}\to g
in
L^{p}(B(0,1))
. ∎

Theorem 4.10
.

If
V\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, then the differential inequality
|\overline{\partial}u|\leq V|u|
has the strong unique continuation property in the following sense: Let

|\overline{\partial}u|\leq V|u|
and

\lim_{R\to 0}R^{-N}\int_{|x|<R}|u|^{2}=0

is
0
.

Proof.

By Sobolev embedding, we have
u\in L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}_{\mathrm{loc}}
. The inequality
|\overline{\partial}u|\leq C|u|
shows that
\overline{\partial}u\in L^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}
. Then
u\in W^{1,\frac{2d}{d-2}}
. Apply Sobolev embedding again we get
u\in W^{\frac{2d}{d-4}}
. Repeat the procedure we eventually obtain
u\in L^{p}
for some

. Hence
u\in C^{0}
. By Proposition
4.8
,
\mathcal{A}=u^{-1}(0)
is a complex analytic variety. If

then we are done. Otherwise suppose
\dim(\mathcal{A})\leq 2n-2
. Define

V=\begin{cases}\frac{\overline{\partial}u}{u}&\text{on $\Omega\setminus\mathcal{A}$};\\

We show that
\overline{\partial}u=Vu
on
\Omega
in the sense of distributions, i.e.

(4.16)

\int u\wedge\overline{\partial}\phi=-\int(Vu)\wedge\phi,\quad\text{for all $(n,n-1)$ form $\phi$ with coefficients in $C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)$}.

Let
\{\widetilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
be the family of cut-off functions from Lemma

\widetilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon}\equiv 1

\left|\nabla\widetilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon}\right|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\alpha,n}\varepsilon^{l-1}\left(\Lambda_{n-lp^{\prime}}(\mathcal{A})+\varepsilon\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}},\quad n-lp^{\prime}>0.

Let
\chi_{\varepsilon}:=1-\widetilde{\chi}_{\varepsilon}
, so that
\chi_{\varepsilon}\equiv 0

\mathcal{A}
and
\chi_{\varepsilon}\equiv 1

\Omega\setminus\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}

\chi_{\varepsilon}

\chi_{\varepsilon}

4.17

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|u(\overline{\partial}\chi_{\varepsilon})\wedge\phi\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\|\overline{\partial}\chi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}

In particular the integral converges to

\varepsilon\to 0

Next, we show that

\overline{\partial}

(0,1)

\int_{\Omega}V\wedge\overline{\partial}\phi=0,\quad\text{ for all $(n,n-2)$ form $\phi$ with coefficients in $C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)$.}

V=\overline{\partial}\log u

\overline{\partial}

\Omega\setminus\mathcal{A}
.

\chi_{\varepsilon}

By the same reasoning as above, the first integral converges to

We now solve
\overline{\partial}f=V

\overline{\partial}

Let

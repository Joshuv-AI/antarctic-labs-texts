Non–generic components of the Emerton–Gee stack for GL 2 \GL_{2}
Kalyani Kansal, Ben Savoie

Abstract
. Let K K be a finite unramified extension of 𝐐 p {\mathbf{Q}}_{p} with p > 3 p>3 . We study the extremely non–generic irreducible components in the reduced part of the Emerton–Gee stack for GL 2 \GL_{2} . We show precisely which irreducible components are smooth, which are normal, and which have Gorenstein normalizations. We show that the normalizations of the irreducible components admit smooth–local covers by resolution–rational varieties. We also determine the singular loci in the components, and use our results to update expectations about the conjectural categorical p p –adic Langlands correspondence.



1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a fixed prime. Let
K
be a finite extension of
{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
with residue field
k
of degree
f
over
{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
, and absolute Galois group
G_{K}
. In
[
EG
]
, Emerton and Gee studied the stack
{\mathcal{X}}_{d}
of étale
(\varphi,\Gamma)
–modules of rank
d
defined over the formal spectrum of the ring of integers of a large finite extension of
{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
with residue field
{\mathbf{F}}
. As discussed in
[
EGH
]
,
{\mathcal{X}}_{d}
is expected to play the role of the stack of
L
–parameters in the so far conjectural categorical
p
–adic Langlands correspondence for
\GL_{d}(K)
.

By
[
EG
, Thm. 1.2.1]
,
{\mathcal{X}}_{d}
is a Noetherian formal algebraic stack and its underlying reduced substack

is an algebraic stack of finite type over
{\mathbf{F}}
. The irreducible components of
{\mathcal{X}}_{d,\text{red}}
admit a natural labelling by Serre weights, which are the irreducible representations of
\GL_{d}(k)
with coefficients in
{\mathbf{F}}
.
Each Serre weight for
\GL_{2}(k)
is described by (ordered) pairs of
f
–tuples of integers
\mathbf{m}=(m_{j})_{j}
and
\mathbf{n}=(n_{j})_{j}
with

for each

(see Section
2.4
for details). Let
{\mathcal{X}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
be the irreducible component of
{\mathcal{X}}_{2,\text{red}}
labelled by
\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}
. The following is our main theorem, upgrading the main result of
[
GKK+
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorems
5.5
,
5.6
)
.

Let
p>3
,
K
unramified over
{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
of degree
f
, and
\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}
a Serre weight. Then the following are true:

(1)

The component
{\mathcal{X}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
is not smooth if and only if either

(a)

n_{j}=p-2
for each
j\in{\mathbf{Z}}/f{\mathbf{Z}}
, or

(b)

there exists a subset
\{i-k,\dots,i\}\subset{\mathbf{Z}}/f{\mathbf{Z}}
with
n_{i-k}=0
,
n_{j}=p-2
whenever
j\in\{i-k,\dots,i\}\smallsetminus\{i-k,i\}
, and
n_{i}=p-1
.

(2)

If (a) holds,
{\mathcal{X}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
is not normal and its normalization admits a smooth–local cover by a resolution–rational variety that is not Gorenstein. The non–normal locus on
{\mathcal{X}}(\sigma)
has codimension
f
and its complement is smooth.

(3)

If (b) holds,
{\mathcal{X}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
is normal and admits a smooth–local cover by a resolution–rational variety. It is additionally Gorenstein, even lci, if and only if every subset
\{i-k,\dots,i\}\subset{\mathbf{Z}}/f{\mathbf{Z}}
as in (b) has cardinality
2
. The singular locus in
{\mathcal{X}}(\sigma)
has codimension
\geq 2
.

Here, the definition of a resolution–rational variety is taken from
[
Kov
, Defn. 9.1]
and is as follows.
A variety
Y
is resolution–rational if it admits a resolution of singularities
f:X\to Y
which is a cohomological equivalence.
This means that
f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}
,
R^{i}f_{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}=0

for
i>0
,
f_{*}\omega_{X}\cong\omega_{Y}
, and
R^{i}f_{*}\omega_{X}=0
for
i>0
, where
\omega_{X}
and
\omega_{Y}
are the dualizing sheaves on
X
and
Y
respectively (in particular,
Y
is Cohen–Macaulay).

Fixing an algebraic closure
\overline{{\mathbf{F}}}
of
{\mathbf{F}}
,
{\mathcal{X}}_{d}(\overline{{\mathbf{F}}})={\mathcal{X}}_{d,\text{red}}(\overline{{\mathbf{F}}})
is the groupoid of continuous two–dimensional representations of
G_{K}
with coefficients in
\overline{{\mathbf{F}}}
. A study of the singular loci in the irreducible components of
{\mathcal{X}}_{2,\text{red}}
allows us to obtain the following two theorems, which can be viewed as partial generalizations of
[

, Thm. 1]
.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
5.12
)
.

Let
p>3
,
K
unramified over
{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
, and
\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}
a Serre weight. The versal ring at
\overline{\rho}\in{\mathcal{X}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})(\overline{{\mathbf{F}}})
is not normal if and only if
n_{j}=p-2
for each
j
and, viewing
\overline{\rho}
as a
G_{K}
–representation,

\overline{\rho}\otimes\left(\prod_{j\in{\mathbf{Z}}/f{\mathbf{Z}}}\omega_{j}^{(1-m_{j})}\right)

is unramified.

Theorem 1.3

(Theorem
5.13
)
.

Let
p>3
,
K
unramified over
{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
, and
\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}
a Serre weight. Suppose there exists
i\in{\mathbf{Z}}/f{\mathbf{Z}}
such that
n_{i+1}=0
,

n_{i}=p-1
, and
n_{j}=p-2
for
j\in{\mathbf{Z}}/f{\mathbf{Z}}\smallsetminus\{i,i+1\}
. The versal ring at
\overline{\rho}\in{\mathcal{X}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})(\overline{{\mathbf{F}}})
is not smooth if and only if, viewing
\overline{\rho}
as a

\overline{\rho}\otimes\left(\omega_{i+1}^{-m_{i+1}}\otimes\prod_{j\neq i+1}\omega_{j}^{1-m_{j}}\right)

In this case, the versal ring is normal and Cohen–Macaulay. It is Gorenstein, even lci, if and only if

.

Here,
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j}
are choices of
G_{K}
–characters extending the
f
distinct niveau
1

Note that the hypothesis in the statement of Theorem

does not encapsulate all normal but non–smooth components unless

.

1.1.
Categorical
p
–adic Langlands

In
[
EGH
, Conj. 6.1.14]
, Emerton, Gee and Hellmann conjecture the existence of an exact fully faithful functor

\mathfrak{A}
from a certain derived category of so–called smooth representations of
\GL_{d}(K)
to a certain derived category of quasicoherent sheaves on
{\mathcal{X}}_{d}
, satisfying various properties. Without going into the details of what the appropriate categories are and the properties
\mathfrak{A}
is expected to satisfy, we focus our attention on a few consequences of their conjecture laid out in Sections 6 and 7 of
loc. cit.
, restricting to the case
d=2
. Let
\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}
be a Serre weight viewed as a representation of
\GL_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
via inflation, where
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
is the ring of integers of
K
. Let
{\mathcal{L}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
denote the conjectural sheaf

\mathfrak{A}\left(\text{c-Ind}_{\GL_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})}^{\GL_{2}(K)}\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}\right).

This conjectural sheaf interpolates certain (actual, and not conjectural) patched modules (see
[
EGH
, Rmks. 6.1.26, 6.1.30]
). Some of the expectations about
{\mathcal{L}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
are as follows:

(1)

It is a coherent sheaf concentrated in degree
0
by
[
EGH
, Rmk. 6.1.26]
and maximal Cohen–Macaulay on its support. The latter essentially follows from exactness of
\mathfrak{A}
and Cohen–Macaulay nature of sheaves associated to locally algebraic types (see
[
EGH
, Rmk. 6.1.34]
). When
\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}
is non–Steinberg, i.e.
n_{j}<p-1
for some
j
, expected compatibility with the geometric Breuil–Mézard conjecture implies that the support of
{\mathcal{L}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
is
{\mathcal{X}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
.

(2)

Ignoring possible shifts of complexes,
{\mathcal{L}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
is Grothendieck–Serre self–dual by
[
EGH
, Rmk. 6.1.35]
. Thus, when
K
is an unramified non–trivial extension of
{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
, and
\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}
is non–Steinberg, Corollary
5.8
implies that
{\mathcal{L}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
is the pushforward of a self–dual maximal Cohen–Macaulay sheaf on the normalization of
{\mathcal{X}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
.

(3)

When
K
is an unramified non–trivial extension of
{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
,
p>5
, and
\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}
is non–Steinberg,
{\mathcal{L}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
has rank
1
generically on
{\mathcal{X}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
. This follows from combining the data on codimension of non–normal locus in potentially Barsotti–Tate deformation rings given in the proof of
[
LHMM
, Thm. 4.6.10]
, conjecture about the rank of the generic fiber of sheaves corresponding to locally algebraic types in
[
EGH
, Rmk. 6.1.34]
, and compatibility with geometric Breuil–Mézard conjecture. The key point is that one can always find a non–scalar tame inertial type
\tau
such that
\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}
appears in the Jordan–Holder decomposition of the
\GL_{2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
–representation associated to
\tau
by inertial local Langlands, and such that the potentially Barsotti–Tate deformation rings of type
\tau
are regular in codimension
1
.

Motivated by these expectations, we obtain the following theorem, whose proof uses the statement about the codimension of the singular locus in Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.4

(Corollary
5.10
)
.

Let
p>3
,
K
an unramified non–trivial extension of
{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
, and
\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}
a Serre weight. Let
\iota:{\mathcal{U}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{X}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
be the smooth open locus in
{\mathcal{X}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
. Suppose
{\mathcal{F}}
is a finite type maximal Cohen–Macaulay sheaf on
{\mathcal{X}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
generically of rank
1
. The following are true:

(1)

The sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
is isomorphic to the pushforward along
\iota
of the invertible sheaf
\iota^{*}{\mathcal{F}}
on
{\mathcal{U}}
.

(2)

If
\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}
is non–Steinberg and there does not exist
i
such that
(n_{i-1},n_{i})=(0,p-1)
, then
{\mathcal{F}}
is the pushforward of a unique invertible sheaf on a smooth algebraic stack of Breuil–Kisin modules admitting a proper, birational map onto
{\mathcal{X}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
.

In the setup of Theorem
1.4
above and assuming a reasonable notion of a dualizing complex on an algebraic stack, note that if
{\mathcal{F}}
is (Grothendieck–Serre) self–dual, then so is
\iota^{*}{\mathcal{F}}
. As we will see later in the proof,
{\mathcal{U}}
is isomorphic to its preimage in the aforementioned stack of Breuil–Kisin modules. If the hypothesis in (ii) holds, then the codimension of the complement of the preimage of
{\mathcal{U}}
turns out to be
\geq 2
. Thus, by an argument involving the algebraic Hartog’s Lemma on the dual of a line bundle on a smooth variety,
{\mathcal{F}}
is seen to be the (non–derived) pushforward of a self–dual invertible sheaf on the stack of Breuil–Kisin modules we are considering.

When
p>5
, we therefore expect that one can uniquely characterize
{\mathcal{L}}(\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}})
for all non–Steinberg
\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}
as the pushforward of the unique self–dual invertible sheaf on
{\mathcal{U}}
(equivalently, on an appropriate stack of Breuil–Kisin modules when the hypothesis in (ii) holds), and indeed use this characterization as a key ingredient towards constructing
\mathfrak{A}
(
c.f.

[
EGH
, Sec. 7.6.10]
).

1.2.
Strategy and outline

We begin in Section
2
by setting up some standard notation and definitions. In Section
3
, we review many of the constructions from
[
LHMM
]
that we use essentially. These constructions pertain to smooth–local charts on various closed substacks of
{\mathcal{X}}_{2,\text{red}}
, as well as explicit auxiliary schemes through which maps from certain stacks of Breuil–Kisin modules to
{\mathcal{X}}_{2,\text{red}}
factor locally in the smooth topology. The constraints on
p
and the requirement for
K
to be unramified over
{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
appear in this section, most critically in the proof of Proposition
3.7
. We use the results of
[
BBH+
]
to impose “shape” conditions that cut out irreducible components in these charts, thus setting up smooth–local charts for the non–Steinberg irreducible components of
{\mathcal{X}}_{2,\text{red}}
.

In Section
4
, we undertake a detailed study of the geometry of these charts. We start off by analyzing the additional relations on the auxiliary schemes that come from imposing shape conditions. Next, in Section
4.2
, we make the crucial observation that the charts for the irreducible components of
{\mathcal{X}}_{2,\text{red}}
can be written as (typically non–trivial) products of varieties, with each factor in the product somewhat easier to study. Each factor admits a resolution of singularities by a smooth scheme, and we determine the locus where this resolution fails to be an isomorphism. The key argument is in Lemma
4.8
. Next, by doing factor–wise computations, we obtain the cohomologies of the dualizing complex for the charts following the approach in
[
LHMM
]
, which reduces the computations to those for the auxiliary schemes and Koszul resolutions. We also compute lower bounds on the ranks of these cohomology groups at the loci where the irreducible components of
{\mathcal{X}}_{2,\text{red}}
fail to be isomorphic to their resolutions of singularities. This allows us to infer that these loci are precisely the singular loci.

Finally, we prove Theorems
5.5
and
5.10
in Section
5
by doing various combinatorial calculations and applying the results from Section
4
to the irreducible components of
{\mathcal{X}}_{2,\text{red}}
labelled by specific non–Steinberg Serre weights. We handle the Steinberg components separately in Theorem
5.6
by directly analyzing the points. We also determine explicitly the Galois representations corresponding to the finite type points of the singular loci in the setup of Theorems
5.12
and
5.13
.

Remark 1.5
.

We use the definition of resolution–rational as given in
[
Kov
, Defn. 9.1]
, as well as prove Corollary
4.18
using
[
Kov
, Prop. 6.10]
. While the paper
[
Kov
]
has now been retracted because of errors in the main statements, the definition of resolution–rational is still meaningful and the proof of
[
Kov
, Prop. 6.10]
is still correct.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let

(1)

(2)

Lemma 4.11
.

(Version of
[
LHMM
, Lem. 4.2.3]
) Let
S\in\{L,R\}^{{\mathbf{Z}}/f{\mathbf{Z}}}
and
\mathfrak{t}\in\mathfrak{T}
.

(1)

The schemes
{Ba}_{j}(\widetilde{z}_{j})_{S_{j}}

are local complete intersections over

. Whenever non–empty,
{Ba}_{j}(\widetilde{z}_{j})_{S_{j}}

(2)

When

, the dualizing sheaf of
{Ba}_{j}(\widetilde{z}_{j})_{S_{j}}
is
{\mathcal{O}}_{{Ba}_{j}(\widetilde{z}_{j})_{S_{j}}}
. Thus, if

First, suppose

([x:y],(B,C,D))\mapsto([x:y],(B,C,D))

. Below is an open cover of
{\mathbf{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbf{A}}^{3}
along with local generators of the ideal sheaf
{\mathcal{I}}
whose vanishing locus gives
{Ba}_{j}(\widetilde{z}_{j})_{S_{j}}

\displaystyle D(y)=\Spec{\mathbf{F}}[B,C,D,t],

\displaystyle D(x)=\Spec{\mathbf{F}}[B,C,D,t^{-1}],

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}(D(x))=(D-Ct^{-1},B+Dt^{-1}).

The rest of the first and second statements is now immediate.

Lemma 5.1

\displaystyle\qquad\left(\gamma_{j}+1,w_{0},w_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\left(p-1-\gamma_{j},\operatorname{id},w_{0}\right)

\displaystyle(\langle\mu_{j},\alpha^{\vee}\rangle,s_{j},s_{\mathrm{or},j})=\left(\gamma_{j},\operatorname{id},\operatorname{id}\right).

\displaystyle s_{f-1}^{-1}s_{f-2}^{-1}\dots s_{f-j}^{-1}(\mu_{f-j})=(a_{f-j}+c_{f-j},b_{f-j}+c_{f-j})\text{ for }1\leq j\leq f-1,

–representations associated to
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{M}^{\prime\prime}
using
[
CEGSc
, Lem. 4.1.4]
, we find that upto twist by a character,
\overline{\rho}
is an extension of
\mathrm{ur}_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{0}}
by
\mathrm{ur}_{\lambda^{\prime}_{0}}
as desired, where the extension class is in a specific one-dimensional space of extensions when
\lambda^{\prime}_{0}=\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{0}
, and is
0
when
\lambda^{\prime}_{0}\neq\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{0}
. (Note that the formula actually gives
G_{K_{\infty}}
–representations for
K_{\infty}
a wildly ramified extension of
K
, but by
[
CEGSc
, Prop. 2.2.6]
, this is enough.)

Finally, to characterize the
1
–dimensional extension class that survives when
\lambda^{\prime}_{0}=\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{0}
, let
{\mathcal{M}}=\varepsilon_{\tau}(\mathfrak{M})
. By Proposition
2.13
, for each
j
, there exists a basis of
{\mathcal{M}}_{j}
with respect to which the matrix of
\varphi_{{\mathcal{M}}}^{(j)}:{\mathcal{M}}_{j-1}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{j}
is given by

A_{\mathfrak{M},\beta}^{(j)}s_{j}^{-1}v^{\mu_{j}}=A_{\mathfrak{M},\beta}^{(j)}\begin{pmatrix}v&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}v^{-m_{j}}.

We claim that the
G_{K_{\infty}}
–representation
T({\mathcal{M}})
splits after restriction to
G_{L_{\infty}}
, where
L
is the unramified extension of
K
of degree
p
with residue field

and
L_{\infty}=LK_{\infty}
.
Indeed, one can check easily that the étale
\varphi
–module

corresponding to

is given by

. Thus, for

, the matrix

for the Frobenius map
\varphi_{{\mathcal{M}}_{L}}^{(j)}:({\mathcal{M}}_{L})_{j-1}\to({\mathcal{M}}_{L})_{j}
with respect to the obvious basis is given by

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}v^{1-m_{j\text{ mod }f}}

\displaystyle\text{ if }j\not\equiv 0\mod f,\vskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\lambda^{\prime}_{0}&x_{0}\\
0&\lambda^{\prime}_{0}\end{pmatrix}v^{1-m_{j\text{ mod }f}}

\displaystyle\text{ if }j\equiv 0\mod f.

Let

\displaystyle g_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-d_{j}x_{0}/\lambda^{\prime}_{0}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

where

, the space of extensions that vanish after restriction to

.

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
\sigma=\sigma_{\mathbf{m},\mathbf{n}}
be a Serre weight with
n_{0}=p-1
,
n_{1}=0
,
and
n_{j}=p-2
for

. The versal ring at
\overline{\rho}\in{\mathcal{X}}(\sigma)(\overline{{\mathbf{F}}})
is not smooth if and only if, viewing
\overline{\rho}
as a
G_{K}
–representation,

\overline{\rho}\otimes\left(\omega_{1}^{-m_{1}}\otimes\prod_{j\neq 1}\omega_{j}^{1-m_{j}}\right)

is unramified.
In this case, the versal ring is normal and Cohen–Macaulay. It is Gorenstein, even lci, if and only if

.

Proof.

As noted in the proof of Theorem
5.5
,
{\mathcal{X}}(\sigma)
is the scheme–theoretic image of

where, by Lemma

,

\displaystyle(1,w_{0},w_{0},L)

\displaystyle\text{ if }j=0,

\displaystyle(1,w_{0},\operatorname{id},R)

\displaystyle\text{ if }j=1,

\displaystyle(1,\operatorname{id},\operatorname{id},R)

\displaystyle\text{ if }j\not\in\{0,1\}

and by
[
GKK+
, Prop. 4.1.2]
,
\tau^{\vee}\cong\eta_{1}\oplus\eta_{2}
where

Thus,
\mu_{j}=(c_{j}+1,c_{j})
for each
j

5.2
),

if

and
c_{1}=-m_{1}-1
.

By Corollary
4.37
and the proof of Lemma
5.4

we are looking for
\overline{\rho}
admitting a lift to
\widetilde{{\mathcal{Z}}}^{\tau}(\widetilde{z})_{S}
for some
\widetilde{z}=(\widetilde{w}_{j}s_{j}^{-1}v^{\mu_{j}})_{j}
with

such that the associated type class tuple

and

, so that the lift lies in the vanishing locus of
N(\mathfrak{t})
where
\mathfrak{t}=(0,1,\dots,f-1,0)\in\mathfrak{T}^{*}
. By comparing
s,\mu
to Tables
1
and
2
, we find that
\widetilde{w}_{0}=\widetilde{w}_{1}=w_{0}t_{\eta}
while
\widetilde{w}_{j}=t_{w_{0}(\eta)}
for
j\not\in\{0,1\}
.

The proof of Lemma
4.1
shows that
V(N(\mathfrak{t}))
is the
image of the closed locus in
\widetilde{Y}^{\eta,\tau}(\widetilde{z})_{S}
cut out by setting
X_{j}=\operatorname{id}
for each
j\neq 0
, and that
X_{0}
is necessarily
\operatorname{id}
.
Let
\mathfrak{M}
be a Breuil–Kisin module with
\overline{{\mathbf{F}}}
–coefficients in the image of this locus under the map
\widetilde{Y}^{\eta,\tau}(\widetilde{z})_{S}\to Y^{\eta,\tau}_{S}
. The matrix
A_{\mathfrak{M},\beta}^{(j)}
is given by
W_{j}
described in (
3.23
).
By
[
LHMM
, Table 3]
,
l_{j}\kappa_{j}=r_{j}
, equivalently
\widetilde{l}_{j}\kappa_{j}\widetilde{r}_{j}^{-1}\in B(\overline{{\mathbf{F}}})
, for each
j\neq 0
.
Therefore, there exist
b_{j}\in B(\overline{{\mathbf{F}}})
for
j\neq 0
and
b_{0}\in\GL_{2}(\overline{{\mathbf{F}}})
such that

(5.14)

\displaystyle A_{\mathfrak{M},\beta}^{(j)}=\begin{cases}b_{0}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
v&0\end{pmatrix}&\text{ if }j=0,\\
b_{1}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
v&0\end{pmatrix}&\text{ if }j=1,\\
b_{j}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&v\end{pmatrix}&\text{ if }j\not\in\{0,1\}.\end{cases}

On the other hand, one verifies immediately that for any

with
b_{j}\in B(\overline{{\mathbf{F}}})
when

and

,

([0:1],b_{j},\operatorname{id},[0:1])_{j}\in\widetilde{Ba}(\widetilde{z})_{S}

gives a point of

in the fiber over

. In other words, the finite type points in the singular locus are precisely the étale

Therefore,

(5.15)

\displaystyle C_{\mathfrak{M},\beta}^{(j)}=\begin{cases}\begin{pmatrix}W&Zu^{p^{f}-1-l_{0}}\\
Uu^{l_{0}}&Vv\end{pmatrix}&\text{ if }j=0,\\

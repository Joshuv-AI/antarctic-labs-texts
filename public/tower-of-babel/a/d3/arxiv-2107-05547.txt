Elekes-Szabó for Groups, and Approximate Subgroups in Weak General Position
Tingxiang Zou

Abstract
We show that with a suitable weak notion of general position, the Elekes-Szabó condition on the group operation of a connected complex algebraic group characterises nilpotence of the group. Along the way, we prove a Mordell-Lang result for generic finitely generated subgroups of commutative complex algebraic groups.

Introduction

The main theorem of
[
ES12
]
shows that any complex subvariety
V\subseteq(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{3}
which has asymptotically large intersection with products
of finite subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
in
general position
must be, up to finite
correspondences, the graph of an algebraic group operation.

[
BW16
]
showed that the group involved must be abelian, and it
follows that the result has content even when
V
is taken to be the graph of
an algebraic group operation: it shows that if
V
has such large
intersections with products of sets in general position, then the group must
be abelian-by-finite. Here, general position of a subset
X\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}

means that
X
has boundedly finite intersections with proper subvarieties
W\subsetneq\mathbb{C}^{n}
. This assumption was slightly weakened in
[
BB21
]
to

coarse general position
, which allows asymptotic growth in such
intersections slower than
|X|^{\epsilon}
for all
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, while
retaining the same conclusions, including commutativity of the resulting
group. Conversely, it was shown there that the graph of every connected
commutative complex algebraic group admits asymptotically large intersections
with products of finite sets in coarse general position.shown there that with this coarse general position assumption, every connected
commutative complex algebraic group arises in this way.

Here we make a start on understanding what happens to these Elekes-Szabó
phenomena when the general position assumption is relaxed yet further, by
considering this special case that
V
is the graph of a complex algebraic
group operation. We show that, just as coarse general position in the
Elekes-Szabó configuration characterises when the group is abelian-by-finite,
a suitably weakened general position assumption characterises when it is
nilpotent-by-finite.
Specifically, we prove Theorem
1.2
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
W
be a complex variety.
Let
\alpha,\tau\in\mathbb{N}
.
A finite subset
X\subset W
is in
weak
(\alpha,\tau)
-general position

in
W
if
|X|\geq\alpha
and for any proper irreducible complex closed
subvariety
W^{\prime}\subsetneq W
of complexity at most
\alpha
,
we have
|W^{\prime}\cap X|\leq|X|^{1-\frac{1}{\tau}}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a complex connected algebraic group.
Then the following are equivalent.

(I)

G
is nilpotent;

(II)

The graph
\Gamma_{G}
of the group operation is
wgp-coherent
:
For all
\epsilon>0
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}

there exist
\tau\in\mathbb{N}

and finite subsets
A,B,C\subseteq G

which are in weak
(\alpha,\tau)
-general position in
G

such that

|\{(a,b,ab)\in A\times B\times C\}|\geq\max(|A|,|B|,|C|)^{2-\epsilon}
.

As mentioned above, the analogous statement with coarse general position and
commutativity was observed in
[
BB21
, Corollary 3.15]
, and the
(II)\Rightarrow(I)
direction of this abelian case was previously obtained in

[
BW16
]
. Our proof of this direction of
Theorem
1.2
is along similar lines, and was heavily inspired by
the ideas of
[
BW16
]
. Briefly, the idea in both cases is to
apply the Balog-Szemeredi-Gowers-Tao theorem to reduce to considering the
existence of suitably approximate subgroups in suitably general position, and
then apply a variation on
[
BGT11
]
to obtain nilpotence or commutativity
of the group. This is treated in Section
2
.
We also observe in Proposition
2.21
that the commutative case goes
through when coarse general position is weakened to a certain
condition related to a result of Larsen and Pink, intermediate between coarse
and weak general position.

For
(I)\Rightarrow(II)
, we will see that we can take
A=B=C
to be the
approximate subgroup given by a nilprogression generated by an
l
-tuple of
independent generics of
G
, for a sufficiently large
l
depending only on

G
. The difficulty is to see that such nilprogressions have small
intersection with proper subvarieties.

We first prove this in Section
3
in the case that
G
is
commutative (where a nilprogression is just a generalised arithmetic
progression). For
G
a semiabelian variety, it is a consequence of the truth
of the Mordell-Lang conjecture that this holds with
l=1
. For
G=\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}

(where
\mathbb{G}_{a}
denotes the additive group over
\mathbb{C}
), it is straightforward to
see that it holds with
l=n
. A general commutative algebraic group in
characteristic 0 can be seen as an extension of a semiabelian variety by some

\mathbb{G}_{a}^{n}
. Although the general case does not just follow from the two extreme
cases, we obtain it by adapting techniques from Hrushovski’s proof

[
Hru96
]
of Mordell-Lang for function fields.

In Section
4
, we extend this inductively to nilpotent
algebraic groups. Let us sketch the method in the case of a group
G
of
nilpotency class 2.
If we take independent generic generators
g_{1},...,g_{2n}
, the intersection
of the nilprogression
P
with the derived subgroup
G^{\prime}
will contain the
arithmetic progression generated by the commutators

[g_{1},g_{2}],...,[g_{2n-1},g_{2n}]
. It may be that these commutators are not
themselves generic in
G^{\prime}
, but if
w
is the commutator width of
G
then
products of
w
of them will be independent generics. Moreover, the fibres of
the quotient
P/G^{\prime}
will essentially be unions of translates of such
arithmetic progressions. Replacing the nilprogression with a “nilbox” as in

[
BG11a
]
and applying further transformations, we will reduce to the case
that the fibres are literally of this form. Meanwhile,
P/G^{\prime}
is itself an
arithmetic progression in independent generators
g_{i}/G^{\prime}
in the commutative
group
G/G^{\prime}
. Applying the commutative case of Section
3
to

G^{\prime}
and to
G/G^{\prime}
will allow us to conclude.

In fact, we work throughout with pseudofinite sets in the style of

[
Hru13
]
, as explained in Section
1.1
. This allows us to
deal more explicitly with the structure behind the combinatorics.

1.1
Setup

Let
\mathcal{U}
be a non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
.
For a set
S
the
internal
subsets of the ultrapower
S^{\mathcal{U}}
are
the elements of
\mathbb{P}(S)^{\mathcal{U}}\subseteq\mathbb{P}(S^{\mathcal{U}})
.
The finite cardinality function
\mathbb{P}(S)\to\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
induces the non-standard finite cardinality function, which assigns to every internal subset
X=\lim_{i\to\mathcal{U}}X_{i}\subseteq S^{\mathcal{U}}
the value
|X|:=\lim_{i\to\mathcal{U}}|X_{i}|\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\})^{\mathcal{U}}=\mathbb{N}^{\mathcal{U}}\cup\{\infty\}
.
We fix some
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{U}}
greater than any standard real,
and for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{U}}_{\geq 0}
we set
\boldsymbol{\delta}(\alpha)=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\xi}(\alpha):=\operatorname{st}\log_{\xi}\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
,
where
\operatorname{st}:\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{U}}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
is
the standard part map, and
\log_{\xi}\alpha=\lim_{i\to\mathcal{U}}\log_{\xi_{i}}\alpha_{i}
for
\xi=\lim_{i\to\mathcal{U}}\xi_{i}
and
\alpha=\lim_{i\to\mathcal{U}}\alpha_{i}
.
We set
\boldsymbol{\delta}(X):=\boldsymbol{\delta}(|X|)
.

A
\bigwedge
-internal
subset of
S^{\mathcal{U}}
is the intersection
\bigcap_{i\in\omega}X_{i}
of
countably many internal subsets
X_{i}
. We define
\boldsymbol{\delta}(\bigcap_{i\in\omega}X_{i}):=\inf_{n\in\omega}\boldsymbol{\delta}(\bigcap_{i<n}X_{i})
.

Following Wagner
[
Wag20
]
, we say a
\bigwedge
-internal set
X

is
broad
if
0<\boldsymbol{\delta}(X)<\infty
.

If
S_{i}
are arbitrary sets, their ultraproduct
\prod_{i\to\mathcal{U}}S_{i}
is an
internal subset of
(\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}S_{i})^{\mathcal{U}}
, so the above definitions
also apply to such ultraproducts.

We will use the
\aleph_{1}
-compactness of ultraproducts over
\mathcal{U}
, which means
that a countable set of internal sets has non-empty intersection if any finite
subset has. This applies not only to internal subsets of
S^{\mathcal{U}}
, but also to
internal subsets of the set
\mathbb{P}(S)^{\mathcal{U}}
of internal subsets of
S^{\mathcal{U}}
, and so
on.
This implies in particular that any
\bigwedge
-internal subset
X=\bigwedge_{i}X_{i}
of
S^{\mathcal{U}}
contains an internal subset of the same
\boldsymbol{\delta}
.
Indeed, suppose
a:=\boldsymbol{\delta}(X)\in\mathbb{R}
(the case
a=\infty
is similar).
Then finite intersections of

\{\{Z\in\mathbb{P}(S)^{\mathcal{U}}:|Z|\geq\xi^{a-\frac{1}{m}}\}:m\}\cup\{\{Z\in\mathbb{P}(S)^{\mathcal{U}}:Z\subseteq X_{i}\}:i\}

are non-empty, as witnessed by
\bigcap_{i<n}X_{i}
, and we conclude by

\aleph_{1}
-compactness.

Let
\mathbb{K}:=\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}}
.

Unless otherwise specified, all algebraic varieties will be over
\mathbb{K}
, and we
identify them with their sets of
\mathbb{K}
-rational points.

2
Approximate subgroups in weak general position

In this section we prove the
(II)\Rightarrow(I)
direction of
Theorem
1.2
, which we reformulate in pseudofinite terms and in
more detail as Theorem
2.14
. The core of the argument is
essentially a matter of
[
BGT11
, Theorem 2.5]
, but the situation is a
little different, and we take the opportunity to obtain a suitable analogue of
it in terms of
\bigwedge
-internal subgroups (Proposition
2.6
) as a
consequence of
[
Hru13
, Theorem 3.1]
(which is itself a recasting of
the arguments in
[
BGT11
]
) and some further arguments taken from

[
BGT11
]
and
[
BG11b
]
. The relation to
[
BGT11
]
is addressed
further in Remark
2.18
. The other main ingredient is the
Balog-Szemeredi-Gowers-Tao theorem
[
Tao08
, 5.4]
, which is behind
Lemma
2.13
below.

2.1
Broad
\bigwedge
-definable subfields and subgroups

We first recall some known consequences of the fact that
\mathbb{K}
is an
ultraproduct of characteristic 0 fields.

First, a version of the sum-product theorem in this context.
Write
\mathbb{G}_{a}
and
\mathbb{G}_{m}
for the additive and multiplicative algebraic groups
(over
\mathbb{K}
).

Fact 2.1
.

Suppose
A\leq\mathbb{G}_{a}
is a
\bigwedge
-internal subgroup with
\boldsymbol{\delta}(A)<\infty

and
M\leq\mathbb{G}_{m}
is a broad
\bigwedge
-internal subgroup
and
M\cdot A\subseteq A
.
Then
A
is trivial.

Proof.

We give the special case for this setting of

[
Wag20
, Corollary 3.10]
.

Let
M^{\prime}:=M-M
and let
R_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}M^{\prime}
, so
R:=\bigcup_{n<\omega}R_{n}
is a subring of
\mathbb{K}
.
Suppose
A
is non-trivial, say
a\in A\setminus\{0\}
.
Then
r\mapsto ra
is an internal injection
R\rightarrow A
,
so
\boldsymbol{\delta}(R):=\sup_{n}\boldsymbol{\delta}(R_{n})\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(A)<\infty
, so say
m\in\omega
is such that
2\boldsymbol{\delta}(R_{m})>\boldsymbol{\delta}(R)
. Let
X:=R_{m}
.
Then for
r,s\in R\setminus\{0\}
, the map
X^{2}\rightarrow R
given by

(x,y)\mapsto rx+sy
is not injective, so we have some equality
rx+sy=rx^{\prime}+sy^{\prime}
with say
y\neq y^{\prime}
, so
\frac{s}{r}=\frac{x-x^{\prime}}{y^{\prime}-y}
. So we find
that
\frac{X-X}{X-X}
is the fraction field of
R
. So we have a broad

\bigwedge
-internal subfield of
\mathbb{K}
, which by sum-product
(
[
Wag20
, Proposition 3.1]
or
[
Hru13
, Example 3.5]
)
is internal. But
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}}
, and
\mathbb{C}
contains no finite subfield.
∎

Next, a version of
[
BGT11
, Proposition 6.2]
.

Fact 2.2
.

A simple linear algebraic group
G
over
\mathbb{K}
has no
\bigwedge
-internal
broad Zariski-dense subgroup.

Proof.

If
H
is such a subgroup, then by
[
Hru13
, Theorem 3.1]
there is a
broad internal dense subgroup
H_{1}\leq G
. By Jordan’s theorem

[
LP11
, Theorem 0.1]
,
H_{1}
has an abelian subgroup of finite index, and
then by density so does
G
.
But
G
is infinite since
H
is broad, so this contradicts simplicity.
∎

2.2
Broad subgroups in homomorphic weak position

Definition 2.3
.

If
G
is an algebraic group over
\mathbb{K}
,
and
X\subseteq G(\mathbb{K})
is a broad
\bigwedge
-internal subset,
say
X
is in
homomorphic weak general position
(
hwgp
) in
G

if
X/N
is broad for any proper normal algebraic subgroup
N\triangleleft G
.

Remark 2.4
.

If
X\subseteq G(\mathbb{K})
is hwgp in
G
and
\theta:G\twoheadrightarrow H

is a surjective homomorphism of algebraic groups, then
\theta(X)
is hwgp
in
H(\mathbb{K})
.

Let
G
be an algebraic group. The
radical

\operatorname{rad}(G)
of
G
is the
largest solvable connected algebraic subgroup of
G
.

G
is
semisimple
if
\operatorname{rad}(G)
is trivial, and
almost simple

if
G/Z(G)
is simple.

Fact 2.5

(
[
Mil17
, 21.51]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected semisimple linear algebraic group. Then there are
almost simple algebraic groups
G_{1},\dots,G_{k}
and an epimorphism
\pi:G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{k}\to G
with finite kernel.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
G
be a connected algebraic group over
\mathbb{K}
.
Let
H\leq G(\mathbb{K})
be a broad hwgp Zariski-dense
\bigwedge
-internal
subgroup.
Then
G
is nilpotent.

Proof.

First, we reduce to the case that
G
is linear.
By e.g.
[
Ros56
, Theorem 13]
,
G/Z(G)
is linear.
If
Z(G)=G
, we are done.
Else,
H/Z(G)
is a broad hwgp Zariski-dense
\bigwedge
-internal subgroup of

G/Z(G)
, and if
G/Z(G)
is nilpotent then so is
G
.
So we may assume that
G
is linear, say
G\leq\operatorname{GL}_{n}
.

Step I
:
G
is solvable.

Suppose not; then
\operatorname{rad}(G)
is a proper normal algebraic subgroup of
G
, so
by hwgp
H/\operatorname{rad}(G)
is broad. By Fact
2.5
, there are almost
simple algebraic groups
G_{1},\dots,G_{k}
and an epimorphism
\pi:G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{k}\to G/\operatorname{rad}(G)
with finite kernel.

Put
H_{1}:=\pi^{-1}(H/\operatorname{rad}(G))
. As
\ker(\pi)
is finite and
H
is broad,

H_{1}
is broad as well. Now
H_{1}
embeds into
\pi_{1}(H_{1})\times\dots\times\pi_{k}(H_{1})
(where
\pi_{i}:G_{1}\times\dots\times G_{k}\to G_{i}
are the
projections) via
h\mapsto(\pi_{1}(h),\dots,\pi_{k}(h))
. Thus, by additivity
of
\boldsymbol{\delta}
we have that
\pi_{i}(H_{1})
is broad for some
i
.
Now
Z(G_{i})
is finite, since otherwise

\pi(0\times 0\times...\times Z(G_{i})\times...\times 0\times 0)
is an
infinite abelian normal subgroup of
G/\operatorname{rad}(G)
, contradicting triviality of

\operatorname{rad}(G/\operatorname{rad}(G))
.
So
\pi_{i}(H_{1})/Z(G_{i})
is also broad.

Now
H_{1}
is Zariski-dense in
\prod_{i}G_{i}
. Indeed,
if
U\subseteq\prod_{i}G_{i}
is Zariski-open and disjoint from
H_{1}
,
then
\pi(U)\subseteq G/\operatorname{rad}(G)
is disjoint from
H/\operatorname{rad}(G)
,
but
\dim(\pi(U))=\dim(U)=\dim(\prod_{i}G_{i})=\dim(G/\operatorname{rad}(G))
,
so this contradicts Zariski-density of
H/\operatorname{rad}(G)
.

Hence
\pi_{i}(H_{1})/Z(G_{i})
is Zariski-dense in
G_{i}/Z(G_{i})
.
As
G_{i}/Z(G_{i})
is simple and linear, this contradicts Fact

2.2
.

Step II
:
G
is nilpotent.

We proceed by translating the proof of
[
BG11b
]
.
By Maltsev, by conjugating by an element of
\operatorname{GL}_{n}
, we may assume that
G

is a subgroup of
\operatorname{Upp}_{n}
.

We argue by induction on
n
.

Let
\pi_{1}:\operatorname{Upp}_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{Upp}_{n-1}
be the homomorphism defined by
deleting the first row and first column,
and
\pi_{2}:\operatorname{Upp}_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{Upp}_{n-1}
the homomorphism defined by
deleting the last row and last column.

Let
h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\in H
.
Let
h:=[h_{1},h^{\prime}]
where

h^{\prime}:=[h_{2},\ldots,[h_{n-1},h_{n}]\ldots]
.
We show
h=1
.

Now for
i=1,2
we argue that
\pi_{i}(G)
is nilpotent of step
n-1
:
if
\pi_{i}(G)=0
, this is clear, and otherwise since
H
is hwgp,

\pi_{i}(H)\leq\pi_{i}(G)
is a broad hwgp Zariski-dense

\bigwedge
-internal subgroup, and we apply the inductive hypothesis.

Hence both
h
and
h^{\prime}
are in
N:=\ker\pi_{1}\cap\ker\pi_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\ldots&0&*\\
0&1&\ldots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\ldots&0&1\end{pmatrix}
.
The conjugation action of
X:=(x_{ij})_{ij}\in G
on
N
is by multiplication
in the top-right corner by
\theta(X):=\frac{x_{11}}{x_{nn}}
.
Note that
\theta:G\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{m}
is a homomorphism.
Let
\alpha:N\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}
be the isomorphism
\alpha((x_{ij})_{ij}):=x_{1n}
.

Suppose first that
\ker\theta
is a proper subgroup of
G
.
Then since
H
is hwgp,
\theta(H)
is a broad
\bigwedge
-internal
subgroup of
\mathbb{G}_{m}(\mathbb{K})
.
But
\theta(H)
acts by multiplication on
\alpha(H\cap N)
via
conjugation, so by Fact
2.1
,
H\cap N
is trivial, so
h=1
.

It remains to consider the case
\ker\theta=G
.
Then
G
commutes with
N
, so in particular
h=[h_{1},h^{\prime}]=1
.

So we conclude that
H
is nilpotent of step
n
, and then by Zariski
density so is
G
.
∎

2.3
Coarse-approximate subgroups

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(G;\cdot)
be an internal group (meaning that
G
is an internal subset
of some
S^{\mathcal{U}}
and the graph of the group operation
\Gamma_{G}\subseteq G^{3}

is also internal), and let

X\subseteq G
be internal. We call
X
a
coarsely approximate subgroup

if
X=X^{-1}
,
\mathsf{1}\in X
,
X
is broad, and
XX\subseteq KX
where

K\subseteq G
is internal with
\boldsymbol{\delta}(K)=0
.

Proposition 2.8
.

If
X\subseteq G
is a coarsely approximate subgroup, then there exists a

\bigwedge
-internal subgroup
H\supseteq X
with
\boldsymbol{\delta}(H)=\boldsymbol{\delta}(X)
.
Conversely, if
H\leq G
is a broad
\bigwedge
-internal subgroup,
then there exists a coarsely approximate subgroup
X\subseteq H
with

\boldsymbol{\delta}(X)=\boldsymbol{\delta}(H)
.

Proof.

Suppose
X
is a coarsely approximate subgroup and
XX\subseteq KX
, where
\boldsymbol{\delta}(K)=0
.
We can assume
\boldsymbol{\delta}(X)=1
.
Let
\nu:=\lfloor\log_{2}(\log_{|K|}\xi)\rfloor\in\mathbb{N}^{\mathcal{U}}
.
Then
\nu>\mathbb{N}
since
\boldsymbol{\delta}(K)=0
, but
\boldsymbol{\delta}(|K|^{2^{\nu}})\leq 1
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
Y_{n}:=X^{2^{\nu-n}}
.
(Explicitly, if
X=\prod_{s\to\mathcal{U}}X_{s}
and
\nu=\lim_{s\to\mathcal{U}}\nu_{s}

with
\nu_{s}\in\mathbb{N}
, then
Y_{n}=\prod_{s\to\mathcal{U}}X_{s}^{2^{\nu_{s}-n}}
.)
Then
Y_{n}Y_{n}=Y_{n-1}
, and
\boldsymbol{\delta}(Y_{n})\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(K^{2^{\nu-n}-1}X)\leq 2^{-n}+1
.
So
H:=\bigcap_{n}Y_{n}
is as required.

Suppose conversely that
H
is a broad
\bigwedge
-internal subgroup. By
\aleph_{1}
-compactness (see Section
1.1
), there is
an internal subset
X\subseteq H
with
\boldsymbol{\delta}(X)=\boldsymbol{\delta}(H)
. We can assume
that
X
is closed under inverse and contains the identity. Now
X^{3}\subseteq H^{3}=H
, so
\boldsymbol{\delta}(X^{3})=\boldsymbol{\delta}(X)
. It follows by

[
Tao08
, Corollary 3.11]
that
X^{3}
is a coarsely approximate subgroup.
∎

2.4
Weak general position

Definition 2.9
.

An internal subset
X
of an irreducible algebraic variety
V
is in

weak general position
(for short:
X
is
wgp
) in
V
if for
any proper subvariety
W\subseteq V
(over
\mathbb{K}
),
\boldsymbol{\delta}(X\cap W)<\boldsymbol{\delta}(X)
.

Given
\eta>0
, we say
X
is wgp with
gap

\eta
if moreover

\boldsymbol{\delta}(X\cap W)\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(X)-\eta
for every
W
. We say
X
is
wgp
with a gap
if this holds for some
\eta>0
.

(See the proof of Theorem
1.2
below for how this relates to the
definition of weak
(\alpha,\tau)
-general position in the introduction.)

Lemma 2.10
.

A broad internal wgp subset of an algebraic group is hwgp.

Proof.

Suppose
X\subseteq G
is not hwgp, so say
\boldsymbol{\delta}(X/H)=0
where
H<G
is a
proper normal algebraic subgroup. Now for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
|X/H|<\xi^{\frac{1}{n}}
and hence, by the pigeonhole principle
|X\cap\gamma H|\geq|X|\xi^{-\frac{1}{n}}
for some
\gamma\in G
.
Applying
\aleph_{1}
-compactness, we find
\gamma\in G
with
\boldsymbol{\delta}(X\cap\gamma H)=\boldsymbol{\delta}(X)

Note that for any normal subgroup

\sum_{b\in D,i\in I_{b}}\frac{t_{b}p_{b,i}}{q_{b,i}}=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{b\in D,i\in I_{b}}\frac{t}{q_{b,i}}(t_{b}p_{b,i}),

with
\sum_{b\in D,i\in I_{b}}\frac{t}{q_{b,i}}(t_{b}p_{b,i})\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Moreover,

\left|\sum_{b\in D,i\in I_{b}}\frac{t}{q_{b,i}}(t_{b}p_{b,i})\right|\leq|C||D|N\cdot\max\{|\frac{t}{q_{b,i}}p_{b,i}|:b,i\}\leq|C||D|Nt^{2}.

Therefore,
x\in\mathcal{A}_{t^{2}|C||D|N}(C)/t
.
∎

Claim 4.16
.

Suppose
A
,
B
, and
C
are subsets of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}})
such that
A
and
C
are internal,
B=A\oplus C
(i.e. for every
b\in B
there are unique
a\in A
and
c\in C
with
a+b=c
),
0<\boldsymbol{\delta}(A),\boldsymbol{\delta}(C)<\infty
and
\exp
injective on
B
. Suppose
\exp(C)
is wgp with gap
\eta^{\prime}
in
G_{n-1}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}})
, then
\exp(B)
is also wgp with gap
\eta^{\prime}
in
G_{n-1}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}})
.

Proof.

First note that
\boldsymbol{\delta}(B)=\boldsymbol{\delta}(C)+\boldsymbol{\delta}(A)
.
Let
W
be a proper subvariety of
G_{n-1}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}})
. Then

\exp^{-1}(W)\cap B=\bigsqcup_{a\in A}\exp^{-1}(W)\cap(a+C),

where
\bigsqcup
denotes disjoint union. Therefore
\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(W)\cap B)\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(A)+\sup\{\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(W)\cap(a+C)):a\in A\}=\boldsymbol{\delta}(A)+\sup\{\boldsymbol{\delta}((\exp^{-1}(W)-a)\cap C):a\in A\}
. Since
{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}
is abelian,
\exp
is a homomorphism, so we get
\exp^{-1}(W)-a=\exp^{-1}(W-\exp(a))
. As
\exp(C)
is wgp with gap
\eta^{\prime}
, we have
\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(W-\exp(a))\cap C)\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(C)-\eta^{\prime}
for all
a\in A
. We conclude that
\boldsymbol{\delta}(W\cap\exp(B))=\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(W)\cap B)\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(A)+\boldsymbol{\delta}(C)-\eta^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{\delta}(B)-\eta^{\prime}
.
∎

Recall that
X
is the tuple of
\ell
independent generics in
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
B_{m}=\langle b_{m,j}:j\rangle_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
,
B_{m,N}=\mathcal{A}_{N^{m}}(\{b_{m,j}:j\})
,
P_{N}(X)=\sum_{m\leq n}B_{m,N}
and
P=\sum_{m\leq n}B_{m,{N^{*}}}
where
N^{*}=\lim_{i\to\mathcal{U}}N_{i}
. Let
(e_{i})_{i<s}
be as given by Claim
4.14
and
put
E=\langle e_{i}:i<s\rangle_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
Consider
B_{m}\cap E
, a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-subspace of
B_{m}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}
. Take a basis
U_{m}=U_{m}^{0}\cup U_{m}^{1}\cup U_{m}^{2}
of
B_{m}
, where
U_{m}^{0}
is a basis of
B_{m}\cap E
and
U_{m}^{0}\cup U_{m}^{1}
is a basis of
B_{m}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}
and such that
U_{n}^{0}
is
\{e_{i}:i<s\}
(note that
\{e_{i}:i<s\}
is indeed a basis of
B_{n}\cap E=E
). Applying Claim
4.15
twice, for
m<n
we obtain
0<t_{m}<t^{\prime}_{m}
not depending on
N
such that

B_{m,N}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{t^{\prime}_{m}N^{m}}(U_{m})/t_{m}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{(t^{\prime}_{m})^{2}N^{m}}(\{b_{m,j}:j\})/(t_{m})^{2}.

Again using Claim
4.15
twice, since
\langle\{b_{n,j}:j\}\cup\bigcup_{m<n}U_{m}^{0}\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}=\langle U_{n}\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}=\langle\{b_{n,j}:j\}\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}}
, we can find
0<t_{n}<t_{n}^{\prime}
such that

B_{n,N}+\sum_{m<n}\mathcal{A}_{t^{\prime}_{m}N^{m}}(U_{m}^{0})/t_{m}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{t^{\prime}_{n}N^{n}}(U_{n})/t_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{(t^{\prime}_{n})^{2}N^{n}}(\{b_{n,j}:j\})/(t_{n})^{2}.

Let
t:=\prod_{m\leq n}(t_{m})^{2}
and
t^{\prime}:=\prod_{m\leq n}(t^{\prime}_{m})^{2}
. Then for any
m\leq n
we have
\mathcal{A}_{(t^{\prime}_{m})^{2}N^{m}}(\{b_{m,j}:j\})/(t_{m})^{2}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{(t^{\prime}N)^{m}}(\{b_{m,j}:j\})/t^{m}
as
(t_{m})^{2}|t^{m}
and
\frac{t^{m}}{(t_{m})^{2}}(t_{m}^{\prime})^{2}\leq(t^{\prime})^{m}
. Also, using multilinearity of Lie monomials, we get

\sum_{m\leq n}\mathcal{A}_{(t^{\prime}N)^{m}}(\{b_{m,j}:j\})/t^{m}=P_{t^{\prime}N}(X/t).

Let

C_{N}:=\mathcal{A}_{t^{\prime}_{n}N^{n}}(U_{n})/t_{n}+\sum_{m<n}\mathcal{A}_{t^{\prime}_{m}N^{m}}(U_{m}^{1})/t_{m}\text{ and }D_{N}:=\sum_{m<n}\mathcal{A}_{t^{\prime}_{m}N^{m}}(U_{m}^{2})/t_{m}.

As
C_{N}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}
and
\langle D_{N}\rangle_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}=\{0\}
, we have
C_{N}+D_{N}=C_{N}\oplus D_{N}
. Then

\displaystyle C_{N}\oplus D_{N}

\displaystyle\supseteq B_{n,N}+\sum_{m<n}\mathcal{A}_{t^{\prime}_{m}N^{m}}(U_{m}^{0})/t_{m}+\sum_{m<n}\mathcal{A}_{t^{\prime}_{m}N^{m}}(U_{m}^{1})/t_{m}+\sum_{m<n}\mathcal{A}_{t^{\prime}_{m}N^{m}}(U_{m}^{2})/t_{m}

\displaystyle=B_{n,N}+\sum_{m<n}\mathcal{A}_{t^{\prime}_{m}N^{m}}(U_{m})/t_{m}\supseteq\sum_{m\leq n}B_{m,N},

and

\displaystyle C_{N}\oplus D_{N}

\displaystyle\subseteq\sum_{m\leq n}\mathcal{A}_{t^{\prime}_{m}N^{m}}(U_{m})/t_{m}

\displaystyle\subseteq\sum_{m\leq n}\mathcal{A}_{(t^{\prime}_{m})^{2}N^{m}}(\{b_{m,j}:j\})/(t_{m})^{2}

\displaystyle\subseteq\sum_{m\leq n}\mathcal{A}_{(t^{\prime}N)^{m}}(\{b_{m,j}:j\})/t^{m}=P_{t^{\prime}N}(X/t),

so

P_{N}(X)=\sum_{m\leq n}B_{m,N}\subseteq C_{N}\oplus D_{N}\subseteq P_{t^{\prime}N}(X/t).

Hence

P=P_{N^{*}}(X)=\sum_{m\leq n}B_{m,{N^{*}}}\subseteq C_{N^{*}}\oplus D_{N^{*}}\subseteq P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X/t).

(1)

Claim 4.17
.

\boldsymbol{\delta}(P)=\boldsymbol{\delta}(C_{N^{*}})+\boldsymbol{\delta}(D_{N^{*}})
and
\boldsymbol{\delta}(\pi(P))=\boldsymbol{\delta}(D_{N^{*}})=\boldsymbol{\delta}(\pi(P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X/t)))
where
\pi:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}
is the quotient map.

Proof.

Note that
P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X)=\sum_{m\leq n}\mathcal{A}_{(t^{\prime})^{m}(N^{*})^{m}}(\{b_{m,j}:j\})
is a sum of generalised arithmetic progressions. Thus there are finite sets
F_{m}
such that

\mathcal{A}_{(t^{\prime})^{m}(N^{*})^{m}}(\{b_{m,j}:j\})\subseteq F_{m}+\mathcal{A}_{(N^{*})^{m}}(\{b_{m,j}:j\})

for all
m\leq n
. Hence,

\displaystyle P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\leq n}\mathcal{A}_{(t^{\prime})^{m}(N^{*})^{m}}(\{b_{m,j}:j\})

\displaystyle\subseteq\sum_{m\leq n}F_{m}+\sum_{m\leq n}\mathcal{A}_{(N^{*})^{m}}(\{b_{m,j}:j\})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\leq n}F_{m}+P_{N^{*}}(X).

As
\sum_{m\leq n}F_{m}
is a finite set, we conclude
\boldsymbol{\delta}(P_{N^{*}}(X))=\boldsymbol{\delta}(P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X))=\boldsymbol{\delta}(P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X/t))
, where the last equality holds since
X
and
X/t
have the same type over
C_{0}
(as they are both generic in
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ell}
over
C_{0}
), so
P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X/t)
is the image of
P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X)
under an internal automorphism of
\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}}
.

Also, by (
1
) we have

\boldsymbol{\delta}(P)\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(C_{N^{*}})+\boldsymbol{\delta}(D_{N^{*}})\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X/t)),

which combined with the above equality gives
\boldsymbol{\delta}(P)=\boldsymbol{\delta}(C_{N^{*}})+\boldsymbol{\delta}(D_{N^{*}})=\boldsymbol{\delta}(P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X/t)).

For the second part of the claim, note that, as
\pi:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism, we have
\pi(P_{N^{*}}(X))=P^{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}}_{N^{*}}(\pi(X))
and
\pi(P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X/t))=P^{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}}_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(\pi(X)/t)
.
By the same arguments as above,

\boldsymbol{\delta}(P^{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}}_{N^{*}}(\pi(X)))=\boldsymbol{\delta}(P^{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}}_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(\pi(X)))=\boldsymbol{\delta}(P^{{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}}_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(\pi(X)/t)).
Therefore,

\boldsymbol{\delta}(\pi(P_{N^{*}}(X)))=\boldsymbol{\delta}(\pi(P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X/t)))
. On the other hand, as
\pi(C_{N^{*}})=\{0\}
and
\pi
is injective on
D_{N^{*}}
, we get by (
1
) that

\boldsymbol{\delta}(\pi(P_{N^{*}}(X)))\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(\pi(D_{N^{*}}))=\boldsymbol{\delta}(D_{N^{*}})\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(\pi(P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X/t))),

so we get the desired equalities.
∎

Claim 4.18
.

\exp(C_{N^{*}})
is wgp with gap
\eta_{1}
in
G_{n-1}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}})
.

Proof.

By definition
C_{N^{*}}=\mathcal{A}_{t^{\prime}_{n}(N^{*})^{n}}(U_{n}^{0})/t_{n}\oplus\sum_{m\leq n}\mathcal{A}_{t^{\prime}_{m}(N^{*})^{m}}(U_{m}^{1})/t_{m}
. Recall that
U_{n}^{0}
is
\{e_{i}:i<s\}
, where
\{\exp(e_{i}):i<s\}
is a set of
s
independent generics in
G_{n-1}
over
C_{0}
by Claim
4.14
. Hence
\{\exp(e_{i}/t_{n}):i<s\}
are also independent and generic in
G_{n-1}
. Let
E_{N^{*}}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{A}_{t^{\prime}_{n}(N^{*})^{n}}(\{e_{i}/t_{n},i<s\})=\mathcal{A}_{t^{\prime}_{n}(N^{*})^{n}}(U_{n}^{0})/t_{n}
. Thus, by the choice of
s
and
\eta_{1}
we know
\exp(E_{N^{*}}^{\prime})
is wgp with gap

.

As
C_{N^{*}}=E_{N^{*}}^{\prime}\oplus\sum_{m\leq n}\mathcal{A}_{t^{\prime}_{m}(N^{*})^{m}}(U_{m}^{1})/t_{m}
and

.

Now we are ready to prove the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
4.12
.

Let
W
be a proper subvariety of
G
over
\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}}
and consider
\exp^{-1}(W)\cap P
; we want to show

\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(W)\cap P)\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(P)-\eta.

There are two cases, the first case is that
W/G_{n-1}
is a proper subvariety of
G/G_{n-1}
.

Claim 4.19
.

Let
W
be a proper subvariety of
G
over
\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}}
. Suppose
W/G_{n-1}
is a proper subvariety of
G/G_{n-1}
. Then
\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(W)\cap P)\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(P)-\eta
.

Proof.

Recall that we chose
X
to have length
|X|\geq l^{\prime}
, where
l^{\prime}=l(G/G_{n-1})
is as in Theorem
4.12
, and
\eta_{2}=\eta(G/G_{n-1})
. We aim to obtain that
\pi(P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X/t))
is wgp.
As
X/t
is a tuple of independent generics in
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\pi:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}
is definable over
C_{0}
,
\pi(X/t)
is a tuple of independent generics in
{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}
over
\Delta
. Moreover
\exp(\pi(X/t))
is a tuple of independent generics in
G/G_{n-1}
as
\exp(X)
is a tuple of independent generics in
G
and
\exp(\pi(x/t))=h_{x}
is interalgebraic with
\exp(\pi(x))
as
(h_{x})^{t}=\exp(\pi(x))
for all
x\in X
.
Since
|\pi(X/t)|\geq\ell^{\prime}
and
\pi(P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X/t))
is the nilbox in
{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}
generated by
\pi(X/t)
of length
t^{\prime}N^{*}
, by the inductive hypothesis (and by the choice of
\ell^{\prime}
and
\eta_{2}
),
\pi(P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X/t))
is wgp with gap
\eta_{2}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(W)\cap P)

\displaystyle\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(W)\cap(C_{N^{*}}\oplus D_{N^{*}}))

\displaystyle\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(\pi(\exp^{-1}(W)\cap(C_{N^{*}}\oplus D_{N^{*}})))+\boldsymbol{\delta}(C_{N^{*}})

\displaystyle\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(\pi(\exp^{-1}(W)\cap P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X/t)))+\boldsymbol{\delta}(C_{N^{*}})\mbox{ (by (\ref{eq*}))}

\displaystyle\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(\pi(\exp^{-1}(W))\cap\pi(P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X/t)))+\boldsymbol{\delta}(C_{N^{*}})

\displaystyle\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(\pi(P_{t^{\prime}N^{*}}(X/t)))+\boldsymbol{\delta}(C_{N^{*}})-\eta_{2}\quad(\text{by IH})

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\delta}(D_{N^{*}})+\boldsymbol{\delta}(C_{N^{*}})-\eta_{2}=\boldsymbol{\delta}(P)-\eta_{2}\quad(\text{by Claim \ref{samefin}})

\displaystyle\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(P)-\eta.\qed

Now we consider the second case, i.e.
\dim(W/G_{n-1})=\dim(G/G_{n-1})
.
Let

V^{\prime}:=\{w\in W:\dim(wG_{n-1}\cap W)=\dim(G_{n-1})\}\text{ and }V:=\{w\in W:\dim(wG_{n-1}\cap W)<\dim(G_{n-1})\}.

Then
\dim(G)>\dim(V^{\prime})=\dim(G_{n-1})+\dim(V^{\prime}/G_{n-1})
. Hence
\dim(V^{\prime}/G_{n-1})<\dim(G/G_{n-1})
. Consider
(V^{\prime})^{Zar}
a proper subvariety of
G
. Then

\dim\left((V^{\prime})^{Zar}/G_{n-1}\right)\leq\dim\left((V^{\prime}/G_{n-1})^{Zar}\right)=\dim(V^{\prime}/G_{n-1})<\dim(G/G_{n-1}).

By Claim
4.19
applied to
(V^{\prime})^{Zar}
,
\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(V^{\prime})\cap P)\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}((V^{\prime})^{Zar})\cap P)\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(P)-\eta
. As

\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(W)\cap P)=\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(V\cup V^{\prime})\cap P)=\max\{\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(V)\cap P),\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(V^{\prime})\cap P)\},

we only need to show that
\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(V)\cap P)\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(P)-\eta
. Note that for
w,w^{\prime}\in W
with
wG_{n-1}=w^{\prime}G_{n-1}
we have
w\in V\iff w^{\prime}\in V
, hence
vG_{n-1}\cap V=vG_{n-1}\cap W
is Zariski-closed for all
v\in V
.

Claim 4.20
.

Suppose
V
is a constructible subset of
G
such that
vG_{n-1}\cap V
is Zariski-closed and
\dim(vG_{n-1}\cap V)<\dim(G_{n-1})
for all
v\in V
. Then
\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(V)\cap P)\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(P)-\eta
.

Proof.

Note that, by (
1
),
\exp^{-1}(V)\cap P\subseteq\exp^{-1}(V)\cap(C_{N^{*}}\oplus D_{N^{*}})
. Therefore, it is enough to show that
\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(V)\cap(C_{N^{*}}\oplus D_{N^{*}}))\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(C_{N^{*}})+\boldsymbol{\delta}(D_{N^{*}})-\eta=\boldsymbol{\delta}(P)-\eta
.
As
\exp^{-1}(V)\cap(C_{N^{*}}\oplus D_{N^{*}})=\bigsqcup_{d\in D_{N^{*}}}\exp^{-1}(V)\cap(C_{N^{*}}+d)
, we get

\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(V)\cap(C_{N^{*}}\oplus D_{N^{*}}))\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(D_{N^{*}})+\sup\{\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(V)\cap(C_{N^{*}}+d)):d\in D_{N^{*}}\}.

It is enough to show that
\sup\{\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(V)\cap(C_{N^{*}}+d)):d\in D_{N^{*}}\}\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(C_{N^{*}})-\eta
.

We claim that

(\exp^{-1}(V)-d)\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}\subseteq\exp^{-1}(\exp(d)^{-1}V\cap G_{n-1}).

(2)

Let
a-d\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}
with
\exp(a)\in V
. Since
a-d\in{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}
, by the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula
\exp(d)\exp(a-d)=\exp(d+a-d)=\exp(a)
. Therefore,
\exp(a-d)=\exp(d)^{-1}\exp(a)\in\exp(d)^{-1}V\cap G_{n-1}
as desired. Now we want to show that
\dim(\exp(d)^{-1}V\cap G_{n-1})<\dim(G_{n-1})
. We may assume
\exp(d)^{-1}V\cap G_{n-1}\neq\emptyset
, then there is
g\in G_{n-1}
such that
v_{d}:=\exp(d)g\in V
. Hence

\dim(\exp(d)^{-1}V\cap G_{n-1})=\dim(V\cap\exp(d)G_{n-1})=\dim(V\cap v_{d}G_{n-1})<\dim(G_{n-1})

by our assumption on
V
.

In conclusion, we have a definable family
\{\exp(d)^{-1}V\cap G_{n-1}:d\in D_{N^{*}}\}
of proper subvarieties of
G_{n-1}
. As
\exp(C_{N^{*}})
is wgp with gap
\eta_{1}
, we have

\sup\{\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(\exp(d)^{-1}V\cap G_{n-1})\cap C_{N^{*}}):d\in D_{N^{*}}\}\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(C_{N^{*}})-\eta_{1}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sup\{\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(V)\cap(C_{N^{*}}+d)):d\in D_{N^{*}}\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\boldsymbol{\delta}((\exp^{-1}(V)-d)\cap C_{N^{*}}):d\in D_{N^{*}}\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\boldsymbol{\delta}((\exp^{-1}(V)-d)\cap{\mathfrak{g}}_{n-1}\cap C_{N^{*}}):d\in D_{N^{*}}\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\{\boldsymbol{\delta}(\exp^{-1}(\exp(d)^{-1}V\cap G_{n-1})\cap C_{N^{*}}):d\in D_{N^{*}}\}\mbox{ (by (\ref{eq7}))}

\displaystyle\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(C_{N^{*}})-\eta_{1}\leq\boldsymbol{\delta}(C_{N^{*}})-\eta.\qed

This concludes the proof of Theorem
4.12
.

Functions on products of pseudocompact spaces
Evgenii Reznichenko

Abstract
For every natural number n n , any continuous function on the product of X 1 × X 2 × … × X n X_{1}\times X_{2}\times...\times X_{n} pseudocompact spaces extends to a separately continuous function on the product β ⁡ X 1 × β ⁡ X 2 × … × β ⁡ X n \operatorname{\beta}X_{1}\times\operatorname{\beta}X_{2}\times...\times\operatorname{\beta}X_{n} of their Stone-Cech compactifications.

Introduction

In
[

it was proved that any continuous function
f:X_{1}\times X_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
,
defined on the product of pseudocompact spaces
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
, extends to a
separately continuous function
\hat{f}:\operatorname{\beta}X_{1}\times\operatorname{\beta}X_{2}\to\mathbb{R}

defined on the product of the Stone-Cech compactifications
\operatorname{\beta}X_{1}
and
\operatorname{\beta}X_{2}
.
In
[
2
]
this result was extended to three factors:
any continuous function
f:X_{1}\times X_{2}\times X_{3}\to\mathbb{R}
,
defined on the product of pseudocompact spaces
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
,
X_{3}
, extends to
separately continuous function
\hat{f}:\operatorname{\beta}X_{1}\times\operatorname{\beta}X_{2}\times\operatorname{\beta}X_{3}\to\mathbb{R}

defined on the product of Stone-Cech compactifications
\operatorname{\beta}X_{1}
,
\operatorname{\beta}X_{2}
,
\operatorname{\beta}X_{3}
.
The question was also raised of whether this result can be extended to an arbitrary finite number of factors. This paper gives a positive answer to this question: for every natural number
n
, any continuous function on the product
X_{1}\times X_{2}\times...\times X_{n}
of pseudocompact spaces can be extended to a separately continuous function on

\operatorname{\beta}X_{1}\times\operatorname{\beta}X_{2}\times...\times\operatorname{\beta}X_{n}
; see Theorem
4
.

Recall the Gliksberg theorem
[
3
]
: given pseudocompact
spaces
X
and
Y
, any continuous function can be extended to a continuous function defined on
\operatorname{\beta}X\times\operatorname{\beta}Y
if and only if
X\times Y
is a pseudocompact.
As shown in
[
1
]
, the Grothendieck theorem
[
4
]

(for every countably compact
X
any countably compact subspace
Y
of
C_{p}(X)
is compact) is, in fact, equivalent to the statement:
any separately continuous function defined on the product
X\times Y
of countably compact spaces can be extended to a separately
continuous function defined on
\operatorname{\beta}X\times\operatorname{\beta}Y
.
The problem of extending functions from products of spaces to Stone-Cech compactifications was considered in detail in the case of two factors in
[
1
]
and in the case of a larger number of factors in
[
2
]
.
We collect information about the extension of functions from a product of spaces in the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Given spaces
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
, …,
X_{n}
, let
X=\prod_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
and
\hat{X}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{\beta}X_{i}
be topological products, and let

\Phi:X\to\mathbb{R}

(a)

If
X
is pseudocompact and
\Phi
is continuous, then
\Phi
extends to a continuous function
\hat{\Phi}:\hat{X}\to\mathbb{R}
.

(b)

If the spaces
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
, …,
X_{n}
are pseudocompact and
\Phi
is continuous, then
\Phi
extends to a separately continuous function
\hat{\Phi}:\hat{X}\to\mathbb{R}
.

(c)

If the spaces
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
, …,
X_{n}
are countably compact and
\Phi
is separately continuous, then
\Phi
extends to a separately continuous function
\hat{\Phi}:\hat{X}\to\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

(a) For
n=2
, this is exactly the Gliksberg theorem. For
n>2

(b) This is Theorem

(c) For
n=2
, this is exactly the Grothendieck theorem (in the product formulation). For
n>2
, the assertion follows from Grothendieck theorem and the fact that the product of a compact space and a countably compact space is a countably compact space (Theorem 3.16 of
[
1
]
).

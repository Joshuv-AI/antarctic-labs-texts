Observational constraints on the fractal cosmology
Mahnaz Asghari, Ahmad Sheykhi

Abstract
In this paper, we explore a fractal model of the universe proposed by Calcagni [JHEP 03 (2010)120] for a power-counting renormalizable field theory living in a fractal spacetime. Considering a timelike fractal profile, we derived field equations in fractal cosmology, in order to explore the structure formation and the expansion history in fractal universe. Numerical investigations based on matter power spectra diagrams report higher structure growth in fractal cosmology, being in contrast to local galaxy surveys. Additionally, according to the evolution of Hubble parameter diagrams, it can be understood that Hubble constant would decrease in fractal cosmology, which is also incompatible with low redshift estimations of H 0 H_{0} . So, concerning primary numerical studies, it seems that fractal cosmology is not capable to alleviate the tensions between local and global observational probes. Then, in pursuance of more accurate results, we constrain the fractal cosmology by observational data, including Planck cosmic microwave background (CMB), weak lensing, supernovae, baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO), and redshift-space distortions (RSD) data. The derived constraints on fractal dimension β \beta indicate that there is no considerable deviation from standard model of cosmology.

Field equations in a fractal universe

The total action in a fractal spacetime is given by
[
21
]

\displaystyle S=\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{\varrho}(x)\,\sqrt{-g}\big(R-2\mathrm{\Lambda}-\omega\partial_{\mu}v\partial^{\mu}v\big)}+S_{m}\,,

(1)

where
\omega
is the fractal parameter,
v
is the fractional
function, and
\mathrm{d}{\varrho}(x)
is a Lebesgue-Stieltjes
measure. It is possible to derive field equations from action
(
1
) similar to scalar-tensor theories. Thus, in a fractal
universe we obtain
[
21
]

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}(R-2\mathrm{\Lambda})+g_{\mu\nu}\frac{\Box{v}}{v}-\frac{\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}v}{v}

\displaystyle+\omega\Big(\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\partial_{\sigma}v\partial^{\sigma}v-\partial_{\mu}v\partial_{\nu}v\Big)=8\pi GT_{\mu\nu}\,.

(2)

Furthermore, continuity equation in a fractal spacetime takes the form
[
21
]

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\big(vT^{\mu}_{\nu}\big)-\partial_{\nu}v\mathcal{L}_{m}=0\,.

(3)

It should be noted that for
v=1
, standard equations in GR
will be recovered.
Here, we focus on a timelike fractal, then
v
is only time dependent given by

v=H_{0}^{\beta}\Big(\frac{a}{a_{0}}\Big)^{\beta}\,,

(4)

in which
\beta=4(1-\alpha)
is the fractal dimension, and the
parameter
\alpha
ranges as
0<\alpha\leq 1
.

We consider a fractal universe with
the following flat Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW)
metric in the synchronous gauge

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=a^{2}(\tau)\Big(-\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}+\big(\delta_{ij}+h_{ij}\big)\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j}\Big)\;,

(5)

in which

h_{ij}(\vec{x},\tau)=\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\,e^{i\vec{k}.\vec{x}}\bigg(\hat{k}_{i}\hat{k}_{j}h(\vec{k},\tau)+\Big(\hat{k}_{i}\hat{k}_{j}-\frac{1}{3}\delta_{ij}\Big)6\eta(\vec{k},\tau)\bigg),

with scalar perturbations
h
and
\eta
, and
\vec{k}=k\hat{k}

[
42
]
.
Then, considering the energy content of the universe as a perfect fluid
with
T_{\mu\nu}=\big(\rho+p\big)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+g_{\mu\nu}p
,
field equations in background level take the form

\displaystyle H^{2}\bigg(1+\beta-\frac{1}{6}\omega H_{0}^{2\beta}\beta^{2}\Big(\frac{a}{a_{0}}\Big)^{2\beta}\bigg)=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\sum_{i}\bar{\rho}_{i}\,,

(6)

\displaystyle(\beta+2)\frac{H^{\prime}}{a}+H^{2}\bigg(3+2\beta+\beta^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\omega H_{0}^{2\beta}\beta^{2}\Big(\frac{a}{a_{0}}\Big)^{2\beta}\bigg)

\displaystyle=-8\pi G\sum_{i}\bar{p}_{i}\,,

(7)

where a prime indicates a deviation with respect to the conformal
time. It can be easily seen that,
\beta=0
restores field
equations in standard cosmology. According to equation
(
6
), total density parameter can be find as

\mathrm{\Omega}_{\mathrm{tot}}=1+\beta-\frac{1}{6}\omega H_{0}^{2\beta}\beta^{2}\Big(\frac{a}{a_{0}}\Big)^{2\beta}\,,

(8)

where we have considered a universe filled with radiation (R),
baryons (B), dark matter (DM) and cosmological constant
(
\mathrm{\Lambda}
). Also field equations to linear order of perturbations
can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\Big(1+\frac{1}{2}\beta\Big)h^{\prime}-2k^{2}\eta=8\pi Ga^{2}\sum_{i}\delta\rho_{i}\,,

(9)

\displaystyle k^{2}\eta^{\prime}=4\pi Ga^{2}\sum_{i}\big(\bar{\rho}_{i}+\bar{p}_{i}\big)\theta_{i}\,,

(10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}h^{\prime\prime}+3\eta^{\prime\prime}+\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\big(h^{\prime}+6\eta^{\prime}\big)\Big(1+\frac{1}{2}\beta\Big)-k^{2}\eta=0\,,

(11)

\displaystyle\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\Big(2+\beta\Big)h^{\prime}+h^{\prime\prime}-2k^{2}\eta=-24\pi Ga^{2}\sum_{i}\delta p_{i}\,.

(12)

In addition, regarding equation (
3
), conservation
equations of fractal cosmology for
i
th component of the universe
in background and perturbation levels become

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}^{\prime}_{i}+\big(3+\beta\big)\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\big(\bar{\rho}_{i}+\bar{p}_{i}\big)=0\,,

(13)

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}_{i}=

\displaystyle-\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\big(3+\beta\big)\bigg[\delta_{i}\big(c^{2}_{si}-w_{i}\big)

\displaystyle+\big(c^{2}_{si}-c^{2}_{ai}\big)\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\big(3+\beta\big)\big(1+w_{i}\big)\frac{\theta_{i}}{k^{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle-\big(1+w_{i}\big)\theta_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\big(1+w_{i}\big)h^{\prime}\,,

(14)

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime}_{i}=

\displaystyle\theta_{i}\bigg[-\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\big(4+\beta\big)+\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\big(3+\beta\big)\big(1+w_{i}+c^{2}_{si}-c^{2}_{ai}\big)\bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{k^{2}c^{2}_{si}}{1+w_{i}}\delta_{i}\,.

(15)

Then, choosing
\beta=0
would recover equations in concordance

\mathrm{\Lambda}
CDM model.

5
Conclusions

We have considered a fractal model of the universe introduced by
Calcagni
[
21
,
22
]
, which is a power counting
renormalizable and also Lorentz invariant model. Taking into
account a timelike fractal, we have investigated the influence of
fractal model on observables, mainly power spectra and Hubble
constant, by using a modified version of the publicly available
CLASS code. Considering matter power spectra diagrams
in Fig.
2
, there is an enhancement in structure growth
for non-zero values of fractal dimension
\beta
,
being incompatible with low redshift structure formation.
Moreover, according to Fig.
3
,
the current value of Hubble parameter decreases in
fractal cosmology, which is inconsistent with local determinations
of
H_{0}
. Consequently, primary numerical results indicate that fractal
cosmology has no positive impact on relieving cosmological
tensions.

Furthermore, we put constraints on the parameters of the fractal
universe by utilizing current observations, chiefly Planck CMB,
weak lensing, supernovae, BAO, and RSD data.
Numerical results based on MCMC analysis, detect
no significant departure from standard cosmological model.
On the other hand, the model selection criterion AIC affirms that

\mathrm{\Lambda}
CDM model is more favored by observations,
however, the fractal model can not be ruled out.

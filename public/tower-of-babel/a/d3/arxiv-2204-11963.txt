Positive and negative exact boundary controllability results for the linear Biharmonic Schrödinger equation
Kaïs Ammari Hedi Bouzidi

Abstract
In this paper, we study the exact boundary controllability of the linear Biharmonic Schrödinger equation i ∂ t y = − ∂ x 4 y + γ ∂ x 2 y \displaystyle i\partial_{t}y=-\partial_{x}^{4}y+\gamma\partial_{x}^{2}y on a bounded domain with hinged boundary conditions and boundary control acts on the second spatial derivative at the left endpoint, where the parameter γ < 0 \displaystyle\gamma<0 . We prove that this system is exactly controllable in time T > 0 \displaystyle T>0 , if and only if, the parameter γ \displaystyle\gamma does not belong to a critical countable set of negative real numbers. The analysis in this work is based on spectral analysis together with the nonharmonic Fourier series method.

Introduction and main result

In this paper, we study the boundary controllability of
the linear fourth-order Schrödinger equation on the bounded interval
\displaystyle\left(0,\ell\right)
, where
\displaystyle\ell>0
. More precisely, we consider the following control system

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}y(t,x)=-\partial_{x}^{4}y(t,x)+\gamma\partial_{x}^{2}y(t,x),&(t,x)\in(0,T)\times(0,\ell),\\
y(t,0)=y(t,\ell)=\partial_{x}^{2}y(t,\ell)=0,\penalty\ \partial_{x}^{2}y(t,0)=f(t),&t\in(0,T),\\
y(0,x)=y_{0}(x),&x\in(0,\ell),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where the parameter
\displaystyle\gamma<0
,
\displaystyle f
is a control that acts on the left end
\displaystyle x=0
, and
the function
\displaystyle y_{0}
is the initial condition. For system (
1.1
), the appropriate control notion to study is the
exact controllability, which is defined as follows: System (
1.1
) is said to be exactly controllable
at time
\displaystyle T>0
if, given any initial state
\displaystyle y_{0}
, there exists a control
\displaystyle f
such that the corresponding
solution
\displaystyle y=y(t,x)
satisfies
\displaystyle y(T,.)=0
.

The fourth-order Schrödinger equation has been modeled by Karpman
[
8
]
, and Karpman
\displaystyle\&
Shagalov
[
9
]
in order to describe the propagation of intense laser beams in a bulk medium with Kerr nonlinearity when small fourth-order dispersion is taken into an account. Nowadays, equation (
1.1
) is often called the biharmonic Schrödinger equation
and has various applications in several areas of physics, such as nonlinear optics, plasma physics and nonuniform dielectric media, see
[
5
,
7
,
10
,
11
]
.

Notice that, in the case where
\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{4}y\equiv 0
, Equation (
1.1
) collapses to the standard second order Schrödinger equation. In this direction, the exact controllability of the second order Schrödinger equation has been extensively investigated, see for instance
[
1
,
3
,
4
]
. In the case where
\displaystyle\gamma=0
, Zheng and Zhongcheng
[
15
]
proved that the biharmonic Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) with clamped boundary conditions and Neumann boundary control is exactly controllable at time
\displaystyle T>0
. Later on, Wen et al.
[
14
]
established well-posedness and exact controllability results for a system described by the fourth order Schrödinger equation in (
1.1
) for
\displaystyle\gamma=0,
on a bounded domain of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}\penalty\ (n\geq 2)
with boundary control and collocated observation. Along similar lines, the same authors in
[
13
]
, extended these results to the case of a linear fourth-order multi-dimensional Schrödinger equation with hinged boundary control and collocated observation. Recently, in the case where
\displaystyle\gamma=1,
Capistrano-Filho and Cavalcante
[
6
]
proved global stabilization and exact controllability results for the biharmonic Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) on a periodic domain
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}
with internal control supported on an arbitrary sub-domain of
\displaystyle\mathbb{T}
. Whereas, in the case where
\displaystyle\gamma(x)\geq 0
, the authors
[
2
]
showed that the fourth-order Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) with clamped boundary conditions and boundary control acting on the first spatial derivative at one endpoint is exactly controllable at time
\displaystyle T>0
.

As we know, the exact controllability of System (
1.1
) with
\displaystyle\gamma<0
has not been studied in the literature yet. As we will see, that the parameter
\displaystyle\gamma
plays a key role in understanding the dynamics of System (
1.1
). Indeed, we first prove that the
eigenvalues
\displaystyle\left({\lambda_{n}}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
associated to
Problem (
1.1
) with
\displaystyle f(t)\equiv 0,
are bounded from below by the quantity
\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}
. Furthermore, we show that the finite number of negative eigenvalues
\displaystyle\left({\lambda_{n}}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
are algebraically simple, if and only if, the parameter
\displaystyle\gamma
does not belong to the following critical countable set of negative real numbers:

{{\Gamma^{*}}=\left\{-\,\frac{\pi^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\left(p^{2}+q^{2}\right)\penalty\ :\penalty\ p,q\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\penalty\ 1\leq p<q\right\}.}

(1.2)

Therefore, using the nonharmonic Fourier series method, we establish positive and negative exact controllability results for System (
1.1
). Namely, our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Given
\displaystyle T>0
and

\displaystyle y_{0}\in H^{-1}\left(0,\ell\right)
. Assume that
\displaystyle\gamma\not\in\Gamma^{*}
. Then, there
exists a control
\displaystyle f\in L^{2}(0,T)
such that the solution
\displaystyle y
of the

)
satisfies

Moreover, if
\displaystyle\gamma\in\Gamma^{*},
then System (
1.1
) is not exactly controllable at time
\displaystyle T>0

2
Spectral analysis

We consider the following spectral problem which arises by applying
separation of variables to system (
1.1
) with
\displaystyle f(t)\equiv 0
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{cases}\phi^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}-\gamma\phi^{\prime\prime}=\lambda\phi,\penalty\ \penalty\ x\in(0,\ell),\\
\phi(0)=\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)=\phi(\ell)=\phi^{\prime\prime}(\ell)=0.\end{cases}

(2.1)

Let
\displaystyle L^{2}(0,\ell)
be the Lebesgue space of all functions
\displaystyle y
defined on
\displaystyle(0,\ell),
being equipped with the norm

\|y\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell)}=\left(\int_{0}^{\ell}|y(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ y\in L^{2}(0,\ell).

We consider the Sobolev space
\displaystyle H^{2}(0,\ell)\cap H^{1}_{0}(0,\ell)

endowed with the norm

\|y\|_{H^{2}\cap H^{1}_{0}(0,\ell)}=\|{y}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,\ell)},\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ y\in H^{2}(0,\ell)\cap H^{1}_{0}(0,\ell).

We introduce the operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
defined in
\displaystyle L^{2}(0,\ell)
by setting:

\mathcal{A}y=\phi^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}-\gamma\phi^{\prime\prime},

on the domain

\mathcal{D}\left(\mathcal{A}\right):=\left\{\phi\in H^{4}(0,\ell)\penalty\ :\penalty\ \phi(0)=\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)=\phi(\ell)=\phi^{\prime\prime}(\ell)=0\right\},

which is dense in
\displaystyle L^{2}(0,\ell)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The linear operator
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
is positive and self-adjoint such
that
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{-1}
is compact. Moreover, the spectrum of

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
is discrete and consists of a
sequence of real eigenvalues

\displaystyle(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
tending to
\displaystyle+\infty
:

-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}\leq\ldots\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\to}+\infty.

The corresponding eigenfunctions
\displaystyle(\Phi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
can be chosen
to form an orthonormal basis of
\displaystyle L^{2}(0,\ell)
.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle y\in\mathcal{D}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)
, then using an integration by parts, we
have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}\phi,\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell}\left(\phi^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(x)-\gamma\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)\right){\phi}(x)dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell}\left(\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)\right)^{2}+\gamma\left(\phi^{\prime}(x)\right)^{2}dx.

Since
\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\ell}\left({\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)}+\frac{\gamma\phi(x)}{2}\right)^{2}dx\geq 0,
then

\langle\mathcal{A}\phi,\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(0,\ell)}\geq-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,\ell)}.

This implies that the quadratic
form is bounded from below by
\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{4}
, and then, the linear operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
is symmetric. Furthermore, it is easy to show that
\displaystyle Ran(\mathcal{A}-iId)=L^{2}(0,\ell)
, and this means that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
is self-adjoint. Since, the space
\displaystyle H^{2}(0,\ell)\cap H^{1}_{0}(0,\ell)
is continuously and compactly embedded in the space

\displaystyle L^{2}(0,\ell)
, then
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{-1}
is compact in

\displaystyle L^{2}(0,\ell)
.
∎

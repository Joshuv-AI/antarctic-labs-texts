Elliptic hypertoric varieties
Naichung Conan Leung, Xiao Zheng

Abstract
. We introduce elliptic hypertoric varieties, which is an elliptic analogue of hypertoric varieties and multiplicative hypertoric varieties. We also prove an elliptic version of Hikita conjecture, which relates elliptic (resp. additive and mulitplicative) hypertoric varieties to the equivariant elliptic cohomology (resp. ordinary cohomology and K-theory) of their 3d mirror hypertoric varieties.



1.
Introduction

Additive hypertoric varieties, or simply hypertoric varieties
\mathfrak{M}^{+}
, are the hyperkahler analogues of toric varieties introduced in
[
BD00
]

as hyperkahler quotient of complex vector spaces by tori. Namely,

\mathfrak{M}^{+}=\mathfrak{M}^{+}(\alpha,\beta)=\mathbb{C}^{2n}/\kern-5.0pt/\kern-5.0pt/_{(\alpha,\beta)}\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{R}}

with
\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{R}}
being a real subtorus of
\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{R}}\cong U(1)^{n}
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{2n}=\left(T^{\ast}\mathbb{C}\right)^{n}
by the standard quaternionic action with real and complex
moment maps

(\mu_{\mathbb{R}},\mu_{\mathbb{C}}):\left(T^{\ast}\mathbb{C}\right)^{n}\rightarrow\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}\oplus\mathfrak{k}^{\vee},

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are real and complex moment map values
respectively. It can also be described as a GIT quotient of the level set
\mu_{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}\left(\beta\right)
.

In recent years, additive hypertoric varieties appear predominantly in the
context of 3-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
SUSY gauge theories. Such a theory
is determined by a pair
\left(G,T^{\ast}\textbf{N}\right),
where
G
is a
complex reductive group and
N
is a
G
-representation
1
1

1

More generally,
T^{\ast}\textbf{N}
could be replaced by a symplectic
G
-representation.
. Additive hypertoric varieties are the Higgs branches of
such theories when
G
is abelian. The gauge theory associated to a
general pair
\left(G,T^{\ast}\textbf{N}\right)
admits two holomorphic symplectic
varieties, called the Higgs branch and the Coulomb branch, which are moduli
spaces of vacua for the theory. The Higgs branch
\mathcal{M}_{H}
has a
simple description as a the hyperkahler quotient of
T^{\ast}\textbf{N}
by the
maximal compact subgroup
G_{\mathbb{R}}\subset G
,

\mathcal{M}_{H}=T^{\ast}\textbf{N}/\kern-5.0pt/\kern-5.0pt/G_{\mathbb{R}}.

The Coulomb branch was defined in
[
Nak15
,
BFN16
]
as an affine variety whose
coordinate ring is the equivariant Borel-Moore homology of the variety of triples equipped with a convolution type
product. When
G=\mathrm{K}
is abelian, the Coulomb branch is also an additive
hypertoric variety, we have

\mathcal{M}_{H}=T^{\ast}\mathfrak{t}/\kern-5.0pt/\kern-5.0pt/\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{R}}\text{
and }\mathcal{M}_{C}=T^{\ast}\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}/\kern-5.0pt/\kern-5.0pt/\mathrm{A}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee},

where
\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathrm{A}_{\mathbb{R}}
are given by a short exact sequence of tori

1\longrightarrow\mathrm{K}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{T}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{A}\longrightarrow 1.

3d mirror symmetry, or symplectic duality, predicts that there is a dual
3-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
, SUSY gauge theory which switches the roles
of the Higgs branch and the Coulomb branch, among many other surprising
predictions. Various predictions of 3d mirror symmetry have been verified
for hypertoric varieties
[
BLPW14
,
KMP21
,
MSY20b
,
MSY20a
,
SZ20
,
Zho21
,
GHMG22
]
etc. This includes the Hikita conjecture

[

]
which in its simplest form states that if
X\rightarrow Y
and
X^{!}\rightarrow Y^{!}
are
dual pairs of symplectic resolutions, then there is an isomorphism

H^{\ast}(X)\cong\mathbb{C}[(Y^{!})^{\mathrm{A}}],

where
\mathrm{A}
denotes the maximal torus in the Hamiltonian automorphism group of

X
. For hypertoric varieties and Springer resolutions, the equivariant and quantum extension of Hikita’s conjecture was proved in
[
KMP21
]
relating the quantized coordinate ring of
Y^{!}
with the quantum cohomology of
X
. On the other hand, a K-theoretic analogue of
[
KMP21
]
was proved in
[

]
, which relates the quantum K-theory of
\mathcal{M}_{H}
with the quantized coordinate ring of the
K
-theoretic Coulomb branch
\mathcal{M}_{C}^{\times}
. For abelian gauge theories,
\mathcal{M}_{C}^{\times}
is a multiplicative hypertoric variety. (The definitions of additive and multiplicative hypertoric varieties are recalled in Appendix
A

It is a natural question whether there exists an analogous result for the elliptic cohomology of
X^{!}
, at
least in the hypertoric cases. The purpose of this paper is twofold. First we give a definition of elliptic
hypertoric varieties
\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}
and describe their basic
properties. Second we establish the following elliptic version of the equivariant Hikita conjecture:

Theorem 1.1
.

(Collorary
5.9
)
There is an isomorphism

\mathrm{Ell}_{\mathrm{K}^{\vee}}(\mathfrak{M}^{+}_{v})\cong(\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}_{u}^{\tau}})^{\mathrm{A}}.

Here the subscripts

and
v
are combinatorial inputs for a pair of dual hypertoric varieties and

\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}_{u}}
is a deformation space of the elliptic hypertoric variety
\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}_{u}

5

An additive (resp. multiplicative) hypertoric variety is defined as the
quotient of the
\mathrm{T}\cong\left(\mathbb{C}^{\times}\right)^{n}
-Hamiltonian (resp. quasi-Hamiltonian) holomorphic symplectic space
\left(T^{\ast}\mathbb{C}\right)^{n}
(resp.
(T^{*}\mathbb{C}\setminus\{zw\!=\!1\})^{n}
) with moment map
\mu_{+}:\left(T^{\ast}\mathbb{C}\right)^{n}\rightarrow\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}\cong\mathfrak{t}
(resp.
\mu_{\times}:(T^{*}\mathbb{C}\setminus\{zw\!=\!1\})^{n}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}
)
by a subtorus
\mathrm{K}
. In order to define elliptic hypertoric varieties, we first need to construct a two dimensional symplectic manifold
X_{\vartheta}
,
as the elliptic analogue to
T^{\ast}\mathbb{C}
and
T^{*}\mathbb{C}\setminus\{zw\!=\!1\}
, which admits a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-symmetry and a morphism

\mu:X_{\vartheta}\rightarrow E_{\tau},

which serves the purpose of a moment map. The definition of the building block
X_{\vartheta}
is given in Section
3.1
. The elliptic hypertoric varieties
\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}
are defined in Section
3.2
as the “elliptic”-Hamiltonian reduction (which we introduce in Section
2
) of
X_{\vartheta}^{n}
by
\mathrm{K}
. The precise statement and the proof of elliptic Hikita conjecture in the hypertoric cases will be
given in Section
5
.

2.
e-Hamiltonian manifolds and reduction

In this section, we introduce a notion of “elliptic”-Hamiltonian (e-Hamiltonian)
\mathrm{T}
-manifolds for which the moment maps are valued in elliptic curves, an important class of complex two-dimensional examples, and the analogue of Hamiltonian reduction.

2.1.
Moment maps valued in elliptic curves

Let
\bm{H}
denote the upper-half plane

\bm{H}=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}|\operatorname{Im}\tau>0\}.

For
\tau\in\bm{H}
, let
\Gamma=\langle 1,\tau\rangle
be the lattice in
\mathbb{C}
generated by
1
and
\tau
, and let
E_{\tau}
denote the elliptic curve

E_{\tau}:=\mathbb{C}/\Gamma=\mathbb{C}^{\times}/q^{\mathbb{Z}},\qquad q=\exp(2\pi i\tau).

Suppose
\mathrm{T}\!=\!(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
acts on a holomorphic symplectic manifold
(X,\omega)
preserving the symplectic form. We will identify
\mathfrak{t}=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{T})
with
\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
via an invariant inner product
\langle-,-\rangle
on
\mathfrak{t}
. For
\zeta\in\mathfrak{t}
, we denote by
v_{\zeta}
the vector field on
X
generated by
\zeta
. Then, the
\mathrm{T}
-action is Hamiltonian if there is a
\mathrm{T}
-invariant holomorphic map
\mu_{+}:X\to\mathfrak{t}
satisfying

\omega(v_{\zeta},-)=d\langle\mu_{+},\zeta\rangle.

On the other hand, the
\mathrm{T}
-action is “quasi”-Hamiltonian (cf.
[
AM98
]
), if it is there is a
\mathrm{T}
-invariant holomorphic map
\mu_{\times}:X\to\mathrm{T}
satisfying

\omega(v_{\zeta},-)=\langle\mu_{\times}^{*}\eta,\zeta\rangle,

where

\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d\log t_{i},\quad(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in\mathrm{T},

is understood as a
\mathfrak{t}
-valued one-form on
\mathrm{T}
.

Since
\eta
is invariant under the
q^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
-action on
\mathrm{T}
defined by

(q^{k_{1}},\ldots,q^{k_{n}})\cdot(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})=(q^{k_{1}}t_{1},\ldots,q^{k_{n}}t_{n}),

where
(q^{k_{1}},\ldots,q^{k_{n}})\in q^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
, it descends to a
\mathfrak{t}
-valued one form
\eta
(via an abuse of notation) on
E_{\tau}^{n}=\mathrm{T}/q^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
. This leads us to the following definition:

Definition 2.1
.

A symplectic
\mathrm{T}
-manifold
(X,\omega)
is called
e
-Hamiltonian if there exists an invariant holomorphic map

\mu:M\to E_{\tau}^{n}
satisfying

(2.1)

\omega(v_{\zeta},-)=\langle\mu^{*}\eta,\zeta\rangle,

where
\eta\in\Omega^{1}(E_{\tau}^{n},\mathfrak{t})
is the
\mathfrak{t}
-valued one-form defined above.

Note that the e-moment map
\mu
is a part of the definition. For brevity, we will simply refer to
(X,\omega)
as an e-Hamiltonian
\mathrm{T}
-manifold or say that the
\mathrm{T}
-action on
(X,\omega)
is e-Hamiltonian.

2.2.
Two dimensional examples

In this subsection we construct a class of complex two dimensional examples
(X_{\theta},\omega_{\theta})
of e-Hamiltonian
\mathrm{T}
-manifolds for
\mathrm{T}=\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. These examples depend on a global section
\theta
of holomorphic line bundle
L
over
E_{\tau}
and a factorization
L=L_{1}\otimes L_{2}
. Topologically,
X_{\theta}
is the total space of a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-fibration over
E_{\tau}
with fibers degenerating to the union
\mathbb{C}\cup_{0}\mathbb{C}
of two affine lines over the vanishing loci of
\theta
. For a detailed construction of
(X_{\theta},\omega_{\theta})
, let’s first recall a few basic facts about holomorphic line bundles over elliptic curves and theta functions (cf.
[
Pol03
]
).

Let
H:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a hermitian form

2

H
is an
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear form which is
\mathbb{C}
-linear in the first argument and
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear in the second argument.
on
\mathbb{C}
such that
E:=\operatorname{Im}H

\Gamma^{2}
. Let
\chi:\Gamma\to U(1)

\chi(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})=\chi(\gamma)\chi(\gamma^{\prime})\exp(i\pi E(\gamma,\gamma^{\prime})),\qquad\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma.

To a pair
(H,\chi)

L_{(H,\chi)}
over

whose total space is the quotient of
\mathbb{C}^{2}

\Gamma

\gamma\cdot(w,x)=\left(e_{(\chi,H)}(\gamma,x)w,x+\gamma\right),

where
e_{(H,\chi)}:\Gamma\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}

e_{(H,\chi)}(\gamma,x)=\chi(\gamma)\exp\left(\pi H(x,\gamma)+\frac{\pi}{2}H(\gamma,\gamma)\right).

More explicitly, for
\gamma=a+b\tau

(2.2)

H(x,y)=k\cfrac{x\bar{y}}{\operatorname{Im}\tau},

\chi(\gamma)=(-1)^{ab}\exp(2\pi iE(x_{0},\gamma))

We denote by
\mathcal{P}

(H,\chi)\cdot(H^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})=(H+H^{\prime},\chi\chi^{\prime})

For a line bundle
L

\theta(x+\gamma)=e_{L}(x,\gamma)\theta(x),

\dim H^{0}(E_{\tau},L)=\begin{cases}0&\text{for }\deg L<0\\

Now, let’s fix a line bundle
L
over
E_{\tau}
with
\deg\mathcal{L}>0
, a nonzero global section
\theta\in H^{0}(E_{\tau},L)
, and a factorization
L=L_{1}\otimes L_{2}
. For simplicity, we will assume their automorphy factors
e_{L}

e_{L_{1}}
,
e_{L_{2}}

\mathcal{P}
. The
total space of the vector bundle
L_{1}\oplus L_{2}
over
E_{\tau}
can be obtained as
the quotient of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
by the action of
\Gamma

(2.4)

\gamma\cdot(z,w,x)=(e_{L_{1}}(\gamma,x)z,e_{L_{2}}(\gamma,x)w,x+\gamma),

where
(z,w,x)
are coordinates on
\mathbb{C}^{3}

We define a hypersurface
X_{\theta}
in
\mathrm{Tot}(L_{1}\oplus L_{2})
by

X_{\theta}=\widetilde{X}_{\theta}/\Gamma,

where
\widetilde{X}_{\theta}
is the
\Gamma
-invariant hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
given by

(2.5)

\widetilde{X}_{\theta}=\{(z,w,x)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}|zw=\theta(x)\}.

We note that
X_{\theta}
has at worst orbifold singularities. We will assume
X_{\theta}
is smooth, this amounts to
\theta
having no repeated zeroes, which is the case for generic choices of
\theta
. We equip
X_{\theta}
with the symplectic form
\omega_{\theta}
given by the
\Gamma
-invariant symplectic form
\widetilde{\omega}_{\theta}
on

\widetilde{X}_{\theta}

(2.6)

\widetilde{\omega}_{\theta}=\operatorname{Res}\cfrac{dz\wedge dw\wedge dx}{zw-\theta(x)}.

The
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
\widetilde{X}_{\theta}
defined by

(2.7)

t\cdot(z,w,x)=(tz,t^{-1}w,x),\quad t\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

is Hamiltonian with respect to
\widetilde{\omega}_{\theta}
and commutes with the
\Gamma
-action. The moment map
\widetilde{\mu}_{\theta}:\widetilde{X}_{\theta}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies

d\widetilde{\mu}_{\theta}=\operatorname{Res}d\log(zw-\theta(x))\wedge dx=dx.

Thus,
\widetilde{\mu}_{\theta}
is the projection to the
x
-plane (up to an additive constant, which we choose to be zero).
By construction, (
2.7
) defines a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on
X_{\theta}
which preserves
\omega_{\theta}
, and
\widetilde{\mu}_{\theta}
descends to the projection map
\mu_{\theta}:X_{\theta}\to E_{\tau}
. It is easy to see that
(X_{\theta},\omega_{\theta})
satisfies the definition of an e-Hamiltonian
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-manifold and
\mu_{\theta,L}
is the e-moment map. We note swapping
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
in the above construction will result in the same holomorphic symplectic manifold with the opposite
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action.

Implicit in the above construction is a more general recipe for producing e-Hamiltonian
\mathrm{T}
-manifolds from Hamiltonian
\mathrm{T}
-manifolds for
\mathrm{T}\!=\!(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
,
i.e.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{\omega})
be a Hamiltomnian
\mathrm{T}
-manifold. Suppose we have a holomorphic, free, proper action of
\Gamma^{n}
on
\widetilde{X}
which commute with the
\mathrm{T}
-action and preserves
\widetilde{\omega}
.
Suppose further that the moment map
\widetilde{\mu}:\widetilde{X}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
intertwines the
\Gamma^{n}
-action on
\widetilde{X}
and the natural
\Gamma^{n}
-action on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then,
X=\widetilde{X}/\Gamma^{n}
equipped with the symplectic from
\omega
induced from
\widetilde{\omega}
is an e-Hamiltonian
\mathrm{T}
-manifold, and
\widetilde{\mu}
descends to an e-moment map
\mu:X\to E_{\tau}^{n}
.

Proof.

Since
(X,\omega)
is a symplectic
\mathrm{T}
-manifold, it suffices to check that
\mu
is an e-moment map, i.e., it satisfies (
2.1
). In coordinates, we have

\widetilde{\omega}(\widetilde{v}_{\zeta},-)=\widetilde{\mu}^{*}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta_{i}dx_{i}\right).

where
\widetilde{v}_{\zeta}
is the vector field on
\widetilde{X}
generated by
\zeta\in\mathfrak{t}
, and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
are coordinates on
\mathfrak{t}=\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Since the actions of
\mathrm{T}
and
\Gamma^{n}
commute,
\widetilde{v}_{\zeta}
descends to vector field
v_{\zeta}
on
X
generated by
\zeta
. Moreover,
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta_{i}dx_{i}
is
\Gamma^{n}
-invariant and defines a
1
-form on
E_{\tau}^{n}
.
This means
\mu

\omega(v_{\zeta},-)=\mu^{*}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta_{i}dx_{i}\right),

which is exactly (
2.1
) since we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta_{i}dx_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\zeta_{i}d\log t_{i}=\langle\eta,\zeta\rangle,\qquad(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in\mathrm{T}.

∎

2.3.
e-Hamiltonian reduction

We now introduce the analogue of Hamiltonian reduction for e-Hamiltonian
\mathrm{T}
-manifolds. This provides a general method for constructing new e-Hamiltonian
\mathrm{T}
-manifolds from the existing ones.

Let
\mathrm{K}\cong(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{k}
be a
k
-dimensional subtorus of
\mathrm{T}
. The inclusion
\phi:\mathrm{K}\to\mathrm{T}
has the form

(2.8)

\phi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}^{\phi_{1j}},\ldots,\prod_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}^{\phi_{nj}}\right),

where
(\phi_{ij})\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n\times k}(\mathbb{Z})
. We defines a morphism
\phi^{\vee}_{\tau}:E_{\tau}^{n}\to E_{\tau}^{k}
by

(2.9)

\phi^{\vee}_{\tau}([x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}])=\left(\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{i1}x_{i},\ldots,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{ik}x_{i}\right]\right).

The following lemma shows that the
\mathrm{K}
-action on an e-Hamiltonian

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
(X,\omega)
is an e-Hamiltonian
\mathrm{T}
-manifold with e-moment map
\mu:X\to E_{\tau}^{n}
. Then it is also an e-Hamiltonian
\mathrm{K}
-manifold with e-moment map
\mu_{\mathrm{K}}=\phi^{\vee}_{\tau}\circ\mu:X\to E_{\tau}^{k}
for any subtorus
\mathrm{K}
in
\mathrm{T}
.

Proof.

Since
(X,\omega)
is a symplectic K-manifold, it suffices to check that
\mu_{\mathrm{K}}
is an e-moment map, i.e, it satisfies (
2.1
). Let
\eta_{T}
and (resp.

\iota:\mathfrak{k}\to\mathfrak{t}
and
\iota^{\vee}:\mathfrak{t}\to\mathfrak{k}

\mathrm{T}

Definition 2.5

Suppose

(X,\omega)

\mathrm{K}
-action on
X
is trivial, the moment map
\mu_{\mathrm{K}}=\phi^{\vee}_{\tau}\circ\mu
is necessarily constant.
We fix
a_{0}\in\operatorname{Im}{\mu}
and define a map
\mu_{\mathrm{A}}:X\to E_{\tau}^{d}
by

(2.11)

\mu_{\mathrm{A}}=(\psi^{\vee}_{\tau})^{-1}\circ t_{a_{0}^{-1}}\circ\mu.

Note that
(\psi^{\vee}_{\tau})^{-1}
is an isomorphism
\ker\phi^{\vee}_{\tau}\xrightarrow{\sim}E_{\tau}^{d}
. Then, the map
\mu_{\mathrm{A}}
satisfies

\langle\mu_{\mathrm{A}}^{*}\eta_{\mathrm{A}},\pi(\zeta)\rangle=\langle\mu_{\mathrm{A}}^{*}(\pi^{\vee}(\eta_{\mathrm{A}})),\zeta\rangle=\langle\mu_{\mathrm{A}}^{*}\circ(\psi^{\vee}_{\tau})^{*}\eta_{\mathrm{T}},\zeta\rangle=\langle\mu^{*}\eta_{\mathrm{T}},\zeta\rangle=\omega(v_{\zeta},-)=\omega(v_{\pi(\zeta)},-),

for all
\zeta\in\mathfrak{t}
, where
\eta_{\mathrm{A}}
denotes the
\mathfrak{a}
-valued one form on
E_{\tau}^{d}
defined as in Definition
2.1
. Since the map
\pi:\mathfrak{t}\to\mathfrak{a}
is surjective, it follows that
\mu_{\mathrm{A}}
is an e-moment map.
∎

3.
Elliptic hypertoric varieties

3.1.
The building block

Additive and multiplicative hypertoric varieties are given by Hamiltonian and quasi-Hamiltonian reduction of
(T^{*}\mathbb{C})^{n}
and
(T^{*}\mathbb{C}\setminus\{zw\!=\!1\})^{n}
by a subtorus
\mathrm{K}
of
\mathrm{T}\!=\!(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{n}
, respectively (see appendix
A
). Analogously, we define elliptic hypertoric varieties to be e-Hamiltonian reduction of
X_{\vartheta}^{n}
by
\mathrm{K}
. Here the building block
X_{\vartheta}
belongs to the class of two dimensional e-Hamiltonian
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-manifold introduced in Section
2.2
. A detailed description of
X_{\vartheta}
is as follows.

Let
\tau\in\bm{H}
and
\Gamma=\langle 1,\tau\rangle
. We set

E_{\tau}=\mathbb{C}/\Gamma=\mathbb{C}^{\times}/q^{\mathbb{Z}},\quad q=\exp(2\pi i\tau).

Let
\widetilde{X}_{\vartheta}
be a hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
defined by

\widetilde{X}_{\vartheta}=\{(z,w,x)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}|zw=\vartheta(x)\}.

Here
\vartheta:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
is the Jacobi theta function

\theta(x)=(t^{1/2}-t^{-1/2})\prod_{m>0}(1-q^{m}t)(1-q^{m}t^{-1}),\quad t=\exp(2\pi ix),

which satisfies

\vartheta(x+\gamma)=(-1)^{b}q^{-b^{2}/2}t^{-b}\vartheta(x),\quad\gamma=a+b\tau\in\Gamma.

The theta function
\vartheta
defines a section of a degree
1
line bundle over
E_{\tau}
with a unique zero at the identity element
e
. We denote this line bundle by
\mathcal{O}(e)
and note that it has the automorphy factor

\alpha:\Gamma\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times},\qquad\alpha(\gamma,x)=(-1)^{b}q^{-b^{2}/2}t^{-b}.

Let
\Gamma
act on
\mathbb{C}^{3}
by

\gamma\cdot(z,w,x)=(z,\alpha(\gamma,x)w,x+\gamma).

The quotient
\mathbb{C}^{3}/\Gamma
is the total space
\mathrm{Tot}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(e))
of the rank
2
bundle
\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(e)
over
E_{\tau}
.

We define
X_{\vartheta}
to be the hypersurface

X_{\vartheta}=\widetilde{X}_{\vartheta}/\Gamma

in
\mathrm{Tot}(\mathcal{O}\oplus\mathcal{O}(e))
.
We equip
\widetilde{X}
with the holomorphic sympletic form
\widetilde{\omega}_{\vartheta}

\widetilde{\omega}_{\vartheta}=\operatorname{Res}\cfrac{dz\wedge dw\wedge dx}{zw-\vartheta(x)}.

This in turn equips
X_{\vartheta}
with a holomorphic symplectic form
\omega_{\vartheta}
.
The
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action defined by (
2.7
) on
(X_{\vartheta},\omega_{\vartheta})
is e-Hamiltonian with the e-moment map given by the projection to
E_{\tau}
. The e-Hamiltonian
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-manifold
(X_{\vartheta},\omega_{\vartheta})
will be our building block for elliptic hypertoric varieties.

3.2.
Hypertoric varieties via e-Hamiltonian reduction

Let
\mathfrak{t}
and
\mathfrak{a}
be complex vector spaces of dimensions
n
and
d
respectively. Let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}\subset\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{Z}}
be an integer basis and
\{e_{1}^{\vee},\ldots,e_{n}^{\vee}\}
the dual basis. The combinatorial input for a hypertoric variety is a collection
u=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
of primitive vectors in
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{Z}}
which span
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{Z}}
over the integers. From which we obtain short exact sequences

(3.1)

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{k}\overset{\iota}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{t}\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{a}\longrightarrow 0,

(3.2)

0\longleftarrow\mathfrak{k}^{\vee}\overset{\iota^{\vee}}{\longleftarrow}\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}\overset{\pi^{\vee}}{\longleftarrow}\mathfrak{a}^{\vee}\longleftarrow 0.

by setting
\pi(e_{i})=u_{i}
. Here
\mathfrak{k}=\ker\pi
, and (
3.2
) is the dual sequence of (
3.1
). Exponentiating (
3.1
) gives us a short exact sequence of tori

(3.3)

1\longrightarrow\mathrm{K}\overset{\phi}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{T}\overset{\psi}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{A}\longrightarrow 1,

where
\mathrm{T}=\mathfrak{t}/\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\mathrm{A}=\mathfrak{a}/\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
\mathrm{K}=\ker(\mathrm{T}\to\mathrm{A})
.

Let’s consider
X_{\vartheta}^{n}
equipped with the holomorphic sympletic form
\omega_{\mathbb{C}}
induced from the
\Gamma^{n}
-invariant symplectic form

\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbb{C}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Res}\cfrac{dz_{i}\wedge dw_{i}\wedge dx_{i}}{z_{i}w_{i}-\vartheta(x_{i},\tau)}

on
\widetilde{X}_{\vartheta}^{n}
. The
\mathrm{T}
-action on
\widetilde{X}_{\vartheta}^{n}
defined by

\vec{t}\cdot(\vec{z},\vec{w},\vec{x})=(t_{1}z_{1},t_{1}^{-1}w_{1},x_{1},\ldots,t_{n}z_{n},t_{n}^{-1}w_{n},x_{n})

is Hamiltonian with respect to
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbb{C}}
with the moment map
\widetilde{\mu}:\widetilde{X}_{\vartheta}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
given by

\widetilde{\mu}(\vec{z},\vec{w},\vec{x})=\vec{x}.

This in turn defines an e-Hamiltonian
\mathrm{T}
-action on
(X_{\vartheta}^{n},\omega_{\mathbb{C}})
with the e-moment map
\mu_{\mathbb{C}}:X_{\vartheta}^{n}\to E_{\tau}^{n}
given by the projection to
E_{\tau}^{n}
. The
\mathrm{K}
-action on
(X_{\vartheta}^{n},\omega_{\mathbb{C}})
is also e-Hamiltonian by Lemma
2.4
with the moment map given by
\mu_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{K}}=\phi^{\vee}_{\tau}\circ\mu_{\mathbb{C}}
, where
\phi^{\vee}_{\tau}:E_{\tau}^{n}\to E_{\tau}^{k}
is the “transpose” of
\phi
defined in (
2.9
).

We choose an ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}=\otimes_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{L}_{i}
over
X_{\vartheta}^{n}
, where
\mathcal{L}_{i}
is the pullback of the line bundle
\mathcal{O}(e)
over
E_{\tau}
via the composition

X_{\vartheta}^{n}\overset{\mu_{\mathbb{C}}}{\longrightarrow}E_{\tau}^{n}\overset{\mathrm{pr}_{i}}{\longrightarrow}E_{\tau}.

Here
\mathrm{pr}_{i}
denotes the projection to the
i
-th component. More explicitly, the total space of
\mathcal{L}
is the quotient of
\mathbb{C}\times\widetilde{X}_{\vartheta}
by the
\Gamma^{n}
-action defined as

\vec{\gamma}\cdot(v,(\vec{z},\vec{w},\vec{x}))=\left(\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\alpha(\gamma_{i},x_{i})\right)v,\vec{\gamma}\cdot(\vec{z},\vec{w},\vec{x})\right).

We will also equip
X_{\vartheta}^{n}
with a Kähler form
\omega_{\mathbb{R}}
induced by the
\Gamma^{n}
-invariant Kähler form

\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbb{R}}=\cfrac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\partial\bar{\partial}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|z_{i}|^{2}+\exp\left(-2\pi\frac{(\operatorname{Im}x_{i})^{2}}{\operatorname{Im}\tau}\right)|w_{i}|^{2}+\frac{|x_{i}|^{2}}{\operatorname{Im}\tau}\right),

on
\widetilde{X}_{\vartheta}^{n}
. Note that
[\omega_{\mathbb{R}}]=c_{1}(\mathcal{L})
.

The restriction of the
\mathrm{T}
-action on
\widetilde{X}_{\vartheta}^{n}
to the compact real torus
\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{R}}\cong U(1)^{k}
in
\mathrm{K}
is Hamiltonian with respect to
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbb{R}}
. The moment map
\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{K}}:\widetilde{X}^{n}_{\vartheta}\to\mathfrak{k}_{R}^{\vee}
given by

\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{K}}(\vec{z},\vec{w},\vec{x})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(|z_{i}|^{2}-\exp\left(-2\pi\frac{(\operatorname{Im}x_{i})^{2}}{\operatorname{Im}\tau}\right)|w_{i}|^{2}\right)\iota^{\vee}e_{i}^{\vee}

is
\Gamma^{k}
-invariant and descends to the moment map
\mu_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{K}}:X_{\vartheta}^{n}\to\mathfrak{k}_{R}^{\vee}
for the Hamiltonian
\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{R}}
-action on
(X^{n}_{\vartheta},\omega_{\mathbb{R}})
.

We are now ready to define elliptic hypertoric varieties.

Definition 3.1

(Elliptic hypertoric varieties)
.

Given a collection
u=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
of primitive vectors in
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\tau\in\bm{H}
, and
a choice of parameters

(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}\times E_{\tau}^{k}
, we define the associated (elliptic) hypertoric variety
\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}_{u}(\alpha,\beta)
to be e-Hamiltonian reduction (Definition
2.5
) of
X_{\vartheta}^{n}
by
\mathrm{K}

(3.4)

\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}_{u}(\alpha,\beta)=(\mu_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{K}})^{-1}(\beta)/\kern-5.0pt/_{\alpha}\mathrm{K}.

Here
\alpha
determines a lift of the
\mathrm{K}
-action to the total space of
\mathcal{L}
. Equivalently, we can define
\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}_{u}(\alpha,\beta)
to be the symplectic reduction of
(\mu_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{K}})^{-1}(\beta)
by
\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{R}}

\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}_{u}(\alpha,\beta)=(\mu_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{K}},\mu_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{K}})^{-1}(\alpha,\beta)/\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{R}}.

The GIT quotient (
3.4
) is a priori only defined for characters
\alpha\in\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathrm{K},\mathbb{C}^{\times})
. However, as GIT stability condition remains unchanged if we replace
\alpha
by multiple of itself, we can formally define (
3.4
) for
\alpha\in\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. Furthermore, since GIT stability depends locally constantly on
\alpha
, we can then extend (
3.4
) to
\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{R}}
. It is also easy to see that
\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}_{u}(\alpha,\beta)
does not depend on the signs of the vectors
u_{i}
in the input datum.

Similar to the case of additive hypertoric varieties,
\mathfrak{M}^{u}_{\tau}(\alpha,\beta)
is the most singular when
(\alpha,\beta)=(0,0)
, and variations of the parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
correspond to partial resolutions and complex deformations of
\mathfrak{M}^{u}_{\tau}(0,0)
, respectively.

When
\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}_{u}(\alpha,\beta)
is smooth (see Theorem
3.10
for smoothness criteria), it is equipped with a holomorphic symplectic form
\bar{\omega}_{\mathbb{C}}
and a Kähler form
\bar{\omega}_{\mathbb{R}}
induced by
\omega_{\mathbb{C}}
and
\omega_{\mathbb{R}}
, respectively.
The residue action of the torus
\mathrm{A}
on
\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}_{u}(\alpha,\beta)
is e-Hamiltonian with respect to
\bar{\omega}_{\mathbb{C}}
(by Lemma
2.6
) and Hamiltonian with respect to
\bar{\omega}_{\mathbb{R}}
. The corresponding moment maps
\mu_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{A}}:\mathfrak{M}_{u}^{\tau}(\alpha,\beta)\to\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}
and
\mu_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{A}}:\mathfrak{M}_{u}^{\tau}(\alpha,\beta)\to E_{\tau}^{d}
are given by

\mu_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{A}}([\vec{z},\vec{w},\vec{x}])=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(|z_{i}|^{2}-\exp\left(-2\pi\frac{(\operatorname{Im}x_{i})^{2}}{\operatorname{Im}\tau}\right)|w_{i}|^{2}-\alpha_{i}\right)e_{i}^{\vee}\in\ker(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}\to\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee})=\mathfrak{a}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}},

and

\mu_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{A}}([\vec{z},\vec{w},\vec{x}])=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[x_{i}-\beta_{i}\right]\in\ker(E_{\tau}^{n}\overset{\phi^{\vee}_{\tau}}{\longrightarrow}E_{\tau}^{k})\cong E_{\tau}^{d},

where
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}
and
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})\in E_{\tau}^{n}
are lifts of
(\alpha,\beta)
. We note that both
\mu_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{A}}
and
\mu_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{A}}
are surjective.

The following are elliptic hypertoric analogues of some familiar spaces:

Example 3.2
.

Let
\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{Z}}\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
be a collection such that
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n-1}
is an integer basis and
u_{n}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}u_{i}
. Then
\mathrm{K}
is the diagonal subtorus. For
\alpha
a regular value and
0\in E_{\tau}
the identity element,
\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}_{u}(\alpha,0)
is the analogue of the additive hypertoric variety
\mathfrak{M}_{u}^{+}(\alpha,0)=T^{*}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
.

Example 3.3
.

Let
u_{1}\in\mathfrak{a}\cong\mathbb{Z}
be a primitive integer vector and define the map
\pi:\mathfrak{t}\to\mathfrak{a}
by
\pi(e_{i})=u_{1}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. Then
\mathrm{K}
is the subtorus

(3.5)

K=\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in T|\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}=1\}.

For
\alpha
a regular value and
0\in E_{\tau}^{n-1}
the identity element,
\mathfrak{M}^{\tau}_{u}(\alpha,0)
is the analogue of the additive hypertoric variety
\mathfrak{M}_{u}^{+}(\alpha,0)=\widetilde{\mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
, and the latter is the minimal resolution of the type-
A_{n-1}
Kleinian singularity.

3.3.
Topology and geometry of elliptic hypertoric varieties

In
[
DS17
]
, Dancer and Swann introduced
additive hypertoric varieties of infinite topological type
as hyperkähler quotients of Hilbert manifolds, generalizing the previous constructions of additive hypertoric varieties of finite topological type
[
BD00
]
, and of complex
2
-dimensional hyperkähler manifolds of
A_{\infty}
-type
[
AKL89
,
Got94
]
. In order for the hyperkähler quotient construction to work, the hyperplane arrangement associated to a resulting hypertoric variety cannot be periodic. On the other hand, the universal cover of an elliptic hypertoric variety can be viewed as a version of additive hypertoric variety of infinite topological type whose associated hyperplane arrangement is periodic. In this subsection, we will study the topology and geometry of elliptic hypertoric varieties through their universal covers.

Let
\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{K}}:\widetilde{X}^{n}_{\vartheta}\to\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}
and
\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{K}}:\widetilde{X}^{n}_{\vartheta}\to\mathfrak{k}^{\vee}
be the moment maps for the actions of
\mathrm{K}
and
\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{R}}
on
\widetilde{X}^{n}_{\vartheta}
with respect to
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbb{C}}
and
\widetilde{\omega}_{\mathbb{R}}
, respectively. Let
(\alpha,\tilde{\beta})\in\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}\times\mathfrak{k}
We define
\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}_{\tau}^{u}(\alpha,\tilde{\beta})
to be the Hamiltonian reduction

\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}_{\tau}^{u}(\alpha,\tilde{\beta}):=(\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{K}},\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{K}})^{-1}(\alpha,\tilde{\beta})/\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{R}}=(\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{K}})^{-1}(\tilde{\beta})/\kern-5.0pt/_{\alpha}\mathrm{K}.

The moment maps for the residual
\mathrm{A}
-action on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}^{u}_{\tau}(\alpha,\tilde{\beta})
are given by

\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{A}}(\vec{z},\vec{w},\vec{x})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(|z_{i}|^{2}-\exp\left(-2\pi\frac{(\operatorname{Im}x_{i})^{2}}{\operatorname{Im}\tau}\right)|w_{i}|^{2}-\alpha_{i}\right)e_{i}^{\vee}\in\ker(\mathfrak{t}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}\to\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee})=\mathfrak{a}^{\vee}_{\mathbb{R}},

and

\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{A}}(\vec{z},\vec{w},\vec{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-\widetilde{\beta_{i}})e_{i}^{\vee}\in\ker(\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}\to\mathfrak{k}^{\vee})\cong\mathfrak{a}^{\vee},

where
(\tilde{\beta}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\beta}_{n})\in\mathfrak{t}^{\vee}
is a lift of
\tilde{\beta}
.

The sequence (
3.2
) induces a short exact sequence of lattices

(3.6)

0\to\Gamma^{d}\to\Gamma^{n}\to\Gamma^{k}\to 0.

The action of
\Gamma^{d}
on
\widetilde{X}^{n}_{\vartheta}
commutes with the
\mathrm{K}
-action and preserves the semi-stable locus of the level set

, and

We now introduce the hyperplane arrangements associated to

and

.

Proof.

Let’s set
\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathrm{K}}=(\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{K}},\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{K}})
. We have
\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}^{u}_{\tau}(\alpha,\tilde{\beta})=\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathrm{K}}^{-1}(\alpha,\tilde{\beta})/\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}^{u}_{\tau}(\alpha^{\prime},\tilde{\beta}^{\prime})=\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathrm{K}}^{-1}(\alpha^{\prime},\tilde{\beta}^{\prime})/\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Therefore, it suffices to show that the level sets
\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathrm{K}}^{-1}(\alpha,\tilde{\beta})
and
\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathrm{K}}^{-1}(\alpha^{\prime},\tilde{\beta}^{\prime})
are
\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{R}}
-equivariantly diffeomorphic. The diffeomorphism can be constructed as follows.

Fisrt, we note that the critical values of
\widetilde{\mu}_{\mathrm{K}}
coincides with the set in
\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}\times\mathfrak{k}=\mathbb{R}^{3k}
for which
d+1

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
have nonempty intersection, which is in real codimension three. Let
U
denote the complement of this set. and note that we have

Appendix A
Additive and Multiplicative hypertoric varieties

We give a brief review of additive and multiplicative hypertoric varieties in this appendix. We will assume the same combinatorial input as given in the beginning of subsection
3.2
.

A.1.
Additive hypertoric varieties

Let’s consider the
T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n}
equipped with the standard hyperkähler structure. We have a Kähler form

\omega_{\mathbb{R}}^{+}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(dz_{i}\wedge d\bar{z}_{i}+dw_{i}\wedge d\bar{w}_{i}),

and holomorphic symplectic form

\omega_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}dz_{i}\wedge dw_{i}.

Here
z_{i}
and
w_{i}
are the base and fiber coordinates, respectively. The action of
\mathrm{T}
(resp. the compact real torus
\mathrm{T}_{\mathbb{R}}
in
\mathrm{T}
) on
T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n}
defined by

(A.1)

\vec{t}\cdot(\vec{z},\vec{w})=(t_{1}z_{1},t_{1}^{-1}w_{1},\ldots,t_{n}z_{n},t_{n}^{-1}w_{n}),

is Hamiltonian with respect to
\omega_{\mathbb{C}}^{+}
(resp.
\omega_{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
). The hyperkähler moment map

(\mu_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{K}}^{+},\mu_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{K}}^{+}):T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}\oplus\mathfrak{k}^{\vee}

for the action of the subtorus
K\subset T^{n}
is given by

\mu_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{K}}^{+}(\vec{z},\vec{w})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(|z_{i}|^{2}-|w_{i}|^{2})\iota^{\vee}e_{i}^{\vee},

\mu_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{K}}^{+}(\vec{z},\vec{w})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(z_{i}w_{i})\iota^{\vee}e_{i}^{\vee}.

Definition A.1

(Additive hypertoric varieties)
.

Given a collection
u=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
of primitive vectors in
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, and a choice of parameters
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}\oplus\mathfrak{k}^{\vee}
,
we defined the associated (additive) hypertoric variety
\mathfrak{M}^{+}_{u}(\alpha,\beta)
to be the (possibly singular) hyperkähler quotient

\mathfrak{M}_{u}^{+}(\alpha,\beta)=T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n}/\kern-5.0pt/\kern-5.0pt/_{(\alpha,\beta)}\mathrm{K}:=(\mu_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{K}}^{+},\mu_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{K}}^{+})^{-1}(\alpha,\beta)/\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{R}}

Equivalently, as a holomorphic symplectyc variety, we can define
\mathfrak{M}_{u}^{+}(\alpha,\beta)
to as the GIT quotient

\mathfrak{M}_{u}^{+}(\alpha,\beta)=(\mu_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{K}}^{+})^{-1}(\beta)/\kern-5.0pt/_{\alpha}\mathrm{K}.

A.2.
Multiplicative hypertoric varierties

For multiplicative hypertoric varieties, we consider the space

(T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\circ}=\{(z,w)\in T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n}|1-z_{i}w_{i}\neq 0,i=1,\ldots,n\}.

equipped with the holomorphic symplectic form

\omega_{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}=\sum_{i}^{n}\cfrac{dz_{i}\wedge dw_{i}}{1-z_{i}w_{i}},

and the Kähler form
\omega_{\mathbb{R}}^{\times}
which is the restriction of
\omega_{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
to
(T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\circ}
.

(T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\circ}
is a
\mathrm{T}
-invariant open subset of
\mathrm{T}
-action on
T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n}
defined in (
A.1
). Moreoever, the
\mathrm{T}
-action on
(T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\circ}
is quasi-Hamiltonian with respect to
\omega_{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
, and there is a torus-valued moment map
\mu_{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}:(T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\circ}\to\mathrm{T}

\mu_{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}(\vec{z},\vec{w})=(1-z_{1}w_{1},\ldots,1-z_{n}w_{n}).

The inclusion map
\phi:\mathrm{K}\to\mathrm{T}
for the subtorus
\mathrm{K}
has the form (
2.8
). We define its transpose
\phi^{\vee}_{\tau}:\mathrm{T}\to\mathrm{K}
by

\phi^{\vee}_{\tau}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{\phi_{i1}},\ldots,\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{\phi_{ik}}\right).

The
\mathrm{K}
-action on
(T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\circ}

is quasi-Hamiltonian with the torus-valued moment map
\mu_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{K}}^{\times}
given by
\mu_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{K}}^{\times}=\phi^{\vee}_{\tau}\circ\mu_{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
.

Definition A.2

(Multiplicative hypertoric varieties)
.

Given a collection
u=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
of primitive vectors in
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, and a choice of parameters
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathfrak{k}_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}\times\mathrm{K}
,
we defined the associated multiplicative hypertoric variety
\mathfrak{M}_{u}^{\times}(\alpha,\beta)
either by the symplectic quotient

\mathfrak{M}_{u}^{\times}(\alpha,\beta):=(\mu_{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{K}}^{\times},\mu_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{K}}^{\times})(\alpha,\beta)/\mathrm{K}_{\mathbb{R}},

or the GIT quotient

\mathfrak{M}_{u}^{\times}(\alpha,\beta):=(\mu_{\mathbb{C},\mathrm{K}}^{\times})^{-1}(\beta)/\kern-5.0pt/_{\alpha}\mathrm{K}.

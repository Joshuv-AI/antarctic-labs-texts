Abstract
. The Lyubeznik size of a monomial ideal I I of a polynomial ring S S is a lower bound for the Stanley depth of I I decreased by 1 1 . A proof given by Herzog-Popescu-Vladoiu had a gap which is solved here. Key words : Stanley depth, Stanley decompositions, Size, lcm-lattices, Polarization. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary 13C15, Secondary 13F55, 13F20, 13P10.



Introduction

Let
S=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
,
n\in{\bf N}
, be a polynomial ring over a field
K
and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{1}.\ldots,x_{n})
.
Let
I\supsetneq J
be two monomial ideals of
S
and
u\in I\setminus J
a monomial.
For
Z\subset\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
with
(J:u)\cap K[Z]=0
, let
uK[Z]
be the linear
K
-subspace of
I/J
generated by the elements
uf
,
f\in K[Z]
. A presentation of
I/J
as a finite direct sum of such spaces
{\mathcal{D}}:\ I/J=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}u_{i}K[Z_{i}]
is called a
Stanley decomposition
of
I/J
. Set
\operatorname{sdepth}(\mathcal{D}):=\operatorname{min}\{|Z_{i}|:i=1,\ldots,r\}
and

\operatorname{sdepth}\ I/J:=\operatorname{max}\{\operatorname{sdepth}\ ({\mathcal{D}}):\;{\mathcal{D}}\;\text{is a
Stanley decomposition of}\;I/J\}.

Let
h
be the height of
a=\sum_{P\in\operatorname{Ass}_{S}S/I}P
and
r
the minimum
t
such that there exist
\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{t}\}\subset\operatorname{Ass}_{S}S/I
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{t}P_{i}=a
. We call the
size
of
I
the integer
\operatorname{size}_{S}I=n-h+r-1
. Lyubeznik
[
6
]
showed that
\operatorname{depth}_{S}I\geq 1+\operatorname{size}_{S}I
.
If
Stanley’s Conjecture
[
14
]
would hold, that is
\operatorname{sdepth}_{S}I/J\geq\operatorname{depth}_{S}I/J
, then we would get also
\operatorname{sdepth}_{S}I\geq 1+\operatorname{size}_{S}I
as it is stated in
[
4
]
. Unfortunately, there exists a counterexample in
[
1
]
of this conjecture for
I=S
,
J\not=0
and it is possible that there are also counterexamples for
J=0
. However, the counterexample of
[
1
]
induces another one for
J\not=0
and
I\not=S
generated by
5
monomials, which shows that our result from
[
9
]
is tight. This counterexample does not affect Question 1 from
[
10
]
.

Y.-H. Shen noticed that the second statement of
[
4
, Lemma 3.2]
is false when
I
is not squarefree and so the proof from
[
4
]
of
\operatorname{sdepth}_{S}I\geq 1+\operatorname{size}_{S}I
is correct only when
I
is squarefree. Since the depth is not a lower bound of sdepth due to
[
1
]
the lower bound of sdepth given by size will have a certain value.
The main purpose of this paper is to show the above inequality in general (see Theorem
22
).

The important tool in the crucial point of the proof is the application of
[
5
, Theorem 4.5]
(a kind of polarization) to the so called the lcm-lattice associated to
I
(see
[
2
]
). Unfortunately, the polarization does not behaves well with size (see e.g.
[
12
, Example 1.2]
). Since it behaves somehow better with the so-called bigsize (very different from that introduced in
[
8
]
, see Definition
3
), we have to replace the size with the bigsize. Our bigsize is the right notion for a monomial squarefree ideal
I\subset S
(see Theorem
14
, an illustration of its proof is given in Examples
15
,
17
). If
I
is not squarefree and
I^{p}\subset S^{p}
is its polarization then it seems that a better notion will be
\operatorname{bigsize}_{S^{p}}(I^{p})-\dim S^{p}+\dim S
.

The inequality
\operatorname{sdepth}_{S}S/I\geq\operatorname{size}_{S}I
conjectured in
[
4
]
was proved in
[
15
]
when
I
is squarefree and it is extended in
[
12
]
. Our bigsize is useless for this inequality (see Remark
16
). A similar inequality is proved by Y.-H. Shen in the frame of the quotients of squarefree monomial ideals
[
13
, Theorem 3.6]
.

We owe thanks to Y.-H. Shen and S. A. Seyed Fakhari who noticed several mistakes in some previous versions of this paper, and to B. Ichim, A. Zarojanu for a bad example.

1.
Squarefree monomial ideals

The proof of the the following theorem is given in
[
4
]
in a more general form, which is correct only for squarefree ideals. For the sake of completeness we recall it here in sketch.

Theorem 1
.

(Herzog-Popescu-Vladoiu) If
I
is a squarefree monomial ideal then

\operatorname{sdepth}_{S}I\geq\operatorname{size}_{S}(I)+1.

Proof.

Write

I=\cap_{i\in[s]}P_{i}
as an irredundant intersection of monomial prime ideals of
S
and assume that
P_{1}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
for some
r\in[n]
. Apply induction on
s
, the case
s=1
being trivial. Assume that
s>1
. Using
[
3
, Lemma 3.6]
we may reduce to the case when
\sum_{i\in[s]}P_{i}={\mathfrak{m}}
.

Set
S^{\prime}=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
,
S^{\prime\prime}=K[x_{r+1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. For every nonempty proper subset
\tau\subset[s]
set

S_{\tau}=K[\{x_{i}:i\in[r],x_{i}\not\in\sum_{j\in\tau}P_{j}\}],

J_{\tau}=(\cap_{i\in[s]\setminus\tau}P_{i})\cap S_{\tau},\ \ L_{\tau}=(\cap_{i\in\tau}P_{i})\cap S^{\prime\prime}.

If
J_{\tau}\not=0
,
L_{\tau}\not=0
define
A_{\tau}=\operatorname{sdepth}_{S_{\tau}}J_{\tau}+\operatorname{sdepth}_{S^{\prime\prime}}L_{\tau}
. Also define
A_{0}=\operatorname{sdepth}_{S}I_{0}
for
I_{0}=(I\cap S^{\prime})S
. By
[
8
, Theorem 1.6]
(the ideas come from
[
7
, Proposition 2.3]
) we have

\operatorname{sdepth}_{S}I\geq\operatorname{min}\{A_{0},\{A_{\tau}:\emptyset\not=\tau\subset[s],J_{\tau}\not=0,L_{\tau}\not=0\}\}.

Using again
[
3
, Lemma 3.6]
we see that if
I_{0}\not=0
then
\operatorname{sdepth}_{S}I_{0}\geq n-r\geq\operatorname{size}_{S}(I)+1
. Fix a nonempty proper subset
\tau\subset[s]
such that

J_{\tau}\not=0,\ L_{\tau}\not=0
.
It is enough to show that
A_{\tau}\geq\operatorname{size}_{S}(I)+1,

that is to verify that

\operatorname{sdepth}_{S^{\prime\prime}}L_{\tau}\geq\operatorname{size}_{S}(I)

because
\operatorname{sdepth}_{S_{\tau}}(J_{\tau})\geq 1
.

Set
P_{\tau}=\sum_{i\in\tau}P_{i}\cap S^{\prime\prime}
, let us say
P_{\tau}=(x_{r+1},\ldots,x_{e})
for some
e\leq n
. Let
j_{1}<\ldots<j_{t}
in
\tau
with
t
minim such that
\sum_{i=1}^{t}P_{j_{i}}\cap S^{\prime\prime}=P_{\tau}
. Thus
\operatorname{size}_{S^{\prime\prime}}L_{\tau}=t-1+n-e
.
Choose
k_{1}<\ldots<k_{u}
in
[s]\setminus(\tau\cup\{1\})
with
u
minim such that

(x_{e+1},\ldots,x_{n})\subset\sum_{i=1}^{u}P_{k_{i}}
. We have
u\leq n-e
. Then
P_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{t}P_{j_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{u}P_{k_{i}}={\mathfrak{m}}
and so
u+t+1\geq\operatorname{size}_{S}(I)+1
. By induction hypothesis on
s
we have
\operatorname{sdepth}_{S^{\prime\prime}}L_{\tau}\geq\operatorname{size}_{S^{\prime\prime}}L_{\tau}+1=t+n-e\geq t+u\geq\operatorname{size}_{S}(I).

∎

Now let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal not necessarily squarefree and
I=\cap_{i\in[s]}Q_{i}
an irredundant decomposition of
I
as an intersection of irreducible monomial ideals,
P_{i}=\sqrt{Q_{i}}
. Set
a=\sum_{i=1}^{s}P_{i}
. Let
\nu
be a total order on
[s]
. We say that
\nu
is
admissible
if given
i,j,k\in[s]
with
j,k>i
with respect to
\nu
and such that from
\operatorname{height}(\sum_{p\in[i]}P_{p}+P_{k})>\operatorname{height}(\sum_{p\in[i]}P_{p}+P_{j})
it follows that
j<k
.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}=(Q_{i_{k}})_{k\in[t]}
be a family of ideals from
(Q_{j})_{j\in[s]}
,
t\in[s]
,
i_{1}<\ldots<i_{t}
with respect to
\nu
such that
P_{i_{k}}
are maximal among
(P_{i})_{i}
, and
set
a_{k,{\mathcal{F}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}P_{i_{j}}\subset a
,
a_{0,{\mathcal{F}}}=0
,

a_{\mathcal{F}}=a_{t,{\mathcal{F}}}
,
t_{\mathcal{F}}=t
,
h_{\mathcal{F}}=\operatorname{height}a_{\mathcal{F}}
. Shortly, we speak about a family
\mathcal{F}
of
I
. If
I
is squarefree then each
P_{j}
is maximal among
(P_{i})
.

Definition 2
.

A family
{\mathcal{F}}
of
I
with respect to
\nu
is
admissible
if
P_{i_{k}}\not\subset a_{k-1,{\mathcal{F}}}
for all
k\in[t]
. The admissible family
{\mathcal{F}}
is
maximal
if
a_{\mathcal{F}}=a
, that is, there exist no prime ideal
P\in\operatorname{Ass}_{S}S/I
which is not contained in
a_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Definition 3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of
I
with respect to
\nu
. If
t_{\mathcal{F}}=1
we set
\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}})=\dim S/P_{i_{1}}
. If
t_{\mathcal{F}}>1
then define by recurrence the
\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}})=\operatorname{min}\{\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}),1+\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}}_{1})\}
, where
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}=(Q_{i_{k}})_{1\leq k<t}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
is the family obtained from the family
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}_{1}}=(Q_{i_{t}}+Q_{i_{k}})_{1\leq k<t}
removing those ideals
Q_{i_{t}}+Q_{i_{k}}
which contain another ideal
Q_{i_{t}}+Q_{i_{k^{\prime}}}
with
k^{\prime}\in[t-1]\setminus\{k\}
.
Note that
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
is given by
\operatorname{Ass}_{S}S/I_{1}
, where
I_{1}=\cap_{1\leq k<t}(Q_{i_{t}}+Q_{i_{k}})
, the decomposition being not necessarily irredundant. Then
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
is a family of
I_{1}
with respect to the order induced by
\nu
such that roughly speaking
Q_{i_{t}}+Q_{i_{k}}
is smaller than

Q_{i_{t}}+Q_{i_{k^{\prime}}}
if
k<k^{\prime}
with respect to
\nu
.
The integer
\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}})
is called the
bigsize
of
\mathcal{F}
. Note that
\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}})\leq t-1+\dim S/a_{\mathcal{F}}
. Set
\operatorname{bigsize}_{\nu}(I)=\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}})
for a maximal admissible family
\mathcal{F}
of
I
with respect to
\nu
. We call the
bigsize
of
I

the maximum
\operatorname{bigsize}_{S}(I)
of
\operatorname{bigsize}_{\nu}(I)
for all total admissible orders
\nu
on
[s]
.

Remark 4
.

Note that given a total admissible order
\nu
there exists just one maximal admissible family
\mathcal{F}
with respect to
\nu
so the above definition has sense.

Example 5
.

Let
n=6
,
P_{1}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{4})
,
P_{2}=(x_{1},x_{3},x_{4},x_{6})
,
P_{3}=(x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{6})
,
P_{4}=(x_{1},x_{4},x_{5},x_{6})
,
P_{5}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{5},x_{6})
and set
I=\cap_{i\in[5]}P_{i}
. Then
{\mathcal{F}}=\{P_{1},P_{2},P_{5}\}
,
{\mathcal{G}}=\{P_{1},P_{3},P_{4}\}
are maximal admissible families of
I
with respect of some total admissible order of
[5]
, but
\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})=\operatorname{min}\{3,1+1\}=2=\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{G}}^{\prime})
and
\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}}_{1})=0
,
\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{G}}_{1})=1
which implies
\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}})=1<2=\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{G}})
. Note that
a_{k,{\mathcal{F}}}=a_{k,{\mathcal{G}}}
for each
k\in[3]
.

Remark 6
.

Assume that
a_{\mathcal{F}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
for some
r\in[n]
. Set
{\tilde{S}}=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
and let
{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}=(Q_{i_{k}}\cap{\tilde{S}})_{k\in[t]}
. Then
\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}})=n-r+\operatorname{bigsize}({\tilde{\mathcal{F}}})
.

Remark 7
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}=(Q_{i_{k}})_{k\in[t]}
be a an admissible family of
I
with respect to a total admissible order
\nu
and
r\in[t-1]
. Then
{\mathcal{G}}=(Q_{i_{k}})_{k\in[r]}
is an admissible family of
I
with respect to
\nu
and
\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}})\leq\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{G}})
.

Remark 8
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}=(Q_{i_{k}})_{k\in[t]}
be a a family of
I
with respect to a total admissible order
\nu
. Then
\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}})=r-1+\dim S/(P_{i_{k_{1}}}+\ldots+P_{i_{k_{r}}})
for some
k_{1}<\ldots<k_{r}
from
[t]
.

Example 9
.

Let
n=5
,
P_{1}=(x_{1},x_{2})
,
P_{2}=(x_{2},x_{3})
,
P_{3}=(x_{1},x_{4},x_{5})
and

. Then
{\mathcal{F}}=(P_{i})_{i\in[3]}
is a maximal admissible family of

\nu

because

{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}=(P_{i})_{i=1,2}

\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})=\operatorname{min}\{3,1+2\}=3

\nu^{\prime}

{\mathcal{G}}=(P_{i})_{i=2,1,3}

\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{H}}^{\prime})=\operatorname{min}\{2,1+1\}=2

\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{H}}_{1})=1
. Thus
\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{H}})=\operatorname{min}\{2,1+1\}=2
and

\operatorname{bigsize}_{\nu^{\prime\prime},S}(I)=2
.

Lemma 13
.

Let
\nu
be a total admissible order on
[s]
and
{\mathcal{F}}=(Q_{i_{k}})_{k\in[t]}
a family of
I
with respect to
\nu
. Then

\operatorname{bigsize}(\mathcal{F})\geq\operatorname{size}_{S}I.

Proof.

By Remark
8
we have
\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}})=r-1+\dim S/(P_{i_{k_{1}}}+\ldots+P_{i_{k_{r}}})
for some
k_{1}<\ldots<k_{r}
from
[t]
.
We may suppose that
\sum_{j\in[r]}P_{i_{k_{j}}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{e})
for some
e\in[n]
. Choose for each
p>e
,
p\leq n
an
u_{p}\in[s]
such that
x_{p}\in P_{u_{p}}
. Then
\sum_{j\in[r]}P_{i_{k_{j}}}+\sum_{p=e+1}^{n}P_{u_{p}}={\mathfrak{m}}
and so
\operatorname{size}I\leq r-1+\dim S/(P_{i_{k_{1}}}+\ldots+P_{i_{k_{r}}})=\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}})
.

∎

Next we present a slightly extension of Theorem
1
.

Theorem 14
.

Let
I=\cap_{i\in[s]}P_{i}
be an irredundant intersection of monomial prime ideals of
S
. Then
\operatorname{sdepth}_{S}I\geq 1+\operatorname{bigsize}_{S}(I)
.

Proof.

Using
[
3
, Lemma 3.6]
we may reduce to the case when
\sum_{i\in[s]}P_{i}={\mathfrak{m}}
. Apply induction on
n
. Assume that
\operatorname{bigsize}_{S}(I)=\operatorname{bigsize}(\mathcal{F})
for a maximal admissible family
{\mathcal{F}}=(P_{i_{k}})_{k\in[t]}
of
I
with respect to a total admissible order
\nu
. We may suppose that
i_{t}=s
and
\sum_{k\in[t-1]}P_{i_{k}}=(x_{r+1},\ldots,x_{n})
,
r\geq 1
. Set
S^{\prime}=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}]
,
S^{\prime\prime}=K[x_{r+1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. We may use
[
8
, Theorem 1.6]
even when
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})\not\in\operatorname{Ass}_{S}S/I
(see
[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
).
In the notations of Theorem
1
we have

\operatorname{sdepth}_{S}I\geq\operatorname{min}\{A_{0},\{A_{\tau}:\emptyset\not=\tau\subset[s],J_{\tau}\not=0,L_{\tau}\not=0\}\}.

If
I_{0}=(I\cap S^{\prime})S\not=0
then
A_{0}\geq 1+(n-r)\geq 1+\dim S/P_{i_{t}}\geq 2+\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}}_{1})\geq 1+\operatorname{bigsize}_{S}(I)
.

Now suppose that
\operatorname{sdepth}_{S}I\geq A_{\tau}
for some
\tau\subset[s]
with
J_{\tau}\not=0
,
L_{\tau}\not=0
. Thus
i_{k}
must be in
\tau
for any
k\in[t-1]
because otherwise
J_{\tau}=0
. Then
{\mathcal{H}}=(P_{i_{k}}\cap S^{\prime\prime})_{k\in[t-1]}
is a maximal admissible family of
L_{\tau}
with respect to
\nu
. Note that
\operatorname{bigsize}(\mathcal{H})\geq\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}}_{1})
. By induction hypothesis on
n
we have

\operatorname{sdepth}_{S^{\prime\prime}}L_{\tau}\geq 1+\operatorname{bigsize}_{S^{\prime\prime}}(L_{\tau})\geq 1+\operatorname{bigsize}(\mathcal{H})\geq 1+\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}}_{1})\geq\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}}).

Therefore,

\operatorname{sdepth}_{S}I\geq A_{\tau}\geq 1+\operatorname{sdepth}_{S^{\prime\prime}}L_{\tau}\geq 1+\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}})=1+\operatorname{bigsize}_{S}(I).

∎

Example 15
.

We illustrate the above proof on the case of
\mathcal{F}
given in Example
12
. Set
S^{\prime}=K[x_{5}]
,
S^{\prime\prime}=K[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{6}]
. Then
\tau=\{1,2,3,4,6\}
is the only
\tau\subset[7]
such that

2.
Bigsize and Stanley depth

Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal and
I=\cap_{i\in[s]}Q_{i}
an irredundant decomposition of
I
as an intersection of irreducible monomial ideals,
P_{i}=\sqrt{Q_{i}}
. Let
G(I)
be the minimal set of monomial generators of
I
. Assume that
\sum_{P\in\operatorname{Ass}_{S}S/I}P={\mathfrak{m}}
. Given
j\in[n]
let
\operatorname{deg}_{j}I
be the maximum degree of
x_{j}
in all monomials of
G(I)
.

Lemma 18
.

Suppose that
c:=\operatorname{deg}_{n}I>1
, let us say
c=\operatorname{deg}_{n}Q_{j}
if and only if
j\in[e]
for some
e\in[s]
.
Assume that
Q_{j}=(J_{j},x_{n}^{c})
for some irreducible ideal
J_{j}\subset S_{n}=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}]
,
j\in[e]
. Let
Q^{\prime}_{j}=(J_{j},x_{n}^{c-1})\subset S
,
Q^{\prime\prime}_{j}=(J_{j},x_{n+1})\subset{\tilde{S}}=S[x_{n+1}]
and set

{\tilde{I}}=(\cap_{i=e+1}^{s}Q_{i}{\tilde{S}})\cap(\cap_{i\in[e]}Q^{\prime}_{i}{\tilde{S}})\cap(\cap_{i=s+1}^{s+e}Q_{i})\subset{\tilde{S}},

where
Q_{i}=Q^{\prime\prime}_{i-s}
for
i>s
, the decomposition of
{\tilde{I}}
being not necessarily irredundant. Then
\operatorname{sdepth}_{{\tilde{S}}}{\tilde{I}}\leq\operatorname{sdepth}_{S}I+1
and
\operatorname{sdepth}_{\tilde{S}}{\tilde{S}}/{\tilde{I}}\leq\operatorname{sdepth}_{S}S/I+1
.

Proof.

Let
L_{I}
,
L_{\tilde{I}}
be the LCM-lattices associated to
I,{\tilde{I}}
. The map
{\tilde{S}}\rightarrow S
given by
x_{n+1}\rightarrow x_{n}
induces a surjective join-preserving map

L_{\tilde{I}}\rightarrow L_{I}
and by
[
5
, Theorem 4.5]
we get
\operatorname{sdepth}_{\tilde{S}}{\tilde{I}}\leq\operatorname{sdepth}_{S}I+1
and
\operatorname{sdepth}_{{\tilde{S}}}{\tilde{S}}/{\tilde{I}}\leq\operatorname{sdepth}_{S}S/I+1
.

∎

With the notations and assumptions of Lemma
18
let

{\tilde{C}}=\{i\in[s]:P_{i}{\tilde{S}}\in\operatorname{Ass}_{\tilde{S}}{\tilde{S}}/{\tilde{I}}\}\cup([s+e]\setminus[s]).

Choose
a total admissible order
\tilde{\nu}
on
{\tilde{C}}
and a total admissible order
\nu
on

extending the restriction of
{\tilde{\nu}}
to
[s]\cap{\tilde{C}}
. Let
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}=({\tilde{Q}}_{i_{k}})_{k\in[t]}
be a family of
\tilde{I}
with respect to
\tilde{\nu}
. Replace in
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
the ideals
{\tilde{Q}}_{i_{k}}
by
Q_{i_{k}}={\tilde{Q}}_{i_{k}}\cap S
when
P_{i_{k}}
is maximal in
\operatorname{Ass}_{S}S/I
and
\tilde{Q}_{i_{k}}
is not of the form
Q^{\prime}_{i}{\tilde{S}}
or
Q^{\prime\prime}_{i}
for some

. When
{\tilde{Q}}_{i_{k}}
is of the form

or

for some

Lemma 19
.

There exists a family
\mathcal{G}
of
I
with respect to
\nu
such that
\operatorname{bigsize}({\tilde{\mathcal{F}}})\geq 1+\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{G}})
.

Proof.

Apply induction on
t
. Assume that
t=1
. Then note that
\operatorname{bigsize}({\tilde{\mathcal{F}}})=\dim{\tilde{S}}/{\tilde{P}}_{i_{1}}=1+\dim S/P_{i_{1}}=1+\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}})

when
P_{i_{1}}
is maximal in
\operatorname{Ass}_{S}S/I
and
\tilde{Q}_{i_{1}}
is not of the form
Q^{\prime}_{i}{\tilde{S}}
or
Q^{\prime\prime}_{i}
for some
i\in[e]
. If
\tilde{Q}_{i_{1}}=Q^{\prime}_{i}{\tilde{S}}
for some

then
\operatorname{bigsize}({\tilde{\mathcal{F}}})=\dim{\tilde{S}}/P_{i}{\tilde{S}}=1+\dim S/P_{i}=1+\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{F}})
. Similarly, it happens when
\tilde{Q}_{i_{1}}=Q^{\prime\prime}_{i}
because then

for some

.

Let

\operatorname{bigsize}(\tilde{\mathcal{F}})=t-1+\dim{\tilde{S}}/a_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}

\dim{\tilde{S}}/a_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}\geq 1+\dim S/a_{\mathcal{F}}

. Otherwise,

\overline{\mathcal{G}}
,
{\mathcal{G}}
corresponding to
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
similarly to
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
,
{\mathcal{F}}
corresponding to
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
. By induction hypothesis (

) we have
\operatorname{bigsize}(\tilde{\mathcal{G}})\geq 1+\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{G}})
. Then

\operatorname{bigsize}(\tilde{\mathcal{F}})\geq\operatorname{bigsize}(\tilde{\mathcal{G}})\geq 1+\operatorname{bigsize}({\mathcal{G}}).

∎

.

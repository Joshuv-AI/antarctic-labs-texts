Reflection representations of Coxeter groups and homology of Coxeter graphs
Hongsheng Hu

Abstract
. We study and classify a class of representations (called generalized geometric representations) of a Coxeter group of finite rank. These representations can be viewed as a natural generalization of the geometric representation. The classification is achieved by using characters of the integral homology group of certain graphs closely related to the Coxeter graph. On this basis, we also provide an explicit description of those representations on which the defining generators of the Coxeter group act by reflections.

1.
Introduction

Since the seminal paper
[
11
]
of Kazhdan and Lusztig appeared in 1979, Coxeter groups and their representations have received much more attention than before and huge progresses have been made. But there are still a lot of problems to be investigated.
Compared with the well-established representation theory of (affine) Weyl groups, which are important examples of Coxeter groups, representations for general Coxeter groups are far from being understood.

Given that the concept of Coxeter groups originates from the pioneering work
[
4
,
5
]
by H. S. M. Coxeter on Euclidean reflection groups, it is natural to consider representations on which the Coxeter group “acts like a reflection group”.
In this paper we are mainly concerned with a class of such representations, called generalized geometric representations, of a Coxeter group of finite rank.
We classify these representations (see Theorem
3.13
and Theorem
5.2
) by using characters of the integral homology group of certain graphs closely related to the Coxeter graph.
Moreover, the characters also tell us which generalized geometric representations admit a nonzero bilinear form which is invariant under the group action (see Theorem
4.14
).

It is not hard to find the composition factors of a generalized geometric representation (see Subsection
4.1
and Subsection
5.3
).
In this way we are able to get a class of irreducible representations of a Coxeter group of finite rank.
These irreducible representations are attached to the second-highest two sided cell(s) in the sense of Kazhdan and Lusztig
[
11
,
13
]
(see Remark
4.9
and
[
9
]
for more details).

In the closing section of this paper, the generalized geometric representations and their quotients serve as a foundation for the investigation of the so-called “reflection representations”.

Below we are presenting the definition of the main subject of our study.

Let
W=\langle s\in S\mid(st)^{m_{st}}=e,\forall s,t\in S\rangle
be a Coxeter group with finite generator set
S
and Coxeter matrix
(m_{st})_{s,t\in S}
.
That is,

m_{ss}=1
,
\forall s\in S
;
m_{st}=m_{ts}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}\cup\{\infty\}
,
\forall s,t\in S
with
s\neq t
.

For convenience, we work over the complex number field
\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 1.1
.

(1)

Suppose
V
is a vector space, and
s:V\to V
is a linear map. If there exists a nonzero vector
\alpha_{s}\in V
and a subspace
H_{s}\subseteq V
of codimension one such that
s|_{H_{s}}=\mathrm{Id}_{H_{s}}
(the identity map of
H_{s}
) and
s\cdot\alpha_{s}=-\alpha_{s}
, then
s
is called a
reflection
on
V
.
In particular,
V=H_{s}\oplus\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}
, and
\alpha_{s}
is unique up to a scalar.
We call
\alpha_{s}
a
reflection vector
of
s
, and
H_{s}
the
reflection hyperplane
of
s
.

(2)

Let
V
be a representation of
W
. If
s
acts on
V
by a reflection for any
s\in S
, then
V
is called a
reflection representation
of
W
.

(3)

Let
V
be a reflection representation of
W
.
If moreover the reflection vectors
\{\alpha_{s}\mid s\in S\}
form a basis of
V
, then
V
is called a
generalized geometric representation
of
W
.

Our notion of generalized geometric representations comes from the “standard” reflection representation
V_{\text{geom}}
(called the geometric representation in the classic
[
2
]
).
As a vector space,
V_{\text{geom}}:=\bigoplus_{s\in S}\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}
has a basis
\{\alpha_{s}\mid s\in S\}
, with a bilinear form
(-|-)
given by

(1.1)

(\alpha_{s}|\alpha_{t}):=-\cos\frac{\uppi}{m_{st}},\quad\forall s,t\in S.

Here we regard
\cos\frac{\uppi}{\infty}=1
.
The action of
W
is defined by

(1.2)

s\cdot\alpha_{t}:=\alpha_{t}-\frac{2(\alpha_{t}|\alpha_{s})}{(\alpha_{s}|\alpha_{s})}\alpha_{s}=\alpha_{t}+2\cos\frac{\uppi}{m_{st}}\alpha_{s},\quad\forall s,t\in S.

In other words, the action of
s
is the orthogonal reflection with respect to the bilinear form
(-|-)
with reflection vector
\alpha_{s}
.
In particular, the bilinear form
(-|-)
is
W
-invariant in the following sense,

(1.3)

(w\cdot x|w\cdot y)=(x|y),\quad\forall w\in W,\quad\forall x,y\in V_{\text{geom}}.

As observed in the literature, the representation
V_{\text{geom}}
captures many significant properties of Coxeter groups.
See
[
2
,
10
]
for more details.

Some generalizations of the geometric representation have been explored in previous research.
See, for example,
[
1
,
3
,
7
,
8
,
12
,
17
]
and references therein.
These works emphasize various combinatorial aspects including root systems, the numbers game, the fundamental domain for the group action, etc.
These topics occupy a prominent position within the research of Coxeter groups.

1.1.
Outline of the paper

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
1
, we give our definition of generalized geometric representations and reflection representations.
In Section
2
, we recollect some preliminary knowledge about dihedral groups and graphs.

For simplicity, we assume that
m_{rt}<\infty
for any
r,t\in S
in Section
3
and Section
4
.
In Section
3
, we examine the structure of generalized geometric representations and provide a classification of their isomorphism classes.
Under our assumption, a generalized geometric representation of
W
can be uniquely determined by a datum
((k_{rt})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t},\chi)
.
Here
(k_{rt})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
is a specific set of natural numbers, and
\chi
is a character (i.e., a one-dimensional representation) of the first integral homology group
H_{1}(\widetilde{G})
of a certain graph
\widetilde{G}
.
Our approach is sketched as follows.
Consider arbitrary two elements
r,t\in S
generating a dihedral subgroup of
W
.
The corresponding reflection vectors
\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}
span a subrepresentation of this dihedral subgroup, which corresponds to a number
k_{rt}
with
1\leq k_{rt}\leq\frac{m_{rt}}{2}
.
We then analyze how these subrepresentations of various dihedral subgroups “glue” together to form the whole generalized geometric representation.
It turns out that the information of “gluing” is encoded in
\chi
.

Section
4
consists of four topics concerning some properties of the generalized geometric representations.
Our first consideration is their reducibility.
In this respect, generalized geometric representations behave like the geometric representation
V_{\text{geom}}
(see Subsection
4.1
).
After that, we investigate a larger class of reflection representations, called R-representations, which are closely related to the generalized geometric representations (see Subsection
4.2
).
Next, Subsection
4.3
gives an answer to the interesting question of which generalized geometric representations admit nonzero
W
-invariant bilinear forms.
Finally, in Subsection
4.4
we examine the structure of the dual of an arbitrary generalized geometric representation, which is also a reflection representation.

In Section
5
, we drop the assumption
m_{rt}<\infty
and present relevant results.

In Section
6
, we consider arbitrary reflection representations without any linearly independence condition on the reflection vectors.
Roughly speaking, such a representation factors through an R-representation studied in Subsection
4.2
of a quotient of the Coxeter group
W
.
At this point, we obtain an explicit description of any reflection representation of
W
.

0&0\end{pmatrix},\text{ if }\varrho=\varrho_{t}^{r};

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\text{ if }\varrho=\varrho_{r}^{t}.

Remark 2.11
.

Different from Lemma
2.6
, there may not be a nonzero
D_{\infty}
-invariant sesquilinear form on the representation space of
\varrho_{z}
, for general
z\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.

2.3.
Graphs and their homologies

As mentioned in the introduction, we will “glue” together various representations of dihedral groups, and the way of “gluing” is encoded in a character of the first integral homology group of some graph.
In this subsection we present some basic notions and results on graphs and their homologies.
One may also refer to
[
16
, Ch. 4]
.

By definition, an
(undirected) graph

G=(S,E)
consists of a set
S
of vertices and a set
E
of edges.
Each edge in
E
is an unordered binary subset
\{s,t\}
of
S
.

For our purpose, we only consider finite graphs without loops and multiple edges (i.e.,
S
is a finite set; there is no edge of the form
\{s,s\}
; and each pair
\{s,t\}
occurs at most once in
E
).
Such a graph can be viewed naturally as a finite simplicial complex: vertices as
0
-simplices, edges as
1
-simplices.
In this way, the graph is regarded as a topological space.
Thus we have the notion of
connected components
of a graph.

A sequence
(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n})
of vertices is called a
path
in
G
if
\{s_{i},s_{i+1}\}\in E
,
\forall i
.
If
s_{1}=s_{n}
, then we say the path is a
closed path
.
We do not distinguish the closed paths
(s_{1},\dots,s_{n-1},s_{1})
and
(s_{2},\dots,s_{n-1},s_{1},s_{2})
.
Further, if
s_{1},\dots,s_{n-1}
are pairwise distinct in this closed path, then we call the path a
circuit
.

For a connected graph
G
, a
spanning tree
is defined to be a subgraph
T=(S,E_{0})
with the same vertex set
S
and an edge set
E_{0}\subseteq E
, such that
T
is connected and there is no circuit in
T
.
If
G
is not required to be connected, then we can choose arbitrarily a spanning tree for each component of
G
, and the union of these trees is called a
spanning forest
of
G
.
Spanning forests always exist, but they are not unique in general.

Since a graph
G
is regarded as a simplicial complex, we can consider its homology groups.
In the sense of
[
14
]
, the chain complex (with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
) of
G
looks like:

0\to C_{1}(G)\xrightarrow{\partial}C_{0}(G)\to 0.

Here
C_{0}(G)
is a free abelian group with free generator set
S
, and
C_{1}(G)
is a free abelian group generated by all edges endowed with an orientation (an oriented edge is denoted in the form
(s,t)
).
By convention, we regard
(s,t)=-(t,s)
in
C_{1}(G)
for an edge
\{s,t\}
.
The map
\partial
is defined by
\partial(s,t)=t-s
.

The first homology group
H_{1}(G)=\ker\partial
is a finitely generated free abelian group since
C_{1}(G)
is such a group (see, for example,
[
14
, Lemma 11.1]
).
We regard a circuit
(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n},s_{1})
as the element
(s_{1},s_{2})+\dots+(s_{n-1},s_{n})+(s_{n},s_{1})
in
C_{1}(G)
.
Clearly, it is also an element in
H_{1}(G)
.

Fix a spanning forest
G_{0}=(S,E_{0})
of
G
, and endow each edge in
E
with an orientation.
For any oriented edge
\mathfrak{e}=(s_{1},s_{2})\in E\setminus E_{0}
, there is a unique circuit in
(S,E_{0}\cup\{\mathfrak{e}\})
of the form
(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n},s_{1})
.
We denote this circuit by
c_{\mathfrak{e}}
.

Lemma 2.12
.

With the spanning forest
G_{0}
fixed as above, we have

H_{1}(G)=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{e}\in E\setminus E_{0}}\mathbb{Z}c_{\mathfrak{e}}.

Proof.

Clearly the elements
\{c_{\mathfrak{e}}\mid\mathfrak{e}\in E\setminus E_{0}\}
are
\mathbb{Z}
-linearly independent in
H_{1}(G)
since all edges in
c_{\mathfrak{e}}
lie in
E_{0}
except
\mathfrak{e}
.
Thus we have an injection

\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{e}\in E\setminus E_{0}}\mathbb{Z}c_{\mathfrak{e}}\hookrightarrow H_{1}(G).

Regard
H_{1}(G)
as a subgroup of
C_{1}(G)
.
Then elements in
H_{1}(G)
can be written in the form
\sum_{\mathfrak{e}\in E}a_{\mathfrak{e}}\mathfrak{e}
with
a_{\mathfrak{e}}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Suppose
\sum_{\mathfrak{e}\in E}a_{\mathfrak{e}}\mathfrak{e}\in H_{1}(G)
.
Let

d:=\sum_{\mathfrak{e}\in E}a_{\mathfrak{e}}\mathfrak{e}-\sum_{\mathfrak{e}\in E\setminus E_{0}}a_{\mathfrak{e}}c_{\mathfrak{e}}\in H_{1}(G).

Then any edge that occurs in
d
with a nonzero coefficient belongs to
E_{0}
, the edge set of the forest
G_{0}
.
It is impossible for such an element,
d
, to be a closed cycle unless
d=0
.
Therefore,
H_{1}(G)=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{e}\in E\setminus E_{0}}\mathbb{Z}c_{\mathfrak{e}}
.
∎

2.4.
The Coxeter graph

Let
(W,S)
be a Coxeter group with Coxeter matrix
(m_{rt})_{r,t\in S}
.
The associated Coxeter graph
G
is a graph with labels on some edges.
The set of vertices is nothing but the set
S
of generators.
Two vertices
r,t\in S
are joined by an edge if
m_{rt}\geq 3
.
In this case the edge
\{r,t\}
is labelled by
m_{rt}
.
By convention, the label
m_{rt}
may be omitted if
m_{rt}=3
.
The number
m_{rt}
is merely a label, rather than a multiplicity of the edge
\{r,t\}
.
We denote again by
H_{1}(G)
the first integral homology group of
G
while forgetting the labels on its edges.

If
G
is connected, we say that the Coxeter group is
irreducible
.

3.
Classification of the generalized geometric representations

Let
(W,S)
be a Coxeter group of finite rank (i.e.,
\lvert S\rvert<\infty
) with Coxeter matrix
(m_{rt})_{r,t\in S}
.
For simplicity, from here to the end of Section
4
, we assume:

(3.1)

m_{rt}<\infty,\quad\forall r,t\in S.

This section is devoted to a classification of the isomorphism classes of generalized geometric representations of
W
.

3.1.
The GGR-datum
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}

We begin with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
V
be a vector space and
s:V\to V
be a reflection with a reflection vector
\alpha_{s}
.
Then for any
v\in V
we have
v-s\cdot v\in\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}
.

Proof.

Recall that we have
V=H_{s}\oplus\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}
where
H_{s}
is the reflection hyperplane (see Definition
1.1
).
For any
v\in V
, write
v=v_{s}+a\alpha_{s}
, where
v_{s}\in H_{s}
,
a\in\mathbb{C}
.
Then
s\cdot v=v_{s}-a\alpha_{s}=v-2a\alpha_{s}
.
It follows that
v-s\cdot v\in\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
V=\bigoplus_{s\in S}\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}
be a generalized geometric representation of
W
, where
\alpha_{s}
is a reflection vector of
s
.

(1)

Suppose
r,t\in S
,
r\neq t
. The vectors
\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}
span in
V
a subrepresentation of the dihedral subgroup
\langle r,t\rangle
.

(2)

This representation of
\langle r,t\rangle
, say
\phi_{rt}:\langle r,t\rangle\to GL(\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}\rangle)
, is isomorphic to
\rho_{k_{rt}}
for some
1\leq k_{rt}\leq\frac{m_{rt}}{2}
.

Proof.

The fact that
\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}\rangle
is
\langle r,t\rangle
-invariant follows from Lemma
3.1
.

For (
2
), if
\phi_{rt}
is reducible, then it splits into a direct sum of two representations of dimension one since
\langle r,t\rangle
is a finite group.
The sign representation
\varepsilon
of
\langle r,t\rangle
can not occur in
\phi_{rt}
because
\alpha_{r}
and
\alpha_{t}
are linearly independent.
In view of Lemma
2.1
,
m_{rt}
must be even and
\phi_{rt}\simeq\varepsilon_{r}\oplus\varepsilon_{t}\simeq\rho_{\frac{m_{rt}}{2}}
.
If
\phi_{rt}
is irreducible, then by Lemma
2.1
again we have
\phi_{rt}\simeq\rho_{k_{rt}}
for some
1\leq k_{rt}<\frac{m_{rt}}{2}
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

If
m_{rt}=2
, i.e.,
rt=tr
, then
k_{rt}
must be
1
, and
\phi_{rt}\simeq\varepsilon_{r}\oplus\varepsilon_{t}
splits.

Let
V
be as in Lemma
3.2
.
Suppose
\phi_{rt}\simeq\rho_{k_{rt}}
as in Lemma
3.2
(
2
) for an arbitrary pair
r,t\in S
.
We use the notations
\beta_{r},\beta_{t}
to indicate the chosen basis of the representation space
V_{r,t}
of
\rho_{k_{rt}}
so that the action is defined as in Equation (
2.1
).
Fix an isomorphism
\psi:\rho_{k_{rt}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\phi_{rt}
(but such an isomorphism is not unique).
Clearly,
\psi(\beta_{r})
is a multiple of
\alpha_{r}
and
\psi(\beta_{t})
is a multiple of
\alpha_{t}
, i.e., there exist
a_{r}^{t},a_{t}^{r}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
\psi(\beta_{r})=a_{r}^{t}\alpha_{r}
and
\psi(\beta_{t})=a_{t}^{r}\alpha_{t}
.
Thus, we have

r\cdot(a_{t}^{r}\alpha_{t})=(a_{t}^{r}\alpha_{t})+2\cos\frac{k_{rt}\uppi}{m_{rt}}(a_{r}^{t}\alpha_{r}),\quad t\cdot(a_{r}^{t}\alpha_{r})=(a_{r}^{t}\alpha_{r})+2\cos\frac{k_{rt}\uppi}{m_{rt}}(a_{t}^{r}\alpha_{t}).

Divided by the coefficients on the left hand side, the equations are equivalent to

r\cdot\alpha_{t}=\alpha_{t}+2\frac{a_{r}^{t}}{a_{t}^{r}}\cos\frac{k_{rt}\uppi}{m_{rt}}\alpha_{r},\quad\quad t\cdot\alpha_{r}=\alpha_{r}+2\frac{a_{t}^{r}}{a_{r}^{t}}\cos\frac{k_{rt}\uppi}{m_{rt}}\alpha_{t}.

Note that
r,t\in S

(r\neq t)
are arbitrary.
Therefore the action of
W
on the representation
V
is determined by the numbers
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
.
For later use, we give a name to the sets of such numbers.

Definition 3.4
.

If a set of numbers
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
satisfies the following:

k_{rt}\in\mathbb{N},\quad 1\leq k_{rt}=k_{tr}\leq\frac{m_{rt}}{2},\quad a_{r}^{t}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times},\quad\forall r,t\in S,r\neq t,

then
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
is called a
GGR-datum
of
W
(“GGR” stands for “generalized geometric representation”).

Thus, we have seen that any generalized geometric representation of
W
can be determined by a GGR-datum.

Conversely, suppose we are given a GGR-datum
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
.
We want to show that these numbers can give rise to a generalized geometric representation of
W
in the way above.
Define for each
r\in S
a linear transformation on the vector space
V:=\bigoplus_{s\in S}\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}
by:

(3.2)

r\cdot\alpha_{r}=-\alpha_{r},\quad r\cdot\alpha_{t}=\alpha_{t}+2\frac{a_{r}^{t}}{a_{t}^{r}}\cos\frac{k_{rt}\uppi}{m_{rt}}\alpha_{r},\quad\forall t\in S,t\neq r.

Then for any
r,t\in S
with
r\neq t
, the subspace
\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}\rangle
spanned by
\alpha_{r}
and
\alpha_{t}
forms a representation of the dihedral subgroup
\langle r,t\rangle
isomorphic to
\rho_{k_{rt}}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

The space
V
is a generalized geometric representation of
W
under the action defined by Equation (
3.2
).

Proof.

First of all, we need to verify that (
3.2
) is a well defined
W
-action on
V
, i.e.,
r^{2}\cdot\alpha_{s}=(rt)^{m_{rt}}\cdot\alpha_{s}=\alpha_{s}
,
\forall r,t,s\in S
.
By direct computations, we know that
r^{2}\cdot\alpha_{s}=\alpha_{s}
whether
r=s
or not.

Now suppose
r\neq t
, we consider the action of
(rt)^{m_{rt}}
.
On the subspace
\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}\rangle
,
(rt)^{m_{rt}}
is the identity map since
\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}\rangle
forms a representation
\rho_{k_{rt}}
of
\langle r,t\rangle
.
For
s\neq r,t
(if it exists), we write
U:=\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{s},\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}\rangle
.
Then
\dim U=3
.
By Lemma
2.2
(
1
), there are nonzero vectors
v_{1}\in\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{r},\alpha_{s}\rangle
,
v_{2}\in\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}\rangle
such that
r\cdot v_{1}=v_{1}
and
r\cdot v_{2}=v_{2}
.
If we write
v_{1}=c_{1}\alpha_{r}+d_{1}\alpha_{s}
,
v_{2}=c_{2}\alpha_{r}+d_{2}\alpha_{t}
where
c_{1},d_{1},c_{2},d_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
, then
d_{1},d_{2}\neq 0
.
Therefore,
\dim\mathbb{C}\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle=2
.
Similarly, there are
v_{3},v_{4}\in U
such that
\dim\mathbb{C}\langle v_{3},v_{4}\rangle=2
and
t\cdot v_{3}=v_{3}
,
t\cdot v_{4}=v_{4}
.
Thus, there exists a nonzero vector
v\in\mathbb{C}\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle\cap\mathbb{C}\langle v_{3},v_{4}\rangle\subseteq U
.
We have
r\cdot v=t\cdot v=v
.
By Lemma
2.2
(
2
),
v\notin\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}\rangle
.
As a result,
\{v,\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}\}
is a basis of
U
.
It follows that
(rt)^{m_{rt}}
acts by identity on
U
, and
(rt)^{m_{rt}}\cdot\alpha_{s}=\alpha_{s}
since
\alpha_{s}\in U
.
To conclude, Equation (
3.2
) gives rise to a
W
-representation structure on
V
.

From the arguments above, we see that for any fixed
r\in S
and any
s\in S\setminus\{r\}
, there is a vector
v_{s}=c_{s}\alpha_{r}+d_{s}\alpha_{s}\in V
(
c_{s},d_{s}\in\mathbb{C}
) such that
r\cdot v_{s}=v_{s}
and
d_{s}\neq 0
.
Hence, the vectors
\{v_{s}|s\in S\setminus\{r\}\}
are linearly independent.
They span a hyperplane
H_{r}
with
r|_{H_{r}}=\mathrm{Id}_{H_{r}}
.
Thus
r
acts on
V
by a reflection with reflection vector
\alpha_{r}
.
By definition,
V
is a generalized geometric representation of
W
.
∎

To conclude, any GGR-datum
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
of
W
defines a generalized geometric representation via Equation (
3.2
), and any generalized geometric representation of
W
can be defined by some GGR-datum in this way.

3.2.
The associated graph
\widetilde{G}
and the character
\chi

We need to clarify in what cases two different GGR-data define isomorphic representations.
Before that, we shall associate a graph
\widetilde{G}
and a character
\chi
of
H_{1}(\widetilde{G})
with an GGR-datum
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
in this subsection.

For
r,t\in S
with
r\neq t
, define

\widetilde{m}_{rt}:=\frac{m_{rt}}{d_{rt}},\text{ where }d_{rt}:=\gcd(m_{rt},k_{rt}).

Then
\widetilde{m}_{rt}\geq 2
.
Moreover, set
\widetilde{m}_{ss}:=1
,
\forall s\in S
.
Then
(\widetilde{m}_{rt})_{r,t\in S}
is a Coxeter matrix.
It defines a new Coxeter group
(\widetilde{W},S)
with the same generator set
S
.
Denote by
\widetilde{G}=(S,E)
the Coxeter graph of
(\widetilde{W},S)
.
Then

E=\Bigl\{\{r,t\}\Bigm|r,t\in S,k_{rt}\neq\frac{m_{rt}}{2}\Bigr\}.

We call
\widetilde{G}
the

of the datum
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
.
Since
\widetilde{G}
only depends on the numbers
(k_{rt})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}

\widetilde{G}
is the associated graph of
(k_{rt})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
.

Remark 3.6
.

(1)

Forgetting the labels on the edges, the graph
\widetilde{G}

G
of

with the same vertex set
S
.
An edge
\{r,t\}
in

.

(2)

It may happen that

\widetilde{G}
has several components.

(3)

We have a canonical homomorphism

.

(4)

Let

be the generalized geometric representation of

defined by the datum

(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
.
By Lemma
2.4
, the representation
\rho
naturally factors through

as follows,

\textstyle{W\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho}
\textstyle{GL(V)}
\textstyle{\widetilde{W}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\widetilde{\rho}}

Moreover,
\widetilde{\rho}:\widetilde{W}\to GL(V)
is a generalized geometric representation of
\widetilde{W}
defined by the GGR-datum
(\widetilde{k}_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
of
\widetilde{W}
, where
\widetilde{k}_{rt}:=\frac{k_{rt}}{d_{rt}}
.

The chain group
C_{1}(\widetilde{G})
is a free abelian group generated by edges
(r,t)
(with
k_{rt}\neq\frac{m_{rt}}{2}
) endowed with an orientation (see Subsection
2.3
).
We define a one dimensional representation of
C_{1}(\widetilde{G})
by

(3.3)

(r,t)\mapsto\frac{a_{t}^{r}}{a_{r}^{t}}.

We denote its restriction to the subgroup
H_{1}(\widetilde{G})
by
\chi:H_{1}(\widetilde{G})\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, and call
\chi
the
associated character
of the datum
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
.

.

Suppose the Coxeter graph

of the Coxeter group

is as follows,

3.3.
Isomorphism classes

Suppose we are given two GGR-data
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
and
(l_{rt},b_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
of
W
.
Denote by
V_{1}=\bigoplus_{s\in S}\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}
,
V_{2}=\bigoplus_{s\in S}\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}^{\prime}
the two generalized geometric representations of
W
defined by the two data respectively, with reflection vectors
\{\alpha_{s}\}_{s\in S}
and
\{\alpha^{\prime}_{s}\}_{s\in S}
.
By Lemma
3.2
, we have the following fact.

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose
\varphi:V_{1}\xrightarrow{\sim}V_{2}
is an isomorphism of representations.
Then
k_{rt}=l_{rt}
for any
r,t\in S
with
r\neq t
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\varphi(\alpha_{s})
is a multiple of
\alpha_{s}^{\prime}
for any
s\in S
.
Thus

\varphi(\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}\rangle)=\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{r}^{\prime},\alpha_{t}^{\prime}\rangle

for any
r,t\in S
with
r\neq t
.
The restriction
\varphi|_{\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}\rangle}
is an isomorphism of
\langle r,t\rangle
-representations between
\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}\rangle
and
\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{r}^{\prime},\alpha_{t}^{\prime}\rangle
.
Therefore
\rho_{k_{rt}}=\rho_{l_{rt}}
, and then
k_{rt}=l_{rt}
.
∎

Suppose now
V_{1},V_{2}
are isomorphic.
Then
k_{rt}=l_{rt}
by Lemma
3.8
.
Thus the two data have the same associated graph
\widetilde{G}
.
Denote by
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
the characters of
H_{1}(\widetilde{G})
associated with
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
and
(k_{rt},b_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
respectively (see Subsection
3.2
).

Lemma 3.9
.

Under the assumption
\varphi:V_{1}\xrightarrow{\sim}V_{2}
, it holds that
\chi_{1}=\chi_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n},s_{1})
be a circuit in
\widetilde{G}
.
We write
\alpha_{i}:=\alpha_{s_{i}}
and
a_{i}^{j}:=a_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}
for convenience, and similar for
m_{ij},\widetilde{m}_{ij},k_{ij},

\alpha_{i}^{\prime},b_{i}^{j}
, etc.

Since
(s_{1},s_{2})
is an edge of
\widetilde{G}
, we have
k_{12}\neq\frac{m_{12}}{2}
.
Thus
\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\rangle
and
\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{1}^{\prime},\alpha_{2}^{\prime}\rangle
are irreducible representations of the subgroup
\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle
, both isomorphic to

\rho_{k_{12}}:\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle\to GL(\mathbb{C}\beta_{1}\oplus\mathbb{C}\beta_{2}).

We denote the two isomorphisms by
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
respectively as follows (not a commutative diagram):

\textstyle{\mathbb{C}\langle\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\rangle\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\sim}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{1}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\rangle\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{\sim}
\textstyle{\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{1}^{\prime},\alpha_{2}^{\prime}\rangle}

As in Subsection

, the two maps

(3.4)

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(\beta_{1})

\displaystyle=a_{1}^{2}\alpha_{1},\quad

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(\beta_{2})

\displaystyle=a_{2}^{1}\alpha_{2},

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(\beta_{1})

\displaystyle=b_{1}^{2}\alpha^{\prime}_{1},

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(\beta_{2})

Since

(3.5)

\psi_{2}^{-1}\varphi\psi_{1}(\beta_{1})=\psi_{2}^{-1}\varphi(a_{1}^{2}\alpha_{1})=\psi_{2}^{-1}(xa_{1}^{2}\alpha_{1}^{\prime})=x\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{b_{1}^{2}}\beta_{1}.

is an automorphism of

By Schur’s Lemma

\psi_{2}^{-1}\varphi\psi_{1}(\beta_{2})=x\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{b_{1}^{2}}\beta_{2}.

\varphi(\alpha_{2})=\frac{1}{a_{2}^{1}}\varphi\psi_{1}(\beta_{2})=\frac{1}{a_{2}^{1}}\cdot x\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{b_{1}^{2}}\psi_{2}(\beta_{2})=x\frac{a_{1}^{2}b_{2}^{1}}{a_{2}^{1}b_{1}^{2}}\alpha_{2}^{\prime}.

In simple terms, for the edge

\varphi(\alpha_{1})=x\alpha_{1}^{\prime}{\mathrel{\to}^{\text{ Schur's lemma }}}\varphi(\alpha_{2})=x\frac{a_{1}^{2}b_{2}^{1}}{a_{2}^{1}b_{1}^{2}}\alpha_{2}^{\prime}.

Do this argument for the edges
(s_{2},s_{3}),\dots,(s_{n-1},s_{n}),(s_{n},s_{1})
recursively, and finally we get

(3.7)

\varphi(\alpha_{1})=x\frac{a_{1}^{2}a_{2}^{3}\cdots a_{n-1}^{n}a_{n}^{1}b_{2}^{1}b_{3}^{2}\cdots b_{n}^{n-1}b_{1}^{n}}{a_{2}^{1}a_{3}^{2}\cdots a_{n}^{n-1}a_{1}^{n}b_{1}^{2}b_{2}^{3}\cdots b_{n-1}^{n}b_{n}^{1}}\alpha_{1}^{\prime}.

Compare Equation (

) with
\varphi(\alpha_{1})=x\alpha_{1}^{\prime}
, then we have

(3.8)

\frac{a_{2}^{1}a_{3}^{2}\cdots a_{n}^{n-1}a_{1}^{n}}{a_{1}^{2}a_{2}^{3}\cdots a_{n-1}^{n}a_{n}^{1}}=\frac{b_{2}^{1}b_{3}^{2}\cdots b_{n}^{n-1}b_{1}^{n}}{b_{1}^{2}b_{2}^{3}\cdots b_{n-1}^{n}b_{n}^{1}}.

The two sides of Equation (

(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n},s_{1})
under
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
respectively.
It follows that
\chi_{1}=\chi_{2}
.
∎

\Biggl\{\begin{gathered}\text{the isomorphism classes of generalized geometric}\\

Lemma 3.10
.

For any fixed numbers
(k_{rt})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
, the map
\Theta
is surjective.

Proof.

Choose a spanning forest
\widetilde{G}_{0}=(S,E_{0})
of the associated graph
\widetilde{G}=(S,E)
.
For any
\mathfrak{e}\in E\setminus E_{0}
, choose an orientation of
\mathfrak{e}
.
Let

be defined as in Subsection

Then by Lemma
2.12

H_{1}(\widetilde{G})=\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{e}\in E\setminus E_{0}}\mathbb{Z}c_{\mathfrak{e}}.

Therefore, a character
\chi
of
H_{1}(\widetilde{G})
is determined by the values
\{\chi(c_{\mathfrak{e}})\mid\mathfrak{e}\in E\setminus E_{0}\}
.

Let
\chi
be an arbitrary character of
H_{1}(\widetilde{G})
.
For any
r,t\in S
with
r\neq t
, let

a_{r}^{t}:=\begin{cases}\chi(c_{(t,r)}),&\mbox{if }\{t,r\}\in E\setminus E_{0},\mbox{ and is oriented as }(t,r);\\
1,&\mbox{otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

For any fixed numbers
(k_{rt})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
, the map
\Theta
is injective.

Proof.

Suppose
\Theta(V_{1})=\Theta(V_{2})
, where
V_{1}=\bigoplus_{s\in S}\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}
and
V_{2}=\bigoplus_{s\in S}\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}^{\prime}
are generalized geometric representations of
W
defined by two GGR-data, say,
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
and
(k_{rt},b_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
, respectively.
We need to find an isomorphism
\varphi:V_{1}\to V_{2}
.

It is possible that the associated graph
\widetilde{G}
have several connected components.
In an arbitrary component of
\widetilde{G}
, we choose and fix a vertex
r
as a base point, and define

\varphi(\alpha_{r}):=\alpha_{r}^{\prime}.

For any other vertex
s
in this component, choose a path
(s_{1}=r,s_{2},\dots,s_{n}=s)
connecting
r
and
s
.
We use the notations
\alpha_{i},a_{i}^{j}
, etc. as in the proof of Lemma
3.9
.
Then we define

(3.9)

\varphi(\alpha_{s})=\varphi(\alpha_{n}):=\frac{a_{1}^{2}a_{2}^{3}\cdots a_{n-1}^{n}b_{2}^{1}b_{3}^{2}\cdots b_{n}^{n-1}}{a_{2}^{1}a_{3}^{2}\cdots a_{n}^{n-1}b_{1}^{2}b_{2}^{3}\cdots b_{n-1}^{n}}\alpha_{n}^{\prime}.

We shall show that
\varphi(\alpha_{n})
is independent of the choice of the path (but
\varphi
does depend on the choice of the base point
r
in each component).
Suppose there is another path connecting
s_{1}=r
and
s_{n}=s
, say
(s_{1},s_{p},s_{p-1},\dots,s_{n+1},s_{n})
,
p\geq n
.
The two paths connected end-to-end form a closed path, i.e.,

(s_{1},\dots s_{n},s_{n+1},\dots,s_{p},s_{1}).

This closed path is an element of
H_{1}(\widetilde{G})
.
Since
\Theta(V_{1})=\Theta(V_{2})
, the two characters
\Theta(V_{1})
and
\Theta(V_{2})
takes the same value on this closed path.
In other words, we have the following equality,

(3.10)

\frac{a_{2}^{1}\cdots a_{n}^{n-1}a_{n+1}^{n}\cdots a_{p}^{p-1}a_{1}^{p}}{a_{1}^{2}\cdots a_{n-1}^{n}a_{n}^{n+1}\cdots a_{p-1}^{p}a_{p}^{1}}=\frac{b_{2}^{1}\cdots b_{n}^{n-1}b_{n+1}^{n}\cdots b_{p}^{p-1}b_{1}^{p}}{b_{1}^{2}\cdots b_{n-1}^{n}b_{n}^{n+1}\cdots b_{p-1}^{p}b_{p}^{1}}.

Equation (
3.10
) is equivalent to

\frac{a_{1}^{2}a_{2}^{3}\cdots a_{n-1}^{n}b_{2}^{1}b_{3}^{2}\cdots b_{n}^{n-1}}{a_{2}^{1}a_{3}^{2}\cdots a_{n}^{n-1}b_{1}^{2}b_{2}^{3}\cdots b_{n-1}^{n}}=\frac{a_{1}^{p}a_{p}^{p-1}\cdots a_{n+1}^{n}b_{p}^{1}b_{p-1}^{p}\cdots b_{n}^{n+1}}{a_{p}^{1}a_{p-1}^{p}\cdots a_{n}^{n+1}b_{1}^{p}b_{p}^{p-1}\cdots b_{n+1}^{n}}.

This indicates that
\varphi(\alpha_{n})
is independent of the choice of the path.
Now, we obtain a linear map
\varphi:V_{1}\to V_{2}
.

Clearly,
\varphi
is a linear isomorphism.
It remains to verify that
\varphi
is a homomorphism of representations.
It suffices to check for any

that

(3.11)

\varphi(s\cdot\alpha_{t})=s\cdot\varphi(\alpha_{t}).

If
s=t
, then this is obvious by definition.
If
s
and
t
are distinct and not adjacent in
\widetilde{G}
, i.e.,
\widetilde{m}_{st}=2
, then
s\cdot\alpha_{t}=\alpha_{t}
and
s\cdot\alpha_{t}^{\prime}=\alpha_{t}^{\prime}
, and Equation (
3.11
) is still valid.
Now assume

, then
s
and
t
are in the same connected component of
\widetilde{G}
.
Suppose
r
is the base point chosen in this component, and
(r,s_{2},\dots,s)
is a path connecting
r
and
s
.
Then
(r,s_{2},\dots,s,t)
is a path connecting
r
and
t
.
Suppose
\varphi(\alpha_{s})=x\alpha_{s}^{\prime}
,

.
Then

\varphi(\alpha_{t})=x\frac{a_{s}^{t}b_{t}^{s}}{a_{t}^{s}b_{s}^{t}}\alpha_{t}^{\prime}.

It is then straightforward to verify Equation (
3.11
) by definitions of
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, as follows,

\displaystyle\varphi(s\cdot\alpha_{t})

\displaystyle=\varphi(\alpha_{t}+2\frac{a_{s}^{t}}{a_{t}^{s}}\cos\frac{k_{st}\uppi}{m_{st}}\alpha_{s})

\displaystyle=x\frac{a_{s}^{t}b_{t}^{s}}{a_{t}^{s}b_{s}^{t}}\alpha_{t}^{\prime}+2x\frac{a_{s}^{t}}{a_{t}^{s}}\cos\frac{k_{st}\uppi}{m_{st}}\alpha_{s}^{\prime};

\displaystyle=x\frac{a_{s}^{t}b_{t}^{s}}{a_{t}^{s}b_{s}^{t}}(s\cdot\alpha_{t}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=x\frac{a_{s}^{t}b_{t}^{s}}{a_{t}^{s}b_{s}^{t}}(\alpha_{t}^{\prime}+2\frac{b_{s}^{t}}{b_{t}^{s}}\cos\frac{k_{st}\uppi}{m_{st}}\alpha_{s}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=x\frac{a_{s}^{t}b_{t}^{s}}{a_{t}^{s}b_{s}^{t}}\alpha_{t}^{\prime}+2x\frac{a_{s}^{t}}{a_{t}^{s}}\cos\frac{k_{st}\uppi}{m_{st}}\alpha_{s}^{\prime}=\varphi(s\cdot\alpha_{t}).

∎

4.
Properties of generalized geometric representations

4.1.
Reducibility of generalized geometric representations

Recall
[
2
, Ch. V, §4]
that if
(W,S)
is reducible then the geometric representation
V_{\text{geom}}
decomposes canonically into a direct sum, with summands corresponding to the components of the Coxeter graph
G
.
If
(W,S)
is irreducible, then the reducibility of
V_{\text{geom}}
can be described by the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1

(
[
2
, Ch. V, §4, no. 7]
)
.

Assume
(W,S)
is irreducible.

(1)

The geometric representation
V_{\text{geom}}
is irreducible if and only if the bilinear form
(-|-)
defined in Equation (
1.1
) is non-degenerate.

(2)

If
V_{\text{geom}}
is reducible, then it has a maximal subrepresentation
V_{0}
with trivial
W
-action, and the quotient
V_{\text{geom}}/V_{0}
is irreducible.

In this subsection we will see that the generalized geometric representations admit a similar criterion.
As an application, we obtain in the next subsection a description of some other reflection representations.

Let
V=\bigoplus_{s\in S}\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}
be a generalized geometric representation of
W
defined by a GGR-datum, say,
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
, and
\widetilde{G}
be the associated graph, with corresponding Coxeter group
\widetilde{W}
(see Subsection
3.2
).
Suppose
S=\sqcup_{i\in I}S_{i}
is the decomposition of
\widetilde{G}
into connected components (i.e., for each
S_{i}
, any two vertices in
S_{i}
are connected by a path in
\widetilde{G}
, while for
i\neq j
and
s\in S_{i}
,
t\in S_{j}
, there is no path in
\widetilde{G}
connecting
s
and
t
).
Let
W_{i}
and
\widetilde{W}_{i}
denote the parabolic subgroups of
W
and
\widetilde{W}
generated by
S_{i}
respectively.
The following lemma is clear.

Lemma 4.2
.

Notations as above.

(1)

\widetilde{W}=\prod_{i\in I}\widetilde{W}_{i}
.

(2)

The representation
V
is decomposed into
V=\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i}
, where

V_{i}=\bigoplus_{s\in S_{i}}\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}

is a subrepresentation of
W
.

(3)

For
j\neq i
,
W_{j}
acts trivially on
V_{i}
.

(4)

Each
V_{i}
is a generalized geometric representation of the Coxeter group
(W_{i},S_{i})
, defined by the datum
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S_{i},r\neq t}
, and factors through
\widetilde{W}_{i}
.

Thus, we can focus our study on the direct summands
V_{i}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Notations as above.
Suppose
\widetilde{G}
is connected, and
U\subsetneq V
is a subrepresentation of
W
.

(1)

The action of
W
on
U
is trivial.

(2)

If
V
is reducible, then
V
has a maximal subrepresentation
V_{0}
with trivial
W
-action, and the quotient
V/V_{0}
is irreducible.

(3)

The representation
V
is indecomposable.

Proof.

Suppose
v\in U
and
s\cdot v\neq v
for some
s\in S
.
Then
v-s\cdot v\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\alpha_{s}
by Lemma
3.1
.
Thus
\alpha_{s}\in U
.
If
r\in S
is adjacent to
s
in
\widetilde{G}
, then
k_{rt}<\frac{m_{rt}}{2}
.
We have
\alpha_{r}\in U
since
\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{s},\alpha_{r}\rangle
forms an irreducible representation of
\langle s,r\rangle
isomorphic to
\rho_{k_{rt}}
.
Inductively, for any
t\in S
we have
\alpha_{t}\in U
since
\widetilde{G}
is connected.
Thus
U=V
which contradicts
U\subsetneq V
.
The points (
2
) and (
3
) follow from (
1
).
∎

Remark 4.4
.

If
\widetilde{G}
is not assumed to be connected and
V
is decomposed into
\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i}
as in Lemma
4.2
, then Proposition
4.3
can be applied to each
V_{i}
.

Suppose
\widetilde{G}
is connected.
We label the elements in
S
so that

S=\{s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n}\},

and we use notations
\alpha_{i},k_{ij},a_{i}^{j},
etc. as in the proof of Lemma
3.9
.
Let

v=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}\alpha_{j}\in V
(
x_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
),

then

\displaystyle s_{i}\cdot v

\displaystyle=-x_{i}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}x_{j}\Bigl(\alpha_{j}+2\frac{a_{i}^{j}}{a_{j}^{i}}\cos\frac{k_{ij}\uppi}{m_{ij}}\alpha_{i}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(-x_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}2x_{j}\frac{a_{i}^{j}}{a_{j}^{i}}\cos\frac{k_{ij}\uppi}{m_{ij}}\Bigr)\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}x_{j}\alpha_{j}.

If
v
is fixed by each
s_{i}
, then

(4.1)

x_{i}-\sum_{j\neq i}x_{j}\frac{a_{i}^{j}}{a_{j}^{i}}\cos\frac{k_{ij}\uppi}{m_{ij}}=0,\quad\forall i=1,\dots,n.

By Proposition
4.3
,
V
is reducible if and only if there exists a nonzero vector
v\in V
fixed by each
s_{i}
.
This amounts to saying that the set of equations (
4.1
) has a nonzero solution for the variables
\{x_{i}\mid i=1,\dots,n\}
, which is also equivalent to saying that the following
n\times n
matrix is singular:

(4.2)

A:=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{a_{2}^{1}}\cos\frac{k_{12}\uppi}{m_{12}}&\dots\\
-\frac{a_{2}^{1}}{a_{1}^{2}}\cos\frac{k_{12}\uppi}{m_{12}}&1&\\
\vdots&&\ddots\end{pmatrix}

(All diagonal elements are
1
, and for any
i\neq j
the element at
(i,j)
-position is
-\frac{a_{i}^{j}}{a_{j}^{i}}\cos\frac{k_{ij}\uppi}{m_{ij}}
. Note that
A
is not symmetric in general.)
The co-rank of
A
equals to the dimension of the solution space, as well as the dimension of the maximal subrepresentation of
V
.
To conclude:

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
V
be a generalized geometric representation of
W
defined by a GGR-datum, say,
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
.
Suppose the associated graph
\widetilde{G}
is connected.

(1)

The representation
V
is irreducible if and only if the matrix
A
defined in Equation (
4.2
) is invertible.

(2)

If
V
is reducible, and
V_{0}
is the maximal subrepresentation, then the quotient
V/V_{0}
is irreducible of dimension
\operatorname{rank}(A)
.

Example 4.6
.

In Example
3.16
, if we choose

\vdots&&\ddots&\ddots&-\frac{1}{2}&0\\

Corollary 4.7
.

Suppose

. Let

-action.
Then
V/V_{0}
is semisimple and
\dim V/V_{0}=\rank(A)
.
In particular,
V
is semisimple if and only if
A
is invertible.

4.2.
R-representations

In this subsection we investigate a larger class of reflection representations, relaxing the requirement that reflection vectors form a basis in the definition of generalized geometric representations.
In order to facilitate discussion, we give the following definition.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
V
be a reflection representation of
W
.
We call
V
an R-representation of
W
if

(1)

V
is spanned by reflection vectors
\{\alpha_{s}\mid s\in S\}
;

(2)

for any pair
r,t\in S

(r\neq t)
such that
m_{rt}<\infty
, the vectors
\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}
are linearly independent.

Remark 4.9
.

The motivation to consider R-representations arises from Kazhdan–Lusztig theory.
Roughly speaking, if
(W,S)
is irreducible (i.e., the Coxeter graph is connected), then the R-representations are those reflection representations of
W
such that any nontrivial composition factor of the representation is a quotient of the cell representation given by the second-highest two-sided cell in the sense of Kazhdan and Lusztig
[
11
,
13
]
.
Moreover, for some specific Coxeter groups, it can be shown that any irreducible representation corresponding to this two-sided cell must be an R-representation.
We shall discuss this issue and present relevant results in another paper
[
9
]
.
See also
[
6
]
for relevant results about the corresponding based ring
J
, which carries the structure of the cell representation.

Note that at present we require
m_{rt}<\infty
for any
r,t
(see the beginning of Section
3
), so any two vectors
\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}
are not proportional in an R-representation.
By definition, the R-representations form a larger class than the generalized geometric representations.
But in fact, there is not too much difference between them, as we shall show now.

Theorem 4.10
.

(1)

Let
V
be an R-representation of
W
. There is a unique (up to isomorphism) generalized geometric representation
V^{\prime}
such that
V
is a quotient of
V^{\prime}
, say,
\pi:V^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow V
, and
W
acts on
\ker\pi
trivially.

(2)

The R-representation
V
is semisimple if and only if
\ker\pi
is the maximal subrepresentation of
V^{\prime}
with trivial
W
-action.

(3)

The isomorphism classes of semisimple R-representations one-to-one correspond to the isomorphism classes of generalized geometric representations, and thus to the set of data
\{((k_{rt})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t},\chi)\}
in Theorem
3.13
.
In particular, simple R-representations correspond to those data such that the associated graph
\widetilde{G}
is connected.

Proof.

In an R-representation
V=\sum_{s\in S}\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}
of
W
, the subspace
\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}\rangle
is two dimensional for any
r,t\in S
with
r\neq t
by assumption (
3.1
).
Clearly, the conclusions of Lemma
3.2
are still valid.
Therefore, we can extract a GGR-datum
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
from the R-representation
V
as we did in subsection
3.1
.
Now let
V^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{s\in S}\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}^{\prime}
be the generalized geometric representation of
W
defined by this datum, and
\pi:V^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow V
be the surjective linear map defined by
\alpha_{s}^{\prime}\mapsto\alpha_{s}
.
One may easily verify that
\pi
is a homomorphism of
W
-representations (that is,
s\cdot\pi(\alpha_{t}^{\prime})=\pi(s\cdot\alpha_{t}^{\prime})
).
Thus
V
is a quotient of
V^{\prime}
.
This proves the “existence” part of (
1
).

Suppose
V^{\prime\prime}=\bigoplus_{s\in S}\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}^{\prime\prime}
is a generalized geometric representation defined by another datum
(l_{rt},b_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
, and suppose there is a surjective homomorphism
\pi^{\prime\prime}:V^{\prime\prime}\twoheadrightarrow V
of
W
-representations.
After rescaling the vectors
\alpha_{s}^{\prime\prime}
, we may assume
\pi^{\prime\prime}(\alpha_{s}^{\prime\prime})=\alpha_{s}
,
\forall s\in S
.
For any
r,t\in S
with
r\neq t
, we must have
k_{rt}=l_{rt}
since
\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{r}^{\prime\prime},\alpha_{t}^{\prime\prime}\rangle
and
\mathbb{C}\langle\alpha_{r},\alpha_{t}\rangle
should be isomorphic as representations of
\langle r,t\rangle
.

Consider the following equalities,

\displaystyle\alpha_{t}+2\frac{a_{r}^{t}}{a_{t}^{r}}\cos\frac{k_{rt}\uppi}{m_{rt}}\alpha_{r}

\displaystyle=r\cdot\alpha_{t}

\displaystyle=r\cdot\pi^{\prime\prime}(\alpha_{t}^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=\pi^{\prime\prime}(r\cdot\alpha_{t}^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=\pi^{\prime\prime}(\alpha_{t}^{\prime\prime}+2\frac{b_{r}^{t}}{b_{t}^{r}}\cos\frac{k_{rt}\uppi}{m_{rt}}\alpha_{r}^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}+2\frac{b_{r}^{t}}{b_{t}^{r}}\cos\frac{k_{rt}\uppi}{m_{rt}}\alpha_{r}.

If
k_{rt}\neq\frac{m_{rt}}{2}
, then we must have
\frac{a_{r}^{t}}{a_{t}^{r}}=\frac{b_{r}^{t}}{b_{t}^{r}}
.
Thus, the two data
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
and
(k_{rt},b_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
define the same character of
H_{1}(\widetilde{G})
, where
\widetilde{G}
is the graph associated with
(k_{rt})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
.
By Theorem
3.13
, we have
V^{\prime}\simeq V^{\prime\prime}
as
W
-representations.
This proves the “uniqueness” part of (
1
).

Let
S=\sqcup_{i\in I}S_{i}
be the decomposition of
\widetilde{G}
into connected components.
Then
V^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i}^{\prime}
, where
V_{i}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{s\in S_{i}}\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}^{\prime}
is a subrepresentation, as in Lemma
4.2
.
Denote by
V_{i,0}^{\prime}
the maximal subrepresentation (may be zero) in
V_{i}^{\prime}
.
The action of
W
on each
V_{i,0}^{\prime}
is the trivial action (see Proposition
4.3
).
Note that
\ker\pi
is a subrepresentation in
V^{\prime}
but
\ker\pi
can not contain any
V_{i}^{\prime}
.

Suppose
0\neq v\in\ker\pi
.
We write
v=\oplus_{i\in I}v_{i}
where
v_{i}\in V_{i}^{\prime}
.
Take arbitrarily
j\in I
and
s\in S_{j}
.
For any
i\neq j
, we have
s\cdot v_{i}=v_{i}
by Lemma
4.2
.
If
s\cdot v_{j}\neq v_{j}
, then
v_{j}-s\cdot v_{j}=v-s\cdot v\in\ker\pi
.
By Lemma
3.1
, we have
\alpha_{s}^{\prime}\in\ker\pi
, which is ridiculous.
Thus
s\cdot v=v
,
\forall s\in S
.
More specifically,
v_{i}\in V_{i,0}^{\prime}
,
\forall i\in I
.
Hence,
W
acts on
\ker\pi
trivially.
The proof of (
1
) is complete.

Let
V_{0}^{\prime}:=\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i,0}^{\prime}
be the maximal subrepresentation of
V^{\prime}
with trivial
W
-action.
We have shown that
\ker\pi\subseteq V_{0}^{\prime}
.
By Corollary
4.7
, the quotient
V^{\prime}/V_{0}^{\prime}
is a semisimple representation of
W
.
Moreover,
V^{\prime}/V_{0}^{\prime}
is an R-representation since
\alpha_{s}^{\prime}\notin V_{0}^{\prime}
,
\forall s\in S
.
On the other hand, if
\ker\pi\subsetneq V_{0}^{\prime}
, then there exists
j\in I
such that
V_{j,0}^{\prime}\nsubseteq\ker\pi
.
It follows that
\pi(V_{j}^{\prime})
is an indecomposable but reducible subrepresentation of
V
.
Thus
V
is not semisimple.
This proves (
2
).

At last, (
3
) is deduced from (
1
) and (
2
).
∎

Remark 4.11
.

Let
v=\oplus_{i}v_{i}\in\ker\pi
as in the proof.
It may happen that
v_{i}\notin\ker\pi
for some
i
.
In fact, for any subspace
K\subseteq\bigoplus_{i}V_{i,0}^{\prime}
, the quotient
V^{\prime}/K
is an R-representation of
W
.

Corollary 4.12
.

Let

\pi_{1}:V^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow V_{1}
,
\pi_{2}:V^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow V_{2}

as in Theorem
4.10
, where
V^{\prime}
is a generalized geometric representation of
W
and
V_{1},V_{2}
are R-representations.
Suppose the associated graph
\widetilde{G}
is connected.
Then
V_{1}\simeq V_{2}
if and only if
\ker\pi_{1}=\ker\pi_{2}
.

Proof.

By the same proof of Corollary
3.17
, we have

(4.3)

\operatorname{End}_{W}(V_{i})\simeq\mathbb{C},\text{ and }\operatorname{Hom}_{W}(V^{\prime},V_{i})\simeq\mathbb{C},\text{ for }i=1,2.

If
\ker\pi_{1}=\ker\pi_{2}
, then obviously
V_{1}\simeq V_{2}
.
Conversely, suppose
\phi:V_{1}\xrightarrow{\sim}V_{2}
is an isomorphism.
Then
\phi\pi_{1}:V^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow V_{2}
is a surjective homomorphism.
By (
4.3
),
\phi\pi_{1}=c\pi_{2}
for some
c\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
It follows that
\ker\pi_{2}=\ker\pi_{1}
.
∎

By Corollary
4.12
, if
V^{\prime}
is a generalized geometric representation with connected associated graph
\widetilde{G}
, then the isomorphism classes of R-representations which are quotients of
V^{\prime}
are parameterized by subspaces of the maximal subrepresentation of
V^{\prime}
(a union of Grassmannians).

When
\widetilde{G}
is not connected, the quotients of a generalized geometric representation by two different subspaces may be isomorphic.
The following is such an example.

Example 4.13
.

Suppose
W
is of type
\widetilde{\mathsf{A}}_{2}\times\widetilde{\mathsf{A}}_{2}
, i.e., the Coxeter graph of
W
is as follows,

s_{1}
s_{2}
s_{3}
s_{4}
s_{5}
s_{6}

The geometric representation
V_{\text{geom}}
is a direct sum of two copies of the geometric representation of
\widetilde{\mathsf{A}}_{2}
.
Let
v_{1}:=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}
,
v_{2}:=\alpha_{4}+\alpha_{5}+\alpha_{6}
.
Then
V_{0}:=\mathbb{C}\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle
is the maximal subrepresentation in
V_{\text{geom}}
with trivial
W
-action.
The two quotients
V_{\text{geom}}/\mathbb{C}v_{1}
and
V_{\text{geom}}/\mathbb{C}v_{2}
are clearly non-isomorphic.
However, let
V_{1}:=V_{\text{geom}}/\mathbb{C}(v_{1}+v_{2})
,
V_{2}:=V_{\text{geom}}/\mathbb{C}(v_{1}+2v_{2})
, then the map
\alpha_{i}\mapsto\alpha_{i},i=1,2,3
,
\alpha_{j}\mapsto 2\alpha_{j},j=4,5,6
defines an isomorphism from
V_{1}
to
V_{2}
.

4.3.
W
-invariant bilinear form

Recall that there is a nonzero bilinear form
(-|-)
on the geometric representation
V_{\text{geom}}
of
W
such that
(-|-)
stays invariant under the action of
W
(see Equations (
1.1
) to (
1.3
)).
Under this bilinear form,
W
acts like an orthogonal reflection group on
V_{\text{geom}}
.
Moreover, it can be shown that if
(W,S)
is irreducible then such a bilinear form is unique up to a scalar.
It is also interesting to ask whether such a bilinear form exists on generalized geometric representations.

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
V=\bigoplus_{s\in S}\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}
be a generalized geometric representation of
W
defined by a GGR-datum
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
, and
\chi
be the associated character. Assume the associated graph
\widetilde{G}
is connected.

(1)

There is a nonzero
W
-invariant bilinear form on
V
if and only if

\chi(H_{1}(\widetilde{G}))\subseteq\{\pm 1\}.

(2)

There is a nonzero
W
-invariant sesquilinear form on
V
if and only if

\chi(H_{1}(\widetilde{G}))\subseteq S^{1}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\lvert z\rvert=1\}.

Proof.

Suppose
(-|-)
is a nonzero
W
-invariant bilinear form on
V
.
For an arbitrary path
(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n})
in
\widetilde{G}
, suppose
(\alpha_{1}|\alpha_{1})=x
(here
\alpha_{i}:=\alpha_{s_{i}}
, similarly
a_{i}^{j}:=a_{s_{i}}^{s_{j}}
,
k_{ij}:=k_{s_{1}s_{2}}
).
Note that
\beta_{s_{1}}\mapsto a_{1}^{2}\alpha_{1}
,
\beta_{s_{2}}\mapsto a_{2}^{1}\alpha_{2}
is an isomorphism of
\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle
-representations from
\rho_{k_{12}}
to the one spanned by
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
.
Then by Lemma
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle(\alpha_{1}|\alpha_{1})=x

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{\phantom{\text{ Lemma \ref{lem-2.7} }}}}(a_{1}^{2}\alpha_{1}|a_{1}^{2}\alpha_{1})=x(a_{1}^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{\text{ Lemma \ref{lem-2.7} }}}(a_{2}^{1}\alpha_{2}|a_{2}^{1}\alpha_{2})=x(a_{1}^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle{\mathrel{\to}^{\phantom{\text{ Lemma \ref{lem-2.7} }}}}(\alpha_{2}|\alpha_{2})=x(\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{a_{2}^{1}})^{2}.

Do this argument recursively along the path
(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
, then we get

(4.4)

(\alpha_{n}|\alpha_{n})=x\left(\frac{a_{1}^{2}a_{2}^{3}\cdots a_{n-1}^{n}}{a_{2}^{1}a_{3}^{2}\cdots a_{n}^{n-1}}\right)^{2}.

We claim that
x\neq 0
.
Otherwise, we have
(\alpha_{s}|\alpha_{s})=0
for any
s\in S
since
\widetilde{G}
is connected.
As a result, if
s,t\in S
such that
\widetilde{m}_{st}\geq 3
, then
(\alpha_{s}|\alpha_{t})=(\alpha_{t}|\alpha_{s})=0
by Lemma
2.5
.
While if
\widetilde{m}_{st}=2
, then
(\alpha_{s}|\alpha_{t})=(s\cdot\alpha_{s}|s\cdot\alpha_{t})=-(\alpha_{s}|\alpha_{t})
, and hence
(\alpha_{s}|\alpha_{t})=0
.
Therefore
(-|-)=0
which is a contradiction. Thus
x\neq 0
.

Suppose now
s_{1}=s_{n}
.
Then the path
(s_{1},\dots,s_{n})
is a closed path, and
(\alpha_{n}|\alpha_{n})=x
.
Equation (
4.4
) implies that

\chi((s_{1},\dots,s_{n}))=\frac{a_{2}^{1}a_{3}^{2}\cdots a_{n}^{n-1}}{a_{1}^{2}a_{2}^{3}\cdots a_{n-1}^{n}}=\pm 1.

Thus, the image of
\chi
is contained in
\{\pm 1\}
.

Conversely, suppose
\chi(H_{1}(\widetilde{G}))\subseteq\{\pm 1\}
.
Choose and fix a vertex
s_{1}
in
\widetilde{G}
.
We define
(\alpha_{1}|\alpha_{1}):=1
.
For another vertex
s
, choose a path
(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n}=s)
in
\widetilde{G}
connecting
s_{1}
and
s
.
In view of Lemma
2.5
, we define

(\alpha_{s}|\alpha_{s}):=\left(\frac{a_{1}^{2}a_{2}^{3}\cdots a_{n-1}^{n}}{a_{2}^{1}a_{3}^{2}\cdots a_{n}^{n-1}}\right)^{2}.

We claim that
(\alpha_{s}|\alpha_{s})
is independent of the choice of the path
(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n}=s)
.
If there is another path, say
(s_{1},s_{p},s_{p-1},\dots,s_{n+1},s_{n})
,
p\geq n
, connecting
s_{1}
and
s
, then the two paths connected end-to-end form a closed path

(s_{1},\dots,s_{n},s_{n+1},\dots,s_{p},s_{1}).

Our assumption says

\chi((s_{1},\dots,s_{n},s_{n+1},\dots,s_{p},s_{1}))=\frac{a_{2}^{1}\cdots a_{n}^{n-1}a_{n+1}^{n}\cdots a_{p}^{p-1}a_{1}^{p}}{a_{1}^{2}\cdots a_{n-1}^{n}a_{n}^{n+1}\cdots a_{p-1}^{p}a_{p}^{1}}=\pm 1.

Thus

\left(\frac{a_{1}^{2}a_{2}^{3}\cdots a_{n-1}^{n}}{a_{2}^{1}a_{3}^{2}\cdots a_{n}^{n-1}}\right)^{2}=\left(\frac{a_{1}^{p}a_{p}^{p-1}\cdots a_{n+1}^{n}}{a_{p}^{1}a_{p-1}^{p}\cdots a_{n}^{n+1}}\right)^{2}.

This suggests that
(\alpha_{s}|\alpha_{s})
is independent of the choice of the path.

Moreover, for
t\neq s
, we define

(4.5)

(\alpha_{t}|\alpha_{s})=(\alpha_{s}|\alpha_{t}):=-\frac{a_{s}^{t}}{a_{t}^{s}}\cos\frac{k_{st}\uppi}{m_{st}}(\alpha_{s}|\alpha_{s}).

(The right hand side also equals to
-\frac{a_{t}^{s}}{a_{s}^{t}}\cos\frac{k_{st}\uppi}{m_{st}}(\alpha_{t}|\alpha_{t})
, for, if
\{s,t\}
is not an edge in
\widetilde{G}
, then
\cos\frac{k_{st}\uppi}{m_{st}}=0
; if
\{s,t\}
is an edge in
\widetilde{G}
, then
(s_{1},\dots,s_{n-1},s,t)
is a path in
\widetilde{G}
connecting
s_{1}
and
t
, and thus
(\alpha_{t}|\alpha_{t})=(\frac{a_{s}^{t}}{a_{t}^{s}})^{2}(\alpha_{s}|\alpha_{s})
.)

Now
(-|-)
is a well defined bilinear form on
V
.
We need to check that
(-|-)
is
W
-invariant. It suffices to check

(4.6)

(s\cdot\alpha_{r}|s\cdot\alpha_{t})=(\alpha_{r}|\alpha_{t}),\quad\forall s,r,t\in S.

If
s=r=t
, this is obvious.
If
s=r\neq t
, or
s=t\neq r
, or
s\neq r=t
, then everything happens in a dihedral world, and Equation (
4.6
) holds by Lemma
2.5
.
If
s,r,t
are distinct, then

\displaystyle(s\cdot\alpha_{r}|s\cdot\alpha_{t})

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\alpha_{r}+2\frac{a_{s}^{r}}{a_{r}^{s}}\cos\frac{k_{sr}\uppi}{m_{sr}}\alpha_{s}\Bigm|\alpha_{t}+2\frac{a_{s}^{t}}{a_{t}^{s}}\cos\frac{k_{st}\uppi}{m_{st}}\alpha_{s}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{r}|\alpha_{t})+2\frac{a_{s}^{t}}{a_{t}^{s}}\cos\frac{k_{st}\uppi}{m_{st}}(\alpha_{r}|\alpha_{s})+2\frac{a_{s}^{r}}{a_{r}^{s}}\cos\frac{k_{sr}\uppi}{m_{sr}}(\alpha_{s}|\alpha_{t})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}{}+4\frac{a_{s}^{t}a_{s}^{r}}{a_{t}^{s}a_{r}^{s}}\cos\frac{k_{st}\uppi}{m_{st}}\cos\frac{k_{sr}\uppi}{m_{sr}}(\alpha_{s}|\alpha_{s})

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{r}|\alpha_{t}).

The last equality is by Equation (
4.5
).

The proof of (
2
) is similar (note that we have Lemma
2.6
).
∎

From the proof of Theorem
4.14
, we can obtain the following result.

Corollary 4.15
.

Let
V
be a generalized geometric representation of
W
.
Suppose the associated graph
\widetilde{G}
is connected and
V
admits a nonzero
W
-invariant bilinear form
(-|-)
.

(1)

Such a bilinear form is symmetric and is unique up to a
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-scalar.

(2)

For any
s\in S
, the action of
s
on
V
is an orthogonal reflection with respect to
(-|-)
, i.e.,

s\cdot\alpha_{t}=\alpha_{t}-\frac{2(\alpha_{t}|\alpha_{s})}{(\alpha_{s}|\alpha_{s})}\alpha_{s},\quad\forall t\in S.

Remark 4.16
.

(1)

If
\widetilde{G}
is not assumed to be connected, and we write
V=\bigoplus_{i}V_{i}
as in Lemma
4.2
, then Theorem
4.14
can be applied to each
V_{i}
.

(2)

By Theorem
4.14
, there are only finitely many generalized geometric representations of
W
admitting a nonzero
W
-invariant bilinear form. The geometric representation
V_{\text{geom}}
is one of them.

In the rest of this subsection, we discuss bilinear forms on R-representations.
Let
V
be a generalized geometric representation with connected
\widetilde{G}
.
Suppose
V
is not irreducible, and
V_{0}
is the maximal subrepresentation.

Assume first that
V
admits a nonzero
W
-invariant bilinear form
(-|-)
.
Let
v=\sum_{s\in S}x_{s}\alpha_{s}\in V_{0}
(
x_{s}\in\mathbb{C}
).
Then

(4.7)

\displaystyle(v|\alpha_{r})

\displaystyle=x_{r}(\alpha_{r}|\alpha_{r})+\sum_{s\neq r}x_{s}(\alpha_{s}|\alpha_{r})

\displaystyle=x_{r}(\alpha_{r}|\alpha_{r})-\sum_{s\neq r}x_{s}\frac{a_{r}^{s}}{a_{s}^{r}}\cos\frac{k_{sr}\uppi}{m_{sr}}(\alpha_{r}|\alpha_{r})

\displaystyle=0,\quad\forall r\in S.

The second equality is due to Lemma
2.5
and the third one is due to Equation (
4.1
).
By Equation (
4.7
), we see that
V_{0}
is contained in the radical of the symmetric bilinear form
(-|-)
.

Now let
V_{1}
be an R-representation which is a quotient of
V
and
\pi:V\twoheadrightarrow V_{1}
be the projection. Then
\ker\pi\subseteq V_{0}
.
For any
x\in V_{1}
, denote by
\widetilde{x}
an arbitrary vector in
V
such that
\pi(\widetilde{x})=x
.
Equation (
4.7
) tells us that
(x|y)^{\prime}:=(\widetilde{x}|\widetilde{y})
is a well defined bilinear form on
V_{1}
.
Clearly,
(-|-)^{\prime}
is also
W
-invariant.

Conversely, let
V_{1}
be an R-representation with connected associated graph
\widetilde{G}
, and
V
be the corresponding generalized geometric representation.
Suppose
V_{1}
has a nonzero
W
-invariant bilinear form
(-|-)
.
By pulling back alone the surjection
V\twoheadrightarrow V_{1}
, we obtain a nonzero
W
-invariant bilinear form on
V
.

To conclude, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 4.17
.

Let
V_{1}
be an R-representation of
W
and
V
be the corresponding generalized geometric representation.
Suppose the associated graph
\widetilde{G}
is connected.
Then
V_{1}
admits a nonzero
W
-invariant bilinear form if and only if
V
admits such a bilinear form.

4.4.
Dual of generalized geometric representations

In this subsection we consider the dual representation of a generalized geometric representation
V
.
Roughly speaking, if
V
corresponds to datum
((k_{rt})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t},\chi)
, then its dual
V^{*}
is either a generalized geometric representation or contains an R-representation corresponding to the datum
((k_{rt})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t},\chi^{*})
.
Here
\chi^{*}
is the dual character,
\chi^{*}(-)=\chi(-)^{-1}
.

In the rest of this subsection, let
V=\bigoplus_{s\in S}\mathbb{C}\alpha_{s}
be a generalized geometric representation of
W
defined by a GGR-datum
(k_{rt},a_{r}^{t})_{r,t\in S,r\neq t}
, and
\{\alpha_{s}^{*}\mid s\in S\}
be the dual basis of
\{\alpha_{s}\mid s\in S\}
of
V^{*}:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(V,\mathbb{C})
.
The dual space

forms a representation of

(s\cdot\alpha_{t}^{*})(-):=\alpha_{t}^{*}(s\cdot-),\quad\forall s,t\in S.

A direct computation shows

(4.8)

\displaystyle s\cdot\alpha_{s}^{*}

\displaystyle=-\alpha_{s}^{*}+2\sum_{t\neq s}\frac{a_{s}^{t}}{a_{t}^{s}}\cos\frac{k_{st}\uppi}{m_{st}}\alpha_{t}^{*},\quad\forall s\in S;

\displaystyle s\cdot\alpha_{t}^{*}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}^{*},\quad\forall s,t\in S,t\neq s.

From this, we can see that the action of

is a reflection, with a reflection vector

(4.10)

\gamma_{s}:=\alpha_{s}^{*}-\sum_{t\neq s}\frac{a_{s}^{t}}{a_{t}^{s}}\cos\frac{k_{st}\uppi}{m_{st}}\alpha_{t}^{*}.

Lemma 4.18
.

Notations as above.

(1)

For
r,t\in S
with
r\neq t

r\cdot\gamma_{t}=\gamma_{t}+2\frac{a_{t}^{r}}{a_{r}^{t}}\cos\frac{k_{rt}\uppi}{m_{rt}}\gamma_{r}

(2)

For
r,t\in S
with

\gamma_{r}
and
\gamma_{t}
are linearly independent.

Proof.

The proof of (

), (
4.9
) and (

),

\displaystyle r\cdot\gamma_{t}

\displaystyle=r\cdot\Bigl(\alpha_{t}^{*}-\sum_{s\neq t}\frac{a_{t}^{s}}{a_{s}^{t}}\cos\frac{k_{st}\uppi}{m_{st}}\alpha_{s}^{*}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\alpha_{t}^{*}-\sum_{s\neq r,t}\frac{a_{t}^{s}}{a_{s}^{t}}\cos\frac{k_{st}\uppi}{m_{st}}\alpha_{s}^{*}\Bigr)-\frac{a_{t}^{r}}{a_{r}^{t}}\cos\frac{k_{rt}\uppi}{m_{rt}}(r\cdot\alpha_{r}^{*})

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\alpha_{t}^{*}-\sum_{s\neq r,t}\frac{a_{t}^{s}}{a_{s}^{t}}\cos\frac{k_{st}\uppi}{m_{st}}\alpha_{s}^{*}\Bigr)-\frac{a_{t}^{r}}{a_{r}^{t}}\cos\frac{k_{rt}\uppi}{m_{rt}}\Bigl(-\alpha_{r}^{*}+2\sum_{s\neq r}\frac{a_{r}^{s}}{a_{s}^{r}}\cos\frac{k_{sr}\uppi}{m_{sr}}\alpha_{s}^{*}\Bigr)

(4.11)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{t}+2\frac{a_{t}^{r}}{a_{r}^{t}}\cos\frac{k_{rt}\uppi}{m_{rt}}\gamma_{r}.

We have

\displaystyle\gamma_{r}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{r}^{*}-\frac{a_{r}^{t}}{a_{t}^{r}}\cos\frac{k_{rt}\uppi}{m_{rt}}\alpha_{t}^{*}-\sum_{s\neq r,t}\frac{a_{r}^{s}}{a_{s}^{r}}\cos\frac{k_{sr}\uppi}{m_{sr}}\alpha_{s}^{*},

\displaystyle\gamma_{t}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{t}^{*}-\frac{a_{t}^{r}}{a_{r}^{t}}\cos\frac{k_{rt}\uppi}{m_{rt}}\alpha_{r}^{*}-\sum_{s\neq r,t}\frac{a_{t}^{s}}{a_{s}^{t}}\cos\frac{k_{st}\uppi}{m_{st}}\alpha_{s}^{*}.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let

First, as in the proof of Theorem

r,t\in S
with
r\neq t

6.
Reflection representations

In this section, we investigate reflection representations without any linearly independence condition on the reflection vectors.

6.1.
Reflection representations and R-representations

Let
(V,\rho)
be a reflection representation of
(W,S)
.
By Lemma
3.1
, the reflection vectors
\{\alpha_{s}\mid s\in S\}
span a subrepresentation
U
, and
W
acts on the quotient
V/U
trivially.
Thus, there is no harm in assuming that

V
is spanned by
\{\alpha_{s}\mid s\in S\}
.

Recall that the definition of the R-representations requires that the vectors
\alpha_{r}
and
\alpha_{t}
are not proportional whenever
m_{rt}<\infty
.
Thus it remains to consider the possibility that
\alpha_{r}=\alpha_{t}
(after rescaling) for some
r,t\in S
with
m_{rt}<\infty
.
However, the following lemma is saying that in this case the action of
r
and
t
must be equal, i.e.,
\rho(r)=\rho(t)
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
V
be a vector space and
0\neq\alpha\in V
.
Suppose
\sigma,\tau:V\to V
are two reflections such that
\sigma\cdot\alpha=\tau\cdot\alpha=-\alpha
.
If there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(\sigma\tau)^{m}=\mathrm{Id}_{V}
, then
\sigma=\tau
.

Proof.

Let
H_{\sigma}
and
H_{\tau}
be the reflection hyperplanes of
\sigma
and
\tau
respectively.
Suppose
H_{\sigma}\neq H_{\tau}
.
Then there exists
v\in H_{\tau}\setminus H_{\sigma}
.
Note that
V=H_{\sigma}\oplus\mathbb{C}\alpha
.
Thus we can write
v=v_{\sigma}+a\alpha
where
v_{\sigma}\in H_{\sigma}
and
a\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
By direct computations, we have

(\sigma\tau)\cdot v=\sigma\cdot v=v_{\sigma}-a\alpha=v-2a\alpha.

Therefore,
(\sigma\tau)^{m}\cdot v=v-2ma\alpha
for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, which contradicts
(\sigma\tau)^{m}=\mathrm{Id}_{V}
.
Thus,
H_{\sigma}=H_{\tau}
and then
\sigma=\tau
.
∎

Now we return to the reflection representation
(V,\rho)
.
We define an equivalence relation
\underset{\mathrm{V}}{\approx}
on the set
S
as follows.
We write
r\underset{\mathrm{V}}{\sim}t
if
\alpha_{r}
and
\alpha_{t}
are proportional and
m_{rt}<\infty
.
Clearly,
\underset{\mathrm{V}}{\sim}
is a reflexive relation.
Let
\underset{\mathrm{V}}{\approx}
be the equivalence relation generated by
\underset{\mathrm{V}}{\sim}
, i.e.,
r\underset{\mathrm{V}}{\approx}t
if
r\underset{\mathrm{V}}{\sim}r_{1}\underset{\mathrm{V}}{\sim}\cdots\underset{\mathrm{V}}{\sim}r_{k}\underset{\mathrm{V}}{\sim}t
for some
r_{1},\dots,r_{k}\in S
.

If
r,t\in S
such that
r\underset{\mathrm{V}}{\approx}t
, then by definition
\alpha_{r}
and
\alpha_{t}
are proportional.
After rescaling, we can set
\alpha_{r}=\alpha_{t}
whenever
r\underset{\mathrm{V}}{\approx}t
.
Therefore, if
S=I_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup I_{k}
is the partition of
S
into equivalence classes with respect to
\underset{\mathrm{V}}{\approx}
, then we can denote by
\alpha_{i}
the reflection vector of elements in
I_{i}
(
i=1,\dots,k
), and then
V=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k}\mathbb{C}\alpha_{i}
.
Let
\{s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\mid s_{i}\in I_{i}\}
be a set of representatives.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
(V,\rho)
,
\underset{\mathrm{V}}{\approx}
,
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}
,
s_{1},\dots,s_{k}
be as above.

(1)

If
r,t\in S
such that
r\underset{\mathrm{V}}{\approx}t
, then
\rho(r)=\rho(t)
.

(2)

The image
\rho(W)\subseteq GL(V)
is generated by
\rho(s_{1}),\dots,\rho(s_{k})
as a group.

(3)

If
r\in I_{i}
and
t\in I_{j}
with
i\neq j
, and if
m_{rt}<\infty
, then
\alpha_{i}
and
\alpha_{j}
are linearly independent.

Proof.

Clear by Lemma
6.1
and the definition of
\underset{\mathrm{V}}{\approx}
.
∎

For two indices
i,j
with
i\neq j
, we set

(6.1)

d_{ij}:=\gcd\{m_{rt}\mid r\in I_{i},t\in I_{j},m_{rt}<\infty\}.

(Here “gcd” means the greatest common divisor.)
By convention, we set
\gcd\emptyset=\infty
.
It is clear that
d_{ij}=d_{ji}
.
Moreover, we have the following fact.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
i,j
be two indices with
i\neq j
.

(1)

If
d_{ij}<\infty
, then
(\rho(s_{i})\rho(s_{j}))^{d_{ij}}=\mathrm{Id}_{V}
.

(2)

In particular,

.

Proof.

For any
r\in I_{i}
and
t\in I_{j}
with
m_{rt}<\infty
, by Lemma
6.2
we have
\rho(s_{i})=\rho(r)
,
\rho(s_{j})=\rho(t)
, and hence
(\rho(s_{i})\rho(s_{j}))^{m_{rt}}=\mathrm{Id}_{V}
.
Since
d_{ij}
is the greatest common divisor of such

’s, we have
(\rho(s_{i})\rho(s_{j}))^{d_{ij}}=\mathrm{Id}_{V}
.

If
d_{ij}=1
, then
\rho(s_{i})=\rho(s_{j})
.
But
d_{ij}<\infty
implies that
m_{rt}<\infty
for some
r\in I_{i}
and
t\in I_{j}
.
By Lemma
6.2
(
3
) this is impossible.
∎

Now we define a Coxeter group
(\overline{W},\overline{S})
as follows.
Let
\overline{S}:=\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k}\}
, and
\overline{W}
be the Coxeter group generated by
\overline{S}
with Coxeter matrix

(we set

).
By Lemma
6.3
we have the following factorization of

.

Theorem 6.4
.

Notations as above.

(1)

We have a surjective homomorphism

such that

.

(2)

The map

\overline{\rho}:\overline{W}\to GL(V)
.

(3)

The reflection representation
(V,\rho)
of

as follows,

\textstyle{W\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho}

\textstyle{GL(V)}
\textstyle{\overline{W}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Proof.

For (

.
If

Suppose

Then we have

By Lemma

∎

Remark 6.5
.

The partition

, the group

, and the R-representation

are uniquely determined by the isomorphism class of the reflection representation

.

In conclusion, a reflection representation of

factors through an R-representation of a quotient group

of

.

6.2.
Admissible partitions

In order to classify all the reflection representations of
(W,S)
, we need to determine which reflection vectors can be proportional.
This is equivalent to the question of what kind of partition

will occur as in the last subsection.
Lemma
6.3
(

is permissible.
But in fact, the restriction in Lemma

(

) is almost sufficient.

Definition 6.6
.

Let

be a partition of the set
S
.
As in (

), for two indices
i,j
with
1\leq i\neq j\leq k

(Recall that we set
\gcd\emptyset=\infty

The partition is called an

of

if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(1)

for any indices
i,j
with

;

(2)

for each index

can not be written as a disjoint union

such that

for any

and

.

Let
S=I_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup I_{k}
be an admissible partition.
We set
d_{ii}=1
for any

.
Then
(d_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq k}
is a Coxeter matrix.
We define a Coxeter group
(\overline{W},\overline{S})
with Coxeter matrix
(d_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq k}
and
\overline{S}=\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k}\}
as in the last subsection.
Then by the same reason of Theorem

(

such that

if

.
Clearly, any R-representation of
\overline{W}
pulls back to a reflection representation of
W
along

.
Therefore we have the following classification of reflection representations.

Theorem 6.7
.

The isomorphism classes of reflection representations (spanned by reflection vectors) of
W
one-to-one correspond to the data

\{(S=I_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup I_{k},(V,\overline{\rho}))\}

where

(1)

is an admissible partition of

;

(2)

(V,\overline{\rho})
is an (isomorphism class of) R-representation of
\overline{W}
, where

is a quotient of

.

The trivial partition of

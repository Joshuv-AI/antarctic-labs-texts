Regularity for the steady Stokes-type flow of incompressible Newtonian fluids in some generalized function settings
Minh-Phuong Tran , Thanh-Nhan Nguyen, Hong-Nhung Nguyen

Abstract
A study of regularity estimate for weak solution to generalized stationary Stokes-type systems involving p p -Laplacian is offered. The governing systems of equations are based on steady incompressible flow of a Newtonian fluids. This paper also provides a relatively complete picture of our main results in two regards: problems with nonlinearity is regular with respect to the gradient variable; and asymtotically regular problems, whose nonlinearity satisfies a particular structure near infinity. For such Stokes-type systems, we derive regularity estimates for both velocity gradient and its associated pressure in two special classes of function spaces: the generalized Lorentz and ψ \psi -generalized Morrey spaces. Mathematics Subject Classification. 35D30, 35Q35, 76D03, 42B25, 46E30. Keywords: Regularity; Stokes system; Fractional maximal operators; Generalized Lorentz spaces, ψ \psi -Morrey spaces; Distribution functions, Asymptotically regular problem.

Fractional maximal operators and distribution functions of them are the key tools of our argument. These are classical operators of interest in harmonic analysis and the theory of partial differential equations,

Let us now recall here the definition for the reader’s convenience.

Definition 1.3
(Fractional maximal operators)

Given
\alpha\in[0,d]
, the fractional maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\mathsf{g}(x)=\sup_{\varrho>0}{\varrho^{\alpha}\fint_{B_{\varrho}(x)}{|\mathsf{g}(z)|dz}},

for

and
\mathsf{g}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Notably, as
\alpha=0
, it coincides with the Hardy-Littlewood operator, i.e.
\mathcal{M}_{0}\equiv\mathcal{M}
.

We also include here one of the most important piece of our interest that regarding
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
: the boundedness property, given in the following lemma. Obviously, such the result allows us to conclude in case
\alpha=0
. See
[
58

for detailed proof.

Lemma 1.4

For every
\alpha\in[0,d)
, one can find a constant
C=C(d,\alpha)>0
such that the following inequality

\displaystyle|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\mathsf{g}(x)>\lambda\}|\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathsf{g}(z)|dz\right)^{\frac{d}{d-\alpha}}

holds for all
\lambda>0
and
\mathsf{g}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

1.2
A key point estimate: level-set inequality

In order to state main results in this paper, we organize to specific subsections. The first one forms a key tool to prove regularity results, in which we establish a weighted distribution inequality on level sets of
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
of the gradient of solutions and the assigned data, associated to a Muckenhoupt weight
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
. This result is based on the idea of
weighted fractional maximal distribution functions
, that we are going to detail in a few lines below.

Definition 1.5
(
\mathcal{A}_{p}
classes and Muckenhoupt weights)

Let
1\leq q<\infty
and
\omega\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a non-negative function. We say that
\omega
belongs to the
\mathcal{A}_{q}
class if
[\omega]_{\mathcal{A}_{q}}<\infty
, where

\displaystyle[\omega]_{\mathcal{A}_{q}}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\fint_{B}\omega(z)dz\right)\sup_{z\in B}[\omega(z)]^{-1}},&\quad\mbox{ if }q=1,\\
\displaystyle{\sup_{B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\fint_{B}\omega(z)dz\right)\left(\fint_{B}[\omega(z)]^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}dz\right)^{q-1}},&\quad\mbox{ if }q>1,\end{cases}

for any ball
B=B_{r}(x)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

As in
[
7
]
, it is well known that when
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
, there are positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
and
\iota_{1},\iota_{2}
such that

\displaystyle C_{1}\left(\frac{|E|}{|B|}\right)^{\iota_{1}}{\omega(B)}\leq{\omega(E)}\leq C_{2}\left(\frac{|E|}{|B|}\right)^{\iota_{2}}{\omega(B)},

(1.11)

for all measurable subset
E
of the ball
B
, where
\omega(E)=\int_{E}\omega(z)dz
. Then, we shall simply write
[\omega]_{\mathcal{A}_{\infty}}=(C_{1},C_{2},\iota_{1},\iota_{2})
and for the rest of paper when dealing with Muckenhoupt weights, we further define

\displaystyle\mathtt{data}_{1}:=(\mathtt{data}_{0},[\omega]_{\mathcal{A}_{\infty}}).

Definition 1.6
(Weighted fractional distribution functions (WFMDs))

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
and
\mathsf{g}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The weighted distribution function
\mathbf{d}^{\omega}_{\mathsf{g}}:\,\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}^{\omega}_{\mathsf{g}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=\omega\left(\left\{x\in\Omega:\ |\mathsf{g}(x)|>\lambda\right\}\right),\quad\text{for all }\ 0\leq\lambda<\infty.

Given
\alpha\in[0,d)
, the weighted fractional maximal distribution function (WFMD), denoted by
\mathbf{D}^{\alpha,\omega}_{\mathsf{g}}:\,\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}^{\alpha,\omega}_{\mathsf{g}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{d}^{\omega}_{\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\mathsf{g}}(\lambda),\quad\text{for all }\ 0\leq\lambda<\infty.

(1.12)

With a view to avoid repetition and also for the sake of brevity, in what follows we shall initially assume that
(\mathbf{u},\mathrm{\pi})\in W^{1,p}_{\mathbf{g},\mathrm{div}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\Omega)
is a weak solution pair to system (
1.2
) under the assumptions (
1.3
)-(
1.5
), with given data
\mathbf{f}\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})
and
\mathbf{g}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In the context, we moreover define

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}(x):=|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{p}+|\mathrm{\pi}|^{p^{\prime}},\quad\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}(x):=\sigma^{p}+|\mathbf{f}|^{p}+|\nabla\mathbf{g}|^{p},\quad x\in\Omega.

and use the abbreviations
\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
for the two distribution functions as following

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}(\lambda):=\mathbf{D}^{\alpha,\omega}_{\mathbb{U}}(\lambda),\quad\mathbb{Y}(\lambda):=\mathbf{D}^{\alpha,\omega}_{\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}}(\lambda),\quad\lambda\geq 0.

(1.13)

Now, we are ready to state the first main result of this work: level-set inequality on the idea of WFMDs, a technical tool in proving our regularity theorems.

Theorem 1.7

There is a constant
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}(\mathtt{data}_{1})>0
such that for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, one can find
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\varepsilon,\mathtt{data}_{1})\in(0,1/81)
,
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}(\varepsilon,\mathtt{data}_{1})>0
and
C=C(\mathtt{data}_{1})>0
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}(\mathfrak{a}\lambda)\leq C\left[\varepsilon\mathbb{X}(\lambda)+\mathbb{Y}(\mathfrak{b}\lambda)\right],

(1.14)

if provided an additional assumption
(\Omega,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{H}(\delta_{0},R_{0})
for some
R_{0}>0
.

This theorem has it own original version studied by Acerbi and Mingione in
[
1
]
in the setting of parabolic equations. They initiated an iteration-covering technique, that is completely harmonic analysis free (for instance the good-
\lambda
-inequality) and avoids the use of the maximal function operator, only argued on certain Calderón-Zygmund-type covering lemma. Here, we exploit the level-set argument and distribution functions acting on fractional maximal operators to obtain a series of regularity results for gradient of solutions via
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
. The plan to prove this theorem is based on the use of reverse Hölder inequality, comparison scheme with suitable homogeneous problems and a standard covering argument. Specially, the structural assumptions on
\Omega
and
\mathbf{A}
:
(\Omega,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{H}(\delta_{0},R_{0})
are imposed to carry results up to the boundary. For the proof we refer to Section
4
.

1.3
Regularity estimates in generalized spaces

As already alluded above, we shall focus our attention on the generalized weighted Lorentz and
\psi
-generalized Morrey spaces. Let us now be a bit more precise and describe how these spaces are defined. First, the key feature of our approach is that we address global bounds on generalized Lorentz spaces with two mixed weights. Here, exploiting an idea given in
[
11
]
, we consider a new weight
\mu\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{+})
and a non-decreasing function
\Psi
given by

\displaystyle\Psi(r)=\int_{0}^{r}\mu(s)ds,\quad r\in[0,\infty),

(1.15)

which allows us to formulate the definition of a space with two weight functions
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
and
\mu
taken into account, as follows.

Definition 1.8
(Generalized weighted Lorentz spaces)

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
and
\mu
be two weight functions defined in the above-mentioned. Given
\mathsf{g}
a measurable function on
\Omega
and
\mathfrak{s}\in(0,\infty)
,
0<\mathfrak{t}\leq\infty
, define

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{g}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}}_{\mu,\omega}(\Omega)}:=\begin{cases}\left[\mathfrak{s}\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{\mathfrak{t}}\left[\Psi\left(\mathbf{d}^{\omega}_{\mathsf{g}}(\lambda)\right)\right]^{\frac{\mathfrak{t}}{\mathfrak{s}}}\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}}\right]^{\frac{1}{\mathfrak{t}}},\ &\mbox{ if }\mathfrak{t}<\infty,\\
\displaystyle{\sup_{\lambda>0}\left\{\lambda\left[\Psi\left(\mathbf{d}^{\omega}_{\mathsf{g}}(\lambda)\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{\mathfrak{s}}}\right\}},\ &\mbox{ if }\mathfrak{t}=\infty.\end{cases}

Then, the set of all functions
\mathsf{g}
with
\|\mathsf{g}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}}_{\mu,\omega}(\Omega)}<\infty
, denoted by
\mathcal{L}^{s,t}_{\mu,\omega}(\Omega)
, is called the generalized weighted Lorentz spaces with indices
\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}
and two imposed weights
\mu,\omega
.

The following remarks are related to a few comments from Definition
1.8
.

Remark 1.9

•

When
\omega\equiv\mu\equiv 1
, then
\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}}_{\mu,\omega}(\Omega)
is the classical Lorentz space
L^{\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}}(\Omega)
. Even further,
L^{\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s}}(\Omega)
is exactly the Lebesgue space
L^{\mathfrak{s}}(\Omega)
.

•

With
\mathfrak{t}=\infty
,
\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{s},\infty}_{\mu,\omega}(\Omega)
is also called the generalized weighted Marcinkiewicz space.

•

The mapping
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}}_{\mu,\omega}(\Omega)}
is a quasi-norm if and only if
\Psi
belongs to
\Delta_{2}
-class, which means that there exists
\beta_{2}>0
satisfying

\displaystyle\Psi(2\lambda)\leq\beta_{2}\Psi(\lambda),\quad\mbox{ for every }\lambda\geq 0.

(1.16)

Our main regularity result concerning generalized Lorentz spaces is the following.

Theorem 1.10

Assume that
\mu\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\mathbb{R}^{+})
and
\Psi
is given as in (
1.15
) such that

\displaystyle\beta_{1}\Psi(\lambda)\leq\Psi(2\lambda)\leq\beta_{2}\Psi(\lambda),\quad\forall\lambda\geq 0,

(1.17)

for some
\beta_{2}>\beta_{1}>1
. For every
\mathfrak{s}\in(0,\infty)
and
0<\mathfrak{t}\leq\infty
, there exists
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\mathtt{data}_{2})\in(0,1/81)
such that if
(\Omega,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{H}(\delta_{0},R_{0})
for some
R_{0}>0
, then the estimate

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{p}+|\mathrm{\pi}|^{p^{\prime}})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}}_{\mu,\omega}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\sigma^{p}+|\mathbf{f}|^{p}+|\nabla\mathbf{g}|^{p})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}}_{\mu,\omega}(\Omega)}

(1.18)

holds true with a constant
C
depending on
\mathtt{data}_{2}:=(\mathtt{data}_{1},\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t},\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
.

Definition 1.11
(
\psi
-generalized Morrey spaces)

Let
\psi:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a measurable function. Then, a measurable map
\mathsf{g}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is said to belong to the
\psi
-generalized Morrey space
\mathrm{M}^{\mathfrak{s},\psi}(\Omega)
with
\mathfrak{s}\in(0,\infty)
if

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{g}\|_{\mathrm{M}^{\mathfrak{s},\psi}(\Omega)}:=\sup_{y\in\Omega;\,0<\varrho<\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{1}{\psi(x,\varrho)}\int_{\Omega_{\varrho}(y)}|\mathsf{g}(z)|^{\mathfrak{s}}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{\mathfrak{s}}}<\infty.

(1.19)

As a consequence of the previous result, the second regularity result reveals the gradient bound of weak solution pair in
\psi
-generalized Morrey spaces. Let us report in Theorem
1.12
below.

Theorem 1.12

Let
\psi:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a measurable function such that there is
\beta_{0}\in(1,2^{d})
satisfying

\displaystyle\psi(x,2\varrho)\leq\beta_{0}\psi(x,\varrho),\quad\forall x\in\Omega\mbox{ and }0<\varrho<\mathrm{diam}(\Omega).

(1.20)

Then, for every
\mathfrak{s}\in(0,\infty)
, there exists
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\mathtt{data}_{3})\in(0,1/81)
such that if
(\Omega,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{H}(\delta_{0},R_{0})
for some
R_{0}>0
, it holds

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{p}+|\mathrm{\pi}|^{p^{\prime}})\|_{\mathrm{M}^{\mathfrak{s},\psi}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(\sigma^{p}+|\mathbf{f}|^{p}+|\nabla\mathbf{g}|^{p})\|_{\mathrm{M}^{\mathfrak{s},\psi}(\Omega)},

(1.21)

where the constant
C
depends on
\mathtt{data}_{3}:=(\mathtt{data}_{0},\mathfrak{s},\beta_{0})
.

1.4
A correlation to asymptotically regular problems

The next result is devoted to study in details the regularity estimates for asymptotically regular Stokes system
1.8
. Let us present in the following the definition of so-called
asymptotically
\delta_{0}>0
-regular problem
. For additional reading, one can refer to
[
62
, Theorem 2.14]
and
[
6
, Definition 2.2]
for some of precise statements.

Definition 1.13
(Asymptotically
\delta_{0}
-regular problem)

Let
\mathbf{A}(x,\nu)
be a regular function that satisfying (
1.3
) and (
1.4
). Then, we say that
\mathbf{a}(x,\nu)
is asymptotically
\delta_{0}
-regular with
\mathbf{A}(x,\nu)
if there exists a uniformly bounded nonnegative function
\epsilon:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}{\epsilon(\lambda)}}\leq\delta_{0},

and

\displaystyle|\mathbf{a}(x,\nu)-\mathbf{A}(x,\nu)|\leq\epsilon(|\nu|)(1+|\nu|^{p-1}),

for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and all
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}
.

Remark 1.14

For any sufficiently small
\delta_{0}>0
, if
\mathbf{a}(x,\nu)
is asymptotically
\delta_{0}
-regular with
\mathbf{A}(x,\nu)
, then one has that

\displaystyle\limsup_{|\nu|\to\infty}{\frac{|\mathbf{a}(x,\nu)-\mathbf{A}(x,\nu)|}{(\sigma^{2}+|\nu|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}}}\leq\delta_{0}

(1.22)

uniformly with respect to
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It remarks that, as mentioned in
[
6
]
, the asymptotically
\delta_{0}
-regular condition given in (
1.22
) is weaker than the one described in
[
62
]
. Throughout this paper we will assume that
\mathbf{a}(x,\nu)
is asymptotically
\delta_{0}
-regular with operator
\mathbf{A}(x,\nu)
in problem (
1.2
) in the sense of (
1.22
), where
\delta_{0}
will be selected later on.

Directly follow from previous results, it allows us to state the global gradient boundedness for solution pair to asymptotically regular systems of Stokes type in (
1.8
) that settled in corresponding generalized function spaces. The next main result of the paper reads as follows.

Theorem 1.15

Let
0<\mathfrak{s}<\infty
,
0<\mathfrak{t}\leq\infty
and
\mu,\Psi,\psi
be given as in (
1.17
), (
1.20
). Suppose that
\mathbf{a}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}
is a Carathédory matrix-valued function. Assume moreover that
(\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}},\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{a}})\in W^{1,p}_{\mathbf{g},\mathrm{div}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\Omega)
is a pair of solutions to system (
1.8
). There exists
\delta_{0}\in(0,1/81)
such that if
(\Omega,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{H}(\delta_{0},R_{0})
for some
R_{0}>0
and (
1.22
) is valid, then the following estimates

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}}|^{p}+|\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{a}}|^{p^{\prime}})\|_{\mathbb{L}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}(1+|\mathbf{f}|^{p}+|\nabla\mathbf{g}|^{p})\|_{\mathbb{L}(\Omega)},

(1.23)

holds. Here, for the sake of shortness, we write
\mathbb{L}(\Omega)=\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}}_{\mu,\omega}(\Omega)
or

corresponding to the generalized weighted Lorentz or

The rest part of this paper is arranged as follows. The core of Section

is to prove the global a priori estimate of weak solution to our problem. Section

that proving main theorems stated above: Theorem
1.10
and Theorem
1.12

.

2
Global a priori estimate for weak solution

This section aims to state and prove a theorem that enables us to establish a priori estimate for weak solution pair
(\mathbf{u},\pi)
to steady problem (
1.2
). Let us first reproduce here the solvability result of divergence equations presented by Acosta
et al.
in
[
2
]
. Regarding that study, via a constructive approach, existence of solutions to the divergence equation on a bounded John domain was proved.

Lemma 2.1

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded John domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
h\in L^{q}_{\mathrm{int}}(\Omega)
for some
q>1
. Then there exists
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
C=C(\Omega,d,q)>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}(\varphi)=h\ \mbox{ in }\Omega,\mbox{ and }\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}^{d})}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}.

Remark 2.2

It should also be noted that in the existence proof pointed out in Lemma
2.1
, domain to be considered is the John domain. However, as shown in
[
44
,
23
]
, any domain that is flat in the sense of Reifenberg is also a John domain. We will make use of this fact and in what follows, the only assumption
(\Omega,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{H}(\delta_{0},R_{0})
is imposed to our problems.

Lemma 2.3

Let
\mathbf{f}\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})
and
\mathbf{g}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. There exists a unique weak solution pair
(\mathbf{u},\mathrm{\pi})\in W^{1,p}_{\mathbf{g},\mathrm{div}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\Omega)
to system (
1.2
) such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}+\|\mathrm{\pi}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\sigma+\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}+\|\nabla\mathbf{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}\right),

(2.1)

where
C=C(\Omega,d,\Upsilon,p)>0
. In particular, there holds

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{\pi}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\sigma+\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}+\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}\right).

(2.2)

Proof.
The existence and uniqueness of weak solutions have been proved by appealing the theory of monotone operators combined with compactness arguments (cf.
[
41
,
42
,
43
,
45
]
). Let us start putting into (
1.6
) the test function
\varphi=\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{g}
, we infer that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathbf{A}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u}),\nabla\mathbf{u}\rangle dx=\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathbf{A}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u}),\nabla\mathbf{g}\rangle dx+\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathbf{B}(x,\mathbf{f}),\nabla\mathbf{u}\rangle dx-\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathbf{B}(x,\mathbf{f}),\nabla\mathbf{g}\rangle dx.

With the use of (
1.3
), (
1.4
), (
1.5
), it allows us to get

\displaystyle\Upsilon^{-1}\int_{\Omega}(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\Upsilon\int_{\Omega}\sigma^{p-1}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|dx+\Upsilon\int_{\Omega}(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|\nabla\mathbf{g}|dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\Upsilon\int_{\Omega}(\sigma^{2}+|\mathbf{f}|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|dx+\Upsilon\int_{\Omega}(\sigma^{2}+|\mathbf{f}|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|\nabla\mathbf{g}|dx.

(2.3)

At this stage, thanks to Young’s inequality, for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant depending on
p,\epsilon
such that the following estimate holds

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{p}\leq\epsilon\left(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+C(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2},

and from (
2
), this implies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq 3\epsilon\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{p}dx+C_{\epsilon}\int_{\Omega}(\sigma^{p}+|\mathbf{f}|^{p}+|\nabla\mathbf{g}|^{p})dx.

(2.4)

By choosing a suitable value of
\epsilon>0
in (
2.4
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}\leq C\left(\sigma+\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}+\|\nabla\mathbf{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}\right).

(2.5)

Next, let us consider the following functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\varphi):=\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathbf{A}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{B}(x,\mathbf{f}),\nabla\varphi\rangle dx,\quad\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Thanks to (
1.7
), we know that
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)=0
for all
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. On the other hand, we are allowed to apply (
1.3
), (
1.5
) and Hölder’s inequality that for every
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it yields that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}(\varphi)|

\displaystyle\leq\Upsilon\left(\int_{\Omega}(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|\nabla\varphi|dx+\int_{\Omega}(\sigma^{2}+|\mathbf{f}|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|\nabla\varphi|dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sigma+\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}+\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}\right)\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}.

(2.6)

It follows from inequality (
2.5
) that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}(\varphi)|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sigma+\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}+\|\nabla\mathbf{g}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}\right)\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})},

and we are able to conclude that
\mathcal{F}
is a bounded linear functional on
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. At this point, thanks to
[
30
, Theorem III.5.3]
, there exists a uniquely determined
\mathrm{\pi}\in L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\Omega)
such that

\mathcal{F}(\varphi)=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{\pi}\mathrm{div}(\varphi)dx,

for all
\varphi\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Finally, it concludes that
(\mathbf{u},\mathrm{\pi})\in W^{1,p}_{\mathbf{g},\mathrm{div}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\Omega)
is the unique pair of solutions to (
1.2
).

In order to deal with (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), let us now consider

\displaystyle h:=|\mathrm{\pi}|^{p^{\prime}-2}\mathrm{\pi}-\fint_{\Omega}|\mathrm{\pi}|^{p^{\prime}-2}\mathrm{\pi}dx,

and it is easy to check that
h\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{int}}(\Omega)
and
\|h\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|\mathrm{\pi}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{p^{\prime}-1}
. Applying Lemma
2.1
, there exists
\varphi_{h}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
C=C(\Omega,d,p)>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}(\varphi_{h})=h\ \mbox{ in }\Omega,\mbox{ and }\|\nabla\varphi_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathrm{R}^{d})}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}.

(2.7)

Here, keeping in mind that
\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{\pi}dx=0
, we therefore obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\mathrm{\pi}|^{p^{\prime}}dx=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{\pi}\left(|\mathrm{\pi}|^{p^{\prime}-2}\mathrm{\pi}-\fint_{\Omega}|\mathrm{\pi}|^{p^{\prime}-2}\mathrm{\pi}dx\right)dx=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{\pi}hdx=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{\pi}\mathrm{div}(\varphi_{h})dx=\mathcal{F}(\varphi_{h}).

Inserting (
2
) and (
2.7
) into this equality, one gets that

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{\pi}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sigma+\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}+\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}\right)\|\nabla\varphi_{h}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sigma+\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}+\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}\right)\|h\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sigma+\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}+\|\mathbf{f}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}\right)\|\mathrm{\pi}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}^{p^{\prime}-1},

and this completes the proof of (
2.2
). Finally, (
2.1
) holds true by combining (
2.5
) and (
2.2
).

3
Comparison procedures

As already described, our general plan to prove Theorem
1.7
is to establish a series of comparison procedures with reference problems. We are going to separately study and prove suitable comparison estimates for solution
(\mathbf{u},\mathrm{\pi})
to the original problem in several steps. Sections
3.1
and
3.2

3.1
Interior comparison scheme

As the first step, we establish the comparison estimate between weak solution
(\mathbf{u},\mathrm{\pi})
of the original inhomogeneous system and solution
(\mathbf{v},\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{v}})
of the associated homogeneous one. This is the content of the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1

Let
(\mathbf{u},\mathrm{\pi})\in W^{1,p}_{\mathbf{g},\mathrm{div}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\Omega)
be a weak solution pair of system (
1.2
) with the given data
\mathbf{f}\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})
and
\mathbf{g}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
R>0
such that
\mathfrak{B}_{4}:=B_{4R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
, assume that
(\mathbf{v},\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{v}})\in W^{1,p}_{\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{g},\mathrm{div}}(\mathfrak{B}_{4};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathfrak{B}_{4})
is the unique solution to the following problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{A}(x,\nabla\mathbf{v}))+\nabla\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{v}}&=\ 0\hskip 35.56593pt\ \,\text{in}\ \mathfrak{B}_{4},\\
\hskip 39.83368pt\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{v})&=\ 0\hskip 36.98866pt\ \text{in}\ \mathfrak{B}_{4},\\
\hskip 51.21504pt\mathbf{v}&=\ \mathbf{u}-\mathbf{g}\hskip 19.91684pt\text{on}\ \partial\mathfrak{B}_{4}.\end{cases}

(3.1)

Then, there exist two constants
C=C(d,\Upsilon,p)>0
and
\overline{p}=\overline{p}(p)>0
such that

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}|\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{p}+|\mathrm{\pi}-\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{v}}|^{p^{\prime}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\left(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+C\varepsilon^{-\overline{p}}\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\left(\sigma^{p}+|\mathbf{f}|^{p}+|\nabla\mathbf{g}|^{p}\right)dx,

(3.2)

for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.

Proof.
First, using the fact that
(\mathbf{u},\mathrm{\pi})\in W^{1,p}_{\mathbf{g},\mathrm{div}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to (
1.2
), we readily obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathbf{A}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u}),\nabla\varphi\rangle dx=\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathbf{B}(x,\mathbf{f}),\nabla\varphi\rangle dx,

(3.3)

for all
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and further,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathbf{A}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u}),\nabla\varphi\rangle dx-\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{\pi}\mathrm{div}(\varphi)dx=\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathbf{B}(x,\mathbf{f}),\nabla\varphi\rangle dx,

(3.4)

for all
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In a similar fashion, the pair
(\mathbf{v},\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{v}})\in W^{1,p}_{\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{g},\mathrm{div}}(\mathfrak{B}_{4};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathfrak{B}_{4})
is a unique solution to homogeneous system (
3.1
), we also have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\langle\mathbf{A}(x,\nabla\mathbf{v}),\nabla\varphi\rangle dx=0,

(3.5)

for every
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\mathfrak{B}_{4};\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\langle\mathbf{A}(x,\nabla\mathbf{v}),\nabla\varphi\rangle dx-\int_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{v}}\mathrm{div}(\varphi)dx=0,

(3.6)

We test the weak formulations (
3.3
) and (
3.5
) with function
\varphi=\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{g}-\overline{\mathbf{v}}\in W^{1,p}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\mathfrak{B}_{4};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\overline{\mathbf{v}}
is defined in
\Omega
according to the form:
\overline{\mathbf{v}}=\mathbf{v}
in
\mathfrak{B}_{4}
and
\overline{\mathbf{v}}=\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{g}
in
\Omega\setminus\mathfrak{B}_{4}
, it then yields

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\langle\mathbf{A}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{A}(x,\nabla\mathbf{v}),\nabla(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{v})\rangle dx=\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\langle\mathbf{B}(x,\mathbf{f}),\nabla(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{g}-\mathbf{v})\rangle dx.

(3.7)

Exploiting the structure assumptions (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) and (
1.5
) on both the operators
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
, from (
3.7
), it enables us to deduce that

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\Phi(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})dx

\displaystyle\leq\Upsilon^{2}\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\left[(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}+(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\right]|\nabla\mathbf{g}|dx

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\Upsilon^{2}\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\left(\sigma^{2}+|\mathbf{f}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\left(|\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\mathbf{v}|+|\nabla\mathbf{g}|\right)dx,

(3.8)

where for the sake of shortness, we introduce the further following function
\Phi:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
defined as

\displaystyle\Phi(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}):=\left(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{2}.

(3.9)

Applying Young’s inequality and reabsorb into the right-hand side of (
3.1
), for every
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}>0
to arrive at

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\Phi(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})dx

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon_{1}\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}dx+\epsilon_{2}\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}|\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+C\left(\epsilon_{1}^{-(p-1)}+\epsilon_{2}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\left(\sigma^{p}+|\mathbf{f}|^{p}+|\nabla\mathbf{g}|^{p}\right)dx,

(3.10)

where
C
is a constant depending only on
\Upsilon,p
. Again, carefully exploiting Young’s inequality for every
\epsilon_{3}>0
, it finds
C=C(p)>0
such that

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{p}\leq\chi_{\{p<2\}}\epsilon_{3}\left(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+C\epsilon_{3}^{-\tilde{p}}\Phi(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}),

(3.11)

where
\chi_{\{p<2\}}
and
\tilde{p}
are defined as

\displaystyle\chi_{\{p<2\}}=\begin{cases}1,\ &\mbox{ if }p<2,\\
0,\ &\mbox{ if }p\geq 2,\end{cases}\ \mbox{ and }\ \tilde{p}=\max\left\{0;\frac{2}{p}-1\right\}.

Combining (
3.11
) with the comparison estimate from (
3.1
), it leads us to obtain

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}|\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\chi_{\{p<2\}}\epsilon_{3}+C\epsilon_{3}^{-\tilde{p}}\epsilon_{1}\right)\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\left(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\quad+C\epsilon_{3}^{-\tilde{p}}\epsilon_{2}\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}|\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+C\epsilon_{3}^{-\tilde{p}}\left(\epsilon_{1}^{-(p-1)}+\epsilon_{2}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\right)\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\left(\sigma^{p}+|\mathbf{f}|^{p}+|\nabla\mathbf{g}|^{p}\right)dx.

(3.12)

Having arrived at this stage, for every
\varepsilon>0
, let us choose
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3}>0
depending on
\varepsilon
such that

\displaystyle\chi_{\{p<2\}}\epsilon_{3}+C\epsilon_{3}^{-\tilde{p}}\epsilon_{1}=\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\quad C\epsilon_{3}^{-\tilde{p}}\epsilon_{2}=\frac{1}{2},

and then, re-absorb in (
3.1
), it yields the following estimate

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}|\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\left(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+C\varepsilon^{-\overline{p}}\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\left(\sigma^{p}+|\mathbf{f}|^{p}+|\nabla\mathbf{g}|^{p}\right)dx.

(3.13)

Here, for ease of notation, we denote where
\overline{p}
by

\displaystyle\overline{p}:=\max\left\{\frac{p\tilde{p}}{p-1};\,p\tilde{p}+p-1\right\}=\begin{cases}p-1,&\quad\mbox{if }p\geq 2,\\
1,&\quad\mbox{if }\frac{3}{2}<p<2,\\
\frac{2-p}{p-1},&\quad\mbox{if }1<p\leq\frac{3}{2}.\end{cases}

(3.14)

Next, we will infer a comparison between two associated pressures. From (
3.4
) and (
3.6
), it leads us to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\langle\mathbf{A}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{A}(x,\nabla\mathbf{v}),\nabla\varphi\rangle dx-\int_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}(\mathrm{\pi}-\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{v}})\mathrm{div}(\varphi)dx=\int_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\langle\mathbf{B}(x,\mathbf{f}),\nabla\varphi\rangle dx,

for all
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathfrak{B}_{4};\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Finally, the strategy of proof is now to apply (
2.2
) in Lemma
2.3
to deduce

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}|\mathrm{\pi}-\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{v}}|^{p^{\prime}}dx\leq C\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\left(\sigma^{p}+|\mathbf{f}|^{p}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{p}\right)dx,

and the desired result follows by (
3.1
). This finishes the proof

The second step consists in considering homogeneous constant coefficients problem and establishing the comparison estimate between weak solution pairs. A bit more precise, we shall focus on the new problem where the matrix-valued function
\mathbf{A}
just keeps integral average over
B_{2R}(x_{0})

Under the assumptions of Lemma

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}(\overline{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}(\nabla\mathbf{w}))+\nabla\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{w}}&=\ 0\hskip 35.56593pt\ \,\text{in}\ \mathfrak{B}_{2},\\
\hskip 39.83368pt\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{w})&=\ 0\hskip 36.98866pt\ \text{in}\ \mathfrak{B}_{2},\\
\hskip 51.21504pt\mathbf{w}&=\ \mathbf{v}\hskip 39.83368pt\text{on}\ \partial\mathfrak{B}_{2}.\end{cases}

(3.15)

Then, there exist
\kappa=\kappa(d,\Upsilon,p)>0
and
C=C(d,\Upsilon,p)>0

[\mathbf{A}]_{R_{0}}\leq\delta
for some

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}|\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\mathbf{w}|^{p}+|\mathrm{\pi}-\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{w}}|^{p^{\prime}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\delta^{\kappa}\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\left(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+C\delta^{-\overline{p}\kappa}\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\left(\sigma^{p}+|\mathbf{f}|^{p}+|\nabla\mathbf{g}|^{p}\right)dx

(3.16)

holds true, where
\overline{p}
has been defined in (
3.14
). Moreover, there holds

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathbf{w}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{B}_{1};\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}})}^{p}+\|\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{w}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{B}_{1})}^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\left(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+C\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\left(\sigma^{p}+|\mathbf{f}|^{p}+|\nabla\mathbf{g}|^{p}\right)dx,

(3.17)

where the constant
C

(d,\Upsilon,p)

Proof.
First, we go once back to the homogeneous problem (
3.1
) with a unique solution
(\mathbf{v},\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{v}})
shown in Lemma
3.1
. From problems (
3.1
) and (
3.15

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}(\overline{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}(\nabla\mathbf{v})-\overline{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}(\nabla\mathbf{w}))+\nabla(\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{v}}-\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{w}})&=\ \mathrm{div}(\mathbf{A}(x,\nabla\mathbf{v})-\overline{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}(\nabla\mathbf{v}))\quad\text{in}\ \mathfrak{B}_{2},\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w})&=\ 0\hskip 129.46011pt\ \text{in}\ \mathfrak{B}_{2},\\
\hskip 68.28644pt\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w}&=\ 0\hskip 132.30513pt\text{on}\ \partial\mathfrak{B}_{2}.\end{cases}

(3.18)

Testing the weak form of (
3.18
) with
\varphi=\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w}\in W^{1,p}_{0,\mathrm{div}}(\mathfrak{B}_{2};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we achieve

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}\langle\overline{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}(\nabla\mathbf{v})-\overline{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}(\nabla\mathbf{w}),\nabla(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w})\rangle dx=\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}\langle\mathbf{A}(x,\nabla\mathbf{v})-\overline{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}(\nabla\mathbf{v}),\nabla(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w})\rangle dx.

Thanks to assumption (
1.4

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}\Phi(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})dx\leq\Upsilon\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}\mathcal{O}(x)\left(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|\nabla(\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w})|dx,

(3.19)

where
\Phi
is just given in (
3.9
), the new function

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(x)=\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|\mathbf{A}(x,\nu)-\overline{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}(\nu)|}{(\sigma^{2}+|\nu|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}}.

Using Hölder’s inequality for the right-hand side of (

(3.20)

The standard higher integrability of
\mathbf{v}
gives us the existence of

such that

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{2})^{\frac{\theta}{2}}dx\leq C\left(\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{\theta}{p}}.

Combining (
3.21
) with (
3.20
) and using again Hölder’s inequality, it gives

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}\Phi(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})dx

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon_{1}\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}|\nabla\mathbf{v}-\nabla\mathbf{w}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\qquad+C\epsilon_{1}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}[\mathcal{O}(x)]^{\frac{p}{p-1}\frac{\theta}{\theta-p}}dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{\theta}}\left(\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}\left(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{\theta}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon_{1}\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}|\nabla\mathbf{v}-\nabla\mathbf{w}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\qquad+C\epsilon_{1}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\left(\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}\mathcal{O}(x)dx\right)^{1-\frac{p}{\theta}}\left(\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\left(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right).

Here, it should be noted that under the structure condition (

),
\mathcal{O}(x)\leq 2\Upsilon
for every

. Further, since
[\mathbf{A}]_{R_{0}}\leq\delta
, we infer that

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}\Phi(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})dx

By the same argument as in (
3.11
), for every
\epsilon_{2}>0

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathbf{v}-\nabla\mathbf{w}|^{p}\leq\chi_{\{p<2\}}\epsilon_{2}\left(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}+C\epsilon_{2}^{-\tilde{p}}\Phi(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}),

where
\tilde{p}
given in (
3.11
). As a result, it follows from (

\displaystyle\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}|\nabla\mathbf{v}-\nabla\mathbf{w}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\chi_{\{p<2\}}\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{2}^{-\tilde{p}}\epsilon_{1}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}{\delta}^{1-\frac{p}{\theta}}\right)\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{4}}\left(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx

\displaystyle\qquad+C\epsilon_{2}^{-\tilde{p}}\epsilon_{1}\fint_{\mathfrak{B}_{2}}|\nabla\mathbf{v}-\nabla\mathbf{w}|^{p}dx.

Now, let us fix

3.2
Up-to-the-boundary comparison scheme

The next step of comparison scheme specifies the up-to-boundary extension of the interior results in Lemma
3.1
and
3.2
. In general, to prove comparison estimates near the boundary of
\Omega
, one could go through the same process as interior proofs. However, the main difficulty is to estimate the
L^{\infty}
-gradient bounds near the boundary, especially when
\partial\Omega
is not sufficiently smooth. Therefore, the structural assumptions (
1.9
) (Reifenberg flatness condition) and (
1.10
) (the small BMO condition) are further imposed to our problem, i.e.

(\Omega,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{H}(\delta,R_{0})
, for some
\delta\in(0,1/81)
and
R_{0}>0
.

The proof of boundary version is non-trivial. By approximation scheme, we are able to approximate a portion of the Reifenberg-flat boundary by an
(d-1)
-dimensional hyper-plane. Before going into details let us briefly describe the idea. At a fixed point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
R\in(0,R_{0}/9)
, Reifenberg flat assumption allows us to find a new coordinate system with its origin
O
belonging to
\Omega
and a ball
\tilde{B}_{\varrho}(O)
with radius
\varrho>0
such that its upper-half
\tilde{B}_{\varrho}^{+}(O)
satisfying

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{\varrho}^{+}(O)\subset\Omega,\quad\text{and}\quad x_{0}\not\in\tilde{B}_{\varrho}^{+}(O)\cap\Omega.

On this intersection
\tilde{B}_{\varrho}^{+}(O)\cap\Omega
, under these structural assumptions, we are able to estimate the
L^{\infty}
-norm for solution of homogeneous boundary value problem.

To be precise, this will be done in a multi-step comparison argument. First, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\Omega_{k}:=B_{kR}(x_{0})\cap\Omega
the “surface ball” of
\Omega
that associated with
B_{kR}(x_{0})
. Similar to the interior case, we construct the comparison between weak solution
(\mathbf{u},\pi_{\mathbf{u}})
and the unique solution
(\mathbf{v},\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{v}})\in W^{1,p}_{\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{g},\mathrm{div}}(\Omega_{10};\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\Omega_{10})
of the corresponding homogeneous boundary-value problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{A}(x,\nabla\mathbf{v}))+\nabla\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{v}}&=\ 0\hskip 35.56593pt\ \,\text{in}\ \Omega_{10},\\
\hskip 39.83368pt\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{v})&=\ 0\hskip 36.98866pt\ \text{in}\ \Omega_{10},\\
\hskip 51.21504pt\mathbf{v}&=\ \mathbf{u}-\mathbf{g}\hskip 19.91684pt\text{on}\ \partial\Omega_{10}.\end{cases}

Next, due to the dilation argument, Reifenberg flatness condition and the small BMO semi-norm, we compare
(\mathbf{v},\pi_{\mathbf{v}})

Lemma 4.1
(Covering lemma)

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
be a Muckenhoupt weight and
\mathcal{S}_{1}\subset\mathcal{S}_{2}
be two measurable subsets of
(\delta_{0},R_{0})
-Reifenberg flat domain
\Omega
for some
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
and
R_{0}>0
. Suppose that
N
balls
\{B^{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
with radius
R_{0}
covers
\Omega
. Assume moreover that there is
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that:

(i)

\omega(\mathcal{S}_{1})\leq\varepsilon\omega(B^{j})
for all
j=1,2,...,N
;

(ii)

for every
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
\varrho\in(0,R_{0}]
, if
\omega(B_{\varrho}(x_{0})\cap\mathcal{S}_{1})\geq\varepsilon\omega(B_{\varrho}(x_{0}))
then
B_{\varrho}(x_{0})\cap\Omega\subset\mathcal{S}_{2}
.

Then, there exists a constant
C=C(d,[\omega]_{\mathcal{A}_{\infty}})
such that
\omega(\mathcal{S}_{1})\leq C\varepsilon\omega(\mathcal{S}_{2})
.

Lemma 4.2

Let
\{B^{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
be
N
balls whose radius
R_{0}
and centers belonging to
\partial\Omega
. Suppose that
\{B^{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
covers
\Omega
. Then for every
\mathfrak{a}>0
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}(\mathfrak{a},\varepsilon,\mathtt{data}_{1})>0
such that
\omega(\mathbb{S}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}))\leq\varepsilon\omega(B^{j})
for all
j=1,2,...,N
, where
\mathbb{S}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}):=\left\{\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\mathbb{U}>\mathfrak{a}\lambda;\ \mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}\leq\mathfrak{b}\lambda\right\}.

(4.1)

Proof.
Without loss of generality, we can reduce ourselves to the case where
\mathbb{S}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})
is a non-empty set. Then, there exists
x_{*}\in\Omega
such that
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}(x_{*})\leq\mathfrak{b}\lambda
. Let
D_{0}=\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{B}=B_{(2D_{0}+R_{0})}(x_{*})
, it is easy to check that

\mathbb{S}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})\subset\Omega\subset\mathcal{B}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad B^{j}\subset\mathcal{B},\quad\mbox{ for all }j=1,2,...,N.

Thanks to property (
1.11
), it yields that

\displaystyle\omega(\mathbb{S}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}))\leq C_{2}\left(\frac{|\mathbb{S}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})|}{|\mathcal{B}|}\right)^{\iota_{2}}\omega(\mathcal{B}),\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\omega(\mathcal{B})\leq C_{1}^{-1}\left(\frac{|\mathcal{B}|}{|B^{j}|}\right)^{\iota_{1}}\omega(B^{j}),

for every
j=1,2,...,N
. Moreover, note that
\displaystyle{\frac{|\mathcal{B}|}{|B^{j}|}}=\displaystyle{\frac{2D_{0}+R_{0}}{R_{0}}}
, and due to this fact, we readily infer

\displaystyle\omega(\mathbb{S}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}))\leq C_{1}^{-1}C_{2}\left(\frac{2D_{0}}{R_{0}}+1\right)^{\iota_{1}}\left(\frac{|\mathbb{S}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})|}{|\mathcal{B}|}\right)^{\iota_{2}}\omega(B^{j}).

(4.2)

In order to bound the ratio
\displaystyle{\frac{|\mathbb{S}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})|}{|\mathcal{B}|}}
, we will make use of the boundedness property of
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
in Lemma
1.4
and the global estimate proved in Lemma
2.3
. In particular, it follows that

\displaystyle|\mathbb{S}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})|\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\mathfrak{a}\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{U}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{d}{d-\alpha}}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\mathfrak{a}\lambda}\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{d}{d-\alpha}}.

(4.3)

One the other hand, exploiting the fact that
\Omega\subset\mathcal{B}
and using
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}(x_{*})\leq\mathfrak{b}\lambda
, it yields

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{B}}\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}(x)dx\leq|\mathcal{B}|(2D_{0}+R_{0})^{-\alpha}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}(x_{*})\leq C|\mathcal{B}|^{1-\frac{\alpha}{d}}\mathfrak{b}\lambda.

And from (
4.3
), we readily obtain
|\mathbb{S}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})|\leq C(\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a})^{\frac{d}{d-\alpha}}|\mathcal{B}|
. Combining this with (
4.2
), we conclude

\displaystyle\omega(\mathbb{S}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}))\leq C\left(\frac{2D_{0}}{R_{0}}+1\right)^{\iota_{1}}(\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a})^{\frac{d\iota_{2}}{d-\alpha}}\omega(B^{j}),

(4.4)

for each
j=1,2,...,N
. Moreover, for every
\mathfrak{a}>0
and
\varepsilon>0
, we observe that this time we can choose
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{b}(\mathfrak{a},\varepsilon,\mathtt{data}_{1})>0
small enough such that

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{2D_{0}}{R_{0}}+1\right)^{\iota_{1}}(\mathfrak{b}/\mathfrak{a})^{\frac{d\iota_{2}}{d-\alpha}}<\varepsilon,

and the previous inequality (
4.4
) directly implies
\omega(\mathbb{S}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}))\leq\varepsilon\omega(B^{j})
.

Lemma 4.3

Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
,
\varrho\in(0,R_{0}]
and assume that there exists

\displaystyle|B_{\varrho}(x_{0})\cap\mathbb{S}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})|\leq|B_{\varrho}(x_{0})\cap\{\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{\varrho}(\chi_{B_{2\varrho}(x_{0})}\mathbb{U})>\mathfrak{a}\lambda\}|,

(4.5)

where
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{\varrho}
is so-called the cut-off fractional maximal operator, defined as

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}^{\varrho}_{\alpha}\mathsf{g}(x)=\sup_{0<r<\varrho}r^{\alpha}\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|\mathsf{g}(\nu)|d\nu,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \mathsf{g}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Proof.
First, we fix a point
y\in B_{\varrho}(x_{0})

and

\displaystyle|z-x_{1}|\leq|z-y|+|y-x_{0}|+|x_{0}-x_{1}|<r+\varrho+\varrho<3r,

which directly implies

. It follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\alpha}^{\varrho}\mathbb{U}(y):=\sup_{r\geq\varrho}r^{\alpha}\fint_{B_{r}(y)}\mathbb{U}(z)dz\leq 3^{d}\sup_{r\geq\varrho}r^{\alpha}\fint_{B_{3r}(x_{1})}\mathbb{U}(z)dz\leq 3^{d-\alpha}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\mathbb{U}(x_{1})\leq 3^{d}\lambda.

Further, it also allows us to get

On the other hand, for every

one also has
B_{r}(y)\subset B_{2\varrho}(x_{0})
and therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{\varrho}\mathbb{U}(y)=\sup_{0<r<\varrho}r^{\alpha}\fint_{B_{r}(y)}\mathbb{U}(z)dz=\sup_{0<r<\varrho}r^{\alpha}\fint_{B_{r}(y)}\left(\chi_{B_{2\varrho}(x_{0})}\mathbb{U}\right)(z)dz.

Due to the reasons just shown above, it leads to the desired result

\displaystyle B_{\varrho}(x_{0})\cap\mathbb{S}(\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b})\subset B_{\varrho}(x_{0})\cap\{\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{\varrho}\mathbb{U}>\mathfrak{a}\lambda\}=B_{\varrho}(x_{0})\cap\{\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{\varrho}(\chi_{B_{2\varrho}(x_{0})}\mathbb{U})>\mathfrak{a}\lambda\}.

for all
\mathfrak{a}>3^{d}
.

Lemma 4.4

We may assume with no loss of generality that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\mathbb{U}(x_{1})\leq\lambda,\quad\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}(x_{2})\leq\mathfrak{b}\lambda.

(4.7)

Note that for

in hand, one gets that

\displaystyle T(y,\varrho)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\psi(y,\varrho)}\int_{B_{\varrho_{1}}(y)}\left[\chi_{\Omega}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}(x)\right]^{\mathfrak{s}}dx

\displaystyle\qquad+C\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\psi(y,\varrho)}\frac{1}{2^{nd\vartheta}}\int_{B_{\varrho_{n+1}}(y)}\left[\chi_{\Omega}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}(x)\right]^{\mathfrak{s}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\beta_{0}}{\psi(y,2\varrho)}\int_{B_{\varrho_{1}}(y)}\left[\chi_{\Omega}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}(x)\right]^{\mathfrak{s}}dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{C\beta_{0}^{n+1}}{2^{nd\vartheta}}\frac{1}{\psi(y,\varrho_{n+1})}\int_{B_{\varrho_{n+1}}(y)}\left[\chi_{\Omega}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}(x)\right]^{\mathfrak{s}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\beta_{0}\left[1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\beta_{0}}{2^{d\vartheta}}\right)^{n}\right]\|\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\mathbb{F}_{\sigma}\|_{\mathrm{M}^{\mathfrak{s},\psi}(\Omega)}^{\mathfrak{s}}.

(4.28)

Since
\beta_{0}\in(1,2^{d})
, it is now possible to take
\vartheta=\vartheta(d,\beta_{0})\in(0,1)
such that

\frac{\beta_{0}}{2^{d\vartheta}}<1\Leftrightarrow\log_{2}(\beta_{0})/d<\vartheta,

and it ensures that the series in (
4.3
) is finite. The proofs of (
1.21
) is complete.

5
Global regularity for asymptotically regular problems

This section is devoted to the proof of regularity estimate for weak solution to the asymptotically regular problem (
1.8
). As mentioned above, it is a consequence of global gradient bounds proved in preceding section. This proof exploits the level-set argument similar to that used for original Stokes problem. The assertion of Theorem
1.15
continues to hold in generalized function spaces.

Proof of Theorem
1.15
.

First, let us introduce a new matrix-valued function
\Theta_{\sigma}
which defined in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}
as below

\displaystyle\Theta_{\sigma}(x,\nu):=\frac{\mathbf{a}(x,\nu)-\mathbf{A}(x,\nu)}{(\sigma^{2}+|\nu|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}},\quad(x,\nu)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}.

The assumption (
1.22
) ensures that there exists
M_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle|\Theta_{\sigma}(x,\nu)|\leq\delta_{0},

(5.1)

for every
\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}\setminus B_{M_{0}}
, uniformly with respect to
x\in\Omega
. Also, for each
x\in\Omega
, let us consider the Poisson integral

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[\Theta_{\sigma}(x,\cdot)](y)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\partial B_{M_{0}}}\Theta_{\sigma}(x,\nu)\mathbb{K}(y,\nu)dS(\nu),

for all
y\in B_{M_{0}}
. Here,
\mathbb{K}
stands for the Poisson kernel given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}(y,\nu):=\left(M_{0}\omega_{n-1}\right)^{-1}\frac{M_{0}^{2}-|y|^{2}}{|y-\nu|^{n}},

where
\omega_{n-1}
denotes the surface area of
\partial B_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}
. Now, we consider

\displaystyle\Pi_{\sigma}(x,y):=\begin{cases}\Theta_{\sigma}(x,y),&\quad\mbox{ if }|y|\geq M_{0},\\
\mathbb{P}[\Theta_{\sigma}(x,\cdot)](y),&\quad\mbox{ if }|y|<M_{0}.\end{cases}

Making use of (
5.1
) and the maximum principle, it allows us to verify that
\Pi_{\sigma}
is Carathéodory map and moreover, we pattern the proof on arguments from
[
6
]
to obtain

\displaystyle|\Pi_{\sigma}(x,y)|\leq\delta_{0},\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}},

(5.2)

uniformly with respect to
x\in\Omega
. Keep in mind the notation already introduced, it allows us to establish the relation between
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{A}
as following

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{A}(x,y)+(\sigma^{2}+|y|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\Pi_{\sigma}(x,y)+(\sigma^{2}+|y|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\chi_{\{|y|<M_{0}\}}\left(\Theta_{\sigma}(x,y)-\Pi_{\sigma}(x,y)\right).

At this point, for the ease of the reader we further consider vector-valued function
\mathcal{E}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(x,y):=\mathbf{A}(x,y)+(\sigma^{2}+|y|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\Pi_{\sigma}(x,\nabla u).

(5.3)

It is now easy to see that
\mathcal{E}(x,y)
is differentiable with respect to
y
, and

\displaystyle\partial_{y}\mathcal{E}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\partial_{y}\mathbf{A}(x,y)+(p-1)(\sigma^{2}+|y|^{2})^{\frac{p-3}{2}}\langle\Pi_{\sigma}(x,\nabla u),y\rangle,

which from (
5.2
) and (
1.3
), we deduce that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}(x,y)|+|\partial_{y}\mathcal{E}(x,y)||y|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathbf{A}(x,y)|+|\partial_{y}\mathbf{A}(x,y)||y|+p\delta_{0}(\sigma^{2}+|y|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(\Upsilon+p)(\sigma^{2}+|y|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}},

(5.4)

for every
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
. In addition, for every
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}
, one gets

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{E}(x,y_{1})-\mathcal{E}(x,y_{2}),y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{A}(x,y_{1})-\mathbf{A}(x,y_{2}),y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle+\left[(\sigma^{2}+|y_{1}|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}-(\sigma^{2}+|y_{2}|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\right]\langle\Pi_{\sigma}(x,\nabla u),y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle.

(5.5)

A closer look at
[
36
, Lemma 2.1]
finds a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|(\sigma^{2}+|y_{1}|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}-(\sigma^{2}+|y_{2}|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\sigma^{2}+|y_{1}|^{2}+|y_{2}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|y_{1}-y_{2}|.

Using (
1.4
), (
5.2
) and (
5
) in the following way:

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{E}(x,y_{1})-\mathcal{E}(x,y_{2}),y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\left(\Upsilon^{-1}-2\delta_{0}c\right)\left(\sigma^{2}+|y_{1}|^{2}+|y_{2}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}.

(5.6)

We now take (
5.3
) into account to clarify
\mathbf{a}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}})
as below

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}})+(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}}|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\chi_{\{|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}}|<M_{0}\}}\left(\Theta_{\sigma}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}})-\Pi_{\sigma}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}})\right).

Since
(\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}},\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{a}})\in W^{1,p}_{\mathbf{g},\mathrm{div}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{int}}(\Omega)
is a pair of solutions to system of type (
1.8
), to be precise one has

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}\left(\mathcal{E}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}})\right)+\nabla\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{a}}&=-\mathrm{div}\left(\mathbf{b}(x,\mathbf{f})\right)\quad\ \text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\hskip 42.67912pt\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}})&=\ 0\hskip 69.70915pt\ \text{in}\ \Omega,\\
\hskip 54.06006pt\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}}&=\ \mathbf{g}\hskip 72.55461pt\text{on}\ \partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(5.7)

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}(x,\mathbf{f})=\mathbf{B}(x,\mathbf{f})+(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}}|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\chi_{\{|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}}|<M_{0}\}}\left(\Pi_{\sigma}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}})-\Theta_{\sigma}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}})\right).

(5.8)

Thanks to (
5.2
), we write

\displaystyle\left|(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}}|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\chi_{\{|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}}|<M_{0}\}}\Pi_{\sigma}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}})\right|\leq 2\delta_{0}\left(\sigma^{2}+M_{0}^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2}},

(5.9)

and on the other hand, assumption (
1.22
) gives us that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{a}(x,\nu)-\mathbf{A}(x,\nu)\right|\leq\epsilon(|\nu|)(1+|\nu|^{p-1}),\mbox{ and }\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\epsilon(\lambda)\leq\delta_{0},

where
\epsilon:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is defined by

\displaystyle\epsilon(|\nu|)=\sup_{x\in\Omega}\frac{\left|\mathbf{a}(x,\nu)-\mathbf{A}(x,\nu)\right|}{(\sigma^{2}+|\nu|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}}.

For this reason, one may estimate as below

\displaystyle\left|(\sigma^{2}+|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}}|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\chi_{\{|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}}|<M_{0}\}}\Theta_{\sigma}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\chi_{\{|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}}|<M_{0}\}}\left[\mathbf{a}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}})-\mathbf{A}(x,\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}})\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\chi_{\{|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}}|<M_{0}\}}\epsilon(|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}}|)(1+|\nabla\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}}|^{p-1})

\displaystyle\leq\|\epsilon\|_{\infty}\left(1+M_{0}^{p-1}\right).

(5.10)

Plugging (
5.9
) and (
5
) into (
5.8
), it yields

\displaystyle\left|\mathbf{b}(x,\mathbf{f})\right|\leq C(p,M_{0},\|\epsilon\|_{\infty},\sigma)\left(1+|\mathbf{f}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}.

(5.11)

By the preceding estimates (
5
), (
5.6
) and (
5.11
), for
0<\delta_{0}<\frac{1}{4c\Upsilon}
, we are able to find
\tilde{\Upsilon}>0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|\mathcal{E}(x,y)|+|\partial_{y}\mathcal{E}(x,y)||y|\leq\tilde{\Upsilon}(1+|y|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}},\\
\langle\mathcal{E}(x,y_{1})-\mathcal{E}(x,y_{2}),y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle\geq{\tilde{\Upsilon}}^{-1}\left(1+|y_{1}|^{2}+|y_{2}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2},\\
\left|\mathbf{b}(x,\mathbf{f})\right|\leq\tilde{\Upsilon}\left(1+|\mathbf{f}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}.\end{cases}

Finally, applying Theorem
1.10
and Theorem
1.12
to solution
(\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{a}},\mathrm{\pi}_{\mathbf{a}})
of problem (
5.7
), it leads to the desired estimate (
1.23
). We have thus completed the proof of Theorem
1.15
.

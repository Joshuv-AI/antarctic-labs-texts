An improved lower bound for the planar Turán number of cycles
Yongxin Lan Zi-Xia Song

Abstract
The planar Turán number of a graph H H , denoted by e x 𝒫 ( n , H ) ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,H) , is the largest number of edges in a planar graph on n n vertices without containing H H as a subgraph. In this paper, we continue to study the topic of “extremal” planar graphs initiated by Dowden [J. Graph Theory 83 (2016) 213–230]. We first obtain an improved lower bound for e x 𝒫 ( n , C k ) ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{k}) for all k ≥ 13 k\geq 13 and n ≥ 5 ​ ( k − 6 + ⌊ ( k − 1 ) / 2 ⌋ ) ​ ( k − 1 ) / 2 n\geq 5(k-6+\lfloor{(k-1)}/2\rfloor)(k-1)/2 ; the construction for each k k and n n provides a simpler counterexample to a conjecture of Ghosh, Győri, Martin, Paulos and Xiao [arxiv:2004.14094v1], which has recently been disproved by Cranston, Lidický, Liu and Shantanam [Electron. J. Combin. 29(3) (2022) #P3.31] for every k ≥ 11 k\geq 11 and n n sufficiently large (as a function of k k ). We then prove that e x 𝒫 ( n , H + ) = e x 𝒫 ( n , H ) ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,H^{+})=ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,H) for all k ≥ 5 k\geq 5 and n ≥ | H | + 1 n\geq|H|+1 , where H ∈ { C k , 2 ​ C k } H\in\{C_{k},2C_{k}\} and H + H^{+} is obtained from H H by adding a pendant edge to a vertex of degree two.

|H|=n-k\geq k+1
and
e(H)=ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-k,C_{k})
. Let
H^{*}
be the disjoint union of
O_{k-2}
and
K_{2}
when
k\geq 4
, and
H^{*}=\overline{K}_{k}
when
k=3
. Finally, let
v\in V(H)
and let
G
be the planar graph obtained from the disjoint union of
H
and
H^{*}
by joining
v
to every vertex in
H^{*}
.
It is simple to check that
G
is
C_{k}
-free with
|G|=n
and

\displaystyle e(G)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}e(H)+e(O_{k-2})+e(K_{2})+k&\text{\hskip 81.09052ptif }k\geq 4\\
e(H)+k&\text{\hskip 81.09052ptif }k=3\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-k,C_{k})+(2(k-2)-3)+1+k&\text{\hskip 28.45274ptif }k\geq 4\\
ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-k,C_{k})+k&\text{\hskip 28.45274ptif }k=3\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle=ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-k,C_{k})+3k-6.

Hence,
ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{k})\geq e(G)=ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-k,C_{k})+3k-6
, as desired.

Theorem 3.2

Let
n
and
k
be integers with
n\geq k+1
and
k\geq 5
. Then

ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{k}^{+})=ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{k}).

Proof.
Let
n
and
k
be integers with
n\geq k+1
and
k\geq 5
. Since
C_{k}
is a subgraph of
C_{k}^{+}
, we see that
ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{k}^{+})\geq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{k})
. We next proceed the proof by induction on
n
to show that
ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{k}^{+})\leq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{k})
. Let
G
be a
C_{k}^{+}
-free planar graph on
n\geq k+1
vertices with
ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{k}^{+})
edges. If
G
is
C_{k}
-free, then
e(G)\leq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{k})
, as desired. We may assume that
G
contains
C_{k}
as a subgraph. Let
A
be the set of vertices of a copy of
C_{k}
in
G
. Then
G[A]
is a component of
G
with
e(G[A])\leq 3k-6
because
G
is
C_{k}^{+}
-free and
k\geq 5
. In addition,
G\setminus A
is a
C_{k}^{+}
-free planar graph on
n-k\geq 1
vertices.

We first show that the statement holds when
k+1\leq n\leq 2k
. Note that
e(G\setminus A)\leq{n-k\choose 2}
when
k+1\leq n\leq k+2
, and
e(G\setminus A)\leq 3(n-k)-6
when
k+3\leq n\leq 2k
. Hence,

\displaystyle e(G)=e(G[A])+e(G\setminus A)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\begin{cases}3k-6&\text{if}\,\quad n=k+1\\
3k-6+1&\text{if}\,\quad n=k+2\\
3k-6+3(n-k)-6&\text{if}\,\quad k+3\leq n\leq 2k\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}3n-9&\text{if}\,\quad n=k+1\\
3n-11&\text{if}\,\quad n=k+2\\
3n-12&\text{if}\,\quad k+3\leq n\leq 2k.\\
\end{cases}

We then construct a
C_{k}
-free planar graph
G^{*}
on
n
vertices with
e(G^{*})\geq e(G)
. Let
n=t(k-2)+\varepsilon+1
, where
t=1
and
2\leq\varepsilon=n-k+1\leq k-2
when
k+1\leq n\leq 2k-3
;
t=2
and
1\leq\varepsilon=n-2k+3\leq 3
when
2k-2\leq n\leq 2k
. Let
G^{*}:=K_{1}+(tO_{k-2}\cup O_{\varepsilon})
.Then
G^{*}
is a
C_{k}
-free planar graph with
|G^{*}|=n
and

\displaystyle e(G^{*})

\displaystyle=(t(k-2)+\varepsilon)+t(2(k-2)-3)+\max\{2\varepsilon-3,0\}

\displaystyle=3(t(k-2)+\varepsilon)-2\varepsilon-3t+\max\{2\varepsilon-3,0\}

\displaystyle=3(n-1)-3t+\max\{-3,-2\varepsilon\}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}3n-9&\text{if}\,\quad k+1\leq n\leq 2k-3\\
3n-11&\text{if}\,\quad n=2k-2\\
3n-12&\text{if}\,\quad 2k-1\leq n\leq 2k\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle\geq e(G).

Since
G^{*}
is
C_{k}
-free, we see that
e(G)\leq e(G^{*})\leq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{k})
for all
n
satisfying
k+1\leq n\leq 2k
.

We may assume that
n\geq 2k+1
. Then
n-k\geq k+1
. By the induction hypothesis, we have
ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-k,C_{k}^{+})\leq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-k,C_{k})
. Note that
e(G\setminus A)\leq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-k,C_{k}^{+})
because
G\setminus A
is
C_{k}^{+}
-free. Hence,

e(G)=e(G[A])+e(G\setminus A)\leq 3k-6+ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-k,C_{k}^{+})\leq 3k-6+ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-k,C_{k}).

By Lemma
3.1
, we see that
e(G)\leq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{k})
, as desired.

This completes the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

C_{4}^{+}
. We need to determine the exact value of

Lemma 3.3

ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(8,C_{4})=11
.

Proof.
Let
G:=C_{8}
with vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{8}
in order. Let
G^{*}
be the graph obtained from
G
by adding edges
v_{i}v_{i+2}
for each
i\in\{1,3,5\}
. Then
G^{*}
is a
C_{4}
-free planar graph with
|G^{*}|=8
and
e(G^{*})=11
. Hence,
ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(8,C_{4})\geq e(G^{*})=11
. Next suppose
ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(8,C_{4})\geq 12
. Let
H
be a
C_{4}
-free planar graph on
8
vertices with
e(H)\geq 12
. Let
v\in V(H)
be such that
d_{H}(v)=\Delta(H)
and let
B:=V(H)\setminus N_{H}[v]
. Then
d_{H}(v)\geq 3
,
|B|=7-d_{H}(v)\leq 4
, and
e(H)=e(H[N_{H}[v]])+e_{H}(N_{H}(v),B)+e(H[B])
. In addition,
H[N_{H}(v)]
is
P_{3}
-free and each vertex in
B
is adjacent to at most one vertex in
N_{H}(v)
. It follows that
e(H[N_{H}(v)])\leq\lfloor d_{H}(v)/2\rfloor
and
e_{H}(N_{H}(v),B)\leq|B|
. If
d_{H}(v)\geq 4
, then
|B|\leq 3
and
e(H[B])\leq{|B|\choose 2}
. Thus
12\leq e(H)\leq d_{H}(v)+\lfloor d_{H}(v)/2\rfloor+|B|+{|B|\choose 2}=7+\lfloor d_{H}(v)/2\rfloor+{7-d_{H}(v)\choose 2}\leq 12
. It follows that
d_{H}(v)=4
,
e(H[N_{H}(v)])=\lfloor d_{H}(v)/2\rfloor=2
,
e_{H}(N_{H}(v),B)=|B|=3
and
H[B]=K_{3}
. One can check that
H
contains
C_{4}
as a subgraph, a contradiction. Thus
d_{H}(v)=3
. Then
|B|=4
and
e(H[B])\leq 4
because
H
is
C_{4}
-free. Thus
12\leq e(H)\leq d_{H}(v)+\lfloor d_{H}(v)/2\rfloor+|B|+4=12
. It follows that

e_{H}(N_{H}(v),B)=|B|=4
and
H[B]=K_{1}+({K_{2}\cup K_{1}})
. But then
\Delta(H)\geq 4
, a contradiction.
Thus,
ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(8,C_{4})\leq 11
and so
ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(8,C_{4})=11
.

Theorem 3.4

Let
n\geq 5
be integer. Then

ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{4}^{+})=\begin{cases}ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{4})&\mbox{if }n\neq 8\\
ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{4})+1=12&\mbox{if }n=8.\end{cases}

Since
C_{4}
is a subgraph of
C_{4}^{+}
and

-free, we see that

. We shall proceed the proof by induction on

. Let
G
be a
C_{4}^{+}
-free planar graph on
n
vertices with
ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{4}^{+})
edges. If
G
is
C_{4}
-free, then

, as desired. We may assume that
G
contains
C_{4}
as a subgraph. Let

C_{4}

We next prove that the statement holds for

Lemma 4.1

(

]

Lemma 4.2

Let

and
k
be integers with

and

. Then

(a)

ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,2C_{k})\geq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-2k,2C_{k})+6k-12
.

(b)

ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,2C_{k})\geq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-2k,C_{k})+6k-6

Proof.
To prove (

), it suffices to construct a
2C_{k}
-free planar graph on

vertices with

be any

-free planar graph with

to every vertex of

and

.
One can see that

is

-free with

and

\displaystyle e(G_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e(H_{1})+e(2O_{k-2})+e(2K_{2})+2k

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-2k,2C_{k})+2(2(k-2)-3)+2+2k

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-2k,2C_{k})+6k-12.

be any

-free planar graph on

vertices with

\displaystyle=

Theorem 4.3

Let
n
and
k
be integers with
n\geq 2k+1
and
k\geq 4
. Then

ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{k}\cup C_{k}^{+})=ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,2C_{k}).

Since
2C_{k}
is subgraph of
C_{k}\cup C_{k}^{+}
, we have
ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{k}\cup C_{k}^{+})\geq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,2C_{k}).
We next prove that
ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{k}\cup C_{k}^{+})\leq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,2C_{k})
by induction on
n
. Let
G
be any
C_{k}\cup C_{k}^{+}
-free planar graph with
|G|=n\geq 2k+1
and
e(G)=ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,C_{k}\cup C_{k}^{+})
. If
G
is
2C_{k}
-free, then
e(G)\leq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,2C_{k})
, as desired. We may assume that
G
contains
2C_{k}
as a subgraph. Let
A
be the set of vertices of a copy of
2C_{k}
in
G
. Then
e(G[A])\leq 3(2k)-6=6k-6
and
G
has no edges between
A
and
V(G\setminus A)
because
k\geq 4
and
G
is
C_{k}\cup C_{k}^{+}
-free. Hence,
e(G)=e(G[A])+e(G\setminus A)\leq 6k-6+e(G\setminus A)
.

We first prove that the statement holds for
n
satisfying
2k+1\leq n\leq 4k
. Note that
e(G\setminus A)\leq{n-2k\choose 2}
when

and
e(G\setminus A)\leq 3(n-2k)-6
when
2k+3\leq n\leq 4k
. Hence,

\displaystyle e(G)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\begin{cases}6k-6&\text{if}\,\quad n=2k+1\\
6k-6+1&\text{if}\,\quad n=2k+2\\
6k-6+3(n-2k)-6&\text{if}\,\quad 2k+3\leq n\leq 4k\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}3n-9,&\text{if}\,\quad n=2k+1\\

3n-12,&\text{if}\,\quad 2k+3\leq n\leq 4k\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(3-\frac{1}{k-2}\right)n-5.

By Lemma
4.1
, we have

e(G)\leq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,2C_{k})
, and so the statement holds when
n\leq 4k
.

We may assume that
n\geq 4k+1
.
We first consider the case when
G[A]
is disconnected. Then
G[A]
has exactly two components each of order
k
because
G[A]
contains a
2C_{k}
as a subgraph. Then

. Note that

-free and

e(G\setminus A)\leq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-2k,C_{k}\cup C_{k}^{+})\leq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-2k,2C_{k})
.

e(G)=e(G[A])+e(G\setminus A)\leq 6k-12+ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-2k,2C_{k}).

4.2
(
a
), we see that
e(G)\leq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,2C_{k})
, as desired. It remains to consider the case

is connected. Then
G[A]
contains a copy of
C_{k}^{+}
and so
G\setminus A
is
C_{k}
-free. Hence,
e(G\setminus A)\leq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-2k,C_{k})
.
It follows that

e(G)=e(G[A])+e(G\setminus A)\leq 6k-6+ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n-2k,C_{k}).

By Lemma
4.2
(
b
), we have
e(G)\leq ex_{{}_{\mathcal{P}}}(n,2C_{k})
.

This completes the proof of Theorem
4.3
.

To prove

, we will construct a

-free planar graph on

vertices with

be any
\Theta_{k}
-free planar graph on

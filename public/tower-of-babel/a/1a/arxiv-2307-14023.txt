Are Transformers with One Layer Self-Attention Using Low-Rank Weight Matrices Universal Approximators?
Tokio Kajitsuka, Issei Sato

Abstract
Existing analyses of the expressive capacity of Transformer models have required excessively deep layers for data memorization, leading to a discrepancy with the Transformers actually used in practice. This is primarily due to the interpretation of the softmax function as an approximation of the hardmax function. By clarifying the connection between the softmax function and the Boltzmann operator, we prove that a single layer of self-attention with low-rank weight matrices possesses the capability to perfectly capture the context of an entire input sequence. As a consequence, we show that one-layer and single-head Transformers have a memorization capacity for finite samples, and that Transformers consisting of one self-attention layer with two feed-forward neural networks are universal approximators for continuous permutation equivariant functions on a compact domain.



1
Introduction

The Transformer model has been ubiquitously used in deep learning since its proposal by
Vaswani et al. 2017
.
Its widespread application spans several domains, not only revolutionizing Natural Language Processing (NLP) through models like BERT
(
Devlin et al. 2019
;
Liu et al. 2019
)
and GPT
(
Brown et al. 2020
;
Radford et al. a
;
Radford et al. b
)
but also making significant advancements in image and graph processing as an alternative to conventional models like convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and graph neural networks (GNNs)
(
Dosovitskiy et al. 2022
;
Ying et al. 2022
)
.

One of the key reasons behind the success of the Transformer model is its ability to represent a wide range of functions. Various studies investigated this aspect, including the universal approximation theorem for Transformer models and its memorization capacity
Yun et al. 2019
;
Kim et al. 2023
;
Mahdavi et al. 2023
;
Edelman et al. 2022
;
Gurevych et al. 2021
;
Takakura & Suzuki 2023
;
Likhosherstov et al. 2023
.

The main challenge in proving universal approximation theorems for Transformer models lies in the fact that the Transformer needs to account for the context of the entire input sequence. Unlike feed-forward neural networks where each input is processed independently, the self-attention mechanism in Transformer models must take into account the dependencies between all elements in each input sequence. In constructive proofs
(
Edelman et al. 2022
;
Yun et al. 2019
;
Kim et al. 2023
;
Mahdavi et al. 2023
;
Gurevych et al. 2021
;
Takakura & Suzuki 2023
)
, these dependencies are often aggregated into a token-wise quantity, which we call a “context id” here, by a self-attention mechanism, and then feed-forward neural networks map each context id to the desired output.

The drawback of existing analyses is that they require excessively deep layers
(
Yun et al. 2019
;
Kim et al. 2023
)
or quite a lot of attention heads
(
Gurevych et al. 2021
;
Takakura & Suzuki 2023
;
Likhosherstov et al. 2023
)
for data memorization, which leads to a discrepancy with Transformers being deployed in practice.
This discrepancy primarily arises from the interpretation of the softmax function as an approximation of the hardmax function. Consequently, to compute the “context id” within the self-attention mechanism, the number of required self-attention parameters scales linearly with the length of an input sequence.

In this work, we address this gap by closely examining the softmax function itself. First, we show that it is impossible to output the “context id” using just one layer of self-attention with the hardmax function. At the same time, we demonstrate that just one layer of single-head and softmax-based self-attention with low-rank weight matrices possesses the capability to perfectly capture the context of an entire input sequence.
This result implies that the Transformer with one self-attention layer is a universal approximator of continuous permutation equivariant functions by using two feed-forward neural networks connected before and after the single-head self-attention mechanism with an arbitrary head size.

Our contributions are summarized as follows.

1.

We show that one layer self-attention with the hardmax function is not a contextual mapping; that is, one layer hardmax-based Transformer has no memorization capacity.

2.

In contrast,
we provide a framework for constructing a context mapping with one-layer and single-head self-attention using the softmax function.

3.

We prove that one layer Transformer has a memorization capacity for finite samples, and Transformers with one-layer and single-head self-attention are universal approximators of continuous permutation equivariant functions.

3.2
Background

Yun et al. 2019
proved affirmatively one of the most fundamental questions on the expressive capacity of Transformer models, namely, whether the universal approximation theorem for Transformer models holds.
Their proof approach is to quantize the input domain and reduce the universal approximation theorem to the memorization analysis of finite samples, i.e., the construction of a model that achieves zero loss for a finite number of training data, which was also analyzed later by
Kim et al. 2023
.
In the analysis of memorization capacity, assumptions are usually made on the inputs in order to perform a meaningful analysis beyond the lower bound of
Sontag 1997

Here, as with the assumptions adopted by
Yun et al. 2019
;
Kim et al. 2023
, we assume that the input tokens are separated by a certain distance.

Definition 1

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and

1.

2.

For any
i\in[N]
and
k\in[n]

\left\|{\bm{Z}}^{(i)}_{:,k}\right\|<r_{\max}
holds.

3.

For any

and

with
{\bm{Z}}^{(i)}_{:,k}\neq{\bm{Z}}^{(j)}_{:,l}

\left\|{\bm{Z}}^{(i)}_{:,k}-{\bm{Z}}^{(j)}_{:,l}\right\|>\delta
holds.

Note that we refer to
{\bm{Z}}^{(1)},\dots,{\bm{Z}}^{(N)}
as tokenwise
(r_{\max},\epsilon)
-separated instead if the sequences satisfy conditions

The achievement of
Yun et al. 2019
was not only to prove the universal approximation theorem for Transformers, but also to clarify the difficulties in the analysis of this kind of expressive capacity of Transformers and elucidated an approach to establishing the proof.
Namely, what makes Transformers’ memorization different from that of feed-forward neural networks is that Transformers need to capture the context of each input sequence as a whole, rather than simply associating each token with a label.

Remarkably,
Yun et al. 2019
;
Kim et al. 2023
formulated this concept as a contextual mapping, which assigns a unique id to a pair of an input sequence and each of their tokens. We define it here using the notion of
(r,\delta)

Definition 2

(Contextual Mapping)

Let
{\bm{X}}^{(1)},\dots,{\bm{X}}^{(N)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
be input sequences. Then, a map

1.

In particular,

If we have such a contextual mapping, a label sequence can be associated with a unique id for each input sequence using the existing analysis of memorization in feed-forward neural networks.

Thus, the central question is: how to construct a contextual mapping in Transformer models?
The only place in Transformer models where interaction between tokens can be taken into account is in the self-attention mechanism; therefore, the self-attention mechanism must be used to construct a contextual mapping.

Yun et al. 2019
first constructed a contextual mapping by using
|\mathcal{V}|^{d}+1

1
1

1

To be precise, when the continuous input range is quantized into

However, this is still far from the practical implementation of Transformers, and it remains unclear whether a reasonably-sized Transformer would possess such memorization capacity or if the universal approximation theorem would hold.
This leads to the following question.

How many self-attention layers are both necessary and sufficient to construct a contextual mapping?

We first point out the reason for requiring a significant number of self-attention layers in the construction of contextual mapping in the analyses of
Yun et al. 2019
;
Kim et al. 2023

Their approach entails interpreting the softmax function in the self-attention mechanism as an approximation of the hardmax function, which also hinders a detailed analysis of the specific properties of the softmax function.
As evidence of this, we illustrate in Section

Next, in Section
3.4

This is somewhat surprising because this implies the probability of fully capturing the context of each input sequence only through the attention coefficients computed by the pairwise dot-product of the softmax function and its weighted average.

Self-attention with hardmax

In previous studies analyzing the memorization capacity of Transformers

\displaystyle c\|{\bm{v}}_{a}-{\bm{v}}_{b}\|\leq\left|{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{v}}_{a}-{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{v}}_{b}\right|\leq\|{\bm{v}}_{a}-{\bm{v}}_{b}\|

(11)

with some constant
0<c<1
. Then, by using the assumption
{\bm{t}}={\bm{X}}^{(i)}_{:,k}={\bm{X}}^{(j)}_{:,l}
for some token
{\bm{t}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|{\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{t}}\right|\cdot\left|\left({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{X}}^{(i)}\right)\sigma_{S}\left[\left({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{X}}^{(i)}\right)^{\top}\left({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{t}}\right)\right]-\left({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{X}}^{(j)}\right)\sigma_{S}\left[\left({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{X}}^{(j)}\right)^{\top}\left({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{t}}\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\left({\bm{a}}^{(i)}\right)^{\top}\sigma_{S}\left[{\bm{a}}^{(i)}\right]-\left({\bm{a}}^{(j)}\right)^{\top}\sigma_{S}\left[{\bm{a}}^{(j)}\right]\right|,

(12)

where we denote
{\bm{a}}^{(i)}=\left({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{X}}^{(i)}\right)^{\top}\left({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{t}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
{\bm{a}}^{(j)}=\left({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{X}}^{(j)}\right)^{\top}\left({\bm{w}}^{\top}{\bm{t}}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Therefore, in order to prove that a self-attention block serves as a contextual mapping, we only have to focus on the separability of the function

\displaystyle\boltz:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R},{\bm{a}}\mapsto{\bm{a}}^{\top}\sigma_{S}[{\bm{a}}],

(13)

which is known as the Boltzmann operator
(
Littman 1996
;
Asadi & Littman 2017
)
.

The following lemma shows that the Boltzmann operator is a mapping that projects input sequences to scalar values while preserving some distance, and is central to our proof that the self-attention function is a contextual mapping.

Lemma 1
.

Let
{\bm{a}}^{(1)},\dots,{\bm{a}}^{(m)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be tokenwise
(r,\delta)
-separated vectors with no duplicate element in each vector and

\delta>2\log n+3.

(14)

Then, the outputs of the Boltzmann operator are
(r,\delta^{\prime})
-separated, that is,

\displaystyle\left|\boltz({\bm{a}}^{(i)})\right|

\displaystyle\leq r,

(15)

\displaystyle\left|\boltz({\bm{a}}^{(i)})-\boltz({\bm{a}}^{(j)})\right|

\displaystyle>\delta^{\prime}=(\log n)^{2}e^{-2r}

(16)

hold for each
i,j\in[m]
with
{\bm{a}}^{(i)}\neq{\bm{a}}^{(j)}
.

Taking into account the above arguments, this separability of the Boltzmann operator allows us to construct one self-attention layer to be a contextual mapping.
∎

Remark 1

(Masked self-attention)
.

In practice, attention matrices are often masked to avoid directing attention to undesired tokens.
This is performed, for example, for autoregressive text generation or padding of inputs with different lengths.
It is relatively straightforward to extend
2
to masked self-attention mechanisms.
See Appendix
C
for more details.

4
Applications of Contextual Mapping

4.1
Memorization capacity of one-layer Transformer

As a first application of
2
, we prove that a 1-layer Transformer can completely memorize finite samples, each of which has no duplicate token.
This result emphasizes that in contrast to the proof of
Kim et al. 2023
, which requires
2n
self-attention layers for Transformer memorization, one layer of self-attention is actually sufficient.
In addition, it is worth noting that the hardmax-based Transformers do not have a memorization capacity, which is implied straightforwardly from
1
.

Corollary 1

(Memorization capacity of one-layer Transformer)
.

Let
\epsilon>0,r_{\max}>r_{\min}>0
and
({\bm{X}}^{(1)},{\bm{Y}}^{(1)}),\dots,({\bm{X}}^{(N)},{\bm{Y}}^{(N)})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}\times[C]^{d\times n}
be sequences of input-output-pairs such that
{\bm{X}}^{(1)},\dots,{\bm{X}}^{(N)}
are tokenwise
(r_{\min},r_{\max},\epsilon)
-separated input sequences with no duplicate token in each sentence and consistently labeled, that is,
{\bm{Y}}^{(i)}_{:,k}={\bm{Y}}^{(j)}_{:,l}

holds for any
i,j\in[N]
and
k,l\in[n]
such that
\mathcal{V}^{(i)}=\mathcal{V}^{(j)}
and
{\bm{X}}^{(i)}_{:,k}={\bm{X}}^{(j)}_{:,l}
.

Then, there exist
4(s+d)+d(2nN+d)
weight parameters such that for any
i\in[N]

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left({\bm{X}}^{(i)}\right)=\mathcal{F}^{(FF)}\left(\mathcal{F}^{(SA)}_{S}\left({\bm{X}}^{(i)}\right)\right)={\bm{Y}}^{(i)}

(17)

holds.

Remark 2

(Parameter efficiency)
.

To achieve the memorization with a one-layer Transformer, the one-hidden-layer feed-forward block has to map each context id to the corresponding label.
Since the possible number of context ids is at most
nN
in the worst case, the linear dependency on
nN
of the number of parameters in
1
is optimal up to logarithmic factors
(
Bartlett et al. 2019
)
.
It is worth mentioning that this linear dependency can be relaxed to the milder requirement
\tilde{O}\left(\sqrt{nN}\right)
by allowing for deeper layers in the feed-forward block
(
Vardi et al. 2022
)
, under the assumption that the size
|\mathcal{V}|
of the vocabulary set is independent of
n
and
N
.

In addition, it is straightforward to show that a
1
-layer Transformer with trainable positional encodings has a memorization capacity for arbitrary input sequences possibly with duplicate tokens.

Corollary 2

(Memorization capacity of one-layer Transformer with positional encodings)
.

Let
\epsilon>0,r_{\max}>r_{\min}>0
and
({\bm{X}}^{(1)},{\bm{Y}}^{(1)}),\dots,({\bm{X}}^{(N)},{\bm{Y}}^{(N)})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}\times[C]^{d\times n}
be sequences of input-output-pairs such that
{\bm{X}}^{(1)},\dots,{\bm{X}}^{(N)}
are tokenwise
(r_{\min},r_{\max},\epsilon)
-separated input sequences and are consistently labeled, that is,
{\bm{Y}}^{(i)}={\bm{Y}}^{(j)}

holds for any
i,j\in[N]
such that
{\bm{X}}^{(i)}={\bm{X}}^{(j)}
.

Then, there exist
4(s+d)+d(2nN+d)
weight parameters and positional encodings
{\bm{E}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
such that for any
i\in[N]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left({\bm{X}}^{(i)}+{\bm{E}}\right)=\mathcal{F}^{(FF)}\left(\mathcal{F}^{(SA)}_{S}\left({\bm{X}}^{(i)}+{\bm{E}}\right)\right)={\bm{Y}}^{(i)}

(18)

holds.

4.2
Transformers with one self-attention layer are universal approximators

As a further application of
2

we here provide a proof that Transformer with one self-attention layer is a universal approximator.
More precisely, let
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{PE}}
be the set of all permutation equivariant continuous functions that take values on a compact domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
, and let
\mathcal{T}_{2}
be the set of all two layer Transformers with one-layer and single-head self-attention, that is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{2}=\left\{\mathcal{F}^{(FF)}_{2}\circ\mathcal{F}^{(SA)}_{S}\circ\mathcal{F}^{(FF)}_{1}:\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}\right\},

(19)

where
\mathcal{F}^{(FF)}_{1},\mathcal{F}^{(FF)}_{2}
and
\mathcal{F}^{(SA)}_{S}
are feed-forward neural network layers and a single-head self-attention layer with the softmax function, respectively.
Then the following proposition holds (see the definition (
1
)).

Proposition 1

(Transformers with one layer self-attention are universal approximators)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then, for any
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{PE}}
and
\epsilon>0
, there exists a Transformer
g\in\mathcal{T}_{2}
with one-layer and single-head self-attention such that

\dist_{p}(f,g)<\epsilon.

holds.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first universal approximation theorem for two-layer Transformers with a self-attention of realistic size.

Takakura & Suzuki 2023
showed that a one-layer Transformer with an embedding layer is capable of approximating shift-equivariant
\gamma
-smooth functions.
However, their construction requires a considerably high number of self-attention heads and a large head size to flatten an input sequence into outputs of self-attention.

Jiang & Li 2023
used the Kolmogorov representation theorem to give a non-constructive proof of the universal approximation theorem for two-layer Transformers.
They make a particular assumption on the domain of functions, which in turn implies the first universal approximation theorem of two-layer Transformer with positional encoding for continuous functions on a compact domain.
Nevertheless, they again require a very high hidden dimension
4n^{2}d+2n
.
In contrast, thanks to
2
, we have shown that Transformers using a single-head self-attention are universal approximators for continuous permutation equivariant functions on an arbitrary compact domain.
Our result can be readily extended for continuous but not necessarily permutation equivariant functions on a compact domain by using positional encoding, and at the same time is significant from the perspective of geometric deep learning.

5
Experiments

As shown in
2
, a self-attention mechanism with rank
1
weight matrices already has enough expressive capacity to become a contextual mapping.
In particular, its proof leads us to consider the following simplified form of a self-attention mechanism: for any input sequence
{\bm{Z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(R1)}_{S}({\bm{Z}})={\bm{Z}}+{\bm{W}}^{(O)}\left({\bm{v}}_{1}^{\top}{\bm{Z}}\right)\sigma_{S}\left[\left({\bm{v}}_{1}^{\top}{\bm{Z}}\right)^{\top}\left({\bm{v}}_{2}^{\top}{\bm{Z}}\right)\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n},

(20)

where
{\bm{v}}_{1},{\bm{v}}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
{\bm{W}}^{(O)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times 1}
are weight matrices.
This architecture corresponds to a common self-attention with the head size
s=1
, and value and query matrices having the same weight vector
{\bm{v}}_{1}
.
In this section, we test whether Transformers with self-attention layers replaced by equation
20
, which we call rank-
1
Transformers, actually have the theoretically predicted expressive capacity by using a real-world dataset.

We train rank-

Transformers on a token classification task with the CoNLL-2003
(
Tjong Kim Sang & De Meulder 2003
)
dataset.
The batch size is
32
and the training are conducted over
400
epochs.

We train three different depths of rank-1 transformers on the dataset and do not use layer normalization to match the situation with our theoretical analysis.

6
Conclusions

We demonstrated that a contextual mapping can be implemented in one-layer and single-head self-attention with low-rank matrices, by clarifying the connection between a self-attention mechanism and the Boltzmann operator. This particularly indicates that one-layer Transformers have a memorization capacity for finite samples, and that Transformers with one-layer and single-head self-attention are universal approximators for continuous permutation equivariant functions on a compact domain.
Our proof of the universal approximation theorem requires one feed-forward neural network layer before the self-attention layer to quantize continuous inputs.
We leave it as future work to clarify whether the one-layer Transformers without such a quantization layer are universal approximators or not.
We also expect that our analysis of the softmax function will have an impact on the evaluation of Transformer’s expressive capability from the perspective of formal languages.

A.1
Proof of
1

Proof.

Let
{\bm{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an arbitrary nonzero vector, and consider the situation that all input tokens can be written as

\mathcal{V}=\{\alpha_{1}{\bm{v}},\alpha_{2}{\bm{v}},\alpha_{3}{\bm{v}},\alpha_{4}{\bm{v}}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

(22)

for some scalars
\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\alpha_{3}<\alpha_{4}
.
Then, the attention matrix inside the hardmax function at head
i
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\left({\bm{W}}_{i}^{(K)}{\bm{v}}{\bm{a}}^{\top}\right)^{\top}\left({\bm{W}}_{i}^{(Q)}{\bm{v}}{\bm{a}}^{\top}\right)={\bm{a}}\left({\bm{W}}_{i}^{(K)}{\bm{v}}\right)^{\top}\left({\bm{W}}_{i}^{(Q)}{\bm{v}}\right){\bm{a}}^{\top}

(23)

with input coefficients
{\bm{a}}\in\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}\}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In particular, when we focus on a certain index, at which the token is, e.g.,
\alpha_{2}{\bm{v}}
, the above expression can further be written as

\displaystyle\left({\bm{W}}_{i}^{(K)}{\bm{v}}{\bm{a}}^{\top}\right)^{\top}\left({\bm{W}}_{i}^{(Q)}\alpha_{2}{\bm{v}}\right)

\displaystyle={\bm{a}}\left({\bm{W}}_{i}^{(K)}{\bm{v}}\right)^{\top}\left({\bm{W}}_{i}^{(Q)}{\bm{v}}\right)\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left({\bm{W}}_{i}^{(K)}{\bm{v}}\right)^{\top}\left({\bm{W}}_{i}^{(Q)}{\bm{v}}\right)\alpha_{2}}_{\in\mathbb{R}}\cdot{\bm{a}}

(24)

The right-hand side is a vector
{\bm{a}}
multiplied by some scalar. So it is evident that the maximum value of the vector on the right-hand side is achieved only at the indices where the values of the input sequence
{\bm{v}}{\bm{a}}^{\top}
are
\alpha_{1}
or
\alpha_{4}
.
This implies that a self-attention with the hardmax function invariably gets distracted by the indices where
\alpha_{1}
or
\alpha_{4}
are present, thereby overlooking information from other tokens in the input sequence.
As a result, no matter how many heads there are, one-layer self-attention with the hardmax function cannot distinguish input sequences, e.g.,
(\alpha_{1}{\bm{v}},\alpha_{2}{\bm{v}},\alpha_{4}{\bm{v}})
and
(\alpha_{1}{\bm{v}},\alpha_{3}{\bm{v}},\alpha_{4}{\bm{v}})
.
∎

Proof.

We show the propositioin by the same steps as in
Yun et al. 2019
. Namely,

1.

First, given a permutation equivariant continuous function
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{PE}}
defined on a compact set, it follows from typical analysis that
f
can be approximated by a step function with arbitrary precision in terms of
p
-norm. Therefore, to show a universal approximation theorem, it is sufficient to show that such a step function can be approximated by a Transformer with one self-attention layer.

2.

Second, we use a first feed-forward neural network layer
\mathcal{F}^{(FF)}_{1}
to quantize the input domain, reducing the problem to memorization of finite samples.

3.

Then, by a similar analysis as in
1
, it can be shown that a combination of the self-attention layer
\mathcal{F}^{(SA)}
and
\mathcal{F}^{(FF)}_{2}
can memorize the step function almost everywhere, in the sense that quantized input domains corresponding to sentences with duplicate tokens are negligibly small.

We hereafter provide rough proofs of the three steps outlined above, because there are multiple ways to construct a model that satisfies the above requirements, and we do not pursue the efficiency of feed-forward neural networks in this paper.

First, without loss of generality, we ignore skip-connections in
\mathcal{F}^{(FF)}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}^{(FF)}_{2}
.

1.

Since
f
is a continuous function on a compact set,
f
has maximum and minimum values on the domain.
By scaling with
\mathcal{F}^{(FF)}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}^{(FF)}_{2}
,
f
is assumed to be normalized without loss of generality: for any
{\bm{Z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}\setminus[0,1]^{d\times n}

\displaystyle f({\bm{Z}})=0,

(51)

and for any
{\bm{Z}}\in[-1,1]^{d\ \times n}

\displaystyle-1\leq f({\bm{Z}})\leq 1.

(52)

Let
D\in\mathbb{N}
be the granularity of a grid

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{D}=\{1/D,2/D,\dots,1\}^{d\times n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}

(53)

such that a piece-wise constant approximation

\displaystyle\overline{f}({\bm{Z}})=\sum_{{\bm{L}}\in\mathbb{G}_{D}}f\left({\bm{L}}\right)\bm{1}_{{\bm{Z}}\in{\bm{L}}+[-1/D,0)^{d\times n}}

(54)

satisfies

\displaystyle\dist_{p}(f,\overline{f})<\epsilon/3.

(55)

Such a
D
always exists because of uniform continuity of
f
.

2.

We use
\mathcal{F}^{(FF)}_{1}
to quantize the input domain into
\mathbb{G}_{D}
.

For any small
\delta>0
, the following
\delta
-approximated step function can be constructed with one-hidden-layer feed-forward neural network: for any
z\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{R}\left[z/\delta\right]-\sigma_{R}\left[z/\delta-1\right]}{D}=\begin{cases}0&z<0\\
z/\delta D&0\leq z<\delta\\
1/D&\delta\leq z\end{cases}.

(56)

By shifting and stacking this step function, we have an approximated multiple-step function

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{D-1}\frac{\sigma_{R}\left[z/\delta-t/\delta D\right]-\sigma_{R}\left[z/\delta-1-t/\delta D\right]}{D}

\displaystyle\approx\quant_{D}(z)=\begin{cases}0&z<0\\
1/D&0\leq z<1/D\\
\vdots&\vdots\\
1&1-1/D\leq z\end{cases},

(57)

and subtracting the last step function from it,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{D}\frac{\sigma_{R}\left[z/\delta-t/\delta D\right]-\sigma_{R}\left[z/\delta-1-t/\delta D\right]}{D}-\left(\sigma_{R}\left[z/\delta-1/\delta\right]-\sigma_{R}\left[z/\delta-1-1/\delta\right]\right)

(58)

approximately quantize
[0,1]
into
\{1/D,\dots,1\}
, while it projects
\mathbb{R}\setminus[0,1]
to
0
.

These operations can be realized by one-hidden-layer neural network, and it is straightforward to approximate its extension
\quant_{D}
to dimension
d\times n
, which we denote
\quant_{D}^{d\times n}:\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
.

In addition to that, we also add a penalty term, with which we identify whether an input sequence is in
[0,1]^{d\times n}
or not.
This is defined by

\displaystyle\sigma_{R}\left[(z-1)/\delta\right]-\sigma_{R}\left[(z-1)/\delta-1\right]-\sigma_{R}\left[-z/\delta\right]-\sigma_{R}\left[-z/\delta-1\right]

\displaystyle\approx\penal(z)=\begin{cases}-1&z\leq 0\\
0&0<z\leq 1\\
-1&1<z\end{cases},

(59)

which can also be implemented by one-hidden-layer feed-forward neural network.

Combining these components together, the first feed-forward neural network layer
\mathcal{F}^{(FF)}_{1}
approximates the following function:

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{F}}^{(FF)}_{1}({\bm{Z}})=\quant_{D}^{d\times n}({\bm{Z}})+\sum_{t=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\penal({\bm{Z}}_{t,k})

(60)

Note that this function quantizes inputs in
[0,1]^{d\times n}
with granularity
D
, while every element of the output is non-positive for inputs outside
[0,1]^{d\times n}
.
In particular, the norm of the output is upper-bounded by

\displaystyle\max_{{\bm{Z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}}\left\|\mathcal{F}^{(FF)}_{1}({\bm{Z}})_{:,k}\right\|=dn\cdot\sqrt{d}

(61)

for any
k\in[n]
.

3.

Let
\tilde{\mathbb{G}}_{D}\subset\mathbb{G}_{D}
be a sub-grid

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{G}}_{D}=\left\{{\bm{L}}\in\mathbb{G}_{D}\mathrel{}\middle|\mathrel{}\forall k,l\in[n],\ {\bm{L}}_{:,k}\neq{\bm{L}}_{:,l}\right\},

(62)

and consider memorization of
\tilde{\mathbb{G}}_{D}
with its labels given by

for each

Note that the label sets are consistent because

is a permutation equivariant function.
Then,
2

can be regarded as tokenwise

From the way the self-attention

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}^{(SA)}_{S}({\bm{Z}})_{:,k}-{\bm{Z}}_{:,k}\right\|<\frac{1}{4\sqrt{d}D}\max_{k^{\prime}\in[n]}\left\|{\bm{Z}}_{:,k^{\prime}}\right\|

(63)

for any

and

This follows from the fact that

In particular, combining this upper-bound with equation

{\bm{a}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let denote
{\bm{p}}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})=\sigma_{S}[{\bm{a}}]
.
In addition, we define the Boltzmann operator, partition function, and entropy as follows.

\displaystyle\boltz({\bm{a}})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}p_{i},

(80)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}({\bm{a}})=\log\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}e^{a_{i}}\right),

(81)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(\bm{p})=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\log p_{i}.

(82)

The following lemma shows that the Boltzmann operator is monotonically decreasing when the maximum and the rest of the arguments are far enough apart.

Lemma 4

(Monotonicity)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
i\in[n]
and
{\bm{a}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then, the derivative of the Boltzmann operator
\boltz({\bm{a}})={\bm{a}}^{\top}\sigma_{S}[{\bm{a}}]
with respect
a_{i}
is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{i}}\boltz({\bm{a}})=p_{i}(1+\log p_{i}+\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})).

(83)

In particular, the Boltzmann operator is monotonically decreasing with respect to
a_{i}
whenever

\displaystyle a_{i}<\max_{j\in[n]}a_{j}-\log n-1

(84)

holds.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}({\bm{a}})-\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})

\displaystyle=\log\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}e^{a_{j}}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{k}\log p_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{k}\log\left(p_{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n}e^{a_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{k}\log e^{a_{k}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{k}a_{k}=\boltz({\bm{a}}),

(85)

we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{i}}\boltz({\bm{a}})=\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{i}}\mathcal{L}({\bm{a}})-\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{i}}\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}}).

(86)

Notice that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial p_{i}}{\partial a_{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{j}}\frac{e^{a_{i}}}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{a_{k}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{j}}e^{a_{i}}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{a_{k}}-e^{a_{i}}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{j}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{a_{k}}}{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{a_{k}}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{ij}e^{a_{j}}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{a_{k}}-e^{a_{i}}\cdot e^{a_{j}}}{\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{a_{k}}\right)^{2}}=p_{j}(\delta_{ij}-p_{i})

(87)

holds for any
i,j\in[n]
. Here
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta, that is,

\displaystyle\delta_{ij}=\begin{cases}0&\text{if $i\neq j$},\\
1&\text{if $i=j$}.\end{cases}

(88)

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}({\bm{a}})}{\partial a_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{a_{i}}}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}e^{a_{j}}}=p_{i},

(89)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})}{\partial a_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})}{\partial p_{j}}\cdot\frac{\partial p_{j}}{\partial a_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{j}}\left(-\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{k}\log p_{k}\right)\cdot p_{i}(\delta_{ji}-p_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(-\log p_{j}-1\right)\cdot p_{i}(\delta_{ji}-p_{j})

\displaystyle=-p_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[\delta_{ji}(\log p_{j}+1)-p_{j}\log p_{j}-p_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=-p_{i}\left(\log p_{i}+1+\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})-1\right)

\displaystyle=-p_{i}(\log p_{i}+\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})).

(90)

Plugging equation
89
and equation
90
into equation
86
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{i}}\boltz({\bm{a}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{i}}\mathcal{L}({\bm{a}})-\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{i}}\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})

\displaystyle=p_{i}+p_{i}(\log p_{i}+\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}}))

\displaystyle=p_{i}(1+\log p_{i}+\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})).

(91)

In particular, the derivative of the Boltzmann operator at index
i
is negative when

\displaystyle 1+\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})+\log p_{i}<0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow 1+\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})+\left(a_{i}-\mathcal{L}({\bm{a}})\right)<0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow a_{i}<\mathcal{L}({\bm{a}})-\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})-1.

(92)

Since
\max_{j\in[n]}a_{j}\leq\mathcal{L}({\bm{a}})
(p.72,
Boyd & Vandenberghe 2004
) and
\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})\leq\log n
, we have
\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{i}}\boltz({\bm{a}})<0
whenever

\displaystyle a_{i}<\max_{j\in[n]}a_{j}-\log n-1

(93)

holds.
∎

Lemma 5

(Concavity)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
i\in[n]
and
{\bm{a}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then, the Boltzmann operator
\boltz({\bm{a}})
is concave with respect to
a_{i}
, that is,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial a_{i}^{2}}\boltz({\bm{a}})<0

(94)

holds in a domain where
{\bm{a}}
satisfies

\displaystyle a_{i}<\max_{j\in[n]}a_{j}-\log n-3.

(95)

Proof.

According to
4
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{i}}\boltz({\bm{a}})=p_{i}(1+\log p_{i}+\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})).

(96)

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial a_{i}^{2}}\boltz({\bm{a}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{i}}\left[p_{i}(1+\log p_{i}+\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}}))\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial p_{i}}{\partial a_{i}}\cdot(1+\log p_{i}+\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}}))+p_{i}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{i}}(1+\log p_{i}+\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}}))

\displaystyle=p_{i}(1-p_{i})\cdot(1+\log p_{i}+\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}}))+p_{i}\cdot\left[\frac{p_{i}(1-p_{i})}{p_{i}}-p_{i}(\log p_{i}+\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}}))\right]

\displaystyle=p_{i}\left[(1-2p_{i})(\log p_{i}+\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})+1)+1\right],

(97)

where we used equation
87
and equation
90
.

Hereafter, we show that the right-hand side of the above equality is negative under the assumption that equation
95
holds. First, the fact that
\max_{j\in[n]}a_{j}\leq\mathcal{L}({\bm{a}})
(p.72,
Boyd & Vandenberghe 2004
) and
\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})\leq\log n
implies

\displaystyle a_{i}<\max_{j\in[n]}a_{j}-\log n-3<\mathcal{L}({\bm{a}})-\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})-3.

(98)

It follows from
a_{i}-\mathcal{L}({\bm{a}})=\log p_{i}
that

\displaystyle a_{i}<\max_{j\in[n]}a_{j}-\log n-3\Rightarrow\log p_{i}<-\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})-3.

(99)

Next, since the entropy
\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})
is always non-negative, we have
\log p_{i}<-3
under equation
95
.
By using an inequality
e^{-x}\leq\frac{1}{1+x}
for
x>-1
, this implies

\displaystyle p_{i}<e^{-3}\leq\frac{1}{1+3}=\frac{1}{4}.

(100)

Thus, as long as equation
95
holds, we have

\displaystyle(1-2p_{i})(\log p_{i}+\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}})+1)+1

\displaystyle<\left(1-2\cdot\frac{1}{4}\right)\cdot(-2)+1=0,

(101)

which in turn implies
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial a_{i}^{2}}\boltz({\bm{a}})<0
.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
{\bm{a}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1},a_{n}),{\bm{b}}=(b_{1},\dots b_{n-1},b_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be sequences such that the first
n-1
elements of
{\bm{a}}
and
{\bm{b}}
match, that is,
a_{i}=b_{i}
for all
i\in[n-1]
. In addition, if

\displaystyle\max_{i\in[n-1]}a_{i}-\delta>a_{n}>b_{n}

(102)

with
\delta>\log n+3
, the difference
\boltz({\bm{a}})-\boltz({\bm{b}})
is lower-bounded by

\displaystyle\boltz({\bm{b}})-\boltz({\bm{a}})>(a_{n}-b_{n})(\delta+a_{n}-b_{n}-\log n-1)\cdot\frac{e^{b_{n}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}e^{b_{i}}}.

(103)

Proof.

According to the monotonicity (
4
) and concavity (
5
) of the Boltzmann operator, we have

\displaystyle\boltz(b_{1},\dots,b_{n-1},x)+(a_{n}-x)\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\boltz(b_{1},\dots,b_{n-1},x)>\boltz(b_{1},\dots,b_{n-1},a_{n})

(104)

for any
x<a_{n}
. In particular, by setting
x=b_{n}
and using equation
83
, this implies

\displaystyle\boltz({\bm{b}})-\boltz({\bm{a}})

\displaystyle>(a_{n}-b_{n})\cdot\left(-\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\boltz(b_{1},\dots,b_{n-1},x)\right|_{x=b_{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=(a_{n}-b_{n})\cdot\left[-p_{n}(1+\log p_{n}+\mathcal{S}({\bm{p}}))\right]

\displaystyle>(a_{n}-b_{n})\cdot\left[-p_{n}\left(1+b_{n}-\max_{i\in[n]}b_{i}+\log n\right)\right]

\displaystyle>(a_{n}-b_{n})\cdot p_{n}(\delta+a_{n}-b_{n}-\log n-1),

(105)

where
{\bm{p}}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the softmax function of
{\bm{b}}
, i.e.,
{\bm{p}}=\sigma_{S}[{\bm{b}}]
.
∎

B.2
Proof of

Before moving on to the proof, we first illustrate the proof sketch of
1
using a simple example.

Since the Boltzmann operator is permutation invariant, without loss of generality we assume the elements of
{\bm{a}}^{(i)}
and
{\bm{a}}^{(j)}
are sorted in descending order, e.g.,
{\bm{a}}^{(i)}=(8,6,5)
and
{\bm{a}}^{(j)}=(4,3,1)
.
In this case, since the Boltzmann operator
\boltz
can be regarded as an approximation of
\max
, we have

\boltz({\bm{a}}^{(i)})\approx 8>4\approx\boltz({\bm{a}}^{(j)}),

(106)

and so the Boltzmann operator can readily separate these two inputs.

The subtle case is where the initial tokens of
a^{(i)}
and
a^{(j)}
are identical, but the rest of each sequences differs, like
{\bm{a}}^{(i)}=(8,6,5)
and
{\bm{a}}^{(j)}=(8,3,1)
.
However, a closer observation reveals that if the first coordinate and the second one of the input
{\bm{a}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
are well-separated, then
\boltz({\bm{a}})
is monotonically decreasing for each coordinate
k=2,\dots,n
. In the above example, this intuitively implies

\boltz\left({\bm{a}}^{(j)}\right)\geq\boltz\left(\overline{{\bm{a}}}^{(j)}\right)\quad\text{with $\overline{{\bm{a}}}^{(j)}=(8,3,3)$}

(107)

and

\boltz\left({\bm{a}}^{(i)}\right)\leq\boltz\left(\underline{{\bm{a}}}^{(i)}\right)\quad\text{with $\underline{{\bm{a}}}^{(i)}=(8,6,-\infty)$},

(108)

or by abuse of notation,
\boltz\left({\bm{a}}^{(i)}\right)\leq\boltz\left(8,6\right)
.

Thus, if we can show
\boltz\left(\underline{{\bm{a}}}^{(i)}\right)<\boltz\left(\overline{{\bm{a}}}^{(j)}\right)
, then
\boltz\left({\bm{a}}^{(i)}\right)<\boltz\left({\bm{a}}^{(j)}\right)
holds and we know that the Boltzmann operator can also separate this pattern of inputs.
Fortunately, this is indeed the case if each element of the inputs is sufficiently separated, because

\displaystyle\boltz\left(\overline{{\bm{a}}}^{(j)}\right)=\frac{8e^{8}+3e^{3}+3e^{3}}{e^{8}+e^{3}+e^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{8e^{8}+2\cdot 3e^{3}}{e^{8}+2\cdot e^{3}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{8e^{8}+(3+\log 2)e^{3+\log 2}}{e^{8}+\cdot e^{3+\log 2}}=\boltz\left(8,3+\log 2\right),

(109)

and we have
\boltz\left(8,6\right)<\boltz\left(8,3+\log 2\right)
by the monotonicity of the Boltzmann operator.

1
.

We only have to show the case of
m=2
, and for notational convenience,
{\bm{a}}^{(1)}
(resp.
{\bm{a}}^{(2)}
) hereafter is denoted by
{\bm{a}}
(resp.
{\bm{b}}
). Also, since the Boltzmann operator is permutation invariant, we assume without loss of generality
a_{1}>\cdots>a_{n}
and
b_{1}>\cdots>b_{n}

{\bm{a}}

a_{k}
is strictly greater than
a_{l}
for any

).

First, since the Boltzmann operator can be regarded as weighting averaging, we have

\displaystyle|\boltz({\bm{a}})|\leq\max(|a_{1}|,|a_{n}|)<r.

(110)

For

Lemma 7
.

Let
{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be tokenwise
\delta
-separated vectors in a decreasing order with no duplicate element in each vector and
\delta>2\log n+3
, that is,

\displaystyle i>j

\displaystyle\Rightarrow a_{i}-a_{j},b_{i}-b_{j}>\delta,

(114)

\displaystyle a_{i}\neq b_{j}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow|a_{i}-b_{j}|>\delta

(115)

for any
i,j\in[n]
.

In addition, suppose there exists
k\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
such that

\displaystyle(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})=(b_{1},\dots,b_{k})\text{ and }a_{k+1}>b_{k+1}.

(116)

Then, the outputs of the Boltzmann operator are
(\log n)^{2}e^{-(a_{1}-b_{k+1})}
-separated, that is,

\displaystyle\left|\boltz({\bm{a}})-\boltz({\bm{b}})\right|

\displaystyle>(\log n)^{2}e^{-(a_{1}-b_{k+1})}

(117)

holds.

Proof.

We show the lemma by dividing it into the following two cases:

1.

k\geq 1

Let
\underline{{\bm{a}}}
and
\overline{{\bm{b}}}
be

\displaystyle\underline{{\bm{a}}}

\displaystyle=\left(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k},a_{k+1}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{k+1}

(118)

\displaystyle\overline{{\bm{b}}}

\displaystyle=\left(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{k},b_{k+1},b_{k+1},\dots,b_{k+1}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(119)

Then, by abuse of notation,
4
implies that

\displaystyle\boltz\left({\bm{a}}\right)<\underline{{\bm{a}}}^{\top}\sigma_{S}\left[\underline{{\bm{a}}}\right]=\boltz\left(\underline{{\bm{a}}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\boltz\left({\bm{b}}\right)>\boltz\left(\overline{{\bm{b}}}\right),

(120)

and we only have to evaluate the magnitude of the difference
\boltz\left(\overline{{\bm{b}}}\right)-\boltz\left(\underline{{\bm{a}}}\right)
.

Let
\gamma_{k}
and
\xi_{k}
be

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}=\sum_{l=1}^{k}a_{l}e^{a_{l}}\quad\text{and}\quad\xi_{k}=\sum_{l=1}^{k}e^{a_{l}}.

(121)

Then,
\boltz\left(\overline{{\bm{b}}}\right)
can be decomposed into

\displaystyle\boltz\left(\overline{{\bm{b}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{k}+(n-k)b_{k+1}e^{b_{k+1}}}{\xi_{k}+(n-k)e^{b_{k+1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{k}+b_{k+1}e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}{\xi_{k}+e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma_{k}+\left(b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)\right)e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}{\xi_{k}+e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}-\frac{\log(n-k)\cdot e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}{\xi_{k}+e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}

\displaystyle=\boltz\left(a_{1},\dots,a_{k},b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)\right)-\frac{\log(n-k)\cdot e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}{\xi_{k}+e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}.

(122)

Therefore, the difference
\boltz\left(\overline{{\bm{b}}}\right)-\boltz\left(\underline{{\bm{a}}}\right)
can be written as

\displaystyle\boltz\left(\overline{{\bm{b}}}\right)-\boltz\left(\underline{{\bm{a}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\boltz\left(a_{1},\dots,a_{k},b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)\right)-\boltz\left(\underline{{\bm{a}}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-\frac{\log(n-k)\cdot e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}{\xi_{k}+e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}.

(123)

According to
6
, the first two terms on the right-hand side are evaluated as

\displaystyle\boltz\left(a_{1},\dots,a_{k},b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)\right)-\boltz\left(\underline{{\bm{a}}}\right)

\displaystyle>(a_{k+1}-b_{k+1}-\log(n-k))(\delta+a_{k+1}-b_{k+1}-\log(n-k)-\log(k+1)-1)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\cdot\frac{e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}{\xi_{k}+e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}

\displaystyle>(\delta-\log n)(2\delta-2\log n-1)\cdot\frac{e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}{\xi_{k}+e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}.

(124)

Since
\delta>2\log n+3
by assumption, the above inequality is further lower-bounded by

\displaystyle\boltz\left(a_{1},\dots,a_{k},b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)\right)-\boltz\left(\underline{{\bm{a}}}\right)

\displaystyle>(\delta-\log n)(2\delta-2\log n-1)\cdot\frac{e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}{\xi_{k}+e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}

\displaystyle>(\log n+3)(2\log n+5)\cdot\frac{e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}{\xi_{k}+e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}.

(125)

Plugging the above inequality into equation
1
, we see

\displaystyle\boltz\left(\overline{{\bm{b}}}\right)-\boltz\left(\underline{{\bm{a}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\boltz\left(a_{1},\dots,a_{k},b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)\right)-\boltz\left(\underline{{\bm{a}}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-\frac{\log(n-k)\cdot e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}{\xi_{k}+e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}

\displaystyle>\frac{e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}{\xi_{k}+e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}\left[(\log n+3)(2\log n+5)-\log(n-k)\right]

\displaystyle>\frac{e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}{\xi_{k}+e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}\cdot 2(\log n)^{2}.

(126)

Lastly, notice that the following inequality follows from
\delta
-separatedness of
{\bm{a}}
and
{\bm{b}}
and the assumption that
{\bm{a}}
has no duplicate token:

\displaystyle\xi_{k}+e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}

\displaystyle<\sum_{l=1}^{k+1}e^{a_{l}}\quad(\because a_{k+1}>b_{k+1}+\log(n-k))

\displaystyle<e^{a_{1}}\sum_{l=1}^{k+1}e^{-(l-1)\delta}

\displaystyle<2e^{a_{1}}\quad(\because\delta>\log 2).

(127)

By using this inequality, equation
126
is lower-bounded by

\displaystyle\boltz\left(\overline{{\bm{b}}}\right)-\boltz\left(\underline{{\bm{a}}}\right)

\displaystyle>\frac{e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}{\xi_{k}+e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}\cdot 2(\log n)^{2}

\displaystyle>\frac{e^{b_{k+1}+\log(n-k)}}{2e^{a_{1}}}\cdot 2(\log n)^{2}

\displaystyle>(\log n)^{2}e^{-(a_{1}-b_{k+1})},

(128)

which implies
\boltz({\bm{b}})-\boltz({\bm{a}})>(\log n)^{2}e^{-(a_{1}-b_{k+1})}
.

2.

k=0

Since the Boltzmann operator can be regarded as weighted averaging,
\boltz({\bm{b}})\leq b_{1}
always holds. Thus, it is enough to evaluate how much greater
\boltz({\bm{a}})
is than
b_{1}
.

Let
\overline{{\bm{a}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be

\displaystyle\overline{{\bm{a}}}=(a_{1},a_{1}-\delta,\dots,a_{1}-\delta).

(129)

Then,
\boltz({\bm{a}})>\boltz(\overline{{\bm{a}}})
follows from
4
, and its value is

\displaystyle\boltz\left(\overline{{\bm{a}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{1}e^{a_{1}}+(n-1)(a_{1}-\delta)e^{a_{1}-\delta}}{e^{a_{1}}+(n-1)e^{a_{1}-\delta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{1}+(n-1)(a_{1}-\delta)e^{-\delta}}{1+(n-1)e^{-\delta}}

\displaystyle=a_{1}-\frac{(n-1)\delta e^{-\delta}}{1+(n-1)e^{-\delta}}.

(130)

Therefore, the difference
\boltz\left({\bm{a}}\right)-\boltz\left({\bm{b}}\right)
is

\displaystyle\boltz\left({\bm{a}}\right)-\boltz\left({\bm{b}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\boltz\left(\overline{{\bm{a}}}\right)-b_{1}

\displaystyle>a_{1}-\frac{(n-1)\delta e^{-\delta}}{1+(n-1)e^{-\delta}}-(a_{1}-\delta)

\displaystyle=\delta-\frac{(n-1)\delta e^{-\delta}}{1+(n-1)e^{-\delta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{1+(n-1)e^{-\delta}}

\displaystyle\geq\log n,

(131)

where the last inequality follows from the assumption
\delta>2\log n
.
Note that the right-hand side is greater than
(\log n)^{2}e^{-(a_{1}-b_{1})}
, because
a_{1}-b_{1}>\log n
implies
\log n\cdot e^{-(a_{1}-b_{1})}<1
.

∎

Appendix C
Extension to Masked Self-Attention

Masked self-attention mechanisms are formulated as follows: the Softmax part in equation
2
is replaced by

\displaystyle\sigma_{S}\left[\left({\bm{W}}_{l,i}^{(K)}{\bm{Z}}\right)^{\top}\left({\bm{W}}_{l,i}^{(Q)}{\bm{Z}}\right)+{\bm{C}}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n},

(132)

with some masking matrix
{\bm{C}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
whose elements are either
0
or
-\infty
.

Our main result
2
can be readily extended to the case where attention masking are conducted. The idea is that

\displaystyle\boltz({\bm{a}}+{\bm{c}})

\displaystyle=({\bm{a}}+{\bm{c}})^{\top}\sigma_{S}\left[{\bm{a}}+{\bm{c}}\right]

\displaystyle={\bm{a}}^{\top}\sigma_{S}\left[{\bm{a}}+{\bm{c}}\right]+{\bm{c}}^{\top}\sigma_{S}\left[{\bm{a}}+{\bm{c}}\right]

\displaystyle={\bm{a}}^{\top}\sigma_{S}\left[{\bm{a}}+{\bm{c}}\right]

(133)

holds for any masking vector
{\bm{c}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
whose elements are either
0
or
-\infty
.
Thus, in order to ensure that the masked attention is a contextual mapping, it is sufficient to verify
\boltz({\bm{a}}+{\bm{c}})

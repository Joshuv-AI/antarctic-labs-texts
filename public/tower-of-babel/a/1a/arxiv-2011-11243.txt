Global Weak Solutions to the Navier–Stokes–Darcy–Boussinesq System for Thermal Convection in Coupled Free and Porous Media Flows
Xiaoming Wang and Hao Wu

Abstract
We study the Navier–Stokes–Darcy–Boussinesq system that models the thermal convection of a fluid overlying a saturated porous medium in a general decomposed domain. In both two and three spatial dimensions, we first prove the existence of global weak solutions to the initial boundary value problem subject to the Lions and Beavers–Joseph–Saffman–Jones interface conditions. The proof is based on a proper time-implicit discretization scheme combined with the Leray–Schauder principle and compactness arguments. Next, we establish a weak-strong uniqueness result such that a weak solution coincides with a strong solution emanating from the same initial data as long as the latter exists. Keywords : Coupled free and porous media flow, thermal convection, Navier–Stokes–Darcy–Boussinesq system, global weak solution, existence, weak-strong uniqueness. AMS Subject Classification : 35D30, 35K61, 76D03, 76D05, 76S05.

Introduction

The study of the coupling free flow
and porous media flow is of considerable interest and
has attracted a lot of attentions in recent years due to its wide applications in geosciences (e.g., karst aquifers, hyporheic flow, contaminant transport), health sciences (e.g., blood flow) and industrial processes, see
[
14
]
and the references therein. In this paper, we investigate a Navier–Stokes–Darcy–Boussinesq system that models thermal convection in an incompressible viscous fluid overlying a saturated porous medium (see, e.g.,
[
34
]
). The convection phenomenon under consideration is much more complicated than that in a single fluid (cf.
[
16
]
for the free-flow and
[
15
,
33
]
for fluids in a porous medium), since more physical parameters will affect the heat transport process. Linear and nonlinear stability analysis, properties of bifurcation and dynamic transition for the coupled system, with the Navier–Stokes equations and Darcy’s equation governing the free-flow and the porous regions, have been provided in
[
34
,
19
]
under suitable interface conditions. Here, our aim is to perform a first-step analysis on the well-posedness of the associated initial boundary value problem, proving the existence of global weak solutions and their uniqueness property.

Assume that the fluid is confined in a bounded connected domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=2,3
) with
C^{2,1}
boundary
\partial\Omega
. The unit outer normal vector on
\partial\Omega
is denoted by

. The domain

is partitioned into two non-overlapping regions such that
\overline{\Omega}=\overline{\Omega}_{f}\cup\overline{\Omega}_{m}
and

, where
\Omega_{f}
and
\Omega_{m}
represent the free-flow region and the porous matrix region, respectively. We denote by
\partial\Omega_{f}
and
\partial\Omega_{m}
the boundaries of the free-flow and the matrix part, with
\widehat{\mathbf{n}}_{f}
,
\widehat{\mathbf{n}}_{m}
being the corresponding unit outer normals on them. Both
\partial\Omega_{f}
and
\partial\Omega_{m}
are assumed to be Lipschitz continuous. The interface between the two parts (i.e.,
\partial\Omega_{f}\cap\partial\Omega_{m}
) is denoted by
\Gamma_{i}
. On the free-flow/matrix interface
\Gamma_{i}
,
\mathbf{n}_{i}
stands for the unit normal on
\Gamma_{i}
pointing from the free-flow region to the matrix, and
\{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{j}\}

(j=1,...,d-1)
stands for a local orthonormal basis for the tangent plane to
\Gamma_{i}
. Let
\Gamma_{f}=\partial\Omega_{f}\backslash\Gamma_{i}
and
\Gamma_{m}=\partial\Omega_{m}\backslash\Gamma_{i}
with
\mathbf{n}_{f},\mathbf{n}_{m}
being the unit outer normals to

\Gamma_{f}
and
\Gamma_{m}
. We assume that
\Gamma_{m}
and
\Gamma_{i}
have positive measure (i.e.,
|\Gamma_{m}|>0
,
|\Gamma_{i}|>0
) but allow
\Gamma_{f}=\emptyset
, namely,

can be enclosed completely by

In the sequel, the subscript

(or

\mathbf{u}
the mean velocity of the (incompressible) fluid and

\mathbf{u}|_{\Omega_{f}}=\mathbf{u}_{f},\ \ \ \ \mathbf{u}|_{\Omega_{m}}=\mathbf{u}_{m},\ \ \ \ \theta|_{\Omega_{f}}=\theta_{f},\ \ \theta|_{\Omega_{m}}=\theta_{m}.

Governing PDE system
. We shall consider the following Navier–Stokes–Darcy–Boussinesq system (in a nondimensionallized form, see
[
34
]
)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{f}+(\mathbf{u}_{f}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{f}=\nabla\cdot\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u}_{f},P_{f})+\theta_{f}\mathbf{k},

\displaystyle\mbox{in}\ \Omega_{f}\times(0,T),

(1.1)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{f}=0,

\displaystyle\mbox{in}\ \Omega_{f}\times(0,T),

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\theta_{f}+(\mathbf{u}_{f}\cdot\nabla)\theta_{f}={\rm div}(\lambda_{f}(\theta_{f})\nabla\theta_{f}),

\displaystyle\mbox{in}\ \Omega_{f}\times(0,T),

(1.3)

\displaystyle\varpi\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{m}+\nu(\theta_{m})\mathbb{K}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{m}=-\nabla P_{m}+\theta_{m}\mathbf{k},

\displaystyle\mbox{in}\ \Omega_{m}\times(0,T),

(1.4)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{m}=0,

\displaystyle\mbox{in}\ \Omega_{m}\times(0,T),

(1.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\theta_{m}+(\mathbf{u}_{m}\cdot\nabla)\theta_{m}={\rm div}(\lambda_{m}(\theta_{m})\nabla\theta_{m}),

\displaystyle\mbox{in}\ \Omega_{m}\times(0,T),

(1.6)

where

is an arbitrary final time and

is the upward pointing unit vector. In the free-flow region, the motion of the incompressible fluid is characterized by the Navier–Stokes equations (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) with Boussinesq approximation (buoyancy force), coupled with the advection-diffusion equation (
1.3
) for the temperature. While for the fluid in porous medium, we employ the Darcy system (
1.4
)–(
1.5
) (valid under the small porosity assumption that is generally applicable to geophysical systems) with the advection-diffusion equation (
1.6
). The Cauchy stress tensor
\mathbb{T}
in equation (
1.1
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u}_{f},p_{f})=2\nu(\theta_{f})\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u}_{f})-P_{f}\mathbb{I},

(1.7)

where
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u}_{f})=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathbf{u}_{f}+\nabla^{T}\mathbf{u}_{f})
is the symmetric rate of deformation tensor and
\mathbb{I}
denotes the
d\times d
identity matrix. The scalar functions
P_{f}
and
P_{m}
stand for the pressures in the free-flow and matrix regions. The fluid viscosity is denoted by
\nu
. The thermal diffusivity coefficients may differ in the free-flow and matrix regions and are denoted by
\lambda_{f}
,
\lambda_{m}
, respectively. The viscosity and thermal diffusivity are allowed to be functions that may depend on the temperature

, which are physically important in the study of non-isothermal fluids (see, e.g.,
[
31
]
). In equation (
1.4
),
\mathbb{K}
is a
d\times d
matrix standing for the permeability of the porous medium, which is usually assumed to be a bounded, symmetric and uniformly positive definite matrix but could be heterogeneous
[
4
]
. The parameter
\varpi
in (
1.4
) is a nonnegative constant related to the so-called Darcy–Prandtl number.
Since the Darcy–Prandtl number for porous medium flows is heuristically small in the regime with a small Darcy number
[
26
]
, the term
\varpi\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{m}
is often neglected in the literature (see e.g.,
[
33
,
41
]
), while in some other works, this time derivative term is kept primarily for the benefit of the energy analysis, and it would allow more accurate description of temporal transitions
[
34
]
. In this paper, we shall treat both cases with or without this time derivative term. For the sake of simplicity, physical coefficients that are not important for our subsequent mathematical analysis are set to be one (for instance, the fluid density and those in the Boussinesq approximation related to the gravitational acceleration and thermal expansion coefficient etc).

Next, we describe the initial, boundary as well as interface conditions of the coupled system (
1.1
)–(
1.7
).

Initial conditions
. The system (
1.1
)–(
1.7
) is subject to the initial conditions

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{f}|_{t=0}=\mathbf{u}_{0f}(x),\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega_{f},\quad\text{and}\qquad\mathbf{u}_{m}|_{t=0}=\mathbf{u}_{0m}(x),

\displaystyle\text{in}\ \Omega_{m},

(1.8)

\displaystyle\theta|_{t=0}=\theta_{0}(x),

\displaystyle\text{in}\ \Omega.

(1.9)

In particular, when
\varpi=0
, we do not need to specify the initial velocity
\mathbf{u}_{0m}
as it can be recovered from
\theta_{0m}
by solving the Darcy equation (see e.g.,
[
33
]
).

Boundary conditions on
\Gamma_{f}
and
\Gamma_{m}
. Since we are mainly interested in the coupling on the interface
\Gamma_{i}
between sub-domains, we impose the following standard boundary conditions on the outer boundaries
\Gamma_{f}
and
\Gamma_{m}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{f}=\mathbf{0},

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Gamma_{f}\times(0,T),

(1.10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{m}=0,

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Gamma_{m}\times(0,T),

(1.11)

\displaystyle\theta_{f}=0,

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Gamma_{f}\times(0,T),

(1.12)

\displaystyle\theta_{m}=0,

\displaystyle\text{on}\ \Gamma_{m}\times(0,T).

(1.13)

Interface conditions on
\Gamma_{i}
. Now on
\Gamma_{i}
, we assume that the system (
1.1
)–(
1.7
) are coupled through the following set of interface conditions:

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{f}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{i}=\mathbf{u}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{i},

\displaystyle\mbox{on}\ \Gamma_{i}\times(0,T),

(1.14)

\displaystyle-\mathbf{n}_{i}\cdot(\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u}_{f},P_{f}){\mathbf{n}_{i}})+\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{u}_{f}|^{2}=P_{m},

\displaystyle\mbox{on}\ \Gamma_{i}\times(0,T),

(1.15)

\displaystyle-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{j}\cdot(\mathbb{T}(\mathbf{u}_{f},P_{f}){\mathbf{n}_{i}})=\frac{\alpha\nu(\theta_{m})}{\sqrt{\text{trace}(\mathbb{K})}}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{f},\quad j=1,..,d-1,

\displaystyle\mbox{on}\ \Gamma_{i}\times(0,T),

(1.16)

\displaystyle\theta_{f}=\theta_{m},

\displaystyle\mbox{on}\ \Gamma_{i}\times(0,T),

(1.17)

\displaystyle\lambda_{f}(\theta_{f})\frac{\partial\theta_{f}}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{i}}=\lambda_{m}(\theta_{m})\frac{\partial\theta_{m}}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{i}},

\displaystyle\mbox{on}\ \Gamma_{i}\times(0,T).

(1.18)

The condition (
1.14
) indicates the continuity in normal velocity that guarantees the conservation of mass, i.e., the exchange of fluid
between the two sub-domains is conservative. The condition (
1.15
)
represents the balance of the forces normal to the interface taking into consideration of the so-called dynamic pressure
\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{u}_{f}|^{2}
. With this quadratic term, condition (
1.15
) is known as the Lions interface condition in the literature (see e.g.,
[
9
,
14
,
17
]
). This specific choice gives rise to a dissipative energy law that facilitates the analysis significantly
[
7
]
. On the other hand, in the absence of this dynamic pressure term, the kinetic energy could increase without external forcing, which is physically unrealistic. Recently in
[
34
]
, the authors showed that the nonlinear dynamic pressure term is somewhat small, thus the difference between solutions produced with the Lions interface condition and its linear counterpart is heuristically small as well. More precisely, with a formal asymptotic argument, they showed that the order of the dynamic pressure term is
\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{Da})
in the small Darcy number limit as
\mathrm{Da}\to 0
and it begins to affect solutions to the perturbed systems at the scale

\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{Da}^{2})
(see
[
34
, Appendix]
).

Next, the interface condition (
1.16
) is
the so-called Beavers–Joseph–Saffman–Jones (BJSJ) condition (cf.
[
24
,
35
]
), where
\alpha>0
is an empirical constant usually determined by the domain geometry and the porous material. We note that the BJSJ condition is a simplified variant of the well-known Beavers–Joseph (BJ) condition (see
[
5
]
) that addresses how the porous medium affects the conduit flow at the interface:

-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{j}\cdot(2\nu\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u}_{f}))\mathbf{n}_{i}=\frac{\alpha_{BJ}\nu}{\sqrt{\text{trace}(\mathbb{K})}}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{j}\cdot(\mathbf{u}_{f}-\mathbf{u}_{m}),\ \ \mbox{on}\ \Gamma_{i},\ j=1,...,d-1.

This empirical condition essentially says that the
tangential component of the normal stress that the free-flow incurs along the interface is proportional to the jump in the tangential velocity over the interface. To get the BJSJ condition, the term
-\boldsymbol{\tau}_{j}\cdot\mathbf{u}_{m}
on the right-hand
side is simply dropped from the corresponding BJ condition
(as long as the Darcy number is small). Mathematically rigorous justification of this simplification under appropriate assumptions can be found in
[
23
]
. At last, the interface conditions (
1.17
)–(
1.18
) involving
\theta
are simply the continuity conditions for the temperature functions and their conormal derivatives across the interface (sometimes also referred to as transmission conditions).

The coupling system (
1.1
)–(
1.7
) contains the Navier–Stokes–Darcy system for an incompressible viscous flow as a subsystem, which has been extensively studied in the literature. We just mention a few results related to the mathematics analysis and refer readers to the references cited therein.

For the simplified case of coupled (stationary) Stokes and Darcy equations, analysis of weak solutions has been done in
[
6
,
25
,
13
]
. We also refer to
[
20
]
for the existence of global weak solutions and weak-strong uniqueness of a Cahn–Hilliard–Stokes–Darcy system for incompressible two-phase fluids. For the coupling of Navier–Stokes and Darcy equations, the stationary case has been studied in
[
2
,
12
,
14
,
17
]
, and in
[
8
]
, a Navier–Stokes/Darcy–transport system modelling the contamination of ground water was analyzed. On the other hand, the time-dependent problem have been investigated in
[

,
9
,
10
]
with different choices of interface conditions. In those works, existence and uniqueness of weak solutions are obtained under certain specific conditions, for instance, the small data assumption.

We also note that our system (
1.1
)–(
1.7
) contains the Boussinesq system either for the free-flow
[
16
,
22
,
27
,
28
,
31
,
32
,
37
,
39
,
42
]
or for the flow in a porous medium
[
15
,
33
]
, which have been studied in a vast literature under various settings. In our current case, we have to deal with new difficulties due to the complicated coupling of flows governed by different physical processes, the complex geometry of domain, and in particular, the coupled nonlinear interface conditions.

The aim of this paper is two-fold. First, we prove the existence of global weak solutions to problem (
1.1
)–(
1.18
) with variable fluid viscosity, thermal diffusivity and a nonnegative Darcy–Prandtl number
\varpi
in both two and three dimensions (see Theorem
2.1
). Here we choose to work with the Lions and BJSJ interface conditions, which lead to a dissipative energy law for the solutions so that no smallness assumptions on the initial data are necessary (cf.
[
7
,
10
]
). The proof is based on a semi-implicit discretization scheme with a Brinkman type regularization that can be solved by the Leray–Schauder principle (cf.
[
1
,
20
]
for related applications to some hydrodynamic systems for two-phase flows). Using the energy inequality, we derive uniform estimates of approximate solutions and then obtain the existence of global weak solutions to the original system by a compactness argument.

Second, we prove the weak-strong uniqueness property of problem (
1.1
)–(
1.18
) (see Theorem
2.2
), namely, a weak solution coincides with a strong solution emanating from the same initial data as long as the latter exists. Uniqueness of weak solutions to problem (
1.1
)–(
1.18
) is not trivial even when the spatial dimension is two due to the nonlinear interface condition of Lions type. Besides the Navier–Stokes equations, additional difficulties come from the thermal advection terms, low regularity of the solution
\mathbf{u}_{m}
to Darcy’s equation and variable viscosity/thermal diffusivity coefficients. We remark that our approach can be further applied to study the Cahn–Hilliard–Navier–Stokes–Darcy–Boussinesq system for thermal convection of two-phase flows in a fluid layer overlying a porous medium (see e.g.,
[
11
]
).

Finally, due to the decomposed domain setting in the Bénard convection problem (
1.1
)–(
1.18
) and in particular, the complicated coupling via interface conditions, existence of strong or classical solutions (especially with higher-order spatial regularity) remains a challenging open problem, which is quite different from the case in a simple domain (cf.
[
16
,
22
,
27
,
32
,
33
,
37
]
and the references therein). We refer to
[
30
]
for an attempt on the Navier–Stokes–Darcy system for isothermal incompressible flows in a two-dimensional strip domain.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we introduce the function spaces, present the definition of weak solutions and state the main results. Section 3 is devoted to the proof for the existence of global weak solutions to problem (
1.1
)–(
1.18
). In Section 4, we prove the weak-strong uniqueness property by the energy method.

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\delta\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\frac{\alpha\nu(\theta^{k}_{m})}{\sqrt{{\rm trace}(\mathbb{K})}}|\mathbf{u}_{f}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_{j}|^{2}dS+\frac{1}{2}\delta\sigma\int_{\Omega}\lambda(\theta^{k})|\nabla\theta|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\big(\mathbf{u}_{f}-\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k},\mathbf{u}_{f}-\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k}\big)_{f}+\frac{\varpi}{2}\big(\mathbf{u}_{m}-\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k},\mathbf{u}_{m}-\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k}\big)_{m}

Proof.

Taking

,
\mathbf{v}_{m}=\mathbf{u}_{m}

\displaystyle a\cdot(a-b)=\frac{1}{2}\left(|a|^{2}-|b|^{2}+|a-b|^{2}\right),\quad\forall\,a,b\in\mathbb{R}\ \ \text{or}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{d},

(3.11)

we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\delta}\left(\mathbf{u}_{f},\mathbf{u}_{f}\right)_{f}+\frac{1}{2\delta}\big(\mathbf{u}_{f}-\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k},\mathbf{u}_{f}-\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k}\big)_{f}+\frac{\varpi}{2\delta}\left(\mathbf{u}_{m},\mathbf{u}_{m}\right)_{m}

\displaystyle+\left(\nu(\theta_{m}^{k})\mathbb{K}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{m},\mathbf{u}_{m}\right)_{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\frac{\alpha\nu(\theta^{k}_{m})}{\sqrt{{\rm trace}(\mathbb{K})}}|\mathbf{u}_{f}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_{j}|^{2}dS

\displaystyle+\xi\left(\nabla\mathbf{u}_{m},\nabla\mathbf{u}_{m}\right)_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\delta}\big(\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k},\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k}\big)_{f}+\frac{\varpi}{2\delta}\big(\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k},\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k}\big)_{m}+(\theta_{f}\mathbf{k},\mathbf{u}_{f})_{f}+(\theta_{m}\mathbf{k},\mathbf{u}_{m})_{m}.

Next, taking the test function

Using Lemma

\displaystyle(\theta_{f}\mathbf{k},\mathbf{u}_{f})_{f}+(\theta_{m}\mathbf{k},\mathbf{u}_{m})_{m}

\displaystyle\leq\|\theta_{f}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f})}\|\mathbf{u}_{f}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{f})}+\|\theta_{m}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{m})}\|\mathbf{u}_{m}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{m})}

In view of assumptions (A1)–(A3), we can take

Then multiplying (

\displaystyle\quad=-\left(\frac{\mathbf{u}_{f}-\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k}}{\delta},\mathbf{v}_{f}\right)_{f}-\varpi\left(\frac{\mathbf{u}_{m}-\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k}}{\delta},\mathbf{v}_{m}\right)_{m}

.

Lemma 3.6
.

The operator

Proof.

For all

Indeed, the estimates for the linear terms are obvious, thus we only need to estimate those terms that are nonlinear:

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{u}_{f}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{f})\|_{\big(\mathbf{H}^{\frac{3}{4}}(\Omega_{f})\big)^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{u}_{f}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{f})\|_{\mathbf{L}^{\frac{4}{3}}(\Omega_{f})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mathrm{div}(\mathbf{u}_{f}\otimes\mathbf{u}_{f})\|_{\mathbf{L}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega_{f})}

\displaystyle\sup_{\|\mathbf{v}_{f}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{\frac{3}{4}}(\Omega_{f})}\leq 1}\left|\int_{\Gamma_{i}}|\mathbf{u}_{f}|^{2}(\mathbf{v}_{f}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{i})dS\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|(\mathbf{u}_{f},\mathbf{u}_{m})\|_{\mathbf{V}}\|\theta\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}.

easily follows from the fact
\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Y}
.
∎

Since the operator
\mathcal{T}_{k}:\mathbf{X}\to\mathbf{Y}

\mathbf{w}\in\mathbf{X}

\mathbf{q}=\mathcal{T}_{k}(\mathbf{w})

,

\mathcal{G}_{k}
is compact.

Now the original problem can be reduced to find a fixed point
\mathbf{q}
of the operator
\mathcal{N}_{k}
in
\mathbf{Y}
, that is,

\mathbf{q}=\mathcal{N}_{k}(\mathbf{q}).

(3.26)

Existence of such a fixed point follows from an application of the Leray–Shauder principle. More precisely, we have

Lemma 3.7
.

Assume that assumptions (A1)–(A3) are satisfied. For any
\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{f,\mathrm{div}}
,
\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{m,\mathrm{div}}
and
\theta^{k}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, the abstract equation (

) admits a solution
\mathbf{q}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}\subset\mathbf{Y}

Proof.

According to the abstract result
[
43

, it remains to show that there exists a constant
R>0
such that if
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbf{Y}
and
0\leq s\leq 1
satisfying
\mathbf{q}=s\mathcal{N}_{k}(\mathbf{q})

\|\mathbf{q}\|_{\mathbf{Y}}\leq R
.

We consider
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbf{Y}
and
0\leq s\leq 1
satisfying
\mathbf{q}=s\mathcal{N}_{k}(\mathbf{q})
. Denote
\mathbf{w}=\mathcal{T}_{k}^{-1}(\mathbf{q})
. Then we have

\mathcal{T}_{k}(\mathbf{w})=s\mathcal{G}_{k}(\mathbf{w})
that is equivalent to the weak formulation

\displaystyle 2\left(\nu(\theta_{f}^{k})\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u}_{f}),\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{v}_{f})\right)_{f}+\left(\nu(\theta_{m}^{k})\mathbb{K}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{m},\mathbf{v}_{m})\right)_{m}

(3.27)

\displaystyle+\xi(\nabla\mathbf{u}_{m},\nabla\mathbf{v}_{m})_{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\frac{\alpha\nu(\theta^{k}_{m})}{\sqrt{{\rm trace}(\mathbb{K})}}(\mathbf{u}_{f}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i})(\mathbf{v}_{f}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i})dS

\displaystyle-s\left(\frac{\mathbf{u}_{f}-\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k}}{\delta},\mathbf{v}_{f}\right)_{f}-s\varpi\left(\frac{\mathbf{u}_{m}-\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k}}{\delta},\mathbf{v}_{m}\right)_{m}

\displaystyle+s(\theta_{f}\mathbf{k},\mathbf{v}_{f})_{f}+s(\theta_{m}\mathbf{k},\mathbf{v}_{m})_{m},

and

\big(\lambda(\theta^{k})\nabla\theta,\nabla\phi\big)=-s\left(\frac{\theta-\theta^{k}}{\delta},\phi\right)-s(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\theta,\phi).

(3.28)

Similar to the derivation of the discrete energy inequality in Lemma
3.2
, we can derive

\displaystyle\frac{s}{2\delta}\left(\mathbf{u}_{f},\mathbf{u}_{f}\right)_{f}+\frac{s}{2\delta}\big(\mathbf{u}_{f}-\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k},\mathbf{u}_{f}-\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k}\big)_{f}+\frac{\varpi s}{2\delta}\left(\mathbf{u}_{m},\mathbf{u}_{m}\right)_{m}

Similar to the previous argument for (

, we can derive the following discrete energy inequality with respect to

\displaystyle s\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}_{f},\mathbf{u}_{m},\theta)+\delta\underline{\nu}\left(\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u}_{f}),\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u}_{f})\right)_{f}+\frac{1}{2}\delta\underline{\nu}\bar{\kappa}^{-1}\left(\mathbf{u}_{m},\mathbf{u}_{m}\right)_{m}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\delta\alpha\underline{\nu}\bar{\kappa}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}|\mathbf{u}_{f}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_{j}|^{2}dS+\delta\xi(\nabla\mathbf{u}_{m},\nabla\mathbf{u}_{m})_{m}+\frac{1}{2}\delta\sigma\underline{\lambda}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\theta|^{2}dx

\displaystyle s\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k},\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k},\theta^{k})\leq\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k},\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k},\theta^{k}),

with a sufficiently large constant
\sigma
that is independent of

.
As a consequence, we have (recall also Lemma

)

\|\mathbf{w}\|_{\mathbf{X}}\leq C_{k},\quad\forall\,s\in[0,1],

where the constant
C_{k}
may depend on

,

and

In order to derive an estimate for

and Lemma

maps bounded sets in

and

where the constant
R
depends on
C_{k}
,
but is independent of

.

The proof of Lemma
3.7
is complete.

Moreover, the solution
(\mathbf{u}_{f},\mathbf{u}_{m},\theta)
satisfies the discrete energy inequality (
3.10
).

3.2
Construction of approximate solutions

Once we have proved the existence of weak solutions to the time-discrete problem (
3.8
)–(
3.9
), we are able to construct approximate solutions to the regularized time-continuous system (
3.5
)–(
3.6
). Recall that
\delta=\frac{T}{N}
, where
T>0
and
N
is an positive integer. We set

t_{k}=k\delta,\quad k=0,1,\cdots,N.

Let
(\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k+1},\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k+1},\theta^{k+1})
(
k=0,1,\cdots,N-1
) be chosen successively as a solution of the discrete problem (
3.8
)–(
3.9
) with
(\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k},\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k},\theta^{k})
being the “initial value” (see Lemma
3.8
). In particular, we set
(\mathbf{u}_{f}^{0},\mathbf{u}_{m}^{0},\theta^{0})=(\mathbf{u}_{0f},\mathbf{u}_{0m},\theta_{0})
with the choice
\mathbf{u}_{m}^{0}=\mathbf{0}
when
\varpi=0
.
Then for
k=0,1,\cdots,N-1
, we define the approximate solutions as follows

\displaystyle\theta^{\delta}:=\frac{t_{k+1}-t}{\delta}\theta^{k}+\frac{t-t_{k}}{\delta}\theta^{k+1},

\displaystyle\text{for}\ t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}],

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}^{\delta}_{f}:=\frac{t_{k+1}-t}{\delta}\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k}+\frac{t-t_{k}}{\delta}\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k+1},

\displaystyle\text{for}\ t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}],

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}^{\delta}_{m}:=\frac{t_{k+1}-t}{\delta}\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k}+\frac{t-t_{k}}{\delta}\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k+1},

\displaystyle\text{for}\ t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}],

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}^{\delta}:=\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k+1},

\displaystyle\text{for}\ t\in(t_{k},t_{k+1}],

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{m}^{\delta}:=\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k+1},

\displaystyle\text{for}\ t\in(t_{k},t_{k+1}],

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}|_{\Omega_{f}}=\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}^{\delta},\quad\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}|_{\Omega_{m}}=\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}_{m},

\displaystyle\text{for}\ t\in(t_{k},t_{k+1}],

\displaystyle\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}:=\theta^{k+1},

\displaystyle\text{for}\ t\in(t_{k},t_{k+1}],

\displaystyle\widetilde{\theta}^{\delta}:=\theta^{k},

\displaystyle\text{for}\ t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}).

Remark 3.1
.

It follows from the above definitions that
\theta^{\delta}
,
\mathbf{u}^{\delta}_{f}
,
\mathbf{u}^{\delta}_{m}
are continuous piecewise linear functions in time, while

\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}^{\delta}
,
\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{m}^{\delta}
,
\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}
are piecewise constant (in time) functions being right continuous at the nodes

\{t_{k+1}\}
and
\widetilde{\theta}^{\delta}
is left continuous at the nodes
\{t_{k}\}
.

Using the above definition of approximate solutions, we can derive from the discrete problem (
3.8
)–(
3.9
) that the following identities hold:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\big(\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{f}^{\delta},\mathbf{v}_{f}\big)_{f}dt+\varpi\int_{0}^{T}\big(\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{m}^{\delta},\mathbf{v}_{m}\big)_{m}dt

(3.32)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\big(\mathrm{div}(\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}^{\delta}\otimes\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}^{\delta}),\mathbf{v}_{f}\big)_{f}dt+2\int_{0}^{T}\left(\nu(\widetilde{\theta}_{f}^{\delta})\mathbb{D}(\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}^{\delta}),\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{v}_{f})\right)_{f}dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\nu(\widetilde{\theta}_{m}^{\delta})\mathbb{K}^{-1}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{m}^{\delta},\mathbf{v}_{m})\right)_{m}dt+\xi\int_{0}^{T}\left(\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{m}^{\delta},\nabla\mathbf{v}_{m}\right)_{m}dt

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\frac{\alpha\nu(\widetilde{\theta}_{m}^{\delta})}{\sqrt{{\rm trace}(\mathbb{K})}}(\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}^{\delta}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_{j})(\mathbf{v}_{f}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_{j})dSdt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\!\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\frac{1}{2}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}^{\delta}|^{2}(\mathbf{v}_{f}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{i})dSdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\big(\widehat{\theta}_{f}^{\delta}\mathbf{k},\mathbf{v}_{f}\big)_{f}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\big(\widehat{\theta}_{m}^{\delta}\mathbf{k},\mathbf{v}_{m}\big)_{m}dt,

for any
\mathbf{v}_{f}\in C^{1}_{0}((0,T);\mathbf{H}_{f,\mathrm{div}})
,
\mathbf{v}_{m}\in C^{1}_{0}((0,T);\mathbf{H}_{m,\mathrm{div}})
with
\mathbf{v}_{f}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{i}=\mathbf{v}_{m}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{i}
on
\Gamma_{i}
, and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\big(\partial_{t}\theta^{\delta},\phi\big)dt+\int_{0}^{T}\big(\lambda(\widetilde{\theta}^{\delta})\nabla\widehat{\theta}^{\delta},\nabla\phi\big)dt=\int_{0}^{T}\big(\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}\widehat{\theta}^{\delta},\nabla\phi\big)dt

(3.33)

for any
\phi\in C_{0}^{1}((0,T);H^{1}(\Omega))
.

Besides, in analogy to the estimates for (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), we can obtain the energy inequalities for
t\in[0,T]
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}_{f}(t)\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{f})}^{2}+\frac{\varpi}{2}\|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}_{m}(t)\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{m})}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\left(\nu(\widetilde{\theta}^{\delta}_{f})\mathbb{D}(\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}_{f}),\mathbb{D}(\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}_{f})\right)_{f}d\tau

(3.34)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\nu(\widetilde{\theta}^{\delta}_{m})\mathbb{K}^{-1}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}_{m},\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}_{m}\right)_{m}d\tau+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\int_{0}^{t}\!\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\frac{\alpha\nu(\widetilde{\theta}^{\delta}_{m})}{\sqrt{{\rm trace}(\mathbb{K})}}|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}_{f}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_{j}|^{2}dSd\tau

\displaystyle+\xi\int_{0}^{t}\left(\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}_{m},\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}_{m}\right)_{m}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{u}_{0f}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{f})}^{2}+\frac{\varpi}{2}\|\mathbf{u}_{0m}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{m})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}_{f}\mathbf{k},\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}_{f}\big)_{f}+\big(\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}_{m}\mathbf{k},\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}_{m}\big)_{m}d\tau,

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\lambda(\widetilde{\theta}^{\delta})|\nabla\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}|^{2}dxd\tau\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

(3.35)

Let
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}^{\delta}(t)
be the piecewise linear interpolation of the discrete energy
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k},\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k},\theta^{k})
(with the same choice for the constant
\sigma
as in Lemma
3.2
) such that

\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}^{\delta}(t)=\frac{t_{k+1}-t}{\delta}\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k},\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k},\theta^{k})+\frac{t-t_{k}}{\delta}\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k+1},\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k+1},\theta^{k+1}),\quad\text{for}\ t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}],

and
\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}^{\delta}(t)
be the approximate energy dissipation

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}^{\delta}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left(\nu(\theta_{f}^{k})\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k+1}),\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k+1})\right)_{f}+\left(\nu(\theta_{m}^{k})\mathbb{K}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k+1},\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k+1}\right)_{m}

\displaystyle+\xi(\nabla\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k+1},\nabla\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k+1})_{m}+\sigma\int_{\Omega}\lambda(\theta^{k})|\nabla\theta^{k+1}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\int_{\Gamma_{i}}\frac{\alpha\nu(\theta^{k}_{m})}{\sqrt{{\rm trace}(\mathbb{K})}}|\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k+1}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_{j}|^{2}dS,\quad\text{for}\ t\in(t_{k},t_{k+1}).

We see from the discrete energy estimate (
3.10
) that for
k=0,1,\cdots,N-1
, it holds

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}^{\delta}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\delta}\left[\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k+1},\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k+1},\theta^{k+1})-\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}_{f}^{k},\mathbf{u}_{m}^{k},\theta^{k})\right]

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}^{\delta}(t),\quad\text{for}\ t\in(t_{k},t_{k+1}).

(3.36)

In particular, we have for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}^{\delta}(t),\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{m}^{\delta}(t),\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}(t))+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}^{\delta}(\tau)d\tau\leq\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}_{0f},\mathbf{u}_{0m},\theta_{0}).

(3.37)

3.3
Proof of Theorem
2.1

We now proceed to finish the proof of Theorem
2.1
. First, we prove the conclusion for the case
\varpi>0
, and then we point out necessary modifications for the case
\varpi=0

3.3.1
Case
\varpi>0

Step 1. Passage to the limit
\delta\to 0
.
First, we pass to the limit as
\delta\to 0
while keeping the regularizing parameter
\xi>0
fixed. From the energy inequality (
3.37
), we can derive estimates on the approximate solutions that are uniform in
\delta

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}_{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{f}))}+\varpi^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}_{m}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{m}))}+\|\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C,

(3.38)

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{D}(\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}_{f})\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{f}))}+\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}^{\delta}\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\Gamma_{i}))}\leq C,

(3.39)

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{m}^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{m}))}+\xi^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{m}^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{m}))}\leq C,

(3.41)

where the constant

depends on
\mathcal{E}_{\sigma}(\mathbf{u}_{0f},\mathbf{u}_{0m},\theta_{0})

and
\Omega
, but is independent of the parameters
\delta
and
\xi
.

From the uniform estimates (
3.38
)–(
3.41
) and Lemma

, we deduce that there exists a convergent subsequence
\{(\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}^{\delta},\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{m}^{\delta},\widehat{\theta}^{\delta})\}
(still denoted by the same symbols for simplicity) as
\delta\to 0
(or equivalently
N\to+\infty
) such that

\begin{cases}\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}^{\delta}\rightarrow\mathbf{u}_{f}^{\xi}&\text{ weakly star in }L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{f})),\\
&\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega_{f})),\\
\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{m}^{\delta}\rightarrow\mathbf{u}_{m}^{\xi}&\text{ weakly star in }L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{m})),\\
&\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega_{m})),\\
\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}\rightarrow\theta^{\xi}&\text{ weakly star in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)),\\
&\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)),\end{cases}

(3.42)

for certain limit functions
(\mathbf{u}_{f}^{\xi},\mathbf{u}_{m}^{\xi},\theta^{\xi})
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{f}^{\xi}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{f,\mathrm{div}})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{H}_{f,\mathrm{div}}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{m}^{\xi}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}_{m,\mathrm{div}})\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{H}_{m,\mathrm{div}}),

\displaystyle\theta^{\xi}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)),

with
\mathbf{u}_{f}^{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{i}=\mathbf{u}_{m}^{\xi}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{i}
on
\Gamma_{i}
.

In order to pass to the limit in those nonlinear terms, we need to obtain some information on the strong convergence of
\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}
(up to a subsequence). It follows from equation (
3.33
), the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality and the Sobolev embedding theorem that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\theta^{\delta}\|^{\frac{4}{3}}_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))}

(3.43)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|\nabla\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{3}}+\|\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{6}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{3}}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{3}}dt+C\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{3}}\int_{0}^{T}\|\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{3}}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\|\nabla\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+1\Big)dt+C\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2}{3}}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\|\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}^{\delta}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{T},

where the constant
C_{T}
is independent of
\delta
, but may depend on
\xi
.
As a result, it follows that
\partial_{t}\theta^{\delta}\in L^{\frac{4}{3}}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))
is bounded and
\partial_{t}\theta^{\delta}\to\partial_{t}\theta^{\xi}
in the sense of distribution. On the other hand, by the definition of
\theta^{\delta}
, it satisfies similar uniform estimates like those for
\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}
. Hence, applying Simon’s compactness lemma (see e.g.,
[
36

), we deduce that there exists

\theta^{*}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1-\beta}(\Omega))\cap C([0,T];H^{-\beta}(\Omega)),

for some
\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
such that up to a subsequence,

\theta^{\delta}\to\theta^{*}\quad\text{strongly in}\ L^{2}(0,T;H^{1-\beta}(\Omega))\cap C([0,T];H^{-\beta}(\Omega))\quad\text{as}\ \delta\to 0.

Due to the uniqueness of limit (for the same convergent subsequence), we have
\theta^{*}=\theta^{\xi}
. Besides, by (
3.42
) and (
3.43
), we also have
\theta^{\xi}\in C_{w}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))

\theta^{\delta}|_{t=0}=\theta_{0}

\theta^{\xi}|_{t=0}=\theta_{0}.

\int_{0}^{T}\|\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}-\theta^{\delta}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{3}}dt\leq\delta^{\frac{4}{3}}\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}\theta^{\delta}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{3}}dt\to 0\quad\text{as}\ \delta\to 0,

(3.44)

which implies

\displaystyle\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}-\theta^{\delta}\to 0\quad\text{strongly in}\ \ L^{\frac{4}{3}}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))\ \ \text{ as }\delta\rightarrow 0.

Similarly, one can show that
\|\widetilde{\theta}^{\delta}-\theta^{\delta}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))}\rightarrow 0
as
\delta\rightarrow 0
. Therefore, the sequences
\{\theta^{\delta}\}
,
\{\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}\}
and
\{\widetilde{\theta}^{\delta}\}

\theta^{\xi}

3.38

3.41

\delta\rightarrow 0

\widehat{\theta}^{\delta},\widetilde{\theta}^{\delta}\to\theta^{\xi}\quad\text{strongly in}\ \ L^{\frac{32}{17}}(0,T;H^{\frac{3}{4}}(\Omega))\cap L^{q}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega)).

(3.45)

for any

\theta^{\delta}

\widehat{\theta}^{\delta}

\widetilde{\theta}^{\delta}
in

Next, using equation (

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{f}^{\delta}\|^{\frac{4}{3}}_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}\big(0,T;(\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{f,\mathrm{div}})^{\prime}\big)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}^{\delta}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{3}(\Omega_{f})}^{\frac{4}{3}}\|\nabla\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}^{\delta}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{f})}^{\frac{4}{3}}+\|\widehat{\mathbf{u}}_{f}^{\delta}\|_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega_{f})}^{\frac{4}{3}}+\|\widehat{\theta}_{f}^{\delta}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{f})}^{\frac{4}{3}}\right)dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{T}.

In a similar manner, taking test functions
\mathbf{v}_{f}=\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{v}_{m}\in L^{2}(0,T;\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{m,\mathrm{div}})

\displaystyle\varpi^{2}\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{u}_{m}^{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;(\widehat{\mathbf{H}}_{m,\mathrm{div}})^{\prime})}

\delta

\xi

\mathbf{v}_{m}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{H}_{m,\mathrm{div}})

where

h(t)=\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{f}(t)\|_{\mathbf{W}^{1,6}(\Omega_{f})}^{4}+\|\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{m}(t)\|_{\mathbf{L}^{6}(\Omega_{m})}^{4}+\|\nabla\bar{\theta}(t)\|_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)}^{8}+1.

By the additional regularity assumptions on
(\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{f},\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{m},\bar{\theta})
, i.e.,

\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{f}\in L^{4}(0,T;\mathbf{W}^{1,6}(\Omega_{f})),\quad\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{m}\in L^{4}(0,T;\mathbf{L}^{6}(\Omega_{m})),\quad\bar{\theta}\in L^{8}(0,T;W^{1,4}(\Omega)),

we see that
h(t)\in L^{1}(0,T)
. This enables us to apply Gronwall’s lemma and Lemma
2.1
to conclude that

\|\mathbf{u}_{f}(t)-\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{f}(t)\|_{\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega_{f})}^{2}+\|\theta(t)-\bar{\theta}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\mathbf{u}(\tau)-\bar{\mathbf{u}}(\tau)\|_{\mathbf{Z}}^{2}d\tau=0,

for a.a.
t\in[0,T]
. As a consequence, we obtain the weak-strong uniqueness result for problem (
1.1
)–(
1.18
) with
\varpi\geq 0
.

The proof of Theorem
2.2
is complete.
\square

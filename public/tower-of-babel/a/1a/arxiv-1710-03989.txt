Locally Lipschitz vector optimization problems: second-order constraint qualifications, regularity condition and KKT necessary optimality conditions
Yi-Bin Xiao, Nguyen Van Tuyen, Jen-Chih Yao, Ching-Feng Wen

Abstract
In the present paper, we are concerned with a class of constrained vector optimization problems, where the objective functions and active constraint functions are locally Lipschitz at the referee point. Some second-order constraint qualifications of Zangwill type, Abadie type and Mangasarian – Fromovitz type as well as a regularity condition of Abadie type are proposed in a nonsmooth setting. The connections between these proposed conditions are established. They are applied to develop second-order Karush–Kuhn–Tucker necessary optimality conditions for local (weak, Geoffrion properly) efficient solutions to the considered problem. Examples are also given to illustrate the obtained results.

Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in second-order optimality conditions for the following constrained vector optimization problem

\displaystyle\text{min}\,f(x)

(VP)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}\ \ x\in Q_{0}:=\{x\in X\,:\,g(x)\leqq 0\},

where
f:=(f_{i})
,
i\in I:=\{1,\ldots,p\}
, and
g:=(g_{j})
,
j\in J:=\{1,\ldots,m\}
are vector-valued functions defined on a Banach space
X
.

As a mainstream in the study of vector optimization problems, optimality condition for vector optimization problems has attracted the attention of many researchers in the field of optimization due to their important applications in many disciplines, such as variational inequalities, equilibrium problems and fixed pointed problems; see, for example,
Lee98
;
Lu18
;
mor06
;
Petrusel18
;
Wang16
;
XS
;
SXC1
;
SXC2
;
Qin
.

It is well-known that if
f_{i}
,
g_{j}
are differentiable at
\bar{x}\in Q_{0}
and
\bar{x}
is a local weak efficient solution of (
VP
), then
there exist Lagrange multipliers
(\lambda,\mu)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
satisfying

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}\lambda_{i}\nabla f_{i}(\bar{x})+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mu_{j}\nabla g_{j}(\bar{x})=0,

(1)

\displaystyle\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{m})\geqq 0,\mu_{j}g_{j}(\bar{x})=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{p})\geqq 0,(\lambda,\mu)\neq 0;

(3)

see
(
Jahn04
, Theorem 7.4)
. Conditions (
1
)–(
3
) are called the first-order F.-John necessary optimality conditions. If
\lambda
is nonzero, then these conditions are called the first-order Karush–Kuhn–Tucker
(KKT)
optimality conditions. By Motzkin’s theorem of the alternative
(
Mangasarian69
, p.28)
, the existence of
KKT
multipliers is equivalent to the inconsistency of the following system

\displaystyle\nabla f_{i}(\bar{x})(v)

\displaystyle<0,\ \ \ i\in I,

(4)

\displaystyle\nabla g_{j}(\bar{x})(v)

\displaystyle\leqq 0,\ \ \ j\in J(\bar{x}),

(5)

with unknown
v\in X
, where
J(\bar{x})
is the active index set at
\bar{x}
. Conditions (
4
)–(
5
) are called
the first-order
KKT
necessary conditions in primal form.

The first-order
KKT
optimality conditions are needed to find optimal solutions of constrained optimization problems. In order to obtain these optimality conditions, constraint qualifications and regularity conditions are indispensable; see, for example,
Andreani11
;
Tuyen18
;
Tung-Luu
;
Luu-Mai
;
TungLT
;
Tuyen-Xiao-Son
;
Movahedian
;
Soleimani
;
Gunther
. We recall here that these assumptions are called constraint qualifications
(CQ)
when they have to be fulfilled by the constraints of the problem, and they are called regularity conditions
(RC)
when they have to be fulfilled by both the objectives and the constraints of the problem; see
Rizvi12
for more details.

Second-order necessary optimality conditions play an important role in both
the theory and practice of constrained optimization problems. These conditions are used to eliminate nonoptimal KKT points of optimization problems. Moreover, the second-order optimality condition is a key tool of numerical analysis in proving convergence and deriving error
estimates for numerical discretizations of optimization problems; see, for example,
Bertsekas99
;
Izmailov08
;
Nocedal99
.

One of the first investigations to obtain second-order optimality conditions of
KKT
-type for smooth vector optimization problems was carried out by Wang
Wang91
. Then, by introducing a new second-order constraint qualification in the sense of Abadie, Aghezzaf et al.
Aghezzaf99
extended Wang’s results to the nonconvex case. Maeda
Maeda04
was the first to propose an Abadie regularity condition and established second-order
KKT
necessary optimality conditions for
C^{1,1}
vector optimization
problems. By using the second-order directional derivatives and introducing a new second-order constraint qualification of Zangwill-type, Ivanov
Ivanov15
introduced some optimality conditions for
C^{1}
vector optimization problems with inequality constraints. Very recently, by proposing some types of the second-order Abadie regularity conditions, Huy et al.
Huy162
;
Huy163
have obtained some second-order
KKT
necessary optimality conditions for
C^{1,1}
vector optimization problems in terms of second-order symmetric subdifferentials. For other contributions to second-order
KKT
optimality conditions for vector optimization, the reader is invited to see the papers
Elena
;
Ginchev08
;
Giorgi09
;
Ivanov152
;
Ivanov10
;
Luu17
;
Kim-Tuyen
;
Huy-Tuyen
with the references therein.

Our aim is to weaken the hypotheses of the optimality conditions in
Aghezzaf99
;
Elena
;
Huy163
;
Ivanov15
;
Luu17
;
Maeda04
;
Wang91
. To obtain second-order
KKT
necessary conditions, by using second-order upper generalized directional derivatives and second-order tangent sets, we introduce some second-order constraint qualifications of Zangwill type, Abadie type and Mangasarian-Fromovitz type as well as a regularity condition of Abadie type.
Our obtained results improve and generalize the corresponding results in
Aghezzaf99
;
Elena
;
Huy163
;
Ivanov15
;
Luu17
;
Maeda04
;
Wang91
, because the objective functions and the active constraint functions are only locally Lipschitz at the referee point and the required constraint qualifications are also weaker. Moreover, the connections between these proposed conditions are established.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some notations, definitions and preliminary material. Section
3
is devoted to investigate second-order constraint qualifications and regularity conditions in a nonsmooth setting for vector optimization problems. In Section
4
and Section
5
, we establish some second-order necessary optimality conditions of
KKT
-type for a local (weak, Geoffrion properly) efficient solution of (
VP
). Section
6
draws some conclusions.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some definitions and introduce basic results, which are useful in our study.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{p}
be the
p
-dimensional Euclidean space. For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, by
a\leqq b
, we mean
a_{i}\leqq b_{i}
for all
i\in I
; by
a\leq b
, we mean
a\leqq b
and
a\neq b
; and by
a<b
, we mean
a_{i}<b_{i}
for all
i\in I
.

We first recall the definition of local (weak, Geoffrion properly) efficient solutions for the considered problem (
VP
). Note that the concept of properly efficient solution has been introduced at first to eliminate the efficient solutions with unbounded trade-offs. This concept was introduced initially by Kuhn and Tucker
Kuhn50
and was followed thereafter by Geoffrion
Geoffrion68
. Geoffrion’s concept enjoys economical interpretations, while Kuhn and Tucker’s one is useful for numerical and algorithmic purposes.

Definition 1

Let
Q_{0}
be the feasible set of (
VP
) and
\bar{x}\in Q_{0}
. We say that:

(i)

\bar{x}
is
an efficient solution
(resp.,
a weak efficient solution
) of (
VP
) iff there is no
x\in Q_{0}
satisfying
f(x)\leq f(\bar{x})
(resp.,
f(x)<f(\bar{x})
).

(ii)

\bar{x}
is a
Geoffrion properly efficient solution
of (
VP
) iff it is efficient and there exists
M>0
and such that, for each
i
,

\frac{f_{i}(x)-f_{i}(\bar{x})}{f_{j}(\bar{x})-f_{j}(x)}\leqq M,

for some
j
such that
f_{j}(\bar{x})<f_{j}(x)
whenever
x\in Q_{0}
and
f_{i}(\bar{x})>f_{i}(x)
.

(iii)

\bar{x}
is a
local efficient solution
(resp.,
local weak efficient solution, local Geoffrion properly efficient solution
) of (
VP
) iff it is an efficient solution (resp., weak efficient solution, Geoffrion properly efficient solution) in
U\cap Q_{0}
, where
U
is some neighborhood of
\bar{x}
.

Hereafter, we assume that
X
is a Banach space equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|
. Let
\Omega
be a nonempty subset in
X
. The
closure
,
convex hull
and
conic hull
of
\Omega
are denoted by
\mbox{cl}\,\Omega
,

\mbox{conv}\,\Omega
and
\mbox{cone}\,\Omega
, respectively.

Definition 2

Let
\bar{x}\in\Omega
and
u\in X
.

(i)

The
tangent cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}\in\Omega
is defined by

T(\Omega;\bar{x}):=\{d\in X\,:\,\exists t_{k}\downarrow 0,\exists d^{k}\to d,\bar{x}+t_{k}d^{k}\in\Omega,\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}\}.

(ii)

The
second-order tangent set
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
with respect to the direction
u
is defined by

T^{2}(\Omega;\bar{x},u):=\left\{v\in X:\exists t_{k}\downarrow 0,\exists v^{k}\to v,\bar{x}+t_{k}u+\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}v^{k}\in\Omega,\ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Clearly,
T(\,\cdot\,;\bar{x})
and
T^{2}(\,\cdot\,;\bar{x},u)
are isotone, i.e., if
\Omega^{1}\subset\Omega^{2}
, then

\displaystyle T(\Omega^{1};\bar{x})

\displaystyle\subset T(\Omega^{2};\bar{x}),

\displaystyle T^{2}(\Omega^{1};\bar{x},u)

\displaystyle\subset T^{2}(\Omega^{2};\bar{x},u).

It is well-known that
T(\Omega;\bar{x})
is a nonempty closed cone. For each
u\in X
, the set
T^{2}(\Omega;\bar{x},u)
is closed, but may be empty. However, we see that the set
T^{2}(\Omega;\bar{x},0)=T(\Omega;\bar{x})
is always nonempty.

Let
F\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
be a real-valued function defined on
X
and
\bar{x}\in X
. The function
F
is said to be
locally Lipschitz
at
\bar{x}
iff there exist a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
and
L\geqq 0
such that

|F(x)-F(y)|\leqq L\|x-y\|,\ \ \ \forall x,y\in U.

Definition 3

Assume that
F\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is locally Lipschitz at
\bar{x}\in X
. Then:

(i)

(See
Clarke83
) The
Clarke’s generalized derivative
of
F
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

F^{\circ}(\bar{x},u):=\limsup\limits_{\mathop{x\to\bar{x}}\limits_{t\downarrow 0}}\dfrac{F(x+tu)-F(x)}{t},\ \ \ u\in X.

(ii)

(See
Pales94
) The
second-order upper generalized directional derivative
of
F
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

F^{\circ\circ}(\bar{x},u):=\limsup\limits_{\mathop{t\downarrow 0}}\dfrac{F(\bar{x}+tu)-F(\bar{x})-tF^{\circ}(\bar{x},u)}{\frac{1}{2}t^{2}},\ \ \ u\in X.

It is easily seen that
F^{\circ}(\bar{x},0)=0
and
F^{\circ\circ}(\bar{x},0)=0
. Furthermore, the function
u\mapsto F^{\circ}(\bar{x},u)
is finite, positively homogeneous, and subadditive on
X
; see, for example,
Clarke83
;
SX2
;
XS3
.

The following lemmas will be useful in our study.

Lemma 1

Suppose that
F\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is locally Lipschitz at
\bar{x}\in X
. Let
u\in X
and let
\{(t_{k},u^{k})\}
be a sequence converging to
(0^{+},u)
. If

F\left(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k}\right)\geqq F(\bar{x})\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ k\in\mathbb{N},

then
F^{\circ}(\bar{x},u)\geqq 0.

Proof

Since
F
is locally Lipschitz at
\bar{x}
and

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k})=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}(\bar{x}+t_{k}u)=\bar{x},

there exist
L\geqq 0
and
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

|F(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k})-F(\bar{x}+t_{k}u)|\leqq Lt_{k}\|u^{k}-u\|\ \ \text{for all}\ \ k\geqq k_{0}.

Thus,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqq F(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k})-F(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=[F(\bar{x}+t_{k}u^{k})-F(\bar{x}+t_{k}u)]+[F(\bar{x}+t_{k}u)-F(\bar{x})]

\displaystyle\leqq Lt_{k}\|u^{k}-u\|+F(\bar{x}+t_{k}u)-F(\bar{x})

for all
k\geqq k_{0}
. This implies that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqq\lim_{k\to\infty}L\|u^{k}-u\|+\limsup_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{F(\bar{x}+t_{k}u)-F(\bar{x})}{t_{k}}

\displaystyle\leqq\limsup\limits_{\mathop{x\to\bar{x}}\limits_{t\downarrow 0}}\dfrac{F(x+tu)-F(x)}{t}.

Therefore,
F^{\circ}(\bar{x},u)\geqq 0
, as required.

Lemma 2

Suppose that
F\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
is locally Lipschitz at
\bar{x}\in X
. Let
(u,v)
be a vector in
X\times X
and let
\{(t_{k},v^{k})\}
be a sequence converging to
(0^{+},v)
satisfying

F\left(\bar{x}+t_{k}u+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}_{k}v^{k}\right)\geqq F(\bar{x})\ \ \mbox{for all}\ \ k\in\mathbb{N}.

If
F^{\circ}(\bar{x},u)=0
, then
F^{\circ}(\bar{x},v)+F^{\circ\circ}(\bar{x},u)\geqq 0.

Proof

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, put
x^{k}:=\bar{x}+t_{k}u+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}_{k}v^{k}
and
y^{k}:=\bar{x}+t_{k}u+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}_{k}v
. Since
F
is locally Lipschitz at
\bar{x}
and

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}x^{k}=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}y^{k}=\bar{x},

there exist
L\geqq 0
and
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

|F(x^{k})-F\left(y^{k}\right)|\leqq\frac{1}{2}t^{2}_{k}L\|v^{k}-v\|\ \ \text{for all}\ \ k\geqq k_{0}.

Thus,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqq F(x^{k})-F(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=[F(x^{k})-F(y^{k})]+[F(y^{k})-F(\bar{x}+t_{k}u)]

\displaystyle+[F(\bar{x}+t_{k}u)-F(\bar{x})-t_{k}F^{\circ}(\bar{x},u)]

\displaystyle\leqq\frac{1}{2}t^{2}_{k}L\|v^{k}-v\|+[F(y^{k})-F(\bar{x}+t_{k}u)]+[F(\bar{x}+t_{k}u)-F(\bar{x})-t_{k}F^{\circ}(\bar{x},u)]

for all
k\geqq k_{0}
. This implies that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leqq\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}L\|v^{k}-v\|+\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{F(\bar{x}+t_{k}u+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}_{k}v)-F(\bar{x}+t_{k}u)}{\frac{1}{2}t^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle+\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{F(\bar{x}+t_{k}u)-F(\bar{x})-t_{k}F^{\circ}(\bar{x},u)}{\frac{1}{2}t^{2}_{k}}

\displaystyle\leqq\limsup\limits_{\mathop{x\to\bar{x}}\limits_{t\downarrow 0}}\dfrac{F(x+tv)-F(x)}{t}+\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{F(\bar{x}+tu)-F(\bar{x})-tF^{\circ}(\bar{x},u)}{\frac{1}{2}t^{2}}

\displaystyle=F^{\circ}(\bar{x},v)+F^{\circ\circ}(\bar{x},u).

Therefore,
F^{\circ}(\bar{x},v)+F^{\circ\circ}(\bar{x},u)\geqq 0
. The proof is complete.

3
Second-order constraint qualification and regularity condition

From now on, we consider problem (
VP
) under the following assumptions:

\begin{cases}\text{The functions}\ \ f_{i},i\in I,g_{j},j\in J(\bar{x}),\ \ \text{are locally Lipschitz at}\ \ \bar{x},\\
\text{The functions}\ \ g_{j},j\in J\setminus J(\bar{x}),\ \ \text{are continuous at}\ \ \bar{x},\end{cases}

where
\bar{x}
is a feasible point of (
VP
) and
J(\bar{x})
is the
active index set
at
\bar{x}
, that is,

J(\bar{x}):=\{j\in J\,:\,g_{j}(\bar{x})=0\}.

For any vectors
a=(a_{1},a_{2})
and
b=(b_{1},b_{2})
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we denote the lexicographic order by

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\leqq_{\rm lex}b,\ \ {\rm iff}\ \ a_{1}<b_{1}\ \ {\rm or}\ \ (a_{1}=b_{1}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ a_{2}\leqq b_{2}),

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle<_{\rm lex}b,\ \ {\rm iff}\ \ a_{1}<b_{1}\ \ {\rm or}\ \ (a_{1}=b_{1}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ a_{2}<b_{2}).

Let us introduce some notations which are used in the sequel. For each
\bar{x}\in Q_{0}
and
u\in X
, put

\displaystyle Q:=Q_{0}\cap\{x\in X\,:\,f_{i}(x)\leqq f_{i}(\bar{x}),\ \ i\in I\},

\displaystyle J(\bar{x};u):=\{j\in J(\bar{x})\,:\,g_{j}^{\circ}(\bar{x},u)=0\},

\displaystyle I(\bar{x};u):=\{i\in I\,:\,f_{i}^{\circ}(\bar{x},u)=0\}.

We say that
u
is a
critical direction
of (
VP
) at
\bar{x}
iff

\displaystyle f_{i}^{\circ}(\bar{x},u)

\displaystyle\leqq 0,\ \ \ \forall i\in I,

\displaystyle f_{i}^{\circ}(\bar{x},u)

\displaystyle=0,\ \ \ \mbox{at least one}\ \ i\in I,

\displaystyle g_{j}^{\circ}(\bar{x},u)

\displaystyle\leqq 0,\ \ \ \forall j\in J(\bar{x}).

The set of all critical directions of (
VP
) at
\bar{x}
is denoted by
\mathcal{C}(\bar{x})
. Obviously,
0\in\mathcal{C}(\bar{x})
.

We now use the following second-order approximation sets for
Q
and
Q_{0}
to introduce second-order constraint qualifications and regularity condition. For each
\bar{x}\in Q_{0}
and
u\in X
, set

\displaystyle L^{2}(Q;\bar{x},u)

\displaystyle:=\bigg\{v\in X\,:\,F^{2}_{i}(\bar{x};u,v)\leqq_{\rm lex}(0,0),\ \ i\in I

\displaystyle\qquad\ \ \,\,\text{and}\ \ G^{2}_{j}(\bar{x};u,v)\leqq_{\rm lex}(0,0),\ \ j\in J(\bar{x})\bigg\},

\displaystyle L^{2}(Q_{0};\bar{x},u)

\displaystyle:=\bigg\{v\in X\,:\,G^{2}_{j}(\bar{x};u,v)\leqq_{\rm lex}(0,0),\ \ j\in J(\bar{x})\bigg\},

\displaystyle L_{0}^{2}(Q_{0};\bar{x},u)

\displaystyle:=\bigg\{v\in X\,:\,G^{2}_{j}(\bar{x};u,v)<_{\rm lex}(0,0),\ \ j\in J(\bar{x})\bigg\},

\displaystyle A(\bar{x};u)

\displaystyle:=\bigg\{v\in X\,:\,\forall j\in J(\bar{x};u)\,\,\exists\delta_{j}>0\ \ \mbox{with}\ \ g_{j}\bigg(\bar{x}+tu+\frac{1}{2}t^{2}v\bigg)\leqq 0

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\,\forall t\in(0,\delta_{j})\bigg\},

\displaystyle B(\bar{x};u)

\displaystyle:=\bigg\{v\in X\,:\,g_{j}^{\circ}(\bar{x},v)+g_{j}^{\circ\circ}(\bar{x},u)\leqq 0,\ \ \forall j\in J(\bar{x};u)\bigg\},

where

\displaystyle F^{2}_{i}(\bar{x};u,v)

\displaystyle:=\left(f_{i}^{\circ}(\bar{x},u),f_{i}^{\circ}(\bar{x},v)+f_{i}^{\circ\circ}(\bar{x},u)\right),\ \ i\in I,v\in X,

\displaystyle G^{2}_{j}(\bar{x};u,v)

\displaystyle:=\left(g_{j}^{\circ}(\bar{x},u),g_{j}^{\circ}(\bar{x},v)+g_{j}^{\circ\circ}(\bar{x},u)\right),\ \ j\in J(\bar{x}),v\in X.

For brevity, we denote
L(Q;\bar{x}):=L^{2}(Q;\bar{x},0)
. It is easily seen that, for each
u\in\mathcal{C}(\bar{x})
, we have

L_{0}^{2}(Q_{0};\bar{x},u)=\bigg\{v\in X\,:\,g_{j}^{\circ}(\bar{x},v)+g_{j}^{\circ\circ}(\bar{x},u)<0,\ \ j\in J(\bar{x},u)\bigg\}.

Definition 4

Let
\bar{x}\in Q_{0}
and
u\in X
. We say that:

(i)

The
Zangwill second-order constraint qualification
holds at
\bar{x}
for the direction
u
iff

B(\bar{x};u)\subset\text{cl}\,A(\bar{x};u).

(ii)

The
Abadie second-order constraint qualification
holds at
\bar{x}
for the direction
u
iff

L^{2}(Q_{0};\bar{x},u)\subset T^{2}(Q_{0};\bar{x},u).

(iii)

The
Mangasarian–Fromovitz second-order constraint qualification
holds at
\bar{x}
for the direction
u
iff

L_{0}^{2}(Q_{0};\bar{x},u)\neq\emptyset.

(iv)

The
weak Abadie second-order regularity condition
holds at
\bar{x}
for the direction
u
iff

L^{2}(Q;\bar{x},u)\subset T^{2}(Q_{0};\bar{x},u).

The (
i
) type was first introduced by Ivanov
(
Ivanov15
, Definition 3.2)
for
C^{1}
functions. The (
ii
) type was proposed by Aghezzaf and Hachimi for (
VP
) with
C^{2}
data; see
(
Aghezzaf99
, p.40)
. The (
iii
) type was first introduced in
Ben-Tal80
for
C^{2}
scalar optimization problems. The (
iv
) type was used for
C^{1,1}
vector optimization problems in
Huy163
. For problems with only locally Lipschitz active constraints and objective functions, these conditions are new.

Definition 5

Let
\bar{x}\in Q_{0}
. We say that the
Zangwill constraint qualification
(ZCQ)
(resp.,
Abadie constraint qualification
(ACQ)
,
Mangasarian–Fromovitz constraint qualification
(MFCQ)
,
weak Abadie regularity condition

(WARC)
) holds at
\bar{x}
iff the (
i
) (resp., (
ii
), (
iii
), (
iv
)) holds at
\bar{x}
for the direction
0
.

The following result shows that the (
iv
) is weaker than other constraint qualification conditions in Definition
4
.

Proposition 1

Let
\bar{x}\in Q_{0}
and
u\in X
. Then the following implications hold:

(i)

(\,B(\bar{x};u)\subset{\rm cl}\,A(\bar{x};u)\,)

\Rightarrow

(\,L^{2}(Q_{0};\bar{x},u)\subset T^{2}(Q_{0};\bar{x},u)\,)

\Rightarrow

(\,L^{2}(Q;\bar{x},u)\subset T^{2}(Q_{0};\bar{x},u)\,),\,\,i.e,

\eqref{SACQ_1}\Rightarrow\eqref{SACQ_2}\Rightarrow\eqref{SACQ_3}.

(ii)

(\,L_{0}^{2}(Q_{0};\bar{x},u)\neq\emptyset\,)\Rightarrow(\,L^{2}(Q_{0};\bar{x},u)\subset T^{2}(Q_{0};\bar{x},u)\,),\,\,i.e.,

\eqref{MFSCQ}\Rightarrow\eqref{SACQ_2}.

(iii)

(\,L_{0}^{2}(Q_{0};\bar{x},0)\neq\emptyset\,)\,\,\Rightarrow\,(\,L_{0}^{2}(Q_{0};\bar{x},u)\neq\emptyset,\,\,\forall\,u\,\in\mathcal{C}(\bar{x})\,).

Proof

(i) Clearly,
L^{2}(Q;\bar{x},u)\subset L^{2}(Q_{0};\bar{x},u)
. Thus the second implication of (i) is trivial. We now assume that the (
i
) holds at
\bar{x}
for the direction
u\in X
. Fix
v\in L^{2}(Q_{0};\bar{x},u)
. Then,

6
Concluding remarks

In this paper we obtain primal second-order
KKT
necessary conditions for vector optimization problems with inequality constraints in a nonsmooth setting using second-order upper generalized directional derivatives. We suppose that the objective functions and active constraints are only locally Lipschitz. Some second-order constraint qualifications of Zangwill type, Abadie type and Mangasarian-Fromovitz type as well as a regularity condition of Abadie type are proposed. They are applied in the optimality conditions. Our results improve and generalize the corresponding results of Aghezza et al.
(
Aghezzaf99
, Theorem 3.3)
, Gupta et al.
(
Gupta2017
, Theorems 3.1 and 3.3)
, Huy et al.
(
Huy163
, Theorem 3.2)
, Ivanov
(
Ivanov15
, Theorem 4.1)
, Constantin
(
Elena
, Theorem 2)
, Luu
(
Luu17
, Corollary 5.2)
Zhao
(
Zhao15
, Theorem 4.1)
, and Burachik et al.
(
Rizvi12
, Theorem 4.3)
.

To obtain second-order
KKT
necessary conditions in dual form, we need assume that the objective functions and constraint functions are of class
C^{1}(X)
. Then one can follow the scheme of the proof of
(
Aghezzaf99
, Theorem 3.4)
and we leave the details to the reader.

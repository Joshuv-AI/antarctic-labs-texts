D-Branes in Para-Hermitian Geometries
Vincenzo Emilio Marotta, Richard J. Szabo

Abstract
. We introduce T-duality invariant versions of D-branes in doubled geometry using a global covariant framework based on para-Hermitian geometry and metric algebroids. We define D-branes as conformal boundary conditions for the open string version of the Born sigma-model, where they are given by maximally isotropic vector bundles which do not generally admit the standard geometric picture in terms of submanifolds. When reduced to the conventional sigma-model description of a physical string background as the leaf space of a foliated para-Hermitian manifold, integrable branes yield D-branes as leaves of foliations which are interpreted as Dirac structures on the physical spacetime. We define a notion of generalised para-complex D-brane, which realises our D-branes as para-complex versions of topological A/B-branes. We illustrate how our formalism recovers standard D-branes in the explicit example of reductions from doubled nilmanifolds.

Introduction

This paper is concerned with the global descriptions of duality symmetric extended geometries and field theories. We focus on the manifestly T-duality invariant extension of the bosonic sector of type II supergravity to double field theory
[

, which is based on the doubled formalism for string theory

(see e.g.

We work in the setting of para-Hermitian geometry and metric algebroids, which encompasses the standard flat space treatments (recovered as instances of para-Kähler geometry) and known examples of global doubled geometries (typically involving non-integrable almost para-complex structures). This approach was originally proposed by Vaisman
[
6
,
7
]
, and has recently undergone a renewed flurry of activity, see e.g.
[

,

]
for a host of developments in this direction.

Given the pertinence of D-branes to non-perturbative aspects and as probes of short-distance geometry in string theory, as well as their role in gauge theories, we pose the question: What is the proper definition of a D-brane in the context of para-Hermitian geometry? This question has not yet been tackled in full generality, and a detailed answer should lead to a natural incorporation of phenomena described by T-duality.
The purpose of this contribution is to fill this gap. We attack this problem from two complementary perspectives, starting from the worldsheet point of view where D-branes are regarded as boundary conditions for two-dimensional non-linear sigma-models. D-branes in doubled geometry have been previously studied from this perspective on doubled torus bundles
[
26
,
27
,
28
]
, on doubled twisted tori
[
29
,
30
]
and on doubled flat space
[
31
]
; they have also been discussed in the U-duality invariant setting of exceptional target space geometries
[
32
]
.

A para-Hermitian manifold endowed with a Born metric and its compatible metric algebroid can be used to define a doubled string sigma-model in a duality symmetric formulation. In
[
19
]
we called this the ‘Born sigma-model’. The Born sigma-model is invariant under rigid T-duality transformations, and it can be viewed as a covariant extension of the metastring sigma-model into string phase spaces
[
33
]
. In this sense it is a direct generalisation of Hull’s doubled sigma-models into doubled torus bundles with manifest worldsheet covariance
[
26
,
28
]
. In this paper we present a new extension of the Born sigma-model by a Wess-Zumino term which encodes topologically non-trivial generalised NS–NS fluxes of the doubled geometry, thus generalising the doubled sigma-models into doubled twisted tori introduced in
[
34
]
.

The para-Hermitian geometry approach to double field theory is largely a theory of foliated manifolds. The choice of an almost para-complex structure represents a choice of polarisation of the doubled geometry, and a maximally isotropic foliation arises as a solution to the section constraint of double field theory
[
6
]
. Physical sigma-models are then obtained from the Born sigma-model by a Lie algebroid gauging along the foliation, which implements the quotient to the leaf space representing the physical spacetime. A para-Hermitian manifold typically admits different maximally isotropic Riemannian foliations, so that T-dual sigma-models are recovered on the respective leaf spaces. In this way the Born sigma-model unifies T-dual non-linear sigma-models in a geometric description.

In this paper we define the open string version of the Born sigma-model. D-branes are then boundary conditions for this two-dimensional field theory. Boundary conditions and D-branes for the Born sigma-model (as well as their extensions to branes in M-theory and IIB string theory) have also been discussed in a local formulation by
[
20
]
, but only in the case of para-Kähler manifolds (which recovers conventional double field theory on flat space), after solving the section constraint and gauging with respect to a coordinate system adapted to the leaves of a foliation. Our analysis instead generalises the approach of
[
29
]
, which considered D-branes in doubled twisted tori, but also in a local setting and assuming integrability properties which are not implied by the boundary conditions. In the following we improve on these approaches by considering global aspects of D-branes as they arise solely from the boundary data of the Born sigma-model, without recourse to additional structures, which we instead discuss as supplementary input towards the more physical and geometrically intuitive notions of D-branes. We refer to the branes obtained in this way as (almost) ‘Born D-branes’.

From this perspective, D-branes in para-Hermitian geometry are seen as maximally isotropic vector bundles
L
. All branes are of the same rank, so that the physical D-branes of different dimensions are treated in a unified geometric setting. In particular, the splitting between tangent and normal directions, which are treated equally due to the manifest T-duality symmetry, is determined by
L
. However, no integrability condition is needed to solve the boundary conditions for the Born sigma-model, and even when it is imposed by hand, it generally only leads to a foliation of spacetime by the D-brane
L
. Thus these branes do not generally follow the standard geometric picture of a D-brane as a submanifold of spacetime. We discuss this point in some detail, as well as the related problem of how to incorporate D-branes carrying non-trivial gauge flux into this picture, which corresponds to bound states of D-branes of various dimensions. Even the ‘physical’ D-branes, obtained by reducing Born D-branes to the leaf space, are generally only described as leaves of foliations of the physical spacetime.

To understand the geometrical characterisation of Born D-branes, we develop a complementary spacetime perspective on D-branes by exploiting the similarities between para-Hermitian geometry and generalised geometry
[
35
,
36
]
. These similarities are part of the crux of the para-Hermitian approach to doubled geometry, as they underlie the geometry behind the reduction of double field theory to supergravity upon solving the section constraint in a particular polarisation. In particular, the metric algebroids of doubled geometry should reduce to Courant algebroids. The ingredients comprising a Born geometry define an almost para-quaternionic structure, together with compatibility conditions with respect to the underlying metrics
[
13
]
. These define a neutral almost hypercomplex structure, which restricts the type of T-dual string backgrounds that can be described in this framework (see
[
37
]
for a recent discussion).

With these connections in mind, we extend Gualtieri’s approach
[
35
]
to viewing abelian generalised complex D-branes as ‘generalised submanifolds’ into the framework of metric algebroids and almost generalised para-complex structures. The setting provided by a generic metric algebroid turns out to be too coarse to provided any meaningful analysis, hence we restrict to the intermediary structure of a pre-Courant algebroid, which represents a structure along the way between the metric algebroids of double field theory and the Courant algebroids of supergravity arising from imposition of the section constraint
[
9
,
16
,
25
]
. We refer to these branes as ‘generalised para-complex D-branes’. In particular, we show that the Born D-branes on an almost para-Hermitian manifold fit into this picture in a natural way: Any two-dimensional non-linear sigma-model naturally corresponds to an exact Courant algebroid (see e.g.
[
38
,
39
,
40
]
); on an almost para-Hermitian manifold this is called the ‘large Courant algebroid’
[
9
,
16
,
18
,
25
]
. Seen in this way, our D-branes provide natural para-complex versions of the A-branes and B-branes of topological string theory. Moreover, using techniques of Courant algebroid reduction, this perspective yields an interpretation of the reduction of Born D-branes to the leaf space as Dirac structures on the physical spacetime.

We illustrate our formalism explicitly in the example of the standard six-dimensional doubled nilmanifold, which was also studied by
[
29
,
30
]
. We show how to recover the standard D-branes on the three-dimensional Heisenberg nilmanifold, and also on the
3
-torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}
with NS–NS
H
-flux; in particular, our formalism gives a natural geometric explanation of the well-known fact that D3-branes wrapping
\mathbb{T}^{3}
with

are prohibited. It would be interesting to study how D-branes in other contexts are also realised by our framework, for instance the D-branes in Drinfel’d doubles which are related by Poisson-Lie T-duality
[
41
]
, and the interplay between D-branes and non-abelian T-duality
[
42
]
. Both of these latter examples can be viewed as instances of the generalised T-duality in para-Hermitian geometry introduced by
[
19
]
.

In this paper we discuss only bosonic string sigma-models, hence our discussion of D-branes is decoupled from issues of stability and preservation of supersymmetry. At the purely bosonic level, all that is needed is the boundary conformality condition, and indeed our Born D-branes are conformally invariant. It would be interesting to develop supersymmetric extensions of the Born sigma-model, and to discern what new types of target space para-complex structures appear; (generalised) para-complex structures appear on target spaces for topologically twisted and supersymmetric sigma-models
[
43
,
12
,
18
]
, as well as in target spaces for
{\mathcal{N}}=2
hypermultiplets in Euclidean signature
[
44
]
. Another subtle issue we do not address in this paper is the extension to (multiple) non-abelian D-branes. In addition to the technical problems involved in the extension to higher rank gauge bundles, which are no longer intrinsic to the para-Hermitian geometry, it is not clear how to include the non-abelian transverse scalars which should now be sections of the tensor product of the normal bundle to the D-brane and the endomorphism bundle of the gauge bundle. A notion of higher rank generalised complex brane is discussed in
[
45
]
.

The remainder of this paper is structured into three parts. In Section
2
we review the Born sigma-model, extend it by a Wess-Zumino term and to open strings, derive and study properties of Born D-branes as boundary conditions for these two-dimensional field theories, and discuss how they induce D-branes on a leaf space representing a physical spacetime. In Section
3
we provide putative spacetime notions of branes on a metric algebroid, introduce our notion of generalised para-complex D-branes showing how they naturally include the Born D-branes of Section
2
as special instances, discuss how they reduce to the physical spacetime, and finally present explicit examples on the doubled nilmanifold. To make the main messages of this paper clear, we have delegated all mathematical details surrounding para-Hermitian geometry and metric algebroids to Appendix
A
at the end of the paper; the reader unaquainted with these technical details may wish to consult there first before moving to the main body of the paper.

2.
Worldsheet Perspective: D-Branes on Para-Hermitian Manifolds

In this section we discuss string worldsheet sigma-models whose target space is a Born manifold, following and extending the treatment of
[
19
]
. When extended to worldsheets with boundaries, an analysis of the open string boundary conditions leads to a global operational definition of a D-brane on a para-Hermitian manifold, generalizing the considerations and results for D-branes in doubled twisted tori from
[
29
]
. We describe several properties and special instances of our definition of D-brane, and particularly how they match with physical expectations from double field theory.

2.2.
Wess-Zumino Terms from Canonical Metric Algebroids

If we wish to incorporate topologically non-trivial generalised fluxes in the doubled spacetime

The almost para-Hermitian manifold admits a unique canonical compatible D-bracket by Example

\displaystyle H_{\texttt{can}}({\mathsf{p}}_{\pm}(X),

\displaystyle\,{\mathsf{p}}_{\pm}(Y),{\mathsf{p}}_{\pm}(Z))

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\big(\eta([{\mathsf{p}}_{\pm}(X),{\mathsf{p}}_{\pm}(Y)],{\mathsf{p}}_{\pm}(Z))

An almost para-Hermitian manifold

2.3.
Equations of Motion and Boundary Conditions

In order to discuss open strings and D-branes in the context of the Born sigma-model, we assume henceforth that the two-dimensional worldsheet manifold
\Sigma
has a non-empty boundary
\partial\Sigma.
Working throughout at lowest order in string perturbation theory, we take

to be a flat worldsheet with metric

and choose the local coordinates

\mathsf{\Gamma}(\Sigma\times M)\simeq C^{\infty}(\Sigma,M)

\underline{\mathrm{d}\phi}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(T^{*}\Sigma\otimes\phi^{*}TM)

\displaystyle(j^{1}\phi)^{*}\Big(\frac{\partial{\mathcal{L}}}{\partial\mathbb{X}^{I}}-\partial_{\alpha}\frac{\partial{\mathcal{L}}}{\partial\mathbb{X}_{\alpha}^{I}}\Big)=0\ ,

is the Lie derivative along the vector field
\varepsilon\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(T\Sigma)
generating the variation.
We use the Cartan formula
\pounds_{\varepsilon}=\mathrm{d}\circ\iota_{\varepsilon}+\iota_{\varepsilon}\circ\mathrm{d}
, where
\iota_{\varepsilon}
is the interior multiplication of forms by
\varepsilon
, together with Stokes’ theorem. We then obtain the contribution to the boundary conditions as

(2.15)

\delta{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\big\rvert_{\partial\Sigma}=\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,\mathrm{d}\big((\iota_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}\phi^{I})\,\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}_{IJ}\star\mathrm{d}\phi^{J}\big)=\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\partial\Sigma}\,(\iota_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}\phi^{I})\,\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}_{IJ}\star\mathrm{d}\phi^{J}\ .

The variation of the topological term (
2.5
) is similarly given by

(2.16)

\delta{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm top}=\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,\pounds_{\varepsilon}\,\bar{\omega}=\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,\iota_{\varepsilon}\,\bar{{\mathcal{K}}}+\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,\mathrm{d}\,\iota_{\varepsilon}\,\bar{\omega}\ .

The first term in the second equality of (
2.16
) contributes to the equations of motion, while the second term appears in the boundary conditions after using Stokes’ theorem. This gives

(2.17)

\delta{\mathcal{S}}_{\rm top}\big\rvert_{\partial\Sigma}=\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\partial\Sigma}\,\iota_{\varepsilon}\,\bar{\omega}\ .

By adding the two variations (
2.15
) and (
2.17
) together, and demanding that
\delta{\mathcal{S}}\big|_{\partial\Sigma}=0
for arbitrary variational vector fields
\varepsilon\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(T\Sigma)
, we obtain the boundary conditions (
2.14
) in the more canonical form

(2.18)

\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}_{IJ}\,\partial_{\sigma}\phi^{J}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma+\bar{\omega}_{IJ}\,\partial_{\tau}\phi^{J}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\big)\big\rvert_{\partial\Sigma}=0\ .

This generalises the boundary conditions which appeared in the approach of
[
29
]
to the doubled sigma-model for doubled twisted tori, and in
[
20
]
for the Born sigma-model. From (
2.18
) it follows that

(2.19)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\phi^{I}\,\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}_{IJ}\,\partial_{\sigma}\phi^{J}\big\rvert_{\partial\Sigma}=0\ .

The left-hand side is the restriction to
\partial\Sigma
of the component of the worldsheet energy-momentum tensor
T_{\tau\sigma}
derived from (
2.7
), and (
2.19
) implies that it is conserved. In other words, the boundary conditions (
2.18
) preserve conformal invariance of the Born sigma-model.

2.4.
D-Branes for Born Sigma-Models

Analysing the conformal boundary conditions (
2.18
) leads to a definition of D-branes for the Born sigma-model, generalizing the approaches of
[
29
,
20
]
.
For this, we require the solution of the equations of motion to satisfy the self-duality constraint
[
19
,
26
,
28
,
34
]

(2.20)

\mathrm{d}\phi=\bar{\eta}^{\sharp}\circ\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\flat}(\star\,\mathrm{d}\phi)\ ,

where
\bar{\eta}^{\sharp}=\phi^{*}(\eta^{\sharp})
and
\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\flat}=\phi^{*}({\mathcal{H}}^{\flat})
are the pullbacks of the musical isomorphisms induced by
\eta
and
{\mathcal{H}}
, respectively. This constraint arises from the Born sigma-model with a Lie algebroid gauging that reduces it to the ordinary non-linear sigma-model on a ‘physical’ target space
[
19
]
. It enables us to provide solutions of the boundary conditions with a geometric interpretation. Written in this way,
the Born sigma-model (
2.7
) is an immediate generalisation of the sigma-models
for doubled torus fibrations that were introduced
in
[
28
]
.

A solution of (
2.18
) is given by a distribution
L\subset TM.
To characterise the distribution
L
, we consider the short exact sequence of vector bundles on
M
given by

0\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow TM\longrightarrow\nu(L)\longrightarrow 0\ ,

where
\nu(L)=TM/L
is the normal bundle of
L.
We then choose an orthogonal splitting
TM=L\oplus L^{\perp}
with respect to the generalised metric
{\mathcal{H}}
. This determines orthogonal projectors
\Pi\colon TM\longrightarrow L
and
\Pi^{\perp}\colon TM\longrightarrow L^{\perp}.

In the local form (
2.3
) for
\mathrm{d}\phi,
the boundary conditions (
2.18
) in the splitting
TM=L\oplus L^{\perp}
are solved by setting

(2.21)

\displaystyle\phi^{*}\big(\Pi^{\perp}(Z_{I})\big)\otimes\partial_{\tau}\phi^{I}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=0\ ,

(2.22)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\,\phi^{*}\big({\mathcal{H}}(\Pi(Z_{I}),Z_{J})\big)\,\partial_{\sigma}\phi^{J}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\phi^{*}\big(\omega(\Pi(Z_{I}),Z_{J})\big)\,\partial_{\tau}\phi^{J}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\ .

On the other hand, the local form of the self-duality constraint (
2.20
) is given by

(2.23)

\displaystyle\bar{Z}_{I}\otimes\partial_{\tau}\phi^{I}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\bar{\eta}^{\sharp}\circ\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\flat}(\bar{Z}_{J})\otimes\star\,\partial_{\sigma}\phi^{J}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\ ,

(2.24)

\displaystyle\bar{Z}_{I}\otimes\partial_{\sigma}\phi^{I}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

\displaystyle=\bar{\eta}^{\sharp}\circ\bar{{\mathcal{H}}}^{\flat}(\bar{Z}_{J})\otimes\star\,\partial_{\tau}\phi^{J}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\ .

By combining (
2.18
), (
2.22
) and (
2.24
) we obtain

\phi^{*}\big(-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\eta(\Pi(Z_{I}),\Pi(Z_{J}))+\omega(\Pi(Z_{I}),\Pi(Z_{J}))\big)\,\partial_{\tau}\phi^{J}\,\mathrm{d}\tau=0\ ,

which is solved by taking

\eta\big(\Pi(Z_{I}),\Pi(Z_{J})\big)=0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\omega\big(\Pi(Z_{I}),\Pi(Z_{J})\big)=0\ .

These equations imply that the distribution
L
must be isotropic with respect to both
\eta
and
\omega
in order to solve the boundary conditions (
2.18
).

By substituting (
2.23
) into (
2.21
) we obtain

\phi^{*}\big(\Pi^{\perp}(\eta^{\sharp}\circ{\mathcal{H}}^{\flat}(Z_{I}))\big)\otimes\partial_{\sigma}\phi^{I}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma=0\ .

This equation together with (
2.22
) gives

\eta\big(\Pi^{\perp}(Z_{I}),\Pi^{\perp}(Z_{J})\big)=0\ ,

where we used the isotropy of
L
with respect to
\omega
as well. It follows that
L^{\perp}
is isotropic with respect to
\eta
, and that the ranks of both
L
and
L^{\perp}
are maximal, i.e.
{\rm{rank}}(L)={\rm{rank}}(L^{\perp})=d.
Thus there are equal numbers of Neumann boundary conditions, given by (
2.22
), and Dirichlet boundary conditions, given by (
2.21
), as necessitated by T-duality. These isotropy conditions are consistent with conformal invariance: Substituting the self-duality constraints (
2.23
) and (
2.24
) into (
2.19
), and using the relation between the metrics
\eta
and
{\mathcal{H}}
from Example
A.8
, we find

\displaystyle\partial_{\sigma}\phi^{I}\,\bar{\eta}_{IJ}\,\partial_{\sigma}\phi^{J}\big|_{\partial\Sigma}=0\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\partial_{\tau}\phi^{I}\,\bar{\eta}_{IJ}\,\partial_{\tau}\phi^{J}\big|_{\partial\Sigma}=0\ ,

which shows that the restrictions of the sections
(j^{1}\phi)^{*}(\mathbb{X}_{\sigma})
and
(j^{1}\phi)^{*}(\mathbb{X}_{\tau})
to the boundary have images belonging to maximally isotropic sub-bundles of
\phi^{*}TM
.

Definition 2.25
.

A
D-brane for a Born sigma-model
, or
almost Born D-brane
for short, is a maximally isotropic distribution
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}\subset TM
which is preserved by the almost para-complex structure
K
, i.e.
K(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}})=L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}.
A
Born D-brane
is an almost Born D-brane
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
which is involutive, i.e.
[{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}),{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}})]\subseteq{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}})
.

Remark 2.26
.

Definition
2.25
implies that
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}\subset TM
is a sub-bundle of constant rank, and moreover that
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
is maximally isotropic with respect to both the split signature metric
\eta
and the fundamental 2-form
\omega
, because of the invariance condition with respect to
K.

Example 2.27
.

Let
(M,K,\eta)
be any almost para-Hermitian manifold. Then the eigenbundles
L_{\pm}
of the almost para-complex structure
K\in{\sf Aut}_{\mathds{1}}(TM)
are always almost Born D-branes.

2.5.
Lagrangian Subspaces and Linear D-Branes

To work towards a better understanding of the general structure of a Born D-brane, we will first unravel the meaning of Definition
2.25
in the simple finite-dimensional setting where the target para-Hermitian vector bundle is the generalised tangent bundle of a point (see Example
A.4
). We start by recalling the general construction of Lagrangian subspaces of
\mathbb{V}=V\oplus V^{*},
where
V
is a
d
-dimensional real vector space and
V^{*}
is its linear dual.

In our framework,
(\mathbb{V},K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,})
is a para-Hermitian vector space with para-complex structure
K\in{\sf{Aut}}(\mathbb{V})
defined by
K|_{V}=\mathds{1}_{V}
and
K|_{V^{*}}=-\mathds{1}_{V^{*}}
, and split signature inner product induced by the duality pairing

\braket{v+\alpha,w+\beta}=\iota_{v}\beta+\iota_{w}\alpha\ ,

where
v,w\in V
and
\alpha,\beta\in V^{*}
.
We denote by
{\rm pr}_{V}:\mathbb{V}\longrightarrow V
the projection onto
V,
i.e.
{\rm pr}_{V}(v+\alpha)=v
for all
v+\alpha\in\mathbb{V}.

Remark 2.28
.

By construction,
K
and
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}
satisfy the compatibility condition

\braket{K(v+\alpha),K(w+\beta)}:=-\braket{v+\alpha,w+\beta}\ ,

for all
v+\alpha,\,w+\beta\in\mathbb{V}
.
Thus there is a canonical 2-form on
\mathbb{V}
defined by

\omega(v+\alpha,w+\beta):=\braket{K(v+\alpha),w+\beta}=\iota_{v}\beta-\iota_{w}\alpha\ .

Definition 2.29
.

A
Lagrangian subspace
of
(\mathbb{V},K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,})
is a maximally isotropic vector subspace
L
of
\mathbb{V}
with respect to the inner product
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}.
We denote by
{\sf Lag}(\mathbb{V})
the set of all Lagrangian subspaces of
\mathbb{V}
.

Notice that
{\sf Lag}(\mathbb{V})
is non-empty, as
V,V^{*}\in{\sf{Lag}}(\mathbb{V}).

We now describe the construction of all Lagrangian subspaces. Let
W\subseteq V
be a vector subspace of
V
and
\Omega\in\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}\,W^{*}
a 2-form on
W
. Let
{\sf Ann}(W)\subseteq V^{*}
be the annihilator subspace of
W
in
V^{*}
, i.e. the set of all
\alpha\in V^{*}
such that
\iota_{w}\alpha=0
for all
w\in W
. Then define

(2.30)

L(W,\Omega)\coloneqq\{v+\iota_{v}\tilde{\Omega}+\alpha\in\mathbb{V}\ |\ v\in W\ ,\ \alpha\in{\sf{Ann}}(W)\}\ \subset\ \mathbb{V}

where
\tilde{\Omega}\in\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}\,V^{*}
is any extension of
\Omega,
i.e.
\tilde{\Omega}\rvert_{W}=\Omega,
or equivalently
\iota_{w}\iota_{v}\tilde{\Omega}=\iota_{w}\iota_{v}\Omega
for all
v,w\in W.
We have the well-known result of

Proposition 2.31
.

L(W,\Omega)\subset\mathbb{V}
is a Lagrangian subspace of
\mathbb{V}
which is independent of the choice of extension of
\Omega.

We can show that any Lagrangian subspace of
\mathbb{V}
has the form
L(W,\Omega)
through

Theorem 2.32
.

For any
L\in\sf{Lag}(\mathbb{V})
, define the vector subspace
W\coloneqq\mathrm{pr}_{V}(L)\subseteq V
and the 2-form
\Omega\in\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}\,W^{*}
by
\Omega(v,w)\coloneqq\iota_{w}\alpha=-\iota_{v}\beta,
for all

v+\alpha,w+\beta\in L.
Then
L=L(W,\Omega)
.

Proof.

Since
W\coloneqq\mathrm{pr}_{V}(L),
it follows that
\ker\big(\mathrm{pr}_{V}\big\rvert_{L}\big)={\sf{Ann}}(W).
Thus there is a short exact sequence of vector spaces given by

(2.33)

0\longrightarrow{{\sf{Ann}}(W)}\xlongrightarrow{\ i\ }L\xlongrightarrow{\mathrm{pr}_{V}}W\longrightarrow 0\ ,

where
i
is the subspace inclusion.
Let
s:W\longrightarrow L
be a splitting of (
2.33
). Then
{\rm{im}}(s)=\{v+A(v)\in L\ |\ v\in W\}
for a linear map
A:W\longrightarrow V^{*}.

Therefore

L={\rm{im}}(s)\oplus{\sf{Ann}}(W)=\{v+A(v)+\alpha\in\mathbb{V}\ |\ v\in W\ ,\ \alpha\in{\sf{Ann}}(W)\}\ .

Since
L
is both isotropic and coisotropic with respect to
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}
, it follows that

0=\braket{v+A(v)+\alpha,w+A(w)+\beta}=\iota_{v}A(w)+\iota_{w}A(v)\ .

In other words, the map
W\longrightarrow W^{*}
given by
v\longmapsto A(v)\rvert_{W}
is skew-symmetric and so determines a 2-form
\Omega
on
W
such that
\iota_{v}\Omega\coloneqq A(v)\rvert_{W}.

Let
\tilde{\Omega}\in\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}\,V^{*}
be any extension of
\Omega
and define

\gamma_{v}\coloneqq A(v)-\iota_{v}\tilde{\Omega}\ \in\ {\sf{Ann}}(W)\ ,

for all
v\in W
.
Then any element
v+A(v)+\alpha\in L
can be written as

v+A(v)+\alpha=v+\iota_{v}\tilde{\Omega}+\gamma_{v}+\alpha=v+\iota_{v}\tilde{\Omega}+\alpha^{\prime}{}

where
\alpha^{\prime}{}=\alpha+\gamma_{v}\in{\sf{Ann}}(W).
Hence
L=L(W,\Omega).

∎

With these preliminary considerations we can now formulate precisely what is meant by a D-brane in this setting.

Definition 2.34
.

A
linear D-brane
in the para-Hermitian vector space
(\mathbb{V},K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,})
is a Lagrangian subspace
L
which is preserved by the para-complex structure
K\in{\sf{Aut}}(\mathbb{V}),
i.e.
K(L)=L.

Remark 2.35
.

The invariance condition on
L
in Definition
2.34
with respect to
K
, as well as its Lagrangian condition, together imply that
L
is also maximally isotropic with respect to the 2-form
\omega
introduced in Remark
2.28
.

Proposition 2.36
.

Let
L
be a linear D-brane in
(\mathbb{V},K,\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,})
, and let
W\subseteq V
be the vector subspace
W:={\rm pr}_{V}(L)
. Then
L=W\oplus{\sf{Ann}}(W).

Proof.

Since
L\in{\sf Lag}(\mathbb{V})
, by Theorem
2.32
it follows that
L=L(W,\Omega)
. We show that
\Omega=0
.
For any
v+\iota_{v}\tilde{\Omega}+\alpha,\,w+\iota_{w}\tilde{\Omega}+\beta\in L(W,\Omega)
, we compute

\displaystyle 0=\omega(v+\iota_{v}\tilde{\Omega}+\alpha,w+\iota_{w}\tilde{\Omega}+\beta)

\displaystyle=\braket{K(v+\iota_{v}\tilde{\Omega}+\alpha),w+\iota_{w}\tilde{\Omega}+\beta}

\displaystyle=\braket{v-\iota_{v}\tilde{\Omega}-\alpha,w+\iota_{w}\tilde{\Omega}+\beta}

\displaystyle=2\,\iota_{v}\iota_{w}\tilde{\Omega}\ .

Thus
\iota_{v}\iota_{w}\Omega=0,
for all
v,w\in W.

∎

2.6.
General Structure of Born D-Branes

We can now describe the general structure of a Born D-brane, beyond the simplest cases provided by Example
2.27
, by applying the linear algebra developed in Section
2.5
fibrewise. Let
(M,K,\eta)
be an almost para-Hermitian manifold. Recall that
TM=L_{+}\oplus L_{-}
, where the eigenbundles
L_{\pm}
of the almost para-complex structure
K
are maximally isotropic sub-bundles of
TM
with respect to
\eta.
Denote by
{\mathsf{p}}_{\pm}:TM\longrightarrow L_{\pm}
the projections onto
L_{\pm}\subset TM.
If
L\subset TM
is any sub-bundle of constant rank, then

W_{L}\coloneqq{\mathsf{p}}_{+}(L)=L_{+}\cap L

is a vector sub-bundle of
L_{+}.
We denote by

\overline{W}{\!}_{L}\coloneqq\eta^{\sharp}\big({\sf{Ann}}(W_{L})\big)

the image in
L_{-}
of the annihilator sub-bundle
{\sf{Ann}}(W_{L})\subset T^{*}M
under the musical isomorphism
\eta^{\sharp}:T^{*}M\longrightarrow TM
induced by the split signature metric
\eta.

Lemma 2.37
.

Let
(M,K,\eta)
be an almost para-Hermitian manifold and
L\subseteq TM
a maximally isotropic sub-bundle such that
W_{L}\coloneqq{\mathsf{p}}_{+}(L)
has constant rank. Then
L
can be expressed in the form

L=\big\{e+\bar{\Omega}(e)+\bar{\alpha}\ \big|\ e\in W_{L}\ ,\ \bar{\alpha}\in\overline{W}\!_{L}\big\}

where
\bar{\Omega}:W_{L}\longrightarrow L_{-}
is a vector bundle morphism covering the identity whose restriction to
W_{L}
induces a 2-form
\Omega\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}\,W_{L}^{*}).

Proof.

Since
L
is maximally isotropic, the restriction
\bar{\Omega}\rvert_{W_{L}}:W_{L}\longrightarrow\overline{W}\!_{L}
is fibrewise skew-symmetric in the sense that
\Omega\coloneqq\eta^{\flat}\circ\bar{\Omega}\rvert_{W_{L}}\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}W^{*}_{L})
.
The result now follows from Theorem
2.32
via the fibrewise identification
TM={L_{+}}\oplus{L_{-}}\simeq{L_{+}}\oplus L_{+}^{*}
by using the metric
\eta.

∎

Proposition 2.38
.

Let
(M,K,\eta)
be an almost para-Hermitian manifold and
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}\subset TM
an almost Born D-brane such that
W_{\textrm{\tiny D}}:=W_{L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}}={\mathsf{p}}_{+}(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}})
has constant rank. Then

L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}=W_{{\textrm{\tiny D}}}\,\oplus\,\overline{W}\!_{{\textrm{\tiny D}}}\ .

Proof.

This is a straightforward consequence of Proposition
2.36
and Lemma
2.37
.
∎

Remark 2.39

(
Worldvolumes).

An almost Born D-brane
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
gives a general solution to the boundary conditions for the Born sigma-model, which suffices for most purposes. However, it does not necessarily admit a geometric interpretation as a brane wrapping a submanifold of the target manifold
M
. This is reminiscent of the algebraic definition of D-branes as boundary states in abstract conformal field theory
[
46
]
, which in some cases also do not admit target space interpretations as open strings ending on worldvolumes, yet they are consistent boundary conditions for the two-dimensional field theory.

The involutivity condition for a Born D-brane ensures that
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
is Frobenius integrable, so that
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}\simeq T{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
induces a foliation
{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
of the Born manifold
M.
A leaf of the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
is then interpreted physically as providing a geometric picture of a ‘D-brane worldvolume’, whose dimension is
{\rm rank}(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}})=d
. The inclusion of all leaves accounts for the moduli of D-branes, i.e. the transverse displacements of the worldvolume in spacetime, as is necessary in any T-duality invariant formulation of D-branes. This is analogous to the considerations of certain D-branes as (singular) foliations in generalised geometry, which appears in e.g.
[
47
,
48
]
. Locally, the integrability condition reads
[\Pi(Z_{I}),\Pi(Z_{J})]\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}})
, which generalises the analogous condition in
[
29
]
.

Remark 2.40

(
Geometry).

An almost Born D-brane
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
naturally inherits a metric from the target para-Hermitian manifold. The Born metric
{\mathcal{H}}
given by (
A.13
) is determined by a metric
g_{+}
on the eigenbundle
L_{+}
, which induces a metric on
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
. Assume as previously that
W_{\textrm{\tiny D}}=L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}\cap L_{+}
has constant rank. Then
W_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
admits the restriction

g_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
of the metric
g_{+}
and
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}=W_{\textrm{\tiny D}}\oplus\overline{W}\!_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
admits the metric

\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{\tiny D}}=\bigg(\begin{matrix}g_{\textrm{\tiny D}}&0\\
0&\eta^{\flat}(g_{\textrm{\tiny D}}^{-1})\end{matrix}\bigg)\ ,

where we note that the ranks of
W_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
and
\overline{W}\!_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
are not generally equal.

Remark 2.41

(
Gauge Fields).

In the quantum theory, the massless states of the open string sigma-model should introduce gauge fields on the D-brane, but in the general setting of Definition
2.25
this is not so straightforward to describe geometrically.
In the case of a Born D-brane
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
with induced foliation
{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
, a field strength
F
can be introduced by applying a
B_{+}
-transformation, as discussed in Appendix
A.3
(a similar approach to incorporating gauge flux appears in the local approach of
[
20
]
in the case of para-Kähler manifolds). Let
B_{+}\colon L_{+}\longrightarrow L_{-}
be a vector bundle morphism over the identity with induced
2
-form
b_{+}\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(\text{\Large$\wedge$}^{2}L_{+}^{*})
. The pullback of a D-brane
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}^{B_{+}}=e^{-B_{+}}(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}})
by the
B_{+}
-transformation is preserved by the pullback of the almost para-complex structure
K_{B_{+}}=e^{-B_{+}}\circ K\circ e^{B_{+}},
but not by
K
itself. In this new polarisation, the D-brane
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
acquires a non-vanishing
2
-form
-\frac{1}{2}\,\omega_{B_{+}}|_{L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}}=b_{+}|_{L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}}
, induced by the map
\bar{\Omega}=B_{+}|_{L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}}
in Lemma
2.37
.

By considering an embedded leaf
{\mathcal{W}}_{\textrm{\tiny D}}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow M
of
{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{\tiny D}},
the 2-form
F\in{\mathsf{\Omega}}^{2}({\mathcal{W}}_{\textrm{\tiny D}})
is then given by

F\coloneqq b_{+}\rvert_{{\mathcal{W}}_{\textrm{\tiny D}}}\ .

If
B_{+}
induces a closed 2-form
b_{+}
on
L_{+}
whose restriction to
{\mathcal{W}}_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
has integer periods, then by Chern-Weil theory
F
is the curvature
2
-form of a connection
\nabla^{C}
on a complex line bundle
C\longrightarrow{\mathcal{W}}_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
, which is interpreted physically as providing a geometric picture of a ‘Chan-Paton bundle’ on the D-brane. Note that despite the requirement
\mathrm{d}b_{+}=0,
the sub-bundle
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}^{B_{+}}\subset TM
still might not be integrable.

In an analogous way, one can introduce the ‘transverse scalar fields’ to a Born D-brane
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
, which is given by a section
\varrho
of the maximally isotropic normal bundle
\nu(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}})\simeq L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}^{\perp}
. Then the pullback
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}^{\varrho}=f_{\varrho}^{*}(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}})
by the diffeormorphism
f_{\varrho}:M\longrightarrow M
generated by the vector field
\varrho
is preserved by the corresponding pullback of the almost para-complex structure
K_{\varrho}=f_{\varrho}^{*}\circ K\circ f_{\varrho}^{*}{}^{-1}
.

Remark 2.42

(
T-Duality).

We can describe how an almost Born D-brane transforms under the
generalised T-duality
discussed by
[
19
]
. These transformations form the subgroup
{\sf O}(TM)\subset{\sf Aut}(TM)
of tangent bundle automorphisms of the almost para-Hermitian manifold
(M,K,\eta)
which preserve the split signature metric
\eta
; its elements are metric-preserving pairs
\vartheta=(\bar{f},f)
of a vector bundle isomorphism
\bar{f}:TM\longrightarrow TM
covering a diffeomorphism
f:M\longrightarrow M
. Examples include isometric diffeomorphisms
f:M\longrightarrow M
, for which
\bar{f}=f^{*}
, and
B_{+}
-transformations, which cover the identity. The natural group of discrete transformations is

\displaystyle{\sf O}(TM;\mathbb{Z}):={\sf O}(TM)\,\cap\,{\sf Diff}(M;\mathbb{Z})\ ,

where
{\sf Diff}(M;\mathbb{Z})\subset{\sf Diff}(M)
is the subgroup of large diffeomorphisms of
M
. This generalises the usual T-duality group of torus bundles.

A generalised T-duality
\vartheta=(\bar{f},f)
pulls back a Born geometry
(K,\eta,{\mathcal{H}})
on
M
to the Born geometry
(K_{\vartheta},\eta,{\mathcal{H}}_{\vartheta})=(\bar{f}\circ K\circ\bar{f}^{-1},\eta,\bar{f}^{*}{\mathcal{H}})
. A D-brane
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}\subset TM
for the Born sigma-model
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{H}},\omega)
into
(M,K,\eta)
is then pulled back to the D-brane
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}^{\vartheta}=\bar{f}(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}})
for the Born sigma-model
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\vartheta},\omega_{\vartheta})
into
(M,K_{\vartheta},\eta)
; indeed, if
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
is maximally isotropic then so is
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}^{\vartheta}
(as
\vartheta
is an isometry of
\eta
), while
K(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}})=L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
implies
K_{\vartheta}(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}^{\vartheta})=L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}^{\vartheta}
.

2.7.
Lagrangian Born D-Branes

A particular instance that naturally leads to a notion of ‘worldvolume’ for an almost Born D-brane is when one wishes to consider D-branes in topologically non-trivial generalised flux backgrounds. When the worldsheet
\Sigma
has a non-empty boundary
\partial\Sigma
, both the notion of admissibility from Definition
2.10
and the definition of the Wess-Zumino term (
2.11
) require modification. Following
[
49
]
, in this case we should reformulate the Born sigma-model as a theory of
relative maps

\phi:(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)\longrightarrow(M,{\mathcal{W}})\ ,

where
{\mathcal{W}}\subset M
is a given fixed submanifold such that
\phi(\partial\Sigma)\subset{\mathcal{W}}
.

We now assume that the relative degree
2
homology of
(M,{\mathcal{W}})
is trivial,
{\sf H}_{2}(M,{\mathcal{W}})=0
, and that there exists a
2
-form
B_{\texttt{can}}
on
{\mathcal{W}}
such that the pair
(H_{\tt can},B_{\tt can})
defines an integer relative cohomology class
\frac{1}{4\pi}\,[(H_{\tt can},B_{\tt can})]\in{\sf H}^{3}(M,{\mathcal{W}};\mathbb{Z})
. The former assumption ensures that the image chain
\phi(\Sigma)
is a relative boundary modulo
{\mathcal{W}}
for all relative maps
\phi:(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)\longrightarrow(M,{\mathcal{W}})
. The latter condition implies, in particular, that the canoncial
3
-form
H_{\tt can}
is again closed and in addition that its restriction to
{\mathcal{W}}
obeys

(2.43)

\displaystyle i^{*}H_{\tt can}=\mathrm{d}B_{\tt can}\ ,

where
i:{\mathcal{W}}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow M
is the embedding of
{\mathcal{W}}
in
M
.

We can subsequently modify (
2.11
) to the
relative
Wess-Zumino action functional defined by
[
49
]

(2.44)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\textrm{\tiny WZ}}[\phi]=\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{V}\,\phi^{*}H_{\tt can}-\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\Delta}\,\phi^{*}B_{\tt can}\ ,

where now
V
is a three-manifold with boundary
\partial V=\Sigma+\Delta
such that
\phi(\Delta)\subset{\mathcal{W}}
, and as before we have smoothly extended the relative map
\phi
to
V
. By virtue of (
2.43
), the canonical
3
-form contributes to the equations of motion (
2.13
) by shifting the curvature
{\mathcal{K}}=\mathrm{d}\omega
to
{\mathcal{K}}+H_{\tt can}
, whereas the
2
-form
B_{\tt can}
only contributes to the boundary conditions (
2.18
) by shifting the fundamental
2
-form
\omega
to
\omega+B_{\tt can}
. Indeed, using
\mathrm{d}H_{\tt can}=0
and Stokes’ theorem, the variation of (
2.44
) is computed to be

\displaystyle\delta{\mathcal{S}}_{\textrm{\tiny WZ}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{V}\,\pounds_{\varepsilon}\bar{H}_{\tt can}-\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\Delta}\,\pounds_{\varepsilon}\bar{B}_{\tt can}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\partial V}\,\iota_{\varepsilon}\bar{H}_{\tt can}-\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\Delta}\,\iota_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}\bar{B}_{\tt can}-\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\partial\Delta}\,\iota_{\varepsilon}\bar{B}_{\tt can}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\Sigma}\iota_{\varepsilon}\bar{H}_{\tt can}+\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\partial\Sigma}\,\iota_{\varepsilon}\bar{B}_{\tt can}\ ,

where in the last step we used
\bar{H}_{\tt can}=\mathrm{d}\bar{B}_{\tt can}
on
\Delta
and
\partial\Delta=-\partial\Sigma
. The relative Dirac quantisation condition on
[(H_{\tt can},B_{\tt can})]
guarantees that the functional integral is independent of the choice of pair
(V,\Delta)
.

The addition of the Wess-Zumino term (
2.44
) also does not affect the self-duality constraint (
2.20
), so that the analysis of the boundary conditions (
2.18
) proceeds exactly as before by replacing the fundamental
2
-form everywhere with
\omega+B_{\tt can}
. As
B_{\tt can}
is defined only on
{\mathcal{W}}\subset M
, in this case it is natural to choose the distribution

\displaystyle L_{{\mathcal{W}}}:={\rm im}(\mathrm{d}i)\ ,

where
\mathrm{d}i:T{\mathcal{W}}\longrightarrow TM
is the derivative of the embedding
i:{\mathcal{W}}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow M
. Demanding that
L_{\mathcal{W}}
be an almost Born D-brane in the sense of Definition
2.25
then means that
{\mathcal{W}}
is a
Lagrangian submanifold
of
M
with respect to
\eta
, such that
K(L_{\mathcal{W}})=L_{\mathcal{W}}
, and moreover (by Remark
2.26
) that

B_{\tt can}(\Pi(Z_{I}),\Pi(Z_{J}))=0.

Since
B_{\tt can}
is only defined on
{\mathcal{W}}
, it follows that
B_{\tt can}=0
, and hence
i^{*}H_{\tt can}=0
by (
2.43
), or equivalently

(2.45)

\displaystyle H_{\tt can}\big(\Pi(Z_{I}),\Pi(Z_{J}),\Pi(Z_{K})\big)=0\ .

This is a generalisation of the ‘orientation’ condition of
[
29
]
for D-branes in doubled twisted tori.

The middle-dimensional submanifold
{\mathcal{W}}\subset M
is regarded as the worldvolume of the almost Born D-brane
L_{\mathcal{W}}\subset TM
, which we call a
Lagrangian Born D-brane
in this case. It is the analogue in para-Hermitian geometry of the conventional A-branes (D-branes of the topological A-model) which are supported on Lagrangian submanifolds of a complex symplectic manifold
[
50
,
51
]
. The condition (
2.45
) forbids Lagrangian Born D-branes whose worldvolumes support non-zero generalised fluxes. Note that this is a stronger requirement than the vanishing of the Freed-Witten anomaly
[
52
]
, which would only require the canonical
3
-form
H_{\tt can}
to become topologically trivial when restricted to
{\mathcal{W}}
, as in (
2.43
).

One virtue of dealing with Lagrangian Born D-branes is that it is straightforward to couple them to gauge fields through the introduction of Chan-Paton factors in the Born sigma-model. For this, let
C
be a complex line bundle on the submanifold
{\mathcal{W}}\subset M
endowed with a unitary connection
\nabla^{C}
whose curvature
2
-form is denoted
F
. Locally,
F=\mathrm{d}A
where
A
is the (local) connection
1
-form characterising
\nabla^{C}
. The string endpoint, which propagates on the boundary
\partial\Sigma
of the worldsheet, is charged with respect to the gauge field
A
on the brane. Since
\phi(\partial\Sigma)\subset{\mathcal{W}}
, its incorporation into the Born sigma-model is achieved by adding the minimal coupling term

(2.46)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\textrm{\tiny CP}}[\phi]:=\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\partial\Sigma}\,\phi^{*}A=-\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\partial\Delta}\,\phi^{*}A=-\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\Delta}\,\phi^{*}F\ ,

where we used
\partial\Sigma=-\partial\Delta
and Stokes’ theorem.

Combining (
2.46
) with (
2.44
) shows that the overall effect is to shift the
2
-form
B_{\tt can}
to
B_{\tt can}+F
, which is also defined only on
{\mathcal{W}}
. Repeating the arguments above shows that now

(2.47)

\displaystyle F+B_{\tt can}=0\ .

This implies that the
2
-form
B_{\tt can}
defines an integer cohomology class
\frac{1}{2\pi}\,[B_{\tt can}]\in{\sf H}^{2}({\mathcal{W}};\mathbb{Z})
, and so (
2.43
) again leads to the vanishing flux constraint (
2.45
). The condition (
2.47
) means that Lagrangian Born D-branes can only couple to
projectively flat
connections on
{\mathcal{W}}
. If we choose to set
B_{\tt can}=0
(which we may do as the role of
B_{\tt can}
is superfluous at this stage), then this is again analogous to the case of Lagrangian A-branes, which necessarily come with flat Chan-Paton bundles
[
50
,
51
]
.

2.8.
D-Branes on the Leaf Space

We shall now discuss how Definition
2.25
induces the canonical notion of a D-brane for the “physical” non-linear sigma-model obtained with the reduction procedure discussed in
[
19
]
.

Let
(M,K,\eta)
be an almost para-Hermitian manifold with a Born metric
{\mathcal{H}}
such that the eigenbundle
L_{-}
of
K
is integrable, i.e.
L_{-}=T{\mathcal{F}}
where
{\mathcal{F}}
is the induced foliation. We further assume that the leaf space
{\mathcal{Q}}=M/{\mathcal{F}}
is a smooth manifold. Let
q:M\longrightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}
be the quotient map, which is covered by its derivative
\mathrm{d}q:TM\longrightarrow T{\mathcal{Q}}
. In the splitting
TM=L_{+}\oplus L_{-}
induced by
K,
the vector bundle morphism
\mathrm{d}q
is fibrewise bijective when restricted to
L_{+},
i.e.
\mathrm{d}q\rvert_{L_{+}}:L_{+}\longrightarrow T{\mathcal{Q}}
is a fibrewise isomorphism. Hence the
C^{\infty}(M)
-module
{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(L_{+})
is isomorphic to the
C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}})
-module
{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(T{\mathcal{Q}}).

We assume that the Riemannian metric
{\mathcal{H}}
is
bundle-like
with respect to the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
, that is,

\displaystyle\pounds_{{\mathsf{p}}_{-}(X)}{\mathcal{H}}\big({\mathsf{p}}_{+}(Y),{\mathsf{p}}_{+}(Z)\big)=0\ ,

for all
X,Y,Z\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(TM)
. Then
(M,{\mathcal{H}},{\mathcal{F}})
is a Riemannian foliation, and the leaf space
{\mathcal{Q}}
admits a Riemannian metric
g
such that the quotient map
q:M\longrightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}
is a Riemannian submersion. We further assume that the fundamental 2-form
\omega
is transversally invariant with respect to the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
, that is,

\displaystyle\pounds_{{\mathsf{p}}_{-}(X)}\omega\big({\mathsf{p}}_{+}(Y),{\mathsf{p}}_{+}(Z)\big)=0\ ,

for all
X,Y,Z\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(TM)
. Then
{\mathcal{Q}}
admits a 2-form
b\in{\mathsf{\Omega}}^{2}({\mathcal{Q}})
inherited from
\omega.

In this way, the leaf space becomes the target space of a non-linear sigma-model
S(g,b)
whose background is the
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
({\mathcal{Q}},g)
with Kalb-Ramond field
b\in{\mathsf{\Omega}}^{2}({\mathcal{Q}})
. The action functional of this sigma-model is

S[\phi]=\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{\Sigma}\,\bar{g}_{ij}\,\mathrm{d}\phi^{i}\wedge\star\,\mathrm{d}\phi^{j}+\int_{\Sigma}\,\bar{b}\ ,

where here
\phi
is a map from
(\Sigma,h)
to
({\mathcal{Q}},g).

Remark 2.48
.

For an admissible almost para-Hermitian manifold
(M,K,\eta)
, it is also possible to reduce the corresponding Wess-Zumino action functional
{\mathcal{S}}_{\textrm{\tiny WZ}}
to the leaf space if the canonical
3
-form
H_{\tt can}
on
M
additionally satisfies

\displaystyle\iota_{{\mathsf{p}}_{-}(X)}H_{\tt can}=0\ ,

for all
X\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(TM)
(see
[
53
]
). Then
H_{\tt can}
can be regarded as the pullback of a
3
-form on the leaf space
{\mathcal{Q}}
by the quotient map
q:M\longrightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Suppose now that
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}\subset TM
is a Born D-brane such that
W_{\textrm{\tiny D}}=L_{+}\cap L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
has constant rank (cf. Proposition
2.38
). This gives another foliation
{\mathcal{F}}_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
of the Born manifold
M
whose leaves can be understood as supported by the physical D-branes (cf. Remark
2.39
). Then the corresponding D-brane for the sigma-model into
{\mathcal{Q}}
is given by the image of
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
under the derivative
\mathrm{d}q
,
\mathrm{d}q(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}})=\mathrm{d}q(W_{\textrm{\tiny D}})\subseteq T{\mathcal{Q}}.
This is a vector sub-bundle of
T{\mathcal{Q}}
.
In particular,
\mathrm{d}q(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}})
is involutive because
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
is involutive, and the restriction of the metric
g
to
\mathrm{d}q(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}})
is positive-definite because
q:(M,{\mathcal{H}})\longrightarrow({\mathcal{Q}},g)
is a Riemannian submersion. The integrability condition inherited from
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
implies that
{\mathcal{Q}}
admits a regular foliation
{\mathcal{F}}^{q}_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
whose leaves are supported by the physical D-branes of the sigma-model
S(g,b)
, where the dimension of each leaf is bounded from above by the rank of
W_{\textrm{\tiny D}}=L_{+}\cap L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}.

Example 2.49
.

Let
(M,K,\eta,{\mathcal{H}})
be a Born manifold which admits a Riemannian foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
such that
L_{-}=T{\mathcal{F}}
as above. Then
L_{-}
is a Born D-brane and its induced physical D-branes are just points (
0
-branes) in
{\mathcal{Q}}=M/{\mathcal{F}}
. This corresponds to fully Dirichlet boundary conditions for the sigma-model
S(g,b)
into
{\mathcal{Q}}
.

At the opposite extreme, if the eigenbundle
L_{+}
of
K
is integrable as well, then
L_{+}
is also a Born D-brane which induces a space-filling physical D-brane whose support is simply the whole leaf space
{\mathcal{Q}}
of the foliation. This corresponds to fully Neumann boundary conditions for the sigma-model
S(g,b)
into
{\mathcal{Q}}
.

3.
Spacetime Perspective: D-Branes on Metric Algebroids

The definition and properties of D-branes given in
[
29
,
20
]
and in Section
2
of the present paper, although physically well motivated from the perspective of open string sigma-models, highlight the construction of a brane solely from the point of view of sub-bundles of the tangent bundle
TM
of the doubled spacetime. On the one hand, this leads to a more general definition of branes which does not involve submanifolds of
M
. On the other hand, to recover the usual geometric picture of D-branes with worldvolumes and Chan-Paton bundles requires, among other things, the imposition of integrability of the sub-bundle as an extra condition, which cannot be derived from the analysis of the worldsheet constraints alone. As discussed in Remarks
2.39
and
2.41
, from this perspective D-branes only arise as foliations, i.e. a single D-brane is given by a submanifold composing the foliation which integrates the distribution that solves the constraints.

The purpose of this section is to develop a complimentary picture of D-branes entirely from the perspective of para-Hermitian geometries on the spacetime, and to discuss how it connects to our worldsheet perspective from Section
2
for integrable branes. In particular, we provide classes of D-branes for Born sigma-models which serve as physically motivated examples of the branes in this section. Regardless of these connections, the treatment which follows is much more general and encompassing, and it mimics the well-known treatment of branes in generalised geometry. The material of this section relies heavily on the theory of metric algebroids, in the settings presented in Appendices
A.4
and
A.5
.

3.1.
D-Structures and Branes

The natural notion of integrability on a metric algebroid is provided by a ‘D-structure’, which was given in
[
13
]
. We start by introducing a different notion.

Definition 3.1
.

An
almost D-structure
on a metric algebroid
(E,\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
is an isotropic vector sub-bundle
L\subset E.
A
D-structure
is an almost D-structure
L
which is involutive with respect to the D-bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket,
i.e.

\llbracket{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(L),{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(L)\rrbracket\subseteq{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(L),
and in this case we say that
L
is
D-integrable
.

If
(E,\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
is an exact Courant algebroid, then a D-structure is called a
small Dirac structure
. A
Dirac structure
is a small Dirac structure which is maximally isotropic.

In this definition we allow for sub-bundles with non-constant rank. An almost D-structure
L
is said to be
regular
if its rank is constant. In particular, if
L
is a Dirac structure, then the restriction of the Dorfman bracket to
L
is skew-symmetric, and thus a Dirac structure is a Lie algebroid.

Example 3.2
.

Let
(E,\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
be an exact pre-Courant algebroid over a manifold
M
with a maximally isotropic splitting
\sigma
, so that
(E,\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
is isomorphic to the pre-Courant algebroid
(\mathbb{T}M,\eta_{\mathbb{T}M},{\rm pr}_{TM},\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{H_{\sigma}})
, where
H_{\sigma}\in{\sf\Omega}^{3}(M)
is given by (
A.33
). Then
{\rm im}(\rho^{*})\simeq T^{*}M
is involutive with respect to
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket
, hence it is a D-structure.

On the other hand, the
3
-form
H_{\sigma}
measures the violation of involutivity of
{\rm im}(\sigma)\simeq TM
with respect to the D-bracket, and thus
{\rm im}(\sigma)
is an almost D-structure which is D-integrable if and only if
H_{\sigma}=0
, in which case it is a Dirac structure.

We can establish a sufficient condition for a maximally isotropic integrable distribution on an almost para-Hermitian manifold
(M,K,\eta)
to be integrable with the respect to the D-bracket of the canonical metric algebroid on its tangent bundle
TM
. This is contained in

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(M,K,\eta)
be an almost para-Hermitian manifold and
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}\subset TM
an almost Born D-brane which is preserved by the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}
of
\eta.
Then
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
is a D-integrable Born D-brane.

Proof.

Since the distribution
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
is preserved by a torsion-free connection, it is Frobenius integrable. Hence
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
is a Born D-brane.

To prove D-integrability we show that

\eta([\![X,Y]\!]_{\tt can},Z)=0\ ,

for all
X,Y,Z\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}).

For this, recall from Example
A.30
that the canonical connection
\nabla^{\tt can}
is given by

\eta(\nabla^{\tt can}_{X}\,Y,Z)=\eta(\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}_{X}\,Y,Z)-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}_{X}\,\omega(Y,K(Z))\ ,

where here we restrict to
X,Y,Z\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}).

Locally we have

\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}\,\omega=\mathrm{d}\omega+\varGamma^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}\wedge\omega\ ,

where
\varGamma^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}
is the (local) connection 1-form characterising
\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}.
Then

(3.4)

\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}_{X}\,\omega(Y,Z)=\iota_{Z}\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}(\mathrm{d}\omega+\varGamma^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}\wedge\omega)\ ,

for all
X,Y,Z\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}).

Since
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}
is involutive and isotropic, it follows that

\iota_{Z}\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}\mathrm{d}\omega=-\iota_{Z}\iota_{[X,Y]}\omega=0\ ,

and similarly the second term on the right-hand side of (
3.4
) vanishes.
Thus
\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}_{X}\,\omega(Y,Z)=0
and

\eta(\nabla^{\tt can}_{X}\,Y,Z)=\eta(\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}_{X}\,Y,Z)

for all
X,Y,Z\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}),
since
K(Z)\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}).

Therefore

\eta([\![X,Y]\!]_{\tt can},Z)=\eta(\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}_{X}\,Y-\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}_{Y}\,X,Z)+\eta(\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}_{Z}\,X,Y)\ ,

which vanishes because
\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}
preserves
L_{\textrm{\tiny D}}.

∎

Proposition
3.3
describes properties of D-branes on the canonical metric algebroid of an almost para-Hermitian manifold, and it inspires the following notion.
Let
(E,\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
be a metric algebroid endowed with a para-complex structure
K\in{\sf Aut}_{\mathds{1}}(E).
The quintuple
(E,K,\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
is called a
split metric algebroid
.
Examples are given by split exact pre-Courant algebroids, see Appendix
A.5
. We can provide a natural notion of a brane on a split metric algebroid, which may be viewed as the real counterpart of the notion of a generalised complex brane from
[
35
]
.

Definition 3.5
.

A
brane on a split metric algebroid

(E,K,\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
is a D-structure
L\subset E
which is preserved by
K,
i.e.
K(L)=L.

Example 3.6
.

Let
(E,K_{\sigma},\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
be a split exact pre-Courant algebroid over a manifold
M
. Then
T^{*}M\subset E
is a brane.

Beyond Example
3.6
, Definition
3.5
as it stands is too general to lead to any meaningful insight into the properties of such branes. Moreover, they do not immediately offer a relation to the more physically intuitive geometric structures surrounding D-branes. Hence we proceed to develop a theory of branes on the slightly stronger structure of a pre-Courant algebroid. One advantage provided by this restricted class of metric algebroids is that the notion of D-integrability leads to Frobenius integrability: If
L\subset E
is a D-structure, then the bracket morphism property of the anchor (see Definition
A.18
) implies that its image
\rho(L)\subset TM
is involutive, and hence induces a foliation of
M
.
Pre-Courant algebroids constitute a physically meaningful intermediary step between the Courant algebroids of generalised geometry, wherein the section constraint is imposed and solved, and the more general metric algebroids of a fully unconstrained doubled geometry; see
[
9
,
16
,
23
,
25
]
for detailed descriptions of the chain of metric algebroids involved between type II supergravity and double field theory.

3.2.
Generalised Para-Complex D-Branes

We shall now extend the definition of generalised submanifolds, guided by the analogue constructions in generalised complex geometry from
[
54
,
55
]
, to the setting of generalised para-complex structures and pre-Courant algebroids.
We begin with the natural extension of
[
55
, Definition 7.1]
, which is stated for an exact Courant algebroid.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
(E,\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
be an exact pre-Courant algebroid over a manifold
M
together with a maximally isotropic splitting
\sigma
such that
(E,\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
is isomorphic to the pre-Courant algebroid

(\mathbb{T}M,\eta_{\mathbb{T}M},\mathrm{pr}_{TM},\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{H_{\sigma}}),
where
H_{\sigma}\in\mathsf{\Omega}^{3}(M).
A
generalised submanifold
is a pair
({\mathcal{W}},L),
where
i\colon{\mathcal{W}}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow M
is a submanifold of
M
such that
i^{*}\,\mathrm{d}H_{\sigma}=0
and
L\subset E
is a maximally isotropic D-integrable sub-bundle over
{\mathcal{W}}
such that
\rho(L)=T{\mathcal{W}}.

Remark 3.8
.

Let us unravel and discuss the physical significance of this definition. The generalised tangent bundle
L
of
{\mathcal{W}}
is isomorphic to the sub-bundle
L_{\sigma}\subset\mathbb{T}M
over
{\mathcal{W}}
by the pre-Courant algebroid isomorphism induced by
\sigma,
where
L_{\sigma}
is the graph

L_{\sigma}=\big\{X+\alpha\in T{\mathcal{W}}\oplus T^{*}M\rvert_{\mathcal{W}}\ \big|\ \alpha\rvert_{\mathcal{W}}=\iota_{X}F_{\sigma}\big\}

of some
2
-form
F_{\sigma}\in\mathsf{\Omega}^{2}({\mathcal{W}}).
It is easy to show that
L_{\sigma}
takes this form by using the fact that it is maximally isotropic, together with
\rho(L)=T{\mathcal{W}}
, and applying the results of Section
2.5
. In particular, there is a one-to-one correspondence between maximally isotropic sub-bundles
L
over
{\mathcal{W}}
such that
\rho(L)=T{\mathcal{W}}
and
2
-forms
F\in\mathsf{\Omega}^{2}({\mathcal{W}}).

Then the D-integrability condition for
L
can be written as

0=\eta_{\mathbb{T}M}(\llbracket X_{1}+\alpha_{1},X_{2}+\alpha_{2}\rrbracket_{H_{\sigma}},X_{3}+\alpha_{3})=\iota_{X_{3}}\iota_{X_{2}}\iota_{X_{1}}(i^{*}H_{\sigma}+\mathrm{d}F_{\sigma})\ ,

for all
X_{1}+\alpha_{1},\,X_{2}+\alpha_{2},\,X_{3}+\alpha_{3}\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(L_{\sigma}).

Hence the pair
({\mathcal{W}},L)
must satisfy

\displaystyle i^{*}H_{\sigma}=-\mathrm{d}F_{\sigma}\ ,

which clearly requires
\mathrm{d}(i^{*}H_{\sigma})=0.
This generalises the condition (
2.43
) on the Lagrangian Born D-branes of Section
2.7
.

Let
(E,\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
be an exact pre-Courant algebroid. In Appendix
A.5
we describe the natural para-Hermitian structures
(K_{\sigma},\eta)
on
E
. More generally, a para-complex structure

Example 3.10

Example 3.11

Example 3.16

We apply the reduction of Theorem

3.4.
D-Branes on Doubled Nilmanifolds

A large class of consistent compactifictions of supergravity are provided by doubled twisted tori
[
59
]
, which are defined as quotients
M={\mathsf{\Gamma}}\setminus{\sf G}
of a doubled Lie group
{\mathsf{G}}

[
19
]
by a discrete cocompact subgroup
{\mathsf{\Gamma}}\subset{\sf G}
acting from the left; these have gauge algebras realised as the isometry algebra of
M

[
34
]
. The smooth manifold
M
is parallelisable, and so has a basis of globally defined left-invariant
1
-forms. The isometry group of
M
is generated by vector fields dual to these
1
-forms. Polarisations of
M
give quotients which are physical spaces realised as torus bundles, with a given monodromy encoded geometrically in
M
.

Here we will focus on the example of the doubled nilmanifold
M_{\mathsf{H}}
, which is obtained as a quotient of the cotangent bundle
T^{*}{\mathsf{H}}={\mathsf{H}}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}
of the three-dimensional Heisenberg group
{\mathsf{H}}
by a discrete cocompact subgroup
{\mathsf{\Gamma}}_{m}
labelled by an integer
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. The doubled nilmanifold
M_{\mathsf{H}}
admits two distinguished fibrations: a principal
\mathbb{T}^{3}
-bundle over the Heisenberg nilmanifold
\mathbb{T}_{\mathsf{H}}
and a fibration over the
3
-torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}
with
\mathbb{T}^{3}
fibres. For further details, including the reductions of the doubled sigma-models in the corresponding polarisations of
M_{\mathsf{H}}
, see
[
19
,
34
]
. We shall illustrate our formalism by reproducing some of the D-branes found in
[
27
,
29
]
.

Definition A.1

-Transformations

We describe an important class of isometries of a para-Hermitian vector bundle called ‘

Definition A.14
.

\eta\big(B_{+}(e_{1}),e_{2}\big)=-\eta\big(e_{1},B_{+}(e_{2})\big)\ ,

for all

.

defines a 2-form

b_{+}(e_{1},e_{2})=\eta\big(B_{+}(e_{1}),e_{2}\big)\ ,

for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(E).

-transformation is given by the map

K_{B_{+}}=e^{-B_{+}}\circ K\circ e^{B_{+}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}&0\\

while the

\displaystyle\omega_{B_{+}}=\omega-2\,b_{+}\ .

A.4.
Metric Algebroids

The extension of type II supergravity to the manifestly duality-invariant framework of double field theory requires a weakening of the well-known notion of Courant algebroid from generalised geometry.

Definition A.18
.

A
metric algebroid
on a manifold
M
is quadruple

(E,\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
of a vector bundle

E\longrightarrow M
with a fibrewise non-degenerate pairing
\eta
, a vector bundle morphism
\rho:E\longrightarrow TM
covering the identity, called the
anchor
, and an
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket:{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(E)\times{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(E)\longrightarrow{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(E)
, called the
D-bracket
, such that

(1)

\llbracket e_{1},f\,e_{2}\rrbracket=f\,\llbracket e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket+\big(\rho(e_{1})\cdot f\big)\,e_{2}\,,

(2)

\rho(e_{1})\cdot\eta(e_{2},e_{3})=\eta(\llbracket e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket,e_{3})+\eta(e_{2},\llbracket e_{1},e_{3}\rrbracket)\,,
and

(3)

\eta(\llbracket e_{1},e_{1}\rrbracket,e_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\,\rho(e_{2})\cdot\eta(e_{1},e_{1})\,,

for all
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(E)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M).

A
pre-Courant algebroid
is a metric algebroid
(E,\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
whose anchor
\rho:E\longrightarrow TM
is a bracket morphism:

(4)

\rho(\llbracket e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket)=[\rho(e_{1}),\rho(e_{2})]\,,

for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(E)
, where
[\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,]
is the usual Lie bracket of vector fields on
TM
.

A
Courant algebroid
is a metric algebroid
(E,\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
which is also a Leibniz-Loday algebroid, i.e. its bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket
, called the
Dorfman bracket
in this case, obeys the Jacobi identity

(5)

\llbracket e_{1},\llbracket e_{2},e_{3}\rrbracket\rrbracket=\llbracket\llbracket e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket,e_{3}\rrbracket+\llbracket e_{2},\llbracket e_{1},e_{3}\rrbracket\rrbracket\,,

for all
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(E)
.

The anchored Leibniz rule (1) follows from property (2), and hence a metric algebroid can be characterised by two axioms alone. The Jacobi identity (5) together with the anchored Leibniz rule (1) implies the bracket morphism property (4). Hence, as the terminology suggests, any Courant algebroid is also a pre-Courant algebroid (but not conversely); pre-Courant and Courant algebroids can thus be specified by a minimal set of three axioms.

A metric algebroid is called
regular
if its anchor
\rho
has constant rank, and
transitive
if
\rho
is surjective.

Remark A.19
.

The anchor map
\rho:E\longrightarrow TM
and the pairing
\eta
induce a map
\rho^{*}:T^{*}M\longrightarrow E
given by

\eta\big(\rho^{*}(\alpha),e\big)\coloneqq\braket{\rho^{\tt t}(\alpha),e}\ ,

for all
e\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(E)
and
\alpha\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(T^{*}M)
, where
\rho^{\tt t}:T^{*}M\longrightarrow E^{*}
is the transpose of
\rho
and
\braket{\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,}:{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(E^{*})\times{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(E)\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(M)
is the canonical dual pairing. In other words,
\rho^{*}=(\eta^{-1})^{\sharp}\circ\rho^{\tt t}.

We will now show that metric algebroids are abundant and arise very naturally. Let
(E,\eta)
be a pseudo-Euclidean vector bundle over a manifold
M.
The vector bundle of metric-preserving first order covariant differential operators on
E
forms a transitive Lie algebroid called the
Atiyah algebroid

see for instance
[

. It fits into a short exact sequence of Lie algebroids over

given by

(A.20)

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{so}(E)\xlongrightarrow{i}{{\sf At}}(E,\eta)\xlongrightarrow{{{\mathsf{a}}}}TM\longrightarrow 0\ ,

where
{\mathsf{a}}
is the anchor of the Atiyah algebroid and

is the subalgebroid inclusion of

\mathfrak{so}(E)\coloneqq\big\{\psi\in{\sf{End}}(E)\ \big|\ \eta\circ(\psi\times\mathds{1})=-\eta\circ(\mathds{1}\times\psi)\big\}\ .

The isomorphism

follows from this definition.

.

Metric Koszul connections

Proof.

{\mathsf{p}}_{\pm}:TM\longrightarrow L_{\pm}
be the projections to the
\pm\,1
-eigenbundles
L_{\pm}
of the almost para-complex structure
K
, and define the
canonical connection

\displaystyle\nabla^{\tt can}:={\mathsf{p}}_{+}\circ\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}\circ{\mathsf{p}}_{+}+{\mathsf{p}}_{-}\circ\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}\circ{\mathsf{p}}_{-}\ ,

where
\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}
is the Levi-Civita connection of the split signature metric
\eta
. Then
\nabla^{\tt can}
also preserves
\eta
, and by definition it preserves
K
as well, i.e.
\nabla^{\tt can}_{X}K=0
for all
X\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(TM)
, hence it preserves the splitting
TM=L_{+}\oplus L_{-}
induced by
K
. The deviation between the two linear connections
\nabla^{\tt can}
and
\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}
(which by Remark
A.21
correspond to different splittings of the corresponding Atiyah sequence (
A.20
) with
E=TM
) can be quantified through

\displaystyle\eta(\nabla^{\tt can}_{X}\,Y,Z)=\eta(\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}_{X}\,Y,Z)-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}_{X}\,\omega(Y,K(Z))\ ,

for all
X,Y,Z\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(TM)
,
where
\omega
is the fundamental
2
-form of the almost para-Hermitian manifold. In particular,
\nabla^{\tt can}=\nabla^{\textrm{\tiny\tt LC}}
if and only if
(M,K,\eta)
is an
almost para-Kähler manifold
, i.e.
\mathrm{d}\omega=0

[
10
]
.

By the construction of Proposition
A.24
, we obtain a D-bracket on
TM
given by

\displaystyle\eta(\llbracket X,Y\rrbracket_{\tt can},Z):=\eta(\nabla_{X}^{\tt can}Y-\nabla_{Y}^{\tt can}X,Z)+\eta(\nabla_{Z}^{\tt can}X,Y)\ ,

for all
X,Y,Z\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(TM)
. It is compatible with the almost para-complex structure
K
, in the sense that both of the sub-bundles
L_{\pm}
are involutive with respect to it:

\llbracket{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(L_{\pm}),{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(L_{\pm})\rrbracket_{\tt can}\ \subseteq\ {\mathsf{\Gamma}}(L_{\pm})\ .

It is moreover compatible with the Lie bracket of vector fields on
TM
, in the sense that

\displaystyle\llbracket{\mathsf{p}}_{\pm}(X),{\mathsf{p}}_{\pm}(Y)\rrbracket_{\tt can}={\mathsf{p}}_{\pm}\big([{\mathsf{p}}_{\pm}(X),{\mathsf{p}}_{\pm}(Y)]\big)\ ,

for all
X,Y\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(TM)
. In this sense, the bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\tt can}
is the unique canonical compatible D-bracket induced by a metric connection on the anchored para-Hermitian vector bundle
(TM,\eta,K,\mathds{1}_{TM})

[
8
]
.

A.5.
Exact Pre-Courant Algebroids

We shall now specialise the discussion of Appendix
A.4
to the class of metric algebroids which are pre-Courant algebroids, i.e. for which the homomorphism property (4) of the anchor map in Definition
A.18
is imposed. Let
(E,\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
be a regular pre-Courant algebroid over a manifold
M
. From the definition of
\rho^{*}=\eta^{-1}{}^{\sharp}\circ\rho^{\tt t}:T^{*}M\longrightarrow E
it follows that
{\rm im}(\rho^{*})
is a coisotropic sub-bundle of
E
with respect to the fibrewise metric
\eta
, and
{\mathsf{\Gamma}}({\rm im}(\rho^{*}))
is a two-sided abelian ideal of
{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(E)
with respect to the D-bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket

[
62
]
. Hence
\rho\circ\rho^{*}=0
and there is a chain complex

(A.31)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow T^{*}M\xlongrightarrow{\rho^{*}}E\xlongrightarrow{\rho}TM\longrightarrow 0\ .

A pre-Courant algebroid is
exact
if (
A.31
) is a short exact sequence.

From the exactness of the
sequence (
A.31
), it follows that the sub-bundle

\mathrm{im}(\rho^{*})\subset E,
which is isomorphic to
T^{*}M,
is maximally isotropic with respect to
\eta.

A para-Hermitian structure on
E
is given by a choice of a maximally
isotropic splitting of (
A.31
):

\sigma:TM\longrightarrow E\qquad\mbox{with}\quad\rho\circ\sigma=\mathds{1}_{TM}\ .

It follows that

E=\mathrm{im}(\sigma)\oplus\mathrm{im}(\rho^{*})

with associated para-complex structure defined by

K_{\sigma}\big(\sigma(X)+\rho^{*}(\alpha)\big)=\sigma(X)-\rho^{*}(\alpha)\ ,

for all
X\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(TM)
and
\alpha\in{\mathsf{\Omega}}^{1}(M).
The para-complex structure
K_{\sigma}
is
compatible with the metric
\eta,
and thus
E
is endowed with a
para-Hermitian structure. This para-Hermitian structure of an exact
pre-Courant algebroid is isomorphic to the para-Hermitian structure of the
generalised tangent bundle
\mathbb{T}M
from
Example
A.4
.

Under the isomorphism
E\simeq\mathbb{T}M=TM\oplus T^{*}M
induced by the splitting
\sigma
, the anchor map
\rho:E\longrightarrow TM
is sent to the projection
{\rm pr}_{TM}:TM\oplus T^{*}M\longrightarrow TM
defined by
{\rm pr}_{TM}(X+\alpha)=X
, for all
X\in TM
and
\alpha\in T^{*}M
. The D-bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket
is sent to the bracket
[
35
,
40
]

(A.32)

\llbracket X+\alpha,Y+\beta\rrbracket_{H_{\sigma}}=[X,Y]+\pounds_{X}\beta-\iota_{Y}\,\mathrm{d}\alpha+\iota_{Y}\iota_{X}H_{\sigma}\ ,

for all
X,Y\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(TM)
and
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathsf{\Omega}}^{1}(M)
, where
\pounds_{X}
denotes the Lie derivative along the vector field
X
and
H_{\sigma}\in{\mathsf{\Omega}}^{3}(M)
is the
3
-form on
M
defined by

(A.33)

\displaystyle H_{\sigma}(X,Y,Z)=\eta\big(\llbracket\sigma(X),\sigma(Y)\rrbracket,\sigma(Z)\big)\ ,

for all
X,Y,Z\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(TM).

By Remark
A.2
, every maximally isotropic splitting of the short exact sequence (
A.31
) is associated with a para-Hermitian structure on
E
, and by Remark
A.16
any two splittings of an exact pre-Courant algebroid are related by a
B_{+}
-transformation, as discussed in Appendix
A.3
. Under the isomorphism above, a
B_{+}
-transformation of a split exact pre-Courant algebroid is given by a
2
-form
b_{+}=B\in{\mathsf{\Omega}}^{2}(M)
on
M
which induces the vector bundle morphism
B_{+}=B^{\flat}:TM\longrightarrow T^{*}M
given by
B^{\flat}(X)=\iota_{X}B
, for all
X\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(TM)
. Thus
e^{B_{+}}:\mathbb{T}M\longrightarrow\mathbb{T}M
is given by

\displaystyle e^{B_{+}}(X+\alpha)=X+\iota_{X}B+\alpha\ ,

for all
X\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(TM)
and
\alpha\in{\mathsf{\Omega}}^{1}(M)
. The image
{\rm im}(e^{B_{+}})\subset\mathbb{T}M
is a maximally isotropic sub-bundle, and it is compatible with the anchor
{\rm pr}_{TM}
, i.e.
{\rm pr}_{TM}\circ e^{B_{+}}=e^{B_{+}}\circ{\rm pr}_{TM}
. The isometry
e^{B_{+}}
acts on the D-bracket (
A.32
) as
[
35
]

\displaystyle\llbracket e^{B_{+}}(X+\alpha),e^{B_{+}}(Y+\beta)\rrbracket_{H_{\sigma}}=e^{B_{+}}\big(\llbracket X+\alpha,Y+\beta\rrbracket_{H_{\sigma}+\mathrm{d}B}\big)\ ,

for all
X+\alpha,Y+\beta\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(\mathbb{T}M)
. On the other hand,
B_{+}
-transformations do not preserve the para-Hermitian structure on
\mathbb{T}M
, but the different para-Hermitian structures are isomorphic, and they have the same D-bracket if and only if the
2
-form
B
is closed,
\mathrm{d}B=0
.

Remark A.34
.

When
H_{\sigma}=0
, the para-Hermitian vector bundle
\mathbb{T}M
from Example
A.4
with anchor
{\rm pr}_{TM}
and D-bracket (
A.32
) is a Courant algebroid called the
standard Courant algebroid
on
M
. In this case the standard Dorfman bracket
\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{0}=\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket_{\nabla}
can also be obtained from the construction (
A.25
) by choosing an arbitrary torsion-free connection
\nabla
on
TM
and extending it to
\mathbb{T}M
by
\nabla_{X}(Y+\beta)=\nabla_{X}Y+\nabla_{X}\beta
, for all
X\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(TM)
and
Y+\beta\in{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(\mathbb{T}M)

[
10
]
. This bracket is compatible with the para-Hermitian structure of Example
A.4
, i.e. the eigenbundles
TM
and
T^{*}M
are both involutive with respect to (
A.32
) for
H_{\sigma}=0
.

More generally, the D-bracket is a Dorfman bracket, i.e. it satisfies the Jacobi identity (5) of Definition
A.18
, if and only if the
3
-form
H_{\sigma}
is closed,
\mathrm{d}H_{\sigma}=0
. In that case the Courant algebroid is called the
H_{\sigma}
-twisted standard Courant algebroid
on
M
, and its isomorphism class depends only on the de Rham cohomology class
[H_{\sigma}]\in{\sf H}^{3}(M;\mathbb{R})
, called the
Ševera class
. In other words, isomorphism classes of exact Courant algebroids over a manifold
M
are in one-to-one correspondence with elements of the cohomology group
{\sf H}^{3}(M;\mathbb{R})
. This is the famous Ševera classification of exact Courant algebroids
[
40
]
.

A.6.
Courant Algebroid Reduction

We briefly recall the main results concerning the reduction of Courant algebroids from
[
54
,
55
]
, beginning with the following notion.

Definition A.35
.

Let
(E,\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
be an exact Courant algebroid over a manifold
M
endowed with an isotropic sub-bundle
A\subset E
over a submanifold
{\mathcal{W}}\subseteq M
whose orthogonal complement
A^{\perp}
obeys
\rho(A^{\perp})=T{\mathcal{W}}.
The subspace of sections of
A^{\perp}
which are
basic with respect to

A
is

\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathtt{bas}}(A^{\perp})\coloneqq\big\{e\in\mathsf{\Gamma}(A^{\perp})\ \big|\ \llbracket e,{\mathsf{\Gamma}}(A)\rrbracket\subseteq\mathsf{\Gamma}(A)\big\}\ .

The circumstances under which the reduction of an exact Courant algebroid is possible is described by

Theorem A.36
.

Let
(E,\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
be an exact Courant algebroid over
M
endowed with an isotropic sub-bundle
A
over a submanifold
{\mathcal{W}}\subseteq M
such that
\rho(A^{\perp})=T{\mathcal{W}}
. Assume that
A^{\perp}
is spanned fibrewise by basic sections

\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathtt{bas}}(A^{\perp}).
Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be the foliation of
{\mathcal{W}}
integrating the involutive distribution
\rho(A)\subset T{\mathcal{W}}
, and suppose that the leaf space
{\mathcal{Q}}={\mathcal{W}}/{\mathcal{F}}
is a smooth manifold. Then there is an exact Courant algebroid with underlying vector bundle
E_{\mathtt{red}}
over
{\mathcal{Q}}
such that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\perp}/A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{\mathtt{red}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{W}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{Q}}}

is a pullback of vector bundles.

The main result concerning the reduction of Dirac structures can be stated as

Proposition A.37
.

Let
(E,\eta,\rho,\llbracket\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rrbracket)
be an exact Courant algebroid over
M
endowed with an isotropic sub-bundle
A
over a submanifold
{\mathcal{W}}\subseteq M
such that
\rho(A^{\perp})=T{\mathcal{W}}
, which fulfils the assumptions of Theorem
A.36
, i.e.

E
induces an exact Courant algebroid
E_{\mathtt{red}}
over
{\mathcal{Q}}={\mathcal{W}}/{\mathcal{F}}.
Let
L
be a maximally isotropic sub-bundle of
E\rvert_{\mathcal{W}}
such that
L\cap A^{\perp}
has constant rank and

(A.38)

\llbracket\mathsf{\Gamma}(A),\mathsf{\Gamma}(L\cap A^{\perp})\rrbracket\ \subseteq\ \mathsf{\Gamma}(L+A)\ .

Then
L
reduces to a maximally isotropic sub-bundle
L_{\mathtt{red}}
of
E_{\mathtt{red}}.

If in addition

(A.39)

\llbracket\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathtt{bas}}(L\cap A^{\perp}),\mathsf{\Gamma}_{\mathtt{bas}}(L\cap A^{\perp})\rrbracket\ \subseteq\ \mathsf{\Gamma}(L+A)\ ,

then
L_{\mathtt{red}}
is a Dirac structure.

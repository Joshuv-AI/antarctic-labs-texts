Towards the essence of Šoltés’ problem
Stijn Cambie

Abstract
We explore the question asking for graphs G G for which the total distance decreases, possibly by a fixed constant k k , upon the removal of any of its vertices. We obtain results leading to intuition and doubts for the Šoltés’ problem ( k = 0 k=0 ) and its conjectures.

Introduction

In 1991, Šoltés
[
14
]
observed that if one removes a vertex of a cycle
C_{11}
, the total distance does not change, and asked whether there are other such graphs
G
(nowadays called Šoltés’ graphs).
It is one of the most elementary questions one can pose
[
10
]
related to
W(G)
, the total distance of
G
.
Infinitely many examples were given by Spiro
[
15
]
and Cambie
[
5
]
for the relaxation of Šoltés’ problem to signed graphs and hypergraphs respectively.

Meanwhile, there are numerous open conjectures (weaker to stronger forms), conjecturing that Šoltés’ graphs are e.g. regular or vertex-transitive, and that
C_{11}
is the only Šoltés’ graph. See
[
10
, conj. 47, 48, 51]
.
One of the arguments, as can be seen in the conclusion of
[
2
]
, was that no other examples were found among the
>10^{8}
vertex-transitive graphs in the census by Holt and Royle
[
9
]
. We will give intuition why all of these (different from the cycles) satisfy
W(G)>W(G\setminus v)
for
v\in V(G)
.

In
Section

2
, we prove that if
W(G)\leq W(G\setminus v)
for every
v\in V(G),
the diameter cannot be too small, and if the value
W(G)-W(G\setminus v)
is independent of the vertex
v\in v(G),
then

is a cycle or the minimum degree of
G
is at least
3.

In
Section

3
(and
Appendix

), we present eight graphs

for which two third of the vertices

satisfy
W(G\setminus v)=W(G)
(these are Šoltés’ vertices), and give conditional examples of graphs for which an even higher fraction of the vertices satisfy the inequality.

In
[
1

(and
[
10
, Prob. 44]
), the authors mentioned that arguments to solve Šoltés’ problem may also work for the following generalisation.

Problem 1

For a fixed

, find all graphs

W(G)-W(G-v)=z

As a first thing, we want to convince the reader that this problem is nearly impossible to solve in general

For fixed

Of course, for every

1.1
Notation and Terminology

We only consider simple connected graphs
G=(V,E).

The degree of a vertex
v
, denoted by
\deg(v)
, is the number of edges containing
v
. The minimum and maximum degree,
\delta
and
\Delta
, represent the smallest and largest degrees. When removing the vertex
v
and its incident edges from
G
, we obtain
G\setminus v
.

The distance
d_{G}(u,v)
or
d(u,v)
between two vertices
u
and
v
is equal to the length of the shortest path between them. The diameter,
diam(G)
, is the largest among all possible distances and the total distance (or Wiener index),
W(G)
, the sum over all of them;
W(G)=\sum_{u,v\in V}d(u,v)
. The radius
rad(G)=\min_{u\in V}\max_{v\in V}d(u,v)
gives the maximum distance from a central vertex.
The transmission of a vertex
v
,
\sigma(v)=\sum_{u\in V}d(u,v)
, is the sum of distances between
v
and the other vertices.
We also define the arc-graph of
G
.

Definition 2
.

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a graph.
Let
A(G)
be the arcs of
G
, i.e., the ordered pairs of neighbouring vertices.
Note that
\left\lvert A(G)\right\rvert=2\left\lvert E(G)\right\rvert.

The
arc-graph

H
of

has vertex set
A(G)
, with two arcs being adjacent if

•

they correspond to the same edge, i.e.,
(u,v)
is adjacent to
(v,u)
when

•

the arcs have the same root, i.e.,
(v,u)
and
(v,w)
are adjacent in
H
.

Equivalently,
H
is equal to the line-graph of the subdivision of
G.

Essentially, ranging over all vertices, a vertex

of degree

is replaced by a clique

whose endvertices are connected with one initial neighbour of

each.
It has been introduced before as the subdivided-line graph in

]
, and for cubic graphs, as the truncation of the graph

.

Necessary condition for Šoltés’ graphs

Forbidding an isolated vertex as a Šoltés’ graph, and remarking that if
u
is a pendent vertex of a connected graph
G
, we have
W(G\setminus u)<W(G)
, we know that the minimum degree (
\delta
) of a Šoltés’ graph is at least
2
.
Here we prove that if
G\not=C_{11}
is a Šoltés’ graph, then
\delta(G)\geq 3.

This is a stronger version of a result by Dragan Stevanović (private communication), that a Šoltes’ graph different from
C_{11}
cannot have internal paths with
3
consecutive degree-
2
-vertices.
In the proofs, we denote
G_{v}
for the graph
G\setminus v
.

Proposition 3
.

A graph for which
\delta(G)=2
and
W(G\setminus v)
is constant for all
v\in V(G),
is a cycle.

Proof.

Let
G=(V,E)
be such a graph with
\delta(G)=2
, let
p
be a vertex with degree
2
and
a,b
be its two neighbours.
We need
W(G)=W(G_{a})=W(G_{p})=W(G_{b}).

Let
X=V\setminus\{a,p,b\}.

Let
H_{a}=G[X\cup a]
and
H_{b}=G[X\cup b].

Let
\sigma(a)=\sum_{w\in X}d_{H_{a}}(a,w)
and
\sigma(b)=\sum_{w\in X}d_{H_{b}}(b,w).

We are now ready to compare
W(G_{a}),W(G_{p})
and
W(G_{b}).

For this, we split these total distances in multiple parts.
First observe that

d_{G_{p}}(a,b)\leq\lvert X\rvert+1
with equality if and only if
G=C_{n},
while

d_{G_{a}}(b,p)=d_{G_{b}}(a,p)=1.

Next, we note that for every choice of
x,y\in X
,

d_{G_{p}}(x,y)\leq d_{G_{a}}(x,y),d_{G_{b}}(x,y).

Finally, since
H_{a},H_{b}\subset G_{p}
we note that

\sum_{w\in X}\left(d_{G_{p}}(a,w)+d_{G_{p}}(b,w)\right)\leq\sigma(a)+\sigma(b)

\sum_{w\in X}\left(d_{G_{a}}(p,w)+d_{G_{a}}(b,w)\right)=2\sigma(b)+\lvert X\rvert

\sum_{w\in X}\left(d_{G_{b}}(a,w)+d_{G_{b}}(p,w)\right)=2\sigma(a)+\lvert X\rvert.

Using these observations, we conclude that

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle 2W(G_{p})-W(G_{a})-W(G_{b})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2d_{G_{p}}(a,b)-d_{G_{a}}(p,b)-d_{G_{b}}(a,p)+\sum_{x,y\in X}\left(2d_{G_{p}}(x,y)-d_{G_{a}}(x,y)-d_{G_{b}}(x,y)\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{w\in X}\left(2d_{G_{p}}(a,w)+2d_{G_{p}}(b,w)-d_{G_{a}}(p,w)-d_{G_{a}}(b,w)-d_{G_{b}}(a,w)-d_{G_{b}}(p,w)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2d_{G_{p}}(a,b)-2-2\lvert X\rvert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 0.

Since equality need to be attained in every step, we have

d_{G_{p}}(a,b)=1+\lvert X\rvert,
which implies that
G_{p}
is a path from
a
to
b
, and thus
G
is a cycle.
∎

Proposition 4
.

A graph
G
of order
n>1
with
diam(G)\leq 2
has at least one vertex
v
for which either
G\setminus v
is disconnected, or
W(G\setminus v)<W(G).

Proof.

This is obvious for a clique (diameter
1
graph).
So now assume
diam(G)=2.

First we observe that
G
cannot have a universal vertex
v
, since for a vertex
u\not=v

W(G)=2\binom{n}{2}-m>2\binom{n-1}{2}-m+\deg(u)=W(G_{u}).

So
rad(G)>1
, i.e. for every
v\in V
its closed neighbourhood
N[v]
is not equal to
V.

The only pairs of vertices
u,u^{\prime}\in V(G_{v})
for which
d_{G_{v}}(u,u^{\prime})>d_{G}(u,u^{\prime}),
have to be both neighbours of
v
in
G
(and have no other common neighbour).

Note that for every
u\in N(v)
and
w\not\in N[v]
,

d_{G_{v}}(u,w)=d_{G}(u,w)\leq 2.

This implies that
d_{G_{v}}(u,u^{\prime})\leq d_{G_{v}}(u,w)+d_{G_{v}}(w,u^{\prime})\leq 4

and thus
d_{G_{v}}(u,u^{\prime})-d_{G}(u,u^{\prime})\leq 2.

Since
\sum_{u\in V\setminus v}d(u,v)=2(n-1)-\deg(v)\geq n
, has to be lower bounded by
\sum_{u,u^{\prime}\in V\setminus v}\left(d_{G_{v}}(u,u^{\prime})-d_{G}(u,u^{\prime})\right),

there are at least
\frac{n}{2}
pairs of vertices
(u,u^{\prime})
for which the unique shortest path uses
v
.

Summing over all
v\in V
, this implies that there are at least
\frac{n^{2}}{2}>\binom{n}{2}
pairs of vertices at distance
2
in
G
, which is a contradiction.
∎

For self-centric graphs (graphs with
diam=rad
), e.g. vertex-transitive graphs, one can also exclude the diameter to be
3.

Proposition 5
.

A graph
G
of order
n>1
with
diam(G)=rad(G)=3
has at least one vertex
v
for which either
G\setminus v
is disconnected, or
W(G\setminus v)<W(G).

Proof.

Assume
G
has connectivity at least two, i.e.,
G\setminus v
is connected for every
v\in V(G)
.

Let
v\in V(G)
and let
v^{\prime}
be an antipodal vertex, that is,
d(v,v^{\prime})=3.

If
a,b
are two vertices with
d(a,b)=d(a,v)+d(v,b)=2
, we note that
d_{G_{v}}(a,b)\leq d_{G_{v}}(a,v^{\prime})+d_{G_{v}}(b,v^{\prime})\leq 2\cdot 3=6.

If
a,b
are two vertices with
d(a,b)=3
,
d(a,v)=1
and
d(v,b)=2
,
we can consider a shortest path between
a
and
v^{\prime}
and let
w
be the neighbour herein of
v^{\prime}.

Then
d_{G_{v}}(a,b)\leq d_{G_{v}}(a,w)+d_{G_{v}}(b,w)\leq 2+3=5.

Assume there are
x
pairs of vertices at distance
2
, and
y
pairs of vertices at distance
3
.
If
d(a,b)=2,
there is at most one vertex
v
for which
d_{G_{v}}(a,b)>d(a,b)
and the increase is at most
6-2=4
.
If
d(a,b)=3,
there are at most two vertices whose removal increase the distance between
a
and
b
, hereby the increase is bounded by
5-3=2.

The total increase,
\sum_{u,u^{\prime}\in V\setminus v}\left(d_{G_{v}}(u,u^{\prime})-d_{G}(u,u^{\prime})\right),
is thus bounded by
4(x+y).

On the other hand, the sum of the transmissions over all vertices, which equals
2W(G)
, is strictly above
2(2x+3y).

As such, there is a vertex
v
for which
W(G\setminus v)<W(G).

∎

Proposition 6
.

A graph
G
for which
W(G)\leq W(G_{v})
for every
v\in V(G)
is twin-free. More generally, there are no
v,v^{\prime}\in G
for which
N(v)\subseteq N(v^{\prime}).

Proof.

If not, for every
2
vertices
x,y\in G_{v}=G\setminus v
for which there exists a shortest path in
G
using
v
, there is also one which uses
v^{\prime}.

Thus
d_{G}(x,y)=d_{G_{v}}(x,y)
for every
x,y\in G_{v}
, implying that
W(G)>W(G_{v})
. ∎

A conditional construction for partial Šoltés’ graphs

An

-Šoltés’ graph, is a graph for which at least an
\alpha
-fraction of its vertices are Šoltés’ vertices.
In

There exists a modified quartic (

Note that the order of

For a fixed edge

For every

Noting that

we finally construct a graph

This gives a

Next, we prove that there are counterexamples to some of the most natural conjectures for the variant of

Proposition 11
.

There are non-regular graphs
G
and values
z

W(G)-W(G\setminus v)=z
for every
v\in V(G).

Hereby the number of orbits or
\Delta-\delta
can be unbounded.

Proof.

Our first example consists of a circular ladder
CL_{2k+4}
(two cycles
C_{2k+4}
connected with a perfect matching), for which we add replace two opposite
C_{4}
s by
K_{4}

4

k=1

\mathopen{}\left\lceil\frac{k}{2}\right\rceil\mathclose{}+1
many vertex orbits.
The initial circular ladder is a planar graph, which can be drawn with an inner- and outer cycle
C_{2k+4}
.
Removing the edges of the two
K_{4}
s, we would obtain two components. We call the vertices of these components the left and the right vertices.
The transmission for all vertices is the same.
For any left vertex of the outer cycle, the sum towards the other vertices on the outer cycle, is fixed (no shortest paths use vertices at the inner cycle).
The ones to the inner cycle are within the same distance as their copies on the outer cycle, except that for the left ones the distance is one larger.
Thus
\sigma(v)=\mathopen{}\left\lfloor\frac{(2k+4)^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor\mathclose{}+(k+2).

Let the outer left vertices be
v_{0}
(a
4
-vertex) up to
v_{k+1}.

Let the outer right vertices be
w_{0}\ldots w_{k+1}
in a symmetric way.
Let
v^{\prime}_{i}
and
w^{\prime}_{i}
denote the inner vertices.
Now removing the vertex
v_{i}
, implies that the distance between
v_{j}
and
v_{m}
where
0\leq j<i<m\leq k+1
increases by two.
The distance between
v_{j}
and
w_{m}
(or
w^{\prime}_{m}
) increases by one, if
j+m>k+1
and
j<i
.
Analogously for
v_{m}
and
w_{j}
when
m>i.

This implies that the sum of increases in the distance equals

2i(k+1-i)+2\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}j+2\sum_{j=1}^{k-i}=k^{2}+k.

Since both the transmission and difference in distances is independent of
i
, and by symmetry between left and right, as well as inner and outer, we conclude.

Now we consider a second family, where the irregularity can be arbirarily large, i.e.
\Delta(G)-\delta(G)=k

5
Conclusion

The problem of Šoltés, due to its charming simplicity, has intrigued many people and resulted in over a
100
citing papers in the past.
In this paper, we gave indications why some of the current conjectures were not well-founded and may be false.
Furthermore, the research lead to the following key question.

Question 13
.

Are there infinitely many negative-Šoltés’ graphs?

We conjecture that if this question has an affirmative answer
, for every
z\in\mathbb{Z},
there are infinitely many graphs
G
for which
W(G)-W(G\setminus v)=z
for every
v\in V(G)
and among them, there are also non-vertex-transitive ones.
As an analogy, we refer to the conjecture of Heath-Brown on solutions of the Diophantine equation
x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}=k
, which says that there are infinitely many solutions whenever
k\not\equiv\pm 4\pmod{9}
(i.e., when there is no simple reason of non-existence). Also for this somewhat computationally simpler question, it has taken a while to find large solutions
(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
for some values
k
, see e.g.
[
3
]
.

If there is a value
n_{0}
such that every graph
G
of order
n\geq n_{0}
satisfies
W(G)>W(G\setminus v)
for
v\in V(G)
, (no negative-Šoltés’ graph exists with order at least
n_{0}
), we know that
n_{0}
is much larger than
10^{4}
by
[
4
, doc.
arcgraph10000
]
.
Under the existence of
n_{0}
, addressing the problem of Šoltés will need ideas to determine or exclude the Šoltés’ graphs with order bounded by
n_{0}.

When
Question

13
has a negative answer, the number of solutions for
Problem

1
may be approximately increasing over positive
z
.

Related to
Question

13
, one can also ask the extremal question on a good lower bound for
W(G)-W(G\setminus v)
for vertex-transitive graphs of order
n
and degree
d
.
Checking the census on cubic vertex-transitive graphs up to
1280
vertices
[
12
]
, we note that there are only a few dozens of negative-Šoltés’ graph among them and all of these are arc-graphs, giving a direction for the extremal question.
Hereby we formulate the following related question for general graphs.

Question 14
.

A graph
G
for which
W(G)\leq W(G\setminus v)
for all
v\in V(G)
has minimum degree upper bounded by
7?

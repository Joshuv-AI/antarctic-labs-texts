Bi-Hamiltonian Systems in (2+1) and Higher Dimensions Defined by Novikov Algebras
Błażej M. SZABLIKOWSKI

Abstract
The results from the article [Strachan I.A.B., Szablikowski B.M., Stud. Appl. Math. 133 (2014), 84–117] are extended over consideration of central extensions allowing the introducing of additional independent variables. Algebraic conditions associated to the first-order central extension with respect to additional independent variables are derived. As result ( 2 + 1 ) (2+1) - and, in principle, higher-dimensional multicomponent bi-Hamiltonian systems are constructed. Necessary classification of the central extensions for low-dimensional Novikov algebras is performed and the theory is illustrated by significant ( 2 + 1 ) (2+1) - and ( 3 + 1 ) (3+1) -dimensional examples.

Introduction

In the article
[
21
]
we have presented construction of
(1+1)
-dimensional integrable bi-Hamiltonian systems associated with Novikov algebras. These systems are multicomponent generalizations of the Camassa–Holm equation
[
8
]
and can be interpreted as Euler equations on the respective centrally extended Lie algebras. On the other hand, the central extension procedure is one of the most effective methods allowing for the construction of
(2+1)
analogs of
(1+1)
-dimensional systems. However, such procedure is not always possible. All the more, there is very limited number of approaches allowing for the systematic construction of
(3+1)
- and higher-dimensional integrable systems, see the recent survey
[
6
]
and references therein.

Novikov algebras naturally appear in the context of the homogeneous first-order Hamiltonian operators
[
4
,
12
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{ij}=\big(b^{ij}_{k}+b^{ji}_{k}\big)u^{k}\partial_{x}+b^{ij}_{k}u^{k}_{x},

(1.1)

which are a special case of Dubrovin–Novikov operators of hydrodynamic type
[
9
,
10

. The algebraic conditions for (
1.1
) to be Hamiltonian means that

are structure constants of a Novikov algebra. The Hamiltonian operators (
1.1

[
21
]
and the recent works
[
19
,
20
,
22
]
.

Novikov algebras also play significant role in the theory of multi-dimensional Hamiltonian operators of Dubrovin–Novikov type:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{ij}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{D}\big[g^{ij\alpha}({\bm{u}})\partial_{x^{\alpha}}+b^{ij\alpha}_{k}({\bm{u}})u^{k}_{x^{\alpha}}\big],\qquad{\bm{u}}=\big(u^{1},\ldots,u^{n}\big),

(1.2)

which in the non-degenerate case,
\det g^{ij\alpha}({\bm{u}})\neq 0
, can be reduced by a change of dependent variables to a linear form,
1

1

One-dimensional Dubrovin–Novikov operator (

where

\displaystyle g^{ij\alpha}({\bm{u}})=\big(b^{ij\alpha}_{k}+b^{ji\alpha}_{k}\big)u^{k}+g^{ij\alpha}_{0},\qquad b^{ij\alpha}_{k}({\bm{u}})=b^{ij\alpha}_{k}=\text{const},\qquad g^{ij\alpha}_{0}=\text{const},

[

The key result of this paper is the use of
(N+1)
-dimensional pencils

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{ij}_{\lambda}=\mathcal{P}^{ij}+\mathcal{P}^{ij}_{1}+\lambda\mathcal{P}^{ij}_{0},\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{C},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{ij}_{\mu}=g^{ij}_{\mu}\partial_{x}+f^{ij}_{\mu}\partial^{2}_{x}+h^{ij}_{\mu}\partial_{x}^{3}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\eta^{ij}_{\mu,\alpha}\partial_{y^{\alpha}},\qquad\mu=0,1,

Further, we show that the Killing forms must practically satisfy the same algebraic conditions. Subsequently, we classify such central extensions with respect to low-dimensional Novikov algebras, that have been originally classified in the articles

(2+1)

3
Central extensions

Consider a bilinear form
g\colon\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, then we will say that:

•

the form satisfies the quasi-Frobenius condition if

\displaystyle g({\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\cdot{\bm{c}})=g({\bm{a}}\cdot{\bm{c}},{\bm{b}}),

•

the form satisfies the cyclic condition if

\displaystyle g({\bm{a}},{\bm{b}}\cdot{\bm{c}})+g({\bm{b}},{\bm{c}}\cdot{\bm{a}})+g({\bm{c}},{\bm{a}}\cdot{\bm{b}})=0,

•

the form is totally symmetric if the trilinear form

\displaystyle c({\bm{a}},{\bm{b}},{\bm{c}}):=g({\bm{a}}\cdot{\bm{b}},{\bm{c}})

is symmetric with respect to all arguments.

We are interested in the central extensions of the Lie algebra
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{A}}
. This means that on the direct sum
\widehat{\mathscr{L}}_{\mathbb{A}}\equiv{\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{A}}}\oplus\mathbb{C}
there is a Lie bracket of the form

\displaystyle[\![{\bm{u}}\oplus\alpha,{\bm{v}}\oplus\beta]\!]:=[\![{\bm{u}},{\bm{v}}]\!]\oplus\omega({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}}),

where
\omega\colon{\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{A}}}\times{\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{A}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a
2
-cocycle. This means that
\omega
is skew-symmetric,

\displaystyle\omega({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}})=-\omega({\bm{v}},{\bm{u}}),

and it satisfies the cyclic condition

\displaystyle\omega\bigl([\![{\bm{u}},{\bm{v}}]\!],{\bm{w}}\bigr)+\omega\bigl([\![{\bm{v}},{\bm{w}}]\!],{\bm{u}}\bigr)+\omega\bigl([\![{\bm{w}},{\bm{u}}]\!],{\bm{v}}\bigr)=0.

2

4
Killing form

The Killing form on
{\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{A}}}
is a symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form
\mathcal{K}\colon{\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{A}}}\times{\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{A}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
which is
ad
-invariant:

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\bigl([\![{\bm{u}},{\bm{w}}]\!],{\bm{v}}\bigr)+\mathcal{K}\bigl({\bm{w}},[\![{\bm{u}},{\bm{v}}]\!]\bigr)=0.

Assume that
\mathcal{K}
is defined by a bilinear form
\kappa\colon\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\bigl({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}}\bigr):=\int_{\Omega}\kappa({\bm{u}},{\bm{v}}){\rm d}\bm{x}\equiv\langle\hat{\kappa}{\bm{u}},{\bm{v}}\rangle,

(4.1)

where
\hat{\kappa}\colon\mathbb{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{*}
. If there exists a Killing form then the dual Lie algebra
{\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{A}}}^{*}
can be identified
with
{\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{A}}}
and the co-adjoint action can be identified with the adjoint action:

\displaystyle\ad^{*}_{\bm{u}}\bm{\xi}\equiv\hat{\kappa}[\![{\bm{u}},{\bm{w}}]\!],\qquad\bm{\xi}\equiv\hat{\kappa}{\bm{w}},\qquad\langle\ad_{\bm{u}}^{*}\bm{\xi},{\bm{v}}\rangle:=-\langle\bm{\xi},[\![{\bm{u}},{\bm{v}}]\!]\rangle,

where
\bm{\xi}\in{\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{A}}}^{*}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

A symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form
\kappa\colon\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
generates the Killing form (
4.1
) if and only if the form
\kappa
satisfies the cyclic and quasi-Frobenius conditions.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}\bigl([\![{\bm{u}},{\bm{w}}]\!],{\bm{u}}\bigr)+\mathcal{K}\bigl({\bm{w}},[\![{\bm{u}},{\bm{v}}]\!]\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad{}=\int_{\Omega}\big[\kappa({\bm{u}}_{x}\cdot{\bm{w}}-{\bm{w}}_{x}\cdot{\bm{u}},{\bm{v}})+\kappa({\bm{w}},{\bm{u}}_{x}\cdot{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{x}\cdot{\bm{u}})\big]{\rm d}\bm{x}

\displaystyle\quad{}=\int_{\Omega}\big[\kappa({\bm{u}}_{x}\cdot{\bm{w}},{\bm{v}})+\kappa({\bm{w}}\cdot{\bm{u}}_{x},{\bm{v}})+\kappa({\bm{w}}\cdot{\bm{u}},{\bm{v}}_{x})+\kappa({\bm{w}},{\bm{u}}_{x}\cdot{\bm{v}})-\kappa({\bm{w}},{\bm{v}}_{x}\cdot{\bm{u}})\big]{\rm d}\bm{x}=0.

Collecting terms with respect to functionally independent variables we obtain two conditions

\displaystyle\kappa({\bm{a}}\cdot{\bm{c}},{\bm{b}})+\kappa({\bm{c}}\cdot{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}})+\kappa({\bm{c}},{\bm{a}}\cdot{\bm{b}})=0

and

\displaystyle\kappa({\bm{c}}\cdot{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}})=\kappa({\bm{c}},{\bm{b}}\cdot{\bm{a}}).

Taking into account the symmetry of the form
\kappa
we get the conditions from the theorem.
∎

We see that the algebraic conditions for the Killing forms (
4.1
) are the same as for the
2
-cocycles (
3.2
) with non-degenerate
\eta

2
-cocycle (
3.2

4.1

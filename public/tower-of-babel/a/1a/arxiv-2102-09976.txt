On the existence of distributional potentials
Jürgen Voigt

Abstract
We present proofs for the existence of distributional potentials F ∈ 𝒟 ′ ​ ( Ω ) F\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega) for distributional vector fields G ∈ 𝒟 ′ ​ ( Ω ) n G\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)^{n} , i.e. grad ⁡ F = G \operatorname{grad}F=G , where Ω \Omega is an open subset of ℝ n \mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n} . The hypothesis in these proofs is the compatibility condition ∂ j G k = ∂ k G j \partial_{j}G_{k}=\partial_{k}G_{j} for all j , k ∈ { 1 , … , n } j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\} , if Ω \Omega is simply connected, and a stronger condition in the general case. A key tool in our treatment is the Bogovskiĭ formula, assigning vector fields v ∈ 𝒟 ​ ( Ω ) n v\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)^{n} satisfying div ⁡ v = φ \operatorname{div}v=\varphi to functions φ ∈ 𝒟 ⁡ ( Ω ) \varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega) with ∫ φ ( x ) d x = 0 \int\varphi(x)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}x=0 . The results are applied to properties of Hilbert spaces of functions occurring in the treatment of the Stokes operator and the Navier–Stokes equations. MSC 2010: 46F10, 46E35 Keywords: Distribution, Poincaré’s lemma, de Rham’s theorem, Bogovskiĭ formula, Stokes operator



Introduction

The most elementary version of Poincaré’s lemma is the statement that, given a
C^{1}
-vector field
v\colon\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n}\to\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n}
satisfying
\partial_{j}v_{k}=\partial_{k}v_{j}
for all

j,k=1,\dots,n
, there exists a potential
p\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})
for
v
, i.e.
\operatorname{grad}p=v
. The main issue of this paper is to present proofs of
the following two distributional versions of this kind of existence theorem.

0.1 Theorem
.

(‘de Rham style’)
Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n}
be open and connected. Let
G=(G_{1},\ldots,G_{n})\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)^{n}
, and suppose that

\langle G,\varphi\rangle=0\qquad(\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)^{n},\ \operatorname{div}\varphi=0).

(0.1)

Then there exists a distribution
F\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
such that
\operatorname{grad}F=G
. If
n\geqslant 2
and
G
has compact support, then
F
can be chosen with compact support.

0.2 Theorem
.

(‘Poincaré style’)
Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n}
be open and simply connected. Let
G\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)^{n}
be such that
\partial_{j}G_{k}=\partial_{k}G_{j}
for all
j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Then there exists
F\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
such that
\operatorname{grad}F=G
.

Proofs of these theorems have been provided by S. Mardare
[
10
, Theorems 4.1 and 2.1]
. In his paper he explains that “to this day, there is no proof, to the best knowledge of the author”​, of Theorem
0.2
in the existing literature;
and concerning Theorem
0.1
, the proof given in
[
12
, § 22, Theorem 17’]
is said to require “an important prerequisite about chains and flows on differential manifolds”​. For more motivation and background we refer to
[
10
]
. A proof of the local part of Theorem
0.2
, on the basis of de Rham’s regularisation, is given in
[
11
, Corollary 3.6(i)]
.

The author was intrigued by these theorems as well
as motivated by related problems connected with the Stokes operator, and this led to proofs of the theorems which are quite different from those presented in
[
11
,
10
]
.
Let us comment briefly on some differences between Mardare’s and our proofs of Theorem
0.2
, which is Theorem 2.1 in
[
10
]
. Given
G\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)^{n}
as in Theorem
0.2
, Mardare first carries out the local part of the proof on sets
\omega=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(a_{j},b_{j})
by presenting – following Schwartz
[
13
, chap. II, § 6]
– a formula for the solution
F\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\omega)
. Our approach is to deal with the local part on bounded open sets that are star-shaped with respect to an open ball and to approximate
G
by
C^{\infty}
-vector fields satisfying the compatibility conditions. In both proofs, the global part is achieved by a homotopy argument; our proof is arranged in a way to permit the application of the divergence theorem on the parameter set
[0,1]^{2}
of the homotopy.

The proofs of Theorems
0.1
and
0.2
are the content of Sections
1
and
2
, respectively.
In the remaining part of the paper we treat applications of the theorems stated above to Sobolev space versions of the theorems. In these results the task is finding potentials in
L_{2}(\Omega)
for suitable vector fields in
H^{-1}(\Omega)^{n}
. Here, the author was motivated, amongst others, by the recent paper
[
1
]
.

1
Proof of de Rham’s theorem

In the proof of Theorem
0.1
we will need the Bogovskiĭ operator which we discuss next. Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n}
be an open set,
\Omega
star-shaped with respect to every point of an open ball
B(x^{0},r_{0})\subseteq\Omega
. Let
\rho\in{\displaystyle C_{\raise 0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\textnormal{c}}$}}^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})_{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigl\{\varphi\in{\displaystyle C_{\raise 0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\textnormal{c}}$}}^{\infty}}(\Omega){;}\;\varphi\geqslant 0\bigr\}
with
\operatorname{spt}\rho\subseteq B(x^{0},r_{0})
and
\int\rho(x)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}x=1
. For
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)
we define
B\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)^{n}
by

B\varphi(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(y)(x-y)\int_{1}^{\infty}\rho(y+r(x-y))r^{n-1}\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}r\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}y\qquad(x\in\Omega).

It is not too difficult to show that indeed
B\varphi
belongs to
C^{\infty}(\Omega)^{n}
, and

\operatorname{spt}B\varphi\subseteq\bigl\{\lambda z_{1}+(1-\lambda)z_{2}{;}\;z_{1}\in\operatorname{spt}\varphi,\ z_{2}\in\operatorname{spt}\rho,\ 0\leqslant\lambda\leqslant 1\bigr\},

(1.1)

a compact subset of
\Omega
.

1.1 Remark
.

The linear mapping
B\colon\mathcal{D}(\Omega)\to\mathcal{D}(\Omega)^{n}
is the
Bogovskiĭ operator
; we refer to
[
3
]
for its first appearance. It has the important property that

\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}x=0
implies that
\operatorname{div}\mkern-1.5muB\varphi=\varphi
; see Remark
2.3
below.
This property will also be used in the less explicit version that for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)
with
\int\mkern-1.5mu\varphi(x)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}x=0
there exists a vector field
\Phi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)^{n}
with the property

\operatorname{div}\Phi=\varphi
.
(The reader should be aware of the fact that the Bogovskiĭ operator depends on the function
\rho
; so the use of the definite article ‘the’ might be somewhat misleading.)

1.2 Lemma
.

The operator
B\colon\mathcal{D}(\Omega)\to\mathcal{D}(\Omega)^{n}
is continuous with respect to the standard topologies.

Proof.

The function
B\varphi
can be rewritten as

B\varphi(x)=\int_{z\in\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.24582pt^{n}}\varphi(x-z)\frac{z}{|z|^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\bigl(x+s\frac{z}{|z|}\bigr)(s+|z|)^{n-1}\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}s\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}z

(where
\rho
and
\varphi
are considered as functions in
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})
). This shows that derivatives of
B\varphi
can be estimated by derivatives of
\varphi
and
\rho
of the same and lower order. This fact together with the support property (
1.1
) shows the assertion.
∎

Proof of Theorem
0.1
.

(i) In this part of the proof we suppose that
\Omega
is star-shaped with respect to an open ball
B(x^{0},r)\subseteq\Omega
. Let
\rho\in{\displaystyle C_{\raise 0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\textnormal{c}}$}}^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})_{+}
,

\operatorname{spt}\rho\subseteq B(x^{0},r)
and
\int\rho(x)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}x=1
, and let
B\colon\mathcal{D}(\Omega)\to\mathcal{D}(\Omega)^{n}
be the corresponding Bogovskiĭ operator. For

\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)
we define

\langle F,\varphi\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-\langle G,B\varphi\rangle.

Then Lemma
1.2
implies that
F\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
.

In order to show that
\operatorname{grad}F=G
let
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)
,
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Then

\int_{\Omega}\partial_{j}\varphi(x)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}x=0
, hence
\operatorname{div}(B\partial_{j}\varphi-\varphi e_{j})=\partial_{j}\varphi-\partial_{j}\varphi=0
(where
e_{j}
denotes the
j
-th unit vector), and hypothesis (
0.1
) implies

\langle G_{j},\varphi\rangle=\langle G,\varphi e_{j}\rangle=\langle G,B\partial_{j}\varphi\rangle=-\langle F,\partial_{j}\varphi\rangle=\langle\partial_{j}F,\varphi\rangle.

This shows that
G=\operatorname{grad}F
.

(ii) For the proof of the general case let
\bigl(B(x^{k},r_{k})\bigr)_{k\in N}
be a countable covering of
\Omega
by open balls contained in
\Omega
, with
1\in N\subseteq\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
. To ease notation we put
\Omega_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=B(x^{k},r_{k})
(
k\in N
). For each
k\in N
let
\rho_{k}\in{\displaystyle C_{\raise 0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\textnormal{c}}$}}^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})_{+}
with
\operatorname{spt}\rho_{k}\subseteq\Omega_{k}
,

\int\rho_{k}(x)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}x=1
, and let
B_{k}\colon\mathcal{D}(\Omega_{k})\to\mathcal{D}(\Omega_{k})^{n}
be the corresponding Bogovskiĭ operator.

For
k\in N
and some
c_{k}\in\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
we define
F_{k}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega_{k})
by

\langle F_{k},\varphi\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=c_{k}{\textstyle\int}\varphi(x)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}x-\langle G,B_{k}\varphi\rangle\qquad(\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega_{k})).

Then part (i) shows that
\operatorname{grad}F_{k}=G
on
\Omega_{k}
. We are going to show that the constants
c_{k}
can be chosen such that the family
(F_{k})_{k\in N}
of distributions is consistent.

For
k\in N
we observe that there exists a function
\Phi_{k}\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)^{n}
such that
\operatorname{div}\Phi_{k}=\rho_{1}-\rho_{k}
; this can be seen by connecting
\Omega_{1}
with
\Omega_{k}
by a finite chain of consecutively intersecting open balls and applying Remark
1.1
repeatedly. With this function we choose
c_{k}
in such a way that
\left\langle F_{k},\rho_{k}\right\rangle=\left\langle G,\Phi_{k}\right\rangle
; then

\left\langle F_{k},\varphi\right\rangle=\left\langle F_{k},\rho_{k}+\operatorname{div}B_{k}(\varphi-\rho_{k})\right\rangle=\left\langle G,\Phi_{k}-B_{k}(\varphi-\rho_{k})\right\rangle

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega_{k})
with
\int\varphi(x)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}x=1
.

In order to show that the family
(F_{k})_{k\in N}
is consistent, let
k,l\in N
be such that
\Omega_{k}\cap\Omega_{l}\neq\varnothing
, and let
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)
with
\operatorname{spt}\varphi\subseteq\Omega_{k}\cap\Omega_{l}
,
\int\varphi(x)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}x=1
. Then

\left\langle F_{k},\varphi\right\rangle-\left\langle F_{l},\varphi\right\rangle=\left\langle G,\Phi_{k}-B_{k}(\varphi-\rho_{k})-\Phi_{l}+B_{l}(\varphi-\rho_{l})\right\rangle=0,

because the divergence of the function to which
G
is applied turns out to be
(\rho_{1}-\rho_{k})-(\varphi-\rho_{k})-(\rho_{1}-\rho_{l})+(\varphi-\rho_{l})=0
.
It follows that
\left\langle F_{k},\varphi\right\rangle=\left\langle F_{l},\varphi\right\rangle
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega_{k}\cap\Omega_{l})
.

We conclude that the family
(F_{k})_{k\in N}
composes to a distribution
F\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
satisfying
\operatorname{grad}F=G
.

(iii) Now suppose that
n\geqslant 2
and that
G
has compact support. There exists
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)
with
\psi=1
in a neighbourhood of the support of
G
. Then

\langle\widehat{G},\varphi\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\langle G,\psi\varphi\rangle\qquad(\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})^{n})

defines an extension of
G
to
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})^{n}
, satisfying
\partial_{j}\widehat{G}_{k}=\partial_{k}\widehat{G}_{j}
for all
j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. According to part (ii) above, there exists
\widehat{F}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})
such that
\operatorname{grad}\widehat{F}=\widehat{G}
. Denote by
\Omega_{\infty}
the unbounded component of
\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n}\setminus\operatorname{spt}G
. Then
\operatorname{grad}\widehat{F}=0
on the
connected open set
\Omega_{\infty}
; hence there exists
c\in\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
such that
\widehat{F}=c

on
\Omega_{\infty}
. Then the restriction of
\widehat{F}-c
to
\mathcal{D}(\Omega)
is as asserted.
∎

2
Proof of the distributional version of Poincaré’s lemma

We start with a property that will be needed in the proof of Theorem
0.2
.

2.1 Lemma
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n}
be open. Let
v\in C^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})
be a vector field satisfying

\partial_{j}v_{k}=\partial_{k}v_{j}\qquad(j,k=1,\dots,n).

Let
\gamma,\widetilde{\gamma}\colon[0,1]\to\Omega
be
C^{1}
-paths with
\gamma(0)=\widetilde{\gamma}(0)=\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}x^{0}
,
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(0)=0
,
\gamma(1)=\widetilde{\gamma}(1)=\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}x^{1}
,
\gamma^{\prime}(1)=\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(1)=0
.
Suppose that
\Gamma\colon[0,1]^{2}\to\Omega
is an FEP-homotopy between
\gamma
and
\widetilde{\gamma}
, i.e.,
\Gamma
is continuous,
\Gamma(\cdot,0)=\gamma
,
\Gamma(\cdot,1)=\widetilde{\gamma}
,
\Gamma(0,\cdot)=x^{0}
,
\Gamma(1,\cdot)=x^{1}
. (FEP stands for ‘fixed end points’.) Then

\int_{\gamma}v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{0}^{1}v(\gamma(s))\cdot\gamma^{\prime}(s)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{1}v(\widetilde{\gamma}(s))\cdot\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(s)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\widetilde{\gamma}}v.

Proof.

(i) In this step we suppose additionally that
\Gamma
is twice continuously differentiable. We define the vector field
w\colon[0,1]^{2}\to\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{2}
,

w(s,t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigl(v(\Gamma(s,t))\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Gamma(s,t),-v(\Gamma(s,t))\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Gamma(s,t)\bigr).

Then, using the hypothesis on
v
, one easily obtains
\operatorname{div}w=0
; hence the divergence theorem yields

\int_{\partial[0,1]^{2}}w(s,t)\cdot\nu(s,t)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(s,t)=0
,
where
\nu
is the outer unit normal and
\sigma
the surface measure. The integrals over the lines with
s=0
and
s=1
vanish, because

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Gamma(s,t)=0
for
s=0
,
s=1
and all
t\in[0,1]
. Hence the remaining integrals yield

\int_{0}^{1}v(\Gamma(s,0))\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Gamma(s,0)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{0}^{1}v(\Gamma(s,1))\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\Gamma(s,1)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}s=0,

which is just the asserted equality.

(ii) In order to apply step (i) by smoothing the given homotopy
\Gamma
we first ‘contract and extend’ it as follows. We define the continuous function

\alpha\colon[-1/4,5/4]\to[0,1]
by
\alpha\vphantom{f}\mskip 1.0mu\vrule\mskip 2.0mu_{[-1/4,1/4]}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=0
,
\alpha\vphantom{f}\mskip 1.0mu\vrule\mskip 2.0mu_{[3/4,5/4]}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1
,
\alpha\vphantom{f}\mskip 1.0mu\vrule\mskip 2.0mu_{[1/4,3/4]}
affine linear,
and then we put
\widetilde{\Gamma}\colon[-1/4,5/4]^{2}\to\Omega
,
\widetilde{\Gamma}(s,t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Gamma(\alpha(s),\alpha(t))
.
It is easy to see that then

\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}(\cdot,0)\vphantom{f}\mskip 1.0mu\vrule\mskip 2.0mu_{[0,1]}}v=\int_{\gamma}v\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}(\cdot,1)\vphantom{f}\mskip 1.0mu\vrule\mskip 2.0mu_{[0,1]}}v=\int_{\widetilde{\gamma}}v.

(2.1)

Observe that

\widetilde{\Gamma}(s,\cdot)\vphantom{f}\mskip 1.0mu\vrule\mskip 2.0mu_{[-1/4,1/4]}=\widetilde{\Gamma}(s,0),\quad\widetilde{\Gamma}(s,\cdot)\vphantom{f}\mskip 1.0mu\vrule\mskip 2.0mu_{[3/4,5/4]}=\widetilde{\Gamma}(s,1)\qquad(s\in[-1/4,,5/4])

and

\widetilde{\Gamma}\vphantom{f}\mskip 1.0mu\vrule\mskip 2.0mu_{[-1/4,1/4]\times[-1/4,5/4]}=x^{0},\quad\widetilde{\Gamma}\vphantom{f}\mskip 1.0mu\vrule\mskip 2.0mu_{[3/4,5/4]\times[-1/4,5/4]}=x^{1}.

Now let
(\rho_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.24582pt}
be a sequence in
{\displaystyle C_{\raise 0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\textnormal{c}}$}}^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt)_{+}
,
\operatorname{spt}\rho_{k}\subseteq[-1/k,1/k]
,
\int\rho_{k}(x)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}x=1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt
, and put

\widetilde{\Gamma}_{k}(s,t)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{-1/4}^{5/4}\int_{-1/4}^{5/4}\widetilde{\Gamma}(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rho_{k}(s-s^{\prime})\rho_{k}(t-t^{\prime})\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}\qquad(s,t\in[0,1],\ k\geqslant 4).

The properties mentioned above imply that
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{k}(\cdot,j)=\widetilde{\Gamma}(\cdot,j)*\rho_{k}
and
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{k}(j,\cdot)=\gamma(j)

on
[0,1]
for
j=0,1
and all
k\geqslant 4
. Using the uniform continuity of
\widetilde{\Gamma}
one shows that there exists
k_{0}\geqslant 4
such that

\widetilde{\Gamma}_{k}([0,1]^{2})\subseteq\Omega
for all
k\geqslant k_{0}
.

Having established these properties, we can apply part (i) of the proof to conclude that

\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{k}(\cdot,0)}v=\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{k}(\cdot,1)}v\qquad(k\geqslant k_{0}).

(2.2)

From
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{k}(\cdot,j)=\widetilde{\Gamma}(\cdot,j)*\rho_{k}\to\widetilde{\Gamma}(\cdot,j)
in
C^{1}([0,1];\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})
(
k\to\infty
) it follows that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}_{k}(\cdot,j)}v=\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}(\cdot,j)\vphantom{f}\mskip 1.0mu\vrule\mskip 2.0mu_{[0,1]}}v\qquad(j=0,1).

(2.3)

Combining (
2.2
), (
2.3
) and (
2.1
) we obtain the assertion of the lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
0.2
.

(i) In this part we prove the theorem for the case that
\Omega
is bounded and star-shaped with respect to an open ball
B(x^{0},r)\subseteq\Omega
. Let

\rho\in{\displaystyle C_{\raise 0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\textnormal{c}}$}}^{\infty}}(\Omega)_{+}
with
\operatorname{spt}\rho\subseteq B(x^{0},r)
and
\int\rho(x)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}x=1
, and let
B
be the corresponding Bogovskiĭ operator.
The transpose of
B\colon\mathcal{D}(\Omega)\to\mathcal{D}(\Omega)^{n}
is the linear mapping
B^{t}\colon\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)^{n}\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
, given by

\langle B^{t}G,\varphi\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\langle G,B\varphi\rangle\qquad(\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega),\ G\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)^{n}),

and
B^{t}
is continuous with respect to the standard topologies.

The restriction of
-B^{t}
to
C^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})
is the operator
A\colon C^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})\to C^{\infty}(\Omega)
, given by

Av(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{\Omega}\rho(y)\int_{0}^{1}v(sy+(1-s)x)\cdot(x-y)\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}s\mathclose{}\,\mathrm{d}y;

(2.4)

see Remark
2.3
.
It has the property that
\operatorname{grad}Av=v
for all

v\in C^{\infty}_{\gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigl\{u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n}){;}\;\partial_{j}u_{k}=\partial_{k}u_{j}\ (j,k=1,\dots,n)\bigr\}.

Now let
G\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)^{n}
with
\partial_{j}G_{k}=\partial_{k}G_{j}
for all
j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. We show that
G
can be approximated by vector fields
v\in C^{\infty}_{\gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})
.
Without loss of generality we assume that
x^{0}
(the centre of the ball mentioned initially) is the origin.
In a first step
we introduce distributions
G^{\lambda}
for
\lambda\in(1,\infty)
, defined on
\lambda\Omega
by

\langle G^{\lambda},\varphi\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigl\langle G,\varphi\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\lambda}\,\cdot\,\bigr)\bigr\rangle\qquad(\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\lambda\Omega)^{n}).

It is easy to see that
G^{\lambda}
satisfies the compatibility condition and that for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)^{n}
one obtains
\bigl\langle G^{\lambda},\varphi\bigr\rangle\to\langle G,\varphi\rangle
as
\lambda\to 1
.

In order to approximate
G^{\lambda}
from
C^{\infty}_{\gamma}(\Omega;\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})
we fix
\lambda>1
and observe that
\lambda\Omega
is an open neighbourhood of the compact set
\overline{\Omega}
, due to the strict
star-shapedness of
\Omega
. Let
(\rho_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}\nonscript\hskip 0.24582pt}
be a
\delta
-sequence in
{\displaystyle C_{\raise 0.60275pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\textnormal{c}}$}}^{\infty}}(\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})_{+}
. Then, for large
l
, the function

x\mapsto v^{\lambda,l}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigl\langle G^{\lambda},\rho_{l}(\cdot-x)\bigr\rangle=\bigl(\bigl\langle G_{k}^{\lambda},\rho_{l}(\cdot-x)\bigr\rangle\bigr)_{k=1,\dots,n}

In this section we treat the existence of potentials in a Hilbert space context. Let

\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n}
by zero belongs to
H^{1}_{0,\sigma}(\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n};\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})
; we keep the notation
\varphi
for the extension. For
\lambda\in(1,\infty)
we note that
\varphi(\lambda\,\cdot)\in H^{1}_{0,\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})
has compact support
contained in
\lambda^{-1}\overline{\Omega}\subseteq\Omega
. Regularisation shows that
\varphi(\lambda\,\cdot)
is approximated by functions in
C^{\infty}_{{\textnormal{c}},\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})
; hence
\varphi(\lambda\,\cdot)\in H^{1}_{\sigma,0}(\Omega;\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})
. Taking
\lambda\to 1
we then obtain
\varphi\in H^{1}_{\sigma,0}(\Omega;\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})
. (This reasoning has also been used in
[
14
, proof of Lemma 9, p. 31]
.)

3.5 Corollary
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n}
be a connected bounded open set, and assume that there exists a covering
(\Omega_{j})_{j=1,\dots,m}
of
\Omega
by open sets

\Omega_{j}\subseteq\Omega
, where each
\Omega_{j}
is
star-shaped with respect to an open ball. Then property (

H
c

) holds for
\Omega
.

These assertions apply to any connected bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary.

Proof.

From Remark
3.4
(c) we know that the sets
\Omega_{j}
have property (

H
c

). Hence Proposition
3.3
implies the assertion.

If
\Omega
has Lipschitz boundary, then it is not difficult to
see that for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
there exists an open
neighbourhood
U_{x}
such that
U_{x}\cap\Omega
is star-shaped with respect to
the points of a ball in
U_{x}\cap\Omega
. This is obvious for
x\in\Omega
, and
for
x\in\partial\Omega
it results from the Lipschitz property of
\partial\Omega
. The compactness of

\overline{\Omega}
implies that there exists a finite open covering

(\Omega_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,m}
of
\Omega
as required in the first part of the corollary. (See also
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
.)
∎

4
A ‘weak version’ of Poincaré’s lemma

In the previous section we have seen how the distributional version of de Rham’s theorem can be applied in the
L_{2}
-context. Here we present an application of the distributional version of
Poincaré’s lemma.

4.1 Theorem
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n}
be a simply connected bounded open set satisfying property (

H
c

). Let
g\in H^{-1}(\Omega)^{n}
be such that
\partial_{j}g_{k}=\partial_{k}g_{j}
for all
j,k=1,\dots,n
(with derivatives in the sense of distributions).
Then there exists
f\in L_{2}(\Omega)
such that
\operatorname{grad}f=g
.

Proof.

Theorem
0.2
implies that there exists
F\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
such that
\operatorname{grad}F=g
. This shows that for all

\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{{\textnormal{c}},\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})
one obtains

\langle g,\varphi\rangle_{H^{-1},H^{1}_{0}}=\langle\operatorname{grad}F,\varphi\rangle=-\langle F,\operatorname{div}\varphi\rangle=0,

i.e.
g\in C^{\infty}_{{\textnormal{c}},\sigma}(\Omega;\mathbb{K}\nonscript\hskip 0.29999pt^{n})^{\textstyle\circ}
. Now property (

H
c

) implies that there exists
f\in L_{2}(\Omega)
such that
\operatorname{grad}f=g
.
∎

Recall from Corollary
3.5
that Theorem
4.1
applies to the case that
\Omega
is a simply connected bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary.
For this case, proofs of Theorem
4.1
are contained in
[
9
, proof of Theorem 2.1]
,
[
1
, Proof of Theorem 4.1]
, without the use of
our Theorem
0.2
.

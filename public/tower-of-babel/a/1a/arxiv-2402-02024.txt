An analogue of Kida’s formula for elliptic curves with additive reduction Thanks: ‡ (Corresponding author) email: anwesh@cmi.ac.in
Anwesh Ray ‡, Pratiksha Shingavekar

Abstract
. We study the Iwasawa theory of p p -primary Selmer groups of elliptic curves E E over a number field K K . Assume that E E has additive reduction at the primes of K K above p p . In this context, we prove that the Iwasawa invariants satisfy an analogue of the Riemann–Hurwitz formula. This generalizes a result of Hachimori and Matsuno. We apply our results to study rank stability questions for elliptic curves in prime cyclic extensions of ℚ \mathbb{Q} . These extensions are ordered by their absolute discriminant and we prove an asymptotic lower bound for the density of extensions in which the Iwasawa invariants as well as the rank of the elliptic curve is stable.

Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and historical context

Let
p
be a prime number and
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be the ring of
p
-adic integers. Iwasawa
[
Iwa73
]
studied growth patterns of
p
-primary parts of class numbers in certain infinite abelian Galois towers of number fields. Let
F
be a number field. Setting
\mu_{p^{\infty}}\subset\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
to be the
p
-primary roots of unity, we let
F(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
denote the Galois extension of
F
that is generated by
\mu_{p^{\infty}}
. There is a unique
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
F_{\operatorname{cyc}}/F
which is contained in
F(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
. This is called the
cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
of
F
and the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(F_{\operatorname{cyc}}/F)
is topologically isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. For a natural number
n
, we define
F_{n}
to be the subfield of
F_{\operatorname{cyc}}
such that
\operatorname{Gal}(F_{n}/F)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, one has the tower of Galois extensions

F=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset F_{2}\subset\cdots\subset F_{n}\subset F_{n+1}\subset\cdots\subset F_{\operatorname{cyc}}.

Let
e_{n}
be the exact power of
p
that divides the class number of
F_{n}
. Iwasawa proved that there exist invariants
\mu=\mu_{p}(F),\lambda=\lambda_{p}(F)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\nu=\nu_{p}(F)\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

e_{n}=p^{n}\mu+n\lambda+\nu,

for all large enough values of
n
.
Moreover, Iwasawa conjectured that the
\mu_{p}(F)=0
for all number fields
F
. The conjecture has been resolved for abelian extensions
F/\mathbb{Q}
by Ferrero and Washington
[
FW79
]
.

Let
K
be a number field and
L/K
be a finite Galois extension such that
\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
is a
p
-group. Kida
[
Kid80
]
showed that there is an explicit relationship between the Iwasawa
\mu
- and
\lambda
-invariants for
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K
and those for
L_{\operatorname{cyc}}/L
. This can be viewed as an analogue of the Riemann-Hurwitz formula for Iwasawa invariants. Iwasawa
[
Iwa81
, Theorem 6]
later proved a generalization of this result using Galois cohomology. Let
w
be a prime of
L_{\operatorname{cyc}}
and
v
be the prime of
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}
such that
w|v
. Set
e(w)
to denote the ramification index of
w
over
v
.
Let
U(L_{\operatorname{cyc}})
be the group of units of
L_{\operatorname{cyc}}
.

Theorem A

(
[
Iwa81
, Theorem 6]
)
.

Let
p
be a prime,
K
be a number field and
L/K
be a finite Galois extension such that
[L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K]
is a power of
p
.
Assume that
\mu_{p}(K)=0
. Then,
\mu_{p}(L)=0
and

\lambda_{p}(L)=[L_{\operatorname{cyc}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K_{\operatorname{cyc}}]\lambda_{p}(K)+\sum_{w}\left(e(w)-1\right)+(p-1)(h_{2}-h_{1}).

The sum is over all primes

of
L_{\operatorname{cyc}}
(above
v
in
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}
) not dividing
p
. The quantity

is the rank of the abelian group
H^{i}(L_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K_{\operatorname{cyc}},U(L_{\operatorname{cyc}}))
.

Main results

Mazur
[
Maz72
]
formulated the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves with good ordinary reduction at the primes that lie above

). Set

to denote the Iwasawa algebra. Let

- and

-invariants associated to the

-primary Selmer group

and

respectively. Stated below is our main result.

Theorem B
.

With respect to notation above, assume that

is a

(1)

There exists a finite Galois extension

continue to have additive reduction in

Then, the following assertions hold

Note that the first of the above conditions imply that

Theorem C
.

Let

be an elliptic curve satisfying Assumption

2.1.
Selmer groups associated to elliptic curves

Let
p
be an odd prime number and
K
be a fixed number field. Denote by
\Sigma_{p}
, the set of primes of
K
that lie above
p
. Let
E
be an elliptic curve that is defined over

. Denote by
\Sigma_{\operatorname{bad}}

of

at which

has bad reduction. Let
\Sigma

\Sigma_{\operatorname{bad}}
and

\bar{K}/K
as well as
\bar{K}_{v}/K_{v}
for any prime

of

of

\iota_{v}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bar{K}\hookrightarrow\bar{K}_{v}

to denote the absolute Galois group of

\iota_{v}^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{v}\rightarrow\operatorname{G}_{K,\Sigma},

which is induced by

.

For

, take

to be the

-torsion subgroup of

. Set

to be the

-primary part of

\mathcal{K}/K
that is contained in

H^{i}(K_{\Sigma}/\mathcal{K},\cdot)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H^{i}(\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\Sigma}/\mathcal{K}),\cdot).

Let
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
denote the ring of

K_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K

such that
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K)

. This extension is called the

\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
. The only primes of
K
that ramify in

are those that lie above

, and hence

is contained in

. We set
\Gamma
to denote the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K)
and choose a topological generator
\gamma
of
\Gamma
. Given any integer

, let
K_{n}/K
be the extension contained in
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}
such that
[K_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K]=p^{n}
. This extension is called the

-th layer
, and one has the following tower of extensions

K=K_{0}\subset K_{1}\subset K_{2}\subset\dots\subset K_{n}\subset K_{n+1}\subset\dots\subset K_{\operatorname{cyc}}.

Identify the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}/K_{n})
with
\Gamma^{p^{n}}
and set
\Gamma_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{n}}
. In particular, one may identify
\Gamma_{n}
with
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{n}/K)
. In the course of this section, we shall introduce certain Selmer groups considered over
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}
. These will be considered as modules over a certain completed group algebra called the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda
, which has nice properties. We take
\Lambda
to denote the inverse limit

\Lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Gamma_{n}],

with respect to natural quotient maps

\pi_{m,n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Gamma_{m}\rightarrow\Gamma_{n}

for
m\geq n\geq 0
. Letting
T
denote
(\gamma-1)
we identify
\Lambda
with the formal power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be an algebraic extension of

which is contained in
K_{\Sigma}
. We introduce the
p
-primary Selmer group of
E
over
\mathcal{K}
. Let
v
be a prime of
\mathcal{K}
, and consider the Kummer exact sequence of Galois modules

(2.1)

0\rightarrow E[p^{n}]\rightarrow E\xrightarrow{\times p^{n}}E\rightarrow 0.

Associated to (
2.1
), we have the exact sequence in cohomology

0\rightarrow E(\mathcal{K}_{v})\otimes\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\delta_{v,n}}H^{1}(\mathcal{K}_{v},E[p^{n}])\xrightarrow{\phi_{v,n}}H^{1}(\mathcal{K}_{v},E)[p^{n}]\rightarrow 0.

Taking the direct limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
, one obtains the following exact sequence

0\rightarrow E(\mathcal{K}_{v})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\xrightarrow{\delta_{v}}H^{1}(\mathcal{K}_{v},E[p^{\infty}])\xrightarrow{\phi_{v}}H^{1}(\mathcal{K}_{v},E)[p^{\infty}]\rightarrow 0.

Definition 2.1
.

With respect to the above notation, the
p
-primary Selmer group of
E
over
\mathcal{K}
is defined as follows

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathcal{K})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left(H^{1}(K_{\Sigma}/\mathcal{K},E[p^{\infty}])\xrightarrow{\Phi_{E,\mathcal{K}}}\prod_{v}H^{1}(\mathcal{K}_{v},E)[p^{\infty}]\right),

where the map
\Phi_{E,\mathcal{K}}
is the product of maps
\phi_{v}
as

ranges over primes of
\mathcal{K}
that lie above
\Sigma
.

Of particular interest is the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})
, which may in fact be identified with the direct limit
\varinjlim_{n}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{n})
. As is well known,
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})
has a canonical
\Lambda
-module structure. Let
\Sigma_{\operatorname{cyc}}
be the set of primes of
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}
that lie above
\Sigma
. Note that all primes of

are finitely decomposed in

, hence
\Sigma_{\operatorname{cyc}}
is finite. Thus, the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is the kernel of the natural restriction map

H^{1}(K_{\Sigma}/K_{\operatorname{cyc}},E[p^{\infty}])\xrightarrow{\Phi_{E,K_{\operatorname{cyc}}}}\prod_{v\in\Sigma_{\operatorname{cyc}}}H^{1}(K_{\operatorname{cyc},v},E)[p^{\infty}].

2.2.
Iwasawa invariants

In this subsection, we introduce the algebraic Iwasawa invariants associated to the
p
-primary Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/K_{\operatorname{cyc}})
. A polynomial
f(T)
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is said to be
distinguished
if it is monic and all non-leading coefficients are divisible by
p
. Let
M
and
M^{\prime}
be finitely generated and torsion
\Lambda
-modules. A
\Lambda
-module homomorphism
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
is said to be a
pseudo-isomorphism
if the kernel and cokernel of
\phi
are finite. It follows from the structure theory of
\Lambda
-modules (cf.
[
Was97
, Chapter 13]
) that if
M
is a finitely generated and torsion
\Lambda
-module, then it is pseudo-isomorphic to a module
M^{\prime}
of the following form

(2.2)

M^{\prime}=\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\Lambda/(p^{m_{i}})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\Lambda/(f_{j}(T)^{n_{j}})\right).

In (
2.2
),
s,t
are non-negative integers,
m_{i},n_{j}
are positive integers, and
f_{j}(T)
are irreducible distinguished polynomials. It is understood in the above notation that if
s
(resp.
t
) is
0
, then the direct sum is empty.

Definition 2.2
.

The
\mu
-invariant is given by

\mu_{p}(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\sum_{i}m_{i}&\text{ if }s>0;\\
0&\text{ if }s=0.\end{cases}

On the other hand, the
\lambda
-invariant is given by

\lambda_{p}(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\sum_{j}n_{j}\operatorname{deg}(f_{j})&\text{ if }t>0;\\
0&\text{ if }t=0.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
M
be finitely generated
\Lambda
-module. Then, the following conditions are equivalent

(1)

M
is torsion with
\mu_{p}(M)=0
,

(2)

M
is finitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module.

Moreover, if these equivalent conditions are satisfied, then

\lambda_{p}(M)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(M).

Proof.

Let us assume that
M
is a finitely generated torsion
\Lambda
-module with
\mu(M)=0
. Then
M
is pseudo-isomorphic to a
\Lambda
-module
M^{\prime}
given as follows

M^{\prime}=\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\Lambda/(f_{j}(T)^{n_{j}})\right),

with
f_{j}(T)
distinguished polynomials. Then
\Lambda/(f_{j}(T)^{n_{j}})
is finitely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module with rank
n_{j}\operatorname{deg}f_{j}
. Therefore,
M^{\prime}
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module and
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(M^{\prime})=\Sigma_{j}n_{j}\operatorname{deg}f_{j}=\lambda_{p}(M^{\prime})
. Since
M
is pseudo-isomorphic to
M^{\prime}
as
\Lambda
-module, it follows that
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(M)=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(M^{\prime})
and
\lambda_{p}(M)=\lambda_{p}(M^{\prime})
.

Conversely, assume that
M
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module. It is easy to see that
M
must be a torsion
\Lambda
-module. We show that
\mu_{p}(M)=0
. Suppose by the way of contradiction that
\mu_{p}(M)\neq 0
. Then from the structure theorem
M
is pseudo-isomorphic to a
\Lambda
-module
M^{\prime}
, which contains
\Lambda/(p^{m})
for some
m>0
. Note that
\Lambda/(p^{m})
can be identified with
\mathbb{Z}/(p^{m})[T]
and therefore is infintely generated as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module. This gives a contradiction and hence
\mu_{p}(M)=0
.
∎

.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
w
be a prime of

and

be the prime of
K
such that
w|v
. Assume that

has good reduction at

and that

. Then, we find that

.

Proof.

Suppose that

. Then, there is a large enough value of

for which

. Since

is a pro-
p
extension, from Lemma
3.12
, it follows that

E(L_{n,w})[p]\neq 0\Rightarrow E(K_{v})[p]\neq 0.

Let
\ell
be the prime number such that

. Then, the kernel of the reduction map

is a pro-
\ell
group. Since

, it follows that there is an injection

This in turn implies that

.
∎

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
E_{/K}
satisfy the conditions of Theorem

. Furthermore, assume that the only primes of

that ramify in

are in

. Then, we have that

\lambda_{p}(E/L)=[L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K]\lambda_{p}(E/K).

Proof.

Let

and

be the prime of
K
such that

. Furthermore, assume that

. From the formula (

), it suffices to show that no such

, it follows that

. Assume first that

. Since

4.
An Euler characteristic computation

Throughout this section
M
will denote a cofinitely generated and cotorsion
\Lambda
-module.
Consider the module of invariants
H^{0}(\Gamma,M)=M^{\Gamma}
and module of co-invariants
H^{1}(\Gamma,M)=M_{\Gamma}=M/TM
. There is a natural map

\phi_{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M^{\Gamma}\rightarrow M_{\Gamma},

that sends
x\in M^{\Gamma}
to
x\mod{TM}
in
M_{\Gamma}
. Since
M
is cofinitely generated as a
\Lambda
-module,
M^{\Gamma}
and
M_{\Gamma}
are cofinitely generated as
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules. Since
\Gamma\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
has cohomological dimension
1
, we have that
H^{i}(\Gamma,\cdot)=0
for
i\geq 2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Letting
M
be as above, we find that

\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}M^{\Gamma}=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}M_{\Gamma}.

Proof.

It follows from
[
How02
, Theorem 1.1]
that

\mathrm{corank}_{\Lambda}M=\operatorname{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}M^{\Gamma}-\mathrm{corank}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}M_{\Gamma}.

Since
M
is assumed to be cotorsion over
\Lambda
, the result follows.
∎

In particular, the above Lemma implies that
M^{\Gamma}
is finite if and only if
M_{\Gamma}
is finite.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
M
be a cofinitely generated and cotorsion
\Lambda
-module. Then, we say that the
Euler characteristic
of
M
is well defined if
M^{\Gamma}
(or equivalently)
M_{\Gamma}
is finite. When this is the case, we define the
Euler characteristic
of
M
as follows

\chi(\Gamma,M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{i\geq 0}\left(H^{i}(\Gamma,M)\right)^{(-1)^{i}}=\left(\frac{\#M^{\Gamma}}{\#M_{\Gamma}}\right).

Let
N
denote the Pontryagin dual of
M
. Thus,
N
is finitely generated and torsion as a
\Lambda
-module. Thus,
N
is pseudo-isomorphic to
N^{\prime}
where

(4.1)

N^{\prime}=\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\Lambda/(p^{m_{i}})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\Lambda/(f_{j}(T)^{n_{j}})\right),

cf. (
2.2
).

Definition 4.3
.

Define the characteristic series as follows

f_{M}(T)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{i}p^{m_{i}}\times\prod_{j}f_{j}(T)^{n_{j}}

and write

f_{M}(T)=a_{0}+a_{1}T+a_{2}T^{2}+\dots+a_{\lambda}T^{\lambda}.

Let
a
and
b
be
p
-adic numbers, we write
a\sim b
to mean that there exists
u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
such that
a=ub
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
M
be a cofinitely generated and cotorsion
\Lambda
-module. Then, with respect to notation above, the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The Euler characteristic
\chi(\Gamma,M)
is well defined in the sense of Definition
4.2
.

(2)

a_{0}\neq 0
.

Furthermore, if the above conditions are satisfied, then,
\chi(\Gamma,M)
is an integer and

a_{0}\sim\chi(\Gamma,M).

Proof.

Let
N
denote the Pontryagin dual of
M
. We can identify the Pontryagin dual of
M^{\Gamma}
with
N/TN
. Note that
N/TN
is finite if and only if none of the distinguished polynomials
f_{j}(T)
in (
4.1
) are divisible by
T
. This in turn is equivalent to the condition that
T\nmid f_{M}(T)
, i.e.,
a_{0}\neq 0
. Thus, we find that the Euler characteristic is well defined if and only if
a_{0}\neq 0
. It is easy to see that if
M
is pseudo-isomorphic to
M^{\prime}
, then,
\chi(\Gamma,M)=\chi(\Gamma,M^{\prime})
. Therefore, we may assume without loss of generality that

N=\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket}{(p^{m_{i}})}\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket T\rrbracket}{(f_{j}(T)^{n_{j}})}\right).

Then, it is easy to see from the decomposition above that
N[T]=0
. Therefore, we have that
M_{\Gamma}=\left(N[T]\right)^{\vee}=0
. On the other hand,

M^{\Gamma}=\left(N/TN\right)^{\vee}\simeq\prod_{i}\mathbb{Z}/p^{m_{i}}\times\prod_{j}\mathbb{Z}/f_{j}(0)^{n_{j}}.

Therefore, we find that

\chi(\Gamma,M)\sim f(0)=a_{0}.

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
M
be a cofinitely generated and cotorsion
\Lambda
-module for which the Euler characteristic is defined. Then the following assertions are equivalent

(1)

\mu_{p}(M)=0
and
\lambda_{p}(M)=0
,

(2)

a_{0}
is not divisible by
p
,

(3)

\chi(\Gamma,M)=1
.

Proof.

Let
N
be the Pontryagin dual of
M
. Then, by the structure theorem for finitely generated and torsion
\Lambda
-modules,
N
is pseudo-isomorphic to

N^{\prime}\simeq\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\Lambda/(p^{m_{i}})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\Lambda/(f_{j}(T)^{n_{j}})\right),

as in (
2.2
).

First we show that the conditions (
1
) and (
2
) are equivalent. Assume that
\mu_{p}(M)=0
and
\lambda_{p}(M)=0
. Therefore,
s=0
and
t=0
in the above decomposition, and consequently,
N^{\prime}=0
. Therefore
f_{M}(T)=1
, and it thus follows that
a_{0}=1
. In particular,
a_{0}
is not divisible by
p
. Conversely, suppose that
p\nmid a_{0}
. By definition,
f_{M}(T)=p^{\mu_{p}(M)}g_{M}(T)
, where
g_{M}(T)
is the distinguished polynomial
\prod_{j}f_{j}(T)^{n_{j}}
. In particular, we find that

a_{0}=p^{\mu_{p}(M)}g_{M}(0)

is not divisible by
p
. This implies that
\mu_{p}(M)=0
and
p\nmid g_{M}(0)
. However,
g_{M}(T)
is distinguished and thus all its non-leading coefficients are divisible by
p
. The only possibility therefore is if
g_{M}(T)=1
. Recall that
\lambda_{p}(M)
is the degree of
g_{M}(T)
, and we thus deduce that
\lambda_{p}(M)=0
. This shows that (
1
) and (
2
) are equivalent.

Since the Euler characteristic is defined, it follows from Lemma
4.4
that
\chi(\Gamma,M)
is an integer and moreover,

a_{0}\sim\chi(\Gamma,M).

Thus we deduce that

p\nmid a_{0}\Leftrightarrow p\nmid\chi(\Gamma,M).

Also note that by definition,
\chi(\Gamma,M)
is a power of
p
, and thus

p\nmid\chi(\Gamma,M)\Leftrightarrow\chi(\Gamma,M)=1.

This proves the equivalence of (
2
) and (
3
). The proof is thus complete.
∎

Throughout the rest of this section we set
K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Q}

Let

Density results

In this section we take

and

will always denote a Galois extension with Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. Set
\Delta_{L}
to be the discriminant of
L/\mathbb{Q}
. We fix an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
.

Assumption 5.1
.

We assume throughout this section that the following assumptions are satisfied by
E
.

(1)

There exists a finite Galois extension
K^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
with Galois group
\Delta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(K^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})
over which
E
has good reduction. Moreover, assume that
p\nmid|\Delta|
.

(2)

Let

be the set of primes
v
of

not dividing

at which

has additive reduction. Then all primes of

continue to have additive reduction in

(this condition is automatically satisfied when

is unramified at all primes of

or if

).

(3)

The Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is cofinitely generated and cotorsion over
\Lambda
with

\mu_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0\text{ and }\lambda_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0.

The Corollary
4.9
gives us an explicit criterion for the above conditions to be satisfied. Recall that it was checked in the previous section that these conditions hold for the elliptic curve

E\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}y^{2}+y=x^{3}-3x-5.

Given a real number
X>0
,
\mathcal{S}(X)
be the set of Galois extensions
L/\mathbb{Q}
with
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
and such that

. It follows from the Hermite-Minkowski theorem that the set

is finite. Let
\mathcal{S}_{E}(X)
be subset of

consisting of the extensions for which the following conditions hold

•

is cofinitely generated and cotorsion over

•

We note that for

, it follows from Proposition

that

. Thus, the rank remains stable in such extensions
L/\mathbb{Q}
. We prove asymptotic formulae for
N_{E}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\#\mathcal{S}_{E}(X)
respectively. First, we recall the Tauberian theorem of Delange, which will be applied to obtain our result.

Theorem 5.2

(Delange’s Tauberian theorem)
.

Let
f(s)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}
be a Dirichlet series with non-negative coefficients and
a>0
be a real number. Assume that
f(s)
converges for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>a
and has a meromorphic continuation to a neighbourhood
U
of
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq a
. For
X>0
, we set

g(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{n\leq X}a_{n}
. Assume that the only pole of
f(s)
is at
s=a
and the order of this pole is
b\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, i.e.,

f(s)=\frac{1}{(s-a)^{b}}h(s)

for some holomorphic function
h(s)
defined on
U
. Then, there is a positive constant
c>0
such that
X\rightarrow\infty
, we have that

g(X)\sim cX^{a}(\log X)^{b-1}.

Proof.

The result is a special case of
[
Ten15
, Theorem 7.28]
.
∎

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a Galois extension with
\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
which is unramified at
p
. Let
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{k}
be primes that ramify in
L
. It follows from class field theory that each of the primes
\ell_{i}
is
1\mod{p}
. Let
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{k}
be prime numbers that are
1\mod{p}
. Then, it is a straightforward exercise in class field theory that the number of
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
-extensions
L/\mathbb{Q}
that are ramified exactly at
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{k}
is equal to
(p-1)^{k-1}
. Moreover, since the primes
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{k}
are tamely ramified, it follows that
L/\mathbb{Q}
has discriminant
\Delta_{L}=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\ell_{i}\right)^{p-1}
. We take
\mathcal{Q}
to denote the set of primes
\ell\in Q_{3}
(cf. Definition
3.13
) such that
\ell\equiv 1\mod{p}
. Let
\alpha
denote the natural density of
\mathcal{Q}
and assume that
\alpha>0
. Let

\rho_{E,p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

be the Galois representation on
E[p]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that the representation
\rho_{E,p}
is surjective, then

\alpha=\left(\frac{p^{2}-p-1}{p^{3}-p^{2}-p+1}\right).

Proof.

Recall that a prime
\ell\in\mathcal{Q}
if
\ell\equiv 1\mod{p}
and
\ell\in Q_{3}
. The set
Q_{3}
consists of primes
\ell\neq p
such that
E
has good reduction at
\ell
and
\widetilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})[p]=0
. Let
a_{\ell}(E)
denote the Frobenius trace of
E
at
\ell
, note that

a_{\ell}(E)=\ell+1-\#\widetilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}).

Since
\ell\equiv 1\mod{p}
, we find taht
a_{\ell}(E)\equiv 2-\#\widetilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
. Thus, we find that

\widetilde{E}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})[p]=0\Leftrightarrow a_{\ell}(E)\not\equiv 2\mod{p}.

Let
\sigma_{\ell}
denote the Frobenius at
\ell
. Since
\rho_{E,p}
is unramified at
\ell
, there is a well defined matrix
\rho_{E,p}(\sigma_{\ell})\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
. Moreover, it follows from the Weil pairing that
\operatorname{det}\rho_{E,p}=\omega
, the mod-
p
cyclotomic character. Therefore, we find that a prime
\ell\neq p
of good reduction for
E
is contained in
\mathcal{Q}
if and only if

\operatorname{trace}\rho_{E,p}(\sigma_{\ell})\neq 2,\text{ and }\operatorname{det}\rho_{E,p}(\sigma_{\ell})=1.

We take
\mathbb{Q}(E[p])
to denote the Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
which is fixed by the kernel of
\rho_{E,p}
. We have a natural isomorphism

\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{Q}(E[p])/\mathbb{Q}\right)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{image}(\rho_{E,p})=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}).

Thus one simply identifies
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\mathbb{Q}(E[p])/\mathbb{Q}\right)
with
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. With respect to this identification,
\ell\in\mathcal{Q}
if and only if
\sigma_{\ell}
is a matrix with trace
\neq 2
and determinant
=1
. It then follows from the Chebotarev density theorem that

\alpha=\frac{\#\{A\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\mid\operatorname{trace}(A)\neq 2\}}{\#\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}.

We count the number of matrices of the form
A=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&2-a\\
\end{array}}\right)
in
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
. This equals the number of triples
(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3}
such that
bc=a(2-a)-1=-a^{2}+2a-1=-(a-1)^{2}
. It is easy to see that the number of such triples is equal to
(p-1)^{2}+(2p-1)
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\#\{A\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\mid\operatorname{trace}(A)\neq 2\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\#\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})-(p-1)^{2}-(2p-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(p^{2}-1)-(p-1)^{2}-(2p-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{3}-p^{2}-p.

Thus, we have shown that

\alpha=\left(\frac{p^{3}-p^{2}-p}{(p^{2}-p)(p^{2}-1)}\right)=\left(\frac{p^{2}-p-1}{p^{3}-p^{2}-p+1}\right).

∎

Remark 5.4
.

Note that for our example from the previous section,
\rho_{E,3}
is indeed surjective, as checked in
LMFDB
.

Given an integer of the form
n=\ell_{1}\dots\ell_{k}
, take
a_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(p-1)^{k-1}
and set
a_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=0
otherwise. Then, we find from the discussion above that
a_{n}
is the number of
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
-extensions
L/\mathbb{Q}
that are ramified at exactly the primes
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{k}
and have discriminant
n^{p-1}
. Setting
g(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{n\leq X}a_{n}
, we find that

\begin{split}g(X)=\#\{L\mid&\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z},|\Delta_{L}|\leq X^{(p-1)},\\
&\text{ and }L\text{ is ramified only at primes in }\mathcal{Q}\}.\end{split}

Proposition 5.5
.

With respect to the notation above, let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
extension which is ramified only at a set of primes in
\mathcal{Q}
. Then, the following conditions hold

(1)

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/L)
is cofinitely generated and cotorsion over
\Lambda
,

(2)

\mu_{p}(E/L)=0
and
\lambda_{p}(E/L)=0
,

(3)

\operatorname{rank}E(L)=0
.

Moreover, we find that

(5.1)

g(X)\leq N_{E}(X^{p-1}).

Proof.

It follows from the assumptions on
E
that the conditions of Theorem
B
are satisfied. Recall taht it is assumed that
\mu_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0
and
\lambda_{p}(E/\mathbb{Q})=0
. It follows from Theorem
B
that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(E/L_{\operatorname{cyc}})
is cofinitely generated and cotorsion as a
\Lambda
-module and
\mu_{p}(E/L)=0
. Since
\mathcal{Q}
is a subset of
Q_{3}
, we deduce from Corollary
3.15
that
\lambda_{p}(E/L)=0
as well, and thus the conditions (1) and (2) above are both satisfied. Finally, since

\operatorname{rank}E(L)\leq\lambda_{p}(E/L)

by Proposition
3.3
, part (3) follows.
That
g(X)\leq N_{E}(X^{p-1})
simply follows as a consequence.

The result has thus been proven.
∎

We apply Delange’s Tauberian theorem (Theorem
5.2
) to prove an asymptotic lower bound for
g(X)
, and thus derive an asymptotic bound for
N_{E}(X)
.

Theorem 5.6
.

With respect to notation above, we have that

N_{E}(X)\gg X^{\frac{1}{(p-1)}}(\log X)^{(p-1)\alpha-1},

where
\alpha
is the density of
\mathcal{Q}
.

Proof.

The proof of the result follows along the same lines as
[
Ser75
, Theorem 2.4]
. Nonetheless, we provide details for completeness. Set

\begin{split}f(s)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}n^{-s}&=\frac{1}{(p-1)}\sum_{T\subset\mathcal{Q}}(p-1)^{|T|}\left(\prod_{\ell\in T}\ell\right)^{-s}\\
&=\frac{1}{(p-1)}\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal{Q}}\left(1+(p-1)\ell^{-s}\right).\end{split}

It is easy to see that

\log f(s)=(p-1)\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{Q}}\ell^{-s}+k_{1}(s),

where
k_{1}(s)
is holomorphic on
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq 1
; and as a consequence,

\log f(s)=(p-1)\alpha\log\left(\frac{1}{s-1}\right)+k_{2}(s),

where
k_{2}(s)
is holomorphic on
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq 1
. Thus, we deduce that

f(s)=(s-1)^{-(p-1)\alpha}h(s),

where
h(s)
is a non-zero holomorphic function on
\operatorname{Re}(s)\geq 1
. It follows from the Theorem
5.2
that

g(X)\sim cX(\log X)^{(p-1)\alpha-1},

where
c>0
is a constant that does not depend on
X
. It then follows from (
5.1
) that

N_{E}(X)\gg X^{\frac{1}{(p-1)}}(\log X)^{(p-1)\alpha-1}.

This completes the proof of the result.
∎

We now give the proof of Theorem
C
.

Proof of Theorem
C
.

Since it is assumed that
\rho_{E,p}
is surjective, it follows from Lemma
5.3
that

\alpha=\left(\frac{p^{2}-p-1}{p^{3}-p^{2}-p+1}\right).

Noting that
-\beta=\alpha(p-1)-1
, the result is thus follows as a direct consequence of Theorem
5.6
.
∎

Global Existence of Weak Solutions for Compresssible Navier–Stokes–Fourier Equations with the Truncated Virial Pressure Law
Didier Bresch, Pierre–Emmanuel Jabin, Fei Wang

Abstract
. This paper concerns the existence of global weak solutions à la Leray for compressible Navier–Stokes–Fourier system with periodic boundary conditions and the truncated virial pressure law which is assumed to be thermodynamically unstable. More precisely, the main novelty is that the pressure law is not assumed to be monotone with respect to the density. This provides the first global weak solutions result for the compressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier system with such kind of pressure law which is strongly used as a generalization of the perfect gas law. The paper is based on a new construction of approximate solutions through an iterative scheme and fixed point procedure which could be very helpful to design efficient numerical schemes. Note that our method involves the recent paper by the authors published in Nonlinearity (2021) for the compactness of the density when the temperature is given.



Keywords:
Compressible Navier-Stokes, Heat-conduction,
Truncated Virial pressure law, Non-monotone pressure, Vacuum state,
Global weak solutions.

AMS Classification numbers:
76N10, 35D30, 35Q30, 35Q86

Introduction and main result

This paper is dedicated to Antonín Novotný who had contributed so many innovative work to the theory of compressible fluids, specifically to compressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier equations, and unfortunately passed away suddenly on Thursday, June 03 2021.

The non-stationary Navier-Stokes-Fourier equations modeling viscous compressible and heat conducting fluids, in the multi-dimensional in space case, have been extensively studied both from a theoretical and a numerical point of view: see
[
10
]
. Yet many questions around the existence, uniqueness, or stability of solutions have remained unsolved. The case of non-stationary barotropic Navier-Stokes equations (namely without temperature) is somewhat better understood, in particular for the global existence of weak solutions à la Leray (
[
13
]
): see for instance
[
14
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
and references cited therein. The present study addresses the theoretical problem of existence of so-called global weak solutions à la Leray for the full system including the evolution of internal energy (temperature dependent case) for the so-called virial pressure law.

One of the well-known difficulty of such nonlinear system of fluid mechanics with heat-conductivity is that the
a priori
bounds based on the energy estimates are not strong enough to get equi-integrability of certain quantities, such as the viscous dissipation quantity (see for instance
[
14
]
). This is compounded in the present paper by a pressure law that is non-monotone in the density and hence thermodynamically unstable.

A first helpful approach is to replace the internal energy equation by the entropy inequality supplemented by the total energy balance, as introduced by E. Feireisl and A. Novotny, explained in
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
with appropriate hypothesis on the pressure state laws. Unfortunately, this approach was initially limited to thermodynamically stable state laws, namely

\partial_{\rho}P|_{\vartheta}>0,\qquad\qquad\partial_{\vartheta}e|_{\rho}>0,

where
P
is the pressure state law and
e
is the internal energy depending on the density
\rho
and the temperature
\vartheta
.

On the other hand, the potential oscillations in the density due to the pressure laws can in principle be controlled through the method in
[
1
,
3
]
. But a major difficulty further lies in combining both approaches at the level of an approximate system. We take a different point of view to bypass most this issue by constructing solutions through a fixed point argument.

Define, in a periodic domain
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{d}
for
d\geq 2
, the so-called truncated virial pressure law

(1.1)

P(\rho,\vartheta)=\rho^{\gamma}+\vartheta\,\sum_{n=0}^{N}B_{n}(\vartheta)\,\rho^{n}

where
\gamma>\max(4,2N,d)
. The virial equation of state seems to have been proposed first by M. Thiesen in 1885 and intensively studied by H. Kammerlingh Onnes (see
[
16
]
) at the beginning of the previous century as an empirical extension of the ideal-gas law. The reader interested by Virial coefficients of pure gases and mixtures is referred to
[
5
]
.

Such pressure laws is not monotone with respect to the density even after a fixed value and therefore is not thermodynamically stable. They are nevertheless commonly used in practice. With proper assumptions on the coefficients
B_{n}(\vartheta)
, one can still ensure that
\partial_{\vartheta}e|_{\rho}>0
so that the system is at least thermodynamically consistent.

We next consider the compressible Navier–Stokes–Fourier (CNSF) equations for the corresponding state laws,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(\rho u)=0,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(\rho u\otimes u)-\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\mathcal{S}+\nabla P=0,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho E)+\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(\rho uE)+\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(Pu)=\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(\mathcal{S}u)+\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(\kappa\nabla\vartheta)

where
E=|u|^{2}/2+e
is the energy with
P=P(\rho,\vartheta)
and
e=e(\rho,\vartheta)
respectively stand for the pressure and the (specific) internal energy.

The initial condition are given by

(1.5)

\rho|_{t=0}=\rho_{0}\ \ \ \ \ (\rho u)|_{t=0}=m_{0}\ \ \ \ \ (\rho E)|_{t=0}=\rho_{0}E_{0}.

Note that the above initial conditions determine the corresponding value at
t=0
of the temperature
\vartheta|_{t=0}=\vartheta_{0}
, provided that
\partial_{\vartheta}e>0
.

For simplicity, we take the isotropic stress tensor

(1.6)

\mathcal{S}=\mu(\nabla u+\nabla u^{T})+\lambda\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u\mbox{Id}

with
\mu
and
\lambda
two constants satisfying the physical constraint
\mu>0

and
\lambda+2\mu/d>0
.
In order to be consistent with the second principle of Thermodynamics which implies
the existence of the entropy as a closed differential form in the energy balance, the
following compatibility condition, called “Maxwell equation” between
P
and
e
has to be
satisfied

(1.7)

P=\rho^{2}\frac{\partial e}{\partial\rho}+\vartheta\frac{\partial P}{\partial\vartheta}.

This allows to define the internal energy directly from the pressure law, up to a function only of
\vartheta
, which we take as
0
for simplicity. Namely for any arbitrary
\rho_{c}>0
, and by using (
1.1
),

(1.8)

\begin{split}e(\rho,\vartheta)&=\int_{\rho_{c}}^{\rho}-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\,\left(P(\rho^{\prime},\vartheta)-\vartheta\,\frac{\partial P}{\partial\vartheta}(\rho^{\prime},\vartheta)\right)\\
&=m+\frac{\rho^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}-\sum_{n=0}^{N}\vartheta^{2}\,\frac{d}{d\vartheta}(B_{n}(\vartheta))\,\frac{\rho^{n}}{n-1}-\vartheta^{2}\,\frac{d}{d\vartheta}(B_{1}(\vartheta))\,\log\rho+\vartheta^{2}\,\frac{d}{d\vartheta}(B_{1}(\vartheta))\,\frac{1}{\rho}.\end{split}

The specific entropy
s=s(\rho,\vartheta)
is now also defined up to an additive constant by

(1.9)

\frac{\partial s}{\partial\vartheta}\bigg|_{\rho}=\frac{1}{\vartheta}\frac{\partial e}{\partial\vartheta}\bigg|_{\rho}\ \ \ \ \ and\ \ \ \ \ \frac{\partial s}{\partial\rho}\bigg|_{\vartheta}=-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\vartheta}\bigg|_{\rho}.

If
(\rho,\ \vartheta)
are smooth and bounded from below away from zero and if the velocity field is smooth, then the total energy balance can formally be replaced by the thermal energy balance

C_{v}\rho(\partial_{t}\vartheta+u\cdot\nabla\vartheta)-\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta)=\mathcal{S}:\nabla u-\vartheta\frac{\partial P(\rho,\vartheta)}{\partial\vartheta}\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u

where
\mathcal{S}:\nabla u=\mathop{\rm Tr}\nolimits(\mathcal{S}\nabla u)
.

Furthermore, dividing by
\vartheta
, we arrive at the entropy equation

(1.10)

\partial_{t}(\rho s)+\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(\rho su)-\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\left(\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta}{\vartheta}\right)=\frac{1}{\vartheta}\left(\mathcal{S}:\nabla u+\frac{\kappa|\nabla\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta}\right).

We will use both of the two equations (
1.4
) and (
1.10
) involving temperature, at different parts of our argument, together with a third technical formulation derived from (
1.4
).

We emphasize that,
a priori
, the system (
1.2
)-(
1.4
) conserves
the total mass

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho(t,\cdot)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho_{0}\,dx.

The total energy of the system, which is the sum of the kinetic and the potential energies, reads

\mathcal{E}(\rho,\vartheta,m)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho E(\rho,\vartheta,\rho u)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{|m|^{2}}{2\rho}+\rho e(\rho,\vartheta)\right)\,dx

and is also conserved, namely,

\mathcal{E}(\rho,\vartheta,m)(t)=\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0},\vartheta_{0},m_{0}),

with
m=\rho u
, where
e
is obtained from equation (
1.7
).

We need several precise assumptions on the various coefficients entering into equations (
1.2
)-(
1.4
) which we now make explicit.

Assumption on the conductivity
\kappa(\vartheta)
:

(1.11)

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}(\vartheta^{\alpha}+1)\leq\kappa(\vartheta)\leq\kappa_{2}(\vartheta^{\alpha}+1),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \kappa_{3}\vartheta^{\alpha-1}\leq\kappa^{\prime}(\vartheta)\leq\kappa_{4}\vartheta^{\alpha-1}

where
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}>0,
and
\alpha\geq 4
.

Assumptions on the pressure law
P
.

(1)

The pressure
P
given by (
1.1
) contains a radiative part, namely

(1.12)

\partial_{\vartheta}^{2}B_{0}>0\ \hbox{for}\ \vartheta=0;

(2)

For
2\leq\gamma_{\vartheta}\leq\alpha/2
with some
\alpha\geq 4

(1.13)

C^{-1}\vartheta^{\gamma_{\vartheta}-1}\leq B_{0}(\vartheta)\leq\vartheta^{\gamma_{\vartheta}-1},\ \ \ \ C^{-1}\vartheta^{\gamma_{\vartheta}-2}\leq B^{\prime}_{0}(\vartheta)\leq\vartheta^{\gamma_{\vartheta}-2};

(3)

We assume

(1.14)

B_{1}\equiv C_{1}\hbox{ for some }C_{1}\in\mathbb{R};

(4)

For
n\geq 2
, the coefficients
B_{n}
is concave in the sense that

(1.15)

\frac{d}{d\vartheta}(\vartheta^{2}B^{\prime}_{n})\leq 0;

(5)

We also assume that the following is true for
n\geq 0
and
n\neq 1

(1.16)

|\vartheta^{3}B^{\prime\prime\prime}_{n}(\vartheta)|+|\vartheta^{2}B^{\prime\prime}_{n}(\vartheta)|+|\vartheta B^{\prime}_{n}(\vartheta)|+|B_{n}(\vartheta)|\leq C\vartheta^{(\gamma-n)\gamma_{\vartheta}/\gamma-1-\varepsilon};

(6)

There exist some constants
\bar{B}_{n}
and
\bar{\alpha}<\min(\alpha,2\,\gamma_{\vartheta})
,

(1.17)

|\vartheta^{2}\,B^{\prime}_{n}(\vartheta)|+\vartheta|B_{n}(\vartheta)|+|\vartheta B_{n}(\vartheta)-\bar{B}_{n}|\leq C\,\vartheta^{\bar{\alpha}(\gamma-2n)/2\gamma-\varepsilon};

(7)

Finally we also assume the following property on the entropy
s

(1.18)

\hbox{ The specific entropy }s\hbox{ is a concave function of }(\rho^{-1},e).

Remark 1.1
.

The above assumption on
s
ensures that the
C_{v}
coefficient is non-negative

(1.19)

C_{v}=\frac{\partial e}{\partial\vartheta}\bigg|_{\rho}=-\frac{1}{\vartheta^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}s}{\partial e^{2}}\bigg|_{\rho}^{-1}\geq 0

where the second equality comes from that
\partial s/\partial e=\vartheta^{-1}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Let us comment that the results described for in instance in
[
8
]

are based on a radiative part and a cold pressure part. In the truncated pressure law,
this corresponds respectively to the terms
\vartheta B_{0}(\vartheta)
and

\rho^{\gamma}
.

We emphasize that none of the assumptions above require a sign on
B_{n}(\vartheta)
, except on
B_{0}
. Hence as claimed, the truncated virial pressure may not be monotone in
\rho
for some values of
\vartheta
or
\rho
.

We are now ready to state our main result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume the initial data
\vartheta_{0}
,
m_{0}
and
\rho_{0}\geq 0
with
\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}\rho_{0}=M_{0}>0
satisfy

\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0},\vartheta_{0},m_{0})=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\frac{|m_{0}|^{2}}{2\rho_{0}}+\rho_{0}e(\rho_{0},\vartheta_{0})\right)\,dx<\infty

where
m_{0}=0
when
\rho_{0}=0
. Suppose that the pressure state law
P(\rho,\vartheta)
is given by (
1.1
) with the assumptions (
1.12
)–(
1.17
) and assume (
1.18
) on the entropy.
Then there exists a global weak solution
(\rho,u,\vartheta)
to Compressible Navier–Stokes–Fourier System. More precisely it satisfies (
1.2
)–(
1.3
) with (
1.6
) in the distribution sense,
the following entropy inequality

(1.20)

\partial_{t}(\rho s)+{\rm div}(\rho su)-{\rm div}\bigl(\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta}{\vartheta}\bigr)\geq\frac{1}{\vartheta}\bigl(S:\nabla u+\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)|\nabla\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta}\bigr)

where
s
is defined by (
1.9
) and the energy inequality

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bigl(\rho\frac{[u|^{2}}{2}+\rho e(\rho,\vartheta)\bigr)(t)\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bigl(\frac{|m_{0}|^{2}}{2\rho_{0}}+\rho_{0}e(\rho_{0},\vartheta_{0})\bigr)\,dx.

Moreover, we have

u\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\qquad|m|^{2}/2\rho\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d}))

\rho u\in{\mathcal{C}}([0,T],L^{2\gamma/(\gamma+2)}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\hbox{ weak }),

for any
T>0
, the weak regularity

\rho\in{\mathcal{C}}([0,T],L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\hbox{ weak })\cap L^{\gamma+a}((0,T)\times\mathbb{T}^{d})\hbox{ where }0<a<1/d

\vartheta\in L^{\alpha}(0,T;L^{\alpha/(1-2/d)}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),\qquad\log\vartheta\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})),

and the initial conditions satisfied by
(\rho,\rho u,\rho s)
in a weak sense

\rho|_{t=0}=\rho_{0},\qquad\rho u|_{t=0}=m_{0},\qquad\rho s|_{t=0^{+}}\geq\rho_{0}s(\rho_{0},\vartheta_{0}).

We remark that we use the notation
\rho\in{\mathcal{C}}([0,T],L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\hbox{ weak })
to mean that
\rho
is weakly continuous in time in
L^{\gamma}
: for any
t_{n}\to t
, we have that
\rho(t_{n},.)\to\rho(t,.)
for the weak topology of
L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Theorem
1.3
is the first result providing global existence of weak solutions for the heat conducting Navier-Stokes equations with a thermodynamical unstable pressure law depending on the density and the temperature.

The main idea in the proof is to separate the density and momentum equations (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) from the energy equation (
1.4
). For a given
\vartheta(t,x)
satisfying appropriate energy bounds, our assumptions on the pressure law let us use
[
3
]
(see also the introductory paper
[
2
]
) more or less directly. This article focused on the barotropic system, namely (
1.2
)-(
1.3
), but with pressure laws that are inhomogeneous in time and space. It is thus a good tool for the task of obtaining existence of
\rho
and
u
for a given
\vartheta
.

We also need to obtain existence of some
\vartheta
solving (
1.4
) for a given
\rho
and
u
, again with appropriate energy bounds. This does not seem to fit in any classical framework of non-linear parabolic equations and therefore requires careful approach. We use a different formulation, that is loosely based on (
1.4
) (and formally equivalent when all quantities are smooth). We also need a proper approximated equation to resolve a potential degeneracy where
\vartheta
is close to
0
. This finally allows us to obtain a global, weak solution to our variant formulation to (
1.4
). We do not have strong enough bounds to recover (
1.4
) rigorously from that but it is enough to obtain an inequality in the entropy formulation (
1.20
) together with the opposite inequality in the propagation of the total energy (as can be surmised from the formulation in Theorem
1.3
).

The last step in the proof is obviously to conclude the fixed point argument, through the Leray-Schauder theorem. This is a rather short but very challenging step. The issue is that we cannot yet recover the usual energy estimate: Before we do obtain a fixed point, the piece of the energy that we obtain from the existence on (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) does not fit with the piece of the energy that we obtain from (
1.4
). This is where the exact formulation of the Leray-Schauder theorem is critical and must be combined with the precise choice we have made of the decomposition.

Previous result concerning the compressible Navier-Stokes-Fourier system.

1)
The pressure law as a monotone perturbation of the barotropic case.
It is due to

where

(2.1)

\begin{split}&P_{c}(0)=0,\quad P^{\prime}_{c}(\rho)\geq a_{1}\rho^{\gamma-1}-b\hbox{ for }\rho>0,\\

and

with constants

,

,

,

in

{\mathcal{C}}[0,+\infty)\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{1}(0,+\infty)
.
In agreement with Maxwell law and the entropy definition, it implies the following form

where

The heat conductivity coefficient

with constants
\kappa_{1}>0
and

.
The thermal energy
Q=Q(\vartheta)=\int_{0}^{\vartheta}C_{v}(z)dz
has not yet been determined and is assumed to satisfy
\int_{z\in[0,+\infty)}C_{v}(z)>0
and
C_{v}(\vartheta)\leq c(1+\vartheta^{\alpha/2-1})
.
Because the energy and pressure satisfy

\frac{\partial e(\rho,\vartheta)}{\partial\vartheta}>0,\qquad\frac{\partial P(\rho,\vartheta)}{\partial\rho}>0

the estimate on
H_{\vartheta}
gives a control on
\rho^{\gamma}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))

and through the entropy equation a control on
\vartheta
in

.

Because the entropy estimates does not provide an

Therefore one obtains the exact equivalent of estimates as in the barotropic case

\begin{split}&\sup_{t}\int_{\Omega}\rho\,|u|^{2}\,dx\leq C+{\mathcal{E}}(\rho_{0},u_{0}),\\

2)
Self-similar pressure laws with large radiative contribution.
It is due to
E. Feireisl
and
A. Novotny
who consider pressure laws exhibiting both coercivity of type

In agreement to Maxwell law and the definition of entropy, it implies the following

They

0<-S^{\prime}(Z)=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\frac{\gamma Q(Z)-Q^{\prime}(Z)Z}{Z}<c<+\infty\hbox{ for all }Z>0,

3.
The direct entropy estimate

3.1.
A formal entropy bound

We explain here the general framework for our result on the Navier–Stokes–Fourier system. The estimates here closely follow the ones pioneered by
P.–L. Lions
, and
E. Feireisl
and
A. Novotny
. With respect to the previous discussion, we only present them here in a more general context as in particular we will not need the monotonicity of
P
.

If one removes the monotonicity assumption on
P
then thermodynamic stability does not hold anymore.
Following
P.–L. Lions
, it is however possible to obtain the entropy dissipation
estimate directly by integrating the entropy equation

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(\mu\,\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{\vartheta}+\kappa{(\vartheta)}\frac{|\nabla\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta^{2}}\Bigr)\,dx\,dt\leq C\,\int_{\Omega}\rho\,s(t,x)\,dx.

Therefore the entropy bound dissipation holds under the general assumption that there exists
C
s.t.

(3.1)

s(\rho,\vartheta)\leq C\,e(\rho,\vartheta)+\frac{C}{\rho}.

Recall that

e=m(\vartheta)+\int_{\rho^{\star}}^{\rho}\frac{(P(\rho^{\prime},\vartheta)-\vartheta\partial_{\vartheta}P(\rho^{\prime},\vartheta))}{\rho^{\prime 2}}\,d\rho^{\prime},

and

\partial_{\rho}s=-\frac{\partial_{\vartheta}P}{\rho^{2}}.

We also have that
\partial_{\vartheta}s=\partial_{\vartheta}e/\vartheta
, therefore as long as
m(\vartheta)\geq 0
with

\int_{\vartheta^{*}}^{\vartheta}\frac{m^{\prime}(s)}{s}\,ds\leq C(1+m(\vartheta)),

and

-\int_{\rho^{*}}^{\rho}\frac{\partial_{\vartheta}P}{\rho^{\prime 2}}\,d\rho^{\prime}\leq C+C\,\int_{\rho^{*}}^{\rho}\frac{P-\vartheta\partial_{\vartheta}P}{\rho^{\prime 2}}\,d\rho^{\prime},

for some
C>0
then (
3.1
) is automatically satisfied
and one obtains the entropy bound dissipation.
Moreover if
e(\vartheta,\rho)\geq\rho^{\gamma-1}/C
then one also has that

\rho\in L^{\infty}_{t}\,L^{\gamma}_{x}
.

Assuming now that

\kappa_{1}\,(\vartheta^{\alpha}+1)\leq\kappa(\vartheta)\leq\kappa_{2}\,(\vartheta^{\alpha}+1),

with
\alpha\geq 2
, one deduces from the entropy estimate that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\vartheta^{\alpha}+1)\,|\nabla\vartheta|\,dxdt<\infty,

showing that
\log\vartheta\in L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
and
\vartheta^{\alpha/2}\in L^{2}_{t}\,H^{1}_{x}
or
by Sobolev embedding
\vartheta\in L^{\alpha}_{t}\,L^{\alpha/(1-2/d)}_{x}
for
d\geq 3
.

By a Hölder estimate, it is also possible to obtain a Sobolev-like,
L^{p_{1}}_{t}W^{1,p_{2}}_{x}
, bound on
u

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p_{2}}\,dx\right)^{p_{1}/p_{2}}\,dt\leq\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{\vartheta}\,dx\,dt\right)^{p_{1}/2}\\
&\qquad\times\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega}\vartheta^{p_{2}/(2-p_{2})}\,dx\right)^{p_{1}(2-p_{2})/(p_{2}(2-p_{1}))}dt\right)^{(2-p_{1})/2}<\infty,\end{split}

provided that
p_{2}/(2-p_{2})=\alpha/(1-2/d)
and
p_{1}/(2-p_{1})=\alpha
or

(3.2)

p_{1}=\frac{2\,\alpha}{1+\alpha},\quad p_{2}=\frac{2\,\alpha\,d}{d\,(\alpha+1)-2}.

Unfortunately this Sobolev estimate does not allow to derive the gain of integrability on the density
as usually. Actually one requires a
L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
estimate on
u
(the critical point is in fact the
L^{2}_{t}
with value in some Sobolev in
x
).
Instead one can easily extend the argument by
E. Feireisl
and
A. Novotny
: For any
\phi(\rho)
, one can write

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\rho|u|^{2}+\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\phi(\rho)+\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{S}}:\nabla u=\int_{\Omega}(P(\vartheta,\rho)-\phi^{\prime}(\rho)\,\rho+\phi(\rho))\,{\rm divu}.

This leads to the assumption that there exists some
\phi
s.t.

(3.3)

\displaystyle C^{-1}\rho^{\gamma}-C\leq\phi(\rho)\leq C\rho^{\gamma}+C,

\displaystyle\left|P(\vartheta,\rho)-\phi^{\prime}(\rho)\,\rho+\phi(\rho)\right|\leq C\Bigl(\rho^{\beta_{1}}+\vartheta^{\beta_{2}}+\sqrt{\rho e(\vartheta,\rho)}\Bigr),

with

(3.4)

\beta_{1}\leq\frac{\gamma}{2},\quad\beta_{2}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}.

Indeed, with (
3.3
), one has

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}{\mathcal{S}}:\nabla u\,dx\,dt\leq{\mathcal{E}}(\rho_{0},\vartheta_{0},m_{0})+C\,\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(\rho^{\beta_{1}}+\vartheta^{\beta_{2}}+\sqrt{\rho e(\vartheta,\rho)}\Bigr)\,|\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u|\,dx\,dt.

Using that
{\mathcal{S}}
is Newtonian, this leads to

(3.5)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\,{\mathcal{E}}(\rho_{0},\vartheta_{0},m_{0})+C\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\,\|\rho^{\beta_{1}}+\vartheta^{\beta_{2}}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}},

and the desired
H^{1}
bound follows from (
3.4
). It is now possible to follow the same steps to obtain an equivalent of the extra integrability on the density if
\gamma>d/2

(3.6)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{\gamma+a}\,dx\,dt\leq C(T,{\mathcal{E}}(\rho_{0},\vartheta_{0},m_{0})),\quad\mbox{for all}\ a<1/d.

Note here that the assumptions (
3.3
)-(
3.4
) are likely not optimal. They nevertheless already cover the truncated virial law we consider here.

3.2.
The assumptions on the pressure law to get the above estimates

For convenience, we repeat here all the assumptions presented above and will show that
the truncated virial pressure laws satisfy them under the assumptions related to the coefficients

B_{n}
. To derive the important estimates mentioned in the sections above, the pressure law
P(\rho,\vartheta)
has to be a positive pressure law satisfying the following
properties: For some
C>0
and
\gamma>d

(3.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &P(\rho,\vartheta)\hbox{ such that }-\int_{\rho^{*}}^{\rho}\frac{\partial_{\vartheta}P}{\rho^{\prime 2}}\,d\rho^{\prime}\leq C+C\,\int_{\rho^{*}}^{\rho}\frac{P-\vartheta\partial_{\vartheta}P}{\rho^{\prime 2}}\,d\rho^{\prime},\\
&e(\rho,\vartheta)=m(\vartheta)+\int_{\rho^{\star}}^{\rho}\frac{(P(\rho^{\prime},\vartheta)-\vartheta\partial_{\vartheta}P(\rho^{\prime},\vartheta))}{\rho^{\prime 2}}\,d\rho^{\prime}\geq m(\vartheta)+\frac{\rho^{\gamma-1}}{C}+\frac{\vartheta^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}}{C\,\rho}.\\
&\quad\hbox{ with }m(\vartheta)\geq 0\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\hbox{ and }\int_{\vartheta^{*}}^{\vartheta}\frac{m^{\prime}(s)}{s}\,ds\leq C(1+m(\vartheta))\leq C\,(1+\vartheta^{\alpha\,(\gamma+a-1)/2(\gamma+a)}),\\
&\kappa_{1}\,(\vartheta^{\alpha}+1)\leq\kappa(\vartheta)\leq\kappa_{2}\,(\vartheta^{\alpha}+1),\quad\mu,\;\lambda\ \mbox{constant and }\alpha\geq 4,\\
&C^{-1}\rho^{\gamma}-C\leq\phi(\rho)\leq C\,\rho^{\gamma}+C,\\
&|P(\rho,\vartheta)-\phi^{\prime}(\rho)\,\rho+\phi(\rho)|\leq C\,\rho^{\beta_{1}}+C\,\vartheta^{\beta_{2}}+C\,\sqrt{\rho e(\rho,\vartheta)},\\
&|\partial_{\vartheta}P(\rho,\vartheta)|\leq C\rho^{\beta_{3}}+C\vartheta^{\beta_{4}}\end{aligned}\right.

for

(3.8)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\beta_{1}\leq\frac{\gamma}{2},\quad\beta_{2}<\frac{\alpha}{2},\\
&\beta_{3}<\frac{\gamma+a+1}{2},\qquad\beta_{4}<\frac{\alpha}{2},\\
&\frac{2}{d}\,\mu+\lambda>0,\quad\gamma_{\vartheta}\geq 2\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where we recall that
a<\min\left(1/d,2\gamma/d-1\right)=1/d
since
\gamma>d
here.
We also assume that the specific heat is positive (as is necessary for the physics)
i.e.

(3.9)

C_{v}=\partial_{\vartheta}e(\rho,\vartheta)>0,\quad\forall\rho,\;\vartheta,

and that the pressure contains a radiative part

(3.10)

\partial_{\vartheta}^{2}P(\rho=0,\vartheta)>0.

We do not need to impose monotony on
P
and it is enough that

(3.11)

\begin{split}&\bigl|\partial_{\rho}P(\rho,\vartheta)\bigr|\leq C\,\rho^{\gamma-1}+C\,\vartheta^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}\qquad\hbox{ with }2\leq\gamma_{\vartheta}<{\alpha}/{2}.\\
\end{split}

Finally the initial data has to satisfy

(3.12)

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\in L^{\gamma}(\Pi^{d}),\quad\vartheta_{0}\in L^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}(\Pi^{d})

\displaystyle\hbox{ with }\rho_{0}\geq 0,\quad\vartheta_{0}>0\hbox{ in }\Pi^{d}\qquad\hbox{ and }\int_{\Pi^{d}}\rho_{0}=M_{0}>0,

and

(3.13)

{\mathcal{E}}_{0}=\int_{\Pi^{d}}\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\frac{|(\rho u)_{0}|^{2}}{\rho_{0}}+\rho_{0}e(\rho_{0},\vartheta_{0})\Bigr)<+\infty.

3.3.
The truncated virial pressure law satisfies the properties needed for the estimates above

The truncated pressure law mentioned in the introduction

P(\rho,\vartheta)=\rho^{\gamma}+\vartheta\,\sum_{n=0}^{N}B_{n}(\vartheta)\,\rho^{n}

with
\gamma>2N\geq 4
satisfy the assumptions described before. Choosing
m=constant
for simplicity in this example, this leads to

e(\rho,\vartheta)=m+\frac{\rho^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}-\sum_{n\geq 2}^{N}\vartheta^{2}\,B_{n}^{\prime}(\vartheta)\,\frac{\rho^{n-1}}{n-1}-\vartheta^{2}B^{\prime}_{1}(\vartheta)\log\rho+\vartheta^{2}B^{\prime}_{0}(\vartheta)\frac{1}{\rho},

For simplicity, let us assume that
B_{1}=constant
, which is the normal virial assumption, so that this term vanishes.
The entropy reads

s(\rho,\vartheta)=-\sum_{n\geq 2}^{N}(\vartheta\,B_{n}^{\prime}(\vartheta)+B_{n}(\vartheta))\,\frac{\rho^{n-1}}{n-1}+B_{1}\,\log\rho+(\vartheta B_{0}^{\prime}(\vartheta)+B_{0}(\vartheta))\frac{1}{\rho}.

1) We assume that the pressure contains a radiative part, namely that
B_{0}
is convex in
\vartheta
with
C^{-1}\,\vartheta^{\gamma_{\vartheta}-1}\leq B_{0}(\vartheta)\leq\vartheta^{\gamma_{\vartheta}-1}
and
C^{-1}\,\vartheta^{\gamma_{\vartheta}-2}\leq B_{0}^{\prime}(\vartheta)\leq\vartheta^{\gamma_{\vartheta}-2}
where
2\leq\gamma_{\vartheta}\leq\alpha/2
. This already satisfies (
3.10
).

2) For
n\geq 2
, the coefficients
B_{n}
can have any sign but we require a concavity assumption:

\frac{d}{d\vartheta}(\vartheta^{2}\,B_{n}^{\prime})\leq 0.

This ensures, with the assumption on
B_{0}
, that the specific heat
C_{v}=\partial_{\vartheta}e(\rho,\vartheta)
satisfies (
3.9
). This is again a classical assumption for the virial. Note that it would be enough to ask this concavity of some of them and moreover that this is automatically satisfied if
B_{n}\sim-\vartheta^{\nu}
, that is precisely for the coefficients contributing to the non-monotony of
P
in
\rho
.

3) We also require some specific bounds on the
B_{n}
namely that there exist
\bar{B}_{n}
and
\varepsilon>0
s.t.

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\vartheta\,B_{n}^{\prime}(\vartheta)|+|B_{n}(\vartheta)|\leq C\,\vartheta^{\frac{\gamma-n}{\gamma}\,\gamma_{\vartheta}-1-\varepsilon},

(3.14)

\displaystyle\displaystyle|B_{n}(\vartheta)|+|\vartheta\,B_{n}(\vartheta)-\bar{B}_{n}|\leq C\vartheta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(1-\frac{2n}{\gamma}\right)-\varepsilon}.

First of all this gives us a bound from below on
e

\begin{split}e(\rho,\vartheta)&\geq m+\frac{\rho^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}+C^{-1}\,\frac{\vartheta^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}}{\rho}-\sum_{n=2}^{N}\vartheta\,\vartheta^{\frac{\gamma-n}{\gamma}\,\gamma_{\vartheta}-1-\varepsilon}\,\frac{\rho^{n-1}}{n-1}\\
&\geq m+\frac{\rho^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}+C^{-1}\,\frac{\vartheta^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}}{\rho}-C\,\sum_{n=2}^{N}\left(\rho^{\gamma-1-\varepsilon^{\prime}}+\frac{\vartheta^{\gamma_{\vartheta}-\varepsilon^{\prime}}}{\rho}\right),\end{split}

by Young’s inequality, so that this implies (
3.7
)
2
. Assumption (
3.11
) is proved with an identical calculation. The same calculation also proves Assumption (
3.7
)
1
by showing that
s\leq C\,(\rho^{\gamma-1}+\vartheta^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}+1)
.

4) Then choosing

(3.15)

\phi(\rho)=\frac{\rho^{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}+\sum_{2\leq n\leq N}\bar{B}_{n}\,\frac{\rho^{n}}{n-1}+\bar{B}_{0},

and using again the second part of (
3.14
)
we have that

\begin{split}|P-\phi^{\prime}(\rho)\,\rho+\phi(\rho)|&\leq C\,\sum_{n\leq N}|\vartheta\,B_{n}(\vartheta)-\bar{B}_{n}|\,\rho^{n}\leq C\,\sum_{n\leq N}\vartheta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(1-\frac{2n}{\gamma}\right)-\varepsilon}\,\rho^{n}\\
&\leq C\,N\,(\rho^{\gamma/2}+\vartheta^{\alpha/2-\varepsilon}),\end{split}

still by Young’s inequality. This yields the wanted estimate with the right inequalities on
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
.
The same calculation also proves that
|\partial_{\vartheta}P|\leq C\,(\rho^{\gamma/2+\varepsilon^{\prime}}+\vartheta^{\alpha/2-\varepsilon^{\prime}})
with required assumptions on
\beta_{3},\;\beta_{4}
.

Note for a fixed
\vartheta
then
P(\rho,\vartheta)
is indeed increasing with respect to
\rho
after a critical
\bar{\rho}_{\vartheta}
which depends on
\vartheta
and can be arbitrarily large where
\vartheta>>1
. This is the reason why
P
does not satisfy any of the classical monotonicity assumption and why our new approach is needed.
Our pressure law has two important parts: the radiative term (corresponding to
n=0
) to get compactness on the temperature and the
\phi(\rho)
term to get compactness on the density
in time and space.

Remark 3.1
.

In our work, the viscosity coefficients
\mu,\;\lambda
are independent of the temperature
\vartheta
. Instead several models use temperature dependent coefficients
\mu(\vartheta),\;\lambda(\vartheta)
. To handle that case, the proof given below would have to be modified; the compactness of the temperature would have to be established first following what has been done in previous work
for monotone pressure laws in previous works.

4.
The new strategy to get global existence of weak solutions

The main difficulty is the construction of regular enough solutions of some approximate system that will allow us to derive our key a priori estimates and pass to the limit. We obtain solutions of the approximate system through a fixed point argument that strongly relies on our recent paper
[
3
]
.

More precisely, we consider the following
First Step:

We start with a prescribed temperature
\vartheta
so that
p(\rho,\vartheta(t,x))
satisfies the assumptions in
[
3
]
for an heterogeneous pressure
p(\rho,t,x)
.
This provides a map
\vartheta\mapsto(\rho,u)
with
(\rho,u)
a global weak solution of

(4.1)

\partial_{t}\rho+{\rm div}(\rho u)=0,

and

(4.2)

\partial_{t}(\rho u)+{\rm div}(\rho u\otimes u)-\mu\Delta u-(\lambda+\mu)\nabla{\rm div}u+\nabla(p(\rho,\vartheta(t,x)))=0.

The novelty in the present paper is the
Second Step
:
The construction of the associated temperature through the energy equation corresponding to the pressure without the barotropic part
\rho^{\gamma-1}/(\gamma-1)

namely the one corresponding to

\widetilde{P}(\rho,\vartheta)=\vartheta\sum_{n=0}^{N}B_{n}(\vartheta)\rho^{n}.

To do so, we rewrite the energy equation

(4.3)

\partial_{t}(\rho\widetilde{e})+{\rm div}(\rho\widetilde{e}u)+\widetilde{P}{\rm div}u={\mathcal{S}}:\nabla u+{\rm div}(\kappa(\vartheta)\nabla\vartheta)

in terms of a quasi-linear parabolic equation on
\widetilde{g}=\rho\widetilde{e}
.

Our goal is then to complete the fixed point argument by solving this equation for a fixed density
\rho
and velocity field
u
. This is a non-trivial problem as the equation is singular and it requires several extra steps:

•

First of all, we need to regularize
\rho
and
u
in space and time, be far from vacuum for the density and remove the singularity in
\widetilde{e}
near the
0
temperature using a parameter
\varepsilon
. That will allow us to use in a first step the classical result
[
12
]
by O.A. Ladyzenskaya, V.A. Solonnikov, N.N. Uraltceva and get existence (
[
12
]
) of some classical solution
\widetilde{g}_{\varepsilon}
for the regularized equation.

•

In a second step, we may pass to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
to obtain the actual solution
\widetilde{g}
, using the expected a priori estimates for classical solutions.

•

The third step consists in recovering the temperature
\vartheta

such that
\rho\widetilde{e}(\rho,\vartheta)=\widetilde{g}
using an implicit function
procedure thanks to the key property
\partial_{\vartheta}\widetilde{e}>0
and the fact
that
\vartheta
is more regular.

•

The last step
consists in deriving uniform estimates on
\vartheta
by transforming estimates from step 1 and making sure that there are uniform in the various regularizing parameters. This uniform
estimate is obtained through the entropy equation derived from the
energy equation as explained earlier.

Once this is done, we obtain a map on the temperature
\vartheta
: From an initial
\vartheta_{i}
, we obtain
(\rho,\,u)
solving (
4.1
)-(
4.2
). We then obtain the “new” temperature
\vartheta
that solves (
4.3
) for those
\rho
and
u
.

The
Third and Last Step
is then to get a fixed point on the temperature, for example through Schauder theorem, we need to obtain some compactness on the map. This turns out to be rather straightforward: if
\vartheta_{i}
is bounded in some appropriate Sobolev space, then
\log\vartheta
belongs to some
H^{1}
and we can derive compactness in space and time using the radiative part in the pressure law.

5.

\rho
and
u
given
\vartheta

The goal of this section is to obtain existence of appropriate solutions
\rho
and

if we already know the temperature

. This will form the first step in our final fixed point argument.

This step heavily relies on the existence result already obtained in

and then

are both strictly positive.
∎

6.4.
Existence of solutions
\vartheta
such that the entropy defined by (
1.9
) satisfies the equation (
1.10
).

From the previous result, we may pass to the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
to obtain the following existence result.

Theorem 6.4
.

Assume that
\rho^{0}\in L^{\gamma}_{x}
,
\vartheta^{0}\in L^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}_{x}
. Assume moreover that
\rho\in L^{\infty}([0,\ T],\;L^{\gamma}(\Pi^{d}))
and
u\in L^{2}([0,\ T],\;H^{1}(\Pi^{d}))
and solve the continuity equation (
1.2
). Then there exists
\vartheta\in L^{\infty}(0,T,\;L^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}(\Pi^{d}))\cap L^{\alpha}([0,\ T]\times\Pi^{d})
such that

(6.29)

\sup_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\vartheta^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\kappa(\vartheta)\,\frac{|\nabla\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta^{2}}\,dx\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{\vartheta}\,dx\,dt\leq C(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}},\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}),

for some constant depending on the norms
(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}},\,\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}
and on the initial data
\rho^{0}
and
\vartheta^{0}
).
Furthermore, defining the entropy through the relation

(6.30)

\rho\,s=\rho\,\left(-\sum_{n=2}^{N}\widetilde{B}_{n}(\vartheta)\,\frac{\rho^{n-1}}{n-1}+\frac{1}{\rho}\,\widetilde{B}_{0}(\vartheta)\right),\quad\widetilde{B}_{n}^{\prime}(\vartheta)=\vartheta\,B_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\vartheta)+2\,B_{n}^{\prime}(\vartheta),

then

(6.31)

\|\rho\,s\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}+\|\rho s\,u\|_{L^{1}_{t,x}}\leq C(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}},\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}),

and
\rho\,s
solves the following inequation in the sense of distributions

(6.32)

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}(\rho\,s)+\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(\rho\,s\,u)\geq\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\,\nabla_{x}\vartheta\right)+\frac{1}{\vartheta}\,\mathcal{S}:\nabla u+\kappa(\vartheta)\,\frac{|\nabla\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta^{2}}\end{split}

with an initial condition satisfied weakly through the following inequality

(6.33)

\rho s|_{t=0+}\geq\rho_{0}s(\rho_{0},\vartheta_{0}).

Finally defining
g
through the identities (
6.2
)-(
6.3
), we also have the energy equality

(6.34)

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g(t,x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g^{0}(x)\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\mathcal{S}:\nabla u-\widetilde{P}\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u)(s,x)\,ds\,dx.

Proof.

The strategy of the proof is straightforward. Given
u\in L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
, we construct
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}
that converges to
u
strongly in
L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
. For simplicity, we consider here an approximation by convolution. Given
\rho\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}
, we construct
\rho_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}
by convoluting through the same kernel, uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}
and converging to
\rho
in
L^{p}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}
for every
p<\infty
. Observe that the standard commutator estimate implies that

\partial_{t}\rho_{\varepsilon}+\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(\rho_{\varepsilon}\,u_{\varepsilon})=R_{\varepsilon},

where
R_{\varepsilon}\to 0
in
L^{2}_{t}L^{p}_{x}
with
1/p=1/2+1/\gamma
.

We also choose
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{0}=\varepsilon+\vartheta^{0}
which is uniformly in
L^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}_{x}
. For any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, we then obtain a classical solution
g_{\varepsilon}
to (
6.12
) with (
6.16
)-(
6.17
). We then have to pass to the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
in the system.

Uniform bounds.

The critical point is hence to derive appropriate estimates on
g_{\varepsilon}
and
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}
that are uniform in
\varepsilon
. This is naturally based on equivalent energy and entropy estimates.
To start with the energy, by directly integrating the equation on
g_{\varepsilon}
first in space and then in time, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g_{\varepsilon}^{0}(x)\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}:\nabla u_{\varepsilon}-\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u_{\varepsilon})(s,x)\,dx\,ds.

From our assumptions on the initial conditions
\rho^{0}
and
\vartheta^{0}
, we have uniform bounds on
g_{\varepsilon}^{0}
in
L^{1}
. Indeed for any
n
,
(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{0})^{n}
converges to
(\rho^{0})^{n}
strongly in
L^{\gamma/n}_{x}
. From assumption (
1.16
),
(\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{0})^{2}\,B_{n}^{\prime}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{0})
converges strongly in
L^{\gamma/(\gamma-n)}
. Since
\frac{\gamma-n}{\gamma}+\frac{n}{\gamma}=1
, we directly obtains that
g_{\varepsilon}^{0}
converges to
g^{0}
strongly in
L^{1}_{x}
and that it is uniformly bounded in
L^{1}_{x}
.

Moreover by convexity of the
H^{1}
norm, we also have that

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}:\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\,dx\,ds\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}^{2}.

This yields that

(6.35)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx\leq&C+\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}^{2}+\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}\,\|\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}.\\
\end{split}

It remains to control the norm in the right-hand side. From the definition of
\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}
, we have that

\|\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\leq\sum_{n=0}^{N}\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\,B_{n}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\rho_{\varepsilon}^{n}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}+\varepsilon\,\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\,\log\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}.

From the
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}
bound on
\rho
, this implies that

\|\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\leq\sum_{n=0}^{N}\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\,B_{n}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{q_{n}}_{x}}\,\|\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}}^{n}+\varepsilon\,\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\,\log\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}},

with
1/q_{n}=1/2-n/\gamma
or
q_{n}=2\,\gamma/(\gamma-2n)
.

We may now use assumption (
1.17
) to further bound

\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\,B_{n}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{q_{n}}_{x}}\leq C+C\,\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\bar{\alpha}}_{x}}^{\bar{\alpha}\,\frac{\gamma-2n}{2\,\gamma}}.

This lets us deduce that for some
\beta<1/2
,

\sup_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx\leq C(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}},\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}})\,(1+\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\bar{\alpha}}_{x}}^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}}).

For further use in a later section, we also note that we have the more precise estimate

(6.36)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}|\,|\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u_{\varepsilon}|\,dx\,dt\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}\,\left(\frac{\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}}^{\gamma/2}}{C}+C\,\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\bar{\alpha}}_{x}}^{\bar{\alpha}/2}\right).

From the definition of
g_{\varepsilon}
, we also have that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx\geq\frac{1}{C}\,\int\vartheta^{\gamma_{\theta}}\,dx-\sum_{n=2}^{N}\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,B_{n}^{\prime}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\rho_{\varepsilon}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}.

Using again the
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}
bound on
\rho
, this implies that

\sum_{n=2}^{N}\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,B_{n}^{\prime}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\rho_{\varepsilon}^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\leq\sum_{n=0}^{N}\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,B_{n}^{\prime}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\widetilde{q}_{n}}_{x}}\,\|\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}}^{n},

with
1/\widetilde{q}_{n}=1-n/\gamma
.

Now use assumption (
1.16
) to obtain, again for some
\widetilde{\beta}<1
,

\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,B_{n}^{\prime}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\widetilde{q}_{n}}_{x}}\leq C\,\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}_{x}}^{\widetilde{\beta}\,\gamma_{\vartheta}\,(\gamma-n)/\gamma}.

This shows that, again for some
\widetilde{\beta}<1
,

\sup_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx\geq\frac{1}{C}\,\sup_{t}\,\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma_{\theta}}\,dx-C\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}}^{\gamma}\,-C\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}_{x}}^{\widetilde{\beta}\,\gamma_{\vartheta}}.

For further use, we even have the more precise estimate

(6.37)

\sup_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx\geq\frac{1}{C}\,\sup_{t}\,\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma_{\theta}}\,dx-C-\frac{1}{C}\,\sup_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho^{\gamma}\,dx.

Therefore, inserting those estimates into (
6.35
) yields that

(6.38)

\sup_{t}\,\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma_{\theta}}\,dx\leq C+C\,\sup_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx\leq C+C(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}},\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}})\,(1+\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\bar{\alpha}}_{x}}^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}}).

On its own, we cannot obtain a priori estimates just from (
6.38
) and we need also an entropy bound.
Since for
\varepsilon>0
,
g_{\varepsilon}
and
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}
are smooth and
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}>0
, we can define

s_{\varepsilon}=\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\,\log\vartheta_{\varepsilon}-\sum_{n=2}^{N}\widetilde{B}_{n}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\frac{\rho_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}}{n-1}+\frac{1}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\,\widetilde{B}_{0}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon}),

with
d\widetilde{B}_{n}(\vartheta)/d\vartheta=\vartheta\,B_{n}^{\prime\prime}(\vartheta)+2\,B_{n}^{\prime}(\vartheta)
so that
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\,\frac{\partial s_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\vartheta}=\frac{\partial\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\vartheta}
.

Note that

\partial_{t}g_{\varepsilon}=\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\vartheta}\,\partial_{t}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}+\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}\,\partial_{t}\rho_{\varepsilon},

where we recall that

\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\vartheta}=\varepsilon-\sum_{2\leq n\leq N}\frac{d}{d\vartheta}(\vartheta\,^{2}B_{n}^{\prime}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon}))\frac{\rho^{n}}{n-1}+\frac{d}{d\vartheta}(\vartheta^{2}B_{0}^{\prime}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon}))>0,

for any
\varepsilon>0
and for
g_{\varepsilon}=\rho_{\varepsilon}\,\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}
,

\frac{\partial\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}=-\varepsilon\,\frac{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}^{2}}-\sum_{n=2}^{N}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,B_{n}^{\prime}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\rho_{\varepsilon}^{n-2}-\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,B_{0}^{\prime}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\frac{1}{\rho_{\varepsilon}^{2}}.

This lets us write that

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\vartheta}\,(\partial_{t}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}+u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon})=-g_{\varepsilon}\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u_{\varepsilon}-\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}\,(\partial_{t}\rho_{\varepsilon}+u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\rho_{\varepsilon})-\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u_{\varepsilon}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}:\nabla u_{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}(\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}).\end{split}

Since we have kept the critical relation,

\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}=\rho^{2}\,\frac{\partial\widetilde{e}_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}+\vartheta\,\frac{\partial\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\vartheta},

this yields

\begin{split}&\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\vartheta}\,(\partial_{t}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}+u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon})=-\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}\,R_{\varepsilon}-\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\,\frac{\partial\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\vartheta}\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u_{\varepsilon}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}:\nabla u_{\varepsilon}+\nabla_{x}(\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}).\end{split}

Because the critical relation above also implies that

\frac{\partial s_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}=-\frac{1}{\rho^{2}}\,\frac{\partial\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\vartheta},

we finally deduce that

(6.39)

\begin{split}&\partial_{t}(\rho_{\varepsilon}\,s_{\varepsilon})+\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(\rho_{\varepsilon}\,s_{\varepsilon}\,u_{\varepsilon})=\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\,\nabla_{x}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad R_{\varepsilon}\,\left(s_{\varepsilon}+\rho_{\varepsilon}\,\frac{\partial s_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}-\frac{1}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\,\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}\right)+\frac{1}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\,\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}:\nabla u_{\varepsilon}+\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\frac{|\nabla\vartheta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{2}}.\end{split}

The first key point to make use of (
6.39
) is that we still have that
\rho_{\varepsilon}\,s_{\varepsilon}\leq C\,g_{\varepsilon}
. Hence

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\frac{|\nabla\vartheta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\,dx\,dt\leq&C\,\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g_{\varepsilon}(t=T,x)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{0}\,s_{\varepsilon}^{0}(x)\,dx\\
&+\|R_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{p}_{x}}\,\left\|s_{\varepsilon}+\rho_{\varepsilon}\,\frac{\partial s_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}-\frac{1}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\,\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{p^{*}}_{x}}.\end{split}

We may hence immediately use (
6.38
) together with the
H^{1}
estimate on
u_{\varepsilon}
. We note that, just as for
g_{\varepsilon}^{0}
, we have initial uniform bounds on
\rho_{\varepsilon}^{0}\,s^{0}_{\varepsilon}
in
L^{1}
. Indeed since
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{0}=\vartheta^{0}+\varepsilon
, and
\vartheta^{0}\in L^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}
then

\|\varepsilon\,\log\vartheta^{0}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\varepsilon\,|\log\varepsilon|+\varepsilon\,\|\vartheta^{0}\|_{L^{1}}\to 0,\quad\mbox{as}\ \varepsilon\to 0.

Moreover, again using assumption (
1.16
), we also have the strong convergence in
L^{1}
of
\widetilde{B}_{n}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{0})\,(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{0})^{n}
just as for
g_{\varepsilon}^{0}
. Consequently we have the strong convergence in
L^{1}_{x}
of
\rho_{\varepsilon}^{0}\,s_{\varepsilon}^{0}
to
\rho^{0}\,s^{0}
with a uniform bound in
\varepsilon
which allows to derive

(6.40)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\frac{|\nabla\vartheta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\,dx\,dt\leq&C+\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}^{2}+C(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}},\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}})\,(1+\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\bar{\alpha}}_{x}}^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}})\\
&+\|R_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{p}_{x}}\,\left\|s_{\varepsilon}+\rho_{\varepsilon}\,\frac{\partial s_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}-\frac{1}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\,\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{p^{*}}_{x}}.\end{split}

Our second critical point is that we have a simplified expression

s_{\varepsilon}+\rho_{\varepsilon}\,\frac{\partial s_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}-\frac{1}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\,\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}=\sum_{n=2}^{N}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\,B_{n}^{\prime}-\widetilde{B}_{n})\,\frac{n}{n-1}\,\rho_{\varepsilon}^{n-1},

where the
\log\vartheta_{\varepsilon}
term vanish. In particular this expression is smooth around
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}=0
and only blows up as
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\to\infty
.

Recalling the definition of
\widetilde{B}_{n}
, we note that

\frac{d}{d\vartheta}(\vartheta\,B_{n}^{\prime}-\widetilde{B}_{n})=B_{n}^{\prime}+\vartheta\,B_{n}^{\prime\prime}-\vartheta\,B_{n}^{\prime\prime}-2B_{n}^{\prime}=-B_{n}^{\prime},

so that in the end

s_{\varepsilon}+\rho_{\varepsilon}\,\frac{\partial s_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}-\frac{1}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\,\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}=\sum_{n=2}^{N}B_{n}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\frac{n}{n-1}\,\rho_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}.

Using the
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}
bound on
\rho
, this implies that

\left\|s_{\varepsilon}+\rho_{\varepsilon}\,\frac{\partial s_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}-\frac{1}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\,\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{p^{*}}_{x}}\leq C\,\sum_{n=2}^{N}\left\|B_{n}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\rho_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{p^{*}}_{x}}\leq C\,\sum_{n=2}^{N}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}}^{n-1}\,\left\|B_{n}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{p_{n}}_{x}},

with

p_{n}=\frac{1}{p^{*}}-\frac{n-1}{\gamma}=1-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n-1}{\gamma}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\gamma}-\frac{n-1}{\gamma}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{\gamma}=q_{n},

which was the exponent defined earlier when controlling the norm of
\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}
in
L^{2}
.

Therefore this term can be bounded in exactly the same way through assumption (
1.17
), yielding

\left\|s_{\varepsilon}+\rho_{\varepsilon}\,\frac{\partial s_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}-\frac{1}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\,\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{p^{*}}_{x}}\leq C+C(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}})\,(1+\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\bar{\alpha}}_{x}}^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}}).

This let us obtain by adding (
6.40
) and (
6.38
) that

(6.41)

\sup_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\frac{|\nabla\vartheta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\,dx\,dt\leq C+C(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}},\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}})\,(1+\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\bar{\alpha}}_{x}}^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}}).

We are now ready to conclude our a priori estimates. By assumption (
1.11
) and Poincaré inequalities,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\bar{\alpha}}\,dx\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}\,dx\right)^{\bar{\alpha}/\gamma_{\vartheta}}+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\frac{|\nabla\vartheta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\,dx,

so that, since
\alpha\geq 2
,

(6.42)

\|\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\bar{\alpha}}_{x}}^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}}\leq C\,T^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/2}\,\left(\sup_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}\,dx\right)^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/\gamma_{\vartheta}}+C_{T}\,\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\frac{|\nabla\vartheta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\beta}.

Because
\beta<1/2
and
\bar{\alpha}\leq 2\,\gamma_{\vartheta}
, this inequality is enough to bound the right-hand side of (
6.41
) in terms of its left-hand side. Hence we eventually have the uniform in
\varepsilon
estimates

(6.43)

\sup_{\varepsilon}\sup_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}\,dx+\sup_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\frac{|\nabla\vartheta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\,dx\,dt\leq C(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}},\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}).

Those bounds directly imply that
\vartheta\in L^{\infty}([0,\ T],\;L^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}(\Pi^{d}))\cap L^{\alpha}([0,\ T]\times\Pi^{d})
as claimed.
Because
\kappa(\vartheta)\geq 1
, (
6.43
) also shows that

(6.44)

\begin{split}&\sup_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla\log\vartheta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}},\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}),\\
&\mbox{and}\ \sup_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\log\vartheta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}},\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}),\end{split}

again by Poincaré inequality.

To conclude those a priori estimates, note that we finally have that

(6.45)

\sup_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{\vartheta}\,dx\,dt\leq C(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}},\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}).

Limit passage
\varepsilon\to 0
.

We can now send
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
to get a weak solution of (
6.12
).
From our previous estimates, we know that
g_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}_{x}
for some
p>1
. This lets us extract a sub-sequence, still denoted
g_{\varepsilon}
, that converges weak-* to some
g
in
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{p}_{x}
for some
p>1
.

To derive the compactness on
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}
through the classical Aubin-Lions approach, we require controls on
g_{\varepsilon}\,u_{\varepsilon}
and
\rho_{\varepsilon}\,s_{\varepsilon}\,u_{\varepsilon}
. We may bound directly by Sobolev embeddings

\|g_{\varepsilon}\,u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}_{t,x}}\leq\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}\,\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}.

It is straightforward to bound the
L^{2}
norm of
g_{\varepsilon}
in the same manner as we bounded the
L^{2}
norm of
\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}
earlier: Assumption (
1.17
) indeed implies the same behavior for
\vartheta^{2}\,B_{n}^{\prime}(\vartheta)
and
\vartheta\,B_{n}(\vartheta)
.

For further use, we also observe that by using the
\varepsilon>0
in (
1.17
), we may use some interpolation on
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}
between
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}_{x}
and
L^{2}_{t}L^{\bar{\alpha}}_{x}
, leading actually to

(6.46)

\|g_{\varepsilon}\,u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}}\leq C(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}},\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}),

for some
p>1
. The same applies to
\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}
so that we also have that

(6.47)

\|\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}_{t,x}}\leq C(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}},\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}),

for some
q>1
.

A similar discussion applies to
\rho_{\varepsilon}\,s_{\varepsilon}
, with in fact much simpler estimates. First of all
\widetilde{B}_{n}
behaves like
\vartheta\,B_{n}^{\prime\prime}+2\,B_{n}^{\prime}
instead of
\vartheta^{2}\,B_{n}^{\prime}(\vartheta)
so that the coefficients
n\geq 2
in the expansion are easier to handle than for
g_{\varepsilon}
. Secondly, the
\varepsilon\,\log\vartheta_{\varepsilon}
in
\rho_{\varepsilon}\,s_{\varepsilon}
is immediately bounded by (
6.44
). Hence we also have that

(6.48)

\|\rho_{\varepsilon}\,s_{\varepsilon}\,u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}_{t,x}}\leq C(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}},\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}),

for some
p>1
.

We now turn to the compactness argument. We may extract a subsequence
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}
, converging weak-* to
\vartheta
in
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}_{x}
. Furthermore by (
6.43
) it follows that
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}
is compact in space. Since
\rho_{\varepsilon}
is also compact in space, the definition (
6.18
) of
g_{\varepsilon}
together with our a priori estimates directly implies that
g_{\varepsilon}
is compact in space. For similar reasons,
\rho_{\varepsilon}\,s_{\varepsilon}
is compact in space.

We now obtain from Equation (
6.15
) and Equation (
6.39
) that both
\partial_{t}g_{\varepsilon}
and
\partial_{t}(\rho_{\varepsilon}\,s_{\varepsilon})
are bounded in
L^{1}_{t}W^{-1,1}_{x}
thanks to (
6.46
)-(
6.48
) and our previous a priori estimates. By Aubin-Lions, this shows that
g_{\varepsilon}
and
\rho_{\varepsilon}\,s_{\varepsilon}
are compact in
L^{1}_{t,x}
.

Upon further extraction, we may therefore assume that both
g_{\varepsilon}
and
\rho_{\varepsilon}\,s_{\varepsilon}
converge pointwise
a.e.
respectively to
g
and some
S
. Of course
\rho_{\varepsilon}
converges
a.e.
to
\rho
. By assumptions (
1.12
) and (
1.15
),
\partial_{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}g_{\varepsilon}\geq 0
and more precisely
\partial_{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}g_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly away from
0
for
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}>\underline{\vartheta}
for any
\underline{\vartheta}>0
. This proves that for a fixed value of
\rho_{\varepsilon}(t,x)
,
g_{\varepsilon}=g_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{\varepsilon},\vartheta_{\varepsilon})
is one-to-one in
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}
.

The pointwise convergence of
g_{\varepsilon}
therefore implies the pointwise convergence of
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}
to some
\vartheta
, and hence the compactness and convergence of
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}
to
\vartheta
in
L^{1}_{t,x}
. A first consequence is that we may pass to the limit in (
6.18
) and obtain that the limits
\rho
,
\vartheta
and
g
solve (
6.3
). Similarly
\rho
,
\vartheta
and
\rho\,s
solve (
6.30
).

Energy equation (
6.34
).

It remains to pass to the limit in the integral of Equation (
6.15
) on
g_{\varepsilon}
and in Equation (
6.39
). Since
u_{\varepsilon}
is converging
a.e.
to
u
, we have the
a.e.
convergence of
g_{\varepsilon}\,u_{\varepsilon}
and
\rho_{\varepsilon}\,s_{\varepsilon}\,u_{\varepsilon}
to respectively
g\,u
and
\rho\,s\,u
. By the equi-integrability provided by (
6.46
) and (
6.48
), we can apply dominated convergence and obtain the strong convergence of
g_{\varepsilon}\,u_{\varepsilon}
and
\rho_{\varepsilon}\,s_{\varepsilon}\,u_{\varepsilon}
.

Obviously we directly have the strong convergence of
\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}:\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
. We also have pointwise convergence inside the formula (
6.19
) defining
\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}
so that
\rho
,
\vartheta
and
\widetilde{P}
satisfy (
6.4
). By (
6.47
), we hence have that
\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}
converges to
\widetilde{P}
in
L^{2}_{t,x}
, again by dominated convergence. Since
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u_{\varepsilon}
converges strongly to
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u
in
L^{2}_{t,x}
, this yields the convergence of
\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u_{\varepsilon}
.
It is now possible to integrate Equation (
6.15
) and pass to the limit in all resulting terms to obtain the claimed energy equality (
6.34
).

Entropy inequation (
6.32
).

It remains to derive the limit of Equation (
6.39
) on
\rho_{\varepsilon}\,s_{\varepsilon}
. Our previous analysis shows that

R_{\varepsilon}\,\left(s_{\varepsilon}+\rho_{\varepsilon}\,\frac{\partial s_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}-\frac{1}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\,\frac{\partial g_{\varepsilon}}{\partial\rho}\right)\longrightarrow 0,

strongly as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

We can also prove
that
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\left(\frac{\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\,\nabla\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\right)\rightarrow\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\left(\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)}{\vartheta}\,\nabla\vartheta\right)
in the distribution sense. Denoting
\widetilde{\kappa}(\vartheta)
s.t.
\widetilde{\kappa}^{\prime}=\frac{\kappa(\vartheta)}{\vartheta}
, we note that

\frac{\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\,\nabla\vartheta_{\varepsilon}=\nabla(\widetilde{\kappa}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})).

As before
\widetilde{\kappa}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})
converges
a.e.
to
\widetilde{\kappa}(\vartheta)
. By assumption (
1.11
),
|\widetilde{\kappa}(\vartheta)|\leq C\,(\log\vartheta+\vartheta^{\alpha})
. On the other hand, by combining (
1.11
) and (
6.43
), we also have that

\sup_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha-2}\,|\nabla\vartheta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx\,dt=\sup_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha/2}|^{2}\,dx\,dt.

By Sobolev embedding, we have that
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\alpha}_{t}L^{\alpha\,2^{*}/2}_{x}
with
1/2^{*}=1/2-1/d
(or
2^{*}<\infty
for
d=2
). By interpolation with the uniform bound in
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}_{x}
, we obtain a uniform bound for
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}
in
L^{p}_{t,x}
for some
p>\alpha
. As a consequence
\widetilde{\kappa}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})
is equi-integrable and converges strongly in
L^{1}_{t,x}
to
\widetilde{\kappa}(\vartheta)
, proving the required limit.

It is important to highlight that the same argument would not apply to
the limit of
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits\left(\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\nabla\vartheta_{\varepsilon}\right)
. Any anti-derivative of
\kappa(\vartheta)
behaves like
\vartheta^{\alpha+1}
as
\vartheta\to\infty
. Therefore it would not in general be possible to control it through our a priori estimates. This is the main objection that prevents us from passing to the limit in the whole equation (
6.15
) for
g_{\varepsilon}
.

We are also not able to pass to the limit in the two remaining terms in the right-hand side of Equation (
6.39
). We have for example the
a.e.
convergence of
\frac{1}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\,\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}:\nabla u_{\varepsilon}
but we cannot prove equi-integrability, as
\frac{1}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}
could be large. However we can obtain inequalities which lead to the limiting inequation.

We recall that the function
(a,b)\to\frac{a^{2}}{b}
is jointly convex in
(a,b)
. Consequently if some functions
a_{n},\,b_{n}
converge to functions
a,\,b
in
L^{1}
(or even in some appropriate weak topology) then
\frac{a^{2}}{b}\leq\liminf\frac{a^{2}_{n}}{b_{n}}
. This immediately implies that

\frac{1}{\vartheta}\,\mathcal{S}:\nabla u\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}}\,\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}:\nabla u_{\varepsilon}.

Second by denoting
\bar{\kappa}(\vartheta)
s.t.
\bar{\kappa}^{\prime}=(\kappa(\vartheta))^{1/2}/\vartheta
, we have that

\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\frac{|\nabla\vartheta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{2}}=|\nabla\bar{\kappa}(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})|^{2}.

Therefore, we also have that

\kappa(\vartheta)\,\frac{|\nabla\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta^{2}}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\kappa(\vartheta_{\varepsilon})\,\frac{|\nabla\vartheta_{\varepsilon}|^{2}}{\vartheta_{\varepsilon}^{2}}.

The same arguments allow us to deduce (
6.29
) from our a priori estimates (
6.43
)-(
6.45
). Concerning
(
6.33
), we use that

\tau\in[0,T]\mapsto\int_{\Omega}(\rho s)(t,\cdot)\varphi dx\qquad\varphi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{1}_{c}(\Omega)\hbox{ and }\varphi\geq 0

is a sum of a non-decreasing function and a continuous function taking
advantage of the entropy inequality. This completes the proof.
∎

7.
Third Step: Fixed Point procedure and proof of main result

We are now ready to prove our main result. Denote

E=L^{\alpha-\varepsilon^{\prime}}([0,T]\times\Pi^{d})\cap L^{1}([0,\ T],\;W^{\lambda,1}(\Pi^{d})),

with
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
as in Theorem
5.2
and any
0<\lambda<1
. For any
R>0
, denote as well

E_{R}=\{\vartheta\in E\,|\;\|\vartheta\|_{L^{\alpha-\varepsilon^{\prime}}_{t,x}}+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{1}_{t}W^{\lambda,1}_{x}}\leq R\}.

We now define the operator
L
on
E
that will have a fixed point. For a given
\vartheta_{i}
in
E
, we may use Theorem
5.2
to obtain solutions
\rho
and
u
to (
1.2
)-(
1.3
), and satisfying the estimates

\rho\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x},\quad u\in L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}.

Hence
\rho
and
u
satisfy all the conditions in Theorem
6.34
. We may hence apply Theorem
6.34
to obtain
\vartheta=L(\vartheta_{i})
that solves the various estimates listed in the statement of Theorem
6.34
. In particular by (
6.29
), we immediately have, from the bounds (
1.11
) on
\kappa
, that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(1+|\vartheta|^{\alpha})\,\frac{|\nabla_{x}\vartheta|^{2}}{\vartheta^{2}}\,dx\,dt<\infty.

Since
\vartheta\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}_{x}
as well, Poincaré inequality immediately shows that
\vartheta\in L^{\alpha}_{t,x}
. Moreover we also have directly from the inequality above that
\vartheta\in L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
. Hence for any
0<\lambda<1
, we have that
\vartheta\in L^{1}_{t}W^{\lambda,1}_{x}
. This implies that
L:\,E\to E
.

We can also check that for any
R>0
, the image
L(E_{R})
is pre-compact in
E
.
Consider therefore any sequence
\vartheta_{n}\in E_{R}
.
From the estimates in Theorem
5.2
, we have that for some
C_{R}
and the
\rho_{n},\,u_{n}
obtained from
\vartheta_{n}
satisfy the uniform bound

\sup_{n}\|\rho_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}}+\sup_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}\leq C_{R}.

Moreover Theorem
5.2
also implies that
\rho_{n}
is compact in
L^{1}_{t,x}
.

Consequently, the estimates (
6.29
) from Theorem
6.34
yields, also for some
C_{R}

\sup_{n}\|L(\vartheta_{n})\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}}+\sup_{n}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(1+|L(\vartheta_{n})|^{\alpha})\,\frac{|\nabla_{x}L(\vartheta_{n})|^{2}}{L(\vartheta_{n})^{2}}\,dx\,dt\leq C_{R}.

We next observe that the entropy inequation (
6.32
) provides a uniform control on
\partial_{t}(\rho_{n}s_{n})
. We may indeed rewrite (
6.32
) as

\begin{split}\partial_{t}(\rho_{n}\,s_{n})+\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(\rho_{n}\,s_{n}\,u_{n})+M_{n}(t,x)=&\nabla_{x}\left(\frac{\kappa(L(\vartheta_{n}))}{L(\vartheta_{n})}\,\nabla_{x}L(\vartheta_{n})\right)+\frac{1}{L(\vartheta_{n})}\,\mathcal{S}_{n}:\nabla u_{n}\\
&+\kappa(L(\vartheta_{n}))\,\frac{|\nabla L(\vartheta_{n})|^{2}}{L(\vartheta_{n})^{2}},\end{split}

where
s_{n}=s(\rho_{n},L(\vartheta_{n}))
and
M_{n}
is a sequence of non-negative Radon measures. Hence by integrating in
t
and
x
, we have the bound

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}M(dt,dx)&=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho^{0}\,s(\rho^{0},\vartheta^{0})-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho_{n}\,s_{n}|_{t=T}\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\frac{1}{\vartheta_{n}}\,\mathcal{S}_{n}:\nabla u_{n}\,dx\,dt\\
&+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\kappa(L(\vartheta_{n}))\,\frac{|\nabla L(\vartheta_{n})|^{2}}{L(\vartheta_{n})}^{2}\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

From (
6.31
) and (
6.29
), we deduce immediately that the total mass of
M_{n}
, as a measure in
t
and
x
, is uniformly bounded in
n
. Using again (
6.31
) and (
6.29
), this implies that
\partial_{t}(\rho_{n}s_{n})
is uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{M}_{t,x}+L^{1}_{t}W^{-1,1}_{x}
, with
\mathcal{M}_{t,x}
the set of Radon measures with bounded mass.

From the compactness of
\rho_{n}
, the compactness in space of
L(\vartheta_{n})
, we have compactness in space for
\rho_{n}s_{n}
and now compactness in time. Up to extracting a subsequence, we can hence deduce the pointwise convergence of
\rho_{n}s_{n}
. Following the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
6.34
, and in particular assumption (
1.18
), this yields the pointwise convergence of
L(\vartheta_{n})
.

From the uniform bounds on
L(\vartheta_{n})
in
L^{\alpha}_{t,x}
, this in turn implies the compactness of
L(\vartheta_{n})
in
L^{\alpha-\varepsilon}_{t,x}
. By interpolation between
L^{\alpha-\varepsilon}_{t,x}
and
L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}
, we also obtain compactness in
L^{1}_{t}W^{\lambda,1}_{x}
, showing that the image
L(E_{R})
is pre-compact.

The last and more delicate point to use the Leray-Schauder fixed point is to show that there exists
R
s.t. for any
\vartheta\in E
with
\vartheta=\ell\,L(\vartheta)
for
\ell\in[0,\ 1]
, we have that
\vartheta\in E_{R}
. For such
\vartheta\in E
with
\vartheta=\ell\,L(\vartheta)
, we start with recalling from Theorem
5.2
that

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\rho^{\gamma}+\rho\,|u|^{2}/2)\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{S}:\nabla_{x}u\,dx\,ds\leq{\mathcal{E}}(\rho_{0},u_{0})\\
&\qquad+\,\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits_{x}u(s,x)\,(P(\vartheta(s,x),\rho(s,x))-P_{0}(\rho(s,x)))\,ds\,dx.\\
\end{split}

From the definition of
P_{0}
,

P-P_{0}=\sum_{n=0}^{N}(B_{n}(\vartheta)-\bar{B}_{n})\,\rho^{n}.

Therefore the coefficients in
P-P_{0}
behave in the same way as for
\widetilde{P}_{\varepsilon}
that we had used before and we may use (
6.36
) with

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|P-P_{0}|\,|\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u|\,dx\,dt\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}_{t}H^{1}_{x}}\,\left(\frac{\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}}^{\gamma/2}}{C}+C\,\|\vartheta\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\bar{\alpha}}_{x}}^{\bar{\alpha}/2}\right).

This implies that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\rho^{\gamma}+\rho\,|u|^{2}/2)\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}\,dx\,ds\leq C+C\,\|\vartheta\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\bar{\alpha}}_{x}}^{\bar{\alpha}}.

We will use as an intermediary object the function

(7.1)

\phi_{\vartheta}(t)=C+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}(s,x)\,dx\,ds+\|\vartheta\|_{L^{2}([0,\ t],\ L^{\bar{\alpha}}(\Pi^{d}))}^{\bar{\alpha}}.

It will be important to note that
\phi_{\vartheta}(t)
is continuous in time for a fixed choice of
\vartheta
, even if it is of course not equi-continuous for all possible choices of
\vartheta
.
On the other hand since
\vartheta=\ell\,L(\vartheta)
, any norm of
\vartheta
is bounded by the corresponding norm of
L(\vartheta)
. Using the Poincaré inequality (
6.42
), and since
\bar{\alpha}<\alpha
, this proves that

\|\vartheta\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\bar{\alpha}}_{x}}^{\bar{\alpha}}\leq C\,T^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/2}\,\left(\sup_{t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}L(\vartheta)^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}\,dx\right)^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/\gamma_{\vartheta}}+C_{T}\,\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\kappa(L(\vartheta))\,\frac{|\nabla L(\vartheta)|^{2}}{L(\vartheta)^{2}}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\beta},

for some
\beta<1
.

Therefore the norms of
\rho
and
u
together with
\phi_{\vartheta}
are controlled through the corresponding norms of
L(\vartheta)
,

(7.2)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\rho^{\gamma}+\rho\,|u|^{2}/2)\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}u|^{2}\,dx\,ds\leq C\,\phi_{\vartheta}(t)\\
&\ \leq C^{2}+C^{2}\,T^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/2}\,\left(\sup_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}L(\vartheta)^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}\,dx\right)^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/\gamma_{\vartheta}}\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+C_{T}\,\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\kappa(L(\vartheta))\,\frac{|\nabla L(\vartheta)|^{2}}{L(\vartheta)^{2}}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\beta},\end{split}

where the constant
C
depends only on the initial data and more precisely the initial total energy.
Turning to Theorem
6.34
, we recall the important Energy equality (
6.34
)
which implies that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g(t,x)\,dx\leq C+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\mathcal{S}:\nabla u-\widetilde{P}\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u)\,dx\,ds

for all time. Using again (
6.36
) and the Poincaré inequality (
6.42
) together with (
7.2
) to control
\int|\nabla u|^{2}
, we obtain that

(7.3)

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g(t,x)\,dx\leq C\,\phi_{\vartheta}(T)\\
&\quad\leq C^{2}+C^{2}\,T^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/2}\,\left(\sup_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}L(\vartheta)^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}\,dx\right)^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/\gamma_{\vartheta}}\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+C_{T}\,\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\kappa(L(\vartheta))\,\frac{|\nabla L(\vartheta)|^{2}}{L(\vartheta)^{2}}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\beta}.\end{split}

From (
6.37
), this shows that

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(L\vartheta)^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}(t,x)\,dx\leq C\,\phi_{\vartheta}(T)\\
&\quad\leq C^{2}+C^{2}\,T^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/2}\,\left(\sup_{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}L(\vartheta)^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}\,dx\right)^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/\gamma_{\vartheta}}+C_{T}\,\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\kappa(L(\vartheta))\,\frac{|\nabla L(\vartheta)|^{2}}{L(\vartheta)^{2}}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\beta}.\end{split}

On the other hand, since
\rho\,s\leq C\,g
, we also obtain by integrating the inequation (
6.32
),
and by combining the result with (
7.3
) and the previous inequality, we finally obtain the critical estimate

(7.4)

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(L\vartheta)^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}(t,x)\,dx+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\kappa(L(\vartheta))\,\frac{|\nabla L(\vartheta)|^{2}}{L(\vartheta)^{2}}\,dx\,dt\leq C\,\phi_{\vartheta}(t)\\
&\quad\leq C^{2}+C^{2}\,T^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/2}\,\left(\sup_{t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}L(\vartheta)^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}\,dx\right)^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/\gamma_{\vartheta}}\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt+C_{T}\,\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\kappa(L(\vartheta))\,\frac{|\nabla L(\vartheta)|^{2}}{L(\vartheta)^{2}}\,dx\,dt\right)^{\beta}.\end{split}

Of course
\beta<1
but unfortunately we only have
\bar{\alpha}\leq 2\,\gamma_{\vartheta}
so that we could have that
\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/\gamma_{\vartheta}>1
, which prevents us from concluding at once and forces us to employ a much more careful argument.
The key point is to use the time continuity of
\phi_{\vartheta}
defined in (
7.1
). Since
\beta<1
, denote

M=\sup_{X>0}C_{T=1}\,X^{\beta}-X/2,

and

\Phi=2\,C+3\,C_{T=1}\,C^{2}+2\,C_{T=1}\,M.

Assuming that
\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/\gamma_{\vartheta}>1
, we choose
T\leq 1
s.t.

T^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/2}\,(C\,\Phi)^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/\gamma_{\vartheta}}<\min(1/2,1/2C).

From the continuity of
\phi_{\vartheta}(t)
in time, we may define
t_{0}\leq T
the largest time s.t.
\phi_{\vartheta}(t)\leq\Phi
.
From (
7.4
), we also have that

\sup_{t\leq t_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(L\vartheta)^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}(t,x)\,dx\leq C\,\Phi.

From using a second time (
7.4
), we deduce that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\kappa(L(\vartheta))\,\frac{|\nabla L(\vartheta)|^{2}}{L(\vartheta)^{2}}\,dx\,dt\leq 2\,C^{2}+2\,C^{2}\,T^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/2}\,(C\,\Phi)^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/\gamma_{\vartheta}}+2\,M<3\,C^{2}+2\,M.

However at
t=T
, taking again (
7.4
) now implies that

\begin{split}\phi_{\vartheta}(T)&\leq C+C\,T^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/2}\,(C\,\Phi)^{\beta\,\bar{\alpha}/\gamma_{\vartheta}-1}+C_{T=1}(3\,C^{2}+2\,M)^{\beta}\\
&<2\,C+3\,C_{T=1}\,C^{2}+2\,C_{T=1}\,M=\Phi.\end{split}

This shows that
t_{0}=T
and yields a corresponding bound on
L(\vartheta)
in
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}
and in
L^{\alpha}_{t,x}
in terms of the initial energy.
The same argument that we used at the beginning of the proof then show that
L(\vartheta)\in E_{R}
for some
R
depending only on the initial energy. Since
\vartheta=\ell\,L(\vartheta)
, we also have that
\vartheta\in E_{R}
and we have checked all assumptions of the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem. Consequently,
for this choice of T
, there exists a fixed point
\vartheta\in E
s.t.
\vartheta=L(\vartheta)
.

We hence obtain a solution
(\rho,\,u,\,\vartheta)
to (
1.2
), (
1.3
) and the entropy inequation (
1.20
) on
[0,\ T]
. By combining the estimates in Theorems
5.2
and
6.34
, we also recover all a priori estimates in Theorem
1.3
and it only remains to derive the global energy bound to have Theorem
1.3
on
[0,\ T]
.
We first add the energy bounds (
5.1
) given by Theorem
5.2
and (
6.34
) given by Theorem
6.34
.

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{E}_{0}(\rho,u)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g(t,x)\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{S}:\nabla_{x}u\,dx\,ds\leq\mathcal{E}_{0}(\rho^{0},u^{0})\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g^{0}(x)\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\mathcal{S}:\nabla_{x}u+(P(\vartheta(s,x),\rho(s,x))-P_{0}(\rho(s,x))-\widetilde{P})\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u)(s,x)\,ds\,dx.\end{split}

By recombining the terms, we obtain that

(7.5)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{E}(\rho,u,\vartheta)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\sum_{n\leq N}\bar{B}_{n}\,\frac{\rho^{n}}{n-1}\right)\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{E}(\rho^{0},\vartheta^{0},m_{0})\,dx\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left(\sum_{n\leq N}\bar{B}_{n}\,\frac{(\rho^{0})^{n}}{n-1}\right)\,dx-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u(s,x)\,\left(\sum_{n\leq N}\bar{B}_{n}\,\rho^{n}\right)\,dx\,ds,\end{split}

as we can easily recognize the total energy
\mathcal{E}
of the system.
We also emphasize that it was critical in the formulations of both Theorem
5.2
and Theorem
6.34
that the terms
P-P_{0}
and
\widetilde{P}
do not contain
\rho^{\gamma}
, as we would not be able to make sense of
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u\,\rho^{\gamma}
. However since
n\leq\gamma/2
, we have no difficulty in handling
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u\,\rho^{n}
. In particular, we may easily remove the
\rho^{n}
terms from (
7.5
) through the use of renormalized solutions. Since
\rho\in L^{2}_{t,x}
and
u\in H^{1}_{t,x}
, the classical theory of renormalized solutions, from
[
4
]
for example, shows that for any smooth, bounded function
f(\rho)
, we have in the sense of distributions that

\partial_{t}f(\rho)+\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits(u\,f(\rho))=(f(\rho)-f^{\prime}(\rho)\,\rho)\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u.

By integrating over
x
, we have that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(\rho)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}f(\rho^{0})\,dx+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(f(\rho)-f^{\prime}(\rho)\,\rho)\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u(s,x)\,dx\,ds.

Since
\rho\in L^{\infty}_{t}L^{\gamma}_{x}
and
\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u\in L^{2}_{t,x}
, we may now apply this to a sequence
f_{\varepsilon}
with
f_{\varepsilon}(x)\to x^{n}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
and obtain

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho^{n}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\rho^{0})^{n}\,dx-(n-1)\,\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho^{n}\,\mathop{\rm div}\nolimits u(s,x)\,dx\,ds,

which leads to the desired energy inequality

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{E}(\rho,\vartheta,m)\,dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0},\vartheta_{0},m_{0})\,dx.

The last remaining point is to extend this solution on
[0,\ T]
to a solution that is global in time. This is naturally achieved by repeating the fixed point argument starting from
T
. To do so, we highlight the conditions on the initial data that Theorem
5.2
and Theorem
6.34
require: one needs
\vartheta^{0}\in L^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}_{x}
,
\rho^{0}\in L^{\gamma}_{x}
together with
\rho^{0}\,|u^{0}|^{2}\in L^{1}_{x}
. Equivalently, we can require
\mathcal{E}(\rho^{0},u^{0},\vartheta^{0})<\infty
. Indeed from (
6.37
), we have that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho\,\frac{|u|^{2}}{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\rho^{\gamma}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\vartheta^{\gamma_{\vartheta}}\,dx\leq C\,\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0},\vartheta_{0},m_{0}).

As seen earlier in the proof, the time of existence
T
is a function of the various norms of the initial data or again equivalently
\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0},\vartheta_{0},m_{0})
. From the propagation of energy, we have that
\mathcal{E}(\rho,\vartheta,m)|_{t=T}
is dominated by
\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0},\vartheta^{0},m_{0})
and therefore the existence time
T
can be chosen uniformly whether starting at
t=0
,
t=T
or
t=2T
. This ensures global existence.

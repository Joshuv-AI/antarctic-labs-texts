Lepton Flavor Violating B → K 2 ∗ ​ ( 1430 ) ​ μ ± ​ τ ∓ B\to K^{*}_{2}(1430)\mu^{\pm}\tau^{\mp} Decays
Suman Kumbhakar, Ria Sain, Juhi Vardani

Abstract
A number of measurements in decays induced by the semileptonic b → s b\to s and b → c b\to c transitions hint towards a possible role of new physics in both sectors. Motivated by these anomalies, we investigate the lepton flavor violating B → K 2 ∗ ​ ( 1430 ) ​ μ ± ​ τ ∓ B\to K^{*}_{2}(1430)\mu^{\pm}\tau^{\mp} decays. We calculate the two-fold angular distribution of B → K 2 ∗ ​ ℓ 1 ​ ℓ 2 B\to K^{*}_{2}\ell_{1}\ell_{2} decay in presence of vector, axial-vector, scalar and pseudo-scalar new physics interactions. We then compute the branching fraction and lepton forward-backward asymmetry in the framework of U 1 2 / 3 U^{2/3}_{1} vector leptoquark which is a viable solution to the current B B anomalies. We find that the upper limits are ℬ ⁡ ( B → K 2 ∗ ​ μ − ​ τ + ) ≤ 1.64 × 10 − 7 \mathcal{B}(B\to K^{*}_{2}\mu^{-}\tau^{+})\leq 1.64\times 10^{-7} and ℬ ⁡ ( B → K 2 ∗ ​ μ + ​ τ − ) ≤ 0.60 × 10 − 7 \mathcal{B}(B\to K^{*}_{2}\mu^{+}\tau^{-})\leq 0.60\times 10^{-7} at 90 % 90\% C.L.

IV
Conclusions

Inspired by the current
B
physics anomalies, we investigate the LFV
B\to K^{*}_{2}\mu^{\pm}\tau^{\mp}
decays. These decays are forbidden in the SM and hence any hint of these decays would imply a smoking gun signal of physics beyond the SM. In this work, we compute the two-fold angular distribution of
B\to K^{*}_{2}\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
in presence of vector, axial-vector, scalar and pseudo-scalar NP interactions. From the differential decay distribution, we extract two observables: the branching fraction and the lepton forward-backward asymmetry. Finally, we calculate these observables in the vector
U_{1}
LQ model which is a very popular model in explaining all the
B
anomalies. In particular, we compute the upper limits of the branching fraction and the forward-backward asymmetry at
90\%
confidence level. We find that the upper limits of branching fractions are
\mathcal{O}(10^{-7})
. We hope these upper limits may be measurable at LHCb.

Appendix A
Polarization of
K^{*}_{2}

The polarization
\epsilon^{\mu\nu}(n)
of tensor meson
K_{2}^{\ast}
, which has four momentum
(k_{0},0,0,\vec{k})
, can be written in terms of the spin-1 polarization vectors
Berger:2000wt

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mu\nu}(\pm 2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mu}(\pm 1)\epsilon_{\nu}(\pm 1),

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mu\nu}(\pm 1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\epsilon_{\nu}(\pm)\epsilon_{\nu}(0)+\epsilon_{\nu}(\pm)\epsilon_{\mu}(0)\right],

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\mu\nu}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left[\epsilon_{\mu}(+)\epsilon_{\nu}(-)+\epsilon_{\nu}(+)\epsilon_{\mu}(-)\right]+\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\epsilon_{\mu}(0)\epsilon_{\nu}(0),

(25)

where the spin-1 polarization vectors are defined as

\epsilon_{\mu}(0)=\frac{1}{m_{K_{2}^{\ast}}}\left(\vec{k}_{z},0,0,k_{0}\right)\,,\hskip 11.74988pt\epsilon_{\mu}(\pm)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(0,1,\pm i,0\right)\

(26)

We study
B\to K^{*}_{2}\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
decay where we have two leptons in the final state so in this case, the
n=\pm 2
helicity states of the
K_{2}^{\ast}

is not realized. Therefore a new polarization vector is introduced
Wang:2010tz

\epsilon_{T\mu}(h)=\frac{\epsilon_{\mu\nu}p^{\nu}}{m_{B}}\,

(27)

The explicit expressions of polarization vectors are

\displaystyle\epsilon_{T\mu}(\pm 1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m_{B}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\epsilon(0).p\epsilon_{\mu}(\pm)=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\sqrt{8}m_{B}m_{K^{*}_{2}}}\epsilon_{\mu}(\pm),

(28)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{T\mu}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m_{B}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\epsilon(0).p\epsilon_{\mu}(0)=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{\sqrt{6}m_{B}m_{K^{*}_{2}}}\epsilon_{\mu}(0),

(29)

where
\lambda(m^{2}_{B},m^{2}_{K^{*}_{2}},q^{2})=m^{4}_{B}+m^{4}_{K^{*}_{2}}+q^{4}-2(m^{2}_{B}m^{2}_{K^{*}_{2}}+m^{2}_{B}q^{2}+m^{2}_{K^{*}_{2}}q^{2})
is the usual Kallen function.
On the other hand, the virtual gauge boson can have three types of polarization states, longitudinal, transverse and time-like, which have following components

\epsilon^{\mu}_{V}(0)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{q^{2}}}(-|\vec{q_{z}}|,0,0,-q_{0})\,,\hskip 11.74988pt\epsilon^{\mu}_{V}(\pm)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(0,1,\pm i,0)\ ,\hskip 11.74988pt\epsilon^{\mu}_{V}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{q^{2}}}(q_{0},0,0,q_{z})\

(30)

where
q^{\mu}=(q_{0},0,0,q_{z})
is four momentum of gauge boson.

Appendix C
Lepton helicity amplitudes

Apart from the hadronic matrix elements, we also need the leptonic matrix elements to compute the decay distribution. To calculate these, we use the method outlined in refs.
Haber:1994pe
;
Gratrex:2015hna
. The leptonic matrix elements are defined as
\langle\ell_{1}(\lambda_{1})\bar{\ell}_{2}(\lambda_{2})|\bar{\ell}\;\Gamma^{X}\ell|0\rangle=\bar{u}(\lambda_{1})\Gamma^{X}v(\lambda_{2})=\mathcal{L}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
where
\Gamma^{X}
are actually the leptonic parts of the NP operators given in Eq. (
II
). The spinors of particle
u
and anti-particle
v
are defined as

u_{\frac{1}{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{E_{1}+m_{\ell_{1}}}\\
0\\
\sqrt{E_{1}-m_{\ell_{1}}}\\
0\end{pmatrix},u_{-\frac{1}{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\sqrt{E_{1}+m_{\ell_{1}}}\\
0\\
-\sqrt{E_{1}-m_{\ell_{1}}}\end{pmatrix},v_{\frac{1}{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{E_{2}-m_{\ell_{2}}}\\
0\\
-\sqrt{E_{2}+m_{\ell_{2}}}\\
0\end{pmatrix},v_{-\frac{1}{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\sqrt{E_{2}-m_{\ell_{2}}}\\
0\\
\sqrt{E_{2}+m_{\ell_{2}}}\end{pmatrix}

where the lepton energies are defined as
E_{1,2}=\sqrt{m^{2}_{1,2}+\lambda(q^{2},m^{2}_{1},m^{2}_{2})/4q^{2}}
and it gives
E_{1}+E_{2}=\sqrt{q^{2}}
. The spinors are normalised as
\bar{u}(\lambda_{1})u(\lambda_{2})=\delta_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}2m_{\ell_{1}}
and

\bar{v}(\lambda_{1})v(\lambda_{2})=-\delta_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}2m_{\ell_{2}}
.
Using these, we find the following expressions of lepton helicity amplitudes

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{L,R}(1/2,1/2,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(m_{-}\beta_{+}\pm m_{+}\beta_{-}\right)/2

(34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{L,R}(-1/2,-1/2,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m_{-}\beta_{+}\mp m_{+}\beta_{-})/2

(35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{L,R}(1/2,1/2,1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m_{+}\beta_{-}\pm m_{-}\beta_{+})/2

(36)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{L,R}(-1/2,-1/2,1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m_{+}\beta_{-}\mp m_{-}\beta_{+})/2

(37)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{L,R}(-1/2,1/2,1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sqrt{q^{2}}(\beta_{-}\pm\beta_{+})/\sqrt{2}

(38)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{L,R}(1/2,-1/2,1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sqrt{q^{2}}(\beta_{+}\mp\beta_{-})/\sqrt{2}

(39)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{S}(\pm 1/2,\pm 1/2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{q^{2}}\beta_{+}

(40)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{P}(\pm 1/2,\pm 1/2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mp\sqrt{q^{2}}\beta_{-}

(41)

where
\beta_{\pm}=\sqrt{1-\frac{(m_{\ell_{1}}\pm m_{\ell_{2}})^{2}}{q^{2}}}
and
m_{\pm}=(m_{1}\pm m_{2})

and

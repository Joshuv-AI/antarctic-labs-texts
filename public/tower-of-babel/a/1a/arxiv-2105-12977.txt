Observation estimate for the heat equations with Neumann boundary condition via logarithmic convexity
Rémi Buffe, Kim Dang Phung

Abstract




Abstract .- We prove an inequality of Hölder type traducing the unique
continuation property at one time for the heat equation with a potential and
Neumann boundary condition. The main feature of the proof is to overcome the
propagation of smallness by a global approach using a refined parabolic
frequency function method. It relies with a Carleman commutator estimate to
obtain the logarithmic convexity property of the frequency function.

Keywords .- heat equation with potential, logarithmic convexity, quantitative
unique continuation.

1
Introduction and main result

In this paper, we establish the observation inequality at one time for the
heat equation with a potential and Neumann boundary condition. The analysis is
based on the parabolic frequency function method
[
K
]
adjusted for a
global approach.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{\text{n}}
be a bounded connected open set with
boundary
\partial\Omega
of class
C^{\infty}
. Consider in
\{(x,t)\in\Omega\times\left(0,T\right)\}
the heat equation with a potential and
Neumann boundary condition

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}{\partial}_{t}u-\Delta u+au=0\text{ ,}&\quad\text{in}~\Omega\times\left(0,T\right)\text{ ,}\\
\partial_{n}u=0\text{ ,}&\quad\text{on}~\partial\Omega\times\left(0,T\right)\text{ ,}\\
u\left(\cdot,0\right)\in L^{2}(\Omega)\text{ .}&\end{array}\right.

Here,
T>0
,
a\in L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\times\left(0,T\right)\right)
and
n
is the unit outward normal vector to
\partial\Omega
.

We propose the following result.

Theorem 1 .-
Let
\omega
be a non-empty open subset of

\Omega
. For any
t\in\left(0,T\right]
,

\left\|u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq\left(e^{K\left(1+\frac{1}{t}+t\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\times\left(0,t\right)\right)}+\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\times\left(0,t\right)\right)}^{2/3}\right)}\left\|u\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\omega\right)}\right)^{\beta}\left\|u\left(\cdot,0\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{1-\beta}\text{ .}

Here
K>0
and
\beta\in\left(0,1\right)
only
depend on
\left(\Omega,\omega\right)
.

Such observation estimate traduces the unique continuation property at one
point in time saying that if
u=0
in
\omega\times\left\{t\right\}
, then

u
is identically null. Applications to bang-bang control and finite time
stabilization are described in
[
PWZ
]
and
[
BuP
]
. Our result is an
interpolation estimate which is more often used in a local way with a
propagation of smallness procedure (
[
AEWZ
]
,
[
FV
]
). Here the way we
choose to establish our main theorem is based on a global approach.

Recall that Theorem 1 implies the observability estimate for the heat
equations with a potential and Neumann boundary condition
[
PW
]
. It is
well-known that the observability estimate for the heat equations can be
obtained from Carleman inequalities. In the literature, at least two
approaches allow to derive Carleman inequalities for parabolic equations: A
local one based on the Garding inequality and interpolation estimates for the
elliptic equations (
[
LR
]
,
[
LRL
]
,
[
BM
]
); A global one based on
Morse functions and integrations by parts over
\Omega\times\left(0,T\right)
(
[
FI
]
,
[
FGGP
]
). Besides, unique continuation results
can be deduced either by Carleman techniques or by logarithmic convexity of a
frequency function
[
EFV
]
. Here we construct a new frequency function
adapted to the global approach. Further, we explicitly give the dependence of
the constants with respect to
\left\|a\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
as in

[
FGGP
]
,
[
DZZ
]
.

and

steps. In Step 1, we derive some estimates on the Morse functions given in
Proposition 1. In Step 2, we introduce the weight functions and establish the

5, key estimates for the Carleman operator is provided. In Step 6, we solve a
system of ordinary differential inequalities thanks to Proposition 3. In Step
7, we conclude the proof by making appear the control domain
\omega\times\left\{T\right\}

3.4
Step 4: Energy estimates

By a standard energy method, we have

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}+\left\langle\mathcal{S}\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle=\left\langle\digamma,\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle\text{ ,}

Indeed, for the energy identity we use the first equality of (
3.3.1
) and

\left\langle\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle=0

&=\left(\left\langle\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\mathcal{S}\boldsymbol{f},\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle\right)\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}-2\left\|\mathcal{S}\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}+2\left\langle\mathcal{S}\boldsymbol{f},\digamma\right\rangle\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}\\
&\quad+2\left\langle\mathcal{S}\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle^{2}-2\left\langle\mathcal{S}\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle\left\langle\digamma,\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle\\
&=\left(\left\langle\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\mathcal{S}\boldsymbol{f},\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle\right)\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}-2\left\|\mathcal{S}\boldsymbol{f}-\frac{1}{2}\digamma\right\|^{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left\|\digamma\right\|^{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}\\

\begin{array}[c]{ll}\left\langle\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\mathcal{S}\boldsymbol{f},\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle&\leq\displaystyle\frac{cs}{\Gamma}\sum_{i=1,..,2d}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla f_{i}\right|^{2}+\displaystyle\frac{c^{2}}{h^{2}}\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}\\
&\quad-\displaystyle\frac{2}{\Gamma}\sum_{i=1,..,2d}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\eta_{i}+\frac{1}{4}\left|\nabla\Phi_{i}\right|^{2}+\frac{s}{4}\nabla\Phi_{i}\nabla^{2}\varphi_{i}\nabla\Phi_{i}\right)\left|f_{i}\right|^{2}\\
&\quad+\displaystyle\frac{cs}{\Gamma}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1,..,d}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\Phi_{i}\right|^{2}\left|f_{i}\right|^{2}\end{array}

which gives that for any
s\in\left(0,1\right]
sufficiently small,

\begin{array}[c]{ll}\left\langle\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\mathcal{S}\boldsymbol{f},\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle&\leq\displaystyle\frac{Cs}{\Gamma}\sum_{i=1,..,2d}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla f_{i}\right|^{2}+\frac{C}{h^{2}}\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}\\
&-\displaystyle\frac{2}{\Gamma}\sum_{i=1,..,2d}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\eta_{i}+\frac{1}{8}\left|\nabla\Phi_{i}\right|^{2}\right)\left|f_{i}\right|^{2}\text{ .}\end{array}

(3.5.5)

Indeed,
-\frac{s}{4}\nabla\Phi_{i}\nabla^{2}\varphi_{i}\nabla\Phi_{i}\leq\frac{s}{4}\left|\nabla^{2}\varphi_{i}\right|\left|\nabla\Phi_{i}\right|^{2}\leq cs\left|\nabla\Phi_{i}\right|^{2}
.

It remains to prove that

-\frac{2}{\Gamma}\sum_{i=1,..,2d}\int_{\Omega}\left(\eta_{i}+\frac{1}{8}\left|\nabla\Phi_{i}\right|^{2}\right)\left|f_{i}\right|^{2}\leq C\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}+\frac{2-s/c}{\Gamma}\sum_{i=1,..,2d}\int_{\Omega}\left(-\eta_{i}\right)\left|f_{i}\right|^{2}\text{ .}

(3.5.6)

By Proposition 5
\left(i\right)
and
\left(ii\right)
,
\left|\varphi_{i,1}\right|\leq\frac{c}{2}\left|\nabla\varphi_{i,1}\right|^{2}
in
\mathcal{B}_{i}\cup\mathcal{D}_{i}
. This implies
that for any
i\in\left\{1,\cdot\cdot,d\right\}

-\left|\nabla\Phi_{i}\right|^{2}=-\frac{s^{2}}{\Gamma^{2}}\left|\nabla\varphi_{i,1}\right|^{2}\leq-\frac{2s^{2}}{c\Gamma^{2}}\left|\varphi_{i,1}\right|=\frac{4s}{c}\left(-\frac{s}{2\Gamma^{2}}\left|\varphi_{i,1}\right|\right)\leq\frac{4s}{c}\eta_{i}\text{ .}

Therefore, we get that for any
i\in\left\{1,\cdot\cdot,d\right\}

-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{i}\cup\mathcal{D}_{i}}\left|\nabla\Phi_{i}\right|^{2}\left|f_{i}\right|^{2}\leq\frac{s}{c}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{i}\cup\mathcal{D}_{i}}\eta_{i}\left|f_{i}\right|^{2}

which yields

-\displaystyle\frac{2}{\Gamma}\sum_{i=1,..,d}\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}_{i}\cup\mathcal{D}_{i}}\left(\eta_{i}+\frac{1}{8}\left|\nabla\Phi_{i}\right|^{2}\right)\left|f_{i}\right|^{2}\leq\displaystyle\frac{2-s/c}{\Gamma}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1,..,d}\int_{\mathcal{B}_{i}\cup\mathcal{D}_{i}}\left(-\eta_{i}\right)\left|f_{i}\right|^{2}\text{ .}

By Proposition 5
\left(iv\right)
, there is
c_{3}>0
such that

\varphi_{i,1}-\varphi_{j,1}\leq-c_{3}
in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
for some
j\neq i
. Therefore, using
\left|\eta_{i}+\frac{1}{8}\left|\nabla\Phi_{i}\right|^{2}\right|\leq\frac{c}{\Gamma^{2}}
and
\left|f_{i}\right|^{2}=e^{s\left(\varphi_{i,1}-\varphi_{j,1}\right)\frac{1}{\Gamma}}\left|f_{j}\right|^{2}
, it holds

-\displaystyle\frac{2}{\Gamma}\sum_{i=1,..,d}\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{i}}\left(\eta_{i}+\frac{1}{8}\left|\nabla\Phi_{i}\right|^{2}\right)\left|f_{i}\right|^{2}\leq\displaystyle\frac{2c}{\Gamma^{3}}e^{-c_{3}\frac{s}{\Gamma}}\left(\sum_{i=1,..,d}\displaystyle\int_{{\textstyle\bigcup\limits_{j\neq i}}\Theta_{p_{j}}}\left|f_{j}\right|^{2}\right)\leq C_{s}\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}\text{ .}

By Proposition 5
\left(v\right)
,
\left|\varphi_{i,2}\right|\leq c_{5}\left|\nabla\varphi_{i,2}\right|^{2}
in a neighborhood

\vartheta
of
\partial\Omega
and similarly one can deduce that,

-\displaystyle\frac{2}{\Gamma}\sum_{i=d+1,..,2d}\displaystyle\int_{\vartheta}\left(\eta_{i}+\frac{1}{8}\left|\nabla\Phi_{i}\right|^{2}\right)\left|f_{i}\right|^{2}\leq\displaystyle\frac{2-s/c}{\Gamma}\sum_{i=d+1,..,2d}\displaystyle\int_{\vartheta}\left(-\eta_{i}\right)\left|f_{i}\right|^{2}\text{ .}

By Proposition 5
\left(vi\right)
, there is
c_{6}>0
such that

\varphi_{i,2}-\varphi_{i,1}\leq-c_{6}
outside the neighborhood
\vartheta

of
\partial\Omega
which implies

-\displaystyle\frac{2}{\Gamma}\sum_{i=d+1,..,2d}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\left\backslash\vartheta\right.}\left(\eta_{i}+\frac{1}{8}\left|\nabla\Phi_{i}\right|^{2}\right)\left|f_{i}\right|^{2}\leq\displaystyle\frac{2c}{\Gamma^{3}}e^{-c_{6}\frac{s}{\Gamma}}\sum_{i=1,..,d}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\left\backslash\vartheta\right.}\left|f_{i}\right|^{2}\leq C_{s}\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}\text{ .}

This completes the proof of (
3.5.6
).

Consequently, by (
3.5.5
) and (
3.5.6
) one can conclude that for any

h\in\left(0,1\right]
and any
s\in\left(0,1\right]
sufficiently
small,

\left\langle\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\mathcal{S}\boldsymbol{f},\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle\leq\frac{Cs}{\Gamma}\sum_{i=1,..,2d}\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla f_{i}\right|^{2}+\frac{C_{s}}{h^{2}}\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}+\frac{2-s/c}{\Gamma}\sum_{i=1,..,2d}\int_{\Omega}\left(-\eta_{i}\right)\left|f_{i}\right|^{2}

which implies

\left\langle\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle+2\left\langle\mathcal{S}\boldsymbol{f},\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle\leq\frac{1+C_{0}}{\Gamma}\left\langle\mathcal{S}\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{f}\right\rangle+\frac{C}{h^{2}}\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}

with
C_{0}\in\left(0,1\right)
and
C>0
. Finally, the system
(
3.4.1
) of ordinary differential inequalities becomes

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{ll}\left|\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}+\mathbf{N}\left(t\right)\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}\right|\leq\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\right\|^{2}\text{ ,}&\\
\mathbf{N}^{\prime}\left(t\right)\leq\displaystyle\frac{1+C_{0}}{\Gamma}\mathbf{N}\left(t\right)+\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}^{2}+\displaystyle\frac{C}{h^{2}}\text{ .}&\end{array}\right.

3.6
Step 6: Solve ODE

Let

From now,

,

,

\left(\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\left(\cdot,T-\ell h\right)\right\|^{2}\right)^{1+M_{\ell}}\leq\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\left(\cdot,T\right)\right\|^{2}\left(\left\|\boldsymbol{f}\left(\cdot,T-2\ell h\right)\right\|^{2}\right)^{M_{\ell}}K_{\ell}\text{ }

3.7

&\quad\times\left(2\displaystyle\sum_{i=1,..,d}\left\|f_{i}\left(\cdot,T-2\ell h\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\right)^{M_{\ell}}K_{\ell}\text{ .}\end{array}

\begin{array}[c]{ll}&\quad\left(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|u\left(\cdot,T\right)\right|^{2}\right)^{1+M_{\ell}}\\

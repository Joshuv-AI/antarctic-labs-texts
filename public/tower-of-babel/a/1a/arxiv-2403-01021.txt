A note on extended genus fields of Kummer extensions of rational function fields
Martha Rzedowski–Calderón, Gabriel Villa–Salvador

Abstract
. We consider the generalization of the extended genus field of a prime degree cyclic Kummer extension of a rational function field obtained by R. Clement in 1992 to general Kummer extensions. We observe that the same approach of Clement works in general.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 11R58; Secondary 11R29

1.
Introduction

The first one to consider genus fields, or more precisely
extended genus fields,
of function fields was R. Clement
in
[
2
]
. She considered a cyclic Kummer extension
K

of
k:={\mathbb{F}}_{q}(T)
, a global rational function field of prime
degree
l
, where
l|q-1
. She used the ideas of H. Hasse developed
in
[
4
]
, where he considered a quadratic extension
K
of
the field of rational numbers
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

In the case of number fields, both, the genus and the extended
genus fields of a number field
K
are canonically defined as the
maximal extension of
K
of the form
KF
contained in the
Hilbert class and in the extended Hilbert class field, respectively,
where
F
is an abelian extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
. The Hilbert
class field (resp. extended Hilbert class field) of
K
is defined
as the maximal unramified (resp. maximal
unramified at the finite primes) abelian extension of
K
.

In the case of global function fields
the situation is quite different. There are several reasonable possible
definitions of Hilbert class field of a function field
K
. The main
reason is that, in the number field case, the Hilbert class
field is a finite extension of
K
. In the case of a function field
K
,
the extensions of constants of
K
are unramified so that the
maximal unramified abelian of
K
is of infinite degree.

For a quadratic number field
K
of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, the
extended Hilbert class field
K_{H^{+}}
of
K
is the abelian extension such
that the fully decomposed primes of
K
in
K_{H^{+}}
are precisely
the nonzero principal prime ideals of
K
generated by a totally
positive element. In the case of a quadratic field
K
of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, an element is totally positive if and only if its norm in
{\mathbb{Q}}
is the square of a real number. In other words, the norm group
in the idèle group
J_{K}
of
K
is
K^{*}(\Delta\times\prod_{p}U_{p})
,
where
p
runs through the prime numbers,
U_{p}
denotes the
group of units of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
and
\Delta=\big\{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t})\in\prod_{i=1}^{t}K_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}\mid\prod_{i=1}^{t}\operatorname{N}_{{K_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}/{\mathbb{R}}}}(\alpha_{i})\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{*}}^{2}\big\}

where
\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{t}
is the set of infinite primes in
K
(
t=1

or
2
and
K_{\mathfrak{p}_{i}}\in\{{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}\}
).

For a cyclic Kummer extension
K/k
of prime degree

l
, where
k={\mathbb{F}}_{q}(T)
and
l|q-1
, the analogue is
to consider as the norm group of
K_{H^{+}}
the subgroup

K^{*}(\Delta\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}})
of the
idèle group
J_{K}
of
K
, where
\Delta=\big\{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t})\in\prod_{i=1}^{t}K_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}\mid\prod_{i=1}^{t}\operatorname{N}_{{K_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}/k_{\infty}}}(\alpha_{i})\in{k_{\infty}^{*l}\big\}}

and
U_{\mathfrak{p}}
denotes the group of units of
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
This is the approach taken by Clement.

In
[
3
]
, E. O. Curiel-Anaya, M. R. Maldonado-Ramírez
and the first author, use this approach to extend the result to general
cyclic Kummer extensions.

In this note we will see that this approach still works for general Kummer
extensions
K
of
k
and obtain a version of extended genus field
for this kind of extensions. In the last section we compare three
definitions of extended genus fields for this kind of extensions
and present some examples.

2.
General Kummer extensions of
k

Let
k:={\mathbb{F}}_{q}(T)
and let
K
be a Kummer extension
of
k
of exponent
n|q-1
. Then

\operatorname{Gal}(K/k)\cong C_{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times C_{n_{s}},

where in general
C_{m}
denotes the cyclic group of order
m

and
n_{s}|\cdots|n_{2}|n_{1}=n
.

We have

\displaystyle K=k\big(\sqrt[n_{1}]{\gamma_{1}D_{1}},\ldots,\sqrt[n_{s}]{\gamma_{s}D_{s}}\big)=K_{1}\cdots K_{s},

(2.1)

where
K_{i}=k\big(\sqrt[n_{i}]{\gamma_{i}D_{i}}\big)
,
\gamma_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}
,

D_{i}\in R_{T}:={\mathbb{F}}_{q}[T]
is a monic polynomial and
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{i}/k)\cong C_{n_{i}}
,
1\leq i\leq s
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}\in R_{T}^{+}:=\{P\in R_{T}\mid P\text{\ is a
monic irreducible polynomial}\}
be the primes of
k

ramified in
K
with ramification indices
e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}

respectively. Let
\Delta:=\big\{(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t})\in\prod_{i=1}^{t}K_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}\mid\prod_{i=1}^{t}\operatorname{N}_{{K_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}/k_{\infty}}}(\alpha_{i})\in{k_{\infty}^{*n}\big\}}
, where
\{\mathfrak{p}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{t}

are the infinite primes in
K
, that is, the primes above
{\mathcal{P}}_{\infty}

(
=\infty
), the infinite prime of
k
and
k_{\infty}:=k_{{\mathcal{P}}_{\infty}}
.

Let
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}:=\{\alpha\in K\mid v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha)\geq 0\text{\ for
all\ }\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty\}
. Then
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
is a Dedekind domain
such that the fractional ideals of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
correspond
canonically to the finite primes of
K
. We denote by
J_{K}
the
idèle group of
K
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
extended Hilbert class field

K_{H^{+}}
of
K

is the finite abelian extension of
K
such that the finite primes of
K
fully
decomposed in
K_{H^{+}}
are precisely the principal nonzero ideals
generated by an element whose norm from
K
to
k
is an
n
-th power
in
k_{\infty}
.

The
extended genus field

\Gamma
of
K

with respect to
k
is the maximal
abelian extension of
k
contained in
K_{H^{+}}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

We have that
[J_{K}:K^{*}\big(\Delta\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}\big)]
is finite.

Proof.

The same proof in
[
2
]
works in this case. We present the
proof for the sake of completeness. Let
A:=\Delta\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
B:=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\infty}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
We have

\displaystyle K^{*}\big(\Delta\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}\big)=K^{*}A\subseteq K^{*}B=K^{*}\big(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\infty}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}\big),

\displaystyle J_{K}/K^{*}B=J_{K}/K^{*}\big(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\infty}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}\big)\xrightarrow[\ \psi\ ]{\ \cong\ }Cl({\mathcal{O}}_{K})

where the isomorphism
\psi
is the one induced by the natural
map
\theta\colon J_{K}\longrightarrow D_{K}

given by
\theta(\vec{\alpha})=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}^{\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}}
,

D_{K}
denotes the divisor group of
K
, and
the ideal class group
Cl({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
is isomorphic to

D_{K}/D_{\infty}P_{K}
,
where
D_{\infty}
is the free group generated by the infinite
primes and
P_{K}
is the group of principal divisors. We have
that
Cl({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
is finite.

Now
A\subseteq B
. Therefore

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} [K^{*}B:K^{*}A]&=\frac{[B:A]}{[K^{*}\cap B:K^{*}\cap A]}=\frac{[\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\infty}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}:\Delta\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}]}{[K^{*}\cap B:K^{*}\cap A]}\\
&=\frac{[\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\infty}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}:\Delta]}{[U_{K}:U_{K}^{(+)}]},\end{aligned}

where

\displaystyle U_{K}:=K^{*}\cap B=\{x\in K^{*}\mid v_{\mathfrak{p}}(x)=0\text{\ for all\ }\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty\}={\mathcal{O}}_{K}^{*}\quad\text{(units of ${\mathcal{O}}_{K}$)}

and

\displaystyle U_{K}^{(+)}:=\{x\in U_{K}\mid\operatorname{N}_{K/k}(x)=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\infty}\operatorname{N}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}/k_{\infty}}(x)\in k_{\infty}^{*n}\}.

Now
n|q-1
so that
\gcd(n,p)=1
where
p
is the characteristic
of
k
. Therefore
(U_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)})^{n}=U_{\mathfrak{p}}^{(1)}
. Since
k_{\infty}^{*}=\langle\pi_{\infty}\rangle\times{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}\times U_{\infty}^{(1)}
where
\pi_{\infty}=1/T
is a uniformizing parameter, we have
k_{\infty}^{*n}=\langle\pi_{\infty}^{n}\rangle\times{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}}^{n}\times(U_{\infty}^{(1)})^{n}=\langle\pi_{\infty}^{n}\rangle\times{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}}^{n}\times U_{\infty}^{(1)}
. Thus

\big|\frac{k^{*}_{\infty}}{k_{\infty}^{*n}}\big|=n^{2}
. Consider

\displaystyle\varphi\colon\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\infty}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\longrightarrow\frac{k_{\infty}^{*}}{k_{\infty}^{*n}},\quad\text{given by}\quad\varphi\big((\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}|\infty}\big)=\Big(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\infty}\operatorname{N}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}/k_{\infty}}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}})\bmod k_{\infty}^{*n}\Big).

Then
\ker\varphi=\Delta
and
\varphi
induces an injection

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}\colon\frac{\Big(\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\infty}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\Big)}{\Delta}\hookrightarrow\frac{k_{\infty}^{*}}{k_{\infty}^{*n}}.

Hence
[B:A]|n^{2}
and finally
[J_{K}:K^{*}A]<\infty
.
∎

We have that
K^{*}\big(\Delta\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}\big)

is an open subgroup of finite index of
J_{K}
. Let
L
be the class field
corresponding to
K^{*}\big(\Delta\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}\big)
.
Then
\operatorname{N}_{L/K}J_{L}=K^{*}\big(\Delta\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}\big)

and
\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\cong\frac{J_{K}}{K^{*}\big(\Delta\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}\big)}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
{\mathfrak{q}}
be a finite prime of
K
, that is,
{\mathfrak{q}}
is a nonzero prime ideal of

{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
. Then
{\mathfrak{q}}
decomposes fully in
L
if and only if
{\mathfrak{q}}
is a
principal ideal with
{\mathfrak{q}}=\langle\delta\rangle
and
\operatorname{N}_{K/k}(\delta)\in k_{\infty}^{*n}
.

Proof.

From class field theory, we
have that
{\mathfrak{q}}
decomposes fully in
L/K
if and only if
\theta\lceil K_{{\mathfrak{q}}}^{*}\rceil_{{\mathfrak{q}}}\subseteq K^{*}\big(\Delta\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}\big)
, where
\theta\lceil x\rceil_{{\mathfrak{q}}}:=(\ldots,1,x,1,\ldots)

for
x\in K_{{\mathfrak{q}}}^{*}

(see
[
6
, Corolario 17.6.196]
) if and only if for all
x\in K_{{\mathfrak{q}}}^{*}

there exist
\beta_{x}\in K^{*}
and
\vec{\alpha}_{x}\in\Delta\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}
with
\theta\lceil x\rceil_{\mathfrak{q}}=\beta_{x}\vec{\alpha}_{x}
.
Thus
\vec{\alpha}_{x}=(\ldots,\beta_{x}^{-1},\ldots,\beta_{x}^{-1},\beta_{x}^{-1}x,\beta_{x}^{-1},\ldots)
. This is equivalent to
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\beta_{x})=0
for all
\mathfrak{p}\neq{\mathfrak{q}}
and
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty

and
\operatorname{N}_{K/k}(\beta_{x}^{-1})=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\infty}\operatorname{N}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}/k_{\infty}}(\beta_{x}^{-1})\in k_{\infty}^{*n}
.

Let
x\in K_{{\mathfrak{q}}}^{*}
. The principal ideal in
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
generated by

\beta_{x}
satisfies
\langle\beta_{x}\rangle={\mathfrak{q}}^{n_{x}}=\beta_{x}{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
, from some
n_{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
In particular for
x\in K_{{\mathfrak{q}}}^{*}
with

v_{{\mathfrak{q}}}(\beta_{x})=1
we have
\langle\beta_{x}\rangle={\mathfrak{q}}
,
\beta_{x}\in K^{*}
. Hence
{\mathfrak{q}}
is a principal ideal,
{\mathfrak{q}}=\langle\beta_{x}\rangle
and

\operatorname{N}_{K/k}(\beta_{x})\in k_{\infty}^{*n}
.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

The extended class field
K_{H^{+}}
of
K
is the class field corresponding
to
K^{*}\big(\Delta\times\prod_{{\mathfrak{q}}\nmid\infty}U_{{\mathfrak{q}}}\big)
and a finite

of

decomposes fully in

if and only if
{\mathfrak{q}}
is
a principal ideal and the norm from

Proposition 2.5
.

The field of constants of
K_{H^{+}}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
.

Proof.

We have (see
[
6
, Teorema 17.6.192]
) that the field of constants
of
K_{H^{+}}
is

. We will see that

.

On the one hand, since
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\infty}\operatorname{N}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}/k_{\infty}}K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}=k_{\infty}^{*n}

there exists
\vec{\alpha}\in\Delta\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}
with

.
On the other hand, let
\vec{\alpha}\in\Delta\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}

v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}})=0
for
all
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty
and

. Therefore (see
[
6

\displaystyle\deg\vec{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}\deg\mathfrak{p}\cdot v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}})=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}|\infty}\deg\mathfrak{p}\cdot v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}})=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}|\infty}f_{\mathfrak{p}}v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}})

. Hence

.
∎

Lemma 2.11

We have

Proof.

\vec{\beta}=\big(\beta_{\infty},\beta_{P}\big)_{P\in R_{T}^{+}}\in J_{k}

f\in k^{*}
be defined by
f=\prod_{i=1}^{t}Q_{i}^{c_{i}}

(
f=1
if
t=0
). Then
f^{-1}\vec{\beta}\in k_{\infty}^{*}\times\prod_{P\in R_{T}^{+}}U_{P}
. The result is obtained.

Another proof is as follows, the class field
F
of
k^{*}\big(k_{\infty}^{*}\times\prod_{P\in R_{T}^{+}}U_{P}\big)
is unramified over
k
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{\infty}
decomposes fully. Hence
F

is an extension of constants and the infinite prime (of degree one) decomposes
fully in
F
. Therefore
F=k
.
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a finite prime in
K
. Let
P

be the irreducible polynomial in
R_{T}
corresponding to the prime in

k
below
\mathfrak{p}
.

(1)

If
\mathfrak{p}
is unramified, then
\operatorname{N}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}/k_{P}}(U_{\mathfrak{p}})=U_{P}
.

(2)

If
\mathfrak{p}
is ramified with ramification index
e_{P}
, then

\operatorname{N}_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}/k_{P}}(U_{\mathfrak{p}})={\mathbb{F}}_{P}^{*e_{P}}\times U_{P}^{(1)}
,
where
{\mathbb{F}}_{P}
is the field of constants of
k_{P}
.

Proof.

For any extension
E/F
of local fields, we have
e_{E/F}=[U_{F}:\operatorname{N}_{E/F}(U_{E})]
(see
[
6
, Corolario 17.3.39]
). If
E/F
is tamely
ramified,
\operatorname{N}_{E/F}(U_{E}^{(1)})=U_{F}^{(1)}
. The result follows.
∎

Proposition 2.13
.

We have

[\Gamma:k]=\big[J_{k}:k^{*}\operatorname{N}_{K/k}\big(\Delta\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}\big)\big]=n\prod_{i=1}^{r}e_{i}

where
e_{i}
is the ramification index of
P_{i}
in
K/k
,
1\leq i\leq r
.

Proof.

From Lemmas
2.11
and
2.12
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big[J_{k}:k^{*}\operatorname{N}_{K/k}\big(\Delta\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}\big)\big]

\displaystyle=\Big[k^{*}\big(\Delta\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}\big):k^{*}\Big(k_{\infty}^{*n}\times\prod_{i=1}^{r}\big({\mathbb{F}}_{P_{i}}^{*e_{i}}\times U_{P_{i}}^{(1)}\big)\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in R_{T}^{+}\\
P\notin\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}\}\end{subarray}}U_{P}\Big)\Big].

Set
C:=\Delta\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}U_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
D:=k_{\infty}^{*n}\times\prod_{i=1}^{r}\big({\mathbb{F}}_{P_{i}}^{*e_{i}}\times U_{P_{i}}^{(1)}\big)\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in R_{T}^{+}\\
P\notin\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}\}\end{subarray}}U_{P}
.

Then,

\displaystyle[k^{*}C:k^{*}D]=\frac{[C:D]}{[k^{*}\cap C:k^{*}\cap D]}

and

\displaystyle\frac{C}{D}\cong\frac{k_{\infty}^{*}}{k_{\infty}^{*n}}\times\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{{\mathbb{F}}_{P_{i}}^{*}\times U_{P_{i}}^{(1)}}{{\mathbb{F}}_{P_{i}}^{*e_{i}}\times U_{P_{i}}^{(1)}}\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in R_{T}^{+}\\
P\notin\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{r}\}\end{subarray}}\frac{U_{P}}{U_{P}}\cong C_{n}^{2}\times\prod_{i=1}^{r}C_{e_{i}}.

Consider now
\vec{\alpha}\in k^{*}\cap C
. Then
\alpha_{P}\in U_{P}
for all
P\in R_{T}^{+}
. Thus
v_{P}(\alpha_{P})=0
for all
P\in R_{T}^{+}
. It follows that

v_{\infty}(\alpha_{\infty})=0
and that
\vec{\alpha}=x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}
.
Thus
k^{*}\cap C={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}
. Similarly
k^{*}\cap D={{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\ast}}^{n}
. Therefore
[k^{*}\cap C:k^{*}\cap D]=n
. It follows that
[k^{*}C:k^{*}D]=\frac{n^{2}\times\prod_{i=1}^{r}e_{i}}{n}=n\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}e_{i}
.
∎

We have the main result of this note.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
K
be given by
(
2.1
)
. Then the extended genus field of
K/k

is

\Gamma={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}\big(T,\sqrt[e_{1}]{P_{1}},\sqrt[e_{2}]{P_{2}},\ldots,\sqrt[e_{r}]{P_{r}}\big).

Proof.

Let
M={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}\big(T,\sqrt[e_{1}]{P_{1}},\sqrt[e_{2}]{P_{2}},\ldots,\sqrt[e_{r}]{P_{r}}\big)
. Then
[M:k]=n\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}e_{i}
.
From Proposition
2.13
we obtain that
[\Gamma:k]=[M:k]
and
from Proposition
2.6
we obtain that
M\subseteq K_{H^{+}}
.
Since
M
is abelian and
\Gamma
is the maximal abelian
extension of
k
contained in
K_{H^{+}}
, it follows that

M\subseteq\Gamma
. Hence
\Gamma=M
.
∎

Remark 2.15
.

We observe that the extended genus field
\Gamma
of a Kummer
extension
K/k
is also a Kummer extension and furthermore that the
extended genus field
\Gamma/k
is
\Gamma
itself.

3.
Comparison among various extended genus fields

Let
K
be given by (
2.1
). We have some different definitions of extended
genus fields. We compare three of them.

First we denote by
K_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{clement}}
the extended genus
field obtained here, that is,
K_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{clement}}=\Gamma={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}\big(T,\sqrt[e_{1}]{P_{1}},\sqrt[e_{2}]{P_{2}},\ldots,\sqrt[e_{r}]{P_{r}}\big)
.

In
[
5
]
the extended genus field of
K
was defined as

K_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{rarzvi}}=E_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{rarzvi}}K
,
where we have

E=k\big(\sqrt[n_{1}]{(-1)^{\deg D_{1}}D_{1}},\sqrt[n_{2}]{(-1)^{\deg D_{2}}D_{2}},\ldots,\sqrt[n_{1}]{(-1)^{\deg D_{s}}D_{s}}\big)
and then

\displaystyle E_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{rarzvi}}=k\big(\sqrt[e_{1}]{(-1)^{\deg P_{1}}P_{1}},\sqrt[e_{2}]{(-1)^{\deg P_{2}}P_{2}},\ldots,\sqrt[e_{r}]{(-1)^{\deg P_{r}}P_{r}}\big).

Therefore

\displaystyle K_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{rarzvi}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}\big(T,\sqrt[e_{1}]{\epsilon_{1}},\sqrt[e_{2}]{\epsilon_{2}},\ldots,\sqrt[e_{r}]{\epsilon_{r}}\big)\big(\sqrt[e_{1}]{P_{1}},\sqrt[e_{2}]{P_{2}},\ldots,\sqrt[e_{r}]{P_{r}}\big),

where
\epsilon_{i}=(-1)^{\deg P_{i}}\gamma_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq r
. Since
e_{i}|n
we have
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\big(\sqrt[e_{1}]{\epsilon_{1}},\sqrt[e_{2}]{\epsilon_{2}},\ldots,\sqrt[e_{r}]{\epsilon_{r}}\big)\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
. Therefore
K_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{rarzvi}}\subseteq K_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{clement}}
.

Next, using the definition of extended genus field given by Anglès and Jaulent
in
[
1
]
, it was obtained in
[
7
]
that

K_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{angjau}}=FK
for some subfield of
E_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{rarzvi}}
. Therefore
K_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{angjau}}\subseteq K_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{rarzvi}}
.

Finally we have

K_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{angjau}}\subseteq K_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{rarzvi}}\subseteq K_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{clement}}.

We also remark that while
K_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{clement}}
is defined just for
Kummer extensions
K/k
,
K_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{rarzvi}}
and

K_{\mathfrak{gex},\text{angjau}}
are defined for any
separable finite extension
K/k
.

Examples 3.1
.

(1)

Let
K:=k(\sqrt[l]{\gamma P})
where
l
is a prime number such that

l|q-1
,
P\in R_{T}^{+}
with
\deg P<l
and

(see

7

(2)

Let
l

,

. Then

. (see
[
7

(3)

Now, let
l

Remark 3.2

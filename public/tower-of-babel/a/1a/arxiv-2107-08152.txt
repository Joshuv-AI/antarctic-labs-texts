Abstract
The ALPHA collaboration has successfully demonstrated the production and the confinement of cold antihydrogen, H ¯ \overline{\mathrm{H}} . An analysis of trapping data allowed a stringent limit to be placed on the electric charge of the simplest antiatom. Charge neutrality of matter is known to a very high precision, hence a neutrality limit of H ¯ \overline{\mathrm{H}} provides a test of CPT invariance. The experimental technique is based on the measurement of the deflection of putatively charged H ¯ \overline{\mathrm{H}} in an electric field. The tendency for trapped H ¯ \overline{\mathrm{H}} atoms to be displaced by electrostatic fields is measured and compared to the results of a detailed simulation of H ¯ \overline{\mathrm{H}} dynamics in the trap. An extensive survey of the systematic errors is performed, with particular attention to those due to the silicon vertex detector, which is the device used to determine the H ¯ \overline{\mathrm{H}} annihilation position. The limit obtained on the charge of the H ¯ \overline{\mathrm{H}} atom is Q = ( − 1.3 ± 1.8 ± 0.4 ) × 10 − 8 Q=(-1.3\pm 1.8\pm 0.4)\times 10^{-8} , representing the first precision measurement with H ¯ \overline{\mathrm{H}} .



I
Introduction

The study of cold antihydrogen is an experimental area is rapid development. The ALPHA collaboration demonstrated the confinement of cold
\overline{\mathrm{H}}
by means of a neutral atom trap
an11ca1s

For
\overline{\mathrm{H}}
to be neutral, the electric charge of the positron,
\mathrm{e}^{+}

\overline{\mathrm{p}}

\overline{\mathrm{H}}
electric charge represents a CPT test, when a similar measurement is performed on hydrogen and the charge of its constituents is inferred. In addition,
\overline{\mathrm{H}}
charge neutrality is related to another important aspect of the Standard Model, namely the cancellation of the
quantum anomaly
. It can be shown that the gauge invariance is guaranteed only if this anomalous contribution cancels out. The cancellation occurs by summing together all the relevant Feynman diagrams, as shown with great care in
pe95itqft705-707

generation

\overline{\mathrm{H}}
. Therefore, the
\overline{\mathrm{H}}
electric charge is intimately connected with the structure of the Standard Model and so it is fundamentally important that it be measured experimentally.

VI
Result and Discussion

A number of sources can contribute to a drift over time of
\left\langle z\right\rangle_{\Delta}
(e.g., the subject of the previous section) and to a change in
s
with respect to its nominal value, given by Eq. (
4
) (e.g., an inaccurate knowledge of the current in the mirror coils). These effects are taken into account in the calculation of the systematic error of
Q
. The investigation of the various errors proceeded by comparing simulations with and without the inclusion of the proposed sources.

By using the measured deflection of Eq. (
3
) and the simulation result of Eq. (
4
), the fractional charge of
\overline{\mathrm{H}}
from Eq.
1
is
Q=-1.3\times 10^{-8}
, which translates into the following limit

|Q|<(1.3\pm 1.1\pm 0.4)\times 10^{-8}\,,

(6)

where the first error is the statistical error due essentially to
\left\langle z\right\rangle_{\Delta}
, since the uncertainty on
s
has a negligible contribution, and the second is the overall systematic error on
Q
. For a detailed discussion of the systematic uncertainties see
am14elca
.

This limit is a million-fold improvement over the previous experimental result obtained with an energetic
\overline{\mathrm{H}}
beam
gr97a
. The best limit on the neutrality of ordinary atoms and molecules, such as
\mathrm{He}
,
\mathrm{H}_{2}
and
\mathrm{SF}_{6}
, is
10^{-21}e
, due, of course, to the availability of large quantities of those species
br11tnmbamsr
, while less than 1000 antiatoms have been trapped to date (and then only singly). The accuracy on
Q

6

The ALPHA collaboration has recently improved the limit on the
\overline{\mathrm{H}}
charge by means of a novel technique called
stochastic acceleration

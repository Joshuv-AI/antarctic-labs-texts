Hermitian Rank and Rigidity of Holomorphic Mappings
Yun Gao

Abstract
Huang’s Lemma is an important tool in CR geometry to study rigidity problems. This paper introduces a generalization of Huang’s Lemma based on the rigidity properties of holomorphic mappings preserving certain orthogonality on projective spaces, which is optimal for the case of partial linearity. By exploring the intricate relationship between rigidity and Huang’s Lemma, we establish that the rigidity properties of Segre maps or proper holomorphic mappings between generalized balls with Levi-degenerate boundaries can be inferred from those between generalized balls with Levi-non-degenerate boundaries by in a coordinate-free and more geometric manner.

The paper is organized as follows:
Section 2 serves as an introduction to the main tools employed in this paper, namely, orthogonal pairs and orthogonal maps. It elaborates on the fundamental concepts and facts related to these tools. Now, we explain the relationship between Huang’s equations and orthogonal pairs.
Let

\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}}
and
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}}

be holomorphic functions in a neighborhood of

.
Suppose there exists a complex-valued
real-analytic function

such that the following equation is satisfied for

\sum_{j=1}^{r^{\prime}}\phi_{j}(z)\overline{\psi_{j}(z)}-\sum_{j=r^{\prime}+1}^{r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}}\phi_{j}(z)\overline{\psi_{j}(z)}=\|z\|_{r,s,t}^{2}H(z,\bar{z}),

(2)

then
\{\phi_{j}\}
and
\{\psi_{j}\}
can induce a local orthogonal pair after homogenization, as follows.

Let
n:=r+s+t
.
Homogenizing
\{\phi_{j}(z)\}
and
\{\psi_{j}(z)\}
by using the substitution

and multiplying the equation by

, then we get an equation of bihomogeneous functions
of degree 1 in

\sum_{j=1}^{r^{\prime}}\tilde{\phi}_{j}\left(x\right)\overline{\tilde{\psi}_{j}\left(x\right)}-\sum_{j=r^{\prime}+1}^{r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}}\tilde{\phi}_{j}\left(x\right)\overline{\tilde{\psi}_{j}\left(x\right)}=\|x\|_{r,s,t+1}^{2}\tilde{H}\left(x,\bar{x}\right),

(3)

where
\tilde{\phi}_{j}(x)=x_{n+1}\phi_{j}\left(\dfrac{x_{1}}{x_{n+1}},\ldots,\dfrac{x_{n}}{x_{n+1}}\right)

\tilde{\psi}_{j}(x)=x_{n+1}\psi_{j}\left(\dfrac{x_{1}}{x_{n+1}},\ldots,\dfrac{x_{n}}{x_{n+1}}\right)

\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}

Therefore, we can now study the holomorphic functions

and

to

\{\phi_{i}\}
and
\{\psi_{i}\}

3
Rigidity of orthogonal maps and pairs

In this section, we will generalize some rigidity theorems obtained in
[
GN
]
and
[
Ga
]
for orthogonal maps and pairs. These generalizations are made to incorporate degeneracy in the Hermitian forms of the domain space. As seen in the previous section, such degeneracy necessarily appears when we homogenize the functions satisfying a Huang’s lemma-type equation (e.g. in Theorem
1.1
). The degeneracy of the Hermitian form in the source space will give us new difficulty in obtaining rigidity theorems, mainly due to the fact that a general hyperplane is no longer an orthogonal complement (of any point). We will use other method to handle these cases.

We have seen that a Huang’s lemma-type equation will give rise to a local orthogonal pair. The following proposition is a converse in some sense.

.

\phi([z])=[\phi_{1}(z),\ldots,\phi_{r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}+t^{\prime}}(z)],\,\,\psi([z])=[\psi_{1}(z),\ldots,\psi_{r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}+t^{\prime}}(z)]

for
z\in\widetilde{V}
and each
\phi_{j}
,
\psi_{j}

(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r+s+t})

\widetilde{V}

\sum_{j=1}^{r^{\prime}}\phi_{j}(z)\overline{\psi_{j}(z)}-\sum_{j=r^{\prime}+1}^{r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}}\phi_{j}(z)\overline{\psi_{j}(z)}=\|z\|_{r,s,t}^{2}H(z,\bar{z}).

(5)

Proof.

If
(\phi,\psi)

The proposition has been proved.

Rewrite the equation in terms of the variables

\psi_{1}:=z_{1}

\phi_{j}:=z_{1}\phi^{\sharp}_{j}(z_{2}/z_{1},\ldots,z_{r+s+t}/z_{1})

Definition 3.2

It is clear that for a given local orthogonal pair

is a real-valued analytic function, which is aslo called the

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(\phi,\psi)
be an orthogonal pair from

is null if and only if

.

(\phi,\psi)

and
h_{2}(z)
such that
H(z,\bar{z})=h_{1}(z)\overline{h_{2}(z)}
.

Proof.

The result concerning the null case is a direct consequence of the definition of a null orthogonal map.

By Proposition
3.1
, write
\phi=[\phi_{1}(z),\cdots,\phi_{r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}+t^{\prime}}(z)]
and
\psi=[\psi_{1}(z),\cdots,\psi_{r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}+t^{\prime}}(z)]
.
Then we have

\sum_{j=1}^{r^{\prime}}\phi_{j}(z)\overline{\psi_{j}(z)}-\sum_{j=r^{\prime}+1}^{r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}}\phi_{j}(z)\overline{\psi_{j}(z)}=\|z\|_{r,s,t}^{2}H(z,\bar{z}).

We present a proof for the ”if” part as the ”only if” statement is obvious. Assume that there exists two non-zero holomorphic functions
h_{1}(z)
and
h_{2}(z)

. So
(\phi,\psi)
is not null.

Let
\pi

to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
as a rational map. Obviously,

. In addition,
(\phi,\psi)
is quasi-standard if and only if
(\pi\circ\phi,\pi\circ\psi)
is quasi-standard.
Therefore, we only need to prove the statement for
t^{\prime}=0
.

Let’s assume that
t^{\prime}=0
from now on, i.e
(\phi,\psi)
is a local orthogonal pair from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime},s^{\prime}}
. Define the map
\Psi=[\psi_{1}(z),\cdots,\psi_{r^{\prime}}(z),

-\psi_{r^{\prime}+1}(z),\cdots,-\psi_{r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}}(z)]
. Then
(\phi,\Psi)
is a local orthogonal pair from
\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
to
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime}+s^{\prime},0}
. It is worth noting that
(\phi,\psi)
is quasi-linear (repectively, null) if and only if
(\phi,\Psi)
is quasi-linear (repectively, null).
Denote the linear span of
\phi(U)
by
K
with

\mathbb{P}^{r,s,t}
to

. On the other hand,
(\pi_{K}^{\perp}\circ\phi,\pi_{K}^{\perp}\circ\Psi)
is null where
\pi_{K}
and
\pi_{K}^{\perp}
are the projection from
\mathbb{P}^{r^{\prime}+s^{\prime},0}

to
K
and
K^{\perp}
, respectively. By Proposition
3.1
, write
\pi_{K}\circ\phi=[\phi^{\prime}_{1}(z),\cdots,\phi^{\prime}_{k+1}(z)]

\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\phi^{\prime}_{j}(z)\overline{\psi^{\prime}_{j}(z)}=\sum_{j=1}^{r^{\prime}}\phi_{j}(z)\overline{\psi_{j}(z)}-\sum_{j=r^{\prime}+1}^{r^{\prime}+s^{\prime}}\phi_{j}(z)\overline{\psi_{j}(z)}=\|z\|_{r,s,t}h_{1}(z)\overline{h_{2}(z)}.

Polarizing the above equation, we get

\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\frac{\phi^{\prime}_{j}(z)}{h_{1}(z)}\frac{\overline{\psi^{\prime}_{j}(w)}}{\overline{h_{2}(w)}}=\langle z,w\rangle_{r,s,t}.

As the linear span

\cdots\\

(6)

We can solve these equations and obtain each

Denote the linear span of

Lemma 3.5

g(z)

.

Proof.

Since
F(z,\bar{z})
is real analytic function,
F(z,\bar{z})|_{\Xi}
is also real analytic on
\Xi
. Since

for any hyperplane

. Hence,
\partial\overline{\partial}\log F(z,\bar{z})\equiv 0
, which means

is a sum of a holomorphic function and an anti-holomorphic function. Hence there exist two holomorphic functions
f(z)
,
g(z)
such that
F(z,\bar{z})=f(z)\overline{g(z)}
.

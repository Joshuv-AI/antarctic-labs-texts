New sign-changing solutions for the 2D Lane-Emden problem with large exponents Thanks: Work partially supported by the MUR-PRIN-20227HX33Z “Pattern formation in nonlinear phenomena” and the INdAM-GNAMPA group
Angela Pistoia, Tonia Ricciardi

Abstract
. We construct a new family of sign-changing solutions for a two-dimensional Lane-Emden problem with large exponent whose shape resembles a tower with alternating sign of bubbles solving different singular Liouville equations on the whole plane.



1.
Introduction and statement of the main results

We consider the classical Lane-Emden problem:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u=&|u|^{p-1}u&&\hbox{in\ }\Omega\\
u=&0&&\hbox{on\ }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a smooth bounded domain and
p>1
.

Standard variational tools ensure the existence of infinitely many (possibly sign-changing) solutions of problem (
1.1
).

In the last decade, a fruitful line of research has been the study the asymptotic behaviour of solutions to (
1.1
)
as
p
approaches
+\infty
.
In the pioneering works
[
22
,
23
]
, Ren and Wei study the profile
of the least energy solution
u_{p}
(which is positive) and prove that it has a single point concentration and converges at zero locally uniformly outside the concentration point. Later, El Mehdi and Grossi
[
10
]
and Adimurthi and Grossi
[
1
]
identify a limit problem by showing that
a suitable scaling converges to a radial solution of

(1.2)

-\Delta U=e^{U}\ \hbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},\ \int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{U}=8\pi.

Concerning general positive solutions (i.e. not necessarily with least energy) the first asymptotic analysis was carried out by
De Marchis, Ianni and Pacella
[
7
]
(see also
[
8
,
6
]
). More recently, other contributions have been given by Kamburov and Sirakov
[
20
]
and
Thizy
[
24
]
. We can summarize the known results as follows: the set of positive solutions
is uniformly bounded in
p
, each positive solution
concentrates at a finite number
k
of points
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
in
\Omega
, converges to zero locally uniformly outside the concentration set
and suitable scalings of it
about each peak converge to the bubble
U
, i.e solution of the limit problem (
1.2
).
Moreover, the
k-
upla of peaks
(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
is a critical point of the

Kirchhoff-Routh

\Psi_{k}:{\mathcal{D}}\to\mathbb{R}
, with
{\mathcal{D}}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in\Omega^{k}\,:\,x_{i}\not=x_{j}\}
defined by

(1.3)

\Psi_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k}):=\sum\limits_{i=1,\dots,k}\sigma_{i}^{2}H(x_{i},x_{i})+\sum\limits_{i,j=1,\dots,k\atop i\not=j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}G(x_{i},x_{j}).

with all the
\sigma_{i}
’s equal to
+1.

Here
G(x,y)
is the Green’s function
and
H(x,y)

its regular part. That is,

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{x}G(x,y)=\delta_{y}&x,y,\in\Omega,\ x\neq y\\
G(x,y)=0&x\in\partial\Omega,\ y\in\Omega\end{cases}

and

G(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{|x-y|}+H(x,y).

We denote by

(1.4)

h(x)=H(x,x),\qquad x\in\Omega,

the Robin function.

The first contribution to the analysis of the concentration phenomenon
for sign-changing solutions of problem (
1.1
) as
p\to+\infty
is due to Grossi, Grumiau and Pacella in
[
13
]
who study the profile and qualitative properties of the least energy nodal solution. In particular, the authors show that it concentrates at two different points
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
in
\Omega

and up to suitable scalings around
each peak it converges to a positive and a negative bubble
U
(see (
1.2
)).
Moreover, the pair of peaks
(x_{1},x_{2})
is a critical point of the

Kirchhoff-Routh
function (
1.3
) with
k=2
and
\sigma_{1}=+1
,
\sigma_{2}=-1.

Successively, the same authors Grossi, Grumiau and Pacella in
[
14
]

analyze the
asymptotic behavior of the least energy radial nodal solution in the ball and prove that
its positive and negative parts concentrate at the origin and the limit profile looks like a tower of two bubbles given by a superposition of the radial solution to (
1.2
) and the radial solution of the
singular
Liouville problem in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

(1.5)

-\Delta U=|x|^{\beta}e^{U}\ \hbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},\ \int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{U}=4\pi(\beta+2)

for a suitable non-integer and positive
\beta
.
Later, such a result has been generalized to other symmetric domains by De Marchis, Ianni and Pacella
[
7
]
.
Recently, Ianni and Saldana
[
18
]
provide a complete and extremely accurate asymptotic analysis of the radial solutions of problem (
1.1
) when the domain is the unit ball.
In particular, they prove that the radial solution with
m
nodal lines looks like a superposition (with alternating sign) of the radial solution to (
1.2
) and the radial solutions of
m
different Liouville problem (
1.5
) with different non-integers and positive
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{m}
.

As far as it concerns the existence of positive and sign-changing solutions with
k
concentration points, Esposito, Musso and Pistoia in
[
11
,
12
]

proved that any
non-degenerate
critical point
(x^{*}_{1},\dots,x^{*}_{k})
of the Kirchhoff-Routh function (
1.3
) with
\sigma_{i}\in\{-1,+1\}
,
generates a solutions with
k-
peaks
approaching the points
x^{*}_{1},\dots,x^{*}_{k}
as
p
is large enough being the peak
x^{*}_{i}
positive or negative if
\sigma_{i}=+1
or
\sigma_{i}=-1
, respectively.
In this regard, it is useful to point out that Micheletti and Pistoia
[
21
]
and Bartsch, Micheletti and Pistoia
[
2
]
proved that for generic domains
\Omega
all the critical points of the Kirchhoff-Routh function are non-degenerate. The limit profile of the solutions built in
[
11
,
12
]
resembles the sum of
k

bubbles
solutions to (
1.2
) concentrated in the different points
x^{*}_{i}
.
In particular, in
[
11
]
it is also shown that if the domain
\Omega
is has a rich geometry (namely it is not contractible or it has a dumbell shape) positive multipeak solutions can be found. On the contrary if the domain
\Omega
is convex, positive solutions with more than one peak do not exist as proved by Grossi and Takahashi in
[
17
]
. The scenery of sign-changing solutions is completely different. Indeed,
in any domain
\Omega
there exist at least two pairs of solutions with one positive peak and one negative peak as proved in
[
12
]
. In particular, Bartsch, Pistoia and Weth
[
3
,
4
]
prove that if the domain is symmetric with respect to the line
\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}

there exist infinitely many sign-changing solutions whose peaks are aligned on the symmetry line
\Omega\cap\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
. We point out that in the case of the ball these solutions are not radially symmetric.

In the present paper, we will build a new type of sign-changing solutions in a general domain whose profile resembles the tower of bubbles as predicted in the radial case by
[
18
]
.

For the sake of simplicity, we will assume that the domain
\Omega
is symmetric with respect to the origin
0\in\Omega
, i.e.
x\in\Omega
iff
-x\in\Omega.

We aim to construct a sign-changing tower of peaks for (
1.1
) concentrating at
0\in\Omega
, in the spirit of
[
11
,
16
]
.

In order to state our main result, we briefly recall some known definitions and facts.

In view of the standard expansion:

(1.6)

\left(1+\frac{v}{p}\right)^{p}=e^{v}\left\{1+O\left(\frac{1}{p}\right)\right\}\qquad\qquad\hbox{as }p\to+\infty,

for any fixed
v\in\mathbb{R}
, the equation

-\Delta v=v^{p}
(corresponding to (
1.1
) for
u\geq 0
) may be viewed as a perturbation of the Liouville equation
-\Delta v=e^{v}
.
Therefore, a family of solutions
u_{p}
to (
1.1
), with a peak at
0\in\Omega
as
p\to+\infty
, is expected to exist in the form

(1.7)

u_{p}(x)=\tau PU_{\delta}(x)+\omega_{p},

where
\tau,\delta>0
are parameters depending on
p
with
\tau,\delta\to 0
,
P
denotes the standard projection on
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
,

U_{\delta}(x)=\ln\frac{8\delta^{2}}{(\delta^{2}+|x|^{2})^{2}}

denotes the concentrating family of radial solutions to the Liouville equation (
1.2
)
and
\omega_{p}
is an error, see (
9.9
) in the Appendix for details.
However, as observed in
[
11
,
12
]
, where multiple isolated peak solutions
to (
1.1
) are constructed,
the first order expansion (
1.6
)
is not sufficiently accurate,
so that two higher order correction terms
w^{0}
and
w^{1}
must be included in ansatz (
1.7
).
More precisely, the
third order
expansion of the nonlinearity in (
1.1
) is needed. For fixed
v,w^{0},w^{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such
an expansion takes the form

(1.8)

\left(1+\frac{v}{p}+\frac{w^{0}}{p^{2}}+\frac{w^{1}}{p^{3}}+o\left(\frac{1}{p^{3}}\right)\right)^{p}=e^{v}\left\{1+\frac{w^{0}-\varphi^{0}(v)}{p}+\frac{w^{1}-\varphi^{1}(v,w^{0})}{p^{2}}+o\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right\},

where
\varphi^{0},\varphi^{1}
are the functions defined by

(1.9)

\varphi^{0}(v):=\frac{v^{2}}{2},\qquad\varphi^{1}(v,w^{0}):=vw^{0}-\frac{v^{3}}{3}-\frac{(w^{0})^{2}}{2}-\frac{v^{4}}{8}+\frac{v^{2}w^{0}}{2},

see Lemma
9.8
in the Appendix for more details on this expansion.
Thus, ansatz (
1.7
) is replaced by the following ansatz employed in
[
11
,
12
]
:

u_{p}(x)=\tau P\left(U_{\delta}(x)+\frac{w^{0}(x/\delta)}{p}+\frac{w^{1}(x/\delta)}{p^{2}}\right)+\omega_{p},

where
w^{0}
,
w^{1}
are the smooth radial solutions, whose existence is established in
[
5
]
(see also Lemma
9.9
in the Appendix), to the linearized equations

\Delta w^{0}+e^{v}w^{0}=e^{v}\varphi^{0}(v)\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\Delta w^{1}+e^{v}w^{1}=e^{v}\varphi^{1}(v,w^{0})\qquad\hbox{on }\mathbb{R}^{2},

satisfying

w^{\ell}(y)=C^{\ell}\ln|y|+O\left(\frac{1}{|y|}\right)\qquad\hbox{as }|y|\to+\infty,\ \ell=0,1.

The first order approximation for the tower of peaks
is given by a superposition of a finite number of bubbles
U_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}
,
solutions to the singular Liouville problems

(1.10)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta U=|y|^{\alpha_{i}-2}e^{U}&&\hbox{on\ }\mathbb{R}^{2}\\
&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|y|^{\alpha_{i}-2}e^{U}\,dx=4\pi\alpha_{i},\end{aligned}\right.

given by

U_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}(x)=v_{\alpha_{i}}(\frac{x}{\delta_{i}})-\alpha_{i}\ln\delta_{i}=\ln\frac{2\alpha_{i}^{2}\delta_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}{(\delta_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}+|x|^{\alpha_{i}})^{2}},

where
\alpha_{i}\geq 2
,
\delta_{i}>0
are suitably chosen and

v_{\alpha_{i}}(y)=\ln\frac{2\alpha_{i}^{2}}{(1+|y|^{\alpha_{i}})^{2}},\quad i=1,2,\ldots,k.

The corresponding lower order corrections
w_{\alpha_{i}}^{0}
,
w_{\alpha_{i}}^{1}
, are given by the radial solutions
to the linearized equations

(1.11)

\displaystyle\Delta w_{\alpha_{i}}^{0}+|y|^{\alpha_{i}-2}e^{v_{\alpha_{i}}}w_{\alpha_{i}}^{0}=|y|^{\alpha_{i}-2}e^{v_{\alpha_{i}}}\,\varphi^{0}(v_{\alpha_{i}}+\ln|y|^{\alpha_{i}-2}),\qquad\hbox{on }\mathbb{R}^{2},

\displaystyle\Delta w_{\alpha_{i}}^{1}+|y|^{\alpha_{i}-2}e^{v_{\alpha_{i}}}w_{\alpha_{i}}^{1}=|y|^{\alpha_{i}-2}e^{v_{\alpha_{i}}}\,\varphi^{1}(v_{\alpha_{i}}+\ln|y|^{\alpha_{i}-2},w_{\alpha_{i}}^{0})\qquad\hbox{on }\mathbb{R}^{2},

satisfying

(1.12)

w_{\alpha_{i}}^{\ell}(y)=C_{\alpha_{i}}^{\ell}\ln|y|+O(|y|^{-1})\qquad\hbox{as }|y|\to+\infty,\qquad\ell=0,1,

whose existence is established in
[
5
]
.

With this notation our main result may be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a sufficiently large
p_{0}>0
such that
for all
p\geq p_{0}
there exists a sign-changing tower of peaks solution to
(
1.1
) of the form
u_{p}=\mathcal{U}_{p}+\omega_{p}
,

\mathcal{U}_{p}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i-1}\tau_{i}P\left(U_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}(x)+\frac{w_{\alpha_{i}}^{0}(\delta_{i}^{-1}x)}{p}+\frac{w_{\alpha_{i}}^{1}(\delta_{i}^{-1}x)}{p^{2}}\right),

where, for
i=1,2,\ldots,k
, there holds
\tau_{i}=O(p^{-1})
,
2=\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\ldots<\alpha_{i}<\alpha_{i+1}\ldots<\alpha_{k}
,

\delta_{i}=C_{i}e^{-b_{i}p}
with
b_{1}>b_{2}>\ldots>b_{k}>0
and
C_{i}>0
and
w_{\alpha_{i}}^{0}
,
w_{\alpha_{i}}^{1}
are defined by
(
1.11
).

Remark 1.1
.

We point out that by the building process we easily deduce that the solution
u_{p}=\mathcal{U}_{p}+\omega_{p}
has
k
nodal regions which shrink to the origin as
p\to\infty.

It is also worthwhile noticing that the main order term of our solution, i.e. the sum of bubbles
\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\tau_{i}PU_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}
with alternating sign, coincide in the radial case with the profile described by
Ianni and Saldana in
[
18
, Theorem 2.5]
.
An interesting problem would be to show that all the solutions to (
1.1
) having
k
nodal regions shrinking to the origin as
p\to\infty
look like the first order approximation term
\mathcal{U}_{p}
. It should be the first step to prove the local uniqueness of the nodal solution in the same spirit of Grossi, Ianni, Luo and Yan
[
15
]
. We observe that the unicity of the radial solution with
k
nodal regions has been proved by Kajikiya in
[
19
]
using ODE techniques.

Remark 1.2
.

Our result claims the existence of symmetric sign-changing solutions which look like a tower of bubbles centered at the origin being the domain
\Omega
symmetric with respect to it.
The symmetry assumptions simplify the computations a lot because the linearized operator is invertible in the space of symmetric functions and the solution can be found merely using a contraction mapping argument. In general
we strongly believe that it is possible to carry out the construction around at any non-degenerate critical point
x_{0}
of the Robin’s function (
1.4
). This could be managed introducing new parameters which are
the centers of the bubbles close to
x_{0}
, which at the prices of heavy technicality should allow to perform a classical Ljapunov-Schmidt reduction.

This article is organized as follows.
Section
2
is dedicated to the choice of the parameters
\alpha_{j},\delta_{j},\tau_{j}
appearing in Theorem
1.1
. More precisely,
we first derive conditions for the parameters in the form of a nonlinear a system (see (
2.26
)) which ensure smallness of the “error"
\mathcal{R}_{p}:=\Delta\mathcal{U}_{p}+\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p})
with respect to
a suitable
L^{\infty}
-weighted norm
\rho_{p}
. By rather involved
ad hoc
arguments we prove solvability of the system and check that
\alpha_{j}\not\in\mathbb{N}
for
j\geq 2
,
which is essential for the invertibility of the linearized operator.
In Section
3
we carry out the details of the estimates of the error
\mathcal{R}_{p}
. To this end, we partition
\Omega
into “shrinking annuli"
A_{j}
in the spirit of
[
16
]
,
although our choice of the
A_{j}
’s is more delicate.
Sections 4 to 7 are dedicated to the analysis of the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}_{p}=\Delta+\mathfrak{g}_{p}^{\prime}(\mathcal{U}_{p})
.
In particular, Sections 4–5 are concerned with estimations in shrinking rings, and include delicate
ad hoc
arguments to compute the integrals involving radial eigenfunctions.
Section 7 extends the estimates by a barrier function in the spirit of
[
9
]
, with new ingredients.
Finally, in Section 8 we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1

by a fixed point argument. For the reader’s convenience, we collect in the Appendix some technical estimates as well as some known results.

2.
Ansatz for the
k
-peak tower and choice of parameters

In what follows, we denote by
\mathfrak{g}_{p}=\mathfrak{g}_{p}(t)
the nonlinearity appearing in (
1.1
), namely we set:

(2.1)

\mathfrak{g}_{p}(t):=|t|^{p-1}t,\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

We recall from Section
1
that we seek solutions to (
1.1
) of the
“sign-changing tower of peaks" form:

(2.2)

\displaystyle u_{p}=\mathcal{U}_{p}+\varphi_{p}

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tau_{i}(-1)^{i-1}P\left(U_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}+\frac{w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{0}}{p}+\frac{w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{1}}{p^{2}}\right),

where for
\alpha_{i}\geq 2
,
\delta_{i}>0
, we have that

(2.3)

U_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}(x)=\ln\frac{2\alpha_{i}^{2}\delta_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}}{(\delta_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}+|x|^{\alpha_{i}})^{2}},

is the family of radial solutions to (
1.10
),

(2.4)

w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{\ell}(x)=w_{\alpha_{i}}^{\ell}(\frac{x}{\delta_{i}}),\qquad\ell=0,1,\quad x\in\Omega,

where
w_{\alpha_{i}}^{0}
,
w_{\alpha_{i}}^{1}
are the correction
terms defined in (
1.11
), see Lemma
9.9
for details.

2.1.
Notation

Henceforth, we denote by
C>0
a general constant independent of
p
.
For any measurable set
E\subset\Omega
we denote by
\chi_{E}
the characteristic function of
E
.
By uniform convergence in
E
we understand uniform convergence in
\overline{E}
.
For any two families of sets
A_{p},B_{p}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
depending on
p
, by
A_{p}\cong B_{p}

we understand that there exists a constant
K>0
independent of
p
such that

(2.5)

K^{-1}B_{p}\subset A_{p}\subset KA_{p},\qquad\mbox{for all }p\to\infty

where
KB=\{Kx:x\in B\}
.
For
i=1,2,\ldots,k
it will be convenient to set

v_{\alpha_{i}}(y):=U_{\alpha_{i},1}(y)=\ln\frac{2\alpha_{i}^{2}}{(1+|y|^{\alpha}_{i})^{2}},\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

so that the scaling property may be written in the form

(2.6)

U_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}(x)=v_{\alpha_{i}}(\frac{x}{\delta_{i}})-\alpha_{i}\ln\delta_{i}.

It is convenient to set

(2.7)

\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{i}}(y):=v_{\alpha_{i}}(y)+(\alpha_{i}-2)\ln|y|=\ln\frac{2\alpha_{i}^{2}|y|^{\alpha_{i}-2}}{(1+|y|^{\alpha_{i}})^{2}},

so that we may write (
1.11
) in the form:

\displaystyle\Delta w_{\alpha_{i}}^{0}+e^{\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{i}}(y)}w_{\alpha_{i}}^{0}=e^{\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{i}}(y)}\varphi^{0}(\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{i}})\qquad\hbox{on }\mathbb{R}^{2}

\displaystyle\Delta w_{\alpha_{i}}^{1}+e^{\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{i}}(y)}w_{\alpha_{i}}^{1}=e^{\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{i}}(y)}\varphi^{1}(\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{i}},w_{\alpha_{i}}^{0})\qquad\hbox{on }\mathbb{R}^{2}.

The “mass" takes the form:

|x|^{\alpha_{i}-2}e^{U_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}(x)}=\frac{|y|^{\alpha_{i}-2}e^{v_{\alpha_{i}}(y)}}{\delta_{i}^{2}}=\frac{e^{\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{i}}(y)}}{\delta_{i}^{2}},\qquad x=\delta_{i}y\in\Omega.

We set

(2.8)

w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{0}(x)=w_{\alpha_{i}}^{0}(\frac{x}{\delta_{i}}),\qquad w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{1}(x)=w_{\alpha_{i}}^{1}(\frac{x}{\delta_{i}}),\qquad i=1,2,\ldots,k.

It follows that
w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{0}
,
w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{1}
, satisfy

(2.9)

\displaystyle\Delta w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{0}+|x|^{\alpha_{i}-2}e^{U_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}}w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{0}=|x|^{\alpha_{i}-2}e^{U_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}}\varphi^{0}(\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{i}}(\frac{x}{\delta_{i}})),\qquad x\in\Omega

\displaystyle\Delta w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{1}+|x|^{\alpha_{i}-2}e^{U_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}}w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{1}=|x|^{\alpha_{i}-2}e^{U_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}}\varphi^{1}(\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{i}}(\frac{x}{\delta_{i}}),w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{0}(x)),\qquad x\in\Omega.

For later estimates it is essential to observe that
\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{i}}
satisfies

(2.10)

\ln\frac{|y|^{\alpha_{i}-2}}{1+|y|^{2\alpha_{i}}}-C\leq\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{i}}(y)\leq\ln\frac{|y|^{\alpha_{i}-2}}{1+|y|^{2\alpha_{i}}}+C

for some
C>0
independent of
y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
We observe that

(2.11)

\displaystyle-\Delta

\displaystyle\left(U_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}+\frac{w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{0}}{p}+\frac{w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{1}}{p^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=|x|^{\alpha_{i}-2}e^{U_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}}\left(1+\frac{w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{0}-\varphi^{0}(\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{i}}(\delta_{i}^{-1}x))}{p}+\frac{w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{1}-\varphi^{1}(\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{i}}(\delta_{i}^{-1}x),w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{0})}{p^{2}}\right).

Occasionally within some proofs it will be convenient to denote by
U_{i}^{({w})}
,
v_{i}^{({w})}
,
\alpha_{i}^{({w})}
,

i=1,2,\ldots,k
,
the “corrections" by the lower order terms
w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{0}
,
w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{1}
, or by the related constants
C_{\alpha_{i}}^{0}
,
C_{\alpha_{i}}^{1}
:

(2.12)

U_{i}^{({w})}=U_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}+\frac{w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{0}}{p}+\frac{w_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}^{1}}{p^{2}},

(2.13)

v_{i}^{({w})}:=v_{\alpha_{i}}+\frac{w_{\alpha_{i}}^{0}}{p}+\frac{w_{\alpha_{i}}^{1}}{p^{2}}.

and

(2.14)

2\alpha_{i}^{({w})}:=2\alpha_{i}-\frac{C_{\alpha_{i}}^{0}}{p}-\frac{C_{\alpha_{i}}^{1}}{p^{2}}.

2.2.
The choice of parameters

Our aim in this section is to
choose
\alpha_{i},\delta_{i},\tau_{i}
, so that the “error"
\mathcal{R}_{p}
defined by

(2.15)

\mathcal{R}_{p}:=\Delta\mathcal{U}_{p}+\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p})

vanishes with order
p^{-4}
in a suitable weighted norm.
More precisely, in Proposition
2.1
we derive conditions for the parameters
\alpha_{i}
,
\delta_{i}
,
\tau_{i}
,
in the form of a nonlinear system, which imply “smallness" of
\mathcal{R}_{p}
.
In Proposition
2.2
we prove the solvability of the system.
Finally, in Proposition
2.3
we show that
\alpha_{i}\not\in\mathbb{N}
for
i\geq 2
,
which is essential to prove invertibility of the linearized problem.

We make the following assumptions on the parameters
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i}(p)
,
\delta_{i}=\delta_{i}(p)
,
\tau_{i}=\tau_{i}(p)>0
,
i=1,2,\ldots,k
:

(A1)

The singularity coefficients
\alpha_{i}
increase as the index
i
increases:

2=\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\ldots<\alpha_{i}<\alpha_{i+1}<\ldots<\alpha_{k}\leq C;

(A2)

the concentration parameters
\delta_{i}
vanish exponentially with respect to
p
,
with decreasing speed with respect to
i
:

The main underlying reason for the following expansions is that

Proof of Proposition

Let us introduce the Lambert function

{1\over s_{j}}={2\over\alpha_{j}+2}{1\over W\left({2\over\alpha_{j}+2}e^{-{2\over\alpha_{j}+2}}\right)}

First of all, straightforward but tedious computations show that

Indeed, using the definition of Lambert’s function, it is equivalent to prove that

\frac{A_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1}}\setminus E_{1}}{\delta_{1}}\subset\left\{R_{1}^{\prime}e^{\frac{p}{8}}\leq|y|\leq\left(\frac{\delta_{2}}{\delta_{1}}\right)^{1-\varepsilon_{1}}\right\},

\displaystyle\frac{A_{j}\setminus E_{j}}{\delta_{j}}\subset\left\{\left(\frac{\delta_{j-1}}{\delta_{j}}\right)^{\varepsilon_{j-1}}\leq|y|\leq r_{j}^{\prime}e^{-\frac{p}{2(\alpha_{j}-2)}}\right\}\cup

\displaystyle\left\{R_{j}^{\prime}e^{\frac{p}{2(\alpha_{j}+2)}}\leq|y|\leq\left(\frac{\delta_{j+1}}{\delta_{j}}\right)^{1-\varepsilon_{j}}\right\},

\displaystyle j=2,\ldots,k-1,

and

\frac{A_{k}\setminus E_{k}}{\delta_{k}}\subset\left\{\left(\frac{\delta_{k-1}}{\delta_{k}}\right)^{\varepsilon_{k-1}}\leq|y|\leq r_{k}^{\prime}e^{-\frac{p}{2(\alpha_{k}-2)}}\right\}\cup\left\{R_{k}^{\prime}e^{\frac{p}{2(\alpha_{k}+2)}}\leq|y|\leq\frac{\diam\Omega}{\delta_{k}}\right\}.

For
0<|y|\leq 1
,
j=1
, there is nothing to prove. For
0<|y|\leq 1
,
j=2,\ldots,k
, we estimate:

\rho_{j}(x)|x|^{\alpha_{j}-2}e^{U_{\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}(x)}\leq C|y|^{\alpha_{j}-2}.

Hence, for
x=\delta_{j}y\in A_{j}\setminus E_{j}
,
|y|\leq 1
, we have

\rho_{j}(x)|x|^{\alpha_{j}-2}e^{U_{\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}(x)}\leq C(e^{-\frac{p}{2(\alpha_{j}-2)}})^{\alpha_{j}-2}=Ce^{-p/2}.

For
x=\delta_{j}y\in A_{j}\setminus E_{j}
,
|y|\geq 1
, we estimate:

\rho_{j}(x)|x|^{\alpha_{j}-2}e^{U_{\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}(x)}\leq C\frac{|y|^{\alpha_{j}-2}}{(1+|y|)^{2\alpha_{j}-2-\eta}}\leq\frac{C}{(1+|y|)^{\alpha_{j}-\eta}}\leq Ce^{-\frac{\alpha_{j}-\eta}{2(\alpha_{j}+2)}p}.

The asserted estimate follows, since
(\alpha_{j}-\eta)/(\alpha_{j}+2)<1
.
∎

Lemma 3.5

(Expansion of
\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p})
)
.

The following expansion holds true:

\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p}(x))=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\tau_{j}|x|^{\alpha_{j}-2}e^{U_{\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}(x)}\left\{1+\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{j}}(\frac{x}{\delta_{j}})+O(1)}{p}\right\}\chi_{E_{j}}(x)+\omega_{p}(x)\chi_{A_{j}\setminus E_{j}},

where
\|\rho_{j}\omega_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{j}\setminus E_{j})}=O(e^{-\varepsilon_{0}p}/p)
, where
\varepsilon_{0}=\frac{\alpha_{j}-\eta}{2(\alpha_{j}+2)}
.

Proof.

We estimate separately in the sets
E_{j}
,
(A_{j}\setminus E_{j})\cap\{\mathcal{U}_{p}>0\}
and
(A_{j}\setminus E_{j})\cap\{\mathcal{U}_{p}<0\}
,
respectively.

Claim 1.
(Estimation of
\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p})
in
(A_{j}\setminus E_{j})\cap\{\mathcal{U}_{p}>0\}
).

There holds:

\rho_{j}(x)\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p})\leq\frac{C}{p}\,e^{-\frac{\alpha_{j}-\eta}{2(\alpha_{j}+2)}p},

uniformly for
x\in A_{j}\setminus E_{j}
,
\mathcal{U}_{p}(x)>0
.

Indeed we have, using Lemma
9.7
–(i) and (
2.36
), for
x=\delta_{j}y\in A_{j}\setminus E_{j}
,

\mathcal{U}_{p}(x)>0
:

\displaystyle\rho_{j}(x)|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p}(x))|\chi_{\{\mathcal{U}_{p}>0\}}\leq C(1+|y|^{2+\eta})\tau_{j}\frac{|y|^{\alpha_{j}-2}}{(1+|y|^{\alpha_{j}})^{2}}\leq C\tau_{j}\frac{|y|^{\alpha_{j}-2}}{(1+|y|)^{2\alpha_{j}-2-\eta}}.

For
x=\delta_{j}y\in A_{j}\setminus E_{j}
,
|y|\leq 1
,
j=2,\ldots,k
, we estimate:

\displaystyle\rho_{j}(x)|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p}(x))|\chi_{\{\mathcal{U}_{p}>0\}}\chi_{A_{j}\setminus E_{j}}\leq C\tau_{j}|y|^{\alpha_{j}-2}\leq C\tau_{j}(e^{-\frac{p}{2(\alpha_{j}-2)}})^{\alpha_{j}-2}=C\tau_{j}e^{-p/2}.

For
x=\delta_{j}y\in A_{j}\setminus E_{j}
,
|y|\geq 1
,
j=1,2,\ldots,k
, we estimate:

\displaystyle\rho_{j}(x)|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p}(x))|\chi_{\{\mathcal{U}_{p}>0\}}\chi_{A_{j}\setminus E_{j}}\leq\frac{C\tau_{j}}{(1+|y|)^{\alpha_{j}-\eta}}\leq C\tau_{j}e^{-\frac{p}{2(\alpha_{j}+2)}(\alpha_{j}-\eta)}.

Now the claim follows recalling that
\tau_{j}=O(p^{-1})
.

Claim 2.
(Estimation of
\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p})
in
A_{j}\cap\{\mathcal{U}_{p}<0\}
).

The following decay estimate holds true in
\{\mathcal{U}_{p}<0\}\cap A_{j}
:

(3.10)

\rho_{j}(x)|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p}(x))|\chi_{\{\mathcal{U}_{p}<0\}}\leq\frac{C}{p}e^{-\frac{\alpha_{j}-\eta}{\alpha_{j}+2}p},

uniformly for
x\in A_{j}
.

Indeed, we recall from (
2.36
) that

\mathcal{U}_{p}(x)=(-1)^{j-1}\tau_{j}p\left\{1+\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{j}}(\frac{x}{\delta_{j}})+O(1)}{p}\right\},\qquad x\in A_{j},

where
\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{j}}
is defined in (
2.7
).
We observe that
\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{j}}(y)<0
for
0<|y|\ll 1
and for
|y|\gg 1
.
Moreover, we have for
x=\delta_{j}y\in A_{j}

|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p}(x))|=\tau_{j}^{p}p^{p}|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(1+\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{j}}(y)+O(1)}{p})|=\frac{\tau_{j}}{\delta_{j}^{2}}|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(1+\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{j}}(y)+O(1)}{p})|,

where we used the last equation in (
2.26
) to derive the last equality.
We have, using Lemma
9.7
–(i),

|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(1+\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{j}}(y)+O(1)}{p})|\chi_{\{\mathcal{U}_{p}<0\}}\leq e^{-\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{j}}(y)-2p+O(1)}\leq C\frac{(1+|y|^{\alpha_{j}})^{2}}{|y|^{\alpha_{j}-2}}e^{-2p}

and therefore, in view of (
3.3
),

(3.11)

\displaystyle\rho_{j}(x)|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p}(x))|\chi_{\{\mathcal{U}_{p}<0\}}\leq C\delta_{j}^{2}(1+|y|^{2+\eta})\frac{\tau_{j}}{\delta_{j}^{2}}\frac{(1+|y|^{\alpha_{j}})^{2}}{|y|^{\alpha_{j}-2}}e^{-2p}\leq C\tau_{j}\frac{(1+|y|)^{2\alpha_{j}+2+\eta}}{|y|^{\alpha_{j}-2}}e^{-2p}.

For
x=\delta_{j}y\in A_{j}
,
|y|\leq 1
,
j\geq 2
(for
j=1
there is nothing to prove) we estimate, recalling that
|y|\geq(\delta_{j-1}/\delta_{j})^{\varepsilon_{j-1}}
:

\displaystyle\rho_{j}(x)|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p}(x))|\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{\tau_{j}e^{-2p}}{|y|^{\alpha_{j}-2}}\leq C\tau_{j}(\frac{\delta_{j}}{\delta_{j-1}})^{\varepsilon_{j-1}(\alpha_{j}-2)}e^{-2p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\tau_{j}e^{\varepsilon_{j-1}(b_{j-1}-b_{j})(\alpha_{j}-2)p}e^{-2p}.

In view of (
2.52
) we may simplify the exponent above:

\varepsilon_{j-1}(b_{j-1}-b_{j})(\alpha_{j}-2)=\varepsilon_{j-1}\frac{1+s_{j-1}}{s_{j-1}(\alpha_{j}-2)}(\alpha_{j}-2)=1,

so that for
x=\delta_{j}y\in A_{j}
,
|y|\leq 1
,
j\geq 2
, we finally obtain that

\rho_{j}(x)|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p}(x))|\leq\frac{Ce^{-p}}{p}.

For
x=\delta_{j}y\in A_{j}
,
|y|\geq 1
, we estimate using (
3.11
) and
|y|\leq(\delta_{j+1}/\delta_{j})^{1-\varepsilon_{j}}
:

\displaystyle\rho_{j}(x)|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p}(x))|\leq

\displaystyle C\tau_{j}(1+|y|)^{\alpha_{j}+4+\eta}e^{-2p}\leq C\tau_{j}(\frac{\delta_{j+1}}{\delta_{j}})^{(1-\varepsilon_{j})(\alpha_{j}+4+\eta)}e^{-2p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\tau_{j}e^{(1-\varepsilon_{j})(b_{j}-b_{j+1})(\alpha_{j}+4+\eta)p}e^{-2p}.

In view of Proposition
2.2
–(v) we may simplify the exponent above:

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon_{j})(b_{j}-b_{j+1})(\alpha_{j}+4+\eta)=

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon_{j})\frac{1+s_{j}}{\alpha_{j}+2+O(p^{-1})}(\alpha_{j}+4+\eta)=\frac{\alpha_{j}+4+\eta}{\alpha_{j}+2}+O(\frac{1}{p}).

Since

2-\frac{\alpha_{j}+4+\eta}{\alpha_{j}+2}=\frac{\alpha_{j}-\eta}{\alpha_{j}+2},

we derive Claim 2.

Finally, from Claim 1 and Claim 2 we deduce that

\rho_{j}(x)|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p}(x))|\chi_{A_{j}\setminus E_{j}}\leq\frac{C}{p}e^{-\frac{\alpha_{j}-\eta}{2(\alpha_{j}+2)}p}.

∎

Finally, we conclude the proof of the main error estimate.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
, Part 2: conclusion.

In view of the decompositions of
\mathcal{R}_{p}
as in (
3.8
)–(
3.9
)
and the estimates in Lemma

3.3
,
Lemma

\|\rho_{j}\mathcal{R}_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{j}\setminus E_{j})}\leq\frac{C}{p}\|\rho_{j}|x|^{\alpha_{j}-2}e^{U_{\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{j}\setminus E_{j})}+\|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p})\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{j}\setminus E_{j})}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon_{0}p},

for
\epsilon_{0}=\frac{\alpha_{j}-\eta}{2(\alpha_{j}+2)}
.
∎

4.
The linearized problem: estimates for
\mathcal{W}_{p}
and choice of the
\varepsilon_{j}
’s

We define the linearized operator

(4.1)

\mathcal{L}_{p}\phi:=\Delta\phi+\mathcal{W}_{p}(x)\phi,\qquad\phi\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega}),

where the “potential"
\mathcal{W}_{p}
is defined by

\mathcal{W}_{p}(x):=\mathfrak{g}_{p}^{\prime}(\mathcal{U}_{p}(x))

and
\mathfrak{g}_{p}^{\prime}(t)=p|t|^{p-1}
.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\alpha_{j}}(y):=

\displaystyle w_{\alpha_{j}}^{0}(y)-\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{j}}(y)-\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{j}}^{2}(y)}{2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}(x):=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\alpha_{j}}(\frac{x}{\delta_{j}}).

Note that

-p\leq\frac{\mathcal{D}_{\alpha_{j}}(y)}{p}\leq C,

uniformly for
y\in A_{j}/\delta_{j}
.
Our aim in this section is to establish the following fact.

Proposition 4.1
.

The following estimate holds true uniformly for
x\in\Omega
:

(4.2)

\mathcal{W}_{p}(x)\leq\overline{C}\,\sum_{i=1}^{k}|x|^{\alpha_{i}-2}e^{U_{\alpha_{i},\delta_{i}}(x)}\chi_{A_{i}}(x),

for some
\overline{C}>0
independent of
p\to+\infty
.
Moreover, for any
j=1,2,\ldots,k
there holds the expansion

\mathcal{W}_{p}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}|x|^{\alpha_{j}-2}e^{U_{\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}(x)}\{1+\frac{\mathcal{D}_{\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}(x)}{p}+\frac{O(|\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{j}}(\frac{x}{\delta_{j}})|^{4}+1)}{p^{2}}\}\chi_{E_{j}}(x)+\omega_{p}(x),

where
\|\omega_{p}\|_{\rho_{p}}\leq Ce^{-\beta_{0}p}
for some
\beta_{0}>0
,
uniformly for
x\in\Omega
.

We beign by establishing some auxiliary results.
The following result justifies the choice of the
\varepsilon_{j}
’s as in (
2.29
), namely

\varepsilon_{j}=\frac{s_{j}}{1+s_{j}},\qquad j=1,2,\ldots,k-1.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\alpha_{j}
,
\delta_{j}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,k
, and
s_{j}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,k-1
be the parameters defined in Proposition
2.2
.
Let
0<\varepsilon<1
.
The following implications hold true:

(i)

For all
j=2,\ldots,k,
if

\varepsilon\leq\frac{s_{j-1}}{1+s_{j-1}}

then

(\alpha_{j}-2)\ln|y|\geq-p-C\quad\hbox{for all}\quad\left(\frac{\delta_{j-1}}{\delta_{j}}\right)^{\varepsilon}\leq|y|\leq 1.

(ii)

For all
j=1,2,\ldots,k-1
, if

\varepsilon\geq\frac{s_{j}}{1+s_{j}},

then

(\alpha_{j}+2)\ln\frac{1}{|y|}\geq-p-C\quad\hbox{for all}\quad 1\leq|y|\leq(\frac{\delta_{j+1}}{\delta_{j}})^{1-\varepsilon}.

Consequently, if
\varepsilon_{j}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,k-1
, is given by (
2.29
), then

(4.3)

\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{j}}(y)\geq-p-C\quad\hbox{uniformly for}\quad y\in\frac{A_{j}}{\delta_{j}},\ j=1,2,\ldots,k.

Proof.

Proof of (i).
Assuming that
\varepsilon\leq s_{j-1}/(1+s_{j-1})
, it suffices to show that

(4.4)

(\alpha_{j}-2)\ln\left(\frac{\delta_{j-1}}{\delta_{j}}\right)^{\varepsilon}\geq-p-C.

We have:

\displaystyle(\alpha_{j}-2)\ln\left(\frac{\delta_{j-1}}{\delta_{j}}\right)^{\varepsilon}=

\displaystyle-(\alpha_{j}-2)\varepsilon(b_{j-1}-b_{j})\,p-C=-(\alpha_{j}-2)\varepsilon\frac{1+s_{j-1}}{s_{j-1}(\alpha_{j}-2)}\,p-C

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\frac{1+s_{j-1}}{s_{j-1}}p-C,

where we used Proposition
2.2
-(v) to derive the last equality.
Hence, (
4.4
) holds true if
\varepsilon(1+s_{j-1})/s_{j-1}\leq 1
and therefore Part (i)
is established.

Proof of (ii).
Assuming that
\varepsilon\geq s_{j}/(1+s_{j})
, it suffices to show that

(4.5)

(\alpha_{j}+2)\ln\left(\frac{\delta_{j}}{\delta_{j+1}}\right)^{1-\varepsilon}\geq-p-C.

We have, using Proposition
2.2
-(v):

\displaystyle(\alpha_{j}+2)\ln\left(\frac{\delta_{j}}{\delta_{j+1}}\right)^{1-\varepsilon}=

\displaystyle-(\alpha_{j}+2)(1-\varepsilon)(b_{j}-b_{j+1})\,p-C

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\alpha_{j}+2)(1-\varepsilon)\frac{1+s_{j}}{\alpha_{j}+2+O(\frac{1}{p})}\,p-C=-(1-\varepsilon)(1+s_{j})\,p-C.

Hence, (
4.5
) holds true if
(1-\varepsilon)(1+s_{j})\leq 1
, that is
if
\varepsilon\geq s_{j}/(1+s_{j})
.
Thus, Part (ii) is established.

Finally, we assume that
\varepsilon_{j}
is given by (
2.29
).
For
j=1
, the asserted inequality (
4.3
) follows from Part (ii), since
\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{1}}

is bounded at
y=0
.
For
j=2,\ldots,k-1
the asserted inequality (
4.3
) follows from Part (i) and Part (ii).
For
j=k
we need only consider the case
|y|\geq 1
.
Recalling from Proposition
2.2
–(v) that
b_{k}=(\alpha_{k}+2+O(p^{-1}))^{-1}
,
we compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{k}}(y)\geq\ln\frac{1}{|y|^{\alpha_{k}+2}}-C\geq

\displaystyle\ln\delta_{k}^{\alpha_{k}+2}-C=-b_{k}(\alpha_{k}+2)\,p-C=-\frac{\alpha_{k}+2}{\alpha_{k}+2+O(\frac{1}{p})}\,p-C

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-p-C.

∎

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Estimate (
4.2
) is a direct consequence of Lemma
2.2
–(ii).

5.
Linearized problem: evaluation of integrals

Let

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\alpha_{j}}(y):=

\displaystyle w_{\alpha_{j}}^{0}(y)-\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{j}}(y)-\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{j}}^{2}(y)}{2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}(x):=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\alpha_{j}}(\frac{x}{\delta_{j}}),

where
\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{j}}
is defined in (
2.7
) and

\Delta z+|y|^{\alpha_{j}-2}e^{v_{\alpha_{j}}(y)}z=0\qquad\hbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Observing that in view of equation (

) we may write

,
and integrating by parts, we obtain the following integrals to be used below:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|y|^{\alpha_{j}-2}e^{v_{\alpha_{j}}(y)}z_{\alpha_{j}}^{0}(y)\,dy=0

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|y|^{\alpha_{j}-2}e^{v_{\alpha_{j}}(y)}z_{\alpha_{j}}^{0}(y)v_{\alpha_{j}}(y)\,dy=4\pi\alpha_{j}

see also (4.30)–(4.31) in
[
16
]
.
We consider the linear operator
\mathcal{L}_{p}^{1}
defined for

by:

Our aim in this section is to show the following.

Proposition 5.1
.

There exists
C>0
such that for any solution
\phi\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
to the problem

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{p}\phi=h&\hbox{in }\Omega\\

,

, is such that

(Asymptotic profile of

.

,

Let

be fixed.
The rescaled function
\phi_{n}^{j}
satisfies

-\Delta\phi_{n}^{j}=\delta_{j}^{2}\,\mathcal{W}_{p_{n}}(\delta_{j}y)\phi_{n}^{j}-\delta_{j}^{2}h_{n}(\delta_{j}y)\qquad\hbox{in }\frac{\Omega}{\delta_{j}},

and
\|\phi_{n}^{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega/\delta_{j})}=1

Let
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}

Since

invades
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we may assume that

. In view of Lemma

–(ii) we have

\delta_{j}^{2}\,\mathcal{W}_{p_{n}}(x)=|y|^{\alpha_{j}-2}e^{v_{\alpha_{j}}(y)}(1+O(\frac{1}{p_{n}})),

, and thus we deduce that

)

) as established in

9
]

.

\gamma_{j}=0
for all

. To this end,
extending and approach in
[

]
,

’s and the

’s.

\mathcal{I}_{\alpha_{j}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|y|^{\alpha_{j}-2}e^{v_{\alpha_{j}}}(z_{\alpha_{j}}^{0}(y))^{2}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha_{j}}(y)\,dy=2\alpha_{j}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|y|^{\alpha_{j}-2}(1-|y|^{\alpha_{j}})^{2}}{(1+|y|^{\alpha_{j}})^{4}}\mathcal{D}_{\alpha_{j}}(y)\,dy.

(Linear system for

)
.

For

, the following linear relations hold true:

.

4.1

\mathcal{W}_{p_{n}}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}|x|^{\alpha_{j}-2}e^{U_{\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}(x)}\{1+\frac{\mathcal{D}_{\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}}{p}\}\chi_{E_{j}}+\widetilde{\omega}_{n},

6.

6.1.

\mathcal{I}_{\alpha}

An easy computation shows that
w

(6.1)

\Delta w+2\alpha^{2}{|y|^{\alpha-2}\over(1+|y|^{\alpha})^{2}}w=2\alpha^{2}{|y|^{\alpha-2}\over(1+|y|^{\alpha})^{2}}f(|y|)\ \hbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}

\Delta\tilde{w}+{8\over(1+|y|^{2})^{2}}\tilde{w}={8\over(1+|y|^{2})^{2}}\tilde{f}(|y|)\ \hbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}

\tilde{f}(|y|):=f\left(|y|^{2\over\alpha}\right)

Now let

(6.3)

Then,

\displaystyle p(p-1)\|\mathfrak{g}_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\mathcal{U}_{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{3}}))\|_{\rho_{p}}\|\phi\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle Cp\left(\frac{\gamma}{p^{3}}\right)^{2}=O(\frac{1}{p^{5}}).

It follows that, for any

Now Claim 1 follows by choosing
\gamma
suitably large.

Claim 2.

\mathcal{T}_{p}
is a contraction.
Indeed, similarly as above, by two applications of the mean value theorem we obtain

\mathcal{N}_{p}(\phi_{1})-\mathcal{N}_{p}(\phi_{2})=\mathfrak{g}_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\mathcal{U}_{p}+\eta_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\phi_{2}+\eta_{p}^{\prime}(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})))(\eta_{p}^{\prime}\phi_{1}+(1-\eta_{p}^{\prime})\phi_{2})(\phi_{1}-\phi_{2})

for some

.
Therefore,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{N}_{p}(\phi_{1})-\mathcal{N}_{p}(\phi_{2})|\leq

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{g}_{p}^{\prime\prime}(\mathcal{U}_{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{3}}))|\max_{i=1,2}\|\phi_{i}\|_{\infty}|\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle p(p-1)|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{3}}))|^{(p-2)/p}\frac{\gamma}{p^{3}}|\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}|

and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}_{p}(\phi_{1})-\mathcal{N}_{p}(\phi_{2})\|_{\rho_{p}}\leq

\displaystyle p(p-1)\||\mathfrak{g}_{p}(\mathcal{U}_{p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{3}}))|^{(p-2)/p}\|_{\rho_{p}}\frac{\gamma}{p^{3}}\|\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C\gamma}{p^{2}}\|\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\|_{\infty}

Therefore, we deduce that

\|\mathcal{T}_{p}(\phi_{1})-\mathcal{T}_{p}((\phi_{2})\|_{\infty}\leq Cp(\|\mathcal{N}_{p}(\phi_{1})-\mathcal{N}_{p}(\phi_{2})\|_{\rho_{p}}\leq\frac{C}{p}\|\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}\|_{\infty}.

This establishes Claim 2.

In particular, for any
q>0
we have the weighted mass estimate

(9.11)

\|\rho_{j}(x)|x|^{\alpha_{j}-2}e^{U_{\alpha_{j},\delta_{j}}(x)}(|\mathcal{V}_{\alpha_{j}}(\frac{x}{\delta_{j}})|^{q}+1)\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{j})}\leq C.

Proof.

Proof of (i).
Recall that

\displaystyle\rho_{p}(x)\,dx=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\rho_{j}(x)\chi_{A_{j}}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\rho_{j}(x)\,dx=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{j}^{2+\eta}+|x|^{2+\eta}}{\delta_{j}^{\eta}}\,dx=\delta_{j}^{2}(1+|y|^{2+\eta})\,dy,\qquad x=\delta_{j}y\in A_{j},

and
\|h\|_{\rho_{p}}=\|\rho_{p}h\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
.
We readily check that:

\int_{A_{j}}|h(x)|\,dx=\int_{A_{j}}\frac{\rho_{j}(x)|h(x)|}{\rho_{j}(x)}\,dx\leq\|h\|_{\rho_{p}}\int_{A_{j}/\delta_{j}}\frac{dy}{1+|y|^{2+\eta}}\leq C\|h\|_{\rho_{p}}.

Proof of (ii).
For all
x\in A_{j}
we compute:

\delta_{j}^{2}\,h(x)\leq\frac{\delta_{j}^{2}\|\rho_{j}h\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{j})}}{\rho_{j}(x)}=\frac{\|\rho_{j}h\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{j})}}{1+|\frac{x}{\delta_{j}}|^{2+\eta}}\leq\|h\|_{\rho_{p}}.

Proof of (iii).
By definition of
\|\cdot\|_{\rho_{p}}
, we have

\|h\|_{\rho_{p}}=\|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\rho_{j}\chi_{A_{j}}h\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|\rho_{j}h\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{j})}\geq\|\rho_{j}h\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{j})}.

Proof of (iv). For any fixed
j=1,2,\ldots,k
and
x\in A_{j}
,

|h(x)|=\frac{|\rho_{j}(x)h(x)|}{\rho_{j}(x)}=\frac{\delta_{j}^{\eta}}{\delta_{j}^{2+\eta}+|x|^{2+\eta}}|\rho_{j}(x)h(x)|\leq\frac{\delta_{j}^{\eta}}{\delta_{j}^{2+\eta}+|x|^{2+\eta}}\|h\|_{\rho_{p}}.

Proof of (v).
Let
j=1,2,\ldots,k
. Recalling that
x=\delta_{j}y\in A_{j}
implies that
|y|\leq(\delta_{j+1}/\delta_{j})^{1-\varepsilon_{j}}
,
we estimate

\displaystyle\rho_{j}(x)=\delta_{j}^{2}(1+|y|^{2+\eta})\leq\delta_{j}^{2}(1+(\frac{\delta_{j+1}}{\delta_{j}})^{(1-\varepsilon_{j})(2+\eta)}).

Thus, a sufficient condition for boundedness of
\rho_{j}
is given by
\eta\leq 2\varepsilon_{j}/(1-\varepsilon_{j})=2s_{j}
.
In view of Proposition
2.2
–(iii), by choosing
\eta\leq 2s_{1}\leq s_{j}
, we obtain
the asserted uniform boundedness for
\rho_{p}
.
∎

Remark 9.1

Lemma 9.7

(Estimates for
\mathfrak{g}_{p}
)
.

The following elementary inequalities hold true:

(i)

|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(1+\frac{s}{p})|=|1+\frac{s}{p}|^{p}\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &e^{s},&&\hbox{if }s\geq-p\\
&e^{-(s+2p)},&&\hbox{if }s<-p;\end{aligned}\right.

(ii)

|\mathfrak{g}_{p}^{\prime}(1+\frac{s}{p})|=p|1+\frac{s}{p}|^{p-1}\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &pe^{\frac{p-1}{p}s},&&\hbox{if }s\geq-p\\
&pe^{-\frac{p-1}{p}(s+2p)},&&\hbox{if }s<-p;\end{aligned}\right.

(iii)

|\mathfrak{g}_{p}^{\prime\prime}(1+\frac{s}{p})|=p(p-1)|1+\frac{s}{p}|^{p-2}\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &p(p-1)e^{\frac{p-2}{p}s},&&\hbox{if }s\geq-p\\
&p(p-1)e^{-\frac{p-2}{p}(s+2p)},&&\hbox{if }s<-p.\end{aligned}\right.

Proof.

The proof of (i) follows by concavity of the logarithmic function and reflection properties.
The proof of (ii)–(iii) follow by the identities
\mathfrak{g}_{p}^{\prime}(t)=p|t|^{p-1}=p|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(t)|^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

and
|\mathfrak{g}_{p}^{\prime\prime}(t)|=p(p-1)|t|^{p-2}=p(p-1)|\mathfrak{g}_{p}(t)|^{\frac{p-2}{p}}
.
∎

Lemma 9.8

(Taylor expansion)
.

Let
a(t)
,
b(t)
,
c(t)
,
t>0
, be smooth, real-valued functions
satisfying

\displaystyle\ln\frac{t^{\sigma}}{1+t^{\tau}}-C\leq a(t)\leq\ln\frac{t^{\sigma}}{1+t^{\tau}}+C

and

|b(t)|+|c(t)|\leq C\ln(t+2),

for some
0\leq\sigma<\tau
and
C>0
.
Let
E_{a}(p)\subset(0,+\infty)
be defined by

E_{a}(p):=\left\{t>0:\ a(t)>-\frac{p}{2}\right\}.

Then, the following expansions hold as
p\to+\infty
, uniformly for
t\in E_{a}(p)
:

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{a(t)}{p}+\frac{b(t)}{p^{2}}+\frac{c(t)}{p^{3}}+o(\frac{1}{p^{3}})\right)^{p}

\displaystyle=e^{a(t)}\left\{1+\frac{b(t)-\varphi^{0}(a(t))}{p}+\frac{c(t)-\varphi^{1}(a(t),b(t))}{p^{2}}+\frac{O(|a(t)|^{6}+1)}{p^{3}}+o(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\right\},

where
\varphi^{0}
,
\varphi^{1}
are defined in (
1.9
).

Moreover, for any fixed
0<\kappa<p
, there holds

(ii)

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{a(t)}{p}+\frac{b(t)}{p^{2}}+o(\frac{1}{p^{2}})\right)^{p-\kappa}

\displaystyle=e^{a(t)}\left\{1+\frac{1}{p}\Big(b(t)-\varphi^{0}(a(t))-\kappa\,a(t)\Big)+\frac{O(|a(t)|^{4}+1)}{p^{2}}+o(\frac{1}{p})\right\},

uniformly with respect to
t\in E_{a}(p)
.

Proof.

We shall repeatedly use the following properties:

C^{-1}e^{-p/(2\sigma)}\leq t\leq Ce^{\frac{p}{2(\tau-\sigma)}}
, for some
C>0
independent of
t\in E_{a}(p)
;

-\frac{p}{2}\leq a(t)\leq C
, in particular
a(t)=O(p)
and
|b(t)|+|c(t)|=O(|a(t)|+1)=O(p)
,
uniformly for
t\in E_{a}(p)
.

Proof of (i).
Let
\xi_{p}=\xi_{p}(t)
be defined by

\xi_{p}(t)=\frac{a(t)}{p}+\frac{b(t)}{p^{2}}+\frac{c(t)+o(1)}{p^{3}}=\frac{a(t)+O(1)}{p}.

Since
a(t)
is bounded from above, by taking
p
sufficiently large we may assume that

|\xi_{p}(t)|\leq 3/4
in
E_{a}(p)
.
Therefore, by Taylor expansion of the logarithmic function up to the third order, we may write

\displaystyle\log(1+\xi_{p})=

\displaystyle\xi_{p}-\frac{\xi_{p}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\xi_{p}^{3}}{3}-\frac{\xi_{p}^{2}}{4(1+\theta_{p}\xi_{p})^{4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{2}}(b-\frac{a^{2}}{2})+\frac{1}{p^{3}}(c-ab+\frac{a^{3}}{3})-\frac{\xi_{p}^{2}}{4(1+\theta_{p}\xi_{p})^{4}}+\frac{O(|a|^{3}+1)}{p^{4}}+o(\frac{1}{p^{3}})

for some
0\leq\theta_{p}(t)\leq 1
, uniformly with respect to
p\to+\infty
and
t\in E_{a}(p)
.
It follows that

p\log(1+\xi_{p})=a+\frac{1}{p}(b-\frac{a^{2}}{2})+\frac{1}{p^{2}}(c-ab+\frac{a^{3}}{3})-\frac{p\,\xi_{p}^{2}}{4(1+\theta_{p}\xi_{p})^{4}}+\frac{O(|a|^{3}+1)}{p^{3}}+o(\frac{1}{p^{2}}),

uniformly with respect to
p\to+\infty
and
t\in E_{a}(p)
.
We set

\widetilde{\xi}_{p}:=\frac{1}{p}(b-\frac{a^{2}}{2})+\frac{1}{p^{2}}(c-ab+\frac{a^{3}}{3})-\frac{p\,\xi_{p}^{2}}{4(1+\theta_{p}\xi_{p})^{4}}+\frac{O(|a|^{3}+1)}{p^{3}}+o(\frac{1}{p^{2}}),

and we observe that
\widetilde{\xi}_{p}(t)\leq C
, namely
\widetilde{\xi}_{p}
is
uniformly bounded from above
.
Therefore, by Taylor expansion of the exponential function to the second order we may write

e^{\widetilde{\xi}_{p}}=\widetilde{\xi}_{p}+\frac{\widetilde{\xi}_{p}^{2}}{2}+\frac{e^{\widetilde{\theta}_{p}\,\widetilde{\xi}_{p}}}{6}\widetilde{\xi}_{p}^{3},

for some
0\leq\widetilde{\theta}_{p}\leq 1
, so that
e^{\widetilde{\theta}_{p}\widetilde{\xi}_{p}}\leq C
,
namely
e^{\widetilde{\theta}_{p}\widetilde{\xi}_{p}}
is
bounded uniformly

with respect to
p\to+\infty
and
t\in E_{a}(p)
.
Observing that

p\,\xi_{p}^{4}=\frac{O(|a|^{4}+1)}{p^{3}},

we obtain the expansions

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi}_{p}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}(b-\frac{a^{2}}{2})+\frac{1}{p^{2}}(c-ab+\frac{a^{3}}{3})+\frac{O(|a|^{4}+1)}{p^{3}}+o(\frac{1}{p^{2}}),

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi}_{p}^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{2}}(b-\frac{a^{2}}{2})^{2}+\frac{O(|a|^{6}+1)}{p^{4}}+o(\frac{1}{p^{2}})

\displaystyle\widetilde{\xi}_{p}^{3}=

\displaystyle\frac{O(|a|^{6}+1)}{p^{3}},

from which we finally derive

e^{\widetilde{\xi}_{p}}=\frac{1}{p}(b-\frac{a^{2}}{2})+\frac{1}{p^{2}}(c-ab+\frac{a^{3}}{3}+\frac{1}{2}(b-\frac{a^{2}}{2})^{2})+o(\frac{1}{p^{2}})+\frac{O(|a|^{6}+1)}{p^{3}},

uniformly
with respect to
p\to+\infty
and
t\in E_{a}(p)
.
This established the asserted expansion (i).

Proof of (ii).
Similarly as above, let

\zeta_{p}:=\frac{a(t)}{p}+\frac{b(t)+o(1)}{p^{2}}=\frac{a(t)+O(1)}{p}.

By taking
p
sufficiently large, we may assume that
|\zeta_{p}(t)|\leq 3/4

for all
t\in E_{a}(p)
.
Expansion of the logarithmic function to the second oder yields

\displaystyle\log(1+\zeta_{p})=

\displaystyle\zeta_{p}-\frac{\zeta_{p}^{2}}{2}+\frac{\zeta_{p}^{3}}{3(1+\theta_{p}\zeta_{p})^{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{2}}(b-\frac{a^{2}}{2})+\frac{O(|a|^{2}+1)}{p^{3}}+\frac{\zeta_{p}^{3}}{3(1+\theta_{p}\zeta_{p})^{3}}+o(\frac{1}{p^{2}}),

uniformly with respect to
p\to+\infty
and
t\in E_{a}(p)
.
We deduce that

(p-\kappa)\log(1+\zeta_{p})=a+\frac{1}{p}(b-\frac{a^{2}}{2}-\kappa a)+\frac{O(|a|^{2}+1)}{p^{2}}+\frac{(p-\kappa)\zeta_{p}^{3}}{3(1+\theta_{p}\zeta_{p})^{3}}+o(\frac{1}{p}).

We set

\widetilde{\zeta}_{p}(t):=\frac{1}{p}(b-\frac{a^{2}}{2}-\kappa a)+\frac{O(|a|^{2}+1)}{p^{2}}+\frac{(p-\kappa)\zeta_{p}^{3}}{3(1+\theta_{p}\zeta_{p})^{3}}+o(\frac{1}{p})

and we observe that
\widetilde{\zeta}_{p}(t)
is
uniformly bounded from above
with respect
to
p\to+\infty
and
t\in E_{a}(p)
.
Therefore, expansion of the exponential function to the first order yields

e^{\widetilde{\zeta}_{p}(t)}=1+\widetilde{\zeta}_{p}(t)+\frac{e^{\widetilde{\theta}_{p}(t)\widetilde{\zeta}_{p}(t)}}{2}\widetilde{\zeta}_{p}^{2}(t),

for some
0\leq\widetilde{\theta}_{p}(t)\leq 1
, so that
e^{\widetilde{\theta}_{p}(t)\widetilde{\zeta}_{p}(t)}\leq C

is
uniformly bounded
with respect
to
p\to+\infty
and
t\in E_{a}(p)
.
We deduce that

e^{\widetilde{\zeta}_{p}(t)}=1+\frac{1}{p}(b-\frac{a^{2}}{2}-\kappa a)+\frac{O(|a|^{4}+1)}{p^{2}}+o(\frac{1}{p}),

and the asserted expansion (ii) follows.
∎

Lemma 9.9

(Chae-Imanuvilov lemma)
.

Fix
\alpha\geq 2
.
Let

be such that

(9.13)

|F(t)|\leq C\frac{(|\ln t|+1)^{4}}{t^{\alpha+2}},\qquad\hbox{as\ }t\to+\infty.

Then, there exists a
C^{2}
radial solution
w_{F}(y)
to the equation

(9.14)

\Delta w+\frac{2\alpha^{2}|y|^{\alpha-2}}{(1+|y|^{\alpha})^{2}}w=F(|y|)\qquad in\mathbb{R}^{2}

satisfying

w_{F}(y)=C_{F}\ln|y|+O(\frac{1}{|y|})\qquad\hbox{as }|y|\to+\infty

where

C_{F}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}t\varphi_{0,\alpha}(t)F(t)\,dt.

Proof.

In view of Lemma 2.1 in
[
5
]
it is known that there esists a
C^{2}
radial solution
w(r)

to (
9.14
) of the form

w(r)=\varphi_{0,\alpha}(r)\left\{\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\phi_{F}(s)-\phi_{F}(1)}{(1-s)^{2}}\,ds+\phi_{F}(1)\frac{r}{1-r}\right\}

(9.15)

\phi_{F}(s)=\frac{(1-s)^{2}}{s\varphi_{0,\alpha}(s)^{2}}\int_{0}^{s}t\varphi_{0,\alpha}(t)F(t)\,dt,

satisfying

|w(r)|\leq C(\ln^{+}r+1)\qquad\hbox{as\ }r\to+\infty,

\phi_{F}(1)
and

are defined as limits of
\phi_{F}(r)
and

as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\phi_{F}(s)-\phi_{F}(1)}{(s-1)^{2}}\,ds=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}\frac{\phi_{F}(s)-\phi_{F}(1)}{(s-1)^{2}}\,ds+\int_{2}^{r}\frac{\phi_{F}(s)}{(s-1)^{2}}\,ds-\phi_{F}(1)\int_{2}^{r}\frac{ds}{(s-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{r}\frac{\phi_{F}(s)}{(s-1)^{2}}\,ds+\int_{0}^{2}\frac{\phi_{F}(s)-\phi_{F}(1)}{(s-1)^{2}}\,ds-\phi_{F}(1)(1-\frac{1}{r-1})

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{r}\frac{\phi_{F}(s)}{(s-1)^{2}}\,ds+D_{F,1}+O(\frac{1}{r}),

where

(9.16)

D_{F,1}=\int_{0}^{2}\frac{\phi_{F}(s)-\phi_{F}(1)}{(s-1)^{2}}\,ds-\phi_{F}(1).

9.15
) and the fact

(9.17)

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{r}\frac{\phi_{F}(s)}{(s-1)^{2}}\,ds=

\displaystyle+4\int_{2}^{r}\frac{ds}{s(1-s^{\alpha})^{2}}\int_{0}^{s}t\varphi_{0,\alpha}(t)F(t)\,dt.

Integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{r}\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}t\varphi_{0,\alpha}(t)F(t)\,dt.

9.13

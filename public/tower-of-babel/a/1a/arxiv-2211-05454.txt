Abstract
. We prove a generalized version of Rogers’ mean value formula in the space X n X_{n} of unimodular lattices in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , which gives the mean value of a multiple sum over a lattice L L and its dual L ∗ L^{*} . As an application, we prove that for L L random with respect to the SL n ⁡ ( ℝ ) \operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R}) -invariant probability measure, in the limit of large dimension n n , the volumes determined by the lengths of the non-zero vectors ± 𝒙 \pm{\text{\boldmath$x$}} in L L on the one hand, and the non-zero vectors ± 𝒙 ′ \pm{\text{\boldmath$x$}}^{\prime} in L ∗ L^{*} on the other hand, converge weakly to two independent Poisson processes on the positive real line, both with intensity 1 2 \frac{1}{2} .

Let

,

\displaystyle X_{n}=G/\Gamma,\qquad\text{with }G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\text{ and }\Gamma=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}).

Also let

Our main goal in the present paper is to prove a generalization of
Rogers’ mean value formula,
giving an explicit formula for the average of a multiple sum over
L

dual
lattice
L^{*}

\displaystyle\int_{X_{n}}\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{k_{1}}\in L}\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$w$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$w$}}_{k_{2}}\in L^{*}}\rho({\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{k_{1}},{\text{\boldmath$w$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$w$}}_{k_{2}})\,d\mu(L).

Recall that the dual of a (full-rank) lattice
L
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined by

\displaystyle L^{*}=\{{\text{\boldmath$w$}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\>:\>{\text{\boldmath$w$}}\cdot{\text{\boldmath$v$}}\in\mathbb{Z}\hskip 7.0pt\forall{\text{\boldmath$v$}}\in L\},

\mathbb{R}^{n}

The covolume of
L^{*}
equals the inverse of that of
L
:
\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{R}^{n}/L^{*})=\operatorname{vol}(\mathbb{R}^{n}/L)^{-1}
;
in particular
L^{*}\in X_{n}
for any
L\in X_{n}
.
In fact, under our identification
X_{n}=G/\Gamma
, the map

L\mapsto L^{*}
on
X_{n}
is given by
g\Gamma\mapsto g^{-\mathsf{T}}\Gamma
,
where
g^{-\mathsf{T}}:=(g^{-1}){\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}=(g{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}})^{-1}

(this notation will be used throughout the paper).
The study of inequalities relating invariants of
L
and
L^{*}

is a classical topic within the geometry of numbers; see, e.g.,

[
4
]

and the references therein.

To prepare for the statement of our main result, we introduce some further notation.
Throughout the paper, we will identify vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\times 1
column matrices.
More generally, for any
m\geq 1
,
we will find it convenient to identify the space

M_{n,m}(\mathbb{R})
of

with the space
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}
of
m
-tuples of vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

(by listing the column vectors of any matrix

in order from left to right).
For
m\leq n
and
x\in M_{n,m}(\mathbb{R})
, we write

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathsf{d}(x):=\sqrt{\det(x{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}x)}.

Note that under our identification

equals the

m

\mathbb{R}^{n}

has rank

We write

(1.3)

\displaystyle U_{m}:=\{x\in M_{n,m}(\mathbb{R})\>:\>\mathsf{d}(x)>0\};

this is a dense open subset of

Given any positive integers

satisfying

m:=m_{1}+m_{2}<n
,
and given any
\beta\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{R})

(1.4)

\displaystyle S(\beta):=\{\langle x,y\rangle\in U_{m_{1}}\times U_{m_{2}}\>:\>x{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}y=\beta\}.

This is a closed, regular submanifold of

U_{m_{1}}\times U_{m_{2}}
of dimension

mn-m_{1}m_{2}
,
since the map
\langle x,y\rangle\mapsto x{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}y

U_{m_{1}}\times U_{m_{2}}

M_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{R})

has everywhere full rank
m_{1}m_{2}
.
We equip
S(\beta)
with a natural Borel

\eta_{\beta}
as follows.
For any
x\in U_{m_{1}}
, we set

(1.5)

\displaystyle S(\beta)^{\prime}_{x}:=\{y\in M_{n,m_{2}}(\mathbb{R})\>:\>x{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}y=\beta\}

and

(1.6)

\displaystyle S(\beta)_{x}:=S(\beta)_{x}^{\prime}\cap U_{m_{2}}=\{y\in M_{n,m_{2}}(\mathbb{R})\>:\>\langle x,y\rangle\in S(\beta)\}.

Note that

S(\beta)^{\prime}_{x}
is an affine linear subspace of
M_{n,m_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
of dimension

m_{2}(n-m_{1})
;
we equip
S(\beta)^{\prime}_{x}
with its structure as Euclidean subspace of

M_{n,m_{2}}(\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^{nm_{2}}
with its standard Euclidean structure,
and let

\eta_{\beta,x}
be the corresponding
m_{2}(n-m_{1})
-dimensional Lebesgue volume measure on

S(\beta)_{x}^{\prime}
.
Note that
S(\beta)_{x}^{\prime}\setminus S(\beta)_{x}
has measure zero with respect to
\eta_{\beta,x}
;
we write
\eta_{\beta,x}
also for the restriction of
\eta_{\beta,x}
to
S(\beta)_{x}
.
Finally we define, for any Borel set
E\subset S(\beta)
:

(1.7)

\displaystyle\eta_{\beta}(E):=\int_{U_{m_{1}}}\int_{S(\beta)_{x}}\chi_{E}(x,y)\,d\eta_{\beta,x}(y)\,\frac{dx}{\mathsf{d}(x)^{m_{2}}},

where
dx
is the standard
nm_{1}
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on

M_{n,m_{1}}(\mathbb{R})
, restricted to
U_{m_{1}}
.

For any
B\in M_{k,m}(\mathbb{R})
, we write

\displaystyle V_{B}:=B\mathbb{R}^{m}=\{B{\text{\boldmath$v$}}\>:\>{\text{\boldmath$v$}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\}

for the image of
B

\mathbb{R}^{m}
to
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
Next, for any
k\geq m
,
we let
M_{k,m}(\mathbb{Z})^{*}

be the set of all
B\in M_{k,m}(\mathbb{Z})

satisfying
\mathsf{d}(B)>0

V_{B}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{k}=B\mathbb{Z}^{m}=\{B{\text{\boldmath$v$}}\>:\>{\text{\boldmath$v$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\}

The group
\operatorname{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{Z})
acts on
M_{k,m}(\mathbb{Z})^{*}
by
multiplication from the right.
We fix a subset
A_{k,m}\subset M_{k,m}(\mathbb{Z})^{*}

containing exactly one representative from each

M_{k,m}(\mathbb{Z})^{*}/\operatorname{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Then, under our identification of
M_{k,m}(\mathbb{R})

(\mathbb{R}^{k})^{m}

M_{k,m}(\mathbb{Z})^{*}
is the set of all
m
-tuples of vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{k}

which span a primitive
m
-dimensional sublattice of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}

(a sublattice

\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}(L)\cap\mathbb{Z}^{k}=L

Furthermore, two matrices

lie in the same
\operatorname{GL}_{m}(\mathbb{Z})

if and only if
V_{B}=V_{B^{\prime}}
.
It follows that the map
B\mapsto V_{B}
gives a bijection from
A_{k,m}
onto the set of

rational

m
-dimensional linear subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{k}

(a linear subspace
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
is said to be rational if

V=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}(V\cap\mathbb{Z}^{k})
).

For any
\beta\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
we define a ’weight’

as follows:
Let
{\mathfrak{W}}_{m_{1}}
be the set of matrices
A=(a_{ij})\in M_{m_{1},m_{1}}(\mathbb{Z})

a_{ij}=0
for all

Let

(1.8)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{W}}_{\beta}=\{A\in{\mathfrak{W}}_{m_{1}}\>:\>A^{-\mathsf{T}}\beta\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})\}.\hskip 10.0pt

Finally set:

(1.9)

\displaystyle W(\beta):=\frac{\sum_{A\in{\mathfrak{W}}_{\beta}}(\det A)^{m_{2}-n}}{\zeta(n)\zeta(n-1)\cdots\zeta(n-m_{1}+1)}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
n>k_{1}+k_{2}
,

\rho:(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k_{1}}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k_{2}}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

be a non-negative Borel measurable function
(which we identify with a function

Then

(1.10)

\displaystyle\int_{X_{n}}\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{k_{1}}\in L}\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$w$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$w$}}_{k_{2}}\in L^{*}}\rho({\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{k_{1}},{\text{\boldmath$w$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$w$}}_{k_{2}})\,d\mu(L)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{k_{1}}\sum_{B_{1}\in A_{k_{1},m_{1}}}\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{k_{2}}\sum_{B_{2}\in A_{k_{2},m_{2}}}\sum_{\beta\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}\!(\mathbb{Z})}W(\beta)\int_{S(\beta)}\rho\bigl(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}},yB_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)\,d\eta_{\beta}(x,y)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.0pt+\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{k_{1}}\sum_{B_{1}\in A_{k_{1},m_{1}}}\int_{M_{n,m_{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\rho\bigl(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}},0\bigr)\,dx+\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{k_{2}}\sum_{B_{2}\in A_{k_{2},m_{2}}}\int_{M_{n,m_{2}}(\mathbb{R})}\rho\bigl(0,yB_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)\,dy+\rho(0,0),

Next, we discuss some applications of our main theorem.

Our main motivation for developing the formula in
Theorem
1.1

comes from questions concerning
the Epstein zeta function of a random lattice
L\in X_{n}
as
n\to\infty
;
cf.
[
21
,
32
,
33
,
29
]
.
Recall that for
\operatorname{Re}s>\frac{n}{2}
and

the Epstein zeta function is defined by the absolutely convergent series

The function
E_{n}(L,s)
can be meromorphically continued to
\mathbb{C}
and satisfies a functional equation of
”Riemann type” relating
E_{n}(L,s)
and
E_{n}(L^{*},\frac{n}{2}-s)
.
An outstanding question from
[
33
]
is whether
E_{n}(L,s)

for
s
on or near the central point
s=\frac{n}{4}
, possesses,
after appropriate normalization, a limit distribution as
n\to\infty
?
It seems clear that Theorem
1.1

should be an important ingredient when seeking to
extend the methods of

[
33
]
to handle this question.
We hope to return to these matters in future work.

Finally, we describe an application of Theorem
1.1

to the limit distribution of the shortest vector lengths of
L
and
L^{*}
,
for
L
random in
(X_{n},\mu)
, as the dimension

tends to infinity. Given a lattice
L\in X_{n}
,
we order its non-zero vectors by increasing lengths as
\pm{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{1},\pm{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{2},\pm{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{3},\ldots
and define, for each
j\geq 1
,

\mathcal{V}_{j}(L):={\mathfrak{V}}_{n}|{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{j}|^{n},

where

denotes the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In
[
31
]
, the second author of the present paper
proved that for

(X_{n},\mu)
,
the sequence

Of course the same limit result holds for the sequence

The following result generalizes these facts by describing the

of the normalized vector lengths of both

.

For

(X_{n},\mu)

and

2.
Rogers’ formula

In this section we explain the translation between Rogers’ original formulation of
his mean value formula,
[
19
]
,
and the statement in Theorem
1.5
.
The following is Rogers’ original formulation:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
1\leq k<n
and let
\rho:(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

be a non-negative Borel measurable function.
Then

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{X_{n}}\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{k}\in L}\rho({\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{k})\,d\mu(L)=\rho(0,\ldots,0)\hskip 170.0pt

\displaystyle+\sum_{D}\Big(\frac{e_{1}}{q}\cdots\frac{e_{m}}{q}\Big)^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\cdots\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{d_{i1}}{q}{\text{\boldmath$x$}}_{i},\ldots,\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{d_{ik}}{q}{\text{\boldmath$x$}}_{i}\Big)\,d{\text{\boldmath$x$}}_{1}\ldots d{\text{\boldmath$x$}}_{m},

where the inner sum is over all integer matrices
D=(d_{ij})\in M_{m,k}(\mathbb{Z})

with
m
arbitrary in
\{1,\ldots,k\}
,
satisfying the following properties:
No column of
D
vanishes identically;
the entries of
D
have greatest common divisor equal to 1;
and finally there exists a division
(\nu;\mu)=(\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{m};\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k-m})

of the numbers
1,\ldots,k
into two sequences
\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{m}
and
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k-m}
, satisfying

\displaystyle 1\leq\nu_{1}<\nu_{2}<\ldots<\nu_{m}\leq k,

(2.2)

\displaystyle 1\leq\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}<\ldots<\mu_{k-m}\leq k,

\displaystyle\nu_{i}\neq\mu_{j},\text{ if $1\leq i\leq m$, $1\leq j\leq k-m$},

such that, for some
q=q(D)\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle d_{i\nu_{j}}=q\delta_{ij},\hskip 10.0pti=1,\ldots,m,\,\,j=1,\ldots,m,

\displaystyle d_{i\mu_{j}}=0,\hskip 10.0pt\text{ if }\,\mu_{j}<\nu_{i},\,\,i=1,\ldots,m,\,\,j=1,\ldots,k-m.

Finally
e_{i}=\gcd(\varepsilon_{i},q)
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, where
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m}
are the elementary divisors of the matrix
D
.

Remark 2.2
.

Regarding convergence in (
2.1
),
Schmidt in
[
23
]
proved that both sides of (
2.1
) are finite
whenever
\rho
is bounded and has bounded support in
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
.
In fact, Schmidt’s proof applies verbatim to any non-negative
\rho
of sufficiently rapid polynomial decay.
It therefore follows that (
2.1
) holds, with absolute convergence in both sides,
for all complex valued
\rho
of sufficiently rapid polynomial decay,
and in particular for any
Schwartz function
\rho
on
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
.

Remark 2.3
.

In the formula (
2.1
) in Theorem

Lemma 3.15
.

Let

,
and let

onto

Proof.

Using the fact that the measure

on

is

-invariant,
by Lemma
3.6
,
together with the relation

\forall g\in G
,
p\in S(\beta)
),
it follows that
J_{*}\eta_{\beta}
is a
G
-invariant measure on
S(\beta{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}})
.
Lemma
3.6
also implies that

\eta_{\beta{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}}
is a
G
-invariant measure on

.
Hence as in the discussion below Lemma
3.7
,
the two measures

and

∎

Next we discuss the weight function

.
Let us first note a basic invariance relation.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
\beta\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Then

(3.28)

\displaystyle W(\gamma_{1}\beta\gamma_{2})=W(\beta)\qquad\forall\gamma_{1}\in\operatorname{GL}_{m_{1}}(\mathbb{Z}),\>\gamma_{2}\in\operatorname{GL}_{m_{2}}(\mathbb{Z}).

Proof.

Let
U
be the set of non-singular matrices in
M_{m_{1},m_{1}}(\mathbb{Z})
,
let
\Delta=\operatorname{GL}_{m_{1}}(\mathbb{Z})\subset U
,
and let
\Delta\backslash U=\{\Delta A\>:\>A\in U\}

be the set of all right
\Delta
-cosets contained in
U
.
Note that the set
{\mathfrak{W}}_{m_{1}}
contains exactly one element in
each coset
x\in\Delta\backslash U
.
Furthermore, for each
x\in\Delta\backslash U
,
the relation

A^{-\mathsf{T}}\beta\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
either holds for

all
or
no
element
A\in x
,
and we denote by
(\Delta\backslash U)_{\beta}
the subset

\{x\in\Delta\backslash U\>:\>A^{-\mathsf{T}}\beta\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})\>\forall A\in x\}
.
We also write
|\det x|
for the common value of
the absolute determinant
|\det A|
for all
A\in x
.
With this notation,
the formula in (
1.9
) may be expressed as

\displaystyle W(\beta)=\frac{\sum_{x\in(\Delta\backslash U)_{\beta}}|\det x|^{m_{2}-n}}{\prod_{j=n-m_{1}+1}^{n}\zeta(j)}.

Now
\Delta
acts on
\Delta\backslash U
by right multiplication;

(\Delta A)\gamma:=\Delta(A\gamma)
for any
A\in U
and
\gamma\in\Delta
.
One verifies that

(\Delta\backslash U)_{\gamma_{1}\beta\gamma_{2}}=\{x\gamma_{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\>:\>x\in(\Delta\backslash U)_{\beta}\}

for any
\gamma_{1}\in\Delta
and
\gamma_{2}\in\operatorname{GL}_{m_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in(\Delta\backslash U)_{\gamma_{1}\beta\gamma_{2}}}|\det x|^{m_{2}-n}=\sum_{x\in(\Delta\backslash U)_{\beta}}|\det(x\gamma_{1}^{\mathsf{T}})|^{m_{2}-n}=\sum_{x\in(\Delta\backslash U)_{\beta}}|\det x|^{m_{2}-n}.

Hence
W(\gamma_{1}\beta\gamma_{2})=W(\beta)
.
∎

Lemma 3.17
.

For any
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
\beta\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
,

W(\beta{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}})=W(\beta)
.

Proof.

Replacing
\beta
by
\beta{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}
if necessary,
we may assume that
m_{1}\leq m_{2}
.
By the Smith Normal Form Theorem,
there exist

\gamma_{1}\in\operatorname{GL}_{m_{1}}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\gamma_{2}\in\operatorname{GL}_{m_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})

such that, in block matrix notation,

\gamma_{1}\beta\gamma_{2}=\bigl(D\hskip 5.0pt0\bigr)
for some diagonal matrix
D\in M_{m_{1},m_{1}}(\mathbb{Z})
.
If
m_{1}=m_{2}
then

\gamma_{1}\beta\gamma_{2}=D=D{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}=\gamma_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}\beta{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}\gamma_{1}^{\mathsf{T}}
,
and so

W(\beta{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}})=W(\beta)

by Lemma
3.16
.

Hence from now on we assume
m_{1}<m_{2}
.
Transposing the relation

\gamma_{1}\beta\gamma_{2}=\bigl(D\hskip 5.0pt0\bigr)

and multiplying from the left by an appropriate permutation matrix,
it follows that there exists

\gamma_{3}\in\operatorname{GL}_{m_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
such that

\gamma_{3}\beta{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}\gamma_{1}^{\mathsf{T}}=\left(\begin{matrix}0\\
D\end{matrix}\right)=:D^{\prime}

in
M_{m_{2},m_{1}}(\mathbb{Z})
,
so that
W(\beta{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}})=W(D^{\prime})
by Lemma
3.16
.
Now for any

A=\left(\begin{matrix}A_{1}&A_{12}\\
0&A_{2}\end{matrix}\right)\in{\mathfrak{W}}_{m_{2}}

(with blocks
A_{1},A_{12},A_{2}
of sizes
(m_{2}-m_{1})\times(m_{2}-m_{1})
,

(m_{2}-m_{1})\times m_{1}
and
m_{1}\times m_{1}
, respectively),
one verifies that
A\in{\mathfrak{W}}_{D^{\prime}}
if and only if
A_{2}\in{\mathfrak{W}}_{D}
;
furthermore, for any given
A_{2}\in{\mathfrak{W}}_{D}

there are exactly

(\det A_{2})^{m_{2}-m_{1}}
possibilities for
A_{12}
which, together with

A_{1}
freely chosen in
{\mathfrak{W}}_{m_{2}-m_{1}}
,
makes
A\in{\mathfrak{W}}_{D^{\prime}}
.
Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{A\in{\mathfrak{W}}_{D^{\prime}}}(\det A)^{m_{1}-n}=\sum_{A_{2}\in{\mathfrak{W}}_{D}}(\det A_{2})^{m_{2}-m_{1}}\sum_{A_{1}\in{\mathfrak{W}}_{m_{2}-m_{1}}}\bigl(\det A_{1}\,\det A_{2}\bigr)^{m_{1}-n}\hskip 50.0pt

\displaystyle=\sum_{A_{2}\in{\mathfrak{W}}_{D}}(\det A_{2})^{m_{2}-n}\prod_{j=n-m_{2}+1}^{n-m_{1}}\zeta(j).

Using this, and the fact that
{\mathfrak{W}}_{(D\>0)}={\mathfrak{W}}_{D}
,
we get

\displaystyle W(\beta{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}})=W(D^{\prime})=\frac{\sum_{A\in{\mathfrak{W}}_{D^{\prime}}}(\det A)^{m_{1}-n}}{\prod_{j=n-m_{2}+1}^{n}\zeta(j)}=\frac{\sum_{A\in{\mathfrak{W}}_{D}}(\det A)^{m_{2}-n}}{\prod_{j=n-m_{1}+1}^{n}\zeta(j)}=W\bigl((D\hskip 5.0pt0)\bigr)=W(\beta).

∎

4.
Outline of an alternative proof using Poisson summation

In this section we give an outline of an alternative proof of our main result,
Theorem
1.1
.

The main idea of the proof is to apply the Poisson summation formula
in both sides of (
1.10
).
The manipulations which we will describe can be justified for any function

\rho:(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k_{1}}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k_{2}}\to\mathbb{C}

such that
\rho
and all its derivatives up to a sufficiently high order are of
rapid polynomial decay
(cf. Remark
2.2
).
Once the equality in (
1.10
)
has been proved for this family of test functions,
it follows that we have a corresponding equality of regular Borel measures on

(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k_{1}}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k_{2}}

(cf. Lemma
3.1
),
and in particular (
1.10
) also holds for the class of
functions appearing in the statement of Theorem
1.1
.

In the left-hand side of (
1.10
),
we apply Poisson summation
to the sums over
L^{*}
,
to obtain

(4.1)

\displaystyle\int_{X_{n}}\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{k}\in L}{\widetilde{\rho}}({\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{k})\,d\mu(L),

with

(4.2)

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\rho}}(x,v):=\int_{M_{n,k_{2}}(\mathbb{R})}\rho(x,y)e(-\operatorname{Tr}(v{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}y))\,dy.

The integral in (
4.1
) may be evaluated using Rogers’ mean value formula,
Theorem
1.5
.
The main work of the proof now consists in applying
Poisson summation to the sum over
\beta

in the right-hand side of (
1.10
),
and seeking to match the resulting expressions.

Let us fix an arbitrary choice of
m_{1},B_{1},m_{2},B_{2}
appearing in
(
1.10
).
In the following we will assume
m_{1}>0
and
0<m_{2}<k_{2}
,
but with appropriate interpretations, the argument which we present
can be seen to also cover the remaining cases
(cf. also Remark
1.2
).
Using the definitions of
\eta_{\beta}
and
W(\beta)

(cf. (
1.9
) and (
1.7
)),
and substituting

\beta=A{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}\gamma

(with
A\in{\mathfrak{W}}
and
\gamma\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
),
we find that
the sum over
\beta
in (
1.10
) can be rewritten as

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{A\in{\mathfrak{W}}}\sum_{\gamma\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})}\frac{(\det A)^{m_{2}-n}}{\zeta(n)\zeta(n-1)\cdots\zeta(n-m_{1}+1)}\int_{U_{m_{1}}}F_{x}(A{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}\gamma)\,\frac{dx}{\mathsf{d}(x)^{m_{2}}},

where, for any
x\in U_{m_{1}}
,
the function
F_{x}
is given by

(4.4)

\displaystyle F_{x}:M_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{C};\qquad F_{x}(\beta)=\int_{S(\beta)_{x}}\rho\bigl(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}},yB_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)\,d\eta_{\beta,x}(y).

In (
4.3
) we move the sum over
\gamma
inside the integral,
and apply the Poisson summation formula to
the resulting inner sum,

\sum_{\gamma}F_{x}(A{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}\gamma)
.
For this we need to compute the Fourier transform of
F_{x}
,

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{x}(\xi):=\int_{M_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{R})}F_{x}(\beta)e(-\operatorname{Tr}(\xi{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}\beta))\,d\beta\qquad(\xi\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{R})).

Noticing that
S(\beta)_{x}^{\prime}=x(x{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}x)^{-1}\beta+(x^{\perp})^{m_{2}}

we may rewrite the definition of
F_{x}
in (
4.4
)
as an integral over
y^{\prime}\in x^{\perp}
,
with the integrand being
\rho(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}},(x(x{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}x)^{-1}\beta+y^{\prime})B_{2}^{\mathsf{T}})
.
One verifies that the map taking

\langle\beta,y^{\prime}\rangle
to
(x(x{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}x)^{-1}\beta+y^{\prime})B_{2}{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}

is a linear bijection from

onto the subspace

of

,
and we thus obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{x}(\xi):=\mathsf{d}(x)^{m_{2}}\mathsf{d}(B_{2})^{-n}\int_{(V_{B_{2}}^{n}){\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}}\rho\bigl(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}},w\bigr)\,e\bigl(-\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\xi{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}x{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}wB_{2}(B_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}B_{2})^{-1}\bigr)\bigr)\,dw.

\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\xi{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}x{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}wB_{2}(B_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}B_{2})^{-1}\bigr)=\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(\bigl(x\xi(B_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}B_{2})^{-1}B_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr){\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}w\bigr)

hence the last integral is the Fourier transform of the

(V_{B_{2}}^{n}){\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}

In view of

\displaystyle\widehat{F}_{x}(\xi):=\mathsf{d}(x)^{m_{2}}\mathsf{d}(B_{2})^{-n}\int_{((V_{B_{2}}^{\perp})^{n}){\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}}{\widetilde{\rho}}\bigl(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}},x\xi(B_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}B_{2})^{-1}B_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}+u\bigr)\,du.

\displaystyle\sum_{A\in{\mathfrak{W}}}\frac{(\det A)^{-n}}{\zeta(n)\zeta(n-1)\cdots\zeta(n-m_{1}+1)}\,\mathsf{d}(B_{2})^{-n}\hskip 180.0pt

to the leftmost position.

runs through

Lemma 4.1

Let

(4.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\in{\mathfrak{W}}\\

(4.8)

\displaystyle N=\#\bigl\{{\text{\boldmath$a$}}\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{m_{1}}\>:\>\theta{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}{\text{\boldmath$a$}}=\mathbf{0}\text{ in $(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{m_{2}}$}\bigr\}.

Let
R
be the ring
\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}
.
The lemma follows from a study of the solution set

\displaystyle S:=\bigl\{{\text{\boldmath$a$}}\in R^{m_{1}}\>:\>{\text{\boldmath$a$}}\theta=\mathbf{0}\text{ in }R^{m_{2}}\bigr\}.

(Note that the equation

{\text{\boldmath$a$}}\leftrightarrow{\text{\boldmath$a$}}{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}

\displaystyle I_{j}:=\bigl\{a_{j}\in R\>:\>\exists a_{j+1},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}\in R\text{ such that }(0\,\cdots\,0\,a_{j}\,a_{j+1}\,\cdots\,a_{m_{1}})\in S\bigr\}.

.

(4.9)

\displaystyle\left\{\text{\parbox{390.0pt}{for any $j\in\{1,\ldots,m_{1}\}$,

${\text{\boldmath$a$}}\in S$ in the 'box'

,

B_{1}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k_{1},m_{1}}^{\prime}
,

m_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,k_{2}\}
,

B_{2}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{k_{2},m_{2}}^{\prime}
.
That is, we consider the following sum:

(5.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}\!(\mathbb{Z})}W(\beta)\int_{S(\beta)}\rho\bigl(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}},yB_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)\,d\eta_{\beta}(x,y).

It is immediate from the definition in (
1.9
) that,
for any
\beta\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}(\mathbb{Z})
,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta(n)\zeta(n-1)\cdots\zeta(n-m_{1}+1)}\leq W(\beta)\leq\frac{\zeta(n-m_{2})\cdots\zeta(n-m_{2}-m_{1}+1)}{\zeta(n)\zeta(n-1)\cdots\zeta(n-m_{1}+1)},

with the second relation being an equality when
\beta=0
.
In particular we have

(5.7)

\displaystyle W(\beta)=1+O_{m}(2^{-n}),

where

\displaystyle m:=m_{1}+m_{2}.

Since
\rho\geq 0
, it follows that (
5.5
)
equals

(5.8)

\displaystyle\bigl(1+O_{m}(2^{-n})\bigr)\sum_{\beta\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}\!(\mathbb{Z})}\int_{S(\beta)}\rho\bigl(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}},yB_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)\,d\eta_{\beta}(x,y).

Let us here write
\rho
as

(5.9)

\displaystyle\rho({\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{k_{1}},{\text{\boldmath$w$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$w$}}_{k_{2}})=\rho^{\prime}({\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{k_{1}}){\widetilde{\rho}}^{\prime}({\text{\boldmath$w$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$w$}}_{k_{2}})

with

(5.10)

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}({\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{k_{1}}):=\prod_{j=1}^{k_{1}}\rho_{j}({\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{j})\quad\text{and}\quad{\widetilde{\rho}}^{\prime}({\text{\boldmath$w$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$w$}}_{k_{2}}):=\prod_{j=1}^{k_{2}}{\widetilde{\rho}}_{j}({\text{\boldmath$w$}}_{j})

(cf. (
5.3
)).
Then (
5.8
) can be expressed as follows,
using also (
1.7
):

\displaystyle\bigl(1+O_{m}(2^{-n})\bigr)\sum_{\beta\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}\!(\mathbb{Z})}\int_{U_{m_{1}}}\int_{S(\beta)_{x}}\rho^{\prime}\bigl(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr){\widetilde{\rho}}^{\prime}\bigl(yB_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)\,d\eta_{\beta,x}(y)\,\frac{dx}{\mathsf{d}(x)^{m_{2}}}\hskip 70.0pt

(5.11)

\displaystyle=\bigl(1+O_{m}(2^{-n})\bigr)\int_{U_{m_{1}}}\rho^{\prime}\bigl(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)\sum_{\beta\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}\!(\mathbb{Z})}\int_{S(\beta)_{x}}{\widetilde{\rho}}^{\prime}\bigl(yB_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)\,d\eta_{\beta,x}(y)\,\frac{dx}{\mathsf{d}(x)^{m_{2}}}.

Let the column vectors of
x
be
{\text{\boldmath$x$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$x$}}_{m_{1}}
,
and let the column vectors of
y

be
{\text{\boldmath$y$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$y$}}_{m_{2}}
.
Set
\nu_{j,1}:=\nu_{j}(B_{1})
and
\nu_{j,2}:=\nu_{j}(B_{2})
.
It then follows from our definitions,
and in particular the assumption that

V_{1}\leq\cdots\leq V_{k_{1}}

and
W_{1}\leq\cdots\leq W_{k_{2}}
,
that

(5.12)

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}\bigl(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)=\prod_{j=1}^{m_{1}}\rho_{\nu_{j,1}}\bigl({\text{\boldmath$x$}}_{j}\bigr)\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\widetilde{\rho}}^{\prime}\bigl(yB_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)=\prod_{j=1}^{m_{2}}{\widetilde{\rho}}_{\nu_{j,2}}\bigl({\text{\boldmath$y$}}_{j}\bigr).

For
x\in U_{m_{1}}

we set

(5.13)

\displaystyle b_{x}:=x(x{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}x)^{-1}\in U_{m_{1}}.

Then for any
y\in M_{n,m_{2}}(\mathbb{R})

we note that
y\in S(\beta)_{x}^{\prime}

holds if and only if

x{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}y=\beta=x{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}b_{x}\beta
,
viz.,
if and only if
y-b_{x}\beta\in(x^{\perp})^{m_{2}}
,
where we recall that
x^{\perp}
denotes the orthogonal complement in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
the column span of
x
.
In other words,
y\in S(\beta)_{x}^{\prime}
holds if and only if

{\text{\boldmath$y$}}_{j}\in b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{j}+x^{\perp}

for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,m_{2}\}
,
where
{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{m_{2}}
are the column vectors of
\beta
.
Hence

(5.14)

\displaystyle\int_{S(\beta)_{x}}{\widetilde{\rho}}^{\prime}\bigl(yB_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)\,d\eta_{\beta,x}(y)=\prod_{j=1}^{m_{2}}\int_{b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{j}+x^{\perp}}{\widetilde{\rho}}_{\nu_{j,2}}\bigl({\text{\boldmath$y$}}_{j}\bigr)\,d{\text{\boldmath$y$}}_{j},

where
d{\text{\boldmath$y$}}_{j}
denotes the
(n-m_{1})
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on the affine subspace

b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{j}+x^{\perp}
.
But
{\widetilde{\rho}}_{\nu_{j,2}}
is the characteristic function of the open
n
-ball of radius

R_{j}:=(W_{\nu_{j,2}}/{\mathfrak{V}}_{n})^{1/n}

centered at the origin, with the origin removed.
Hence the expression in (
5.14
)
equals
\prod_{j=1}^{m_{2}}f_{j}\bigl(\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{j}\|\bigr)

where
f_{j}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is given by

\displaystyle f_{j}(r)=\begin{cases}{\mathfrak{V}}_{n-m_{1}}\cdot(R_{j}^{2}-r^{2})^{(n-m_{1})/2}&\text{if }\>r\leq R_{j}\\
0&\text{if }\>r\geq R_{j}.\end{cases}

It follows that, for every
x\in U_{m_{1}}
,

(5.15)

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}\!(\mathbb{Z})}\int_{S(\beta)_{x}}{\widetilde{\rho}}^{\prime}\bigl(yB_{2}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)\,d\eta_{\beta,x}(y)=\sum_{\beta\in M_{m_{1},m_{2}}\!(\mathbb{Z})}\prod_{j=1}^{m_{2}}f_{j}\bigl(\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{j}\|\bigr)=\prod_{j=1}^{m_{2}}\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}}f_{j}\bigl(\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{j}\|\bigr).

In the last product, we will approximate each sum

\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}}f_{j}\bigl(\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}_{j}\|\bigr)

by the corresponding
integral
,

(5.16)

\displaystyle\int_{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}}f_{j}\bigl(\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\|\bigr)\,d{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}=\mathsf{d}(b_{x})^{-1}\int_{{\text{\boldmath$z$}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}}f_{j}\bigl(\|{\text{\boldmath$z$}}\|\bigr)\,d{\text{\boldmath$z$}}=\mathsf{d}(b_{x})^{-1}{\mathfrak{V}}_{n}R_{j}^{n}=\mathsf{d}(x)W_{\nu_{j,2}},

where in the last equality we used the fact that

\mathsf{d}(b_{x})^{-1}=\mathsf{d}(x)
.
The following lemma gives a bound on the error in this approximation.
From now on we will write

C:=\bigl[-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}\bigr]^{m_{1}}
;
a closed unit cube in
\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}
.
We also define:

\displaystyle\ell_{x}:=\sup_{{\text{\boldmath$u$}}\in C}\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$u$}}\|.

Lemma 5.3
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,m_{2}\}
.
Assume that
n
is so large that
n\geq m_{1}+3

and
\varepsilon^{n}\leq W_{\nu_{j,2}}\leq\varepsilon^{-n}
.
Then for every
x\in U_{m_{1}}
satisfying
\ell_{x}\leq 1
, we have

(5.17)

\displaystyle\biggl|\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}}f_{j}\bigl(\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\|\bigr)-\mathsf{d}(x)W_{\nu_{j,2}}\biggr|\ll_{m_{1},\varepsilon}W_{\nu_{j,2}}\mathsf{d}(x)\ell_{x}.

Proof.

We split the integral in (
5.16
) by tesselating
\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}

by translates of the unit cube
C
;
this gives:

(5.18)

\displaystyle\mathsf{d}(x)W_{\nu_{j,2}}=\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}}\int_{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}+C}f_{j}\bigl(\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$u$}}\|\bigr)\,d{\text{\boldmath$u$}}.

Using this equality, and the triangle inequality,
it follows that the left-hand side of (
5.17
) is
bounded above by

\displaystyle\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}}\int_{C}\Bigl|f_{j}\bigl(\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\|\bigr)-f_{j}\bigl(\|b_{x}({\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}+{\text{\boldmath$u$}})\|\bigr)\Bigr|\,d{\text{\boldmath$u$}}.

Since
n\geq m_{1}+3
, the function
f_{j}(r)
is
C^{1}
,
and we have

(5.19)

\displaystyle\bigl|f_{j}(a)-f_{j}(b)\bigr|\leq(b-a)\sup_{s\in[a,b]}|f_{j}^{\prime}(s)|\qquad\text{for all $0\leq a\leq b$.}

Hence, recalling the definition of
\ell_{x}
,
it follows that the integrand in (
5.18
) is everywhere
bounded above by
\ell_{x}\cdot F_{j}\bigl(\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\|\bigr)
,
where

\displaystyle F_{j}(r):=\sup\Bigl\{|f_{j}^{\prime}(s)|\>:\>s\in\bigl[\max(0,r-\ell_{x}),r+\ell_{x}\bigr]\Bigr\}.

Hence (
5.19
)
(and thus also the left-hand side of (
5.17
))
is bounded above by

(5.20)

\displaystyle\ell_{x}\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}}F_{j}\bigl(\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\|\bigr)=-\ell_{x}\int_{0}^{\infty}F_{j}^{\prime}(r)A(r)\,dr

where

\displaystyle A(r):=\#\{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}\>:\>\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\|\leq r\}.

(The equality in (
5.20
)
holds by integration by parts;
note that
F_{j}(r)=0
for all
r\geq R_{j}+\ell_{x}
,
so that the range of integration can just as well be taken to be
[0,R_{j}+\ell_{x}]
.)

We compute

\displaystyle\bigl|f_{j}^{\prime}(s)\bigr|={\mathfrak{V}}_{n-m_{1}}(n-m_{1})\bigl(R_{j}^{2}-s^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{n-m_{1}}{2}-1}s\qquad(0\leq s\leq R_{j}).

and this function is verified to be increasing for
0\leq s\leq\kappa R_{j}
and decreasing for
\kappa R_{j}\leq s\leq R_{j}
, where

\displaystyle\kappa:=(n-m_{1}-1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(Note that
0<\kappa\leq 2^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.)
Hence the function
F_{j}(r)
is increasing for

0\leq r\leq\kappa R_{j}+\ell_{x}

(in fact it is constant on the interval

\max(0,\kappa R_{j}-\ell_{x})\leq r\leq\kappa R_{j}+\ell_{x}
),
and decreasing for
r\geq\kappa R_{j}+\ell_{x}
.
Note also that for
r\geq\kappa R_{j}+\ell_{x}
we have

F_{j}(r)=|f_{j}^{\prime}(r-\ell_{x})|
,
and thus
for all
r\in\bigl[\kappa R_{j},R_{j}\bigr)
we have:

\displaystyle F_{j}^{\prime}(r+\ell_{x})=\frac{d}{dr}|f_{j}^{\prime}(r)|={\mathfrak{V}}_{n-m_{1}}(n-m_{1})\bigl(R_{j}^{2}-r^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{n-m_{1}}{2}-2}\Bigl(R_{j}^{2}-(n-m_{1}-1)r^{2}\Bigr)

It follows that (
5.20
)
(and thus also the left-hand side of (
5.17
))
is bounded above by

\displaystyle-\ell_{x}\int_{\kappa R_{j}+\ell_{x}}^{R_{j}+\ell_{x}}F_{j}^{\prime}(r)A(r)\,dr\hskip 280.0pt

(5.21)

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{V}}_{n-m_{1}}(n-m_{1})\ell_{x}\int_{\kappa R_{j}}^{R_{j}}\bigl(R_{j}^{2}-r^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{n-m_{1}}{2}-2}\Bigl((n-m_{1}-1)r^{2}-R_{j}^{2}\Bigr)\,A(r+\ell_{x})\,dr.

In order to bound
A(r)
, note that for every

{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}
with
\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\|\leq r
,
the parallelogram
b_{x}({\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}+C)
is contained in

{\mathcal{B}}_{r+\ell_{x}}^{n}\cap V_{x}
(recall that
V_{x}=x\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}=\operatorname{Span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{{\text{\boldmath$x$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$x$}}_{m_{1}}\}
).
Furthermore these
parallelograms are pairwise disjoint,
and each of them has volume
\mathsf{d}(b_{x})=\mathsf{d}(x)^{-1}
,
whereas the ball
{\mathcal{B}}_{r+\ell_{x}}^{n}\cap V_{x}
has volume
{\mathfrak{V}}_{m_{1}}(r+\ell_{x})^{m_{1}}
.
(Here “volume” refers to the
m_{1}
-dimensional Lebesgue measure on
V_{x}
.)
Hence:

\displaystyle A(r)\leq{\mathfrak{V}}_{m_{1}}\,\mathsf{d}(x)\,(r+\ell_{x})^{m_{1}},\qquad\forall r\geq 0.

Using this bound in (
5.21
),
we conclude that the left-hand side of (
5.17
)
is bounded above by

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{V}}_{n-m_{1}}{\mathfrak{V}}_{m_{1}}(n-m_{1})\mathsf{d}(x)\ell_{x}\int_{\kappa R_{j}}^{R_{j}}\bigl(R_{j}^{2}-r^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{n-m_{1}}{2}-2}\Bigl((n-m_{1}-1)r^{2}-R_{j}^{2}\Bigr)\,(r+2\ell_{x})^{m_{1}}\,dr.

(5.22)

\displaystyle\ll_{m_{1}}{\mathfrak{V}}_{n-m_{1}}n^{2}\mathsf{d}(x)\ell_{x}\int_{\kappa R_{j}}^{R_{j}}\bigl(R_{j}^{2}-r^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{n-m_{1}}{2}-2}r^{2}\,(r+2\ell_{x})^{m_{1}}\,dr.

Here the integral is

\displaystyle\ll_{m_{1}}\sum_{a=0}^{m_{1}}\ell_{x}^{m_{1}-a}\int_{0}^{R_{j}}\bigl(R_{j}^{2}-r^{2}\bigr)^{\frac{n-m_{1}}{2}-2}r^{2+a}\,dr=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a=0}^{m_{1}}\ell_{x}^{m_{1}-a}R_{j}^{n-m_{1}-1+a}\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{n-m_{1}}{2}-1\bigr)\Gamma\bigl(\frac{a+3}{2}\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{n-m_{1}+a+1}{2}\bigr)}

(5.23)

\displaystyle\ll_{m_{1}}\sum_{a=0}^{m_{1}}\ell_{x}^{m_{1}-a}R_{j}^{n-m_{1}-1+a}n^{-\frac{a+3}{2}}\ll_{m_{1},\varepsilon}R_{j}^{n}\cdot n^{-\frac{m_{1}}{2}-2},

where in the last step we
used
\ell_{x}\leq 1
,
and we also used

R_{j}=(W_{\nu_{j,2}}/{\mathfrak{V}}_{n})^{1/n}

and
\varepsilon^{n}\leq W_{\nu_{j,2}}\leq\varepsilon^{-n}

to see that
R_{j}\asymp_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{n}
.
Combining (
5.22
) and (
5.23
),
and using
{\mathfrak{V}}_{n-m_{1}}\asymp_{m_{1}}{\mathfrak{V}}_{n}n^{m_{1}/2}

and
{\mathfrak{V}}_{n}R_{j}^{n}=W_{\nu_{j,2}}
,
we obtain the first bound in (
5.17
).
∎

We complement Lemma
5.3
with a cruder bound which is valid regardless of the size of
\ell_{x}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,m_{2}\}
.
Assume that
n
is so large that
n\geq m_{1}+3

and
\varepsilon^{n}\leq V_{k_{1}},W_{\nu_{j,2}}\leq\varepsilon^{-n}
.
Then for every
x\in U_{m_{1}}

with
\rho^{\prime}(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}})=1
, we have

(5.24)

\displaystyle\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}}f_{j}\bigl(\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\|\bigr)\ll_{m_{1},\varepsilon}W_{\nu_{j,2}}\,n^{m_{1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{d}(x)W_{\nu_{j,2}}\ll_{m_{1},\varepsilon}W_{\nu_{j,2}}\,n^{m_{1}}.

Proof.

It follows from

\rho^{\prime}(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}})=1

that
\|{\text{\boldmath$x$}}_{j}\|<(V_{k_{1}}/{\mathfrak{V}}_{n})^{1/n}\ll_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{n}
for each
j
.
Hence
\mathsf{d}(x)\ll_{m_{1},\varepsilon}n^{m_{1}}
,
and thus the second bound in (
5.24
) holds.

In order to prove the first bound
in (
5.24
),
we start by noticing

(5.25)

\displaystyle\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}}f_{j}\bigl(\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\|\bigr)\leq{\mathfrak{V}}_{n-m_{1}}R_{j}^{n-m_{1}}\cdot\#\bigl\{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}\>:\>\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\|<R_{j}\bigr\},

since
f_{j}(r)\leq{\mathfrak{V}}_{n-m_{1}}R_{j}^{n-m_{1}}

for all
r\geq 0
.
Now note that for all
{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}

we have
\|x{\hskip-1.0pt{}^{\mathsf{T}}}b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\|=\|{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\|\geq 1
,
and combining this with the fact that

\|{\text{\boldmath$x$}}_{j}\|\ll_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{n}
for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,m_{1}\}
,
it follows that
\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\|\gg_{m_{1},\varepsilon}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.
It follows that the distance between any two vectors

b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}
and
b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}^{\prime}
for
{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\neq{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}

is
\gg_{m_{1},\varepsilon}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,
and hence there exists a number
r\gg_{m_{1},\varepsilon}n^{-\frac{1}{2}}

such that the balls
{\mathcal{B}}_{r}^{n}+b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}
are pairwise disjoint as
\beta
runs through
\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}
.
We may also require that
r\leq n^{-\frac{1}{2}}

(indeed otherwise replace
r
by
n^{-\frac{1}{2}}
).
Then it follows that for every

{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}
with
\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\|<R_{j}
,
the ball
{\mathcal{B}}_{r}^{n}+b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}

is contained in
{\mathcal{B}}_{R_{j}+n^{-1/2}}^{n}
.
Intersecting these balls with
V_{x}
and considering the volume,
it follows that

(5.26)

\displaystyle\#\bigl\{{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m_{1}}\>:\>\|b_{x}{\text{\boldmath$\beta$}}\|<R_{j}\bigr\}\leq\frac{{\mathfrak{V}}_{m_{1}}(R_{j}+n^{-1/2})^{m_{1}}}{{\mathfrak{V}}_{m_{1}}r^{m_{1}}}\ll_{m_{1},\varepsilon}R_{j}^{m_{1}}r^{-m_{1}}\ll_{m_{1},\varepsilon}R_{j}^{m_{1}}n^{\frac{m_{1}}{2}}.

(Here we used
\varepsilon^{n}\leq W_{\nu_{j,2}}\leq\varepsilon^{-n}

to conclude that
n^{-1/2}\leq n^{1/2}\ll_{\varepsilon}R_{j}
.)
Combining (
5.25
)
and (
5.26
),
and using
{\mathfrak{V}}_{n-m_{1}}\asymp_{m_{1}}{\mathfrak{V}}_{n}n^{\frac{m_{1}}{2}}
,
we obtain the first bound in (
5.24
).
∎

We will now apply
Lemma
5.3

and
Lemma
5.4

to estimate the expression in (
5.15
).
Let us pick
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}
in both the lemmas;
thus in order for the lemmas to apply,
from now on we assume that
n
is so large that

n\geq m_{1}+3
and so that all the
V_{i}
s and all the
W_{i}
s
lie in the interval
[2^{-n},2^{n}]
.
Recall that we also assume
n>k_{1}+k_{2}\geq m_{1}+m_{2}
.
Using Lemma
5.3
,
we conclude that for
every
x\in U_{m_{1}}
satisfying
\ell_{x}\leq 1
,
the expression in (
5.15
) equals

\displaystyle\biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{m_{2}}W_{\nu_{j,2}}\biggr)\mathsf{d}(x)^{m_{2}}\Bigl(1+O_{m}(\ell_{x})\Bigr).

Using Lemma
5.4
,
we conclude that for
every
x\in U_{m_{1}}
satisfying
\rho^{\prime}(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}})=1
,
the expression in (
5.15
) equals

(5.27)

\displaystyle\biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{m_{2}}W_{\nu_{j,2}}\biggr)\mathsf{d}(x)^{m_{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{m_{2}}\Bigl(1+O_{m_{1}}\Bigl(\frac{n^{m_{1}}}{\mathsf{d}(x)}\Bigr)\Bigr)=\biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{m_{2}}W_{\nu_{j,2}}\biggr)\mathsf{d}(x)^{m_{2}}\Bigl(1+O_{m}\Bigl(\frac{n^{m_{1}m_{2}}}{\mathsf{d}(x)^{m_{2}}}\Bigr)\Bigr).

We will use the first of these estimates when
\ell_{x}\leq c
and the second when
\ell_{x}>c
,
where
c
is a parameter in the interval
(0,1]

which we will choose later.
Let
\chi_{c}
be the characteristic function of
\ell_{x}\leq c

and let
\widetilde{\chi}_{c}=1-\chi_{c}
be the characteristic function of
\ell_{x}>c
.
Then we conclude that
(
5.11
) (and thus also (
5.5
))
equals

\displaystyle\bigl(1+O_{m}(2^{-n})\bigr)\biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{m_{2}}W_{\nu_{j,2}}\biggr)\Biggl(\int_{U_{m_{1}}}\rho^{\prime}(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}})\,dx+O_{m}\biggl(\int_{U_{m_{1}}}\rho^{\prime}(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}})\,\chi_{c}(x)\,\ell_{x}\,dx\hskip 40.0pt

(5.28)

\displaystyle+n^{m_{1}m_{2}}\int_{U_{m_{1}}}\rho^{\prime}(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}})\,\widetilde{\chi}_{c}(x)\,\frac{dx}{\mathsf{d}(x)^{m_{2}}}\biggr)\Biggr)

Here we have, by (
5.12
),

\displaystyle\int_{U_{m_{1}}}\rho^{\prime}(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}})\,dx=\prod_{j=1}^{m_{1}}V_{\nu_{j,1}}.

Furthermore, the first error term is

\displaystyle\int_{U_{m_{1}}}\rho^{\prime}(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}})\,\chi_{c}(x)\,\ell_{x}\,dx\leq c\int_{U_{m_{1}}}\rho^{\prime}(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}})\,dx=c\prod_{j=1}^{m_{1}}V_{\nu_{j,1}}.

In order to bound the second error term in (
5.28
),
we apply

[
29
, Lemma 5.2]
;
combined with (
5.12
) this gives that

\displaystyle\int_{U_{m_{1}}}

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}})\,\widetilde{\chi}_{c}(x)\,\frac{dx}{\mathsf{d}(x)^{m_{2}}}

(5.29)

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{j=n-m_{1}+1}^{n}(j{\mathfrak{V}}_{j})}{\prod_{j=1}^{m_{1}}(j{\mathfrak{V}}_{j})}\int_{U_{m_{1}}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}\times\{\mathbf{0}\})^{m_{1}}}\rho^{\prime}(xB_{1}^{\mathsf{T}})\,\widetilde{\chi}_{c}(x)\,\mathsf{d}(x)^{n-m_{1}-m_{2}}\,dx,

where in the second line,

\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}\times\{\mathbf{0}\}
denotes the subspace

\{{\text{\boldmath$x$}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\>:\>x_{m_{1}+1}=\cdots=x_{n}=0\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
and
dx

\displaystyle n^{k_{1}k_{2}}\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{k_{1}}\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{k_{2}}\sum_{\langle D_{1},D_{2}\rangle\in({\mathfrak{A}}(k_{1},m_{1})\times{\mathfrak{A}}(k_{2},m_{2}))\smallsetminus(\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\prime}_{k_{1},m_{1}}\times\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\prime}_{k_{2},m_{2}})}J_{n-m_{2}}(\sigma^{\prime};D_{1})\,J_{n-m_{1}}({\widetilde{\sigma}}^{\prime};D_{2}),

where for any
1\leq m\leq k<\widetilde{n}
,
D\in{\mathfrak{A}}(k,m)
and (measurable) function

\sigma:(\mathbb{R}^{\widetilde{n}})^{m}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
J_{\widetilde{n}}(\sigma;D)
is defined by

\displaystyle J_{\widetilde{n}}(\sigma;D):=\Big(\frac{e_{1}}{q}\cdots\frac{e_{m}}{q}\Big)^{\widetilde{n}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{\widetilde{n}})^{m}}\sigma(xq^{-1}D)\,dx,

where
q=q(D)
is as in Theorem
2.1
,
and
e_{1},\ldots,e_{m}
are the elementary divisors of
D
.
Note here that for any
1\leq m_{1}\leq k_{1}
and
1\leq m_{2}\leq k_{2}
,
the sum over
\langle D_{1},D_{2}\rangle
appearing in (
5.37
)
can be decomposed as the sum of
the three products

(5.38)

\displaystyle\Biggl(\sum_{D_{1}\in{\mathfrak{A}}(k_{1},m_{1})\smallsetminus\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\prime}_{k_{1},m_{1}}}J_{n-m_{2}}(\sigma^{\prime};D_{1})\Biggr)\Biggl(\sum_{D_{2}\in{\mathfrak{A}}(k_{2},m_{2})\smallsetminus\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\prime}_{k_{2},m_{2}}}J_{n-m_{1}}({\widetilde{\sigma}}^{\prime};D_{2})\Biggr)

and

(5.39)

\displaystyle\Biggl(\sum_{D_{1}\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\prime}_{k_{1},m_{1}}}J_{n-m_{2}}(\sigma^{\prime};D_{1})\Biggr)\Biggl(\sum_{D_{2}\in{\mathfrak{A}}(k_{2},m_{2})\smallsetminus\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\prime}_{k_{2},m_{2}}}J_{n-m_{1}}({\widetilde{\sigma}}^{\prime};D_{2})\Biggr)

and

(5.40)

\displaystyle\Biggl(\sum_{D_{1}\in{\mathfrak{A}}(k_{1},m_{1})\smallsetminus\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\prime}_{k_{1},m_{1}}}J_{n-m_{2}}(\sigma^{\prime};D_{1})\Biggr)\Biggl(\sum_{D_{2}\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\prime}_{k_{2},m_{2}}}J_{n-m_{1}}({\widetilde{\sigma}}^{\prime};D_{2})\Biggr).

Now the sum

(5.41)

\displaystyle\sum_{D_{1}\in{\mathfrak{A}}(k_{1},m_{1})\smallsetminus\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\prime}_{k_{1},m_{1}}}J_{n-m_{2}}(\sigma^{\prime};D_{1})

which appears as a factor in (
5.38
) and (
5.40
),
is exactly the sum which is treated in

[
31
, pp. 950(top)–951(middle)]

(applied with “
n
” taken to be
n-m_{2}
in our notation).
It should here be noted that
(cf. (
5.10
))

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}({\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{1},\ldots,{\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{k_{1}}):=\prod_{j=1}^{k_{1}}\sigma_{j}({\text{\boldmath$v$}}_{j})

where for each
j
,
\sigma_{j}
is the characteristic function of the open
(n-m_{2})
-ball of
radius
(V_{j}/{\mathfrak{V}}_{n})^{1/n}
centered at the origin, with the origin removed.
The volume of the ball which is the support of
\sigma_{j}
is therefore

(5.42)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{V}}_{n-m_{2}}(V_{j}/{\mathfrak{V}}_{n})^{(n-m_{2})/n}\ll_{m}V_{j}

(in fact the left-hand side tends to
e^{m_{2}/2}V_{j}
as
n\to\infty
).
The key points for us is that these volumes stay
bounded
as
n\to\infty
,
since the volumes
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k_{1}}
are fixed.
Therefore, it follows from the bounds in

[
31
, pp. 950(top)–951(middle)]

that
the sum in (
5.41
)
is
\ll_{m}(3/4)^{\frac{n-m_{2}}{2}}\ll_{m}(3/4)^{\frac{n}{2}}

(the implied constant also depends on the
V_{j}
s).

In the same way, we also have

(5.43)

\displaystyle\sum_{D_{2}\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\prime}_{k_{2},m_{2}}}J_{n-m_{1}}({\widetilde{\sigma}}^{\prime};D_{2})\ll_{m}(3/4)^{\frac{n}{2}}.

Furthermore, by the proof of

[
31
, Prop. 1]
,
for each
D_{1}
in the finite set

\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\prime}_{k_{1},m_{1}}

we have that
J_{n-m_{2}}(\sigma^{\prime};D_{1})
tends to a finite limit as
n\to\infty
;
the same thing of course also holds for

J_{n-m_{1}}({\widetilde{\sigma}}^{\prime};D_{2})
for each fixed
D_{2}\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{M}}}^{\prime}_{k_{2},m_{2}}
.

In view of these bounds, we conclude that the expression in
(
5.37
) tends to zero
exponentially rapidly
as
n\to\infty
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

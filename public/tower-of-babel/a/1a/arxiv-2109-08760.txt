Non-density of stable mappings on non-compact manifolds
Shunsuke Ichiki

Abstract
. Around 1970, Mather established a significant theory on the stability of C ∞ C^{\infty} mappings and gave a characterization of the density of proper stable mappings in the set of all proper mappings. The result yields a characterization of the density of stable mappings in the set of all mappings in the case where the source manifold is compact. The aim of this paper is to complement Mather’s result. Namely, we show that the set of stable mappings in the set of all mappings is never dense if the source manifold is non-compact. Moreover, as a corollary of Mather’s result and the main theorem of this paper, we give a characterization of the density of stable mappings in the set of all mappings in the case where the source manifold is not necessarily compact.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

(r>0)
be a mapping such that

\displaystyle f_{p}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}+a,

If

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\partial f_{p}}{\partial x_{i}}(x)-\frac{\partial g_{p}}{\partial x_{i}}(x)\right)^{2}}<\frac{r}{2}

.

lemma

2.1
.

\displaystyle D(x):=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial f_{p}}{\partial x_{1}}(x)-\frac{\partial g_{p}}{\partial x_{1}}(x),\ldots,\frac{\partial f_{p}}{\partial x_{n}}(x)-\frac{\partial g_{p}}{\partial x_{n}}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(x_{1}-\frac{\partial g_{p}}{\partial x_{1}}(x),\ldots,x_{n}-\frac{\partial g_{p}}{\partial x_{n}}(x)\right).

Remark 2.2
.

As in

8
]

On the other hand, note that in general, they are not equivalent conditions as follows.
First, Mather gives a non-proper mapping which is infinitesimally stable but not stable in

6

Moreover, the non-proper function

is stable but not infinitesimally stable
1
1

1

In

1
]

for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and
\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}R_{\alpha}=\infty
, there exists
\alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\delta(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying
\alpha\geq\alpha_{1}
.
Here, note that for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})\right\|<\delta(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})

by
eq.

3.4
since
j^{1}g(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})\in O_{\alpha}
.
Thus, it follows that for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\alpha\geq\alpha_{1}\Longrightarrow\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})\right\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, since
q_{\alpha},q_{\alpha}^{\prime}\in U_{\alpha}^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha})\right\|=\left\|\eta_{\alpha}(\varphi_{\alpha}(q_{\alpha}^{\prime}))-\gamma(\alpha)\right\|=\frac{\left\|\varphi_{\alpha}(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})\right\|^{2}}{2}<\frac{r_{\alpha}^{2}}{2}.

Since
\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}r_{\alpha}=0
, there exists
\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\alpha\geq\alpha_{2}\Longrightarrow\left\|(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha})\right\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Since
(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha})=\gamma(\alpha)
for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\set{(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha})}{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}=\mathbb{Q}^{p}.

Hence, there exists
\alpha_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha_{3}>\max\set{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}
and

(3.11)

\displaystyle\left\|(\psi\circ f)(q_{\alpha_{3}})-y_{0}\right\|<\frac{\varepsilon}{3}.

Thus, we have
\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{3}}^{\prime})-y_{0}\right\|<\varepsilon
by (
3.8
) to
eq.

3.11
, which implies that
g(q_{\alpha_{3}}^{\prime})\in V^{\prime}
.
∎

STEP 3
.
The purpose of this step is to show that any mapping in
\mathcal{U}
is not infinitesimally stable.
Let
g\in\mathcal{U}
be any mapping, and let
\Sigma
be the set consisting of all critical points of
g
.
Set
K=\psi^{-1}(\overline{B^{p}(0,r)})
, where
r
is a positive real number.
Note that
K
is a compact set in
P
.
Then, from
lemma

3.1

item

2
,
(g|_{\Sigma})^{-1}(K)
contains a countable subset of
\set{q_{\alpha}^{\prime}}{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Since
F(q_{\alpha}^{\prime})\not\in\overline{B^{\ell}(z_{0},R_{\alpha})}
for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and
\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}R_{\alpha}=\infty
, the set
F((g|_{\Sigma})^{-1}(K))
is not compact, which implies that
(g|_{\Sigma})^{-1}(K)
is not compact.
Since
g|_{\Sigma}:\Sigma\to P
is not proper,
g
is not infinitesimally stable (note that this fact follows from
[
8
, Proposition 5.1]
).

STEP 4
.
The purpose of this step is to show that any mapping in
\mathcal{U}
is not stable.
Let
g\in\mathcal{U}
be an arbitrary mapping, and let
\mathcal{U}_{g}
be any open neighborhood of
g
.
Then, there exist a non-negative integer
k
and an open set
O^{\prime}
of
J^{k}(N,P)
such that

\displaystyle g\in\set{h\in C^{\infty}(N,P)}{j^{k}h(N)\subset O^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{U}_{g}.

In order to prove that
g
is not stable, it is sufficient to show that there exists a mapping
h\in C^{\infty}(N,P)
satisfying the following properties.

•

We have
j^{k}h(N)\subset O^{\prime}
.

•

There exist
(p+1)
-critical points of
h
which share the same
critical value.

Note that the second property implies that
h
is not stable.

For any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, let
G_{\alpha,c}:N\to P
be the mapping defined by

\displaystyle G_{\alpha,c}=\psi^{-1}\circ(\psi\circ g+\rho_{\alpha}c).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\alpha
be any positive integer.
Then, there exists a positive real number
r_{\alpha}^{\prime}
such that
j^{k}G_{\alpha,c}(N)\subset O^{\prime}
for any
c\in B^{p}(0,r_{\alpha}^{\prime})
.

Proof of
lemma

3.2
.

Let
\Gamma_{\alpha}:N\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\to J^{k}(N,P)
be the mapping defined by
\Gamma_{\alpha}(q,c)=j^{k}G_{\alpha,c}(q)
.
For any
q\in\supp\rho_{\alpha}
, since
\Gamma_{\alpha}(q,0)=j^{k}g(q)\in O^{\prime}
and
\Gamma_{\alpha}
is continuous at
(q,0)
, there exist an open neighborhood
U_{q}
of
q\in N
and an open neighborhood
W_{q}
of
0\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that
\Gamma_{\alpha}(U_{q}\times W_{q})\subset O^{\prime}
.
Since
\set{U_{q}}_{q\in\supp\rho_{\alpha}}
is an open covering of the compact set
\supp\rho_{\alpha}
, there exists a finite subset
S
of
\supp\rho_{\alpha}
such that
\supp\rho_{\alpha}\subset\bigcup_{q\in S}U_{q}
.
Since
\bigcap_{q\in S}W_{q}
is an open neighborhood of
0\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, there exists a positive real number
r_{\alpha}^{\prime}
such that
B^{p}(0,r_{\alpha}^{\prime})\subset\bigcap_{q\in S}W_{q}
.

Let
c\in B^{p}(0,r_{\alpha}^{\prime})
and
q\in N
be any points.
If
q\not\in\supp\rho_{\alpha}
, then we have
j^{k}G_{\alpha,c}(q)\in O^{\prime}
since
G_{\alpha,c}=g
on the open neighborhood
(\supp\rho_{\alpha})^{c}
of
q
.
If
q\in\supp\rho_{\alpha}
, then there exists a point
q_{0}\in S
such that
q\in U_{q_{0}}
.
Since
c\in W_{q_{0}}
, we obtain
j^{k}G_{\alpha,c}(q)=\Gamma_{\alpha}(q,c)\in O^{\prime}
.
∎

Since
g\in\mathcal{U}
, note that there exists a sequence
\set{q_{\alpha}^{\prime}}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}
of points in
N
satisfying
item

1
and
item

2
of
lemma

3.1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
m
be any positive integer.
Then, there exist
(m+1)
distinct positive integers
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m+1}
and
m
positive real numbers
r_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime},\ldots,r_{\alpha_{m}}^{\prime}

(r_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime}>\cdots>r_{\alpha_{m}}^{\prime})
such that for any
j\in\set{1,\ldots,m}
,

(1)

j^{k}G_{\alpha_{j},c}(N)\subset O^{\prime}
for any
c\in B^{p}(0,r_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})
,

(2)

\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{j+1}}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})\right\|<\displaystyle\frac{r_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime}}{p}
.

Proof of
lemma

3.3
.

We prove the lemma by induction on
m
.

Let
\alpha_{1}
be any positive integer.
By
lemma

3.2
, there exists a positive real number
r_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime}
such that
j^{k}G_{\alpha_{1},c}(N)\subset O^{\prime}
for any
c\in B^{p}(0,r_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime})
.
By
lemma

3.1

item

2
, there exists
\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\set{\alpha_{1}}
such that

\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{2}}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime})\right\|<\frac{r_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime}}{p}.

Hence, the case
m=1
holds.

We assume that the lemma is true for
m=i
, where
i
is a positive integer.
By
lemma

3.2
, there exists a positive real number
r_{\alpha_{i+1}}^{\prime}

(r_{\alpha_{i}}^{\prime}>r_{\alpha_{i+1}}^{\prime})
such that
j^{k}G_{\alpha_{i+1},c}(N)\subset O^{\prime}
for any
c\in B^{p}(0,r_{\alpha_{i+1}}^{\prime})
.
By
lemma

3.1

item

2
, there exists
\alpha_{i+2}\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\set{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{i+1}}
such that
\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{i+2}}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{i+1}}^{\prime})\right\|<\frac{r_{\alpha_{i+1}}^{\prime}}{p}
.
Therefore, the case
m=i+1
holds.
∎

For simplicity, set
I=\set{1,\ldots,p}
.
By
lemma

3.3
in the case
m=p
, there exist
(p+1)
distinct positive integers
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p+1}
and
p
positive real numbers
r_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime},\ldots,r_{\alpha_{p}}^{\prime}

(r_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime}>\cdots>r_{\alpha_{p}}^{\prime})
such that for any
j\in I
,

(a)

j^{k}G_{\alpha_{j},c}(N)\subset O^{\prime}
for any
c\in B^{p}(0,r_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})
,

(b)

\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{j+1}}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})\right\|<\displaystyle\frac{r_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime}}{p}
.

Let
h:N\to P
be the mapping defined by

\displaystyle h=\psi^{-1}\circ\left(\psi\circ g+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\rho_{\alpha_{i}}c_{i}\right),

where
c_{i}=(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{p+1}}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{i}}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
.

First, we show that
j^{k}h(N)\subset O^{\prime}
.
Let
q\in N
be an arbitrary point.
In the case where
q
is an element of
(\bigcup_{j=1}^{p}\supp\rho_{\alpha_{j}})^{c}
, since
h=g
on the open neighborhood
(\bigcup_{j=1}^{p}\supp\rho_{\alpha_{j}})^{c}
of
q
, we have
j^{k}h(q)=j^{k}g(q)\in O^{\prime}
.
We consider the case where there exists
j\in I
such that
q\in\supp\rho_{\alpha_{j}}
.
Since
\supp\rho_{\alpha_{j}}\subset\bigcap_{i\in I\setminus\set{j}}(\supp\rho_{\alpha_{i}})^{c}
and
h=G_{\alpha_{j},c_{j}}
on the open neighborhood
\bigcap_{i\in I\setminus\set{j}}(\supp\rho_{\alpha_{i}})^{c}
of
q
, we have
j^{k}h(q)=j^{k}G_{\alpha_{j},c_{j}}(q)
.
Moreover, since

(3.12)

\begin{split}\left\|c_{j}\right\|&=\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{p+1}}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})\right\|\\
&\leq\sum_{i=j}^{p}\left\|(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{i+1}}^{\prime})-(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{i}}^{\prime})\right\|\\
&<\sum_{i=j}^{p}\frac{r_{\alpha_{i}}^{\prime}}{p}\\
&\leq r_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime},\end{split}

we have
c_{j}\in B^{p}(0,r_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})
.
Note that the last two inequalities in
eq.

3.12
follow from
item

b
and the fact that
r_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime}>\cdots>r_{\alpha_{p}}^{\prime}
, respectively.
Thus, we obtain
j^{k}G_{\alpha_{j},c_{j}}(q)\in O^{\prime}
by
item

a
, which implies that
j^{k}h(q)\in O^{\prime}
.

Finally, we show that there exist
(p+1)
-critical points of
h
which share the same critical value.
For any
i,j\in I
, since
\rho_{\alpha_{i}}(q_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})=\delta_{ij}
and
\rho_{\alpha_{i}}(q_{\alpha_{p+1}}^{\prime})=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\psi\circ g+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\rho_{\alpha_{i}}c_{i}\right)(q_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})=(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime})+c_{j}=(\psi\circ g)(q_{\alpha_{p+1}}^{\prime})=(\psi\circ h)(q_{\alpha_{p+1}}^{\prime}),

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta.
Thus, we have
h(q_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime})=\cdots=h(q_{\alpha_{p+1}}^{\prime})
.
Moreover, for any
j\in I
, the point
q_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime}
(resp.,
q_{\alpha_{p+1}}^{\prime}
) is a critical point of
h
since
h=\psi^{-1}\circ(\psi\circ g+c_{j})
on an open neighborhood of
q_{\alpha_{j}}^{\prime}
(resp.,
h=g
on an open neighborhood of
q_{\alpha_{p+1}}^{\prime}
).
Namely,
q_{\alpha_{1}}^{\prime},\ldots,q_{\alpha_{p+1}}^{\prime}
share the same critical value of
h
.

\Box

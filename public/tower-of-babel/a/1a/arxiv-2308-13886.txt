Existence and Uniqueness of Nonsimple Multiple SLE
Dapeng Zhan

Abstract
We prove the existence and uniqueness of multiple SLE κ associated with any given link pattern for κ ∈ ( 4 , 6 ] \kappa\in(4,6] . We also have the uniqueness for κ ∈ ( 6 , 8 ) \kappa\in(6,8) . The multiple SLE κ law is constructed by first inductively constructing a σ \sigma -finite multiple SLE κ measure and then normalizing the measure whenever it is finite. The total mass of the measure satisfies the conformal covariance, asymptotics and PDE for multiple SLE κ partition functions in the literature subject to the assumption that it is smooth.



1
Introduction

The Schramm-Loewner evolution (SLE) is a one-parameter (
\kappa\in(0,\infty)
) family of random curves, which are known to be the scaling limits of various two-dimensional discrete models. A chordal SLE curve grows in a simply connected domain connecting two distinct boundary points (more precisely, prime ends). It arises naturally as the candidate of the scaling limit of some lattice model with Dobrushin boundary condition.

When the Dobrushin boundary condition is replaced by alternative boundary condition, the scaling limit is expected to be multiple SLE. In a multiple
N
-SLE
κ
configuration, there are
N
random curves connecting
2N
distinct marked boundary points of a simply connected domain with the property that the conditional law of any curve given all other curves is a chordal SLE
κ
curve in one connected component of the complement of the other curves.

When
\kappa\in(0,4]
, multiple SLE
κ
associated with any given link pattern exists (cf.
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
12
]
) and is unique in law (cf.
[
1
]
). This is also true for
\kappa\in\{16/3,6\}
(
[
1
]
). When
N=2
, the existence and uniqueness of
2
-SLE
κ
are known for all
\kappa\in(0,8)
(cf.
[
15
,
11
]
).

In this paper we study multiple SLE
κ
for
\kappa\in(4,8)
. The main result is the existence and uniqueness for any
\kappa\in(4,6]
. We also prove the uniqueness in the case
\kappa\in(6,8)
. In fact, for all
\kappa\in(0,8)
, given any link pattern
\alpha
in a domain
D
, we construct a
\sigma
-finite measure
Q^{D}_{\alpha}
on the space of
N
-tuples of curves associated with
\alpha
using a cascade relation. When
\kappa\leq 6
,
Q^{D}_{\alpha}
is finite, and its normalization is the law of multiple SLE associated with
\alpha
. For
\kappa\in(6,8)
, the same statement holds as long as
Q^{D}_{\alpha}
is known to be finite.

We call the total mass
H^{D}_{\alpha}=|Q^{D}_{\alpha}|
a multiple SLE
κ
partition function. We focus on the case
D=\mathbb{H}
, in which case
H(\alpha):=H^{\mathbb{H}}_{\alpha}
is a function on an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
. Using the construction of
Q^{D}_{\alpha}
, we prove that
H
satisfies conformal covariance and asymptotics for multiple SLE pure partition functions (cf.
[
7
]
). We construct a continuous local martingale using
H
. If
H
is known to be smooth, then Itô’s calculus shows that
H
satisfies a system of PDEs for multiple SLE partition function introduced by Dubédat in
[
4
]
.

Preliminary

2.1
Measures and kernels

We use
f(\mu)
(instead of
f_{*}(\mu)
) to denote the pushforward of a measure
\mu
under a measurable map
f
. We use
g\cdot\mu
or
g(\omega)\cdot\mu(d\omega)
to denote a measure obtained by weighting a measure
\mu
by a nonnegative function
g
.

Let
(\Omega_{j},{\cal F}_{j})
,
j=1,2
, be measurable spaces. A function
\nu:\Omega_{1}\times{\cal F}_{2}\to[0,\infty)
is called a finite kernel from
\Omega_{1}
to
\Omega_{2}
if (i) for any
\omega_{1}\in\Omega_{1}
,
\nu(\omega_{1},\cdot)
is a finite measure on
(\Omega_{2},{\cal F}_{2})
; and (ii) for any
B\in{\cal F}_{2}
,
\nu(\cdot,B)
is a measurable function on
(\Omega_{1},{\cal F}_{1})
. Suppose
\mu
is a
\sigma
-finite measure on
\Omega_{1}
, and
\nu
is a finite kernel from
\Omega_{1}
to
\Omega_{2}
. Using Carathéodory’s extension theorem, we define
\mu{\otimes}\nu
to be the unique
\sigma
-finite measure on
\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}
, which satisfies that,

\mu{\otimes}\nu(B_{1}\times B_{2})=\int_{B_{1}}\mu(d\omega_{1})\nu(\omega_{1},B_{2}),\quad B_{1}\in{\cal F}_{1},\quad B_{2}\in{\cal F}_{2}.

(2.1)

Note that
\mu\otimes\nu
is well defined even if
\nu(\omega_{1},\cdot)
is not defined for
\omega_{1}
in some
\mu
-null set.
Sometimes we write
\mu{\otimes}\nu
as
\mu(d\omega_{1}){\otimes}\nu(\omega_{1},d\omega_{2})
to emphasize the dependence of
\nu
on
\omega_{1}
.

We list a few simple facts.

•

Let
P_{1}
be the projection
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\mapsto\omega_{1}
. Then

P_{1}(\mu{\otimes}\nu)=\nu(\cdot,\Omega_{2})\cdot\mu\ll\mu.

(2.2)

•

If
f
is a nonnegative measurable function on
\Omega_{1}
, then

f(\omega_{1})\cdot\mu(d\omega_{1}){\otimes}\nu(\omega_{1},d\omega_{2})=\mu(d\omega_{1}){\otimes}f(\omega_{1})\nu(\omega_{1},d\omega_{2}).

(2.3)

•

Let
\mu
be a
\sigma
-finite measure on
(\Omega_{1},{\cal F}_{1})
, and
\nu,\nu^{\#}
be finite kernels from
(\Omega_{1},{\cal F}_{1})
to
(\Omega_{2},{\cal F}_{2})
, which satisfy
\nu(\omega_{1},\cdot)=\nu(\omega_{1},\Omega_{2})\nu^{\#}(\omega_{1},\cdot)
. Let
P_{1}
be the projection
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\mapsto\omega_{1}
. Then

\mu{\otimes}\nu=P_{1}(\mu{\otimes}\nu){\otimes}\nu^{\#}.

(2.4)

•

An
\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}
-valued random element
(X,Y)
satisfies that the conditional law of
Y
given
X
is
\nu(X,\cdot)
iff the law
\mu
of
(X,Y)
satisfies

\mu=P_{1}(\mu)(dx)\otimes\nu(x,dy).

(2.5)

2.2
Schramm-Loewner evolution

We focus on chordal SLE, which is first defined using chordal Loewner equation. Below we provide a brief review (cf.
[
8
]
). Let
W\in C([0,T);\mathbb{R})
for some
T\in(0,\infty]
. The chordal Loewner equation driven by
W
is

\partial_{t}g_{t}(z)=\frac{2}{g_{t}(z)-W_{t}},\quad g_{0}(z)=z.

(2.6)

For every
z\in\mathbb{C}
, there is
\tau(z)\in[0,T]
such that
[0,\tau(z))
is the biggest interval on which
t\mapsto g_{t}(z)
is defined. Let
K_{t}=\{z\in\mathbb{H}:\tau(z)\leq t\}
,
0\leq t<T
, where
\mathbb{H}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\Imm z>0\}
. The
g_{t}
and
K_{t}
are respectively called chordal Loewner maps and hulls driven by
W
. It turns out that, for each
t
,
g_{t}
maps
\mathbb{H}\setminus K_{t}
conformally onto
\mathbb{H}
, and fixes
\infty
.

If we differentiate (
2.6
) w.r.t.
z
, then we get

\partial_{t}g_{t}^{\prime}(z)=\frac{-2g_{t}^{\prime}(z)}{g_{t}(z)-W_{t}},\quad g_{0}^{\prime}(z)=1.

(2.7)

If the map
(t,z)\mapsto g_{t}^{-1}(z)
extends continuously from
[0,T)\times\mathbb{H}
to
[0,T)\times\overline{\mathbb{H}}
, then the continuous curve
\eta(t):=g_{t}^{-1}(W_{t})
,
0\leq t<T
, in
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
, is called the chordal Loewner curve driven by
W
. Such
\eta
may not exist in general. When it exists, then for any
t\in[0,T)
,
\mathbb{H}\setminus K_{t}
is the unbounded component of
\mathbb{H}\setminus\eta[0,t]
.

Let
W_{t}=\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t}
, where
\kappa>0
and
B_{t}
is a standard Brownian motion. Then the chordal Loewner curve
\eta
driven by
W
is known to exist (
[
13
]
), and is called a chordal SLE
κ
in
\mathbb{H}
from
0
to
\infty
. If
D
is a simply connected domain with two distinct prime ends
a,b
, we may find a conformal map
f
from
\mathbb{H}
onto
D
, which sends
0,\infty
to
a,b
. Then
f\circ\eta
modulo time parametrization (due to the non-uniqueness of
f
) is called a chordal SLE
κ
in
D
from
a
to
b
. Such
f\circ\eta
lies in the prime end closure of
D
, and when
\partial D
is locally connected, lies in
\overline{D}
.

An SLE
κ
curve is simple if
\kappa\in(0,4]
; space-filling if
\kappa\geq 8
; non-simple and non-space-filling if
\kappa\in(4,8)
. For the rest of the paper we assume
\kappa\in(0,8)
. The following proposition follows immediately from
[
1
, Lemma 3.6]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
D^{\prime}\subset D
be two simply connected domains with locally connected boundary that share two distinct prime ends
a,b
. Let
\mathbb{P}
be the law of chordal SLE
κ
in
D
from
a
to
b
. Then
\mathbb{P}[\eta\subset\overline{D^{\prime}}]>0
.

2.3
Multiple SLE

Let
D
be a simply connected domain with locally connected boundary and
2N
distinct prime ends
a_{1},b_{1},\dots,a_{N},b_{N}
. An
N
-tuple
(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{N})
of random curves in
\overline{D}
is called a multiple
N
-SLE
κ
in
D
associated with
\alpha:=((a_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(a_{N},b_{N}))
if, for any fixed
r\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, the conditional law of
\eta_{r}
given all
\eta_{j}
,
j\neq r
, is an SLE
κ
curve from
a_{r}
to
b_{r}
in the connected component of
D\setminus\bigcup_{j\neq r}\eta_{j}
which contains neighborhoods of
a_{r},b_{r}
in
D
. A necessary condition for the existence of these curves is that there exist
N
mutually disjoint simple curves
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{N}
in
D
such that
\gamma_{j}
connects
a_{j}
with
b_{j}
, in which case
\alpha
is called a link pattern.

By conformal invariance of SLE, one may assume that
\partial D
is analytic at the points in
\alpha
. In the case
\kappa\leq 4
, the
N
-SLE
κ
associated with any link pattern is known to exist (cf.
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
12
]
). The important idea in
[
6
]
is that it is useful to view SLE
κ
as a non-probability measure, which is the SLE
κ
law (probability measure) multiplied by its partition function.

Let
\mu^{D}_{(a,b)}
denote the chordal SLE
κ
law in
D
from
a
to
b
.
Assume that
\partial D
is analytic at
a,b
. The boundary Poisson kernel in
D
at
(a,b)
is defined by

P^{D}_{(a,b)}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\pi}{\varepsilon^{2}}G_{D}(a+\varepsilon{\bf n}_{a},b+\varepsilon{\bf n}_{b}),

where
G_{D}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Dirichlet Green’s function for
D
, and
{\bf n}_{a},{\bf n}_{b}
are inward normals.

Define two constants depending on
\kappa
:

\bb=\frac{6-\kappa}{2\kappa},\qquad\cc=\frac{(6-\kappa)(3\kappa-8)}{2\kappa},

(2.8)

which are respectively called the boundary scaling exponent and central charge of SLE
κ
.
The
H^{D}_{(a,b)}:=(P^{D}_{(a,b)})^{\bb}
and
Q^{D}_{(a,b)}:=H^{D}_{(a,b)}\mu^{D}_{(a,b)}
are respectively called the SLE
κ
partition function and SLE
κ
measure for
(D;a,b)
.
The partition function satisfies the conformal covariance: if
f
is a conformal map on
D
that is extended in neighborhoods of
a,b
, then

H^{D}_{(a,b)}=|f^{\prime}(a)|^{\bb}|f^{\prime}(b)|^{\bb}H^{f(D)}_{(f(a),f(b))}.

(2.9)

When
D=\mathbb{H}
, we have the special value
H^{\mathbb{H}}_{(a,b)}=|a-b|^{-2\bb}
. If
\kappa\leq 6
, then
\bb\geq 0
, and
H^{D}_{(a,b)}
satisfies the monotonicity: if
D^{\prime}\subset D
contains neighborhoods of
a,b
in
D
, then
H^{D^{\prime}}_{(a,b)}\leq H^{D}_{(a,b)}
. The direction of the inequality is reversed if
\kappa>6
.

Let
\alpha=((a_{1},b_{1}),\dots,(a_{N},b_{N}))
be a link pattern in
D
. Assume that
\partial D
is analytic at points in
\alpha
. Let
\kappa\in(0,4]
. The
N
-SLE
κ
measure in
D
with link pattern
\alpha
is defined as a measure
Q^{D}_{\alpha}
defined by

Q^{D}_{\alpha}(d\underline{\eta})=Q^{D}_{\alpha}(d(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{N}))=Y(\underline{\eta})\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{N}Q^{D}_{(a_{j},b_{j})}(d\eta_{j}),

(2.10)

where

Y(\underline{\eta})=I(\underline{\eta})\exp\Big(\frac{\cc}{2}\sum_{j=2}^{N}m_{D}(K_{j}(\underline{\eta}))\Big),

I(\underline{\eta})
is the indicator of the event that
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{N}
are mutually disjoint,
\cc
is given by (
2.8
),
m_{D}
is the Brownian loop measure in
D
(cf.
[
9
]
), and
K_{j}(\underline{\eta})
is the collection of loops that intersect at least
j
of the curves
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{N}
. Such
Q^{D}_{\alpha}
is a finite measure, and its normalization is an
N
-SLE
κ
law in
D
with link pattern
\alpha
.

The uniqueness of multiple SLE
κ
for
\kappa\leq 4
is established in
[
1
]
, which also proves the existence and uniqueness of multiple SLE
16/3
using the convergence of FK Ising model. The same argument works for multiple SLE
6
as scaling limit of critical percolation. The work also studies multiple SLE
κ
for other
\kappa\in(4,6]
and proves the existence and uniqueness based on a conjecture about the convergence of some random cluster model. When there are only
2
curves, the existence and uniqueness of
2
-SLE
κ
hold for all
\kappa\in(0,8)
(cf.
[
15
,
11
]
).

3
Existence

The main goal of the paper is to prove the existence and uniqueness of multiple SLE
κ
associated with any given link pattern for
\kappa\in(4,8)
, in which case the curves are nonsimple and have positive probability to intersect. We do not consider the case
\kappa\geq 8
because in that case the existence is trivial and the uniqueness does not hold.

If one uses (
2.10
) to construct
Q^{D}_{\alpha}
for
\kappa\in(4,8)
, then by
[
1
, Lemma 2.3]
its normalization is not the law of multiple SLE
κ
but the law of
(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{N})
with the properties that, every
\eta_{r}
conditional on all other
\eta_{j}
’s is an SLE
κ
curve from
a_{r}
to
b_{r}
in some complement domain of the given curves
conditioned to avoid these curves
.

Fortunately,
[
6
, Proposition 3.2]
provides an cascade relation of
Q^{D}_{\alpha}
, which could be used for the construction.
It says that the marginal measure on
(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{N-1})
in
Q^{D}_{\alpha}
is absolutely continuous w.r.t.
Q^{D}_{\alpha^{\prime}}
,
\alpha^{\prime}
being the
\alpha
without the link
(a_{N},b_{N})
, with Radon-Nikodym derivative
H^{D^{\prime}}_{(a_{N},b_{N})}
, where
D^{\prime}
is the connected component of
D\setminus\cup_{k=1}^{N-1}\eta_{k}
whose boundary contains
a_{N},b_{N}
. Since the
\eta_{N}
in
Q^{U}_{\alpha}
conditionally on
(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{N-1})
has the law of
\mu^{D^{\prime}}_{(a_{N},b_{N})}
, we may obtain
Q^{D}_{\alpha}
by first weighting
Q^{D}_{\alpha^{\prime}}
by
H^{D^{\prime}}_{(a_{N},b_{N})}
and then sampling
\eta_{N}
according to
\mu^{D^{\prime}}_{(a_{N},b_{N})}
.

We will follow the above approach to construct
Q^{D}_{\alpha}
for
\kappa\in(4,8)
inductively on
N
.
It is useful to construct multiple SLE in possibly disconnected open sets.
Let
\cal U
denote the collection of open subsets of
\mathbb{C}
.
For
U\in\cal U
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\widehat{\LP}_{N}^{U}
denote the set of tuples

of

, such that each of the
2N

U
, which is simply connected with locally connected boundary and whose boundary is analytic at this point. We write
\alpha_{j}
for the couple
(a_{j},b_{j})
or the set
\{a_{j},b_{j}\}
. If there exist
N
mutually disjoint simple curves
\gamma_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq N
, in
U
, such that each
\gamma_{j}
connects points of
\alpha_{j}
, then
\alpha
is called a link pattern of
U
of size

. Let
\LP^{U}_{N}
denote the collection of all such

for any

on the space

, where

is the space of unparametrized curves from

to

.

If
\alpha=(a,b)\in\LP^{U}_{1}
, there exists a unique connected simply connected component

. Then we define

,

Using the above convention and (

Q^{U}_{\alpha}=Q^{U}_{P_{\widehat{N}}(\alpha)}(d(\eta_{1},\dots\eta_{N-1})){\otimes}Q^{U\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{N-1}\eta_{j}}_{\alpha_{N}}(d\eta_{N}),

where

We introduce some symbols. Let

.

For any

,

.

-finite measure

Let
\alpha^{k}=P_{\leq k}(\alpha)
,

. Then

, and

.

.

Proof.

, then there are

-a.s.
Q^{U\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{k-1}\eta_{j}}_{\alpha_{k}}=0
. By (
3.4
) we get
0=Q^{U}_{\alpha^{k}}=Q^{U}_{\alpha^{k+1}}=\cdots=Q^{U}_{\alpha^{N}}=Q^{U}_{\alpha}
.

Suppose
\alpha\in\LP^{U}_{N}
.
We call
T
a tube in
U
connecting
\alpha_{j}
if it is the closure of a Jordan subdomain of one component
D_{j}
of
U
, whose boundary is a union of the closure of two crosscuts of
D_{j}
and two disjoint open boundary arcs, which respectively contain points of
\alpha_{j}
. Since
\alpha\in\LP^{U}_{N}
, there exist mutually disjoint tubes
T_{j}
in
U
connecting
\alpha_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq N
. Let
{\cal T}_{j}=\{\eta\in X^{U}_{j}:\eta\subset T_{j}\}
. By Proposition
2.1
,
Q^{U}_{\alpha^{1}}({\cal T}_{1})>0
and for any
2\leq k\leq N
, when
(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k-1})\in\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}{\cal T}_{j}
,
Q^{U\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{k-1}\eta_{j}}_{\alpha_{k}}({\cal T}_{k})>0
. Thus, by (
3.1
) and induction,
Q^{U}_{\alpha^{k}}(\prod_{j=1}^{k}{\cal T}_{j})>0
for
1\leq k\leq N
, which means that
Q^{U}_{\alpha}=Q^{U}_{\alpha^{N}}
is not trivial.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

If
\kappa\leq 6
, then
Q^{U}_{\alpha}
is finite.

Proof.

By (
3.1
) and the monotonicity of
H^{D}_{(a,b)}
when
\kappa\leq 6
,

|Q^{U}_{\alpha^{k}}|\leq\int Q^{U}_{\alpha^{k-1}}(d(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k-1}))|Q^{U}_{(a_{k},b_{k})}|=|Q^{U}_{\alpha^{k-1}}|H^{U}_{(a_{k},b_{k})},\quad 2\leq k\leq N,

which implies that
|Q^{U}_{\alpha}|\leq\prod_{k=1}^{N}H^{U}_{(a_{k},b_{k})}<\infty
.
∎

The following lemma follows from Wu’s work
[
15
]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
\alpha\in\widehat{\LP}^{U}_{2}
,
Q^{U}_{\alpha}
is finite, and

\sigma(Q^{U}_{\alpha})=Q^{U}_{\sigma(\alpha)}
for
\sigma=(1,2)\in S_{2}
.

Proof.

If
\alpha\not\in\LP^{U}_{2}
, then
\sigma(\alpha)\not\in\LP^{U}_{2}
. By Lemma
3.3
,
Q^{U}_{\alpha}=Q^{U}_{\sigma(\alpha)}=0
.
Suppose now
\alpha\in\LP^{U}_{2}
. For
j=1,2
, there is a simply connected component
D_{j}
of
U
whose boundary contains
\alpha_{j}
. Then
Q^{U}_{\alpha_{1}}=Q^{D_{1}}_{\alpha_{1}}
. If
D_{1}\neq D_{2}
, then for
Q^{D_{1}}_{\alpha_{1}}
-a.s. every
\eta_{1}
,
Q^{U\setminus\eta_{1}}_{\alpha_{2}}=Q^{D_{2}}_{\alpha_{2}}
. Thus, by (
3.1
),
Q^{U}_{\alpha}=Q^{D_{1}}_{\alpha_{1}}\times Q^{D_{2}}_{\alpha_{2}}
. Similarly,
Q^{U}_{\sigma(\alpha)}=Q^{D_{2}}_{\alpha_{2}}\times Q^{D_{1}}_{\alpha_{1}}
. So
\sigma(Q^{U}_{\alpha})=Q^{U}_{\sigma(\alpha)}
.

Now consider the case
D_{1}=D_{2}
. We may assume
U=D_{1}
since other components of
U
are irrelevant. By reversibility of chordal SLE
κ
and the fact that
H^{U}_{(a,b)}=H^{U}_{(b,a)}
, we may relabeling the points of
\alpha_{j}=\{a_{j},b_{j}\}
,
j=1,2
, such that
a_{1},b_{1},b_{2},a_{2}
are ordered clockwise or counterclockwise on
\partial U
. Let
H^{U}_{\alpha}=|Q^{U}_{\alpha}|
.
By (
3.1
) and (
2.1
),

H^{U}_{\alpha}=H^{U}_{\alpha_{1}}\int\mu^{U}_{\alpha_{1}}(d\eta_{1})H^{U\setminus\eta_{1}}_{\alpha_{2}}.

(3.5)

Let
f
be a conformal map from
U
onto
\mathbb{H}
such that
f(a_{1})=0
and
f(b_{1})=\infty
. Let
x=f(a_{2})
and
y=f(b_{2})
. By conformal covariance,

\int\mu^{U}_{\alpha_{1}}(d\eta_{1})H^{U\setminus\eta_{1}}_{\alpha_{2}}=|f^{\prime}(a_{2})|^{\bb}|f^{\prime}(b_{2})|^{\bb}\int\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{(0,\infty)}(d\gamma)H^{\mathbb{H}\setminus\gamma}_{(x,y)}.

(3.6)

Now
r:=x/y\in(0,1)
is the cross ratio of
\alpha
in
U
.
By
[
15
, Lemma 3.5 and Proposition 3.6]
, if
\gamma
is SLE
κ
in
\mathbb{H}
from
0
to
\infty
, there is an associated uniformly integrable martingale
(M_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfying

M_{0}=|x-y|^{-2\bb}r^{\frac{2}{\kappa}}F(r),\quad M_{\infty}=H^{\mathbb{H}\setminus\gamma}_{(x,y)},

where
F:=_{2}F_{1}(\frac{4}{\kappa},1-\frac{4}{\kappa},\frac{8}{\kappa},\cdot)
is a hypergeometric function. Let
G(x)=x^{\frac{2}{\kappa}}F(x)
. Then

\int\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{(0,\infty)}(d\gamma)H^{\mathbb{H}\setminus\gamma}_{(x,y)}=\mathbb{E}[M_{\infty}]=M_{0}=H^{\mathbb{H}}_{(x,y)}G(r),

which combined with (
3.5
,
3.6
) and the conformal covariance of
H^{U}_{\alpha_{j}}
,
j=1,2
, implies that

H^{U}_{\alpha}=G(r)H^{U}_{\alpha_{1}}H^{U}_{\alpha_{2}}\in(0,\infty)
. Thus,
Q^{U}_{\alpha}
is finite.
Since the cross ratio of
\alpha
and
\sigma(\alpha)
in
U
agree, we get
H^{U}_{\alpha}=H^{U}_{\sigma(\alpha)}
.

By
[
15
, Proposition 6.10]
, for any simply connected domain
D
and
\beta\in\LP^{D}_{2}
, the
2
-SLE
κ
law associated with
\beta
, denoted by
\mu^{D}_{\beta}
, exists uniquely and satisfies conformal invariance. This immediately implies that
\sigma(\mu^{U}_{\alpha})=\mu^{U}_{\sigma(\alpha)}
and
\mu^{U}_{\alpha}=f^{-1}(\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{((0,\infty),(x,y))})
.
That proposition and
[
15
, Proposition 3.6]
together imply that the first marginal of
\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{((0,\infty),(x,y))}
is absolutely continuous w.r.t.
\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{(0,\infty)}
with Radon-Nikodym derivative
M_{\infty}/M_{0}=H^{\mathbb{H}\setminus\gamma}_{(x,y)}/(H^{\mathbb{H}}_{(x,y)}G(r))
. Thus, by (
2.5
),

\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{((0,\infty),(x,y))}(d(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}))=H^{\mathbb{H}\setminus\gamma_{1}}_{(x,y)}/(H^{\mathbb{H}}_{(x,y)}G(r))\cdot\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{(0,\infty)}(d\gamma_{1}){\otimes}\mu^{\mathbb{H}\setminus\gamma_{1}}_{(x,y)}(d\gamma_{2}).

By this formula and the conformal invariance of SLE
κ
and
2
-SLE
κ
,

\mu^{U}_{\alpha}(d(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}))=H^{\mathbb{H}\setminus f(\eta_{1})}_{(x,y)}/(H^{\mathbb{H}}_{(x,y)}G(r))\cdot\mu^{U}_{\alpha_{1}}(d\eta_{1}){\otimes}\mu^{U\setminus\eta_{1}}_{\alpha_{2}}(d\eta_{2}).

By the construction of
Q^{U}_{\alpha}
and (
2.3
),

Q^{U}_{\alpha}(d(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}))=H^{U}_{\alpha_{1}}H^{U\setminus\eta_{1}}_{\alpha_{2}}\cdot\mu^{U}_{\alpha_{1}}(d\eta_{1}){\otimes}\mu^{U\setminus\eta_{1}}_{\alpha_{2}}(d\eta_{2}).

Comparing the above two displayed formulas and using
H^{U\setminus\eta_{1}}_{\alpha_{2}}/{H^{\mathbb{H}\setminus f(\eta_{1})}_{(x,y)}}=H^{U}_{\alpha_{2}}/H^{\mathbb{H}}_{(x,y)}
and
H^{U}_{\alpha}=G(r)H^{U}_{\alpha_{1}}H^{U}_{\alpha_{2}}
, we get
Q^{U}_{\alpha}=H^{U}_{\alpha}\mu^{U}_{\alpha}
, which together with
H^{U}_{\alpha}=H^{U}_{\sigma(\alpha)}
and
\sigma(\mu^{U}_{\alpha})=\mu^{U}_{\sigma(\alpha)}
implies
\sigma(Q^{U}_{\alpha})=Q^{U}_{\sigma(\alpha)}
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

For any
\alpha\in\widehat{\LP}^{U}_{N}
and
\sigma\in S_{N}
,
\sigma(Q^{U}_{\alpha})=Q^{U}_{\sigma(\alpha)}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.5
, the statement holds for
N=1,2
. Suppose
N\geq 3
.
By (
3.4
),

Q^{U}_{\alpha}=Q^{U}_{\alpha^{N-2}}(d(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{N-2})){\otimes}Q^{U\setminus\bigcup_{j=1}^{N-2}\eta_{j}}_{(\alpha_{N-1},\alpha_{N})}(d(\eta_{N-1},\eta_{N})).

By Lemma
3.5
, for
\sigma_{N}:=(N-1,N)\in S_{N}
,
\sigma_{N}(Q^{U}_{\alpha})=Q^{U}_{\sigma_{N}(\alpha)}
. This result applied to
\alpha^{k}
and
\sigma_{k}=(k-1,k)\in S_{k}
together with Lemma
3.5
implies that
\sigma_{k}(Q^{U}_{\alpha^{k}})=Q^{U}_{\sigma_{k}(\alpha^{k})}
,
2\leq k\leq N
. We view
\sigma_{k}
as an element of
S_{m}
for any
k\leq m\leq N
. By (
3.4
) and induction, we get
\sigma_{k}(Q^{U}_{\alpha^{m}})=Q^{U}_{\sigma_{k}(\alpha^{m})}
for
k\leq m\leq N
. Taking
m=N
, we get
\sigma_{k}(Q^{U}_{\alpha})=Q^{U}_{\sigma_{k}(\alpha)}
. The proof now finishes since
\{\sigma_{k}:2\leq k\leq N\}
generates the group
S_{N}
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

For
\alpha\in\LP^{U}_{N}
,
Q^{U}_{\alpha}
is an
N
-SLE
κ
measure in
U
with link pattern
\alpha
.

Proof.

Fix
j\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
. We need to show that,

Q^{U}_{\alpha}=\sigma_{[j,N]}(P_{\widehat{j}}(Q^{U}_{\alpha})(d(\eta_{1},\dots,\widehat{\eta}_{j},\dots,\eta_{N}))){\otimes}\mu^{U\setminus\bigcup_{k\neq j}\eta_{k}}_{\alpha_{j}}(d\eta_{j}).

(3.7)

That (
3.7
) holds for
j=N
follows from (
3.1
) and (
2.4
). By Lemma
3.6
,
{\sigma}_{[j,N]}^{-1}(Q^{U}_{\alpha})=Q^{U}_{\sigma_{[j,N]}^{-1}(\alpha)}
. Since
\sigma_{[j,N]}^{-1}(\alpha)=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{j-1},\alpha_{j+1},\dots,\alpha_{N},\alpha_{j})
and (
3.7
) holds for
j=N
,

{\sigma}_{[j,N]}^{-1}(Q^{U}_{\alpha})=P_{\widehat{N}}({\sigma}_{[j,N]}^{-1}(Q^{U}_{\alpha}))(d(\eta_{1},\dots,\widehat{\eta}_{j},\dots,\eta_{N})){\otimes}\mu^{U\setminus\bigcup_{k\neq j}\eta_{k}}_{\alpha_{j}}(d\eta_{j})

=P_{\widehat{j}}(Q^{U}_{\alpha})(d(\eta_{1},\dots,\widehat{\eta}_{j},\dots,\eta_{N})){\otimes}\mu^{U\setminus\bigcup_{k\neq j}\eta_{k}}_{\alpha_{j}}(d\eta_{j}),

where the last “
=
” follows from (
3.2
).
This immediately implies (

).
∎

Combining Lemmas

,
3.3
and

, we obtain the existence theorem.

Theorem 3.8

.

Let
\kappa\in(0,6]
. Then for any
\alpha\in\LP^{U}_{N}
,
Q^{U}_{\alpha}/|Q^{U}_{\alpha}|
is an
N
-SLE
κ
law in
U
with link pattern
\alpha
.

Apparently, the existence statement holds for any

4
Uniqueness

In this section we prove the uniqueness of
N
-SLE
κ
law in a simply connected domain with any given link pattern.
We will use the Markov chain theory in
[
10
]
.
By Proposition 10.1.1 and Theorem 10.4.9 of the book, if a Markov chain
\Phi
is
irreducible
and admits an invariant probability measure
\mu
, then all (
\sigma
-finite) invariant measures of
\Phi
are some constant times
\mu
. By Section 4.2.1 of the book,
\Phi
is called (
\phi
-)irreducible if there is a nontrivial
\sigma
-finite measure
\phi
such that if
A
satisfies
\phi(A)>0
, then for any
x
in the space,
L(x,A)>0
, which is equivalent to
\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}P^{m}(x,A)>0
. Such
\phi
is called an irreducibility measure for
\Phi
.

Fix a simply connected domain
D
with locally connected boundary and
\alpha\in\LP^{D}_{N}
. For
j\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
, let
X_{j}
be the space of unparametrized curves in
\overline{D}
which connect points of
\alpha_{j}
. Let
\underline{X}
be the space of
(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{N})\in\prod_{j=1}^{N}X_{j}
such that for any
j\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
,
\alpha_{j}\in\LP^{D\setminus\bigcup_{k\neq j}\eta_{k}}_{1}
. For any
\underline{A}\subset\underline{X}
,
\underline{\eta}\in\underline{X}
, and
j\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
, let
\pi^{\underline{\eta}}_{j}(\underline{A})
denote the set of
\gamma\in X_{j}
such that if the
j
-th component of
\underline{\eta}
were replaced by
\gamma
, then the modified
\underline{\eta}
would lie in
\underline{A}
.

Define a Markov chain
\Phi
on
\underline{X}
, whose transition kernel is
P=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}P_{j}
, where

P_{j}(\underline{\eta},\underline{A})=P_{j}((\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{N}),\underline{A})=\mu^{D\setminus\bigcup_{k\neq{j}}\eta_{k}}_{\alpha_{j}}(\pi^{\underline{\eta}}_{j}(\underline{A})).

In plain words, given
\Phi_{0}=\underline{\eta}^{0}
, the next step
\Phi_{1}=\underline{\eta}^{1}
is given by
\eta^{1}_{k}=\eta^{0}_{k}
for
k\neq\bf j
and
\eta^{1}_{\bf j}=\gamma
, where
\bf j
is chosen from
\mathbb{N}_{N}
uniformly randomly, and
\gamma
is sampled according to the SLE
κ
law
\mu^{D\setminus\bigcup_{k\neq{\bf j}}\eta_{k}^{0}}_{\alpha_{\bf j}}
. It is obvious that if
\mu
is an
N
-SLE
κ
measure in
D
, then it is also an invariant measure for
\Phi
.

Lemma 4.1
.

\Phi
is irreducible.

Proof.

We use the notion of tubes in the proof of Lemma
3.3
, and find mutually disjoint tubes
{T}_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
, in
D
such that
T_{j}
connects
\alpha_{j}
. Let
{\cal T}_{j}=\{\eta\in X_{j}:\eta\subset T_{j}\}
, and
\phi_{j}=\mathbbm{1}_{{{T}_{j}}}\mu^{D}_{\alpha_{j}}
, i.e., the law of the chordal SLE
κ
in
D
connecting
\alpha_{j}
restricted to the event that the curve stays in
{T}_{j}
. By Proposition
2.1
, each
\phi_{j}
is a nontrivial finite measure on
X_{j}
. Let
\phi=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{j}
. We are going to show that
\phi
is an irreducibility measure for
\Phi
.

Suppose
\underline{\cal A}\subset\underline{X}
satisfies that
\phi(\underline{\cal A})>0
. Let
\underline{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{N})\in\underline{X}
. We need to show that
P^{m}(\underline{\eta},\underline{\cal A})>0
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
. It suffices to construct measurable
\underline{\cal A}^{0},\dots,\underline{\cal A}^{m}\subset\underline{X}
with
\underline{\cal A}^{0}=\{\underline{\eta}\}
and
\underline{\cal A}^{m}=\underline{\cal A}
such that for any
\underline{\gamma}\in\underline{\cal A}^{s-1}
,
1\leq s\leq m
,
P(\underline{\gamma},\underline{\cal A}^{s})>0
, which holds if
there is
j_{s}\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
such that
P_{j_{s}}(\underline{\gamma},\underline{\cal A}^{s})>0
.

First, suppose
\underline{\cal A}=\underline{\cal T}:=\prod_{j=1}^{N}{{\cal T}_{j}}
. By reordering the components of
\alpha
and using Lemma
3.6
, we may assume that for any
1\leq j\leq N
, there is an open boundary arc
I_{j}
of
D
with endpoints
\alpha_{j}
such that
{\cal I}_{j}:=\{k\in\mathbb{N}_{N}:\alpha_{k}\subset I_{j}\}\subset\mathbb{N}_{j-1}
. We have the following fact.

(F)

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
. Let
A,B
be closed subset of
\overline{D}
such that
A\cup{I_{r}}
is connected,
A\cap({\partial D\setminus I_{r}})=\emptyset
,
B\cup({\partial D\setminus I_{r}})
is connected,
B\cap\overline{I_{r}}=\emptyset
, and
A\cap B=\emptyset
. Then there is a tube connecting
\alpha_{r}
in
D
, which is disjoint from
A\cup B
.

Applying (F) to
r=1
,
A=\emptyset
and
B=\bigcup_{j=2}^{N}(\eta_{j}\cup T_{j})
, we construct a tube
S_{1}
connecting
\alpha_{1}
, which is disjoint from all
\eta_{j},T_{j}
,
2\leq j\leq N
. Suppose for some
m\leq N-1
, we have constructed mutually disjoint tubes
S_{j}
connecting
\alpha_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq m
, which are disjoint from all
\eta_{k},T_{k}
for
k\geq m+1
. Applying (F) to
r=m+1
,
A=\bigcup_{j\in{\cal I}_{m+1}}S_{j}
and
B=\bigcup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{m}\setminus{\cal I}_{m+1}}S_{j}\cup\bigcup_{j=m+2}^{N}(\eta_{j}\cup T_{j})
, we construct a tube
S_{m+1}
connecting
\alpha_{m+1}
, which is disjoint from
S_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq m
and
\alpha_{j}\cup T_{j}
for
m+2\leq j\leq N
. By induction we get mutually disjoint tubes
S_{j}
connecting
\alpha_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq N
, such that
S_{j}
is disjoint from
\eta_{k}
and
T_{k}
whenever
j<k
. Let
{\cal S}_{j}=\{\eta\in X_{j}:\eta\subset S_{j}\}
. For
1\leq s\leq N
, we define
\underline{\cal A}^{s}=\prod_{j=1}^{s}{\cal S}_{j}\times\prod_{j={s+1}}^{N}\{\eta_{j}\}
. Note that
\underline{\cal A}^{0}=\{\underline{\eta}\}
and
\underline{\cal A}^{N}=\prod_{j=1}^{N}{\cal S}_{j}=:\underline{\cal S}
. Let
s\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
and
\underline{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{N})\in{\cal A}^{s-1}
. Then
\gamma_{j}\subset S_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq s
and
\gamma_{j}=\eta_{j}
for
s+1\leq j\leq N
. Since
S_{s}
is disjoint from
S_{j}
,
j<s
, and
\eta_{j}
,
j>s
, it is a tube in
D\setminus\bigcup_{j\neq s}\gamma_{j}
connecting
\alpha_{j}
. Thus, by Proposition
2.1
,

P_{s}(\underline{\gamma},\underline{\cal A}^{s})=\mu^{D\setminus\bigcup_{j\neq s}\gamma_{j}}_{\alpha_{s}}(\pi^{\underline{\gamma}}_{s}(\underline{\cal A}^{s}))=\mu^{D\setminus\bigcup_{j\neq s}\gamma_{j}}_{\alpha_{s}}({\cal S}_{s})>0
.

For
1\leq r\leq N
, we let
\underline{\cal A}^{N+r}=\prod_{j=1}^{N-r}{\cal S}_{j}\times\prod_{j=N+1-r}^{N}{\cal T}_{j}
. Note that
\underline{\cal A}^{2N}=\underline{\cal T}
. Let
r\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
and
\underline{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{N})\in\underline{\cal A}^{N+r-1}
. Since
T_{N+1-r}
is disjoint from
T_{j}
, which contains
\gamma_{j}
, for
j>N+1-r
and
S_{j}
, which also contains
\gamma_{j}
, for
j<N+1-r
, it is a tube in
D\setminus\bigcup_{j\neq s}\gamma_{j}
connecting
\alpha_{N+1-r}
. Thus, by Proposition
2.1
,

P_{N+1-r}(\underline{\gamma},\underline{\cal A}^{N+r})=\mu^{D\setminus\bigcup_{j\neq N+1-r}\gamma_{j}}_{\alpha_{N+1-r}}(\pi^{\underline{\gamma}}_{N+1-r}(\underline{\cal A}^{N+r}))=\mu^{D\setminus\bigcup_{j\neq N+1-r}\gamma_{j}}_{\alpha_{N+1-r}}({\cal T}_{N+1-r})>0.

So the sequence
\underline{\cal A}^{s}
,
0\leq s\leq 2N
, satisfy the desired properties for
\underline{\cal A}=\underline{\cal T}
.

Now we consider the general case. Since
\phi
is supported by
\underline{\cal T}
, we may assume
\underline{\cal A}\subset\underline{\cal T}
. It suffices to further construct
\underline{\cal A}^{2N+t}
,
1\leq t\leq N
, with
\underline{\cal A}^{3N}=\underline{\cal A}
, such that for any
\underline{\gamma}\in\underline{\cal A}^{2N+t-1}
,
1\leq t\leq N
,
P_{t}(\underline{\gamma},\underline{\cal A}^{2N+t})>0
. We define these
\underline{\cal A}^{2N+t}
’s backward inductively such that
\underline{\cal A}^{3N}=\underline{\cal A}
and

\underline{\cal A}^{2N+t-1}=\{\underline{\gamma}\in\underline{\cal T}:\phi_{t}(\pi^{\underline{\gamma}}_{t}(\underline{\cal A}^{2N+t}))>0\}

(4.1)

for

2\leq t\leq N
. Since
\phi=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\phi_{j}
and
\phi(\underline{\cal A}^{3N})>0
, by Fubini theorem and backward induction, each
\underline{\cal A}^{2N+t}
has the form
{\cal C}_{t}\times\prod_{j=t+1}^{N}{\cal T}_{t}
for some
{\cal C}_{t}\subset\prod_{j=1}^{t}{\cal T}_{j}
with
\prod_{j=1}^{t}\phi_{j}({\cal C}_{t})>0
. From
\underline{\cal A}^{2N+1}={\cal C}_{1}\times\prod_{j=2}^{N}{\cal T}_{j}
and
\phi_{1}({\cal C})>0
we see that (
4.1
) also holds for
t=1
.

Let
1\leq t\leq N
and
\underline{\gamma}\in\underline{\cal A}^{2N+t-1}
. By (
4.1
),
\mu^{D}_{\alpha_{t}}(\pi^{\underline{\gamma}}_{t}(\underline{\cal A}^{2N+t}))=\phi_{t}(\pi^{\underline{\gamma}}_{t}(\underline{\cal A}^{2N+t}))>0
. Since
T_{t}
is a tube in
D\setminus\cup_{j\neq t}\gamma_{j}
connecting
\alpha_{t}
, by Proposition
2.1
,

P_{t}(\underline{\gamma},\underline{\cal A}^{2N+t})=\mu^{D\setminus\bigcup_{j\neq t}\gamma_{j}}_{\alpha_{t}}(\pi^{\underline{\gamma}}_{t}(\underline{\cal A}^{2N+t}))=\mu^{D_{t}(\underline{\gamma})}_{\alpha_{t}}(\pi^{\underline{\gamma}}_{t}(\underline{\cal A}^{2N+t}))>0.

Thus,
\underline{\cal A}^{s}
,
0\leq s\leq 3N
, satisfy the desired properties for the general
\underline{\cal A}
.
∎

The irreducible Markov chain theory then implies the uniqueness theorem.

Theorem 4.2

(Uniqueness)
.

Let
\kappa\in(0,8)
. Let
D
be a simply connected domain with locally connected boundary and
\alpha\in\LP^{D}_{N}
. If there exists an
N
-SLE
κ
law
\mu
in
D
with link pattern
\alpha
, then
Q^{D}_{\alpha}
is finite, and its normalization
Q^{D}_{\alpha}/|Q^{D}_{\alpha}|
equals
\mu
.

5
Partition function

For
\alpha\in\LP^{U}_{N}
, we call
H^{U}_{\alpha}:=|Q^{U}_{\alpha}|
a multiple SLE
κ
partition function. When
N=1,2
, the function is finite and has a closed formula. For the rest of the section we assume
\kappa\leq 6
, in which case
H^{U}_{\alpha}
is known to be finite by Lemma
3.4
. By (
2.9
,
3.1
),
H^{U}_{\alpha}
satisfies the following conformal covariance. Suppose
f
is a conformal map on
U
, which extends to neighborhoods of points in

\alpha
. Then

H^{U}_{\alpha}=\prod_{x\in\alpha}|f^{\prime}(x)|^{\bb}H^{f(U)}_{f(\alpha)}.

(5.1)

Thus, it suffices to consider the case that
U=\mathbb{H}
. In that case,
\LP_{N}:=\LP^{\mathbb{H}}_{N}
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
. By a slight abuse of notation, we also call the function
H(\alpha):=H^{\mathbb{H}}_{\alpha}
defined on
\LP_{N}
a multiple (
N
-)SLE
κ
partition function.

Our definition follows the theme of
[
6
]
, where the partition function is defined as the total mass of a measure. It is different from the multiple SLE partition function introduced in
[
4
,
3
]
, in which the function is assumed to satisfy a system of
2N
second-order linear PDE (cf.
[
7
, Formula (1.2)]
), which guarantees the existence of local multiple SLE.

A local multiple SLE
κ
consists of
2N
SLE
κ
-type curves emanating from the points of
\alpha
and stopped before leaving neighborhoods of these points, which satisfy some commutation relation. These curves may or may not be extended to form an
N
-SLE
κ
associated with
\alpha
. Some authors call the usual multiple SLE
global multiple SLE
, and call a partition function
multiple SLE pure partition function
whenever it generates a local multiple SLE that is a part of a global multiple SLE with a fixed link pattern.

By (
5.1
),
H
satisfies the conformal covariance for multiple SLE partition function (cf.
[
7
, Formula (1.3)]
). We will show that
H
satisfies the asymptotics for pure partition function (cf.
[
15
, Formula (1.3)]
) in Lemma
5.5
.

For
N\geq 2
and
j\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
, let
P_{\widehat{j}}
denote the map
\underline{x}\mapsto(x_{1},\dots,\widehat{x_{2j-1}},\widehat{x_{2j}},\dots,x_{2N})
from
\mathbb{R}^{2N}
to
\mathbb{R}^{2(N-1)}
. This is consistent with our definition of
P_{\widehat{j}}:\LP_{N}\to\LP_{N-1}
.

Let
D
be a simply connected domain and
\alpha\in\LP^{D}_{N}
. For two prime ends
a\neq b
of
D
, which are different from points in
\alpha
, we define

I^{D;(a,b)}_{\alpha}=\int\mu^{D}_{\alpha}(d\eta)H^{U\setminus\eta}_{\alpha}.

(5.2)

The conformal covariance of
H^{U}_{\alpha}
implies the following conformal covariance of
I^{D;(a,b)}_{\alpha}
. If
f
satisfies the conditions of the
f
in (
5.1
), then

I^{U;(a,b)}_{\alpha}=\prod_{x\in\alpha}|f^{\prime}(x)|^{\bb}H^{f(U);(f(a),f(b))}_{f(\alpha)}.

(5.3)

By (
3.1
) and Lemma
3.6
, if
N\geq 2
, then for any
j\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
,

H^{D}_{\alpha}=H^{D}_{\alpha_{j}}I^{D;\alpha_{j}}_{P_{\widehat{j}}(\alpha)}.

(5.4)

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
N\geq 2
and
j\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
. Let
\underline{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}
be such that
y_{2j}=y_{2j-1}\neq y_{k}
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}_{2N}\setminus\{2j,2j-1\}
, and
P_{\widehat{j}}(\underline{y})\in\LP_{N-1}
. Then

\lim_{\LP_{N}\ni\underline{x}\to\underline{y}}|x_{2j}-x_{2j-1}|^{2\bb}H(\underline{x})\to H(P_{\widehat{j}}(\underline{y})).

(5.5)

Proof.

By (
5.2
,
5.4
),

|x_{2j}-x_{2j-1}|^{2\bb}H(\underline{x})=\int\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{(x_{2j},x_{2j-1})}H^{\mathbb{H}\setminus\eta}_{P_{\widehat{j}}(\underline{x})}.

(5.6)

As
\underline{x}\to\underline{y}
,
x_{2j},x_{2j-1}\to y_{2j}
, so the SLE
κ
curve in
\mathbb{H}
from
x_{2j}
to
x_{2j-1}
shrinks to the single point
y_{2j}
, which implies that the integrand in (
5.6
) tends to the RHS of (
5.5
). So we get (
5.5
) using dominated convergence theorem and monotonicity of
H
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
H
is continuous on
{\LP}_{N}
.

Proof.

The continuity of
H
when
N=1
is obvious because
H(a,b)=|a-b|^{-2\bb}
. Suppose
H
is continuous on
\LP_{k}
for any
k\leq N
. We now show that
H
is continuous on
{\LP}_{N+1}
.
Suppose
\underline{x}^{n}\to\underline{x}^{0}
in
\LP_{N+1}
. We need to show that
H(\underline{x}^{n})\to H(\underline{x}^{0})
. We may and will assume that
x^{n}_{1}=0
and
x^{n}_{2}=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
by applying (
2.9
) to linear functions.

By (
5.2
,
5.4
),

H(\underline{x}^{n})=\int\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{(0,1)}(d\eta)H^{\mathbb{H}\setminus\eta}_{P_{\widehat{1}}(\underline{x}^{n})},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.

For any fixed
\eta
,
H^{\mathbb{H}\setminus\eta}_{P_{\widehat{1}}(\underline{x}^{n})}
equals the product of the partition functions in each component of
\mathbb{H}\setminus\eta
whose boundary contains points of
\underline{x}^{n}
.
By applying the continuity of
H
on
\LP_{k}
for
k\leq N
and the conformal covariance of
H
to each component of
\mathbb{H}\setminus\eta
, we get

H^{\mathbb{H}\setminus\eta}_{P_{\widehat{1}}(\underline{x}^{n})}\to H^{\mathbb{H}\setminus\eta}_{P_{\widehat{1}}(\underline{x}^{0})}
. By monotonicity,
H^{\mathbb{H}\setminus\eta}_{P_{\widehat{1}}(\underline{x}^{n})}\leq H(P_{\widehat{1}}(\underline{x}^{n}))\to H(P_{\widehat{1}}(\underline{x}^{0}))
. So we get
H(\underline{x}^{n})\to H(\underline{x}^{0})
by dominated convergence theorem. The proof is completed by induction.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\underline{u}=(u_{1},\dots,u_{2N})\in\LP_{N}
,
N\geq 2
. Let
B_{t}
be a standard Brownian motion. Let
\{a,b\}=\{2j_{0}-1,2j_{0}\}
for some
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{N}
. Let
W_{t}
and
V_{t}
solve the system of SDE/ODE

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}dW_{t}=\frac{\kappa-6}{W_{t}-V_{t}}\,dt+\sqrt{\kappa}dB_{t},&W_{0}=u_{a};\\
dV_{t}=\frac{2}{V_{t}-W_{t}}\,dt,&V_{0}=u_{b}.\end{array}\right.

(5.7)

Let
g_{t}
be the chordal Loewner maps driven by
W_{t}
, and let
\tau
be the first time that
g_{t}(u_{j})
is not defined for some
j\in\mathbb{N}_{2N}\setminus\{a\}
. Let
U^{j}_{t}=g_{t}(u_{j})
for
j\neq a
, and
U^{a}_{t}=W_{t}
. Note that
U^{b}_{t}=V_{t}
. Then

M_{t}:=\Big(\prod_{j\not\in\{a,b\}}|g_{t}^{\prime}(u_{j})|\Big)^{\bb}H(W_{t},V_{t})^{-1}H(U^{1}_{t},\dots,U^{2N}_{t}),\quad 0\leq t<\tau,

(5.8)

is a bounded local martingale.

Proof.

If
N=1
,
M_{t}
is constant
1
, and the conclusion is obvious. Suppose
N\geq 2
. By symmetry we may assume that
a=1
and
b=2
. Let
K_{t}
and
\gamma(t)
,
0\leq t<\tau
, be the chordal Loewner hulls and curve driven by
W_{t}
. By SLE coordinate changes (
[
14
]
),
\gamma
is a part of a chordal SLE
κ
curve, say
\eta
, in
\mathbb{H}
from
x_{1}
to
x_{2}
. Let
Z=H^{\mathbb{H}\setminus\eta}_{(u_{3},\dots,u_{2N})}
. Let
T
be any stopping time. Let
\eta_{T}
and
\eta^{T}
be respectively the parts of
\eta
before and after
T
. By DMP of SLE
κ
, conditionally on
{\cal F}_{T}
and the event
E_{T}:=\{T<\tau\}
,
\eta^{T}
is an SLE
κ
in
\mathbb{H}\setminus K_{T}
from
\eta(T)
to
u_{2}
. Since

Z=H^{(\mathbb{H}\setminus\eta_{T})\setminus\eta^{T}}_{(u_{3},\dots,u_{2N})}=H^{(\mathbb{H}\setminus K_{T})\setminus\eta^{T}}_{(u_{3},\dots,u_{2N})}
on
E_{T}
, we get

\mathbb{E}[Z|{\cal F}_{T},E_{T}]=I^{\mathbb{H}\setminus K_{t};(\eta(T),u_{2})}_{(u_{3},\dots,u_{2N})}
.
Since
g_{T}
maps
\mathbb{H}\setminus K_{T}
conformally onto
\mathbb{H}
, and sends
\eta(T)
and
u_{2}
respectively to
W_{t}
and
V_{T}
, by (
5.3
,
5.4
), on the event
E_{T}
,

M_{T}=\Big(\prod_{j\not\in\{1,2\}}|g_{t}^{\prime}(u_{j})|\Big)^{\bb}I^{\mathbb{H};(W_{t},V_{t})}_{(U^{3}_{t},\dots,U^{2N}_{t})}=I^{\mathbb{H}\setminus K_{t};(\eta(T),u_{2})}_{(u_{3},\dots,u_{2N})}=\mathbb{E}[Z|{\cal F}_{T}]

on the event
E_{T}=\{T<\tau\}
. Since this holds for any stopping time
T
, and
Z\leq H(u_{3},\dots,u_{2N})
by monotonicity, we get the conclusion.
∎

Once we know that
H
is smooth, Itô’s calculus together with (

,
2.7
) implies that

{\cal D}_{r}H:=\Big[\frac{\kappa}{2}\partial_{r}^{2}+\sum_{j\neq r}\Big(\frac{2}{x_{j}}\partial_{j}-\frac{2\bb}{x_{j}^{2}}\Big)\Big]H=0,\quad 1\leq r\leq N,

(5.9)

For
\kappa\in(0,6]
, if
(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{N})
is an
N
-SLE
κ

\alpha
, then for every

,

(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{N})
be multiple SLE
κ
in

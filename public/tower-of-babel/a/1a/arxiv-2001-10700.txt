On the Steady Prandtl Boundary Layer Expansions
Chen Gao, Liqun Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the zero-viscosity limit of the 2D steady Navier-Stokes equations in ( 0 , L ) × ℝ + (0,L)\times\mathbb{R}^{+} with non-slip boundary conditions. By estimating the stream-function of the remainder, we justify the validity of the Prandtl boundary layer expansions.



1.
Introduction

We consider the vanishing viscosity limit of steady Navier-Stokes equations

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &U^{\varepsilon}U^{\varepsilon}_{X}+V^{\varepsilon}U^{\varepsilon}_{Y}-\varepsilon\Delta U^{\varepsilon}+P^{\varepsilon}_{X}=0,\\
&U^{\varepsilon}V^{\varepsilon}_{X}+V^{\varepsilon}V^{\varepsilon}_{Y}-\varepsilon\Delta V^{\varepsilon}+P^{\varepsilon}_{Y}=0,\\
&U^{\varepsilon}_{X}+V^{\varepsilon}_{Y}=0,\\
&U^{\varepsilon}|_{Y=0}=V^{\varepsilon}|_{Y=0}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

in a two dimensional domain
\Omega=\{(X,Y):0\leqslant X\leqslant L,Y\geqslant 0\}.
A formal limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
should lead to the Euler flow
[U^{0},V^{0}]
inside
\Omega
:

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &U^{0}U^{0}_{X}+V^{0}U^{0}_{Y}+P^{0}_{X}=0,\\
&U^{0}V^{0}_{X}+V^{0}V^{0}_{Y}+P^{0}_{Y}=0,\\
&U^{0}_{X}+V^{0}_{Y}=0,\\
&V^{0}|_{Y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Generically, there is a mismatch between the tangential velocities of the Euler flow
U_{0}(X,0)\neq 0
and the prescribed Navier-Stokes flows
U^{\varepsilon}(X,0)=0
on the boundary, because of the difference of boundary conditions imposed on the two systems.

Due to the mismatch on the boundary, Prandtl in 1904, proposed a thin fluid
boundary layer of size
\sqrt{\varepsilon}
to connect different velocities

U_{0}(X,0)
and
0.
In the following discussion, we shall make use of the scaled boundary layer, or
Prandtl’s variables:

(1.3)

\displaystyle x=X,\qquad y=\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}.

In these variables, we express the solution of the NS equation
[U^{\varepsilon},V^{\varepsilon}]
via
[u^{\varepsilon},v^{\varepsilon}]
as

\begin{split}[U^{\varepsilon}(X,Y),V^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)]=[u^{\varepsilon}(x,y),\sqrt{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}(x,y)]\end{split}

in which we note that the scaled normal velocity
v^{\varepsilon}
is
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}
of the original velocity
V^{\varepsilon}
. Similarly,
P^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)=p^{\varepsilon}(x,y)
. In these new variables, the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
) now read

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}_{x}+v^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}_{y}+p^{\varepsilon}_{x}&=u^{\varepsilon}_{yy}+\varepsilon u^{\varepsilon}_{xx},\\
\varepsilon[u^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}_{x}+v^{\varepsilon}v^{\varepsilon}_{y}]+p^{\varepsilon}_{y}&=\varepsilon[v^{\varepsilon}_{yy}+\varepsilon v^{\varepsilon}_{xx}],\\
u^{\varepsilon}_{x}+v^{\varepsilon}_{y}&=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, it leads to the Prandtl equations:

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &u^{0}_{p}u^{0}_{px}+v^{0}_{p}u^{0}_{py}-u^{0}_{pyy}+p^{0}_{px}=0,\\
&p^{0}_{py}=0,\\
&u^{0}_{px}+v^{0}_{py}=0.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Prandtl hypothesized that when viscosity
\varepsilon
is the Navier-Stokes flow can be approximately decomposed into two parts:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &U^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)\approx u^{0}_{e}(X,Y)-u^{0}_{e}(X,0)+u^{0}_{p}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}),\\
&V^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)\approx v^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+\sqrt{\varepsilon}v^{0}_{p}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}),\end{aligned}

in which
(u^{0}_{e},v^{0}_{e})
denotes the Euler flow.

We attempt to verify the Prandtl boundary layer expansion (
1.6
) under more general conditions. Now let us review the main problems in boundary layer theory. Two important open problems in this area are the well-posedness of Prandtl equations and the justification of viscosity vanishing limits. The first problem is relatively well understood and proved the well-posedness in some cases. Sammartino and Caflisch
[
23

[

obtained the local existence of classical solutions of 2D Prandtl equations by using the Crocco transformation. Xin and Zhang
[

proved the global existence of weak solutions to this system for the favorable pressure, which by their regularity result are classical solutions. Lately, Alexandre
[
1

and Masmoudi and Wong
[
20

independently proved by the energy methods, the local well-posedness of prandtl equations in Sobolev space under the monotonic assumptions. Meanswhile, Liu, Wang and Yang generalized some results in 3D case with special structure. There are some results of well-posedness in Gervey class
[
2
]
and
[
7
]
. On the hands, Gerard-Varet and Dormy
[
4

established the linearized ill-posedness without the monotonicity condition in Sobolev space for Prandtl equations. There are some relevant results in
[
3
]
and
[
8

.

For the second problem, the verification of the viscosity vanishing limits is more difficult and remains a challenge problem in general. The problem in the analytic case was proved in
[
24
]
and
[
25
]
. The problem in 2D case was studied by Maekawa
[

and proved the convergence under the assumption on the initial vorticity vanishing in the neighbourhood of boundary. The auther in

\mathbf{U}:=\mathbf{U}^{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{U}_{s}

(1.22)

\displaystyle-\varepsilon\Delta\mathbf{U}+\mathbf{U}_{s}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{U}+\mathbf{U}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{U}_{s}+\nabla P=\mathbf{F}.

The method of

This paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we construct the approximation solutions by the asymptotic expansion method. In Section 3, we estimate the stream-function of remainder. In Section 4, we prove the main theorems.

2.
Construction of the approximate solution

To construct the approximate solutions, we follow the idea in
[
13
]
. We will need higher order approximations, as compared to (
1.6
), in order to control the remainders. Precisely, we search for approximate solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations in the following form:

(2.1)

\displaystyle U^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)\approx

\displaystyle u^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+u^{0}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}[u^{1}_{e}(X,Y)+u^{1}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})]

\displaystyle+\varepsilon[u^{2}_{e}(X,Y)+u^{2}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})],

\displaystyle V^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)\approx

\displaystyle v^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+\sqrt{\varepsilon}[v^{0}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+v^{1}_{e}(X,Y)]+\varepsilon[v^{1}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+v^{2}_{e}(X,Y)]

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}v^{2}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}),

\displaystyle P^{\varepsilon}(X,Y)\approx

\displaystyle p^{0}_{e}(X,Y)+p^{0}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}[p^{1}_{e}(X,Y)+p^{1}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})]

\displaystyle+\varepsilon[p^{2}_{e}(X,Y)+p^{2}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}})]+\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}p^{3}_{b}(X,\frac{Y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}),

in which

&\phi|_{x=0}=\int_{0}^{y}u_{0}(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime},\hskip 8.53581pt\phi|_{y=0}=\phi_{y}|_{y=0}=0,\hskip 8.53581pt\phi_{y}|_{y\rightarrow\infty}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

In order to give a priori estimate of (
4.8
), we denote
g=\frac{\phi}{\bar{u}}
. Recall
\bar{u}\sim y
when
y\leqslant 1
and
\bar{u}\sim 1
, when
y\geqslant 1
, and
\phi|_{y=0}=\phi_{y}|_{y=0}=0
,
g
is well-defined. And
g
satisfies

(4.9)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{x}[\bar{u}^{2}g_{y}]-\partial^{3}_{y}[\bar{u}g]+\bar{v}\partial^{2}_{y}[\bar{u}g]-\bar{u}\bar{v}_{yy}g=h,\\
&g|_{x=0}=\frac{\int_{0}^{y}u_{0}}{\bar{u}},\hskip 8.53581ptg|_{y=0}=0,\hskip 8.53581ptg_{y}|_{y\rightarrow\infty}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

In the appendix, we write
\|\cdot\|
for
L^{2}_{x,y}(\Omega)
,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,y}}
,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{x=x_{0}}=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}_{y}(x=x_{0})}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{y=0}=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}_{x}(y=0)}
, now we define the norms of
g
:

(4.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}:=\sup_{0\leqslant x_{0}\leqslant L}\|\bar{u}g_{y}\rho\|_{x=x_{0}}+\|\sqrt{\bar{u}}g_{yy}\rho\|,\\
\|g\|_{\Xi_{1}}:=\sup_{0\leqslant x_{0}\leqslant L}\|\bar{u}g_{xy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|_{x=x_{0}}+\|\sqrt{\bar{u}}g_{xyy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|,\end{aligned}

here
\rho=\langle y\rangle^{N}
, for
N
large constant. Next, let us prove the following priori estimate of
g
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose
g
be a smooth solution of (
4.9
),
L>0
small enough, then there exists a positive constant
C
independent on
L
, s.t.
g
satisfies

(4.11)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}^{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}g_{y}\rho\|_{x=0}^{2}+C\|h\rho\|^{2},

(4.12)

\displaystyle\|g_{x}\|_{\Xi_{1}}^{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}g_{xy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|_{x=0}^{2}+C\|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}^{2}+C\|h_{x}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}.

Proof:

Multiply equation (
4.9
) by
g_{y}\rho^{2}
and integrate in
(0,x_{0})\times(0,\infty)
.

\begin{split}\langle[\bar{u}^{2}g_{y}]_{x},g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle=&\langle\bar{u}^{2}g_{xy},g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle+2\langle\bar{u}\bar{u}_{x}g_{y},g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{u}g_{y}\rho\|^{2}_{x=x_{0}}-\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{u}g_{y}\rho\|^{2}_{x=0}+\langle\bar{u}\bar{u}_{x}g_{y},g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle.\end{split}

We can dominate
\|g_{y}\|
by
\|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}
. Let
0<\xi\leqslant 1
be a constant being choosing later.
\chi(y)
is smooth cut-off function, satisfies

\chi|_{[0,1]}=1
,
\chi|_{[2,\infty]}=0
. Then,

\begin{split}\langle g_{y},g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle\lesssim\langle g_{y},g_{y}[1-\chi(\frac{y}{\xi})]^{2}\rho^{2}\rangle+\langle g_{y},g_{y}\chi(\frac{y}{\xi})^{2}\rho^{2}\rangle.\end{split}

When
y\leqslant 1
,
1-\chi(\frac{y}{\xi})\lesssim\frac{y}{\xi}\lesssim\frac{\bar{u}}{\xi}
, when
y>1
,
1-\chi(\frac{y}{\xi})\lesssim\bar{u}\lesssim\frac{\bar{u}}{\xi}
. So

\begin{split}\langle g_{y},g_{y}[1-\chi(\frac{y}{\xi})]^{2}\rho^{2}\rangle\lesssim\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}\|\bar{u}g_{y}\rho\|^{2}\lesssim\frac{L}{\xi^{2}}\|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}^{2}.\end{split}

While

\begin{split}\langle g_{y},g_{y}\chi(\frac{y}{\xi})^{2}\rho^{2}\rangle=&-2\langle yg_{y},g_{yy}\chi^{2}(\frac{y}{\xi})\rho^{2}\rangle-\frac{2}{\xi}\langle yg_{y},g_{y}\chi(\frac{y}{\xi})\chi^{\prime}(\frac{y}{\xi})\rho^{2}\rangle-2\langle yg_{y},g_{y}\chi^{2}(\frac{y}{\xi})\rho\rho_{y})\rangle\\
\lesssim&\|y\chi(\frac{y}{\xi})g_{yy}\rho\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\xi^{2}}\|\bar{u}g_{y}\rho\|^{2}\\
\lesssim&\xi\|\sqrt{\bar{u}}g_{yy}\rho\|^{2}+\frac{L}{\xi^{2}}\|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}^{2}.\end{split}

So we have

\begin{split}\langle g_{y},g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle\lesssim\xi\|\sqrt{\bar{u}}g_{yy}\rho\|^{2}+\frac{L}{\xi^{2}}\|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}^{2},\end{split}

select
\xi=L^{\frac{1}{3}}
, then

(4.13)

\displaystyle\langle g_{y},g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle\lesssim L^{\frac{1}{3}}\|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}^{2}.

So the first term is

(4.14)

\displaystyle\langle[\bar{u}^{2}g_{y}]_{x},g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{u}g_{y}\rho\|^{2}_{x=x_{0}}-\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{u}g_{y}\rho\|^{2}_{x=0}+O\big(L^{\frac{1}{3}}\|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}^{2}\big).

The second term:

\begin{split}-\langle\partial^{3}_{y}[\bar{u}g],g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle=\langle\partial^{2}_{y}[\bar{u}g],g_{yy}\rho^{2}\rangle+2\langle\partial^{2}_{y}[\bar{u}g],g_{yy}\rho_{y}\rho\rangle+\langle\partial^{2}_{y}[\bar{u}g],g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle_{y=0}.\end{split}

\begin{split}\langle\partial^{2}_{y}[\bar{u}g],g_{yy}\rho^{2}\rangle=&\langle\bar{u}g_{y}y+2\bar{u}_{y}g_{y}+\bar{u}_{yy}g,g_{yy}\rho^{2}\rangle\\
=&\|\sqrt{\bar{u}}g_{yy}\rho\|^{2}-\langle\bar{u}_{y}g_{y},g_{y}\rangle_{y=0}+\langle(\bar{u}_{y}\rho^{2})_{y},g^{2}_{y}\rangle\\
&-\langle\bar{u}_{yy}g_{y},g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle-\langle(\bar{u}_{yy}\rho^{2})_{y}g,g_{y}\rangle\\
=&\|\sqrt{\bar{u}}g_{yy}\rho\|^{2}-\langle\bar{u}_{y}g_{y},g_{y}\rangle_{y=0}+O\big(\|g_{y}\rho^{2}\|+\|y(\bar{u}_{yy}\rho^{2})_{y}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\frac{g}{y}\|\|g_{y}\|\big)\\
=&\|\sqrt{\bar{u}}g_{yy}\rho\|^{2}-\langle\bar{u}_{y}g_{y},g_{y}\rangle_{y=0}+O\big(\|g_{y}\rho\|^{2}\big),\end{split}

\begin{split}2\langle\partial^{2}_{y}[\bar{u}g],g_{yy}\rho_{y}\rho\rangle=&\langle\bar{u}(g_{y}^{2})_{y},\rho_{y}\rho\rangle+4\langle\bar{u}_{y}g_{y},g_{y}\rho_{y}\rho\rangle+2\langle\bar{u}_{yy}g,g_{y}\rho_{y}\rho\rangle\\
=&\langle(\bar{u}\rho_{y}\rho,g^{2}_{y}\rangle+O\big(\|g_{y}\rho^{2}\|+\|y\bar{u}_{yy}\rho_{y}\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\frac{g}{y}\|\|g_{y}\|\big))\\
=&O\big(\|g_{y}\rho\|^{2}\big),\end{split}

\begin{split}\langle\partial^{2}_{y}[\bar{u}g],g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle_{y=0}=2\langle\bar{u}_{y}g_{y},g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle_{y=0}.\end{split}

So the second term is

(4.15)

\displaystyle\langle\partial^{2}_{y}[\bar{u}g],g_{yy}\rho^{2}\rangle=\|\sqrt{\bar{u}}g_{yy}\rho\|^{2}+\langle\bar{u}_{y}g_{y},g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle_{y=0}+O\big(L^{\frac{1}{3}}\|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}^{2}\big).

The third term is

(4.16)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle\bar{v}(\bar{u}g)_{yy},g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle=&\langle\bar{v}(\bar{u}g_{y}y+2\bar{u}_{y}g_{y}+\bar{u}_{yy}g),g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}\langle(\bar{v}\bar{u}\rho^{2})_{y},g^{2}_{y}\rangle+O\big(\|\bar{v}\bar{u}_{y}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g_{y}\rho\|^{2}+\|y\bar{v}\bar{u}_{yy}\rho^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\frac{g}{y}\|\|g_{y}\|\big)\\
=&O\big(\|\frac{(\bar{v}\bar{u}\rho^{2})_{y}}{\rho^{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g_{y}\rho\|^{2}+\|g_{y}\rho\|^{2}\big)\\
=&O\big(L^{\frac{1}{3}}\|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}^{2}\big).\end{aligned}

And the last one is

(4.17)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle\bar{v}_{yy}\bar{u}g,g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle=O\big(\|yvb_{yy}\bar{u}\rho^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\frac{g}{y}\|\|g_{y}\|\big)=O\big(\|g_{y}\rho\|^{2}\big).\end{aligned}

Collect (
4.14
), (
4.15
), (
4.16
), (
4.17
), we have

(4.18)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2}\|\bar{u}g_{y}\rho\|^{2}_{x=x_{0}}+\|\sqrt{\bar{u}}g_{yy}\rho\|^{2}+\langle\bar{u}_{y}g_{y},g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle_{y=0}=O\big(L^{\frac{1}{3}}\|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}^{2}\big)+\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{u}g_{y}\rho\|^{2}_{x=0}+\langle h,g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle.\end{aligned}

Take the supremum of
0\leqslant x_{0}\leqslant L
, notice that
L
small enough,

(4.19)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sup_{0\leqslant x_{0}\leqslant L}\|\bar{u}g_{y}\rho\|^{2}_{x=x_{0}}+\|\sqrt{\bar{u}}g_{yy}\rho\|^{2}+\langle\bar{u}_{y}g_{y},g_{y}\rho^{2}\rangle_{y=0}\lesssim\|\bar{u}g_{y}\rho\|^{2}_{x=0}+\|h\rho\|^{2}.\end{aligned}

The inequality (
4.12
) is similar to the (
4.11
). Differential equation (
4.9
) respect to
x
,

(4.20)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\partial_{x}[\bar{u}^{2}g_{xy}]-\partial^{3}_{y}[\bar{u}g_{x}]+\bar{v}\partial^{2}_{y}(\bar{u}g_{x})-\bar{u}\bar{v}_{yy}g_{x}+\partial_{x}[2\bar{u}\bar{u}_{x}g_{y}]-\partial^{3}_{y}[\bar{u}_{x}g]\\
&+\bar{v}_{x}\partial^{2}_{y}(\bar{u}g)+\bar{v}\partial^{2}_{y}(\bar{u}_{x}g)-\bar{u}_{x}\bar{v}_{yy}g-\bar{u}\bar{v}_{xyy}g=h_{x}.\end{aligned}

Take
g_{xy}\frac{\rho^{2}}{\langle y\rangle^{2}}
as the test function, like (
4.18
),

(4.21)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\langle\partial_{x}[\bar{u}^{2}g_{xy}]-\partial^{3}_{y}[\bar{u}g_{x}]+\bar{v}\partial^{2}_{y}(\bar{u}g_{x})-\bar{u}\bar{v}_{yy}g_{x},g_{xy}\frac{\rho^{2}}{\langle y\rangle^{2}}\rangle\\
=&\frac{1}{2}(\|\bar{u}g_{xy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}_{x=x_{0}}-\|\bar{u}g_{xy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}_{x=0})+\|\sqrt{\bar{u}}g_{xyy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}+\langle\bar{u}_{y}g_{xy},g_{xy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\rangle_{y=0}+O\big(L^{\frac{1}{3}}\|g\|_{\Xi_{1}}^{2}\big).\end{aligned}

While

(4.22)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\langle\partial_{x}[2\bar{u}\bar{u}_{x}g_{y}]+\bar{v}_{x}\partial^{2}_{y}(\bar{u}g)+\bar{v}\partial^{2}_{y}(\bar{u}_{x}g)-\bar{u}_{x}\bar{v}_{yy}g-\bar{u}\bar{v}_{xyy}g,g_{xy}\frac{\rho^{2}}{\langle y\rangle^{2}}\rangle\\
=&O\big(\|\sqrt{\bar{u}}g_{yy}\rho\|^{2}+\|g_{y}\rho\|^{2}+\|g_{xy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}\big)\\
=&O\big(\|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}^{2}+L^{\frac{1}{3}}\|g\|_{\Xi_{1}}^{2}\big).\end{aligned}

The difficult term is

(4.23)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle-\partial^{3}_{y}[\bar{u}_{x}g],g_{xy}\frac{\rho^{2}}{\langle y\rangle^{2}}\rangle=&-\langle\bar{u}_{x}g_{yyy}+3\bar{u}_{xy}g_{yy},g_{xy}\frac{\rho^{2}}{\langle y\rangle^{2}}\rangle+O\big(\|g_{y}\rho\|^{2}+\|g_{xy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}\big)\\
=&O\big((\|\bar{u}g_{yyy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|+\|g_{yy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|)\|g_{xy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|+\|g_{y}\rho\|^{2}+\|g_{xy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}\big).\end{aligned}

From equation (
4.8
), we have

\begin{split}\|\phi_{yyy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}=O\big(\|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}^{2}+L^{\frac{1}{3}}\|g\|_{\Xi_{1}}^{2}+\|h\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}\big),\end{split}

notice that the fact

\begin{split}\|\partial^{2}_{y}(\frac{\phi}{y})\|_{L^{2}_{loc}}=O\big(\|\phi_{yyy}\|_{L^{2}_{loc}}+\|\phi_{yy}\|_{L^{2}_{loc}}\big),\end{split}

similarly, we can get

\begin{split}\|g_{yy}\|_{L^{2}_{loc}}=O\big(\|\phi_{yyy}\|_{L^{2}_{loc}}+\|\phi_{yy}\|_{L^{2}_{loc}}\big),\end{split}

so we have

\begin{split}\|g_{yy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}=&\|g_{yy}\chi\|^{2}+\|g_{yy}(1-\chi)\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}\\
=&O\big(\|\phi_{yyy}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{loc}}+\|\phi_{yy}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{loc}}+\|\sqrt{\bar{u}}g_{yy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}\big),\\
=&O\big(\|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}^{2}+L^{\frac{1}{3}}\|g\|_{\Xi_{1}}^{2}+\|h\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}\big),\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\|\bar{u}g_{yyy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}=&\|(\phi_{yyy}-3\bar{u}_{y}g_{yy}-3\bar{u}_{yy}g_{y}-\bar{u}_{yyy}g)\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}\\
=&O\big(\|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}^{2}+L^{\frac{1}{3}}\|g\|_{\Xi_{1}}^{2}+\|h\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}\big).\end{split}

We conclude (
4.23
) as

(4.24)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle-\partial^{3}_{y}[\bar{u}_{x}g],g_{xy}\frac{\rho^{2}}{\langle y\rangle^{2}}\rangle=O\big(\|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}^{2}+L^{\frac{1}{3}}\|g\|_{\Xi_{1}}^{2}+\|h\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}\big).\end{aligned}

Collect (
4.21
), (
4.22
), (
4.24
), we have

(4.25)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{u}g_{xy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}_{x=x_{0}}+\|\sqrt{\bar{u}}g_{xyy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}+\langle\bar{u}_{y}g_{xy},g_{xy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\rangle_{y=0}\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{u}g_{xy}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}_{x=0}+O\big(\|g\|_{\Xi_{0}}^{2}+L^{\frac{1}{3}}\|g\|_{\Xi_{1}}^{2}+\|h_{x}\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}+\|h\frac{\rho}{\langle y\rangle}\|^{2}\big).\end{aligned}

So we finish the proof of (
4.12
).

Finite-Length Analysis of Wiretap Codes using Universal Hash Functions
Kagan Akcay

Abstract
This paper investigates the relation between the second-order coding rate, where the second-order turns out to be strictly larger than n \sqrt{n} , and the mutual information as the leaked information for a fixed error probability by using wiretap codes constructed by universal 2 hash functions. We first generalize the upper bound on ϵ \epsilon -smooth max information in [ 1 ] and use it in our analysis where we adopt the method in [ 2 ] , which uses universal hashing for compressing a source and making it secure from another correlated source, and apply it to the wiretap channel. We prove first- and second-order achievability results by assuming that the conjecture we state holds true.



I
Introduction

The study of secure communication over a noisy channel with an eavesdropper, which is called a wiretap channel, began with Wyner
[
3
]
and continued with Csiszár and Körner
[
4
]
. It was shown that if the transmission rate is below the secrecy capacity, the error probability and the information leakage can be made arbitrarily small as long as the number of channel usage is sufficiently large. Whereas the studies above analyze the wiretap channel in the asymptotic regime, the analysis in the finite length regime is also studied in several papers such as
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
, where
[
5
,
6
]
adopt the leaked information criterion based on the variational distance and
[
7
]
on the mutual information. This paper adopts the leaked information criterion based on mutual information.

The main purpose of this paper is to find the relation between the second-order coding rate and the leaked information for a fixed error probability. Since the mutual information can be unbounded, we don’t fix the leaked information together with the error probability and analyze the asymptotic expansion as done in
[
5
]
where the variational distance as the leaked information is bounded. We fix only the error probability and investigate the first- and second-order asymptotics of the leaked information by adopting the method in
[
2
]
.
[
2
]
proves first- and second-order results for the mutual information between a compressed version of a source
A^{n}
, where universal hashing is used for compression, and another correlated source
E^{n}
, where
(A^{n},E^{n})
is an independent identical distributed (i.i.d) sequence. We follow the achievability proof method of
[
2
]
and apply it to the wiretap channel by proving an upper bound on
\epsilon
-max smooth information.

In Section

.

III
Channel Model

The wiretap channel was first introduced by Wyner in
[
3
]
. In the wiretap channel, we have a sender, a legitimate receiver, and an eavesdropper. The sender aims to transmit a message reliably to the legitimate receiver while keeping it secret from the eavesdropper. In the discrete memoryless degraded wiretap channel (DM-DWTC), we have
V^{n}:\textbf{X}^{n}\rightarrow{\textbf{Y}^{n}}
and
W^{n}:\textbf{Y}^{n}\rightarrow{\textbf{E}^{n}}
where
V
and
W
are discrete memoryless channels (DMCs),
X
,
Y
and
E
are finite sets and
n
is the number of the channel usage.
V
corresponds to the channel from the sender to the legitimate receiver, whereas
W
corresponds to the degraded channel to the eavesdropper. A secrecy code
C_{n}
for a DM-WTC consists of

•

a message
M
which distributed on a message set
\textbf{M}=\{1,...,|{}\textbf{M}|{}\}
,

•

a randomized encoder
e:\textbf{M}\rightarrow{\textbf{X}^{n}}
which generates a codeword
x^{n}
according to a probability distribution
P_{X^{n}|{}M=m}
,

•

a decoder
d:\textbf{Y}^{n}\rightarrow{\textbf{M}}
which maps each channel observation
y^{n}
to an estimate
\hat{m}\in{\textbf{M}}
.

We require that
M
is uniformly distributed on
M
and the encoder and the decoder of the legitimate receiver satisfy the average error probability constraint
Pr(d(Y^{n})\neq{}M)\leq{}\varepsilon
.
We assume that the channel statistics and properties of the code

are known to all parties. However, the eavesdropper cannot access realizations
x^{n}
and
y^{n}
. We use mutual information
I(M\wedge{}E^{n})
as the secrecy metric. We fix
\varepsilon
and let the message set
|{}\textbf{M}|{}=e^{nR+(\sqrt{n})^{1+\gamma}L}
where
0<\gamma<1
and
\gamma
is fixed and analyse the asymptotic behaviour of
I(M\wedge{}E^{n})
with respect to
\varepsilon
,
R
and
L
. The reason why
\gamma>0
and not exactly
0
as in
[
5
,
6
,
2
]
will be clear in Section
VIII
.

Divergence Bounds

Lemma 6
.

Let
W:\textbf{A}\rightarrow{\textbf{E}}
be a subnormalized channel, where
A
and
E
are finite sets. Let
A
be a random variable uniformly distributed on
A
and
P_{E|{}A}=W
. Then

D_{2}(P_{AE}|{}|{}P_{A}\times{}P_{E})\leq{}I_{max}(W).

Proof.

\displaystyle D_{2}(P_{AE}|{}|{}P_{A}\times{}P_{E})

\displaystyle=\log\sum_{a,e}\frac{(P_{AE})^{2}}{P_{A}P_{E}}

\displaystyle=\log\sum_{a,e}\frac{P_{A}^{2}(a)W^{2}(e|{}a)}{P_{A}(a)\sum_{a^{\prime}}P_{A}(a^{\prime})W(e|{}a^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\log\sum_{e}\frac{\sum_{a}W^{2}(e|{}a)}{\sum_{a^{\prime}}W(e|{}a^{\prime})}

\displaystyle{\leq{}}\log\sum_{e}\max_{a}W(e|{}a)=I_{max}(W)

∎

\displaystyle\log\frac{P_{AE}}{P_{A}P_{E}}

\displaystyle=\log\frac{P_{A}(a)W(e|{}a)}{P_{A}(a)\sum_{a^{\prime}}P_{A}(a^{\prime})W(e|{}a^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\log\frac{|{}\textbf{A}|{}W(e|{}a)}{\sum_{a^{\prime}}W(e|{}a^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq{}\log\frac{|{}\textbf{A}|{}W(e|{}a)}{W(e|{}a)}=\log|{}\textbf{A}|{}.

\sum_{(a,e)\in{\textbf{T}}}P_{AE}\log\frac{P_{AE}}{P_{A}P_{E}}\leq\log{\sum_{(a,e)\in{\textbf{T}}}\frac{(P_{AE})^{2}}{P_{A}P_{E}}}-(1-\epsilon)\log(1-\epsilon)\leq{}I_{max}(W_{\textbf{T}})-(1-\epsilon)\log(1-\epsilon)
where the last inequality comes from Lemma

IX
Conclusion

We have generalized the upper bound on
\epsilon
-max information and proved an upper bound on the mutual information between any two random variables, taking values on finite sets by
\epsilon

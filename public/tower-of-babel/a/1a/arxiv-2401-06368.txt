Arithmetic Siegel-Weil formula on 𝒳 0 ​ ( N ) \mathcal{X}_{0}(N) : Singular terms
Baiqing Zhu

Abstract
. For arbitrary level N N , we relate the generating series of codimension 2 special cycles on 𝒳 0 ​ ( N ) \mathcal{X}_{0}(N) to the derivatives of a genus 2 Eisenstein series, especially the singular terms of both sides. On the analytic side, we use difference formulas of local densities to relate the singular Fourier coefficients of the genus 2 Eisenstein series to the nonsingular Fourier coefficients of a genus 1 Eisenstein series. On the geometric side, we study the reduction of cusps to compute the divisor class of the Hodge bundle and the heights of special divisors. When N N is square-free, this gives a different proof of the main results in the works of Du and Yang [ DY19 ] and Sankaran, Shi, and Yang [ SSY22 ] .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

The classical Siegel-Weil formula relates certain Siegel Eisenstein series to the arithmetic of quadratic forms, namely it expresses special values of these series as theta functions – generating series of representation numbers of quadratic forms. Kudla initiated an influential program to establish the arithmetic Siegel-Weil formula relating certain Siegel Eisenstein series to objects in arithmetic geometry.

In this article, we study the case of modular curves. Let
N
be a positive integer, the classical modular curve
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)_{\mathbb{C}}
over
\mathbb{C}
is defined as the following smooth
1
-dimensional complex curve,

\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)_{\mathbb{C}}\coloneqq\textup{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathbb{H}_{1}^{\pm}\times\textup{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})/\Gamma_{0}(N)(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}_{1}^{\pm},

where
\mathbb{H}_{1}^{\pm}=\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{H}_{1}^{+}=\{z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}:x\in\mathbb{R},y\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\}
is the upper half plane. The group
\Gamma_{0}(N)(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
is the following open compact subgroup of
\textup{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
,

\Gamma_{0}(N)(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})=\left\{x=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
Nc&d\end{pmatrix}\in\textup{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})\,\,:\,\,a,b,c,d\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}\right\},

and
\Gamma_{0}(N)=\Gamma_{0}(N)(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})\bigcap\textup{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

The smooth curve
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)_{\mathbb{C}}
is not proper, its compactification
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)_{\mathbb{C}}\coloneqq\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)_{\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\textup{cusps}\}
is a smooth projective curve over
\mathbb{C}
. Katz, Mazur
[
KM85
]
and Cesnavicius
[
Ces17
]
constructed an integral model
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
for the complex curve
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)_{\mathbb{C}}
. The model
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
is a 2-dimensional regular flat Deligne-Mumford stack. For every pair
(T,\mathsf{y})
where
T
is a
2\times 2
symmetric matrix with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathsf{y}
is a positive definite
2\times 2
symmetric matrix with coefficients in
\mathbb{R}
, we define the arithmetic special cycle
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(T,\mathsf{y})
on the stack
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
and study their arithmetic degrees. Finally, we prove that these arithmetic degrees are identified with the derivatives of Fourier coefficients of certain Siegel Eisenstein series of genus 2.

When
N
is an odd, square-free positive integer, the relation has already been obtained for all the pair
(T,\mathsf{y})
in the work of Sankaran, Shi, and Yang
[
SSY22
, Theorem 2.14]
by computing both sides explicitly based on the previous works of Yang
[
Yn98
]
and Kudla, Rapoport and Yang
[
KRY06
]
. When
T
is nonsingular, the relation has been obtained for all the level
N
in the previous work of the author
[
Zhu23
, Theorem 1.2.1]
by establishing difference formulas on both the analytic and geometric sides. In this article, we give proof for all the pairs
(T,\mathsf{y})
, and all the levels
N
.

When the matrix
T
is singular of rank 1. On the analytic side, we use difference formulas of local densities to relate the singular Fourier coefficients of an Eisenstein series of genus 2 to the nonsingular Fourier coefficients of an Eisenstein series of genus 1. On the geometric side, the cycle
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(T,\mathsf{y})
is essentially the intersection of a codimension
1
cycle and the metrized Hodge line bundle on
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
. We compute this intersection number by investigating the irreducible components of the special fiber
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\coloneqq\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)\times_{\textup{Spec}\,\mathbb{Z}}\textup{Spec}\,\mathbb{F}_{p}
of the model
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
, and the reduction mod
p
of the cuspidal divisor of the curve
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
.

When the matrix
T=0
, both the analytic side and geometric side can be computed explicitly. Here again, the computation of the analytic side is based on the difference formulas of local densities, while the computation of the geometric side is based on the intersection of irreducible components of the special fiber
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
.

1.2.
Summary of the main results

Let
\Delta(N)
be the following rank 3 quadratic lattice over
\mathbb{Z}
,

(1)

\Delta(N)=\left\{x=\begin{pmatrix}-Na&b\\

equipped with the quadratic form

We use

to denote a place of
\mathbb{Q}
. For every finite place
v
, let
\delta_{v}(N)=\Delta(N)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{v}
be a rank 3 quadratic lattice over
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
. Let
\mathbb{A}
be the ring of ad
\grave{\textup{e}}
les over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
\mathbb{V}=\{\mathbb{V}_{v}\}
be the incoherent collection of quadratic spaces of

of rank 3 nearby

at

(2)

\mathbb{V}_{v}=\delta_{v}(N)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{v}\,\,\textup{if $v<\infty$, and $\mathbb{V}_{\infty}$ is positive definite.}

There is a classical incoherent Eisenstein series
E(\mathsf{z},s,\Delta(N)^{2})
(cf. §
3.1
) on the Siegel upper half space of genus 2,

\mathbb{H}_{2}=\{\mathsf{z}=\mathsf{x}+i\mathsf{y}\,\,|\,\,\mathsf{x}\in\textup{Sym}_{2}(\mathbb{R}),\mathsf{y}\in\textup{Sym}_{2}(\mathbb{R})_{>0}\}.

This is essentially the Siegel Eisenstein series associated to a standard Siegel-Weil section of the degenerate principal series. The Eisenstein series here has a meromorphic continuation and a functional equation relating

. The central value
E(\mathsf{z},0,\Delta(N)^{2})=0
by the incoherence. We thus consider its central derivative

\partial\textup{Eis}(\mathsf{z},\Delta(N)^{2})\coloneqq\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}s}\bigg|_{s=0}E(\mathsf{z},s,\Delta(N)^{2}).

Associated to the standard additive character
\psi:\mathbb{A}/\mathbb{Q}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, it has a decomposition into the central derivatives of the Fourier coefficients

\partial\textup{Eis}(\mathsf{z},\Delta(N)^{2})=\sum\limits_{T\in\textup{Sym}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})}\partial\textup{Eis}_{T}(\mathsf{z},\Delta(N)^{2})

On the geometric side, there is a regular integral model of the modular curve
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)_{\mathbb{C}}
over
\mathbb{Z}
defined by Katz and Mazur: for any scheme

, the groupoid
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)(S)
consists of objects
(E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}E^{\prime})
where

,

are elliptic curves over
S

is a cyclic isogeny such that
\pi^{\vee}\circ\pi=N
. They proved that
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)
is 2-dimensional regular flat Deligne-Mumford stack (cf.
[
KM85
, Theorem 5.1.1]
), but
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)
is not proper. There is a moduli stack
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
defined in
[
Ces17
]
which serves as a “compactification” of
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)
. It is a proper regular flat 2-dimensional Deligne-Mumford stack that contains
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)
as an open substack, so we can consider the arithmetic intersection theory on
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
following the lines in
[
Gil09
]

1.2.1.
Special cycles of codimension 1

The key concept is that of a special cycle. For every integer
t
, we define a closed substack of
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)
as follows: For an object
(E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}E^{\prime})
of
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)(S)
, the stack
\mathcal{Z}(t,\Delta(N))
parameterizes isogenies

and

with

and orthogonal to the cyclic isogeny

. It can be shown that the stack

is a generalized Cartier divisor even on the stack
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)

. For every positive number

\mathcal{Z}^{\ast}(t,y,\Delta(N))=\mathcal{Z}(t,\Delta(N))+g(t,y,\Delta(N))\cdot\mathsf{Cusp}(\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)).

where
\mathsf{Cusp}(\mathcal{X}_{0}(N))
is the cuspidal divisor of the stack
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
, and
g(t,y,\Delta(N))
is a smooth function in

defined in (
48
), it’s identically
0
when
t>0
.

For every pair
(t,y)
such that
t
is a nonzero integer and
y>0
, a green function
\mathfrak{g}(t,y,\Delta(N))
of the divisor
\mathcal{Z}^{\ast}(t,y,\Delta(N))
is constructed by Kudla
[
Kud97
, (12.21)]
(see also
[
DY19
, §5]
). We will recall the construction in
\lx@sectionsign
7.1.2
. Finally, we define the following element in the codimension 1 arithmetic Chow group
\widehat{\textup{CH}}^{1}(\mathcal{X}_{0}(N))
of

(Definition
7.1.3
):

(3)

\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(t,y)=(\mathcal{Z}^{\ast}(t,y,\Delta(N)),\mathfrak{g}(t,y,\Delta(N))).

These elements
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(t,y)
are invariant under the Atkin-Lehner involution
W_{N}
of the stack
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
, i.e.,
W_{N}^{\ast}\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(t,y)=\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(t,y)
(Lemma

).

When

, the definition of
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(0,y)
is slightly different from (

\widehat{\omega}_{N}
on the stack
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
(Example

), however, this bundle is not invariant under the Atkin-Lehner involution

(Corollary

). Therefore we consider the following element

\widehat{\omega}=-\widehat{\omega}_{N}-W_{N}^{\ast}\widehat{\omega}_{N},

the element
\widehat{\omega}
is clearly invariant under the Atkin-Lehner involution. We define

(4)

\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(0,y,\Delta(N))=\widehat{\omega}+(\mathcal{Z}^{\ast}(0,y,\Delta(N)),\mathfrak{g}(0,y,\Delta(N)))-(0,\log y).

Now for any rational number
t
which is not an integer, we simply define
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(t,y)=0\in\widehat{\textup{CH}}^{1}(\mathcal{X}_{0}(N))
. Let

, we consider the following generating series with coefficients in
\widehat{\textup{CH}}^{1}(\mathcal{X}_{0}(N))
,

\widehat{\phi}_{1}(\tau)=\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{Q}}\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(t,y,\Delta(N))\cdot q^{t}

Theorem 1.2.1

(Theorem
8.2.5
)

Let

be a positive integer. The generating series
\widehat{\phi}_{1}

When

1.2.2.
Special cycles of codimension 2

We are mainly interested in the elements in the codimension 2 arithmetic Chow group
\widehat{\textup{CH}}^{2}(\mathcal{X}_{0}(N))

Let
T\in\textup{Sym}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})

If the rank of

{{}^{t}g}Tg=\textup{diag}\{0,t\}

There is an isomorphism

When

1.3.
Strategy of the proof of main results

In this section, we mainly explain the proof of Theorem

1.2.1

We will prove Theorem

\mathsf{z}=\mathsf{x}+i\mathsf{y}\in\mathbb{H}_{2}

(5)

\widehat{\textup{deg}}\,\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(T,\mathsf{y})\cdot q^{T}=\frac{\psi(N)}{24}\cdot\partial\textup{Eis}_{T}(\mathsf{z},\Delta(N)^{2}).

When

is nonsingular, this has been proved in
[
Zhu23
, Theorem 1.2.1]
. In this article, we will focus on the case that
T
is singular. In the following we refer to

as the geometric side and
\partial\textup{Eis}_{T}(\mathsf{z},\Delta(N)^{2})
the analytic side.

1.3.1.
The singular coefficients of Eisenstein series

Our primary goals in the analytic side are: (a). When the rank of
T
is 1, we relate the singular Fourier coefficients of the Eisenstein series
E_{T}(\mathsf{z},s,\Delta(N)^{2})
to nonsingular Fourier coefficients of another Eisenstein series of lower genus. (b). When
T=0
, we compute the exact value of
\partial\textup{Eis}_{\mathbf{0}_{2}}(\mathsf{z},\Delta(N)^{2})
.

Before explaining the main idea, we give some necessary definitions. For any two integral quadratic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattices
L
and
M
. Let
\textup{Rep}_{M,L}
be the scheme of integral representations, an
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-scheme such that for any
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-algebra
R
,
\textup{Rep}_{M,L}(R)=\textup{QHom}(L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}R,M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}R)
, where
QHom
denotes the set of quadratic module homomorphisms. The local density of integral representations is defined to be

\textup{Den}(M,L)=\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\textup{Rep}_{M,L}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}/p^{d})}{p^{d\cdot\textup{dim}(\textup{Rep}_{M,L})_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}}.

Let
H_{2}^{+}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}
be the rank 2 quadratic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattice equipped with the quadratic form
q_{H_{2}^{+}}(x,y)=xy
. For any positive integer
k
, let
H_{2k}^{+}=\left(H_{2}^{+}\right)^{\obot k}
be the rank
2k
quadratic lattice obtained by orthogonal direct sum of
k
copies of
H_{2}^{+}
.

Let’s explain the idea shortly. As is well-known, the Fourier coefficients of Eisenstein series are (linear combinations) of Whittaker functions which are essentially products of local representation densities of quadratic lattices. Our main tool is the difference formula of local representation densities of quadratic lattices proved in
[
Zhu23
, §7.2]
(see also Theorem
2.2.5
) which roughly says that for any number
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and any quadratic lattice
M
, the following two local densities are related

(6)

\textup{Den}(\langle-l\rangle\obot H_{2k+2}^{+},M)\leftrightarrow\textup{Den}(H_{2k+4}^{+},M\obot\langle l\rangle).

This relation has two applications:

(a)
.  Let
l=N
, the quadratic lattice
\langle-l\rangle\obot H_{2k+2}^{+}
is isometric to
\delta_{p}(N)\obot H_{2k}^{+}
. The relation (
6
) reduces the computation of a local density function with level structure to that of an unramified local density function, which has very explicit expression and functional equation by the works of Cho and Yamauchi
[
CY20
, (3.4)]
, Ikeda
[
Ike17
, Theorem 4.1]
, see also the works of Li and Zhang
[
LZ22
,
\lx@sectionsign
3]
.

(b)
.  Taking the limit
\nu_{p}(l)\rightarrow\infty
, the right-hand side of (
6
) converges to the Whittaker function with singular coefficient, while the left-hand side of (
6
) converges to
\textup{Den}(H_{2k+2}^{+},M)
(Lemma
2.2.4
), i.e., we have the following relation,

(7)

\textup{Den}(H_{2k+2}^{+},M)=\lim\limits_{\nu_{p}(l)\rightarrow\infty}\textup{Den}(H_{2k+4}^{+},M\obot\langle l\rangle).

When the rank of
T
is 1, we assume
T=\textup{diag}\{0,t\}
with
t\neq 0
for simplicity, let
\mathsf{y}=\textup{diag}\{y_{1},y_{2}\}
be a positive definite symmetric matrix, then there is a well-known relation (see
[
GS19
, Lemma 5.4]
)

E_{T}(i\mathsf{y},s,\Delta(N)^{2})=y_{1}^{s/2}y_{2}^{-3/4}W_{t}(g_{iy_{2}},s+\frac{1}{2},\Delta(N))+(y_{1}y_{2})^{-3/4}W_{T}(g_{i\mathsf{y}},s,\Delta(N)^{2}),

where both of the terms
W_{t}(g_{iy_{2}},s+\frac{1}{2},\Delta(N))
and
W_{T}(g_{i\mathsf{y}},s,\Delta(N)^{2})
are product of local Whittaker functions (see (
13
) and (
14
) for the precise definition).

Since
t\neq 0
, the term
W_{t}(g_{iy_{2}},s+\frac{1}{2},\Delta(N))
is already the nonsingular Fourier coefficient of an Eisenstein series of the lower genus. Let’s now consider the term
W_{T}(g_{i\mathsf{y}},s,\Delta(N)^{2})
, which is the product of local Whittaker functions
W_{T,v}
over all the places
v
of
\mathbb{Q}
. The relation between
W_{T,v}
and
W_{t,v}
is sketched in the following way: Let
p
be a finite prime, for any integer
m
, let
T_{m}=\textup{diag}\{p^{m},t\}
be a
2\times 2
nonsingular matrix. By the well-known relation between Whittaker functions and local densities (Proposition
3.2.1
), we have

W_{T,p}(1,k,1_{\delta_{p}(N)^{2}})=\lim\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}W_{T_{m},p}(1,k,1_{\delta_{p}(N)^{2}})\leftrightarrow\textup{Den}(\delta_{p}(N)\obot H_{2k}^{+},\langle t\rangle\obot\langle p^{m}\rangle).

Applying
(a)
of (
6
) twice,

(8)

\textup{Den}(\delta_{p}(N)\obot H_{2k}^{+},\langle t\rangle\obot\langle p^{m}\rangle)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l=N}}{{\leftrightarrow}}\textup{Den}(H_{2k+4}^{+},\langle t\rangle\obot\langle p^{m}\rangle\obot\langle N\rangle)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l=p^{m}}}{{\leftrightarrow}}\textup{Den}(\langle-p^{m}\rangle\obot H_{2k+2}^{+},\langle t\rangle\obot\langle N\rangle).

Taking limit
m\rightarrow\infty
, the formula (
7
) tells us that

(9)

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\textup{Den}(\langle-p^{m}\rangle\obot H_{2k+2}^{+},\langle t\rangle\obot\langle N\rangle)=\textup{Den}(H_{2k+2}^{+},\langle t\rangle\obot\langle N\rangle).

Applying
(a)
again, we get

(10)

\textup{Den}(H_{2k+2}^{+},\langle t\rangle\obot\langle N\rangle)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l=N}}{{\leftrightarrow}}\textup{Den}(\delta_{p}(N)\obot H_{2k-2}^{+},\langle t\rangle).

The last term
\textup{Den}(\delta_{p}(N)\obot H_{2k-2}^{+},\langle t\rangle)
is clearly related to
W_{t,p}(1,k-\frac{1}{2},1_{\delta_{p}(N)})
by Proposition

. Therefore we have successfully built the bridge from

and

for a finite prime

However, we warn the reader that all the symbols “

” are not exact equal, it just means that there are some relations between the two sides of the symbol. Detailed calculations based on the principles above are given in
\lx@sectionsign
4
. Our final result is the following proposition.

Proposition 1.3.1

(Proposition
4.2.4
)
.

Let
T
be a

matrix of rank 1 which can be diagonalized to
\textup{diag}\{0,t\}

\displaystyle=y_{1}^{k/2}E_{t}(iy_{2},\frac{1}{2}+k,\Delta(N))

\displaystyle+y_{1}^{-k/2}\cdot\frac{k-1}{k+1}\cdot N^{-k}\cdot\frac{\Lambda(2-2k)}{\Lambda(2+2k)}\cdot\prod\limits_{p|N}\beta_{p}(k)\cdot E_{t}(iy_{2},\frac{1}{2}-k,\Delta(N)),

The same formula in Proposition

When

, all the local Whittaker functions

1.3.2.
Vertical components of the Hodge line bundle

The primary goal on the geometric side is to compute the height pairings of (metrized) special divisors
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(t,y,\Delta(N))
and the modified metrized Hodge line bundle
\widehat{\omega}
, and the self-intersection numbers of the metrized line bundle
\widehat{\omega}
.

The main difficulty comes from the fact that the bundle
\widehat{\omega}
contains vertical components. These components should be a linear combination of irreducible components of the reduction mod
p
of the stack
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
for prime numbers
p
. We need to know the explicit multiplicities of these irreducible components appearing in
\widehat{\omega}
and their intersections.

We first study the special fiber of the stack
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
. Let
p
be a prime number, by the works of Katz and Mazur, if
n=\nu_{p}(N)\geq 0
is the
p
-adic valuation of the number
N
, then
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)_{p}\coloneqq\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)\times_{\textup{Spec}\,\mathbb{Z}}\textup{Spec}\,\mathbb{F}_{p}
has
n+1
irreducible components
\mathcal{X}_{p}^{a}(N)
and they meet each other at every supersingular point, where the index
a
satisfies that
-n\leq a\leq n
and has the same parity with
n
. Moreover, every component
\mathcal{X}_{p}^{a}(N)
has two natural morphisms to the stack
\mathcal{X}_{0}(Np^{-n})_{p}
, one of them is an isomorphism while the other one is finite flat of degree
p^{|a|}
(see Theorem
5.4.2
). Later in
\lx@sectionsign
6.3
, we compute the intersection numbers between these irreducible components based on the explicit local equations of these components at supersingular points obtained by Katz and Mazur
[
KM85
, Theorem 13.4.7]
(see also
[
Zhu23
, Corollary 6.2.7]
).

After that, in
\lx@sectionsign
6.4
we use an explicit rational section
\Delta_{N}
of the bundle
\widehat{\omega}_{N}^{\otimes 12\varphi(N)}
to give an explicit expression of this bundle in the Chow group
\textup{CH}^{1}(\mathcal{X}_{0}(N))
by computing the element
\textup{div}(\Delta_{N})\in\textup{CH}^{1}(\mathcal{X}_{0}(N))
(Theorem
45
), this idea originates from the work of Du and Yang
[
DY19
]
, where they consider the case that
N
is squarefree.

Our idea of computing the element
\textup{div}(\Delta_{N})
for general
N
comes from the observation that there is a correspondence between cusps and the special fibers of the stack
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
. Let’s explain this in the specific case that
N=p^{n}
for some integer
n\geq 0
. There is a cuspidal divisor
\mathsf{Cusp}(\mathcal{X}_{0}(p^{n}))
on the stack
\mathcal{X}_{0}(p^{n})
, it is a disjoint union of
n+1
connected components (Proposition
5.3.1
),

\mathsf{Cusp}(\mathcal{X}_{0}(p^{n}))=\coprod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}-n\leq a\leq n\\
a\equiv n\,\textup{mod}\,2\end{subarray}}\mathsf{C}^{a}(p^{n}).

Note that the index set is exactly the same as the index set for the irreducible components of the stack
\mathcal{X}_{0}(p^{n})_{p}
! Actually we will prove that the connected component
\mathsf{C}^{a}(p^{n})
pulls back to the cusp of the curve
\mathcal{X}_{p}^{a}(p^{n})
(Proposition
5.4.5
). Then we pick a specific cusp lying in the component
\mathsf{C}^{a}(p^{n})
and consider the local expansion of the section
\Delta_{p^{n}}
around this point, the multiplicity of
p
appearing in the local expansion gives the multiplicity of the vertical component of
\mathcal{X}_{p}^{a}(p^{n})
in the element
\textup{div}(\Delta_{p^{n}})
. The final result is

\textup{div}(\Delta_{N})=\psi(N)\varphi(N)P_{\infty}(N)+\sum\limits_{p|N}f_{p}(N),

where
P_{\infty}(N)
is the connected component of the cuspidal divisor containing the cusp
\infty
, and for any
p|N
,
f_{p}(N)
is the following vertical divisor,

f_{p}(N)=12p^{n-1}\varphi(N_{p})\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}-n\leq a<n\\
a\equiv n\,\textup{mod 2}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1-p}{2}(n-a)-1\right)\varphi(p^{(n-|a|)/2})\cdot\mathcal{X}_{p}^{a}(N).

This expression is the main tool to compute the self intersection of the modified Hodge bundle
\widehat{\omega}
and the intersection pairing
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}(t,y,\Delta(N))\cdot\widehat{\omega}
.

2.1.
Notations on quadratic lattices

Let
p
be a prime number. Let
F
be a nonarchimedean local field of residue characteristic
p
, with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, residue field
\kappa=\mathbb{F}_{q}
of size
q
, and uniformizer
\pi
. Let
\nu_{\pi}:F\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
be the valuation on
F
and
|\cdot|:F\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be the normalized absolute value on
F
. Let
(\cdot,\cdot)_{F}
be the Hilbert symbol on the local field
F
.

A quadratic lattice
(L,q_{L})
is a finite free
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-module equipped with a quadratic form
q_{L}:L\rightarrow F
. The quadratic form
q_{L}
also induces a symmetric bilinear form
L\times L\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\cdot,\cdot)}}{{\longrightarrow}}F
by
(x,y)=q_{L}(x+y)-q_{L}(x)-q_{L}(y)
. Let
L^{\vee}=\{x\in L\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}F:(x,L)\subset\mathcal{O}_{F}\}
. We say a quadratic lattice is integral if
q_{L}(x)\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
for all
x\in L
, is self-dual if it is integral and
L
=
L^{\vee}
. There are three invariants associated to a quadratic lattice: the rank of
L
over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
, the discriminant
\chi(L)\in\{0,\pm 1\}
and the Hasse invariant
\epsilon(L)\in\{\pm 1\}
. We refer the readers to
[
Zhu23
, §2]
and
[
LZ22
, §2]
for details about the last two invariants.

When
p
is odd, we use
H_{k}^{\varepsilon}
to denote the unique (up to isometry) self-dual lattice of rank
k
and discriminant
\chi(L)=\varepsilon
. When
p=2
, let
H_{2n}^{+}=(H_{2}^{+})^{\obot n}
be a self-dual lattice of rank
2n
, where the quadratic form on
H_{2}^{+}=\mathcal{O}_{F}^{2}
is given by
(x,y)\in\mathcal{O}_{F}^{2}\mapsto xy
.

Example 2.1.1
.

Let
N\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Let
\delta_{F}(N)
be the following rank 3 quadratic lattice over
\mathcal{O}_{F}
,

\delta_{F}(N)=\left\{x=\begin{pmatrix}-Na&b\\
c&a\end{pmatrix}:\,\,a,b,c\in\mathcal{O}_{F}\right\}.

equipped with the quadratic form induced by
x\mapsto\textup{det}(x)
. Under the following basis of
\delta_{F}(N)
,

e_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}-N&\\
&1\end{pmatrix},\,\,e_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\,&1\\
\,&\end{pmatrix},\,\,e_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}\,&\,\\
1&\,\end{pmatrix}.

the quadratic form can be represented by the following symmetric matrix,

T=\begin{pmatrix}-N&0&0\\
0&0&-\frac{1}{2}\\
0&-\frac{1}{2}&0\end{pmatrix}.

therefore
\chi(\delta_{F}(N))=\left(\frac{-N}{p}\right)
, where
\left(\frac{\cdot}{p}\right)
is the extended quadratic residue symbol (see
[
Zhu23
, §2.1]
), and
\epsilon(\delta_{F}(N))=(N,-1)_{F}
. Moreover,

\delta_{F}(N)^{\vee}=\left\{x=\begin{pmatrix}-Na&b\\
c&a\end{pmatrix}:\,\,a\in\frac{1}{2N}\mathcal{O}_{F},b,c\in\mathcal{O}_{F}\right\}.

therefore
\delta_{F}(N)^{\vee}/\delta_{F}(N)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{F}/2N
.

Throughout this article, we will mainly focus on the case that
F=\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. In this case, we simply use
\delta_{p}(N)
to denote the lattice
\delta_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(N)
(as we did in the introduction).

2.2.
Local densities of quadratic lattices

Definition 2.2.1
.

Let
L,M
be two quadratic
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-lattices. Let
\textup{Rep}_{M,L}
be the scheme of integral representations, an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-scheme such that for any
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-algebra
R
,

\textup{Rep}_{M,L}(R)=\textup{QHom}(L\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}R,M\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}R),

where QHom denotes the set of injective module homomorphisms which preserve the quadratic forms. The local density of integral representations is defined to be

\textup{Den}(M,L)=\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\textup{Rep}_{M,L}(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\pi^{d})}{q^{d\cdot\textup{dim}(\textup{Rep}_{M,L})_{F}}}.

Definition 2.2.2
.

Let
L,M
be two quadratic
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-lattices. Let
\textup{PRep}_{M,L}
be the
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-scheme of primitive integral representations such that for any
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-algebra
R
,

\textup{PRep}_{M,L}(R)=\{\phi\in\textup{Rep}_{M,L}(R):\textup{$\phi$ is an isomorphism between $L_{R}$ and a direct summand of $M_{R}$}\}.

where
L_{R}
(resp.
M_{R}
) is
L\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}R
(resp.
M\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{F}}R
). The primitive local density is defined to be

\textup{Pden}(M,L)=\lim\limits_{d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\textup{PRep}_{M,L}(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\pi^{d})}{q^{d\cdot\textup{dim}(\textup{Rep}_{M,L})_{F}}}.

For any
N\in F
, let
(\langle N\rangle,q_{\langle N\rangle})
be the rank 1
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-lattice with an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
generator
l_{N}
such that
q_{\langle N\rangle}(l_{N})=N
. For
a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n}\in F
, we define
\langle a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n}\rangle\coloneqq\langle a_{1}\rangle\obot\langle a_{2}\rangle\obot\cdots\obot\langle a_{n}\rangle
.

Example 2.2.3
.

When
p
odd, it has been calculated explicitly that for any
N\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
(cf.
[
LZ22
, (3.3.2.1)]
),

(11)

\textup{Pden}(H_{k}^{\varepsilon},\langle N\rangle)=\begin{cases}1-q^{1-k},&\textup{when $k$ is odd and $\pi\,|\,N$;}\\
1+\varepsilon\chi_{F}(N)q^{(1-k)/2},&\textup{when $k$ is odd and $\pi\nmid N$;}\\
(1-\varepsilon q^{-k/2})(1+\varepsilon q^{1-k/2}),&\textup{when $k$ is even and $\pi\,|\,N$;}\\
1-\varepsilon q^{-k/2},&\textup{when $k$ is even and $\pi\nmid N$.}\end{cases}

When
p=2
, the same formula makes sense and holds true only in the case that
k
is even and
\varepsilon=+1
.

Lemma 2.2.4
.

Let
L,M
be two quadratic
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-lattices such that the quadratic form on
L
is nondegenerate, then there exists a positive number
m
such that for any
a\in\mathcal{O}_{F}
with
\nu_{\pi}(a)\geq m
, the following identity holds,

\textup{Den}(\langle a\rangle\obot M,L)=\textup{Den}(M,L).

Proof.

We assume that the rank of
L
is
n
and the rank of
M
is
m
. Let
\{l_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be an
\mathcal{O}_{F}
-basis of
L
, and let
T=(t_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
be the moment matrix of the this basis under the quadratic form on
L
. For any positive integer
d
, we have

\displaystyle\textup{Rep}_{\langle a\rangle\obot M,L}(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\pi^{d})=\{(\overline{m}_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,\overline{m}_{n}^{\prime})\in(\langle a\rangle/\pi^{d}\langle a\rangle\obot M/\pi^{d}M)^{n}:(\overline{m}_{i}^{\prime},\overline{m}_{j}^{\prime})\equiv 2t_{ij}\,\textup{mod}\,2\pi^{d}\,\,\textup{for}\,i,j\geq 1.\}.

For any element
(\overline{m}_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,\overline{m}_{n}^{\prime})\in(\langle a\rangle/\pi^{d}\langle a\rangle\obot M/\pi^{d}M)^{n}
in the set
\textup{Rep}_{\langle a\rangle\obot M,L}(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\pi^{d})
, let
(\overline{m}_{1}^{\prime\prime},\cdots,\overline{m}_{n}^{\prime\prime})\in(M/\pi^{d}M)^{n}
be the
M/\pi^{d}M
-component of this element. Let
(m_{1}^{\prime\prime},\cdots,m_{n}^{\prime\prime})\in M^{n}
be an arbitrary lift of the element
(\overline{m}_{1}^{\prime\prime},\cdots,\overline{m}_{n}^{\prime\prime})
to
M
, when
\nu_{\pi}(a)
and
d
are sufficiently large, the sublattice spanned by
(m_{1}^{\prime\prime},\cdots,m_{n}^{\prime\prime})
inside
M
is isometric to
L
due to the local constancy of equivalent class of nondegenerate quadratic lattice (see the work of Durfee
[
Dur44
]
).

Therefore when
\nu_{\pi}(a)
and
d
are sufficiently large, we have a natural map by taking the
M/\pi^{d}M
-component,

\textup{Rep}_{\langle a\rangle\obot M,L}(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\pi^{d})\longrightarrow\textup{Rep}_{M,L}(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\pi^{d}).

hence
\#\textup{Rep}_{\langle a\rangle\obot M,L}(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\pi^{d})=q^{nd}\cdot\#\textup{Rep}_{M,L}(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\pi^{d})
.

Notice that
\textup{dim}(\textup{Rep}_{M,L}=mn-\frac{n(n+1)}{2}
and
\textup{dim}(\textup{Rep}_{\langle a\rangle\obot M,L}=(m+1)n-\frac{n(n+1)}{2}
, hence when
\nu_{\pi}(a)
and
d
are sufficiently large,

\frac{\#\textup{Rep}_{\langle a\rangle\obot M,L}(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\pi^{d})}{q^{d\cdot\textup{dim}(\textup{Rep}_{\langle a\rangle\obot M,L})}}=\frac{\#\textup{Rep}_{M,L}(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\pi^{d})}{q^{d\cdot\textup{dim}(\textup{Rep}_{M,L})}}.

Taking limit
d\rightarrow\infty
, we get
\textup{Den}(\langle a\rangle\obot M,L)=\textup{Den}(M,L)
under the assumption that
\nu_{\pi}(a)
is sufficiently large.
∎

3.1.
Incoherent Eisenstein series

For an positive integer
r
, let
\mathsf{W}_{r}
be the standard symplectic space over
\mathbb{Q}
of dimension 2
r
. Let
P=MN\subset\textup{Sp}(\mathsf{W}_{r})
be the standard Siegel parabolic subgroup, which take the following form under the standard basis of
\mathsf{W}_{r}
,

\displaystyle M(\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle=\left\{m(a)=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&{}^{t}a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}:a\in\textup{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{Q})\right\},

\displaystyle N(\mathbb{Q})

\displaystyle=\left\{n(b)=\begin{pmatrix}1_{2}&b\\
0&1_{2}\end{pmatrix}:b\in\textup{Sym}_{r}(\mathbb{Q})\right\}.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be the ad
\grave{\textup{e}}
le ring over
\mathbb{Q}
. There is an isomorphism
\textup{Mp}(\mathsf{W}_{r,\mathbb{A}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\textup{Sp}(\mathsf{W}_{r})({\mathbb{A}})\times\mathbb{C}^{1}
with the multiplication on the latter is given by the global Rao cycle, therefore we can write an element of
\textup{Mp}(\mathsf{W}_{r,\mathbb{A}})
as
(g,t)
where
g\in\textup{Sp}(\mathsf{W}_{r})({\mathbb{A}})
and
t\in\mathbb{C}^{1}
.

Let
P(\mathbb{A})=M(\mathbb{A})N(\mathbb{A})
be the standard Siegel parabolic subgroup of
\textup{Mp}(\mathsf{W}_{r,\mathbb{A}})
where

\displaystyle M(\mathbb{A})

\displaystyle=\left\{(m(a),t):a\in\textup{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{A}),t\in\mathbb{C}^{1}\right\},

\displaystyle N(\mathbb{A})

\displaystyle=\left\{n(b):b\in\textup{Sym}_{r}(\mathbb{A})\right\}.

Recall the following incoherent collection of rank
3
quadratic spaces
\mathbb{V}=\{\mathbb{V}_{v}\}
over
\mathbb{A}
we defined in (
2
),

\mathbb{V}_{v}=\delta_{v}(N)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{v}\,\,\textup{if $v<\infty$, and $\mathbb{V}_{\infty}$ is positive definite.}

then we can verify immediately that
\prod\limits_{v}\epsilon(\mathbb{V}_{v})=-1
.

Let
\chi:\mathbb{A}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be the quadratic character given by
\chi(x)=\prod\limits_{v\leq\infty}\chi_{v}(x_{v})=\prod\limits_{v\leq\infty}(x_{v},-N)_{v}
for all
x=(x_{v})\in\mathbb{A}^{\times}
. Fix the standard additive character
\psi:\mathbb{A}/\mathbb{Q}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
\psi_{\infty}(x)=e^{2\pi ix}
. We may view
\chi
as a character on
M(\mathbb{A})
by

\chi(m(a),t)=\chi(\textup{det}(a))\cdot\gamma(\textup{det}(a),\psi)^{-1}\cdot t.

and extend it to
P(\mathbb{A})
trivially on
N(\mathbb{A})
. Here
\gamma(\textup{det}(a),\psi)
is the Weil index (see the work of Kudla
[
Kud97
, p. 548]
). We define the degenerate principal series to be the unnormalized smooth induction

I(s,\chi)\coloneqq\textup{Ind}_{P(\mathbb{A})}^{\textup{Mp}(\mathsf{W}_{r,\mathbb{A}})}(\chi\cdot|\cdot|_{\mathbb{Q}}^{s+(r+1)/2}),\,\,\,s\in\mathbb{C}.

For a standard section
\Phi(-,s)\in I(s,\chi)
(i.e., its restriction to the standard maximal compact subgroup of

E(g,s,\Phi)=\sum\limits_{\gamma\in P(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\textup{Sp}(\mathbb{Q})}\Phi(\gamma g,s),

Recall that
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{V}^{r})
is the space of Schwartz functions on
\mathbb{V}^{r}
. The fixed choice of

In this paper, we will mainly work on the case that

3.2.

Let

-1_{r}&0\end{pmatrix}.

The fixed choice of
\chi_{v}
and

3.3.

We have a Fourier expansion of the Siegel Eisenstein series defined above,

E(g,s,\boldsymbol{\varphi})=\sum\limits_{T\in\textup{Sym}_{r}(\mathbb{Q})}E_{T}(g,s,\boldsymbol{\varphi}),

This lemma follows from unravelling the integral defining the function

Part II
The Geometric Side

5.
The geometry of the modular curve
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)

5.1.
Integral model

Let
N
be a positive integer, the classical modular curve
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)_{\mathbb{C}}
over
\mathbb{C}
is defined as the following smooth
1
-dimensional complex curve,

\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)_{\mathbb{C}}\coloneqq\textup{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathbb{H}^{\pm}\times\textup{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})/\Gamma_{0}(N)(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}^{+},

where
\mathbb{H}^{\pm}=\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{H}^{+}=\{z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}:x\in\mathbb{R},y\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\}
is the upper half plane. The group
\Gamma_{0}(N)(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
is the following open compact subgroup of
\textup{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
,

\Gamma_{0}(N)(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})=\left\{x=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
Nc&d\end{pmatrix}\in\textup{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})\,\,:\,\,a,b,c,d\in\hat{\mathbb{Z}}\right\},

and
\Gamma_{0}(N)=\Gamma_{0}(N)(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})\bigcap\textup{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. The modular curve
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)_{\mathbb{C}}
parametrizes cyclic
N
-isogenies of elliptic curves over the complex number
\mathbb{C}
by the following map:

\Gamma_{0}(N)\tau\in\Gamma_{0}(N)\backslash\mathbb{H}^{+}\cdot\tau\longmapsto(E_{\tau}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\times N}}{{\rightarrow}}E_{N\tau}),

where for a given
\tau\in\mathbb{H}^{+}
, the elliptic curve
E_{\tau}
is given by the complex torus
\mathbb{C}/(\mathbb{Z}+\mathbb{Z}\tau)
. The smooth curve
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)_{\mathbb{C}}
is not proper, its compactification
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)_{\mathbb{C}}\coloneqq\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)_{\mathbb{C}}\cup\{\textup{cusps}\}
is a smooth projective curve over
\mathbb{C}
.

Katz and Mazur
[
KM85
]
constructs an integral model of
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)
over
\mathbb{Z}
by extending the concept of cyclic isogeny to arbitrary base scheme, we refer the readers to
[
KM85
, §3(3.4)]
and the arthor’s previous work
[
Zhu23
, §4]
for details. The moduli interpretation of the stack
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)
is given as follows: for a scheme
S
, the objects of the groupoid
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)(S)
are cyclic
N
-isogenies
(E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}E^{\prime})
where
E
and
E^{\prime}
are elliptic curves over
S
, a morphism between two cyclic isogenies
(E_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{1}}}{{\rightarrow}}E_{1}^{\prime})
and
(E_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi_{2}}}{{\rightarrow}}E_{2}^{\prime})
is a pair of isomorphisms of elliptic curves
a:E_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}E_{2}
and
a^{\prime}:E_{1}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}E_{2}^{\prime}
such that
a^{\prime}\circ\pi_{1}=\pi_{2}\circ a
.

Cesnavicius
[
Ces17
]
defined a compactification of
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)
as the moduli stack of cyclic
N
-isogenies between generalized elliptic curves, we denote this compactification by
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
, its complex fiber is isomorphic to
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)_{\mathbb{C}}
.

There are two natural morphisms from
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)
to
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(1)
given as follows

\displaystyle p_{1},p_{2}:\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}_{0}(1)

\displaystyle(E_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}E_{2})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{1}}}{{\longmapsto}}E_{1};

\displaystyle(E_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}E_{2})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{2}}}{{\longmapsto}}E_{2}.

Both of the morphism
p_{1},p_{2}:\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}_{0}(1)
extends to morphisms from
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
to
\mathcal{X}_{0}(1)
, we still use the symbols
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
to denote them.

Theorem 5.1.1
.

The stack
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
is a regular proper 2-dimensional Deligne-Mumford stack, both of the morphisms
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are finite flat of degree
\psi(N)\coloneqq N\cdot\prod\limits_{p|N}(1+p^{-1})
over
\mathcal{X}_{0}(1)
. Moreover, the stack
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
is smooth over
\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{N}]

Proof.

This is proved in
[
Ces17
, Theorem 5.13]
, the degree of
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are computed in
[
KM85
, (13.4.9)]
.
∎

Let
N=M_{1}\cdot M_{2}
where

, there is a natural isomorphism

5.2.
The Atkin-Lehner involution on
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)

Lemma 5.2.1
.

Let
S
be a scheme, and
\pi:E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
be a cyclic
N
-isogeny between elliptic curves over
S
, then
\pi^{\vee}:E^{\prime}\rightarrow E
is also a cyclic
N
-isogeny.

Proof.

We only need to show that the order
N
quotient group scheme
E[N]/\textup{ker}(\pi)
is cyclic, and this is proved in
[
KM85
, Corollary 5.5.4(3)]
.
∎

With Lemma
5.2.1
, we can define the Atkin-Lehner involution
W_{N}
on the stack
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)
by the following,

\displaystyle W_{N}:\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)

\displaystyle(E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}E^{\prime})

\displaystyle\longmapsto(E^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi^{\vee}}}{{\rightarrow}}E)

Lemma 5.2.2
.

The Atkin-Lehner involution
W_{N}
extends to the compactified stack
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
.

Proof.

By
[
Ces17
, Proposition 4.2.7 (c)]
, the cyclicity condition of a
N
-isogeny is closed, hence
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
is a closed substack of
\mathcal{X}_{0}(1)\times\mathcal{X}_{0}(1)
. Let
\iota:\mathcal{X}_{0}(1)\times\mathcal{X}_{0}(1)\rightarrow\mathcal{X}_{0}(1)\times\mathcal{X}_{0}(1)
be the involution which switches the two copies of
\mathcal{X}_{0}(1)
, let
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)^{\prime}=\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)_{\mathcal{X}_{0}(1)\times\mathcal{X}_{0}(1),\iota}\mathcal{X}_{0}(1)\times\mathcal{X}_{0}(1)
be the image of
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
under the involution
W_{N}
, it is also a closed substack of
\mathcal{X}_{0}(1)\times\mathcal{X}_{0}(1)
, but
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
and
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)^{\prime}
have common dense open dense substack
\mathcal{Y}_{0}(N)
, hence
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)=\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)^{\prime}
, therefore the Atkin-Lehner involution
W_{N}
extends to the compactified stack
\mathcal{X}_{0}(N)
.
∎

and

Let

Proof.

Let first assume

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{P\in\mathcal{X}^{\textup{ss}}_{0}(N)(\mathbb{F})}\frac{1}{\#\textup{Aut}(P)}\cdot p^{\textup{min}\{a,b\}}\cdot\log(p).

By (e) of Theorem

Recall that the principal arithmetic divisor associated to the constant function

therefore formula (

6.4.
An explicit section of the Hodge line bundle

In this section, we define and study the associated divisor class of an explicit rational section of the line bundle
\widehat{\omega}_{N}^{\otimes 12\varphi(N)}
.

For any positive integer
N
, define the following function
a_{N}
on the set of positive integers,

a_{N}(t)=\sum\limits_{r|t}\mu(\frac{t}{r})\mu(\frac{N}{r})\frac{\varphi(N)}{\varphi(N/r)},

where
\mu(\cdot)
is the Möbius function and
r
ranges over all the positive integers dividing
t
.

Lemma 6.4.1
.

The function
a_{N}
has the following properties,

(a).
\sum\limits_{t|N}a_{N}(t)=\varphi(N)
and
\sum\limits_{t|N}t^{-1}a_{N}(t)=0
.

(b). Let
p
be a prime number such that
p|N
, for any positive integer
t
such that
pt|N
,

a_{N}(pt)=pa_{p^{-1}N}(t).

Proof.

The part (a) has been proved in
[
DY19
, Lemma 3.2]
. We prove part (b) as follows. Let
n=\nu_{p}(N)\geq 1
be the
p
-adic valuation of the integer
N
, and
N_{p}=p^{-n}N
. By definition,

\displaystyle a_{N}(pt)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{r|pt}\mu(\frac{pt}{r})\mu(\frac{N}{r})\frac{\varphi(N)}{\varphi(N/r)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|pt\\
p\nmid r\end{subarray}}\mu(\frac{pt}{r})\mu(\frac{N}{r})\frac{\varphi(N)}{\varphi(N/r)}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|pt\\
p|r\end{subarray}}\mu(\frac{pt}{r})\mu(\frac{N}{r})\frac{\varphi(N)}{\varphi(N/r)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}r|pt\\
p\nmid r\end{subarray}}\mu(\frac{pt}{r})\mu(\frac{N}{r})\frac{\varphi(N)}{\varphi(N/r)}+\sum\limits_{r^{\prime}|t}\mu(\frac{t}{r^{\prime}})\mu(\frac{p^{-1}N}{r^{\prime}})\frac{\varphi(N)}{\varphi(p^{-1}N/r^{\prime})}

If
n\geq 2
, we have
\mu(\frac{N}{r})=0
when
p\nmid r
, then

\displaystyle a_{N}(pt)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{r^{\prime}|t}\mu(\frac{t}{r})\mu(\frac{p^{-1}N}{r^{\prime}})\frac{\varphi(N)}{\varphi(p^{-1}N/r^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=p\sum\limits_{r^{\prime}|t}\mu(\frac{t}{r^{\prime}})\mu(\frac{p^{-1}N}{r^{\prime}})\frac{\varphi(p^{-1}N)}{\varphi(p^{-1}N/r^{\prime})}=pa_{p^{-1}N}(t).

If
n=1
, we have
t|p^{-1}N=N_{p}
, especially,
p\nmid t
,

\displaystyle a_{N}(pt)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{r|pt}\mu(\frac{pt}{r})\mu(\frac{N}{r})\frac{\varphi(N)}{\varphi(N/r)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{r|t}\mu(\frac{pt}{r})\mu(\frac{N}{r})\frac{\varphi(N)}{\varphi(N/r)}+\sum\limits_{r^{\prime}|t}\mu(\frac{t}{r^{\prime}})\mu(\frac{p^{-1}N}{r^{\prime}})\frac{\varphi(N)}{\varphi(p^{-1}N/r^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{r|t}\mu(\frac{t}{r})\mu(\frac{p^{-1}N}{r})\frac{\varphi(p^{-1}N)}{\varphi(p^{-1}N/r)}+\sum\limits_{r^{\prime}|t}\mu(\frac{t}{r^{\prime}})\mu(\frac{p^{-1}N}{r^{\prime}})\frac{(p-1)\varphi(p^{-1}N)}{\varphi(p^{-1}N/r^{\prime})}=pa_{p^{-1}N}(t).

∎

Let

Corollary 6.4.4
.

\widehat{\omega}_{N}-W_{N}^{\ast}\widehat{\omega}_{N}=\sum\limits_{p|N}\widehat{\mathcal{X}}_{p}(N).

2
Abstract homogeneity over IV-functions

The following definition naturally extends abstract homogeneity of real-valued functions to interval-valued ones.

Definition 2.1

(Abstract homogeneous IV-function)
.

Let be
G:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{2}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
and
\varPhi:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
be an order isomorphism. An IV-function
F:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
is said to be
abstract homogeneous with respect to
G
and
\varPhi
, or
(G,\varPhi)
-homogeneous
, if

F\big(G(\Lambda,X_{1}),\ldots,G(\Lambda,X_{n})\big)=G\big(\varPhi(\Lambda),F(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\big)

for all
\Lambda,X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
. If
\varPhi
is the identity, then
F
is simple said to be
G
-homogeneous
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\pi_{2}:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{2}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
be given by
\pi_{2}(X_{1},X_{2})=X_{2}
for all
(X_{1},X_{2})\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{2}
.Then every IV-function
F:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
is
\pi_{2}
-homogeneous.

Proof.

Let
\pi_{2}:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{2}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
be given by
\pi_{2}(X_{1},X_{2})=X_{2}
for all
(X_{1},X_{2})\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{2}
, and let
F:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
be an IV-function. Then for all
\Lambda,X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
,

F\big(\pi_{2}(\Lambda,X_{1}),\ldots,\pi_{2}(\Lambda,X_{n})\big)=F(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})=\pi_{2}\big(\Lambda,F(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\big)

Thus
F
is
\pi_{2}
-homogeneous.
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
G:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{2}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
be a function. If
F:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
is
G
-homogeneous and there exists
A\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
such that
F(A,\ldots,A)=A
and
G_{A}:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
, given by
G_{A}(X)=G(X,A)
, is a bijection, then
F
is idempotent.

Proof.

Let
X\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
. Since
G_{A}
is a bijection on
\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
,
X=G_{A}(Y)
for some
Y\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
. Thus, from the
G
-homogeneity of
F
, it follows:

\displaystyle F(X,\ldots,X)

\displaystyle=F(G_{A}(Y),\ldots,G_{A}(Y))=F(G(Y,A),\ldots,G(Y,A))=G(Y,F(A,\ldots,A))

\displaystyle=G(Y,A)=G_{A}(Y)=X.

∎

3
Homogeneity in IV-fuzzy logic

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
F:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
be an IV-function and let
P:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{2}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
be the IV-product function, i.e. it is given by
P(X,Y)=X\cdot Y
. If
F
is
P
-homogeneous then the IV-function
F_{N_{S}}:\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])^{n}\to\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
given by
F_{N_{S}}\big(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\big)=N_{S}\big(F\big(N_{S}(X_{1}),\ldots,N_{S}(X_{n})\big)\big)
is homogeneous with respect to
P_{N_{S}}(X,Y)=N_{S}\big(P\big(N_{S}(X),N_{S}(Y)\big)\big)
.

Proof.

Due to the previous definition,

\underline{F_{N_{S}}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})}=1-\overline{F(\boldsymbol{1}-X_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{1}-X_{n})}

and

\overline{F_{N_{S}}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})}=1-\underline{F(\boldsymbol{1}-X_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{1}-X_{n})}.

Regarding
P_{N_{S}}
,

\displaystyle\underline{P_{N_{S}}(X,Y)}

\displaystyle=1-\overline{P(\boldsymbol{1}-X,\boldsymbol{1}-Y)}=1-\overline{\boldsymbol{1}-X}\cdot\overline{\boldsymbol{1}-Y}=1-(1-\underline{X})\cdot(1-\underline{Y})

\displaystyle=\underline{X}+\underline{Y}-\underline{X}\underline{Y}=\underline{X}+\left(1-\underline{X}\right)\underline{Y}.

Similarly,
\overline{P_{N_{S}}(X,Y)}=\overline{X}+\overline{Y}-\overline{X}\overline{Y}=\overline{X}+\left(1-\overline{X}\right)\overline{Y}
.

Thus, for all
\Lambda,X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
,

\displaystyle\underline{F_{N_{S}}(P_{N_{S}}(\Lambda,X_{1}),\ldots,P_{N_{S}}(\Lambda,X_{n}))}

\displaystyle=1-\overline{F(\boldsymbol{1}-P_{N_{S}}(\Lambda,X_{1}),\ldots,\boldsymbol{1}-P_{N_{S}}(\Lambda,X_{n}))}

\displaystyle=1-\overline{F(\boldsymbol{1}-[\underline{\Lambda}+(1-\underline{\Lambda})\underline{X_{1}},\overline{\Lambda}+(1-\overline{\Lambda})\overline{X_{1}}],\ldots,\boldsymbol{1}-[\underline{\Lambda}+(1-\underline{\Lambda})\underline{X_{n}},\overline{\Lambda}+(1-\overline{\Lambda})\overline{x_{n}}])}

\displaystyle=1-\overline{F\left((\boldsymbol{1}-\Lambda)(\boldsymbol{1}-X_{1}),\ldots,(\boldsymbol{1}-\Lambda)(\boldsymbol{1}-X_{n})\right)}

\displaystyle=1-\overline{F\left(P(\boldsymbol{1}-\Lambda,\boldsymbol{1}-X_{1}),\ldots,P(\boldsymbol{1}-\Lambda,\boldsymbol{1}-X_{n})\right)}

\displaystyle=1-\overline{P\left(\boldsymbol{1}-\Lambda,F(\boldsymbol{1}-X_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{1}-X_{n})\right)}\quad\text{[because of $P$-homogeneity of $F$]}

\displaystyle=1-\overline{(\boldsymbol{1}-\Lambda)\cdot F(\boldsymbol{1}-X_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{1}-X_{n})}

\displaystyle=1-\overline{\boldsymbol{1}-\Lambda}\cdot\overline{F(\boldsymbol{1}-X_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{1}-X_{n})}

\displaystyle=1-\left(1-\underline{\Lambda}\right)\cdot\overline{F(\boldsymbol{1}-X_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{1}-X_{n})}

Analogously,

\overline{F_{N_{S}}(P_{N_{S}}(\Lambda,X_{1}),\ldots,P_{N_{S}}(\Lambda,X_{n}))}=1-\left(1-\overline{\Lambda}\right)\cdot\underline{F(\boldsymbol{1}-X_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{1}-X_{n})}

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\underline{P_{N_{S}}(\Lambda,F_{N_{S}}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}))}

\displaystyle=\underline{\Lambda}+\left(1-\underline{\Lambda}\right)\cdot\underline{F_{N_{S}}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})}

\displaystyle=\underline{\Lambda}+\left(1-\underline{\Lambda}\right)\cdot\left(1-\overline{F(\boldsymbol{1}-X_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{1}-X_{n})}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\left(1-\underline{\Lambda}\right)\cdot\overline{F(\boldsymbol{1}-X_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{1}-X_{n})}.

Therefore
F_{N_{S}}
is
P_{N_{S}}
-homogeneous.
∎

Example 3.2
.

IV-functions
\min
and
\max
are
P
-homogeneous. Indeed, for all
\Lambda,X,Y\in\mathbb{I}([0,\!1])
,
\min(P(\Lambda,X),P(\Lambda,Y))=\min(\Lambda\cdot X,\Lambda\cdot Y)=\Lambda\cdot\min(X,Y)=P(\Lambda,\min(X,Y))
. Analogously,
\max(P(\Lambda,X),P(\Lambda,Y))=\Lambda\cdot\max(X,Y)=P(\Lambda,\max(X,Y)).

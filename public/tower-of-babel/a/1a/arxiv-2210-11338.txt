Degenerate Turán densities of sparse hypergraphs II: a solution to the Brown-Erdős-Sós problem for every uniformity
Chong Shangguan

Abstract
For fixed integers r ≥ 3 , e ≥ 3 r\geq 3,e\geq 3 , and v ≥ r + 1 v\geq r+1 , let f r ​ ( n , v , e ) f_{r}(n,v,e) denote the maximum number of edges in an n n -vertex r r -uniform hypergraph in which the union of arbitrary e e distinct edges contains at least v + 1 v+1 vertices. In 1973, Brown, Erdős and Sós proved that f r ​ ( n , e ​ r − ( e − 1 ) ​ k , e ) = Θ ⁡ ( n k ) f_{r}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)=\Theta(n^{k}) and conjectured that the limit lim n → ∞ f 3 ​ ( n , e + 2 , e ) n 2 \lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{3}(n,e+2,e)}{n^{2}} always exists for all fixed integers e ≥ 3 e\geq 3 . In 2020 Shangguan and Tamo conjectured that the limit lim n → ∞ f r ​ ( n , e ​ r − ( e − 1 ) ​ k , e ) n k \lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}} always exists for all fixed integers r > k ≥ 2 r>k\geq 2 and e ≥ 3 e\geq 3 , which contains the BES conjecture as a special case for r = 3 , k = 2 r=3,k=2 . Recently, based on a result of Glock, Joos, Kim, Kühn, Lichev, and Pikhurko, Delcourt and Postle proved the BES conjecture. Extending their result, we show that the limit lim n → ∞ f r ​ ( n , e ​ r − 2 ​ ( e − 1 ) , e ) n 2 \lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,er-2(e-1),e)}{n^{2}} always exists, thereby proving the BES conjecture for every uniformity.

Introduction

We will focus on the sparse hypergraph problem introduced by Brown, Erdős and Sós
[
2
]
in 1973. To move forward, we will begin with some notations.

For an integer
r\geq 2
, an
r
-uniform hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
(or
r
-graph, for short) on the vertex set
V(\mathcal{H})
, is a family of
r
-element subsets of
V(\mathcal{H})
, called the
edges
of
\mathcal{H}
.
For fixed integers
r\geq 3,e\geq 3
, and
v\geq r+1
, an
r
-graph
\mathcal{H}
is called
\mathscr{G}_{r}(v,e)
-free
if the union of arbitrary
e
distinct edges of it contains at least
v+1
vertices, namely, for arbitrary distinct
A_{1},\ldots,A_{e}\in\mathcal{H}
one has
|\cup_{i=1}^{e}A_{i}|\geq v+1
. Due to the sparsity of its edges, such
r
-graphs are also termed
sparse hypergraphs

[
8
]
. Let
f_{r}(n,v,e)
denote the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
\mathscr{G}_{r}(v,e)
-free
r
-graph.

In 1973, Brown, Erdős and Sós
[
2
]
initiated the study of the function
f_{r}(n,v,e)
, which has attracted considerable attention throughout the years.
More concretely, they showed that

\displaystyle\Omega(n^{\frac{er-v}{e-1}})=f_{r}(n,v,e)=O(n^{\lceil\frac{er-v}{e-1}\rceil}).

(1)

Improvements on lower or upper bounds of (
1
) for less general parameters were obtained in a series of works, see, e.g.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
13
,
15
,
17
,
16
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
.

In this paper we are interested in the special case where
k=\frac{er-v}{e-1}\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}
is an integer. For
k=1
the authors of
[
11
]
(see also
[
6
]
(4) for the case
r=2
) noted that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,er-(e-1),e)}{n}=\frac{(e-1)n}{(e-1)(r-1)+1}.

(2)

According to (
1
) it is easy to see that

\displaystyle f_{r}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)=\Theta(n^{k}),

(3)

and it is natural to ask whether the limit

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}

exists. For
e=2
the above question is completely resolved by a classical result of Rödl
[
14
]
. So we will focus on the case
e\geq 3
. For
e\geq 3,r=3,k=2
, Brown, Erdős and Sós
[
2
]
posed the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1

(see Section 5 in
[
2
]
)
.

The limit

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{3}(n,e+2,e)}{n^{2}}

exists for every fixed
e\geq 3
.

In 2019 Glock
[
10
]
solved the
e=3
case of
1
by showing that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{3}(n,5,3)}{n^{2}}=\frac{1}{5}
. In a recent work, Glock, Joos, Kim, Kühn, Lichev, and Pikhurko
[
11
]
solved the
e=4
case of
1
by showing that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{3}(n,6,4)}{n^{2}}=\frac{7}{36}
. Quite recently, based on a result of
[
11
]
Delcourt and Postle
[
5
]
solved
1
in the full generality, without determining the limit precisely.

In the spirit of (
3
) and Conjecture
1
Shangguan and Tamo
[
18
]
posed the following conjecture, which largely generalizes
1
:

Conjecture 2

(see Question 2 in
[
18
]
)
.

The limit
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}
exists for all fixed integers
r>k\geq 2,e\geq 3
.

In
[
18
]
, such a limit, if it exists, is called

the degenerate Turán density of sparse hypergraphs
. Extending Glock’s result from
r=3,k=2,e=3
to arbitrary fixed
r\geq 4
, Shangguan and Tamo
[
18
]
showed that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,3r-4,3)}{n^{2}}=\frac{1}{r^{2}-r-1}.

Following this line of work, Glock, Joos, Kim, Kühn, Lichev, and Pikhurko
[
11
]
proved that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,3r-2k,3)}{n^{k}}=\frac{2}{k!(2\binom{r}{k}-1)}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,4r-3k,4)}{n^{k}}=\frac{1}{k!}\binom{r}{k}^{-1},

(4)

thereby settling
2
completely for
e\in\{3,4\}
.

The main result of this paper is the following theorem, which settles
2
for
k=2
and proves
1
for every uniformity.

Theorem 3
.

The limit
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,er-2(e-1),e)}{n^{2}}
exists for all fixed integers
r\geq 3
and
e\geq 3
.

The idea of our proof is sketched as follows. Let us begin with an attempt that aims to prove the general case of
2
. We will make use of the following result proved in
[
11
]
.

Lemma 4

(see Corollary 3.2 in
[
11
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an
m
-vertex
r
-graph which simultaneously
\mathscr{G}_{r}(er-(e-1)k,e)
-free and
\mathscr{G}_{r}(ir-(i-1)k-1,i)
-free for all
2\leq i\leq e-1
. Then

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}\geq\frac{|\mathcal{F}|}{m^{k}}.

For
t\in\{2,\ldots,e-1\}
, let
f_{r}^{(t)}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)
denote the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
r
-graph which simultaneously satisfy all of the following properties:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\text{it is $\mathscr{G}_{r}(er-(e-1)k,e)$-free,
and is $\mathscr{G}_{r}(ir-(i-1)k-1,i)$-free for all $t\leq i\leq e-1$;}&\\
\text{for every $(k-1)$-subset $T$, the number of edges containing $T$ is either $0$ or at least $e$.}&\end{array}\right.\end{split}

(5)

Then it is clear that

\displaystyle f_{r}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)\geq f_{r}^{(e-1)}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)\geq\cdots\geq f_{r}^{(2)}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)

and hence

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}^{(e-1)}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}

(6)

\displaystyle\geq\cdots\geq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}^{(2)}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}.

Moreover, as an easy consequence of
Lemma

4
we have the following inequality:

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}\geq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}^{(2)}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}.

(7)

Therefore, to prove the existence of
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}
, it suffices to show

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}^{(e-1)}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}

(8)

\displaystyle\leq\cdots\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}^{(2)}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}.

Indeed, it follows by (
6
) and (
8
) that
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}^{(2)}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}
, which together with (
7
) would imply that
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}
, and hence the existence of the corresponding limit. However, we are only able to prove (
8
) for
k=2
, stated as below.

Lemma 5
.

For all fixed
r\geq 3
and
e\geq 3
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,er-2(e-1),e)}{n^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}^{(e-1)}(n,er-2(e-1),e)}{n^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\cdots\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}^{(2)}(n,er-2(e-1),e)}{n^{2}}.

In Delcourt and Postle’s solution to
1
, their proof was based on a result which is similar to
Lemma

5
for the special case
r=3
. Using our notation, they essentially proved that
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{3}(n,e+2,e)}{n^{2}}\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f^{(2)}_{3}(n,e+2,e)}{n^{2}}
(see Theorem 1.7 in
[
5
]
). To prove that result, they first proved a structural lemma for
\mathcal{G}_{3}(e+2,e)
-free 3-graphs which are not
\mathcal{G}_{3}(i+1,i)
-free for some
2\leq i\leq e+1
(see Lemma 2.1 in
[
5
]
). Our proof of
Lemma

5
is inspired by their approach. At the core of our proof is a more powerful structural lemma (see
Lemma

9
below) for
r
-graphs that satisfy the assumptions of (
5
) but are not
\mathscr{G}_{r}((t-1)r-(t-2)k-1,t-1)
-free. Based on this new structural lemma and a density increment argument, we are able to show that for
k=2
, all such
r
-graphs must contain a sufficiently large
\mathscr{G}_{r}((t-1)r-(t-2)k-1,t-1)
-free subhypergraph with non-decreasing edge density (see
Lemma

10
below). This enables us to prove the inequality
\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}^{(t)}(n,er-2(e-1),e)}{n^{2}}\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}^{(t-1)}(n,er-2(e-1),e)}{n^{2}}
for every fixed
3\leq t\leq e-1
. We suspect that the above assertion holds for all
k\geq 3
. If so, it would settle
2
in the full generality.

is presented in

.

2
Proof of
Lemma

5

2.1
The first inequality

In this subsection, we will prove the first inequality of
Lemma

5
, and in fact we can prove it for every
k\geq 2
. Let us begin with some notations. For a positive integer
n
, let
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. For a finite set
X
and a positive integer
r
, let
\binom{X}{r}
denote the collection of all distinct
r
-element subsets of
X
. Therefore,
\binom{[n]}{r}=\{A\subseteq[n]:|A|=r\}
. For an
r
-graph
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r}
and a subset
T\subseteq[n]
, the
codegree
of
T
in
\mathcal{F}
, denoted by
\deg_{\mathcal{F}}(T)
, is the number of edges in
\mathcal{H}
which contain
T
as a subset, i.e.,
\deg_{\mathcal{F}}(T)=|\{A\in\mathcal{F}:T\subseteq A\}|.

The following lemma shows that any
r
-graph can be made to have no
(k-1)
-subset of codegree belonging to
\{1,\ldots,e-1\}
by deleting at most
(e-1)\binom{n}{k-1}
of its edges.

Lemma 6
.

For every
r
-graph
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r}
there exists a subhypergraph
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
such that
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|\geq\max\{0,|\mathcal{F}|-(e-1)\binom{n}{k-1}\}
and for every
T\in\binom{[n]}{k-1}
, we have either
\deg_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}(T)=0
or
\deg_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}(T)\geq e
.

Proof.

Successively remove the edges of
\mathcal{F}
which contain at least one
(k-1)
-subset of codegree at least one and at most
e-1
, where the codegree is counted with respect to the yet unmoved edges of
\mathcal{F}
. Let
A_{i}
be the
i
-th removed edge, and
T_{i}
be some
(k-1)
-subset of codegree at least one and at most
e-1
contained in
A_{i}
. In such a case we say that
A_{i}
is removed due to
T_{i}
and
T_{i}
is responsible for
A_{i}
. On one hand, every
(k-1)
-subset of
[n]
can be responsible for at most
e-1
edges, since otherwise it would have codegree at least
e
and could not be responsible for any removed edge in the beginning. On the other hand, every removed edge is removed due to some edge. Therefore we conclude that the removal process must terminate after at most
(e-1)\binom{n}{k-1}
edge removals. Note that the resulting
r
-graph is possibly empty.
∎

The next lemma proves a lower bound of
f_{r}^{(e-1)}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)
in terms of
f_{r}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)
.

Lemma 7
.

f_{r}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)-(e-1)\binom{n}{k-1}\leq f_{r}^{(e-1)}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an arbitrary
n
-vertex
\mathscr{G}_{r}(er-(e-1)k,e)
-free
r
-graph. Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be the subhypergraph of
\mathcal{F}
given by
Lemma

6
. To prove the lemma, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
satisfies the condition of (
5
) for
t=e-1
. Therefore, it is enough to show that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is
\mathscr{G}_{r}((e-1)r-(e-2)k-1,e-1)
-free.

Assume otherwise, then there exist distinct edges
A_{1},\ldots,A_{e-1}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
such that
|\cup_{i=1}^{e-1}A_{i}|\leq(e-1)r-(e-2)k-1
. Let
T
be an
(k-1)
-subset of
A_{1}
. According to
Lemma

6
, there exists
A_{e}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\setminus\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{e-1}\}
such that
T\subseteq A_{e}
. It follows that

\displaystyle|\cup_{i=1}^{e}A_{i}|\leq|\cup_{i=1}^{e-1}A_{i}|+|A_{e}\setminus A_{1}|\leq\big((e-1)r-(e-2)k-1\big)+\big(r-k+1\big)=er-(e-1)k,

contradicting the fact that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is
\mathscr{G}_{r}(er-(e-1)k,e)
-free.
∎

Proof of the first inequality of
Lemma

5
.

According to the lemma above, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}^{(e-1)}(n,er-(e-1)k,e)}{n^{k}}.

(9)

Setting
k=2
in (
9
) proves the first inequality of
Lemma

5
. ∎

2.2
The structural and density lemmas

First of all let us mention the following easy lemma.

Lemma 8
.

Let
n
be sufficiently large enough as a function of
e
and
r
. Then

(i)

f_{r}(n,er-(e-1),e)<\frac{n}{r-1}
;

(ii)

if
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r}
is
\mathscr{G}_{r}(er-(e-1)k,e)
-free, then for every
T\in\binom{[n]}{k-1}
,
\deg_{\mathcal{F}}(T)<\frac{n}{r-k}
.

Proof.

The first inequality is an easy consequence of (
2
). To prove the second inequality, it is straightforward to verify by definition that for every
T\in\binom{n}{k-1}
, the
(r-k+1)
-graph
\{A\setminus T:A\in\mathcal{F}\}
defined on the vertex set
[n]\setminus T
is
\mathscr{G}_{r-k+1}(e(r-k+1)-(e-1),e)
-free, and therefore the result follows by (i).
∎

Next we present a structural lemma for
\mathscr{G}_{r}(er-(e-1)k,e)
-free
r
-graphs which are not
\mathscr{G}_{r}(ir-(i-1)k-1,i)
-free for some
2\leq i\leq e-1
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
t\geq 3
and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r}
be an
r
-graph satisfying the assumptions of (
5
).
If it is not
\mathscr{G}_{r}((t-1)r-(t-2)k-1,t-1)
-free, i.e., there are
t-1
distinct edges
A_{1},\ldots,A_{t-1}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
|\cup_{i=1}^{t-1}A_{i}|\leq(t-1)r-(t-2)k-1
, then the following hold:

(i)

let
X=\cup_{i=1}^{t-1}A_{i}
, then
|X|=(t-1)r-(t-2)k-1
;

(ii)

for every
A\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{t-1}\}
,
|A\cap X|\leq k-1
;

(iii)

let
\mathcal{I}(X)=\big\{A\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{t-1}\}:|A\cap X|=k-1\big\}
, then

|\mathcal{I}(X)|\leq\max\big\{e-t+1,f_{r-k+1}(n-|X|,(e-t+1)(r-k+1)-(e-t),e-t+1)\big\}.

In particular, if
n
is large enough as a function of
r
and
e
, then
|\mathcal{I}(X)|<\frac{n-|X|}{r-k}
.

Proof.

(i) For the ease of notation let
x=(t-1)r-(t-2)k-1
. Assume for contradiction that
|X|\leq x-1
. Let
T
be an
(k-1)
-subset of
A_{1}
. As
\deg_{\mathcal{F}}(T)\geq e
there exists
A_{t}\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{t-1}\}
such that
T\subseteq A_{t}
. It follows that

\displaystyle|\cup_{i=1}^{t}A_{i}|\leq|X|+|A_{t}\setminus A_{1}|\leq(x-1)+(r-k+1)=tr-(t-1)k-1,

violating the assumption that
\mathcal{F}
is
\mathscr{G}_{r}(tr-(t-1)k-1,t)
-free.

(ii) If there exists
A_{t}\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{t-1}\}
such that
|A_{t}\cap X|\geq k
, then similar to the proof of (i), it is clear that

|\cup_{i=1}^{t}A_{i}|\leq|X|+|A_{t}\setminus X|\leq x+(r-k)=tr-(t-1)k-1,

a contradiction.

(iii) Assume that
|\mathcal{I}(X)|\geq e-t+1
, otherwise there is nothing to prove. As
\mathcal{F}
is
\mathscr{G}_{r}(er-(e-1)k,e)
-free, for arbitrary
e-t+1
distinct edges
A_{t},\ldots,A_{e}\in\mathcal{I}(X)
we have

\displaystyle|\cup_{i=1}^{e}A_{i}|=|X|+|\cup_{i=t}^{e}(A_{i}\setminus X)|\geq er-(e-1)k+1,

which implies that

\displaystyle|\cup_{i=t}^{e}(A_{i}\setminus X)|\geq er-(e-1)k+1-|X|=(e-t+1)(r-k+1)-(e-t)+1.

It follows that the
(r-k+1)
-graph

\mathcal{J}(X):=\{A\setminus X:A\in\mathcal{I}(X)\}

defined on the vertex set
V(\mathcal{F})\setminus X
is
\mathscr{G}_{r-k+1}((e-t+1)(r-k+1)-(e-t),e-t+1)
-free.
Moreover, it is clear that for all distinct
A,B\in\mathcal{I}(X)
, we have
A\setminus X\neq B\setminus X
, since otherwise

|\cup_{i=1}^{t-1}A_{i}\cup A\cup B|\leq x+r-k+1=tr-(t-1)k,

contradicting the fact that
\mathcal{F}
is
\mathscr{G}_{r}((t+1)r-tk-1,t+1)
-free.
We conclude that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}(X)|=|\mathcal{J}(X)|\leq f_{r-k+1}(n-|X|,(e-t+1)(r-k+1)-(e-t),e-t+1),

as needed. Finally, the last sentence of (iii) follows by
Lemma

8
(i).
∎

Applying
Lemma

9
with
k=2
yields the following result, which shows that any
r
-graph that satisfy the assumptions of (
5
) but are not
\mathscr{G}_{r}((t-1)r-2(t-2)-1,t-1)
-free must contain a sufficiently large
\mathscr{G}_{r}((t-1)r-2(t-2)-1,t-1)
-free subhypergraph with non-decreasing edge density. Note that we did not try to optimize the constants written in
Lemma

10
.

Lemma 10
.

Let
t\geq 3
and
r\geq 3
. Let
n
be sufficiently large as a function of
e
and
r
. Let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\binom{[n]}{r}
be an
r
-graph satisfying the assumptions of (
5
). Moreover, assume that
\frac{|\mathcal{F}|}{n^{2}}\geq\max\{\frac{1}{r(r-1)}-\frac{\delta}{r(r-2)},\frac{1}{2r^{2}-2r-5}\}
, where
\delta=\frac{1}{(3r-4)(r-1)+1}
. Then there exists a subhypergraph
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
with the following properties:

(i)

\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is
\mathscr{G}_{r}((t-1)r-2(t-2)-1,t-1)
-free;

(ii)

|V(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})|\geq\alpha n
, where
\alpha=\sqrt{\frac{3r^{2}-4r-14}{3r^{2}+2r-8}}
;

(iii)

\frac{|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|}{|V(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})|^{2}}\geq\frac{|\mathcal{F}|}{n^{2}}
.

Proof.

Let
x=(t-1)r-2(t-2)-1
. Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is not
\mathscr{G}_{r}(x,t-1)
-free, otherwise there is nothing to prove. Call a family of
t-1
distinct edges
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{t-1}\}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
a bad configuration if
|\cup_{i=1}^{t-1}A_{i}|\leq x
. By
Lemma

9
(i), the union of every bad configuration has size exactly
x
.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\mathcal{F}
. We will successfully construct hypergraphs
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2},\ldots
as follows. We aim to show that such a process will terminate and produce a hypergraph as needed. For
j\geq 0
, if
\mathcal{F}_{j}
is not
\mathscr{G}_{r}(x,t-1)
-free, say, it contains a bad configuration
\{A_{1,j},\ldots,A_{t-1,j}\}
with
X_{j}:=\cup_{i=1}^{t-1}A_{i,j}
. Then we set
\mathcal{F}_{j+1}
to be the subhypergraph of
\mathcal{F}_{j}
formed by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{j+1}:=\mathcal{F}_{j}\setminus(\mathcal{I}(X_{j})\cup\{A_{1,j},\ldots,A_{t-1,j}\})\quad\text{and}\quad V(\mathcal{F}_{j+1}):=V(\mathcal{F}_{j})\setminus X_{j}

where
\mathcal{I}(X_{j})
is the
r
-graph defined in
Lemma

9
with
k=2
and
A_{1,j},\ldots,A_{t-1,j},X_{j}
playing the roles of
A_{1},\ldots,A_{t-1},X
, respectively.

Before going any further, we need to show that
V(\mathcal{F}_{j+1})
is well-defined, namely, for every
A\in\mathcal{F}_{j+1}
, we have
A\cap X_{j}=\emptyset
. Indeed, by applying
Lemma

9
(ii) with
k=2
and the definition of
\mathcal{I}(X_{j})
, it is not hard to see that

\displaystyle\{A\in\mathcal{F}_{j}:A\cap X_{j}\neq\emptyset\}=\{A\in\mathcal{F}_{j}:|A\cap X_{j}|\geq 1\}=\mathcal{I}(X_{j})\cup\{A_{1,j},\ldots,A_{t-1,j}\},

which proves that
V(\mathcal{F}_{j+1})
is well-defined.

For each
j\geq 0
, denote
e_{j}=|\mathcal{F}_{j}|
and
v_{j}=|V(\mathcal{F}_{j})|
.
We claim that for
0\leq j\leq\frac{(1-\alpha)n}{x}
, the following hold:

\displaystyle e_{j+1}>e_{j}-\frac{v_{j}}{r-2}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{j+1}=v_{j}-x.

(10)

Indeed, by definition we have
e_{j+1}=e_{j}-|\mathcal{I}(X_{j})|-(t-1)
; moreover, by applying
Lemma

9
(iii) with
k=2
we have for large enough
n
(thus
v_{j}
is also large enough for
0\leq j\leq\frac{(1-\alpha)n}{x}
),

\displaystyle|\mathcal{I}(X_{j})|+(t-1)<\frac{v_{j}-x}{r-2}+(t-1)<\frac{v_{j}}{r-2},

which proves the inequality. The equality follows from the definition of
V(\mathcal{F}_{j+1})
and the fact that
X_{j}\subseteq V(\mathcal{F}_{j})
.

Applying (
10
) repeatedly yields that for every
0\leq j\leq\frac{(1-\alpha)n}{x}
,

\displaystyle e_{j}>e_{0}-\frac{1}{r-2}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}v_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{j}=v_{0}-xj.

(11)

Since
\mathcal{F}_{j}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
, it has to be
\mathscr{G}_{r}(er-2(e-1),e)
-free. Therefore, it follows by
Lemma

8
(ii) that the average degree of
V(\mathcal{F}_{j})
must be less than
\frac{v_{j}}{r-2}
, or equivalently,

\displaystyle\frac{e_{j}r}{v_{j}}<\frac{v_{j}}{r-2}.

(12)

According to (
11
) and (
12
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{e_{0}-\frac{v_{0}j}{r-2}+\frac{j(j-1)x}{2(r-2)}}{(v_{0}-xj)^{2}}<\frac{e_{j}}{v_{j}^{2}}<\frac{1}{r(r-2)}.

(13)

Substituting
v_{0}=n,e_{0}\geq(\frac{1}{r(r-1)}-\frac{\delta}{r(r-2)})n^{2}
, setting
y=xj
, and rearranging yields that

\displaystyle y(2n-y)-\frac{ry}{2x}(2n-y)-\frac{ry}{2}<(\frac{1}{r-1}+\delta)n^{2}.

(14)

Note that
\frac{ry}{2}\leq\frac{rn}{2}
. Observe that
x
is at least the size of the union of two distinct
r
-subsets, so
x\geq r+1
. Substituting the above inequalities to (
14
) gives that

\displaystyle\frac{r+2}{2r+2}n^{2}-\frac{r+2}{2r+2}(n-y)^{2}<(\frac{1}{r-1}+\delta)n^{2}+\frac{rn}{2}<\frac{3n^{2}}{3r-4},

where the last inequality follows from the choice of
\delta
and the assumption that
n
is sufficiently large as a function of
r
. Rearranging gives that

\displaystyle(n-y)^{2}>\alpha^{2}n^{2}.

As
y\leq n
, it follows that
y<(1-\alpha)n
and hence
j<\frac{(1-\alpha)n}{x}
. We conclude that the above process must stop in at most
\frac{(1-\alpha)n}{x}
steps, and therefore at most
(1-\alpha)n
vertices of
V(\mathcal{F})
could be removed. By our construction, it is clear that the resulting
r
-graph, denoted by
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
, satisfies conditions (i) and (ii) in the lemma.

In order to prove that
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
also satisfies (iii), it is enough to show that for every
j\geq 1
,
\frac{e_{j}}{v_{j}^{2}}>\frac{e_{0}}{v_{0}^{2}}
. Indeed, by (
13
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{e_{j}}{v_{j}^{2}}-\frac{e_{0}}{v_{0}^{2}}>\frac{e_{0}-\frac{v_{0}j}{r-2}+\frac{j(j-1)x}{2(r-2)}}{(v_{0}-xj)^{2}}-\frac{e_{0}}{v_{0}^{2}}.

Plugging
y=jx
and rearranging, we have

\displaystyle\frac{e_{j}}{v_{j}^{2}}-\frac{e_{0}}{v_{0}^{2}}>\frac{1}{(n-y)^{2}}\Big(\frac{e_{0}}{v_{0}^{2}}(2ny-y^{2})-\frac{ny}{(r-2)x}+\frac{y^{2}}{2(r-2)x}-\frac{y}{2(r-2)}\Big)

Observe that
-\frac{ny}{(r-2)x}+\frac{y^{2}}{2(r-2)x}
is negative. Then as
x\geq r+1
and
\frac{e_{0}}{v_{0}^{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2r^{2}-2r-5}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{e_{j}}{v_{j}^{2}}-\frac{e_{0}}{v_{0}^{2}}>\frac{y}{(n-y)^{2}}\Big(\frac{n}{(2r^{2}-2r-5)(r^{2}-r-2)}-\frac{y/2}{(2r^{2}-2r-5)(r^{2}-r-2)}-\frac{1}{2(r-2)}\Big)>0,

completing the proof of (iii), where the last inequality follows from the fact that
y/2<n/2
and the assumption that
n
is sufficiently large.
∎

2.3
The remaining inequalities

In this subsection we will prove the remaining inequalities of
Lemma

5
. We will make use of the following result, proved independently in
[
4
,
12
]
.

Lemma 11

(see Theorem 1.3 in
[
4
]
and Theorem 1.1 in
[
12
]
)
.

Let
r\geq 3
and
e\geq 3
be fixed integers. Then there exists
n_{0}
and
\epsilon>0
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
there exists an
n
-vertex
r
-graph which has at least
(1-n^{-\epsilon})\frac{n^{2}}{r^{2}-r}
edges and is
\mathscr{G}_{r}(tr-2(t-1),t)
-free for every
2\leq t\leq e
.

We will use the following easy consequence of
Lemma

11
.

Lemma 12
.

Let
r\geq 3
and
e\geq 3
be fixed integers. Then there exists
n_{0}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
there exists an
n
-vertex
r
-graph
\mathcal{F}
which satisfies
\frac{|\mathcal{F}|}{n^{2}}>\max\{\frac{1}{r(r-1)}-\frac{\delta}{r(r-2)},\frac{1}{2r^{2}-2r-5}\}
, where
\delta=\frac{1}{(3r-4)(r-1)+1}
, and the assumptions of (
5
) for
k=2
.

Proof.

It is clear that the
r
-graph given by
Lemma

11
satisfies the first line of (
5
). It is also clear by
Lemma

6
that the
r
-graph given by
Lemma

11
can be made to satisfy the second line of (
5
) by removing at most
(e-1)n
of its edges. Lastly, the required lower bound on
\frac{|\mathcal{F}|}{n^{2}}
holds for sufficiently large
n
since for every
r\geq 3
,
\frac{1}{r^{2}-r}
is strictly larger than both
\frac{1}{r(r-1)}-\frac{\delta}{r(r-2)}
and
\frac{1}{2r^{2}-2r-5}
.
∎

Given Lemmas
10
and
12
, the remaining inequalities of
Lemma

5
can be proved by standard analysis. We will follow the proof of Theorem 1.7 in
[
5
]
.

Proof of the remaining inequalities of
Lemma

5
.

For every
3\leq t\leq e-1
, let

a_{t}=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f_{r}^{(t)}(n,er-2(e-1),e)}{n^{2}}.

It suffices to show that for every
t
,
a_{t}\leq a_{t-1}
.

Assume for the sake of contradiction that there exists some
3\leq t\leq e-1
such that
a_{t}>a_{t-1}
. Let
\epsilon=a_{t}-a_{t-1}
,
b=\max\{\frac{1}{r(r-1)}-\frac{\delta}{r(r-2)},\frac{1}{2r^{2}-2r-5}\}
with
\delta=\frac{1}{(3r-4)(r-1)+1}
, and
c=\max\{a_{t}-\epsilon/2,b\}
. Since
a_{t}>b
by
Lemma

12
, it follows that

\displaystyle a_{t}>c\geq a_{t}-\epsilon/2>a_{t-1}.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{t}=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\frac{f_{r}^{(t)}(n,er-2(e-1),e)}{n^{2}}\geq c\}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{t-1}=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\frac{f_{r}^{(t-1)}(n,er-2(e-1),e)}{n^{2}}\geq c\}.

Then, as
a_{t}>c
,
\mathcal{I}_{t}
is infinite. As
a_{t-1}<c
,
\mathcal{I}_{t-1}
is finite. Let
n_{t-1}=\max\{n:n\in\mathcal{I}_{t-1}\}
.
Since
\mathcal{I}_{t}
is infinite, there exists
n_{t}\in\mathcal{I}_{t}
such that
n_{t}>n_{t-1}/\alpha
, where
\alpha
is defined in
Lemma

10
(ii). According to the definition of
\mathcal{I}_{t}
, there exists an
n_{t}
-vertex
r
-graph
\mathcal{F}
which satisfies the conditions of (
5
) with
k=2
, and
\frac{|\mathcal{F}|}{|V(\mathcal{F})|^{2}}\geq c
. As
\mathcal{F}
also satisfies the assumptions of
Lemma

10
, it follows that there exists a subhypergraph
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
that satisfies all of the three properties listed in
Lemma

10
. In particular, we have
\frac{|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|}{|V(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})|^{2}}\geq\frac{|\mathcal{F}|}{n^{2}}\geq c
, which implies that
|V(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})|\in\mathcal{I}_{t-1}
. However, we also have
|V(\mathcal{F}^{\prime})|\geq\alpha|V(\mathcal{F})|>n_{t-1}
, which contradicts the maximality of
n_{t-1}
.
∎

𝒜 \mathcal{A} -harmonic approximation and partial regularity, revisited
Matthias Bärlin, Franz Gmeineder, Christopher Irving, Jan Kristensen

Abstract
. We give a direct harmonic approximation lemma for local minima of quasiconvex multiple integrals that entails their C 1 , α \operatorname{C}^{1,\alpha} or C ∞ \operatorname{C}^{\infty} -partial regularity. Different from previous contributions, the method is fully direct and elementary, only hinging on the L p \operatorname{L}^{p} -theory for strongly elliptic linear systems and Sobolev’s embedding theorem. Especially, no heavier tools such as Lipschitz truncations are required.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we provide an alternative approach to arrive at the gradient partial regularity of local minimisers of the basic multiple integral

(
1.1
)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}[u;\Omega]:=\int_{\Omega}F(\nabla u)\operatorname{d}\!x,\qquad u\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N},

for variational integrands
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
and open and bounded subsets
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
. It is well-known that even under strong smoothness and convexity assumptions on
F
, local minima need not be of class
\operatorname{C}^{\infty}
in the entire
\Omega
, but merely
partially
\operatorname{C}^{\infty}
-regular
: There exists an open subset
\Omega_{0}
of
\Omega
such that
u
is of class
\operatorname{C}^{\infty}
in
\Omega_{0}
(see
[
1
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
21
,

for a non-exhaustive list of contributions). To state our result conveniently, we shall make the following assumptions on
F
: There exist
L>0
and, for each
m>0
,
\ell_{m}>0
such that

(
1.2
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|F(z)|\leq L(1+|z|^{p})&\text{for all}\;z\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n},\\
z\mapsto F(z)-\ell_{m}V_{p}(z)&\text{is quasiconvex at all $z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}$ with $|z_{0}|\leq m$},\\
F\in\operatorname{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}).&\end{cases}

Here,
V_{p}(z):=(1+|z|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}-1
, and we recall that a continuous integrand
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
is called
quasiconvex at
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
provided we have

(
1.3
)

\displaystyle F(z_{0})\leq\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}F(z_{0}+\nabla\varphi)\operatorname{d}\!x\qquad\text{for all $\varphi\in\operatorname{W}_{0}^{1,\infty}(\operatorname{B}_{1}(0);\mathbb{R}^{N})$},

and simply
quasiconvex
provided it is quasiconvex at every
z_{0}
.
The first two conditions in (
1.2
) embody the fact that the variational integral (
1.1
) is well-defined on subclasses of
\operatorname{W}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
by
\eqref{eq:pgrowth}_{1}
, and both coercive and lower semicontinuous for weak convergence on Dirichlet subclasses of
\operatorname{W}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
by

(cf.
[

In establishing partial regularity theorems, there are by now a wealth of methods, so for instance the
blow-up method
or the

-harmonic approximation

Broadly speaking, it asserts that if a map is almost
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic in a quantifiable way for a suitable bilinear form
\mathcal{A}\colon\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
(to be tacitly identified with its matrix representative), then it is
close
to an
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic map. As usual, the
\mathcal{A}
-harmonicity of a map
u\in\operatorname{W}_{\operatorname{loc}}^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
means that we have

(
1.4
)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{A}[\nabla u,\nabla\varphi]\operatorname{d}\!x:=\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathcal{A}\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle\operatorname{d}\!x=0\quad\text{for all}\;\varphi\in\operatorname{W}_{0}^{1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Typically, the aforementioned closeness is measured by a
V
-function type distance, e.g. see (
1.10

V
-functions. By now, there are various techniques to arrive at such approximation lemmas, either by
indirect means
(cf. the approaches by

[

14

15
]
in the more general context of Legendre-Hadamard elliptic bilinear forms) or

Diening
et al.
[

,
12
]
).

In this paper, we present a construction of
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic comparison maps that is
fully direct
and does not utilise heavier tools such as Lipschitz truncations, yet yields the necessary estimates to conclude partial regularity of minima for a wealth of variational problems. The proof itself only uses elementary embeddings for Sobolev spaces and routine results on the solvability of Legendre-Hadamard elliptic systems. The key approximation theorem of this paper is stated most conveniently for Legendre-Hadamard elliptic integrands, the connection of which to partial regularity proofs being discused below. More precisely, we assume slightly more generally than in (
1.2
) that
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the following: There exist
L>0
and, for each
m>0
, a number
\ell_{m}>0
such that

(
1.5
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|F(z)|\leq L(1+|z|^{p})&\text{for all}\;z\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n},\\
z\mapsto F(z)-\ell_{m}V_{p}(z)&\text{is rank-one-convex at all $z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}$ with $|z_{0}|\leq m$},\\
F\in\operatorname{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}).&\end{cases}

As usual, rank-one convexity of an integrand
G\colon\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
at some
z_{0}
means that for all
a\in\mathbb{R}^{N},b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the function
\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto G(z_{0}+ta\otimes b)
is convex.
Our main theorem then reads as follows:

Theorem
.

Let
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy (
1.5
) and
\operatorname{B}_{r}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega
be an open ball. For any
m>0
and all affine-linear maps
a\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
|\nabla a|\leq m
there exist a constant
c=c(n,N,p,m,F)>0
and a continuous function
H\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
depending on
n,p,m,F
such that

(
1.6
)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\searrow 0}\frac{H(t)}{t}=0

with the following property: Whenever
u\in\operatorname{W}_{\operatorname{loc}}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is an
extremiser
of (
1.1
) subject to (
1.5
), so satisfies

(
1.7
)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle F^{\prime}(\nabla u),\nabla\varphi\rangle\operatorname{d}\!x=0\qquad\text{for all}\;\varphi\in\operatorname{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),

then we have

(
1.8
)

\displaystyle\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{r}(x_{0})}V_{p}\Big(\frac{\widetilde{u}-\widetilde{h}}{r}\Big)\operatorname{d}\!x\leq cH\Big(\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{r}(x_{0})}V_{p}(D\widetilde{u})\operatorname{d}\!x\Big),

where
\widetilde{u}:=u-a
and
\widetilde{h}
is the unique solution of the Legendre-Hadamard elliptic system

(
1.9
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}(\mathcal{A}\nabla\widetilde{h})=0&\;\text{in}\;\operatorname{B}_{r}(x_{0}),\\
\widetilde{h}=\widetilde{u}&\;\text{on}\;\partial\!\operatorname{B}_{r}(x_{0})\end{cases}

with
\mathcal{A}=F^{\prime\prime}(\nabla a)
. Here, we have set for an arbitrary normed space
(X,|\cdot|)

(
1.10
)

\displaystyle V_{p}(z):=\big(1+|z|^{2}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}-1,\qquad z\in X.

The previous theorem will be established in Section
2
, where we also address the dependence of the constant
c
in Remark
2.5
. In Section
3
, we sketch how it enters partial regularity proofs for functionals of the form (
1.1
) in Section
3.1
, and it is here where we will see the importance of condition (
1.6
); see Remark
3.3
for more detail. We then conclude with improvements that incorporate uniformly controlled growth in Section
3.2
, where also links to Hausdorff dimension bounds for the singular set à la
Mingione
and the last named author
[
26
]
are addressed.

Before we embark on these matters, let us briefly address the entering of the single notions of convexity in the partial regularity proof in the setting of (
1.2
). Aiming for an excess decay to access the
Campanato-Meyers
characterisation of Hölder continuity, one combines the above theorem with the Caccioppoli inequality of the second kind. Whereas the above theorem holds for extremisers of Legendre-Hadamard elliptic multiple integrals (which is in particular satisfied by strongly quasiconvex integrands), it is the Caccioppoli inequality that seems to require strong quasiconvexity. It is also at the Caccioppoli inequality of the second kind where minimality instead of extremality is needed: In fact, otherwise the above argument would yield partial regularity of extremisers of strongly quasiconvex integrals, and the latter is well-known to be false by the fundamental work of
Müller & Šverák

[
29
]
. Hence, even though (
1.2

1.5
), we will only address the partial regularity results in Section
3
for local minimisers of multiple integrals satisfying (
1.2
).

2.
Direct approximation by
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic maps

2.1.
Four elementary lemmas

We now provide the proof of the above theorem. To this end, we merely require four elementary ingredients that we display next. We begin with

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and let
(X,\lvert\cdot\rvert)
be a normed space. Then the following hold for all
z\in X
:

(a)

V_{p}(z)\simeq_{p}(\mathbbm{1}_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}|z|^{2}+\mathbbm{1}_{\{|z|>1\}}|z|^{p})
.

(b)

If
p\geq 2
, then
V_{p}(z)\simeq_{p}|z|^{2}+|z|^{p}
.

(c)

For any
m>0
we have
V_{p}(z)\simeq_{p,m}|z|^{2}
for all
z\in X
with
|z|\leq m
.

(d)

For any
1\leq r\leq\min\{2,p\}
, the function
t\mapsto V_{p}(t^{\frac{1}{r}})
is convex.

(e)

V_{p^{\prime}}(V_{p}(z)/\lvert z\rvert)\lesssim_{p}V_{p}(z).

(f)

V_{p^{\prime}}(V^{\prime}_{p}(z))\lesssim_{p}V_{p}(z)

(g)

V_{p}(\lambda z)\lesssim_{p}\lambda^{\max\{2,p\}}V_{p}(z)
for all
\lambda\geq 1
.

(h)

V_{p}(\lambda z)\lesssim_{p}\lambda^{\min\{2,p\}}V_{p}(z)
for all
\lambda\leq 1
.

Moreover for
z,w\in X
we additionally have

(i)

V_{p}(z+w)\lesssim_{p}V_{p}(z)+V_{p}(w)
(subadditivity)

(j)

\lvert z\rvert\lvert w\rvert\lesssim_{p}V_{p}(z)+V_{p^{\prime}}(w)
(Young’s inequality).

These assertions are well-known and can be found scattered throughout the literature, however we will outline the proofs for completeness.

Proof.

Property
(a)
is a direct consequence of the definition of
V_{p}
, from which
(b)
,
(c)
,
(e)
and
(f)
immediately follow.
By
(a)
we see that
V_{p}(z)\simeq_{p}\max\{\lvert z\rvert^{p},\lvert z\rvert^{2}\}
for
p>2
and
V_{p}(z)\simeq_{p}\min\{\lvert z\rvert^{p},\lvert z\rvert^{2}\}
for
p\leq 2
, from where
(g)
and
(h)
follow. For
(i)
we simply note that
V_{p}(z_{1}+z_{2})\leq V_{p}(2z_{1})+V_{p}(2z_{2})
and use
(g)
with
\lambda=2.

For
(j)
we will use
(a)
and the corresponding power-type estimates

(
2.1
)

\lvert z\rvert\lvert w\rvert\leq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\lvert z\rvert^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lvert w\rvert^{2},\\
\frac{1}{p}\lvert z\rvert^{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\lvert w\rvert^{p^{\prime}},\end{cases}

which immediately yield the claim for
p\geq 2
by
(b)
. If
p<2
, we distinguish between the four different cases which arise: We use
\eqref{eq:younginequality_power}_{1}
if
\lvert z\rvert\geq 1
and
\eqref{eq:younginequality_power}_{2}
if
\lvert z\rvert\leq 1.

Finally for
(d)
, for
t>0
we can compute

\displaystyle\frac{\operatorname{d}\!^{2}}{\operatorname{d}\!t^{2}}V_{p}(t^{\frac{1}{r}})=\frac{1-r}{r^{2}}t^{\frac{1-2r}{r}}V^{\prime}_{p}(t^{\frac{1}{r}})+\frac{1}{r^{2}}t^{\frac{2-2r}{r}}V^{\prime\prime}_{p}(t^{\frac{1}{r}}).

Substituting
\xi=t^{\frac{1}{r}}
this expression is non-negative if and only if

(
2.2
)

\displaystyle(r-1)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\leq}}\frac{\xi V^{\prime\prime}_{p}(\xi)}{V^{\prime}_{p}(\xi)}=\frac{(p-1)\xi^{2}+1}{\xi^{2}+1}\qquad\text{for all}\;\xi>0.

It is easily seen that the ultimate expression is decreasing in
\xi>0
for
p<2
, leading to its infimal value
(p-1)
by sending
\xi\to\infty
, and non-decreasing in
\xi>0
provided
1<p\leq 2
, leading to its infimal value
1
by inserting
\xi=0
. Summarising, the right-hand side of (
2.2
) is bounded below by
\min\{p,2\}-1
, and so
t\mapsto V_{p}(t^{\frac{1}{r}})
is convex if and and only if
r\leq\min\{2,p\}
as claimed.
The proof is complete.
∎

Given
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
of class
\operatorname{C}^{1}
and
w\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
, we denote

(
2.3
)

\displaystyle F_{w}(z):=F(w+z)-F(w)-\langle F^{\prime}(w),z\rangle,\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n},

the corresponding
shifted integrand
. We then have

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and suppose that
F\in\operatorname{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
satisfies
\eqref{eq:pgrowth1}_{1}
and
\eqref{eq:pgrowth1}_{2}
. Then for each
m>0
there exist constants
L_{m}>0
,
\nu_{m}>0,
and a non-negative, non-decreasing concave function
\omega_{m}\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,2]
with
\lim_{t\searrow 0}\omega_{m}(t)=0
such that for all
w\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
with
|w|\leq m
the following hold for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}
:

(a)

|F^{\prime}_{w}(z)|\leq L_{m}(\mathbbm{1}_{\{|z|\leq 1\}}|z|+\mathbbm{1}_{\{|z|>1\}}|z|^{\max\{1,p-1\}})
.

(b)

|F^{\prime\prime}_{w}(0)z-F^{\prime}_{w}(z)|\leq L_{m}\omega_{m}(\lvert z\rvert)\lvert z\rvert^{\max\{1,p-1\}}
.

(c)

\nu_{m}\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\lvert\eta\rvert^{2}\leq\langle F^{\prime\prime}_{w}(0)(\xi\otimes\eta),(\xi\otimes\eta)\rangle\leq L_{m}\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\lvert\eta\rvert^{2}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Especially,
(a)
–
(c)
hold if
F\in\operatorname{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
satisfies
\eqref{eq:pgrowth}_{1}
and
\eqref{eq:pgrowth}_{2}
.

Proof.

Note that the rank-one convexity of
F
and the growth condition (
1.2
) combine to the estimate
|F^{\prime}(z)|\leq cL(1+|z|^{p-1})
; see, e.g.,
[
19
, Lem. 5.2]
.
Also since
F
is of class
\operatorname{C}^{2}
on
\mathbb{B}_{m+1}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\colon\;|\xi|\leq m+1\}
, we have that
F^{\prime\prime}
is bounded and uniformly continuous on
\mathbb{B}_{m+1}
; that is, there is
\Lambda_{m}>0
and a modulus of continuity
\omega_{m}
as in the statement of the lemma such that

(
2.4
)

\displaystyle\lvert F^{\prime\prime}(\xi)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda_{m},

(
2.5
)

\displaystyle\lvert F^{\prime\prime}(\xi)-F^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq 2\Lambda_{m}\omega_{m}(\lvert\xi-\zeta\rvert)

for all
\xi,\zeta\in\mathbb{B}_{m+1}.
Moreover replacing
\omega_{m}
by
\min\{2,2t+\omega_{m}(t)\},
we can assume that
\omega_{m}(t)=2
for all
t\geq 1.

Now to show
(a)
, noting if
|z|\leq 1
, we have
w+tz\in\mathbb{B}_{m+1}
for all
t\in[0,1]
and so

(
2.6
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|F^{\prime}_{w}(z)|&\leq|F^{\prime}(w+z)-F^{\prime}(w)|\leq\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\operatorname{d}\!}{\operatorname{d}\!t}F^{\prime}(w+tz)\operatorname{d}\!t\right|\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{1}|F^{\prime\prime}(w+tz)|\,|z|\operatorname{d}\!t\leq\Lambda_{m}|z|,\end{split}

whereas for
|z|\geq 1
we have

\displaystyle|F^{\prime}_{w}(z)|\leq|F^{\prime}(w+z)|+|F^{\prime}(w)|\,|z|^{p-1}\leq c(n,N,p,m,L)|z|^{p-1},

and so
(a)
follows. For
(b)
, we note that

(
2.7
)

\displaystyle|F^{\prime\prime}_{w}(0)z-F^{\prime}_{w}(z)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{1}(F^{\prime\prime}(w)-F^{\prime\prime}(w+tz))\operatorname{d}\!t\right|\,|z|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:dexter2}}}{{\leq}}2\Lambda_{m}\omega_{m}(\lvert z\rvert)|z|

provided
|z|\leq 1
and, again using the bound
|F^{\prime}(z)|\leq L(1+|z|^{p-1})
,

\displaystyle|F^{\prime\prime}_{w}(0)z-F^{\prime}_{w}(z)|\leq|F^{\prime\prime}(w)|\,|z|^{p-1}+c(m,L,p)|z|\leq c(m,L,p,\Lambda_{m})|z|^{\max\{1,p-1\}}

for
|z|\geq 1
. Combining the two cases yields the claim.
Finally
(c)
follows by noting that for
\varphi\in\operatorname{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
the functional

\mathcal{J}(t)=\int_{\Omega}F(z_{0}+t\nabla\varphi)-F(z_{0})-\ell_{m}V_{p}(z_{0}+t\nabla\varphi)+\ell_{m}V_{p}(z_{0})\operatorname{d}\!x

is non-negative and attains its minimum when
t=0.

Hence
\mathcal{J}^{\prime\prime}(0)\geq 0,
which implies the Gårding inequality

\int_{\Omega}\langle F^{\prime\prime}(z_{0})\nabla\varphi,\nabla\varphi\rangle\operatorname{d}\!x\geq\ell_{m}\,V_{p}^{\prime\prime}(z_{0})\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert^{2}\operatorname{d}\!x,

from which the Legendre-Hadamard condition
(c)
follows. Since quasiconvexity at
z_{0}
implies rank-one convexity at
z_{0}
, the proof is complete.
∎

Third, we record a Poincaré-Sobolev inequality for
V
-type functionals. The exponents can be improved, but we only require the following basic version:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
. Then for any
p\geq 1
and any
1\leq s\leq\frac{n}{n-1}
there exists a constant
C=C(n,M,p,s)>0
such that

(
2.8
)

\left(\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{r}(x_{0})}\left(V_{p}\left(\frac{u-(u)_{\operatorname{B}_{r}(x_{0})}}{r}\right)\right)^{s}\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}\leq C\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{r}(x_{0})}V_{p}(\nabla u)\operatorname{d}\!x

holds for all
u\in\operatorname{W}^{1,p}(\operatorname{B}_{r}(x_{0});\mathbb{R}^{M}).

Proof.

By translating and rescaling we can assume
x_{0}=0
and
r=1,
and that
(u)_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}=0.
Define, for
x\in\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)
, a measure
\mu_{x}
by
\mu_{x}:=|x-\cdot|^{1-n}\mathscr{L}^{n}
. Then we have
c_{1}\leq\mu_{x}(\operatorname{B}_{1}(0))\leq c_{2}
for some constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
independent of
x
. In consequence, by the usual Riesz potential bound with some
c_{0}=c_{0}(n)>0

\displaystyle|u(x)|\leq c_{0}\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}\frac{|\nabla u(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-1}}\operatorname{d}\!y\qquad\text{for $\mathscr{L}^{n}$-almost all}\;x\in\operatorname{B}_{1}(0),

we deduce by Jensen’s inequality and
\widetilde{\mu}_{x}:=\mu_{x}/\mu_{x}(\operatorname{B}_{1}(0))

(
2.9
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}V_{p}\left(u(x)\right)\operatorname{d}\!x&\leq\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}V_{p}\left(c_{0}\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}\frac{|\nabla u(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-1}}\operatorname{d}\!y\right)\operatorname{d}\!x\\
&\leq\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}V_{p}\left(c_{0}c_{2}\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}|\nabla u(y)|\operatorname{d}\!\widetilde{\mu}_{x}(y)\right)\operatorname{d}\!x\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Lem. \ref{lem:Vaux}\ref{item:Vprops5}, Jensen}}}{{\leq}}c(n,p)\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}V_{p}(\nabla u(y))\operatorname{d}\!\mu_{x}(y)\operatorname{d}\!x\\
&\leq c(n,p)\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}V_{p}(\nabla u(y))\operatorname{d}\!y,\end{split}

the ultimate estimate being a consequence of Fubini’s theorem. Now
\nabla\left(V_{p}(u)\right)=V_{p}^{\prime}(u)\nabla u
lies in
\operatorname{L}^{1}(\operatorname{B};\mathbb{R}^{M\times n})
using Lemma
2.1

(e)
and
(j)
. Using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality in the second step, we then find

\displaystyle\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}V_{p}\left(u\right)^{s}\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle\leq c(s)\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}(V_{p}\left(u\right)-(V_{p}(u))_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)})^{s}\operatorname{d}\!x

\displaystyle+c(s)\Big(\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}V_{p}(u(x))\operatorname{d}\!x\Big)^{s}

\displaystyle\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{GNS}}}{{\leq}}c(n,s)\Big(\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}|\nabla(V_{p}(u))|\operatorname{d}\!x\Big)^{s}+c(s)\Big(\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}V_{p}(u)\operatorname{d}\!x\Big)^{s}

\displaystyle\leq c(n,s)\Big(\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}V_{p^{\prime}}(V^{\prime}_{p}(u))+V_{p}(|\nabla u|)\operatorname{d}\!x\Big)^{s}

3.
Application to partial regularity

3.1.
Partial regularity

We conclude the paper by outlining how the main theorem can be used to derive the partial regularity of local minimisers of variational integrals subject to (
1.2
). We state the corresponding result first:

Theorem 3.1

(Partial regularity)
.

Let
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfy (
1.2
),
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and bounded and let
u\in\operatorname{W}_{\operatorname{loc}}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a local minimiser of the variational integral (
1.1
). Moreover, denote the
regular set

\displaystyle\mathrm{Reg}_{u}:=\{x\in\Omega\colon\;u\;\text{is of class $\operatorname{C}^{1,\alpha}$ for all $0<\alpha<1$ in a neighbourhood of $x$}\}

and hereafter the
singular set

\mathrm{Sing}_{u}:=\Omega\setminus\mathrm{Reg}_{u}
. Then we have the decomposition
\mathrm{Sing}_{u}=\mathrm{Sing}_{u,1}\cup\mathrm{Sing}_{u,2}
where

(
3.1
)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sing}_{u,1}

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in\Omega:\liminf_{r\searrow 0}\,\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{r}(x)}V_{p}(\nabla u-(\nabla u)_{\operatorname{B}_{r}(x)})\operatorname{d}\!y>0\right\},

(
3.2
)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sing}_{u,2}

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in\Omega:\liminf_{r\searrow 0}\,\lvert(\nabla u)_{\operatorname{B}_{r}(x)}\rvert=+\infty\right\}.

Especially,
\mathrm{Reg}_{u}
is relatively open in
\Omega
,
\mathscr{L}^{n}(\mathrm{Sing}_{u})=0
and hence
u
is of class
\operatorname{C}^{1,\alpha}
in
\mathrm{Reg}_{u}
for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

The previous theorem is essentially due to
Evans

[
16
]
and
Acerbi & Fusco

[
1
]
for the superquadratic regime
p\geq 2
and due to
Carozza, Fusco & Mingione
in the subquadratic case
1<p<2
.
Theorem
3.1
arises as an
\varepsilon
-regularity result, in turn being approached by an excess decay and the Campanato-Meyers characterisation of Hölder continuity. Since the passage from the excess decay to Hölder continuity is by now standard, we will focus on the former.

Besides our main theorem, the present proof of Theorem
3.1
only requires the Caccioppoli inequality of the second kind: Subject to the hypotheses from (
1.2
) and given
m>0
, there exists a constant
c=c(n,N,p,L_{m}/\nu_{m})>0
such that

(
3.3
)

\displaystyle\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{r}(x_{0})}V_{p}(\nabla(u-a))\operatorname{d}\!x\leq c\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{2r}(x_{0})}V_{p}\Big(\frac{u-a}{r}\Big)\operatorname{d}\!x

holds whenever
u\in\operatorname{W}_{\operatorname{loc}}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a local minimiser of
\mathscr{F}
,
a\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is affine-linear with
|\nabla a|\leq m
, and
\operatorname{B}_{2r}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega
. To this end, we define the
excess
of
u
on the ball
\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega
via

\mathbb{E}_{p}[u;\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})]:=\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}V_{p}(\nabla u-(\nabla u)_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})})\operatorname{d}\!x

and assume that we have for some
M>0
and
0<\varepsilon<1

(
3.4
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left|\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\nabla u\operatorname{d}\!x\right|<M,\\
&\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla u-(\nabla u)_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}|^{p}\operatorname{d}\!x<\varepsilon.\end{split}

Without loss of generality, we assume
0<R<1
in the sequel.
We let
a\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
be given by

, so that we have
|\nabla a|\leq M
by
\eqref{eq:smallnessconds}_{1}
, and write
\widetilde{u}=u-a
as before. In the following, let

be the unique solution of the Legendre-Hadamard elliptic system

(
3.5
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}(F^{\prime\prime}((\nabla u)_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})})\nabla h)=0&\;\text{in}\;\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0}),\\
h=u&\;\text{on}\;\partial\!\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0}).\end{cases}

In particular,
\widetilde{h}:=h-a
satisfies

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}(F^{\prime\prime}((\nabla u)_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})})\nabla\widetilde{h})=0\qquad\text{in}\;\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0}),

with its own boundary values along
\partial\!\operatorname{B}_{R}(0)
.
As a consequence, we obtain by Lemma
2.4

(c)
for any
1<r\leq p

(
3.6
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Big(\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla\widetilde{h}|\operatorname{d}\!x\Big)&\leq\Big(\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla\widetilde{h}|^{r}\operatorname{d}\!x\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\\
&\leq c\Big(\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{\widetilde{u}}{R}\right|^{r}\operatorname{d}\!x+\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{r}\operatorname{d}\!x\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}\\
&\leq c\Big(\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{r}\operatorname{d}\!x\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}(\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:smallnessconds}_{2}}}{{<}}c\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}<c)\end{split}

with
c=c(n,N,p,r,L_{m}/\nu_{m})
, where we used Poincaré’s inequality in the last line noting that
(\widetilde{u})_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}=0
.
Again by Lemma
2.4

(c)
, this implies

(
3.7
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\nabla\widetilde{h}(x_{0})|\leq c\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R/2}(x_{0})}|\nabla\widetilde{h}|\operatorname{d}\!y\leq c\Big(\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}}|\nabla\widetilde{h}|^{p}\operatorname{d}\!y\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:hbound0}}}{{<}}c\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}\end{split}

with a constant
c=c(n,N,p,L_{m}/\nu_{m})>0
, and thus

(
3.8
)

\displaystyle|\nabla h(x_{0})|\leq|\nabla\widetilde{h}(x_{0})|+|\nabla a|<c+M=:m,

fixing the threshold number
m>0
in the sequel.

Now let
0<\sigma<\frac{1}{3}
be arbitrary but fixed. By Jensen’s inequality we first deduce for some constant
c=c(p)>0

(
3.9
)

\displaystyle\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{\sigma R}(x_{0})}V_{p}(\nabla u-(\nabla u)_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})})\operatorname{d}\!x\leq c\inf_{\pi\,\text{affine-linear}}\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{\sigma R}(x_{0})}V_{p}(\nabla(u-\pi))\operatorname{d}\!x.

We now set
b(x):=\nabla h(x_{0})(x-x_{0})+h(x_{0})
, so that
|\nabla b|<m
by (
3.8
).
Recalling our notation
\widetilde{u}:=u-a
, we then invoke the Caccioppoli inequality (
3.3
) with
m>0
as in (
3.8
) to find for
0<\sigma<\frac{1}{3}

(
3.10
)

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{p}[u;\operatorname{B}_{\sigma R}(x_{0})]&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Cacc2},\,\eqref{eq:jensenoptimal}}}{{\leq}}c\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{2\sigma R}(x_{0})}V_{p}\Big(\frac{u-b}{\sigma R}\Big)\operatorname{d}\!x\\
&\;\;\;\;=c\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{2\sigma R}(x_{0})}V_{p}\Big(\frac{(\widetilde{u}-\widetilde{h})-(b-h)}{\sigma R}\Big)\operatorname{d}\!x\\
&\!\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Lem.}~\ref{lem:Vaux}~\ref{item:Vprops8}}}{{\leq}}c\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{2\sigma R}(x_{0})}V_{p}\Big(\frac{\widetilde{u}-\widetilde{h}}{\sigma R}\Big)\operatorname{d}\!x+c\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{2\sigma R}(x_{0})}V_{p}\Big(\frac{b-h}{\sigma R}\Big)\operatorname{d}\!x\\
&\!\!\!=:\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{II}.\end{split}

The estimation of
\mathrm{I}
is carried out by virtue of our main theorem, whereas
\mathrm{II}
is dealt with by routine bounds on
\mathcal{A}
-harmonic maps. More precisely, recalling the function
H
from the main theorem, we have

(
3.11
)

\begin{split}\mathrm{I}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Lem.}~\ref{lem:Vaux}~\ref{item:Vprops5}}}{{\leq}}\frac{c}{\sigma^{n+\max\{2,p\}}}\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}V_{p}\Big(\frac{\widetilde{u}-\widetilde{h}}{R}\Big)\operatorname{d}\!x\\
&\;\;\;\;\;\leq\frac{c}{\sigma^{n+\max\{2,p\}}}H\Big(\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}V_{p}(\nabla\widetilde{u})\operatorname{d}\!x\Big),\end{split}

with
c=c(n,N,p,L_{m}/\nu_{m})
by Remark
2.5
. We turn to the estimation of
\mathrm{II}
. For future reference, we fix
r:=\min\{2,p\}
, so that the function
\mathfrak{V}_{p}(t):=V_{p}(t^{\frac{1}{r}})
is convex by Lemma
2.1

(e)
. We first note that we have for any
x\in\operatorname{B}_{2\sigma R}(x_{0})

(
3.12
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup_{x\in\operatorname{B}_{2\sigma R}(x_{0})}\frac{|b(x)-h(x)|}{\sigma R}&\leq c\sup_{x\in\operatorname{B}_{2\sigma R}(x_{0})}\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{\sigma R}|\nabla^{2}\widetilde{h}(x)|\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Lem. \ref{lem:Lpbounds}\ref{item:Lpbound3}}}}{{\leq}}c\sigma\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla\widetilde{h}|\operatorname{d}\!x\\
&\leq c\sigma\Big(\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla\widetilde{h}|^{p}\operatorname{d}\!x\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\mathtt{C},\end{split}

the ultimate estimate being valid by
\eqref{eq:hbound1}_{2},\eqref{eq:hbound1}_{3}
, where
\mathtt{C}=\mathtt{C}({\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0.5}n},N,p,L_{m}/\nu_{m})>0
is a constant. Therefore, Lemma
2.1

(c)
yields for another constant
c=c({\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0.5}n},N,p,m,L_{m}/\nu_{m})>0
that

(
3.13
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{II}&\leq c\sup_{x\in\operatorname{B}_{2\sigma R}(x_{0})}\left|\frac{b(x)-h(x)}{\sigma R}\right|^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:harmonicbounds}_{1},\,\eqref{eq:harmonicbounds}_{2}}}{{\leq}}c\sigma^{2}\Big(\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla\widetilde{h}(x)|\operatorname{d}\!x\Big)^{2}\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:hbound0},\,\text{Lem. \ref{lem:Lpbounds}\ref{item:Lpbound3}}}}{{\leq}}c\sigma^{2}V_{p}\Big(\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla\widetilde{h}|\operatorname{d}\!x\Big)\\
&\!\!\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Jensen}}}{{\leq}}c\sigma^{2}\mathfrak{V}_{p}\Big(\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla\widetilde{h}|^{r}\operatorname{d}\!x\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:hbound0}}}{{\leq}}c\sigma^{2}\mathfrak{V}_{p}\Big(\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{r}\operatorname{d}\!x\Big)\\
&\!\!\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Jensen}}}{{\leq}}c\sigma^{2}\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\mathfrak{V}_{p}(|\nabla\widetilde{u}|^{r})\operatorname{d}\!x=c\sigma^{2}\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}V_{p}(\nabla\widetilde{u})\operatorname{d}\!x\end{split}

by definition of
\mathfrak{V}_{p}
. Thus, combining the estimates for
\mathrm{I}
and
\mathrm{II}
yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{II}\leq c\Big(\frac{1}{\sigma^{n+\max\{2,p\}}}H\Big(\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}V_{p}(\nabla\widetilde{u})\operatorname{d}\!x\Big)+\sigma^{2}\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}V_{p}(\nabla\widetilde{u})\operatorname{d}\!x\Big),

whereby we conclude with the auxiliary function
\Psi(t):=H(t)/t
that

(
3.14
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p}[u;\operatorname{B}_{\sigma R}(x_{0})]\leq c\Big(\frac{1}{\sigma^{n+\max\{2,p\}}}\Psi(\mathbb{E}_{p}[u;\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})])+\sigma^{2}\Big)\mathbb{E}_{p}[u;\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})].

Estimate (
3.14
) now gives rise to the following excess decay estimate:

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a variational integrand that satisfies (
1.2
) and let
\omega_{m}\colon[0,\infty]\to[0,1]
be the modulus of continuity from Lemma
2.2
. Given
0<\alpha<1
and
m>0
, there exist parameters
0<\sigma=\sigma(n,N,p,\alpha,\ell,L,m,\omega_{m})<1
and
0<\varepsilon=\varepsilon(n,N,p,\alpha,\ell,L,m,\omega_{m})<1
such that every local minimiser
u\in\operatorname{W}_{\operatorname{loc}}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfies the following: If
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
0<R_{0}<1
are such that
\operatorname{B}_{r}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega
and

(
3.15
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p}[u;\operatorname{B}_{R_{0}}(x_{0})]<\varepsilon\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;|(\nabla u)_{\operatorname{B}_{R_{0}}(x_{0})}|<m

then we have

(
3.16
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p}[u;\operatorname{B}_{\sigma R_{0}}(x_{0})]\leq\sigma^{1+\alpha}\mathbb{E}_{p}[u;\operatorname{B}_{R_{0}}(x_{0})].

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
be given. We choose
\varepsilon_{0}>0
so small such that
\mathbb{E}[u;\operatorname{B}_{R_{0}}(x_{0})]<\varepsilon_{0}
and
|(\nabla u)_{\operatorname{B}_{R_{0}}(x_{0})}|<m
imply the validity of (
3.14
) for any
\sigma\in(0,\frac{1}{3})
. Denoting
c>0
the constant from
(
3.14
)
, we choose
\sigma\in(0,\frac{1}{3})
such that
2c\sigma^{2}\leq\sigma^{1+\alpha}
. Since
\Psi
satisfies
\limsup_{t\searrow 0}\Psi(t)=0
, we find
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that
0<t<\varepsilon_{1}
implies
\Psi(t)\leq\sigma^{n+2+\max\{2,p\}}
. We subsequently choose
0<\varepsilon<\min\left\{\varepsilon_{0},\varepsilon_{1}\right\}
, whereby we infer (
3.16
) from (
3.14
) at once. The proof is complete.
∎

Working from the previous corollary, the proof of Theorem
3.1
is routine, cf.
[
4
,
20
,
21
]
.

Remark 3.3

(On condition (
1.6
))
.

As the above proof shows, the requisite excess decay estimate requires a superlinear decay of
\Psi
in (
3.14
), as we cannot compensate the blow-up of
\frac{1}{\sigma^{n+2}}
by a suitable smallness assumption. This superlinear power decay, in turn being manifested by (
1.6
), is everything we need to make the excess decay work.

3.2.
Uniformly controlled growth

In this section we will assume
p\geq 2
, the subquadratic case being slightly more technical but less natural to have a power-type decay as shall be imposed in the following. More precisely, we will show that the results of Section
3.1
can be strengthened subject to the additional hypothesis

(
3.17
)

z\mapsto\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(z)}{(1+\lvert z\rvert)^{p-2}}\quad\text{ is bounded and uniformly continuous on $\mathbb{R}^{Nn}$},

allowing us to discard the part
\mathrm{Sing}_{u,2}
in the description of the singular set (
3.1
). Condition (
3.17
) is satisfied for instance if
F\in\operatorname{C}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{N\times n})
and

(
3.18
)

\lvert F^{\prime\prime\prime}(z)\rvert\leq L(1+\lvert z\rvert)^{p-3}\qquad\text{for all}\;z\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}.

In particular this holds if
F
is a polynomial, and hence includes the example of
Alibert-Dacorogna-Marcellini

[
3
,
8
]
. In the following, we say that (
1.2
)
holds with uniform
\ell(>0)
provided the constant

as in
\eqref{eq:pgrowth}_{2}
can be chosen independently of
m>0
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
F
satisfy (
1.2
) with uniform
\ell
and (
3.17
) with
p\geq 2.

Then for each
\alpha\in(0,1),
there is
\varepsilon>0
such that if

is open,

is a local minimiser of (
1.1
), and

is a ball for which we have

(
3.19
)

then

is of class
\operatorname{C}^{1,\alpha}
in
\operatorname{B}_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0}).

We emphasise in particular that

is

, and does not require any normalisation.
As an application, we can adapt the results of

and the last named author

Let

Then given

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Given an affine-linear map

We then record the following estimates on

\displaystyle\lvert F_{\nabla a}^{\prime}(z)|

3.30
)

\begin{split}\fint_{B_{2\sigma R}(x_{0})}V_{p}\left(\frac{b-h}{\sigma R}\right)\operatorname{d}\!x&\leq V_{p}\left(\sup_{x\in\operatorname{B}_{2\sigma R}(x_{0})}\left\lvert\frac{b(x)-h(x)}{\sigma R}\right\rvert\right)\\
&\leq cV_{p}\left(\sigma\left(\fint_{\operatorname{B}B_{R}(x_{0})}\lvert\nabla\tilde{h}\rvert^{r}\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)\\
&\leq c\sigma^{\min\{2,p\}}V_{p}\left(\left(\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\lvert\nabla\tilde{u}\rvert^{r}\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\right)\\
&\leq c\sigma^{\min\{2,p\}}\mathbb{E}_{p}[u;\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})],\end{split}

from which we can deduce the slightly modified excess decay estimate

(
3.31
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{p}[u;\operatorname{B}_{\sigma R}(x_{0})]\leq c\Big(\frac{1}{\sigma^{n+\max\{2,p\}}}\Psi(\mathbb{E}_{p}[u;\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})])+\sigma^{\min\{2,p\}}\Big)\mathbb{E}_{p}[u;\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})],

where
c=c(n,N,p,\widetilde{L},\ell)>0,
and
\Psi
depends on
n,p
and
\omega
only.
Now iterating this gives the desired conclusion.
∎

Proof of Corollary
3.5
.

This will follow from the Carleson condition

(
3.32
)

I:=\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{0}^{R}\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\mathbb{E}_{p}[u;\operatorname{B}_{r}(x)]\,\frac{\operatorname{d}\!r}{r}\operatorname{d}\!x\leq C_{0},

from which one can argue exactly as in
[
26
, Theorem 5.1]
to show
\Omega^{\prime}\cap\Sigma_{u}
is
(\lambda,\kappa)
-porous for all
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega,
with
\lambda=\lambda(p,C_{0},\varepsilon)
where
C_{0}>0
is as above and
\varepsilon>0
is as in Theorem
3.4
.

To show (
3.32
), we choose

(
3.33
)

a(x)=(u)_{\operatorname{B}_{2R}(x_{0})}+(\nabla u)_{\operatorname{B}_{2R}(x_{0})}\cdot(x-x_{0})

in (
3.3
), which together with Lemma
2.1

(a)
gives

(
3.34
)

I\leq C\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{0}^{R}\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{R}(x_{0})}\left(\lvert u-a\rvert^{2}+\lvert u-a\rvert^{p}\right)\,\frac{\operatorname{d}\!r}{r}\operatorname{d}\!x.

Now we apply the estimate of
Dorronsoro
proved in
[
26
, Lemma 2.2]
to arrive at

(
3.35
)

\begin{split}I&\leq C\fint_{\operatorname{B}_{4}R(x_{0})}\left(\lvert\nabla u-(\nabla u)_{\operatorname{B}_{4R}(x_{0})}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\nabla u-(\nabla u)_{\operatorname{B}_{4R}(x_{0})}\rvert^{p}\right)\operatorname{d}\!x\\
&\leq C\left([\nabla u]_{\mathrm{BMO}(\operatorname{B}_{4R}(x_{0}))}^{2}+[\nabla u]_{\mathrm{BMO}(\operatorname{B}_{4R}(x_{0}))}^{p}\right),\end{split}

where we have used the John-Nirenberg inequality (see for instance

) in the last line.
We remark that in

]

∎

Sketch of proof of Lemma
2.4
.

For
(a)
,
(b)
, the case
2\leq p<\infty
is shown in
[
19
, Chapter 10]
using the method of Stampacchia.
In both cases we can extend this to the range
1<p<2
by means of a duality argument.
Here it suffices to establish an a-priori estimate, for which we assume the right-hand side
G
and
H
respectively lie in
L^{2},
and let
v,w
be the respective weak solutions.
For
(b)
we choose
\varphi
to be the weak solution to the dual problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}(\mathcal{A}^{*}\nabla\varphi)=-\mathrm{div}\left(\lvert\nabla v\rvert^{p-2}\nabla v\right)&\text{in}\;\operatorname{B}_{1}(0),\\
\varphi=0&\text{on}\;\partial\!\operatorname{B}_{1}(0),\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{A}^{*}[\xi,\zeta]=\mathcal{A}[\zeta,\xi]
for all
\xi,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{Nn}.

Then testing the equation for
v
with
\varphi
and applying
(b)
with
p^{\prime}\geq 2
we deduce that

(
3.36
)

\begin{split}&\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}\lvert\nabla v\rvert^{p}\operatorname{d}\!x=\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}\langle\mathcal{A}\nabla v,\nabla\varphi\rangle\operatorname{d}\!x=\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}\langle\nabla\varphi,H\rangle\operatorname{d}\!x\\
&\quad\lesssim\lVert\nabla\varphi\rVert_{\operatorname{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}\lVert G\rVert_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}\lesssim\lVert\nabla v\rVert_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}^{p-1}\lVert H\rVert_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}.\end{split}

By using this a-priori estimate and regularising
G,
unique solvability follows.
Similarly for
(a)
we choose
\psi\in(W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{0}\cap W^{2,p^{\prime}}(\operatorname{B}_{1}(0),\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}N})
to be the weak solution to the dual problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}(\mathcal{A}^{*}\nabla\psi)=-\lvert\nabla^{2}w\rvert^{p-2}\nabla^{2}w&\text{in}\;\operatorname{B}_{1}(0),\\
\psi=0&\text{on}\;\partial\!\operatorname{B}_{1}(0).\end{cases}

Then we can estimate

(
3.37
)

\begin{split}&\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}\lvert\nabla^{2}w\rvert^{p}\operatorname{d}\!x=\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}\langle\nabla^{2}w,-\mathrm{div}(\mathcal{A}^{*}\nabla\psi)\rangle\operatorname{d}\!x=\int_{\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}\langle HI_{n},\nabla^{2}\psi\rangle\operatorname{d}\!x\\
&\quad\lesssim\lVert\nabla^{2}\psi\rVert_{\operatorname{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}\lVert H\rVert_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}\lesssim\lVert\nabla^{2}w\rVert_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}^{p-1}\lVert H\rVert_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(\operatorname{B}_{1}(0)}.\end{split}

Finally
(c)
is a standard interior regularity result; we refer to
[
6
, Proposition 2.10]
for an elementary proof.
∎

Proof of Remark
2.6
for
p\geq 2
.

Recalling that
p^{\#}=\frac{np}{n-1},
we will also write
p_{\#}=\frac{np}{n+1}.

If
p\geq 2,
using the same notation and rescaling as in the above proof we choose
\Phi
to solve the auxiliary system

(
3.38
)

\begin{cases}-\mathrm{div}\big(\mathcal{A}\nabla\Phi\big)=-\mathrm{div}\big(\lvert\nabla\Psi\rvert^{s-2}\nabla\Psi\big)&\;\text{in}\;\operatorname{B},\\
\Phi=0&\;\text{on}\;\partial\!\operatorname{B},\end{cases}

where
s=2_{\{}\#\}=\frac{2n}{n+1}.
Then noting
\frac{1}{s-1}=\frac{n+1}{n-1},
by Lemma
2.4

(b)
we can estimate

(
3.39
)

\lVert\nabla\Phi\rVert_{\operatorname{L}^{\frac{np}{n-1}}(\operatorname{B})}\leq\lVert\nabla\Psi\rVert_{\operatorname{L}^{\frac{np}{n+1}}(\operatorname{B})}^{s-1}.

Hence proceeding similarly as in (
2.30
) we get

(
3.40
)

\begin{split}\int_{\operatorname{B}}\lvert\nabla\Psi\rvert^{s}\operatorname{d}\!x&\lesssim\int_{\operatorname{B}}\omega_{m}(\lvert\nabla\widetilde{U}\rvert)\lvert\nabla\widetilde{U}\rvert^{p-1}\lvert\nabla\Phi\rvert\operatorname{d}\!x\\
&\leq\lVert\omega_{m}(\lvert\nabla\widetilde{U}\rvert)\rVert_{\operatorname{L}^{np}(\operatorname{B})}\lVert\nabla\widetilde{U}\rVert_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(\operatorname{B})}^{p-1}\lVert\nabla\Phi\rVert_{\operatorname{L}^{\frac{np}{n-1}}(\operatorname{B})}\\
&\leq\rho_{m}\left(\int_{\operatorname{B}}V_{p}(\nabla\widetilde{U})\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{1}{np}}\left(\int_{\operatorname{B}}V_{p}(\nabla\widetilde{U})\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\operatorname{B}}V_{p}(\nabla\Psi)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{n-1}{np}}\end{split}

where

) in the last line.
Analogously to (

\lVert\lvert\nabla\Psi\rvert\rVert_{\operatorname{L}^{\frac{np}{n+1}}(\operatorname{B})}^{p}\leq\left(\int_{\operatorname{B}}V_{p}(\nabla\widetilde{U})\operatorname{d}\!x\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}

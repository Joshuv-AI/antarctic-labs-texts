1.
Introduction

Let
L
be an even lattice. The form
v\mapsto\frac{1}{2}v^{2}
on
L
induces a quadratic form
q
on
L_{2}:=L/2L
; we want to investigate what information on
L
we get from the quadratic
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-vector space
L_{2}
. In general not much can be said, but for the lattices associated to certain root systems (specifically
A_{1}\times A_{2},A_{4},D_{5},E_{6},E_{7},E_{8}
),
the relation is tight: the roots correspond to the vectors of
L_{2}
with
q=1
(with a grain of salt for
E_{7}
), and the group
\operatorname{O}(L)
is very close to the orthogonal group
\operatorname{O}(L_{2})
. We call the corresponding lattices
del Pezzo lattices
, since they appear naturally in the Picard group of del Pezzo surfaces
[
M
, § 25]
, but this will play no role in this note.

2.
Reduction mod.
2
of roots

Let
L
be an even lattice.
We endow
L_{2}:=L/2L
with the bilinear form
(\ |\ )
deduced from the scalar product on
L
, and the quadratic form
q
deduced from the
form
v\mapsto\frac{1}{2}v^{2}
. We have
q(x+y)=q(x)+q(y)+(x|y)
for
x,y
in
L_{2}
.

Lemma
.

Let
R
be a root system of type
A,D,E
, and
L
the corresponding root lattice.

1)
The inclusion
R\subset L
induces an injection
R/\{\pm 1\}\hookrightarrow L_{2}
.

2)
The reduction map
\rho:\operatorname{O}(L)\rightarrow\operatorname{O}(L_{2})
induces an injection
\operatorname{O}(L)/\{\pm 1\}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{O}(L_{2})
.

Proof
: 1) Let
\alpha,\beta\in R
such that
\alpha-\beta=2\lambda
, with
\lambda\in L
,
\lambda\neq 0
. Then
8\leq(\alpha-\beta)^{2}=4-2(\alpha\cdot\beta)
, hence
(\alpha\cdot\beta)\leq-2
; by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality this implies
\beta=-\alpha
.

2) Let
u\in\operatorname{O}(L)
such that
\rho(u)=1
. For each root
\alpha
,
u(\alpha)
is a root, with
u(\alpha)-\alpha\in 2L
. By 1), this implies
u(\alpha)=\varepsilon\alpha
, with
\varepsilon=\pm 1
. Let
\beta
be another root with
(\alpha\cdot\beta)\neq 0
; since
(u(\alpha)\cdot u(\beta))=(\alpha\cdot\beta)
, we have
u(\beta)=\varepsilon\beta
. Taking for
\alpha
a simple root, it follows that
u(\gamma)=\varepsilon\gamma
for all simple roots
\gamma
, hence
u=\varepsilon\cdot 1_{L}
.∎

From now on, we will only consider a particular type of lattices, which we call
del Pezzo lattices
. For
3\leq n\leq 8
, let
L_{0}
be the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module with basis
E_{0},\ldots,E_{n}
, endowed with the Lorentzian scalar product defined by
E_{0}^{2}=-1
,
E_{i}^{2}=1
for
i\geq 1
, and
(E_{i}\cdot E_{j})=0
for
i\neq j
. Let
K
be the element
3E_{0}-\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}E_{i}
. We put
L=K^{\perp}\subset L_{0}
; this is an even lattice, of discriminant
9-n
. The vectors of square 2 form a root system
R\subset L
, of type
A_{1}\times A_{2},A_{4},D_{5},E_{6},E_{7},E_{8}
for
n=3,\ldots,8

[
M
, § 25]
.

Let
\varphi:R\rightarrow L_{2}
be the reduction mod. 2. By the Lemma,
\varphi
induces an injective map
R/\{\pm 1\}\hookrightarrow L_{2}
; its image is contained in
q^{-1}(1)
.

Proposition 1
.

\varphi
induces a bijection from
R/\{\pm 1\}
onto
q^{-1}(1)
if
n\neq 7
, and onto
q^{-1}(1)\smallsetminus\{k\}
if
n=7
, where
k
is the nonzero element of the kernel of
(\ |\ )
.

Proof
: Let
e_{0},\ldots,e_{n}
be the images of
E_{0},\ldots,E_{n}
in
L_{0}/2L_{0}
; then
L_{2}
is the orthogonal in
L_{0}/2L_{0}
of the vector
k=\sum e_{i}
. We have

q(e_{0}+e_{i})=0\,,\quad q(e_{i}+e_{j})=1\quad\mbox{for }0<i<j\,.

The bilinear form
(\ |\ )
on
L_{2}
is non-degenerate if
n
is even; if
n
is odd its kernel is
\{0,k\}
.

Let
v\in q^{-1}(1)
. Then
v=\sum\limits_{i\in I}e_{i}
, with
\#I=m
even. We put
E_{I}:=\sum\limits_{i\in I}E_{i}
.

a) Suppose
0\notin I
. If
m=2
,
v=e_{i}+e_{j}=\varphi(E_{i}-E_{j})
. If
m=6
,
v=\varphi(2E_{0}-E_{I})
.

b) Suppose
0\in I
. If
m=4
,
v=\varphi(2E_{0}-E_{I})
. If
m=n=8
,
v=\varphi(4E_{0}-E_{I}-2E_{\ell})
, with
\ell\notin I
.

c) It remains to show that for
n=7
,
the vector
k=\sum e_{i}
cannot be written
\varphi(\alpha)
for some
\alpha\in R
. Indeed we would have
\alpha=\sum n_{i}E_{i}
with each
n_{i}
odd; but then
n_{i}^{2}\equiv 1\ \operatorname{mod.}\,8
, hence
\alpha^{2}\equiv 6\ \operatorname{mod.}\,8
, contradicting
\alpha^{2}=2
.∎

It is instructive to check the equality
\frac{1}{2}\#R=\#q^{-1}(1)
(or
\#q^{-1}(1)-1
for
n=7
) in each case:

a)
n=2m
.

In this case the form
(\ |\ )
is non-degenerate; the order of
q^{-1}(1)
is
2^{m-1}(2^{m}+(-1)^{a})
, where
a
is the
Arf invariant

\operatorname{Arf}(q)
: if
(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m};\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m})
is a symplectic basis of
L_{2}
,
a=\sum q(\delta_{i})q(\varepsilon_{i})
.

We can take for instance
\delta_{k}:=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2k-1}e_{i}
,
\varepsilon_{k}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2k-2}e_{i}+e_{2k}
. Then
q(\delta_{k})=q(\varepsilon_{k})=0
for
k
odd,
1
for
k
even, hence
\operatorname{Arf}(q)=0
for
n=4
or
6
,
\operatorname{Arf}(q)=1
for
n=8
. Therefore we get
\#q^{-1}(1)=10,36,120=\frac{1}{2}\#R
for
n=4,6,8
.

b)
n=3
or
7
.

For
n
odd, the vector
k
is in
L_{2}
, and spans the kernel of the form
(\ |\ )
. For
n=3
or
7
we have
q(k)=1
. The involution
v\mapsto v+k
exchanges
q^{-1}(1)
and
q^{-1}(0)
, hence
\#q^{-1}(1)=\frac{1}{2}\#L_{2}=2^{n-1}
. Indeed
\#(A_{1}\times A_{2})=2+6=8
, and
\#E_{7}=126=2(2^{6}-1)
.

c)
n=5
.

Here
q(k)=0
. The form
q
descends to a non-degenerate form
\bar{q}
on
L_{2}/\langle{k}\rangle
; we claim that
L_{2}/\langle{k}\rangle
is isomorphic to the quadratic
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-space
L^{\prime}_{2}
associated to
A_{4}
. Indeed, the subspace
N
of
L_{2}
spanned by
e_{i}+e_{j}
for
i<j<5
, with the quadratic form induced by
q
, is isomorphic to
L^{\prime}_{2}
, and the quotient map
N\rightarrow L_{2}/\langle{k}\rangle
is an isomorphism.
Thus
\#q^{-1}(1)=2\#\bar{q}^{-1}(1)=\#A_{4}=20=\frac{1}{2}\#D_{5}
.

3.
Orthogonal groups

Proposition 2
.

Let
L
be a del Pezzo lattice of rank
\geq 4
. The reduction map
\rho:\operatorname{O}(L)\rightarrow\operatorname{O}(L_{2})
induces an isomorphism of
\operatorname{O}(L)/\{\pm 1\}
onto
\operatorname{O}(L_{2})
.

Proof
: The induced map is injective by the Lemma, so we have to prove that
\rho
is surjective.

a)
n=4,6,8
.

In this case the form
(\ |\ )
is non-degenerate. By
[
D
, § 10]
, the group
\operatorname{O}(L_{2})
is generated by the reflections
r_{v}:x\mapsto x+(x|v)v
for
v
in
L_{2}
with
q(v)=1
– except for one case that we will consider below. By Proposition 1, we have
v=r(\alpha)
for some root
\alpha
; then
r_{v}
is the reduction mod. 2 of the reflection with respect to
\alpha
.

The exception occurs for
n=4
, if
L_{2}
contains a 2-dimensional subspace
N
such that
q_{|N}=0
(
loc. cit.
). This does not happen in our case, since the nonzero vectors
v
with
q(v)=0
are
f_{0}:=k+e_{0}
and
f_{i}:=e_{0}+e_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 4
, and they satisfy
(f_{i}|f_{j})=1
for
i\neq j
. ∎

b)
n=3,7
.

In this case the nonzero element
k
of
\operatorname{Ker}(\ |\ )
satisfies
q(k)=1
. Let
H
be a hyperplane in
L_{2}
such that
L_{2}=H\oplus\mathbb{F}_{2}k
, and let
p_{H}
be the projector from
L_{2}
onto
H
. The restriction of
(\ |\ )
to
H
is a symplectic form, and the map
u\mapsto p_{H}\circ u_{|H}
is an isomorphism from
\operatorname{O}(L_{2})
onto
\operatorname{Sp}(H)

[
D
, § 7]
; a transvection
x\mapsto(x|v)v
in
\operatorname{Sp}(H)
corresponds to the reflection with respect to
v+(1+q(v))k
. Since
\operatorname{Sp}(H)
is generated by transvections,
\operatorname{O}(L_{2})
is generated by reflections, and we conclude as in a) (note that the reflection with respect to
k
is the identity).

c)
n=5
.

Here it is simpler to use a counting argument. The group
\operatorname{O}(L)/\{\pm 1\}
is isomorphic to the Weyl group of
D_{5}
, that is, the semi-direct product
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{4}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{5}

[
B
]
. As we have seen in c) above, the space
L_{2}/\{k\}
with the form induced by
q
is isomorphic to the quadratic
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-space
L^{\prime}_{2}
for
n=4
; the quotient map induces a surjective map
\pi:\operatorname{O}(L_{2})\rightarrow\operatorname{O}(L^{\prime}_{2})
. The kernel of
\pi
consists of all automorphisms of
L_{2}
of the form
x\mapsto x+\ell(x)k
, with
\ell\in L_{2}^{*}
,
\ell(k)=0
; it is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{4}
. By a), the group
\operatorname{O}(L^{\prime}_{2})
is isomorphic to the Weyl group
\mathfrak{S}_{5}
of
A_{4}
. The result follows.∎

Corollary
.

\rho
induces an isomorphism of the Weyl group
W
of
L
onto
\operatorname{O}(L_{2})

for
n=4,5
or
6
, and of
W/\{\pm 1\}
onto
\operatorname{O}(L_{2})
for
n=7
or
8
.

This follows from the relation between
W
and
\operatorname{O}(L)
, see for instance
[
B
]
.

Remarks
.-\ 1)
For
n=3
,
\rho
is surjective, with kernel
\{\pm 1\}\times\{\pm 1\}
. More precisely,
we have
L=L^{\prime}\oplus L^{\prime\prime}
, where
L^{\prime}
and
L^{\prime\prime}
are lattices of type
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
; then
\operatorname{O}(L)=\operatorname{O}(L^{\prime})\times\operatorname{O}(L^{\prime\prime})\allowbreak=\{\pm 1\}\times(\mathfrak{S}_{3}\times\{\pm 1\})
, and
\operatorname{O}(L_{2})=\operatorname{O}(L_{2}^{\prime})\times\operatorname{O}(L_{2}^{\prime\prime})=\{1\}\times\mathfrak{S}_{3}
.

2) Propositions 1 and 2 do not hold for other root systems. For
A_{n}
for instance, the number of roots is
n(n+1)
, while
\#q^{-1}(1)
is roughly
2^{n-1}
. Similarly the order of
\operatorname{O}(L_{2})
is roughly
2^{n^{2}/2}
, much larger than
n!
.

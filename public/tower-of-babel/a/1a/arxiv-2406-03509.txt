Coherent states of the asymmetric harmonic oscillator
G.Chadzitaskos

Abstract
We constructed formal coherent states for an asymmetric harmonic oscillator, where the asymmetry parameter is the square root of the ratio of spring constants. Although these states are constructed based on both Glauber’s and Perelomov’s approaches, in general they do not satisfy all the properties required for coherent states. Over time, the coherent states introduced in this way generally become incoherent. However, there are some specific parameters for the square root ratios of the spring constants 4 ​ k + 1 4 ​ l + 1 \frac{4k+1}{4l+1} or 4 ​ k + 3 4 ​ l + 3 \frac{4k+3}{4l+3} . For these parameters it is possible to construct coherent states on the subspace of the Hilbert space of eigenstates. These coherent states keep their coherence during the time evolution. This case is also analyzed.



keywords: Harmonic oscillator, Coherent states, Parabolic cylinder functions

\frac{d^{2}x}{dt^{2}}+\omega^{2}(x)x=0,\mbox{ where }\omega(x)=\{\begin{smallmatrix}\omega_{-}\hskip 5.69046pt\mbox{for}\hskip 2.84544ptx<0\\

{\cal H}=\{\begin{smallmatrix}\frac{p_{x}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{1}{2}m\omega_{+}^{2}x^{2}\hskip 2.84544pt\mbox{for}\hskip 2.84544ptx\geq 0\\

(1)

The solution describes a periodic motion with the period

(2)

The Schrödinger equation is

\hat{H}\psi(x)=E\psi(x).

2.1
An asymmetric oscillator

The standard procedure for solving this Schrödinger equation,

{}[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}m\omega_{\pm}^{2}x^{2}]\psi(x)=E\psi(x),

(3)

is as follows:
Let’s substitute into (

\xi_{\pm}=\sqrt{\frac{2m\omega_{\pm}}{\hbar}}x,\,\,\,E=\hbar\omega_{\pm}(\nu_{\pm}+\frac{1}{2}).

(4)

We get the Weber equation

[\frac{d^{2}}{d\xi_{\pm}^{2}}-\frac{1}{4}\xi_{\pm}^{2}+\nu_{\pm}+\frac{1}{2}]\psi(\xi_{\pm})=0,

(5)

for positive axis and negative axis separately where
\xi_{-}\in(\infty,0)
and
\xi_{+}\in\langle 0,\infty).

Solutions of the Schrödinger equation are the parabolic cylinder functions
D_{\nu_{+}}(\xi_{+})
for positive values of the arguments
\xi
and
D_{\nu_{-}}(-\xi_{-}),
and for negative arguments
\xi
. The eigenfunctions of the Hamiltonian have the form

\psi(x)_{\nu}=\{\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha_{\nu_{-}}\,D_{\nu_{-}}(-\sqrt{\frac{2m\omega_{-}}{\hbar}}x),\mbox{ for }x<0\\
\,\alpha_{\nu_{+}}\,D_{\nu_{+}}(\sqrt{\frac{2m\omega_{+}}{\hbar}}x),\hskip 2.84544pt\mbox{ for }\hskip 2.84544ptx\geq 0\end{smallmatrix}.

The
\nu_{-}
can be expressed as a linear function of
\nu_{+}
because it must be an eigenfunction with eigenvalue
E

E,

\nu_{+}
and
\nu_{-}
are done,

\displaystyle{}E=\hbar\omega_{+}(\nu_{+}+\frac{1}{2})=\hbar\omega_{-}(\nu_{-}+\frac{1}{2}),\,\,\nu_{-}=s\nu_{+}+\frac{s-1}{2},

(6)

where

is the parameter that describes the oscillator.

We can assume that the parameter

without losing generality. The parameter
\nu_{+}
and the coefficients

are determined by the condition that the Hamiltonian must be self-adjoint. The continuity of the eigenfunction and the continuity of its derivative require the following two conditions:

\alpha_{\nu_{-}}D_{\nu_{-}}(0)=\alpha_{\nu_{+}}D_{\nu_{+}}(0)
and

-\alpha_{\nu_{-}}\sqrt{\frac{2m\omega_{-}}{\hbar}}D^{\prime}_{\nu_{-}}(0)=\alpha_{\nu_{+}}\sqrt{\frac{2m\omega_{+}}{\hbar}}D^{\prime}_{\nu_{+}}(0),

which lead to the transcendental equation.

3
Fock representation

The ratio of the frequencies
s
is given by the spring constants. The corresponding eigenvalues
E(\nu_{+})=\hbar\omega_{+}(\nu_{+}+\frac{1}{2})
can be labelled by integers
n=0,1,2,\ldots,

\nu_{n}=\nu_{n+}
in ascending order. The number
n
also indicates the number of zeros in the eigenfunction. Eigenfunctions can be written using the Heaviside step function
\theta(x),

\psi(x)_{E(\nu_{n})}=\theta(x)(D_{\nu_{-n}}(0)D_{\nu_{+n}}(\sqrt{2\omega_{+}}x))+\theta(-x)(D_{\nu_{+n}}(0)D_{\nu_{-n}}(-\sqrt{2\omega_{-}}x)),

for the following numerical calculation. To normalise the eigenfunctions, we can divide them by their norm,

|{s,n}\rangle=\frac{\psi(x)_{E(\nu_{n})}}{\sqrt{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi(x)_{E(\nu_{n})}|^{2}dx}}.

The frequencies
\omega_{+}
and
\omega_{-}
are fixed by the arrangement. Then, using Fock notation, we can formally define the creation and annihilation operators as weighted shift operators – in the same way as for the symmetric harmonic oscillator,

\hat{a}|s,n\rangle=\sqrt{n}|s,n-1\rangle,\,\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\hat{a}^{+}|s,n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}|s,n+1\rangle,

with
\hat{a}|s,0\rangle=0.

The annihilation and creation operators cannot be expressed in terms of position and momentum operators as they are in the symmetric case,
The operator Lie algebra is the same as for symmetric case,

[\hat{a},\hat{a}^{+}]=\hat{I},\,\,\hat{a}^{+}\hat{a}=\hat{n},\,\,[\hat{n},\hat{a}^{+}]=+\hat{a}^{+},\,\,[\hat{n},\hat{a}]=-\hat{a},

i.e. Lie algebra
\mathsf{h}_{4}.
The representative space of
\mathsf{h}_{4}
is the Hilbert space ( Fock space)
\{|{s,0}\rangle,|{s,1}\rangle,|{s,2}\rangle,|{s,3}\rangle,\ldots\},

\hat{n}|{s,n}\rangle=n|{s,n}\rangle,\,\,|{s,n}\rangle=\frac{(\hat{a}^{+})^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}|{s,0}\rangle.

4
Coherent states

Generalised coherent states (Glauber states) can be defined in a formal way as follows

\alpha\mapsto|{s,\alpha}\rangle=e^{-|\alpha|^{2}/2}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}|{s,n}\rangle.

Two examples of these coherent states are shown in Figures 2 and 3.

4.1
Properties of coherent states

The properties of these coherent states differ from generalised coherent states and are as follows

1.

\hat{a}|{s,\alpha}\rangle=\alpha|{s,\alpha}\rangle,\,\,\langle{s,\alpha}|{s,\alpha}\rangle=1,

2.

Coherent states can be created by the action of Weyl–Heisenberg group on the basic state
|s,0\rangle
.
Coherent states are its orbits,

|{s,\alpha}\rangle=\exp(\alpha\hat{a}^{+}-\alpha^{*}\hat{a})|{s,0}\rangle,

3.

The overlapping holds,

\langle{s,\alpha}|{s,\beta}\rangle=e^{-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}}{2}+\alpha^{*}\beta}\neq 0,

4.

The identity resolution is also valid,

\hat{I}=\int|{s,\alpha}\rangle\langle{s,\alpha}|\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}\alpha}{\pi}

5.

Coherent states are not Gaussian states

6.

Coherent states are not coherent states in the Schrödinger sense

7.

The time evolution does not preserve the coherence of states.

4.2
An illustrative example of coherent states in subspace of the Hilbert space

Let us start with an example for the simplest special case when
s=5.
.
Among the eigenstates there are eigenstates with equidistant energy levels. These eigenstates are chosen as the basis of the subspace,

|{5,\tilde{n}}\rangle_{s}=|{5,3\tilde{n}+1}\rangle,\,\,\,\tilde{n}=0,1,2,\ldots.

The corresponding eigenfunctions are glued Hermite functions.
We define the creation and annihilation operators by the action on this subspace as follows:

\hat{A}|5,\tilde{n}\rangle_{s}=\sqrt{\tilde{n}}|5,\tilde{n}-1\rangle_{s},\,\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\hat{A}^{+}|5,\tilde{n}\rangle_{s}=\sqrt{\tilde{n}+1}|5,\tilde{n}+1\rangle_{s},

where
\hat{A}|5,0\rangle_{s}=0.

The operator Lie algebra is also
\mathsf{h}_{4}
, i.e.

[\hat{A},\hat{A}^{+}]=\hat{I},\,\,\hat{A}^{+}\hat{A}=\hat{N},\,\,[\hat{N},\hat{A}^{+}]=+\hat{A}^{+},\,\,[\hat{N},\hat{A}]=-\hat{A},

and

\hat{H}|5,\tilde{n}\rangle_{s}=\hbar\omega_{+}(\tilde{n}+1/2)|5,\tilde{n}\rangle_{s}.

Coherent states are defined:

\alpha\mapsto|{5,\alpha}\rangle_{s}=e^{-|\alpha|^{2}/2}\sum^{\infty}_{\tilde{n}=0}\frac{\alpha^{\tilde{n}}}{\sqrt{\tilde{n}!}}|{5,\tilde{n}}\rangle_{s}.

The time evolution of coherent states
|5,\alpha\rangle_{s}
preserve the coherence,

|{s,\alpha_{t}}\rangle_{s}=e^{-\frac{\imath}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}|{s,\alpha_{0}}\rangle_{s}=e^{-\frac{\imath\omega_{+}}{2}t}|{s,\alpha_{0}e^{-\imath\omega_{+}t}}\rangle_{s}.

A similar conclusion can be drawn in the case of coherent states
|7/3,\alpha\rangle_{s},

|{s,\alpha_{t}}\rangle_{s}=e^{-\frac{\imath}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}|{s,\alpha_{0}}\rangle_{s}=e^{-\frac{\imath 3\omega_{+}}{2}t}|{s,\alpha_{0}e^{-\imath 3\omega_{+}t}}\rangle_{s}.

Examples of such coherent states are shown in Fig. 4 and in the Appendix.

Figure 4:
Coherent states

4.3
General formulation of the coherent states in the sub-space of the Hilbert space

The basic fact we will use is that for integer values of
\nu_{+}
and
\nu_{-}
the parabolic cylinder functions are Hermite functions, and the energy levels between such eigenstates are equidistant.
It is therefore sufficient for us to investigate only the cases where the eigenfunctions are a union of Hermite functions.

Let us denote these integer values
n_{+}
and
n_{-}.

Eigenfunctions for equidistant energy levels
means that we have to glue Hermite functions for
x<0
and
x\geq 0,

E_{k}=E(n_{+}(k))=E(n_{-}(k))=(n_{+}(k)+\frac{1}{2})\hbar\omega_{+}=(n_{-}(k))+\frac{1}{2})\hbar\omega_{-},

where we have introduced the integer parameter
k.

The corresponding eigenfunctions

|\psi_{k}(x)\rangle
of
H
are

|\psi_{k}(x)\rangle={\Huge{\{}}\begin{matrix}\alpha_{n_{-}}H_{n_{-}}(\sqrt{\frac{m\omega_{-}}{\hbar}}x),\hskip 2.84544ptx<0\\
\alpha_{n_{+}}H_{n_{+}}(\sqrt{\frac{m\omega_{+}}{\hbar}}x),\hskip 2.84544ptx\geq 0,\end{matrix}

(7)

where
n_{+}
and
n_{-}
must be expressed in terms of the integer
k,

where
\alpha_{n_{+}}
and
\alpha_{n_{-}}
are numbers to guarantee the continuity and normalisation of the eigenfunctions, and they depend on
\omega_{+},\omega_{-},n_{+},n_{-}.
They are

|\psi_{k}(x)\rangle={\Huge{\{}}\begin{matrix}|\psi_{n_{-}(k)}(x)\rangle,\hskip 5.69046ptx<0\\
|\psi_{n_{+}(k)}(x)\rangle,\hskip 5.69046ptx\geq 0\end{matrix}.

(8)

Selection rules for parameters
s
are done by requirements:

•

The requirement of continuity of the derivative of the eigenfunctions makes it necessary to distinguish two cases:
n_{+}
and
n_{-}
both take odd values or both take even values.

•

The requirement that
E_{k}
has to be eigenvalue gives the condition that the ratio of frequencies

s=\frac{\omega_{+}}{\omega_{-}}=\frac{(2n_{-}(k)+1)}{(2n_{+}(k)+1)}=\frac{p}{q}

must be rational number, i.e.
p
and
q
must be odd. Without loosing the generality we can consider
p>q.

Because

(2n_{-}(k)+1)=\frac{p}{q}(2n_{+}(k)+1)

and odd integers
p
and
q
have no common divisor,
2n_{+}(k)+1
takes values
q,3q,5q\ldots,(2l+1)\ldots
for non negative integer
l
.
Thus we have

n_{+}(k)=kq+\frac{q-1}{2},\hskip 2.84544ptn_{-}(k)=kp+\frac{p-1}{2},\,\,k=0,1,2,\ldots.

Taking in consideration
n_{+}(k),
we have to chose subspace spanned on eigenfunctions corresponding to eigenvalues
E_{k}=(n_{+}(k)+\frac{1}{2})\hbar\omega_{+}.

A minimal energy quantum for transition between two states is
kq\omega_{+}.

Restrict to the chosen subspace with basis vectors
|s,k\rangle_{s}=|s,\nu_{+}=n_{+}(k)\rangle
, i.e.

|s,0\rangle_{s}=|s,\frac{q-1}{2}\rangle,\,\,|s,k\rangle_{s}=|n_{+}(k)=kq+\frac{q-1}{2}\rangle

we can define coherent states on the subspace

\alpha\mapsto|{s,\alpha}\rangle_{s}=e^{-|\alpha|^{2}/2}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\frac{\alpha^{k}}{\sqrt{k!}}|{s,k}\rangle_{s}.

These coherent states preserve coherency during time evolution.

|{s,\alpha_{t}}\rangle_{s}=e^{-\frac{\imath}{\hbar}\hat{H}t}|{s,\alpha_{0}}\rangle_{s}=e^{-\frac{\imath q\omega_{+}}{2}t}|{s,\alpha_{0}e^{-\imath q\omega_{+}t}}\rangle_{s}.

5
Conclusion

We have formally introduced generalised coherent states for an asymmetric harmonic oscillator. Since in general the energy levels are not equidistant, we found special cases where there are eigenstates with equidistant energy levels. We constructed the coherent states on these eigenstates.

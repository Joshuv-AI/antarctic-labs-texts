Spectrum-preserving Deformations of Integrable Spin Chains with Open Boundaries
Yunfeng Jiang, Yuan Miao

Abstract
We discover a family of local deformations that leave part of the spectrum intact for strongly interacting and exactly solvable quantum many-body systems. Since the deformation preserves the Bethe Ansatz equations (BAE), it is dubbed the iso-BAE flow. Although all theories on the flow share the same BAE, the spectra are different. Part of the spectrum remains intact along the whole flow. Such states are protected by an emergent symmetry. The remaining parts of the spectrum change continuously along the flow and are doubly degenerate for even length spin chains. For odd length chains, the deformed spectrum also comprises doubly degenerate pairs apart from the sector with magnon number ( L + 1 ) / 2 (L+1)/2 , where L L is the length of the spin chain. We discuss the iso-BAE flow for the XXX 1 / 2 {\rm XXX}_{1/2} model in detail and show that the iso-BAE flows exist for more general models including q q -deformed XXZ as well as higher spin XXX s {\rm XXX}_{s} spin chains.

I
Introduction

In quantum mechanics, we frequently encounter the problem of finding the spectrum of the Hamiltonian
\mathbf{H}_{\alpha}=\mathbf{H}_{0}+\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}
, where
\mathbf{H}_{0}
is solvable and
\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}
is a local deformation (
i.e.

\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}
is a sum of local interactions) with
\alpha
being a continuous parameter. The spectrum of
\mathbf{H}_{\alpha}
can be found by perturbation theory
[
1
,
2
]
or other approximation methods such as Hamiltonian truncation method
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
. Generically, the spectra of
\mathbf{H}_{0}
and
\mathbf{H}_{\alpha}
are different and the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{H}_{\alpha}
depend on the parameter
\alpha
. In some situations,
\mathbf{H}_{\alpha}
is related to
\mathbf{H}_{0}
by a unitary transformation, resulting in identical spectra. An interesting general question is: are there deformations
\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}
such that
\mathbf{H}_{\alpha}
and
\mathbf{H}_{0}
share
part of the spectrum
. Namely, part of the eigenvalues remain intact against the perturbation while the rest are deformed. Such deformations, if exist, may reveal the existence of hidden or emergent symmetry which protect the invariant spectra. How to construct such deformations for a generic quantum many-body system is unclear. In this work, we aim to offer insights on a strongly interacting and exactly solvable set-up. In particular, we focus on quantum integrable models and provide a class of such examples.

We will consider quantum integrable spin chains which can be solved by Bethe ansatz
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
.
Integrability provides us powerful tools to analyze the spectrum and offers useful guidance for the construction of deformations that preserve part of the spectrum. As it turns out, such a deformation is linked to another phenomenon in Bethe ansatz, which has been hitherto unexplored (see
[
9
]
for an exception). We find that the deformed models share the same Bethe ansatz equations (BAE), hence dubbed an iso-BAE flow. The spectra of models on the flow are closely related. More precisely, eigenstates of the Hamiltonian
\mathbf{H}_{\alpha}
fall into two categories, referred to as type-I and type-II respectively. The eigenvalues of type-I states remain invariant along the flow while those of the type-II states change with
\alpha
.

We will analyze the flow by two complementary approaches. In the first approach, we construct an operator
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
and show that it commutes with the Hamiltonian
[\mathbf{H}_{\alpha},\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}]=0
and is nilpotent (for even length)
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}^{2}=0
. It then follows that, once acting
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
on a given eigenstate
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}|\psi\rangle
there are only two possibilities: either the result is vanishing or we obtain another eigenstate which is degenerate with
|\psi\rangle
, which correspond to type-I and type-II states, respectively.
We then study more closely the eigenvalues by a careful scrutiny of BAE. To perform this analysis, we use the rational
Q
-system
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
9
,
13
,
14
]
, which gives the complete physical spectrum. From the
Q
-system, we find that the results are also in accord with the classification of states mentioned before.

We first perform the analysis for the XXX
1/2
type spin chain for simplicity. Then we will show that the construction can be generalized to the
q
-deformed and higher spin models. While each generalization has certain new features, the main characteristic, namely the undeformed BAE and the classification of type-I and type-II states, remains the same as in the XXX
1/2
case.

(3)

at

. We will also use the notation

(4)

II.1
Integrability

Following standard Bethe ansatz approach, the eigenstates of (
3

|\{u_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M}\rangle
, where
M
is the number of magnons (down spins). The Bethe roots are the solutions of the following BAE,

\frac{g(u_{m}-\mathrm{i}/2)}{f(u_{m}+\mathrm{i}/2)}\left(\frac{u_{m}+\mathrm{i}/2}{u_{m}-\mathrm{i}/2}\right)^{2L}\prod_{k\neq m}^{M}S(u_{m},u_{k})=1,

(5)

where

S(u_{m},u_{k})=\frac{u_{m}-u_{n}-\mathrm{i}}{u_{m}-u_{n}+\mathrm{i}}\frac{u_{m}+u_{n}-\mathrm{i}}{u_{m}+u_{n}+\mathrm{i}}\,,

(6)

and
f(u)
and
g(u)
are related to boundary magnetic fields

f(u)=g(-u)=(u-\mathrm{i}\alpha)(u+\mathrm{i}\beta).

(7)

The corresponding energy for an eigenstate
|\{u_{m}\}\rangle
is given by

E_{M}=-\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{2}{u_{k}^{2}+1/4}\,.

(8)

An interesting observation
[
9
,
17
]
is that when
\beta=\alpha-1
, we have
f(u+\mathrm{i}/2)=g(u-\mathrm{i}/2)
, and they cancel out in (
5
), leading to

\left(\frac{u_{m}+\mathrm{i}/2}{u_{m}-\mathrm{i}/2}\right)^{2L}\prod_{k\neq m}^{M}S(u_{m},u_{k})=1.

(9)

Naively (
9
) can be identified with the BAE of (
2
).

When
\alpha=-\beta=1

However, one immediately sees a problem because the BAE (

II.2
Type-I and type-II states

We will now show that for the model
\mathbf{H}_{\alpha}
, there exists an operator
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
commuting with the Hamiltonian,
i.e.

\displaystyle[\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha},\mathbf{H}_{\alpha}]=0.

(10)

Explicitly, the operator is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}=\sum_{M=0}^{\lfloor L/2\rfloor}\Pi_{L-M+1}\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}^{L-2M+1}\Pi_{M}\,.

(11)

Here
\Pi_{M}
is the projector which projects to the
M
-magnon sector of the model with respect to the ferromagnetic state
|\uparrow\uparrow\cdots\uparrow\rangle
and
\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}
reads

\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}=-2\left(\alpha\mathbf{S}^{-}+\sum_{m<n}\sigma^{-}_{m}\sigma^{z}_{n}\right)\,,

(12)

with
\mathbf{S}^{-}=\sigma_{1}^{-}+\ldots\sigma_{L}^{-}
being the global spin lowering operator. From the construction, one can see that acting
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
on an
M
-magnon state leads to an
(L-M+1)
-magnon state.

One special case occur for odd
L
and
M=(L+1)/2
where
\Pi_{L-M+1}\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}^{L-2M+1}\Pi_{M}=\Pi_{M}
becomes a projector. Using the property of the projectors, we have

\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}^{2}=\begin{cases}\Pi_{(L+1)/2},&\,L\,\mathrm{mod}\,2=1,\\
0,&\,L\,\mathrm{mod}\,2=0.\end{cases}

(13)

Hence, operator
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
is nilpotent for even length.

Commutativity of (
10
) and
\mathbf{H}_{\alpha}
is proven in Sec.
A.1
of SM. We would like to stress that although the operator
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
commutes with the Hamiltonian, it is not one of the higher conserved charges which can be generated from the transfer matrix,
i.e.
it does not belong to the Bethe algebra. The existence of
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
reflects a novel emergent symmetry of the iso-BAE flow.

Due to the property (
10
), for any given eigenstate
|\psi_{\alpha}\rangle
of the model,
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}|\psi_{\alpha}\rangle
is either vanishing or is another eigenstate with the same eigenvalue, but with a different magnon number. It is straightforward to check that both cases exist. For
M\leq\lfloor L/2\rfloor
, we call the eigenstates that are annihilated by
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
type-I states, the rest of the eigenstates are type-II states.

The eigenvalues of the type-I states do not depend on
\alpha
and they remain invariant along the flow. On the other hand, the eigenvalues of type-II states change with
\alpha
, as are shown in Fig.
1
. In this sense, we can say that the type-I states are ‘protected’ by the emergent symmetry
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
. This observation is based on extensive numerical checks, while it would be desirable to have a more rigorous proof. Yet another compelling observation from numerical results is that the type-I states actually span the kernel of
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
.

II.3
Structure of the Hilbert space

We now discuss the structure of the Hilbert space.

The undeformed model

For the undeformed model (corresponding to
\alpha=\infty
), which is SU(2) invariant, eigenstates form SU(2) multiplets. The highest weight states correspond to physical solutions of the BAE (
9
). Moreover, the operator
\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}
is proportional to the global spin lower operator
\mathbf{S}^{-}
. Therefore, all the highest-weight states are type-I states, while all the descendants are type-II. In the
M
-magnon sector, there are
\mathcal{N}_{\text{I}}(L,M)=\begin{pmatrix}L\\
M\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}L\\
M-1\end{pmatrix}
physical solutions. Each solution corresponds to a spin-
(\frac{L}{2}-M)
representation with dimension
(L-2M+1)
.

The deformed model

For finite
\alpha
, the SU(2) symmetry is broken by
\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}
. The highest weight states at
\alpha=\infty
become type-I states at finite
\alpha
. The descendant states at
\alpha=\infty
become type-II states and their energy eigenvalues change with
\alpha
. In other words, the degeneracies are lifted, this is shown in Fig.
2
.

Figure 2:
Lifting of the degeneracies for the spectrum for finite
\alpha
. The blue lines correspond to the energy eigenvalues for the type-I states, while the red lines are the type-II states. In this figure, the two type-I states are
M=2
and
M=1
for system size
L=6
from bottom to top respectively. The type-II states are doubly degenerate in this case. Here we present eigenstates with different magnetization sectors, which are different from the ones in Fig.
1
.

While lifting degeneracies after certain deformation is a common feature, what is special for the iso-BAE flow is that the type-II states are organized by the emergent symmetry
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
and have residual degeneracies. For even
L
, the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
has the following decomposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{L}=\bigoplus_{M=0}^{L/2}\{|\psi_{M}\rangle^{\text{I}}\}\oplus\{|\psi_{M}\rangle^{\text{II}},\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}|\psi_{M}\rangle^{\text{II}}\}

(14)

where
|\psi_{M}\rangle^{\text{I}}
and
|\psi_{M}\rangle^{\text{II}}
stand for type-I and type-II states with
M
magnons.
For odd
L
, we have the following decomposition

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}_{L}=\,\bigoplus_{M=0}^{(L-1)/2}&\{|\psi_{M}\rangle^{\text{I}}\}\oplus\{|\psi_{M}\rangle^{\text{II}},\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}|\psi_{M}\rangle^{\text{II}}\}\\
&\,\oplus\{|\psi_{(L+1)/2}\rangle^{\text{II}}\}\end{split}

(15)

The sector
M=(L+1)/2
is special because in this sector
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}=\Pi_{(L+1)/2}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}|\psi_{(L+1)/2}\rangle^{\text{II}}=|\psi_{(L+1)/2}\rangle^{\text{II}}
.

We will show that in the
M
-magnon sector (
M\leq\lfloor\frac{L}{2}\rfloor
), the number of type-I and type-II states are
\mathcal{N}_{\text{I}}(L,M)
and
\mathcal{N}_{\text{II}}(L,M)={L\choose M-1}
respectively. This can be seen clearly in Fig.
1
for the iso-BAE systems with 6 sites.

For
M\leq\lfloor\frac{L}{2}\rfloor
, the state
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}|\psi_{M}\rangle^{\text{II}}
is an
M^{\prime}=L-M+1
eigenstate with the same eigenvalue. Therefore, there is an effective ‘equator’ at
\tfrac{1}{2}(L+1)
. For
M>\lfloor L/2\rfloor
, there are no type-I eigenstates and the number of type-II eigenstates is
{L\choose M}
. It is straightforward to check that

\displaystyle\sum_{M=0}^{\lfloor\frac{L}{2}\rfloor}(\mathcal{N}_{\text{I}}(L,M)+\mathcal{N}_{\text{II}}(L,N))+\sum_{M=\lfloor\frac{L}{2}\rfloor+1}^{L}{L\choose M}=2^{L}\,.

We thus conclude that the type-I and type-II states for different magnon numbers constitute the complete Hilbert space.

A natural question is whether the ground state is type-I or type-II ? It turns out that this depends on the value of
\alpha
, and we have two phase transition points at
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=1
. A detailed discussion on the phases of the ground state is given in Sec.
D
of SM.

III
BAE,
TQ
and
QQ
analysis

We will now analyze the spectrum of the iso-BAE flow from the perspective of Bethe roots and show that we can reach the same result as in the previous sections. As alluded before, the solutions to BAE (
9
) only describe part of the spectrum. To find out the ‘missing’ solutions, we need to consider Baxter’s
TQ
-relation

\begin{split}-uT_{1/2}(u)Q(u)=&\,u^{+}T_{0}^{+}(u)g^{-}(u)Q^{2-}(u)\\
&+u^{-}T_{0}^{-}(u)f^{+}(u)Q^{2+}(u),\end{split}

(16)

where we use the shorthand notation
f^{n\pm}=f(u\pm n\mathrm{i}/2)
. Here
T_{1/2}(u)
is the eigenvalue of the transfer matrix (cf. Sec.
A.1
of SM),
T_{0}(u)=u^{2L}
and

Q(u)
is the Baxter’s
Q
-polynomial

Q(u)=\prod_{m=1}^{M}(u-u_{m})(u+u_{m})

(17)

whose zeros are the Bethe roots. For generic boundary magnetic fields
\alpha
and
\beta
, taking
u=u_{m}
in (
16
) gives BAE in (
5
). For
\beta=\alpha-1
, the
TQ
-relation (
16
) can be written as

\displaystyle-u\,T_{1/2}Q=\big(u^{2}+(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2})^{2}\big)\left(u^{+}T_{0}^{+}Q^{2-}+u^{-}T_{0}^{-}Q^{2+}\right)

(18)

For
u=\pm\mathrm{i}(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2})
, the right-hand side vanishes. If
Q(u)
contains the roots
u=v_{M}=\pm\mathrm{i}(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2})
, the
TQ
-relation (
18
) can still be satisfied. Let us denote the rest of the Bethe roots by
\{v_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M-1}
, it follows that
v_{j}
satisfy the following reduced BAE

\frac{\tilde{g}(v_{m}-\mathrm{i}/2)}{\tilde{f}(v_{m}+\mathrm{i}/2)}\left(\frac{v_{m}+\mathrm{i}/2}{v_{m}-\mathrm{i}/2}\right)^{2L}\prod_{k\neq m}^{M-1}S(v_{m},v_{k})=1,

(19)

where
S(u,v)
is the same as (
6
) and the polynomials
\tilde{f}(u)
and
\tilde{g}(u)
are

\tilde{f}(u)=\tilde{g}(-u)=(u+\mathrm{i}\alpha)(u-\mathrm{i}(\alpha-1)).

(20)

This implies that the set of
(M-1)
Bethe roots
\{v_{m}\}_{m=1}^{M-1}
solves the BAE with
\tilde{\alpha}=-\alpha
and
\tilde{\beta}=-\beta=-(\alpha-1)
,
i.e.
the BAE for the Hamiltonian with opposite boundary magnetic fields. To conclude, we have the following two types of solutions

•

Type-I.
\{u_{m}\}
,
m=1,\ldots,M
which satisfies (
9
); These Bethe roots do not depend on the boundary magnetic fields and the corresponding eigenvalues remain invariant under the iso-BAE flow;

•

Type-II.
\{\mathrm{i}(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2})\}\cup\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}
,
m=1,\ldots,M-1
where
\{v_{m}\}
satisfy the reduced BAE (
19
). These Bethe roots depend on the boundary magnetic field in a non-trivial manner and the corresponding eigenvalues flow with
\alpha
.

Obviously the two types of solutions correspond to the two kinds of states that we discussed in the previous section.
Let us comment that, in order to find all the physical solutions of the BAE, we use the rational
Q
-system approach, which is reviewed in Sec.
B
of SM. From the solution of rational
Q
-system, one can check explicitly that the number of solutions for type-I and type-II states are indeed
\mathcal{N}_{\text{I}}(L,M)
and
\mathcal{N}_{\text{II}}(L,M)
respectively.

Generalizations

So far we have discussed the iso-BAE flow for the spin-
1/2

-deformed spin-
1/2

IV.1
XXZ model

For the XXZ model, we construct the following iso-BAE flow

\mathbf{H}^{\rm XXZ}_{\alpha}=\mathbf{H}^{\rm XXZ}_{0}+\mathbf{V}^{\rm XXZ}_{\alpha}.

(21)

where
\mathbf{H}_{0}^{\text{XXZ}}
is the
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
-invariant XXZ model
[
18
]
,

\begin{split}\mathbf{H}^{\rm XXZ}_{0}=&\sum_{n=1}^{L-1}\left[\sigma^{x}_{n}\sigma^{x}_{n+1}+\sigma^{y}_{n}\sigma^{y}_{n+1}\right.\\
&\left.+\Delta(\sigma^{z}_{n}\sigma^{z}_{n+1}-1)\right]+\sinh\eta(\sigma^{z}_{L}-\sigma^{z}_{1}),\end{split}

(22)

with
\Delta=\tfrac{q+q^{-1}}{2}=\cosh\eta
being the anisotropy.

The deformation term is given by

\begin{split}\mathbf{V}^{\rm XXZ}_{\alpha}&=\sinh\eta\left[\big(\frac{1}{\tanh(\alpha\eta)}-1\big)(\sigma^{z}_{L}-1)\right.\\
&\left.-\big(\frac{1}{\tanh((\alpha-1)\eta)}-1\big)(\sigma^{z}_{1}-1)\right]\end{split}

(23)

where
\eta\neq 0
is either real or imaginary, corresponding to two regimes
|\Delta|>1
and
0<|\Delta|<1
.

The model (
21
) can be solved by Bethe ansatz. The invariant part of the spectrum is described by the following BAE

\left(\frac{\sinh(u_m+\eta/2)}{\sinh(u_m-\eta/2)}\right)^{2L}\prod_{k\neq m}^{M}S^{\rm XXZ}(u_{m},u_{k})=1.

(24)

More details can be found in Sec.
B.2
of SM.

The structure of Hilbert space for the XXZ iso-BAE systems is similar to the XXX case. Here we focus on the model with generic
q
. The reason is that at root of unity, the representation theory of the quantum group
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
is different
[
18
,
19
]
, which leads to additional degeneracies in the spectrum
[
17
,
20
]
. As a result, we need to treat the iso-BAE flow with more care, dealing with additional degeneracies at finite
\alpha
.

We have type-I states that are invariant under the change of the boundary fields, as well as the type-II states that do vary. Similarly, we find that the type-I states span the kernel of the following operator,

\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}(q)=\sum_{M=0}^{\lfloor L/2\rfloor}\Pi_{L-M+1}\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}^{L-2M+1}(q)\Pi_{M}\,.

(25)

Here
\Pi_{M}
is the same projector which projects to the
M
-magnon sector of the model and
\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}(q)
is the
q
-deformed version of
\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}
defined in (
12
)

\begin{split}\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}(q)&=\sum_{m=1}^{L}\left(-q^{-\alpha-1}\prod_{n=m+1}^{L}q^{-\sigma^{z}_{n}}\right.\\
&\left.+q^{\alpha-1}\prod_{n=m+1}^{L}q^{\sigma^{z}_{n}}\right)\sigma^{-}_{m}\,.\end{split}

(26)

The Hilbert spaces for even and odd lengths of the chain are decomposed precisely into the same way in (
14
) and (
15
), where the same double degeneracies for type-II states appear.

V
Summary and discussions

In this work, we found an interesting deformation in quantum many-body systems which keeps part of the spectra intact while deforming the rest. The undeformed spectra are protected by an emergent symmetry. Although easily stated, explicit constructions of such deformations have not been studied much. In the current work we rely on integrability, for obvious reasons: integrability offers us the necessary tools to find the complete spectrum. Careful analysis of the Bethe ansatz provides us clues to construct such flows. It would be very interesting to find examples with non-integrable models, but so far we still lack a clear guidance.

Even if we restrict ourselves to integrable models, there are many questions to be answered. An immediate question is how general our construction is. The spin chain models we have considered in the current work are not the most general ones. More works are needed to see whether similar constructions exist for the XXZ model when
q
is a root of unity. There are some numerical evidences but the full structure is more subtle. Moreover, the operators
\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}
resemble the Yangian generators in the XXX case. The algebraic origin is yet to be elucidated. Also, all the models we have considered so far preserve at least the U(1) symmetry, or magnon numbers. What about the models without U(1) symmetry such as the XYZ model
[
23
,
24
,
25
]
or models with un-parallel boundary magnetic fields
[
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
?
Those questions remain elusive, and they might reveal the underlying algebraic structure of the iso-BAE flow.

Going beyond spin chain models, it is natural to ask about other types of models such as integrable field theories with boundaries. One possible way is considering the proper continuum limit of the spin chain models
[
33
,
34
,
35
,
36
]
. For example, in the case of the
{\rm XXX}_{1/2}
model, the field theory limit yields the
c=1
free compactified boson BCFT at self-dual radius
R=\sqrt{2}

[
15
,
37
]
. The spectra of Neumann-Neumann and Dirichlet-Dirichlet boundary conditions coincide
[
38
]
, which seems to match our expectation from iso-BAE flow. Following this route, it is highly likely that similar deformations exist for rather general integrable models.

We would like to emphasize that although the spectrum of the type-I states is invariant under the flow, the eigenvectors are deformed. This implies that even for type-I states, other physical quantities such as correlation functions
[
39
,
40
]
or entanglement measures
[
41
,
42
,
43
]
might vary with
\alpha
. Since the models along the iso-BAE flow are closely related to each other, this provides a new set-up to study quench dynamics
[
44
,
45
,
46
]
along the iso-BAE flow. Such process may be under better analytic control and shed new light on the out-of-equilibrium dynamics of integrable spin chains.

Appendix A
Construction of the
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
operator

In this section, we discuss the construction and prove the main properties of the operator
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
for the XXX
1/2
, XXZ and XXX
s
spin chains.

A.1
XXX
1/2
model

Algebraic Bethe ansatz

Algebraic Bethe ansatz (ABA) provides us the necessary tools to study iso-BAE flows. Therefore we first review the ABA for integrable chains with open boundary conditions. For XXX
1/2
spin chain, we start with the rational
R
-matrix
[
23
,
7
]

\mathbf{R}_{ab}(u)=\begin{pmatrix}u+\mathrm{i}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&u&\mathrm{i}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&\mathrm{i}&u&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&u+i\end{pmatrix},

(34)

which satisfies the Yang-Baxter equation

\mathbf{R}_{ab}(u-v)\mathbf{R}_{ac}(u)\mathbf{R}_{bc}(v)=\mathbf{R}_{bc}(v)\mathbf{R}_{ac}(u)\mathbf{R}_{ab}(u-v).

(35)

In order to describe open boundaries, we also need the boundary
K
-matrices
[
16
]

\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{L},a}(u,\beta)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{i}(\beta-\frac{1}{2})-u&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&\mathrm{i}(\beta+\frac{1}{2})+u\end{pmatrix}_{a},\quad\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{R},a}(u,\alpha)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{i}(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})+u&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&\mathrm{i}(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})-u\end{pmatrix}_{a}.

(36)

where L and R stand for the left end and right boundaries. Here we restrict to diagonal boundary conditions where the
K
-matrices are diagonal.
The
K
-matrices satisfy the boundary Yang-Baxter equation
[
16
]

\begin{split}&\mathbf{R}_{ab}(u-v)\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{L},a}(u,\beta)\mathbf{R}_{ba}(u+v)\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{L},v}(v,\beta)=\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{L},v}(v,\beta)\mathbf{R}_{ba}(u+v)\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{L},a}(u,\beta)\mathbf{R}_{ab}(u-v),\\
&\mathbf{R}_{ab}(-u+v)\mathbf{K}^{t}_{\mathrm{R},a}(u,\alpha)\mathbf{R}_{ab}^{t}(-u-v-2\mathrm{i})\mathbf{K}^{t}_{\mathrm{R},v}(v,\alpha)=\mathbf{K}^{t}_{\mathrm{R},v}(v,\alpha)\mathbf{R}^{t}_{ba}(-u-v-2\mathrm{i})\mathbf{K}^{t}_{\mathrm{R},a}(u,\alpha)\mathbf{R}_{ab}(-u+v).\end{split}

(37)

The monodromy matrix for the open chain is defined as

\begin{split}\mathbf{M}_{a}(u,\alpha,\beta)&=\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{L},a}(u,\beta)\prod_{n=1}^{L}\mathbf{R}_{an}(u-\mathrm{i}/2)\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{R},a}(u,\alpha)\prod_{n=L}^{1}\mathbf{R}_{an}(u-\mathrm{i}/2)\\
&=\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{L},a}(u,\beta)\mathscr{M}_{a}(u,\alpha)\\
&=\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{L},a}(u,\beta)\begin{pmatrix}\mathscr{A}(u,\alpha)&\mathscr{B}(u,\alpha)\\
\mathscr{C}(u,\alpha)&\mathscr{D}(u,\alpha)\end{pmatrix}_{a}.\end{split}

(38)

Taking the trace in the auxiliary space gives the double-row transfer matrix

\mathbf{T}_{1/2}(u,\alpha,\beta)=\mathrm{Tr}_{a}\mathbf{M}_{a}(u,\alpha,\beta)=(-u+\mathrm{i}(\beta-\tfrac{1}{2}))\mathscr{A}(u,\alpha)+(u+\mathrm{i}(\beta+\tfrac{1}{2}))\mathscr{D}(u,\alpha).

(39)

The subscript
1/2
denotes that the auxiliary space
a
is in the spin-
1/2
representation of the SU(2) algebra.

From the Yang-Baxter and boundary Yang-Baxter equations (
35
) and (
37
), we have

\mathbf{R}_{ab}(u-v)\mathscr{M}_{a}(u,\alpha)\mathbf{R}_{ab}(u+v-\mathrm{i})\mathscr{M}_{b}(v,\alpha)=\mathscr{M}_{b}(v,\alpha)\mathbf{R}_{ab}(u+v-\mathrm{i})\mathscr{M}_{a}(u,\alpha)\mathbf{R}_{ab}(u-v),

(40)

which gives a set of algebra between the quantum operators
\mathscr{A}
,
\mathscr{B}
,
\mathscr{C}
, and
\mathscr{D}
. For example, we have

\mathscr{B}(u,\alpha)\mathscr{A}(v,\alpha)-\frac{(u+v)(u-v)}{(u-v+\mathrm{i})(u+v-\mathrm{i})}\mathscr{A}(v,\alpha)\mathscr{B}(u,\alpha)=\frac{\mathrm{i}\mathscr{B}(v,\alpha)\mathscr{A}(u,\alpha)}{u-v+\mathrm{i}}+\frac{\mathrm{i}(u-v)\mathscr{B}(v,\alpha)\mathscr{D}(u,\alpha)}{(u-v+\mathrm{i})(u+v-\mathrm{i})},

(41)

\begin{split}\mathscr{D}(u,\alpha)\mathscr{B}(v,\alpha)-&\frac{(u-v+\mathrm{i})(u+v-\mathrm{i})}{(u+v)(u-v)}\mathscr{B}(v,\alpha)\mathscr{D}(u,\alpha)=\frac{\mathscr{A}(u,\alpha)\mathscr{B}(v,\alpha)}{(u+v-\mathrm{i})(u-v)}-\frac{\mathrm{i}(u+v)\mathscr{B}(u,\alpha)\mathscr{D}(v,\alpha)}{(u+v-\mathrm{i})(u-v)}\\
&+\frac{\mathrm{i}(u-v+\mathrm{i})^{2}}{(u-v)^{2}(u+v)}\mathscr{B}(u,\alpha)\mathscr{A}(v,\alpha)-\frac{\mathrm{i}\mathscr{B}(v,\alpha)\mathscr{A}(u,\alpha)}{(u-v)^{2}(u+v)}.\end{split}

(42)

Using (
40
), one can prove that the transfer matrices are in involution,

\left[\mathbf{T}_{1/2}(u,\alpha,\beta),\mathbf{T}_{1/2}(v,\alpha,\beta)\right]=0,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathbb{C}.

(43)

The eigenvalues of the transfer matrix is the
T
-polynomial that appears in the
TQ
-relation (
16
). As a result of (
43
), the transfer matrix can be seen as a generating function of conserved charges of the model. In particular, the Hamiltonian (
3
) can be found as the logarithmic derivative of the transfer matrix,

\mathbf{H}_{\alpha,\beta}=-\mathrm{i}\left.\partial_{u}\log\mathbf{T}_{1/2}(u,\alpha,\beta)\right|_{u=0}.

(44)

More explicitly, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{\alpha,\beta}=\sum_{n=1}^{L-1}\left(\vec{\sigma}_{n}\cdot\vec{\sigma}_{n+1}-1\right)+\frac{\sigma^{z}_{L}-1}{\alpha}-\frac{\sigma^{z}_{1}-1}{\beta}.

(45)

The eigenstates of the Hamiltonian (
45
) or the transfer matrix (
43
) can be constructed using the off-diagonal element of the monodromy matrix,

|\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\rangle_{\alpha}=\prod_{j=1}^{M}\mathscr{B}(u_{j},\alpha)|\Uparrow\rangle,

(46)

where
u_{j}
are the Bethe roots that solve the BAE (
5
) and
TQ
relations (
16
).
The eigenvalue of the transfer matrix for a the eigenstate
|\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\rangle_{\alpha}
is

{T}_{1/2}(u,\alpha,\beta)=-\frac{\big(u+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\big)^{2L+1}g\big(u-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\big)Q(u-\mathrm{i})+\big(u-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\big)^{2L+1}f\big(u+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\big)Q(u+\mathrm{i})}{uQ(u)},

(47)

where
Q(u)
is the
Q
-polynomial with zeros at
\pm u_{j}
associated with the eigenstate
|\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\rangle_{\alpha}
.

The operator
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}

It turns out that the operator
\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
are intimately related to the expansion of the monodromy matrix elements at
u=\infty
. More precisely, the operators
\mathscr{A}
,
\mathscr{B}
,
\mathscr{C}
,
\mathscr{D}
have the following expansion at
u\to\infty
,

\begin{split}&\mathscr{A}(u,\alpha)=u^{2L+1}\left(\mathscr{A}_{\infty}^{(1)}+\frac{1}{u}\mathscr{A}_{\infty}^{(2)}+O\big(\frac{1}{u^{2}}\big)\right),\\
&\mathscr{D}(u,\alpha)=u^{2L+1}\left(\mathscr{D}_{\infty}^{(1)}+\frac{1}{u}\mathscr{D}_{\infty}^{(2)}+O\big(\frac{1}{u^{2}}\big)\right),\\
&\mathscr{B}(u,\alpha)=u^{2L-1}\left(\mathscr{B}_{\infty}^{(1)}+O\big(\frac{1}{u}\big)\right),\\
&\mathscr{C}(u,\alpha)=u^{2L-1}\left(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}^{(1)}+O\big(\frac{1}{u}\big)\right).\end{split}

(48)

From the definition of the monodromy matrix, it is straightforward to obtain

\mathscr{A}_{\infty}^{(1)}=1,\quad\mathscr{A}_{\infty}^{(2)}(\alpha)=2\mathrm{i}\mathbf{S}^{z}+\mathrm{i}\big(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}\big),

(49)

\mathscr{D}_{\infty}^{(1)}=-1,\quad\mathscr{D}_{\infty}^{(2)}(\alpha)=2\mathrm{i}\mathbf{S}^{z}+\mathrm{i}\big(\alpha+\tfrac{1}{2}\big),

(50)

\mathscr{B}_{\infty}^{(1)}(\alpha)=-2\left(\alpha\mathbf{S}^{-}+\sum_{m<n}\sigma^{-}_{m}\sigma^{z}_{n}\right),

(51)

\mathscr{C}_{\infty}^{(1)}(\alpha)=2\left(\alpha\mathbf{S}^{+}+\sum_{m<n}\sigma^{+}_{m}\sigma^{z}_{n}\right).

(52)

When
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}^{(1)}(\alpha))^{\dagger}=-\mathscr{B}_{\infty}^{(1)}(\alpha)
. Importantly, the operator
\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}
in (
12
) is identified with
\mathscr{B}_{\infty}^{(1)}(\alpha)
,
i.e.

\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}=\mathscr{B}_{\infty}^{(1)}(\alpha)=-2\left(\alpha\mathbf{S}^{-}+\sum_{m<n}\sigma^{-}_{m}\sigma^{z}_{n}\right).

(53)

Commutativity of
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
and the transfer matrix

To prove the commutativity of
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
and the transfer matrix, we need the commutation relation of
\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}
and the monodromy matrix elements. This can be achieved by taking the large
u
limit of the relations (
40
). Specifically, we consider the limit of
\frac{\eqref{eq:BA1}}{u^{2L-1}}
and take the limit
u\to\infty
, arriving at

\begin{split}\left[\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha},\mathscr{A}(v,\alpha)\right]&=\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{\mathscr{B}(v,\alpha)}{u^{2L-1}}\left[\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}}{u}+\frac{\mathrm{i}v+1}{u^{2}}+O\big(\frac{1}{u^{3}}\big)\right)(u^{2L+1}\mathscr{A}_{\infty}^{(1)}+u^{2L}\mathscr{A}_{\infty}^{(2)}+O(u^{2L-1}))+\right.\\
&\left.\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}}{u}+\frac{-\mathrm{i}v}{u^{2}}+O\big(\frac{1}{u^{3}}\big)\right)(u^{2L+1}\mathscr{D}_{\infty}^{(1)}+u^{2L}\mathscr{D}_{\infty}^{(2)}+O(u^{2L-1}))\right]\\
&=\mathscr{B}(v,\alpha)\left((2\mathrm{i}v+1)-2(2\mathbf{S}^{z}+\alpha)\right).\end{split}

(54)

Similarly, we perform the same calculation for the limit of
\frac{\eqref{eq:BD1}}{v^{2L-1}}
and take the limit
v\to\infty
, and we obtain

\left[\mathscr{D}(u,\alpha),\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}\right]=\mathscr{B}(u,\alpha)\left((3-2\mathrm{i}u)-2(2\mathbf{S}^{z}+\alpha)\right).

(55)

From now on we focus on the iso-BAE case with
\beta=\alpha-1
. Let us consider an eigenstate
|\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\rangle_{\alpha}
of the transfer matrix

\mathbf{T}_{1/2}(u,\alpha,\alpha-1)|\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\rangle_{\alpha}=T_{1/2}(u,\alpha,\alpha-1)|\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\rangle_{\alpha},

(56)

We shall prove that the state
(\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha})^{p}|\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\rangle_{\alpha}
with
p=L-2M+1
is again an eigenstate of
\mathbf{T}_{1/2}(u,\alpha,\alpha-1)
, unless it is annihilated by
(\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha})^{p}
. This can be proven as follows.

Assuming that
(\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha})^{p}|\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\rangle_{\alpha}\neq 0
, by using (
54
) and (
55
) repeatedly, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}_{1/2}(u,\alpha,\alpha-1)(\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha})^{p}|\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\rangle_{\alpha}=(\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha})^{p}\mathbf{T}_{1/2}(u,\alpha,\alpha-1)|\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\rangle_{\alpha}+\chi(u,\alpha)(\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha})^{p-1}\mathscr{B}(u,\alpha)|\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\rangle_{\alpha},

(57)

where the function
\chi(u,\alpha)
is

\begin{split}\chi(u,\alpha)&=\sum_{m=1}^{p}\left[-(-u+\mathrm{i}(\alpha-\tfrac{3}{2}))(2\mathrm{i}u+1-2(L-2(M+p-n)+\alpha))\right.\\
&\left.+(u+\mathrm{i}(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}))(3-2\mathrm{i}u-2(L-2(M+p-n)+\alpha))\right]\,.\end{split}

(58)

It is easy to check that when
p=L-2M+1
, we have
\chi(u,\alpha)=0
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle[\mathbf{T}_{1/2}(u,\alpha,\alpha-1),(\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha})^{p}]|\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\rangle_{\alpha}=0\,.

(59)

For type-I states
\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}^{L-2M+1}|\psi\rangle^{\rm I}=0
. For type-II states
\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}^{L-2M+1}|\psi\rangle^{\rm II}\neq 0
and is degenerate with
|\psi\rangle^{\rm II}
. Combing all magnon sectors, it is natural to define the operator

\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}=\sum_{M=0}^{[L/2]}\Pi_{L-M+1}\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}^{L-2M+1}\Pi_{M},

(60)

and from (
59
), we have

\left[\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha},\mathbf{T}_{1/2}(u,\alpha,\alpha-1)\right]=\left[\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}(q),\mathbf{H}_{\alpha}\right]=0,

(61)

which proves the statement in Sec.
II.2
.

A.2
XXZ model

Now we perform a similar analysis for the XXZ model.

Algebraic Bethe ansatz

The
R
-matrix for the XXZ model is

\mathbf{R}_{ab}(t)=t\begin{pmatrix}q^{\sigma^{z}_{b}/2}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&q^{-\sigma^{z}_{b}/2}\end{pmatrix}_{a}+(q-q^{-1})\begin{pmatrix}{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&\sigma^{-}_{b}\\
\sigma^{+}_{b}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\end{pmatrix}_{a}+t^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}-q^{-\sigma^{z}_{b}/2}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&-q^{\sigma^{z}_{b}/2}\end{pmatrix}_{a},

(62)

where
q
is the
q
-deformation parameter with the anisotropy parameter
\Delta=(q+q^{-1})/2
.

The boundary
K
-matrices are

\begin{split}&\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{L},a}(t,\beta)=t^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}q^{\beta-1/2}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&-q^{-\beta-1/2}\end{pmatrix}_{a}+t\begin{pmatrix}-q^{-\beta+1/2}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&q^{\beta+1/2}\end{pmatrix}_{a},\\
&\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{R},a}(t,\alpha)=t\begin{pmatrix}q^{\alpha-1/2}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&-q^{-\alpha-1/2}\end{pmatrix}_{a}+t^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}-q^{-\alpha+1/2}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&q^{\alpha+1/2}\end{pmatrix}_{a}.\end{split}

(63)

The
R
- and
K
matrices satisfy the YBE and boundary YBE respectively.

The monodromy matrix and the double-row transfer matrices are defined similarly as

\begin{split}\mathbf{M}_{a}(t,\alpha,\beta)&=\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{L},a}(t,\beta)\prod_{n=1}^{L}\mathbf{R}_{an}(tq^{-1/2})\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{R},a}(t,\alpha)\prod_{n=L}^{1}\mathbf{R}_{an}(tq^{-1/2})\\
&=\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{L},a}(t,\beta)\mathscr{M}_{a}(t,\alpha)\\
&=\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{L},a}(t,\beta)\begin{pmatrix}\mathscr{A}(t,\alpha)&\mathscr{B}(t,\alpha)\\
\mathscr{C}(t,\alpha)&\mathscr{D}(t,\alpha)\end{pmatrix}_{a},\end{split}

(64)

and

\mathbf{T}_{1/2}(t,\alpha,\beta)=\mathrm{Tr}_{a}\mathbf{M}_{a}(t,\alpha,\beta).

(65)

The Hamiltonian can be obtained from the transfer matrix and is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{\rm XXZ}_{\alpha,\beta}=\sum_{n=1}^{L-1}\left[\sigma^{x}_{n}\sigma^{x}_{n+1}+\sigma^{y}_{n}\sigma^{y}_{n+1}+\Delta(\sigma^{z}_{n}\sigma^{z}_{n+1}-1)\right]+\frac{\sinh\eta}{\tanh(\alpha\eta)}(\sigma^{z}_{L}-1)-\frac{\sinh\eta}{\tanh(\beta\eta)}(\sigma^{z}_{1}-1)

(66)

where
\Delta=\cosh\eta=(q+q^{-1})/2
.

The iso-BAE flow

The iso-BAE flow requires that
\beta=\alpha-1
in (
66
). Moreover, it is convenient to split into an undeformed Hamiltonian together with additional boundary terms. The undeformed Hamiltonian is obtained by taking following limit

\mathbf{H}^{\rm XXZ}_{0}=\lim_{\alpha\eta\to\infty}\mathbf{H}^{\rm XXZ}_{\alpha,\alpha-1}=\sum_{n=1}^{L-1}\left[\sigma^{x}_{n}\sigma^{x}_{n+1}+\sigma^{y}_{n}\sigma^{y}_{n+1}+\Delta(\sigma^{z}_{n}\sigma^{z}_{n+1}-1)\right]+\sinh\eta(\sigma^{z}_{L}-\sigma^{z}_{1})\,,

(67)

while the iso-BAE XXZ Hamiltonian becomes

\mathbf{H}^{\rm XXZ}_{\alpha}=\mathbf{H}^{\rm XXZ}_{\alpha,\alpha-1}=\mathbf{H}^{\rm XXZ}_{0}+\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}\,,

(68)

where

\mathbf{V}^{\rm XXZ}_{\alpha}=\sinh\eta\left[\big(\frac{1}{\tanh(\alpha\eta)}-1\big)(\sigma^{z}_{L}-1)-\big(\frac{1}{\tanh((\alpha-1)\eta)}-1\big)(\sigma^{z}_{1}-1)\right].

(69)

The operator
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}

Now we focus on the iso-BAE case with
\beta=\alpha-1
. The operator
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
can be constructed similarly as in the XXX
1/2
case. The operator
\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}(q)
is again proportional to the leading order of
\mathscr{B}(t,\alpha)
operator in the
t\to\infty
expansion, and we have

\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}(q)=\frac{1}{q-q^{-1}}\mathscr{B}_{\infty}^{(1)}=\sum_{m=1}^{L}\left(-q^{-\alpha-1}\prod_{n=m+1}^{L}q^{-\sigma^{z}_{n}}+q^{\alpha-1}\prod_{n=m+1}^{L}q^{\sigma^{z}_{n}}\right)\sigma^{-}_{m}.

(70)

The operator
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
is constructed exactly as in (
60
). Following the same steps as in the previous subsection, we can prove that

\left[\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}(q),\mathbf{T}_{1/2}(u,\alpha,\alpha-1)\right]=\left[\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}(q),\mathbf{H}_{\alpha}\right]=0.

(71)

We would like to comment on the case when
q=\exp(\ri\pi\frac{\ell_1}{\ell_2})
is a root of unity. In this case, we have

\left(\hat{\mathfrak{q}}_{\alpha}(q)\right)^{\ell_{2}}=0.

(72)

This implies that if
\ell_{2}
is sufficiently small, we are not able to obtain all the type-II states with
M>\lfloor\frac{L}{2}\rfloor
using
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}(q)
anymore. In the meantime, when
q
is at root of unity, additional degeneracies appear in the spectrum of
\mathbf{H}^{\rm XXZ}_{0}
due to the special properties of the representation theory of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
at root of unity. All of these subtleties have to be taken in account and we postpone the discussion for the root of unity case to a later work.

A.3
\mathrm{XXX}_{s}
model

Finally, we consider the higher spin
\mathrm{XXX}_{s}
model.

Algebraic Bethe ansatz

We take the auxiliary space to be spin-
1/2
, which is sufficient for our purpose.
The Lax operator in this case reads

\mathbf{L}_{ab}(u)=u\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{b}&{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}\\
{\color[rgb]{0.5,0.5,0.5}0}&\mathbb{I}_{b}\end{pmatrix}_{a}+\mathrm{i}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{S}^{z}_{b}&\mathbf{S}^{-}_{b}\\
\mathbf{S}^{+}_{b}&-\mathbf{S}^{z}_{b}\end{pmatrix}_{a},

(73)

where
\mathbf{S}^{\alpha}
are spin-
s
generators of the SU(2) algebra. The boundary
K
-matrices are the same as the spin-1/2 case,
i.e.
(
36
). The monodromy matrix becomes

\begin{split}\mathbf{M}_{a}(u,\alpha,\beta)&=\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{L},a}(u,\beta)\prod_{n=1}^{L}\mathbf{L}_{an}(u-\mathrm{i}/2)\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{R},a}(u,\alpha)\prod_{n=L}^{1}\mathbf{L}_{an}(u-\mathrm{i}/2)\\
&=\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{L},a}(u,\beta)\mathscr{M}_{a}(u,\alpha)\\
&=\mathbf{K}_{\mathrm{L},a}(u,\beta)\begin{pmatrix}\mathscr{A}(u,\alpha)&\mathscr{B}(u,\alpha)\\
\mathscr{C}(u,\alpha)&\mathscr{D}(u,\alpha)\end{pmatrix}_{a},\end{split}

(74)

and the double-row transfer matrix reads

\mathbf{T}_{1/2}(u,\alpha,\beta)=\mathrm{Tr}_{a}\mathbf{M}_{a}(u,\alpha,\beta)=(-u+\mathrm{i}(\beta-\tfrac{1}{2}))\mathscr{A}(u,\alpha)+(u+\mathrm{i}(\beta+\tfrac{1}{2}))\mathscr{D}(u,\alpha).

(75)

We again have the same relations between
\mathscr{M}_{a}(u,\alpha)
as in (
40
), since the intertwiner remains the same. Hence, the algebra between the quantum operators
\mathscr{A}
,
\mathscr{B}
,
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{D}
remain the same as the spin-1/2 case too.

Note that in the higher spin XXX
s
model, if we want to construct the eigenstate and find the BAE, it is sufficient to consider the transfer matrix with spin-
1/2
representation in the auxiliary space. However, if we want to obtain the Hamiltonian, we have to take the auxiliary space to be in the same representation of the local quantum space. These transfer matrices can be obtained by the fusion procedure
[

]

s=1

\begin{split}\mathbf{H}^{(1)}_{\alpha,\beta}&=\sum_{n=1}^{L-1}\left[\vec{\mathbf{S}}_{n}\cdot\vec{\mathbf{S}}_{n+1}-\big(\vec{\mathbf{S}}_{n}\cdot\vec{\mathbf{S}}_{n+1}\big)^{2}\right]\\
&+\left(\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}+\frac{1}{\alpha-1/2}\right)\mathbf{S}^{z}_{L}+\left(\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}-\frac{1}{\alpha-1/2}\right)\big(\mathbf{S}^{z}_{L}\big)^{2}\\
&-\left(\frac{1}{\beta+1/2}+\frac{1}{\beta-1/2}\right)\mathbf{S}^{z}_{1}+\left(\frac{1}{\beta+1/2}-\frac{1}{\beta-1/2}\right)\big(\mathbf{S}^{z}_{1}\big)^{2}+2\left(\frac{1}{\beta-1/2}-\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}\right),\end{split}

(76)

where the spin-1 operators are defined as

\mathbf{S}^{z}_{m}=\mathbb{I}^{\otimes(m-1)}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}\otimes\mathbb{I}^{\otimes(L-m)},\quad\mathbf{S}^{+}_{m}=\mathbb{I}^{\otimes(m-1)}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}0&\sqrt{2}&0\\
0&0&\sqrt{2}\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\otimes\mathbb{I}^{\otimes(L-m)}=\left(\mathbf{S}^{-}_{m}\right)^{\dagger}.

(77)

The local Hilbert space is
\mathbb{C}^{3}
and we choose the three basis states as

\displaystyle|+\rangle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\\
0\end{array}\right),\qquad|0\rangle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
1\\
0\end{array}\right),\qquad|-\rangle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
0\\
1\end{array}\right)\,.

The Hamiltonian for spin-
s
model can be calculated by the fused higher spin transfer matrices. For more details we refer to
[

,
22

For higher spin cases, the iso-BAE flow again occurs at

(87)

where the Hamiltonian with SU(2) symmetry is

(88)

and the deformation term is

\begin{split}\mathbf{V}^{(1)}_{\alpha}&=\left(\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}+\frac{1}{\alpha-1/2}\right)\mathbf{S}^{z}_{L}+\left(\frac{1}{\alpha+1/2}-\frac{1}{\alpha-1/2}\right)\big(\mathbf{S}^{z}_{L}\big)^{2}\\

The

Here the only difference between different

(91)

The proof of commutativity of

Appendix B
TQ
-relation and rational
Q
-system

The spectrum of integrable spin chains is encoded in the solutions of BAE. However, working directly with BAE has a number of drawbacks. First, there are solutions of BAE whose corresponding Bethe states are not eigenstates of the transfer matrix. These solutions are called unphysical solutions. Second, as we have seen in the main text, in the iso-BAE flow the naive BAE do not give the complete physical states. To solve these problems, it is better to apply the
TQ
-relation or the rational
Q
-system.

The
TQ
-relation for open spin chains have been known for some time
[
16
]
(see
[
32
]
for detailed discussions and more references). It can be derived either from ABA or by an explicit construction of Baxter’s
Q
-operator
[
48
,
49
,
50
]
. The
TQ
-relation is sufficient to describe all physical states, which solves the second drawback mentioned above. However,
TQ
-relation still has unphysical solutions. To solve this problem, we exploit the rational
Q
-system, which contain all and only physical solutions. This powerful new approach was developed only recently
[
12
,
9
]
for open chains. In this section, we review the
TQ
-relation and the rational
Q
-system for the three type of iso-BAE flows in the main text. The rational
Q
-system for XXX
s
model with open boundary conditions have not been worked out in the literature. It is constructed in this section, which also constitutes a new result of this work. We also present concrete examples which demonstrate the structure of the Hilbert space based on the solutions of rational
Q
-systems.

B.1
The XXX
1/2
model

The
TQ
-relation of the open XXX
1/2
reads

-uT_{1/2}(u)Q(u)=u^{+}T_{0}^{+}(u)g^{-}(u)Q^{2-}(u)+u^{-}T_{0}^{-}(u)f^{+}(u)Q^{2+}(u),

(92)

where
T_{0}(u)=u^{2L}
and
Q(u)
is a polynomial whose zeros are the Bethe roots,

Q(u)=\prod_{m=1}^{M}(u-u_{m})(u+u_{m})\,.

(93)

T_{1/2}(u)
is the eigenvalue of the transfer matrix defined in Sec.
A
, which is also a polynomial in
u
. The BAE can be obtained from
TQ
-relation (
92
) by taking
u=u_{m}
. Therefore, solutions of the
TQ
relation also solve BAE.

Rational
Q
-system

As mentioned above, solutions to the
TQ
-relation contain unphysical solutions
[
10
,
12
,
9
,
13
]
. Therefore, we need additional conditions to restrict the number of solutions. One nice way to achieve this is the rational
Q
-system
[
10
,
12
,
9
]
.
The rational Q-system for the spin-
1/2
XXX spin chain with open boundaries
[
9
]
is defined by the following set of functional equations called
QQ
-relations

\begin{split}&uQ_{1,n}=f^{(n-1)-}Q_{1,n-1}^{+}-g^{(n-1)+}Q_{1,n-1}^{-},\quad n=0,1,\ldots,M\\
&uQ_{0,n}Q_{1,n-1}=Q_{0,n-1}^{+}Q_{1,n}^{-}-Q_{0,n-1}^{-}Q_{1,n}^{+},\quad n=0,1,\ldots,L-M,\end{split}

(94)

where
Q_{1,0}(u)
is identified with Baxter’s
Q
-polynomial in the
TQ
-relation. We require that all the functions
Q_{a,s}(u)
are polynomials in
u
and solve the
QQ
-relations (
94
) with the boundary conditions

\displaystyle Q_{0,0}(u)=u^{2L},\qquad Q_{1,0}(u)=\prod_{m=1}^{M}(u-u_{m})(u+u_{m})=\prod_{m=1}^{M}(u^{2}-u_{m}^{2}).

(95)

We find that even though the BAE (
5
) for the iso-BAE systems with different values of
\alpha
are identical, the rational
Q
-systems differ. Therefore the
Q
-system indeed describe the full spectrum for the iso-BAE flow. By solving the
Q
-system, we indeed obtain two kinds of solutions. The solutions associated with type-I states remain the same for the iso-BAE systems with different values of
\alpha
, while the solutions associated with type-II states differ.

The undeformed model

The undeformed model
\mathbf{H}_{0}
in (
1
) is obtained in the limit
\alpha,\beta\to\infty
and is simply the open XXX spin chain with free boundary conditions at both ends. In terms of the
TQ
-relation or the rational
Q
-system, this limit corresponds to taking
f(u)=g(u)=1
. This model has the global SU(2) symmetry. The highest weight states correspond to the type-I states in the deformed model.

B.2
XXZ model

The
TQ
-relation for the XXZ spin chain becomes

\displaystyle-\sinh(2u)T_{1/2}(u)Q(u)=

\displaystyle\,\sinh(2u+\eta)\sinh^{2L}(u+\tfrac{\eta}{2})g^{-}(u)Q^{--}(u)

(96)

\displaystyle\,+\sinh(2u-\eta)\sinh^{2L}(u-\tfrac{\eta}{2})f^{+}(u)Q^{++}(u)

with

\displaystyle Q(u)=\prod_{k=1}^{M}\sinh(u-u_k)\sinh(u+u_k)\,.

(97)

and
T_{1/2}(u)
being the eigenvalue of the transfer matrix. Taking
u=u_{m}
in the
TQ
-relation (
96
), we obtain the BAE

\frac{g(u_{m}-\eta/2)}{f(u_{m}+\eta/2)}\left(\frac{\sinh(u_m+\eta/2)}{\sinh(u_m-\eta/2)}\right)^{2L}\prod_{k\neq m}^{M}S^{\rm XXZ}(u_{m},u_{k})=1,

(98)

where the scattering phase becomes

S^{\rm XXZ}(u_{m},u_{k})=\frac{\sinh(u_m - u_k -\eta)}{\sinh(u_m - u_k+\eta)}\frac{\sinh(u_m + u_k -\eta)}{\sinh(u_m + u_k+\eta)}.

(99)

The boundary polynomials are

f(u)=g(-u)=\sinh(u - \alpha\eta)\sinh(u+ \beta\eta).

(100)

We have introduced the shorthand notation
F^{\pm}(u)=F(u\pm\tfrac{\eta}{2})
. In the trigonometric case, sometimes it is more convenient to work with the multiplicative variables
t=e^{u}
. In terms of multiplicative variables we have
F^{n\pm}(t)=F(tq^{\pm n/2})
and with a slight abuse of notation

\displaystyle f(t)=g(t^{-1})=\frac{1}{4}(tq^{-\alpha}-t^{-1}q^{\alpha})(tq^{\beta}-t^{-1}q^{-\beta}).

(101)

Rational
Q
-system

The rational
Q
-system for the XXZ model with open boundaries is similar to the
Q
-system for the XXX model. The main difference is that
Q
-polynomials are now Laurent polynomials in
t
instead of polynomials in
u
. With
q=e^{\eta}
, the
QQ
-relations for the XXZ spin chain become
[
9
]

\begin{split}&(t^{2}-t^{-2})Q_{1,n}=f^{(n-1)-}Q_{1,n-1}^{+}-g^{(n-1)+}Q_{1,n-1}^{-},\quad n=1,\ldots,M,\\
&(t^{2}-t^{-2})Q_{0,n}Q_{1,n-1}=Q_{0,n-1}^{+}Q_{1,n}^{-}-Q_{0,n-1}^{-}Q_{1,n}^{+},\quad n=1,\ldots,L-M\,.\end{split}

(102)

To solve the
Q
-system, we require that the functions
Q_{a,s}(t)
to be Laurent polynomials,

Q_{a,s}(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{L}c^{(a,s)}_{n}(t^{2n}+t^{-2n})

(103)

with the following boundary condition

\begin{split}&Q_{0,0}(t)=\frac{1}{2^{2L}}\left(t-t^{-1}\right)^{2L},\\
&Q_{1,0}(t)=Q(t)=\frac{1}{2^{2M}}\prod_{m=1}^{M}\left(tt_{m}^{-1}-t^{-1}t_{m}\right)\,.\\
\end{split}

(104)

The iso-BAE flow is at
\beta=\alpha-1
where
f^{+}=g^{-}
and they cancel out in (
98
). In this case, the BAE (
98
) describe the type-I states.

The type-II states correspond to the solutions with a fixed root
v_{M}=\pm(\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2})\eta
and the rest
(M-1)
roots being the solutions of the following reduced BAE

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{g}(v_{m}-\tfrac{\eta}{2})}{\tilde{f}(v_{m}+\tfrac{\eta}{2})}\left(\frac{\sinh(v_m+\tfrac{\eta}{2})}{\sinh(v_m-\tfrac{\eta}{2})}\right)^{2L}\prod_{j\neq m}^{M-1}S^{\text{XXZ}}(v_{m},v_{j})=1,\qquad m=1,\ldots,M-1\,,

(105)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(u)=\tilde{g}(-u)=\sinh(u+\alpha\eta)\sinh(u-(\alpha-1)\eta)\,.

(106)

We again have an effective BAE with
\tilde{\alpha}=-\alpha
and
\tilde{\beta}=-\beta=-(\alpha-1)
.

The structure of Hilbert space in the XXZ case is the same as the XXX case described above, when
q
is not a root of unity. We shall not repeat the results here.

For the higher spin XXX

We can generalize the rational

-relations by requiring
\frac{1}{\prod_{k=1}^{2s-1}(u-\mathrm{i}(s-k))^{L}}Q_{0,1}
and the rest of

Structure of the Hilbert space

As an explicit example, we focus on the spin-1 model. Higher spin cases are analogous. For
\mathrm{XXX}_{1}
model, we have

Q_{0,0}(u)=\left(u+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\right)^{2L}\left(u-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\right)^{2L}=(u^{+})^{2L}(u^{-})^{2L}.

(111)

The iso-BAE condition is the same
\beta=\alpha-1
as before. The iso-BAE spin-1 Hamiltonian can be found in (
76
).

The
{\rm XXX}_{1}
iso-BAE spectrum again splits into two parts. The type-I states are invariant under the change of parameter
\alpha
; type-II states have a fixed Bethe root at
v_{M}=\mathrm{i}(\alpha-\frac{1}{2})
and the energies change with
\alpha
.

From the numerical solutions to the rational
Q
-system, we find that the kernel operator
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}^{(1)}
is spanned by the type-I states. In addition, similar to the spin-
1/2
case, the type-II states are doubly degenerate. Specifically, for a type-II state with
M\leq L
, we have

\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}^{(1)}|\{v_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\rangle_{\alpha}^{\rm II}=|\{v_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\cup\{\infty\times p^{\prime}\}\rangle_{\alpha}^{\rm II}

(112)

is a degenerate eigenstate of the transfer matrix (and Hamiltonian) with
|\{v_{j}\}_{j=1}^{M}\rangle_{\alpha}^{\rm II}
.

The Hilbert space decomposes into type-I and type-II states in the spin-1 case. This can be shown by the following counting.
First of all, from combinatorics, the total number of states with
M
magnons on the ferromagnetic state
|++\cdots+\rangle
is

\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(L,M)=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor M/2\rfloor}\begin{pmatrix}L\\
j\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}L-j\\
M-2j\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(L,2L-M),\quad M\leq L

(113)

where
j
is the number of
|-\rangle
and
(M-2j)
is the number of
|0\rangle
in a state with
M
magnons in total. It is straightforward to verify that

\sum_{M=0}^{2L}\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(L,M)=3^{L}.

(114)

From BAE analysis, the number of type-I states is equal to the number of highest weight states for the undeformed model,
i.e.

\mathcal{N}_{\text{I}}^{(1)}(L,M)=\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(L,M)-\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(L,M-1),\quad M\leq L.

(115)

The number of type-II states for
M\leq L
is

\mathcal{N}_{\text{II}}^{(1)}(L,M)=\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(L,M)-\mathcal{N}^{(1)}_{\rm I}(L,M)=\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(L,M-1),\quad 1\leq M\leq L,

(116)

and
\mathcal{N}_{\text{II}}^{(1)}(L,0)=0
.Since there’s no type-I states for
M>L
, we have

\mathcal{N}_{\text{II}}^{(1)}(L,M)=\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(L,M)=\mathcal{N}^{(1)}(L,2L-M),\quad M>L.

(117)

All the counts are also supported by numerical checks. We present an example of the number of type-I and type-II states for the spin-1 iso-BAE systems with
L=4
in Fig.

.

Appendix C
\alpha\to 1^{+}
limit

When we take the limit
\alpha\to 1^{+}
(or effectively
\alpha\to 0^{-}
, which is analogous and we shall not expand the discussion), one of the boundary magnetic fields diverges.

This implies that we can separate the spectrum into the finite-valued part and the infinite-valued part. Since the spectrum of the type-I states remain intact, they should belong to the finite-valued part of the spectrum.

Effectively, we can decouple the spin at the first site, since the boundary magnetic field
\frac{-1}{\alpha-1}\sigma^{z}_{1}
in (
3
) diverges. The finite-valued part of the spectrum consists of the eigenstates in the form of

|\!\uparrow\rangle_{1}\otimes|\psi\rangle_{2,3,\cdots L}.

(118)

After freezing the first spin, we obtain an effective Hamiltonian acting on
2,3,\cdots L
sites with an effective boundary magnetic field on the second site. More specifically, the state
|\psi\rangle_{2,3,\cdots L}
is hence an eigenstate of the effective Hamiltonian,

\mathbf{H}_{\rm eff}=\sum_{n=2}^{L-1}\left(\vec{\sigma}_{n}\cdot\vec{\sigma}_{n+1}-1\right)+(\sigma^{z}_{L}-1)+(\sigma^{z}_{1}-1),

(119)

which is an open
{\rm XXX}_{1/2}
model with
(L-1)
sites and
\alpha_{\rm eff}=-\beta_{\rm eff}=1
, cf. the footnote in Sec.
III
of the main text.

Since the effective Hamiltonian (
119
) has only
(L-1)
sites, the BAE again coincide with the iso-BAE case, but with a different mechanism,

\frac{g(u_{m}-\mathrm{i}/2)}{f(u_{m}+\mathrm{i}/2)}\left(\frac{u_{m}+\mathrm{i}/2}{u_{m}-\mathrm{i}/2}\right)^{2L-2}\prod_{k\neq m}^{M}S(u_{m},u_{k})=1\,\,\Rightarrow\,\,\left(\frac{u_{m}+\mathrm{i}/2}{u_{m}-\mathrm{i}/2}\right)^{2L}\prod_{k\neq m}^{M}S(u_{m},u_{k})=1,

(120)

where
f(u)=(u-\mathrm{i})^{2}=g(-u)
in this case.

Similarly, we can use the rational
Q
-system to study the spectrum of the effective Hamiltonian. We conclude that the effective Hamiltonian (
119
) with
(L-1)
sites again contains the type-I solutions of the iso-BAE systems (with
L
sites). The same observation in the
q
-deformed case has been used in
[
51
]
to prove the reality of the spectrum of the quantum-group-invariant XXZ spin chain in the easy-plane regime.

Appendix D
Ground states along the iso-BAE flow

Since all eigenstates are classified into type-I and type-II, one natural question is which class the ground state(s) belong(s) to. It turns out that this depends on the value of
\alpha
and the ground state degeneracies change at some critical values. To this end, it is more convenient to introduce the boundary magnetic fields

\displaystyle h_{L}=-\frac{1}{\alpha-1},\qquad h_{R}=\frac{1}{\alpha}

(121)

Along the iso-BAE flow, they satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{L}}+\frac{1}{h_{R}}=1\,,

(122)

which is a hyperbola in the
h_{L}
-
h_{R}
plane, as is shown in figure
6
.

h_{L}=-\frac{1}{\alpha-1}
and
h_{R}=\frac{1}{\alpha}

h_{R}=\frac{h_{L}}{h_{L}-1}
. The SU(2) symmetric point is at the origin.

The right branch (colored in red) of the hyperbola correspond to
h_{R}>1
or equivalently
0<\alpha<1
. The left branch (colored in blue) of the hyperbola correspond to
h_{R}<1
, which is further divided into
0<h_{R}<1
and
h_{R}<0
, corresponding to
\alpha>1
and
\alpha<0
respectively. A quantum phase transition occur at the point
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=1
. We discuss the cases for even and odd
L
separately.

•

Even
L

0\leq\alpha\leq 1
, the ground state is a type-II state and is doubly degenerate. The ground state energy varies with
\alpha
. For

, the ground state is a type-I eigenstate. It is unique and the ground state energy does not vary with
\alpha
. Therefore, when varying
\alpha
from
-\infty
to
+\infty
, there will be two phase transition points, corresponding to
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=1
where the ground state degeneracies change.

•

Odd
L
: For
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
, the ground states is a type-II. However, it is not doubly degenerate as for even
L
. This is due to the fact that the ground state is in the
(L+1)/2
-magnon sector where
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
becomes a projector and acts trivially on the type-II state. The energy of the state flows with
\alpha
. For
\alpha>1\cup\alpha<0
, the ground state is a type-I, which is unique and does not change with
\alpha
. Therefore in this case the ground state degeneracy do not change, but the type of the ground state is changed at
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=1
.

The ground state energy as a function of
\alpha
is plotted in Fig.
7
. As we can see clearly, the type of the ground state changes at
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=1
. It may seem confusing that the ground state energy jumps from finite value to
-\infty
when
\alpha
changes from
1^{+}
to
1^{-}
(and
0^{-}
to
0^{+}
). We explained in Sec.
C
that in the
\alpha\to 1^{+}
(and
\alpha\to 0^{-}
) limit, the ground state energy is the same as the effective Hamiltonian (
119
) with
(L-1)
sites, which coincide with the ground state energy of the iso-BAE system with
\alpha<0
or
\alpha>1
,
i.e.
the ground state energy of type-I states. However, when we take the other limit
\alpha\to 0^{+}
(and
\alpha\to 1^{-}
), one of the boundary spins is fixed to be
|\downarrow\rangle
, where the ground state energy goes to
-\infty
, as shown in Fig.
7
for instance.

Let us further comment that in
[
52
]
, the authors have studied the ground state degeneracies for generic
\alpha
,
\beta
in the thermodynamic limit. Our study is different from
[
52
]
in two aspects. Firstly we focus on the iso-BAE flow, which are the models on the two hyperbolas. Second, our result is valid at any finite length of the spin chain.

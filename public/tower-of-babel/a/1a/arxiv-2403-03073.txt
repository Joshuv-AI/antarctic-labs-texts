Entanglement of elliptic curves upon base extension
Tori Day and Rylan Gajek-Leonard

Abstract
. Fix distinct primes p p and q q and let E E be an elliptic curve defined over a number field K K . The ( p , q ) (p,q) -entanglement type of E E over K K is the isomorphism class of the group Gal ⁡ ( K ⁡ ( E ⁡ [ p ] ) ∩ K ⁡ ( E ⁡ [ q ] ) / K ) \operatorname{Gal}(K(E[p])\cap K(E[q])/K) . The size of this group measures the extent to which the image of the mod p ​ q pq Galois representation attached to E E fails to be a direct product of the mod p p and mod q q images. In this article, we study how the ( p , q ) (p,q) -entanglement group varies over different base fields. We prove that for each prime ℓ \ell dividing the greatest common divisor of the size of the mod p p and q q images, there are infinitely many fields L / K L/K such that the entanglement over L L is cyclic of order ℓ \ell . We also classify all possible ( 2 , q ) (2,q) -entanglement types that can occur as the base field L L varies.

1.
Introduction

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over a number field
K
. For any integer
n\geq 2
, the action of
G_{K}=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{K}/K)
on the
n
-torsion points
E[n]
yields a representation

\rho_{E,n}:G_{K}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(E[n])\cong\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})

whose image is well-defined up to conjugation. A classical problem, often referred to as Mazur’s ‘Program B’
[
6
]
, is to determine all possible images of this representation. When
n=p
is prime and
E
does not have complex multiplication, a well-known theorem of Serre
[
8
]
asserts that
\rho_{E,p}
is surjective for all but finitely many
exceptional
primes
p
. Recent work of Zywina
[
9
]
classifies the possible images of
\rho_{E,p}
for rational elliptic curves at exceptional primes.

When
n
is composite, the problem of classifying images becomes even more subtle. This is due to the fact that it is not always possible to determine the image mod
n
solely in terms of the images modulo the prime factors of
n
. In general, one only has an injection

\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,n}\hookrightarrow\prod_{p\mid n}\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,p^{\operatorname{ord}_{p}(n)}}.

The failure of surjectivity in the above map can be attributed to coincidental intersections, or
entanglements
, between various torsion fields at primes (or prime powers) dividing
n
.

For example, if
n=pq
is the product of distinct primes
p
and
q
then the index of
\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,pq}
inside
\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,p}\times\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,q}
is equal to the degree

[K(E[p])\cap K(E[q]):K],

where the
p
and
q
-torsion fields
K(E[p])
and
K(E[q])
are (by definition) those fixed by the kernels of
\rho_{E,p}
and
\rho_{E,q}
, respectively. If this degree is nontrivial,
E
is said to have
(p,q)
-entanglement over
K
.
Thus,
(p,q)
-entanglements occur precisely when the mod
pq
image fails to be the full direct product of the mod
p
and mod
q
images. The entanglement
type
is the isomorphism class of the Galois group

\operatorname{Gal}(K(E[p])\cap K(E[q])/K),

which we denote
T_{p,q}(E/K)
. Knowing the entanglement type, together with the mod
p
and mod
q
image, allows one to compute the full image mod
pq
.

In this article, we consider the behavior of entanglements along base extensions. Specifically, we ask the following question:

Question 1.1
.

For a fixed elliptic curve
E/K
and distinct primes
p
and
q
, what groups
T_{p,q}(E/L)
occur as we vary over base extensions
L/K
?

Our focus is therefore on the set

(1)

\operatorname{Ent}_{p,q}(E/K)=\{T_{p,q}(E/L)\mid\text{$L/K$}\}.

This set is finite and consists of finite groups (even when
L/K
is an infinite extension), since for any
L/K
we have (see Proposition
2.2
)

(2)

\#T_{p,q}(E/L)\,\bigg|\,\,\gcd(\#\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,p},\#\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,q}).

It is easy to see that
\operatorname{Ent}_{p,q}(E/K)
always contains the trivial group: if
L
is any field containing
K(E[pq])
then
T_{p,q}(E/L)=1
. For arbitrary
L/K
, the possibilities for
(p,q)
-entanglement are less obvious. (For example, the entanglement can both increase and decrease in size over different base fields – see Examples
1.4
and Examples
2.4
).
To ease notation, we define

d(E,p,q)=\gcd(\#\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,p},\#\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,q}).

Our main theorem, stated as follows (see also Theorem
4.3
), shows that in addition to the trivial group, the set
\operatorname{Ent}_{p,q}(E/K)
contains
all cyclic groups of prime order dividing
d(E,p,q)
, i.e., it contains the maximal number of prime-order cyclic groups permissible by (
2
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\ell
be a prime dividing
d(E,p,q)
. Then there exists a field
L\subseteq K(E[pq])
such that

T_{p,q}(E/L)=\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}.

For an extension
L/K
, let
\rho_{E/L,n}
denote the restriction of
\rho_{E,n}
to the subgroup
\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{K}/L)\subseteq G_{K}
. Theorem
1.2
immediately implies the following corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

For each
\ell
as in Theorem
1.2
, there is a field
L/K
such that the image of
\rho_{E/L,pq}
contains a normal subgroup of index
\ell
.

Remark 1.5
.

If
E
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
then Theorem
1.2
implies that

\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\in\operatorname{Ent}_{p,q}(E/\mathbb{Q})

for all distinct odd primes
p
and
q
. This is due to the fact that
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(E[p])
(by the Weil pairing), so
2
divides
d(E,p,q)
whenever
p
and
q
are both odd. When
p=2
, since the density of elliptic curves with nonsquare discriminant is 1 (those with square discriminant have
j
-invariant
1728+t^{2}
), one still expects
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\in\operatorname{Ent}_{2,q}(E/\mathbb{Q})
for ‘most’ elliptic curves.

If both the mod
p
and
q
representations of
E/K
are surjective (true for all but finitely many
(p,q)
by
[
8
]
) then

d(E,p,q)=\gcd\big((p^{2}-1)(p^{2}-p),(q^{2}-1)(q^{2}-q)\big).

It is straightforward to check that this integer is always divisible by 6 (for any choice of
p
and
q
) and that it is divisible by
p
if
q\equiv\pm 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ p
. Theorem
1.2
and divisibility (
2
) immediately imply the following.

Corollary 1.6
.

If
\rho_{E,p}
and
\rho_{E,q}
are surjective then

(1)

\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\in\operatorname{Ent}_{p,q}(E/K)
, and

(2)

if
q\equiv\pm 1\ \mathrm{mod}\ p
then
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\in\operatorname{Ent}_{p,q}(E/K)
.

We take this idea a step further in Theorem
5.1
where we show that when
p=2
,

\operatorname{Ent}_{2,q}(E/\mathbb{Q})\subseteq\{1,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z},S_{3}\},

for all odd primes
q
, with equality if
\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,2q}
is maximal.

The size of
d(E,p,q)
can be drastically smaller when either
p
or
q
is exceptional, or when
E
has complex multiplication, and in these cases the divisibility (
2
) forces
\operatorname{Ent}_{p,q}(E/K)
to also shrink in size. We remark that, for any given elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
, the number
d(E,p,q)

is explicitly computable using
[
9
]
.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is constructive in the sense that it describes an explicit field
L
(as the fixed field of a certain subgroup of
\operatorname{Gal}(K(E[pq]/K)
) which yields the described cyclic entanglement. It does not, however, describe
all such
fields. Indeed, if there exists some
L_{0}/K
such that
T_{p,q}(E/L_{0})=T
for a fixed group
T
, we suspect that the set

(3)

\{L/K\mid T_{p,q}(E/L)=T\}

is infinite. We prove this for
T=\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
, where
\ell
is as in Theorem
1.2
(see Theorem
4.5
). Note that the set (
3
) has an obvious partial order under field inclusion. It would be interesting to obtain a description of the
minimal
fields
L
which achieve a desired entanglement. We suspect that such minimal fields always occur as subfields of
K(E[pq])
.

1.1.
Related questions and results

There has been much work in recent years on classifying all elliptic curves defined over a
fixed
base field
K
(often
K=\mathbb{Q}
) having some prescribed entanglement properties. See, for example,
[
2
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
, and
[
5
]
, where variations of the following question are considered.

Question 1.7
.

For which tuples
(p,q,T)
do there exist infinitely many (non
\bar{K}
-isomorphic) elliptic curves
E/K
having
(p,q)
-entanglement of type
T
?

This type of question is amenable to study using the theory of modular curves, where (roughly speaking) one constructs a modular curve whose
K
-rational points correspond to elliptic curves with the desired entanglement properties. For example, by considering certain genus 0 and genus 1 (positive rank) modular curves, it is shown in
[
3
]
that the only
unexplained
(see Definition 4.5 of
op. cit.
) entanglement types
T
which can occur for infinitely many non-
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
-isomorphic elliptic curves are
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
, and
S_{3}
, and that they can occur only at the pairs
(p,q)
in the sets
\{(2,3),(2,5),(2,7),(2,13),(3,5)\}
,
\{(2,3),(2,5),(2,7)\}
, and
\{(2,3)\}
, respectively.

We take a different approach, by instead beginning with a fixed tuple
(p,q,E/K)
, and considering what
(p,q)
-entanglements are produced upon base extensions by
L/K
. Other related questions are as follows:

(A)

Is there an algorithm that computes
\operatorname{Ent}_{p,q}(E/K)
directly from the group structure of
\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,p}
,
\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,q}
, and
\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,pq}
? In other words, if one knows the images mod
p
and
q
, and how the
(p,q)
-entanglement over
K
fits into them, is it possible to determine all
(p,q)
-entanglement types over every extension of
K
? We discuss this question when
p=2
in §
5
.

(B)

For fixed
(K,p,q)
, does there always exist an elliptic curve
E/K
having nontrivial
(p,q)
-entanglement over
K
? When
K=\mathbb{Q}
and
p=2
, the answer to this question is ‘yes’: one can force
explained
(see
[
3
]
) entanglement using the Weil pairing. Specifically, any elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
with
j
-invariant

1728\pm qt^{2},

where
\pm
is the sign of
(-1)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}
and
t\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
, will have
(2,q)
-entanglement: the
j
-invariant forces
\Delta_{E}\equiv\pm q\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}
, thus
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta_{E}})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\pm q})
is contained in both
\mathbb{Q}(E[2])
and
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(E[q])
.

(C)

Suppose that
E
has no
(p,q)
-entanglement over its minimal basefield
K=\mathbb{Q}(j(E))
. Is the set
\operatorname{Ent}_{p,q}(E/K)
always nontrivial? That is, can one always base extend by some
L/K
so that
E
gains
(p,q)
-entanglement? When
p
and
q
are odd and
E
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, the answer to this question is ‘yes’ (see Remark
1.5
). See also Remark
2.9
.

2.
Entanglement Over Number Fields

Fix an algebraic extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
, an elliptic curve
E/K
, and distinct primes
p
and
q
. Define

\displaystyle K_{p,q}(E)

\displaystyle=K(E[p])\cap K(E[q]),

\displaystyle T_{p,q}(E/K)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Gal}\big(K_{p,q}(E)/K\big),

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ent}_{p,q}(E/K)

\displaystyle=\{T_{p,q}(E/L)\mid\text{$L/K$}\}.

We often suppress the elliptic curve
E
from the notation for the
(p,q)
-entanglement field and simply write
K_{p,q}
. Since the compositum

K\mathbb{Q}(E[p])=K(E[p])
we note that
[K(E[p]):K]=[\mathbb{Q}(E[p]):\mathbb{Q}(E[p])\cap K]
divides the integer
[\mathbb{Q}(E[p]):\mathbb{Q}]
, hence the group
T_{p,q}(E/K)
is always finite.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that
E
has
(p,q)
-entanglement
over an extension
L/K
if
T_{p,q}(E/L)
is nontrivial. The
entanglement type
of
E
over
L
is the isomorphism class of
T_{p,q}(E/L)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For any algebraic extension
L/K
we have

\#T_{p,q}(E/L)\,\,\big|\,\,\gcd\big([K(E[p]):K],[K(E[q]):K]\big).

In particular,
\displaystyle\sup_{L/K}\#T_{p,q}(E/L)<\infty
and the set
\operatorname{Ent}_{p,q}(E/K)
is finite.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\#T_{p,q}(E/L)

\displaystyle=[L_{p,q}:L]

\displaystyle\big|\,\,\gcd\big([L(E[p]):L],[L(E[q]):L]\big)

\displaystyle=\gcd\big([K(E[p]):L\cap K(E[p])],[K(E[q]):L\cap K(E[q])]\big)

\displaystyle\,\,\big|\,\,\gcd\big([K(E[p]):K],[K(E[q]):K]\big).

∎

2.1.
Group-theoretic formulation

Fix a basis for
E[pq]
and let
\rho_{E,pq}:G_{K}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/pq\mathbb{Z})
denote the Galois representation corresponding to the action of
G_{K}
on
E[pq]
. Following
[
3
]
, the group
\mathcal{G}:=\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,pq}
is said to
represent
a
(p,q)
-entanglement if

\langle\ker\pi_{p}\cap\mathcal{G},\ker\pi_{q}\cap\mathcal{G}\rangle\subsetneq\mathcal{G},

where
\pi_{p}
and
\pi_{q}
are the natural reduction mod
p
and
q
maps on
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/pq\mathbb{Z})
. In particular,
E
has
(p,q)
-entanglement if and only if
\mathcal{G}
represents a
(p,q)
-entanglement. From this perspective, the entanglement type of
E
over
K
is

T_{p,q}(E/K)\cong\mathcal{G}/\langle\ker\pi_{p}\cap\mathcal{G},\ker\pi_{q}\cap\mathcal{G}\rangle.

2.2.
Stable and decreasing entanglement

A simple way to decrease entanglement size is to base-extend by a subfield
L
of the
(p,q)
-entanglement field
K_{p,q}
, in which case one can identify the entanglement over
L
with a subgroup of the entanglement over
K
.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
K\subseteq L\subseteq K_{p,q}
then

T_{p,q}(E/L)\cong\operatorname{Gal}(K_{p,q}/L)\subseteq T_{p,q}(E/K)
.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that if
L\subseteq K_{p,q}
then
L(E[p])=K(E[p])
and
L(E[q])=K(E[q])
.
∎

2.3.
Entanglement along subfields of
K(E[pq])
.

The following proposition allows one to determine the size of the entanglement along subfields of
K(E[pq])
.

Proposition 2.8
.

If
L/K
is a subfield of
K(E[pq])
then

\#T_{p,q}(E/L)=\#T_{p,q}(E/K)\frac{[L:K]}{[K(E[p])\cap L:K][K(E[q])\cap L:K]}.

In particular, if
L
trivially intersects
K(E[p])
and
K(E[q])
then

\#T_{p,q}(E/L)=\#T_{p,q}(E/K)[L:K].

Proof.

Since
K(E[pq])=L(E[pq])
we have

(4)

[K(E[pq]):L]=\frac{[L(E[p]):L][L(E[q]):L]}{[L_{p,q}:L]}.

We can also write

(5)

[K(E[pq]):L]=\frac{[K(E[pq]):K]}{[L:K]}=\frac{[K(E[p]):K][K(E[q]):K]}{[L:K][K_{p,q}:K]}.

Combining (
4
) and (
5
) we have

\displaystyle[L_{p,q}:L]

\displaystyle=[L:K][K_{p,q}:K]\frac{[L(E[p]):L]}{[K(E[p]):K]}\frac{[L(E[q]):L]}{[K(E[q]):K]}.

The result now follows from the fact that
[L(E[p]):L]=[K(E[p]):L\cap K(E[p])]
, and similarly for
q
.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

Proposition

provides a partial answer to Question §

(C): if

) from both the

-torsion fields then

Corollary 2.10
.

Suppose that

has no
(p,q)
-entanglement over
\mathbb{Q}
. If
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}})
and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{2}})

and
\mathbb{Q}(E[q])

In particular, for all odd primes

and

T_{p,q}(E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\pm pq}))=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},

where
\pm=\operatorname{sgn}(-1)^{\frac{p+q}{2}+1}

Proof.

Since
E
has no
(p,q)
-entanglement over
\mathbb{Q}
, the field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}D_{2}})

. ∎

Remark 2.11
.

The existence of multiple distinct quadratic subfields of
p
and
q
-torsion fields is directly related to whether or not the primes
p
and
q
are exceptional for
E
. In what follows, we write
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p^{*}})
for the unique quadratic subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
. There are three cases to consider:

(1)

The only quadratic subfields of
\mathbb{Q}(E[p])
and
\mathbb{Q}(E[q])
are those forced by the Weil pairing, i.e., the fields
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p^{*}})
and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{q^{*}})
contained in the cyclotomic parts of
\mathbb{Q}(E[p])
and
\mathbb{Q}(E[q])
. In this case, base extending by
L=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p^{*}q^{*}})
forces a quadratic
(p,q)
-entanglement. (If
p=2
, replace
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p^{*}})
by
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta_{E}})
.)

(2)

Without loss of generality, only the
p
-torsion field contains a quadratic subfield
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
which is not forced by the Weil pairing. Then base extending to either
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{Dq^{*}})
or
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p^{*}q^{*}})
will force quadratic
(p,q)
-entanglement.

(3)

Both
\mathbb{Q}(E[p])
and
\mathbb{Q}(E[q])
have distinct quadratic subfields, call them
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D^{\prime}})
, different from those forced by the Weil pairing. Then base-extending to any of the fields
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{DD^{\prime}})
,
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{Dq^{*}})
,
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D^{\prime}p^{*}})
, or
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p^{*}q^{*}})
yields quadratic
(p,q)
-entanglement.

Since
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
and
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})
have unique index 2 subgroups, the latter two cases can only occur when only
p
(case (2)), or both
p
and
q
(case (3)), are
exceptional
primes. (Recall that a prime is called exceptional for
E
if the residual Galois representation at that prime is not surjective.) See Example
1.4
for a specific curve illustrating case (2) above.

Corollary 2.12
.

Suppose that
E/\mathbb{Q}
has no
(p,q)
-entanglement over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then

T_{p,q}(E/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{pq})^{+})=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}

where
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{pq})^{+}
is the maximal real subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{pq})
.

Since

has no
(p,q)
-entanglement over

, Lemma

implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}(E[p])\cap\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{pq})^{+}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})^{+}

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}(E[q])\cap\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{pq})^{+}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})^{+}.

that

Lemma 2.13
.

If
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})^{+}\subseteq F\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{pq})^{+}
and
F\cap\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})^{+}=\mathbb{Q}
then
F=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})^{+}
.

Proof.

Identify
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{pq})^{+}/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})^{+})
and
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{pq})^{+}/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{q})^{+})
with
\mathbb{Z}/\frac{q-1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}/\frac{p-1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
, respectively. If
F
properly contains
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})^{+}
then

is a proper subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}/\frac{q-1}{2}\mathbb{Z}

is

, and this forces

, a contradiction.
∎

3.
Entangleable Fields

In this section we consider the notion of
entangleable fields
. Roughly speaking, a pair of fields are entangleable if they can be ‘glued together’ by some auxilliary field. The relation between field entangleability and that of elliptic curves will be discussed in §
4
.

Let
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
be finite extensions of a fixed algebraic extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are
entangleable over
K
if there exists some
L/K
such that
LF_{1}=LF_{2}\supsetneq L
. The group
\operatorname{Gal}(LF_{i}/L)
is called the
entanglement type
of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
with respect to
L
.

Usually the field
K
is clear from context, in which case we simply say that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are
entangleable
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
be distinct squarefree integers. Then
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}})
and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{2}})
are entangleable by
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}D_{2}})
. This is easy to see directly from the definition, though we show it using Galois theory as well in Example
3.6
below.

Remark 3.3
.

In the case where
F:=F_{1}=F_{2}
, we see that
F
is
self-entangleable
in the sense that
LF_{1}=LF_{2}=F
has positive degree over
L
for any proper subextension
K\subseteq L\subsetneq F
.

We focus the remainder of this section on determining various sufficient conditions that will allow us to conclude when a pair of fields are entangleable. We begin by observing that not all field pairs are entangleable.

Proposition 3.4
.

If
[F_{1}:K]
and
[F_{2}:K]
are coprime then
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are not entangleable.

Proof.

Suppose they are entangleable by some
L
. Then the integer
d=[LF_{1}:L]
is larger than 1 by assumption, but since we can also write
d=[F_{1}:F_{1}\cap L]=[F_{2}:F_{2}\cap L]
, we see that
d
is a positive divisor of
[F_{1}:K]
and
[F_{2}:K]
, a contradiction.
∎

The coprimality hypothesis in Proposition
3.4
is essentially the only obstruction to fields being entangleable (see Proposition
3.11
below). In order to see this, we recast field entangleability in group-theoretic terms. Let
F/K
be any finite Galois extension containing both
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
and write
G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)
,
G_{1}=\operatorname{Gal}(F/F_{1})
, and
G_{2}=\operatorname{Gal}(F/F_{2})
.

Lemma 3.5
.

F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are entangleable by a subfield
L\subseteq F
if and only if there exists a subgroup
H\leq G
such that
H\cap G_{1}=H\cap G_{2}\subsetneq H
. If this is the case, then
L
and
H
are related by the correspondence

L=F^{H}\longleftrightarrow H=\operatorname{Gal}(F/L).

Furthermore, if
L/K
or
F_{i}/K
is Galois then
\operatorname{Gal}(LF_{i}/L)\cong H/(H\cap G_{i})
.

Proof.

This follows trivially from the Galois correspondence and the fact that the compositum of two fields corresponds to the intersection of the corresponding groups.
∎

In the next few examples, we illustrate the utility of Lemma
3.5
.

Example 3.6
.

Let
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
be squarefree integers. Let
\sigma
and
\tau
be generators of
G=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}},\sqrt{D_{2}})/\mathbb{Q})
that restrict to the nontrivial elements of
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}})/\mathbb{Q})
and
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{2}})/\mathbb{Q})
, respectively. Then the subgroup
H\leq G
generated by
\sigma\tau
trivially intersects
G_{i}=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}},\sqrt{D_{2}})/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{i}}))
, hence
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}})
and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{2}})
are entangleable by
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}},\sqrt{D_{2}})^{H}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{1}D_{2}})
.

Example 3.7
.

Fix positive integers
n
and
m
, neither of which are equal to
2
. The fields
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})
and
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})
are entangleable by
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{mn})^{+}:=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{nm}+\zeta_{nm}^{-1})
, the maximal real subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{nm})
. (If

this is obvious – see Remark

G=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{nm})/\mathbb{Q})

H\leq G
generated by

(complex conjugation) satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma

(again one has
G_{i}\cap H=1
).

Example 3.8
.

Let
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
be any two distinct

\mathbb{Q}

Letting

3.1.
Criteria for field entangleability

Proposition 3.9
.

Suppose that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are Galois over
K
and that
d:=\gcd([F_{1}:F_{1}\cap F_{2}],[F_{2}:F_{1}\cap F_{2}])>1
. For each prime prime
\ell\mid d
there is a subfield
L\subseteq F_{1}F_{2}
such that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are entangleable by
L
and
[LF_{i}:L]=\ell
.

Proof.

Recall that
G=\operatorname{Gal}(F_{1}F_{2}/K)
is isomorphic to the fibre product

\operatorname{Gal}(F_{1}/K)\times_{\operatorname{Gal}(F_{1}\cap F_{2}/K)}\operatorname{Gal}(F_{2}/K)=\{(g_{1},g_{2})\mid g_{1}|_{F_{1}\cap F_{2}}=g_{2}|_{F_{1}\cap F_{2}}\},

under the mapping
g\mapsto(g|_{F_{1}},g|_{F_{2}})
. Let
\ell
be a prime divisor of
d
. By Cauchy’s theorem, there are elements
g_{i}\in\operatorname{Gal}(F_{i}/F_{1}\cap F_{2})
of order

. Let
H
be the preimage in
G
of the subgroup
\langle(g_{1},g_{2})\rangle
of the fibre product above. (Note that
(g_{1},g_{2})
is indeed an element of the fibre product.) Then
H
has order
\ell
and
H\cap G_{i}
is trivial. To see this, let
g\in G
be the element mapping to
(g_{1},g_{2})
(thus
H=\langle g\rangle
) and observe that if
g\in G_{i}=\operatorname{Gal}(F_{1}F_{2}/F_{i})
then
g|_{F_{i}}=1=g_{i}
, a contradiction. It follows from Lemma
3.5
that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are entangleable by
L=F^{H}
.
∎

Notice that in Examples
3.6
and

F_{1}F_{2}
. This turns out to be true in general, as we now show.

.

Suppose that

and
F_{2}
are entangleable by a field

. If either

\operatorname{Gal}(L_{0}F_{i}/L_{0})\cong\operatorname{Gal}(LF_{i}/L)

Write

for the Galois closure of

\displaystyle LF_{i}

\displaystyle L_{0}

is normal in

and

are entangleable by

The final statement follows from the diagram below, where the dashed lines indicate extensions with isomorphic Galois groups.

Let

H

4.
Applications of Field Entanglement to Elliptic Curves

One can use the idea of field entanglements to study the size of the
(p,q)
-entanglement of
E
, as illustrated in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
be (not necessarily distinct) subfields of
K(E[p])
and
K(E[q])
, respectively. Suppose that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are entangleable by some
L/K
. Then,

(1)

[LF_{1}:L]
divides
\#T_{p,q}(E/L)
, and

(2)

if
F_{1}=K(E[p])
then

T_{p,q}(E/L)\cong\operatorname{Gal}(LF_{1}/L)\cong\operatorname{Gal}(K(E[p])/L\cap K(E[p]))\cong\operatorname{Gal}(F_{2}/L\cap F_{2}).

Proof.

(1) Since
F_{1}\subseteq K(E[p])
we know that
LF_{1}\subseteq LK(E[p])=L(E[p])
. Similarly,
LF_{2}\subseteq L(E[q])
. Thus, the field
LF_{1}=LF_{2}
is contained in both
L(E[p])
and
L(E[q])
, i.e.,
LF_{1}\subseteq L_{p,q}
, whence
[LF_{1}:L]
divides
[L_{p,q}:L]
.

(2) Suppose
F_{1}=K(E[p])
. Then

L(E[p])=LF_{1}=L_{p,q}.

The first equality is obvious, and the second follows from the inclusion
LF_{1}\subseteq L_{p,q}
(shown in part (1)) together with the fact that if
x\in L_{p,q}
then
x\in L(E[p])=LF_{1}
. The result now follows from the fact that
LF_{1}=LF_{2}
and

\operatorname{Gal}(LF_{i}/L)\cong\operatorname{Gal}(F_{i}/L\cap F_{i}).

∎

Notice in particular that if
T_{p,q}(E/K)=1
and if
K(E[p])
and
K(E[q])
contain a pair of subfields that are entangleable by some
L/K
, then Proposition
4.1
guarantees
T_{p,q}(E/L)\neq 1
. In other words, one can
force

(p,q)
-entanglements on
E
by entangling various subfields of its
p
and
q
-torsion fields. (See Example
1.4
for a concrete illustration of entangling quadratic subfields).

Example 4.2
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be the non-CM elliptic curve with Cremona label
1323l1
.
Neither 2 nor 3 are exceptional for
E
, so
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[2])/\mathbb{Q})=S_{3}
and
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[3])/\mathbb{Q})=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
. Observe that
E
has quadratic
(2,3)
-entanglement over
\mathbb{Q}
since
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta_{E}})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
and
\mathbb{Q}(E[2])
is not contained in
\mathbb{Q}(E[3])
. Let
F_{1}=\mathbb{Q}(E[2])
and let
F_{2}
be the Galois closure of the (unique) cubic subfield of
\mathbb{Q}(E[3])
. Then both
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are
S_{3}
-extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
and
F_{1}\cap F_{2}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
. By Example
3.8
, there are exactly five fields
L_{1},\dots,L_{5}\subseteq F_{1}F_{2}
which entangle
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
, and it follows from Proposition
4.1
(2) and Example
3.8
that

\displaystyle T_{2,3}(E/L)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},&\text{if $L=\mathbb{Q}$ or $L_{1}$}\\
1,&\text{if $L=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$}\\
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z},&\text{if $L=L_{2}$ or $L_{3}$}\\
S_{3}&\text{if $L=L_{4}$ or $L_{5}$.}\\
\end{cases}

(The same holds for any elliptic curve with
j
-invariant
1728-3t^{2}
,
t\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
, and which has maximal image mod
2
and mod 3.)

4.1.
Cyclic entanglements

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\ell
be a prime such that
\ell\mid\gcd\big([K(E[p]):K],[K(E[q]):K]\big)
. Then there exists a field
K\subseteq L\subseteq K(E[pq])
such that

T_{p,q}(E/L)=\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

First, suppose that
\ell\mid[K_{p,q}:K]
. Then there is a subfield
K\subseteq L\subseteq K_{p,q}
such that
[K_{p,q}:L]=\ell
. It follows from Proposition
2.3
that
T_{p,q}(E/L)=\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
.

Now assume that
\ell\nmid[K_{p,q}:K]
, and set
F_{1}=K(E[p])
and
F_{2}=K(E[q])
. Then
\ell\mid\gcd([F_{1}:F_{1}\cap F_{2}],[F_{2}:F_{1}\cap F_{2}])
and it follows from Proposition
3.9
that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are entangleable by a subfield
L\subseteq F_{1}F_{2}
with
[LF_{i}:L]=\ell
. The result now follows from Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Suppose that

(i)
p>2
, or
p=2
and
\Delta_{E}\notin K^{\times 2}
, and

(ii)
\sqrt{p^{*}},\sqrt{q^{*}}\notin K
.

Then for any prime
q\geq 3
there exists a field
K\subseteq L\subseteq K(E[pq])
such that

T_{p,q}(E/L)=\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

If
p
and
q
are both odd then
K(\sqrt{p^{*}})
and
K(\sqrt{q^{*}})
are nontrivial quadratic extensions of
K
contained in
K(\zeta_{p})\subseteq K(E[p])
and
K(\zeta_{q})\subseteq K(E[q])
, respectively. In particular,
\gcd\big([K(E[p]):K],[K(E[q]):K]\big)
is divisible by 2 and the result follows from Theorem
4.3
. If
p=2
and
\Delta_{E}\notin K^{\times 2}
then
K(\sqrt{\Delta_{E}})
is a quadratic extension of
K
contained in
K(E[2])
, and again we have that
2\mid\gcd\big([K(E[p]):K],[K(E[q]):K]\big)
.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\ell
be a prime such that
\ell\mid\gcd\big([K(E[p]):K],[K(E[q]):K]\big)
. Then the set

\{L/K\mid T_{p,q}(E/L)=\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}\}

is infinite.

Proof.

First, we claim that it suffices to prove that there exist subfields
F_{1},F_{2}
, and
L
of
K(E[pq])
such that
F_{1}/K
is Galois,
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are entangleable by
L
, and
T_{p,q}(E/L)=\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}.
Assuming that this is true, let
L^{\prime}/K
be any field for which
LL^{\prime}\cap F_{1}=L\cap F_{1}
. By Proposition
3.14
, there are infinitely many such
L^{\prime}
. By Proposition
3.13
, we know that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are entangleable by
LL^{\prime}
and

(6)

[LL^{\prime}F_{1}:LL^{\prime}]=[LF_{1}:L].

Now, since
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are entangleable by
L
, Proposition
4.1
.(1) implies

T_{p,q}(E/L)\cong\operatorname{Gal}(LF_{1}/L),

and since
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are also entangleable by
LL^{\prime}
, again Proposition
4.1
.(1) implies

T_{p,q}(E/LL^{\prime})\cong\operatorname{Gal}(LL^{\prime}F_{1}/LL^{\prime}).

The result now follows from (
6
).

It remains to show the existence of the fields
F_{1}
,
F_{2}
, and
L
. If
\ell\mid[K_{p,q}:K]
then there is a subfield
K\subseteq L\subseteq K_{p,q}
such that
[K_{p,q}:L]=\ell
, hence
T_{p,q}(E/L)=\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}
. In this case, take
F_{1}=F_{2}=K_{p,q}
and note that
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are entangleable by
L
(see Remark
3.3
). If
\ell\nmid[K_{p,q}:K]
, then by (the proof of) Theorem
4.3
there exist subfields
L\subseteq K(E[pq])
and
F_{2}\subseteq K(E[q])
such that
F_{1}=K(E[p])
and
F_{2}
are entangleable by
L
and

T_{p,q}(E/L)=\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}.

∎

5.
Classification of
(2,q)
-Entanglement Types

In this section we classify the possible
(p,q)
-entanglement types that can occur when
p=2
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Fix an odd prime
q
and let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve. Then

\operatorname{Ent}_{2,q}(E/\mathbb{Q})\subseteq\{1,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z},S_{3}\}.

Furthermore, if
E
has no
(2,q)
-entanglement over
\mathbb{Q}
and
\rho_{E,q}
is surjective then

\operatorname{Ent}_{2,q}(E/\mathbb{Q})=\begin{cases}\{1,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z},S_{3}\},&\text{if $\rho_{E,2}$ is surjective}\\
\{1,\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\},&\text{if $\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,2}\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ }\\
\{1,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\},&\text{if $\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,2}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ }\\
\{1\},&\text{if $\rho_{E,2}$ is trivial.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
d=\gcd(\#\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,2},\#\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,q})
. Then

d\mid\gcd(\#\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}),\#\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}))=6.

Proposition
2.2
therefore implies that the only possibilities for
(2,q)
-entanglement are
1,
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
,
S_{3}
and
\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}
. Type
\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}
cannot occur: if
T_{2,q}(E/L)=\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}
for some
L/\mathbb{Q}
then, since
\operatorname{Gal}(L(E[2])/L)
can be identified with the subgroup
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[2])/\mathbb{Q}(E[2])\cap L)
of
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[2])/\mathbb{Q})=S_{3}
, the inclusion
L\subseteq L_{2,q}\subseteq L(E[2])
would force
S_{3}
to have a subgroup with a
\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}
quotient, a contradiction.

Now assume that
\rho_{E,q}
is surjective and that
E
has no
(2,q)
-entanglement over
\mathbb{Q}
. If
\rho_{E,2}
is not surjective then
d=3,
2, or
1
, depending on whether the image of
\rho_{E,2}
has order 3, 2, or 1, respectively. The result in these cases now follows from Theorem
4.3
and Proposition
2.2
.

If
\rho_{E,2}
is surjective then
d=6
, so all
(2,q)
-entanglement types must have order dividing 6 by Proposition
2.2
. Clearly we can base extend to get trivial entanglement, and Theorem
4.3
yields entanglement types
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
. It remains to show that
S_{3}\in\operatorname{Ent}_{2,q}(E/\mathbb{Q})
. Since
\mathbb{Q}(E[pq])
is the compositum of
\mathbb{Q}(E[2])
and
\mathbb{Q}(E[q])
, and
E
has no
(2,q)
-entanglement over
\mathbb{Q}
, we know that

\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[2q])/\mathbb{Q})\cong\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\times\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})

By Lemma
3.5
(with
F=\mathbb{Q}(E[2q])
,
F_{1}=\mathbb{Q}(E[2])
, and
F_{2}=\mathbb{Q}(E[q])
) and Proposition
4.1
, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\times\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})
contains a subgroup
H
isomorphic to
S_{3}
such that both
H\cap\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\times 1
and
H\cap 1\times\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})
are trivial. This fact follows from Lemma
5.2
below.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For all primes
q
, there exists a subgroup
H
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\times\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})
such that

(1)

H\cong S_{3}
, and

(2)

H
intersects
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\times 1
and
1\times\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})
trivially.

Proof.

Fix generators
\sigma
and
\tau
of
S_{3}\cong\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
. The matrices

\sigma^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\tau^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}

generate a copy of
S_{3}
inside
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})
. (Both
\sigma^{\prime}
and
\tau^{\prime}
have order two, and their product has order 3.) The diagonal subgroup
H\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\times\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})
generated by
(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})
and
(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
now satisfies properties (1) and (2).
∎

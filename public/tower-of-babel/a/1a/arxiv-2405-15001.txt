On Concatenations of Two k k -Generalized Fibonacci Numbers
Alaa Altassan, Murat Alan

Abstract
. Let k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 be an integer. One of the generalization of the classical Fibonacci sequence is defined by the recurrence relation F n ( k ) = F n − 1 ( k ) + ⋯ + F n − k ( k ) F_{n}^{(k)}=F_{n-1}^{(k)}+\cdots+F_{n-k}^{(k)} for all n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 with the initial values F i ( k ) = 0 F_{i}^{(k)}=0 for i = 2 − k , … , 0 i=2-k,\ldots,0 and F 1 ( k ) = 1 . F_{1}^{(k)}=1. F n ( k ) F_{n}^{(k)} is an order k k generalization of the Fibonacci sequence and it is called k − k- generalized Fibonacci sequence or shortly k − k- Fibonacci sequence. In 2020, Banks and Luca, among other things, determined all Fibonacci numbers which are concatenations of two Fibonacci numbers. In this paper, we consider the analogue of this problem and determine all k − k- generalized Fibonacci numbers which are concatenations of two terms of the same sequence. We completely solve this problem for all k ≥ 3 . k\geq 3. Keywords. Fibonacci numbers, repdigits, Diophantine equations, linear forms in logarithms AMS Classification. 11B39, 11J86, 11D61.



1.
Introduction

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer. One of the generalization of the classical Fibonacci sequence is a sequence defined by the recurrence relation

F_{n}^{(k)}=F_{n-1}^{(k)}+F_{n-2}^{(k)}+\cdots+F_{n-k}^{(k)}
for all
n\geq 2
with the initial conditions
F_{i}^{(k)}=0
for
i=2-k,\ldots,0
and
F_{1}^{(k)}=1.

F_{n}^{(k)}
is an order
k
generalization of the Fibonacci sequence and it is called the
k-
generalized Fibonacci sequence or shortly
k-
Fibonacci sequence. Fibonacci sequence is a special case of this sequence which is corresponding the case
k=2.
In 2000, Luca and Banks, among other things, determined all Fibonacci (Lucas) numbers that are concatenations of two Fibonacci (Lucas) numbers
[
7
]
. In particular, they proved that
F_{10}=55
is the greatest Fibonacci number with this property. In fact, they obtained more general results related to finiteness of these concatenations under some mild hypotheses. In
[
4
]
, the author found all Fibonacci (Lucas) numbers that are concatenations of two Lucas (Fibonacci) numbers whereas in
[
5
]
the authors take into account the mixed concatenations of these sequences. In
[
12
,
17
,
18
]
, the authors consider the concatenations of Padovan and Perrin numbers. Recently, some authors extended the investigation of concatenations of some recurrence sequences by taking into account their base
-b
representation
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.

In this paper, for arbitrary
k\geq 2
, we consider the concatenations of two
k-
generalized Fibonacci numbers that forms another
k-
generalized Fibonacci number. As a mathematical formulation of this problem, we consider the Diophantine equation

(1.1)

F_{n}^{(k)}=F_{m}^{(k)}10^{d}+F_{l}^{(k)},\quad k\geq 3

in non negative integers
n,m>0
and
l
where
d
is the number of digits of
F_{l}^{(k)}.
We state the main result of this paper as follows.

Theorem 1
.

The Diophantine equation (
1.1
) has solutions only in the cases

\displaystyle F_{7}^{(3)}=24=\overline{F_{3}^{(3)}F_{4}^{(3)}},

\displaystyle F_{8}^{(3)}=44=\overline{F_{4}^{(3)}F_{4}^{(3)}},

\displaystyle F_{16}^{(8)}=16128=\overline{F_{6}^{(8)}F_{9}^{(8)}}

It is known that for
k=2,
the equation in (
1.1
) has solutions only for
F_{n}\in\{13,21,55\}

[
7
]
so that we state Theorem
1
only for
k\geq 3.

There exists some effective methods for handling Diophantine equations similar to (
1.1
) such as linear forms in logarithms of algebraic numbers due to Matveev
[
19
]
or some versions of reduction algorithm due to Dujella and Pethő
[
14
]
. We use these tools together with some properties of
k-
Fibonacci numbers and continued fractions. It is worth to note that when carrying out these methods, we used the software Maple for all computations by taking into account 1000 digit. We give the details of these methods in the next chapter.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\xi
be an algebraic number and let

\sum_{j=0}^{n}u_{j}x^{n-j}=u_{0}(x-\xi^{(1)})\cdots(x-\xi^{(n)})\in\mathbb{Z}

be its minimal polynomial over the rational numbers where the
u_{j}
’s are coprime integers with
u_{0}>0
and the
\xi^{(i)}
’s are conjugates of
\xi
.

The logarithmic height of
\xi
is defined by

h(\xi)=\frac{1}{n}\left(\log u_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\left(\max\{|\xi^{(i)}|,1\}\right)\right).

In particular, for a rational number
r/s,
with
\gcd(r,s)=1
and
s>0,

h(r/s)=\log\max\{|r|,s\}
. We use the following properties in the more complicated case.

\bullet

h(\xi_{1}\pm\xi_{2})\leq h(\xi_{1})+h(\xi_{2})+\log 2
.

\bullet

h(\xi_{1}\xi_{2}^{\pm 1})\leq h(\xi_{1})+h(\xi_{2})
.

\bullet

h(\xi^{s})=|s|h(\xi),

s\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Theorem 2

(
[
19
]
)
.

Consider
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{t}
as positive real algebraic numbers within a real algebraic number field
\mathbb{F}
of degree
d_{\mathbb{F}},
and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{t}
be rational integers. Define

U:=\alpha_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots\alpha_{t}^{b_{t}}-1,

such that
U
is non-zero. Then the following inequality holds:

|U|>\exp\left(C(t)d_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(1+\log d_{\mathbb{F}})(1+\log B)A_{1}\cdots A_{t}\right),

where the constant
C(t)
is given by

C(t):=-1.4\cdot 30^{t+3}\cdot t^{4.5},

and
B
is defined as
B\geq\max\{|b_{1}|,\ldots,|b_{t}|\}.
Additionally, each
A_{i}
satisfies

A_{i}\geq\max\{d_{\mathbb{F}}h(\alpha_{i}),|\log\alpha_{i}|,0.16\},\quad\text{for}\quad i=1,\ldots,t.

Let
\varsigma
be a real number, and define the distance from
\varsigma
to the nearest integer by
||\varsigma||=\min\{|\varsigma-n|:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

.

[

, Lemma 4]

Let
\tau
be an irrational and
M
be a positive integer. Suppose that

is a convergent of the continued fraction of

Suppose also

and

are real numbers with

and

. If
\epsilon:=||\mu q||-M||\tau q||>0
, then there is no positive integer solutions

and

to the inequality

under the conditions

s\leq M\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma\geq\frac{\log(Kq/\epsilon)}{\log L}.

Lemma 2
.

[
6
]

Let

be an integer. If

then

has a prime divisor not dividing

for any

3.
Some Properties of
k-
Fibonacci Numbers

The characteristic polynomial of
k-
generalized Fibonacci sequence is given by

\psi_{k}(t)=t^{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}t^{k-j}.

\psi_{k}(t)
is an irreducible polynomial over
\mathbb{Q}[t]
and it has exactly one real root
\alpha(k)
located in the outside of the unit circle
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
. The other
k-1
roots are properly within the unit circle
[
21
]
. The root
\alpha(k),
denoted simply as
\alpha,
lies in the interval
2(1-2^{-k})<\alpha<2
and furthermore

(3.1)

F_{n}^{(k)}\in\left[\alpha^{n-2},\alpha^{n-1}\right]

for all
n>0
and
k\geq 2

[
10
]
. Let us define

f_{k}(t)=\dfrac{t-1}{2+(k+1)(t-2)}.

f_{k}(t)
has the following properties

(3.2)

0.5<f_{k}(\alpha)<0.75\quad\text{and}\quad\left\lvert f_{k}(\alpha_{(i)})\right\rvert<1,\quad 2\leq i\leq k,

where
\alpha:=\alpha_{(1)},\alpha_{(2)}\cdots,\alpha_{(k)}
are all the roots of
\psi_{k}(t)

[
8
, Lemma 2]
. Moreover, from the first one of the above inequalities, we see that
f_{k}(\alpha)
is not an algebraic integer. Moreover, in the same lemma,
\forall k\geq 2
, the logarithmic height of
f_{k}(\alpha)
is also calculated as

(3.3)

h(f_{k}(\alpha))<3\log{k}.

Another important property of
F_{n}^{(k)}
for our purpose is given by

(3.4)

F_{n}^{(k)}=f_{k}(\alpha)\alpha^{n-1}+\sum_{i=2}^{k}f_{k}(\alpha_{(i)})(\alpha_{(i)})^{n-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\lvert F_{n}^{(k)}-f_{k}(\alpha)\alpha^{n-1}\right\rvert<1/2,

for all
k\geq 2

[
13
]
. Thus,

(3.5)

F_{n}^{(k)}=f_{k}(\alpha)\alpha^{n-1}+\xi_{k}(n)

where
\lvert\xi_{k}(n)\rvert<1/2.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1

Assume that (
1.1
) holds. We can find the number of digits of
F_{l}^{(k)}
as
d=\lfloor\log_{10}F_{l}^{(k)}\rfloor+1
where
\lfloor\xi\rfloor
is the greatest integer less than or equal to
\xi
, the floor function. So

\displaystyle d=\lfloor\log_{10}F_{l}^{(k)}\rfloor+1\leq 1+\log_{10}F_{l}^{(k)}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\log_{10}{\alpha^{l-1}}<1+{(l-1)}\log_{10}{2}

\displaystyle<1+\dfrac{l-1}{3}=\dfrac{l+2}{3}

and

d=\lfloor\log_{10}F_{l}^{(k)}\rfloor+1>\log_{10}F_{l}^{(k)}\geq\log_{10}{\alpha^{l-2}}\geq{(l-2)}\log_{10}{\alpha}>\frac{l-2}{5}.

where we used the fact that
\log_{10}{2}\cong 0.301<1/3
and
(1/5)<0.208...\cong\log_{10}{\dfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}}\leq\log_{10}{\alpha}.
Hence we have that

(4.1)

\frac{l-2}{5}<d<\frac{l+2}{3}.

Moreover, from the definition of
d,
we write

F_{l}^{(k)}<10^{d}\leq 10F_{l}^{(k)}.

So, from (
3.1
) and (
1.1
), we write

\alpha^{n-2}\leq F_{n}^{(k)}=10^{d}F_{m}^{(k)}+F_{l}^{(k)}\leq 10F_{m}^{(k)}F_{l}^{(k)}+F_{l}^{(k)}\leq 11F_{m}^{(k)}F_{l}^{(k)}\leq 11\alpha^{m+l-2}<\alpha^{m+l+4}

and

\alpha^{n-1}\geq F_{n}^{(k)}=10^{d}F_{m}^{(k)}+F_{l}^{(k)}>F_{m}^{(k)}F_{l}^{(k)}+F_{l}^{(k)}\geq F_{m}^{(k)}F_{l}^{(k)}\geq\alpha^{m+l-4}.

Hence we have that

(4.2)

m+l-3<n<m+l+6.

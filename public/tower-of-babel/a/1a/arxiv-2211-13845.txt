Abstract
. We define a derived enhancement of the classical quot functor of quotients associated to a coherent sheaf on a nonsingular quasiprojective variety. We prove its representability and show that it has the expected tangent complex. The derived quot scheme of points can be covered by affine charts obtained as spectra of commutative graded differential algebras (cdgas) and we compute an example. As a demonstration of the usefulness of this presentation, we write down an explicit shifted form on this example and conjecture that it agrees with the shifted symplectic structure on the derived stack of perfect complexes constructed by Pantev-Toën-Vaquié-Vezzosi ( [ Pan+13 ] ) and Brav-Dyckerhoff ( [ BD21 ] ).



1.
Introduction

A (classical) moduli problem over
k=\mathbb{C}
is a functor

\{\text{commutative $k$-algebras}\}\to\{\text{sets}\}.

A
derived moduli problem
is a functor

\{\text{commutative graded differential $k$-algebras (cdgas)}\}\to\{\text{spaces}\}.

A derived moduli problem
\bf F
is a
derived enhancement
of a classical moduli problem
F
when the space that
\bf F
assigns to an arbitrary commutative
k
-algebra
A
(thought of as a cdga) is homotopy equivalent to
F(A)
, where the latter has been endowed with the discrete topology.

In the framework developed in
[
TV08
]
,
affine derived schemes
form the opposite category of cdgas;
derived schemes
are constructed by gluing affine derived schemes. A derived scheme has a global tangent complex. Given a representable classical moduli problem
F
, it is possible, in many cases, to construct a derived enhancement
\bf F
, representable by a derived scheme, such that

(1)

the tangent space at a
k
-point of
F
is a cohomology group of the tangent complex of
\bf F
and

(2)

the higher cohomology groups of the tangent complex naturally encode obstructions to smoothness of
F
.

In such a situation, the ranks of the tangent spaces of
k
-points of
F
may jump at singularities, but the tangent complex of
\bf F
is a global invariant that can be represented by a perfect complex with terms having constant rank. This is a manifestation of the “hidden smoothness” philosophy of derived geometry.

Let
X
be a smooth quasiprojective variety and
\mathcal{F}
a coherent sheaf on
X
. Recall that the classical quot functor
Quot_{\mathcal{F}}
parameterizes quotient sheaves of
\mathcal{F}
. In §3, we define the derived quot functor
\textbf{Quot}_{\mathcal{F}}
as a derived moduli problem. It classifies quotient complexes of sheaves (appropriately defined). Here is our main result about the derived quot functor:

Theorem 1.1
.

(1)

The derived quot functor
\textbf{Quot}_{\mathcal{F}}
is representable by a derived scheme.

(2)

There is a natural map

Quot_{\mathcal{F}}\to t_{0}(\textbf{Quot}_{\mathcal{F}})

which is an equivalence of ordinary schemes. Here
t_{0}(\bf G)
, for a derived moduli functor
\bf G
, is its restriction to commutative
k
-algebras. In other words, the derived quot functor is a derived enhancement of the classical quot functor.

(3)

The tangent complex of
\textbf{Quot}_{\mathcal{F}}
at a
k
-point corresponding to a quotient
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
with kernel
\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{F}
is naturally equivalent to
\mathbb{R}\text{Hom}_{X}(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{G})
.

A derived version of the quot scheme has been constructed earlier in
[
CK01
]
, but only as a dg-scheme and not as a derived scheme representing a moduli functor. The first definition of a derived quot functor appeared in
[
Hel16
]
and was for projective schemes. Our definition is for quasiprojective varieties and agrees with the former in the case of projective varieties.

The construction of affine charts of the derived quot scheme as spectra of cdgas makes local computations for derived quot schemes tractable in many cases. Here is an interesting application of this feature to enumerative geometry.

Motivic Donaldson-Thomas theory, introduced in
[
KS08
]
and further developed in
[
BJM19
]
, is a categorification of the classical Donaldson-Thomas theory which is concerned with virtual counts of stable coherent sheaves on Calabi-Yau threefolds. One of the ingredients in defining motivic DT invariants is the algebraic d-critical locus structure on moduli spaces of coherent sheaves in the sense of
[
Joy15
]
.

Let
X
be a Calabi-Yau threefold. The derived moduli stack of coherent sheaves on
X
, and more generally the derived moduli stack of perfect complexes on
X
, has a canonical
(-1)
-shifted symplectic structure, as shown in
[
Pan+13
]
. This shifted symplectic structure truncates to an algebraic d-critical locus on the classical moduli stacks. While it was later shown, in
[
BBJ19
]
and
[
Ben+15
]
, that such shifted symplectic structures always locally have a (non-unique) “Darboux form”, the
canonical
structure guaranteed by the main theorem in
[
Pan+13
]
has been computed in few examples and that too only in the case of the moduli stack of sheaves with constant Hilbert polynomial: for
X=\mathbb{C}^{3}
in
[
RS21
]
and more generally for toric local Calabi-Yau threefolds in
[
KS21
]
.

The presentation we obtain for the derived quot scheme can simplify computations of the canonical shifted symplectic structure on these derived moduli stacks for a larger class of Calabi-Yau threefolds. We describe the pullback of a shifted symplectic structure to an affine chart of the derived quot scheme and state a conjecture (Conjecture
5.5
) that it coincides with the canonical one.

Outline of the paper

In section 2 we recall some properties of the derived stack of perfect complexes that will be useful later. In section 3 we define the derived quot functor and prove Theorem
1.1
. In section 4 we do explicit calculations of the derived quot scheme and obtain a presentation in select cases. Finally, in section 5, we discuss shifted symplectic structures on derived stacks of perfect complexes and describe the conjectured pullback of one to the derived quot scheme of the Fermat quintic: we choose this space to work with because it is an example of a
projective
Calabi-Yau threefold and also because of the Fermat quintic’s prominence in the study of mirror symmetry.

3.
Derived quot functor

In this section we define the derived quot functor and prove Theorem
1.1
. We need two definitions:

Definition 3.1

(
[
TV08
, Lemma 2.2.2.2]
,
[
Low11
, Definition 3.15]
)
.

For a cdga
A
, an
A
-dg-module
M
is
flat
if

(1)

H^{0}(M)
is flat over
H^{0}(A)

(2)

M
is
strong
over
A
, i.e. the natural map

H^{i}(A)\otimes_{H^{0}(A)}H^{0}(M)\to H^{i}(M)

is an isomorphism for all
i
.

We say a quasicoherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{O}_{\textbf{Spec}A}
-modules is
flat over

\textbf{Spec}A
if the associated
A
-module is. In particular, if
A
is discrete (i.e.
A\simeq H^{0}(A)
) then a flat
A
-dg-module
M
is itself discrete (i.e.
M\simeq H^{0}(M)
) with
H^{0}(M)
a flat
A
-module.

For a map
\pi:{\bf X}\to{\bf Y}
of derived schemes, we say a quasicoherent
\mathcal{O}_{\bf X}
-module
\mathcal{G}
is
flat over

\bf Y
if, for any discrete
\mathcal{O}_{\bf Y}
-module
\mathscr{L}
,
\mathcal{G}\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}\pi^{*}\mathscr{L}
is also discrete.

Definition 3.2
.

A map
\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{G}
in
QCoh({\bf Y})
is called
surjective
if
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{F})\to\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{G})
is surjective.

For the rest of this section,
\mathcal{F}
is a fixed (discrete) coherent sheaf on
X
.

Definition 3.3
.

The
derived quot functor

\textbf{Quot}_{\mathcal{F}}
is defined as the simplicial presheaf that maps
A\in\text{cdga}^{\leq 0}
to the full
\infty
-subgroupoid of
Perf({\bf X_{A}})_{\mathcal{F}_{A}/}^{\leq 0,\simeq}
generated by the objects
\phi:\mathcal{F}_{A}\to\mathcal{G}
such that

(1)

\mathcal{G}
is flat over
\textbf{Spec}A
and has proper support over
\textbf{Spec}A

(2)

\phi
is surjective

By definition,
\textbf{Quot}_{\mathcal{F}}
is a simplicial subpresheaf of
\textbf{PsMor}(\mathcal{F},-)^{\leq 0}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

The functor
\textbf{Quot}_{\mathcal{F}}
is a derived stack.

Proof.

By construction, the simplicial sets of
\textbf{Quot}_{\mathcal{F}}
are full simplicial subsets of those of
\textbf{PsMor}(\mathcal{F},-)^{\leq 0}
i.e. for any cdga
A
, the inclusion

\textbf{Quot}_{\mathcal{F}}(A)\subset\textbf{PsMor}(\mathcal{F},-)^{\leq 0}(A)

identifies the former with a union of connected components of the latter. Therefore it will follow that
\textbf{Quot}_{\mathcal{F}}
is a derived stack once we prove the conditions of flatness and surjectivity are local for the étale topology. But that is clear since an étale hypercover is in particular a faithfully flat map of classical schemes that is also strong.
∎

3.1.
Geometricity

In this subsection, we will prove the geometricity of the derived stack
\textbf{Quot}_{\mathcal{F}}
. First, we recall a result about open immersions of derived stacks that we will use often:

Proposition 3.5

(
[
STV15
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

Let
\bf F
be a derived stack and
t_{0}(\bf F)
its truncation. There is a bijective correspondence

\phi_{\bf F}:\{\text{ Zariski open substacks of }t_{0}({\bf F})\;\}\to\{\text{ Zariski open derived substacks of }{\bf F}\;\}

For any open substack
U_{0}\hookrightarrow t_{0}({\bf F})
, we have a homotopy cartesian diagram of derived stacks

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{U_{0}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{t_{0}({\bf F})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\phi_{\bf F}(U_{0})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bf F}}

where the vertical maps are the canonical closed immersions.

Lemma 3.6
.

The map
\textbf{Quot}_{\mathcal{F}}\to\textbf{PsMor}(\mathcal{F},-)^{\leq 0}
is a Zariski open immersion of derived stacks.

Proof.

We establish the shorthand
\mathscr{Q}:=\textbf{Quot}_{\mathcal{F}}
and
\mathscr{M}:=\textbf{PsMor}(\mathcal{F},-)^{\leq 0}
. By Proposition
3.5
, it suffices to show that
t_{0}(\mathscr{Q})
is an (ordinary) open substack of
t_{0}(\mathscr{M})
and that (using the notation from the Proposition)
\phi_{\mathscr{M}}(t_{0}(\mathscr{Q}))\simeq\mathscr{Q}
. We conclude the former by noting that for a map
\text{Spec}A\to\mathscr{M}
corresponding to
\phi:\mathcal{F}_{A}\to\mathcal{G}
where
\text{Spec}A
is a classical affine scheme, the pullback map
t_{0}(\mathscr{Q})\times_{t_{0}(\mathscr{M})}\text{Spec}A\to\text{Spec}A
is a Zariski open immersion of classical schemes, corresponding to the locus over which
\mathcal{G}
is flat and
\phi
is surjective. For the latter, we first recall the definition of
\phi_{\mathscr{M}}(U)
for an ordinary open substack
U\to t_{0}(\mathscr{M})
:

\displaystyle\text{cdga}^{\leq 0}

\displaystyle\to\infty\text{-Grpd}

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\mapsto\mathscr{M}(A)\times_{t_{0}(\mathscr{M})(H^{0}(A))}U(H^{0}(A))

and deduce that we need to show the equivalence

\mathscr{Q}(A)\simeq\mathscr{M}(A)\times_{\mathscr{M}(H^{0}(A))}\mathscr{Q}(H^{0}(A))

for all cdgas
A
. Since we have already shown that
\mathscr{Q}(A)\subset\mathscr{M}(A)
is a union of connected components, it suffices to show that the above is a bijection at the level of 0-simplices. The 0-simplices of the right hand side are the maps
\phi:\mathcal{F}_{A}\to\mathcal{G}
such that
\mathcal{G}_{0}:=\mathcal{G}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\bf X_{A}}}\mathcal{O}_{\bf X_{H^{0}(A)}}
is flat over
\text{Spec}H^{0}(A)
and
\phi_{0}:\mathcal{F}_{H^{0}(A)}\to\mathcal{G}_{0}
is surjective. There is a natural inclusion of
\mathscr{Q}(A)_{0}
into this set of 0-simplices since any map
\phi
which has the flatness and surjectivity properties will induce these properties on
\mathcal{G}_{0}
and
\phi_{0}
respectively. It remains to check that if
\phi_{0}
and
\mathcal{G}_{0}
have these properties then so do
\phi
and
\mathcal{G}
.

Suppose
\phi_{0}
and
\mathcal{G}_{0}
have these properties. Then, since
\mathcal{G}_{0}
is flat over
\text{Spec}H^{0}(A)
, it is discrete, and for any discrete sheaf
\mathscr{L}
over
\text{Spec}A
,
\mathcal{G}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\bf X_{A}}}\pi^{*}\mathscr{L}\simeq\mathcal{G}_{0}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\bf X_{H^{0}(A)}}}\pi^{*}\mathscr{L}
is also discrete, which implies that
\mathcal{G}
is flat over
\textbf{Spec}A
. Finally, the natural map
\mathcal{G}_{0}\to\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{G})
is surjective and, composed with the surjective map
\phi_{0}:\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{F}_{A})\simeq\mathcal{F}_{H^{0}(A)}\to\mathcal{G}_{0}
, gives the desired surjectivity of
\phi
.
∎

Corollary 3.7

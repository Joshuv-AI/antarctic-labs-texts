1.
Introduction

Let
e(t)=e^{2\pi it}
. The Schrödinger equation for
u(x,t)
,

\frac{i}{2\pi}\ \frac{\partial u}{\partial t}-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\ \frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}=0

with periodic boundary condition

u(x,0)=\sum_{k}a_{k}e(kx)

has the solution

u(x,t)=\sum_{k}a_{k}e(kx+k^{2}t).

The associated maximal function

\sup_{t}|u(x,t)|

was studied by Carleson
[
3
]
for solutions of the free Schrödinger equation on the real line; see Pierce
[
7
]
and Moyau and Vega
[
6
]
for further results and references. In the periodic case, Moyau and Vega showed that for every
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Bigg\|\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\Bigg|\sum_{n=1}^{N}

\displaystyle a_{n}e(nx+n^{2}t)\Bigg|\ \Bigg\|_{L^{p}[0,1]}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C(\varepsilon)N^{1/3+\varepsilon}\|a\|_{2}\quad(1\leq p\leq 6),

where
\|a\|_{2}=\left(\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}|a_{n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}
. When
p=6
, this is sharp up to the loss of
N^{\varepsilon}
(let
a_{n}=1
and restrict to
[0,10^{-6}N^{-1}]
). However, in a recent paper Barron
[
2
]
improved (
1.1
) for
1\leq p\leq 4
in the special case where
a_{n}=1
. Let

M_{p}(N)=\int_{0}^{1}\max_{0\leq t\leq 1}\Bigg|\sum_{n=1}^{N}e(nx+n^{2}t)\Bigg|^{p}dx.

Barron showed that
M_{4}(N)\ll_{\varepsilon}N^{3+\varepsilon}
, improving (
1.1
) in this case.

In the present paper we sharpen Barron’s result and determine the order of magnitude of
M_{p}(N)
for
p\geq 1
.

Theorem
.

We have

(1.2)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q1.2}N^{a(p)}(\log N)^{b(p)}\ll M_{p}(N)\ll N^{a(p)}(\log N)^{b(p)},

where

\displaystyle a(p)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{3p}{4}&(1\leq p\leq 4)\\[2.84526pt]
p-1&(p>4)\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle b(p)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&(p=4)\\[2.84526pt]
0&(p\geq 1,p\neq 4).\end{cases}

The implied constants depend at most on
p
.

After collecting some lemmas in Section
2
, the upper bound in (
1.2
) is proved in Section
3
. For
p>4
, the lower bound is essentially trivial (as above, restrict integration to
[0,10^{-6}N^{-1}]
). The lower bound for
1\leq p\leq 4
is proved in Section
4
.

We indicate briefly how these proofs proceed. Let

S(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}e(nx+n^{2}t).

We define
t(x)
to be the least number in
[0,1]
satisfying

|S(x,t(x))|=\max_{0\leq t\leq 1}\Bigg|\sum_{n=1}^{N}e(nx+n^{2})\Bigg|.

For the upper bound in (
1.2
), we may restrict integration to the set where
|S(x,t(x))|
is large. For
x
in this set, we approximate
|S(x,t(x))|
by

(1.3)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q1.3}q^{-1}S(q,\boldsymbol{a})I(\boldsymbol{\beta}),

where
\frac{\boldsymbol{a}}{q}=\left(\frac{a_{1}}{q},\frac{a_{2}}{q}\right)
is close to
(x,t(x))
, and
\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
is given by

\boldsymbol{\beta}=\left(x-\frac{a_{1}}{q},t(x)-\frac{a_{2}}{q}\right).

Here

S(q,\boldsymbol{a})=\sum_{y=1}^{q}e\left(\frac{ya_{1}+y^{2}a_{2}}{q}\right)\,,\,I(\boldsymbol{\beta})=\int_{0}^{N}e(\beta_{1}\gamma+\beta_{2}\gamma^{2})d\gamma.

We can now use standard upper bounds for
S(q,\boldsymbol{a})
and
I(\boldsymbol{\beta})
to complete the proof. The existence of
\frac{\boldsymbol{a}}{q}
with small enough
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
, which ‘jump-starts’ the argument, depends on the work of Baker
[
1
]
.

For the lower bound in (
1.2
), we restrict integration to

(1.4)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q1.4}E=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq q\leq\frac{1}{6}\,N^{1/2}\\
q\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\ \bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a_{1}\leq q\\
(a_{1},q)=1\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{a_{1}}{q}+\frac{15}{N},\frac{a_{1}}{q}+\frac{16}{N}\right].

We define a suitable
v(x)

(x\in E)
for which
S(x,v(x))
is sufficiently closely approximated by (
1.3
) with
a_{2}=1
, and now

(1.5)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q1.5}\boldsymbol{\beta}=\left(x-\frac{a_{1}}{q}\,,\,v(x)-\frac{1}{q}\right).

We find that

(1.6)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q1.6}|I(\boldsymbol{\beta})|\gg N.

We also have

(1.7)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q1.7}|S(q,a_{1},1)|\gg q^{1/2}

because
q
is odd. Thus the quantity in (
1.3
) is
\gg Nq^{-1/2}
, leading quickly to the lower bound in (
1.2
).

2.
Lemmas for the proof of the theorem.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose that

|S(x,t)|\geq P\geq N^{1/2+\varepsilon}.

Then there exist a natural number
q
and integers
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
such that

(2.1)

\displaystyle q\ll(NP^{-1})^{2}N^{\varepsilon}\ ,\ (q,a_{1},a_{2})=1,\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q2.1}

(2.2)

\displaystyle|qx-a_{1}|\ll(NP^{-1})^{2}N^{\varepsilon-1}\ ,\ |qt-a_{2}|\ll(NP^{-1})^{2}N^{\varepsilon-2}.\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q2.2}

Proof.

This is a special case of the main result of
[
1
]
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
x=\frac{a_{1}}{q}+\beta_{1}
and
t=\frac{a_{2}}{q}+\beta_{2}
. Then

S(x,t)=q^{-1}S(q,\boldsymbol{a})I(\boldsymbol{\beta})+\Delta

where

\Delta\ll q(1+|\beta_{1}|N+|\beta_{2}|N^{2}).

Proof.

This is a special case of Vaughan
[
8
]
, Theorem 7.2.
∎

Lemma 3
.

(i) For
q\geq 1
,
(q,a_{1},a_{2})=1
, we have

S(q,\boldsymbol{a})\ll q^{1/2}.

(ii) Let
q
be odd and
(q,a_{1})=1
. Let
[4,q]
denote the inverse of
4\pmod{q}
. Then

S(q,a_{1},1)=\begin{cases}e\left(-\frac{[4,q]a_{1}^{2}}{q}\right)q^{1/2}&(q\equiv 1\pmod{4})\\[11.38109pt]
e\left(-\frac{[4,q]a_{1}^{2}}{q}\right)iq^{1/2}&(q\equiv 3\pmod{4}).\end{cases}

Proof.

Part (i) is a consequence of Lemmas 3–9 of Estermann
[
5
]
. Part (ii) follows from Lemma 3 of
[
5
]
in conjunction with the evaluation of the sum
S
in Chapter 2 of Davenport
[
4
]
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
A
,
X
be positive numbers. Then

\int_{-X}^{X}e(A\gamma^{2})d\gamma=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{A}}+\frac{\lambda}{AX},

where
|\lambda|\leq 7/12
.

Proof.

Let
Y>0
. The integral

\int_{C}e(z^{2})dz

is 0, where
C
consists of straight line segments
[0,Y]
and
\left[Ye\left(\frac{1}{8}\right),0\right]
, together with the circular arc
C_{1}
from
Y
to
Ye(1/8)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{Y}e(x^{2})dx+\int_{C_{1}}e(z^{2})dz

\displaystyle=e\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)\int_{0}^{Y}e^{-2\pi x^{2}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{e\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)}{2\sqrt{2}}+\nu\int_{Y}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{2\pi x^{2}}=\frac{e\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)}{2\sqrt{2}}+\frac{\nu}{2\pi Y}

where
|\nu|\leq 1
. The integral over
C_{1}
has absolute value

\displaystyle\leq Y\int_{0}^{\pi/4}\exp(-2\pi Y^{2}\sin 2\theta)d\theta

\displaystyle\leq Y\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-8Y^{2}\theta)d\theta=\frac{1}{8Y}.

Hence

\int_{-Y}^{Y}e(x^{2})dx=\frac{e\left(\frac{1}{8}\right)+e\left(-\frac{1}{8}\right)}{2\sqrt{2}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{Y}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{Y}

where
|\lambda_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{\pi}<\frac{7}{12}
. The general case follows on a change of variable.
∎

Lemma 5
.

We have

I(\boldsymbol{\beta})\ll N(1+|\beta_{1}|N+|\beta_{2}|N^{2})^{-1/2}.

Proof.

This is a special case of Vaughan
[
8
]
, Theorem 7.3.
∎

Lemma 6
.

(i) Let
Y\geq 2
. We have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\leq Y\\
y\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\phi(y)=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\,Y^{2}+O(Y\log Y).

(ii) For large
Y
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\frac{Y}{2}<y\leq Y\\
y\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\,\frac{\phi(y)}{y^{\alpha}}\gg_{\alpha}Y^{2-\alpha}\quad(\alpha\text{ real}).

(iii) For large
Y
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\leq Y\\
y\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\,\frac{\phi(y)}{y^{2}}\gg\log Y.

Proof.

(i) We have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\leq Y\\
y\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\phi(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\leq Y\\
y\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}y\sum_{d\mid y}\ \frac{\mu(d)}{d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq Y\\
d\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq Y/d\\
n\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}n

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq Y\\
d\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\mu(d)\left(\frac{Y^{2}}{4d^{2}}+O\left(\frac{Y}{d}\right)\right).

Since

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d=1\\
d\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\ \frac{\mu(d)}{d^{2}}=\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{2^{2}}}\ \prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)=\frac{4}{3}\cdot\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}=\frac{8}{\pi^{2}}\,,

the result follows easily.

Now (ii) is an obvious consequence of (i). Next,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2^{j-1}<y\leq 2^{j}\\
y\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\ \frac{\phi(y)}{y^{2}}\gg 1

from (ii). Summing over
\gg\log Y
values of
j
yields (iii).
∎

3.
Proof of the upper bound in (
1.2
).

Let

F=\{x\in[0,1]\cdot|S(x,t(x))|>N^{3/4}\}.

Then

\int_{0}^{1}|S(x,t(x))|^{p}dx\leq N^{3p/4}\ll N^{a(p)}\quad(p\geq 1).

Thus we may confine integration of
|S(x,t(x))|^{p}
to the set
F
.

Let
x\in F
. By Lemma
1
there exist integers
q
,
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
with
(q,a_{1},a_{2})=1
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle 1\leq q\ll N^{1/2+\varepsilon}\ ,\

\displaystyle\beta_{1}:=x-\frac{a_{1}}{q}\ll q^{-1}N^{-1/2+\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\hskip 54.2025pt\beta_{2}:=t(x)-\frac{a_{2}}{q}\ll q^{-1}N^{-3/2+\varepsilon}.

By Lemma
2
,

S(x,t(x))=q^{-1}S(q,\boldsymbol{a})I(\boldsymbol{\beta})+\Delta

where

\Delta\ll N^{1/2+\varepsilon}.

It follows at once that

\displaystyle N^{3/4}

\displaystyle\leq|S(x,t(x))|\ll q^{-1}|S(q,\boldsymbol{a})I(\boldsymbol{\beta})|

\displaystyle\ll q^{-1/2}\min(N,N^{1/2}|\beta_{1}|^{-1/2}).

Sharpening (
3.1
) a little, we find that

q\ll N^{1/2}\ ,\ \beta_{1}\ll q^{-1}N^{1/2}.

Hence, for a suitable constant
C>0
,

(3.2)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q3.2}\int_{F}|S(x,t(x))|^{p}dx\ll\sum_{q\leq CN^{1/2}}\ \sum_{a_{1}=0}^{q}q^{-p/2}\int_{-Cq^{-1}N^{-1/2}}^{Cq^{-1}N^{-1/2}}\min(N^{p},N^{p/2}|\beta_{1}|^{-p/2})d\beta_{1}.

At this stage we exclude
p=2
from the argument. The contribution to the integral on the right-hand side of (
3.2
) from
|\beta_{1}|\leq N^{-1}
is
\ll N^{\beta-1}
. From the two remaining intervals we obtain a contribution

\ll\begin{cases}N^{p/2}(N^{-1})^{1-\frac{p}{2}}=N^{p-1}&\text{if }p>2\\[5.69054pt]
N^{p/2}(q^{-1}N^{-1/2})^{1-p/2}=N^{\frac{3p}{4}-\frac{1}{2}}q^{\frac{p}{2}-1}&\text{if }p<2.\end{cases}

We conclude that

(3.3)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q3.3}\int_{F}|S(x,t(x))|^{p}dx\ll\sum_{q\leq CN^{1/2}}q^{1-p/2}(N^{p-1}+N^{3p/4-1/2}q^{\frac{p}{2}-1}).

For
p<4
,
p\neq 2
,

(3.4)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q3.4}\sum_{q\leq CN^{1/2}}q^{1-p/2}\ll(N^{\frac{1}{2}})^{2-\frac{p}{2}}=N^{1-\frac{p}{4}}.

For
p=4
,

(3.5)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q3.5}\sum_{q\leq CN^{1/2}}q^{1-p/2}\ll\log N.

For
p>4
,

(3.6)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q3.6}\sum_{q\leq CN^{1/2}}q^{1-p/2}\ll 1.

The desired upper bound follows on combining (
3.3
)–(
3.6
), except for
p=2
. Since
\|f\|_{L^{p}[0,1]}
is increasing with
p
for a given function
f
on
[0,1]
, we obtained the desired result for
p=2
by comparison with the result for
p=3
.

4.
Proof of the lower bound in (
1.2
) for
1\leq p\leq 4
.

Let

J(q,a_{1})=\left[\frac{a_{1}}{q}+\frac{15}{N}\ ,\ \frac{a_{1}}{q}+\frac{16}{N}\right]

be one of the intervals in the definition of
E
. The intervals
J(q_{1},a_{1})
,
J(q_{2},a_{2})
of
E
with
\frac{a_{1}}{q_{1}}\neq\frac{a_{2}}{q_{2}}
are non-overlapping, since

\left|\frac{a_{1}}{q_{1}}-\frac{a_{2}}{q_{2}}\right|\geq\frac{1}{q_{1}q_{2}}\geq\frac{36}{N}.

For
x\in J(q,a_{1})
, we write
a_{2}=1
,
\beta_{1}=x-\frac{a_{1}}{q}
,
\beta_{2}=-\frac{\beta_{1}}{N}
,

(4.1)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q4.1}v(x)=\frac{a_{2}}{q}-\frac{\beta_{1}}{N}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{\beta_{1}}{N}.

We have

(4.2)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q4.2}M_{p}(N)\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq q\leq N^{1/2}/6\\
q\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}=1\\
(a_{1},q)=1\end{subarray}}^{q}\int_{J(q,a_{1})}|S(x,v(x))|^{p}dx

We shall show that for
x
in any of the intervals
J(q,a_{1})
in (
4.2
), we have

(4.3)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q4.3}|S(x,v(x))|\gg Nq^{-1/2}.

The lower bound in (
1.2
) follows from (
4.2
), (
4.3
) on an application of Lemma
6
(ii), (iii):

\displaystyle M_{p}(N)

\displaystyle\gg\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq q\leq N^{1/2}/6\\
q\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\ \sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}=1\\
(a_{1},q)=1\end{subarray}}^{q}\int_{J(q,a)}q^{-p/2}N^{p}d\beta_{1}

\displaystyle\gg\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}3\leq q\leq N^{1/2}/6\\
q\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}\phi(q)q^{-p/2}N^{p-1}

\displaystyle\gg N^{p-1}(N^{1/2})^{2-p/2}(\log N)^{b(p)}=N^{3p/4}(\log N)^{b(p)}.

Thus it remains to prove (
3.2
). With
x\in J(q,a_{1})
and
v(x)
as in (
4.1
), we have

(4.4)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q4.4}|S(x,v(x))|=q^{-1}S(q,a,1)I(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})+\Delta

where

(4.5)

\addcontentsline{lla}{section}{\numberline q4.5}\Delta\ll q(1+|\beta_{1}|N+|\beta_{2}|N^{2})\ll N^{1/2}.

Now

\displaystyle I(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{N}e\left(\beta_{1}\gamma-\frac{\beta_{1}}{N}\,\gamma^{2}\right)d\gamma

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{N}e\left(-\frac{\beta_{1}}{N}\,\left(\gamma-\frac{N}{2}\right)^{2}\right)e\left(\frac{\beta_{1}N}{4}\right)d\gamma,

\displaystyle|I(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{-N/2}^{N/2}e\left(\frac{\beta_{1}\gamma^{2}}{N}\right)d\gamma\right|.

Now we apply Lemma
4
with
X=N/2
,
A=\beta_{1}/N
to obtain

\int_{-N/2}^{N/2}e\left(\frac{\beta_{1}\gamma^{2}}{N}\right)d\gamma=\frac{N^{1/2}}{2\beta_{1}^{1/2}}+\frac{2\lambda}{\beta_{1}},|\lambda|\leq\frac{7}{12}.

Since
\beta_{1}\geq\frac{15}{N}
, it is easily seen that

\frac{N^{1/2}}{2\beta_{1}^{1/2}}>\frac{3\lambda}{\beta_{1}}

and so

|I(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})|\gg N.

Recalling Lemma
3
(ii), we have

|q^{-1}S(q,a_{1})I(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})|\gg\frac{N}{q^{1/2}}\gg N^{3/4}.

Combining this with (
4.4
), (
4.5
) we conclude that (
4.3
) holds in
J(q,a_{1})
. This completes the proof of the lower bound in (
1.2
).

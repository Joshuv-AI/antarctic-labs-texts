Bigness of the tangent bundle of a Fano threefold with Picard number two Thanks: MSC 2010: 14J45, 14J30, 14E30. Key words: Fano threefold, conic bundle, total dual VMRT, tangent bundle
Hosung Kim, Jeong-Seop Kim, and Yongnam Lee

Abstract
. In this paper, we study on the property of bigness of the tangent bundle T X T_{X} of a Fano threefold X X with Picard number 2. We determine the bigness of the tangent bundle of whole 36 deformation types. Our result shows that T X T_{X} is big if and only if ( − K X ) 3 ≥ 34 (-K_{X})^{3}\geq 34 . And as a corollary we prove that the tangent bundle is not big when X X has a standard conic bundle structure with non-empty discriminant. Our main methods are to produce irreducible effective divisors on ℙ ⁡ ( T X ) \mathbb{P}(T_{X}) constructed from the total dual VMRT associated to a family of rational curves. Additionally, we present some criteria to determine the bigness of T X T_{X} .



1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper we will work over the field of complex numbers.
Let
X
be a smooth projective variety.
We say that the tangent bundle
T_{X}
of
X
is pseudoeffective (resp. big) if the tautological class
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(T_{X})}(1)
of the projectivized bundle
\mathbb{P}(T_{X})
is pseudoeffective (resp. big).

In general it is difficult to give a numerical characterization for pseudoeffective or bigness of the tangent bundle, even in low dimension with low rank of Picard group.
It has been shown by Hsiao
[
Hsi15
, Corollary 1.3]
that the tangent bundle of a toric variety is big.
Höring, Liu, and Shao
[
HLS20
, Theorem 1.4]
give a complete answer to del Pezzo surfaces: If
X
is a del Pezzo surface of degree
d
then

(a)

T_{X}
is pseudoeffective if and only if
d\geq 4
,

(b)

T_{X}
is big if and only if
d\geq 5
.

Also in the paper
[
HLS20
]
, they solve these problems for del Pezzo threefolds.
In
[
HL21
]
, Höring and Liu consider Fano manifolds
X
with Picard number one, and they prove that if
X
admits a rational curve with trivial normal bundle and with big
T_{X}
then
X
is isomorphic to the del Pezzo threefold of degree five.
These all results indicate that assuming bigness should lead to strong restrictions on Fano manifolds, and lead us to consider naturally Fano threefolds with Picard number 2.
In this paper, we prove the following main theorem.
The details are in Table
1
in the introduction.

Main Theorem
.

We determine the bigness of the tangent bundle
T_{X}
of whole 36 deformation types of Fano threefolds
X
with Picard number 2.
In particular, the tangent bundle
T_{X}
is big if and only if
(-K_{X})^{3}\geq\,34
.

As a corollary, we give a complete answer for the bigness of the tangent bundle
T_{X}
when
X
has a standard conic bundle structure.

Corollary
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold with Picard number 2.
Suppose
X
to have a standard conic bundle structure.
Then
T_{X}
is not big if and only if
X
has standard conic bundle structure with a non-empty discriminant.

Our method and explicit description of total dual VMRTs are also applicable to some general cases other than the case of Fano threefolds.
Besides our main theorem, we obtain the following.

Theorem
4.3
.

Let
X
be the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
along a smooth nondegenerate curve
\Gamma
.
Assume that
\Gamma
has no quadrisecant line on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
.
Then
T_{X}
is big if and only if
\Gamma
is a twisted cubic curve.

A Fano threefold
X
is called primitive if it is not isomorphic to the blow-up of a Fano threefold along a smooth irreducible curve.
Due to Batyrev’s classification of toroidal Fano threefolds
[
Bat82
]
and Mori-Mukai’s classification of Fano threefolds with Picard number 2, No. 32 in
[
MM81
, Table 2]
(a divisor on
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
of bidegree
(1,1)
) is the only non-toric case which has a conic bundle structure with empty discriminant. In this case,
T_{X}
is big (see Remark
2.5
).
We note that if
X
is a primitive Fano threefold with Picard number 2 then
X
has a standard conic bundle structure
[
MM81
, Theorem 5]
.

Let
X
be a Fano threefold with a standard conic bundle structure.
A conic bundle is a proper flat morphism
\pi:X\to S
of nonsingular varieties such that it is of relative dimension 1 and the anticanonical divisor
-K_{X}
is relatively ample.
A conic bundle
\pi:X\to S
is called standard if for any prime divisor
D\subset S
its inverse image
\pi^{*}(D)
is irreducible.
Then Picard number 2 condition implies that
X
has a conic bundle structure over the projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
Let
\pi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a conic bundle structure over
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with the discriminant curve
\Delta\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{2}
of degree
d=\deg\Delta
.
By
[
Isk87
, §1]
and
[
Pro18
, Corollary 3.3.3, Corollary 3.9.1]
,
\Delta
has only normal crossings in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
d\geq 3
.
Also, from
[
Pro18
, Lemma 3.6]
,

b_{3}(X)=2b_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{2})+2b_{2}(X)-2b_{2}(\mathbb{P}^{2})+2p_{a}(\Delta)-4=2p_{a}(\Delta)-2=d^{2}-3d.

Assume that
X
is a Fano threefold with Picard number 2 which has a standard conic bundle structure with non-empty discriminant.
Then according to
[
MM81
, Table 2]
,

3\leq d\leq 8\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,d\neq 7,

and there are 9 deformation types (No. 2, 6, 8, 9, 11, 13, 18, 20 and 24 in
[
MM81
, Table 2]
).

Let
\mathbb{P}(T_{X})
be the projectivized bundle
\Pi:\mathbb{P}(T_{X})\to X
of the tangent bundle
T_{X}
of
X
.
We will denote by
\zeta=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(T_{X})}(1)
the tautological class of
\mathbb{P}(T_{X})
. Here, we use Grothendieck’s notion for
\mathbb{P}(T_{X})
.
Our main strategy of the proof of the main theorem is to find two irreducible effective divisors
\breve{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
and
\breve{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
on
\mathbb{P}(T_{X})
which are the total dual VMRTs associated to families of rational curves, and express a positive multiple of
\zeta
as a combination of
[\breve{\mathcal{C}}_{1}]
,
[\breve{\mathcal{C}}_{2}]
and
\alpha(\text{effective divisor})
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}
.

In particular, if
X
has a standard conic bundle structure
\pi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
, we have a natural irreducible effective divisor
\breve{\mathcal{C}}
on
\mathbb{P}(T_{X})
induced from the fibers of
\pi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
according to
[
HLS20
, Corollary 2.13]
, and
[\breve{\mathcal{C}}]\sim\zeta+\Pi^{*}(K_{X}-\pi^{*}K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}).
To find irreducible effective divisors
\breve{\mathcal{C}}
constructed from the total dual VMRT associated to a family of rational curves, we use explicit descriptions of Fano threefolds with Picard number 2 in
[
MM81
, Table 2]
.
Especially, we describe irreducible effective divisors
\breve{\mathcal{C}}
induced from the total dual VMRT associated to a family of rational curves when
X
are imprimitive Fano threefolds.
Additionally, we provide some criteria to disprove the bigness of
T_{X}
when
X
is the blow-up of a smooth curve in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
or a quadric hypersurface
Q
in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
or the quintic del Pezzo threefold
V_{5}
.

In this present paper, we determine the bigness of the tangent bundle

We also expect our methods give some answer for Fano threefolds with higher Picard number.

2.
Total Dual VMRTs and some Criteria to Disprove Bigness

In this section, we briefly introduce the theory related to the total dual VMRT, which is firstly introduced by
[
HR04
]
in a study of Hecke curves on the moduli of vector bundles on curves.
Later,
[
OSW16
]
generalizes the theory to the case of minimal rational curves, and
[
HLS20
]
develops explicit formulas in the case where a variety has zero dimensional VMRTs in a study of the tangent bundle of del Pezzo manifolds.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and
\text{RatCurves}^{n}(X)
be the normalized space of rational curves on
X
(see
[
Kol96
, Chapter II]
).
We mean by a
family of rational curves
on
X
an irreducible component
\mathcal{K}
of
\text{RatCurves}^{n}(X)
.
Let
q:\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{K}
be the normalization of the universal family and
e:\mathcal{U}\to X
be the evaluation morphism.

\mathcal{K}_{x}
the normalization of
q(e^{-1}(x))\subseteq\mathcal{K}
.
We say that the family
\mathcal{K}
is
dominating
if

is dominant, and is
locally unsplit
if
\mathcal{K}_{x}
is proper for general
x\in X
.
For a locally unsplit dominating family
\mathcal{K}

\tau_{x}:\mathcal{K}_{x}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{X}|_{x})
for general

tangent map

\tau_{x}
sending a curve to its tangent direction at
x
.
The image
\mathcal{C}_{x}
of
\tau_{x}
is called the

(

, for short) of
\mathcal{K}
at
x
.

Let
\ell
be a rational curve on
X
and
\nu:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\ell\subseteq X
be its normalization.
Then it has the
splitting type

\nu^{*}T_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(a_{1})^{\oplus m_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}(a_{k})^{\oplus m_{k}}\ \ \text{for some $a_{1}>\cdots>a_{k}$ and $m_{1},\,\ldots,\,m_{k}>0$}.

We say that
\ell
is

if

\nu^{*}T_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2)\oplus{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)}^{\oplus r}\oplus{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}}^{\oplus s}

.
For a standard rational curve
\ell
on
X
, a minimal section of
\ell
is a rational curve
\widetilde{\ell}
on
\mathbb{P}(T_{X})
with the normalization

\widetilde{\nu}:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\widetilde{\ell}\subseteq\mathbb{P}(T_{X}|_{\ell})\subseteq\mathbb{P}(T_{X})

associated to a trivial quotient
\nu^{*}T_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
.

.

, we can observe that
\breve{\mathcal{C}}
is

\text{dominated by a family of rational curves $\widetilde{\ell}$ satisfying $\zeta.\widetilde{\ell}=0$ on $\mathbb{P}(T_{X})$}.

\widetilde{\ell}

Our main strategy to disprove the bigness of
T_{X}
is to find an effective divisor
\mathcal{D}
on
\mathbb{P}(T_{X})
which attains the property (
2
) and apply the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathcal{D}_{i}}
for some irreducible and reduced effective divisors
\mathcal{D}_{i}
on
\mathbb{P}(T_{X})
.
Assume that
\mathcal{D}_{i}
satisfies (
2
) for all
i=1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,n
.
If
\mathcal{D}\sim k\zeta+\Pi^{*}H
for some
k>0
and effective divisor
H
on
X
(possibly
H=0
), then
\zeta
is not big on
\mathbb{P}(T_{X})
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\zeta
is big.
We can write
\mathcal{D}_{i}=k_{i}\zeta+\Pi^{*}H_{i}
for some
k_{i}\geq 0
and divisor
H_{i}
on
X
.
Let
A
be an ample divisor on
X
such that
A+\sum_{i=1}^{l}H_{i}
is also ample for all
1\leq l\leq n
.
Then there exists the smallest integer
m>0
such that
m\zeta-\Pi^{*}A
is effective by Kodaira’s lemma.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{0}\sim m\zeta-\Pi^{*}A
be an effective divisor.
For a general curve
\widetilde{\ell_{1}}
in the family whose members cover the divisor
\mathcal{D}_{1}
, we have

\mathcal{E}_{0}.\widetilde{\ell_{1}}=(m\zeta-\Pi^{*}A).\widetilde{\ell_{1}}=-A.\Pi(\widetilde{\ell_{1}})<0.

Thus
\mathcal{E}_{0}-\mathcal{D}_{1}\geq 0
, and we can find an effective divisor
\mathcal{E}_{1}=\mathcal{E}_{0}-\mathcal{D}_{1}
.
For a general curve
\widetilde{\ell_{2}}
in the family whose members cover the divisor

Introduction and Main results

The two-component higher order Camassa-Holm systems with fractional inertia operator was firstly introduced
by Escher and Lyons to describe the geometrical geodesic flow on an appropriate infinite dimensional Lie group
[
30
]
, especially for the cases that
s
is a fractional number larger than
1
. It is the following system:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}m_{t}+2u_{x}m+um_{x}=\beta u_{x}-\kappa\rho\rho_{x},&t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R},\\
\rho_{t}+(u\rho)_{x}=0,&t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R},\\
m(t,x)=(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{a}u(t,x),&t\geq 0,\,x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\\
\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\end{array}\right.

where
s\geq 0
is a constant,
\beta
is a constant which represents the vorticity of underlying flow, and
\kappa>0
is an arbitrary real parameter.
This model attracted a lot of attention. In
[
8
]
, the authors study the local well-posedness in Besov spaces with high-regularity. In
[
37
]
, the authors established the local-wellposedness in Besov spaces with lower-regularity, they also give the global solutions of (
1.1
) for the case
a=2,\kappa>0
. In
[
38
]
, the authors study the local and global analyticity and Gevrey regularity of (
1.1
).

We can take it as the generalization of the shallow water wave systems introduced in the nonlinear dynamics to describe nonlinear phenomena in shallow water.

Indeed,
for
a=1
, System (
1.1
) becomes the two-component Camassa-Holm system:

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}m_{t}+2u_{x}m+um_{x}=\beta u_{x}-\kappa\rho\rho_{x},&t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R},\\
\rho_{t}+(u\rho)_{x}=0,&t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R},\\
m(t,x)=u(t,x)-u_{xx}(t,x),&t\geq 0,\,x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R},\\
\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{array}\right.

The Cauchy problems of 2CH
(
1.2
) with

\kappa=-1
and with
\kappa=1
have been discussed in
[

]

and
[
19
]

For

\displaystyle u_{t}-u_{xxt}+2u_{x}(u-u_{xx})+u(u-u_{xx})_{x}-\alpha u_{x}=0,

Proposition 2.1

[

Let

3.1.  Uniformly bound and existence.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
:
Starting with

, we construct a sequence of the approximate solutions

by solving the following linear transport equations:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{n+1}_{t}+u^{n}u^{n+1}_{x}=[(1-\partial_{xx})^{-a},u^{n}\partial_{x}]m^{n}-2(1-\partial_{xx})^{-a}(u^{n}_{x}m^{n}),\\[4.30554pt]

\end{array}\right.

Without loss of generality, we consider the low regularity case
s=1+\frac{d}{p}
.
Assume that
u^{n}\in E_{p}(T)=C([0,T];B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1})\cap C^{1}([0,T];B^{\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1})
. Taking advantage of the standard theory of the transport equations, we can deduce that there exist a unique solutions
u^{n+1}\in E_{p}(T)
. Using the lemma
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\|u^{n+1}(t)\|_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{C\int^{t}_{0}\|u^{n}\|_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}}ds}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,1}}

\displaystyle\quad+e^{C\int^{t}_{0}\|u^{n}\|_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}}ds}\int^{t}_{0}e^{-C\int^{t^{\prime}}_{0}\|u^{n}\|_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}}ds}\|[(1-\partial_{xx})^{-a},u^{n}\partial_{x}]m^{n}-2(1-\partial_{xx})^{-a}(u^{n}_{x}m^{n})\|_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}}dt^{\prime},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq e^{C\int^{t}_{0}\|u^{n}\|_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}}ds}\bigg(\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,1}}+\int^{t}_{0}e^{-C\int^{t^{\prime}}_{0}\|u^{n}\|_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}}ds}\|u^{n}\|^{2}_{B^{1+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,1}}dt^{\prime}\bigg),

[(1-\partial_{xx})^{-a},u^{n}\partial_{x}]m^{n}=[(1-\partial_{xx})^{-a},T_{u^{n}}\partial_{x}]m^{n}+(1-\partial_{xx})^{-a}T_{m^{n}_{x}}u^{n}+T_{u^{n}_{x}}u^{n}+(1-\partial_{xx})^{-a}R(u^{n},m^{n}_{x})+R(u^{n},u^{n}_{x}),

This implies that there exist a

and the Bony decomposition, we have

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{B^{-\min\{\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\}}_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{u_{0}}(\|w(0)\|_{B^{-\min\{\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\}}_{p,\infty}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|-wm_{2x}+2u_{1x}w+2(1-\partial_{xx})^{-a}w_{x}m_{2}\|_{B^{-\min\{\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\}}_{p,\infty}}ds)

\displaystyle\leq C_{u_{0}}(\|w(0)\|_{B^{-\min\{\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\}}_{p,\infty}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{1},u_{2}\|_{B^{1+2a-\min\{\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\}}_{p,1}}\|w\|_{B^{-\min\{\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\}}_{p,\infty}}ds)

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq C_{u_{0}}(\|w(0)\|_{B^{-\min\{\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\}}_{p,\infty}})

Therefore, the uniqueness is obvious in view of (
3.2
).

3.3.  Continuous dependent.

This subsection devote to study about the continuous dependent on initial.
Assume that
u^{n}_{0}\to u^{\infty}_{0}
in
B^{s}_{p,1},s>1+2a-\min\{\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\}
and
u^{n},u^{\infty}
are the solutions of (
1.3
) with the initial data
u^{n}_{0},u^{\infty}_{0}
respectively. Notice that their corresponding solutions
u^{n},u^{\infty}
are uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}_{T}(B^{1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})
. An interpolation
argument and (
3.2
) yield that
u^{n}\to u^{\infty}
in
C([0,T);B^{s-\varepsilon}_{p,1})
for any
\varepsilon>0
.
In order to prove that
u^{n}\to u^{\infty}
in
C([0,T);B^{s}_{p,1})
, it sufficient to show that
m^{n}_{x}\to m^{\infty}_{x}
in
C([0,T);B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1})
.
Let
V^{n}=m^{n}_{x}
for all
n\in\overline{\mathbb{N}}
. Split
V^{n}
into
W^{n}+Z^{n}
with
(W^{n},Z^{n})
satisfying that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}W^{n}_{t}+u^{n}W^{n}_{x}=-u^{\infty}_{x}m^{\infty}_{x}+\partial_{x}{u^{\infty}_{x}m^{\infty}}:=F^{\infty},\\[4.30554pt]
W^{n}|_{t=0}(x)=V^{n}_{0}=m^{\infty}_{x0}(x).\\[4.30554pt]
\end{array}\right.

and

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}Z^{n}_{t}+u^{n}Z^{n}_{x}=F^{n}-F^{\infty},\\[4.30554pt]
Z^{n}|_{t=0}(x)=m^{n}_{x0}-m^{\infty}_{x0}.\\[4.30554pt]
\end{array}\right.

By virtue of the lemma (
2.5
) and
(1-\partial_{xx})^{-a}u^{n}_{x}=m^{n}_{x}=V^{n}=W^{n}+Z^{n}
, we verify that

(3.10)

\displaystyle W^{n}\to W^{\infty}\quad\quad\text{in}\quad\quad C([0,T];B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1}).

Using Bony decomposition, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|F^{n}-F^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|W^{n}-W^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1}}+\|z^{n}-0\|_{B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1}})\|m^{n},m^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-2a}_{p,1}}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq C_{u_{0}}(\|W^{n}-W^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1}}+\|z^{n}-0\|_{B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1}}).

Indeed, the uniqueness of transport equation ensures that
z^{\infty}\equiv 0
. Taking advantage of lemma (
2.4
), we get

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|z^{n}\|_{B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1}}&\leq C(\|u^{n}_{0}-u^{\infty}_{0}\|_{B^{1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}+\int^{t}_{0}\|F^{n}-F^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1}}d\tau)\\
&\leq C_{u_{0}}(\|u^{n}_{0}-u^{\infty}_{0}\|_{B^{1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}+\int^{t}_{0}\|W^{n}-W^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1}}+\|z^{n}-0\|_{B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1}}d\tau).\end{split}

Using the facts that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u^{n}_{0}-u^{\infty}_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,1}}=0
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u^{n}-u^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,1}}=0
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|W^{n}-W^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1}}=0
, and the Gronwall inequality yields that
z_{n}
tends to
0
in
C([0,T];B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1})
. Since
(1-\partial_{xx})^{-a}u^{n}_{x}=m^{n}_{x}=V^{n}=W^{n}+Z^{n}
, it follows that

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|V^{n}-V^{\infty}\|_{B^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}&\leq C(\|W^{n}-W^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1}}+\|Z^{n}-Z^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1}})\\
&\leq C(\|W^{n}-W^{\infty}\|_{B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1}}+\|Z^{n}\|_{B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1}})\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty,\end{split}

that is
m^{n}_{x}\to m^{\infty}_{x}
in
C([0,T);B^{s-1-2a}_{p,1})
. Therefore we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Similarly, we can obtain Theorem
1.2
.

4
Global existence.

In this section we construct a class of special data such that the corresponding solution is global in time when
a>1
.

Proof of theorem
1.3
:

Proof.

Set
T
be the maximal time of
m(t,x)
.
Firstly,
one can easily deduce that
m(t,x)\geq 0
if
m_{0}(x)\geq 0
by the characteristic method. Since the functional calculus tells us that

u(t,x)=(1-\partial_{xx})^{-a}m(t,x)=C\int_{0}^{+\infty}s^{a-1}e^{-s}e^{s\Delta}m(t,x)ds:=(G*m)(t,x),

we deduce that
u(t,x)\geq 0
if
m_{0}(x)\geq 0
. Moreover, we have
G(z)=G(-z)
.

Then, by
m_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, one have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|m(t)\|_{L^{1}}=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}m(t,x)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}(um)(t,x)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u_{x}m)(t,x)dx

\displaystyle=0-<G*m_{x},m>

\displaystyle=0+<G*m,m_{x}>

(4.1)

\displaystyle=0+<m,G*m_{x}>,

where the last inequality holds by
G(x)=G(-x)
. So we have
-<G*m_{x},m>=+<m,G*m_{x}>=0
and

\frac{d}{dt}\|m(t)\|_{L^{1}}=0.

Finally, since

\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|m\|_{B^{1-2a}_{\infty,1}}\leq C\|m\|_{B^{2-2a}_{1,1}}\leq C\|m\|_{L^{1}}\leq C\|m_{0}\|_{L^{1}},~~if~~a>1,

we obtain the global existence by the blow-up criteria and the bootstrap argument.

∎

Next we prove another global solution of (
1.3
) with different form of the initial data.

Proof of theorem
1.4
:

Proof.

Set
T
be the maximal time of
m(t,x)
.
Firstly, since
u(t,x)=(G*m)(t,x)
and
G(z)=G(-z)
, one can easily deduce that
m(t,x)
is an odd function if
m_{0}(x)
is an odd function.
Similar to the proof of Theorem
1.3
, by the characteristic method we deduce that if
m_{0}\leq 0
when
x\leq 0
,
m_{0}\geq 0
when
x\geq 0
, then we obtain

(4.2)

\displaystyle m_{(}t,x)\leq 0~~when~~x\leq q(t,0);~~m_{0}\geq 0~~when~~x\geq q(t,0),~~\forall t\in[0,T),

where
q(t,\xi)=\xi+\int_{0}^{t}m(s,q(s,\xi))ds
is the characteristic curves.

Next we want to prove that

(4.3)

\displaystyle m(t,x)\leq 0~~when~~x\leq 0,~~m_{0}\geq 0~~when~~x\geq 0,~~\forall t\in[0,T).

If
q(t,0)=0
for any
t\in[0.T)
, then by (
4.2
) we immediately get (
4.3
). Other if
q(t,0)>0
(or
q(t,0)<0
) for some

m(t,x)\leq 0~~when~~x\in[-q(t,0),0];~~m_{0}\leq 0~~when~~x\in[0,q(t,0)].

Since

By (

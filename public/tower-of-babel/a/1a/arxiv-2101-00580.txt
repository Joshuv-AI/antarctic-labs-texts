Verma modules over deformed generalized Heisenberg-Virasoro algebras


Abstract
Let 𝔤 ⁡ ( G , λ ) \mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda) denote the deformed generalized Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra related to a complex parameter λ ≠ − 1 \lambda\neq-1 and an additive subgroup G G of ℂ \mathbb{C} . For a total order on G G that is compatible with addition, a Verma module over 𝔤 ⁡ ( G , λ ) \mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda) is defined. In this paper, we completely determine the irreducibility of these Verma modules. Keywords : Verma module, Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra, deformed Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra, deformed generalized Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra. MSC(2020) : 17B10, 17B68.

Introduction

In this paper we study the irreducibility of Verma modules over deformed generalized Heisenberg-Virasoro algebras, which are generalizations of the Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra, deformed Heisenberg-Virasoro algebras
and generalized Heisenberg-Virasoro algebras.

It is well known that the Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra, first introduced in
[
1
]
,
is the universal central extension of the Lie algebra of differential operators on a circle
of order no more than one.
The structure and representation theory of the Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
HV
has been well developed.
For example, derivations and automorphism group of
HV
were computed in
[
13
]
.
Irreducibility for Verma modules over
HV
was completely determined in
[
1
,
2
]
.
In
[
11
]
, Lü and Zhao classified irreducible Harish-Chandra modules over
HV
.
For more, one may see
[
5
,
16
]
and references therein.

The Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
HV
is graded by
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Replacing
{\mathbb{Z}}
by an arbitrary additive subgroup
G
of
{\mathbb{C}}
,
one gets the so-called generalized Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
HV[G]
.
Structure and representations of
HV[G]
were studied in
[
8
,
10
,
12
]
.
In particular, the irreducible Harish-Chandra modules over
HV[G]

were classified in
[
8
]
.

Infinitesimal deformation of a Lie algebra is one way to give new Lie algebras.
The infinitesimal deformations of the Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
HV
are called deformed Heisenberg-Virasoro algebras,
which were given in
[
9
]
.
Inspired by these algebras, Xu
[
14
]
introduced deformed generalized Heisenberg-Virasoro algebras
\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)
,
where
\lambda\neq-1
is a deformation parameter and
G
is an additive subgroup of
{\mathbb{C}}

such that
G
is free of rank
\nu
if
\lambda=-2
.
We also mention that the algebra
\mathfrak{g}(G,1)
is a high rank generalization of the
W
-algebra
W(2,2)
, which was first introduced in
[
15
]
and extensively studied by others, for example
[
3
,
6
,
7
]
.

With respect to a total order on
G
compatible with addition, there is a triangular decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)
, and Verma module can be defined accordingly.
In the present paper, we give a complete description of the irreducibility of the Verma modules over
\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)
in both cases where the total order on
G
are dense and discrete. In particular, the irreducibility of the Verma modules over the deformed Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra is determined and used in the proof for the discrete order case for
\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)
.

We arrange this paper as follows.
In section 2, we introduce the algebra
\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)
and its Verma modules.
We prove the irreducibility criterion for Verma modules over the deformed Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra in section 3, and for Verma modules over
\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)
in section 4.

Throughout this paper, the symbols
{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}
refer to the set of integers,
positive integers, complex numbers respectively. We denote by
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{m})
the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{m}
. Moreover, any sum is considered as a finite sum, and for a sequence of elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
, we denote by
x_{1}\cdots\widehat{x_{i}}\cdots x_{n}
the product of these elements with
x_{i}
missing.

2
Verma modules over the algebra
\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)

In this section we give some basics and notations about the deformed generalized Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)
and its Verma modules.

Let
\lambda\neq-1
, and let
G
be an additive subgroup of
{\mathbb{C}}

such that
G
is free of rank
\nu\geq 1
if
\lambda=-2
.
Recall from
[
14
]
the
deformed generalized Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra

\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)

with one deformation parameter
\lambda\neq-1
.
It is formed as the universal central extension of the semi-direct product
LI
of the centerless generalized Virasoro algebra
L=\text{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{L_{a}\mid a\in G\}
and its module of intermediate series
I=\text{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{I_{b}\mid b\in G\}
with action
[L_{a},I_{b}]=(b-\lambda a)I_{a+b}
. When
\lambda=-1
, the algebra
LI
has no universal central extension, hence the case was not considered in
[
14
]
, and also is not considered in this paper. Explicitly, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)
has a spanning set

\{L_{a},I_{a},C_{L},C_{I},C_{LI}^{(i)}\mid a\in G,0\leq i\leq\nu\}
subjecting to Lie brackets

\displaystyle[L_{a},I_{b}]=(b-\lambda a)I_{a+b}+\delta_{a+b,0}\left(C_{LI}^{(0)}(a^{2}+a)\delta_{\lambda,0}+\frac{1}{12}(a^{3}-a)C_{LI}^{(1)}\delta_{\lambda,1}+\sum\limits_{i=2}^{\nu}a_{(i)}C_{LI}^{(i)}\delta_{\lambda,-2}\right),

(2.1)

\displaystyle[L_{a},L_{b}]=(b-a)L_{a+b}+\frac{1}{12}(a^{3}-a)C_{L}\delta_{a+b,0},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ [I_{a},I_{b}]=aC_{I}\delta_{a+b,0}\delta_{\lambda,0},

where
C_{L},C_{I},C_{LI}^{(i)},0\leq i\leq\nu
, are central elements and
{a}_{(i)},1\leq i\leq\nu
, are coefficients of
a
with respect to a fixed
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
{\epsilon}_{1},\cdots,{\epsilon}_{\nu}
of
G
for the
\lambda=-2
case.

For later clarity, we should mention something about the case
\lambda=-2
.
From calculations in
[
14
]
the central extension of
LI
is determined by
\nu-1
independent nontrivial 2-cocycles.
The term
\sum\limits_{i=2}^{\nu}a_{(i)}C_{LI}^{(i)}\delta_{\lambda,-2}
in equation (
2.1
) is given by taking the
\nu-1
nontrivial 2-cocycles with respect to the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
{\epsilon}_{1},\cdots,{\epsilon}_{\nu}
of
G
. In particular, when
\nu=1
, i.e.,
G\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
, any 2-cocycle of
LI
is trivial and the term
\sum\limits_{i=2}^{\nu}a_{(i)}C_{LI}^{(i)}\delta_{\lambda,-2}
simply dispears.

When
\lambda=0
, the algebra
\mathfrak{g}(G,0)
is a generalized Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra and the irreducibility of Verma modules over
\mathfrak{g}(G,0)
was determined in
[
12
]
.
We shall assume that
\lambda\neq 0
in this paper.
Then equation (
2.1
) simply becomes

\displaystyle[L_{a},I_{b}]=(b-\lambda a)I_{a+b}+\delta_{a+b,0}\left(\frac{1}{12}(a^{3}-a)C_{LI}^{(1)}\delta_{\lambda,1}+\sum\limits_{i=2}^{\nu}a_{(i)}C_{LI}^{(i)}\delta_{\lambda,-2}\right),

(2.2)

\displaystyle[L_{a},L_{b}]=(b-a)L_{a+b}+\frac{1}{12}(a^{3}-a)C_{L}\delta_{a+b,0},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ [I_{a},I_{b}]=0.

We will simply denote
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)
if no confusion.
Fix a total order
\succ
on
G
which is compatible with addition,
i.e.,
b\succ c
implies
a+b\succ a+c
for any
a,b,c\in G
.
Set
G_{+}=\{a\in G\mid a\succ 0\}
.
For
a,b\in G
, we shall also write
b\prec a
if
a\succ b
,
and write
a\succeq b
if
a\succ b
or
a=b
.
With respect to the order
\succ
the algebra
\mathfrak{g}
has a triangular decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{-}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{+}
, where

\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\text{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{L_{0},I_{0},C_{L},C_{LI}^{(i)}\mid 1\leq i\leq\nu\},\hskip 14.22636pt\mathfrak{g}_{\pm}=\text{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{L_{a},I_{a}\mid\pm a\succ 0\}.

Let
\mu
be a linear function on
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
.
Denote by
I(\mu)
the left ideal of
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})
generated by

\{L_{a},I_{a},x-\mu(x)\mid a\succ 0,x\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}\}.

Then the
Verma module with highest weight

\mu
over
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
\succ

is defined to be

M=M(\mu,\succ)=\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})/I(\mu).

For
r>0,\ a_{1}\succeq a_{2}\succeq\cdots\succeq a_{r}\succ 0
,
we write for convenience that
\underline{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{r})

and call
\underline{a}
a
G_{+}
-vector
.
Moreover, write

L_{-\underline{a}}=L_{-a_{1}}\cdots L_{-a_{r}},\hskip 14.22636ptI_{-\underline{a}}=I_{-a_{1}}\cdots I_{-a_{r}},

and denote by
|\underline{a}|
the length of
\underline{a}
.
Let
\mathcal{G}
be the set of all
G_{+}
-vectors.
We may define a total order
\succ
on
\mathcal{G}
as follows
(although we use the same symbol for orders on
G
and
\mathcal{G}
, there should be no ambiguity).
For
\underline{a},\underline{b}\in\mathcal{G}
, if
k=|\underline{a}|>|\underline{b}|=l
, set
b_{l+1}=\cdots=b_{k}=0
.
Then we define

\underline{a}\succ\underline{b}
if and only if there exists
1\leq i\leq k
such that
a_{i}\succ b_{i}

and
a_{j}=b_{j}
for
j<i
.

Write
v=1+I(\mu)
.
Then by the PBW theorem, the Verma module
M
has a basis

L_{-\underline{a}}I_{-\underline{b}}v,\ \ \ |\underline{a}|,|\underline{b}|\geq 0.

We have a subspace filtration of the Verma module
M

0\subset M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots M_{r}\subset\cdots\subset M,

where
M_{r}=\text{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{v,L_{-\underline{a}}I_{-\underline{b}}v\mid|\underline{a}|\leq r,\underline{b}\in\mathcal{G}\}
for any
r\geq 0
.
We shall write
M_{r}=0
if
r<0
.
It is clear that
I_{a}M_{r}\subseteq M_{r-1}
for any
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
a\in G_{+}
.

For
a\in G_{+}
set
B_{a}=\{b\in G\mid a\succ b\succ 0\}
.
The total order
\succ
on
G
is called
dense
if
B_{a}
is infinite for any
a\in G_{+}
,
called
discrete
if
B_{a}
is empty for some
a\in G_{+}
.
Clearly, if
\succ
is discrete, there is a unique minimal element in
G_{+}
,
which we will denote by
{\epsilon}
in this paper.
Particularly,
\mathfrak{g}
has a subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
generated by
\{L_{k{\epsilon}},I_{k{\epsilon}}\mid k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
.
Since
{\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
as additive groups, from the statement above for the case
\lambda=-2
and equation (
2.2
), the algebra
\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)=\text{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{L_{k{\epsilon}},I_{k{\epsilon}},C_{L},C_{LI}^{(1)}\mid k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}

subjects to

\displaystyle[L_{m{\epsilon}},L_{n{\epsilon}}]=(n-m){\epsilon}L_{(m+n){\epsilon}}+\frac{1}{12}\left((m{\epsilon})^{3}-m{\epsilon}\right)C_{L}\delta_{m+n,0};\ \ \ \ [I_{m{\epsilon}},I_{n{\epsilon}}]=0;

(2.3)

\displaystyle[L_{m{\epsilon}},I_{n{\epsilon}}]=(n-\lambda m){\epsilon}I_{(m+n){\epsilon}}+\frac{1}{12}\left((m{\epsilon})^{3}-m{\epsilon}\right)C_{LI}^{(1)}\delta_{m+n,0}\delta_{\lambda,1}.

Moreover, the
\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
-submodule
M_{\epsilon}(\mu)=\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda))v
of
M
is actually a Verma module over
\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
with respect to the triangular decomposition
\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)=\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)_{-}\oplus\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)_{+}
, where

\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)_{\pm}=\text{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{L_{k{\epsilon}},I_{k{\epsilon}}\mid\pm k>0\},\ \ \ \ \mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)_{0}=\text{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{L_{0},I_{0},C_{L},C_{LI}^{(1)}\}.

When take
G={\mathbb{Z}}
and one gets the deformed Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
for
\lambda\neq 0,-1
,
which has a basis
\{L_{n},I_{n},C_{L},C_{LI}^{(1)}\mid n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}

satisfying

\displaystyle[L_{m},L_{n}]=(n-m)L_{m+n}+\frac{1}{12}(m^{3}-m)C_{L}\delta_{m+n,0},\ \ \ \ [I_{m},I_{n}]=0,

(2.4)

\displaystyle[L_{m},I_{n}]=(n-\lambda m)I_{m+n}+\frac{1}{12}(m^{3}-m)C_{LI}^{(1)}\delta_{\lambda,1}\delta_{m+n,0}.

At last we mention that there is a Lie algebra isomorphism from
\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
onto
\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
defined by(for
\lambda\neq 0,-1
)

\displaystyle L_{k}

\displaystyle\mapsto{\epsilon}^{-1}L_{k{\epsilon}}+\delta_{k,0}\frac{{\epsilon}^{-1}-{\epsilon}}{24}C_{L},\ \ \ \ C_{L}\mapsto{\epsilon}C_{L},

(2.5)

\displaystyle I_{k}

\displaystyle\mapsto{\epsilon}^{-1}I_{k{\epsilon}}+\delta_{k,0}\delta_{\lambda,1}\frac{{\epsilon}^{-1}-{\epsilon}}{24}C_{LI}^{(1)},\ \ \ \ C_{LI}^{(1)}\mapsto{\epsilon}C_{LI}^{(1)}.

3
Verma modules over the algebra
\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)

In this section we give a sufficient and necessary condition for a Verma module
over the deformed Heisenberg-Virasoro algebra
\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
to be irreducible, which will be used to prove the irreducibility criterion of the Verma module
M(\mu,\succ)
over
\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)
for discrete order
\succ
in Section
4
.

Recall the algebra
\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
and its triangular decomposition from the last section.
According to this decomposition one can define a Verma module over
\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
as follows.
Let
\varphi
be a linear function on
\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)_{0}
and
I(\varphi)
the left ideal of
the universal enveloping algebra
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda))
generated by

\{L_{n},I_{n}\mid n>0\}\cup\{x-\varphi(x)\mid x\in\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)_{0}\}.

Then the
Verma module with highest weight
\varphi
over
\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
is defined as the quotient

M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)=\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda))/I(\varphi).

For simplicity denote
\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
and
v=1+I(\varphi)
.
By the PBW theorem the Verma module
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)=\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{d}_{-})v
has a basis

I_{-{n}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{n}_{s}}L_{-{m}_{1}}\cdots L_{-{m}_{r}}v,

(3.1)

where
r,s\geq 0,\ n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{s}>0,\ m_{1}\geq\cdots\geq m_{r}>0
.
Moreover,
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)
has a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)_{n}

where
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)_{n}
is spanned by vectors of the form in (
3.1
) such that

n_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}+m_{1}+\cdots+m_{r}=n.

The algebra
\mathfrak{d}
has an anti-involution (an anti-isomorphism of order 2)
\sigma
such that

\sigma(L_{n})=L_{-n},\ \ \ \sigma(I_{n})=I_{-n},\ \ \ \ \sigma(x)=x\text{ for }x\in\mathfrak{d}_{0}.

Moreover, the universal enveloping algebra
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{d})
has a decomposition

\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{d})=\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{d}_{0})\oplus\left(\mathfrak{d}_{-}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{d})+\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{d})\mathfrak{d}_{+}\right).

Let
\pi:\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{d})\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{d}_{0})
denote the projection onto the first summand.
Then we have a symmetric bilinear form
(\cdot\mid\cdot)
on
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)
defined by

(xv\mid yv)v=\pi(\widetilde{\sigma}(x)y)v,

where
x,y\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{d}_{-})
and
\widetilde{\sigma}
is the anti-involution of
\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{d})

extended from
\sigma
by

\widetilde{\sigma}(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})=\sigma(x_{n})\cdots\sigma(x_{1})\ \ \ \text{ for any }x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{d}.

Clearly, we have
(v\mid v)=1
and

(xu\mid w)=(u\mid\widetilde{\sigma}(x)w)\ \ \ \text{ for any }x\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{d})\text{ and }u,w\in M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi).

Notice that
(M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)_{m}\mid M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)_{n})=0
if
m\neq n
,
and the radical of the bilinear form is the maximal
\mathfrak{d}
-submodule of
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)
.
Then to determine irreducibility of
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)
,
it suffices to consider the restriction of the bilinear form on each component
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)_{n}
.

Define a total order
\succ
on the set

\mathcal{Z}=\{(m_{1},\cdots,m_{s})\mid s\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},m_{1}\geq\cdots\geq m_{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}\}

in the same fashion as the order on
\mathcal{G}
in Section
2
.
Fix an integer
n\geq 0
and denote by
B_{n}
the set of the basis of
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)_{n}

consisting of vectors of the form in (
3.1
).
We define a total order
\succ
on
B_{n}
as follows. Write

I_{-{n}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{n}_{r}}L_{-{m}_{1}}\cdots L_{-{m}_{s}}v\succ I_{-{k}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{k}_{p}}L_{-{l}_{1}}\cdots L_{-{l}_{q}}v

if one of the following conditions stands,

(C1)

\sum n_{i}<\sum k_{i}
;

(C2)

\sum n_{i}=\sum k_{i}
and
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})\succ(k_{1},\cdots,k_{p})
;

(C3)

\sum n_{i}=\sum k_{i},\ (n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{p})
and

(m_{1},\cdots,m_{s})\prec(l_{1},\cdots,l_{q})
.

Write
B_{n}=\{u_{1},\cdots,u_{d}\}
with
u_{i}\prec u_{j}
if
i<j
, where
d=\dim M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)_{n}
.
Denote
A_{n}=(A_{ij})
the
d\times d
matrix with
A_{ij}=(u_{d+1-i}\mid u_{j})
.
In the following we compute the determinant
\det A_{n}
of
A_{n}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})\succ(m_{1},\cdots,m_{s})\in\mathcal{Z}
, then

(L_{-{n}_{1}}\cdots L_{-{n}_{r}}v\mid I_{-{m}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{m}_{s}}v)=(I_{-{m}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{m}_{s}}v\mid L_{-{n}_{1}}\cdots L_{-{n}_{r}}v)=0.

Proof.

For any integer
m\geq m_{1}
, we have

L_{m}I_{-{m}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{m}_{s}}v=\left(-m(1+\lambda)\varphi(I_{0})+\frac{1}{12}(m^{3}-m)\varphi(C_{LI}^{(1)})\delta_{\lambda,1}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial I_{-m}}(I_{-{m}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{m}_{s}})v.

(3.2)

Notice that there exists
1\leq k\leq\min\{r,s\}
such that
n_{k}>m_{k}
and
m_{i}=n_{i}
for
i<k
.
We see that
L_{n_{r}}\cdots L_{n_{1}}I_{-{m}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{m}_{s}}v=0
,
and then the lemma follows.

\Box

Lemma 3.2
.

The determinant
\det A_{n}
is a product of a nonzero integer and some

f(k)=-k(1+\lambda)\varphi(I_{0})+\frac{1}{12}(k^{3}-k)\varphi(C_{LI}^{(1)})\delta_{\lambda,1},\ \ \ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}.

Proof.

Let
1\leq a<b\leq d
, then
u_{a}\prec u_{b}
. Write

u_{a}=I_{-{n}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{n}_{r}}L_{-{m}_{1}}\cdots L_{-{m}_{s}}v,\ \ \ \ \ u_{b}=I_{-{k}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{k}_{p}}L_{-{l}_{1}}\cdots L_{-{l}_{q}}v.

Then we have

u_{d+1-a}=I_{-{m}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{m}_{s}}L_{-{n}_{1}}\cdots L_{-{n}_{r}}v.

Recall the order
\succ
on
B_{n}
.
If case (C1) stands, i.e.,
\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}<\sum_{i=1}^{p}k_{j}
, then we have
\sum_{i=1}^{s}m_{i}>\sum_{i=1}^{q}l_{j}
.
It follows from Lemma
3.1
that
I_{m_{s}}\cdots I_{m_{1}}L_{-{l}_{1}}\cdots L_{-{l}_{q}}v=0
.
Hence

L_{n_{r}}\cdots L_{n_{1}}I_{-{k}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{k}_{p}}I_{m_{s}}\cdots I_{m_{1}}L_{-{l}_{1}}\cdots L_{-{l}_{q}}v=0

and
A_{ab}=(u_{d+1-a}\mid u_{b})=0
.

If
\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}k_{j}
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{s}m_{i}>\sum_{i=1}^{q}l_{j}
and we have

A_{ab}=(L_{-{n}_{1}}\cdots L_{-{n}_{r}}v\mid I_{-{k}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{k}_{p}}v)(I_{-{m}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{m}_{s}}v\mid L_{-{l}_{1}}\cdots L_{-{l}_{q}}v),

which is zero for both case (C2) and case (C3) by Lemma
3.1
.
This proves that the matrix
A_{n}
is upper triangular.

Moreover, by (
3.2
) we have

\displaystyle A_{aa}

\displaystyle=(u_{d+1-a}\mid u_{a})=(I_{-{m}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{m}_{s}}L_{-{n}_{1}}\cdots L_{-{n}_{r}}v\mid I_{-{n}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{n}_{r}}L_{-{m}_{1}}\cdots L_{-{m}_{s}}v)

\displaystyle=(L_{-{n}_{1}}\cdots L_{-{n}_{r}}v\mid I_{-{n}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{n}_{r}}v)(I_{-{m}_{1}}\cdots I_{-{m}_{s}}v\mid L_{-{m}_{1}}\cdots L_{-{m}_{s}}v)

\displaystyle=K_{a}\prod_{i=1}^{r}f(n_{i})^{p_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{s}f(m_{j})^{q_{j}},

where
K_{a}
is some nonzero integer,

p_{i},q_{j}
are the times of
n_{i},m_{j}
appearing in
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})
,
(m_{1},\cdots,m_{s})

respectively.
This proves the lemma.

\Box

We can prove our main theorem in this section.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\lambda\neq 0,-1
. The Verma module
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)
over
\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
is irreducible if and only if
12(1+\lambda)\varphi(I_{0})-(k^{2}-1)\varphi(C_{LI}^{(1)})\delta_{\lambda,1}\neq 0
for any nonzero integer
k
.

Proof.

If
12(1+\lambda)\varphi(I_{0})-(k^{2}-1)\varphi(C_{LI}^{(1)})\delta_{\lambda,1}\neq 0
for any nonzero integer
k
, then
f(k)\neq 0
for any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.
Hence the bilinear form on
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)
is non-degenerate by Lemma
3.2
,
which implies that the
\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
-module
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)
is irreducible.

Suppose otherwise
and let
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
be the smallest integer such that

12(1+\lambda)\varphi(I_{0})=(p^{2}-1)\varphi(C_{LI}^{(1)})\delta_{\lambda,1}
.
So
f(p)=0
and
f(k)\neq 0
for any
0<k<p
.
Hence the bilinear form on
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)
is degenerate by Lemma
3.2
,
whose radical forms a nonzero proper
\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
-submodule of
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)
.

\Box

We mark that for the case
\lambda=1
,
\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
is the
W
-algebra
W(2,2)
,
for which the structure of the Verma module
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)
is determined in
[
7
]
.

4
Irreducibility of Verma modules over
\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)

In this section we give an irreducibility criterion for the Verma module
M(\mu,\succ)
over the algebra
\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)
with
\lambda\neq 0,-1
, and
G
being an additive subgroup of
{\mathbb{C}}
such that
G
is free of rank
\nu
if
\lambda=-2
.
In specific, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

(1) Suppose the order
\succ
on
G
is dense.
The Verma module
M(\mu,\succ)
over
\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)
is irreducible if and only if

\mu(\mathfrak{i})\neq 0
, where

\mathfrak{i}=\text{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{I_{0},C_{LI}^{(i)}\mid 1\leq i\leq\nu\}.

When
\mu(\mathfrak{i})=0
,
the Verma module
M(\mu,\succ)
contains a proper
\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)
-submodule

N=\text{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{I_{-\underline{b}}v\mid\underline{b}\in\mathcal{G}\},

which is maximal if and only if
(\mu(L_{0}),\mu(C_{L}))\neq(0,0)
.
If
\mu=0
then
M(\mu,\succ)
contains a unique maximal
\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)
-submodule

\text{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{L_{-\underline{a}}I_{-\underline{b}}v\mid a,b\in\mathcal{G},|\underline{a}|+|\underline{b}|>0\}
.

(2) Suppose the order
\succ
on
G
is discrete.
The Verma module
M(\mu,\succ)
over
\mathfrak{g}(G,\lambda)
is irreducible if and only if

24(1+\lambda)\mu(I_{0})+\left(1+\lambda+{\epsilon}^{2}(1-\lambda-2k^{2})\right)\mu(C_{LI}^{(1)})\delta_{\lambda,1}\neq 0\text{ for any }k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}.

Proof.

(1) Let
M^{\prime}
be a nonzero
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of
M

and recall the subspace
M_{0}
of
M
.

Claim 1
:
M^{\prime}\cap M_{0}\neq 0
. Let

u=\sum_{\underline{a},\underline{b}\in\mathcal{G}}A_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}L_{-\underline{a}}I_{-\underline{b}}v\in M^{\prime},\ \ \ \ \ A_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}\neq 0.

Notice that since the
L_{0}
-action on
M
is semisimple,
we may demand that
u
is a
L_{0}
- eigenvector.
This means for all
\underline{a},\underline{b}
such that
A_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}\neq 0
in the above equation,
the sum of their entries
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}+b_{1}+\cdots+b_{s}
are equivalent.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\{\underline{a}\mid A_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}\neq 0\text{ for some }\underline{b}\in\mathcal{G}\}
and

r=\max\{|\underline{a}|\mid\underline{a}\in\mathcal{A}\}
.
If
r=0
, Claim 1 is trivial.
Assume
r>0
and denote
\mathcal{A}_{r}=\{\underline{a}\in\mathcal{A}\mid|\underline{a}|=r\}
.
Since the order
\succ
is dense, there exists
c\in G_{+}
such that
c\prec\min\{a_{r}\mid\underline{a}\in\mathcal{A}_{r}\},\ c\notin\{-\lambda a_{i}\mid\underline{a}\in\mathcal{A}_{r},1\leq i\leq r\}
and

\{a_{i}-c\mid\underline{a}\in\mathcal{A}_{r}\}\cap\{b_{j}\mid A_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}\neq 0\text{ for all }\underline{a}\in\mathcal{A}_{r},1\leq j\leq|\underline{b}|\}=\emptyset.

Notice that
I_{a}M_{s}\subseteq M_{s-1}
for any
a\in G_{+},s\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We have

\displaystyle I_{c}u

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{\underline{a}\in\mathcal{A}_{r},\underline{b}\in\mathcal{G}}A_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}[I_{c},L_{-\underline{a}}]I_{-\underline{b}}v+M_{r-2}

\displaystyle\equiv-\sum_{\underline{a}\in\mathcal{A}_{r},\underline{b}\in\mathcal{G}}A_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}(c+\lambda a_{i})L_{-a_{1}}\cdots\widehat{L_{-a_{i}}}\cdots L_{-a_{r}}I_{c-a_{i}}I_{-\underline{b}}v+M_{r-2}.

Since for different
\underline{a},\underline{b}
, the corresponding vectors

L_{-a_{1}}\cdots\widehat{L_{-a_{i}}}\cdots L_{-a_{r}}I_{c-a_{i}}I_{-\underline{b}}v

are linearly independent, one gets
I_{c}u\in M_{r-1}\setminus M_{r-2}
.
Claim 1 follows by induction on
r
.

Claim 2
: There exists some vector
I_{-\underline{e}}v\in M^{\prime}
.
By Claim 1 we may assume the vector
u\in M^{\prime}
has the form

u=\sum_{\underline{b}\in\mathcal{G}}A_{\underline{b}}I_{-\underline{b}}v,\ \ \ A_{\underline{b}}\neq 0.

Set
T_{u}=\{\underline{b}\mid A_{\underline{b}}\neq 0\}
and
let
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{r})
be the maximal element in
T_{u}
.
We may find some
e_{1}\in G_{+}
such that
e_{1}\prec a_{r},\ a_{1}+\lambda(a_{1}-e_{1})\neq 0
and

\{x\in G\mid a_{1}-e_{1}\prec x\prec a_{1}\}\cap\{b_{1},b_{2}\mid\underline{b}=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{s})\in T_{u}\}=\emptyset.

Notice that
a_{1}-e_{1}-b_{j}\succ 0
for any
\underline{b}=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{s})\in T_{u}
and
j\in\{1,\cdots,s\}

except those
b_{j}=a_{1}
.
Then since
u
is a
L_{0}
-eigenvector, we get

u_{1}=L_{a_{1}-e_{1}}u=\sum_{\underline{b}}A_{\underline{b}}[L_{a_{1}-e_{1}},I_{-\underline{b}}]v=-\sum p(\underline{b})A_{\underline{b}}(a_{1}+\lambda(a_{1}-e_{1}))I_{-b_{2}}\cdots I_{-b_{s}}I_{-e_{1}}v,

(4.1)

where the second sum takes over those
\underline{b}\in T_{u}
such that
b_{1}=a_{1}
,
and
p(\underline{b})
is the multiple of
a_{1}
in
\underline{b}
.
Let
T_{u_{1}}
denote the set of
G_{+}
-vectors
\underline{c}=(c_{1},\cdots,c_{s})
such that

\{c_{1},\cdots,c_{s}\}=\{e_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{s}\}

where
\underline{b}
appears in the last summand in (
4.1
).
One can see that
u_{1}\neq 0
and

\underline{a}^{(1)}=(a_{2},\cdots,a_{r},e_{1})
is the maximal element in
T_{u_{1}}
.
Set
A_{\underline{b}}^{(1)}=-pA_{\underline{b}}(a_{1}+\lambda(a_{1}-e_{1}))\neq 0
.

For
k=2,\cdots,r
, we define recursively and can easily prove by induction that

(i)

Let
0\prec e_{k}\prec e_{k-1},\ a_{k}+\lambda(a_{k}-e_{k})\neq 0
and

\{x\in G\mid a_{k}-e_{k}\prec x\prec a_{k}\}\cap\{b_{k+1},b_{k}\mid\underline{b}\in T_{u_{k-1}}\}=\emptyset.

Then

u_{k}=L_{a_{k}-e_{k}}u_{k-1}=-\sum p(\underline{b})A_{\underline{b}}^{(k-1)}(a_{k}+\lambda(a_{k}-e_{k}))I_{-b_{k+1}}\cdots I_{-b_{s}}I_{-e_{1}}\cdots I_{-e_{k}}v\neq 0,

where the sum takes over
\underline{b}\in T_{u_{k-1}}
such that
b_{k}=a_{k}

and
p(\underline{b})
is the multiple of
a_{k}
in
\underline{b}
.

(ii)

Let
T_{u_{k}}
be the set of
G_{+}
-vectors formed by

b_{k+1},\cdots,b_{s},e_{1},\cdots,e_{k}
, where
b_{k+1},\cdots,b_{s}
appear in
u_{k}
.
Clearly,
T_{u_{k}}\neq\emptyset,\ A_{\underline{b}}^{(k)}=-p(\underline{b})A_{\underline{b}}^{(k-1)}(a_{k}+\lambda(a_{k}-e_{k}))\neq 0
and

\underline{a}^{(k)}=(a_{k+1},\cdots,a_{r},e_{1},\cdots,e_{k})
is the maximal element in
T_{u_{k}}
.

Take
k=r
and one gets Claim 2.

Suppose
\mu(\mathfrak{i})\neq 0
, i.e., at least one of
\mu(I_{0})
and
\mu(C_{LI}^{(i)})
is nonzero. Define a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linear function
f_{\mu}
on
G
by

f_{\mu}(a)=\mu([L_{a},I_{-a}])=-(1+\lambda)a\mu(I_{0})+\frac{1}{12}(a^{3}-a)\mu(C_{LI}^{(1)})\delta_{\lambda,1}+\sum_{i=2}^{\nu}{a}_{(i)}\mu(C_{LI}^{(i)})\delta_{\lambda,-2}.

Since the image of
f_{\mu}
is not a dense set and the total order
\succ
on
G
is dense, we may demand that the vector
I_{-\underline{e}}v\in M^{\prime}
as in Claim 2 satisfies that
f_{\mu}(e_{i})\neq 0
for all
e_{1},\cdots,e_{r}
. Then we have

L_{e_{r}}\dots L_{e_{1}}I_{-\underline{e}}v=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{r}k_{i}f_{\mu}(e_{i})v\neq 0,

where
k_{i}
are the multiple of
e_{i}
in
\underline{e}
.
This proves
v\in M^{\prime}
. So
M
is irreducible.

When
\mu(\mathfrak{i})=0
, one can easily check that
N
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule of
M
.
Notice that the quotient
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M/N
is equivalent to the Verma module over
the generalized Virasoro algebra
\text{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{L_{a},C_{L}\mid a\in G\}
generated by
v
.
Then the rest part of (1) follows from Theorem 3.1 (1) in
[
4
]
.

(2) Suppose the order
\succ
on
G
is discrete and
\lambda\neq 0,-1
.
Recall the minimal element
{\epsilon}
in
G_{+}
, the subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
of
\mathfrak{g}

and the
\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
-module
M_{\epsilon}(\mu)
.
We first prove that
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M(\mu,\succ)
is irreducible if and only if the
\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
-module
M_{\epsilon}(\mu)
is irreducible.

Write
a\succ{\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}}
if
a\succ n{\epsilon}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Denote
H_{+}=\{a\in G\mid a\succ{\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}}\},\ H_{-}=-H_{+}
and

\mathfrak{g}_{H_{+}}=\text{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{L_{a},I_{a}\mid a\in H_{+}\},\ \ \ \mathfrak{g}_{H_{-}}=\text{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{L_{a},I_{a}\mid a\in H_{-}\}

Then we have
G=H_{-}\cup{\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}}\cup H_{+}
,
\mathfrak{g}_{H_{+}}M_{\epsilon}(\mu)=0
and

M=\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{H_{+}})}M_{\epsilon}(\mu)=\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}_{H_{-}})M_{\epsilon}(\mu).

It is clear that the irreducibility of the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
implies
the irreducibility of the
\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
-module
M_{\epsilon}(\mu)
.

Suppose otherwise that the
\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
-module
M_{\epsilon}(\mu)
is irreducible.
We want to prove that
M_{\epsilon}(\mu)\cap M^{\prime\prime}\neq 0
for any nonzero
\mathfrak{g}
-submodule
M^{\prime\prime}
of
M
, from which one deduces that the
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M
is irreducible.

Denote by
\mathcal{H}
the subset of
\mathcal{G}
consisting of
\underline{a}
with all entries
a_{i}\in H_{+}
.
For
r\geq 0
set

M_{(r)}=\text{span}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\{L_{-\underline{a}}I_{-\underline{b}}M_{\epsilon}(\mu)\mid\underline{a},\underline{b}\in\mathcal{H},|\underline{a}|\leq r\}+M_{\epsilon}(\mu),

and for
r<0
set
M_{(r)}=0
.
Clearly,
I_{a}M_{(r)}\subseteq M_{(r-1)}
for any
r\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ a\in H_{+}
,
and for any
w\in M_{(r)}
, there exists some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
such that

I_{k{\epsilon}}w\in M_{(r-1)}
.

Claim 3
:
M_{(0)}\cap M^{\prime\prime}\neq 0
.
Choose a nonzero vector
u\in M^{\prime\prime}
.
If
u\in M_{(0)}
, the claim is trivial.
If
u\in M_{(r)}\setminus M_{(r-1)}
for some
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, then we may write

u=\sum A_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}L_{-\underline{a}}I_{-\underline{b}}v_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}+w,

(4.2)

where the sum takes over
\underline{a},\underline{b}\in\mathcal{H}
with
|\underline{a}|=r
and

A_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}\neq 0,\ 0\neq v_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}\in M_{\epsilon}(\mu),\ w\in M_{(r-1)}
.
Choose
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
such that
I_{k{\epsilon}}w\in M_{(r-2)},\ I_{k{\epsilon}}v_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}=0

for all
v_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}
in (
4.2
)
and
\lambda a_{i}+k{\epsilon}\neq 0
for all entries
a_{i}
of
\underline{a}
in (
4.2
).
Then

I_{k{\epsilon}}u=-\sum A_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\lambda a_{i}+k{\epsilon})L_{-a_{1}}\cdots\widehat{L_{-a_{i}}}\cdots L_{-a_{r}}I_{-\underline{b}}I_{-a_{i}+k{\epsilon}}v_{\underline{a},\underline{b}}\mod M_{(r-2)}

is a nonzero vector in
M^{\prime\prime}\cap M_{(r-1)}
.
The claim follows by induction on
r
.

By Claim 3 we have a nonzero vector

w=\sum A_{\underline{a}}I_{-\underline{a}}v_{\underline{a}}\in M^{\prime\prime},

(4.3)

where
\underline{a}\in\mathcal{H},A_{\underline{a}}\neq 0,0\neq v_{\underline{a}}\in M_{\epsilon}(\mu)
.
For
r\geq 0
, denote by
M_{(0,r)}
the subspace of
M_{(0)}
spanned by
I_{-\underline{a}}M_{\epsilon}(\mu)

with
\underline{a}\in\mathcal{H}
and
|\underline{a}|\leq r
,
and for
r<0
let
M_{(0,r)}=0
.
Notice that
M_{(0,0)}=M_{\epsilon}(\mu)
.

Denote by
S_{w}
the set of
\underline{a}
such that
A_{\underline{a}}\neq 0
in (
4.3
)
and set
r=\max\{|\underline{a}|\mid\underline{a}\in S_{w}\}
.
If
r=0
then
w\in M^{\prime\prime}\cap M_{\epsilon}(\mu)
and the proof is done.
Suppose
r>0
and denote
c=\max\{a_{1}\mid(a_{1}\cdots,a_{s})\in S_{w}\}
.
For
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
we have

L_{c-k{\epsilon}}w=-\sum A_{\underline{a}}\sum_{i=1}^{p(\underline{a})}(a_{i}+\lambda c-k\lambda{\epsilon})I_{-a_{1}}\cdots\widehat{I_{-a_{i}}}\cdots I_{-a_{s}}I_{c-a_{i}-k{\epsilon}}v_{\underline{a}},

where the first sum takes over
\underline{a}
with
a_{1}=c
, and
p(\underline{a})
is the times of
I_{-c}
appearing in
I_{-\underline{a}}
.
Take
k
large enough and we see that
L_{c-k{\epsilon}}w\neq 0
lies in
M_{(0,s)}
for some
s<r
.
Applying induction on
r
we prove
M^{\prime\prime}\cap M_{\epsilon}(\mu)\neq 0
.
Till now we have proved that
\mathfrak{g}
-module
M(\mu,\succ)
is irreducible if and only if the
\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
-module
M_{\epsilon}(\mu)
is irreducible.

Recall the algebra
\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
, its Verma module
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)
from Section
3
.
Through the isomorphism given in (
2.5
) from
\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
onto
\mathfrak{g}({\epsilon}{\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
, we consider
M_{\epsilon}(\mu)
as a
\mathfrak{g}({\mathbb{Z}},\lambda)
-module, which is isomorphic to
M_{\mathbb{Z}}(\varphi)
with
\varphi
satisfying

\displaystyle\varphi(L_{0})

\displaystyle={\epsilon}^{-1}\mu(L_{0})+\frac{{\epsilon}^{-1}-{\epsilon}}{24}\mu(C_{L}),\ \ \ \ \varphi(C_{L})={\epsilon}\mu(C_{L}),

\displaystyle\varphi(I_{0})

\displaystyle={\epsilon}^{-1}\mu(I_{0})+\frac{{\epsilon}^{-1}-{\epsilon}}{24}\mu(C_{LI}^{(1)})\delta_{\lambda,1},\ \ \ \ \varphi(C_{LI}^{(1)})={\epsilon}\mu(C_{LI}^{(1)}).

Now applying Theorem
3.3
, we get (2).

\Box

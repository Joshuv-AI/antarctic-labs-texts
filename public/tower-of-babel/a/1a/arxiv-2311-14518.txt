1.
Introduction

The effect of non-linearity in conservation laws is two-folds: on the one hand it prevents the existence of smooth solutions due to shocks formation. On the other hand it has some regularizing effects.
The most striking example is the estimate by Oleinik
[
35
]
, which proves that
L^{\infty}
initial data are immediately regularized to
BV_{\mathrm{loc}}
at positive times, for entropy solutions of the classical Burgers’ equation

u_{t}+[u^{2}/2]_{x}=0.

Furthermore,
[
4
]
proved that
BV_{\mathrm{loc}}
regularity improves to
SBV_{\mathrm{loc}}
, then generalized to more general
1d
scalar balance laws in
[
36
]
and to systems of conservation laws in
[
9
]
.
Similar results have been proven for Hamilton-Jacobi equations in several space dimensions starting from
[
10
]
.

A possible approach to prove the
BV_{\mathrm{loc}}
estimate in the scalar case relies on the existence of a family non-crossing (backward) characteristics
[
21
, Chapter 10]
along which the solution is constant. The non-crossing condition is a geometric constraint which forces
BV_{\mathrm{loc}}
regularity.
This approach has been extended to the more general conservation law of the form:

(1.1)

\displaystyle u_{t}+[f(u)]_{x}=0,

see
[
13
,
1
]
for the case of convex fluxes
f
and
[
19
,
17
,
32
]
, for the case of general smooth fluxes. The final result is that a fractional regularity of entropy solutions can be obtained quantifying the non-linearity of the flux
f
.

Motivated by some applications to the theory of rectifiable sets in the Heisenberg group, in this paper we consider the presence of the source
g\in L^{\infty}
in (
1.1
), i.e.

(1.2)

\displaystyle u_{t}+[f(u)]_{x}=g,

We prove a regularity result for the continuous solutions of (
1.2
)
where
f\in C^{\ell}(\mathbb{R})
is nonlinear of order
\ell\geq 2
(see Definition
A
). More precisely, we first demonstrate that every continuous solution to (
1.2
) is automatically locally
1/\ell-
Hölder continuous and, even better,

(1.3)

u(t,x+h)-u(t,x)=o\left(\sqrt[\ell]{|h|}\right)\quad\mbox{as}\quad h\to 0

at Lebesgue almost every point
(t,x)
.
It is known that, even with analytic fluxes
f
and continuous sources terms
g
, continuous solutions to (
1.2
) improve to Hölder continuous regularity but do not go beyond. Indeed, even with quadratic flux continuous solutions to (
2.1
) might exhibit nasty fractal behaviour and in general they are neither Sobolev nor BV
[
29
,
3
]
. Moreover, if the flux
f
is strictly convex but not analytic the result generally fails
[
3
, § 5]
, which motivates the finite order nonlinearity Assumption
A
on the flux. Similar results have been obtained also for conservation laws in several space dimensions by means of the kinetic formulation (see for example
[
31
,
27
]
), nevertheless the conjectured optimal regularity has not been proven yet.
We mention that also in the case of Hamilton-Jacobi equations, optimal Hölder regularity has been recently proved (see
[
18
]
and references therein).

In order to describe the application of our result to the theory of rectifiable sets in the Heisenberg group we recall that
the notion of Lipschitz submanifolds in sub-Riemannian geometry was introduced, at least in the setting of Carnot groups, by B. Franchi, R. Serapioni and F. Serra Cassano in a series of papers
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
through the theory of intrinsic Lipschitz graphs (see also
[
15
,
16
,
22
]
). Roughly speaking, a subset
S\subset\mathbb{G}
of a Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
is intrinsic Lipschitz if at each point
P\in S
there is an intrinsic cone with vertex P and fixed opening, intersecting
S
only in
P
. Remarkably, this notion turned out to be the right one in the setting of the intrinsic rectifiability in the simplest Carnot group, namely the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Indeed, it was proved in
[
26
]
that the notion of rectifiable set in terms of an intrinsic regular hypersurfaces is equivalent to the one in terms of intrinsic Lipschitz graphs. We also remark that intrinsic Lipschitz functions played a crucial role in the recent paper
[
33
]
, where the longstanding question of determining the approximation ratio of the Goemans-Linial algorithm for the Sparsest Cut Problem was settled, see also
[
34
]
for different applications.
We address the interested reader to
[
25
,
38
]
for a complete introduction to the theory of intrinsic Lipschitz functions. One of the main open questions in this area of research is whether a Rademacher type theorem holds.
Namely, assume that a splitting
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{W}\mathbb{V}
of a Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
is fixed,
is it true that every intrinsically Lipschitz function
u:\mathbb{W}\to\mathbb{V}
is intrinsically differentiable almost everywhere? In
[
23
,
25
]
, it is proved that the answer is
yes
if
\mathbb{G}
is of step two or a group of type
\star
and
\mathbb{V}=\mathbb{R}
. More recently, D. Vittone in
[
38
]
proved that the answer is also
yes
in the case of the Heisenberg group without any a priori assumption on the splitting. We also address the interested reader to
[
7
,
8
,
30
]
for further partial results. Remarkably, in
[
28
]
the authors constructed intrinsic Lipschitz graphs of codimension
2
in Carnot groups which are not intrinsically differentiable almost everywhere. Insipired by the results contained in
[
6
]
, in
[
11
]
, the first named author together with Bigolin and Serra Cassano provided a characterization of intrinsic Lipschitz graphs in the Heisenberg group in terms of a system of non linear first order PDEs. Moreover, they proved the equivalence of different notions of continuous weak solutions to the equation

(1.4)

\displaystyle u_{t}+[u^{2}/2]_{x}=g,

where
g
is a bounded function. It turns out that the question whether an intrinsically Lipschitz function
u
is intrinsically differentiable or is equivalent to (
1.3
).
This observation allows us to provide a completely PDEs based proof of the Rademacher theorem in
\mathbb{H}^{1}
.
We point out that the proof provided in
[
25
]
requires some deep and nontrivial results in geometric measure theory, namely the fact that the subgraph of an intrinsic Lipschitz function
u
is a set with locally finite
\mathbb{H}-
perimeter and that at almost every point of the graph of
u
there is an approximate tangent plane. Another interesting feature of our approach is its potential applicability to those Carnot groups where the aforementioned geometric tools are not yet available e.g. the Engel group.

Structure of the paper:

In §
2

When the source is bounded, such estimate is the key to Hölder continuity and Lipschitz continuity along characteristics.
We prove in §

.
In §
5
we finally prove the Rademacher theorem for intrinsic Lipschitz functions in
\mathbb{H}^{1}
.

Proposition 4.3

•

We already discussed

•

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{k}(t)}^{\gamma_{k}(t)+\delta_{j}x_{k}}u(t,x)dx-\int_{x_{k}}^{x_{k}+\delta_{j}x_{k}}u(0,x)dx\leq

5.
A new proof of the Rademacher theorem for intrinsic Lipschitz functions

Aim of this section is to provide a new proof of the Rademacher theorem for intrinsic Lipschitz functions in the first Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{1}
. We start by recalling the main definitions, we refer the interested reader to
[
37
]
for a complete introduction to the subject.

We denote the points of
\mathbb{H}^{1}\equiv\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{3}

by

P=[z,t]=[x+iy,t]=(x,y,t),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C},\ x,y\in\mathbb{R},\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

If
P=[z,t]
,
Q=[z^{\prime},t^{\prime}]\in\mathbb{H}^{1}
and
r>0
, the group
operation reads as

(5.1)

P\cdot Q:=\left[z+z^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\Ima(\langle z,\bar{z^{\prime}}\rangle)\right].

The group identity is the origin
0
and one has

[z,t]^{-1}=[-z,-t]
. In
\mathbb{H}^{1}
there is a natural one
parameter group of non isotropic dilations

\delta_{r}(P):=[rz,r^{2}t]
,
r>0
.
The group
\mathbb{H}^{1}
can be endowed with the homogeneous norm

\|P\|_{\infty}:=\max\{|z|,|t|^{1/2}\}

and with the left-invariant and homogeneous distance

d_{\infty}(P,Q):=\|P^{-1}\cdot Q\|_{\infty}.

The metric
d_{\infty}
is equivalent to the standard
Carnot-Carathéodory distance. It follows that the Hausdorff
dimension of
(\mathbb{H}^{1},d_{\infty})
is
4
, whereas
its topological dimension is clearly
3
.
The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}_{1}
of left invariant vector fields
is (linearly) generated by

X=\partial_{x}-\frac{1}{2}y\partial_{t},\quad Y=\partial_{y}+\frac{1}{2}x\partial_{t},\quad T=\partial_{t}

and the only nonvanishing commutators are

[X,Y]=T.

In the spirit of
[
6
]
we set

\mathbb{W}:=\{(x,y,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{1}:\ x=0\}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Therefore, if
A\in\mathbb{W}
, we write

A=(y,t)
.

Following
[
6
]
, we define the graph quasidistance as:

Definition 5.1
.

For
A=(y,t),\,B=(y^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\omega
we define

d_{\varphi}(A,B)=|y-y^{\prime}|+\left|t^{\prime}-t-\frac{1}{2}(\varphi(A)+\varphi(B))(y^{\prime}-y)\right|^{1/2}.

An intrinsic differentiable structure can be induced on
\mathbb{W}
by means of
d_{\varphi}
, see
[
6
]
.
We remind that a map
L:\mathbb{W}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\mathbb{W}
-linear if it is a group homeomorphism and
L(ry,r^{2}t)=rL(y,t)
for all
r>0
and
(y,t)\in\mathbb{W}
. We recall then the notion of
\varphi
-differentiablility.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\varphi:\omega\subset\mathbb{W}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a fixed continuous function, and let

A_{0}\in\omega
and
\psi:\omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be given.
We say that
\psi
is
\varphi
-differentiable at
A_{0}
if there is an
\mathbb{W}
-linear functional
L:\mathbb{W}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

(5.2)

\lim_{A\rightarrow A_{0}}\frac{\psi(A)-\psi(A_{0})-L(A_{0}^{-1}\cdot A)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}_{\varphi}(A_{0},A)}=0.

It is well known
[
6
]
that
given a
\mathbb{W}
-linear functional
L:\mathbb{W}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
there exists a unique
\hat{w}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

L(A)=L((y,t))=\hat{w}y
.
Let us now introduce the concept of intrinsic Lipschitz function.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\varphi:\omega\subset\mathbb{W}\to\mathbb{R}
. We say that
\varphi
is an intrinsic Lipschitz continuous function and we write
\varphi\in Lip_{\mathbb{W}}(\omega)
if there exists a constant
L>0
such that

|\varphi(A)-\varphi(B)|\leq Ld_{\varphi}(A,B)\quad\forall A,B\in\omega.

We say that
\varphi
is locally intrinsic Lipschitz and we write
\varphi\in Lip_{\mathbb{W},loc}(\omega)
if
\varphi\in Lip_{\mathbb{W},loc}(\omega^{\prime})
for any
\omega^{\prime}\Subset\omega
.

The following characterization is proved in
[
11
]
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open set and let
\varphi:\omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function. Then
\varphi\in Lip_{\mathbb{W},loc}(\omega)
if and only if there exists
g\in L^{\infty}(\omega)
such that

\varphi_{y}+[\varphi^{2}/2]_{t}=g\quad\mbox{in}\ \mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\omega).

Let us finally recall the following Rademacher type Theorem, proved in
[
25
]
(see also
[
38
]
). As recalled in the Introduction the original proof requires deep results in geometric measure theory, namely the fact that the subgraph of an intrinsic Lipschitz function
\varphi
is a set with locally finite
\mathbb{H}
-perimeter and that at almost every point of the graph of
\varphi
there is an approximate. Here instead we propose a completely PDEs based proof which uses the results of Section
4
.

Theorem 5.5
.

If
\varphi\in Lip_{\mathbb{W}}(\omega)
then
\varphi
is
\varphi
-differentiable
\mathcal{L}^{2}-
a.e. in
\omega
.

More precisely, we prove
\varphi
-differentiability at those points which are Lebesgue points of
g
for all the coverings (
4.4
), for any fixed sequence
\rho_{i}
decreasing to 0.

Proof.

The proof relies on the fine estimates of the previous sections: we prefer within this proof to come back to the notations we had there for the variables: we call from now on
(t,x)
the variables that at the beginning of this section were denoted as
(y,t)
.

Let
A_{0}=(t,x)
be a Lebesgue point of
g
with respect to the coverings
\mathcal{V}_{\varrho_{j}}
of Lemma
4
and for simplicity we assume
A_{0}=(0,0)
.
We show that any sequence
A_{n}=(t_{n},x_{n})\to A_{0}
has a (not relabeled) subsequence for which

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{|\varphi(A_{n})-\varphi(A_{0})-g(A_{0})t_{n}|}{d_{\varphi}(A_{n},A_{0})}=0.

Let
\gamma_{n}\in C^{1}([0,t_{n}])
be a characteristic of
\varphi

and set

.
Since
A_{n}\to 0
and
\gamma_{n}
are uniformly Lipschitz, then
A^{\prime}_{n}\to 0
. Without loss of generality we assume that
t_{n},x^{\prime}_{n}\in\left(0,\frac{1}{1+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}{(\omega)}}}\right)
.
Up to a (not relabeled) subsequence one of the following two cases is satisfied.

Case 1
: It holds

as

In this case it follows from Proposition
4

(5.3)

\displaystyle|\varphi(A_{n})-\varphi(A_{0})-g(A_{0})t_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}t_{n}+|\varphi(A_{n})-\varphi(A^{\prime}_{n})|+|\varphi(A^{\prime}_{n})-\varphi(A_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}t_{n}+o\left(\sqrt{x^{\prime}_{n}}\right)=o\left(\sqrt{x^{\prime}_{n}}\right)\,.

Let us write,

(5.4)

\displaystyle d_{\varphi}(A_{n},A_{0})=t_{n}+\sqrt{x_{n}^{\prime}}+B(n).

where

B(n):=\left|x_{n}-\frac{\varphi(A_{n})+\varphi(A_{0})}{2}\cdot t_{n}\right|^{1/2}-\sqrt{x_{n}^{\prime}}

we claim that

(5.5)

B(n)=o(\sqrt{x_{n}^{\prime}})\quad\mbox{as}\ n\to+\infty.

First we observe that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\bigg|x_{n}-\frac{\varphi(A_{n})+\varphi(A_{0})}{2}

\displaystyle\cdot t_{n}-x^{\prime}_{n}\bigg|=\left|\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\varphi(s,\gamma_{n}(s))\,ds-\frac{\varphi(A_{n})+\varphi(A_{0})}{2}\cdot t_{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\penalty\ \left|\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\varphi(s,\gamma_{n}(s))\,ds-\frac{\varphi(A_{n})+\varphi(A_{n}^{\prime})}{2}\cdot t_{n}\right|+\left|\frac{\varphi(A_{n}^{\prime})-\varphi(A_{0})}{2}\cdot t_{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\penalty\ \int_{0}^{t_{n}}\left|\frac{\varphi(s,\gamma_{n}(s))-\varphi(A_{n})}{2}\right|+\left|\frac{\varphi(s,\gamma_{n}(s))-\varphi(A_{n}^{\prime})}{2}\right|ds+\left|\frac{\varphi(A_{n}^{\prime})-\varphi(A_{0})}{2}\cdot t_{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\penalty\ \|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}t_{n}^{2}+o(\sqrt{x_{n}^{\prime}})t_{n},

where in the last inequality we used that
\varphi
is Lipschitz along the trajectory passing through

and
A_{n}^{\prime}
for the first two terms and the little-Hölder regularity of
\varphi
in the

Recalling that we are assuming

as

, we deduce

\bigg|x_{n}-\frac{\varphi(A_{n})+\varphi(A_{0})}{2}\cdot t_{n}-x^{\prime}_{n}\bigg|=o(x^{\prime}_{n})\qquad\mbox{as }n\to+\infty,

and since we assumed

, then
x_{n}-\frac{\varphi(A_{n})+\varphi(A_{0})}{2}\cdot t_{n}>0
for

sufficiently large.
Therefore we can estimate

|B(n)|^{2}\leq\bigg|x_{n}-\frac{\varphi(A_{n})+\varphi(A_{0})}{2}\cdot t_{n}-x^{\prime}_{n}\bigg|=o(x^{\prime}_{n})\qquad\mbox{as }n\to+\infty

and this proves the claim (

).

Case 2
: there is
\delta\in(0,1)
such that
\frac{t_{n}}{\sqrt{x^{\prime}_{n}}}\geq\delta>0
for any

sufficiently large.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and consider

S_{n}^{\varepsilon}=\{(s,x)\in[0,t_{n}]\times\mathbb{R}:\gamma_{n}(s)-\varepsilon t_{n}^{2}\leq x\leq\gamma_{n}(s)+\varepsilon t_{n}^{2}\}.

Denote by
\mathtt{c}
the
\frac{1}{2}-
Hölder constant of
\varphi
in a ball
B
centered at
A_{0}
. For
n
sufficiently large

: by estimates like (

)-(

) we get

(5.7)

|\varphi(A_{n})-\varphi(A_{0})-g(A_{0})t_{n}|\leq 4\mathtt{c}\sqrt{\varepsilon}t_{n}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon t_{n}^{2}}\int_{S_{n}^{\varepsilon}}|g-g(A_{0})|.

Just for notational convenience, suppose
\varrho_{0}=1
. Since
S_{n}^{\varepsilon}\subset S_{1}\left(\gamma_{n},t_{n}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}+\varepsilon},0\right)\in\mathcal{V}_{1}
and
A_{0}
is a Lebesgue point of
g
with respect to the covering
\mathcal{V}_{1}
, then

\eta_{n}\doteq\frac{1}{\mathscr{L}^{2}\left(S_{1}\left(\gamma_{n},t_{n}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}+\varepsilon},0\right)\right)}\int_{S_{n}^{\varepsilon}}|g-g(A_{0})|

vanishes as
n\to\infty
.
The measure of the set is
4\left({\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}+\varepsilon}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}t_{n}^{3}
.
It follows from (
5.7
) that

\begin{split}\frac{|\varphi(A_{n})-\varphi(A_{0})-g(A_{0})t_{n}|}{t_{n}}\leq&\penalty\ 4\mathtt{c}\sqrt{\varepsilon}+\frac{\mathscr{L}^{2}\left(S_{1}\left(\gamma_{n},t_{n}\sqrt{\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}+\varepsilon},0\right)\right)}{2\varepsilon t_{n}^{3}}\eta_{n}\\
=&\penalty\ 4\mathtt{c}\sqrt{\varepsilon}+\frac{2\left(\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}+\varepsilon\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\varepsilon}\eta_{n}.\end{split}

Recalling that
t_{n}\leq d_{\varphi}(A_{n},A_{0})
, the claim follows by letting
n\to\infty
since
\varepsilon
is arbitrarily small.

∎

Appendix A
Nonlinear functions of a given order

We specify the assumption of finite order nonlinearity, for brevity also called
\ell
-nonlinearity.

Definition A.1
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval.
A function
f
differentiable at
v
is “nonlinear of order
\ell>1
with constant
c>0
at
v
” if there exists
\delta>0
such that for every
v+h\in I\cap B_{\delta}(v)
one has

\left|f(v+h)-f(v)-f^{\prime}(v)h\right|\geq c\left|h\right|^{\ell}\,.

If the inequality holds for all
v,v+h\in I
we call
f
“nonlinear in
I
of order
\ell
with constant
c
”.

Of course, if
f
is nonlinear of order
\ell>1
with constant
c>0
at
v
then it is also nonlinear at
v
of every order
\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell
with any constant
0<c^{\prime}
just because

c|h|^{\ell}=c^{\prime}\cdot\left(\frac{c}{c^{\prime}|h|^{\ell^{\prime}-\ell}}\right)\cdot|h|^{\ell^{\prime}}>c^{\prime}|h|^{\ell^{\prime}}\qquad\text{for $|h|^{\ell^{\prime}-\ell}\leq\frac{c}{c^{\prime}}$.}

Remark A.2
.

The best order of nonlinearity at a point
v
is the order
\ell
of the first non-vanishing derivative at the point, higher than the first.
It can be proved just applying the definition of differentiability
\ell
-times recursively, and the constant is arbitrarily close to
\frac{1}{\ell!}|f^{(\ell)}(v)|
.

Lemma A.3
.

Suppose
f\in C^{\ell}(I)
. If
\sum_{j=2}^{\ell}\frac{1}{j!}\left|f^{(j)}\right|\geq c
in the interval
I
; then
f
is nonlinear at any
v\in I
of order
\ell
with constant
c^{\prime}
, for any
0<c^{\prime}<c
.

Let
k>1
be the minimum exponent of nonlinearity of
f
at
v
with
c_{k}
the supremum of the relative constants: then
f^{(k)}(v)=k!c_{k}
and
f^{(j)}(v)=0
for
j=2,\dots,k-1
.

Finally, if
f^{\prime\prime}(v)=0
and
k
is the minimum exponent of nonlinearity of
f
at
v
, then

(A.1)

\liminf_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{\min\{f^{\prime\prime}(x)\ :\ 2\delta\leq|x-v|\leq 3\delta\}}{\max\{f^{\prime\prime}(x)\ :\ \delta\leq|x-v|\leq 4\delta\}}\geq\frac{1}{2^{k-2}}\,.

Proof.

Let
v,v+h\in I
.
Suppose
f^{(j)}(v)=0
for
j=2,\dots,k-1
and
f^{(k)}(v)\neq 0
for some
2\leq k\leq\ell
.
Just by Taylor’s expansion

\left|f(v+h)-f(v)-f^{\prime}(v)h\right|=\left|\sum_{j=2}^{k-1}\frac{1}{j!}f^{(j)}(v)h^{j}+\frac{1}{k!}f^{(k)}(\xi_{h})h^{k}\right|=\left|\frac{f^{(k)}(\xi_{h})}{k!}\right|\cdot|h|^{k}\,,

for some
\xi_{h}
between
v
and
v+h
.
When
k=\ell
, by assumption
\frac{1}{\ell!}\left|f^{(\ell)}(v)\right|\geq c
and thus
f
is nonlinear at
v
of order
\ell
with constant arbitrarily close to
c
, thanks to the continuity of
f^{(\ell)}
.
If
2\leq k<\ell
then by the same argument
f
is nonlinear of order
k
with some positive constant, and thus nonlinear of order
\ell
for any positive constant.

Suppose now that
k
is the minimum exponent of nonlinearity of
f
at
v
and denote by
c_{0}
the supremum of the relative constants: by the first part of the statement in particular
f^{(j)}(v)=0
for
j=2,\dots,k-1
and
|f^{(k)}(v)|\leq c_{0}k!
, otherwise there would be a nonlinearity constant bigger than
c_{0}
. We now prove
|f^{(k)}(v)|\geq c_{0}k!
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by Definition
A
of nonlinear function there is
\delta_{n}
decreasing to
0
such that

\left|f(v+h)-f(v)-f^{\prime}(v)h\right|\geq(c_{0}-\nicefrac{{1}}{{n}})|h|^{\ell}\qquad\forall|h|\leq\delta_{n}\,.

Let
\xi_{h}
between
v
and
v+h
such that
f(v+h)-f(v)-f^{\prime}(v)h=\frac{f^{(k)}}{k!}(\xi_{h})
: thus
|f^{(k)}(\xi_{h})|\geq(c_{0}-\nicefrac{{1}}{{n}})k!
when
|h|\leq\delta_{n}
. Since
\xi_{h}\to v
as
n\to+\infty
, by continuity we conclude
|f^{(k)}(v)|\geq c_{0}k!
.

Consider now
\overline{x}=v+{\overline{h}}
attaining the minimum of
f^{\prime\prime}
in the interval
[2\delta,3\delta]
for
\delta
small, then

|f^{\prime\prime}(\overline{x})|=\left|f^{\prime\prime}(v+{\overline{h}})-f^{\prime\prime}(v)\right|=\left|\frac{f^{(k)}(\xi_{{\overline{h}}})}{(k-2)!}{\overline{h}}^{k-2}\right|\geq\frac{\left|f^{(k)}(\xi_{{\overline{h}}})\right|}{(k-2)!}(2\delta)^{{}^{k-2}}\,.

Consider now
\widehat{x}=v+{\widehat{h}}
attaining the maximum of
f^{\prime\prime}
in the interval
[\delta,4\delta]
for
\delta
small, then

|f^{\prime\prime}(\widehat{x})|=\left|f^{\prime\prime}\left(v+{\widehat{h}}\right)-f^{\prime\prime}(v)\right|=\left|\frac{f^{(k)}({\widehat{h}})}{(k-2)!}{\widehat{h}}^{k-2}\right|\leq\frac{\left|f^{(k)}(\xi_{{\widehat{h}}})\right|}{(k-2)!}(4\delta)^{{}^{k-2}}\,.

In particular, since
f^{(k)}(\xi_{{\widehat{h}}})
and
f^{(k)}(\xi_{{\overline{h}}})
converge to the same value
f^{(k)}(v)\neq 0
as
\delta\downarrow 0
,

\liminf_{\delta\downarrow 0}\frac{\min\{f^{\prime\prime}(x)\ :\ 2\delta\leq|x-v|\leq 3\delta\}}{\max\{f^{\prime\prime}(x)\ :\ \delta\leq|x-v|\leq 4\delta\}}\geq\frac{1}{2^{k-2}}\,.

∎

Nonlinearity provides a lower bound on the increments also of the first derivative of
f
:

Lemma A.4
.

Suppose
f
is nonlinear in an interval
I
of order
\ell
with constant
c
. Then

(A.2)

\left|f^{\prime}(v)-f^{\prime}(w)\right|\geq 2c|v-w|^{\ell-1}\qquad\forall v,w\in I\,.

Proof.

Just by Definition (
A
) of nonlinearity when
h=w-v
we have

\left|f^{\prime}(w)h-f^{\prime}(v)h\right|=\left|f(v+h)-f(v)-f^{\prime}(v)h+f(w-h)-f(w)+f^{\prime}(w)h\right|\geq 2c|h|^{\ell}\,.\qed

Lemma A.5
.

If
f\in C^{2}(J)
is nonlinear of order
\ell
and constant
c
in a compact interval
J
, then:

(1)

When
\ell=2
the second derivative of
f
has lower bound
2c
in

and it holds

(A.3)

{|f^{\prime}(v)-f^{\prime}(w)|}{}\geq q_{w,v}\cdot\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(J)}\cdot|v-w|\,,

for all

q_{w,v}\doteq\frac{2c}{\|f^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(J)}}
belonging in
(0,1]
and independent on
w,v
.

(2)

When
\ell>2
the set
Z=\{v\in J:f^{\prime\prime}(v)=0\}
is finite and inequality (
A.3
) holds whenever
[w,v]\cap Z=\emptyset
with
q_{w,v}\doteq\frac{\min_{[w,v]}|f^{\prime\prime}|}{\max_{J}|f^{\prime\prime}|}
belonging in
(0,1]
.

Proof.

(1)

When
\ell=2
the statement follows immediately by (
A.2
).

(2)

At any accumulation point
\overline{v}
of
D
necessarily
f^{(j)}(\bar{v})=0
for every

Explicit formulas for Eigenvalues of Capelli operators for the Lie superalgebra 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 1 | 2 ​ n ) \mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
Dene Lepine

Abstract
. We define a natural basis for the algebra of 𝔤 ​ 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 1 | 2 ​ n ) \mathfrak{gosp}(1|2n) -invariant differential operators on the affine superspace ℂ 1 | 2 ​ n \mathbb{C}^{1|2n} . We prove that these operators lie in the image of the centre of the enveloping algebra of 𝔤 ​ 𝔬 ​ 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 1 | 2 ​ n ) \mathfrak{gosp}(1|2n) . Using this result, we compute explicit formulas for the eigenvalues of these operators on irreducible summands of 𝒫 ⁡ ( ℂ 1 | 2 ​ n ) \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{C}^{1|2n}) . This settles the Capelli eigenvalue problem for orthosymplectic Lie superalgebras in the cases that were not addressed in [ SSS20 ] and [ SSS21 ] . Our main technique relies on an explicit calculation of a certain determinant with polynomial entries.

Introduction

This paper grew out of the results of my Masters Thesis at the University of Ottawa under the supervision of Professor Hadi Salmasian. The author would like thank Professor Siddhartha Sahi for his suggestions on an earlier draft of this article, which improved the presentation of Theorem
2.6
. He also thanks Jérémy Champagne for the useful discussions that eventually lead to the idea for Lemma
3.16
.

The Capelli identity, discovered by Alfredo Capelli
[
Cap87
]
, is an equality between a differential operator on the space of
n\times n
matrices, and a non-commutative determinant whose entries are natural generators of the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{gl}(n)
. This identity played an integral role in Hermann Weyl’s proof of the FFT in invariant theory
[
Wey39
]
. Howe and Umeda’s seminal work
[
HU91
]
transcends this identity from the viewpoint of multiplicity-free actions and formulates the
abstract Capelli problem
, see Problem
2.1
. In
[
Sah94
]
, Sahi introduced a natural basis (the
Capelli operators
) for the algebra of invariant polynomial coefficient differential operators on a multiplicity-free module of a reductive Lie algebra, and a multi-parameter family of polynomials whose special values yield the eigenvalues of these operators. The Capelli operators of
[
Sah94
]
include the Capelli element as a special case. The
Capelli eigenvalue problem
is the problem of describing the eigenvalues of this natural basis, see Problem
2.5
. Other examples of Capelli operators were explored by Konstant, Sahi, and Wallach, see
[
KS91
]
and
[
Wal92
]
.

The solution to the Capelli eigenvalue problem was followed by a further investigation of
interpolation Jack polynomials
by Sahi, Knop-Sahi, and around the same time by Okounkov-Olshanski
[
Sah94
]
,
[
KS96
]
,
[
OO97
]
. In the past few years, the Capelli eigenvalue problem has emerged again in the setting of Lie superalgebras, and nearly completely solved for multiplicity-free actions that arise from the Tits-Kantor-Koecher construction for Jordan superalgebras
[
ASS18
]
,
[
SS16
]
,
[
SSS20
]
,
[
SSS21
]
. However, for a small number of cases the uniform methods used in the aforementioned works did not apply. In particular, for the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{gosp}(m,2n):=\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathfrak{osp}(m,2n)
acting on its natural representation
\mathbb{C}^{m|2n}
, which corresponds to the Jordan superalgebra
(m-1,2n)_{+}
in Kac’s notation
[
Kac77
]
, the problem was solved for all cases in
[
SSS20
]
and
[
SSS21
]
, except for when
m=1
and
n>0
. The reason why the case
m=1
is different is that the highest weights of the irreducible components in the decomposition of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{C}^{m|2n})
into
\mathfrak{gosp}(m|2n)
-modules look different from the generic case: they are of the form
\delta_{1}+\dotsb+\delta_{i}
, unlike the generic case which has highest weights of the form
k\delta_{1}
. Furthermore when
m=1
, the number of irreducible components in
\mathcal{S}^{d}(\mathbb{C}^{1|2n})
does not tend to infinity. Therefore, a priori it is not clear how to parametrize irreducible components suitably by partitions to obtain a polynomial formula for the eigenvalues of the Capelli operators. Another issue is that the general argument of
[
SSS20
]
for surjectivity of the map from the centre of the enveloping algebra
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{gosp}(1|2n))
onto the algebra of invariant differential operators fails. This surjectivity is a crucial step in the proofs of
[
SSS20
]
.

In this paper, we circumvent the above issues and give solutions to both the Capelli eigenvalue problem and the abstract Capelli problem for the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
, see Theorem
2.6
and Theorem
2.7
respectively. The two new ideas that arise are a proper indexing of the irreducible components by partitions and an explicit formula for the determinant of a matrix whose entries are the eigenvalues of the operators
C^{i}E^{j}
, where
C
is the Casimir operator and
E
is the Euler operator.

2.
Main Results

Let
V:=\mathbb{C}^{1|2n}
be the super vector space over
\mathbb{C}
of dimension
1|2n
. Equip
V
with the non-degenerate even supersymmetric bilinear form
B\colon V\times V\to\mathbb{C}
given by the
(1|n+n)
-block matrix

\displaystyle\mathfrak{J}_{1|2n}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&0&I_{n}\\
0&-I_{n}&0\end{bmatrix}.

Denote by
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
the orthosymplectic Lie superalgebra that leaves
B
invariant. Let
\mathfrak{h}
be the standard Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
with basis
\mathscr{B}=\{E_{i+1,i+1}-E_{i+n+1,i+n+1}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, where the
E_{i,j}
denote the usual matrix units. Let
\{\delta_{1},\dotsc,\delta_{n}\}
be the basis of
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
dual to
\mathscr{B}
. Let
\mathfrak{b}
be the Borel subalgebra of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
corresponding to the fundamental system

\displaystyle\Sigma:=\{\delta_{i}-\delta_{i+1}\}_{i=1}^{n-1}\cup\{\delta_{n}\}.

Set

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{gosp}(1|2n):=\mathbb{C}Z\oplus\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n),

where
Z
is a central element of
\mathfrak{g}
. We can extend the natural
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
-module structure on
V
to
\mathfrak{g}
by defining the action of
Z
to be
-I_{V}
.

Let
\mathcal{P}(V)
be the superalgebra of (super)polynomials on
V
. We denote the generators of
V
dual to the standard basis of
V
by
\{y,x_{1},\dotsc,x_{2n}\}
, where
y
is even and
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{2n}
are odd. Define the (super)derivations
\{\partial_{y},\partial_{1},\dotsc,\partial_{2n}\}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq 2n
and homogeneous
u,v\in\mathcal{P}(V)
by the relations

\displaystyle\partial_{y}(y)

\displaystyle:=1,

\displaystyle\partial_{y}(x_{j}):=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{y}(uv)

\displaystyle:=\partial_{y}(u)v+u\partial_{y}(v),

\displaystyle\partial_{i}(y)

\displaystyle:=0,

\displaystyle\partial_{i}(x_{j}):=\delta_{i,j},

\displaystyle\partial_{i}(uv)

\displaystyle:=\partial_{i}(u)v+(-1)^{|u|}u\partial_{i}(v).

Let
\mathcal{D}(V)
be the superalgebra of constant-coefficient (super)differential operators generated by
\{\partial_{y},\partial_{1},\dotsc,\partial_{2n}\}
and let
\mathcal{PD}(V)
be the superalgebra of (super)polynomial coefficient (super)differential operators. We can consider
\mathcal{PD}(V)
as the subalgebra of
\End(\mathcal{P}(V))
generated by
y,x_{1},\dotsc,x_{2n},\partial_{y},\partial_{1},\dotsc,\partial_{2n}
. As a vector space we have
\mathcal{PD}(V)\cong\mathcal{P}(V)\otimes\mathcal{D}(V)
and we use
\mathcal{PD}^{k}(V)
to denote the image of
\mathcal{P}^{k}(V)\otimes\mathcal{D}^{k}(V)
in
\mathcal{PD}(V)
, where
\mathcal{P}^{k}(V)
and
\mathcal{D}^{k}(V)
are the homogenous degree
k
components. For more details on our notation see
[
SS16
, Sec. 2]
.

The
\mathfrak{g}
-module structure on
V
induces a
\mathfrak{g}
-module structure on
\mathcal{P}(V)
. Explicitly, the action of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
is given by the restricted action from
\mathfrak{gl}(1|2n)
to
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
, where the action of
\mathfrak{gl}(1|2n)
on
\mathcal{P}(V)
is defined for all
1\leq i,j\leq 2n
by

\displaystyle E_{1,1}

\displaystyle\mapsto y\partial_{y},

\displaystyle E_{1,j+1}

\displaystyle\mapsto y\partial_{j},

\displaystyle E_{j+1,1}

\displaystyle\mapsto x_{j}\partial_{y},

\displaystyle E_{i+1,j+1}

\displaystyle\mapsto x_{i}\partial_{j}.

Furthermore,
Z
acts on
\mathcal{P}(V)
by the
degree operator
. That is,

\displaystyle Z\mapsto E:=y\partial_{y}+\sum_{i=1}^{2n}x_{i}\partial_{i}.

Since
V
is self-dual, then
\mathcal{D}(V)\cong\mathcal{P}(V^{*})\cong\mathcal{P}(V)
. Thus, the action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathcal{P}(V)
induces an action on
\mathcal{D}(V)
and hence on
\mathcal{PD}(V)\cong\mathcal{P}(V)\otimes\mathcal{D}(V)
. Observe that the action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathcal{P}(V)
induces a map

\displaystyle\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathcal{PD}(V),

where
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})
denotes the universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. An element
D\in\mathcal{PD}(V)
is
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant if and only if
Dx=(-1)^{|D||x|}xD
, for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. Denote by
\mathcal{PD}(V)^{\mathfrak{g}}
the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant polynomial coefficient differential operators. Notice that the image of the centre of
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})
, denoted by
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g})
, lies in
\mathcal{PD}(V)^{\mathfrak{g}}
. That is, we have a map

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathcal{PD}(V)^{\mathfrak{g}}.

Problem 2.1

(Abstract Capelli Problem for
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
)
.

Determine whether or not the map (
2.1
) is surjective.

To put Problem
2.1
in perspective, recall that in the non-super setting, this problem was investigated in the work of Howe and Umeda
[
HU91
]
. In
[
SSS20
]
this problem was studied in the general setting of multiplicity-free modules of basic classical Lie superalgebras that arise from the super Tits-Kantor-Koecher construction. More precisely, in
[
SSS20
, Corollary 1.18]
the authors addressed Problem
2.1
for pairs
(\mathfrak{g},V)
associated to a simple unital Jordan superalgebra
J
such that
J_{1}\neq\{0\}
and
J
is not of type
(0,2n)_{+}
or
JP(0,n)
, in the Kac notation. The answer turns out to be affirmative for all such pairs
(\mathfrak{g},V)
, except when
V
is a
\mathfrak{gosp}(2|4)
-module constructed from the exceptional Jordan superalgebra of type
F
. The proof technique of
[
SSS20
]
uses the connection between the image of the Harish-Chandra homomorphism and Sergeev-Veselov interpolation polynomials. This technique is not available when
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
and
V=\mathbb{C}^{1|2n}
, corresponding to
J=(0,2n)_{+}
.

Our first main result of this paper is an affirmative answer to Problem
2.1
(see Theorem
2.7
). This resolves the abstract Capelli problem for pairs
(\mathfrak{g},V)
associated to the Jordarn superalgebra
(0,2n)_{+}
which were left out in
[
SSS20
]
.

To explain our second main result, we begin by constructing the Capelli basis of
\mathcal{PD}(V)^{\mathfrak{g}}
. Let us define two operators
R^{2}
and
\nabla^{2}
on
\mathcal{P}(V)
as follows:
R^{2}
is the operator of left multiplication by
y^{2}-2\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}x_{i+n}x_{i}
and
\nabla^{2}
is the constant coefficient differential operator
\partial_{y}-2\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}\partial_{i+n}\partial_{i}.
We call
\nabla^{2}
the
super Laplace operator
. These operators admit the following relations

\displaystyle\left[(1/2)R^{2},-(1/2)\nabla^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=E+\frac{1-2n}{2},

\displaystyle\left[E+\frac{1-2n}{2},(1/2)R^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=R^{2},

\displaystyle\left[E+\frac{1-2n}{2},-(1/2)\nabla^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\nabla^{2}.

That is, the elements
\left\{E+\frac{1-2n}{2},(1/2)R^{2},-(1/2)\nabla^{2}\right\}
form a standard
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-triple. Furthermore, one can check directly that the actions of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
commute, so that
\mathcal{P}(V)
is an
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)\times\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-module. There is a natural grading of
\mathcal{P}(V)
by degree and we have
Ep=kp
for
p\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(V)
. Furthermore, define
\mathcal{H}=\ker(\nabla^{2})
and
\mathcal{H}_{k}=\mathcal{P}^{k}(V)\cap\mathcal{H}
. The elements of
\mathcal{H}
are called
harmonic superpolynomials
. The next Lemma is a special case of
[
Cou13
, Th. 5.2]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
0\leq k\leq 2n+1
, the space
\mathcal{H}_{k}
is an irreducible
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
-module of highest weight

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\min(2n-k+1,k)}\delta_{j}.

Using Lemma
2.2
and the fact that multiplication by
R^{2}
commutes with the
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
action it follows that
R^{2\ell}\mathcal{H}_{k}
is an irreducible
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
-module of highest weight
\lambda_{k}
, for all
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. For the following Lemma see
[
Cou13
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.3

(Fischer Decomposition)
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\ell=\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor
, the decomposition of
\mathcal{P}(V)
as an
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
-module is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{k}(V)

\displaystyle\simeq\begin{cases}\mathcal{H}_{k}\oplus R^{2}\mathcal{H}_{k-2}\oplus\dotsb\oplus R^{2\ell}\mathcal{H}_{k-2\ell}&\textrm{ for }k\leq 2n+1\textrm{ and }\\
R^{2}\mathcal{P}^{k-2}(V)&\textrm{ for }k>2n+1.\end{cases}

Thus as a
\mathfrak{g}
-module, the space
\mathcal{P}(V)
admits a multiplicity-free decomposition. Define

\mathbb{P}:=\{(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})\colon\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},~\nu_{1}\geq\nu_{2}\},

and for any
\nu\in\mathbb{P}
set
|\nu|:=\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}
. We will parametrize the irreducible submodules of
\mathcal{P}^{k}(V)
by the set

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{k,n}^{*}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\nu\in\mathbb{P}\colon|\nu|=k\textrm{ and }\nu_{2}\geq\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor-n\right\},

via the assignment
\nu\mapsto V_{\nu}:=R^{2\nu_{2}}\mathcal{H}_{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}},
for
\nu\in\mathbb{P}_{k,n}^{*}
. Then we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{k}(V)=\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathbb{P}_{k,n}^{*}}V_{\nu}.

Let
\mathcal{D}^{k}(V)\otimes\mathcal{P}^{k}(V)\to\mathbb{C}
be the canonical non-degenerate pairing between
\mathcal{D}^{k}(V)
and
\mathcal{P}^{k}(V)
. This pairing induces a
\mathfrak{g}
-module isomorphism
\mathcal{D}^{k}(V)\simeq\mathcal{P}^{k}(V)^{*}.
That is,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{k}(V)\simeq\bigoplus_{\nu\in\mathbb{P}_{k,n}^{*}}V_{\nu}^{*}.

We remark that the latter isomorphism can be chosen such that for every
\nu\in\mathbb{P}_{k,n}^{*}
, the restriction of the natural map
\mathcal{D}^{k}(V)\otimes\mathcal{P}^{k}(V)\to\mathbb{C}
to
V_{\nu}^{*}\otimes V_{\nu}
is the standard duality pairing. Define

(2.5)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{d,n}^{*}:=\bigcup_{k=0}^{d}\mathbb{P}_{k,n}^{*}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\Lambda_{n}^{*}:=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}\mathbb{P}_{k,n}^{*}.

Using (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{PD}(V)^{\mathfrak{g}}\simeq\bigoplus_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{n}^{*}}(V_{\nu}\otimes V_{\nu^{\prime}}^{*})^{\mathfrak{g}}\simeq\bigoplus_{\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{n}^{*}}\Hom_{\mathfrak{g}}(V_{\nu^{\prime}},V_{\nu})\simeq\bigoplus_{\nu\in\Lambda_{n}^{*}}\Hom_{\mathfrak{g}}(V_{\nu},V_{\nu}).

Definition 2.4

(Capelli Operator)
.

For
\nu\in\Lambda_{n}^{*}
, define
D_{\nu}\in\mathcal{PD}(V)^{\mathfrak{g}}
to be the element mapped to
I_{\nu}\in\Hom_{\mathfrak{g}}(V_{\nu},V_{\nu})
by (
2.6
). The operator
D_{\nu}
is called a
Capelli Operator
.

Indeed, Schur’s Lemma implies
\dim\Hom_{\mathfrak{g}}(V_{\nu},V_{\nu})=1
and therefore the family
\{D_{\nu}\}_{\nu\in\Lambda_{n}^{*}}
forms a basis of
\mathcal{PD}(V)^{\mathfrak{g}}
. Schur’s Lemma also implies that for any
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda_{n}^{*}
the Capelli operator
D_{\mu}
acts on
V_{\nu}
by a scalar. This gives rise to the following problem called the
Capelli Eigenvalue problem
for
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
:

Problem 2.5

(Capelli Eigenvalue Problem for
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
)
.

Let
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda_{n}^{*}
. Determine the scalar
c_{\nu}(\mu)
by which
D_{\nu}
acts on
V_{\mu}
.

Our answer to Problem
2.5
makes use of a special case of
Knop-Sahi polynomials
. These polynomials are uniquely defined by certain vanishing conditions. In particular, we let
\rho:=(-n-\frac{1}{2},0)
and let
P_{\nu}^{\rho}
, for
\nu\in\mathbb{P}
, be the polynomial defined in
[
KS96
]
that satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

P_{\nu}^{\rho}
is symmetric in two variables
x
and
y
,

(2)

\deg(P^{\rho}_{\nu})\leq|\nu|
,

(3)

P_{\nu}^{\rho}(\mu+\rho)=0
for all
\mu\in\mathbb{P}
with
|\mu|\leq|\nu|
and
\mu\neq\nu
,

(4)

P_{\nu}^{\rho}(\nu+\rho)=(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})!(\nu_{2}!)(\nu_{1}-(n+\frac{1}{2}))^{\underline{\nu_{2}}}
,

where
x^{\underline{i}}=x(x-1)\dotsb(x-i+1)
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda^{*}_{n}
. Then
c_{\nu}(\mu)=\frac{P_{\nu}^{\rho}(\mu_{1}-n-\frac{1}{2},\mu_{2})}{(\nu_{1}-\nu_{2})!(\nu_{2}!)(\nu_{1}-(n+\frac{1}{2}))^{\underline{\nu_{2}}}}
. In particular, the explicit formula is given by
c_{\nu}(\mu)=ab
, where

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{2}^{\underline{\nu_{2}}}(\mu_{1}-(n+\frac{1}{2}))^{\underline{\nu_{2}}}}{(\nu_{2}!)(\nu_{1}-(n+\frac{1}{2}))^{\underline{\nu_{2}}}(n+\frac{1}{2})^{\underline{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}}}}

and

\displaystyle b=\sum_{i=0}^{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}}{n+\frac{1}{2}\choose\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}-i}{n+\frac{1}{2}\choose i}(\mu_{1}-\nu_{2}-i)^{\underline{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}-i}}(\mu_{2}-\nu_{2})^{\underline{i}}.

Even though the formula obtained for eigenvalues is typical of Capelli operators, i.e. Knop-Sahi polynomials for a specific
\rho
, we note that the standard technique of verifying the vanishing conditions (used in
[
SSS20
]
) does not apply. The reason is that for
\nu
such that
|\nu|>2n+1
, there are not enough vanishing conditions to allow us to use the uniqueness of the Knop-Sahi polynomials. However, we circumvent this issue by reducing the formula for
D_{\nu}
to one for another operator, for which there are enough vanishing conditions.

The proof of Theorem
2.6
needs Theorem
2.7
below, which answers the abstract Capelli problem (Problem
2.1
) for the action of
\mathfrak{gosp}(1|2n)
on
\mathbb{C}^{1|2n}
affirmatively. Define

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{k,n}

\displaystyle:=\{\mu\in\mathbb{P}\colon|\mu|=k\textrm{ and }\mu_{2}\leq n\}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\Lambda_{d,n}:=\bigcup_{k=0}^{d}\mathbb{P}_{k,n}.

Let
C
denote the Casimir operator of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2n)
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
\nu\in\Lambda_{d,n}^{*}
. The Capelli operator
D_{\nu}
can be expressed as a linear combination of elements from the image of
B_{d,n}
under the map (
2.1
), where

(2.8)

\displaystyle B_{d,n}:=\{C^{\mu_{2}}Z^{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}\colon\mu\in\Lambda_{d,n}\}.

We remark that Theorem
2.7
cannot be proven by the general technique of
[
SSS20
]
.

3.
The matrices
M_{d}
and
M_{d}^{\prime}
.

The strategy to prove Theorem
2.7
is as follows. We define a family of square matrices denoted by
M_{d}
, for
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, with coefficients in the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[x]
. For all
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
the matrix
M_{d}
will have the following property: if
\det(M_{d})(n)\neq 0
then
D_{\nu}
, for all
\nu\in\Lambda_{d,n}^{*}
, can be written as a linear combination of images of elements of
\emph{B}_{d,n}
.

We equip
\Lambda_{d,n}
, from (
2.7
), and
\Lambda_{d,n}^{*}
, from (
2.5
), with the following total orderings.

Definition 3.1
.

For
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{d,n}
, define

\displaystyle\mu\preceq\mu^{\prime}\textrm{ if and only if }\mu_{2}<\mu_{2}^{\prime}\textrm{ or }(\mu_{2}=\mu_{2}^{\prime}\textrm{ and }\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{1}^{\prime})

and for
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{d,n}^{*}
define

\displaystyle\nu\leqslant\nu^{\prime}\textrm{ if and only if }\left\lfloor\frac{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}}{2}\right\rfloor<\left\lfloor\frac{\nu_{1}^{\prime}-\nu_{2}^{\prime}}{2}\right\rfloor\textrm{ or }\left(\left\lfloor\frac{\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}}{2}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{\nu_{1}^{\prime}-\nu_{2}^{\prime}}{2}\right\rfloor\textrm{ and }|\nu|\leq|\nu^{\prime}|\right).

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we have
|\Lambda_{d,n}|=|\Lambda_{d,n}^{*}|.

Proof.

This follows from the definition of
\Lambda_{d,n}^{*}
and
\Lambda_{d,n}
and the observation that for all

,
|\mathbb{P}_{k,n}|=\min\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor,n\right)+1=|\mathbb{P}_{k,n}^{*}|.

Remark 3.3
.

In particular, Lemma
3.2
implies
|B_{d,n}|=|\{D_{\nu}\colon\nu\in\Lambda_{d,n}^{*}\}|
.

For
0\leq j\leq\min\left(\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor,n\right)
define a family of subsets of
\Lambda_{d,n}
and
\Lambda_{d,n}^{*}
by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{d,n,j}

\displaystyle:=\{\mu\in\Lambda_{d,n}\colon\mu_{2}\geq j\},

\displaystyle\Lambda_{d,n,j}^{*}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\nu\in\Lambda_{d,n}^{*}\colon\nu_{2}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{|\nu|}{2}\right\rfloor-j\right\},

and

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

. Then
\sum_{s=0}^{2n-2}f(d,s)=\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda_{d,n}}\mu_{2}.

First, we have the identity

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\min\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor,n\right)\left(\min\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor,n\right)+1\right),

\displaystyle\bigcup_{s=0}^{2n-2}\{(\nu,\nu^{\prime})\in\mathbb{P}_{k,n}^{*}\times\mathbb{P}_{k,n}^{*}\colon\ell_{\nu^{\prime}}<\ell_{\nu},~\ell_{\nu}+\ell_{\nu^{\prime}}-1=s\}=\{(\nu,\nu^{\prime})\in\mathbb{P}_{k,n}^{*}\times\mathbb{P}_{k,n}^{*}\colon\ell_{\nu^{\prime}}<\ell_{\nu}\}

\min\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor,n\right)+1
possible

. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{2n-2}f(d,s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{2n-2}\sum_{k=0}^{d}g_{k}(s)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}\sum_{s=0}^{2n-2}g_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{d}\frac{1}{2}\min\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor,n\right)\left(\min\left(\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor,n\right)+1\right).

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda_{d,n}}\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu_{1}=0}^{\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{\mu_{2}=0}^{\mu_{1}}\mu_{2}+\sum_{\mu_{1}=\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+1}^{d}\sum_{\mu_{2}=0}^{d-\mu_{1}}\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu_{1}=0}^{\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor}\frac{\mu_{1}(\mu_{1}+1)}{2}+\sum_{\mu_{1}=\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+1}^{d}\frac{(d-\mu_{1})(d-\mu_{1}+1)}{2}=\sum_{\mu_{1}=0}^{d}\frac{\left\lfloor\frac{\mu_{1}}{2}\right\rfloor(\left\lfloor\frac{\mu_{1}}{2}\right\rfloor+1)}{2}.

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda_{d,n}}\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu_{1}=0}^{n}\sum_{\mu_{2}=0}^{\mu_{1}}\mu_{2}+\sum_{\mu_{1}=n+1}^{d-n-1}\sum_{\mu_{2}=0}^{n}\mu_{2}+\sum_{\mu_{1}=d-n}^{d}\sum_{\mu_{2}=0}^{d-\mu_{1}}\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu_{1}=0}^{2n+1}\frac{\left\lfloor\frac{\mu_{1}}{2}\right\rfloor\left(\left\lfloor\frac{\mu_{1}}{2}\right\rfloor+1\right)}{2}+(d-2n-1)\frac{n(n+1)}{2}.

\sum_{s=0}^{2n-2}f(d,s)=\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda_{d,n}}\mu_{2}.

divides
p(x)
, for all
s=0,\dotsc,2n-2
. Therefore there is some monic
q(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
such that

\displaystyle p(x)

\displaystyle=q(x)\prod_{s=0}^{2n-2}\left(x-\frac{s}{2}\right)^{f(d,s)}.

In particular,
\deg(p(x))\geq\sum_{s=0}^{2n-2}f(d,s).
But Remark
3.9
tells us that
\deg(p(x))=\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda_{d,n}}\mu_{2}
. It then follows from Lemma
4.6
that

\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda_{d,n}}\mu_{2}=\sum_{s=0}^{2n-2}f(d,s)\leq\deg(p(x))=\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda_{d,n}}\mu_{2}.

That is,
q(x)=1
and

\displaystyle\det(M_{d})

\displaystyle=\det(M_{d}^{\prime})p(x)=\det(M_{d}^{\prime})\prod_{s=0}^{2n-2}\left(x-\frac{s}{2}\right)^{f(d,s)}.\qed

Thus we can prove Theorem
2.7
and thereby answer Problem
2.1
in the affirmative.

Proof of Theorem
2.7
.
Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Recall that the image of
C^{\mu_{2}}Z^{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}
is a differential operator of order
|\mu|
and therefore is an element of
\bigoplus_{k=0}^{|\mu|}\mathcal{PD}^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{1|2n})^{\mathfrak{g}}
. Lemma
3.2
implies
|B_{d,n}|=|\{D_{\nu}\colon|\nu|\leq d\}|
. Therefore if
B_{d,n}
is a linearly independent set then it is a basis of
\bigoplus_{k=0}^{d}\mathcal{PD}^{k}(\mathbb{C}^{1|2n})^{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Identify
C^{\mu_{2}}Z^{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}
with its image and fix non-zero
v_{\nu}\in V_{\nu}
, for each
\nu\in\Lambda_{d,n}^{*}
. Let
a_{\mu}\in\mathbb{C}
be such that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda_{d,n}}a_{\mu}C^{\mu_{2}}Z^{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}=0.

We evaluate (
4.4
) on each
v_{\nu}
. Since
C^{\mu_{2}}Z^{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}
acts on
v_{\nu}
by
\lambda_{\mu,\nu}(n)
we have the following matrix relation

\displaystyle[a_{\mu}]_{\mu\in\Lambda_{d,n}}[\lambda_{\mu,\nu}(n)]_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\Lambda_{d,n}\\
\nu\in\Lambda_{d,n}^{*}\end{subarray}}=0,

where
[a_{\mu}]_{\mu\in\Lambda_{d,n}}
denotes a row vector. The matrix
[\lambda_{\mu,\nu}(n)]_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\Lambda_{d,n}\\
\nu\in\Lambda_{d,n}^{*}\end{subarray}}
is exactly
M_{d}
with entries evaluated at
x=n
. Theorem
4.7
implies that
\det(M_{d})(n)\neq 0
and therefore
[\lambda_{\mu,\nu}(n)]_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu\in\Lambda_{d,n}\\
\nu\in\Lambda_{d,n}^{*}\end{subarray}}
is invertible. In particular,
a_{\mu}=0
, for all
\mu\in\Lambda_{d,n}
and hence
B_{d,n}
is linearly independent.∎

5.
Solution of the Capelli Eigenvalue Problem

In the previous section we proved Theorem
2.7
and thereby answered Problem
2.1
. For the remainder of this article we will answer Problem
2.5
by proving Theorem
2.6
. We will need the following lemma, which can be found in
[
DBES09
, Lemma 1]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
w\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(V)
. Then

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}(R^{2t}w)

\displaystyle=2t(2k+1-2n+2(t-1))R^{2(t-1)}w+R^{2t}\nabla^{2}w.

Proof.

By induction on
t
, using
[\nabla^{2},R^{2}]=4E+2(1-2n)
we have

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}(R^{2}w)

\displaystyle=(4k+2(1-2n))w+R^{2}\nabla^{2}w.\qed

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}^{*}
such that
\lambda_{2}\geq 1
and set
\nu=(\lambda_{1}-1,\lambda_{2}-1)
. The Capelli operators
D_{\lambda}
and
D_{\nu}
are related by

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{2}(2\lambda_{1}-2n-1)D_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=R^{2}D_{\nu}\nabla^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\{R^{2\nu_{2}}v_{1},\dotsc,R^{2\nu_{2}}v_{\ell}\}
, with
v_{i}
harmonic, be a basis to
V_{\nu}
and let
\{v_{1}^{*},\dotsc,v_{\ell}^{*}\}
be its dual basis. It follows that
D_{\nu}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(R^{2\nu_{2}}v_{i}\right)v_{i}^{*}.
Furthermore,
\{R^{2\lambda_{2}}v_{1},\dotsc,R^{2\lambda_{2}}v_{\ell}\}
is a basis to
V_{\lambda}
. Notice that Lemma
5.1
implies that for all
1\leq i,j\leq\ell
we have

\displaystyle v_{i}^{*}\nabla^{2}(R^{2\lambda_{2}}v_{j})

\displaystyle=v_{i}^{*}\left(2\lambda_{2}(2\lambda_{1}-2n-1)R^{2\nu_{2}}v_{j}+R^{2\lambda_{2}}\nabla^{2}v_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=2\lambda_{2}(2\lambda_{1}-2n-1)\delta_{i,j}.

Thus
\left\{\frac{v_{i}^{*}\nabla^{2}}{2\lambda_{2}(2\lambda_{2}-2n-1)}\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell\right\}
is dual to
\{R^{2\lambda_{2}}v_{1},\dotsc,R^{2\lambda_{2}}v_{\ell}\}
and

\displaystyle D_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{(R^{2\lambda_{2}}v_{i})(v_{i}^{*}\nabla^{2})}{2\lambda_{2}(2\lambda_{1}-2n-1)}=\frac{R^{2}D_{\nu}\nabla^{2}}{2\lambda_{2}(2\lambda_{1}-2n-1)}.\qed

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda_{n}^{*}
be such that
\lambda_{2},\mu_{2}\geq 1
. Set
\nu=(\lambda_{1}-1,\lambda_{2}-1)
and
\eta=(\mu_{1}-1,\mu_{2}-1)
. Then

\displaystyle c_{\lambda}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{2}(\mu_{1}-(n+\frac{1}{2}))}{\lambda_{1}(\lambda_{1}-(n+\frac{1}{2}))}c_{\nu}(\eta).

Proof.

Take any
R^{2\mu_{2}}w\in V_{\mu}
, with
w
harmonic, then using Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
we have

\displaystyle c_{\lambda}(\mu)R^{2\mu_{2}}w

\displaystyle=D_{\lambda}(R^{2\mu_{2}}w)=\frac{R^{2}D_{\nu}\nabla^{2}}{2\lambda_{2}(2\lambda_{1}-2n-1)}\left(R^{2\mu_{2}}w\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{2}(2\mu_{1}-2n-1)}{\lambda_{2}(2\lambda_{1}-2n-1)}R^{2}D_{\nu}R^{2\eta_{2}}w=\frac{\mu_{2}(\mu_{1}-(n+\frac{1}{2}))}{\lambda_{2}(\lambda_{1}-(n+\frac{1}{2}))}c_{\nu}(\eta)R^{2\mu_{2}}w.\qed

Indeed, applying Proposition
5.3
recursively relates the eigenvalues of
D_{\lambda}
to those of
D_{\nu}
, where
V_{\nu}
is harmonic. That is, this reduces Problem
2.5
to calculating the eigenvalues of
D_{\nu}
, for
\nu
such that
V_{\nu}
harmonic.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^{*}_{n}
. Let
\ell=\min(\lambda_{2},\mu_{2})
and set
\nu=(\lambda_{1}-\ell,\lambda_{2}-\ell)
and
\eta=(\mu_{1}-\ell,\mu_{2}-\ell)
. Then

(5.1)

\displaystyle c_{\lambda}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{2}^{\underline{\lambda_{2}}}(\mu_{1}-(n+\frac{1}{2}))^{\underline{\lambda_{2}}}}{\lambda_{2}!(\lambda_{1}-(n+\frac{1}{2}))^{\underline{\lambda_{2}}}}c_{\nu}(\eta).

Proof.

If
\mu_{2}<\lambda_{2}
, then the right hand side of Equation (
5.1
) is
0
. Furthermore, Lemma
5.2
can be applied
\lambda_{2}
times to write
d_{\lambda,\mu}D_{\lambda}=R^{2\lambda_{2}}D_{\nu}\nabla^{2\lambda_{2}},
for some non-zero
d_{\lambda,\mu}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus for all
R^{2\mu_{2}}w\in V_{\mu}
, with
w
harmonic, we have the following

\displaystyle c_{\lambda}(\mu)R^{2\mu_{2}}w

\displaystyle=D_{\lambda}R^{2\mu_{2}}w

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{\lambda,\mu}}R^{2\lambda_{2}}D_{\nu}\nabla^{2\lambda_{2}}R^{2\mu_{2}}w=0,

where the last equality follows by applying Lemma
5.1

\mu_{2}+1
times. Thus for
\mu_{2}<\lambda_{2}
we have Equation (
5.1
).

If
\lambda_{2}\leq\mu_{2}
, then Lemma
5.3
can be applied
\ell
-times to get

\displaystyle c_{\lambda}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\lambda_{2}}\frac{(\mu_{2}-i+1)(2(\mu_{1}-i)+1-2n)}{(\lambda_{2}-i+1)(2(\lambda_{1}-i)+1-2n)}\right)c_{\nu}(\eta).\qed

Thus we focus on computing the eigenvalues of
D_{\nu}
, for
V_{\nu}
harmonic. The set of irreducible harmonic submodules of
\mathcal{P}(V)
are indexed by

\Lambda_{n,\mathcal{H}}^{*}:=\{(i,0)\mid 0\leq i\leq 2n+1\}.

The following lemma will allow us to relate the eigenvalues of
D_{\nu}
, for
\nu\in\Lambda_{n,\mathcal{H}}^{*}

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\nu\in\Lambda_{n,\mathcal{H}}^{*}
and
\mu\in\Lambda_{n}^{*}
. Then
c_{\nu}(\mu)
is a polynomial in
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
such that

(1)

c_{\nu}(\mu)
is symmetric in
x=\mu_{1}-\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)
and
y=\mu_{2}
,

(2)

\deg(c_{\nu}(\mu))\leq|\nu|,
and

(3)

c_{\nu}(\mu)=\delta_{\nu,\mu}
for all
|\mu|\leq|\nu|
.

Proof.

Identify
C
and
Z
with their images under the map in Equation (
2.1
). Theorem
2.7
lets us write
D_{\nu}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{|\nu|,n}}a_{\lambda}C^{\lambda_{2}}Z^{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}
for some
a_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{C}
. Observe that the action of
C
and
Z
on
V_{\mu}
are given by scalar multiplication by
(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(2n+1+\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})
and
\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}
, respectively. Indeed, the action of
D_{\nu}
on
V_{\mu}
is given by

\displaystyle c_{\nu}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{|\nu|,n}}a_{\lambda}(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})^{\lambda_{2}}(2n+1+\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})^{\lambda_{2}}(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})^{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}

(5.2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{|\nu|,n}}a_{\lambda}\left(x-y+n+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\lambda_{2}}\left(y-x+n+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\lambda_{2}}\left(x+y-n-\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}},

where
x=\mu_{1}-n-\frac{1}{2}
and
y=\mu_{2}
. Condition (1) follows from Equation (
5
). Furthermore, every summand of (
5
) has degree
|\lambda|
. This yields Condition (2). For Condition (3), the argument is similar to
[
SS16
, Lemma 5.4]
the point being
\bigcup_{k=0}^{2n+1}\mathbb{P}_{k,n}^{*}=\{\mu\in\mathbb{P}\colon|\mu|\leq 2n+1\}
. Indeed, if
|\mu|<|\nu|
then the order of
D_{\nu}
is
|\nu|
and the degree of
V_{\mu}
is
|\mu|
. That is,
c_{\nu}(\mu)=0
. If
|\nu|=|\mu|
, then
D_{\nu}
results in a
\mathfrak{g}
-module homomorphism
V_{\mu}\to V_{\nu}
which can be non-zero only if
\mu=\nu
.
∎

The next proposition which follows from

Proposition 5.6
.

Let

\displaystyle P_{\nu}^{\rho}

\displaystyle={n+\frac{1}{2}\choose\nu_{1}}^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^{\nu_{1}}{n+\frac{1}{2}\choose\nu_{1}-i}{n+\frac{1}{2}\choose i}\left(x+n+\frac{1}{2}-i\right)^{\underline{\nu_{1}-i}}\left(y\right)^{\underline{i}}.

Lemma

Proposition 5.7

Let
\nu\in\Lambda_{n,\mathcal{H}}^{*}
and

\displaystyle c_{\nu}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n+\frac{1}{2})^{\underline{\nu_{1}}}}\sum_{i=0}^{\nu_{1}}{n+\frac{1}{2}\choose\nu_{1}-i}{n+\frac{1}{2}\choose i}\left(\mu_{1}-i\right)^{\underline{\nu_{1}-i}}\left(\mu_{2}\right)^{\underline{i}}.

The proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem

Notice that

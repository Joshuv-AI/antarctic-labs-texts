Generalized Calogero-Moser system and supergroup gauge origami
Taro Kimura, Norton Lee

Abstract
We study the integrability and the Bethe/Gauge correspondence of the Generalized Calogero-Moser system proposed by Berntson, Langmann and Lenells [ 1 ] which we call the elliptic quadruple Calogero-Moser system (eqCM). We write down the Dunkl operators which give commuting Hamiltonians of the quantum integrable system. We identify the gauge theory in correspondence is a supergroup version of the gauge origami, from which we construct the transfer matrix of the eqCM system.

Introduction

The intrinsic connection between the 4d
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric gauge theories and algebraic integrable system has been an intense interest and fruitful research ground since the groundbreaking work of Seiberg and Witten through the identification between the Seiberg-Witten curve of the supersymmetric gauge theory and the spectral curve of the algebraic integrable system
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
Nekrasov and Shatashvili promoted the relation to between the quantum integrable system and 4d
\mathcal{N}=2
symmetric gauge theory subject to
\Omega
-deformation. The
\Omega
-deformation allows localization computation of the supersymmetric partition function and other protected observables. This was afterwards named as
Bethe/Gauge correspondence
.
In general, there can be multiple deformation parameters
\{{\varepsilon}_{a}\}
in the localization colputation based on the dimensionality of the supersymmetric gauge theory considered.
It is proposed that the
\Omega
-deformation parameters
\{{\varepsilon}_{a}\}
can be regarded in general as Planck constants when quantizing the classical integrable system.
In the Nekrasov-Shatashvili limit where one of the
\Omega
-deformation parameter is turned off, an effective
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
supersymmetry is restored on the corresponding two dimensional subspace.
Simultaneously, the Bethe ansatz equation (BAE) of the quantum integrable system is recovered from the saddle point equation of the supersymmetric gauge theory partition function.
In this relationship the effective twisted superpotential of the
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
gauge theory corresponds to the Yang-Yang functional of the quantum integrable system. In this regard, the stationary state of the quantum integrable systems are the vacuua of the effective
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
theory.
The quantum Hamiltonians are identified with the generator of the chiral rings
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
. Well-known examples include
\mathcal{N}=2
SYM/Toda lattice
[
10
]
,
\mathcal{N}=2
SQCD/XXX spin chain-Gaudin model
[
11
,
8
,
12
]
, and
\mathcal{N}=2^{*}
/Calogero-Moser system
[
13
,
14
]
.

Recent studies show that on top of the
\Omega
-deformation and taking the appropriate limit
[
15
]
, a co-dimensional two full type surface defect needs to be introduced through appropriate orbifolding procedures
[
16
,
17
]
to fully understand the relation between the conserving quantum Hamiltonian and (twisted) chiral ring operator
[
18
,
11
]
.
Introduction of such full type surface defect generates the chainsaw quiver gauge theory on the two dimensional surface.
For a
G
-gauge theory, the quiver gauge theory is equipped with a set of gauge couplings
(\mathfrak{q}_{i})_{i=1,\dots{\rm rk}G}
, which would be identified as the canonical coordinates in the quantum integrable system.
This allows us to construct the quantum conserved Hamiltonians from gauge theory
[
19
]
, and the further identification between instanton partition function and quantum wave function
[
18
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
.

Supergroup and superalgebra

The supergroup and underlying superalgebra is well known to be an extension of Lie group and Lie algebra by including Grassmannian generators.
In the study of supersymmetric quantum field theory, the supergroup often encodes both the bosonic and fermionic degrees of freedom of the global spacetime symmetry.
However, quantum field theory with supergroup as its gauge group is inevitably non-unitary due to the violation of spin-statistics theorem, leaving these class of theories to be often over-looked and unexplored in the literature.
The recent advance in computational techniques brings back the interests of studying these exotic theories.
Most notably, the explicit constructions of supergroup gauge theory via D-branes and so-called negative/ghost D-branes were given in
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
(See
[
27
]
also for earlier work), moreover the partition functions for supergroup gauge instantons have been computed in
[
28
,
29
]
using equivariant localization.

Algebraic integrable system such as Calogero-Moser system can be defined with any given Lie algebra (see
[
30
]
for a good introduction).
The supermatrix provide natural generalization of the Lax-formalism of integrable system to superalgebraic integrable system
[
31
]
, including super Toda lattice
[
32
]
, superspin chain
[
33
,
34
]
, and superanalog of Calogero-Moser system
[
35
,
36
,
37
]
.

It is natural to investigate if the well-established Bethe/Gauge correspondence extends to their corresponding super version.
At the classical level,
we can naturally generalize the Lax operators/matrices of ordinary integrable systems to construct the so-called super-Lax operators which take values on the supermatrices.
The super analog of the Calogero-Moser (CM) system is the double Calogero–Moser (dCM) system with both trigonometric version (trigonometric dCM; tdCM)
[
38
,
37
]
and elliptic version (elliptic dCM; edCM)
[
36
,
39
]
.
Integrability, which is again defined by the existence of many commuting conserved Hamiltonians, of the dCM system was proved in
[
38
]
for the trigonometric, and in
[
19
]
for the elliptic cases.
There are two gauge theories in correspondence to the edCM system. One is 4d
\mathcal{N}=2^{*}
supergroup gauge theory
[
40
]
. The other one is the
folded instanton

[
19
,
18
]
based on the gauge origami
[
41
,
42
]
.

In this paper we study the integrability and Bethe/Gauge correspondence of a generalization of the edCM system, which we will call the elliptic quadruple Calogero-Moser system (eqCM), proposed in
[
1
]
. It is a 1+1 dimensional system of arbitrary number of four kinds of particles. Our goal is to prove its integrability by explicit construct its Dunkl operator, and establish its Bethe/Gauge correspondence with supergroup folded instanton.

Quadruple Calogero-Moser system

The elliptic Calogero-Moser (eCM) system is defined for any finite-dimenaional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
by the Hamiltonian
[
44
]
:

\displaystyle{\rm H}_{\mathfrak{g}}=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}^{+}(\mathfrak{g})}g_{\alpha}(g_{\alpha}-1)(\alpha,\alpha)\wp(\alpha\cdot\mathrm{x})

(1)

where
\mathcal{R}^{+}(\mathfrak{g})
is the set of all positive roots of Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
,
\Delta_{\mathfrak{g}}
is the Laplace operator defined on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
The Weierstrass
\wp
-function
\wp(z;2\ell,2\mathrm{i}\delta)=\wp(z)
has half-period
(\ell,\mathrm{i}\delta)
. See also (
95
).
g_{\alpha}
is the potential coupling that depends on the length of the fundamental root
\alpha
.
The eCM system defined based on the Lie algebra
A_{N-1}
, which is a one-dimensional quantum mechanics system of
N
particles with the Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle{\rm H}_{N;g}(\mathrm{x})=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\mathrm{x}_{j}^{2}}+g(g-1)\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq N}\wp(\mathrm{x}_{j}-\mathrm{x}_{k}),

(2)

is closely related to four dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2^{*}

SU(N)
gauge theory.

The algebraic integrable system can be defined on the Lie superalgebra. Let
I=I_{\bar{0}}\amalg I_{\bar{1}}
be the union of "even" indices
I_{\bar{0}}=\{1,\dots,N\}
and "odd" indices
I_{\bar{1}}=\{\bar{1},\dots,\bar{M}\}
.
Let
V=\mathbb{C}^{N|M}
and
e_{1},\dots,e_{N},{e}_{\bar{1}},\dots,{e}_{\bar{M}}
be its basis such that each vector’s parity be aligned with its index.
Let
e^{*}_{1},\dots,e^{*}_{N},e^{*}_{\bar{1}},\dots,e^{*}_{\bar{M}}
be the basis of the dual space
V^{*}
. Then the root system of the Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}(N|M)
can be described as follows:
\mathcal{R}=\amalg_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}=\pm}\mathcal{R}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
where

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{R}_{++}=\{e^{*}_{i}-e^{*}_{j}|\ i,j\in I_{\bar{0}}\},\quad\mathcal{R}_{+-}=\{e^{*}_{i}-e^{*}_{j}|\ i\in I_{\bar{0}},\ j\in I_{\bar{1}}\},\\
&\mathcal{R}_{-+}=\{e^{*}_{i}-e^{*}_{j}|\ i\in I_{\bar{1}},\ j\in I_{\bar{0}},\},\quad\mathcal{R}_{--}=\{e^{*}_{i}-e^{*}_{j}|\ i,j\in I_{\bar{1}}\}.\end{split}

(3)

On the dual space
V^{*}
, define inner product that depends on a parameter
g
by

\displaystyle(v^{*},w^{*})_{g}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}v^{*}(e_{i})w^{*}(e_{i})-g\sum_{j=1}^{M}v^{*}(e_{\bar{j}})w^{*}(e_{\bar{j}}).

(4)

The elliptic Calogero-Moser system associated with the root system of the superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}(N|M)
, called elliptic double Calogero-Moser (edCM) system, is defined by:

\displaystyle{\rm H}_{N,M;g}(\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y})={\rm H}_{N;g}(\mathrm{x})-g{\rm H}_{M;1/g}(\mathrm{y})+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{M}(1-g)\wp(\mathrm{x}_{j}-{\mathrm{y}}_{k}).

(5)

The integrability of the edCM system was proven in
[
19
]
. It is related to two gauge theories: the four dimensional
\mathcal{N}=2^{*}

SU(N|M)
supergroup gauge theory
[
40
]
and folded instanton
[
19
,
18
]
.

Bernston, Langmann and Lenells proposed a generalization of the edCM system in
[
1
]
.
The generalized eCM model, which we call elliptic quadruple Calogero-Moser system (eqCM) here, is a one-dimensional quantum mechanics system of
L=N_{+}+N_{-}+M_{+}+M_{-}
particles governed by the Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\rm H}_{N_{+},M_{+},N_{-},M_{-};g}(\mathrm{x}^{+},\mathrm{y}^{+},\mathrm{x}^{-},{\mathrm{y}}^{-};g)\\
&={\rm H}_{N_{+},M_{+};g}(\mathrm{x}^{+},{\mathrm{y}}^{+})+{\rm H}_{N_{-},M_{-};g}(\mathrm{x}^{-},{\mathrm{y}}^{-})\\
&\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{+}}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{-}}g(g-1)\wp(\mathrm{x}_{j}^{+}-\mathrm{x}_{k}^{-}+\mathrm{i}\delta)+\sum_{j=1}^{M_{+}}\sum_{k=1}^{M_{-}}\left(1-\frac{1}{g}\right)\wp({\mathrm{y}}^{+}_{j}-{\mathrm{x}}_{k}^{-}+\mathrm{i}\delta)\\
&\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{+}}\sum_{k=1}^{M_{-}}(1-g)\wp(\mathrm{x}^{+}_{j}-{\mathrm{y}}^{-}_{k}+\mathrm{i}\delta)+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{-}}\sum_{k=1}^{M_{+}}(1-g)\wp({\mathrm{y}}^{+}_{j}-\mathrm{x}^{-}_{k}+\mathrm{i}\delta).\end{split}

(6)

The eqCM (
6
) describes a 1+1 dimensional system with four kinds particles interact through two-body interaction based on their physical distance
|x|
.
The particles can be labeled
(m_{j},c_{j})=(1,+)
,
(1,-)
,
(1/g,+)
, and
(1/g,-)
.
The label
m_{j}
characterizes the mass of the particles. The two types of particles of different mass corresponds to electrons and holes respectively in the condense matter system
[
45
]
.
The label
c_{j}=\pm
can be realized as chirality of the particles, corresponding to the left and right moving particles.
The particles of the same chirality interact through the potential proportional to
\wp(x)
, while the particles of opposite chirality interact through potential proportional to
\wp(x+\mathrm{i}\delta)
. The former is singular when
x\to 0
and is repulsive, while the later is non-singular in
x\to 0
and is attractive

\lim_{x\to 0}\wp(x)=\frac{(\pi/\delta)^{2}}{\sinh^{2}\frac{\pi x}{\delta}},\quad\lim_{x\to 0}\wp(x+\mathrm{i}\delta)=-\frac{(\pi/\delta)^{2}}{\cosh^{2}\frac{\pi x}{\delta}}.

(7)

2.1
Integrability

Before we dive into the gauge theory construction of the eqCM system, we would like to investigate its integrability discussed in
[
1
]
.
It turns out that the integrability of the eqCM system solely depends on the integrability of the edCM. The proof is straightforward: By shifting
\mathrm{y}^{\pm}\to\mathrm{y}^{\pm}+\mathrm{i}\delta
the eqCM becomes an edCM.
The integrability of the edCM has been proved in
[
19
]
. Hence, the eqCM system is integrable.

We construct the Dunkl operator for the eqCM system by considering a family of functions

\displaystyle\sigma_{t}(x)=\frac{\theta_{11}(x)\theta^{\prime}_{11}(0)}{\theta_{11}(x)\theta_{11}(-t)},\quad t\in\mathbb{C}/(\mathbb{Z}\oplus\tau\mathbb{Z})

(8)

where
\tau
is a modular parameter and
\theta_{11}
is the theta function (Recall that we have identify complex gauge coupling with the elliptic modulus at the end of previous chapter).
The function
\sigma_{t}(x)
has the following properties

\displaystyle\sigma_{t}(x+2\pi i)=\sigma_{t}(x),

(9a)

\displaystyle\sigma_{t}(x)=-\sigma_{-t}(-x),

(9b)

\displaystyle\sigma_{t}(x)=-\sigma_{x}(t),

(9c)

\displaystyle\sigma_{t}(x)\sigma_{-t}(x)=\wp(x)-\wp(t),

(9d)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{d}{dx}\sigma_{t}(x)=-\wp(x)-2\zeta\left(\frac{1}{2}\right).

(9e)

For the later convenience, we will collectively denote the coordinates by
\mathrm{z}_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,L=N_{1}+N_{2}+M_{1}+M_{2}
.

\displaystyle\mathrm{z}_{j}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{x}_{j}^{+}&j=1,\dots,N_{+},\\
\mathrm{x}_{j-N_{+}}^{-}-\mathrm{i}\delta&j=N_{1}+1,\dots,N_{+}+N_{-},\\
\mathrm{y}_{j-N_{+}-N_{-}}^{+}&j=N_{+}+N_{-}+1,\dots,N_{+}+N_{-}+M_{+},\\
\mathrm{y}_{j-N_{+}-N_{-}-M_{+}}^{-}-\mathrm{i}\delta&j=N_{+}+N_{-}+M_{+}+1,\dots,N_{+}+N_{-}+M_{+}+M_{-}\end{cases}

(10)

We define the parity function

\displaystyle\mathtt{p}(j)=\begin{cases}0&j=1,\dots,N_{+}+N_{-}\\
1&j=N_{+}+N_{-}+1,\dots,L\end{cases}

(11)

such that the partricles’ chiralities are given by
c_{j}=(-1)^{\mathtt{p}(j)}
.
Let
t_{j}\in\mathbb{C}/(\mathbb{Z}\oplus\tau\mathbb{Z})
(
j=1,\dots,L
) be
L
complex numbers. The elliptic Dunkl operator is defined by

\displaystyle D_{j}=g^{\mathtt{p}(j)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{z}_{j}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{L}g^{1-\mathtt{p}(k)}\sigma_{t_{j}-t_{k}}(\mathrm{z}_{j}-\mathrm{z}_{k})S_{jk}

(12)

Here
S_{jk}=S_{kj}
are permutation operators acting on
\{\mathrm{z}_{j}\}
by
\mathrm{z}_{j}S_{jk}=S_{jk}\mathrm{z}_{k}
.
Here we show the Dunkl operator defined in (
12
) are pairwise commuting:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left[D_{j},D_{k}\right]&=\left[g^{\mathtt{p}(j)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{z}_{j}},\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq k\end{subarray}}^{L}g^{1-\mathtt{p}(l)}\sigma_{t_{k}-t_{l}}(\mathrm{z}_{k}-\mathrm{z}_{l})S_{kl}\right]+\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq j\end{subarray}}^{L}g^{1-\mathtt{p}(l)}\sigma_{t_{j}-t_{l}}(\mathrm{z}_{j}-\mathrm{z}_{l})S_{jl},g^{\mathtt{p}(k)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{z}_{k}}\right]\\
&=\left[g^{\mathtt{p}(j)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{z}_{j}},g^{1-\mathtt{p}(j)}\sigma_{t_{k}-t_{j}}(\mathrm{z}_{k}-\mathrm{z}_{j})S_{kj}\right]+\left[g^{1-\mathtt{p}(k)}\sigma_{t_{j}-t_{k}}(\mathrm{z}_{j}-\mathrm{z}_{k})S_{jk},g^{\mathtt{p}(k)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{z}_{k}}\right]\\
&=\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{z}_{j}}\sigma_{t_{k}-t_{j}}(\mathrm{z}_{k}-\mathrm{z}_{j})S_{kj}-\sigma_{t_{k}-t_{j}}(\mathrm{z}_{k}-\mathrm{z}_{j})\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{z}_{k}}S_{kj}\\
&\qquad+\sigma_{t_{j}-t_{k}}(\mathrm{z}_{j}-\mathrm{z}_{k})\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{z}_{j}}S_{jk}-\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{z}_{k}}\sigma_{t_{j}-t_{k}}(\mathrm{z}_{j}-\mathrm{z}_{k})S_{jk}\\
&=\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{z}_{j}},\sigma_{t_{k}-t_{j}}(\mathrm{z}_{k}-\mathrm{z}_{j})\right]S_{jk}+\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{z}_{k}},\sigma_{t_{k}-t_{j}}(\mathrm{z}_{k}-\mathrm{z}_{j})\right]S_{jk}=0.\end{split}

(13)

The conserved charges are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(r)}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}g^{-\mathtt{p}(j)}(D_{j})^{r}.

(14)

In the classical integrable system, the conserving Hamiltonians are obtained by the (super)trace of the
L\times L
Lax matrix. Eq. (
14
) is the quantum uplift and generalization of the classical conserved charge. The "Lax matrix" is a diagonal
L\times L
matrix with diagonal components
D_{j}
. The trace is deformed in the same manner by inner product (
4
).

Since individual Dunkl operators
D_{j}
are pairwise commuting, it is obvious that
\mathcal{L}^{(r)}
are also pairwise commuting

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{L}^{(r)},\mathcal{L}^{(s)}\right]=0,\quad\forall\;r,s=1,\dots,L.

(15)

In particular, to recover the eqCM Hamiltonian, we consider

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{(2)}=&\sum_{j=1}^{L}g^{-\mathtt{p}(j)}(D_{j})^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{N_{1}+N_{2}}(D_{j})^{2}+\sum_{j=N_{1}+N_{2}+1}^{L}\frac{1}{g}(D_{j})^{2}\\
=&\sum_{j=1}^{N_{1}+N_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\mathrm{z}_{j}^{2}}+g\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{N_{1}+N_{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{z}_{j}}\sigma_{t_{j}-t_{k}}(\mathrm{z}_{j}-\mathrm{z}_{k})+g^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{N_{1}+N_{2}}\sigma_{t_{j}-t_{k}}(\mathrm{z}_{j}-\mathrm{z}_{k})\sigma_{t_{k}-t_{j}}(\mathrm{z}_{k}-\mathrm{z}_{j})\\
&\qquad\qquad+\sum_{k=N_{1}+N_{2}+1}^{L}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{z}_{j}}\sigma_{t_{j}-t_{k}}(\mathrm{z}_{j}-\mathrm{z}_{k})+\sum_{k=N_{1}+N_{2}+1}^{L}\sigma_{t_{j}-t_{k}}(\mathrm{z}_{j}-\mathrm{z}_{k})\sigma_{t_{k}-t_{j}}(\mathrm{z}_{k}-\mathrm{z}_{j})\\
&+\sum_{j=N_{1}+N_{2}+1}^{L}\frac{g^{2}}{g}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\mathrm{z}_{j}^{2}}+\frac{g^{2}}{g}\sum_{{k=1}}^{N_{1}+N_{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{z}_{j}}\sigma_{t_{j}-t_{k}}(\mathrm{z}_{j}-\mathrm{z}_{k})+\frac{g^{2}}{g}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{1}+N_{2}}\sigma_{t_{j}-t_{k}}(\mathrm{z}_{j}-\mathrm{z}_{k})\sigma_{t_{k}-t_{j}}(\mathrm{z}_{k}-\mathrm{z}_{j})\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{g}{g}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=N_{1}+N_{2}+1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{L}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathrm{z}_{j}}\sigma_{t_{j}-t_{k}}(\mathrm{z}_{j}-\mathrm{z}_{k})+\frac{1}{g}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=N_{1}+N_{2}+1\\
k\neq j\end{subarray}}^{L}\sigma_{t_{j}-t_{k}}(\mathrm{z}_{j}-\mathrm{z}_{k})\sigma_{t_{k}-t_{j}}(\mathrm{z}_{k}-\mathrm{z}_{j})\\
\end{split}

(16)

In the limit that all
t_{j}=t
identical, we use the fourth and fifth property of
\sigma_{t}(x)
in (
9
) to obtain the eqCM Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\left.\mathcal{L}^{(2)}\right|_{t_{j}=t}={\rm H}_{N_{+},M_{+},N_{-},M_{-}}(\mathrm{x}^{+},\mathrm{y}^{+},\mathrm{x}^{-},\mathrm{y}^{-};-g).

(17)

We hence prove the integrability of the eqCM system.

3.1
Supergroup from D-branes

The D-brane acts as the boundary on the string world sheet. In the presence of multiple D-branes, a Chan-Paton factor, which takes a value in the vector space
\mathbb{C}^{N}
in the case of
N
D-branes, is assigned to each of the string to identify which D-brane it ends on. These Chan-Paton factors, though non-dynamical on the world sheet, give rise to the
N
labels that generate the
U(N)
gauge group in the target space.
The Dirac quantization condition restricts
N
to be integer. It does not, however, rule out the possibility of negative
N
.
A negative Chan-Paton factor can be realized by introducing additional minus sign to certain subset of boundary states, i.e. the Chan-Paton factor now takes a value in a graded vector space
\mathbb{C}^{N_{+}|N_{-}}
with the mixed signatures:

\begin{pmatrix}+\mathbf{1}_{N_{+}}&0\\
0&-\mathbf{1}_{N_{-}}\end{pmatrix}.

(18)

The additional vector space with negative signature corresponds to the additional
N_{-}

negative branes
which carry an additional negative sign with respect to the boundary states, hence the Chan-Paton factors corresponding to the usual
positive branes
. This distinguishes them from the more commonly encountered anti-branes which only carry an negative sign for the RR sector, and preserve the opposite space-time supersymmetries. A system consisting of both positive and negative branes can preserve the same space time supersymmetries. Moreover due to the negative sign in their NS-NS sector, a negative brane carries negative tension and anti-gravitates, this feature again differs from the anti-brane which gravitates.

Let us consider the scenario of
N_{+}
positive and
N_{-}
negative D3 branes place in parallel. A string that connects between one positive brane and one negative brane have the opposite statistics comparing to a string having both ends on positive brane. This is caused by the additional minus sign on the negative brane.
An open string with both ends on the negative brane will have usual statistics since the minus signs from both ends cancel each other.
This enhances
U(N_{+})\times U(N_{-})
quiver gauge group to a
U(N_{+}|N_{-})
supergroup,

\displaystyle U=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in U(N_{+}|N_{-}),\quad U^{-1}=U^{\dagger}.

(19)

Here
A
and
D
are
N_{+}\times N_{+}
and
N_{-}\times N_{-}
even graded matrix with complex number entries, while
B
and
C
are
N_{+}\times N_{-}
and
N_{-}\times N_{+}
odd graded matrix whose entries are Grassmannian.
An odd graded matrix means its entries obey anti-commutation relation instead of commutation relation,

\displaystyle B_{ij}C_{kl}=(-1)C_{kl}B_{ij}.

(20)

3.2
Instanton moduli space of supergroup gauge origami

Here we recall the supergroup instanton moduli space studied in
[
28
]
and extend it to the gauge origami.
We apply the supergroup D-brane construction to the gauge origami, which is inspired by type IIB string theory
[
41
,
19
]
.
Let us start with four complex planes
\mathbb{C}^{4}
with coordinates
\mathbf{z}_{a}
,
a=1,2,3,4
, and consider picking out two of them such that the six possible copies
\mathbb{C}^{2}_{A}\subset\mathbb{C}^{4}
are labeled by two digit indices,

\displaystyle A\in\{(12),(13),(14),(23),(24),(34)\}=\underline{6}.

(21)

We also define

\displaystyle\underline{4}=\{1,2,3,4\}.

(22)

Define the complement of
A
by
\bar{A}=\underline{4}\backslash A
, for instance if
A=(12)
,
\bar{A}=(34)
.
The complementary two complex planes transverse to
\mathbb{C}^{2}_{A}
is thus denoted by

(94)

where

. The elliptic modulus of the Weierstrass

3.

In the case

. The eqCM system proposed in

One further shifts

4.5
Trigonometric limit

The trigonometric limit of both eCM and edCM are obtained in the
\delta\to\infty
limit

\lim_{\delta\to\infty}\wp(z)=\frac{1}{\sin^{2}z},

which we denote as trigonometric Calogero-Moser (tCM) and trigonometric double Calogero-Moser (tdCM) respetively.
In the context of gauge theory, this corresponds to the weak coupling limit (
95
).

The eqCM system with all particles sharing the same chirality is no different of an edCM system. The trigonometric limit of eqCM Hamiltonian
H_{2}(\theta_{1}=\theta_{3}^{-1}=g)
(
94
) of a single chirality is the tdCM of
L=N_{+}+N_{-}+M_{+}+M_{-}
particles.
In the case of eqCM system consisting particles of different chirality, the potential term between particles with different chirality vanishes in the trigonometric limit:

\lim_{\delta\to\infty}\wp(z+\mathrm{i}\delta)=0.

The operator
H_{2}(\theta_{1}=\theta_{3}^{-1}=g)
is reduced to a sum of two commuting tdCM Hamiltonians.
Instead, one can consider the trigonometric limit of the Hamiltonian
H_{2}(\theta_{1},\theta_{3})
in (
94

\theta_{1},\theta_{3}

5
Summary and Discussion

We have proven the integrability of eqCM system by explicitly constructing the corresponding Dunkl operator.
We have then established the Bethe/Gauge correspondence of the eqCM system with supergroup folded instanton.
The supergroups must be deliberately broken to have non-vanishing interaction between particles with opposite chirality. By introducing the full-type co-dimensional two defect, we reconstruct the quantum Hamiltonian of the eqCM system from supergroup folded instanton through its
qq
-character.

Let us end with discussing a few loose ends in this note and commenting on a few future directions.

From this point of view, it is natural to ask what is the root system corresponding to the eqCM system defined in

The eigenfunction of the tqCM system, which is the defect folded instanton partition function, would be a double-graded generalization of Jack polynomial under proper Higgsing condition.

A.1
Superalgebra

A superalgebra is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded algebra. It is an algebra over a commutative ring or field
\mathbb{K}
with decomposition into even and odd elements and a multiplication operator that respects the grading. A superalgebra over
\mathbb{K}
is defined by direct sum decomposition

\displaystyle A=A_{0}\oplus A_{1}

(100)

with a bilinear multiplication
A\times A\to A
such that

\displaystyle A_{i}\times A_{j}\subseteq A_{i+j}.

(101)

A parity is assigned to every element
x\in A
, denoted as
|x|
, is either
0
or
1
depending on whether
x
is in
A_{0}
or
A_{1}
. We define supercommutator by

\displaystyle[x,y]=xy-(-1)^{|x||y|}yx.

(102)

A superalgebra
A
is said to be supercommutative if

\displaystyle[x,y]=0\quad\forall x,y\in A.

(103)

A.2
Supermatrix

A supermatrix is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded analog of ordinary matrix. It is a
2\times 2
block matrix with entries in superalgebra
R
.
R
can be either commutative superalgebra (e.g. Grassmannian algebra) or ordinary field. A supermatrix of dimension
(r|s)\times(p|q)
is a matrix

\displaystyle X=\begin{pmatrix}X_{00}&X_{01}\\
X_{10}&X_{11}\end{pmatrix}

(104)

with
r+s
rows and
p+q
columns. The block matrices
X_{00}
and
X_{11}
consist solely of even graded element in
R
, while

X_{01}
and
X_{10}
consist solely of odd graded elements in
R
.
If
X
is a square matrix, its supertrace is defined as

\displaystyle\operatorname{str}(X)=\operatorname{tr}(X_{00})-\operatorname{tr}(X_{11}).

(105)

For an invertible supermatrix over commutative superalgebra, its superdeterminant (Berezinian) is defined as

\displaystyle\operatorname{sdet}X=\det(X_{00}-X_{01}X^{-1}_{11}X_{10})\det(X_{11})^{-1}=\det(X_{00})\det(X_{11}-X_{10}X_{00}^{-1}X_{01})^{-1}.

(106)

Here we write down explicitly the supergroup gauge origami maps
B_{a}
,
I_{A}
, and
J_{A}
,

,

\displaystyle B_{a}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}B_{a,00}&B_{a,01}\\

(107a)

\displaystyle I_{A}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}I_{A,00}&I_{A,01}\\

(107b)

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}J_{A,00}&J_{A,01}\\

The components of the off-diagonal block matrix are Grassmannian numbers.

The real moment map of the gauge origami (

Appendix B
List of relevant mathematical functions

In this appendix, we provide some relevant details about the mathematical functions used in the main text.

B.1
Random partition

A partition is defined as a way of expressing a non-negative integer
n
as a summation over other non-negative integers. Each partition can be labeled by a Young diagram
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)}>0)
with
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

n=|\lambda|=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\lambda_{i},

(109)

where
\ell(\lambda)
denotes the number of rows in
\lambda
, i.e.
\ell(\lambda)=\lambda_{1}^{\text{T}}

We define the generating function of such a partition as

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}\mathfrak{q}^{|\lambda|}=\frac{1}{(\mathfrak{q};\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}},\quad(\mathfrak{q};\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\mathfrak{q}^{n}\right);

(110a)

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}t^{\ell(\lambda)}\mathfrak{q}^{|\lambda|}=\frac{1}{(\mathfrak{q}t;\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}};\quad(\mathfrak{q}t;\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-t\mathfrak{q}^{n}),

(110b)

for
|\mathfrak{q}|<1
.
Here we also define the
\mathfrak{q}
-shifted factorial (the
\mathfrak{q}
-Pochhammer symbol) as

(z;\mathfrak{q})_{n}=\prod_{m=0}^{n-1}(1-z\mathfrak{q}^{m}).

(111)

B.2
The elliptic functions

Here we fix our notation for the elliptic functions. The so-called Dedekind eta function is denoted as

\eta(\tau)=e^{\frac{\pi i\tau}{12}}(\mathfrak{q};\mathfrak{q})_{\infty},

(112)

where
\mathfrak{q}=\exp\left(2\pi i\tau\right)
.
The first Jacobi theta function is denoted as:

\theta_{11}(z;\tau)=ie^{\frac{\pi i\tau}{4}}z^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathfrak{q};\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}(\mathfrak{q}z;\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}(z^{-1};\mathfrak{q})_{\infty},

(113)

whose series expansion

\theta_{11}(z;\tau)=i\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}(-1)^{r-\frac{1}{2}}z^{r}e^{\pi i\tau r^{2}}=i\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}(-1)^{r-\frac{1}{2}}e^{rx}e^{\pi i\tau r^{2}},

(114)

implies that it obeys the heat equation

\frac{1}{\pi i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\theta_{11}(z;\tau)=(z\partial_{z})^{2}\theta_{11}(z;\tau).

(115)

The Weierstrass
\wp
-function

\wp(z)=\frac{1}{z^{2}}+\sum_{p,q\geq 0}\left\{\frac{1}{(z+p+q\tau)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(p+q\tau)^{2}}\right\},

(116)

is related to the theta and eta functions by

\wp(z;\tau)=-(z\partial_{z})^{2}\log\theta_{11}(z;\tau)+\frac{1}{\pi i}\partial_{\tau}\log\eta(\tau).

(117)

B.3
Higher rank theta function

Let us also define

\Theta_{\widehat{A}_{N-1}}(\vec{z};\tau)=\eta(\tau)^{N}\prod_{\alpha>\beta}\frac{\theta_{11}(z_{\alpha}/z_{\beta};\tau)}{\eta(\tau)}

(118)

as the rank
N-1
theta function, which also satisfies the generalized heat equation

N\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\Theta_{\widehat{A}_{N-1}}(\vec{z};\tau)=\pi i\Delta_{\vec{z}}\Theta_{\widehat{A}_{N-1}}(\vec{z};\tau),

(119)

with the
N
-variable Laplacian:

\displaystyle\Delta_{\vec{z}}=\sum_{\omega=0}^{N-1}(z_{\omega}\partial_{z_{\omega}})^{2}.

(120)

B.4
The orbifolded partition

For the purpose in the main text, we consider the orbifolded coupling

\displaystyle\mathfrak{q}=\prod_{\omega=0}^{N-1}\mathfrak{q}_{\omega};\quad\mathfrak{q}_{\omega+N}=\mathfrak{q}_{\omega},

(121)

and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{q}_{\omega}=\frac{z_{\omega}}{z_{\omega-1}};\quad z_{\omega+N}=\mathfrak{q}z_{\omega}.

(122)

We also consider the orbifolded version of the generating function of partitions
(\mathfrak{q};\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}^{-1}
in (
110
). Given a finite partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)})
, we define

\mathbb{Q}^{\lambda}_{\omega}=\prod_{j=1}^{\lambda_{1}}\mathfrak{q}_{\omega+1-j}^{\lambda_{j}^{\text{T}}}=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\frac{z_{\omega}}{z_{\omega-\lambda_{i}}}.

(123)

where the transposed partition is denoted by
\lambda^{\text{T}}
.
The summation over all possible partitions is given by

\mathbb{Q}_{\omega}=\sum_{\lambda}\mathbb{Q}^{\lambda}_{\omega}=\sum_{\lambda}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\left(\frac{z_{\omega}}{z_{\omega-\lambda_{i}}}\right)=\sum_{l_{0},\dots,l_{N-1},l\geq 0}\prod_{\alpha=1}^{N-1}\left(\frac{z_{\omega}}{z_{\alpha}}\right)^{l_{\alpha}}\mathfrak{q}^{l}.

(124)

The function
\mathbb{Q}(\vec{z};\tau)
is the orbifolded version of the generating function of partitions (
110
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{Q}(\vec{z};\tau)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\omega=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{Q}_{\omega}(\vec{z};\tau)

\displaystyle=\prod_{N-1\geq\alpha>\beta\geq 0}\frac{1}{(\frac{z_{\alpha}}{z_{\beta}};\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}(\mathfrak{q}\frac{z_{\beta}}{z_{\alpha}};\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}}\prod_{\alpha=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{(\mathfrak{q};\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{N-1\geq\alpha>\beta\geq 0}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{1/12}\eta(\tau)\sqrt{z_{\alpha}/z_{\beta}}}{\theta_{11}(z_{\alpha}/z_{\beta};\tau)}\times\left[\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{1/24}}{\eta(\tau)}\right]^{N}

\displaystyle=\left[\eta(\tau)^{-N}\prod_{N-1\geq\alpha>\beta\geq 0}\frac{\eta(\tau)}{\theta_{11}(z_{\alpha}/z_{\beta};\tau)}\right]\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{N^{2}/24}}{\vec{z}^{\vec{\rho}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Theta_{\widehat{A}_{N-1}}(\vec{z};\tau)}\frac{\mathfrak{q}^{N^{2}/24}}{\vec{z}^{\vec{\rho}}},

(125)

where
\vec{\rho}
is the Weyl vector associated with
\mathfrak{sl}(N)
Lie algebra, whose entries are given as

\vec{\rho}=(\rho_{0},\dots,\rho_{N-1});\quad\rho_{\omega}=\omega-\frac{N-1}{2};\quad|\vec{\rho}|^{2}=\sum_{\omega=0}^{N-1}\rho_{\omega}^{2}=\frac{N(N^{2}-1)}{12};\quad\vec{z}^{\vec{\rho}}=\prod_{\omega=0}^{N-1}z_{\omega}^{\rho_{\omega}}.

(126)

Using eq. (
119

\mathbb{Q}
-function satisfies

0=\sum_{\omega}\nabla^{\mathfrak{q}}_{\omega}\log\mathbb{Q}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\vec{z}}\log\mathbb{Q}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\omega}(\nabla^{z}_{\omega}\log\mathbb{Q})^{2},

(127)

with

\sum_{\omega}\nabla_{\omega}^{\mathfrak{q}}=N\nabla^{\mathfrak{q}}+\vec{\rho}\cdot{\nabla}^{\vec{z}}.

(128)

Superdiffusion transition for a phonon Boltzmann equation
Gaëtan Cane gaetancane.ch

Abstract
. We consider an infinite harmonic chain of charged particles submitted to the action of a magnetic field of intensity B B and subject to the action of a stochastic noise conserving the energy. In [ 21 ] it has been proved that if B = 0 B=0 the transport of energy is described by a 3 / 4 3/4 -fractional diffusion while it has been proved in [ 31 ] that if B ≠ 0 B\neq 0 it is described by a 5 / 6 5/6 -fractional diffusion. In [ 21 , 31 ] the authors used a two step argument, i.e. they first proved that the kinetic limit of the Wigner distribution is the solution of a phonon Boltzmann equation and then proved that this solution converges to the solution of a fractional diffusion equation with exponent 3 / 4 3/4 if B = 0 B=0 (see [ 21 ] ) and exponent 5 / 6 5/6 if B ≠ 0 B\neq 0 (see [ 31 ] ). In this paper we quantify the intensity of the magnetic field required to switch from one macroscopic regime to the other one from the phonon Boltzmann equation. We also describe the transition mechanism to cross the two different phases.

To prove Lemma
6.3
we need the following result.

Lemma D.1
.

Let
u>0
then

(133)

\forall\sigma>0,\quad\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[\left|\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{\beta_{\delta}}u\rfloor}\sum_{n=0}^{\lfloor N^{\beta_{\delta}}u\rfloor}\lambda_{B_{N}}\left(X_{B_{N}}^{n}\right)\tau_{N}^{n}-\frac{u}{2}\right|>\sigma\right]=0\ .

Proof of Lemma
D.1
.

In order to prove the convergence in probability we prove the convergence in
\mathbb{L}^{1}
. Let
N
be fixed, we define the sequence of function
\left(\lambda_{B_{N}}^{\varepsilon}\right)_{\varepsilon>0}
where for any
k
in
\mathbb{T}
and
i
in
\{1,2\}
we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{B_{N}}^{\varepsilon}(k,i)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{B_{N}}(k,i)\mathds{1}_{[-\tfrac{1}{2}+\varepsilon]\cup[\varepsilon,\tfrac{1}{2}-\varepsilon]}\hskip 9.24994pt\text{and}\hskip 9.24994pt\overline{\lambda}_{B_{N}}^{\varepsilon}=\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\{1,2\}}\lambda_{B_{N}}^{\varepsilon}(k,i)\;d\pi_{B_{N}}(k,i)\ .

Observe that for any
\varepsilon
,
\lambda_{B_{N}}^{\varepsilon}
is in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\pi_{B_{N}})
.
Using the stationnarity of the Markov chain
\left(X_{B_{N}}^{n}\right)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and the definition of
\pi_{B_{N}}
given by Eq. (
41
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{\beta_{\delta}}u\rfloor}\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\lfloor N^{\beta_{\delta}}u\rfloor}\left|\lambda_{B_{N}}\left(X_{B_{N}}^{n}\right)\tau_{N}^{n}-\lambda_{B_{N}}^{\varepsilon}\left(X_{B_{N}}^{n}\right)\tau_{N}^{n}\right|\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon)\ ,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{\beta_{\delta}}u\rfloor}\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\lfloor N^{\beta_{\delta}}u\rfloor}\left|\bar{\lambda}_{B_{N}}^{\varepsilon}-\frac{u}{2}\right|\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon)\ ,

where
|C(\varepsilon)|\lesssim\varepsilon
.

By the triangle inequality, to end the proof it is sufficient to prove that for any
\varepsilon
we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{\beta_{\delta}}u\rfloor}\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\lfloor N^{\beta_{\delta}}u\rfloor}\lambda_{B_{N}}^{\varepsilon}\left(X_{B_{N}}^{n}\right)\tau_{N}^{n}-\bar{\lambda}_{B_{N}}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}}=0\ .

Using Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality and that for any
N
the random variables
\left(X_{B_{N}}^{n},\tau_{N}^{n}\right)
are independent and centered we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{\beta_{\delta}}u\rfloor}\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\lfloor N^{\beta_{\delta}}u\rfloor}\lambda_{B_{N}}^{\varepsilon}\left(X_{B_{N}}^{n}\right)\tau_{n}^{N}-\bar{\lambda}_{B_{N}}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K(\varepsilon)}{\sqrt{N^{\beta_{\delta}}}}\ ,

where
K(\varepsilon)\lesssim\varepsilon
.
Finally we get that

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{\beta_{\delta}}u\rfloor}\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\lfloor N^{\beta_{\delta}}u\rfloor}\lambda_{B_{N}}\left(X_{B_{N}}^{n}\right)\tau_{N}^{n}-\bar{\lambda}_{B_{N}}^{\varepsilon}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon)\ .

By sending
\varepsilon
to zero we end the proof of Lemma
D.1
.
∎

From this result we can prove Lemma
6.3
.

Proof of Lemma
6.3
.

Let
t_{0}>0
,
T\geq t_{0}
and
\varepsilon>0
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[\sup_{t\in[t_{0},T]}|S_{N}(t)-S(t)|>\varepsilon\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[\exists t\in[t_{0},T],\;\;S_{N}(t)\leq S(t)-\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[\exists t\in[t_{0},T],\;\;S_{N}(t)\geq S(t)+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]

Let
m
in
{\mathbb{N}}
, since
S
is a continuous function there exists a subdivision
(t_{i})_{i\in\{0,\cdots,m\}}
such that
t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{m}=T
with

\forall i\in\{0,\cdots,m\},\quad S(t_{i+1})-S(t_{i})\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{10}\ .

Using the fact that
S
and
S_{N}
are increasing functions we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[\exists t\in[t_{0},T],\;\;S_{N}(t)\leq S(t)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[\exists t\in[t_{i},t_{i+1}],\;\;S_{N}(t)\leq S(t)-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[S_{N}(t_{i})\leq S(t_{i+1})-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[S_{N}(t_{i})\leq S(t_{i})-\frac{2\varepsilon}{5}\right]\ .

Using the same techniques we get

\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[\exists t\in[t_{0},T],\;\;S_{N}(t)\geq S(t)+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[S_{N}(t_{i+1})-S(t_{i+1})\geq\tfrac{2\varepsilon}{5}\right]\ .

Hence we proved that

\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[\sup_{t\in[t_{0},T]}|S_{N}(t)-S(t)|>\varepsilon\right]\leq 2m\sup_{i=0,\cdots,m}\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[\left|S_{N}(t_{i})-S(t_{i})\right|\geq\tfrac{2\varepsilon}{5}\right]\ .

To conclude the proof it is sufficient to prove that

\forall t\in[t_{0},T],\quad\forall\delta>0,\quad\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[\left|S_{N}(t)-S(t)\right|>\delta\right]=0\ .

This result follows from Lemma
D.1
, indeed let
\delta>0
then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[\left|S_{N}(t)-S(t)\right|>\delta\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[S_{N}(t)<S(t)+\delta\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[S_{N}(t)>S(t)+\delta\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{\beta_{\delta}}u\rfloor}\sum_{n=0}^{\lfloor N^{\beta_{\delta}}[S(t)+\delta]\rfloor}\lambda_{B_{N}}\left(X_{B_{N}}^{n}\right)\tau_{N}^{n}>t\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{B_{N}}}\left[\frac{1}{\lfloor N^{\beta_{\delta}}u\rfloor}\sum_{n=0}^{\lfloor N^{\beta_{\delta}}[S(t)-\delta]\rfloor}\lambda_{B_{N}}\left(X_{B_{N}}^{n}\right)\tau_{N}^{n}\leq t\right]\ .

Using the dominated convergence theorem and Lemma
D.1
, we conclude the proof.
∎

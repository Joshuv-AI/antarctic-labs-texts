Pro-nilpotently extended dgca-s and SH Lie-Rinehart pairs
Damjan Pištalo

Abstract
. Category of pro-nilpotently extended differential graded commutative algebras is introduced. Chevalley-Eilenberg construction provides an equivalence between its certain full subcategory and the opposite to the full subcategory of strong homotopy Lie Rinehart pairs with strong homotopy morphisms, consisting of pairs ( A , M ) (A,M) where M M is flat as a graded A A -module. It is shown that pairs ( A , M ) (A,M) , where A A is a semi-free dgca and M M a cell complex in Mod ( A ) \!\!\mathop{\rm~Mod}\nolimits(A) , form a category of fibrant objects by proving that their Chevalley-Eilenberg complexes form a category of cofibrant objects.

55U35, 17B55, 16W25

1.
Introduction

Lie algebras have a solid homotopy theory. Its development dates back Quillen’s work on rational homotopy theory
[
18
]
, which, among many other things, establishes an adjunction
between dg Lie algebras (denoted by
\tt DGL
) and a certain class of differential graded cocommutative coalgebras (denoted by
\tt DGC
), whose right adjoint sends a dg Lie algebra to its homological Chevalley-Eilenberg complex. In
[
5
]
, Hinich puts cofibrantly generated model structures on both categories, promoting the above adjunction to a Quillen equivalence. Fibrant objects in
\tt DGC
are exactly Chevalley-Eilenberg complexes of Lie
\infty
-algebras, and morphisms between them are equivalently
\infty
-morphisms of Lie
\infty
-algebras. In
[
16
]
, J. Pridham dualized differential graded cocommutative coalgebras into so-called
\mathbb{Z}
-graded pro-Artinian chain algebras (denoted by
dg_{\mathbb{Z}}\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{k}
) which form a fibrantly generated model category, whose cofibrant objects are cohomological Chavelley-Eilenberg complexes of Lie
\infty
-algebras. Further on, Pridham shows that weak equivalences between cofibrant objects are precisely those maps whose weight-zero part in the grading of the completed symmetric tensor product dualizes to a weak equivalence of chain complexes; equivalently, that weak equivalences between fibrant objects in
\tt DGC
are
\infty
-quasi-isomorphisms of Lie
\infty
-algebras in the sense of
[
11
]
.

The above story has been partially generalized to Lie Rinehart pairs mainly due to the work of G. Vezzosi (
[
21
]
) and J. Nuiten (
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
). More concretely, it is shown that the category of dg Lie Rinehart pairs
(A,M)
with a fixed base algebra
A
(denoted by
\!\!\mathop{\rm~LieAlgd_{A}^{dg}}\nolimits
) is a semi-model category, and that its homotopy theory is equivalent to that of formal moduli problems over
A
. The equivalence builds on the relation between dg Lie algebras and deformation problems studied in the above cited work of Hinich and Pridham and elsewhere.

Homotopy theory for Lie-Rinehart pairs over a variable basis is implied in the construction of the BV-BRST complex, which can be interpreted (see for example
[
4
]
) as a two-step resolution of a Lie-Rinehart pair
(A,M)
by a strong homotopy Lie Rinehart pair
(QA,P)
in the sense of
[
22
]
, where
QA\to A
is a Koszul-Tate resolution of
A
, and
P\to M
a free resolution of
M
as a
QA
-module. Although different variants of such resolutions of Lie Rinehart pars have been formalized for example in
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
, and
[
10
]
, to the best of my knowledge, they have not yet been embedded into a solid homotopy theory.

In the present paper, I define differential pro-graded modules and algebras as pro-object in the categories of graded modules and algebras, together with a differential which satisfies the Leibniz rule in an appropriate sense. Category of pro-nilpotently extended or fat dgca-s (denoted by
{\tt fcdga}(k)
) is defined as the full subcategory of the arrow category of differential pro-graded commutative algebras consisting of maps
A\to A_{0}
, with
A_{0}
a non-positively graded dgca, and
A
an inverse system of unbounded graded-commutative algebras which are nilpotent extensions of
A_{0}
. Denote by
{\tt SHRL}_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~flat}\nolimits}(k)
the full subcategory of strong homotopy Lie-Rinehart pairs
(A,M)
with
M
flat as a graded
A
-module. Chevalley-Eilenberg construction provides an equivalence between
{\tt SHLR}_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~flat}\nolimits}(k)
, and the opposite to a certain full subcategory of
{\tt fcdga}(k)
.

Generalizing an above-mentioned Pridham’s result, I show that Chevalley-Eilenberg complexes of strong homotopy Lie-Rinehart pairs
(A,M)
, with
A
a semi-free dgca and
M
a cell complex in
\!\!\mathop{\rm~Mod}\nolimits(A)
, form a category of cofibrant objects, in which the map dual to a SH morphism
(A,M)\to(B,N)
is a weak equivalence if its weight-zero components
B\to A
and
M\to A\otimes_{B}N
are both quasi-isomorphisms.

\tt A,B,M,N,\ldots

.

. The index

\tt A

2.
Pro-finite and flat graded modules – duality

{\tt M}\in{\tt Mod}({\tt A})
is called free if it is isomorphic to the tensor product of
\tt A
with a graded vector space (
{\tt M}={\tt A}\otimes_{k}\mathbb{V})
. It is free finite if the graded vector space
\mathbb{V}
is finite dimensional in each degree and non-zero in at most finitely many degrees. The full subcategory of free finite
\tt A
-modules (throughout the text referred to as
finite
\tt A
-modules
for simplicity) is denoted by
\tt Mod_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~finite}\nolimits}(A)
.

{\tt Mod}({\tt A})
is a closed symmetric monoidal Abelian category, the compatibility condition being that the tensor product
-\otimes_{\tt A}-
is additive in both arguments. To prove that the later category is indeed Abelian, notice first that the category
{\tt gVec}(k)
of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector spaces is Abelian (
[
19
, 12.16.2]
). By
[
1
]
, the category
{\tt Mod}({\tt A})
is Abelian as well, as the monad
{\tt A}\otimes_{k}-:{\tt gVec}(k)\to{\tt gVec}(k)
of the free-forgetful adjunction

{\tt A}\otimes_{k}-:{\tt gVec}(k)\rightleftarrows{\tt Mod}({\tt A}):\!\!\mathop{\rm~For}\nolimits

is additive and preserves cokernels.

With this, one can define flat
\tt A
modules:

Definition 1
.

\tt M\in Mod(A)
is called flat if the tensor product functor
-\otimes_{\tt A}\tt M:{\tt Mod}({\tt A})\to{\tt Mod}({\tt A})
is exact.

Below generalizations of classical results are immediate:

Proposition 2
.

Every free
\tt A
-module is projective, and every projective
\tt A
-module is flat.

Theorem 3
.

(Lazard’s theorem)
\tt M\in{\tt Mod}({\tt A})
is flat if and only if it is the colimit of a directed system of finite
\tt A
-modules.

It follows that flat
\tt A
-modules are dualizable in a specific sense:

Lemma 4
.

The full subcategory
\tt Mod_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~flat}\nolimits}(A)\subset\tt Mod(A)
of flat
\tt A
-modules is equivalent to the opposite of the category
\!\!\mathop{\rm~Pro}\nolimits(\tt Mod_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~finite}\nolimits}(A))
of pro finite
\tt A
-modules.

Proof.

By the Lazard’s theorem, the functor

(2.1)

\varinjlim:\!\!\mathop{\rm~Ind}\nolimits(\tt Mod_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~finite}\nolimits}(A))\to Mod_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~flat}\nolimits}(A)

from the category of ind finite
\tt A
-modules to the category of flat
\tt A
modules, given by mapping a direct system to its colimit, is well-defined and essentially surjective. It is also full and faithful. Namely, for
({\tt M}_{\alpha})_{\alpha},({\tt N}_{\beta})_{\beta}\in\!\!\mathop{\rm~Ind}\nolimits(\tt Mod_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~finite}\nolimits}(A))
,

(2.2)

\!\!\mathop{\rm~Hom}\nolimits_{\tt Mod(A)}(\varinjlim_{\alpha}{\tt M}_{\alpha},\varinjlim_{\beta}{\tt N}_{\beta})=\varprojlim_{\alpha}\!\!\mathop{\rm~Hom}\nolimits_{\tt Mod(A)}({\tt M}_{\alpha},\varinjlim_{\beta}{\tt N}_{\beta})=\varprojlim_{\alpha}\varinjlim_{\beta}\!\!\mathop{\rm~Hom}\nolimits_{\tt Mod(A)}({\tt M}_{\alpha},{\tt N}_{\beta}),

since free finite
\tt A
-modules are compact objects in
\tt Mod(A).

As the functor

\underline{\!\!\mathop{\rm~Hom}\nolimits}_{\tt Mod(A)}(-,{\tt A}):\tt Mod_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~finite}\nolimits}(A)^{\!\!\mathop{\rm~op}\nolimits}\to\tt Mod_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~finite}\nolimits}(A),

is an equivalence, it follows that

\tt Mod_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~flat}\nolimits}(A)\cong\!\!\mathop{\rm~Ind}\nolimits(\tt Mod_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~finite}\nolimits}(A))\cong\!\!\mathop{\rm~Pro}\nolimits(\tt Mod_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~finite}\nolimits}(A)^{\!\!\mathop{\rm~op}\nolimits})^{\!\!\mathop{\rm~op}\nolimits}\cong\!\!\mathop{\rm~Pro}\nolimits(\tt Mod_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~finite}\nolimits}(A))^{\!\!\mathop{\rm~op}\nolimits}.

∎

3.
Differential pro-graded modules and algebras

Dualizing differential graded modules requires care.
Consider
A=({\tt A},d_{A})\in{\tt dgca}(k)
.
A
-module is called
graded-flat
if the underlying graded
\tt A
-module is flat. The full subcategory of graded-flat
\tt A
-modules is denoted by
{\tt Mod}_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~flat}\nolimits}(A)
.
Given
M=({\tt M},d)\in{\tt Mod}_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~flat}\nolimits}(A)
and the isomorphism
\tt M\cong\varinjlim_{\alpha}M_{\alpha}
of Lazard’s theorem, the differential on
\tt M
does not in general restrict to
\tt M_{\alpha}
-s. One should, rather, endow the corresponding ind finite
\tt A
-module and its dual pro finite
\tt A
module with appropriate differentials. As categories of both pro and ind
k
-vector spaces are Abelian (see for example
[
7
]
), it makes sense to speak of chain complexes there. However, it is not a priory clear what should be pro and ind versions of the Leibniz identity (
1.1
). In the sequel, I only detail a notion of differential pro-graded
A
-modules, as the construction of ind-
A
-modules is analogous. To begin with, recall a characterization of morphisms in pro-categories from the book of Mardešić and Segal
[
13
]
:

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category;
A
and
B
directed sets;
(X_{\alpha},p_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}})
and
(Y_{\beta},p_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}})
inverse systems in
\mathcal{C}
. A morphism of inverse systems
(X_{\alpha})\to(Y_{\beta})
consists of a function
\phi:B\to A
and of morphisms
f_{\beta}:X_{\phi(\beta)}\to Y_{\beta},
one for each
\beta\in B
, such that for all
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in B
,
\beta>\beta^{\prime}
there exists
\alpha>\phi(\beta),\phi(\beta^{\prime})
for which the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{\phi(\beta)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{\alpha}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{\phi(\beta^{\prime})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{\beta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{\beta^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{\beta}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{\alpha,\phi(\beta)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{\alpha,\phi(\beta^{\prime})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{\beta^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}}}

commutes. Morphisms of inverse systems are denoted by
(f_{\beta},\phi).
We say that morphisms of inverse systems
(f_{\beta},\phi)
and
(g_{\beta},\psi)
are equivalent if for every
\beta\in B
there exists
\alpha\in A
,
\alpha>\phi(\beta),\psi(\beta)
such that the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{\phi(\beta)}}

Let
f
be a morphism in
\!\!\mathop{\rm~Pro}\nolimits(C)
represented by a map of inverse systems
(f_{\beta},\phi)
. Denote for
\alpha>\phi(\beta)

f_{\alpha,\beta}=f_{\phi(\beta),\beta}\circ p_{\alpha,\phi(\beta)}
. The family
(f_{\alpha,\beta})_{\beta\in B,\alpha\geq\phi(\beta)}
will be called a
representing family
of
f
. Observe that
(f_{\alpha,\beta})_{\beta\in B,\alpha\geq\phi(\beta)}
and
(f^{\prime}_{\alpha,\beta})_{\beta\in B,\alpha\geq\psi(\beta)}
represent the same morphism if and only if for any
\beta\in B
there exists
\alpha\in A
such that
f_{\alpha,\beta}=f^{\prime}_{\alpha,\beta}.
In a slight abuse of terminology, I identify maps with their representing families, writing
f=(f_{\alpha,\beta})
. For
X=(X_{\alpha},p_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}})\in\!\!\mathop{\rm~Pro}\nolimits\mathcal{C}
,
\!\!\mathop{\rm~id}\nolimits_{X}=(p_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}})
.

Coming back to graded
\tt A
-modules, differential is defined by means of a representing family. For this, the Leibniz identity
1.1
is employed relative to underlying maps
p_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha}
. Generally, derivations relative to a map are defined as follows:

Definition 5
.

Given a differential graded
k
-algebra
A=({\tt A},d_{A})
and a map
f:\tt M\to N
in
\tt Mod(A)
, a morphism of graded
k
-modules
d_{f}:\tt M\to N[-1]
is called a
derivation over
f
if for all
a\in A

d_{f}(a\cdot m)=d_{A}(a)\cdot f(m)+(-1)^{|a|}a\cdot d_{f}(m).

The set of all derivations over
f
is denoted by
\!\!\mathop{\rm~Der}\nolimits_{f}(\tt M,N).

Crucially, derivations over graded
\tt A
-module maps compose with the
\tt A
-module maps. Proof is straight-forward.

Proposition 6
.

Let
f:\tt L\to M
and
g:\tt M\to N
be morphisms of
\tt A
-modules.

(1)

Given a derivation
d_{f}:\tt L\to M[-1]
over
f
,
g[-1]\circ d_{f}
is a derivation over
g\circ f
.

(2)

Given a derivation
d_{g}:\tt M\to N[-1]
over
g
,
d_{g}\circ f
is a derivation over
g\circ f
.

Finally, derivations of a pro-graded
A
-module are defined as follows:

Definition 7
.

Given a differential graded
k
-algebra
A=({\tt A},d_{A})\in{\tt dgca}(k)
and a graded
\tt A
-module
\tt M
, derivations of
M
are the following endomorphisms of pro-graded
k
-vector spaces:

\!\!\mathop{\rm~Der}\nolimits({\tt M})=\varprojlim_{\beta}\varinjlim_{\alpha\geq\beta}\!\!\mathop{\rm~Der}\nolimits_{p_{\alpha\beta}}({\tt M}_{\alpha},{\tt M}_{\beta})\subset\varprojlim_{\beta}\varinjlim_{\alpha\geq\beta}\!\!\mathop{\rm~Hom}\nolimits_{k}({\tt M}_{\alpha},{\tt M}_{\beta}[-1])=\!\!\mathop{\rm~Hom}\nolimits_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~Pro}\nolimits({\tt gMod}(k))}(\tt M,M[-1]).

More generally, given a map of pro-graded
\tt A
-modules
f=(f_{\alpha,\beta}):\tt M\to N

derivation over

f
is a map in

\!\!\mathop{\rm~Der}\nolimits_{f}({\tt M,N})=\varprojlim_{\beta}\varinjlim_{\alpha}\!\!\mathop{\rm~Der}\nolimits_{f_{\alpha\beta}}({\tt M}_{\alpha},{\tt N}_{\beta}).

It is readily verified that the notion of a derivation over
f
is independent of the choice of a representing family
(f_{\alpha,\beta})
.
Unsurprisingly, differential pro-graded
A
modules are defined as pro-graded
\tt A
modules together with a square-zero derivation:

Definition 8
.

Given a differential graded
k
-algebra
A=({\tt A},d_{A})
, a
pro-
A
-module

M=({\tt M},d)
is a pair of a pro-graded
\tt A
-module
M
and a square-zero derivation
d\in\!\!\mathop{\rm~Der}\nolimits(\tt M)
. Morphisms of pro-
A
-modules are morphisms of underlying pro-graded
\tt A
-modules which intertwine the differential. The category of pro-
A
-modules is denoted by
{\tt ProMod}(A)
. Its full subcategory whose objects are pairs
({\tt M},d)
such that
\tt M
is pro finite is called the category of
pro finite
A
-modules
, and denoted by
{\tt ProMod}_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~finite}\nolimits}(A)
. Categories of
ind-
A
-modules
and
ind finite
A
-modules
are defined analogously, and denoted respectively by
{\tt IndMod}(A)
and
{\tt IndMod}_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~finite}\nolimits}(A)
.

Both the square-zero property of the differential, and the intertwining property of morphisms have concrete characterizations on the level of representing families:

Proposition 9
.

(1)

Given a pro-graded
\tt A
-module
{\tt M}=({\tt M}_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}
, a derivation
d=(d_{\alpha,\beta})\in\!\!\mathop{\rm~Der}\nolimits(\tt M)
, squares to zero if and only if for every
\beta\in A
there exist
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in A
such that
d_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta}\circ d_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}=0
.

(2)

Given
M=(({\tt M}_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A},d^{M}),N=(({\tt N}_{\beta})_{\beta\in B},d^{N})\in{\tt ProMod}(A)
, a map
f=(f_{\alpha,\beta}):\tt M\to N
intertwines the differentials if and only if for every
\beta\in B
, there exist
\beta^{\prime}\in B
,
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in A
such that
f_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta}\circ d^{M}_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}=d^{N}_{\beta^{\prime},\beta}\circ f_{\alpha,\beta^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Both statements follow immediately from the concrete realizations of diagrams in pro-categories, elaborated in the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 3.3.]
.

Duality of Lemma
4
extends to the dg world:

Proposition 10
.

The full subcategory
{\tt Mod_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~flat}\nolimits}}(A)\subset{\tt Mod}(A)
of graded-flat
A
-modules is equivalent to the opposite of the category
{\tt ProMod}_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~finite}\nolimits}(A)
of pro finite
A
-modules. Equivalence and its inverse are both denoted by
(-)^{*}.

Proof.

Let
M
and
N
be graded-flat
A
-modules, and let
f:M\to N
be a morphism.
By Lazard’s theorem, underlying graded
\tt A
-modules are colimits of directed systems of finite
\tt A
-modules
\tt M=\varinjlim_{\alpha}M_{\alpha}
, and
\tt N=\varinjlim_{\beta}N_{\beta}
. Let
(f_{\alpha,\beta}):(M_{\alpha})\to(N_{\beta})
be the corresponding map of ind-finite
\tt A
-modules, in view of equivalence
2.1
. Assume for now that

\!\!\mathop{\rm~Der}\nolimits_{f}(\varinjlim_{\alpha}{\tt M}_{\alpha},\varinjlim_{\beta}{\tt N}_{\beta})\cong\varprojlim_{\alpha}\varinjlim_{\beta}\!\!\mathop{\rm~Der}\nolimits_{f_{\alpha\beta}}({\tt M}_{\alpha},{\tt N}_{\beta}).

The statement is proven later in the text. In fact, it is a special case of the isomorphism
5.6
for
l=1
.

Recall that dual of a finite
\tt A
-module
\tt M_{\alpha}
is defined as the module of graded homomorphisms of
\tt A
-modules
\underline{\!\!\mathop{\rm~Hom}\nolimits}_{\tt A}(\tt M_{\alpha},A).
Explicitly, degree
k
elements of
\underline{\!\!\mathop{\rm~Hom}\nolimits}_{\tt A}(\tt M_{\alpha},A)
are maps
f:{\tt M_{\alpha}}[k]\to\tt A
of graded vector spaces such that
f(a\cdot x)=(-1)^{k|a|}a\cdot f(x)
, and the
\tt A
module structure is given by
(a\cdot f)(x)=a\cdot f(x)
. Functor
\underline{\!\!\mathop{\rm~Hom}\nolimits}_{\tt A}(\tt-,A)
is a contravariant self-equivalence of
{\tt Mod}_{\!\!\mathop{\rm~finite}\nolimits}(\tt A)
, denoted by
(-)^{*}
.
Derivations between finite
\tt A
modules also dualize. More precisely, there is a bijection

\!\!\mathop{\rm~Der}\nolimits_{f_{\alpha\beta}}({\tt M}_{\alpha},{\tt N}_{\beta})\cong\!\!\mathop{\rm~Der}\nolimits_{f_{\alpha\beta}^{*}}({\tt N}_{\beta}^{*},{\tt M}_{\alpha}^{*})

which assigns to
X\in\!\!\mathop{\rm~Der}\nolimits_{f_{\alpha\beta}}({\tt M}_{\alpha},{\tt N}_{\beta})
a derivation given by

\phi\mapsto d_{A}\circ\phi\circ f_{\alpha\beta}-(-1)^{|\phi|}\phi\circ X.

Hence,

(3.1)

\!\!\mathop{\rm~Der}\nolimits_{f}({\tt M},{\tt N})\cong\varprojlim_{\alpha}\varinjlim_{\beta}\!\!\mathop{\rm~Der}\nolimits_{f_{\alpha\beta}^{*}}({\tt N}_{\beta}^{*},{\tt M}_{\alpha}^{*}).

Setting
f:\tt M\to M
to the identity, one concludes that differentials on
\tt M
are in 1-1 correspondence with the differentials of
\tt M^{*}
, establishing the equivalence on objects (for the square-zero property, see Proposition
9
). Given
M,N\in{\tt Mod}(A)
and an
\tt A
-module morphism
f:\tt M\to N
, derivations
d_{N}\circ f,f\circ d_{M}\in\!\!\mathop{\rm~Der}\nolimits_{f}(\tt M,N)
are equal if and only if the dual derivations
\varprojlim_{\alpha}\varinjlim_{\beta}\!\!\mathop{\rm~Der}\nolimits_{f_{\alpha\beta}^{*}}({\tt N}_{\beta}^{*},{\tt M}_{\alpha}^{*})
are equal. This establishes the equivalence on morphisms.
∎

I conclude the section with a rather obvious observation, which turns out to be quite useful:

Proposition 11
.

Given
A,B\in{\tt dgca}(k)
, and a morphism
f:A\to B
, the forgetful functor
\!\!\mathop{\rm~Pro}\nolimits({\tt Mod}(B))\to\!\!\mathop{\rm~Pro}\nolimits({\tt Mod}(A))
has a left adjoint base-change functor

B\otimes_{A}-:\!\!\mathop{\rm~Pro}\nolimits({\tt Mod}(A))\to\!\!\mathop{\rm~Pro}\nolimits({\tt Mod}(B)),\hskip 5.0pt(M_{\alpha})\mapsto(B\otimes_{A}M_{\alpha}).

Denote by
{\tt gca}_{\mathbb{Z}}(k)
the category of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded commutative
k
-algebras. As with differential pro-graded modules, the correct notion of a differential pro-graded algebra involves a Leibniz rule relative to a morphism of graded algebras:

Definition 12
.

Given
{\tt A,B}\in{\tt gca}_{\mathbb{Z}}(k)
and a morphism
f:\tt A\to B
, we say that a graded
k
-linear map
d_{f}:\tt A\to B[-1]
is a
derivation over
f

4.
Pro-nilpotently extended cdga-s

My reason to introduce differential pro-graded algebras is to deal with Chevalley-Eilenberg complexes of SH Lie Rinehart pairs. Let
(A,L)
be Lie Rinehart pair, where

is a commutative algebra over
k

a finitely generated projective

over

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\!\!\mathop{\rm~Sym}\nolimits_{A}(L^{*}[1]),d)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\!\!\mathop{\rm~Sym}\nolimits_{B}(M^{*}[1]),d)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{0}}

First generalization is to allow for

to be a perfect differential graded
A
-module concentrated in non-positive degrees. Any differential on

extending that on

]

]

If one either drops the non-positivity assumption on

to be a differential graded algebra (example of the later is the BV complex), or both, two new features appear:

•

Firstly,

]
,
\ldots

•

Secondly, not every differential on the completion will be intertwined by the projection

As formal power series are pro-nilpotent extensions of the base algebra, the appropriate category to study Chevalley-Eilenberg complexes of SH Lie Rinehart pairs is that of pro-nilpotent extensions of differential graded algebras. This motivates the following definition:

Definition 16
.

(A,A_{0})

with nilpotent kernels. A morphism of pro-nilpotently extended (fat) dgca-s

Duality between Lie algebroids and their Chevalley-Eilenberg complexes was first observed in the celebrated work of Vaintrob

Remark 25

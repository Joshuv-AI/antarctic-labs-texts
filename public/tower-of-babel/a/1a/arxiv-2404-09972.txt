Center construction for group-crossed tensor categories
Mizuki Oikawa

Abstract
We define the notion of the ( G , Γ ) (G,\Gamma) -crossed center of a ( G , Γ ) (G,\Gamma) -crossed tensor category in the sense of Natale. We show that the ( G , Γ ) (G,\Gamma) -crossed center is a ( G ⋈ Γ , G × Γ ) (G\bowtie\Gamma,G\times\Gamma) -braided tensor category. This construction generalizes the graded center construction for graded tensor categories and the equivariant center construction for tensor categories with group actions.



1
Introduction

Groups have been extensively studied as fundamental symmetries in various mathematical and physical contexts. However, it is sometimes important to consider symmetries beyond groups, which can provide a more general framework for understanding various structures. One such example of more general symmetries is the concept of a
tensor category
. A tensor category is a category equipped with a product structure, allowing the composition of objects in a manner that generalizes the concept of group multiplication. An important example of tensor categories is the representation category of conformal field theory
[
DHR71
]
[
FRS89
]
. The product structure in these tensor categories is commutative, leading to what is known as a
braided tensor category
. Braided tensor categories are crucial for understanding the correlation functions in conformal field theory
[
FRS02
]
. Moreover, from braided tensor categories, one can construct the Reshetikhin–Turaev topological quantum field theory
[
RT91
]
, which highlights the importance of these structures from a topological perspective as well.

When a conformal field theory admits a group action, it is possible to consider the equivariant version of its representation category
[
Müg05
]
. Although this category is not braided in the usual sense, it possesses a structure called a
G
-braiding
(in the sense of Turaev
[
Tur00
]
), which can be regarded as an equivariant version of braiding. A
G
-braided tensor category is a tensor category equipped with both a compatible
G
-action and
G
-grading. These structures modify the commutativity of the product in a specific way. The categorical fixed points under the
G
-action in a
G
-braided tensor category yield a braided tensor category in the usual sense. In the case of a finite group action on a rational conformal field theory, the categorical fixed points of the category of twisted representations correspond to the ordinary representation category of the fixed points in the conformal field theory
[
Müg05
]
. This fact allows us to describe the representation category of the finite group fixed points in the original conformal field theory. In general, while defining group fixed points is straightforward, providing an explicit description is often challenging, making this result particularly useful.

Furthermore, through the equivariant version of the Reshetikhin–Turaev construction, one can construct a
homotopy quantum field theory
from a
G
-braided tensor category
[
Tur00
]

[
TV14
]
, which serves as an invariant of manifolds with principal
G
-bundles. This offers intriguing insights from a topological perspective. Thus, a
G
-braided tensor category is interesting and significant for two main reasons: it provides a fascinating invariant in its own right, and it yields a usual braided tensor category through categorical fixed points, thereby offering a more refined description of that braided tensor category.

Through the Drinfeld center
[
JS91
]
[
Maj91
]
, which serves as the categorical version of the center of a unital noncommutative ring, it is possible to construct a braided tensor category from a tensor category that is not necessarily braided. There are at least two generalizations of the Drinfeld center construction that produce
G
-braided tensor categories. One is the graded center construction for graded tensor categories
[
GNN09
]
[
TV13
]
, and another is the equivariant center construction for tensor categories with group actions
[
BGM19
]
[
She19
]
. These constructions are important for providing examples of
G
-braided tensor categories; however, they utilize only one of the two structures: either the
G
-action or the
G
-grading. Therefore, it is natural to consider constructions for tensor categories where these structures are compatible, as in the case of
G
-braided tensor categories.

In this article, we define the notion of the
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed center
of a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed pivotal tensor category in the sense of Natale
[
Nat16
]
. This construction reproduces the graded center construction when the group
G
acting on the tensor category is trivial. Moreover, it reproduces the equivariant center construction when the group
\Gamma
that grades the tensor category is trivial.

We show that the
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed center of a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed pivotal tensor category is a
(G\bowtie\Gamma,G\times\Gamma)
-braided
tensor category, where
G\bowtie\Gamma
denotes the
Zappa–Szép product

[
Zap42
]
of
G
and
\Gamma
. Indeed, our construction produces a group-crossed tensor category in the sense of Natale even when we start from one in the sense of Turaev.

This article is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we review some preliminaries on group-crossed and group-braided monoidal categories in the sense of Natale. In Section
3
, we show the coherence theorem for
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal categories. In Section
4
, we define the notion of the
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed center
of a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed pivotal tensor category.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall the notion of a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal category in the sense of Natale
[
Nat16
]
. In short, it is a monoidal category with a compatible group action and a grading in some sense. We also recall the counterpart of a braiding on such a category.

Our terminologies on tensor category theory follow those in
[
EGNO15
]
. In particular, a
multitensor category
over a field
k
is a locally finite
k
-linear abelian rigid monoidal category with a bilinear monoidal product as in
[
EGNO15
, Definition 4.1.1]
. A
tensor category
is a multitensor category such that the endomorphism space of the unit is one-dimensional. Here, an
abelian monoidal category
is a monoidal category that is an abelian category with a biexact monoidal product. A
linear monoidal category
is a monoidal category that is a linear category (i.e. a category with linear hom spaces whose composition of morphisms are bilinear) with a bilinear monoidal product.

First, we recall the notion of a
grading
on an additive category. Let
\{\mathcal{A}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be a family of additive categories. Then, its
direct sum

\mathcal{C}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathcal{A}_{i}
is an additive category with a family of additive functors
\{I_{i}:\mathcal{A}_{i}\to\mathcal{A}\}_{i\in I}
which has the universality that for any additive category
\mathcal{B}
and a family of additive functors
\{F_{i}:\mathcal{A}_{i}\to\mathcal{B}\}_{i\in I}
there exists an additive functor
F:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
with a family of natural isomorphisms
\sigma^{F}=\{\sigma^{F,i}:FI_{i}\cong F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that for any other such pair
(\tilde{F},\tilde{\sigma}^{F})
and a family of natural transformations
\{\tau^{i}:F_{i}\to\tilde{F}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
we have a unique natural transformation
\tau:F\to\tilde{F}
with
\tau^{i}\sigma^{F,i}=\sigma^{\tilde{F},i}(\tau\ast I_{i})
for any
i\in I
, where
\ast
denotes the horizontal composition of natural transformations. Such a category
\mathcal{A}
always exists, see e.g.
[
EGNO15
, Section 1.3]
. The datum
(\{\mathcal{A}_{i}\}_{i\in I},\{I_{i}\}_{i\in I})
is called an
I
-grading
on
\mathcal{A}
and an additive category with an
I
-grading is called an
I
-graded category
.

We can indeed take
\sigma^{F,i}
’s to be identities. In this case, we refer to the functor
F
as the
extension
of the functors
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. Also, we refer to the natural transformation
\tau
obtained by universality as the
extension
of the natural transformations
\{\tau^{i}\}_{i\in I}
. Thus, we often define functors on
\mathcal{C}
and natural transformations between them by defining them on
\mathcal{A}_{i}
’s.

More explicitly, the notion of an
I
-grading on an additive category is equivalent to a family of additive subcategories
\{\mathcal{A}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that every object is a direct sum of objects of
\mathcal{A}_{i}
’s and
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(\lambda,\mu)=\{0\}
for
\lambda\in\obj(\mathcal{A}_{i})
and
\mu\in\obj(\mathcal{A}_{j})
with
i\neq j
. A decomposition of an object into objects of
\mathcal{A}_{i}
’s is called a
homogeneous decomposition
, which is essentially unique by
[
Oik24
, Lemma 2.10]
.

We write
\partial_{\mathcal{A}}\lambda=i
or simply
\partial\lambda=i
if
\lambda\in\obj(\mathcal{A}_{i})
. Since
\obj(\mathcal{A}_{i})\cap\obj(\mathcal{A}_{j})
consists of the zero objects of
\mathcal{A}
for
i\neq j
, the assignment
\partial
is single-valued on nonzero objects. We put
\homog(\mathcal{A})\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\in I}\obj(\mathcal{A}_{i})
.

We are interested in the case where
\mathcal{A}
is a monoidal category,
I
is a group and they are compatible in an appropriate sense.

.

Let
\Gamma

\Gamma

\mathcal{C}

\obj(\mathcal{C}_{s})\otimes\obj(\mathcal{C}_{t})\subset\obj(\mathcal{C}_{st})

A
\Gamma

F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
between
\Gamma

Next, we recall the notion of an
action
of a group on a (not necessarily monoidal) category.

Definition 2.2
.

An

is a monoidal functor from the monoidal category

of elements of

\mathcal{E}

When

is additive (resp. linear), we require that an action of a group values in the category of additive (resp. linear) endofunctors on
\mathcal{E}

More explicitly, an action

on

consists of an assignment

of endofunctors and natural isomorphisms
\chi^{\gamma}_{g,h}:\gamma(g)\gamma(h)\cong\gamma(gh)
for

and
\iota^{\gamma}:\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{E}}\cong\gamma(e)
with certain coherence relations, see e.g.
[
EGNO15
, Section 2.4]
. By the coherence theorem for monoidal functors (see e.g.
[
Gur13
, Subsection 2.3.3]
), a natural isomorphism obtained by vertical and horizontal compositions of
\chi^{\gamma}_{g,h}

\iota^{\gamma}
is canonical, see also
[

, Proposition 4.1]

Here, we introduce some graphical notations in the bicategory of categories. Let the natural isomorphisms
\chi^{\gamma}_{g,h}
and
\iota^{\gamma}

\gamma(g)\gamma(h)
to

and a half line with a circle from

For

and

Definition 2.3
.

A

is a tuple
(G,\Gamma)=(G,\Gamma,\rhd_{1},\rhd_{2})
of groups

\rhd_{1}:G\times\Gamma\to\Gamma

\displaystyle g\rhd_{1}(st)

\displaystyle=((t\rhd_{2}g)\rhd_{1}s)(g\rhd_{1}t)

\displaystyle s\rhd_{2}(gh)

\displaystyle=((h\rhd_{1}s)\rhd_{2}g)(s\rhd_{2}h)

for all
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\obj(\mathcal{D})
. The monoidal product is given by
h^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}_{\mu}\coloneqq(h^{\lambda_{1}}_{\mu}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\lambda_{2}})(\mathrm{id}_{\lambda_{1}}\otimes h^{\lambda_{2}}_{\mu})
for
(\lambda_{1},h^{\lambda_{1}}),(\lambda_{2},h^{\lambda_{2}})\in\obj(\rmz_{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{C}))
and
\mu\in\obj(\mathcal{D})
. The unit is given by
h^{\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}}}\coloneqq\mathrm{id}
.

We also recall that a
pivotal monoidal category
is a rigid monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
with a monoidal isomorphism
\delta^{\mathcal{C}}:\mathrm{id}\cong{}^{\vee\vee}
, where
∨
denotes the left dual, see e.g.
[
EGNO15
, Definition 4.7.7]
.

Example 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
\Gamma
-graded tensor category. If
\mathcal{C}
and
\Gamma
are finite
[
GNN09
, Theorem 3.3]
or
\mathcal{C}
is (not necessarily finite but) pivotal
[
TV13
, Theorem 4.1]
, then the
\Gamma
-graded center

\rmz_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{C})\coloneqq\rmz_{\mathcal{C}_{e}}(\mathcal{C})
of
\mathcal{C}
is a
\Gamma
-braided tensor category in the sense of Turaev.

Example 2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an additive monoidal category with an action
\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}
of a group
G
. Note that the assignment
g\mapsto\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g^{-1})
defines an action of
G^{\mathrm{op}}
on
\mathcal{C}
. Then, the
G
-equivariant center

\rmz^{G}(\mathcal{C})\coloneqq\rmz_{\mathcal{C}}(G^{\mathrm{op}}\ltimes\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{rev}}
of
\mathcal{C}
is a
G
-braided monoidal category in the sense of Turaev
[
BGM19
, Theorem 6.2]
[
She19
, Proposition 4.4.6]
.

Here, we do not give the details of these two constructions because we give their generalization in Section
4
.

3
Coherence theorems for crossed monoidal categories

It is known that a group action on a monoidal category can be
strictified
in an appropriate sense
[
Gal17
, Theorem 4.3]
(see also
[
BGM19
, Theorem 3.1]
). In this section, we generalize this fact in the setting of group-crossed monoidal categories in the sense of Natale (Definition
2.5
). As a consequence, we can see that isomorphisms obtained from the crossed structure are indeed canonical (Corollary
3.9
), and therefore we may suppress them.

First, we define the appropriate notion of an
equivalence
between group-crossed monoidal categories. For a monoidal functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
between monoidal categories, let
J^{F}
and
\varphi^{F}
denote the monoidal structure of
F
. Namely, for a monoidal functor
F
, we have the isomorphisms
J^{F}_{\lambda,\mu}:F(\lambda)F(\mu)\cong F(\lambda\mu)
for
\lambda,\mu\in\obj(\mathcal{C})
and
\varphi^{F}:\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{D}}\cong F(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}})
, see e.g.
[
EGNO15
, Definition 2.4.5]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
be
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal categories for a matched pair
(G,\Gamma)
of groups. A
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal functor
from
\mathcal{C}
to
\mathcal{D}
is the pair
F=(F,\eta^{F})
of a
\Gamma
-graded monoidal functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
and a family of natural isomorphisms
\eta^{F}=\{\eta^{F}_{g}:F\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)\cong\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)F\}_{g\in G}
such that

1.

\eta^{F}_{gh}(\mathrm{id}_{F}\ast\chi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}}_{g,h})=(\chi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}}_{g,h}\ast\mathrm{id}_{F})(\mathrm{id}_{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)}\ast\eta^{F}_{h})(\eta^{F}_{g}\ast\mathrm{id}_{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(h)})
for all
g,h\in G
.

2.

(\eta^{F}_{g})_{\lambda\mu}F(J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)}_{\lambda,\mu})J^{F}_{{}^{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g}\lambda,{}^{g}\mu}={}^{g}J^{F}_{\lambda,\mu}J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)}_{F(\lambda),F(\mu)}((\eta^{F}_{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g})_{\lambda}\otimes(\eta^{F}_{g})_{\mu})
for all
\lambda\in\obj(\mathcal{C}),\mu\in\homog(\mathcal{C})
and
g\in G
.

Let
F,F^{\prime}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal functors. A
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed natural transformation
from
F
to
F^{\prime}
is a monoidal natural transformation
\tau:F\to F^{\prime}
such that
(\mathrm{id}_{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)}\ast\tau)\eta_{g}^{F}=\eta^{F^{\prime}}_{g}(\tau\ast\mathrm{id}_{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)})
for all
g\in G
.

Remark 3.2
.

When
G
is trivial, the notion of a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed functor is equivalent to that of a
\Gamma
-graded functor (Definition
2.1
) by taking
\eta^{F}
to be the identity transformation. When
\Gamma
is trivial, the notion of a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed functor is equivalent to that of an additive
monoidal
G
-functor
, see e.g.
[
Oik24
, Definition 2.8]
. When
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
are
G
-crossed monoidal categories in the sense of Turaev (Definition
2.10
), the notion of a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed functor is equivalent to that of a
G
-crossed monoidal functor
, see
[
Oik24
, Definition 2.11]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(G,\Gamma)
be a matched pair of groups. Then,
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal categories,
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal functors and
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal natural transformations form a 2-category (i.e. strict bicategory).

Proof.

It is easy to see that for
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
,
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal functors from
\mathcal{C}
to
\mathcal{D}
and
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal natural transformations between them form a category, where the composition of morphisms is given by the vertical composition.

Let
F_{1}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
,
F_{2}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{E}
be
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed functors between
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal categories. By a standard argument,
F_{2}F_{1}
can be made into a
\Gamma
-graded monoidal functor.

We make
F_{2}F_{1}
into a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal functor by putting
\eta^{F_{2}F_{1}}_{g}\coloneqq(\eta^{F_{2}}_{g}\ast\mathrm{id}_{F_{1}})(\mathrm{id}_{F_{2}}\ast\eta_{g}^{F_{1}})
for
g\in G
. Indeed, axiom 1 in Definition
3.1
for
(F_{2}F_{1},\eta^{F_{2}F_{1}})
follows by a standard argument. Namely, for
g\in G
, let a crossing from
F\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)
to
\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)F
graphically denote
\eta_{g}^{F}
in the 2-category of categories. Also, let a fork from
\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(h)
to
\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(gh)
(resp. from
\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(h)
to
\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(gh)
) graphically denote
\chi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}}_{g,h}
(resp.
\chi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}}_{g,h}
). Then, axiom 1 in Definition
3.1
graphically means that a fork can pass under a crossing. Then, axiom 1 for
(F_{2}F_{1},\eta^{F_{2}F_{1}})
follows since a fork can pass under two crossings by a standard graphical calculation.

Axiom 2 follows since

\displaystyle(\eta^{F_{2}F_{1}}_{g})_{\lambda\mu}F_{2}F_{1}(J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)}_{\lambda,\mu})J^{F_{2}F_{1}}_{{}^{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g}\lambda,{}^{g}\mu}

\displaystyle=(\eta^{F_{2}}_{g})_{F_{1}(\lambda\mu)}F_{2}((\eta_{g}^{F_{1}})_{\lambda\mu}F_{1}(J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)}_{\lambda,\mu})J^{F_{1}}_{{}^{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g}\lambda,{}^{g}\mu})J^{F_{2}}_{F_{1}({}^{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g}\lambda),F_{1}({}^{g}\mu)}

\displaystyle=(\eta^{F_{2}}_{g})_{F_{1}(\lambda\mu)}F_{2}({}^{g}J^{F_{1}}_{\lambda,\mu}J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)}_{F_{1}(\lambda),F_{1}(\mu)}((\eta_{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g}^{F_{1}})_{\lambda}\otimes(\eta_{g}^{F_{1}})_{\mu}))J^{F_{2}}_{F_{1}({}^{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g}\lambda),F_{1}({}^{g}\mu)}

\displaystyle={}^{g}F_{2}(J^{F_{1}}_{\lambda,\mu})(\eta^{F_{2}}_{g})_{F_{1}(\lambda)F_{1}(\mu)}F_{2}(J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)}_{F_{1}(\lambda),F_{1}(\mu)})J^{F_{2}}_{{}^{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g}F_{1}(\lambda),{}^{g}F_{1}(\mu)}(F_{2}((\eta_{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g}^{F_{1}})_{\lambda})\otimes F_{2}((\eta_{g}^{F_{1}})_{\mu}))

\displaystyle={}^{g}J^{F_{2}F_{1}}_{\lambda,\mu}J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{E}}(g)}_{F_{2}F_{1}(\lambda),F_{2}F_{1}(\mu)}((\eta^{F_{2}F_{1}}_{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g})_{\lambda}\otimes(\eta^{F_{2}F_{1}}_{g})_{\mu})

for
\lambda\in\obj(\mathcal{C})
,
\mu\in\homog(\mathcal{C})
and
g\in G
by axiom 2 for
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
and the naturality of
\eta_{g}^{F_{2}}
and
J^{F_{2}}
. Note that we used that
F_{1}
is
\Gamma
-graded at the final equality.

It is easy to see that the horizontal composition of
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal transformation is again
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal, and therefore the composition of
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal functors is a bifunctor.

We have
\eta^{(F_{3}F_{2})F_{1}}=\eta^{F_{3}(F_{2}F_{1})}
for composable
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal functors
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}
by definition. Moreover, we have
\eta^{\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{D}}F}=\eta^{F\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}}}=\eta^{F}
for a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
by definition. Thus, we obtain a 2-category.
∎

Definition 3.4
.

A
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal equivalence
between
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal categories is an equivalence in the 2-category of
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal categories (see e.g.
[
Lei04
, Section 1.5]
). Equivalently, a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
between
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal categories is a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal equivalence
if there exists a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal functor
F^{-1}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}
with
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed natural isomorphisms
F^{-1}F\cong\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}}
and
FF^{-1}\cong\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{D}}
.

The following lemma is useful.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal (resp. tensor) functor between
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal (resp. multiring) categories. Then,
F
is a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal equivalence if and only if it is an equivalence as a functor.

Proof.

We show the “if” part since the “only if” part is trivial. Suppose
F
is an equivalence as a functor. Then, by a standard argument, an adjoint inverse
F^{-1}
of
F
is additive (resp. linear exact faithful). Moreover, by a standard argument (see e.g.
[
EGNO15
, Remark 2.4.10]
), we can make
F^{-1}
into a monoidal functor and obtain a monoidal evaluation map
\mathrm{ev}^{F}:F^{-1}F\cong\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{C}}
and a monoidal coevaluation map
\mathrm{coev}^{F}:\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{D}}\cong FF^{-1}
.

Since
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
are
\Gamma
-graded, for
\lambda\in\obj(\mathcal{D}_{s})
, we can consider the homogeneous decomposition
F^{-1}(\lambda)=\bigoplus_{t\in\Gamma}F^{-1}(\lambda)_{t}
of
F^{-1}(\lambda)
. Since
F
is
\Gamma
-graded and
FF^{-1}\cong\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{D}}
by assumption,
FF^{-1}(\lambda)_{t}
is a zero object unless
t=s
. Thus,
F^{-1}FF^{-1}(\lambda)_{t}
is a zero object unless
t=s
, and therefore
F^{-1}
is
\Gamma
-graded since
F^{-1}\cong F^{-1}FF^{-1}
.

We make
F^{-1}
into a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal (resp. tensor) functor. Put

\displaystyle\eta^{F^{-1}}_{g}\coloneqq(\mathrm{ev}^{F}\ast\mathrm{id}_{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)F^{-1}})(\mathrm{id}_{F^{-1}}\ast(\eta^{F}_{g})^{-1}\ast\mathrm{id}_{F^{-1}})(\mathrm{id}_{F^{-1}\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)}\ast\mathrm{coev}^{F})

for
g\in G
. We check the axioms in Definition
3.1
for
(F^{-1},\eta^{F^{-1}})
. Let us use the graphical notations in the proof of Proposition
3.3
. Then,
\eta^{F^{-1}}_{g}
is graphically represented by a rotated crossing, and therefore axiom 1 for
(F^{-1},\eta^{F^{-1}})
follows from axiom 1 and a conjugate equation for
F
.
Axiom 2 follows since

\displaystyle(\eta^{F^{-1}}_{g})_{\lambda\mu}F^{-1}(J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)}_{\lambda,\mu})J^{F^{-1}}_{{}^{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g}\lambda,{}^{g}\mu}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ev}^{F}_{{}^{g}F^{-1}(\lambda\mu)}F^{-1}((\eta^{F}_{g})^{-1}_{F^{-1}(\lambda\mu)}{}^{g}\mathrm{coev}^{F}_{\lambda\mu}J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)}_{\lambda,\mu})J^{F^{-1}}_{{}^{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g}\lambda,{}^{g}\mu}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ev}^{F}_{{}^{g}F^{-1}(\lambda\mu)}

\displaystyle\quad F^{-1}((\eta^{F}_{g})^{-1}_{F^{-1}(\lambda\mu)}{}^{g}F(J^{F^{-1}}_{\lambda,\mu}){}^{g}J^{F}_{F^{-1}(\lambda),F^{-1}(\mu)}J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)}_{FF^{-1}(\lambda),FF^{-1}(\mu)}({}^{g}\mathrm{coev}^{F}_{\lambda}\otimes{}^{g}\mathrm{coev}^{F}_{\mu}))J^{F^{-1}}_{{}^{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g}\lambda,{}^{g}\mu}

\displaystyle={}^{g}J^{F^{-1}}_{\lambda,\mu}\mathrm{ev}^{F}_{{}^{g}(F^{-1}(\lambda)F^{-1}(\mu))}

\displaystyle\quad F^{-1}((\eta^{F}_{g})^{-1}_{F^{-1}(\lambda)F^{-1}(\mu)}{}^{g}J^{F}_{F^{-1}(\lambda),F^{-1}(\mu)}J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)}_{FF^{-1}(\lambda),FF^{-1}(\mu)}({}^{g}\mathrm{coev}^{F}_{\lambda}\otimes{}^{g}\mathrm{coev}^{F}_{\mu}))J^{F^{-1}}_{{}^{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g}\lambda,{}^{g}\mu}

\displaystyle={}^{g}J^{F^{-1}}_{\lambda,\mu}\mathrm{ev}^{F}_{{}^{g}(F^{-1}(\lambda)F^{-1}(\mu))}F^{-1}F(J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)}_{F^{-1}(\lambda),F^{-1}(\mu)})F^{-1}(J^{F}_{{}^{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g}\lambda,{}^{g}\mu})

\displaystyle\quad F^{-1}((\eta^{F}_{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g})^{-1}_{F^{-1}(\lambda)}{}^{g}\mathrm{coev}^{F}_{\lambda}\otimes(\eta^{F}_{g})^{-1}_{F^{-1}(\mu)}{}^{g}\mathrm{coev}^{F}_{\mu})J^{F^{-1}}_{{}^{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g}\lambda,{}^{g}\mu}

\displaystyle={}^{g}J^{F^{-1}}_{\lambda,\mu}J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)}_{F^{-1}(\lambda),F^{-1}(\mu)}\mathrm{ev}^{F}_{{}^{g}F^{-1}(\lambda){}^{g}F^{-1}(\mu)}J^{F^{-1}F}_{{}^{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g}\lambda,{}^{g}\mu}

\displaystyle\quad(F^{-1}((\eta^{F}_{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g})^{-1}_{F^{-1}(\lambda)}{}^{g}\mathrm{coev}^{F}_{\lambda})\otimes F^{-1}((\eta^{F}_{g})^{-1}_{F^{-1}(\mu)}{}^{g}\mathrm{coev}^{F}_{\mu}))

\displaystyle={}^{g}J^{F^{-1}}_{\lambda,\mu}J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)}_{F^{-1}(\lambda),F^{-1}(\mu)}((\eta^{F^{-1}}_{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g})_{\lambda}\otimes(\eta^{F^{-1}}_{g})_{\mu})

for
\lambda\in\obj(\mathcal{D})
,
\mu\in\homog(\mathcal{D})
and
g\in G
by the naturality of
J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)}
,
J^{F^{-1}}
,
\mathrm{ev}^{F}
and
\mathrm{coev}^{F}
, axiom 2 for
F
and the monoidality of
\mathrm{ev}^{F}
and
\mathrm{coev}^{F}
. Thus,
(F^{-1},\eta^{F^{-1}})
is a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal (resp. tensor) functor.

Finally, recall that
\eta^{F^{-1}F}_{g}=(\eta^{F^{-1}}_{g}\ast\mathrm{id}_{F})(\mathrm{id}_{F^{-1}}\ast\eta_{g}^{F})
and
\eta^{FF^{-1}}_{g}=(\eta^{F}_{g}\ast\mathrm{id}_{F^{-1}})(\mathrm{id}_{F}\ast\eta_{g}^{F^{-1}})
for
g\in G
by the proof of Proposition
3.3
. Then,
\mathrm{ev}^{F}
and
\mathrm{coev}^{F}
are
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed natural isomorphisms since

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)}\ast\mathrm{ev}^{F})(\eta^{F^{-1}}_{g}\ast\mathrm{id}_{F})(\mathrm{id}_{F^{-1}}\ast\eta_{g}^{F})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ev}^{F}\ast\mathrm{id}_{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)}

\displaystyle(\eta^{F}_{g}\ast\mathrm{id}_{F^{-1}})(\mathrm{id}_{F}\ast\eta_{g}^{F^{-1}})(\mathrm{coev}^{F}\ast\mathrm{id}_{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{id}_{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)}\ast\mathrm{coev}^{F}

for
g\in G
by conjugate equations for
F
. Thus,
(F^{-1},\eta^{F^{-1}})
is a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal (resp. tensor) equivalence.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal category, and let
\mathcal{D}
be an additive monoidal category. Given an additive monoidal equivalence
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
, we can endow
\mathcal{D}
a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed structure so that
F
is a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal equivalence.

Proof.

First, we give a
\Gamma
-grading on
\mathcal{D}
. For
s\in\Gamma
, put
\mathcal{D}_{s}\coloneqq\mathcal{C}_{s}
, where
\mathcal{C}_{s}
is regarded as an additive subcategory of
\mathcal{D}
by using
F
. Then, since
\mathcal{C}
is
\Gamma
-graded and
F
is essentially surjective, any object of
\mathcal{D}
is a direct sum of homogeneous objects. Since
F
is moreover an equivalence,
\obj(\mathcal{D}_{s})\cap\obj(\mathcal{D}_{t})
consists of zero objects if
s\neq t
. Thus,
\mathcal{D}=\bigoplus_{s\in\Gamma}\mathcal{D}_{s}
. Note that
F
is
\Gamma
-graded by the definition of the
\Gamma
-grading on
\mathcal{D}
.

Next, we give a
G
-action on
\mathcal{D}
. Fix an adjoint inverse
F^{-1}
and put
\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)\coloneqq F\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)F^{-1}
for
g\in G
. By a standard argument,
\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}
defines a
G
-action on the category
\mathcal{D}
by putting
\chi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}}_{g,h}\coloneqq\mathrm{id}_{F\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)}\ast\mathrm{ev}^{F}\ast\mathrm{id}_{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(h)F^{-1}}
and
\iota^{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}}\coloneqq\mathrm{coev}^{F}
. Moreover, axiom 1 in Definition
2.5
is satisfied since
F^{-1}
is
\Gamma
-graded by the proof of Lemma
3.5
.

Then, we give a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed structure. Put
J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)}_{\lambda,\mu}\coloneqq F(J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)}_{F^{-1}(\lambda),F^{-1}(\mu)})
and
\varphi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)}\coloneqq F(\varphi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)})
for
\lambda\in\obj(\mathcal{D})
,
\mu\in\homog(\mathcal{D})
and
g\in G
. Note that
J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}(g)}_{\lambda,\mu}
is a well-defined isomorphism
{}^{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}g}\lambda{}^{g}\mu\cong{}^{g}(\lambda\mu)
since
F^{-1}
is
\Gamma
-graded. Then, axioms 2 and 3 in Definition
2.5
follow from those for
\mathcal{C}
. Thus,
\mathcal{D}
is a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal category.

Finally, we make
F
into a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed equivalence. By Lemma
3.5
, it suffices to make it into a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal functor. We have already seen that
F
is
\Gamma
-graded. Put
\eta^{F}_{g}\coloneqq\mathrm{id}_{F\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)}\ast(\mathrm{ev}^{F})^{-1}
for
g\in G
. It is easy to check that
(F,\eta^{F})
satisfies axiom 1 in Definition
3.1
. Axiom 2 follow from the definition of
J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}}
and
\varphi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{D}}}
and the monoidality of
\mathrm{ev}^{F}
.
∎

Definition 3.7
.

A
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
is
strictly
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed
if the action
\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}
is strict and
J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}}
and
\varphi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}}
in Definition
2.5
consist of identity maps.

The following theorem should be called the coherence theorem for crossed tensor categories.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(G,\Gamma)
be a matched pair of groups. Then, any
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal category is
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed equivalent to a strictly
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed strict monoidal category.

Proof.

Recall from
[
Gur13
, Subsection 2.2.3]
that for a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
, by letting
\st(\mathcal{C})
be the strict monoidal category whose objects are strings of objects of
\mathcal{C}
, we obtain a monoidal equivalence
\tilde{F}_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\simeq\st(\mathcal{C})
, which is defined by regarding the objects of
\mathcal{C}
as the strings of length 1. By definition, when
\mathcal{C}
is additive monoidal,
\st(\mathcal{C})
is again additive monoidal and
\tilde{F}_{\mathcal{C}}
is additive. Therefore,
\st(\mathcal{C})
can be made into a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal category so that
\tilde{F}_{\mathcal{C}}
is a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal equivalence by Lemma
3.6
. Thus, we may assume that
\mathcal{C}
is a strict monoidal category from the beginning.

For a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed strict monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
, let us define the category
\mathcal{C}(G)
as in
[
Gal17
, Section 4]
. Namely, an object of
\mathcal{C}(G)
is a pair
L=(L,\xi^{L})
of a family
L=\{L_{g}\}_{g\in G}
of objects of
\mathcal{C}
and a family of isomorphisms
\xi^{L}=\{\xi_{g,h}^{L}:{}^{g}L_{h}\cong L_{gh}\}_{g,h\in G}
such that
\xi_{gh,k}^{L}=\xi_{g,hk}^{L}{}^{g}\xi_{h,k}^{L}
and
\xi_{e,h}^{L}=\mathrm{id}_{L_{h}}
for all
g,h,k\in G
, where we suppressed some canonical morphisms (see Section
2
). A morphism
f:L\to L^{\prime}
is a family
f=\{f_{g}:L_{g}\to L_{g}^{\prime}\}_{g\in G}
of morphisms of
\mathcal{C}
such that
f_{gh}\xi^{L}_{g,h}=\xi^{L^{\prime}}_{g,h}{}^{g}f_{h}
for all
g,h\in G
.

By a standard argument,
\mathcal{C}(G)
is an additive category. As in
[
Gal17
, Section 4]
, we obtain a strict
G
-action
\gamma^{\mathcal{C}(G)}
on the additive category
\mathcal{C}(G)
by putting

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mathcal{C}(G)}(g)(L)\coloneqq(\{L_{hg}\}_{h},\{\xi^{L}_{k,hg}\}_{k,h})

for
(L,\xi^{L})\in\obj(\mathcal{C}(G))
and
g\in G
. Moreover,
\mathcal{C}(G)=\bigoplus_{s\in\Gamma}\mathcal{C}(G)_{s}
by putting

\displaystyle\obj(\mathcal{C}(G)_{s})\coloneqq\{L\in\obj(\mathcal{C}(G))\mid L_{e}\in\obj(\mathcal{C}(G)_{s})\}

for
s\in\Gamma
as in the proof of
[
Gal17
, Corollary 5.3]
. For any
g\in G
and
s\in\Gamma
,
{}^{g}\obj(\mathcal{C}(G)_{s})\subset\obj(\mathcal{C}(G)_{g\rhd_{1}s})
since for
L\in\obj(\mathcal{C}(G)_{s})
we have
({}^{g}L)_{e}=L_{g}\cong{}^{g}L_{e}\in\obj(\mathcal{C}_{g\rhd_{1}s})
.

We make
\mathcal{C}(G)
into a monoidal category by putting

\displaystyle LM\coloneqq(\{L_{\partial M\rhd_{2}h}M_{h}\}_{h},\{(\xi^{L}_{(h\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}g,\partial M\rhd_{2}h}\otimes\xi^{M}_{g,h})J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)-1}_{L_{\partial M\rhd_{2}h},M_{h}}\}_{g,h})

for
L\in\obj(\mathcal{C}(G))
and
M\in\homog(\mathcal{C}(G))
. Note that
\xi^{LM}_{g,h}
is indeed an isomorphism
{}^{g}(LM)_{h}\cong(LM)_{gh}
by the matching equation
((h\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}g)(\partial M\rhd_{2}h)=\partial M\rhd_{2}(gh)
. By similar matching equations, we have

\displaystyle\xi^{L}_{(k\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}(gh),\partial M\rhd_{2}k}

\displaystyle=\xi^{L}_{((hk\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}g)((k\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}h),\partial M\rhd_{2}k}

\displaystyle=\xi^{L}_{(hk\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}g,((k\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}h)(\partial M\rhd_{2}k)}{}^{(hk\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}g}\xi^{L}_{(k\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}h,\partial M\rhd_{2}k}

\displaystyle=\xi^{L}_{(hk\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}g,\partial M\rhd_{2}hk}{}^{(hk\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}g}\xi^{L}_{(k\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}h,\partial M\rhd_{2}k}

and therefore

\displaystyle\xi^{LM}_{gh,k}

\displaystyle=(\xi^{L}_{(hk\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}g,\partial M\rhd_{2}hk}{}^{(hk\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}g}\xi^{L}_{(k\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}h,\partial M\rhd_{2}k}\otimes\xi^{M}_{g,hk}{}^{g}\xi^{M}_{h,k})J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(gh)-1}_{L_{\partial M\rhd_{2}k},M_{k}}

\displaystyle=(\xi^{L}_{(hk\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}g,\partial M\rhd_{2}hk}{}^{(hk\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}g}\xi^{L}_{(k\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}h,\partial M\rhd_{2}k}\otimes\xi^{M}_{g,hk}{}^{g}\xi^{M}_{h,k})

\displaystyle\qquad J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)-1}_{{}^{(k\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}h}L_{\partial M\rhd_{2}k},{}^{h}M_{k}}{}^{g}J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(h)-1}_{L_{\partial M\rhd_{2}k},M_{k}}

\displaystyle=(\xi^{L}_{(hk\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}g,\partial M\rhd_{2}hk}\otimes\xi^{M}_{g,hk})J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)-1}_{L_{\partial M\rhd_{2}hk},M_{hk}}{}^{g}((\xi^{L}_{(k\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}h,\partial M\rhd_{2}k}\otimes\xi^{M}_{h,k})J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(h)-1}_{L_{\partial M\rhd_{2}k},M_{k}})

\displaystyle=\xi^{LM}_{g,hk}{}^{g}\xi^{LM}_{h,k}

for
g,h,k\in G
by axiom 3 in Definition
2.5
and the naturality of
J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)}
. We also have
\xi^{LM}_{e,h}=(\xi^{L}_{e,\partial M\rhd_{2}h}\otimes\xi^{M}_{e,h})=\mathrm{id}
for
g\in G
by axiom 3 in Definition
2.5
and
(h\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}e=e
. Thus,
LM
is well-defined. Moreover, for morphisms
f:L\to L^{\prime}
and
\tilde{f}:M\to M^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{C}(G)
, define a morphism
f\otimes\tilde{f}:LM\to L^{\prime}M^{\prime}
by putting
(f\otimes\tilde{f})_{g}\coloneqq f_{\partial M\rhd_{2}g}\otimes\tilde{f}_{g}
for
g\in G
. Then,

\displaystyle(f\otimes\tilde{f})_{gh}\xi^{LM}_{g,h}

\displaystyle=(f_{\partial M\rhd_{2}gh}\xi^{L}_{(h\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}g,\partial M\rhd_{2}h}\otimes\tilde{f}_{gh}\xi^{M}_{g,h})J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)-1}_{L_{\partial M\rhd_{2}h,M_{h}}}

\displaystyle=(\xi^{L^{\prime}}_{(h\rhd_{1}\partial M^{\prime})\rhd_{2}g,\partial M^{\prime}\rhd_{2}h}{}^{(h\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}g}f_{h}\otimes\xi^{M^{\prime}}_{g,h}{}^{g}\tilde{f}_{h})J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)-1}_{L_{\partial M\rhd_{2}h,M_{h}}}

\displaystyle=(\xi^{L^{\prime}}_{(h\rhd_{1}\partial M^{\prime})\rhd_{2}g,\partial M^{\prime}\rhd_{2}h}\otimes\xi^{M^{\prime}}_{g,h})J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)-1}_{L^{\prime}_{\partial M^{\prime}\rhd_{2}h,M^{\prime}_{h}}}{}^{g}(f\otimes\tilde{f})

for
g,h\in G
by the naturality of
J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)}
. Note that we may assume
\partial M=\partial M^{\prime}
since otherwise
\tilde{f}=0
. Therefore,
f\otimes\tilde{f}
is well-defined. Thus, the product
\otimes
defines a biadditive bifunctor
\mathcal{C}(G)\times\mathcal{C}(G)\to\mathcal{C}(G)
. Moreover, it makes
\mathcal{C}(G)
into a
\Gamma
-graded strict monoidal category with unit
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}(G)}\coloneqq(\{\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\}_{h},\{\varphi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)-1}\}_{g,h})
. Indeed,

\displaystyle((LM)N)_{h}=L_{\partial M\rhd_{2}(\partial N\rhd_{2}h)}M_{\partial N\rhd_{2}h}N_{h}=L_{\partial(MN)\rhd_{2}h}M_{\partial N\rhd_{2}h}N_{h}=(L(MN))_{h}

for
L\in\obj(\mathcal{C}(G))
,
M,N\in\homog(\mathcal{C}(G))
and
h\in G
since
\rhd_{2}
is an action and
\partial M\partial N=\partial M_{e}\partial N_{e}=\partial(M_{e}N_{e})=\partial(M_{\partial N\rhd_{2}e}N_{e})=\partial(MN)
. Moreover, since

\displaystyle\xi^{(LM)N}_{g,h}

\displaystyle=(\xi^{LM}_{(h\rhd_{1}\partial N)\rhd_{2}g,\partial N\rhd_{2}h}\otimes\xi^{N}_{g,h})J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)-1}_{(LM)_{\partial N\rhd_{2}h},N_{h}}

\displaystyle=(\xi^{L}_{((\partial N\rhd_{2}h)\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}((h\rhd_{1}\partial N)\rhd_{2}g),\partial M\partial N\rhd_{2}h}\otimes\xi^{M}_{(h\rhd_{1}\partial N)\rhd_{2}g,\partial N\rhd_{2}h}\otimes\xi^{N}_{g,h})

\displaystyle\qquad(J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)-1}_{L_{\partial M\partial N\rhd_{2}h},M_{\partial N\rhd_{2}h}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{{}^{g}N_{h}})J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)-1}_{L_{\partial M\partial N\rhd_{2}h}M_{\partial N\rhd_{2}h},N_{h}}

\displaystyle=(\xi^{L}_{(h\rhd_{1}\partial M\partial N)\rhd_{2}g,\partial M\partial N\rhd_{2}h}\otimes\xi^{M}_{(h\rhd_{1}\partial N)\rhd_{2}g,\partial N\rhd_{2}h}\otimes\xi^{N}_{g,h})

\displaystyle\qquad(J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)-1}_{L_{\partial M\partial N\rhd_{2}h},M_{\partial N\rhd_{2}h}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{{}^{g}N_{h}})J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)-1}_{L_{\partial M\partial N\rhd_{2}h}M_{\partial N\rhd_{2}h},N_{h}}

\displaystyle\xi^{L(MN)}_{g,h}

\displaystyle=(\xi^{L}_{(h\rhd_{1}\partial M\partial N)\rhd_{2}g,\partial M\partial N\rhd_{2}h}\otimes\xi^{MN}_{g,h})J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)-1}_{L_{\partial M\partial N\rhd_{2}h},(MN)_{h}}

\displaystyle=(\xi^{L}_{(h\rhd_{1}\partial M\partial N)\rhd_{2}g,\partial M\partial N\rhd_{2}h}\otimes\xi^{M}_{(h\rhd_{1}\partial N)\rhd_{2}g,\partial N\rhd_{2}h}\otimes\xi^{N}_{g,h})

\displaystyle\qquad(\mathrm{id}_{{}^{(h\rhd_{1}\partial M\partial N)\rhd_{2}g}L_{\partial M\partial N\rhd_{2}h}}\otimes J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)-1}_{M_{\partial N\rhd_{2}h},N_{h}})J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)-1}_{L_{\partial M\partial N\rhd_{2}h},M_{\partial N\rhd_{2}h}N_{h}}

for
g,h\in G
by the matching relation
h\rhd_{1}\partial M\partial N=((\partial N\rhd_{2}h)\rhd_{1}\partial M)(h\rhd_{1}\partial N)
, we obtain
\xi^{(LM)N}=\xi^{L(MN)}
by axiom 2 in Definition
2.5
. The unit
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}(G)}
is indeed an object of
\mathcal{C}(G)
by axiom 3 in Definition
2.5
. We have
(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}(G)}M)_{h}=M_{h}=(M\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}(G)})_{h}
since
\partial\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}}=e
. Moreover,
\xi^{\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}(G)}M}=\xi^{M\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}(G)}}=\xi^{M}
follows from axiom 2 in Definition
2.5
.

For
L\in\obj(\mathcal{C}(G))
,
M\in\homog(\mathcal{C}(G))
and
g,h\in G
, the matching relation
\partial M\rhd_{2}hg=((g\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}h)(\partial M\rhd_{2}g)
, we have

\displaystyle({}^{g}(LM))_{h}

\displaystyle=(LM)_{hg}=L_{\partial M\rhd_{2}hg}M_{hg}=L_{((g\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}h)(\partial M\rhd_{2}g)}M_{hg}

\displaystyle=({}^{\partial M\rhd_{2}g}L)_{(g\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}h}({}^{g}M)_{h}=({}^{\partial M\rhd_{2}g}L)_{\partial{}^{g}M\rhd_{2}h}({}^{g}M)_{h}

\displaystyle=({}^{\partial M\rhd_{2}g}L{}^{g}M)_{h}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\xi^{{}^{\partial M\rhd_{2}g}L{}^{g}M}_{k,h}

\displaystyle=(\xi^{{}^{\partial M\rhd_{2}g}L}_{(h\rhd_{1}\partial{}^{g}M)\rhd_{2}k,\partial{}^{g}M\rhd_{2}h}\otimes\xi^{{}^{g}M}_{k,h})J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(k)-1}_{({}^{\partial M\rhd_{2}g}L)_{\partial{}^{g}M\rhd_{2}h},({}^{g}M)_{h}}

\displaystyle=(\xi^{{}^{\partial M\rhd_{2}g}L}_{(hg\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}k,(g\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}h}\otimes\xi^{{}^{g}M}_{k,h})J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(k)-1}_{({}^{\partial M\rhd_{2}g}L)_{(g\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}h},({}^{g}M)_{h}}

\displaystyle=(\xi^{L}_{(hg\rhd_{1}\partial M)\rhd_{2}k,\partial M\rhd_{2}hg}\otimes\xi^{M}_{k,hg})J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(k)-1}_{L_{\partial M\rhd_{2}hg},M_{hg}}

\displaystyle=\xi^{LM}_{k,hg}=\xi^{{}^{g}(LM)}_{k,h}

for
g,h,k\in G
. Therefore,
{}^{g}(LM)={}^{\partial M\rhd_{2}g}L{}^{g}M
. We also have
{}^{g}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}(G)}=\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}(G)}
by definition. Thus, the
G
-action
\gamma^{\mathcal{C}(G)}
and the
\Gamma
-grading
\mathcal{C}(G)=\bigoplus_{s\in\Gamma}\mathcal{C}(G)_{s}
make
\mathcal{C}(G)
into a strictly
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed strict monoidal category.

The functor
I_{\mathcal{C}}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}(G);\lambda\mapsto(\{{}^{h}\lambda\}_{h},\{(\chi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}}_{g,h})_{\lambda}\}_{g,h})
is an equivalence of categories as in the proof of
[
Gal17
, Proposition 4.1]
. It is also
\Gamma
-graded by definition. We can make it into a monoidal functor by putting
J^{I_{\mathcal{C}}}_{\lambda,\mu}\coloneqq\{J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(h)}_{\lambda,\mu}\}_{h}
for
\lambda\in\obj(\mathcal{C})
and
\mu\in\homog(\mathcal{C})
and
\varphi^{I_{\mathcal{C}}}\coloneqq\{\varphi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(h)}\}_{h}
. Indeed, they are morphisms of
\mathcal{C}(G)
by axiom 3 in Definition
2.5
since
\xi^{I_{\mathcal{C}}(\lambda)I_{\mathcal{C}}(\mu)}_{g,h}=J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)-1}_{{}^{\partial\mu\rhd_{2}h}\lambda,{}^{h}\mu}
and
\xi^{\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}(G)}}_{g,h}=\varphi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)-1}
by definition. They satisfy the coherence relations for a monoidal functor by axiom 2 in Definition
2.5
.

Moreover, we can make
I_{\mathcal{C}}
into a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal functor and therefore prove the theorem by Lemma
3.5
. Indeed, since

\displaystyle I_{\mathcal{C}}({}^{g}\lambda)

\displaystyle=(\{{}^{h}({}^{g}\lambda)\}_{h},\{(\chi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}}_{k,h})_{{}^{g}\lambda}\}_{k,h})

\displaystyle{}^{g}I_{\mathcal{C}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=(\{{}^{hg}\lambda\}_{h},\{(\chi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}}_{k,hg})_{\lambda}\}_{k,h})

for
\lambda\in\obj(\mathcal{C})
and
g\in G
by definition, we can put
(\eta^{I_{\mathcal{C}}}_{g})_{\lambda}\coloneqq\{(\chi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}}_{h,g})_{\lambda}\}_{h}:I_{\mathcal{C}}({}^{g}\lambda)\cong{}^{g}I_{\mathcal{C}}(\lambda)
, which is indeed a morphism of
\mathcal{C}(G)
by coherence. Then, the first two axioms in Definition
3.1
are satisfied by coherence. The remaining two axioms follow by coherence from the definition of
J^{I_{\mathcal{C}}}
and
\varphi^{I_{\mathcal{C}}}
, respectively, and axiom 3 in Definition
2.5
. Thus,
(I_{\mathcal{C}},\eta^{I_{\mathcal{C}}})
is a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal functor.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal category. Define a set
W=\sqcup_{n\geq 0}W_{n}
of words recursively by the following rules:
\mathbf{1}\in W_{0}
,
-\in W_{1}
,
\otimes\in W_{2}
,
\gamma(g)\in W_{1}
for
g\in G
and
w^{\prime}((w_{i})_{i})\in W_{\sum_{i}n_{i}}
for
w^{\prime}\in W_{n}
and a tuple
(w_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
with
w_{i}\in W_{n_{i}}
. For
w\in W_{n}
, let
w
again denote the functor
\mathcal{C}^{n}\to\mathcal{C}
corresponding to
w
, where
\mathcal{C}^{0}
denotes the category with only one object and its identity morphism. Define a set
I
of morphisms recursively by the following rules: the components of
a^{\mathcal{C}},l^{\mathcal{C}},r^{\mathcal{C}},J^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)}
,
\chi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}}_{g,h}
and
\iota^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}}
are in
I
for any
g,h\in G
,
\varphi^{\gamma^{\mathcal{C}}(g)}\in I
for any
g\in G
,
f^{-1}\in I
for any
f\in I
,
f\circ f^{\prime}\in I
for any composable
f,f^{\prime}\in I
and
w((f_{i})_{i})
for any
(f_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\subset I
and
w\in W_{n}
. Then, for any
w,w^{\prime}\in W_{n}
and objects
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of
\mathcal{C}
, a morphism
w((\lambda_{i})_{i})\to w^{\prime}((\lambda_{i})_{i})
in
I
is unique if it exists.

Proof.

By theorem
3.8
, there exists a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal equivalence
F
from
\mathcal{C}
to a strictly
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed strict monoidal category
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
. For
w\in W_{n}
and
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset\obj(\mathcal{C})
, we define a natural isomorphism
C^{w}:Fw\cong\tilde{w}F^{n}
, where
\tilde{w}
denotes the functor
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{n}\to\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
corresponding to
w
, recursively by the following rules:
C^{\mathbf{1}}\coloneqq\varphi^{F-1}
,
C^{\otimes}\coloneqq J^{F-1}
,
C^{\gamma(g)}\coloneqq\eta^{F}_{g}
for

and
C^{v((w_{i})_{i})}\coloneqq(\mathrm{id}_{\tilde{v}}\ast(C^{w_{i}})_{i})(C^{v}\ast\mathrm{id}_{(w_{i})_{i}})
for

.

We show that

for any

in

, which uniquely determines

. It suffices to prove this when

is a generator of

. The statement for

follows since

is monoidal, which is standard. The statement for

∎

4
Crossed center

In this section, we give the group-crossed version of the Drinfeld center construction (Theorem
4.8
) by generalizing the graded center construction (see Example
2.13
) by Turaev and Virelizier
[
TV13
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal category. Then, the
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed center

\rmz^{G}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{C})
of
\mathcal{C}
is defined as follows. For
g\in G
, an object of
\rmz^{G}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{C})_{g}
is the pair
(\lambda,h^{\lambda})
of
\lambda\in\obj(\mathcal{C})
and a natural isomorphism
h^{\lambda}=\{h^{\lambda}_{\nu}:\lambda\nu\cong{}^{g}\nu\lambda\}_{\nu\in\obj(\mathcal{C}_{e})}
such that
h^{\lambda}_{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}}=(\mathrm{id}_{{}^{g}\nu_{1}}\otimes h^{\lambda}_{\nu_{2}})(h^{\lambda}_{\nu_{1}}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\nu_{2}})
for all
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\in\obj(\mathcal{C}_{e})
. A morphism
f:(\lambda,h^{\lambda})\to(\mu,h^{\mu})
of
\rmz^{G}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{C})_{g}
is a morphism
f:\lambda\to\mu
of
\mathcal{C}
such that
h^{\mu}_{\nu}(f\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\nu})=(\mathrm{id}_{{}^{g}\nu}\otimes f)h^{\lambda}_{\nu}
for all
\nu\in\obj(\mathcal{C}_{e})
. Then,
\rmz^{G}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{C})_{g}
is an additive category, where the composition of morphisms and identity morphisms are given by those of
\mathcal{C}
. The category
\rmz^{G}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{C})
is defined to be
\bigoplus_{g\in G}\rmz^{G}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{C})_{g}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal category. Then, the
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed center
\rmz^{G}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{C})
of
\mathcal{C}
becomes a
G\times\Gamma
-graded monoidal category by putting

\displaystyle\obj(\rmz^{G}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{C})_{(g,s)})\coloneqq\{(\lambda,h^{\lambda})\in\obj(\rmz^{G}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{C})_{g})\mid\lambda\in\obj(\mathcal{C}_{s})\}

for
(g,s)\in G\times\Gamma
,
(\lambda,h^{\lambda})\otimes(\mu,h^{\mu})\coloneqq(\lambda\mu,(h^{\lambda}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\mathrm{id}\otimes h^{\mu}))
for
(\lambda,h^{\lambda}),(\mu,h^{\mu})\in\homog(\rmz^{G}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{C}))
and
\mathbf{1}_{\rmz^{G}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{C})}\coloneqq(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{C}},\mathrm{id})
.

Proof.

By coherence (Corollary
3.9
), we can do the same graphical calculation as that for the ordinary center of
\mathcal{C}
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal category. If
\lambda\in\homog(\mathcal{C})
has a left dual
\lambda^{\vee}
, then, for any
g\in G
,
{}^{g}\lambda
has a left dual
{}^{\partial\lambda\rhd_{2}g}(\lambda^{\vee})
with evaluation and coevaluation maps given by
\mathrm{ev}_{{}^{g}\lambda}\coloneqq{}^{g}\mathrm{ev}_{\lambda}
and
\mathrm{coev}_{{}^{g}\lambda}\coloneqq{}^{\partial\lambda\rhd_{2}g}\mathrm{coev}_{\lambda}
, where we suppressed canonical isomorphisms in Corollary
3.9
. We also have a similar statement for right duals.

Proof.

We only show the statement for left duals. Note that the target of
\mathrm{coev}_{{}^{g}\lambda}
is
{}^{\partial\lambda\rhd_{2}g}(\lambda\lambda^{\vee})={}^{\partial\lambda^{\vee}\rhd_{2}(\partial\lambda\rhd_{2}g)}\lambda{}^{\partial\lambda\rhd_{2}g}(\lambda^{\vee})={}^{g}\lambda{}^{\partial\lambda\rhd_{2}g}(\lambda^{\vee})
. Then, we have

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{{}^{g}\lambda}\otimes{}^{g}\mathrm{ev}_{\lambda})({}^{\partial\lambda\rhd_{2}g}\mathrm{coev}_{\lambda}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{{}^{g}\lambda})

\displaystyle=({}^{e\rhd_{2}g}\mathrm{id}_{\lambda}\otimes{}^{g}\mathrm{ev}_{\lambda})({}^{\partial\lambda\rhd_{2}g}\mathrm{coev}_{\lambda}\otimes{}^{g}\mathrm{id}_{\lambda})

\displaystyle={}^{g}((\mathrm{id}_{\lambda}\otimes\mathrm{ev}_{\lambda})(\mathrm{coev}_{\lambda}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\lambda}))=\mathrm{id}_{{}^{g}\lambda}

and

\displaystyle({}^{g}\mathrm{ev}_{\lambda}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{{}^{\partial\lambda\rhd_{2}g}(\lambda^{\vee})})(\mathrm{id}_{{}^{\partial\lambda\rhd_{2}g}(\lambda^{\vee})}{}^{\partial\lambda\rhd_{2}g}\mathrm{coev}_{\lambda})

\displaystyle=({}^{\partial\lambda^{\vee}\rhd_{2}(\partial\lambda\rhd_{2}g)}\mathrm{ev}_{\lambda}\otimes{}^{\partial\lambda\rhd_{2}g}\mathrm{id}_{\lambda^{\vee}})({}^{e\rhd_{2}(\partial\lambda\rhd_{2}g)}\mathrm{id}_{\lambda^{\vee}}{}^{\partial\lambda\rhd_{2}g}\mathrm{coev}_{\lambda})

\displaystyle={}^{\partial\lambda\rhd_{2}g}((\mathrm{ev}_{\lambda}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\lambda^{\vee}})(\mathrm{id}_{\lambda^{\vee}}\otimes\mathrm{coev}_{\lambda}))=\mathrm{id}_{{}^{\partial\lambda\rhd_{2}g}\lambda^{\vee}}

by coherence (Corollary
3.9
).
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a
(G,\Gamma)
-crossed monoidal category. If
\mathcal{C}
is rigid, then so is
\rmz^{G}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

We can rotate a crossing and do the usual graphical calculations by Lemma
4.3
and coherence (Corollary
3.9

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate global existence of large solutions for the parabolic-elliptic Keller–Segel system in the homogeneous Besov type spaces. A class of initial data was presented, generating a global smooth solution although the B ˙ ∞ , ∞ − 2 \dot{B}^{-2}_{\infty,\infty} -norm of the initial data may be chosen arbitrarily large.



Keywords
: Keller–Segel system; large solutions; global existence; Besov spaces.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the global existence of large solutions for the Cauchy problem of the parabolic-elliptic Keller–Segel system:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)=0,\\
-\Delta v=u,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where two unknown functions
u
and
v
representing the cell density and the concentration of the chemical attractant, respectively, and both are unknown functions of the space variable
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and of the time variable
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

The system (
1.1
) is a simplified version of the mathematical model of chemotaxis, which was first introduced by E.F. Keller and L.A. Segel
[
14
]
in 1970. This basic model
was used to describe the collective movement of bacteria possibly leading to cell aggregation by chemotactic
effect. It is also connected with astrophysical models of
gravitational self-interaction of massive particles in a cloud or a nebula, see
[
4
,
3
]
. We refer to
[
2
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
12
,
15
,
16
,
18
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
for more details concerning about the existence and nonexistence (blow-up) of global in time solutions to the different Keller–Segel
models studied since the 1970s.

Our aim here is to show the global existence of large solutions of the system (
1.1
) in critical Besov type spaces. Karch
[
13
]
established global well-posedness with small initial data in critical Besov space
\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\frac{d}{2}<p<d
. Subsequently, Iwabuchi
[
10
]
proved local well-posedness and global well-posedness for small initial data in critical Besov spaces
\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and Fourier–Herz spaces
\dot{\mathcal{B}}^{-2}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
1\leq p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, moreover, the ill-posedness is also established in critical Besov spaces
\dot{B}^{-2}_{\infty,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
2<q\leq\infty
. Finally, Iwabuchi–Nakamura
[
11
]
showed global existence of solutions for small initial data in
\dot{F}^{-2}_{\infty,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=BMO^{-2}
through the Triebel–Lizorkin spaces, which combining the well-posed results in
[
10
]
, we know that, the simplified Keller–Segel system (
1.1
) is well-posed in
\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\dot{F}^{-2}_{\infty,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\max\{1,\frac{d}{2}\}<p<\infty
, but ill-posed in
\dot{B}^{-2}_{\infty,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
2<q\leq\infty
. For more well-posed and ill-posed results to more general chemotaxis models, we refer the readers to see
[
6
,
17
,
20
,
21
,
26
]
.

Before we state the main results, let us first introduce the definition of the homogeneous Besov spaces.

Definition 1.1

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a positive radial function such that
\varphi
is supported in the shell
\mathcal{C}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \frac{3}{4}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{8}{3}\}
, and

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1\ \ \text{for}\ \ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\}.

Let
h=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\varphi
. Then for any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we define the homogeneous dyadic block
\Delta_{j}
as

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}f(x):=2^{dj}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(2^{j}y)f(x-y)dy,

and for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
, we define the homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\Big\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})/\mathcal{P}:\ \ \|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}}<\infty\Big\},

\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}}:=\begin{cases}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{srj}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}}^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\ \ &\text{for}\ \ 1\leq r<\infty,\\
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{sj}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}}\ \ &\text{for}\ \ r=\infty,\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{P}
denotes the set of all polynomials.

Next, we recall the definition of the so-called Chemin–Lerner mixed time-space spaces.

Definition 1.2

For
0<T\leq\infty
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and

1\leq p,r,\rho\leq\infty
. We define the mixed time-space
\mathcal{L}^{\rho}(0,T;\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

as the completion of
\mathcal{C}([0,T],\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
by the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\rho}_{T}(\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r})}:=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{srj}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|\Delta_{j}f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{p}}^{\rho}dt\right)^{\frac{r}{\rho}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}<\infty

with the usual change if
\rho=\infty

or
r=\infty
.

Now we are ready to state our main results. The first result is a new global existence result, under a nonlinear smallness assumption on the initial data.

Theorem 1.3

For any
u_{0}\in\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
1<p<\infty
, there exist two
positive constants
c_{0}
and
C_{0}
such that if
the initial data
u_{0}
satisfies

\displaystyle C_{0}\|e^{t\Delta}u_{0}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}e^{t\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}\exp\{C_{0}\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}\}\leq c_{0},

(1.2)

then the system (
1.1
) admits a unique global
solution
u
associated with
u_{0}
, satisfying

u\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})).

(1.3)

Moreover, there exists a positive constant
\kappa
such that

\|u-e^{t\Delta}u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}+\kappa\|u-e^{t\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}\leq\frac{c_{0}}{2}.

(1.4)

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is given in Section 2 below. It consists in writing the solution
u
(which exists for a short
time at least), as
u=e^{t\Delta}u_{0}+\bar{u}
and in proving a global existence result for the perturbed Keller–Segel system satisfied by
\bar{u}
, under assumption (
1.2
). Moreover, the second result of this paper is to exhibit examples of applications of Theorem
1.3
which go beyond the
assumption of smallness of
\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2}_{\infty,\infty}}
-norm. For the sake of simplicity, we take
d=3
to construct such an initial data.

Theorem 1.4

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be a given function, and consider two real numbers
\varepsilon
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Define

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x):=\frac{(-\log\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{5}}}{\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}}\cos(\frac{x_{3}}{\varepsilon})\phi(x_{1},\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}},x_{3}).

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for
\varepsilon
small enough, the smooth function
u_{0,\varepsilon}:=\nabla\cdot w_{0,\varepsilon}
satisfies

\|u_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2}_{\infty,\infty}}=\|\nabla\cdot w_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2}_{\infty,\infty}}\approx\|w_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-1}_{\infty,\infty}}\geq C^{-1}(-\log\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{5}},

(1.5)

where
w_{0,\varepsilon}
is defined as

w_{0,\varepsilon}:=(\partial_{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon},\partial_{1}\varphi_{\varepsilon},0).

Moreover, we have

\|e^{t\Delta}u_{0,\varepsilon}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}e^{t\Delta}u_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}(-\log\varepsilon)^{\frac{2}{5}}.

(1.6)

Notice that, for initial data
u_{0,\varepsilon}
given by Theorem
1.4
, we can easily calculate that

\displaystyle u_{0,\varepsilon}=\nabla\cdot w_{0,\varepsilon}=\frac{2}{\varepsilon}(-\log\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{5}}\cos(\frac{x_{3}}{\varepsilon})\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\phi(x_{1}\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}},x_{3}),

and

\displaystyle\|u_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}\leq C\varepsilon^{\alpha}(-\log\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{5}}\|\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\phi\|_{L^{1}}.

Based on this fact, we know that, although the
\dot{B}^{-2}_{\infty,\infty}
-norm of
u_{0,\varepsilon}
can be arbitrary large, but the
L^{1}
-norm of
u_{0,\varepsilon}
tends to 0 as
\varepsilon
tends to
0
. Hence, such initial data given by Theorem
1.4
can not lead to blow-up of solutions for the system (
1.1
).

The proof of Theorem
1.4
will be given in Section 3. Throughout the paper,
C
stands for a real positive constant which may be different in each occurrence.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Let us denote
u_{L}:=e^{t\Delta}u_{0}
,
\bar{u}:=u-u_{L}
. Then we can rewrite system (
1.1
) as

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\bar{u}-\Delta\bar{u}=-\nabla\cdot[(u_{L}+\bar{u})\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}(u_{L}+\bar{u})],\\
\bar{u}(x,0)=0.\end{cases}

(2.1)

Moreover, by using the Duhamel’s principle, the system (
2.1
) can be further reduced into the following equivalent integral equations:

\bar{u}(t)=-\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\nabla\cdot[(u_{L}+\bar{u})\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}(u_{L}+\bar{u})]ds.

(2.2)

Since
u_{L}
is a solution of the heat equation with initial data
u_{0}
, one can easily obtain that for any
0<t\leq\infty
,

\|u_{L}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}.

(2.3)

Before giving the desired estimate of
\bar{u}
, we denote
f(t):=\|u_{L}(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}
,
and for any positive real number
\lambda
, we introduce the weighted function

\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}(x,t):=\bar{u}(x,t)\exp\{-\lambda\int_{0}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau\}
.
Back to (
2.2
), it holds that

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}e^{-\lambda\int_{0}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau}\nabla\cdot(u_{L}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}u_{L})ds

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}e^{-\lambda\int_{s}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau}\nabla\cdot\big[u_{L}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}+\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}u_{L}+\bar{u}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\big]ds.

We know from the solvability of heat equation in the framework of the homogeneous Besov spaces that there exists a positive constant
\kappa
such that

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}+\kappa\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}\leq\|e^{-\lambda\int_{0}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau}u_{L}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}u_{L}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}

\displaystyle+\|e^{-\lambda\int_{s}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau}\big[u_{L}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}+\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}u_{L}+\bar{u}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\big]\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}

\displaystyle:=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.

(2.4)

For
I_{1}
, one obtains that

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq C\|u_{L}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}u_{L}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}.

(2.5)

For
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
, by using the following symmetric structure:

u\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}v+v\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}u=-\nabla\cdot\big(\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}u\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}v\big),

(2.6)

we can deduce that

\displaystyle I_{2}+I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda\int_{s}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau}\|\nabla\cdot(\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}u_{L}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\bar{u}_{\lambda,f})\|_{\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda\int_{s}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau}\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}u_{L}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda\int_{s}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau}\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}u_{L}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda\int_{s}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau}\|u_{L}\|_{\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda\int_{s}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau}\|u_{L}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{L}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda\int_{s}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau}\big(\varepsilon\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}+\|u_{L}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}\|u_{L}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}\big)ds

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}+\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda\int_{s}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau}\|u_{L}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}ds,

(2.7)

where we have used the fact that

\|u_{L}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}\leq\|u_{L}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}.

For
I_{4}
, since it has a nice structure as

\bar{u}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}=-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\bar{u}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}),

we can estimate it as

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda\int_{s}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau}\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\bar{u}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|\bar{u}\|_{\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|\bar{u}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\bar{u}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\|\bar{u}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}ds

\displaystyle\leq C\|\bar{u}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}.

(2.8)

Notice that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda\int_{s}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau}\|u_{L}(s)\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}ds=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda\int_{s}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau}f(s)ds=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{t}d\Big(e^{-\lambda\int_{s}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}.

Thus taking all above estimates (
2.5
)–(
2
) into (
2
), there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}+\kappa\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}\leq C\Big(\|u_{L}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}u_{L}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\lambda}\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}+\varepsilon\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}+\|\bar{u}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}\Big).

(2.9)

Now we complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
. We know from
[
10
]
that there exists
T>0
such that the system (
1.1
) admits a unique local
solution
u\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap L^{1}(0,T;\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. Let us denote by
T_{*}
the maximal existence time of this solution. Then to prove Theorem
1.3
,
it suffices to prove
T_{*}=\infty
under the initial condition (
1.2
), and (
1.4
) holds. We prove it by contradiction. Assume that
T_{*}<\infty
, based on the estimate (
2
), let
\eta
be a small enough positive constant which the exact value will be determined later, we define
T_{\eta}
as

\displaystyle T_{\eta}:=\max\Big\{t\in[0,T_{*}):\ \|\bar{u}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}+\kappa\|\bar{u}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}\leq\eta\Big\}.

(2.10)

Taking
\lambda
large enough,
\varepsilon
and
\eta
small enough such that

\lambda\geq 2C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}},\ \ \ \varepsilon<\frac{\kappa}{4C}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \eta\leq\min\{1,\frac{\kappa}{4C}\},

we can deduce from (
2
) to get that

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}+\kappa\|\bar{u}_{\lambda,f}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}

\displaystyle\leq 2C\|u_{L}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}u_{L}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}.

(2.11)

As a consequence, for all
t\leq T_{\eta}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}+\kappa\|\bar{u}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}\leq 2C\|u_{L}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}u_{L}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}\exp\Big\{\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\|u_{L}(\tau)\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}d\tau\Big\}.

(2.12)

Based on (
2.3
), we obtain that there exists a positive constant
C
such that for all
t\leq T_{\eta}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}+\kappa\|\bar{u}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}\leq C\|u_{L}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}u_{L}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}\exp\Big\{C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1}}\Big\}.

(2.13)

We finally conclude that if we take
C_{0}
large enough and
c_{0}
small enough in (
1.2
), then it follows from (
2.13
) that

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{t}(\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}+\kappa\|\bar{u}\|_{L^{1}_{t}(\dot{B}^{\frac{d}{p}}_{p,1})}\leq\frac{\eta}{2}

for all
t<T_{\eta}
, which contradicts with the maximality of
T_{\eta}
, thus
T^{*}=\infty
, and (
1.4
) holds true. We complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.4

In this section we give the proof of Theorem
1.4
. We shall check that the initial data
u_{0,\varepsilon}
introduced in Theorem
1.4
satisfies the nonlinear smallness assumption (
1.2
), but its
\dot{B}^{-2}_{\infty,\infty}
norm is greater than
(-\log\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{5}}
.

Lemma 3.1

(see
[
5
]
)
Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be given and
\sigma\in(0,3-\frac{3}{p})
. Then there
exists a constant
C
such that for any
\varepsilon,\alpha\in(0,1)
, the function
f_{\varepsilon}(x):=e^{i\frac{x_{3}}{\varepsilon}}f(x_{1},\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}},x_{3})
satisfies,
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-\sigma}_{p,1}}\leq C\varepsilon^{\sigma+\frac{\alpha}{p}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-\sigma}_{\infty,\infty}}\geq C^{-1}\varepsilon^{\sigma}.

(3.1)

Lemma 3.2

(see
[
19
]
)
Let
f
and
g
be in
\dot{B}^{-1}_{2p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap\dot{H}^{-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then for all
3<p\leq\infty
, we have

\|e^{t\Delta}fe^{t\Delta}g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}_{p,1})}\leq C\big(\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{-1}_{2p,1}}\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{-1}_{2p,1}}\big)^{\frac{2p}{3p-3}}\big(\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\big)^{\frac{p-3}{3p-3}}.

(3.2)

Define a smooth function as
u_{0,\varepsilon}:=\nabla\cdot w_{0,\varepsilon}
, where
w_{0,\varepsilon}
is defined as

w_{0,\varepsilon}:=(\partial_{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon},\partial_{1}\varphi_{\varepsilon},0)
and

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x):=\frac{(-\log\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{5}}}{\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}}\cos(\frac{x_{3}}{\varepsilon})\phi(x_{1},\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}},x_{3}).

Then we have the following upper and lower bound of
w_{0,\varepsilon}
in Besov type space.

Lemma 3.3

(see
[
5
]
)
A constant
C
exists such that, for all
p\geq 1
, we have

\|w_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-1}_{p,1}}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha}{p}}(-\log\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{5}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \|w_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-1}_{\infty,\infty}}\geq C^{-1}(-\log\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{5}}.

(3.3)

By using Lemma
3.1
, we know that there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\|u_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2}_{\infty,\infty}}=\|\nabla\cdot w_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2}_{\infty,\infty}}\approx\|w_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-1}_{\infty,\infty}}\geq C^{-1}(-\log\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{5}}.

Then for all
1\leq p<\infty
, it follows the Sobolev embedding result that

\|u_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2+\frac{3}{p}}_{p,1}}\geq\|u_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-2}_{\infty,\infty}}\geq C^{-1}(-\log\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{5}}.

(3.4)

On the other hand, it can easily calculate

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot w_{0,\varepsilon}=\frac{2}{\varepsilon}(-\log\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{5}}\cos(\frac{x_{3}}{\varepsilon})\partial_{1}\partial_{2}\phi(x_{1},\frac{x_{2}}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}},x_{3}),

which yields that

e^{t\Delta}w_{0,\varepsilon}\nabla\cdot e^{t\Delta}w_{0,\varepsilon}=\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2-\alpha}}(-\log\varepsilon)^{\frac{2}{5}}e^{t\Delta}f_{\varepsilon}e^{t\Delta}g_{\varepsilon}+\frac{2}{\varepsilon^{2}}(-\log\varepsilon)^{\frac{2}{5}}e^{t\Delta}\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}e^{t\Delta}\tilde{g}_{\varepsilon},

(3.5)

where
f_{\varepsilon},g_{\varepsilon}
,
\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}
and
\tilde{g}_{\varepsilon}
are smooth functions. By using Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, it follows that for all
3<p\leq\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\|e^{t\Delta}f_{\varepsilon}e^{t\Delta}g_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}_{p,1})}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-1}_{2p,1}}\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-1}_{2p,1}}\big)^{\frac{2p}{3p-3}}\big(\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}\big)^{\frac{p-3}{3p-3}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-1}_{2p,1}}\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-1}_{2p,1}}\big)^{\frac{2p}{3p-3}}\big(\|f_{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-1}_{2,1}}\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{B}^{-1}_{2,1}}\big)^{\frac{p-3}{3p-3}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2+\frac{\alpha}{3}}.

(3.6)

Similarly,

\displaystyle\|e^{t\Delta}\tilde{f}_{\varepsilon}e^{t\Delta}\tilde{g}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}_{p,1})}\leq C\varepsilon^{2+\frac{\alpha}{3}}.

(3.7)

Combining (
3.5
), (
3
) and (
3.7
), we immediately obtain

\|e^{t\Delta}w_{0,\varepsilon}\nabla\cdot e^{t\Delta}w_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}_{p,1})}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}(-\log\varepsilon)^{\frac{2}{5}}.

Notice that
u_{0,\varepsilon}=\nabla\cdot w_{0,\varepsilon}
, which gives us to
w_{0,\varepsilon}=-\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}u_{0,\varepsilon}
, thus we finally obtain

\|e^{t\Delta}u_{0,\varepsilon}\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}e^{t\Delta}u_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+};\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{3}{p}}_{p,1})}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{\alpha}{3}}(-\log\varepsilon)^{\frac{2}{5}}.

We complete the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Introduction

A subset
S
of a group
(G,+)
is
sequenceable
if there is an ordering
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})
of its elements such that the partial sums
s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}
, given by
s_{0}=0
and
s_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}y_{j}
for
1\leq i\leq k
, are distinct, with the possible exception that we may have
s_{k}=s_{0}=0
.
Throughout the paper, we will use the additive notation also for non-abelian groups, i.e.
(G,+_{G})
, and we avoid using the subscript in
+_{G}
if it is clear from the context.

Several conjectures and questions concerning the sequenceability of subsets of groups arose in the Design Theory context: indeed this problem is related to Heffter Arrays and
G
-regular Graph Decompositions, see
[
4
,
5
,
12
,
19
,
22
]
. Alspach and Liversidge combined and summarized many of them into the conjecture that if a subset of an abelian group does not contain
0
then it is sequenceable (see
[
3
]
).

Note that, given a sequencing of a set
S
, if
s_{k}\not=0
, then the partial sums define a simple path
P=(s_{0},s_{1},\dots,s_{k})
in the Cayley graph
Cay[G:\pm S]
such that
\Delta(P)=\pm S
where
\Delta(P)
is the multiset
\pm[s_{1}-s_{0},s_{2}-s_{1},\dots,s_{k}-s_{k-1}]
. Here
Cay[G:\pm S]
is the graph whose vertex set is
G
and whose edges are the pairs
\{x,y\}
such that
x-y\in\pm S
. Inspired by this interpretation, in
[
8
]
the authors proposed a weakening of this concept. In particular, they want to find an ordering of a subset
S
of
G\setminus\{0\}
whose partial sums
s_{i}
and
s_{j}
are different whenever
1\leq|i-j|\leq t
. If such an ordering exists, we say that
S
is
t
-
weakly sequenceable
. This question has been also studied in
[
11
]
and
[
9
]
. In particular, in
[
9
]
the authors, using a hybrid approach that combines both Ramsey theory and the probabilistic method, proved the following asymptotic result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a generic group. Then subsets of size
k
of
G\setminus\{0\}
are
t
-weakly sequenceable whenever
k
is large enough with respect to
t
.

In this paper, we consider the weak sequenceability problem on multisets. In particular, given a multiset
M
of non-identity elements of a group
G
, we want to find an ordering of
M
whose partial sums
s_{i}
and
s_{j}
are different whenever
1\leq|i-j|\leq t
.

Modifying the approach of
[
9
]
, here we are able to prove that a multiset
M=[a_{1}^{\lambda_{1}},a_{2}^{\lambda_{2}},\dots,a_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}]
(here
a_{i}^{\lambda_{i}}
means
\lambda_{i}
repetitions of
a_{i}
) of non-identity elements of a generic group
G
is weakly sequenceable whenever the underlying set
\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\}
is sufficiently large and the smallest prime divisor
p
of
|G|
is larger than
t
.

A related question is the one first posed by Buratti for groups of prime order and then generalized by Horak and Rosa in
[
18
]
: the so called BHR conjecture (see also
[
21
]
,
[
23
]
and
[
24
]
).
Given a multiset
M
and a simple path
P
in
Cay[G:\pm M]
, we say that
P
is a realization of
M
if
\Delta(P)=\pm M
. In this case,
M
is said to be realizable. Roughly speaking, the difference between a realization and a sequencing of a multiset

is that, in a realization, we can either add an element
a_{i}\in M
or subtract it while in a sequencing we only add elements. The BHR conjecture states that any multiset
M=[a_{1}^{\lambda_{1}},a_{2}^{\lambda_{2}},\dots,a_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}]
of
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
of size
|M|=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\dots+\lambda_{n}=v-1
is realizable assuming that, for any divisor
d
of

, the number of multiples of
d
in
M
is at most
v-d
.

In this paper we consider this conjecture, again, in the weak sense: we look for a realization of
M
through a walk (i.e. we allow repeated vertices) and not through a simple path but we require that, locally, the vertices of the walk are different. More precisely, we prove that a multiset
M
of non-identity elements of
G
admits a realization
W=(w_{0},\dots,w_{t})
such that
w_{i}\neq w_{j}
whenever
1\leq|i-j|\leq t
assuming that
|M|
is sufficiently large and the smallest prime divisor
p
of
|G|
is larger than
t(2t+1)
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
we revisit the probabilistic approach of
[
9
]
, to prove our asymptotic result on multiset weak sequenceability.

Finally, in the last section, we consider the weak variant of the BHR conjecture.

2
Multiset weak sequenceability

The goal of this section is to prove the asymptotic result on multiset sequenceability. For this purpose, we will recall a Ramsey-Theory kind proposition:

Proposition 2.1
.

[

Let

With a similar proof of Proposition 2.2 of

Proposition 2.2
.

Let

and that the smallest prime divisor

. Then there is a

-weak sequenceable multiset

such that

M_{1}\subseteq M_{3}\subseteq M_{1}\cup M_{2}

and
|M_{3}\setminus M_{1}|\leq t^{2}
.

Proof.

First of all, we consider the sequence of
\alpha_{1}
occurrences of
a_{1}
.
Since the order of
a_{1}
is at least
p>t
,
(a_{1},\dots,a_{1})
is a

-weak sequencing of
[a_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}]
. Now we note that the system of equations

\mu_{1}a_{1}+\mu_{2}x\not=0:\ \mu_{1}\leq\min(t,\beta_{1}),\ \mu_{2}\leq\min(t,\beta_{2})

admits a solution

In this case, there exists
\bar{i}
, that we may assume (without loss of generality) to be

(a_{1},\dots,a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{2})
is a
t
-weak sequencing of
[a_{1}^{\alpha_{1}},a_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}]

Reiterating, we find a
t
-weak sequencing of all the occurrences of

different

s of
M_{1}
. Thus, we may assume (without loss of generality) that
(a_{1},\dots,a_{1},\dots,a_{n-t^{2}},\dots,a_{n-t^{2}})
is a
t
-weak sequencing of
[a_{1}^{\alpha_{1}},a_{2}^{\alpha_{2}},\dots,a_{n-t^{2}}^{\alpha_{n-t^{2}}}]
.

Now we need to place the last elements of
M_{1}
that are
[a_{n-t^{2}+1}^{\alpha_{n-t^{2}+1}},\dots,a_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}]
. In order to do it, we denote by
m_{1}=|[a_{1}^{\alpha_{1}},a_{2}^{\alpha_{2}},\dots,a_{n-t^{2}}^{\alpha_{n-t^{2}}}]|
. Then we note that the system of equations

s_{i}\not=s_{m_{1}}+x+\mu_{n-t^{2}+1}a_{n-t^{2}+1}:\mu_{1}\leq\min(t,\beta_{n-t^{2}+1}),|m_{1}+1+\mu_{n-t^{2}+1}-i|\leq t

admits a solution

provided that the underlying set of

has size larger than

.
In this case, there exists
\bar{i}
, so that
(a_{1},\dots,a_{1},\dots,a_{n-t^{2}},\dots,a_{n-t^{2}},a_{\bar{i}},a_{n-t^{2}+1},\dots,a_{n-t^{2}+1})
is a

-weak sequencing of

Reiterating, and assuming that the underlying set of

has size larger than

M_{1}\subseteq M_{3}\subseteq M_{1}\cup M_{2}

Then, using a probabilistic approach, we can improve Proposition

to the following one:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
M
and
S
be, respectively, a multiset of size
m
and a set of size
k
of non-identity elements of a group
G
, and let us suppose that

is large enough with respect to

. Then, given a
t

(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})
of

, there exists an ordering of

(y_{m+1},\dots,y_{m+k})
, such that

(y_{1},\dots,y_{m},y_{m+1},\dots,y_{m+k})

is a
t
-weak ordering of

Proof.

Let
S
be a subset of size
k
of
G\setminus\{0\}
and let
(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})
be a
t
weak ordering of a multiset
M
. According to Proposition
2.1
, however we choose
\ell
, if we assume that
k
is large enough (with respect to
t
and

), there exists a subset

of

whose size is

. Let us set
U=S\setminus T
and let
k^{\prime}=k-\ell
be the size of

. According to Proposition
2.3
, given

, we can order

, namely
(y_{m+1},\dots,y_{m+h})
, in such a way that, for any
{0\leq i<j\leq\min(m+h,m+i+t)}

We denote by

Now it suffices to order the remaining

that extend the one of

. Let
X
be the random variable that represents the number of pairs

such that
s_{i}=s_{j}
with

and
j-i\leq t
. We evaluate the expected value of

Because of the linearity of the expectation, we have that:

\mathbb{E}(X)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i<j\leq m+h\\
j-i\leq t\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}(s_{i}=s_{j})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq m+h<j\leq m+k\\
j-i\leq t\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}(s_{i}=s_{j})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m+h<i<j\leq m+k\\
j-i\leq t\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}(s_{i}=s_{j}).

(1)

Due to the choice of
U^{\prime}
, when
0\leq i<j\leq m+h
, the probability
\mathbb{P}(s_{i}=s_{j})=0
. Assuming that
0\leq i\leq m+h<j\leq m+k
,
since the equation

s_{i}=y_{1}+y_{2}+\dots+y_{i}=y_{1}+y_{2}+\dots+y_{m+h}+z_{m+h+1}+\dots+z_{j-1}+x=s_{j}

admits only one solution,
\mathbb{P}(s_{i}=s_{j})
is smaller than or equal to
\frac{1}{|S\setminus(U^{\prime}\cup\{z_{h+1},\dots,z_{j-1}\})|}\leq 1/\ell.
Here the last inequality holds because

is at most
m+h+t
and hence the cardinality of
|S\setminus(U^{\prime}\cup\{z_{m+h+1},\dots,z_{m+j-1}\})|
is at least

.
It follows that Equation (
1
) can be rewritten as

\mathbb{E}(X)\leq 0+\frac{t^{2}}{\ell}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m+h<i<j\leq m+k\\
j-i\leq t\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}(s_{i}=s_{j}).

(2)

It is easy to see that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m+h<i<j\leq m+k\\
j-i\leq t\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}(s_{i}=s_{j})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m+h<i<j\leq m+k\\
j-i\leq t\end{subarray}}\mathbb{P}(z_{i+1}+z_{i+2}+\dots+z_{j}=0).

We divide the evaluation of this probability in two cases according to whether
\{z_{i+1},

z_{i+2},\dots,z_{j}\}\subseteq T
or not. In the second case, since at least one of the elements
z_{i+1},

z_{i+2},\dots,z_{j}
is not in
T
, we can assume that
z_{i+1}\not\in T
and since
z_{i+1}\not=0
we can assume

. Reasoning as before we obtain that, under these assumptions, the probability of
z_{i+1}+z_{i+2}+\dots+z_{j}=0
is at most
\frac{1}{\ell}
.
Therefore we have that:

\mathbb{P}(z_{i+1}+z_{i+2}+\dots+z_{j}=0)\leq\frac{1}{\ell}\mathbb{P}(\{z_{i+1},z_{i+2},\dots,z_{j}\}\not\subseteq T)+

\mathbb{P}(z_{i+1},z_{i+2},\dots,z_{j}\in T)\mathbb{P}(z_{i+1}+z_{i+2}+\dots+z_{j}=0|z_{i+1},z_{i+2},\dots,z_{j}\in T)

and, since
z_{i+1}+z_{i+2}+\dots+z_{j}\not=0
whenever
z_{i+1},z_{i+2},\dots,z_{j}\in T
,

\mathbb{P}(z_{i+1}+z_{i+2}+\dots+z_{j}=0)\leq\frac{1}{\ell}\mathbb{P}(\{z_{i+1},z_{i+2},\dots,z_{j}\}\not\subseteq T).

Since

are at most

\frac{{|T|\choose j-i}}{{|S\setminus U^{\prime}|\choose j-i}}\geq\frac{{|T|\choose t}}{{|S\setminus U^{\prime}|\choose t}}=\frac{{\ell\choose t}}{{\ell+t\choose t}}\geq\frac{(\ell-(t-1))^{t}}{(\ell+t)^{t}}.

It follows that

\mathbb{P}(z_{i+1}+z_{i+2}+\dots+z_{j}=0)\leq\frac{1}{\ell}\left(1-\frac{(\ell-(t-1))^{t}}{(\ell+t)^{t}}\right)

and hence Equation (

\mathbb{E}(X)\leq\frac{t^{2}}{\ell}+\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m+h<i<j\leq m+k\\
j-i\leq t\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{(\ell-(t-1))^{t}}{(\ell+t)^{t}}\right).

(3)

Finally, since there are less than

(i,j)
such that
m+h<i<j\leq m+k
and

, from Equation (
3
) we obtain

\mathbb{E}(X)\leq\frac{t^{2}}{\ell}+\frac{t(\ell+t)}{\ell}\left(1-\frac{(\ell-(t-1))^{t}}{(\ell+t)^{t}}\right).

(4)

Here we note that the right-hand side of the last inequality goes to zero as

goes to infinite. Therefore for

, or more precisely for

for a suitable

, we have that

. This means that if

is large enough with respect to

(i.e. if

) there exists an ordering on which

that is, it is possible to extend the

-weak ordering of

with the elements of

.

First of all, we note that we can split

3
Weak BHR conjecture

The goal of this section is to prove that any multiset
M
of non-identity elements of
G
admits a realization
W=(w_{0},\dots,w_{t})
such that
w_{i}\neq w_{j}
whenever

assuming that
|M|
is sufficiently large and the smallest prime divisor

of

is larger than

.
We begin by considering multisets
M=[a_{1}^{\lambda_{1}},a_{2}^{\lambda_{2}}]
of two elements. We begin with a technical lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
a_{1}
be an invertible element in
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
and let
d
be a divisor of

such that
v\geq dt(2t+1)
.
Denoted by
W
the walk
W=(0,a_{1},2a_{1},\dots,ta_{1})
we have that either

W\cap\{1,2,\dots,dt\}=\emptyset
or
W\cap\{-1,-2,\dots,-dt\}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

We consider the walk
W^{\prime}=(w_{0},w_{1},\dots,w_{2t})
where
w_{i}=ia_{1}
. Since
W^{\prime}
divides
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
in

such that

W^{\prime}\cap\{w_{\bar{i}}+1,w_{\bar{i}}+2,\dots,w_{\bar{i}}+\lfloor v/(2t+1)\rfloor\}=\emptyset

or

W^{\prime}\cap\{w_{\bar{i}}-1,w_{\bar{i}}-2,\dots,w_{\bar{i}}-\lfloor v/(2t+1)\rfloor\}=\emptyset.

Since
v\geq dt(2t+1)
, it follows that

W^{\prime}\cap\{w_{\bar{i}}+1,w_{\bar{i}}+2,\dots,w_{\bar{i}}+dt\}=\emptyset

or

W^{\prime}\cap\{w_{\bar{i}}-1,w_{\bar{i}}-2,\dots,w_{\bar{i}}-dt\}=\emptyset.

Here, because of the symmetry, we may assume
\bar{i}\in\{0,1,\dots,t\}
.
It follows that the walk
W^{\prime\prime}=(w^{\prime\prime}_{0},w^{\prime\prime}_{1},\dots,w^{\prime\prime}_{2t-\bar{i}})
where
w^{\prime\prime}_{i}=ia_{1}=w_{i+\bar{i}}-w_{\bar{i}}
contains
W
and is such that

W^{\prime\prime}\cap\{1,2,\dots,dt\}=\emptyset

or

W^{\prime\prime}\cap\{-1,-2,\dots,-dt\}=\emptyset.

Hence the same property holds for the walk
W
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
M=[a_{1}^{\lambda_{1}},a_{2}^{\lambda_{2}}]
be a multiset of non-identity elements of the group
G
and let us assume that
\lambda_{1}\geq 2t
and that the smallest prime divisor

of
|G|
is larger than

.
Then, there exists a realization
W=(w_{0},\dots,w_{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}})
of

such that

(a)

w_{i}-w_{i-1}=a_{1}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,t\}
;

(b)

w_{i}-w_{i-1}=a_{1}
for all
i\in\{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}-(t-1),\dots,\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\}
;

(c)

w_{i}\neq w_{j}
whenever
1\leq|i-j|\leq t
.

Proof.

If the groups
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
generated respectively by
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are disjoint, then they have cardinality larger than
p
and hence than
t
. It follows that the ordering
(a_{1},\dots,a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{2},a_{1},\dots,a_{1})
where we have
\lambda_{1}-t
occurrences of
a_{1}
at the beginning and
t
occurences at the end, is a
t

M
. Then the walk of the partial sums of this ordering provides a realization of
M
which satisfies the conditions
(a),(b)
, and
(c)
of this proposition.

Now, let us assume that
G_{1}\cap G_{2}=G_{3}\not=\{0\}
. Since both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are cyclic, we may assume
G_{3}
is generated by
d\in G_{1}
. Also, again since
G_{1}
is cyclic, we can identify
G_{1}
with
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
where
v=|G_{1}|
,
d
with a divisor of
v
and
a_{1}
with an invertible element of
\mathbb{Z}_{v}
. In this case we have that
|G_{3}|=v/d\geq p\geq t(2t+1)
and hence
v\geq dt(2t+1)
.

Now we consider the walk
W^{\prime}=(w^{\prime}_{0},\dots,w^{\prime}_{t})=(0,a_{1},2a_{1},\dots,ta_{1})
. Because of Lemma
3.1
(applied at the end of
W^{\prime}
) we have that either

W^{\prime}\cap\{ta_{1}+1,ta_{1}+2,\dots,ta_{1}+dt\}=\emptyset
or
W^{\prime}\cap\{ta_{1}-1,ta_{1}-2,\dots,ta_{1}-dt\}=\emptyset
. In the following discussion, we assume we are in the first case (the second one can be treated similarly).
Here we define the walk
W=(w_{0},w_{1},\dots,w_{t+\lambda_{2}},\dots,w_{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}})

where

w_{i}=\begin{cases}w^{\prime}_{i}=ia_{1}\mbox{ if }i\leq t;\\
ta_{1}+(i-t)a_{2}\mbox{ if }t<i\leq t+\lambda_{2};\\
ta_{1}+\lambda_{2}a_{2}+(i-t-\lambda_{2})a_{1}\mbox{ if }t+\lambda_{2}<i\leq\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}.\end{cases}

Here, if
ta_{1}+\lambda_{2}a_{2}\not\in G_{1}
,
W
is a realization of
M
that satisfies the conditions
(a),(b)
and
(c)
. Indeed, for construction,
W^{\prime}\cap\{w_{t+1},\dots,w_{t+\min(\lambda_{2},t)}\}=\emptyset
, and hence, for symmetry

\{w_{t+\max(1,\lambda_{2}-t)},\dots,w_{t+\lambda_{2}}\}\cap\{w_{t+\lambda_{2}+1},w_{t+\lambda_{2}+2},\dots,w_{2t+\lambda_{2}}\}=\emptyset.

Moreover, since
\{w_{t+\lambda_{2}+1},w_{t+\lambda_{2}+2},\dots,w_{2t+\lambda_{2}}\}
belongs to the coset of
G_{1}
in
G
represented by
w_{t+\lambda_{1}}=ta_{1}+\lambda_{2}a_{2}
, we have that
\{w_{t+\lambda_{2}+1},

w_{t+\lambda_{2}+2},\dots,w_{2t+\lambda_{2}}\}
is also disjoint from
W^{\prime}
.

Now, let us assume that
ta_{1}+\lambda_{2}a_{2}\in G_{1}
. This means that
\lambda_{2}a_{2}=\lambda_{3}d
for some
\lambda_{3}\leq\lambda_{2}
. As before, we have that, for construction,

W^{\prime}\cap\{w_{t+1},\dots,w_{t+\min(\lambda_{2},t)}\}=\emptyset

(5)

and, for symmetry,

\{w_{t+\max(1,\lambda_{2}-t)},\dots,w_{t+\lambda_{2}}\}\cap\{w_{t+\lambda_{2}+1},w_{t+\lambda_{2}+2},\dots,w_{2t+\lambda_{2}}\}=\emptyset.

It is left to prove that
w_{i}\not=w_{j}
when
1\leq|i-j|\leq t
,
i\leq t
and
j\geq t+\lambda_{2}+1
.
Let us assume, by contradiction that there exist pairs
w_{i},w_{j}
with
w_{i}=w_{j}
. Let
\{i,j\}
be chosen with minimal
j
among such pairs. Note that, since

it follows that
i\geq j-t\geq(t+\lambda_{2}+1)-t>1
. Therefore, from

w_{j}=ta_{1}+\lambda_{2}a_{2}+(j-t-\lambda_{2})a_{1}
and
w_{i}=ia_{1}
, from
w_{i}=w_{j}
it follows that also

w_{j-1}=ta_{1}+\lambda_{2}a_{2}+(j-t-\lambda_{2})a_{1}-a_{1}=ia_{1}-a_{1}=w_{i-1}.

Due to the minimality of
j
, this means that
j-1=t+\lambda_{2}
. On the other hand
|(j-1)-(i-1)|=|j-i|\leq t
and hence Equation (
5
) and the fact that the order of

is at least
p>t

w_{i-1}\not=w_{j-1}=a_{1}t+a_{2}\lambda_{2}
.
This is in contradiction with the assumption that

.
It follows that
W
is a realization of

that satisfies the conditions of the proposition.

The thesis follows because we can proceed analogously also when
W^{\prime}\cap\{ta_{1}-1,ta_{1}-2,\dots,ta_{1}-dt\}=\emptyset
.
∎

We are now ready to consider the general case:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
M=[a_{1}^{\lambda_{1}},a_{2}^{\lambda_{2}},\dots,a_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}]
be a multiset of non-identity elements of the group
G
and let us assume that
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\dots+\lambda_{n}
is large enough with respect to
t
and that the smallest prime divisor
p
of
|G|
is larger than
t(2t+1)
.
Then, there exists a realization
W=(w_{0},\dots,w_{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}})
of
M
such that
w_{i}\neq w_{j}
whenever
1\leq|i-j|\leq t
.

Proof.

Since
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\dots+\lambda_{n}
is large enough, we have that either
n
is large enough with respect to
t
or that there exists
\bar{i}
so that
\lambda_{\bar{i}}
is larger than
2nt
. In the first case, the thesis follows from Theorem
2.5
. Therefore we can assume, without loss of generality, that
\lambda_{1}>2nt
.

In this case we can split
M
into
n-1
multisets
M_{2},\dots,M_{n}
so that

M_{i}=[a_{1}^{\lambda_{1,i}},a_{i}^{\lambda_{i}}]
and each
\lambda_{1,i}\geq 2t
. Because of Proposition
3.2
, each
M_{i}
admits a realization
W_{i}=(w_{0,i},\dots,w_{\lambda_{1,i}+\lambda_{i},i})
that satisfies the hypothesis. Now, in order to obtain a realization of
M
, we glue together these walks. More precisely, at first we consider
W_{2}=(w_{0,2},\dots,w_{\lambda_{1,2}+\lambda_{2},2})
and
W_{3}=(w_{0,3},\dots,w_{\lambda_{1,3}+\lambda_{3},3})
which give

W^{\prime}=(w_{0,2},\dots,w_{\lambda_{1,2}+\lambda_{2},2},w_{\lambda_{1,2}+\lambda_{2},2}+w_{0,3},\dots,w_{\lambda_{1,2}+\lambda_{2},2}+w_{\lambda_{1,3}+\lambda_{3},3}).

Since
w_{i,2}-w_{i-1,2}=a_{1}
whenever
i>t+\lambda_{2}
and
w_{i,3}-w_{i-1,3}=a_{1}
whenever
i\leq t
, we have that
W^{\prime}
is a realization of
M_{2}\cup M_{3}
such that
w^{\prime}_{i}\not=w^{\prime}_{j}
whenever
|i-j|\leq t
. Reiterating this procedure we obtain a realization
W=(w_{0},\dots,w_{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\dots+\lambda_{n}})
of
M
such that
w_{i}\neq w_{j}
whenever
1\leq|i-j|\leq t
.
∎

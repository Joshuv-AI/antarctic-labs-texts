Radiation Pressure Induced Oscillations of an Optically Levitating Mirror
Satyam Shekhar Jha 1,2 , Tal Carmon 3 , Fan Cheng 3 , {}^{3}, Lev Deych 1,2

Abstract
Optical Fabry-Perot cavity with a movable mirror is a paradigmatic optomechanical systems. While usually the mirror is supported by a mechanical spring, it has been shown that it is possible to keep one of the mirrors in a stable equilibrium purely by optical levitation without any mechanical support. In this work we expand previous studies of nonlinear dynamics of such a system by demonstrating a possibility for mechanical parametric instability and emergence of the “phonon laser” phenomenon.

is also a significant difference. In a simplest single mode laser

state oscillations are harmonic. In the case considered in this work
the situation is more complicated - the effective gain oscillates
around zero as shown in Fig.

III
Conclusion

In this work we analyzed the motion of a center of mass of a levitated
mirror in the presence of mechanical damping. We showed that the damping
stabilizes nonlinear dynamics of mirror resulting in a behavior reminiscent
to phonon lasing. Unlike the simplest lasing dynamics, however, the
time dependence of the mirror’s displacement in the steady state is
in our case is anharmonic with the effective gain parameter oscillating
around rather then being pinned to zero.

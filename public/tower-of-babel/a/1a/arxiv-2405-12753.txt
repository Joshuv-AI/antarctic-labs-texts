The Laplace and Leray transforms on some (weakly) convex domains in ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2}
Agniva Chatterjee

Abstract
. The space of Laplace transforms of holomorphic Hardy-space functions have been characterized as weighted Bergman spaces of entire functions in two cases: that of planar convex domains (Lutsenko–Yumulmukhametov, 1991), and that of strongly convex domains in higher dimensions (Lindholm, 2002). In this paper, we establish such a Paley–Wiener result for a class of (weakly) convex Reinhardt domains in ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} that are well-modelled by the so-called egg domains. We consider Hardy spaces on these domains with respect to a canonical choice of boundary Monge–Ampère measure. This class of domains was introduced by Barrett–Lanzani (2009) to study the L 2 L^{2} -boundedness of the Leray transform in the absence of either strongly convexity or 𝒞 2 \mathcal{C}^{2} -regularity. The boundedness of the Leray transform plays a crucial role in understanding the image of the Laplace transform. As a supplementary result, we expand the known class of convex Reinhardt domains for which the Leray transform is L 2 L^{2} -bounded (with respect to the aforementioned choice of boundary measure). Finally, we also produce an example to show that the Lutsenko–Yumulmukhametov result cannot be expected to generalize to all convex domains in higher dimensions.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a topological vector space of functions on a subset of
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
such that all exponential functions of the form
\zeta\mapsto e^{\langle z,\zeta\rangle}
,
z\in{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
, belong to
\mathcal{X}
. It is sometimes useful to identify
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
, the dual space of
\mathcal{X}
, with a space of functions on
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
via the Laplace transform

\mathcal{L}(f)(z)=f(e^{\langle z,\cdot\rangle}),\quad f\in\mathcal{X}^{\prime}.

Some classical examples of
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
, for which this identification has been used to great effect, include the space of
L^{2}
-functions with compact convex support in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(the Paley–Wiener theorem), the space of distributions with compact convex support in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(the Paley-Wiener-Schwartz theorem), the space of hyperfunctions with convex compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(the Paley-Wiener-Martineau theorem), and the space of analytic functionals carried by compact convex sets in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
(the Pólya-Martineau-Ehrenpreis theorem). More recently, results in this spirit have appeared in the context of interpolation and sampling,
[
35
,
9
]
, and in the proofs of certain geometric inequalities for convex bodies,
[
32
,
10
]
.

1.1.
Laplace transforms of holomorphic Hardy functions

In this paper, we consider the case when
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
is a Hardy space of holomorphic functions on a convex Reinhardt domain of the type considered by Barrett–Lanzani
[
5
]
. Our motivation is as follows. Given a bounded convex domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
and a Radon measure
\mu
on
b\Omega
, let
L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu)
denote the space of complex-valued functions on
b\Omega
that are square-integrable with respect to
\mu
, and
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
denote the space of continuous
\mathbb{C}
-valued functions on
\overline{\Omega}
that are holomorphic on
\Omega
. Consider the Laplace transform
\mathcal{L}
on
L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu)
given by

\mathcal{L}:f\mapsto F(z)=\int_{b\Omega}\overline{f(\zeta)}\,e^{\langle z,\zeta\rangle}\,d\mu(\zeta),\quad\forall f\in L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu).

Since
\mathcal{L}
annihilates the orthogonal complement of

\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu)=\text{the $L^{2}(\mu)$-closure of $\mathcal{A}(\Omega)|_{b\Omega}$ in }L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu),

we might as well consider the restriction of
\mathcal{L}
to
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
(also denoted by
\mathcal{L}
).

The Pólya-Martineau-Ehrenpreis theorem gives a necessary condition for an entire function to be the Laplace transform of some
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
. In some cases, it is possible to completely characterize the range of
\mathcal{L}
as a weighted Bergman space of the form

A^{2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}},\omega)=\left\{F\in\mathcal{O}({\mathbb{C}^{n}}):\|F\|_{\omega}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|F|^{2}d\omega<\infty\right\}\text{ with norm }\|\cdot\|_{\omega}.

One advantage of shifting the analysis to a weighted Bergman space is the availability of Hörmander’s
L^{2}
-theory to construct functions with desired properties. The following rather comprehensive result completely tackles the case of planar convex domains when
\mu
is the arc-length measure; see
[
30
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Lutsenko–Yumulmukhametov)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a bounded convex domain, and
\sigma_{\Omega}
be the arc-length measure on
b\Omega
. Let
H_{\Omega}
denote the support function of
\Omega
(see (

)). Then,
\mathcal{L}
is a normed space isomorphism between
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\sigma_{\Omega})
and
A^{2}(\mathbb{C},\widetilde{\omega}_{\Omega})
, where

(1.1)

\widetilde{\omega}_{\Omega}(z)=\|e^{\langle z,\cdot\rangle}\|^{-2}_{L^{2}(\sigma_{\Omega})}\Delta H_{\Omega}(z),\quad z\in\mathbb{C},

and
\Delta H_{\Omega}
is the Riesz measure of the convex function
H_{\Omega}
.

Special cases of this theorem were previously proved by Levin (polygons), Likht (discs), Katsnel’son and Lyubarskii (strongly convex domains); see
[
27

,
[
28
]
,
[
22
]
and
[
31
]
, respectively. The case of strongly convex domains in
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
is also well-understood.

Theorem 1.2

(Lindholm)
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
be a bounded strongly convex domain, and
\sigma_{\Omega}
be the surface area measure on

. Let
H_{\Omega}
denote the support function of
\Omega

is a normed space isomorphism between
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\sigma_{\Omega})
and
A^{2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}},\widetilde{\omega}_{\Omega})

(1.2)

\widetilde{\omega}_{\Omega}(z)=\|e^{\langle z,\cdot\rangle}\|^{-2}_{L^{2}(\sigma_{\Omega})}\left(dd^{c}H_{\Omega}\right)^{n}(z),\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}^{n}},

and
(dd^{c}H_{\Omega})^{n}

.

The measure

[

]
, but we show in Proposition

below that

works equally well. Theorem
1.2
raises the

Question

Are the Laplace transforms of holomorphic Hardy functions on weakly convex domains in
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
characterized by a weighted Bergman condition?

A natural class of model domains in this context are the so-called egg domains:

(1.3)

\left\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:a_{1}|z_{1}|^{p_{1}}+a_{2}|z_{2}|^{p_{2}}<1\right\},\quad a_{1},a_{2}\in(0,\infty),p_{1},p_{2}\in(1,\infty).

, if one considers the

.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}

1.2.
Boundedness of the Leray transform

A key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.3
is the
L^{2}
-boundedness of the so-called Leray transform. Since the Leray transform is of independent interest, we state the relevant result separately. Classically, the
Leray integral
of a function
f
on the boundary of a bounded
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth convex domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
is defined as

(1.5)

\mathbb{L}(f)(z)=\int_{b\Omega}\frac{f(\zeta)}{\langle\partial\rho(\zeta),\zeta-z\rangle^{n}}\lambda_{\rho}(\zeta),\quad z\in\Omega,\quad

where
\rho
is a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth defining function of
\Omega
,
\partial\rho
is shorthand for
(\partial\rho/{\partial\zeta_{1}},...,\partial\rho/{\partial\zeta_{n}})
, and
\lambda_{\rho}
is the
Leray–Levi measure
given by the pull-back of the form
d^{c}\rho\wedge\left(dd^{c}\rho\right)^{n-1}
under the inclusion of
b\Omega
into
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Due to the convexity of
\Omega
,
\mathbb{L}
is a well-defined operator from
L^{2}(b\Omega,\lambda_{\rho})
to
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
. It reproduces holomorphic functions that are continuous up to the boundary; see
[
34
, Theorem 3.14]
. If there is a dense subspace
\mathcal{D}\subset L^{2}(b\Omega,\lambda_{\rho})
such that holomorphic functions in
\mathbb{L}(\mathcal{D})
admit boundary values in
L^{2}(b\Omega,\lambda_{\rho})
, then one obtains a densely-defined operator
\mathbb{L}_{b}:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow L^{2}(b\Omega,\lambda_{\rho})
given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{L}_{b}:f

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\text{b.v. }\mathbb{L}(f).

This is the
Leray transform
on
\Omega
, and one can ask the

Question
.

Does
\mathbb{L}_{b}
extend to a bounded operator from
L^{2}(b\Omega,\lambda_{\rho})
onto
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\lambda_{\rho})
?

If yes, then the reproducing property of
\mathbb{L}
allows for functions in
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\lambda_{\rho})
to be decomposed into rational functions. In the plane,
\mathbb{L}_{b}
is the Cauchy transform, and
\lambda_{\rho}
is equivalent to the arc-length measure
\sigma_{\Omega}
on
b\Omega
. The
L^{p}(\sigma)
-regularity,
1<p<\infty
, of the Cauchy transform, was established in the general setting of Lipschitz domains in the works of Calderón
[
11
]
, Coifman-McIntosh-Meyer
[
13
]
, and David
[
14
]
. For
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth domains in higher dimensions, the
L^{2}(\lambda_{\rho})
-regularity of
\mathbb{L}_{b}
was established for egg domains by Hansson
[
20
]
, and for strongly convex bounded domains by Kerzman–Stein
[
23
]
and Lanzani–Stein
[
24
]
. Lanzani–Stein
[
25
]
,
[
26
]
, also gave a counterexample to
L^{2}(\lambda_{\rho})
-regularity of
\mathbb{L}_{b}
when strong convexity is dropped. Some unbounded hypersurfaces were considered by Barrett–Edholm
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
, Edholm–Shelah
[
16
]
, and Ha–Trung
[
19
]
.

The Leray integral can be defined for any
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth convex domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
by replacing
\rho
and
\lambda_{\rho}
in (
1.5
) by the Minkowski functional
m_{\Omega}
of
\Omega
, and the boundary Monge–Ampère measure
\mu_{\Omega}
associated to
m_{\Omega}
, respectively. When
\Omega
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth, this coincides with the original definition of
\mathbb{L}
. For
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
domains, the
L^{p}(\mu_{\Omega})
-boundedness of
\mathbb{L}_{b}
,
1<p<\infty
, was established for strongly
\mathbb{C}
-linearly convex domains by Lanzani–Stein
[
24
]
. The class

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}=\{\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}:\Omega\ \text{is a bounded convex $\mathcal{C}^{1}$-smooth Reinhardt domain}\quad

(1.6)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{that is $\mathcal{C}^{2}$-smooth and strongly convex away from $\mathcal{Z}$}\}.

was studied by Barrett–Lanzani as a generalization of egg domains. They showed that for each
\Omega\in\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}
,
\mathbb{L}_{b}
is a densely-defined operator on
L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu)
, where
\mu
belongs to a class of rotation-invariant finite measures on
b\Omega
, which always includes
\mu_{\Omega}
. They further described a subclass of domains
\mathcal{R}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}
for which
\mathbb{L}_{b}
extends as a bounded projection operator from
L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu)
onto
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
. The class
\mathcal{R}
has a technical definition, but essentially consists of domains in
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}
that are osculated by weighted
L^{p}
-balls even at the axes. For
\mu=\mu_{\Omega}
, we describe a larger class of domains in
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}
for which
\mathbb{L}_{b}
is
L^{2}(\mu_{\Omega})
-bounded. Membership in this class imposes a weaker condition on the limiting behavior of
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
and
\kappa_{3}
than in
\mathcal{R}
; see Example
3.1
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\Omega\in\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
, and
\mathbb{L}_{b}
denote the (densely-defined) Leray transform on
\Omega
.

(1)

If
(\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2})^{-1}\kappa_{3}
is uniformly continuous and bounded away from zero on
b\Omega\setminus\mathcal{Z}
, then
\mathbb{L}_{b}
extends to a bounded projection operator from
L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
onto
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
.

(2)

If
(\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2})^{-1}\kappa_{3}
is either not bounded above or not bounded away from zero, then
\mathbb{L}_{b}
does not extend as a bounded operator to
L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
.

The case where
(\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2})^{-1}\kappa_{3}
is bounded above and away from zero, but does not extend continuously to
b\Omega
, appears to be quite subtle. We have briefly elaborated on this in Section
4.3
.

1.3.
Relating the Leray and the Laplace transform

Lindholm’s original version of Theorem
1.2
states that on strongly convex domains,
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\sigma))=A^{2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}},\nu_{\Omega})
, where

(1.7)

\nu_{\Omega}(z)=e^{-2H_{\Omega}(z)}\|z\|^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\left(dd^{c}H_{\Omega}\right)^{n}(z),\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}^{n}}.

We show that this choice of measure works for precisely those domains in
\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
whose Leray transform is
L^{2}
-bounded. In order to recover Theorem
1.3
from this result, we must compare
A^{2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}},\nu_{\Omega})
and
A^{2}({\mathbb{C}^{n}},\omega_{\Omega})
. While these spaces are the same (with comparable norms) for strongly convex domains, see Proposition
7.2
, we are only able to provide a sufficient condition for this equality to hold for a domain in
\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Omega\in\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}
, and
\mathbb{L}_{b}
be the Leray transform on
\Omega
. Let
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
and
\kappa_{3}
be as in Theorem
1.3
. Then, the following hold.

(1)

The Leray transform
\mathbb{L}_{b}
extends to a bounded operator on
L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
if and only if
\mathcal{L}
is a normed space isomorphism between
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
and
A^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2},\nu_{\Omega})
.

(2)

If the function
(\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2})^{-1}\kappa_{3}
is bounded above and away from zero on
b\Omega\setminus\mathcal{Z}
, then the identity map is a normed space isomorphism between
A^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2},\omega_{\Omega})
and
A^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2},\nu_{\Omega})
.

See Section
1.6
for bounds on
\|\mathcal{L}\|_{op}
and
\|\mathcal{L}^{-1}\|_{op}
in the case when
\mathbb{L}_{b}
is
L^{2}(\mu_{\Omega})
-bounded.

We elaborate briefly on the role that the Leray transform plays in studying the range of
\mathcal{L}
. On strongly convex domains, the Laplace and Leray transforms are related via the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}^{2}(b\Omega^{*},\mu_{\Omega^{*}})}

Here,
\Omega^{*}
denotes the dual complement of

, see (

). There is a natural bijection

, which induces the map

1.4.
A negative result

The following example shows that the measure
\omega_{\Omega}
will not yield as comprehensive a result in higher dimensions as Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\Omega=\{(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:|z_{1}|+|z_{2}|<1\}
. Then,

\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{H}^{2}\left(\Omega,\mu_{\Omega}\right)\right)\subsetneq A^{2}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2},\nu_{\Omega}\right)\subsetneq A^{2}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2},\omega_{\Omega}\right).

Here, unlike in the case of
\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
, the measure
(dd^{c}H_{\Omega})^{2}
is supported on a three-dimensional surface in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. This is analogous to the case of planar polygons, where the Riesz measure
\triangle H_{\Omega}
is supported on a union of real lines. However, for planar polygons, we have that
A^{2}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2},\nu_{\Omega}\right)\subsetneq A^{2}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2},\omega_{\Omega}\right)=\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{H}^{2}\left(\Omega,\mu_{\Omega}\right)\right)
. Currently, we do not know of any
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth domains in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
for which
\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{H}^{2}\left(\Omega,\mu_{\Omega}\right)\right)\neq A^{2}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2},\omega_{\Omega}\right)
.

1.5.
Remarks on the choice of boundary measure

The suitability of
\mu_{\Omega}
for studying
\mathcal{L}
was already indicated by Lindholm
[
29
]
. We collect some observations to support this.

(a)

Since Leray–Levi measures appear explicitly in the Leray kernel, boundedness of the Leray operator has often been studied with respect to these measures. Hansson makes a strong case for this measure in
[
20
]
. However, Leray–Levi measures are not independent of the choice of defining functions. For convex domains, the Minkowski functional allows for a canonical choice of defining function. Furthermore, this choice behaves well under the duality operation:
s^{*}(\mu_{\Omega^{*}})=\mu_{\Omega}
, where
s:b\Omega\rightarrow b\Omega^{*}
is the natural bijection mentioned above.

(b)

Up to a constant factor,
\mu_{\Omega}
coincides with the special measure
\mu_{0}
of order
0
considered in
[
5
, §8]
; see (
3.13
). The
L^{2}
-adjoint of
\mathbb{L}_{b}
with respect to this measure is somewhat special, as demonstrated in
[
5
, Prop. 49]
.

(c)

When tackling the Borel transform
\mathcal{B}
, Lindholm uses a coarea-type formula over the level sets of
\mu_{\Omega}
; see Proposition
2.5
for a version for
\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
. Such a formula is needed to transfer data from
b\Omega
to
{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
. It is in this formula that the measures
\omega_{\Omega}
and
\mu_{\Omega}
appear naturally as the analogues of
\omega_{std}
and
\sigma_{\Omega}
in the classical coarea formula.

1.6.
Bounds on the operator norms

In applications, the classical Paley–Wiener theorem is used in conjunction with the Plancherel theorem. In all the results above,
\mathcal{L}
is only shown to be a normed space isomorphism (as opposed to an isometric isomorphism). By tracking the constants in our proofs, we obtain the following bounds.

(a)

If
\Omega\in\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
and
\mathbb{L}_{b}
extends as a bounded operator on
L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
, then

(1.8)

\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{e^{2}}c_{\Omega}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|f\|_{\mu_{\Omega}}^{2}\leq\|\mathcal{L}f\|_{\nu_{\Omega}}^{2}\leq\frac{5^{3}\sqrt{e}}{2^{15/2}\pi}C_{\Omega}^{\frac{3}{2}}\|\mathbb{L}_{b}\|_{op}^{2}\|f\|_{\mu_{\Omega}}^{2},

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
, where
c_{\Omega}^{-1}=\sup_{\|z\|=1}H_{\Omega}(z)
and
C_{\Omega}^{-1}=\inf_{\|z\|=1}H_{\Omega}(z).

(b)

If
\nu_{\Omega}
is replaced by the equivalent measure

\widetilde{\nu}_{\Omega}=e^{-2H_{\Omega}(z)}H_{\Omega}(z)^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(dd^{c}H_{\Omega}\right)^{2}(z),\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}^{n}},

above, then
c_{\Omega}^{3/2}

2.3.
Monge–Ampère measures associated to domains in
\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}

Recall that, for a bounded convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the
support function of
\Omega
is the function
H_{\Omega}:{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

(2.6)

H_{\Omega}(z)=\sup_{\zeta\in\Omega}\operatorname{Re}\langle\zeta,z\rangle,\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Since
H_{\Omega}
is a convex function, one can make sense of
(dd^{c}H_{\Omega})^{n}
as a Radon measure on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
; see
[
7
, §2]
. In particular, if
n=2
,
\nabla H_{\Omega}\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
, and

(2.7)

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\psi(dd^{c}H_{\Omega})^{2}=-\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}dH_{\Omega}\wedge d^{c}H_{\Omega}\wedge dd^{c}\psi,\qquad\forall\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{2}).

In general,
(dd^{c}H_{\Omega})^{n}
may be mutually singular with respect to
\omega_{std}
, the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. However, when
\Omega
is strongly convex,
H_{\Omega}\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
(see
[
36
, §2.5]
) and consequently

. When
\Omega\in\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
, we have that
(dd^{c}H_{\Omega})^{2}\ll\omega_{std}
on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\Omega\in\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
. Then,
(dd^{c}H_{\Omega})^{2}=\mathfrak{H}_{\Omega}\omega_{std}
as measures, where

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}_{\Omega}(z)=\begin{cases}\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\left(\dfrac{\partial^{2}H_{\Omega}}{\partial z_{1}\partial{\overline{z}_{1}}}\dfrac{\partial^{2}H_{\Omega}}{\partial z_{2}\partial{\overline{z}_{2}}}-\dfrac{\partial^{2}H_{\Omega}}{\partial z_{1}\partial\overline{z}_{2}}\dfrac{\partial^{2}H_{\Omega}}{\partial z_{2}\partial{\overline{z}_{1}}}\right)(z),&\text{if }z\notin\mathcal{Z},\\
0,&\text{if }z\in\mathcal{Z}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\mu=(dd^{c}H_{\Omega})^{2}\Big|_{\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{Z}}
and
\nu=\mathfrak{H}_{\Omega}\omega_{std}\Big|_{\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{Z}}
. By
[
7
, Corollary 3.5]
,
(dd^{c}H_{\Omega})^{2}
does not charge
\mathcal{Z}
. Thus, it suffices to show that,

on
\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{Z}
. Since
\mu
and
\nu
are Radon measures on

(2.9)

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{Z}}\varphi d\mu=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{Z}}\varphi d\nu,\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{Z}).

Since
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{Z})
is dense in

in the sup-norm, and

and

via integration, it suffices to establish (

is

-smooth on

is strongly admissible in the sense of
[
18
, Definition 3.4]
, then the map
F|_{b\Omega}\mapsto F
extends from
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
to an isometric isomorphism
\mathcal{I}:\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}(\Omega,\mu)
, where
\mathfrak{X}(\Omega,\mu)
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space of holomorphic functions on
\Omega
containing
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
. This gives a unique function
s:\Omega\times\text{supp}\>\mu\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, with
s(\cdot,w)\in\mathfrak{X}(\Omega,\nu)
for all
w\in\text{supp}\>\mu
and
s(z,\cdot)\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
for all
z\in\Omega
, such that

(\mathcal{I}f)(z)=\int_{b\Omega}f(w)s(z,w)d\mu(w),\quad z\in\Omega,f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu).

This extends to a well-defined bounded transformation
\mathbb{S}:L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu)\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}(\Omega,\mu)
that “produces” and “reproduces” holomorphic functions:

\mathbb{S}f(z)=\int_{b\Omega}f(w)s(z,w)d\mu(w),\quad z\in\Omega.

Further, a projection operator
\mathbb{S}_{b}:L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
can be defined as follows.

\mathbb{S}_{b}f=\mathcal{I}^{-1}(\mathbb{S}f),\quad f\in L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu).

In the case when
\Omega
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth and convex, and
\mu=\sigma
is the surface area measure,
\mathcal{A}
is indeed strongly admissible,
\mathbb{S}
and
\mathbb{S}_{b}
are bounded operators on
L^{2}(b\Omega,\sigma)
,
\mathbb{S}_{b}f(\zeta)=\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\mathbb{S}f(r\zeta)
for
\sigma_{\Omega}
-a.e.
\zeta\in b\Omega
and
f\in L^{2}(b\Omega,\sigma)
, and
\mathbb{S}_{b}
is in fact the (orthogonal) Szegő projection from
L^{2}(b\Omega,\sigma)
onto
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\sigma)
; see
[
1
]
. For a general convex domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
and
\mu=\mu_{\rho}
as in (
2.10
), the obvious candidate for
\mathfrak{X}(\Omega,\mu)
is the Poletsky–Stessin Hardy space on
\Omega
corresponding to
\rho
; see
[
33
]
. However, the strong admissibility of
\mathcal{A}
or the existence of boundary values for functions in
\mathfrak{X}(\Omega,\mu)
is not known in this general setting. For
\Omega\in\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
, we give an explicit description of
\mathfrak{X}(\Omega,\mu)
using series expansions in Section
3.2
.

3.
Some technical tools

Our computations are facilitated by a special parametrization of domains in
\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
. We describe the relevant function spaces and operators in terms of this parametrization in this section.

3.1.
An alternate parametrization

Let
\Omega
be a bounded convex Reinhardt domain in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
that is strongly convex away from
\mathcal{Z}
. Let
\phi,b_{1}
and
b_{2}
be as granted by (
2.1
). Since
\Omega
is Reinhardt, any parameterization of the curve
\gamma_{\Omega}
induces a parameterization of
b\Omega
. Thus, the parameterization of
\gamma_{\Omega}
given in (
2.1
) induces a parameterization of
b\Omega
. However, it is fruitful to consider a different parameterization of
\gamma_{\Omega}
that was introduced in
[
5
, §2]
. Let

(3.1)

\displaystyle s(r)=\begin{cases}\frac{-r\phi^{\prime}(r)}{\phi(r)-r\phi^{\prime}(r)},&r\in(0,b_{1}),\\
0,&r=0,\\
1,&r=b_{1}.\end{cases}

Then, due to properties
(i)
,
(ii)
and
(iii^{\prime})
of
\phi
,
s:[0,b_{1}]\rightarrow[0,1]
is a continuous monotone function that is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-smooth on
\left(0,b_{1}\right)
; see the discussion after Prop. 8 in
[
5
]
. Denote
s^{-1}
by
r_{1}
. Then, we have that

(3.2)

b\Omega=\left\{\left(r_{1}(s)e^{i\theta_{1}},r_{2}(s)e^{i\theta_{2}}\right):0\leq s\leq 1,(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in[0,2\pi)\times[0,2\pi)\right\},

where
r_{2}=\phi\circ r_{1}
. The functions,
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
are related to the exponent function
p
from (
2.2
) as follows. Let
\check{p}:(0,1)\rightarrow(1,\infty)
be given by
\check{p}(s)=p\left(r_{1}(s),r_{2}(s)\right)
. Then, for
s\in(0,1)
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle r_{1}(s)=b_{1}\exp\left(-\int_{s}^{1}\frac{dt}{t{\check{p}}(t)}\right),

(3.4)

\displaystyle r_{2}(s)=b_{2}\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{s}\frac{dt}{(1-t)\check{p}(t)}\right).

Moreover,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{t{\check{p}}(t)}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{(1-t){\check{p}}(t)}=\infty.

If
\Omega\in\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
, then in addition to (
3.5
),

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left(1-\frac{1}{\check{p}(t)}\right)\frac{dt}{t}=\int_{0}^{1}\left(1-\frac{1}{\check{p}(t)}\right)\frac{dt}{(1-t)}=\infty.

Conversely, by
[
5
, Theorem 9]
, each tuple
\left(\check{p},b_{1},b_{2}\right)
, where
b_{1},b_{2}>0
and
\check{p}:(0,1)\rightarrow(1,\infty)
is a continuous function satisfying (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), determines a unique
\Omega\in\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
.

Example 3.1
.

Domains in
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
(see (
2.4
)) are characterized by tuples of the form
(\check{p},b_{1},b_{2})
, where
b_{1},b_{2}
are positive constants and
\check{p}:[0,1]\rightarrow(1,\infty)
is a continuous function which satisfies (
3.5
) and (
3.6
).
By
[
5
, Theorem 19]
, the additional condition

(3.7)

\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{\check{p}(s)}-\frac{1}{\check{p}(0)}\right)\frac{ds}{s}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{\check{p}(s)}-\frac{1}{\check{p}(1)}\right)\frac{ds}{1-s}<\infty

characterizes domains in
\mathcal{R}
. Thus,
\mathcal{R}\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
. The tuple
(\check{p},1,1)
, where

\check{p}(s)=2+\frac{1}{\log\left(\frac{10}{s}\right)},\quad s\in[0,1],

satisfies (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), but does not satisfy the integral conditions in (
3.7
). Hence the corresponding domain is in
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
, but not in
\mathcal{R}
.

The parametrization discussed above also yields a quick proof of Lemma (
2.1
).

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Let
\zeta\in b\Omega_{+}
. By (
3.2
), we have that

\zeta=\left(r_{1}(s)e^{i\theta_{1}},r_{2}(s)e^{i\theta_{2}}\right)
for some
\left(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\right)\in(0,1)\times[0,2\pi)^{2}
. From
[
5
, (4.5)]
, the three principal curvatures at
\zeta
are given by

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}\left(\zeta\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{s}{{r_{1}(s)}^{2}\sqrt{\left(\frac{s}{r_{1}(s)}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{1-s}{r_{2}(s)}\right)^{2}}},

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}\left(\zeta\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-s}{{r_{2}(s)}^{2}\sqrt{\left(\frac{s}{r_{1}(s)}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{1-s}{r_{2}(s)}\right)^{2}}},

\displaystyle\kappa_{3}\left(\zeta\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\check{p}(s)-1\right)\frac{s(1-s)}{{r_{1}(s)}^{2}{r_{2}(s)}^{2}}\left(\left(\frac{s}{r_{1}(s)}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{1-s}{r_{2}(s)}\right)^{2}\right)^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

Since
p
is rotation-invariant in each coordinate,
p\left(\zeta\right)=\check{p}(s)
. Thus,

(3.8)

p(\zeta)=1+\left(\frac{\kappa_{3}}{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}}\left(\zeta\right)\times\sqrt{\left(\frac{s}{r_{1}(s)}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{1-s}{r_{2}(s)}\right)^{2}}\right).

Let
\phi
be as in (
2.1
), and

(3.9)

\widetilde{\rho}(z_{1},z_{2})=|z_{2}|-\phi(|z_{1}|),\qquad(z_{1},z_{2})\in\overline{\mathbb{B}^{1}(b_{1})}\times\mathbb{C}.

Note that
\widetilde{\rho}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth defining function of
\Omega
near any
\zeta\in b\Omega_{+}
. By
[
5
, (2.23)]
,
\phi^{{}^{\prime}}\left({r_{1}(s)}\right)=-\frac{sr_{2}(s)}{(1-s)r_{1}(s)}
.
Thus, for this specific choice of defining function, we obtain that

\frac{\|\nabla\widetilde{\rho}(\zeta)\|}{2\operatorname{Re}\left<\partial{\widetilde{\rho}}(\zeta),\zeta\right>}=\frac{\sqrt{1+\phi^{{}^{\prime}}\left({r_{1}(s)}\right)^{2}}}{r_{2}(s)-r_{1}(s)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}\left({r_{1}(s)}\right)}=\sqrt{\left(\frac{s}{r_{1}(s)}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{1-s}{r_{2}(s)}\right)^{2}}.

Combining this with (
3.8
), we obtain (
3.9
) for this specific choice of defining. However,
\frac{\|\nabla\rho(\zeta)\|}{2\operatorname{Re}\left<\partial{\rho}(\zeta),\zeta\right>}
is independent of the choice of
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-defining function near
\zeta
. Our proof is complete.

∎

The above parameterization transforms particularly well under the action of taking the dual complement of
\Omega
; see
[
5
, §7]
. Let
\left(\check{p}^{*},b_{1}^{*},b_{2}^{*}\right)
be the tuple corresponding to
\Omega^{*}
. Then

(3.10)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\check{p}}+\frac{1}{\check{p}^{*}}\equiv 1\,\,\text{and}\,\,b_{1}^{*}b_{1}=b_{2}^{*}b_{2}=1.

Consequently,
b\Omega^{*}=\left\{\left(r_{1}^{*}(s)e^{i\theta_{1}},r_{2}^{*}(s)e^{i\theta_{2}}\right):0\leq s\leq 1,(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in[0,2\pi)\times[0,2\pi)\right\}
,
where

(3.11)

\displaystyle r_{1}^{*}(s)=\frac{s}{r_{1}(s)}\,\,\text{and}\,\,r_{2}^{*}(s)=\frac{(1-s)}{r_{2}(s)},\quad\text{for all }s\in[0,1].

To compute the map
T_{\Omega}:(0,\infty)\times b\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2}
as in (
2.12
), observe that as
\Omega
is Reinhardt and
m_{\Omega}
is
1
-homogeneous
2\left<\partial m_{\Omega}(\zeta),\zeta\right>=1,\,\forall\zeta\in b\Omega
; see
[
29
, Proposition 18]
. Thus, for any
\zeta\in b\Omega_{+}
,

2\dfrac{\partial{m_{\Omega}}}{\partial{z_{l}}}(\zeta)=\frac{\dfrac{\partial{m_{\Omega}}}{\partial{z_{l}}}(\zeta)}{\left<\partial m_{\Omega}(\zeta),\zeta\right>}=\frac{\dfrac{\partial{\widetilde{\rho}}}{\partial{z_{l}}}(\zeta)}{\left<\partial\widetilde{\rho}(\zeta),\zeta\right>},\quad l=1,2,

where
\widetilde{\rho}
is as in (
3.9
), and the last equality holds since both
m_{\Omega}-1
and
\tilde{\rho}
are local defining functions of
b\Omega_{+}
. Thus, for
\zeta=\left(r_{1}(s)e^{i\theta_{1}},r_{2}(s)e^{i\theta_{2}}\right)
,

(3.12)

T_{\Omega}(\zeta)=r\left(\frac{s}{r_{1}(s)}e^{-i\theta_{1}},\frac{(1-s)}{r_{2}(s)}e^{-i\theta_{2}}\right)=r\left(r_{1}^{*}(s)e^{-i\theta_{1}},r_{2}^{*}(s)e^{-i\theta_{2}}\right).

3.2.
Series expansions for Hardy and Bergman-space functions

It is well-known that the functions in the Hardy space of the unit disc are precisely those whose Fourier expansions have no negative Fourier coefficients. Due to the toric symmetry and convexity of domains in
\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
, an analogous statement holds for functions in Hardy spaces on such domains. To see this, let
\vartheta:(0,1)\times[0,2\pi)^{2}\rightarrow b\Omega_{+}
denote the parametrization given by
(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\mapsto\left(r_{1}(s)e^{i\theta_{1}},r_{2}(s)e^{i\theta_{2}}\right)
. Then, by Proposition
2.4
and
[
5
, (4.2)]
,

(3.13)

\left(\vartheta^{*}\mu_{\Omega}\right)(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\>ds\,d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}.

Thus, there is a normed space isomorphism between
L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
and
L^{2}((0,1)\times[0,2\pi)^{2},ds\>d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2})
. In view of this, for any
f\in L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
, we abbreviate
f(r_{1}(s)e^{i\theta_{1}},r_{2}(s)e^{i\theta_{2}})
to
f(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
, and identify the two spaces without further comment.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Omega\in\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
. Then,
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}\left(\Omega,\mu_{\Omega}\right)
if and only if

(3.14)

f(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}a_{m_{1},m_{2}}{r_{1}(s)}^{m_{1}}{r_{2}(s)}^{m_{2}}e^{i({\theta_{1}{m_{1}}+\theta_{2}{m_{2}}})}\quad\left(\text{in }L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})\right)

for some sequence of complex numbers
(a_{m_{1},m_{2}})_{\mathbb{N}^{2}}
satisfying

(3.15)

\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}|a_{m_{1},m_{2}}|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}r_{1}(s)^{2m_{1}}r_{2}(s)^{2m_{2}}ds\right)<\infty.

Moreover, if
f
and
(a_{m_{1},m_{2}})_{\mathbb{N}^{2}}
are related as in (
3.14
), then

(3.16)

\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})}\approx\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}|a_{m_{1},m_{2}}|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}r_{1}(s)^{2m_{1}}r_{2}(s)^{2m_{2}}ds\right).

Proof.

Let
\Omega\in\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
, and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
. For each
s\in(0,1)
, let
f_{s}:[0,2\pi)\times[0,2\pi)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be given by

f_{s}\left(\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\right)=f\left(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\right).

Then,
f_{s}\in L^{2}\left(d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}\right)
for almost every
s\in(0,1).

Thus, for almost every
s\in(0,1)
,

(3.17)

f_{s}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\widehat{f_{s}}(m_{1},m_{2})e^{i(\theta_{1}m_{1}+\theta_{2}m_{2})}\quad\text{in }L^{2}\left(d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}\right),

where
\widehat{f_{s}}(m_{1},m_{2})
,
(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, are the Fourier coefficients of
f_{s}
.

Since holomorphic polynomials are dense in
\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
, there is a sequence of polynomials

p_{k}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)=\sum_{m_{1}=0}^{j_{1}(k)}\sum_{m_{2}=0}^{j_{2}(k)}a_{m_{1},m_{2}}^{(k)}z_{1}^{m_{1}}z_{2}^{m_{2}},\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

that converges to
f
in
L^{2}\left(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega}\right)
. For each
s\in(0,1)
, we have that

\displaystyle\widehat{\left(p_{k}\right)_{s}}(m_{1},m_{2})=\begin{cases}a_{m_{1},m_{2}}^{(k)}r_{1}(s)^{m_{1}}r_{2}(s)^{m_{2}},\quad&0\leq m_{1}\leq j_{1}(k),0\leq m_{2}\leq j_{2}(k),\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

By Parseval’s formula and Fubini’s theorem, we obtain that

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|p_{k}-f|^{2}d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}\,ds\approx\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\widehat{\left(p_{k}\right)_{s}}(m_{1},m_{2})-\widehat{f_{s}}(m_{1},m_{2})\right|^{2}ds.

Since the sequence on the left-hand side converges to
0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
,

(3.18)

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\widehat{\left(p_{k}\right)_{s}}(m_{1},m_{2})-\widehat{f_{s}}(m_{1},m_{2})\right|^{2}ds=0,\quad\forall(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

In particular, for each
(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, there exists some
a_{m_{1},m_{2}}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\widehat{\left(p_{k}\right)_{s}}(m_{1},m_{2})\>ds

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{1}r_{1}(s)^{m_{1}}r_{2}(s)^{m_{2}}ds\right)\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}a_{m_{1},m_{2}}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{1}r_{1}(s)^{m_{1}}r_{2}(s)^{m_{2}}ds\right)a_{m_{1},m_{2}}.

Since
\left(a_{m_{1},m_{2}}^{(k)}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is the constant zero sequence whenever
\min\{m_{1},m_{2}\}<0
, we have that
a_{m_{1},m_{2}}=0
whenever
\min\{m_{1},m_{2}\}<0
. Now, by the fact that
L^{2}
-convergence implies a.e. pointwise convergence of a subsequence, we obtain that, for a.e.
s\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\widehat{f_{s}}(m_{1},m_{2})=\begin{cases}a_{m_{1},m_{2}}r_{1}(s)^{m_{1}}r_{2}(s)^{m_{2}},\quad&\min\{m_{1},m_{2}\}\geq 0,\\
0,&\min\{m_{1},m_{2}\}<0.\end{cases}

This yields that (
3.14
), (
3.15
), and (
3.16
) hold for any
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
.

Conversely, if
(a_{m_{1},m_{2}})_{\mathbb{N}^{2}}
is a sequence of complex numbers satisfying (
3.15
), then the function
f
defined as in (
3.14
) is in
L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
, and is approximable therein by holomorphic polynomials of the form
p_{k}(z_{1},z_{2})=\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}=0}^{k}a_{m_{1},m_{2}}z_{1}^{m_{1}}z_{2}^{m_{2}}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus,
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
.
∎

It is now possible to write the power series expansion of the Laplace transform of a Hardy-space function.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Omega\in\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
, and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
admit the expansion (
3.14
) in
L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
. Then, the Laplace transform of
f
is an entire function on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
with power series expansion

(3.19)

\mathcal{L}\left(f\right)(z_{1},z_{2})=\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}t_{m_{1},m_{2}}z_{1}^{m_{1}}z_{2}^{m_{2}},

where the coefficients
t_{{m_{1}},{m_{2}}}
are given by

(3.20)

t_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\frac{1}{4}\frac{\overline{a_{m_{1},m_{2}}}}{m_{1}!m_{2}!}\left(\int_{0}^{1}r_{1}(s)^{2m_{1}}r_{2}(s)^{2m_{2}}ds\right).

Proof.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, for any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
, there is a
C_{K}>0
such that

\|\mathcal{L}(f)\|^{2}_{\mathcal{C}(K)}\leq C_{K}\|f\|^{2}_{\mu_{\Omega}},\quad f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\Omega}).

Thus, it suffices to establish the claim for polynomials of the form

p_{k}\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)=\sum\limits_{m_{1},m_{2}=0}^{k}a_{m_{1},m_{2}}z_{1}^{m_{1}}\,z_{2}^{m_{2}}.

Expanding
e^{\langle.,z\rangle}
in terms of its power series and using the parameterization of
b\Omega
discussed in Section
3.1
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(p_{k}\right)\left(z_{1},z_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{b\Omega}\overline{{p_{k}}(\zeta)}e^{\langle\zeta,z\rangle}d\mu_{\Omega}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left({\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}=0}^{k}\overline{a_{m_{1},m_{2}}}{r_{1}(s)}^{m_{1}}{r_{2}(s)}^{m_{2}}e^{-i({\theta_{1}m_{1}+\theta_{2}m_{2}})}}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\times\left(\sum_{(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\frac{r_{1}(s)^{k_{1}}r_{2}(s)^{k_{2}}e^{i(k_{1}\theta_{1}+k_{2}\theta_{2})}}{k_{1}!k_{2}!}z_{1}^{k_{1}}z_{2}^{k_{2}}\right)d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}{\sum_{m_{1},m_{2}=0}^{k}}\frac{\overline{a_{m_{1},m_{2}}}}{m_{1}!m_{2}!}\left(\int_{0}^{1}r_{1}(s)^{2m_{1}}r_{2}(s)^{2m_{2}}\,ds\right)z_{1}^{m_{1}}z_{2}^{m_{2}},

where the last equality is obtained by integrating over the
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
variables.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Omega\in\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
. Let
F
be an entire function on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
with power series expansion

(3.21)

F(z_{1},z_{2})=\sum_{\left(m_{1},m_{2}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\beta_{m_{1},m_{2}}z_{1}^{m_{1}}z_{2}^{m_{2}},

for some sequence of complex numbers
(\beta_{m_{1},m_{2}})_{\mathbb{N}^{2}}
. Then,
F\in\mathcal{A}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2},\nu_{\Omega})
if and only if

(3.22)

\sum_{\left(m_{1},m_{2}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}|\beta_{m_{1},m_{2}}|^{2}(m_{1}+m_{2}+1)!^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}(r_{1}^{*}(s))^{2m_{1}}(r_{2}^{*}(s))^{2m_{2}}ds\right)<\infty.

Moreover, if
F\in\mathcal{A}^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2},\nu_{\Omega})
, then

(3.23)

\|F\|^{2}_{\nu_{\Omega}}\approx\sum_{\left(m_{1},m_{2}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}|\beta_{m_{1},m_{2}}|^{2}(m_{1}+m_{2}+1)!^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}(r_{1}^{*}(s))^{2m_{1}}(r_{2}^{*}(s))^{2m_{2}}ds\right).

Proof.

First, since
B\left(0,r_{1}\right)\Subset\Omega\Subset B\left(0,r_{2}\right)
, for some
r_{1},r_{2}>0
, we have that
r_{1}\|z\|\leq H_{\Omega}(z)\leq r_{2}\|z\|
, i.e.,
H_{\Omega}(z)\approx\|z\|
. Now, we apply (
2.13
) to
\psi=|F|^{2}e^{-2H_{\Omega}}H_{\Omega}^{3/2}
, noting that
H_{\Omega}(T_{\Omega}(r,\zeta))=r
. Then, parametrizing
b\Omega
as in Section
3.1
, expressing
T_{\Omega}
and
\mu_{\Omega}
in terms of this parametrization as in (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\|F\|_{\nu_{\Omega}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\left|F(z)\right|^{2}e^{-2H_{\Omega}(z)}\|z\|^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(dd^{c}H_{\Omega}\right)^{2}(z)

\displaystyle\approx\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\left|F(z)\right|^{2}e^{-2H_{\Omega}(z)}H_{\Omega}(z)^{\frac{3}{2}}\left(dd^{c}H_{\Omega}\right)^{2}(z)

\displaystyle\approx\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{b\Omega}\left|F\left(T_{\Omega}\left(r,\zeta\right)\right)\right|^{2}e^{-2r}r^{\frac{5}{2}}d\mu_{\Omega}(\zeta)dr

\displaystyle\approx\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\,\int_{0}^{2\pi}\,\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|F(rr_{1}^{*}(s)e^{-i\theta_{1}},rr_{2}^{*}(s)e^{-i\theta_{2}})\right|^{2}e^{-2r}r^{\frac{5}{2}}d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}dsdr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(4\pi^{2}\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}r^{2(m_{1}+m_{2})}|\beta_{m_{1},m_{2}}|^{2}\int_{0}^{1}{r_{1}^{*}(s)}^{2m_{1}}{r_{2}^{*}(s)}^{2m_{2}}ds\right)e^{-2r}r^{\frac{5}{2}}dr

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}|\beta_{m_{1},m_{2}}|^{2}(m_{1}+m_{2}+1)!^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}r_{1}^{*}(s)^{2m_{1}}r_{2}^{*}(s)^{2m_{2}}\,ds\right),

where, in the final step, we use the following consequence of Stirling’s approximation:

(3.24)

\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{2k+1}e^{-2r}r^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,dr\right)\approx(k!)^{2}.

From this comparison, the lemma immediately follows.

3.3.
The Leray transform.

We briefly recall the construction and properties of the Leray transform as given in
[
5
, §3]
. Given
\Omega\in\mathcal{\widetilde{R}}
,

\mathbb{L}(f)(z)=\int_{b\Omega}f(\zeta)L(\zeta,z),\quad z\in\Omega,

where, the (Leray) kernel is given by

L(\zeta,z)=\frac{d\mu_{\Omega}(\zeta)}{\langle\partial m_{\Omega}(\zeta),\zeta-z\rangle^{2}}=\mathds{1}_{b\Omega_{+}}(\zeta)\frac{j^{*}\left(d^{c}m_{\Omega}\wedge dd^{c}m_{\Omega}\right)(\zeta)}{\langle\partial m_{\Omega}(\zeta),\zeta-z\rangle^{2}},

by Proposition
2.4
. Since the right-hand side is independent of the choice of
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth defining function of
b\Omega_{+}
, we replace
m_{\Omega}
by
\rho
from
(
3.9
), and use the parametrization given in (
3.2
). Thus, for
\zeta=\left(r_{1}(s)e^{i\theta_{1}},r_{2}(s)e^{i\theta_{2}}\right)\in b\Omega
and
z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle L(\zeta,z)=\frac{ds\,d\theta_{1}\,d\theta_{2}}{4\pi^{2}}\sum\limits_{m_{1},m_{2}=0}^{\infty}\frac{(m_{1}+m_{2}+1)!}{m_{1}!m_{2}!}r_{1}^{*}(s)^{m_{1}}r_{2}^{*}(s)^{m_{2}}z_{1}^{m_{1}}z_{2}^{m_{2}}e^{-i\left(m_{1}\theta_{1}+m_{2}\theta_{2}\right)},

where the above series converges uniformly in
z
on compact subsets of
\Omega
.

Following
[
5
]
, a function of the form
g(s)e^{i\left(m_{1}\theta_{1}+m_{2}\theta_{2}\right)}
is called an
(m_{1},m_{2})
-monomial, and the space of all
(m_{1},m_{2})
-monomials that are in
L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
is denoted by
L^{2}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
. The spaces
L^{2}_{m_{1},m_{2}}\left(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega}\right)
,
(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
, are mutually orthogonal, and

(3.25)

L^{2}\left(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega}\right)=\underset{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}{\bigoplus}L^{2}_{m_{1},m_{2}}\left(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega}\right).

By direct computation, it is shown in
[
5
, Corollary 23]
that
\mathbb{L}
and
\mathbb{L}_{b}
are well-defined on each
L^{2}_{m_{1},m_{2}}(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
, and therefore densely-defined on
L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})
. Furthermore,
\mathbb{L}_{b}
reproduces (the restrictions to

-monomial with

. Finally, since the measure

is admissible in the sense of
[

, Definition 28]
, we have the following result as a combination of Proposition 26, Theorem 30 and Proposition 32 in
[

]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

. Given

, let

to

(1)

If

6.1.
Proof of Theorem

By (

\kappa_{\Omega}(\zeta)=\frac{\left|\zeta_{1}\right|\phi^{\prime\prime}(\left|\zeta_{1}\right|)\phi(\left|\zeta_{1}\right|)}{\phi^{\prime}(\left|\zeta_{1}\right|)\left(1+\phi^{\prime}(\left|\zeta_{1}\right|)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

The hypothesis on

implies that
\lim_{|\zeta_{1}|\rightarrow 0}\kappa_{\Omega}(\zeta)
and
\lim_{|\zeta_{1}|\rightarrow b_{2}}\kappa_{\Omega}(\zeta)
are finite and positive. Due to the strong convexity of

is a strictly decreasing negative

\lim_{\left|\zeta_{1}\right|\rightarrow 0}\phi^{\prime}(\left|\zeta_{1}\right|)=l\in(-\infty,0]

\lim_{\left|\zeta_{1}\right|\rightarrow 0}\left|\zeta_{1}\right|\phi^{\prime\prime}(\left|\zeta_{1}\right|)<0.

\lim_{\left|\zeta_{1}\right|\rightarrow 0}\phi^{\prime}(\left|\zeta_{1}\right|)=\infty

\lim_{\left|\zeta_{1}\right|\rightarrow 0}\phi^{\prime}(\left|\zeta_{1}\right|)=0

\lim_{\left|\zeta_{1}\right|\rightarrow b_{1}}\phi^{\prime}(\left|\zeta_{1}\right|)=-\infty

\Omega

\mathcal{C}^{1}

6.2.

Note that

(i)

The boundary of

admits the

\displaystyle\vartheta:[0,1]\times[0,2\pi)^{2}

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle b\Omega,

(6.1)

\displaystyle(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})

\displaystyle\left(se^{i\theta_{1}},(1-s)e^{i\theta_{2}}\right).

(ii)

The Minskwoski functional and the support function of

are given by
m_{\Omega}(z_{1},z_{2})=|z_{1}|+|z_{2}|

(iii)

(iv)

The Monge–Ampère measure of

\displaystyle(dd^{c}H_{\Omega})^{2}(z)

\displaystyle\approx j^{*}\left(\frac{\overline{z_{1}}}{|z_{1}|}\;dz_{1}dz_{2}d\overline{z_{2}}-\frac{\overline{z_{2}}}{|z_{2}|}\;dz_{1}dz_{2}d\overline{z_{1}}\right).

Parametrizing

\vartheta_{M}\left(r,\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\right)=\left(re^{i\psi_{1}},re^{i\psi_{2}}\right)

(6.2)

\vartheta_{M}^{*}(dd^{c}H_{\Omega})^{2}\left(r,\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\right)\approx drd\psi_{1}d\psi_{2},\quad(r,\psi_{1},\psi_{2})\in(0,\infty)\times[0,2\pi)^{2}.

(v)

Analogous to Lemma

if and only if

(6.3)

f(s,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}a_{m_{1},m_{2}}{s}^{m_{1}}{(1-s)}^{m_{2}}e^{i({\theta_{1}{m_{1}}+\theta_{2}{m_{2}}})}\quad\left(\text{in }L^{2}(b\Omega,\mu_{\Omega})\right)

(6.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbb{N}^{2}}|a_{m_{1},m_{2}}|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{1}s^{2m_{1}}(1-s)^{2m_{2}}ds\right)=\sum_{\mathbb{N}^{2}}|a_{m_{1},m_{2}}|^{2}\dfrac{2m_{1}!2m_{2}!}{(2m_{1}+2m_{2}+1)!}<\infty.

Moreover,

(6.5)

\|f\|^{2}_{\mu_{\Omega}}\approx\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}|a_{m_{1},m_{2}}|^{2}\dfrac{2m_{1}!2m_{2}!}{(2m_{1}+2m_{2}+1)!}.

(vi)

Analogous to Lemma
3.3
, if
f
is as in (
6.3
), we have that
\mathcal{L}(f)
is entire on

and admits the following power series expansion:

(6.6)

\mathcal{L}(f)(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\dfrac{\overline{a_{m_{1},m_{2}}}}{m_{1}!m_{2}!}\dfrac{2m_{1}!2m_{2}!}{(2m_{1}+2m_{2}+1)!}z_{1}^{m_{1}}z_{2}^{m_{2}},

where the above series converges uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

(vii)

Analogous to Lemma
3.4
, if
F\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
has expansion
\sum_{\mathbb{N}^{2}}\beta_{m_{1},m_{2}}z_{1}^{m_{1}}z_{2}^{m_{2}}
, then

\displaystyle\|F\|^{2}_{\nu_{\Omega}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|F\left(re^{i\psi_{1}},re^{i\psi_{2}}\right)\right|^{2}e^{-2r}r^{\frac{3}{2}}d\psi_{1}d\psi_{2}dr

(6.7)

\displaystyle\approx\sum\limits_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\frac{|\beta_{m_{1},m_{2}}|^{2}}{2^{2m_{1}+2m_{2}}}\Gamma\left(2m_{1}+2m_{2}+5/2\right).

It remains to characterize the power series expansions of entire functions in
A^{2}(\Omega,\omega_{\Omega})
. For this, we analyze the function

on
M
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\Omega,\mu_{\Omega}
be as above. Then

(6.8)

\|e^{\langle z,.\rangle}\|^{2}_{\mu_{\Omega}}\approx{e^{2H_{\Omega}(z)}}{\|z\|^{-1}},\quad z\in M\cap\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:H_{\Omega}(z)>1\}.

Proof.

Parametrizing

\{\left(re^{i\psi_{1}},re^{i\psi_{2}}\right):r>0,\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in[0,2\pi)\}
, we have that

\displaystyle{e^{-2H_{\Omega}(z)}}\|z\|\|e^{\langle z,.\rangle}\|^{2}_{\mu_{\Omega}}

\displaystyle\approx r\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{2r\left(s\cos(\theta_{1}-\psi_{1})+(1-s)\cos(\theta_{2}-\psi_{2})-1\right)}d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}ds

\displaystyle\approx r\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{2r\left(s\cos(\theta_{1})+(1-s)\cos(\theta_{2})-1\right)}d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}ds

\displaystyle=r\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{-4r\left(s\sin^{2}(\theta_{1}/2)+(1-s)\sin^{2}(\theta_{2}/2)\right)}d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}ds.

Now, using that,
\sin^{2}\frac{\theta}{2}\approx\theta^{2}
on

, and that

∎

Combining the above lemma with (
6.2
), we conclude that if
F\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{2})
has expansion
\sum_{\mathbb{N}^{2}}\beta_{m_{1},m_{2}}z_{1}^{m_{1}}z_{2}^{m_{2}}
, then

(6.9)

\displaystyle\|F\|^{2}_{\omega_{\Omega}}

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|F\left(re^{i\psi_{1}},re^{i\psi_{2}}\right)\right|^{2}e^{-2r}rd\psi_{1}d\psi_{2}dr

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\frac{|\beta_{m_{1},m_{2}}|^{2}}{2^{2m_{1}+2m_{2}}}\left(2m_{1}+2m_{2}+1\right)!.

To see that
A^{2}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2},\nu_{\Omega}\right)\subsetneq A^{2}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2},\omega_{\Omega}\right),
consider

, where

\displaystyle t_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\begin{cases}\frac{2^{2k}}{k^{\frac{3}{4}}{\left(4k+1\right)!}^{\frac{1}{2}}},&\text{when }m_{1}=m_{2}=k>1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then

, and by (

),
\|G\|^{2}_{\omega_{\Omega}}\approx\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{\frac{3}{2}}}<\infty
. Thus

. However, using Stirling’s approximation and (

\|G\|^{2}_{\nu_{\Omega}}\approx\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}=\infty
. Thus

.

To see that
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{2}(\Omega,\mu_{\Omega}))\subsetneq A^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{2},\nu_{\Omega})
, consider
F(z_{1},z_{2})=\sum\limits_{m_{1},m_{2}=0}^{\infty}b_{m_{1},m_{2}}z_{1}^{m_{1}}z_{2}^{m_{2}},
where

\displaystyle b_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\begin{cases}\frac{2^{2k}}{k^{\frac{3}{4}}{\Gamma\left(4k+\frac{5}{2}\right)}^{\frac{1}{2}}},&\text{when }m_{1}=m_{2}=k>1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then,
F\in\mathcal{O}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right)
, and, by (

)

\|F\|^{2}_{\nu_{\Omega}}\approx\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{\frac{3}{2}}}<\infty.

Thus,
F\in A^{2}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2},\nu_{\Omega}\right)
. Now, suppose there is an
f\in\mathcal{H}^{2}\left(\Omega,\mu_{\Omega}\right)
such that
\mathcal{L}(f)=F
. Then,
f
admits an expansion as in (

) for some sequence
(a_{m_{1},m_{2}})_{\mathbb{N}^{2}}
, satisfying (

\displaystyle a_{m_{1},m_{2}}

\displaystyle=4\frac{\overline{b_{m_{1},m_{2}}}}{2m_{1}!2m_{2}!}{\left(2m_{1}+2m_{2}+1\right)!}{\left(m_{1}!m_{2}!\right)},

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{2^{2k+2}k!^{2}}{k^{\frac{3}{4}}(2k!)^{2}}{\Gamma\left(4k+\frac{5}{2}\right)}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(4k+1)!,&\text{when }m_{1}=m_{2}=k>1,\\

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mu_{\Omega}}^{2}\approx\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{4k}(k!)^{4}}{k^{\frac{3}{2}}(2k!)^{2}}\frac{(4k+1)!}{\Gamma\left(4k+\frac{5}{2}\right)}=\infty,

by Stirling’s approximation. This is a contradiction, and our proof is complete.

7.
Comparison of two weighted Bergman spaces

For a strongly convex domain
\Omega
in the plane, it is known that

(7.1)

\omega_{\Omega}(z)\approx e^{-2H_{\Omega}(z)}|z|^{\frac{1}{2}}dd^{c}H_{\Omega}(z)=\nu_{\Omega}(z),\quad\text{for }|z|>1.

However, unlike
\omega_{\Omega}
,
\nu_{\Omega}
does not capture the growth rate of the Laplace transforms of Hardy space functions on other convex planar domains, such as polygons. Thus,
\omega_{\Omega}
has the advantage of providing a unified expression for the Paley–Wiener spaces of Hardy spaces on convex planar domains. Although tacitly implied in
[
29
]
, it is never shown that the weights
\omega_{\Omega}
and
\nu_{\Omega}
are similarly comparable for higher-dimensional strongly convex domains. For the sake of completeness, we provide a proof here.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded strongly convex domain. For
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\mathbb{B}^{n}
,

(7.2)

\|e^{\langle z,\cdot\rangle}\|^{2}_{\sigma_{\Omega}}\approx\frac{e^{2H_{\Omega}(z)}}{\|z\|^{n-\frac{1}{2}}}.

Proof.

First, we specialize to the case of balls. Let
R>0
. We show that, for
\|z\|>1
,

(7.3)

\left\lVert e^{\langle z,\cdot\rangle}\right\rVert^{2}_{\sigma_{\mathbb{B}^{n}(R)}}\approx\frac{e^{2R{\|z\|}}}{\|z\|^{n-\frac{1}{2}}}.

Fix
z
such that
\|z\|>1
.
Then,

e^{-2R{\|z\|}}\|z\|^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\|e^{\langle z,\cdot\rangle}\|^{2}_{\sigma_{\mathbb{B}^{n}(R)}}=\|z\|^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{b\mathbb{B}^{n}(R)}e^{2R\|z\|\left(\operatorname{Re}\langle\lambda,\frac{z}{R\|z\|}\rangle-1\right)}d\sigma_{\mathbb{B}^{n}(R)}(\lambda).

After applying the change of variable
\lambda=R\zeta
, we obtain that

\|z\|^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{b\mathbb{B}^{n}(R)}e^{2R\|z\|\left(\operatorname{Re}\langle\lambda,\frac{z}{R\|z\|}\rangle-1\right)}d\sigma_{\mathbb{B}^{n}(R)}(\lambda)\approx\|z\|^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{b\mathbb{B}^{n}}e^{2R\|z\|\left(\operatorname{Re}\langle\zeta,\frac{z}{\|z\|}\rangle-1\right)}d\sigma_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\zeta).

Next, consider the unitary transformation
U_{z}:{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
such that
U_{z}\left(\frac{\overline{z}}{\|z\|}\right)=\left(1,0,\cdots,0\right)
. After applying the change of variable
\zeta=U_{z}^{*}\left(\eta\right)
, combined with the fact that
\sigma_{b\mathbb{B}^{n}}
is invariant under unitary transformations, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{b\mathbb{B}^{n}}e^{2R\|z\|\left(\operatorname{Re}\langle\zeta,\frac{z}{\|z\|}\rangle-1\right)}d\sigma_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int_{b\mathbb{B}^{n}}e^{-2R\|z\|\left(1-\operatorname{Re}\left\langle\eta,\left(1,0,\cdots,0\right)\right\rangle\right)}d\sigma_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{b\mathbb{B}^{n}}e^{-2R\|z\|\left(1-\operatorname{Re}\eta_{1}\right)}d\sigma_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\eta),

where
\eta=\left(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\cdots,\eta_{n}\right)
.
Using spherical co-ordinates on
b\mathbb{b}\mathbb{B}^{n}
, we obtain that

\int_{b\mathbb{B}^{n}}e^{-2R\|z\|\left(1-\operatorname{Re}\eta_{1}\right)}d\sigma_{b\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\eta)\approx\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{-2R\|z\|\left(1-\cos\theta\right)}\left(\sin\theta\right)^{2n-2}d\theta.

When
\theta\in\left(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi\right)
,
1<\left(1-\cos(\theta_{1})\right)<2
. Thus,

(7.4)

0\leq\|z\|^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\pi}e^{-2R\|z\|\left(1-\cos\theta\right)}\left(\sin\theta\right)^{2n-2}d\theta\lesssim 1.

For
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
,
\sin\theta\approx\theta.
Thus, for all
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, we have that

\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}e^{-\alpha\theta^{2}}\theta^{2n-2}d\theta\lesssim\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}e^{-2R\|z\|\left(1-\cos\theta\right)}\left(\sin\theta\right)^{2n-2}d\theta\lesssim\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}e^{-\beta\theta^{2}}\theta^{2n-2}d\theta,

where
\alpha=C_{1}\|z\|
and
\beta=C_{2}\|z\|
for some constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
that are independent of
z
.

Fix a
k>0
, then applying the change of variable
x=\sqrt{\gamma}\theta
, we have that, for
\gamma\geq k
,

\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}e^{-\gamma\theta^{2}}\theta^{2n-2}d\theta=\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}-n}\int_{0}^{\frac{\sqrt{\gamma}\pi}{2}}e^{-x^{2}}x^{2n-2}dx\approx\gamma^{\frac{1}{2}-n}.

Hence,

(7.5)

\|z\|^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}e^{-2R\|z\|\left(1-\cos\theta\right)}\left(\sin\theta\right)^{2n-2}d\theta\approx 1.

Finally, combining (
7.4
) and (
7.5
), we conclude the proof in the special case of balls.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
be a bounded strongly convex domain. Without loss of generality, assume
0\in\Omega
. We collect some facts about the support function
H_{\Omega}
of
\Omega
; see
[
36
, §2.5]
. Note that the definition of support function
h_{\Omega}
considered in
[
36
]
is different from
H_{\Omega}
. However, they are related by
H_{\Omega}(z)=h_{\Omega}(\bar{z})
,
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

(1)

H_{\Omega}
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
smooth on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.

(2)

The map
\lambda\rightarrow 2\partial H_{\Omega}(\overline{\lambda})
is a
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-difeomorphsim from
b\mathbb{B}^{n}
onto
b\Omega
.

(3)

Owing to the
1
-homegeneity of
H_{\Omega}
, we may write (abusing notation):

(7.6)

H_{\Omega}(z)=2\operatorname{Re}\langle\partial H_{\Omega}(z),z\rangle,\quad z\in{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\setminus\{0\}.

(4)

For any real-valued function
f
that is measurable with respect to
\sigma_{\Omega}
,

(7.7)

\int_{b\Omega}f(\zeta)d\sigma_{\Omega}(\zeta)\approx\int_{b\mathbb{B}^{n}}f(2\partial H_{\Omega}(\overline{\lambda}))d\sigma_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\lambda).

(5)

The polar domain
\Omega^{\circ}
of
\Omega
given by
\Omega^{\circ}=\left\{z:H_{\Omega}(\overline{z})<1\right\}
is also strongly convex.

By (
7.7
) and (
7.6
), we have that

(7.8)

\frac{1}{e^{2H_{\Omega}(\overline{z})}}\int_{b\Omega}e^{2\operatorname{Re}\langle\zeta,\overline{z}\rangle}d\sigma_{\Omega}(\zeta)\approx\int_{b\mathbb{B}^{n}}e^{4\|z\|\left(\operatorname{Re}\left\langle\partial H_{\Omega}(\overline{\lambda})-\partial H_{\Omega}(\overline{z}),\frac{\overline{z}}{\|z\|}\right\rangle\right)}d\sigma_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\lambda).

Note that, using (
7.6
) twice, we obtain that for
\left(\lambda,z\right)\in b\mathbb{B}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
,

(7.9)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\left\langle\partial H_{\Omega}(\overline{z})-\partial H_{\Omega}(\overline{\lambda}),\frac{\overline{z}}{\|z\|}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\left\langle\partial H_{\Omega}(\overline{z})-\partial H_{\Omega}(\overline{\lambda}),\frac{\overline{z}}{H_{\Omega}(\overline{z})}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-\operatorname{Re}\left\langle\partial H_{\Omega}(\overline{\lambda}),\frac{\overline{\lambda}}{H_{\Omega}(\overline{\lambda})}-\frac{\overline{\lambda}}{H_{\Omega}(\overline{\lambda})}+\frac{\overline{z}}{H_{\Omega}(\overline{z})}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\left\langle\partial H_{\Omega}(\overline{\lambda}),\frac{\overline{\lambda}}{H_{\Omega}(\overline{\lambda})}-\frac{\overline{z}}{H_{\Omega}(\overline{z})}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\left\langle\partial H_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\overline{\lambda}}{H_{\Omega}(\overline{\lambda})}\right),\frac{\overline{\lambda}}{H_{\Omega}(\overline{\lambda})}-\frac{\overline{z}}{H_{\Omega}(\overline{z})}\right\rangle.

Now, by the definition of
\Omega^{\circ}
, it is clear that for any
w\neq 0
,
\frac{w}{H_{\Omega}(\overline{w})}\in b\Omega^{\circ}
. Considering
H_{\Omega}(\bar{z})-1
to be the defining function of
\Omega^{\circ}
, we get from
[
17
, Lemma 5.1]
that, for all
(\lambda,z)\in b\mathbb{B}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
,

(7.10)

\operatorname{Re}\left\langle\partial H_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\overline{\lambda}}{H_{\Omega}(\overline{\lambda})}\right),\frac{\overline{\lambda}}{H_{\Omega}(\overline{\lambda})}-\frac{\overline{z}}{H_{\Omega}(\overline{z})}\right\rangle\approx\left\|\frac{z}{H_{\Omega}(\overline{z})}-\frac{\lambda}{H_{\Omega}(\overline{\lambda})}\right\|^{2}.

Moreover, for all
(\lambda,z)\in b\mathbb{B}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
,

(7.11)

\left\|\frac{z}{H_{\Omega}(\overline{z})}-\frac{\lambda}{H_{\Omega}(\overline{\lambda})}\right\|^{2}\approx\left\|\frac{z}{\|z\|}-\lambda\right\|^{2}=2\operatorname{Re}\left\langle\frac{z}{\|z\|}-\lambda,\frac{\overline{z}}{\|z\|}\right\rangle.

Combining (
7.9
), (
7.10
), (
7.11
), and (
7.8
), we note that it suffices to estimate

\int_{b\mathbb{B}^{n}}e^{2C\|z\|\left(\operatorname{Re}\left\langle\lambda,\frac{\bar{z}}{\|z\|}\right\rangle-1\right)}d\sigma_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\lambda)=C^{1-2n}\|e^{\langle\overline{z},\cdot\rangle}\|^{2}_{\sigma_{\mathbb{B}^{n}(C)}}e^{-2C\|z\|},

where
C>0
is some constant. Now, applying (
7.3
) to the right-hand side, we conclude the proof.
∎

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded strongly convex domain. Then, the identity map is a normed space isomorphism between
A^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},\omega_{\Omega})
and
A^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},\nu_{\Omega})
.

Proof.

We must show that

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|F(z)|^{2}d\omega_{\Omega}(z)\approx\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|F(z)|^{2}d\nu_{\Omega}(z),

uniformly for all
F\in\mathcal{O}\left(\mathbb{C}^{2}\right).
Due to Lemma
7.1
, it suffices to prove that

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|F(z)|^{2}\left(dd^{c}H_{\Omega}\right)^{n}(z)\approx\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|F(z)|^{2}\|z\|^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\left(dd^{c}H_{\Omega}\right)^{n}(z),

where
F
is a holomorphic polynomial. This can be verified easily using spherical coordinates and the fact that
\|z\|^{n}\left(dd^{c}H_{\Omega}\right)^{n}\approx\omega_{std}
.
∎

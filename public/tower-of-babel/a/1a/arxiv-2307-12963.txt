On the asymptotic expansions of various quantum invariants I: the colored Jones polynomial of twist knots at the root of unity e 2 ​ π ​ − 1 N + 1 2 e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{N+\frac{1}{2}}}

Abstract
. This is the first article in a series devoted to the study of the asymptotic expansions of various quantum invariants related to the twist knots. In this paper, by using the saddle point method developed by Ohtsuki, we obtain an asymptotic expansion formula for the colored Jones polynomial of twist knots 𝒦 p \mathcal{K}_{p} with p ≥ 6 p\geq 6 at the root of unity e 2 ​ π ​ − 1 N + 1 2 e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{N+\frac{1}{2}}} .

1.
Introduction

In this series of articles, we study the asymptotic expansions of various quantum invariants at different roots of unit and make a connection between them.

This work is motivated by the volume conjectures, let us briefly review the background. In
[
20
]
, by using the quantum dilogarithm function, R. Kashaev defined a link invariant
\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle_{N}
for a link
\mathcal{L}
, which depends on a positive integer
N
. Furthermore, in
[
21
]
, he conjectured that for any hyperbolic link
\mathcal{L}
, the asymptotics at
N\rightarrow\infty
of
|\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle_{N}|
gives its volume, i.e.

(1.1)

\displaystyle 2\pi\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle_{N}|}{N}=vol(S^{3}\setminus\mathcal{L})

where
vol(S^{3}\setminus\mathcal{L})
denotes the hyperbolic volume of the complement of
\mathcal{L}
in
S^{3}
, and gave evidence for the conjecture. In
[
27
]
, H. Murakami and J. Murakami proved that for any link
\mathcal{L}
, Kashaev’s invariant

\langle\mathcal{L}\rangle_{N}
is equal to
N
-th normalized colored Jones polynomial evaluated at the root of unity
e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{N}}
, which is written as
J_{N}(\mathcal{L};e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{N}})
, and they extended Kashaev’s conjecture as follows

(1.2)

\displaystyle 2\pi\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log|J_{N}(\mathcal{L};e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{N}})|}{N}=vol(S^{3}\setminus\mathcal{L}),

where
vol(S^{3}\setminus\mathcal{L})
denotes the simplicial volume of the complement of
\mathcal{L}
in
S^{3}
. This is usally called the (Kashaev-Murakami-Murakami) volume conjecture. Furthermore, as a complexification of the volume conjecture, it is conjectured in
[
29
]
that, for a hyperbolic link
\mathcal{L}
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle 2\pi\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\log J_{N}(\mathcal{L};e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{N}})}{N}=vol(S^{3}\setminus\mathcal{L})+\sqrt{-1}cs(S^{3}\setminus\mathcal{L}),

for an appropriate choice of a branch of the logarithm, where
cs
denotes the Chern-Simons invariant
[
26
]
.
From the viewpoint of the
SL(2,\mathbb{C})
Chern-Simons theory, S. Gukov conjectured in
[
16
]
that the asymptotic expansion of
J_{N}(\mathcal{K};e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{k}})
of a hyperbolic knot as
N,k\rightarrow\infty
fixing
u=\frac{N}{k}
is presented by the following form,

(1.4)

\displaystyle J_{N}(\mathcal{K};e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{k}})\sim e^{N\zeta}N^{\frac{3}{2}}\omega\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\kappa_{i}\left(\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{N}\right)^{i}\right)

for some scalars
\zeta,\omega,\kappa_{i}
depending on
\mathcal{K}
and
u
, also see
[
10
,
17
]
. Moreover, T. Ohtuski showed when
\mathcal{K}
is a hyperbolic knot with up to 7 crossings
[
31
,
34
,
32
]
, the asymptotic expansions of the Kashaev invariant is presented by the following form

(1.5)

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{K}\rangle_{N}=e^{N\zeta}N^{\frac{3}{2}}\omega(\mathcal{K})\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\kappa_{i}(\mathcal{K})\left(\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{N}\right)^{i}+O\left(\frac{1}{N^{d+1}}\right)\right),

for any
d
, where
\omega(\mathcal{K})
and
\kappa_{i}(\mathcal{K})
’s are some scalars.

The volume conjecture has been rigorously proved for some particular knots and links such as torus knots
[
22
,
11
]
, the figure-eight knot
[
1
]
, Whitehead doubles of
(2,p)
-torus knots
[

]
, positive ierated torus knots
[

]
, the
5_{2}

[

, the knots with 6 crossings

and some links
[

On the other hand, it is known that the quantum invariant of a closed 3-manifold at
q=e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{N}}
is of polynomial order as
N\rightarrow\infty
. However, the first author and T. Yang
[

observed that Reshetikhin-Turaev invariants and Turaev-Viro invariants at

. Furthermore, they proposed the volume conjecture for Reshetikhin-Turaev invariants and Turaev-Viro invariants.

In
[
12

, Detcherry, Kalfagianni and Yang gave a formula relating the Turaev-Viro invariants of the

In addition, they proposed the following

(
[
12
]

.

For a hyperbolic link
\mathcal{L}
in

(1.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log|J_{N}(\mathcal{L};e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{N+\frac{1}{2}}})|=vol(S^{3}\setminus\mathcal{L}).

The asymptotic behavior of

is not predicted
either by the original volume conjecture (
1.2

1.4

The purpose of this paper is to study Conjecture

Let

be the potential function of the colored Jones polynomial for the twist knot

By Proposition
5.3

\displaystyle\zeta(p)

\displaystyle=V(p,t_{0},s_{0})

\displaystyle=\pi\sqrt{-1}\left((2p+1)s_{0}^{2}-(2p+3)s_{0}-2t_{0}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\left(\text{Li}_{2}(x_{0}y_{0})+\text{Li}_{2}(x_{0}/y_{0})-3\text{Li}_{2}(x_{0})+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right)

and

(1.8)

\displaystyle\omega(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(2\pi s_{0})e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}t_{0}}}{(1-e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}t_{0}})^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{\det Hess(V)(t_{0},s_{0})}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(y_{0}-y_{0}^{-1})x_{0}}{-4\pi(1-x_{0})^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{H(p,x_{0},y_{0})}}

(1.9)

\displaystyle H(p,x_{0},y_{0})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{-3(2p+1)}{\frac{1}{x_{0}}-1}+\frac{2p+1}{\frac{1}{x_{0}y_{0}}-1}+\frac{2p+1}{\frac{1}{x_{0}/y_{0}}-1}-\frac{3}{(\frac{1}{x_{0}}-1)(\frac{1}{x_{0}y_{0}}-1)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{3}{(\frac{1}{x_{0}}-1)(\frac{1}{x_{0}/y_{0}}-1)}+\frac{4}{(\frac{1}{x_{0}y_{0}}-1)(\frac{1}{x_{0}/y_{0}}-1)}\right).

Then, we have

.

For

, the asymptotic expansion of the colored Jones polynomial

\displaystyle J_{N}(\mathcal{K}_{p};e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{N+\frac{1}{2}}})

\displaystyle\cdot\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\kappa_{i}(p)\left(\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{N+\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{i}+O\left(\frac{1}{(N+\frac{1}{2})^{d+1}}\right)\right),

, where
\omega(p)
and

\displaystyle 2\pi\zeta(p)=vol(S^{3}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{p})+cs(S^{3}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{p})\mod\pi^{2}\sqrt{-1}\mathbb{Z}.

For

(1.12)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{2\pi}{N}\log J_{N}(\mathcal{K}_{p};e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{N+\frac{1}{2}}})=vol(S^{3}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{p})+cs(S^{3}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{p})\mod\pi^{2}\sqrt{-1}\mathbb{Z}.

Remark 1.5
.

We need the condition
p\geq 6
in Theorem
1.2
which makes sure the volume
vol(S^{3}\setminus\mathcal{K}_{p})
is not too small, so we can construct the required homotopy and verify the assumptions of the saddle point method successfully. We remark that our method can also work for the cases of
p\leq-1
with some exceptions.

We use the saddle point method developed by Ohtsuki in a series papers
[
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
to prove Theorem
1.2
. An outline of the proof is follows. First, we write the colored Jones polynomial of the twist knot
J_{N}(\mathcal{K}_{p};e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{N+\frac{1}{2}}})
as a summation of Fourier coefficients with the help of quantum dilogarithm function and the Poisson summation formula. Next, we will show that infinite terms of these Fourier coefficients can be neglected in the sense that they can be sufficiently small order at
N\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain formula (
5.69
). Then we estimate the remained finite terms of Fourier coefficients by using the saddle point method. Finally, we find that only two main Fourier coefficients will contribute to the asymptotic expansion formula. Hence we finish the proof Theorem
1.2
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we fix the notations and review the related materials that will be used in this paper. In Section
3
, we compute the potential function for the colored Jones polynomials of the twist knot
\mathcal{K}_{p}
and obtain Proposition
3.1
. In Section
4
, we prove Proposition
4.4
which expresses the colored Jones polynomial of the twist knot

J_{N}(\mathcal{K}_{p};e^{\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{N+\frac{1}{2}}})
as a summation of Fourier coefficients by Poisson summation formula.
In Section
5
,
we first show that infinite terms of the Fourier coefficients can be neglected. Then we estimate the remained finite terms of Fourier coefficients by using the saddle point method, we obtain that only two main Fourier coefficients will contribute to the final form of the asymptotic expansion. Hence we finish the proof Theorem
1.2
. Section
6
is devoted to the proof of several lemmas which will be used in previous sections.

\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm 1}]
-module generated by isotopy classes of framed links in

The Kauffman bracket

gives a map from
\mathcal{K}(S^{3})
to

The Kauffman bracket skein module of the solid torus

The twist map

is a map induced by a full right handed twist on the solid torus. There exists a basis

of

, which are eigenvectors of the twist map

(see e.g
[

).
\{e_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
can be defined recursively by

(2.3)

\displaystyle e_{0}=1,\ e_{1}=z,\ e_{i}=ze_{i-1}-e_{i-2}.

Moreover, the

(2.4)

\displaystyle\langle e_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=(-1)^{i}\frac{A^{2(i+1)}-A^{-2(i+1)}}{A^{2}-A^{-2}}

\displaystyle t(e_{i})

\displaystyle=\mu_{i}e_{i}

where

is also called the framing factor. Throughout this paper, we make the convention

(2.6)

\displaystyle q=A^{4},\ \{n\}=q^{\frac{n}{2}}-q^{-\frac{n}{2}}\ \text{for an integer }\ n.

-th colored Jones polynomial

\displaystyle J_{N}(\mathcal{K};q)=\frac{\langle\mathcal{K}(e_{N-1})\rangle}{\langle e_{N-1}\rangle}.

For a sufficiently small

\displaystyle J_{N}(\mathcal{K}_{p};\xi_{N})=\sum_{(k,l)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}h_{N}(k,l)+O\left(e^{(N+\frac{1}{2})\left(\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}(p)-\epsilon\right)}\right).

\displaystyle\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}h_{N}\left((N+\frac{1}{2})t-\frac{1}{2},(N+\frac{1}{2})s-\frac{1}{2}\right)e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\frac{(2N+1)mt}{2}-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\frac{(2N+1)ns}{2}}dtds

\displaystyle\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\psi(t,s)\sin(2\pi s)e^{(N+\frac{1}{2})\left(V_{N}\left(p,t,s\right)-2\pi\sqrt{-1}mt-2\pi\sqrt{-1}ns\right)}dtds,

\displaystyle=\pi\sqrt{-1}\left((2p+1)s^{2}-(2p+2(-n-2)+3)s)+\left(\frac{4}{2N+1}-2m-2\right)t-\frac{1}{12}\right)

Proposition 4.6

For any

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{N}(m,-n-2)=(-1)^{n}\hat{h}_{N}(m,n).

\displaystyle=\psi(t,s)\sin(2\pi s)\exp\left((N+\frac{1}{2})\left(V_{N}\left(p,t,s;m,-n-2\right)-2\pi\sqrt{-1}(n+1)\right)\right)

where in the third “=”, we have let

(4.26)

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{N}(m,-n-2)=(-1)^{n}\hat{h}_{N}(m,n).

∎

Corollary 4.7
.

We have

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{N}(m,-1)=0,

\displaystyle\hat{h}_{N}(m,-2)=\hat{h}_{N}(m,0).

Remark 4.8
.

The case (

) is the big cancellation. The first situation of such phenomenon of “Big cancellation” happened in quantum invariants is discovered in the Volume Conjecture of the Turaev-Viro invariants by Chen-Yang
[

The hidden reason behind that was found and described as a precise statement of symmetric property of asymptotics of quantum 6j-symbol which is on the Poisson Summation level by Chen-Murakami which is Conjecture 3 in
[
5
]

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first time that such a phenomenon of big cancellation on the Poisson Summation level on the case of colored Jones polynomial is proved.

We consider the following potential function for the twist knot

\displaystyle V(p,t,s;m,n)=\pi\sqrt{-1}\left((2p+1)s^{2}-(2p+3+2n)s-(2m+2)t\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\left(\text{Li}_{2}(e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}(t+s)})+\text{Li}_{2}(e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}(t-s)})-3\text{Li}_{2}(e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}t})+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\right).

We define the function

(5.2)

\displaystyle f(p,t,X,s,Y;m,n)=ReV(p,t+X\sqrt{-1},s+Y\sqrt{-1};m,n),

which will also be denoted by

for brevity in the following.

We have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial X}

\displaystyle=Re\left(\sqrt{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}V(p,t+X\sqrt{-1},s+Y\sqrt{-1};m,n)\right)

\displaystyle=-Im(-(2m+2)\pi\sqrt{-1}+3\log(1-x)-\log(1-xy)-\log(1-x/y))

\displaystyle=-3arg(1-x)+arg(1-xy)+arg(1-x/y)+(2m+2)\pi,

and

(5.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial Y}

\displaystyle=Re\left(\sqrt{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}V(p,t+X\sqrt{-1},s+Y\sqrt{-1};m,n)\right)

\displaystyle=-Im(-(2p+3+2n)\pi\sqrt{-1}+(4p+2)\pi\sqrt{-1}s

\displaystyle-\log(1-xy)+\log(1-x/y))

\displaystyle=arg(1-xy)-arg(1-x/y)+(2p+3+2n)\pi-(4p+2)\pi s,

where we put
x=e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}(t+X\sqrt{-1})}
and

Since
\frac{dx}{dX}=-2\pi x
, we compute

(5.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial X^{2}}

\displaystyle=-Im\frac{\partial}{\partial X}\left(3\log(1-x)-\log(1-xy)-\log(1-x/y)\right)

\displaystyle=-Im\left(\left(-\frac{3}{1-x}+\frac{y}{1-xy}+\frac{1/y}{1-x/y}\right)\frac{\partial x}{\partial X}\right)

\displaystyle=2\pi Im\left(-\frac{3x}{1-x}+\frac{xy}{1-xy}+\frac{x/y}{1-x/y}\right)

\displaystyle=2\pi Im\left(-\frac{3}{1-x}+\frac{1}{1-xy}+\frac{1}{1-x/y}\right).

Furthermore, we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial X\partial Y}

\displaystyle=2\pi Im\left(\frac{1}{1-xy}-\frac{1}{1-x/y}\right)

and

(5.7)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial Y^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\pi Im\left(\frac{1}{1-xy}+\frac{1}{1-x/y}\right).

Therefore, the Hessian matrix of
f
is presented by

(5.8)

\displaystyle 2\pi\begin{pmatrix}3a+b+c&b-c\\
b-c&b+c\end{pmatrix}

where we put

(5.9)

\displaystyle a=-Im\frac{1}{1-x},\ b=Im\frac{1}{1-xy},\ c=Im\frac{1}{1-x/y}.

(5.10)

\displaystyle 3a

\displaystyle=-\frac{3\sin 2\pi t}{e^{2\pi X}+e^{-2\pi X}-2\cos(2\pi t)},

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(2\pi(t+s))}{e^{2\pi(X+Y)}+e^{-2\pi(X+Y)}-2\cos(2\pi(t+s))},

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(2\pi(t-s))}{e^{2\pi(X-Y)}+e^{-2\pi(X-Y)}-2\cos(2\pi(t-s))}.

\displaystyle ReV(p,T_{1}(c),c;0,n)-ReV\left(p,0,n,T_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right),\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{c}\log\left(\cot\left(\frac{\pi\tau}{2}\right)\right)d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{c}\log\left(2\cos\left(\frac{\pi\tau}{2}\right)\right)d\tau-\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{c}\log\left(2\sin\left(\frac{\pi\tau}{2}\right)\right)d\tau.

Let
x=\frac{1}{2}(1-\tau)
, we obtain

(6.125)

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{c}\log\left(2\cos\left(\frac{\pi\tau}{2}\right)\right)d\tau=-2\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{1}{2}(1-c)}\log(2\sin\pi x)dx=2\left(\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{c}{2}\right)-\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)\right).

Let
y=\frac{\tau}{2}
, we obtain

(6.126)

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{c}\log\left(2\sin\left(\frac{\pi\tau}{2}\right)\right)d\tau=2\int_{\frac{1}{4}}^{\frac{c}{2}}\log(2\sin(\pi y))dy=-2\left(\Lambda\left(\frac{c}{2}\right)-\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)\right).

Hence, for
c>\frac{1}{2}
, we have

(6.127)

\displaystyle ReV(p,T_{1}(c),c;0,n)=2\left(\Lambda\left(\frac{c}{2}\right)-\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{c}{2}\right)\right).

∎

Let
(t_{0},s_{0})=(t_{0R}+X_{0}\sqrt{-1},s_{0R}+Y_{0}\sqrt{-1})
be a critical point of the potential function
V(p,t,s)
as given in Proposition
5.3
.

By the proof of Lemma
5.5
in Appendix
6.5
, we have
0<\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}(p)<\frac{v_{8}}{2\pi}
. We assume
c(p)
is a solution to the following equation

(6.128)

\displaystyle ReV(p,T_{1}(c),c)=2\left(\Lambda\left(\frac{c}{2}\right)+\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{c}{2}\right)\right)=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}(p).

Lemma 6.16
.

We have the following inequality:

(6.129)

\displaystyle c(p)<c_{upper}(p).

Proof.

Let
h(c)=\Lambda(\frac{c}{2})+\Lambda(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{c}{2})
, then

2h(c(p))=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}(p)
.
By Lemma
6.18
, we have the following convergent power series

(6.130)

\displaystyle h(c)=2\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)-\frac{\pi}{4}\left(c-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{\pi^{3}}{48}\left(c-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{4}-\frac{\pi^{5}}{144}\left(c-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{6}-\cdots.

Then we obtain

(6.131)

\displaystyle 2h(c_{upper}(p))

\displaystyle=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}(p)-2\left(\frac{\pi^{3}}{48}\left(c_{upper}(p)-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{4}+\frac{\pi^{5}}{144}\left(c_{upper}(p)-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{6}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle<\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}(p)=2h(c(p)).

Hence, by Lemma
6.15
, we obtain
c(p)<c_{upper}(p)
.
∎

As a consequence of formula (
6.129
), we have

Corollary 6.17
.

For
c>c_{upper}(p)
, we have

\displaystyle ReV(p,T_{1}(c),c)<ReV(p,T_{1}(c_{upper}(p)),c_{upper}(p))<ReV(p,T_{1}(c(p)),c(p))=\zeta_{\mathbb{R}}(p).

Lemma 6.18
.

For
\frac{1}{2}<c<1
, we have the following convergent power series

(6.132)

\displaystyle h(c)=2\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)-\frac{\pi}{4}\left(c-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{\pi^{3}}{48}\left(c-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{4}-\frac{\pi^{5}}{144}\left(c-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{6}-\cdots.

Proof.

We use the following power series expansion for
\sec(x)
,

(6.133)

\displaystyle\sec(x)=1+\frac{1}{2}x+\frac{5}{24}x^{4}+\cdots,\ \text{for}\ |x|<\frac{\pi}{2}

From
h(c)=\Lambda(\frac{c}{2})+\Lambda(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{c}{2})
, we obtain

(6.134)

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(c)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log\cot\left(\frac{\pi c}{2}\right),

\displaystyle h^{\prime\prime}(c)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi}{2\sin(\pi c)}=-\frac{\pi}{2}\sec\left(\pi\left(c-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right).

Since
0<\left(c-\frac{1}{2}\right)\pi<\frac{\pi}{2}
, by using formula (
6.133
), we obtain

(6.135)

\displaystyle h^{\prime}(c)

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{c}h^{\prime\prime}(t)dt-h^{\prime}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi}{2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{c}\sec\left(\pi\left(t-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right)dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\pi\left(c-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\sec(x)dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\pi\left(c-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\left(1+\frac{1}{2}x^{2}+\frac{5}{24}x^{4}+\cdots\right)dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\pi\left(c-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{1}{6}\left(\pi(c-\frac{1}{2})\right)^{3}+\frac{1}{24}\left(\pi(c-\frac{1}{2})\right)^{5}+\cdots\right)

Hence

(6.136)

\displaystyle h(c)

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{c}h^{\prime}(t)dt+h\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{c}\left(\pi\left(t-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{\pi^{3}}{6}\left(t-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{3}+\frac{\pi^{5}}{24}\left(t-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{5}\right)dt+2\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)

\displaystyle=2\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi\left(c-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\left(x+\frac{1}{6}x^{3}+\frac{1}{24}x^{5}+\cdots\right)dx

\displaystyle=2\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)-\frac{\pi}{4}\left(c-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{\pi^{3}}{48}\left(c-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{4}-\frac{\pi^{5}}{144}\left(c-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{6}-\cdots.

∎

Lemma 6.19
.

For
t\in D^{\prime}_{0}(c)
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(6.137)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}ReV(p,t+X\sqrt{-1},c;0,n)}{\partial X^{2}}>0.

Proof.

By straightforward computations, we have

(6.138)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\partial^{2}ReV(p,t+X\sqrt{-1},c;0,n)}{\partial X^{2}}

\displaystyle=-3\frac{\sin(2\pi t)}{e^{2\pi X}+e^{-2\pi X}-2\cos(2\pi t)}

\displaystyle+\frac{\sin(2\pi(t+c))}{e^{2\pi X}+e^{-2\pi X}-2\cos(2\pi(t+c))}+\frac{\sin(2\pi(t-c))}{e^{2\pi X}+e^{-2\pi X}-2\cos(2\pi(t-c))}.

Clearly, for
t\in D^{\prime}_{0}(c)
, we have
\sin(2\pi t)<0
and
\cos(2\pi c)\leq 0
which imply that

(6.139)

\displaystyle-3\frac{\sin(2\pi t)}{e^{2\pi X}+e^{-2\pi X}-2\cos(2\pi t)}>0,

and

(6.140)

\displaystyle\cos(2\pi c)(e^{2\pi X}+e^{-2\pi X})-2\cos(2\pi t)<2\cos(2\pi c)-2\cos(2\pi t)<0.

Hence

(6.141)

\displaystyle\frac{\sin(2\pi(t+c))}{e^{2\pi X}+e^{-2\pi X}-2\cos(2\pi(t+c))}+\frac{\sin(2\pi(t-c))}{e^{2\pi X}+e^{-2\pi X}-2\cos(2\pi(t-c))}

\displaystyle=2\sin(2\pi t)\frac{\cos(2\pi c)(e^{2\pi X}+e^{-2\pi X})-2\cos(2\pi t)}{(e^{2\pi X}+e^{-2\pi X}-2\cos(2\pi(t+c)))(e^{2\pi X}+e^{-2\pi X}-2\cos(2\pi(t-c)))}>0.

∎

Lemma 6.20
.

For
t\in D^{\prime}_{0}(c)
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(6.142)

\displaystyle ReV(p,t+X\sqrt{-1},c;0,n)\ \text{goes to $\infty$ uniformly},\ \text{as}\ X^{2}\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

Based on Lemma
2.3
, we introduce the following function for
t\in D^{\prime}_{0}(c)

(6.143)

\displaystyle F(X;n)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &X&\ (\text{if}\ X\geq 0),\\
&\left(\frac{1}{2}-t\right)X&\ (\text{if}\ X<0).\end{aligned}\right.

since
t>\frac{1}{2}
, we have

(6.144)

\displaystyle F(X;n)\rightarrow\infty\ \text{as}\ X^{2}\rightarrow\infty,

and by Lemma
2.3
, we obtain

(6.145)

\displaystyle 2\pi F(X;n)-C<ReV(p,t+X\sqrt{-1},c;0,n)<2\pi F(X;n)+C,

which implies formula (
6.142
).
∎

Now, we can finish the proof of Proposition
6.11
.

Proof.

We show that there exists a homotopy
D^{\prime}_{\delta}(c)
(

D^{\prime}_{0}(c)
and
D^{\prime}_{1}(c)

(6.146)

\displaystyle(T_{1}(c),c)\in D^{\prime}_{1}(c),

\displaystyle D^{\prime}_{1}(c)-\{(T_{1}(c),c)\}\subset\{t\in\mathbb{C}|ReV(p,t,c;0,n)<ReV(p,T_{1}(c),c)\},

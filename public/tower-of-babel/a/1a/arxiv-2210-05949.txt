Defect in Gauge Theory and Quantum Hall States
Taro Kimura ♠ and Norton Lee ♢

Abstract
We study the surface defect in 𝒩 = 2 ∗ \mathcal{N}=2^{*} U ⁡ ( N ) U(N) gauge theory in four dimensions and its relation to quantum Hall states in two dimensions. We first prove that the defect partition function becomes the Jack polynomial of the variables describing the brane positions by imposing the Higgsing condition and taking the bulk decoupling limit. Further tuning the adjoint mass parameter, we may obtain various fractional quantum Hall states, including Laughlin, Moore-Read, and Read-Rezayi states, due to the admissible condition of the Jack polynomial.

Introduction

The relation of low-energy physics of supersymmetric gauge theory and integrable system has been an active research for decades
Gorsky:1993pe
;
Gorsky:1995zq
;
Nekrasov:1995nq
. One of the best-known story is the Seiberg-Witten curve of the
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric gauge theories can be identified as the spectral curve of the integrable systems.
This correspondence was later extended to the quantum level by Nekrasov and Shatashivilli in
Nekrasov:2009uh
;
Nekrasov:2009ui
, with the gauge theories subjected to the
\Omega
-deformation. This deformation introduces two parameters
({\varepsilon}_{1},{\varepsilon}_{2})
associated to the rotation on the two orthogonal plane in
\mathbb{R}^{4}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
. The partition function
\mathcal{Z}
and BPS observables can be computed exactly by localization technique for a variety of gauge theories
Nikita:I
. In the limit
({\varepsilon}_{1},{\varepsilon}_{2})\to(0,0)
, the classical integrable system is recovered. The Nekrasov-Shatashivilli limit (NS-limit for short)
{\varepsilon}_{1}\to\hbar
and
{\varepsilon}_{2}\to 0
results in an
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
supersymmetry being preserved in the fixed plane. One expects to get the quantum integrable system.

From gauge theory to integrable model

One is naturally to ask the question of computing the wavefunction of the integrable system. The stationary state wave function, in the context of Bethe/gauge correspondence, are the vacua of the two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
theory. In order to get the stationary wavefunction, we compute the expectation value of a special observable in the two dimensional theory - a surface defect in the four dimensional theory
Nikita:V
;
Chen:2019vvt
;
Chen:2020rxu
;
Lee:2020hfu
;
Jeong:2018qpc
;
Jeong:2017mfh
. It turns out that induction of co-dimensional two surface defect provides a powerful tool in the study of Bethe/gauge correspondence. The parameter of the defect becomes the coordinates that the wavefunction depends on. The four dimensional theory with a co-dimensional two surface defect can be realized as a theory on an orbifold. The localization computations extend so as to compute the defect partition function and expectation value of BPS observables.

Our scope is on the class of
qq
-characters observable in the gauge theory
Nikita:I
. The main statement in
Nikita:II
proves certain vanishing conditions for the expectation values of the
qq
-observables, both with or without defects. These vanishing conditions, called
non-perturbative Dyson-Schwinger equations
, can be used to construct KZ-type equations
Knizhnik:1984nr
satisfied by the partition function
Nekrasov:2021tik
;
jeong2021intersecting
. In the NS-limit, the KZ-equations becomes a Schrödinger-type equation satisfied by the partition function.

Jack polynomial and quantum Hall state

The Laughlin wavefunction has provied a key to understand the quantum Hall effect (QHE). It models the simplest abelian FQH and is the building blocks of model wavefuntion of more general states, both abelian and non-abelian such as Moore-Read and Read-Rezayi state. The wavefunctions of such models, aside from the Gaussian factor which we will drop, are conformally-invariant multivariable polynomials.
All three of Laughlin, Moore-Read, and Read-Rezayi state wavefunctions are proven to be special cases of the Jack polynomial
J^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}_{\mathbf{n}}
with the Jack parameter
\kappa
taking negative rational value
Bernevig:2007nek
;
Bernevig:2008rda
;
Bernevig:2009zz
.

\displaystyle\mathfrak{q}=e^{2\pi i\tau},\ \tau=\frac{4\pi i}{g^{2}}+\frac{\vartheta}{2\pi}

(1)

The instanton configuration is labeled by a set of Young diagrams

\displaystyle\lambda^{(\alpha)}_{i}\geq\lambda^{(\alpha)}_{i+1}\geq 0

(2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{a}\mathsf{n}_{a}e^{\mathsf{x}_{a}}\right]=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod_{a}\mathsf{x}_{a}^{-\mathsf{n}_{a}}&(\text{rational})\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\prod_{a}(1-e^{-\mathsf{x}_{a}})^{-\mathsf{n}_{a}}&(\text{trigonometric})\\[10.00002pt]

In this paper, we mostly apply the rational convention, which corresponds to four dimensional gauge theory.
The pseudo measure associated to the instanton configuration
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is computed by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[\boldsymbol{\lambda}]=\mathbb{E}\left[(1-e^{m})(\mathbf{N}\mathbf{K}^{*}+q_{1}q_{2}\mathbf{N}^{*}\mathbf{K}-P_{1}P_{2}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{K}^{*})\right].

(5)

q_{i}=e^{{\varepsilon}_{i}}

\Omega

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{\rm inst}(\mathbf{a},m,\vec{\varepsilon};\mathfrak{q})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\mathfrak{q}^{|\boldsymbol{\lambda}|}\mathcal{Z}(\mathbf{a},m,\vec{\varepsilon})[\boldsymbol{\lambda}]

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}[\boldsymbol{\lambda}]=\prod_{(\alpha i)\neq(\beta j)}

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma({\varepsilon}_{2}^{-1}(x_{\alpha i}-x_{\beta j}-{\varepsilon}_{1}))}{\Gamma({\varepsilon}_{2}^{-1}(x_{\alpha i}-x_{\beta j}))}\times\frac{\Gamma({\varepsilon}_{2}^{-1}(\mathring{x}_{\alpha i}-\mathring{x}_{\beta j}))}{\Gamma({\varepsilon}_{2}^{-1}(\mathring{x}_{\alpha i}-\mathring{x}_{\beta j}-{\varepsilon}_{1}))}

\displaystyle x_{\alpha i}=a_{\alpha}+(i-1){\varepsilon}_{1}+\lambda^{(\alpha)}_{i}{\varepsilon}_{2},\quad\mathring{x}_{\alpha i}=a_{\alpha}+(i-1){\varepsilon}_{1}.

(8)

The 1-loop contribution to the partition function can be expressed in terms of

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma({\varepsilon}_{2}^{-1}(x_{\alpha i}-x_{\beta j}-{\varepsilon}_{1}))}{\Gamma({\varepsilon}_{2}^{-1}(x_{\alpha i}-x_{\beta j}))}\times\frac{\Gamma({\varepsilon}_{2}^{-1}(x_{\alpha i}-x_{\beta j}-m))}{\Gamma({\varepsilon}_{2}^{-1}(x_{\alpha i}-x_{\beta j}-m-{\varepsilon}_{1}))}.

3
Jack Polynomial

In this section we collect some facts about the Jack polynomial.
See, e.g.,
macdonald1998symmetric
;
stanley1989some
for more details.
A Jack polynomial
J_{\mathbf{n}}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})
is a symmetric polynomial in variables
\{z_{1},\dots,z_{N}\}
labeled by the partition
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{N})
:

\displaystyle n_{i}\geq n_{i+1}\geq 0,\qquad i=1,\dots,N-1.

(78)

and a parameter
\kappa
.
In the context of QHE, the partition
\mathbf{n}
can be represented as a (bosonic) occupation number configuration
\mathbf{l}(\mathbf{n})=\{l_{\mathtt{m}}(\mathbf{n}),\ \mathtt{m}=0,1,2,\dots\}
of each of the lowest Landau level (LLL) orbits of angular momentum
L=\mathtt{m}\hbar
, where for
\mathtt{m}>0
the number
l_{\mathtt{m}}(\mathbf{n})
is the multiplicity of
\mathtt{m}
in
\mathbf{n}
.
Given a partition
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{N})
, let

\displaystyle M_{\mathbf{n}}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}}z_{1}^{\mathbf{n}_{\sigma_{1}}}\cdots z_{N}^{\mathbf{n}_{\sigma_{N}}}

(79)

be the orbit sum. The
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{N}
is a permutation of the set
\{1,\dots,N\}
.
When
\kappa\to 0
,
J^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}_{\mathbf{n}}\to M_{\mathbf{n}}
is the monomial wavefunction of the free boson state with occupancy number
\mathbf{l}(\mathbf{n})
.

The Dunkl operator
D_{i}
is defined by

\displaystyle D_{i}=z_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{z_{i}+z_{j}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}(1-\sigma_{ij}).

(80)

Here

\displaystyle(\sigma_{ij}f)(\cdots,x_{i},\cdots,x_{j},\cdots)=f(\cdots,x_{j},\cdots,x_{i},\cdots)

(81)

are the operators that exchange of the
i
-th variable and the
j
-the variable and the differentiation operations with respect to those variables.
Two Dunkl operators commute with each other

\displaystyle\left[D_{i},D_{j}\right]=0,

(82)

such that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}D^{2}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(z_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}\right)^{2}-\kappa\sum_{i<j}\frac{z_{i}+z_{j}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}\left(z_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}-z_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\right).

(83)

It has been known that the eigenfunction of Laplace-Beltrami operator are the Jack polynomials

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm LB}(\kappa)J_{\mathbf{n}}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})=E(\mathbf{n})J_{\mathbf{n}}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N}).

(84)

The energy spectrum is given by

\displaystyle E(\mathbf{n})=e_{\mathbf{n}}-e_{\varnothing}

(85)

where

\displaystyle e_{\mathbf{n}}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\left(n_{\alpha}+\kappa\left(\frac{N+1}{2}-\alpha\right)\right)^{2}.

(86)

Jack polynomial with negative rational value of
\kappa
is used to construct wavefunction of fractional quantum Hall effect. In particular Laughlin, Moore-Read, and Read-Razayi fractional quantum Hall effect wave function can be explicitly written as single Jack symmetric polynomials, whose partitions
\mathbf{n}
obey the
(k,r)
-admissible condition
Bernevig:2007nek
;
Bernevig:2008rda
;
Bernevig:2009zz

\displaystyle n_{i}-n_{i+k}\geq r,\ i=1,\dots,N-k.

(87)

and the coupling
\kappa=-\frac{r-1}{k+1}
is set to negative rational number where
r-1
and
k+1
are co-prime. See
Feigin:2002IMRN
for properties of Jack polynomial at negative rational coupling.

Concrete expressions

We define the power sum polynomial,

\displaystyle p_{n}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}^{n}\,.

(88)

here we list a few the Jack polynomials
J_{\mathbf{n}}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(z)
given in terms of the power sum polynomial:

\displaystyle J_{(1)}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(z)

\displaystyle=p_{1}

(89a)

\displaystyle J_{(2)}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+\kappa}p_{2}+\frac{\kappa}{1+\kappa}p_{1}^{2}

(89b)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}p_{2}+\frac{1}{2}p_{1}^{2}

(89c)

\displaystyle J_{(3)}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{(1+\kappa)(2+\kappa)}p_{3}+\frac{3\kappa}{(1+\kappa)(2+\kappa)}p_{2}p_{1}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{(1+\kappa)(2+\kappa)}p_{1}^{3}

(89d)

\displaystyle J_{(2,1)}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{1+2\kappa}p_{3}+\frac{1-\kappa}{1+2\kappa}p_{2}J_{1}+\frac{\kappa}{1+2\kappa}p_{1}^{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}p_{3}-\frac{1}{2}p_{2}+\frac{1}{6}p_{1}^{3}

(89f)

The Jack polynomial

can have divergent coefficients when

needs to be increased
\alpha-\beta
times but not any further. Since each
\lambda^{(\alpha)}_{i}
only increase the reading by 1, The
\beta
-th column will have exactly
\lambda^{(\beta)}_{\alpha-\beta}-\lambda^{(\alpha)}_{\alpha+1-\beta}
readings of the number
\alpha
.

The wave function (
99
) can be rewritten as an ensemble over the semi-standard Young tableaux
\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{n}}
whose reading at the
(\alpha,j)
square satisfy

\displaystyle\alpha\leq T_{\alpha,j}\leq T_{\alpha,j+1}\leq N,\ T_{\alpha,j}<T_{\alpha+1,j}.

(122)

Given a Young Tableaux
\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{n}}[\boldsymbol{\lambda}]
whose largest reading is less or equal to
N
(not necessary equal to
N
). We can define a series of sub Young Tableaux

\displaystyle\varnothing=\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{n}}^{(0)}[\boldsymbol{\lambda}]\subset\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{n}}^{(1)}[\boldsymbol{\lambda}]\subset\cdots\subset\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{n}}^{(N)}[\boldsymbol{\lambda}]=\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{n}}[\boldsymbol{\lambda}]

(123)

The sub Young Tableaux
\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{n}}^{(i)}[\boldsymbol{\lambda}]=\mathbf{n}^{(i)}=(n_{1}^{(i)},n_{2}^{(i)},\dots,n_{N}^{(i)})
has its reading less or equal to
i
. By its construction

\displaystyle n^{(i)}_{j}=0

(124)

if
j>i
.
The instanton configuration
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
can be obtained by

\displaystyle\lambda^{(\alpha)}_{k}=n_{\alpha}-n^{(\alpha+k-1)}_{\alpha}.

(125)

The weight
t_{\alpha}
of the Young Tableaux
\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{n}}[\boldsymbol{\lambda}]
equals

\displaystyle t_{\alpha}=|\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha)}|-|\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha-1)}|.

(126)

where

\displaystyle|\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha)}|=\sum_{j=1}^{\alpha}n_{j}^{(\alpha)}.

(127)

The wave function (
2.3
) from the defect instanton partition function can be now written in terms of ensemble over the Young Tableaux

\displaystyle\Psi=\sum_{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{n}}}z^{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{n}}}\psi_{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{n}}}

(128)

where
z^{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{n}}}=\prod_{j=1}^{N}z_{i}^{t_{i}}
and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi_{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{n}}}&=\prod_{\omega=2}^{N}\prod_{1\leq\beta<\alpha<\omega}\prod_{j=1}^{n^{(\omega)}_{\beta}-n_{\beta}^{(\omega-1)}}\frac{\kappa(\beta-\alpha-1)+n_{\alpha}^{(\omega-1)}-n_{\beta}^{(\omega)}+j}{\kappa(\beta-\alpha)+n_{\alpha}^{(\omega-1)}-n_{\beta}^{(\omega)}+j-1}\times\frac{\kappa(\beta-\alpha)+n_{\alpha+1}^{(\omega)}-n_{\beta}^{(\omega)}+j-1}{\kappa(\beta-\alpha-1)+n_{\alpha+1}^{(\omega)}-n_{\beta}^{(\omega)}+j}\\
&=\prod_{\omega=2}^{N}\prod_{1\leq\beta<\alpha<\omega}\frac{\Gamma(\kappa(\beta-\alpha-1)+n_{\alpha}^{(\omega-1)}-n_{\beta}^{(\omega-1)}+1)}{\Gamma(\kappa(\beta-\alpha-1)+n^{(\omega-1)}_{\alpha}-n_{\beta}^{(\omega)})}\frac{\Gamma(\kappa(\beta-\alpha)+n_{\alpha}^{(\omega-1)}-n_{\beta}^{(\omega)}-1)}{\Gamma(\kappa(\beta-\alpha)+n_{\alpha}^{(\omega-1)}-n_{\beta}^{(\omega-1)})}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\frac{\Gamma(\kappa(\beta-\alpha)+n_{\alpha+1}^{(\omega)}-n_{\beta}^{(\omega-1)})}{\Gamma(\kappa(\beta-\alpha)+n_{\alpha+1}^{(\omega)}-n_{\beta}^{(\omega)}-1)}\frac{\Gamma(\kappa(\beta-\alpha-1)+n_{\alpha+1}^{(\omega)}-n_{\beta}^{(\omega)})}{\Gamma(\kappa(\beta-\alpha-1)+n_{\alpha+1}^{(\omega)}-n_{\beta}^{(\omega-1)}+1)}\,.\end{split}

Eq. (
128
) is the combinatorial formula for Jack polynomial
(
macdonald1998symmetric
, Chapter VI, §10)
.

In the massless limit
m\to 0
, which translates to
\kappa\to 1
limit (Schur limit) of the Jack parameter under the Bethe/gauge correspondence, all instanton configuration
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
satisfying (
100
) shares a common pseudo-measure in the ensemble

\displaystyle\lim_{\nu\to 0}\mathcal{Z}_{\rm defect}[\boldsymbol{\lambda}]=1.

(129)

The wave function
\Psi
is an ensemble over the instanton configuration that satisfies (

\displaystyle\lim_{\kappa\to 0}\Psi=\sum_{\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{n}}}z_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots z_{N}^{t_{N}}=s_{\mathbf{n}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N}).

(130)

4.2

5
Quantum Hall States

In this section, we discuss a possible connection between four dimensional gauge theory and two dimensional fractional quantum Hall (FQH) effect.
The idea is as follows.
On the four dimensional gauge theory side, we apply the
\Omega
-background for each complex plane, which plays a role of the background
U(1)
magnetic field.
See, e.g.,
Nekrasov:2002qd
;
Hama:2012bg
.
Then, imposing the surface defect and taking the bulk decoupling limit, we may focus on the two dimensional system with the background field, which realizes the QH effect.
In fact, it has been shown by Bernevig–Haldane
Bernevig:2007nek
;
Bernevig:2008rda
;
Bernevig:2009zz
that the wide class of FQH wave functions for the ground state and with the quasi-hole excitations are realized using the Jack polynomial (multiplying a trivial Gaussian factor which we will drop here) that we have already obtained from the gauge theory analysis.
We pursue the direction suggested by Nekrasov
Nekrasov:2019AMJ
for more details, and construct more generic FQH wave functions.

5.1
Laughlin State

The lowest Landau level (LLL) wave function is in general given by a product of conformally-invariant holomorhic multi-variable polynomial
\psi_{\text{LLL}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{N})
and a trivial Gaussian factor that we will drop here.
The Laughlin wave function is a key for understanding the physics of the FQH effect. It models the simplest abelian FQH state and is the building block to more general cases, both abelian and non-abelian ones.
The Laughlin wave function of filling fraction
\frac{1}{r}

3

3

Here we consider the bosonic FQH states, which are directly related to the Jack polynomial.
Hence the parameter

One can obtain the fermionic FQH states by multiplying the free fermion factor,

5.3
Admissible Condition

Jack polynomials
J^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}_{\mathbf{n}}
are defined on an integer partition
\mathbf{n}
and a parameter
\kappa
.
Let
k
,
r
be positive integers such that
r\geq 2
and
k+1
,
r-1
are coprime.
The partition
\mathbf{n}
is called
(k,r)
-admissible
if it satisfies

\displaystyle n_{i}-n_{i+k}\geq r,\ 1\leq i\leq N-k.

(163)

In general, Jack polynomial can have poles at a negative rational value of
\kappa
. However if the partition
\mathbf{n}
is
(k,r)
-admissible then the Jack polynomial will not have a pole at
Feigin:2002IMRN
,
4
4

4

It has been discussed that a different parameter specialization provides a simplification of the wave function
Sokolov:2014kma
.

\displaystyle\kappa=-\frac{r-1}{k+1}.

(164)

This is known as the
admissible condition
. The admissible condition considers the pole structure at a handful of particular values of Jack parameter
\kappa
. It does not mean the Jack polynomial is entire function for
\kappa
.

It has been pointed out
Bernevig:2007nek
;
Bernevig:2008rda
;
Bernevig:2009zz
that this admissible condition properly captures the clustering property of the FQH state, so that the corresponding wave function is generally obtained as the Jack polynomial with the negative couplings.
For example, the Laughlin state and the MR state correspond to
k=1
and
2
, respectively.
The higher
k
cases are the Read-Rezayi states, which are associated with
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-parafermion CFT, while the
k=2
case corresponds to the Ising CFT.
One can also apply this formalism to the FQH states with spin degrees of freedom
Estienne:2011
;
Kimura:2012sc
.

We have seen in the example in the
N=2
case with partition
\mathbf{n}={\tiny\hbox{}\hskip 0.0pt{\vbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.3pt\hbox{\vrule height=4.35048pt,width=0.3pt,depth=1.0876pt\hbox to5.4381pt{\hfil}\vrule height=4.35048pt,width=0.3pt,depth=1.0876pt\hbox to5.4381pt{\hfil}\vrule height=4.35048pt,width=0.3pt,depth=1.0876pt}\hrule height=0.3pt}\vskip-0.3pt}}\hskip 0.0pt}=(2,0)
. This partition
\mathbf{n}
is
(1,2)
admissible

\displaystyle n_{1}-n_{2}\geq 2.

(165)

The Jack polynomial defined based on this partition

\displaystyle J_{(2)}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{1}{1+\kappa}p_{2}+\frac{\kappa}{1+\kappa}p_{1}^{2}

(166)

does not have pole at

\displaystyle\kappa=-\frac{2-1}{1+1}=-\frac{1}{2}.

(167)

Instead it has pole at
\kappa=-1
. This corresponds to the
(1,2)
-admissible condition, which
\mathbf{n}
does not obey.

In this paper we have established relations between three objects: the surface operator of 4d gauge theory, the Jack polynomials, and fractional quantum Hall states. In particular, the main result is to realize the fractional quantum Hall states as the instanton partition function of four dimensional

With proper Higgsing condition (

A.1
Random Partition

A partition is defined as a way of expressing a non-negative integer
n
as summation over other non-negative integers. Each partition can be labeled by a Young diagram
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{\ell(\lambda)})
with
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

n=|\lambda|=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}\lambda_{i}.

(195)

We define the generating function of such a partition as

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}\mathfrak{q}^{|\lambda|}=\frac{1}{(\mathfrak{q};\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}},\quad(\mathfrak{q};\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\mathfrak{q}^{n}\right);

(196a)

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}t^{\ell(\lambda)}\mathfrak{q}^{|\lambda|}=\frac{1}{(\mathfrak{q}t;\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}};\quad(\mathfrak{q}t;\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-t\mathfrak{q}^{n}).

(196b)

The
\mathfrak{q}
-shifted factorial (the
\mathfrak{q}
-Pochhammer symbol) is defined as

(z;q)_{n}=\prod_{m=0}^{n-1}(1-zq^{m}).

(197)

A.2
Elliptic Functions

Here we fix our notation for the elliptic functions. The so-called Dedekind eta function is denoted as

\eta(\tau)=e^{\frac{\pi i\tau}{12}}(\mathfrak{q};\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}.

(198)

The first Jacobi
\theta

\theta_{11}(z;\tau)=ie^{\frac{\pi i\tau}{4}}z^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathfrak{q};\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}(\mathfrak{q}z;\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}(z^{-1};\mathfrak{q})_{\infty},

(199)

whose series expansion

\theta_{11}(z;\tau)=i\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}(-1)^{r-\frac{1}{2}}z^{r}e^{\pi i\tau r^{2}}=i\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}(-1)^{r-\frac{1}{2}}e^{rx}e^{\pi i\tau r^{2}},

(200)

implies that it obeys the heat equation

\frac{1}{\pi i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\theta_{11}(z;\tau)=(z\partial_{z})^{2}\theta_{11}(z;\tau).

(201)

We also use another convention for the theta function,

\displaystyle\theta(z;\mathfrak{q})=(z;\mathfrak{q})_{\infty}(\mathfrak{q}/z;\mathfrak{q})\,,

(202)

199

The Weierstrass

\displaystyle\wp(z)=\frac{1}{z^{2}}+\sum_{p,q\geq 0}\left\{\frac{1}{(z+p+q\tau)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(p+q\tau)^{2}}\right\},

(203)

is related to theta and eta functions by

\wp(z;\tau)=-(z\partial_{z})^{2}\log\theta_{11}(z;\tau)+\frac{1}{\pi i}\partial_{\tau}\log\eta(\tau).

(204)

We define the elliptic
\Gamma

\displaystyle\Gamma(z;p,q)=\prod_{0\leq n,m\leq\infty}\frac{1-z^{-1}p^{n+1}q^{m+1}}{1-zp^{n}q^{m}}

obeying the functional relation,

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(qz;p,q)}{\Gamma(z;p,q)}=\theta(z;p)\,,\qquad\frac{\Gamma(pz;p,q)}{\Gamma(z;p,q)}=\theta(z;q)\,.

(206)

How to Partition a Quantum Observable
Caleb M. Webb, Charles A. Stafford

Abstract
We present a partition of quantum observables in an open quantum system which is inherited from the division of the underlying Hilbert space or configuration space. It is shown that this partition leads to the definition of an inhomogeneous continuity equation for generic, non-local observables. This formalism is employed to describe the local evolution of the von Neumann entropy of a system of independent quantum particles out of equilibrium. Crucially, we find that all local fluctuations in the entropy are governed by an entropy current operator, implying that the production of entanglement entropy is not measured by this partitioned entropy. For systems linearly perturbed from equilibrium, it is shown that this entropy current is equivalent to a heat current, provided that the system-reservoir coupling is partitioned symmetrically. Finally, we show that any other partition of the coupling leads directly to a divergence of the von Neumann entropy. Thus, we conclude that Hilbert-space partitioning is the only partition of the von Neumann entropy which is consistent with the Laws of Thermodynamics.



I
Introduction

Consider a global Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
, which contains all single-particle degrees of freedom available to the universe. From this we construct the Fock space
\mathcal{F}\left({\mathscr{H}}\right)
, within which all many-body theories are described. In the study of open quantum systems, one typically divides the universe into complementary sets of orthogonal, single-particle states
\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}_{S}\oplus\mathscr{H}_{R}
describing the subsystem of interest
\mathscr{H}_{S}
and a thermal reservoir
\mathscr{H}_{R}
. The Fock space then decomposes as the familiar product
\mathcal{F}\left({\mathscr{H}}\right)\cong\mathcal{F}\left({\mathscr{H}_{S}}\right)\otimes\mathcal{F}\left({\mathscr{H}_{R}}\right)
.

Denote by
\mathcal{L}\left({\mathcal{F}\left({\mathscr{H}}\right)}\right)
the set of linear operators acting on
\mathcal{\mathcal{F}\left({\mathscr{H}}\right)}

\hat{\mathcal{O}}\in\mathcal{L}\left({\mathcal{F}\left({\mathscr{H}}\right)}\right)
may, of course, be written in the form
\hat{\mathcal{O}}=\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S}+\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{R}+\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{SR}
, where
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{S/R}\in\mathcal{L}\left({\mathcal{F}\left({\mathscr{H}_{S/R}}\right)}\right)
and
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{SR}
describes the coupling between the two subsystems. One may then ask whether the expectation value
\braket{\hat{\mathcal{O}}}
can be sensibly divided into system and reservoir contributions. In particular, what fraction of the averaged coupling term
\braket{\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{SR}}
should be assigned to each subsystem? For a partitioning of
\mathscr{H}
into subsystems of comparable size it seems intuitive that the coupling should be partitioned symmetrically, assigning
\frac{1}{2}\braket{\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{SR}}
to each subsystem. However, in the context of the thermodynamics of open quantum systems, an answer to this question has remained elusive, due to subtleties in the distinction between system internal energy and heat
Ludovico et al. 2014
;
Esposito et al. 2015
;
Bruch et al. 2016
;
Haughian et al. 2018
;
Talkner and Hänggi 2020
;
Strasberg and Winter 2021
;
Bergmann and Galperin 2021
;
Lacerda et al. 2023
. This thermodynamic bookkeeping typically falls into one of two camps: either half of the coupling Hamiltonian is assigned to the system, as in the symmetric partition, or all of it is. The remaining fraction is then assigned to the reservoir and used to describe heat dissipated.

In this article, we maintain that any partition of the energetics between two subsystems should be inherited from the division of the single-particle states
\mathscr{H}\rightarrow\mathscr{H}_{S}\oplus\mathscr{H}_{R}
. Moreover, it will be shown that such a “Hilbert-space partition” of any observable leads directly to the symmetric partition.

Hilbert-Space Partition

In what follows, we will denote by

III
Time Dependence

In order to construct the local dynamics of a partitioned expectation value
\braket{\hat{\mathcal{O}}|{}_{S}}(t)
, it will be useful to consider the Heisenberg evolution of the partitioned operators themselves. In what follows, we consider only non-interacting theories. Even in this simple case there is, however, some ambiguity in constructing this evolution: Should one first evolve the operator
\hat{\mathcal{O}}(t)
forward in time, and then construct the partition,
\hat{\mathcal{O}}(t)|_{S}
? Or partition
\hat{\mathcal{O}}(0)
and evolve this forward in time,

\hat{\mathcal{O}}|_{S}(t)
? Contrary to what one may expect, the two approaches are not generally equivalent.

Claim
.

\hat{\mathcal{O}}(t)|_{S}=\hat{\mathcal{O}}|_{S}(t)
if and only if
\left[{H},{\mathbb{P}_{S}}\right]=0
.

Proof.

In the absence of interactions, we have in general that
\hat{U}^{\dagger}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\hat{U}=\overset{}{\underset{nm}{\sum}}\bra{n}U^{\dagger}OU\ket{m}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{n}\hat{c}_{m}
. Where
\hat{U}
is the usual evolution operator generated by Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
. Then the partition of the time-evolved operator is

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{O}}(t)|_{S}=\frac{1}{2}\overset{}{\underset{nm}{\sum}}\bra{n}\left\{O_{H}(t),\mathbb{P}_{S}\right\}\ket{m}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{n}\hat{c}_{m},

(11)

with
O_{H}(t)=U^{\dagger}OU
the Heisenberg evolution of
O
. On the other hand, the time evolution of the partitioned operator gives

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{O}}|_{S}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\overset{}{\underset{nm}{\sum}}\bra{n}U^{\dagger}\left\{O,\mathbb{P}_{S}\right\}U\ket{m}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{n}\hat{c}_{m}.

(12)

Clearly, then, these two expressions will only be equivalent if
\left[{H},{\mathbb{P}_{S}}\right]=0
so that
U^{\dagger}\mathbb{P}_{S}U=\mathbb{P}_{S}
.
∎

In order to preserve the equivalence between the Schrödinger and Heisenberg pictures, one must use
Eq.

12
to describe the dynamics of the partitioned observable. We emphasize that in general
\left[{H},{\mathbb{P}_{S}}\right]\neq 0
, and so contrary to what one may expect, the evolution of the partition is not given by the partitioning of
\hat{\mathcal{O}}(t)
.

The operator
\mathcal{O}|_{S}
therefore obeys the inhomogeneous continuity equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{O}|_{S}=J_{\mathcal{O}}|_{S}+\Sigma_{\mathcal{O}}|_{S}.

(13)

With

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{O}}|_{S}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\{O_{H},J|_{S}\right\},

(14)

\displaystyle J|_{S}

\displaystyle=i\left[{H},{\mathbb{P}_{S}}\right]{,}\hskip 6.0pt\textrm{and}\hskip 6.0pt

(15)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\mathcal{O}}|_{S}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\frac{d}{dt}O_{H},\mathbb{P}_{S}\right\}.

(16)

In the above, the time dependence of the projection operator
\mathbb{P}_{S}(t)=U^{\dagger}\mathbb{P}_{S}U
has been suppressed. Using the fact that the second quantization of a commutator is the commutator of the second quantized operators, it can readily be shown that
\hat{J}|_{S}=\frac{d}{dt}\hat{N}_{S}
, where
\hat{N}_{S}
is the number operator for subsystem
\mathcal{F}\left({\mathscr{H}_{S}}\right)
, implying that
J|_{S}
is a probability current operator. Thus, we interpret
J_{\mathcal{O}}|_{S}
as a current operator for transport of
\mathcal{O}
into the subspace
\mathscr{H}_{S}
.
Eq.

16
can be written
\Sigma_{\mathcal{O}}|_{S}=\left[\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{O}\right]|_{S}
, and so we interpret this term as the local production of
\mathcal{O}
within the subspace
\mathscr{H}_{S}
.
The Fock-space operator
\hat{\mathcal{O}}|_{S}
therefore obeys a similar continuity equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{\mathcal{O}}|_{S}=\hat{J}_{\mathcal{O}}|_{S}+\hat{\Sigma}_{\mathcal{O}}|_{S}.

(17)

As an illustrative application of this partition, consider the energy current passing through site
\ket{i}
in the tight-binding model

\displaystyle\hat{H}=-t\overset{}{\underset{i,\delta}{\sum}}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i}\hat{c}_{i+\delta}+\mbox{h.c.},

(18)

where the sum on
\delta
denotes a sum over nearest neighbors. Using
Eq.

14
, we find for the energy current

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{H}|_{\ket{i}}=\frac{it^{2}}{2}\overset{}{\underset{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}{\sum}}\left({\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i+\delta+\delta^{\prime}}\hat{c}_{i}-\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{i}\hat{c}_{i-\delta-\delta^{\prime}}}\right),

(19)

which implies the textbook definition
Mahan 2000
for the energy current operator:

\displaystyle\hat{\vec{J}}_{H}|_{\ket{i}}=-\frac{it^{2}}{2}\overset{}{\underset{\delta,\delta^{\prime}}{\sum}}\left({\vec{\delta}+\vec{\delta}^{\prime}}\right)c^{\dagger}_{i+\delta+\delta^{\prime}}c_{i}.

(20)

Density of One-Body Observables

Up until now, we have discussed only partitions over a discrete subspace of
\mathscr{H}

\mathbb{P}_{S}

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathcal{O}}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left\{O,\ket{x}\bra{x}\right\}

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{\mathcal{O}}(x)=\overset{}{\underset{nm}{\sum}}\bra{n}\rho_{\mathcal{O}}(x)\ket{m}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{n}\hat{c}_{m}.

(22)

Note that, as with the discrete partition,

describes the influence of the non-local observable

This interpretation may be clarified by considering the operator
\hat{\mathcal{O}}
in position representation

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{O}}

\displaystyle=\int dx\,dy\,\hat{\mathcal{O}}(x,y){,}\hskip 6.0pt\textrm{with}\hskip 6.0pt

(23)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{O}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\overset{}{\underset{nm}{\sum}}\left[O_{nm}\psi_{n}(x)\psi^{*}_{m}(y)\right]\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(x)\hat{\psi}(y),

(24)

where
\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(x),\hat{\psi}(x)
are the usual fermionic field creation and annihilation operators, and
\psi_{n}(x)=\braket{x|n}
. The density in
Eq.

22
can be rewritten in terms of

as

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{\mathcal{O}}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int dy\left({\hat{\mathcal{O}}(x,y)+\hat{\mathcal{O}}(y,x)}\right).

(25)

Following the same arguments as in the previous section, we find that the density operator obeys a continuity equation

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{\rho}_{\mathcal{O}}(x,t)=-\nabla\cdot\hat{\vec{j}}_{\mathcal{O}}(x,t)+\hat{\sigma}_{\mathcal{O}}(x,t),

(26)

where

\displaystyle\vec{j}_{\mathcal{O}}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\{O_{H},\vec{j}(x,t)\right\},

(27)

\displaystyle\vec{j}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2m}U^{\dagger}\left\{\vec{p},\ket{x}\bra{x}\right\}U,

(28)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathcal{O}}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\frac{d}{dt}O_{H},U^{\dagger}\ket{x}\bra{x}U\right\}.

(29)

In the above,
\vec{p}=-i\nabla
is the usual momentum operator. Moreover, in slight contrast to
Eqs.

15
and
16

U^{\dagger}\ket{x}\bra{x}U
. The definitions of current density in

Claim

i\left[{H},{\ket{x}\bra{x}}\right]=-\nabla\cdot\vec{j}(x)

Only the kinetic part of the Hamiltonian is non-vanishing in the commutator, since for any two states

\displaystyle i\left[{H},{\ket{x}\bra{x}}\right]=\frac{i}{2m}\left[{\vec{p}^{\,2}},{\ket{x}\bra{x}}\right].

To proceed, we consider the matrix elements

\displaystyle i\bra{n}\left[{\vec{p}^{\,2}},{\ket{x}\bra{x}}\right]\ket{m}

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot\left({\psi_{m}\left({i\nabla\psi_{n}}\right)^{*}+\psi_{n}^{*}\left({i\nabla\psi_{m}}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=-\nabla\cdot\bra{n}\left\{\vec{p},\ket{x}\bra{x}\right\}\ket{m}.

Therefore, for any two states
\ket{n}
and
\ket{m}
,
\bra{n}i\left[{H},{\ket{x}\bra{x}}\right]\ket{m}=-\frac{1}{2m}\nabla\cdot\bra{n}\left\{\vec{p},\ket{x}\bra{x}\right\}\ket{m}

V
Entropy Partitions

Of particular interest is the partition of the von Neumann entropy. Define in the Schrödinger picture the entropy operator

\displaystyle\hat{S}(t)=-\text{ln}\left({\hat{\rho}(t)}\right),

(30)

where
\hat{\rho}(t)
is the density matrix for the global ensemble. Then
S(t)=\braket{\hat{S}(t)}=S(0)
due to the unitary evolution of
\hat{\rho}
. The Heisenberg picture entropy operator is then
\hat{S}_{H}(t)=-\hat{U}^{\dagger}\text{ln}\left({\hat{\rho}(t)}\right)\hat{U}=\hat{S}(0)
. So the entropy operator is constant, in agreement with our expectation that the global entropy should be constant under unitary evolution.

In the absence of inter-particle interactions, the state of the system may be taken to be a product of the form

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}=\overset{}{\underset{\ket{k}}{\prod}}\left[f_{k}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{c}_{k}+(1-f_{k})\hat{c}_{k}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{k}\right],

(31)

where
\left\{\ket{k}\right\}
is any set of single-particle orbitals which span
\mathscr{H}
and
f_{k}
describes the probability that the state
\ket{k}
is occupied. We define the
statistical basis
to be the set of single-particle orbitals over which the density matrix factorizes.
Such a state may describe, for example, a quantum system initially in equilibrium that is subsequently acted upon by a time-dependent external force.

For such a product state, the entropy operator becomes a sum of particle- and hole-ordered single-particle operators

\displaystyle\hat{S}=-\overset{}{\underset{k}{\sum}}\text{ln}\left({f_{k}}\right)\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{k}\hat{c}_{k}-\overset{}{\underset{k}{\sum}}\text{ln}\left({1-f_{k}}\right)\hat{c}_{k}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{k}.

(32)

The arguments of the previous sections hold just as well for a hole-ordered operator, and so the entropy may be partitioned as in
Eqs.

7
and
8
. In particular, its partition and density obey the continuity equations

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{S}|_{S}=\hat{J}_{S}|_{S},

(33)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{\rho}_{S}(x)=-\nabla\cdot\hat{\vec{j}}_{S}(x),

(34)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{S}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}\psi_{k^{\prime}}(x)\psi^{\ast}_{k}(x)\left[\text{ln}\left({f_{k}f_{k^{\prime}}}\right)c^{\dagger}_{k}c_{k^{\prime}}\right.

(35)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\text{ln}\left({(1-f_{k})(1-f_{k^{\prime}})}\right)c_{k^{\prime}}c^{\dagger}_{k}\right],

\displaystyle\hat{\vec{j}}_{S}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}\langle k|\,\vec{j}(x)|k^{\prime}\rangle\left[\text{ln}\left({f_{k}f_{k^{\prime}}}\right)c^{\dagger}_{k}c_{k^{\prime}}\right.

(36)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left.\text{ln}\left({(1-f_{k})(1-f_{k^{\prime}})}\right)c_{k^{\prime}}c^{\dagger}_{k}\right]

are the entropy density operator and the entropy current density operator, respectively. The net entropy current operator
\hat{J}_{S}|_{S}
can be obtained as minus the surface integral of Eq. (
36

Crucially, as a consequence of global entropy conservation under unitary evolution, we find that there is no local entropy production in
Eqs.

33
and
34
. Comparing to
Eq.

1

Consistency with the Third Law

Applying the Hilbert space partition to the entropy operator leads to a system entropy operator

\displaystyle\hat{S}|_{S}=\hat{S}_{S}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{S}_{SR}.

(37)

In light of the above discussion, this would imply that the heat added to an open quantum system should be defined as
Kumar et al. 2024

\displaystyle\dj Q_{S}=d\!\braket{\hat{H}_{S}+\frac{1}{2}\hat{H}_{SR}}-\mu d\!\braket{\hat{N}_{S}}-\dj W_{S}

(38)

for linear deviations from equilibrium, where
\hat{N}_{S}
is the system number operator and

Various authors

;
Esposito et al. 2015
;
Bruch et al. 2016
;
Haughian et al. 2018
;
Strasberg and Winter 2021
;
Bergmann and Galperin 2021
;
Lacerda et al. 2023
have suggested including different fractions of the coupling energy
\braket{\hat{H}_{SR}}
in the heat partition. However, it can be shown that any partition of the entropy apart from

;
Bruch et al. 2016
;
Haughian et al. 2018
;

VII
Conclusions

In this article, we have developed a framework to partition quantum observables based on a partition of the underlying single-particle Hilbert space. We have provided explicit expressions for the partition of Fock-space operators corresponding to generic
N
-body observables. For the case of an open quantum system, a bipartite partition between system (S) and reservoir (R) was applied to both the Hamiltonian and the entropy of the system. The Hilbert-space partition was shown to correspond to a symmetric (
\alpha=1/2
) partition
Ludovico et al. 2014
;
Bruch et al. 2016
;
Haughian et al. 2018
;
Kumar et al. 2024
of the off-diagonal 1-body observables, such as the coupling Hamiltonian
H_{SR}
between system and reservoir. Any partition with
\alpha\neq 1/2
was shown to lead to a singular entropy, and hence does not provide a basis to construct a consistent thermodynamics from the statistical mechanics of the problem.

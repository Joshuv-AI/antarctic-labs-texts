One-Shot Coherence Distillation: Towards Completing the Picture
Qi Zhao, Yunchao Liu, Xiao Yuan, Eric Chitambar and Andreas Winter

Abstract
The resource framework of quantum coherence was introduced by Baumgratz, Cramer and Plenio [ Phys. Rev. Lett. 113, 140401 (2014)] and further developed by Winter and Yang [ Phys. Rev. Lett. 116, 120404 (2016)]. We consider the one-shot problem of distilling pure coherence from a single instance of a given resource state. Specifically, we determine the distillable coherence with a given fidelity under incoherent operations (IO) through a generalisation of the Winter-Yang protocol. This is compared to the distillable coherence under maximal incoherent operations (MIO) and dephasing-covariant incoherent operations (DIO), which can be cast as a semidefinite programme, that has been presented previously by Regula et al. [ Phys. Rev. Lett. 121, 010401 (2018)]. Our results are given in terms of a smoothed min-relative entropy distance from the incoherent set of states, and a variant of the hypothesis-testing relative entropy distance, respectively. The one-shot distillable coherence is also related to one-shot randomness extraction. Moreover, from the one-shot formulas under IO, MIO, DIO, we can recover the optimal distillable rate in the many-copy asymptotics, yielding the relative entropy of coherence. These results can be compared with previous work by some of the present authors [Zhao et al. , Phys. Rev. Lett. 120, 070403 (2018)] on one-shot coherence formation under IO, MIO, DIO and also SIO. This shows that the amount of distillable coherence is essentially the same for IO, DIO, and MIO, despite the fact that the three classes of operations are very different. We also relate the distillable coherence under strictly incoherent operations (SIO) to a constrained hypothesis testing problem and explicitly show the existence of bound coherence under SIO in the asymptotic regime.

, the dephasing-covariant incoherent operation (DIO)

An important problem in the resource theory is convertibility of states via free operations, especially those between an arbitrary state
\rho
and a maximal resource state
|\Psi_{M}\rangle
. In an
M

|\Psi_{M}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{i=1}^{M}|i\rangle
has the maximal coherence with respect to the aforementioned, and indeed all, coherence measures, and the qubit state
|\Psi_{2}\rangle
serves as resource unit in the theory, the “cosbit”
[
23

The process of converting a given state
\rho
to
|\Psi_{M}\rangle
is referred to as
coherence distillation
and the reverse process as
coherence dilution

In the asymptotic case where an unbounded number of independent and identically distributed
(i.i.d.) copies of the initial state are provided, the optimal rates of asymptotic distillation and dilution are quantified by the relative entropy of coherence and the coherence of formation, respectively
[
17
]
.

In practical scenarios, i.i.d. resources are not available and an analysis of non-asymptotic tasks becomes crucial. In recent work
[
24
]
, the formation problem was addressed in the one-shot setting under all four of the above operational classes. Subsequently, the converse question of one-shot coherence distillation was solved for the classes MIO and DIO
[
25
]
. In the present paper, we close the remaining gap by providing an analysis of general one-shot coherence distillation under IO and SIO. Our results show that, while not being exactly identical, the three classes MIO, DIO, and IO all lead to essentially the same expression for the distillable coherence in terms of a min-relative entropy distance from the incoherent states; the differences are in the smoothing parameters and universal additive terms. When extended to the asymptotic case, our one-shot results imply that MIO, DIO, and IO all have distillable coherence given by the relative entropy, thereby recovering earlier work found in
[
17
]
. In contrast, we show that the coherence distillation by SIO can behave quite differently than its more general counterparts, and we characterize the one-shot distillable coherence as a constrained hypothesis testing problem. We further show that coherence can be a
bound
resource under SIO, meaning that states exist with zero distillable coherence but nonzero coherence cost.
We also show the relationship among the extractable randomness, IO and DIO distillable rate, which are are indeed closely related. The reason is that the distillation proceeds by a kind of decoupling process, and can thus be related to randomness extraction.

II-B

Coherence dilution

The one-shot coherence dilution problem characterizes the minimal resource required for the formation of a target state with an allowed error
\varepsilon
. The definition of one-shot coherence dilution is as follows.

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\in\{\text{MIO},\,\text{DIO},\,\text{IO},\,\text{SIO}\}
denote some class of
incoherent operations. Then for a given state
\rho
and
\varepsilon\geq 0
,
the one-shot coherence formation cost with error
\varepsilon
under
\mathcal{O}

is defined as

C_{c,\mathcal{O}}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)=\min_{\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}}\bigl\{\log M\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,F(\Lambda(\Psi_{M}),\rho)^{2}\geq 1-\varepsilon\bigr\}.

(3)

where
F(\rho,\sigma)=\operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\sqrt{\rho}}

is the usual mixed state fidelity.
By default, here and throughout the paper,
\log\equiv\log_{2}
is the binary
logarithm, in accordance with information theoretic use.

In Ref.
[
24
]
, several coherence monotones were proposed to estimate
the one-shot coherence cost. The first two are based on the max relative entropy

D_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)=\log\min\{\lambda\;|\;\rho\leq\lambda\sigma\}
. Alternatively, one can introduce this quantity using the sandwiched quantum
\alpha
-Rényi divergence

\tilde{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\left(\operatorname{Tr}\left[\left(\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\rho\sigma^{\frac{1-\alpha}{2\alpha}}\right)^{\alpha}\right]\right),

(4)

letting
\alpha\to\infty

[
27
]
.
One then defines

\displaystyle C_{\max}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\min_{\delta\in\mathcal{I}}D_{\max}(\rho\|\delta)=\log\min\{\lambda\;|\;\rho\leq\lambda\delta\},

(5)

\displaystyle C_{\Delta,\max}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\min_{\sigma\in\overline{A_{\rho}}}D_{\max}(\rho\|\sigma)=\log\min\{\lambda\;|\;\rho\leq\lambda\Delta(\rho)\},

(6)

where
A_{\rho}=\{\sigma\geq 0\;|\;\sigma+t\rho=(1+t)\Delta(\rho),\;t>0\}

[
28
]
and
\overline{A_{\rho}}
is its closure. The quantity
C_{\max}(\rho)
is a monotone under MIO, while
C_{\Delta,\max}(\rho)
is a monotone under DIO, but not IO in general. However, for a pure state
|\psi\rangle
,
C_{\Delta,\max}
reduces to its
incoherent rank

[
28
]
, defined as

C_{0}(\psi)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=S_{0}(\Delta(\psi))=\log\text{rank}[\Delta(\psi)],

(7)

and the incoherent rank is a monotone under IO even for stochastic pure state transformations
[
12
]
. Thus, one can obtain a general mixed state monotone for IO by taking a convex roof extension:

\displaystyle C_{0}(\rho)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\min_{p_{i},|\psi_{i}\rangle}\max_{i}C_{0}(\psi_{i}),

(8)

where the minimization is over all pure state ensembles satisfying
\rho=\sum_{i}p_{i}|\psi_{i}\rangle\langle\psi_{i}|
. Note, the minimization is followed by a maximization rather than an averaging over the
|\psi_{i}\rangle
(as typically done in such measures) because
C_{0}(\psi)
is a stochastic IO monotone.

Since we allow
\varepsilon
error in our one-shot tasks, we likewise apply an
\varepsilon
smoothing to our coherence measures. Depending on the quantity, this is done by either minimizing or maximizing the measure over all states
\rho^{\prime}
lying in an
\varepsilon
-ball around
\rho
. For example, if
C
represents any one of the measures
C_{\max}
,
C_{\Delta,\max}
or
C_{0}
, its
\varepsilon
-smoothed variant is given by

C^{\varepsilon}(\rho)=\min_{\rho^{\prime}\in B_{\varepsilon}(\rho)}C(\rho^{\prime}).

(9)

Here
B_{\varepsilon}(\rho)=\{\rho^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}P(\rho^{\prime},\rho)<\varepsilon\}
is
defined with respect to the purified distance
P(\rho^{\prime},\rho)=\sqrt{1-F(\rho^{\prime},\rho)^{2}}
.
Note that the purified distance is related to the trace distance by
[
29
]

1-\sqrt{1-P(\rho^{\prime},\rho)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\rho-\rho^{\prime}\|_{1}\leq P(\rho^{\prime},\rho).

As shown in
[
24
]
, the measures
C^{\varepsilon}_{\max}
,
C^{\varepsilon}_{\Delta,\max}

and
C^{\varepsilon}_{0}
precisely characterize the one-shot coherence formation for MIO, DIO,
and IO/SIO, respectively (see Table
I
). Note that our definition of

B_{\varepsilon}(\rho)

differs from
[
24
]
by replacing
\varepsilon\leftrightarrow\sqrt{\varepsilon}
.

The regularized (many-copy) coherence cost of formation can then be defined using the one-shot quantities.
For
\mathcal{O}\in\{\text{MIO},\,\text{DIO},\,\text{IO},\,\text{SIO}\}
, the asymptotic rate of coherence cost for a state
\rho
under operations
\mathcal{O}
is defined as

C_{c,\mathcal{O}}^{\infty}(\rho)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}C_{c,\mathcal{O}}^{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\otimes n}).

(10)

Remarkably, for all four operational classes, the coherence cost has a single-letter characterization.
For MIO and DIO, the regularized coherence cost is given by the relative entropy of coherence, which we call the asymptotic coherence cost under
\text{MIO},\,\text{DIO}

[
24
,
26
]
,

C_{r}(\rho)=\min_{\delta\in\mathcal{I}}D(\rho\|\delta)=S(\Delta(\rho))-S(\rho),

(11)

where
D(X\|Y)=-\operatorname{Tr}[X(\log Y-\log X)]
is the relative entropy, and we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}C_{c,MIO/DIO}^{\varepsilon}\left(\rho^{\otimes n}\right)=C_{r}(\rho).

(12)

In the following, we use
\lim
instead of
\limsup
or
\liminf
for simplicity. On the other hand, for IO and SIO, the regularized coherence cost
is given by the so-called coherence of formation
[
17
]
, which we call the asymptotic coherence cost under
\text{IO},\,\text{SIO}
and is defined as

C_{f}(\rho)=\min_{p_{i},|\psi_{i}\rangle}\sum_{i}p_{i}C_{r}(\psi_{i}),

(13)

where

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}C_{c,IO/SIO}^{\varepsilon}\left(\rho^{\otimes n}\right)=C_{f}(\rho).

(14)

In contrast to the entanglement of formation, the coherence of formation is an
additive function
[
17
]
.

II-C

Coherence distillation

We next turn to the main focus of this paper, which is the task of transforming a given state
\rho
into a maximally coherent pure state. The one-shot distillation of the problem with
\varepsilon
error is stated as follows.

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\in\{\text{MIO},\,\text{DIO},\,\text{IO},\,\text{SIO}\}
denote some class of
incoherent operations. Then for a given state
\rho
and
\varepsilon\geq 0
,
the one-shot coherence distillation rate with error
\varepsilon
under
\mathcal{O}

is defined as

C_{d,\mathcal{O}}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)=\max_{\Lambda\in\mathcal{O}}\bigl\{\log M\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,F(\Lambda(\rho),\Psi_{M})^{2}\geq 1-\varepsilon\bigr\}.

(15)

In order to quantify the one-shot distillable rates, we first introduce two additional functions. The first one is based on the min quantum Rényi divergence
D_{\min}(\rho\|\sigma)=-\log F(\rho,\sigma)^{2}=\tilde{D}_{1/2}(\rho\|\sigma)

[
27
]
,
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
in Eq. (
4
). Using this quantity, the
min-entropy of coherence

C_{\min}(\rho)
can be defined as

\displaystyle C_{\min}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\min_{\delta\in\mathcal{I}}D_{\min}(\rho\|\delta).

(16)

The properties of
C_{\min}
and its relationship between other coherence monotones are discussed in
[
30
]
.

The second quantifier of coherence relevant to our study is based on the hypothesis testing relative entropy
[
31
,
32
]
. In the task of hypothesis testing, a positive
operator valued measure (POVM) with two elements
\{W,\mathbbm{1}-W\}
is used to distinguish
two possible states
\rho
and
\sigma
.
The probability of obtaining a correct guess on
\rho
is
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho W)
and the probability of obtaining a wrong guess on
\sigma
is
\mathrm{Tr}(\sigma W)
. The hypothesis testing relative entropy characterizes the minimal wrong guessing probability on
\sigma
, with the constraint that the probability of the correct guess on
\rho
is no less than
1-\varepsilon
:

\begin{split}D_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho\|\sigma)=-\log\min\bigl\{\operatorname{Tr}\sigma{W}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,0\leq{W}&\leq\mathbbm{1},\bigr.\\
\bigl.\operatorname{Tr}\rho{W}&\geq 1-\varepsilon\bigr\}.\end{split}

(17)

This serves as a parent quantity for two other coherence functions. Namely, we have

\displaystyle C^{\varepsilon}_{H}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\min\{D^{\varepsilon}_{H}(\rho\|\delta)\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\delta\in\mathcal{I}\},

(18)

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}^{\varepsilon}_{H}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\min\{D^{\varepsilon}_{H}(\rho\|\sigma)\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\sigma=\Delta(\sigma),\ \operatorname{Tr}\sigma=1\}.

(19)

The function
\widetilde{C}^{\varepsilon}_{H}(\rho)
characterizes the one-shot distillable distillable coherence under MIO and DIO.

In the many-copy scenario, the regularized coherence distillation rate
by operations
\mathcal{O}\in\{\text{MIO},\,\text{DIO},\,\text{IO},\,\text{SIO}\}
is defined as

C_{d,\mathcal{O}}^{\infty}(\rho)=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}C_{d,\mathcal{O}}^{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\otimes n}).

(20)

For MIO, DIO, and IO, the distillable coherence of
\rho
is given by the relative entropy of coherence, which we call the asymptotic coherence distillation rate
[
17
]
, where

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}C_{d,MIO/DIO/IO}^{\varepsilon}\left(\rho^{\otimes n}\right)=C_{r}(\rho).

(21)

Combining Eq. (
12
), (
21
), we can conclude that coherence is strongly converse under MIO/DIO. In contrast, as we show below, certain coherent states are undistillable under SIO. A summary of the distillation/formation rates for different operations is given in Table

III
One-shot distillation

under MIO and DIO

In this section, we review the results of
[
25
]

on MIO and DIO distillation.

For a quantum channel

\Lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{L}(A)\to\mathcal{L}(B)
,
consider its Choi operator

\Gamma=({\operatorname{id}}\otimes\Lambda)\Phi=\sum_{i,j}|i\rangle\langle j|_{R}\otimes\Lambda(|i\rangle\langle j|_{A}),

(22)

where
R
is isomorphic to
A
. Let
\Gamma_{ij}=\Lambda(|i\rangle\langle j|_{A})
.
By Choi’s theorem,
\Lambda
is completely positive if and only if

\Gamma\geq 0
. Also,
\Lambda
is trace preserving if and only if

\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma_{ij}=\delta_{ij}
.

Now consider
\Lambda\in\text{MIO}
, meaning
\Lambda(\delta)\in\mathcal{I}

for all
\delta\in\mathcal{I}
, which is equivalent to
\Lambda(|i\rangle\langle i|)\in\mathcal{I}

for all
i
. For the Choi matrix this says
\Gamma_{ii}\in\mathcal{I}
for all
i
.
For
\Lambda\in\text{DIO}
, from
\Lambda(\Delta(|i\rangle\langle j|))=\Delta(\Lambda(|i\rangle\langle j|))
for all
i
and
j
, we get
\delta_{ij}\Gamma_{ij}=\Delta(\Gamma_{ij})
.

Since
\Lambda(\rho)=\sum_{i,j}\rho_{ij}\Gamma_{ij}
(by linearity), we have now,

F(\Lambda(\rho),\Psi_{M})^{2}=\operatorname{Tr}[\Lambda(\rho)\Psi_{M}]=\sum_{i,j}\rho_{ij}\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma_{ij}\Psi_{M}.

(23)

The one-shot distillation under MIO/DIO can thus be expressed as

\begin{split}\max M\text{ s.t. }\sum_{i,j}\rho_{ij}\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma_{ij}\Psi_{M}&\geq 1-\varepsilon,\\
\sum_{i,j}|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes\Gamma_{ij}&\geq 0,\\
\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma_{ij}&=\delta_{ij},\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\begin{cases}\phantom{\delta_{ij}}\Gamma_{ii}\in\mathcal{I}&\text{[MIO]},\\
\delta_{ij}\Gamma_{ij}=\Delta(\Gamma_{ij})&\text{[DIO]}.\end{cases}\end{split}

(24)

Suppose that the
\Gamma_{ij}
satisfy the above constraints.
Consider the twirling transformation

\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ij}=\frac{1}{M!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{M}}U_{\pi}\Gamma_{ij}U_{\pi}^{\dagger},

(25)

where
S_{M}
denotes the permutation group on
M
objects,
and
U_{\pi}
is the unitary representation of
\pi
as a permutation matrix.
It is easy to see that then the
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{ij}
also satisfy the constraints,
and lead to the same objective function, due to the permutation
invariance of
\Psi_{M}
.
Therefore, without loss of generality, we can assume that each of the

\Gamma_{ij}
is permutation-invariant and has hence the form

\Gamma_{ij}=\alpha_{ij}\Psi_{M}+\beta_{ij}\frac{\mathbbm{1}-\Psi_{M}}{M-1}.

(26)

Furthermore,
\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma_{ij}=\alpha_{ij}+\beta_{ij}
.
We can see that both MIO and DIO lead to the following constraints:
When
i\neq j
, we have
\beta_{ij}=-\alpha_{ij}
and when
i=j
,
we have
\alpha_{ii}=\frac{1}{M}
,
\beta_{ii}=1-\frac{1}{M}
.
For the matrices
A=(\alpha_{ij})_{i,j}
and
B=(\beta_{ij})_{i,j}
, this
translates into the simple relation
B=\mathbbm{1}-A
.
Notice that

\Gamma=\sum_{i,j}\alpha_{ij}|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes\Psi_{M}+\sum_{i,j}\beta_{ij}|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes\frac{\mathbbm{1}-\Psi_{M}}{M-1},

(27)

and we must satisfy
\langle\psi|\Gamma|\psi\rangle\geq 0
for all
|\psi\rangle
.
Consider the special case
|\psi\rangle=|u\rangle\otimes|v\rangle
;
by choosing
|v\rangle=|\Psi_{M}\rangle
, we learn that
A\geq 0
.
By choosing
|v\rangle
orthogonal with
|\Psi_{M}\rangle
, we see likewise
that
B\geq 0
. Conversely,
A,B\geq 0
implies that
\Gamma\geq 0
.

In conclusion, one-shot distillation under MIO/DIO can be solved
by the following SDP:

C_{d,MIO/DIO}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)=\log M_{\text{opt}}
, with

\begin{split}M_{\text{opt}}=\max M\text{ s.t. }\operatorname{Tr}\rho^{T}A&\geq 1-\varepsilon,\\
0\leq A&\leq\mathbbm{1},\ A_{ii}=\frac{1}{M}\,\forall i.\end{split}

(28)

To express this result in terms of a suitable relative-entropy distance,
we recall the
hypothesis testing relative entropy
introduced above
[
31
,
32
]
,

\begin{split}D_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho\|\sigma)=-\log\min\bigl\{\operatorname{Tr}\sigma W\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,0\leq W&\leq\mathbbm{1},\bigr.\\
\bigl.\operatorname{Tr}\rho W&\geq 1-\varepsilon\bigr\},\end{split}

(29)

and the corresponding coherence measure

C_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)=\min_{\delta\in\mathcal{I}}D_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho\|\delta).

(30)

We show that the coherence measure
C_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)
can be computed by SDP.
Notice that

\begin{split}C_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)&=-\log\max_{\delta\in\mathcal{I}}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}W\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}0\leq W\leq\mathbbm{1}\\
\operatorname{Tr}\rho W\geq 1-\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\operatorname{Tr}\delta W\\
&=-\log\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}W\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}0\leq W\leq\mathbbm{1}\\
\operatorname{Tr}\rho W\geq 1-\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\max_{\delta\in\mathcal{I}}\operatorname{Tr}\delta W\\
&=-\log\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}W\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}0\leq W\leq\mathbbm{1}\\
\operatorname{Tr}\rho W\geq 1-\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\max_{i}W_{ii},\end{split}

(31)

where in the second line we have appealed to the minimax theorem
[
33
]
,
noting that the objective function,
\operatorname{Tr}\delta W
, is linear in each
of the two arguments, and that the domains of optimization are closed convex sets.

The third line in Eq. (
31
) can now be expressed as an SDP as follows:

\begin{split}\mu_{\text{opt}}=\max\mu\text{ s.t. }\operatorname{Tr}\rho W&\geq 1-\varepsilon,\\
0\leq W&\leq\mathbbm{1},\ W_{ii}\leq\frac{1}{\mu}\,\forall i,\end{split}

(32)

where
C_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)=\log\mu_{\text{opt}}
.
Notice that in Eq. (
28
) we have a transpose on
\rho
,
while in Eq. (
32
) we don’t. We can simply change
A
into
A^{T}

in the SDP (
28
) without changing its value, so that the two SDP has similar
form, except that one has an equality sign where the other other
has a “
\leq
”. In
[
25
]
, it was shown that
the r.h.s. of Eq. (
28
) can be expressed in terms of
D_{H}^{\varepsilon}
,
as well:

\begin{split}C_{d,MIO/DIO}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)=\max\log M\\
\text{ s.t. }\operatorname{Tr}\rho^{T}A&\geq 1-\varepsilon,\\
0\leq A&\leq\mathbbm{1},\ A_{ii}=\frac{1}{M}\,\forall i\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=\min D_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho\|\delta)\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\text{ s.t. }\delta\text{ diagonal and }\operatorname{Tr}\delta=1\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\widetilde{C}_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho),\end{split}

(33)

where the minimization is crucially over Hermitian, but not necessarily positive semidefinite matrices
\delta
.

We record these findings in the following theorem.

Theorem 3

(Regula
et al.

[
25
]
)
.

For any state
\rho
, the one-shot MIO- and DIO-distillable coherence
is given by

C_{d,\text{DIO}}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)=C_{d,\text{MIO}}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)=\widetilde{C}_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)\leq C_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho).

(34)

IV
One-shot distillation under IO

Now we come to the main contribution of the present paper, the extension
of the one-shot distillation protocols to the class IO.

To start, recall the definition of the min-relative entropy and
its smoothed version,

\begin{split}D_{\min}(\rho\|\sigma)&=-\log F(\rho,\sigma)^{2},\\
D_{\min}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)&=\max_{\rho^{\prime}\in B^{\varepsilon}(\rho)}D_{\min}(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma).\end{split}

(35)

The
smooth min-relative entropy of coherence
is defined as

C_{\min}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)=\max_{\rho^{\prime}\in B^{\varepsilon}(\rho)}\min_{\delta\in\mathcal{I}}D_{\min}(\rho\|\delta).

(36)

One might wonder why we do not interchange min and max here, but this
is the version of the quantity that appears naturally both in the achievability
bound we will derive on Subsection
IV-A
, and in the
upper bound in Subsection
IV-B
.

IV-A

An achievable lower bound

We will generalize the protocol in
[
17
, Thm. 6]
to
obtain a min-relative-entropic lower bound on the distillable
coherence. In the process, the privacy amplification aspect will
become even more apparent.

Theorem 4
.

For an arbitrary state
\rho
and
0<\varepsilon<1
,

C^{\varepsilon}_{d,IO}(\rho)\geq C^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\eta}_{\min}(\rho)-2\log\frac{1}{\eta},

(37)

for any
0<\eta<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
.

Proof.

In order to accomplish our proof, we need to introduce the conditional min/max entropy and their smoothed versions. For a bipartite quantum state
\rho^{AB}
, the min-entropy of
A
conditioned on
B
is defined as

H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho^{AB}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-\min_{\sigma^{B}}D_{\max}(\rho^{AB}\|\mathbbm{1}^{A}\otimes\sigma^{B}),

(38)

and the max-entropy of
A
conditioned on
B
is defined as

H_{\max}(A|B)_{\rho^{AB}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-H_{\min}(A|C)_{\rho^{AC}},

(39)

where
\rho^{ABC}
is a purification of
\rho^{AB}
and
\rho^{AC}=\operatorname{Tr}_{B}\rho^{ABC}
. It is proven that the max-entropy has an alternative form
[
34
]

H_{\max}(A|B)_{\rho^{AB}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=-\min_{\sigma^{B}}D_{\min}(\rho^{AB}\|\mathbbm{1}^{A}\otimes\sigma^{B}).

(40)

Furthermore, the smoothed conditional min- and max-entropy are defined by

\displaystyle H_{\min}^{\varepsilon}(A|B)_{\rho^{AB}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\max_{\rho^{\prime}\in B^{\varepsilon}(\rho^{AB})}H_{\min}^{\varepsilon}(A|B)_{\rho^{\prime}},

(41)

\displaystyle H_{\max}^{\varepsilon}(A|B)_{\rho^{AB}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\min_{\rho^{\prime}\in B^{\varepsilon}(\rho^{AB})}H_{\max}^{\varepsilon}(A|B)_{\rho^{\prime}}.

In the following proof, we denote the system of interest as
A
, i.e.
\rho^{A}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\rho
, and the incoherent basis as
\{|x\rangle\}
. Denote
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
.
Choose a purification of
\rho^{A}
,

|\psi\rangle^{AE}=\sum_{x}\sqrt{p_{x}}|x\rangle^{A}|\psi_{x}\rangle^{E},

where
\operatorname{Tr}_{E}\psi^{AE}=\rho^{A}
, and introduce the
dephased cq-state

\omega^{AE}=(\Delta\otimes{\operatorname{id}})\psi=\sum_{x}p_{x}|x\rangle\langle x|^{A}\otimes\psi_{x}^{E}.

According to
[
35
, Cor. 5.6.1]
(actually, a slight adaptation
to get rid of a factor of
2
), for every

\log M\leq H_{\min}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\eta}(A|E)_{\omega}-2\log\frac{1}{\eta}

there exists a function
G\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{X}\rightarrow[M]=\{1,2,\ldots,M\}

such that the state

\Omega^{KE}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(G\otimes{\operatorname{id}})\omega^{AE}=\sum_{x}p_{x}|G(x)\rangle\langle G(x)|^{K}\otimes\psi_{x}^{E}

satisfies

\frac{1}{2}\left\|\Omega^{KE}-\tau_{K}\otimes\sigma^{E}\right\|_{1}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

for
\tau^{K}=\frac{1}{M}\mathbbm{1}_{K}
and a suitable state
\sigma^{E}

(which may be equal to
\omega^{E}
, but it doesn’t have to be).
Hence, because of the well-known relation
between trace distance and fidelity
[
29
]
,

1-F(\rho,\sigma)\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{1-F(\rho,\sigma)^{2}},

we have
F(\Omega^{KE},\tau_{K}\otimes\sigma^{E})\geq 1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
.

To turn this property, which encapsulates the uniformity and independence
of the “key”
K=G(X)
from
E
, into a coherence distillation protocol,
consider the following purification of
\Omega^{KE}
:

|\Omega\rangle^{KAE}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{x}\sqrt{p_{x}}|G(x)\rangle^{K}|x\rangle^{A}|\psi_{x}\rangle^{E},

which can be obtained at from
|\psi\rangle^{AE}
by applying the isometry

U\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}|x\rangle^{A}\longmapsto|G(x)\rangle^{K}|x\rangle^{A}.

Crucially,
U
is incoherent (even SIO).
For
\tau_{K}\otimes\sigma^{E}
, on the other hand, we choose a purification

|\Phi\rangle^{KL}\otimes|\zeta\rangle^{EF}
, with the standard maximally
entangled state
|\Phi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}|i\rangle|i\rangle
.
By Uhlmann’s theorem
[
36
]
,
there exists an isometry
V\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\hookrightarrow LF
such that

|\langle\Phi|\langle\zeta|\cdot(\mathbbm{1}_{KE}\otimes V)|\Omega\rangle|\geq 1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

(42)

If we had
|\Phi\rangle^{KL}|\zeta\rangle^{EF}
, we could clearly create
a maximally coherent state
\Psi_{M}
on system
K
by tracing out
F
,
and destructively measuring
L
in the Fourier conjugate
basis
|\widehat{\alpha}\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=Z^{\alpha}|\Psi_{M}\rangle
. Here
Z
is the phase unitary defined in Eq. (
1
).

With this, the protocol is clear: starting from
\rho^{A}
, first
apply
U
; then apply
V
, followed by tracing out
F
,
measuring
L
in the Fourier basis
\{|\widehat{\alpha}\rangle\}_{\alpha=0,\ldots,M-1}
;
finally, apply
Z^{\alpha}
on
K
.
The first and the third step clearly are incoherent (even SIO); the second
seems suspicious, and indeed
V
may not be incoherent at all, but notice that
we follow it by a destructive measurement, and these are all IO.
We can write Kraus operators of the resulting map
\Lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\rightarrow K

as follows:

M_{\alpha\beta}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigl(Z^{\alpha}_{K}\otimes\langle\widetilde{\alpha}|^{L}\langle\beta|^{F}V\bigr)U,

where
\{|\beta\rangle\}
is an arbitrary basis of
F
.

To analyze the fidelity of the protocol, we pass to the purifications
and look at eq. (
42
); by the monotonicity of the
fidelity under the CPTP maps
\Lambda
and
\operatorname{Tr}_{E}
, we obtain

F\bigl(\Lambda(\rho),\Psi_{M}\bigr)\geq 1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
and
hence
F\bigl(\Lambda(\rho),\Psi_{M}\bigr)^{2}\geq 1-\varepsilon
.

This shows that
\log M\approx H_{\min}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\eta}(A|E)_{\omega}-2\log\frac{1}{\eta}

is achievable. Now, introducing the purification

|\omega\rangle^{ABE}=\sum_{x}\sqrt{p_{x}}|x\rangle^{A}|x\rangle^{B}|\psi_{x}\rangle
, we have

\begin{split}H_{\min}^{\alpha}(A|E)_{\omega}&=-H_{\max}^{\alpha}(A|B)_{\omega}\\
&=-\log\min_{\omega^{\prime}\in B^{\alpha}(\omega)}\max_{\sigma}F(\omega^{\prime},\mathbbm{1}\otimes\sigma)^{2}\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\geq-\log\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega^{\prime}\in B^{\alpha}(\omega)\\
\text{max.\,correlated}\end{subarray}}\max_{\sigma}F(\omega^{\prime},\mathbbm{1}\otimes\sigma)^{2}\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=-\log\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega^{\prime}\in B^{\alpha}(\omega)\\
\text{max.\,correlated}\end{subarray}}\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal{I}}F(\omega^{\prime},\mathbbm{1}\otimes\sigma)^{2}\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=-\log\min_{\rho^{\prime}\in B^{\alpha}(\rho)}\max_{\delta\in\mathcal{I}}F(\rho^{\prime},\delta)^{2}\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=\max_{\rho^{\prime}\in B^{\alpha}(\rho)}\min_{\delta\in\mathcal{I}}D_{\min}(\rho^{\prime}\|\delta)\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!=C_{\min}^{\alpha}(\rho).\end{split}

(43)

Here, the second line follows from Eq. (
40
), and the third line is motivated by the observation that

\omega^{AB}=\sum_{xy}\sqrt{p_{x}p_{y}}\langle\psi_{y}|\psi_{x}\rangle|xx\rangle\langle yy|

is a maximally correlated state, so it is natural to impose the same
structure on
\omega^{\prime}
; the fourth line follows from the
Z\otimes Z^{\dagger}
-invariance
of maximally correlated states, so by the concavity of the fidelity we
can impose w.l.o.g. the same structure on
\mathbbm{1}\otimes\sigma
, meaning that

\sigma
is diagonal. The rest is straightforward algebra.

\blacksquare

Note that the equality in Eq. (
43
) can also be achieved by
directly using the results by Coles
[
37
]
, or
[
30
]
:

H_{\min}(X|E)_{\rho_{XE}}=\min_{\delta\in\mathcal{I}}D_{\min}(\rho_{A}\|\delta).

(44)

Remark 5
.

The CPTP map
\Lambda(\cdot)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}M_{\alpha\beta}(\cdot)M_{\alpha\beta}^{\dagger}

we constructed in the proof is not only IO but also DIO. To see this, first expand

\displaystyle\Lambda(|x\rangle\langle y|)=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}Z^{\alpha}|G(x)\rangle\langle G(y)|Z^{\alpha\dagger}\langle\widetilde{\alpha}\beta|V|x\rangle\langle y|V^{\dagger}|\widetilde{\alpha}\beta\rangle

for any incoherent basis states
|x\rangle
and
|y\rangle
of system
A
. The key observation is that fully dephased states are invariant under conjugation by
Z^{\alpha}
, and moreover this conjugation commutes with
\Delta
, i.e.
\Delta[Z^{\alpha}(\cdot)Z^{\alpha\dagger}]=\Delta(\cdot)
. Hence if we dephase system
K
after applying the map
\Lambda
, we find

\displaystyle\Delta[\Lambda(|x\rangle\langle y|)]

\displaystyle=\delta_{G(x)G(y)}|G(x)\rangle\langle G(y)|

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\langle\widetilde{\alpha}\beta|V|x\rangle\langle y|V^{\dagger}|\widetilde{\alpha}\beta\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta_{xy}|G(x)\rangle\langle G(y)|=\Lambda[\Delta(|x\rangle\langle y|)],

(45)

where the second equality follows from the fact that
\{|\widetilde{\alpha}\beta\rangle\}_{\alpha,\beta}
forms a complete basis.
Thus,
\Lambda
is a DIO map.

Corollary 6
.

For an arbitrary state
\rho
and
0<\varepsilon<1
,

C^{\varepsilon}_{d,DIIO}(\rho)\geq C^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\eta}_{\min}(\rho)-2\log\frac{1}{\eta},

(46)

for any
0<\eta<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
,
where DIIO refers to the intersection of IO and DIO.

\blacksquare

IV-B

Upper bound and comparison with MIO distillation

We have a partial converse theorem to Theorem
4
which
can bound
C^{\varepsilon}_{d,IO}(\rho)
from both sides, as follows.

Theorem 7
.

For an arbitrary state
\rho
and
0<\varepsilon<1
,

C^{\varepsilon}_{d,IO}(\rho)\leq C_{\min}^{\sqrt{\varepsilon(2-\varepsilon)}}(\rho).

(47)

Proof.

Due to the inclusion of the classes of operations, and
Theorems
3
and
4
, we have the first
four of the following (in)equalities:

\begin{split}C_{\min}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\eta}(\rho)-2\log\frac{1}{\eta}&\leq C_{d,\text{IO}}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)\\
&\leq C_{d,\text{M/D\,IO}}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)=\widetilde{C}_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)\\
&\leq C_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)\\
&\leq C_{\min}^{\sqrt{\varepsilon(2-\varepsilon)}}(\rho).\end{split}

(48)

The last one also follows essentially from known facts, namely
[
32
, Prop. 4.2]
.
Note only that our definition of
D_{H}^{\varepsilon}
differs from

[
32
]
by
\varepsilon\leftrightarrow 1-\varepsilon
, and
an additional term of
\log(1-\varepsilon)
added. We made this choice for
easier comparison with the results from
[
25
]
.
With this in mind,
[
32
, Eq. (51)]
reads

D_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq D_{\min}^{\sqrt{2\varepsilon}}(\rho\|\sigma),

and looking at the last step of the proof, one observes
that
\sqrt{2\varepsilon}
can be improved to
\sqrt{\varepsilon(2-\varepsilon)}
.
We can adapt the proof in
[
32
]
to include the
minimization over
\delta\in\mathcal{I}
, according to the following Lemma
9
. By applying it with
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{I}
, and
maximizing over the
\sqrt{\varepsilon(2-\varepsilon)}
-ball on the
right hand side, we precisely obtain the last inequality in
Eq.
48
.

\blacksquare

Remark 8
.

Combining Theorem
4
and Theorem
7
, we conclude that
C_{d,IO}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)\approx C_{\min}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}(\rho)
, with
\varepsilon^{\prime}\in[\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\sqrt{(2-\varepsilon)}]
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a closed convex set of states on a Hilbert space
A
,
and
\rho
a state. Then, for every
0<\varepsilon<1
there exists a
subnormalized density matrix
\rho^{\prime}
with

P(\rho,\rho^{\prime})\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon(2-\varepsilon)}
, such that

\min_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}}D_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\min_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}}D_{\min}^{\sqrt{\varepsilon(2-\varepsilon)}}(\rho^{\prime}\|\sigma).

Proof.

The crucial observation is that due to the convexity of the sets of
operators,
\mathcal{S}
and
\{0\leq W\leq\mathbbm{1}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,\operatorname{Tr}\rho W\geq 1-\varepsilon\}
,
we can invoke the minimax theorem
[
33
]
, to obtain

\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}}\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq W\leq\mathbbm{1}\\
\operatorname{Tr}\rho W\geq 1-\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\operatorname{Tr}\sigma W=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq W\leq\mathbbm{1}\\
\operatorname{Tr}\rho W\geq 1-\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{Tr}\sigma W.

Thus, there exists an optimizer
W_{0}
of the second expression,

0\leq W_{0}\leq\mathbbm{1}
,
\operatorname{Tr}\rho W_{0}\geq 1-\varepsilon
, with

\min_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}}D_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho\|\sigma)=\min_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}}-\log\operatorname{Tr}\sigma W_{0}.

(49)

Following the the example of
[
32
, Prop. 4.2]
,
we define a subnormalized state
\rho^{\prime}=\sqrt{W_{0}}\rho\sqrt{W_{0}}
,
which we claim to be the sought-after object.

To start with, from optimality of
W_{0}
, we have
\operatorname{Tr}\rho^{\prime}=\operatorname{Tr}\rho W_{0}=1-\varepsilon
,
hence from
[
32
, Lemma A.3]
(see also
[
38
, Lemma 7]
),

P(\rho^{\prime},\rho)\leq\sqrt{1-(\operatorname{Tr}\rho W_{0})^{2}}=\sqrt{\varepsilon(2-\varepsilon)}
.

At the same time, choosing a purification of
\rho^{A}=\operatorname{Tr}_{B}|\varphi\rangle\langle\varphi|^{AB}
,
we get a purification of
\rho^{\prime}
by letting

|\varphi^{\prime}\rangle=(\sqrt{W_{0}}\otimes\mathbbm{1})|\varphi\rangle
. Conjugating the
inequality
\varphi\leq\mathbbm{1}
by
\sqrt{W_{0}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}
this results in

\varphi^{\prime}\leq W_{0}\otimes\mathbbm{1}
. Now, just as in the proof of
[
32
, Prop. 4.2]
,
we employ the dual variational characterization of the fidelity,

F(\rho^{\prime},\sigma)^{2}=\min\operatorname{Tr}\sigma Z\text{ s.t. }\varphi^{\prime}\leq Z\otimes\mathbbm{1}.

This implies, that
\operatorname{Tr}\sigma W_{0}\geq F(\rho^{\prime},\sigma)
in (
49
),
for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}
, and so

\min_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}}D_{H}^{\varepsilon}(\rho\|\sigma)\leq\min_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}}-\log F(\rho^{\prime},\sigma)^{2}
, as claimed.

\blacksquare

V
Distillation under SIO

V-A

Characterizing one-shot SIO distillation

We now turn to coherence distillation under strictly incoherent operations
(SIO). Ever since
[
17
]
, it has been an open question whether
coherence distillation such as the protocol in Sec.
IV-A
,
or in
[
17
, Thm. 6]
, really requires IO, or can be performed
within the smaller class of SIO (as all other protocols discussed in
[
17
]

can).
The crucial object in this setting turns out to be the incoherent rank.
Recall that the incoherent rank of a positive operator
\Omega
is defined by

C_{0}(\Omega)=\min_{\{\lambda_{j},|\phi_{j}\rangle\}}\max_{j}\log\text{rank}[\Delta(\phi_{j})],

(50)

where the minimization is taken over all positive rank-one decompositions of
\Omega
.

Theorem 10
.

For any state
\rho
, the one-shot distillable coherence under SIO is given by

\displaystyle C_{d,SIO}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)=\max\log M\text{ s.t. }\operatorname{Tr}\rho A

\displaystyle\geq 1-\varepsilon,

\displaystyle 0\leq A

\displaystyle\leq\mathbbm{1},\ A_{ii}=\frac{1}{M}\,\forall i

\displaystyle C_{0}(A)

\displaystyle\leq\log M.

(51)

Proof.

Suppose that
\operatorname{Tr}[\Lambda(\rho)\Psi_{M}]\geq 1-\varepsilon
for some SIO map

\Lambda
and
|\Psi_{M}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}\sum_{i=1}^{M}|i\rangle
.
Let
\Pi_{>M}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{x=M+1}^{d_{A}}|x\rangle\langle x|
. Notice that

\displaystyle 1-\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Tr}[\Lambda(\rho)\Psi_{M}]

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{Tr}[\Lambda(\rho)(\Psi_{M}+\Pi_{>M})]

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}[\rho A],

(52)

where
A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Lambda^{*}(\Psi_{M})+\Lambda^{*}(\Pi_{>M})
and
\Lambda^{*}
is the adjoint channel of
\Lambda
. Using the form of SIO Kraus operators, we have

\displaystyle\Lambda^{*}(\Psi_{M})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\;s.t.\\
f_{\alpha}(x)\in[M]\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{\prime}\;s.t.\\
f_{\alpha}(x^{\prime})\in[M]\end{subarray}}c_{\alpha,x}^{*}|x\rangle\langle x^{\prime}|c_{\alpha,x^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{\alpha}|\phi_{\alpha}\rangle\langle\phi_{\alpha}|,

(53)

and likewise

\Lambda^{*}(\Pi_{>M})=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\;s.t.\\
f_{\alpha}(x)\not\in[M]\end{subarray}}|c_{\alpha,x}|^{2}|x\rangle\langle x|.

(54)

Thus
A
has a decomposition into rank-one vectors each having an incoherent rank no greater than
M
. Also, for any
y\in\{1,\cdots,d_{A}\}
, we see that

\displaystyle\langle y|A|y\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\;s.t.\\
f_{\alpha}(y)\in[M]\end{subarray}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\;s.t.\\
f_{\alpha}(y)\not\in[M]\end{subarray}}\right)|c_{\alpha,y}|^{2}=\frac{1}{M},

(55)

i.e.
A_{yy}=\frac{1}{M}
for all
y
.

The converse involves essentially reversing these steps. Suppose that

\operatorname{Tr}\rho A\geq 1-\varepsilon
for some operator
0\leq A\leq\mathbbm{1}
with

C_{0}(A)\leq\log M
and
A_{ii}=\frac{1}{M}
.
Then there exists a decomposition

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{\alpha}|\phi_{\alpha}\rangle\langle\phi_{\alpha}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{x,x^{\prime}=1}^{d_{A}}c_{\alpha,x}c^{*}_{\alpha,x^{\prime}}|x\rangle\langle x^{\prime}|,

(56)

where
(c_{\alpha,x})_{x}
contains at most
M
nonzero elements for each
\alpha
. Hence we can define permutations
f_{\alpha}
on the set
\{1,\cdots,|A|\}
such that

f_{\alpha}(x)\in[M]
for every
x
and
\alpha
with
c_{\alpha,x}\not=0
.
The Kraus operators
K_{\alpha}=\sum_{x=1}^{d_{A}}c^{*}_{\alpha,x}|f_{\alpha}(x)\rangle\langle x|
satisfy

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}K_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\Psi_{M}K_{\alpha}=\Omega.

(57)

Furthermore,

\sum_{\alpha}K^{\dagger}_{\alpha}K_{\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{x=1}^{d_{A}}|c_{\alpha,x}|^{2}|x\rangle\langle x|=\mathbbm{1}
,
since by assumption

\frac{1}{M}=\langle x|A|x\rangle=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{\alpha}|c_{\alpha,x}|^{2}.

Therefore, the
\{K_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha}
define a CPTP SIO map

\Lambda
satisfying
\operatorname{Tr}\Lambda(\rho)\Psi_{M}\geq 1-\varepsilon
.

\blacksquare

V-B

Bound coherence exists under SIO

The constraint on the incoherent rank of
A
in Theorem
10

greatly diminishes the power of SIO to distill coherence.
Here we illustrate this effect by a dramatic example. Consider the state

\rho=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(|+\rangle\langle+|\otimes|+\rangle\langle+|+|-\rangle\langle-|\otimes|\widetilde{+}\rangle\langle\widetilde{+}|\bigr),

(58)

where

\displaystyle|+\rangle|+\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(|00\rangle+|01\rangle+|10\rangle+|11\rangle)

\displaystyle|-\rangle|\widetilde{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(|00\rangle+i|01\rangle-|10\rangle-i|11\rangle).

The
n
-copy state
\rho^{\otimes n}
is then an equal mixture of states belonging
to the ensemble

\mathfrak{E}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{|+\rangle|+\rangle,|-\rangle|\widetilde{+}\rangle\}^{\otimes n}.

We will show that not a single cosbit of coherence can be distilled
from
\rho^{\otimes n}
by SIO with an error smaller than the minimal
one-copy error. In comparison,
n
bits of coherence can be distilled by IO error-free: the first system in each copy of
\rho
is simply measured
with the IO Kraus operations
\{|0\rangle\langle+|,|1\rangle\langle-|\}
followed by a suitable
controlled phase on the second qubit.
Such a measurement is not possible by SIO.

Theorem 13
.

For the state
\rho
defined in Eq. (
58
),
C_{d,SIO}^{\infty}(\rho)=0
.

The proof of this will follow by studying the structure of
\rho^{\otimes n}
and showing that for a fixed value of
\epsilon
(independent of
n
),
\operatorname{Tr}\rho^{\otimes n}A<1-\epsilon
for any operator
A
having an incoherent rank of two and satisfying the conditions of Theorem
10
. The key property we use is that the eigenvectors of
\rho^{\otimes n}
will always be maximally coherent states with complex phases belonging to
\{0,\frac{\pi}{2},\pi,\frac{3\pi}{2}\}
. For the
n
-copy analysis to be tractable, we need to introduce some new notation. Let
\mathbf{b}_{j}=(b_{j,0},b_{j,1},\cdots,b_{j,n-1})\in\{0,1\}^{n}
denote the
j^{\text{th}}
binary sequence of length
n
. We then define an ensemble of
2^{n}
equiprobable states
\{|\mathbf{b}_{j}\rangle\}_{j=1}^{2^{n}}
, where

\displaystyle|\mathbf{b}_{j}\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{4^{n}}}\sum_{m_{0},\cdots,m_{n-1}=0}^{3}\exp\left(i\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}b_{j,k}m_{k}\right)\bigg|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}4^{k}m_{k}\bigg\rangle.

(59)

We claim that, up to relabelling, this ensemble is precisely
\mathfrak{E}_{n}
. For example, when
n=1
we have

\displaystyle|\mathbf{b}_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(|0\rangle+|1\rangle+|2\rangle+|3\rangle\right)

\displaystyle|\mathbf{b}_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(|0\rangle+i|1\rangle-|2\rangle-i|3\rangle\right),

and for
n=2
we have

\displaystyle|\mathbf{b}_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(|0\rangle+|1\rangle+|2\rangle+|3\rangle+|4\rangle

\displaystyle+|5\rangle+|6\rangle+\cdots+|14\rangle+|15\rangle)

\displaystyle|\mathbf{b}_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(|0\rangle+i|1\rangle-|2\rangle-i|3\rangle+|4\rangle

\displaystyle+i|5\rangle-|6\rangle-\cdots-|14\rangle-i|15\rangle)

\displaystyle|\mathbf{b}_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(|0\rangle+|1\rangle+|2\rangle+|3\rangle+i|4\rangle

\displaystyle+i|5\rangle+i|6\rangle+\cdots-i|14\rangle-i|15\rangle)

\displaystyle|\mathbf{b}_{4}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}(|0\rangle+i|1\rangle-|2\rangle-i|3\rangle+i|4\rangle

\displaystyle-|5\rangle-i|6\rangle+\cdots+i|14\rangle-|15\rangle).

The case of general
n
can be checked by induction.

Now for any two distinct vectors
\vec{m}=(m_{0},\cdots,m_{n-1})
and
\vec{m}^{\prime}=(m_{0}^{\prime},\cdots,m_{n-1}^{\prime})
belonging to
\{0,1,2,3\}^{n}
, let us denote the kets

\displaystyle|\vec{m}\rangle

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigg|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}4^{k}m_{k}\bigg\rangle,

\displaystyle|\vec{m}^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigg|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}4^{k}m^{\prime}_{k}\bigg\rangle.

(60)

The relative phase between
|\vec{m}\rangle
and
|\vec{m}^{\prime}\rangle
in any
|\mathbf{b}_{j}\rangle
is defined to be

\frac{\pi}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}b_{j,k}(m_{k}^{\prime}-m_{k})\in\left\{0,\frac{\pi}{2},\pi,\frac{3\pi}{2}\right\}.

(61)

We now make a crucial observation about the distribution of relative phases among the
|\mathbf{b}_{j}\rangle
in
\mathfrak{E}_{n}
.

Proposition 14
.

For any fixed pair of distinct vectors
\vec{m}
and
\vec{m}^{\prime}
, at most half of the
|\mathbf{b}_{i}\rangle
in
\mathfrak{E}_{n}
have the same relative phase between
|\vec{m}\rangle
and
|\vec{m}^{\prime}\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
k\in\{0,\cdots,n-1\}
be chosen such that
m_{k}^{\prime}-m_{k}\not=0
. Let
\Delta m=m_{k}^{\prime}-m_{k}
. Consider all the states
|\mathbf{b}_{j}\rangle\in\mathfrak{E}_{n}
with binary sequence
\mathbf{b}_{j}
such that
b_{j,k}=0
; this represents exactly half of all states in the ensemble
\mathfrak{E}_{n}
. We partition these states into four groups,
A_{0}
,
A_{\pi/2}
,
A_{\pi}
, and
A_{3\pi/2}
, according to their respective relative phases between
|\vec{m}\rangle
and
|\vec{m}^{\prime}\rangle
. Now we consider the other half of the states in
\mathfrak{E}_{n}
, those having
b_{j,k}=1
. We likewise partition these states into sets
B_{0}
,
B_{\pi/2}
,
B_{\pi}
, and
B_{3\pi/2}
of common relative phase between
|\vec{m}\rangle
and
|\vec{m}^{\prime}\rangle
. Notice that for any
|\mathbf{b}_{j}\rangle
in, say,
A_{\pi/2}
, there will be a corresponding
|\mathbf{b}_{j^{\prime}}\rangle
in
B_{\pi/2(1+\Delta m})
and vice versa, the only difference between
\mathbf{b}_{j}
and
\mathbf{b}_{j^{\prime}}
being their
k^{th}
component. Hence
|A_{0}|=|B_{\pi/2\Delta m}|
,
|A_{\pi/2}|=|B_{\pi/2(1+\Delta m)}|
,
|A_{\pi}|=|B_{\pi/2(2+\Delta m)}|
and
|A_{3\pi/2}|=|B_{\pi/2(3+\Delta m)}|
, where all arithmetic is done modular
4
. Therefore, the total number of states in the ensemble having a relative phase of, say,
\pi/2
is

\displaystyle|A_{\pi/2}|+|B_{\pi/2}|

\displaystyle=|A_{\pi/2}|+|A_{\pi/2(1-\Delta m)}|\leq 2^{n-1}.

The same bound likewise holds for the other three relative phases.

\blacksquare

Proof of Theorem
13
.

Consider an arbitrary vector in the complex linear span of
|\vec{m}\rangle
,
|\vec{m}^{\prime}\rangle
,

|\psi\rangle=\cos\theta|\vec{m}\rangle+\sin\theta e^{i\phi}|\vec{m}^{\prime}\rangle.

(62)

Let
N_{0}
,
N_{\pi/2}
,
N_{\pi}
,
N_{3\pi/2}
denote the number of states in
\mathfrak{E}_{n}
having a relative phase between
|\vec{m}\rangle
and
|\vec{m}^{\prime}\rangle
of
0
,
\pi/2
,
\pi
, and
3\pi/2
, respectively. We can then explicitly compute

\displaystyle\langle\psi|\rho^{\otimes n}|\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{2^{n}4^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{2^{n}}|\langle\psi|\mathbf{b}_{i}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{N_{0}}{2^{n}4^{n}}|\cos\theta\!+\!e^{-i\phi}\sin\theta|^{2}+\frac{N_{\pi/2}}{2^{n}4^{n}}|\cos\theta\!+\!ie^{-i\phi}\sin\theta|^{2}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\frac{N_{\pi}}{2^{n}4^{n}}|\cos\theta\!-\!e^{-i\phi}\sin\theta|^{2}+\frac{N_{3\pi/2}}{2^{n}4^{n}}|\cos\theta\!-\!ie^{-i\phi}\sin\theta|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4^{n}}+\frac{\sin 2\theta}{2^{n}4^{n}}\left[(N_{0}\!-\!N_{\pi})\cos\phi+(N_{3\pi/2}\!-\!N_{\pi/2})\sin\phi\right],

(63)

where the last line follows by expanding out the squared amplitudes, the identity

2\cos\theta\sin\theta=\sin 2\theta
, and using the
fact that
N_{0}+N_{\pi/2}+N_{\pi}+N_{3\pi/2}=2^{n}
. Our goal is to
maximize (
63
) under the constraint that

\max\{N_{0},N_{\pi/2},N_{\pi},N_{3\pi/2}\}\leq 2^{n-1}
.
This constraint implies that
|N_{0}-N_{\pi}|\leq 2^{n-1}
and
|N_{3\pi/2}-N_{\pi/2}|\leq 2^{n-1}
,
and so

\displaystyle\langle\psi|\rho^{\otimes n}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4^{n}}\left(1+\cos\theta\sin\theta(|\cos\phi|+|\sin\phi|)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4^{n}}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right).

(64)

Suppose now that
A
has an incoherent rank of two and satisfies
\operatorname{Tr}[A]=\frac{4^{n}}{2}
. Then by the previous calculation we have the fidelity bound

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\rho^{\otimes n}A\leq(\operatorname{Tr}A)\frac{1}{4^{n}}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\varepsilon,

(65)

where
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}
is independent of
n
.
This is precisely the single-copy error bound.
As a consequence, it follows that
C_{d,SIO}^{\infty}(\rho)=0
,
proving the theorem.

\blacksquare

Remark 15
.

This result should be compared with the recent proof,
by Marvian
[
40
]
, that coherence distillation is
generally impossible in the resource theory of energy conservation,
which is characterized by the class of so-called time-translation-covariant
operations (TIO). That class is difficult to compare with DIO, as
at the single-system level, TIO is contained in DIO, but since the
composition of systems works differently, it may result in TIO operations
outside DIO on the multi-system level.

The result of
[
40
]
shows that for generic mixed states,
the rate of distilling cosbit states
|\Psi_{2}\rangle
is zero, but
that at the same time it is possible to obtain a single cosbit (or a sublinear
number) with fidelity going to
1
as asymptotically many copies of the
mixed resource become available.
In contrast, here we showed that under DIO the fidelity remains bounded
away from
1
, irrespective of the number of resource states.

VI
Recovering the information theoretic limit

In the asymptotic limit, the coherence distillation rate under operation class

\mathcal{O}
is defined as

C_{d,\mathcal{O}}^{\infty}(\rho)=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}C_{d,\mathcal{O}}^{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\otimes n}).

(66)

From
[
17
]
and
[
25
]
(see also
[
26
]
) we know that

C_{d,IO}^{\infty}(\rho)=C_{d,DIO}^{\infty}(\rho)=C_{d,MIO}^{\infty}(\rho)=C_{r}(\rho)
.
Below we show that our results on one-shot IO distillation can be used
to recover the asymptotic limit, at the same time improving the result by showing
that the limit exists and equals
C_{r}(\rho)
for any fixed
0<\varepsilon<1
;
such a statement is known as a
strong converse
in information theory.

Theorem 16
.

For any state
\rho
and any
0<\varepsilon<1
,

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}C_{d,IO}^{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\otimes n})&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}C_{d,DIO}^{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\otimes n})\\
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}C_{d,MIO}^{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\otimes n})=C_{r}(\rho).\end{split}

Proof.

Recall the results in Theorems
4
and

7
, which state that for any
\eta<\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon
,

\begin{split}C_{\min}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\eta}(\rho)-2\log\frac{1}{\eta}&\leq C_{d,IO}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)\\
&\leq C_{d,MIO}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)\\
&=C_{d,DIO}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)\leq C_{\min}^{\sqrt{\varepsilon(2-\varepsilon)}}(\rho).\end{split}

(67)

Hence, to show the theorem, we only need to prove that for all
0<\delta<1
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}C_{\min}^{\delta}(\rho^{\otimes n})=C_{r}(\rho),

(68)

which is equivalent to

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\max_{\rho^{\prime}\in B_{\delta}({\rho^{A}}^{\otimes n})}H_{\min}(X^{n}|E^{n})_{\omega^{\prime}}=C_{r}(\rho).

(69)

Here,
\omega_{X^{n}E^{n}}^{\prime}=(\Delta_{A^{n}}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{E^{n}})|\psi^{\prime}\rangle\langle\psi^{\prime}|

and
|\psi^{\prime}\rangle
is an arbitrary purification of
\rho_{A}^{\prime}
.
Recall the quantum asymptotic equipartition theorem
[
41
]
,
which states that for any
0<\eta<1
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\max_{\rho_{AB}^{\prime}\in B_{\eta}(\rho_{AB}^{\otimes n})}H_{\min}(A|B)_{\rho_{AB}^{\prime}}=H(A|B)_{\rho_{AB}}.

(70)

Now, in one direction, if we have a state
\rho^{\prime}\in B_{\delta}(\rho^{\otimes n})
,
then by Uhlmann’s characterization of the fidelity
there exists a purification
\psi^{\prime}\in B_{\delta}(\psi^{\otimes n})
,
hence
\omega^{\prime}=(\Delta_{A^{n}}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{E^{n}})\psi^{\prime}\in B_{\delta}(\omega^{\otimes n})
,
with
\omega^{XE}=(\Delta_{A}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{E})\psi
.
In the other direction, for
\omega^{\prime\prime}\in B_{\delta}(\omega^{\otimes n})
,
it is known that since
\omega
is a cq-state, an optimal

\omega^{\prime\prime}
for
H_{\min}(X^{n}|E^{n})

may be assumed to be a cq-state as well
[
35
]
,
hence we can find a
\psi^{\prime}\in B_{\delta}(\psi^{\otimes n})

such that
\omega^{\prime\prime}=(\Delta_{A^{n}}\otimes{\operatorname{id}}_{E^{n}})\psi^{\prime}
.
Thus, we can conclude that

\max_{\rho_{A}^{\prime}\in B_{\delta}({\rho^{A}}^{\otimes n})}H_{\min}(X^{n}|E^{n})_{\omega^{\prime}}=\max_{\omega^{\prime\prime}\in B_{\delta}(\omega^{\otimes n})}H_{\min}(X^{n}|E^{n})_{\omega^{\prime\prime}},

where the left hand side corresponds to Eq. (
69
).

But this means that we can apply the quantum AEP directly, and get

\begin{split}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}C_{\min}^{\delta}(\rho^{\otimes n})&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\max_{\omega^{\prime}\in B_{\delta}({\omega}^{\otimes n})}H_{\min}(X^{n}|E^{n})_{\omega^{\prime}}\\
&=H(X|E)_{\omega}=C_{r}(\rho),\end{split}

as claimed.

\blacksquare

VII
Coherence distillation

and randomness generation

Suppose a purification of
\rho^{A}
is written as

|\psi\rangle^{AE}=\sum_{x}\sqrt{p_{x}}|x\rangle^{A}|\psi_{x}\rangle^{E},

we use this state to generate randomness by first performing a
computational basis measurement.
The dephased cq-state after measuring
A
in the computational basis is

\omega^{AE}=(\Delta\otimes{\operatorname{id}})\psi=\sum_{x}p_{x}|x\rangle\langle x|^{A}\otimes\psi_{x}^{E}.

Considering the measurement as a raw randomness generation process,
a subsequent randomness extraction (via a deterministic function
G
)
can further extract a random string that is almost uniform and independent of
E
.
We think of the function as an incoherent operation,
by letting
G(|x\rangle\langle y|)=\delta_{xy}|G(x)\rangle\langle G(x)|
. This identification
is natural as every incoherent (MIO) operation
\Lambda
defines an
associated classical channel via
\Lambda(|x\rangle\langle x|)=\sum_{y}\Lambda(y|x)|y\rangle\langle y|
.

Denote
\ell^{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{ext}}
to be the maximum length of the extractable randomness
that is
\varepsilon
-close to a string that it is perfectly uniform and independent of
E
, i.e.

\ell^{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{ext}}(\rho_{A})=\max_{G}\left\{\log M\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\frac{1}{2}\left\|(G\!\otimes\!{\operatorname{id}})\omega^{AE}\!-\!\tau^{K}\!\otimes\!\omega^{E}\right\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon\right\}\!,

(71)

where we recall the notation
\tau^{K}=\frac{1}{M}\mathbbm{1}_{K}
for the maximally
mixed state of the
M
-dimensional key system.

Note that our definition of extractable randomness
differs somewhat from the one in
[
42
]
; in that work, a
model based on incoherent operations was proposed, which
is shown in Fig.
1
(a).
The main process consists of three parts, incoherent operations
\Lambda
, dephasing operation
\Delta
, a random hashing function as the extractor
\mathrm{Ext}
. Here our definition is more straightforward and we do not need to perform the real incoherent operations. As shown in Fig.
1
(b), after the dephasing operation
\Delta
, we use a function
G
as an extractor to extract the secure randomness. This function has to be deterministic, as opposed to a noisy channels, since otherwise infinite randomness can be generated independent of
E
.

Moreover, in order to obtain the optimal
G
in our definition, we first consider the randomness extraction process via DIO which is shown in Fig.
1
(c), where we apply the DIO distillation followed by dephasing operation
\Delta
, a classical extractor (which may not be needed). Benefiting from the property of DIO, we can change the order of DIO and
\Delta
, which implies that the DIO distillation may act as a good extractor (the blue part in Fig.
1
(d)). The only remaining problem here is that we would have to show that this DIO operation gives rise to a deterministic classical channel, which is in general not true.
For instance, the optimal coherence distillation process under DIO derived in Theorem
3

has the property that
\Lambda(|x\rangle\langle x|)=\tau_{K}
for all
x
. Via the permutation twirling Eq. (
25
), this can be imposed equally on any optimal IO distillation process, and hence
even on DIIO = IO
\cap
DIO.

Instead, inspired by Remark
5
, we know that a suboptimal but achievable IO distillation operation is also a DIO operation and after a modification (another dephasing channel) we can construct a valid extractor
G
from it, which is shown in the proof of Theorem
17
.

Fig. 1:
The different schemes for extracting randomness. (a) Extracting randomness using incoherent strategy in
[
42
]
. Alice is allowed to perform the unitary IO operation on her system together with another ancilla system prepared in
|0\rangle\langle 0|
.
(b) The randomness extraction process defined in our protocol. The extractor is implemented by a deterministic function
G
(c) and (d) Applying DIO distillation for extracting secure randomness. Due to the property of DIO, we can achieve a new operation (blue part) combining the DIO distillation operation and original extractor.

We first recall that every valid
G
can be used for IO distillation.
Here, we consider
G
as a deterministic function, and

\omega^{E}=\psi^{E}
is the reduced state of
\omega^{AE}
on system E.
For every function
G
satisfying

\frac{1}{2}\left\|(G\otimes{\operatorname{id}})\omega^{AE}-\tau^{K}\otimes\omega^{E}\right\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon
,
we can substitute it in the proof of
Theorem
4
and obtain an incoherent distillation channel.
Thus as the maximal distillable rate
C^{\varepsilon}_{d,IO}(\rho)
, it satisfies

C^{2\varepsilon}_{d,IO}(\rho)\geq\ell^{\varepsilon}_{\textrm{ext}}(\rho).

(72)

The IO map achieving
C^{2\varepsilon}_{d,IO}(\rho)
can also be applied to
extract randomness by the following theorem.

Theorem 17
.

For an arbitrary state
\rho
and
0<\varepsilon<1
,

\ell^{\sqrt{2\varepsilon}}_{\textrm{ext}}(\rho)\geq C^{(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\eta)^{2}/2}_{d,IO}(\rho)-2\log\frac{1}{\eta},

(73)

for any
0<\eta<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
.

Proof.

Recall that the distillable coherence under IO is given by

C_{d,IO}^{\varepsilon}(\rho)=\max_{\Lambda\in\mathcal{IO}}\bigl\{\log M\,\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\,F(\Lambda(\rho),\Psi_{M})^{2}\geq 1-\varepsilon\bigr\}.

Suppose
\Lambda
is the IO that achieves the right hand side of Theorem
4
,
that is
C^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\eta}_{\min}(\rho)-2\log\frac{1}{\eta}=\log M
where
0<\eta<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
and

F(\Lambda(\rho),\Psi_{M})^{2}\geq 1-\varepsilon
.
Note that
\Lambda
is not necessarily an optimal IO coherence distillation operation.
For the purification state
|\psi\rangle^{AE}
, the resulting state
by applying
\Lambda
on system
A
is given by

\Omega^{AE}=(\Lambda\otimes{\operatorname{id}})(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|^{AE}),

and it follows that

F(\Omega^{AE},\Psi_{M}\otimes\Omega^{E})^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{2}.

To prove that, suppose a purification of
\Omega^{AE}
is
|\Omega\rangle^{AA^{\prime}E}
.
Then, considering an orthogonal basis
\{|\phi_{x}\rangle^{A}\}
of system
A

such that
|\phi_{0}\rangle^{A}=|\Psi_{M}\rangle
, we can write

|\Omega\rangle^{AA^{\prime}E}=\sum_{x}\alpha_{x}|\phi_{x}\rangle^{A}|\psi_{x}\rangle^{A^{\prime}E}.

As
F(\Lambda(\rho),\Psi_{M})^{2}\geq 1-\varepsilon
and

\Lambda(\rho)=\sum_{xx^{\prime}}\alpha_{x}\alpha_{x}^{\prime}\langle\psi_{x^{\prime}}|\psi_{x}\rangle^{A^{\prime}E}|\phi_{x}\rangle\langle\phi_{x^{\prime}}|^{A},

we have

F(\Lambda(\rho),\Psi_{M})^{2}=|\alpha_{0}|^{2}\geq 1-\varepsilon
.
The fidelity between
\Omega^{AA^{\prime}E}
and
\Psi_{M}^{A}\otimes\psi_{0}^{A^{\prime}E}
is

F({\Omega}^{AA^{\prime}E},\Psi_{M}^{A}\otimes{\psi_{0}}^{A^{\prime}E})^{2}=|\alpha_{0}|^{2}\geq 1-\varepsilon.

Denoting
\Omega^{A^{\prime}E}=\operatorname{Tr}_{A}{\Omega}^{AA^{\prime}E}
, then the
fidelity between
{\Omega}^{AA^{\prime}E}
and
\Psi_{M}^{A}\otimes\Omega^{A^{\prime}E}
is

F({\Omega}^{AA^{\prime}E},\Psi_{M}^{A}\otimes\Omega^{A^{\prime}E})^{2}=|\alpha_{0}|^{4}\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{2}.

Then
F(\Omega^{AE},\Psi_{M}\otimes\Omega^{E})^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{2}
can be obtained by tracing out system
A^{\prime}
.

Applying the dephasing operation
\Delta
on system
A
,

F\left((\Delta\otimes{\operatorname{id}})\Omega^{AE},\tau^{K}\otimes\Omega^{E}\right)^{2}\geq F(\Omega^{AE},\Psi_{M}\otimes\Omega^{E})^{2}.

From the Remark
5
, we know that
\Lambda
is also a DIO which
commutes with
\Delta
, so we have equivalently

\displaystyle F\left((\Lambda\otimes{\operatorname{id}})\omega^{AE},\tau^{K}\otimes\Omega^{E}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq F(\Omega^{AE},\Psi_{M}\otimes\Omega^{E})^{2}

(74)

\displaystyle\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{2}.

In order to construct a deterministic
G
, we apply another dephasing operation
after the incoherent channel
\Lambda
,

\displaystyle F\left((\Delta\circ\Lambda\otimes{\operatorname{id}})\omega^{AE},\tau^{K}\otimes\Omega^{E}\right)^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon)^{2},

(75)

hence

\frac{1}{2}\left\|(\Delta\circ\Lambda\otimes{\operatorname{id}})\omega^{AE}-\tau^{K}\otimes\Omega^{E}\right\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{2\varepsilon}.

Note that from Remark
5
, the map
\Delta\circ\Lambda
acts on the
incoherent basis states
|x\rangle\langle x|
as

\displaystyle\Delta[\Lambda(|x\rangle\langle x|)]=|G(x)\rangle\langle G(x)|,

(76)

which is deterministic. From the achievable distillation IO map, we can construct an extractor and obtain

\ell^{\sqrt{2\varepsilon}}_{\textrm{ext}}(\rho)\geq C^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\eta}_{\min}(\rho)-2\log\frac{1}{\eta}.

(77)

Recall the result in Theorem
7
,

C^{\varepsilon}_{d,IO}(\rho)\leq C_{\min}^{\sqrt{\varepsilon(2-\varepsilon)}}(\rho)\leq C_{\min}^{\sqrt{2\varepsilon}}(\rho),

(78)

and we obtain

\ell^{\sqrt{2\varepsilon}}_{\textrm{ext}}(\rho)\geq C^{(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\eta)^{2}/2}_{d,IO}(\rho)-2\log\frac{1}{\eta},

(79)

finishing the proof.

\blacksquare

Combining with Eq. (
72
), we have

C^{\sqrt{8\varepsilon}}_{d,IO}(\rho)\geq\ell^{\sqrt{2\varepsilon}}_{\textrm{ext}}(\rho)\geq C^{(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}-\eta)^{2}/2}_{d,IO}(\rho)-2\log\frac{1}{\eta}.

(80)

In the regime of vanishingly small
\varepsilon
, the distillable coherence rate

C_{d,IO}(\rho)
and
\ell_{\textrm{ext}}(\rho)
are hence essentially the same. Whether
C_{d,DIO}(\rho)
and
\ell_{\textrm{ext}}(\rho)
are the same is still an open problem. Though DIO can commute with dephasing operation, the difficulty stems from that the combination of DIO and extractor (the blue part in Fig.
1
(d)) may be not deterministic thus not a valid extractor.

VIII
Discussion

We have considered the problem of one-shot coherence distillation under
the classes MIO, DIO, IO, and SIO of incoherent operations.
Our results indicate that the distillation rates under
IO, MIO, DIO are roughly the same,
up to different smoothing parameters and universal additive terms.
The results allow us to recover the asymptotic (many-copy) limit, in
which the distillation rates for all these three classes tend to be
relative entropy of coherence, which is consistent with the previous
results in
[
17
,
25
,
26
]
.

The smallest class for which we have been able to show a non-trivial
distillation of coherence is that of dephasing-covariant incoherent IOs,
DIIO = IO
\cap
DIO.
On the other hand, interestingly, there is a gap between distillation rates
under SIO and DIIO, both in the one-shot, and more importantly in the
asymptotic regime. As a matter of fact, we showed that there is

bound coherence
under SIO; no pure coherence can be distilled under
SIO from these states though they possess coherence, as shown by
distillable coherence under IO.

An interesting future direction is then to study the case involving another
system to help this distillation process, which referred as assisted coherence
distillation for such bound coherence with
SIO
[
43
,
44
,
45
,
46
]
.
Furthermore, our work also connects the distillation of coherence to randomness
extraction. The distillation process is also related to the decoupling in
cryptography. Thus our results also shed light on other quantum information
processing tasks like random number generation, extraction and cryptography.

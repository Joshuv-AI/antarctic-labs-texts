General position sets, colinear sets, and Sierpiński product graphs
Jing Tian, Sandi Klavžar

Abstract
Let G ⊗ f H G\otimes_{f}H denote the Sierpiński product of graphs G G and H H with respect to the function f f . The Sierpiński general position number gp ( G , H ) S {\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(G,H) is introduced as the cardinality of a largest general position set in G ⊗ f H G\otimes_{f}H over all possible functions f f . Similarly, the lower Sierpiński general position number gp ¯ ( G , H ) S \underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(G,H) is the corresponding smallest cardinality. The concept of vertex-colinear sets is introduced. Bounds for the general position number in terms of extremal vertex-colinear sets, and bounds for the (lower) Sierpiński general position number are proved. The extremal graphs are investigated. Formulas for the (lower) Sierpiński general position number of the Sierpiński products with K 2 K_{2} as the first factor are deduced. It is proved that if m , n ≥ 2 m,n\geq 2 , then gp ( K m , K n ) S = m ( n − 1 ) {\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})=m(n-1) and that if n ≥ 2 ​ m − 2 n\geq 2m-2 , then gp ¯ ( K m , K n ) S = m ( n − m + 1 ) \underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})=m(n-m+1) .



Keywords
: general position set; colinear set; Sierpiński product of graphs; Sierpiński general position number

Introduction

General position sets were introduced to graph theory in
[
17
]
, but Chandran S.V. and Parthasarathy in
[
3
]
earlier studied the concept of geodetic irredundant sets which turned out to be an equivalent concept to general position sets. Moreover, much earlier Körner
[
15
]
studied the general position sets of hypercubes (although in a completely different context). Briefly, a general position set is a set of vertices such that no three vertices from the set lie on a common shortest path.

The problem of finding a cardinality of a largest general position set in a graph, that is, the general position number of the graph, is NP-hard
[
17
]
. In
[
1
]
, general position sets of a graph were characterized. Afterwards, the problem of determining the general position number of (classes of) graphs received extensive attention, see
[
8
,
20
,
26
,
28
]
. General position sets were generalized to
d
-position sets
[
11
]
and to Steiner general position sets
[
9
]
. In addition, in
[
4
]
the lower general position was investigated, that is, the cardinality of a smallest maximal general position set in a graph. The edge version of general position sets were investigated in
[
13
,
18
,
24
]
, while for the monophonic version see
[
23
]
.

A lot of attention has been given to the general position sets in Cartesian product graphs, see
[
12
,
14
,
25
]
. In this paper we are interested in general position sets in the recently introduced graph operation called the Sierpiński product. The operation was introduced by Kovič, Pisanski, Zemljič, and Žitnik in
[
16
]
with the idea to generalize the Sierpiński graphs
[
10
]
, cf. Fig.
1
. The investigation of the latter graphs up to 2017 is summarized in the survey
[
7
]
, some of the recent research of Sierpiński graphs can be found, for instance, in
[
19
,
21
,
27
]
, see also the references therein. The Sierpiński product was further investigated in
[
5
,
6
]
, where the (upper) Sierpiński domination number and the (upper) Sierpiński metric dimension were respectively introduced. Following this line of investigation, in this paper we introduce the Sierpiński general position number
{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(G,H)
, and the lower Sierpiński general position number
\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(G,H)
, of the Sierpiński product of graphs
G
and
H
.

In the rest of the paper we proceed as follows. In the next section we give definitions and recall known results that we will use in the paper. In Section
3
we introduce a new concept into the theory of graph general position sets, the vertex-colinear sets. This concept is useful while studying the general position sets in Sierpiński product graphs, but we believe to be also of independent interest. In particular, we prove bounds for the general position number of a graph in terms of extremal vertex-colinear sets and show their usefulness for graphs with bridges. In Section
4
, we present bounds for the (lower) Sierpiński general position number for general graphs and give formulas for these numbers for the Sierpiński product graphs with
K_{2}
as the first factor. In Section
5
, we consider the Sierpiński product of complete graphs
K_{m}\otimes_{f}K_{n}
. We determine their Sierpiński general position number, more precisely, if
m,n\geq 2
, then
{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})=m(n-1)
. For the lower Sierpiński general position number we prove that
\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})=m(n-m+1)
provided that
n\geq 2m-2
. The assumption
n\geq 2m-2
is required as demonstrated by our last result which asserts that
\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{6},K_{9})=25
.

Preliminaries

In this section we define concepts, notation, and results needed, and introduce the (lower) Sierpiński general position number. We begin with basic definitions, and follow by introducing the general position sets and the Sierpiński product graphs.

For a positive integer
k

At the end of the section, we will recall several known results needed later on. For the first one, known as the

[

If

{\rm gp}(G)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}{\rm gp}(H_{i})\,.

Theorem 2.2

[
17
, Theorem 3.6]

If
S
is the set of simplicial vertices of a block graph

, then
{\rm gp}(G)=|S|
.

If

, and

. The following characterization of general position sets will be used either implicitly or explicitly in the rest of the paper.

Theorem 2.3

[

Let

3
Colinear sets

In this section we introduce a new concept into the theory of graph general position sets, the vertex-colinear sets. We believe that the concept is interesting in its own right. Moreover, from our perspective it turns out to be extremely useful for studying the general position sets in Sierpiński product graphs.

Let
G
be a connected graph and
u\in V(G)
. A set
S\subseteq V(G)
is a
u
-colinear set
if
S
is a general position set such that
u\notin S
and
y\notin I_{G}[x,u]
for any
x,y\in S
. Before moving on, we point out that a similar concept named
u
-position set
was independently introduced in
[
22
]
. More precisely, a set
S
is a
u
-colinear set if and only if
S
is both a general position set and a
u
-position set.

We are interested in
u
-colinear sets of largest cardinality and hence set

\xi_{G}(u)=\max\{|S|:\ S\ {\rm is\ a}\ u\mbox{-}{\rm colinear\ set}\}\,.

Further, let

\displaystyle\xi^{-}(G)

\displaystyle=\min\{\xi_{G}(u):\ u\in V(G)\}\,,

\displaystyle\xi(G)

\displaystyle=\max\{\xi_{G}(u):\ u\in V(G)\}\,.

To illustrate these concepts, we state the following easy result which follows from
[
22
, Corollary 19]
. To be self-contained, we include its proof here.

Proposition 3.1

If
T
is a tree with
n(T)\geq 3
and
u\in V(T)
, then
\xi_{T}(u)=\ell(T)-1
if
u
is a leaf, and
\xi_{T}(u)=\ell(T)
, otherwise. In particular,
\xi^{-}(T)=\ell(T)-1
and
\xi(T)=\ell(T)
.

Proof.
Assume first that
u
is a leaf and let
S
be a
u
-colinear set. Let
u^{\prime}
be the support vertex adjacent to
u
. In the following arguments we use the the consequence of Theorem
2.2
asserting that the general position number of a tree is the number of its leaves. If
\deg_{T}(u^{\prime})>2
, then
{\rm gp}(T-u)=\ell(T)-1
, and then the leaves of
T-u
form a largest
u
-colinear set. Assume next that
\deg_{T}(u^{\prime})=2
. Then
T-u
contains
\ell(T)
leaves (one of them being
u^{\prime}
). However, if
u^{\prime}
lies in a
u
-colinear set, then no other vertex lies in such a set. Hence we can again conclude that
\xi_{T}(u)=\ell(T)-1
. Since
n(T)\geq 3
, there exists a vertex
u
which is not a leaf. Then the leaves of
T
form a
u
-colinear set and hence
\xi_{T}(u)=\ell(T)
and so also
\xi(T)=\ell(T)
.

\square

The above invariants fulfil the following chain of inequalities involving the general position number. This in part demonstrates that our definitions are meaningful, further reasons for their introduction will be given later on.

Theorem 3.2

If
G
is a connected graph of order at least
2
and
u\in V(G)
, then

\xi^{-}(G)\leq\xi_{G}(u)\leq\xi(G)\leq{\rm gp}(G)\leq 2\xi^{-}(G)\,.

Proof.
The left three inequalities follow directly from the definition. To prove the last inequality, let
u
be a vertex of
G
such that
\xi_{G}(u)=\xi^{-}(G)
and let
X
be an arbitrary gp-set of
G
. We distinguish two cases.

Assume first that
u\in X
. Since
X
is a general position set of
G
, we infer that
X\setminus\{u\}
is a
u
-colinear set. Then it follows that
\xi_{G}(u)\geq|X|-1
, hence
|X|\leq\xi^{-}(G)+1
. Since the order of
G
is at least two,
\xi^{-}(G)\geq 1
and thus we have
|X|\leq\xi^{-}(G)+\xi^{-}(G)\leq 2\xi^{-}(G)
.

Assume second that
u\notin X
. In this case we partition
X
into two sets
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
as follows. For every
x\in X
, we put each vertex from
(I_{G}[x,u]\setminus\{x\})\cap X
into
X_{2}
. This defines
X_{2}
, and then
X_{1}=X\setminus X_{2}
. We claim that each of
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
is a
u
-colinear set. By the definition, this clearly holds for
X_{1}
. Consider now two vertices
x,x^{\prime}\in X_{2}
and suppose by way of contradiction that there exists a shortest
x,u
-path
P_{x,u}
that contains
x^{\prime}
. Then
d_{G}(x,u)=d_{G}(x,x^{\prime})+d_{G}(x^{\prime},u)
. Since
x\in X_{2}
, there exists a vertex
x^{\prime\prime}\in X
and a shortest
x^{\prime\prime},u
-path
P_{x^{\prime\prime},u}
. We are going to show that
x^{\prime\prime}
,
x
, and
x^{\prime}
lie on a common shortest path. If not, then
d_{G}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime})<d_{G}(x^{\prime\prime},x)+d_{G}(x,x^{\prime})
, but then

\displaystyle d_{G}(x^{\prime\prime},u)

\displaystyle=d_{G}(x^{\prime\prime},x)+d_{G}(x,u)

\displaystyle=d_{G}(x^{\prime\prime},x)+d_{G}(x,x^{\prime})+d_{G}(x^{\prime},u)

\displaystyle>d_{G}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime})+d_{G}(x^{\prime},u)

\displaystyle=d_{G}(x^{\prime\prime},u)\,,

which is not possible. We have thus seen that also

is a

-colinear set. Therefore,

\xi_{G}(u)\geq\max\{|X_{1}|,|X_{2}|\}\geq\frac{1}{2}|X|=\frac{1}{2}{\rm gp}(G)\,,

hence
{\rm gp}(G)\leq 2\xi_{G}(u)=2\xi^{-}(G)
.

\square

4
Sierpiński products of arbitrary graphs

In this section we bound the (lower) Sierpiński general position number for general graphs. Then we give formulas for the (lower) Sierpiński general position number for the Sierpiński product of graphs with
K_{2}
as the first factor. We begin with the following simple, but useful lemma.

Lemma 4.1

Let
X
be a general position set of
K_{2}\otimes_{f}H
, where
V(K_{2})=[2]
and
f(2)=u\in V(H)
. If
|X\cap V(1H)|\geq 2
and
(1,u)\in X
, then
X\cap V(2H)=\emptyset
.

Proof.
Let
(1,v)
be a vertex of
X
different from
(1,u)
. Then every vertex from
2H
, say
(2,x)
, is an endvertex of a shortest
(1,v),(2,x)
-path passing through
(1,u)
, hence
(2,x)\notin X
.

\square

Theorem 4.2

If
G
and
H
are two connected graphs of order at least
2
, then

{\rm gp}(H)\leq\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(G,H)\leq{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(G,H)\leq n(G){\rm gp}(H).

Moreover,
{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(G,H)=n(G){\rm gp}(H)
if and only if
{\rm gp}(H)=\xi(H)
.

Proof.
Let
G\otimes_{f}H
be an arbitrary Sierpiński product of graphs
G
and
H
. By Theorem
2.4
, the graph
G\otimes_{f}H
contains
n(G)
convex subgraphs
gH
,
g\in V(G)
. Since a convex subgraph is an isometric subgraph, these subgraphs form an isometric cover. Therefore, by Theorem
2.1
, we have
{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(G,H)\leq n(G){\rm gp}(H)
, hence the rightmost inequality holds. The convexity of the subgraphs
gH
also implies the left inequality, while the middle inequality is obvious.

Assume now that
{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(G,H)=n(G){\rm gp}(H)
. Consider an arbitrary function
f\in H^{G}
. Using once more the convexity of the subgraph
gH
, there exists a gp-set
X
of
G\otimes_{f}H
, such that
|V(gH)\cap X|={\rm gp}(H)
for each
g\in V(G)
. Consider a fixed subgraph
gH
and let
gg^{\prime}\in E(G)
. Since

|V(gH)\cap X|={\rm gp}(H)
and
{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(G,H)=n(G){\rm gp}(H)
, Lemma
4.1
implies that
(g,f(g^{\prime}))\notin X
. Setting
u=(g,f(g^{\prime}))
we obtain, having in mind Lemma
4.1
again, that
\xi_{gH}(u)={\rm gp}(H)
which in turn implies that
\xi(H)={\rm gp}(H)
.

Assume next that
\xi(H)={\rm gp}(H)
. Let
u
be a vertex of
H
such that
\xi_{H}(u)=\xi(H)
and let
f\in H^{G}
be the constant function
f(g)=u
for any
g\in V(G)
. Each
gH
has at least
\xi(H)
vertices from some gp-set of the graph
G\otimes_{f}H
, hence
{\rm gp}(G\otimes_{f}H)\geq n(G)\xi(H)=n(G){\rm gp}(H)
. Thus we have
{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(G,H)\geq n(G){\rm gp}(H)
.
Since each
gH
is convex in
G\otimes_{f}H
, Theorem
2.1
gives
{\rm gp}(G\otimes_{f}H)\leq n(G){\rm gp}(H)
and then
{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(G,H)\leq n(G){\rm gp}(H)
.
From the above, we conclude that
{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(G,H)=n(G){\rm gp}(H)
.

\square

Corollary 4.3

If
G
is a connected graph with
n(G)\geq 2
and
T
is a tree with
n(T)\geq 3
, then
{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(G,T)=n(G)\ell(T)
.

Proof.
Since
n(T)\geq 3
, Proposition
3.1
gives
{\rm gp}(T)=\ell(T)=\xi(T)
. Hence Theorem
4.2
yields the conclusion.

\square

Theorem 4.4

If
H
is a connected graph with
n(H)\geq 2
, then the following assertions hold.

(i)
\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{2},H)=2\xi^{-}(H)
.

(ii)
{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{2},H)=2\xi(H)
.

Proof.
Set
V(K_{2})=[2]
for the rest of the proof.

(i) Let
f\in H^{K_{2}}
be an arbitrary function, say
f(1)=u
and
f(2)=v
. By Proposition
3.3
we have
{\rm gp}(K_{2}\otimes_{f}H)\geq\xi_{H}(v)+\xi_{H}(u)\geq\xi^{-}(H)+\xi^{-}(H)
. It follows that
\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{2},H)\geq 2\xi^{-}(H)
.

To prove the reverse inequality, let
u
be a vertex of
H
such that
\xi_{H}(u)=\xi^{-}(H)
. Define
f\in H^{K_{2}}
by
f(1)=f(2)=u
and let
X
be a gp-set of
K_{2}\otimes_{f}H
. If
V(1H)\cap X=\emptyset
or
V(2H)\cap X=\emptyset
, then
{\rm gp}(K_{2}\otimes_{f}H)\leq{\rm gp}(H)
holds and then Theorem
3.2
gives
{\rm gp}(K_{2}\otimes_{f}H)\leq 2\xi^{-}(H)
. Assume second that
V(1H)\cap X\neq\emptyset
and
V(2H)\cap X\neq\emptyset
. Then we claim that
|V(1H)\cap X|\leq\xi_{H}(u)
and
|V(2H)\cap X|\leq\xi_{H}(u)
. By establishing this claim,
{\rm gp}(K_{2}\otimes_{f}H)\leq 2\xi^{-}(H)
follows. Suppose on the contrary that this is not the case and assume without loss of generality that
|V(1H)\cap X|\geq\xi_{H}(u)+1
. Then there exist two vertices
(1,h),(1,h^{\prime})\in V(1H)\cap X
, where
h\neq h^{\prime}
, such that the vertices
(1,h)
,
(1,h^{\prime})
, and
(2,u)
lie on a common shortest path. If
(2,h^{\prime\prime})
is an arbitrary vertex from
V(2H)\cap X
, then the vertices
(1,h)
,
(1,h^{\prime})
, and
(2,h^{\prime\prime})
lie on a common shortest path. It is impossible because we have assumed that
V(2H)\cap X\neq\emptyset
. This contradiction proves the claim.

(ii) Let
f\in H^{K_{2}}
be an arbitrary function, say
f(1)=u
and
f(2)=v
and let
X
be an arbitrary gp-set of
K_{2}\otimes_{f}H
.
We claim that
{\rm gp}(K_{2}\otimes_{f}H)\leq 2\xi(H)
. Suppose on the contrary that
{\rm gp}(K_{2}\otimes_{f}H)\geq 2\xi(H)+1
. Since the order of
H
is at least
2
, Theorem
3.2
implies that
2\xi(H)\geq{\rm gp}(H)\geq 2
. Then
{\rm gp}(K_{2}\otimes_{f}H)\geq{\rm gp}(H)+1\geq 3
and thus

|X\cap V(1H)|\geq 2
or
|X\cap V(2H)|\geq 2
.

Assume, without loss of generality, that
|X\cap V(1H)|\geq 2
. If
(1,v)\in X
, then Lemma
4.1
implies that
X\cap V(2H)=\emptyset
and then, using Theorem
3.2
once more,
{\rm gp}(K_{2}\otimes_{f}H)\leq{\rm gp}(H)\leq 2\xi^{-}(H)\leq 2\xi(H)
, a contradiction. Hence assume in the rest that
(1,v)\notin X
. Since
{\rm gp}(K_{2}\otimes_{f}H)\geq{\rm gp}(H)+1
, we get
|X\cap V(2H)|\geq 2
. But as argued in (i), this is not possible for
(2,u)\in X
. Hence
|X\cap V(2H)|\leq\xi_{H}(u)\leq\xi(H)
. By symmetry we also have that if
|X\cap V(2H)|\geq 2
, then
|X\cap V(1H)|\leq\xi_{H}(v)\leq\xi(H)
holds. We can thus conclude that
{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{2},H)\leq 2\xi(H)
.

To complete the argument we need to demonstrate that
{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{2},H)\geq 2\xi(H)
. Let
x
be a vertex of
H
such that
\xi_{H}(x)=\xi(H)
and let the function
f\in H^{K_{2}}
be defined by
f(1)=f(2)=x
. By Proposition
3.3
, it follows that
{\rm gp}(K_{2}\otimes_{f}H)\geq 2\xi_{H}(x)=2\xi(H)
.
Then we have
{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{2},H)\geq 2\xi(H)
, and we are done.

\square

5
Sierpiński products of complete graphs

In this section we consider the Sierpiński product of complete graphs
K_{m}\otimes_{f}K_{n}
. In the first main result we determine their Sierpiński general position number. For the lower Sierpiński general position number we prove that
\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})=m(n-m+1)
provided that
n\geq 2m-2
. The latter assumption is required as demonstrated by our last result which asserts that
\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{6},K_{9})=25
.

Throughout this section we will assume that
V(K_{m})=[m]
and
V(K_{n})=\{x_{i}:\ i\in[n]\}
.

Theorem 5.1

If
m,n\geq 2
, then

{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})=m(n-1)\,.

Proof.
Let
f\in K_{n}^{K_{m}}
be defined by
f(i)=x_{1}
for
i\in[m]
. Let

X=\{(i,x_{j}):\ i\in[m],\ 2\leq j\leq n\}
. Since
f
is a constant function, for any two vertices
(i,x_{j}),(i^{\prime},x_{j}^{\prime})\in X
, where
i\neq i^{\prime}
, we have
d_{K_{m}\otimes_{f}K_{n}}((i,x_{j}),(i^{\prime},x_{j}^{\prime}))=3
. By Theorem
2.3
,
X
is a general position set, hence
{\rm gp}(K_{m}\otimes_{f}K_{n})\geq m(n-1)
. It follows that
{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})\geq{\rm gp}(K_{m}\otimes_{f}K_{n})\geq m(n-1)
.

To prove equality, suppose on the contrary that
{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})\geq m(n-1)+1
. It means that there exists a function
f^{\prime}\in K_{n}^{K_{m}}
and an index
i\in[m]
, such that
V(iK_{n})
completely lies in some general position set
S
of
K_{m}\otimes_{f^{\prime}}K_{n}
. Since
m,n\geq 2
, there exists a vertex
(i^{\prime},x_{j})\in S
, where
i\neq i^{\prime}
. Assume that
f^{\prime}(i^{\prime})=x_{p}
and let
q\in[n]
be such that
q\neq p
. Then the vertices
(i,x_{q})\in S
,
(i,x_{p})\in S
, and
(i^{\prime},x_{j})\in S
lie on a common shortest path (of length
2
) of
K_{m}\otimes_{f^{\prime}}K_{n}
, a contradiction. This contradiction implies that
{\rm gp}(K_{m}\otimes_{f^{\prime}}K_{n})\leq m(n-1)
, hence we have
{\rm gp}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})=m(n-1)
.

\square

In the remainder we focus on the lower Sierpiński general position number.

Lemma 5.2

Let
m,n\geq 2
and let
f\in K_{n}^{K_{m}}
. Then the following hold.

(i) If
m\leq n
, then
{\rm gp}(K_{m}\otimes_{f}K_{n})\geq v_{n-1}(K_{m}\otimes_{f}K_{n})\geq(n-m+1)m
. In particular,

\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})\geq(n-m+1)m\,.

(ii) If
m\geq n
, then
{\rm gp}(K_{m}\otimes_{f}K_{n})\geq\max\{2(n-1),m\}
. In particular,

\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})\geq\max\{2(n-1),m\}\,.

Proof.
Let
f\in K_{n}^{K_{m}}
, and set
Z=K_{m}\otimes_{f}K_{n}
for the rest of the proof.

(i) Note that a vertex of
Z
is of degree
n-1
if and only if it is not incident to a connecting edge. For each
i\in[m]
, let
X_{i}
be the set of vertices from
V(Z)\cap V(iK_{n})
of degree
n-1
. (Note that it is possible that
X_{i}=\emptyset
.) Then
X_{i}
clearly induces a complete subgraph. Moreover, if
i\neq j
, and if
(i,x_{k})\in X_{i}
and
(j,x_{\ell})\in X_{j}
, then
d_{Z}((i,x_{k}),(j,x_{\ell}))=3
. Theorem
2.3
implies that
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}
is a general position set. This in turn yields
{\rm gp}(Z)\geq v_{n-1}(Z)
because each vertex not in
\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}
is of degree at least
n
. Moreover, since be definition, each
iK_{n}
contains at most
m-1
vertices incident to connecting edges, we have
|X_{i}|\geq n-m+1
, so we also have
v_{n-1}(Z)\geq(n-m+1)m
.

The above argument holds true for any function
f\in K_{n}^{K_{m}}
, hence we have
\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})\geq(n-m+1)m
.

(ii) Assume first that
f
is a constant function, without loss of generality let
f(i)=x_{1}
for
i\in[m]
. By Theorem
2.3
we then get that
V(Z)\setminus\{(1,x_{1}),\ldots,(m,x_{1})\}
is a general position set, hence in this case
{\rm gp}(Z)\geq 2(n-1)
. Assume second that
f
is not an identity function and assume without loss of generality that
f(1)=x_{1}
and
f(2)=x_{2}
. Now we claim that
S=V(1K_{n})\cup V(2K_{n})\setminus\{(1,x_{2}),(2,x_{1})\}
is a general position set. To prove this claim it suffices to show (again in view of Theorem
2.3
) that if
(1,x_{i}),(2,x_{j})\in S
, then
d_{Z}((1,x_{i}),(2,x_{j}))=3
. Suppose on the contrary that
d_{Z}((1,x_{i}),(2,x_{j}))=2
. Then there exists a vertex
(p,x_{k})
, where
k\neq 1,2
, such that

(1,x_{i})(p,x_{k})\in E(Z)
and
(2,x_{j})(p,x_{k})\in E(Z)
. But this means that
f(1)=x_{k}
and
f(2)=x_{k}
, a contradiction since we have assumed that
f(1)\neq f(2)
. This contradiction proves the claim. We conclude that
S
is a general position set of
Z
and as
|S|=2(n-1)
we have proved that
{\rm gp}(Z)\geq 2(n-1)
.

To prove that
{\rm gp}(Z)\geq m
, we may assume without loss of generality that the function
f
is non-decreasing with respect to the indices of
x_{i}
s. That is, let
k_{1}\geq\cdots\geq k_{n}\geq 0
, where
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}=m
, and set
k_{0}=0
. Then for
i\in[n]
,

f(j)=x_{i}\quad{\rm for}\quad j\in\{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i-1}+1,\ldots,k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i}\}\,.

For
i\in[n]
, set

X_{i}=\big\{(j,x_{i}):\ j\in\{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i-1}+1,\ldots,k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i}\}\big\}\,.

Note that for some
i
we can have
X_{i}=\emptyset
, but in any case
\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X_{i}|=m
.

We claim that
X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
is a general position set of
Z
. Note first that
X_{i}
,
i\in[n]
, induces a complete subgraph
K_{k_{i}}
of
Z
. Therefore, in view of Theorem
2.3
, to prove that
X
is a general position set, it suffices to demonstrate that if
(j,x_{i})\in X_{i}
and
(j^{\prime},x_{i^{\prime}})\in X_{i^{\prime}}
, where
i\neq i^{\prime}
, then
d_{Z}((j,x_{i}),(j^{\prime},x_{i^{\prime}}))=3
. Since
(j,x_{i})
and
(j^{\prime},x_{i^{\prime}})
are not adjacent, suppose on the contrary that
d_{Z}((j,x_{i}),(j^{\prime},x_{i^{\prime}}))=2
. Then there exists a vertex
(p,x_{q})
, where
p\neq j,j^{\prime}
, such that
(j,x_{i})(p,x_{q})\in E(Z)
and
(j^{\prime},x_{i^{\prime}})(p,x_{q})\in E(Z)
. Then it follows that
f(j)=x_{q}
and
f(j^{\prime})=x_{q}
. But this is not possible, since
(j,x_{i})\in X_{i}
and
(j^{\prime},x_{i^{\prime}})\in X_{i^{\prime}}
, where
i\neq i^{\prime}
, which in particular means that
f(j)\neq f(j^{\prime})
. This contradiction proves the claim, hence
{\rm gp}(Z)\geq m
.

Again, the above arguments hold true for any function
f\in K_{n}^{K_{m}}
, hence we have
\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})\geq\max\{2(n-1),m\}
.

\square

For the lower Sierpiński general position number of two complete graphs we have the following result, where we need to assume that the second factor is relatively large with respect to the first factor.

Theorem 5.3

If
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 2m-2
, then

\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})=m(n-m+1)\,.

Proof.
Using Lemma
5.2
(i) once more we have
\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})\geq m(n-m+1)
.

To prove that
\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})=m(n-m+1)
, consider the function
f\in K_{n}^{K_{m}}
defined by
f(i)=x_{i}
for
i\in[m]
. Setting
G=K_{m}\otimes_{f}K_{n}
we are going to show that
{\rm gp}(G)\leq m(n-m+1)
.

Suppose on the contrary that
{\rm gp}(G)\geq m(n-m+1)+1
and let
R
be a gp-set of
G
. Then it follows that there exists an index
i\in[m]
such that
|V(iK_{n})\cap R|\geq n-m+2\geq 2
. Assume, without loss of generality, that
|V(1K_{n})\cap R|
is as large as possible.

We claim that if
(1,x_{j})\in V(1K_{n})\cap R
, then
V(jK_{n})\cap R=\emptyset
,
j\in[m]
. Indeed, since
|V(1K_{n})\cap R|\geq 2
, there exists a vertex
(1,x_{j^{\prime}})\in R
, where
j^{\prime}\neq j
and
j^{\prime}\in[m]
. Then the vertices
(1,x_{j^{\prime}})
,
(1,x_{j})
,
(j,x_{1})
, and
(j,x_{p})\in V(jK_{n})
form a shortest path, hence we conclude that
(j,x_{p})\notin R
for
p\in[n]
.

As we have assumed that
{\rm gp}(G)\geq m(n-m+1)+1
, we can write
|V(1K_{n})\cap R|=n-m+1+k
, where
k\geq 1
(and
k\leq m-1
). Then
|V(sK_{n})\cap R|\leq n-m+1+k
, where
2\leq s\leq m
. By the above,
G
contains at least
k
copies
iK_{n}
such that
V(iK_{n})\cap R=\emptyset
. Then we have

\displaystyle|R|

\displaystyle\leq(n-m+1+k)+(m-k-1)(n-m+1+k)

\displaystyle=m(n-m+1)+k(2m-n-1-k)

\displaystyle<m(n-m+1)+1.

Here the last inequality holds because
n\geq 2m-2
and
k\geq 1
which implies that
2m-n-1-k\leq 2-1-k=1-k\leq 0
. This contradiction implies that
{\rm gp}(G)\leq m(n-m+1)
. We conclude that
\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})=m(n-m+1)
.

\square

6
Concluding remarks

In Proposition
3.3
we have bounded from below the general position number of graphs with bridges. It would be interesting to characterize the graphs that attain the equality in the proved bound.

In view of Theorem
4.2
it would be interesting to characterize the graphs
G
with
{\rm gp}(G)=\xi(G)
. Such graphs are, for instance, grid graphs
P_{n}\,\square\,P_{m}
,
n,m\geq 3
, for which we have
{\rm gp}(P_{n}\,\square\,P_{m})=4=\xi(P_{n}\,\square\,P_{m})
. Similarly, in view of Theorem
4.4
it would be interesting to study the graphs
G
with
\xi^{-}(G)=\xi(G)
.

In Theorem
5.3
we have proved that
\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})=m(n-m+1)
if
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 2m-2
. It remains as an open problem to determine
\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{m},K_{n})
for the cases when
n<2m-2
. In Proposition
5.4
the particular case of
\underline{{\rm gp}}{{}_{\rm S}}(K_{6},K_{9})
has been solved.

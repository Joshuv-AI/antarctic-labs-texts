Banach limits and traces on ℒ 1 , ∞ \mathcal{L}_{1,\infty} . Journal: Advances in Mathematics
Evgeniy Semenov, Fedor Sukochev, Alexandr Usachev, Dmitriy Zanin

Abstract
We introduce a new approach to traces on the principal ideal ℒ 1 , ∞ \mathcal{L}_{1,\infty} generated by any positive compact operator whose singular value sequence is the harmonic sequence. Distinct from the well-known construction of J. Dixmier, the new approach provides the explicit construction of every trace of every operator in ℒ 1 , ∞ \mathcal{L}_{1,\infty} in terms of translation invariant functionals applied to a sequence of restricted sums of eigenvalues. The approach is based on a remarkable bijection between the set of all traces on ℒ 1 , ∞ \mathcal{L}_{1,\infty} and the set of all translation invariant functionals on l ∞ l_{\infty} . This bijection allows us to identify all known and commonly used subsets of traces (Dixmier traces, Connes-Dixmier traces, etc.) in terms of invariance properties of linear functionals on l ∞ l_{\infty} , and definitively classify the measurability of operators in ℒ 1 , ∞ \mathcal{L}_{1,\infty} in terms of qualified convergence of sums of eigenvalues. This classification has led us to a resolution of several open problems (for the class ℒ 1 , ∞ \mathcal{L}_{1,\infty} ) from [ 7 ] . As an application we extend Connes’ classical trace theorem to positive normalised traces.

Introduction

Let
B(H)
denote the algebra of all bounded linear operators on a separable Hilbert space
H
.
Denote by
\{\mu(n,A)\}_{n\geq 0}
the sequence of singular values of a compact operator
A\in B(H)
.
Define the principal ideal
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
(also termed the weak-
\mathcal{L}_{1}
ideal) of the algebra
B(H)
by setting

\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}:=\left\{A\in B(H)\text{ is compact}:\sup_{n\geq 0}(1+n)\mu(n,A)<\infty\right\}.

A trace on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is a unitarily invariant linear functional on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
.

We present a new approach to the construction of traces on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
, which, in a way, completes the original idea of J. Dixmier,
[
12
]
. Our construction was inspired by that of A. Pietsch,
[
46
]
.
Let
\{\lambda(n,A)\}_{n\geq 0}
be a sequence of eigenvalues of a compact operator
A\in B(H)
, ordered in a such way that the sequence
\{|\lambda(n,A)|\}_{n\geq 0}
is decreasing.
Observing that, for every operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
, the sequence

\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}

is bounded, we construct the functional

\tau(A)=\theta\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right),\quad A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty},

(1)

where
\theta
is a linear translation invariant functional on
l_{\infty}

The remarkable fact we show is that this construction provides a linear
bijective
association between traces on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
and linear translation invariant functionals on
l_{\infty}
.

Before continuing, let us compare the construction (
1
) with the very well known construction of J. Dixmier. As Dixmier stated in a letter to the conference “Singular traces and their applications” at Luminy, 2012 (see the notes to Chapter 6 of
[
36
]
),
his first idea was to construct a singular (non-normal) trace
A\mapsto t(A)
on the ideal
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
by using the formula

t(A):=\theta(\left\{(n+1)\lambda(n,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}),\quad 0\leq A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty},

where
\theta
is an extended limit on
l_{\infty}
(a Hahn-Banach extension to
l_{\infty}
of the ordinary limit on the set of convergent sequences
c
). However, Dixmier “wasn’t able to prove the additivity of
t(A)
”.
Following N. Aronszajn’s advice, Dixmier changed the setting from the ideal
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
to the larger ideal
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
and succeeded.
Being more precise, for an arbitrary translation and dilation invariant extended limit
\omega
on
l_{\infty}
(a precise definition can be found in the subsequent sections),
J. Dixmier
[
12
]
constructed the weight

{\rm Tr}_{\omega}(A):=\omega\left(\left\{\frac{1}{\log(2+n)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mu(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right),\quad 0\leq A\in\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty},

(2)

where

\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}:=\left\{A\in B(H)\text{ is compact}:\sup_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{\log(2+n)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mu(k,A)<\infty\right\}.

The weight
{\rm Tr}_{\omega}
extends to a singular trace (called by subsequent authors a Dixmier trace) on
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
.
Evidently
\mathrm{Tr}_{\omega}
on
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
restricts to a positive trace on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
.
It follows directly from Definitions
5.40
and
5.41
below that
every Dixmier trace on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
extends to a Dixmier trace on
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
.
Here we use the term "Dixmier trace on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
" for the restriction of a Dixmier trace from
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}

to
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
(see Definition
5.41
below).

However, not every positive trace on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is the restriction
of a positive trace on
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
(see Theorem
4.30
below).
Therefore Dixmier’s construction does not generate all traces on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
.
In addition, in Dixmier’s construction (
2
) the extended limit
\omega
is far from being unique (see e.g.
[
56
, Theorem 40]
). As a consequence, we are denied a neat characterisation of traces by using known characterisations of extended limits.
We also remark that the construction (
2
) was given in terms of singular values, and it has been a non-trivial task to formulate traces in terms of eigenvalues (see
[
25
]
and Lidkii formulas in
[
50
,
36
]
).

As stated, and shown below, the advantage of the construction (
1
) is that it is a bijective association between traces on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
and linear translation invariant functionals on
l_{\infty}
, and that it is formulated in terms of eigenvalues.
This has fundamental consequences for the study of traces on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
and allied topics
(in particular, those parts of noncommutative geometry which employ singular traces).
Indeed, this approach has led us to a complete description of various sets of traces on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
and measurable operators in
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
introduced by A. Connes.

As an example, let us recall that Connes observed in
[
11
]
, that in order to ensure that the functional
{\rm Tr}_{\omega}
be a trace,
it is sufficient to only assume in (
2
) that
\omega
is a dilation invariant extended limit on
l_{\infty}
.
Dixmier’s original construction used a dilation and translation invariant extended limit. Later it was proved in
[
56
, Theorem 2]
(see also
[
36
, Theorem 9.6.9]
) that the set of traces constructed by dilation invariant extended limits coincided with the set of traces constructed using translation and dilation invariant extended limits. Therefore there is no ambiguity in calling the set of all traces generated by dilation invariant extended limits the set of Dixmier traces, and we denote the set by
\mathcal{D}
.
Using (
1
) we find (see Theorem
5.47
below) that the set
\mathcal{D}
is isometric to the set of “factorisable” Banach limits,
[
47
]
.
Recall a Banach limit
\theta
on
l_{\infty}
is “factorisable” if it is of the form
\theta=\gamma\circ C
for an extended limit
\gamma
and
C:l_{\infty}\to l_{\infty}
the Cesaro operator.
As a consequence, a compact operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is measurable with respect to
\mathcal{D}

(that is, it has the same value for every Dixmier trace)
if and only if its eigenvalue sequence satisfies the condition that

C\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}\text{ is convergent}.

Combining this characterisation with the fact that the classes of Dixmier traces (on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
)
and normalised fully symmetric functionals on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
coincide (see Corollary
5.46
below),
we are able to resolve (for the class
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
) an open problem (iii) stated
[
7
, p. 1061]
,
concerning the measurability with respect to the class of all normalised fully symmetric functionals.

A smaller subclass of Dixmier traces was suggested by A. Connes in
[
11
, Section IV, 2
\beta
]
.
It was observed that for any extended limit
\gamma
on
l_{\infty}
the functional
\omega:=\gamma\circ M
is dilation invariant.
Here, the bounded operator
M:l_{\infty}\to l_{\infty}
is given by the formula

(Mx)_{n}=\frac{1}{\log(2+n)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{x_{k}}{k+1},\ n\geq 1.

Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the set of traces generated by using a dilation extended limit
\omega
of the form
\omega:=\gamma\circ M
in (
2
).
This set of traces was termed “Connes-Dixmier traces” in
[
34
]
. Evidently
\mathcal{C}
is a subset of
\mathcal{D}
, i.e. every Connes-Dixmier trace is a Dixmier trace.
It is a strict subset since it is known that the set of Dixmier and Connes-Dixmier traces are distinct
[
42
, Theorem 6.1]
.
This distinction was studied by A. Pietsch in a series of three papers
[
43
,
44
,
42
]
, where the deep techniques were developed,
which are of a wider interest in the theory of singular traces.
The inclusion
\mathcal{C}\subsetneq\mathcal{D}
was also proved in
[
57
, Theorem 2.2]
using a different approach.
Using (
1
) we find (see Theorem
5.52
below) the neat classification establishing the (isometric) bijection between the set
\mathcal{C}

and that of Banach limits of the form
\theta=\gamma\circ C^{2}
for an extended limit
\gamma
.
Thus a compact operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is measurable with respect to
\mathcal{C}
(having the same value for every Connes-Dixmier trace)
if and only if its eigenvalue sequence satisfies the condition that

C^{2}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}\text{ is convergent}.

This leads to a surprising result. It is known that on the positive cone of
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
the notions of Dixmier- and Connes-Dixmier measurable coincide,

[
34
, Corollary 3.9]
. See
[
7
,
11
,
34
,
35
,
36
,
57
]
for properties and concrete examples of Dixmier- and Connes-Dixmier measurable operators.
Using the above classification and a Tauberian result of G. Hardy we show that for every operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}

(not necessarily positive) the notion of Dixmier- and Connes-Dixmier measurable coincide.
This result resolves the problem (i) stated in
[
7
, p. 1061]
(in the ideal
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
) in the affirmative.
Whether this result remains true on the larger ideal
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
remains unknown.

Finally, an even smaller subclass of Dixmier traces was considered by many authors (see e.g.
[
3
,
5
]
).
Within the set of dilation invariant extended limits of the form
\omega:=\gamma\circ M

. For evident reasons such an extended limit is called an

-invariant extended limit.
Let

denote the set of traces generated by using an

-invariant extended limit

in (

).
Evidently
\mathcal{D}_{M}
is a subset of

.
Dixmier traces from the set

are used in various important formulae in noncommutative geometry,
such as (a) Connes’ formula for a representative of the Hochschild class of the Chern character for
(p,\infty)
-summable spectral triples
(see e.g.
[
4
, Theorem 7]
and
[

), and (b) the formulae involving heat kernel estimates and generalised

-functions residues
(see e.g.
[

,
4
,
3

6
,

).

Using (
1
) we prove in Theorem

below that the set
\mathcal{D}_{M}
is isometric to the set of Banach limits of the form
\theta=\theta\circ C

(the set of Cesaro invariant Banach limits).
This is sufficient to show that
\mathcal{D}_{M}
is a strict subset of the set
\mathcal{C}
of Connes-Dixmier traces.
It follows from some further results that a compact operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is measurable with respect to
\mathcal{D}_{M}

(having the same value for every
M
-invariant Dixmier trace) if and only if its eigenvalue sequence satisfies the condition that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}C^{m}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}C^{m}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}

(this result should be compared with that
[

).
We show (see Theorem

\mathcal{D}_{M}
contains as a strict subset those measurable with respect to the sets traces
\mathcal{D}
or
\mathcal{C}
.

Going beyond Dixmier traces, the construction (
1
) gives us a clear path to study the set of all positive normalised traces on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
.
Recall that a trace
\tau
on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is normalised if
\tau(A)=1
for every
0\leq A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
with
\mu(n,A)=(1+n)^{-1}
,

.
The set of positive normalised traces, denoted
\mathcal{PT}
, and the notion of operators measurable with respect to the set
\mathcal{PT}
is studied here for the first time.
Using (

\mathcal{PT}
and the set of all Banach limits on
l_{\infty}
.
This makes the set of positive normalised traces a very natural set of traces to consider.
Further, the notion of

[
37

An operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is measurable with respect to the set
\mathcal{PT}
of traces (that is, all positive normalised traces

\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}\text{ is almost convergent}.

We also prove that the class of Dixmier measurable operators is strictly wider than the class of
\mathcal{PT}
-measurable operators within
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
(see Theorem

To summarise the above result on traces in symbols, let us introduce

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a subset of traces on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}

The set
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}^{\mathcal{A}}
of all
\mathcal{A}
-measurable elements consists of all elements

A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
such that

takes the same value for all
\tau\in\mathcal{A}
.

We have the various sets of normalised positive traces on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}

\mathcal{D}_{M}\subsetneq\mathcal{C}\subsetneq\mathcal{D}\subsetneq\mathcal{PT},

where
\mathcal{D}
is the traditional set of Dixmier traces and, using (
1
),
these sets are isometric (respectively) to

\{\theta=\theta\circ C:\theta\text{ is an extended limit}\}\subsetneq\{\theta=\gamma\circ C^{2}:\gamma\text{ is an extended limit}\}\\
\subsetneq\{\theta=\gamma\circ C:\gamma\text{ is an extended limit}\}\subsetneq\{\theta:\theta\text{ is a Banach limit}\}

(see Theorems
5.54
,
5.52
,
5.47

4.25

Summing up all the results about measurability we obtain the following chain of inclusions

\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}^{\mathcal{PT}}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}^{\mathcal{D}}=\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}^{\mathcal{C}}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}^{\mathcal{D}_{M}}.

is Wodzicki’s (noncommutative) residue of

1. It is standard to reduce questions concerning traces on

to questions concerning functionals on its commutative counterpart

.
Then we show the three-way bijection between: symmetric functionals on the weak-

, traces on the corresponding ideal

of compact operators
and translation invariant linear functionals on the space

and Banach limits on

in Corollary

2. In Section

The main result of this section (Theorem

)
introduces the bijection between the set

of positive normalised traces on

2
Preliminaries

We denote by
L_{\infty}:=L_{\infty}(0,\infty)
the space of all (equivalence classes of) real-valued
essentially bounded Lebesgue measurable functions on
(0,\infty)
equipped with the norm

\|x\|_{L_{\infty}}:=\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t>0}|x(t)|.

Let
C_{0}:=C_{0}(0,\infty)
denote the subspace of all bounded continuous functions on
(0,\infty)
that vanish at infinity.

Let
\pi
be the isometric embedding
\pi:l_{\infty}\to L_{\infty}

given by

\{x_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\mapsto}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x_{n}\chi_{[n,n+1)}.

(4)

For every operator
A\in B(H)
a generalised singular value function
\mu(A)
is defined by the formula

\mu(t,A)=\inf\{\|Ap\|_{\infty}:\ p\ \text{is a projection in}\ B(H)\ \text{with}\ {\rm Tr}(1-p)\leq t\}.

Since
B(H)
is an atomic von Neumann algebra and traces of all atoms equal to 1, it follows that
\mu(A)
is a step function for every
A\in B(H)
(see e.g.
[
36
, Chapter 2]
).
In particular, for a compact operator
A\in B(H)
we have that
\mu(n,A)
,
n\geq 0
, is the
n
-th singular value of the operator
A
.

We give the definition of extended limits on functions and sequences.

Definition 2.4
.

A positive linear functional on
L_{\infty}
is called an extended limit if it coincides with the ordinary limit on every convergent (at
+\infty
) function.
A positive linear functional on
l_{\infty}
is called an extended limit if it coincides with the ordinary limit on every convergent sequence.

Remark 2.5
.

It is well-known that for every extended limit
\gamma
and every
x\in l_{\infty}
the following inequalities hold

\liminf_{n\to\infty}x_{n}\leq\gamma(x)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}x_{n}.

Hence, every extended limit is a positive norm-one functional on
l_{\infty}
.
Moreover, for every
x\in l_{\infty}
the following equality holds

\{\gamma(x):\gamma\ \text{is an extended limit}\}=[\liminf_{n\to\infty}x_{n},\limsup_{n\to\infty}x_{n}].

Similar statements hold for extended limits on
L_{\infty}
.

Definition 2.6
.

An extended limit
\omega
on
L_{\infty}
is called dilation invariant if

\omega\circ\sigma_{\beta}=\omega\ \text{for every}\ \beta>0.

Here,

(\sigma_{\beta}x)(t):=x(t/\beta),t>0.

Let
S:l_{\infty}\to l_{\infty}
be the right shift operator defined as follows

S(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)=(0,x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)

and let
T:l_{\infty}\to l_{\infty}
be the left shift operator defined as follows

T(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots).

We also define a dilation operator
\sigma_{2}:l_{\infty}\to l_{\infty}
as follows

\sigma_{2}(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)=(x_{0},x_{0},x_{1},x_{1},x_{2},x_{2},\dots).

It is evident that any left-shift-invariant functional on
l_{\infty}
is invariant with respect to the right shift.
The converse statement is false in general. However, the converse statement holds for bounded functionals.

Proposition 2.7
.

For every bounded linear functional
\theta
on
l_{\infty}
we have

\theta=\theta\circ S
if and only if
\theta=\theta\circ T
.

Proof.

We show the “if” direction; the “only if” direction is trivial.
Let
\theta
be a bounded functional on
l_{\infty}
such that
\theta=\theta\circ S
.
We first show that
\theta(1,0,0,\dots)=0
.
Assume contrapositively that
\theta(1,0,0,\dots)=a\neq 0
.
Since
\theta=\theta\circ S
, it follows that

\theta(\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{n\ \text{times}},0,0,\dots)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\theta\circ S^{k}(1,0,0,\dots)=na.

Hence, the functional
\theta
is not bounded.

The obtained contradiction shows that for every bounded functional
\theta
on
l_{\infty}

such that
\theta=\theta\circ S
one has
\theta(1,0,0,\dots)=0
or equivalently

\theta(x_{0},0,0,\dots)=0
.

Hence,

\displaystyle\theta(x)=\theta(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots)-\theta(x_{0},0,0,\dots)=\theta(0,x_{1},x_{2},\dots)=\theta(STx)=\theta(Tx).

∎

Definition 2.8
.

A linear functional
B
on
l_{\infty}
is called a Banach limit if

(i)

B\geq 0
, that is
B(x)\geq 0
for
x\geq 0
,

(ii)

B(\mathrm{1\hskip-3.00003ptI})=1
, where
\mathrm{1\hskip-3.00003ptI}=(1,1,1,\dots)
,

(iii)

B(Sx)=B(x)
for all
x\in l_{\infty}
.

Note that, originally, Banach limits were defined to be
T
-invariant
[
2
, Chapter II, §3, Example 4]
.
In view of Proposition
2.7
our definition is equivalent to that of Banach.
We denote the set of all Banach limits by
\mathfrak{B}
.

The following concept was introduced by G. G. Lorentz,
[
37
]
.

Definition 2.9
.

A sequence
x\in l_{\infty}
is said to be almost convergent (to
a\in\mathbb{R}
) if
Bx=a
for every Banach limit
B
.

Denote the set of all almost convergent sequences by
ac
.
Denote by
ac_{0}
the subset of all sequences almost convergent to zero.
The following criterion of almost convergence was proved by Lorentz,
[
37
]
.

Theorem 2.10
.

A sequence
x\in l_{\infty}
is almost convergent to
a\in\mathbb{R}
if and only if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=m}^{m+n-1}x_{k}=a

uniformly in
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

3
Symmetric functionals on
l_{1,\infty}

Denote by
l_{1,\infty}
the linear space (frequently called the weak
l_{1}
-space) of all bounded sequences for which the quasi-norm

\|x\|_{l_{1,\infty}}=\sup_{n\geq 0}(n+1)x_{n}^{*}

is finite. Recall that by
x^{*}
we denote a decreasing rearrangement of
|x|
.

The following definition should be compared with a similar notion studied in
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
.

Definition 3.11
.

A linear functional
\varphi
on
l_{1,\infty}
is called symmetric if

\varphi(x)=\varphi(y)
for every
0\leq x,y\in l_{1,\infty}
such that
x^{*}=y^{*}
.

Denote by

Z:={\rm Lin}\{u-v:0\leq u,v\in l_{1,\infty},u^{*}=v^{*}\}.

It should be noticed that every symmetric functional
\varphi
on
l_{1,\infty}
vanishes on
Z
.
In particular, for every symmetric functional
\varphi
we have
\varphi(x)=\frac{1}{2}\varphi(\sigma_{2}x)
for every
x\in l_{1,\infty}
.
We also have that every symmetric functional vanishes on
l_{1}
,
[
15
, Proposition 2.6]
.

The following lemma is proved for the case of a Banach symmetric sequence space in
[
36
, Proposition 4.2.8]
.
Although we state it for a quasi-Banach symmetric sequence space, the proof is exactly the same and is, therefore, omitted.

Lemma 3.12
.

For every continuous symmetric functional
\varphi
on
l_{1,\infty}
the functional

\varphi_{+}(x):=\sup_{0\leq u\leq x}\varphi(u),\ 0\leq x\in l_{1,\infty}

satisfies the following properties:

(i)
\varphi_{+}(x+y)=\varphi_{+}(x)+\varphi_{+}(y)
,
x,y\geq 0
;

(ii)
\varphi_{+}(x^{*})=\varphi_{+}(x)
,
x\geq 0
;

In particular, the functional
\varphi_{+}
extends to a positive symmetric functional on
l_{1,\infty}
.

Denote by
l_{1,\infty}^{*}
the space of all linear functionals on
l_{1,\infty}
.
For
\varphi,\psi\in l_{1,\infty}^{*}
define the functionals
\varphi\vee\psi,\varphi\wedge\psi\in l_{1,\infty}^{*}

by the following formulae

(\varphi\vee\psi)(x)=\sup\{\varphi(u)+\psi(v):\ 0\leq u,v\in l_{1,\infty},x=u+v\},\ 0\leq x\in l_{1,\infty},

(\varphi\wedge\psi)(x)=\inf\{\varphi(u)+\psi(v):\ 0\leq u,v\in l_{1,\infty},x=u+v\},\ 0\leq x\in l_{1,\infty}.

Observe that
\varphi\vee 0=\varphi_{+}
.

Proposition 3.13
.

The set of all continuous symmetric functionals on
l_{1,\infty}
is a lattice
with respect to the operations
\vee
and
\wedge
defined above.

Proof.

We shall show that the set of all continuous symmetric functionals on
l_{1,\infty}
is a sublattice (in the lattice
l_{1,\infty}^{*}
),
that is for every continuous symmetric functionals
\varphi
and
\psi
the functional
\varphi\vee\psi
is continuous and symmetric.

We have

\displaystyle(\varphi\vee\psi)(x)

\displaystyle=\sup\{\varphi(u)+\psi(v):\ 0\leq u,v\in l_{1,\infty},x=u+v\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\varphi(x-v)+\psi(v):\ 0\leq u,v\in l_{1,\infty},x=u+v\}

\displaystyle=\varphi(x)+\sup\{(\psi-\varphi)(v):\ 0\leq v\leq x\}

\displaystyle=\varphi(x)+(\psi-\varphi)_{+}(x).

By Lemma
3.12
the functional
\varphi\vee\psi
is continuous and symmetric.
Consequently, the set of all continuous symmetric functionals is a sublattice.
∎

The following linear operator from
l_{\infty}
into
l_{1,\infty}
given by

D(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\dots):=\log 2\cdot(\frac{x_{0}}{2^{0}},\underbrace{\frac{x_{1}}{2^{1}},\frac{x_{1}}{2^{1}}}_{2\ \text{times}},\underbrace{\frac{x_{2}}{2^{2}},\frac{x_{2}}{2^{2}},\frac{x_{2}}{2^{2}},\frac{x_{2}}{2^{2}}}_{4\ \text{times}},\dots,\underbrace{\frac{x_{n}}{2^{n}},\dots,\frac{x_{n}}{2^{n}}}_{2^{n}\ \text{times}},\dots)

plays an important role in this paper. The concept of the operator
D
was suggested by A.Pietsch in
[
46
]
.

It is easy to see that the operator
D
is continuous from
l_{\infty}
into
l_{1,\infty}

and that

\|D\|_{l_{\infty}\to l_{1,\infty}}=2\log 2.

The following two lemmas are crucial technical elements in the construction of symmetric functionals on
l_{1,\infty}
.

Lemma 3.14
.

For every
0\leq x,y\in l_{1,\infty}
we have:

(i)

\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}\in l_{\infty};

(ii)

\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n+1}-2}(x_{k}^{*}+y_{k}^{*}-(x+y)_{k}^{*})\right\}_{n\geq 0}\in l_{\infty}.

Proof.

(i) Since
x^{*}
is decreasing, it follows that

\left|\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}^{*}\right|\leq 2^{n}x_{2^{n}-1}^{*}\leq\|x\|_{l_{1,\infty}},\ n\geq 0.

(ii) For every
n\geq 0
the following estimates hold

\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n+1}-2}(x_{k}^{*}+y_{k}^{*})\leq\sum_{k=0}^{2(2^{n+1}-2)+1}(x+y)_{k}^{*}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n+1}-2}(x+y)_{k}^{*}+2^{n+1}(x+y)_{2^{n+1}-1}^{*}.

Hence,

0\leq\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n+1}-2}(x_{k}^{*}+y_{k}^{*}-(x+y)_{k}^{*})\leq\|x+y\|_{l_{1,\infty}}.

∎

Lemma 3.15
.

For every
0\leq x\in l_{1,\infty}
such that
x_{n}\leq\frac{\alpha}{n+1}
,
n\geq 0
, for some
\alpha>0
we have

\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}(x_{k}^{*}-x_{k})\right|\leq\alpha,\ n\geq 0.

Proof.

For every
n\geq 0
there exists a subset
A\subset\mathbb{N}
, such that
|A|=n+1
and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}x_{k}^{*}=\sum_{k\in A}x_{k}.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}x_{k}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in A\cap[0,n]}x_{k}+\sum_{k\in A\cap(n,\infty)}|x_{k}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}x_{k}+\sum_{k\in A\cap(n,\infty)}\frac{\alpha}{k+1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}x_{k}+\sum_{k=n+1}^{2n+1}\frac{\alpha}{k+1}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}x_{k}+\alpha.

∎

The following lemma establishes the most important property of the operator
D
.

Lemma 3.16
.

For every
0\leq x\in l_{1,\infty}
the sequence

x-D\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\cdot\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)

belongs to
Z
.

Proof.

By the definition, for every positive
x\in l_{\infty}
, we have
x-x^{*}\in Z
. Therefore, since
Z
is a linear space it is sufficient to prove the statement for
x=x^{*}
.
In this case we have

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle:=x-D\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\cdot\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)

\displaystyle=(0,x_{1}-\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{2},x_{2}-\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{2},

\displaystyle\quad\quad x_{3}-\frac{x_{3}+x_{4}+x_{5}+x_{6}}{4},\dots,x_{6}-\frac{x_{3}+x_{4}+x_{5}+x_{6}}{4},\dots).

Note, that
\sum_{i=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}z_{i}=0
for every
n\geq 0
.
For every
2^{n}-1\leq k\leq 2^{n+1}-2
(
n\geq 0
), since
x=x^{*}
, it follows that

|z_{k}|=|x_{k}-\frac{x_{2^{n}-1}+x_{2^{n}}+\dots+x_{2^{n+1}-2}}{2^{n}}|\leq x_{2^{n}-1}

and so, we also have

|\sum_{i=0}^{k}z_{i}|=|\sum_{i=2^{n}-1}^{k}z_{i}|\leq\sum_{i=2^{n}-1}^{k}x_{2^{n}-1}\leq 2^{n}x_{2^{n}-1}\leq 2\|x\|_{l_{1,\infty}}.

For every
2^{n}-1\leq k\leq 2^{n+1}-2
(
n\geq 0
) we set

u_{k}=\sum_{i=2^{n}-1}^{k}z_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{k}=\begin{cases}0,\ k=2^{n}-1;\\
u_{k-1},\ 2^{n}\leq k\leq 2^{n+1}-2\end{cases}.

A direct verification shows that
0\leq u,v\in l_{1,\infty}
and

z=u-v
.

Since
\sum_{i=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}z_{i}=0
, it follows that
u_{2^{n+1}-2}=0
.
Thus, the sequence
v
is a permutation of
u
.
Hence,
u^{*}=v^{*}
and, so,
z\in Z
.

∎

The following theorem describes a correspondence between the class of all symmetric functionals on
l_{1,\infty}

and the class of all
S
-invariant linear functionals on
l_{\infty}
.
This correspondence (in a slightly different form) was first found by A. Pietsch in
[
46
]
(see also
[
45
]
).
The idea of studying symmetric functionals on
l_{1,\infty}
via
S
-invariant linear functionals on
l_{\infty}

has become an important motive for the present paper.

Theorem 3.17
.

(i) For every symmetric functional
\varphi
on
l_{1,\infty}
there exists
a unique
S
-invariant linear functional
\theta=\varphi\circ D
on
l_{\infty}
such that

\varphi(x)=\theta\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\cdot\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right),\quad x\geq 0.

(5)

(ii) For every linear functional
\theta
on
l_{\infty}
such that
\theta=\theta\circ S

the functional
\varphi
defined by the formula (
5
) extends by linearity to a symmetric functional on
l_{1,\infty}
.

Proof.

(i) Let
\varphi
be a symmetric functional on
l_{1,\infty}
. Set
\theta:=\varphi\circ D
.
Due to the linearity of
D
, the functional
\theta
is linear on
l_{\infty}
. We prove that
\theta
is invariant under the operator
S
.

We firstly prove an auxiliary fact that
\varphi(\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{2}(Dx)-DSx)=0
. Indeed,

\displaystyle y:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log 2}\cdot(\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{2}(Dx)-DSx)

\displaystyle=(\frac{x_{0}}{2},\frac{x_{0}}{2};\frac{x_{1}}{2^{2}},\frac{x_{1}}{2^{2}},\frac{x_{1}}{2^{2}},\frac{x_{1}}{2^{2}};\dots)-(0;\frac{x_{0}}{2^{1}},\frac{x_{0}}{2^{1}};\frac{x_{1}}{2^{2}},\frac{x_{1}}{2^{2}},\frac{x_{1}}{2^{2}},\frac{x_{1}}{2^{2}};\dots)

\displaystyle=(\frac{x_{0}}{2};0,\frac{x_{1}}{2^{2}}-\frac{x_{0}}{2^{1}};0,0,0,\frac{x_{2}}{2^{3}}-\frac{x_{1}}{2^{2}};\dots).

For
u:=(\frac{x_{0}}{2};0,\frac{x_{1}}{2^{2}};0,0,0,\frac{x_{2}}{2^{3}};\dots)
and
v:=(0;0,\frac{x_{0}}{2^{1}};0,0,0,\frac{x_{1}}{2^{2}};\dots)

we have
y=u-v
and
u^{*}=v^{*}
. So,
\varphi(y)=0
, since
\varphi
is symmetric.

Next, using the fact that
\varphi(\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{2}(Dx))=\varphi(DSx)
and that
\varphi
is symmetric, we have

\theta(Sx)=\varphi(DSx)=\varphi(\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{2}(Dx))=\varphi(Dx)=\theta(x).

Hence,
\theta
is an
S
-invariant linear functional on
l_{\infty}
.

By Lemma
3.16
for every
0\leq x\in l_{1,\infty}
we have

x-D\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\cdot\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)\in Z.

Using the fact that
\varphi
vanishes on
Z
, we derive

\varphi(x)=(\varphi\circ D)\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\cdot\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)

and, since
\theta=\varphi\circ D
, the representation (
5
) is proved. We now show that this representation is unique.

Let
\theta_{1}
be an
S
-invariant linear functional on
l_{\infty}
such that

\varphi(x)=\theta_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\cdot\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right),0\leq x\in l_{1,\infty}.

According to the definition of the functional
\theta
, we have

\theta(x)=\varphi(Dx)=\theta_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\cdot\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}(Dx)_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right),0\leq x\in l_{\infty}.

To complete the proof it is sufficient to verify that the expression on the right equals to
\theta_{1}(x)
.
Set

y_{n}:=\log 2\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{n}x_{k}-\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n+1}-2}(Dx)_{k}^{*},\ n\geq 0.

By the definition of the operator
D
, we have

\log 2\cdot\sum_{k=0}^{n}x_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n+1}-2}(Dx)_{k},\ \forall n\geq 0

and so

y_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n+1}-2}(Dx)_{k}-\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n+1}-2}(Dx)_{k}^{*},\ n\geq 0.

Since
\|D\|_{l_{\infty}\to l_{1,\infty}}=2\log 2
, it follows that

(Dx)_{n}\leq\frac{2\log 2}{n+1}\|x\|_{l_{\infty}},\ n\geq 0.

Hence, for every
0\leq x\in l_{\infty}
the sequence
Dx
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
3.15
.
Therefore, we conclude that
y\in l_{\infty}.

Obviously, we have

y_{n+1}-y_{n}=\log 2\cdot x_{n+1}-\sum_{k=2^{n+1}-1}^{2^{n+2}-2}(Dx)_{k}^{*},\ n\geq 0

and, using the definition of the operator
S
,

\log 2\cdot x-\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}(Dx)_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}=y-Sy.

Since
\theta_{1}
is an
S
-invariant linear functional, it follows that
\theta_{1}(Sy-y)=0
and

\theta_{1}(x)=\theta_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\cdot\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}(Dx)_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right),0\leq x\in l_{\infty}

and
\theta(x)=\theta_{1}(x)
for every
0\leq x\in l_{\infty}.
Hence,
\theta_{1}(x)=\theta(x)
for all
x\in l_{\infty}
.

(ii) Let
\theta=\theta\circ S
be a linear functional on
l_{\infty}
. It is clear that the functional
\varphi

given by (
5
)
is positive homogeneous on the positive cone of
l_{1,\infty}
. We shall prove its additivity on the positive cone of
l_{1,\infty}
.
For
0\leq x,y\in l_{1,\infty}

we set

z_{n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n+1}-2}(x_{k}^{*}+y_{k}^{*}-(x+y)_{k}^{*}),\ n\geq 0.

By Lemma
3.14
,
z\in l_{\infty}
. An immediate computation yields

(z-Sz)_{n}=\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}(x_{k}^{*}+y_{k}^{*}-(x+y)_{k}^{*}),\ n\geq 0.

Due to
S
-invariance of
\theta
we have
\theta(Sz-z)=0
and hence

\theta\left(\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}(x_{k}^{*}+y_{k}^{*}-(x+y)_{k}^{*})\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)=0.

(6)

Using the definition (
5
), the equality (
6
) and the linearity of
\theta
, for every
0\leq x,y\in l_{1,\infty}
we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi(x+y)

\displaystyle=\theta\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\cdot\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}(x+y)_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)

\displaystyle=\theta\left(\left\{\frac{1}{\log 2}\cdot\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}(x_{k}^{*}+y_{k}^{*})\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)

\displaystyle=\theta\left(\left\{\frac{1}{\log 2}\cdot\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)+\theta\left(\left\{\frac{1}{\log 2}\cdot\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}y_{k}^{*})\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)

\displaystyle=\varphi(x)+\varphi(y).

So, the functional
\varphi
given by (
5
) is positive homogeneous and additive on the positive cone of
l_{1,\infty}
.
Hence, it extends by linearity to the whole space
l_{1,\infty}
.

It is clear, that for every
0\leq x,y\in l_{1,\infty}
such that
x^{*}=y^{*}
we have
\varphi(x)=\varphi(y)
.
Hence, the formula (
5
) defines a symmetric functional on
l_{1,\infty}
.
∎

Now we specialise the result of Theorem
3.17
to the case of positive symmetric functionals and
to the case of continuous symmetric functionals.

Corollary 3.18
.

(i) For every positive symmetric functional
\varphi
on
l_{1,\infty}
there exists
a unique positive linear functional
\theta=\theta\circ S
on
l_{\infty}
such that (
5
)
holds.

(ii) For every positive linear functional
\theta=\theta\circ S
on
l_{\infty}

the functional
\varphi
defined by the formula (
5
) extends by linearity to a positive symmetric functional on
l_{1,\infty}
.

Proof.

In view of Theorem
3.17
we only need to show that the functional
\varphi
is positive if and only if
\theta=\varphi\circ D
is positive.
If
\varphi\geq 0
, then
\theta=\varphi\circ D\geq 0
, since
D
is a positive operator from
l_{\infty}
into
l_{1,\infty}
.
If
\theta\geq 0
, then the positivity of
\varphi
follows from (
5
).
∎

Corollary 3.19
.

(i) For every continuous symmetric functional
\varphi
on
l_{1,\infty}
there exists
a unique continuous linear functional
\theta=\theta\circ S
on
l_{\infty}
such that (
5
)
holds.

(ii) For every continuous linear functional
\theta=\theta\circ S
on
l_{\infty}

the functional
\varphi
defined by the formula (
5
) extends by linearity to a continuous symmetric functional on
l_{1,\infty}
.

Proof.

In view of Theorem
3.17
we only need to show that the functional
\varphi
is continuous if and only if
\theta=\varphi\circ D
is continuous.

If
\varphi
is continuous on
l_{1,\infty}
, then
\theta=\varphi\circ D
is continuous on
l_{\infty}
,
since
D:l_{\infty}\to l_{1,\infty}
is continuous.

By Proposition
3.13
the set of all continuous symmetric functionals is a lattice.
Since every vector lattice is a linear hull of its positive elements (see e.g.
[

), the statement follows from Corollary

.
∎

Recall that a symmetric functional on
l_{1,\infty}
is normalised if

\varphi(\{\frac{1}{n+1}\}_{n\geq 0})=1
.
The following corollary describes the correspondence between the set of all positive normalised symmetric functionals on
l_{1,\infty}

and the set
\mathfrak{B}
of all Banach limits.

Theorem 3.20
.

A linear functional
\varphi
is a positive normalised symmetric functional on
l_{1,\infty}

if and only if
B=\varphi\circ D\in\mathfrak{B}
.

Proof.

For every positive symmetric functional
\varphi
on
l_{1,\infty}
by Corollary
3.18

we obtain that the functional
B=\varphi\circ D
is a positive translation invariant functional on
l_{\infty}
,
that is
B
proportional to a Banach limit. We only need to check that
B(\mathrm{1\hskip-3.00003ptI})=1
.
Indeed,

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\varphi(\{\frac{1}{n+1}\}_{n\geq 0})=B\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\cdot\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\frac{1}{k+1}\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)

\displaystyle=B\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}(\log 2+o(1))\right)=B(\mathrm{1\hskip-3.00003ptI}).

The assertion has been proved. The proof of the “only if” part is similar.
∎

.

Definition 3.21
.

A linear functional
\varphi
on
l_{1,\infty}
is called fully symmetric if

\varphi(x)\leq\varphi(y)
for every
0\leq x,y\in l_{1,\infty}
such that
x\prec\prec y
,
that is
\sum_{k=0}^{n}x_{k}^{*}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}y_{k}^{*}
for every
n\geq 0
.

Observe that every fully symmetric functional is automatically positive and symmetric.

Define the Cesàro operator
C:l_{\infty}\to l_{\infty}
as follows

(Cx)_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}x_{k},\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

Before we state the main result, describing fully symmetric functionals on
l_{1,\infty}

in terms of factorisable Banach limits (that is a functional of the form
\gamma\circ C
for some extended limit
\gamma

[
47
]
)
, we prove an auxiliary technical lemma.

Lemma 3.22
.

Let
x\in l_{1,\infty}
be such that
|x_{n}|\leq\frac{\alpha}{n+1}
,
n\geq 0
(for some
\alpha>0
).
If
\sum_{k=0}^{n}x_{k}\leq 0
,
n\geq 0
, then
\varphi(x)\leq 0
for every fully symmetric functional
\varphi
on
l_{1,\infty}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a fully symmetric functional on
l_{1,\infty}
.
Without loss of generality,
\varphi(\{\frac{1}{n+1}\}_{n\geq 0})=1.

Set
y_{n}=x_{n}+\frac{\alpha}{n+1}
. Observe that
y_{n}\geq 0
for every
n\geq 0
.
By Lemma
3.15
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}y_{k}^{*}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}y_{k}+\alpha,\ n\geq 0.

Since
\sum_{k=0}^{n}x_{k}\leq 0
, it follows that

\sum_{k=0}^{n}y_{k}^{*}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\alpha}{k+1}+\alpha.

Setting
z_{0}:=\alpha
,
z_{k}:=0
(
k\geq 1
), we obtain

\sum_{k=0}^{n}y_{k}^{*}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}(\frac{\alpha}{k+1}+z_{k}).

Hence, using the fact that
\varphi(\{\frac{1}{n+1}\}_{n\geq 0})=1
we have

\varphi(y)\leq\varphi(\{\frac{\alpha}{n+1}\}_{n\geq 0})+\varphi(z)=\alpha.

To obtain the last equality we used the fact that every symmetric functional on
l_{1,\infty}
is singular,
that is it vanishes on finitely supported sequences
(alternatively, one can use Theorem
3.20
and the fact that Banach limits vanish on finitely supported sequences).

Hence,
\varphi(x)\leq 0
.
∎

It was established in

that every normalised fully symmetric functional can be written in the form (

with some dilation invariant extended limit
\omega
.
The following theorem shows that every normalised fully symmetric functional can be written in the form (

with some factorisable Banach limit

Theorem 3.23
.

(i) For every normalised fully symmetric functional
\varphi
on

there exists
an extended limit
\gamma
on
l_{\infty}
such that (

holds for
\theta=\gamma\circ C

(ii) For every extended limit
\gamma
on
l_{\infty}
and
\theta=\gamma\circ C

the functional
\varphi
defined by the formula (
5
) extends by linearity to a normalised fully symmetric functional on
l_{1,\infty}

Proof.

(i) Let
\varphi
be a normalised fully symmetric functional on
l_{1,\infty}
.
Hence,
\varphi
is a normalised positive symmetric functional on
l_{1,\infty}
.
By Theorem
3.20
, there exists a unique Banach limit
B
on
l_{\infty}
given by the formula
B=\varphi\circ D
.
We need to show that
B
can be expressed as
B=\gamma\circ C
for some extended limit
\gamma
on
l_{\infty}
.

For every
x\in l_{\infty}
and
2^{m}-1\leq n\leq 2^{m+1}-2
(
m\geq 0
) it follows from the definition of the operator
D
that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}(Dx)_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{m}-2}(Dx)_{k}+\sum_{k=2^{m}-1}^{n}(Dx)_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}x_{k}+\frac{n-2^{m}+2}{2^{m}}x_{m}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}x_{k},\sum_{k=0}^{m}x_{k}\right\}.

In particular, it follows from the above, that for every
x\in l_{\infty}
such that
Cx\leq 0
, we have
CDx\leq 0
and, by Lemma
3.22

(we can apply this lemma since
\|D\|_{l_{\infty}\to l_{1,\infty}}=2\log 2
and so,
(Dx)_{n}\leq\frac{2\log 2}{n+1}\|x\|_{l_{\infty}}
),
since
\varphi
is positive, it follows that
\varphi(Dx)\leq 0
.
Consequently,
B(x)=\varphi(Dx)\leq 0
for every
x\in l_{\infty}
such that
Cx\leq 0
.

We have
C(x-\sup_{n\geq 0}(Cx)_{n})\leq 0
for every
x\in l_{\infty}
.
Hence,

B(x-\sup_{n\geq 0}(Cx)_{n})\leq 0
and

B(x)\leq\sup_{n\geq 0}(Cx)_{n}

(7)

for every
x\in l_{\infty}
.

Denote by
C(l_{\infty})
the range of the Cesàro operator
C:l_{\infty}\to l_{\infty}
.
Since
Cx=Cy\Leftrightarrow x=y
, the operator
C:l_{\infty}\to C(l_{\infty})
is a bijection.

Define a linear functional
\gamma
on
C(l_{\infty})
by the formula

\gamma(x):=B(C^{-1}x),\ x\in C(l_{\infty}).

For every
x\in C(l_{\infty})
, using (
7
), we have that

\gamma(x)=B(C^{-1}x)\leq\sup_{n\geq 0}(CC^{-1}x)_{n}=\sup_{n\geq 0}x_{n}.

Using Hahn-Banach theorem we extend
\gamma
from
C(l_{\infty})
to
l_{\infty}
preserving the inequality
\gamma\leq\sup.

For every
x\in l_{\infty}
, we have

-\gamma(x)=\gamma(-x)\leq\sup_{n}(-x_{n})=-\inf_{n}x_{n}.

Hence,

\inf_{n}x_{n}\leq\gamma(x)\leq\sup_{n}x_{n}.

(8)

Hence
\gamma
is a positive norm-one functional on
l_{\infty}
.

It remains to show that
\gamma
is an extended limit.
Due to (
8
), it is sufficient to show that
\gamma
vanishes on every sequence with finite support.

For every sequence
x\in l_{\infty}
a direct verification shows that
(C^{-1}x)_{0}=x_{0}
and

(C^{-1}x)_{n}=(n+1)x_{n}-nx_{n-1},\ n\geq 1.

Hence, if

is a sequence with finite support we conclude,
that

is also a sequence with finite support.
Hence,
\gamma(x)=B(C^{-1}x)=0
, since
B
is a Banach limit.

Consequently,
\gamma
is an extended limit.
Finally, for every
x\in l_{\infty}
we trivially have
Cx\in C(l_{\infty})
and

B(x)=B(C^{-1}Cx)=\gamma(Cx).

(ii) Let
\theta=\gamma\circ C
for some extended limit
\gamma
on
l_{\infty}
.
Since
C\circ S-C:l_{\infty}\to c_{0}
and since
\gamma
is an extended limit, it follows that
\theta
is
S
-invariant.
Hence, by Theorem
3.17
the formula (
5
) defines a symmetric functional
\varphi
on
l_{1,\infty}
.
We only need to show that the functional
\varphi
is fully symmetric.

Let
0\leq x,y\in l_{1,\infty}
be such that
x\prec\prec y
.
We have

\left(C\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)_{m}=\frac{1}{m+1}\sum_{n=0}^{m}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}=\frac{1}{m+1}\sum_{n=0}^{2^{m+1}-2}x_{n}^{*}

and (since
x\prec\prec y
)

\left(C\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)_{m}\leq\left(C\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}y_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)_{m},\ \forall\ m\geq 0.

Next by (
5
), using the linearity of
\varphi
and since
\theta=\gamma\circ C
, we write

\displaystyle\varphi(x-y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log 2}\theta\left(\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}-\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}y_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log 2}\gamma\left(C\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}-C\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}y_{k}^{*}\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 0,

where the latter inequality is due to the positivity of
\gamma
(see Remark

).
Consequently,
\varphi(x)\leq\varphi(y)
, that is,
\varphi
is a fully symmetric functional.
∎

(i) For every positive normalised trace

Now we show that not every trace on

\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}

\|A\|_{\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}}:=\sup_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{\log(n+2)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mu(k,A),\quad A\in\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}.

For a given

Lemma 4.28
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have
A_{n}\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
.
Moreover,
\|A_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}}\leq 1
, however
\|A_{n}\|_{\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}}\leq 2/n
.

Proof.

Since the sequence
\{\mu(m,A_{n})\}_{m\geq 0}
is piecewise constant, it follows that the supremum of
m\cdot\mu(m,A_{n})
attains at the right endpoints of the intervals of constancy. That is,

\sup_{m}m\cdot\mu(m,A_{n})=\sup_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}:j\leq i}2^{j+n(i+2)^{2}}\mu(2^{j+n(i+2)^{2}},A_{n})=1

and, so,
\|A_{n}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}}\leq 1
.

For every
m\in\mathbb{N}
select
k\geq 0
such that

m\in[2^{n(k+2)^{2}},2^{n(k+3)^{2}}),
then

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{m}\mu(l,A_{n})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=0}^{2^{n(k+3)^{2}}-1}\mu(l,A_{n})=1+\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{l=2^{n(i+2)^{2}}}^{2^{n(i+3)^{2}}-1}\mu(l,A_{n})

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{i=0}^{k}i\leq(k+2)^{2}.

Due to the choice of

we have that
(k+2)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{n}\log_{2}(m+2)
.
Hence,

\sum_{l=0}^{m}\mu(l,A_{n})\leq\frac{1}{n}\log_{2}(m+2)\leq\frac{2}{n}\log(2+m).

∎

Recall that
\mathcal{PT}
denotes the set of all positive normalised traces on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}

Lemma 4.29
.

We have

\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{PT}}\tau(A_{n})\geq\frac{1}{2\log 2}.

Proof.

It was proved in
[
54
]
that for every

, we have

\sup_{B\in\mathfrak{B}}Bx=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=m}^{k+m-1}x_{i},\ x\in l_{\infty}.

Moreover, the supremum on the left-hand side is attained.

From Theorem
4.25
for every positive operator

we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{PT}}\tau(A)

\displaystyle=\sup_{B\in\mathfrak{B}}B\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\left\{\sum_{i=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\mu(i,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log 2}\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=2^{m}-1}^{2^{k+m}-2}\mu(i,A).

For fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for every

we set

\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{PT}}\tau(A)\geq\frac{1}{\log 2}\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{l=2^{n(k+2)^{2}}}^{2^{k+n(k+2)^{2}-1}}\mu(l,A).

The assertion follows immediately.

There is a positive trace on

Proof.

Consider the operators

constructed above. By Lemma

The following theorem describes the correspondence

Theorem 4.33
.

A trace
\tau\in ext(\mathcal{PT})
if and only if

B=\tau\circ{\rm diag}\circ D\in ext\mathfrak{B}
.

Proof.

Let
\tau\in\mathcal{PT}
and let
B=\tau\circ{\rm diag}\circ D\in ext\mathfrak{B}
. Suppose that

\tau=\frac{1}{2}(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})\ \text{ on }\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty},

(11)

for some
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{PT}
.

It follows from Corollary
4.25
,
that
B:=\tau\circ{\rm diag}\circ D
,
B_{1}:=\tau_{1}\circ{\rm diag}\circ D
,
B_{2}:=\tau_{2}\circ{\rm diag}\circ D
are Banach limits.
Moreover, we obtain from (
11
) that
B=\frac{1}{2}(B_{1}+B_{2})
on
l_{\infty}
and, due to the assumption
B\in ext\mathfrak{B}
we have
B=B_{1}=B_{2}
.
Hence, formula (
9
) yields
\tau=\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}
and
\tau\in ext(\mathcal{PT})
.

Let now
\tau\in ext(\mathcal{PT})
and let
B=\tau\circ{\rm diag}\circ D\in\mathfrak{B}
. Suppose

B=\frac{1}{2}(B_{1}+B_{2})\ \text{ on }l_{\infty},

(12)

for some
B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathfrak{B}
.

Due to Corollary
4.25
applying the formula (
9
) to
Banach limits
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
yields positive normalised traces
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
.
It follows from (
12
) that
\tau=\frac{1}{2}(\tau_{1}+\tau_{2})
on
l_{1,\infty}
and, so
\tau=\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}
.
Hence,
B=B_{1}=B_{2}
and

.
∎

It was shown in
[
51
]
that every sequence
B_{i}
,

, of distinct extreme points of
\mathfrak{B}
spans the space
l_{1}
of all summable sequences.
The following theorem is an analogue of this result for positive normalised traces on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
.

Theorem 4.34
.

Let
\tau_{k}\in ext(\mathcal{PT})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, be a sequence of distinct elements.
For every
\{c_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\in l_{1}
we have

\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}\tau_{k}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}^{*}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|c_{k}|.

Proof.

The assertion follows from the fact that the lattices
\mathcal{PT}
and
\mathfrak{B}
are order isomorphic and isometric
(Theorem
4.32
) and the corresponding result for Banach limits
[
51
, Theorem 4]
.
∎

Define the diameter of the set
\mathcal{PT}
in
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}^{*}
as follows

d(\mathcal{PT},\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}^{*})=\sup\limits_{\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{PT}}\|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}^{*}}.

The set
\mathcal{PT}
is a subset of the unit sphere of
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}^{*}
and
so, its diameter can not exceed
2
.
The following result shows, in particular, that
d(\mathcal{PT},\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}^{*})=2
.
It is a straightforward consequence of Theorem
4.34
.

Corollary 4.35
.

For every
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in ext(\mathcal{PT})
such that
\tau_{1}\neq\tau_{2}

we have

\|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}^{*}}=2.

Although the norm of a difference of two exteme points of
\mathcal{PT}
equals 2, it is not always attained.
We state this fact rigorously in Corollary
4.37
below. To this end we need some preparations.

It is well-known (see e.g.
[
24
, Theorem 9]
and
[
8
, Chapter 16]
) that by the Riesz representation theorem every positive normalised linear functional
l
on
l_{\infty}

can be be written in the following form

l(x)=\int_{\beta\mathbb{N}}x(p)d\nu(p),\ \forall\ x\in l_{\infty}

where
\nu
is a measure on
\beta\mathbb{N}
, the Stone-Čech compactification of
\mathbb{N}
.
Moreover, according to
[
20
, 436J, 436K]
this measure is Radon.

It was proved in
[
9
, Proposition 2.5]
that there exist
B_{1},B_{2}\in ext(\mathfrak{B})
(with corresponding probability measures
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
)
such that
{\rm supp}\ \nu_{1}\subset{\rm supp}\ \nu_{2}
(the support of
\nu
is well-defined, since the measures are Radon).

Theorem 4.36
.

There exist
B_{1},B_{2}\in ext(\mathfrak{B})
such that

|B_{1}x-B_{2}x|<2
for every
x\in l_{\infty}
with
\|x\|_{l_{\infty}}\leq 1
.

Proof.

By
[
9
, Proposition 2.5]
there exist
B_{1},B_{2}\in ext(\mathfrak{B})
such that
{\rm supp}\ \nu_{1}\subset{\rm supp}\ \nu_{2}
,
where
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
are the probability measures on
\beta\mathbb{N}
corresponding to
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, that is

B_{1}x=\int_{\beta\mathbb{N}}x(p)d\nu_{1}(p),\ B_{2}x=\int_{\beta\mathbb{N}}x(p)d\nu_{2}(p),\ \ x\in l_{\infty}.

Denote
S_{1}:={\rm supp}\ \nu_{1}
and
S_{2}:={\rm supp}\ \nu_{2}
. We have
S_{1}\subset S_{2}
.
Fix
x\in l_{\infty}
with
\|x\|_{l_{\infty}}\leq 1
. Define the closed sets

C:=\{p\in\beta\mathbb{N}:x(p)\geq 0\},\ D:=\{p\in\beta\mathbb{N}:x(p)\leq 0\}.

We have

\displaystyle B_{1}x-B_{2}x

\displaystyle=\int_{S_{1}\cap C}x(p)d\nu_{1}(p)+\int_{S_{1}\cap D}x(p)d\nu_{1}(p)

\displaystyle-\int_{S_{2}\cap C}x(p)d\nu_{2}(p)-\int_{S_{2}\cap D}x(p)d\nu_{2}(p).

Assume that
B_{1}x-B_{2}x=2
. We obtain

\displaystyle 2

\displaystyle=B_{1}x-B_{2}x\leq\int_{S_{1}\cap C}x(p)d\nu_{1}(p)-\int_{S_{2}\cap D}x(p)d\nu_{2}(p)

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|_{l_{\infty}}(\nu_{1}(S_{1}\cap C)+\nu_{2}(S_{2}\cap D))\leq 2,

since
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
are probability measures.

Therefore,
\nu_{1}(S_{1}\cap C)=\nu_{2}(S_{2}\cap D)=1
.
Since the support of a measure is the smallest closed set of a full measure, it follows that
S_{1}\cap C=S_{1}
and
S_{2}\cap D=S_{2}
.

Since
S_{1}\subset S_{2}
, it follows that

(S_{1}\cap C)\cap D=S_{1}\cap D=(S_{1}\cap S_{2})\cap D=S_{1}\cap(S_{2}\cap D)=S_{1}\cap S_{2}=S_{1}.

Consequently,
x=0
on
S_{1}
, the support of
\nu_{1}
. Hence,
B_{1}x=0
and
B_{1}x-B_{2}x<2
,
which contradicts the assumption.
∎

Corollary 4.37
.

There exist
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in ext(\mathcal{PT})
such that

|\tau_{1}(A)-\tau_{2}(A)|<2
for every
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
with
\|A\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}}\leq 1
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.36
there exist
B_{1},B_{2}\in ext(\mathfrak{B})
such that

|B_{1}x-B_{2}x|<2
for every
x\in l_{\infty}
with
\|x\|_{l_{\infty}}\leq 1
.

Let
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathcal{PT}
be traces corresponding to
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
(by Theorem
4.24
).
Theorem
4.33
yields
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in ext(\mathcal{PT})
.

By formula (
9
) we obtain

\displaystyle|\tau_{1}(A)-\tau_{2}(A)|=\left|(B_{1}-B_{2})\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\mu(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)\right|.

Since

\left\|\frac{1}{\log 2}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\mu(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right\|_{l_{\infty}}\leq 1

for every
\|A\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}}\leq 1
,
the assertion follows from Theorem

.

∎

It follows from
[
59
]
(see also
[
29
,
41
]
) that the set

is not
\sigma(l_{\infty}^{*},l_{\infty})
-closed.
As a straightforward consequence of Theorem

we obtain that the set
ext(\mathcal{PT})
is not
\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}^{*},\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty})
-closed.
It is known that the set
\mathfrak{B}
is convex and
\sigma(l_{\infty}^{*},l_{\infty})
-compact. Hence, by the Krein-Milman theorem we have

\mathfrak{B}=\overline{conv(ext(\mathfrak{B}))}^{\sigma(l_{\infty}^{*},l_{\infty})},

where

It is also easy to see that the set

is convex and
\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}^{*},\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty})
-compact
and by the Krein-Milman theorem

\mathcal{PT}=\overline{conv(ext(\mathcal{PT}))}^{\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}^{*},\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty})},

that is for every

there is a net
\tau_{\alpha}\in conv(ext(\mathcal{PT}))

such that
\tau_{\alpha}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{w^{*}}\tau.

The following theorem show that the previous statement fails if one changes nets to sequences.

Theorem 4.38
.

Let
\tau\in\mathcal{D}_{M}
.
The set
conv(ext(\mathcal{PT}))
does not contain a sequence which is
\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}^{*},\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty})
-convergent to

.

Proof.

Assume the contrary, that is, there exists a sequence

such that

\tau_{n}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{w^{*}}\tau.

Corollary
4.25
yields that the functionals

are Banach limits.

we also have that

.

For every

we have

|B_{n}(x)-B(x)|=|\tau_{n}({\rm diag}(Dx))-\tau({\rm diag}(Dx))|.

Since

, the right-hand side tends to zero.
Hence,

B_{n}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{w^{*}}B

which contradicts the result of
[
51
, Theorem 12]
(in the view of Theorem

below).
∎

5
Subclasses of positive normalised traces

In this section we describe various classes of Dixmier traces on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
, which are of importance in noncommutative geometry (see e.g.
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
58
]
,
in terms of the functional
\theta
from (
9
).

Definition 5.40
.

A trace
\tau
on
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
is called a Dixmier trace if
it is a linear extension of the weight

{\rm Tr}_{\omega}(A):=\omega\left(t\mapsto\frac{1}{\log(1+t)}\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s,A)ds\right),\quad 0\leq A\in\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty},

for some dilation invariant extended limit
\omega
on
L_{\infty}
.

This definition is equivalent to the original construction (
2
) of Dixmier.

Definition 5.41
.

A trace
\tau
on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is called a Dixmier trace if
it is a restriction of a Dixmier trace on
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
,
that is linear extension of a weight

{\rm Tr}_{\omega}(A):=\omega\left(t\mapsto\frac{1}{\log(1+t)}\int_{0}^{t}\mu(s,A)ds\right),\quad 0\leq A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty},

for some dilation invariant extended limit
\omega
on
L_{\infty}
.

Despite the fact (Theorem
4.30
) that not every positive trace on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
extends to a positive trace on
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
,
it follows directly from Definitions
5.40
and
5.41
that
every Dixmier trace on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
extends to a Dixmier trace
\tau
on
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
.

In Section
3
we have discussed fully symmetric functionals on
l_{1,\infty}
.
Now we define fully symmetric functionals on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}

and study their relation to Dixmier traces (on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
).

Definition 5.42
.

A linear functional
\varphi
on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is called fully symmetric if

\varphi(A)\leq\varphi(B)
for every
0\leq A,B\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
such that
A\prec\prec B
,
that is
\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mu(k,A)\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mu(k,B)
for every
n\geq 0
.

The following interesting result is proved in
[
27
]
.

Theorem 5.43
.

A trace on
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
is a Dixmier trace if and only if it is a normalised fully symmetric functional on
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
.

A natural question arises: Does a similar result hold for the ideal
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
?

In Theorem
5.45
we show that every fully symmetric functional on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}

extends to a fully symmetric functional on
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
.
Using this powerful result we show below that the class of Dixmier traces on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}

coincides with the class of all normalised fully symmetric functionals on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
.

We shall use the classical G. G. Lorentz and T. Shimogaki result
[
38
, Theorem 1]
.

Theorem 5.44
.

If
g_{1},g_{2},f
are positive locally integrable functions such that
g_{1}+g_{2}\prec\prec f
, then
there exist positive functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}

such that
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
and
g_{1}\prec\prec f_{1}
,
g_{2}\prec\prec f_{2}
.

Theorem 5.45
.

Every fully symmetric functional on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}

extends to a fully symmetric functional on
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a fully symmetric functional on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
.
Set

\varphi^{\prime}(A):=\inf\left\{\varphi(B):0\leq B\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty},A\prec\prec B\right\},\ 0\leq A\in\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}.

(13)

It is clear that
\varphi^{\prime}
is a positive homogeneous functional on the positive cone of
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
.
We shall show that
\varphi^{\prime}
is additive on the positive cone of
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
.

By Theorems 3.3.3 and 3.3.4 from
[
36
]
we have

A_{1}\oplus A_{2}\prec\prec A_{1}+A_{2}\prec\prec 2\sigma_{1/2}\mu(A_{1}\oplus A_{2}),

(14)

where
\oplus
is the direct sum operation defined as in
[
36
, Definition 2.4.3]

and
\sigma_{1/2}
is a dilation operator.
Due to the definition of the direct sum operation we have

\varphi(B_{1}+B_{2})=\varphi(B_{1}\oplus B_{2})

every positive
B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
.

Hence, using the properties of fully symmetric functionals and (
14
) we infer that

\varphi^{\prime}(A_{1}+A_{2})=\varphi^{\prime}(A_{1}\oplus A_{2}).

for every positive
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
.

It should be pointed out that the Lorentz-Shimogaki’s result is “commutative”
and can not be applied in the noncommutative setting.
However, it can be applied if one changes the sum of operators to their direct sum.

Fix positive
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
.
By Lorentz-Shimogaki’s result for positive operators
A_{1},A_{2},B
such that
A_{1}\oplus A_{2}\prec\prec B

there exist positive operators
B_{1}
and
B_{2}

such that
B=B_{1}+B_{2}
and
A_{1}\prec\prec B_{1}
,
A_{2}\prec\prec B_{2}
.

Hence, due to the property of infimum we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(A_{1}+A_{2})

\displaystyle=\varphi^{\prime}(A_{1}\oplus A_{2})

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\varphi(B):0\leq B\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty},A_{1}\oplus A_{2}\prec\prec B\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\inf\left\{\varphi(B_{1}+B_{2}):0\leq B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty},A_{1}\prec\prec B_{1},A_{2}\prec\prec B_{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\varphi(B_{1}):0\leq B_{1}\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty},A_{1}\prec\prec B_{1}\right\}

\displaystyle+\inf\left\{\varphi(B_{2}):0\leq B_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty},A_{2}\prec\prec B_{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\varphi^{\prime}(A_{1})+\varphi^{\prime}(A_{2}).

Now we prove the converse inequality.

Due to the definition of
\varphi^{\prime}
(
13
) for every
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
and every
\varepsilon>0

there are
0\leq B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
such that
A_{1}\prec\prec B_{1}
,
A_{2}\prec\prec B_{2}
and

\varphi(B_{1})\leq\varphi^{\prime}(A_{1})+\varepsilon\ \text{and}\ \varphi(B_{2})\leq\varphi^{\prime}(A_{2})+\varepsilon.

Due to the choice of
B_{1},B_{2}
and (
14
), we obtain

A_{1}\oplus A_{2}\prec\prec B_{1}\oplus B_{2}\prec\prec B_{1}+B_{2}.

Hence,

\varphi^{\prime}(A_{1}+A_{2})=\varphi^{\prime}(A_{1}\oplus A_{2})\leq\varphi(B_{1}+B_{2})\leq\varphi^{\prime}(A_{1})+\varphi^{\prime}(A_{2})+2\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
can be chosen arbitrary small, it follows that

\varphi^{\prime}(A_{1}+A_{2})\leq\varphi^{\prime}(A_{1})+\varphi^{\prime}(A_{2}).

Consequently, the functional
\varphi^{\prime}
is positive homogeneous and additive on the positive cone of
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
.
So, it extends to a linear functional of
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
.
Due to the construction (
13
)
\varphi^{\prime}
is a fully symmetric functional on
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}

which coincides with
\varphi
on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
.
∎

Corollary 5.46
.

The class of all Dixmier traces on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
and that of all normalised fully symmetric functionals on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
coincide.

Proof.

It follows directly from the construction of Dixmier trace (on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
)
that every Dixmier trace is a normalised fully symmetric functional on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
.

Conversely, by Theorem
5.45
every normalised fully symmetric functional
\varphi
on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}

extends to a normalised fully symmetric functional on
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
,
that is, due to Theorem
5.43
, to Dixmier trace, say
{\rm Tr}_{\omega}
, on
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
.
The restriction of the Dixmier trace
{\rm Tr}_{\omega}
to
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
coincide with the original functional
\varphi
.
However, this restriction is a Dixmier trace on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
, by Definition
5.41
.
∎

Proof.

(i) Let
\tau

]

(see

, we obtain

which proves the assertion of the first part of the theorem.

(ii) A direct verification shows that for every extended limit
\gamma
on

the functional
\theta=\gamma\circ C

Hence, by Corollary
4.25
the functional
\tau
defined by the formula (

)

We have

\displaystyle\tau(A)

\displaystyle=(\gamma\circ C)\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\mu(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)

(15)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log 2}\gamma\left(\left\{\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n+1}-2}\mu(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right).

Let

for every

.
Due to the positivity of

) yields
\tau(A)\leq\tau(B)
.
Therefore
\tau
is a fully symmetric functional on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
.
The assertion follows from Theorem
5.46
.
∎

Next we characterise the Connes-Dixmier traces and the class
\mathcal{D}_{M}
, in terms of Banach limits of special types.
To this end we need some preparations.

Recall that
\pi
denotes the isometric embedding
\pi
of
l_{\infty}
into

given by the formula (

6
Lidskii Formula

In the present section we first prove the Lidskii formula for self-adjoint operators
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
,
then, using Ringrose’s representation
[
48
, Theorems 1,6,7]
of compact operators, we extend the formula to an arbitrary
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
.

The following elementary lemma will be frequently used in this and subsequent sections.

Lemma 6.55
.

For every
x\in l_{\infty}
such that
\sum_{k=0}^{n}x_{k}=O(1)
, there exists a sequence
y\in l_{\infty}
such that

\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}\right\}_{n\geq 0}=\{y-Sy\}_{n\geq 0}.

In particular, every translation invariant functional on
l_{\infty}
vanishes on the sequence
\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}\right\}_{n\geq 0}
.

Proof.

Setting
y_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}-2}x_{k}
, we have

\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n+1}-2}x_{k}-\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}-2}x_{k}=y_{n+1}-y_{n}=(y-Sy)_{n+1}.

∎

The following theorem is a Lidskii formula for traces on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
and for self-adjoint operators.

Theorem 6.56
.

Let
A=A^{*}\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
. For every trace
\tau
on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}

with the corresponding (by Theorem
4.24
)
S
-invariant functional
\theta
on
l_{\infty}
the following identity holds

\tau(A)=\theta\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right).

Proof.

By Theorem
4.24
and the linearity of the trace we have

\tau(A)=\tau(A_{+})-\tau(A_{-})=\theta\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\mu(k,A_{+})-\mu(k,A_{-})\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right).

(23)

By
[
36
, Lemma 5.2.7]
for every compact self-adjoint operator
A
the following estimate holds

\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lambda(k,A)-\mu(k,A_{+})+\mu(k,A_{-})\right|\leq 2(n+1)\mu(n,A),\ n\geq 0.

Hence, if
A=A^{*}\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
, then the right-hand side of the latter inequality is majorized by
2\|A\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}}
.
So, by Lemma
6.55
every
S
-invariant functional on
l_{\infty}
equals zero on the sequence

\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)-\mu(k,A_{+})+\mu(k,A_{-})\right\}_{n\geq 0}

Therefore,

\theta\left(\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)=\theta\left(\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\mu(k,A_{+})-\mu(k,A_{-})\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)

for every
S
-invariant functional
\theta
on
l_{\infty}
.
Combining the latter equality with (

) we obtain the required assertion.
∎

Theorem 6.57

\tau(A)=\tau(N)=\tau(\Re(N))+i\tau(\Im(N)),

(where

\tau(A)=\theta\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,\Re(N))+i\lambda(k,\Im(N))\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right).

\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lambda(k,N)-\lambda(k,\Re(N))-i\lambda(k,\Im(N))\right|\leq 5n\mu(n,N).

Hence, for
N\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
the right-hand side is majorized by
5\|N\|_{\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}}
.
Lemma
6.55
now yields that every
S
-invariant functional on
l_{\infty}
equals zero on the sequence

\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,N)-\lambda(k,\Re(N))-i\lambda(k,\Im(N))\right\}_{n\geq 0}.

Therefore, for every
S
-invariant functional
\theta
on
l_{\infty}
we have

\theta\left(\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,N)\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)=\theta\left(\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,\Re(N))+i\lambda(k,\Im(N))\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right).

Combining the latter equality with (
24
) we obtain the claim.

∎

Using the results of Corollary
4.25
together with those of Theorems
5.47
,
5.52
,
5.54
and the Lidskii formula
from the preceeding section (Theorem
6.57
), we can easily infer criteria for measurability of operators within
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
with respect to various subclasses

\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}

Definition 7.58
.

Let
\mathcal{A}

Propositions
7.59
,
7.60
,
7.62
,
7.65
provide definitive results, in terms of eigenvalue sequences, concerning

),

For the ideal

these results strengthen and complete corresponding results from
[

,

,
56
,
57

.

The following theorem resolves (in the class of positive normalized traces) an open problem discussed in
[
7
, p. 1061]
.
In fact, it appears that the class
\mathcal{PT}
is the largest class of traces for which the meaningful description
of the corresponding measurable elements is possible.

Proposition 7.59
.

An operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is
\mathcal{PT}
-measurable if and only if
the sequence

\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}

is almost convergent.
Here
\{\lambda(n,A)\}_{n\geq 0}
is any eigenvalue sequence of
A
.

Proof.

An operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is
\mathcal{PT}
-measurable if and only if

\tau(A)=a
for every positive normalised trace
\tau
on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
.
By Corollary
4.25
and Theorem
6.57
, the previous statement is equivalent to the fact that

B\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)=a

for every Banach limit
B
. The assertion follows now from Definition
2.9
.
∎

It is shown in Corollary
5.46
that the classes of Dixmier traces (on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
)
and normalised fully symmetric functionals on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
coincide.
Hence, the following theorem also resolves (for the class
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
) an open problem (iii) stated
[
7
, p. 1061]
,
concerning the measurability with respect to the class of all normalised fully symmetric functionals.

Proposition 7.60
.

An operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is Dixmier-measurable if and only if
the sequence

C\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}

is convergent.
Here
\{\lambda(n,A)\}_{n\geq 0}
is any eigenvalue sequence of
A
.

Proof.

An operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is
\mathcal{D}
-measurable if and only if

\tau(A)=a
for every Dixmier trace
\tau
on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
.
By Theorems
5.47
and
6.57
, the previous statement is equivalent to the fact that

(\gamma\circ C)\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)=a

for every extended limit
\gamma
. The assertion follows from the Remark
2.5
.
∎

The following result shows that the concepts of Dixmier and
\mathcal{PT}
-measurability
differ even on the positive cone of
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
.

Theorem 7.61
.

The class of
\mathcal{D}
-measurable operators is strictly wider than the class of
\mathcal{PT}
-measurable operators.

Proof.

Consider the sequence

y=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\chi_{[2^{n},2^{n}+n]}+\mathrm{1\hskip-3.00003ptI}\in l_{\infty}.

It is easy to check that
(Cy)_{n}\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{n\to\infty}1
and
y
is not almost convergent.
Since
y_{n}\geq\frac{y_{n+1}}{2}
for every
n\geq 0
, it follows that
\frac{y_{n}}{2^{n}}\geq\frac{y_{n+1}}{2^{n+1}}
and
(Dy)^{*}=Dy
.

For

\lambda(A)=(Dy)
.

Using the definition of the operator

, we obtain

\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)=\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}(Dy)_{k}=y_{n}.

By Propositions
7.59
and
7.60
we obtain that the operator
A
is
\mathcal{D}
-measurable,
but
A
is not
\mathcal{PT}
-measurable.
∎

The following proposition characterises Connes-Dixmier measurability of an operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}

in terms of its eigenvalue sequence.

Proposition 7.62
.

An operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is Connes-Dixmier measurable if and only if
the sequence

C^{2}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}

is convergent.
Here
\{\lambda(n,A)\}_{n\geq 0}
is any eigenvalue sequence of
A
.

Proof.

An operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is
\mathcal{C}
-measurable if and only if

\tau(A)=a
for every Connes-Dixmier trace
\tau
on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
.
By Theorem
5.52
and Theorem
6.57
, the previous statement is equivalent to the fact that

(\gamma\circ C^{2})\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)=a

for every extended limit
\gamma
. The assertion follows from Remark
2.5
.
∎

To prove the main result of this section we need Hardy’s Tauberian theorem for Cesàro summability (see, e.g.
[
22
, Chapter 6.8]
).

Theorem 7.63
.

If
x\in l_{\infty}
is such that the sequence
\{n(x_{n}-x_{n-1})\}_{n\geq 1}
is bounded from below,
then the sequence
Cx
is convergent if and only if the sequence
x
is convergent.

The result of the following theorem complements Theorem 3.7 from
[

]
. The cited
theorem showed the coincidence of the sets of positive Dixmier- and Connes-Dixmier measurable operators from
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
.
On the smaller ideal
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
the condition of positivity can be dropped.

The following result resolves in the affirmative the problem (i) stated in
[
7
, p. 1061]
(in the ideal
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
).

Theorem 7.64
.

An operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is Connes-Dixmier measurable if and only if it is Dixmier-measurable.

Proof.

The condition of
\mathcal{D}
-measurability evidently implies
\mathcal{C}
-measurability.

Let an operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
be
\mathcal{C}
-measurable, that is by Proposition
7.62

the sequence

C^{2}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}

is convergent.
We have to show that the operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is
\mathcal{D}
-measurable, or equivalently by Proposition
7.60
, that
the sequence

C\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}

is convergent.

To this end, we need to show that for every
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
the sequence

C\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}

.

Indeed, a direct verification shows that for every
y\in l_{\infty}
the following estimate holds

n((Cy)_{n}-(Cy)_{n-1})=y_{n}-(Cy)_{n}\geq-2\|y\|_{l_{\infty}}.

∎

The following result should be compared with
[
52
, Corollary 21]
, describing the set of

\mathcal{D}_{M}
-measurable operators from
\mathcal{M}_{1,\infty}
.

Proposition 7.65
.

An operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is
\mathcal{D}_{M}
-measurable if and only if

\lim_{m\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}C^{m}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}C^{m}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}.

Here
\{\lambda(n,A)\}_{n\geq 0}
is any eigenvalue sequence of
A
.

Proof.

From Theorem
5.54
and Theorem
6.57
we obtain that the operator
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}
is
\mathcal{D}_{M}
-measurable
if and only if all Cesàro invariant Banach limits (that is
B=B\circ C
) take the same value on the sequence
\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-1}^{2^{n+1}-2}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}
.
By
[
52

all Cesàro invariant Banach limits take the same value on the sequence
x\in l_{\infty}
if and only

\lim_{m\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}(C^{m}x)_{n}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}(C^{m}x)_{n},

∎

The following theorem shows that Dixmier-measurability

The class of

.

Since
y_{n}\geq\frac{y_{n+1}}{2}

8
Application to pseudo-differential operators

Connes’ trace theorem,
[
10
, Theorem 1]
, states that a Dixmier trace applied to a compactly supported classical pseudo-differential operator of order
-d

yields the Wodzicki’s residue up to a constant.
This enables the Dixmier trace of any compactly supported classical pseudo-differential operator of order
-d
to be calculated from its symbol.

In this section, with the aid of the results established, we provide a version of Connes’ trace theorem for positive normalised traces.
Following the ideas of
[
26
]
(see also
[
36
]
) we introduce the class of so-called Laplacian modulated operators
and the residue mapping
{\rm Res}
, which extends the Wodzicki’s residue.

Let us first give a definition of a pseudo-differential operator, see e.g.
[
36
, Definition 10.2.6]
.

Definition 8.67
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
.
A function
p\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying the condition

\sup_{x,s}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{s}^{\beta}p(x,s)|(1+|s|^{2})^{\frac{|\beta|-m}{2}}<\infty

for every multi-indices
\alpha,\beta\in(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{d}
is called
a symbol of order
m
.

In general terminology, we have just defined the uniform symbol of

Hörmander type
(1,0)
, see e.g.
[
23
]
and
[
49
, Chapter 2]
.

By
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we denote the space of Schwartz functions (the smooth functions of rapid decay).

Definition 8.68
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
and let
p
be a symbol of order
m
.
The operator
A:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
given by the formula

(Au)(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\langle x-y,s\rangle}p(x,s)u(y)\ dy\ ds,\ u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

is called a pseudo-differential operator of order
m
.

Definition 8.69
.

A pseudo-differential operator
A
of order
m
is called classical if its symbol has an asymptotic expansion

p\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}p_{m-j},

where each
p_{m-j}:=p_{m-j}(x,s)
is a symbol of order
m-j
and is a homogeneous function of order
m-j
in the variable
s\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
except near zero.

Next we introduce a pseudo-differential operator of a particular type.
For a smooth function with compact support
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we define the multiplication operator

(M_{\phi}f)(x)=\phi(x)f(x)
,
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Definition 8.70
.

A pseudo-differential operator
A:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is said to be compactly supported if

M_{\phi}AM_{\psi}=A

for some
\phi,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Pseudo-differential operators
A:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
associated to the class of symbols of order
m<0

generally do not extend to a compact linear operators
A:L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
However, by
[
36
, Theorem 10.2.22]
a compactly supported pseudo-differential operator
A
of order
m<0
extends to a compact linear operator
A:L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and a compactly supported pseudo-differential operator
A
of order
m<-d
extends to a trace class operator.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{2}
denote the class of Hilbert-Schmidt operators on the Hilbert space
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Let
\Delta=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}
be the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The following definitions were introduced in
[
26
]
.

Definition 8.71
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
. A bounded operator
A:L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

is called Laplacian modulated if

\sup_{t>0}t^{1/2}\|A(1+t(1-\Delta)^{-d/2})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}_{2}}<\infty.

If follows from the definition that every Laplacian modulated operator
A
is Hilbert-Schmidt,
so it has a unique symbol in
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
denoted by
p_{A}
.

By
[
36
, Theorem 11.3.17]
for every compactly supported pseudo-differential operator
A:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

of order
-d
its extension to a compact linear operator
A:L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is Laplacian modulated.

According to
[
36
, Remark 11.3.14]
an operator
A
on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is Laplacian modulated if and only its symbol
p_{A}

satisfies the condition

\sup_{t>0}(1+t)^{d/2}\left(\int_{|s|>t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|p_{A}(x,s)|^{2}\ dxds\right)^{1/2}<\infty.

(25)

It was shown in
[
26
, Lemma 6.12]
that, for every compactly supported Laplacian modulated operator
A
with symbol
p_{A}
,
the sequence

\left\{\frac{1}{\log(2+n)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|s|\leq n^{1/d}}p_{A}(x,s)ds\,dx\right\}_{n\geq 0}

is bounded. Therefore, the following definition makes sense.

Definition 8.72
.

The linear map

A\mapsto{\rm Res}(A):=\left[\frac{1}{\log(2+n)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|s|\leq n^{1/d}}p_{A}(x,s)ds\,dx\right]

from the set of all compactly supported Laplacian modulated operators to
\ell_{\infty}/c_{0}

is called the residue, where
[\cdot]
denotes the equivalence class in
\ell_{\infty}/c_{0}
.

Note that any sequence
\{{\rm Res}_{n}(A)\}_{n\geq 0}\in l_{\infty}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|s|\leq n^{1/d}}p_{A}(x,s)ds\,dx=\mathrm{Res}_{n}(A)\log n+o(\log n)

(26)

defines the residue
{\rm Res}(A)=[{\rm Res}_{n}(A)]\in\ell_{\infty}/c_{0}
.
In this section, by “scalars ”in
\ell_{\infty}/c_{0}
we mean the classes of convergent sequences. That is if
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, then
\alpha\equiv[a_{n}]
where
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=\alpha
.

The following result shows that the residue
{\rm Res}
is the extension of Wodzicki’s residue
{\rm Res}_{W}
, introduced in
[
60
]
.
It was proved in
[
26
, Proposition 6.16]
(see also
[
36
, Proposition 11.3.21]
).

Proposition 8.73
.

Let
P
be a compactly supported classical pseudo-differential operator of order
-d
. We have that

{\rm Res}(P)
is the scalar

{\rm Res}(P)={\rm Res}_{W}(P):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}p_{-d}(x,s)ds\,dx

where
{\rm Res}_{W}
denotes the Wodzicki’s residue and
p_{-d}
denotes the principal symbol of
P
.

The following generalisation of Connes’ trace theorem was proved in
[
26
, Theorem 6.32]
.

Theorem 8.74
.

Let
A
be a compactly supported Laplacian modulated operator with symbol
p_{A}
. We have
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. Moreover,

(i)

for a Dixmier trace
\mathrm{Tr}_{\omega}
,

\mathrm{Tr}_{\omega}(A)=\frac{1}{d(2\pi)^{d}}\omega({\rm Res}(A))

where
{\rm Res}(A)\in l_{\infty}/c_{0}
is the residue of
A
;

(ii)

\mathrm{Tr}_{\omega}(A)=\frac{1}{d(2\pi)^{d}}{\rm Res}(A)

for every Dixmier trace
{\rm Tr}_{\omega}
if and only if the residue
{\rm Res}(A)
is scalar;

(iii)

\tau(A)=\frac{1}{d(2\pi)^{d}}{\rm Res}(A)

for every normalised trace
\tau
on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

if and only if the residue
{\rm Res}(A)
is a scalar and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|s|\leq n^{1/d}}p_{A}(x,s)ds\,dx=\frac{1}{d}{\rm Res}(A)\,\log n+O(1).

(27)

The following result proved in
[
26
, Theorem 6.23]
lies at the heart of Connes’ trace theorem.

Theorem 8.75
.

Suppose
A:L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is compactly supported and Laplacian modulated.
We have that
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lambda(k,A)-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|s|\leq n^{1/d}}p_{A}(x,s)dsdx=O(1)

(28)

where
\{\lambda(k,A)\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is an eigenvalue sequence of
A
and
p_{A}
is the symbol of
A
.

A consequence of Theorem
8.74
part (iii) is that all traces on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}

applied to a classical pseudo-differential operator yield the same value,

[
26
, Corollary 6.35]
.
One of the reasons for generalising Connes’ trace theorem is to understand traces of pseudo-differential operators of order
-d

that are not classical pseudo-differential operators.
Theorem
6.57
provides an explicit formula for the positive trace of a compactly supported Laplacian modulated operator in terms of eigenvalues,
and therefore it stands to reason given Theorem
8.75
that we can obtain a formula for calculating

any
positive trace of a pseudo-differential operator of order
-d
using its symbol.
The following theorem is the main result of this section, it extends part (i) in Theorem
8.74
above
and complements parts (ii) and (iii). It should be compared with Theorem 11.5.1 in
[
36
]
.

Theorem 8.76
.

Let
A
be a compactly supported Laplacian modulated operator with symbol
p_{A}
. We have
A\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. Moreover,

(i)

for any normalised positive trace
\tau
,

\tau(A)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}\log 2}B\left(\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{2^{n/d}<|s|\leq 2^{(n+1)/d}}p_{A}(x,s)dsdx\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right),

where
B
is the Banach limit corresponding to
\tau
(given by Corollary
4.25
);

(ii)

the equality

\tau(A)=\frac{1}{d(2\pi)^{d}}{\rm Res}(A)

holds for every positive normalised trace
\tau
on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

if and only if the residue
{\rm Res}(A)
is a scalar and the sequence

\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{2^{\frac{n}{d}}<|s|\leq 2^{\frac{n+1}{d}}}p_{A}(x,s)ds\,dx\right\}_{n\geq 0}

(29)

is almost convergent to the number
\frac{1}{d}\log 2\cdot{\rm Res}(A)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
8.75
we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(\lambda(k,A)-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{(k-1)^{1/d}<|s|\leq k^{1/d}}p_{A}(x,s)dsdx\right)=O(1).

Hence, by Lemma
6.55
the sequence

\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}+1}^{2^{n+1}}\left(\lambda(k,A)-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{(k-1)^{1/d}<|s|\leq k^{1/d}}p_{A}(x,s)dsdx\right)\right\}_{n\geq 0}

belongs to the space
ac_{0}
of almost convergent sequences, or equivalently, the sequence

\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-2}^{2^{n+1}-1}\lambda(k,A)-\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{2^{n/d}<|s|\leq 2^{(n+1)/d}}p_{A}(x,s)dsdx\right\}_{n\geq 0}\in ac_{0}.

(30)

By Corollary
4.25
and Theorem
6.57
we have that for every positive normalised trace
\tau
on
\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty}

there exists a Banach limit
B
such that

\tau(A)=B\left(\frac{1}{\log 2}\left\{\sum_{k=2^{n}-2}^{2^{n+1}-1}\lambda(k,A)\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right).

We have now proved the assertion of (i), since every Banach limit vanishes on
ac_{0}
(see (
30
)).

Now, the equality

\tau(A)=\frac{1}{d(2\pi)^{d}}{\rm Res}(A)

holds for every positive normalised trace
\tau
if and only if for every Banach limit
B
we have

B\left(\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{2^{\frac{n}{d}}<|s|\leq 2^{\frac{n+1}{d}}}p_{A}(x,s)ds\,dx\right\}_{n\geq 0}\right)=\frac{\log 2}{d(2\pi)^{d}}{\rm Res}(A).

That is if and only if the sequence

\left\{\int_{2^{\frac{n}{d}}<|s|\leq 2^{\frac{n+1}{d}}}p_{A}(x,s)ds\,dx\right\}_{n\geq 0}

is almost convergent to the number
\frac{1}{d}\log 2\cdot{\rm Res}(A).

∎

To show that Theorem
8.76
(i) is truly an extension of Theorem
8.74
(i)
we need to show that there are pseudo-differential operators whose value
for a positive trace cannot be calculated by the formula for a Dixmier trace.
An example is given by Theorem
8.79
below.
Before we state it we need some technical preparations.

Lemma 8.77
.

We have

\int_{3}^{s}\sin\left(\frac{t}{\log t}\right)dt=O(\log s),\quad s>3.

Proof.

Set

I:=\int_{3}^{s}\sin\left(\frac{t}{\log t}\right)dt=\int_{3}^{s}\sin\left(\frac{t}{\log t}\right)\frac{\log t-1}{\log^{2}t}\frac{\log^{2}t}{\log t-1}dt.

Integrating by parts we obtain

I=-\cos\left(\frac{t}{\log t}\right)\frac{\log^{2}t}{\log t-1}\Big|_{3}^{s}+\int_{3}^{s}\cos\left(\frac{t}{\log t}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{t}\frac{\log^{2}t-2\log t}{(\log t-1)^{2}}dt.

We clearly have

\cos\left(\frac{t}{\log t}\right)\frac{\log^{2}t}{\log t-1}\Big|_{3}^{s}=O(\log s)

and

\int_{3}^{s}\cos\left(\frac{t}{\log t}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{t}\frac{\log^{2}t-2\log t}{(\log t-1)^{2}}dt=O\left(\int_{3}^{s}\frac{dt}{t}\right)=O(\log s).

Hence,
I=O(\log s)
.
∎

Lemma 8.78
.

The sequence

\left\{\int_{\log 2^{n/d}}^{\log 2^{(n+1)/d}}\sin\frac{z}{\log z}dz\right\}_{n\geq 0}

(31)

is not almost convergent to zero.

Proof.

We shall show that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=m}^{m+n-1}\int_{\frac{k}{d}\log 2}^{\frac{k+1}{d}\log 2}\sin\frac{z}{\log z}dz

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{n}\int_{\frac{m}{d}\log 2}^{\frac{n+m}{d}\log 2}\sin\frac{z}{\log z}dz

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\log 2}{d},

which means, in view of Theorem
2.10
, that the sequence (
31
) is not almost convergent to zero.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
z_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\frac{z_{n}}{\log z_{n}}=2^{n^{2}}\pi+\pi/2
.
Note that

\frac{1}{\log z_{n}}=O(\frac{1}{n^{2}})
.

Set
m=\lfloor z_{n}\frac{d}{\log 2}\rfloor
(the integral part of
z_{n}\frac{d}{\log 2}
). We have

J:=\int_{\frac{m}{d}\log 2}^{\frac{n+m}{d}\log 2}\sin\frac{z}{\log z}dz=\int_{z_{n}}^{z_{n}+\frac{n}{d}\log 2}\sin\frac{z}{\log z}dz+O(1).

By the Mean Value Theorem, for every
z\in[z_{n},z_{n}+\frac{n}{d}\log 2]
we obtain

\sin\frac{z}{\log z}-\sin\frac{z_{n}}{\log z_{n}}=(z-z_{n})\cdot\left(\frac{d}{dt}\left(t\mapsto\sin\frac{t}{\log t}\right)\right)\Big|_{t=\xi},

for some
\xi\in[z_{n},z]
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sin\frac{z}{\log z}-\sin\frac{z_{n}}{\log z_{n}}=O(n)\cdot O(1)\cdot\frac{\log\xi-1}{\log^{2}\xi}=O(n)\cdot O(\frac{1}{n^{2}})=O(\frac{1}{n}).

Hence,

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\int_{z_{n}}^{z_{n}+\frac{n}{d}\log 2}\left(\sin\frac{z_{n}}{\log z_{n}}+O(\frac{1}{n})\right)dz+O(1)

\displaystyle=\int_{z_{n}}^{z_{n}+\frac{n}{d}\log 2}dz+O(1)=n\frac{\log 2}{d}+O(1).

Consequently,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=m}^{m+n-1}\int_{\frac{k}{d}\log 2}^{\frac{k+1}{d}\log 2}\sin\frac{z}{\log z}dz\geq\frac{\log 2}{d},

which proves the assertion.
∎

The following theorem provides the example of a Dixmier measurable pseudo-differential operator
such that the class of all positive normalised traces does not coincide on this operator.

Theorem 8.79
.

There exists a compactly supported pseudo-differential operator
Q
of order
-d
such that

Q
is Dixmier-measurable but
Q
is not
\mathcal{PT}
-measurable.

Proof.

The construction of the operator
Q
is similar, at least in spirit, to that of
[
26
, Proposition 6.19]
(see also
[
36
, Proposition 11.3.22]
).
Consider the function

q(s):=|s|^{-d}\sin\left(\frac{\log|s|}{\log\log|s|}\right),\ s\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ |s|\geq 4.

Similarly to
[
36
, Proposition 10.2.10]
it can be proved that
q
is a symbol of some pseudo-differential operator, say
Q^{\prime}
.
Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
\|\phi\|_{2}=({\rm Vol}\ \mathbb{S}^{d-1})^{-1/2}
,
where
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:=\{s\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|s|=1\}
is
(d-1)
-sphere.
The operator
Q=M_{\phi}Q^{\prime}M_{\phi}^{*}
is compactly supported.
By
[
26
, Lemma 6.18]
the principal symbol of
Q
is

(x,s)\mapsto|\phi(x)|^{2}q(s),\ x,s\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

To show that
Q
is Laplacian modulated we check the condition (
25
).
We have for
t>4

\int_{|s|>t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|p_{A}(x,s)|^{2}\ dxds=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\phi(x)|^{2}dx\int_{|s|>t}q^{2}(s)ds.

The transformation from spherical coordinates to Cartesian gives

\displaystyle\int_{|s|>t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|p_{A}(x,s)|^{2}\ dxds=\int_{t}^{\infty}r^{-2d}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\log r}{\log\log r}\right)r^{d-1}\ dr=O(t^{-d}).

Hence,

\sup_{t>4}(1+t)^{d/2}\left(\int_{|s|>t}\int{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|p_{A}(x,s)|^{2}\ dxds\right)^{1/2}<\infty

and by (
25
) the operator
Q
is Laplacian modulated.

For every
n\geq 4^{d}
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|s|\leq n^{1/d}}|\phi(x)|^{2}q(s)ds\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\phi(x)|^{2}dx\int_{4\leq|s|\leq n^{1/d}}q(s)ds+O(1).

The transformation from spherical coordinates to Cartesian gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|s|\leq n^{1/d}}|\phi(x)|^{2}q(s)ds\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{4}^{n^{1/d}}\sin\left(\frac{\log r}{\log\log r}\right)r^{-d}r^{d-1}dr+O(1)

(32)

\displaystyle=\int_{4}^{n^{1/d}}\sin\left(\frac{\log r}{\log\log r}\right)\frac{dr}{r}+O(1)

\displaystyle=\int_{\log 4}^{\log n^{1/d}}\sin\left(\frac{z}{\log z}\right)dz

\displaystyle=O(\log\log n)=o(\log n),

where the penultimate equality is provided by Lemma
8.77
.

Combining this observation with Definition
8.72
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm Res}(Q)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{\log(2+n)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{|s|\leq n^{1/d}}p_{Q}(x,s)ds\,dx\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{\log(2+n)}\int_{4}^{n^{1/d}}\sin\left(\frac{\log r}{\log\log r}\right)\frac{dr}{r}\right]=0.

So,
{\rm Res}(Q)
is a scalar and, by Theorem
8.74
(ii),
{\rm Tr}_{\omega}(Q)=0
for every Dixmier trace
{\rm Tr}_{\omega}
.

Combining the result of Theorem
8.76
with (
32
), we conclude that
the pseudo-differential operator
Q
is
\mathcal{PT}
-measurable if and only if the sequence

\left\{\int_{\log 2^{n/d}}^{\log 2^{(n+1)/d}}\sin\frac{z}{\log z}dz\right\}_{n\geq 0}

(33)

is almost convergent (to zero, since all Dixmier traces vanish on
Q
).
However, this is not the case due to Lemma
8.78
.

∎

Remark 8.80
.

The example of the operator
Q
given in Theorem
8.79
is interesting,
because it shows how different traces of compactly supported pseudo-differential operators of order
-d

are from traces of classical compactly supported pseudo-differential operators of order
-d
.
On the classical operators there is one trace and one Wodzicki’s residue.
Even the natural vector generalisation
{\rm Res}
of the Wodzicki’s residue,
whilst it does capture the behaviour of Dixmier traces on the non-classical operators,
still does not capture the full behaviour of positive traces on the non-classical operators.
Indeed, the proof of Theorem
8.79
shows that
{\rm Res}(Q)

may be zero but still there are positive normalised traces which yield a non-zero value on
Q
.

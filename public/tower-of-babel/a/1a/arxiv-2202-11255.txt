Abstract
. Using one-dimensional branching Brownian motion in a periodic environment, we give probabilistic proofs of the asymptotics and uniqueness of pulsating travelling waves of the F-KPP equation in a periodic environment. This paper is a sequel to [Ren et al. Branching Brownian motion in a periodic environment and existence of pulsating travelling waves], in which we proved the existence of the pulsating travelling waves in the supercritical and critical cases using the limits of the additive and derivative martingales of branching Brownian motion in a periodic environment.

Keywords and phrases
: Branching Brownian motion; periodic environment; F-KPP equation; pulsating travelling waves; asymptotic behavior; uniqueness;
Bessel-3 process; Brownian motion; martingale change of measures

1.
Introduction

McKean
[
14
]
established the connection between branching Brownian motion (BBM) and
the Fisher-Kolmogorov-Petrovskii-Piskounov (F-KPP)
reaction-diffusion equation

(1.1)

\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{u}}{\partial x^{2}}+\beta({\mathbf{f}}({\mathbf{u}})-{\mathbf{u}}),

where
\mathbf{f}
is the generating function of the offspring distribution and
\beta
is the (constant) branching rate of BBM.
The F-KPP equation has been studied intensively by both analytic techniques (see, for example, Kolmogorov et al.
[
11
]
and Fisher
[
6
]
) and probabilistic methods
(see, for instance, McKean
[
14
]
, Bramson
[
2
,
3
]
, Harris
[
7
]
and Kyprianou
[
12
]
).

A travelling wave solution of (
1.1
) with speed
c
is a solution of the following equation:

(1.2)

\frac{1}{2}\Phi_{c}^{\prime\prime}+c\Phi_{c}^{\prime}+\beta({\mathbf{f}}(\Phi_{c})-\Phi_{c})=0.

If
\Phi_{c}
is a solution of (
1.2
), then
{\mathbf{u}}(t,x)=\Phi_{c}(x-ct)
satisfies (
1.1
).
Using the relation between the F-KPP equation (
1.1
) and BBM,
Kyprianou
[
12
]
gave probabilistic proofs of the existence, asymptotics and uniqueness of travelling wave solutions.
In this paper, we study the following more general F-KPP equation in which the constant
\beta
is replaced by a continuous and 1-periodic function
\mathbf{g}
:

(1.3)

\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{u}}{\partial x^{2}}+\mathbf{g}\cdot(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{u}),

where
{\mathbf{u}}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
.
In
[
15
]
, we have shown that the above equation is related to branching Brownian motion in a periodic environment.

Now we describe the branching Brownian motion in a periodic environment.
Initially there is a single particle
v
at the origin of the real line. This particle moves as a standard Brownian motion
B=\{B(t),t\geq 0\}
and produces a random number of offspring,

1+L
, after a random time
\eta_{v}
. We assume that
L
has distribution
\{p_{k},k\geq 0\}
with
m:=\sum_{k\geq 0}kp_{k}\in(0,\infty)
. Let
b_{v}
and
d_{v}
be the birth time and death time of the particle
v
, respectively, and
X_{v}(s)
be the location of the particle
v
at time
s
, then
\eta_{v}=d_{v}-b_{v}
, the lifetime of
v
, satisfies

(1.4)

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\eta_{v}>t\;|\;b_{v},\{X_{v}(s):s\geq b_{v}\}\right)=\exp\left\{-\int_{b_{v}}^{b_{v}+t}\mathbf{g}(X_{v}(s))\mathrm{d}s\right\},

where we assume the branching rate function
\mathbf{g}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is strictly positive and 1-periodic.
Starting from their points of creation, each of these children evolves independently.

Let
N_{t}
be the set of particles alive at time
t
and
X_{u}(s)
be the position of the particle
u
or its ancestor at time
s
for any
u\in N_{t}
,
s\leq t
. Define

Z_{t}=\sum_{u\in N_{t}}\delta_{X_{u}(t)},

and
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma(Z_{s}:s\leq t)
.

\{Z_{t}:t\geq 0\}
is called a branching Brownian motion in a periodic environment
(BBMPE).
Let
\mathbb{P}_{x}
be the law of
\{Z_{t}:t\geq 0\}
when the initial particle starts at
x\in\mathbb{R}
, that is
\mathbb{P}_{x}(Z_{0}=\delta_{x})=1
and
\mathbb{E}_{x}
be expectation with respect to
\mathbb{P}_{x}
.
For simplicity,
\mathbb{P}_{0}
and
\mathbb{E}_{0}
will be written as
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{E}
, respectively. Notice that the distribution of
L
does not depend on the spatial location.
In the remainder of this paper, expectations with respect to
L
will be written as

\mathbf{E}
. The notation in this paper is the same as those in
[
15
]
.

As stated in
[
15
]
, the F-KPP equation related to BBMPE
is given by (
1.3
) with
{\mathbf{f}}(s)={\mathbf{E}}(s^{L+1})
.
Travelling wave solutions, that is solutions satisfying (
1.2
), do not exist. However, we can consider so-called pulsating travelling waves, that is, solutions
\mathbf{u}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
to (
1.3
) satisfying

(1.5)

\mathbf{u}(t+\frac{1}{\nu},x)=\mathbf{u}(t,x-1),

as well as the boundary condition

(1.6)

\lim_{x\rightarrow-\infty}\mathbf{u}(t,x)=0,\quad\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{u}(t,x)=1,

when
\nu>0
, and

(1.7)

\lim_{x\rightarrow-\infty}\mathbf{u}(t,x)=1,\quad\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbf{u}(t,x)=0,

when
\nu<0
.
\nu
is called the wave speed.
It is known that there is a constant
\nu^{*}>0
(defined below) such that
when
|\nu|<\nu^{*}
(called subcritical case) no such solution exists, whereas for each
|\nu|\geq\nu^{*}
(where
|\nu|>\nu^{*}
is called the supercritical case and
|\nu|=\nu^{*}
is called the critical case)
there exists a unique, up to time-shift, pulsating travelling wave (see Hamel et al.
[
10
]
).

In
[
15
]
, we studied the limits of the additive and derivative martingales of BBMPE, and by using these limits, gave a probabilistic proof for the existence of pulsating travelling waves.
In this paper, using the relation between BBMPE and related F-KPP equation, we give probabilistic proofs of the asymptotics and uniqueness of pulsating travelling waves.
These extend the results of Kyprianou
[
12
]
for classical BBM to BBMPE.
However, the methods in Kyprianou
[
12
]
do not work for BBMPE.
We will adapt the ideas from
[
7
]
. The non-homogeneous nature of the environment makes the actual arguments much more delicate.

Before stating our main results, we first introduce the minimal speed
\nu^{*}
.
For every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\gamma(\lambda)
and
\psi(\cdot,\lambda)
be the principal eigenvalue and the corresponding positive eigenfunction of the periodic problem: for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

(1.8)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\psi_{xx}(x,\lambda)-\lambda\psi_{x}(x,\lambda)+(\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}+m\mathbf{g}(x))\psi(x,\lambda)&=\gamma(\lambda)\psi(x,\lambda),\\
\psi(x+1,\lambda)&=\psi(x,\lambda).\end{split}

We normalize
\psi(\cdot,\lambda)
such that
\int_{0}^{1}\psi(x,\lambda)dx=1
. Define

(1.9)

\nu^{*}:=\min_{\lambda>0}\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda},\quad\lambda^{*}:=\underset{\lambda>0}{\arg\min}\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}.

\nu^{*}
is the minimal wave speed (see
[
10
]
) and the existence of
\lambda^{*}
is proved in
[
13
]
.

Using the property
\mathbf{u}\left(t+\frac{1}{\nu},x\right)=\mathbf{u}(t,x-1)
, we can define
\mathbf{u}(-t,x)
for any
t>0
. To be more specific, let
\lceil x\rceil
be the smallest integer greater than or equal to
x

and
\lfloor x\rfloor
be the integral part of
x
.
When
\nu>0
, define

(1.10)

\mathbf{u}(-t,x)=\mathbf{u}\left(-t+\frac{\lceil\nu t\rceil}{\nu},x+\lceil\nu t\rceil\right),\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}.

When
\nu<0
, define

(1.11)

\mathbf{u}(-t,x)=\mathbf{u}\left(-t+\frac{\lfloor\nu t\rfloor}{\nu},x+\lfloor\nu t\rfloor\right),\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}.

Then
\mathbf{u}(t,x)
satisfies the F-KPP equation (
1.3
) and (
1.5
) in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Our first two main results give the asymptotic behaviors of pulsating travelling waves in the supercritical case of
|\nu|>\nu^{*}
and the critical case of
|\nu|=\nu^{*}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
\mathbf{u}(t,x)
is
a pulsating travelling wave with speed
\nu>\nu^{*}
and

\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{*})
satisfies

\nu=\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}
. If
\mathbf{E}(L\log^{+}L)<+\infty
, then there exists
\beta>0
such that

(1.12)

1-\mathbf{u}\left(\frac{y-x}{\nu},y\right)\sim\beta e^{-\lambda x}\psi(y,\lambda)\text{ as }x\rightarrow+\infty\text{ uniformly in }y\in[0,1].

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
\mathbf{u}(t,x)
is
a pulsating travelling wave with speed
\nu=\nu^{*}
. If
\mathbf{E}(L(\log^{+}L)^{2})<\infty
, then there exists
\beta>0
such that

(1.13)

1-\mathbf{u}\left(\frac{y-x}{\nu^{*}},y\right)\sim\beta xe^{-\lambda^{*}x}\psi(y,\lambda^{*})\text{ as }x\rightarrow+\infty\text{ uniformly in }y\in[0,1].

Remark 1
.

By symmetry, we also have the asymptotic behaviors of pulsating travelling waves with negative speed.
In the supercritical case of
\nu<-\nu^{*}
, suppose
\mathbf{u}(t,x)
is
a pulsating travelling wave with speed

\nu
and

\lambda\in(-\lambda^{*},0)
satisfies

\nu=\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}
. If
\mathbf{E}(L\log^{+}L)<+\infty
, then there exists
\beta>0
such that

(1.14)

1-\mathbf{u}\left(\frac{y-x}{\nu},y\right)\sim\beta e^{-\lambda x}\psi(y,\lambda)\text{ as }x\rightarrow-\infty\text{ uniformly in }y\in[0,1].

In the critical case, suppose
\mathbf{u}(t,x)
is
a pulsating travelling wave with speed
-\nu^{*}
. If
\mathbf{E}(L(\log^{+}L)^{2})<\infty
, then there exists
\beta>0
such that

(1.15)

1-\mathbf{u}\left(\frac{y-x}{-\nu^{*}},y\right)\sim\beta|x|e^{\lambda^{*}x}\psi(y,-\lambda^{*})\text{ as }x\rightarrow-\infty\text{ uniformly in }y\in[0,1].

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, define

(1.16)

W_{t}(\lambda)=e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t}\sum_{u\in N_{t}}e^{-\lambda X_{u}(t)}\psi(X_{u}(t),\lambda),

and

(1.17)

\partial W_{t}(\lambda):=e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t}\sum_{u\in N_{t}}e^{-\lambda X_{u}(t)}\bigg(\psi(X_{u}(t),\lambda)(\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+X_{u}(t))-\psi_{\lambda}(X_{u}(t),\lambda)\bigg).

It follows from
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
that,
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\{(W_{t}(\lambda))_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P}_{x}\}
is a martingale, and called the additive martingale.
The limit
W(\lambda,x):=\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}W_{t}(\lambda)
exists
\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely.
Moreover,
W(\lambda,x)
is an
L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{x})
-limit when
|\lambda|>\lambda^{*}
and
\mathbf{E}(L\log^{+}L)<\infty
;
and
W(\lambda,x)=0

\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely when
|\lambda|\leq\lambda^{*}
or
|\lambda|>\lambda^{*}
and
\mathbf{E}(L\log^{+}L)=\infty
.
It follows from
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
that,
for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

\{(\partial W_{t}(\lambda))_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P}_{x}\}
is a martingale, and called the derivative martingale. For all
|\lambda|\geq\lambda^{*}
, the limit
\partial W(\lambda,x):=\lim_{t\uparrow\infty}\partial W_{t}(\lambda)
exists
\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely.
Moreover, if
\mathbf{E}(L(\log^{+}L)^{2})<\infty
,
\partial W(\lambda,x)\in(0,\infty)
when
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
, and
\partial W(\lambda,x)\in(-\infty,0)
when
\lambda=-\lambda^{*}
. If
|\lambda|>\lambda^{*}
or
|\lambda|=\lambda^{*}
and
\mathbf{E}(L(\log^{+}L)^{2})=\infty
,
\partial W(\lambda,x)=0

\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely.

Using Theorem
1.1
, Theorem
1.2
and
[
15
, Theorem 1.3]
, we can prove the following result, which gives the existence and uniqueness of pulsating travelling waves.

Theorem 1.3
.

\mathrm{(i)}

Supercriticality case.

If
|\nu|>\nu^{*}
and
\mathbf{E}(L\log^{+}L)<\infty
,
then there is a unique, up to time-shift, pulsating travelling wave with speed
\nu
given by

(1.18)

\mathbf{u}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\exp\left\{-e^{\gamma(\lambda)t}W(\lambda,x)\right\}\right),

where
|\lambda|\in(0,\lambda^{*})
is such that

\nu=\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}
.

\mathrm{(ii)}

Criticality case.

If
|\nu|=\nu^{*}
and
\mathbf{E}(L(\log^{+}L)^{2})<\infty
, then there is a unique,
up to time-shift, pulsating travelling wave with speed
\nu
given by

(1.19)

\mathbf{u}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\exp\left\{-e^{\gamma(\lambda)t}\partial W(\lambda,x)\right\}\right),

where
\lambda=\lambda^{*}
if
\nu=\nu^{*}
, and
\lambda=-\lambda^{*}
if
\nu=-\nu^{*}
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Properties of principal eigenvalue and eigenfunction

In this section, we recall some properties of
\gamma(\lambda)
and
\psi(x,\lambda)
from
[
15
]
.
By
[
15
, Lemma 2.1]
, the function
\gamma
is analytic, strictly convex and even on
\mathbb{R}
. There exists a unique
\lambda^{*}>0
such that

\nu^{*}=\frac{\gamma(\lambda^{*})}{\lambda^{*}}=\min_{\lambda>0}\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}>0.

Furthermore

(2.1)

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow-\infty}\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)=-\infty,\quad\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow+\infty}\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)=+\infty.

By
[
15
, Lemma 2.2]
,
we have
\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda^{*})=\dfrac{\gamma(\lambda^{*})}{\lambda^{*}}
,

(2.2)

\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)<\dfrac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda},\quad\mbox{ on }(0,\lambda^{*})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)>\dfrac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda},\quad\mbox{ on }(\lambda^{*},\infty).

By
[
15
, Lemma 2.5]
, we have
\psi(x,\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\})
and
\psi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)
satisfies

(2.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\psi_{\lambda xx}(x,\lambda)-\psi_{x}(x,\lambda)-\lambda\psi_{\lambda x}(x,\lambda)+(\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}+m\mathbf{g}(x))\psi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)+\lambda\psi(x,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\gamma(\lambda)\psi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\psi(x,\lambda).

Define

(2.4)

\phi(x,\lambda):=e^{-\lambda x}\psi(x,\lambda),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Then
\phi(x,\lambda)
satisfies

(2.5)

\frac{1}{2}\phi_{xx}(x,\lambda)+m\mathbf{g}(x)\phi(x,\lambda)=\gamma(\lambda)\phi(x,\lambda),

and
\phi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)
satisfies

(2.6)

\frac{1}{2}\phi_{\lambda xx}(x,\lambda)+m\mathbf{g}(x)\phi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)=\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)\phi(x,\lambda)+\gamma(\lambda)\phi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda).

Define

(2.7)

h(x):=x-\frac{\psi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)}{\psi(x,\lambda)},

we also have

(2.8)

h(x)=-\frac{\phi_{\lambda}(x,\lambda)}{\phi(x,\lambda)}.

It is easy to see that
h^{\prime}
is 1-periodic and continuous and
[
15
, Lemma 2.10]
shows
h^{\prime}
is strictly positive.

2.2.
Measure change for Brownian motion

Martingale change of measures for Brownian motion will play important roles in our arguments.
In this section, we state the results of
[
15
]
about martingale change of measures.

Define

(2.9)

\Xi_{t}(\lambda):=e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t-\lambda B_{t}+m\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}x}\psi(B_{t},\lambda),

then by
[
15
, Lemma 2.6]
,
\{\Xi_{t}(\lambda),t\geq 0\}
is a
\Pi_{x}
-martingale. Define a probability measure
\Pi^{\lambda}_{x}
by

(2.10)

\frac{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}}{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{x}}\bigg|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{B}}=\frac{\Xi_{t}(\lambda)}{\Xi_{0}(\lambda)},

where
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{B}:t\geq 0\}

is the natural filtration of Brownian motion.
We have shown in
[
15
]
that

\{B_{t},\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\}
is a diffusion with infinitesimal generator

(2.11)

(\mathcal{A}f)(x)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}f(x)}{\partial x^{2}}+\left(\frac{\psi_{x}(x,\lambda)}{\psi(x,\lambda)}-\lambda\right)\frac{\partial f(x)}{\partial x}.

In the remainder of this paper, we always assume
that
\{Y_{t},t\geq 0;\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\}
is a diffusion with infinitesimal generator (
2.11
).

It follows from
[
15
, Lemma 2.8]
that, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.12)

\frac{Y_{t}}{t}\rightarrow-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda),\quad\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\mbox{-a.s.}

Define

(2.13)

M_{t}:=\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+h(Y_{t})-h(Y_{0}),\quad t\geq 0.

By
[
15
, Lemma 2.12]
,
\{M_{t},t\geq 0;\Pi^{\lambda}_{x}\}
is a martingale.
Moreover, there exist two constants
c_{2}>c_{1}>0

such that the quadratic variation
\langle M\rangle_{t}
satisfying

(2.14)

\langle M\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\left(h^{\prime}(Y_{s})\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}s\in[c_{1}t,c_{2}t].

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
, define an
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{B}\}
stopping time

(2.15)

\tau^{x}_{\lambda}:=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:\;h(B_{t})\leq-x-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t\right\}.

Define

(2.16)

\Lambda_{t}^{(x,\lambda)}:=e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t-\lambda B_{t}+m\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\psi(B_{t},\lambda)\left(x+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+h(B_{t})\right)\textbf{1}_{\{\tau^{x}_{\lambda}>t\}},

then
[
15
, Lemma 2.11]
shows that for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
with
y>h^{-1}(-x)
,
\{\Lambda_{t}^{(x,\lambda)},t\geq 0\}
is a
\Pi_{y}
-martingale. For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
with
y>h^{-1}(-x)
, define a new probability measure
\Pi^{(x,\lambda)}_{y}
by

(2.17)

\frac{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{y}^{(x,\lambda)}}{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{y}}\bigg|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{B}}=\frac{\Lambda_{t}^{(x,\lambda)}}{\Lambda_{0}^{(x,\lambda)}}.

By
[
15
, Section 2.2]
, if
\{B_{t},t\geq 0;\Pi_{y}\}
is a standard Brownian motion starting at
y
, then
\left\{x+h(y)+M_{T(t)},t\geq 0;\Pi_{y}^{(x,\lambda)}\right\}

is a standard Bessel-3 process
starting at
x+h(y)
, where
M_{t}=\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)t+h(B_{t})-h(B_{0})
and
T(s)=\inf\left\{t>0:\langle M\rangle_{t}>s\right\}=\inf\left\{t>0:\int_{0}^{t}\left(h^{\prime}(B_{s})\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}s>s\right\}
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We fix
\nu>\nu^{*}
in this proof and so
\lambda
is fixed also.
We will prove the theorem in five steps. In the first four steps, we assume the number of offspring is 2, that is
L=1
. In the last step, we prove the result for general
L
.

Step 1
Suppose
L=1
and thus
m=1
. Let
\mathbf{w}(t,x)=1-\mathbf{u}(t,x)
, then
\mathbf{w}(t,x)
satisfies

(3.1)

\begin{cases}&\frac{\partial\mathbf{w}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{w}}{\partial x^{2}}+\mathbf{g}\cdot(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{2}),\\
&\mathbf{w}(t+\frac{1}{\nu},x)=\mathbf{w}(t,x-1),\end{cases}

for
t\geq 0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
. Define

(3.2)

\mathbf{w}(-t,x)=\mathbf{w}\left(-t+\frac{\lceil\nu t\rceil}{\nu},x+\lceil\nu t\rceil\right),\quad\mbox{for }t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

By the periodicity of
\mathbf{w}
, we get that
\mathbf{w}(t,x)
satisfies (
3.1
).
Put

(3.3)

\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(t,x):=\frac{e^{\lambda x-\gamma(\lambda)t}\mathbf{w}(t,x)}{\psi(x,\lambda)}.

Recall that
\{Y_{t},\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\}
is a diffusion with infinitesimal generator (
2.11
).
Define

(3.4)

f(Y_{t})=\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(-t,Y_{t})e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(Y_{s})\mathbf{w}(-s,Y_{s})\mathrm{d}s},\quad t\geq 0.

In this step we prove that

\{(f(Y_{t}))_{t\geq 0},\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\}

is a positive martingale.

By the Feynman-Kac formula, we have

(3.5)

\mathbf{w}(T,x)=\Pi_{x}\left[\mathbf{w}(T-t,B_{t})e^{\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})(1-\mathbf{w}(T-s,B_{s}))\mathrm{d}s}\right],\quad\mbox{for $T\in\mathbb{R}$, $t>0$.}

Recall that, since
m=1
,

(3.6)

\frac{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}}{\mathrm{d}\Pi_{x}}\bigg|_{\mathcal{F}_{t}^{B}}=\frac{\Xi_{t}(\lambda)}{\Xi_{0}(\lambda)}=\frac{e^{-\gamma(\lambda)t-\lambda B_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\psi(B_{t},\lambda)}{e^{-\lambda x}\psi(x,\lambda)}.

Therefore

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}(T,x)

\displaystyle=\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\left[\frac{\Xi_{0}(\lambda)}{\Xi_{t}(\lambda)}\mathbf{w}(T-t,B_{t})e^{\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})(1-\mathbf{w}(T-s,B_{s}))\mathrm{d}s}\right]

(3.8)

\displaystyle=\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\left[e^{-\lambda x}\psi(x,\lambda)\frac{e^{\lambda B_{t}+\gamma(\lambda)t-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{w}(T-t,B_{t})}{\psi(B_{t},\lambda)}e^{\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})(1-\mathbf{w}(T-s,B_{s}))\mathrm{d}s}\right]

(3.9)

Put

(3.69)

A(w)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}m-\frac{1-w-\mathbf{f}(1-w)}{w},&\quad w\in(0,1],\\
0,&\quad w=0.\end{array}\right.

Since
(B_{t},\Pi^{\lambda}_{x})
and
(Y_{t},\Pi^{\lambda}_{x})
have the same law, by (
3.3
), we have

(3.70)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(T,x)=\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\left[\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(T-t,Y_{t})e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(Y_{s})A(\mathbf{w})(T-s,Y_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\right].

It follows from
[
1

that
A(\cdot)
is non-negative and non-decreasing.
Moreover, for any
r,c\in(0,1)
,

(3.71)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A(cr^{n})<+\infty\quad\mbox{ iff }\quad\mathbf{E}(L\log^{+}L)<+\infty.

Using the argument of Step 1, we get that

\{\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(-t,Y_{t})e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(Y_{s})A(\mathbf{w})(-s,Y_{s})\mathrm{d}s},\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a non-negative martingale.
Using the argument at the beginning of Step 2, we get

\mathbf{w}(t,x)\rightarrow 0
as
x\rightarrow\infty
uniformly in
t\in[0,\frac{1}{\nu}]
,

\mathbf{w}(-t,Y_{t})
decays exponentially with rate at least
-\lambda
.
So
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(-t,Y_{t})
converges
\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}
-almost surely, if we can show that

(3.72)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{g}(Y_{s})A(\mathbf{w})(-s,Y_{s})\mathrm{d}s<+\infty,\quad\mbox{ $\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}$-a.s.}

By (
3.24
) and (
3.71
),
if
\mathbf{E}(L\log^{+}L)<\infty
, we have

(3.73)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{g}(Y_{s})A(\mathbf{w})(-s,Y_{s})\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{g}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}A(Ce^{-(\lambda-\delta)(Y_{s}+\nu s)})\mathrm{d}s

(3.74)

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{g}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}A(C(e^{-(\lambda-\delta)(\nu-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda))s}))\mathrm{d}s

(3.75)

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{g}\|_{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A(C(e^{-(\lambda-\delta)(\nu-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda))n}))<+\infty\quad\mbox{ $\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}$-a.s. }

where
C
is a constant depending on
\omega
and may change values from line to line.

Also by the arguments in Steps 2 and 3,

\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(t,x)
satisfies

(3.76)

\frac{\partial\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}}{\partial x^{2}}+(\frac{\psi_{x}(x,\lambda)}{\psi(x,\lambda)}-\lambda)\frac{\partial\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}}{\partial x}-\mathbf{g}\cdot A({\mathbf{w}})\widetilde{\mathbf{w}},

and the maximum principle holds.
Hence,
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(t,x)
is bounded in
[0,\frac{1}{\nu}]\times\mathbb{R}
.
Since
A(\cdot)
is non-decreasing,
(
3.51
) implies

(3.77)

A(\mathbf{w})(t-s,Y_{s})\leq A(C_{1}e^{-\lambda(Y_{s}+\nu s)}).

By the periodicity of
\{Y_{t}\}
, we have

(3.78)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(t,y+n)\geq\beta\Pi_{y+n}^{\lambda}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\mathbf{g}\|_{\infty}A(C_{1}e^{-\lambda(Y_{s}+\nu s)})\mathrm{d}s}\right]=\beta\Pi_{y}^{\lambda}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\mathbf{g}\|_{\infty}A(C_{1}e^{-\lambda(Y_{s}+n+\nu s)})\mathrm{d}s}\right].

To prove the theorem, it suffices to show

(3.79)

\Pi_{y}^{\lambda}\left[e^{-\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\mathbf{g}\|_{\infty}A(C_{1}e^{-\lambda(Y_{s}+n+\nu s)})\mathrm{d}s}\right]\rightarrow 1,\quad\mbox{ as $n\rightarrow\infty$ uniformly for $y\in[0,1]$.}

We know

(3.80)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\mathbf{g}\|_{\infty}A(C_{1}e^{-\lambda(Y_{s}+n+\nu s)})\mathrm{d}s<+\infty\quad\mbox{ $\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}$-a.s.}

Using an argument similar to that in Step 3, (
3.79
) follows from

\lim_{u\downarrow 0}A(u)=0
,
the dominated convergence theorem and Dini’s theorem.
This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we prove the asymptotic behavior in the critical case.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We prove the theorem in seven steps. In the first six steps, we consider the case that
L=1
. In the last step we consider general
L
.

Step 1
Suppose
L=1
. Put
\mathbf{w}(t,x)=1-\mathbf{u}(t,x)
. We first prove that
\mathbf{w}(t,x)
decays exponentially with rate at least
-\lambda
uniformly in
t\in[0,\frac{1}{\nu}]
.
Using an argument similar to that of Theorem
1.1
, we have that, for
t,x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbf{w}(t,x)
satisfies

(4.1)

\begin{cases}&\frac{\partial\mathbf{w}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{w}}{\partial x^{2}}+\mathbf{g}\cdot(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{2}),\\
&\mathbf{w}(t+\frac{1}{\nu^{*}},x)=\mathbf{w}(t,x-1).\end{cases}

By the Feynman-Kac formula, we get

(4.2)

\mathbf{w}(T,x)=\Pi_{x}\left[\mathbf{w}(T-t,B_{t})e^{\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})(1-\mathbf{w}(T-s,B_{s}))\mathrm{d}s}\right],\quad\mbox{for $T\in\mathbb{R}$, $t>0$.}

For any
\lambda<\lambda^{*}
, define

(4.3)

\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(t,x)=\frac{e^{\lambda x-\gamma(\lambda)t}\mathbf{w}(t,x)}{\psi(x,\lambda)}.

Changing measure with
\Xi_{t}(\lambda)

and following the same ideas in Step 1 of the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we get that

(4.4)

\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(T,x)=\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\left[\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(T-t,Y_{t})e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(Y_{s})\mathbf{w}(T-s,Y_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\right],

and
\{\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(-t,Y_{t})e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(Y_{s})\mathbf{w}(-s,Y_{s})\mathrm{d}s},\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\}_{t\geq 0}

is a positive martingale.
Therefore, we have

(4.5)

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left\{\frac{\ln\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(-t,Y_{t})}{Y_{t}+\nu^{*}t}-\frac{1}{Y_{t}+\nu^{*}t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(Y_{s})\mathbf{w}(-s,Y_{s})\mathrm{d}s\right\}\leq 0\quad\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\mbox{-a.s.}

Notice that for
\lambda<\lambda^{*}
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{Y_{t}+\nu^{*}t}{t}\rightarrow-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)+\nu^{*}>0.

Combining this with (
4.3
), we get

(4.7)

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(-t,Y_{t})}{Y_{t}+\nu^{*}t}

\displaystyle=\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln(e^{\lambda(Y_{t}+\nu^{*}t)+(\gamma(\lambda)-\lambda\nu^{*})t}\mathbf{w}(-t,Y_{t}))}{Y_{t}+\nu^{*}t}

(4.8)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln\mathbf{w}(-t,Y_{t})}{Y_{t}+\nu^{*}t}+\lambda+\frac{\gamma(\lambda)-\lambda\nu^{*}}{\nu^{*}-\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda)}\leq 0\quad\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\mbox{-a.s.}

where
\gamma(\lambda)-\lambda\nu^{*}>0
.
Hence
\mathbf{w}(-t,Y_{t})
decays exponentially with rate at least
-\lambda

\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}
-almost surely. This implies

(4.9)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{g}(Y_{s})\mathbf{w}(-s,Y_{s})\mathrm{d}s<+\infty\quad\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}\mbox{-a.s.}

Then,
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(-t,Y_{t})
converges
\Pi_{x}^{\lambda}
-almost surely. Using a coupling method similar to that of Step 2 in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we get that the limit of
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(-t,Y_{t})
is a constant. Notice that

(4.10)

\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(t+\frac{1}{\nu^{*}},x+1)=\frac{e^{\lambda(x+1)-\gamma(\lambda)(t+\frac{1}{\nu^{*}})}\mathbf{w}(t+\frac{1}{\nu^{*}},x+1)}{\psi(x+1,\lambda)}=e^{\lambda-\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\nu^{*}}}\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(t,x)<\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(t,x),

where we used
\mathbf{w}(t+\frac{1}{\nu^{*}},x+1)=\mathbf{w}(t,x)
and
\nu^{*}<\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}
for
\lambda<\lambda^{*}
. Therefore, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(4.11)

\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(-t+\frac{k}{\nu},Y_{t}+k)\leq\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(-t,Y_{t}).

Using an argument similar to that of Step 3 in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we get
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}(t,x)
is bounded in

[0,\frac{1}{\nu^{*}}]\times\mathbb{R}
.
Then, by (
4.3
),
\mathbf{w}(t,x)
decays exponentially with rate at least
-\lambda
uniformly in

t\in[0,\frac{1}{\nu^{*}}]
.

Step 2

Recall the definitions of

,
\tau_{\lambda}^{x}
,
\Lambda_{t}^{(x,\lambda)}

and

given in (

2.16
) and (

in (

\tau_{z}:=\inf\{t\geq 0:y+\gamma^{\prime}(\lambda^{*})t+h(B_{t})\leq z\}.

For any

Using (

(4.16)

\widehat{\mathbf{w}}(T,x,y+\nu^{*}T)=\Pi_{x}^{(y,\lambda^{*})}\left(\widehat{\mathbf{w}}(T-t\wedge\tau_{z},B_{t\wedge\tau_{z}},y+\nu^{*}T)e^{-\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{z}}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathbf{w}(T-s,B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\right).

(4.17)

(4.18)

\displaystyle\Pi_{x}^{(y,\lambda^{*})}\left(\psi(x,\lambda^{*})(y+h(x))e^{-\lambda^{*}x+\gamma(\lambda^{*})T}\widehat{\mathbf{w}}(T-t\wedge\tau_{z},B_{t\wedge\tau_{z}},y+\nu^{*}T)e^{-\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{z}}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathbf{w}(T-s,B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi_{x}\left(\mathbf{w}(T-t\wedge\tau_{z},B_{t\wedge\tau_{z}})e^{\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{z}}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})(1-\mathbf{w}(T-s,B_{s}))\mathrm{d}s}\right)=\mathbf{w}(T,x),

4.17

Then using the definition of

given in (

we get (

By (

(4.24)

\widehat{\mathbf{w}}(T,x,y)=\Pi_{x}^{(y-\nu^{*}T,\lambda^{*})}\left(\widehat{\mathbf{w}}(T-t\wedge\tau_{z}(y-\nu^{*}T),B_{t\wedge\tau_{z}(y-\nu^{*}T)},y)e^{-\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{z}(y-\nu^{*}T)}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathbf{w}(T-s,B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\right).

By the same method, we can get

Next we use a coupling method to prove that

-almost surely all

)

(4.48)

\widetilde{\Pi}_{x}^{(y,T)}(\exists\ T>0,\mbox{ s.t. for any }t>T,\widehat{R}_{t}>c_{2})=1.

Combining (
4.45

4.47

Remark 2
.

The asymptotic behavior of pulsating travelling waves has been studied analytically in
[
8
]
. They considered the following more general equation:

(4.98)

u_{t}-\nabla\cdot(A(z)\nabla u)+q(z)\cdot\nabla u=f(z,u),\quad z\in\overline{\Omega},

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an unbounded domain,
A(z),q(z),f(z,u)
are periodic in some sense. In our case,

(4.99)

\Omega=\mathbb{R},\;A(z)\equiv\frac{1}{2},\;q(z)\equiv 0,\mbox{ and }f(z,u)=\mathbf{g}(z)(1-u-\mathbf{f}(1-u)).

[
8
, Theorem 1.3]
, the main result of the paper,
is similar to our Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, where
\phi(t,x)
in
[
8
]
corresponds to
1-\mathbf{u}\left(\frac{t+x}{\nu},x\right)
in this paper, and
p^{-}(x,y),p^{+}(x,y)
correspond to
0,1
respectively.

[
8
, Theorem 1.3]
was proved under the assumptions
[
8
, (1.4), (1.7) and (1.8)]
.
In our setup,

[
8
, (1.4)]
is equivalent to
\gamma(0)>0
, which is true under our assumptions.

[
8
, (1.8)]
is equivalent to

(4.100)

f(x,s)=\mathbf{g}(x)(1-s-\mathbf{f}(1-s))\leq m\mathbf{g}(x)s\quad\mbox{ for }s\in[0,1],

which is true for any generating function
\mathbf{f}
.

[
8
, (1.7)]
says that there exist
\alpha>0
and
\gamma>0
such that the map
(x,s)\mapsto\mathbf{g}(x)(\mathbf{f}^{\prime}(1-s)-1)
belongs to
C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\gamma])
, which is equivalent to
g\in C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
and
\mathbf{f}^{\prime}(1-s)\in C^{0,\alpha}([0,\gamma])
.
We claim that

(4.101)

\mathbf{f}^{\prime}(1-s)\in C^{\alpha}([0,\gamma])\Longrightarrow\forall p\geq 1,\;\mathbf{E}(L(\log^{+}L)^{p})<\infty.

Thus
the condition
\mathbf{f}^{\prime}(1-s)\in C^{0,\alpha}([0,\gamma])

is stronger than the condition
\mathbf{E}(L\log^{+}L)<\infty
in the supercritical case and
\mathbf{E}(L(\log^{+}L)^{2})<\infty
in the critical case.
Now we prove the claim. Notice that

(4.102)

\displaystyle A(w)=m-\frac{1-w-\mathbf{f}(1-w)}{w}=m+1-\frac{1-f(1-w)}{w}=m+1-f^{\prime}(1-\theta w),

where
\theta\in[0,1]
and the last equality follows from the mean value theorem. Since
f^{\prime}(1)=m+1
and
\mathbf{f}^{\prime}(1-s)\in C^{\alpha}([0,\gamma])
, we have

(4.103)

A(w)=|m+1-f^{\prime}(1-\theta w)|\leq C(\theta w)^{\alpha}\leq Cw^{\alpha},\quad\forall w\leq\gamma,

for some constant
C
.
Therefore, for any constant
c>0
,

(4.104)

\int_{0}^{\infty}A(e^{-ct^{1/p}})\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}Ce^{-c\alpha t^{1/p}}\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

Using the substitution

\int_{0}^{1}A(s)\frac{|\log s|^{p-1}}{s}\mathrm{d}s<\infty.

By (

Proof of Theorem

The uniqueness of the pulsating travelling wave was proved analytically in

.
In this section, we will use probabilistic methods to prove the uniqueness in the supercritical case
|\nu|>\nu^{*}
and critical case

.

5.1.
Martingales on stopping lines

First, we introduce the space of Galton-Watson trees. Let
\mathbb{T}
be the space of Galton-Watson trees. A Galton-Watson tree
\tau\in\mathbb{T}
is a point in the space of possible Ulam-Harris labels

\Omega=\emptyset\cup\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\mathbb{N})^{n},

where
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
such that

(i)

\emptyset\in\tau
(the ancestor);

(ii)

if
u,v\in\Omega
,
uv\in\tau
implies
u\in\tau
;

(iii)

for all
u\in\tau,
there exists
A_{u}\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}

such that for
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
j\in\tau
if and only if
1\leq j\leq 1+A_{u}
.

(Here
1+A_{u}
is the number of offspring of
u
,
and
A_{u}
has the same distribution as
L
.)

Each particle
u\in\tau
has a mark
(\eta_{u},B_{u})\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times C(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{R})
, where
\eta_{u}
is the lifetime of
u
and
B_{u}
is the motion of
u
relative to its birth position. Then the birth time of
u
can be written as
b_{u}=\sum_{v<u}\eta_{v}
, the death time of
u
is
d_{u}=\sum_{v\leq u}\eta_{v}
and the position of
u
at time
t
is given by
X_{u}(t)=\sum_{v<u}B_{v}(\eta_{v})+B_{u}(t-b_{u})
, where
v<u
denotes that
u
is a descendant of
v
.

Now, on the space-time half plane
\{(y,t):y\in\mathbb{R},t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\}
, consider the barrier
\Gamma^{(x,\nu)}
described by the line
y+\nu t=x
for
x>0

and
\nu\geq\nu^{*}
.
When a particle hits this barrier,
it is stopped immediately.
Let
C(x,\nu)
denote the random collection of particles stopped at the barrier, which is known as a stopping line.

By
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
,
W_{t}(\lambda^{*})\rightarrow 0

\mathbb{P}_{x}
-almost surely, so we have

(5.1)

e^{-\lambda^{*}(m_{t}+\nu^{*}t)}\min_{x\in[0,1]}\psi(x,\lambda^{*})\leq W_{t}(\lambda)\rightarrow 0,

where
m_{t}=\min\{X_{u}(t):u\in N_{t}\}
.
This yields

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}(m_{t}+\nu^{*}t)=+\infty.

Therefore, all lines of descent from the ancestor will hit
\Gamma^{(x,\nu)}
with probability one for all
x>x_{0}
, where
x_{0}
is the position of the ancestor at time
t=0
. Similar to the argument in
[
12
]
for BBM, we have
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\inf\{|u|:u\in C(x,\nu)\}=\infty
where
|u|
is the generation of particle
u
.

For any
u\in C(x,\nu)
, let
\sigma_{u}
denote the time at which the particle
u
hits the barrier
\Gamma^{(x,\nu)}
.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{C(x,\nu)}
be the
\sigma
-field generated by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(w,A_{w},\eta_{w},\{B_{w}(s):s\in[0,\eta_{w}]\}:\exists u\in C(x,\nu),\,s.t.\,w<u)\mbox{ and }\\
(u,\{B_{u}(s):s\in[0,\sigma_{u}-b_{u}]\}:u\in C(x,\nu))\end{array}\right\}.

Using travelling wave solutions of the KPP equation, Chauvin
[
5
]
exhibited an intrinsic class of martingales.
An argument similar to the one used in
[
5
]

gives the analogous martingales for BBMPE.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
\nu>\nu^{*}
and that
\mathbf{u}(t,x)
is a pulsating travelling wave with speed
\nu
. Define

(5.4)

M_{x}(\nu):=\prod_{u\in C(x,\nu)}\mathbf{u}(-\sigma_{u},X_{u}(\sigma_{u})).

Then, for any
z\in\mathbb{R}
,
\{M_{x}(\nu):x\geq z\}
is a
\mathbb{P}_{z}
-martingale with respect to
\{\mathcal{F}_{C(x,\nu)},x\geq z\}
, has expectation
u(0,z)
, and converges
\mathbb{P}_{z}
-almost surely and in
L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{z})
.

We prove this theorem via several lemmas.

Lemma 5.2
.

Consider BBMPE starting from
x
and with branching rate function
\mathbf{g}
.
Let
\sigma
be the first fission time and
1+A
denote the number of offspring of the initial particle. Let
\mathbf{f}(s)=\mathbf{E}s^{1+A}
and
\mathbf{P}(A=k)=p_{k}
.
Then

(5.5)

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma>t\}}\mathbf{u}(-t,B_{t})+\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma\leq t\}}\mathbf{u}^{1+A}(-\sigma,B_{\sigma})\right)=\mathbf{u}(0,x).

Proof.

Note that

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\sigma>t\;|\;\{B_{s}:s\leq t\}\right)=e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}.

Put

(5.6)

f(t,B_{t})=e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{u}(-t,B_{t})+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})e^{-\int_{0}^{s}\mathbf{g}(B_{r})\mathrm{d}r}\sum_{k}p_{k}\mathbf{u}^{k+1}(-s,B_{s})\mathrm{d}s.

A standard computation using Itô’s formula shows that

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}f(t,B_{t})

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}(-t,B_{t})}{\partial x}\mathrm{\mathrm{d}}B_{t}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{u}(-t,B_{t})}{\partial x^{2}}\mathrm{d}t-\mathbf{g}(B_{t})e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{u}(-t,B_{t})\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}(-t,B_{t})}{\partial t}\mathrm{d}t+\mathbf{g}(B_{t})e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\sum_{k}p_{k}\mathbf{u}^{k+1}(-t,B_{t})\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}(-t,B_{t})}{\partial x}\mathrm{\mathrm{d}}B_{t}

\displaystyle+e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\left(\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{u}(-t,B_{t})}{\partial x^{2}}-\mathbf{g}(B_{t})\mathbf{u}(-t,B_{t})-\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}(-t,B_{t})}{\partial t}+\mathbf{g}(B_{t})\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}(-t,B_{t}))\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}(-t,B_{t})}{\partial x}\mathrm{\mathrm{d}}B_{t}.

Hence
f(t,B_{t})
is a martingale and

\Pi_{x}f(t,B_{t})=\Pi_{x}f(0,B_{0})=\mathbf{u}(0,x).

Note that

(5.7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}(\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma>t\}}\mathbf{u}(-t,B_{t}))=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma>t\}}\mathbf{u}(-t,B_{t})\;|\;\{B_{s}:s\leq t\}\right]\right)

(5.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\mathbf{u}(-t,B_{t})\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma>t\}}\;|\;\{B_{s}:s\leq t\}\right]\right)

(5.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi_{x}\left(e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})\mathrm{d}s}\mathbf{u}(-t,B_{t})\right).

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma\leq t\}}\mathbf{u}^{A+1}(-\sigma,B_{\sigma})\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\mathbb{P}_{x}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma\leq t\}}\mathbf{u}^{A+1}(-\sigma,B_{\sigma})\;|\;\{B_{s}:s\leq t\}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=\Pi_{x}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{g}(B_{s})e^{-\int_{0}^{s}\mathbf{g}(B_{r})\mathrm{d}r}\sum_{k}p_{k}\mathbf{u}^{k+1}(-s,B_{s})\mathrm{d}s\right).

Therefore,

\mathbb{P}_{x}\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma>t\}}\mathbf{u}(-t,B_{t})+\mathbf{1}_{\{\sigma\leq t\}}\mathbf{u}^{1+A}(-\sigma,B_{\sigma})\right)=\Pi_{x}f(t,B_{t})=\mathbf{u}(0,x).

∎

Recall that
\Omega=\emptyset\cup\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(\mathbb{N})^{n}

\displaystyle D_{\tau}

\displaystyle:=\{u\in\Omega:\;\exists v\in\Omega,\;v<u,\;v\neq u,\;v\in L_{\tau}\},

\displaystyle A_{\tau}^{(n)}

\displaystyle:=\{u\in\Omega:\;|u|=n,\;u\notin D_{\tau},\;u\notin L_{\tau}\}.

In other words,

Let

Recall that

Lemma 5.3
.

For any

,

Proof.

For

, similar to
[

, we introduce the following approximation of

M^{(n)}=\prod_{u\in L_{\tau},|u|\leq n}\mathbf{u}(-\sigma_{u},X_{u}(\sigma_{u}))\prod_{u\in A_{\tau}^{(n)}}\mathbf{u}^{1+A_{u}}(-d_{u},X_{u}(d_{u})).

It is easy to see that

We first prove that

is an

-martingale.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}(M^{(n+1)}\;|\;\mathcal{H}_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\prod_{u\in L_{\tau},|u|\leq n}\mathbf{u}(-\sigma_{u},X_{u}(\sigma_{u}))

(5.14)

\displaystyle\times\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg(\prod_{u\in L_{\tau},|u|=n+1}\mathbf{u}(-\sigma_{u},X_{u}(\sigma_{u}))\prod_{u\in A_{\tau}^{(n+1)}}\mathbf{u}^{1+A_{u}}(-d_{u},X_{u}(d_{u}))\;|\;\mathcal{H}_{n+1}\bigg).

Note that

Consider any particle

and

if not, it occurs in the third one. By the Markov property and the branching property, we have

\displaystyle\;\mathbb{E}_{x}\bigg(\prod_{u\in L_{\tau},|u|=n+1}\mathbf{u}(-\sigma_{u},X_{u}(\sigma_{u}))\prod_{u\in A_{\tau}^{(n+1)}}\mathbf{u}^{1+A_{u}}(-d_{u},X_{u}(d_{u}))\;|\;\mathcal{H}_{n+1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{|u|=n+1,u\notin D_{\tau}}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\textbf{1}_{\{\sigma_{u}<d_{u}\}}\mathbf{u}(-\sigma_{u},X_{u}(\sigma_{u}))+\textbf{1}_{\{\sigma_{u}\geq d_{u}\}}\mathbf{u}^{1+A_{u}}(-d_{u},X_{u}(d_{u}))\;|\;\mathcal{H}_{n+1}\right)

\displaystyle\prod_{|u|=n+1,u\notin D_{\tau}}\mathbb{E}_{X_{u}(b_{u})}\left(\textbf{1}_{\{\sigma_{u}<d_{u}\}}\mathbf{u}(-\sigma_{u},X_{u}(\sigma_{u}-b_{u}))+\textbf{1}_{\{\sigma_{u}\geq d_{u}\}}\mathbf{u}^{1+A_{u}}(-d_{u},X_{u}(d_{u}-b_{u}))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{|u|=n+1,u\notin D_{\tau}}\mathbb{E}_{X_{u}(b_{u})}\left(\textbf{1}_{\{\sigma_{u}-b_{u}<d_{u}-b_{u}\}}\mathbf{v}_{u}(-(\sigma_{u}-b_{u}),X_{u}(\sigma_{u}-b_{u}))+\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 99.58464pt\left.\textbf{1}_{\{\sigma_{u}-b_{u}\geq d_{u}-b_{u}\}}\mathbf{v}_{u}^{1+A_{u}}(-(d_{u}-b_{u}),X_{u}(d_{u}-b_{u}))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{|u|=n+1,u\notin D_{\tau}}\mathbf{v}_{u}(0,X_{u}(b_{u})),

where
\mathbf{v}_{u}(t,x)=\mathbf{u}(t-b_{u},x)
and given

satisfies (

\displaystyle\prod_{|u|=n+1,u\notin D_{\tau}}\mathbf{v}_{u}(0,X_{u}(b_{u}))=\prod_{|u|=n+1,u\notin D_{\tau}}\mathbf{u}(-b_{u},X_{u}(b_{u}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{|u|=n,u\notin D_{\tau},u\notin L_{\tau}}\mathbf{u}^{1+A_{u}}(-d_{u},X_{u}(d_{u}))=\prod_{u\in A_{\tau}^{(n)}}\mathbf{u}^{1+A_{u}}(-d_{u},X_{u}(d_{u})).

This shows that

Since

\mathbb{E}_{z}(M_{x}(v))=\mathbb{E}_{z}(M^{(0)})=\mathbf{u}(0,z).

Proof of Theorem

5.2.
Uniqueness in the supercritical and critical cases

In this section, we give a probabilistic proof of the uniqueness of the pulsating travelling wave with speed
|\nu|\geq\nu^{*}
.

Theorem
1.1
implies that for
\nu>\nu^{*}

(5.15)

-\log\mathbf{u}\left(\frac{y-x}{\nu},y\right)\sim\beta e^{-\lambda x}\psi(y,\lambda)\text{ as }x\rightarrow+\infty\text{ uniformly in }y\in[0,1].

Theorem
1.2
implies that

(5.16)

-\log\mathbf{u}\left(\frac{y-x}{\nu^{*}},y\right)\sim\beta xe^{-\lambda^{*}x}\psi(y,\lambda^{*})\text{ as }x\rightarrow+\infty\text{ uniformly in }y\in[0,1].

Recall that
C(x,\nu)
was defined at the beginning of Section
5.1
.
In the spirit of
[
12
]
, we define

(5.17)

W_{C(x,\nu)}(\lambda):=\sum_{u\in C(x,\nu)}e^{-\lambda X_{u}(\sigma_{u})-\gamma(\lambda)\sigma_{u}}\psi(X_{u}(\sigma_{u}),\lambda),

where
\nu=\gamma(\lambda)/\lambda
.
Using arguments similar to those of
[
12
, Theorem 8]
, we can obtain the following result, whose proof is omitted.

Proposition 5.4
.

For any
z\in\mathbb{R}
,
\{W_{C(x,\nu)}(\lambda):x\geq z\}
is a
\mathbb{P}_{z}
-martingale with respect to the filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{C(x,\nu)}:x\geq z\}
, and, as
x\to\infty
,
W_{C(x,\nu)}(\lambda)
converges almost surely and in
L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{z})
to
W(\lambda,z)
when
|\lambda|\in[0,\lambda^{*})
and
\mathbf{E}(L\log^{+}L)<\infty
.

Let
Y_{x}=\sum_{u\in C(x,\nu)}\delta_{\{X_{u}(\sigma_{u})\}}
where
\{x\}
is the fractional part of
x
. Then
Y_{x}
is a point measure on
[0,1]
.
Notice that

\displaystyle W_{C(x,\nu)}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in C(x,\nu)}e^{-\lambda(X_{u}(\sigma_{u})+\nu\sigma_{u})}\psi(X_{u}(\sigma_{u}),\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in C(x,\nu)}e^{-\lambda x}\psi(X_{u}(\sigma_{u}),\lambda)=e^{-\lambda x}\langle Y_{x},\psi\rangle.

Thus by Proposition
5.4
, we have

(5.18)

e^{-\lambda x}\langle Y_{x},\psi\rangle\overset{\mathbb{P}_{z}\mbox{-a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}W(\lambda,z).

Theorem 5.5
.

Suppose
|\nu|>\nu^{*}
and
\mathbf{E}(L\log^{+}L)<\infty
. If
\mathbf{u}(t,x)
is a pulsating travelling wave with speed
\nu
,
then there exists
\beta>0
such that

(5.19)

\mathbf{u}(t,x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\exp\left\{-\beta e^{\gamma(\lambda)t}W(\lambda,x)\right\},

where
|\lambda|\in(0,\lambda^{*})
is such that

\nu=\frac{\gamma(\lambda)}{\lambda}
.

Proof.

We assume that
\lambda\geq 0
. The case
\lambda<0
can be analyzed by symmetry.
By Theorem
5.1
,
M_{x}(\nu)
is a
\mathbb{P}_{z}
-martingale with respecct to
\{\mathcal{F}_{C(x,\nu)}:x\geq z\}
with expectation
\mathbf{u}(0,z)
and converges almost surely and in
L^{1}(\mathbb{P}_{z})
, where

M_{x}(\nu)=\exp\left\{\sum_{u\in C(x,\nu)}\log\mathbf{u}(-\sigma_{u},X_{u}(\sigma_{u}))\right\}.

So there exists a non-negative random variable
Y
such that

-\sum_{u\in C(x,\nu)}\log\mathbf{u}(-\sigma_{u},X_{u}(\sigma_{u}))\overset{\mathbb{P}_{z}\mbox{-a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}Y.

Note that

\displaystyle X_{u}(\sigma_{u})=\{X_{u}(\sigma_{u})\}+\lfloor X_{u}(\sigma_{u})\rfloor,

\displaystyle-\sigma_{u}=\frac{X_{u}(\sigma_{u})-x}{\nu}=\frac{\{X_{u}(\sigma_{u})\}-x}{\nu}+\frac{\lfloor X_{u}(\sigma_{u})\rfloor}{\nu}.

The previous convergence can be written as

\left\langle Y_{x},\;-\log\mathbf{u}\left(\frac{\cdot-x}{\nu},\cdot\right)\right\rangle\overset{\mathbb{P}_{z}\mbox{-a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}Y,\quad\mbox{as }x\to\infty.

By (

),

\lim_{x\to\infty}\left\langle Y_{x},-\log\mathbf{u}\left(\frac{\cdot-x}{\nu},\cdot\right)\right\rangle=\beta\lim_{x\to\infty}\langle e^{-\lambda x}Y_{x},\psi(\cdot,\lambda)\rangle,

and thus

.
By the dominated convergence theorem

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}(0,z)

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}_{z}e^{\left\langle Y_{x},\;\log\mathbf{u}\left(\frac{\cdot-x}{\nu},\cdot\right)\right\rangle}=\mathbb{E}_{z}\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}e^{\left\langle Y_{x},\;\log u\left(\frac{\cdot-x}{\nu},\cdot\right)\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{z}e^{-Y}=\mathbb{E}_{z}e^{-\beta W(\lambda,z)}.

[
15

shows that

, as a function of

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}\mathbf{u}}{\partial x^{2}}+\mathbf{g}\cdot(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u})-\mathbf{u}),\quad\mathbf{u}(0,x)=\mathbb{E}_{x}e^{-\beta W(\lambda,x)}.

\mathbf{u}(t,x)

The uniqueness of solutions of initial value problem implies (

) holds.
∎

Now we consider the critical case.
Recall that on the space-time half plane

Define
\widetilde{C}(z,\nu^{*})

Fix

Define
Y_{z}^{(-x,\lambda^{*})}=\sum_{u\in\widetilde{C}(z,\nu^{*})}\delta_{\{X_{u}(\sigma_{u})\}}
,

.
Consider the sequence
\{V_{\widetilde{C}(z,v^{*})}^{x},z\geq y\}

\displaystyle V_{\widetilde{C}(z,\nu^{*})}^{x}

\displaystyle:=

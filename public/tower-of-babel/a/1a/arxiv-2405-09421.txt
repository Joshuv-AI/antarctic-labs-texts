A positive proportion of monic odd-degree hyperelliptic curves of genus g ≥ 4 g\geq 4 have no unexpected quadratic points
Jef Laga, Ashvin A. Swaminathan

Abstract
Let ℱ g \mathcal{F}_{g} be the family of monic odd-degree hyperelliptic curves of genus g g over ℚ \mathbb{Q} . Poonen and Stoll have shown that for every g ≥ 3 g\geq 3 , a positive proportion of curves in ℱ g \mathcal{F}_{g} have no rational points except the point at infinity. In this note, we prove the analogue for quadratic points: for each g ≥ 4 g\geq 4 , a positive proportion of curves in ℱ g \mathcal{F}_{g} have no points defined over quadratic extensions except those that arise by pulling back rational points from ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} .



1
Introduction

Let
C/\mathbb{Q}
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
.
Faltings has shown that the set of rational points
C(\mathbb{Q})
is finite
[
Fal83
, Satz 7]
. It is therefore natural to ask how the quantity
\#C(\mathbb{Q})
is distributed as
C
varies in a family of curves. Many authors (see, e.g.,
[
PV04
, Conjecture 2.2]
,
[
Poo06
]
,
[
Gra07
, Conjecture 1.3(ii)]
,
[
Sto09
, Conjecture 1]
) have formulated heuristics or conjectures suggesting that in a family of curves
C
of genus
g\geq 2
, the quantity
\#C(\mathbb{Q})
is very often as small as possible: i.e., most members ought to possess no rational points beyond those that exist generically among all members in the family. Put rather simply, most curves in a family should be as “pointless” as can be.

This pithy principle has been proven (or partially proven) for several families of curves of interest; see, e.g.,
[
Bha13
]
,
[
BGW17
]
(cf.
[
BSS21
, Remark 3.3]
),
[
RT21
]
,
[
SW18
]
,
[
Lag22
]
.
Of particular relevance to this note is the major result of Poonen and Stoll
[
PS14
]
, which concerns the family of monic odd-degree hyperelliptic curves

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{g}\vcentcolon=\{C\colon y^{2}=f(x)=x^{2g+1}+a_{1}x^{2g}+\dots+a_{2g+1}\mid a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},\,\text{disc}(f)\neq 0\},

ordered by the height
0pt(C)=\max|a_{i}|^{1/i}
. Here
C
denotes the unique smooth projective curve with affine equation
y^{2}=f(x)
; it has a unique point at infinity
P_{\infty}
, which is
\mathbb{Q}
-rational.
If
g\geq 3
, Poonen and Stoll showed that a positive proportion of curves
C\in\mathcal{F}_{g}
satisfy
C(\mathbb{Q})=\{P_{\infty}\}
, and that this proportion tends to
1
exponentially fast as
g\rightarrow\infty
.

In this note, we consider quadratic points on curves in the family
\mathcal{F}_{g}
.
Recall that a point
P\in C(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
is quadratic if its field of definition
\mathbb{Q}(P)
is a quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
.
When
C
is hyperelliptic, it visibly has infinitely many quadratic points: since
C
has a degree-
2
map
\pi\colon C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, for all but finitely many points
P\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
the preimage
\pi^{-1}(P)
consists of a Galois-conjugate pair of quadratic points.
Explicitly, if
P=(x:1)\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
then
\pi^{-1}(P)=(x,\pm\sqrt{f(x)})\in C(\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
.
It follows from a more general result of Faltings
[
Fal91
, Theorem 1 & Example 4.5]
that if
C/\mathbb{Q}
is a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g\geq 4
, then
C
has only finitely many quadratic points not arising as pullbacks from
rational points on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

1.1
Results

If
C\in\mathcal{F}_{g}
, we say a rational point
P
on
C
is
expected
if
P=P_{\infty}
, and unexpected otherwise.
We say a quadratic point
P
on
C
is
expected
if it maps to a rational point on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
, and unexpected otherwise.
Our main result shows that when
g\geq 4
, a positive proportion of curves in
\mathcal{F}_{g}
have no unexpected rational or quadratic points at all.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{g,X}\vcentcolon=\{C\in\mathcal{F}_{g}\mid\operatorname{ht}(C)<X\}
, and define the lower density

\displaystyle\delta_{g}\vcentcolon=\liminf_{X\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{C\in\mathcal{F}_{g,X}\mid C\text{ has no unexpected rational points or quadratic points}\}}{\#\mathcal{F}_{g,X}}.

Theorem 1
.

Let
g\geq 4
. When ordered by height, a positive proportion of monic odd hyperelliptic curves
C/\mathbb{Q}
of genus
g
possess no unexpected rational points or quadratic points.
In other words,
\delta_{g}>0
.

Theorem
1
is the first unconditional result of its kind to effectivize Faltings’ theorem on the finiteness of unexpected quadratic points in a “large” family of curves.

1.2
Methods

The proof of Theorem
1
relies on partially generalizing the method of Poonen and Stoll
[
PS14
]
(later dubbed “Selmer group Chabauty” in
[
Sto17
]
) to symmetric squares of curves.
Roughly speaking, we proceed in three steps as follows:

1.

Given a subvariety
X
of a
g
-dimensional abelian variety
J/\mathbb{Q}
and a prime number
p
, we prove a criterion guaranteeing that
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\cap J(\mathbb{Q})
is “small”, under suitable assumptions concerning the disjointness of two sets: (1) the image of
\Sel_{p}J
in
J(\mathbb{Q}_{p})/pJ(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, and (2) the image of
X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
under the re-scale and reduce mod-
p
logarithm map
\rho\log\colon X(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{g-1}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
; see §
2
— specifically, Proposition
2
.

2.

Given a curve
C\in\mathcal{F}_{g}
, we wish to apply the criterion to
J=
the Jacobian of
C
,
X=
the image
\Sym^{2}C\rightarrow J
, and
p=2
. This leads to a criterion for
C
to have no unexpected rational or quadratic points; see §
3
— specifically, Corollary
5
.

3.

We show that the latter criterion is satisfied for a positive proportion of
C\in\mathcal{F}_{g}
when
g\geq 4
using two inputs: (1) an equidistribution theorem concerning the image of
\Sel_{2}J\rightarrow J(\mathbb{Q}_{2})/2J(\mathbb{Q}_{2})
as
C
varies in
\mathcal{F}_{g}
due to Bhargava–Gross
[
BG13
, Theorem 12.4]
, and (2) the construction of an explicit subfamily
\mathcal{F}_{g}^{(2)}\subset\mathcal{F}_{g}
having positive density on which we can control the image
\rho\log(X(\mathbb{Q}_{2}))
; see §
5
— specifically, Proposition
8
.

By calculating the density of
\mathcal{F}_{g}^{(2)}
, we obtain explicit lower bounds on the density
\delta_{g}
, proving that
\delta_{g}\geq 2^{-4g^{2}-6g-7}
; since the curves in
\mathcal{F}_{g}^{(2)}
all have good reduction at
2
, the bound must be exponentially small in
g
: indeed, it is exceedingly rare for a hyperelliptic curve in short Weierstrass form to have good reduction at
2
.

If one would like to go beyond Theorem
1
and prove — analogously to Poonen and Stoll — that
\delta_{g}\rightarrow 1
as
g\rightarrow\infty
, one would need to control the image of
\rho\log\colon X(\mathbb{Q}_{2})\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{g-1}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
for a density
1
set of curves
C\in\mathcal{F}_{g}
, with arbitrary reduction type at
2
.
The methods of
[
PS14
, §3.2, §5]
do not directly apply, due to the lack of the Weierstrass preparation theorem for power series over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
in more than one variable.
It therefore seems that new ideas are needed here.

1.3
Previous work

Using just their result that the average size of
\operatorname{Sel}_{2}J
is at most
3
in conjunction with an effective version of Chabauty’s method, Bhargava and Gross show that a positive proportion of curves in
\mathcal{F}_{g}
have at most
3
rational points. It is natural to ask whether a similar strategy would work to control points of degree
d
. A version of Chabauty’s method for symmetric powers of curves was developed by Siksek
[
Sik09
]
, and Park
[
Par16
]
used to tropical intersection theory to develop an effective version of Siksek’s method. However, it was later discovered by Gunther and Morrow
[
GM19
, Remark 5.6]
that Park’s work is missing a transversality hypothesis; while it may be feasible to verify this hypothesis for individual curves, it is currently unknown how to do so in a family of curves. Conditional on this transversality hypothesis, Gunther and Morrow prove that a positive proportion of curves in
\mathcal{F}_{g}
have at most 24 unexpected quadratic points for each
g\geq 3
. Our main result, Theorem
1
, unconditionally reduces their bound for quadratic points to zero when
g\geq 4
.

In a different direction, there are several papers in the literature where versions of Chabauty’s method are used to obtain uniform bounds on the number of rational points on curves; here the bounds tend to depend on the genus as well as on the Mordell–Weil rank of the Jacobian. See, e.g., the works of Stoll
[
Sto19
]
and Katz, Rabinoff, and Zureick-Brown
[
KRZB16
]
. Analogous uniform bounds were obtained for quadratic points by Vemulapalli and Wang
[
VW21
]
, but their results depend on the aforementioned work of Park
[
Par16
]
and are thus conditional.

We end by mentioning the work of Caro and Pasten
[
CP23
]
, which has consequences for quadratic points on non-hyperelliptic curves whose Jacobian has Mordell–Weil rank at most one.

3
A criterion for every rational and quadratic point to be expected

In this section, we specialize the content of §
2
to the case of Jacobians of monic odd-degree hyperelliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
, and we obtain a criterion — namely, Corollary
5
— for every rational point and every quadratic point on such a curve to be expected.

We start slightly more generally and introduce some notation that we will use in the remainder of the paper.
Let
C
be a hyperelliptic curve of genus
g\geq 2
.
Denote its Jacobian variety by
J
. There is by definition a degree-
2
map
\pi\colon C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, called the hyperelliptic map, which is unique up to automorphism of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
We call the pullback along
\pi
of a
\mathbb{Q}
-point of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
a hyperelliptic divisor, and the linear equivalence class of such a divisor the hyperelliptic divisor class
H
.
The associated linear system is denoted
|H|\vcentcolon=\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{H}^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}_{C}(H)))\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and consists of divisors of the form
\pi^{*}(t)
, where
t
is a point of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
(When
C\in\mathcal{F}_{g}
, a convenient choice for
H
is the class
[2P_{\infty}]
.)

The symmetric square
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}C
is a smooth, projective, and geometrically integral variety parametrizing effective divisors of degree
2
on
C
.
We may therefore view
|H|
as a closed subscheme of
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}C
.
Let
\operatorname{AJ}\colon\Sym^{2}C\rightarrow J
be the Abel–Jacobi morphism sending an effective degree-
2
divisor
D
to the divisor class
[D]-H
.
Let

\displaystyle X\vcentcolon=\operatorname{AJ}(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}C)

(2)

be the scheme-theoretic image of
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}C
under
\operatorname{AJ}
. The tangent space of the point
0
in
X
has dimension
g

[
ACGH85
, Chapter IV, Proposition (4.2)]
, so if
g\geq 3
, then
X
has a singular point, resolved by the map
\operatorname{AJ}\colon\operatorname{Sym}^{2}C\rightarrow X
.

Lemma 3
.

The morphism
\mathrm{AJ}\colon\operatorname{Sym}^{2}C\rightarrow J
is birational onto its image

.
It is an isomorphism above

and the
(

)

is the closed subscheme
|H|\subset\operatorname{Sym}^{2}C
.

Proof.

The fiber above
[E]\in J
is the projective space of divisors

in the linear system
|E+H|
by
[
ACGH85
, Chapter IV, Lemma (1.1)]
.
Since
|H|
is the only positive dimensional degree-
2

∎

Lemma 4
.

Suppose that
X(\mathbb{Q})=\{0\}
.
Then
C(\mathbb{Q})
consists of at most one Weierstrass point, and every quadratic point on
C
maps to
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
under

Proof.

Using Lemma
3
, the condition
X(\mathbb{Q})=\{0\}
implies that
(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}C)(\mathbb{Q})=|H|(\mathbb{Q})
.
In other words, every
\mathbb{Q}
-rational effective degree-

for some

.
One can check that this implies the conclusions of the lemma.
∎

Applying Proposition

to our choice of

Corollary 5
.

Let

•

•

\mathbb{P}\sigma(\Sel_{p}J)\cap\rho\log(X(\mathbb{Q}_{p}))=\varnothing

•

Then

4
Controlling the image
(\Sym^{2}C)(\mathbb{Q}_{2})
under
\rho\log

The purpose of this section is to construct a subfamily
\mathcal{F}_{g}^{(2)}\subset\mathcal{F}_{g}
of positive density for which (in the notation of §
3
)
X(\mathbb{Q}_{2})\cap J(\mathbb{Q}_{2})[2^{\prime}]=\{0\}
and for which we can control the image
\rho\log(X(\mathbb{Q}_{2}))
.

In
[
PS14
]
, which concerns rational points on
C
, the corresponding problem is to control the set
\rho\log(C(\mathbb{Q}_{2}))
. To do this, Poonen and Stoll consider a particular curve
C_{0}/\mathbb{Q}_{2}
— namely, the curve with long Weierstrass equation
C_{0}\colon y^{2}+y=x^{2g+1}+x+1
— and they prove for this choice of
C_{0}
that
\#\rho\log(C_{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{2}))=1
.
They then separately prove that as
C
varies in the moduli space of monic odd hyperelliptic curves of genus
g
over
\mathbb{Q}_{2}
, the set
\rho\log(C(\mathbb{Q}_{2}))
is locally constant, from which it follows that for every
g\geq 3
a positive proportion of curves
C\in\mathcal{F}_{g}
have
\#\rho\log(C(\mathbb{Q}_{2}))=1
.

Here, we adopt a slightly different approach: we construct a subfamily
\mathcal{F}_{g}^{(2)}\subset\mathcal{F}_{g}
, defined by congruence conditions, such that the image
\rho\log((\operatorname{Sym}^{2}C)(\mathbb{Q}_{2}))
can be explicitly bounded for
all

C\in\mathcal{F}_{g}^{(2)}

5
Proof of Theorem
1

The proof of Theorem
1
is completed by the next proposition.
Just as in
[
PS14
]
, a crucial ingredient in the proof is an equidistribution result of Bhargava–Gross
[
BG13
, Theorem 12.4]
.
If
S\subset T\subset\mathcal{F}_{g}
are subsets, the relative density of
S
in
T
is by definition the quantity

\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\#(S\cap\mathcal{F}_{g,X})/\#(T\cap\mathcal{F}_{g,X})
,
whenever it exists.
Define the relative lower and upper density of
S
in
T
by replacing the limit by
\liminf
and
\limsup
, respectively.

Proposition 10
.

The relative lower density of the set

\displaystyle\{C\in\mathcal{F}_{g}^{(2)}\mid C\text{ has no unexpected rational or quadratic points}\}

(11)

in
\mathcal{F}_{g}^{(2)}
is at least
1-6\times 2^{1-g}
.
Consequently, if
g\geq 4
, a positive proportion of curves in
\mathcal{F}_{g}
have no unexpected rational or quadratic points.

Proof.

This is a standard adaptation of
[
PS14
, Proposition 8.13]
to our setting.
Proposition
8
shows that
\mathcal{F}_{g}^{(2)}
is partitioned into finitely many subsets
\mathcal{G}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{G}_{m}
, each defined by congruence conditions modulo a power of
2
, such that the subset
\rho\log(X(\mathbb{Q}_{2}))\subset\mathbb{P}^{g-1}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
is constant when
h
varies in
\mathcal{G}_{i}
.
Fix such an
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
.
If
C\in\mathcal{G}_{i}
, then
J(\mathbb{Q}_{2})[2]=0
by Lemma
7
(a)
, so the logarithm map defined using the basis (
5
) induces an isomorphism
J(\mathbb{Q}_{2})/2J(\mathbb{Q}_{2})\simeq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{g}
.
Write
\sigma\colon\Sel_{2}J\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}^{g}
for the corresponding composition
\Sel_{2}J\rightarrow J(\mathbb{Q}_{2})/2J(\mathbb{Q}_{2})\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}^{g}
considered in §
3
.
Bhargava–Gross have shown
[
BG13
, Theorem 12.4]
that nontrivial elements of
\Sel_{2}J
equidistribute under
\sigma
: when
C
varies in
\mathcal{G}_{i}
and
I\subset\mathbb{F}_{2}^{g}
is a subset, they show that the average size of
\{s\in\Sel_{2}J\setminus\{0\}\colon\sigma(s)\in I\}
is at most
\#I\times 2^{1-g}
.
It follows that the relative upper density of curves
C\in\mathcal{G}_{i}
such that
\{s\in\Sel_{2}J\setminus\{0\}\colon\sigma(s)\in I\}
is nonempty is at most
\#I\times 2^{1-g}
.
Applying this to

and using Proposition
8
, we see that the relative lower density of curves

such that the first two conditions of Corollary

are satisfied is at least

.
The third condition of Corollary

is always satisfied for

by Lemma

(c)
.
We conclude that the intersection of the set (

) with
\mathcal{G}_{i}
has relative lower density at least
1-6\times 2^{1-g}
in
\mathcal{G}_{i}
.
Summing over

, the set (
11
) itself has relative lower density at least
1-6\times 2^{1-g}
in
\mathcal{F}_{g}^{(2)}
.

Thus, the lower density of curves in
\mathcal{F}_{g}
that have no unexpected rational or quadratic points is at least
(1-6\times 2^{1-g})\times(\text{density of }\mathcal{F}_{g}^{(2)})=(1-6\times 2^{1-g})\times 2^{-4g^{2}-6g-5}
by Lemma
9
.
This is positive when
g\geq 4
.
∎

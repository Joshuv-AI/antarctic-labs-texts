MODULAR WELCH BOUNDS WITH APPLICATIONS
K. MAHESH KRISHNA

Abstract




Abstract
: We prove the following two results.

(1)

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital commutative C*-algebra and
\mathcal{A}^{d}
be the standard Hilbert C*-module over
\mathcal{A}
. Let
n\geq d
. If
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is any collection of vectors in
\mathcal{A}^{d}
such that
\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{j}\rangle=1
,
\forall 1\leq j\leq n
, then

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle||^{2m}\geq\frac{1}{n-1}\left[\frac{n}{{d+m-1\choose m}}-1\right],\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\sigma
-finite commutative W*-algebra or a commutative AW*-algebra and
\mathcal{E}
be a rank d Hilbert C*-module over
\mathcal{A}
. Let
n\geq d
. If
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is any collection of vectors in
\mathcal{E}
such that
\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{j}\rangle=1
,
\forall 1\leq j\leq n
, then

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle||^{2m}\geq\frac{1}{n-1}\left[\frac{n}{{d+m-1\choose m}}-1\right],\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

Results (1) and (2) reduce to the famous result of Welch [
IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, 1974
] obtained 48 years ago. We introduce the notions of modular frame potential, modular equiangular frames and modular Grassmannian frames. We formulate Zauner’s conjecture for Hilbert C*-modules.

Keywords
: Welch bound, C*-algebra, W*-algebra, invariant basis number, Grassmannian frames, Zauner’s conjecture.

1.
Introduction

In the study of cross correlations of signals, L. Welch proved the following result in 1974.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
87
]
(
Welch bounds
)
Let
n\geq d
. If
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is any collection of unit vectors in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle|^{2m}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{{d+m-1\choose m}},\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle|^{2}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{{d}}.

Further,

(1)

\displaystyle\text{({Higher order Welch bounds})}\quad\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle|^{2m}\geq\frac{1}{n-1}\left[\frac{n}{{d+m-1\choose m}}-1\right],\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\text{({First order Welch bound})}\quad\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle|^{2}\geq\frac{n-d}{d(n-1)}.

2.
Modular Welch bounds

We first derive Welch bound for standard Hilbert C*-modules over unital commutative C*-algebras. First we need a definition.

Definition 2.1
.

A collection
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
in a Hilbert C*-module
\mathcal{E}
over unital C*-algebra
\mathcal{A}
with identity
1
is said to have
unit inner product
if

\displaystyle\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{j}\rangle=1,\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq n.

It is clear that unit inner product vectors have unit norm but not the converse.

Theorem 2.2
.

(Modular Welch bounds)

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital commutative C*-algebra and
\mathcal{A}^{d}
be the standard Hilbert C*-module over
\mathcal{A}
. Let
n\geq d
. If
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is any collection of unit inner product vectors in
\mathcal{A}^{d}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2m}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle^{m}\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle^{m}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{{d+m-1\choose m}},\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle\geq\frac{n^{2}}{{d}}.

Further,

\displaystyle\text{({Higher order modular Welch bounds})}\quad\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2m}\geq\frac{1}{n-1}\left[\frac{n}{{d+m-1\choose m}}-1\right],\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\text{({First order modular Welch bound})}\quad\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2}\geq\frac{n-d}{d(n-1)}.

Proof.

Our proof is motivated from the proof of Welch
[
87
]
. Let
\tau_{j}\coloneqq(a_{1}^{(j)},a_{2}^{(j)},\dots,a_{d}^{(j)})
for all
1\leq j\leq n
. Define

\displaystyle B_{m}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle^{m}\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle^{m}.

Then using C*-algebra identity,

\displaystyle B_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle^{m}\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle^{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{j}\rangle^{2m}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle^{m}\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle^{m}\|+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{j}\rangle^{2m}\|

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle^{m}\|^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{j}\rangle\|^{2m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle^{m}\|^{2}+n

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2m}+n

\displaystyle\leq(n^{2}-n)\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2m}+n.

On the other hand, using the definition of standard inner product and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in Hilbert C*-modules,

\displaystyle B_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{r=1}^{d}a_{r}^{(j)}(a_{r}^{(k)})^{*}\right)\left(\sum_{s=1}^{d}a_{s}^{(k)}(a_{s}^{(j)})^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{u_{1}\cdots u_{m}=1,v_{1}\cdots v_{m}=1}^{d}\prod_{p=1}^{m}a_{u_{p}}^{(j)}(a_{v_{p}}^{(j)})^{*}(a_{u_{p}}^{(k)})^{*}a_{v_{p}}^{(k)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{u_{1}\cdots u_{m}=1,v_{1}\cdots v_{m}=1}^{d}\left(\prod_{p=1}^{m}a_{u_{p}}^{(j)}(a_{v_{p}}^{(j)})^{*}\right)\left(\prod_{q=1}^{m}a_{u_{q}}^{(j)}(a_{v_{q}}^{(j)})^{*}\right)^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{1}\cdots x_{d},y_{1}\cdots y_{d},\sum_{r=1}^{d}x_{r}=\sum_{s=1}^{d}y_{s}=m}{m\choose x_{1},\dots,x_{d}}{m\choose y_{1},\dots,y_{d}}\times

\displaystyle\quad\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{r=1}^{d}(a_{r}^{(j)})^{x_{r}}((a_{r}^{(j)})^{*})^{y_{r}}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{s=1}^{d}(a_{s}^{(k)})^{x_{s}}((a_{s}^{(k)})^{*})^{y_{s}}\right)^{*}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x_{1}\cdots x_{d},\sum_{r=1}^{d}x_{r}=m}\left({m\choose x_{1},\dots,x_{d}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{r=1}^{d}(a_{r}^{(j)})^{x_{r}}((a_{r}^{(j)})^{*})^{x_{r}}\right)\times

\displaystyle\quad\left({m\choose x_{1},\dots,x_{d}}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\prod_{l=1}^{d}(a_{l}^{(t)})^{x_{l}}((a_{l}^{(t)})^{*})^{x_{l}}\right)^{*}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\left\|\sum_{x_{1}\cdots x_{d},\sum_{r=1}^{d}x_{r}=m}\right\|}\left(\sum_{x_{1}\cdots x_{d},\sum_{r=1}^{d}x_{r}=m}{m\choose x_{1},\dots,x_{d}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{r=1}^{d}(a_{r}^{(j)})^{x_{r}}((a_{r}^{(j)})^{*})^{x_{r}}\right)\times

\displaystyle\quad\left(\sum_{x_{1}\cdots x_{d},\sum_{s=1}^{d}x_{s}=m}{m\choose x_{1},\dots,x_{d}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{s=1}^{d}(a_{s}^{(k)})^{x_{s}}((a_{s}^{(k)})^{*})^{x_{s}}\right)^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{d+m-1\choose m}}\left(\sum_{x_{1}\cdots x_{d},\sum_{r=1}^{d}x_{r}=m}{m\choose x_{1},\dots,x_{d}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{r=1}^{d}(a_{r}^{(j)})^{x_{r}}((a_{r}^{(j)})^{*})^{x_{r}}\right)\times

\displaystyle\quad\left(\sum_{x_{1}\cdots x_{d},\sum_{s=1}^{d}x_{s}=m}{m\choose x_{1},\dots,x_{d}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\prod_{s=1}^{d}(a_{s}^{(k)})^{x_{s}}((a_{s}^{(k)})^{*})^{x_{s}}\right)^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{d+m-1\choose m}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{x_{1}\cdots x_{d},\sum_{r=1}^{d}x_{r}=m}{m\choose x_{1},\dots,x_{d}}\prod_{r=1}^{d}(a_{r}^{(j)})^{x_{r}}((a_{r}^{(j)})^{*})^{x_{r}}\right)\times

\displaystyle\quad\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{x_{1}\cdots x_{d},\sum_{s=1}^{d}x_{s}=m}{m\choose x_{1},\dots,x_{d}}\prod_{s=1}^{d}(a_{s}^{(k)})^{x_{s}}((a_{s}^{(k)})^{*})^{x_{s}}\right)^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{d+m-1\choose m}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{p=1}^{d}a_{p}^{(j)}(a_{p}^{(j)})^{*}\right)^{m}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{q=1}^{d}a_{q}^{(k)}(a_{q}^{(k)})^{*}\right)^{m}\right)^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{d+m-1\choose m}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(1\right)^{m}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(1\right)^{m}\right)^{*}=\frac{n^{2}}{{d+m-1\choose m}}.

Hence

\displaystyle(n^{2}-n)\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2m}+n

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2m}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle^{m}\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle^{m}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n^{2}}{{d+m-1\choose m}}.

∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Theorem
1.1
is a corollary of Theorem
2.2
.

Note that proof of Theorem
2.2
works for the standard Hilbert C*-module w.r.t. standard inner product. We now try to get the result for arbitrary Hilbert C*-module. For this, we need some results and concepts.

It is well known that trace of an operator can be determined using orthonormal basis and also from frames in Hilbert spaces (see
[
34
]
). We now try to derive such results for Hilbert C*-modules.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a commutative unital C*-algebra and
\mathcal{E}
be a finite rank (of rank
d
) Hilbert C*-module over
\mathcal{A}
. If
T:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
is an adjointable homomorphism, then for any two (and hence for all) orthonormal bases
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}
and
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}
for
\mathcal{E}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle T\tau_{j},\tau_{j}\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle T\omega_{j},\omega_{j}\rangle.

Proof.

Note that commutative C*-algebras have invariant basis number property
[
30
]
(see
[
55
,
54
,
53
,
52
,
51
,
50
]
for more on invariant basis number property for rings). Hence any two bases have same number of elements. By using the commutativity of the C*-algebra, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle T\tau_{j},\tau_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\langle T\tau_{j},\sum_{k=1}^{d}\langle\tau_{j},\omega_{k}\rangle\omega_{k}\right\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\langle T\tau_{j},\omega_{k}\rangle\langle\omega_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle T\tau_{j},\omega_{k}\rangle\langle\omega_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\langle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\omega_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle\tau_{j},T^{*}\omega_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\left\langle\omega_{k},T^{*}\omega_{k}\right\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\langle T\omega_{k},\omega_{k}\rangle.

∎

Theorem
2.4
makes the following definition meaningful.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a commutative unital C*-algebra and
\mathcal{E}
be a finite rank (of rank
d
) Hilbert C*-module over
\mathcal{A}
. If
T:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
is an adjointable homomorphism, then the
trace
of
T
is defined as

\displaystyle\text{Tra}(T)\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle T\tau_{j},\tau_{j}\rangle,

for some orthonormal basis
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}
for
\mathcal{E}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Given a collection
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
in a Hilbert C*-module, the
frame homomorphism

S_{\tau}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
is defined as

\displaystyle S_{\tau}x\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle x,\tau_{j}\rangle\tau_{j},\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{E}.

Next result shows that trace of frame homomorphism can be recovered from its defining elements.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a commutative unital C*-algebra and
\mathcal{E}
be a
d
-rank Hilbert C*-module over
\mathcal{A}
. If
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is any collection in
\mathcal{E}
, then

\displaystyle\text{Tra}(S_{\tau})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{j}\rangle,

\displaystyle\text{Tra}(S_{\tau}^{2})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle.

Proof.

Let
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{E}
. Then

\displaystyle\text{Tra}(S_{\tau})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle S_{\tau}\omega_{j},\omega_{j}\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\langle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\omega_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\tau_{k},\omega_{j}\right\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\omega_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\langle\tau_{k},\omega_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle\omega_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\langle\tau_{k},\omega_{j}\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\langle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle\tau_{k},\omega_{j}\rangle\omega_{j},\tau_{k}\right\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{k}\rangle

and

\displaystyle\text{Tra}(S_{\tau}^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle S_{\tau}^{2}\omega_{j},\omega_{j}\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle S_{\tau}\omega_{j},S_{\tau}\omega_{j}\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\langle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\omega_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\tau_{k},S_{\tau}\omega_{j}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\omega_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\langle\tau_{k},S_{\tau}\omega_{j}\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\langle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle\tau_{k},S_{\tau}\omega_{j}\rangle\omega_{j},\tau_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\langle\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle S_{\tau}^{*}\tau_{k},\omega_{j}\rangle\omega_{j},\tau_{k}\right\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle S_{\tau}^{*}\tau_{k},\tau_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle S_{\tau}\tau_{k},\tau_{k}\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\langle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\right\rangle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\right\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle.

∎

For vector spaces we have the following result.

Theorem 2.8
.

[
17
,
10

If
\mathcal{V}
is a vector space of dimension
d
and
\text{Sym}^{m}(\mathcal{V})

\displaystyle\text{dim}(\text{Sym}^{m}(\mathcal{V}))={d+m-1\choose m},\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

2.8
easily for modules and get the following result.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a ring with invariant basis number property. If
\mathcal{M}
is a module of rank
d
and
\text{Sym}^{m}(\mathcal{M})
denotes the module of symmetric m-tensors, then

\displaystyle\text{rank}(\text{Sym}^{m}(\mathcal{M}))={d+m-1\choose m},\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

Definition 2.10
.

[
57
]

A W*-algebra is called
\sigma
-finite
if it contains no more than a countable set of mutually orthogonal projections.

Definition 2.11
.

[
41
]

A C*-algebra
\mathcal{A}
is called an
AW*-algebra
if the following conditions hold.

(i)

Any set of orthogonal projections has supremum.

(ii)

Any maximal commutative self-adjoint subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}
is generated by its projections.

Theorem 2.12
.

[
57
,
35
]

(
Spectral theorem for Hilbert C*-modules
) Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
\sigma
-finite W*-algebra or an AW*-algebra and let
\mathcal{E}
be a finite rank Hilbert C*-module over
\mathcal{A}
. If
T:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}
is a normal adjointable homomorphism, then it can be diagonalized (by a unitary homomorphism).

Theorem 2.13
.

(Modular Welch bounds)

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a commutative
\sigma
-finite W*-algebra or a commutative AW*-algebra. Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Hilbert C*-module over
\mathcal{A}
of rank
d
. Let
n\geq d
. If
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is any collection of unit inner product vectors in
\mathcal{E}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2m}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle^{m}\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle^{m}\geq\frac{n^{2}}{{d+m-1\choose m}},\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle\geq\frac{n^{2}}{{d}}.

Further,

\displaystyle\text{({Higher order modular Welch bounds})}\quad\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2m}\geq\frac{1}{n-1}\left[\frac{n}{{d+m-1\choose m}}-1\right],\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\text{({First order modular Welch bound})}\quad\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2}\geq\frac{n-d}{d(n-1)}.

Proof.

We first note that a W*-algebra as well as an AW*-algebra is always unital
[
71
,
72
]
. Next note that commutative C*-algebras have invariant basis number property
[
30
]
and hence the notion rank is meaningful. We consider the collection
\{\tau^{\otimes m}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
in
\text{Sym}^{m}(\mathcal{E})
. Theorem
2.12
says that the adjointable positive (self-adjoint) homomorphism

\displaystyle S_{\tau}:\text{Sym}^{m}(\mathcal{E})\ni x\mapsto\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle x,\tau^{\otimes m}_{j}\rangle\tau^{\otimes m}_{j}\in\text{Sym}^{m}(\mathcal{E})

can be diagonalized. Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\text{rank}(\text{Sym}^{m}(\mathcal{E}))}
be the eigenvalues of
S_{\tau}
. Using Theorem
2.7
, Theorem
2.9
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in Hilbert C*-module, we then get

\displaystyle n^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{j}\rangle^{m}\right)^{2}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j}^{\otimes m},\tau_{j}^{\otimes m}\rangle\right)^{2}=(\operatorname{Tra}(S_{\tau}))^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\text{rank}(\text{Sym}^{m}(\mathcal{E}))}\lambda_{k}\right)^{2}\leq\text{rank}(\text{Sym}^{m}(\mathcal{E}))\sum_{k=1}^{\text{rank}(\text{Sym}^{m}(\mathcal{E}))}\lambda_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle={d+m-1\choose m}\operatorname{Tra}(S^{2}_{\tau})={d+m-1\choose m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j}^{\otimes m},\tau^{\otimes m}_{k}\rangle\langle\tau^{\otimes m}_{k},\tau_{j}^{\otimes m}\rangle.

\displaystyle={d+m-1\choose m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle^{m}\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle^{m}

and hence

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}}{{d+m-1\choose m}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle^{m}\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle^{m}=\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle^{m}\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle^{m}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{j}\rangle^{2m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle^{m}\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle^{m}+n\leq\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle^{m}\langle\tau_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle^{m}\|+n

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2m}+n\leq(n^{2}-n)\max_{1\leq j,k\leq n,j\neq k}\|\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle\|^{2m}+n

which gives the required inequality.
∎

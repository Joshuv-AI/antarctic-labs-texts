Universal Cusp Scaling in Random Partitions
Taro Kimura, Ali Zahabi

Abstract
We study the universal scaling limit of random partitions obeying the Schur measure. Extending our previous analysis [ KZ20 ] , we obtain the higher-order Pearcey kernel describing the multi-critical behavior in the cusp scaling limit. We explore the gap probability associated with the higher Pearcey kernel, and derive the coupled nonlinear differential equation and the asymptotic behavior in the large gap limit.



1
Introduction and summary

Universality of the eigenvalue statistics in the scaling limit is one of the key concepts in the study of random matrices
[
Meh04
,
For10
,
ABDF11
]
.
For example, the Tracy–Widom distribution
[
TW92
]
, which was originally introduced to describe the largest eigenvalue statistics of the Gaussian Unitary Ensemble (also known as edge statistics), is now applied to a large number of statistical problems in various contexts.
Typically, the eigenvalue density function
\rho(x)
of the standard Hermitian single random matrix is obtained by solving a second order algebraic equation with respect to the auxiliary function, called the resolvent.
Hence, in the vicinity of the spectral edge
x_{*}
, the density function shows square root singularity,
\rho(x)\sim(x_{*}-x)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, where the eigenvalue statistics is well described by the Airy kernel
[
For93
,
NW93
]
.
Replacing such a square root singularity with a higher order one,
\rho(x)\sim(x_{*}-x)^{\frac{1}{p}}
with
p
even, which would be realized in the fermion momenta distribution in the non-harmonic trap
[
LDMS18
]
, one may obtain the higher-order analog of the Airy kernel and the corresponding Tracy–Widom distribution
[
CKI09
,
AA12
,
LDMS18
,
CCG19
]
.

A similar singular behavior is observed at the cusp point, appearing in the collision limit of the eigenvalue supports, that we call the cusp statistics.
In this case, we shall apply the Pearcey kernel, an analog of the Airy kernel using the Pearcey integral function
[
Pea46
]
, to describe the eigenvalue statistics in the vicinity of the cusp singularity
[
BH98a
,
BH98b
]
.
See also
[
BH16
]
.
Similar to the edge statistics, the cusp statistics is widely discussed in various contexts, including stochastic process
[
TW04
]
and asymptotics of the combinatorial problems
[
OR05
]
.

In this paper, we study the random partition distribution based on the Schur measure
[
Oko99
]
, which depends on two sets of parameters,
\mathsf{X}=(\mathsf{x}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathsf{Y}=(\mathsf{y}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.
It has been known that the Schur measure random partition is a discrete determinantal point process:
The correlation function is obtained as a determinant of the associated discrete kernel
K(x,y)
for
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
.
See (
2.11
) for the definition of the kernel.
In our previous paper
[
KZ20
]
(see also
[
BBW20
]
), we showed that this kernel is asymptotic to the higher Airy kernel in the scaling limit under the condition
\mathsf{X}=\mathsf{Y}
.
In this paper, we relax this condition and consider generic parameters
\mathsf{X}
and
\mathsf{Y}
.
Then, we obtain the following scaling limit of the Schur measure kernel.

Proposition 1.1

(Proposition
3.8
)
.

We have the following asymptotic behavior of the Schur measure kernel under the multicritical condition (
3.15
),

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\quantity(\frac{\alpha_p}{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{p+1}}K\quantity(\frac{\beta}{\epsilon} + \qty(\frac{\alpha_p}{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{p+1}} x, \frac{\beta}{\epsilon} + \qty(\frac{\alpha_p}{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{p+1}} y)=K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,y)\,,

(1.1)

where

are the constants defined in (
3.4
), and

is the higher Airy kernel constructed with the higher Airy functions (Definition
3.6
).

The kernel

is identified with the known higher Airy kernel for even
p
, while it gives the higher-order analog of the Pearcey kernel for odd
p
.
We emphasize that the higher Pearcey kernel appearing in the odd
p
scaling limit is realized only when we relax the condition
\mathsf{X}=\mathsf{Y}
.

As in the case of the ordinary Airy kernel, the higher Airy kernel is defined as an integral of the bilinear form of the corresponding higher Airy functions, which can be rewritten in the following form.

Proposition 1.2

(Chistoffel–Darboux-type formula, Proposition
3.10
)
.

The
p
-Airy kernel is written as follows,

\displaystyle K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x-y}\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k}\operatorname{Ai}_{p}^{(p-k)}(x)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}^{(k-1)}(y)\,,

(1.2)

where
\operatorname{Ai}_{p}^{(k)}
and
\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}^{(k)}
are
k
-th derivatives of the
p
-Airy functions (Definition
3.24
).

This formula reproduces the known result for even
p

[
LDMS18
]
, and generalizes the result for
p=3,5
presented in
[
BH98a
,
BH98b
]
.
See Example
3.11
.

In addition, we apply the saddle point approximation to study the asymptotic behavior of the higher Airy functions.
Although the saddle point analysis presented here is not rigorous, rather heuristic, it provides an efficient way to discuss the asymptotic behavior.
As an application of this analysis, we obtain the asymptotic behavior of the scaled density function, which is given by the diagonal value of the
p
-Airy kernel (Proposition
3.14
).

Once given such a kernel, one can formulate the probability such that no “particle” is found in the interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, which is called the gap probability, using the Fredholm determinant associated with the kernel.
We study the gap probability based on the higher Pearcey kernel, and obtain the underlying Hamiltonian system (Proposition
4.10
) similarly to the Fredholm determinant associated with other kernels, i.e., the Fredholm determinant behaves as the isomonodromic
\tau
-function.
We in particular consider the so-called level spacing distribution associated with the interval

.
In this case, we obtain the coupled nonlinear differential equations (Proposition
5.8
).
We also obtain the large gap asymptotics of the level spacing distribution.

Proposition 1.3

(Proposition
5.10
)
.

Let

be the Fredholm determinant defined by the

-Airy kernel with the interval

, which provides the gap probability for the determinantal point process associated with the corresponding kernel.
We have the following large gap behavior,

\displaystyle F(s)\ \xrightarrow{s\to\infty}\ \exp\quantity( - C_p s^{\frac{2}{p}+2} )

(1.3)

-dependent positive constant

This behavior is consistent with the Forrester–Chen–Eriksen–Tracy conjecture
[
For93
,
CET95
]
, relating the local behavior of the density function to the large gap behavior of the gap probability.

Schur measure

We consider the random distribution of partitions with the Schur measure defined as follows
[
Oko99
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Schur measure)
.

We define the Schur measure on the set of partitions
\mathscr{Y}
,

\displaystyle\mu(\lambda)=\frac{1}{Z(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y})}\,s_{\lambda}(\mathsf{X})s_{\lambda}(\mathsf{Y})\,,\qquad\lambda\in\mathscr{Y}\,,

(2.1)

where
s_{\lambda}(\cdot)
is the Schur function.
We denote sets of the (infinitely many) parameters by
\mathsf{X}=(\mathsf{x}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathsf{Y}=(\mathsf{y}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, and
Z(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y})
is the normalization constant to be specified below.

In this paper, we assume that all the parameters are real.
We also use another parametrization with the Miwa variables,

\displaystyle t_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathsf{x}_{i}^{n}\,,\qquad\tilde{t}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathsf{y}_{i}^{n}\,.

(2.2)

These series are absolutely convergent if all
|\mathsf{x}_{i}|
,
|\mathsf{y}_{i}|<1
.
The constant
Z(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y})
is the partition function imposing the normalization condition,
\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\mathscr{Y}}\mu(\lambda)=1
, which is obtained via the Cauchy sum formula (assuming all
|\mathsf{x}_{i}|
,
|\mathsf{y}_{i}|<1
),

\displaystyle Z(\mathsf{X},\mathsf{Y})=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathscr{Y}}s_{\lambda}(\mathsf{X})s_{\lambda}(\mathsf{Y})=\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq\infty}\quantity( 1 - \mathsf{x}_i \mathsf{y}_j )^{-1}=\exp\quantity( \sum_{n = 1}^\infty n \, t_n \tilde{t}_n )\,.

(2.3)

We then define correlation functions for the random partition as follows.

Definition 2.2

(Correlation function)
.

The
k
-point correlation function associated with the Schur measure is defined with a set
W=(w_{i})_{i=1,\ldots,k}\subset\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
as follows,

\displaystyle\rho_{k}(W)=\expectationvalue{ \prod_{w \in W} \delta_{w}({X}(\lambda)) }=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathscr{Y}}\mu(\lambda)\prod_{w\in W}\delta_{w}({X}(\lambda))

(2.4)

with the “density function”

\displaystyle\delta_{w}(\mathscr{X})=\begin{cases}1&(w\in\mathscr{X})\\
0&(w\not\in\mathscr{X})\end{cases}

(2.5)

and the boson-fermion map,

\displaystyle{X}(\lambda)=\quantity( x_i = \lambda_i - i + \frac{1}{2} )_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}\,.

(2.6)

In order to discuss the correlation functions of the random partitions, we introduce two distinct wave functions as follows.

Definition 2.3

(Wave functions)
.

We define two distinct wave functions,

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}(z)

\displaystyle=\exp\quantity( \sum_{n=1}^\infty\qty(t_n z^n - \tilde{t}_n z^{-n}) )=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}J(n)\,z^{n}\,,\qquad J(n)=\oint\frac{\differential{z}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\mathscr{J}(z)}{z^{n+1}}\,,

(2.7a)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathscr{J}}(z)

\displaystyle=\exp\quantity( \sum_{n=1}^\infty\qty(\tilde{t}_n z^n - {t}_n z^{-n}) )=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widetilde{J}(n)\,z^{n}\,,\qquad\widetilde{J}(n)=\oint\frac{\differential{z}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{\widetilde{\mathscr{J}}(z)}{z^{n+1}}\,,

(2.7b)

which are biorthonormal,

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}J(n+k)\widetilde{J}(m+k)=\delta_{n,m}\,,

(2.8)

and related to each other as
\widetilde{\mathscr{J}}(z)^{-1}=\mathscr{J}(z^{-1})
.

Remark 2.4
.

Imposing the relations
t_{n}=\pm\tilde{t}_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the following relations between the wave functions,

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}(z)=\begin{cases}\widetilde{\mathscr{J}}(z)&(t_{n}=+\tilde{t}_{n})\\
\widetilde{\mathscr{J}}(z)^{-1}&(t_{n}=-\tilde{t}_{n})\end{cases}

(2.9)

Then, one can describe the determinantal formula for the
k
-point correlation function defined in (
2.4
).

Proposition 2.5

(Determinantal formula
[
Oko99
]
)
.

The
k
-point correlation function of the random partitions with the Schur measure is given by a size
k
determinant constructed from the kernel,

\displaystyle\rho_{k}(W)=\det_{1\leq i,j\leq k}K(w_{i},w_{j})\,,

(2.10)

where the kernel associated with the Schur measure is given by

\displaystyle K(r,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{2}}\oint_{|z|>|w|}\hskip-15.00002pt\differential{z}\differential{w}\,\frac{\mathscr{K}(z,w)}{z^{r+1/2}w^{-s+1/2}}\qquad\quantity(r, s \in\mathbb{Z} + \frac{1}{2})\,,

(2.11a)

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathscr{J}(z)}{\mathscr{J}(w)}\,\frac{1}{z-w}=\frac{\mathscr{J}(z)\widetilde{\mathscr{J}}(w^{-1})}{z-w}\,.

(2.11b)

Lemma 2.6
.

From the formula (
2.11
), we obtain the expression of the kernel using the wave functions,

\displaystyle K(r,s)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}J\quantity(r+k-\frac{1}{2})\widetilde{J}\quantity(s+k-\frac{1}{2})\,.

(2.12)

Proof.

We first expand
(\mathscr{J}(z),\widetilde{\mathscr{J}}(w))
with
(J(n),\widetilde{J}(m))
in
\mathscr{K}(z,w)
based on the definition (
2.7
),

\displaystyle\mathscr{K}(z,w)=\sum_{n,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{z^{n}w^{-m}}{z-w}J(n)\widetilde{J}(m)\,.

(2.13)

Then, substituting this expression and expanding the geometric series, we obtain

\displaystyle K(r,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi\mathrm{i})^{2}}\oint_{|z|>|w|}\hskip-15.00002pt\differential{z}\differential{w}\,\sum_{n,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{n-r-\frac{1}{2}-k-1}w^{-m+s-\frac{1}{2}+k}J(n)\widetilde{J}(m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}J\quantity(r+k-\frac{1}{2})\widetilde{J}\quantity(s+k-\frac{1}{2})\,.

(2.14)

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

From this expression (
2.12
) together with the biorthonormal condition (
2.8
), we see that the kernel is projective,

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}K(r,t)K(t,s)=K(r,s)\,.

(2.15)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Bi}_{2n}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\differential{x}}{\pi}\quantity( \exp\qty( - \frac{x^{2n+1}}{2n+1} + x z ) + \sin\qty( \frac{x^{2n+1}}{2n+1} + x z ) )\,.

(3.27)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ai}_{2n}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\differential{x}}{\pi}\cos\quantity( \frac{x^{2n+1}}{2n+1} + x z )\,,\quad\operatorname{Ai}_{2n-1}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\differential{x}}{\pi}\exp\quantity( - \frac{x^{2n}}{2n} )\cos\quantity( x z )\,,

(3.28a)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{2n-1}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\differential{x}}{\pi}\exp\quantity(- \frac{x^{2n}}{2n})\Big(\mathrm{e}^{-\sin\quantity(\frac{\pi}{2n})xz}\cos\quantity(\cos\qty(\frac{\pi}{2n}) xz + \frac{\pi}{2n})

\displaystyle\hskip 120.00018pt-\mathrm{e}^{\sin\quantity(\frac{\pi}{2n})xz}\cos\quantity(\cos\qty(\frac{\pi}{2n}) xz - \frac{\pi}{2n})\Big)\,.

(3.28b)

We remark that
\operatorname{Ai}_{p=2n-1}(z)
is an even function, while
\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p=2n-1}(z)
is an odd function.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, the Airy functions obey the formal biorthonormal condition as follows,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\differential{z}\operatorname{Ai}_{p}(x+z)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(y+z)=\delta(x-y)\,.

(3.29)

Proof.

Using the integral form of the delta function,

\displaystyle\delta(z)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\differential{x}}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}xz}\,,

(3.30)

we have

LHS of

(
3.29
)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\differential{z}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\differential{u}}{2\pi}\int_{\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}}\frac{\differential{v}}{2\pi}\exp\quantity( - \frac{u^{2n}}{2n} + \frac{v^{2n}}{2n} - \ii(ux + vy) - \ii z (u + v) )

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\differential{u}\int_{\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}}\differential{v}\exp\quantity( - \frac{u^{2n}}{2n} + \frac{v^{2n}}{2n} - \ii(ux + vy) )\delta(u+v)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\differential{u}}{2\pi}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}u(x-y)}=\text{RHS}

(3.31)

where the modified contour is given by
\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}=\tilde{\gamma}_{+}^{\prime}+\tilde{\gamma}_{-}^{\prime}
with
\tilde{\gamma}_{\pm}^{\prime}:\ \mp\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta}\infty\ \longrightarrow\ 0\ \longrightarrow\ \pm\mathrm{e}^{+\mathrm{i}\theta}\infty
.
∎

Differential equations

These Airy functions obey the differential equations,

\displaystyle\quantity[ (-1)^{n-1} \dv[2n]{}{z} - z ]\operatorname{Ai}_{2n}(z)=0\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\quantity[ (-1)^{n} \dv[2n]{}{z} - z ]\operatorname{Ai}_{2n}(-z)=0\,,

(3.32a)

\displaystyle\quantity[ (-1)^{n} \dv[2n-1]{}{z} - z ]\operatorname{Ai}_{2n-1}(z)=0\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\quantity[ (-1)^{n-1} \dv[2n-1]{}{z} - z ]\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{2n-1}(z)=0\,.

(3.32b)

Therefore, the solutions to the differential equations (

\displaystyle\phi(\xi)=\begin{cases}\operatorname{Ai}_{p}((-1)^{n-1}\xi)&(p=2n)\\
\operatorname{Ai}_{p}(\xi)&(p=4n-1)\\
\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(\xi)&(p=4n-3)\end{cases}\qquad\psi(\xi)=\begin{cases}\operatorname{Ai}_{p}((-1)^{n-1}\xi)&(p=2n)\\
\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(\xi)&(p=4n-1)\\
\operatorname{Ai}_{p}(\xi)&(p=4n-3)\end{cases}

(3.33)

The normalizations are fixed by comparing their biorthonormality conditions (Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.5
).

3.2.1
Asymptotic behavior:
\operatorname{Ai}_{p}

Let us explore the asymptotic behavior of the higher Airy functions based on the saddle point approximation at
z\to\pm\infty
.
3
3

3

The asymptotics of the higher Airy function discussed in this part is known in the literature (See, e.g.,
[
ACvM11
]
).
We show them here for the sake of self-contained presentation.

Although the saddle point analysis presented here is not rigorous, rather heuristic, it provides an efficient way to discuss the asymptotic behavior.
We may need an alternative approach, e.g., Riemann–Hilbert analysis, to confirm rigorously the results shown in this part.

In order to apply the saddle point approximation, we rewrite the
p
-Airy function for
z\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ai}_{p}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\gamma}\frac{\differential{x}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\,\mathrm{e}^{W(x,z)}\,,

(3.34)

where we introduce the potential function,

\displaystyle W(x,z)=(-1)^{n-1}\frac{x^{p+1}}{p+1}-xz\qquad(p=2n,2n-1)\,.

(3.35)

The stationary equation with respect to this potential function is given by

\displaystyle 0=\partialderivative{W(x,z)}{x}=(-1)^{n-1}x^{p}-z\,,

(3.36)

which leads to the saddle point

\displaystyle x_{*}^{p}=\begin{cases}-z&(n=2m)\\
+z&(n=2m-1)\end{cases}.

(3.37)

Positive
z
regime

We first consider the positive
z
regime.
For the case with
n=2m
(
p=4m,4m-1
), the saddle point (
3.37
) is given by

\displaystyle x_{*}=\tilde{\omega}_{k}z^{\frac{1}{p}}\,,\qquad\tilde{\omega}_{k}=\exp\quantity(\frac{2k+1}{p}\pi\ii)\,,\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\,.

(3.38)

Namely,
(\tilde{\omega}_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}
is a
p
-th root of
-1
, such that
\tilde{\omega}_{k}^{p}=-1
.
For the case with
n=2m-1
, we instead consider the saddle point given by

\displaystyle x_{*}=\omega_{k}z^{\frac{1}{p}}\,,\qquad\omega_{k}=\exp\quantity(\frac{2k}{p}\pi\ii)\,,\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\,,

(3.39)

where
(\omega_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}
is a
p
-th root of
+1
, such that
\omega_{k}^{p}=+1
.

We focus on the case with
n=2m
for the moment.
Expanding the potential function up to the quadratic order, we obtain

\displaystyle W(x,z)

\displaystyle\approx W(x_{*},z)+\frac{1}{2}{\partialderivative[2]{W(x_*,z)}{x}}(x-x_{*})^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{p}{p+1}\tilde{\omega}_{k}z^{1+\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{p}{2}\tilde{\omega}_{k}^{-1}z^{1-\frac{1}{p}}(x-x_{*})^{2}\,.

(3.40)

Hence, the saddle point contribution with
\real\quantity(\tilde{\omega}_k)>0
, which decays at
z\to+\infty
, is given by

\displaystyle\exp\quantity(- \frac{p}{p+1} \tilde{\omega}_k z^{1 + \frac{1}{p}})\int_{-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{+\mathrm{i}\infty}\frac{\differential{x}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\exp\quantity( \frac{p}{2} \tilde{\omega}_k^{-1} z^{1 - \frac{1}{p}} (x - x_*)^2 )

\displaystyle=\exp\quantity(- \frac{p}{p+1} \tilde{\omega}_k z^{1 + \frac{1}{p}})z^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2p}}\frac{\tilde{\omega}_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{2p\pi}}\,.

(3.41)

Although we may deform the contour to pick up all the saddle points with a positive real part, we only focus on the most dominating factors,
x_{*}=\tilde{\omega}^{\pm 1}_{m-1}z^{\frac{1}{p}}
.
For the case with
n=2m-1
, we similarly take the saddle points
x_{*}={\omega}^{\pm 1}_{m-1}z^{\frac{1}{p}}
.
Then, we obtain the asymptotic behavior of the
p
-Airy function at
z\to+\infty
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ai}_{p}(z)\xrightarrow{z\to+\infty}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{p\pi}}z^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2p}}

\displaystyle\times\begin{cases}\displaystyle\exp\quantity(\frac{-p}{p+1} \sin\qty(\frac{\pi}{p}) z^{1 + \frac{1}{p}})\cos\quantity( \frac{p}{p+1} \cos\qty(\frac{\pi}{p}) z^{1 + \frac{1}{p}} - \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{\pi}{2p} )&(p=2n)\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\exp\quantity(\frac{-p}{p+1} \sin\qty(\frac{\pi}{2p}) z^{1 + \frac{1}{p}})\cos\quantity( \frac{p}{p+1} \cos\qty(\frac{\pi}{2p}) z^{1 + \frac{1}{p}} - \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{\pi}{4p} )&(p=2n-1)\end{cases}\,.

(3.42)

This expression is available both for
n=2m
and
n=2m-1
.

Negative
z
regime

We consider the negative
z
-regime
z<0
.
In this case, the saddle point (
3.37
) is given by

\displaystyle x_{*}=\begin{cases}\omega_{k}|z|^{\frac{1}{p}}&(n=2m)\\
\tilde{\omega}_{k}|z|^{\frac{1}{p}}&(n=2m-1)\end{cases}\qquad\text{for}\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\,.

(3.43)

For
n=2m
, expansion of the potential function up to the quadratic order is given by

\displaystyle W(x,z)\approx\frac{p}{p+1}\omega_{k}|z|^{1+\frac{1}{p}}+\frac{p}{2}\omega_{k}^{-1}|z|^{1-\frac{1}{p}}(x-x_{*})^{2}\,.

(3.44)

In contrast to the previous case
z\to+\infty
, we shall take the saddle point with
\imaginary(\omega_{k})\leq 0
to obtain a decaying contribution in
z\to-\infty
.
Then, we obtain the asymptotic behavior of the
p
-Airy function at
z\to-\infty
from the saddle points
x_{*}=\omega_{m}^{\pm 1}|z|^{\frac{1}{p}}
as follows,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ai}_{p}(z)\xrightarrow{z\to-\infty}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{p\pi}}|z|^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2p}}

\displaystyle\times\begin{cases}\displaystyle\cos\quantity( \frac{p}{p+1} |z|^{1 + \frac{1}{p}} - \frac{\pi}{4} )&(p=2n)\\[10.00002pt]
\displaystyle\exp\quantity(\frac{-p}{p+1} \sin\qty( \frac{\pi}{2p} ) |z|^{1 + \frac{1}{p}})\cos\quantity( \frac{p}{p+1} \cos\qty( \frac{\pi}{2p} ) |z|^{1+\frac{1}{p}} - \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{\pi}{4p} )&(p=2n-1)\end{cases}.

(3.45)

This expression is in fact available both for
n=2m
and
n=2m-1
.
In particular for

.
We remark that
\operatorname{Ai}_{p=2n-1}(z)
is an even function.

3.2.2
Asymptotic behavior:
\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}

We study the asymptotic behavior of
\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(z)
for
z\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{2n-1}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{\gamma}}\frac{\differential{x}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\widetilde{W}(x,z)}\,,\qquad\widetilde{W}(x,z)=(-1)^{n}\frac{x^{2n}}{2n}-xz\,.

(3.46)

From the stationary equation with respect to this potential function, we obtain the saddle point as follows,

\displaystyle 0={\partialderivative{\widetilde{W}(x,z)}{x}}=(-1)^{n}x^{p}-z\quad\implies\quad x_{*}^{p}=\begin{cases}+z&(n=2m)\\
-z&(n=2m-1)\end{cases}

(3.47)

For positive

\displaystyle\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(z)\xrightarrow{z\to+\infty}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{p\pi}}z^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2p}}\exp\quantity( \frac{p}{p+1} \sin\qty(\frac{\pi}{2p}) z^{1+\frac{1}{p}} )\cos\quantity( \frac{p}{p+1} \cos\qty(\frac{\pi}{2p}) z^{1+\frac{1}{p}} - \frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{\pi}{4p} )\,.

(3.48)

Since
\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(z)
is an odd function,
\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(-z)=-\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(z)

We remark that the function
\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(z)
is not bounded at infinity,
\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(z)\xrightarrow{z\to\pm\infty}\mp\infty

See §

3.3
Higher Airy kernel

We now discuss the asymptotic form of the kernel in the scaling limit.

Definition 3.6

(Higher Airy kernel)
.

We define the
p
-Airy kernel with the higher Airy functions,

\displaystyle K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{z}\operatorname{Ai}_{p}(x+z)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(y+z)\,.

(3.49)

This integral is understood as an improper integral for odd
p
, while the integral converges absolutely for even
p
:
From the asymptotic behavior of the Airy functions discussed in §
3.2.1
and §
3.2.2
, we see that
\operatorname{Ai}_{p}(z){\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(z)\xrightarrow{z\to+\infty}z^{-1+\frac{1}{p}}\times
(exponential damping factor) for even
p
,
\operatorname{Ai}_{p}(z)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(z)\xrightarrow{z\to\pm\infty}|z|^{-1+\frac{1}{p}}\times
(oscillatory factor) for odd
p
.
See (
3.72
) in §
3.3.2
for more precise expressions.
Meanwhile, we will show an alternative expression of the
p
-Airy kernel in terms of the derivatives of the
p
-Airy functions (Proposition
3.10
), which are clearly finite.

In the following, we call the kernel for even
p
the higher Airy kernel, and the higher Pearcey kernel for odd
p
.

Lemma 3.7

(Projectivity)
.

The
p
-Airy kernel is projective,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\differential{z}K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,z)K_{p\text{-Airy}}(z,y)=K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,y)\,.

(3.50)

Proof.

This immediately follows from the biorthogonaliry of the
p
-Airy functions (Lemma
3.5
).
∎

Then, we obtain the following behavior of the kernel in the scaling limit.

Proposition 3.8
.

The Schur measure kernel is asymptotic to the higher Airy kernel in the scaling limit under the multicritical condition (
3.15
),

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\quantity(\frac{\alpha_p}{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{p+1}}K\quantity(\frac{\beta}{\epsilon} + \qty(\frac{\alpha_p}{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{p+1}} x, \frac{\beta}{\epsilon} + \qty(\frac{\alpha_p}{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{p+1}} y)=K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,y)\,.

(3.51)

Proof.

The derivation is parallel with our previous paper
[
KZ20
]
.
Before taking the scaling limit, the kernel is given by the summation formula (
2.12
).
In terms of the scaling variables, we have

\displaystyle\quantity(\frac{\alpha_p}{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{p+1}}K\quantity(\frac{\beta}{\epsilon} + \qty(\frac{\alpha_p}{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{p+1}} x, \frac{\beta}{\epsilon} + \qty(\frac{\alpha_p}{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{p+1}} y)

\displaystyle=\quantity(\frac{\epsilon}{\alpha_p})^{\frac{1}{p+1}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\quantity(\frac{\alpha_p}{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{p+1}}J\quantity(\frac{\beta}{\epsilon} + \qty(\frac{\alpha_p}{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{p+1}} \qty(x + \qty(\frac{\epsilon}{\alpha_p})^{\frac{1}{p+1}}(k-\frac{1}{2})))

\displaystyle\hskip 80.00012pt\times\quantity(\frac{\alpha_p}{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{p+1}}\widetilde{J}\quantity(\frac{\beta}{\epsilon} + \qty(\frac{\alpha_p}{\epsilon})^{\frac{1}{p+1}} \qty(y + \qty(\frac{\epsilon}{\alpha_p})^{\frac{1}{p+1}}(k-\frac{1}{2})))\,.

(3.52)

In the scaling limit
\epsilon\to 0
, we replace the wave function with the Airy functions and the Riemann sum with the integral, which provides the integral form of the higher Airy kernel (
3.49
).
∎

As mentioned in Remark
3.3
, for odd
p
, one can make the wave functions either even of odd function.
Therefore, we correspondingly consider the symmetrized kernel as follows,

\displaystyle p=2n-1\ :\ K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{z}\quantity[
\operatorname{Ai}_p (x + z) \widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_p (y + z)
- \operatorname{Ai}_p (x - z) \widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_p (y - z)
]\,,

(3.53)

so that, for
p=2n-1
, we have

\displaystyle K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,y)=K_{p\text{-Airy}}(-x,-y)\,.

(3.54)

3.3.1
Christoffel–Darboux-type formula

We consider the Christoffel–Darboux-type formula for the higher Airy kernel.
In order to derive this formula, we start with the following property.

Lemma 3.9
.

For odd
p=2n-1
, the
p
-Airy kernel (
3.53
) obeys the following,

\displaystyle xK_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\lfloor p/2\rfloor}\operatorname{Ai}_{p}^{(p-2m)}(x)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}^{(2m-1)}(0)\,.

(3.55)

Proof.

Denoting

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\quantity( \operatorname{Ai}_p(z+x) - \operatorname{Ai}_p(z-x) )\,,\qquad\mathscr{A}^{(k)}(z)=\derivative[k]{z}\mathscr{A}(z)\,,

(3.56)

we have

\displaystyle x\mathscr{A}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\quantity( (z+x) \operatorname{Ai}_p(z+x) - (z-x) \operatorname{Ai}_p(z-x) )-\frac{z}{2}\quantity( \operatorname{Ai}_p(z+x) - \operatorname{Ai}_p(z-x) )

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}^{(p)}(z)-z\mathscr{A}(z)\,.

(3.57)

Then, we obtain

\displaystyle xK_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{z}x\mathscr{A}(z)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{z}\quantity[ \mathscr{A}^{(p)}(z) \widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_p(z) + \mathscr{A}(z) \widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_p^{(p)}(z)]\,.

(3.58)

Applying the integration by parts recursively, we have

\displaystyle xK_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k+1}\quantity[\mathscr{A}^{(p-k)}(z) \widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_p^{(k-1)}(z)]_{0}^{\infty}\,.

(3.59)

We remark
\mathscr{A}^{(k)}(z)\xrightarrow{z\to\infty}0
.
In addition,
\operatorname{Ai}_{p}^{(2k)}(x)
and
\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}^{(2k-1)}(x)
are even,
\operatorname{Ai}_{p}^{(2k-1)}(x)
and
\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}^{(2k)}(x)
are odd, so that
\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}^{(2k)}(0)=0
.
Hence, the odd
k
terms are zero in the summation, which proves the result (
3.55
).
∎

Then, we arrive at the following formula.

Proposition 3.10

(Christoffel–Darboux-type formula)
.

The
p
-Airy kernel defined in (
3.49
) is written in terms of the higher Airy functions,

\displaystyle K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x-y}\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k}\operatorname{Ai}_{p}^{(p-k)}(x)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}^{(k-1)}(y)\,.

(3.60)

Proof.

The even case
p=2n
is already known
[
LDMS18
]
.
Hence, we focus on the odd case (
p=2n-1
) here.
From the definition, we have the relation

\displaystyle\derivative{z}K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x+z,y+z)=-\operatorname{Ai}_{p}(x+z)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(y+z)\,.

(3.61)

This is also obtained from the RHS of (
3.60
).
Therefore, we have the following expression with the constant of integration,

\displaystyle K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{x-y}\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k}\operatorname{Ai}_{p}^{(p-k)}(x)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}^{(k-1)}(y)+C(x,y)\,.

(3.62)

To be compatible with another relation,

\displaystyle\quantity( \pdv{}{x} + \pdv{}{y} )K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,y)=-\operatorname{Ai}_{p}(x)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(y)\,,

(3.63)

we have the constraint for the constant
(\partial_{x}+\partial_{y})C(x,y)=0\implies C(x,y)=C(x-y)
.
Then, putting
y=0
and from the relation (
3.55
), we conclude that the constant is zero,
C(x)=0
.
This proves the equality (
3.60
).
∎

Corollary 3.12

(Diagonal value of the kernel)
.

The diagonal value of the kernel is given as follows,

\displaystyle K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,x)

\displaystyle=-x\operatorname{Ai}_{p}(x)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(x)-\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{k}\operatorname{Ai}_{p}^{(p-k)}(x)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}^{(k)}(x)\,,

(3.65)

and obeys the relation

\displaystyle\derivative{x}K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,x)=-\operatorname{Ai}_{p}(x)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(x)\,.

(3.66)

This relation will be useful to study the asymptotic behavior of the density function in the scaling limit.
See §
3.3.2
.

In order that the relation (
3.65
) holds, we shall have the following relation.

Lemma 3.13
.

The following relation holds,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k}\operatorname{Ai}_{p}^{(p-k)}(x)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}^{(k-1)}(x)=0\,.

(3.67)

Proof.

The derivative of the LHS is zero,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k}\derivative{x}\quantity( \operatorname{Ai}_p^{(p-k)}(x) \widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_p^{(k-1)}(x) )=0\,.

(3.68)

The constant of integration can be fixed by
\operatorname{Ai}_{p}^{(p-k)}(x)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}^{(k-1)}(x)=\operatorname{Ai}_{p}^{(p-k)}(x){\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}^{(k-1)}(x)\xrightarrow{x\to\infty}0
for even
p
and
\operatorname{Ai}_{p}^{(2k-1)}(0)=\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}^{(2k)}(0)=0
for odd
p
.
∎

3.3.2
Density function

Due to the determinantal formula of the correlation function (
2.10
), the one-point function, which is called the density function, is given by the diagonal value of the kernel.
Hence, the scaled density function is obtained from the
p
-Airy kernel (
3.65
),
\rho(x)\to\bar{\rho}(x)=K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,x)
, as in (
3.51
).
We have the following asymptotic behavior of the density function.

Proposition 3.14

(Asymptotic behavior of the density function)
.

Define the density function from the
p
-Airy kernel,

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}(x)=K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,x)\,.

(3.69)

The asymptotic behaviors of the density function are given as follows:

\displaystyle p=2n\ :\ \bar{\rho}(x)\xrightarrow{x\to+\infty}\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2p\pi x}\exp\quantity(\frac{-2p}{p+1} \sin\qty(\frac{\pi}{p}) x^{1 + \frac{1}{p}})\quantity[ \frac{1}{\sin\qty(\pi/p)} + \cos\qty(\frac{2p}{p+1} \cos\qty(\frac{\pi}{p}) x^{1 + \frac{1}{p}}) ]

(3.70a)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{x\to-\infty}\

\displaystyle\frac{|x|^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\pi}-\frac{\cos\quantity( \frac{2p}{p+1} |x|^{1 + \frac{1}{p}} )}{2p\pi|x|}

(3.70b)

\displaystyle p=2n-1\ :\ \bar{\rho}(x)\xrightarrow{x\to\pm\infty}\

\displaystyle\frac{\cos\quantity(\frac{\pi}{2p})}{\pi}|x|^{\frac{1}{p}}-\frac{\cos\quantity( \frac{2p}{p+1} \cos\qty(\frac{\pi}{2p}) |x|^{1 + \frac{1}{p}})}{2p\pi\cos\quantity(\frac{\pi}{2p})|x|}

(3.70c)

Proof.

From the relation (
3.66
), we obtain

\displaystyle\derivative{\bar{\rho}(x)}{x}=-\operatorname{Ai}_{p}(x)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(x)\,.

(3.71)

Then, applying the asymptotic behavior of the Airy functions obtained in §
3.2.1
and §
3.2.2
, we obtain the asymptotic behavior of the density function.

Even case:
p=2n

In this case, the derivative of the density function is given as follows,

\displaystyle\derivative{\bar{\rho}(x)}{x}\xrightarrow{x\to+\infty}

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{p\pi}x^{-1+\frac{1}{p}}\exp\quantity(\frac{-2p}{p+1} \sin\qty(\frac{\pi}{p}) x^{1 + \frac{1}{p}})\cos^{2}\quantity( \frac{p}{p+1} \cos\qty(\frac{\pi}{p}) x^{1 + \frac{1}{p}} - \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{\pi}{2p} )

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{p\pi}x^{-1+\frac{1}{p}}\exp\quantity(\frac{-2p}{p+1} \sin\qty(\frac{\pi}{p}) x^{1 + \frac{1}{p}})\quantity[1 + \sin\qty( \frac{2p}{p+1} \cos\qty(\frac{\pi}{p}) x^{1 + \frac{1}{p}} + \frac{\pi}{p} )]

(3.72a)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{x\to-\infty}

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{p\pi}|x|^{-1+\frac{1}{p}}\cos^{2}\quantity( \frac{p}{p+1} |x|^{1 + \frac{1}{p}} - \frac{\pi}{4} )

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{p\pi}|x|^{-1+\frac{1}{p}}\quantity[ 1 +
\sin\qty( \frac{2p}{p+1} |x|^{1 + \frac{1}{p}} ) ]

(3.72b)

Odd case:
p=2n-1

For the odd case, we have the following asymptotic behavior,

\displaystyle\derivative{\bar{\rho}(x)}{x}\xrightarrow{x\to\pm\infty}-\frac{1}{p\pi}|x|^{-1+\frac{1}{p}}\quantity( \sin\qty( \frac{2p}{p+1} \cos\qty(\frac{\pi}{2p}) |x|^{1 + \frac{1}{p}} ) + \cos\qty(\frac{\pi}{2p}) )\,.

(3.72c)

Integrating these expressions, we obtain (
3.70
).
∎

Remark 3.15
.

This expression is compatible with the results for even
p

]
.

Remark 3.16
.

In the odd case
p=2n-1
, the density function is an even function, while it is asymmetric for the even case
p=2n
.
Asymptotic values of the density function are non-zero except for
\bar{\rho}(x)
at

for

3.3.3
Modification of the kernel

As shown in (
3.23
), we can modify the differential equations to contain the subleading terms.
In this case, the corresponding modified Airy functions are given as follows,

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ai}_{p}(z)=\int_{\gamma}\frac{\differential{x}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\mathrm{e}^{W(x,z)}\,,\qquad\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(z)=\int_{\tilde{\gamma}}\frac{\differential{x}}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\mathrm{e}^{\widetilde{W}(x,z)}\,,

(3.73)

where the potential functions obey

\displaystyle\partialderivative{x}W(x,z)=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\rho_{k}x^{k}-z\,,\qquad\partialderivative{x}\widetilde{W}(x,z)=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\tilde{\rho}_{k}x^{k}-z

(3.74)

for the coefficients
(\rho_{k},\tilde{\rho}_{k})_{k=1,\ldots,p}
obtained in (
3.23
).
We remark that we apply the same integration contours
(\gamma,\tilde{\gamma})
as before (
3.25
).

Under this modification, the kernel is accordingly modified as follows,

\displaystyle K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{x-y}\sum_{\ell=1}^{p}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{k}\rho_{\ell}\operatorname{Ai}_{p}^{(\ell-k)}(x)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}^{(k-1)}(y)\,,

(3.75)

which obeys the same relation as (
3.61
),

\displaystyle\derivative{z}K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x+z,y+z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x-y}\sum_{\ell=1}^{p}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{k}\rho_{\ell}\quantity( \operatorname{Ai}_p^{(\ell+ 1 - k)}(x+z) \widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_p^{(k-1)}(y+z) + \operatorname{Ai}_p^{(\ell- k)}(x+z) \widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_p^{(k)}(y+z) )

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{x-y}\sum_{\ell=1}^{p}\quantity( \rho_\ell\operatorname{Ai}_p^{(\ell)}(x+z) \widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_p(y+z) + \tilde{\rho}_\ell\operatorname{Ai}_p(x+z) \widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_p^{(\ell)}(y+z) )

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{Ai}_{p}(x+z)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}(y+z)\,.

(3.76)

The diagonal value of the kernel is given by

\displaystyle K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{p}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{k}\rho_{\ell}\operatorname{Ai}_{p}^{(\ell+1-k)}(x)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}^{(k-1)}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{p}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{k+1}\rho_{\ell}\operatorname{Ai}_{p}^{(\ell-k)}(x)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}^{(k)}(x)\,,

(3.77)

and from the regularity of the kernel at
x=y
, we have the relation,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{k}\rho_{\ell}\operatorname{Ai}_{p}^{(\ell-k)}(x)\widetilde{\operatorname{Ai}}_{p}^{(k-1)}(x)=0\,.

(3.78)

4
Gap probability

Once given a kernel, the probability such that no “particle” is found in the interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, called the gap probability, is given by the Fredholm determinant,

\displaystyle\det(1 - \hat{K})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\int_{I}\differential{x}_{1}\cdots\int_{I}\differential{x}_{k}\det_{1\leq i,j\leq k}K(x_{i},x_{j})\,,

(4.1)

where
\hat{K}
is the integral operator defined in (
4.17
).
In this Section, we explore the Fredholm determinant associated with the higher Airy kernel for both
p
even and odd.
Hence, it provides the gap probabilities of the determinantal point process defined by the limiting kernel.
We will consider the specific cases
I=[-s,s]
in §
5
and
I=[s,\infty)
in §
A
.

4.1
Operator formalism

Definitions

We introduce the operator formalism to study the Fredholm determinant.
For any integrable function
f(x)
, we define the corresponding state
\ket{f}
in the Hilbert space,

\displaystyle f(x)=\innerproduct{x}{f}=\innerproduct{f}{x}\,.

(4.2)

In this formalism, the delta function is given by

\displaystyle\innerproduct{x}{y}=\delta(x-y)\,.

(4.3)

The identity operator is constructed by the complete set,

\displaystyle 1=\int\differential{x}\ket{x}\bra{x}\,.

(4.4)

The trace of the operator is given by

\displaystyle\Tr\mathcal{O}=\int\differential{x}\bra{x}\mathcal{O}\ket{x}=\int\differential{x}\mathcal{O}(x,x)\,.

(4.5)

Coordinate and differential operators

We define the coordinate and differential operators,

\displaystyle xf(x)=\bra{x}Q\ket{f}=\bra{f}Q\ket{x}\,,\qquad f^{\prime}(x)=\bra{x}P\ket{f}=-\bra{f}P\ket{x}\,,

(4.6)

whose kernels are given by

\displaystyle\bra{x}Q\ket{y}=x\delta(x-y)\,,\qquad\bra{x}P\ket{y}=\partialderivative{x}\delta(x-y)\,.

(4.7)

Kernels and wave functions

We define the operator
K
corresponding to the
p
-Airy kernel,

\displaystyle K(x,y)\equiv K_{p\text{-Airy}}(x,y)=\bra{x}K\ket{y}\,.

(4.8)

We then introduce the states associated with the wave functions,

\displaystyle\phi_{k}(x)=\derivative[k]{\phi(x)}{x}=\innerproduct{x}{\phi_k}\,,\qquad\psi_{k}(x)=\derivative[k]{\psi(x)}{x}=\innerproduct{\psi_k}{x}\,,

(4.9)

and we put

\displaystyle\ket{\phi_0}=\ket{\phi}\,,\qquad\ket{\psi_0}=\ket{\psi}\,.

(4.10)

For even
p
, they are equivalent to each other,
\ket{\phi_k}=\ket{\psi_k}
.
We remark the relation

\displaystyle P\ket{\phi_k}=\ket{\phi_{k+1}}\,,\qquad P\ket{\psi_k}=\ket{\psi_{k+1}}\,,

(4.11)

and also

\displaystyle\bra{\psi_k}=(-1)^{k}\bra{\psi}P^{k}\,.

(4.12)

From the relation

\displaystyle\bra{x}\commutator{Q}{K}\ket{y}=(x-y)K(x,y)\,,

(4.13)

we obtain a useful formula

\displaystyle\commutator{Q}{K}=\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k}\ket{\phi_{p-k}}\bra{\psi_{k-1}}\,.

(4.14)

4.2
Fredholm determinant

We consider the intervals
I=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{m}[a_{2j-1},a_{2j}]\subset\mathbb{R}
.
We denote the parameter set by
\underline{a}=(a_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,2m}
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\chi_{I}(\cdot)
be the characteristic function associated with the interval
I
:

\displaystyle\chi_{I}(x)=\begin{cases}1&(x\in I)\\
0&(x\not\in I)\end{cases}

(4.15)

and the corresponding projection operator

\displaystyle\bra{x}\Pi_{I}\ket{f}=f(x)\chi_{I}(x)\,,\qquad\Pi_{I}^{2}=\Pi_{I}\,.

(4.16)

We define the restricted kernels and the corresponding operators

\displaystyle\bra{x}\hat{K}\ket{y}=K(x,y)\chi_{I}(y)\,,\qquad\hat{K}=K\Pi_{I}\,,

(4.17a)

\displaystyle\bra{x}\check{K}\ket{y}=\chi_{I}(x)K(x,y)\,,\qquad\check{K}=\Pi_{I}K\,.

(4.17b)

Lemma 4.2
.

We have the following commutation relation,

\displaystyle\commutator{\Pi_I}{P}=\sum_{j=1}^{2m}(-1)^{j}\ket{a_j}\bra{a_j}\,.

(4.18)

Proof.

Taking the derivative of the expression (
4.16
), we obtain

\displaystyle\bra{x}P\Pi_{I}\ket{f}

\displaystyle=\derivative{x}\quantity( f(x) \chi_I(x) )

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}(x)\chi_{I}(x)-\sum_{j=1}^{2m}(-1)^{j}f(a_{j})\delta(x-a_{j})

\displaystyle=\bra{x}\Pi_{I}P\ket{f}-\sum_{j=1}^{2m}(-1)^{j}\innerproduct{x}{a_j}\innerproduct{a_j}{f}\,,

(4.19)

which yields the relation (
4.18
).
∎

We consider the Fredholm determinant associated with the
p
-Airy kernel,

\displaystyle F(\underline{a})=\det( 1 - \hat{K} )=\det\quantity( 1 - \check{K} )\,.

(4.20)

Taking the logarithm of the Fredholm determinant, we obtain

\displaystyle\log F(\underline{a})=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\Tr\hat{K}^{n}=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\Tr\check{K}^{n}\,,

(4.21)

where we have

\displaystyle\Tr\hat{K}^{n}=\Tr\check{K}^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{I}\differential{x_1}\cdots\int_{I}\differential{x_n}K(x_{1},x_{2})K(x_{2},x_{3})\cdots K(x_{n},x_{1})\,.

(4.22)

4.2.1
Resolvent operators

We define the resolvent operators from the kernels,

\displaystyle R=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\hat{K}^{n}=\frac{\hat{K}}{1-\hat{K}}\,,\qquad L=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\check{K}^{n}=\frac{\check{K}}{1-\check{K}}\,.

(4.23)

One can show the following formulas for the resolvent,

\displaystyle\commutator{X}{(1 - \hat{K})^{-1}}

\displaystyle=(1-\hat{K})^{-1}\commutator{X}{\hat{K}}(1-\hat{K})^{-1}\,,

(4.24a)

\displaystyle\partial(1-\hat{K})^{-1}

\displaystyle=(1-\hat{K})^{-1}\quantity(\partial\hat{K})(1-\hat{K})^{-1}\,.

(4.24b)

The same relation holds for
\check{K}
.
Then, the parameter dependence of the Fredholm determinant is given as follows.

Lemma 4.3
.

The total derivative of the Fredholm determinant is

\displaystyle\differential{ \log F(\underline{a}) }

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{2m}(-1)^{j-1}R(a_{j},a_{j})\differential{a_j}=\sum_{j=1}^{2m}(-1)^{j-1}L(a_{j},a_{j})\differential{a_j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{2m}(-1)^{j-1}H_{j}\differential{a}_{j}

(4.25)

where we define the Hamiltonians,

\displaystyle H_{j}=R(a_{j},a_{j})=L(a_{j},a_{j})\,,\qquad j=1,\ldots,2m\,.

(4.26)

Proof.

The parameter derivative of the projection operator is given by

\displaystyle\partialderivative{a_j}\Pi_{I}=(-1)^{j}\ket{a_j}\bra{a_j}\,,

(4.27)

which yields

\displaystyle\partialderivative{a_j}\hat{K}=(-1)^{j}K\ket{a_j}\bra{a_j}\,,\qquad\partialderivative{a_j}\check{K}=(-1)^{j}\ket{a_j}\bra{a_j}K\,.

(4.28)

Then, the parameter derivative of the Fredholm determinant is given by

\displaystyle\partialderivative{a_j}\log\det(1 - \hat{K})

\displaystyle=-\Tr\quantity(1 - \hat{K})^{-1}\partialderivative{\hat{K}}{a_j}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j-1}\Tr R\ket{a_j}\bra{a_j}=(-1)^{j-1}R(a_{j},a_{j})\,,

(4.29a)

\displaystyle\partialderivative{a_j}\log\det(1 - \check{K})

\displaystyle=-\Tr\quantity(1 - \check{K})^{-1}\partialderivative{\check{K}}{a_j}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j-1}\Tr\ket{a_j}\bra{a_j}L=(-1)^{j-1}L(a_{j},a_{j})\,.

(4.29b)

Taking into account all the contributions of the parameters
(a_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,2m}
, we obtain (
4.25
).
∎

Remark 4.4
.

For
x,y\in I
, we have

\displaystyle R(x,y)=L(x,y)\,.

(4.30)

Hence, we have

\displaystyle R(a_{i},a_{j})=L(a_{i},a_{j})\,,\qquad i,j=1,\ldots,2m\,,

(4.31)

so that we denote

\displaystyle R_{ij}:=R(a_{i},a_{j})=L(a_{i},a_{j})\,.

(4.32)

The expression (
4.25
) means that the parameter dependence of the Fredholm determinant is fixed by the diagonal values of the resolvent kernels, that we call the Hamiltonians.
In fact, the Fredholm determinant plays a role of the isomonodromic
\tau
-function, and the parameters
(a_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,2m}
are interpreted as the corresponding time variables in this context
[
JMU81
,
JM81b
,
JM81a
]
.
See also
[
Har21
]
.

4.2.2
Auxiliary wave functions

As shown above, we shall evaluate the Hamiltonians to describe the Fredholm determinant.
For this purpose, we introduce auxiliary wave functions,
(\underline{\mathsf{q}},\underline{\mathsf{p}})=(\mathsf{q}_{k},\mathsf{p}_{k})_{k=0,\ldots,p-1}
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{q}_{k}(x)=\bra{x}(1-\hat{K})^{-1}\ket{\phi_k}\,,\qquad\tilde{\mathsf{q}}_{k}(x)=\bra{x}(1-\check{K})^{-1}\Pi_{I}\ket{\phi_k}=\mathsf{q}_{k}(x)\chi_{I}(x)\,,

(4.33a)

\displaystyle\mathsf{p}_{k}(x)=\bra{\psi_k}(1-\check{K})^{-1}\ket{x}\,,\qquad\tilde{\mathsf{p}}_{k}(x)=\bra{\psi_k}\Pi_{I}(1-\hat{K})^{-1}\ket{x}=\mathsf{p}_{k}(x)\chi_{I}(x)\,.

(4.33b)

Using the resolvent kernels, we may write them as follows,

\displaystyle\mathsf{q}_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\phi_{k}(x)+\bra{x}R\ket{\phi_k}\,,

(4.34a)

\displaystyle\mathsf{p}_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\psi_{k}(x)+\bra{\psi_k}L\ket{x}\,.

(4.34b)

Then, we obtain the expression of the resolvent kernels using these auxiliary wave functions.

Lemma 4.5

(Resolvent kernel via the auxiliary wave functions)
.

The resolvent kernels are written in terms of the auxiliary wave functions,

\displaystyle R(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x-y}\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k}\mathsf{q}_{p-k}(x)\tilde{\mathsf{p}}_{k-1}(y)\,,

(4.35a)

\displaystyle L(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x-y}\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k}\tilde{\mathsf{q}}_{p-k}(x){\mathsf{p}}_{k-1}(y)\,.

(4.35b)

Proof.

This expression is obtained from the relation,

\displaystyle(x-y)\bra{x}R\ket{y}

\displaystyle=\bra{x}\commutator{Q}{R}\ket{y}

\displaystyle=\bra{x}(1-\hat{K})^{-1}\commutator{Q}{\hat{K}}(1-\hat{K})^{-1}\ket{y}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k}\bra{x}(1-\hat{K})^{-1}\ket{\phi_{p-k}}\bra{\psi_{k-1}}\Pi_{I}(1-\hat{K})^{-1}\ket{y}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k}\mathsf{q}_{p-k}(x)\tilde{\mathsf{p}}_{k-1}(y)\,.

(4.36)

We may apply the same analysis to the other kernel
L(x,y)
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

For
x\in I\ \quantity(\iff\chi_I(x) = 1)
, the diagonal value of the resolvent kernel is given by

\displaystyle R(x,x)=L(x,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k}\mathsf{q}_{p-k}^{\prime}(x)\mathsf{p}_{k-1}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k-1}\mathsf{q}_{p-k}(x)\mathsf{p}_{k-1}^{\prime}(x)\,.

(4.37)

See also Lemma
4.8
.

Lemma 4.7

(Parameter dependence of
(\mathsf{q}_{k},\mathsf{p}_{k})
)
.

The auxiliary wave functions show the following parameter dependence,

\displaystyle\partialderivative{\sfq_k}{a_j}=(-1)^{j}R(x,a_{j})\mathsf{q}_{k}(a_{j})\,,\qquad\partialderivative{\sfp_k}{a_j}=(-1)^{j}\mathsf{p}_{k}(a_{j})L(a_{j},x)\,.

(4.38)

Proof.

We notice the relation

\displaystyle\ket{a_j}=\Pi_{I}\ket{a_j}\,,

(4.39)

so that
K\ket{a_j}=\hat{K}\ket{a_j}
and
\bra{a_j}K=\bra{a_j}\check{K}
.
Then, we obtain as follows,

\displaystyle\partialderivative{\sfq_k}{a_j}

\displaystyle=\bra{x}(1-\hat{K})^{-1}{\partialderivative{ \hat{K} }{a_j}}(1-\hat{K})^{-1}\ket{\phi_k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j}\bra{x}(1-\hat{K})^{-1}K\ket{a_j}\bra{a_j}(1-\hat{K})^{-1}\ket{\phi_k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j}R(x,a_{j})\mathsf{q}_{k}(a_{j})\,.

(4.40)

The derivatives of
\mathsf{p}_{k}
are similarly obtained.
∎

Then, from the regularity of the resolvent kernel at
x=y
, we have the following relation, which is an analog of the relation (
3.67
).

Lemma 4.8
.

The following relation holds for the auxiliary wave functions,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k}\mathsf{q}_{p-k}(x)\mathsf{p}_{k-1}(x)=0\,.

(4.41)

Proof.

Applying the parameter derivative of the auxiliary functions shown in Lemma
4.7
, we can show that there is no parameter dependence,

\displaystyle\partialderivative{a_j}\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k}\mathsf{q}_{p-k}(x)\mathsf{p}_{k-1}(x)=0\,,\qquad j=1,\ldots,2m\,.

(4.42)

Hence, we can modify them to obtain
I=\emptyset
, where all the auxiliary functions become zero.
Therefore, the constant of integration turns out to be zero.
∎

Lemma 4.9

(
x
-dependence of
(\mathsf{q}_{k},\mathsf{p}_{k})
)
.

The derivatives of the auxiliary wave functions are given as follows,

\displaystyle\partialderivative{x}\mathsf{q}_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{q}_{k+1}(x)-\mathsf{q}_{0}(x)u_{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{2m}(-1)^{j}R(x,a_{j})\mathsf{q}_{k}(a_{j})\,,

(4.43a)

\displaystyle\partialderivative{x}\mathsf{p}_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{p}_{k+1}(x)-v_{k}\mathsf{p}_{0}(x)-\sum_{j=1}^{2m}(-1)^{j}\mathsf{p}_{k}(a_{j})L(a_{j},x)\,,

(4.43b)

where we define auxiliary functions
(\underline{u},\underline{v})=(u_{k},v_{k})_{k=0,\ldots,p-1}
of the parameters
(a_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,2m}
,

\displaystyle u_{k}

\displaystyle=\bra{\psi}\Pi_{I}(1-\hat{K})^{-1}\ket{\phi_k}=\int_{I}\differential{x}\psi(x)\phi_{k}(x)+\int_{I}\differential{x}\int_{I}\differential{y}\psi(x)R(x,y)\phi_{k}(y)\,,

(4.44a)

\displaystyle v_{k}

\displaystyle=\bra{\psi_k}(1-\check{K})^{-1}\Pi_{I}\ket{\phi}=\int_{I}\differential{x}\psi_{k}(x)\phi(x)+\int_{I}\differential{x}\int_{I}\differential{y}\psi_{k}(x)R(x,y)\phi_{(}y)\,.

(4.44b)

We remark

\displaystyle u_{0}=v_{0}\,.

(4.45)

Proof.

In order to show these expressions, we use the formula

\displaystyle\bra{x}\commutator{P}{\hat{K}}\ket{y}

\displaystyle=\quantity( \pdv{}{x} + \pdv{}{y} )K(x,y)\chi_{I}(y)

\displaystyle=-\phi(x)\psi(y)\chi_{I}(y)-\sum_{j=1}^{2m}(-1)^{j}K(x,y)\delta(a_{j}-y)

\displaystyle=\bra{x}\quantity[- \ket{\phi}\bra{\psi} \Pi_I - \sum_{j=1}^{2m} (-1)^{j} K \ket{a_j} \bra{a_j} ]\ket{y}

(4.46)

where we have

\displaystyle\quantity( \pdv{}{x} + \pdv{}{y} )K(x,y)=-\phi(x)\psi(x)\quad\iff\quad\commutator{P}{K}=-\ket{\phi}\bra{\psi}\,.

(4.47)

We may also obtain this expression by writing

\displaystyle\bra{x}\commutator{P}{\hat{K}}\ket{y}

\displaystyle=\bra{x}\commutator{P}{K}\Pi_{I}+K\commutator{P}{\Pi_I}\ket{y}\,,

(4.48)

with the relation (
4.18
).
A similar relation holds for
\check{K}
,

\displaystyle\bra{x}\commutator{P}{\check{K}}\ket{y}

\displaystyle=\quantity( \pdv{}{x} + \pdv{}{y} )\chi_{I}(x)K(x,y)

\displaystyle=\bra{x}\quantity[- \Pi_I \ket{\phi}\bra{\psi} - \sum_{j=1}^{2m} (-1)^j \ket{a_j} \bra{a_j} K ]\ket{y}\,.

(4.49)

Then, we compute

\displaystyle\partialderivative{x}\mathsf{q}_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\bra{x}P(1-\hat{K})^{-1}\ket{\phi_k}

\displaystyle=\bra{x}(1-\hat{K})^{-1}\commutator{P}{\hat{K}}(1-\hat{K})^{-1}+(1-\hat{K})^{-1}P\ket{\phi_k}

(4.50b)

to obtain the formulas (

Rewriting

We denote

Proposition 4.10

(Hamiltonian system)
.

The Hamilsonians associated with the Fredholm determinant are written as follows,

\displaystyle H_{j}

\displaystyle=R_{jj}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k}\partialderivative{\sfq_{p-k,j}}{a_j}\mathsf{p}_{k-1,j}=\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k-1}\mathsf{q}_{p-k,j}\partialderivative{\sfp_{k-1,j}}{a_j}\,,

(4.60)

whose explicit form is given by

\displaystyle H_{j}

\displaystyle=-a_{j}\mathsf{q}_{0,j}\mathsf{p}_{0,j}-\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}(-1)^{k}\mathsf{q}_{p-k,j}\mathsf{p}_{k,j}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k=1}^{p}(-1)^{k}\quantity( \sfq_{p-k,j} \sfp_{0,j} v_{k-1} - \sfq_{0,j} \sfp_{k-1,j} u_{p-k} )-\sum_{\ell(\neq j)}^{2m}(-1)^{\ell}(a_{\ell}-a_{j})R_{j\ell}R_{\ell j}\,.

(4.61)

Corollary 4.11

(Hamilton equations)
.

From the Hamiltonians obtained above, we have the Hamilton equations for the wave functions,

\displaystyle\partialderivative{\sfq_{p-k,j}}{a_j}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\partialderivative{H_j}{\sfp_{k-1,j}}\,,

\displaystyle\partialderivative{\sfp_{k-1,j}}{a_j}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k-1}\partialderivative{H_j}{\sfq_{p-k,j}}\,,

(4.62a)

\displaystyle\partialderivative{u_{p-k,j}}{a_j}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j+k}\partialderivative{H_j}{v_{k-1,j}}\,,

\displaystyle\partialderivative{v_{k-1,j}}{a_j}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j+k-1}\partialderivative{H_j}{u_{p-k,j}}\,.

(4.62b)

This shows that
(\mathsf{q}_{p-k},\mathsf{p}_{k-1})_{k=1,\ldots,p}
and
(u_{p-k},v_{k-1})_{k=1,\ldots,p}
form the canonical pairs.
For even

\mathsf{q}_{k}
and
\mathsf{p}_{k}
(

Hence,
(\mathsf{q}_{p-k},\mathsf{q}_{k-1})_{k=1,\ldots,p}
(and

\underline{a}=(a_{j})_{j=1,\ldots,2m}

Lemma 4.12
.

The parameter dependence of the Hamiltonians is given by

\displaystyle\derivative{H_j}{a_j}

\displaystyle=-\mathsf{q}_{0,j}\mathsf{p}_{0,j}-\sum_{\ell(\neq j)}^{2m}(-1)^{\ell}R_{j\ell}R_{\ell j}\,.

Proof.

This is obtained as follows,

\displaystyle\derivative{H_{j}}{a_j}=\derivative{R_{jj}}{a_j}

(4.65)

∎

(Integral of motion)

Proposition 4.18

(Schlesinger equation)
.

The matrix coefficients obey the Schlesinger equation,

\displaystyle\partialderivative{A_i}{a_j}=\frac{\commutator{A_i}{A_j}}{a_{i}-a_{j}}\,,\qquad\partialderivative{A_j}{a_j}=-\sum_{\ell(\neq j)}^{2m}\frac{\commutator{A_j}{A_\ell}}{a_{j}-a_{\ell}}-\commutator{A_j}{A_\infty}\Big|_{x=a_{j}}\,.

i\neq j

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{i+j+k^{\prime}}}{a_{i}-a_{j}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{p-1}(-1)^{\ell}\quantity( \sfq_{k,i} \sfp_{p-\ell-1,i} \sfq_{\ell,j} \sfp_{p-k'-1,j} - (i \leftrightarrow j) )

As shown before, the logarithmic derivative of the Fredholm determinant is given by the Hamiltonian.

Lemma 5.9
.

In the large
s
limit, the
s
-derivative of the Hamiltonian behaves as follows,

\displaystyle H^{\prime}\ \xrightarrow{s\to\infty}\ O(s^{\frac{2}{p}})\,.

(5.35)

Proof.

We first consider the large
s
behavior of the function

,

\displaystyle R=R(-s,s)=K(-s,s)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{I}\differential{z}_{1}\cdots\int_{I}\differential{z}_{n}K(-s,z_{1})K(z_{1},z_{2})\cdots K(z_{n-1},z_{n})K(z_{n},s)\,.

(5.36)

From the Cristoffel–Darboux formula (
3.60
) together with the asymptotic behavior of the higher Airy functions, (
3.42
) and (
3.48
), we have the following asymptotic behavior of the kernel,

\displaystyle K(s,s)\ \xrightarrow{s\to\infty}\

\displaystyle O(s^{\frac{1}{p}})\,,

(5.37a)

\displaystyle K(-s,s)\ \xrightarrow{s\to\infty}\

\displaystyle O(s^{-1})\,.

(5.37b)

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{I}\differential{z}K(-s,z)K(z,s)\ \xrightarrow{s\to\infty}\ O(s^{\frac{1}{p}})\,.

(5.38)

Recalling the projectivity property of the kernel (
3.50
),

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to\infty}\int_{I}\differential{z}K(x,z)K(z,y)=K(x,y)\,,

(5.39)

we see that the

5.36
) provide the same order contribution, from which we obtain

\displaystyle R\ \xrightarrow{s\to\infty}\ O(s^{\frac{1}{p}})\,.

(5.40)

We then consider the following term,

\displaystyle u_{1}+v_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{I}\differential{x}\quantity( \psi(x) \phi'(x) + \psi'(x) \phi(x) )+\int_{I}\differential{x}\int_{I}\differential{y}\quantity( \psi(x) R(x,y) \phi'(x) + \psi'(x) R(x,y) \phi(y) )

\displaystyle=2\psi(s)\phi(s)+\int_{I}\differential{x}\int_{I}\differential{y}\quantity( \psi(x) K(x,y) \phi'(x) + \psi'(x) K(x,y) \phi(y) )+\cdots\,.

(5.41)

The asymptotic behavior of the first term is immediately obtained from (
3.42
) and (

),

\displaystyle\psi(s)\phi(s)\ \xrightarrow{s\to\infty}\ O(s^{-1+\frac{1}{p}})\,.

(5.42)

The integral term behaves as follows,

\displaystyle\int_{I}\differential{x}\int_{I}\differential{y}\quantity( \psi(x) K(x,y) \phi'(x) + \psi'(x) K(x,y) \phi(y) )

\displaystyle=\int_{I}\differential{x}\int_{I}\differential{y}\quantity[ \qty( \pdv{}{x} + \pdv{}{y} ) \psi(x) K(x,y) \phi(y)
- \psi(x) \qty( \pdv{}{x'} + \pdv{}{y'} ) K(x',y')\Big|_{(x',y') \to(x,y)} \psi(y)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:P_K_commutator}}}{{=}}\quantity[\int_I \dd{y} \psi(x) K(x,y) \phi(y)]^{x=+s}_{x=-s}+\quantity[\int_I \dd{y} \psi(x) K(x,y) \phi(y)]^{y=+s}_{y=-s}+\quantity( \int_I \dd{x} \psi(x) \phi(x) )^{2}

We consider the asymptotic behavior in the large

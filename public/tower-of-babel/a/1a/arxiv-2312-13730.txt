Fidelity Estimation of Entangled Measurements with Local States
Zanqiu Shen, Kun Wang

Abstract
We propose an efficient protocol to estimate the fidelity of an n n -qubit entangled measurement device, requiring only qubit state preparations and classical data post-processing. It works by measuring the eigenstates of Pauli operators, which are strategically selected according to their importance weights and collectively contributed by all measurement operators. We rigorously analyze the protocol’s performance and demonstrate that its sample complexity is uniquely determined by the number of Pauli operators possessing non-zero expectation values with respect to the target measurement. Moreover, from a resource-theoretic perspective, we introduce the stabilizer Rényi entropy of quantum measurements as a precise metric to quantify the inherent difficulty of estimating measurement fidelity.

Conversely, measurement fidelity estimation protocols prove significantly more efficient, albeit their potential dependence on entangled state preparation. In scenarios necessitating entangled state preparation, the precision of the estimated fidelity can be notably affected by errors associated with state preparation,

In this work, we present an efficient protocol for estimating the fidelity of quantum measurement devices, exhibiting an exponential acceleration compared to quantum detector tomography and applicability to diverse quantum measurement schemes. First, we articulate the problem formulation. Consider a system comprising
n

\mathcal{H}
with a dimension of

Our devised protocol operates by conducting measurements on a randomly selected subset of Pauli observables, employing an importance sampling technique

of interest,
the desired additive error
\varepsilon
, and the targeted failure probability denoted as
\delta

We derive analytic bounds on the average sample complexity

We compare our efficient protocol with two other protocols—the globally optimal estimation protocol that involves entangled state preparation and the “direct” measurement fidelity estimation protocol

2.1
Protocol description

The fidelity between a known ideal
n
-qubit projective measurement
\mathcal{M}

and its actual implementation
\mathcal{N}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}\tr[\psi_{k}V_{k}],

(1)

where
\mathcal{M}\equiv\{\psi_{k}=\left|\psi_{k}\right\rangle\!\left\langle\psi_{k}\right|\}_{k}
represents the ideal PVM
satisfying
\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}\psi_{k}=\operatorname{id}
,
where
\operatorname{id}
is the identity operator,
while
\mathcal{N}\equiv\left\{V_{k}\right\}_{k}

denotes the actual positive operator-valued measure (POVM)
satisfying
0\leq V_{k}\leq\operatorname{id}
and
\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}V_{k}=\operatorname{id}
.
Note that the specification of
\mathcal{M}
is known to the experimenters
but the specification of
\mathcal{N}
is completely unknown.
The task is to estimate the fidelity
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
given accesses to
\mathcal{N}
.
In the following, we construct an efficient protocol
to accomplish this task using local state preparations
and rigorously analyze the protocol’s performance.

Expanding the measurement operators
\psi_{k}
in terms of the set of
normalized Pauli operators
\{P_{k}\}_{k},k=1,2,...,4^{n}
,
which constitutes an orthonormal basis of the
n
-qubit operators, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}\tr\left[\left(\sum_{l=1}^{4^{n}}\tr[\psi_{k}P_{l}]P_{l}\right)V_{k}\right]=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}\sum_{l=1}^{4^{n}}\tr[\psi_{k}P_{l}]\tr[V_{k}P_{l}].

(2)

In order to construct an estimator for
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
,
we define a probability mass function
\{q_{l}\}_{l=1}^{4^{n}}
with respect to the Pauli operators as

\displaystyle q_{l}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}\tr[\psi_{k}P_{l}]^{2}}{2^{n}}.

(3)

Thanks to the property of PVM, we can show that
\sum_{l=1}^{4^{n}}q_{l}=1

and thus
\{q_{l}\}_{l}
is a valid probability mass function.
Now, we employ the importance sampling technique
[
KR21
]

to estimate
\mathcal{F}
via Eq. (
2
).
To this end, we rewrite
\mathcal{F}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\sum_{l=1}^{4^{n}}\left(\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{2^{n}}\frac{\tr[\psi_{k\textquoteright}P_{l}]^{2}}{2^{n}}\right)\times\left(\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}\tr[V_{k}P_{l}]\frac{\tr[\psi_{k}P_{l}]}{\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{2^{n}}\tr[\psi_{k\textquoteright}P_{l}]^{2}}\right)\equiv\sum_{l=1}^{4^{n}}q_{l}X_{l},

(4)

where the random variable
X_{l}
is defined as

\displaystyle X_{l}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}\tr[V_{k}P_{l}]\tr[\psi_{k}P_{l}]}{\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{2^{n}}\tr[\psi_{k\textquoteright}P_{l}]^{2}}.

(5)

We can show that
\mathbb{E}[X_{l}]=\mathcal{F}
,
where
\mathbb{E}[\cdot]
denotes the expectation value,
signifying that
X_{l}
serves as an unbiased estimator of
\mathcal{F}
.
From Eq. (
4
),
we can construct an estimator of
\mathcal{F}
using the sample mean in the following manner.
Initially,
m
indices
l_{i}
, where
i=1,2,...,m
and
l_{i}\in\{1,2,...,4^{n}\}
, are sampled according to the importance sampling
distribution
\{q_{l}\}_{l}
defined in Eq. (
3
),
completely determined by the known ideal PVM
\mathcal{M}
, where

\displaystyle m=\left\lceil 1/(\varepsilon^{2}\delta)\right\rceil

(6)

and
\lceil\cdot\rceil
denotes rounding up.
Given the estimated values
\{X_{l_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{m}
,
whose estimation procedure will be presented below,
we can construct an estimator of
\mathcal{F}
using the following formula:

\displaystyle Y=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{l_{i}}.

(7)

By Chebyshev’s inequality (see Appendix
A
for detailed arguments),
we assert that
Y
is an
\varepsilon
-accurate estimator of
\mathcal{F}
satisfying

\displaystyle{\rm Pr}\left(|Y-\mathcal{F}|\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq\delta,

(8)

where
\varepsilon
and
\delta
are predefined constant additive error and failure probability, respectively.

It is noteworthy that for each randomly chosen
l
,
the quantity
\tr[V_{k}P_{l}]
need to be estimated from the measurement statistics in order to determine
X_{l}
.
Since
P_{l}
is not a quantum state, it cannot be used as an input to the measurement device directly.
Thanks to the linearity of the trace function, we can consider the spectral decomposition

\displaystyle P_{l}=\sum_{a=1}^{2^{n}}\lambda_{a}^{(l)}{|\phi_{a}^{(l)}\rangle\langle\phi_{a}^{(l)}|}\equiv\sum_{a=1}^{2^{n}}\lambda_{a}^{(l)}\phi_{a}^{(l)},

(9)

where
\{\lambda_{a}^{(l)}\}_{a}
and
\{\phi_{a}^{(l)}\equiv|\phi_{a}^{(l)}\rangle\langle\phi_{a}^{(l)}|\}_{a}

are the eigenvalues and eigenstates of
P_{l}
, respectively.
Based on the spectral decomposition,
we can rewrite
X_{l}
in Eq. (
5
) as follows:

\displaystyle X_{l}=\sum_{a=1}^{2^{n}}\lambda_{a}^{(l)}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}\frac{\tr[V_{k}\phi_{a}^{(l)}]\tr[\psi_{k}P_{l}]}{\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{2^{n}}\tr[\psi_{k^{\prime}}P_{l}]^{2}}\right).

(10)

Since each eigenstate
\phi_{a}^{(l)}
is a product state,
it can be prepared with high fidelity in NISQ quantum devices.
Correspondingly,
\tr[V_{k}\phi_{a}^{(l)}]
can be estimated
by first measuring
\phi_{a}^{(l)}
many times with the measurement device
\mathcal{N}
and
then post-processing the measurement statistics.
Specifically, we consider the following subroutine:

1.

Sample an index.
Choose an integer
a_{j}\in\{1,\cdots,2^{n}\}
uniformly at random;

2.

Measure the eigenstate.

Use the measurement device
\mathcal{N}
to measure
the eigenstate
\phi^{(l_{i})}_{a_{j}}
(corresponding to the Pauli operator
P_{l_{i}}
)
and record the measurement outcome
o_{j}
.

We define a new random variable as follows:

\displaystyle\widehat{X}_{a_{j}}^{(l_{i})}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{2^{n}}{\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{2^{n}}\tr[\psi_{k^{\prime}}P_{l_{i}}]^{2}}\times\lambda^{(l_{i})}_{a_{j}}\times\tr[\psi_{o_{j}}P_{l_{i}}].

(11)

It is established that
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{a_{j}}^{(l_{i})}]=X_{l_{i}}
as demonstrated in Appendix
A
.
By executing this procedure a total of
n_{l_{i}}
times,
we can estimate

to desired accuracy via the estimator

\displaystyle\widetilde{X}_{l_{i}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{n_{l_{i}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{l_{i}}}\widehat{X}_{a_{j}}^{(l_{i})}.

(12)

Finally, we introduce the following estimator:

\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\widetilde{X}_{l_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{l_{i}}}\frac{1}{mn_{l_{i}}}\times\frac{2^{n}}{\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{2^{n}}\tr[\psi_{k\textquoteright}P_{l_{i}}]^{2}}\times\lambda^{(l_{i})}_{a_{j}}\times\tr[\psi_{o_{j}}P_{l_{i}}].

(13)

To meet the confidence interval requirement that

\displaystyle{\rm Pr}\left(|\widetilde{Y}-Y|\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq\delta,

(14)

we choose

\displaystyle n_{l_{i}}

\displaystyle=\left\lceil\frac{1}{\left(\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{2^{n}}\tr[\psi_{k\textquoteright}P_{l_{i}}]^{2}\right)^{2}}\times\frac{2}{m\varepsilon^{2}}\ln\frac{2}{\delta}\right\rceil,

(15)

where
\ln
denotes the natural logarithm.
Using the confidence intervals (
8
) and (
14
)
and the union bound, we establish a confidence interval of the final estimation

as

\displaystyle{\rm Pr}\left\{|\widetilde{Y}-\mathcal{F}|\geq 2\varepsilon\right\}\leq 2\delta.

(16)

2.2
Performance analysis

5
Conclusions

We have introduced an efficient protocol
for estimating the performance of quantum measurements,
leveraging the preparation and measurement of product eigenstates of Pauli operators.
This protocol confers several advantages over existing methods, surpassing the speed of
detector tomography and demonstrating an exponential acceleration compared to the
direct fidelity estimation protocol for measurement devices.
Furthermore, in specific scenarios, such as well-conditioned quantum measurements,
our protocol demands only a constant number of measurement calls.
This protocol is not only applicable to symmetric informationally complete POVMs
and quantum measurements relying on mutually unbiased bases
[
RBKSC04
,
AS10
,
CGGŻ20
]
,
but also the most general positive operator-valued measure measurements via Naimark’s theorem.
Its significance lies in its ability to facilitate the development of reliable quantum devices and
propel continuous enhancements in their performances.
Our protocol opens up new possibilities
for certifying quantum measurements to high accuracy,
providing a valuable tool for advancing the field of quantum information processing
and enabling the realization of reliable and high performance quantum measurement devices.

The quest for even more efficient fidelity estimation lies in navigating the intricacies of sophisticated sampling strategies and adaptive approaches. This path presents exciting challenges and opportunities to minimize variance and sample complexity, ultimately yielding sharper accuracy and broader applicability of quantum measurements.

(158)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{2^{n}}\tr[V_{k}^{2}]\cdot\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}}{2^{n}}}

(159)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

(160)

where the first inequality is due to AM-QM inequality, the second inequality is due to

\displaystyle\|\psi_{k^{\prime}}-\psi_{k}\|_{2}^{2}=2-2\tr[\psi_{k}^{\prime}\psi_{k}]=2-2\left[1-\sum_{l\notin\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}\tr[\psi_{k}P_{l}]^{2}\right]=2-2\left[1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{8}\frac{|\overline{\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\prime}}|}{|\mathcal{T}_{k}^{\prime}|}\right]\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4},

where

and we employ Lemma
8
in the final inequality. Consequently, we deduce

. Subsequently, we utilize

\displaystyle={\rm Pr}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{L}{C_{k_{i}l_{i}o_{i}}}^{\prime}-L\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\leq L\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)

(164)

\displaystyle\geq 1-2\exp\left[-\frac{2\cdot\frac{L^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{4}}{\sum_{i=1}^{L}(b_{k_{i}l_{i}}^{\prime}-a_{k_{i}l_{i}}^{\prime})^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=1-\delta,

\displaystyle\geq\min_{k}\frac{\varepsilon}{2\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{\frac{\exp[-M_{0}(\psi_{k})]}{2^{n}}}.

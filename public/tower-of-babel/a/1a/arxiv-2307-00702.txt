Transport and localization of indirect excitons in a van der Waals heterostructure
L. H. Fowler-Gerace ∗, Zhiwen Zhou ∗, E. A. Szwed, D.J. Choksy, L. V. Butov

Abstract
Long lifetimes of spatially indirect excitons (IXs), also known as interlayer excitons, allow implementing both quantum exciton systems and long-range exciton transport. Van der Waals heterostructures (HS) composed of atomically thin layers of transition-metal dichalcogenides (TMD) offer the opportunity to explore IXs in moiré superlattices. The moiré IXs in TMD HS form the materials platform for exploring the Bose-Hubbard physics and superfluid and insulating phases in periodic potentials. IX transport in TMD HS was intensively studied and diffusive IX transport with 1 / e 1/e decay distances d 1 / e d_{1/e} up to ∼ 3 \sim 3 μ \mu m was realized. In this work, we present in MoSe 2 /WSe 2 HS the IX long-range transport with d 1 / e d_{1/e} exceeding 100 μ \mu m and diverging at the optical excitation resonant to spatially direct excitons. The IX long-range transport vanishes at high temperatures. With increasing IX density, IX localization, then IX long-range transport, and then IX reentrant localization is observed. The results are in qualitative agreement with the Bose-Hubbard theory of bosons in periodic potentials predicting superfluid at N ∼ 1 / 2 N\sim 1/2 and insulating at N ∼ 0 N\sim 0 and N ∼ 1 N\sim 1 phases for the number of bosons per site of the periodic potential N N .

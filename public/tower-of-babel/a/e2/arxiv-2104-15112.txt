Localization operators associated with the windowed Opdam–Cherednik transform on modulation spaces Thanks: Research supported by ERC Starting Grant No. 713927.
Anirudha Poria

Abstract
. In this paper, we study a class of pseudodifferential operators known as time-frequency localization operators, which depend on a symbol ς \varsigma and two windows functions g 1 g_{1} and g 2 g_{2} . We first present some basic properties of the windowed Opdam–Cherednik transform. Then, we use modulation spaces associated with the Opdam–Cherednik transform as appropriate classes for symbols and windows, and study the boundedness and compactness of the localization operators associated with the windowed Opdam–Cherednik transform on modulation spaces. Finally, we show that these operators are in the Schatten–von Neumann class.

Primary 47G30; Secondary 44A15, 42B35, 47B10.

1.
Introduction

Time-frequency localization operators are a mathematical tool to define a restriction of functions to a region in the time-frequency plane to extract time-frequency features. The role of these operators to localize a signal simultaneously in time and frequency domains, this can be seen as the uncertainty principle. The localization operators were introduced and studied by Daubechies
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
, Ramanathan and Topiwala
[
30
]
, and extensively investigated in
[
16
,
33
,
35
]
. This class of operators occurs in various branches of pure and applied mathematics and has been studied by many authors. Localization operators are recognized as an important new mathematical tool and have found many applications to time-frequency analysis, quantum mechanics, the theory of differential equations, and signal processing (see
[
7
,
22
,
23
,
18
,
30
,
35
]
). They are also known as anti-Wick operators, wave packets, Toeplitz operators, or Gabor multipliers (see
[
23
,
5
,
8
,
16
]
). For a detailed study of the theory of localization operators, we refer to the series of papers of Wong
[
6
,
34
,
36
,
37
]
, and also the book of Wong
[
35
]
. Considerable attention has been devoted to study the localization operators to new contexts. For instance, localization operators were investigated in
[
3
]
for the wavelet transform associated to the Riemann–Liouville operator, in
[
4
]
for the windowed Hankel transform and in
[
26
]
for the k-Hankel wavelet transform. However, upto our knowledge, the localization operators have not been studied for the Opdam–Cherednik transform on modulation spaces. In this paper, we attempt to study the localization operators for the windowed Opdam–Cherednik transform on modulation spaces associated with the Opdam–Cherednik transform.

The motivation to study the localization operators for the windowed Opdam–Cherednik transform on modulation spaces arises from the classical results on these operators for the short-time Fourier transform. Since the last decade modulation spaces have found to be very fruitful in various current trends (e.g., pseudo-differential operators, partial differential equations, etc..) of investigation and have been widely used in several fields in analysis, physics and engineering. Localization operators have implications in two main areas: quantum mechanics and signal analysis, and modulation spaces are widely used in these areas. We hope that the study of these operators on the modulation spaces makes a significant impact in these areas. Another important motivation to study the Jacobi–Cherednik operators arises from their relevance in the algebraic description of exactly solvable quantum many-body systems of Calogero–Moser–Sutherland type (see
[
12
,
20
]
) and they provide a useful tool in the study of special functions with root systems (see
[
13
,
19
]
). These describe algebraically integrable systems in one dimension and have gained considerable interest in mathematical physics. Other motivation for the investigation of the Jacobi–Cherednik operator and the Opdam–Cherednik transform is to generalize the previous subjects which are bound with physics. For a more detailed discussion, we refer to
[
25
]
.

Time-frequency localization operators were defined using the Schrödinger representation and the short-time Fourier transform, which suggests studying these operators as a part of time-frequency analysis. Modulation spaces were used as the appropriate function spaces for understanding these operators, as these spaces are associated to the short-time Fourier transform. However, without using the short-time Fourier transform, we define the localization operator using the windowed Opdam–Cherednik transform. As the harmonic analysis associated with the Opdam–Cherednik transform has known remarkable development (see
[
2
,
24
,
27
,
28
,
31
]
), the natural question to ask whether there exists the equivalent of the theory of localization operators in the framework of the Opdam–Cherednik transform. In this paper, we mainly concern the windowed Opdam–Cherednik transform under the setting of the Opdam–Cherednik transform. To measure the time-frequency concentration of functions and distributions, we use norms and function spaces that are associated with the Opdam–Cherednik transform. Here, we consider the modulation spaces associated with the Opdam–Cherednik transform, as the standard modulation spaces are not suited to this transform. Our main aim in this paper is to expose and study the boundedness and compactness of the localization operators associated with the windowed Opdam–Cherednik transform under suitable conditions on symbols and windows and show that these operators are in the Schatten–von Neumann class.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some basic facts about the Jacobi–Cherednik operator and we discuss the main results for the Opdam–Cherednik transform. In Section
3
, we discuss the modulation spaces associated with the Opdam–Cherednik transform. In Section
4
, we define and study the windowed Opdam–Cherednik transform. Also, we present some basic properties of the windowed Opdam–Cherednik transform and show that this transform shares many properties with the short-time Fourier transform. In particular, we prove Plancherel’s formula, orthogonality relation, and provide a reconstruction formula. Finally, in Section
5
, we use modulation spaces associated with the Opdam–Cherednik transform as appropriate classes for symbols and windows, and study the boundedness and compactness of the localization operators associated with the windowed Opdam–Cherednik transform on modulation spaces. Also, we show that these operators are in the Schatten–von Neumann class.

2.
Harmonic analysis and the Opdam–Cherednik transform

In this section, we collect the necessary definitions and results from the harmonic analysis related to the Opdam–Cherednik transform. The main references for this section are
[
2
,
24
,
27
,
28
,
31
]
. However, we will use the same notation as in
[
21
,
29
]
.

Let
T_{\alpha,\beta}
denote the Jacobi–Cherednik differential–difference operator (also called the Dunkl–Cherednik operator)

T_{\alpha,\beta}f(x)=\frac{d}{dx}f(x)+\Big[(2\alpha+1)\coth x+(2\beta+1)\tanh x\Big]\frac{f(x)-f(-x)}{2}-\rho f(-x),

where
\alpha,\beta
are two parameters satisfying
\alpha\geq\beta\geq-\frac{1}{2}
and
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
, and
\rho=\alpha+\beta+1
. Let
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
. The Opdam hypergeometric functions
G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}
on
\mathbb{R}
are eigenfunctions
T_{\alpha,\beta}G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)=i\lambda G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)
of
T_{\alpha,\beta}
that are normalized such that
G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(0)=1
. The eigenfunction
G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}
is given by

G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)=\varphi^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)-\frac{1}{\rho-i\lambda}\frac{d}{dx}\varphi^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)=\varphi^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)+\frac{\rho+i\lambda}{4(\alpha+1)}\sinh 2x\;\varphi^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}_{\lambda}(x),

where
\varphi^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)={}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{\rho+i\lambda}{2},\frac{\rho-i\lambda}{2};\alpha+1;-\sinh^{2}x\right)
is the hypergeometric function.

For every
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the eigenfunction

G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}
satisfy

|G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)|\leq C\;e^{-\rho|x|}e^{|\text{Im}(\lambda)||x|},

where
C
is a positive constant. Since
\rho>0
, we have

(2.1)

|G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)|\leq C\;e^{|\text{Im}(\lambda)||x|}.

Let us denote by
C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
the space of continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
with compact support. The Opdam–Cherednik transform is the Fourier transform in the trigonometric Dunkl setting, and it is defined as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\alpha\geq\beta\geq-\frac{1}{2}
with
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2}
. The Opdam–Cherednik transform
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(f)
of a function
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
is defined by

{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f)(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\;G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(-x)\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)dx\quad\text{for all }\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}},

where
A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)=(\sinh|x|)^{2\alpha+1}(\cosh|x|)^{2\beta+1}
. The inverse Opdam–Cherednik transform for a suitable function
g
on
\mathbb{R}
is given by

{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}(g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(\lambda)\;G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(x)\;d\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda)\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R},

where

d\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda)=\left(1-\dfrac{\rho}{i\lambda}\right)\dfrac{d\lambda}{8\pi|C_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda)|^{2}}

and

C_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda)=\dfrac{2^{\rho-i\lambda}\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(i\lambda)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{\rho+i\lambda}{2}\right)\;\Gamma\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta+1+i\lambda}{2}\right)},\quad\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus i\mathbb{N}.

The Plancherel formula is given by

(2.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f)(\lambda)\overline{{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(\check{f})(-\lambda)}\;d\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda),

where
\check{f}(x):=f(-x)
. Since the hypergeometric functions
G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}
satisfy

G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda}(tx)=G^{\alpha,\beta}_{\lambda t}(x)
, for every
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
x,t\in\mathbb{R}
, using the definition of
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}
, we obtain
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(\check{f})(-\lambda)=\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(f)(\lambda)
. Therefore, we can rewrite the Plancherel formula (
2.2
) as follows:

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathcal{H}_{\alpha,\beta}(f)(\lambda)\right|^{2}d\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda).

Let
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
(resp.
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
),
p\in[1,\infty]
, denote the
L^{p}
-spaces corresponding to the measure
A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)dx
(resp.
d|\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}|(x)
). The Schwartz space
{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{R})=(\cosh x)^{-\rho}{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R})
is defined as the space of all differentiable functions
f
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\;(1+|x|)^{m}e^{\rho|x|}\left|\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}f(x)\right|<\infty,

for all
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}={\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
, equipped with the obvious seminorms. The Opdam–Cherednik transform
{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}
and its inverse
{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}^{-1}
are topological isomorphisms between the space
{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbb{R})
and the space
{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R})
(see
[
31
]
, Theorem 4.1).

The generalized translation operator associated with the Opdam–Cherednik transform is defined by
[
1
]

(2.4)

\tau_{x}^{(\alpha,\beta)}f(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(z)\;{d\mu}_{\;x,y}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(z),

where
{d\mu}_{\;x,y}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
is given by

(2.5)

{d\mu}_{\;x,y}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(z)=\begin{cases}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y,z)\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(z)\;dz&\text{if}\;\;xy\neq 0\\
d\delta_{x}(z)&\text{if}\;\;y=0\\
d\delta_{y}(z)&\text{if}\;\;x=0\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,\beta}{}

\displaystyle(x,y,z)=M_{\alpha,\beta}|\sinh x\cdot\sinh y\cdot\sinh z|^{-2\alpha}\int_{0}^{\pi}g(x,y,z,\chi)_{+}^{\alpha-\beta-1}

\displaystyle\times\left[1-\sigma^{\chi}_{x,y,z}+\sigma^{\chi}_{x,z,y}+\sigma^{\chi}_{z,y,x}+\frac{\rho}{\beta+\frac{1}{2}}\coth x\cdot\coth y\cdot\coth z(\sin\chi)^{2}\right]\times(\sin\chi)^{2\beta}\;d\chi,

where

M_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\alpha-\beta)\Gamma\left(\beta+\frac{1}{2}\right)},

if
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
satisfy the triangular inequality
||x|-|y||<|z|<|x|+|y|
, and
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y,z)=0
otherwise. Here

\sigma^{\chi}_{x,y,z}=\begin{cases}\frac{\cosh x\cdot\cosh y-\cosh z\cdot\cos\chi}{\sinh x\cdot\sinh y}&\text{if}\;\;xy\neq 0\\
0&\text{if}\;\;xy=0\end{cases}\quad\text{for}\;x,y,z\in\mathbb{R},\;\chi\in[0,\pi],

g(x,y,z,\chi)=1-\cosh^{2}x-\cosh^{2}y-\cosh^{2}z+2\cosh x\cdot\cosh y\cdot\cosh z\cdot\cos\chi
, and

g_{+}=\begin{cases}g&\text{if}\;\;g>0\\
0&\text{if}\;\;g\leq 0.\end{cases}

The kernel
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y,z)
satisfies the following symmetry properties:

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y,z)=\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,\beta}(y,x,z)
,
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y,z)=\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,\beta}(-z,y,-x)
,
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y,z)=\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,-z,-y)
.

Next, we recall some basic properties of
\tau_{x}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
from
[
1
]
. For every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(2.6)

\tau_{x}^{(\alpha,\beta)}f(y)=\tau_{y}^{(\alpha,\beta)}f(x),

and

(2.7)

{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(\tau_{x}^{(\alpha,\beta)}f)(\lambda)=G_{\lambda}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)\;{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f)(\lambda),

for
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
.

If
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, then

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tau_{x}^{(\alpha,\beta)}f(y)\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(y)\;dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(z)\;{d\mu}_{\;x,y}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(z)\right)\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(y)\;dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(z)\;\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,y,z)\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(z)\;dz\right)\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(y)\;dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(z)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\;\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,-z,-y)\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(y)\;dy\right)\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(z)\;dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(z)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\;\mathcal{K}_{\alpha,\beta}(x,-z,y)\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(y)\;dy\right)\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(z)\;dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(z)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\;{d\mu}_{\;x,-z}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(y)\right)\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(z)\;dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(y)\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(y)\;dy\qquad\qquad\left(\text{since}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\;{d\mu}_{\;x,-z}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(y)=1\right).\qquad\qquad

For every
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
and every
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the function
\tau_{x}^{(\alpha,\beta)}f
belongs to the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
and

(2.9)

\left\|\tau_{x}^{(\alpha,\beta)}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})},

where
C_{\alpha,\beta}
is a positive constant.

The convolution product associated with the Opdam–Cherednik transform is defined for two suitable functions
f
and
g
by
[
1
]

(f*_{\alpha,\beta}g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tau_{x}^{(\alpha,\beta)}f(-y)\;g(y)\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(y)\;dy

and

(2.10)

{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f*_{\alpha,\beta}g)={\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(f)\;{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(g).

3.
Modulation spaces associated with the Opdam–Cherednik transform

The modulation spaces were introduced by Feichtinger
[
14
,
15
]
, by imposing integrability conditions on the short-time Fourier transform (STFT) of tempered distributions. More specifically, for
x,w\in\mathbb{R}
, let
M_{w}
and
T_{x}
denote the operators of modulation and translation. Then, the STFT of a function
f
with respect to a window function
g\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R})
is defined by

V_{g}f(x,w)=\langle f,M_{w}T_{x}g\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\overline{g(t-x)}e^{-2\pi iwt}dt,\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Here we are interested in modulation spaces with respect to measure
A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)dx
.

Definition 3.1
.

Fix a non-zero window
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
. Then the modulation space
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
consists of all tempered distributions
f\in\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})
such that
V_{g}f\in L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. The norm on
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
is

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|V_{g}f\|_{L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|V_{g}f(x,w)|^{p}A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)dx\bigg)^{q/p}A_{\alpha,\beta}(w)dw\bigg)^{1/q}<\infty,

with the usual adjustments if
p
or
q
is infinite. If
p=q
, then we write
M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
instead of
M^{p,p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. Also, we denote by
M^{p}(\mathbb{R},\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
the modulation space corresponding to the measure
d|\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}|(x)
and
M^{p}(\mathbb{R})
the modulation space corresponding to the Lebesgue measure
dx
.

The definition of
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
is independent of the choice of
g
in the sense that each different choice of
g
defines an equivalent norm on
M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. Each modulation space is a Banach space. For
p=q=2
, we have that
M^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})=L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}).
For other
p=q
, the space
M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
is not
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. In fact for
p=q>2
, the space
M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
is a superset of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. Here, we collect some basic properties and inclusion relations of modulation spaces with respect to
A_{\alpha,\beta}
. We define the space of special windows
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{R})
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):\;f=V_{g}^{*}F=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}F(x,w)\;M_{w}T_{x}g\;dx\;dw,\right.

\displaystyle\left.\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{where}\;F\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\;\text{and}\;\hbox{supp\,}F\;\text{is compact}\right\}.

Then
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
and

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
is dense in
M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
(see
[
18
]
, Lemma 11.4.1). Let
B
be a Banach space of tempered distributions with the following properties: (1)
B
is invariant under time-frequency shifts, and
\left\|T_{x}M_{w}f\right\|_{B}\leq C\|f\|_{B}
for all
f\in B
, (2)
M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\cap B\neq\{0\}
. Then
M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
is embedded in
B
(see
[
18
]
, Theorem 12.1.9). Also,
M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
is invariant under time-frequency shifts and
\left\|T_{x}M_{w}f\right\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq C\|f\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}
.
Since
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)\subseteq M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\cap M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, using Corollary 12.1.10 from
[
18
]
, we obtain the following inclusions

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})\subset M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\subset M^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})=L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\subset M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\subset\mathcal{S^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}).

In particular, we have
M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
for
1\leq p\leq 2
, and
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\hookrightarrow M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
for
2\leq p\leq\infty
. Furthermore, the dual of a modulation space is also a modulation space, if
p<\infty
,
q<\infty
,
(M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))^{{}^{\prime}}=M^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, where
p^{\prime},\;q^{\prime}
denote the dual exponents of
p
and
q
, respectively. We refer to Gröchenig’s book
[
18
]
for further properties and uses of modulation spaces.

4.
The windowed Opdam–Cherednik transform

Let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, the modulation operator of
g
associated with the Opdam–Cherednik transform is defined by

(4.1)

{\mathcal{M}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{\xi}g={\mathcal{H}}^{-1}_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\sqrt{\tau_{\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}|{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha,\beta}(g)|^{2}}\right).

Then, for every
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, by using the Plancherel formula (
2.2
) and
the translation invariance of the Plancherel measure

d\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
, we obtain

(4.2)

\left\|{\mathcal{M}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{\xi}g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}=\left\|g\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Now, for a non-zero window function
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
and
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we consider the function
g_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
defined by

(4.3)

g_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}=\tau_{x}^{(\alpha,\beta)}{\mathcal{M}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{\xi}g.

For any function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, we define the windowed Opdam–Cherednik transform by

(4.4)

{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g}(f)(x,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s)\;\overline{g_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-s)}\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(s)\;ds,\quad(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

which can be also written in the form

(4.5)

{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g}(f)(x,\xi)=\left(f*_{\alpha,\beta}\overline{{\mathcal{M}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{\xi}g}\right)(x).

We define the measure
A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by

(4.6)

d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi)=A_{\alpha,\beta}(x)dx\;d\sigma_{\alpha,\beta}(\xi).

The windowed Opdam–Cherednik transform satisfies the following properties.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
be a non-zero window function. Then we have

(1)

(
Plancherel’s formula
)
For every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
,

(4.7)

\left\|{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\;A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

(2)

(
Orthogonality relation
)
For every
f,h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, we have

(4.8)

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g}(f)(x,\xi)\;\overline{{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g}(h)(x,\xi)}\;d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi)=\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(s)\overline{h(s)}\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(s)\;ds.

(3)

(
Reproducing kernel Hilbert space
)
The space

\displaystyle K_{g}((x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime});(x,\xi))

\displaystyle=

where

Proof.

(3) From relations (

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g}(f)(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g}(f)(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})\;\overline{{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g}\left(g_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-\;\cdot)\right)(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})}\;d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g}(f),\;K_{g}((\cdot,\cdot);(x,\xi))\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})},

\displaystyle K_{g}((x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime});(x,\xi))

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}\;{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g}\left(g_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-\;\cdot)\right)(x^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})

5.
Localization operators for the windowed Opdam–Cherednik transform

In this section, we define the localization operators for the windowed Opdam–Cherednik transform and we show that these operators are bounded. Also, we prove that localization operators are compact and in the Schatten–von Neumann class.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\varsigma\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})\cup L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
. The localization operator for the windowed Opdam–Cherednik transform associated with the symbol
\varsigma
and two window functions
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
, is denoted by
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)
, and defined on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, by

(5.1)

\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)(f)(y)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varsigma(x,\xi)\;{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{1}}(f)(x,\xi)\;{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-y)\;d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi),\quad y\in\mathbb{R}.

Also, it is useful to rewrite the definition of
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)
in a weak sense as, for every
f,h\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})

(5.2)

\left\langle\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)(f),h\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varsigma(x,\xi)\;{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{1}}(f)(x,\xi)\;\overline{{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{2}}(h)(x,\xi)}\;d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi).

We denote by
\mathcal{B}(L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the space of all bounded linear operators from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
into itself. In particular,
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))
denote the C
∗
-algebra of bounded linear operator
\mathcal{A}
from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
into itself, equipped with the norm

\|\mathcal{A}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))}=\sup_{\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq 1}\|\mathcal{A}(f)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Next, we define the Schatten–von Neumann class
S_{p}
. For a compact operator
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))
, the eigenvalues of the positive self-adjoint operator
|\mathcal{A}|=\sqrt{\mathcal{A}^{*}\mathcal{A}}
are called the singular values of
\mathcal{A}
and denoted by
\{s_{n}(\mathcal{A})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, the Schatten–von Neumann class
S_{p}
is defined to be the space of all compact operators whose singular values lie in
\ell^{p}
.
S_{p}
is equipped with the norm

\|\mathcal{A}\|_{S_{p}}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(s_{n}(\mathcal{A}))^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

For
p=\infty
, the Schatten–von Neumann class
S_{\infty}
is the class of all compact operators with the norm
\|\mathcal{A}\|_{S_{\infty}}:=\|\mathcal{A}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))}
. In particular, for
p=1
, we define the trace of an operator
\mathcal{A}
in
S_{1}
by

tr(\mathcal{A})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{A}v_{n},v_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})},

where
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
is any orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{A}
is positive, then

tr(\mathcal{A})=\|\mathcal{A}\|_{S_{1}}.

A compact operator
\mathcal{A}
on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
is called the Hilbert–Schmidt operator, if the positive operator
\mathcal{A}^{*}\mathcal{A}
is in the trace class
S_{1}
. Then for any orthonormal basis
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, we have

\|\mathcal{A}\|_{HS}^{2}:=\|\mathcal{A}\|_{S_{2}}^{2}=\|\mathcal{A}^{*}\mathcal{A}\|_{S_{1}}=tr(\mathcal{A}^{*}\mathcal{A})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|\mathcal{A}v_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

5.1.
Boundedness and compactness of localization operators

In this subsection, we consider window functions
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
and establish the following boundedness and compactness results of localization operators.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\varsigma\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})

∎

.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
,
1<p<\infty
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. Then, for fixed
\varsigma\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
the operator
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}
can be uniquely extended to a bounded linear operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, such that

\left\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Proof.

Let
1<p<\infty
. For every
\varsigma\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})\cap M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
, using Proposition
5.3
, Proposition
5.4
, the fact that the modulation spaces
M^{p,q}
interpolate exactly like the corresponding mixed-norm spaces
L^{p,q}
and the Riesz–Thorin interpolation theorem (see
[
32
]
), we obtain

\left\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
and
\{\varsigma_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of functions in
M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})\cap M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
such that
\varsigma_{n}\to\varsigma
in
M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
as
n\to\infty
. Hence for every
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\left\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma_{n})-\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma_{k})\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))}\leq\|\varsigma_{n}-\varsigma_{k}\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Therefore,
\{\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma_{n})\}_{n\geq 1}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))
. Let
\{\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma_{n})\}_{n\geq 1}
converges to
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)
. Then the limit
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)
is independent of the choice of
\{\varsigma_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
and we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma_{n})\right\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\varsigma_{n}\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\varsigma\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
,
1\leq p<\infty
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. Then the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma):L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
is compact.

Proof.

Assume that
\varsigma\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
. Let
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
be an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. Since
M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})\subset L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
, using Fubini’s theorem and Parseval’s identity, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)(v_{n}),v_{n}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varsigma(x,\xi)\langle v_{n},{g_{1}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-\;\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\langle{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-\;\cdot),v_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varsigma(x,\xi)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle v_{n},{g_{1}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-\;\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\langle{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-\;\cdot),v_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\;d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varsigma(x,\xi)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle v_{n},{g_{1}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-\;\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-\;\cdot),v_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\;d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\varsigma\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;(\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;(\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}).\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

Therefore, the operator
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)
is in
S_{1}
. Next, assume that
\varsigma\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
. We consider a sequence of functions
\{\varsigma_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
in
M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})\cap M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
such that
\varsigma_{n}\to\varsigma
in
M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
as
n\to\infty
. Then, using Theorem
5.5
, we get

\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma_{n})-\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))}\leq\|\varsigma_{n}-\varsigma\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
. Hence,
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma_{n})\to\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)
in
\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))
as
n\to\infty
. From the above, we obtain that
\{\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma_{n})\}_{n\geq 1}
is a sequence of linear operators in
S_{1}
and hence compact, so
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)
is compact.
∎

5.2.
Boundedness of
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)
on
S_{p}

In this subsection, we prove that the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)
is in
S_{p}
and provide an upper bound of the norm
\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{S_{p}}
.
We begin with the following proposition.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. Then the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma):L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
is in
S_{1}
and

\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{S_{1}}\leq 2\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;(\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}).

Proof.

If
\varsigma\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
, then from the first part of the proof of Theorem
5.6
, the operator
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)
is in
S_{1}
. Now, to prove the estimate, assume that
\varsigma
is non-negative and in
M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
. Then
(\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)^{*}\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma))^{1/2}=\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)
(see
[
4
,
35
]
). Let
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
be an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
consisting of eigenvalues of
(\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)^{*}\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma))^{1/2}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
.
Then by using the estimate obtained in the first part of the proof of Theorem
5.6
, we get

(5.5)

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle(\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)^{*}\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma))^{1/2}(v_{n}),v_{n}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)(v_{n}),v_{n}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;(\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}).

Next, assume that
\varsigma\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
is an arbitrary real-valued function. We can write
\varsigma=\varsigma_{+}-\varsigma_{-}
, where
\varsigma_{+}=\max(\varsigma,0)
and
\varsigma_{-}=-\min(\varsigma,0)
. Then, using relation (
5.5
), we obtain

(5.6)

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma_{+})-\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma_{-})\|_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma_{+})\|_{S_{1}}+\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma_{-})\|_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})})(\|\varsigma_{+}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}+\|\varsigma_{-}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})})\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Finally, assume that
\varsigma\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
is a complex-valued function. Then, we can write
\varsigma=\varsigma_{1}+i\varsigma_{2}
, where
\varsigma_{1},\varsigma_{2}
are the real and imaginary parts of
\varsigma
respectively. Then, using relation (
5.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma_{1})+i\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma_{2})\|_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma_{1})\|_{S_{1}}+\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma_{2})\|_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})})(\|\varsigma_{1}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}+\|\varsigma_{2}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}(\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}).

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. Then the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma):L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
is in
S_{p}
and

\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{S_{p}}\leq 2^{1/p}\|\varsigma\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;(\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})})^{1/p}.

Proof.

The proof follows from Proposition
5.4
, Proposition
5.7
and by interpolation theorems (see
[
35
]
, Theorems 2.10 and 2.11).
∎

Next, we improve the constant given in the previous proposition and also we provide a lower bound of the norm
\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{S_{1}}
, more precisely we have the following.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. Then the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)
is in
S_{1}
and we have

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}}\;\|\tilde{\varsigma}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{S_{1}}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})})\;\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})},

where
\tilde{\varsigma}
is given by
\tilde{\varsigma}(x,\xi)=\left\langle\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)({g_{1}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}),{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}
.

Proof.

Since
\varsigma\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
, by Proposition
5.7
,
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)
is in
S_{1}
. Using the canonical form of compact operators (see
[
35
]
, Theorem 2.2), we obtain

(5.7)

\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)(f)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma))\langle f,v_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}u_{n},

where
\{s_{n}(\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma))\}_{n}
are the positive singular values of
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)
,
\{v_{n}\}_{n}
is an orthonormal basis for the orthogonal complement of the null space of
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)
consisting of eigenvectors of
|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)|
and
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
is an orthonormal set in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. Then we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)(v_{n}),u_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma))=\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{S_{1}}.

Now, using Fubini’s theorem, Cauchy–Schwarz’s inequality, and Bessel’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)(v_{n}),u_{n}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varsigma(x,\xi)\;{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{1}}(v_{n})(x,\xi)\;\overline{{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{2}}(u_{n})(x,\xi)}\;d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\varsigma(x,\xi)|\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{1}}(v_{n})(x,\xi)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{2}}(u_{n})(x,\xi)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\;\times\;d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\varsigma\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|{g_{1}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})})\;\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Next, we show that
\tilde{\varsigma}\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
. Using formula (
5.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\tilde{\varsigma}(x,\xi)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)({g_{1}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}),{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma))\left\langle{g_{1}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)},v_{n}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\left\langle u_{n},{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma))\left(\left|\left\langle{g_{1}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)},v_{n}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\right|^{2}+\left|\left\langle{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)},u_{n}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\right|^{2}\right).

Now, using Plancherel’s formula (
4.7
) and Fubini’s theorem, we get

(5.8)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\varsigma}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\tilde{\varsigma}(x,\xi)|\;d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma))\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left\langle{g_{1}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)},v_{n}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\right|^{2}d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi)\right.

\displaystyle\;\;\left.+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left\langle{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)},u_{n}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\right|^{2}d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})})\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s_{n}(\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})})\;\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{S_{1}}.

Also, we have

(5.9)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\varsigma}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}=\|V_{g}\tilde{\varsigma}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\varsigma}*M_{\xi}g^{*}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\varsigma}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})},

where we obtain the estimate with respect to some
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\setminus\{0\}
with
\|g\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq 1
. Finally, using (
5.8
) and (
5.9
), we get

\|\tilde{\varsigma}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\|g_{1}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}+\|g_{2}\|^{2}_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})})\;\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{S_{1}}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

5.3.
M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
Boundedness

In this section, we prove that the localization operators
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma):M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\to M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
are bounded. We begin with the following propositions.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
,
g_{1}\in M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
and
g_{2}\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma):M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\to M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
is a bounded linear operator, and we have

\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{\mathcal{B}(M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Proof.

Let
f\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
g\in M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. Then from Hölder’s inequality and the relation (
4.4
), we have

(5.10)

\left|{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g}(f)(x,\xi)\right|\leq\|f\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g\|_{M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

For every
f\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
and
h\in M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, using the relations (
5.2
) and (
5.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)(f),h\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\varsigma(x,\xi)|\;\left|{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{1}}(f)(x,\xi)\right|\left|{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{2}}(h)(x,\xi)\right|\;d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|f\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|h\|_{M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Hence,

\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{\mathcal{B}(M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

∎

Next, we obtain an
M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
-boundedness result using the Schur technique. The estimate obtained for the norm
\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{\mathcal{B}(M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))}
is different from the previous Proposition.

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. Then there exists a bounded linear operator
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma):M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\to M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{\mathcal{B}(M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))}

\displaystyle\leq\max(\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\|g_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})},\|g_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})})\;\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by

(5.11)

\mathcal{K}(y,z)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varsigma(x,\xi)\;\overline{{g_{1}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-z)}\;{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-y)\;d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi).

Then we define

\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)(f)(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{K}(y,z)f(z)\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(z)\;dz.

Now, using Fubini’s theorem, for any
z\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{K}(y,z)|\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(y)\;dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\varsigma(x,\xi)|\left|\overline{{g_{1}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-z)}\right|\left|{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-y)\right|d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi)\right)A_{\alpha,\beta}(y)\;dy

\displaystyle\leq\|g_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})},

and for any
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathcal{K}(y,z)|\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(z)\;dz\leq\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Thus using Schur’s lemma (see
[
17
]
), we conclude that, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma):M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\to M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
is a bounded linear operator, and we have

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{\mathcal{B}(M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))}

\displaystyle\leq\max(\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\|g_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})},\|g_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})})\;\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}.

∎

Remark 5.12
.

From the Proposition
5.11
, we conclude that the bounded linear operator on
M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, obtained in Proposition
5.10
is actually the integral operator on
M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
with the kernel
\mathcal{K}
given by (
5.11
).

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. Then the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)
is in
\mathcal{B}(M^{q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
and we have

\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{\mathcal{B}(M^{q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Proof.

Let
f\in M^{q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
and
h\in M^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, where
q^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent of
q
. Using the duality between the modulation spaces
M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
and
M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
, we obtain

(5.12)

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)(f),h\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\varsigma(x,\xi)|\;\left|{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{1}}(f)(x,\xi)\;\overline{{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{2}}(h)(x,\xi)}\right|\;d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\varsigma\right\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\left\|{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{1}}(f)\cdot\overline{{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{2}}(h)}\right\|_{M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Since the definition of
M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
is independent of the choice of the window
g
, we estimate the STFT of
{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{1}}(f)\cdot\overline{{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{2}}(h)}
with respect to some
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\setminus\{0\}
with
\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq 1
. Using Young’s convolution inequality and Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{1}}(f)\cdot\overline{{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{2}}(h)}\right\|_{M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle=\left\|V_{g}\left({\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{1}}(f)\cdot\overline{{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{2}}(h)}\right)\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{4},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left({\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{1}}(f)\cdot\overline{{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{2}}(h)}\right)*M_{\xi}g^{*}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{4},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{1}}(f)\cdot\overline{{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{2}}(h)}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\left\|M_{\xi}g^{*}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{1}}(f)\right\|_{M^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\left\|{\mathcal{W}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{g_{2}}(h)\right\|_{M^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle=\left\|f*_{\alpha,\beta}\overline{\mathcal{M}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{\xi}g_{1}}\right\|_{M^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\left\|h*_{\alpha,\beta}\overline{\mathcal{M}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{\xi}g_{2}}\right\|_{M^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}

(5.13)

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{M^{q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|h\|_{M^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

Thus from (
5.12
) and (
5.3
), we obtain

\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\|_{\mathcal{B}(M^{q}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}))}\leq\|\varsigma\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}.

∎

5.4.
Compactness of
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)
for symbols in
M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})

In this section, we prove that the localization operators
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma):M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\to M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}),\;1<p<\infty
are compact for symbols
\varsigma
in
M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
. Let us start with the following proposition.

Proposition 5.14
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
. Then, for fixed
1<p<\infty
, the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma):M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\to M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
is compact.

Proof.

Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of functions in
M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
such that
f_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
as
n\to\infty
. It is sufficient to prove that
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)(f_{n})\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}=0
. From the relation (
5.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)(f_{n})(y)\right|

(5.14)

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\varsigma(x,\xi)|\left|\left\langle f_{n},{g_{1}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-\;\cdot)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\right|\left|{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-y)\right|\;d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi).

Since
f_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, we deduce that

(5.15)

\lim_{n\to\infty}|\varsigma(x,\xi)|\left|\left\langle f_{n},{g_{1}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-\;\cdot)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\right|\left|{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-y)\right|=0,\quad\text{for all}\;\;x,y,\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

Moreover, as
f_{n}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
as
n\to\infty
, then there exists a constant
C>0
such that
\|f_{n}\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\leq C
. Hence, for all
x,y,\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain

(5.16)

|\varsigma(x,\xi)|\left|\left\langle f_{n},{g_{1}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-\;\cdot)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\right|\left|{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-y)\right|\leq C|\varsigma(x,\xi)|\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\left|{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-y)\right|.

Further, using Fubini’s theorem, we get

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)(f_{n})\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}

\displaystyle=\left\|V_{g}\left(\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\left(f_{n}\right)\right)\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\left(f_{n}\right)\right)*M_{\xi}g^{*}\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)\left(f_{n}\right)\right\|_{L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}\right)}\left\|M_{\xi}g^{*}\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\varsigma(x,\xi)|\left|\left\langle f_{n},{g_{1}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-\;\cdot)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\right|\left|{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-y)\right|\;d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi)\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(y)\;dy

\displaystyle\leq C\;\|g_{1}\|_{M^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\varsigma(x,\xi)|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|{g_{2}}_{x,\xi}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-y)\right|\;A_{\alpha,\beta}(y)dy\;d(A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})(x,\xi)

(5.17)

\displaystyle\leq C\;\|g_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|g_{2}\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}\;\|\varsigma\|_{M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})}<\infty.

Thus, using the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem and the relations (
5.4
) – (
5.4
), we obtain that

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma)(f_{n})\|_{M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})}=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.15
.

Let
\varsigma\in M^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},A_{\alpha,\beta}\otimes\sigma_{\alpha,\beta})
,
g_{1}\in M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
and
g_{2}\in M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, for
1<p<\infty
. Then the localization operator
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma):M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\to M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
is compact.

Proof.

Let
p^{\prime}
be the conjugate exponent of
p
. We first show that the conclusion of the previous proposition holds for
p^{\prime}
. The operator
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma):M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\to M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
is the adjoint of the operator
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\overline{\varsigma}):M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\to M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, which is compact by the previous proposition. Hence, by the duality properties of modulation spaces,
\mathfrak{L}_{g_{1},g_{2}}(\varsigma):M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})\to M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
is compact. Finally, using the interpolation of the compactness on
M^{p}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
and on
M^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R},A_{\alpha,\beta})
, the proof is complete.
∎

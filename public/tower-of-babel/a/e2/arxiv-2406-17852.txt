Non-perturbative topological strings from resurgence
Murad Alim

Abstract
The partition function of topological string theory on any family of Calabi-Yau threefolds is defined perturbatively as an asymptotic series in the topological string coupling and encodes, in a holomorphic limit, higher genus Gromov-Witten as well as Gopakumar-Vafa invariants. We prove that the partition function of topological strings of any CY in this limit can be written as a product, where each factor is given by the partition function of the resolved conifold with shifted arguments, raised to the power of certain sheaf invariants. We use this result to put forward an expression for the non-perturbative topological string partition function in this limit, as a product over analytic functions in the topological string coupling which correspond to the Borel sums for the resolved conifold found previously. We furthermore find an expression for the Borel transform of the full asymptotic series in this limit expressed in terms of the sheaf invariants. We use this to define the Borel sums and compute the corresponding Stokes jumps which constitute non-perturbative corrections to the partition function. The jumps depend only on genus zero GV invariants and their sum can be expressed entirely in terms of a single function which is introduced as a deformation of the prepotential.

2
Topological strings and enumerative geometry

To a mirror family of CY threefolds, topological string theory associates the topological string partition function which is defined as an asymptotic series in the topological string coupling
\lambda

associated to Riemann surfaces of genus
g

Z_{top}(\lambda,t)=\exp\left(\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}\lambda^{2g-2}\mathcal{F}^{g}(t)\right)\,,

(2.1)

where
t=(t^{1},\dots,t^{n})
is a set of distinguished local coordinates on the underlying moduli space

. Given any ray
\rho
from
0
to
\infty
different from
\{\pm l_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{\pm l_{\infty}\}
, and
\lambda
in the half-plane
\mathbb{H}_{\rho}
centered at
\rho
, we define the Borel sum of
\widetilde{F}^{con}(\lambda,t)
along
\rho

F^{con}_{\rho}(\lambda,t):=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathrm{Li}_{3}(Q)+\frac{B_{2}}{2}\mathrm{Li}_{1}(Q)+\int_{\rho}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-\xi/\check{\lambda}}G^{con}(\xi,t)\,.

(3.7)

(iii)

Let
\rho_{k}
be a ray in the sector determined by the Stokes rays
l_{k}
and
l_{k-1}
. Then if
\mathrm{Im}(t)>0
, on the overlap of their domains of definition in the
\lambda

\displaystyle\phi_{\pm l_{k}}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle:=F^{con}_{\pm\rho_{k+1}}(\lambda,t)-F^{con}_{\pm\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\partial_{\check{\lambda}}\Big(\check{\lambda}\,\mathrm{Li}_{2}\big(e^{\pm 2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+k)/\check{\lambda}}\big)\Big)\;,\,

(3.8)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\left(\mathrm{Li}_{2}\big(e^{\pm 2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+k)/\check{\lambda}}\big)\pm 2\pi{\mathrm{i}}\frac{(t+k)}{\check{\lambda}}\log\left(1-e^{\pm 2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+k)/\check{\lambda}}\right)\right)\;.

(3.9)

If
\mathrm{Im}(t)<0
, then the previous jumps also hold provided
\rho_{k+1}
is interchanged with
\rho_{k}
in the above formula.

(iv)

Let
\rho_{k}
denote any ray between the rays
l_{k}
and
l_{k-1}
. Furthermore, assume that
0<\mathrm{Re}(t)<1
,
\mathrm{Im}(t)>0
,
\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)>0
,
\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)<0
, and
\mathrm{Re}\,t<\mathrm{Re}(\check{\lambda}+1)
. Then

\lim_{k\to\infty}F^{con}_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)=\lim_{k\to\infty}F^{con}_{-\rho_{k}}(-\lambda,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}kt}}{k\big(2\sin\big(\frac{\lambda k}{2}\big)\big)^{2}}\,.

(3.10)

Furthermore, we can write the sum of the Stokes jumps along
l_{k}
for
k\geq 0
as

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\phi_{l_{k}}(\lambda,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\partial_{\lambda}\Big(\lambda\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{Q^{\prime l}}{m^{2}(1-q^{\prime m})}\Big),\;\;\;\;Q^{\prime}:=e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t/\check{\lambda}},\;\;q^{\prime}:=e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}/\check{\lambda}}\;.

(3.11)

If, on the other hand, we take
0<\mathrm{Re}(t)<1
,
\mathrm{Im}(t)>0
,
\mathrm{Re}(\lambda)>0
,
\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)>0
,
\mathrm{Re}(t)<\mathrm{Re}(\check{\lambda}+1)
and furthermore assume that
|e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t/\check{\lambda}}|<1
, then we also have

\lim_{k\to-\infty}F^{con}_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)=\lim_{k\to-\infty}F^{con}_{-\rho_{k}}(-\lambda,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}kt}}{k\big(2\sin\big(\frac{\lambda k}{2}\big)\big)^{2}}.

(3.12)

\lim_{k\to\infty}F^{con}_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)
differs from
F^{con}_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}({\lambda},t)
by the sum over all jumps

\phi_{l_{k}}(\lambda,t)
for
k\geq 0
, leading to the decomposition, see

25
]

F^{con}_{{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}}({\lambda},t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}kt}}{k\big(2\sin\big(\frac{\lambda k}{2}\big)\big)^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\partial_{\lambda}\Big(\lambda\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{Q^{\prime l}}{m^{2}(1-q^{\prime m})}\Big).

(3.13)

F^{con}_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}({\lambda},t)
could furthermore be identified with the solution
\widetilde{F}^{con}_{\text{np}}(\lambda,t)
of the difference equation (
3.1
).

\begin{split}F^{con}_{\mathbb{R}_{>0}}({\lambda},t)=\widetilde{F}^{con}_{\text{np}}(\lambda,t):=\left(\frac{\pi{\mathrm{i}}}{6}\,B_{3,3}\left(t+\check{\lambda}\,|\,\check{\lambda},\check{\lambda},1\right)\right)+\log\left(\sin_{3}\left(t+\check{\lambda}\,|\,\check{\lambda},\check{\lambda},1\right)\right).\end{split}

(3.14)

We proceed by giving the product structure of the partition functions obtained from the Borel sums corresponding to the free energies, these follow from the results of
[
25
]
but were not contained in that paper.

Proposition 3.1
.

Product form of the partition functions.

For the partition functions

Z_{\rho}(\lambda,t):=\exp(F_{\rho}(\lambda,t))\,,

we have the following product forms:

\displaystyle Z_{\rho_{\infty}}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{m=0}^{\infty}(1-Qq^{m+1})^{m+1}\,,

(3.15)

\displaystyle Z_{\rho_{0}}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{m=0}^{\infty}(1-Q\,q^{m+1})^{m+1}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\operatorname{Li}_{2}(Q^{\prime}q^{\prime m})\right)\cdot(1-Q^{\prime}q^{\prime m})^{-\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\left(t+m\right)}\,,

(3.16)

\displaystyle Z^{con}_{\rho_{k}}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\prod_{m=0}^{\infty}(1-Q\,q^{m+1})^{m+1}\cdot\prod_{m=k}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\operatorname{Li}_{2}(Q^{\prime}q^{\prime m})\right)\cdot(1-Q^{\prime}q^{\prime m})^{-\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\left(t+m\right)}\,,

(3.17)

where
Q=\exp(2\pi it),\quad q=e^{\textnormal{i}\lambda}\,,\quad Q^{\prime}=\exp(2\pi it/\check{\lambda})\,,\quad q^{\prime}=\exp(2\pi i/\check{\lambda})
. For the two special rays
\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{\infty}
we will use special names for the corresponding partition functions:

Z^{con}_{GV}(\lambda,t):=Z_{\rho_{\infty}}(\lambda,t)\,,\quad Z^{con}_{np}(\lambda,t):=Z_{\rho_{0}}(\lambda,t)\,.

Proof.

To prove the first product form we use the following expansion:

\frac{1}{\left(2\sin\left(k\lambda/2\right)\right)^{2}}=-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}me^{\textnormal{i}mk\lambda}\,,

(3.18)

valid for
\textrm{Im}\lambda>0
and which can be obtained from the geometric series by a derivation in
\lambda
. We have

F_{GV}(\lambda,t):=F_{\infty}(\lambda,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}kt}}{k\big(2\sin\big(\frac{\lambda k}{2}\big)\big)^{2}}\,,

(3.19)

which can be re-written using the identity (
3.18
) as:

F_{GV}(\lambda,t)=-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}kt}e^{\textnormal{i}mk\lambda}}{k}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m\,\log(1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}t}e^{\textnormal{i}m\lambda})\,,

(3.20)

the product form then follows by taking
Z^{con}_{GV}(\lambda,t)=\exp(F_{GV}(\lambda,t))
and shifting the summation index.
The product form for
Z_{\rho_{0}}
follows from the form:

F_{\rho_{0}}({\lambda},t)=F^{con}_{\mathbb{R}>0}(\lambda,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}kt}}{k\big(2\sin\big(\frac{\lambda k}{2}\big)\big)^{2}}-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\phi_{l_{k}}(\lambda,t)\,,

(3.21)

and the expressions for the Stokes jumps:

\displaystyle\phi_{l_{k}}(\lambda,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\left(\mathrm{Li}_{2}\big(e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+k)/\check{\lambda}}\big)+2\pi{\mathrm{i}}\frac{(t+k)}{\check{\lambda}}\log\left(1-e^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t+k)/\check{\lambda}}\right)\right)\;,

(3.22)

the product expression then follows by exponentiation, the product form for
Z_{\rho_{k}}
follows from taking into account the exponentiated Stokes factors.

∎

3.2
Constant maps contributions

For the formal series giving the constant map contributions to the GW potential for any CY we have

\displaystyle F_{c}(\lambda,t)=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}F^{g}_{c}

\displaystyle=F_{c}^{0}(t)+F_{c}^{1}(t)+\chi(X)\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{g-1}\,B_{2g}\,B_{2g-2}}{4g(2g-2)\,(2g-2)!}\lambda^{2g-2}\,,\quad\check{\lambda}=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\,\,

(3.23)

\displaystyle=F_{c}^{0}(t)+F_{c}^{1}(t)+\Phi_{c}(\check{\lambda})\,,\quad\check{\lambda}=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\,\,,

(3.24)

the terms at genus
0
and
1
are
t
dependent and encode also classical topological data of the CY such as the triple intersections and second Chern class, see
[
53
]
for details.

In
[
25
]
, the case of the resolved conifold was treated for which
\chi(X)=2
and the constant map contribution has the form:

\displaystyle F^{con}_{c}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=F_{c}^{0}(t)+F_{c}^{1}(t)+\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{g-1}\,B_{2g}\,B_{2g-2}}{2g(2g-2)\,(2g-2)!}\lambda^{2g-2}\,,\quad\check{\lambda}=\frac{\lambda}{2\pi}\,

(3.25)

\displaystyle=F_{c}^{0}(t)+F_{c}^{1}(t)+\Phi_{c}^{con}(\check{\lambda})\,.

(3.26)

It was shown in
[
25
]
that the Borel transform of
\Phi_{c}^{con}(\check{\lambda})
is given by:

G_{c}^{con}(\xi)=-G^{con}(\xi,0)-\frac{1}{12\xi}\,,

(3.27)

and that correspondingly the Borel sums of the constant map contributions are given by

F^{con}_{c,\rho}(\lambda)=-F^{con}_{\rho}(\lambda,0)-\frac{1}{12}\log\check{\lambda}+C\,,

(3.28)

with
C
a constant which does not play a role.
We thus obtain that the Borel transform of
\Phi_{c}(\check{\lambda})
in the more general case is given by:

G_{c}(\xi)=-\frac{\chi(X)}{24\xi}-\frac{\chi(X)}{2(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{m^{3}}\frac{1}{2\xi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\bigg(\frac{\xi^{2}}{1-e^{\xi/m}}-\frac{\xi^{2}}{1-e^{-\xi/m}}\bigg),

(3.29)

which can be analytically continued in the
\xi
variable to a meromorphic function with poles at
\xi=2\pi{\mathrm{i}}k
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Furthermore given any ray
\rho
from
0
to
\infty
different from
\{\pm l_{\infty}\}
, and
\lambda
in the half-plane
\mathbb{H}_{\rho}
centered at
\rho
, the Borel sum of
F_{c}(\lambda)
along
\rho
is given by

F_{c,\rho}(\lambda):=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}F^{0}_{c}(t)+F^{1}_{c}(t)+\int_{\rho}\mathrm{d}\xi\,e^{-\xi/\check{\lambda}}G_{c}(\xi)\,.

(3.30)

Proposition 3.2
.

Product form of the partition function.

For the partition function

Z^{con}_{c,np}(\lambda):=\exp(F^{con}_{c,\rho}(\lambda))\check{\lambda}^{-1/12}e^{C}

we have the following product form:

\displaystyle Z^{con}_{c,np}(\lambda)=\left(e^{C}\check{\lambda}^{-1/12}\exp\left(\frac{\textnormal{i}\pi}{12}\right)\prod_{m=0}^{\infty}(1-q^{m+1})^{m+1}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\operatorname{Li}_{2}(q^{\prime m})\right)\cdot(1-q^{\prime m})^{-\frac{2\pi m}{\lambda}}\right)^{-1}\,,

(3.31)

where
q=\exp(i\lambda)\quad q^{\prime}=\exp(2\pi i/\check{\lambda})
,

Proof.

This follows from evaluating the

(3.32)

4
Non-perturbative topological strings

4.1
Sheaf invariants

Our first goal is to re-write the topological string free energy on an arbitrary CY threefold in terms of the free energy of the resolved conifold. This will be possible using a re-arrangement of the GV invariants in terms of a set of sheaf invariants
\Omega_{\beta,n}
defined in the following way:

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\,}\Omega_{\beta,n}y^{n}=\sum_{g\geq 0}[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,g}\,(-1)^{g}(y^{1/2}-y^{-1/2})^{2g}\,,

(4.1)

since the RHSis invariant under
y\rightarrow y^{-1}
we have:

\Omega_{\beta,n}=\Omega_{\beta,-n}\,,\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

(4.2)

these are up to a sign the sheaf invariants put forward by Maulik and Toda
[
41
]
, following works of Hosono-Saito-Takahashi
[
55
]
and Kiem-Li
[
56
]
to define the Gopakumar-Vafa invariants in terms of moduli spaces of sheaves, we are using the notation of
[
43
]
which should also be consulted for further details and references.

It is useful to note the relation of how each
\Omega_{\beta,k}\,
can be obtained from the GV invariants. We recall the finiteness of the GV invariants discussed in
2.3
, stating that for every curve class
\beta
, there exists a
g_{\beta}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,g^{\prime}}=0\,,\quad\forall g^{\prime}\geq g_{\beta}\,,

by comparing the
y^{k}
coefficient on both sides of (
4.1
) we obtain:

\Omega_{\beta,k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-1)^{k}\sum_{g=k}^{g_{\beta}-1}[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,g}\binom{2g}{g-k}\,,\quad k\geq 0\,,&\mbox{if }k<g_{\beta}\\
0&\mbox{if }k\geq g_{\beta}\end{array}\right.

(4.3)

the
k<0
invariants are obtained by (
4.2
).

We further note that accordingly the sum
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\beta,n}
is finite. This sum is equal to the limit
y\rightarrow 1
of the LHS of

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\,}\Omega_{\beta,n}y^{n}=\sum_{g\geq 0}[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,g}\,(-1)^{g}(y^{1/2}-y^{-1/2})^{2g}\,,

evaluating the limit on the RHS we obtain:

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\beta,n}=[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,0}\,.

(4.4)

4.2
Free energy in terms of the resolved conifold

Theorem 4.1
.

The generating function of GW invariants, excluding the classical terms as well as the contributions from constant maps for any Calabi-Yau threefold:

\tilde{F}_{GW}(\lambda,t)=\sum_{g=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\beta>0}\lambda^{2g-2}[GW]_{\beta,g}Q^{\beta}\,,

(4.5)

can be written as:

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{GW}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\beta>0}\Omega_{\beta,n}\widetilde{F}^{con}(\lambda,t^{\beta,n})\,

(4.6)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\beta>0}\Omega_{\beta,n}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathrm{Li}_{3}(Q^{\beta,n})+\sum_{g=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{2g-2}\frac{(-1)^{g-1}B_{2g}}{2g(2g-2)!}\mathrm{Li}_{3-2g}(Q^{\beta,n})\,\right)\,,

(4.7)

where
t^{\beta,n}:=t^{\beta}+n\check{\lambda}\,,Q^{\beta,n}:=\exp(2\pi\textnormal{i}t^{\beta,n})
and
\Omega_{\beta,n}
are the integers defined in (
4.1
). A similar relation holds for the generating function of GV invariants:

F_{GV}(\lambda,t)=\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{g\geq 0}[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,g}\,\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}\left(2\sin\left(\frac{k\lambda}{2}\right)\right)^{2g-2}Q^{k\beta}\,,

(4.8)

namely:

\displaystyle F_{GV}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\beta>0}\Omega_{\beta,n}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\log\left(1-Q^{\beta}\exp(\textnormal{i}(n+m)\,\lambda)\right)^{m}\,,

(4.9)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\beta>0}\Omega_{\beta,n}F_{GV}^{con}(\lambda,t^{\beta}+n\check{\lambda})\,.

(4.10)

Proof.

We start with the Gopakumar-Vafa form of the topological string free energy:

F_{GV}(\lambda,t)=\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{Q^{k\beta}}{k\left(2\sin\left(\frac{k\lambda}{2}\right)\right)^{2}}\sum_{g\geq 0}[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,g}\,\left(2\sin\left(\frac{k\lambda}{2}\right)\right)^{2g}\,,

(4.11)

using (
4.1
) with
y=\exp(\textnormal{i}k\lambda)
this becomes:

F_{GV}(\lambda,t)=\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{Q^{k\beta}}{k\left(2\sin\left(\frac{k\lambda}{2}\right)\right)^{2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\,}\Omega_{\beta,n}\exp(\textnormal{i}nk\lambda)\,,

(4.12)

using the following identity which can be derived from the geometric series:

\frac{1}{\left(2\sin\left(k\lambda/2\right)\right)^{2}}=-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m\exp(\textnormal{i}mk\lambda)\,,

(4.13)

we obtain:

F_{GV}(\lambda,t)=-\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{m\,Q^{k\beta}}{k}\Omega_{\beta,n}\exp(\textnormal{i}(n+m)k\,\lambda)\,,

(4.14)

we can further re-write this as:

F_{GV}(\lambda,t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\beta>0}\Omega_{\beta,n}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\log\left(1-Q^{\beta}\exp(\textnormal{i}(n+m)\,\lambda)\right)^{m}\,,

(4.15)

which can be expressed in terms of the free energy of the resolved conifold as:

\displaystyle F_{GV}(\lambda,t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\beta>0}\Omega_{\beta,n}F_{GV}^{con}(\lambda,t^{\beta}+n\check{\lambda})\,.

(4.16)

In this last expression we introduce
t^{\beta,n}:=t^{\beta}+n\check{\lambda}
and consider this as a formal variable and look at the asymptotic expansion of each summand in
\lambda
keeping
t^{\beta,n}
fixed, obtaining the following formal series, which is asymptotic in
\lambda\rightarrow 0
:

\tilde{F}_{GW}(\lambda,t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\beta>0}\Omega_{\beta,n}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\mathrm{Li}_{3}(Q^{\beta,n})+\sum_{g=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{2g-2}\frac{(-1)^{g-1}B_{2g}}{2g(2g-2)!}\mathrm{Li}_{3-2g}(Q^{\beta,n})\,\right)\,,

(4.17)

where
Q^{\beta,n}:=\exp(2\pi\textnormal{i}t^{\beta,n}).

∎

4.3
Non-perturbative free energy

Having expressed the generating function of GW invariants in an arbitrary CY as a sum over generating functions of the GW invariants of the resolved conifold, it is now natural to use the previously obtained analytic functions in the topological string coupling to put forward a non-perturbative expression for the topological string free energy in the holomorphic limit.

The expression of the asymptotic series of GW invariants of an arbitrary CY threefold in terms of the resolved conifold:

\tilde{F}_{GW}(\lambda,t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\beta>0}\Omega_{\beta,n}F_{GW}^{con}(\lambda,t^{\beta}+n\check{\lambda})\,,

(4.18)

suggests the use of the resurgence analysis of the resolved conifold to define the following:

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{top,np}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\beta>0}\Omega_{\beta,n}F^{con}_{np}(\lambda,t^{\beta}+n\check{\lambda})\,

(4.19)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\beta>0}\Omega_{\beta,n}\log\left(\mathcal{S}_{3}(t+n\check{\lambda}|\check{\lambda},1)\right)\,.

(4.20)

On the RHS of this equation each summand has good analytic properties in
\lambda
from the triple sine function, to be able to say whether this sum also defines a good analytic function one must however be careful with the summation coefficients, which are, especially for compact Calabi-Yau manifolds generically unknown. We note however that for each curve class
\beta
the summation of the coefficients
\Omega_{\beta,n}
is given by (
5.15
):

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\beta,n}=[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,0}\,,

(4.21)

although now there are generically infinitely many
[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,0}
which are non-vanishing for compact CY manifolds, it is known by mirror symmetry that all these invariants are generated by rational functions on the moduli space of the mirror manifolds, the Yukawa couplings, see
[
57
]
for more details. It seems thus reasonable to expect that the proposed non-perturbative expression could correspond to an actual function. We will later see however that although the issue with potential growth of the
\Omega_{\beta,n}
remains the same as for the perturbative expansion, the non-perturbative corrections obtained in this way nicely assemble into a function which will be given in sec.
4.4
.

We then have the following:

Proposition 4.2
.

Define

\tilde{Z}_{top,np}(\lambda,t)=\exp\left(\tilde{F}_{top,np}(\lambda,t)\right)

then we have the following product expansion:

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}_{top,np}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle:=Z_{top,wk}(\lambda,t)\times Z_{top,sg}(\lambda,t)

(4.22)

where

\displaystyle Z_{top,wk}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle:=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{\beta>0}\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(1-Q^{\beta}\exp(\textnormal{i}(n+m)\,\lambda)\right)^{m\,\Omega_{\beta,n}}\,,

(4.23)

\displaystyle Z_{top,sg}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle:=\prod_{\beta>0}\exp\left(-\frac{[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,0}}{2\pi i}\operatorname{Li}_{2}(Q^{\prime\beta}q^{\prime m})\right)\cdot(1-Q^{\prime\beta}q^{\prime m})^{-\frac{[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,0}}{\check{\lambda}}\left(t^{\beta}+m\right)}\,,

(4.24)

t^{\beta,n}:=t^{\beta}+n\check{\lambda}\,,t^{\beta,0}:=t^{\beta}\,,Q^{\beta,n}:=\exp(2\pi\textnormal{i}t^{\beta,n})\,,Q^{\beta}:=Q^{\beta,0}

and
q=e^{\textnormal{i}\lambda}\,,\quad Q^{\prime}=\exp(2\pi it/\check{\lambda})\,,\quad q^{\prime}=\exp(2\pi i/\check{\lambda})
. The subscripts
wk
and
sg
stand for weak and strong and refer to the pieces of the partition function which are expansions in
q
or
q^{\prime}
respectively.

Proof.

From the definition

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{top,np}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\beta>0}\Omega_{\beta,n}F^{con}_{np}(\lambda,t^{\beta}+n\check{\lambda})\,,

(4.25)

we obtain:

\displaystyle Z_{top,np}(\lambda,t)=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{\beta>0}Z^{con}_{np}(\lambda,t^{\beta}+n\check{\lambda})^{\Omega_{\beta,n}}\,,

(4.26)

we can now use the product expansion of (
3.1
):

Z^{con}_{np}(\lambda,t)=\prod_{m=0}^{\infty}(1-Q\,q^{m+1})^{m+1}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\operatorname{Li}_{2}(Q^{\prime}q^{\prime m})\right)\cdot(1-Q^{\prime}q^{\prime m})^{-\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\left(t+m\right)}\,,

to obtain:

\displaystyle Z_{top,np}(\lambda,t)=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{\beta>0}\left(\prod_{m=0}^{\infty}(1-Q^{\beta,n}\,q^{m+1})^{m+1}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\operatorname{Li}_{2}(Q^{\prime\beta}q^{\prime m})\right)\cdot(1-Q^{\prime\beta}q^{\prime m})^{-\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\left(t^{\beta,n}+m\right)}\,\right)^{\Omega_{\beta,n}}\,,

(4.27)

where
t^{\beta,n}:=t^{\beta}+n\check{\lambda}\,,t^{\beta,0}:=t^{\beta}\,,Q^{\beta,n}:=\exp(2\pi\textnormal{i}t^{\beta,n})\,,Q^{\beta}:=Q^{\beta,0}
as before, and the first surprise is that the primed variables are independent of
n
since:

Q^{\prime\beta,n}:=\exp(2\pi\textnormal{i}t^{\beta,n}/\check{\lambda})=\exp(2\pi\textnormal{i}t^{\beta}/\check{\lambda})\cdot\exp(2\pi\textnormal{i}n)=Q^{\prime\beta}\,,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

we define

Z_{np,sg}(\lambda,t):=\prod_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\prod_{\beta>0}\prod_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\operatorname{Li}_{2}(Q^{\prime\beta}q^{\prime m})\right)\cdot(1-Q^{\prime\beta}q^{\prime m})^{-\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\left(t^{\beta,n}+m\right)}\,\right)^{\Omega_{\beta,n}}\,,

(4.28)

and continue to show that it is of the desired form by first writing:

Z_{np,sg}(\lambda,t):=\prod_{\beta>0}\prod_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\beta,n}\,\operatorname{Li}_{2}(Q^{\prime\beta}q^{\prime m})\right)\cdot(1-Q^{\prime\beta}q^{\prime m})^{-\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\beta,n}\left(t^{\beta,n}+m\right)}\,\right)\,,

(4.29)

noting that the argument of the dilogarithm does not depend on
n
, the prefactor becomes
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\beta,n}
. This sum is equal equal to (
5.15
)

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\beta,n}=[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,0}\,.

(4.30)

We now consider the exponent of the second factor:

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\check{\lambda}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\beta,n}\left(t^{\beta,n}+m\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\check{\lambda}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\beta,n}\left(t^{\beta}+n\check{\lambda}+m\right)

(4.31)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\check{\lambda}}[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,0}\,\left(t^{\beta}+m\right)-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\,\Omega_{\beta,n}

(4.32)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\check{\lambda}}[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,0}\,\left(t^{\beta}+m\right)\,.

(4.33)

the summand

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n\,\Omega_{\beta,n}=0
since
\Omega_{\beta,n}=\Omega_{\beta,-n}\,\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}
. These cancellations yield the desired form:

\displaystyle Z_{top,sg}(\lambda,t)=\prod_{\beta>0}\exp\left(-\frac{[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,0}}{2\pi i}\operatorname{Li}_{2}(Q^{\prime\beta}q^{\prime m})\right)\cdot(1-Q^{\prime\beta}q^{\prime m})^{-\frac{[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,0}}{\check{\lambda}}\left(t^{\beta}+m\right)}\,.

(4.34)

∎

We pause to comment on this result, it seems rather remarkable that, due to some non-trivial cancellation only the genus zero GV invariants appear in the non-perturbative corrections to the topological string partition function. We will now proceed to show that these non-perturbative corrections can be cast in a rather simple function which we introduce as a deformation of the genus zero prepotential.

4.4
Deformation of the prepotential

Recall that the non-classical part of the prepotential of a Calabi-Yau threefold has an expression in terms of GV invariants given by:

\tilde{F}^{0}(t)=\sum_{\beta>0}[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,0}\operatorname{Li}_{3}(Q^{\beta})=\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,0}}{k^{3}}Q^{k\beta}\,,\quad Q^{\beta}=\exp(2\pi it^{\beta})\,.

(4.36)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\varepsilon\,\tilde{F}^{0}_{\textrm{def}}(\varepsilon,t)=\tilde{F}^{0}(t)\,.

(4.37)

5
Resurgence

We now proceed to study the analytic structure behind the asymptotic expansion of GW theory by using resurgence. We therefore first re-write the asymptotic expansion of the GW invariants in terms of the sheaf invariants.

5.1
Asymptotic series in terms of sheaf invariants

Proposition 5.1
.

The asymptotic series giving the non-constant part of the generating function of GW invariants

\displaystyle F_{GW}(\lambda,t)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\tilde{F}^{0}(t)+\tilde{F}^{1}(t)+\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\lambda^{2g-2}\tilde{F}^{g}(t)\,,

(5.1)

can be written in terms of the sheaf invariants as:

\tilde{F}^{0}(t)=\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\beta,n}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{Q^{k\beta}}{k^{3}}\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{F}^{1}(t)=\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\beta,n}\frac{1}{2}\left(n\,2\,B_{1}(n)-B_{2}(n)\right)\operatorname{Li}_{1}(Q^{\beta})\,,

(5.2)

and

\tilde{F}^{g}(t)=\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{g}\Omega_{\beta,n}}{2\cdot 2g!}\left((2g-1)(B_{2g}(n)+B_{2g}(-n))-2gn(B_{2g-1}(n)-B_{2g-1}(-n))\right)\operatorname{Li}_{3-2g}(Q^{\beta})\,,

(5.3)

for
g\geq 2
, where
B_{m}(x)
denotes Bernoulli polynomials.

Proof.

To obtain a new expression for the Gromov-Witten potential in terms of the sheaf invariants we start again with the Gopakumar-Vafa form of the topological string free energy:

F_{GV}(\lambda,t)=\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{Q^{k\beta}}{k\left(2\sin\left(\frac{k\lambda}{2}\right)\right)^{2}}\sum_{g\geq 0}[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,g}\,\left(2\sin\left(\frac{k\lambda}{2}\right)\right)^{2g}\,,

(5.4)

using (
4.1
) with
y=\exp(\textnormal{i}k\lambda)
this becomes:

\displaystyle F_{GV}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{Q^{k\beta}}{k\left(2\sin\left(\frac{k\lambda}{2}\right)\right)^{2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\,}\Omega_{\beta,n}\exp(\textnormal{i}nk\lambda)\,,

(5.5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\beta,n}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{Q^{k\beta}}{k}\,\frac{\exp(\textnormal{i}nk\lambda)}{{\left(2\sin\left(\frac{k\lambda}{2}\right)\right)^{2}}}\,,

(5.6)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\Omega_{\beta,n}}{2}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{Q^{k\beta}}{k}\,\frac{\exp(\textnormal{i}nk\lambda+\exp(-\textnormal{i}nk\lambda)}{{\left(2\sin\left(\frac{k\lambda}{2}\right)\right)^{2}}}\,,

(5.7)

where we have used that
\Omega_{\beta,n}=\Omega_{\beta,-n}
and introduced a factor
1/2
to compensate for the doubling in the sum. We want to use this expression to extract the asymptotic expansion as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
and then study the Borel-Laplace summation of the obtained series. This form of the expression makes it clear that it is invariant under
\lambda\rightarrow-\lambda
and hence the asymptotic expansion should only contain even powers of
\lambda
. We therefore want to study the asymptotic expansion of

\frac{\exp(\textnormal{i}nk\lambda+\exp(-\textnormal{i}nk\lambda)}{{\left(2\sin\left(\frac{k\lambda}{2}\right)\right)^{2}}}\,.

We recall the generating function of Bernoulli polynomials:

\frac{we^{wx}}{e^{w}-1}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_{m}(x)}{m!}w^{m}\,,

(5.8)

by taking a logarithmic derivative
w\partial_{w}
of both sides we obtain:

\frac{we^{wx}}{e^{w}-1}+\frac{w^{2}xe^{wx}}{e^{w}-1}-\frac{w^{2}e^{w(x+1)}}{(e^{w}-1)^{2}}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{m}(x)}{(m-1)!}w^{m}\,,

(5.9)

by re-arranging we get:

\displaystyle\frac{e^{w(x+1)}}{(e^{w}-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{w^{2}}(1+wx)-\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{B_{m}(x)}{m(m-2)!}w^{m-2}+x\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{B_{m}(x)}{m!}w^{m-1}\,,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{w^{2}}(1+wx)-\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\left((m-1)B_{m}(x)-xmB_{m-1}(x)\right)w^{m-2}\,,

(5.10)

we thus obtain for

\displaystyle\frac{e^{w(x+1)}+e^{w(-x+1)}}{(e^{w}-1)^{2}}=\frac{2}{w^{2}}+\left(-\frac{1}{6}+x^{2}\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2g!}\left((2g-1)(B_{2g}(x)+B_{2g}(-x))-2gx(B_{2g-1}(x)-B_{2g-1}(-x))\right)w^{2g-2}\,.

(5.11)

We use this for the expression

\frac{\exp(\textnormal{i}nk\lambda+\exp(-\textnormal{i}nk\lambda)}{{\left(2\sin\left(\frac{k\lambda}{2}\right)\right)^{2}}}=-\frac{\exp(\textnormal{i}(n+1)k\lambda)+\exp(\textnormal{i}(-n+1)k\lambda)}{{\left(e^{ik\lambda}-1\right)^{2}}}

with
w=\textnormal{i}k\lambda
and
x=n
, to obtain:

\displaystyle\frac{\exp(\textnormal{i}nk\lambda+\exp(-\textnormal{i}nk\lambda)}{{\left(2\sin\left(\frac{k\lambda}{2}\right)\right)^{2}}}=\frac{2}{k^{2}\lambda^{2}}+\left(\frac{1}{6}-n^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2g!}\left((2g-1)(B_{2g}(n)+B_{2g}(-n))-2gn(B_{2g-1}(n)-B_{2g-1}(-n))\right)(\textnormal{i}k\lambda)^{2g-2}

(5.12)

we thus get

\displaystyle F_{GV}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\Omega_{\beta,n}}{2}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{Q^{k\beta}}{k}\times\Bigg(\frac{2}{k^{2}\lambda^{2}}+\left(\frac{1}{6}-n^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2g!}\left((2g-1)(B_{2g}(n)+B_{2g}(-n))-2gn(B_{2g-1}(n)-B_{2g-1}(-n))\right)(\textnormal{i}k\lambda)^{2g-2}\Bigg)\,\,.

(5.13)

We noted in Sec.
4.1
that the sum over
n
of all
\Omega_{\beta,n}
is given by the limit of
y\rightarrow 1
of

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\,}\Omega_{\beta,n}y^{n}=\sum_{g\geq 0}[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,g}\,(-1)^{g}(y^{1/2}-y^{-1/2})^{2g}\,,

giving

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\beta,n}=[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,0}\,,

(5.14)

similarly by taking two logarithmic derivatives with respect to
y
and then taking the limit we obtain:

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}n^{2}\Omega_{\beta,n}=2[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,1}\,.

(5.15)

Both of these equalities can be used to express

\displaystyle F_{GV}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\tilde{F}^{0}(t)+\tilde{F}^{1}(t)+\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\lambda^{2g-2}\tilde{F}^{g}(t)\,,

(5.16)

where

\tilde{F}^{0}(t)=\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\beta,n}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{Q^{k\beta}}{k^{3}}=\sum_{\beta>0}[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,0}\operatorname{Li}_{3}(Q^{\beta})

and
the constant term in
\lambda
in the expansion (
5.16
) is:

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\beta,n}\frac{1}{2}\left(n\,2\,B_{1}(n)-B_{2}(n)\right)\operatorname{Li}_{1}(Q^{\beta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega_{\beta,n}\frac{1}{12}(-1+6n^{2})\operatorname{Li}_{1}(Q^{\beta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta>0}\left([\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,1}-\frac{1}{12}[\textrm{GV}]_{\beta,0}\right)\operatorname{Li}_{1}(Q^{\beta})

\displaystyle=\tilde{F}^{1}(t)\,,

(5.17)

and

\tilde{F}^{g}(t)=\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(-1)^{(g-1)}\Omega_{\beta,n}}{2\cdot 2g!}\left((2g-1)(B_{2g}(n)+B_{2g}(-n))-2gn(B_{2g-1}(n)-B_{2g-1}(-n))\right)\operatorname{Li}_{3-2g}(Q^{\beta}).

(5.18)

∎

5.2

Theorem 5.2
.

\displaystyle\Phi(\check{\lambda},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(2\pi\textnormal{i})^{2g-2}\Omega_{\beta,n}}{2\cdot 2g!}\left((2g-1)(B_{2g}(n)+B_{2g}(-n))-2gn(B_{2g-1}(n)-B_{2g-1}(-n))\right)

\displaystyle G(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{(2\pi\textnormal{i})^{2g-2}\Omega_{\beta,n}}{2\cdot 2g!}\left((2g-1)(B_{2g}(n)+B_{2g}(-n))-2gn(B_{2g-1}(n)-B_{2g-1}(-n))\right)

\displaystyle G(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\xi}\sum_{g=2}^{\infty}\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\Omega_{\beta,n}}{2\cdot(2g)!}\left((2g-1)(B_{2g}(n)+B_{2g}(-n))-2gn(B_{2g-1}(n)-B_{2g-1}(-n))\right)

(5.23)

we can now use (
5.11

G(\xi,t)=\frac{1}{\xi}\sum_{\beta>0}\sum_{k,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{\Omega_{\beta,n}}{2}\left(\frac{2(t^{\beta}+k)^{2}}{\xi^{2}}+\left(n^{2}-\frac{1}{6}\right)-\frac{e^{\frac{\xi(n+1)}{t^{\beta}+k}}+e^{\frac{\xi(-n+1)}{t^{\beta}+k}}}{\left(e^{\frac{\xi}{t^{\beta}+k}}-1\right)^{2}}\right)\,.

(5.24)

\displaystyle=\frac{[GV]_{\beta,0}}{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}}\left(\mathrm{Li}_{2}\big(e^{\pm 2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t^{\beta}+k)/\check{\lambda}}\big)\pm 2\pi{\mathrm{i}}\frac{(t+k)}{\check{\lambda}}\log\left(1-e^{\pm 2\pi{\mathrm{i}}(t^{\beta}+k)/\check{\lambda}}\right)\right)\;.

(5.27)

Defining

Proof.

6
Discussion and Conclusions

In this work we have put forward an expression for the non-perturbative topological string partition function on any Calabi-Yau threefold, compact or non-compact, in the holomorphic limit which provides the asymptotic expansion of topological strings encoding the enumerative GW as well as GV invariants of the underlying CY threefold. A crucial step in obtaining this expression was identifying the partition function associated to any CY manifold as a product over basic building blocks which correspond to the constant map contributions on the one hand while on the other hand the non-constant maps are captured by resolved conifold partition functions with shifted arguments, raised to the power of sheaf invariants. While the general convergence properties of the full non-perturbative expression requires more knowledge of the sheaf invariants which are obtained from all higher genus GV invariants, it was found that the non-perturbative corrections are governed by only the genus 0 GV invariants, which via mirror symmetry are encoded in rational functions on the complex structure moduli space of the mirror manifold and have thus good convergence properties. It was furthermore shown that the non-perturbative corrections could be entirely captured in terms of a single function which was identified as an
\varepsilon
deformation of the genus 0 prepotential. This function corresponds in the case of the resolved conifold to the refined topological string in the NS limit which was shown to capture the non-perturbative corrections in that case
[
24
,
29
]
.

At the end it is perhaps possible to speculate along some future directions and questions:

•

Global properties

The non-perturbative expression for the topological string partition function in this work is obtained from the holomorphic limit which contains the enumerative geometry information of the underlying CY threefold. On the A-model side, this is the large radius region, on the mirror B-side this is the vicinity of a point of maximal unipotent monodromy. It is known, for instance from the computations using the topological vertex
[
60
]
, that the obtained expression does not give a good global function on the underlying moduli space and it is in general difficult, if not impossible to analytically continue. The route to obtain global expressions for the topological string partition functions is using the holomorphic anomaly equations
[
2
]
together with the recursive polynomial construction of the genus expansion put forward in
[
3
,
4
]
as was recently revisited in connection to non-perturbative topological strings in
[
10
]
. In
[
34
]
, the approach of making a transseries ansatz and using the holomorphic anomaly as studied in
[
8
,
9
]
as well as the results of
[
61
,
33
,
10
]
were used to put forward an expectation for the general form of the Stokes automorphisms acting on the topological string partition function. The expected Stokes factors are of the form obtained from the analytic results of
[
15
,
25
]
as well as their generalizations in this work, obtained without use of the holomorphic anomaly. One may expect that the non-holomorphic topological string has an even richer non-perturbative structure. In fact, in
[
62
]
the holomorphic anomaly equations were used together with the polynomial structure of higher genus topological strings to derive an Airy differential equation in the topological string coupling governing a universal piece of the higher genus free energies for all CY families, in addition to the perturbative piece captured by one solution of the Airy differential equation, the second solution suggests corrections with
e^{-1/\lambda^{2}}
behaviour, this was explained by the effects of NS-5 branes in
[
63
]
. These non-perturbative effects seem to be completely missing in the results of the current paper which is likely an artefact of starting with the asymptotic series of topological strings in the limit leading to the enumerative geometry content.

•

Non-perturbative absence of the gap?

Although the non-perturbative expression of this work may not be the full non-holomorphic one, one may however speculate at this stage, whether the
gap condition
used in
[
6
]
and which refers to a leading singularity in the topological string free energies

t\rightarrow 0

[
29

that the analytic continuation in
t

\lambda
gives a leading singular behavior of the expected form and that moreover the constant term of this analytic continuation contained the information of the constant map contribution. A surprise of the non-perturbatively complete Borel sums put forward in
[
25

is that their limit
t\rightarrow 0
is well behaved and it moreover encodes the constant map contributions. Furthermore, the combination of the non-constant and constant map contributions leads to a partition function which is canonically normalized as
t\rightarrow 0

\lambda

Another speculation is whether the topological string partition functions are normalized in a canonical way as well or whether this was an accident of the resolved conifold, the general case would require non-trivial relations between
\chi(X)
the Euler character of the CY and sums of its GV invariants.

•

Quantum Picard-Fuchs equations?
The difference equation of the asymptotic expansion of the resolved conifold free energy of

[

•

The Nekrasov-Shatshvili limit?
The deformed prepotential introduced in this work, which is capturing the non-perturbative corrections to the topological string coincides with the Nekrasov-Shatashvili limit of the refined topological string in the case of the resolved conifold. It is not entirely clear whether such a relation can be expected more generally. What is clear however, is that the piece coming from the deformed prepotential in the non-perturbative expression is responsible for the cancellation of poles of the topological string free energy in

Multiple sine functions

We introduce several special functions which are used throughout the paper following the notation of
[
67
]
. Suppose
\omega_{1}\,,\dots,\omega_{r}\in\mathbb{C}
all lie on the same side of some straight line through the origin, then the multiple zeta function is defined by the series

\zeta_{r}(s,z|\underline{\omega})=\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{r}=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n_{1}\omega_{1}+\dots+n_{r}\omega_{r}+z)^{s}}\,,

(A.1)

for
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
\textrm{Re}(s)>r
, where the exponential is rendered one-valued. This series is holomorphic in the domain
\{\textrm{Re}(s)>r\}
and is analytically continued to
s\in\mathbb{C}
.

Since it is holomorphic at
s=0
we can define the multiple gamma function by

\Gamma_{r}(z|\underline{\omega})=\exp\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\zeta_{r}(s,z|\underline{\omega})|_{s=0}\right),

(A.2)

where

|\underline{\omega}|=\omega_{1}+\dots+\omega_{r}\,,

and the multiple sine function is given by

\sin_{r}(z|\underline{\omega})=\Gamma_{r}(z|\underline{\omega})^{-1}\,\Gamma_{r}(|\underline{\omega}|-z|\underline{\omega})^{(-1)^{r}}\,.

(A.3)

This satisfies the functional equations:

\displaystyle\sin_{r}(z+\omega_{j}|\underline{\omega})=\sin_{r-1}(z|\underline{\omega}^{-}(j))^{-1}\sin_{r}(z|\underline{\omega})\,,

(A.4)

\displaystyle\sin_{r}(z|\underline{\omega})\,\sin_{r}(|\underline{\omega}|-z|\underline{\omega})^{(-1)^{r}}=1\,,

(A.5)

where

\underline{\omega}^{-}(j)=(\omega_{1},\dots,\overset{\vee}{\omega_{j}},\dots,\omega_{r})\,,

and
\overset{\vee}{\omega_{j}}
means omitting
\omega_{j}
.

For
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
\underline{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r})
,
\omega_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
we define the multiple Bernoulli polynomials
B_{r,n}(z,\underline{\omega})
in terms of the generating function

\mathcal{B}_{r}(z,x|\,\omega_{1},\,\dots,\omega_{r})=\frac{x^{r}e^{zx}}{\prod_{j=1}^{r}(e^{\omega_{j}x}-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{r,n}(z|\underline{\omega})\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\,.

(A.6)

The multiple sine functions are defined using the Barnes multiple Gamma functions
[
68
]
. For a variable
z\in\mathbb{C}
and parameters
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{r}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
these are defined by

\sin_{r}(z\,|\,\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r}):=\Gamma_{r}(z\,|\,\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r})^{-1}\cdot\Gamma_{r}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\omega_{i}-z\,|\,\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r}\right)^{(-1)^{r}}\,.

(A.7)

For further definitions see e.g.
[
32
,
69
]
and references therein.

Another special function is (related to) Fadeev’s quantum dilogarithm

\mathcal{S}_{2}(z\,|\,\omega_{1},\omega_{2}):=\exp\left(-\frac{\pi{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\cdot B_{2,2}(z|\,\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\right)\cdot\sin_{2}(z\,|\,\omega_{1},\omega_{2}),

(A.8)

which we have introduced in terms of the double sine function as in
[
32
]
.

The final special function is defined by

\mathcal{S}_{3}(z\,|\,\omega_{1},\omega_{2}):=\exp\left(\frac{\pi{\mathrm{i}}}{6}\cdot B_{3,3}(z+\omega_{1}\,|\,\omega_{1},\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\right)\cdot\sin_{3}(z+\omega_{1}\,|\,\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}),

(A.9)

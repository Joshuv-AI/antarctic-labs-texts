Infinite-dimensional Thurston theory and transcendental dynamics IV: dependence on parameters and escape on (pre-)periodic rays
Konstantin Bogdanov

Abstract
. We consider transcendental entire functions that are compositions of a polynomial and the exponential for which all singular values escape on disjoint rays. Based on their classification in [ B3 ] we investigate their dependence on parameters, that is, on the potentials and external addresses of singular values. Using continuity argument we generalize the classification in [ B3 ] to the case when dynamic rays containing singular values are (pre-)periodic.



This is the forth out of four articles publishing the results of the author’s doctoral thesis
[
B0
]
. The machinery needed for the Thurston iteration on an infinite-dimensional Teichmüller space is developed in
[
B1
]
. In
[
B2
,
B3
]
we classify transcendental entire functions that are compositions of a polynomial and the exponential for which their singular values escape on disjoint dynamic rays.
[
B2
]
covers the general case of such escape when the distance between points on different singular orbits is bounded below, whereas in
[
B3
]
one removes this restriction. In the present article we investigate continuity of such families of functions with respect to potentials and external addresses and use continuity argument to extend our classification to the case of escape on the (pre-)periodic rays.

1.
Introduction

In transcendental dynamics, as in polynomial dynamics, one is interested
in understanding the
escaping set
. For a transcendental entire function
f
, its
escaping set
is defined as

I(f)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:f^{n}(z)\to\infty\text{ as }n\to\infty\}.

Since the Julia set coincides with the boundary of
I(f)
, both Julia and Fatou sets can be reconstructed from
I(f)
, which is thus one of the most fundamental invariant sets for the dynamics of entire functions. It is shown in
[
RRRS
]
for transcendental entire functions of bounded type and finite order that the escaping set of every function in the family is organized as the union
dynamic rays
and their endpoints. About the escaping points that belong to a dynamic ray one says that they
escape on rays
. This is the standard mode of escape for many important families of transcendental entire functions.

In
[
B2
,
B3
]
we developed a classification of entire functions of the form
p\circ\exp
where
p
is a monic polynomial such that all their singular points escape on disjoint rays (i.e. every dynamic ray contains no more than one post-singular point). Denote

\mathcal{N}_{d}:=\{p\circ\exp:p\text{ is a monic polynomial of degree }d\}.

As for the exponential family
[
SZ
]
, for functions in
\mathcal{N}_{d}
the points escaping on rays can be described by their potential (or “speed of escape”) and external address (or “combinatorics of escape”, i.e. the sequence of dynamic rays containing the escaping orbit). So the classification was accomplished in terms of potentials and external addresses. More precisely, the classification was obtained separately within each
parameter space
(corresponding to functions in
\mathcal{N}_{d}
). The parameter space of an entire function
g
is the set of entire functions
\varphi\circ f\circ\psi
where
\varphi,\psi
are quasiconformal self-homeomorphisms of
\mathbb{C}
. For the exponential family
\mathcal{N}_{1}
this definition of the parameter space coincides with the parametrization of
e^{z}+\kappa
(up to affine conjugation). Earlier classification results for
\mathcal{N}_{1}
were obtained in
[
FRS
,
FS
,
F
]
.

In this article we prove two results. First, we extend the classification
[
B3
, Theorem 1.1]
of functions in
\mathcal{N}_{d}
to the case of (pre-)periodic external addresses.

Theorem 1.1

(Classification Theorem)
.

Let
f_{0}\in\mathcal{N}_{d}
be a transcendental entire function with singular values
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
. Let also
\{\underline{s}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be exponentially bounded external addresses that are non-overlapping, and
\{T_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be real numbers such that
T_{i}>t_{\underline{s}_{i}}
. Then in the parameter space of
f_{0}
there exists a unique entire function
g=\varphi\circ f_{0}\circ\psi
such that each of its singular values
\varphi(v_{i})
escapes on rays, has potential
T_{i}
and external address
\underline{s}_{i}
.

Conversely, every function in the parameter space of
f_{0}
such that its singular values escape on rays without orbit overlaps is one of these.

The condition that external addresses are non-overlapping means that the dynamic rays corresponding to different singular values of
g
do not have a common image under
f
, i.e. there is no overlap of orbits of these rays (for details see Subsection
2.1
).

Remark 1.2
.

The restriction to the non-overlapping external addresses is used largely for notational convenience. The machinery described in
[
B1
,
B2
,
B3
]
could also be applied in full generality though it would lead to even more complicated notation and consideration of quite a few special cases which are not interesting from the conceptual point of view.

The second result is about existence of higher dimensional analogues of parameter rays. It is a special case of Theorem
3.11
which declares a more general continuous dependence on parameters that is presented in Theorem
1.3
(but requires somewhat more preliminaries for stating it precisely).

Theorem 1.3

(Multidimensional parameter rays)
.

Let
f_{0}\in\mathcal{N}_{d}
be a transcendental entire function with singular values
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
. Let also
\{\underline{s}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be exponentially bounded external addresses that are non-overlapping. For every
m
-tuple
T=(T_{1},...,T_{m})
of real numbers such that
T_{i}>t_{\underline{s}_{i}}
denote by
g_{T}\in\mathcal{N}_{d}
the function
\varphi_{T}\circ f_{0}\circ\psi_{T}
from Theorem
1.1
, that is, such that each of its singular values
\varphi_{T}(v_{i})
escapes on rays, has potential
T_{i}
and external address
\underline{s}_{i}
. Then
g_{T}
depends continuously on
T
(in the sense of coefficients’ continuity).

The proof of both theorems is based on the fact that the nearby parameters make possible a “similar” construction of the invariant compact subset
[
B3
, Theorem 4.1]
for both parameters, even though they are contained in different Teichmüller spaces. This is made precise in Proposition
3.8
.

2.
Prerequisites

In this section we assemble definitions and results mostly from
[
B1
,
B2
,
B3
]
that are needed in our constructions.

2.1.
Escaping set of functions in
\mathcal{N}_{d}

From
[
RRRS
]
we know that for every function
f\in\mathcal{N}_{d}
its escaping set is modeled on dynamic rays. In particular, that every escaping point is mapped on a dynamic ray after finitely many iterations. A related notion is a ray tail.

Definition 2.1

(Ray tails)
.

Let
f
be a transcendental entire function. A
ray tail
of
f
is a continuous curve
\gamma:[0,\infty)\to I(f)
such that for every
n\geq 0
the restriction
f^{n}|_{\gamma}
is injective with
\lim_{t\to\infty}f^{n}(\gamma(t))=\infty
, and furthermore
f^{n}(\gamma(t))\to\infty
uniformly in
t
as
n\to\infty
.

Definition 2.2

(Dynamic rays, escape on rays)
.

A
dynamic ray
of a transcendental entire function
f
is a maximal injective curve
\gamma:(0,\infty)\to I(f)
such that
\gamma|_{[t,\infty)}
is a ray tail for every
t>0
.

If
z\in I(f)
belongs to a dynamic ray, we say that
z

escapes on rays
.

We showed in
[
B2
]
that for functions in
\mathcal{N}_{d}
its dynamic rays (at least their parts near
\infty
) are characterized by an exponentially bounded external address and parametrized by potential.

Fix an integer
d>0
, and denote by
F:\mathbb{R^{+}}\to\mathbb{R^{+}}
the function

F(t):=\exp(dt)-1.

The
external address

\underline{s}=(s_{0}s_{1}s_{2}...)
is an infinite sequence of integers
s_{0},s_{1},s_{2},...
On the set of all external addresses we can consider the standard shift-operator
\sigma:(s_{0}s_{1}s_{2}...)\mapsto(s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}...)
. Two external addresses
\underline{s}_{1}
and
\underline{s}_{2}
are called
overlapping
if there are integers
k,l\geq 0
so that
\sigma^{k}\underline{s}_{1}=\sigma^{l}\underline{s}_{2}
.

Further, an external address
\underline{s}=(s_{0}s_{1}s_{2}...)
is called
exponentially bounded
if there exists
t>0
such that
s_{n}/{F^{n}(t)}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. The infimum of such
t
is denoted by
t_{\underline{s}}
.

Exponentially bounded external addresses correspond to dynamic rays (more precisely, to their part near
\infty
) of functions in
\mathcal{N}_{d}
. Furthermore, from the formula
2.1
, dynamic rays look near
\infty
almost like straight horizontal lines with the imaginary part that can be recovered from their external address.

Theorem 2.3

(Escape on rays in
\mathcal{N}_{d}

[
B2
]
)
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{N}_{d}
. Then for every exponentially bounded external address there exists a dynamic ray realizing it.

If
\mathcal{R}_{\underline{s}}
is dynamic ray having an exponentially bounded external address
\underline{s}=(s_{0}s_{1}s_{2}...)
, and no strict forward iterate of
\mathcal{R}_{\underline{s}}
contains a singular value of
f
, then
\mathcal{R}_{\underline{s}}
is the unique dynamic ray having external address
\underline{s}
, and it can be parametrized by
t\in(t_{\underline{s}},\infty)
so that

\mathcal{R}_{\underline{s}}(t)=t+\frac{2\pi is_{0}}{d}+O(e^{-t/2}),

(2.1)

and
f^{n}\circ\mathcal{R}_{\underline{s}}=\mathcal{R}_{\sigma^{n}\underline{s}}\circ F^{n}
. Asymptotic bounds
O(.)
for
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma^{n}\underline{s}}(t)
are uniform in
n
on every ray tail contained in
\mathcal{R}_{\underline{s}}
.

If none of the singular values of
f
escapes, then
I(f)
is the disjoint union of dynamic rays and their escaping endpoints.

The parametrization
2.1
allows us to define the potential of a point on a dynamic ray: if
z\in\mathcal{R}_{\underline{s}}
corresponds to the parameter
t
of formula
2.1
, we say that
t
is the
potential
of
z
. From Theorem
2.3
follows immediately that the potential is defined in a canonical way, and if the potential of
z
is equal to
t
, then the potential of
f(z)
is equal to
F(t)
. Further, different points on the same ray have different potentials.

2.2.
Setup of Thurston iteration

In this subsection we give an overview a few important definitions from
[
B2
]
and sketch the setup of the Thurston’s iteration.

One starts with the construction of a quasiregular function
f
modeling escaping behavior of singular values. Let
f_{0}\in\mathcal{N}_{d}
, and
v_{1},...,v_{m}
be the finite singular values of
f_{0}
. Further, let
\mathcal{O}_{1}=\{a_{1j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty},...,\mathcal{O}_{m}=\{a_{mj}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
be some orbits of
f_{0}
escaping on disjoint rays
\mathcal{R}_{ij}
, and let
R_{ij}
be the part of the ray
\mathcal{R}_{ij}
from
a_{ij}
to
\infty
(containing also
a_{ij}
).

Now, we introduce the
capture
. Choose some bounded Jordan domain
U\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=\overline{1,m}\\
j=\overline{1,\infty}\end{subarray}}R_{ij}
containing all singular values of
f_{0}
and the first point
a_{i0}
on each escaping orbit
\mathcal{O}_{i}
. Define an isotopy
c_{u}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
through quasiconformal maps, such that
c_{0}=\operatorname{id}
,
c_{u}=\operatorname{id}
on
\mathbb{C}\setminus U
, and for each
i=\overline{1,m}
we have
c_{1}(v_{i})=a_{i0}
. Denote
c=c_{1}
. Thus,
c
is a quasiconformal homeomorphism mapping singular values to the first points on the orbits
\mathcal{O}_{i}
.

Define a function
f:=c\circ f_{0}
. It is a quasiregular function whose singular orbits coincide with
\{\mathcal{O}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
. Its post-singular set of
f
is defined in a standard way:

P_{f}:=\cup_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{O}_{i}.

We also call
P_{f}
the set of
marked points
.

The Thurston’s
\sigma
-map acts on the Teichmüller space of
\mathbb{C}\setminus P_{f}
.

Definition 2.4

(Teichmüller space of
\mathbb{C}\setminus P_{f}
)
.

The
Teichmüller space

\mathcal{T}_{f}
of the Riemann surface
\mathbb{C}\setminus P_{f}
is the set of quasiconformal homeomorphisms of
\mathbb{C}\setminus P_{f}
modulo post-composition with an affine map and isotopy relative
P_{f}
.

The map

\sigma:[\varphi]\in\mathcal{T}_{f}\mapsto[\tilde{\varphi}]\in\mathcal{T}_{f}

is defined in the standard way via Thurston’s diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C},P_{f}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C},\tilde{\varphi}(P_{f})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C},P_{f}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C},\varphi(P_{f})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\varphi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f=c\circ f_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

More precisely, for every quasiconformal map
\varphi:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
there exists another quasiconformal map
\tilde{\varphi}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
g=\varphi\circ f\circ\tilde{\varphi}^{-1}
is entire function. We define
\sigma[\varphi]:=[\tilde{\varphi}]
. For more details and the proof that this setup is well defined see
[
B1
]
.

2.3.
\operatorname{Id}
-type maps

Let
f=c\circ f_{0}
be the quasiregular function defined in Section
2.2
where
f_{0}=p\circ\exp
. The union of post-singular ray tails
S_{0}=\cup_{i,j}R_{ij}
is called the
standard spider
of
f
.

Definition 2.5

(
\operatorname{Id}
-type maps
[
B2
]
)
.

A quasiconformal map
\varphi:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
is of
\operatorname{id}
-type if there is an isotopy
\varphi_{u}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C},\ u\in[0,1]
such that
{\varphi_{1}=\varphi},\ \varphi_{0}=\operatorname{id}
and
\left|\varphi_{u}(z)-z\right|\to 0
uniformly in
u
as
S_{0}\ni z\to\infty
.

We also say that
[\varphi]\in\mathcal{T}_{f}
is of
\operatorname{id}
-type if
[\varphi]
contains an
\operatorname{id}
-type map. Further,
\psi_{u},u\in[0,1]
is an
isotopy of
\operatorname{id}
-type maps
if
\psi_{u}
is an isotopy through maps of
\operatorname{id}
-type such that
\left|\psi_{u}(z)-z\right|\to 0
uniformly in
u
as
S_{0}\ni z\to\infty
.

The following theorem says that the
\sigma
-map is invariant on the subset of
\operatorname{id}
-type points in
\mathcal{T}_{f}
.

Theorem 2.6

(Invariance of
\operatorname{id}
-type points
[
B2
]
)
.

If
[\varphi]
is of
\operatorname{id}
-type, then
\sigma[\varphi]
is of
\operatorname{id}
-type as well.

More precisely, if
\varphi
is of
\operatorname{id}
-type, then there is a unique
\operatorname{id}
-type map
\hat{\varphi}
such that
\varphi\circ f\circ\hat{\varphi}^{-1}
is entire.

Moreover, if
\varphi_{u}
is an isotopy of
\operatorname{id}
-type maps, then the functions
\varphi_{u}\circ f\circ\hat{\varphi}_{u}^{-1}
have the form
p_{u}\circ\exp
where
p_{u}
is a monic polynomial with coefficients depending continuously on
u
.

Remark 2.7
.

We keep using the hat-notation from Theorem
2.6
:
\hat{\varphi}
denotes the unique
\operatorname{id}
-type map so that
\varphi\circ f\circ\hat{\varphi}^{-1}
is entire.

2.4.
Spiders

We also make use of infinite-legged spiders which provide a more suitable way of to describe points of
[\mathcal{T}_{f}]
. The image of a ray tail
R_{ij}
under a spider map is called a
leg
.

Definition 2.8

(Spider)
.

An image
S_{\varphi}
of the standard spider
S_{0}
under an
\operatorname{id}
-type map
\varphi
is called a
spider
.

For every leg
\varphi(R_{ij})
of a spider
S_{\varphi}
we associate its
leg homotopy word

W_{ij}^{\varphi}
which is an element of certain free group. The leg homotopy word help to measure the wiggling of the spider leg in certain homotopy type, for details see
[
B2
, Definition 4.9]
. As
W_{ij}^{\varphi}
is an element of a free group, by
\left|W_{ij}^{\varphi}\right|
we denote the length of the word representing this element.

Definition 2.9

(Equivalence of spiders
[
B1
]
)
.

We say that two spiders
S_{\varphi}
and
S_{\psi}
are
equivalent
if for all pairs
i,j
we have
\varphi(a_{ij})=\psi(a_{ij})
and legs with the same index are homotopic, i.e.
[\varphi(R_{ij})]=[\psi(R_{ij})]
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\varphi(P_{f})
.

For any integer
k\geq 0
, we denote by a finite spider
S_{\varphi}^{k}
the image under
\varphi
of the first
k+1
legs on each singular orbit, i.e.
\varphi(R_{ij}),j\leq k
. One can also generalize equivalence to finite spider: two spiders
S_{\varphi}^{k}
and
S_{\psi}^{k}
are
equivalent
if for all pairs
i,j
with
j\leq K
, we have
\varphi(a_{ij})=\psi(a_{ij})
and legs with the same index are homotopic, i.e.
[\varphi(R_{ij})]=[\psi(R_{ij})]
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{\varphi(a_{ij}):j\leq K\}
.

2.5.
Clusters

We consider the quasiconformal function
f=c\circ f_{0}
with
m
singular orbits
\mathcal{O}_{i}=\{a_{ij}\}
constructed in Subsection
2.2
with its associated
\sigma
-map. We denote by
t_{ij}
the potential of
a_{ij}
and by
s_{ij}
the first number in the external address of
a_{ij}
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{t_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be the set of all potentials of points in
P_{f}
ordered so that
t_{i}<t_{i+1}
. Further, we define a set

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}:=\{\rho_{i}:\rho_{i}=\frac{t_{i}+t_{i+1}}{2}\}.

For all
\rho\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
we use the following notation. Denote
D_{\rho}:=\mathbb{D}_{\rho}(0)
, and for
i\in\{1,...,m\}
let
N_{i}=N_{i}(\rho)
be the maximal
j
-index of the points
a_{ij}
contained in
D_{\rho}
. For
\rho>t^{\prime}
the disk
D_{\rho}
contains first
N_{i}+1
points
\{a_{i0},...,a_{iN_{i}}\}
of
\mathcal{O}_{i}
, whereas the other points of
\mathcal{O}_{i}
are in
\mathbb{C}\setminus{D}_{\rho}
.

Definition 2.10

(Cluster
[
B2
]
)
.

We say that
a_{ij}
and
a_{kl}
are in the same
cluster
Cl(t,s)
if they have the same potential
t=t_{ij}=t_{kl}
and the same first entry
s=s_{ij}=s_{kl}
in the external address.

A cluster is called non-trivial if it contains more than on point of
P_{f}
.

The set
P_{f}
is a disjoint union of clusters and each cluster contains at most
m
points. As can be seen from the asymptotic formula
2.1
, for points
a_{ij}
and
a_{kl}
with big potentials being in the same cluster implies that distance between them is very small, whereas the distance between any pair of clusters is bounded from below.

Denote by
\operatorname{SV}(f)
the set of finite singular values of
f
, i.e. the image of (finite) singular values of
f_{0}
under the capture
c
. Since we consider only the parameter space of
f_{0}
, without loss of generality we might assume that
\operatorname{SV}(f_{0})\cap I(f_{0})=\emptyset
. Then Theorem
2.3
claims that all exponentially bounded external addresses and potentials are realized.

We define two types of sets that will help us to describe the behavior of points inside of a cluster under the Thurston’s iteration.

Definition 2.11

(
A_{x,n}

[
B3
]
)
.

For
x>0
and integer
n\geq 0
let
A_{x,n}
be the set of all complex numbers
\alpha
such that

(1)

\left|\log\left|\alpha\right|\right|<e^{-x/3}+...+e^{-F^{n}(x)/3}
,

(2)

\left|\arg\alpha\right|<e^{-x/3}+...+e^{-F^{n}(x)/3}
.

Visually
A_{x,n}
is an annular sector containing
1
. The next lemma is the direct corollary of Definition
2.11
.

Lemma 2.12

(Pseudo-Multiplicativity of
A_{x,n}

[
B3
]
)
.

If
\alpha_{i}\in A_{F^{i-1}(x),0}
for
i\in\{1,...,k\}
, then
\alpha_{1}\cdot...\cdot\alpha_{k}\in A_{x,k-1}
.

The next notion helps to characterize the difference between marked point that have the same potential but are in different clusters.

Definition 2.13

(
D_{ij}^{kl}

[
B3
]
)
.

For
a_{ij}
and
a_{kl}
having the same potential but belonging to different clusters define

D_{ij}^{kl}:=\left\{\frac{d(w-z)}{2\pi i(s_{kl}-s_{ij})}:w\in\mathbb{D}_{1/l}(a_{kl}),z\in\mathbb{D}_{1/j}(a_{ij})\right\}.

Next lemma provides an estimate on how the Thurston iteration changes the relative position of marked points inside of the clusters with high potentials.

Proposition 2.14

(Negligible rotation within clusters
[
B3
]
)
.

For all
\rho\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
big enough holds the following statement.

If
a_{ij},a_{kl}\notin{D}_{\rho}
belong to the same cluster
Cl(t,s)
, and
\varphi_{u},u\in[0,1]
is an isotopy of
\operatorname{id}
-type maps such that
\varphi_{0}=c^{-1}
and for all
u\in[0,1]
:

(1)

\varphi_{u}(\operatorname{SV}(f))\subset D_{\rho}
,

(2)

\left|\varphi_{u}(a_{i(j+1)})-a_{i(j+1)}\right|<1/(j+1)
,

(3)

\left|\varphi_{u}(a_{k(l+1)})-a_{k(l+1)}\right|<1/(l+1)
,

then for every
u\in[0,1]
holds

\hat{\varphi}_{u}(a_{kl})-\hat{\varphi}_{u}(a_{ij})=\frac{\varphi_{u}(a_{k(l+1)})-\varphi_{u}(a_{i(j+1)})}{F^{\prime}(t)}\alpha_{u},

where
\alpha_{u}\in A_{t,0}
.

Some more notation is needed.

Definition 2.15

(
H,L
)
.

For every pair
a_{ij},a_{kl}
belonging to the same cluster define

•

H=H(a_{ij},a_{kl})
to be the smallest positive integer such that
a_{i(j+H)}
and
a_{k(l+H)}
belong to different clusters,

•

L=L(a_{ij},a_{kl})
to be the smallest positive integer such that
a_{i(j-L)}
and
a_{k(l-L)}
are defined and belong to different clusters, if there is no such integer we define
L:=\infty
.

2.6.
Compact invariant subset

Of special importance for us is the construction of the compact invariant subset
\mathcal{C}_{f}(\rho)
. We agree that
\mathcal{O}_{1}=\{a_{1j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
is the orbit of the asymptotic value of
f
.

Theorem 2.16

(Invariant subset
[
B3
]
)
.

Let
f=c\circ\exp
be the quasiregular function defined in Subsection
2.2
. Further, let
\rho\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{C}_{f}(\rho)\subset\mathcal{T}_{f}
be the
closure
of the set of points in the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{f}
represented by
\operatorname{id}
-type maps
\varphi
for which there exists an isotopy
\varphi_{u},u\in[0,1]
of
\operatorname{id}
-type maps such that
\varphi_{0}=\operatorname{id}
,
\varphi_{1}=\varphi
, and the following conditions are simultaneously satisfied.

(1)

(Marked points stay inside of
D_{\rho}
) If
j\leq N_{i}
,

\varphi_{u}(a_{ij})\in D_{\rho}.

(2)

(Precise asymptotics outside of
D_{\rho}
) If
j>N_{i}
, then

\left|\varphi_{u}(a_{ij})-a_{ij}\right|<1/j.

(3)

(Separation inside of
D_{\rho}
) If
j\leq N_{i},l\leq N_{k},ij\neq kl
, and
n=\min\{N_{i}+1-j,N_{k}+1-l\}
, then

\left|\varphi_{u}(a_{kl})-\varphi_{u}(a_{ij})\right|>\frac{\beta\left(a_{i(j+n)},a_{k(l+n)}\right)}{(M_{\rho})^{n}}.

(4)

(Bounded homotopy) If
j\leq N_{i}
, then

\left|W_{ij}^{\varphi_{u}}\right|<A^{N_{i}+1-j}\left(\frac{(N_{i}+1)!}{j!}\right)^{4}C

where
A
and
C
are constants.

(5)

(Rigidity in clusters) For all
a_{ij},a_{kl}
with
j>N_{i},l>N_{k}
belonging to the same cluster, holds

\varphi_{u}(a_{kl})-\varphi_{u}(a_{ij})=\frac{2\pi i(s_{k(l+H)}-s_{i(j+H)})}{d(F^{\circ H}(t))^{\prime}}\nu_{u}\delta_{u}

where
t
is the potential of
a_{ij}
and
a_{kl}
,
H=H(a_{ij},a_{kl})
,
\nu_{u}=\nu_{u}(\varphi_{u},i,j,k,l)\in A_{t,H-1}
and
\delta_{u}=\delta_{u}(\varphi_{u},i,j,k,l)\in D_{i(j+H)}^{k(l+H)}
.

(6)

(Clusters inside of
D_{\rho}
) If
a_{k(N_{k}+1)}
and
a_{1(N_{1}+1)}
belong to the same cluster,
L=L(a_{k(N_{k}+1)},a_{1(N_{1}+1)})
, and
N=\max_{i\leq m}N_{i}
, then:

(a)

if
0\leq n<L
, then

\left|\varphi_{u}(a_{k(N_{k}+1-n)})-\varphi_{u}(a_{1(N_{1}+1-n)})\right|<4\beta\left(a_{k(N_{k}+1)},a_{1{(N_{1}+1)}}\right)M_{\rho}^{2dNn},

(b)

if
n\geq L
, then

\left|\varphi_{u}(a_{k(N_{k}+1-n)})-\varphi_{u}(a_{1(N_{1}+1-n)})\right|>\left(\frac{1}{M_{\rho}}\right)^{2d^{4}N+n-L}.

Then if
\rho\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is big enough, the subset
\mathcal{C}_{f}(\rho)
is well-defined, invariant under the
\sigma
-map and contains
[\operatorname{id}]
.

We are mostly interested in conditions (1),(2) and (5), so we omit giving precise definitions of
\beta,A,C
and
M_{\rho}
— they are not needed for the purposes of this article. For details and their definitions we send the reader to
[
B3
]
.

Note that conditions (1)-(2) say that the maps
\varphi
are “uniformly of
\operatorname{id}
-type”, that is, the marked points outside of a disk
D_{\rho}
have precise asymptotics, while inside of
D_{\rho}
we allow some more freedom. A the same time, condition (5) helps us to control the behavior of marked points within clusters. It prevents points within clusters to approach and rotate around each other.

3.
Continuity in parameters and (pre-)periodicity

Let
\mathcal{S}=\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}
be the topological space consisting of all external addresses equipped with the product topology, and
\mathcal{S}_{0}
be its subspace consisting of all exponentially bounded external addresses.

Definition 3.1

(Wedge

)
.

For an exponentially bounded external address
\underline{s}
, potential

and constant

around

Clearly, a wedge around an exponentially bounded external address contains only exponentially bounded external addresses. First, we prove two preliminary lemmas about dependence of dynamic rays on their external addresses.

Lemma 3.2

(Continuity of rays w.r.t. external address)
.

Let

be such that
\operatorname{SV}(f)\notin I(f)
, and
\tau>t_{\underline{s}}
be a potential for the external address
\underline{s}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
. If
\{\underline{s}^{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of external addresses contained in a wedge

around
\underline{s}
and converging to
\underline{s}
, then

(1)

\left|\mathcal{R}_{\underline{s}^{n}}(t)-\mathcal{R}_{\underline{s}}(t)\right|\to 0
as

uniformly on
[\tau,\infty)
,

(2)

\left|\operatorname{Re\,}\mathcal{R}_{\sigma^{k}\underline{s}^{n}}(t)-t\right|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
uniformly in
t
and

.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that
\operatorname{Re\,}\mathcal{R}_{\sigma^{k}\underline{s}^{n}}(t)
is defined only on

so we consider the ray whenever it is defined (depending on

).

Proof.

For the proof one has to notice that the

-estimates for
\delta_{k}
in the proof of
[

, Theorem 2.12]
(i.e. of Theorem
2.3
) do not change if we replace
\underline{s}
by
\underline{s}^{\prime}
in a wedge, i.e. the

bigger than some fixed constant and all

, the ray tail
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma^{i}\underline{s}^{n}}|_{[\tau,\infty)}
is fully contained in its tract (see
[
B2
, Lemma 2.2]
for the definition of tracts) and also holds the asymptotic formula
2.1
for each
\underline{s}^{n}
with uniform

-estimates. Since
f^{-1}
is uniformly strictly contracting on tracts, the claim follows.
∎

Lemma 3.4

(Continuity of rays w.r.t. external address and function)
.

Let
h\in\mathcal{N}_{f_{0}}
and
\tau>t_{\underline{s}}
be a potential for the external address
\underline{s}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
. If

and converging to
\underline{s}
, and

\left|\mathcal{R}_{\sigma^{N}\underline{s}^{n}}^{h_{n}}(t)-\mathcal{R}^{h}_{\sigma^{N}\underline{s}}(t)\right|\to 0

as

uniformly on

for all

big enough, where
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma^{N}\underline{s}^{n}}^{h_{n}}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma^{N}\underline{s}^{n}}^{h}
are parametrizations of dynamic rays of functions

and
h
, respectively.

Proof.

The proof is almost the same as of Lemma
3.2
. In
[
B2
, Theorem 2.12]
we constructed the ray parametrizations as limits of functions

(see
[

for the definition of

). In particular, one sees that for all

∎

Now, consider a function
f_{0}\in\mathcal{N}_{d}
having
m
singular values. Let
\mathcal{N}_{f_{0}}
be the intersection of
\mathcal{N}_{d}
with the parameter space of

. Further, for the topological space

\underbrace{\mathcal{S}\times...\times\mathcal{S}}_{m\text{ times}}\times\underbrace{(0,\infty)\times...\times(0,\infty)}_{m\text{ times}}

such that
\underline{s}_{1},...,\underline{s}_{m}

for

. Let also

be the subspace of

Definition 3.5

(Wedge at

)
.

For a point

, a

at

is a set of the form

Define the function

so that for

is the unique function from

such that its singular values escape on rays as prescribed by
(\underline{s}_{1},...,\underline{s}_{m},t_{1},...,t_{m})
. We study the continuity properties of this function and prove that it extends to
\mathcal{E}
.

In this paragraph we introduce some notation. Let
\alpha=(\underline{s}_{1},...,\underline{s}_{m},t_{1},...,t_{m})\in\mathcal{E}^{0}
. Then there is a well-defined Thurston’s
\sigma_{f}
-map for a quasiregular map
f=c\circ f_{0}
with a capture
c
. Further, consider a sequence
(\alpha_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}=(\underline{s}_{1}^{n},...,\underline{s}_{m}^{n},t_{1}^{n},...,t_{m}^{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{E}^{0}
. Again, for each element of the sequence, there is the well-defined Thurston’s
\sigma_{f_{n}}
-map for quasiregular maps

with captures
(c_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
. Note that generally speaking all

act on different Teichmüller spaces. By

and

we denote
\operatorname{id}
-type maps for the Thurston’s iteration associated to

and

, respectively. Also, let

be the corresponding points on the singular orbit of

, while

be the identity map corresponding to

(this is needed to avoid confusion between different Teichmüller spaces).

Generally speaking, one can choose the capture in many different ways (even up to homotopy) and the Thurston iteration depends on this choice (though provides the same entire function realizing the required singular dynamics). In the next lemma we show that in some cases the choice of the captures can be made in a consistent way, so that they yield “similar” Thurston’s iterations.

Lemma 3.6

(Similar pull-back)
.

Consider
\alpha\in\mathcal{E}^{0}
, and let
(\alpha_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{E}^{0}
be a sequence converging to
\alpha
so that
(\underline{s}_{1}^{n},...,\underline{s}_{m}^{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is contained in a wedge at
\alpha
. For all big enough
n
, the captures
c
and
(c_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
can be chosen in such a way that whenever the subspiders
S_{\varphi}^{0}
and
S_{\varphi_{n}}^{0}
(corresponding to different Teichmüller spaces!) are equivalent, we have
\varphi\circ f\circ\hat{\varphi}^{-1}=\varphi_{n}\circ f\circ\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{-1}
.

Proof.

A particular choice of
c
is irrelevant so just pick an arbitrary one. Let
v_{1},...,v_{m}
be the finite singular values of
f_{0}
. Surround each
c(v_{i})
by an open disk

, so that these disks are mutually disjoint and each

intersects only one leg of the standard spider of
f
(the one having
c(v_{i})
as its endpoint).

Due to Lemma
3.2
, without loss of generality we might assume that
a_{i0}^{n}\in B_{i}
for all
n
. Define
c_{n}
to be the capture
c
postcomposed with a quasiconformal homeomorphism
\theta_{n}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
mapping each
a_{i0}
to
a_{i0}^{n}
and equal to identity on the complement of
\cup_{i}B_{i}
. Note that due to Theorem
3.2
legs
R_{ij}^{n}
converge to
R_{ij}
as

, and
R_{ij}^{n}
converge to
\infty
(as sets) as

uniformly in
n
. This means that for
R_{i0}^{n}
(corresponding to
f_{n}
) is the only leg intersecting
B_{i}
for all big enough
n
. Hence, such

indeed satisfies our definition of capture. Assume that this holds for all
n
.

Let
\operatorname{id}
and
\operatorname{id}_{n}
be the identity maps corresponding Thurston’s iterations via
f
and
f_{n}
, respectively. Next, again from Theorem
3.2
, after increasing

we might assume that the subspiders
\theta_{n}(S_{\operatorname{id}}^{0})
and
S_{\operatorname{id}_{n}}^{0}
are equivalent: in fact we get
S_{\operatorname{id}_{n}}^{0}
(up to leg homotopy) from
S_{\operatorname{id}}^{0}
by moving each

to
a_{i0}^{n}
inside of
B_{i}
.

Since
\varphi_{n}\circ c_{n}=(\varphi_{n}\circ\theta_{n})\circ c
, we have
[\varphi_{n}\circ\theta_{n}]=[\varphi]
in the Teichmüller space of the complement of the singular values of
f
, i.e. of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{a_{10},...,a_{m0}\}
. Further,
[\varphi_{n}\circ c_{n}]=[\varphi\circ c]
in the Teichmüller space of the complement of the singular values of

. But then
\varphi\circ f\circ\hat{\varphi}^{-1}=\varphi_{n}\circ f\circ\hat{\varphi}_{n}^{-1}
.
∎

Before proceeding, we need to prove a technical lemma.

Lemma 3.7

(Expansivity near
\infty
)
.

Fix a disk
D_{\rho}=\{z:\left|z\right|<\rho\}
. For all
w\in\mathbb{C}
big enough and every continuous path
g_{u},u\in[0,1]
of functions (coefficients’ continuity) from
\mathcal{N}_{d}
such that
\operatorname{SV}(g_{u})\subset D_{\rho}
, holds

z_{1}=z_{0}+O(1/\left|w\right|)

where
z_{u},u\in[0,1]
is a continuous path in
\mathbb{C}
satisfying
g_{u}(z_{u})=w
.

Proof.

This lemma is basically a simplified version of
[
B2
, Proposition 5.3 (3)]
. Since the singular values of
g_{u}
belong to the bounded set
D_{\rho}
, the coefficients of
g_{u}
are bounded by some constant depending only on
\rho
. All the computations below make sense for big enough
\left|w\right|
. Now, let
g_{u}(z_{u})=w
. So
g_{u}(z_{u})=g_{0}(z_{0})
, and

e^{dz_{u}}(1+O(e^{-z_{u}}))=e^{dz_{0}}(1+O(e^{-z_{0}})).

After taking logarithm of both sides, we obtain

z_{u}=z_{0}+\frac{2\pi in_{u}}{d}+O(e^{-z_{u}})+O(e^{-z_{0}}).

Due to continuity of
z_{u}
, we must have
n_{u}=0
. Further, since
g_{u}(z_{u})=w
, we have
e^{dz_{u}}(1+O(e^{-z_{u}}))=w,
and
e^{\operatorname{Re\,}dz_{u}}(1+O(e^{-z_{u}}))=\left|w\right|.
Hence, we may assume that

\operatorname{Re\,}z_{u}>C\log\left|w\right|

for some constant
C>0
. But then
z_{1}=z_{0}+O(1/\left|w\right|)
. ∎

The next proposition is the most important in our constructions. Roughly speaking, it tells that the nearby points in
\mathcal{E}^{0}
allow uniform choice of

Proposition 3.8

(Invariant subspiders)
.

There is a

Remark 3.9

Note that the subspiders

(Continuity of

Consider the sequence
(\varphi^{\prime}_{n})_{n}^{\infty}
of
\operatorname{id}
-type maps such that

After changing to a subsequence we may assume that every sequence
\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(a_{ij}^{n})

is bounded, the sequence

Let
\tilde{R}_{ij}^{n}
be the ray tail of

, there exists a limit

which is a ray tail of

Theorem 3.11

(Continuity of

.

Consider

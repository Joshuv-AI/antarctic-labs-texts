Isotropic cosmic birefringence from early dark energy
Kai Murai, Fumihiro Naokawa, Toshiya Namikawa, Eiichiro Komatsu

Abstract
A tantalizing hint of isotropic cosmic birefringence has been found in the E ​ B EB cross-power spectrum of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) polarization data with a statistical significance of 3 ​ σ 3\sigma . A pseudoscalar field coupled to the CMB photons via the Chern-Simons term can explain this observation. The same field may also be responsible for early dark energy (EDE), which alleviates the so-called Hubble tension. Since the EDE field evolves significantly during the recombination epoch, the conventional formula that relates E ​ B EB to the difference between the E E - and B B -mode auto-power spectra is no longer valid. Solving the Boltzmann equation for polarized photons and the dynamics of the EDE field consistently, we find that currently favored parameter space of the EDE model yields a variety of shapes of the E ​ B EB spectrum, which can be tested by CMB experiments.

In MCMC, the posterior distribution of parameters is derived from the number of Monte Carlo steps spent in the parameter space. When some parameters are unconstrained, the MCMC “wastes” many steps and enhances the posterior probability in that parameter space. In the case of EDE, this occurs in a dramatic way: when

II
Isotropic cosmic birefringence

We consider an axionlike field,
\phi
, coupled to photons through the Chern-Simons term.
The Lagrangian density is written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{\mu}\phi\right)^{2}-V(\phi)-\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}g\phi F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\,,

(1)

where
V(\phi)
is the potential which is specified in Sec.
III
,
F_{\mu\nu}
is the field strength tensor of the photon field,
\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}
is its dual, and
g
is the Chern-Simons coupling constant of mass dimension
-1
.

When
\phi
varies slowly compared to the photon frequency,
the dispersion relations of photons are modified as
Carroll et al. 1990
;
Carroll and Field 1991
;
Harari and Sikivie 1992

\displaystyle\omega_{\pm}\simeq k\mp\frac{g}{2}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+\frac{\bm{k}}{k}\cdot\bm{\nabla}\phi\right)=k\mp\frac{g}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi}{\mathrm{d}t}\,,

(2)

where
\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}t
denotes a total derivative along the photon trajectory, and
+
and
-
correspond to the right- and left-handed circular polarization states of photons, respectively, in right-handed coordinates with the
z
axis taken in the direction of propagation of photons.

In this paper we focus on isotropic cosmic birefringence and ignore its anisotropy, which is not found yet
Contreras et al. 2017
;
Namikawa et al. 2020
;
Bianchini et al. 2020
;
Gruppuso et al. 2020
;
Bortolami et al. 2022
. See Refs.
Li and Zhang 2008
;
Pospelov et al. 2009
;
Caldwell et al. 2011
;
Zhao and Li 2014
;
Agrawal et al. 2020
;
Zhai et al. 2020
;
Jain et al. 2021
;
Greco et al. 2022
;
Kitajima et al. 2022
;
Jain et al. 2022
for study on anisotropic cosmic birefringence.

The helicity-dependent dispersion relation given in Eq. (
2
) induces a rotation of the plane of linear polarization. The rotation angle from a given time
t
to the present time
t_{0}
is given by
Carroll et al. 1990
;
Carroll and Field 1991
;
Harari and Sikivie 1992

\displaystyle\beta(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{t_{0}}\mathrm{d}\tilde{t}\,(\omega_{+}-\omega_{-})=\frac{g}{2}\left[\phi(t_{0})-\phi(t)\right]\,.

(3)

Here, we use the CMB convention for the position angle of linear polarization,
i.e.
,
\beta>0
represents a clockwise rotation of linear polarization in the sky.
The rotation angle depends only on the difference in the axionlike field values between the emission and observation and does not depend on the evolution history
Harari and Sikivie 1992
.

Next, we discuss the effect of cosmic birefringence on the CMB polarization field (see Ref.
Komatsu 2022
for a review).
In this paper we focus on scalar-mode perturbations and ignore tensor modes.
The evolution of the CMB polarization field is described by the Boltzmann equation
Kosowsky 1996
.
We define the Fourier transform of the Stokes parameters of linear polarization,
Q\pm iU
, by
{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}(\eta,q,\mu)
.
Here,
\eta
is the conformal time,

\bm{q}
is a wave vector in the Fourier space, and
\mu\equiv\bm{q}\cdot\bm{k}/(qk)
parameterizes the angle between
\bm{q}
and the photon momentum
\bm{k}
.
The Boltzmann equation for
{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}(\eta,q,\mu)
is given by
Finelli and Galaverni 2009
;
Liu et al. 2006
;
Gubitosi et al. 2014

{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}^{\prime}+iq\mu\,_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}=

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}\left[-_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}+\sqrt{\frac{6\pi}{5}}\,_{\pm 2}Y_{2}^{0}(\mu)\Pi(\eta,q)\right]

\displaystyle\pm 2i\beta^{\prime}\,_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}\,,

(4)

where
{}_{\pm 2}Y_{l}^{m}
is the spin-2 spherical harmonics,
\Pi(\eta,q)
is the polarization source term
Zaldarriaga and Seljak 1997
, and
\tau^{\prime}\equiv a(\eta)n_{e}(\eta)\sigma_{T}
is the differential optical depth with the Thomson scattering cross section
\sigma_{T}
and the number density of electrons
n_{e}
.
Here, the prime denotes the derivative with respect to
\eta
.
It is convenient to expand
{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}
with the spin-2 spherical harmonics as

{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P}(\eta,q,\mu)\equiv\sum_{l}i^{-l}\sqrt{4\pi(2l+1)}\,_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P,l}(\eta,q)\,_{\pm 2}Y_{l}^{0}(\mu)\,.

(5)

Then, we obtain the formal solution to the Boltzmann equation as

{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P,l}(\eta_{0},q)=-\frac{3}{4}\sqrt{\frac{(l+2)!}{(l-2)!}}\int_{0}^{\eta_{0}}\mathrm{d}\eta\,

\displaystyle\tau^{\prime}e^{-\tau(\eta)}\Pi(\eta,q)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\frac{j_{l}(x)}{x^{2}}e^{\pm 2i\beta(\eta)}\,,

(6)

where
j_{l}(x)
is the spherical Bessel function with
x\equiv q(\eta_{0}-\eta)
and
\tau(\eta)\equiv\int^{\eta_{0}}_{\eta}\mathrm{d}\eta_{1}\,\tau^{\prime}(\eta_{1})
.

To discuss the parity violation, we work with parity eigenstates of the CMB polarization,
E
and
B
modes
Zaldarriaga and Seljak 1997
;
Kamionkowski et al. 1997
.
In this basis, parity violation is imprinted in the
EB
correlation
Lue et al. 1999
.
We write the coefficients of
E
and
B
modes as

\displaystyle\Delta_{E,l}(q)\pm i\Delta_{B,l}(q)\equiv-\,_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P,l}(\eta_{0},q)\,.

(7)

Cosmic birefringence,
\beta\neq 0
, induces the imaginary part of
{}_{\pm 2}\Delta_{P,l}
, or equivalently
B
modes.
Using these coefficients, we obtain the polarization power spectra as

\displaystyle C_{l}^{XY}=4\pi\int\mathrm{d}(\ln q)\,\mathcal{P}_{s}(q)\Delta_{X,l}(q)\Delta_{Y,l}(q)\,,

(8)

where
\mathcal{P}_{s}(q)
is the primordial scalar curvature power spectrum, and
X,Y=E
or
B
.

If
\beta
is independent of
\eta
, the coefficients of
E
and
B
modes are given by

\displaystyle\Delta_{E,l}\pm i\Delta_{B,l}=e^{\pm 2i\beta}\left(\tilde{\Delta}_{E,l}\pm i\tilde{\Delta}_{B,l}\right)\,,

(9)

where the tildes denote quantities without cosmic birefringence.
Thus, cosmic birefringence transforms the polarization power spectra as
Liu et al. 2006
;
Feng et al. 2005

\displaystyle C_{l}^{EE}

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}(2\beta)\tilde{C}_{l}^{EE}+\sin^{2}(2\beta)\tilde{C}_{l}^{BB}\,,

(10)

\displaystyle C_{l}^{BB}

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}(2\beta)\tilde{C}_{l}^{BB}+\sin^{2}(2\beta)\tilde{C}_{l}^{EE}\,,

(11)

\displaystyle C_{l}^{EB}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sin(4\beta)\left(\tilde{C}_{l}^{EE}-\tilde{C}_{l}^{BB}\right)\,,

(12)

which can be combined to give
C_{l}^{EB}=\frac{1}{2}\tan(4\beta)(C_{l}^{EE}-C_{l}^{BB})

Zhao et al. 2015
. When the primordial
B
modes are negligible compared to the
E
modes, we obtain
C_{l}^{EB}\simeq\tan(2\beta)C_{l}^{EE}
.

In general,
\beta
depends on
\eta
through the evolution of
\phi
.
Roughly speaking, there are two main contributions to the CMB polarization power spectra.
One is the reionization epoch contributing to low multipoles,
l\lesssim 10

Zaldarriaga 1997
, and the other is the recombination epoch contributing to higher
l

Bond and Efstathiou 1987
.
Thus, the
EB
power spectrum at low and high
l
is largely determined by the evolution of
\phi
after the reionization and recombination epochs, respectively
Liu et al. 2006
;
Komatsu et al. 2009
;
Sherwin and Namikawa 2021
;
Nakatsuka et al. 2022
.

If
\phi
starts to oscillate after the reionization epoch,
\beta
is approximately constant and we obtain
C_{l}^{EB}\propto C_{l}^{EE}
.
If
\phi
starts to oscillate before the reionization epoch, the reionization bump in the
EB
power spectrum at low
l
is suppressed
Sherwin and Namikawa 2021
;
Nakatsuka et al. 2022
.
If
\phi
oscillates during the recombination epoch, the
shape
of the
EB
power spectrum can be different from that of the
EE
power spectrum at high
l

Finelli and Galaverni 2009
;
Nakatsuka et al. 2022
, which is relevant to our study.
The information in the shape of the
EB
power spectrum thus allows for “tomographic approach,” with which we can constrain the mechanism inducing cosmic birefringence or axion parameters
Sherwin and Namikawa 2021
;
Nakatsuka et al. 2022
.

III
Early dark energy

EDE is an additional energy component that behaves like dark energy at early times and contributes to the energy density budget around the recombination epoch
Karwal and Kamionkowski 2016
.
This contribution increases the Hubble expansion rate around the recombination epoch, which, in turn, reduces the size of the sound horizon
Bernal et al. 2016
.
As a result, EDE increases the inferred value of
H_{0}
from CMB observations and alleviates the Hubble tension.

The EDE energy density should decay faster than the matter density contribution after recombination, so as not to affect the late-time cosmic expansion.
The EDE models that satisfy this property are based on a pseudoscalar field potential given by
Poulin et al. 2019

\displaystyle V(\phi)=m^{2}f^{2}\left[1-\cos\left(\frac{\phi}{f}\right)\right]^{n}\,,

(13)

where
f
is the breaking scale of the symmetry related to
\phi
and
m
determines the potential scale.
Around
\phi=0
, this potential is approximated by
V(\phi)\propto\phi^{2n}
.
Thus, once
\phi
starts to oscillate, its energy density decreases as
\rho_{\phi}\propto a^{-6n/(n+1)}

Turner 1983
, which means that
\rho_{\phi}
decreases faster than the matter density for
n>1
.
In the following, we choose
n=3
as in the previous work
Herold et al. 2022
.

EDE is usually characterized by three parameters,
(f_{\mathrm{EDE}},z_{c},\theta_{i})
, where
f_{\mathrm{EDE}}
is the maximum energy density fraction of EDE at the critical redshift
z_{c}
, and
\theta_{i}
is the initial value of
\theta\equiv\phi/f
.
The previous work
Herold et al. 2022
has performed a profile likelihood analysis to avoid volume effects in the MCMC analysis and derived a robust constraint of
f_{\mathrm{EDE}}=0.072\pm 0.036
at 68% C.L.
They have also obtained the best-fitting values of the 6 parameters of the base-
\Lambda
CDM model and
(z_{c},\theta_{i})
for fixed values of

from the
Planck

\displaystyle\chi^{2}(\bm{p})=f_{\rm sky}\sum_{l=l_{\rm min}}^{l_{\rm max}}(2l+1)\frac{[\hat{C}^{EB}_{l}-C^{EB,\rm th}_{l}(\bm{p})]^{2}}{\hat{C}^{EE}_{l}\hat{C}^{BB}_{l}}\,,

V
Summary

Polarization of the CMB is a powerful probe of new physics beyond the standard model of elementary particles and fields
Komatsu 2022
. In this paper we connected two hints of new physics from the current cosmological datasets: isotropic cosmic birefringence and EDE. We included the dynamics of the EDE field,
\phi
, and its coupling to photons in the Boltzmann equation for the CMB polarization and evaluated the
EB
power spectrum,
C_{l}^{EB}
, induced by isotropic cosmic birefringence.
The shape of
C_{l}^{EB}
(Fig.
2
) is different from that of
C_{l}^{EE}
and depends sensitively on the EDE parameters given in Table
1
. We understood this result in terms of the time evolution of
\phi
shown in Fig.
1
.

We roughly translated the obtained
C_{l}^{EB}
into an effective rotation angle,
\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}
.
By requiring that
\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}
is equal to the observed rotation angle, we found
g=\mathcal{O}(0.01\,\text{--}\,0.1)f^{-1}
.
We also discussed the testability of the EDE model and showed that the cosmic birefringence predicted by the EDE model can be distinguished from that with a constant rotation angle by measuring
C_{l}^{EB}
in future CMB experiments such as SO and CMB-S4.

Our analysis can also be applied to other EDE models
Agrawal et al. 2019
;
Ye and Piao 2020
.
Since the shape of
C_{l}^{EB}
is sensitive to the oscillation phase of the EDE field during the recombination and reionization epoch, the shape of
C_{l}^{EB}
can be different in other models.
Although we have focused on the effect of isotropic cosmic birefringence on
C_{l}^{EB}
, the perturbations of the EDE field will induce the anisotropic cosmic birefringence
Capparelli et al. 2020
, which may provide a distinct signature in
C_{l}^{EB}
.
We leave this to future work.

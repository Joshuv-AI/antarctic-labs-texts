Distinct Partitions and Some q-Binomial Summation Identities
M.J. Kronenburg

Abstract
The partition functions P ⁡ ( n , m , p ) P(n,m,p) , the number of integer partitions of n n into exactly m m parts with each part at most p p , and Q ⁡ ( n , m , p ) Q(n,m,p) , the number of integer partitons of n n into exactly m m distinct parts with each part at most p p , are related by double summation identities which follow from their generating functions. From these identities and some identities from an earlier paper, some other identities involving distinct partitions and some q-binomial summation identities are proved, and from these follow some combinatorial identities.



Keywords
: q-binomial coefficient, integer partition function.

Theorem 3.5
.

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/3\rfloor}\sum_{l=0}^{\lfloor m/3\rfloor}(-1)^{l}P(n-3k,m-3l,p)Q(k,l,p)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\cos(\frac{2l-m}{3}\pi)Q(n-k,m-l,p)Q(k,l,p)

In the previous theorem replacing

When comparing this with the previous theorem it is clear that

and

are interchanged,
which gives:

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/3\rfloor}\sum_{l=0}^{\lfloor m/3\rfloor}(-1)^{l}P(n-3k,m-3l,p)Q(k,l,p)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{m}(-1)^{\frac{m-2l}{3}}Q(n-k,m-l,p)Q(k,l,p)

Equating the imaginary parts of this identity gives:

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\sin(\frac{m-2l}{3}\pi)Q(n-k,m-l,p)Q(k,l,p)=0

(3.28)

As in the previous theorem this identity is trivial.
Equating the real parts of the previous identity and using

gives the theorem.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/4\rfloor}\sum_{l=0}^{\lfloor m/4\rfloor}Q(n-4k,m-4l,p)P(k,l,p)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\sum_{l=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}(-1)^{l}P(n-2k,m-2l,p)P(k,l,p)

(3.29)

Proof.

Using
(1+izq^{j})(1-izq^{j})(1-z^{2}q^{2j})=1-z^{4}q^{4j}

\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(1+izq^{j})}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(1-z^{4}q^{4j})}=\frac{1}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(1-izq^{j})\prod_{j=1}^{p}(1-z^{2}q^{2j})}

(3.30)

The proof is similar to the previous proofs.

Theorem 3.7
.

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/4\rfloor}\sum_{l=0}^{\lfloor m/4\rfloor}(-1)^{l}P(n-4k,m-4l,p)Q(k,l,p)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\sum_{l=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}Q(n-2k,m-2l,p)Q(k,l,p)

(3.31)

Proof.

Using
(1+(-1)^{1/4}zq^{j})(1-(-1)^{1/4}zq^{j})(1+iz^{2}q^{2j})=1+z^{4}q^{4j}

\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(1+z^{4}q^{4j})}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(1-(-1)^{1/4}zq^{j})}=\prod_{j=1}^{p}(1+(-1)^{1/4}zq^{j})\prod_{j=1}^{p}(1+iz^{2}q^{2j})

(3.32)

The proof is similar to the previous proofs.
∎

Theorem 3.8
.

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{m}(-1)^{l}P(n-k,m-l,p)Q(k,l,p)=\delta_{n,0}\delta_{m,0}

(3.33)

Proof.

\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(1+(-z)q^{j})}{\prod_{j=1}^{p}(1-zq^{j})}=1

(3.34)

\begin{split}&\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\delta_{n,0}\delta_{m,0}q^{n}z^{m}=(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}P(n,m,p)q^{n}z^{m})(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}Q(n,m,p)(-1)^{m}q^{n}z^{m})\\
&=\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m_{1}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m_{2}=0}^{\infty}P(n_{1},m_{1},p)Q(n_{2},m_{2},p)(-1)^{m_{2}}q^{n_{1}+n_{2}}z^{m_{1}+m_{2}}\\
\end{split}

The coefficients on both sides must be equal, so

and

which is equivalent to

and
m_{1}=m-m_{2}
, which gives the theorem.
∎

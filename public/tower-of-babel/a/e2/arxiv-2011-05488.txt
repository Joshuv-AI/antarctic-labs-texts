Graph-like Distributions and Types in Ultrapowers
Michael Wheeler

Abstract
. We study types that appear in ultraproducts that have distributions which can be thought of as a sequence of graphs. The property of having distributions that are captured by graphs is motivated by a commonality of SOP 2 \mathrm{SOP}_{2} -types and types corresponding to pre-cuts in ultrapowers of linear orders. Through this study, we come to a simple set-theoretic description of the distributions for these types and to a new description of good ultrafilters in terms of the difference between “small” external complete subgraphs and internal complete subgraphs in ultraproducts of particular families of finite graphs arising from the comparability relation on the complete binary tree or the intersection of intervals in an infinite linear order. Key Words : distribution, good ultrafilter, graph-like type, ultragraph, ultraproduct MSC2020 : 03C20; 03E05, 05C05, 05C63, 06A05



1.
Introduction

The idea of a distribution for a type in an ultrapower using a regular ultrafilter on an infinite cardinal
\lambda
was introduced by Keisler in
[
1
]
. Distributions arise naturally in the study of types over ultraproducts created using regular ultrafilters. In particular, if
(\mathcal{M}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
is a sequence of structures in a common language and
p(x)=\{\varphi_{\beta}(\bar{x};\bar{a}_{\beta}):\beta<\lambda\}
is a type of the ultraproduct
\mathcal{M}:=\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}
, then we can use the regularity of
\mathcal{D}
to show that
p(\bar{x})
is realized in
\mathcal{M}
if and only if there is a way to “distribute” the conditions
\varphi_{\beta}(\bar{x};\bar{a}_{\beta})
to the various index structures so that each index structure is only required to satisfy finitely many of the conditions, each index can simultaneously satisfy the conditions placed on it, and every finite subset of conditions is distributed to ultrafilter-many indices. We study types we call graph-like that have the simplifying properties that
\varphi_{\beta}=\varphi_{\gamma}
for all
\beta,\gamma<\lambda
and that a finite collection of the constraints is simultaneously satisfiable in a given index if and only if every subcollection of two constraints can be satisfied by a single element of the given index structure.

Graph-like types are so called because the structure of which conditions can be simultaneously realized in any given index structure is captured by a graph, where each vertex represents a condition and an edge between the vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
indicates that the two conditions represented by
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are compatible. In general, we would need to worry about whether arbitrarily large finite subsets of conditions are compatible (which one might wish to represent as edges in a hypergraph), but the property of being graph-like allows us to ignore these higher levels of complexity.

A small (by small, we will mean of cardinality no larger than the cardinality of the index set we have chosen a regular ultrafilter on) graph-like type
p(\bar{x})
, along with a collection of representatives for the parameters that appear in
p(\bar{x})
, is represented by a finite graph
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
in each index structure
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
, and the type
p(\bar{x})
can be recovered from the sequence of graphs
(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
if the collection of representations is also known (see the proof of Corollary
5.10
). One might then hope that the ultraproduct (we call this the ultragraph)
\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}
carries some information about
p(\bar{x})
. In this vein we show in Corollary
5.10
that
p(\bar{x})
is realized in the ultraproduct
\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}
if and only if the image of a natural (set) embedding of
p(\bar{x})
in the ultragraph is contained within a complete subgraph of the form
\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}K_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}
where
K_{\alpha}
is a complete subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
. The image of this embedding being a complete subgraph of the ultragraph is equivalent to the fact that
p(\bar{x})
is a type and says nothing about whether or not the type
p(\bar{x})
is realized.

Besides allowing us to reframe the question of whether or not a graph-like type
p(\bar{x})
is realized in an ultrapower, we also show that graph-like types have distributions that must reflect the class of graphs that can appear in the sequence
(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
. In cases where there is a discernible pattern in which graphs are allowed to appear in
(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
, we can exploit this information to gain a characterization of the distributions of
p(\bar{x})
(see Theorem
7.16
).

Our primary application will be to the study of distributions in theories with
\mathrm{SOP}_{n}
for
n\geq 2
(defined in
[
5
]
). In particular, the compatibility of conditions coming from an
\mathrm{SOP}_{2}
-type (as defined in
[
3
]
) has the same structure as graphs arising from the compatibility relation on finite trees, and the compatibility of conditions that arise from types indicating a cut within an ultraproduct of infinite linear orders has the structure of graphs where the vertices are intervals in the order (or any infinite linear order) and the edges indicate non-empty intersections, allowing a characterization of the distributions for these sorts of types in terms of the corresponding classes of graphs. Moreover, since we know from
[
5
]
and
[
3
]
that an ultrafilter
\mathcal{D}
is good if and only if
\mathcal{D}

\lambda^{+}
-saturates structures having
\mathrm{SOP}_{2}
, we find a characterization in Theorem
8.4
of good ultrafilters both in terms of whether a relatively small class of distributions has multiplicative refinements and in terms of whether ultraproducts of comparability graphs of finite trees allow small complete subgraphs to have internal complete extensions.

We also show that every theory with
\mathrm{SOP}_{3}
(or with
\mathrm{SOP}_{n}
for
n>3
) admits types that mimic arbitrary distributions of types corresponding to (pre-)cuts in ultrapowers of infinite linear orders. The idea is similar to Shelah’s proof in
[
5
, 2.11]
that theories with
\mathrm{SOP}_{3}
are maximal in the Keisler order, but avoids use of the bi-interpretability ordering defined in the same paper.

It is worth pointing out that the characterization of
\lambda^{+}
-good ultrafilters in terms of (external) complete subgraphs of cardinality
\lambda
being lifted to internal complete subgraphs is related to the result of Douglas Ulrich
[
6
, Lem. 2.4]
showing that small uses of external recursion can be lifted to an internal use of recursion in
\omega
-nonstandard models
\hat{V}
of set theory where
\mathfrak{p}_{\hat{V}}>\lambda
. The results presented here show that
\lambda^{+}
-good ultrafilters are characterized by being able to lift small, external, complete subgraphs of an ultraproduct of specific families of graphs to an internal complete subgraph; whereas Lemma 2.4 of
[
6
]
can be thought of as showing that, in any ultraproduct of graphs via a
\lambda^{+}
-good ultrafilter, it is possible to extend all complete subgraphs of cardinality
\lambda
to internal complete subgraphs. In particular, if we take
\hat{V}
to be an ultrapower of
V\vDash\mathrm{ZFC}
via a good ultrafilter, then we can think of finding an internal complete subgraph of an ultragraph
\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}\in V
as finding a complete subgraph of the graph represented by the function
\alpha\mapsto\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}

within

\hat{V}
. This process could be done by lifting a small external induction to an internal one.

Background Definitions and Conventions

Convention 2.1
.

Let
I
be an infinite set and
\mathcal{D}
an ultrafilter on
I
. If for all
i\in I
the structure
\mathcal{M}_{i}
is in the language
\mathcal{L}
, then
\mathcal{M}_{I}^{\mathcal{D}}:=\prod_{i\in I}\mathcal{M}_{i}/\mathcal{D}
is the ultraproduct of the
\mathcal{M}_{i}
with respect to
\mathcal{D}
.

Typically, we will take the
I
in the above convention to be an infinite cardinal
\lambda
.

Convention 2.2
.

If
\lambda
is an infinite cardinal and for all
\alpha<\lambda
the structure
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
is in the language
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{D}
is an ultrafilter on
\lambda
, then
p(\bar{x})=\{\varphi_{\beta}(\bar{x},\bar{a}_{\beta}):\beta<\lambda\}
is a
\lambda
-type of
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{D}}
if whenever
(\bar{r}_{\beta})_{\beta<\lambda}
is a sequence of representatives coming from
\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
for the
\bar{a}_{\beta}
in
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{D}}
and whenever
\Delta\subseteq\lambda
is finite it is the case that

\llbracket\Delta\rrbracket_{p(\bar{x})}^{\mathcal{D}}:=\left\{\alpha:\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\vDash\exists x,\bigwedge_{\beta\in\Delta}\varphi_{\beta}(\bar{x},\bar{r}_{\beta}(\alpha))\right\}\in\mathcal{D}.

The convention above agrees with the usual definition of type for a structure by the compactness theorem and Łoś theorem.

Convention 2.3
.

Whenever we use
\alpha
we will mean an index for the structures
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
being considered and whenever we use
\beta
we will mean an index to be used either for the formula
\varphi_{\beta}
or for the representative/parameter
\bar{r}_{\beta}/\bar{a}_{\beta}
for a given
\lambda
-type
p(\bar{x})
. We will variously use
\gamma,\delta,
and
\epsilon
as indices when more variables are needed.

Convention 2.4
.

We fix an infinite cardinal
\lambda
, a regular ultrafilter
\mathcal{D}
on
\lambda
, a sequence of structures
(\mathcal{M}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
in the common language
\mathcal{L}
, and a
\lambda
-type
p(\bar{x})
on
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}}
. We will continue to use these general fixed values throughout unless otherwise specified.

We use the following notation that frequently appears in the literature for set theory and Ramsey theory.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
A
be any set and
\kappa
be any cardinal.

(1)

[A]^{\kappa}
is the set
\{B\subseteq A:|B|=\kappa\}
.

(2)

\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(A)
is the set
\{B\subseteq A:|B|<\omega\}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(\bar{r}_{\beta})_{\beta<\lambda}
be given as in the definition of
\lambda
-type. The
Łoś map
for
p(\bar{x})
in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
given
(\bar{r}_{\beta})_{\beta<\lambda}
is the function
L\colon\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)\to\mathcal{D}
that is defined for each
\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)
by

L(\Delta)=\llbracket\Delta\rrbracket_{p(x)}^{\mathcal{D}}.

For any finite
\Delta\subseteq\lambda
we have that
L(\Delta)\in\mathcal{D}
by the definition of a
\lambda
-type of
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
.

We will frequently have the sequence of representations
(\bar{r}_{\beta})_{\beta<\lambda}
for the parameters appearing in a
\lambda
-type
p(\bar{x})
implicit.

Definition 2.7
.

A
distribution
for a
\lambda
-type
p(\bar{x})
in
\mathcal{M}^{\mathcal{D}}
is a function
f\colon\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)\to\mathcal{D}
such that

(1)

for all
\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)
we have
f(\Delta)\subseteq L(\Delta)
, and

(2)

all
\Gamma\in[\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)]^{\omega}
satisfy
\bigcap_{\Delta\in\Gamma}f(\Delta)=\emptyset
.

A distribution
f
is called
monotone
if for all
\Delta,\Phi\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)
we have that
f
has the property that
\Delta\subseteq\Phi
implies
f(\Delta)\supseteq f(\Phi)
and the distribution
f
is called
multiplicative
if
f
satisfies
f(\Delta\cup\Phi)=f(\Delta)\cap f(\Phi)
.

Remark 2.8
.

A distribution that is multiplicative must also be monotone. Throughout, we will assume that all distributions are monotone.

Fact 2.9

(Keisler)
.

The
\lambda
-type
p(\bar{x})
is realized in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
if and only if there is a multiplicative distribution for
p(\bar{x})
in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
.

Definition 2.10
.

Suppose that
f
and
g
are both distributions for the type
p(\bar{x})
. We say that
f

refines

g
(or
f

is a refinement of

g
) and write
f\subseteq g
if, for all
\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)
we have that
f(\Delta)\subseteq g(\Delta)
.

Remark 2.11
.

Condition
(1)
of
2.7
is frequently stated as “
f
refines the Łoś map
L
.”

One may view the definition of a distribution
f
as saying that given a finite subset
\Delta
of a type in the ultrapower the distribution outputs a set of indices
f(\Delta)
in
\mathcal{D}
so that if a representative for a potential realization satisfied
\Delta
in
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha\in f(\Delta)
, then the potential realization at least realizes
\Delta
in the ultrapower (Note: this statement is also true of the Łoś map and is based entirely on part
(1)
of the definition of a distribution). Moreover, a distribution uses the regularity of the ultrafilter to divide up (distribute) the indices where these finite subsets must be realized so that the representative for the potential realization need only realize finitely many conditions in each index. The finiteness aspect of the distribution comes from part
(2)
of the definition of a distribution.

It may appear that the existence of a distribution is enough to show that a type is realized (if each index structure only needs to satisfy finitely many of the formula in the type and all finite types are realized, just take a realization of the needed finite type in the given index structure). However, we are not guaranteed that the projections of
p(\bar{x})
to the various index structures remain finitely satisfiable (i.e. such projections may fail to be types in the index structures). The type
p(\bar{x})
may not be finitely satisfiable in
any
of the structures
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
.

In general, distributions are difficult to work with, so in the next section we propose a different way of looking at the distributions of certain “nice” types that appear to be important when working with Keisler’s order.

Definition 2.12
.

A subset
A\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
is called
internal
if there is a sequence of unary predicates
(P_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
so that if
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
is
\mathcal{M}
expanded by the predicate
P_{\alpha}
in the language
\mathcal{L}\cup\{P\}
then
A=P(\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{D}})
. Equivalently,
A\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
is
internal
if for each
\alpha<\lambda
there are sets
A_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
such that
A=\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}A_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}
.

One can think of internal subsets as being subsets that arise from the product structure on an ultraproduct. It is not obvious that there will be subsets of an ultraproduct that do not arise from the product structure (the quotient may collapse some subsets that do not come from a product to subsets that do). However, any
\aleph_{1}
-incomplete ultraproduct of any infinite set will have subsets that are not internal (frequently called external subsets).

One class of types that we frequently reference are the
\mathrm{SOP}_{2}
-types defined by Malliaris and Shelah in
[
3
]
. We record an equivalent definition here for ease of reference.

Definition 2.13
.

We say that a structure
\mathcal{M}

has an
\mathrm{SOP}_{2}
-tree
if there is a formula
\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
and a sequence of tuples
(\bar{a}_{\eta})_{\eta\in 2^{<\omega}}
from
\mathcal{M}
all the same length as
\bar{y}
and for all
\Gamma\subseteq 2^{<\omega}
the sets

\{\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{a}_{\eta}):\eta\in\Gamma\}

are consistent if and only if
\Gamma
is a chain in
2^{<\omega}
. We say that the pair
(\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{y}),(\bar{a}_{\eta})_{\eta\in 2^{<\omega}})
is an
\mathrm{SOP}_{2}
-tree in
\mathcal{M}
.

A first-order complete theory
T
is said to have
\mathrm{SOP}_{2}
if there is some
\mathcal{M}\vDash T
such that
\mathcal{M}
has an
\mathrm{SOP}_{2}
-tree.

A
\lambda
-type

p(\bar{x})=\{\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{a}_{\beta}):\beta<\lambda\}

is an
\mathrm{SOP}_{2}
-type
if there is a sequence
(\bar{r}_{\beta})_{\beta<\lambda}
of tuples from
\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
such that

(1)

for each
\beta<\lambda
we have that
\bar{r}_{\beta}/\mathcal{D}=\bar{a}_{\beta}
, and

(2)

for each
\alpha<\lambda
there is an
\mathrm{SOP}_{2}
-tree
(\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{y}),(\bar{b}_{\eta})_{\eta\in 2^{<\omega}})
in
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
with

\{\bar{r}_{\beta}(\alpha):\beta<\lambda\}\subseteq\{\bar{b}_{\eta}:\eta\in 2^{<\omega}\}.

3.
Graph-like Distributions

3.1.
Graph-like Distributions and Conjugates

We begin by identifying a simplifying property that distributions may have. We will later see that
\mathrm{SOP}_{2}
-types and types corresponding to pre-cuts in ultrapowers of linear orders both have this nice simplifying property.

Definition 3.1
.

A distribution
f
for
p(\bar{x})
in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
is called
graph-like
if it satisfies the condition

f(\Delta)=\bigcap_{\Phi\in[\Delta]^{2}}f(\Phi)

whenever
\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)
has
|\Delta|\geq 2
.

A graph-like distribution is a distribution in which the combinatorial information contained in the distribution can be fully captured by a (simple) graph. Put another way, a graph-like distribution
f
is a distribution where a finite
\Delta\subseteq p(\bar{x})
is guaranteed to be realized in
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
by
f
if and only if each of the two-element subsets of
\Delta
are guaranteed to be realized in
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
by
f
. Note: If
\Delta
is guaranteed to be realized by
f
then all of its two element subsets are also guaranteed to be realized by the monotonicity of
f
. Thus the content of being a graph-like distribution is that
f
is both monotone and that finite sets of size strictly grater than
2
have their simultaneous realizability determined by the two-element subsets of
p(\bar{x})
.

A distribution being graph-like is also an approximation of the distribution being multiplicative.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
f
is a distribution such that
for all
\Delta,\Gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)
with
|\Delta|,|\Gamma|\geq 2
we have that
f(\Delta\cup\Gamma)=f(\Delta)\cap f(\Gamma)
then
f
is a graph-like distribution.

Proof.

By induction, the property given above can be extended to any finite number of subsets of cardinality at least
2
. In particular, if
\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)
with
|\Delta|\geq 2
, then
[\Delta]^{2}
is a finite collection of two-element subsets of
\lambda
. We thus get that

f(\Delta)=f\left(\bigcup\,[\Delta]^{2}\right)=\bigcap_{\Phi\in[\Delta]^{2}}f(\Phi).\qed

Lemma 3.3
.

If
f
is a graph-like distribution then
f
is multiplicative if and only if
f
satisfies the property

(3.1)

f(\{\beta,\gamma\})=f(\{\beta\})\cap f(\{\gamma\})

for all
\beta,\gamma\in\lambda
.

Proof.

We will first show that the property (
3.1
) can be extended to all finite subsets of
\lambda
using the fact that
f
is graph-like, and then derive multiplicativity from this expanded property.
Suppose that
\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)
with
|\Delta|>2
. Since
f
is graph-like, we get that

f(\Delta)=\bigcap_{\Phi\in[\Delta]^{2}}f(\Phi).

Because each
\Phi\in[\Delta]^{2}
has exactly two elements, we may use the property (
3.1
) to further expand this equation to

(3.2)

f(\Delta)=\bigcap_{\Phi\in[\Delta]^{2}}\bigcap_{\beta\in\Phi}f(\{\beta\})=\bigcap_{\beta\in\Delta}f(\{\beta\}).

Now suppose that
\Gamma,\Theta\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)
. Using Equation (
3.2
) we derive that

\displaystyle f(\Gamma\cup\Theta)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{\beta\in(\Gamma\cup\Theta)}f(\{\beta\})

\displaystyle=\left(\bigcap_{\beta\in\Gamma}f(\{\beta\})\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Theta}f(\{\gamma\})\right)

\displaystyle=f(\Gamma)\cap f(\Theta).

The other direction is a direct consequence of the definition of a distribution being multiplicative.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

If
f
is a distribution then
f
is multiplicative if and only if
f
is graph-like and
f
satisfies

f(\{\delta,\epsilon\})=f(\{\delta\})\cap f(\{\epsilon\})

for all
\delta,\epsilon\in\lambda
.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) A multiplicative distribution
f
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
3.2
and the property (
3.1
).

(
\Leftarrow
) This is Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Because the information of a graph-like distribution
f
is captured by a graph, we wish to explicitly say what these graphs are. In order to do this, it will be convenient to change the question (asked by a distribution) from “Given a finite subset
\Delta
of the type
p(\bar{x})
, in what set of indices do we know that
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\vDash\Delta
by looking at
f
?” to the question (asked by what we call the conjugate distribution) “Given an index structure
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
, what are the finite subsets
\Delta
of
p(\bar{x})
so that we are guaranteed by
f
that
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\vDash\Delta
?”

Definition 3.5
.

Let
f
be a distribution for
p(\bar{x})
in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
. The
conjugate of
f
is the
\omega
-sequence of functions
g_{n}\colon\lambda\to\mathcal{P}_{\omega}([\lambda]^{n})
defined for each
\alpha<\lambda
by

(3.3)

g_{n}(\alpha)=\{\Delta\in[\lambda]^{n}:\alpha\in f(\Delta)\}.

Remarks 3.6
.

(1)

One could also reasonably define the conjugate as the single function
\hat{g}\colon\displaystyle\alpha\mapsto\bigcup_{n\in\omega}g_{n}(\alpha)
for all
\alpha<\lambda
, although this would be more cumbersome for our purposes. The function
\hat{g}
has crucially appeared in several proofs before but has not been thoroughly studied.

(2)

The fact that
g_{n}(\alpha)
is finite for each
n\in\omega
and each
\alpha<\lambda
is a consequence of the definition of a distribution (in particular, Definition
2.7
(2)), and the existence of functions satisfying this definition is equivalent to the ultrafilter
\mathcal{D}
being regular. In short, we are able to make the
g_{n}(\alpha)
finite because we are working with a regular ultrafilter.

(3)

For elements of
g_{1}(\alpha)
we will not be careful about the distinction between elements of
\lambda
and elements of
[\lambda]^{1}
. In particular, we will usually think of
g_{1}(\alpha)
as a finite subset of
\lambda
.

The conjugate
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of the distribution
f
contains all of the information that is contained within the distribution
f
.

Lemma 3.7
.

If
f
is a distribution with conjugate
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
then

(3.4)

f(\Delta)=\{\alpha\in\lambda:\Delta\in g_{|\Delta|}(\alpha)\}.

Proof.

The result follows from the string of equalities

\displaystyle\{\alpha\in\lambda:\Delta\in g_{|\Delta|}(\alpha)\}

\displaystyle=\big\{\alpha\in\lambda:\Delta\in\{\Gamma\in[\lambda]^{|\Delta|}:\alpha\in f(\Gamma)\}\big\}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in\lambda:\alpha\in f(\Delta)\}

\displaystyle=f(\Delta).\qed

Remark 3.8
.

The previous lemma shows that distributions
f
are uniquely associated with their conjugate
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
as
f
can be recovered from the conjugate using Equation (
3.4
).

In the case of a graph-like distribution, the behavior of the two-element subsets determines the behavior of the larger subsets, so we may expect that a graph-like distribution and its conjugate are uniquely determined by just
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
, as the following theorem demonstrates.

Theorem 3.9
.

If
f
is a graph-like distribution with conjugate
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
then for
n\geq 3
we have that

(3.5)

g_{n}(\alpha)=\{\Delta\in[\lambda]^{n}:[\Delta]^{2}\subseteq g_{2}(\alpha)\}.

In particular, the conjugate
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of a graph-like distribution is determined by
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
.

Proof.

The result follows from the following chain of equalities:

\displaystyle g_{n}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\{\Delta\in[\lambda]^{n}:\alpha\in f(\Delta)\}

\displaystyle=\{\Delta\in[\lambda]^{n}:\alpha\in\bigcap_{\Gamma\in[\Delta]^{2}}f(\Gamma)\}

\displaystyle=\{\Delta\in[\lambda]^{n}:\forall\Gamma\in[\Delta]^{2},\,\alpha\in f(\Gamma)\}

\displaystyle=\{\Delta\in[\lambda]^{n}:\forall\Gamma\in[\Delta]^{2},\,\Gamma\in g_{2}(\alpha)\}

\displaystyle=\{\Delta\in[\lambda]^{n}:[\Delta]^{2}\subseteq g_{2}(\alpha)\}.\qed

From the previous two theorems, we see that the conjugate of a distribution
f
carries with it all the information that is contained in
f
. Fortunately, we can also translate the axioms of a distribution into axioms for a sequence of functions
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of the type
{g_{n}\colon\lambda\to\mathcal{P}([\lambda]^{n})}
being the conjugate of a distribution or a graph-like distribution. In fact, it will be easier for us to work with the conjugates of graph-like distributions than with graph-like distributions themselves.

Lemma 3.10
.

Suppose that
f\colon\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)\to\mathcal{D}
is an arbitrary function and
p(\bar{x})
is a
\lambda
-type of the ultraproduct
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}:=\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}
where
\mathcal{D}
is a regular ultrafilter on the infinite cardinal
\lambda
. Suppose that the conjugate of
f
is defined in the same manner as in Definition
3.5
and that the conjugate of
f
is
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
(the codomain of
g_{n}
is now
\mathcal{P}([\lambda]^{n})
instead of
\mathcal{P}_{\omega}([\lambda]^{n})
). Then

(1)

The function
f
refines the Łoś map
L
of
p(\bar{x})
if and only if for all
n\in\omega
, all
\alpha\in\lambda
, and all
\Delta\in g_{n}(\alpha)
we have that

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\vDash\exists\bar{x},\bigwedge_{\beta\in\Delta}\varphi_{\beta}(\bar{x},\bar{r}_{\beta}(\alpha))

where the
\bar{r}_{\beta}
are the fixed representatives in
\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
of the parameters in
p(\bar{x})
.

(2)

The function
f
satisfies
\bigcap_{\Delta\in\Gamma}f(\Delta)=\emptyset
for all
\Gamma\in[\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)]^{\omega}
if and only if for all
\alpha\in\lambda
the set
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}g_{n}(\alpha)
is finite.

(3)

The function
f
is monotone if and only if for all
n>m
in
\omega
, all
\alpha\in\lambda
, and all
\Delta\in g_{n}(\alpha)
we have that
[\Delta]^{m}\subseteq g_{m}(\alpha)
.

Proof.

(1)
: (
\Rightarrow
) Fix an
\alpha\in\lambda
and
n\in\omega
and suppose that
\Delta\in g_{n}(\alpha)
. We then have that
\alpha\in f(\Delta)\subseteq\llbracket\Delta\rrbracket_{p(\bar{x})}^{\mathcal{D}}
. That is,

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\vDash\exists\bar{x},\bigwedge_{\beta\in\Delta}\varphi_{\beta}(\bar{x},\bar{r}_{\beta}(\alpha)).

(
\Leftarrow
) Suppose that
\Delta\in[\lambda]^{n}
and
\alpha\in f(\Delta)
. This setup guarantees that
\Delta\in g_{n}(\alpha)
so

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\vDash\exists\bar{x},\bigwedge_{\beta\in\Delta}\varphi_{\beta}(\bar{x},\bar{r}_{\beta}(\alpha)).

That is,
\alpha\in\llbracket\Delta\rrbracket_{p(\bar{x})}^{\mathcal{D}}=L(\Delta)
.

(2)
: Both directions will be easier using the claim that
\Gamma
is a subset of
\bigcup_{n\in\omega}g_{n}(\alpha)
of any cardinality if and only if
\alpha
is an element of
\bigcap_{\Delta\in\Gamma}f(\Delta)
. The claim follows from the equivalences

\displaystyle\Gamma\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\omega}g_{n}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\iff\forall\Delta\in\Gamma,\,\alpha\in f(\Delta)

\displaystyle\iff\alpha\in\bigcap_{\Delta\in\Gamma}f(\Delta)

where the first equivalence comes from the definition of the conjugate and the fact that
\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}[\lambda]^{n}
.

(
\Leftarrow
) Proceeding using the contrapositive, pick
\Gamma\in[\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)]^{\omega}
so that
{A:=\bigcap_{\Delta\in\Gamma}f(\Delta)}
is non-empty. Choose
\alpha\in A
. By the above claim,
\Gamma\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\omega}g_{n}(\alpha)
so

\left|\bigcup_{n\in\omega}g_{n}(\alpha)\right|\geq|\Gamma|=\aleph_{0}.

(
\Rightarrow
) Again proceeding using the contrapositive, pick an
\alpha\in\lambda
so that
\Gamma^{\prime}=\bigcup_{n}g_{n}(\alpha)
is infinite and define
\Gamma
to be a countable infinite subset of
\Gamma^{\prime}
. By the above claim, we have that
\alpha\in\bigcap_{\Delta\in\Gamma}f(\Delta)
where
\Gamma\in[\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)]^{\omega}
.

(3)
: (
\Rightarrow
) Suppose that
\alpha\in f(\Delta)
and
|\Delta|=n
(i.e.
\Delta\in g_{n}(\alpha)
). If
\Gamma\in[\Delta]^{m}
with
m<n
then
f(\Gamma)\supseteq f(\Delta)
by monotonicity, so
\alpha\in f(\Gamma)
. That is,
\Gamma\in g_{m}(\alpha)
.

(
\Leftarrow
) Suppose that
\Gamma\subseteq\Delta
where
|\Gamma|=m
and
|\Delta|=n
. Then
\alpha\in f(\Delta)
if and only if
\Delta\in g_{n}(\alpha)
. As
\Gamma\in[\Delta]^{m}
, we get that
\Gamma\in g_{m}(\alpha)
which is equivalent to saying
\alpha\in f(\Gamma)
. Thus
f(\Delta)\subseteq f(\Gamma)
.
∎

We will mostly use the preceding lemma in order to show that a given sequence of functions
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
where

is the conjugate sequence of some distribution

. Generally, we will use this strategy to create a graph-like distribution out of

, so we present the theory both in the general case and in the specialized case that we are most concerned with.

Lemma 3.11
.

If
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}

(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}

for the
\lambda
-type

in
\widehat{M}=\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}
with the representatives
(\bar{r}_{\beta})_{\beta\in\lambda}
if and only if the sequence

satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

For all
n\in\omega
, all

, and all
\Delta\in g_{n}(\alpha)

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\vDash\exists\bar{x},\bigwedge_{\beta\in\Delta}\varphi_{\beta}(\bar{x},\bar{r}_{\beta}(\alpha)).

(2)

For all
n>m
in
\omega
, all
\alpha\in\lambda
, and all
\Delta\in g_{n}(\alpha)
it is the case that
[\Delta]^{m}\subseteq g_{m}(\alpha)
.

(3)

For all

and all

\{\alpha\in\lambda:\Delta\in g_{n}(\alpha)\}
is an element of
\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

(
\Leftarrow
): In order to use Lemma
3.10
, we need to show that

is the conjugate of a function

\Delta\mapsto\{\alpha\in\lambda:\Delta\in g_{|\Delta|}(\alpha)\}
for all

(3)
above. If

\displaystyle h_{n}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\{\Delta\in[\lambda]^{n}:\alpha\in f(\Delta)\}

\displaystyle=\{\Delta\in[\lambda]^{n}:\alpha\in\{\gamma\in\lambda:\Delta\in g_{n}(\gamma)\}\}

\displaystyle=\{\Delta\in[\lambda]^{n}:\Delta\in g_{n}(\alpha)\}

\displaystyle=g_{n}(\alpha)

so

is the conjugate of

.

We now immediately get from Lemma
3.10
that

refines the Łoś map using condition
(1)
of this lemma and that

is monotone using condition
(2)
for this lemma. It remains to check that

3.10
(2)
.

Fix

. We claim that

whenever

. By using the claim, we get that

\bigcup_{m\in\omega}g_{m}(\alpha)\subseteq\bigcup_{m\in\omega}[g_{1}(\alpha)]^{m}=\bigcup_{m=0}^{|g_{1}(\alpha)|}[g_{1}(\alpha)]^{m}=\mathcal{P}(g_{1}(\alpha))

where the right-hand side is a finite union of finite sets, so

must also be finite. By Lemma
3.10
(2)
, the sequence

.

To prove the claim: Suppose that

. Using condition
(2)
with

with

(
\Rightarrow
): From Lemma

(1) and (3) we immediately have that

satisfies conditions (1) and (2) above. The condition (3) of this lemma is satisfied because

is

.
∎

.

If

in

\Leftarrow

4.
Graphs Arising from Graph-like Distributions

We now assume that
p(\bar{x})
has a graph-like distribution given by
f:\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)\to\mathcal{D}
and that the conjugate of
f
is
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
.

As mentioned before, the information carried by a graph-like distribution can be represented in each index by a finite simple graph. This idea is made formal in the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

The
distribution graph sequence of
f
is the
\lambda
-sequence of graphs
(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\lambda}
such that the vertex set of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
is
G_{\alpha}:=g_{1}(\alpha)
and the edge relation in
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
is
E_{\alpha}:=g_{2}(\alpha)
.

Remarks 4.2
.

(1)

The distribution
f
and conjugate sequence can be easily recovered from the graph sequence as
g_{1}(\alpha)
is the set of vertices of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
and
g_{2}(\alpha)
is the set of edges in
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
. Using Theorem
3.9
, this information is enough to determine
f
as a graph-like distribution.

(2)

If
\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)
then
\alpha\in f(\Delta)
if and only if
\Delta\subseteq g_{1}(\alpha)
and
\Delta
induces a complete subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
. One consequence of this is that we know that the set of formulas
\{\varphi_{\beta}(x,\bar{r}_{\beta}(\alpha)):\beta\in\Delta\}
is satisfiable in
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
if (but usually not only if)
\Delta
induces a complete subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
.

It will be useful to record some translations of concepts coming from distributions in terms of the graph sequence. As the distribution graph sequence contains all of the information of a graph-like distribution, one of the translations we will get is a version of Lemma
3.12
in terms of the distribution graph sequence instead of the conjugate. We will also see how to characterize refinements and multiplicative distributions in terms of the distribution graph sequence.

Lemma 4.3
.

A sequence of finite graphs
(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
where the set of vertices of each
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}=(G_{\alpha},E_{\alpha})
is a subset of
\lambda
is a distribution graph sequence of a monotone graph-like distribution
f
for the
\lambda
-type
p(\bar{x})
in
\widehat{M}=\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}
with the representatives
(\bar{r}_{\beta})_{\beta\in\lambda}
if and only if the sequence
(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

If
\Delta\subseteq G_{\alpha}
induces a complete subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
then

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\vDash\exists\bar{x},\bigwedge_{\beta\in\Delta}\varphi_{\beta}(\bar{x},\bar{r}_{\beta}(\alpha)).

(2)

For all
\Delta\in[\lambda]^{2}
the set
\{\alpha\in\lambda:\Delta\in E_{\alpha}\}
is an element of
\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

Set
g_{1}(\alpha)=G_{\alpha}
and
g_{2}(\alpha)=E_{\alpha}
. We claim that
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
satisfy the conditions of Lemma
3.12
to determine a graph-like distribution for
p(\bar{x})
. The conditions of Lemma
3.12
(1)
–
(2)
are satisfied because of condition
(1)
above. That is, single vertices induce complete subgraphs and any
\Delta\subseteq g_{1}(\alpha)
induces a complete subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
with
|\Delta|\geq 2
if and only if every pair of elements of
\Delta
has an edge between them in
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
if and only if
[\Delta]^{2}\subseteq g_{2}(\alpha)
.
The condition Lemma
3.12
(3) is satisfied as the edge set of a graph must be a subset of the two element subsets of the set of vertices. The condition Lemma
3.12
(4) is implied by the condition
(2)
above using the fact that
E_{\alpha}=g_{2}(\alpha)
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose that
(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
is the distribution graph sequence of a monotone graph-like distribution
f
for the type
p(\bar{x})
. Then a sequence of finite graphs
(\mathbb{H}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
is the graph distribution sequence of a graph-like refinement of
f
if and only if
\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}
is a subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha<\lambda
and for all
\Delta\in[\lambda]^{2}
the set
\{\alpha\in\lambda:\Delta\in E^{\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}}\}
is an element of
\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

(
\Leftarrow
) The condition in Lemma
4.3
(1) is preserved by subgraphs and the condition in Lemma
4.3
(2) is assumed to be true of
(\mathbb{H}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
, so
(\mathbb{H}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
is the distribution graph sequence of some distribution
\ell
. We wish to check that
\ell
is a refinement of
f
. Let
(h_{n})_{n\in\omega}
be the conjugate of
\ell
and
\Delta
a fixed element of
\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)
. Suppose that
\epsilon\in\ell(\Delta)
or, equivalently,
\Delta\in h_{|\Delta|}(\epsilon)
. If
|\Delta|\geq 2
, then
[\Delta]^{2}\subseteq h_{2}(\epsilon)
by Lemma
3.10
(2) and
h_{2}(\epsilon)\subseteq g_{2}(\epsilon)
because the set of edges in
\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}
is a subset of the set of edges in
\mathbb{G}_{\epsilon}
. Now using Lemma
3.10
(2) on
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
yields
\Delta\in g_{|\Delta|}(\epsilon)
, which is equivalent to
\epsilon\in f(\Delta)
, so
\ell(\Delta)\subseteq f(\Delta)
. The case where
|\Delta|=1
follows from the set of vertices of
\mathbb{H}_{\epsilon}
being a subset of the set of vertices of
\mathbb{G}_{\epsilon}
.

(
\Rightarrow
) We know that if
(\mathbb{H}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
is the distribution graph sequence of graph-like distribution then
(\mathbb{H}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
must satisfy Lemma
4.3
(2), so for any
\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)
we have that
\{\alpha\in\lambda:\Delta\in E^{\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}}\}
is an element of
\mathcal{D}
. Let
\ell\colon\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)\to\mathcal{D}
be the distribution whose distribution graph-sequence is
(\mathbb{H}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
. Then for all
\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)
we have that
\ell(\Delta)\subseteq f(\Delta)
, so
\Delta\in h_{|\Delta|}(\alpha)
implies
\Delta\in g_{|\Delta|}(\alpha)
. Thus
h_{n}(\alpha)\subseteq g_{n}(\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in\lambda
and
n\in\omega
, which, when applied with
n=1,2
, implies that
\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}
is a subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose that
(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
is the distribution graph sequence of a graph-like distribution
f
for the type
p(\bar{x})
. Then
f
is multiplicative if and only if
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
is complete for every
\alpha\in\lambda
.

Proof.

From Corollary
3.4
we know that
f
is multiplicative if and only if
f(\{\beta,\gamma\})=f(\{\beta\})\cap f(\{\gamma\})
for all distinct
\beta,\gamma\in\lambda
as we have already assumed that
f
is graph-like. That is,
\{\beta,\gamma\}\in g_{2}(\alpha)
if and only if
\beta,\gamma\in g_{1}(\alpha)
where
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
is the conjugate of
f
. This is equivalent to asking that
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
is a complete graph.
∎

Because we get a finite graph
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
for each index
\alpha<\lambda
and because the graphs were ultimately derived from an ultraproduct, it would make sense to take the ultraproduct of all the
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
and explore what this hyperfinite graph can tell us about the original type
p(\bar{x})
. There is a slight technicality here; the distribution graph sequence may be empty in some indices, making the ultraproduct empty. However, most of the elements (according to
\mathcal{D}
) of the distribution graph sequence must be nonempty, so we can take the ultraproduct of just the nonempty elements of the sequence.

Definition 4.6
.

Suppose that
f
is a distribution. Then the
essential range of
f
is the set

\mathrm{ess}(f):=\bigcup_{\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)}f(\Delta).

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose that
f
is a graph-like distribution with distribution graph sequence
(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
. Then

(1)

\mathrm{ess}(f)\in\mathcal{D}
and

(2)

\mathrm{ess}(f)=\{\alpha\in\lambda:\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\neq\langle\emptyset,\emptyset\rangle\}
.

Proof.

(1)
: Pick
\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)
. Then
f(\Delta)\subseteq\mathrm{ess}(f)
and
f(\Delta)\in\mathcal{D}
.

(2)
: Let
g_{1},g_{2}
determine the conjugate of
f
.
From the definition of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
, we have that
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\neq\langle\emptyset,\emptyset\rangle
if and only if
g_{1}(\alpha)\neq\emptyset
. From the definition of the conjugate,
g_{1}(\alpha)=\emptyset
if and only if
\alpha\notin f(\{\gamma\})
for all
\gamma\in\lambda
. By the monotonicity of
f
,
\alpha\notin f(\{\gamma\})
implies that
\alpha\notin f(\Delta)
for any
\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)
with
\gamma\in\Delta
. Thus

\{\alpha:\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\neq\langle\emptyset,\emptyset\rangle\}\subseteq\mathrm{ess}(f).

Conversely, if
\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(f)
, then
\alpha\in f(\Delta)
for some
\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(\lambda)
. By the monotonicity of
f
, if
\gamma\in\Delta
, then
\alpha\in f(\{\gamma\})
. We then follow the same chain of if and only ifs in the previous paragraph to get the other inclusion.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

Suppose that
f
is a distribution and
I=\mathrm{ess}(f)
. Then

(1)

If
A\subseteq\lambda
then
A\in\mathcal{D}
if and only if
A\cap I\in\mathcal{D}
.

(2)

The set
I
has cardinality
\lambda
.

(3)

The set

\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}I=\{A\in\mathcal{P}(I):A\in\mathcal{D}\}=\{A\cap I:A\in\mathcal{D}\}

is a regular ultrafilter on
I
.

Proof.

Statement
(1)
and the fact that
\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}I
is an ultrafilter on
I
are well known to hold for
I\in\mathcal{D}
. Checking that restriction preserves regularity follows from the associativity of general intersections. The set
I
must have cardinality
\lambda
since
I\in\mathcal{D}
and all regular ultrafilters are uniform.
∎

Convention 4.9
.

Suppose
f
is a distribution for the type
p(\bar{x})
and
I=\mathrm{ess}(f)
.
If
p(\bar{x})
is a type of
\widehat{M}=\mathcal{M}_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{D}}
then we will use the same symbol
p
for the type in

whose parameters are the projections of the parameters of

Definition 4.10
.

Let
f
be a graph-like distribution with distribution graph sequence
(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
and
I=\mathrm{ess}(f)
. Then the
ultragraph of

is the graph
\mathbb{G}_{f}=\mathbb{G}_{I}^{\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}I}
.

Since the element

represents the realizability of the formula

in
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
(see e.g. Lemma

(1)), we should expect that the equivalence class of the constant function

in

in

. Unfortunately,

will frequently not be an element of

for all

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
f
be a graph-like distribution with distribution graph sequence
(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
and
I=\mathrm{ess}(f)
. Then there is a unique injective function
{\eta:\lambda\to\mathbb{G}_{f}}
such that if
(s_{\beta})_{\beta\in I}
has the property that for all
\beta\in I
we have that
s_{\beta}\in\prod_{\alpha\in I}g_{1}(\alpha)
and
{s_{\beta}/(\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}I)=\eta(\beta)}
, then the set
\{\alpha\in I:s_{\beta}(\alpha)=\beta\}
is an element of
\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}I
.

Proof.

For each
\beta\in\lambda
define a function
h_{\beta}\in\prod_{\alpha\in I}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
so that

(4.1)

h_{\beta}(\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\beta,&\text{if }\beta\in G_{\alpha}\\
\min(G_{\alpha}),&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

for all
\alpha\in I
.
We note that

\{\alpha\in\lambda:\beta\in G_{\alpha}\}=f(\{\beta\})\in\mathcal{D}
and
f(\{\beta\})\subseteq I

so, whenever
\beta,\gamma\in\lambda
with
\beta\neq\gamma
, we have that

\{\alpha\in I:h_{\beta}(\alpha)\neq h_{\gamma}(\alpha)\}\supseteq f(\{\beta\})\cap f(\{\gamma\})\in\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}I.

By Łoś theorem, the equivalence classes of
h_{\beta}
and
h_{\gamma}
are not equal in
\mathbb{G}_{f}
and the function
\eta(\beta)=h_{\beta}/(\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}I)
is injective.

Now suppose that
(s_{\beta})_{\beta\in I}
is such that
s_{\beta}/(\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}I)=\eta(\beta)
. Then, since

A=\{\alpha\in I:h_{\beta}(\alpha)=\beta\}\in\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}I\text{ and }B=\{\alpha\in I:h_{\beta}(\alpha)=s_{\beta}(\alpha)\}\in\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}I

we have that

\{\alpha\in I:s_{\beta}(\alpha)=\beta\}\supseteq A\cap B\in\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}I.

We note that this property determines the value of
\eta(\beta)
in
\mathbb{G}_{f}
.
∎

In Equation (
4.1
), the choice of the element of
G_{\alpha}
in the case that
\beta\notin G_{\alpha}
does not affect the value of
\eta(\beta)
as the collection of indices in which we have to make this choice is not in
\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}\mathrm{ess}(f)
.

Convention 4.12
.

For any distribution

we will fix

to be the unique function guaranteed by using Lemma

.

5.
Complete Subgraphs of
\mathbb{G}_{f}

Here we take a slight departure from talking about the type
p(\bar{x})
specifically in order to get a feeling for what sort of information is contained in
\mathbb{G}_{f}
and how that information might be accessed. One of our main results in this section is that the distribution
f
has a multiplicative refinement if and only if the subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{f}
induced by
\eta_{f}[\lambda]
sits inside an internal complete subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{f}
(see Corollary
5.10
). More generally, we show how to take a complete subgraph
H
of
\mathbb{G}_{f}
of cardinality
\lambda
and shuffle the vertices of
\mathbb{G}_{f}
so that
\eta_{f}[\lambda]
is moved to
H
and so that the number of edges we need to remove from the domain copy of
\mathbb{G}_{f}
to make this rearrangement a homomorphism is small enough to still have the domain copy of
\mathbb{G}_{f}
be an ultragraph for a distribution
f_{H}
refining
f
. We also show, under some assumptions on
H
, that
f_{H}
is a distribution for a new type
q(\bar{x})
that is related to
H
. Finally, we show that
f=f_{\eta_{f}[\lambda]}
and that
f_{H}
has a multiplicative refinement if and only if
H\subseteq K\subseteq\mathbb{G}_{f}
where
K
is an
internal
complete subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{f}
. We consider complete subgraphs of
\mathbb{G}_{f}
other than
\eta_{f}[\lambda]
in order to show that there is a strong connection between such graphs and
\lambda
-types of
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
that have distributions that are refinements of
f
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
f
be a distribution with conjugate
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
and let

-equivalence class of any function where

Remarks 5.2
.

(1)

Given an element
h\in\prod_{\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(f)}g_{1}(\alpha)

is well-defined because the representation of
\bar{r}_{h}
given in the definition is defined for each

and

.

(2)

The prototypical example of the sort of

that we wish to consider are the

defined by Equation (
4.1
) in Lemma

. Our main use of this definition will be to take
\lambda

and treat a given set of their representations as if they were

.

(3)

The condition that there exists a

is not only to guarantee that we can say that the length of

is finite according to

as a parameter in the formula

.

(4)

The condition that there is such a

becomes trivial in the case that

for all

(in this case we say that

is a
\varphi
-type). This case is what occurs for the types we consider later (see e.g. the definition of

-types in Definition

). More generally, it was shown by Malliaris in
[
4
]
that the study of Keisler’s order can be reduced to the study of whether

-types are realized in regular ultrapowers.

(5)

The choice of

such that

is unique in the sense that

will also have

if and only if

.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
f
be a graph-like distribution with conjugate determined by
g_{1},g_{2}
and with graph distribution sequence
(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
and ultragraph
\mathbb{G}_{f}=\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{ess}(f)}^{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}
where
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}\mathrm{ess}(f)
. Let

induce a complete subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{f}
. If

(1)

H^{\prime}
is a set of representatives for the elements of

is a subset of
\prod_{\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(f)}g_{1}(\alpha)

(2)

each element of

, and

(3)

for each

there exists a

B_{h}:=\{\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(f):\varphi_{h(\alpha)}(\bar{x};\bar{y})=\varphi_{\beta_{h}}(\bar{x};\bar{y})\}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}

(5.1)

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}

Proof.

By the characterization of a type in an ultraproduct, it will be enough to show that
\llbracket\Delta\rrbracket_{q(\bar{x})}^{\mathcal{D}}\in\mathcal{D}
for every finite
\Delta\subseteq H^{\prime}
. For each
\Delta\in\mathcal{P}_{\omega}(H^{\prime})
define

\llbracket\Delta\rrbracket_{q(\bar{x})}^{\mathcal{D}}\supseteq\Big\{\alpha\in B_{\Delta}:\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\vDash\exists x,\bigwedge_{h\in\Delta}\varphi_{h}(x,\bar{r}_{h(\alpha)}(\alpha))\Big\}=:\llbracket\Delta\rrbracket^{B_{\Delta}}_{q(\bar{x})}

where
\bar{r}_{h}
is the parameter induced by

. For all

define
\Delta(\alpha):=\{h(\alpha):h\in\Delta\}
. If
|\Delta|\geq 2
, define

(5.2)

K_{\Delta}:=\{\alpha\in B_{\Delta}:[\Delta(\alpha)]^{2}\subseteq g_{2}(\alpha)\}.

The set

is the set of indices
\alpha
for which
\Delta(\alpha)
induces a complete subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
. Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}\mathrm{ess}(f)
. Then, because
\Delta
induces a finite complete subgraph of

after modding out by

, we have that

\mathbb{G}_{f}\vDash\bigwedge_{\{h_{1},h_{2}\}\in[\Delta]^{2}}\left(h_{1}/\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\mathrel{E}h_{2}/\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\right)\vee\left(h_{1}/\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=h_{2}/\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\right),

so, by Łoś theorem we have that

\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\vDash\bigwedge_{\{h_{1},h_{2}\}\in[\Delta(\alpha)]^{2}}\left(h_{1}(\alpha)\mathrel{E}h_{2}(\alpha)\right)\vee\left(h_{1}(\alpha)=h_{2}(\alpha)\right)

for
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
-many

, and hence
\mathcal{D}
-many

by Corollary
4.8
(1). Because

, we have that

is an element of

. By Lemma

.

The case of

.
∎

Remarks 5.4
.

(1)

The converse to the above lemma is not true in general. There may be types of the same form as
q(\bar{x})
(that is, types with representations
(h_{\beta})_{\beta<\lambda}
for the parameters coming from
\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}\{r_{\beta}(\alpha):\beta<\lambda\}
and with
\beta_{h}
’s) but such that
\{h_{\beta}/\mathcal{D}:\beta<\lambda\}
does not induce a complete subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{f}
. This can be thought of as saying that
f
carries enough information to recognize that
p(\bar{x})
is a type, but generally does not carry enough information to recognize that all types with similar representations for their parameters are types. There is a converse of this sort when
i_{f}[\mathbb{G}_{f}]
is an induced subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{L}
(the argument is similar to the proof of Corollary
6.15
). See Section 6 for the definitions of
i_{f}
and
\mathbb{G}_{L}
.

(2)

The type
q(\bar{x})
does not depend on the choice of the representatives in
H^{\prime}
. If there are two representatives
h,h^{\prime}
for the same element of
\mathbb{G}_{f}
they must be equal at
\mathcal{D}
-many indices, the representations for
\bar{r}_{h}
and
\bar{r}_{h^{\prime}}
must agree at
\mathcal{D}
-many indices, and hence
\bar{r}_{h}
and
\bar{r}_{h^{\prime}}
are equal. A similar argument shows that
\beta_{h}=\beta_{h^{\prime}}
in this case.

We wish to study the connection between induced complete subgraphs of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{ess}(f)}^{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}=\mathbb{G}_{f}
and the types of the form given in Equation (
5.1
). In order to gain an idea of where this analysis will go, recall that complete subgraphs of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
indicate subsets of
p(\bar{x})
that, when viewed in the
\alpha
-th index structure, are satisfiable (Lemma
4.3
(1)). A major reason why we are able to make this conclusion is that each
g_{1}(\alpha)
is finite. If
g_{1}(\alpha)
were
not
finite we would still be able to say that an infinite complete subgraph corresponds to a finitely satisfiable set of formula in the
\alpha
-th index structure (i.e. corresponds to a type) but there is no reason to assume that such a type is realized. Take, for example, the type
\{0<x<1/n:n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}\}
in the dense linear ordering
\mathbb{Q}
. The satisfiability graph for this type is complete, but there is no realization of the type in
\mathbb{Q}
. Since the type
p(\bar{x})
corresponds to the (infinite!) image of
\eta_{f}
, why should we expect that we will get any information from
\mathbb{G}_{f}
other than the observation that
p(\bar{x})
is a type?

If we treat
\mathbb{G}_{f}
as an arbitrary graph and forget that it comes from an ultraproduct of finite graphs then we cannot extract any more information about whether
p(\bar{x})
is realized. However, if we do remember this extra information, we then find that Łoś theorem tells us that
\mathbb{G}_{f}
has many of the features of a finite graph so long as we restrict ourselves to looking at internal parts of the structure. We continue by exploring this interaction of apparent finiteness of internal subsets and the types defined in the previous lemma, after taking care of a few technical details allowing us to pinpoint particular distributions for the types described in Lemma
5.3
.

Lemma 5.5

if and only if

\{\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(f):\{h_{j(\alpha)}(\alpha),h_{k(\alpha)}(\alpha)\}\in g_{1}(\alpha)\cup g_{2}(\alpha)\}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}.

By the definition of
\ell_{2}
, this is equivalent to
\{j(\alpha),k(\alpha)\}\in\ell_{2}(\alpha)
or
j(\alpha)=k(\alpha)
for
\mathcal{D}_{2}
-many
\alpha
in
\mathrm{ess}(f)
(by the definition of
\mathbb{G}_{f_{H^{\prime}}}
we have
j(\alpha)
,
k(\alpha)\in\ell_{1}(\alpha)
), which is equivalent to
j/\mathcal{D}_{2}
and
k/\mathcal{D}_{2}
being edge related in
\mathrm{loop}(\mathbb{G}_{f_{H^{\prime}}})
. As each step in this argument is an equivalence, we must have that
\Psi(j/\mathcal{D}_{2})
is edge related to
\Psi(k/\mathcal{D}_{2})
in
\mathrm{loop}(\mathbb{G}_{f})
if and only if
j/\mathcal{D}_{2}
and
k/\mathcal{D}_{2}
are edge related in
\mathrm{loop}(\mathbb{G}_{f_{H^{\prime}}})
.

(2)
Now that we know that

is a graph homomorphism that also preserves non-edges, showing that
\Psi\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}\eta_{f_{H^{\prime}}}[\lambda]
is an isomorphism amounts to showing that the restriction is bijective. Having that

for some

is equivalent, by Lemma
4.11
, to having that

\{\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(f):j(\alpha)=\beta\}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}.

Then the statement

is equivalent to

\{\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(f):k(\alpha)=h_{\beta}(\alpha)\}\in\mathcal{D}_{1},

which is equivalent by Łoś theorem to

. As this argument consists of a series of “if and only if” statements, we have that

is bijective when restricted to
\eta_{f_{H^{\prime}}}[\lambda]
.
∎

Convention 5.7
.

We continue to make the distinction between
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
as defined above. However, since
\mathcal{D}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{1}
and

if and only if

, we will treat the statements

and

as if they were equivalent and do not spend time worrying about how to translate between the statements.

In order to get to the result that
\Psi
allows some transfer of information between the types

and
q(\bar{x})
, we will first prove that “nice enough” homomorphisms between ultraproducts of graphs using essentially the same ultrafilter preserve internal subsets and complete subsets. The “nice enough” referers to functions that are what non-standard analysts would call “internal functions” that are either homomorphisms or preserve non-edges. The following proof is well-known in non-standard analysis, but we feel that it is worth presenting here.

Lemma 5.8
.

Suppose that
I
and
J
are sets with
J\subseteq I
and
(\mathbb{G}_{i})_{i\in I}
and
(\mathbb{H}_{j})_{j\in J}
are sequences of graphs. Further suppose that
\mathcal{D}_{I}
is an ultrafilter on
I
and
\mathcal{D}_{J}
is an ultrafilter on
J
with
\mathcal{D}_{J}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{I}
and that there is a sequence of functions
(\theta_{j})_{j\in J}
where
\theta_{j}\colon\mathbb{H}_{j}\to\mathbb{G}_{j}
for each
j\in J
. Define

\mathbb{G}:=\prod_{i\in I}\mathbb{G}_{i}/\mathcal{D}_{I}\,\text{ and }\,\mathbb{H}:=\prod_{j\in J}\mathbb{H}_{j}/\mathcal{D}_{J}.

Then

(1)

The function
\Theta:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{G}
defined for all
p/\mathcal{D}_{J}
in
\mathbb{H}
by

\Theta(p/\mathcal{D}_{J}):=\left[i\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\theta_{i}(p(i));&\text{if }i\in J\\
\text{any element of }\mathbb{G}_{i};&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.\right]\bigg/\mathcal{D}_{I}

is well-defined and unique,

(2)

if
A\subseteq\mathbb{G}
and
B\subseteq\mathbb{H}
are internal then so are
\Theta[B]
and
\Theta^{-1}[A]
,

(3)

if
\{j\in J:\theta_{j}\text{ is a graph homomorphism}\}
is an element of
\mathcal{D}_{J}
and
K\subseteq\mathbb{H}
induces a complete subgraph of
\mathbb{H}
then
\Theta[K]
induces a complete subgraph of
\mathbb{G}
, and

(4)

if
\{j\in J:\theta_{j}\text{ preserves non-edges as a map }\mathbb{H}_{j}\to\mathrm{loop}(\mathbb{G}_{j})\}
is an element of
\mathcal{D}_{J}
and
K\subseteq\mathbb{G}
induces a complete subgraph of
\mathbb{G}
then
\Theta^{-1}[K]
is a complete subgraph of
\mathbb{H}
.

Proof.

(1)
: If
p/\mathcal{D}_{J}=p^{\prime}/\mathcal{D}_{J}
, then
p(i)=p^{\prime}(i)
for
\mathcal{D}_{j}
-many
i\in J
, so
\theta_{i}(p(i))=\theta_{i}(p^{\prime}(i))
for
\mathcal{D}_{J}
-many
i\in J
as well. Since
\mathcal{D}_{J}\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{I}
, this is enough to show well-definedness and uniqueness.

(2)
: We start with
\Theta[B]
. As
B
is internal, we can write
B
as an ultraproduct of the form

\prod_{j\in J}B_{j}/\mathcal{D}_{J}

where each
B_{j}\subseteq H_{j}
. Define for each
i\in J
the set

B^{\prime}_{i}=\{\theta_{i}(h):h\in B_{i}\}

and for
i\in I\setminus J
define
B^{\prime}_{i}=G_{i}
.
We claim that
\Theta[B]=\prod_{i\in I}B^{\prime}_{i}/\mathcal{D}_{I}=:\widehat{B}
. Suppose that
p\in\prod_{i\in I}B^{\prime}_{i}
. Then

(\theta_{i}(p(i)))_{i\in I}/\mathcal{D}_{I}=\Theta((p\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}J)/\mathcal{D}_{J}).

As every element of
\mathbb{H}
can be written as
\Theta((p\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}J)/\mathcal{D}_{J})
for some
p\in\prod_{i\in I}B^{\prime}_{i}
, we have that
\Theta[B]=\widehat{B}
and so is internal.

The proof that
\Theta^{-1}[A]
is internal in
\mathbb{G}
is essentially the same after swapping
A
and
B
,
I
and
J
, and
\Theta
and
\Theta^{-1}
.

(3)
: Suppose that
k_{1},k_{2}
are representatives for distinct elements of
K
. Then
\theta_{j}(k_{1}(j))
and
\theta_{j}(k_{2}(j))
are edge related for
\mathcal{D}_{J}
-many
j\in J
. Then the representatives given for
\Theta(k_{1})
and
\Theta(k_{2})
are edge related for
\mathcal{D}_{I}
-many
i\in I
. In particular,
\Theta
is a graph homomorphism.

(4)
: As in
(3)
,
\Theta\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathrm{loop}(\mathbb{G})
will inherit the property that non-edges are preserved. Suppose that
k_{1},k_{2}
are distinct elements of
\Theta^{-1}[K]
. Then either there is an edge or a loop between
\Theta(k_{1})
and
\Theta(k_{2})
, so
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
must be edge related in
\mathbb{H}
.
∎

Theorem 5.9
.

Let

be a graph-like distribution for the

-type

with graph distribution sequence

\Rightarrow

.

A graph-like distribution

for

has a multiplicative refinement if and only if the image of

in

is a subset of an internal complete subgraph.

Let

and

where the

are the functions defined in Equation (

). Then

is a transversal of

and

and type

defined in Lemma

are equal to

and

respectively. The desired result then follows from Theorem

. To check that

by Theorem

6.
Graph-like Types

The particular types (
\mathrm{SOP}_{2}
-types and types expressing that cuts in linear orders are filled) that we wish to study are not only special in that there are distributions for the type that are graph-like but that, for certain choices of the representations
\bar{r}_{\beta}\in\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
,
every
distribution for the type can be extended to one that is graph-like. As we will see in Lemma
6.6
, this special property is related to the fact that the Łoś map exhibits graph-like behavior.

Convention 6.1
.

From now on, we will assume that
p(\bar{x})
is a
(\varphi,\lambda)
-type (that is, both a
\varphi
-type and a
\lambda
-type) unless otherwise stated.

Definition 6.2
.

The type
p(\bar{x})
is called a

if there is a choice of representatives
(\bar{r}_{\beta})_{\beta<\lambda}
for the parameters of
p(\bar{x})
so that

p(\bar{x})
with this choice of representatives, satisfies

L(\Delta)=\bigcap_{\Phi\in[\Delta]^{2}}L(\Phi)

for all finite
\Delta\subseteq\lambda
with
|\Delta|\geq 2
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Every type of the form

of the ultraproduct
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=\prod_{\beta<\lambda}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}

is realized in

If
f
is a multiplicative distribution of
p(\bar{x})
with parameters given by
(\bar{r}_{\beta})_{\beta<\lambda}
, then
f
is graph-like by Lemma
3.2
and every graph in its distribution graph sequence is complete by Lemma
4.5
. A choice of representatives that witness

being a graph-like type is given by

\bar{s}_{\beta}(\alpha)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bar{r}_{h_{\beta}(\alpha)}(\alpha);&\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(f)\\
\bar{\gamma}_{\alpha};&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

where the

are the functions in

defined in Equation (

) and

is a fixed tuple of parameters from

L(\Delta)=\mathrm{ess}(f)\cup\big\{\alpha\in\lambda\setminus\{\mathrm{ess}(f)\}:\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\vDash\exists\bar{x},\,\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{\gamma}_{\alpha})\big\}

for all

In particular, over a

-type that is also a

-type is graph-like
. Thus, if being graph-like is not a trivial property, it must depend on the ultrafilter

being considered. We are most interested in types that are graph-like independent of the choice of

as this would appear to reveal something about the underlying structure of the type and the complete theory from which it came.

To continue the theme of investigating the ways in which the Łoś map acts in ways similar to a distribution and to rephrase Lemma

Corollary 6.7

behaves almost as if it were a maximal graph-like distribution. In particular, we can construct an ultraproduct of graphs using the Łoś map as a template just as we did with graph-like distributions, and this ultragraph will contain all of the distribution ultragraphs in a compatible way (i.e. all of the embeddings of
\lambda
(representing the type

) in the various ultragraphs (see Lemma

) will coincide in the Łoś ultragraph). By using Corollary

and Lemma

is realized precisely in the case that the image of
\lambda
in the Łoś ultragraph is contained within an internal complete subgraph.

Definition 6.8
.

If
p(\bar{x})
is graph-like we say that the
Łoś ultragraph
of
p(\bar{x})
is

\mathbb{G}_{L}:=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(L)}(k_{1}(\alpha),k_{2}(\alpha))/\mathcal{D}^{\prime}

where
\mathrm{ess}(L)
is defined analogously to
\mathrm{ess}(f)
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}\mathrm{ess}(L)
.

Lemma 6.9
.

Suppose that
p(\bar{x})
is a graph-like
(\varphi,\lambda)
-type and that

is a graph-like distribution for
p(\bar{x})
with conjugate
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
. Then
\mathbb{G}_{f}
is an internal (not necessarily induced) subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{L}
. In particular, the map

induced by the inclusions
g_{1}(\alpha)\to k_{1}(\alpha)
for each
\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(f)
is an injective graph homomorphism with internal image.

Proof.

Let the conjugate of
f
be
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
. We have that
g_{1}(\alpha)\subseteq k_{1}(\alpha)
and
g_{2}(\alpha)\subseteq k_{2}(\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(f)
as
f\subseteq L
. For each
\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(f)
, let
i_{\alpha}:g_{1}(\alpha)\to k_{1}(\alpha)
be the inclusion map and note that it is a graph homomorphism. For each
\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(L)
, choose an element
\gamma_{\alpha}
in
k_{1}(\alpha)
(recall that
\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(L)
if and only if
k_{1}(\alpha)\neq\emptyset

\mathcal{D}_{1}=\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}\mathrm{ess}(L)

\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}\mathrm{ess}(f)

given for all

by the formula

(6.1)

i_{f}(j/\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\prime})=\left[\alpha\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}j(\alpha);&\text{if }\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(f)\\
\gamma_{\alpha};&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.\right]\bigg/\mathcal{D}_{1}.

As

, the function

is well-defined and injective with internal image (see Lemma
5.8
).
∎

The value of
i_{f}(j/\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\prime})
in the above proof does not depend on the choice of
\gamma_{\alpha}
as
\mathrm{ess}(f)\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
.

Lemma 6.10
.

Let
f,f^{\prime}
be two graph-like distributions for the graph-like
\varphi
-type
p(\bar{x})
using a common sequence of representatives
(\bar{r}_{\beta})_{\beta<\lambda}
for the parameters of
p(\bar{x})
. Recall the definitions of

(Lemma

(Lemma

and

\mathbb{G}_{L}
.

Proof.

Let the conjugates of
f
and
f^{\prime}
be given by
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
and
(g^{\prime}_{n})_{n\in\omega}
respectively.
Fix some
\beta\in\lambda
and let

h_{\beta}\in\prod_{\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(f)}g_{1}(\alpha)\;\text{ and }\;h^{\prime}_{\beta}\in\prod_{\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}{f^{\prime}}}g^{\prime}_{1}(\alpha)

be the representatives for
\eta_{f}(\beta)
and
\eta_{f^{\prime}}(\beta)
given by Equation (

) (applied to
\eta_{f},h_{\beta}
and
\eta_{f^{\prime}},h_{\beta}^{\prime}

\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\prime}=\mathcal{D}\text{\,}\mathord{\upharpoonright}\text{\,}\mathrm{ess}(f^{\prime})

\displaystyle\{\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(L):h_{\beta}(\alpha)=h^{\prime}_{\beta}(\alpha)\}

\displaystyle\supseteq\{\alpha\in\mathrm{ess}(L):\beta\in g_{1}(\alpha)\cap g^{\prime}_{1}(\alpha)\}

\displaystyle=f(\{\beta\})\cap f^{\prime}(\{\beta\})

Applying Łoś theorem to the representatives for

.
i_{f}\circ\eta_{f}

and
\eta_{f}
are.
∎

Definition 6.11
.

If
p(\bar{x})
is a graph-like
\varphi
-type, we will denote by
\eta_{L}
the unique map
\lambda\to\mathbb{G}_{L}
that can be written as
i_{f}\circ\eta_{f}
for any graph-like distribution
f
of
p(\bar{x})
.

Identifying the subgraph induced by
\eta_{L}[\lambda]
in
\mathbb{G}_{L}
allows us to capture much of the information contained within distributions by looking at internal subgraphs of
\mathbb{G}_{L}
that contain
\eta_{L}[\lambda]
as a complete subgraph and that are
hyperfinite
. We record a proof of this correspondence here but will not use it until the next section.

Definition 6.12
.

A subset
A
of an ultraproduct
\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}
is called
hyperfinite
if it can be expressed as an ultraproduct
\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}A_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}
where each
A_{\alpha}
is a finite subset of
M_{\alpha}
. We say that a structure is
hyperfinite
if its underlying set is hyperfinite.

A hyperfinite subset of an ultraproduct
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}
is necessarily an internal subset of
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
as every hyperfinite subset is expressible as an ultraproduct of subsets of
M_{\alpha}
.

Theorem 6.13
.

Let
p(\bar{x})
be a graph-like type with Łoś conjugate
(k_{n})_{n\in\omega}
and let
H\subseteq\mathbb{G}_{L}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

H
is hyperfinite with
\eta_{L}[\lambda]\subseteq H
.

(2)

There is a graph-like distribution
f
for
p(\bar{x})
such that
i_{f}[G_{f}]=H
.

Moreover, if
H=\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}H_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}
with each
H_{\alpha}
finite, then
f
can be taken so that
i_{f}[\mathbb{G}_{f}]
is the subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{L}
induced by
H
and so that, if
(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
is the distribution graph sequence of
f
, the graph
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
is the subgraph of
\langle k_{1}(\alpha),k_{2}(\alpha)\rangle
induced by
H_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha<\lambda
.

Proof.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
: The ultragraph
\mathbb{G}_{f}
is hyperfinite because the distribution graph sequence of
f
consists of finite graphs. Let
i_{\alpha}
be the inclusion
g_{1}(\alpha)\to k_{1}(\alpha)
. Then the image of
i_{f}
is equal to
\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}i_{\alpha}[G_{\alpha}]/\mathcal{D}
, so we have that the set
H=i_{f}[G_{f}]
is hyperfinite. This is a specific case of internal functions preserving hyperfinite sets.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: Let
\mathbb{H}=\langle H,E^{\mathbb{H}}\rangle
be the subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{L}
induced by
H
and pick finite
H_{\alpha}\subseteq k_{1}(\alpha)
so that
H=\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}H_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}
. Then
\mathbb{H}=\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}
where each
\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}
is the finite subgraph of
\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}:=\langle k_{1}(\alpha),k_{2}(\alpha)\rangle
induced by
H_{\alpha}
. We claim that
(\mathbb{H}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
is the distribution graph sequence of a graph-like distribution
f
of
p(\bar{x})
. We verify that
(\mathbb{H}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
6.5
(
g_{1}(\alpha)=H_{\alpha}
and
g_{2}(\alpha)=E^{\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}}
following the definition of a graph distribution sequence).

Conditions
(1)
-
(3)
of Lemma
6.5
are equivalent to
\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}
being a subgraph of
\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}
given that
p(\bar{x})
is graph-like. Condition
(4)
is equivalent to
\eta_{L}[\lambda]
inducing a complete subgraph in
\mathbb{H}
, which is equivalent to
\eta_{L}[\lambda]
inducing a complete subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{L}
.

The result now follows from the fact that
i_{f}
is the inclusion of
\mathbb{H}
in
\mathbb{G}_{L}
.
∎

Theorem 6.14
.

Suppose that
p(\bar{x})
is a graph-like
\varphi
- and
\lambda
-type with Łoś map
L
. Then
p(\bar{x})
is realized in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
if and only if the image of
\eta_{L}:\lambda\to\mathbb{G}_{L}
is a subset of an internal
A\subseteq G_{L}
that induces a complete subgraph.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
): The type
p(\bar{x})
is realized if and only if there is a multiplicative distribution
j
for
p(\bar{x})
by Fact
2.9
. Then, by Corollary
3.4
,
j
is a graph-like distribution for
p(\bar{x})
and, by Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
6.9
, with
\mathbb{G}_{j}
being an internal complete subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{L}
. Moreover, by Lemma
6.10
,
i_{j}\circ\eta_{j}=\eta_{L}
so
i_{j}[\mathbb{G}_{j}]
contains the image of
\eta_{L}
. Thus
i_{j}[\mathbb{G}_{j}]
is an internal subset of
\mathbb{G}_{L}
extending
\eta_{L}[\lambda]
and inducing a complete subgraph.

(
\Leftarrow
): By Lemma
6.10
we have that
\eta_{f}[\lambda]=\eta_{L}[\lambda]
for any graph-like distribution
f
of
p(\bar{x})
. Using Corollary
5.10
then shows that every graph-like distribution has a multiplicative refinement, which is equivalent to
p(\bar{x})
being realized by Fact
2.9
.
∎

Corollary 6.15
.

If
p(\bar{x})
is a graph-like type and
f
is a graph-like distribution such that
i_{f}[\mathbb{G}_{f}]
is an induced subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{L}
then the type
p(\bar{x})
is realized if and only if
f
has a multiplicative refinement.

Proof.

(\Rightarrow)
: If
p(\bar{x})
is realized, then, by Theorem
6.14
, there is a complete internal subgraph
\mathbb{K}
of
\mathbb{G}_{L}
containing
\eta_{L}[\lambda]=i_{f}\circ\eta_{f}[\lambda]
. We must have that
i_{f}
preserves non-edges because the image of
i_{f}
is an induced subgraph and we already know that
i_{f}
is injective. By Lemma
5.8
(2) and (4) and the internal definition of

, we have that
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}:=i_{f}^{-1}[\mathbb{K}]
is a complete internal subgraph of
\mathbb{G}_{f}
containing
\eta_{f}[\lambda]
. Thus, by Corollary
5.10
, the distribution

has a multiplicative refinement.

(\Leftarrow)
: If
f
has a multiplicative refinement, then
p(\bar{x})
is realized by Fact
2.9
.
∎

Corollary 6.16
.

For a graph-like type
p(\bar{x})
with representatives witnessing

being graph-like,
p(\bar{x})
is realized in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
if and only if every distribution
f
extends to a distribution
\hat{f}
that has a multiplicative refinement. Moreover, the extension
\hat{f}
can be taken so that if
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
is the conjugate of

and
(\hat{g}_{n})_{n\in\omega}
is the conjugate of
\hat{f}
then

(1)

g_{1}(\alpha)=\hat{g}_{1}(\alpha)
and

(2)

\langle\hat{g}_{1}(\alpha),\hat{g}_{2}(\alpha)\rangle
is an induced subgraph of
\langle k_{1}(\alpha),k_{2}(\alpha)\rangle
where
(k_{n})_{n\in\omega}
is the Łoś conjugate of

.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
): Let
\hat{g}_{1}(\alpha)=g_{1}(\alpha)
and
\hat{g}_{2}(\alpha)=k_{2}(\alpha)\cap[g_{1}(\alpha)]^{2}
. Then
\mathbb{G}_{\hat{f}}
is an induced subgraph of

and we can use Corollary

.

(
\Leftarrow

.
∎

7.
Graph-like Formula Sets and their Shapes

It is frequently useful (as in the definition of
\mathrm{SOP}_{2}
-types) to restrict attention to certain subsets of the possible parameters for a
\varphi
-type by specifying where the representatives for the parameters can come from in each index structure
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}
. We will generally choose these parameters to be such that the resulting
\varphi
-types are guaranteed to be graph-like. We begin with some definitions that allow us to talk about this situation more concretely.

Definition 7.1
.

Given a formula
\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{y})
in a language
\mathcal{L}
, a
\varphi
-set
is a nonempty collection
A\subseteq M^{n}
where
M
is the universe of some structure
\mathcal{M}
in the language
\mathcal{L}
and the length of the tuple
\bar{y}
is
n
.

Note: unless explicitly stated, we will not require
\mathcal{M}
to be an ultraproduct.

Definition 7.2
.

If
\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
is a formula and
A
is a
\varphi
-set in some structure
\mathcal{M}
then we call
A
a
graph-like
\varphi
-set
if for all
B\subseteq A
such that
2\leq|B|<\aleph_{0}
we have that the set
\{\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{a}):\bar{a}\in B\}
is consistent if and only if for all
C\in[B]^{2}
the set
\{\varphi(\bar{x};\bar{a}):\bar{a}\in C\}
is consistent.

We study what the collection of graphs arising from these types look like and what these collections can tell us about the possible range of types that can be created by using parameters from
A
.

Definition 7.3
.

If
A
is a graph-like
\varphi
-set coming from the structure
\mathcal{M}
then
the shape of
A
, denoted
\mathrm{Sh}(A)
, is defined to be the set of pairs
(\mathbb{G},\theta)
where

(1)

there is an
m\in\omega\setminus\{0\}
so that
\mathbb{G}
is a finite simple graph with vertices
\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
,

(2)

\theta
is a function
G\to A
,

(3)

for each
i\in G
we have that
\mathcal{M}\vDash\exists\bar{x},\,\varphi(\bar{x};\theta(i))
, and

(4)

there is an edge between distinct
i
and
j
in
\mathbb{G}
if and only if

\mathcal{M}\vDash\exists\bar{x},\,\varphi(\bar{x};\theta(i))\wedge\varphi(\bar{x};\theta(j)).

Definition 7.4
.

Let
(A_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
be a sequence of
\varphi
-sets in the structures
(\mathcal{M}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
. We say that a
\varphi
-type
p(\bar{x})
of
\widehat{M}=\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}
is an
(A_{\alpha})
-type
when there is a sequence
(\bar{r}_{\beta})_{\beta<\lambda}
of representatives for the parameters in
p(\bar{x})
such that for each index
\alpha<\lambda
the set
\{\bar{r}_{\beta}(\alpha):\beta<\lambda\}
is a subset of
A_{\alpha}
. That is,
p(\bar{x})
is an
(A_{\alpha})
-type precisely when the parameters for
p(\bar{x})
come from the set
A_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{D}}
.

The next result is a demonstration of the fact that
\mathrm{Sh}(A_{\alpha})
(where
(A_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
is a sequence of
\varphi
-sets) captures the notion of which graphs are induced subgraphs of the corresponding graph in the distribution graph sequence for the Łoś ultragraph of an
(A_{\alpha})
-type. This will allow us to apply Corollary
6.15
to distributions
f
of the
(A_{\alpha})
-type
p(\bar{x})
whose distribution graph sequence comes from
\mathrm{Sh}(A_{\alpha})
for each
\alpha<\lambda
. That is, all such distributions
f
having multiplicative refinements will be equivalent to all
(A_{\alpha})
-types in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
being realized in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
. The remainder of this section will be dedicated to determining the form that such distributions must take and how to interpret these statements using the conjugate distribution and ultragraph.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
(A_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
be a sequence of graph-like
\varphi
-sets coming from structures
(\mathcal{M}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
all in the common language
\mathcal{L}
and let
p(\bar{x})
be an
(A_{\alpha})
-type. Let
f
be a distribution for
p(\bar{x})
with representatives
(\bar{r}_{\beta})_{\beta<\lambda}
witnessing that
p(\bar{x})
is an
(A_{\alpha})
-type and let
(g_{n})_{n\in\omega}
and
(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
be the conjugate and distribution graph sequence of
f
. Fix
\alpha<\lambda
. Then there is an element
(\mathbb{G},\theta)
of
\mathrm{Sh}(A_{\alpha})
and a graph isomorphism
\psi\colon\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{G}
with the property that
\theta(\psi(\beta))=\bar{r}_{\beta}(\alpha)
for each
\beta\in\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
if and only if
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
is an induced subgraph of
\langle k_{1}(\alpha),k_{2}(\alpha)\rangle
where
(k_{n})_{n\in\omega}
is the Łoś conjugate for
p(\bar{x})
.

Proof.

Let
\beta_{0},\ldots,\beta_{m}
enumerate the vertices of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
and define
G:=\{1,\ldots,m\}
,

\theta(i):=\bar{r}_{\beta_{i}}(\alpha)

for each
i\in G
, and
\psi(\beta_{i}):=i
. Let
\mathbb{G}
be the unique graph with underlying set
G
and such that
(\mathbb{G},\theta)\in\mathrm{Sh}(A_{\alpha})
. Then
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
being an induced subgraph of
\langle k_{1}(\alpha),k_{2}(\alpha)\rangle
is the same as saying that there is an edge between
\beta,\gamma
in
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
if and only if

\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\vDash\exists\bar{x},\,\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{r}_{\beta}(\alpha))\wedge\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{r}_{\gamma}(\alpha))

which is equivalent to saying that
\psi(\beta)
and
\psi(\gamma)
are edge related in
\mathbb{G}
. Note that we did not have any choice (up to permuting the enumeration given for
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
) in picking
G
,
\theta
, and
\psi
.
∎

It will be useful in characterizing the distributions of
(A_{\alpha})
-types to know which sequences of graphs
(\mathbb{G}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
occurring in
\mathrm{Sh}(A_{\alpha})
can appear as the distribution graph sequence of an
(A_{\alpha})
-type (see Theorem
7.16
). For such a graph sequence, it is necessary that we can find an induced subgraph
\mathbb{H}
of
\mathbb{G}:=\prod_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}/\mathcal{D}
that is complete, has cardinality
\lambda
, and has maps
\iota_{\alpha}\colon G_{\alpha}\to\lambda
such that the induced map
\iota:\mathbb{G}\to\lambda^{\mathcal{D}}
maps
\mathbb{H}
bijectively to
\lambda\subseteq\lambda^{\mathcal{D}}
(this
\mathbb{H}
will be the image of
\eta_{f}
where
f
is the distribution under consideration). As the following lemma shows, this is enough.

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
(A_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
be a sequence of graph-like
\varphi
-sets coming from the structures
(\mathcal{M}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
all in the common language
\mathcal{L}
. Suppose that

(1)

there is a set

and a sequence

(2)

for each

of

with

and for each

\mathcal{D}
, and

(3)

there are fixed bijections

for each

.

Then there is a graph-like
\varphi
-type

in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{D}}
and a distribution

for

for which

(4)

(\mathbb{H}_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\lambda}
, the distribution graph sequence of

, is such that
\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}\cong\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in B
,

(5)

\mathrm{ess}(f)=B
, and

(6)

for all

\mathbb{H}_{\alpha}
is an induced subgraph of
\langle k_{1}(\alpha),k_{2}(\alpha)\rangle
where
(k_{n})_{n\in\omega}
is the Łoś conjugate.

Convention 7.7
.

In order to make sense of (3) in the lemma statement and several similar statements made in the proof below, we say that
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}\vDash\psi(\bar{a})
is false if any of the elements of
\bar{a}
are not in the underlying set of
\mathbb{G}_{\alpha}
or are undefined values of a function.

Proof of Lemma
7.6
.

We begin by defining the type

and the distribution
f
, and then check that these values of
p(\bar{x})
and

satisfy conditions
(4)
–
(6)
. For each

\bar{r}_{\beta}
defined by

when

is defined and
\beta\in Y_{\alpha}
, and
\bar{r}_{\beta}(\alpha)
is an arbitrary value of
A_{\alpha}
for all other values of
\alpha
in
\lambda
. Define a collection of formulas

by

p(\bar{x}):=\{\varphi\left(\bar{x},\bar{r}_{\beta}/\mathcal{D}\right):\beta<\lambda\}.

The collection
p(\bar{x})
is a type because, for any finite
\Delta\subseteq\lambda
with
|\Delta|\geq 2
, we have that

\displaystyle\llbracket\Delta\rrbracket^{\mathcal{D}}_{p(\bar{x})}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in\lambda:\forall\{\beta,\gamma\}\in[\Delta]^{2},\,\mathcal{M}_{\alpha}\vDash\exists\bar{x},\,\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{r}_{\beta}(\alpha))\wedge\varphi(\bar{x},\bar{r}_{\gamma}(\alpha))\}

Sometimes we will want to talk about induced subgraphs of graphs

Convention 7.10

(or isomorphism type

and write

if there is an injective graph homomorphism

that also preserves non-edges.

We will characterize the isomorphism shape not by which isomorphism types belong to the isomorphism shape, but by the minimal graphs that cannot appear as induced subgraphs of elements of the isomorphism shape. This follows, roughly speaking, from the fact that induced subgraphs of
\mathbb{G}
with
(\mathbb{G},\theta)\in\mathrm{Sh}(A)
are easily obtained by taking a subset of the vertices in
\mathbb{G}
and the corresponding restriction to the function

and from the well-used fact in graph combinatorics that classes

of graphs that are closed under taking subgraphs are characterized by the minimal graphs not occurring in
\mathcal{C}
.

Lemma 7.11

be

Entanglement phases in large- N N hybrid Brownian circuits with long-range couplings
Subhayan Sahu, Shao-Kai Jian, Gregory Bentsen, Brian Swingle

Abstract
We develop solvable models of large- N N hybrid quantum circuits on qubits and fermions with tunable long-range power-law interactions and continuous local monitoring. These models provide analytical access to the entanglement phase diagram and error-correcting properties of many-body entangled non-equilibrium states generated by such dynamics. In one dimension, the long-range couplings are irrelevant for α > 3 / 2 \alpha>3/2 , where α \alpha is the power-law exponent, and the models exhibit a conventional measurement-induced phase transition between volume- and area-law entangled phases. For 1 / 2 < α < 3 / 2 1/2<\alpha<3/2 the long-range couplings become relevant, leading to a nontrivial dynamical exponent at the measurement-induced phase transition. More interestingly, for α < 1 \alpha<1 the entanglement pattern receives a sub-volume correction for both area-law and volume-law phases, indicating that the phase realizes a quantum error correcting code whose code distance scales as L 2 − 2 ​ α L^{2-2\alpha} . While the entanglement phase diagram is the same for both the interacting qubit and fermionic hybrid Brownian circuits, we find that long-range free-fermionic circuits exhibit a distinct phase diagram with two different fractal entangled phases.

It is highly desirable to develop theoretical tools to easily estimate entanglement properties of many-body states generated by strongly-interacting quantum dynamics. In this work we develop exactly solvable models composed of large-
N
clusters of qubits or fermions in a 1D chain [Fig.

In particular, these methods provide simple pen-and-paper calculations for the dynamics of many-body entanglement that can be immediately applied to problems of experimental interest.
We understand the entanglement phases in these models in terms of a replica-symmetry-breaking transition of a corresponding statistical mechanical system, and derive analytical expressions for entanglement entropies, code properties of the QECC phase, and critical properties of the phase transition. We summarize our primary findings in Figs.
1

\alpha

Our analysis of these large-

First, our models go beyond the standard set of tools – Clifford circuits, matrix product states, and exact diagonalization – typically used to study entanglement dynamics, and are able to provide an analytical mean-field understanding of entanglement phases and measurement-induced phase transitions. Second, the analytical control afforded by our models allows us to derive new results, namely that long-range interactions generate novel sub-region entanglement structure, leading to a non-trivial QECC phase with a tunable sub-extensive code distance
L^{2-2\alpha}

V
Discussion

In this paper we introduced and studied analytically-solvable models at large-
N
which exhibit entanglement phase transitions in the presence of measurements and tunable long-range couplings. We considered power-law couplings in large-
N
Brownian circuits in continuous time for both spins and Majorana fermions and for both interacting and non-interacting models. We discovered that the long-range couplings non-perturbatively modify the entanglement structure of the usual measurement-induced phase diagram with volume and area law phases. In the interacting models, we found that the quantum states generated by these circuit possess good quantum code properties, namely a finite code rate and a power-law code distance. In the non-interacting models (but still with power-law hoppings), we identified multiple fractally-entangled phases. We demonstrated the generic nature of these properties by showing the same behavior for both spin and fermionic models.

The phase diagram in Fig.
1
can be readily generalized to Brownian chains in higher dimensions, and demonstrates an entanglement transition for all
2\alpha>d
, with non-trivial dynamical exponent
z=(2\alpha-d)/2
for
d<2\alpha<d+2
. The subextensive correction arising from (
11
) is
A^{2d-2\alpha}
. Interestingly, in the long-range interacting models with
2\alpha<2
, we found a non-perturbative code distance that is a tunable sub-extensive power of system size. A fixed power-law scaling has also been numerically observed for local hybrid Clifford circuits
Li and Fisher 2021
, which has been attributed to the quenched disorder in the models
Li et al. 2021
. It will be interesting to investigate the effect of disorder on the power-law code distance in the long-range case.

Since all the phases and transitions discussed here were obtained in the large-
N
limit, it interesting to ask whether perturbative corrections in
1/N
match the well-known anomalous dimensions for the 2D Ising model or for the percolation transition, or if they constitute distinct universality classes.
It will also be interesting for future studies to study the Rényi entropy for general
n
and the entanglement entropy at
n\rightarrow 1
.

Appendix A
Setup of the Rényi entropy calculation

In the main part of the paper we focus on a 1-dimensional lattice of size L with periodic boundary conditions, however the results can be readily extended to higher dimensions. For the spin model, each spin operator is labeled as
\mathcal{S}_{ri\alpha}
, with
i

r

The dofs in Q (vectors in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
) undergo a non-unitary random evolution
V(t)
. The randomness comes from the Brownian nature of the unitary evolution that we consider in our setup. Under this evolution, a density matrix (when viewed as vector in the doubled Hilbert space

\left|\rho\right\rangle\rangle\to V(t)\otimes V^{*}(t)\left|\rho\right\rangle\rangle

A

\rho_{A}

\displaystyle S^{(n)}_{A}=\frac{1}{1-n}\mathbb{E}\log\frac{\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}^{n}(t)\right]}{\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}(t)\right]^{n}},

(29)

where
\mathbb{E}
refers to the averaging over the realizations of the random circuit

Estimating the actual Rényi-2 entropy averaged over the randomness requires taking a non-trivial replica limit since this is an average of a ratio of two multi-replica quantities. Instead, we will calculate the quasi Rényi-2 entropy
Napp et al. 2020
, which is the ratio of the averages,

\displaystyle\hat{S}^{(n)}_{A}=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\frac{\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}^{n}(t)\right]}{\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}(t)\right]^{n}}

(30)

This quantity, while distinct from the disorder averaged Rényi-n entropy, is easier to calculate. In this work we focus on estimating
\hat{S}^{(2)}_{A}
, for which it is convenient to consider the dynamics in 4 replicas of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{(4)}
. We use a

1,2
(

) denote the first (second) replica, and
1,3
(

) denote the forward (backward) evolution. In

the time evolution is given by
\mathbf{V}(t)=V^{(1)}(t)\otimes V^{(2)*}(t)\otimes V^{(3)}(t)\otimes V^{(4)*}(t)
, and the normalization factor is given by
\mathcal{N}(\rho)=\sqrt{\langle\left\langle\mathcal{I}\right|\mathbf{V}(t)\left|\rho\right\rangle\rangle\left|\rho\right\rangle\rangle}
. To define
\left|\mathcal{I}\right\rangle\rangle\in\mathcal{H}^{(4)}
, we first define two normalized states,

\displaystyle\left|+\right\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{ab=0,1}\left|aabb\right\rangle\rangle

(31)

\displaystyle\left|-\right\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{ab=0,1}\left|abba\right\rangle\rangle,

(32)

which leads to the definition,
\left|\mathcal{I}\right\rangle\rangle=\bigotimes_{i,r}\left|\mathcal{+}\right\rangle\rangle
. This definition ensures that the normalization factor is the usual norm of a state. In our setup, the system dofs in

We further define a SWAP state in

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{S}_{A}\right\rangle\rangle=\bigotimes_{i,r\in A}\left|-\right\rangle\rangle\bigotimes_{i,r\in A^{c}=Q-A}\left|+\right\rangle\rangle,

(33)

and the quasi Rényi-2 entropy is given by,

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\hat{S}^{(2)}_{A}\right)=\frac{\langle\left\langle\mathcal{S}_{A}\right|\mathbb{E}\mathbf{V}(t)\left|\mathcal{I}\right\rangle\rangle}{\langle\left\langle\mathcal{I}\right|\mathbb{E}\mathbf{V}(t)\left|\mathcal{I}\right\rangle\rangle}.

A.1
Relation between the quasi Rényi, Rényi, and von Neumann entropies

One can show that the Rényi entropy and the quasi Rényi entropy can be treated as a part of the same family of generalized entropic quantities. Consider the generalized function,

\displaystyle\chi^{(nm)}_{A}=\frac{1}{m(1-n)}\log\frac{\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}^{n}\right]\right)^{m}}{\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}\right]\right)^{nm}}

(35)

One can show,

\displaystyle\chi^{(nm)}_{A}\big|_{m\to 0}

\displaystyle=S^{(n)}_{A}

(36)

\displaystyle\chi^{(nm)}_{A}\big|_{m\to 1}

\displaystyle=\hat{S}^{(n)}_{A}.

(37)

Intriguingly, one can also extract the von Neumann entropy directly from the quasi Rényi entropy. First let us define the normalized density matrix,
\tilde{\rho}_{A}=\rho_{A}/\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}\right]
. The probability of each trajectory is given by
\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}\right]
. The trajectory averaged von-Neumann entropy is given by,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{S_{A}}]=-\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}\right]\mathrm{Tr}\left[\tilde{\rho}_{A}\log{\tilde{\rho}_{A}}\right]\big].

(38)

We find that the trajectory averaged von-Neumann entropy is the
n\to 1
limit of the quasi-Rényi entropy,

\displaystyle\hat{S}^{(n\to 1)}_{A}=\mathbb{E}{[S_{A}]}.

(39)

Let us sketch this proof.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}{[S_{A}]}

\displaystyle=-\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}\log{\rho_{A}}\right]-\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}\right]\log{\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}\right]}\big]

\displaystyle=-\partial_{n}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}^{n}\right]-\left(\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}\right]\right)^{n}\big]\bigg|_{n\to 1}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{n}\left[\frac{\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho^{n}\right]}{\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}\right]\right)^{n}}\right]\bigg|_{n\to 1}\text{ (using $\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}\right]=1$)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-n}\log\frac{\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho^{n}\right]}{\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{A}\right]\right)^{n}}\bigg|_{n\to 1}

\displaystyle=\hat{S}^{(n\to 1)}_{A}.

Hence we find that although the quasi Rényi and the averaged Rényi entropies are different replica limits of a generalized entropy function, a particular replica limit of the quasi Rényi entropy can access the physical von Neumann entropy of the state generated by the hybrid circuit.

Appendix B
Path integral representation of the replicated dynamics

In this section of the Appendix we show how to derive the spin path integral for the replicated dynamics and derive Eqs.

B.1
Integrating out disorder and spin path integral

The Brownian disorders are explicitly given by,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[n_{i}^{\alpha}(t)n_{j}^{\beta}(t^{\prime})\right]=\frac{\gamma}{(\mathcal{S}+1)^{2}}\frac{\delta_{tt^{\prime}}}{\Delta t/2}\delta_{ij}\delta^{\alpha\beta},

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[J_{ij\alpha\beta}(t)J_{kl\mu\rho}(t^{\prime})\right]=\frac{J}{N(\mathcal{S}+1)^{4}}\frac{\delta_{tt^{\prime}}}{\Delta t/2}\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}\delta_{\alpha\mu}\delta_{\beta\rho},

(40)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{J}^{r_{1}r_{2}}_{ij\alpha\beta}(t)\tilde{J}^{r^{\prime}_{1}r^{\prime}_{2}}_{kl\mu\rho}(t^{\prime})\right]=\frac{gJ_{r_{1}r_{2}}}{N(\mathcal{S}+1)^{4}}\frac{\delta_{tt^{\prime}}}{\Delta t/2}\delta_{r_{1}r_{1}^{\prime}}\delta_{r_{2}r_{2}^{\prime}}\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}\delta_{\alpha\mu}\delta_{\beta\rho}.

We want to write down a path integral expression for the averaged circuit evolution in
n
replicated copies of the physical system, which includes
2n
copies of the circuit (counting the time-reversed copies). This quantity,

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}(t)=\mathbb{E}\left[V^{(1)}(t)\otimes V^{(2)*}(t)\otimes\ldots V^{(2n-1)}(t)\otimes V^{(2n)*}(t)\right],

(41)

can give us access to the
n
-th Rényi entropies, and in particular, the
n=2
result corresponds to the Rényi-2 entropy that we consider in this paper. Let
u,v
denote the replica index. The time evolution operator in any given copy is generated by an interaction Hamiltonian and a non-unitary evolution generated by a weak measurement followed by post-selection, as described in the main text. Schematically this can be expressed as,

\displaystyle V^{(u)}(\Delta t)\sim\exp\left[-\frac{i\Delta t}{2}\left(H^{(u)}(t)-i\mathcal{O}^{(u)}(t)\right)\right],

(42)

where the
H^{(u)}
and
\mathcal{O}^{(u)}
are the Hamiltonian and the measured operator (in replica
u
) respectively. These operators are taken to be Brownian in our model, which implies that the time evolution is uncorrelated in the time direction. However the randomness is same for each replica, which implies that in
\mathbf{V}
different replicas become correlated when the disorder is integrated away, using the Gaussian nature of the Brownian variables defined in Eq. 3 of the main text. One way to derive this is by expanding the evolution in each time step in the product in (
42
) to second order in
\Delta t
, and collecting terms like
\mathbb{E}\left[H^{(u)}H^{(v)}\right]
,
\mathbb{E}\left[H^{(u)}H^{(u)}\right]
or
\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{O}^{(u)}\mathcal{O}^{(v)}\right]
together. This procedure is discussed in detail in the Appendix B of
Bentsen et al. 2021
. Exponentiating back, one obtains that
\mathbf{V}(\Delta t)\sim\exp\left(-NI_{n}(t)\Delta t\right)
. The variances of the random terms have been scaled in a way that the exponential comes with a prefactor of
N
. Dropping some constant terms (which arise from the intra-replica terms)
I_{n}(t)
is given by,

\displaystyle I_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\Bigg[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
u<v\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u+v}\frac{J}{4(\mathcal{S}+1)^{4}}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\mathcal{S}_{ri}^{u}\cdot\mathcal{S}_{ri}^{v}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\neq r^{\prime}\\
u<v\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u+v}\frac{gJ_{rr^{\prime}}}{2(\mathcal{S}+1)^{4}}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\mathcal{S}_{ri}^{u}\cdot\mathcal{S}_{ri}^{v}\right)\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\mathcal{S}_{r^{\prime}i}^{u}\cdot\mathcal{S}_{r^{\prime}i}^{v}\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
u<v\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u+v}\frac{\gamma}{2(\mathcal{S}+1)^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\mathcal{S}_{ri}^{u}\cdot\mathcal{S}_{ri}^{v}\right)\Bigg].

(43)

In this expression the spin operator
\mathcal{S}
refers to the spin operator at time
t
.

We can now stack
\mathbf{V}_{u}(\Delta t)
at different times by repeating sequence of the Brownian interactions and measurements, insert resolutions of the identity between each layer, and take the limit
\Delta t\rightarrow 0
with
T
fixed to express
\mathbb{V}(t)
as a path integral over
2nN
unit-norm
\mathrm{SO}(3)
spins
\mathbf{\mathcal{S}}_{ri}^{u}
, using spin coherent states as the basis. The completeness relation for the coherent states for a single spin is given by,

\mathbb{I}=\int\frac{2\mathcal{S}+1}{4\pi}d\mathbf{\Omega}_{i}\left|\mathbf{\Omega}_{i}\right\rangle\left\langle\mathbf{\Omega}_{i}\right|.

(44)

To turn the spins into coherent states, we use the upper symbols for single spin-
S
Pauli operators
Klauder and Skagerstam 1985
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{i\alpha}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{2\mathcal{S}+1}{4\pi}d\mathbf{\Omega}_{i}\left|\mathbf{\Omega}_{i}\right\rangle\left\langle\mathbf{\Omega}_{i}\right|(\mathcal{S}+1)\Omega_{i\alpha}

(45)

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{S}_{i\alpha}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{2\mathcal{S}+1}{4\pi}d\mathbf{\Omega}_{i}\left|\mathbf{\Omega}_{i}\right\rangle\left\langle\mathbf{\Omega}_{i}\right|\left[(\mathcal{S}+1)\left(\mathcal{S}+\frac{3}{2}\right)\left(\Omega_{i\alpha}\right)^{2}-\frac{\mathcal{S}+1}{2}\right],

(46)

and introduce a measure for the coherent spin states in the path integral,

\mathcal{D}\Omega_{ri}^{u}=\prod_{t_{n}}\frac{2\mathcal{S}+1}{4\pi}d\mathbf{\Omega}^{u}_{r,i,t_{n}}\langle\mathbf{\Omega}^{u}_{r,i,t_{n+1}}|\mathbf{\Omega}^{u}_{r,i,t_{n}}\rangle,

(47)

with implicit time dependent terms.

In terms of the coherent states
\mathbb{V}(t)
is given by a path integral,

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}(t)=\mathbb{E}\left[V\otimes V_{\mathcal{T}}\otimes\cdots\otimes V\otimes V_{\mathcal{T}}\right]=e^{-NI[\mathbf{\Omega}]}

\displaystyle I[\mathbf{\Omega}]=\int_{0}^{T}dt

\displaystyle\Bigg[\frac{J}{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
u<v\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u+v}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\mathbf{\Omega}_{ri}^{u}\cdot\mathbf{\Omega}_{ri}^{v}\right)^{2}\

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\neq r^{\prime}\\
u<v\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u+v}\frac{gJ_{rr^{\prime}}}{2}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\mathbf{\Omega}_{ri}^{u}\cdot\mathbf{\Omega}_{ri}^{v}\right)\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\mathbf{\Omega}_{r^{\prime}i}^{u}\cdot\mathbf{\Omega}_{r^{\prime}i}^{v}\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
u<v\end{subarray}}(-1)^{u+v}\frac{\gamma}{2(S+1)^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\mathbf{\Omega}_{ri}^{u}\cdot\mathbf{\Omega}_{ri}^{v}\right)\Bigg]

(48)

To decouple the non-linear interactions in
\mathbf{\Omega}_{ri}^{u}\cdot\mathbf{\Omega}_{ri}^{v}
, we introduce Hubbard Stratonovich type fields which couple different replicas
F_{uv}^{(r)}
and
G_{uv}^{(r)}
, satisfying the operator identity,

1=\left(\prod_{r,u<v}\int\mathcal{D}F^{uv}_{r}\mathcal{D}G^{uv}_{r}\right)\exp\left[iN\int_{0}^{T}dtF^{uv}_{r}\left(G^{uv}_{r}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\mathbf{\Omega}_{ri}^{u}\cdot\mathbf{\Omega}_{ri}^{v}\right)\right].

(49)

The action can thus be re-written as,

\displaystyle I[F,G,\mathbf{\Omega}]

\displaystyle=\int dt\Bigg[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
u<v\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{u+v}J}{4}\left(G^{uv}_{r}\right)^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\neq r^{\prime}\\
u<v\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{u+v}gJ_{rr^{\prime}}}{2}\left(G^{uv}_{r}\right)\left(G^{uv}_{r^{\prime}}\right)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
u<v\end{subarray}}\frac{(-1)^{u+v}\gamma}{2}\left(G^{uv}_{r}\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
u<v\end{subarray}}\left(iF^{uv}_{r}G^{uv}_{r}\right)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
u<v\end{subarray}}iF^{uv}_{r}\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\mathbf{\Omega}_{ri}^{u}\cdot\mathbf{\Omega}^{v}_{ri}\right)\Bigg]

(50)

Note that (
B.1
) is applicable in any general dimensions, where the position label
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
can be identified as a
d
dimensional vector, with
L
being the linear size along each dimension.

B.2
Field theory with periodic boundary condition

We can further simplify (
B.1
) by assuming periodic boundary condition and going to momentum space. We also assume that the interaction
J_{rr^{\prime}}
is translationally invariant and even, i.e.
J_{rr^{\prime}}\sim J_{|r-r^{\prime}|}
.

We consider discrete d-dimensional cubic lattice in the space
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
L
being the linear extent of the cube. In the limit
L\gg 1
, the momentum space domain is
k\in\{0,2\pi\}^{d}
. We use the following schematic definitions,

\displaystyle G_{k}=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{-ik\cdot r}G_{r}

\displaystyle G_{r}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cdots\int_{0}^{2\pi}dk_{1}\cdots dk_{d}e^{ik\cdot r}G_{k}\equiv\int d\hskip-0.80002pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt^{d}ke^{ik\cdot r}G_{k}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{ik\cdot r}=\delta(k)\ ,\ \int d\hskip-0.80002pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt^{d}ke^{ikr}=\delta_{r,0}.

(51)

We also introduce the notation,

\displaystyle\Omega_{r}^{uv}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\mathbf{\Omega}_{ri}^{u}\cdot\mathbf{\Omega}_{ri}^{v}.

(52)

In momentum space, we can rewrite Eq.
B.1
can be rewritten as (dropping the constant terms),

\displaystyle I[F,G,\mathbf{\Omega}]

\displaystyle=\int dt\int d\hskip-0.80002pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt^{d}k\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u<v\end{subarray}}\Bigg[\frac{(-1)^{u+v}}{4}\left(\hat{J}_{k}G_{k}^{uv}G_{-k}^{uv}-4\pi\gamma G_{k}^{uv}\delta(k)\right)-iF_{k}^{uv}G_{-k}^{uv}+iF_{k}^{uv}\Omega^{uv}_{-k}\Bigg],

\displaystyle\ \text{where, }\hat{J}_{k}=J+\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}e^{-ik\cdot r}J_{|r|}

(53)

Note that all the
G^{uv}_{k}
fields can be integrated out, by satisfying the equations of motion,

\displaystyle G^{uv}_{k}=(-1)^{u+v}\frac{2iF^{uv}_{k}}{\hat{J}_{k}}

\displaystyle\ \text{for }k\neq 0,

\displaystyle G^{uv}_{0}=\frac{2\pi\gamma}{\hat{J}_{0}}+(-1)^{u+v}\frac{2iF^{uv}_{0}}{\hat{J}_{0}}

\displaystyle\ \text{for }k=0.

(54)

Integrating out the G fields, we get (again dropping constant terms),

\displaystyle I[F,\mathbf{\Omega}]

\displaystyle=\int dt\int d\hskip-0.80002pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt^{d}k\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u<v\end{subarray}}\Bigg[-\frac{(-1)^{u+v}}{\hat{J}_{k}}\left(iF^{uv}_{k}\right)\left(iF^{uv}_{-k}\right)-\frac{2\pi\gamma}{\hat{J}_{0}}\left(iF^{uv}_{k}\right)\delta(k)+iF_{k}^{uv}\Omega^{uv}_{-k}\Bigg].

(55)

Going back to real space, we get,

\displaystyle I[F,\mathbf{\Omega}]

\displaystyle=\int dt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
u<v\end{subarray}}\Bigg[-(-1)^{u+v}\sum_{r^{\prime}}\mathcal{J}_{rr^{\prime}}\left(iF^{uv}_{r}\right)\left(iF^{uv}_{r^{\prime}}\right)-\frac{\gamma}{\hat{J}_{0}}\left(iF^{uv}_{r}\right)+\left(iF_{r}^{uv}\right)\Omega^{uv}_{r}\Bigg],

(56)

with an effective real space interaction
\mathcal{J}_{rr^{\prime}}
given by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{rr^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\int d\hskip-0.80002pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt^{d}k\frac{e^{ik\cdot(r-r^{\prime})}}{\hat{J}_{k}}.

(57)

By identifying the last term in (
56
) as a partition function for the spins with external fields
iF
, one gets the Eq.
III.1
in the main text.

B.3
Effective real space interaction

(
56
) holds for any general number of dimensions. The microscopic interaction between the spins i.e.
J_{rr^{\prime}}
can be Fourier transformed to give the momentum space interaction
\hat{J}_{k}
between the
G
fields. This can then be transformed to an interaction
\mathcal{J}_{rr^{\prime}}
between the
iF
fields via (
57
).

Here we consider two forms of the interaction, nearest neighbor (NN) or power-law interacting (PL). In the main text results are used for the PL case. Including the on-site term, the real space interaction between spins in a d-dimensional lattice is defined as follows,

\displaystyle J_{rr^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}J\left(\delta_{r,r^{\prime}}+g\sum_{i}\left(\delta_{r,r^{\prime}+e_{i}}+\delta_{r,r^{\prime}-e_{i}}\right)\right)&\text{ (NN)}\\
J\left(\delta_{r,r^{\prime}}+g\left(1-\delta_{r,r^{\prime}}\right)\frac{1}{|r-r^{\prime}|^{2\alpha}}\right)&\text{ (PL)}.\end{cases}

(58)

Here
e_{i}
is a d-dimensional vector
\{0,0,..,i,..,0\}
with 1 in the i-th position.

The momentum space interaction is given by its discrete-time Fourier transform,

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{k}=\begin{cases}J\left(1+2g\sum_{i}\cos{k_{i}}\right)&\text{ (NN)}\\
J\left(1+g\sum_{s\neq 0}\frac{e^{-ik\cdot s}}{|s|^{2\alpha}}\right)&\text{ (PL)}.\end{cases}

(59)

To get the effective real space interaction
\mathcal{J}
between the replica fields
iF
, one has to take the Fourier transform of
\hat{J}_{k}^{-1}
. This can be done exactly for
d=1
.

B.3.1
d=1

In
d=1
we have,

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{k}=\begin{cases}J\left(1+2g\cos{k}\right)&\text{ (NN)}\\
J\left(1+g\left(\text{Li}_{2\alpha}\left(e^{-ik}\right)+\text{Li}_{2\alpha}\left(e^{ik}\right)\right)\right)&\text{ (PL)},\end{cases}

(60)

where
\text{Li}_{n}\left(z\right)
is the Polylogarithm function. For large
\alpha
,
\text{Li}_{2\alpha}(z)\to z
, and
\hat{J}_{k}
reduces to the Nearest Neighbor case.

In the NN case, the real space interaction between the replica fields is given by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\text{NN}}_{rr^{\prime}}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{dk}{2\pi}\frac{e^{ik(r-r^{\prime})}}{1+2g\cos{k}}.

(61)

Changing the variables to
z=e^{ik}
, we get contour integral defined along the unit circle in the complex
z
-plane, with two isolated poles along the negative real axis. Performing the contour integral picks up the pole within the unit circle and one obtains,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\text{NN}}_{rr^{\prime}}=\frac{(-1)^{r-r^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{1-4g^{2}}}e^{-\text{acosh}\frac{1}{2g}|r-r^{\prime}|}.

(62)

In the PL case, with the same change of variables, the real space interaction is given by the following contour integral defined along the unit circle,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\text{PL}}_{rr^{\prime}}=\int_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\frac{z^{r-r^{\prime}-1}}{1+g\left(\text{Li}_{2\alpha}(z)+\text{Li}_{2\alpha}(1/z)\right)}.

(63)

For large enough
g
(including
g=1
) there is an isolated pole along the negative real axis within the unit circle, and a branch cut due to the
\text{Li}_{2\alpha}(1/z)
term along the positive real axis
z\in(0,\infty)
. The pole gives an exponential decay like the NN case, while we will show that the branch cut contribution leads to a power law interaction.

Deforming the contour to hug the branch cut, the integral is proportional to the discontinuity along the branch cut (
\int_{0+i\varepsilon}^{1+i\varepsilon}-\int_{0-i\varepsilon}^{1-i\varepsilon}
), which for the function
\text{Li}_{2\alpha}(1/z)
is proportional to
\log^{2\alpha-1}(1/z)
. Thus we get,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\text{PL}}_{rr^{\prime}}=\text{Pole contribution}-\int_{0}^{1}dzz^{r-r^{\prime}-1}\log^{2\alpha-1}(z)g(z),

(64)

where
g(z)
is a smooth function. For large
|r-r^{\prime}|
the integrand is heavily suppressed away from
z=1
, so we can change variables
z\sim e^{-w}
, and after dropping the regular terms, we have an integral,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dwe^{-w|r-r^{\prime}|}w^{2\alpha-1}\sim|r-r^{\prime}|^{-2\alpha},\text{ for }|r-r^{\prime}|\gg 1.

Along with the pole contribution we thus get the effective real space interaction,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\text{PL}}_{rr^{\prime}}\sim(-1)^{r-r^{\prime}}e^{-\mu|r-r^{\prime}|}-(1-\delta_{rr^{\prime}})\frac{1}{|r-r^{\prime}|^{2\alpha}}.

(65)

At large
|r-r^{\prime}|
,
\mathcal{J}_{rr^{\prime}}\sim|r-r^{\prime}|^{-2\alpha}
, as was noted in the main text.

B.3.2
General d

In this section we will derive the effective real space interaction in any general dimension,
d
.

For the NN case, we have,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\text{NN}}_{rr^{\prime}}=\int d\hskip-0.80002pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt^{d}k\frac{e^{ik\cdot(r-r^{\prime})}}{1+2g\sum_{i}\cos{k_{i}}},

(66)

which can be expanded around
k_{i}=\pi
. Furthermore, we can consider
r-r^{\prime}
to be along a particular dimension, say 1. With these manipulations we get,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\text{NN}}_{rr^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\sim\int_{-\pi}^{+\pi}d\hskip-0.80002pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt^{d}k\frac{e^{ik_{1}(r-r^{\prime})}}{1+\tilde{g}\sum_{i}k_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}dkk^{d-2}\int d\hskip-0.80002pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006ptk_{1}\frac{e^{ik_{1}(r-r^{\prime})}}{1+k_{1}^{2}+k^{2}}

\displaystyle\sim\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{k^{d-2}}{\sqrt{1+k^{2}}}e^{-\sqrt{1+k^{2}}|r-r^{\prime}|}\sim e^{-\mu|r-r^{\prime}|}.

(67)

For the PL case, one needs to evaluate,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\text{PL}}_{rr^{\prime}}\sim\int d\hskip-0.80002pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt^{d}k\frac{e^{ik\cdot s}}{1+g\sum_{u\neq 0}\frac{e^{-ik\cdot u}}{|u|^{2\alpha}}}.

(68)

Firstly we have,

\displaystyle\sum_{u\neq 0}\frac{e^{-ik\cdot u}}{|u|^{2\alpha}}\sim\int^{\prime}d\hskip-0.80002pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt^{d}u\frac{e^{-ik\cdot u}}{|u|^{2\alpha}}\sim k^{2\alpha-d}.

(69)

With this, we can expand the function to be Fourier Transformed at small
k
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\text{PL}}_{rr^{\prime}}\sim\int d\hskip-0.80002pt\bar{}\hskip 1.00006pt^{d}ke^{ik\cdot s}\left(1-gk^{2\alpha-d}\right)\sim\delta(s)-g|s|^{-2\alpha}.

(70)

These results generalize
65
for any general dimension.

Appendix C
Saddle point analysis of the mean field

C.1
Effective Bulk Action and saddle points

To understand the saddle points of the action
I[\phi,\Theta]
we first consider the bulk mean-field limit in which the fields are independent of space and time:
\phi_{r}(t)=\phi,\Theta_{r}(t)=\Theta
. In this case the propagator
\mathcal{K}_{r}
is dominated at long times by the ground state of the r-bit effective Hamiltonian
\phi\sigma_{r}^{x}+\Theta\sigma_{r}^{z}
, which yields

\sum_{r}\ln\mathcal{K}_{r}=\frac{LT}{2}\sqrt{\phi^{2}+\Theta^{2}}+\frac{BLT}{2}.

(71)

By substituting this into Eq. (
III.1
) (and again dropping additive constant terms) we obtain the time- and space-independent effective bulk action,

\displaystyle I_{\text{MF}}[\phi,\Theta]=NTL\left[\mathcal{J}\left(\phi^{2}-3\Theta^{2}\right)-9\left(\Gamma+\frac{1}{9}\right)\Theta-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\phi^{2}+\Theta^{2}}\right],

(72)

where we have defined
\Gamma\equiv\frac{\gamma}{\hat{J}_{0}}
and
\mathcal{J}\equiv 27/16\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{J}_{r(r+s)}
.

Saddle point analysis of this action
Bentsen et al. 2021
reveals a phase transition at
\Gamma_{c}=1/9
(see Fig.
5
). For
\Gamma>\Gamma_{c}
the action is dominated by the symmetric saddle point with
\phi^{*}=0
and
\Theta^{*}=-3(\Gamma_{c}+2\Gamma)/4\mathcal{J}
. By contrast, for
\Gamma<\Gamma_{c}
, the action is dominated by the symmetry-broken saddle-point where the field
\phi^{*}
is non-zero and comes in a pair,
\phi^{*}\propto\pm\sqrt{\Gamma_{c}-\Gamma}
, while
\Theta^{*}=-9(\Gamma_{c}+\Gamma)/8\mathcal{J}
. The replica permutation
1\leftrightarrow 3
is equivalent to
\phi\leftrightarrow-\phi
symmetry, which is spontaneously broken for
\Gamma<\Gamma_{c}
.

\Gamma<\Gamma_{c}
, the action cost
\mathrm{Re}[I_{MF}]
is minimized by the symmetry-broken saddle points (solid orange) relative to the symmetric saddle point (dashed purple). Above the critical point
\Gamma>\Gamma_{c}
the path integral is dominated by the symmetric saddle point (solid purple) because the symmetry-broken saddles (dashed orange) are imaginary and therefore do not contribute to the integral (see Fig.

).

Note,
\gamma_{c}
is given by
\Gamma_{c}\hat{J_{0}}\sim\Gamma_{c}\left(1+g\sum_{s\neq 0}|s|^{-2\alpha}\right)\approx\Gamma_{c}\left(1+g\int^{\prime}d^{d}s|s|^{-2\alpha}\right)
. Thus
\gamma_{c}
diverges when
2\alpha<d
, when the system effectively becomes all-to-all.

C.2
Effective Field Theory Near Criticality

In the previous analysis we understood the bulk mean-field physics of the model by ignoring the space- and time- dependence of the fields
\phi_{r}(t),\Theta_{r}(t)
. Here we restore the space- and time-dependence and study the model near the critical point. In this limit we can transform the effective r-bit action Eq. (
III.1
) into a
\phi^{4}
field theory with long-range interactions. In particular, near the critical point the dynamics is governed entirely by the small fluctuations of the symmetry-breaking field
\phi_{r}(t)
. In the following we expand the action
I[\phi,\Theta]
in fluctuations of this small parameter to obtain an effective long-range
\phi^{4}
field theory for the system.

First we evaluate the leading term of the effective action
I[\phi,\Theta]
in Eq. (
III.1
), corresponding to the long-range power-law interactions. Substituting
\mathcal{J}_{rr^{\prime}}=\mathcal{J}_{rr^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{PL}}
from Eq. (
65
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{r^{\prime}}(-1)^{r-r^{\prime}}e^{-\mu|r-r^{\prime}|}\phi_{r}\phi_{r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{q}e^{-\mu|q|}\phi_{r}^{2}+\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{q}\frac{q^{2}}{2}e^{-\mu|q|}\phi_{r}\partial_{r}^{2}\phi_{r}+O(\phi_{r}\partial_{r}^{4}\phi_{r})

\displaystyle\approx\frac{(1-e^{-\mu})}{(1+e^{-\mu})}\phi_{r}^{2}-\frac{e^{-2\mu}\left(1-e^{-\mu}\right)}{\left(1+e^{-\mu}\right)^{3}}\phi_{r}\partial_{r}^{2}\phi_{r}

(73)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{r^{\prime}}(1-\delta_{rr^{\prime}})\frac{1}{|r-r^{\prime}|^{2\alpha}}\phi_{r}\phi_{r^{\prime}}\rightarrow\int^{\prime}_{s}\frac{1}{|r-s|^{2\alpha}}\phi_{r}\phi_{s}

(74)

in the continuum limit, where the delta-function term
1-\delta_{rr^{\prime}}
is responsible for the UV cut-off in the continuum integral
\int^{\prime}_{s}=\int_{\mathbb{R}\backslash(r-\varepsilon,r+\varepsilon)}ds
. Here we have assumed that the parameter
\mu
is large such that the exponential
e^{-\mu\left|q\right|}
decays rapidly and suppresses higher derivative terms e.g.
\phi_{r}\partial_{r}^{4}\phi_{r}
. Next, we fix
\Theta_{r}(t)=\Theta=-3(\Gamma_{c}+2\Gamma)/4\mathcal{J}
to its saddle-point value and expand the propagators
\ln\mathcal{K}_{r}
in the small parameter
\phi_{r}(t)

Bentsen et al. 2021
:

\displaystyle\ln\mathcal{K}_{r}

\displaystyle=\ln 2\cosh{\frac{\Theta T}{2}}+\frac{1}{8}\int dt_{1}dt_{2}\phi_{r}(t_{1})\phi_{r}(t_{2})e^{-\left|\Theta\right|\left|t_{1}-t_{2}\right|}-\int dt\frac{\phi_{r}^{4}}{16\left|\Theta\right|^{3}}+O(\phi_{r}^{6})

\displaystyle\approx\int dt\left[\frac{\phi_{r}\partial_{t}^{2}\phi_{r}}{4\left|\Theta\right|^{3}}+\frac{\phi_{r}^{2}}{4\left|\Theta\right|}-\frac{\phi_{r}^{4}}{16\left|\Theta\right|^{3}}\right]+\mathrm{const}.

(75)

Finally, summing the results, taking the continuum limit, and ignoring additive constants, we find the near-critical Landau-Ginzburg effective field theory

\displaystyle\frac{I_{\text{eff}}[\phi]}{N}=\frac{1}{4\left|\Theta\right|^{3}}\int_{t,r}dtdr\Bigg[-\phi_{r}\left(\partial_{t}^{2}+\beta\partial_{r}^{2}\right)\phi_{r}-b\int^{\prime}_{s}\frac{\phi_{r}\phi_{s}}{|r-s|^{2\alpha}}-\frac{\delta}{2}\phi_{r}^{2}+\frac{\ \phi_{r}^{4}}{4}\Bigg],

(76)

for small fluctuations
\phi_{r}(t)
around the critical point. Here we have introduced the numerical coefficients

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=4\left|\Theta\right|^{3}\frac{27J}{16}\frac{e^{-2\mu}\left(1-e^{-\mu}\right)}{\left(1+e^{-\mu}\right)^{3}}

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=4\left|\Theta\right|^{3}\frac{27J}{16}

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle=8\left|\Theta\right|^{3}\left(\frac{1}{4\left|\Theta\right|}-\frac{27J}{16}\frac{(1-e^{-\mu})}{(1+e^{-\mu})}\right).

(77)

By rescaling space and
b
we can get rid of the
\beta
parameter, and end up with the Landau-Ginzburg theory in Eq. 6 in the main text.

Appendix D
Scaling of entanglement entropy in the symmetry broken phase of the nearest neighbor model

In this section we derive results for
d=1
. For the nearest neighbor case, the power law term in the action is absent and we will be working with the short-range action,

\displaystyle I[\phi]

\displaystyle=\int dt\int dr\left[-\phi\left(\partial_{t}^{2}+\beta\partial_{r}^{2}\right)\phi-\frac{\delta}{2}\phi^{2}+\frac{\phi^{4}}{4}\right],

(78)

D.1
Entropy of a maximally mixed initial state

The SWAP action acts on the whole spatial slice, and we need to only solve for the equations of motion with the time derivative,

\partial_{t}^{2}\phi=-\delta\phi+\phi^{3}.

(79)

For
\delta<0
, we have an instanton-like solution,

\displaystyle\phi^{*}(r,t)=\sqrt{\delta}\tanh\left[\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{2}}\left(t-t_{0}\right)\right].

(80)

Hence for
\delta>0
, we have the following scaling of the quasi-entropy of the full system,

\displaystyle\hat{S}^{(2)}_{Q}\sim N\left(I[\phi^{*}]-I[\sqrt{\delta}]\right)\sim NL\delta^{3/2}+...

(81)

The correction to this term will be given by the fluctuations of the domain wall within periodic boundary condition, which was estimated using Capillary Wave Theory in
Li and Fisher 2021
. For
T\gg\sqrt{L}
, this will be given by,

\displaystyle-\log\frac{T}{\sqrt{L}}-\log\sqrt{\frac{\beta N\delta^{3/2}}{12\pi}}\sim-\log\frac{T}{\sqrt{L}}+\text{const.}

(82)

The expression for the entropy of the entire system
S_{Q}
is essentially the same for the power-law interacting model, with suitably renormalized
\delta
,

\delta\to\delta_{\alpha}=\delta+\int^{\prime}ds|r-s|^{-2\alpha}.

(83)

D.2
Sub-system entropy

For a subsystem between the region
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
, the domain wall must be pinned to the future time boundary at
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
. We assume the solution of the fields is of the form,

\displaystyle\phi^{*}(r,t)=\sqrt{\delta}\tanh\left[\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{2}}\left(t-y(r)\right)\right],

(84)

where
y(r)
is the ‘height’ of the domain wall (or equivalently the position of the instanton). Due to the boundary pinning effect,
y(r)
must be
\epsilon\to 0
at
r=r_{1}
and
r=r_{2}
. The action for the quasi-entropy is given by a functional of
y(r)
,

\displaystyle I[y(r)]

\displaystyle=I[\phi^{*}]-I[\sqrt{\delta}]

\displaystyle=\int dt\int dr\delta^{2}\Bigg[\left(1+\beta y^{\prime}(r)^{2}\right)\text{sech}^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{2}}(t-y)-\frac{1}{4}\left(3+4\beta y^{\prime}(r)^{2}\right)\text{sech}^{4}\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{2}}(t-y)

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2\delta}}y^{\prime\prime}(r)\text{sech}^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{2}}(t-y)\text{tanh}\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{2}}(t-y)\Bigg]

Rescale the time variable,

\displaystyle z=\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{2}}(t-y(r)).

The limits for z integration are approximately
0\to\infty
.
The action for quasi-entropy is thus given by,

\displaystyle I[y(r)]

\displaystyle\sim\delta^{3/2}\int dr\int dz\left[\left(1+\beta y^{\prime}(r)^{2}\right)\text{sech}^{2}z-\frac{1}{4}\left(3+4\beta y^{\prime}(r)^{2}\right)\text{sech}^{4}z+\mathcal{O}(y^{\prime\prime}(r))\right]

\displaystyle\sim\delta^{3/2}\int dr\left(1+\frac{\beta}{6}y^{\prime}(r)^{2}+\mathcal{O}(y^{\prime\prime}(r)\right).

(85)

We can ignore the
\mathcal{O}(y^{\prime\prime}(r))
term as it is irrelevant under RG flow, since
[y]=1
and
[y^{\prime\prime}]=-1
. Thus, the quasi-entropy is thus given by,

\displaystyle\hat{S}^{(2)}(A)

\displaystyle=-\ln\int\mathcal{D}(y(r))\exp\left[-N\delta^{3/2}\int_{A}dr\left(1+\frac{\beta}{6}y^{\prime}(r)^{2}\right)\right]

(86)

\displaystyle=N\delta^{3/2}|A|-\ln\int\mathcal{D}(y(r))\exp\left[-\frac{\beta N\delta^{3/2}}{6}\int_{A}dr\left(y^{\prime}(r)^{2}\right)\right]

(87)

This is exactly the action conjectured by Li and Fisher and solved using Capillary Wave Theory in
Li and Fisher 2021
. Using their result, the quasi-entropy is given by,

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{A}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\approx N\delta^{3/2}|A|+\frac{3}{2}\ln|A|-\ln\left[\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\epsilon^{2}\left(\frac{\beta N\delta^{3/2}}{6}\right)^{3/2}\right]

(88)

\displaystyle=N\delta^{3/2}|A|+\frac{3}{2}\ln|A|+\text{const...}

(89)

The approximations a la Li and Fisher
Li and Fisher 2021
behind this are
|A|\ll L
,
\sqrt{A}\ll T
, and for a spatial lattice cut-off,
\epsilon
, such that,
\epsilon\ll\sqrt{|A|/(\beta N\delta^{3/2})}
. Note, we further have our large N approximation,
T\lesssim\text{poly}(N)
. These can all be satisfied in the regime,
\sqrt{A}\ll T\lesssim\text{poly}(N)
. The logarithmic correction term is
1/N
suppressed in our model.

For general long-range interactions, the simple capillary wave picture fails, as the gradient expansion doesn’t converge for
2\alpha<3
(for the effectively short-ranged case, the story is the same for the nearest neighbor model). In this case, we can’t compute the
\mathcal{O}(N^{0})
correction to
\hat{S}_{A}^{(2)}
. However, compared to the nearest neighbor case, long-range interaction adds an energy cost to the domain wall, and the entropy, as captured in Eq.
12
in the main text.

Appendix E
Monitored Brownian SYK: the interacting case

In this section we discuss the Landau Ginzburg theory of the quasi-entropy for the interacting case
\tilde{U}>0
.

The parameter
\lambda
is determined by (
20
).
For small
\tilde{U}
,
\lambda=\sqrt{1-\tilde{\gamma}^{2}}[1+\tilde{\gamma}^{2}(1-\tilde{\gamma}^{2})^{q/2-2}\tilde{U}+O(\tilde{U}^{2})]

is well defined when
\tilde{\gamma}<1
, and vanishes continuous as
\tilde{\gamma}\rightarrow 1
.
In the following we will focus on
q=4

At the critical point,

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}\big((2\tilde{U}-1)+(\tilde{U}^{2}-2\tilde{U})\lambda^{2}-\tilde{U}^{2}\lambda^{4}\big)=0,

(90)

which shows that for
\tilde{U}>1/2

Thus, the condition for a continuous transition is

.
On the other hand, when

IV.2
) at

What symmetry out of
O(2)\times O(2)

\tilde{U}>0

((O^{-1})^{uu^{\prime}})^{q/2}S^{u^{\prime}v^{\prime}}(O^{v^{\prime}v})^{q/2}=S^{uv}

The generator is still given by
\gamma_{(13)}
and
\gamma_{(24)}

\pi/2

The relative rotation symmetry is spontaneously broken by nonzero
\lambda
in (
IV.2

\gamma<J

\lambda
serves as an order parameter of the
C_{4}
symmetry breaking transition.
With a slight modification that replaces
\cos k
to
\epsilon_{k}
and
J
to
\hat{J}
, the Landau-Ginzburg effective theory reads

\displaystyle\frac{I_{\text{eff}}}{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1,2;k}\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\gamma}+\hat{J}(1-\epsilon_{k})\right)|\phi_{i,k}(\Omega)|^{2}+\sum_{r}\int_{t}\left(\frac{\gamma-\hat{J}}{2}\vec{\phi}_{r}^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{8}\vec{\phi}_{r}^{4}-\frac{U}{4}(\phi_{1,r}^{4}+\phi_{2,r}^{4})\right),

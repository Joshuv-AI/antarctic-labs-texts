Local strong magnetic fields and the Little-Parks effect
Ayman Kachmar, Mikael Sundqvist

Abstract
. Starting from the Ginzburg–Landau model in a planar simply connected domain, with a local compactly supported applied magnetic field, we derive an effective model in the strong field limit, defined on a non-simply connected domain. The effective model features oscillations in the Little-Parks and Aharonov–Bohm spirit. We discuss also a similar question for the lowest eigenvalue of the magnetic Laplacian.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Little-Parks effect

As the applied magnetic flux varies, superconductors undergo phase transitions between normal and superconducting states. The Little-Parks effect is observed when the transition is non-monotone and exhibit oscillations between superconducting and normal phases
[
14
]
. Within the Ginzburg-Landau theory,
examples of this sort were provided previously in several settings:

a)

Under a constant magnetic field, when imposing a Robin condition
[
7
,
13
]
or when restricting the superconducting sample to a thin domain
[
6
,
10
,
15
,
16
]
;

b)

under Aharonov-Bohm magnetic fields
[
12
]
;

c)

under certain positive non-homogeneous radial magnetic fields
[
6
]
.

In this paper, we consider a strong local magnetic field and prove that oscillations occur indefinitely in the Little–Parks spirit.

1.2.
The local magnetic field

Consider two simply connected domains
\omega,\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\overline{\omega}\subset\Omega
, and assume that their boundaries,
\partial\omega
and
\partial\Omega
, are simple smooth
C^{1}
curves.
By a local magnetic field we mean a non homogeneous magnetic field of the form
b\mathbf{1}_{\omega}\hat{z}
, where
\hat{z}=(0,0,1)^{\top}
,
b>0
and
\mathbf{1}_{\omega}
is the characteristic function of
\omega
,

\mathbf{1}_{\omega}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }x\in\omega,\\
0&\text{ if }x\not\in\omega.\end{cases}

(1.1)

This is an example of a magnetic step, which has recently received considerable attention in the context of superconductivity and spectral theory
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
8
]
.
Corresponding to the magnetic field
b\mathbf{1}_{\omega}\hat{z}
is the magnetic flux

\Phi:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Omega}b\mathbf{1}_{\omega}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x=\frac{b|\omega|}{2\pi}.

(1.2)

We introduce the vector potential
\mathbf{F}:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
defined as

\mathbf{F}=(-\partial_{2}\phi,\partial_{1}\phi),

(1.3)

where
\phi
is the unique solution of

-\Delta\phi=\frac{2\pi}{|\omega|}\mathbf{1}_{\omega}\quad\text{ in }\Omega,\quad\phi=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

and notice that
\mathbf{F}
satisfies

\curl\mathbf{F}=\frac{2\pi}{|\omega|}\mathbf{1}_{\omega},\quad{\rm div}\mathbf{F}=0\text{ on }\Omega,\quad\text{ and }\nu\cdot\mathbf{F}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

(1.4)

where
\nu
is the unit normal to
\partial\Omega
pointing inward
\Omega
. Hence,
\Phi\mathbf{F}
is a vector potential corresponding to the magnetic field
b\mathbf{1}_{\omega}\hat{z}
, as
\curl(\Phi\mathbf{F})=b\mathbf{1}_{\omega}
in view of (
1.2
).

1.3.
The Ginzburg-Landau model

Let us suppose that
\Omega
is the cross section of a cylindrical superconductor subject to the magnetic field
b\mathbf{1}_{\omega}\hat{z}
, with corresponding magnetic flux
\Phi
in (
1.2
). As the magnetic flux
\Phi
increases, it is well known that the superconductor undergoes a phase transition between the superconducting and normal states. The state of superconductivity is described by a configuration
(\psi,\mathbf{A})
such that

\psi:\Omega\to\mathbb{C},\quad\mathbf{A}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{2},

with
|\psi|^{2}
representing the local density of superconductivity,
\curl\mathbf{A}
representing the local induced magnetic field, and

\mathbf{j}(\psi,\mathbf{A}):=\re\langle\psi,(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\mathbf{A})\psi\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}

(1.5)

measures the supercurrent. At equilibrium, the physically interesting configurations are the minimizers (or even critical points) of the energy functional

\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}(\psi,\mathbf{A})=\int_{\Omega}\Biggl(|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}-\kappa^{2}|\psi|^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}|\psi|^{4}+\Phi^{2}|\curl(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{F})|^{2}\Biggr)\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x,

(1.6)

defined on the following function space

\mathcal{H}=\{(\psi,\mathbf{A})\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})\times H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\colon\Div\mathbf{A}=0\text{ on }\Omega,~\nu\cdot\mathbf{A}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\}.

(1.7)

The definition of the functional
\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}
involves a positive constant
\kappa
called the Ginzburg–Landau parameter, which is a characteristic of the superconducting material. Throughout this paper,
\kappa
will be fixed.

We introduce also the ground state energy
\mathrm{E}(\Phi)
of the functional
\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}
as

\mathrm{E}(\Phi)=\inf\{\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}(\psi,\mathbf{A})\colon(\psi,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{H}\}.

(1.8)

We say that a configuration
(\psi,\mathbf{A})
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}
if
(\psi,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}(\psi,\mathbf{A})=\mathrm{E}(\Phi)
.

1.4.
The effective model

Our aim is to understand the behavior of
\mathrm{E}(\Phi)
and the minimizing configurations in the limit of large
\Phi
(which by (
1.2
) is equivalent to the limit of large
b
). We will show that the strong magnetic field in the interior domain
\omega
will essentially force the minimizing
\psi
to be zero there. This motivates the introduction of an effective functional, defined on the space

\mathcal{H}_{0}=\{(u,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{H}\colon u=0\text{ on }\omega\},

(1.9)

as

\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(u,\mathbf{A})=\int_{\Omega_{0}}\Bigl(|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{A})u|^{2}-\kappa^{2}|u|^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}|u|^{4}\Bigr)\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x+\Phi^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\curl(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{F})|^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x,

(1.10)

where
\Omega_{0}:=\Omega\setminus{\overline{\omega}}
. Note that
\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}
acts on configurations
(u,\mathbf{A})
where the function
u
has support in the non-simply connected domain
\Omega_{0}
. We can and will view it as being defined in
\Omega_{0}
with a Dirichlet boundary condition
u=0
on
\partial\omega
. In fact, thanks to the smoothness of
\partial\omega
, a function
u
belongs to the space

\mathcal{X}(\Omega_{0})=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega_{0};\mathbb{C})\colon u=0\mbox{ on }\partial\omega\}

(1.11)

if and only if it has an extension
\widetilde{u}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
and
\widetilde{u}=0
on
\omega
.

The functional
\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}
turns out to be the relevant approximation of the functional
\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}
in the limit of large
\Phi
. We describe this approximation in Theorem
1.1
below, which involves the effective ground state energy that we define as

\mathrm{G}(\Phi)=\inf\{\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(u,\mathbf{A})\colon(u,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{H}_{0}\}.

(1.12)

Thanks to invariance under gauge transformations, it suffices to study
\mathrm{G}(\Phi)
for
0\leq\Phi<1
. In fact, on
\Omega_{0}
, we define the function

U_{n}(x)=\exp\left(\mathrm{i}n\int_{\ell(x_{*},x)}\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}\mathrm{r}\right),

(1.13)

where
x_{*}
is a fixed point in
\Omega_{0}
and
\ell(x_{*},x)
is any path in
\Omega_{0}
connecting
x_{*}
and
x
. The definition of
U_{n}
is independent of the choice of the path and the relevance of
U_{n}
is that it is
C^{1}
smooth and satisfies (see
[
5
, p. 21]
)

\nabla U_{n}=\mathrm{i}nU_{n}\mathbf{F}\text{ on }\Omega_{0}.

(1.14)

Consequently, we have the following identity

U_{n}^{-1}(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{F})U_{n}=-\mathrm{i}\nabla-(\Phi-n)\mathbf{F},

(1.15)

which allows us to shift
\Phi
by an integer without changing the energy
\mathrm{G}(\Phi)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

As
\Phi\to+\infty
, the ground state energies
\mathrm{E}(\Phi)
and
\mathrm{G}(\Phi)
introduced in (
1.8
) and (
1.12
) satisfy

\mathrm{E}(\Phi)=\mathrm{G}(\Phi)+o(1).

Moreover, it holds the following.

i)

The function
\Phi\mapsto\mathrm{G}(\Phi)
is periodic with period
1
.

ii)

If
0\leq\Phi_{0}<1
and
(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a sequence such that, every
(\psi_{n},\mathbf{A}_{n})
is a minimzing configurations of
\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}
for
\Phi=\Phi_{n}:=\Phi_{0}+n
, then there exists a minimizer
(u_{*},\mathbf{A}_{*})\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
of
\mathcal{G}_{\Phi_{0}}
and a subsequence
(n_{k})
such that

\begin{gathered}|\psi_{b_{n_{k}}}|\to|u_{*}|\text{ in }H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}),\\
U_{n_{k}}^{-1}\psi_{n_{k}}\to u_{*}\text{ in }H^{1}(\Omega_{0};\mathbb{C}),\\
\mathbf{j}(\psi_{n_{k}},\mathbf{A}_{n_{k}})\to\mathbf{j}_{*}\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}),\end{gathered}

where
\mathbf{j}_{*}
is defined as

\quad\mathbf{j}_{*}=\langle u_{*},(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-b_{0}\mathbf{A}_{*})u_{*}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}.

iii)

If
\Phi\in\mathbb{N}
, then any minimizer
(u,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
of
\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}
satisfies

|u|>0,\quad\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{F},\quad\mathbf{j}(u,\mathbf{A})=0.

We will prove Theorem
1.1
in Section
2
.

1.5.
Oscillations for the effective model

Consider the magnetic Laplacian in the non-simply connected domain
\Omega_{0}=\Omega\setminus\overline{\omega}
,

\mathsf{H}_{\Phi}^{\Omega_{0}}=(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{F})^{2}\,,

(1.16)

with Neumann boundary condition

\nu\cdot\nabla u=0
on
\partial\Omega
, and Dirichlet boundary condition
u=0
on
\partial\omega
.
Since we do not impose Neumann boundary condition on the inner boundary
\partial\omega
,
\mathsf{H}_{\Phi}^{\Omega_{0}}
differs from the Neumann realization considered in
[
9
]
, but it can still be studied using the same methods as in
[
9
]
.
The operator
\mathsf{H}_{\Phi}^{\Omega_{0}}
has compact resolvent, its spectrum is purely discrete, and its lowest eigenvalue is

\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mathcal{X}(\Omega_{0})\\
u\not=0\end{subarray}}\frac{\|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{F})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}},

(1.17)

where
\mathcal{X}(\Omega_{0})
is the space introduced in (
1.11
).

With the help of
\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi)
, we may characterize whether a minimizer of
\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}
is identically zero or not. Actually, it is straightforward to verify that

\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi)<\kappa^{2}\Longrightarrow\mathrm{G}(\Phi)<0,

(1.18)

by using
(tv,0)
as a trial state, with
v
a ground state of
\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi)
and
t>0
sufficiently small.
Since
\mathrm{G}(\Phi)\leq\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(0)=0
, then denoting by
u_{\Phi}
a minimizer of
\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}
, we have that
u_{\Phi}
is not identically zero whenever
\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi)<\kappa^{2}
.

The transition from
u_{\Phi}=0
to
u_{\Phi}\not=0
is then related to the monotonicity of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi)
, which we would like to explore next.
The diamagnetic inequality yields that

\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi)\geq\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(0)>0,

(1.19)

and thanks to (
1.15
), the following holds.

Proposition 1.2

(
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

The function
\Phi\mapsto\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi)
is continuous, periodic with period
1
, non-constant, and its minimum is attained at
\Phi=0
while its maximum is attained at
\Phi=\frac{1}{2}
. Moreover,

\begin{gathered}\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi+\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$})=\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$})\,,\\
\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi)>\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(0)\quad\Bigl(\Phi\not\in\mathbb{N}\Bigr)\,,\end{gathered}

(1.20)

and

\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi)<\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$})\quad\Bigl(\Phi\not\in\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}+\mathbb{N}\Bigr).

(1.21)

Consequently, we get that a minimizer of
u_{\Phi}
oscillates between
u_{\Phi}=0
(normal state) and
u_{\Phi}\not=0
(superconducting state) as
\Phi
varies.

Corollary 1.3
.

A)

There exists a positive constant
c_{*}\in(0,1]
such that the following is true. Suppose that
0<\kappa<c_{*}\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(1/2)}
, and consider the two sequences
(\Phi_{n}:=n-\frac{1}{2})
and
(\Phi_{n}^{\prime}:=n)
. Then, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
0<\Phi_{n}<\Phi_{n}^{\prime}<\Phi_{n+1}
and

i)

u_{\Phi_{n}}=0
for any critical point
(u_{\Phi_{n}},\mathbf{A}_{\Phi_{n}})
of
\mathcal{G}_{\Phi_{n}}
;

ii)

u_{\Phi_{n}^{\prime}}\not=0
for any minimizer
(u_{\Phi_{n}^{\prime}},\mathbf{A}_{\Phi_{n}^{\prime}})
of
\mathcal{G}_{\Phi_{n}^{\prime}}
.

B)

If
\kappa>\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(1/2)}
, then any minimizer
(u_{\Phi},\mathbf{A}_{\Phi})
of
\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}

satisfies
u_{\Phi}\not=0
.

1.6.
The magnetic Laplacian with a local magnetic field

In order to investigate the occurrence of oscillations for the initial model in (
1.6
), one has to study

\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})\\
u\not=0\end{subarray}}\frac{\|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{F})u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}},

(1.22)

the lowest eigenvalue of the magnetic Laplacian in
L^{2}(\Omega)
,

\mathcal{H}_{\Phi}^{\Omega}=(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{F})^{2},

(1.23)

with Neumann boundary conditions
\nu\cdot\nabla u=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Subject to the local magnetic field
\Phi\curl\mathbf{F}=\frac{2\pi}{|\omega|}\Phi\mathbf{1}_{\omega}=b\mathbf{1}_{\omega}
, this eigenvalue problem is also related to the existence of discrete spectrum for locally deformed leaky wires
[
4
]
.

Guided by Theorem
1.1
, we anticipate that the eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi)
, introduced in (
1.17
), approximates the eigenvalue

\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi)
in the limit of large
\Phi
. This can likely be obtained from
[
11
]
in the framework of norm resolvent convergence, but we give here another proof which is closer to the one of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.4
.

As
\Phi\to+\infty
, the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi)
and
\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi)
introduced in (
1.22
) and (
1.20
) satisfy

\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi)=\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi)+o(1).

Notice that, by comparing with the Dirichlet problem in
\Omega_{0}
, we have the non-asymptotic bound,

\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi)\leq\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi),\quad\forall\,\Phi>0.

(1.24)

To prove Theorem
1.4
, we will establish the matching asymptotic lower bound,

\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi)\geq\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi)+o(1).

(1.25)

This will be done in Section
3
. As a direct consequence of Theorem
1.4
, we get that the minimizers of the functional
\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}
in (
1.6
) undergo indefinite oscillations between the normal and superconducting phases.

Corollary 1.5
.

Suppose that
0<\kappa<c_{*}\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(1/2)}
, where
c_{*}\in(0,1)
is a constant independent of
\kappa
and
\Phi
. There exist two sequences
(\Phi_{n})
and
(\Phi_{n}^{\prime})
such that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
0<\Phi_{n}<\Phi_{n}^{\prime}<\Phi_{n+1}
and

i)

\psi_{\Phi_{n}}=0
for any critical point
(\psi_{\Phi_{n}},\mathbf{A}_{\Phi_{n}})
of
\mathcal{E}_{\Phi_{n}}
;

ii)

\psi_{\Phi_{n}^{\prime}}\not=0
for any minimizer
(\psi_{\Phi_{n}},\mathbf{A}_{\Phi_{n}^{\prime}})
of
\mathcal{E}_{\Phi_{n}^{\prime}}
.

Moreover, if
\kappa>\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(1/2)}
, then for all
\Phi>0
, any minimizer
(\psi_{\Phi},\mathbf{A}_{\Phi})
of
\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}
satisfies
\psi_{\Phi}\not=0
.

To construct the sequences in the above corollary, we note that a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}
is not identically zero whenever
\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi)<\kappa^{2}
, and we use Proposition
1.2
and Theorem
1.4
to construct
(\Phi_{n})
and
(\Phi_{n}^{\prime})
such that
\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi_{n})>\kappa^{2}
and
\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi_{n}^{\prime})<\kappa^{2}
.

2.
Convergence to the effective functional

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.3
.
In the various calculations, we will encounter, in addition to the flux
\Phi
, the integer and fractional parts

\lfloor\Phi\rfloor=\sup\{m\in\mathbb{Z}\colon m\leq\Phi\},\quad\{\Phi\}=\Phi-\lfloor\Phi\rfloor.

(2.1)

We start by proving that the effective ground state energy is periodic with respect to
\Phi
. For any
\Phi>0
and
n\geq 0
, we define the transformation

\mathcal{L}_{\Phi,n}(u,\mathbf{A})=\left(U_{n}u,\frac{\Phi}{\Phi+n}\mathbf{A}+\frac{n}{\Phi+n}\mathbf{F}\right).

Proposition 2.1
.

For all
\Phi>0
, we have

\mathrm{G}(\Phi+1)=\mathrm{G}(\Phi),

where
\mathrm{G}(\cdot)
is introduced in (
1.12
). Moreover, with
n=\lfloor\Phi\rfloor
and
\Phi_{0}=\{\Phi\}
, we have

(u_{*},\mathbf{A}_{*})\text{ is a minimizer of }\mathcal{G}_{\Phi_{0}}\iff(u,\mathbf{A})=\mathcal{L}_{\Phi_{0},n}(u_{*},\mathbf{A}_{*})\text{ is a minimizer of }\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}.

Proof.

Let
(u,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is the space introduced in (
1.9
). With
\tilde{u}=U_{1}u
and
\tilde{\mathbf{A}}=\frac{\Phi}{\Phi+1}\mathbf{A}+\frac{1}{\Phi+1}\mathbf{F}
, the configuration
\bigl(\tilde{u},\tilde{\mathbf{A}})\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, and thanks to (
1.15
), we have

\mathcal{G}_{\Phi+1}(\tilde{u},\tilde{\mathbf{A}})=\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(u,\mathbf{A}).

Minimizing over
(u,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, we get
\mathrm{G}\bigl(\Phi+1\bigr)=\mathrm{G}(\Phi)
, and if
(u,\mathbf{A})
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}
, then
(\tilde{u},\tilde{\mathbf{A}})
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{G}_{\Phi+1}
. By iteration, we cover the case where
\Phi=\Phi_{0}+n
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Suppose that
\Phi\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, by Proposition
2.1
, the set of minimizers of
\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}
is
\{(cU_{\Phi}u_{*},\mathbf{F})\colon c\in\mathbb{C}\text{ with }|c|=1\}
,
where
u_{*}
is the unique positive solution of

-\Delta u_{*}=\kappa^{2}(1-u^{2})u\text{ on }\Omega_{0},\quad u_{*}=0\text{ on }\partial\omega,\quad\nu\cdot\nabla u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

Consequently, for a minimizer
(u_{\Phi},\mathbf{F})
of
\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}
, we have

|u_{\Phi}|>0,\quad\mathbf{j}(u_{\Phi},\mathbf{F})=\re\langle u_{\Phi},(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{F})u_{\Phi}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}=0.

In fact,
u_{\Phi}=cU_{\Phi}u_{*}
, with
|c|=1
and
u_{*}
real-valued, and by (
1.13
),

\langle u_{\Phi},(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{F})u_{\Phi}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}=\langle u_{*},(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{F}+\Phi\mathbf{F})u_{*}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathrm{i}u_{*}\nabla u_{*}.

We next give a non-asymptotic upper bound on the ground state energy
\mathrm{E}(\Phi)
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathrm{E}(\Phi)
and
\mathrm{G}(\Phi)
be the ground state energies introduced in (
1.8
) and (
1.12
) respectively. Then, for all
\Phi\geq 0
, we have

\mathrm{E}(\Phi)\leq\mathrm{G}(\Phi)\leq 0.

Proof.

Firstly,
\mathrm{G}(\Phi)\leq\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(0,\mathbf{F})=0
. Secondly, letting
(u,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
be a minimizer of
\mathrm{G}(\Phi)
, we observe that
(u,\mathbf{A})\in\mathcal{H}
and
\mathrm{E}(\Phi)\leq\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}(u,\mathbf{A})=\mathcal{G}_{\Phi}(u,\mathbf{A})=\mathrm{G}(\Phi)
.
∎

To establish an asymptotic lower bound on
\mathrm{E}(\Phi)
, we collect below some known estimates on the minimizing configurations of
the functional
\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}
(see
[
5
, Prop. 10.3.1 & Lem. 10.3.2]
), where
\|\cdot\|_{p}
denotes the standard norm on
L^{p}(\Omega)
.

Proposition 2.4
.

There exists a positive constant
C
such that, if
\Phi>0
and
(\psi_{\Phi},\mathbf{A}_{\Phi})\in\mathcal{H}
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}
, then

\begin{gathered}\|\psi\|_{\infty}\leq 1,\quad\|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{A})\psi\|_{2}\leq\kappa\|\psi\|_{2},\quad\|\curl(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{F})\|_{2}\leq\frac{\kappa}{\Phi}\|\psi\|_{2},\end{gathered}

and

\|\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{F}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C\kappa}{\Phi}\|\psi\|_{2}.

(2.2)

Notice that (
2.2
) results from the curl-div inequality

\|\mathbf{a}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega}\|\curl\mathbf{a}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

valid for any vector field satisfying

\mathbf{a}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}),\quad\curl\mathbf{a}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}),\quad\mathrm{div\,}\mathbf{a}=0\text{ on }\Omega,\quad\nu\cdot\mathbf{a}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

The vector field
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{F}
satisfies the aforementioned conditions and (see
[
5
, p. 143]
)

\nabla^{\bot}\curl(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{F})=-\Phi^{-1}\mathbf{j}(\psi,\mathbf{A})\text{ on }\Omega,\quad\curl(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{F})=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

(2.3)

where
\nabla^{\bot}=(-\partial_{x_{2}},\partial_{x_{1}})
and
\mathbf{j}(\psi,\mathbf{A})
is the supercurrent introduced in (
1.5
). We get then (
2.2
) with
C=2C_{\Omega}
.

Starting with any sequence of minimizing configurations of
\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}
, we can extract, by a standard compactness argument, limits of the magnetic potential and the modulus of the order parameter.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(\psi_{\Phi},\mathbf{A}_{\Phi})_{\Phi>0}\subset\mathcal{H}
be a family of configurations such that
(\psi_{\Phi},\mathbf{A}_{\Phi})
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}
for every
\Phi>0
. Then, there exists a configuration
(\rho_{*},\mathbf{a}_{*})\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
and a sequence
(\Phi_{n})
such that
\Phi_{n}\to+\infty
and

\begin{gathered}|\psi_{\Phi_{n}}|\to\rho_{*}\text{ weakly in }H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})\text{ and strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}),\\
\Phi_{n}(\mathbf{A}_{\Phi_{n}}-\mathbf{F})\to\mathbf{a}_{*}\text{ weakly in }H^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\text{ and strongly in }H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}).\end{gathered}

Proof.

Let
\rho_{\Phi}=|\psi_{\Phi}|
and
\mathbf{a}_{\Phi}=\Phi(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{F})
. Thanks to Proposition
2.4
, we have

\|\psi_{\Phi}\|_{2}^{2}\leq|\Omega|,\quad\|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{A})\psi_{\Phi}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\kappa^{2}|\Omega|,\quad\|\mathbf{a}_{\Phi}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C^{2}\kappa^{2}|\Omega|,

and by the diamagnetic inequality, we get

\|\rho_{\Phi}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla\rho_{\Phi}\|^{2}_{2}\leq(1+\kappa^{2})|\Omega|.

By the Banach–Alaoglu theorem, we get a sequence
(\Phi_{n})
and a weak limit
(\rho_{*},\mathbf{a}_{*})
such that

\Phi_{n}\to+\infty,\quad\rho_{\Phi_{n}}\to\rho_{*}\text{ weakly in }H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})\text{ and }\mathbf{a}_{\Phi_{n}}\to\mathbf{a}_{*}\text{ weakly in }H^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Thanks to the compact embeddings of
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
and
H^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
and
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
respectively, we can arrange for the sequences to converge strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
and
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, respectively. The strong convergence in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
yields

\mathrm{div\,}\mathbf{a}_{*}=0\text{ on }\Omega,\quad\nu\cdot\mathbf{a}_{*}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

hence
(\rho_{*},\mathbf{a}_{*})\in\mathcal{H}
. It remains to show that
\rho_{*}=0
on
\omega
. For this, we write by (
2.2
) and Cauchy’s inequality,

\|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{A})\psi\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{F})\psi\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}-\widehat{C}\kappa^{2}|\Omega|,

where
\widehat{C}
is a positive constant.
Then, we use the min-max principle and write

\|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{A})\psi\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{1}(\Phi\mathbf{F},\omega)\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}-\widehat{C}\kappa^{2}|\Omega|,

where
\lambda_{1}(\Phi\mathbf{F},\omega)
is the lowest eigenvalue for
(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{F})^{2}
in
L^{2}(\omega)
, with Neumann boundary condition on
\omega
. Knowing that
\lambda_{1}(\Phi\mathbf{F},\omega)\to+\infty
as
\Phi\to+\infty
(see
[
5
, Thm. 8.1.1]
), we deduce that

\|\rho_{\Phi_{n}}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\to 0
,
hence
\rho_{*}=0
on
\omega
.
∎

Our next task is to refine the convergence of the minimizing order parameter when restricted to
\Omega_{0}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(\psi_{\Phi},\mathbf{A}_{\Phi})_{\Phi>0}\subset\mathcal{H}
be a family of configurations such that
(\psi_{\Phi},\mathbf{A}_{\Phi})
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}
for every
\Phi>0
. Then, there exists a sequence
(\Phi_{n})
and a function
u_{*}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{C})
such that
\Phi_{n}\to+\infty
and

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{E}_{\Phi_{n}}(\psi_{\Phi_{n}},\mathbf{A}_{\Phi_{n}})\geq\mathrm{G}(\Phi_{n})+o(1),\\
U_{\lfloor\Phi_{n}\rfloor}^{-1}\psi_{\Phi_{n}}\to\psi_{*}\text{ in }H^{1}(\Omega_{0};\mathbb{C}),\\
\psi_{*}=0\text{ on }\omega,\end{gathered}

where, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
U_{k}
is the function introduced in (
1.13
).

Proof.

The proof relies on a compactness argument and the extraction of a subsequence. To simplify the exposition, we will skip the reference to the subsequence.

For
\Phi>0
, we write
\Phi=\Phi_{0}+n
where
n=\lfloor\Phi\rfloor
and
\Phi_{0}=\{\Phi\}\in[0,1)
. Without loss of generality, we will assume that
\Phi_{0}
is a fixed constant. Starting from
(\psi,\mathbf{A})=(\psi_{\Phi},\mathbf{A}_{\Phi})
, we put

\rho=|\psi|,\quad u=u_{\Phi}:=U_{n}^{-1}\psi,\quad\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{a}_{\Phi}:=\Phi_{0}\mathbf{F}+\Phi(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{F}),

and we observe by (
2.2
) that

\|\mathbf{a}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{0},

where
C_{0}
is a positive constant, independent of
\Phi
. Moreover, note that
u
is defined on
\Omega_{0}
,
|u|=\rho|_{\Omega_{0}}
, and the following identities hold in
\Omega_{0}
,

\begin{gathered}(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\mathbf{a})u=U_{n}^{-1}(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{A})\psi,\\
(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\mathbf{a})^{2}u=U_{n}^{-1}(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{A})^{2}\psi=\kappa^{2}(1-|u|^{2})u.\end{gathered}

Consequently, with

0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}:=\mathrm{dist}(\partial\omega,\partial\Omega),\quad\Omega_{\varepsilon}:=\{x\in\Omega_{0}\colon\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\omega)>\varepsilon\},

(2.4)

we have by Proposition
2.4
and elliptic estimates,

\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\leq C_{\varepsilon},

where
C_{\varepsilon}
depends on
\varepsilon
but is independent of
\Phi
. Cantor’s diagonal argument yields a sequence and a function
u_{*}\in H^{2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega_{0};\mathbb{C})
such that, for
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
,

u\to u_{*}\text{ weakly in }H^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{C})\mbox{ and strongly in }H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{C}),

(2.5)

and by Propositions
2.4
and
2.5
,

\begin{gathered}\|u_{*}\|_{\infty}\leq 1,\\
\rho\to\rho_{*}\text{ in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})\text{ with }\rho_{*}|_{\omega}=0,\\
\mathbf{a}\to\Phi_{0}\mathbf{F}+\mathbf{a}_{*}\text{ in }H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}).\end{gathered}

(2.6)

Consequently,
|u_{*}|=\rho_{*}|_{\Omega_{0}}
. Moreover, by monotone convergence

\int_{\Omega_{0}}|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi_{0}\mathbf{A}_{*})u_{*}|^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi_{0}\mathbf{A}_{*})u_{*}|^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x,

where

\mathbf{A}_{*}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{F}+\Phi_{0}^{-1}\mathbf{a}_{*}&\text{if }\Phi_{0}>0,\\
\mathbf{F}&\text{if }\Phi_{0}=0,\end{cases}

and we have by (
2.5
)-(
2.6
) and Proposition
2.4
,

\forall\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}),\quad\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi_{0}\mathbf{A}_{*})u_{*}|^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x\leq\limsup_{\Phi\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{A})\psi|^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x\leq\kappa^{2}|\Omega|,

hence it follows that

\int_{\Omega_{0}}|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi_{0}\mathbf{A}_{*})u_{*}|^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x\leq\kappa^{2}|\Omega|.

Thus, we have
(u_{*},\mathbf{A}_{*})\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
and, for
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}(\Phi)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{\Phi}(\psi,\mathbf{A})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\Bigl(|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\mathbf{a})u|^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}|u|^{4}\Bigr)\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x-\kappa^{2}\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega}|\curl\mathbf{a}|^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x.

Sending
\Phi\to+\infty
(along the sequence
(\Phi_{n})
) and then
\varepsilon\to 0
, we get by monotone convergence

\liminf_{\Phi_{n}\to+\infty}\mathrm{E}(\Phi)\geq\mathcal{G}_{\Phi_{0}}(u_{*},\mathbf{A}_{*})\geq\mathrm{G}(\Phi_{0}).

To finish the proof, we use Propositions
2.3
and
2.1
, and this yields that
(u_{*},\mathbf{A}_{*})
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{G}_{\Phi_{0}}
.
∎

Collecting Proposition
2.3
and
2.6
, we get the conclusion of Theorem
1.1
. Corollary
1.3
also results from a standard argument, and we refer to the proof of
[
12
, Corollary 1.5]
for details.

3.
The effective eigenvalue and oscillations under local fields

In this section we prove Theorem
1.4
, from which Corollary
1.5
follows by a standard argument (see
[
12
, Proof of Thm. 1.7(2)]
).

For every
\Phi>0
, let
u_{\Phi}
denote a normalized ground state of
\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi)
.
Similar to Proposition
2.5
, we can prove that the function
\rho_{\Phi}:=|u_{\Phi}|
is bounded in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exist positive constants
C_{1},\Phi_{1}
such that, for all
\Phi\geq\Phi_{1}
, we have

\|\rho_{\Phi}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{1}

and

\int_{\omega}|\rho_{\Phi}|^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\Phi}.

Proof.

The first inequality is the consequence of Proposition
1.2
and the non-asymptotic bound in (
1.24
). The second inequality follows from the min-max principle,

\lambda_{1}(\Phi\mathbf{F},\omega)\int_{\omega}|\rho_{\Phi}|^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x\leq\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi),

where
\lambda_{1}(\Phi\mathbf{F},\Omega)
is the lowest eigenvalue for
(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi\mathbf{F})^{2}
in
L^{2}(\omega)
, with Neumann boundary condition on
\omega
. We obtain the desired inequality thanks to the known asymptotics
\lambda_{1}(\Phi\mathbf{F},\omega)\sim\Theta_{0}|\curl\mathbf{F}|\Phi
, where
\Theta_{0}\approx 0.59
is the de Gennes constant (see
[
5
, Thm. 8.1.1]
).
∎

Next we define the function
v_{\Phi}
on
\Omega_{0}
as

v_{\Phi}=U_{n}^{-1}u_{\Phi},\quad n=\lfloor\Phi\rfloor,

where
U_{n}
is the function introduced in (
1.13
). Similar to Proposition
2.6
, we can prove that
v_{\Phi}
is bounded locally in
H^{2}(\Omega_{0})
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}:=\mathrm{dist}(\partial\omega,\partial\Omega)
. There exist positive constants
C_{\varepsilon},\Phi_{\varepsilon}
such that, for all
\Phi\geq\Phi_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\|v_{\Phi}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{C})}\leq C_{\varepsilon},

where
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is introduced in (
2.4
).

Proof.

With
\Phi_{0}=\{\Phi\}
, we have

(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi_{0}\mathbf{F})^{2}v_{\Phi}=\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(b)v_{\Phi}\text{ on }\Omega_{0}.

The bound in
H^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{C})
is the result of elliptic
L^{2}
estimates.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Thanks to Proposition
3.1
, the function
v_{\Phi}
is almost normalized in
L^{2}(\Omega_{0})
; in fact, we have as
\Phi\to+\infty
,

\int_{\Omega_{0}}|v_{\Phi}|^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x=1+\mathcal{O}(1/\Phi).

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Thanks to (
1.24
), we have

\limsup_{\Phi\to+\infty}\bigl(\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi)-\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi)\bigr)\leq 0,

so it suffices to show that

\liminf_{\Phi\to+\infty}\bigl(\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi)-\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi)\bigr)\geq 0.

If this does not hold, then there exist a positive constant
c_{*}
and an unbounded set
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that,

\forall\,\Phi\in\mathcal{I},\quad\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi)-\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi)<-c_{*}.

(3.1)

Since
(\rho_{\Phi})_{\Phi\in\mathcal{I}}
is bounded in
H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
, and
(v_{\Phi})_{\Phi\in\mathcal{I}}
is bounded in
H^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{C})
for every
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, there are functions

\rho_{*}\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}),\quad v_{*}\in H^{2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega_{0};\mathbb{C}),

and a sequence
(\Phi_{k})\subset\mathcal{I}
such that

\Phi_{k}\to+\infty,\quad\{\Phi_{k}\}\to\Phi_{*}\in[0,1],

and

\rho_{\Phi_{k}}\to\rho_{*}\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}),\quad v_{\Phi_{k}}\to v_{*}\text{ strongly in }H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}).

Moreover, we have

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\{\Phi_{k}\}\mathbf{F})v_{\Phi_{k}}|^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi_{k}\mathbf{F})u_{\Phi_{k}}|^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x\leq\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi_{k})\leq\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(1/2),

so that, by taking
k\to+\infty
then
\varepsilon\to 0_{+}
we have by monotone convergence

\int_{\Omega_{0}}|\nabla v_{*}|^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x\leq\|\mathbf{F}\|_{\infty}^{2}+\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(1/2).

Thus,
v_{*}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{0};\mathbb{C})
and
|v_{*}|=\rho_{*}
. By Proposition
3.1
we get that
\rho_{*}|_{\partial\omega}=0
, hence
v_{*}|_{\partial\omega}=0
, and by Remark
3.3
, we have

\int_{\Omega_{0}}|v_{*}|^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x=1
.

Finally, for ever
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, we have

\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi_{k})\geq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\{\Phi_{k}\}\mathbf{F})v_{b_{k}}|^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x,

and by taking
k\to+\infty
, we get

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi_{k})\geq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi_{*}\mathbf{F})v_{*}|^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x,

then by taking
\varepsilon\to 0_{+}
, we get by monotone convergence and the min-max principle

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi_{k})\geq\int_{\Omega_{0}}|(-\mathrm{i}\nabla-\Phi_{*}\mathbf{F})v_{*}|^{2}\mathop{}\!{\mathrm{d}}x\geq\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi_{*}),

and eventually

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\bigl(\lambda_{1}^{\Omega}(\Phi_{k})-\lambda_{1}^{\Omega_{0}}(\Phi_{k})\bigr)\geq 0

which violates (
3.1
).
∎

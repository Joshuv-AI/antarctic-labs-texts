Sutured manifolds and tangles

2.1.
Balanced sutured manifolds and tangles

This subsection reviews some terminologies on balanced sutured manifolds and tangles.

Definition 2.1

(
[
1
]
,
[
6
]
)
.

Let
M
be a compact oriented
3
-manifold with boundary. Suppose
\gamma\subset\partial M
is an embedded closed oriented
1
-submanifold such that

(1)

every component of
\partial M
contains at least one component of
\gamma
,

(2)

[\gamma]=0\in H_{1}(\partial M;\mathbb{Z}).

Let
A(\gamma)\subset\partial M
be an open tubular neighborhood of
\gamma
, and let
R(\gamma)=\partial M\backslash A(\gamma)
.
Let
R_{+}(\gamma)
be the subset of

consisting of components whose boundaries have the same orientation as
\gamma
, and let
R_{-}(\gamma)=R(\gamma)\backslash R_{+}(\gamma)
. We say that
(M,\gamma)
is a
balanced sutured manifold
if it satisfies the following properties:

(1)

The
3
–manifold
M
has no closed components.

(2)

\chi(R_{+}(\gamma))=\chi(R_{-}(\gamma))
.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose
(M,\gamma)
is a balanced sutured manifold.

(1)

A
tangle
in
(M,\gamma)
is a properly embedded
1

T\subset M
so that
\partial T\cap\gamma=\emptyset
.

(2)

A component
T_{0}
of
T
is called
vertical
if
\partial T_{0}\cap R_{+}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
and
\partial T_{0}\cap R_{-}(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
.

(3)

A tangle
T
is called
vertical
if every component of
T
is vertical.

(4)

A tangle
T
is called
balanced
if
|T\cap R_{+}(\gamma)|=|T\cap R_{-}(\gamma)|
.

Remark 2.3
.

In the following, we will use the notation
T\subset(M,\gamma)
to indicate that the tangle
T
is in
(M,\gamma)
.

Remark 2.4
.

Note that a tangle
T\subset(M,\gamma)
is, a priori, un-oriented. When the tangle is vertical, we may orient each component of
T
so that it goes from
R_{+}(\gamma)
to
R_{-}(\gamma)
.

Remark 2.5
.

The tangle
T
in Definition

is allowed to be empty.

(
[

]
)

Suppose
(M,\gamma)
is a balanced sutured manifold,

. If

Suppose

, define the

\displaystyle x_{T}(\alpha)=\min\{x_{T}(S)~|

When

(
[
20
]
)

Suppose

2.2.
Sutured manifold hierarchy

Suppose
(M,\gamma)
is a balanced sutured manifold and
T\subset(M,\gamma)
is a tangle. Suppose
S\subset M
is an oriented properly embedded surface. Under certain mild conditions, Scharlemann
[
20
]
generalized the concepts from
[
1
]
and introduced a process to decompose the triple
(M,\gamma,T)
into a new triple
(M^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},T^{\prime})
, and this process is called a
sutured manifold decomposition
. Extending the work of Gabai in
[
1
]
, Scharlemann
[
20
]
also proved the existence of
sutured manifold hierarchies
for a
T
-taut balanced sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
. In this paper, we will use a modified version of the sutured manifold hierarchy, which is presented in the current subsection.

Definition 2.8

(c.f.
[
2
]
)
.

Suppose
(M,\gamma)
is a balanced sutured manifold and
T\subset(M,\gamma)
is a tangle. An oriented properly embedded surface
S\subset M
is called
admissible
in
(M,\gamma,T)
if

(1)

Every component of
\partial S
intersects
\gamma
nontrivially and transversely.

(2)

For every component
T_{0}
of
T
, choose an arbitrary orientation for
T_{0}
, then all intersections between
T_{0}
and
S
are of the same sign.

(3)

S
has no closed components.

(4)

The number
\frac{1}{2}|S\cap\gamma|-\chi(S)
is even.

The following theorem is essentially a combination of
[
20
, Theorem 4.19]
and
[
7
, Theorem 8.2]
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Suppose
(M,\gamma)
is a balanced sutured manifold. Suppose
K
is a knot in the interior of
M
and view
K
as a tangle in
(M,\gamma)
. If
(M,\gamma)
is
K
–taut, then there is a finite sequence of sutured manifold decompositions

(M,\gamma,K)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle S_{1}}}{{\leadsto}}(M_{1},\gamma_{1},K)\leadsto...\leadsto(M_{n-1},\gamma_{n-1},K)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle S_{n}}}{{\leadsto}}(M_{n},\gamma_{n},T)

so that the following is true.

(1)

For
i=1,...,n
, the surface
S_{i}
is an admissible surface in
(M_{i-1},\gamma_{i-1})
. (
M_{0}=M
and
\gamma_{0}=\gamma
.)

(2)

For
i=1,...,n-1
, we have
S_{i}\cap K=\emptyset
.

(3)

T
is a vertical tangle in
(M_{n},\gamma_{n})
.

(4)

For
i=1,...,n-1
,
(M_{i},\gamma_{i})
is
K
-taut.

(5)

(M_{n},\gamma_{n})
is
T
-taut.

Proof.

The proof of the theorem is essentially a combination of the proofs of
[
20
, Theorem 4.19]
and
[
7
, Theorem 8.2]
. We will only sketch the proof here and point out the adaptions to our current setup.

First, suppose there is a homology class
\alpha\in H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{Z})
so that
\partial_{*}(\alpha)\neq 0\in H_{1}(\partial M;\mathbb{Z})
. The argument in
[
20
, Section 3]
then produces a surface
S
properly embedded in
M
so that

(1)

[S]=[\alpha]\in H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{Z})
.

(2)

The sutured manifold decomposition of
(M,\gamma,K)
along
S
yields a taut sutured manifold.

Note that
S
must have a non-empty boundary. We further perform the following modifications on
S
:

a)

Disregard any closed components of
S
. This makes the decomposition along
S
resulting in a balanced sutured manifold.

b)

Apply the argument in
[
1
, Section 5]
to make
S
satisfying the following extra property: for every component
V
of
R(\gamma)
, the intersection of
\partial S
with
V
is a collection of parallel oriented non-separating simple closed curves or arcs. Note the argument in
[
1
, Section 5]
happens in a collar of
\partial M
inside
M
, and the tangle
K
is in the interior of
M
. So the argument applies to our case.

c)

After step b), we can make
S
admissible by further isotope
\partial S
on
\partial M
via positive stabilizations on
S
, in the sense of
[
14
, Definition 3.1]
. Note that by definition, a positive stabilization creates a pair of intersection points between
\partial S
and
\gamma
, and thus it is possible to modify
S
so that every component of
S
intersects
\gamma
and
\frac{1}{2}|S\cap\gamma|-\chi(S)
is even. By
[
14
, Lemma 3.2]
, we know that after positive stabilizations, the sutured manifold decomposition along
S
still yields a
K
–taut (or
T
–taut, if
S\cap K\neq\emptyset
) balanced sutured manifold.

Suppose
S_{1}
is the resulting surface after the above modifications. If
S_{1}
has a non-trivial intersection with
K
then we are done. If
S_{1}\cap K=\emptyset
, we then know that
S_{1}
is admissible and the sutured manifold decomposition

(M,\gamma,K)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle S_{1}}}{{\leadsto}}(M_{1},\gamma_{1},K)

yields a
K
-taut balanced sutured manifold
(M_{1},\gamma_{1})
. We can run the above argument repeatedly. By
[
20
, Section 4]
, the triple
(M_{1},\gamma_{1},K)
has a smaller complexity than
(M,\gamma,K)
, so this decomposition procedure must end after finitely many steps. There are two possibilities when the procedure ends:

Case 1
. We have a surface
S_{n}
that intersects
K
nontrivially, and
S_{1}
, …,
S_{n-1}
are all disjoint from
K
. After the decomposition along
S_{n}
, the knot
K
becomes a collection of arcs

inside
(M_{n},\gamma_{n})
, and we have a sutured manifold decomposition

(M_{n-1},\gamma_{n-1},K)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle S_{n}}}{{\leadsto}}(M_{n},\gamma_{n},T)

so that
(M_{n},\gamma_{n})
is
T
-taut. Note that
T
has no closed components and by Condition (4) in Definition
2.7
,

is a vertical tangle. Hence the desired properties of the theorem are verified.

Case 2
. All decomposing surfaces
S_{n}
are disjoint from
K
, and we end up with a balanced sutured manifold
(M_{n},\gamma_{n})
so that

\partial_{*}:H_{2}(M_{n},\partial M_{n};\mathbb{Z})\to H_{1}(\partial M_{n};\mathbb{Z})

is the zero map. In this case, the map

\partial_{*}:H_{1}(\partial M_{n};\mathbb{Z})\to H_{1}(M_{n};\mathbb{Z})

is injective.
It then follows from a straightforward diagram chasing (see, for example,
[
4
, Lemma 3.5]
) that
\partial M_{n}
must be a union of

\hat{M}_{n}
be the component of
M_{n}
that contains

, and let
\hat{S}
be a sphere in
\hat{M}_{n}
that is parallel to one of the boundary components. Then
\hat{S}
is a reducing sphere for
\hat{M}_{n}\backslash K
, therefore
(M_{n},\gamma_{n})
cannot be
K
–taut, which yields a contradiction.
∎

(M,\gamma)

Suppose
(M,\gamma)

The boundary of

2.4.
A module structure on
\SHI

We introduce an additional module structure on

when

.

As before, suppose
(M,\gamma)
is a balanced sutured manifold and

is a balanced tangle. Pick an arbitrary closure
(Y,R,L,u)
of
(M,\gamma)
such that
|L\cap R|
is odd and at least
3
(so that the condition of Definition
2.11

and a point

, (2), Proposition 2.1]
, the point

\sigma(q)^{2}=0
. Moreover,
\sigma(q)
commutes with
\mu(R)
and
\mu(p)

\sigma(q)

. The proof of
[

, Theorem 7.12]
applies verbatim to verify the following theorem:

Theorem 2.16
.

For a triple

, the isomorphism class of the sutured instanton Floer homology

(M,\gamma,T)
. Moreover, if

, then the module structure induced by

. ∎

For

, we will call the
\mathbb{C}[X]/(X^{2})
–module structure on
\SHI(M,\gamma,T)
defined as above the

q
.

.

If

has only one component, then the isomorphism class of the module structure is independent of

. In this case, it makes sense to refer to the isomorphism class of the module structure on

.

.

Suppose
(M,\gamma)
is a balanced sutured manifold and

is a balanced tangle. Suppose further that

is an admissible surface in the sense of Definition

Suppose

, and let

in

This follows directly from Equation (

∎

Lemma 2.23

Suppose

(M,\gamma)

. Let

3.1.

This subsection proves the following theorem. The result is inspired by the work of Wang

]

Theorem 3.1

Suppose

is a balanced sutured manifold with

Applying Corollary

In fact, the proof of

\displaystyle\SHI(M,\gamma^{\prime},K)

\displaystyle\cong\SHI(\hat{M},\hat{\gamma},T\cup K),

\displaystyle\SHI(M,\gamma^{\prime},U)

\displaystyle\cong\SHI(\hat{M},\hat{\gamma},T\cup U).

By Lemma
2.23
, we have

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\SHI(\hat{M},\hat{\gamma},T\cup U;\Gamma_{U}(h))=2\cdot\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\SHI(\hat{M},\hat{\gamma},T)

By Lemma

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\SHI(\hat{M},\hat{\gamma},K\cup T;\Gamma_{K}(h))=2\cdot\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\SHI(\hat{M},\hat{\gamma},T)

for all

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\SHI(M,\gamma^{\prime},K)=~

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\SHI(\hat{M},\hat{\gamma},T\cup K)

\displaystyle\geq~

\displaystyle 2\cdot\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\SHI(\hat{M},\hat{\gamma},T)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle 2\cdot\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\SHI(M,\gamma^{\prime}).

Thus by (

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\SHI(M,\gamma,K)\geq 2\cdot\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\SHI(M,\gamma),

and the lemma is proved.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

By Theorem
3.2
,
\SHI(M,\gamma,K)
is a non-trivial free module over
\mathbb{C}[X]/(X^{2})
. Hence by Theorem

,

Therefore,

, where

after isotopy. If

is a closed manifold, then the desired result holds. If
M_{2}
is a manifold with boundary,

\SHI(M,\gamma)\neq 0
, by

and

are both norm-minimizing, therefore

is also a balanced sutured manifold.
By the connected sum formula for

, we have

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\SHI(M_{2},\gamma,K)=2\cdot\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\SHI(M_{2},\gamma).

Hence the desired result follows from induction on the number of factors in the prime decomposition of

.
∎

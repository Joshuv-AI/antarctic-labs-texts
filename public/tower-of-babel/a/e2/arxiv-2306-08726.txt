Orthogonal Analysis of Space-Time Crystals Journal: opticajournal
Zoé-Lise Deck-Léger, Amir Bahrami, Zhiyu Li, Christophe Caloz

Abstract
This paper presents a space-time-wise orthogonal analysis of space-time crystals. This analysis provides a solution consisting of a pair of explicit parametric equations that result from a separate application of the Bloch-Floquet theorem in the (orthogonal) directions of space and time. Compared to previous approaches, this solution offers the benefits of greater simplicity, clearer emphasis on space-time duality and deeper physical insight.



The emergence of photonic crystals
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
in the late 1980s as the optical counterparts of solid-state crystals
[
4
]
represented a major development in the field of electromagnetics. Recently, these crystals have gained even greater interest with the
addition of temporal modulation
to their spatial modulation within the framework of research on space-time metamaterials
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. The resulting
space-time crystals
have already been demonstrated to support a diversity of novel effects, such as distributed parametric amplification
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
, magnetless isolation
[
11
,
12
]
, space-time weighted averaging “dragging”
[
13
,
7
]
, beam deflection
[
14
]
and beam curving
[
15
]
.

The addition of the time dimension dramatically complicates the computation of the dispersion diagram (or photonic bandgap structure) of photonic crystals. An early attempt to address the related challenge has been the plane-wave expansion method reported in
[
16
,
17
]
. However, this approach results in transcendental equations that are intractable and offer no insight into the physics of the crystals. An a priori promising approach to resolve these issues might a priori seem to be the (spectral) frame-hopping technique
[
18
]
, using respectively stationary and instantaneous moving frames for the space-like and time-like regimes
[
19
,
6
]
, but this technique would in fact involve cumbersome bianisotropic parameters
[
20
,
21
,
7
]
and complex space-time frequency coupling
[
5
,
6
]
. A
partial
frame-hopping approach, applying periodicity in the direction of the moving frame but keeping quantities in the laboratory frame, was used in
[
22
,
13
]
to remove the bianisotropic complication; however, this approach could not resolve the issue of coupling complexity.

We present here a
space-time-wise orthogonal approach
of space-time crystals, completely conducted in the laboratory frame, that avoids both bianisotropy and space-time frequency coupling. This approach offers thus maximum simplicity. Furthermore, it unveils fundamental symmetries between the spatial and temporal frequencies, hence highlighting strong duality between the two, and provides deeper insight into the physics of the crystal.

[
2
]
. Figure

[
23
]
), with discrete translation symmetry, or periodicity (duration

\delta z
) in space, corresponding to momentum conservation
[
6
]
. Figures

[

, under different symmetry perspectives: Figure

[

,

, with continuous temporal and discrete spatial symmetries, which could be considered as the logical extension of the spatial crystal in a frame-hoping logic, whereas Fig.

According to the elected strategy of orthogonal treatment of space and time, we shall proceed as follows in our analysis of the crystal: 1) establish the field waveforms with their motion-shifted frequencies and motion-deflected propagation angles in each layer of the crystal; 2) separately compute the corresponding spatial and temporal unit-cell [Fig.
1
(d)] transmission matrices; 3) apply the Bloch-Floquet theorem to each of these matrices to obtain parametric relations for the Bloch-Floquet wavevector and frequency; 4) combine these relations to plot the dispersion diagram and isofrequency curves of the crystal.

Assuming propagation and scattering in the
x-z
plane and s-polarization (the p-polarization case can be treated analogously), as illustrated in Fig.
2
, the electric field waveform in the crystal may be written as the succession of individual layer waveforms, which read

E_{n}^{\pm}=A_{n}^{\pm}\text{e}^{i(k_{x,n}^{\pm}x\pm k_{z,n}^{\pm}z-\omega_{n}^{\pm}t)},

(1a)

with

k_{z,n}^{\pm}=\frac{n_{n}}{c}\omega_{n}^{\pm}\cos\theta_{n}^{\pm},\quad k_{x,n}^{\pm}=\frac{n_{n}}{c}\omega_{n}^{\pm}\sin\theta_{n}^{\pm},

(1b)

where the subscript
n
refers to an arbitrary crystal layer and where the superscript signs correspond to propagation in the
(+z,+x)
– or forward – and
(-z,+x)
– or backward – directions, respectively.

Crystal unit-cell features. (a) Frequency transitions and deflection angles at the interface between layers

and
j
. (b) Spatial and temporal unit-cell transfer matrices.

The motion-shifted frequencies
\omega_{n}^{\pm}
and motion-deflected propagation angles
\theta_{n}^{\pm}
in Eqs. (
1b
) may be found with the help of Fig.
2
(a), which represents the corresponding wavevectors across two layers, labeled
i
and
j
. The phases
\phi_{n}^{\pm}=k_{x,n}^{\pm}x\pm k_{z,n}^{\pm}z-\omega_{n}^{\pm}t
(
n=i,j
) in (
1a
) must be conserved at the interface. Due to the absence of motion in the
x
direction, the component
k_{x}
of the wavevector is conserved at the crystal’s interfaces and is hence unique across the crystal. In contrast, the frequencies,
\omega_{n}^{\pm}
, are not conserved, due to motion in the
z
direction. Enforcing the continuity of the phases at
z=z_{n}+vt
, with
k_{z,n}^{\pm}
given in (
1b
) and
k_{x,n}^{\pm}=k_{x}
yields
[
24
]

\omega^{\pm}_{n}(\omega_{i}^{+})=\frac{1-\beta n_{i}\cos\theta_{i}^{+}}{1\mp\beta n_{n}\cos\theta_{n}^{\pm}}\omega^{+}_{i},

(2)

where
\beta=v/c
is the normalized modulation velocity. The corresponding deflection angles are then found by substituting
\omega_{n,i}^{\pm}
from the second expression in (
1b
) into (
2
), and solving for
\cos\theta_{n}^{\pm}
, which yields
[
24
]

\cos\theta_{n}^{\pm}(\theta_{i}^{+})=\pm\frac{C^{2}\beta\pm D_{n}}{(1+C^{2}\beta^{2})n_{n}},

(3a)

with

C=\frac{n_{i}\sin\theta_{i}^{+}}{1-\beta n_{i}\cos\theta_{i}^{+}}\quad\text{and\quad}D_{n}=\sqrt{(1+C^{2}\beta^{2})n_{n}^{2}-C^{2}}.

(3b)

Note that, at this point, all the quantities in Eqs. (
1
) are expressed in terms of the parameters
\omega_{i}^{+}
and
\theta_{i}^{+}
via Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
).

We can now compute the spatial and temporal transfer matrices of the unit cell. The spatial matrix, corresponding to the horizontal arrows in Fig.
2
(b),
[M_{ki}^{\text{s}}]
, relates the fields at the output of the unit cell,
E_{k}(z_{k})
, to those at the input,
E_{i}(z_{i})
, as

\begin{bmatrix}E_{k}^{+}\\
E_{k}^{-}\end{bmatrix}_{z=z_{k}}=[M_{ki}^{\text{s}}]\begin{bmatrix}E_{i}^{+}\\
E_{i}^{-}\end{bmatrix}_{z=z_{i}}=[I_{kj}][P_{j}^{\text{s}}][I_{ji}][P_{i}^{\text{s}}]\begin{bmatrix}E_{i}^{+}\\
E_{i}^{-}\end{bmatrix}_{z=z_{i}},

(4)

where
[M_{ki}^{\text{s}}]
is composed of interface matrices
[I_{kj}]
and
[I_{ji}]
and propagation matrices
[P_{j}^{\text{s}}]
and
[P_{i}^{\text{s}}]
, which are respectively defined by

\begin{bmatrix}E_{n+1}^{+}\\
E_{n+1}^{-}\end{bmatrix}_{z_{n}}=[I_{n+1,n}]\begin{bmatrix}E_{n}^{+}\\
E_{n}^{-}\end{bmatrix}_{z_{n}},\,\begin{bmatrix}E_{n}^{+}\\
E_{n}^{-}\end{bmatrix}_{z_{n+1}}=[P_{n}^{\text{s}}]\begin{bmatrix}E_{n}^{+}\\
E_{n}^{-}\end{bmatrix}_{z_{n}}.

(5)

The interface matrices are found by applying the boundary conditions in terms of the electric fields at the moving interface and read (see App.
A
)

[I_{mn}]=\frac{1}{2f_{m}^{+}f_{m}^{-}}\begin{bmatrix}f_{n}^{+}g_{m}^{-}+f_{m}^{-}g_{n}^{+}&-f_{n}^{-}g_{m}^{-}+f_{m}^{-}g_{n}^{-}\\
-f_{n}^{+}g_{m}^{+}+f_{m}^{+}g_{n}^{+}&f_{n}^{-}g_{m}^{+}+f_{m}^{+}g_{n}^{-}\end{bmatrix},

(6a)

with

f_{n}^{\pm}=1\mp\beta n_{n}\cos\theta_{n}^{\pm},\quad\text{and}\quad g_{n}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{\eta_{n}}\left(\cos\theta_{n}^{\pm}\mp\beta n_{n}\right),

(6b)

while the propagation matrices are obtained by inserting (
1a
) into the second equation of (
5
) with
z_{n+1}=z_{n}+\ell_{n}
, and read

[P_{n}^{\text{s}}]=\begin{bmatrix}e^{ik_{z,n}^{+}\ell_{n}}&0\\
0&e^{-ik_{z,n}^{-}\ell_{n}}\end{bmatrix}.

(7)

Similarly, the temporal matrix, following the vertical arrows in Fig.
2
(b),
[M_{ik}^{\text{t}}]
, relates the fields at the output of the unit cell,
E_{i}(t_{i})
, to those at the input,
E_{k}(t_{k})
, as

\begin{bmatrix}E_{i}^{+}\\
E_{i}^{-}\end{bmatrix}_{t=t_{i}}=[M_{ik}^{\text{t}}]\begin{bmatrix}E_{k}^{+}\\
E_{k}^{-}\end{bmatrix}_{t=t_{k}}=[P_{i}^{\text{t}}][I_{ij}][P_{j}^{\text{t}}][I_{jk}]\begin{bmatrix}E_{k}^{+}\\
E_{k}^{-}\end{bmatrix}_{t=t_{k}},

(8)

where the interface matrices correspond to those for the spatial unit-cell [Eqs.
6
] with inverted indices, while the temporal propagation matrices reads

[P_{n}^{\text{t}}]=\begin{bmatrix}e^{i\omega^{+}_{n}d_{n}}&0\\
0&e^{i\omega_{n}^{-}d_{n}}\end{bmatrix}.

(9)

We can now proceed to the application of the Bloch-Floquet theorem in the spatial and temporal directions of the crystal to obtain the corresponding wavevectors and frequencies. In the spatial direction, the theorem states
[
25
,
26
]

E^{\pm}(z+\ell)=e^{\pm ik_{z}^{\pm}\ell}E^{\pm}(z),

(10)

where
k_{z}^{\pm}
are the sought-after Bloch-Floquet wavevectors. Successively substituting
E^{\pm}(z)=E^{\pm}_{i}(z_{i})
and
E^{\pm}(z+\ell)=E_{k}^{\pm}(z_{k})
from Fig.
2
(b) and the first relation of (
4
) into this equation yields

\begin{bmatrix}E_{k}^{+}\\
E_{k}^{-}\end{bmatrix}_{z_{k}}=\text{e}^{\pm ik_{z}^{\pm}\ell}\begin{bmatrix}E_{i}^{+}\\
E_{i}^{-}\end{bmatrix}_{z_{i}}=[M_{ki}^{\text{s}}]\begin{bmatrix}E_{i}^{+}\\
E_{i}^{-}\end{bmatrix}_{z_{k}}.

(11)

The second equality in (
11
) is an eigenvalue matrix equation, whose characteristic equation reads

\text{e}^{\pm 2ik_{z}^{\pm}\ell}-\text{tr}[M_{ki}^{\text{s}}]\text{e}^{\pm ik_{z}^{\pm}\ell}+\text{det}[M_{ki}^{\text{s}}]=0,

(12a)

with, using (
4
) with Eqs. (
6
) and (
7
), the explicit terms

\text{det}[M_{ki}^{\text{s}}]=e^{i\Delta\phi^{\text{s}}},

(12b)

and

\text{tr}[M_{ki}^{\text{s}}]=2e^{i\Delta\phi^{\text{s}}/2}\left(C_{i}^{\text{s}}C_{j}^{\text{s}}-AS_{i}^{\text{s}}S_{j}^{\text{s}}\right)=2e^{i\Delta\phi^{\text{s}}/2}\text{tr}[M_{ki,0}^{\text{s}}],

(12c)

where

\Delta\phi^{\text{s}}=\Delta\phi_{i}^{\text{s}}+\Delta\phi_{j}^{\text{s}}
,
C_{n}^{\text{s}}=\cos(\Sigma\phi^\tx{s}_n/2)
,
S_{n}^{\text{s}}=\sin(\Sigma\phi^\tx{s}_n/2)
,

\Delta\phi_{n}^{\text{s}}=(k_{z,n}^{+}-k_{z,n}^{-})\ell_{n},\quad\Sigma{\phi}^{\text{s}}_{n}=(k_{z,n}^{+}+k_{z,n}^{-})\ell_{n},

(12d)

and

A(\theta_{i}^{+})=\frac{{f_{j}^{+}}^{2}{g_{i}^{+}}^{2}+{f_{i}^{+}}^{2}{g_{j}^{+}}^{2}}{2f_{i}^{+}f_{j}^{+}g_{i}^{+}g_{j}^{+}}.

(12e)

Multiplying now (
12a
) with (
12b
) by
\text{e}^{-i\Delta\phi^{\text{s}}}
leads to

\text{e}^{2i(\pm k_{z}^{\pm}\ell-\Delta\phi^{\text{s}}/2)}-\text{tr}[M_{ki,0}^{\text{s}}]\text{e}^{i(\pm k_{z}^{\pm}\ell-\Delta\phi^{\text{s}}/2)}+1=0,

(13)

which is a quadratic equation with solutions

\text{e}^{\pm i(k_{z}^{\pm}\ell\mp\Delta\phi^{\text{s}}/2)}=\frac{\text{tr}[M_{ki,0}^{\text{s}}]}{2}\pm i\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{\text{tr}[M_{ki,0}^{\text{s}}]}{2}\right)^{2}}.

(14)

According to Euler’s formula,
e^{\pm i\theta}=\cos\theta\pm i\sin\theta
, this relation is equivalent to
\cos( k_z^\pm\ell\mp\Delta\phi^{\tx{s}}/2)=\text{tr}[M_{ki,0}^{\text{s}}]/2
, whose inversion yields the explicit formula

k_{z}^{\pm}(\omega_{i}^{+},\theta_{i}^{+})=\frac{1}{\ell}\left[\arccos\left(\frac{\text{tr}[M_{ki,0}^{\text{s}}]}{2}\right)\pm\Delta\phi^{\text{s}}/2\right].

(15)

Similarly, in the temporal direction, the Bloch-Floquet theorem states

E^{\pm}(t+d)=e^{i\omega^{\pm}d}E^{\pm}(t),

(16)

where
\omega^{\pm}
are the sought-after Bloch-Floquet frequencies. Successively substituting
E^{\pm}(t)=E^{\pm}_{k}(t_{k})
and
E^{\pm}(t+d)=E_{i}^{\pm}(t_{i})
from Fig.
2
(b) and the first relation of (
8
) into this equation yields

\begin{bmatrix}E_{i}^{+}\\
E_{i}^{-}\end{bmatrix}_{t_{i}}=\text{e}^{-i\omega^{\pm}d}\begin{bmatrix}E_{k}^{+}\\
E_{k}^{-}\end{bmatrix}_{t_{k}}=[M_{ij}^{\text{t}}]\begin{bmatrix}E_{k}^{+}\\
E_{k}^{-}\end{bmatrix}_{t_{k}}.

(17)

A treatment of this (second equality) eigenvalue matrix equation analogous to that applied to the spatial case straightforwardly leads to the explicit formula

\pm\omega^{\pm}(\omega_{i}^{+},\theta_{i}^{+})=\frac{1}{d}\left(\arccos(\frac{\tx{tr}[M_{ik,0}^\tx{t}]}{2})\pm\Sigma\phi^{\text{t}}/2\right),

(18)

where, from (
8
) with Eqs. (
6
) and (
9
),

\text{det}[M_{ik}^{\text{t}}]=e^{i\Sigma\phi^{\text{t}}}

(19)

and

\text{tr}[M_{ki}^{\text{t}}]=e^{i\Sigma\phi^{\text{t}}/2}\left(C_{i}^{\text{t}}C_{j}^{\text{t}}-AS_{i}^{\text{t}}S_{j}^{\text{t}}\right)=e^{i\Sigma\phi^{\text{t}}/2}\text{tr}[M_{ik,0}^{\text{t}}],

(20)

with
\Sigma\phi^{\text{t}}=\Sigma\phi_{i}^{\text{t}}+\Sigma\phi_{j}^{\text{t}}
,
C_{n}=\cos(\Delta\phi^\tx{t}_n/2)
,
S_{n}=\sin(\Delta\phi^\tx{t}_n/2)
,

\Sigma{\phi}_{n}^{\text{t}}=(\omega_{n}^{+}+\omega_{n}^{-})d_{i},\quad\text{and}\quad\Delta\phi^{\text{t}}_{n}=(\omega_{n}^{+}-\omega_{n}^{-})d_{n},

(21)

and with
A
given in (
12e
).

Our orthogonal spatio-temporal treatment of the problem, illustrated in Fig.
1
(d), has thus led to a pair of
separate
and
explicit
parametric (parameters
\omega_{i}^{+}
and
\theta_{i}^{+}
) formulas for the Bloch-Floquet wavevectors and frequencies of the crystal, namely formulas (
15
) and (
18
), which represents the central result of the paper. These formulas offer the following benefits:

\bullet
Being separate and explicit, they offer maximal simplicity.

\bullet
Their duality, in terms of the substitutions
k_{z}^{\pm}\leftrightarrow\pm\omega^{\pm}
,
\ell\leftrightarrow d
,
\Delta\phi^{\text{s}}\leftrightarrow\Sigma\phi^{\text{t}}
and
\Sigma\phi^{\text{s}}\leftrightarrow\Delta\phi^{\text{t}}
, reveals perfect spatio-temporal symmetry.

\bullet
Their
\arccos
function term, being multi- and complex-valued, provides physical insight into the spatio-temporal periodicity and band structure of
the crystal.

\bullet
The non-zero value of their parameters
\Delta\phi^{\text{s}}
and
\Sigma\phi^{\text{t}}
, due to different forward and backward features, is a direct manifestation and measure of nonreciprocity
[
27
,
28
]
.

Appendix A
Interface Matrix

The boundary conditions at an interface moving in the direction
z
at the velocity
v
, read, in the case of s-polarization
[
18
]
,

\left.E_{y1}-vB_{x1}=E_{y2}-vB_{x2}\right|_{z-vt=0},

(22a)

\left.H_{x1}-vD_{y1}=H_{x2}-vD_{y2}\right|_{z-vt=0}.

(22b)

Inserting forward (
+
) and backward (
-
) plane waves with the fields
E_{yn}^{\pm}=E_{n}^{\pm}
,
D_{yn}^{\pm}=\epsilon_{n}E_{n}^{\pm}
,
H_{xn}^{\pm}=B_{xn}^{\pm}/\mu_{n}
and
cB_{xn}^{\pm}=\pm n_{n}E_{n}^{\pm}\cos\theta
(with
n=1,2
) into these equations yields

\begin{split}E_{1}^{+}&(1-\beta n_{1}\cos\theta_{1}^{+})+E_{1}^{-}(1+\beta n_{1}\cos\theta_{1}^{-})\\
&=E_{2}^{+}(1-\beta n_{2}\cos\theta_{2}^{+})+E_{2}^{-}(1+\beta n_{2}\cos\theta_{2}^{-}),\end{split}

(23a)

\begin{split}E_{1}^{+}&(\cos\theta_{1}^{+}-\beta n_{1})/\eta_{1}-E_{1}^{-}(\cos\theta_{1}^{-}+\beta n_{1})/\eta_{1}\\
&=E_{2}^{+}(\cos\theta_{2}^{+}-\beta n_{2})/\eta_{2}-E_{2}^{-}(\cos\theta_{2}^{-}+\beta n_{2})/\eta_{2},\end{split}

(23b)

where we used
n_{n}=c\sqrt{\epsilon_{n}\mu_{n}}
and
\eta_{n}=\sqrt{\mu_{n}/\epsilon_{n}}
. More compactly, these equations read

\begin{bmatrix}f_{1}^{+}&f_{1}^{-}\\
g_{1}^{+}&-g_{1}^{-}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}E_{1}^{+}\\
E_{1}^{-}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}f_{2}^{+}&f_{2}^{-}\\
g_{2}^{+}&-g_{2}^{-}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}E_{2}^{+}\\
E_{2}^{-}\end{bmatrix}.

(24)

Isolating the column vector
E_{2}
gives then Eqs. (
6
).

Appendix B
Empty-Lattice Approximation

The empty-lattice (or infinitesimal-perturbation) approximation is obtained by letting the refractive index and impedance contrast tend to zero, which corresponds to setting
A=1
in (
12e
). This transforms the spatial relation (
15
) into

\displaystyle\cos(k_z^\pm\ell\mp\Delta\phi^\tx{s}/2)

\displaystyle=\cos\frac{\Sigma\phi^{\text{s}}_{i}}{2}\cos\frac{\Sigma\phi^{\text{s}}_{j}}{2}-\sin\frac{\Sigma\phi^{\text{s}}_{i}}{2}\sin\frac{\Sigma\phi^{\text{s}}_{j}}{2}

\displaystyle=\cos(\Sigma\phi^\tx{s}_i/2+\Sigma\phi^\tx{s}_j/2)

(25)

Setting the arguments of the first and last terms of this equation to be equal, isolating
k_{z}^{\pm}
in the resulting relation, and substituting next (
12d
), yields

k_{z}^{\pm}=\left(\Sigma\phi^{\text{s}}_{i}+\Sigma\phi^{\text{s}}_{j}\pm\Delta\phi^{\text{s}}\right)/(2\ell)=\left(k_{zi}^{\pm}\ell_{i}+k_{zj}^{\pm}\ell_{j}\right)/(2\ell).

(26)

Similarly, the temporal relation (
18
) transforms to

\omega^{\pm}=\left(\omega_{i}^{\pm}d_{i}+\omega_{j}^{\pm}d_{j}\right)/(2d).

(27)

Combining Eqs. (
26
) and (
27
) yields the empty-lattice approximation

\frac{n_{\text{av}}^{\pm}}{c}=\left(\frac{k_{zi}^{\pm}\ell_{i}+k_{zj}^{\pm}\ell_{j}}{\omega_{i}^{\pm}d_{i}+\omega_{j}^{\pm}d_{j}}\right)\frac{d}{\ell}.

(28)

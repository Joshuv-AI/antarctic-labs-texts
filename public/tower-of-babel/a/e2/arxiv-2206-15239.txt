Abstract
Tin-vacancy centers in diamond are promising spin-photon interfaces owing to their high quantum-efficiency, large Debye-Waller factor, and compatibility with photonic nanostructuring. Benchmarking their single-photon indistinguishability is a key challenge for future applications. Here, we report the generation of single photons with 99.7 − 2.5 + 0.3 % 99.7^{+0.3}_{-2.5}\% purity and 63 ​ ( 9 ) % 63(9)\% indistinguishability from a resonantly excited tin-vacancy center in a single-mode waveguide. We obtain quantum control of the optical transition with 1.71 ​ ( 1 ) 1.71(1) -ns-long π \pi -pulses of 77.1 ​ ( 8 ) % 77.1(8)\% fidelity. A modest Purcell enhancement factor of 12 would enhance the indistinguishability to 95 95 %. The greater than 100 100 ms spectral stability shown would then enable strings of up to 10 6 10^{6} identical photons to be generated.



Indistinguishable photons from quantum emitters provide a fundamental resource for scalable quantum communication and have been employed to realize linear optical quantum computation

;
Lanyon et al. 2013
;
Wang et al. 2019
;
Barrett and Kok 2005
, spin-photon and spin-spin entanglement

;
Azuma et al. 2015
;
Li et al. 2019
;
Lago-Rivera et al. 2021
. Experimentally, the photonic indistinguishability can be benchmarked through two-photon quantum interference known as the Hong-Ou-Mandel (HOM) effect. The HOM indistinguishability places a bound on the fidelities achievable in photon-mediated gates and entanglement distribution in measurement-based protocols
Hong et al. 1987
;
Zwerger et al. 2016
;
Rudolph 2017
. This effect has been observed across multiple solid-state emitters such as the nitrogen-, silicon- and germanium-vacancies in diamond

;
Sipahigil et al. 2014
;
Chen et al. 2022
, defects in silicon-carbide
Morioka et al. 2020
, and semiconductor quantum dots
Santori et al. 2002
.

Within solid-state emitters, the negatively charged group-IV centers in diamond stand as promising spin-photon interfaces due to their large Debye-Waller factor (60% - 80%)
Neu et al. 2011
;
Palyanov et al. 2015
;
Bhaskar et al. 2017
;
Görlitz et al. 2020
, competitive quantum efficiency (10% - 80%)
Sukachev et al. 2017
;
Görlitz et al. 2020
;
Bhaskar et al. 2017
;
Iwasaki et al. 2017
and first-order insensitivity to electric-field noise
De Santis et al. 2021
;
Thiering and Gali 2018
. This electric-field agnosticism makes them inherently compatible with complex photonic nanostructuring, as the disordered charge environment present at the diamond interface does not couple deleteriously to a proximate emitter. Accordingly, high collection efficiency
Knall et al. 2022
;
Bhaskar et al. 2020
, Purcell enhanced emission
Rugar et al. 2021
;
Kuruma et al. 2021
, and incorporation into photonic integrated circuits
Wan et al. 2020
have been demonstrated using group-IV centers embedded in diamond. The negatively charged tin-vacancy (SnV) is particularly promising. Its large ground-state orbital splitting inhibits phonon-mediated dephasing
Bradac et al. 2019
, allowing for operation at a temperature accessible in standard helium closed-cycle cryostats. Accordingly, a spin-coherence time (
T_{2}
) of
0.30(8)\;\text{ms}
at
1.7\;\text{K}
has been achieved using a SnV center
Debroux et al. 2021
; longer than that of other group-IV emitters at this temperature. Observation of transform-limited emission in bulk diamond
Trusheim et al. 2020
and advancements in fabrication and charge stability
Görlitz et al. 2022
further demonstrate that SnV centers are a suitable spin-photon interface for quantum networking and measurement-based computation protocols
Zwerger et al. 2016
. Probing the optical coherence of the emitted photons is the only major unexplored benchmark for this spin-photon interface.

In this work, we report the observation of quantum interference of single photons from a SnV center in a single-mode diamond waveguide with an indistinguishability of
63(9)\%
and a single-photon purity of
99.7^{+0.3}_{-2.5}\%
. This is achieved by realizing coherent control of the optical transition of the SnV center with a
\pi
-rotation fidelity of
77.1(8)\%
realized in
1.71(1)
ns. Thus, we show that the SnV center has sufficient photonic coherence to satisfy the requirements for quantum networking.

We perform our experiments on a diamond grown via chemical vapor deposition that has been implanted with Sn
++
ions (350 keV; 7

10^{9}

-2

;

50

200

270

9~\upmu

hom
. A support diamond structure connects the waveguides and provides sufficient structural integrity to allow the chiplet to be pick-and-placed onto the edge of a silicon substrate

The SnV fluorescence collected through the lensed fiber is then routed from inside the cryostat, held at a temperature of 3.6 K, to our optical setup

We first demonstrate optical control of the SnV center through resonant excitation and collection of the PSB. Figure
2
(a) shows the averaged PSB fluorescence during a fixed-length 20-ns-long resonant 619-nm pulse starting at time
\tau=0

P
is parameterized by the saturation parameter
s=P/P_{\text{sat}}
, where
P_{\text{sat}}=31(2)\;\text{nW}
is the resonant laser power at which the Rabi rate
\Omega
equals
\Gamma_{0}/\sqrt{2}
. For each power the excited-state population is fit to a two-level master equation, from which we extract the corresponding
\Omega
and dephasing rate

;
Gorini et al. 1976
;
Lindblad 1976

s=367(1)

1.71(1)\;\text{ns}
to perform an optical
\pi

T_{1}
) of the emitter, with an extracted fidelity of
77.1(8)\%

To investigate the effects of decoherence, the master equation used to fit Fig.
2

We confirm in Fig.
2

\Omega=\Gamma_{0}\sqrt{s/2}

Figure

By varying the frequency of the resonant laser, we also probe the dependence of the fluorescence on the detuning

\Gamma_{\text{PD}}

\Gamma_{\text{PD}}/2\pi=6.39(14)\;\text{MHz}
and an inferred homogeneous linewidth of

64(1)\;\text{MHz}

To probe the indistinguishability of the emitted photons, we isolate the ZPL using spectral filtering. We employ two electro-optic modulators for fast optical pulsing of the resonant excitation laser and time-tag the ZPL fluorescence. This reduces the background from laser scattering in excess of
30

90

4

23.91(3)

following the excitation pulse, which corresponds to
80(1)\%
of the integrated ZPL fluorescence
hom

Routing the ZPL emission through a Hanbury-Brown and Twiss interferometer

, as shown in Fig.
4

;
Debroux et al. 2021

99.7^{+0.3}_{-2.5}\%
.

Figure
4

39.93(2)~\text{ns}

Figure

In this work, we demonstrate coherent control of the optical transition of the SnV in diamond. We achieve a
\pi
-pulse time of
1.71(1)~\text{ns}
and two-photon quantum interference of resonant photons with an indistinguishability of
63(9)\%
. Purcell enhancement of the emitter, such as through optical microcavities
Riedel et al. 2017
or photonic crystal cavities
Bhaskar et al. 2020
;
Rugar et al. 2021
;
Kuruma et al. 2021
, increases the radiative decay rate and thus reduces the sensitivity to dephasing. An overall Purcell enhancement factor of 12, already achieved using SnV centers in nanocavities
Rugar et al. 2021
;
Kuruma et al. 2021
, would yield a photon indistinguishability in excess of 95%. Moreover, fabrication and material processing improvements, known to result in improved optical quality of SnV centers in bulk diamond
Görlitz et al. 2020
, should enhance the optical coherence. These advancements provide a feasible route to near-unity photon indistinguishability in the near-term.

Using the two-photon interference presented in this work, in combination with control of the spin degree of freedom
Debroux et al. 2021
, one could realize spin-photon entanglement
Vasconcelos et al. 2020
;
Togan et al. 2010
;
Gao et al. 2012
and entanglement of remote emitters
Cabrillo et al. 1999
;
Humphreys et al. 2018
;
Stockill et al. 2017
. Modest improvements in the indistinguishability of the photons would position multi-photon entangled states as the next achievement using the SnV center
Buterakos et al. 2017
;
Michaels et al. 2021
.

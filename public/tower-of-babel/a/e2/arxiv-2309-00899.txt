Abstract




Abstract.

We introduce a new class of weighted local approximate atoms including classical weighted local atoms. Then we further obtain the weighted local approximate atomic decompositions of weighted local Hardy spaces
h_{\omega}^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
with
0<p\leq 1
and weight
\omega\in A_{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. As an application, we prove the boundedness of inhomogeneous Calderón-Zygmund operators on
h_{\omega}^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
via weighted local approximate atoms and molecules.

Introduction

It is well known that the Hardy space
H^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
is a good substitute of
L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
when
0<p\leq 1

in pure Fourier analysis
[
4
]
. However, as Goldberg in
[
6
]
pointed out that
H^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

space is well suited only to the Fourier analysis, and is not stable under multiplications by the Schwartz test functions. The one reason is that
H^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
does not contain
\mathcal{S}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
,
the space of the Schwartz test functions. To circumvent those drawbacks, Goldberg in
[
6
]

introduced the local Hardy spaces
h^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
,
0<p<\infty
. The theory of local Hardy space plays an important role in various fields of
analysis and partial differential equations; see
[
12
]
,
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
. In particular, pseudo-differential operators are bounded on local Hardy spaces
h^{p}
for

0<p\leq 1
, but they are not bounded on Hardy spaces
H^{p}
for
0<p\leq 1
; see
[
6
]
.
Bui
[
8
]
further studied the weighted version of the local Hardy space considered by Goldberg
[
6
]
, where the weight is assumed
to satisfy the condition
(A_{\infty})
of Muckenhoupt.

The theory of Calderón-Zygmund singular integrals began in the 1950s when
Calderón and Zygmund studied convolution operators appeared in elliptic partial differential equations with constant coefficients. To deal with partial differential equations with variable coefficients, Calderón and Zygmund introduced the so called second generation of Calderón-Zygmund singular integrals. These operators are not convolution operators, but kernels of
these operators are given by the form
K(x,x-y)
. In 1965, Calderón considered the minimal regularity problem of the symbolic calculus, which opened the door of
the study for the third generation of Calderón-Zygmund singular integrals. Precisely, the
operator
T
is said to be in the
third generation of Calderón-Zygmund singular integrals
if
T
is
a continuous linear operator from
\mathcal{S}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
defined by

\langle Tf,g\rangle=\iint_{{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\times{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}}K(x,y)f(y)g(x)dxdy

for all
f
,
g\in\mathcal{S}^{(}{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
with disjoint supports, where
K(x,y)
, the kernel of
T
, is a locally
integrable function defined on
{{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\times{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}}
away from the diagonal
x=y
. Moreover,
K(x,y)

satisfies the following conditions: for
\delta\in(0,1]
and a constant
C>0
,

\displaystyle|K(x,y)|\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{n}}\quad\mathrm{for}\quad x\neq y

(1.1)

and

\displaystyle|K(x,y)-K(x,z)|+|K(y,x)-K(z,x)|\leq C\frac{|y-z|^{\delta}}{|x-z|^{n+\delta}}\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\mathrm{all}\,\,|x-z|\geq 2|y-z|.

(1.2)

It is easy to see that dilations of the kernel,
\delta^{n}K(\delta x,\delta y)
with
\delta>0
, satisfy the same
estimates above with the same bounds.
In 2020, Ding-Han-Zhu
[
3
]
introduced
inhomogeneous Calderón-Zygmund operators
T^{\mu}_{\delta}
, which the operator
T^{\mu}_{\delta}
satisfies similar
conditions as mentioned for the third generation of Calderón-Zygmund singular integrals, but

K(x,y)
also satisfies a restrictive size condition, namely, the condition (
1.1
) is replaced by

|K(x,y)|\leq C\min\left\{\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+\mu}}\right\},\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\mathrm{some}\,\,\mu>0\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,x\neq y.

We remark that the above condition for kernels of operators appeared when one studied pseudo-differential operators; see
[
7
,
10
]
. They obtained that the operator
T^{\mu}_{\delta}
is bounded on local Hardy spaces
h^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
with
\max(\frac{n}{n+\mu},\frac{n}{n+\delta})<p\leq 1
when it satisfies a
n(\frac{1}{p}-1)
-Lip type condition.
The operator
T^{\mu}_{\delta}
satisfies similar
conditions for the third generation of Calderón-Zygmund singular integrals, as the same time the invariant property of dilations for the third generation of Calderón-Zygmund singular integrals does not hold for
inhomogeneous Calderón-Zygmund singular integrals. Another difference between these
two kinds of singular integrals is that the third generation of Calderón-Zygmund singular
integrals have singularities at the diagonal
x=y
and the infinity, but inhomogeneous Calderón-Zygmund singular integrals have the singularity at the diagonal
x=y
only. In 2023, Tan-Chen
[
1
]
further obtained that the operator
T^{\mu}_{\delta}
is bounded on weighted local Hardy spaces
h^{p}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
with
0<p<\infty
when it satisfies some cancellation conditions. However, the cancellation conditions are strict for the operator
T^{\mu}_{\delta}
of non-convolution type. In 2022, Dafni-Lau-Picon-Vasconcelos
[
2
]
established the approximate atomic decompositions of local Hardy spaces, which including classical atomic decompositions of local Hardy spaces. They further obtained the boundedness of the operator
T^{\mu}_{\delta}
on local Hardy spaces
h^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
with
0<p\leq 1
when it satisfies an appropriate cancellation condition including cancellation condition as special case.

Inspired by
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
, we will introduce weighted local approximate atoms with the cancellation condition of classical atoms, i.e.,

\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}a(x)x^{\alpha}dx=0\quad\ \,\,\mathrm{with}\ |\alpha|\leq s,\,\,s\geq[n(\frac{1}{p}-1)]

replaced by ball-control conditions , i.e.,

\begin{cases}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a(x)(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}dx\right|\leq C\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\omega(B)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\text{if}\quad|\alpha|<\gamma_{p}:=n(\frac{1}{p}-1)\neq s\\
\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}a(x)(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}dx\right|\leq C\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\omega(B)}\right)^{\beta}\omega(B)^{\eta}&\text{if}\quad|\alpha|=\gamma_{p}=s\end{cases},

where weight
\omega\in A_{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
\beta:=\eta+\frac{1}{p}
. We will discuss the equivalence of weighted local Hardy spaces defined via approximate atoms with classical weighted local Hardy spaces.
The proof idea comes from that of unweighted case of Dafni-Lau-Picon-Vasconcelos
[
2
, Proposition 3.3]
. The main difficulty we overcame is that the weighted Lebesgue measure no longer satisfies the translation invariance property of Lebesgue measure, i.e., for any measurable set
E\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
|E|=|E+x|
but
\omega(E)=\omega(E+x)
can not be used for weight
\omega
; see the proof of Theorem
2.7
for more details.
Furthermore, we will introduce approximate molecules including classical molecules and obtain that they are bounded uniformly on
h^{p}_{w}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
norm with
0<p\leq 1
.
As an application, we will show the operator
T^{\mu}_{\delta}
is bounded on
h^{p}_{w}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
when it satisfies ball-control conditions, which is weaker than the classical cancellation condition.

This article is organized as follows. In Sect. 2, we introduce weighted local approximate atoms, i.e., the cancellation condition of classical atoms replaced by ball-control conditions (see Definition
2.4
). And we discuss the equivalence of weighted local Hardy spaces defined via approximate atoms with classical weighted local Hardy spaces (see Theorem

norm with

(see Theorem

T^{\mu}_{\delta}
on

and

.

It leaves an open problem: is it still holds true for the approximate atomic decompositions of

Throughout this article, a weight
\omega
is a non-negative locally integrable function on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, we shall denote
\omega(E):=\int_{E}\omega(x)dx

We first recall the weight class of Muckenhoupt.

Definition 2.1
.

For
1<q<\infty
, a locally integrable function
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is said to be an
A_{q}
weight
if

[\omega]_{A_{q}}:=\sup_{B}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega(x)^{\frac{1}{1-q}}\mathrm{~d}x\right)^{q-1}<\infty.

A locally integrable function
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is said to be an
A_{1}
weight
if

[\omega]_{A_{1}}:=\sup_{B}\frac{1}{|B|}\left(\int_{B}\omega(x)\mathrm{d}x\right){\underset{x\in B}{\operatorname{ess}\sup}[\omega(x)]^{-1}}<\infty.

Define
A_{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}):=\bigcup_{q\in[1,\infty)}A_{q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}).

For any given
\omega\in A_{q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, define the
critical index of
\omega

by

\displaystyle q_{\omega}:=\inf\{q\in[1,\infty):\omega\in A_{q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\}.

Given a weight function
\omega\in A_{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. For
1\leq q<\infty
, the
weighted Lebesgue space

L^{q}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
is the space of functions
f
such that

\|f\|_{L^{q}_{\omega}}:=\left(\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|f(x)|^{q}\omega(x)\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty.

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{2B}M(x)(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}\omega(x)^{\frac{1}{q}}\omega(x)^{-\frac{1}{q}}dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim(2r)^{|\alpha|}\left(\int_{2B}|M(x)|^{q}\omega(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{2B}\omega(x)^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}dx\right)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(2r)^{|\alpha|}\omega(B)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\frac{|B|}{\omega(B)^{\frac{1}{q}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(2r)^{n+|\alpha|}\omega(B)^{-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{n+|\alpha|}\omega(B)^{-\frac{1}{p}}

Next, let us estimate
\mathrm{K}_{2}.
Let
A_{k}:=\{2^{k}r\leq\left|x-x_{B}\right|<2^{k+1}r\}
. By (
\mathrm{M}_{2}
), Definition
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
(i), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{(2B)^{c}}M(x)(x-x_{B})^{\alpha}\frac{|x-x_{B}|^{\frac{\lambda}{q}}}{|x-x_{B}|^{\frac{\lambda}{q}}}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{(2B)^{c}}|M(x)|^{q}|x-x_{B}|^{\lambda}\omega(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{(2B)^{c}}|x-x_{B}|^{(\alpha-\frac{\lambda}{q})\frac{q}{q-1}}\omega(x)^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}dx\right)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{(2B)^{c}}|M(x)|^{q}|x-x_{B}|^{\lambda}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{A_{k}}|x-x_{B}|^{(\alpha-\frac{\lambda}{q})\frac{q}{q-1}}\omega(x)^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}dx\right)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{\frac{\lambda}{q}}\omega(B)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(2^{k}r)^{|\alpha|-\frac{\lambda}{q}}\frac{|B(x_{B},2^{k+1}r)|}{\omega(B(x_{B},2^{k+1}r))^{\frac{1}{q}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r^{n+|\alpha|}\omega(B)^{-\frac{1}{p}}2^{k[|\alpha|-\frac{\lambda}{q}+n(1-\frac{1}{q})]}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{n+|\alpha|}\omega(B)^{-\frac{1}{p}},

where the above series converges due to

n\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)+|\alpha|-\frac{\lambda}{q}<0

implied by
|\alpha|\leq\gamma_{p}\leq n(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})<\frac{\lambda}{q}
.

Combining the estimates of
\mathrm{K}_{1}
and
\mathrm{K}_{2}
, we obtain the proof of Lemma
3.4
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

The proof is inspired by the classical molecular decompositions for real Hardy spaces (
[
5
, Theorem 7.16]
). Let
M
be
a

(p,q,s,\lambda,\omega)
-approximate molecule associated to a ball
B=B\left(x_{B},r\right)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Let
B_{0}:=E_{0}:=B
,
B_{k}:=B\left(x_{B},2^{k}r\right)
,
E_{k}:=B_{k}\backslash B_{k-1}
and
M_{k}(x):=M(x)\chi_{E_{k}}(x)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
P_{k}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s}m_{\alpha}^{k}\phi_{\alpha}^{k}
to be the polynomial of degree at most
s
, restricted to the set
E_{k}
. For every
|\alpha|\leq s
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|E_{k}|}\int_{E_{k}}P_{k}(x)\left(x-x_{B}\right)^{\alpha}dx=m_{\alpha}^{k}\frac{1}{|E_{k}|}\int_{E_{k}}\phi_{\alpha}^{k}(x)\left(x-x_{B}\right)^{\alpha}dx

(3.1)

\displaystyle=m_{\alpha}^{k}:=\frac{1}{|E_{k}|}\int_{E_{k}}M(x)\left(x-x_{B}\right)^{\alpha}dx.

This is done by choosing the polynomials
\phi_{\alpha}^{k}
to have
\beta
-th moment equal to

|E_{k}|
when
\beta=\alpha
, and zero otherwise. And noting that

\left(2^{k}r\right)^{|\alpha|}\left|\phi_{\alpha}^{k}(x)\right|\lesssim 1

uniformly in
k
.
From this, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left|P_{k}(x)\right|\leqslant\sum_{|\alpha|\leqslant s}\left|m_{\alpha}^{k}\phi_{\alpha}^{k}(x)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{\left|E_{k}\right|}\int_{E_{k}}\left|M(x)\right|dx.

(3.2)

For
|\alpha|\leq s
, by (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\nu_{\alpha}:=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left|E_{j}\right|m_{\alpha}^{j}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}M(x)\left(x-x_{B}\right)^{\alpha}dx

(3.3)

and

\displaystyle N_{\alpha}^{k}:=\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\left|E_{j}\right|m_{\alpha}^{j}=\int_{E_{k}^{c}}M(x)\left(x-x_{B}\right)^{\alpha}dx,\quad k=0,1,2,\ldots.

(3.4)

From (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we can represent the sum of
P_{k}
as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(N_{\alpha}^{k-1}-N_{\alpha}^{k}\right)\left|E_{k}\right|^{-1}\phi_{\alpha}^{k}(x)+\left(\nu_{\alpha}-N_{\alpha}^{0}\right)\left|E_{0}\right|^{-1}\phi_{\alpha}^{0}(x)\right)

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}N_{\alpha}^{k}\left|E_{k+1}\right|^{-1}\phi_{\alpha}^{k+1}(x)-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}N_{\alpha}^{k}\left|E_{k}\right|^{-1}\phi_{\alpha}^{k}(x)+\nu_{\alpha}\left|E_{0}\right|^{-1}\phi_{\alpha}^{0}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s}\Phi_{\alpha}^{k}(x)+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s}\nu_{\alpha}\left|E_{0}\right|^{-1}\phi_{\alpha}^{0}(x),

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{\alpha}^{k}(x):=N_{\alpha}^{k}\left[\left|E_{k+1}\right|^{-1}\phi_{\alpha}^{k+1}(x)-\left|E_{k}\right|^{-1}\phi_{\alpha}^{k}(x)\right],\quad k=0,1,2,\ldots.

(3.6)

Note that (
3.5
) appears since we do not have the vanishing moment conditions on the molecule.
This allows us to decompose
M
as follows:

M=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(M_{k}-P_{k}\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s}\Phi_{\alpha}^{k}(x)+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s}\nu_{\alpha}\left|E_{0}\right|^{-1}\phi_{\alpha}^{0}(x)=:\mathrm{I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}}.

Let us first estimate
\mathrm{I_{1}}
. For
1<q<\infty
, by Lemma
2.2
(ii), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left|E_{k}\right|}\int_{E_{k}}\left|M_{k}(x)\right|dx\leq\frac{1}{\left|B_{k}\right|}\int_{B_{k}}\left|M_{k}(x)\right|dx\lesssim\omega(B(x_{B},2^{k}r))^{-{\frac{1}{q}}}\left\|M_{k}\right\|_{L_{\omega}^{q}}.

(3.7)

From (
3.2
) and (
3.7
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\left|P_{k}(x)\right|^{q}\lesssim\left(\omega(B(x_{B},2^{k}r))^{-1}\left\|M\right\|_{L_{\omega}^{q}}\right)^{q}={C}\omega(B(x_{B},2^{k}r))^{-1}\left\|M_{k}\right\|_{L_{\omega}^{q}}^{q}.

(3.8)

Then, by (
3.8
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{E_{k}}\left|P_{k}(x)\right|^{q}\omega(x)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{E_{k}}\omega\left(B(x_{B},2^{k}r)\right)^{-1}\|M_{k}\|_{L_{\omega}^{q}}^{q}\omega(x)dx.

\displaystyle\lesssim\omega\left(B(x_{B},2^{k}r)\right)^{-1}\|M_{k}\|_{L_{w}^{q}}^{q}\int_{B_{k}}\omega(x)dx.

\displaystyle=C\left\|M_{k}\right\|_{L_{\omega}^{q}}^{q},

which implies that

\displaystyle||P_{k}||_{L_{\omega}^{q}}\lesssim\|M_{k}\|_{L_{\omega}^{q}}

and

\displaystyle||M_{k}-P_{k}||_{L_{\omega}^{q}}\leq\|M_{k}\|_{L_{\omega}^{q}}+\|-P_{k}\|_{L_{\omega}^{q}}\lesssim\|M_{k}\|_{L_{\omega}^{q}}.

(3.9)

For
k=0,1,2,\cdots
, by
(\mathrm{M}_{1})
and
(\mathrm{M}_{2})
of Definition
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}|M(x)\chi_{E_{k}}(x)|^{q}\omega(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{E_{k}}|M(x)|^{q}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-k\frac{\lambda}{q}}\omega\left(B(x_{B},r)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}.

By (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we obtain

\left\|M_{k}-P_{k}\right\|_{L_{\omega}^{q}}\lesssim 2^{-k\frac{\lambda}{q}}\omega\left(B(x_{B},r)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}.

Therefore, for
k=0,1,2,\cdots
, it follows that

\displaystyle a_{k}:=C^{-1}2^{k\frac{\lambda}{q}}\left(M_{k}-P_{k}\right),

(3.11)

where
a_{k}
is a
\left(p,q,s,\omega\right)
-atom supported on
B_{k}
,
in other words,

M_{k}-P_{k}=t_{k}a_{k},\quad t_{k}:=C2^{-k\frac{\lambda}{q}}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|t_{k}\right|^{p}=C\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-kp\frac{\lambda}{q}}\lesssim 1.

(3.12)

For
\mathrm{I_{2}}
, let us first estimate
N_{\alpha}^{k}
of
\Phi_{\alpha}^{k}(x)
.
By (
3.4
), (
3.7
), (
3.10
) and Lemma
2.2
(i), we have

\displaystyle\left|N_{\alpha}^{k}\right|\leq\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\left|E_{j}\right|\frac{1}{|E_{j}|}\int_{E_{j}}\left|M(x)\left(x-x_{B}\right)^{\alpha}\right|dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\left(2^{j}r\right)^{n+|\alpha|}\frac{1}{|E_{j}|}\int_{E_{j}}|M(x)|dx

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}(2^{j}r)^{n+|\alpha|}\omega\left(B\left(x_{B},2^{j}r\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{q}}2^{-j\frac{\lambda}{q}}\omega\left(B\left(x_{B},r\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}-{\frac{1}{p}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}2^{j[n(1-\frac{1}{q})+|\alpha|-\frac{\lambda}{q}]}r^{n+|\alpha|}\omega\left(B\left(x_{B},r\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\qquad\lesssim 2^{[n(1-\frac{1}{q})+|\alpha|-\frac{\lambda}{q}]}r^{n+|\alpha|}\omega\left(B\left(x_{B},r\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}},

where the above series converges due to

n\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)+|\alpha|-\frac{\lambda}{q}<0

implied by
|\alpha|\leq\gamma_{p}\leq n(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})<\frac{\lambda}{q}
.

By this and

, we have

\displaystyle|N_{\alpha}^{k}\varphi_{\alpha}^{k}|\lesssim 2^{k[n(1-\frac{1}{q})+|\alpha|-\frac{\lambda}{q}]}r^{n+\alpha}\omega\left(B\left(x_{B},r\right)\right)^{-\frac{1}{p}}(2^{k}r)^{-n-|\alpha|}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\lesssim 2^{k(-\frac{n}{q}-\frac{\lambda}{q})}\omega(B\left(x_{B},r\right))^{-\frac{1}{p}}.

From this, we deduce that

\displaystyle\Phi_{\alpha}^{k}(x)=s_{k}b_{k},

(3.13)

where

is a
\left(p,\infty,s,\omega\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}s_{k}^{p}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}C_{\omega,n}2^{-kp(\frac{n}{q}+\frac{\lambda}{q})}\lesssim 1.

(3.14)

Now, for
\mathrm{I_{3}}
, let us show that

is a multiple of a
(p,q,s,\omega)
-approximate atom.

, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\nu_{\alpha}\right|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}M(x)\left(x-x_{B}\right)^{\alpha}dx\right|\leq r^{n+|\alpha|}{\omega\left(B\left(x_{B},r\right)\right)}^{-\frac{1}{p}}

, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s}\nu_{\alpha}|E_{0}|^{-1}\phi_{\alpha}^{0}(x)\right\|_{L_{\omega}^{q}}

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|t_{k}\right|^{p}\lesssim 1\text{ and }\ \ \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|s_{k}\right|^{p}\lesssim 1,

where
\{a_{k}\}
and
\{b_{k}\}
are
(p,q,s,\omega)
-atoms and

-atoms, respectively, and

(p,q,s,\omega)
-approximate atom.

Let

with

For any

3.15

\displaystyle\left|m_{\phi}(M)(x)\right|=\sup_{0<t<1}\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\phi_{t}(x-y)M(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<t<1}\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\phi_{t}(x-y)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}a_{k}(y)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}s_{k}b_{k}(y)+a(y)\right)dy\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\sup_{0<t<1}\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\phi_{t}(x-y)a(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0<t<1}\varliminf_{N\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{N}|t_{k}|\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\phi_{t}(x-y)a_{k}(y)dy\right|+\sup_{0<t<1}\varliminf_{N\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{N}|s_{k}|\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\sum_{k=0}^{N}s_{k}\phi_{t}(x-y)b_{k}(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\sup_{0<t<1}\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\phi_{t}(x-y)a(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\sup_{0<t<1}\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\phi_{t}(x-y)a(y)dy\right|

4
T_{\delta}^{\mu}
:
h_{\omega}^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\rightarrow h_{\omega}^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})

This section is devoted to show the boundedness of inhomogeneous Calderón-Zygmund operators
T_{\delta}^{\mu}
on weighted local Hardy spaces
h_{\omega}^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.

Definition 4.1
.

[
3
]

K(x,y)
is said to be an
inhomogeneous Calderón-Zygmund kernel
if it is a locally integrable function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
away from the diagonal
x=y
and satisfies the following conditions: if there exist
\mu>0
,
0<\delta\leq 1
and a constant
C>0
,

\displaystyle|K(x,y)|\leq C\min\left\{\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}},\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+\mu}}\right\},\quad x\neq y;

(4.1)

\displaystyle|K(x,y)-K(x,z)|+|K(y,x)-K(z,x)|\leq C\frac{|y-z|^{\delta}}{|x-z|^{n+\delta}},\quad|x-z|\geq 2|y-z|.

(4.2)

Furthermore,
T_{\delta}^{\mu}
is said to be an
inhomogeneous Calderón-Zygmund operator
if the following property is satisfied:

T_{\delta}^{\mu}
is associated to an
(\mu,\delta)-
inhomogeneous standard kernel given (formally) by

\langle T_{\delta}^{\mu}f,g\rangle:=\iint_{{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\times{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}}K(x,y)f(y)g(x)dydx,

for all
f,\,g\in\mathcal{S}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
with disjoint supports.

Lighted by
[
2
, Definition 5.1]
, we introduce the following Definition
4.2
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\omega\in A_{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. We denote by

such that
\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}g(x)x^{\alpha}dx=0
for all
|\alpha|\leq N
. For such an
\alpha
, define
T^{*}\left(x^{\alpha}\right)
in the distributional sense by

\displaystyle\left\langle T^{*}\left(x^{\alpha}\right),g\right\rangle=\left\langle x^{\alpha},T(g)\right\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}Tg(x)dx,\quad\forall g\in L_{c,N}^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};\omega\right).

(4.3)

The space
L_{c,s}^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n};\omega\right)
corresponds to multiples of local weighted
(p,q,s,\omega)
-atom in
h_{\omega}^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. That

is well-defined by (

in
[

where

.

,

, then
T_{\delta}^{\mu}
can be extended to a bounded operator from
h_{w}^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
to itself.

Proof of Theorem
4.3
.

We assume local weighted
(p,q,s,\omega)
-atom a is supported in
B(x_{B},r)
. We shall show

T_{\delta}^{\mu}a
is a
(p,q,s,\lambda,\omega)
-approximate molecule associated with
2B=B(x_{B},2r)
, where
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
\lambda>n(\frac{q}{p}-1)
. By the
L_{\omega}^{q}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
-boundedness of
T_{\delta}^{\mu}
(see
[
1
, Theorem 1.9]
) and
(A_{2})
condition of atom
a
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{2B}|T_{\delta}^{\mu}a(x)|^{q}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\|a\|_{L_{\omega}^{q}}\lesssim w(B(x_{B},r))^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}.

(4.5)

Form this, condition
(\mathrm{M}_{1})
holds true for
T_{\delta}^{\mu}a
.

Let us now verify
(\mathrm{M}_{2})
condition of
T_{\delta}^{\mu}a
by two cases:
r\geq 1
and
r<1
.

Case I : prove
(\mathrm{M}_{2})
under
r\geq 1

.
If
|x-x_{B}|\geq 2r
, by (
4.2
) and Definition
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle|T_{\delta}^{\mu}a(x)\leq\int_{B}|K(x,y)|a(y)dy\leq C|x-x_{B}|^{-n-\mu}\int_{B}|a(y)|dy

(4.6)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\lesssim|x-x_{B}|^{-n-\mu}\left(\int_{B}|a(y)|^{q}\omega(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B}\omega(y)^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}dy\right)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\lesssim|x-x_{B}|^{-n-\mu}\omega(B)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\frac{|B|}{\omega(B)^{\frac{1}{q}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\lesssim|x-x_{B}|^{-n-\mu}\frac{|B|}{\omega(B)^{\frac{1}{p}}}.

Since
\gamma_{p}<\mu
, then we have
n(\frac{q}{p}-1)<q(n+\mu)-n
. Therefore we can pick a
\lambda>0
such that

n\left(\frac{q}{p}-1\right)<\lambda<q(n+\mu)-n.

Let
A_{k}:=\{2^{k}r\leq\left|x-x_{B}\right|<2^{k+1}r\}
,
k=1,2,3,\cdots
. By condition
(\mathrm{M}_{2})
and Lemma
2.2
(i) with
\omega\in A_{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{|x-x_{B}|\geq 2r}|T_{\delta}^{\mu}a(x)|^{q}|x-x_{B}|^{\lambda}\omega(x)dx\lesssim\left(\frac{|B|}{\omega(B)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\right)^{q}\int_{(2B)^{c}}|x-x_{B}|^{\lambda-q(n+\mu)}\omega(x)dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{|B|}{\omega(B)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\right)^{q}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{A_{k}}|x-x_{B}|^{\lambda-q(n+\mu)}\omega(x)dx\lesssim\left(\frac{|B|}{\omega(B)^{\frac{1}{p}}}\right)^{q}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(2^{k}r)^{\lambda-q(n+\mu)}(2^{kn})\omega(B)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r^{\lambda-qn-q\mu+qn}2^{k(n+\lambda-qn-q\mu)}\omega(B)^{-\frac{q}{p}}\omega(B)\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r^{\lambda}2^{k(n+\lambda-qn-q\mu)}\omega(B)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{\lambda}\omega(B)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}.

(4.7)

Case II : prove
(\mathrm{M}_{2})
under
r<1

. Since
\gamma_{p}<\delta
, then we have
n\left(\frac{q}{p}-1\right)<q(n+\delta)-n
. Therefore we can pick a
\lambda>0
such that

n\left(\frac{q}{p}-1\right)<\lambda<q(n+\delta)-n.

By the vanishing moment condition of the atom, (
4.2
), Lemma
2.2
with
\omega\in A_{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, condition
(\mathrm{M}_{2})
and general Minkowski’s
inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{(2B)^{c}}|T_{\delta}^{\mu}a(x)|^{q}|x-x_{B}|^{\lambda}\omega(x)dx

(4.8)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{A_{k}}\left|\int_{B}|K(x,y)-K(x,x_{B})|a(y)dy\right|^{q}|x-x_{B}|^{\lambda}\omega(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(2^{k+1}r)^{\lambda}\left\{\int_{B}|a(y)|\left[\int_{A_{k}}\left|K(x,y)-K(x,x_{B})\right|^{q}\omega(x)dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}dy\right\}^{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(2^{k}r)^{\lambda}\left\{\int_{B}|a(y)|\left[\int_{A_{k}}\frac{|y-x_{B}|^{q\delta}}{|x-x_{B}|^{q(n+\delta)}}\omega(x)dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}dy\right\}^{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(2^{k}r)^{\lambda}\frac{r^{q\delta}}{(2^{k}r)^{(n+\delta)q}}2^{kn}\omega(B)r^{nq}\omega(B)^{-\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(2^{k}r)^{\lambda}2^{-k(n+\delta)q}\omega(B)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k(\lambda+n)}2^{-k(n+\delta)q}r^{\lambda}\omega(B)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k(\lambda+n-nq-q\delta)}r^{\lambda}\omega(B)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{\lambda}\omega(B)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}.

Therefore, condition
(\mathrm{M}_{2})
holds true for
r<1
. Combining the estimates of cases I and II, we finally obtain that
T_{\delta}^{\mu}a
satisfies condition
(\mathrm{M}_{2})
.

Finally, let us verify
(\mathrm{M}_{3})
when
r<1
. By (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and setting
f=(T_{\delta}^{\mu})^{*}\left[\left(\cdot-x_{B}\right)^{\alpha}\right]
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}T_{\delta}^{\mu}a(x)\left(x-x_{B}\right)^{\alpha}dx\right|=|\langle f,a\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{B}\left|f(x)-P_{B}^{s}(f)(x)\right|^{q^{\prime}}\omega(x)^{-{q^{\prime}/q}}dx\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}\|a\|_{L_{\omega}^{q}(B)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\omega(B)}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\text{ if }|\alpha|<\gamma_{p}\neq s\\
\left(\frac{\left|B\right|}{\omega(B)}\right)^{\beta}\omega(B)^{\eta}&\text{ if }|\alpha|=\gamma_{p}=s\end{cases},

(4.9)

where
\beta=\eta+\frac{1}{p}
.

Thus, condition
(\mathrm{M}_{3})
holds true for
r<1
. Therefore,
T_{\delta}^{\mu}a(x)
is a
(p,q,\eta,\lambda,\omega)
-approximate molecule and we finish the proof of Theorem
4.3
.

∎

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

Pick a function
f\in h^{p}_{\omega}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\cap L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, by
[
14
, Theorem 4.8 and Remark 4.9]
, we know that there exist local weighted
(p,2,s,\omega)
-atoms
\{a_{j}\}
and
\{\lambda_{j}\}\subset\mathbb{C}
such that
f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}a_{j}
holds true both in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and
L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
, and

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|^{p}\sim\|f\|_{h_{w}^{p}}^{p}.

(4.10)

Then, by the
L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
-boundedness of inhomogeneous Calderón-Zygmund operator
T_{\delta}^{\mu}
, we have

\displaystyle T_{\delta}^{\mu}f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}T_{\delta}^{\mu}a_{j}\quad\mathrm{holds}\,\,\mathrm{true}\,\,\mathrm{both}\,\,\mathrm{in}\,\,\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}).

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{S}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
with
\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\phi(x)dx\neq 0
. Then, for any
x\in{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}
, we further obtain

\displaystyle m_{\phi}(T_{\delta}^{\mu}f)(x)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|m_{\phi}T_{\delta}^{\mu}a_{j}(x).

From this,
0<p\leq 1
, Theorem
4.3
, Theorem
3.3
and (
4.10
), it follows that

\displaystyle\|T_{\delta}^{\mu}f\|_{h_{w}^{p}}^{p}=\|m_{\phi}(T_{\delta}^{\mu}f)\|_{L_{w}^{p}}^{p}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{j}|\|T_{\delta}^{\mu}a_{j}\|_{h_{w}^{p}}^{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{h_{w}^{p}}^{p},

which together with the fact that
{h_{w}^{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})\bigcap L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
is dense in
{h_{w}^{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
(see
[
14
, Corollary 4.10]
), we further obtain

\displaystyle\|T_{\delta}^{\mu}f\|_{h_{w}^{p}}^{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{h_{w}^{p}}^{p}\quad\mathrm{for}\,\,\mathrm{any}\,\,f\in{h_{w}^{p}}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}).

We finished the proof of Theorem
4.4
.
∎

Determining the Nonperturbative Collins-Soper Kernel From Lattice QCD Preprint: MIT–CTP 5049
Markus A. Ebert, Iain W. Stewart, Yong Zhao

Abstract
At small transverse momentum q T q_{T} , transverse-momentum dependent parton distribution functions (TMDPDFs) arise as genuinely nonperturbative objects that describe Drell-Yan like processes in hadron collisions as well as semi-inclusive deep-inelastic scattering. TMDPDFs naturally depend on the hadron momentum, and the associated evolution is determined by the Collins-Soper equation. For q T ∼ Λ QCD q_{T}\sim\Lambda_{\rm QCD} the corresponding evolution kernel (or anomalous dimension) is nonperturbative and must be determined as an independent ingredient in order to relate TMDPDFs at different scales. We propose a method to extract this kernel using lattice QCD and the Large-Momentum Effective Theory, where the physical TMD correlation involving light-like paths is approximated by a quasi-TMDPDF, defined using equal-time correlation functions with a large-momentum hadron state. The kernel is determined from a ratio of quasi-TMDPDFs extracted at different hadron momenta.



I
Introduction

In the past decades, advances in theory and experiment have made it possible to explore the structure of the proton beyond the simplest longitudinal momentum distributions. Key observables are transverse momentum distributions (TMDs), which measure the intrinsic transverse momentum
q_{T}
of partons in the proton, as well as describing the probability to produce particles at larger
q_{T}
in high energy collisions. These TMDs are probed directly by experiments on Drell-Yan, semi-inclusive deep inelastic scattering (SIDIS), and other processes.
Recently, progress has been made in determining TMDPDFs by using lattice QCD
Hägler et al. 2009
;
Musch et al. 2011
;
Musch et al. 2012
;
Engelhardt et al. 2016
;
Yoon et al. 2015
;
Yoon et al. 2017
to study equal-time correlators.
Such correlators are a key ingredient in the large-momentum effective theory (LaMET)
Ji 2013
;
Ji 2014
, where one computes a lightcone correlator using an equal-time correlator in a boosted proton state. For TMDPDFs the first theoretical studies in LaMET have been carried out in Refs.
Ji et al. 2015
;
Ji et al. 2018a
;
Ebert et al. 2019
.

The TMDPDF
f_{i}^{\mathrm{TMD}}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu,\zeta)
for a parton of flavor
i
depends on
x
, the fraction of the hadron momentum carried by the struck parton,
\vec{b}_{T}
, the Fourier-conjugate of the transverse momentum
\vec{q}_{T}
, and on a virtuality scale
\mu
. In addition, it depends on a scale
\zeta
, which is related to the momentum of the hadron or equivalently the hard scale of the scattering process.
Measuring nonperturbative TMDPDFs, whether from experiment or lattice, thus requires to specify the scales
(\mu_{0},\zeta_{0})
where the TMDPDF is extracted.
For instance, lattice calculations are restricted to
\mu_{0}^{2}\sim\zeta_{0}\sim\mathcal{O}(4\>\,\mathrm{GeV}^{2})
due to finite lattice spacing,
while for example the application to Drell-Yan production uses
\mu^{2}\sim\zeta\sim m_{Z}^{2}\approx(91~\,\mathrm{GeV})^{2}
.
The TMDPDFs thus need be evolved from
(\mu_{0},\zeta_{0})
to the phenomenologically relevant scales
(\mu,\zeta)
,
using

\displaystyle f_{i}^{\mathrm{TMD}}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu,\zeta)=f_{i}^{\mathrm{TMD}}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu_{0},\zeta_{0})

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\biggl[\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu^{\prime}}{\mu^{\prime}}\gamma_{\mu}^{i}(\mu^{\prime},\zeta_{0})\biggr]\exp\biggl[\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}(\mu,b_{T})\ln\frac{\zeta}{\zeta_{0}}\biggr]\,.

(1)

The first exponential in Eq. (
I
) is the
\mu
evolution and the second exponential is the Collins-Soper evolution in
\zeta

Collins and Soper 1981
;
Collins and Soper 1982
;
Collins et al. 1985a
in the formulation of Ref.
Collins 2011
, with
\gamma_{\mu}^{i}
and
\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}
being the associated anomalous dimensions. Here
\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}
is the Collins-Soper kernel, often denoted by
\tilde{K}
.

The
\mu
evolution in Eq. (
I
) is perturbative as long as both
\mu_{0},\mu\gg\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
, analogous to the perturbative DGLAP evolution for collinear PDFs.
In contrast, the Collins-Soper kernel involves the
b_{T}
-dependent anomalous dimension
\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}(\mu,b_{T})
,
which becomes nonperturbative in the region
b_{T}^{-1}\sim q_{T}\sim\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
, even if
\mu\gg\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
. Relating the nonperturbative TMDPDF extracted at some reference scales
(\mu_{0},\zeta_{0})
to the phenomenologically relevant scales
(\mu,\zeta)
thus crucially relies on the nonperturbative knowledge of the Collins-Soper kernel.

Due to the simple form of Eq. (
I
), the Collins-Soper evolution can be factored out by taking the ratio of two TMDPDFs extracted at different values
\zeta_{1}\neq\zeta_{2}
,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}(\mu,b_{T})=\frac{2}{\ln(\zeta_{1}/\zeta_{2})}\,\ln\frac{f^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{i}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu,\zeta_{1})}{f^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{i}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu,\zeta_{2})}\,.

(2)

One option is to extract
\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}(\mu,b_{T})
experimentally. For example, one can use Drell-Yan production at small
q_{T}\ll Q
, where
Q
is the invariant mass of the Drell-Yan pair, and use different values of
Q
to obtain
f^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{i=q}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu,\zeta)
at different
\zeta
values. This can be done using results from global fits, see e.g. Refs.
Bacchetta et al. 2017
;
Scimemi and Vladimirov 2018a
. Experimentally, the TMDPDF is extracted as a function of
\vec{q}_{T}
, which makes it challenging to use Eq. (
2
) since it requires the Fourier transformation into
\vec{b}_{T}
space.
Interestingly, Eq. (
2
) is independent of the momentum fraction
x
and choice of
\zeta_{1,2}
,
which is useful to assess associated systematics and to validate the applicability of TMD factorization.

In this paper, we propose a first-principle method of determining the nonperturbative
\gamma_{\zeta}^{q}
using lattice QCD.
A potential benefit is that one has, in principle, more control over the systematics in the calculation.
The TMDPDFs in Eq. (
2
) are not directly computable on the lattice because they involve time dependent operators with Wilson lines on (or close to) the light cone. We therefore consider the LaMET approach for calculating the ratio in Eq. (
2
) from lattice QCD, which will involve additional perturbative matching corrections. Our proposed formula for
\gamma_{\zeta}^{q}(\mu,b_{T})
is analogous to Eq. (
2
) and is given in Sec.
IV
.

Below in Sec.
II
, we briefly review the definition of TMDPDFs in the context of TMD factorization for proton-proton collisions.
In Sec.
III
, we present the construction of quasi-TMDPDFs using LaMET that are computable on lattice, and briefly address issues arising from the presence of both collinear and soft matrix elements.
The strategy to extract
\gamma_{\zeta}^{q}
from lattice is presented in Sec.
IV
.
We conclude in Sec.
V
.

II
TMD Factorization

TMD factorization was originally derived by Collins, Soper and Sterman (CSS)
in Refs.
Collins and Soper 1981
;
Collins and Soper 1982
;
Collins et al. 1985a

The cancellation of potentially factorization-violating Glauber contributions has been shown in
Refs.
Collins et al. 1985b
;
Collins et al. 1988
;
Collins et al. 1989
;
Collins 2011
;
Diehl et al. 2016
.
The factorization has also been considered in the framework of Soft-Collinear Effective Theory (SCET)

Bauer et al. 2000
;
Bauer et al. 2001
;
Bauer and Stewart 2001
;
Bauer et al. 2002

by various authors
Becher and Neubert 2011
;
Becher et al. 2012
;
Becher et al. 2013
;
Echevarria et al. 2012
;
Echevarría et al. 2013
;
Echevarria et al. 2014
;
Chiu et al. 2012a
,
see e.g. Ref.
Collins and Rogers 2017
for a detailed discussion of the different approaches.

We consider the production of a color-singlet final state
F

with invariant mass
Q
, rapidity
Y
and small transverse momentum
q_{T}=|\vec{q}_{T}|\ll Q

in the scattering of two energetic protons moving close to the

n^{\mu}=(1,0,0,1)
and
{\bar{n}}^{\mu}=(1,0,0,-1)
directions with a center of mass energy
E_{\rm cm}
.
In the limit
q_{T}\ll Q
the cross section can be factorized as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\sigma}{\mathrm{d}Q\mathrm{d}Y\mathrm{d}^{2}\vec{q}_{T}}=\sum_{i,j}H_{ij}(Q,\mu)\int\!\!\mathrm{d}^{2}\vec{b}_{T}\,e^{\mathrm{i}\vec{b}_{T}\cdot\vec{q}_{T}}

\displaystyle\times f^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{i}(x_{a},\vec{b}_{T},\mu,\zeta_{a})\,f^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{j}(x_{b},\vec{b}_{T},\mu,\zeta_{b})\,,

(3)

which holds up to power corrections of relative order
q_{T}^{2}/Q^{2}
and
\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{2}/Q^{2}
, but remains valid in the nonperturbative regime
q_{T}\sim\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
.
Here,
H_{ij}
is the hard function describing virtual corrections to the underlying hard process
ij\to F
, where
i,j
are the parton flavors
(for gluon-induced process,
ij=gg
, the hard function and the TMDPDFs carry helicity indices, which are suppressed here),

x_{a,b}=Qe^{\pm Y}/E_{\rm cm}
are the longitudinal momentum fractions carried by the struck partons,
and
f^{\mathrm{TMD}}
are the TMDPDFs in Fourier space.
(Our
H
and
f^{\mathrm{TMD}}
s agree with the definitions used in Refs.
Collins 2011
;
Catani et al. 2001
;
Becher and Neubert 2011
;
Becher and Bell 2012
;
Chiu et al. 2012a
;
Echevarria et al. 2012
;
Gehrmann et al. 2012
;
Li et al. 2016
;
Echevarria et al. 2016
, but differ from those of Refs.
Collins and Soper 1981
;
Collins and Soper 1982
;
Collins et al. 1985a
;
Ji et al. 2005
, see Refs.
Prokudin et al. 2015
;
Collins and Rogers 2017
for relations.) Note that these physical TMDs involve staple Wilson line paths, and hence differ from the straight line paths considered in Refs.
Hägler et al. 2009
;
Musch et al. 2011
;
Radyushkin 2017
.
A similar result is obtained for SIDIS,
ep\to h+X
, where the transverse momentum of the hadron
h
is measured by a TMD fragmentation function rather than a TMDPDF,
which obeys the same Collins-Soper equation
Collins 2011
.

An important feature of TMDs is that bare matrix elements not only suffer from UV divergences regulated by
\epsilon
(for example in dimensional regularization with
d=4-2\epsilon
), but also from
rapidity divergences
(also known as lightcone divergences) which require another regulator
Collins and Soper 1981
;
Collins and Tkachov 1992
;
Collins 2008
;
Becher and Neubert 2011
;
Echevarria et al. 2012
;
Chiu et al. 2012b
;
Chiu et al. 2012a
that we will generically denote by
\tau
.
Many such regulators have been suggested in the literature
Collins and Soper 1981
;
Collins 2011
;
Ji et al. 2005
;
Beneke and Feldmann 2004
;
Chiu et al. 2008
;
Becher and Bell 2012
;
Chiu et al. 2012b
;
Chiu et al. 2012a
;
Chiu et al. 2009
;
Echevarria et al. 2012
;
Li et al. 2016
. As a consequence, TMDPDFs depend on both the renormalization scale
\mu
and the parameter
\zeta
. In Eq. (
II
)
\mu
formally cancels between the
H
and
f^{\mathrm{TMD}}
functions, but in practice is chosen as
\mu\sim Q
to avoid large logarithms
\ln(Q/\mu)
in the hard function.
The values for
\zeta_{a}
and
\zeta_{b}
are not fixed individually, but their product is fixed to
\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}=Q^{4}
.
This forces one to evaluate the TMDPDFs at
\mu^{2}\sim\zeta\sim Q^{2}
, and requires the use of Eq. (
I
) to evolve them from some other scales like
(\mu_{0}^{2},\zeta_{0})\sim\mathcal{O}(4\>\,\mathrm{GeV}^{2})
where they are nonperturbatively determined (or parameterized).

A definition of the quark TMDPDF consistent with Refs.
Collins 2011
;
Chiu et al. 2012a
;
Echevarria et al. 2012
can be given by

\displaystyle f^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{q}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\epsilon\to 0\\
\tau\to 0\end{subarray}}\,Z_{\rm uv}(\mu,\zeta,\epsilon)\,B_{q}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\epsilon,\tau,\zeta)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\Delta_{S}^{q}(b_{T},\epsilon,\tau)\,.

(4)

Here
Z_{\rm uv}
is the ultraviolet (UV) renormalization factor, and we refer to
B_{q}
and
\Delta^{q}_{S}
as the bare beam function (where we follow the naming scheme of Ref.
Stewart et al. 2010
) and soft factor, respectively, to distinguish them from the TMDPDF
f_{q}^{\mathrm{TMD}}
. Refs.
Collins 2011
;
Chiu et al. 2012a
;
Echevarria et al. 2012
use different definitions for
\tau
,
B_{q}
, and
\Delta_{S}^{q}
but all choices yield the same
f_{q}^{\mathrm{TMD}}
. As
\tau\to 0
only the combination
1/\tau-\ln\sqrt{\zeta}
shows up in the bare function
B_{q}
. Importantly,
1/\tau
divergences cancel out in Eq. (
II
) yielding a well-defined TMDPDF. A remnant of this cancellation is the appearance of
\zeta
in
f_{q}^{\mathrm{TMD}}
. In Eq. (
II
) we have

\displaystyle\zeta_{a}=x_{a}^{2}(P_{a}^{-})^{2}e^{-2y_{n}}\,,\quad\zeta_{b}=x_{b}^{2}(P_{b}^{+})^{2}e^{2y_{n}}\,,

(5)

where
P_{a,b}
are the hadron momenta and
y_{n}
is an arbitrary parameter controlling the split of soft radiation into the TMDPDFs (where the precise specification of
y_{n}
differs in Refs.
Collins 2011
;
Chiu et al. 2012a
;
Echevarria et al. 2012
).
Their product is always fixed to

\zeta_{a}\zeta_{b}=Q^{4}=(x_{a}x_{b}E_{\rm cm}^{2})^{2}\,.

(6)

Eqs. (
5

6

A well-known example of Eq. (
II

Collins 2011

\displaystyle\Delta_{S}^{q}(b_{T},\epsilon,\tau)

\displaystyle=\lim_{y_{A}\to\infty}\sqrt{\frac{S^{y_{A},y_{n}}}{S^{y_{A},y_{B}}S^{y_{n},y_{B}}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{S^{q}_{\rm C}\bigl(b_{T},\epsilon,2y_{n}-2y_{B}\bigr)}}\,,

(7)

(see Ref.
Buffing et al. 2018

Here,
S^{y_{a},y_{b}}
denotes a soft matrix element with Wilson lines parameterized by the rapidities
y_{a}
and
y_{b}
,
and
y_{A}
and
y_{B}
are the rapidities of the Wilson lines entering
B_{q}
for the
n
-collinear proton and
{\bar{n}}
-collinear proton, respectively.
The regulator is given by
\tau=1/(y_{B}-y_{n})
with
y_{B}\to-\infty

For Collins’ beam function we have

\displaystyle B_{q}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\epsilon,\tau,\zeta)

\displaystyle=B_{q}^{\rm C}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\epsilon,y_{P}-y_{B})\,,

(8)

which only depends on the rapidity difference
y_{P}-y_{B}
since
1/\tau-\ln\sqrt{\zeta}=y_{B}-y_{P}-\ln(x\,m_{P})
, where
m_{P}
and
y_{P}
are the proton mass and rapidity, and
P^{-}=m_{P}\,e^{y_{P}}

In the EIS scheme
Echevarria et al. 2012
;
Echevarría et al. 2013
one regulates eikonal propagators by basically shifting

and two zero-bin subtraction

which avoid double counting between the soft function

. With the

\displaystyle\Delta_{S}^{q}(b_{T},\epsilon,\tau)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{S^{q}_{\rm EIS}\bigl(b_{T},\epsilon,\delta^{-}e^{-y_{n}}\bigr)}}\,.

(9)

Here the regulator is
1/\tau=\ln(\delta^{-}e^{-y_{n}})
. In this scheme

\displaystyle B_{q}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\epsilon,\tau,\zeta)

\displaystyle=B_{q}^{\rm EIS}\bigl(x,\vec{b}_{T},\epsilon,\delta^{-}/(xP^{-})\bigr)\,,

(10)

where
B_{q}^{\rm EIS}\equiv J_{n}
in the notation of Ref.

Finally, in the
\eta
-regulator and scheme of CJNR in Ref.
Chiu et al. 2012a
the Wilson lines in the proton and vacuum matrix elements are regulated with factors of
|2k^{z}|^{-\eta}
,
where

\tau=\eta

S/(S_{0}S_{0})=S=\sqrt{S}\sqrt{S}

\displaystyle\Delta_{S}^{q}(b_{T},\epsilon,\tau)=\sqrt{S^{q}_{\rm CJNR}(b_{T},\epsilon,\eta)}\,.

(11)

In this approach the regulator is chosen to act symmetrically on the two proton matrix elements, so
y_{n}=0
,
\zeta=(xP^{-})^{2}
, and the bare beam function is

\displaystyle B_{q}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\epsilon,\tau,\zeta)

\displaystyle=B_{q}^{\rm CJNR}\bigl(x,\vec{b}_{T},\epsilon,\eta,(xP^{-})^{2}\bigr)\,.

(12)

\displaystyle B_{q}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\epsilon,\tau,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}b^{+}}{4\pi}e^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{1}{2}b^{+}(xP^{-})}\Bigl<h(P)\Bigr|\Bigl[\bar{q}(b^{\mu})W(b^{\mu})\frac{\gamma^{-}}{2}W_{T}\bigl(-\infty{\bar{n}};\vec{b}_{T},\vec{0}_{T}\bigr)W^{\dagger}(0)q(0)\Bigr]_{\tau}\Bigl|h(P)\Bigr>,

The dependence of the TMDPDF on the scales
\mu
and
\zeta
is governed by the renormalization group equations

\displaystyle\mu\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}

\displaystyle f^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{i}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu,\zeta)=\gamma_{\mu}^{i}(\mu,\zeta)f^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{i}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu,\zeta)\,,

\displaystyle\zeta\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\zeta}

\displaystyle f^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{i}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu,\zeta)=\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}(\mu,b_{T})f^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{i}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu,\zeta)\,,

\displaystyle\mu\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}

\displaystyle\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}(\mu,b_{T})=2\zeta\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\zeta}\gamma_{\mu}^{i}(\mu,\zeta)=-2\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{i}[\alpha_{s}(\mu)]\,,

(16)

where the second equation is the Collins-Soper equation
Collins and Soper 1982
;
Collins and Soper 1981
.
It can also be written as a convolution in momentum space
Collins and Soper 1982
, where its solution is more complicated
Ebert and Tackmann 2017
.
The combined solution to Eq. (
II
) yields the evolution in Eq. (
I
), where we have chosen a specific path in the
(\mu,\zeta)
plane, but one is free to choose any path connecting
(\mu_{0},\zeta_{0})\to(\mu,\zeta)
since the last equation in Eq. (
II
) ensures path independence (see also Ref.
Scimemi and Vladimirov 2018b
).

The subscripts on the anomalous dimensions
\gamma_{\mu}^{i}
and
\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}
label the scale evolution they govern.
Their all-order forms are given by

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mu}^{i}(\mu,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{i}[\alpha_{s}(\mu)]\ln\frac{\mu^{2}}{\zeta}+\gamma_{\mu}^{i}[\alpha_{s}(\mu)]\,,

(17)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}(\mu,b_{T})

\displaystyle=-2\int_{1/b_{T}}^{\mu}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu^{\prime}}{\mu^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{i}[\alpha_{s}(\mu^{\prime})]+\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}[\alpha_{s}(1/b_{T})]\,.

They both have a piece governed by the cusp anomalous dimension
\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{i}[\alpha_{s}]
, and a noncusp piece
\gamma^{i}[\alpha_{s}]
.
Both anomalous dimension differ for quarks,
i=q
, and gluons,
i=g
, but are independent of the choice of hadron state and the light quark flavor in the operator in Eq. (
13
) (for
b
-quarks see Ref.
Pietrulewicz et al. 2017
).
For
\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}
this follows because

\displaystyle\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}

\displaystyle=2\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln f_{i}^{\mathrm{TMD}}}{\mathrm{d}\ln\zeta}=2\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln B_{i}}{\mathrm{d}\ln\zeta}=-\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln B_{i}}{\mathrm{d}(1/\tau)}=\frac{\mathrm{d}\ln\Delta_{S}^{i}}{\mathrm{d}(1/\tau)}\,,

(18)

and
\Delta_{S}^{i}
does not depend on the hadron state.

Eq. (
17
) clearly shows that if
b_{T}\sim\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{-1}
then
\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}(\mu,b_{T})
has an intrinsically nonperturbative component.
Once
\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}(\mu,b_{T})
is determined at a scale
\mu_{0}\gg\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
,
it can be perturbatively determined at any scale
\mu\gg\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
via

\displaystyle\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}(\mu,b_{T})=\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}(\mu_{0},b_{T})-2\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu^{\prime}}{\mu^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{i}[\alpha_{s}(\mu^{\prime})]\,.

(19)

The result for
\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}(\mu_{0},b_{T})
is known to 3-loop order for perturbative
b_{T}

Li et al. 2016
;
Li and Zhu 2017
;
Vladimirov 2017
.
The focus of this paper is to determine
\gamma_{\zeta}^{i}(\mu_{0},b_{T})
nonperturbatively, which can be used for the evolution even when
b_{T}\sim\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{-1}
.

III
TMDPDFs on the Lattice

While lattice QCD provides a practical tool for first-principle calculations of nonperturbative quantities, it has long been challenging to compute lightcone correlators on the Euclidean lattice due to their real-time dependence.
LaMET has been proposed to overcome this hurdle by relating the light-cone correlator to an equal-time correlator in a highly boosted hadron state
Ji 2013
;
Ji 2014
.
The latter can be calculated on lattice, and can then be matched onto the corresponding lightcone matrix elements through a systematic expansion in the hadron momentum
P^{z}
,
as proven in Refs.
Ma and Qiu 2018a
;
Ma and Qiu 2018b
;
Izubuchi et al. 2018
.

Using the LaMET approach, it has been suggested to calculate TMDPDFs in a similar fashion
Ji et al. 2015
;
Ji et al. 2018a
;
Ebert et al. 2019
.
An earlier and related approach used in lattice calculations is to exploit Lorentz invariance for the spacelike correlator
Musch et al. 2011
;
Musch et al. 2012
.
To begin with, we define a bare
quasi beam function
in position space as

\displaystyle\tilde{B}_{q}(b^{z},\vec{b}_{T},a,L,P^{z})=\Bigl<h(P)\Big|

\displaystyle\bar{q}(b^{\mu})W_{\hat{z}}(b^{\mu};L-b^{z})\frac{\Gamma}{2}W_{T}(L\hat{z};\vec{b}_{T},\vec{0}_{T})W^{\dagger}_{\hat{z}}(0;L)q(0)\Big|h(P)\Bigr>\,,

(20)

where
b^{\mu}=(0,\vec{b}_{T},b^{z})
and
a
denotes the lattice spacing which acts as an UV regulator, but for simplicity we stick to continuum notation for the fields.
In Eq. (
20
), one has either
\Gamma=\gamma^{0}
or
\Gamma=\gamma^{z}
, as both can be boosted onto
\gamma^{-}
. Just like for the quasi-PDF the choice of
\Gamma=\gamma^{0}
might be preferred on the lattice to avoid operator mixing
Constantinou and Panagopoulos 2017
;
Green et al. 2018
;
Chen et al. 2017
.

Compared to Eq. (
13
), in Eq. (
20
) the lightcone Wilson lines are replaced by purely spatial Wilson lines
of length
L
because of the finite lattice size,

\displaystyle W_{\hat{z}}(x^{\mu};L)

\displaystyle=P\exp\left[\mathrm{i}g\int_{L}^{0}\mathrm{d}s\,\mathcal{A}^{z}(x^{\mu}+s\hat{z})\right]\,.

(21)

This also regulates the analog of rapidity divergences in
\tilde{B}_{q}
,
as has been shown explicitly in Refs.
Ji et al. 2018a
;
Ebert et al. 2019
, and thus
L
, in part, takes the role of the rapidity regulator
\tau
in Eq. (
13
).
The inclusion of transverse gauge links ensures gauge invariance
Ji and Yuan 2002
;
Belitsky et al. 2003
;
Idilbi and Scimemi 2011
;
Garcia-Echevarria et al. 2011
.
The resulting Wilson line structure of Eq. (
20
) is illustrated in Fig.
2
a. The same correlator in Eq. (
20
) has been used in the lattice calculation of ratios of TMDPDFs with
b^{z}=0
in Refs.
Musch et al. 2012
;
Engelhardt et al. 2016
;
Yoon et al. 2015
;
Yoon et al. 2017
.

Under a Lorentz boost along the
z
direction with velocity
v\,{\to}\,1
and boost parameter
\gamma=1/\sqrt{1-v^{2}}
, the spatial separation behaves as
\hat{z}=(0,0,0,1)\to\gamma(-v,0,0,1)\approx-\gamma{\bar{n}}
.
This boost behavior is illustrated in Fig.
2
b.
It is easy to see that by applying this relation to Eq. (
20
), one recovers Eq. (
13
) in the limit
v\to 1
.
This suggests that a matching between Eqs. (
13
) and (
20
) could be possible, similar to the collinear PDF,
up to possible issues from regulating rapidity divergences, as such regulators necessarily break boost invariance, see Ref.
Ebert et al. 2019
.

For the soft matrix element defined in Eq. (
14
), a simple quasi construction is not possible,
since the Wilson lines involve both lightcone directions
n
and
{\bar{n}}
, which would require opposite boosts to be recovered from spatial Wilson lines along the
\pm\hat{z}
directions.
A detailed study of this issue is given in Ref.
Ebert et al. 2019
.
Due to this issue, we will simply introduce an intrinsically nonperturbative quantity
\tilde{\Delta}_{S}^{q}(b_{T},a,L)
to describe the missing infrared (IR) physics and dependence on
b_{T}
.

We define the quasi-TMDPDF in the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme analogous to Eq. (
II
) as

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{q}^{\mathrm{TMD}}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu,P^{z})=\!\int\!\frac{\mathrm{d}b^{z}}{2\pi}e^{\mathrm{i}b^{z}(xP^{z})}\tilde{f}_{q}^{\mathrm{TMD}}(b^{z},\vec{b}_{T},\mu,P^{z})\,,

(22)

where the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
position-space quasi-TMDPDF is

\displaystyle\tilde{f}^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{q}(b^{z},\vec{b}_{T},\mu,P^{z})

\displaystyle=\tilde{Z}^{\prime}(b^{z},\mu,\tilde{\mu})\tilde{Z}_{\rm uv}(b^{z},\tilde{\mu},a)

(23)

\displaystyle\times\tilde{B}_{q}(b^{z},\vec{b}_{T},a,L,P^{z})\tilde{\Delta}_{S}^{q}(b_{T},a,L)\,.

Here the quasi soft factor
\tilde{\Delta}_{S}^{q}
also serves as a counterterm to cancel
L/b_{T}
divergences in
\tilde{B}_{q}
.
The
\tilde{Z}_{\rm uv}
carries out UV renormalization, which could be done nonperturbatively on the lattice, and
\tilde{\mu}
denotes any renormalization scales this introduces. The conversion factor
\tilde{Z}^{\prime}
converts the result into the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme at the scale
\mu
, which necessarily is perturbative. On the lattice there will be linear power divergences
\sim 1/a
coming from the spacelike Wilson-line self energies. The quasi soft factor
\tilde{\Delta}_{S}^{q}
cancels the
L/a
Wilson line self energy divergences appearing in
\tilde{B}_{q}
, which leaves only
b^{z}/a
divergences to be canceled by
\tilde{Z}_{\rm uv}
.
One can consider removing these
1/a
divergences with a counterterm determined from the static quark-antiquark potential as in Ref.
Musch et al. 2011
, or with the RI/MOM scheme like in the quasi-PDF
Constantinou and Panagopoulos 2017
;
Stewart and Zhao 2018
, or with the gradient flow method
Monahan and Orginos 2017
.

In Eq. (
23
) we suppress the leftover
L
dependence which vanishes in the physical limit
L\to\infty
. It would be interesting to construct a direct proof of these renormalization properties of the quasi-TMDPDF, along the lines of those for the quasi-PDF in Ref.
Ji et al. 2018b
;
Ishikawa et al. 2017
. Equation (
23
) has been explicitly verified in perturbation theory at one-loop order, where definitions of
\tilde{\Delta}_{S}^{q}
that cancel all divergences in
L
have also been given
Ji et al. 2018a
;
Ebert et al. 2019
.

The quasi-TMDPDF defined in Eq. (
22
) is not a boost invariant quantity and thus explicitly depends on the hadron momentum
P^{z}
, which also plays the role of the variable
\zeta
in the TMDPDF. We will exploit this
P^{z}
dependence for our method to extract
\gamma_{\zeta}^{q}
. Importantly,
\tilde{\Delta}_{S}^{q}
does not depend on
x
or
P^{z}
.
It also does not depend on the quark flavor, but differs from
\tilde{\Delta}_{S}^{g}
for gluons.
Thus,
\tilde{\Delta}_{S}^{q}
drops out of ratios that are flavor blind, which will be a crucial ingredient to our proposed method, as one does not need to precisely define or calculate
\tilde{\Delta}_{S}^{q}
on the lattice. Note that we convert
\tilde{f}_{q}^{\mathrm{TMD}}
to the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme with
\tilde{Z}^{\prime}
to simplify carrying out the matching onto the TMDPDF, though matching results for other schemes could be considered.

III.1
Relating quasi-TMDPDF and TMDPDF

For the collinear PDF, LaMET gives a perturbative matching between the quasi-PDF and PDF, and the same may be true between the quasi beam function and beam function. However, for the full TMDPDF this matching is potentially spoiled by the presence of the soft factors
\Delta_{S}^{q}
and
\tilde{\Delta}_{S}^{q}
that cannot be related simply through a Lorentz boost. For our purposes, we define
g^{S}_{q}(b_{T},\mu)
as the mismatch of the lightlike and quasi soft factors, which we allow to be nonperturbative, and we will exploit the fact that due to its soft origin it is independent of
x
,
P^{z}
, and quark flavor. For a flavor nonsinglet channel such as
u{-}d
the relation between the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
quasi-TMDPDF and TMDPDF is thus expected to take the form
Ebert et al. 2019

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{\text{ns}}^{\mathrm{TMD}}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu,P^{z})=C^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{\text{ns}}\bigl(\mu,xP^{z}\bigr)\>g^{S}_{q}(b_{T},\mu)

\displaystyle\times\exp\biggl[\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{\zeta}^{q}(\mu,b_{T})\ln\frac{(2xP^{z})^{2}}{\zeta}\biggr]f^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{\text{ns}}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu,\zeta)\,.

(24)

We suppress explicit corrections to Eq. (
III.1
) in
b_{T}/L
,
1/(b_{T}P^{z})
,
1/(P^{z}L)
and
\Lambda_{\rm QCD}/P^{z}
arising from the finite hadron momentum
P^{z}
and finite lattice size. Like for the quasi-PDF
Chen et al. 2016
there are also hadron mass corrections,
M_{h}/P^{z}
, which can likely be accounted for exactly.
Eq. (
III.1
) has been explicitly verified at one loop in Ref.
Ebert et al. 2019
.
It involves a perturbative short distance coefficient
C_{\text{ns}}^{\mathrm{TMD}}
, which is independent of
b_{T}
.
It is multiplicative in
x
space, which is known to hold at least to one-loop order
Ji et al. 2018a
;
Ebert et al. 2019
,
in contrast to the quasi-PDF, whose matching onto the PDF involves a convolution in
x

Xiong et al. 2014
.

The exponential in Eq. (
III.1
) contains the nonperturbative Collins-Soper kernel
\gamma_{\zeta}^{q}(\mu,b_{T})
that we are after.
It guarantees that
\tilde{f}_{\text{ns}}^{\mathrm{TMD}}
is independent of
\zeta
by balancing the Collins-Soper evolution of
f^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{\text{ns}}
.
This exponential is
\vec{b}_{T}
dependent and thus can not be included in the short-distance
C_{\text{ns}}^{\mathrm{TMD}}
.
The
\zeta
dependence in this exponential is balanced by
\tilde{\zeta}\equiv(2xP^{z})^{2}
, which is the Collins-Soper scale of Eq. (
5
) away from the lightcone, corresponding with the
z
-momentum of the struck quark. For our use of Eq. (
III.1
) we are considering
\tilde{\zeta}
and
\zeta
to be independent variables. This can be equivalently thought of as having different values of
P^{z}
in the quasi-TMDPDF
\tilde{f}_{\text{ns}}^{\mathrm{TMD}}
and TMDPDF
f_{\text{ns}}^{\mathrm{TMD}}
, where the nonperturbative
\gamma_{\zeta}^{q}(\mu,b_{T})
is then needed to connect these two different values of
P^{z}
. Note that for our purposes any mismatch in the multiplicative constant in
\zeta
and
\tilde{\zeta}
, the analogs of
e^{-2y_{n}}
in Eq. (
5
), can be compensated by a change to
g_{q}^{S}
, so we can take these
e^{-2y_{n}}
constants to be
1
.

If a definition of
\tilde{\Delta}_{S}^{q}
can be found which is calculable on the lattice and matches the IR physics of
\Delta_{S}^{q}
, then
g^{S}_{q}
would become independent of
b_{T}
and calculable in perturbation theory. In this case Eq. (
III.1
) would become a true matching relation between the quasi-TMDPDF and TMDPDF. For our analysis here we will not need to assume such an operator definition of
\tilde{\Delta}_{S}^{q}
exists.

Note that our result in Eq. (
III.1
) differs somewhat from the analog in Ref.
Ji et al. 2018a
. There the
P^{z}
of the quasi-TMDPDF and TMDPDF were taken to be equal, so the exponential term involving
\gamma_{\zeta}^{q}
does not appear. Also they interpreted the
b_{T}
dependence of their
g_{q}^{S}(b_{T},\mu)
to be short distance without considering its nonperturbative nature for
b_{T}\sim\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{-1}
, thus (incorrectly) concluding that Eq. (
III.1
) with a nontrivial
g_{q}^{S}(b_{T},\mu)
could still be interpreted as a matching equation.

III.2
Explicit relation at one loop

One-loop calculations comparing the quark nonsinglet quasi-TMDPDF and TMDPDF in the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme have been carried out in Refs.
Ji et al. 2018a
;
Ebert et al. 2019
in the limit of large
P^{z}
and
L
.
Both employ a spatial soft matrix element
\tilde{\Delta}_{S}^{q}
obtained by replacing
n\,{\to}\,\hat{z}
and
{\bar{n}}\,{\to}-\!\hat{z}
in Eq. (
14
).
Ref.
Ebert et al. 2019
separately calculates
\tilde{B}_{q}
and
\tilde{\Delta}_{S}^{q}
, allowing one to easily verify the structure of Eq. (
III.1
).
One obtains

\displaystyle C^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{\text{ns}}\bigl(\mu,xP^{z}\bigr)=1+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}C^{(1)}(\mu,xP^{z})+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,,\!\!

(25)

\displaystyle C^{(1)}(\mu,xP^{z})=-\ln^{2}\frac{(2xP^{z})^{2}}{\mu^{2}}+2\ln\frac{(2xP^{z})^{2}}{\mu^{2}}-4+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6},

\displaystyle g^{S}_{q}(b_{T},\mu)=1+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{2\pi}\ln\frac{b_{T}^{2}\mu^{2}}{4e^{-2\gamma_{E}}}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,.

(26)

The result of Ref.
Ji et al. 2018a
corresponds to combining Eqs. (
25
) and (
26
) as in Eq. (
III.1
),
and agrees with Ref.
Ebert et al. 2019
up the factor
\pi^{2}/6
due to a different definition of the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme.
We and Ref.
Ebert et al. 2019
use
\mu^{2}_{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}=4\pi e^{-\gamma_{E}}\mu^{2}_{\rm MS}
, whereas Ref.
Ji et al. 2018a
uses
\mu^{2\epsilon}_{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}=(4\pi\mu^{2}_{\rm MS})^{\epsilon}/\Gamma(1-\epsilon)
.

IV
Extracting
\gamma_{\zeta}
on Lattice

The relation between the quasi-TMDPDF and TMDPDF in Eq. (
III.1
) allows us to extract
\gamma_{\zeta}^{q}(\mu,b_{T})
using lattice QCD.
By considering the ratio of two copies of Eq. (
III.1
) with different hadron momenta
P^{z}_{1}\neq P^{z}_{2}
in the quasi-TMDPDF and the same
\zeta
in the TMDPDFs, the TMDPDFs and unknown soft contribution
g^{S}_{q}
cancel out, yielding our main result

\displaystyle\gamma^{q}_{\zeta}(\mu,b_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ln(P^{z}_{1}/P^{z}_{2})}\ln\frac{C^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{\text{ns}}(\mu,xP_{2}^{z})\,\tilde{f}^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{\text{ns}}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu,P^{z}_{1})}{C^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{\text{ns}}(\mu,xP_{1}^{z})\,\tilde{f}^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{\text{ns}}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu,P^{z}_{2})}

(27)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ln(P^{z}_{1}/P^{z}_{2})}\ln\frac{C^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{\text{ns}}(\mu,xP_{2}^{z})\,\int\!\mathrm{d}b^{z}\,e^{ib^{z}xP_{1}^{z}}\tilde{Z}^{\prime}(b^{z},\mu,\tilde{\mu})\tilde{Z}_{\rm uv}(b^{z},\tilde{\mu},a)\tilde{B}_{{\text{ns}}}(b^{z},\vec{b}_{T},a,L,P_{1}^{z})}{C^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{\text{ns}}(\mu,xP_{1}^{z})\,\int\!\mathrm{d}b^{z}\,e^{ib^{z}xP_{2}^{z}}\tilde{Z}^{\prime}(b^{z},\mu,\tilde{\mu})\tilde{Z}_{\rm uv}(b^{z},\tilde{\mu},a)\tilde{B}_{{\text{ns}}}(b^{z},\vec{b}_{T},a,L,P_{2}^{z})}\,.

The first line of Eq. (
27
) employs the quasi-TMDPDFs from Eqs. (
22
) and (
23
), while in the second line
we have explicitly canceled out the soft factors
\Delta^{q}_{S}(b_{T},a,L)
to express the result entirely in terms of quasi beam functions and renormalization factors. In the second line the divergences from
L\to\infty
cancel in the ratio.

It is important to note that
\gamma_{\zeta}^{q}
is independent of the choice for
x
,
P_{1}^{z}
and
P_{2}^{z}
on the right hand side of Eq. (
27
), and any residual dependence on these can thus be used to study systematic uncertainties.
Due to the universality of
\gamma_{\zeta}^{q}
, Eq. (
27
) can be evaluated with any hadron state (such as a pion).

It is currently not clear if the gluon anomalous dimension
\gamma^{g}_{\zeta}(\mu,b_{T})
can be obtained in the same manner. The concern is that in the analog of Eq. (
23
) the gluon could mix with the singlet quark, making the cancellation of soft factors problematic since
\tilde{\Delta}_{S}^{q}\neq\tilde{\Delta}_{S}^{g}
.
Also
\gamma^{g}_{\zeta}(\mu,b_{T})
can not be obtained from
\gamma^{q}_{\zeta}(\mu,b_{T})
with Casimir scaling, which is violated at
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{4})
for
\Gamma_{\rm cusp}^{i}

Boels et al. 2017
;
Moch et al. 2017
;
Grozin et al. 2017
and thus does not hold for
\gamma^{i}_{\zeta}(\mu,b_{T})
nonperturbatively.

IV.1
Illustration at one loop

For illustration, we explicitly show that we recover the correct Collins-Soper kernel at one loop.
This requires the ratio of the NLO coefficient Eq. (
25
),

\displaystyle\frac{C^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{\text{ns}}(\mu,xP_{2}^{z})}{C^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{\text{ns}}(\mu,xP_{1}^{z})}

(28)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\ln\frac{P^{z}_{1}}{P^{z}_{2}}\biggl(\ln\frac{4x^{2}P^{z}_{1}P^{z}_{2}}{\mu^{2}}-1\biggr)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,,

and likewise the ratio of the perturbative quasi-TMDPDFs, calculated with on-shell quark states,

\displaystyle~\frac{\tilde{f}^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{\text{ns}}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu,P^{z}_{1})}{\tilde{f}^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{\text{ns}}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\mu,P^{z}_{2})}

(29)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\ln\frac{P^{z}_{1}}{P^{z}_{2}}\biggl(-\ln\frac{x^{2}P^{z}_{1}P^{z}_{2}b_{T}^{2}}{e^{-2\gamma_{E}}}+1\biggr)+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,,

which can be obtained from the results given in appendix
A
.
Inserting Eqs. (
28
) and (
29
) into Eq. (
27
), we obtain

\displaystyle\gamma^{q}_{\zeta}(\mu,b_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ln\frac{P^{z}_{1}}{P^{z}_{2}}}\ln\biggl[1\!-\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\ln\frac{P^{z}_{1}}{P^{z}_{2}}\ln\frac{b_{T}^{2}\mu^{2}}{4e^{-2\gamma_{E}}}+\!\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\biggr]

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{\pi}\ln\frac{b_{T}^{2}\mu^{2}}{4e^{-2\gamma_{E}}}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,,

(30)

which is exactly the one-loop anomalous dimension.

V
Conclusions and Outlook

In this paper, we have proposed a novel approach to determine from lattice QCD the nonperturbative anomalous dimension
\gamma^{q}_{\zeta}(\mu,b_{T})
governing the Collins-Soper evolution of quark TMDPDFs, given in Eq. (
27
).
It involves matrix elements of an equal-time operator with boosted hadron states (referred to as quasi beam functions), UV renormalization, and a perturbative short distance kernel
C_{\text{ns}}^{\mathrm{TMD}}
. These are taken in a ratio with different hadron momentum, such that soft contributions cancel out.
The nonperturbative contribution to
\gamma^{q}_{\zeta}(\mu,b_{T})
is required in order to evolve TMDPDFs, determined at some initial scales from experiment (or perhaps in the future a separate lattice calculation), to the scales appearing in other phenomenological applications.

So far the coefficient
C_{\text{ns}}^{\mathrm{TMD}}
is known up to one-loop order for matching an
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
quasi-TMDPDF to the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
TMDPDF.
To make use of this result for lattice calculations using nonperturbative renormalization schemes would require explicit computations of the corresponding scheme conversion factor
\tilde{Z}^{\prime}
in Eq. (
23
). Related examples of nonperturbative renormalization are Refs.
Hägler et al. 2009
;
Musch et al. 2011
for straight-Wilson line quasi-TMDs, Refs.
Constantinou and Panagopoulos 2017
;
Stewart and Zhao 2018
for application of the RI/MOM scheme to the quasi-PDF, and Ref.
Monahan and Orginos 2017
for the gradient flow method.
First lattice studies of the equal-time TMD quasi beam function in Eq. (
20
) have been carried out in Refs.
Musch et al. 2012
;
Engelhardt et al. 2016
;
Yoon et al. 2015
;
Yoon et al. 2017
. There, ratios of this quasi beam function with
b^{z}=0
and the same
P^{z}
were considered, in which case renormalization factors cancel.
Our method to determine
\gamma_{\zeta}^{q}
instead requires to Fourier transform the renormalized results from
b^{z}
into
x
space, and consider a ratio with two different
P^{z}
values in order to expose the Collins-Soper kernel.

A nonperturbative result for
\gamma_{\zeta}^{q}(\mu,b_{T})
could also have interesting applications for perturbative
b_{T}^{-1}\sim q_{T}\gg\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
. Here it is known both from factorization and renormalon analyses
Becher and Bell 2014
;
Scimemi and Vladimirov 2017
that the perturbative series for
\gamma_{\zeta}^{q}(\mu,b_{T})
has
\sim b_{T}^{2}\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{2}
power corrections, so one could attempt to obtain the coefficient of this
b_{T}^{2}
power correction from the small
b_{T}
limit of a nonperturbative lattice QCD result. In practice this may be difficult due to the need for a
1/(b_{T}P^{z})
expansion in the relation between the quasi-TMDPDF and TMDPDF in Eq. (
III.1
).

A possible modification to our method is to consider a boosted quark state rather than a hadron state, which could have computational advantages.
(We thank Will Detmold for this suggestion.)
Since one can only simulate off-shell gauge-fixed quark states with Euclidean momentum
p_{E}^{2}\geq(p^{z})^{2}
in lattice QCD, this would require a reconsideration of various ingredients in the proposal made here. It would be interesting to pursue this in the future.

Appendix A
(Quasi) TMDPDF at NLO

Here we summarize explicit results for the bare TMDPDF
f^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{q}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\epsilon,\zeta)

and quasi-TMDPDF
\tilde{f}^{\mathrm{TMD}}_{q}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\epsilon,P^{z})
at one loop. These are
obtained by evaluating the operators Eqs. (
13
) and (
20
) in an external quark state
of lightlike momentum
p^{\mu}=(xP^{z},0,0,xP^{z})
, and
combining them with the appropriate soft or quasi soft function as in
Eqs. (
II
) and (
22
) without performing the UV renormalization.
In both cases, we use pure dimensional regularization to regulate both UV and IR divergences,
and defined the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
renormalization scale as
\mu^{2}_{\overline{\mathrm{MS}}}=4\pi e^{-\gamma_{E}}\mu^{2}_{\rm MS}
.

The result for the physical TMDPDF is given by

\displaystyle f_{q}^{\mathrm{TMD}}(x,\vec{b}_{T},\epsilon,\zeta)=\delta(1-x)

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{2\pi}\biggl[-\biggl(\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm IR}}+L_{b}\biggr)P_{qq}(x)+(1-x)\biggr]_{+}^{1}\Theta(1-x)\Theta(x)

(31)

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{2\pi}\delta(1-x)\biggl[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}^{2}}+\biggl(\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\rm UV}}+L_{b}\biggr)\biggl(\frac{3}{2}+\ln\frac{\mu^{2}}{\zeta}\biggr)-\frac{1}{2}L_{b}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}\biggr]+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})\,.

This result is obtained in the rapidity regulators of Refs.
Collins 2011
;
Becher and Neubert 2011
;
Becher and Bell 2012
;
Chiu et al. 2012b
;
Chiu et al. 2012a
;
Echevarria et al. 2012
;
Li et al. 2016
,
which only differ by the precise definition of
\zeta
.
For a more detailed comparison of results in the literature see Ref.
Ebert et al. 2019
.

For the quasi-TMDPDF, we take the physical limits
P^{z}\gg b_{T}^{-1},L\to\infty

Abstract
. We prove that the automorphism group of Hilbert square of a Cayley’s K3 surface of Picard number 2 2 is the free product of three cyclic groups of order two. The generators are three Beauville involutions.

2.2.
Hyperbolic geometry

Recall that the
n

{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
, is the unique simply connected,
n
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold with a constant negative sectional curvature equal to
-1
. One of the models of hyperbolic plane is the upper half-plane

(2.7)

{\mathbb{H}}^{2}=\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\mid y>0\}.

Recall that the group of all orientation-preserving isometries of
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
is

(2.8)

\text{M}\ddot{\text{o}}\text{b}^{+}({\mathbb{H}}^{2}):=\{g(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\mid a,b,c,d\in{\mathbb{R}},ad-bc=1\},

where
z=x+\sqrt{-1}y
, a complex number. Note that such an isometry corresponds to a matrix

(2.9)

\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}}).

Moreover, since two matrices in
{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
induce the same isometry if and only if their matrices differ by a factor of
\pm 1
, hence
\text{M}\ddot{\text{o}}\text{b}^{+}({\mathbb{H}}^{2})\cong{\rm PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
.

Any orientation-preserving isometry of
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
is one of the following three types.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
g(\neq\operatorname{id})
be any M
\ddot{\text{o}}
bius transformation in (
2.8
). Then

(1)

g
is
elliptic
if and only if
g
has a unique fixed point in
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
;

(2)

g
is

if and only if
g
has two fixed points on
\partial{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
;

(3)

is
parabolic
if and only if
g
has a unique fixed point on
\partial{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
,

where
\partial{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
means the set of boundary points of
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
.

Two M
\ddot{\text{o}}
bius transformations

are

if there is a M
\ddot{\text{o}}
bius transformation

such that

. Any M
\ddot{\text{o}}
bius transformation is conjugate to one of the above three types. Indeed, a parabolic isometry is conjugate to

,

. An elliptic one is conjugate to
g(z)=\frac{s}{-1/sz}=-s^{2}/z
,

. A hyperbolic one is conjugate to

,

(x,y)\in\operatorname{Amp}(X)
or
-(x,y)\in\operatorname{Amp}(X)
by (
2.4
). Moreover, since
(1,0),(1,1)\in\operatorname{Amp}(X)
,
(1,-1,0)
and
(1,-1,1)
are ample on
X^{[2]}
by Lemma
2.1
, hence intersections with these imply that
x>0
, i.e.,
(x,y)
is an ample divisor of
X
.
∎

For a divisor
D\in NS(X^{[2]})
, let
[D]_{e}
be the
e
-coordinate with respect to the basis
\{h_{1},e,h_{2}\}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

\operatorname{Aut}(X^{[2]})\cong\langle\iota_{0},\iota_{1},\iota_{2}\rangle.

Proof.

Let
f\in\operatorname{Aut}(X^{[2]})
be a non-natural automorphism of
X^{[2]}
. By Lemma
3.1
, we have that
f^{*}(e)=(x,\lambda,y)
with
(x,y)\in\operatorname{Amp}(X)
and
\lambda<0
. If
[\iota_{k}^{*}f^{*}(e)]_{e}\geq 0
for some
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, then again by Lemma
3.1
,
\iota_{k}^{*}f^{*}(e)=e
, i.e.,
\iota_{k}^{*}f^{*}=g^{n*}
for some
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Now by (
1.4
),
g^{n}=f\iota_{k}
in
\operatorname{Aut}(X^{[2]})
. Since
g=\iota_{0}\iota_{1}\iota_{2}
and by Theorem
2.13
,
f\in\langle\iota_{0},\iota_{1},\iota_{2}\rangle.

Next, suppose that for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
[\iota_{k}^{*}f^{*}(e)]_{e}<0
. Then we claim the following.

Claim
.

For some
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
[\iota_{m}^{*}f^{*}(e)]_{e}>\lambda
.

Proof of Claim
.

Suppose that for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
[\iota_{k}^{*}f^{*}(e)]_{e}\leq\lambda
.
In
[
15
, Example 1]
,
\iota_{0}^{*},\iota_{1}^{*},\iota_{2}^{*}\in O(H^{2}(X^{[2]},{\mathbb{Z}}))
are given as follows with respect to the basis
\{h_{1},e,h_{2}\}
:

(3.1)

\iota_{0}^{*}=\begin{bmatrix}3&2&2\\
-4&-3&-2\\
0&0&-1\end{bmatrix},\hskip 5.69054pt\iota_{1}^{*}=\begin{bmatrix}5&2&-2\\
-6&-3&2\\
6&2&-3\end{bmatrix},\hskip 5.69054pt\iota_{2}^{*}=\begin{bmatrix}27&4&-16\\
-14&-3&8\\
42&6&-25\end{bmatrix}.

In particular,
[\iota_{0}^{*}f^{*}(e)]_{e}=-4x-3\lambda-2y\leq\lambda
, i.e.,
2x+y\geq-2\lambda
. Similarly,
[\iota_{1}^{*}f^{*}(e)]_{e}=-6x-3\lambda+2y\leq\lambda
, i.e.,
3x-y\geq-2\lambda
and
[\iota_{2}^{*}f^{*}(e)]_{e}=-14x-3\lambda+8y\leq\lambda
, i.e.,
7x-4y\geq-2\lambda
. Moreover, by Theorem
2.13
,
\iota_{3l+k}^{*}=g^{-l*}\circ\iota_{k}^{*}\circ g^{l*}
for
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
k=0,1,2
. Since
g
is natural,
g^{*}(e)=e
, hence
[\iota_{3l+k}^{*}f^{*}(e)]_{e}=[\iota_{k}^{*}g^{l*}f^{*}(e)]_{e}
, where
k=0,1,
or
2
. Let
g^{l*}f^{*}(e)=(x^{\prime},\lambda,y^{\prime})
with
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\operatorname{Amp}(X)
. If we let
r^{\prime}=y^{\prime}/x^{\prime}
, then
(g^{l*}f^{*}(e))^{2}=(e)^{2}=-2
gives

(3.2)

4x^{\prime 2}(1+r^{\prime}-r^{\prime 2})=2\lambda^{2}-2.

Now for
k=0
,
[\iota_{0}^{*}g^{l*}f^{*}(e)]_{e}=-4x^{\prime}-3\lambda-2y^{\prime}\leq\lambda
gives that
2x^{\prime}+y^{\prime}\geq-2\lambda
. By taking the squares, we have that
(x^{\prime}(2+2r^{\prime}))^{2}\geq 4\lambda^{2}
. By substituting (
3.2
), we have that
x^{\prime 2}(4+4r^{\prime}+r^{\prime 2})\geq 8x^{\prime 2}(1+r^{\prime}-r^{\prime 2})+4
or
x^{\prime 2}(9r^{\prime 2}-4r^{\prime}-4)\geq 4
. However, the left hand side is negative if
\frac{2-2\sqrt{10}}{9}<r^{\prime}<\frac{2+2\sqrt{10}}{9}
.

Similarly for
k=1
, we have
x^{\prime 2}(9r^{\prime 2}-14r^{\prime}+1)\geq 4
, and the left hand side is negative if
\frac{7-2\sqrt{10}}{9}<r^{\prime}<\frac{7+2\sqrt{10}}{9}
. For
k=2
, we have
x^{\prime 2}(24r^{\prime 2}-64r^{\prime}+41)\geq 4
, and the left hand side is negative if
\frac{16-\sqrt{10}}{12}<r^{\prime}<\frac{16+\sqrt{10}}{12}
. Since the ample cone of
X
is given in (
2.4
),
for some power
l
, at least one of three inequalities induces a contradiction, hence
[\iota_{m}^{*}f^{*}(e)]_{e}>\lambda
.

If we continue this process at least
|\lambda|-1
times, we get a contradiction to
[\iota_{k}^{*}f^{*}(e)]_{e}<0
for all
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Hence, we may assume that for some
\iota^{*}=\iota_{k_{1}}^{*}\iota_{k_{2}}^{*}\cdots\iota_{k_{j}}^{*}
,
[\iota^{*}f^{*}(e)]_{e}\geq 0
. Then again by Lemma
3.1
,
\iota^{*}f^{*}(e)=e
, i.e.,
f\iota=g^{l}
in
\operatorname{Aut}(X^{[2]})
for some
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Again by
g=\iota_{0}\iota_{1}\iota_{2}
and by Theorem
2.13
,
f\in\langle\iota_{0},\iota_{1},\iota_{2}\rangle.

∎

3.2.

Next, we will show that
\langle\iota_{0},\iota_{1},\iota_{2}\rangle\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\ast{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\ast{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.

Note that the Picard lattice
NS(X^{[2]})
of Hilbert square of the K3 surface
X
has signature
(1,2)
. For an ample divisor
H
on
X^{[2]}
, we consider the
light cone
of
NS(X^{[2]})
:

(3.3)

\mathcal{L}^{+}:=\{D\in\mathop{\rm NS}\nolimits(X^{[2]})\otimes{\mathbb{R}}\mid D^{2}>0,D.H>0\}.

If we let
H=(1,-1,0)
with respect to the basis
\{h_{1},e,h_{2}\}
, then

(3.4)

\mathcal{L}^{+}=\{(x,\lambda,y)\in\mathop{\rm NS}\nolimits(X^{[2]})\otimes{\mathbb{R}}\mid 2(x^{2}+xy-y^{2})>\lambda^{2},2x+y+\lambda>0\}.

Since the signature of
\mathop{\rm NS}\nolimits(X^{[2]})
is
(1,2)
,
\mathcal{L}^{+}/{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
with distance
|AB|
defined by

(3.5)

\|A\|\|B\|\cosh|AB|=(A.B)

is isomorphic to
2
-dimensional hyperbolic geometry
\mathbb{H}^{2}
, where
\|A\|=\sqrt{(A.A)}
.

Note that for each
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\iota_{k}^{*}
preserves distances of
{\mathcal{L}}^{+}/{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
, hence
\iota_{k}^{*}\in\operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{H}}^{2})
and it is an involution. Fixed points of
\iota_{0}^{*}
,
\iota_{1}^{*}
and
\iota_{2}^{*}
are
[1,-1,0]
,
[1,-1,1]
, and
[2,-1,3]
, respectively. Here
[x,\lambda,y]
denotes a class of points modulo
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
. Note that since these fixed points correspond to interior points of
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
,
\iota_{0}^{*},\iota_{1}^{*},\iota_{2}^{*}
are all elliptic isometries.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that fixed points
[1,-1,0],[1,-1,1],[2,-1,3]
correspond to ample divisors of square
2
on
X^{[2]}
with respect to the Beauville-Bogomolov-Fujiki’s form.

In the rest, we will show that there is no relation between
\iota_{0}^{*},\iota_{1}^{*},\iota_{2}^{*}
.

It is known that if
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
are two geodesics in
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
and
z_{i}\in\gamma_{i}
are two points on them, then there exists an isometry
g\in M\ddot{\text{o}}b^{+}({\mathbb{H}}^{2})
such that
g(\gamma_{1})=\gamma_{2}
and
g(z_{1})=z_{2}
. Hence we may assume that two points of
[1,-1,0],[1,-1,1],[2,-1,3]
map to
(0,1),(0,k)\in{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
with
k>1
on the
y
-axis of
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
, a hyperbolic line. Then the remaining point maps to
(b,m)\in{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
with
b\neq 0
. Moreover,
\iota_{0}^{*},\iota_{1}^{*},\iota_{2}^{*}
correspond to the elliptic isometries
T,U,V
whose fixed points are
(b,m),(0,1),(0,k)
respectively. Hence these M
\ddot{\text{o}}
bius transformations correspond to the following matrices in
{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
;

(3.6)

U=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{bmatrix},\hskip 5.69054ptV=\begin{bmatrix}0&k\\
-1/k&0\end{bmatrix}

and

(3.7)

T=\begin{bmatrix}1&b\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0&m\\
-1/m&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&-b\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}b/m&-(b^{2}+m^{2})/m\\
1/m&-b/m\end{bmatrix}.

Note that all traces of
T,U,V
are zero, i.e, elliptic isometries with fixed points
(b,m),(0,1),(0,k)
respectively. Note also that the first matrix in (
3.7
)
corresponds to the translation
z\mapsto z+b
.

Consider the group
G
generated by
T,U,V
. Note that any two of three generates a free group
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\ast{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
(Example
2.12
). Hence any element in
G
can be written as
TX_{1}TX_{2}\cdots TX_{k}
, where each
X_{i}
is of the form

(3.8)

(UV)^{n},(UV)^{n}U,(VU)^{s},(VU)^{s}V

in the free group
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\ast{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
generated by
U,V
, where
n,s\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

TX_{i}
is hyperbolic for any
X_{i}
in (
3.8
).

Proof.

We use the classification of a hyperbolic isometry by the trace (Theorem
2.6
). First,

(3.9)

TU=\begin{bmatrix}(b^{2}+m^{2})/m&b/m\\
b/m&1/m\end{bmatrix}

and its trace is
b^{2}/m+m+1/m>2
, hence it is hyperbolic. Similarly,

(3.10)

T(UV)^{s}U=TU(VU)^{s}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{b^{2}+m^{2}}{m}k^{s}&*\\
*&\frac{1}{m}k^{-s}\end{bmatrix}

and its trace is
\frac{b^{2}}{m}k^{s}+mk^{s}+(mk^{s})^{-1}>2
, hence it is hyperbolic.
Moreover,

(3.11)

T(VU)^{s-1}V=TU(UV)^{s}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{b^{2}+m^{2}}{m}k^{-s}&*\\
*&\frac{1}{m}k^{s}\end{bmatrix}

and its trace is
\frac{b^{2}}{m}k^{-s}+mk^{-s}+(mk^{-s})^{-1}>2
, hence it is also hyperbolic.

Next,

(3.12)

T(VU)^{s}=TU(UV)^{s}U=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{b}{m}k^{s}&*\\
*&\frac{b}{m}k^{-s}\end{bmatrix}

and its trace is
|\frac{b}{m}(k^{s}-k^{-s})|>2
by induction. Indeed for
s=1
,
TVU
corresponds to one of
\iota_{i}^{*}\iota_{j}^{*}\iota_{k}^{*}
where
i,j,k\in\{0,1,2\}
are distinct. It is enough to show that
\iota_{2}^{*}\iota_{1}^{*}\iota_{0}^{*}
is hyperbolic by Theorem
2.7
.

(3.13)

\iota_{2}^{*}\iota_{1}^{*}\iota_{0}^{*}=\begin{bmatrix}5&0&8\\
0&1&0\\
8&0&13\end{bmatrix}

has two fixed points

(3.14)

((-1+\sqrt{5})/2,0,1)\hskip 5.69054pt\text{ and }\hskip 5.69054pt(-(1+\sqrt{5})/2,0,1)

on the boundary of
{\mathcal{L}}^{+}
, hence they correspond to two points on
\partial{\mathbb{H}}^{2}
.
Thus
TVU
is hyperbolic, i.e., the absolute value of its trace
|\frac{b}{m}(k-k^{-1})|>2
. For
s=2
,

(3.15)

\begin{array}[]{lcl}|\frac{b}{m}(k^{2}-k^{-2})|&=&|\frac{b}{m}(k-k^{-1})|(k+k^{-1})\\
&>&|\frac{b}{m}(k-k^{-1})|\\
&>&2.\\
\end{array}

Now suppose that
|k^{n}-k^{-n}|\geq|k^{n-1}-k^{-n+1}|
. Then

(3.16)

\begin{array}[]{lcl}|k^{n+1}-k^{-n-1}|&=&|(k^{n}-k^{-n})(k+k^{-1})-(k^{n-1}-k^{-n+1})|\\
&\geq&|(k^{n}-k^{-n})(k+k^{-1}-1)|\\
&\geq&|k^{n}-k^{-n}|.\\
\end{array}

Hence the trace
|\frac{b}{m}(k^{s}-k^{-s})|>2
and
T(VU)^{s}
is hyperbolic.

Similarly,

(3.17)

T(UV)^{s+1}=TU(VU)^{s}V=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{b}{m}k^{-s-1}&*\\
*&\frac{b}{m}k^{s+1}\end{bmatrix}

and its trace is
|\frac{b}{m}(k^{s+1}-k^{-s-1})|>2
, hence
T(UV)^{s+1}
is hyperbolic.
∎

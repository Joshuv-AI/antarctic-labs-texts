The centre of the modular affine vertex algebra
Tomoyuki Arakawa, Lewis Topley and Juan J. Villarreal

Abstract
. The Feigin–Frenkel theorem states that, over the complex numbers, the centre of the universal affine vertex algebra at the critical level is an infinite rank polynomial algebra. The first author and W. Wang observed that in positive characteristics, the universal affine vertex algebra contains a large central subalgebra known as the p p -centre. They conjectured that at the critical level the centre should be generated by the Feigin–Frenkel centre and the p p -centre. In this paper we prove the conjecture for classical simple Lie algebras for p p larger than the Coxeter number, and for exceptional Lie algebras in large characteristics. Finally, we give an example which shows that at non-critical level the center is larger than the p p -centre.



Dedicated to Victor Kac on the occasion of his 80th birthday.

Group schemes, jets and arcs

2.1.
Arcs and jets

Throughout this paper we fix an algebraically closed field
\mathbbm{k}
of characteristic
p>0
. All vector spaces, algebras and schemes will be defined over
\mathbbm{k}
, with the exception of Section
5.1
where we discuss modular reduction.

Here we discuss jet schemes and arc spaces. We refer the reader to
[
Is
]
for slightly more detail.

A
higher derivation
of a commutative algebra
A
is a tuple
\partial=(\partial^{(i)}\mid i\geq 0)
of linear endomorphisms of
A
with
\partial^{(0)}
equal to the identity, satisfying the following relations for
i\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\partial^{(i)}(ab)=\sum_{i_{1}+i_{2}=i}\partial^{(i_{1})}(a)\partial^{(i_{2})}(b).\end{array}

Similarly a
higher derivation of order
m
is a tuple
\partial=(\partial^{(i)}\mid i=0.,,,.m)
satisfying (
2.1
) for
i,j\leq m
. A differential algebra (of order
m
)
(A,\partial)
is an algebra equipped with a higher derivation (of order
m
).

There is a universal differential algebra
(J_{\infty}A,\partial)
with a homomorphism
\alpha:A\to J_{\infty}A
such that, for every other differential algebra
(B,\sigma)
admitting a homomorphism
\beta:A\to B
, there is a unique homomorphism
\varphi
of differential algebras such that
\varphi\circ\alpha=\beta
.

We construct
J_{\infty}A
as follows. First assume
A=\mathbbm{k}[x_{k}\mid k\in K]
is a polynomial ring, where
K
is an index set. Define
J_{\infty}A:=\mathbbm{k}[x_{k}^{(j)}\mid k\in K,\ 0\leq j]
. The homomorphism
\alpha:A\to J_{\infty}A
is given by
x_{k}\mapsto x_{k}^{(0)}
and the differential
\partial
is determined uniquely by (
2.1
) along with
\partial^{(i)}x_{k}^{(0)}=x_{k}^{(i)}
and

\partial^{(i)}\partial^{(j)}=\binom{i+j}{i}\partial^{(i+j)}

for all
i,j,k
in the appropriate ranges. If
I\subseteq A
is an ideal then we can define
J_{\infty}I:=(\partial^{(i)}a\mid 0\leq i,\ a\in I)
, which is a differential ideal of
J_{\infty}A
.

Now if
A
has a presentation
A=\mathbbm{k}[x_{k}\mid k\in K]/I
then we define
J_{\infty}A:=J_{\infty}\mathbbm{k}[x_{k}\mid k\in K]/J_{\infty}I
. The map
A\to J_{\infty}A
and higher derivation
\partial
are defined as above, and

is readily seen to inject. It is not hard to see that the isomorphism type of
J_{\infty}A
is independent the presentation, and that
J_{\infty}A
satisfies the universal property mentioned above. It follows immediately that
J_{\infty}
is an endofunctor on the category of commutative algebras, and

is known as the arc algebra of

.

If
A
is a Hopf algebra with comultiplication
\Delta:A\to A\otimes A
, counit
\eta:A\to\mathbbm{k}
and antipode

, then there is a natural Hopf structure on

extending the structure on

. The existence of such a structure follows from functoriality of

, however we can describe it explicitly: for

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}(\Delta(\partial^{(i)}a))=\partial^{(i)}\Delta(a);\\
S(\partial^{(i)}(a))=\partial^{(i)}S(a);\\
\eta(a)=a+J_{m}\Ker\eta.\end{array}

where we equip

with the structure of a differential algebra in the obvious manner, and note that
J_{\infty}A/J_{\infty}\Ker\eta\cong J_{\infty}\mathbbm{k}=\mathbbm{k}
.

For

there is an analogous construction of a universal differential algebra

of order

. Briefly,
J_{m}\mathbbm{k}[x_{k}\mid k\in K]
is the subalgebra of

generated by

If

and the

\displaystyle\Hom_{\operatorname{Sch}/\mathbbm{k}}(Y,J_{m}X)=\Hom_{\operatorname{Sch}/\mathbbm{k}}(Y\times_{\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbbm{k})}\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbbm{k}[t]/(t^{m+1})),X),

and so the

If

and

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
G
is a group scheme which is generated by a collection
(H_{k})_{k\in K}
of subgroup schemes. Then
J_{\infty}G
is generated by
(J_{\infty}H_{k})_{k\in K}
.

Proof.

Let
A
be a commutative Hopf algebra. Observe that the collection of Hopf ideals of
A
is stable under sums, but not intersections. It follows that for any algebra ideal
I\subseteq A
there is a unique maximal Hopf ideal subject to
H(I)\subseteq I
. More precisely it is sum of all Hopf ideals contained in
I
.

We claim that for any ideal
I
we have
J_{\infty}H(I)=H(J_{\infty}I)
. Using properties (
2.1
) one can show that
J_{\infty}H(I)
is a Hopf ideal of
J_{\infty}A
contained in
J_{\infty}I
, and so
J_{\infty}H(I)\subseteq H(J_{\infty}I)
. Conversely we have
H(I)\subseteq I\subseteq J_{\infty}I
and so
H(I)\subseteq HJ_{\infty}(I)
. In order to obtain

it suffices to show that

is closed under

) once again one can easily show that

preserves the class of differential-closed ideals, and this completes the proof of the claim.

For

be the Hopf ideal defining the subgroup

. The hypothesis of the lemma is equivalent to the assertion

, and the goal is to show that
H(\cap_{k}J_{\infty}I_{k})=0
. Now we have
H(\cap_{k}J_{\infty}I_{k})=H(J_{\infty}\cap_{k}I_{k})=J_{\infty}H(\cap_{k}I_{k})=0
, and the proof is complete.
∎

\gr Z(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{\mathfrak{g}}
.

We write
\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{p}=\{f^{p}\mid f\in\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]\}
for the subalgebra of
p
th powers. There is an obvious inclusion
\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{p}\subseteq\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{\mathfrak{g}}
since
\mathfrak{g}
acts by derivations.

Recall that
\mathfrak{g}
admits a natural
G
-equivariant restricted structure
x\mapsto x^{[p]}
, which gives rise to a large central subalgebra of
U(\mathfrak{g})
known as the
p
-centre
. It is generated by elements
x^{p}-x^{[p]}
. Since the map
x\mapsto x^{p}-x^{[p]}
is
p
-semilinear
Z_{p}(\mathfrak{g})
is a polynomial ring in
\dim(\mathfrak{g})
generators, and its spectrum is naturally identified as a
G
-set with the first Frobenius twist of the coadjoint representation
\operatorname{Spec}Z_{p}(\mathfrak{g})=(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{(1)}
, and
\gr Z_{p}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{p}
under the identification
\gr U(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]
; see
[
Ja1
]
for more detail.

The next result, known as Veldkamp’s theorem, describes the centre of
U(\mathfrak{g})
for
p>h
;
[
Ve
]
. Numerous authors improved the bound on
p
(see
[
Ja1
, §9]
), and in fact the theorem holds under the standard hypotheses
[
BG
, Theorem 3.5]
.

The statement presented here can easily be deduced from
[
BG
, Section 3]
and
[
Sk
, Theorem 5.4(1)]
, see also
[
KW
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

We have the following:

(1)

\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G}
is generated by
r
algebraically independent elements
P_{1},...,P_{r}
.

(2)

\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{\mathfrak{g}}
is generated by
\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G}
and
\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{p}

(3)

\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{\mathfrak{g}}
is a free
\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{p}
-module with basis given by restricted monomials

\{P_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots P^{k_{r}}_{r}\mid 0\leq k_{j}<p\text{ for all }j\}.

(4)

\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{\mathfrak{g}}\cong\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G}\otimes_{(\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{p})^{G}}\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{p}
.

(5)

The
P_{i}
admit filtered lifts
Z_{1},...,Z_{r}
and
U(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
is a polynomial algebra on
r
generators.

(6)

Z(\mathfrak{g})
is generated by
U(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
and
Z_{p}(\mathfrak{g})
.

(7)

Z(\mathfrak{g})
is a free
Z_{p}(\mathfrak{g})
-module of rank
p^{\dim\mathfrak{g}}
, with basis given by restricted monomials

\{Z_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots Z_{r}^{i_{r}}\mid 0\leq i_{j}<p\text{ for all }j\}.

(8)

Z(\mathfrak{g})\cong U(\mathfrak{g})^{G}\otimes_{Z_{p}(\mathfrak{g})^{G}}Z_{p}(\mathfrak{g})
.
\hfill\qed

3.
Modular vertex algebras

3.1.
Spaces of formal series

Let
V
a
\mathbbm{k}
-vector space, and
V[z]
(resp.
V[[z]]
, resp.
V(\!(z)\!)
) the space of polynomials (resp. formal power series, resp. formal Laurent series) in
z
with coefficients in
V
. We warn the reader that
V\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}\mathbbm{k}[[z]]\subsetneq V[[z]]
when
V
is infinite dimensional. These spaces are equipped with a higher derivation
\partial_{z}=(\partial_{z}^{(i)})_{\geq 0}
where
\partial_{z}^{(0)}
is the identity map and
\partial_{z}^{(k)}
is defined by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\partial^{(i)}z^{m}=\binom{m}{i}z^{m-i}\text{ for }i\in\mathbb{N},\,\,m\in\mathbb{Z}.

The reader can easily check that (
2.1
) holds.
Note that if
i=i_{0}+i_{1}p+\cdots+i_{k}p^{k}
is the
p
-adic expansion of
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
we have a factorisation

(3.2)

\displaystyle\partial^{(i)}=(\partial)^{i_{0}}(\partial^{(p)})^{i_{1}}\cdots(\partial^{(p^{k})})^{i_{k}}.

3.2.
Modular vertex algebras

Vertex algebras have been widely studied over the complex numbers, and so to emphasise the fact that our underlying field has positive characteristic we call them
modular vertex algebras
, see
[
Bo1
,
BR
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A modular vertex algebra is a
\mathbbm{k}
-vector space
V
equipped with a family of bilinear products

\begin{split}&V\otimes V\rightarrow V\\
&a\otimes b\rightarrow a_{(n)}b\,,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}\,,\end{split}

and a vector
{\boldsymbol{1}}\in V
such that

(1)

a_{(n)}b=0
for
n\gg 0
,

(2)

{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(n)}a=\delta_{n,-1}a
,

(3)

a_{(-1)}{\boldsymbol{1}}=a
,

(4)

The following identity is satisfied

(3.3)

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(-1)^{j}\binom{n}{j}\left(a_{(m+n-j)}b_{(k+j)}-(-1)^{n}b_{(n+k-j)}a_{(m+j)})\right)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\binom{m}{j}(a_{(n+j)}b)_{(m+k-j)}\,.

We also have the following equivalent definition.

Definition 3.2
.

A modular vertex algebra is a
\mathbbm{k}
-vector space
V
, equipped with a vector
{\boldsymbol{1}}\in V
, a family of endomorphism
T^{(k)}\in\End(V)
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, an a linear map

Y\colon V\to\Hom_{\mathbbm{k}}(V,V(\!(z)\!))\,,\qquad a\mapsto Y(a,z)\,,

subject to the following axioms:

(1
′
)

Y({\boldsymbol{1}},z)=I
,
Y(a,z){\boldsymbol{1}}\in V[[z]]
,
Y(a,z){\boldsymbol{1}}\big|_{z=0}=a
.

(2
′
)

Y(T^{(k)}a,z)=\partial^{(k)}_{z}Y(a,z)
and
T^{(k)}{\boldsymbol{1}}=0
.

(3
′
)

For every
a,b\in V
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\begin{split}(z_{1}-z_{2})^{N}Y(a,z_{1})Y(b,z_{2})=(z_{1}-z_{2})^{N}Y(b,z_{2})Y(a,z_{1})\,.\\
\end{split}

The equivalence between the Definition
3.1
and Definition
3.2
is well-known over
\mathbb{C}
and is explained in
[
Mas
, Theorem 4.3]
in case the base is an arbitrary ring. We sketch the correspondence here. From Definition
3.1
we define
T^{(k)}\in\End(V)
for
k\geq 1
and
Y:V\rightarrow\End(V)((z))
as follows

\begin{split}&Y(a,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{(n)}z^{-n-1}\,,\qquad T^{(k)}(a):=a_{(-k-1)}{\boldsymbol{1}}\,,\qquad\text{ for }a\in V.\end{split}

The conditions (1), (2) and (3) follow easily from (
3.3
). On the other hand, using Definition
3.2
we can write
Y(a,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{(n)}z^{-n-1}
and the operators
a_{(i)}
satisfy the required axioms.

Definition 3.3
.

For a vertex algebra
V
the centre is the subspace

\mathfrak{z}(V):=\{b\in V\mid a_{(n)}b=0\text{ for all }a\in V\text{ and }n\geq 0\}\,.

From (
3.3
) with
n=0
we see that
the centre coincides with the set of elements
v\in V
such that

[v_{(m)},a_{(k)}]=0\qquad\text{for all }a\in V

for all
m,k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

3.3.
Modular Poisson vertex algebras

A commutative vertex algebra
V
is one such that
u_{(n)}v=0
for all
u,v\in V
and
n\geq 0
. Equivalently it satisfies
Y(\bullet,z):V\to V[[z]]
. In this case, the product
a,b\mapsto a_{(-1)}b
is commutative, associative and unital with unit
{\boldsymbol{1}}
. And, the endomorphisms
T^{(k)}
define a higher derivation of this commutative algebra (
2.1
). Moreover the category of commutative differential algebras is equivalent to the category of commutative vertex algebras. The key difference between characteristic zero and the modular setting is that differential algebras over
\mathbb{C}
are equipped with a higher differential of order 1, as opposed to a higher differential over
\mathbbm{k}
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
(V,{\boldsymbol{1}},T)
be a commutative vertex algebra, with
T=(T^{(k)})_{k\geq 0}
.

For a formal power series
a(z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{m}z^{m}
the
polar part
of
a(z)
is defined to be
a_{-}(z)=\sum_{m<0}a_{(m)}z^{m}
.

We say that
V
is a Poisson vertex algebra if there is a linear map

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}Y_{-}&:&V\to\Hom(V,z^{-1}V[z^{-1}]),\\
&&a\mapsto Y_{-}(a,z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{(n)}z^{-n-1}\end{array}

subject to the following axioms:

(i)

Y_{-}(T^{(k)}a,z)=\partial^{(k)}_{z}Y(a,z)
.

(ii)

Y_{-}(a,z)b=\left(\sum_{k\geq 0}z^{k}T^{(k)}Y(b,-z)a\right)_{-}
.

(iii)

[a_{(m)},Y_{-}(b,w)]=\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{m}{k}\left(w^{m-k}Y_{-}(a_{(k)}b,w)\right)_{-}\,.

(iv)

Y_{-}(a,w)(b\cdot c)=(Y_{-}(a,w)b)\cdot c+(Y_{-}(a,w)c)\cdot b
.

We refer the reader to
[
FB
, §16.2]
for an introduction.

Now suppose that
V
is a graded vertex algebra
V=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}V_{i}
. Then we can define a filtration
V=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}F_{i}V
by letting
F_{d}V
be the subspace spanned by

(3.7)

\displaystyle a_{-n_{1}-1}^{1}a_{-n_{2}-1}^{2}\cdots a_{-n_{m}-1}^{m}{\boldsymbol{1}}

where
a^{1},...,a^{m}\in V
are strong generators with
a^{i}\in V_{d_{i}}
and

\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i}\leq d.
For the following theorem see for example
[
Ar
, §3.7]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

This filtration is compatible with the vertex algebra structure, and the associated graded algebra
\gr V
inherits the structure of a Poisson vertex algebra.

3.4.
Modular affine vertex algebras

Let
G
be a connected, simple group scheme over
\mathbbm{k}
such that the characteristic of
\mathbbm{k}
is very good for the root system. Recall that
\kappa
denotes the normalised Killing form (
2.7
) on
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}(G)
.

The loop Lie algebra is
\mathfrak{g}(\!(t)\!):=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathbbm{k}(\!(t)\!)
and the Kac–Moody affinization
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}:=\mathfrak{g}(\!(t)\!)\oplus\mathbbm{k}K
of
\mathfrak{g}
is the central extension of
\mathfrak{g}(\!(t)\!)
afforded by
\kappa
. Note that
K\in\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
is central and the Lie brackets in
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
are determined by
[xt^{i},yt^{j}]=[x,y]t^{i+j}+\delta_{i,-j}\kappa(x,y)iK
. We use the standard notation
xt^{i}:=x_{i}
which may denote either an element or an endomorphism of
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Let
\mathbbm{k}_{k}
be the one dimensional representation of
\mathfrak{g}[[t]]\oplus\mathbbm{k}K
upon which
\mathfrak{g}[[t]]
acts trivially and
K
acts by
k
. The vacuum module for
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
is the induced module

(3.8)

V^{k}(\mathfrak{g}):=U(\widehat{{\mathfrak{g}}})\otimes_{\mathfrak{g}[[t]]\oplus\mathbbm{k}K}\mathbbm{k}_{k},

and
k
is known as
the level
of
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
.

The vacuum module carries a canonical structure of modular vertex algebra, see
[
FB
,
Ka2
]
.
If we pick and ordered basis
\{x^{i}\mid i=1,...,\dim\mathfrak{g}\}
for
\mathfrak{g}
, then the Poincare–Birkhoff–Witt (PBW) theorem implies that we have a basis given by ordered monomials

(3.9)

x^{i_{1}}_{-n_{1}-1}\cdots x^{i_{m}}_{-n_{m}-1}{\boldsymbol{1}}

such that
0\leq n_{1}\leq n_{2}\leq\cdots\leq n_{m}
, and
i_{j}\leq i_{j+1}
whenever
n_{j}=n_{j+1}
. The vacuum vector in
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
is
{\boldsymbol{1}}=1\otimes 1
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
the translation operator
T^{(k)}
is determined by
T^{(k)}{\boldsymbol{1}}=0
and

(3.10)

\displaystyle T^{(k)}x^{i}_{-n-1}{\boldsymbol{1}}=\binom{n+k}{k}x^{i}_{-n-k-1}{\boldsymbol{1}}

whilst in general,
T^{(k)}
acts on
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
by

(3.11)

\displaystyle T^{(k)}(x^{i_{1}}_{n_{1}}\cdots x^{i_{m}}_{n_{m}}{\boldsymbol{1}})=\sum_{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{m}=m}T^{(j_{1})}(x^{i_{1}}_{n_{1}})\cdots T^{(j_{m})}(x^{a_{m}}_{n_{m}}){\boldsymbol{1}}\,.

The fields are defined as follows. We have
Y({\boldsymbol{1}},z)=\Id_{V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})}
and

Y(x^{i}_{-1}{\boldsymbol{1}},z)=x^{i}(z):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}x^{i}_{n}z^{-n-1},

whilst in general we have

(3.12)

Y(x^{i_{1}}_{-n_{1}-1}\cdots x^{i_{m}}_{-n_{m}-1}{\boldsymbol{1}},z)=:\partial_{z}^{(n_{1})}x^{i_{1}}(z)\cdots\partial_{z}^{(n_{m})}x^{i_{m}}(z):

where
:\ \ :
denotes the normally ordered product (see
[
Ka2
]
). The locality of the fields is a consequence of the explicit description of Lie brackets in
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
.

In this article we are interested in describing the centre
\mathfrak{z}(V^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}))
. Consider the space of
\mathfrak{g}[[t]]
invariants

V^{k}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\mathfrak{g}[[t]]}=\{b\in V^{\kappa}({\mathfrak{g}})\mid(x_{n})\/b=0\text{ for }n\geq 0\}\,.

The following useful property follows from (
3.12
); see
[
Fr
, Lemma 3.1.1]
for more detail.

Lemma 3.6
.

\mathfrak{z}V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})=V^{k}({\mathfrak{g}})^{{\mathfrak{g}}[[t]]}.

\hfill\qed

3.5.
The
p
-centre of modular affine vertex algebras

We introduce a central subalgebra of

, following
[

]
. The restricted structure on

gives rise to a natural restricted structure on
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}
determined by
(xt^{i})^{[p]}=x^{[p]}t^{pi}
and

. We denote the

Lemma 3.7

\mathfrak{z}_{p}V^{\kappa}({\mathfrak{g}})\subseteq\mathfrak{z}V^{\kappa}({\mathfrak{g}}).

\hfill\qed

3.6.
The current group and the centre

The
current group

G[[t]]
of
\mathbbm{k}[[t]]
-points of

is equal to the group of
\mathbbm{k}
-points of

, by definition. Let
\epsilon
be a formal variable satisfying
\epsilon^{2}=0
. The Lie algebra
\operatorname{Lie}G[[t]]
of

is defined to be the kernel of the homomorphism
G(\mathbbm{k}[[t]][\epsilon])\to G(\mathbbm{k}[[t]])
coming from the map
\mathbbm{k}[[t]][\epsilon]\to\mathbbm{k}[[t]]
by functoriality. By
[

, p. 192]
we have
\operatorname{Lie}G[[t]]=\mathfrak{g}[[t]]
. See also
[

]
.

Lemma 3.8
.

For any
k\in\mathbbm{k}
there is a natural rational
G[[t]]
-action on
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
such that the differential at the identity element is the left action of
\mathfrak{g}[[t]]
.

Proof.

The action of
G[[t]]
on
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\cong\mathfrak{g}((t))\oplus\mathbbm{k}K
is given by
g\cdot(A(t)+cK)=gA(t)g^{-1}+K\Res_{t}(g^{-1}dg,y)+Kc
where

is the logarithmic differential, see also
[

. This action lift to an action on

and on the vertex algebra through the surjective map

.

∎

The next result follows from Lemma
3.6
and Lemma

V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})^{G[[t]]}\subseteq\mathfrak{z}V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
.

4.
Invariants and Arc spaces

4.1.
The semiclassical limit of the affine vertex algebra

We equip
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
with the filtration described in (
3.7
). This filtration is ascending, exhaustive
V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})=\bigcup_{d\geq 0}F_{d}V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
, and connected
F_{0}V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbbm{k}{\boldsymbol{1}}
. Furthermore,
G[[t]]
,
\mathfrak{g}[[t]]
and
\{T^{(i)}\mid i\geq 0\}
all preserve the filtered pieces.

This gives rise to an associated graded space

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\gr V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})&=&\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}F_{d}V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})/F_{d-1}V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})\\
&=&S(\mathfrak{g}[t^{-1}]t^{-1})=\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]\end{array}

where the final identification follows from the remarks of Section
2.1
(see
[
Ar
, §3.8, (34)]
for more detail). There is an induced action of
G[[t]]
and
\mathfrak{g}[[t]]
on
\gr V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
. Via (
4.1
) these actions coincide with the natural ones on
S(\widehat{\mathfrak{g}})/(\mathfrak{g}_{+}^{0})
which arise from the adjoint action of
G[[t]]
on
\mathfrak{g}(\!(t)\!)/\mathfrak{g}[[t]]
, in the same manner as Lemma
3.8
. This is an isomorphism of
\mathfrak{g}[[t]]
-modules, and we will identify
\gr V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]
in the sequel.

Similarly the translation operator
T
gives rise to a higher derivation
\partial=(\partial^{(0)},\partial^{(1)},\partial^{(2)},...)
on
S(\mathfrak{g}[t^{-1}]t^{-1})
by the rule
\partial^{(i)}(v+F^{d-1}V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})):=T^{(i)}(v)+F^{d-1}V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})
for
i,d\geq 0
. More explicitly,
\partial^{(i)}
coincides with
(-1)^{i}\partial_{t}^{(i)}
where
\partial_{t}
is described in Section
3.1
.

Combining our remarks with Lemma
3.6
we have the inclusion

(4.3)

\displaystyle\gr\mathfrak{z}V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})\subseteq\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{\mathfrak{g}[[t]]}.

We also have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\gr\mathfrak{z}_{p}V^{k}(\mathfrak{g})=S(\mathfrak{g}[t^{-1}]t^{-1})^{p}.

By Theorem
2.3
we have a homogeneous invariant generating set
P_{1},...,P_{r}\in\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G}
. If we let
G[[t]]
act on
\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]
via the homomorphism
G[[t]]\twoheadrightarrow G
then the inclusion
\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]\subseteq\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]
given by
x\mapsto x_{-1}
is
G[[t]]
-equivariant. Therefore the elements
\{P_{i,-1}\mid i=1,...,r\}
we obtain are
G[[t]]
-invariant.

For
j>0
we define regular functions
P_{i,-j}\in\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}]
by

(4.5)

P_{i,-j}:=\partial^{(j-1)}P_{i,-1}.

We can define these regular functions on
J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
equivalently using fields. For
i=1,...,\dim\mathfrak{g}
we let
x^{i}(z)=\sum_{n<0}x^{i}_{n}z^{-n-1}
. The change of variables
x^{i}\mapsto x^{i}(z)
produces a map
\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]\mapsto\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}][z]
and the image of
P_{i}
under this map is the generating function for the series
(P_{i,-n-1})_{n\geq 0}
.

In the following lemma we write
x^{1},...,x^{n}
for a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
P\in\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]
consider
P_{-1}\in\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]
as above. Then

\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}_{-1-s}}\partial^{(m)}P_{-1}=\begin{cases}\partial^{(m-s)}\frac{\partial P_{-1}}{\partial x^{i}_{-1}},\qquad m\geq s\geq 0\,,\\
0\,,\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad m<s\,.\end{cases}

Proof.

It suffices to check the claim for monomials in
x^{1},...,x^{n}
. The case
m<s
is clear because
\partial^{(m)}P_{-1}
only depends on variables
x^{j}_{-1},...,x^{j}_{-1-m}
with
j=1,...,\dim\mathfrak{g}
.

We prove the statement for
m\geq s
by induction on the total degree of
P
. The degree 1 case is easily verified. Now suppose it has been verified for all
P
of degree less than
d
. Any monomial of degree greater than 1 can be written as a product
PQ
of monomials of degrees strictly less than
d
.

Using (
2.1
) have

\displaystyle\partial^{(m-s)}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}_{-1}}(P_{-1}Q_{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m-s}\big((\partial^{(j)}\frac{\partial P_{-1}}{\partial x^{i}_{-1}})(\partial^{(m-s-j)}Q_{-1})+(\partial^{(j)}P_{-1})(\partial^{(m-s-j)}\frac{\partial Q_{-1}}{\partial x^{i}_{-1}})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=s}^{m}(\partial^{(j-s)}\frac{\partial P_{-1}}{\partial x^{i}_{-1}})(\partial^{(m-j)}Q_{-1)})+\sum_{j=0}^{m-s}(\partial^{(j)}P_{-1})(\partial^{(m-s-j)}\frac{\partial Q_{-1}}{\partial x^{i}_{-1}}).

In the second line we shift the indexes for half of the summands.

On the other hand we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}_{-1-s}}\partial^{(m)}(P_{-1}Q_{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}\big((\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}_{-1-s}}\partial^{(j)}P_{-1})(\partial^{(m-j)}Q_{-1})+(\partial^{(j)}P_{-1})(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}_{-1-s}}\partial^{(m-j)}Q_{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=s}^{m}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}_{-1-s}}\partial^{(j)}P_{-1})(\partial^{(m-j)}Q_{-1})+\sum_{j=0}^{m-s}(\partial^{(j)}P_{-1})(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}_{-1-s}}\partial^{(m-j)}Q_{-1})

where the second line uses the vanishing statement of the lemma for
s<m
. Since the degree of both
P
and
Q
is less than the degree of
PQ
the proof concludes using the inductive hypothesis.
∎

Over the complex numbers the
G[[t]]
-invariance of the polynomials
P_{i,-j}
is equivalent to the
\mathfrak{g}[[t]]
-invariance. In our setting this is no longer the case, and so we check the invariance.

Lemma 4.2
.

P_{i,-j}\in\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G[[t]]}
.

Proof.

The coadjoint action
G\times\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is given by
\mu:\mathbbm{k}[{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}]\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}[{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}]\otimes\mathbbm{k}[G]
, we define
\iota:\mathbbm{k}[{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}]\rightarrow\mathbbm{k}[{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}]\otimes\mathbbm{k}[G]
by
\iota(f)=f\otimes 1
for
f\in\mathbbm{k}[{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}]
. Then,
\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G}=\Ker(\mu-\iota)
. Moreover,
\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{J_{\infty}G}=\Ker(J_{\infty}\mu-J_{\infty}\iota)
and
P_{i,-j-1}=\partial^{(j)}P_{i,-1}
then
(J_{\infty}\mu-J_{\infty}\iota)P_{i,-j-1}=\partial^{(j)}(J_{\infty}\mu-J_{\infty}\iota)P_{i,-1}=0
. Note that
J_{\infty}\mu
and
J_{\infty}\iota
commute with
\partial
since
J_{\infty}
is a functor to differential algebras.

∎

We provide one example to show that the invariance of
P_{i,-j}
can be derived directly in special cases.

Example 4.3
.

Let
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n+1}
so that

G[[t]]=\{g\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n+1}(\mathbbm{k}[[t]]))\mid\det(g)=1\}

Let
\{x^{i,j}\mid 1\leq i,j\leq n+1\}
be the standard basis for
\operatorname{Mat}_{n+1}(\mathbbm{k})
. Define a matrix
X\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n+1}(\mathbbm{k}[t^{-1}]t^{-1}[[z]])
whose
i,j
entry is the series
x^{i,j}(z)=\sum_{m<0}x^{i,j}_{m}z^{-1-m}
. Write

\det(\lambda-X)=\lambda^{n}+\sum_{i=0}^{n}P_{i}(z)\lambda^{i}

We identify
\operatorname{Mat}_{n+1}(\mathbbm{k}[t^{-1}]t^{-1})
with
\Hom_{\mathbbm{k}}(\operatorname{Mat}_{n+1}(\mathbbm{k}[[t]]),\mathbbm{k})
via the residue pairing. Then the
z^{j}
coefficient of the series
P_{i}(z)
restricts to
P_{i,-1-j}
on
J_{\infty}\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}\subseteq\operatorname{Mat}_{n+1}(\mathbbm{k}[[t]])
.

The group
G[[t]]
acts on
\operatorname{Mat}_{n+1}(\mathbbm{k}[t^{-1}]t^{-1})=J_{\infty}\mathfrak{gl}_{n+1}^{*}
and the invariance of the
P_{i,-j}
is now a simple consequence of the fact that the characteristic polynomial is
G[[t]]
-invariant.

Finally, we prove the main theorem of this section, which is a vertex algebra analogue of Theorem
2.3
(1)-(4).

Theorem 4.4
.

(1)

\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G[t]]}=\mathbbm{k}[P_{i,-j}\mid i=1,..,r,\ j<0]
polynomial ring on infinitely many generators.

(2)

\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{\mathfrak{g}[[t]]}
is generated by
\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G[[t]]}
and
\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{p}
.

(3)

\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{\mathfrak{g}[[t]]}
is a free
\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{p}
-module with basis

(4.6)

\displaystyle\Big\{\prod_{i,j}P_{i,-j}^{k_{i,j}}\mid 0\leq k_{i,j}<p,\text{ finitely many nonzero}\Big\}.

(4)

\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{\mathfrak{g}[[t]]}
is isomorphic to the tensor product of
\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{p}
and
\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G[[t]]}
over the intersection.

Proof.

In the proof we make use of the group of
m
-jets
J_{m}G
which has Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{m}:=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathbbm{k}[t]/(t^{m}+1)
and
\mathbbm{k}
-points
G_{m}
, known as the truncated current group.

The map
\mathfrak{g}^{*}[[t]]\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{m}^{*}
gives rise to an inclusion
\mathbbm{k}[\mathfrak{g}_{m}^{*}]\to\mathbbm{k}[J_{\infty}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]
. This subalgebra is a
G[[t]]
-submodule and the action factors through the homomorphism
G[[t]]\twoheadrightarrow G_{m}
arising from the inclusion of Hopf algebras
J_{m}\mathbbm{k}[G]\hookrightarrow J_{\infty}\mathbbm{k}[G]
(see (
2.1
)). Now in order to prove the theorem it suffices to prove the corresponding claims about
\mathbbm{k}[J_{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G_{m}}
and
\mathbbm{k}[J_{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{\mathfrak{g}_{m}}
, mutatis mutandis.

In order to prove (2) and (3), we apply a result of Skryabin
[
Sk
, Theorem 5.4]
. To satisfy the conditions of his theorem we must demonstrate that:

i)

\ind({\mathfrak{g}}_{m})=(m+1)\rank(\mathfrak{g})

ii)

\operatorname{codim}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{m}^{*}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{m}^{*}\setminus J(P_{i,-j}))\geq 2

where
\ind(\mathfrak{g}_{m}):=\min_{\chi\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}}\dim\mathfrak{g}_{m}^{\chi}
is the index of the Lie algebra, and
J(P_{i,-j})
denotes the Jacobian locus of the
P_{i,-j}
, i.e. the open set of
\mathfrak{g}_{m}^{*}
consisting of points
\chi
such that the functions
d_{\chi}P_{i,-j}
are linearly independent.

Part (i) is a special case of a theorem of Raïs–Tauvel (where they work over the complex numbers) and we include a short proof valid in our setting. Pick a triangular decomposition
\mathfrak{g}={\mathfrak{n}}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
, which gives
\mathfrak{g}_{m}={\mathfrak{n}}^{-}_{m}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{m}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}_{m}^{+}
. Let
\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\circ}=\{\sum_{i=0}^{m}h_{i}t^{i}\mid h_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}^{\reg}\}
.

The differential of the adjoint action
G_{m}\times\mathfrak{h}_{m}\to\mathfrak{g}_{m}
at a point
(1,h)
is the linear map
\mathfrak{g}_{m}\times\mathfrak{h}_{m}\to\mathfrak{g}_{m}
given by
x,z\mapsto[x,h]+z
. When
h\in\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\circ}
we have that

(4.7)

\displaystyle[h,\mathfrak{g}_{m}]={\mathfrak{n}}_{m}^{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}_{m}^{+}

and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{m}^{h}=\mathfrak{h}_{m}\,,

in more detail,
[h_{0},\mathfrak{n}^{\pm}]=\mathfrak{n}^{\pm}
then
[h,\mathfrak{n}_{m}^{\pm}]=\mathfrak{n}_{m}^{\pm}
and easily
[h,\mathfrak{h}_{m}]=0
.
And so the differential is surjective for such a point. Consequently the conjugates of
\mathfrak{h}_{m}^{\circ}
are dense in
\mathfrak{g}_{m}
. On the other hand
\dim\mathfrak{g}_{m}^{h}=(m+1)\rank(\mathfrak{g})
by (
4.7
), and so by upper-semicontuinuity of dimensions of fibres of morphisms, we have that
\min_{x\in\mathfrak{g}_{m}}\dim\mathfrak{g}_{m}^{x}=(m+1)\rank(\mathfrak{g})
.

There is a
\mathfrak{g}_{m}
-invariant bilinear form on
\mathfrak{g}_{m}
given by
xt^{i},yt^{j}\mapsto\delta_{i,m+1-j}\kappa(x,y)
, and this induces an isomorphism
\theta:\mathfrak{g}_{m}\to\mathfrak{g}_{m}^{*}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{m}
-modules. We deduce that
\ind(\mathfrak{g}_{m})=(m+1)\rank(\mathfrak{g})
, which proves (i).

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{m}^{\circ}=\{\sum_{i=0}^{m}x_{i}t^{i}\mid x_{0}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\reg}\}
. Thanks to
[
BG
, Proposition 3.2]
we have
\operatorname{codim}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{g}\setminus\mathfrak{g}^{\circ})=3
hence we have
\operatorname{codim}_{\mathfrak{g}_{m}}(\mathfrak{g}_{m}\setminus\mathfrak{g}_{m}^{\circ})=3
. We shall show that
\theta(\mathfrak{g}_{m}^{\circ})\subseteq J(P_{i,-j})
, which shall prove (ii).

Once again we write
x^{1},...,x^{n}
for a basis of linear functions on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, which gives a basis
\{x^{i}_{-j}\mid i=1,...,r,\ j=1,...,m+1\}
of linear functions on
J_{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Consider the Jacobian of the functions
\{P_{i,-j}\mid 1\leq i\leq r,\ 1\leq j\leq m+1\}
, a matrix of size
(m+1)\dim\mathfrak{g}\times(m+1)\rank\mathfrak{g}
. It has block form

(4.8)

\left(\begin{matrix}\frac{\partial P_{i,-1}}{\partial x^{j}_{-1}}&&&0\\
\frac{\partial P_{i,-2}}{\partial x^{j}_{-1}}&\frac{\partial P_{i,-2}}{\partial x^{j}_{-2}}&\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\\
\frac{\partial P_{i,-1-m}}{\partial x^{j}_{-1}}&\frac{\partial P_{i,-1-m}}{\partial x^{j}_{-2}}&\dots&\frac{\partial P_{i,-m-1}}{\partial x^{j}_{-m-1}}\end{matrix}\right)

where each block has size
(\dim\mathfrak{g})\times(\rank\mathfrak{g})
. Using Lemma
4.1
we see that the diagonal blocks are all equal to
(\frac{\partial P_{i,-1}}{{\partial x^{j}_{-1}}})
. Evaluating at any point of
\mathfrak{g}_{m}^{\circ}
we see that this matrix has full rank, thanks to the differential criterion for regularity of
\mathfrak{g}
(see Lemma 3.4 and Proposition 3.4 of
[
BG
]
). We have now shown that
\theta(\mathfrak{g}_{m}^{\circ})\subseteq J(P_{i,-j})
, which completes the proof of (ii), and part (2) and (3) of the current theorem.

For part (1) it suffices to show that
\mathbbm{k}[J_{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G_{m}}=\mathbbm{k}[P_{i,-j}\mid i=1,...,r,\ -m-1\leq-j<0]
is a polynomial ring. Suppose that
f\in\mathbbm{k}[J_{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G_{m}}
. Then by part (1) we can write
f
uniquely as
f=\sum_{b\in B}g_{b}b
where
B
denotes the collection of monomials described in (
4.6
) (with all
j\leq m+1
) and
g_{b}\in\mathbbm{k}[J_{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{p}
. By uniqueness, and using Lemma
4.2
, we see that each
g_{b}
is
G_{m}
-invariant. Since
\mathbbm{k}[J_{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]
has no nilpotent elements it follows that
g_{b}=(\bar{g}_{b})^{p}
for some
\bar{g}_{b}\in\mathbbm{k}[J_{m}\mathfrak{g}^{*}]^{G_{m}}
. Now part (1) may be proven by a downward induction on total degree.

Part (4) follows from
[
GT
, Lemma 2.1]
, whose proof does not require the free basis to be finite.
∎

5.2.
A criterion for the existence of Segal–Sugawara vectors

Recall that
R
is the subring of
\mathbb{Q}

\{1/n\mid 1\leq n\leq h\}
, and now let

be a finitely generated
R

Proposition 5.1

Suppose that there is a non-zero homomorphism

(5.5)

\displaystyle\gr S_{i,-1}=P_{i,-1}

for

(2)

\mathfrak{z}V_{\mathbbm{k}}

\mathfrak{z}_{p}V_{\mathbbm{k}}

(3)

\mathfrak{z}V_{\mathbbm{k}}

\mathfrak{z}_{p}V_{\mathbbm{k}}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\Big\{\prod_{i,j}S_{i,-j}^{k_{i,j}}\mid 0\leq k_{i,j}<p,\text{ finitely many nonzero}\Big\}.

(4)

\mathfrak{z}V_{\mathbbm{k}}
is isomorphic to the tensor product of
\mathfrak{z}_{p}V_{\mathbbm{k}}
and

Proof.

Recall Lemma
3.6

It is clear that

is a vertex

\gr S_{i,-j}=P_{i,-j}

. Since

Now let

\mathbbm{k}

2.2

is generated by
\{J_{\infty}u_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi\}
. The group of

is isomorphic to the additive group

. For each

and

we consider the morphism of schemes

We now fix

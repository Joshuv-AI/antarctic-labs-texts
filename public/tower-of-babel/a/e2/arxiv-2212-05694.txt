Abstract
The complete elliptic integral of the first kind (CEI-1) plays a significant role in mathematics, physics and engineering. There is no simple formula for its computation, thus numerical algorithms are essential for coping with the practical problems involved. The commercial implementations for the numerical solutions, such as the functions ellipticK and EllipticK provided by MATLAB and Mathematica respectively, are based on 𝒦 cs ​ ( m ) {\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{cs}}(m) instead of the usual form K ⁡ ( k ) K(k) such that 𝒦 cs ​ ( k 2 ) = K ⁡ ( k ) {\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{cs}}(k^{2})=K(k) and m = k 2 m=k^{2} . It is necessary to develop open source implementations for the computation of the CEI-1 in order to avoid potential risks of using commercial software and possible limitations due to the unknown factors. In this paper, the infinite series method, arithmetic-geometric mean (AGM) method, Gauss-Chebyshev method and Gauss-Legendre methods are discussed in details with a top-down strategy. The four key algorithms for computing CEI-1 are designed, verified, validated and tested, which can be utilized in R& D and be reused properly. Numerical results show that our open source implementations based on K ⁡ ( k ) K(k) are equivalent to the commercial implementation based on 𝒦 cs ​ ( m ) {\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{cs}}(m) . The general algorithms for computing orthogonal polynomials developed are significant byproducts in the sense of STEM education and scientific computation. Keywords : Complete elliptic integral of the first kind (CEI-1); STEM education; Algorithm design; Orthogonal polynomials; Verification-Validation-Testing (VVT); Infinte series; Arithmetic-geometric mean (AGM); Gauss numerical integration

•

The Gauss numerical method can be expressed by

Mathematical Preliminaries

2.1
Infinite Series Method

The first method for computing the CEI-1 is to take the series expansion of variable
k
. Actually, with the help of the generalized Newton’s binomial theorem, we have
[
5
]

(1+z)^{\alpha}=\sum^{\infty}_{j=0}\binom{\alpha}{j}z^{j},\quad\left|z\right|\leq 1,\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{R}

(6)

where

\binom{\alpha}{0}=1

(7)

and

\binom{\alpha}{j}=\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-j+1)}{j!}=\prod^{j-1}_{t=0}\frac{\alpha-t}{j-t}

(8)

is the binomial coefficient.
Put
z=-k^{2}\sin^{2}u
and
\alpha=-\frac{1}{2}
, we can obtain

\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-k^{2}\sin^{2}u}}=\sum^{\infty}_{j=0}(-1)^{j}\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{j}k^{2j}\sin^{2j}u.

(9)

According to the identity

(-1)^{j}\binom{-r}{j}=\binom{r+j-1}{j},\quad r\in\mathbb{R},j\in\mathbb{N}

(10)

and integral formula

\int^{\pi/2}_{0}\sin^{2j}u\dif u=\frac{(2j-1)!!}{(2j)!!}\cdot\frac{\pi}{2},\quad j\in\mathbb{N}

(11)

where

\left\{\begin{split}&(2j-1)!!=(2j-1)(2j-3)\cdots 3\cdot 1\\
&(2j)!!=(2j)(2j-2)\cdots 4\cdot 2\\
&0!!=(-1)!!=1\end{split}\right.

(12)

are the fundamental results for the double factorial. Therefore, we can find the infinite series for the CEI-1, viz.,

K(k)=\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\sum^{\infty}_{j=0}\left[\frac{(2j-1)!!}{(2j)!!}\right]^{2}k^{2j}

(13)

Simple algebraic operations show that

K(k)=\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\left[1+\frac{1}{4}k^{2}+\frac{9}{64}k^{4}+\frac{25}{256}k^{6}+\cdots\right]

(14)

Let

\quad c_{j}=\left[\frac{(2j-1)!!}{(2j)!!}\right]^{2},\quad j\in\mathbb{N},

(15)

then it is easy to find that

\left\{\begin{aligned} K(k)&=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum^{\infty}_{j=0}c_{j}k^{2j}\\
{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{cs}}(m)&=\frac{\pi}{2}\sum^{\infty}_{j=0}c_{j}m^{j}\end{aligned}\right.

(16)

according to (
3
) and (
16
).

From a computational point of view, we just need to calculate the following convergent infinite series

S(x)=\sum^{\infty}_{j=0}c_{j}x^{j},\quad x\in[0,1].

(17)

Therefore, the key for determining CEI-1 lies in designing an algorithm for computing the infinite series (
17
) for
x=m
or
x=k^{2}
and designing a procedure to calculate the coefficient
c_{j}
specified by (
15
).

2.2
AGM Method

The second method for calculating the CEI-1 is the AGM method proposed by King
[
6
]
. The AGM method was first discovered by Lagrange and then rediscovered and pushed forward greatly by Gauss. The relation of AGM method and CEI-1 was also discussed by Borwein
[
7
]
for the purpose of calculating the constant
\pi
. For the 2-dim sequence
\left\{(u_{i},v_{i})\right\}^{\infty}_{j=0}

such that

\left\{\begin{split}u_{i+1}&=\frac{u_{i}+v_{i}}{2},\quad i\geq 0\\
v_{i+1}&=\sqrt{u_{i}\cdot v_{i}},\quad i\geq 0\\
u_{0}&=a\geq 0\\
v_{0}&=b\geq 0\end{split}\right.

(18)

it is easy to show that: both the limit of arithmetic mean
u_{i}
and the limit of geometric mean
v_{i}
exist, and the limits for
u_{i}
and
v_{i}
are equal. Obviously, the common limit just depends on the initial non-negative values
a
and
b
. This limit specified by the arithmetic-geometric mean (AGM) can be denoted by

\AGM(a,b)=\lim_{i\to\infty}u_{i}=\lim_{i\to\infty}v_{i}.

(19)

The updating relation in equation (
18
) implies that both the symmetric property

\AGM(a,b)=\AGM(b,a)

(20)

and the iterative property

\AGM(a,b)=\AGM\left(\frac{a+b}{2},\sqrt{ab}\right)

(21)

hold obviously.

There are lots of formula about the AGM and the CEI-1, we just list the following necessary ones according to our requirements:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\AGM(1+x,1-x)}

\displaystyle=\sum^{\infty}_{j=0}\left[\frac{(2j-1)!!}{(2j)!!}\right]^{2}x^{2j}

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\AGM(a,b)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\pi}\int^{\frac{\pi}{2}}_{0}\frac{\dif\theta}{\sqrt{a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta+b^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}}

(23)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{(a+b)\pi}K\left(\left|\frac{a-b}{a+b}\right|\right)

(24)

For
k\in[0,1],a=1+k
and
b=1-k
, we can deduce that

K(k)=\frac{\pi/2}{\AGM(1+k,1-k)}

(25)

from (
22
). Consequently, we can obtain

\AGM(1+k,1-k)=\AGM(1,\sqrt{1-k^{2}})

(26)

by the iterative property (
21
). Hence we have

K(k)=\frac{\pi/2}{\AGM(1,\sqrt{1-k^{2}})}

(27)

This is the famous AGM method proposed by King in 1924.
Thus, in the sense of computation, the calculation of CEI-1 is equivalent to computing the function
\AGM(a,b)
via iterative algorithm and calling it with 2-dim parameter
(a,b)=(1,\sqrt{1-k^{2}})
or equivalently
(a,b)=(1+k,1-k)
. Moreover, the symmetric property of AGM says that the position of
a
and
b
in
\AGM(a,b)
can be swapped without changing the value.

2.3
Gauss-Chebyshev Method

According to the Appendix
A.1
, for the abstract function

{F}_{\mathrm{C}}(k)=\int^{a}_{-a}\frac{f(x,k)}{\sqrt{a^{2}-x^{2}}}\dif x=\int^{1}_{-1}\frac{f(at,k)}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}\dif t,

(28)

of the Chebyshev type defined by the integral of
\displaystyle{\frac{f(x,k)}{\sqrt{a^{2}-x^{2}}}}
with parameter
k
, we can computed
it by the extended Gauss-Chebyshev formula of numerical integration. Let

{x}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{i}
denote the
i
-th root of
T_{n}(x)=\cos(n\arccos x)
, i.e. the
n
-th order Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind, and
{w}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{i}
denote the
i
-th weight corresponding to
{x}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{i}
, then we have

{x}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{i}=\cos\frac{(2i-1)\pi}{2n},\quad{w}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{i}=\frac{\pi}{n},\quad 1\leq i\leq n

(29)

and

{F}_{\mathrm{C}}(k)=\sum^{n}_{i=1}{w}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{i}f(a{x}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{i},k)+{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{n}(f,a,k).

(30)

where

{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{n}(f,a,k)=\frac{2\pi\cdot a^{2n}}{2^{2n}(2n)!}f^{(2n)}(\xi,k),\quad\xi\in(-a,a)

(31)

is the approximation error.

We now introduce three
n
-dim vectors

\left\{\begin{split}&{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{gc}}={[{x}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{1},\cdots,{x}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{n}]}^{\textsf{T}},\\
&{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{gc}}={[{w}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{1},\cdots,{w}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{n}]}^{\textsf{T}},\\
&{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{f}(k,a)={[f(a{x}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{1},k),\cdots,f(a{x}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{n},k)]}^{\textsf{T}}\end{split}\right.

(32)

then CEI-1 can be represented by the inner product of
{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{gc}}
and
{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{f}(k)
, viz.,

{F}_{\mathrm{C}}(k)=\langle{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{gc}}|{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{f}(k,a)\rangle+{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{n}(f,a,k)

(33)

in which the inner product is defined by

\langle\mathbf{w}|\mathbf{v}\rangle=\sum^{n}_{i=1}w_{i}v_{i},\quad\forall\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{{n}\times{1}}.

(34)

Our objective here is to give a computational method for CEI-1 by establishing the relation of CEI-1 and
{F}_{\mathrm{C}}(k)
. Let
t=\sin u
, then we have

\begin{split}K(k)&=\int^{\pi/2}_{0}\frac{\dif t}{\sqrt{1-k^{2}\sin^{2}u}}=\int^{1}_{0}\frac{\dif t}{\sqrt{(1-t^{2})(1-k^{2}t^{2})}}\\
&=\int^{1}_{-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}}}\cdot\frac{1/2}{\sqrt{1-k^{2}t^{2}}}\dif t\end{split}

(35)

Now put
a=1
and

{f}_{\mathrm{gc}}(t,k)=\frac{1/2}{\sqrt{1-k^{2}t^{2}}},\quad t\in(-1,1),k\in[0,\sqrt{2}/2)

(36)

then

\begin{split}K(k)&=\sum^{n}_{i=1}{w}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{i}{f}_{\mathrm{gc}}({x}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{i},k)+{R}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{n}({f}_{\mathrm{gc}})\\
&=\langle{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{gc}}|{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{{f}_{\mathrm{gc}}}({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{gc}},k,1)\rangle+{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{n}({f}_{\mathrm{gc}},1,k)\end{split}

(37)

Therefore, for any
\varepsilon>0
, the CEI-1 can be computed directly with the inner product of vectors
{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{gc}}
and
{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{{f}_{\mathrm{gc}}}(k,1)
if we take a sufficiently large
n
such that
\left|{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{n}({f}_{\mathrm{gc}},1,k)\right|<\varepsilon
for approximating the integral with fixed parameter
k
.

2.4
Gauss-Legendre Method

According to the Appendix
A.2
, the abstract function

{F}_{\mathrm{L}}(k)=\int^{b}_{a}f(x,k)\dif x=\frac{b-a}{2}\int^{1}_{-1}f\left(\frac{b+a}{2}+\frac{b-a}{2}t\right)\dif t

(38)

defined by the integral of
f(x,k)
with parameter
k
such that
f(\cdot,k)\in C^{2n}[a,b]
can be computed by the extended Gauss-Legendre formula of numerical integration. Let
{x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i}
denote the
i
-th root of the
n
-th order Legendre polynomial
P_{n}(x)
and

{w}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i}
denote the
i
-th weight corresponding to
{x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i}
such that

{w}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i}=\frac{2}{n}\cdot\frac{1}{P_{n-1}({x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i})P^{\prime}_{n}({x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i})}=\frac{2}{[1-({x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i})^{2}]\cdot[P^{\prime}_{n}({x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i})]^{2}}

(39)

where
P^{\prime}_{n}(x)
is the derivative function of
P_{n}(x)
, then we have

\begin{split}{F}_{\mathrm{L}}(k)&=\frac{b-a}{2}\sum^{n}_{i=1}{w}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i}f\left(\frac{b+a+(b-a){x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i}}{2},k\right)\\
&\quad+{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{n}(f,a,b,k)\end{split}

(40)

where

{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{n}(f,a,b,k)=\frac{(n!)^{4}}{[(2n)!]^{3}}\cdot\frac{(b-a)^{2n+1}}{2n+1}f^{(2n)}(\xi,k)

(41)

is the approximation error in which
\xi\in(a,b)
.

We remark that there are no analytical solutions for the roots of
P_{n}(x)=0
if
n\geq 4
. Fortunately, it can be solved by the Newton’s method for nonlinear equation. For small
n
, say
n\leq 20
, we can look up some available handbooks of mathematics to get the roots
\left\{{x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\right\}
. However, this method is not suggested if the
n
is unknown or not sure. In this case, a feasible, convenient and effective way is to calculate the roots of
P_{n}(x)
automatically with proper algorithms implemented via some concrete computer programming language and programs.

Let

\left\{\begin{split}&{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{gl}}={\left[{x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{1},\cdots,{x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{n}\right]}^{\textsf{T}}\\
&{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{gl}}={\left[{w}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{1},\cdots,{w}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{n}\right]}^{\textsf{T}}\\
&{u}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i}=\frac{b+a+(b-a){x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i}}{2},\quad 1\leq i\leq n\\
&{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{f}({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{gl}},k,a,b)=\frac{b-a}{2}{\left[f({u}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i},k),\cdots,f({u}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{n})\right]}^{\textsf{T}}\end{split}\right.

(42)

then
{F}_{\mathrm{L}}(k)
can be represented by the inner product of
{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{gl}}
and
{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{f}({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{gl}},k,a,b)
, viz.,

{F}_{\mathrm{L}}(k)=\langle{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{gl}}|{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{f}({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{gl}},k,a,b)\rangle+{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{n}(f,a,b,k)

(43)

Suppose that the root-weight pairs
({x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i},{w}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq n
were known, we can establish the relation of
{F}_{\mathrm{L}}(k)
and CEI-1. For
k\in[0,1)
, let
a=0
,
b=\pi/2
, and

{f}_{\mathrm{gl}}(u,k)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-k^{2}\sin^{2}u}}

(44)

then

\begin{split}K(k)&=\frac{\pi}{4}\sum^{n}_{i=1}{w}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i}{f}_{\mathrm{gl}}\left(\frac{\pi}{4}(1+{x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i}),k\right)+{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{n}\left({f}_{\mathrm{gl}},0,\frac{\pi}{2},k\right)\\
&=\langle{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{gl}}|{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{{f}_{\mathrm{gl}}}({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{gl}},k,0,\frac{\pi}{2})\rangle+{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{n}\left({f}_{\mathrm{gl}},0,\frac{\pi}{2},k\right)\end{split}

(45)

For any positive
\varepsilon>0
and fixed parameter
k\in[0,1)
, we can choose a sufficiently large integer
n
such that the approximation error is small enough, i.e.,

\left|{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{n}\left({f}_{\mathrm{gl}},0,\frac{\pi}{2},k\right)\right|<\varepsilon.

The expression above for CEI-1 means that the essential work left for calculating
K(k)
is to find the root-weight pairs for the Legendre polynomial
P_{n}(x)
.

Although there are several equivalent definitions for the Legendre polynomial, the best way for computing the Legendre polynomial
P_{n}(x)
is the following iterative formula

\left\{\begin{split}P_{n}(x)&=\frac{2n-1}{n}xP_{n-1}(x)-\frac{n-1}{n}P_{n-2}(x),~n\geq 2\\
P_{0}(x)&=1;\\
P_{1}(x)&=x.\end{split}\right.

(46)

This formula implies that we can compute
P_{n}(x)
one by one with an iterative process.

The Newton’s method for solving root of a nonlinear equation
f(x,\alpha)=0
with a constant parameter
\alpha
is specified by the iterative process with the form

\begin{split}x[j+1]&={\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{Newton}}(x[j])\\
&=x[j]-\frac{f(x[j],\alpha)}{f^{\prime}(x[j],\alpha)},\quad j=0,1,2,\cdots\end{split}

(47)

which starts from the initial value (guess of the root)
x[0]=x_{0}
and ends when

\left|x[j+1]-x[j]\right|<\varepsilon

(48)

for sufficiently large
j
and fixed computational error
\varepsilon
. The updating mapping
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{Newton}}:x[j]\mapsto x[j+1]
is a contractive mapping defined by

\begin{split}{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{Newton}}:\mathbb{R}&\to\mathbb{R}\\
x&\mapsto x-\frac{f(x,\alpha)}{f^{\prime}(x,\alpha)}\end{split}

(49)

For the Legendre polynomial
P_{n}(x)
, there are
n
different roots
{x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i}
where
i\in\left\{1,2,\cdots,n\right\}
. The iterative process can be described by

\left\{\begin{split}{x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i}[j+1]&={x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i}[j]-\frac{P_{n}({x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i}[j])}{P^{\prime}_{n}({x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i}[j])},\quad j=0,1,2\cdots\\
{x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{i}[0]&=\cos\frac{\pi(i+0.75)}{n+0.25},\quad 1\leq i\leq n.\end{split}\right.

(50)

This iterative process is the basis for designing algorithm for solving the roots of interest. Note that we can set
f(x,\alpha)=P_{n}(x)
where
\alpha=n
here.

3
Numerical Algorithms

Different numerical algorithms can be obtained according to the four methods discussed to compute the CEI-1 efficiently, robustly and precisely. In order to broaden the applications of the algorithms, we will generalize some specific constants to corresponding variables.

3.1
Infinite Series Algorithm

The equation (
13
) and its connection with the general infinite series show that there are some key issues for computing CEI-1:

•

designing an algorithm to compute the general infinite series
S(x)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}c_{j}x^{j}
which consists of four sub-tasks:

–

calculating the coefficient
c_{j}
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

–

computing the value of the general term
c_{j}x^{j}
for fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

–

deciding whether the general term
c_{j}x^{j}
is negligible or not for the sum of the infinite series, and

–

calculating the sum of the not-negligible terms;

•

specifying the parameters involved

–

designing a procedure to compute the coefficient
c_{j}
in the infinite series of CEI-1, and

–

specifying the variable
x
according to its connection with the variable
k
, i.e.,
x=k^{2}
.

•

calling the procedure for the infinite series and multiplying some constant if necessary.

There is a practical technique for computing
c_{j}
. The double factorials
(2j)!!
and
(2j-1)!!
may be large numbers if
j>10
and the overflow problem may happen in some computer language if the type of data is integer. However, we can cope with the the value of the fraction safely instead of dealing with its denominator and numerator separately. Actually, we have

\frac{(2j-1)!!}{(2j)!!}=\prod^{j-1}_{t=0}\frac{2(j-t)-1}{2(j-t)}=\prod^{j}_{t=1}\left[1-\frac{0.5}{t}\right],\quad t\geq 1.

(51)

Thus the fraction
\frac{(2j-1)!!}{(2j)!!}
can be computed by multiplying
1-0.5/t
one by one with a simple iteration, which not only reduces the computational complexity but also avoid the overflow problem of the factorial operations.

3.4
Gauss-Legendre Algorithm

The key idea of the Gauss-Legendre method is the same as that of the Gauss-Chebyshev. However, it is more complex due to the following reasons:

•

it is necessary for us to compute the Legendre polynomials
P_{n}(x)
and their derivatives
P^{\prime}_{n}(x)
automatically;

•

the root vector
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{gl}}={\left[{x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{1},\cdots,{x}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{n}\right]}^{\textsf{T}}
of
P_{n}(x)
must be solved iteratively with the Newton’s method;

•

the weight vector
{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{gl}}={\left[{w}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{1},\cdots,{w}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{n}\right]}^{\textsf{T}}
must be solved with the help of
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{gl}}
,
P_{n}(x)
and
P^{\prime}_{n}(x)
;

•

the Newton’s method for solving roots of nonlinear equation should be designed.

The strategy for design algorithm for CEI-1 with Gauss-Legendre method is still top-down. In other words, we first design an algorithm to the general problem of computing the integral
\displaystyle{\int^{b}_{a}f(x,k)\dif x}=\langle{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{gl}}|{\mathbf{v}}^{\mathrm{gl}}_{f}({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{gc}},k,a,b)\rangle+\mathcal{E}_{n}(f)
with
inner product, then we calculate the vectors
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{gl}}
and
{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{gl}}
with orthogonal polynomials and Newton’s method for solving roots.

There is also an alternative way to get the root vector
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{gl}}
and
{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathrm{gl}}
by reading some handbooks about special functions and/or numerical analysis. However, the tables obtained are just for some fixed significant digits and fixed order
n
such that
n\leq 20
for
P_{n}(x)
. It is necessary for us to point out that a smart way born in 1900s is not always a wise way in 2020s. Moreover, the tables given in references can be used to check whether the output of the implementation of our algorithms is correct or not. It is a good idea that we respect the tradition, stand on the tradition, but also try to go beyond the tradition.

The complete design for the algorithm of Gauss-Legendre for CEI-1 can be divided into four sub-tasks. We now discuss them one-by-one with a strategy of top-down.
Figure

3.8
Generating the Legendre polynomials and their derivatives

The general form of four typical different orthogonal polynomials (such as Legendre polynomials
P_{n}(x)
, Chebyshev polynomials
T_{n}(x)
, Laguerre polynomials
L_{n}(x)
and Hermite polynomials
H_{n}(x)
), denoted by
Y_{n}(x)
, can be specified by the following iterative formula

\left\{\begin{split}Y_{n}(x)&=(A_{n}x+B_{n})Y_{n-1}(x)-C_{n}Y_{n-2}(x),\quad n\geq 2\\
Y_{0}(x)&=c_{0}\\
Y_{1}(x)&=c_{1}x+c_{2}\end{split}\right.

(57)

where
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}
are constants,
A_{n},B_{n},C_{n}
are functions depending on
n
.
Taking the derivative on the two sides, we immediately have

\left\{\begin{split}Y^{\prime}_{n}(x)&=A_{n}Y_{n-1}(x)+(A_{n}x+B_{n})Y^{\prime}_{n-1}(x)-C_{n}Y^{\prime}_{n-2}(x)\\
Y^{\prime}_{0}(x)&=0\\
Y^{\prime}_{1}(x)&=c_{1}\end{split}\right.

(58)

where
n\geq 2
.
Particularly, let

\left\{\begin{split}{A}^{\mathrm{le}}_{n}&=\frac{2n-1}{n},{B}^{\mathrm{le}}_{n}=0,{C}^{\mathrm{le}}_{n}=\frac{n-1}{n}\\
{c}^{\mathrm{le}}_{0}&=1,{c}^{\mathrm{le}}_{1}=1,{c}^{\mathrm{le}}_{2}=0\end{split}\right.

(59)

then for
n\geq 2
we can deduce that

\left\{\begin{split}P_{n}(x)&={A}^{\mathrm{le}}_{n}xP_{n-1}(x)-{C}^{\mathrm{le}}_{n}P_{n-2}(x)\\
P_{0}(x)&=1\\
P_{1}(x)&=x\end{split}\right.

(60)

and

\left\{\begin{split}P^{\prime}_{n}(x)&={A}^{\mathrm{le}}_{n}P_{n-1}(x)+{A}^{\mathrm{le}}_{n}xP^{\prime}_{n-1}(x)-{C}^{\mathrm{le}}_{n}P^{\prime}_{n-2}(x)\\
P^{\prime}_{0}(x)&=0\\
P^{\prime}_{1}(x)&=1\end{split}\right.

(61)

This implies that we can compute the
n
-th order Legendre polynomial
P_{n}(x)
and its derivative
P^{\prime}_{n}(x)
iteratively with a strategy of top-down:

•

firstly, we design general procedures
OrthoPolynom
in
Algorithm

3.8
and
DerivOrthoPolynom
in
Algorithm

3.8
for generating
Y_{n}(x)
and
Y^{\prime}_{n}(x)
respectively with the iterative formulas directly;

•

secondly, we design specific procedures —
AnLegendre
in
Algorithm

3.8
,
BnLegendre
in
Algorithm

3.8
and
CnLegendre
in
Algorithm

3.8
— to calculate
A(n),B(n)
and
C(n)
respectively and use pointer to functions to compute the Legendre polynomials
P_{n}(x)
and
P^{\prime}_{n}(x)
.

The procedures involved in computing the Legendre polynomial

5
Conclusions

The numerical schemes for computing the CEI-1 is essential for its applications. The available numerical solutions obtained by MATLAB and Mathematica are different due to the different of definitions for
K(k)
and
{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathrm{cs}}(m)
, which implies that we should pay attention to the verification, validation and testing stage in research, development and STEM education in order to avoid potential errors.

Theoretical analysis shows that the correctness of the numerical solution can be verified by the analytic formula in the form of infinite series. As alternative choices, we proposed four feasible numerical schemes and designed the
Kseries
,
Kagm
,
Kintgc
and
Kintgl
solvers for CEI-1 to obtain consistent numerical results based on infinite series method, AGM method, Gauss-Chebyshev & Gauss-Legendre numerical integrations. The key algorithms developed have been verified, validated and tested.

Although our emphasis is put on the numerical algorithms for the CEI-1,
the methods adopted can also be used for the complete elliptic integrals of the second and third kinds. Furthermore, the key modules for the four solvers are designed for more general problems so as to satisfy various applications as much as possible. Particularly, the iterative algorithms for generating orthogonal polynomials with iterative formula
Y_{n}(x)=[A(n)x+B(n)]Y_{n-1}(x)-C(n)Y_{n-2}(x)
and their derivatives can be taken to compute the Legendre, Chebyshev, Laguerre and Hermite polynomials which have wide applications in mathematics, physics and engineering.

Theorem 1
.

where

{\mathcal{E}}^{\mathrm{gc}}_{n}(f)=\frac{2\pi}{2^{2n}(2n)!}f^{(2n)}(\eta),\quad\eta\in(-1,1)

(64)

By changing the variable
x

(65)

where
\xi\in(-a,a)
and

Theorem 3
.

(72)

\xi=[b+a+(b-a)\eta]/2

\eta\in(-1,1)
,

\xi\in(a,b)

&+\left[\frac{b-a}{2}\right]^{2n+1}\cdot\frac{(n!)^{4}}{[(2n)!]^{3}}\cdot\frac{2^{2n+1}}{2n+1}f^{(2n)}(\xi)\end{split}

(73)

from Theorem
3
.

\displaystyle{\int^{b}_{a}f(x)\dif x}

.

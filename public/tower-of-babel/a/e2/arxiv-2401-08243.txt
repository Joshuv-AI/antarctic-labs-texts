Abstract
. This paper primarily establishes an asymptotic variance estimate for smooth linear statistics associated with zero sets of systems of random holomorphic sections in a sequence of positive Hermitian holomorphic line bundles on a compact Kähler manifold ( X , ω ) (X,\omega) . Using this variance estimate and the expected distribution, we derive an equidistribution result for zeros of these random systems, which, in terms of approximation theory, proves that the smooth positive closed form ω k \omega^{k} can be approximated by currents of integration along analytic subsets of X X of codimension k k , k ∈ { 1 , … , n } k\in\{1,\ldots,n\} . The probability measures taken into consideration in this paper are sufficiently general to include a wide range of the measures commonly encountered in the literature, for which we give equidistribution results at the end, such as the standard Gaussian measure, Fubini-Study measure, the area measure of spheres, probability measures whose distributions have bounded densities with logarithmically decaying tails and locally moderate measures among others.

In the standard setup of geometric quantization, one works with a compact Kähler manifold
X
with a fixed Kähler form
\omega
on it, denoted by
(X,\omega)
, equipped with a Hermitian holomorphic line bundle
(L,h)
, known as the prequantum line bundle, fulfilling the prequantization condition given by

(1.2)

\omega=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}R^{L}=c_{1}(L,h).

Here
R^{L}
is the curvature of the Chern connection on
L
, and
c_{1}(L,h)
is the Chern curvature form of
(L,h)
. The existence of the prequantum line bundle
(L,h)
allows the investigation into the Hilbert space
H^{0}(X,L)
of holomorphic sections and to define a mapping between classical observables on
X
and quantum operators on
H^{0}(X,L)
in the setting where the Planck constant approaches zero. The modification of Planck’s constant corresponds to scaling the Kähler form via tensor powers
L^{\otimes p}

\hbar=\frac{1}{p}
.

The stipulation in (
1.2
) is recognized as an integrality condition. The existence of a prequantum holomorphic line bundle is strongly connected with the integral nature of the de Rham cohomology class
[\omega]
, i.e.,
[\omega]\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
. When dealt with a Kähler form
\omega
that is not integral, one can construct an associated family of positive line bundles
(L_{p},h_{p})
. The curvatures of these bundles approximate integer multiples of
\omega
, thus serving as a prequantization of the non-integral Kähler form
\omega

The approximation condition (
1.1
) is derived naturally as follows: Starting with a Kähler form
\omega
, one may initially approximate the associated cohomology class
[\omega]\in H^{2}(X,\mathbb{R})
with integral classes in
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
through diophantine approximation, as described by Kronecker’s lemma, and one can construct smooth forms corresponding to these integral approximations. The paper
[
12
]
provides a full asymptotic expansion of the Bergman kernel restricted to the diagonal in case there is such a good diophantine approximation of
\omega
. Using the same approximation, we will have an equidistribution result on the zeros of systems of random holomorphic sections. For the equidistribution problem, following
[
12
]
, we work in a setting as general as possible, where metrics are of class
\mathscr{C}^{2}
and the convergence of Chern forms in (
1.1
) is in the
\mathscr{C}^{0}
-topology.

It is also important to note that, within the examples of sequences of line bundles
(L_{p},h_{p})
fulfilling the condition (
1.1

(L_{p},h_{p})=(L^{\otimes p},h^{\otimes p})

Other examples include cases where

with appropriate weights

\langle s_{1},s_{2}\rangle_{p}:=\int_{X}{\big\langle s_{1}(x),s_{2}(x)\big\rangle_{h_{p}}\omega^{n}}\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\|s\|^{2}_{p}:=\langle s,s\rangle_{p}.

By virtue of Cartan-Serre finiteness theorem (see, e.g., chapter 6,

\mathcal{L}^{2}(X,L_{p})

with respect to this norm, forming the Hilbert space of square integrable sections of

. Consider the orthogonal projection operator

The Bergman kernel, denoted as

, section

K_{p}(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{p}}{S^{p}_{j}(x)\otimes S^{p}_{j}(y)^{*}}\in L_{p,x}\otimes L_{p,y}^{*},

where
S^{p}_{j}(y)^{*}=\langle\,.\,,S^{p}_{j}(y)\rangle_{h_{p}}\in L^{*}_{p,y}

, which we denote by

. This kernel function has the dimensional density property, meaning that

(B)
There exists a constant

\frac{A_{p}^{n}}{M_{0}}\leq K_{p}(x)\leq M_{0}A_{p}^{n},

To introduce randomness, let us fix an orthonormal basis

(C2)

Unlike

\mathcal{D}^{p,q}(X)

\langle[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}],\phi\rangle:=\int_{\operatorname{Reg}(Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}})}\phi,

The expectation and the variance of the current valued random variable

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\langle[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}],\phi\rangle

.

(ii)

For codimension one, a method that circumvents the use of variance and expected distribution, the approach employed by Marinescu, Coman, and Bayraktar
[
3

At the same time, our principal result, which has been established for codimension

, is applicable to a multitude of frequently investigated probability measures. These include the area measure of spheres, Gaussian measure, Fubini-Study measure, and measures with bounded density having logarithmic decaying tails. When our main result is applied to these measures in the context of tensor powers of a fixed prequantum line bundle, the required summability assumption can be dropped. Our methods extend the results of Shiffman and Zelditch (
[

]
).

2.
Background

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold with
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{X}=n
.
A holomorphic line bundle
L
over
X
is defined by compiling complex lines
\{L_{x}\}_{x\in X}

and constructing a complex manifold of dimension
1+\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{X}
with a projection map

\pi:L\rightarrow X
such that
\pi
assigns each line (or fiber)
L_{x}
to
x
and is holomorphic.
Using an open cover
\{U_{\alpha}\}
of
X
, we can locally trivialize
L
through biholomorphisms

\Psi_{\alpha}:\pi^{-1}(U_{\alpha})\rightarrow U_{\alpha}\times\mathbb{C}
which map
L_{x}=\pi^{-1}(x)

isomorphically onto
\{x\}\times\mathbb{C}
. The line bundle
L
is then uniquely (i.e., up to isomorphism)
determined by these transition functions
g_{\alpha\beta}
, which are non-vanishing holomorphic functions
on
U_{\alpha\beta}:=U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}
defined by
g_{\alpha\beta}=\Psi_{\alpha}\circ\Psi^{-1}_{\beta}|_{\{x\}\times\mathbb{C}}
.
These functions
g_{\alpha\beta}
satisfy the cocycle condition
g_{\alpha\beta}g_{\beta\gamma}g_{\gamma\alpha}=1
.

Because the transition functions satisfy the cocycle condition, they define a cohomology class, denoted as

[g_{\alpha\beta}]\in H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}^{*})
. Here,
H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}^{*})
is the first sheaf
cohomology group of the manifold
X
with coefficients in the sheaf of non-zero holomorphic functions,
denoted by
\mathcal{O}^{*}
. The exponential short exact sequence
0\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{*}\rightarrow 0
produces a mapping
c_{1}:H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}^{*})\rightarrow H^{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
and the first Chern class

c_{1}(L,h)
is defined by the image of
[g_{\alpha\beta}]
under this mapping.

Let
\{U_{\alpha}\}
be an open covering of
X
and
e_{\alpha}
be holomorphic frames of
L
on each
U_{\alpha}
. Then the metric is given by a family of functions
h_{\alpha}=|e_{\alpha}|^{2}_{h}:U_{\alpha}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
with local weights
\varphi_{\alpha}=-\frac{1}{2}\log{h_{\alpha}}\in\mathscr{C}^{2}(U_{\alpha})
. Given the transition functions
g_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{e_{\beta}}{e_{\alpha}}\in\mathcal{O}^{*}_{X}(U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta})
of
L
, then on
U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta},
we have that
h_{\beta}=|g_{\alpha\beta}|^{2}h_{\alpha}
and therefore
\log{h_{\beta}}=\log{|g_{\alpha\beta}|^{2}}+\log{h_{\alpha}},
which is equivalent to the following

\varphi_{\alpha}=\varphi_{\beta}+\log{|g_{\alpha\beta}|}\,\,\text{in}\,\,\mathscr{C}^{2}(U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}).

In this paper, the notion of positivity we use will be what is known in the literature as Nakano-Griffiths positivity for line bundles: A holomorphic line bundle
(L,h),
with a Hermitian metric (that may not be
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}
) is said to be positive, semi-positive if the local
\mathscr{C}^{2}
weight functions
\varphi
corresponding to the metric
h
are strictly plurisubharmonic and plurisubharmonic, respectively.

Since we will be dealing with positive line bundles, the local weight functions
\varphi_{\alpha}
above are strictly plurisubharmonic, which means that, in addition to being of the class
\mathscr{C}^{2}
,
dd^{c}\varphi_{\alpha}>0
holds pointwisely. By Proposition 2.4 of
[
10
]
, the line bundles we consider are ample and hence
X
is projective.

We follow the anticommutativity convention for a wedge product of a current
T
of bidegree
(p,q)
and a differential form
\theta
of type
(r,s)
(recall that
(T\wedge\theta)(\varphi):=T(\theta\wedge\varphi)
for
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}^{n-p-r,\,n-q-s}(X)
),

(2.1)

\theta\wedge T:=(-1)^{(p+r)(q+s)}(T\wedge\theta).

Given that all the objects we consider are of bidegree or type
(p,p)
, we will not distinguish between
\theta\wedge T
and
T\wedge\theta
. This simplification will be helpful in the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

The following fact (see section 4 of
[
3
]
) will be required in the next section: There exists a constant
b>0
such that for every
\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{n-k,n-k}(X)
,
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
,

(2.2)

-b\,\|\phi\|_{\mathscr{C}^{2}}\,\omega^{n-k+1}\leq dd^{c}\phi\leq b\,\|\phi\|_{\mathscr{C}^{2}}\,\omega^{n-k+1},

which amounts to saying that the total variation of
dd^{c}\phi
verifies the inequality
|dd^{c}\phi|\leq b\,\|\phi\|_{\mathscr{C}^{2}}\,\omega^{n-k+1}
.

In our local analysis throughout the paper, we always consider trivializing open neighborhoods of given point
x\in X
. Let
U_{p}
be such a trivializing neighborhood of
x,
and let
\{S_{1}^{p},\ldots,S^{p}_{d_{p}}\}
be an orthonormal basis for
H^{0}(X,L_{p})
. For a local holomorphic frame
e_{p}
of
L_{p}
in
U_{p}
, we have
S^{p}_{j}=s^{p}_{j}e_{p},
where
s^{p}_{j}
is a holomorphic function in
U_{p}.
Then the Bergman kernel functions and the Fubini-Study currents are defined as follows:

(2.3)

K_{p}(x)=\sum^{d_{p}}_{j=1}{|S^{p}_{j}(x)|^{2}_{h_{p}}}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\gamma_{p}|_{U_{p}}=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\log\big(\sum^{d_{p}}_{j=1}{|s^{p}_{j}|^{2}}\big),

where
d=\partial+\overline{\partial},
and
d^{c}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}(\partial-\overline{\partial})
. It is important to emphasize that
K_{p}
and
\gamma_{p}
are independent of the chosen basis
\{{S_{1}^{p},\ldots,S^{p}_{d_{p}}}\}
, (see
[
9
]
, section 3). Moreover,
\gamma_{p}
is a positive closed current of bidegree
(1,1),
smooth away from the base locus
BsH^{0}(X,L_{p})
of
H^{0}(X,L_{p}),
and by (
2.3
) we have that

(2.4)

\log{K_{p}}\in L^{1}(X,\omega^{n})\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\gamma_{p}-c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})=\frac{1}{2}dd^{c}\log{K_{p}}.

which essentially means that
\gamma_{p}
has the same de Rham cohomology class as
c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})
.

For large enough
p\in\mathbb{N}
, if
\Phi_{p}:X\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{d_{p}-1}
is the Kodaira map defined by the basis
\{S_{j}^{p}\}_{j=1}^{d_{p}}
via

\Phi_{p}(x)=[S_{1}^{p}(x):\ldots:S_{d_{p}}^{p}(x)],\,\,\,\,

then we have
\gamma_{p}=\Phi_{p}^{*}(\omega_{FS}),
which justifies their name. This mapping
\Phi_{p}
is also a holomorphic embedding by Corollary 1.3 of
[
12
]
whose proof can be carried out by the same arguments in
[
25
]
(p. 189), and thus it is a generalization of the classical Kodaira embedding theorem to the current setting.

Before finishing this section, let us give an important tool also known as Poincaré-Lelong formula(see

]
, Theorem 2.3.3): Given

we have

(2.5)

[Z_{s}]=c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})+dd^{c}\log{|s|_{h_{p}}}.

3.
Variance Estimate

In this section, we will give the proof of Theorem
1.1
using an inductive approach based on the codimension
k
. We will start with the case of codimension
1
, serving as the initial step in our induction process. To begin, let us make some initial observations.

Let
s_{p}\in H^{0}(X,L_{p})
. We can write it as

s_{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{p}}{a_{j}^{p}S^{p}_{j}}=\langle a,\Gamma_{p}\rangle,

where
\Gamma_{p}=(S_{1}^{p},\ldots,S^{p}_{d_{p}}),\,a=(a_{1}^{p},\ldots,a_{d_{p}}^{p})\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{p}}
and
\{S^{p}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d_{p}}
is an orthonormal basis of
H^{0}(X,L_{p})
.

For
x\in X
, let
U
be a small open neighborhood of
x
such that
for each
p
, we take a local holomorphic frame
e_{p}
of
L_{p}
on
U
. Then locally
S^{p}_{j}=f_{j}e_{p}
, where
f_{j}
are holomorphic functions in
U
and so, by writing
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{d_{p}}),

s_{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{p}}{a_{j}^{p}f_{j}e_{p}}=\langle a,f\rangle e_{p}.

By Poincaré-Lelong formula (
2.5
), on the neighborhood
U
, we have

[Z_{s_{p}}]=dd^{c}\log{|\langle a,f\rangle|}=dd^{c}\log|\langle a,\Gamma_{p}\rangle|_{h_{p}}+c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p}).

Now, for any
\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{n-1,n-1}(X)
, we define the following random variable

(3.1)

W_{s_{p}}:=[Z_{s_{p}}]-c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})=dd^{c}\log{|\langle a,\Gamma_{p}\rangle|_{h_{p}}}.

By a basic feature of the variance,

(3.2)

\mathrm{Var}\langle[Z_{s_{p}}],\phi\rangle=\mathrm{Var}\langle W_{s_{p}},\phi\rangle.

Therefore, in the light of (
3.2
) it is enough to estimate
\mathrm{Var}\langle W_{s_{p}},\phi\rangle
.
Employing certain methods from
[
39
]
and
[
40
]
in our setting, we have the following theorem for codimension one.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(L_{p},h_{p})_{p\geq 1}
,
(X,\omega)
, and
\sigma_{p}
be as defined above. Assume that they satisfy the conditions
(A)
,
(B)
, and
(C2)
. If
s_{p}\in H^{0}(X,L_{p})
, then for all
p\geq 1
and any
\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{n-1,n-1}(X)
, we have the following variance estimate

\mathrm{Var}\langle[\widehat{Z}_{s_{p}}],\phi\rangle\leq\frac{(C_{p})^{2/\alpha}}{A^{2}_{p}}(B_{\phi}\mathrm{Vol}(X))^{2},

where
B_{\phi}
is a constant depending on the form
\phi
.

Proof.

First, by (
3.1
), we have

(3.3)

\mathbb{E}\langle W_{s_{p}},\,\phi\rangle^{2}=\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{X}\int_{X}{\log{|\langle a,\Gamma_{p}(x)\rangle|_{h_{p}}}\log{|\langle a,\Gamma_{p}(y)\rangle|_{h_{p}}}dd^{c}\phi(x)dd^{c}\phi(y)d\sigma_{p}(s)}.

Now, writing

|\Gamma_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}:=\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{d_{p}}{|S^{p}_{j}(x)|^{2}_{h_{p}}}\Big)^{1/2}=\sqrt{K_{p}(x)}

allows us to express
\Gamma_{p}(x)
as
\Gamma_{p}(x)=|\Gamma_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}u_{p}(x)
, where
u_{p}(x)
is a unit vector in the direction of
\Gamma_{p}(x)
such that
|u_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}=1
. Substituting
\Gamma_{p}(x)=|\Gamma_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}u_{p}(x)
into the integrand in (
3.3
) breaks it into four terms:

(3.4)

\displaystyle\log{|\Gamma_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}}\log{|\Gamma_{p}(y)|_{h_{p}}}+\log{|\Gamma_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}}\log{|\langle a,u_{p}(y)\rangle|_{h_{p}}}+\log{|\Gamma_{p}(y)|_{h_{p}}}\log{|\langle a,u_{p}(x)\rangle|_{h_{p}}}

(3.5)

\displaystyle+\log{|\langle a,u_{p}(x)\rangle|_{h_{p}}}\log{|\langle a,u_{p}(y)\rangle|_{h_{p}}}.

Before continuing with the variance estimate, we will see that
\mathbb{E}\langle W_{s_{p}},\,\phi\rangle
is bounded. To do this, we prove an auxiliary inequality to begin with. First, by (
2.2
) and noting that for sufficiently large
p
, condition
(B)
ensures
\log{|\Gamma_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}}\geq 0
, we have

(3.6)

\int_{X}{\big|\log{|\Gamma_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}}\big|\,\big|dd^{c}\phi(x)\big|}\leq\frac{b\,\|\phi\|_{\mathscr{C}^{2}}}{2}\int_{X}{\big|\log{K_{p}(x)}\big|\,\omega^{n}}<\infty,

since
\log{K_{p}}\in L^{1}(X,\omega^{n})
for every
p\geq 1
. Now, it is evident that

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}\langle W_{s_{p}},\phi\rangle\big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{X}{\Big(\log{|\Gamma_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}}+\log{|\langle a,u_{p}(x)\rangle|_{h_{p}}}\Big)}\,dd^{c}\phi(x)\,d\sigma_{p}(s_{p})\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{X}{\big|\log{|\Gamma_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}}\big|\,\big|dd^{c}\phi(x)\big|\,d\sigma_{p}(s_{p})}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{X}{\big|\log{|\langle a,u_{p}(x)\rangle|_{h_{p}}}\big|}\,\big|dd^{c}\phi(x)\big|\,d\sigma_{p}(s_{p}).

The first integral has an upper bound by (
3.6
), given that
\sigma_{p}
is a probability measure on
H^{0}(X,L_{p})
. For the second double integral, we identify
H^{0}(X,L_{p})\simeq\mathbb{C}^{d_{p}}
. Using the moment condition
(C2)
, Hölder’s inequality, and (
2.2
), we have

(3.7)

\int_{X}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{d_{p}}}{\big|\log{|\langle a,\rho_{p}(x)\rangle|}\big|}\,d\sigma_{p}(a)\,dd^{c}\phi(x)\leq(C_{p})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\,b\,\|\phi\|_{\mathscr{C}^{2}}\,\mathrm{Vol}(X),

where

\rho_{p}(x)=\left(\frac{f_{1}(x)}{\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{d_{p}}{|f_{j}(x)|^{2}}}},\ldots,\frac{f_{d_{p}}(x)}{\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{d_{p}}{|f_{j}(x)|^{2}}}}\right).

Applying Fubini-Tonelli’s theorem, we can write

(3.8)

\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{X}{\big|\log{|\langle a,u_{p}(x)\rangle|_{h_{p}}}\big|}\,\big|dd^{c}\phi(x)\big|\,d\sigma_{p}(s_{p})=\int_{X}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{d_{p}}}{\big|\log{|\langle a,\rho_{p}(x)\rangle|}\big|}\,d\sigma_{p}(a)\,\big|dd^{c}\phi(x)\big|,

and thus we obtain a bound for the second integral.

We now return to estimating the variance of
W_{s_{p}}
. Expanding the term
\langle\mathbb{E}[W_{s_{p}}],\,\phi\rangle^{2}
using the expression for the expected distribution, we have

\big(\mathbb{E}\langle W_{s_{p}},\phi\rangle\big)^{2}=J_{1}+2J_{2}+J_{3},

where

(3.9)

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{X}{\log{|\Gamma_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}}\,dd^{c}\phi(x)\,d\sigma_{p}(s_{p})}\right)^{2},

(3.10)

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{X}{\log{|\Gamma_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}}\,dd^{c}\phi(x)\,d\sigma_{p}(s_{p})}\right)\times\left(\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{X}{\log{|\langle a,u_{p}(x)\rangle|_{h_{p}}}\,dd^{c}\phi(x)\,d\sigma_{p}(s_{p})}\right),

(3.11)

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{X}{\log{|\langle a,u_{p}(x)\rangle|_{h_{p}}}\,dd^{c}\phi(x)\,d\sigma_{p}(s_{p})}\right)^{2}.

Since
\mathbb{E}\langle W_{s_{p}},\phi\rangle
is bounded, it follows that
J_{1},J_{2}
, and
J_{3}
are all finite.

From the expressions (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we write
\mathbb{E}\langle W_{s_{p}},\phi\rangle^{2}=B_{1}+2B_{2}+B_{3}
, where

(3.12)

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{X}\int_{X}{\log{|\Gamma_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}}\,\log{|\Gamma_{p}(y)|_{h_{p}}}\,dd^{c}\phi(x)\,dd^{c}\phi(y)\,d\sigma_{p}(s_{p})},

(3.13)

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{X}\int_{X}{\log{|\Gamma_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}}\,\log{|\langle a,u_{p}(y)\rangle|_{h_{p}}}\,dd^{c}\phi(x)\,dd^{c}\phi(y)\,d\sigma_{p}(s_{p})},

(3.14)

\displaystyle B_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\int_{X}{dd^{c}\phi(y)\,dd^{c}\phi(x)}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{d_{p}}}{\log{|\langle a,\rho_{p}(x)\rangle|}\,\log{|\langle a,\rho_{p}(y)\rangle|}\,d\sigma_{p}(a)}.

From (
3.6
), the moment assumption
(C2)
, and Fubini-Tonelli’s theorem, we observe that
B_{1},B_{2}
, and
B_{3}
are all finite, and also that
B_{1}=J_{1}
and
B_{2}=J_{2}
. Thus, the remaining terms are
J_{3}
and
B_{3}
, and we find

(3.15)

\mathrm{Var}\langle W_{s_{p}},\phi\rangle=B_{3}-J_{3}.

It suffices to estimate
B_{3}
from above to complete the variance estimation. Using Tonelli’s theorem and Hölder’s inequality with
\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}=1
, where
\alpha\geq 2
is the constant satisfying the moment condition
(C2)
, we have

\displaystyle B_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\int_{X}{dd^{c}\phi(y)\,dd^{c}\phi(x)}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{d_{p}}}{\big|\log{|\langle a,\rho_{p}(x)\rangle|}\big|\,\big|\log{|\langle a,\rho_{p}(y)\rangle|}\big|\,d\sigma_{p}(a)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\int_{X}{dd^{c}\phi(y)\,dd^{c}\phi(x)}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{C}^{d_{p}}}{\big|\log{|\langle a,\rho_{p}(x)\rangle|}\big|^{\alpha}d\sigma_{p}(a)}\right\}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{C}^{d_{p}}}{\big|\log{|\langle a,\rho_{p}(y)\rangle|}\big|^{\beta}d\sigma_{p}(a)}\right\}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\int_{X}{dd^{c}\phi(y)\,dd^{c}\phi(x)\,(C_{p})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{C}^{d_{p}}}{\big|\log{|\langle a,\rho_{p}(y)\rangle|}\big|^{\beta}d\sigma_{p}(a)}\right\}^{\frac{1}{\beta}}.

Applying Hölder’s inequality again to the innermost integral in the last line (since
\alpha\geq 2\geq\beta
allows us to do so), we get

(3.16)

B_{3}\leq(C_{p})^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\int_{X}\int_{X}{dd^{c}\phi(y)\,dd^{c}\phi(x)}\leq(C_{p})^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\,\,(\int_{X}{|dd^{c}\phi|})^{2}.

Consequently, applying the total variation inequality (
2.2
) for
dd^{c}
twice in (
3.16
), we obtain the following inequality:

(3.17)

B_{3}\leq(C_{p})^{2/\alpha}\,b^{2}\,\|\phi\|^{2}_{\mathscr{C}^{2}}\,\mathrm{Vol}(X)^{2},

which, after dividing by
A^{2}_{p}
and defining
B_{\phi}:=b\,\|\phi\|_{\mathscr{C}^{2}}
, provides the variance estimate as intended.
∎

For
\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{n-1,n-1}(X)
, from the arguments in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we have

\mathbb{E}\langle[\widehat{Z}_{s_{p}}],\phi\rangle=\frac{1}{A_{p}}\int_{X}{c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})\wedge\phi}+\int_{X}{\log{|K_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}dd^{c}\phi(x)}}+\int_{X}\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}{\log{|\langle a,u_{p}(x)\rangle|_{h_{p}}d\sigma_{p}(s_{p})dd^{c}\phi(x)}}.

Notice that even though
dd^{c}\phi
is a signed measure, it is still possible to apply Fubini-Tonelli’s theorem to interchange the integrals using the Jordan decomposition of
dd^{c}\phi
. Since
\log{\big|\langle a,u_{p}(x)\rangle\big|_{h_{p}}}=\log{\frac{|s_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}}{\sqrt{K_{p}(x)}}}
, it follows from this last expression that

(3.18)

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{Z}_{s_{p}}]=\frac{1}{A_{p}}c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})+\frac{1}{2A_{p}}dd^{c}\log K_{p}(x)+\frac{1}{A_{p}}dd^{c}\big(\int_{s_{p}\in H^{0}(X,L_{p})}{\log\big|\langle a,u_{p}(x)\rangle\big|_{h_{p}}d\sigma_{p}(s_{p})}\big),

which shows that
\mathbb{E}\langle[\widehat{Z}_{s_{p}}],\phi\rangle=\langle\mathbb{E}[\widehat{Z}_{s_{p}}],\phi\rangle
, that is, the expected value of a smooth linear statistic can be seen as a current, also referred to as the expected current of integration, which is a positive, closed
(1,1)
-current.

Before proceeding to the proof of our main theorem, we present a lemma concerning cohomology classes of integration currents that will be instrumental in the sequel. This lemma has been previously proven in
[
40
]
(as part of Proposition 2.2), and for the sake of reader, we include its proof here.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold with the fixed Kähler form
\omega.
If
Z_{s_{p}^{1}},\ldots,Z_{s_{p}^{k}}
are smooth and intersect transversally, we have

(3.19)

\left\langle\big[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big],\omega^{n-k}\right\rangle=\int_{X}c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

Proof.

For
k=1,
this is just a consequence of Poincaré-Lelong formula and the fact that
\omega
is a closed form. Indeed,

\left\langle\big[Z_{s_{p}}\big],\omega^{n-1}\right\rangle=\int_{X}c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})\wedge\omega^{n-1}+\int_{X}dd^{c}\log|s_{p}|_{h_{p}}\wedge\omega^{n-1}=\int_{X}c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})\wedge\omega^{n-1}.

Let us now suppose that the assertion (
3.19
) is true for
k-1
sections
\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}=(s_{p}^{2},\ldots,s_{p}^{k}).
Then by the induction hypothesis and the base step of induction, it yields that

\displaystyle\left\langle\big[Z_{s_{p}^{1}}\cap Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}\big],\omega^{n-k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{Z_{s_{p}^{1}}}c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})\wedge c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})^{k-1}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=\int_{X}c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}

Since
\big[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big]=\big[Z_{s_{p}^{1}}\cap Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}\big]
by our assumption, we complete the proof. ∎

For
p\geq p_{0}
, let
\Sigma_{p}^{k}=(s_{p}^{1},s_{p}^{2},\cdots,s_{p}^{k})
be a typical system of independent random holomorphic sections
s_{p}^{j}\in H^{0}(X,L_{p})
for
j=1,2,\cdots,k\,
where
1\leq k\leq\textup{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}X
. Let
\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{n-k,n-k}(X)
. Then

(3.20)

\big|\big\langle\big[\widehat{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big],\phi\big\rangle\big|=\Big|\frac{1}{A_{p}^{k}}\int_{Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}}\phi\,\Big|\leq\frac{1}{A_{p}^{k}}\sup\left\|\phi\right\|\mathrm{Vol}(Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}).

By the diophantine approximation (
1.1
), there exists
p_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\frac{A_{p}}{2}\,\,\omega\leq c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})\leq 2A_{p}\,\,\omega

for all
p\geq p_{1}
, which implies that

(3.21)

\int_{X}{\omega^{n-k+1}\wedge c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})^{k-1}}\leq(2A_{p})^{k-1}\mathrm{Vol}(X).

Using Lemma
3.2
above and (
3.21
)

(3.22)

\mathrm{Vol}(Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}})=\big\langle\big[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big],\omega^{n-k}\big\rangle=\int_{X}c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq(2A_{p})^{k}\,\mathrm{Vol}(X)

for all
p\geq\max{\{p_{0},p_{1}\}}
. Thus, combining both of the inequalities above, we obtain that for
p\geq\max{\{p_{0},p_{1}\}}

(3.23)

\big|\big\langle\big[\widehat{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big],\phi\big\rangle\big|\leq 2^{k}\ \mathrm{Vol}(X)\sup\left\|\phi\right\|<\infty,

which means that
\big\langle\big[\widehat{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big],\phi\big\rangle
is bounded for almost all
\Sigma_{p}^{k}.
Consequently,
\mathbb{E}\langle[\widehat{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}],\phi\rangle
is well-defined.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(L_{p},h_{p})_{p\geq 1}
,
(X,\omega)
, and
\sigma_{p}
be as defined above. Assume that they satisfy the conditions
(A)
,
(B)
, and
(C1)
-
(C2)
. Then, for
1\leq k\leq\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X
,
p
sufficiently large and
\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{n-k,n-k}(X)
,

\mathbb{E}\langle[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}],\phi\rangle=\langle\mathbb{E}\big[Z_{s_{p}^{1}}\big]\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbb{E}\big[Z_{s_{p}^{k}}\big],\phi\rangle:=\langle\mathbb{E}\big[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big],\phi\rangle.

As a result,
\mathbb{E}\big[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big]
is a positive closed
(k,k)
-current.

Proof.

We induct on the codimension
k.
The base step
k=1
follows from (
3.18
). Suppose that the claim holds for
k-1
sections, say
\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}=(s_{p}^{2},\ldots,s_{p}^{k}).
Fix
s_{p}^{1}\in H^{0}(X,L_{p})
so that
Y:=Z_{s_{p}^{1}}
is a complex submanifold. For almost all
s_{p}^{j},
we write
(s_{p}^{j})^{\prime}=s_{p}^{j}|_{Y}
and
H^{0}(X,L_{p})^{\prime}:=H^{0}(X,L_{p})\big|_{Y},
which in turn for almost all
\Sigma_{p}^{k}=(s_{p}^{1},\ldots,s_{p}^{k})\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{k}
give rise to the notation
(\Sigma_{p}^{k})^{\prime}=\Sigma_{p}^{k}\big|_{Y},
and
(\mathcal{H}_{p}^{k})^{\prime}=\mathcal{H}_{p}^{k}\big|_{Y},
where
\Sigma_{p}^{k}\big|_{Y}=(s_{p}^{1}|_{Y},\ldots,s_{p}^{k}|_{Y}),
and
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{k}=H^{0}(X,L_{p})^{k}
respectively. We endow
H^{0}(X,L_{p})^{\prime}
with the push-forward probability measure
\sigma_{p}^{\prime}:=\theta_{*}\sigma_{p},
where
\theta:H^{0}(X,L_{p})\rightarrow H^{0}(X,L_{p})^{\prime}
is the restriction map. The induced measure on
(\mathcal{H}_{p}^{k})^{\prime}
will be denoted by
(\sigma_{p}^{\prime})^{k}.
By the independence and induction hypothesis applied to
Y=Z_{s_{p}^{1}}
and
(\mathcal{H}_{p}^{k-1})^{\prime}
, and observation that
Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}=Z_{(\Sigma_{p}^{k-1})^{\prime}}
we have, for
\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{n-k,n-k}(X),

(3.24)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}_{p}^{k-1}}\big\langle\big[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big],\phi\big\rangle d\sigma_{p}^{k-1}(\Sigma_{p}^{k-1})

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathcal{H}_{p}^{k-1})^{\prime}}\left\langle\big[Z_{(\Sigma_{p}^{k-1})^{\prime}}\big],\phi\big|_{Y}\right\rangle d(\sigma_{p}^{\prime})^{k-1}((\Sigma_{p}^{k-1})^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathbb{E}[Z_{(s_{p}^{2})^{\prime}}]\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbb{E}[Z_{(s_{p}^{k})^{\prime}}],\phi\big|_{Y}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{Z_{s_{p}^{1}}}\mathbb{E}[Z_{s_{p}^{2}}]\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbb{E}[Z_{s_{p}^{k}}]\wedge\phi

Finally, integrating over all
s_{p}^{1}
in the last expression gives what is desired.

∎

Now we go on with the proof of the variance estimate in higher codimensions. We adapt some of the methods in
[
37
]
into our setting.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem
3.1
provides the case
k=1
of induction on the codimension
k
. Now let
n\geq k\geq 2
and we suppose that the variance estimate of Theorem 1.1 is true for
k-1
sections. We pick a system of
k
independent random holomorphic sections
\Sigma_{p}^{k}=(s^{1}_{p},\ldots,s^{k}_{p})\in H^{0}(X,L_{p})^{k}
and we write
\Sigma_{p}^{k}=(\Sigma_{p}^{k-1},s^{k}_{p})
, where
\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}=(s^{1}_{p},\ldots,s_{p}^{k-1})
and
[{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}]=[{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]\wedge[{Z}_{s^{k}_{p}}].
Let
\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{n-k,n-k}(X)
be a test form. Since
\mathbb{E}[{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}]=\mathbb{E}[{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]\wedge\mathbb{E}[{Z}_{s^{k}_{p}}]
in view of Theorem
3.3
, we first have

(3.25)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\langle[{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}],\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\langle[{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}],\phi\rangle^{2}-(\mathbb{E}\langle[{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}],\phi\rangle)^{2}

(3.26)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\langle[{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]\wedge[{Z}_{s^{k}_{p}}],\phi\rangle^{2}-(\langle\mathbb{E}[{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]\wedge\mathbb{E}[{Z}_{s^{k}_{p}}],\phi\rangle)^{2}.

We write

\langle[{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]\wedge[{Z}_{s^{k}_{p}}],\phi\rangle^{2}-\langle\mathbb{E}[{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]\wedge\mathbb{E}[\widehat{Z}_{s^{k}_{p}}],\phi\rangle^{2}=I_{1}+I_{2}

where

I_{1}:=I_{1}(\Sigma_{p}^{k-1},s^{k}_{p})=\langle[{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]\wedge[{Z}_{s^{k}_{p}}],\phi\rangle^{2}-\langle[{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]\wedge\mathbb{E}[{Z}_{s^{k}_{p}}],\phi\rangle^{2}

and

I_{2}:=I_{2}(\Sigma_{p}^{k-1})=\langle[{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]\wedge\mathbb{E}[{Z}_{s^{k}_{p}}],\phi\rangle^{2}-(\langle\mathbb{E}[{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]\wedge\mathbb{E}[{Z}_{s^{k}_{p}}],\phi\rangle)^{2},

which are well-defined for typical choices of
\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}
and
s^{k}_{p}
(see the proof of Theorem
3.3
).

We observe that

(3.27)

\mathrm{Var}\langle[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}],\phi\rangle=\mathbb{E}[I_{1}]+\mathbb{E}[I_{2}].

Now let
Y:=\big\{x\in X:\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}(x)=0\big\}
. Notice that
\langle[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]\wedge[Z_{s^{k}_{p}}],\phi\rangle=\langle[Z_{s^{k}_{p}}]\big|_{Y},\phi|_{Y}\rangle
.
Initially, we will find an estimation on
\mathbb{E}[I_{1}]
. To do so, the first step is to integrate
I_{1}
over
H^{0}(X,L_{p})
then use the observation above and apply Theorem
3.1
:

\displaystyle\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}{I_{1}(\Sigma_{p}^{k-1},s^{k}_{p})d\sigma_{p}(s^{k}_{p})}

\displaystyle=\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}{\langle[Z_{s^{k}_{p}}]\big|_{Y},\phi|_{Y}\rangle^{2}-(\langle\mathbb{E}\langle[Z_{s^{k}_{p}}]\big|_{Y},\phi|_{Y}\rangle})^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}\langle[Z_{s^{k}_{p}}]\,\big|_{Y},\phi|_{Y}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq(C_{p})^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\Big(B_{\phi}\big|_{Y}\mathrm{Vol}(Y)\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(C_{p})^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}\Big(B_{\phi}\int_{X}{\omega^{n-k+1}}\wedge c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})^{k-1}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(C_{p})^{2/\alpha}\,\big(B_{\phi}\,\mathrm{Vol}(X)(2A_{p})^{k-1}\big)^{2}.

Here, for the last inequality, we have used the fact that
\int_{X}{\omega^{n-k+1}\wedge c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})^{k-1}}\leq(2A_{p})^{k-1}\mathrm{Vol}(X)
for
p\geq\max{\{p_{0},p_{1}\}},
which is a simple consequence of the diophantine approximation condition (
1.1
),
B_{\phi}\big|_{Y}:=b\,\|\phi|_{Y}\|_{\mathscr{C}^{2}}
for
b>0
. We get the second inequality by using the inequality
B_{\phi}\big|_{Y}\leq B_{\phi}=b\,\|\phi\|_{\mathscr{C}^{2}}
.

Now integrating over
H^{0}(X,L_{p})^{k-1}
and taking the last inequality just above into account yield that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[I_{1}]

\displaystyle=\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})^{k-1}}\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}{I_{1}(\Sigma_{p}^{k-1},s^{k}_{p})d\sigma_{p}(s^{k}_{p})d\sigma^{k-1}_{p}(\Sigma_{p}^{k-1})}\leq(C_{p})^{2/\alpha}\,\big(B_{\phi}\,\mathrm{Vol}(X)(2A_{p})^{k-1}\big)^{2}

since
\sigma^{k-1}_{p}
is a product probability measure on
H^{0}(X,L_{p})^{k-1}
. This finishes the estimation of
\mathbb{E}[I_{1}]
.

In order to get the upper bound for
\mathbb{E}[I_{2}]
, first observe that

(3.28)

\mathbb{E}[I_{2}]=\mathbb{E}\big\langle\big([Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]-\mathbb{E}[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]\big)\wedge\mathbb{E}[Z_{s^{k}_{p}}],\phi\big\rangle^{2}.

Also we see that

\displaystyle\big\langle\big([Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]-\mathbb{E}[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]\big)\wedge\mathbb{E}[Z_{s^{k}_{p}}],\phi\big\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}{\big\langle\big([Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]-\mathbb{E}[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]\big)\wedge[Z_{s^{k}_{p}}],\phi\big\rangle d\sigma_{p}(s^{k}_{p})}\Big\}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}{\Big\{\big\langle([Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]-\mathbb{E}[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}])\wedge[Z_{s^{k}_{p}}],\phi\big\rangle\Big\}^{2}d\sigma_{p}(s^{k}_{p})},

where, in the second line, we have used Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Analogous to the case for
\mathbb{E}[I_{1}]
, this time we consider the zero set of single
s^{k}_{p}
, namely, the set
Y:=\big\{x\in X:s^{k}_{p}(x)=0\big\}
. We first get

(3.29)

\mathbb{E}[I_{2}]\leq\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})^{k-1}}\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\Big\{\big\langle([Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]-\mathbb{E}[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}])\wedge[Z_{s^{k}_{p}}],\phi\big\rangle\Big\}^{2}d\sigma_{p}(s^{k}_{p})d\sigma^{k-1}_{p}(\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}).

As has been argued for
\mathbb{E}[I_{1}]
above, since
\langle(Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}])\wedge Z_{s^{k}_{p}},\phi\rangle=\langle(Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}-\mathbb{E}[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}])\big|_{Y},\phi\big|_{Y}\rangle
, by invoking Fubini-Tonelli’s theorem, (
3.29
) becomes

(3.30)

\mathbb{E}[I_{2}]\leq\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})^{k-1}}{\Big\{\big\langle([Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]-\mathbb{E}[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}])\big|_{Y},\phi\big|_{Y}\big\rangle\Big\}^{2}d\sigma^{k-1}_{p}(\Sigma_{p}^{k-1})d\sigma_{p}(s^{k}_{p})}.

The inner integral is, by definition, the variance of
Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}\big|_{Y}
, so (
3.30
) takes the following form:

(3.31)

\mathbb{E}[I_{2}]\leq\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}{\mathrm{Var}\big\langle[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]\big|_{Y},\phi|_{Y}\big\rangle d\sigma_{p}(s^{k}_{p})}.

By using the induction hypothesis for
Y
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\big\langle[Z_{\Sigma_{p}^{k-1}}]\big|_{Y},\phi|_{Y}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\leq(C_{p})^{2/\alpha}\,\Big(B_{\phi}\big|_{Y}(2A_{p})^{k-2}\,\mathrm{Vol}(Y)\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(C_{p})^{2/\alpha}\,\Big(B_{\phi}\ (2A_{p})^{k-2}\int_{X}\omega^{n-1}\wedge c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(C_{p})^{2/\alpha}\,\big(B_{\phi}\,\mathrm{Vol}(X)(2A_{p})^{k-1}\big)^{2},

where the second inequality is, as before, due to the inequality
B_{\phi}\big|_{Y}\leq B_{\phi}=b\,\|\phi\|_{\mathscr{C}^{2}}
and the last inequality is obtained by using the fact that
\int_{X}{\omega^{n-1}\wedge c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})}\leq 2A_{p}Vol(X)
for
p\geq\max{\{p_{0},p_{1}\}}
.
Now integrating over
H^{0}(X,L_{p})
and taking the last inequality just above into account implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[I_{2}]\leq(C_{p})^{2/\alpha}\,\big(B_{\phi}\,\mathrm{Vol}(X)(2A_{p})^{k-1}\big)^{2}

since
\sigma^{1}_{p}
is a product probability measure on
H^{0}(X,L_{p}).
Lastly, using the relation (
3.27
) and applying normalization end the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

∎

4.
Equidistribution of Zeros of Random Sections

In this section, we provide the proof for Theorem
1.2
. We divide the proof of it into three separate theorems. We begin with the asymptotic behavior of the expected zero distribution. Building on the variance estimates obtained earlier and the expected distribution, we prove that the zero sets possess the self-averaging property provided a summability condition is met.

4.1.
Expected Distribution of Zeros

We will prove expected distribution for the case of codimension one, followed by a generalization of the proof to handle higher codimensions.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(L_{p},h_{p})_{p\geq 1}
,
(X,\omega)
and
\sigma_{p}
be as defined above. Assume that they satisfy the conditions
(A)
,
(B)
and
(C1)
. If
\ \lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\frac{C_{p}^{1/\alpha}}{A_{p}}=0,
then

(4.1)

\frac{1}{A_{p}}\,\mathbb{E}[Z_{s_{p}}]\longrightarrow\omega

in the weak* topology of currents as
p\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

Let
\{S^{p}_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}^{d_{p}}
be an orthonormal basis of
H^{0}(X,L_{p})
and
s_{p}\in H^{0}(X,L_{p})
. Then

s_{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{p}}{a_{j}^{p}S^{p}_{j}}=\langle a,\Gamma_{p}\rangle,

where
\Gamma_{p}=(S_{1}^{p},\ldots,S^{p}_{d_{p}}),\ \text{and}\,a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d_{p}})\in\mathbb{C}^{d_{p}}
. Let
x\in X
,
U\subseteq X
be an open neighborhood of
x
and
e_{p}
be a holomorphic frame of
L_{p}
in
U
. Then locally
S^{p}_{j}=f_{j}e_{p}
, where
f_{j}
are holomorphic functions in
U
and so, by writing
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{d_{p}}),
we have

s_{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{p}}{a_{j}^{p}f_{j}e_{p}}=\langle a,f\rangle e_{p}.

By Poincaré-Lelong formula (
2.5
), on the neighborhood
U
, we have

(4.2)

[Z_{s_{p}}]=dd^{c}\log{|\langle a,f\rangle|}=dd^{c}\log|\langle a,\Gamma_{p}\rangle|_{h_{p}}+c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p}).

Let us now fix
\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{n-1,n-1}(X),
without lost of generality we may assume that
supp(\phi)\subset U
as the general case follows by covering
supp(\phi)
by
U_{\alpha}
’s and using the compatibility conditions. Using the definition of expectation and (
4.2
), we have

(4.3)

\frac{1}{A_{p}}\langle\mathbb{E}[Z_{s_{p}}],\phi\rangle=\frac{1}{A_{p}}\langle c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p}),\phi\rangle+\frac{1}{A_{p}}\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{X}{\log{|\langle a,\Gamma_{p}(x)\rangle|_{h_{p}}}}dd^{c}\phi(x)d\sigma_{p}(s_{p})

Let us denote the second term above by
I_{p}
, then by exploiting the fact that
\Gamma_{p}(x)=|\Gamma_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}u_{p}(x)
(so that
|u_{p}|_{h_{p}}=1
) we get

(4.4)

\displaystyle|I_{p}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{A_{p}}\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{X}{\log{|\Gamma_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}}|dd^{c}\phi(x)|d\sigma_{p}(s_{p})+\frac{1}{A_{p}}\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{X}{\big|\log{|\langle a,u_{p}(x)\rangle|_{h_{p}}}\big|}}\big|dd^{c}\phi(x)\big|\big|d\sigma_{p}(s_{p})

Utilizing (
2.2
) and the simple observation that
\frac{1}{A_{p}}\log K_{p}(x)\rightarrow 0
as
p\rightarrow\infty
from (
1.3
),

(4.5)

\frac{1}{A_{p}}\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{X}\log{|\Gamma_{p}(x)|_{h_{p}}}dd^{c}\phi(x)d\sigma_{p}(s)\leq\frac{B_{\phi}}{2A_{p}}\int_{X}\log K_{p}(x)\omega^{n}(x)\rightarrow 0

as
p\rightarrow\infty.
Also, using the identification
H^{0}(X,L_{p})\simeq\mathbb{C}^{d_{p}}
, the moment condition
(C2)
along with Hölder’s inequality and (
2.2
), we get

(4.6)

\int_{X}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{d_{p}}}{\big|\log{|\langle a,\rho_{p}(x)\rangle|}\big|}d\sigma_{p}(a)dd^{c}\phi(x)\leq(C_{p})^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}B_{\phi}\,Vol(X),

where

\rho_{p}(x)=\Big(\frac{f_{1}(x)}{\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{d_{p}}{|f_{j}(x)|^{2}}}},\ldots,\frac{f_{d_{p}}(x)}{\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{d_{p}}{|f_{j}(x)|^{2}}}}\Big).

It follows from Fubini-Tonelli’s theorem that

\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{X}{\big|\log{|\langle a,u_{p}(x)\rangle|_{h_{p}}}\big|}\big|dd^{c}\phi(x)\big|d\sigma_{p}(s_{p})=\int_{X}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{d_{p}}}{\big|\log{|\langle a,\rho_{p}(x)\rangle|}\big|}d\sigma_{p}(a)\big|dd^{c}\phi(x)\big|.

Consequently, employing the given hypothesis, we deduce that

(4.7)

\frac{1}{A_{p}}\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\int_{X}{\big|\log{|\langle a,u_{p}(x)\rangle|_{h_{p}}}\big|}\big|dd^{c}\phi(x)\big|d\sigma_{p}(s_{p})\leq\frac{C_{p}^{1/{\alpha}}}{A_{p}}\,B_{\phi}\,Vol(X)\rightarrow 0,

as
p\rightarrow\infty
. In turn, (
4.5
) and (
4.7
) imply that
I_{p}\rightarrow 0,
as
p\rightarrow\infty.
Finally, by using (
1.1
) we have that
\frac{1}{A_{p}}\langle c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p}),\phi\rangle\rightarrow\langle\omega,\phi\rangle,
thus concluding the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Under the assumptions
(A)
,
(B)
,
(C1)
-
(C2)
, and

(4.8)

\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}{\frac{C_{p}^{1/\alpha}}{A_{p}}}=0,

we have

\mathbb{E}\big[\widehat{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big]\longrightarrow\omega^{k}

in the weak* topology of currents as
p\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

We follow the lines of the proof of Proposition 3.5 in
[
12
]
. There exists
c>0
such that for every
\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{n-k,n-k}(X)
,
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, and every real
(1,1)
-form
\theta
on
X
, the following inequality holds

(4.9)

-c\|\phi\|_{\mathscr{C}^{0}}\|\theta\|_{\mathscr{C}^{0}}\omega^{n-k+1}\leq\phi\wedge\theta\leq c\|\phi\|_{\mathscr{C}^{0}}\|\theta\|_{\mathscr{C}^{0}}\omega^{n-k+1}.

For
p>\max\{p_{0},p_{1}\}
, define
\Theta_{p}:=\mathbb{E}[Z_{s^{1}_{p}}]
. Using (
3.18
) and Theorem
3.3
, we write

\mathbb{E}[\widehat{Z}_{\Sigma^{k}_{p}}]=[\mathbb{E}[\widehat{Z}_{s^{1}_{p}}]]^{k}=\frac{\Theta_{p}^{k}}{A_{p}^{k}}.

Now, define

(4.10)

\mathcal{Y}_{p}:=\frac{\Theta_{p}^{k}}{A_{p}^{k}}-\omega^{k},\quad\upsilon_{p}:=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{\Theta_{p}^{j}}{A_{p}^{j}}\wedge\omega^{k-1-j},\quad\beta_{p}:=\frac{c_{1}(L_{p},h_{p})}{A_{p}}-\omega.

Note that
\upsilon_{p}
is a positive
(k-1,k-1)
-current from Theorem
3.3
. By the diophantine approximation (
1.1
) and (
3.18
), we obtain:

(4.11)

\|\beta_{p}\|_{\mathscr{C}^{0}}\leq\frac{C_{0}}{A_{p}^{a}},\quad\frac{\Theta_{p}}{A_{p}}-\omega=\beta_{p}+\frac{1}{2A_{p}}dd^{c}\log K_{p}+\frac{1}{A_{p}}dd^{c}\Bigg(\int_{s_{p}\in H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\log\big|\langle a,u_{p}(x)\rangle\big|_{h_{p}}d\sigma_{p}(s_{p})\Bigg).

For
\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{n-k,n-k}(X)
, substituting (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) yields

(4.12)

\langle\mathcal{Y}_{p},\phi\rangle=\langle\Big(\frac{\Theta_{p}}{A_{p}}-\omega\Big)\wedge\upsilon_{p},\phi\rangle=\int_{X}\upsilon_{p}\wedge\beta_{p}\wedge\phi+\int_{X}\frac{\log K_{p}}{2A_{p}}\upsilon_{p}\wedge dd^{c}\phi+\int_{X}\Bigg(\int_{H^{0}(X,L_{p})}\frac{\log|\langle a,u_{p}\rangle|_{h_{p}}}{A_{p}}d\sigma_{p}\Bigg)\upsilon_{p}\wedge dd^{c}\phi.

Continuing as in the proof of Proposition 3.5 of
[
12
]
and applying (
2.2
), (
4.9
), (
2.1
) and (
1.5
) where appropriate, we find

\big|\langle\mathbb{E}[\widehat{Z}_{\Sigma^{k}_{p}}]-\omega^{k},\phi\rangle\big|=\big|\langle\mathcal{Y}_{p},\phi\rangle\big|\leq C^{\prime}_{n}\|\phi\|_{\mathscr{C}^{2}}\mathrm{Vol}(X)\Big(\frac{\log A_{p}}{A_{p}}+\frac{C_{p}^{1/\alpha}}{A_{p}}+A_{p}^{-a}\Big),

for all
p\geq\max\{p_{0},p_{1},p^{\prime}\}
, where
C^{\prime}_{n}>0
is a constant depending only on the dimension
n
.
Thus,
\mathbb{E}[\widehat{Z}_{\Sigma^{k}_{p}}]\to\omega^{k}
in the weak* topology of currents as
p\to\infty
, completing the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold of
\textup{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}X=n
and let
(L_{p},h_{p})_{p\geq 1},
be a sequence of Hermitian holomorphic line bundles on
X
with
\mathscr{C}^{2}
metrics
h_{p}
. Assume that the conditions
(A)
,
(B)
and
(C1)-(C2)
hold. If

\ \sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{C_{p}^{2/\alpha}}{A_{p}^{2}}<\infty,

then for
\sigma_{\infty}^{k}
- almost every sequence
\mathbf{\Sigma_{k}}=\left\{\Sigma_{p}^{k}\right\}_{p\geq 1}\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{k},

\big[\widehat{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big]\longrightarrow\omega^{k}

in the weak* topology of currents as
p\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

Fix
\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{n-k,n-k}(X),
and pick
\mathbf{\Sigma_{k}}=\left\{\Sigma_{p}^{k}\right\}_{p\geq 1}\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{k}.
Let us examine the non-negative random variables

(4.13)

\mathcal{X}_{p}(\mathbf{\Sigma_{k}}):=\big\langle\big[\widehat{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\widehat{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big],\phi\big\rangle^{2}\geq 0

By appealing to the equivalent characterization of variance, notice that

(4.14)

\int_{\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{k}}\mathcal{X}_{p}(\mathbf{\Sigma_{k}})d\sigma_{\infty}^{k}(\mathbf{\Sigma_{k}})=\mathrm{Var}\big\langle\big[\widehat{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big],\,\phi\big\rangle

Using Theorem
1.1
along with the summability condition given by the hypothesis, we get

(4.15)

\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{k}}\mathcal{X}_{p}(\mathbf{\Sigma_{k}})d\sigma_{\infty}^{k}(\mathbf{\Sigma_{k}})=\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Var}\big\langle\big[\widehat{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big],\,\phi\big\rangle<\infty

By (
4.14
) above and invoking Beppo-Levi Theorem from the standard measure theory, we get

(4.16)

\int_{\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{k}}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{X}_{p}(\mathbf{\Sigma_{k}})d\sigma_{\infty}^{k}(\mathbf{\Sigma_{k}})=\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Var}\big\langle\big[\widehat{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big],\,\phi\big\rangle<\infty

This implies that, for
\sigma_{\infty}^{k}
- almost every sequence
\mathbf{\Sigma_{k}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{k}
of systems, the series
\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{X}_{p}(\mathbf{\Sigma_{k}})
converges, leading to the conclusion that
\mathcal{X}_{p}\rightarrow 0

\sigma_{\infty}^{k}
-almost surely. By definition (
4.13
) of random variables
\mathcal{X}_{p}
this also indicates that

(4.17)

\big\langle\big[\widehat{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\widehat{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big],\phi\big\rangle\rightarrow 0

\sigma_{\infty}^{k}
-almost surely. Combining this last information with Theorem
4.2
, we conclude that for
\sigma_{\infty}^{k}
-almost every sequence,

(4.18)

\big[\widehat{Z}_{\Sigma_{p}^{k}}\big]\longrightarrow\omega^{k}

in the weak* topology of currents as
p\rightarrow\infty
.
∎

5.
Some Special Cases

In
[
3
]
, certain types of measures which satisfy the assumption
(C2)
have been investigated as special cases. We will now provide some insights concerned with most of these measures in connection with Theorem
1.1
and
1.2
. The first two measures to be considered here will be the Gaussian and the Fubini-Study measures, both of which are unitary invariant measures that come with certain advantages in estimations.

5.1.
Gaussian and Fubini-Study

In what follows,
\lambda_{n}
represents the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
(identified with
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
). We will present the variance estimate simultaneously for both Gaussian and Fubini-Study cases, with detailed explanations provided for the Gaussian case as the computations are exactly the same. It turns out that, in these cases, the constants
C_{p}
reduce to the ones independent of
p
and Theorem
1.1
remains valid for every
\alpha\geq 1
. The standard Gaussian measure is precisely defined as follows, for
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

(5.1)

6.
appendix

In this section, by adapting the proof of Proposition 3.2 in
[
11
]
, we present a general version of Bertini’s theorem applicable to any probability measure
\sigma_{d}
defined on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
that assigns zero mass to pluripolar subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. As a result, we demonstrate that the intersection current is almost surely well-defined with respect to the product measure induced by
\sigma_{d}
.

Definition 6.1
.

The analytic subsets
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k},\ k\leq n
of a compact complex manifold
X
of dimension
n
are said to be in general position if
\mathrm{codim}A_{i_{1}}\cap\ldots\cap A_{i_{m}}\geq m
for every
1\leq m\leq k
and
1\leq i_{1}<\ldots<i_{m}\leq k.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
L\to X
be a holomorphic line bundle over a compact complex manifold
X
with
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}{X}=n
. Suppose that:

(i)

V
is a subspace of
H^{0}(X,L)
with a basis
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{d}\}
, and the base locus
\text{Bs}(V)=\{x\in X:S_{1}(x)=\ldots=S_{d}(x)=0\}\subseteq X
such that
\dim{\text{Bs}(V)}\leq n-k
.

(ii)

Z(t)=\{x\in X:\sum_{j=1}^{d}t_{j}S_{j}(x)=0\}
, where
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d})\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
.

If
\sigma_{d}^{k}
is the product measure on
(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{k}
induced by the probability measure
\sigma_{d}
on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
coming from the identification
V\simeq\mathbb{C}^{d}
, then analytic sets
Z(t^{1}),\ldots,Z(t^{k})
are in general position for
\sigma_{d}^{k}
-almost every
(t^{1},\ldots,t^{k})\in(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{k}
.

Proof.

The proof will be based on induction on
k
. Let

(6.1)

H_{k}:=\{(t^{1},\ldots,t^{k})\in(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{k}:\dim{Z(t^{1})\cap\ldots\cap Z(t^{k})\cap Bs(V)}\leq n-k\}.

We start with the case
k=1
. If
Z(t^{1})\cap Bs(V)=\emptyset
, then
Z(t^{1})
is a complex submanifold of dimension
n-1
whatever
t^{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
is chosen, that is
\{t^{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:\dim{Z(t^{1})}\leq n-1\}=\mathbb{C}^{d}
. If
Z(t^{1})\cap Bs(V)\neq\emptyset
, corresponding to the set
H_{1}=\{t^{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:\dim{Z(t^{1})\cap Bs(V)}\leq n-1\}
, we consider the decomposition of the base locus

(6.2)

Bs(V)=\bigcup_{l=1}^{N_{0}}{E_{l}\cup Y},

where
E_{l}
are the irreducible components of
Bs(V)
with
\dim{E_{l}}=n-1
and
\dim{Y}\leq n-2
. The set
\{t^{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:E_{l}\subset Z(t^{1})\}
has to be a proper linear subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
, because, if
E_{l}\subset Z(t^{1})
for all
t^{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, then since
E_{l}\subset Bs(V)
, we must have that
\dim{Z(t^{1})}\geq\dim{Z(t^{1})\cap Bs(V)}\geq n-1
, which is a contradiction since any single analytic variety is always in general position. Therefore, for any point
t^{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\backslash H_{1}
, we get
\dim{Z(t^{1})\cap Bs(V)}\geq n-1
. Since
Z(t^{1})\cap Bs(V)
is an analytic subvariety of
Bs(V)
, there exists
l\in\{1,\ldots,N_{0}\}
such that
E_{l}\subset Z(t^{1})\cap Bs(V)
, so we can write

\mathbb{C}^{d}\backslash H_{1}=\bigcup_{l=1}^{N_{0}}{\{t^{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:E_{l}\subset Z(t^{1})\}}.

Now since any proper linear subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
is pluripolar and
\sigma_{d}
puts no mass on pluripolar sets, it follows that
\sigma_{d}(\mathbb{C}^{d}\backslash H_{1})=0
, which completes the initial step for induction. Suppose that
\sigma^{k}_{d}(H_{k})=1
for all
H_{k}
defined as in (
6.1
). Let

(6.3)

H_{k+1}=\{(t^{1},\ldots,t^{k+1})\in(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{k+1}:\dim{Z(t^{1})\cap\ldots\cap Z(t^{k+1})\cap Bs(V)}\leq n-k-1\}.

We need to show that
\sigma^{k+1}_{d}(H_{k+1})=1
. For this purpose, we show that the
\sigma^{k+1}_{d}
-measure of the complement set
H^{c}_{k+1}
is zero. First, let us fix
t=(t^{1},\ldots,t^{k})\in H_{k}
. Define
Z(t):=Z(t^{1})\cap\ldots\cap Z(t^{k})
and

(6.4)

G(t):=\{t^{k+1}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:\dim{Z(t)\cap Bs(V)\cap Z(t^{k+1})}\geq n-k-1\}.

It will suffice to prove that
\sigma_{d}(G(t))=0
. These sets
G(t)
are called the slices of the set
H^{c}_{k+1}
. Let

(6.5)

Z(t)\cap Bs(V)=\bigcup_{k=1}^{N_{0}}{E_{l}\cup Y},

where
E_{l}
are, as in the case
k=1
, the irreducible components of
Z(t)\cap Bs(V)
with
\dim{E_{l}}=n-k
and
\dim{Y}=n-k-1
. If
t^{k+1}\in G(t)
, then
Z(t)\cap Z(t_{k+1})\cap Bs(V)
is an analytic subset of
Z(t)\cap Bs(V)
with
\dim{Z(t)\cap Z(t_{k+1})\cap Bs(V)}=n-k
, and this gives that there is some
l\in\{1,\ldots N_{0}\}
such that

(6.6)

E_{l}\subset Z(t)\cap Z(t_{k+1})\cap Bs(V).

Hence we have

(6.7)

G(t)=\bigcup_{l=1}^{N_{0}}{A_{l}(t)},\,\,A_{l}(t):=\{t^{k+1}\in\mathbb{C}^{d}:E_{l}\subset Z(t^{k+1})\}.

Now we see that not all sections become zero on
E_{l}
. Indeed, if it were not so, by arguing as in the case
k=1
above,
E_{l}\subset Bs(V)
would imply that
\dim{Z(t)\cap Bs(V)}\geq n-k
, contradicting
t\in H_{k}
. We may then assume that
S_{d}\not\equiv 0
on
E_{l}
. As before, by examining, this time, the slices of
A_{l}(t)
, for any
(t^{k+1}_{1},\ldots,t^{k+1}_{d-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{d-1}
, there exist at most one
h\in\mathbb{C}
such that
(t^{k+1}_{1},\ldots,t^{k+1}_{d-1},h)\in A_{l}(t)
, otherwise, if there exist two different elements

with this property, we have

\displaystyle t^{k+1}_{1}S_{1}+\ldots+t^{k+1}_{d-1}S_{d-1}+hS_{d}=0

\displaystyle t^{k+1}_{1}S_{1}+\ldots+t^{k+1}_{d-1}S_{d-1}+h^{\prime}S_{d}=0,

which immediately implies that
S_{d}\equiv 0
on
E_{l}
, which is a contradiction. Thus,
\sigma_{d}(A_{l}(t))=0
. This implies that
\sigma_{d}(G(t))=0
, which finishes the proof.

Abstract




Neutrino oscillation phenomenology and
impact of Professor Masatoshi Koshiba

Osamu Yasuda

Abstract

Neutrino oscillation
phenomenology is briefly reviewed,
and impact of the late Professor Masatoshi Koshiba
on research on neutrino oscillation
is discussed from the viewpoint of phenomenology.

1
Introduction

Since the announcement of the discovery of atmospheric
neutrino oscillation by the Superkamiokande Collaboration
[
1
]

in 1998, there has been remarkable progress in research on
neutrino oscillation. In this article I briefly describe
neutrino oscillation phenomenology
and I emphasize the impact of Professor Koshiba
on neutrino oscillation study.

2
Three flavor neutrino oscillation

2.1
Preliminary

If we assume neutrino mass, we have to describe
neutrinos in terms of the Dirac equation
for spinors with masses.
The mass eigenstates
\nu_{j}~(j=1,2,3)
of neutrinos
with mass
m_{j}
in vacuum
can be described by the free Dirac equation.
The Dirac equation of a spinor with mass
m
and
momentum
\vec{p}

has the energy eigenvalues
E,E,-E,-E

where
E\equiv\sqrt{\vec{p}^{\,2}+m^{2}}
.
If we extract one component with the positive energy of
\nu_{j}
,
then the mass eigenstates
\nu_{j}~(j=1,2,3)
of neutrinos
satisfy the Dirac equation in vacuum

\displaystyle\hskip-116.6563pt\displaystyle i{d{\ }\over dt}\nu_{j}(t)=E_{j}~\nu_{j}(t)\,,\quad E_{j}\equiv\sqrt{\vec{p}^{\,2}+m_{j}^{2}}.

(1)

On the other hand, the flavor eigenstates of neutrinos
(
(\nu_{e},\nu_{\mu},\nu_{\tau})^{T}
)
are those which can be observed
in the processes

\nu_{e}+n\to e^{-}+p
,

\nu_{\mu}+n\to\mu^{-}+p
,

\nu_{\tau}+n\to\tau^{-}+p
.
The mass (
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3})^{T}
) and
flavor (
(\nu_{e},\nu_{\mu},\nu_{\tau})^{T}
) eigenstates
of the neutrino sector are related by

\displaystyle\hskip-173.5618pt\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nu_{e}\\
\nu_{\mu}\\
\nu_{\tau}\end{array}\right)=U\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nu_{1}\\
\nu_{2}\\
\nu_{3}\end{array}\right)\,,

where

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptU\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
0&c_{23}&s_{23}\\
0&-s_{23}&c_{23}\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}c_{13}&0&s_{13}e^{-i\delta}\\
0&1&0\\
-s_{13}e^{i\delta}&0&c_{13}\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}c_{12}&s_{12}&0\\
-s_{12}&c_{12}&0\\
0&0&1\\
\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-17.07164pt=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}c_{12}c_{13}&s_{12}c_{13}&s_{13}e^{-i\delta}\\
-s_{12}c_{23}-c_{12}s_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta}&c_{12}c_{23}-s_{12}s_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta}&s_{23}c_{13}\\
s_{12}s_{23}-c_{12}c_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta}&-c_{12}s_{23}-s_{12}c_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta}&c_{23}c_{13}\\
\end{array}\right)

is the
3\times 3
unitary neutrino mixing
matrix
[
2
,
3
]
,

\theta_{12}
,
\theta_{23}
,
\theta_{13}

are three mixing angles,

\delta
is a CP phase,
and
c_{jk}\equiv\cos\theta_{jk}
,
s_{jk}\equiv\sin\theta_{jk}
.
It is known that the propagation of neutrinos
in matter is described with the matter effect

[
4
,
5
]
:

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581pt\displaystyle i{d\over dt}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nu_{e}(t)\\
\nu_{\mu}(t)\\
\nu_{\tau}(t)\end{array}\right)=M~\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nu_{e}(t)\\
\nu_{\mu}(t)\\
\nu_{\tau}(t)\end{array}\right)\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581ptM\equiv[U\,\mbox{\rm diag}(E_{1},E_{2},E_{3})\,U^{-1}+\mbox{\rm diag}(A_{\mbox{\tiny CC}}+A_{\mbox{\tiny NC}},A_{\mbox{\tiny NC}},A_{\mbox{\tiny NC}})]

\displaystyle\hskip 8.53581pt=[U\,\mbox{\rm diag}(0,\Delta E_{21},\Delta E_{31})\,U^{-1}+\mbox{\rm diag}(A_{\mbox{\tiny CC}},0,0)+(E_{1}+A_{\mbox{\tiny NC}})\,{\bf 1}]\,,

(30)

where
\Delta E_{jk}\equiv E_{j}-E_{k}\simeq(m_{j}^{2}-m_{k}^{2})/2|\vec{p}|\equiv\Delta m_{jk}^{2}/2|\vec{p}|\simeq\Delta m_{jk}^{2}/2E
,

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptA_{\mbox{\tiny CC}}\equiv\sqrt{2}\,G_{F}N_{e}=\left[\frac{\rho}{2.6~\mathrm{(g\cdot cm^{-3})}}\right]\cdot\left(\frac{Y_{e}}{0.5}\right)\cdot 1.0\times 10^{-13}\,\mbox{\rm eV}

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptA_{\mbox{\tiny NC}}\equiv-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,G_{F}N_{n}=-\left[\frac{\rho}{2.6~\mathrm{(g\cdot cm^{-3})}}\right]\cdot\left(\frac{1-Y_{e}}{0.5}\right)\cdot 5.0\times 10^{-14}\,\mbox{\rm eV}

stand for
the matter effect due to
the charged and neutral current interactions,

N_{e}
and
N_{n}
stand for the density
of electrons and neutrons,
G_{F}=1.166\times 10^{-5}
GeV
-2
is
the Fermi coupling constant, and
Y_{e}=N_{p}/(N_{p}+N_{n})
is the
relative number density of electrons in matter,
where
N_{p}
represents the number of protons
per unit volume.
Since the term which is proportional to the
3\times 3
identity matrix
{\bf 1}

only affects the over all phase of the probability
amplitude
A(\nu_{\alpha}\to\nu_{\beta})
,

(E_{1}+A_{\mbox{\tiny NC}})\,{\bf 1}
in Eq. (
30
)
can be ignored.

2.2
Mixing angles and mass squared differences

If the density is constant, then Eq. (
30
) can be
formally diagonalized as

\displaystyle\hskip-173.5618ptM=\tilde{U}\,{\mbox{\rm diag}}\left(\tilde{E}_{1},\tilde{E}_{2},\tilde{E}_{3}\right)\,\tilde{U}^{-1},

where
\tilde{E}_{j}~(j=1,2,3)
are
the energy eigenvalues of
M
in matter, and

\tilde{U}
is a unitary matrix which diagonalizes
M
.
The probability
P(\nu_{\alpha}\rightarrow\nu_{\beta})

can be expressed as

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptP(\nu_{\alpha}\rightarrow\nu_{\beta})=\delta_{\alpha\beta}-4\sum_{j<k}\mbox{\rm Re}(\tilde{U}_{\alpha j}\tilde{U}_{\beta j}^{\ast}\tilde{U}_{\alpha k}^{\ast}\tilde{U}_{\beta k})\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta\tilde{E}_{jk}L}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt-2\sum_{j<k}\mbox{\rm Im}(\tilde{U}_{\alpha j}\tilde{U}_{\beta j}^{\ast}\tilde{U}_{\alpha k}^{\ast}\tilde{U}_{\beta k})\sin(\Delta\tilde{E}_{jk}L)\,,

(31)

where
\Delta\tilde{E}_{jk}\equiv\tilde{E}_{j}-\tilde{E}_{k}
.

From the parametrization (
2.1
),
the simplest mixing angle to determine
is
\theta_{13}
. If we consider the
disappearance experiment of reactor neutrinos
with a baseline length
L
=2 km,
where the mean neutrino energy is
E\simeq
4 MeV,
and if we assume that the contribution from
the smaller mass squared difference

\Delta m_{21}^{2}L/2E
is small,
then, because the matter effect, which
appears in the form of
A_{\mbox{\tiny CC}}L/2\sim L/(4000~\mbox{\rm km})
in the
oscillation probability, can be ignored,
and we obtain

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055ptP(\bar{\nu}_{e}\rightarrow\bar{\nu}_{e})\simeq 1-4|U_{e3}|^{2}(1-|U_{e3}|^{2})\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta m_{31}^{2}L}{4E}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526pt=1-\sin^{2}2\theta_{13}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta m_{31}^{2}L}{4E}\right)\,.

(32)

Eq. (
32
) has the same form as the oscillation
probability in the two flavor framework, and
one can get information on
\theta_{13}

from the result which is expressed in terms of the two flavor
oscillation analysis.
From the negative result of the CHOOZ reactor neutrino
experiment
[
6
]
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\sin^{2}2\theta_{13}\lesssim 0.15\quad\mbox{\rm for}~|\Delta m_{31}^{2}|=2.5\times 10^{-3}\mbox{\rm eV}^{2}~(90\%~\mbox{\rm CL})

(33)

Eq. (
33
) shows that
\theta_{13}
is small,
so we can put
\theta_{13}\to 0
in the zeroth approximation.
If we consider the case where

|\Delta E_{21}|\simeq|\Delta m_{21}^{2}/2E|\ll|\Delta E_{31}|\simeq|\Delta m_{31}^{2}/2E|\sim A_{\mbox{\tiny CC}}
,
then the term which is proportional to
\Delta E_{21}

in Eq. (
30
) can be ignored, so
\theta_{12}

as well as
\theta_{13}
disappear, the
\nu_{e}
state
decouples from the
\nu_{\mu}
and
\nu_{\tau}
states
(i.e., the matter effect disappears from
the sector of
\nu_{\mu}
and
\nu_{\tau}
), and we get

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055ptP(\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{\mu})\simeq 1-4|U_{\mu 3}|^{2}(1-|U_{\mu 3}|^{2})\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta m_{31}^{2}L}{4E}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 2.84526pt\simeq 1-\sin^{2}2\theta_{23}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta m_{31}^{2}L}{4E}\right)\,.

(34)

Again Eq. (
34
) has the same form as the oscillation
probability in the two flavor framework.
From the results of atmospheric neutrino measurements
of the Superkamiokande experiment
[
1
]
,
we have

\displaystyle\hskip-199.16928pt\sin^{2}\theta_{23}\simeq 0.5

(35)

\displaystyle\hskip-199.16928pt|\Delta m_{31}^{2}|\simeq 2.5\times 10^{-3}\mbox{\rm eV}^{2}\,.

(36)

Notice that the sign of
\Delta m_{31}^{2}
cannot be determined
from Eq. (
34
) because it is invariant under the
change of the sign of
\Delta m_{31}^{2}
.
The mass pattern for
\Delta m_{31}^{2}>0
(
\Delta m_{31}^{2}<0
)
is called Normal (Inverted) Ordering, and is depicted in Fig.
1
.

Figure 1:
Two mass patterns:
(a) Normal Ordering (
\Delta m^{2}_{31}>0
),
(b) Inverted Ordering (
\Delta m^{2}_{31}<0
).
Both mass patterns are allowed as of 2021.

As for (
\theta_{12}
,
\Delta m_{21}^{2}
), there are two ways
to determine them. The first one is from KamLAND
[
7
]
,
the long baseline reactor experiment.
If we put
\theta_{13}\to 0
again, then
in the zeroth approximation we have

A_{\mbox{\tiny CC}}\ll|\Delta E_{21}|\simeq|\Delta m_{21}^{2}/2E|\ll|\Delta E_{31}|\simeq|\Delta m_{31}^{2}/2E|
,
because the matter effect, which
appears in the form of
A_{\mbox{\tiny CC}}L\sim L/(2000~\mbox{\rm km})
in the
oscillation probability, can be ignored.
Hence we obtain the oscillation probability

\displaystyle\hskip-62.59605ptP(\bar{\nu}_{e}\rightarrow\bar{\nu}_{e})\simeq 1-4|U_{e1}|^{2}|U_{e2}|^{2}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta m_{21}^{2}L}{4E}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69054pt\simeq 1-\sin^{2}2\theta_{12}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\Delta m_{21}^{2}L}{4E}\right)\,,

(37)

which has the same form as the oscillation
probability in the two flavor framework.
From the KamLAND result, we get

\displaystyle\hskip-176.40707pt\sin^{2}\theta_{12}\simeq 0.3

(38)

\displaystyle\hskip-176.40707pt|\Delta m_{21}^{2}|\simeq 8\times 10^{-5}~\mbox{\rm eV}^{2}\,.

(39)

The other way to determine (
\theta_{12}
,
\Delta m_{21}^{2}
)
is from solar neutrino observations.
In the case of solar neutrinos, we have to take into
account the fact that the density of electrons varies
(we know now that it varies adiabatically) from the
production point of neutrinos near the center of the Sun
all the way to the Earth.
If we assume that the electron density varies adiabatically,
then the oscillation probability
P(\nu_{e}\rightarrow\nu_{e})

is given by

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0ptP(\nu_{e}\rightarrow\nu_{e})=\sum_{j,k}\tilde{U}_{ej}(L)\tilde{U}_{ek}^{\ast}(L)\tilde{U}_{ej}^{\ast}(0)\tilde{U}_{ek}(0)\exp\left(-i\int_{0}^{L}dt~\Delta\tilde{E}_{jk}\right)\,,

(40)

where
t=0
(
t=L
) stands for the production (detection) point,
and we discuss here detection of solar neutrinos during the day,
i.e., the density at the detection point is approximately zero.
Since the distance
L
between the Sun and the Earth is
literally astronomically large, we have

|\int_{0}^{L}dt~\Delta\tilde{E}_{jk}|\gg 1~(j\neq k)
,
so by averaging over rapid oscillations, we can put

\exp(-i\int_{0}^{L}dt~\Delta\tilde{E}_{jk})\rightarrow\delta_{jk}
.
Thus we get

\displaystyle\hskip-73.97716ptP(\nu_{e}\rightarrow\nu_{e})=\sum_{j}\tilde{U}_{ej}(L)\tilde{U}_{ej}^{\ast}(L)\tilde{U}_{ej}^{\ast}(0)\tilde{U}_{ej}(0)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt=\sum_{j}|U_{ej}|^{2}|\tilde{U}_{ej}(0)|^{2}\,,

(41)

where the mixing matrix

\tilde{U}(L)
at the detection point
t=L

was replaced by the one
U
in vacuum
because the density there is assumed to be zero.
If take the limit
\theta_{13}\to 0
, then
Eq. (
41
) reduces to the two flavor case.
In the two flavor case, the effective mixing angle
is given by

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\cos 2\tilde{\theta}_{12}\equiv\frac{1}{\Delta\tilde{E}_{21}}\left(\Delta E_{21}\cos 2\theta_{12}-A_{\mbox{\tiny CC}}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\Delta\tilde{E}_{21}\equiv\left\{\left[\Delta E_{21}\cos 2\theta_{12}-A_{\mbox{\tiny CC}})\right]^{2}+\left(\Delta E_{21}\sin 2\theta_{12}\right)^{2}\right\}^{1/2}\,.

Since the effective mixing matrix elements in the two flavor case
are given by
|\tilde{U}_{e1}(0)|^{2}=\cos^{2}\tilde{\theta}_{12}

and
|\tilde{U}_{e2}(0)|^{2}=\sin^{2}\tilde{\theta}_{12}
,
we have

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274ptP(\nu_{e}\rightarrow\nu_{e})\simeq\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\cos 2\theta_{12}\frac{\Delta E_{21}\cos 2\theta_{12}-A_{\mbox{\tiny CC}}(0)}{\Delta\tilde{E}_{21}(0)}\right)\,,

(42)

where the argument
(0)
stands for the quantity
evaluated at the production point of neutrinos.
From the oscillation probability (
42
)
and the data of various experiments which measure

P(\nu_{e}\rightarrow\nu_{e})
at different solar neutrino
energies, the following results are obtained
[
8
]
:

\displaystyle\hskip-176.40707pt\sin^{2}\theta_{12}\simeq 0.3

(43)

\displaystyle\hskip-176.40707pt\Delta m_{21}^{2}\simeq 6\times 10^{-5}\mbox{\rm eV}^{2}\,,

(44)

which are approximately consistent with the results
(
38
) and (
39
)
from KamLAND. Notice that the result from
solar neutrino measurements is powerful
to determine the sign of
\Delta m_{21}^{2}

because of the matter effect, while
KamLAND gives little information
on the sign of
\Delta m_{21}^{2}

since the oscillation probability
(
37
) for KamLAND is basically
that in vacuum and therefore it is invariant
under the change of the sign of
\Delta m_{21}^{2}
.

2.3
Parameter degeneracy

To determine the CP phase
\delta
,
precise measurements of the oscillation
probabilities are required, since
the effect of
\delta
appears only
in the combination of
s_{13}e^{\pm i\delta}

as we can see from Eq. (
2.1
).
It is expected that long baseline accelerator-based
experiments are advantageous to perform
precise measurements, because one can
control the baseline length and the neutrino energy.
Conventional neutrino beams, which can be obtained
from pion decays, are
\nu_{\mu}
and
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}
,
so
\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{e}
and
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\to\bar{\nu}_{e}

are the two major channels to determine the CP phase.
Now the question is, given the appearance probabilities

P(\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{e})=\mbox{\rm constant}\equiv P
and

P(\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\to\bar{\nu}_{e})=\mbox{\rm constant}\equiv\bar{P}

in addition to the disappearance probabilities

P(\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{\mu})=
constant and

P(\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\to\bar{\nu}_{\nu})=
constant,
is it possible to determine
\delta
uniquely?
The answer to this question turns out to be negative
because there can be 8 possible values for
\delta
,
and this is called 8-fold parameter degeneracy.
In the approximation to second order in
\theta_{13}

and
\Delta m_{21}^{2}
, the appearance probabilities
can be written as
[
9
,
10
]

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636ptP(\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{e})=x^{2}F^{2}+2\,\mbox{\rm sign}(\Delta m_{31}^{2})\,xyFg\cos\left[\delta+\mbox{\rm sign}(\Delta m_{31}^{2})\,\Delta\right]+y^{2}g^{2}=P

(45)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636ptP(\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\to\bar{\nu}_{e})=x^{2}\bar{F}^{2}+2\,\mbox{\rm sign}(\Delta m_{31}^{2})\,xy\bar{F}g\cos\left[\delta-\mbox{\rm sign}(\Delta m_{31}^{2})\,\Delta\right]+y^{2}g^{2}=\bar{P}\,,

(46)

where

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636ptx\equiv s_{23}\sin 2\theta_{13}

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pty\equiv\alpha c_{23}\sin 2\theta_{12}

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt(F,\bar{F})\equiv\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}(f,\bar{f})~~\mbox{\rm for NO}\\
(\bar{f},f)~~\mbox{\rm for IO}\\
\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}f\\
\bar{f}\end{array}\right\}\equiv\frac{\sin\left(\Delta\mp A_{\mbox{\tiny CC}}L/2\right)}{\left(1\mp A_{\mbox{\tiny CC}}L/2\Delta\right)}\,,

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636ptg\equiv\frac{\sin\left(A_{\mbox{\tiny CC}}L/2\right)}{A_{\mbox{\tiny CC}}L/2\Delta}

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\Delta\equiv\frac{|\Delta m_{31}^{2}|L}{4E}=1.27\times\frac{(|\Delta m_{31}^{2}|/{\rm eV^{2}})(L/{\rm km})}{(E/{\rm GeV})}

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\alpha\equiv\left|\frac{\Delta m^{2}_{21}}{\Delta m^{2}_{31}}\right|

Defining
X\equiv\sin^{2}2\theta_{13}
,
Y\equiv 1/s^{2}_{23}
,
and eliminating
\delta
, we obtain the following
expression from Eqs. (
45
) and
(
46
)
[
11
]
:

\displaystyle\hskip-36.98857pt16CX(Y-1)=\frac{1}{\cos^{2}\Delta}\left[\left(\frac{P-C}{F}+\frac{\bar{P}-C}{\bar{F}}\right)(Y-1)-(F+\bar{F})X+\frac{P}{F}+\frac{\bar{P}}{\bar{F}}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 36.98857pt+\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\Delta}\left[\left(\frac{P-C}{F}-\frac{\bar{P}-C}{\bar{F}}\right)(Y-1)-(F-\bar{F})X+\frac{P}{F}-\frac{\bar{P}}{\bar{F}}\right]^{2}\,,

(53)

where

\displaystyle\hskip-85.35826ptC\equiv\left(\frac{\Delta m^{2}_{21}}{\Delta m^{2}_{31}}\right)^{2}\left[\frac{\sin(A_{\mbox{\tiny CC}}L/2)}{A_{\mbox{\tiny CC}}L/2\Delta}\right]^{2}\sin^{2}2\theta_{12}\,.

Eq. (
53
) gives a quadratic curve
in the
(X,Y)
-plane.
At the oscillation maximum (
|\Delta m_{31}^{2}|L/4E=\pi/2
),
the numerator of the first term on the right hand side
of Eq. (
53
) must vanish, and it yields
a straight line in the
(X,Y)
-plane:

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\left(\frac{P-C}{F}+\frac{\bar{P}-C}{\bar{F}}\right)(Y-1)-(F+\bar{F})X+\frac{P}{F}+\frac{\bar{P}}{\bar{F}}=0\,.

(54)

Eqs. (
53
) and (
54
) are depicted
in Fig.
2
(a) and (b).

Figure 2:
Parameter degeneracy for Normal Ordering
and Inverted Ordering:
(a) Off the oscillation maximum (
|\Delta m^{2}_{31}|L/4E\neq\pi/2
), (b) At the oscillation maximum (
|\Delta m^{2}_{31}|L/4E=\pi/2
).
In the case of (a), there are eight solutions with a different
value of
\delta
.
In the case of (b), there are four solutions with two-fold degeneracy.

As can be seen from Fig.
2
,
there are eight (four) solutions in general off (at) the oscillation maximum.
This parameter degeneracy is in general eight fold
[
10
]
, since
there are two intersections between the curve  (
53
) and
Y
= constant
due to quadratic nature of the curve (
53
)
(intrinsic degeneracy
[
12
]
),
there are two curves depending on whether the mass ordering
is Normal (red in Fig.
2
) or Inverted (blue)
(sign degeneracy
[
13
]
),
and there are two possibilities for the
\theta_{23}
octant given a value
of
\sin^{2}2\theta_{23}
(octant degeneracy
[
14
]
).
The problem with this parameter degeneracy is that
the value of
\delta
is different for a different point in Fig.
2
,
and we must resolve this degeneracy to determine
\delta
precisely.
In particular, it is known
[
10
]
that the value
of
\delta
depends strongly on the mass ordering,
so even if the long baseline experiment is done at
the oscillation maximum, resolution of sign degeneracy
is important to determine
\delta
.

New physics beyond the Standard Model with massive three neutrinos

As of 2021, there are several anomalies which cannot be
accounted for by the standard three-flavor framework.
One is a class of anomalies which may be explained
if we assume the existence of
light sterile neutrinos
[
50

whose
mass is of order 1 eV.
They are the results of LSND
[
51
]
,
MiniBooNE
[
52
]
,

[
53
,
54
]

and gallium anomaly
[
55
]
.
Also the recent IceCube data
[
56
]

suggests a mild preference for light sterile neutrinos
in the disappearance channel
\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{\mu}

+
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\to\bar{\nu}_{\mu}

The other anomaly is the discrepancy
[
57

between
the mass squared differences of the solar and

This may be explained
[
58

59

if we assume either flavor-dependent nonstandard

is of order
10^{-5}
eV

.
These anomalies have attracted a lot of attention
because they may provide us a key to physics
beyond the Standard Model.

3.1
Light sterile neutrinos

If we assume four flavor and mass eigenstates,
then the fourth flavor eigenstate must be
sterile neutrino state
\nu_{s}
, which is
singlet with respect to the gauge group of the Standard Model
because the number of weakly interacting light
neutrinos is three from the LEP data
[
62
]
.
So the equation of motion is described by
the following
4\times 4
Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581pt\displaystyle i{d\over dt}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nu_{e}(t)\\
\nu_{\mu}(t)\\
\nu_{\tau}(t)\\
\nu_{s}(t)\end{array}\right)=M~\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nu_{e}(t)\\
\nu_{\mu}(t)\\
\nu_{\tau}(t)\\
\nu_{s}(t)\end{array}\right)\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581ptM\equiv[U\,\mbox{\rm diag}(E_{1},E_{2},E_{3},E_{4})\,U^{-1}+\mbox{\rm diag}(A_{\mbox{\tiny CC}},0,0,-A_{\mbox{\tiny NC}})+A_{\mbox{\tiny NC}}\,{\bf 1}]

\displaystyle\hskip 8.53581pt=[U\,\mbox{\rm diag}(0,\Delta E_{21},\Delta E_{31},\Delta E_{41})\,U^{-1}+\mbox{\rm diag}(A_{\mbox{\tiny CC}},0,0,-A_{\mbox{\tiny NC}})+(E_{1}+A_{\mbox{\tiny NC}})\,{\bf 1}]\,,

(64)

The
4\times 4
mixing matrix
U
has 6 mixing angles and
3 CP phases, and one of the parametrization for
U
is given by

\displaystyle U=R_{34}(\theta_{34},\,\delta_{34})\;R_{24}(\theta_{24},\,0)\;R_{14}(\theta_{14},\,\delta_{14})\;R_{23}(\theta_{23},\,0)\;R_{13}(\theta_{13},\,\delta_{13})\;R_{12}(\theta_{12},\,0)\,,

(65)

where
R_{ij}(\theta_{ij},\ \delta_{l})
are the complex
rotation matrices in the
ij

\displaystyle\hskip-184.9429pt[R_{ij}(\theta_{ij},\ \delta_{ij})]_{pq}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\cos\theta_{ij}&p=q=i,j\\
1&p=q\not=i,j\\
\sin\theta_{ij}\ e^{-i\delta_{ij}}&p=i;q=j\\
-\sin\theta_{ij}\ e^{i\delta_{ij}}&p=j;q=i\\
0&\mbox{\rm otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

The angles
\theta_{14}
,
\theta_{24}
,

\theta_{34}
stand for the mixing
of oscillations at short baseline experiments of reactor neutrino
(
\bar{\nu}_{e}\to\bar{\nu}_{e}
) and radioactive sources
(
\nu_{e}\to\nu_{e}
), that of
oscillations at short baseline accelerator neutrino experiments
(
\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{\mu}
and
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\to\bar{\nu}_{\mu}

and the ratio between
\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{\tau}

and
\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{s}

\theta_{j4}\to 0~(j=1,2,3)

\delta=\delta_{13}

In the case of four neutrino mixing scenarios,

3.2
Non-standard interactions in propagation

If there is a
flavor-dependent neutrino non-standard interaction (NSI) in neutrino
propagation:

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\mbox{\tiny{\rm NSI}}}=-2\sqrt{2}\,\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{ff^{\prime}P}G_{F}\left(\overline{\nu}_{\alpha L}\gamma_{\mu}\nu_{\beta L}\right)\,\left(\overline{f}_{P}\gamma^{\mu}f_{P}^{\prime}\right)\,,

(78)

where
f_{P}
and
f_{P}^{\prime}
are the fermions with chirality
P
,

\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{ff^{\prime}P}
is a dimensionless constant
normalized in terms of the Fermi coupling constant
G_{F}
,
then the matter potential in the flavor basis
is modified as

\displaystyle A\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}1+\epsilon_{ee}&\epsilon_{e\mu}&\epsilon_{e\tau}\\
\epsilon_{\mu e}&\epsilon_{\mu\mu}&\epsilon_{\mu\tau}\\
\epsilon_{\tau e}&\epsilon_{\tau\mu}&\epsilon_{\tau\tau}\end{array}\right),

where
A\equiv\sqrt{2}G_{F}N_{e}

stands for the matter effect,

\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}
is defined by

\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\equiv\sum_{f=e,u,d}(N_{f}/N_{e})\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{f}

and
N_{f}~(f=e,u,d)
stands for number densities of fermions
f
.
Here we defined the new NSI parameters as

\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{f}\equiv\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{ffL}+\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{ffR}

since the matter effect is sensitive only to the coherent scattering
and only to the vector part in the interaction.

It was pointed out
[
57
]

that there was tension at 2
\sigma
between the mass squared difference
from the solar neutrino data
(
\Delta m^{2}_{21}\simeq 4\times 10^{-5}
eV
2
)
and that of the KamLAND experiment (
\Delta m^{2}_{21}=7.5\times 10^{-5}
eV
2
).
Ref.
[
58
]

showed that a nonvanishing value of the new NSI parameters

\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}^{f}
solves this tension.
This fact gives a motivation to take seriously NSI in propagation.
The significance
of the anomaly was approximately 2
\sigma

in 2016
[
57
]
, but
it has reduced to 1.4
\sigma
in 2020
[
30
]
.
If the significance keeps decreasing in the future, then
this should not be listed in the list of anomalies.

Today we have a lot of information on neutrinos, such as the two mass
squared differences, three mixing angles, and a hint of a CP violating
phase. In the process of gaining such information, Professor
Masatoshi Koshiba made outstanding contributions to the field, by the
Kamiokande experiment which was proposed by him in

In the standard framework of

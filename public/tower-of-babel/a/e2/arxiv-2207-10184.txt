On Leclerc’s conjectural cluster structures for open Richardson varieties
Peigen Cao, Bernhard Keller

Abstract
. In 2016, Leclerc constructed conjectural cluster structures on open Richardson varieties using representations of preprojective algebras. A variant with more explicit seeds was obtained by Ménard in his thesis. We show that Ménard’s seeds do yield upper cluster algebra structures on open Richardson varieties and discuss the problems that remain in order to prove that they are cluster algebra structures.

Open Richardson varieties were introduced by Kazhdan–Lusztig

[

]

[

It is natural to ask whether open Richardson varieties carry cluster structures

an
upper
cluster algebra structure on each open Richardson variety.
We also discuss the problems that remain
in order to prove that it is a cluster algebra structure.

In type A, a (possibly different) upper cluster algebra structure was obtained
using completely different methods by Gracie Ingermanson
in her thesis
[
19

under the supervision
of David Speyer. Open Richardson varieties are special cases of braid varieties.
In this more general framework, much stronger results will be contained
in the forthcoming work of two groups of mathematicians:

2.
Open Richardson varieties

Let
\Delta
be a simply laced Dynkin diagram, for example the diagram
A_{4}

given by a chain of length
4
.
Let
G
be the associated simple, simply connected complex algebraic group,
for example
Sl_{5}(\mathbb{C})
. Let
B,B^{-}\subseteq G
be opposite Borel subgroups,
for example the subgroups of upper/lower triangular matrices in
Sl_{5}(\mathbb{C})
.
Let
H=B\cap B^{-}
be the associated maximal torus and
W=N_{B}(H)/H
the
Weyl group, for example the subgroup
H\subseteq Sl_{5}(\mathbb{C})
of diagonal
matrices and the symmetric group
S_{5}
. Let
X=B^{-}\setminus G
be the flag variety
and
\pi:G\to X
the canonical projection, for example the variety of
complete flags

0=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset\cdots\subset F_{5}=\mathbb{C}^{5}

in the space of rows
\mathbb{C}^{5}
with its natural right action by
Sl_{5}(\mathbb{C})
.
We have the Schubert decomposition

X=\coprod_{w\in W}C_{w}

into the Schubert cells
C_{w}=\pi(wB^{-})
, which are affine spaces of
dimension equal to the length
l(w)
. Dually, we have the opposite Schubert
decomposition

X=\coprod_{v\in W}C^{v}

into the opposite Schubert cells
C^{v}=\pi(vB)
, which are affine spaces
of dimension equal to
l(w_{0})-l(v)
, where
w_{0}
is the longest element of
W
.
For a pair of Weyl group elements
(v,w)
, the
open Richardson variety

{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
is defined as the intersection

. It is non-empty
if and only if
v\leq w
for the Bruhat order and in this case, it is a smooth,
irreducible, locally closed subvariety of
C_{w}
of dimension
l(w)-l(v)

which is affine but not an affine space, in general.
In the minimal example where
G=Sl_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, the flag variety
X=B^{-}\setminus G

identifies with the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
by the map sending a

2\times 2
-matrix to the line generated by its first row. Under this identification, if
s
generates the Weyl group
S_{2}
, we have

\textstyle{C_{e}=\{0\}}
\textstyle{C_{s}=\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0\}}
\textstyle{C^{e}=\mathbb{A}^{1}(\mathbb{C})}
\textstyle{C^{s}=\{\infty\}}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{R}}_{e,e}=\{0\}}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{R}}_{e,s}=\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})\setminus\{0,\infty\}}
\textstyle{{\mathcal{R}}_{s,s}={\infty}.}

is an open positroid variety (i.e. a type
A
open
Richardson variety in the Grassmannian). Moreover, the conjecture holds in
this case.

Notice that in the situation of the theorem, we may have
v\not\leq_{R}w
.

4.
Ménard’s results

We keep the notations and assumptions of the preceding section.

Theorem 4.1

(Ménard
[
28
]
)
.

There is an explicit sequence of mutations
transforming
T_{\overline{w}}
into a cluster-tilting object
T^{\prime}_{\overline{w}}
such that any maximal
direct summand
M_{v,\overline{w}}
of
T^{\prime}_{\overline{w}}
lying in
{\mathcal{C}}_{v,w}
is a cluster-tilting
object of
{\mathcal{C}}_{v,w}
.

The sequence of mutations in the theorem was conjectured by Jan Schröer.
The cluster-tilting object
M_{v,\overline{w}}
is expected to be isomorphic to
T_{v,\overline{w}}
.
It yields a (possibly new) candidate seed for
\mathbb{C}[N]^{v,w}
and
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}]
.
In his thesis
[
27
,
28
]
, Ménard has developed an algorithm
allowing to explicitly compute the seed associated with
M_{v,\overline{w}}
.
It follows from his theorem above that the quiver
Q(M_{v,\overline{w}})
is a

cluster reduction
of
Q(T_{\overline{w}})
, i.e. it is obtained from
Q(T_{\overline{w}})

by mutating, freezing vertices and deleting certain frozen vertices (in this order).
By a theorem of Muller
[
31
]
, the existence of reddening sequences
is preserved under cluster reduction. Since the existence of a reddening sequence
for the ice quiver
Q(T_{\overline{w}})
is known
[
15
]
, it follows that the ice quiver

Q(M_{v,\overline{w}})
has a reddening sequence. Moreover, the exchange matrix
associated with
Q(M_{v,\overline{w}})
has full rank (this follows
essentially from
[
2
]
). Thus, the
upper cluster algebra
{\mathcal{U}}(Q(M_{v,\overline{w}}))
(with invertible coefficients) admits a theta
basis in the sense of Gross–Hacking–Keel–Kontsevich

[
18
]
and also a generic basis,
as shown by Qin
[
33
]
. In particular, the image of the
Caldero–Chapoton map spans the upper cluster algebra

{\mathcal{U}}(Q(M_{v,\overline{w}}))
over the algebra of Laurent polynomials
in the coefficients.

5.
Upper cluster structure

We keep the notations and assumptions of the preceding section. Let

T
be Ménard’s cluster-tilting object
M_{v,\overline{w}}
.
Let
\varphi:{\mathcal{A}}(Q(T))\to\mathbb{C}[N]^{v,w}

with the seed
(Q(T),(\varphi(T_{i})))

Theorem 5.1
.

The map
\varphi
yields a commutative square

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

.

We do not know whether the statement of the
theorem also holds if
T
is Leclerc’s cluster tilting object
T_{v,\overline{w}}
.

Proof.

Since the
\varphi(T_{i})
are algebraically

x_{i}\mapsto\varphi(T_{i})
defines a

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}(x_{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}(N).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\varphi}}

Let

denote the cluster
character associated with the cluster-tilting object
T\in{\mathcal{C}}_{v,w}

in
[
8
]
.
By Theorem 4 of
[
16
]
, the triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}(x_{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}(N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}}_{v,w}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\varphi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces CC}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

commutes. Now by definition, the map

actually takes its values in
\mathbb{C}[x_{i}^{\pm}]
and by Theorem 1.1 of

[
32

, it even takes its values in the upper cluster
algebra
{\mathcal{U}}^{+}={\mathcal{U}}^{+}(Q(T))
with non invertible coefficients.
Clearly, the field embedding

\widetilde{\varphi}:\mathbb{C}(x_{i})\to\mathbb{C}(N)

induces an isomorphism

{\mathcal{U}}^{+}\xrightarrow{{}_{\sim}}\widetilde{\varphi}({\mathcal{U}}^{+})\subset\mathbb{C}(N)

and we have the commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}}_{v,w}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}(N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{U}}^{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\varphi}({\mathcal{U}}^{+}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces CC}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

By Theorem

(a), the
\mathbb{C}

Since

Therefore, from the above inclusion, we deduce that we have

introduced at the end of section

Since

has a reddening sequence.
By

6.
Towards a cluster structure

Our hope is that for Ménard’s cluster-tilting object
M_{v,\overline{w}}
,
we have
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{U}}
for the corresponding cluster and upper cluster
algebra with invertible coefficients. Recall that by Ménard’s theorem,
the ice quiver
Q(M_{v,\overline{w}})
is obtained from
Q(T_{\overline{w}})
by
cluster reduction, i.e. by mutation, freezing and deletion of
frozen vertices (in this order).

Theorem 6.1

(Geiss–Leclerc–Schröer
[
15
]
)
.

a)

We have
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{U}}
for
Q(T_{\overline{w}})
.

b)

The ice quiver
Q(T_{\overline{w}})
admits a reddening sequence.

The second property is preserved under cluster reduction by
Muller’s theorem
[
31
]
. However, it is not clear
under which conditions this holds for the first property.

6.2.
Preservation of
{\mathcal{U}}={\mathcal{A}}
under freezing?

Let
Q
be an ice quiver and
Q^{\prime}
the quiver obtained
from
Q
by freezing the cluster variable
x
associated
with a non frozen vertex. We then have the algebra inclusions

{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{A}}[x^{-1}]\subseteq{\mathcal{U}}[x^{-1}]\subseteq{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}\>,\;

where
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{A}}(Q)
, … . Following Muller
[
30
]
, we define

{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
to be a
cluster localization
of
{\mathcal{A}}
at
x
if
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{A}}[x^{-1}]
.
Unfortunately, it is not clear whether the freezing occuring in
the passage from Geiss–Leclerc–Schröer’s seed for
C_{w}

to Ménard’s for
{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
is a composition of cluster localizations.
The following theorem may nevertheless be useful.

Theorem 6.3
.

Suppose the exchange matrix associated with the ice
quiver
Q
is of full rank. Let
{\mathcal{A}}
and

{\mathcal{U}}
be the associated cluster and upper cluster algebra. If we have
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{U}}

and
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
is a cluster localization of
{\mathcal{A}}
at
x
, then we have
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since the exchange matrix
B
associated with the ice quiver
Q
is of full rank and
Q^{\prime}
is obtained from
Q
by freezing the non frozen vertex of
Q
labeled by
x
, we know that the exchange matrix
B^{\prime}
associated with the ice quiver
Q^{\prime}
is also of full rank. Hence, the starfish lemma
[
3
, Corollary 1.9]
holds for
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
.

Denote by
t_{0}
the initial seed of
\mathcal{U}
and
I_{\rm uf}
the set of non frozen vertices of the ice quiver
Q
. Let
k
be the non frozen vertex of
Q
such that
x=x_{k;t_{0}}
. By the starfish lemma
[
3
, Corollary 1.9]
, we have

(6.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{L}(t_{0})\bigcap\left(\bigcap\limits_{i\in I_{\rm uf}}\mathcal{L}(\mu_{i}(t_{0}))\right),

(6.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}(t_{0})\bigcap\left(\bigcap\limits_{k\neq i\in I_{\rm uf}}\mathcal{L}(\mu_{i}(t_{0}))\right),

where
\mathcal{L}(\mu_{i}(t_{0}))
is the Laurent polynomial ring associated with the seed
\mu_{i}(t_{0})
.

Since
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{U}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
is a cluster localization of
{\mathcal{A}}
at
x
, we have that

{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{A}}[x^{-1}]={\mathcal{U}}[x^{-1}]\subseteq{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}.

It remains to show the converse inclusion
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{U}[x^{-1}]
.

By the equality (
6.2
), we know that for any
v\in\mathcal{U}^{\prime}
, there exists a positive integer
d
such that the exponents of
x=x_{k;t_{0}}
in the Laurent expansion of
vx^{d}
with respect to the seed
\mu_{i}(t_{0})
are positive for any
k\neq i\in I_{\rm uf}
. In this case, we have
vx^{d}\in\mathcal{L}(\mu_{k}(t_{0}))
. Then by the equality (
6.1
), we get
vx^{d}\in\mathcal{U}
and thus
v\in\mathcal{U}[x^{-1}]
. So we have
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{U}[x^{-1}]
and

{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{A}}[x^{-1}]={\mathcal{U}}[x^{-1}]={\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}.

∎

6.4.
Preservation of
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{U}}
under deletion?

In general,
the property
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{U}}
is not preserved under deletion of frozen
vertices.
The hypotheses of the following proposition do hold for the
deletion occuring in the passage from Geiss–Leclerc–Schröer’s seed
for
C_{w}
to Ménard’s for
{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
.

Proposition 6.5
.

Suppose
Q^{\prime}
is obtained from
Q
by deleting a frozen
vertex, that
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
are of full rank and that the ice quiver
Q
admits a reddening
sequence. Denote by
{\mathcal{A}}
,
{\mathcal{U}}
,
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
the cluster algebras
and the upper cluster algebras associated with

and

.
If
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathcal{U}}
, then
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\prime}
denote the groups of Laurent monomials
in the coefficients of
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
. Consider the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{U}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}[x_{i}^{\pm 1}][\mathbb{P}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{U}}^{\prime}}

Let

and

be the Caldero–Chapoton maps associated with

and

and let

By Qin’s work

33
]
,
we know that the image of

Thus, the image of

and

.
∎

Abstract
This paper is a natural companion of [Alekseevsky D.V., Alonso Blanco R., Manno G., Pugliese F., Ann. Inst. Fourier (Grenoble) 62 (2012), 497–524, arXiv:1003.5177], generalising its perspectives and results to the context of third-order (2D) Monge–Ampère equations, by using the so-called “meta-symplectic structure” associated with the 8D prolongation M ( 1 ) M^{(1)} of a 5D contact manifold M M . We write down a geometric definition of a third-order Monge–Ampère equation in terms of a (class of) differential two-form on M ( 1 ) M^{(1)} . In particular, the equations corresponding to decomposable forms admit a simple description in terms of certain three-dimensional distributions, which are made from the characteristics of the original equations. We conclude the paper with a study of the intermediate integrals of these special Monge–Ampère equations, herewith called of Goursat type.

2.2
The meta-symplectic structure on
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}^{1}}

The (local) conformal symplectic structure
\varpi=d\theta|_{\mathcal{C}}
admits a “global” analog, namely

\displaystyle\varpi_{\textrm{glob}}\colon\ \mathcal{C}\wedge\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\frac{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}{\mathcal{C}}=\frac{TM}{\mathcal{C}}.

(2.3)

Indeed,
since

\frac{TM}{\mathcal{C}}
is rank-one,

locally identifies with

One of the main gadgets of our analysis is the co-restriction to

of the Levi form of
\mathcal{C}^{1}

[

and called, for this reason,

\displaystyle\Omega\colon\ \mathcal{C}^{1}\wedge\mathcal{C}^{1}\longrightarrow\frac{(\mathcal{C}^{1})^{\prime}}{\mathcal{C}^{1}}.

(2.4)

Notice that,

-horizontal 2D subspace of

.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let

4.5
Smoothness and singularities issues

For generic PDEs, we just assume that
\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow M^{(1)}
is the zero locus of a smooth function, without requiring the non-vanishing of its differential. In all the statements and reasonings, we tacitly restrict ourselves to the open and dense subset
\mathcal{E}_{\textrm{reg}}
of smooth points.

According to our definition of a PDE
\mathcal{E}
as a sub-bundle of
M^{(1)}
, the natural projection
\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow M^{(1)}
is surjective,
and that each fibre
\mathcal{E}_{m^{1}}
is a smooth submanifold of
M^{(2)}_{m^{1}}
, possibly with singularities.

For example, the singularities which can occur for
3^{\textrm{rd}}
Monge–Ampère equations are of “algebraic type”, i.e.,
\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow M^{(1)}
is a bundle of projective varieties. Indeed, each fibre
M^{(2)}_{m^{1}}
is naturally understood as the Grassmannian variety of isotropic elements with respect to the meta-symplectic structure which, in analogy with the standard Lagrangian Grassmannian, embeds into a suitable projective space via the Plücker embedding
8
8

8

The general theory of this would lead us beyond the scope of the present paper.
. More precisely, a straightforward computation shows that the subset of the singular points of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{D}}
is made by the
m^{2}
’s such that
L_{m^{2}}\subset\mathcal{D}_{m^{1}}
. Such a possibility occurs only when
\dim(L_{m^{2}}\cap\mathcal{D}_{m^{1}})=2
for
m^{2}\in M^{(2)}
(in the case when
L_{m^{2}}
and
\mathcal{D}_{m^{1}}
are not transversal, generically the dimension is 1, otherwise is generically 0).

Methodologically, this paper is at the crossroad between algebraic and differential geometry. The geometric approach to non-linear PDEs is traditionally differential, while recent developments revealed that many features of PDEs are genuinely algebraic-geometric. Even though a rigorous algebraic-geometric approach to the present topics is feasible, the authors have opted for the traditional and – in a sense, easier – methods, based on elementary differential geometry.

Summing up, all the objects are herewith assumed to be smooth, and all maps to be of class
C^{\infty}
, except for:

•

the fibres of
\mathcal{E}
, which are the zero loci of smooth functions (and, as such, may display singularities where the differential vanishes);

•

the sub-bundles
\mathcal{V}
of the projectivised contact bundle
\mathbb{P}\mathcal{C}^{1}
, which are smooth families of projective sub-varieties in each fibre, i.e., locally isomorphic to the product of an open subset of
\mathcal{V}
by a projective subvariety in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
.

If
\mathcal{V}_{\textrm{I}}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\textrm{II}}
are sub-bundles of
\mathcal{V}
, in the aforementioned sense, we say that
\mathcal{V}_{\textrm{I}}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\textrm{II}}
are irreducible components of
\mathcal{V}
if, for any point
m^{1}\in M^{(1)}
, the projective variety
\mathcal{V}_{m^{1}}
is reducible, and
(\mathcal{V}_{\textrm{I}})_{m^{1}}
and
(\mathcal{V}_{\textrm{II}})_{m^{1}}
are its irreducible components.

We conclude this preliminary part of the paper by observing that, locally,

is the

jet-extension of the trivial bundle

, so that the reader more at ease with jet formalism may perform the substitution

and easily adapt the main results to the new setting.

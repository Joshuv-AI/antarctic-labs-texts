A variation of the L p L^{p} uncertainty principles for the Weinstein transform
Ahmed Saoudi

Abstract
The Weinstein operator has several applications in pure and applied Mathematics especially in Fluid Mechanics and satisfies some uncertainty principles similar to the Euclidean Fourier transform. The aim of this paper is establish a generalization of uncertainty principles for Weinstein transform in L α p L_{\alpha}^{p} -norm. Firstly, we extend the Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle to more general case. Then we establish three continuous uncertainty principles of concentration type. The first and the second uncertainty principles are L α p L_{\alpha}^{p} versions and depend on the sets of concentration Ω \Omega and Σ \Sigma , and on the time function φ \varphi . However, the third uncertainty principle is also L α p L_{\alpha}^{p} version depends on the sets of concentration and he is independent on the band limited function φ \varphi . These L α p L_{\alpha}^{p} -Donoho-Stark-type inequalities generalize the results obtained in the case p = q = 2 p=q=2 . Keywords . Weinstein operator, Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl-type inequality, Concentration uncertainty principles, Donoho-Stark-type inequality. Mathematics Subject Classification . Primary 43A32; Secondary 44A15

Introduction

Uncertainty principles appear in harmonic analysis and signal theory in a variety of different forms
involving not only the signal
\varphi
and its Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}(\varphi)
, but essentially every representation of a signal in the time-frequency space. They are mathematical results that give limitations on the simultaneous concentration of a signal and its Fourier transform and they have implications in signal analysis and quantum physics. In quantum physics they tell us that a particle’s speed and position cannot both be measured with infinite precision. In signal analysis they tell us that if we observe a signal only for a finite period of time, we will lose information about the frequencies the signal consists of. Timelimited functions and bandlimited functions are basic tools of signal and image
processing. Like, the simple form of the uncertainty principle tells us that a signal cannot be simultaneously time and bandlimited. This leads to the investigation
of the set of almost time and almost bandlimited functions, which has been initially
by Landau, Pollak
[

2
Preliminaires

The Weinstein operator
\Delta_{W,\alpha}^{d}
defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
, by

\Delta_{W,\alpha}^{d}=\sum_{j=1}^{d+1}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}+\frac{2\alpha+1}{x_{d+1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{d+1}}=\Delta_{d}+L_{\alpha},\;\alpha>-1/2,

where
\Delta_{d}
is the Laplacian operator for the
d
first variables and
L_{\alpha}
is the Bessel operator for the last variable defined on
(0,\infty)
by

L_{\alpha}u=\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{d+1}^{2}}+\frac{2\alpha+1}{x_{d+1}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{d+1}}.

The Weinstein operator
\Delta_{W,\alpha}^{d}
has several applications in pure and applied mathematics, especially in fluid mechanics
[
2
,
18
]
.

For all
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{d+1})\in\mathbb{C}^{d+1}
, the system

\begin{gathered}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}(x)=-\lambda_{j}^{2}u(x),\quad\text{if }1\leq j\leq d\\
L_{\alpha}u(x)=-\lambda_{d+1}^{2}u(x),\\
u(0)=1,\quad\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{d+1}}(0)=0,\quad\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}(0)=-i\lambda_{j},\quad\text{if }1\leq j\leq d\end{gathered}

(2.1)

has a unique solution denoted by
\Lambda_{\alpha}^{d}(\lambda,.),
and given by

\Lambda_{\alpha}^{d}(\lambda,x)=e^{-i<x^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}>}j_{\alpha}(x_{d+1}\lambda_{d+1})

(2.2)

where
x=(x^{\prime},x_{d+1}),\;\lambda=(\lambda^{\prime},\lambda_{d+1})
and
j_{\alpha}
is is the normalized Bessel function of index
\alpha
defined by

j_{\alpha}(x)=\Gamma(\alpha+1)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}x^{2k}}{2^{k}k!\Gamma(\alpha+k+1)}.

The function
(\lambda,x)\mapsto\Lambda_{\alpha}^{d}(\lambda,x)
is called the Weinstein kernel and has a unique extension to
\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d+1}
, and satisfied the following properties.

(i)

For all
(\lambda,x)\in\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d+1}
we have

\Lambda_{\alpha}^{d}(\lambda,x)=\Lambda_{\alpha}^{d}(x,\lambda).

(ii)

For all
(\lambda,x)\in\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d+1}
we have

\Lambda_{\alpha}^{d}(\lambda,-x)=\Lambda_{\alpha}^{d}(-\lambda,x).

(iii)

For all
(\lambda,x)\in\mathbb{C}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{C}^{d+1}
we get

\Lambda_{\alpha}^{d}(\lambda,0)=1.

(iv)

For all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{d+1},\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{d+1}
we have

\left|D_{\lambda}^{\nu}\Lambda_{\alpha}^{d}(\lambda,x)\right|\leq\left\|x\right\|^{\left|\nu\right|}e^{\left\|x\right\|\left\|\operatorname{Im}\lambda\right\|}

where
D_{\lambda}^{\nu}=\partial^{\nu}/(\partial\lambda_{1}^{\nu_{1}}...\partial\lambda_{d+1}^{\nu_{d+1}})
and
\left|\nu\right|=\nu_{1}+...+\nu_{d+1}.
In particular, for all
(\lambda,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, we have

\left|\Lambda_{\alpha}^{d}(\lambda,x)\right|\leq 1.

(2.3)

In the following we denote by

(i)

C_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
, the space of continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1},
even with respect to the last variable.

(ii)

S_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
, the space of the
C^{\infty}
functions, even with respect to the last variable, and rapidly decreasing together with their derivatives.

(iii)

L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}),\;1\leq p\leq\infty,
the space of measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
such that

\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,p}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}\left|f(x)\right|^{p}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{1/p}<\infty,\;p\in[1,\infty),

\left\|f\right\|_{\alpha,\infty}=\textrm{ess}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}\left|f(x)\right|<\infty,

where
d\mu_{\alpha}(x)
is the measure on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(0,\infty)
given by

d\mu_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{x^{2\alpha+1}_{d+1}}{(2\pi)^{d}2^{2\alpha}\Gamma^{2}(\alpha+1)}dx.

For a radial function
\varphi\in L_{\alpha}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
the function
\tilde{\varphi}
defined on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\varphi(x)=\tilde{\varphi}(|x|)
, for all

x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}
, is integrable with respect to the measure
r^{2\alpha+d+1}dr
, and we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\varphi(x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x)=a_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{\varphi}(r)r^{2\alpha+d+1}dr,

(2.4)

where

a_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2^{\alpha+\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma(\alpha+\frac{d}{2}+1)}.

The Weinstein transform generalizing the usual Fourier transform, is given for

\varphi\in L_{\alpha}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}
, by

\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\varphi(x)\Lambda_{\alpha}^{d}(x,\lambda)d\mu_{\alpha}(x),

We list some known basic properties of the Weinstein transform are as follows. For the proofs, we refer
[
13
,
1
]
.

(i)

For all
\varphi\in L^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
, the function
\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
and we have

\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,\infty}\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,1}.

(2.5)

(ii)

The Weinstein transform is a topological isomorphism from
\mathcal{S}_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
onto itself. The inverse transform is given by

\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}^{-1}\varphi(\lambda)=\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}\varphi(-\lambda),\;\textrm{for\;all}\;\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}.

(2.6)

(iii)

Parseval formula: For all
\varphi,\phi\in\mathcal{S}_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}\varphi(x)\overline{\phi(x)}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)(x)\overline{\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\phi)(x)}d\mu_{\alpha}(x).

(v)

Plancherel formula: For all
\varphi\in L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
, we have

\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,2}=\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,2}.

(2.7)

(vi)

Plancherel Theorem: The Weinstein transform
\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}
extends uniquely to an isometric isomorphism on
L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}).

(vii)

Inversion formula: Let
\varphi\in L^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
such that
\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}\varphi\in L^{1}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
, then we have

\varphi(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}\varphi(x)\Lambda_{\alpha}^{d}(-\lambda,x)d\mu_{\alpha}(x),\;\textrm{a.e. }\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}.

(2.8)

Using relations (
2.5
) and (
2.7
) with Marcinkiewicz’s interpolation theorem
[
17
]
we deduce that for every
\varphi\in L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
for all
1\leq p\leq 2
, the function
\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\in L^{q}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}),q=p/(p-1),
and

\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

(2.9)

L^{p}
-Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl inequality

In this section, we extend the Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl uncertainty principle (
1.1
)
to more general case for the Weinstein operator. We need to use the method of Ciatti et al.
[
3

, which is the

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
1<p\leq 2
,
q=p/(p-1)
and
0<s<(2\alpha+d+2)/q
. Then for all
\varphi\in L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
and
z>0
,

\left\|e^{-z|y|^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq\left(1+\frac{K_{\alpha}}{(2q)^{(\alpha+\frac{1}{d}+1)}\frac{1}{q}}\right)z^{-s/2}\left\||x|^{s}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p},

(3.1)

where

K_{\alpha}=\left((2\alpha+d+2-qs)2^{\alpha+\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma(\alpha+\frac{d}{2}+1)\right)^{-1/q}

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
. The inequality (
3.1
) holds if

\left\||x|^{s}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}=\infty.

Let us now assume that
\left\||x|^{s}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}<\infty.
For
\rho>0
, we put
B_{\rho}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}:|x|<\rho\}
and
B_{\rho}^{c}=\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}\backslash B_{\rho}.
Denote by
\chi_{B_{\rho}}
and
\chi_{B_{\rho}^{c}}
the characteristic functions. Let
\varphi\in L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
,
1<p\leq 2
and
q=p/(p-1)
. Like that

|\varphi\chi_{B_{\rho}^{c}}(x)|\leq\rho^{-s}|x|^{s}|\varphi(x)|,

then, by inequality (
2.9
), we get

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-z|y|^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi\chi_{B_{\rho}^{c}})\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-z|y|^{2}}\right\|_{\alpha,\infty}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi\chi_{B_{\rho}^{c}})\right\|_{\alpha,q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-z|y|^{2}}\right\|_{\alpha,\infty}\left\|\varphi\chi_{B_{\rho}^{c}}\right\|_{\alpha,p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-z|y|^{2}}\right\|_{\alpha,\infty}\rho^{-s}\left\||x|^{s}|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

On the other hand, according to (
2.5
) and Höolder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-z|y|^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi\chi_{B_{\rho}})\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-z|y|^{2}}\right\|_{\alpha,q}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi\chi_{B_{\rho}})\right\|_{\alpha,\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-z|y|^{2}}\right\|_{\alpha,q}\left\|\varphi\chi_{B_{\rho})}\right\|_{\alpha,1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-z|y|^{2}}\right\|_{\alpha,q}\left\||x|^{-s}\chi_{B_{\rho})}\right\|_{\alpha,q}\left\||x|^{s}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

According to integral relationship (
2.4
), we have the following identity

\left\|e^{-z|y|^{2}}\right\|_{\alpha,q}=\frac{1}{(2q)^{(\alpha+\frac{d}{2}+1)}}z^{-(\alpha+\frac{d}{2}+1)}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\||x|^{-s}\chi_{B_{\rho}}\right\|_{\alpha,q}=K_{\alpha}\rho^{-s+(2\alpha+d+2)/q}.

Hence, we get

\left\|e^{-z|y|^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi\chi_{B_{\rho}})\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq\frac{C_{\alpha}}{(2q)^{(\alpha+\frac{d}{2}+1)}}z^{-(\alpha+\frac{d}{2}+1)}\rho^{-s+(2\alpha+d+2)/q}\left\||x|^{s}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p},

and

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-z|y|^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-z|y|^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi\chi_{B_{\rho}})\right\|_{\alpha,q}+\left\|e^{-z|y|^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi\chi_{B_{\rho}^{c}})\right\|_{\alpha,q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho^{-s}\left(1+\frac{K_{\alpha}}{(2q)^{(\alpha+\frac{d}{2}+1)\frac{1}{q}}}\rho^{(2\alpha+d+2)/q}z^{-(\alpha+\frac{d}{2}+1)\frac{1}{q}}\right)\left\||x|^{s}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

By choosing
\rho=z^{1/2}
we get the result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
1<p\leq 2
,
q=p/(p-1)
,
0<s<(2\alpha+d+2)/q
and
t>0
, then for all
\varphi\in L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
,

\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq K(s,t)\left\||x|^{s}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}^{\frac{t}{s+t}}\left\||y|^{t}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}^{\frac{s}{s+t}},

(3.2)

where
K(s,t)
is a positive constant.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
and
1<p\leq 2
, such that

\left\||x|^{s}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}+\left\||y|^{t}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}<\infty.

Suppose that
0<s<(2\alpha+d+2)/q
and
t<2
. Then, by Lemma
3.1
we have, for all
z>0
,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-z|y|^{2}}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}+\left\|(1-e^{-z|y|^{2}})\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{K_{\alpha}}{(2q)^{(\alpha+\frac{1}{d}+1)}\frac{1}{q}}\right)z^{-s/2}\left\||x|^{s}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}+\left\|(1-e^{-z|y|^{2}})\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}.

on the other hand, we have

\left\|(1-e^{-z|y|^{2}})\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}=z^{t/2}\left\|(s|y|^{2})^{-s/2}(1-e^{-z|y|^{2}})|y|^{2}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}.

Take into account
(1-e^{-u})u^{-s/2}
is bounded for all
u\geq 0
, if
t\leq 2
. Consequently,

\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq K\left(z^{s/2}\left\||x|^{s}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}+z^{t/2}\left\||y|^{s}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}\right).

By taking

z=\left(\frac{s\left\||x|^{s}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}}{t\left\||y|^{s}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}}\right)^{\frac{2}{s+t}},

we get the result for all
t\leq 2.

It remains to show the result for
t>0
. Since, we have for all
\varepsilon>0
,

|y|\leq\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{1-t}|y|^{t},

then it follows that

\left\||y|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq\varepsilon\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}+\varepsilon^{1-t}\left\||y|^{t}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}.

(3.3)

By choosing,

\varepsilon=(t-1)^{\frac{1}{t}}\left(\frac{\left\||y|^{t}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}}{\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}}\right)^{\frac{1}{t}},

we get

\left\||y|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq\frac{t}{t-1}(t-1)^{\frac{1}{t}}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}^{\frac{t-1}{t}}\left\||y|^{t}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}^{\frac{1}{t}}.

(3.4)

Combining the inequalities (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}

\displaystyle\leq K\left\||x|^{s}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}^{\frac{1}{1+s}}\left\||y|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}^{\frac{s}{1+s}}

\displaystyle\leq K\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}^{\frac{s(t-1)}{t(s+1)}}\left\||x|^{s}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}^{\frac{1}{1+s}}\left\||y|^{t}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}^{\frac{s}{t(s+1)}}.

which implies

\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}^{\frac{s+t}{t(s+1)}}\leq K\left\||x|^{s}\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,p}^{\frac{1}{1+s}}\left\||y|^{s}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}^{\frac{s}{t(s+1)}}.

which gives the rsult for
t>0.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

Let

2.7
), we get

\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,2}\leq K(s,t)\left\||x|^{s}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,2}^{\frac{t}{s+t}}\left\||y|^{t}\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,2}^{\frac{s}{s+t}},

which is the general case of the Heisenberg-Pauli-Weyl inequality (
1.1
) proved by Mejjaoli
[
11
]
.

4
L^{p}
-Donoho-Strak uncertainty principles

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Omega
and
\Sigma
be a measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
. We define the timelimiting
operator
P_{\Omega}
by
P_{\Omega}\varphi:=\varphi\chi_{\Omega}
and define the Weinstein integral operator
Q_{\Sigma}
by
\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(Q_{\Sigma}\varphi)=\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\chi_{\Sigma}.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
,
1\leq p\leq 2.
If
\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)<\infty
, then we have

Q_{\Sigma}\varphi(x)=\int_{\Sigma}\Lambda_{\alpha}^{d}(x,\lambda)\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)(\lambda)d\mu_{\alpha}(\lambda).

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
,
1\leq p\leq 2
and put
q=p/(p-1)
. According to inequalities (
2.3
) and (
2.9
) we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\chi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{\alpha,1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)(x)|d\mu_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)\right)^{1/p}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)\right)^{1/p}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,q}.

In the same way, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\chi_{\Sigma}\right\|_{\alpha,2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\Sigma}|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)(x)|^{2}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)\right)^{\frac{q-2}{2q}}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)\right)^{\frac{q-2}{2q}}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,q}.

Hence,
\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\chi_{\Sigma}\in L^{1}_{\alpha}\cap L^{2}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
and by the definition of the Weinstein integral operator
Q_{\Sigma}
we obtain

Q_{\Sigma}\varphi=\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}^{-1}(\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\chi_{\Sigma}).

Finally, the inversion formula (
2.8
) gives the result.
∎

It is easy to see the following result by inequality (
2.9
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
,
1<p\leq 2
and let
q=p/(p-1)
. Then we have

\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(Q_{\Sigma}\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\Omega
and
\Sigma
be a measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
. Let
\varphi\in L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
,
1<p\leq 2
and
q=p/(p-1)
. Then we have

\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(Q_{\Sigma}P_{\Omega}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

Proof.

Assume that
\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)<\infty
and
\mu_{\alpha}(\Omega)<\infty
. Let
\varphi\in L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
,
1\leq p\leq 2
and
q=p/(p-1)
. From the definition of the Weinstein integral operator
Q_{\Sigma}
, we get

\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(Q_{\Sigma}P_{\Omega}\varphi)=\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(P_{\Omega}\varphi)\chi_{\Sigma}.

Therefore

\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(Q_{\Sigma}P_{\Omega}\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}=\left(\int_{\Sigma}\left|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(P_{\Omega}\varphi)(\lambda)\right|^{q}d\mu_{\alpha}(\lambda)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

(4.1)

Since

\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(P_{\Omega}\varphi)(\lambda)=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\Lambda_{\alpha}^{d}(x,\lambda)d\mu_{\alpha}(x),

then according to Holder’s inequality and (
2.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(P_{\Omega}\varphi)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|\Lambda_{\alpha}^{d}(x,\lambda)\right|^{q}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|\varphi(x)\right|^{p}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,q}.

Hence, by (
4.1
), we get

\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(Q_{\Sigma}\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

∎

4.1
Concentration uncertainty principle

In this section we present two continuous-time uncertainty
principles of concentration type and we show there are depend on the sets
of concentration
\Omega
and
\Sigma
, and on the time function
\varphi
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\Omega
,
\Sigma
be a measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
and
\varphi\in L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
,
1\leq p\leq 2
.

(i)

We say that
\varphi
is
\varepsilon_{\Omega}
-concentrated to
\Omega
in
L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
-norm, if

\left\|\varphi-P_{\Omega}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}\leq\varepsilon_{\Omega}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

(4.2)

(ii)

\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)
is
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}
-concentrated to
\Sigma
in
L^{q}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
-norm,
q=p/(p-1)
,
if

\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)-\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(Q_{\Sigma}\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq\varepsilon_{\Sigma}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}.

(4.3)

The following theorem, states the first continuous-time uncertainty
principle of concentration type for the
L^{p}_{\alpha}
-theory.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Omega
,
\Sigma
be a measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
and
\varphi\in L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
,
1<p\leq 2
. If
\varphi
is
\varepsilon_{\Omega}
-concentrated to
\Omega
in
L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
-norm and
\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)
is
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}
-concentrated to
\Sigma
in
L^{q}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
-norm,
q=p/(p-1)
, then we have

\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq\frac{\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\varepsilon_{\Omega}}{1-\varepsilon_{\Sigma}}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
,
1<p\leq 2
. According to (
4.2
), (
4.3
) and Theorem
4.4
it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)-\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(Q_{\Sigma}P_{\Omega}\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)-\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(Q_{\Sigma}\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}

\displaystyle+\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(Q_{\Sigma}\varphi)-\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(Q_{\Sigma}P_{\Omega}\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\Sigma}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}+\left\|\varphi-P_{\Omega}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\Sigma}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}+\varepsilon_{\Omega}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

Applying the triangle inequality and think to Theorem
4.4
, we show that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(Q_{\Sigma}P_{\Omega}\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}+\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)-\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(Q_{\Sigma}P_{\Omega}\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}+\varepsilon_{\Omega}\right)\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}+\varepsilon_{\Sigma}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}.

which gives the desired result.
∎

The following result gives the second continuous-time uncertainty principle of
concentration type for the
L^{1}_{\alpha}\cap L^{p}_{\alpha}
theory.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\Omega
,
\Sigma
be a measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
and
\varphi\in(L^{1}_{\alpha}\cap L^{p}_{\alpha})(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
,
1<p\leq 2
. If
\varphi
is
\varepsilon_{\Omega}
-concentrated to
\Omega
in
L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
-norm and
\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)
is
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}
-concentrated to
\Sigma
in
L^{q}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
-norm,
q=p/(p-1)
, then we have

\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq\frac{\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}}{(1-\varepsilon_{\Omega})(1-\varepsilon_{\Sigma})}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

Proof.

Let
\Omega
,
\Sigma
be a measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
such that
\mu_{\alpha}(\Omega)<\infty
and
\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)<\infty
. Assume that
\varphi\in(L^{1}_{\alpha}\cap L^{p}_{\alpha})(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
,
1<p\leq 2
. Like that
\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)
is
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}
-concentrated to
\Sigma
in
L^{q}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
-norm,
q=p/(p-1)
,then we get

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{\Sigma}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}+\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(Q_{\Sigma}\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{\Sigma}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}+\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,\infty}.

According to inequality (
2.5
), it follows

\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\varphi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}\leq\frac{\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}}{1-\varepsilon_{\Sigma}}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,1}.

(4.4)

On the other hand, since
\varphi
is
\varepsilon_{\Omega}
-concentrated to
\Omega
in
L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+})
-norm, then

\displaystyle\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,1}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{\Omega}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,1}+\left\|P_{\Sigma}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,1}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{\Omega}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,q}+\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

Hence

\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,1}\leq\frac{\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}}{1-\varepsilon_{\Omega}}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,q}.

(4.5)

Combining (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) we obtain the result of this theorem.
∎

4.2
Continuous-bandlimited uncertainty principle

In this section, we establish continuous-bandlimited uncertainty principle of
concentration. This principle depends on the sets of concentration

\Omega
and
\Sigma
, but he is independent on the bandlimited function
\varphi
.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
1\leq p\leq 2
and
\psi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
.

(i)

We say that
\psi
is bandlimited to
\Sigma
if

Q_{\Sigma}\psi=\psi
and denote by
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}^{p}(\Sigma)
the set of functions
\psi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
that are bandlimited to
\Sigma
.

(ii)

We say that
\varphi
is
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}
-bandlimited to
\Sigma
in
L^{p}_{\alpha}
-norm if there exists
\psi\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}^{p}(\Sigma)
such that

\left\|\varphi-\psi\right\|_{\alpha,p}\leq\varepsilon\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

The space of bandlimited functions
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}^{p}(\Sigma)
satisfies the following property.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\Omega
,
\Sigma
be a measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
. For all
\psi\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}^{p}(\Sigma)
,
1\leq p\leq 2
, we have

\left\|P_{\Omega}\psi\right\|_{\alpha,p}\leq\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\psi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

Proof.

The estimation is trivial if the measure of
\Omega
or
\Sigma
is infinite. Suppose that
\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)<\infty
and
\mu_{\alpha}(\Omega)<\infty
. Let
\psi\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}^{p}(\Sigma)
such that
1\leq p\leq 2
, then from Propostion
4.2
, we have

\psi(x)=\int_{\Sigma}\Lambda_{\alpha}^{d}(x,\lambda)\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\psi)(\lambda)d\mu_{\alpha}(\lambda).

From the property (
2.3
) and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain for all
q=p/(p-1)

\displaystyle\psi(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\mathcal{F}_{W,\alpha}(\psi)\right\|_{\alpha,q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\psi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

Applying now the time-limiting operator
P_{\Omega}
to the bandlimited function
\psi
and observing the
L^{p}_{\alpha}
-norm, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|P_{\Omega}\psi\right\|_{\alpha,p}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\psi(x)|^{p}d\mu_{\alpha}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\psi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

which yields the desired result.
∎

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\Omega
,
\Sigma
be a measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
and let
\varphi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
such that
1\leq p\leq 2
. Then if
\varphi
is
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}
-bandlimited to
\Sigma
in
L^{p}_{\alpha}
-norm, we have

\left\|P_{\Omega}\psi\right\|_{\alpha,p}\leq\left((1+\varepsilon_{\Sigma})\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\varepsilon_{\Sigma}\right)\left\|\psi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
such that
1\leq p\leq 2
. Like that
\varphi
is
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}
-bandlimited to
\Sigma
in
L^{p}_{\alpha}
-norm, then there exists
\psi\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}^{p}(\Sigma)
such that

\left\|\varphi-\psi\right\|_{\alpha,p}\leq\varepsilon\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}

and we have

\left\|P_{\Omega}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}\leq\left\|P_{\Omega}\psi\right\|_{\alpha,p}+\left\|P_{\Omega}(\varphi-\psi)\right\|_{\alpha,p}\leq\left\|P_{\Omega}\psi\right\|_{\alpha,p}+\varepsilon_{\Sigma}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

(4.6)

Observe now the result of Proposition
4.10
and the fact that

\left\|\psi\right\|_{\alpha,p}\leq(1+\varepsilon_{\Sigma})\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}

we obtain the desired result.
∎

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
\Omega
,
\Sigma
be a measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
and let
\varphi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
such that
1\leq p\leq 2
. Then if
\varphi
is
\varepsilon_{\Omega}
-concentrated to
\Omega
and
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}
-bandlimited to
\Sigma
in
L^{p}_{\alpha}
-norm, we have

1-\varepsilon_{\Omega}-\varepsilon_{\Sigma}\leq(1+\varepsilon_{\Sigma})\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Sigma)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\mu_{\alpha}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
such that
1\leq p\leq 2
. Like that
\varphi
is
\varepsilon_{\Omega}
-concentrated to
\Omega
in
L^{p}_{\alpha}
-norm, then by estimation (
4.2
) we obtain

\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}\leq\varepsilon_{\Omega}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}+\left\|P_{\Omega}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

Therefore

\left\|\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}\leq\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon_{\Omega}}\left\|P_{\Omega}\varphi\right\|_{\alpha,p}.

Finally, we deduce the desired estimation by inequality (
4.6
) and Theorem
4.11
.
∎

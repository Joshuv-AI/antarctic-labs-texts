Enumeration of connected bipartite graphs with given Betti number
Taro Hasui, Tomoyuki Shirai, Satoshi Yabuoku

Abstract
. We obtain first order linear partial differential equations which are satisfied by exponential generating functions of two variables for the number of connected bipartite graphs with given Betti number. By solving these equations inductively, we obtain the explicit form of generating functions and derive the asymptotic behavior of their coefficients. We also introduce a family of basic graphs to classify connected bipartite graphs and give another expression of the generating functions as the sum over basic graphs of rational functions of those for the number of labeled bipartite rooted spanning trees.

Primary 05A15, 05C31; Secondary 05C30.

1.
Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple graph, i.e., no self-loops and
multiple edges, and we call it an

(n,q)
-graph if
|V|=n
and
|E|=q
.
We denote the number of connected graphs with
k
independent cycles, by
N(n,k)
, which is also equal to the number of connected
(n,n-1+k)
-graphs.
Since we are dealing
with connected graphs, we note that
k
corresponds to the Betti number, the
rank of the first homology group, of each
(n,n-1+k)
-graph.
Note that
k-1
is often called
excess
since such
a connected graph has
k-1
more edges than vertices.
Connected
(n,n-1)
-graphs are
spanning trees
in
the complete graph
K_{n}
over
n
vertices
and it is known as Cayley’s formula
[
1
]
that
N(n,0)=n^{n-2}
.
Connected
(n,n)
-graphs are called
unicycles
and the formula for
N(n,1)

was found by Rényi
[
9
]
, which is given by

(1.1)

N(n,1)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{h(n)}{n}-n^{n-2}(n-1)\right)\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}}n^{n-1/2}\quad(n\to\infty),

where

h(n)=\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}{n\choose s}s^{s}(n-s)^{n-s}.

The asymptotic behavior of
N(n,k)
for general
k
as
n\to\infty
was
also discussed in
[
12
]
,
where the proofs are based
on recurrence equations which
N(n,k)
’s satisfy,
the algebraic structures of generating functions and their
derivatives, and the combinatorial aspect as will be
seen in Theorem
1.6
below.

We consider a bipartite simple graph
G=(V,E)
with
bipartition
V=V_{1}\sqcup V_{2}
and call it a bipartite
(r,s,q)
-graph if

|V_{1}|=r
,
|V_{2}|=s
and
|E|=q
, which is also considered
as a connected spanning subgraph with
q
-edges in the bipartite graph
K_{r,s}
.
We denote by
N_{{\rm bi}}(r,s,k)
the number of connected bipartite

(r,s,r+s+k-1)
-graphs, whose Betti number is
k
.
Similarly as before, connected bipartite

(r,s,r+s-1)
-graphs are spanning trees in
K_{r,s}
and it is well known
[
11
]

that

(1.2)

N_{{\rm bi}}(r,s,0)=r^{s-1}s^{r-1},

which is the bipartite version of Cayley’s formula.

When
rs=0
, we understand
N_{{\rm bi}}(r,s,0)=1
if
(r,s)=(1,0),(0,1)
;
=0
otherwise, i.e., the one-vertex simple graph is regarded as
a spanning tree.
Connected bipartite
(r,s,r+s)
-graphs are unicycles in

and discussed in the context of cuckoo
hushing by
[
8
]
. In the present paper, we discuss

N_{{\rm bi}}(r,s,k)
for

behavior of sum of

N_{{\rm bi}}(r,s,k)
with
r+s=n
.

We consider the exponential generating function of

defined as
follows: for
k=0,1,\dots

(1.3)

F_{k}(x,y):=\sum_{r,s=0}^{\infty}\frac{N_{{\rm bi}}(r,s,k)}{r!s!}x^{r}y^{s}.

For simplicity, we write the exponential generating function

1.2

2.
Recurrence equations

Let
N_{{\rm bi}}(r,s,q-r-s+1)
be the number of connected bipartite

(r,s,q)
-graphs as defined in the introduction.
Since an
(r,s,r+s-1)
-bipartite graph is a spanning tree and
we are dealing with simple graphs,
it is clear that

(2.1)

N_{{\rm bi}}(r,s,q-r-s+1)=0\quad\text{if $q<r+s-1$ or $q>rs$}.

As mentioned in (
1.2
),
N_{{\rm bi}}(r,s,0)=r^{s-1}s^{r-1}
.
Here we understand
0^{a}=\delta_{0,a}
as Kronecker’s
delta. For example,
N_{{\rm bi}}(1,0,0)=N_{{\rm bi}}(0,1,0)=1
and
N_{{\rm bi}}(0,0,0)=0
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
(r,s)\not=(0,0)
and
q=-1,0,1,\dots
, we have the following recurrence
equations:

(2.2)

(q+1)N_{{\rm bi}}(r,s,q-r-s+2)=(rs-q)N_{{\rm bi}}(r,s,q-r-s+1)+Q(r,s,q),

where

\displaystyle Q(r,s,q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r_{1}=0}^{r}\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{s}\sum_{t=0}^{q}{r\choose r_{1}}{s\choose s_{1}}\{(r-r_{1})s_{1}+r_{1}(s-s_{1})\}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\quad\times N_{{\rm bi}}(r_{1},s_{1},t-r_{1}-s_{1}+1)N_{{\rm bi}}(r-r_{1},s-s_{1},q-t-(r-r_{1})-(s-s_{1})+1)

and
Q(r,s,-1)=0
.

Proof.

Here we give a sketch of the proof.
Let
G=(V_{1},V_{2},E)
be an
(r,s)
-bipartite graph with
q
edges
and we add an edge to make a connected
(r,s)
-bipartite
graph with
q+1
edges.
There are two cases: (i)
G
itself is connected and (ii)
G

consists of two connected bipartite components.
For the case (i), we add an edge joining
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
.
For the case (ii), if
V_{j}=V_{j,1}\sqcup V_{j,2}\ (j=1,2)
, then there are four ways to add an edge joining
two bipartitions, i.e.,

V_{1,1}
and
V_{2,1}
,

V_{1,1}
and
V_{2,2}
,

V_{1,2}
and
V_{2,1}
, or

V_{1,2}
and
V_{2,2}
.
∎

Proposition 2.2

For

\displaystyle(D_{x}+D_{y}+k)F_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(D_{x}D_{y}-D_{x}-D_{y}+1-k)F_{k}+\sum_{l=0}^{k+1}D_{x}F_{l}\cdot D_{y}F_{k+1-l},

\displaystyle(r+s+k)N_{{\rm bi}}(r,s,k+1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r_{1}=0}^{r}\sum_{s_{1}=0}^{s}\sum_{t=0}^{k+1}{r\choose r_{1}}{s\choose s_{1}}\{(r-r_{1})s_{1}+r_{1}(s-s_{1})\}

\displaystyle\quad\times N_{{\rm bi}}(r_{1},s_{1},t)N_{{\rm bi}}(r-r_{1},s-s_{1},k-t+1).

\displaystyle=T_{xx}+T_{yx}=T_{x}+(T_{x}+1)T_{xy},

\displaystyle=Z-2W+\{(1-T_{y})(T_{x}+1)+(1-T_{x})(T_{y}+1)\}T_{xy}

4.1.
Asymptotic behavior of the coefficients of

We use the notation (

We recall the convolution of exponential generating
functions

(4.1)

\langle x^{n}\rangle A(x)B(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}a_{k}b_{n-k}

when
\langle x^{n}\rangle A(x)=a_{n}
and
\langle x^{n}\rangle B(x)=b_{n}

For an exponential power series
C(x,y)=\sum_{r,s=0}^{\infty}c_{rs}\frac{x^{r}y^{s}}{r!s!}
of two variables, we use the notation

\langle x^{r}y^{s}\rangle C(x,y)=c_{rs},

and we note that the coefficients of the diagonal

is given by

\langle x^{n}\rangle C(x,x)=\sum_{r+s=n}{n\choose r}c_{rs}.

In Section
3.1
, we derived the generating function

F_{1}(x,y)
for unicycles.
In this section, we focus on the coefficients of the
diagonal
F_{1}(x,x)
,

N_{\rm{bi}}(n,1):=\langle x^{n}\rangle F_{1}(x,x)=\sum_{r+s=n}{n\choose r}N_{{\rm bi}}(r,s,1),

which corresponds to the total of the numbers of complete

We will see the asymptotic behavior of

as

.

From Proposition
1.1
, we have

(4.2)

F_{1}(x,x)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{W(x,x)^{k}}{k}.

First we consider the coefficients of the diagonal
W(x,x)
.
Since
W=T_{x}+T_{y}-T
from (

), it is easy to see that

W(x,y)=\sum_{r,s=1}^{\infty}\frac{w(r,s)}{r!s!}x^{r}y^{s},

where

.
Hence, we have

W(x,x)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{r+s=n}\frac{w(r,s)n!}{r!s!}\right)\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=:\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}w_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!},

where

\displaystyle w_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r+s=n}\frac{r^{s-1}s^{r-1}(r+s-1)n!}{r!s!}

(4.3)

\displaystyle=(n-1)\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\binom{n}{r}r^{n-r-1}(n-r)^{r-1}.

The sum in (
4.1
) can be computed by the following
identity (cf.
[
6
]
).

Lemma 4.1
.

For
n=2,3,\dots
,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\binom{n}{r}r^{n-r-1}(n-r)^{r-1}=2n^{n-2}.

Proof.

Here we give a combinatorial proof of the identity.
Let
S_{n}
and
S_{n}^{b}
be the set of labeled
spanning trees on
K_{n}
and that of
labeled spanning trees on the complete bipartite graph with

n
vertices, respectively. Also, let
S_{r,s}^{b}
be the
set of labeled spanning trees on the complete bipartite
graph
K_{r,s}
. Then

\displaystyle S_{n}^{b}=\bigsqcup_{1\leq r\leq n-1}S_{r,n-r}^{b}.

For
(V_{1}\sqcup V_{2},E_{r,n-r})\in S_{r,n-r}^{b}
with

|V_{1}|=r
and
|V_{2}|=n-r
,
we define a map
\eta:S_{n}^{b}\to S_{n}
by

\displaystyle\eta((V_{1}\sqcup V_{2},E_{r,n-r})):=(V,E_{r,n-r}),

i.e., the map of forgetting partitions.
Since every spanning tree on
K_{n}
is bipartite,
\eta

is surjective. Moreover,
\eta
is two-to-one
mapping. Indeed, for
1\leq r\leq n-1
and
(V_{1}\sqcup V_{2},E_{r,n-r})\in S_{r,n-r}^{b}
, there exists a
unique spanning tree
(V_{1}^{\prime}\sqcup V_{2}^{\prime},E_{n-r,r})\in S_{n-r,r}^{b}
such that
V_{1}^{\prime}=V_{2},V_{2}^{\prime}=V_{1}
and

E_{n-r,r}=\{(i,j)\in V_{1}^{\prime}\times V_{2}^{\prime}:(j,i)\in E_{r,n-r}\}
. Now we derive (
4.4
).
For
1\leq r\leq n-1
,
|S_{r,n-r}^{b}|=\binom{n}{r}N_{{\rm bi}}(r,n-r,0)=\binom{n}{r}r^{n-r-1}(n-r)^{r-1}

by the choice of labeled
r

and
(

).
Hence

\displaystyle|S_{n}^{b}|=\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\binom{n}{r}r^{n-r-1}(n-r)^{r-1}.

On the other hand,

by Cayley’s formula.
Therefore, we conclude that (
4.4
) holds from the two-to-one correspondence of
\eta
.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

For
n=1,2,\dots
,

(4.5)

w_{n}=\langle x^{n}\rangle W(x,x)=2(n-1)n^{n-2}.

Now we proceed to the case of the power of

.
For

In particular,
w_{n}^{*1}=w_{n}
in Corollary

.
Note that the smallest degree of the terms in

is 2
and hence
w_{n}^{*k}=0
for

.
From (

is the
k

(w_{n})_{n=2,3,\dots}
and inductively defined by

(4.6)

w_{n}^{*(k+1)}=\sum_{r=2k}^{n-2}\binom{n}{r}w_{r}^{*k}w_{n-r}.

From (
4.2
), the coefficients

of

F_{1}(x,x)
are given by

(4.7)

\displaystyle N_{\rm{bi}}(n,1)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{2\leq k\leq n/2}\frac{w_{n}^{*k}}{k}.

Proposition 4.3
.

For
k=1,2,\dots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor
,

(4.8)

\displaystyle w_{n}^{*k}=2k\cdot(2k)!n^{n-2k-1}{n\choose 2k}.

Proof.

For fixed
n
, we prove the (
4.8
) by induction in
k
.
For
k=1
, it is obviously true since
w_{n}^{*1}=w_{n}
.
Suppose that (
4.8
) holds for up to
k
, then by (
4.5
) and
(
4.6
), we have

\displaystyle w_{n}^{*(k+1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{n}{n\choose r}w_{r}^{*k}w_{n-r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=2k}^{n-2}\binom{n}{r}2k\cdot(2k)!r^{r-2k-1}{r\choose 2k}\cdot 2(n-r-1)(n-r)^{n-r-2}

\displaystyle=4k\sum_{r=2k}^{n-2}\binom{n}{r}(r-1)\cdots(r-(2k-1))r^{r-1-(2k-1)}(n-r-1)(n-r)^{n-r-2}.

Now we introduce a class of polynomials which appears in Abel’s generalization of the binomial formula
[
10
, Section 1.5]
:

\displaystyle A_{n}(x,y;p,q):=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\binom{n}{r}(x+r)^{r+p}(y+n-r)^{n-r+q}.

In particular, when
p=q=-1
, it is known
[
10
, p.23]
that

(4.9)

A_{n}(x,y;-1,-1)=(x^{-1}+y^{-1})(x+y+n)^{n-1}.

Multiplying both sides by
xy
yields

\displaystyle(x+y)Q(x,y)

\displaystyle=xy\sum_{r=0}^{n}\binom{n}{r}(x+r)^{r-1}(y+n-r)^{n-r-1}

\displaystyle=x(x+n)^{n-1}+y(y+n)^{n-1}+xyS(x,y),

where
Q(x,y):=(x+y+n)^{n-1}
and

\displaystyle S(x,y):=\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\binom{n}{r}(x+r)^{r-1}(y+n-r)^{n-r-1}.

By the generalized Leibniz rule,
for

, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{p}(xS(x,y))

\displaystyle=p\partial_{x}^{p-1}S(x,y)+x\partial_{x}^{p}S(x,y),

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{p}((x+y)Q(x,y))

\displaystyle=p\partial_{x}^{p-1}Q(x,y)+(x+y)\partial_{x}^{p}Q(x,y),

which gives

(4.11)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{p}\partial_{y}^{2}(xyS(x,y))\Big|_{x=y=0}

\displaystyle=2pS^{(p-1,1)}(0,0),

(4.12)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{p}\partial_{y}^{2}((x+y)Q(x,y))\Big|_{x=y=0}

\displaystyle=(p+2)Q^{(p-1,2)}(0,0),

where
S^{(p,q)}(x,y):=\partial_{x}^{p}\partial_{y}^{q}S(x,y)
and

Q^{(p,q)}(x,y):=\partial_{x}^{p}\partial_{y}^{q}Q(x,y)
.

For

differentiating both sides of (
4.1
)
2k

and twice with respect to

) and (

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2k}\partial_{y}^{2}({\rm RHS\ of\ \eqref{eqS}})\Big|_{x=y=0}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}^{2k}\partial_{y}^{2}(xyS(x,y))\Big|_{x=y=0}

\displaystyle=4kS^{(2k-1,1)}(0,0)=w_{n}^{*(k+1)},

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2k}\partial_{y}^{2}({\rm LHS\ of\ \eqref{eqS}})\Big|_{x=y=0}

\displaystyle=(2k+2)Q^{(2k-1,2)}(0,0)

\displaystyle=2(k+1)\cdot(n-1)\cdots(n-(2k+1))n^{n-1-(2k+1)}

\displaystyle=2(k+1)\cdot(2(k+1))!n^{n-2(k+1)-1}\binom{n}{2(k+1)},

which complete the proof of (

∎

Now we derive the leading asymptotic behavior of
N_{\rm{bi}}(n,1)
as

.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we have

\displaystyle N_{\rm{bi}}(n,1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{2\leq k\leq n/2}(2k)!n^{n-2k-1}{n\choose 2k}

\displaystyle=n^{n-1}\sum_{2\leq k\leq n/2}\dfrac{n!}{(n-2k)!n^{2k}}.

The last summation is similar to the Ramanujan
Q
-function, so we treat this summation in the same way as in
[
3
, Section 4]
. Let
k_{0}
be an integer such that
k_{0}=o(n^{2/3})
and we split the summation into two parts:

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq k\leq n/2}\dfrac{n!}{(n-2k)!n^{2k}}=\sum_{2\leq k\leq k_{0}}\dfrac{n!}{(n-2k)!n^{2k}}+\sum_{k_{0}<k\leq n/2}\dfrac{n!}{(n-2k)!n^{2k}}.

For
k=o(n^{2/3})
, by
[
3
, Theorem 4.4]
we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{n!}{(n-2k)!n^{2k}}=e^{-2k^{2}/n}\left(1+O\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)+O\left(\frac{k^{3}}{n^{2}}\right)\right).

Because the terms in the summation are decreasing in
k
, and
e^{-2k^{2}/n}
are exponentially small for
k>k_{0}
, the second summation is negligible. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq k\leq n/2}\dfrac{n!}{(n-2k)!n^{2k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{2\leq k\leq k_{0}}e^{-2k^{2}/n}\left(1+O\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)+O\left(\frac{k^{3}}{n^{2}}\right)\right)+o(1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{2\leq k\leq k_{0}}e^{-2k^{2}/n}+O(1).

Again, since
e^{-2k^{2}/n}
are exponentially small for
k>k_{0}
, we can take the summation for
2\leq k\leq n/2
. Therefore, by Euler-Maclaurin’s formula we have

\displaystyle\sum_{2\leq k\leq n/2}e^{-2k^{2}/n}=\sqrt{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2x^{2}}dx+O(1)=\sqrt{\dfrac{\pi}{8}}\sqrt{n}+O(1),

which completes the proof.
∎

The proof is by induction in

\displaystyle=kx^{k+1}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+k)^{n-1}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\right)\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)^{n-1}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}\right)

so that

\displaystyle Y(x)^{k+1}

\displaystyle=x^{k+1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(k+1)(n+k+1)^{n-1}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=(k+1)\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}(n-1)(n-2)\cdots(n-k)n^{n-(k+1)}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

Hence, (

) holds for

Lemma

From Corollary

•

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}\frac{1}{g_{\mathcal{B}_{{\rm id}}}},

where we used
M=3(k-1)
in the last equation.
∎

Proposition 6.6

(

)
.

For

is expressed by the summation with respect to basic graphs
\mathcal{A}
:

\displaystyle W_{k}(x)=\sum_{\mathcal{A}\in BG^{\prime}_{k}}J_{\mathcal{A}}(x)

with

(6.9)

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{A}}(x)=\frac{Y^{L^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})}}{g_{\mathcal{A}}(1-Y)^{M^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})}},

where

is the set of basic graphs on complete graphs having

M^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})\leq 3(k-1)
,

and
g_{\mathcal{A}}

\mathcal{A}
.

Lemma 6.7
.

For
\mathcal{A}\in BG^{\prime}_{k}
, there exist unique constants
\{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}_{i}(\mathcal{A})\}_{i=1}^{M^{\prime}},\{\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{j}(\mathcal{A})\}_{j=0}^{L^{\prime}-M^{\prime}}
such that

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{A}}=\sum_{i=1}^{M^{\prime}}\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}_{i}(\mathcal{A})}{(1-Y)^{i}}+\sum_{j=0}^{L^{\prime}-M^{\prime}}\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}_{j}(\mathcal{A})Y^{j},\ \ \mathfrak{a}^{\prime}_{M^{\prime}}(\mathcal{A})=\frac{1}{g_{\mathcal{A}}}.

Proof.

To show the second equation, put
\theta=1-Y
in

∎

For each
\mathcal{A}\in BG^{\prime}_{k}
we contract their special cycles and paths and obtain a multigraph
\mathcal{A}^{*}
. Define
\psi:BG^{\prime}_{k}\to MG_{k}
be the mapping of the contraction. Note that
\psi
is not injective, but if
\psi(\mathcal{A}_{1})=\psi(\mathcal{A}_{2})=\mathcal{A}^{*}
for some
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}
, then the difference of the two graphs is only due to the difference of their
\delta^{\prime}
-paths and
\delta^{\prime}
-edges. Define

BG^{\prime}_{k}|_{3(k-1)}:=\{\mathcal{A}\in BG^{\prime}_{k}:M^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})=3(k-1)\}.

Let
\psi|_{3(k-1)}
be the restriction to

BG^{\prime}_{k}|_{3(k-1)}
of
\psi
, then this mapping is
bijective from
BG^{\prime}_{k}|_{3(k-1)}
to
MG_{k}
i.e,

\psi|_{3(k-1)}^{-1}(\mathcal{A}^{*})
is a singleton. Indeed, if

\mathcal{A}^{*}
has self-loops, replace them to
minimal
\alpha^{\prime}

\mathcal{A}^{*}
has single edges
or multiple edges, replace them to
\gamma^{\prime}
-paths or

\psi|_{3(k-1)}^{-1}(\mathcal{A}^{*})=\{\mathcal{A}\}
.

6.3.
Proof of the asymptotic equality (
1.10
)

We will prove the asymptotic equality (
1.10
). Take
\mathcal{B}^{*}\in MG_{k}
such that there exists
\mathcal{B}\in\phi^{-1}(\mathcal{B}^{*})
satisfying
M(\mathcal{B})=3(k-1)
. Then, there exist unique
\mathcal{B}_{{\rm id}}\in\phi^{-1}(\mathcal{B}^{*})
and
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{B}^{*}}:=\psi^{-1}|_{3(k-1)}(\mathcal{B}^{*})\in BG^{\prime}_{k}
. Then, we have

(6.10)

\displaystyle g_{\mathcal{B}_{{\rm id}}}=g_{\mathcal{A}},

because mappings
\phi
and
\psi
preserve the connectivity between each of vertices in
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{A}
, respectively. By Proposition
6.5
and (
6.10
), we have

(6.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{B}\in\phi^{-1}(\mathcal{B}^{*})\\
M(\mathcal{B})=3(k-1)\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{a}_{3(k-1)}(\mathcal{B})=\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}\frac{1}{g_{\mathcal{A}}}.

From Proposition
6.6
, the asymptotic behavior of
\langle x^{n}\rangle W_{k-1}(x)
is determined by the summation of
J_{\mathcal{A}}(x)
with respect to
\mathcal{A}
such that
M^{\prime}(\mathcal{A})=3(k-1)
. Hence, by Lemma
6.7
we have

\displaystyle N(n,k)

\displaystyle=\langle x^{n}\rangle W_{k}(x)

\displaystyle\sim\langle x^{n}\rangle\sum_{\mathcal{A}\in BG^{\prime}_{k}|_{3(k-1)}}J_{\mathcal{A}}(x)

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{\mathcal{A}\in BG^{\prime}_{k}|_{3(k-1)}}\langle x^{n}\rangle\left(\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}_{3(k-1)}(\mathcal{A})}{(1-Y)^{3(k-1)}}\right)

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\sum_{\mathcal{A}\in BG^{\prime}_{k}|_{3(k-1)}}\frac{1}{g_{\mathcal{A}}}\Bigg)t_{n}(3(k-1)),\quad n\to\infty,

where
t_{n}(p)
is the tree polynomials defined by (
4.19
).
On the other hand,
by Lemma
6.1
, Proposition
6.3
, (
6.11
) and the fact that
\psi|_{3(k-1)}
is bijective, we have

\displaystyle N_{\rm{bi}}(n,k)

\displaystyle=\langle x^{n}\rangle F_{k}(x,x)

\displaystyle\sim\langle x^{n}\rangle\sum_{\mathcal{B}^{*}\in MG_{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{B}\in\phi^{-1}(\mathcal{B}^{*})\\
M(\mathcal{B})=3(k-1)\end{subarray}}J_{\mathcal{B}}(x,x)

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{\mathcal{B}^{*}\in MG_{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{B}\in\phi^{-1}(\mathcal{B}^{*})\\
M(\mathcal{B})=3(k-1)\end{subarray}}\langle x^{n}\rangle\left(\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{3(k-1)}(\mathcal{B})}{(1-Y)^{3(k-1)}}\right)

\displaystyle=\Bigg(\sum_{\mathcal{B}^{*}\in MG_{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{B}\in\phi^{-1}(\mathcal{B}^{*})\\
M(\mathcal{B})=3(k-1)\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{a}_{3(k-1)}(\mathcal{B})\Bigg)t_{n}(3(k-1))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}\Bigg(\sum_{\mathcal{A}\in BG^{\prime}_{k}|_{3(k-1)}}\frac{1}{g_{\mathcal{A}}}\Bigg)t_{n}(3(k-1))

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}N(n,k),\quad n\to\infty,

hence the asymptotic equality (
1.10
) holds.

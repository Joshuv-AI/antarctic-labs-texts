The Bochner-Riesz problem: An old approach revisited
Shaoming Guo, Changkeun Oh, Hong Wang, Shukun Wu, Ruixiang Zhang

Abstract
. We show that the recent techniques developed to study the Fourier restriction problem apply equally well to the Bochner-Riesz problem. This is achieved via applying a pseudo-conformal transformation and a two-parameter induction-on-scales argument. As a consequence, we improve the Bochner-Riesz problem to the best known range of the Fourier restriction problem in all high dimensions.



1.
Introduction

For
\alpha\geq 0
, the Bochner-Riesz multiplier of order
\alpha
is defined by

(1.1)

m^{\alpha}(\xi):=(1-|\xi|^{2})^{\alpha}_{+},

where
(1-|\xi|^{2})_{+}
is defined to be
1-|\xi|^{2}
whenever
|\xi|\leq 1
, and
0
otherwise.
We define the Bochner-Riesz operator
m^{\alpha}(D)(f)
to be

(1.2)

m^{\alpha}(D)f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi i\langle x,\xi\rangle}m^{\alpha}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi.

The Bochner-Riesz conjecture is as follows.

Conjecture 1.1

(Bochner-Riesz conjecture)
.

For every
n\geq 2
and
p\geq 2n/(n-1)
, it holds that

(1.3)

\|m^{\alpha}(D)f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim_{n,\alpha,p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

whenever
\alpha>n(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{1}{2}
.

Let
S^{n-1}
denote the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
d\sigma
denote the surface measure of
S^{n-1}
. Take
f\in L^{\infty}(S^{n-1},d\sigma)
. Define

(1.4)

\widehat{fd\sigma}(x):=\int_{S^{n-1}}e^{2\pi i\langle x,\omega\rangle}f(\omega)d\sigma(\omega),\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The (dual form of) Fourier restriction conjecture is as follows.

Conjecture 1.2

(Restriction conjecture)
.

Let
n\geq 2
. It holds that

(1.5)

\|\widehat{fd\sigma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim_{n,p}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(S^{n-1},d\sigma)},

whenever
p>2n/(n-1)
.

Let
\mathfrak{R}f=\widehat{f}|_{S^{n-1}}
be the sphere restriction operator. By duality and the factorization theory
of Maurey, Nikishin and Pisier (see Bourgain
[
Bou91a
]
), the restriction conjecture is equivalent to that

(1.6)

\|\mathfrak{R}f\|_{L^{p}(S^{n-1})}\lesssim_{n,p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for every
1\leq p<2n/(n+1)
. Indeed, (
1.6
) is the original Fourier restriction conjecture of Stein (see for instance page 345 of
[
SS11
]
). Here we state the equivalent version as in Conjecture
1.2
since it is closer to the operator under investigation in the current paper.

Tao
[
Tao99
]
proved that the Bochner-Riesz conjecture implies the restriction conjecture. Moreover, he mentioned in his paper that these two conjectures “are widely believed to be at least heuristically equivalent”.
The information we would like to convey in the current paper is that, after applying the pseudo-conformal transformation (see (
2.12
) below), the recently developed techniques in the Fourier restriction literature apply equally well to the Bochner-Riesz problem. These techniques include, but are not limited to, the broad-narrow analysis of Bourgain and Guth
[
BG11
]
, the polynomial method of Guth in
[
Gut16
,
Gut18
]
, and the polynomial Wolff axioms obtained by Guth
[
Gut16
]
, Zahl
[
Zah18
]
, and Katz and Rogers
[
KR18
]
that was applied in the Fourier restriction setting in Guth
[
Gut16
]
, Hickman and Rogers
[
HR19
]
and Hickman and Zahl
[
HZ20
]
. To be slightly more precise, we will see that after the pseudo-conformal transformation, the above mentioned techniques do not see the differences between the Bochner-Riesz problem and the Fourier restriction problem. As a consequence, we show that the Bochner-Riesz conjecture holds for every
p
for which the restriction conjecture has been verified in the above mentioned papers.

To state our result, let us recall what is known about the restriction conjecture. For
n=3
, Guth
[
Gut16
]
proved the restriction conjecture for
p>3.25
; moreover, for
n\geq 4
, he proved in
[
Gut18
]
that the restriction conjecture holds if

(1.7)

\begin{split}&p>2\cdot\frac{3n+1}{3n-3}\text{ for }n\text{ odd}.\\
&p>2\cdot\frac{3n+2}{3n-2}\text{ for }n\text{ even}.\end{split}

These results improved prior ones due to Tao
[
Tao03
]
and Bourgain and Guth
[
BG11
]
. More recently, for certain dimensions
n
, in particular all “large”
n
, Hickman and Rogers
[
HR19
]
and Hickman and Zahl
[
HZ20
]
applied the polynomial Wolff axioms established by Katz and Rogers
[
KR18
]
and further improved the results of Guth. The new ranges of
p
in
[
HR19
]
and
[
HZ20
]
are a bit technical to state, and we refer the interested readers to Figure 2 in
[
HZ20
]
.

Let
p_{\mathrm{restr}}
denote the minimum of the exponent
p
for which the restriction conjecture has been verified in
[
Gut16
]
,
[
Gut18
]
,
[
HR19
]
and
[
HZ20
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For every
n\geq 3
, the Bochner-Riesz conjecture holds for every
p\geq p_{\mathrm{restr}}
.

Before we comment on the proof of Theorem
1.3
, let us briefly review some known results about the Bochner-Riesz conjecture in the literature. When
n=2
, the Bochner-Riesz conjecture was resolved by Carleson and Sjölin in
[
CS72
]
; see also Hörmander
[
H7̈3
]
and Fefferman
[
Fef73
]
for alternative proofs. When
n\geq 3
, this conjecture remains open; Fefferman
[
Fef70
]
, Bourgain
[
Bou91a
,
Bou91b
]
, Lee
[
Lee04
]
and Bourgain and Guth
[
BG11
]
made significant partial progress towards this conjecture; see also Christ
[
Chr88
]
, Seeger
[
See96
]
, Tao
[
Tao98
]
and Lee
[
Lee06
,
Lee18
]
for more related results and some endpoints results.
The most recent progress was made by Guth, Hickman and Iliopoulou
[
GHI19
]
. The approach in the current paper has closer relation with those in Bourgain and Guth
[
BG11
]
and Guth, Hickman and Iliopoulou
[
GHI19
]
, and therefore we expand a discussion on these two papers.

In these two papers, the authors there viewed the Bochner-Riesz operator as an oscillatory integral operator of the Hörmander type with positive-definite phase. To be more precise, they followed Carleson and Sjölin

The way that Guth, Hickman and Iliopoulou
[
GHI19
]
proved the sharpness of their result is built on the work of Bourgain
[
Bou91b

. The sharp examples in these two papers rely crucially on the fact that for general operators

) below) to the Bochner-Riesz operator, all the new wave packets admit similar behavior as those in the Fourier restriction problem, say in
[

]

[

]

[

]

[

Here we would like to emphasize that the use of the pseudo-conformal transformation in the context of the Bochner-Riesz problem is not new. Indeed, Carbery
[
Car92
]
already used it to prove that the Fourier restriction conjecture for paraboloids implies the Bochner-Riesz conjecture for paraboloids.

In the end, we would like to briefly mention some other interesting features of the proof of our main theorem, and compare them with those in the literature aforementioned, in particular, in
[
GHI19
]
where the latest progress on the Bochner-Riesz conjecture were made. Firstly, in
[
GHI19
]
, the authors there always first reduce phase functions to normal forms (see
[

[

[

]
(see also Remark

and
[

, which gives us a slightly simpler proof. More details are included in Section 6.

2.
Several reductions

For
\lambda\geq 1
, we define the Carleson-Sjölin operator

(2.1)

S^{\lambda}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi i\lambda|x-y|}a(x-y)f(y)\,dy,

where
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
has compact support away from the origin. To prove Theorem
1.3
, it is standard to reduce it to the following theorem. For the reduction, we refer to Stein
[
Ste93
, Chapter IX]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For every
n\geq 3
, it holds that

(2.2)

\|S^{\lambda}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\lambda^{-n/p+\epsilon}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for every
p\geq p_{\mathrm{restr}}
,
\lambda\geq 1
and
\epsilon>0
.

Next, we will reduce the
L^{p}
-boundedness of the Carleson-Sjölin operator to that of some operator
\bar{S}^{\lambda}:g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})\mapsto L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by freezing one variable. We define the operator
\bar{S}^{\lambda}g
by

(2.3)

\bar{S}^{\lambda}g(u,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}e^{2\pi i\lambda t^{-1}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|u-t\omega|^{2}}}a^{\lambda}(u,t;\omega)g(\omega)\,d\omega,

where
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
,
\omega=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, and

(2.4)

a^{\lambda}(u,t;\omega):=a\big(\frac{u}{\lambda}-\frac{t}{\lambda}\omega,\frac{t}{\lambda},\omega\big),

with
a(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
being a smooth function compactly supported in all variables and away from zero in its second variable.

Theorem 2.2
.

Under the above notation, it holds that

(2.5)

\|\bar{S}^{\lambda}g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\lambda^{\epsilon}\|g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}

for every
n\geq 3
,
p\geq p_{\mathrm{restr}}
,
\lambda\geq 1
and
\epsilon>0
.

Let us prove Theorem
2.1
by assuming Theorem
2.2
.
We fix
a
in the definition of the operator (
2.1
).
Since
a
has a compact support, by a partition of unity and rotation, we may assume that

(2.6)

\mathrm{supp}(a)\subset\{(x^{\prime},x_{n},y^{\prime},y_{n}):|x_{n}-y_{n}|\simeq 1\},

where
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})
and
y^{\prime}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1})
. We write the Carleson-Sjölin operator as

(2.7)

S^{\lambda}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\bar{S}_{y_{n}}^{\lambda}f_{y_{n}}(x)\,dy_{n},

where

(2.8)

\bar{S}_{y_{n}}^{\lambda}f_{y_{n}}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}e^{2\pi i\lambda|x-y|}a(x-y)f_{y_{n}}(y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}

and

(2.9)

f_{y_{n}}(y^{\prime}):=f(y^{\prime},y_{n}).

By Hölder’s inequality and by the fact that
a
has a compact support, it suffices to prove

(2.10)

\|\bar{S}^{\lambda}_{y_{n}}f_{y_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\lambda^{-n/p+\epsilon}\|f_{y_{n}}\|_{L^{p}_{y^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}

uniformly for every
|y_{n}|\lesssim 1
.
By a translation
x_{n}\mapsto x_{n}-y_{n}
, we obtain an operator independent of the variable
y_{n}
. Hence, we without loss of generality assume that
y_{n}=0
. To proceed, we write the phase function in the oscillatory integral (
2.8
) as follows:

(2.11)

\lambda\big|(x^{\prime},x_{n})-(y^{\prime},0)\big|=\lambda|x_{n}|\sqrt{1+|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{2}/x_{n}^{2}}.

We apply a change of variables

(2.12)

(u_{1},\dots,u_{n-1},t):=(x^{\prime}/x_{n},1/x_{n}),

which is the pseudo-conformal change of variables (see for instance Carbery
[
Car92
]
, Tao
[
Tao06
, Section 2.3]
or Rogers
[
Rog08
]
). By the support assumption on
a
, the Jacobian is comparable to one. After this change of variables, our operator
\bar{S}^{\lambda}_{0}f_{0}
becomes

(2.13)

\tilde{S}^{\lambda}f_{0}(u,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}e^{2\pi i\lambda t^{-1}\sqrt{1+|u-ty^{\prime}|^{2}}}\tilde{a}(u-ty^{\prime},t)f_{0}(y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime},

where
y^{\prime}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1})
and
\tilde{a}(\cdot,\cdot)
is a smooth function that has compact supports in all its variables and is supported away from the origin in its second variable. At this point, due to spatial orthogonality, we see that in (
2.13
) we can insert for free a smooth cut-off function
\tilde{a}(y^{\prime})
, supported near the origin, and it is equivalent to bound

(2.14)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}e^{2\pi i\lambda t^{-1}\sqrt{1+|u-ty^{\prime}|^{2}}}\tilde{a}(u-ty^{\prime},t)\tilde{a}(y^{\prime})f_{0}(y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}.

In the end, we pass from scale one to scale
\lambda
via the change of variables
(u,t)\mapsto\lambda^{-1}(u,t)
. This gives an operator of the form in (
2.3
), and therefore finishes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

In the end, we make a reduction to the amplitude function
a^{\lambda}(u,t;\omega)
so that it will have a product form
a_{1}^{\lambda}(u,t)a_{2}^{\lambda}(\omega)
. Before the reduction, let us fix the notation. In the definition of
\bar{S}^{\lambda}
, we see that the variable
t
plays a distinguished role, compared with other variables. Therefore, from now on, we will write
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})
and
{\bf x}=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Moreover, define

(2.15)

\phi^{\lambda}(x,t;\omega):=\lambda\phi\big(\frac{x}{\lambda},\frac{t}{\lambda};\omega\big):=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{t}\sqrt{1+|\frac{x}{\lambda}-\frac{t}{\lambda}\omega|^{2}}.

Under this notation, we can write

(2.16)

\bar{S}^{\lambda}g({\bf x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)}a^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)g(\omega)d\omega.

Via a standard Fourier expansion, it suffices to consider

(2.17)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}(x,t;\omega)}a_{1}^{\lambda}(x,t)a_{2}(\omega)g(\omega)d\omega,

where

(2.18)

a_{1}^{\lambda}(x,t):=a_{1}(x/\lambda,t/\lambda),

with
a_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)
being a compactly supported function in both variables and supported away from the origin in its second variable, and
a_{2}(\cdot)
is compactly supported near the origin. We need to prove

(2.19)

\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}(x,t;\omega)}a_{1}^{\lambda}(x,t)a_{2}(\omega)g(\omega)d\omega\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\lambda^{\epsilon}\|g\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}.

Such an estimate will be proven via an inductive argument on
\lambda
. In order to set up the induction, we need to be more quantitative about the choice of amplitude functions. Define

(2.20)

H^{\lambda}g({\bf x}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}(x,t;\omega)}g(\omega)d\omega.

Let
C_{n}
be a large enough constant such that

(2.21)

B_{C_{n}\lambda}(0)\setminus\{{\bf x}:|t|\leq\lambda/C_{n}\},\text{ with }B_{\lambda}(0):=[-\lambda,\lambda]^{n},

contains the support of
a^{\lambda}_{1}
.
To prove (
2.19
), it is elementary to see that it suffices to prove

Theorem 2.3
.

Under the above notation, it holds that

(2.22)

\Big\|H^{\lambda}g\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{C_{n}\lambda}(0)\setminus\{{\bf x}:|t|\leq\lambda/C_{n}\})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\lambda^{\epsilon}\|g\|_{L^{p}([0,1]^{n-1})},

for every
\epsilon>0
, every
g:[0,1]^{n-1}\mapsto\mathbb{C}
, dimension
n\geq 3
and exponent
p\geq p_{\mathrm{restr}}
.

3.1.
The induction hypothesis

Remark 3.1
.

We will see that it is crucial to run an induction on both parameters
\lambda
and
R
. It is worth mentioning that the smaller

behaves, which, roughly speaking, can be explained by that

appears in the denominator in (

). To deal with the case that

. In

Remark 3.2
.

The requirement that

The base case of the induction

\Big\|H^{\lambda^{\prime}}g\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{R^{\prime}})}\leq C_{n,p,\epsilon}(R^{\prime})^{\epsilon}\|g\|_{L^{p}([0,1]^{n-1})},

for every

3.2.
Reduction to broad norm estimate

For
{\bf x}\in[-\lambda_{R},\lambda_{R}]\times[R/C_{n},C_{n}\lambda]
and
\omega\in[0,1]^{n-1}
, we define Gauss maps and rescaled Gauss maps. Define

(3.6)

G_{0}({\bf x};\omega):=\partial_{\omega_{1}}\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi\wedge\dots\wedge\partial_{\omega_{n-1}}\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi.

Moreover, define

(3.7)

G({\bf x};\omega):=\frac{G_{0}({\bf x};\omega)}{|G_{0}({\bf x};\omega)|}.

Define rescaled Gauss map

(3.8)

G^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega):=G({\bf x}/\lambda;\omega).

Via some elementary computation, we obtain

Lemma 3.3
.

Under the above notation, we have that the rescaled Gauss maps equals

(3.9)

G^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)=\frac{(\omega,1)}{\sqrt{1+|\omega|^{2}}}.

In particular, the rescaled Gauss map does not depend on
{\bf x}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

For future use, we collect a few useful computations regarding the phase function
\phi^{\lambda}
. First of all,

(3.10)

\nabla_{x}\phi^{\lambda}(x,t;\omega)=\frac{\lambda}{t}\frac{(x-t\omega)}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x-t\omega|^{2}}};\ \ \partial_{t}\phi^{\lambda}(x,t;\omega)=-\frac{\lambda}{t^{2}}\frac{\lambda^{2}+x\cdot(x-t\omega)}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x-t\omega|^{2}}}.

Next, we compute the gradient in
\omega
:

(3.11)

\partial_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x,t;\omega)=\frac{\lambda(t\omega-x)}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x-t\omega|^{2}}}.

Regarding the related second order derivatives, we have

(3.12)

\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{\omega_{j}}\phi^{\lambda}(x,t;\omega)=\frac{\lambda(x_{i}-t\omega_{i})(x_{j}-t\omega_{j})}{\big(\lambda^{2}+|x-t\omega|^{2}\big)^{3/2}}\text{ if }i\neq j,

(3.13)

\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{\omega_{i}}\phi^{\lambda}(x,t;\omega)=-\frac{\lambda\big(\lambda^{2}+|x-t\omega|^{2}-(x_{i}-t\omega_{i})^{2}\big)}{\big(\lambda^{2}+|x-t\omega|^{2}\big)^{3/2}},

and

(3.14)

\partial_{t}\partial_{\omega_{j}}\phi^{\lambda}(x,t;\omega)=\frac{-x_{j}\omega\cdot(x-t\omega)+\omega_{j}x\cdot(x-t\omega)}{\big(\lambda^{2}+|x-t\omega|^{2}\big)^{3/2}}.

Combining all these, we obtain

(3.15)

\begin{split}&\partial_{\omega_{1}}\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}\wedge\dots\wedge\partial_{\omega_{n-1}}\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}\end{split}

is parallel to
(\omega,1)
. By normalization, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Let
K\geq 1
. We divide
[0,1]^{n-1}
into caps
\tau
of side length
K^{-2}
. Let
g_{\tau}
denote the restriction of
g
to
\tau
. Moreover,

(3.16)

G^{\lambda}(\tau):=\{G^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega):\omega\in\tau\}.

Let
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a linear subspace. Let
\measuredangle(G^{\lambda}(\tau),V)
denote the smallest angle between any non-zero vector
v\in V
and
v^{\prime}\in G^{\lambda}(\tau)
. Moreover, we say that
\tau\notin_{K}V
if
\measuredangle(G^{\lambda}(\tau),V)\geq K^{-1}
; otherwise, we say
\tau\in_{K}V
. If the value of
K
is clear from the context, we often abbreviate
\tau\notin_{K}V
to
\tau\notin V
.
Next, let us introduce the notion of broad norms. Fix
B_{K^{2}}\subset[-\lambda_{R},\lambda_{R}]\times[R/C_{n},C_{n}\lambda]
centered at
{\bf x}_{0}
. Define

(3.17)

\mu_{H^{\lambda}g}(B_{K^{2}}):=\min_{V_{1},\dots,V_{A}\in\text{Gr}(k-1,n)}\Big(\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\notin V_{a}\\
\text{ for any }1\leq a\leq A\end{subarray}}\|H^{\lambda}g_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(B_{K^{2}})}^{p}\Big).

Here
\mathrm{Gr}(k-1,n)
is the Gressmannian manifold of all
(k-1)
-dimensional subspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
k
is to be determined, and

is a parameter that is less important and its choice will become clear later. For

, define

(3.18)

\|H^{\lambda}g\|_{\mathrm{BL}_{k,A}^{p}(U)}:=\Big(\sum_{B_{K^{2}}}\frac{|B_{K^{2}}\cap U|}{|B_{K^{2}}|}\mu_{H^{\lambda}g}(B_{K^{2}})\Big)^{1/p}.

Next we should study and prove broad norm estimates.

Theorem 3.4

(Broad norm estimate)
.

Let
2\leq k\leq n-1
, and

(3.19)

p\geq p_{n}(k):=2+\frac{6}{2(n-1)+(k-1)\prod_{i=k}^{n-1}\frac{2i}{2i+1}}.

Then for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
A
such that

(3.20)

\|H^{\lambda}g\|_{\mathrm{BL}_{k,A}^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim_{K,\epsilon}R^{\epsilon}\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{2/p}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-2/p},

for every
K\geq 1
,
1\leq R\leq\lambda
, where
B_{R}
is a ball of radius
R
satisfying
B_{R}\subset[-3C_{n}\lambda,3C_{n}\lambda]^{n-1}\times[R/C_{n},C_{n}\lambda]
. Moreover, the implicit constant depends polynomially on
K
.

In the rest of this section, we will finish the proof of Theorem
2.3
by assuming Theorem

. We will show that Theorem
3.4
implies (
2.22
) whenever

and

2+\frac{4}{2n-k}\leq p\leq 2+\frac{2}{k-2}.

The same optimization process as in Hickman and Zahl
[
HZ20
, page 4]
will give Theorem

.

Let us begin the proof. The main steps are the proofs of the following Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.8
. After proving these two lemmas, it is standard to deduce Theorem
2.3
whenever
p
satisfies (
3.21
). For the sake of completeness, we provide some details for this step. By a restricted type interpolation, we may assume that
g=\chi_{E}
for some set
E
. Let us take
K=R^{\epsilon^{\prime}}
for some
0<\epsilon^{\prime}\ll\epsilon
. By Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.8
, and a standard application of the broad-narrow analysis (see for instance
[
GHI19
, page 358]
) of Bourgain and Guth
[
BG11
]
and Guth
[
Gut18
]
, one obtains that

(3.22)

\|H^{\lambda}g\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim_{n,p,\epsilon}R^{\epsilon/2}\|g\|_{2}^{p/2}\|g\|_{\infty}^{1-2/p}+R^{\epsilon}R^{-C\epsilon^{\prime}}\|g\|_{p}.

Since
g
is a characteristic function, the above term is bounded by a constant multiple of

(3.23)

\big(R^{-\epsilon/2}+R^{-C\epsilon^{\prime}}\big)R^{\epsilon}\|g\|_{L^{p}}.

Since

is a large number, this closes the induction and completes the proof.

From now on, we will focus on the proofs of those two lemma. We start with Lemma
3.5
, a decoupling inequality.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
2\leq m\leq n
and
V
be an
m
-dimensional linear subspace in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
K=K_{R}
and
B_{K^{2}}\subset[-\lambda_{R},\lambda_{R}]\times[R/C_{n},C_{n}\lambda]
. For every
2\leq p\leq 2m/(m-1)
and every
\varepsilon>0
, it holds that

(3.24)

\begin{split}&\Big\|\sum_{\tau\in V:l(\tau)=K^{-1}}H^{\lambda}g_{\tau}\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{K^{2}})}\lesssim_{N,\varepsilon,p}\mathrm{RapDec}(\lambda)\|g\|_{2}+\\
&\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n-1}}\sum_{\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}\sum_{{\bf w}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}\frac{2^{-N|\beta^{\prime}|}K^{(m-1)(1/2-1/p)+\varepsilon}}{(1+|\beta|)^{10n}(1+|{\bf w}|)^{100n}}\Big(\sum_{\tau}\|H^{\lambda}(g_{\tau}(\omega)b_{{\bf w},\beta^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{1};\omega)e^{2\pi i\beta\cdot\omega})\|_{L^{p}(B_{K^{2}})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}.\end{split}

Here
{\bf x}_{1}
is the center of
B_{R}
(not
B_{K^{2}}
),
\tau\in V
means
\tau\in_{K}V
, and
N
can be as large as we would like. The function
b_{{\bf w},\beta^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{1};\omega)
satisfies the uniform bound
|b_{{\bf w},\beta^{\prime}}|\lesssim 1
in all parameters.

The proof is based on the decoupling inequality of Bourgain and Demeter
[
BD15
]
and Taylor’s expansion. It is quite important that on the right hand side we have
B_{K^{2}}
without any tails.

Remark 3.6
.

We would like to mention that it is crucial for
b_{{\bf w},\beta^{\prime}}
not to depend on the location of
B_{K^{2}}
. This will allow us sum over all balls
B_{K^{2}}\subset B_{R}
. If
b_{{\bf w},\beta^{\prime}}
were allowed to depend on the location of
B_{K^{2}}
, the proof of Lemma
3.5
would be much simpler and we would not need the requirement that
R\leq\lambda^{1-\epsilon}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

Let us use
{\bf x}_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})
to denote the center of
B_{K^{2}}
. We will approximate the phase function
\phi^{\lambda}
on
B_{K^{2}}
. Write

(3.25)

\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)-\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega)+\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega){\bf x}_{0}=\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega){\bf x}+e^{\lambda}_{2,{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x};\omega).

Here
e^{\lambda}_{2,{\bf x}_{0}}
is the second order remainder term in Taylor’s expansion. To see what estimates it satisfies, let us first collect some useful estimates for various derivatives. First of all, from (
3.10
), we see that

(3.26)

\begin{split}&\big|\nabla_{x}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)\big|\lesssim\lambda/R,\ \ \big|\partial_{t}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)\big|\lesssim(\lambda/R)^{2}.\end{split}

By taking a further derivative in
{\bf x}
, we obtain

(3.27)

\big|\nabla^{2}_{x}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)\big|\lesssim 1/R,\ \ \big|\partial_{tt}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)\big|\lesssim\lambda^{2}/R^{3}.

We see the second order derivative in
x
is not bad, but that in
t
is always very bad when
R\ll\lambda
. In other words, we do not necessarily know that the “error” term
e^{\lambda}_{2,{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x};\omega)
has amplitude smaller than one. One way of fixing this problem is to observe that

(3.28)

\big|e^{\lambda}_{2,{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x};\omega)-e^{\lambda}_{2,{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x};0)\big|\lesssim K^{4}/\lambda,

which follows from

(3.29)

\big|\nabla_{x,t}^{2}\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)\big|\lesssim\lambda^{-1}

and mean value theorems; the pointwise bound (
3.29
) follows from taking a further derivative in
{\bf x}
for the terms in (
3.12
), (
3.13
) and (
3.14
). For later use, we record more estimates on higher order derivatives of the phase function. Via the chain rule, we obtain

(3.30)

|\nabla_{{\bf x}}^{\beta}\nabla_{\omega}^{\beta^{\prime}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)|\lesssim_{\beta}\lambda^{-|\beta|+1}

for every multi-indices
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
with
|\beta|,|\beta^{\prime}|\geq 1
. In particular, these imply

(3.31)

|\nabla^{\beta}_{\omega}e^{\lambda}_{2,{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x};\omega)|\lesssim_{\beta}K^{4}/\lambda,\text{ for every }|\beta|\geq 1.

Let us write

(3.32)

\begin{split}&\sum_{\tau\in V}H^{\lambda}g_{\tau}=\sum_{\tau}\int e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)}g_{\tau}(\omega)d\omega\\
&=\sum_{\tau}e^{2\pi ie^{\lambda}_{2,{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x};0)}\int e^{2\pi i\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega){\bf x}}e^{2\pi ie^{\lambda}_{2,{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x};\omega)-2\pi ie^{\lambda}_{2,{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x};0)}\big(g_{\tau}(\omega)e^{2\pi i(\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega)-\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega){\bf x}_{0})}\big)d\omega.\end{split}

Denote

(3.33)

a^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x};\omega):=e^{2\pi ie^{\lambda}_{2,{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x};\omega)-2\pi ie^{\lambda}_{2,{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x};0)}

and

(3.34)

g^{\lambda}_{\tau,{\bf x}_{0}}(\omega):=\big(g_{\tau}(\omega)e^{2\pi i(\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega)-\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega){\bf x}_{0})}\big).

We apply the Fourier expansion to
a^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x};\omega)
and write it as

(3.35)

\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n-1}}a_{\beta,{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}({\bf x})e^{2\pi i\beta\cdot\omega};

the bound in (
3.28
) guarantees that such an expansion is meaningful. So far we have

(3.36)

\sum_{\tau}H^{\lambda}g_{\tau}=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n-1}}\sum_{\tau}e^{2\pi ie^{\lambda}_{2,{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x};0)}\int e^{2\pi i(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega))\cdot{\bf x}}a^{\lambda}_{\beta,{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x})g^{\lambda}_{\tau,{\bf x}_{0}}(\omega)e^{2\pi i\beta\cdot\omega}d\omega.

By the triangle inequality,

(3.37)

\begin{split}\|\sum_{\tau}H^{\lambda}g_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(B_{K^{2}})}&\leq\sum_{\beta}\Big\|\sum_{\tau}\int e^{2\pi i(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega))\cdot{\bf x}}a^{\lambda}_{\beta,{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x})g^{\lambda}_{\tau,{\bf x}_{0}}(\omega)e^{2\pi i\beta\cdot\omega}d\omega\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{K^{2}})}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{\beta}(1+|\beta|)^{-10n}\Big\|\sum_{\tau}\int e^{2\pi i(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega))\cdot{\bf x}}g^{\lambda}_{\tau,{\bf x}_{0}}(\omega)e^{2\pi i\beta\cdot\omega}d\omega\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{K^{2}})},\end{split}

where the decay in
\beta
follows from (
3.30
) and (
3.31
). Via a direct computation, we obtain
(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega))\cdot{\bf x}
equals

(3.38)

\begin{split}&\frac{\lambda}{t_{0}}\frac{x_{0}-t_{0}\omega}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x_{0}-t_{0}\omega|^{2}}}\cdot x-\frac{\lambda(\lambda^{2}+|x_{0}|^{2}-t_{0}(x_{0}\cdot\omega))}{t_{0}^{2}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x_{0}-t_{0}\omega|^{2}}}\cdot t.\end{split}

Regarding the parametrized surface

(3.39)

\Big(\frac{\lambda}{t_{0}}\frac{x_{0}-t_{0}\omega}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x_{0}-t_{0}\omega|^{2}}},-\frac{\lambda(\lambda^{2}+|x_{0}|^{2}-t_{0}(x_{0}\cdot\omega))}{t_{0}^{2}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x_{0}-t_{0}\omega|^{2}}}\Big),

we have that the Jacobian of the first
(n-1)
components in
\omega
is comparable to
1
; more precisely, without loss of generality, we may assume that the first component of
x_{0}-t_{0}w
is nonzero. Then the Jacobian matrix
\nabla_{\omega}\nabla_{x}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega)
, which are explicitly calculated in (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), has eigenvalue
-\lambda^{3}(\lambda^{2}+|x_{0}-t_{0}\omega|^{2})^{-3/2}
with the eigenvector
x_{0}-t_{0}w
, and eigenvalue and
-\lambda(\lambda^{2}+|x_{0}-t_{0}\omega|^{2})^{-1/2}
with the
n-1
linearly independent eigenvectors

(3.40)

(-(x_{0}-t_{0}w)_{2},(x_{0}-t_{0}w)_{1},0,\ldots,0),\ldots,(-(x_{0}-t_{0}w)_{n},0,\ldots,0,(x_{0}-t_{0}w)_{1}),

where
(x_{0}-t_{0}w)_{i}
is the
i
th component of the vector
x_{0}-t_{0}w
. This can be verified by a direct computation. Therefore, we obtain

(3.41)

\big|\det\nabla_{\omega}\nabla_{x}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega)\big|=\lambda^{n+2}\big(\lambda^{2}+|x_{0}-t_{0}\omega|^{2}\big)^{-(n+2)/2}\simeq 1.

As a consequence, we obtain that the above parametrized surface is regular. Next, we will show that it has non-vanishing Gaussian curvatures. Recall the computation of normal directions in Lemma
3.3
. Via a direct computation, we see that the Gaussian curvature at a fixed point
\omega_{0}
is comparable to

(3.42)

\begin{split}&\det\Big(\nabla^{2}_{\omega}\langle\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega),(\omega_{0},1)\rangle\Big)\Big|_{\omega=\omega_{0}}\simeq 1.\end{split}

This, combined with the fact that

(3.43)

\nabla^{2}_{\omega}\langle\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega),(\omega_{0},1)\rangle\big|_{x_{0}=0;\omega_{0}=0}

is the identity matrix and a simple continuity argument, further implies that the parametrized surface in (
3.39
) is elliptic. This allows us to apply decoupling inequalities for surfaces of non-vanishing Gaussian curvatures, due to Bourgain and Demeter
[
BD15
]
, and bound (
3.37
) by

(3.44)

\sum_{\beta}(1+|\beta|)^{-10n}K^{(m-1)(1/2-1/p)+\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{\tau}\Big\|\int e^{2\pi i(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega))\cdot{\bf x}}g^{\lambda}_{\tau,{\bf x}_{0}}(\omega)e^{2\pi i\beta\cdot\omega}d\omega\Big\|_{L^{p}(w_{B_{K^{2}}})}^{p}\Big)^{1/p},

where
w_{B_{K^{2}}}
is given by

(3.45)

\big(1+K^{-2}|{\bf x}-{\bf x}_{0}|\big)^{-N(\epsilon)},

where
N(\epsilon)
is a large constant depending on
\epsilon
and its choice will become later.

Remark 3.7
.

We will need
N(\epsilon)\to\infty
much faster than
1/\epsilon
as
\epsilon\to 0
. For instance, taking
N(\epsilon)=\epsilon^{-100}
will be more than enough. This choice of weight will be used in the following way: If
|{\bf x}-{\bf x}_{0}|\geq K^{2}\lambda^{\epsilon^{10}}
, then the weight function can be controlled by
\lambda^{-\epsilon^{-90}}
, and therefore is negligible as
\mathrm{RapDec}(\lambda)
.

The weight function
w_{B_{K^{2}}}
has tails that are not allowed in the study of the Bochner-Riesz problem, and we need to get rid of it. For
{\bf w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, let
B_{K^{2},{\bf w}}
be the translation of
B_{K^{2}}
by
K^{2}{\bf w}
.

(3.46)

\begin{split}&\Big\|\int e^{2\pi i(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega))\cdot{\bf x}}g^{\lambda}_{\tau,{\bf x}_{0}}(\omega)e^{2\pi i\beta\cdot\omega}d\omega\Big\|_{L^{p}(w_{B_{K^{2}}})}\\
&\leq\mathrm{RapDec}(\lambda)\|g\|_{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
|{\bf w}|\leq\lambda^{\epsilon^{10}}\end{subarray}}(1+|{\bf w}|)^{-N(\epsilon)}\Big\|\int e^{2\pi i(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega))\cdot{\bf x}}g^{\lambda}_{\tau,{\bf x}_{0}}(\omega)e^{2\pi i\beta\cdot\omega}d\omega\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{K^{2},{\bf w}})}.\end{split}

As we have a constant coefficient operator in the last display, we can modulate the function
g^{\lambda}_{\tau,{\bf x}_{0}}
such that the ball
B_{K^{2},{\bf w}}
is shifted back to
B_{K^{2}}
. Let us use
e_{{\bf w},K}({\bf x}_{0};\omega)
to denote the modulation function. Therefore,

(3.47)

e_{{\bf w},K}({\bf x}_{0};\omega)=e^{2\pi iK^{2}(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega))\cdot{\bf w}},

and we have the bound

(3.48)

\begin{split}&\Big\|\int e^{2\pi i(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega))\cdot{\bf x}}g^{\lambda}_{\tau,{\bf x}_{0}}(\omega)e^{2\pi i\beta\cdot\omega}d\omega\Big\|_{L^{p}(w_{B_{K^{2}}})}\\
&\leq\mathrm{RapDec}(\lambda)\|g\|_{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf w}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
|{\bf w}|\leq\lambda^{\epsilon^{10}}\end{subarray}}(1+|{\bf w}|)^{-N(\epsilon)}\Big\|\int e^{2\pi i(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega))\cdot{\bf x}}g^{\lambda}_{\tau,{\bf x}_{0}}(\omega)e_{{\bf w},K}({\bf x}_{0};\omega)e^{2\pi i\beta\cdot\omega}d\omega\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{K^{2}})}.\end{split}

Unfortunately the modulation function in (
3.47
) depends on
{\bf x}_{0}
. Let us try to get rid of this dependence via Taylor’s expansion; we learnt this idea from Beltran, Hickman and Sogge
[
BHS20
]
. First of all, let us write the
L^{p}
norm on the right hand side of (
3.48
) as

(3.49)

\Big\|\int e^{2\pi i(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega))\cdot{\bf x}}g^{\lambda}_{\tau,{\bf x}_{0}}(\omega)b_{{\bf w},K}({\bf x}_{0};\omega)e^{2\pi i\beta\cdot\omega}d\omega\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{K^{2}})},

with

(3.50)

b_{{\bf w},K}({\bf x}_{0};\omega):=e^{2\pi iK^{2}{\bf w}\cdot(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega)-\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};0))}.

To get rid of the dependence of
b_{{\bf w},K}
on
{\bf x}_{0}
, we carry out a Taylor expansion of it about
{\bf x}_{1}
, an arbitrary point inside
B_{R}
; for convenience, we take
{\bf x}_{1}
to be the center of
B_{R}
. Recall the bound in (
3.30
). As a consequence, we obtain that

(3.51)

\Big|\nabla_{{\bf x}}^{\beta}\Big[K^{2}{\bf w}\cdot(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)-\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};0))\Big]\Big|\lesssim K^{2}|{\bf w}|\lambda^{-|\beta|},\text{ for every }\beta,

which further implies that

(3.52)

\big|\nabla_{{\bf x}}^{\beta}b_{{\bf w},K}({\bf x};\omega)\big|\lesssim(K^{2}|{\bf w}|)^{|\beta|}\lambda^{-|\beta|}\text{ for every }|\beta|\geq 1.

This allows us to write

(3.53)

b_{{\bf w},K}({\bf x}_{0};\omega)=\sum_{\beta^{\prime}}2^{-N|\beta^{\prime}|}(K^{2}|{\bf w}|)^{|\beta^{\prime}|}\lambda^{-|\beta^{\prime}|}b_{{\bf w},\beta^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{1};\omega)({\bf x}_{0}-{\bf x}_{1})^{\beta^{\prime}}+\mathrm{RapDec}(\lambda),

where the dependence of
b_{{\bf w},\beta^{\prime}}
on
K
has been compressed, and these Taylor coefficients satisfy the uniform bound
|b_{{\bf w},\beta^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{1};\omega)|\lesssim 1
.
We go back to (
3.49
) and bound it by

(3.54)

\sum_{\beta^{\prime}}2^{-N|\beta^{\prime}|}(K^{2}\lambda^{\epsilon^{10}})^{|\beta^{\prime}|}R^{|\beta^{\prime}|}\lambda^{-|\beta^{\prime}|}\Big\|\int e^{2\pi i(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega))\cdot{\bf x}}g^{\lambda}_{\tau,{\bf x}_{0}}(\omega)b_{{\bf w},\beta^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{1};\omega)e^{2\pi i\beta\cdot\omega}d\omega\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{K^{2}})}.

Recall the assumption on
R
that
R\leq\lambda^{1-\epsilon}
. It can be used to control the constant factors in the last display. So far we have controlled (
3.46
) by

(3.55)

\mathrm{RapDec}(\lambda)\|g\|_{2}+\sum_{\beta^{\prime}}\sum_{{\bf w}}2^{-N|\beta^{\prime}|}(1+|{\bf w}|)^{-N(\epsilon)}\Big\|\int e^{2\pi i(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega))\cdot{\bf x}}g^{\lambda}_{\tau,{\bf x}_{0}}(\omega)b_{{\bf w},\beta^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{1};\omega)e^{2\pi i\beta\cdot\omega}d\omega\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{K^{2}})}.

Now each term we have is of the form

(3.56)

\begin{split}&\int e^{2\pi i(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega))\cdot{\bf x}}g^{\lambda}_{\tau,{\bf x}_{0}}(\omega)b_{{\bf w},\beta^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{1};\omega)e^{2\pi i\beta\cdot\omega}d\omega\\
&=\int e^{2\pi i(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega))\cdot{\bf x}}g_{\tau}(\omega)e^{2\pi i(\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega)-\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega){\bf x}_{0})}b_{{\bf w},\beta^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{1};\omega)e^{2\pi i\beta\cdot\omega}d\omega\\
&=\int e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)-2\pi ie^{\lambda}_{2,{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x};\omega)}g_{\tau}(\omega)b_{{\bf w},\beta^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{1};\omega)e^{2\pi i\beta\cdot\omega}d\omega.\end{split}

We apply a Fourier expansion “back” (compared with the one in (
3.33
)–(
3.35
)) to get rid of
e^{\lambda}_{2,{\bf x}_{0}}({\bf x};\omega)
and obtain

(3.57)

\begin{split}&\Big\|\int e^{2\pi i(\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega))\cdot{\bf x}}g^{\lambda}_{\tau,{\bf x}_{0}}(\omega)b_{{\bf w},\beta^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{1};\omega)e^{2\pi i\beta\cdot\omega}d\omega\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{K^{2}})}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{\beta^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n-1}}(1+|\beta^{\prime\prime}|)^{-10n}\Big\|\int e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)}g_{\tau}(\omega)b_{{\bf w},\beta^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{1};\omega)e^{2\pi i(\beta+\beta^{\prime\prime})\cdot\omega}d\omega\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{K^{2}})},\end{split}

which, when substituted into (
3.55
) and (
3.44
), implies the desired the decoupling inequality.
∎

Lemma 3.8

(Parabolic rescaling)
.

Let
w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n-1})\in[0,1]^{n-1}
and
K=K_{R}
. Let
\tau=[w_{1},w_{1}+K^{-1}]\times\dots\times[w_{n-1},w_{n-1}+K^{-1}]
with
\tau\subset[0,1]^{n-1}
.
Under the induction hypothesis, for every ball
B_{R}
with
1\leq R\leq\lambda^{1-\epsilon}
satisfying

(3.58)

B_{R}\subset[-\lambda_{K,R},\lambda_{K,R}]\times[R/C_{n},C_{n}\lambda]

we obtain

(3.59)

\|H^{\lambda}g_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}(B_{R})}\lesssim_{\epsilon,\delta}R^{\epsilon}R^{\delta}(K^{-1})^{(n-1)-\frac{2n}{p}}\|g_{\tau}\|_{L^{p}([0,1]^{n-1})},

for every
\delta>0
.

Proof of Lemma
3.8
.

We use a change of variables

(3.60)

\eta:=-w+\omega.

Recall that our phase function is given by

(3.61)

\phi^{\lambda}(x,t;\omega)=\frac{\lambda}{t}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x-t\omega|^{2}}.

Under the change of variables, it becomes

(3.62)

\frac{\lambda}{t}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x-t(\eta+w)|^{2}}=\frac{\lambda}{t}\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|(x-tw)-t\eta|^{2}}.

Next we apply the change of variables

(3.63)

x-tw\mapsto x;\ t\mapsto t.

Under this change of variables, let us assume that
B_{R}
becomes
\widetilde{B}_{R}
. Note that
\widetilde{B}_{R}
is a parallelogram: The smallest rectangle that contains it is at most twice as large as
B_{R}
.
Now our phase becomes

(3.64)

\frac{\lambda}{t}\sqrt{1+|x-t\eta|^{2}},

and therefore we need to bound

(3.65)

\|H^{\lambda}g_{\widetilde{\tau}}\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{B}_{R})},\text{ with }\widetilde{\tau}=[0,1/K]^{n-1}.

Next we apply a scaling argument and set
\eta\mapsto\eta/K.

Our phase becomes

(3.66)

\frac{\lambda}{t}\sqrt{1+|x-t\frac{\eta}{K}|^{2}}.

In the end, we apply the change of variables
x\mapsto Kx;t\mapsto K^{2}t.

It is important to keep track of the domain of evaluating the
L^{p}
norm. Let us use
\widetilde{D}_{R}
to denote the smallest rectangle that contains the image of
\widetilde{B}_{R}
after the above change of variables. After some elementary computation, we see that

(3.67)

\widetilde{D}_{R}\subset[-\frac{\lambda_{K,R}}{K}-\frac{C_{n}R}{K},\frac{\lambda_{K,R}}{K}+\frac{C_{n}R}{K}]^{n-1}\times[\frac{R}{K^{2}}\frac{1}{C_{n}},\frac{\lambda}{K^{2}}C_{n}],

and
\widetilde{D}_{R}
is of dimension

(3.68)

(2\frac{R}{K})\times(2\frac{R}{K})\times\dots\times(2\frac{R}{K})\times\frac{R}{K^{2}}.

Set
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda/K
,
R^{\prime}=R/K
and
R^{\prime\prime}=R/K^{2}
. What is important in (
3.67
) is that

(3.69)

\frac{\lambda_{K,R}}{K}+\frac{C_{n}R}{K}\leq\lambda^{\prime}_{K,R^{\prime\prime}}\leq\lambda^{\prime}_{R^{\prime\prime}},

which is exactly the reason of having a complicated expression of
\lambda_{K,R}
in our induction hypothesis. As a consequence, we know that

(3.70)

\widetilde{D}_{R}\subset[-\lambda^{\prime}_{K,R^{\prime\prime}},\lambda^{\prime}_{K,R^{\prime\prime}}]\times[R^{\prime\prime}/C_{n},\lambda^{\prime}C_{n}].

Remark 3.9
.

Here we make a remark on the choice of the
t
-interval
[R/C_{n},C_{n}\lambda]
in (
3.3
) from our induction hypothesis. In our current setup, it should not be replaced by anything like
[R/C_{n},C_{n}R]
or
[R/C_{n},C_{n}R^{\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}}]
. For instance, if we replace it by the latter, then the analogue of (
3.70
), which is

(3.71)

\widetilde{D}_{R}\subset[-\lambda^{\prime}_{K,R^{\prime\prime}},\lambda^{\prime}_{K,R^{\prime\prime}}]\times[R^{\prime\prime}/C_{n},(R^{\prime\prime})^{\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}}C_{n}],

does not hold, and therefore we can not apply the induction hypothesis. If one would really like to use a time interval of the form
[R/C_{n},C_{n}R^{\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}}]
in the induction hypothesis, which may bring some convenience like
t
is always essentially comparable to
R
, then one can cut
\widetilde{D}_{R}
into smaller balls and then make use of the extra gain in
K
from (
3.72
), which is not explored in the current setup.

From now on, we will try to bound
\|H^{\lambda^{\prime}}\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{D}_{R})},

where
\widetilde{g}
is now a function supported on
[0,1]^{n-1}
. It remains to prove

(3.72)

\|H^{\lambda^{\prime}}\widetilde{g}\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{D}_{R})}\lesssim_{\epsilon,\delta}(R^{\prime}/K)^{\epsilon}(R^{\prime}/K)^{\delta}\|\widetilde{g}\|_{p}.

Actually we will not need the gain in
K
, and only need to prove

(3.73)

\|H^{\lambda^{\prime}}g\|_{L^{p}(\widetilde{D}_{R})}\lesssim_{\epsilon,\delta}R^{\epsilon}R^{\delta}\|g\|_{p},

for every
\delta>0
and every
g
.

Let us denote

(3.74)

D_{R^{\prime}}\subset[-\lambda^{\prime}_{K,R^{\prime\prime}},\lambda^{\prime}_{K,R^{\prime\prime}}]\times[R^{\prime\prime}/C_{n},\lambda^{\prime}C_{n}]

a rectangular box of dimension

(3.75)

R^{\prime}\times\dots R^{\prime}\times R^{\prime\prime}.

We need to prove

(3.76)

\|H^{\lambda^{\prime}}g\|_{L^{p}(D_{R^{\prime}})}\lesssim_{\epsilon,\delta}R^{\epsilon}R^{\delta}\|g\|_{p}.

To prove such a bound, we need the following discrete version of the operator
H^{\lambda^{\prime}}
. This step is where we apply our induction hypothesis.

Lemma 3.10

(Lemma 11.8
[
GHI19
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a maximal
(R^{\prime\prime})^{-1}
-separated discrete subset of
[0,1]^{n-1}
. Then

(3.77)

\Big\|\sum_{\omega_{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}}e^{i\phi^{\lambda}(\cdot\ ;\omega_{\theta})}F(\omega_{\theta})\Big\|_{L^{p}(B_{R^{\prime\prime}})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}(R^{\prime\prime})^{\epsilon}(R^{\prime\prime})^{(n-1)/p^{\prime}}\|F\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathcal{D})},

for every
F:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and every ball
B_{R^{\prime\prime}}\subset D_{R^{\prime}}
of radius
R^{\prime\prime}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.10
.

The proof relies an approximation argument via Taylor’s expansion, and is essentially the same as that of Lemma 11.8
[
GHI19
]
, see also Section 5 of
[
BG11
]
. Let
{\bf x}_{0}
be the center of
B_{R^{\prime\prime}}
. Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function supported on the ball of radius 2 centered at the origin, satisfying
0\leq\psi\leq 1
and
\psi(\omega)=1
for every
|\omega|\leq 1
. For each
\omega_{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}
, define
\psi_{\theta}(\omega):=\psi(10R^{\prime\prime}(\omega-\omega_{\theta}))
. For every
x\in B_{R^{\prime\prime}}
, the sum on the left hand side of (
3.77
) is a constant multiple of

(3.78)

\begin{split}(R^{\prime\prime})^{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)}e^{-2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega)+2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega_{\theta})}\Big[\sum_{\omega_{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}}e^{-2\pi i\Omega^{\lambda}_{\theta}({\bf x};\omega)}F(\omega_{\theta})\psi_{\theta}(\omega)\Big]d\omega,\end{split}

where

(3.79)

\Omega^{\lambda}_{\theta}({\bf x};\omega):=\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)-\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega_{\theta})-\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega)+\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega_{\theta}).

By mean value theorems and the bound (
3.30
), we see that

(3.80)

|\Omega_{\theta}^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)|\lesssim|{\bf x}-{\bf x}_{0}||\omega-\omega_{\theta}|\lesssim 1,

whenever
x\in B_{R^{\prime\prime}}
and
\omega
is in the support of
\psi_{\theta}
.
Roughly speaking, this allows us to treat
e^{-2\pi i\Omega^{\lambda}_{\theta}({\bf x};\omega)}
as the constant function
1
, which can be made rigorous via Fourier expansion. To apply Fourier expansion in
{\bf x}
, we need to examine higher order derivatives of the function. Similar to how we obtained (
3.80
), we obtain from (
3.30
) and mean value theorems that

(3.81)

\begin{split}\big|\nabla_{{\bf x}}^{\beta}\Omega_{\theta}^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)\big|\lesssim 1,\ \ \big|\nabla_{{\bf x}}^{\beta}e^{2\pi i\Omega_{\theta}^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)}\big|\lesssim 1\end{split}

for every multi-index
\beta
. As a consequence, we are able to bound (
3.78
) by

(3.82)

(R^{\prime\prime})^{n-1}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(1+|k|)^{-(n+1)}|H^{\lambda}f_{k}(x)|,

where

(3.83)

f_{k}(\omega):=\sum_{\omega_{\theta}\in\mathcal{D}}F(\omega_{\theta})c_{k,\theta}(\omega)\psi_{\theta}(\omega),

with
c_{k,\theta}
satisfying the uniform bound
\|c_{k,\theta}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. We apply our induction hypothesis to
H^{\lambda}f_{k}
on the ball
B_{R^{\prime\prime}}
; in particular, (
3.74
) guarantees that our induction hypothesis is applicable. This is gives us the desired estimate.
∎

Let us begin the proof of (
3.76
). Out of certain technical reason of handling tails, we introduce new notation. For given
R,\lambda
satisfying
R\leq\lambda^{1-\epsilon}
, let

be a non-negative smooth bump function supported on
[-2\lambda_{R},2\lambda_{R}]\times[R/(2C_{n}),2C_{n}\lambda]
, and equal to one on
[-\lambda_{R},\lambda_{R}]\times[R/C_{n},C_{n}\lambda]
. Define

(3.84)

H^{\lambda,R}g({\bf x}):=a_{\lambda,R}(x,t)\int e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)}g(\omega)d\omega.

This notation will only be used in this section. Note that for all the
{\bf x}
that we care about, that is,
{\bf x}\in[-\lambda_{R},\lambda_{R}]\times[R/C_{n},C_{n}\lambda]
, it always holds that

(3.85)

H^{\lambda,R}g({\bf x})=H^{\lambda}g({\bf x}).

Let us return to the proof of (
3.76
).
Cover
[0,1]^{n-1}
by caps
\theta
of side length
(R^{\prime\prime})^{-1}
. Decompose
g
as
g=\sum_{\theta}g_{\theta}
. Let
\omega_{\theta}
be the center of
\theta
. Define

(3.86)

H^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{\theta}g({\bf x}):=e^{-2\pi i\phi^{\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf x};\omega_{\theta})}H^{\lambda^{\prime}}g({\bf x}),

and

(3.87)

H^{\lambda^{\prime},R^{\prime\prime}}_{\theta}g({\bf x}):=a_{\lambda^{\prime},R^{\prime\prime}}(x,t)e^{-2\pi i\phi^{\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf x};\omega_{\theta})}H^{\lambda^{\prime}}g({\bf x}),

so that for every
{\bf x}\in B_{R^{\prime\prime}}\subset D_{R^{\prime}}
, it holds that

(3.88)

\begin{split}H^{\lambda^{\prime}}g({\bf x})=H^{\lambda^{\prime},R^{\prime\prime}}g({\bf x})&=\sum_{\theta}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf x};\omega_{\theta})}H^{\lambda^{\prime}}_{\theta}g_{\theta}({\bf x})\\
&=\sum_{\theta}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf x};\omega_{\theta})}H^{\lambda^{\prime},R^{\prime\prime}}_{\theta}g_{\theta}({\bf x}).\end{split}

To proceed, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.11
.

For every
\delta>0
, the following statement holds. For every
{\bf x}
we have

(3.89)

H^{\lambda^{\prime},R^{\prime\prime}}_{\theta}g_{\theta}({\bf x})=H^{\lambda^{\prime},R^{\prime\prime}}_{\theta}g_{\theta}*\eta_{(R^{\prime\prime})^{1-\delta}}({\bf x})+\mathrm{RapDec}(\lambda^{\prime})\|g\|_{2},

for some rapidly decreasing function
\eta
such that
|\eta|
admits a smooth, rapidly decreasing majorant
\zeta:\mathbb{R}^{n}\mapsto[0,\infty)
which is locally constant at scale 1.

The proof of this lemma is pretty standard, see for instance the bottom of page 363 in
[
GHI19
]
and Lemma 5.8 there.

Remark 3.12
.

The parameter
\delta>0
will be picked to be extremely small, and much smaller compared with
\epsilon
. The use of the parameter
\delta
and the scale

is the reason for

to depend on

, which further explains the necessity of the polynomial dependence of the implicit constant on

in Theorem

One way we will be using the locally constant property of

is

\zeta_{(R^{\prime\prime})^{1-\delta}}({\bf x})\lesssim(R^{\prime\prime})^{\delta}\zeta_{(R^{\prime\prime})^{1-\delta}}({\bf y})\text{ if }|{\bf x}-{\bf y}|\lesssim R^{\prime\prime}.

To control the

norm of

on

, we cover

by finitely-overlapping

be some member of this cover. Let

denote the center of

|H^{\lambda^{\prime},R^{\prime\prime}}g({\bf x}_{0}+{\bf z})|\lesssim R^{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big|\sum_{\theta}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{0}+{\bf z};\omega_{\theta})}H^{\lambda^{\prime},R^{\prime\prime}}_{\theta}g_{\theta}({\bf y})\Big|\zeta_{(R^{\prime\prime})^{1-\delta}}({\bf x}_{0}-{\bf y})d{\bf y},

for every
{\bf z}
with

. We follow the same lines of proof as in
[
GHI19
, page 364]
. To be more precise, we take the

norm in
{\bf z}
and see that
\|H^{\lambda^{\prime}}g\|_{L^{p}(B_{R^{\prime\prime}})}
can be bounded by

(3.92)

R^{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\|\sum_{\theta}e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda^{\prime}}({\bf x}_{0}+{\bf z};\omega_{\theta})}H^{\lambda^{\prime},R^{\prime\prime}}_{\theta}g_{\theta}({\bf y})\Big\|_{L^{p}(\{|{\bf z}|\leq R^{\prime\prime}\})}\zeta_{(R^{\prime\prime})^{1-\delta}({\bf x}_{0}-{\bf y})}d{\bf y}.

For each fixed
{\bf y}
, we apply Lemma
3.10
and see that the

norm in the above display can be bounded by

(3.93)

(R^{\prime\prime})^{\epsilon}(R^{\prime\prime})^{(n-1)/p}\Big(\sum_{\theta}|H_{\theta}^{\lambda^{\prime},R^{\prime\prime}}g_{\theta}({\bf y})|^{p}\Big)^{1/p}.

We then apply the locally constant property (
3.90

B_{R^{\prime\prime}}

and obtain

(3.94)

\|H^{\lambda^{\prime}}g\|_{L^{p}(D_{R^{\prime}})}\lesssim_{\epsilon}(R^{\prime\prime})^{\epsilon+O(\delta)}(R^{\prime\prime})^{(n-1)/p^{\prime}-n/p}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sum_{\theta}\|H^{\lambda^{\prime},R^{\prime\prime}}g_{\theta}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(D_{R^{\prime}}-{\bf y})}\zeta_{(R^{\prime\prime})^{1-\delta}}({\bf y})d{\bf y}\Big)^{1/p},

This is the step where it is convenient to have a cut-off function in space and time in the definition of
H^{\lambda^{\prime},R^{\prime\prime}}f

\|H^{\lambda^{\prime},R^{\prime\prime}}g_{\theta}\|_{L^{p}(D_{R^{\prime}}-{\bf y})}\lesssim(R^{\prime\prime})^{-(n-1)/p^{\prime}+n/p}\|g_{\theta}\|_{p},

3.95

and

Lemma 3.13
.

For every
1\leq R\leq\lambda
, it holds that

(3.97)

\|H^{\lambda,R}g(x,t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|g\|_{2},

uniformly in

3.13
.

H7̈3

∎

As a consequence of Lemma

(3.99)

\|H^{\lambda^{\prime},R^{\prime\prime}}g_{\theta}\|_{L^{2}(D_{R^{\prime}}-{\bf y})}\lesssim R^{-(n-1)(1/2-1/p)+1/2}\|g_{\theta}\|_{p}.

(3.100)

\|H^{\lambda^{\prime},R^{\prime\prime}}g_{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}(D_{R^{\prime}}-{\bf y})}\leq R^{-(n-1)/p^{\prime}}\|g_{\theta}\|_{p}.

4.
Wave packets and their essential supports

The rest of the paper is devoted to the proof of Theorem
3.4
, the broad norm estimate. When we were handling the narrow part in the previous section, we used an inductive argument, and in order to close induction we had to be very careful with the choice of various amplitude functions
\bar{a}_{1},\bar{a}_{2}
, and regions of taking integrations; we also had to introduce
H^{\lambda,R}g({\bf x})
and distinguish it from
H^{\lambda}g({\bf x})
as it was very often inconvenient to carry amplitude functions in our operator. From now on, these points are not as crucial as before, and we will always work with
H^{\lambda,R}g({\bf x})
, a function that is compactly supported in
[-4C_{n}\lambda,4C_{n}\lambda]\times[R/(2C_{n}),2C_{n}\lambda]
. Moreover, to simplify notation, we always abbreviate
H^{\lambda,R}
to
H^{\lambda}
: The dependence on
R
will be emphasized through the region we evaluate the
L^{p}
norm of
H^{\lambda}
.

4.1.
Wave packet decomposition

Let
r\geq 1
and take a collection
\Theta_{r}
of dyadic cubes of side length
\frac{9}{11}r^{-1/2}
covering the ball
B^{n-1}(0,2)
. We take a smooth partition of unity
(\psi_{\theta})_{\theta\in\Theta_{r}}
with
\mathrm{supp}\psi_{\theta}\subset\frac{11}{10}\theta
for the ball
B^{n-1}(0,2)
such that

(4.1)

\|\partial_{w}^{\alpha}\psi_{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{\alpha}r^{|\alpha|/2}

for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n-1}
. We denote by
\omega_{\theta}
the center of
\theta
. Given a function
g
, we perform a Fourier series decomposition to the function
g\psi_{\theta}
on the region
\frac{11}{9}\theta
and obtain

(4.2)

g(w)\psi_{\theta}(w)\cdot\mathbbm{1}_{\frac{11}{10}\theta}(\omega)=\Big(\frac{r^{1/2}}{2\pi}\Big)^{n-1}\sum_{v\in r^{1/2}\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}}(g\psi_{\theta})^{\wedge}(v)e^{2\pi iv\cdot w}\mathbbm{1}_{\frac{11}{10}\theta}(\omega).

Let
\widetilde{\psi}_{\theta}
be a non-negative smooth cutoff function supported on
\frac{11}{9}\theta
and equal to 1 on
\frac{11}{10}\theta
. We can therefore write

(4.3)

g(w)\psi_{\theta}(w)\cdot\widetilde{\psi}_{\theta}(\omega)=\Big(\frac{r^{1/2}}{2\pi}\Big)^{n-1}\sum_{v\in r^{1/2}\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}}(g\psi_{\theta})^{\wedge}(v)e^{2\pi iv\cdot w}\widetilde{\psi}_{\theta}(\omega).

If we also define

(4.4)

g_{\theta,v}(w):=\Big(\frac{r^{1/2}}{2\pi}\Big)^{n-1}(g\psi_{\theta})^{\wedge}(v)e^{2\pi iv\cdot\omega}\widetilde{\psi}_{\theta}(\omega).

then we have

(4.5)

g=\sum_{(\theta,v)\in\Theta_{r}\times r^{1/2}\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}}g_{\theta,v}.

This finishes our wave packet decomposition.

Let

1\leq r\leq R
. We fix a ball
B({\bf x}_{0},r)
of radius
r
satisfying

(4.6)

B({\bf x}_{0},r)\subset[-3C_{n}\lambda,3C_{n}\lambda]^{n-1}\times[R/C_{n},\lambda C_{n}].

Let us fix a function
f
. We define a collection of tubes associated to the ball
B({\bf x}_{0},r)
by

(4.7)

\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]:=\{T_{\theta,v}({\bf x}_{0}):(\theta,v)\in\Theta_{r}\times r^{1/2}\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}\},

where
T_{\theta,v}({\bf x}_{0})
is some set that will be determined later. Given
T_{\theta,v}({\bf x}_{0})\in\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]
, we define a function

(4.8)

g_{T_{\theta,v}({\bf x}_{0})}(\omega):=e^{-2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega)}(g(\cdot)e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\ \cdot)})_{\theta,v}(\omega).

Notice that

(4.9)

H^{\lambda}g(x,t)=\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]}H^{\lambda}g_{T}(x,t).

In the rest of the section, we will define tubes

so that

.

Remark 4.1
.

It is worth mentioning the motivation and advantages of the notation

]

{\bf x}_{0}

Lemma 4.2

(
L^{2}
-orthogonality)
.

For any set
\mathbb{T}\subset\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]
, it holds that

(4.13)

\|\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}g_{T}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}}\|g_{T}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\|g\|_{2}^{2}.

Moreover, if

Lemma

follows immediately from Plancherel’s theorem, and the proof is omitted.

Remark 4.3
.

In the above lemma, without the extra assumption, (
4.14
) may not be correct, due to the frequency overlapping.

The definition of
T_{\theta,v}({\bf x}_{0})
relies on which of the following two cases we are in:

(4.15)

|v-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega_{\theta})|\geq\frac{10C_{n}\lambda}{\sqrt{1+(10C_{n})^{2}}}\text{ or }|v-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega_{\theta})|\leq\frac{10C_{n}\lambda}{\sqrt{1+(10C_{n})^{2}}}.

In the former case, we define

to be an empty set, and show that
H^{\lambda}f_{T}
always decays rapidly, that is, it is bounded by
\lambda^{-N}\|f\|_{2}
pointwise. In the latter case, we define
T_{\theta,v}
to be as follows: Define a line
l_{\theta,v}
as

(4.16)

l_{\theta,v}:=\big\{(x,t):\omega_{\theta}t-x=\frac{\lambda(\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega_{\theta})-v)}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-|\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega_{\theta})-v|^{2}}}\big\}

and a
r^{1/2+\delta}
tube
T_{\theta,v}
as
T_{\theta,v}=\mathcal{N}_{r^{1/2+\delta}}(l_{\theta,v})
. Here

is a small parameter whose choice will become clear later, and
\mathcal{N}_{r^{1/2}+\delta}
means the
r^{1/2+\delta}
-neighbourhood. Next, we show that the essential support of
H^{\lambda}f_{T}
is
T
. The precise meaning of essential supports will be made clear in the statements of the following two lemmas.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
T=T_{\theta,v}({\bf x}_{0})
is such that the former case in (
4.15
) holds, then we have

(4.17)

\|H^{\lambda}g_{T}\|_{L^{\infty}(B({\bf x}_{0},r))}\lesssim_{N}\lambda^{-N}\|g\|_{2},

for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, whenever
B({\bf x}_{0},r)
satisfies (
4.6
).

Proof of Lemma
4.4
.

For given
{\bf x}_{0}
, we denote
g_{{\bf x}_{0}}(\omega)=g(\omega)e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega)}
. Recall the definition of
g_{T}
that

(4.18)

g_{T}(\omega)=e^{-2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega)}(g_{{\bf x}_{0}}\psi_{\theta})^{\wedge}(v)\Big(\frac{r^{1/2}}{2\pi}\Big)^{n-1}e^{2\pi iv\cdot\omega}\widetilde{\psi}_{\theta}(\omega).

Let
H^{\lambda}
act on
f_{T}
and we obtain

(4.19)

H^{\lambda}g_{T}(x,t)=\Big(\frac{r^{1/2}}{2\pi}\Big)^{n-1}(g_{{\bf x}_{0}}\psi_{\theta})^{\wedge}(v)a_{\lambda,R}(x,t)\int e^{2\pi i\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)+2\pi iv\cdot\omega}\widetilde{\psi}_{\theta}(\omega)d\omega,

where

(4.20)

\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega):=\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)-\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega).

We make the change of variable
\omega\to\omega+\omega_{\theta}
and
consider the oscillatory integral

(4.21)

\begin{split}&\int e^{2\pi i\big(\phi^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}(x,t;\omega+\omega_{\theta})+v\cdot\omega\big)}\widetilde{\psi}(r^{1/2}\omega)d\omega\\
&=r^{-(n-1)/2}\int e^{2\pi i\big(\phi^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}(x,t;r^{-1/2}\omega+\omega_{\theta})+r^{-1/2}v\cdot\omega\big)}\widetilde{\psi}(\omega)d\omega.\end{split}

Denote
\omega^{\prime}:=r^{-1/2}\omega
. First of all,
\nabla_{\omega}
of the phase function equals

(4.22)

r^{-1/2}\Big(\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}(x,t;\omega^{\prime}+\omega_{\theta})+v\Big).

We write

(4.23)

\begin{split}&\eqref{2011227_13}=r^{-1/2}\Big(\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}(x,t;\omega^{\prime}+\omega_{\theta})-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}(x,t;\omega_{\theta})\Big)\\
&+r^{-1/2}\Big(\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}(x,t;\omega_{\theta})+v\Big).\end{split}

We first look at the second term. By the definition of
\phi^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}
, it equals to

(4.24)

\begin{split}&r^{-1/2}\Big(\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x,t;\omega_{\theta})-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x_{0},t_{0};\omega_{\theta})+v\Big).\end{split}

Recall we are in the case that

(4.25)

|\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x_{0},t_{0};\omega_{\theta})-v|\geq\frac{10C_{n}\lambda}{\sqrt{1+(10C_{n})^{2}}}.

Moreover, recall that
|t|\leq 2C_{n}\lambda
and
|x|\leq 4C_{n}\lambda,
and therefore we obtain

(4.26)

\begin{split}&|\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x,t;\omega_{\theta})|=\lambda\Big|\frac{t\omega_{\theta}-x}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x-t\omega_{\theta}|^{2}}}\Big|\leq\frac{6C_{n}\lambda}{\sqrt{1+(6C_{n})^{2}}}\end{split}

By the triangle inequality, we obtain that

(4.27)

\eqref{201122e7_15}\gtrsim\Big(\frac{10C_{n}\lambda}{\sqrt{1+(10C_{n})^{2}}}-\frac{6C_{n}\lambda}{\sqrt{1+(6C_{n})^{2}}}\Big)r^{-1/2}\gtrsim\lambda/r^{1/2}.

Let us continue to compute the first term in (
4.23
).
First,

(4.28)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}(x,t;\omega)=\lambda\Big(\frac{t\omega-x}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x-t\omega|^{2}}}-\frac{t_{0}\omega-x_{0}}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x_{0}-t_{0}\omega|^{2}}}\Big).

Therefore

(4.29)

\begin{split}&\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}(x,t;\omega^{\prime}+\omega_{\theta})-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}(x,t;\omega_{\theta})\\
&=\lambda\Big(\frac{t(\omega^{\prime}+\omega_{\theta})-x}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x-t(\omega^{\prime}+\omega_{\theta})|^{2}}}-\frac{t_{0}(\omega^{\prime}+\omega_{\theta})-x_{0}}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x_{0}-t_{0}(\omega^{\prime}+\omega_{\theta})|^{2}}}\Big)\\
&-\lambda\Big(\frac{t\omega_{\theta}-x}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x-t\omega_{\theta}|^{2}}}-\frac{t_{0}\omega_{\theta}-x_{0}}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x_{0}-t_{0}\omega_{\theta}|^{2}}}\Big)\end{split}

We use the bound (
3.30
) and mean value theorems, and see that the above expression can be bounded from above by
r^{1/2}
, which, together with the leading coefficient
r^{-1/2}
, produces the bound
1
for the first term. Putting everything together, we see that

(4.30)

\eqref{2011227_14}\gtrsim\lambda/r^{1/2}-1\gtrsim\lambda/r^{1/2}.

The desired rapid decay follows immediately from integration by parts.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

For
T=T_{\theta,v}
and
(x,t)\in B({\bf x}_{0},r)\setminus T_{\theta,v}

Here

).

This lemma describes the essential support of a wave packet, and it is the right hand side of (

) below that connects the expression of the core line in (

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

Note that each piece
H^{\lambda}g_{T}
has the expression

(4.32)

H^{\lambda}g_{T}(x,t)=\Big(\frac{r^{1/2}}{2\pi}\Big)^{n-1}(g_{{\bf x}_{0}}\psi_{\theta})^{\wedge}(v)a_{\lambda,R}(x,t)\int e^{i2\pi\big(\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)+v\cdot\omega\big)}\widetilde{\psi}_{\theta}(\omega)d\omega,

We will see that the proof is quite similar to that of Lemma
4.4
. We make the change of variable
\omega\to\omega+\omega_{\theta}
and
consider the oscillatory integral

(4.33)

\begin{split}&\int e^{i2\pi\big(\phi^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}(x,t;\omega+\omega_{\theta})+v\cdot\omega\big)}\widetilde{\psi}(r^{1/2}\omega)d\omega\\
&=r^{-(n-1)/2}\int e^{i2\pi\big(\phi^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}(x,t;r^{-1/2}\omega+\omega_{\theta})+r^{-1/2}v\cdot\omega\big)}\widetilde{\psi}(\omega)d\omega.\end{split}

Let us compute the derivative of the phase function. We write the derivative as in (

). There are two terms there. The first term contributes

|\eqref{2011227_14}|\gtrsim r^{-1/2}\big|\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}(x,t;\omega_{\theta})+v\big|-1.

It remains to show

for the variables

to have

\frac{\lambda\big(t\omega_{\theta}-x\big)}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x-t\omega_{\theta}|^{2}}}=-v+\frac{\lambda(t_{0}\omega_{\theta}-x_{0})}{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+|x_{0}-t_{0}\omega_{\theta}|^{2}}}=-v+\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega_{\theta}),

which indeed is the straight line

. Moreover, we can solve (

4.2.
L^{2}
properties for wave packets

In this subsection, we prove two lemmas about

properties of wave packets. One of these two lemmas, Lemma

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
B({\bf x}_{0},r)
satisfy (
4.6
). Given a tube
T_{1}=T_{\theta_{1},v_{1}}\in\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]
, all but
r^{O(\delta)}
many tubes
T_{2}=T_{\theta_{2},v_{2}}\in\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]
satisfy

(4.37)

\int_{|t-t_{0}|\leq r}\int H^{\lambda}g_{T_{1}}(x,t)\overline{H^{\lambda}g_{T_{2}}}(x,t)dxdt={\rm{RapDec}}(r)\|g\|_{2}^{2}.

Proof of Lemma
4.6
.

Note that each piece
T^{\lambda}g_{T}
has the expression

(4.38)

H^{\lambda}g_{T}(x,t)=\Big(\frac{r^{1/2}}{2\pi}\Big)^{n-1}(g_{{\bf x}_{0}}\psi_{\theta})^{\wedge}(v)a_{\lambda,R}(x,t)\int e^{i2\pi\big(\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}({\bf x};\omega)+v\cdot\omega\big)}\widetilde{\psi}_{\theta}(\omega)d\omega,

To estimate (
4.37
), it suffices to consider the oscillatory integral

(4.39)

\int_{|t-t_{0}|\leq r}\int e^{2\pi i\big(\phi^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}(x,t;\omega_{1})-\phi^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}(x,t;\omega_{2})\big)}a_{\lambda,R}(x,t)dxdt,

with
|\omega_{1}-\omega_{\theta_{1}}|\leq r^{-1/2}
and
|\omega_{2}-\omega_{\theta_{2}}|\leq r^{-1/2}
, and show that it decays rapidly. As we will apply the argument of integration by parts in
x
, we do the change of variable
x\mapsto\lambda x
and obtain

(4.40)

\lambda^{n-1}\int_{|t-t_{0}|\leq r}\int e^{2\pi i\lambda\big(\phi(x,t^{\prime};\omega_{1})-\phi(x^{\prime}_{0},t^{\prime}_{0};\omega_{1})-\phi(x,t^{\prime};\omega_{2})+\phi(x^{\prime}_{0},t^{\prime}_{0};\omega_{2})\big)}a_{\lambda,R}(\lambda x,t)dxdt,

where we have also set
t^{\prime}:=t/\lambda
,
x^{\prime}_{0}:=x_{0}/\lambda
and
t^{\prime}_{0}:=t_{0}/\lambda
.

Let us first assume that we are in the case
|\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}|\geq r^{-1/2+\delta}
. We compute the partial derivative of the phase function in the
x
variable
and obtain

(4.41)

\begin{split}&\frac{\lambda}{t^{\prime}}\frac{x-t^{\prime}\omega_{1}}{\sqrt{1+|x-t^{\prime}\omega_{1}|^{2}}}-\frac{\lambda}{t^{\prime}}\frac{x-t^{\prime}\omega_{2}}{\sqrt{1+|x-t^{\prime}\omega_{2}|^{2}}}.\end{split}

Recall that the mixed Hessian of
\phi(x,t;\omega)
in
x
and
\omega
is non-vanishing, that is,

(4.42)

|\det\nabla_{\omega}\nabla_{x}\phi(x,t;\omega)|\simeq 1,

as has been verified in (
3.41
). This, combined with mean value theorems, implies that

(4.43)

|\eqref{210202e4_38}|\gtrsim\lambda|\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}|.

Now we can apply integration by parts and finish the proof of the case
|\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}|\geq r^{-1/2+\delta}
.

In the end, we consider the case
|\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}|\leq r^{-1/2+\delta}
and
|v_{1}-v_{2}|\geq r^{1/2+\delta}
. However, this case follows immediately from the pointwise estimate in Lemma
4.5
.
∎

5.
Comparing wave packets at different scales

In the previous section, we built up a wave packet decomposition for a function
g
on the ball
B({\bf x}_{0},r)
. Since our proof of Theorem
3.4
relies on the multiscale argument as in
[
Gut18
]
and
[
GHI19
]
, it is necessary to compare wave packets at two different scales. In this section, we will prove lemmas in Section 9 of
[
GHI19
]
in our setting. It is worth mentioning that in their paper they consider more general operators. However, their lemmas are valid only when the operators are of “reduced form”. Since we do not reduce our operator to reduced form, we are unable to simply cite their lemmas. Instead, we follow their arguments and prove the lemmas in our setting. We include the details for the completeness of the paper.

Consider another ball
B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)\subset B({\bf x}_{0},r)
for some
\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0}=(\widetilde{x}_{0},\widetilde{t}_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r^{1/2}<\rho<r
.
Since we are considering wave packets at different scales, it would be convenient to introduce some notations to distinguish wave packets from different scales. We will use the notation
\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}[B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]
for the collection
{\mathbb{T}}[B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]
in (
4.7
). We will denote the elements of
\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}[B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]
by
\widetilde{T}_{\widetilde{\theta},\widetilde{v}}
. Here, the symbol
\sim
indicates that the objects are generated at a smaller scale.

We find it appropriate to introduce three more definitions here.

Definition 5.1
.

We say a function
h
is concentrated on wave packets from a tube set
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}
, if

(5.1)

h=\sum_{T\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}}h_{T}+\mathrm{RapDec}(r)\|h\|_{2}.

Definition 5.2
.

For a ball
B
and
\mathbb{W}\subset\mathbb{T}[B]
, we define

(5.2)

h|_{\mathbb{W}}:=\sum_{T\in\mathbb{W}}h_{T}.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
({\theta},v)\in\Theta_{r}\times r^{1/2}\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
and let
(\tilde{\theta},\widetilde{v})\in\Theta_{\rho}\times\rho^{1/2}\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
. we define a collection of smaller tubes
\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\widetilde{v}}
that are “close to” the bigger tube
T_{\theta,v}
as

(5.3)

\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{\theta,v}[B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]:=\big\{\widetilde{T}_{\widetilde{\theta},\widetilde{v}}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}[B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]:{\rm{dist}}(\theta,\widetilde{\theta})\lesssim\rho^{-1/2},\;|\tilde{v}-(\nabla_{\omega}\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\omega_{\theta})+v)|\lesssim{r^{(1+\delta)/2}}\big\}.

We sometimes abbreviate the collection
\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{\theta,v}[B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]
to
\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{\theta,v}
for simplicity.

Notice that we can write

(5.4)

\tilde{v}-(\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\omega_{\theta})+v)=\big(\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\omega_{\theta})+\tilde{v}\big)-\big(\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\omega_{\theta})+v\big).

Heuristically, on the ball
B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)
, only those new wave packets concentrated in
\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{\theta,v}[B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]
would make significant contribution to our old wave packet
g_{T_{\theta,v}}
. This will be stated rigorously in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
T_{\theta,v}\in\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]
. Then it holds that

(5.5)

g_{T_{\theta,v}}=(g_{T_{\theta,v}})|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{\theta,v}[B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]}+{\rm{RapDec}}(r)\|g\|_{2}.

Proof.

By the definition of wave packets,

(5.6)

\begin{split}(g_{T_{\theta,v}({\bf x}_{0})})_{\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\tilde{v}}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0})}(\omega)&=e^{-2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\omega)}(g_{T_{\theta,v}}(\cdot)e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\,\cdot\,)})_{\tilde{\theta},\tilde{v}}(\omega)\\
&=e^{-2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\omega)}\Big(e^{2\pi i(\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\,\cdot\,)-\phi^{\lambda}({{\bf x}}_{0};\,\cdot\,))}(g(\cdot)e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0};\,\cdot\,)})_{{\theta,v}}(\cdot)\Big)_{\tilde{\theta},\tilde{v}}(\omega).\end{split}

Since a function
g_{\theta,v}
is supported near
\theta
for every function
g
, by the above expression, we see that

(5.7)

(g_{T_{\theta,v}({\bf x}_{0})})_{\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\tilde{v}}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0})}\equiv 0\;\;\text{unless}\;\;\text{dist}(\theta,\widetilde{\theta})\lesssim\rho^{-1/2}.

By renaming the function
g(\omega)e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}({\bf x}_{0},\omega)}
by
g(\omega)
,
it remains to show that

(5.8)

\big(e^{2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}_{{\bf x}_{0}}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\,\cdot\,)}g_{{\theta,v}}(\cdot)\big)_{\tilde{\theta},\tilde{v}}=\mathrm{RapDec}(r)\|g\|_{2},

whenever

|-v+\tilde{v}-\nabla_{\omega}\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\omega_{\theta})|\gtrsim{r^{(1+\delta)/2}}.

By the definition, it amounts to proving

(5.9)

\big(e^{2\pi i\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\,\cdot\,)}g_{\theta,v}(\cdot)\psi_{\tilde{\theta}}(\cdot)\big)^{\wedge}(\tilde{v})=\mathrm{RapDec}(r)\|g\|_{2}.

The left hand side can be written as

(5.10)

\big(\widehat{\psi}_{\tilde{\theta}}*\big(e^{2\pi i\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\,\cdot\,)}g_{\theta,v}(\cdot)\big)^{\wedge}\big)(\tilde{v}).

Since
the function
\widehat{\psi}_{\tilde{\theta}}
is essentially supported in
B(0,\rho^{1/2})
, it suffices to show that

(5.11)

\begin{split}\big(e^{2\pi i\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\,\cdot\,)}g_{\theta,v}(\cdot)\big)^{\wedge}(z)=(1+r^{-1/2}|z-v-\nabla_{\omega}\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\bf x}_{0};\omega_{\theta})|)^{-(n+1)}\mathrm{RapDec}(r)\|g\|_{2}\end{split}

for every
|z-v-\nabla_{\omega}\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\bf x}_{0};\omega_{\theta})|\gtrsim r^{(1+\delta)/2}.

We take a function
\widetilde{\widetilde{\psi}}_{\theta}(\omega)=\widetilde{\widetilde{\psi}}(r^{1/2}(\omega-\omega_{\theta}))
for some compactly supported function
\widetilde{\widetilde{\psi}}
so that this function is adapted to
\theta
but is equal to one on the support of
\widetilde{\psi}_{\theta}
. Since
g_{\theta,v}
is supported on the support of
\widetilde{\psi}_{\theta}
, the left hand side of (
5.11
) can be written as

(5.12)

\big(e^{2\pi i\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\,\cdot\,)}\widetilde{\widetilde{\psi}}_{\theta}(\cdot)g_{\theta,v}(\cdot)\big)^{\wedge}(z)=\big(e^{2\pi i\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\,\cdot\,)}\widetilde{\widetilde{\psi}}_{\theta}(\cdot)\big)^{\wedge}*\widehat{g_{\theta,v}}(z).

Since the function
\widehat{g_{\theta,v}}
is concentrated on
B(v,r^{(1+\delta)/2})
, by the above expression, the claim (
5.11
) is reduced to

(5.13)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi i(-z\cdot\omega+\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\omega))}\widetilde{\widetilde{\psi}}_{\theta}(\omega)\,d\omega=\big(1+r^{-1/2}|z-\nabla_{\omega}\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\omega_{\theta})|\big)^{-(n+1)}\mathrm{RapDec}(r)\end{split}

whenever
|z-\nabla_{\omega}\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\omega_{\theta})|\gtrsim r^{(1+\delta)/2}
.
We apply the change of variables:
\omega\mapsto r^{-1/2}\omega+\omega_{\theta}
and the above integral becomes

(5.14)

r^{-(n-1)/2}e^{-2\pi iz\cdot\omega_{\theta}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi i(-r^{-1/2}z\cdot\omega+\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\omega_{\theta}+r^{-1/2}\omega))}\widetilde{\widetilde{\psi}}(\omega)\,d\omega.

By the stationary phase method, the estimate (
5.13
) follows from

(5.15)

|z-\nabla_{\omega}\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\omega_{\theta}+r^{-1/2}\omega)|\gtrsim r^{(1+\delta)/2}

for every
|z-\nabla_{\omega}\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0};\omega_{\theta})|\gtrsim r^{(1+\delta)/2}
. By using a mean value theorem with
|{\bf x}_{0}-\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0}|\lesssim r
, this follows from

(5.16)

|\nabla^{2}_{\omega}\nabla_{{\bf x}}\phi^{\lambda}(x,t;\omega)|\lesssim 1

for every
|x|+|t|\lesssim\lambda
, which has been verified in (
3.30
).
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose that
T_{\theta,v}\in\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]
.
If
\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\tilde{v}}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{\theta,v}[B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]
, then it holds that

(5.17)

\mathrm{HausDist}(\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\tilde{v}},T_{\theta,v}\cap B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho))\lesssim r^{1/2+\delta}

and

(5.18)

\measuredangle(G(w_{\theta}),G(w_{\tilde{\theta}}))\lesssim\rho^{-1/2}.

Proof.

The bound (
5.18
) is trivial, and we only need to prove (
5.17
). Let us assume that neither
\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\tilde{v}}
nor
T_{\theta,v}\cap B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)
is empty. Let
l_{\theta,v}
and
l_{\tilde{\theta},\tilde{v}}
be the core lines of
T_{\theta,v}
and
\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\tilde{v}}
respectively. The two tubes involved in (
5.17
) have width
\lesssim r^{1/2+\delta}
, and therefore to prove (
5.17
), it suffices to consider the core lines of the tubes only. From the definition of core lines (see also (
4.36
)), we can write
l_{\theta,v}
as

(5.19)

\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x,t;\omega_{\theta})-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x_{0},t_{0};\omega_{\theta})=-v;

moreover, we can write the core line of
l_{\tilde{\theta},\tilde{v}}
as

(5.20)

\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x,t;\omega_{\tilde{\theta}})-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{x}_{0},\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\tilde{\theta}})=-\tilde{v}.

Suppose that
l_{\theta,v}
passes through a point
(x_{1},\widetilde{t}_{0})
and that
l_{\tilde{\theta},\tilde{v}}
passes through
(x_{2},\widetilde{t}_{0})
. Note that the angle between
l_{\theta,v}
and
l_{\tilde{\theta},\tilde{v}}
is at most
\rho^{-1/2}
and that we are computing a Hausdorff distance within a ball of radius
\rho
. To prove (
5.17
), it therefore suffices to show that
|x_{1}-x_{2}|\lesssim r^{1/2+\delta}
, which, by (
3.41
), is the same as saying that

(5.21)

\Big|\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x_{1},\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\theta})-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x_{2},\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\theta})\Big|\lesssim r^{1/2+\delta}.

We consider two cases
\rho\leq r^{1/2+\delta}
and
\rho\geq r^{1/2+\delta}
separately.

Assume we are in the former case. By the assumption that
T_{\theta,v}\cap B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)
is not empty, we obtain
|x_{1}-\widetilde{x}_{0}|\lesssim r^{1/2+\delta}
. Moreover, by (
5.3
), (
5.4
), (
5.19
), (
5.20
) and the triangle inequality, we obtain that

(5.22)

\begin{split}&\big|\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x_{2},\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\tilde{\theta}})-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{x}_{0},\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\tilde{\theta}})\big|\\
&\lesssim r^{1/2+\delta}+\big|\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x_{1},\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\theta})-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{x}_{0},\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\theta})\big|.\end{split}

This, together with (
3.41
) and the mean value theorem, implies that

(5.23)

|x_{2}-\widetilde{x}_{0}|\lesssim r^{1/2+\delta},

which further leads to the desired bound for
|x_{1}-x_{2}|
.

Assume we are in the latter case. The starting point of the proof of this is similar as above. By the assumption that
T_{\theta,v}\cap B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)
is not empty, we obtain
|x_{1}-\widetilde{x}_{0}|\lesssim\rho
. This, combined with (
5.22
), implies that
|x_{2}-\widetilde{x}_{0}|\lesssim\rho
. Next, the proof starts to be different.

(5.24)

\begin{split}&\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x_{1},\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\theta})-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x_{2},\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\theta})\\
&=\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x_{1},\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\theta})-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x_{2},\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\tilde{\theta}})+\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x_{2},\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\tilde{\theta}})-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x_{2},\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\theta})\end{split}

By (
5.19
) and (
5.20
), the last expression can be written as

(5.25)

\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{x}_{0},\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\theta})-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{x}_{0},\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\tilde{\theta}})+\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x_{2},\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\tilde{\theta}})-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x_{2},\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\theta}).

By the mean value theorem and the bound (
3.30
) with
\beta=1
and
\beta^{\prime}=2
, the absolute value of the last display can be bounded by

(5.26)

|x_{2}-\widetilde{x}_{0}||\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}-\omega_{\theta}|\lesssim\rho\rho^{-1/2}\lesssim\rho^{1/2}.

Recall that
\rho\leq r
. This finishes the proof of latter case, thus the proof of the whole lemma.
∎

6.
The transverse equidistribution property

The proof of the transverse equidistribution estimate requires us to study wave packets from different scales. To make ourselves clear, we sometimes use “large wave packet” to mean the scale
r
wave packet, and use “small wave packet” to mean the scale
\rho
one.

Recall the admissible parameters in (
1.11
).
Let us introduce more notations for the next several sections. The first notation is about the transverse complete intersection. The second definition is about the tangency between tubes and a transverse complete intersection. The third definition is about collections of tangent tubes at two different scales.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n-m}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be polynomials. We consider the common zero set

(6.1)

Z(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n-m}):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:P_{1}(x)=\cdots=P_{n-m}(x)=0\}.

Suppose that for all
z\in Z(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n-m})
, one has

(6.2)

\bigwedge_{j=1}^{n-m}\nabla P_{j}(z)\neq 0.

Then a connected branch of this set, or a union of connected branches of this set, is called an
m
-dimensional transverse complete intersection. Given a set
Z
of the form (
6.1
), the degree of
Z
is defined by

(6.3)

\min\Big({\prod_{i=1}^{n-m}\mathrm{deg}(P_{i})}\Big),

where the minimum is taken over all possible representations of
Z=Z(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n-m})
.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
r\geq 1
and
Z
be an
m
-dimensional transverse complete intersection. A tube
T_{\theta,v}({\bf x}_{0})\in\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]
is said to be
r^{-1/2+\delta_{m}}
-tangent to
Z
in
B({\bf x}_{0},r)
if it satisfies

•

T_{\theta,v}({\bf x}_{0})\subset N_{r^{1/2+\delta_{m}}}(Z)\cap B({\bf x}_{0},r)
;

•

For every
z\in Z\cap B({\bf x}_{0},r)
, if there is
y\in T_{\theta,v}({\bf x}_{0})
with
|z-y|\lesssim r^{1/2+\delta_{m}}
, then one has

(6.4)

\measuredangle(G(\theta),T_{z}Z)\lesssim r^{-1/2+\delta_{m}}.

Here,
T_{z}Z
is the tangent space of
Z
at
z
.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
r\geq\rho\geq 1
and
Z
be an
m
-dimensional transverse complete intersection and let
B(\tilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)\subset B({\bf x}_{0},r)
. Define a collection of bigger tangent tubes inside a smaller ball as

(6.5)

\begin{split}\mathbb{T}_{Z}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]:=\{T\in\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]:T\text{ is $r^{-1/2+\delta_{m}}$-tangent to }Z\text{ in }B({\bf x}_{0},r)\}.\end{split}

Given an arbitrary translation
b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, define

(6.6)

\begin{split}\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{b}[B(\tilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]:=\{\widetilde{T}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}[B(\tilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]:\widetilde{T}\text{ is $\rho^{-1/2+\delta_{m}}$-tangent to $Z+b$ in $B(\tilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)$}\}.\end{split}

For simplicity, we sometimes use the notation
\mathbb{T}_{Z}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{b}
in short for
\mathbb{T}_{Z}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{b}[B(\tilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]
, respectively.

Let
1\leq r^{1/2}\leq\rho\leq r
and
\widetilde{\bf x}_{0}=(\widetilde{x}_{0},\widetilde{t}_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For every pair
(\tilde{\theta},w)\in\Theta_{\rho}\times r^{1/2}\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
, we define a collection of bigger tubes

(6.7)

\mathbb{T}_{\tilde{\theta},w}:=\{T_{\theta,v}\in\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]:\mathrm{dist}(\theta,\tilde{\theta})\lesssim\rho^{-1/2}\;\;\mathrm{and}\;\;|v+\nabla_{\omega}\phi_{{\bf x}_{0}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\bf x}_{0};\omega_{\theta})-w|\lesssim r^{1/2}\}.

Then for every
T\in\mathbb{T}_{\tilde{\theta},w}\cap\mathbb{T}_{Z}
, if
T\cap B(\widetilde{\bf x}_{0},2\rho)\not=\varnothing
, the intersection of
T
and the horizontal plane
\{t=\widetilde{t}_{0}\}
is contained in the ball
B:=B^{n-1}(w_{H},Cr^{1/2+\delta})\subset B^{n-1}(\widetilde{x}_{0},3\rho)
, where the center
w_{H}
is defined as

(6.8)

w_{H}:=\widetilde{t}_{0}\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}+\frac{\lambda(\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\bf x}_{0};\omega_{\tilde{\theta}})-w)}{(\lambda^{2}-|\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\bf x}_{0};\omega_{\tilde{\theta}})-w|^{2})^{1/2}}.

This is due to the coreline equation (
4.16
) for the tube
T
, and Lemma
5.5
. We remark that if we perturb
\widetilde{t}_{0}
by an extremely small factor
\beta
, the intersection between
T
and the horizontal plane
\{t=\bar{t}_{0}\}
with
\bar{t}_{0}=\widetilde{t}_{0}+\beta
is still contained in the ball
B
.

The main goal of this section is to prove the following lemma.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
|b|\lesssim r^{1/2+\delta_{m}}
and recall (
5.2
).
Suppose that
h
is concentrated on large wave packets from
\mathbb{T}_{Z}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\tilde{\theta},w}
for some
(\tilde{\theta},w)\in\Theta_{\rho}\times r^{1/2}\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}
. Then for every
\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}\subset\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{b}
,

(6.9)

\big\|h|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}}\big\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim r^{O(\delta_{m})}(r/\rho)^{-(n-m)/2}\|h\|_{2}^{2}.

As a consequence, for every function
h
concentrated on
\mathbb{T}_{Z}
,

(6.10)

\big\|h|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}}\big\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim r^{O(\delta_{m})}(r/\rho)^{-(n-m)/2}\|h\|_{2}^{2}.

We first show that (
6.10
) follows from the local one (
6.9
). Decompose the function
h
as

(6.11)

h=\sum_{(\tilde{\theta},w)}h_{\tilde{\theta},w}

such that
h_{\tilde{\theta},w}
is concentrated on wave packets from
\mathbb{T}_{Z}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\tilde{\theta},w}
. Since when
|w-x|\geq Cr^{1/2+\delta}
,
\widehat{h}_{\tilde{\theta},w}(x)=\mathrm{RapDec}(r)\|h\|_{2}
, we have the almost
L^{2}
orthogonality

(6.12)

\big\|h|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}}\big\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim r^{O(\delta)}\sum_{(\tilde{\theta},w)}\big\|h_{\tilde{\theta},w}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}}\big\|_{2}^{2}.

Finally, we use the local estimate (
6.9
) and Lemma
4.2
to conclude the global estimate (
6.10
).

To prove (
6.9
), note that
\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{b}
only consists smaller tubes
\widetilde{T}
that
\widetilde{T}\cap B(\widetilde{\bf x}_{0},3\rho/2)\not=\varnothing
. By Lemma
4.5
, Lemma
5.4
and Lemma
5.5
, we can assume without loss of generality that for every bigger tube
T\in\mathbb{T}_{\tilde{\theta},w}\cap\mathbb{T}_{Z}
, one has
T\cap B(\widetilde{\bf x}_{0},2\rho)\not=\varnothing
.

We break the proof of the local transverse equidistribution estimate (
6.9
) into several smaller lemmas. The first thing we would like to find out is the location of
(h|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}})^{\wedge}
. Consider
Z_{0}
, the intersection between of variety
Z+b
and the horizontal hyperplane
\{(x,t):t=\bar{t}_{0}\}
. By the transversality theorem (For example, Theorem
10.2
), we can choose
\bar{t}_{0}=\widetilde{t}_{0}+\beta
for an extremely small number
\beta
, so that
Z_{0}
is a transverse complete intersection. Since the small perturbation
\beta
is harmless in our proof, to save us from abundant notations, let us assume
\bar{t}_{0}=\widetilde{t}_{0}
. Hence
Z_{0}=Z+b\cap\{t=\widetilde{t}_{0}\}
can be considered as a transverse complete intersection in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, and
\deg(Z_{0})=O(\deg(Z))
.

If
H^{\lambda}
was the Fourier extension operator,
(h|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}})^{\wedge}
is just contained in a thin neighborhood of
Z_{0}
. While in our case,
(h|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}})^{\wedge}
is roughly contained in a thin neighborhood of
\Phi(Z_{0})
, where
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
is a smooth map defined as

(6.13)

\Phi(x):=-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x,\widetilde{t}_{0};\omega_{\tilde{\theta}})=-\frac{\lambda\big(\widetilde{t}_{0}\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}-x\big)}{(\lambda^{2}+|x-\bar{t}_{0}\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}|^{2})^{1/2}}.

Before proving something about the support of
(h|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}})^{\wedge}
rigorously, let us take a look at the smooth function
\Phi
. The next lemma shows that
\Phi
indeed looks like the identity map of
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and
r\geq\ell>0
that
B^{n-1}(x,\ell)\subset B^{n-1}(0,3C_{n}\lambda)
, one has

(6.14)

B(\Phi(x),\ell/C)\subset\Phi(B(x,\ell))\subset B(\Phi(x),C\ell).

The constant
C
only depends on the choice of
C_{n}
in Theorem
3.4
.

Proof.

By Taylor’s theorem, we have that for any
y\in B(x,r)
,

(6.15)

\Phi(y)-\Phi(x)=(y-x)J_{\Phi}(x)+O(\lambda^{-1}|y-x|^{2}).

The Jacobian
J_{\Phi}
is a
(n-1)\times(n-1)
symmetric matrix
A=(a_{kl})
, with

(6.16)

a_{kl}:=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\lambda(\widetilde{t}\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}-x)_{k}(\widetilde{t}\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}-x)_{l}}{(\lambda^{2}+|x-\widetilde{t}\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}|^{2})^{3/2}}&\text{ when }k\not=l,\\[12.91663pt]
\dfrac{\lambda[\lambda^{2}+\sum_{j\not=k}(\widetilde{t}\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}-x)_{j}^{2}]}{(\lambda^{2}+|x-\widetilde{t}\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}|^{2})^{3/2}}&\text{ when }k=l.\end{cases}

Similar to the argument in (
3.39
), we can simplify
A
as

(6.17)

A=\frac{\lambda}{(\lambda^{2}+|x-\widetilde{t}\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}|^{2})^{1/2}}\Big(\lambda^{2}I_{n}-\frac{(\widetilde{t}\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}-x)^{T}(\widetilde{t}\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}-x)}{\lambda^{2}+|x-\widetilde{t}\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}|^{2}}\Big).

Inside the bracket, the first matrix has eigenvalues
1
, while the second matrix has only one eigenvalue
|x-\widetilde{t}\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}|^{2}/(\lambda^{2}+|x-\widetilde{t}\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}|^{2})\leq 4C_{n}/(1+4C_{n})<1
since
x\in B(0,3C_{n}\lambda)
and since
t\leq C_{n}\lambda
. This proves that the eigenvalues of
A
are all positive and have lower bound
C^{-1}
and upper bound
C
for an absolute constant that depends only on
C_{n}
, uniformly in
x
. Thus,

(6.18)

|\Phi(y)-\Phi(x)|\sim|y-x|+O(\lambda^{-1}|y-x|^{2}),

which yields
\Phi(B(x,\ell))\subset B(\Phi(x),C\ell)
since
|y-x|\leq r\leq R\leq\lambda^{1-\varepsilon}
as assumed in (
3.3
). For the other side, by the implicit function theorem, we know that
\Phi^{-1}
is well define in the domain
\Phi(B^{n-1}(0,3C_{n}\lambda))
and
J_{\Phi^{-1}}(x)=(J_{\Phi}(x))^{-1}
. So the eigenvalues of
J_{\Phi^{-1}}(x)
have an upper bound
C^{-1}
and a lower bound
C
. Hence we can prove
B(\Phi(x),\ell/C)\subset\Phi(B(x,\ell))
similarly.
∎

A direct corollary of this lemma is the following:

Corollary 6.6
.

Let
Z_{0}
and
B
be as above. Then

(6.19)

N_{\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}/C}(\Phi(Z_{0}))\cap\Phi((1/C)B)\subset\Phi(N_{\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}}(Z_{0})\cap B)\subset N_{C\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}}(\Phi(Z_{0}))\cap\Phi(CB).

Now via Lemma
6.5
and Corollary
6.6
, we can say something rigorously related to the support of
(h|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}})^{\wedge}
. This is shown in the next lemma.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
h
be concentrated on bigger wave packets from
\mathbb{T}_{\tilde{\theta},w}\cap\mathbb{T}_{Z}
. Recall that
Z_{0}
was defined in above (
6.13
), and
h|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}}
was introduced in Lemma
6.4
. Then

(6.20)

\big\|(h|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}})^{\wedge}\big\|_{2}\lesssim\big\|\widehat{h}\cdot{\bf 1}_{\{N_{C\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}}(\Phi(Z_{0}))\cap\Phi(CB)\}}\big\|_{2}+\mathrm{RapDec}(\rho)\|h\|_{2}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, one has

(6.21)

\|(h|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}})^{\wedge}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{\widetilde{T}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}}\|{h}_{\widetilde{T}}\|_{2}^{2}.

The scale
\rho
wave packet
\widehat{h}_{\tilde{T}}
was defined in (
4.18
). We use its definition to have

(6.22)

\sum_{\widetilde{T}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}}\|\widehat{h}_{\widetilde{T}}\|_{2}^{2}=\rho^{n-1}\sum_{\widetilde{T}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}}|(h_{\widetilde{\bf x}_{0}}\psi_{\tilde{\theta}})^{\wedge}(v)|^{2}\|\widetilde{\psi}_{\tilde{\theta}}\|_{2}^{2}=\rho^{(n-1)/2}\sum_{\widetilde{T}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}}|(h_{\widetilde{\bf x}_{0}}\psi_{\tilde{\theta}})^{\wedge}(v)|^{2}.

Since we can write
(h_{\widetilde{\bf x}_{0}}\psi_{\tilde{\theta}})^{\wedge}(v)=\widehat{h}\ast(e^{-2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\bf x}_{0};\ \cdot)}\widetilde{\psi}_{\tilde{\theta}}(\cdot))^{\wedge}(v)
, and since the
L^{1}
norm of the second function
(e^{-2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\bf x}_{0};\ \cdot)}\widetilde{\psi}_{\tilde{\theta}}(\cdot))^{\wedge}
is bounded above by
O(1)
, one can use Hölder’s inequality for
|(h_{\widetilde{\bf x}_{0}}\psi_{\tilde{\theta}})^{\wedge}(v)|^{2}
and obtain

(6.23)

\displaystyle\sum_{\widetilde{T}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}}\|\widehat{h}_{\widetilde{T}}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\int|\widehat{h}(y)|^{2}\Big(\sum_{\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\tilde{v}}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}}|\rho^{(n-1)/2}(e^{-2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\bf x}_{0};\ \cdot)}\widetilde{\psi}_{\tilde{\theta}}(\cdot))^{\wedge}(y-\widetilde{v})|\Big)dy.

We claim that the sum inside the bracket is
O(1)
, and it decays rapidly outside of the set
N_{C\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}}(\Phi(Z_{0}))\cap\Phi(CB)
. This proves the lemma.

To prove the
O(1)
upper bound, one just needs to notice that there are
O(1)
caps
\tilde{\theta}
making contribution in
\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}
. It remains to prove the rapidly-decaying property.

To prove the rapidly-decaying property, we first notice that
h_{\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\widetilde{v}}}=\mathrm{RapDec}(\rho)\|h\|_{2}
unless
\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\widetilde{v}}\cap\{t=\widetilde{t}_{0}\}\subset N_{2{\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}}}(Z_{0})\cap 2B
for every smaller tube
\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\widetilde{v}}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}
. This is because on one hand
\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\widetilde{v}}\cap\{t=\widetilde{t}_{0}\}\subset N_{2{\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}}}(Z_{0})
since
\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\widetilde{v}}
is tangent to
Z+b
; on the other hand, since
h
is concentrated on
\mathbb{T}_{\tilde{\theta},w}
, by Lemma
5.4
we know that
h_{\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\widetilde{v}}}=\mathrm{RapDec}(\rho)\|h\|_{2}
unless
\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\widetilde{v}}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{\theta,v}[B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]
for some
(\theta,v)
with
T_{\theta,v}\in\mathbb{T}_{\widetilde{\theta},w}
. But for such
T_{\theta,v}
, one has
T_{\theta,v}\cap\{t=\widetilde{t}_{0}\}\subset B
as shown in above (
6.8
). Hence by Lemma
5.5
we have that
h_{\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\widetilde{v}}}=\mathrm{RapDec}(\rho)\|h\|_{2}
unless
\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\widetilde{v}}\cap\{t=\widetilde{t}_{0}\}\subset 2B
.

Now we only need to consider those smaller tubes
\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\widetilde{v}}
with
\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\widetilde{v}}\cap\{t=\widetilde{t}_{0}\}\subset N_{2{\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}}}(Z_{0})\cap 2B
. Using this information, we would like to find out the location of
\widetilde{v}
in the bracket of (
6.23
). Recall that the coreline of the smaller tube
\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\widetilde{v}}
satisfies (
4.16
), which indeed is (
4.36
). On the hyperplane
\{t=\widetilde{t}_{0}\}
, we can rewrite (4.36) as
\widetilde{v}=-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(x,\widetilde{t};\omega_{\tilde{\theta}})+\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\bf x}_{0};\omega_{\tilde{\theta}})=:\Psi(x)
. Hence by Corollary
6.6
one has

(6.24)

\widetilde{v}\in\Psi(T_{\tilde{\theta},\widetilde{v}}\cap\{t=\widetilde{t}_{0}\})\subset\Psi(N_{2{\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}}}(Z_{0})\cap 2B)\subset N_{C\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}}\Psi(Z_{0})\cap\Psi(2B).

Finally, we know from Lemma
4.5
that the function
(e^{-2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\bf x}_{0};\ \cdot)}\widetilde{\psi}_{\tilde{\theta}}(\cdot))^{\wedge}(y-\widetilde{v})
in (
6.23
) decays rapidly unless
|y-\widetilde{v}+\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\bf x}_{0},\omega_{\tilde{\theta}})|\lesssim\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}
, that is,
y-\widetilde{v}\in B^{n-1}(-\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\bf x}_{0},\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}),C\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}})
. Plugging this back into (
6.24
) and noticing
\Phi+\nabla_{\omega}\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\bf x}_{0};\omega_{\tilde{\theta}})=\Psi
, we therefore can conclude that
(e^{-2\pi i\phi^{\lambda}(\widetilde{\bf x}_{0};\ \cdot)}\widetilde{\psi}_{\tilde{\theta}}(\cdot))^{\wedge}(y-\widetilde{v})
decays rapidly unless

(6.25)

y\in N_{C\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}}\Phi(Z_{0})\cap\Phi(CB),

where
\Phi
was defined in (

). This proves the claim and hence the lemma.
∎

To conclude (

), we need to prove the transverse equidistribution estimate stated below.

Proposition 6.8
.

Recall
Z_{0}=(Z+b)\cap\{t=\widetilde{t}_{0}\}
and
B=B^{n-1}(w,r^{1/2+\delta_{m}})
. Suppose that
h
is concentrated on scale
r
wave packets in

. Then

(6.26)

\int|\widehat{h}|^{2}\cdot{\bf 1}_{\{N_{C\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}}(\Phi(Z_{0}))\cap\Phi(CB)\}}\lesssim r^{O(\delta_{m})}\Big(\frac{\rho}{r}\Big)^{(n-m)/2}\|h\|_{2}^{2}.

Note that the desired estimate (
6.9
) is a direct corollary of (

) and Proposition
6.8
.

The proof of Proposition
6.8
relies on an auxiliary lemma. Let us introduce some more definitions. First, define

(6.27)

\mathbb{T}_{Z,B,\tilde{\theta}}:=\{T_{\theta,v}\in\mathbb{T}_{Z}:\mathrm{dist}(\theta,\tilde{\theta})\lesssim\rho^{-1/2}~\text{and}~T_{\theta,v}\cap\{t=\widetilde{t}_{0}\}\cap B\not=\varnothing\},

so as proved in above (
6.8
), one has
\mathbb{T}_{\tilde{\theta},w}\cap\mathbb{T}_{Z}\subset\mathbb{T}_{Z,B,\tilde{\theta}}
. We will prove Proposition
6.8
with
\mathbb{T}_{\tilde{\theta},w}\cap\mathbb{T}_{Z}
replaced by
\mathbb{T}_{Z,B,\tilde{\theta}}
. Next, for every (linear) subspace

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define a collection of bigger wave packets

as

(6.28)

\mathbb{T}_{V,B,\tilde{\theta}}:=\{(\theta,v):T_{\theta,v}\cap B\not=\varnothing,~\measuredangle(G(\omega_{\theta}),V)\lesssim r^{-1/2+\delta_{m}}~\text{and}~\mathrm{dist}(\theta,\tilde{\theta})\lesssim\rho^{-1/2}\}.

Lemma 6.9
.

Suppose that
V
is a subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
V_{0}:=V\cap\{t=\widetilde{t}_{0}\}
and define
V^{\prime}
as
V_{0}^{\perp}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. If

is concentrated on bigger wave packets from
\mathbb{T}_{V,B,\tilde{\theta}}
, if
\Pi\subset\{t=\widetilde{t}_{0}\}
is any affine subspace parallel to

and if

, then

(6.29)

\int_{\Pi\cap B(y_{1},\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}})}|\widehat{g}|^{2}\lesssim r^{O(\delta_{m})}\Big(\frac{\rho^{1/2}}{r^{1/2}}\Big)^{\dim(V^{\prime})}\int_{\Pi}|\widehat{g}|^{2}.

Proof.

Since
g
is concentrated on large wave packets from
\mathbb{T}_{V,B,\tilde{\theta}}
and since

is linear (up to a scalar depending on
\omega

), there is a shift
\omega_{V}\in B^{n-1}(0,1)
such that

(6.30)

\{\omega:\measuredangle(G(\omega),V)\lesssim r^{-1/2+\delta_{m}}\}\subset\{\omega:\text{dist}(\omega,V_{0}+\omega_{V})\lesssim r^{-1/2+\delta_{m}}\}.

It implies that

is supported in the

neighborhood of

inside the unit ball

. As a result, the Fourier transform of

is supported in an

, which implies (See also Lemma

(6.31)

|(\widehat{g}|_{\Pi})|\lesssim|(\widehat{g}|_{\Pi})|\ast\eta_{r^{1/2-\delta_{m}}}.

Finally, we integrate
|(\widehat{g}|_{\Pi})|^{2}
inside the ball

and invoke Hölder’s inequality to conclude (
6.29
).
∎

Proof of Proposition
6.8
.

We prove Proposition

]

Since wave packets in

It implies that the function

is indeed concentrated on wave packets from

. So we can apply Lemma

to obtain a subspace

that

(6.34)

\int_{\Pi\cap B(y_{1},\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}})}|\widehat{h}|^{2}\lesssim r^{O(\delta_{m})}\Big(\frac{\rho^{1/2}}{r^{1/2}}\Big)^{\dim(V^{\prime})}\int_{\Pi}|\widehat{h}|^{2}

for any

Claim 1.

Assume at first that the three claims were verified. We plug Claim 3 back to (

\int_{\Pi}|\widehat{h}|^{2}\cdot{\bf 1}_{\{N_{C\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}}(\Phi(Z_{0}))\cap\Phi(CB)\}}\lesssim r^{O(\delta_{m})}\Big(\frac{\rho}{r}\Big)^{(n-m)/2}\int_{\Pi}|\widehat{h}|^{2}.

Proof of Claim 1.
We follow essentially the proof strategy in
[
Gut18
]
. Suppose that Claim 1 fails. It means that there is a point
\Phi(z)\in\Phi(Z_{0})\cap\Phi(B(0,C\lambda))
and a subspace

with
\dim Z_{0}-\dim W_{\Phi}+\dim V^{\prime}<n-1
, such that for any non-zero vector
w\in W_{\Phi}
, there is a big constant
C^{\prime}
only depending on the

in Theorem
3.4
that

(6.36)

\measuredangle(w,V^{\prime})\leq(C^{\prime})^{-2}.

Let
(\Phi^{-1})^{\ast}_{z}:T_{\Phi(z)}\Phi(Z_{0})\to T_{z}Z_{0}
be the map between tangent spaces. Then
\measuredangle(v,(\Phi^{-1})_{z}^{\ast}(v))<\pi/2-(C^{\prime})^{-1}
for any

because of the positivity of

(See Lemma

). Hence if we define

as the pullback

, then

since

is perpendicular to

. As we will show below, this indeed implies for arbitrary non-zero vector

(6.37)

\measuredangle(w,V)\gtrsim 1.

To prove (

), let

with

and
w_{2}\in V_{0}
, so
\text{orth}_{V}(w)=\text{orth}_{V}(w_{1})
(here we use
\text{orth}_{V}(w):=w-\text{proj}_{V}(w)
in convention). Note that the lower bound
\measuredangle(w,V_{0})\gtrsim 1
gives
|w_{1}|\gtrsim|w|
. Now for any unit vector

, we write

where
v_{1}\perp V_{0}
and

and

due to (

for some absolute constant

, which implies

and hence (
6.37
). Finally, since

, one has
\dim(Z_{0})-\dim(W)<\dim(V)
. Hence the angle estimate (
6.37
) contradicts the minimality of

, which can be seen via repeating the proof in page 115 of
[
Gut18
]
. This proves Claim 1.

Proof of Claim 2.

Since
\Pi
is an affine subspace, it is the intersection of
\kappa:=n-1-\dim(V^{\prime})
mutually orthogonal hyperplanes
L_{1},\ldots,L_{\kappa}
. Suppose that each
L_{j}
is parameterized as

(6.38)

L_{j}:m_{j}\cdot x+l_{j}=0,

where
m_{j}
is the normal vector of
L_{j}
and
l_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
\Phi^{-1}(L_{j})
is indeed a branch of the quadratic variety

(6.39)

\Phi^{-1}(L_{j}):|\lambda m_{j}\cdot(\widetilde{t}_{0}\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}-x)|^{2}-(\lambda^{2}+|x-\widetilde{t}_{0}\omega_{\tilde{\theta}}|^{2})l_{j}=0.

Similar to the proof of Lemma
6.5
, we can show
J_{\Phi^{-1}}(x)
is not degenerate for all

. Hence
\Phi^{-1}(L_{1})\cap\ldots\cap\Phi^{-1}(L_{\kappa})
is a transverse complete intersection.

Proof of Claim 3.
Similar to Claim 1, we know that
\Phi^{-1}(\Pi)\cap Z_{0}
is a transverse complete intersection inside
B(0,C_{n}\lambda)
(In fact, by Lemma
10.3
, the pullback
\Phi^{-1}(\Pi)
is transverse to
Z
for generic affine spaces
\Pi
). Note that the dimension of the variety
\Phi^{-1}(\Pi)\cap Z_{0}
is
\dim Z_{0}-\dim V_{0}
. By Wongkew’s theorem
[
Won93
]
, the set
N_{C\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}}(\Phi^{-1}(\Pi)\cap Z_{0})\cap CB
can be covered by

(6.40)

r^{O(\delta_{m})}\Big(\frac{r^{1/2}}{\rho^{1/2}}\Big)^{\dim Z_{0}-\dim V_{0}}

many
(n-1)
-dimensional balls. Hence via Lemma
6.5
we know that
N_{C\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}}(\Pi\cap\Phi(Z_{0}))\cap\Phi(CB)
can be covered the the same amount of
(n-1)
-dimensional balls, up to a constant.

Since
T_{z}\Phi(Z_{0})
is quantitatively transverse to
\Pi
at every point

by Claim 1, one can argue similarly as in Lemma 8.13 in

]
to conclude

\Pi\cap N_{C\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}}(\Phi(Z_{0}))\cap\Phi(CB)\subset{\rm Proj}_{\Pi}[N_{C\rho^{1/2+\delta_{m}}}(\Pi\cap\Phi(Z_{0}))\cap\Phi(CB)].

Therefore

can be covered by

of radius

∎

Multigrains and functions concentrated near a variety

In this section, following
[
HR19
]
and
[
HZ20
]
, we introduce some definitions and propositions for later use in Section
8
and
9
. In contrast to
[
HZ20

, the definitions and propositions are stated in terms of the “dimension” of the transverse complete intersection instead of the codimension so that the notations are consistent with those in the previous sections.

7.1.
Multigrain and nested structure

Here we introduce some definitions and lemmas about grains and multigrains, following Section 3 of
[
HZ20
]
.

Definition 7.1
.

A grain is defined to be a pair
(S,B_{r})
where
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a transverse complete intersection and
B_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a ball of some radius
r>0
. The dimension of a grain
(S,B_{r})
is the dimension of the transverse complete intersection
S
, and its degree is the degree of
S
.

Definition 7.2
.

Let
(S,B({\bf x}_{0},r))
be a grain of dimension
m
. A function
f
is said to be tangent to
(S,B({\bf x}_{0},r))
if it is concentrated on wave packets belonging to the collection

(7.1)

\{T_{\theta,v}({\bf x}_{0})\in\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]:T_{\theta,v}({\bf x}_{0})\;\mathrm{is}\;r^{-1/2+\delta_{m}}\mathrm{-tangent\;to}\;S\;\mathrm{in}\;B({\bf x}_{0},r)\}.

Definition 7.3
.

A multigrain
\vec{S}_{m}
is an
(n-m+1)
-tuple of grains

\vec{S}_{m}=(\mathcal{G}_{n},\dots,\mathcal{G}_{m}),\qquad\mathcal{G}_{i}=(S_{i},B_{r_{i}})\qquad\textrm{ for $m\leq i\leq n$}

satisfying

•

\mathrm{dim}(S_{i})=i
for
m\leq i\leq n
,

•

S_{n}\supset S_{n-1}\supset\cdots\supset S_{m}
,

•

B_{r_{n}}\supset B_{r_{n-1}}\supset\cdots\supset B_{r_{m}}
.

The parameter
n-m
is referred to as the
level
of the multigrain
\vec{S}_{m}
. The
complexity
of the multigrain is defined to be the maximum of the degrees
\deg S_{i}
over all
m\leq i\leq n
. Finally, the
multiscale
of
\vec{S}_{m}
is the tuple
\vec{r}=(r_{n},r_{n-1},\dots,r_{m})
.
For two multigrains
\vec{S}_{l}
and
\vec{S}_{m}
with
m\leq l
, we write

\vec{S}_{m}\preceq\vec{S}_{l}

if the first
n-l+1
components of
\vec{S}_{m}
agree those of
\vec{S}_{l}
.

Definition 7.4
.

Let
\vec{S}_{m}=(\mathcal{G}_{n},\dots,\mathcal{G}_{m})
be a multigrain and

\mathcal{G}_{i}=\big(S_{i},B({\bf x}_{i},r_{i})\big)\quad\textrm{for $m\leq i\leq n$.}

Define
\mathbb{T}[\vec{S}_{m}]
to be the set of scale
R:=r_{n}
tubes
T_{\theta_{n},v_{n}}({\bf x}_{n})\in\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{n},r_{n})]
satisfying the following hypothesis:

Nested tube hypothesis.
There exists
T_{\theta_{i},v_{i}}({\bf x}_{i})\in\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{i},r_{i})]
for
m\leq i<n
such that

(1)

\mathrm{dist}(\theta_{i},\theta_{j})\lesssim r_{j}^{-1/2}
,

(2)

\mathrm{dist}\big(T_{\theta_{j},v_{j}}({\bf x}_{j}),\,T_{\theta_{i},v_{i}}({\bf x}_{i})\cap B({\bf x}_{j},r_{j})\big)\lesssim r_{i}^{(1+\delta)/2}
,

(3)

T_{\theta_{j},v_{j}}({\bf x}_{j})\subset N_{r_{j}^{1/2+\delta_{j}}}S_{j}

hold true for all
i,j
with
m\leq j\leq i\leq n
.

The direction set of
\mathbb{T}[\vec{S}_{m}]
is defined by

(7.2)

\Theta[\vec{S}_{m}]:=\{\theta\in\Theta_{R}:T_{\theta,v}\in\mathbb{T}[\vec{S}_{m}]\text{ for some $v\in R^{1/2}\mathbb{Z}^{n-1}$}\}.

A main ingredient of the proof of Theorem
3.4
is the following lemma.

Lemma 7.5

(Lemma 3.7 of
[
HZ20
]
)
.

Let
\vec{S}_{m}
be a level
n-m
multigrain with multiscale
\vec{r}_{m}=(r_{n},\ldots,r_{m})
and complexity at most
d
. If
R=r_{n}
and the constants in (
1.11
) are chosen appropriately, then

(7.3)

\#\Theta[\vec{S}_{m}]\lesssim_{\epsilon_{\circ},d}\Big(\prod_{i=m}^{n-1}r_{i}^{-1/2}\Big)R^{(n-1)/2+\epsilon_{\circ}}.

Since our tubes
T_{\theta,v}
are straight, and the tubes
T_{\theta,v}
corresponding to the same
\theta
indicate the same direction (see the definition of the tube
T_{\theta,v}
(
4.16
)), the above lemma can be proved by using the nested polynomial Wolff axioms of Zahl
[
Zah21
]
and Hickman, Rogers and Zhang
[
HRZ19
]
; for instance, one can follow the proof of Lemma 3.7 of
[
HZ20
]
. We leave out the details.

7.2.
Some lemmas

There are more definitions and lemmas that we will need in Section 8 and 9. Let us state them here, following Section 8 of
[
HR19
]
and Section 5 of
[
HZ20
]
. In this subsection, we fix a scale
r\geq 1
and a smaller scale
r^{1/2}\leq\rho\leq r
, and we consider balls

(7.4)

B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)\subset B({\bf x}_{0},r)\subset[-3C_{n}\lambda,3C_{n}\lambda]^{n-1}\times[R/C_{n},C_{n}R].

Recall some definitions in Section 6:

(7.5)

\begin{split}\mathbb{T}_{Z}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]&=\{T\in\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]:T\text{ is $r^{-1/2+\delta_{m}}$-tangent to }Z\text{ in }B({\bf x}_{0},r)\},\\
\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{b}[B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]&=\{\widetilde{T}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}[B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]:\widetilde{T}\text{ is $\rho^{-1/2+\delta_{m}}$-tangent to }Z+b\text{ in }B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)\}.\end{split}

For some technical issue and rigorousness, we introduce a “thickening” of an arbitrary subset of

\mathbb{W}^{*}:=\{T_{\theta,v}\in\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]:\mathrm{dist}(\theta,{\theta}^{\prime})\lesssim r^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{and}\,|v-{v}^{\prime}|\lesssim r^{(1+\delta)/2}\,\mathrm{for\;some}\,({\theta}^{\prime},{v}^{\prime})\subset\mathbb{W}\}.

Note that

is a set slightly larger than

. Intuitively, one can identify
\mathbb{W}^{\ast}
with

.

Definition 7.6
.

For
\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}\subset\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}[B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]
we define
\uparrow\!\!\!\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}
to be a collection of all pairs
T_{\theta,v}({\bf x}_{0})\in\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]
such that there exists a non-empty set
\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\tilde{v}}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0})\in\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}
satisfying

(1)

\mathrm{dist}(\tilde{\theta},{\theta})\lesssim\rho^{-1/2}

(2)

\mathrm{dist}(\widetilde{T}_{\tilde{\theta},\tilde{v}}(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0}),T_{\theta,v}({\bf x}_{0})\cap B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho))\lesssim r^{1/2+\delta}
.

This definition naturally appears when comparing wave packets at different scales by the following property: For every
\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}\subset\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}[B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]
and

(7.7)

g|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}}=(g|_{\uparrow\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}})|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}}+\mathrm{RapDec}(r)\|g\|_{2}.

It can be thought of as a reverse version of Lemma
5.4
. Since the proof is straightforward, we leave out the details.

Lemma 7.7
.

For every set
\mathbb{W}_{1},\mathbb{W}_{2}\subset\mathbb{T}[B({\bf x}_{0},r)]
and function
g
, it holds that

(7.8)

\|(g|_{\mathbb{W}_{1}})|_{\mathbb{W}_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|g|_{{\mathbb{W}_{1}}\cap{\mathbb{W}_{2}^{*}}}\|_{L^{2}}+\mathrm{RapDec}(r)\|g\|_{2}.

Proof.

We split our function into two parts:

(7.9)

(g|_{\mathbb{W}_{1}})|_{\mathbb{W}_{2}}=(g|_{\mathbb{W}_{1}\cap\mathbb{W}_{2}^{*}})|_{\mathbb{W}_{2}}+(g|_{\mathbb{W}_{1}\setminus\mathbb{W}_{2}^{*}})|_{\mathbb{W}_{2}}.

We apply Lemma
4.2
to the function
g|_{\mathbb{W}_{1}\cap\mathbb{W}_{2}^{*}}
and obtain

(7.10)

\|(g|_{\mathbb{W}_{1}\cap\mathbb{W}_{2}^{*}})|_{\mathbb{W}_{2}}\|_{2}\lesssim\|g|_{\mathbb{W}_{1}\cap\mathbb{W}_{2}^{*}}\|_{2}.

On the other hand, by the proof of Lemma
5.4
, we see that

(7.11)

\|(g|_{\mathbb{W}_{1}\setminus\mathbb{W}_{2}^{*}})|_{\mathbb{W}_{2}}\|_{2}=\mathrm{RapDec}(r)\|g\|_{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 7.8
.

Let

\|{g}|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim_{d}r^{O(\delta_{m})}\Big(\frac{r}{\rho}\Big)^{-\frac{n-m}{2}}\|g|_{\uparrow\widetilde{\mathbb{W}}}\|_{2}^{2}+\mathrm{RapDec}(r)\|g\|_{2}^{2}.

The above lemma is a corollary of Lemma

Lemma 7.9
.

Let

Suppose that

(7.13)

g_{b}:=g|_{\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}^{\prime}_{b}[B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]}\text{ \, for some set \, $\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}^{\prime}_{b}[B(\widetilde{{\bf x}}_{0},\rho)]\subset\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{b}$}.

The proof of Lemma

Lemma 7.10

Let

The appendix is devoted to a transversality lemma . First, let us introduce one more definition.

Definition 10.1
.

Let

(

]

.

Then the complement of the set

\pitchfork(F;A):=\{s\in S:F_{s}\pitchfork A\}

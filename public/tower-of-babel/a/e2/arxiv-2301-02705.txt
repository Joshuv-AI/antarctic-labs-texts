Covariance loss, Szemeredi regularity, and differential privacy
March Boedihardjo, Thomas Strohmer, Roman Vershynin

Abstract
. We show how randomized rounding based on Grothendieck’s identity can be used to prove a nearly tight bound on the covariance loss–the amount of covariance that is lost by taking conditional expectation. This result yields a new type of weak Szemeredi regularity lemma for positive semidefinite matrices and kernels. Moreover, it can be used to construct differentially private synthetic data.



1.
Introduction

Consider a random vector
X
taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}
.
Let
Y
denote the conditional expectation:
Y=\E[X|\mathcal{F}]
.
In dimension
d=1
, where
X
and
Y
are random variables, the law of total variance states that

(1.1)

\Var(X)-\Var(Y)=\E X^{2}-\E Y^{2}=\E(X-Y)^{2}\geq 0.

Thus, taking conditional expectation underestimates the variance. This observation extends to higher dimensions. Namely, let
\Sigma_{X}=\E(X-\E X)(X-\E X)^{\mathsf{T}}
denote the covariance matrix of
X
,
and similarly for
\Sigma_{Y}
. Then

(1.2)

\Sigma_{X}-\Sigma_{Y}=\E XX^{\mathsf{T}}-\E YY^{\mathsf{T}}=\E(X-Y)(X-Y)^{\mathsf{T}}\succeq 0,

where
\succeq
denotes the Loewner order, in which
A\succeq B
if
A-B
is positive semidefinite.
Just like in the one-dimensional case, we see that taking conditional expectation underestimates the covariance.

In
[
2
]
, we asked the basic question: “How big is the
covariance loss

\Sigma_{X}-\Sigma_{Y}
?”
The answer will obviously depend on the choice of the sigma-algebra
\mathcal{F}
, prompting the next question: “What sigma-algebra
\mathcal{F}
of given complexity minimizes the covariance loss?”

It was shown in
[
2
]
that there exists a partition of the sample space into at most
k
sets
such that for the sigma-algebra
\mathcal{F}
generated by this partition, the covariance loss (
1.2
) is upper bounded by
C\sqrt{\log\log k/\log k}
, where
C
is an absolute constant. In
[
6
]
, Jain, Sah, and Sawhney were able to improve this bound to
C/\sqrt{\log k}
. It follows from Proposition 3.14 in
[
2
]
that this bound is optimal up to the value of the constant
C
. The proofs of the bounds in both
[
2
]
and
[
6
]
are somewhat technical. In this paper we give a new approach to this problem, which is conceptually related to Szemeredi regularity. This approach yields a tighter bound in terms of the constant on the covariance loss (Corollary
5.1
), as well as a much shorter and more elementary proof.

The celebrated Szemeredi regularity lemma states that that for every large enough graph, the set of nodes can be divided into subsets of about the same size so that the edges between different subsets behave almost randomly
[
10
]
. Expressed in the language of linear algebra, it says that the adjacency matrix of a graph can be approximated by a sum of cut matrices
1
1

1

A cut matrix is a rank-one matrix which is constant on a block and zero elsewhere
[
5
]
.
.
The Szemeredi regularity lemma, both in the combinatorial and linear-algebraic forms, has many deep algorithmic and combinatorial applications, see e.g.
[
5
,
8
,
7
,
1
]
.
We prove a new version of (weak) Szemeredi regularity lemma,
which states that any positive semidefinite kernel
K(x,y)
whose diagonal is uniformly bounded can be decomposed into a sum of
k
step-functions plus an error term whose
L^{2}
-norm is
O(1/\sqrt{\log k})
.

The outline of the paper is as follows.
We will first bound the covariance loss by the “covariance increment” (Proposition
2.1
), find a nice representation of the covariance increment (Proposition
2.3
), and combine it with randomized rounding based on Grothendieck’s identity to bound the covariance increment (Theorem
3.1
). Thus we can not only bound the covariance loss (Corollary
5.1
) but also derive a new type of weak Szemeredi regularity for matrices (Theorem
5.3
) and kernels (Theorem
5.6
).
We conclude by illustrating how our results can be used in connection with differential privacy and synthetic data to improve the accuracy bounds obtained in
[
2
]
.

1.1.
Notation

The subgaussian norm of a random variable
X
is denoted by

\|X\|_{{\psi_{2}}}
(see e.g.
[
11
]
),
\E(X)
is the expectation of
X
and
\|X\|_{L^{p}}=(\mathbb{E}|X|^{p})^{1/p}
. For
d\times d
matrices
A,B
, define the inner product
\langle A,B\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}(AB^{T})
and the Frobenius norm
\|A\|_{F}=\sqrt{\langle A,A\rangle}
.

2.
The covariance increment

We start out by bounding the covariance loss
\E XX^{\mathsf{T}}-\E YY^{\mathsf{T}}
by the covariance increment.

In the sequel,
X
is a random vector taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathcal{F}
is a
\sigma
-algebra and
Y=\E[X|\mathcal{F}]
. Moreover,
(X^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},Y^{\prime})
is an independent copy of
(X,\mathcal{F},Y)
so that the sample space is a product space for which
X,\mathcal{F},Y
are based on the first component of the product space and
X^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},Y^{\prime}
are based on the second.

With a slight abuse of notation, the product
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F}
denotes the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. Moreover, the inner product
\langle Y,Y^{\prime}\rangle=\mathbb{E}(\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle|\,\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F})
.

Proposition 2.1

(Covariance loss and covariance increment)
.

(2.1)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\E XX^{\mathsf{T}}-\E YY^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rVert}_{F}^{2}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\E XX^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rVert}_{F}^{2}-\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\E YY^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rVert}_{F}^{2}.

The proof of Proposition
2.1
will be based on the following lemma:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(U,V)
be a pair of random vectors taking values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
(U^{\prime},V^{\prime})
be an independent copy.
Then

\langle\E UU^{\mathsf{T}},\E VV^{\mathsf{T}}\rangle=\E\langle U,V^{\prime}\rangle^{2}.

In particular, setting
V=U
, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\E UU^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rVert}_{F}^{2}=\E\langle U,U^{\prime}\rangle^{2}.

Proof.

The first identity readily follows if we first use the identical distribution, and then independence:

\langle\E UU^{\mathsf{T}},\E VV^{\mathsf{T}}\rangle=\langle\E UU^{\mathsf{T}},\E V^{\prime}(V^{\prime})^{\mathsf{T}}\rangle=\E\langle UU^{\mathsf{T}},V^{\prime}(V^{\prime})^{\mathsf{T}}\rangle=\E\langle U,V^{\prime}\rangle^{2}.

The lemma is proved.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Expanding the square of the Frobenius norm, we can express the left hand side of (
2.1
) as

\mathinner{\!\bigl\lVert\E XX^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr\rVert}_{F}^{2}-2\langle\E XX^{\mathsf{T}},\E YY^{\mathsf{T}}\rangle+\mathinner{\lVert\E YY^{\mathsf{T}}\rVert}_{F}^{2}
.
After simplification, we see that inequality (
2.1
) is equivalent to

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\E YY^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rVert}_{F}^{2}\leq\langle\E XX^{\mathsf{T}},\E YY^{\mathsf{T}}\rangle.

Using Lemma
2.2
we can rewrite this as

(2.2)

\E\langle Y,Y^{\prime}\rangle^{2}\leq\E\langle X,Y^{\prime}\rangle^{2},

To check (
2.2
), recall that
Y=\E[X|\mathcal{F}]
and apply the conditional Jensen’s inequality.
∎

The covariance increment has a nice representation, which will come handy in our further analysis.

Proposition 2.3
.

(2.3)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\E XX^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rVert}_{F}^{2}-\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\E YY^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rVert}_{F}^{2}=\E\Big(\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle-\langle Y,Y^{\prime}\rangle\Big)^{2}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, the left hand side of (
2.3
) equals

\E\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle^{2}-\E\langle Y,Y^{\prime}\rangle^{2}
.
Note that
\langle Y,Y^{\prime}\rangle=\E(\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle|\,\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F})
.
To finish the proof, apply the law of total variance (
1.1
) for
\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle
instead of
X

and
\langle Y,Y^{\prime}\rangle
instead of
Y
.
∎

3.
Bounding the covariance increment

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a random vector taking values
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X\right\rVert}_{2}\leq 1
a.s.
Then, for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a partition of the sample space into at most
2^{r}
parts
such that for the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}
generated by this partition, the conditional expectation

Y=\E[X|\mathcal{F}]
satisfies

\E\Big(\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle-\langle Y,Y^{\prime}\rangle\Big)^{2}\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{r}.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
will utilize Grothendieck’s identity (see e.g.
[
11
, Lemma 3.6.6]
):

Lemma 3.2

(Grothendieck’s identity)
.

Let
x,x^{\prime}
be a pair of unit vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
g\sim N(0,I_{d})
.
Then

\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle=\sin\left[\frac{\pi}{2}\E\sign\langle x,g\rangle\sign\langle x^{\prime},g\rangle\right].

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Step 1.
Let us first make a stronger assumption, namely that
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X\right\rVert}_{2}=1
a.s.,
and prove a weaker conclusion, namely that there exists a random variable
Z
measurable with respect to the product
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F}
and such that

(3.1)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle-Z\right\rVert}_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{r}}.

To this end, consider independent random vectors
g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}\in N(0,I_{d})
,
and for
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define the random variable

F_{g}(x,x^{\prime})=\sin\left[\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{r}\sum_{k=1}^{r}\sign\langle x,g_{k}\rangle\sign\langle x^{\prime},g_{k}\rangle\right].

Denoting
\xi_{k}=\sign\langle x,g_{k}\rangle\sign\langle x^{\prime},g_{k}\rangle
, noting that the function
\sin(\cdot)
is
1
-Lipschitz and applying Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

(3.2)

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle-F_{g}(x,x^{\prime})\right\rvert}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{r}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\sum_{k=1}^{r}(\xi_{k}-\E\xi_{k})\right\rvert}.

By independence, this yields

\E_{g}\left(\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle-F_{g}(x,x^{\prime})\right)^{2}\leq\left(\frac{\pi}{2r}\right)^{2}\cdot r\Var(\xi_{1})\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{4r}.

Substitute
x=X
,
x^{\prime}=X^{\prime}
and take expectation with respect to
X
and
X^{\prime}
.
By Fubini theorem, there exists a realization of the random vectors
g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}
such that

\E\left(\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle-F_{g}(X,X^{\prime})\right)^{2}\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{4r},

Fix such a realization.
Let
\mathcal{F}=\sigma(V(X))
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the random vector
V(X)=(\sign\langle x,g_{k}\rangle)_{k=1}^{r}
. Since
V(X)
takes at most
2^{r}
values,
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the requirement of the theorem. Moreover, the random vector

Z=F_{g}(X,X^{\prime})=\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2r}\langle V(X),V(X^{\prime})\rangle\right)

is measurable with respect to the product sigma-algebra
\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F}
.
Thus, we proved (
3.1
).

Step 2: replacing
Z
with
\langle Y,Y^{\prime}\rangle
.

The conditional expectation
\E\left[\cdot|\,\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F}\right]
is an orthogonal projection in
L^{2}

onto the subspace of random variables that are
\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F}
-measurable.
Since the random variable
Z
constructed in the previous step is
\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F}
-measurable,
and
\langle Y,Y^{\prime}\rangle=\E\left[\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle|\,\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F}\right]
, it follows from (
3.1
) that

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle-\langle Y,Y^{\prime}\rangle\right\rVert}_{L^{2}}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle-Z\right\rVert}_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{r}}.

Step 3: removing the unit norm requirement.

We proved the theorem under the additional assumption that
X
is a unit random vector.
Now let
X
be an arbitrary random vector satisfying
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X\right\rVert}_{2}\leq 1
.
Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and consider the random vector

W=\sqrt{1-\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X\right\rVert}_{2}^{2}}\cdot\theta
,
where
\theta
is a random vector that is
uniformly distributed in the unit basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{N}\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Then the direct sum
X\oplus W=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d},W_{1},\ldots,W_{N})
is a unit random vector
in
\mathbb{R}^{d+N}
. Applying the previous step for
X\oplus W
, we find that

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{r}}

\displaystyle\geq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle X\oplus W,X^{\prime}\oplus W^{\prime}\rangle-\langle Y\oplus\eta,Y^{\prime}\oplus\eta^{\prime}\rangle\right\rVert}_{L^{2}}\quad\text{(where $\eta=\E(W|\mathcal{F})$)}

\displaystyle=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle-\langle Y,Y^{\prime}\rangle+\langle W,W^{\prime}\rangle-\langle\eta,\eta^{\prime}\rangle\right\rVert}_{L^{2}}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\geq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle-\langle Y,Y^{\prime}\rangle\right\rVert}_{L^{2}}-\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle W,W^{\prime}\rangle-\langle\eta,\eta^{\prime}\rangle\right\rVert}_{L^{2}}.

Since
\langle\eta,\eta^{\prime}\rangle=\E(\langle W,W^{\prime}\rangle|\,\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{F})
,
the law of total variance (
1.1
) and definition of
W
yields

(3.4)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle W,W^{\prime}\rangle-\langle\eta,\eta^{\prime}\rangle\right\rVert}_{L^{2}}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle W,W^{\prime}\rangle\right\rVert}_{L^{2}}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle\theta,\theta^{\prime}\rangle\right\rVert}_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}},

where the last identity follows since
\langle\theta,\theta^{\prime}\rangle
is Bernoulli with parameter
1/N
.
Taking
N
to be large enough, we conclude from (
3.3
) that

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle-\langle Y,Y^{\prime}\rangle\right\rVert}_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{r}}.

Theorem
3.1
is proved.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

In the conclusion of Theorem
3.1
, we can replace the constant
\pi^{2}
by
\pi^{2}/4+\epsilon
for any fixed
\epsilon>0
.

4.
Subgaussian error

We can extend the bound in Theorem
3.1
to the subgaussian norm,
which is the Orlicz norm with respect to the Young function
{\psi_{2}}(x)=e^{x^{2}}-1
.
Thus, a random variable
X
is subgaussian if

(4.1)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}}=\inf\{t>0\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\;\E{\psi_{2}}(X/t)\leq 1\}<\infty,

and this quantity is called the subgaussian norm of
X
,
see
[
11
, Sections 2.5, 2.7.1]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a random vector taking values
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X\right\rVert}_{2}\leq 1
a.s.
Then, for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a partition of the sample space into at most
2^{r}
parts
such that for the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}
generated by this partition, the conditional expectation

Y=\E[X|\mathcal{F}]
satisfies

(4.2)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle-\langle Y,Y^{\prime}\rangle\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{r}}.

We can prove this result by modifying the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
Let us explain how to do this.

In Step 1, instead of using additivity of variance, we can use Hoeffding’s inequality to control the sum of independent Bernoulli random variables
\xi_{k}
. Denoting
\zeta_{k}=\xi_{k}-\E\xi_{k}
, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\sum_{k=1}^{r}\zeta_{k}\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}}\lesssim\sqrt{r}
, see
[
11
, Proposition 2.6.1]
. Using this in (
3.2
), we get

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle-F_{g}(x,x^{\prime})\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}}\lesssim 1/\sqrt{r}.

By definition of the subgaussian norm, this means that

\E_{g}{\psi_{2}}\left((\langle x,x^{\prime}\rangle-F_{g}(x,x^{\prime}))c\sqrt{r}\right)\leq 1,

where
c>0
is some absolute constant.
Substituting here
x=X
and
x^{\prime}=X^{\prime}
and applying the Fubini inequality as in Step 1,
we obtain the following version of (
3.1
):

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle-Z\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}.

In Step 2, although the conditional expectation is not a metric projection in the subgaussian norm,
it is an approximate metric projection:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
X
be a random variable and
\mathcal{F}
be a
\sigma
-algebra.
Then, for any random variable
Z
that is
\mathcal{F}
-measurable, and any
p\geq 1
, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X-\E(X|\mathcal{F})\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}}\leq 2\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X-Z\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}}.

Proof.

Subtracting and adding
Z
and using triangle inequality, we get

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X-\E(X|\mathcal{F})\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X-Z\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}}+\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\E(X|\mathcal{F})-Z\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}}.

Since
Z
is
\mathcal{F}
-measurable, we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\E(X|\mathcal{F})-Z\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}}=\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\E(X-Z|\mathcal{F})\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X-Z\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}},

where the last step follows from definition of subgaussian norm (
4.1
)
and conditional Jensen’s inequality.
Combine the two bounds to complete the proof.
∎

Using this lemma for
\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle
instead of
X
, we obtain in Step 2 that

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle-\langle Y,Y^{\prime}\rangle\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}}\leq 2\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle X,X^{\prime}\rangle-Z\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}.

In Step 3, we argue in a similar way about the subgaussian norm.
The bound (
3.4
) becomes

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle W,W^{\prime}\rangle-\langle\eta,\eta^{\prime}\rangle\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}}\leq 2\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle W,W^{\prime}\rangle\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}}\leq\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\langle\theta,\theta^{\prime}\rangle\right\rVert}_{\psi_{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log N}},

Here in the first step we use Lemma
4.2
for
X=\langle W,W^{\prime}\rangle
and
Z=0
,
and the last step is a straightforward bound on the subgaussian norm of
a Bernoulli random variable with parameter
1/N
.
This completes the proof.

5.
Implications

5.1.
Covariance loss

Combining Proposition
2.1
, Proposition
2.3

and Theorem
3.1
, we obtain:

Corollary 5.1

(Covariance loss)
.

Let
X
be a random vector taking values
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert X\right\rVert}_{2}\leq 1
a.s.
Then, for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a partition of the sample space into at most
2^{r}
parts
such that for the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}
generated by this partition, the conditional expectation

Y=\E[X|\mathcal{F}]
satisfies

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\E XX^{\mathsf{T}}-\E YY^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rVert}_{F}\leq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{r}}.

In particular, for any
k
with
\log_{2}k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a partition of the sample space into at most
k
parts
such that for the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}
generated by this partition, we have

(5.1)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\E XX^{\mathsf{T}}-\E YY^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rVert}_{F}\leq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\log_{2}k}}.

Remark 5.2

(Optimality)
.

Proposition 3.14 in
[
2
]
implies that there exists a random vector
X
taking values in the unit ball of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\E XX^{\mathsf{T}}-\E YY^{\mathsf{T}}\right\rVert}_{F}\geq\frac{\sqrt{\log_{2}(e)}}{80\sqrt{\log_{2}k}}
for any
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}
generated by a partition into at most
k
parts.
Thus the bound in (
5.1
) is sharp up to an absolute constant.

5.2.
Weak Szemeredi regularity

Theorem 5.3

(Weak Szemeredi regularity)
.

Let
A
be an
n\times n
positive semidefinite matrix such that
A_{ii}\leq 1
for all
i
.
Then, for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a partition

[n]=I_{1}\cup\ldots\cup I_{k}
with
k\leq 2^{r}
,
and a matrix
B
that is constant on each block
I_{i}\times I_{j}
and such that

(5.2)

\frac{1}{n}\mathinner{\!\left\lVert A-B\right\rVert}_{F}\leq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{r}}.

Moreover,
B
can be computed by averaging the entries of
A
in each block.

Proof.

By assumption,
A
can be represented as the Gram matrix of some vectors
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
satisfying
\mathinner{\!\left\lVert x_{i}\right\rVert}_{2}\leq 1
for all
i
. Thus
A=[\langle x_{i},x_{j}\rangle]_{i,j=1}^{n}
.
Apply Theorem
3.1
for the random vector
X
that is uniformly distributed on
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
to complete the proof.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Rewriting the approximation error in Theorem
5.3
as

\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(A_{ij}-B_{ij})^{2}\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{r},

we can interpret it as a bound on the
mean squared error
of the entries.

Remark 5.5
.

Applying Theorem
4.1
instead of Theorem
3.1
, we can replace the Frobenius norm in (
5.2
) by stronger matrix norms, such as the
\ell_{p}
norm of the entries.

Theorem 5.6

(Weak Szemeredi regularity, analytic form)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a probability measure space. Let
K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable positive definite kernel such that
K(t,t)\leq 1
for all
t\in\Omega
. Then for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a partition
\Omega=I_{1}\cup\ldots\cup I_{k}
with
k\leq 2^{r}
, and a function
L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
that is constant on each block
I_{i}\times I_{j}
such that

(5.3)

\|K-L\|_{L^{2}(\mu\times\mu)}\leq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{r}}.

Moreover,
L
can be computed by averaging
K
on each block.

Proof.

When
\Omega
is finite, using Theorem
3.1
(see also Remark
3.3
), one can easily establish

\|K-L\|_{L^{2}(\mu\times\mu)}\leq\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{r}}+\epsilon
for any fixed
\epsilon>0
.
The general case follows by a standard approximation argument, which we leave to the reader.
∎

5.3.
Comparison to the existing work on weak Szemeredi regularity

Our result is more restrictive but stronger that the classical weak regularity lemma by Frieze and Kannan
[
5
]
. There the approximation error
R=K-L
is measured in the
cut norm

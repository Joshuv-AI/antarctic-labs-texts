Slowly rotating quantum dust cores and black holes
R. Casadio, L. Tabarroni

Abstract
We study the effect of rotation on the spectrum of bound states for dust cores that source (quantum) black holes found in Eur. Phys. J. C 82 (2022) 10. The dust ball is assumed to spin rigidly with sufficiently slow angular velocity that perturbation theory can be applied. Like the total mass, the total angular momentum is shown to be quantised in Planck units, hence so is the horizon area. For sufficiently small fraction of mass in the outermost layer, the model admits ground states which can spin fast enough within the perturbative regime so as to describe regular rotating objects rather than black holes.

where

.

\displaystyle\frac{E_{\mu}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}-\dot{R}^{2}+\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,M_{0}}{R}-\left(1-\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,M_{0}}{R}\right)\frac{L_{\mu}^{2}}{R^{2}\,\mu^{2}}=1\ ,

2.1
Zero angular momentum and mass quantisation

The case of purely radial motion was analysed in Refs.
[
14
,
15
]
,
which we briefly review here.
For
L_{\mu}=0
, Eq. (
2.2
) reads

\displaystyle H_{0}\equiv\frac{P^{2}}{2\,\epsilon\,M}-\frac{\epsilon\left(1-\epsilon\right)G_{\rm N}\,M^{2}}{R}=\frac{\epsilon\,M}{2}\left(\frac{E_{\mu}^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}\,M^{2}}-1\right)\equiv\mathcal{E}\ ,

(2.3)

where
P=\epsilon\,M\,\dot{R}
is the momentum conjugated to
R=R(\tau)
.
The canonical quantisation prescription with
\hat{P}=-i\,\hbar\,\partial_{R}

allows us to write Eq. (
2.3
) as the time-independent Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{0}\,\Psi_{\bar{n}}=\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\,\epsilon\,M}\left(\frac{\mbox{${\rm d}$}^{2}}{\mbox{${\rm d}$}R^{2}}+\frac{2}{R}\,\frac{\mbox{${\rm d}$}}{\mbox{${\rm d}$}R}\right)-\frac{\epsilon\left(1-\epsilon\right)G_{\rm N}\,M^{2}}{R}\right]\Psi_{\bar{n}}=\mathcal{E}_{\bar{n}}\,\Psi_{\bar{n}}\ ,

(2.4)

which is formally the same as the one for the
s
-states of the hydrogen atom.
The solutions are thus given by the Hamiltonian eigenfunctions

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bar{n}}(R)=\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon^{6}\left(1-\epsilon\right)^{3}M^{9}}{\pi\,\ell_{\rm p}^{3}\,m_{\rm p}^{9}\,\bar{n}^{5}}}\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{2}\,(1-\epsilon)\,M^{3}\,R}{\bar{n}\,m_{\rm p}^{3}\,\ell_{\rm p}}\right)L_{\bar{n}-1}^{1}\!\!\left(\frac{2\,\epsilon^{2}\,(1-\epsilon)\,M^{3}\,R}{\bar{n}\,m_{\rm p}^{3}\,\ell_{\rm p}}\right)\ ,

(2.5)

where the normalisation is defined in the scalar product

\displaystyle\mbox{$\langle\,\bar{n}\mid\bar{n}^{\prime}\,\rangle$}=4\,\pi\,\int_{0}^{\infty}R^{2}\,\Psi_{\bar{n}}^{*}(R)\,\Psi_{\bar{n}^{\prime}}^{\phantom{}}(R)\,\mbox{${\rm d}$}R=\delta_{\bar{n}\bar{n}^{\prime}}\ ,

(2.6)

the functions
L_{{\bar{n}}-1}^{1}
are Laguerre polynomials and the non-negative integer quantum number
{\bar{n}}

corresponds to the eigenvalues

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\bar{n}}=-\frac{\epsilon^{3}\,(1-\epsilon)^{2}\,M^{5}}{2\,\bar{n}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{4}}\ .

(2.7)

The expectation value of the areal radius on these states is given by

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{\bar{n}}\equiv\bra{\bar{n}}\hat{R}\ket{\bar{n}}=\frac{3\,\ell_{\rm p}\,\bar{n}^{2}\,m_{\rm p}^{3}}{2\,\epsilon^{2}\,(1-\epsilon)\,M^{3}}\ ,

(2.8)

so that the quantum picture is the same that one would have in Newtonian physics.
In particular, the ground state
\bar{n}=1
has a width
\bar{R}_{1}\sim\ell_{\rm p}\,(m_{\rm p}/M)^{3}
and energy
\mathcal{E}_{1}\sim-M\,(M/m_{\rm p})^{4}
,
which makes it practically indistinguishable from a point-like singularity if
M\gg m_{\rm p}
.

In fact, the only general relativistic feature that the model retains is given by
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}(E_{\mu})

in Eq. (
2.3
).
By then assuming that
E_{\mu}
is well-defined for the allowed quantum states, we obtain

\displaystyle 0\leq\frac{E_{\mu}^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}\,M^{2}}=1-\frac{\epsilon^{2}\,(1-\epsilon)^{2}}{\bar{n}^{2}}\left(\frac{M}{m_{\rm p}}\right)^{4}\ ,

(2.9)

which yields the lower bound

\displaystyle\bar{n}\geq N_{M}\equiv\epsilon\,(1-\epsilon)\left(\frac{M}{m_{\rm p}}\right)^{2}\ .

(2.10)

The fundamental state of the outermost layer hence corresponds to
\bar{n}=N_{M}\gg 1
, with

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N_{M}}=-\frac{\epsilon\,M}{2}\ .

(2.11)

This result clearly resembles Bekenstein’s famous area law quantisation
[
22
]
,
since the allowed values of the mass and gravitational area are quantised in Planck units
according to
4
4

4

We recall that
R_{\rm H}=2\,G_{\rm N}\,M
is the classical Schwarzschild (or gravitational) radius of the ball.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{M}=16\,\pi\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}=\frac{16\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}\,N_{M}}{\epsilon\,(1-\epsilon)}\ ,

(2.12)

where the entire spectrum is given by
\bar{n}=N_{M}+n
, with
n
a non-negative integer.

We can now see that the singularity is precluded, as one expects from semiclassical
models
[
8
,
9
]
, since

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{N_{M}}=\frac{3}{2}\,(1-\epsilon)\,G_{\rm N}\,M=\frac{3}{4}\,(1-\epsilon)\,R_{\rm H}\ ,

(2.13)

and
\bar{R}_{N_{M}}\sim R_{\rm H}\gg\ell_{\rm p}
unless the value of
\epsilon
is extremely close to
1
.
Furthermore
\bar{R}_{N_{M}}<(3/4)\,R_{\rm H}
for any value of
0<\epsilon<1
, so that a non-spinning
dust ball in the ground state can be the matter core of a quantum black hole for any mass
M
.
We also notice that the relative uncertainty in the areal radius is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta R_{\bar{n}}}{\bar{R}_{\bar{n}}}\equiv\frac{\sqrt{\bra{\bar{n}}\hat{R}^{2}\ket{\bar{n}}-\bar{R}_{n}^{2}}}{\bar{R}_{\bar{n}}}=\frac{\sqrt{\bar{n}^{2}+2}}{3\,\bar{n}}\ ,

(2.14)

which asymptotes to a minimum of
1/3
for
\bar{n}\to\infty
.
In the following, for simplicity, we will mostly consider the ground state
\bar{n}=N_{M}
with
M\gg m_{\rm p}
.

2.2
Rigidly rotating ball

Next, we consider the case of a dust ball which rotates rigidly with angular velocity
\omega
.
A proper general relativistic treatment would require replacing the outer Schwarzschild geometry with the
Kerr metric, but we will here be satisfied with a perturbative approach for the quantum system described
previously.
In particular, the outermost layer would have classical angular momentum

\displaystyle L_{\epsilon}=\frac{2}{3}\,\epsilon\,M\,R^{2}\,\omega\ ,

(2.15)

which we assume is small enough and replace for
L_{\mu}
in Eq. (
2.2
).
This yields the total Hamiltonian
H=H_{0}+V_{L}
, where
H_{0}
is given in Eq. (
2.3
)
and

\displaystyle V_{L}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{2\,G_{\rm N}\,(1-\epsilon)\,M)}{R}\right)\frac{L_{\epsilon}^{2}}{2\,\epsilon\,M\,R^{2}}

(2.16)

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{2}{9}\,\epsilon\,M\left[R^{2}-2\,G_{\rm N}\,(1-\epsilon)\,M\,R\right]\omega^{2}\ .

In perturbation theory, this term will result in a correction to the eigenvalues (
2.7
) given by

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{E}_{\bar{n}}\equiv\bra{\bar{n}}\,\hat{V}_{L}\,\ket{\bar{n}}\simeq\frac{2}{9}\,\epsilon\,M\,\bar{R}_{\bar{n}}\left[\frac{10}{9}\,\bar{R}_{\bar{n}}-2\,G_{\rm N}\,(1-\epsilon)\,M\right]\omega^{2}\ ,

(2.17)

where we used Eq. (
2.14
) for
\bar{n}\gg 1
and
\bar{R}_{\bar{n}}
is given in Eq. (
2.8
).

For the ground state
\bar{n}=N_{M}
, we thus find

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{E}_{N_{M}}

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{m_{\rm p}^{4}-\epsilon^{2}\,(1-\epsilon)^{2}\,M^{4}}{9\,\epsilon\,M}\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,\omega^{2}

(2.18)

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{9}\,\epsilon\,M\,(1-\epsilon)^{2}\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}\,\omega^{2}\ ,

in which we assumed that
\epsilon\,(1-\epsilon)\,M^{2}\gg m_{\rm p}^{2}
.
We then have

\displaystyle\bra{N_{M}}\,\hat{H}\,\ket{N_{M}}=\mathcal{E}_{N_{M}}+\Delta\mathcal{E}_{N_{M}}\simeq\mathcal{E}_{N_{M}}\left[1+\frac{2}{9}\,(1-\epsilon)^{2}\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}\,\omega^{2}\right]\ .

(2.19)

This result is acceptable as long as
|\Delta\mathcal{E}_{N_{M}}|\ll|\mathcal{E}_{N_{M}}|
, or

\displaystyle|\omega|\ll\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}\,(1-\epsilon)\,G_{\rm N}\,M}\equiv\omega_{\rm max}\ .

(2.20)

We further notice that
\omega_{\rm max}
corresponds to a total angular momentum per unit mass
given by

\displaystyle a_{\rm max}\equiv\frac{L_{\rm c}}{M}=\frac{2}{5}\,\bra{N_{M}}\,\hat{R}^{2}\,\ket{N_{M}}\,\omega_{\rm max}\simeq 2\,(1-\epsilon)\,G_{\rm N}\,M\ .

(2.21)

In the same approximation, we can compute the correction to the ground state quantum
number to leading order in
\omega
, which is given by

\displaystyle N_{ML}\simeq N_{M}\left[1+\frac{1}{9}\,(1-\epsilon)^{2}\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}\,\omega^{2}\right]\ .

(2.22)

As expected, the angular momentum acts as an effective potential barrier and increases the
minimum allowed quantum number from
N_{M}
to
N_{ML}
, corresponding to a larger radius

\displaystyle\bar{R}_{N_{ML}}\simeq\bar{R}_{N_{M}}\left[1+\frac{2}{9}\,(1-\epsilon)^{2}\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}\,\omega^{2}\right]\ .

(2.23)

In particular, for
\omega=\omega_{\rm max}
, one obtains

\displaystyle N_{\rm max}=\frac{3}{2}\,\epsilon\,(1-\epsilon)\left(\frac{M}{m_{\rm p}}\right)^{2}=\frac{3}{2}\,N_{M}\ ,

(2.24)

corresponding to a radius
\bar{R}_{\rm max}=2\,\bar{R}_{N_{M}}=3\,(1-\epsilon)\,G_{\rm N}\,M
.

We can now study the geometry outside the ball in the ground state by assuming that it is

We recall that the outer horizon for a Kerr metric is located at

\displaystyle R_{+}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle G_{\rm N}\,M+\sqrt{G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}-a^{2}}

(2.25)

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle 2\,G_{\rm N}\,M\left[1-\frac{1}{4}\,(1-\epsilon)^{4}\,G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}\,\omega^{2}\right]\ ,

\displaystyle R_{-}

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle G_{\rm N}\,M-\sqrt{G_{\rm N}^{2}\,M^{2}-a^{2}}

\displaystyle\!\!\simeq\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,(1-\epsilon)^{4}\,G_{\rm N}^{3}\,M^{3}\,\omega^{2}\ll\bar{R}_{N_{M}}\ .

Since

vacuum there and the inner horizon cannot be realised (at least) within the perturbative approximation.
On the other hand,
R_{+}
is larger than the core for

\displaystyle|\omega|<\omega_{\rm H}=\sqrt{\frac{3+9\,\epsilon}{(1-\epsilon)^{3}\,(5-3\,\epsilon)}}\,\frac{1}{G_{\rm N}\,M}\ ,

(2.27)

which is approximately equal to
\omega_{\rm max}
in Eq. (

) for

(see Fig.

The inner matter core approaches the outer horizon radius
R_{+}
for
\omega\simeq\omega_{\rm H}
,
with
\omega_{\rm H}<\omega_{\rm max}
for
0<\epsilon\lesssim 0.5
and

\omega_{\rm H}>\omega_{\rm max}
for
0.5\lesssim\epsilon<1
.
In general, it would seem that one can consider small enough values of
\omega
so that the outer horizon is realised.
Moreover, for sufficiently small
\epsilon
, one can also describe cores that spin fast enough,
so as not to form a black hole, within the perturbative regime.
We will come back to this point in the following.

2.4
Angular momentum quantisation

In the above analysis, we considered the angular velocity
\omega
as a (free) perturbative parameter,
but Eq. (
2.22
) implies the necessary quantisation of the angular momentum.
In fact, both
N_{M}
and
N_{ML}
must be (positive) integers in order to correspond to allowed
states in the spectrum (
2.5
).
This implies that
N_{ML}=N_{M}+n
, with
n
a (non-negative) integer (much smaller than
N_{M}

for the perturbative result to hold), and we can write

\displaystyle\omega^{2}\simeq\omega_{n}^{2}=\frac{9\,\epsilon\,n}{(1-\epsilon)\,N_{M}^{2}\,\ell_{\rm p}^{2}}\ ,

(2.28)

corresponding to a quantised angular momentum

\displaystyle L_{n}=\frac{3}{\epsilon}\,\ell_{\rm p}\,m_{\rm p}\,\sqrt{N_{M}\,n}\ ,

(2.29)

or

\displaystyle a_{n}\equiv\frac{L_{n}}{M}=3\,\ell_{\rm p}\,\sqrt{\frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon}\,n}\ ,

(2.30)

which is plotted in the left panel of Fig.
2
for a first few values of
n
and the same values of
\epsilon
used
in Fig.
1
.
Note that the condition (
2.20
) of small angular velocity then simply yields
n\ll N_{M}/2\sim N_{M}
.

We can further notice that the quantisation of the mass and angular momentum lead to a quantisation
of the outer horizon radius (
2.25
) and area in Planck units, namely

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{+}=4\,\pi\,R_{+}^{2}\simeq{\mathcal{A}}_{M}\left[1-\frac{9}{2}\,(1-\epsilon)^{2}\,\frac{n}{N_{M}}\right]\ ,

(2.31)

where
{\mathcal{A}}_{M}
is the horizon area (
2.12
) for a Schwarzschild black hole of mass
M
.
We then see that increasing the mass (that is
N_{M}
) makes the horizon larger, whereas increasing
the angular momentum (that is
n
) makes it smaller, consistently with the classical theory.

3
Final remarks

We have considered the perturbation induced by rotation on the quantum spectrum of dust balls found
in Ref.
[
14
]
.
Quite expectedly, we have found that adding angular momentum increases the size of the ground state.
Moreover, no inner Cauchy horizon can appear within the perturbative regime, and the event horizon
area (
2.31
) is now quantised in terms of two quantum numbers, namely
\bar{n}=N_{M}
corresponding
to the total mass
M
and
n
for the quantised total angular momentum of the system.

Increasing
N_{M}
makes both core and horizon larger, whereas increasing
n
makes the core larger
but the event horizon smaller.
There is therefore a critical angular velocity
\omega_{n_{\rm c}}\simeq\omega_{\rm H}
, that is

\displaystyle\frac{n_{\rm c}}{N_{M}}\simeq\frac{1+3\,\epsilon}{3\,(1-\epsilon)\,(5-3\,\epsilon)}\ ,

(3.1)

above which the core remains larger than the (would-be outer) horizon and the system is not a black hole.
From the right panel of Fig.
2
, we see that this can occur within the perturbative regime if

0<\epsilon\lesssim 0.5
, consistently with the top left panel of Fig.
1
.
An example is provided by the bottom right panel of Fig.
1
, where
\epsilon=0.1

and the transition from a rotating black hole to a horizonless compact object occurs for
G_{\rm N}\,M\,\omega\simeq 1
.
Of course, beside the qualitative behaviour, the precise values obtained from such a simple model should
not be trusted to bear a phenomenological relevance.

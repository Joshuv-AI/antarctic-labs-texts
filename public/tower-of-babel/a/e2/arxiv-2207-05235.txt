Abstract
. We establish a geometrisation of the Breuil–Mézard conjecture for potentially Barsotti–Tate representations, as well as of the weight part of Serre’s conjecture, for moduli stacks of two-dimensional mod p p representations of the absolute Galois group of a p p -adic local field. These results are first proved for the stacks of [ CEGS20b , CEGS20c ] , and then transferred to the stacks of [ EG23 ] by means of a comparison of versal rings.



1.
Introduction

Let
K/{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
be a finite extension with residue field

k
,
let
\overline{K}
be an algebraic closure of
K
, and let
d\geq 1

be a positive integer. Two of us (M.E. and T.G.
[
EG23
]
) have
constructed moduli stacks of representations of the absolute Galois group

G_{K}:=\Gal(\overline{K}/K)
, globalizing Mazur’s classical deformation
theory of Galois representations. These stacks are expected to be the backbone of
a categorical
p
-adic Langlands correspondence, playing
the role anticipated by the stacks of
[
DHMK20
,
Zhu20
]
in the
\ell\neq p
setting.

To be precise, the book
[
EG23
]
defines the
category
\mathcal{X}_{d}
fibred in groupoids over
\Spf{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}
whose

A
-valued points, for any
p
-adically complete
{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}
-algebra
A
,
are the groupoid of rank
d
projective étale

(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules with
A
-coefficients. Then the finite type
points of
\mathcal{X}_{d}
correspond to representations
\overline{r}:G_{K}\to\GL_{d}(\overline{{\mathbf{F}}}_{p})
, the versal rings of
\mathcal{X}_{d}
at finite type points recover classical
Galois deformation rings, and one has the following, which is one of
the main results of
[
EG23
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
EG23
, Thm. 1.2.1]

Each
\mathcal{X}_{d}
is a Noetherian formal algebraic stack. Its underlying
reduced substack
\mathcal{X}_{d,\operatorname{red}}
is an algebraic stack of
finite type over
{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
, and is equidimensional of dimension
[K:{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}]\binom{d}{2}
. The irreducible components of
\mathcal{X}_{d,\operatorname{red}}

have a natural bijective labeling by Serre weights.

Recall that a
Serre weight
in this context is an irreducible

\overline{{\mathbf{F}}}_{p}
-representation of
\GL_{d}(k)
(or rather an
isomorphism class thereof). The description in
[
EG23
]
of
the labeling of components of

\mathcal{X}_{d,\operatorname{red}}
by Serre weights is to some extent combinatorial. Namely, it is shown that each irreducible component of

\mathcal{X}_{d,\operatorname{red}}
has a dense set of
\overline{{\mathbf{F}}}_{p}
-points
which are successive extensions of characters, with extensions as
non-split as possible. The restrictions of these characters to the
inertia group yield discrete data (their tame inertia weights) which,
together with some further information about peu and très ramifiee
extensions, amounts precisely to the data of (a highest weight of)
a Serre weight.

It is expected, however, that there is another description of the irreducible components
of
\mathcal{X}_{d,\operatorname{red}}
that is more precise and more informative from the perspective of the

p
-adic Langlands program. The weight part of Serre’s conjecture, as
described for instance in
[
GHS18
, Sec. 3]
,
associates to each
\overline{r}:G_{K}\to\GL_{d}(\overline{{\mathbf{F}}}_{p})
a set of Serre
weights
W(\overline{r})
. One expects for each Serre weight
\sigma
that
there is a set of components of
\mathcal{X}_{d,\operatorname{red}}
, including the
irreducible component labelled by
\sigma
in
[
EG23
]
,
the union of whose
\overline{{\mathbf{F}}}_{p}
-points are
precisely
the representations
\overline{r}
with
\sigma\in W(\overline{r})
.
Equivalently, after adding additional
labels to some of the components (so that components will be labelled
by a set of weights, rather than a single weight), the set

W(\overline{r})
is precisely the collection of labels of the various
components of
\mathcal{X}_{d,\operatorname{red}}
on which
\overline{r}
lies.

One of the aims of this paper is to establish this expectation
in the case
d=2
, taking as input the weight part of Serre’s conjecture for
\GL_{2}

[
GLS15
]
and the Breuil–Mézard conjecture for two-dimensional potentially Barsotti–Tate representations
[
GK14
]
, and thus obtain a description of

all
the finite type points of each irreducible component of

\mathcal{X}_{2,\operatorname{red}}
, as opposed to just a dense set
of points. Indeed we have the following theorem, which can be regarded
as a geometrisation of the weight part of Serre’s conjecture for

\GL_{2}
. If
\sigma
is a Serre weight, let

\mathcal{X}^{\sigma}_{d,\operatorname{red}}
denote the irreducible component of

\mathcal{X}_{d,\operatorname{red}}
labelled by
\sigma
in
[
EG23
]
. (We refer the reader to Section
2

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
p>2
. For each Serre weight
\sigma
we define a cycle
Z^{\sigma}
as follows:

—

Z^{\sigma}=\mathcal{X}_{2,\operatorname{red}}^{\sigma}
, if the weight
\sigma
is not Steinberg, while

—

Z^{\chi\otimes\mathrm{St}}=\mathcal{X}_{2,\operatorname{red}}^{\chi}+\mathcal{X}_{2,\operatorname{red}}^{\chi\otimes\mathrm{St}}
if the weight
\sigma\cong\chi\otimes\mathrm{St}
is Steinberg.

Then
\sigma\in W(\overline{r})
if and only if
\overline{r}
lies in the support of
Z^{\sigma}
.

Indeed a stronger statement is true: the cycles
Z^{\sigma}=\mathcal{X}_{2,\operatorname{red}}^{\sigma}

(
for
\sigma
non-Steinberg
)
and
Z^{\chi\otimes\mathrm{St}}=\mathcal{X}_{2,\operatorname{red}}^{\chi}+\mathcal{X}_{2,\operatorname{red}}^{\chi\otimes\mathrm{St}}
constitute the cycles in a geometric version of the Breuil–Mézard conjecture
(
to be explained below
)
.

We emphasise that the existence of such a geometric interpretation of
the sets
W(\overline{r})
is far from
obvious, and indeed we know of no direct proof using any of the
explicit descriptions of
W(\overline{r})
in the literature; it seems hard to understand in any explicit way which Galois
representations arise as the limits of a family of extensions of given
characters, and the description of the sets

is
very complicated (for example, the description

[

W(\overline{r})
given in
[
GK14
]
, which is in terms

\overline{r}
and is
motivated by the Taylor–Wiles method. We interpret this description
in the geometric language of
[
EG14
]
, which we
in turn interpret as the formal completion of a “geometric

Galois theory

Let

Our representations of

Inertial local Langlands

A two-dimensional

is (the isomorphism

\tau:I_{K}\to\GL_{2}(\overline{{\mathbf{Z}}}_{p})

Such a representation is of the form

In this case we say

\tau

Henniart’s appendix to

Since we are only working

Serre weights and tame types

By definition, a

is an irreducible

-representation of

\overline{\sigma}_{\vec{t},\vec{s}}:=\otimes^{f-1}_{j=0}(\det{\!}^{t_{j}}\Sym^{s_{j}}k^{2})\otimes_{k,\sigma_{j}}{\mathbf{F}},

If

). For any

\gamma_{i}-\delta_{J}(i)&\text{if }i-1\notin J,\end{cases}

In the principal series case we let

p
-adic Hodge theory

We normalise Hodge–Tate weights so that all Hodge–Tate weights of
the cyclotomic character are equal to
-1
. We say that a potentially
crystalline representation

restrictive definition of potentially Barsotti–Tate than is sometimes

consideration. Note also that it is more usual in the literature to
say that

is potentially Barsotti–Tate if it is potentially
crystalline, and

has Hodge type

We say
that a potentially crystalline representation

has
inertial type

\tau
if the traces of
elements of
I_{K}
acting on
\tau
and on

\operatorname{D_{pcris}}(r)=\varinjlim_{K^{\prime}/K}(\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{cris}}\otimes_{{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}}V_{r})^{G_{K^{\prime}}}

are
equal (here

is the underlying vector space
of
V_{r}
).
A representation
\overline{r}:G_{K}\to\GL_{2}(\overline{{\mathbf{F}}}_{p})

has a potentially
Barsotti–Tate lift of

\tau
if and
only if
\overline{r}
admits a lift to a representation

of Hodge type

and inertial type

.

Serre weights of mod
p
Galois representations

Tag

[

04XB

For a commutative ring

whose domain

Sta13
,
Tag 0AIL
]

and which is representable by algebraic spaces,

Let

denote the affine formal
scheme (or affine formal algebraic space, in the terminology
of
[
Sta13
]
) obtained by
\varpi
-adically completing

Moduli stacks of Breuil–Kisin modules and étale

The main object of study in this paper is the

Stacks of Breuil–Kisin modules

For each tame type
\tau

\varpi

whose
\Spf({\mathcal{O}}_{E^{\prime}})

2

\tau

[
CEGS20b
]
as well as to the summary in
[
CEGS20c
, Sec. 2.3]
for complete definitions, recalling here only what will be used in this paper.)

For each integer
a\geq 1
, we write
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd},a}
for the
fppf
stack over
{\mathcal{O}}/\varpi^{a}
which associates to any
{\mathcal{O}}/\varpi^{a}
-algebra
A

the groupoid
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd},a}(A)

of rank
2
Breuil–Kisin modules of height at most
1
with
A

K^{\prime}
to

{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd}}=\varinjlim_{a}{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd},a}
. The closed substack
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd},\operatorname{BT}}
of

is cut out by a Kottwitz-type determinant condition, which can be thought of (on the Galois side) as cutting out the tamely potentially Barsotti–Tate representations from among all tamely potentially crystalline representations with Hodge–Tate weights in
\{0,1\}

[

, Cor. 4.2.13]
the stack

then decomposes as a disjoint union of closed substacks

, one for each tame type
\tau

\tau
. Finally, for each

we write
{\mathcal{C}}^{\tau,\operatorname{BT},a}={\mathcal{C}}^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}}\times_{{\mathcal{O}}}{\mathcal{O}}/\varpi^{a}

[
CEGS20b
, Cor. 3.1.8, Cor. 4.5.3, Prop. 5.2.21]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The stacks

,
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd},\operatorname{BT},a}
, and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\tau,\operatorname{BT},a}

{\mathcal{O}}
, while the stacks

,
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd},\operatorname{BT}}
, and

are
\varpi
-adic formal algebraic stacks. Moreover:

(1)

{\mathcal{O}}
.

(2)

The stacks
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd},\operatorname{BT},a}
and

.

(3)

The special fibres
{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{dd},\operatorname{BT},1}
and
{\mathcal{C}}^{\tau,\operatorname{BT},1}
are reduced.

Theorem 3.2

The stacks

, while the stacks

and

(1)

(2)

4.
Generic reducedness of
\Spec R^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}}_{\overline{r}}/\varpi

Fix a Galois representation
\overline{r}:G_{K}\to\GL_{2}({\mathbf{F}}^{\prime})
, where
{\mathbf{F}}^{\prime}/{\mathbf{F}}

is a finite extension. Our goal in this section is to prove that the scheme
\Spec R^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}}_{\overline{r}}/\varpi
is generically reduced; this will be a key ingredient in the proof of our geometric
Breuil–Mézard result in Section

. Recall that a scheme is
generically reduced if it contains an open reduced subscheme whose
underlying topological space is dense; in the case of a Noetherian
affine scheme
\Spec A
, this is equivalent to requiring
that the localisation of
A
at each of its minimal
primes is reduced.

We may of course suppose that
R^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}}_{\overline{r}}\neq 0
, so that
\overline{r}
has a potentially Barsotti–Tate lift of type
\tau
, and so corresponds to a finite type point

for any

It follows from Proposition
3.4
that
\Spec R^{\tau,a}_{x}
is a closed subscheme of

\Spec R^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}}_{\overline{r}}/\varpi^{a}
, but we have no reason to
believe that equality holds.
It follows from Theorem
3.2
(2),
together with Lemma
4.5
below, that

\Spec R^{\tau,1}_{x}
is the underlying reduced subscheme
of
\Spec R^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}}_{\overline{r}}/\varpi
, so that equality holds in the case
a=1

if and only
if
\Spec R^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}}_{\overline{r}}/\varpi
is reduced; however, again, we have no
reason to believe that this holds in general.

Nevertheless it
is
the case that

\Spec R^{\tau,\operatorname{BT}}_{\overline{r}}/\varpi
is generically
reduced;
cf.
Theorem
4.6
below. We will deduce
Theorem
4.6

from the following global statement.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\tau
be a tame type. There is a dense open substack
{\mathcal{U}}
of
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\tau}

such that
{\mathcal{U}}_{/{\mathbf{F}}}
is reduced.

Proof.

The proposition will follow from an application of
Lemma
A.6
,
and the key to this application will be to find a

of
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\tau,1}
,
which we will do using our study of the irreducible components
of
{\mathcal{C}}^{\tau,\operatorname{BT},1}
and
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\tau,1}
.

Recall that, for each profile
J\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\tau}
,
we let
\overline{{\mathcal{Z}}}(J)
denote the scheme-theoretic image of

\overline{{\mathcal{C}}}(J)
under the proper morphism

{\mathcal{C}}^{\tau,\operatorname{BT},1}\to{\mathcal{Z}}^{\tau,1}

Each
\overline{{\mathcal{Z}}}(J)

is a closed substack of
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\tau,1}
, and so,
if we let

be the complement in
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\tau,1}
of the
union of the
\overline{{\mathcal{Z}}}(J^{\prime})
for all profiles

is

\overline{{\mathcal{Z}}}(J)
, by Theorem

(2) and Remark
3.7

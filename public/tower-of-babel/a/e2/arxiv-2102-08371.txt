Explicit Motivic Mixed Elliptic Chabauty-Kim
David Corwin

Abstract
. The main point of the paper is to take the explicit motivic Chabauty-Kim method developed in papers of Dan-Cohen–Wewers and Dan-Cohen and the author and make it work for non-rational curves. In particular, we calculate the abstract form of an element of the Chabauty-Kim ideal for ℤ ⁡ [ 1 / ℓ ] {\mathbb{Z}}[1/\ell] -points on a punctured elliptic curve, and lay some groundwork for certain kinds of higher genus curves. For this purpose, we develop an “explicit Tannakian Chabauty-Kim method” using ℚ ℓ {\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell} -Tannakian categories of Galois representations in place of ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} -linear motives. In future work, we intend to use this method to explicitly apply the Chabauty-Kim method to a curve of positive genus in a situation where Quadratic Chabauty does not apply.

8.
Tannakian Selmer Varieties

The goal of this section is to explicitly understand

via the Tannakian categories of §
7.3
. We refer back to §
3
for the relationship between

and

We have by Remark
4.10
that
H^{1}_{f,S}(G_{k};M_{a,b})=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\operatorname{\bf{Rep}}_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\rm f,S}(G_{k},E)}({\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(0),M_{a,b})
and more generally that

where
H^{1}(\operatorname{\bf{Rep}}_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\rm f,S}(G_{k},E);\Pi)
denotes the set of torsors under
\Pi
in the Tannakian category
\operatorname{\bf{Rep}}_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\rm f,S}(G_{k},E)
. In order to express this set as the group cohomology of a pro-algebraic group, we introduce fiber functors.

We recall there is a tensor functor
\operatorname{\bf{Rep}}_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\rm f,S}(G_{k},E)\to\operatorname{\bf{Rep}}_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\rm ss}(G_{k},E)
sending a representation
V
to its associated graded
\Gr^{W}_{\bullet}V
for the weight filtration.

Definition 8.1
.

We define

\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{ME}}}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)

to be the Tannakian fundamental group of the category

\operatorname{\bf{Rep}}_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\rm f,S}(G_{k},E),

defined in Remark
4.10
, with respect to the de Rham fiber functor
V\mapsto V^{{\rm{dR}}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{D}_{{\rm{dR}}}{V}
.

We define

\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{ME}}}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)^{\Gr}

to be the Tannakian fundamental group of the same category with respect to the graded de Rham fiber functor

V\mapsto V^{\Gr{\rm{dR}}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{D}_{{\rm{dR}}}{\Gr^{W}_{\bullet}V}.

The two fiber functors are isomorphic (in a manner compatible with associated graded) by
[
SR72
, IV.2.2.2]
and
[
Zie15
, Main Theorem 1.2]
. We describe the issue more in §
8.2
below.

Note that both fiber functors are canonically isomorphic when restricted to the subcategory
\operatorname{\bf{Rep}}_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\rm ss}(G_{k},E)\subseteq\operatorname{\bf{Rep}}_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\rm f,S}(G_{k},E)
. We denote its Tannakian Galois group by
\pi_{1}(\operatorname{\bf{Rep}}_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\rm ss}(G_{k},E))
, and it is isomorphic to
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
.

For a unipotent group
\Pi
in the Tannakian category
\operatorname{\bf{Rep}}_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\rm f,S}(G_{k},E)
, we denote by
\Pi^{{\rm{dR}}}
the unipotent group over
{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
with
\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{ME}}}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)
-action associated to the Lie algebra
(\Lie{\Pi})^{{\rm{dR}}}
, with its induced Lie algebra structure.

We have

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\operatorname{\bf{Rep}}_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\rm f,S}(G_{k},E)}({\mathbb{Q}_{p}}(0),M_{a,b})=H^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{ME}}}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E);{M_{a,b}}^{{\rm{dR}}})=H^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{ME}}}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)^{\Gr};{M_{a,b}}^{\Gr{\rm{dR}}}),

and

H^{1}(\operatorname{\bf{Rep}}_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\rm f,S}(G_{k},E);\Pi)=H^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{ME}}}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E);{\Pi}^{{\rm{dR}}})=H^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{ME}}}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)^{\Gr};{\Pi}^{\Gr{\rm{dR}}}).

8.1.
Structure of the Graded Tannakian Galois Group

The inclusion

\operatorname{\bf{Rep}}_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\rm ss}(G_{k},E)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{\bf{Rep}}_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\rm f,S}(G_{k},E)

induces a map

that is surjective, with kernel the unipotent radical

. We denote the Lie algebra of

by

, its universal envelopping algebra by

The functor

inducing actions of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
on

,

, and their associated algebras.

The theory of such extensions is described in §
A.2

. In particular, by Theorem

, we have the following:

, with

8.2.
Graded vs. Ungraded

We similarly have a projection

\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{ME}}}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)\to\pi_{1}(\operatorname{\bf{Rep}}_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\rm ss}(G_{k},E))\cong\operatorname{GL}_{2},

with kernel denoted

, but it is not canonically split. We denote the associated objects by
\mathfrak{n}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)
,
{\mathcal{U}}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)
, and
A({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)
, respectively.

The scheme

\operatorname{\underline{Isom}}^{\otimes,\Gr W}({\rm{dR}},\Gr{\rm{dR}})

of isomorphisms from the de Rham fiber functor to the graded de Rham fiber functor inducing the identity on associated graded is a
U({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)
-
U({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)^{\Gr}
bitorsor in the fpqc topology over
{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
by
[
Zie15
, Main Theorem 1.2]
. As already mentioned, it is trivial by
[
SR72
, IV.2.2.2]
. In particular, the fundamental exact sequence

splits, although not canonically. In fact, we have

Proposition 8.3
.

The set

\operatorname{\underline{Isom}}^{\otimes,\Gr W}({\rm{dR}},\Gr{\rm{dR}})

is naturally in bijection with the set

\operatorname{Sec}(\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{ME}}}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E))

of sections of the fundamental exact sequence. The
U({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)
-torsor structure on
\operatorname{\underline{Isom}}^{\otimes,\Gr W}({\rm{dR}},\Gr{\rm{dR}})
corresponds to the action of
U({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)
on
\operatorname{Sec}(\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{ME}}}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E))
by conjugation.

Proof.

Any
\alpha\in\operatorname{\underline{Isom}}^{\otimes,\Gr W}({\rm{dR}},\Gr{\rm{dR}})
induces an isomorphism
\beta\colon\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{ME}}}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)^{\Gr}\to\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{ME}}}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)
over
\pi_{1}(\operatorname{\bf{Rep}}_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\rm ss}(G_{k},E))
defined by
\beta(g)=\alpha^{-1}g\alpha
. The composition

\beta\circ s\colon\pi_{1}(\operatorname{\bf{Rep}}_{{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}^{\rm ss}(G_{k},E))\to\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{ME}}}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E),

with
s
as in (
10
), is an element of
\operatorname{Sec}(\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{ME}}}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E))
. In other words, we have a map:

\operatorname{\underline{Isom}}^{\otimes,\Gr W}({\rm{dR}},\Gr{\rm{dR}})\to\operatorname{Sec}(\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{ME}}}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)).

If we replace
\alpha
by
\alpha\circ u^{-1}
for
u\in U({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)
, we replace
\beta(g)
by
u\beta(g)u^{-1}
. In particular, this map intertwines the (left) action of
U({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)
on the bitorsor with its (left) conjugation action on
\operatorname{Sec}(\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{\mathbf{ME}}}({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E))

It therefore suffices to show that the latter is a torsor under
U({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)
. This is true because
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
has trivial higher cohomology and because
U({\mathcal{O}}_{k,S},E)^{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}=0
(for any choice of splitting).
∎

\displaystyle\phi_{\lambda e_{i}}^{w\pi_{j}}=\phi_{e_{i}\lambda}^{\pi_{j}w}=\delta_{ij}w_{1}\phi_{\lambda}^{w}.

Proof.

Suppose

Z^{1}(G;\Pi)\cong\ker(\operatorname{Hom}(G,\Pi\rtimes G)\to\operatorname{Hom}(G,G)).

given by

c\mapsto\rho_{c},

where
\rho_{c}(g)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}c_{g}g\in\Pi\rtimes G
. The action of
\Pi
is given by conjugation on
\Pi\rtimes G
.

Proof.

Since

\rho_{c}(g_{1})\rho_{c}(g_{2})=(c_{g_{1}}g_{1})(c_{g_{2}}g_{2})=c_{g_{1}}g_{1}(c_{g_{2}})g_{1}g_{2}=c_{g_{1}}g_{1}(c_{g_{2}})g_{1}g_{2},

while

\rho_{c}(g_{1}g_{2})=c_{g_{1}g_{2}}g_{1}g_{2},

the homomorphism condition for
c
is equivalent to the cocycle condition for
\rho
.

To check the action of
\Pi
, note that for
\pi\in\Pi
and
g\in G
, we have

\displaystyle\pi\rho_{c}(g)\pi^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi(c_{g}g)\pi^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi c_{g}g\pi^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi c_{g}g(\pi^{-1})g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi(c)_{g}g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{\pi(c)}(g),

as desired.∎

Recall that
{\mathbb{G}}
acts on
Z^{1}(U;\Pi)

by

g(c)_{u}=g(c_{g^{-1}(u)}),

and the
\Pi

{\mathbb{G}}

are compatible in that

g(\pi(c))=g(\pi)(g(c))),

so that the
{\mathbb{G}}

The main result of this section is the following generalization of
[
DCW16
, Proposition 5.2]
:

Theorem A.4
.

Suppose that
\Pi^{{\mathbb{G}}}=1
. Then each equivalence class of cocycles
[c]
in
H^{1}(G;\Pi)
contains a unique representative
c_{0}
such that

c_{0}(g)=1\mbox{ for each }g\in{\mathbb{G}},

and its restriction to
U
is a
{\mathbb{G}}
-equivariant cocycle

{\left.\kern-1.2ptc_{0}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{U}}\colon U\to\Pi.

The map

[c]\mapsto{\left.\kern-1.2ptc_{0}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{U}}

defines a bijection

H^{1}(G,\Pi)\cong Z^{1}(U;\Pi)^{{\mathbb{G}}}.

We prove this result via a series of lemmas.

Lemma A.5
.

The restriction map

Z^{1}(G;\Pi)\to Z^{1}(U;\Pi)

induces a bijection

\ker(Z^{1}(G;\Pi)\to Z^{1}({\mathbb{G}};\Pi))\cong Z^{1}(U;\Pi)^{{\mathbb{G}}}

Proof.

Suppose
c\in\ker(Z^{1}(G;\Pi)\to Z^{1}({\mathbb{G}};\Pi))
. Then for
u\in U
, we have

\displaystyle g(c)_{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(c_{g^{-1}(u)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(c_{g^{-1}ug})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(c_{g^{-1}u}g^{-1}(u(c_{g})))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(c_{g^{-1}u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(c_{g^{-1}}g^{-1}(c_{u}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(g^{-1}(c_{u}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{u}

Conversely, suppose
c\in Z^{1}(U;\Pi)^{{\mathbb{G}}}
. We extend
c
to an element of
Z^{1}(G;\Pi)
as follows. For
ug\in G
, we set

c_{ug}=c_{u}.

It is clear by construction that it is trivial when restricted to
{\mathbb{G}}
. To show that it is a cocycle, suppose we have
g_{1},g_{2}\in{\mathbb{G}}
and
u_{1},u_{2}\in U
. Then

\displaystyle c_{u_{1}g_{1}u_{2}g_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{u_{1}g_{1}u_{2}g_{1}^{-1}g_{1}g_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{u_{1}g_{1}(u_{2})g_{1}g_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{u_{1}g_{1}(u_{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{u_{1}}u_{1}(c_{g_{1}(u_{2})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{u_{1}}u_{1}(g_{1}(c_{u_{2}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{u_{1}g_{2}}u_{1}(g_{1}(c_{u_{2}g_{2}})),

as desired.

Given
c\in Z^{1}(U;\Pi)^{{\mathbb{G}}}
, it is clear that the maps are mutual inverses. Given
c\in\ker(Z^{1}(G;\Pi)\to Z^{1}({\mathbb{G}};\Pi))
, note that

c_{ug}=c_{u},

so the maps are indeed mutual inverses.

∎

Lemma A.6
.

The natural map

H^{1}(G;\Pi)\to H^{1}(U;\Pi)

induces an isomorphism

H^{1}(G;\Pi)\cong H^{1}(U;\Pi)^{{\mathbb{G}}}

Proof.

As
{\mathbb{G}}
is reductive, all higher group cohomology of
{\mathbb{G}}
vanishes, so the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence for the inclusion
U\hookrightarrow G
degenerates. In particular, we get

H^{1}(G;\Pi)=H^{0}({\mathbb{G}};H^{1}(U;\Pi))=H^{1}(U;\Pi)^{{\mathbb{G}}}.

∎

By Lemma
A.5
and Lemma
A.6
, we have a diagram

\textstyle{\ker(Z^{1}(G;\Pi)\to Z^{1}({\mathbb{G}};\Pi))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{H^{1}(G;\Pi)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{Z^{1}(U;\Pi)^{{\mathbb{G}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H^{1}(U;\Pi)^{{\mathbb{G}}}}

It now suffices to prove that either horizontal arrow of the diagram is an isomorphism. We prove this for the top horizontal arrow whenever
\Pi^{{\mathbb{G}}}=0
.

Lemma A.7
.

Suppose
\Pi^{{\mathbb{G}}}=0
. Then the set of splittings of
\Pi\rtimes{\mathbb{G}}\to{\mathbb{G}}
forms a pointed torsor under
\Pi
acting by conjugation.

Proof.

To check transitivity, note that the set of splittings modulo
\Pi
is the cohomology set
H^{1}({\mathbb{G}};\Pi)
. But this vanishes because
{\mathbb{G}}
is reductive. The action is simply transitive because the stabilizer of a section is
\Pi^{{\mathbb{G}}}
, which is trivial by assumption. The point of the torsor is the trivial splitting
{\mathbb{G}}\hookrightarrow\Pi\rtimes{\mathbb{G}}
.
∎

Lemma A.8
.

The set

\ker(Z^{1}(G;\Pi)\to Z^{1}({\mathbb{G}};\Pi))\subseteq Z^{1}(G;\Pi)

maps bijectively onto
H^{1}(G;\Pi)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.3
, under the identification

Z^{1}(G;\Pi)=\ker(\operatorname{Hom}(G,\Pi\rtimes G)\to\operatorname{Hom}(G,G)),

the action of
\Pi
is by conjugation. Via the inclusion
{\mathbb{G}}\subseteq G
, we may consider
\Pi\rtimes{\mathbb{G}}
as a subgroup of
\Pi\rtimes G
(it is not normal, but this does not matter). For any
\rho\in\ker(\operatorname{Hom}(G,\Pi\rtimes G)\to\operatorname{Hom}(G,G))
,
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\rho_{c}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{{\mathbb{G}}}}
is a splitting of
\Pi\rtimes{\mathbb{G}}\to{\mathbb{G}}
.

For
\pi\in\Pi
, we have
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\rho_{\pi(c)}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{{\mathbb{G}}}}=\pi{\left.\kern-1.2pt\rho_{c}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{{\mathbb{G}}}}\pi^{-1}
. Therefore, by Lemma
A.7
, there is a unique
\pi\in\Pi
for which
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\rho_{\pi(c)}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{{\mathbb{G}}}}
is the trivial splitting.

Now
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\rho_{\pi(c)}\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{{\mathbb{G}}}}
is the trivial splitting iff
{\left.\kern-1.2ptc\vphantom{\big|}\right|_{{\mathbb{G}}}}
is trivial, i.e. if
c\in\ker(Z^{1}(G;\Pi)\to Z^{1}({\mathbb{G}};\Pi))
. Therefore, each class
[c]\in H^{1}(G;U)
contains a unique representative in
\ker(Z^{1}(G;\Pi)\to Z^{1}({\mathbb{G}};\Pi))
.
∎

Remark A.9
.

One may alternatively show that the bottom horizontal arrow is an isomorphism, as follows. If
\Pi
is abelian, this amounts to considering the short exact sequence

0\to\Pi/\Pi^{U}\to Z^{1}(U,\Pi)\to H^{1}(U,\Pi)\to 0

and then taking
{\mathbb{G}}
-fixed points and using the fact that
{\mathbb{G}}
is reductive and
\Pi^{{\mathbb{G}}}=0
to get

Z^{1}(U,\Pi)^{{\mathbb{G}}}\cong H^{1}(U,\Pi)^{{\mathbb{G}}}.

One may replicate this argument for general
\Pi
by showing that if
\Pi
is a nonabelian group acting on a pointed set (or variety)
Y
, and both have a compatible action of
{\mathbb{G}}
, then there is a long exact sequence of pointed sets

\Pi^{{\mathbb{G}}}\to Y^{{\mathbb{G}}}\to(Y/\Pi)^{{\mathbb{G}}}\to H^{1}({\mathbb{G}};\Pi).

Posets of finite GK-dimensional graded pre-Nichols algebras of diagonal type Thanks: The work of both authors was partially supported by CONICET and Secyt (UNC)
Iván Angiono, Emiliano Campagnolo

Abstract
. We classify graded pre-Nichols algebras of diagonal type with finite Gelfand-Kirillov dimension. The characterization is made through an isomorphism of posets with the family of appropriate subsets of the set of positive roots coming from central extensions of Nichols algebras of diagonal type, generalizing the corresponding extensions for small quantum groups in de Concini-Kac-Procesi forms of quantum groups. On the way to achieving this result, we also classify graded quotients of algebras of functions of unipotent algebraic groups attached to semisimple Lie algebras.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.
The so called quantized enveloping algebras
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, where
q
is a parameter and
\mathfrak{g}
is a semisimple Lie algebra, emerged after the works of Drinfeld and Jimbo as examples of non-commutative and non-cococommutative Hopf algebras over the field
\Bbbk(q)
: They were obtained by
deforming
the structure of the corresponding enveloping algebra over
\Bbbk(q)
. When we consider the evaluation of the parameter
q
in elements of
\Bbbk
, we get a Hopf algebra over
\Bbbk
, which behaves as
U(\mathfrak{g})
in terms of representations if
q
is not a root of unity.
In the nineties, de Concini, Kac and Procesi
[
DKP
,
DP
]
studied the case in which
q
is a root of unity of order
N
, under some mild conditions on
N
, and found a completely different story. To begin with, the centre of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is larger than in the case where
q
is not a root of unity, and it contains a Hopf subalgebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
which gives rise to an extension of Hopf algebras

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{q}\hookrightarrow U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g}).

Here
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is the Frobenius-Lusztig kernel, a finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebra. The name comes from the evaluation of a different form of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
studied by Lusztig
[
L2
,
L1
]
in connection with the representation theory of algebraic groups in positive characteristic. He took an integral form generated by
divided powers
of the generators and the algebra
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
obtained after evaluation in
q
fits into an extension of Hopf algebras

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\twoheadrightarrow U(\mathfrak{g}).

Coming back to (
1.1
),
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is
\mathbb{Z}^{\theta}
-graded, where
\theta
is the rank of
\mathfrak{g}
, and has a triangular decomposition
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\simeq U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes U_{q}^{0}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes U_{q}^{-}(\mathfrak{g})
, where
U_{q}^{0}(\mathfrak{g})
is the group algebra of a free abelian group in generators
K_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq\theta
, and
U_{q}^{\pm}(\mathfrak{g})
has a PBW basis made of PBW generators
E_{\alpha}
, respectively
F_{\alpha}
, of degree
\pm\alpha\in\varDelta_{+}
, where
\varDelta
is the set of roots of
\mathfrak{g}
viewed as a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{\theta}
. In addition,
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
is the subalgebra generated by
E_{\alpha}^{N}
,
F_{\alpha}^{N}
,
\alpha\in\varDelta_{+}
, and
K_{i}^{N}
,
1\leq i\leq\theta
, while
\mathfrak{u}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
has a
restricted
PBW basis with the same set of generators, but we restrict the powers up to
N
. Also, the restriction of (
1.1
) to the corresponding positive parts gives an extension

\mathcal{Z}_{q}^{+}\hookrightarrow U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{g})\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{u}_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{g})
of Hopf algebras, but in the braided tensor category
{}^{\Bbbk\mathbb{Z}^{\theta}}_{\Bbbk\mathbb{Z}^{\theta}}\mathcal{YD}
of Yetter-Drinfeld modules over
\mathbb{Z}^{\theta}
.

A few years later, Andruskiewitsch and Schneider
[
AS1
]
introduced the so called Lifting Method to classify finite-dimensional pointed Hopf algebras.
In a nutshell, this method is based on the decomposition of the associated coradically graded Hopf algebra into the bosonization between a group algebra
\Bbbk\Gamma
and a coradically graded Hopf algebra
R
in the category
{}^{\Bbbk\Gamma}_{\Bbbk\Gamma}\mathcal{YD}
by solving the following steps: first, to classify all finite dimensional Nichols algebras (see §
2.1
and references therein for the definition and examples); then classify the possible finite dimensional Hopf algebras
R
extending the Nichols algebras as before, called
post-Nichols algebras
; and finally to obtain all the liftings of the corresponding bosonizations. This method was widely applied, with the first main result
[
AS4
]
being the classification in the case of abelian groups with moderate restriction on the order, where the examples are certain deformations of the bosonizations of
\mathfrak{u}_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{g})
with appropriate abelian groups. The general answer for abelian groups involves the classification of finite-dimensional Nichols algebras
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}
of diagonal type (depending on a braiding matrix
\mathfrak{q}\in(\Bbbk^{\times})^{\theta\times\theta}
), which in turn is contained in the classification of those with finite root system (i.e., a finite number of PBW generators) given by Heckenberger
[
H2
]
. To do so, we attach a (kind of) Dynkin diagram with labels depending on
\mathfrak{q}
and consider the connected components of this diagrams: the root system is, as expected, the disjoint union of the root systems of the connected components, so the list in
[
H2
]
contains just those matrices
\mathfrak{q}
with connected Dynkin diagram. In
[
AA
]
this list was split into the following families:

•

Cartan type

•

Super type

•

Standard type

•

Modular type

•

Supermodular type

•

UFO.

Here, Cartan type is essentially the case of quantized enveloping algebras, while super type is related with quantized enveloping Lie superalgebras.

Andruskiewitsch and Schneider also started the classification of pointed Hopf algebras with finite Gelfand-Kirillov dimension (i.e. infinite-dimensional ones with some kind of moderate growth) in
[
AS3
]
by classifying those which are domains and satisfy a technical condition. To do so, they extend the Lifting Method to this context and obtained that all possible Nichols algebras are close to
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{g})
for
q
not a root of unity, and the unique possible Hopf algebras
R
extending the Nichols algeras are just the Nichols algebras themselves.

If we want to get all Hopf algebras including those which are not domains, the answer is fully different: indeed, Lusztig examples provide post Nichols algebras
\mathcal{U}_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{g})
properly extending the Nichols algebras
\mathfrak{u}_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{g})
. Taking graded duals, the extension between the positive parts in (
1.2
) becomes that of (
1.1
), where
U_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{g})
is a pre-Nichols algebra (a graded intermediate quotient between the tensor algebra and the Nichols algebra
\mathfrak{u}_{q}^{+}(\mathfrak{g})
) of finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
, and classifying post-Nichols algebras with finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
is related to classifying pre-Nichols algebras with finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
. In general, finite-dimensional Nichols algebras
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}
of diagonal type fit into an exact sequence of braided Hopf algebras
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathfrak{q}}^{+}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}
, generalizing the one between positive parts in (
1.1
), where
\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
is the distinguished pre-Nichols algebra
[
An3
]
: it has a PBW basis with the same set of generators as
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}
, but where we allow the powers of some of them to be arbitrary as in (
1.1
). Thus we may identify first those Nichols algebras of diagonal type with finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
and then obtain all possible pre-Nichols algebras of finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
covering each one of these Nichols algebras. For the first question the answer was given in
[
AnG
]
, following the conjecture made in
[
AAH
]
: a Nichols algebra of diagonal type has finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
if and only if its root system is finite, i.e. it appears in the lists in
[
H2
]
. Thus we have to classify all pre-Nichols algebras
\mathscr{B}
with finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
covering the Nichols algebras
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}
with finite root system.

Fixing a braiding matrix
\mathfrak{q}
, the corresponding set of pre-Nichols algebras form a poset whose maximal element is
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}
and we may wonder if there exists a minimal element between those of finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
, called the
eminent
pre-Nichols algebra
\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
in
[
ASa
]
. This is the case for all
\mathfrak{q}
with connected diagrams up to Cartan types
A_{\theta}
,
D_{\theta}
with label
q=-1
, thanks to
[
ASa
,
ACSa
,
ACSa2
,
C
]
: in most cases eminent and distinguished pre-Nichols algebras coincide, i.e.
\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}=\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
. In any case,
\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
fits into an exact sequence of braided Hopf algebras

\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{q}}\hookrightarrow\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}
, where
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
is an algebra of
q
-polynomials whose variables are homogeneous: we collect the
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-degrees of these variables in a set denoted by
\underline{\widehat{\mathfrak{O}}^{\mathfrak{q}}_{+}}
, which is the set of positive roots of a classical root system when
\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}=\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
by
[
AAR
]
. As we will explain later, we are interested in subsets
B\subseteq\underline{\widehat{\mathfrak{O}}^{\mathfrak{q}}_{+}}
closed by sums: it means if
\alpha,\beta\in B
are such that
\alpha+\beta\in\underline{\widehat{\mathfrak{O}}^{\mathfrak{q}}_{+}}
, then
\alpha+\beta\in B
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathfrak{q})
be the set of all subsets of
\underline{\widehat{\mathfrak{O}}^{\mathfrak{q}}_{+}}
closed by sums, which is a subposet of the poset of subsets of
\underline{\widehat{\mathfrak{O}}^{\mathfrak{q}}_{+}}
.

Due to the results stated above, the determination of the poset of pre-Nichols algebras with finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
is equivalent to the characterization of all intermediate quotients
\mathscr{B}
between
\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
and
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}
. The main result of this paper deals with this question:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a braiding matrix whose connected components are not of Cartan types
A_{\theta}
,
D_{\theta}
with label
q=-1
neither one dimensional with label
q=\pm 1
.
For each
\underline{\beta}\in\underline{\widehat{\mathfrak{O}}^{\mathfrak{q}}_{+}}
let
z_{\beta}
be a generator of
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
of degree
\underline{\beta}
, and for each
B\in\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathfrak{q})
let

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}(\mathfrak{q},B)

\displaystyle:=\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}/\langle z_{\underline{\beta}}|\beta\in\underline{\widehat{\mathfrak{O}}^{\mathfrak{q}}_{+}}-B\rangle.

Then each
\mathscr{B}(\mathfrak{q},B)
is an
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded pre-Nichols algebra such that

\operatorname{GKdim}\mathscr{B}(\mathfrak{q},B)=|B|
. The assignment
B\mapsto\mathscr{B}(\mathfrak{q},B)
gives an anti-isomorphism of posets between

•

the set
\mathcal{P}_{c}(\mathfrak{q})
of all subsets of
\underline{\widehat{\mathfrak{O}}^{\mathfrak{q}}_{+}}
closed by sums, and

•

the set of all
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded pre-Nichols algebras of
\mathfrak{q}
with finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
.

In addition, we prove that the poset in the non-connected case decomposes as the product of the corresponding posets of the connected components and give a closed formula for the Hilbert series of each pre-Nichols algebra
\mathscr{B}(\mathfrak{q},B)
as well as a PBW basis.

Going back through the steps of the Lifting Method, we classify by taking graded duals all post-Nichols algebras of diagonal type and finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
(up to few exceptions on the connected components), which in turn give all coradically graded pointed Hopf algebras with abelian coradical and finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
after bosonization with suitable abelian groups.

We can observe that any
\mathscr{B}(\mathfrak{q},B)
fits into an exact sequence of the Nichols algebra
\mathfrak{q}
by a
q
-central Hopf subalgebra, so we may ask:

Question 1.2
.

Are there examples of pre-Nichols algebras of finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
which are not a “central” extension of braided Hopf algebras of the corresponding Nichols algebra?

Although the restriction to the
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded case may seem very strong, it has both a
realisation-independence
reason and also a reduction to a problem with a closed answer: the general case depends strongly on the realisation of the braided vector space of diagonal type as Yetter-Drinfeld module, and a general answer may be somewhat unmanageable, see Remark
2.6
.

The organization of the paper is the following. First we recall several notions about Nichols algebras, distinguished and eminent pre-Nichols algebras when the braiding is of diagonal type; we also summarize known results about eminent pre-Nichols algebras and solve some questions on quotients of pre-Nichols algebras, extensions of Nichols algebras by central subalgebras and twist equivalence of braidings of diagonal type in §
2
. Motivated by the results in this section we consider quotients of the algebras of functions of unipotent algebraic groups which are the positive parts of semisimple ones; hence, in §
3
we give the classification of these quotients in terms of subsets closed by sums of the set of positive roots of the associated semisimple Lie algebra. Finally we attack the determination of the poset of
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded pre-Nichols algebras with finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
of a matrix
\mathfrak{q}
such that
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}
has a finite root system (or equivalently, such that
\operatorname{GKdim}\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}<\infty
). Due to the results in §
2
, we can relate these quotients to those of the skew central Hopf subalgebra
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
(the subalgebra of coinvariants of the projection
\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}
), and also we can move to the case in which
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
is central. We attack first the connected case: we apply results in §
3
to solve all the cases where
\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}=\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
, and compute explicitly the poset for the few exceptions where
\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}\neq\widetilde{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
. Then we deal with the non-connected case using tools from
[
ASa
]
and the answer for the connected case.

Notation

We fix
\theta\in\mathbb{N}
and set
\mathbb{I}=\mathbb{I}_{\theta}:=\{1,2,...,\theta\}
.
Let
(\alpha_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{I}}
be the canonical basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{\theta}
and
\alpha_{ij}
denote
\alpha_{i}+\cdots+\alpha_{j}
,
i\leq j
.
Let
\beta=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}a_{i}\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\theta}
, sometimes also denoted
\beta=1^{a_{1}}\cdots\theta^{a_{\theta}}
to shorten expressions.
The
support
and the
height
of

are given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}\beta

\displaystyle=\{i\in\mathbb{I}|a_{i}\neq 0\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{ht}(\beta)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}.

If
\gamma=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{I}}b_{i}\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\theta}
is such that
a_{i}\leq b_{i}
for all
i\in\mathbb{I}
, then we say that
\beta\leq\gamma
.

If
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
v\in\Bbbk^{\times}
, then
(N)_{v}:=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}v^{j}
.
We denote by
\mathbb{G}_{N}
to the group of roots of unity of order
N
in
\Bbbk
, and
\mathbb{G}_{N}^{\prime}
the subset of primitive roots of order
N
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra (with unit). We denote by
\operatorname{GKdim}A
the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of
A
. We refer to
[
KL
]
for the definition and properties.

We will deal with
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded objects
U=\oplus_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}}U_{\alpha}
.
The Hilbert series of
U
is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{U}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}}\dim U_{\alpha}\,t^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}[[t_{1},\dots,t_{\theta}]]

\displaystyle\text{where }t^{\alpha}=t_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots t_{\theta}^{a_{\theta}}\text{ for }\alpha=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{\theta}).

Given
{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}}a_{\alpha}\,t^{\alpha},{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}}b_{\alpha}\,t^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}[[t_{1},\dots,t_{\theta}]]
, we say that
{\mathcal{H}}\geq{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}

for all

. Thus, if
U^{\prime}\subseteq U

\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded objects, then
{\mathcal{H}}_{U}\geq{\mathcal{H}}_{U^{\prime}}
.

Let
C
be a coalgebra. We will use Sweedler notation for
C
and any (left) comodule

; explicitly,
\Delta(c)=c_{(1)}\otimes c_{(2)}\in C\otimes C
for all

, and if
\rho:V\to C\otimes V
is the coaction, then
\rho(v)=v_{(-1)}\otimes v_{(0)}
for all
v\in V
.

2.
On pre-Nichols algebras of diagonal type

We start by recalling notions and results related with Nichols and pre-Nichols algebras, with special focus on the diagonal case.
Let
H
be a Hopf algebra. As usual, we denote by
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
the category of (left) Yetter-Drinfeld modules over
H
. We refer to
[
A2
,
R
]

for unexplained notions and notations on Yetter-Drinfeld modules and braided vector spaces, and to
[
AA
]
for more information on Nichols algebras of diagonal type, and to
[
KL
]
for definitions and basic results on Gelfand-Kirillov dimension.

2.1.
Nichols algebras and pre-Nichols algebras

Recall that
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
is a braided tensor category:
for each pair
V,W\in{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
, the braiding is given by

\displaystyle c_{V,W}

\displaystyle:V\otimes W\to W\otimes V,

\displaystyle c_{V,W}(x\otimes y)

\displaystyle=x_{(-1)}\cdot y\otimes x_{(0)},

\displaystyle x\in V,y\in W.

Therefore each pair
(V,c_{V,V})
,
V\in{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
, is a braided vector space.

The tensor algebra
T(V)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}V^{\otimes n}
becomes a graded Hopf algebra in
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
by declaring that every element in
V
is primitive.
The Nichols algebra
\mathscr{B}(V)
of
V
is the quotient of
T(V)
by the maximal Hopf ideal
\mathcal{J}(V)=\oplus_{n\geq 2}\mathcal{J}^{n}(V)
generated by homogeneous elements of degree
\geq 2
. Hence,
\mathscr{B}(V)
is an
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded Hopf algebra over
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
, where the degree one part is
V
, coincides with the set of primitive elements and generates
\mathscr{B}(V)
.

It is known that the structure of the Nichols algebra
\mathscr{B}(V)
depends on the braiding
c:=c_{V,V}\in\operatorname{GL}(V^{\otimes 2})
, not really on the realisation as a Yetter-Drinfeld module. This is why we consider braided vector spaces throughout this paper: i.e. pairs
(V,c)
, where
V
is a vector space and
c\in\operatorname{GL}(V^{\otimes 2})
is a solution of the braid equation.

Prominent examples are braided vector spaces of
diagonal type
. It means that there exist a basis
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{I}}
and a matrix
\mathfrak{q}=(q_{ij})\in(\Bbbk^{\times})^{\mathbb{I}\times\mathbb{I}}
such that the braiding is

\displaystyle c^{\mathfrak{q}}:

\displaystyle V\otimes V\to V\otimes V,

\displaystyle c^{\mathfrak{q}}(x_{i}\otimes x_{j})

\displaystyle=q_{ij}\,x_{j}\otimes x_{i},\,i,j\in\mathbb{I}.

The matrix
\mathfrak{q}
is called the braiding matrix. The information of
\mathfrak{q}
is encoded in the associated Dynkin diagram. This is a labelled graph with
\theta
vertices, each of them labelled with
q_{ii}
, and a edge between vertices
i
and
j
if
\widetilde{q}_{ij}:=q_{ij}q_{ji}\neq 1
, labelled with this scalar. Different braiding matrices can have the same Dynkin diagram: the associated Nichols algebras are not isomorphic but equivalent in some sense as we will see in §
2.4
.

Nichols algebras of diagonal type depend only on
\mathfrak{q}
, so we will denote it by
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}
; we denote accordingly
\mathcal{J}_{\mathfrak{q}}
to the defining ideal of
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}
. In addition,
(V,c)
is realized as a Yetter-Drinfeld module over
\Bbbk\mathbb{Z}^{\theta}
in a canonical way: We set the coaction on
V
given by
\rho(x_{i})=\alpha_{i}\otimes x_{i}
and the action given by
\alpha_{i}\cdot x_{j}=q_{ij}\,x_{j}
,
i,j\in\mathbb{I}
. From here we can deduce that
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}
is
\mathbb{Z}^{\theta}
-graded, where each
x_{i}
has degree
\alpha_{i}
.

A pre-Nichols algebra of
V
is a braided Hopf algebra
\mathscr{B}
which is the quotient of braided Hopf algebras of
T(V)
by an
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-homogeneous Hopf ideal
\mathcal{J}=\oplus_{n\geq 2}\mathcal{J}^{n}
. Thus
\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathcal{J}(V)
and there exist canonical graded Hopf algebra epimorphisms

T(V)\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{B}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{B}(V)

whose restriction to degree one is
\operatorname{id}_{V}
.
The set of pre-Nichols algebras of
V
becomes a poset
\mathfrak{Pre}(V)
, where
\mathscr{B}_{1}\leq\mathscr{B}_{2}
if
\operatorname{id}_{V}
induces an epimorphism of braided Hopf algebras
\mathscr{B}_{1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{B}_{2}
. This poset has maximum and minimum elements, i.e.
\mathscr{B}(V)
and
T(V)
.

Assume that
\operatorname{GKdim}\mathscr{B}(V)<\infty
. The subset
\mathfrak{Pre}_{\operatorname{fGK}}(V)
of pre-Nichols algebras of
V
with finite Gelfand-Kirillov dimension is a subposet with maximum element
\mathscr{B}(V)
.
In case it admits a minimum
\widehat{\mathscr{B}}(V)
, we will say that
\widehat{\mathscr{B}}(V)
is the
eminent pre-Nichols algebra
of
V
.
The existence and computation of eminent pre-Nichols algebras
\widehat{\mathscr{B}}(V)
reduces the problem of finding the set of all pre-Nichols algebras of
V
with finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
to the problem of finding quotients of
\widehat{\mathscr{B}}(V)
. As we will recall in §
2.3
, eminent pre-Nichols algebras exist for most
V
of diagonal type.

2.2.
Central extensions of braided Hopf algebras

As we want to study posets of pre-Nichols algebras, we have to deal with extensions of connected Hopf algebras in
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
.
Motivated by
[
T
, Theorem 3.2]
and
[
A+
, Proposition 3.6]
we can state the following correspondence between Hopf ideals and
normal coideal subalgebras in
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
R
be a connected Hopf algebra in
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
. The assignements

\displaystyle\mathtt{A}\mapsto\mathtt{I}(\mathtt{A}):=R/R\mathtt{A}^{+},

\displaystyle I\mapsto\mathtt{A}(I):={}^{\operatorname{co}R/I}R,

give a bijection between the set of normal right coideal subalgebras
\mathtt{A}
of
R
and the set of Hopf ideals
I
of
R
.

Proof.

By
[
A+
, Proposition 3.6 (c)]
we have a bijection between the set of right coideal subalgebras
\mathtt{A}
of
R
and the set of coideals
I
of
R
whose quotient map is of
R
-modules. Now
\mathtt{A}
is normal if and only if
\mathtt{I}(\mathtt{A})
is an ideal: the proof is by direct computation, analogous to that in
[
T
, Proposition 1.4]
.
∎

Recall that an
extension of braided Hopf algebras

[
AN
, §2.5]
is a sequence of morphisms of braided Hopf algebras

\Bbbk\rightarrow\mathtt{A}\overset{\iota}{\to}\mathtt{C}\overset{\pi}{\to}\mathtt{B}\rightarrow\Bbbk

such that
\iota
is injective,
\pi
is surjective,
\ker\pi=\mathtt{C}\iota(\mathtt{A}^{+})
and
\mathtt{A}=\mathtt{C}^{\,\operatorname{co}\pi}
.
For the sake of simplicity we just write
\mathtt{A}\overset{\iota}{\hookrightarrow}\mathtt{C}\overset{\pi}{\twoheadrightarrow}\mathtt{B}
.
If
\mathtt{C}
is connected then any surjective braided Hopf algebra morphism
\mathtt{C}\overset{\pi}{\twoheadrightarrow}\mathtt{B}
gives an extension by choosing
\mathtt{A}=\mathtt{C}^{\,\operatorname{co}\pi}
, see
[
A+
, 3.6]
.

We say that an extension is
central
if
\mathtt{A}
is contained in the center of
\mathtt{C}
.

In case that
\mathtt{A}
,
\mathtt{B}
,
\mathtt{C}
are
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded with finite-dimensional homogeneous components and the maps
\iota
,
\pi
preserve the
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-grading, the Hilbert series of these algebras satisfy the equality

{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathtt{C}}={\mathcal{H}}_{\mathtt{A}}{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathtt{B}}
, cf.
[
ACSa
, Lemma 2.4]
.

Now we deal with central extensions of Hopf algebras in
{}^{H}_{H}\mathcal{YD}
whose right hand side term is a Nichols algebra.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}{\hookrightarrow}\mathscr{B}\overset{\pi}{\twoheadrightarrow}\mathscr{B}(V)
be a central extension of connected graded braided Hopf algebras, where
\mathscr{B}=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{B}^{n}
is a pre-Nichols algebra of
V
, i.e.
\mathscr{B}^{1}=V
.

(i)

The assignment
I\mapsto\mathscr{B}I
is a bijective correspondence between graded Hopf ideals of
\mathcal{Z}
and graded Hopf ideals of
\mathscr{B}
generated in degree
\geq 2
.

(ii)

Assume that
\mathscr{B}
,
\mathscr{B}(V)
are
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded and
\pi
preserves the
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-grading, so
\mathcal{Z}
is also
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded.
Let
I
be a
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded Hopf ideal of
\mathcal{Z}
,
\mathscr{B}^{\prime}:=\mathscr{B}/\mathscr{B}I
,
\mathtt{Z}=\mathcal{Z}^{\,\operatorname{co}\mathcal{Z}/I}
. Then
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathscr{B}}={\mathcal{H}}_{\mathscr{B}^{\prime}}{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathtt{Z}}
.

Proof.

(i)

Let
A=\oplus_{n\geq 0}A^{n}
be a graded right coideal subalgebra of
\mathscr{B}
such that
A^{1}=0
. We claim that
A^{n}\subset\mathcal{Z}
for all
n\geq 0
: The proof is by induction on
n
. For
n=0
,
A^{0}=\Bbbk 1\subset\mathcal{Z}
. Now assume that
A^{k}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}
for all
k\leq n
. For each
x\in A^{n+1}
,

\displaystyle\Delta(x)-x\otimes 1-1\otimes x\in\oplus_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}\otimes\mathscr{B}_{n+1-i}.

Thus, by inductive hypothesis,
(\pi\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta(x)=\pi(x)\otimes 1+1\otimes x
, so

\displaystyle\Delta(\pi(x))=(\pi\otimes\pi)\Delta(x)=\pi(x)\otimes 1+1\otimes\pi(x).

Hence
\pi(x)
is a primitive element of
\mathscr{B}(V)
in degree
n+1\geq 2
, so
\pi(x)=0
. Thus
x\in{}^{\operatorname{co}\pi}\mathscr{B}=\mathcal{Z}
, and the inductive step follows.
Therefore, the set of graded right coideal subalgebras of
\mathscr{B}
such that
A^{1}=0
is the set of graded coideal subalgebras of
\mathcal{Z}
, and all these coideal subalgebras are normal since
\mathcal{Z}
is central.

Using Proposition
2.1
, we obtain a bijective correspondence between the corresponding Hopf ideals; that is, between the graded Hopf ideals of
\mathcal{Z}
and the graded Hopf ideals of
\mathscr{B}
generated in degree
\geq 2
.

(ii)
Notice that
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{Z}}={\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}}{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathtt{Z}}
and
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}{\hookrightarrow}\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\overset{\pi}{\twoheadrightarrow}\mathscr{B}(V)
is also a central extension of connected graded braided Hopf algebras. From this exact sequence and the one of
\mathscr{B}
we have that

{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathscr{B}}={\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathscr{B}(V)}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathscr{B}^{\prime}}={\mathcal{H}}_{\mathcal{Z}/I}{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathscr{B}(V)}
. Thus the statement follows by putting together the three equalities involving Hilbert series.
∎

2.3.
Distinguished pre-Nichols algebras

Let
(V,c^{\mathfrak{q}})
be a braided vector space of diagonal type with
\operatorname{GKdim}\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}<\infty
.
By
[
AnG
]
, this means that
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}
has a PBW basis with a finite set of homogeneous generators; in other words,
\mathfrak{q}
belongs to the lists in
[
H2
]
.
The set
\varDelta_{+}^{\mathfrak{q}}
of

of
\mathfrak{q}

-degrees of these generators, which is independent of the chosen PBW basis

Assume from now on that

. For
\alpha=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{\theta}),\beta=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{\theta})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
set

\displaystyle q_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle:=\prod_{i,j=1}^{\theta}q_{ij}^{a_{i}b_{j}},

\displaystyle N_{\beta}:=\operatorname{ord}q_{\beta\beta}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}.

A total order
\succ
on
\varDelta_{+}^{\mathfrak{q}}
is
convex
if for all
\alpha\succ\beta\in\varDelta_{+}^{\mathfrak{q}}
such that
\alpha+\beta\in\varDelta_{+}^{\mathfrak{q}}
we have that
\alpha\succ\alpha+\beta\succ\beta
. For each convex order
\beta_{1}\succ\cdots\succ\beta_{M}
there exists a PBW basis with set of generators
x_{\beta}
,
\beta\in\varDelta_{+}^{\mathfrak{q}}
, with each
x_{\beta}
of degree
\beta
. More explicitly, the set

\displaystyle x_{\beta_{1}}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{\beta_{M}}^{n_{M}},

\displaystyle 0\leq n_{i}<N_{\beta_{i}},

\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}
, see e.g.
[
An2
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}}(t)=\left(\prod_{\beta\in\varDelta_{+}^{\mathfrak{q}}:N_{\beta}=\infty}\frac{1}{1-t^{\beta}}\right)\left(\prod_{\beta\in\varDelta_{+}^{\mathfrak{q}}:N_{\beta}<\infty}\frac{1-t^{N_{\beta}\beta}}{1-t^{\beta}}\right).

Next we move to pre-Nichols algebras of diagonal type and connected Dynkin diagram.
Between the Nichols algebras
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}
with finite

, some of them are infinite-dimensional. By
[

for most of these

\operatorname{GKdim}
when

for all

. In this case, there exists a pre-Nichols algebra with finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
, called the

]

by the ideal

generated by the set of defining relations in

adding a few extra relations and removing relations of the form

for

\displaystyle\mathfrak{O}^{\mathfrak{q}}:=\left\{\alpha\in\varDelta^{\mathfrak{q}}:q_{\alpha\beta}q_{\beta\alpha}\in\{q_{\alpha\alpha}^{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}\text{ for all }\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{\theta}\right\}

for all

For example, let

Fix

2.4.
Twist equivalence and pre-Nichols algebras

Recall that two braiding matrices
\mathfrak{q}=(q_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{I}}
and
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{I}}

for all

[

Let
(V,c)
and
(W,d)

,
\mathfrak{p}
, respectively.
We want to relate Hopf ideals of

and

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}=(q_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{I}}
and
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{I}}
be twist-equivalent matrices.

(i)

There exists an isomorphism of posets
\Psi:\mathfrak{Pre}^{\operatorname{gr}}(\mathfrak{q})\to\mathfrak{Pre}^{\operatorname{gr}}(\mathfrak{p})
preserving the Hilbert series.

(ii)

\Psi
restricts to an isomorphism
\Psi:\mathfrak{Pre}_{\operatorname{fGK}}^{\operatorname{gr}}(\mathfrak{q})\to\mathfrak{Pre}_{\operatorname{fGK}}^{\operatorname{gr}}(\mathfrak{p})

Proof.

(i)

In
[
AS2
, Proposition 3.9]
the authors introduce a linear isomorphism
\psi:\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}\to\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, which is a coalgebra isomorphism: Let us recall more details about this isomorphism.
In loc. cit. the authors take the group cocycle

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle:\mathbb{Z}^{\theta}\times\mathbb{Z}^{\theta}\to\Bbbk^{\times},

\displaystyle\sigma(g_{i},g_{j})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}p_{ij}q_{ij}^{-1},&i\leq j\\
1&i>j.\end{cases}

For any Hopf algebra

.
As stated in
[

, Lemma 2.12]

, with

as vector spaces.

Let
V
,
W
be as above: as usual we consider

. Working as in
[

, Proposition 3.9 & Remark 3.10]
we check that

. Any

-graded pre-Nichols algebra

of

-graded components. Thus, we have a map

.

(ii)
As

by
[

.
∎

Next we want to reduce to the case in which

If

3.
Hopf ideals of algebras of functions of unipotent groups

According with the previous section we have to understand the coalgebra structure of the algebra of functions of the unipotent algebraic group whose Lie algebra is the positive part

of a semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with root system
\varDelta

As an algebra,

Let

Now we relate

Lemma 3.1
.

(1)

The

-graded dual of

is (isomorphic to)

, the enveloping algebra of the nilpotent Lie algebra

.

(2)

The subspace
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{Z}_{\varDelta})
of primitive elements of
\mathcal{Z}_{\varDelta}
has basis
\{z_{\alpha_{i}}\,|\,i\in\mathbb{I}\}

Proof.

For (
1
), let

be the graded dual of
\mathcal{Z}_{\varDelta}
, with
U_{\beta}
the component of degree
\beta
. As

is connected (as algebra) and commutative,

is connected (as coalgebra), and cocommutative. Thus

is the enveloping algebra of its primitive elements.

The subspace

of primitive elements of

For (

is the space of derivations

This space has dimension

,

∎

For each

Next we introduce the family of subsets of

.

Let

(i)

with maximum

and minimum

To deal with subsets of roots as above we need first a statement on sums of roots.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

.
If
\alpha,\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{m}\in\varDelta_{+}

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\gamma_{i}

By induction on

\alpha=2\alpha_{r}+\alpha_{s}

or else
\alpha=\alpha_{r}+\alpha_{s}+\alpha_{t}

and the

\alpha_{r}+\alpha_{s}\in\varDelta_{+}
, while in the second
\alpha_{r}+\alpha_{s}\in\varDelta_{+}
up to permute the sub-indices.

Now assume that the statement holds for roots of height
\leq h
and take
\alpha,\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{m}\in\varDelta_{+}
such that

\alpha=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\gamma_{i}

•

There exists

We know that

and

If

\mathfrak{q}
by a twist equivalent matrix.

By Lemma
4.3
, the map above is injective: if
B\neq B^{\prime}
are two different sets closed by sums, then

{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathscr{B}(\mathfrak{q},B)}(t)\neq{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathscr{B}(\mathfrak{q},B^{\prime})}(t)
. Also, it is a anti morphism of posets.

On the other hand, we will see that the map is also surjective. Let
\mathscr{B}\in\mathfrak{Pre}_{\operatorname{fGK}}^{\operatorname{gr}}(\mathfrak{q})
:

•

By definition, there exists an
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded Hopf ideal
\mathcal{I}
such that
\mathscr{B}\simeq\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}/\mathcal{I}
.

•

By Proposition
2.2

(i)
there exists a graded Hopf ideal
I
of
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
such that
\mathcal{I}=\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}I
.

•

By Theorem
3.10
there exists a set
B\subseteq\underline{\widehat{\mathfrak{O}}^{\mathfrak{q}}_{+}}=\varDelta_{+}
closed by sums such that
I=I(B)
.

All in all, we have that

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}/\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}I(B)=\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}/\langle z_{\underline{\beta}}|\underline{\beta}\in A\rangle=\mathscr{B}(\mathfrak{q},B),

so the map is also surjective, and the statement follows.
∎

4.3.
The non-connected case

Here we take an arbitraty matrix
\mathfrak{q}
(whose diagram is not necessarily connected). Following the spirit of
[
ASa

we set

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}^{\pm}

\displaystyle:=\{i\in\mathbb{I}:q_{ii}=\pm 1,\widetilde{q}_{ij}=1\text{ for all }j\neq i\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}^{(c)}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{I}-\left(\mathbb{I}^{+}\cup\mathbb{I}^{-}\right).

that is,
\mathbb{I}^{\pm}
contains all the connected components of an isolated vertex labelled with
\pm 1
while
\mathbb{I}^{(c)}
is the union of those points labelled with
q_{ii}\neq\pm 1
and those connected components with at least two vertices.
Let
C_{1},\cdots,C_{d}
be the partition of
\mathbb{I}^{(c)}
in connected components: i.e.
\mathbb{I}^{(c)}=\cup_{\ell=1}^{d}C_{d}
, where the diagram of each
\mathfrak{q}^{(\ell)}:=(q_{ij})_{i,j\in C_{\ell}}
is connected and
\widetilde{q}_{ij}=1
if
i\in C_{\ell_{1}}
,
j\in C_{\ell_{2}}
,
\ell_{1}\neq\ell_{2}
.

Let
\mathscr{B}
be an
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded pre-Nichols algebra of
\mathfrak{q}
.
For each
1\leq\ell\leq d
, let
\mathscr{B}^{(\ell)}
be the subalgebra of
\mathscr{B}
generated by
x_{i}
,
i\in C_{\ell}
, which is a pre-Nichols algebra of
\mathfrak{q}^{(\ell)}
. Similarly, let
\mathscr{B}^{\pm}
be the subalgebra of
\mathscr{B}
generated by
x_{i}
,

, and
\mathfrak{q}^{(\pm)}:=(q_{ij})_{i,j\in\mathbb{I}^{\pm}}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
1\leq\ell\leq d
,
i\in C_{\ell}
,
j\in\mathbb{I}-C_{\ell}
such that
q_{jj}\neq 1
. If
\operatorname{GKdim}\mathscr{B}<\infty
, then
x_{ij}=0
in
\mathscr{B}
.

Proof.

By hypothesis,
i
and
j
are not connected, so
x_{ij}
is primitive in
\mathscr{B}
. Also,
\operatorname{ord}q_{ii}\geq 2
since
C_{\ell}\subseteq\mathbb{I}^{(c)}
, and also
\operatorname{ord}q_{jj}\geq 2
since
q_{jj}\neq 1
. If either
\operatorname{ord}q_{ii}>2
or
\operatorname{ord}q_{jj}>2
, then
x_{ij}=0
by
[
ASa
, Proposition 3.2]
. Otherwise
q_{ii}=q_{jj}=-1
and there exists
k\neq i,j
such that
\widetilde{q}_{ik}\neq 1=\widetilde{q}_{jk}
. Suppose that
x_{ij}\neq 0
in
\mathscr{B}
. Set
y_{1}=x_{i}
,
y_{2}=x_{k}
,
y_{3}=x_{ij}
and
W:=\Bbbk y_{1}+\Bbbk y_{2}+\Bbbk y_{3}
is a three-dimensional subspace of
\mathcal{P}(\mathscr{B})
.
Working as in the proof of
[
ASa
, Proposition 3.2]
,
\operatorname{GKdim}\mathscr{B}(W)<\infty
where

is of diagonal type with matrix
\mathfrak{p}=(p_{rs})_{1\leq r,s\leq 3}
. By direct computation,
p_{33}=1
and
\widetilde{p}_{23}=\widetilde{q}_{ik}\neq 1
, so we get a contradiction with
[
AAH
, Proposition 4.16]
. Hence

in
\mathscr{B}
.
∎

(4.7)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{q}}

\displaystyle:=\bigotimes_{1\leq\ell\leq d}\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{q}^{(\ell)}},

\displaystyle\underline{\widehat{\mathfrak{O}}^{\mathfrak{q}}_{+}}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{1\leq\ell\leq d}\underline{\widehat{\mathfrak{O}}^{\mathfrak{q}^{(\ell)}}_{+}},

extending the definitions we have made from the connected to the non-connected case. Hence we have an extension of

-graded Hopf algebras

\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{q}}\hookrightarrow\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{q}
be such that
\mathbb{I}^{+}=\mathbb{I}^{-}=\emptyset
.

(i)

If
\mathscr{B}
is an
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded pre-Nichols algebra of
\mathfrak{q}
such that
\operatorname{GKdim}\mathscr{B}<\infty
, then

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}\simeq\otimes_{\ell=1}^{d}\mathscr{B}^{(\ell)},

and
\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
is the eminent pre-Nichols algebra of
\mathfrak{q}
.

(ii)

There exists an anti-isomorphism of posets

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Pre}_{\operatorname{fGK}}^{\operatorname{gr}}(\mathfrak{q})\simeq\prod_{\ell=1}^{d}\mathfrak{Pre}_{\operatorname{fGK}}^{\operatorname{gr}}(\mathfrak{q}^{(\ell)})\simeq\mathcal{P}_{c}(\underline{\widehat{\mathfrak{O}}^{\mathfrak{q}}_{+}}).

Proof.

(i)
Notice that
\mathscr{B}^{\prime}=\otimes_{\ell=1}^{d}\mathscr{B}^{(\ell)}
is an
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded Hopf algebra with defining relations those defining each
\mathscr{B}^{(\ell)}
together with
x_{ij}=0
for
i\in C_{k}
,
j\in C_{\ell}
,
k\neq\ell
. Thus Lemma
4.7
says that there exists a surjective map
\mathscr{B}^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{B}
of
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded Hopf algebras, which is the identity on
V
, i.e. a map of pre-Nichols algebras of
\mathfrak{q}
. As we also have a map

\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{B}^{\prime}
, the composition of both map gives a map of pre-Nichols algebras. As
\mathscr{B}
is arbitrary with finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
,
\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
is eminent.

Hence we move to the description of
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded quotients of
\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
.
As before, we can use Proposition
2.7
and Lemma
2.8
to assume that
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
is central. By Proposition
2.2
we have to compute all the Hopf ideals of
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
, which in turn (by taking graded duals) are classified by
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded Lie subalgebras of the nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{q}}=\prod_{1\leq\ell\leq d}\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{q}^{(\ell)}}
: any of these Lie subalgebras of
\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{q}}
is the product of
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\theta}
-graded Lie subalgebras of each
\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{q}^{(\ell)}}
, and these are classified by subsets
B^{(\ell)}
of
\varDelta_{+}^{\mathfrak{q}^{(\ell)}}
closed by sums, applying Theorem
3.10
and Proposition
4.1
(depending on
\ell
).

Coming back to
\widehat{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathfrak{q}}
, we quotient this Hopf algebra by
z_{\underline{\beta}}
for those
\underline{\beta}\notin B:=\cup_{1\leq\ell\leq d}B^{(\ell)}
. We obtain then the pre-Nichols algebra

\mathscr{B}(\mathfrak{q},B):=\widehat{\mathscr{B}}_{\mathfrak{q}}/\langle z_{\underline{\beta}}|\underline{\beta}\notin B\rangle\simeq\bigotimes_{1\leq\ell\leq d}\mathscr{B}(q^{(\ell)},B^{(\ell)}),

and any
\mathscr{B}\in\mathfrak{Pre}_{\operatorname{fGK}}^{\operatorname{gr}}(\mathfrak{q})
is of this shape, with
\mathscr{B}^{(\ell)}\simeq\mathscr{B}(q^{(\ell)},B^{(\ell)})
,
1\leq\ell\leq d
.

Now
(ii)
follows from
(i)
and the fact that a subset
B\in\underline{\widehat{\mathfrak{O}}^{\mathfrak{q}}_{+}}
is closed by sums if and only if each
B^{(\ell)}=B\cap\underline{\widehat{\mathfrak{O}}^{\mathfrak{q}^{(\ell)}}_{+}}
is closed by sum, since the sum
\alpha+\beta
of two roots
\alpha\in\underline{\widehat{\mathfrak{O}}^{\mathfrak{q}^{(k)}}_{+}}
,
\beta\in\underline{\widehat{\mathfrak{O}}^{\mathfrak{q}^{(\ell)}}_{+}}
,
k\neq\ell
, is not a root.
∎

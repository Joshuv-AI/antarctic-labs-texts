Fractional Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg type inequalities on the Heisenberg group
Rama Rawat, Haripada Roy, Prosenjit Roy

Abstract
. The aim of this work is to establish some cases of the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities on the Heisenberg group for the fractional Sobolev spaces. Here we work with the fractional Sobolev spaces as given by Adimurthi and Mallick in [ 1 ] . Our inequalities also give an improvement on the range of indices for the Hardy type inequality established in [ 1 ] .

46E35; 26D15; 43A80; 49J52

1.
Introduction

In
[
7
]
, L. Caffarelli, R. Kohn and L. Nirenberg established a family of interpolation inequalities on the Euclidean space, now known as, Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg (CKN) inequalities. The Hardy and Sobolev inequalities are included as particular cases of these CKN inequalities. Later analogous versions of Hardy-Sobolev inequalities were obtained in
[
11
,
12
,
15
,
23
]
in the setting of fractional Sobolev spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, in
[
1
]
on the Heisenberg group and in
[
18
]
for homogeneous spaces.

In this paper we establish some cases of the fractional Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg (CKN) inequalities on the Heisenberg group. We will be working with the fractional Sobolev spaces as defined by Adimurthi and Mallick in
[
1
]
which are more natural when the index
p
is not necessarily
2.
This differs from the fractional Sobolev spaces considered in
[
18
,
28
]
and the results therein for more general homogeneous spaces including the Heisenberg group.
As in the Euclidean case, our version of CKN fractional inequalities contains in particular fractional Hardy’s inequality and Sobolev inequality as proved in
[
1
]
. Further, we work with decomposition of the Heisenberg group into sets which allow us to estimate required integrals, circumventing the use of extension domains. We are not aware if half spaces or the domains which appear in our proofs are extension domains for the fractional Sobolev spaces we are considering in this paper. Our results also improve on range of indices for which the fractional Hardy’s inequality holds.

We begin by setting notations and recalling results which we need in the sequel. Let
n\geq 1
,
p>1
,
s\in(0,1)
and
\eta,\,\eta_{1},\,\eta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}=\eta
. For any measurable subset
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p,\eta}(\Omega)
as

W^{s,p,\eta}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega):[u]_{W^{s,p,\eta}(\Omega)}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|x|^{\eta_{1}p}|y|^{\eta_{2}p}|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}dxdy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\right\},

endowed with the norm

\lVert u\rVert_{W^{s,p,\eta}(\Omega)}=\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+[u]_{W^{s,p,\eta}(\Omega)}.

When
\eta_{1}=\eta_{2}=\eta=0
, we denote the quantities
\lVert u\rVert_{W^{s,p,0}(\Omega)}
and
[u]_{W^{s,p,0}(\Omega)}
by
\lVert u\rVert_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}
and
[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}
respectively.

Let
p>1
,
0<s<1
,
q\geq 1
,
\tau>0
,
0\leq a\leq 1
and
\beta,

\gamma,

\eta,

\eta_{1},

\eta_{2}

\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}=\eta
and

(1.1)

\frac{1}{\tau}+\frac{\gamma}{n}=a\left(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\eta-s}{n}\right)+(1-a)\left(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{\beta}{n}\right).

When
a>0
, we assume the following additional conditions

(1.2)

\gamma=a\sigma+(1-a)\beta,\ \ 0\leq\eta-\sigma

and

(1.3)

\eta-\sigma\leq s,\ \mathrm{when}\ \ \frac{1}{\tau}+\frac{\gamma}{n}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\eta-s}{n}.

The following fractional Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities were proved by Hoai-Minh Nguyen and Marco Squassina in
[
24
]
.

Theorem 1
.

[
24
]

Let
n,p,s,q,\tau,a,\beta,\gamma,\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
and
\eta
be as above satisfying the conditions (
1.1
), (

) and (

).

(i)
If
\frac{1}{\tau}+\frac{\gamma}{n}>0
, then we have

(1.4)

\lVert|x|^{\gamma}u\rVert_{L^{\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C[u]^{a}_{W^{s,p,\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lVert|x|^{\beta}u\rVert^{1-a}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\ \forall u\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(ii)
If
\frac{1}{\tau}+\frac{\gamma}{n}<0
, then we have

(1.5)

\lVert|x|^{\gamma}u\rVert_{L^{\tau}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C[u]^{a}_{W^{s,p,\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lVert|x|^{\beta}u\rVert^{1-a}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\ \forall u\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}).

If we consider
a=1
and
\eta_{1}=\eta_{2}=\eta=0
in (
1.1
),
\gamma=0
implies
\tau=p^{*}=\frac{np}{n-p}
, and inequality (

[

,

]
. On the other hand, the corresponding inequality for

and

is the fractional Hardy inequality obtained in
[

,
23
]
.

Inequalities (
1.4
) and (

) are not true in general for the case

. Instead the following weighted version holds in this case as was proved in
[
24

.

Theorem 2
.

[

Let

and

be as above satisfying the condition (

(i)

(1.6)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|x|^{\tau\gamma}}{\ln^{\tau}\left(4R/|x|\right)}|u|^{\tau}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\tau}}\leq C[u]^{a}_{W^{s,p,\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lVert|x|^{\beta}u\rVert^{1-a}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(ii)
If
\frac{1}{\tau}+\frac{\gamma}{n}=0
and

with supp
(u)\cap B(0,r)=\phi
, then we have

(1.7)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|x|^{\tau\gamma}}{\ln^{\tau}\left(4|x|/r\right)}|u|^{\tau}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{\tau}}\leq C[u]^{a}_{W^{s,p,\eta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lVert|x|^{\beta}u\rVert^{1-a}_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Before going further, we recall the preliminaries on the Heisenberg group. The Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is defined as

\mathbb{H}^{n}:=\{\xi=(x,y,t)=(z,t):(x,y)=z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \mathrm{and}\ t\in\mathbb{R}\}

with the group law

\xi\circ\xi^{\prime}=(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+2\langle y,x^{\prime}\rangle-2\langle x,y^{\prime}\rangle),

where
\xi=(x,y,t)
,
\xi^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the usual Euclidean inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is easy to see that
0\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
is the identity element and
-\xi
is the inverse of
\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
. A basis for the left invariant vector fields is given by

X_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}+2y_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n,

Y_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}-2x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n,

T=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}.

For any
C^{1}
function
u
,
\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(u)
is called the sub-gradient, defined by

\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(u)=\left(X_{1}(u),\cdots,X_{n}(u),Y_{1}(u),\cdots,Y_{n}(u)\right)

and

|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(u)|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(|X_{i}(u)|^{2}+|Y_{i}(u)|^{2}\right).

For any
\xi=(x,y,t)=(z,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
d(\xi)=\left((|x|^{2}+|y|^{2})^{2}+t^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}=\left(|z|^{4}+t^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}
is the Koranyi-Folland non isotropic gauge and
Q=2n+2
is the homogeneous dimension of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. The left-invariant Haar measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}.
We define the ball centered at
\xi
with radius
r
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
as

(1.8)

B_{r}(\xi)=\{\xi^{\prime}=(z^{\prime},t^{\prime}):d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})<r\},

and the annular domain as

(1.9)

A_{r,R}(\xi)=\{\xi^{\prime}=(z^{\prime},t^{\prime}):r<d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})<R\}.

We also define the following set

(1.10)

D_{r}(\xi)=\{\xi^{\prime}=(z^{\prime},t^{\prime}):|z^{\prime}-z|<r\ \mathrm{and}\ \ |t^{\prime}-t-2\langle y,x^{\prime}\rangle+2\langle x,y^{\prime}\rangle|<r^{2}\}.

Various versions of Hardy’s inequality on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
were proved by several authors in last two decades. The following inequality is one of the general version of the same, proved by L. D’Ambrozio in
[
10
]
:

(1.11)

\int_{\Omega}\frac{|z|^{\beta}}{d(\xi)^{\alpha}}|u(\xi)|^{p}d\xi\leq C_{n,p,\alpha,\beta}^{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(u)|^{p}|z|^{\beta-p}d(\xi)^{2p-\alpha},\ \forall u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega),

where
\Omega
is an open subset of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
,
p>1
,
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy
Q>\alpha-\beta
and
2n>p-\beta
and
C_{n,p,\alpha\beta}=\frac{p}{Q+\beta-\alpha}
is the optimal constant.

Inequality (
1.11
) with
\alpha=4
and
\beta=2
was proved in
[
16
]
for the domain
\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Whereas (
1.11
) was proved for the domain
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with
\alpha=2p
and
\beta=p
in
[
25
]
. Adimurthi and Sekar proved (
1.11
) with
\alpha=2p
and
\beta=2
, for
u\in FS_{0}^{1,p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
in
[
3
]
, where the space
FS_{0}^{1,p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
is the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
under the norm

|u|^{p}_{1,p}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(u)|^{p}}{|z|^{p-2}}dzdt.

In
[
17
]
, Yazhou Han established Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg type inequalities for
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Further, D. Suragan and his coauthors had extended some cases of CKN type inequalities on stratified Lie groups (see
[
27
,
29
]
) and homogeneous groups (see
[
26
]
) with optimal constants.

For
1\leq p<\infty
,
s\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R},
consider the fractional order Sobolev space on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
as given in
[
1
]
:

W_{0}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})=\mathrm{Cl}\left\{f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n}):\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}<\infty\right\},

where
\xi=(z,t)
,
\xi^{\prime}=(z^{\prime},t^{\prime}).

The closure is considered under the norm
\lVert f\rVert_{s,p,\alpha}^{p}=\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}^{p}+[f]_{s,p,\alpha}^{p}
, where

[f]_{s,p,\alpha}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Let
\Omega
be any open set in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. We define the space

W^{s,p,\alpha}(\Omega)=\left\{f\in L^{p}(\Omega):[f]_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\right\},

with the norm
\lVert f\rVert_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}=\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+[f]_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}
.

Throughout this work, we assume
(p-2)\alpha\geq 0
. We also assume
(p-2)\alpha<Q-2
, which follows from the non triviality of the space
W_{0}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
(see
[
1
]
, Section 3). The following Sobolev embedding theorem for
W_{0}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
is proved in
[
1
]
.

Theorem 3
.

[
1
]

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
s\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
ps+(p-2)\alpha<Q
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
, depends only on
n
,
p
,
s
and
\alpha
, such that

(1.12)

\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leq C[f]_{s,p,\alpha},\ \forall f\in W_{0}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n}),

where
p^{*}=\frac{Qp}{Q-ps-(p-2)\alpha}
is the fractional critical Sobolev exponent.

A fractional Hardy type inequality for the functions of
W_{0}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
has been proved in
[
1
]
which is as follows:

Theorem 4
.

[
1
]

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
s\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy the following conditions:

(a)

ps>2
;

(b)

ps+(p-2)\alpha<Q
.

Then there exists a positive constant
C
, depends only on
n
,
p
,
s
and
\alpha
, such that

(1.13)

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}d\xi}{d(\xi)^{ps}|z|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\leq C[f]_{s,p,\alpha}^{p},\ \forall f\in W_{0}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n}).

Theorem
4
holds without the condition (a) only for the case

or
\alpha=0
i.e., when
(p-2)\alpha=0
(see
[
1
]
, Remark 1.3.). In
[
18

A. Kassymov and D. Suragan established a fractional Hardy type inequality on the homogeneous Lie groups, which in particular on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
gives (

) with

. If

, condition (a) forces

to be large enough when

. To be precise, Theorem

.

Theorem 1.1

Let

\fint_{\mathscr{A}_{k,j}}|f-(f)_{\mathscr{B}_{k,j}}|^{\tau}d\xi\leq C\,2^{-\frac{k}{\tau}\left(Q+\tau\gamma\left(1+(p-2)\frac{\alpha}{ps}\right)\right)}[f]_{s,p,\alpha,\mathscr{B}_{k,j}}^{a\tau}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathscr{B}_{k,j})}^{(1-a)\tau},

where

(1.27)

\mathscr{B}_{k,j}:=\{\xi=(z,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:2^{k-1}<|z|\leq 2^{k+2},\ (j-1)2^{2k}<t<(j+2)2^{2k}\},

(see Figure 1.2). Note that
\mathscr{A}_{k,j}\Subset\mathscr{B}_{k,j}
. By the notation
A\Subset B
we mean that the closure of the set
A
is a compact subset of

. The constant
C
in (

) is independent of
k
and

. The dependency of the constant

in (

) on the sets is being taken care of by the factor

.

Remark 1
.

Note that the sets

For the case

A_{k}:=A_{2^{k},2^{k+1}}(0)=\{\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:2^{k}<d(\xi)\leq 2^{k+1}\},\ \ k\in\mathbb{Z},

of

2.
Some basic properties of fractional Sobolev spaces

The following result gives the relation between the spaces
W^{s,p,\alpha}(\Omega)
and
W^{s^{\prime},p,\alpha}(\Omega)
for
s^{\prime}<s
. This result for the case
(p-2)\alpha=0
is already established in
[
22
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<s^{\prime}\leq s<1
and
\Omega
be an open set in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Then for any measurable function
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

(2.1)

\lVert f\rVert_{s^{\prime},p,\alpha,\Omega}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}

for some positive constant
C=C(n,p,s,\alpha)
. In other word,
W^{s,p,\alpha}(\Omega)\subseteq W^{s^{\prime},p,\alpha}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We have

(2.2)

[f]_{s^{\prime},p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega\cap D_{1}(\xi)}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps^{\prime}}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\\
+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega\cap D_{1}(\xi)^{C}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps^{\prime}}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}=I_{1}+I_{2},

where
D_{1}(\xi)
is the set defined in (
1.10
). Since
d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})<1
in
D_{1}(\xi)
,

(2.3)

I_{1}\leq\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega\cap D_{1}(\xi)}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\leq[f]_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}.

We now estimate the integral
I_{2}
.

I_{2}\leq 2^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega\cap D_{1}(\xi)^{C}}\frac{(|f(\xi)|^{p}+|f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p})d\xi^{\prime}d\xi}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps^{\prime}}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}.

Using symmetry, we obtain

(2.4)

I_{2}\leq 2^{p}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{\Omega\cap D_{1}(\xi)^{C}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps^{\prime}}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\right)|f(\xi)|^{p}d\xi.

Let

J=\int_{\Omega\cap D_{1}(\xi)^{C}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps^{\prime}}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\\
\leq\int_{D_{1}(\xi)^{C}}\frac{dz^{\prime}dt^{\prime}}{(|z-z^{\prime}|^{4}+(t^{\prime}-t-2\langle y,x^{\prime}\rangle+2\langle x,y^{\prime}\rangle)^{2})^{\frac{Q+ps^{\prime}}{4}}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}.

We use the change of variable
\zeta=z^{\prime}-z
and
\mu=t^{\prime}-t-2\langle y,x^{\prime}\rangle+2\langle x,y^{\prime}\rangle
, and obtain

J\leq\int_{D_{1}(0)^{C}}\frac{d\zeta d\mu}{(|\zeta|^{4}+\mu^{2})^{\frac{Q+ps^{\prime}}{4}}|\zeta|^{(p-2)\alpha}}.

Let us define the following sets:

B_{1}=\{(z,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}\times\mathbb{R}:|z|>1,\,|t|>1\},

B_{2}=\{(z,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}\times\mathbb{R}:|z|>1,\,|t|<1\},

B_{3}=\{(z,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}\times\mathbb{R}:|z|<1,\,|t|>1\},

and the integrals

J_{1}=\int_{B_{1}}\frac{d\zeta d\mu}{(|\zeta|^{4}+\mu^{2})^{\frac{Q+ps^{\prime}}{4}}|\zeta|^{(p-2)\alpha}},

J_{2}=\int_{B_{2}}\frac{d\zeta d\mu}{(|\zeta|^{4}+\mu^{2})^{\frac{Q+ps^{\prime}}{4}}|\zeta|^{(p-2)\alpha}},

J_{3}=\int_{B_{3}}\frac{d\zeta d\mu}{(|\zeta|^{4}+\mu^{2})^{\frac{Q+ps^{\prime}}{4}}|\zeta|^{(p-2)\alpha}}.

Clearly
D_{1}(0)^{C}=B_{1}\cup B_{2}\cup B_{3}
up to a measure zero set, and
J\leq J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}
.

Estimate for
J_{1}
:
Let
\omega_{2n}
be the measure of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

J_{1}=2\omega_{2n}\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{Q-3}drd\mu}{(r^{4}+\mu^{2})^{\frac{Q+ps^{\prime}}{4}}r^{(p-2)\alpha}}\\
(\mathrm{using}\ \mu=r^{2}t)\ =2\omega_{2n}\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\frac{1}{r^{2}}}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{(1+t^{2})^{\frac{Q+ps^{\prime}}{4}}}\right)\frac{dr}{r^{1+ps^{\prime}+(p-2)\alpha}}\\
\leq 2\omega_{2n}\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{(1+t^{2})^{\frac{Q+ps^{\prime}}{4}}}\right)\frac{dr}{r^{1+ps^{\prime}+(p-2)\alpha}}.

Since
Q+ps^{\prime}>2
and
ps^{\prime}+(p-2)\alpha>0
, both the integrals of R.H.S is finite. Hence
J_{1}\leq C_{1}
.

Estimate for
J_{2}
:

J_{2}=2\omega_{2n}\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r^{Q-3}drd\mu}{(r^{4}+\mu^{2})^{\frac{Q+ps^{\prime}}{4}}r^{(p-2)\alpha}}\\
(\mathrm{using}\ \mu=r^{2}t)\ =2\omega_{2n}\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{r^{2}}}\frac{dt}{(1+t^{2})^{\frac{Q+ps^{\prime}}{4}}}\right)\frac{dr}{r^{1+ps^{\prime}+(p-2)\alpha}}\\
\leq 2\omega_{2n}\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{(1+t^{2})^{\frac{Q+ps^{\prime}}{4}}}\right)\frac{dr}{r^{1+ps^{\prime}+(p-2)\alpha}}\leq C_{2}.

Estimate for
J_{3}
:

J_{3}\leq 2\omega_{2n}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{d\mu}{\mu^{\frac{Q+ps^{\prime}}{2}}}\right)r^{Q-3-(p-2)\alpha}dr.

Since
\frac{Q+ps^{\prime}}{2}>1
and
(p-2)\alpha<Q-2
, we have
J_{3}\leq C_{3}
.
Hence
J
is integrable.

Hence from (
2.4
), we get

(2.5)

I_{2}\leq 2^{p}C\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}.

Combining (
2.2
), (
2.3
) and (
2.5
), we obtain

(2.6)

\lVert f\rVert_{s^{\prime},p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}\leq(2^{p}C+1)\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+[f]_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}\leq C(n,p,s,\alpha)\lVert f\rVert_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}.

∎

Definition 1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
s\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
be an open set. We say that
\Omega
is an extension domain, if there exists a positive constant
C=C(n,p,s,\alpha,\Omega)
such that, for any function
f\in W^{s,p,\alpha}(\Omega)
, there exists
\bar{f}\in W_{0}^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
with
\bar{f}|_{\Omega}=f
, and

(2.7)

\lVert\bar{f}\rVert_{s,p,\alpha}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}.

In the following result we extend any function of
W^{s,p,\alpha}(\Omega)
, supported in
K\Subset\Omega
to a function in
W^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
s\in(0,1)
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\Omega
be any open set in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. If
f\in W^{s,p,\alpha}(\Omega)
and
K
is any compact subset of
\Omega
such that
f\equiv 0
in
\Omega\setminus K
, then the extension function

\bar{f}(\xi)=\begin{cases}f(\xi)&\text{if $\xi\in\Omega$}\\
0&\text{if $\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus\Omega$}\end{cases}

belongs to
W^{s,p,\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, and there exists a positive constant
C
depending on
n
,
p
,
s
,
\alpha
,
K
,
\Omega
and is such that

(2.8)

\lVert\bar{f}\rVert_{s,p,\alpha}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}.

Proof.

We have

(2.9)

[\bar{f}]_{s,p,\alpha}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|\bar{f}(\xi)-\bar{f}(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\\
=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}+2\int_{K}\int_{\Omega^{C}}\frac{|f(\xi)|^{p}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\\
=[f]^{p}_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}+2\int_{K}|f(\xi)|^{p}\int_{\Omega^{C}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}d\xi.

We claim that
\exists\,\beta>0
such that
d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})\geq\beta
for all
\xi\in K
and
\xi^{\prime}\in\Omega^{C}
. If not, then
\exists\,\xi\in K
and
\{\xi_{m}^{\prime}\}\subset\Omega^{C}
such that
d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi_{m}^{\prime})<\frac{1}{m}

\forall m\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular,
|z-z_{m}^{\prime}|<\frac{1}{m}
and
|t_{m}^{\prime}-t-2\langle y,x_{m}^{\prime}\rangle+2\langle x,y_{m}^{\prime}\rangle|<\frac{1}{m^{2}}
, where
\xi_{m}^{\prime}=(x_{m}^{\prime},y_{m}^{\prime},t_{m}^{\prime})=(z_{m}^{\prime},t_{m}^{\prime})
. Now

|t_{m}^{\prime}-t|\leq|t_{m}^{\prime}-t-2\langle y,x_{m}^{\prime}\rangle+2\langle x,y_{m}^{\prime}\rangle|+2|\langle y,x_{m}^{\prime}\rangle-\langle x,y_{m}^{\prime}\rangle|\\
=|t_{m}^{\prime}-t-2\langle y,x_{m}^{\prime}\rangle+2\langle x,y_{m}^{\prime}\rangle|+2|\langle(-y,x),z-z_{m}^{\prime}\rangle|\\
\leq|t_{m}^{\prime}-t-2\langle y,x_{m}^{\prime}\rangle+2\langle x,y_{m}^{\prime}\rangle|+2|z||z-z_{m}^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{m^{2}}+\frac{2R}{m},

where
R>0
is such that
K\subset B(0,R)
, which implies that the sequence
\{\xi_{m}^{\prime}\}
converges to
\xi
and contradicts the fact
K
is a compact subset of
\Omega
.

Hence we have for any
\xi\in K
,

\int_{\Omega^{C}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\\
\leq\int_{B_{\beta}(\xi)^{C}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\leq\int_{B_{\beta}(0)^{C}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{d(\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}.

One can show the last integral is finite by an argument similar to one as in Proposition
2.1
.
This gives

(2.10)

[\bar{f}]_{s,p,\alpha}^{p}\leq[f]^{p}_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}+C\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
s\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
ps+(p-2)\alpha<Q
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
be an open set and
K
be any compact subset of
\Omega
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
, which depends on
n
,
p
,
s
,
\alpha
,
K,

\Omega
and is such that

(2.11)

\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p^{*}}(K)}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega},\ \forall f\in C(\bar{\Omega}),

where
p^{*}=\frac{Qp}{Q-ps-(p-2)\alpha}
.

Proof.

Let
K^{\prime}
be an open set such that
K\Subset K^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
. Choose a function
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
such that
0\leq\phi\leq 1
,
\phi\equiv 1
in
K
and
\phi\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}\setminus K^{\prime}
.

Clearly
f\phi\in C_{c}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
. Sobolev inequality (
1.12
) gives,

(2.12)

\left(\int_{K}|f|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|f\phi|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}\leq C[f\phi]_{s,p,\alpha}.

It follows from (
2.8
),

(2.13)

\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p^{*}}(K)}\leq C\lVert f\phi\rVert_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}\leq C(\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+[f\phi]_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}).

We now estimate the quantity
[f\phi]_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}
in terms of
\lVert f\rVert_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}
.

(2.14)

[f\phi]^{p}_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}\leq 2^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{p}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\phi(\xi)-\phi(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\\
+2^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}|\phi(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\\
\leq 2^{2p-1}\int_{\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{p}\int_{B(\xi,1)}\frac{|\phi(\xi)-\phi(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\\
+2^{2p-1}\int_{\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{p}\int_{B(\xi,1)^{C}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}d\xi+2^{p-1}[f]^{p}_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}.

In the last integral we use
\phi\leq 1
.
Since
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
,
\exists M>0
such that
\frac{|\phi(\xi)-\phi(\xi^{\prime})|}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})}\leq M
for all
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\Omega
, (see
[
1
]
, Proposition 3.1). Hence for any
\xi\in\Omega
,

\int_{B(\xi,1)}\frac{|\phi(\xi)-\phi(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}d\xi^{\prime}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\leq M^{p}\int_{B(\xi,1)}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps-p}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}.

Both the integrals

\int_{B(\xi,1)}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps-p}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}d\xi

and

\int_{B(\xi,1)^{C}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}

are shown to be finite in
[
1
]
, (Proposition 3.1). Hence

(2.15)

[f\phi]^{p}_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}\leq C\left(\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+[f]^{p}_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}\right)\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}.

Inequality (
2.11
) follows from (
2.13
) and (
2.15
).
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
s\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
be such
ps+(p-2)\alpha<Q
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
be an extension domain. Then there exists a positive constant
C=C(n,p,s,\alpha,\Omega)
such that for any
q\in[p,p^{*}]
and for any
f\in W^{s,p,\alpha}(\Omega)
, we have

(2.16)

\lVert f\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}.

In other word,
W^{s,p,\alpha}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
L^{q}(\Omega)
for any
q\in[p,p^{*}]
.

Proof.

The case
q=p
and
q=p^{*}
is trivial. For the case
q\in(p,p^{*})
, choose
0<\theta<1
such that
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{\theta}{p}+\frac{1-\theta}{p^{*}}
. Inequality (
2.16
) follows from Hölder’s inequality with the conjugets
\frac{p}{\theta q}
and
\frac{p^{*}}{(1-\theta)q}
, (
1.12
) and (
2.7
).
∎

Remark 2
.

When
ps+(p-2)\alpha\rightarrow Q
, clearly the fractional critical Sobolev exponent
p^{*}
goes to
\infty
. Hence if
ps+(p-2)\alpha=Q
, one may expect that
W^{s,p,\alpha}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
L^{q}(\Omega)
for any
q\geq p
. Our next result assures that this embedding is true even in the case
ps+(p-2)\alpha\geq Q
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
s\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
be such
ps+(p-2)\alpha\geq Q
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
be an extension domain. Then there exists a positive constant
C=C(n,p,s,\alpha,\Omega)
such that for any
q\geq p
and for any
f\in W^{s,p,\alpha}(\Omega)
, we have

(2.17)

\lVert f\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}.

Proof.

Since
(p-2)\alpha<Q-2
, we can choose
0<s^{\prime}<s
such that
ps^{\prime}+(p-2)\alpha<Q
and
q\leq\frac{pQ}{Q-ps^{\prime}-(p-2)\alpha}
. By Theorem
2.3
, we have

(2.18)

\lVert f\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{s^{\prime},p,\alpha,\Omega}.

Inequality (
2.17
) then follows from Proposition
2.1
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

The following lemma, which uses Corollary
1
is key to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p>1
,
s\in(0,1)
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
\tau>1
,
\,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<a\leq 1
and
q>(1-a)\tau
satisfy (
1.14
) and (
1.15
). Let
\lambda>0
,
0<r^{\prime}<r<R<R^{\prime}
,
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\ell,\ell^{\prime}>0
be such that
\mu^{\prime}<\mu
and
\mu+\ell<\mu^{\prime}+\ell^{\prime}
and consider the following sets:

\mathscr{D}_{\lambda}=\mathscr{D}_{r,R,\mu,\ell}(\lambda):=\{\xi=(z,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:\lambda r<|z|<\lambda R\ \mathrm{and}\ \ \mu\lambda^{2}<t<(\mu+\ell)\lambda^{2}\}

and
\mathscr{D}_{\lambda}^{\prime}=\mathscr{D}_{r^{\prime},R^{\prime},\mu^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}}(\lambda)
. Then for
f\in C(\bar{\mathscr{D}}_{\lambda}^{\prime})
, we have

(3.1)

\left(\fint_{\mathscr{D}_{\lambda}}|f-(f)_{\mathscr{D}_{\lambda}^{\prime}}|^{\tau}\right)^{\frac{1}{\tau}}\leq C\left(\lambda^{ps+(p-2)\alpha-Q}[f]_{s,p,\alpha,{\mathscr{D}_{\lambda}^{\prime}}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{a}{p}}\left(\fint_{\mathscr{D}_{\lambda}^{\prime}}|f|^{q}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1-a}{q}},

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
f
and
\lambda
,
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
.

Proof.

From (
1.14
), we have

\frac{1}{\tau}=a\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s^{\prime}\left(1+(p-2)\frac{\alpha}{ps}\right)}{Q}\right)+\frac{1-a}{q},

where
s^{\prime}=s+\gamma/a<s
. Let
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha s^{\prime}/s
and
{p^{\prime}}^{*}=\frac{pQ}{Q-ps^{\prime}-(p-2)\alpha^{\prime}}

(3.7)

Inequality (

\int_{E^{C}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\geq C|E|^{-\frac{ps+(p-2)\alpha}{Q}},

where
\xi^{\prime}=(z^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in E^{C}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
s\in(0,1)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
(p-2)\alpha\geq 0
and
ps+(p-2)\alpha<Q
. Let
\{e_{i}\}
and
\{d_{i}\}
be two non-negative sequences of real numbers satisfying the following properties:

(a)

e_{i}
is decreasing;

(b)

e_{i}=\sum_{j=i}^{\infty}d_{j}
.

Then the followings hold.

(i)
For any fixed
T>0
we have

(4.2)

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}e_{i}^{\frac{Q-ps-(p-2)\alpha}{Q}}T^{i}\leq T^{\frac{Q}{Q-ps-(p-2)\alpha}}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z},e_{i}\neq 0}e_{i+1}e_{i}^{-\frac{ps+(p-2)\alpha}{Q}}T^{i}.

(ii)
Moreover, if
T>1
then

(4.3)

\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z},e_{i-1}\neq 0}\,\sum_{j\geq i+1}T^{pi}e_{i-1}^{-\frac{ps+(p-2)\alpha}{Q}}d_{j}\leq\frac{1}{T^{p}-1}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z},e_{i-1}\neq 0}T^{pi}e_{i-1}^{-\frac{ps+(p-2)\alpha}{Q}}d_{i}.

We next need the following lemma to prove our theorem.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\beta\leq 0
be such that
Q-2+\beta>0
. Then for any measurable set
D\subset B_{\lambda}(0)
, we have

(4.4)

\int_{D}|z|^{\beta}dzdt\leq C\lambda^{2}|D|^{\frac{Q-2+\beta}{Q}},

where the constant
C
is independent of
\lambda
.

Proof.

Let
R>0
be such that
|D|=2\omega_{2n}R^{Q}=|D_{R}(0)|
, where the set
D_{R}(0)
is defined in (
1.10
). Note that
R\leq C\lambda
. Now

I:=\int_{D}|z|^{\beta}dzdt=\int_{D\cap D_{R}(0)}|z|^{\beta}dzdt+\int_{D\cap D_{R}(0)^{C}}|z|^{\beta}dzdt.

Let us define the sets

D_{R,1}:=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}:|z|>R\}\times\{t\in\mathbb{R}:|t|>R^{2}\},

D_{R,2}:=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}:|z|>R\}\times\{t\in\mathbb{R}:|t|<R^{2}\},

D_{R,3}:=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}:|z|<R\}\times\{t\in\mathbb{R}:|t|>R^{2}\},

and the quantities

I_{0}:=\int_{D\cap D_{R}(0)}|z|^{\beta}dzdt,

I_{i}:=\int_{D\cap D_{R,i}}|z|^{\beta}dzdt,\ \ i=1,2,3.

Clearly,
D_{R}(0)^{C}=D_{R,1}\cup D_{R,2}\cup D_{R,3}
and
I=I_{0}+I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}
. We will estimate the integrals separately.

Estimate for
I_{0}
:

(4.5)

I_{0}\leq\int_{D_{R}(0)}|z|^{\beta}dzdt=C\int_{0}^{R}\int_{0}^{R^{2}}r^{Q-3+\beta}drdt\\
=CR^{2}\frac{R^{Q-2+\beta}}{Q-2+\beta}\leq C\lambda^{2}R^{Q-2+\beta}=C\lambda^{2}|D|^{\frac{Q-2+\beta}{Q}}.

Estimate for
I_{1}
:

(4.6)

I_{1}=\int_{D\cap D_{R,1}}|z|^{\beta}dzdt\leq R^{\beta}|D|=CR^{Q+\beta}=\leq C\lambda^{2}|D|^{\frac{Q-2+\beta}{Q}}.

Estimate for
I_{2}
:

(4.7)

I_{2}=\int_{D\cap D_{R,2}}|z|^{\beta}dzdt\leq R^{\beta}|D|=CR^{Q+\beta}=\leq C\lambda^{2}|D|^{\frac{Q-2+\beta}{Q}}.

Estimate for
I_{3}
:

(4.8)

I_{3}\leq C\int_{0}^{R}\int_{0}^{\lambda^{2}}r^{Q-3+\beta}drdt\leq C\lambda^{2}\frac{R^{Q-2+\beta}}{Q-2+\beta}=C\lambda^{2}|D|^{\frac{Q-2+\beta}{Q}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Our next proposition is a fractional CKN type inequality in a bounded domain which doesn’t contain a ball around origin.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
p
,
s
,
\alpha
,
\tau
,
a
,
\gamma
and
q
be as in Theorem
1.1
satisfying the relation (
1.14
), (
1.15
) and
Q+\tau\gamma\left(1+(p-2)\frac{\alpha}{ps}\right)\geq 0
. In addition we assume
\tau\gamma\leq-2
when
Q+\tau\gamma\left(1+(p-2)\frac{\alpha}{ps}\right)\\
>0
and
\tau\gamma<-2
when
Q+\tau\gamma\left(1+(p-2)\frac{\alpha}{ps}\right)=0
. Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
such that
\Omega\cap B_{r}(0)=\phi
for some
r>0
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
which depends on
n
,
p
,
s
,
\alpha
,
r
and
\Omega
such that for
q>\frac{(1-a)\tau Q}{Q-2+\tau\gamma(p-2)\frac{\alpha}{ps}}
, we have

(4.9)

\left(\int_{\Omega}d(\xi)^{\tau\gamma}|z|^{\tau\gamma(p-2)\frac{\alpha}{ps}}|f(\xi)|^{\tau}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{\tau}}\leq C[f]_{s,p,\alpha,\Omega}^{a}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{1-a}\ \forall f\in C_{c}(\Omega).

Proof.

It is enough to prove (
4.9
) for
0\leq f\in C_{c}(\Omega)
. For
i\in\mathbb{Z},
define the following sets:

E_{i}:=\{\xi\in\Omega:f(\xi)\geq 2^{i}\},

D_{i}:=E_{i}\setminus E_{i+1}=\{\xi\in\Omega:2^{i}\leq f(\xi)<2^{i+1}\}.

Let
e_{i}=|E_{i}|
and
d_{i}=|D_{i}|
. Clearly
d_{i}\leq e_{i}
and they satisfy the conditions of Lemma
4.2
.

Relation (
1.14
) gives

(4.10)

\frac{Q-2+\tau\gamma(p-2)\frac{\alpha}{ps}}{\tau Q}=a\left(\frac{Q-ps^{\prime}-(p-2)\alpha}{pQ}\right)+\frac{1-a}{q},

where
0<s^{\prime}=s+\frac{\gamma}{a}+\frac{2}{a\tau}\leq s
and
Q-ps^{\prime}-(p-2)\alpha>0
follows from the range of
q
. We first estimate the quantity
[f]_{s^{\prime},p,\alpha,\Omega}
. Since
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, for any
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
with
j\leq i-2
, if
\xi=(z,t)\in D_{i}
and
\xi^{\prime}=(z^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in D_{j}
, then we have
2^{j}\leq f(\xi^{\prime})\leq 2^{j+1}\leq 2^{i-1}<2^{i}\leq f(\xi)\leq 2^{i+1}
, which gives
f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})\geq 2^{i-1}.
Hence

[f]_{s^{\prime},p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}\geq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\sum_{j\leq i-2}\int_{D_{i}}\int_{D_{j}}\frac{|f(\xi)-f(\xi^{\prime})|^{p}d\xi^{\prime}d\xi}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps^{\prime}}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\\
\geq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\,\sum_{j\leq i-2}2^{p(i-1)}\int_{D_{i}}\int_{D_{j}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}d\xi}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps^{\prime}}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\\
=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{p(i-1)}\int_{D_{i}}\int_{\cup_{j\leq i-2}\,D_{j}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}d\xi}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps^{\prime}}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}\\
=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{p(i-1)}\int_{D_{i}}\int_{E_{i-1}^{C}}\frac{d\xi^{\prime}d\xi}{d({\xi}^{-1}\circ\xi^{\prime})^{Q+ps^{\prime}}|z-z^{\prime}|^{(p-2)\alpha}}.

Using Lemma
4.1
we obtain

(4.11)

[f]_{s^{\prime},p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}\geq C\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z},e_{i-1}\neq 0}2^{p(i-1)}e_{i-1}^{-\frac{ps^{\prime}+(p-2)\alpha}{Q}}d_{i}.

Putting
d_{i}=e_{i}-\sum_{j\geq i+1}d_{j}
and then using Lemma
4.2
(ii) with
T=2
, we get

[f]_{s^{\prime},p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}\geq\frac{C}{2^{p}}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z},e_{i-1}\neq 0}2^{pi}e_{i}e_{i-1}^{-\frac{ps^{\prime}+(p-2)\alpha}{Q}}-\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z},e_{i-1}\neq 0}\,\sum_{j\geq i+1}2^{pi}e_{i-1}^{-\frac{ps^{\prime}+(p-2)\alpha}{Q}}d_{j}\right)\\
\geq\frac{C}{2^{p}}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z},e_{i-1}\neq 0}2^{pi}e_{i}e_{i-1}^{-\frac{ps^{\prime}+(p-2)\alpha}{Q}}-\frac{1}{2^{p}-1}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z},e_{i-1}\neq 0}2^{pi}e_{i-1}^{-\frac{ps^{\prime}+(p-2)\alpha}{Q}}d_{i}\right)\\
\geq\frac{C}{2^{p}}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z},e_{i-1}\neq 0}2^{pi}e_{i}e_{i-1}^{-\frac{ps^{\prime}+(p-2)\alpha}{Q}}-\frac{1}{2^{p}-1}[f]_{s^{\prime},p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p},\ \left[\mathrm{using}\ \mathrm{inequality}\ \eqref{3.19}\right]

which gives

[f]_{s^{\prime},p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}\geq\frac{2^{p}-1}{2^{2p}}C\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z},e_{i-1}\neq 0}2^{pi}e_{i}e_{i-1}^{-\frac{ps^{\prime}+(p-2)\alpha}{Q}}.

By Lemma
4.2
(i) with
T=2^{p}
we get

(4.12)

[f]_{s^{\prime},p,\alpha,\Omega}^{p}\geq C\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{pi}e_{i}^{\frac{Q-ps^{\prime}-(p-2)\alpha}{Q}}.

On the other hand,

(4.13)

\int_{\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{q}d\xi=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{D_{i}}|f(\xi)|^{q}d\xi\geq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{qi}d_{i}.

Now let

It follows from the assumptions that

. Therefore, from Lemma
4.3
with
\beta=\tau\gamma(p-2)\frac{\alpha}{ps},
we get

(4.14)

Relation (
4.10
) gives

(4.15)

By inequality (

) and by the fact

(4.16)

Inequality (

) and (

) together imply

\left(\int_{\Omega}|z|^{\tau\gamma(p-2)\frac{\alpha}{ps}}|f(\xi)|^{\tau}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{\tau}}\leq C[f]_{s^{\prime},p,\alpha,\Omega}^{a}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{1-a}.

Inequality (
4.9
) follows from (
4.17
) and the facts that
d(\xi)\geq r
in

and

.
∎

),

(4.27)

(1-\delta)\sum_{k=m}^{n_{0}}2^{kQ^{\prime}}|(f)_{F_{k}}|^{\tau}\leq C\left(\sum_{k=m}^{n_{0}}[f]_{s,p,\alpha,G_{k}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{a\tau}{p}}\left(\sum_{k=m}^{n_{0}}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{q}(G_{k})}^{q}\right)^{\frac{(1-a)\tau}{q}}.

Inequality (
1.16
) follows from (
4.25
) and (
4.27
) and from the facts

(4.28)

\sum_{k=m}^{n_{0}}\int_{F_{k}}f(\xi)d\xi\leq 3\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(\xi)d\xi\ \ \mathrm{and}\ \sum_{k=m}^{n_{0}}\int_{G_{k}}f(\xi)d\xi\leq 5\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(\xi)d\xi

for any non-negative function
f
.
∎

Remark 4
.

If
Q-ps-(p-2)\alpha>0
and
\tau\gamma<-2
, Theorem
1.2
can be proved directly by the technique used in
[
1
]
to prove Theorem
4
as follows:

For
0\leq f\in C_{c}(\Omega)
and for
i\in\mathbb{Z},
let us define the sets:

E_{i}:=\{\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:f(\xi)\geq 2^{i}\},

D_{i}:=E_{i}\setminus E_{i+1}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:2^{i}\leq f(\xi)<2^{i+1}\},

and let
e_{i}=|E_{i}|
and
d_{i}=|D_{i}|
. One can show that (see
[
1
]
, Section 5)

J:=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}d(\xi)^{\tau\gamma}|z|^{\tau\gamma(p-2)\frac{\alpha}{ps}}|f(\xi)|^{\tau}d\xi\leq C\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\tau i}d_{i}^{\frac{Q+\tau\left(1+\gamma(p-2)\frac{\alpha}{ps}\right)}{Q}}.

By the relation (
1.14
) and inequality (
3.12
) it follows that

(4.29)

J\leq C\left(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{pi}d_{i}^{\frac{Q-ps-(p-2)\alpha}{Q}}\right)^{\frac{a\tau}{p}}\left(\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{qi}d_{i}\right)^{\frac{(1-a)\tau}{q}}.

On the other hand, we have

(4.30)

[f]_{s,p,\alpha}^{p}\geq C\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{pi}e_{i}^{\frac{Q-ps-(p-2)\alpha}{Q}}\geq C\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{pi}d_{i}^{\frac{Q-ps-(p-2)\alpha}{Q}}.

and

(4.31)

\int_{\Omega}|f(\xi)|^{q}d\xi=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{D_{i}}|f(\xi)|^{q}d\xi\geq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{qi}d_{i}.

Inequality (
4.29
), (
4.30
) and (
4.31
) together establish (
1.18
) for the case
Q-ps-(p-2)\alpha>0
along with
\tau\gamma<-2
.

5.
Proof of the limiting case

Before proving Theorem
1.3
we discuss the following remark:

Remark 5
.

In the proof of the limiting case
1/\tau+\gamma\left(1+(p-2)\frac{\alpha}{ps}\right)/{Q}=0
we will be using Lemma
4.4
. Though the condition
\tau\gamma\leq-2
is crucial for this, it automatically follows from the assumption (
1.19
) and
(p-2)\alpha<Q-2
as follows:

Relation (
1.14
) gives

\frac{1}{\tau}=a\left(\frac{Q-(1+\gamma/as)(ps+(p-2)\alpha)}{pQ}\right)+\frac{1-a}{q}.

We have

\frac{aps}{-\tau\gamma}=a\left(\frac{Q-(1+\gamma/as)(ps+(p-2)\alpha)}{(-\gamma/as)Q}\right)+\frac{1-a}{(-\gamma/as)}\frac{p}{q}\\
=a+(1-a)\frac{p}{q}+\frac{p(1+\gamma/as)}{(-\gamma/as)}\left[a\left(\frac{Q-ps-(p-2)\alpha}{pQ}\right)+\frac{1-a}{q}\right]\\
=a+(1-a)\frac{p}{q}+\frac{p(1+\gamma/as)}{(-\gamma/as)}\left[\frac{Q+\tau\gamma\left(1+(p-2)\frac{\alpha}{ps}\right)}{\tau Q}\right].

Assumption (
1.19
) implies
\frac{aps}{-\tau\gamma}\geq a
, i.e.,
-\tau\gamma\leq ps
. Therefore we have
Q-2+\tau\gamma(p-2)\frac{\alpha}{ps}>Q-2-(p-2)\alpha>0
. This together with (
1.19
) implies
\tau\gamma<-2
.

Proof of Theorem

1.3
.

Let
A_{k}
,
F_{k}
and
G_{k}
be as defined earlier. Choose
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
2^{n_{0}}<R\leq 2^{n_{0}+1}
. From equation (
4.24
) we get

(5.1)

\int_{A_{k}}d(\xi)^{\tau\gamma}|z|^{\tau\gamma(p-2)\frac{\alpha}{ps}}|f|^{\tau}d\xi\leq C[f]_{s,p,\alpha,F_{k}}^{a\tau}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{q}(F_{k})}^{(1-a)\tau}+C|(f)_{F_{k}}|^{\tau}.

Observe that,
\ln(4R/d(\xi))\geq(n_{0}-k+1)\ln 2,\,
in
A_{k}
. Summing with respect to
k
and using (
3.12
) and (
4.28
) we obtain

(5.2)

\int_{d(\xi)>2^{m}}\frac{d(\xi)^{\tau\gamma}|z|^{\tau\gamma(p-2)\frac{\alpha}{ps}}}{\ln^{\tau}(4R/d(\xi))}|f|^{\tau}d\xi\leq C[f]_{s,p,\alpha}^{a\tau}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}^{(1-a)\tau}+C\sum_{k=m}^{n_{0}}\frac{|(f)_{F_{k}}|^{\tau}}{(n_{0}-k+1)^{\tau}}.

Here we use
\frac{1}{(n_{0}-k+1)^{\tau}}\leq 1
in the first term of right hand side. We now apply Lemma
3.2
in (
4.26
) with
c=\left(\frac{n_{0}-k+1}{n_{0}-k+\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{\tau-1}
and obtain

|(f)_{F_{k}}|^{\tau}\leq\frac{(n_{0}-k+1)^{\tau-1}}{(n_{0}-k+\frac{1}{2})^{\tau-1}}|(f)_{F_{k+1}}|^{\tau}+2^{\tau-1}(n_{0}-k+1)^{\tau-1}[f]_{s,p,\alpha,G_{k}}^{a\tau}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{q}(G_{k})}^{(1-a)\tau},

which implies

\frac{|(f)_{F_{k}}|^{\tau}}{(n_{0}-k+1)^{\tau-1}}-\frac{|(f)_{F_{k+1}}|^{\tau}}{(n_{0}-k+\frac{1}{2})^{\tau-1}}\leq C[f]_{s,p,\alpha,G_{k}}^{a\tau}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{q}(G_{k})}^{(1-a)\tau}.

Summation with respect to
k
gives

(5.3)

\sum_{k=m}^{n_{0}}\frac{|(f)_{F_{k}}|^{\tau}}{(n_{0}-k+1)^{\tau-1}}-\sum_{k=m}^{n_{0}}\frac{|(f)_{F_{k+1}}|^{\tau}}{(n_{0}-k+\frac{1}{2})^{\tau-1}}\leq C[f]_{s,p,\alpha}^{a\tau}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}^{(1-a)\tau}.

On the R.H.S we use (
3.12
) and (
4.28
). Using a change of variable in the second sum of L.H.S, we obtain

(5.4)

\frac{|(f)_{F_{m}}|^{\tau}}{(n_{0}-m+1)^{\tau-1}}+\sum_{k=m+1}^{n_{0}}\left[\frac{1}{(n_{0}-k+1)^{\tau-1}}-\frac{1}{(n_{0}-k+\frac{3}{2})^{\tau-1}}\right]|(f)_{F_{k}}|^{\tau}\\
\leq C[f]_{s,p,\alpha}^{a\tau}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}^{(1-a)\tau}.

One can observe that
\frac{1}{(n_{0}-k+1)^{\tau-1}}-\frac{1}{(n_{0}-k+\frac{3}{2})^{\tau-1}}\sim\frac{1}{(n_{0}-k+1)^{\tau}}
. Hence

(5.5)

\sum_{k=m}^{n_{0}}\frac{|(f)_{F_{k}}|^{\tau}}{(n_{0}-k+1)^{\tau}}\leq C[f]_{s,p,\alpha}^{a\tau}\lVert f\rVert_{L^{q}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}^{(1-a)\tau}.

Inequality (
1.20
) follows from (
5.2
) and (
5.5
) and by passing to the limit
m\rightarrow-\infty
.

One can prove (
1.21
) by a similar technique used in the proof of (
1.17
) and (
1.20
).

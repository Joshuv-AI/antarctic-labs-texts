Asymptotic behavior for multi-scale SDEs with monotonicity coefficients driven by Lévy processes
Yinghui Shi, Xiaobin Sun, Liqiong Wang, Yingchao Xie

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the asymptotic behavior for multi-scale stochastic differential equations driven by Lévy processes. The optimal strong convergence order 1/2 is obtained by studying the regularity estimates for the solution of Poisson equation with polynomial growth coefficients, and the optimal weak convergence order 1 is got by using the technique of Kolmogorov equation. The main contribution is that the obtained results can be applied to a class of multi-scale stochastic differential equations with monotonicity coefficients, as well as the driven processes can be the general Lévy processes, which seems new in the existing literature.

Primary 34D08, 34D25; Secondary 60H20

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

In this paper, we consider the following multi-scale stochastic system driven by Lévy processes:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle dX^{\varepsilon}_{t}=b(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})dt+\sigma(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})dW^{1}_{t}\!+\!\int_{\mathcal{Z}_{1}}h_{1}(X_{t-}^{\varepsilon},z)\tilde{N}^{1}(dz,dt),\\
dY^{\varepsilon}_{t}\!=\!\frac{1}{\varepsilon}f(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})dt\!+\!\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}g(X^{\varepsilon}_{t},Y^{\varepsilon}_{t})dW^{2}_{t}\!+\!\int_{\mathcal{Z}_{2}}h_{2}(X_{t-}^{\varepsilon},Y_{t-}^{\varepsilon},z)\tilde{N}^{2,\varepsilon}(dz,dt)\end{array}\right.

with initial values
X^{\varepsilon}_{0}=x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
Y^{\varepsilon}_{0}=y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where
{\varepsilon}
is a small and positive parameter describing the ratio of the time scale between the slow component
X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t}
and fast component
Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{t}
.
W^{1}
and
W^{2}
are independent
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
dimensional standard Wiener processes,
\tilde{N}^{1}
and
\tilde{N}^{2,{\varepsilon}}
are compound Poisson random measures with Lévy measures
\nu_{1}
and
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\nu_{2}
respectively.

The averaging principle describes the asymptotic behavior of the slow component as
{\varepsilon}
goes to
0
, i.e.,
X^{{\varepsilon}}
weakly converges to the solution of a limiting equation which usually satisfies the following form:

(1.2)

d\bar{X_{t}}=\bar{b}(\bar{X}_{t})dt+\bar{\sigma}(\bar{X}_{t})dW_{t}+\int_{\mathcal{Z}_{1}}h_{1}(\bar{X}_{t-},z)\tilde{N}^{1}(dz,dt)

with initial value
\bar{X}_{0}=x
, and
\bar{b}
and
\bar{\sigma}
are the corresponding averaged coefficients, thus
(
1.2
)
is called the averaged equation. Since the pioneer works for the averaging principle for multi-scale stochastic differential equations (SDEs) by Khasminskii
[
17
]
, and multi-scale stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) by Cerrai and Freidlin
[
6
]
, there have been various results on the averaging principle for multi-scale stochastic systems, see e.g.
[
5
,
13
,
15
,
19
,
21
,
33
,
35
]
.

In addition to the convergence of
X^{{\varepsilon}}
, one is also interested in the rate of convergence, since the rate can be used to construct the efficient numerical schemes, see e.g.
[
4
]
. Moreover, the optimal rate of convergence is also known to be very important for diffusion approximation or homogenization problems and normal derivation or central limit type theorems, see e.g.
[
16
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
28
,
34
]
. In the past several decades, there have been many results in studying the convergence rate in the averaging principle. A common strategy used to study is to apply the technique of classical Khasminskii’s time discretization, see e.g.
[
3
,
9
,
19
]
,
and the method of asymptotic expansion of solutions of Kolmogorov equations in the parameter
{\varepsilon}
is used to study the weak convergence rate, see e.g.
[
3
,
10
,
11
,
18
]
.
Recently, the technique of Poisson equation is widely used to study the optimal strong and weak convergence rates, see e.g.
[
4
,
27
]
.

The mentioned references all considered stochastic systems driven by Gaussian noise, whose solutions have continuous paths. However, solutions with discontinuous paths appears naturally in many applications. The averaging principle for stochastic system driven by jump noise has been researched by many scholars, see e.g.
[
2
,
12
,
14
,
25
,
29
,
30
,
37
,
38
]
. However, only few studies have shown the optimal convergence rate in the strong and weak sense. For example, Liu
[
20
]
obtained the optimal convergence order
1/2
in the strong sense for a class of jump-diffusion processes. Sun et.al.
[
32
]
obtained the optimal strong convergence order
1-1/\alpha
and weak convergence order
1
for a class of SDEs driven by
\alpha
-stable processes, where
\alpha\in(1,2)
. Sun and Xie
[
31
]
obtained the optimal strong convergence order
1-1/\alpha
and weak convergence order
1-r
(for any
r\in(0,1)
) for a class of SPDEs driven by
\alpha
-stable processes, where
\alpha\in(1,2)
.

The main purpose of this paper focuses on studying the optimal strong and weak convergence rates for stochastic system
(
1.1
)
. More precisely, for any initial value
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
T>0
,
p>1
and small enough
{\varepsilon}>0
, under some proper conditions on the coefficients and
\sigma(x,y)\equiv\sigma(x)
, we have

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|X_{t}^{{\varepsilon}}-\bar{X}_{t}|^{p}\right)\leqslant C{\varepsilon}^{p/2},

where
C
is a constant depending on
T,|x|,|y|,p
and
\bar{X}
is the solution of the corresponding averaged equation (see
(
3.23
)
below).
Furthermore, for some proper function
\phi
, we obtain

(1.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\mathbb{E}\phi(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t})-\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}_{t})|\leqslant C{\varepsilon},

where
\bar{X}
is the solution of another averaged equation (see
(
5.14
)
below). This means that the strong and weak convergence orders are
1/2
and
1
respectively, which are the optimal convergence orders.

It is worth pointing out an independent interest of the paper is that the coefficient
b(x,y)
satisfies the monotonicity condition and polynomial growth with respect to
x
and
y
respectively, such as the form
b(x,y)=-|x|^{2}x+x+|y|^{2}y
(see Example 2.7 below for detailed discussion). As far as we know, there seems to be few results on this topic, even in the case of the Wiener noises. Compare with
[
21
]
, where Liu. et. al. have proved the strong averaging principle holds without the convergence order for a class of SDEs with locally Lipschitz coefficients, we here achieve the optimal strong convergence order 1/2. Compare with
[
8
]
, where Crisan et. al. study the optimal weak convergence order
1
in the sense of
uniform in time
for multi-scale SDEs with locally-Lipschitz coefficients, we here obtain the optimal strong convergence order
1/2
and the driving noises can be the general Lévy processes. To the best of our knowledge, it seems the first result about the optimal strong convergence rate for SDEs with monotonicity coefficients.

1.2.
Main techniques for the proofs

For reading convenience, we are in a position to show the main techniques of the proofs.

Techniques for strong convergence:
Firstly, using the monotonicity condition, one can get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|X^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\bar{X}_{t}|^{p}\right)\leqslant\!\!C_{p,T}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\int^{t}_{0}\langle X_{s}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}_{s},b(X^{\varepsilon}_{s},Y^{\varepsilon}_{s})-\bar{b}(X^{\varepsilon}_{s})\rangle ds\right|^{p/2}\right].

In order to prove
(
1.3
)
, the key step is how to deal with the term
b(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s},Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})-\bar{b}(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})
. To do this, we will use the technique of Poisson equation. Roughly speaking,
b(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s},Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})-\bar{b}(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})
can be replaced by
-\mathscr{L}_{2}(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})\Phi(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s},\cdot)(Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})
, i.e., considering the following Poisson equation:

-\mathscr{L}_{2}(x)\Phi(x,\cdot)(y)=b(x,y)-\bar{b}(x),

where
\mathscr{L}_{2}(x)
is the generator of the frozen equation (
2.13
) for fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then applying Itô’s formula on
\langle\Phi(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t},Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{t}),X_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}_{t}\rangle
, one would obtain the new term

\int^{t}_{0}\langle X_{s}^{\varepsilon}-\bar{X}_{s},\mathscr{L}_{2}(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})\Phi(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{s},\cdot)(Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})\rangle ds

has an expression in terms of the solution
\Phi
to the Poisson equation (see
(
5.8
)
below), thus the remaining works are devoted to studying the regularity estimates of the solution
\Phi
. However, for the purpose of covering some cases of the polynomial growth coefficients, this paper is devoted to studying the regularity estimates for the solution of the corresponding Poisson equation with polynomial growth coefficients.

Techniques for weak convergence:
We shall use the technique based on a combination of Kolmogorov equation and Poisson equation to prove
(
1.4
)
. Roughly speaking, we first introduce the Kolmogorov equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\bar{\mathscr{L}}_{1}u(t,x),\quad t\geqslant 0,\\
u(0,x)=\phi(x),\end{array}\right.

where
\bar{\mathscr{L}}_{1}
is the generator of the transition semigroup of the averaged equation
(
5.14
)
. For fixed
t>0
, denote

\tilde{u}^{t}(s,x):=u(t-s,x),\quad s\in[0,t].

Then we observe that

\tilde{u}^{t}(t,X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t})=\phi(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t}),\quad\tilde{u}^{t}(0,x)=\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}^{x}_{t}).

Therefore, using Itô’s formula on
\tilde{u}^{t}(t,X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t})
and taking expectation on both sides (see
(
5.31
)
below), one would get

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\phi(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t})-\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}_{t})=\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\left[F^{t}(s,x,y)-\bar{F}^{t}(s,x)\right]ds,

where

\displaystyle F^{t}(s,x,y):=\langle b(x,y),\partial_{x}\tilde{u}^{t}(s,x)\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}\big[\sigma\sigma^{*}(x,y)\partial_{x}^{2}\tilde{u}^{t}(s,x)\big]

and
\bar{F}^{t}(s,x)
is the corresponding averaged coefficient. In order to prove
(
1.5
)
, we consider another Poisson equation:

-\mathscr{L}_{2}(x)\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,x,\cdot)(y)=F^{t}(s,x,y)-\bar{F}^{t}(s,x).

The remaining proof returns to the previous procedure for applying the technique of Poisson equation.

1.3.
Organization

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give some notations and under some suitable assumptions, we formulate our main results and give several concrete examples to illustrate the applicability of our main results. Section 3 is devoted to giving some a priori estimates of the solution
(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t},Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{t})
and studying the frozen and averaged equations. We study the regularity estimates of the solution of Poisson equation in Section 4. The details proofs of the strong convergence and weak convergence are given in Sections 5.1 and 5.2 respectively. In last section is the appendix, where we give detailed proofs about the differentiability of solution to the frozen equation, and the well-posedness of the Kolmogorov equation for the averaged equation.

Throughout this paper,
C
,
C_{T}
and
C_{p,T}
stand for constants whose value may change from line to line, and
C_{T}
and
C_{p,T}
is used to emphasize that the constant depend on
T
and
p,T
respectively.

Main results

(

)

\displaystyle\Big|P^{x}_{t}\varphi(y)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y^{\prime})\mu^{x}(d{y^{\prime}})\Big|=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi(Y_{t}^{x,y})-\varphi(Y_{t}^{x,{y^{\prime}}})\right]\mu^{x}(dy^{\prime})\Big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{Lip}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathbb{E}|Y_{t}^{x,y}-Y_{t}^{x,{y^{\prime}}}|\mu^{x}(dy^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{Lip}e^{-\frac{\gamma}{\ell}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}|y-y^{\prime}|\mu^{x}(dy^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\|\varphi\|_{Lip}(1+|y|)e^{-\frac{\gamma}{\ell}t},

where
\|\varphi\|_{Lip}:=\sup_{x\neq y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{|x-y|}
. Now, let
\nu^{x}
be another invariant measure, by the same argument above, we have for any
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y^{\prime})\nu^{x}(d{y^{\prime}})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y^{\prime})\mu^{x}(d{y^{\prime}})\Big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\Big|P^{x}_{t}\varphi(y)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y^{\prime})\nu^{x}(d{y^{\prime}})\Big|+\Big|P^{x}_{t}\varphi(y)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(y^{\prime})\mu^{x}(d{y^{\prime}})\Big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle C\|\varphi\|_{Lip}(1+|y|)e^{-\frac{\gamma}{\ell}t}.

Taking
t\rightarrow\infty
and by the arbitrariness of the
\varphi
, we have
\mu^{x}=\nu^{x}
. Hence
\mu^{x}
is the unique invariant measure.

Furthermore, by the definition of invariant measure
\mu^{x}
,
(
2.2
)
, Lemmas
3.3
and (
3.22
), we have

\displaystyle\big|\mathbb{E}b(x,Y_{t}^{x,y})-\bar{b}(x)\big|\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}b(x,Y_{t}^{x,y})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}b(x,z)\mu^{x}(dz)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathbb{E}\left|b(x,Y^{x,y}_{t})-b(x,Y^{x,z}_{t})\right|\mu^{x}(dz)

\displaystyle\leqslant C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\big[\mathbb{E}\big(1+|Y^{x,y}_{t}|^{2k}+|Y^{x,z}_{t}|^{2k}\big)\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\mathbb{E}\left|Y^{x,y}_{t}-Y^{x,z}_{t}\right|^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{x}(dz)

\displaystyle\leqslant Ce^{-\frac{\gamma t}{\ell}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}(1+|y|^{k}+|z|^{k})|y-z|\mu^{x}(dz)

\displaystyle\leqslant Ce^{-\frac{\gamma t}{\ell}}(1+|y|^{k+1}).

The proof is complete.
∎

4.

\displaystyle=\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(0,x,y)+\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\partial_{s}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,X_{s}^{{\varepsilon}},Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})ds

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\mathscr{L}_{1}(Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,\cdot,Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})(X_{s}^{{\varepsilon}})ds+\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\mathscr{L}_{2}(X_{s}^{{\varepsilon}})\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,X_{s}^{{\varepsilon}},\cdot)(Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})ds,

which implies

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\mathscr{L}_{2}(X_{s}^{{\varepsilon}})\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,X_{s}^{{\varepsilon}},\cdot)(Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})ds

(5.37)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle{\varepsilon}\big[\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(0,x,y)-\mathbb{E}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(t,X_{t}^{{\varepsilon}},Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{t})+\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\partial_{s}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,X_{s}^{{\varepsilon}},\cdot)(Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})ds

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\mathscr{L}_{1}(Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,\cdot,Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})(X_{s}^{{\varepsilon}})ds\big].

Combining
(
5.31
)
,
(
5.32
)
and
(
5.37
)
, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\mathbb{E}\phi(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t})-\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}_{t})\right|=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\left[F^{t}(s,X_{s}^{{\varepsilon}},Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})-\bar{F}^{t}(s,X_{s}^{{\varepsilon}})\right]ds\right|

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\mathscr{L}_{2}(X_{s}^{{\varepsilon}})\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,X_{s}^{{\varepsilon}},\cdot)(Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})ds\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle{\varepsilon}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(0,x,y)|+\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\mathbb{E}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(t,X_{t}^{{\varepsilon}},Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{t})\right|

\displaystyle+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\left|\partial_{s}\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,X_{s}^{{\varepsilon}},Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})\right|ds.

\displaystyle+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}\int^{t}_{0}\left|\mathscr{L}_{1}(Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})\tilde{\Phi}^{t}(s,\cdot,Y^{{\varepsilon}}_{s})(X_{s}^{{\varepsilon}})\right|ds\Big].

Finally, using
(
5.33
)
-
(
5.36

, we obtain for some

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\left|\mathbb{E}\phi(X^{{\varepsilon}}_{t})-\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}_{t})\right|\leqslant C_{T}\left(1+|x|^{k_{2}}+|y|^{k_{2}}\right){\varepsilon}.

The proof is complete.

Proposition 6.3

Under the assumptions
1

3
and
1
-
3
. For any

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\bar{\mathscr{L}}_{1}u(t,x),\quad t\geqslant 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\\

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\int_{\mathcal{Z}_{1}}\left[\phi(x+h_{1}(x,z))\!-\!\phi(x)\!-\!\langle D\phi(x),h_{1}(x,z)\rangle\right]\nu_{1}(dz).

Existence:
Using Itô’s formula, it is easy to see
u(t,x)=\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}^{x}_{t})
is differentiable with respect to
t
. Moreover, using the chain rule and
\phi\in C^{2}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it is easy to see
u(t,x)
is first and second differentiable with respect to
x
. Hence
u\in C^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In order to prove
u(t,x)
solves equation
(
6.11
)
, we use the definition of generator
\bar{\mathscr{L}}_{1}
, more precisely, by the Markov property and homogeneous property, we have for any
s>0

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}u(t,\bar{X}^{x}_{s})-u(t,x)}{s}=\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}^{y}_{t})|_{y=\bar{X}^{x}_{s}}\right]-\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}^{x}_{t})}{s}

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}^{x}_{t+s})|\mathscr{F}_{s}\right]-\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}^{x}_{t})}{s}

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}^{x}_{t+s})-\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}^{x}_{t})}{s}

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{u(t+s,x)-u(t,x)}{s}.

Then letting

(
6.11
)
.

Let

6.11

Then using Itô’s formula on
\tilde{w}(t,\bar{X}^{x}_{t})
and taking expectation, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\tilde{w}(t,\bar{X}^{x}_{t})=\!\!\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\tilde{w}(0,x)+\int^{t}_{0}\left[\partial_{s}\tilde{w}(s,\bar{X}^{x}_{s})+\bar{\mathscr{L}}_{1}\tilde{w}(s,\bar{X}^{x}_{s})\right]ds=\tilde{w}(0,x).

\tilde{w}

\mathbb{E}\tilde{w}(t,\bar{X}^{x}_{t})=\mathbb{E}\phi(\bar{X}^{x}_{t}),\quad\tilde{w}(0,x)=w(t,x).

Hence, we obtain
w(t,x)=u(t,x)
.

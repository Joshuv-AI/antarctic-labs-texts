Central limit theorems for heat equation with time-independent noise: the regular and rough cases
Raluca M. Balan, Wangjun Yuan

Abstract
In this article, we investigate the asymptotic behaviour of the spatial integral of the solution to the parabolic Anderson model with time independent noise in dimension d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 , as the domain of the integral becomes large. We consider 3 cases: (a) the case when the noise has an integrable covariance function; (b) the case when the covariance of the noise is given by the Riesz kernel; (c) the case of the rough noise, i.e. fractional noise with index H ∈ ( 1 4 , 1 2 ) H\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2}) in dimension d = 1 d=1 . In each case, we identify the order of magnitude of the variance of the spatial integral, we prove a quantitative central limit theorem for the normalized spatial integral by estimating its total variation distance to a standard normal distribution, and we give the corresponding functional limit result.



Primary 60H15; Secondary 60H07, 60G15, 60F05

Keywords:
parabolic Anderson model, spatially homogeneous Gaussian noise, rough noise, Malliavin calculus, Stein’s method for normal approximations

1
Introduction

In the recent years, there has been a lot of interest in investigating the asymptotic properties of the spatial average of the solution to the stochastic heat and wave equations driven by a Gaussian noise, which is white or colored in space and time. This line of investigation was initiated in the seminal article
[
16
]
which considered the case of the heat equation driven by a space-time white noise in dimension
d=1
, with initial condition 1 and non-linear factor
\sigma(u)
multiplying the noise, for a Lipschitz function
\sigma
.
In
[
16
]
, the authors proved that for any fixed time
t>0
, the
spatial average of the solution
u
:

F_{R}(t)=\int_{-R}^{R}u(t,x)dx

has approximately a normal distribution, as
R\to\infty
. More precisely, these authors showed that the variance
\sigma_{R}^{2}(t):=\mathbb{E}[F_{R}^{2}(t)]\sim CR
as
R\to\infty
, and estimated that the total variation distance (
d_{TV}
) between
F_{R}(t)/\sigma_{R}(t)
and
Z\sim N(0,1)
is of the order
R^{-1/2}
, a result which is known in the literature as the
quantitative central limit theorem
(QCLT). This was achieved by using the powerful methods of Malliavin calculus, combined with Stein’s method for normal approximations, an area which is explored in depth in the monograph
[
20
]
. Since then, there has been a steady flow of new contributions to this area, which includes the present article.
We review below some of the most important contributions in this area.

The case of the heat equation with white noise in time, Lipschitz function
\sigma
, and spatial covariance given by the Riesz kernel
\gamma(x)=|x|^{-\beta}
for
\beta\in(0,d\wedge 2)
was studied in
[
17
]
, where it was proved that
\sigma_{R}^{2}(t)\sim CR^{2d-\beta}
and
d_{TV}\leq CR^{-\beta/2}
. When the noise is colored in time, the situation is more complex, and even the basic question of existence of solution remains an open problem for general functions
\sigma
, except in the linear case
\sigma(u)=u
(known as the
parabolic Anderson model
).
The problem of QCLT for this model with fractional noise in time with index
H_{0}>1/2
was considered in
[
26
]
in two cases: (a)
\gamma\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and (b)
\gamma(x)=x^{-\beta}
for
\beta\in(0,d\wedge 2)
. It was shown there that
\sigma_{R}^{2}(t)\sim CR^{d}
in case (a), and
\sigma_{R}^{2}(t)\sim CR^{2d-\beta}
in case (b). The method of
[
26
]
uses the multivariate chaotic central limit theorem, which yields the normal approximation, but does not give the rate of the convergence. This rate was obtained in the recent preprint
[
24
]
, where it was shown that

d_{TV}\leq CR^{-d/2}
in case (a) and
d_{TV}\leq CR^{-\beta/2}
in case (b), using a different method based on an improved version of the second-order Gaussian Poincaré inequality due to
[
28
]
, which was used for the first time in this context in
[
5
]
. In addition,
[
24
]
considers the more difficult case (c) of the fractional noise in space with index
H<1/2
(in dimension
d=1
), which is also fractional in time with index
H_{0}>1/2
satisfying
H_{0}+H>3/4
. In this case, the authors discovered the surprising rates

\sigma_{R}^{2}(t)\sim CR
and
d_{TV}\leq CR^{-1/2}
, which show that once we descend below the value
1/2
, the spatial index
H
does not have any impact on the rates in the QCLT. In the present article, using similar methods to
[
24
]
, we consider the QCLT problem for the parabolic Anderson model with time-independent noise (which corresponds formally to the case
H_{0}=1)
, and spatial covariance given by cases (a),(b),(c) above.
In addition, we prove the corresponding functional limit result.

The case of the wave equation in dimension
d=1
with white noise in time and non-linear factor
\sigma(u)
multiplying the noise (for a Lipschitz function
\sigma
) was studied in
[
13
]
in the case of fractional noise in space with index
H\geq 1/2
, where it was shown that
\sigma_{R}^{2}(t)\sim CR^{2H}
and
d_{TV}\leq CR^{H-1}
. The extension to the case
d=2
was done in
[
26
]
and
[
8
]
in cases (a), respectively (b) mentioned above, assuming in addition that
\gamma\in L^{\ell}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
\ell>1
in case (a). More precisely, it was shown in
[
8
]
that
\sigma_{R}^{2}(t)\sim CR^{d}
and
d_{TV}\leq CR^{-1}
in case (a), and
\sigma_{R}^{2}(t)\sim CR^{4-\beta}
and
d_{TV}\leq CR^{-\beta/2}
in case (b). Similarly to the heat equation, for the wave equation with colored noise in time, the existence of solution is an open problem, except in the case
\sigma(u)=u
(known as the
hyperbolic Anderson model
). The QCLT problem for this model with colored noise in time was considered in
[
5
]
in dimension
d\leq 2
, in cases (a) and (b) above: in case (a),

\sigma_{R}^{2}(t)\sim CR^{d}
and
d_{TV}\leq CR^{-d/2}
, while in case (b),
\sigma_{R}^{2}(t)\sim CR^{2d-\beta}
and
d_{TV}\leq CR^{-\beta/2}
. The same problem for the time-independent noise has been considered in the recent preprint
[
6
]
.

After this brief literature review, we explain now the problem considered in the present article. We are interested in the parabolic Anderson model with time-independent noise:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t}(t,x)=\dfrac{1}{2}\Delta u(t,x)+u(t,x)\dot{W}(x),\ t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},(d\geq 1)\\
u(0,x)=1.\end{cases}

(1)

The noise is given by a zero-mean Gaussian process
\{W(\varphi);\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
, defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, with covariance

\mathbb{E}[W(\varphi)W(\psi)]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}\psi(\xi)}\mu(d\xi)=:\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{P}_{0}},

where
\mu
is a tempered measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, called the spectral measure of the noise. Here
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the space of infinitely differentiable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with compact support, and
\mathcal{F}\varphi(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\varphi(x)dx
is the Fourier transform of
\varphi
.

We let
\mathcal{P}_{0}
be the Hilbert space defined as completion of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with respect to the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{P}_{0}}
. By the isometry property, the noise can be extended to an isonormal Gaussian process
\{W(\varphi);\varphi\in\mathcal{P}_{0}\}
, as defined in Malliavin calculus.

We say that the noise is white if
\mu(d\xi)=(2\pi)^{-d}d\xi
. In this case,
\mathcal{P}_{0}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{P}_{0}}=\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.

We are interested in two cases:

(I)

(
the regular case
)
\gamma=\mathcal{F}\mu
is a non-negative locally-integrable function and

\mu
satisfies Dalang’s condition:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)<\infty,

(D)

or the noise is white and
d=1
;

(II)

(
the rough case
)
d=1
and
\mu(d\xi)=c_{H}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi
for some
H\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2})
, where

c_{H}=\frac{\Gamma(2H+1)\sin(\pi H)}{2\pi}.

(2)

Alternatively, the inner product in
\mathcal{P}_{0}
can be represented as follows: in Case
(I)
,

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{P}_{0}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\gamma(x-y)\varphi(x)\psi(y)dxdy,

with the convention
\gamma=\delta_{0}
if the noise is white; in Case
(II)
,

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{P}_{0}}=C_{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)\big)\big(\psi(x)-\psi(y)\big)|x-y|^{2H-2}dxdy,

(3)

where
C_{H}=\frac{H(1-2H)}{2}
. We say that (
3
) is the
Gagliardo representation
.

A typical example of a function
\gamma
as in Case
(I)
is the Riesz kernel:

\gamma(x)=|x|^{-\beta}\quad\mbox{for some}\quad\beta\in(0,d).

In this case,
\mu(d\xi)=C_{d,\beta}|\xi|^{-(d-\beta)}d\xi
, and (D) holds if and only if
\beta<2
.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t>0
, let
p_{t}(x)=(2\pi t)^{-d/2}\exp(-|x|^{2}/(2t))
be the heat kernel, where
|\cdot|
is the Euclidean norm. Note that the Fourier transform of
p_{t}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}p_{t}(\xi)=\exp\left(-\frac{t|\xi|^{2}}{2}\right),\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

A process
u=u(t,x);t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
is called a (Skorohod)
solution
of (
1
) if it satisfies the following integral equation:

\displaystyle u(t,x)=1+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p_{t-s}(x-y)u(s,y)W(\delta y)ds,

(4)

where
W(\delta y)
denotes the Skorokod integral with respect to
W
. The definition of this integral is given in Section
2
below.

We are interested in the asymptotic behavior as
R\to\infty
of the spatial integral:

F_{R}(t)=\int_{B_{R}}\big(u(t,x)-1\big)dx,

where
B_{R}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d};|x|<R\}
. We let
\omega_{d}
be the Lebesque measure of
B_{1}
. We denote

\sigma_{R}^{2}(t)={\rm Var}\big(F_{R}(t)\big)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rho_{t,s}(x-y)=\mathbb{E}[(u(t,x)-1)(u(s,y)-1)].

We consider the following assumptions:

Assumption A.
(
integrable kernel
)
\gamma=\mathcal{F}\mu
is a non-negative function,
\gamma\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mu
satisfies condition (D), or
d=1
and the noise is white.

Assumption B.
(
Riesz kernel
)
\gamma(x)=|x|^{-\beta}
for some
\beta\in(0,d\wedge 2)
.

Assumption C.
(
rough noise
)
d=1
and
\mu(d\xi)=c_{H}|\xi|^{1-2H}d\xi
for some
H\in(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2})
, where the constant
c_{H}
is given by (
2
).

We write
f(R)\sim g(R)
if
f(R)/g(R)\to 1
as
R\to\infty
.

The main results of this article are summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

(i)
(Limiting Covariance)
For any
t>0
and
s>0
,

\mathbb{E}[F_{R}(t)F_{R}(s)]\sim\begin{cases}R^{d}K(t,s)&\mbox{under Assumptions A or C}\\
R^{2d-\beta}K(t,s)&\mbox{under Assumption B}\end{cases}

where

K(t,s)=\begin{cases}\omega_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{t,s}(z)dz&\mbox{under Assumption A}\\
ts\int_{B_{1}^{2}}|x-x^{\prime}|^{-\beta}dxdx^{\prime}&\mbox{under Assumption B}\\
2\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}[e^{\mathcal{I}_{t,s}^{1,2}(z)}-\mathcal{I}_{t,s}^{1,2}(z)-1]dz&\mbox{under Assumption C}\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{I}_{t,s}^{1,2}(z)
is defined by (
94
) below; in particular,

\sigma_{R}^{2}(t)\sim R^{d}K(t,t)
under Assumptions A or C, and

\sigma_{R}^{2}(t)\sim R^{2d-\beta}K(t,t)
under Assumption B.

(ii)
(Quantitative Central Limit Theorem)
For any
t>0
,

d_{\rm TV}\left(\frac{F_{R}(t)}{\sigma_{R}(t)},Z\right)\leq\begin{cases}C_{t}R^{-d/2}&\mbox{under Assumptions A or C},\\
C_{t}R^{-\beta/2}&\mbox{under Assumption B}\end{cases}

where
Z\sim N(0,1)
and
C_{t}>0
is a constant depending on
t
.

(iii)
(Functional Central Limit Theorem)
The process
\{Q_{R}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
given by:

Q_{R}(t)=\begin{cases}R^{-d/2}F_{R}(t)&\mbox{under Assumptions A or C},\\
R^{-d+\beta/2}F_{R}(t)&\mbox{under Assumption B}\end{cases}

has a continuous modification which converges in distribution in
C[0,\infty)
as
R\to\infty
, to a zero-mean Gaussian process
\{\mathcal{G}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
with covariance

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}(t)\mathcal{G}(s)]=K(t,s)
.

This article is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give some preliminaries about Malliavin calculus. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is given Section 3 in the regular case (under Assumptions A or B), respectively Section 4 in the rough case (under Assumption C). In each case, we prove the existence of (Skorohod) solution, and we give some estimates on its Malliavin derivatives of first and second order, which play a key role in our developments. The appendices contain some auxiliary results needed in the sequel.

2
Malliavin Calculus Preliminaries

In this section, we include some preliminaries on Malliavin calculus and we establish the existence and uniqueness of the solution to (
1
).

Since
W=\{W(\varphi);\varphi\in\mathcal{P}_{0}\}
is an isonormal Gaussian process,
every square-integrable random variable
F

which is measurable with respect to
W
has the Wiener chaos expansion:

F=E(F)+\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}(f_{n})\quad\mbox{for some}\quad f_{n}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n},

(5)

where
\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}
is the

n
-th tensor product of
\mathcal{P}_{0}
and
I_{n}

is the multiple Wiener integral with respect to
W
.
By the orthogonality of the Wiener chaos spaces,

E[I_{n}(f)I_{m}(g)]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n!\,\langle\widetilde{f},\widetilde{g}\rangle_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}&\mbox{if $n=m$}\\
0&\mbox{if $n\not=m$}\end{array}\right.

where
\widetilde{f}
is the symmetrization of
f
in all
n

variables:

\widetilde{f}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\rho\in S_{n}}f(x_{\rho(1)},\ldots,x_{\rho(n)}),

and
S_{n}
is the set of all permutations of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. It can be proved that:

\|\widetilde{f}\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}},

(6)

If
F
has the chaos expansion (
5
), then

E|F|^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 0}E|I_{n}(f_{n})|^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 0}n!\,\|\widetilde{f}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the class of “smooth” random variables, i.e variables of the form

F=f(W(\varphi_{1}),\ldots,W(\varphi_{n})),

(7)

where
f\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\varphi_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
,
n\geq 1
, and

C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the class of bounded
C^{\infty}
-functions
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, whose partial derivatives of all orders are bounded. The

Malliavin derivative
of
F
of the form (
7
) is the

\mathcal{P}_{0}
-valued random variable given by:

DF:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(W(\varphi_{1}),\ldots,W(\varphi_{n}))\varphi_{i}.

We endow
\mathcal{S}
with the norm

\|F\|_{\mathbb{D}^{1,2}}:=(E|F|^{2})^{1/2}+(E\|DF\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}}^{2})^{1/2}
. The
operator
D
can be extended to the space
\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
, the
completion of
\mathcal{S}
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{D}^{1,2}}
.

The
divergence operator

\delta
is the adjoint of
the operator
D
. The domain of
\delta
, denoted by
\mbox{Dom}\ \delta
, is the set of
u\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{P}_{0})
such that

|E\langle DF,u\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|\leq c(E|F|^{2})^{1/2},\quad\forall F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2},

where
c
is a constant depending on
u
. If
u\in{\rm Dom}\ \delta
, then
\delta(u)
is the element of
L^{2}(\Omega)

characterized by the following duality relation:

E(F\delta(u))=E\langle DF,u\rangle_{\mathcal{P}_{0}},\quad\forall F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}.

(8)

In particular,
E(\delta(u))=0
. If
u\in\mbox{Dom}\ \delta
, we
use the notation

\delta(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u(x)W(\delta x),

and we say that
\delta(u)
is the
Skorohod integral
of
u

with respect to
W
.

If
F
has the chaos expansion (
5
), we define the
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck generator

LF=\sum_{n\geq 1}nI_{n}(f_{n})

provided that the series converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
It can be proved that

F\in{\rm Dom}\ L
if and only if
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
and
DF\in{\rm Dom}\ \delta
; in this case,
LF=-\delta(DF)
.
The pseudo-inverse
L^{-1}
of
L
is defined by

L^{-1}F=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}I_{n}(f_{n}).

For any
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
with
\mathbb{E}(F)=0
, the process
u=-DL^{-1}F
belongs to
{\rm Dom}\ \delta

and

F=\delta(-DL^{-1}F).

(9)

(see e.g. Proposition 6.5.1 of
[
22
]
).

We return now to our problem.

If the solution exists, it should be given by the series

\displaystyle u(t,x)=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}(f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)),

(10)

where the kernel
f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)
is given by

\displaystyle f_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x;t)=\int_{T_{n}(t)}p_{t-t_{n}}(x-x_{n})\ldots p_{t_{2}-t_{1}}(x_{2}-x_{1})dt_{1}\ldots dt_{n},

and
T_{n}(t)=\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}):0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}<t\}
. The necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of the solution is that this series converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, i.e.

\sum_{n\geq 1}n!\|\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}<\infty.

(11)

Under this condition, the solution is unique, and

\mathbb{E}|u(t,x)|^{2}=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}n!\|\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}.

3
The Regular Case

In this section we consider Case (I) (the regular case). One of the most important properties which is specific to this is case is a maximal principle:
if
\gamma=\mathcal{F}\mu
is a non-negative and non-negative-definite function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\mu
satisfies Dalang’s condition (
D
), then

\displaystyle\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}h(\xi+\eta)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}h(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi),

(12)

for any non-negative function
h
such that
h\in\mathcal{P}_{0}\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
or
\mathcal{F}h\geq 0
(see Lemma 2.6 of
[
5
]
and Lemma 3.6 of
[
11
]
). In particular, this holds for
h(t)=e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}
for any
t>0
.

3.1
Existence of Solution

In this section, we examine the question of existence of solution.

For any
t>0
, we denote
h_{0}(t)=1
and

\displaystyle h_{n}(t)=\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{-(t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\xi_{j}|^{2}}\mu(d\xi_{1})\ldots\mu(d\xi_{n})d\boldsymbol{t}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad n\geq 1,

(13)

where
\boldsymbol{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
and we let
t_{n+1}=t
.
We recall the following result.

Lemma 3.1

(Lemma 3.8 of
[
2
]
)
.

Under condition (D), for any
t>0
and
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle H(t;\gamma):=\sum_{n\geq 0}\gamma^{n}h_{n}(t)<\infty\quad\mbox{and}\quad\widetilde{H}(t;\gamma):=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sqrt{\gamma^{n}h_{n}(t)}<\infty.

By Lemma 2.6 of
[
10
]
,
h_{n}
is non-decreasing for any
n\geq 1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that
\gamma
is non-negative and non-negative definite and the spectral measure
\mu
satisfies (D), or the noise is white. Then for any
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}\leq\frac{t^{n}}{n!}h_{n}(t).

(14)

Consequently, equation (
1
) has a unique solution
u
, and for any
p\geq 2
and
T>0
,

\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|u(t,x)\|_{p}<\infty.

(15)

Proof.

The Fourier transform of
f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)
is given by:

\mathcal{F}f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})=e^{-i(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n})\cdot x}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}|^{2}}d\boldsymbol{t},

where
\boldsymbol{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
and
t_{n+1}=t
.
By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\|f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}|\mathcal{F}f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})|^{2}\mu(d\xi_{1})\ldots\mu(d\xi_{n})\leq\frac{t^{n}}{n!}J_{n}(t),

(16)

where

J_{n}(t)=\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{-(t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}|^{2}}\mu(d\xi_{1})\ldots\mu(d\xi_{n})d\boldsymbol{t}.

(17)

By the maximal principle (
12
),

J_{n}(t)\leq h_{n}(t).

(18)

Relation (
14
) follows.
By the rough bound (
6
) and Lemma
3.1
, we have:

\sum_{n\geq 1}n!\|\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}n!\|f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}t^{n}J_{n}(t)\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}t^{n}h_{n}(t)=H(t,t)<\infty.

This proves the existence and uniqueness of the solution. By Minkowski’s inequality and hypercontractivity,

\|u(t,x)\|_{p}\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}(p-1)^{n/2}(n!)^{1/2}\|\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}(p-1)^{n/2}t^{n/2}\sqrt{h_{n}(t)}=\widetilde{H}(t;(p-1)t).

The last term is bounded by
\widetilde{H}(T;(p-1)T)
, for any
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
∎

Remark 3.3

(Comparison with the white noise in time)
.

Consider the parabolic Anderson model with Gaussian noise
\mathfrak{X}
which is white noise in time and has the same spatial covariance structure as
W
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial t}(t,x)=\Delta v(t,x)+v(t,x)\dot{\mathfrak{X}}(t,x),\ t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
v(0,x)=1,\end{cases}

(19)

More precisely,
\mathfrak{X}=\{\mathfrak{X}(\varphi);\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
is a zero-mean Gaussian process with covariance

\mathbb{E}[\mathfrak{X}(\varphi)\mathfrak{X}(\psi)]=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\gamma(x-y)\varphi(t,x)\psi(t,y)dxdydt=:\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{0}},

We let
\mathcal{H}_{0}
be the completion of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
with respect to the inner product
\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{0}
. Then
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is isomorphic to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathcal{P}_{0})
.
If (D) holds, equation (
19
) has a unique solution which has the chaos expansion:

v(t,x)=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}^{\mathfrak{X}}(f_{n}(\cdot,t,x))

where
I_{n}^{\mathfrak{X}}
is the multiple integral with respect to
\mathfrak{X}
and the kernel
f_{n}(\cdot,t,x)
is given by

f_{n}(t_{1},x_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{n},t,x)=p_{t-t_{n}}(x-x_{n})\ldots p_{t_{2}-t_{1}}(x_{2}-x_{1})1_{\{0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}<t\}}.

It can be proved that
\|f_{n}(\cdot,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}=J_{n}(t)
.
Using (
18
), followed by Lemma 3.3 of
[
15
]
, we obtain the following inequality:

\|f_{n}(\cdot,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}\leq h_{n}(t)\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom{n}{\ell}\frac{C_{N}^{n-\ell}}{\ell!}(tD_{N})^{\ell},

(20)

for any
N>0
, where

C_{N}=\int_{|\xi|>N}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)\quad\mbox{and}\quad D_{N}=\int_{\{|\xi|\leq N\}}\mu(d\xi).

Note that
\mathbb{E}|v(t,x)|^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 0}J_{n}(t)
, whereas
\mathbb{E}|u(t,x)|^{2}\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}t^{n}J_{n}(t)
.

3.2
Estimates on the Malliavin derivatives

In this subsection, we give the chaos expansions for the first and second Malliavin derivatives of the solution to (
1
). For each of these derivatives, we establish a moment estimate which shows that the first term in the chaos expansion dominates the rest.

We begin with the first Malliavin derivative. We fix
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We will prove that for any
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have the following chaos expansion:

D_{z}u(t,x)=\sum_{n\geq 1}nI_{n-1}(\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,z,x;t))=:\sum_{n\geq 1}A_{n}(z,x;t)\quad\mbox{in $L^{2}(\Omega)$}.

(21)

In order to do this, we first note that, as in the case of the wave equation (studied in
[
6
]
), we have the decomposition:

\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,z,x;t)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}h_{j}^{(n)}(\cdot,z,x;t),

(22)

where
h_{j}^{(n)}(\cdot,z,x;t)
is the symmetrization of the function
f_{j}^{(n)}(\cdot,z,x;t)
given by:

\displaystyle f_{j}^{(n)}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},z,x;t)=f_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j-1},z,x_{j},\ldots,x_{n-1},x;t)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\{0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{j-1}<r<t_{j}<\ldots<t_{n-1}<t\}}p_{t-t_{n-1}}(x-x_{n-1})\ldots p_{t_{j}-r}(x_{j}-z)p_{r-t_{j-1}}(z-x_{j-1})\ldots

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad p_{t_{2}-t_{1}}(x_{2}-x_{1})dt_{1}\ldots dt_{n-1}dr

We will use the following functions:

\displaystyle f_{n}(t_{1},x_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{n},t,x)

\displaystyle=p_{t-t_{n}}(x-x_{n})\ldots p_{t_{2}-t_{1}}(x_{2}-x_{1})

(23)

\displaystyle g_{n}(t_{1},x_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{n},r,z,t,x)

\displaystyle=p_{t-t_{n}}(x-x_{n})\ldots p_{t_{2}-t_{1}}(x_{2}-x_{1})p_{t_{1}-r}(x_{1}-z),

(24)

We use the following notational convention: for any function
f:(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, we denote

f(\boldsymbol{t_{n}},\boldsymbol{x_{n}}):=f(t_{1},x_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{n}).

where
\boldsymbol{t_{n}}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
and
\boldsymbol{x_{n}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
.
We denote

\boldsymbol{t_{j:n}}=(t_{j},\ldots,t_{n})
for any
j\leq n
.

We let
g_{0}(r,z,t,x)=p_{t-r}(x-z)
. The following decomposition holds:

\displaystyle f_{j}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{x_{n-1}},z,x;t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{T_{j-1}(r)}f_{j-1}(\boldsymbol{t_{j-1}},\boldsymbol{x_{j-1}},r,z)d\boldsymbol{t_{j-1}}\right)

(25)

\displaystyle\quad\left(\int_{\{r<t_{j}<\ldots<t_{n-1}<t\}}g_{n-j}(\boldsymbol{t_{j:n-1}},\boldsymbol{x_{j:n-1}},r,z,t,x)d\boldsymbol{t_{j:n-1}}\right)dr.

Recall that
\mathcal{H}_{0}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathcal{P}_{0})
is the Hilbert space associated with the white noise in time. By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
20
),

\displaystyle\left(\int_{T_{n}(t)}\|f_{n}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}},\bullet,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}\|f_{n}(\cdot,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}\leq\dfrac{t^{n}}{n!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom{n}{\ell}\frac{C_{N}^{n-\ell}}{\ell!}(tD_{N})^{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{(2t)^{n}}{n!}\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\frac{C_{N}^{n-\ell}}{\ell!}(tD_{N})^{\ell}.

(26)

A similar inequality holds for
g_{n}
.
To see this, we recall the following estimate which was proved in
[
24
]
:

\|g_{n}(\cdot,r,z,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}}^{2}\leq p_{t-r}^{2}(x-z)\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom{n}{\ell}\frac{(4C_{N})^{n-\ell}}{\ell!}[(t-r)D_{N}]^{\ell}

(27)

(see the proof of relation (3.17) of
[
24
]
). By convention, the sum is equal to
1
if
n=0
. Applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain:

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\{r<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}<t\}}\|g_{n}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}},\bullet,r,z,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\right)^{2}\leq\dfrac{(t-r)^{n}}{n!}\|g_{n}(\cdot,r,z,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\leq\dfrac{(t-r)^{n}}{n!}p_{t-r}^{2}(x-z)\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\binom{n}{\ell}\frac{(4C_{N})^{n-\ell}}{\ell!}[(t-r)D_{N}]^{\ell}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\leq\dfrac{(2t)^{n}}{n!}p_{t-r}^{2}(x-z)\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}\frac{(4C_{N})^{n-\ell}}{\ell!}(tD_{N})^{\ell}.

(28)

Here is the first result of this section.

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose that
\gamma=\mathcal{F}\mu
is a non-negative locally-integrable function and
\mu
satisfies (D), or
d=1
and the noise is white. Then the series in (
21
) converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, and for any
p\geq 2
,
T>0
,
t\in[0,T]
and
x,z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|D_{z}u(t,x)\|_{p}\leq C_{T}\int_{0}^{t}p_{t-r}(x-z)dr,

(29)

where the constant
C_{T}
depends on
(T,p,\gamma)
.

Proof.

We first prove the convergence of series (
21
) in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. By relation (26) of
[
6
]
(which obviously continues to hold for the heat equation), we have:

\mathbb{E}|A_{n}(z,x;t)|^{2}\leq n!\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|f_{j}^{(n)}(\cdot,z,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(n-1)}}^{2}.

(30)

We estimate separately the terms of this sum.
By (
25
) and Minkowski’s inequality,

\displaystyle\|f_{j}^{(n)}(\cdot,z,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(n-1)}}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{T_{j-1}(r)}\|f_{j-1}(\boldsymbol{t_{j-1}},\bullet,r,z)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(j-1)}}d\boldsymbol{t_{j-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left(\int_{\{r<t_{j}<\ldots<t_{n-1}<t\}}\|g_{n-j}(\boldsymbol{t_{j:n-1}},\bullet,r,z,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(n-j)}}d\boldsymbol{t_{j:n-1}}\right)dr.

(31)

For each
r\in(0,t)
fixed, we estimate separately each of the inner integrals above. (By convention, the first integral is equal 1 if
j=1
, and the second one is equal to
p_{t-r}(x-z)
if
j=n
.)
We use (
26
) for the first integral and (
28
) for the second integral. We obtain:

\displaystyle\|f_{j}^{(n)}(\cdot,z,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(n-1)}}^{2}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}p_{t-r}(x-z)dr\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\frac{(2t)^{n-1}}{(j-1)!(n-j)!}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-1}\frac{C_{N}^{j-1-\ell}}{\ell!}(tD_{N})^{\ell}\right)\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-j}\frac{(4C_{N})^{n-j-\ell}}{\ell!}(tD_{N})^{\ell}\right).

(32)

We consider two cases.

Case 1.
Suppose that there exists
N_{0}\geq 1
such that
C_{N_{0}}=0
. Then
C_{N}=0
for all
N\geq N_{0}
. Let
N\geq N_{0}
be arbitrary. Then the only non-zero terms in the two sums on the right-hand side of (
32
) are those corresponding to
\ell=j-1
(for the first sum), respectively
\ell=n-j
(for the second sum). Therefore, relation (
32
) becomes:

\displaystyle\|f_{j}^{(n)}(\cdot,z,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(n-1)}}^{2}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}p_{t-r}(x-z)dr\right)^{2}\frac{(2t^{2}D_{N})^{n-1}}{[(j-1)!]^{2}[(n-j)!]^{2}}

Taking the sum for all
j=1,\ldots,n
we obtain:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|f_{j}^{(n)}(\cdot,z,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(n-1)}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}p_{t-r}(x-z)dr\right)^{2}\frac{(2t^{2}D_{N})^{n-1}}{[(n-1)!]^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j-1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}p_{t-r}(x-z)dr\right)^{2}\frac{(8t^{2}D_{N})^{n-1}}{[(n-1)!]^{2}},

using the fact that
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{j-1}^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{j-1}\right)^{2}=4^{n-1}
.
Coming back to (
30
), we get:

\mathbb{E}|A_{n}(z,x;t)|^{2}\leq n\left(\int_{0}^{t}p_{t-r}(x-z)dr\right)^{2}\frac{(8t^{2}D_{N})^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}.

This proves that
\sum_{n\geq 1}A_{n}(z,x;t)
converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, and

\mathbb{E}|D_{z}u(t,x)|^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}|A_{n}(z,x;t)|^{2}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}p_{t-r}(x-z)dr\right)^{2}\exp(16t^{2}D_{N}).

Relation (
29
) follows by hypercontractivity:

\|D_{z}u(t,x)\|_{p}\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}(p-1)^{(n-1)/2}\|A_{n}(z,x;t)\|_{2}\leq C_{t,p}\left(\int_{0}^{t}p_{t-r}(x-z)dr\right),

where
C_{t,p}=\exp(c(p-1)t^{2}D_{N})
for some constant
c>0
.

Case 2.
Suppose that
C_{N}>0
for all
N>0
. Bounding the two sums on the right-hand side of (
32
) by
C_{N}^{j-1}e^{C_{N}^{-1}tD_{N}}
, respectively
(4C_{N})^{n-j}e^{(4C_{N})^{-1}tD_{N}}
, we obtain:

\|f_{j}^{(n)}(\cdot,z,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(n-1)}}^{2}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}p_{t-r}(x-z)dr\right)^{2}\frac{(2t)^{n-1}}{(j-1)!(n-j)!}(4C_{N})^{n-1}e^{2tC_{N}^{-1}D_{N}}.

Taking the sum over
j=1,\ldots,n
and using the fact that
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{j-1}=2^{n-1}
, we get:

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|f_{j}^{(n)}(\cdot,z,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(n-1)}}^{2}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}p_{t-r}(x-z)dr\right)^{2}\frac{(16tC_{N})^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}e^{2tC_{N}^{-1}D_{N}}.

Coming back to (
30
) and using the fact that
n\leq 2^{n-1}
, we get:

\mathbb{E}|A_{n}(z,x;t)|^{2}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}p_{t-r}(x-z)dr\right)^{2}(32tC_{N})^{n-1}e^{2tC_{N}^{-1}D_{N}}.

Due to condition (D),
\lim_{N\to\infty}C_{N}=0
. Hence, we can choose
N=N_{T}
such that

32TC_{N}<1.

Then for any
t\in[0,T]
, the series
\sum_{n\geq 1}A_{n}(z,x;t)
converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, and

\mathbb{E}|D_{z}u(t,x)|^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}|A_{n}(z,x;t)|^{2}\leq\left(\int_{0}^{t}p_{t-r}(x-z)dr\right)^{2}\frac{1}{1-32TC_{N}}e^{2TC_{N}^{-1}D_{N}}.

Relation (
29
) follows by hypercontractivity, as in Case 1 above.

∎

We now examine the second Malliavin derivative. We fix
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We will show below that for any
w,z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have the following chaos expansion:

D_{w,z}^{2}u(t,x)=\sum_{n\geq 2}n(n-1)I_{n-2}(\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,w,z,x;t))=:\sum_{n\geq 2}B_{n}(w,z,x;t)\quad\mbox{in}\quad L^{2}(\Omega).

(33)

As in the case of the wave equation (see
[
6
]
), we note that:

\widetilde{f}_{n}(\cdot,w,z,x;t)=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i,j=1,i\not=j}^{n}h_{ij}^{(n)}(\cdot,w,z,x;t),

where
h_{ij}^{(n)}(\cdot,w,z,x;t)
is the symmetrization of the function

f_{ij}^{(n)}(\cdot,w,z,x;t)
defined as follows. If
i<j
,

\displaystyle f_{ij}^{(n)}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-2},w,z,x;t)=f_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},w,x_{i},\ldots,x_{j-2},z,x_{j-1},\ldots,x_{n-2},x;t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{t_{1}<\ldots<t_{i-1}<\theta<t_{i}<\ldots<t_{j-2}<r<t_{j-1}<\ldots<t_{n-2}<t\}}p_{t-t_{n-2}}(x-x_{n-2})\ldots p_{t_{j-1}-r}(x_{j-1}-z)

\displaystyle p_{r-t_{j-2}}(z-x_{j-2})\ldots p_{t_{i}-\theta}(x_{i}-w)p_{\theta-t_{i-1}}(w-x_{i-1})\ldots p_{t_{2}-t_{1}}(x_{2}-x_{1})dt_{1}\ldots dt_{n-2}drd\theta,

and we have the decomposition:

\displaystyle f_{ij}^{(n)}(\boldsymbol{x_{n-2}},w,z,x;t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\{0<\theta<r<t\}}\left(\int_{T_{i-1}(\theta)}f_{i-1}(\boldsymbol{t_{i-1}},\boldsymbol{x_{i-1}},\theta,w)d\boldsymbol{t_{i-1}}\right)

(34)

\displaystyle\quad\left(\int_{\{\theta<t_{i}<\ldots<t_{j-2}<r\}}g_{j-i-1}(\boldsymbol{t_{i:j-2}},\boldsymbol{x_{i:j-2}},\theta,w,r,z)d\boldsymbol{t_{i:j-2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\left(\int_{\{r<t_{j-1}<\ldots<t_{n-2}<t\}}g_{n-j}(\boldsymbol{t_{j-1:n-2}},\boldsymbol{x_{j-1:n-2}},r,z,t,x)\boldsymbol{t_{j-i:n-2}}\right)d\theta dr.

If
j<i
,

f_{ij}^{(n)}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-2},w,z,x;t)=f_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j-1},z,x_{j},\ldots,x_{i-2},w,x_{i-1},\ldots,x_{n-2},x;t)

In both cases,
w
is on position
i
and
z
is on position
j
.

After introducing these notations, we are now ready to prove the following result.

Theorem 3.5
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
3.4
, the series in (
33
) converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, and for any
p\geq 2
,
T>0
,
t\in[0,T]
and
x,w,z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|D_{w,z}^{2}u(t,x)\|_{p}\leq C_{T}\widetilde{f}_{2}(w,z,x;t),

(35)

where
C_{T}>0
is a constant that depends on
(T,p,\gamma)
.

Proof.

As in the case of Theorem
3.4
, the main effort will be dedicated to proving that the series (
33
) converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. To this end, we will use the following estimate, which is proved exactly as in the case of the wave equation (see
[
6
]
):

\mathbb{E}|B_{n}(w,z,x;t)|^{2}\leq n!\sum_{i,j=1,i\not=j}^{n}\left\|f_{ij}^{(n)}(\cdot,w,z,x;t))\right\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(n-2)}}^{2},

(36)

We proceed with the estimation of
\|f_{ij}^{(n)}(\cdot,w,z,x;t))\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(n-2)}}^{2}
. Let
1\leq i<j\leq n
be arbitrary.
By Minkowski’s inequality and (
34
),

\displaystyle\big\|f_{ij}^{(n)}(\cdot,w,z,x;t)\big\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(n-2)}}\leq\int_{0<\theta<r<t}\left(\int_{T_{i-1}(\theta)}\big\|f_{i-1}(\boldsymbol{t_{i-1}},\bullet,\theta,w)\big\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(i-1)}}d\boldsymbol{t_{i-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\left(\int_{\{\theta<t_{i}<\ldots<t_{j-2}<r\}}\big\|g_{j-i-1}(\boldsymbol{t_{i:j-2}},\bullet,\theta,w,r,z)\big\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(j-i-1)}}d\boldsymbol{t_{i:j-2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\left(\int_{\{r<t_{j-1}<\ldots<t_{n-2}<t\}}\big\|g_{n-j}(\boldsymbol{t_{j-1:n-2}},\bullet,r,z,t,x)\big\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(n-j)}}d\boldsymbol{t_{j-1:n-2}}\right)d\theta dr.

(37)

We estimate each of the 3 inner integrals above using (
26
) and (
28
). We have

\displaystyle\big\|f_{ij}^{(n)}(\cdot,w,z,x;t)\big\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(n-2)}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\quad\left(\int_{0<\theta<r<t}p_{t-r}(x-z)p_{r-\theta}(z-w)drd\theta\right)^{2}\frac{(2t)^{n-2}}{(i-1)!(j-i-1)!(n-j)!}

\displaystyle\quad\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{i-1}\frac{C_{N}^{i-1-\ell}}{\ell!}(tD_{N})^{\ell}\right)\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-i-1}\frac{(4C_{N})^{j-i-1-\ell}}{\ell!}(tD_{N})^{\ell}\right)\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-j}\frac{(4C_{N})^{n-j-\ell}}{\ell!}(tD_{N})^{\ell}\right).

(38)

The same argument shows that for all
1\leq j<i\leq n
,

\displaystyle\big\|f_{ij}^{(n)}(\cdot,w,z,x;t)\big\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(n-2)}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\quad\left(\int_{0<r<\theta<t}p_{t-\theta}(x-w)p_{\theta-r}(w-z)drd\theta\right)^{2}\frac{(2t)^{n-2}}{(j-1)!(i-j-1)!(n-i)!}

\displaystyle\quad\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{j-1}\frac{C_{N}^{j-1-\ell}}{\ell!}(tD_{N})^{\ell}\right)\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{i-j-1}\frac{(4C_{N})^{i-j-1-\ell}}{\ell!}(tD_{N})^{\ell}\right)\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-i}\frac{(4C_{N})^{n-i-\ell}}{\ell!}(tD_{N})^{\ell}\right).

(39)

We now consider two cases.

Case 1.
Suppose that there exists
N_{0}>0
such that
C_{N_{0}}=0
. Then
C_{N}=0
for all
N\geq N_{0}
. Let
N\geq N_{0}
be arbitrary. Then the only non-zero terms in the 3 sums appearing on the right-hand-side of (
38
) are those corresponding to
\ell=i-1
(for the first sum),
\ell=j-i-1
(for the second sum), respectively
\ell=n-j
(for the third sum). Therefore, (
38
) becomes:

\displaystyle\big\|f_{ij}^{(n)}(\cdot,w,z,x;t)\big\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(n-2)}}^{2}\leq f_{2}^{2}(w,z,x;t)\frac{(2t^{2}D_{N})^{n-2}}{[(i-1)!(j-i-1)!(n-j)!]^{2}}.

Taking the sum for all
1\leq i<j\leq n
, and using the fact that

\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\binom{n-2}{i-1,j-i-1,n-j}^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\binom{n-2}{i-1,j-i-1,n-j}\right)^{2}=9^{n-2}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\big\|f_{ij}^{(n)}(\cdot,w,z,x;t)\big\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(n-2)}}^{2}\leq f_{2}^{2}(w,z,x;t)\frac{(18t^{2}D_{N})^{n-2}}{[(n-2)!]^{2}}.

For the sum for
j<i
, we use (
39
) and obtain that:

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq n}\big\|f_{ij}^{(n)}(\cdot,w,z,x;t)\big\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes(n-2)}}^{2}\leq f_{2}^{2}(z,w,x;t)\frac{(18t^{2}D_{N})^{n-2}}{[(n-2)!]^{2}}.

Coming back to (
36
) and using the fact that
n(n-1)\leq 4^{n-2}
, we get:

\mathbb{E}|B_{n}(w,z,x;t)|^{2}\leq\Big(f_{2}^{2}(w,z,x;t)+f_{2}^{2}(z,w,x;t)\Big)\frac{(72t^{2}D_{N})^{n-2}}{(n-2)!}.

This proves that
\sum_{n\geq 2}B_{n}(w,z,x;t)
converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Moreover,

\|D_{w,z}^{2}u(t,x)\|_{2}\leq\sum_{n\geq 2}\|B_{n}(w,z,x;t)\|_{2}\leq C\widetilde{f}_{2}(w,z,x;t)\exp(Ct^{2}D_{N}).

For higher moments (of order
p>2
), we use hypercontractivity to obtain (
35
).

Case 2.
Suppose that
C_{N}>0
for all
N>0

4.1
Existence of solution

In this subsection, we establish the existence of the solution to (

We will use frequently the following properties.
By Stirling’s formula, for any

\Gamma(an+1)\sim(n!)^{a}a^{an}a^{1/2}(2\pi n)^{\frac{1-a}{2}}

c_{1}^{n}(n!)^{a}\leq\Gamma(an+1)\leq c_{2}^{n}(n!)^{a}\quad\mbox{for all $n\geq 1$},

where

\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{x^{n}}{(n!)^{a}}\leq c_{1}\exp(c_{2}x^{1/a})\quad\mbox{for all $x>0$}.

(56)

We denote by

a constant depending on

that may be different in each of its appearances. We first list some inequalities which we will use frequently below.

From relation (4.16) of
[

]
(with

and

\|f_{n}(\cdot,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}=\int_{T_{n}(t)}\|f_{n}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}},\bullet,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\leq c^{n}\frac{t^{nH}}{\Gamma(nH+1)}\leq c^{n}\frac{t^{nH}}{(n!)^{H}}.

Therefore, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain:

\displaystyle\left(\int_{T_{n}(t)}\|f_{n}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}},\bullet,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\|f_{n}(\cdot,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\otimes n}}^{2}\leq c^{n}\frac{t^{n(H+1)}}{(n!)^{H+1}}.

(58)

By relation (4.19) of
[

]
(with

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\{r<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}<t\}}\|g_{n}(\boldsymbol{t_{n}},\bullet,r,z,t,x)\|_{\mathcal{P}_{0}^{\otimes n}}d\boldsymbol{t_{n}}\right)^{2}\leq c^{n}\frac{(t-r)^{n(H+1)}}{(n!)^{H+1}}p_{t-r}^{2}(x-z).

(60)

\mathcal{I}_{t,t^{\prime}}^{j,k}(z):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\xi(B_{s}^{j}-B_{r}^{k}+z)}\mu(d\xi)drds,

(94)

and is understood as the
L^{p}(\Omega)
-limit (for any
p\geq 1
) as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
of

\mathcal{I}_{t,t^{\prime},\varepsilon}^{j,k}(z)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\varepsilon|\xi|^{2}}e^{-i\xi(B_{s}^{j}-B_{r}^{k}+z)}\mu(d\xi)drds,

where
B^{1},\ldots,B^{n}

-dimensional Brownian motions, independent of

.

Proof.

We only sketch the proof. We omit the details since they are similar to the proof of Proposition 1.3 of
[
23
]
.
See also Theorem 3.6 of
[
15
]
for the regular noise in space (colored in time), or Theorem 4.2 of
[
18
]
for the rough noise in space (white in time). We consider only the case
t_{j}=t
and
x_{j}=x
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
. The general case is similar. We let
\mathcal{I}_{t}^{j,k}=\mathcal{I}_{t,t}^{j,k}(0)
and
\mathcal{I}_{t,\varepsilon}^{j,k}=\mathcal{I}_{t,t,\varepsilon}^{j,k}(0)
.

Step 1.
From (
93
), it follows that

\mathbb{E}[(u^{\varepsilon}(t,x))^{n}]=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\langle A_{t,x}^{\varepsilon,B^{j}},A_{t,x}^{\varepsilon,B^{k}}\rangle_{\mathcal{P}_{0}}\right)\right].

By direct calculation,

\langle A_{t,x}^{\varepsilon,B^{j}},A_{t,x}^{\varepsilon,B^{k}}\rangle_{\mathcal{P}_{0}}=\mathcal{I}_{t,\varepsilon}^{j,k}
for any
1\leq j<k\leq n
.

Step 2.
Fix
1\leq j<k\leq n
.
It can be proved that the limit
\mathcal{I}_{t}^{j,k}
of
\mathcal{I}_{t,\varepsilon}^{j,k}
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
exists in
L^{p}(\Omega)
(for any
p\geq 1
). Note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(\mathcal{I}_{t,\varepsilon}^{j,k})^{n}]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-\varepsilon\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{2}}\int_{[0,t]^{n}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}(s_{j}\wedge s_{k}+r_{j}\wedge r_{k})\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}d\boldsymbol{r_{n}}d\boldsymbol{s_{n}}\mu(d\xi_{1})\ldots\mu(d\xi_{n})

\displaystyle\leq t^{n}n!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{T_{n}(t)}e^{-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(s_{j}-s_{j-1})|\xi_{j}+\ldots+\xi_{n}|^{2}}d\boldsymbol{s_{n}}\mu(d\xi_{1})\ldots\mu(d\xi_{n}).

From this, one can infer that

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\mathbb{E}[e^{\lambda\mathcal{I}_{t,\varepsilon}^{j,k}}]<\infty.

Hence, the family
\{e^{\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\mathcal{I}_{t,\varepsilon}^{j,k}}\}_{\varepsilon>0}

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathbb{E}[(u^{\varepsilon}(t,x))^{n}]=\mathbb{E}[e^{\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\mathcal{I}_{t,\varepsilon}^{j,k})}].

Moreover,
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\mathbb{E}[(u^{\varepsilon}(t,x))^{n}]<\infty
and the family
\{u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is uniformly integrable.

Step 3.
It follows that
u^{\varepsilon}(t,x)

Privacy Preserving Large Language Models: ChatGPT Case Study Based Vision and Framework
Imdad Ullah, Najm Hassan, Sukhpal Singh Gill, Basem Suleiman, Tariq Ahamed Ahanger, Zawar Shah, Junaid Qadir, Salil S. Kanhere

Abstract
The generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools based on Large Language Models (LLMs) use billions of parameters to extensively analyse large datasets and extract critical information such as, context, specific details, identifying information, use this information in the training process, and generate responses for the requested queries. The extracted data also contain sensitive information, which have raised serious threats to user privacy and reluctance to use such tools. This article proposes the conceptual model called PrivChatGPT, a privacy-preserving model for LLMs that consists of two main components i.e., preserving user privacy during the data curation/pre-processing together with preserving private context and the private training process for large-scale data. To demonstrate the applicability of PrivChatGPT, we show how a private mechanism could be integrated into the existing model for training LLMs to protect user privacy, specifically, we employed differential privacy and private training using Reinforcement Learning (RL). We measure the privacy loss and evaluate the measure of uncertainty or randomness once differential privacy is applied. It further recursively evaluate the level of privacy guarantees and the measure of uncertainty of public database and resources, during each update when new information is added for training purposes. To critically evaluate the use of differential privacy for private LLMs, we hypothetically compared other mechanisms such as Blockchain, private information retrieval, randomisation, obfuscation, anonymisation, and the use of ToR for various performance measures such as the model performance and accuracy, computational complexity, privacy vs. utility, training latency, vulnerability to attacks, and resource consumption. We conclude that the differential privacy, randomisation, and obfuscation can impact the utility and performance of the trained models, conversely, the use of ToR, Blockchain, and PIR may introduce additional computational complexity and high training latency. We believe that the proposed model could be used as a benchmark for proposing privacy preserving LLMs for generative AI tools.

V
PrivChatGPT: Proposed Model for Preserving User Privacy

Fig. 2:
PrivChatGPT: Proposed model for LLMs generative AI tools for preserving user privacy during training process.

Our proposed privacy-preserving model for Language Model (LM), such as ChatGPT, comprises two key components. The first component focuses on privacy preservation during data curation and pre-processing. This ensures that sensitive information is protected throughout the initial stages of model development. The second component involves private context management and training a large-scale data model using the provided private data for training purposes. Preserving Privacy with LLMs: Recommendations

An interesting fact to note is that majority of the LLM-based generative AI tools, such as OpenAI’s ChatGPT, are run by private and for-profit organization, hence, their interests may not align with the greater society’s needs. These models have access to large amounts of user data, which alarms several privacy issues, hence we should be extremely careful about sharing personal and sensitive information with the ChatGPT and other generative AI tools and technologies. Following, we provide detailed discussion over privacy policies for individuals and organizations, how to enable private interactions with generative AI tools, and how to introduce awareness program while interacting with such platforms.

VI-A

Enabling Private Interactions with LLMs

Following, we present few guidelines to ensure individuals privacy while interacting the generative AI tools:

VI-A
1
Avoid sharing sensitive/confidential information

It is strongly advised to refrain from sharing personal and sensitive information during interactions with generative AI tools. This includes PII such as name, address, birth date, social security number, and financial data, as well as authentication details like usernames, passwords, and other proprietary data with limited distribution rights. It is recommended to use the generic information, in addition, a user may subscribe to such AI tools with ‘pretend’ subscriber IDs/information. Furthermore, it is urged to stick to the topic of discussion and obtain the information or intended guidance.

VI-A
2
Use hypothetical examples

As mentioned earlier, using various prompts with the ChatGPT, that such generative AI tools can predict user behaviour based on their interactions with AI platforms. An important practice during interactions would be to use generic or hypothetical examples instead of real or very specific details or discussing your own problems or scenarios. It is advised to inquire randomised queries instead of asking questions within specific domain. These recommendations will help maintain privacy and prevent unintended exposure of sensitive information.

VI-A
3
Review and obscure before sharing

It is a good practice to review and redact the generated response for any personal/sensitive information before it is being shared with colleagues or published on any platform. There is a possibility that sensitive information might have been inadvertently included in the responses, hence, to reassure for any confidential data before dissemination. Furthermore, it is important to look for any behavioural or profiling information and censor part of the text for legal, privacy, or security purposes.

VI-A
4
Cautions with organization-specific details

If it is crucial to discuss organization-specific details during the prompts, then a suitable practice would be to stay cautious and avoid sharing highly sensitive information. Furthermore, it is important to understand and consider the level of confidentiality of information and make sure it aligns with the organization’s data sharing policies. Sharing context-based data, e.g., data/files classified based on metadata such as application names, the person (e.g., finance staff) who created the data, or locations, would also breach the privacy of specific organization.

VI-A
5
Follow organizational policies and guidelines

The foremost obligations of the personnels are to adhere to the organization’s policies and guidelines, more specifically, the policies that were devised for interacting with the AI tools and follow them accordingly. It is critical to consider the internal and external data handling procedures, sharing data with third-parties, privacy impact assessments, compliance with regulations, and incident response plans for data breaches or security or privacy incidents.

VI-A
6
Report any privacy concerns

During interactions with generative AI tools, it is highly possible to encounter with privacy risks, hence, it is important to report any such incidents to the responsible department within the company. Note that prompt reporting and actions may help address privacy issues and mitigate potential privacy risks.

VI-A
7
Cautious use of generative AI tools

To ensure privacy when using generative AI tools, organizations can implement various measures. These include removing PII from datasets to prevent re-identification, limiting access to sensitive data, implementing strong authentication and promoting responsible use of AI tools, employing secure protocols for data transfer, and utilizing secure and robust infrastructure for data storage and processing. In addition, organizations can conduct regular privacy impact assessments or regular access audits and reviews to identify and mitigate potential risks to privacy associated with using generative AI tools.

We note that the generative AI tools, such as ChatGPT, claims to protect user privacy, it is desirable to take precautionary and preventive measures before sharing any personal or behavioural information
[
52
]

Furthermore, the storing and processing data on the blockchain provides additional privacy of the training data due to its cryptographic security nature of the system. In addition, the smart contracts can also be utilised to enforce the privacy policies and control data access. We note that the use of blockchain additionally ensures the data integrity, which will also help accurately training vast amount of data in LLMs, specifically where the data is collected in distributed environments.

However, implementing such techniques for LLMs can be complex and resource intensive. This requires additional considerations such as scalability, integrating with existing machine learning frameworks, and evaluating their effectiveness for preserving privacy in large-scale language models during training processes. Blockchain technology involves distributed networks of high computation devices that additionally require significantly high energy consumptions and high-volume storages. The transactions in the blockchain rely on the consensus mechanism that can introduce latency and slow down the training process, impacting the overall performance training large language models and associated applications that require real-time responses. It is also evident the blockchain poses privacy concerns, despite it provides transparency and immutability, if the data is stored on public blockchain, causing leakage of private information to unauthorised parties. Henceforth, these challenges require robust design and optimisation, e.g., development of specialised blockchain architecture including consensus mechanism, of learning models for training large language models using blockchain technology.

VII-D

Randomisation

Randomisation can be effective for preserving user privacy in training large language models by introducing random sampling of training examples or noise injection. However, randomisation also present certain challenges, e.g., the introduced noise can impact the utility of the trained model, hence it is important to find a decent balance between the privacy and utility. It is also worth noting, to achieve the desired level of privacy, that randomisation may require large amount of training data for efficient privacy protection. The excessive randomisation can also lead to increased risk of overfitting; hence it may impact the generalisation ability of the trained model, which may result in decreased performance on real-world testing tasks.

In addition, the excessive randomisation can lead to loss of accuracy and performance and additionally computational overheads whereases small randomisation may not provide adequate privacy protection. Similarly, the randomisation may make it more difficult to interpret and explain the decision of trained model since the added noise may obscure the relationship in the data, which can make it difficult to extract the contextual information and make it harder to understand the model’s reasoning. It requires an ongoing research and development for developing effective randomisation techniques that can tailor the needs of training large language models for preserving privacy.

VII-E

Obfuscation

Depending on the specific privacy requirements and trade-offs between privacy and utility, the obfuscation i.e., to modify the training data or the model parameters, can be effective in preventing direct inference of sensitive information during the training process.

We argue that the obfuscation may potentially lead to a decrease in model performance and accuracy since the obfuscated data may contain less informative features that can impact the model’s capability to learn and make accurate predictions. Hence, it is important to find the right level of obfuscation that provides strong obfuscation and make it useful for training the LLMs. The process of obfuscation may involve the tokenising or anonymising the data that require extra computational resources and can slow down the training process. This may increase the overall complexity of the system, especially integrating with existing applications. In addition, the obfuscation techniques may face scalability issues while dealing with large scale and complex datasets that may result in increased computational costs. Furthermore, the obfuscation techniques may still be susceptible to reverse-engineering attacks where the adversary may uncover the sensitive information.

VII-F

Anonymisation

Training the large language models using anonymisation techniques can minimise the risk of exposing sensitive information while ensuring that the sensitive information is not directly linked to individuals. It also allows for secure data sharing and collaborations, with external parties, stakeholders, or among diverse large language models for training purposes. This can be particularly useful, for ethical considerations, when dealing with sensitive data, such as financial data or healthcare records; hence the organisations and individuals may be more willing to share their data.

There are several challenges that requires careful considerations for a comprehensive approach that combines appropriate anonymisation techniques for ensuring privacy protection during training large language models. As an example, anonymisation techniques require to remove or modify certain attributes from the data, it may lead to loss of context or other important features that may result in biased outcome and reduced performance. This may also impact the data its quality and representativeness, leading to less reliable models. It is also important to note that the adversaries may use auxiliary data or advanced re-identification techniques to de-anonymise the data and compromise privacy. Similar other techniques, the anonymisation process can introduce computational overheads and may increase the complexity of training large language models, resulting in low scalability and performance. Furthermore, it is challenging to consider adhering to privacy regulations and legal requirements when anonymising the data, which additionally requires a balance between training large language models and meeting the legal obligations and compliance. Assessing the effectiveness and privacy guarantees of anonymisation techniques can be challenging, especially overfitting or underfitting models, which requires the design of robust machine learning algorithms for private LLMs.

VII-G

Use of ToR

ToR would be particularly useful during collecting data, hence by leveraging the ToR during the LLMs training process, various features of the data can be hidden such as IP address, participant’s locations, etc., which can add an extra layer of privacy to the training process.

We note that it may limit to collect large-scale and diverse data because of the decentralised and anonymous nature of ToR, making it hard to train models over representative datasets. Introducing ToR into the existing structure of LLMs will introduce additional latency in routing traffic due to multiple hops, which will have a greater impact on the performance and speed of collecting data, with added impact of slowing down the training process. It is worth noting that ToR may not be accessible in some regions or due to network restrictions; this will limit the data availability, which may have issues with generalising learning models and the effectiveness of the resultant trained model. It is also evident that ToR may not guarantee end-to-end security, hence malicious attacks may compromise the data collection process, exposing sensitive information. Similarly, the authenticity and integrity of the data via ToR can be intriguing since it is difficult to validate the source and quality of the data because of anonymity and encryption provided by ToR.

VIII
Summary and Conclusions

The generative AI tools are maintained and run by private organisations with their own business interests more willingly than the society’s benefits, which alarms that the users must be very extremely proactive about their own privacy by not sharing sensitive information. We present several recommendations and guidelines for privately interacting with generative AI tools, various policies for organisations and individuals to protect organisational sensitive data, and guidelines for awareness program about the use of LLM-based generative AI tools. Our proposed model, PrivChatGPT for privacy-preserving model for LLMs, aims to protect user privacy during the information extraction and further provides steps for private training and its integration into the existing language models. To demonstrate its practicality, we present an approach for implementing PrivChatGPT, discussing how a private mechanism can be integrated into the existing model to protect user privacy. Finally, we present several challenges, trends and future research directions for use of various privacy preserving techniques such as, differential privacy, blockchain, private information retrieval, randomisation, obfuscation, anonymisation, and the use of ToR. We further present detailed comparison of these privacy protection mechanisms for training LLMs for various important performance metrics. It is vital to evaluate the performance and applicability of the privacy-preserving mechanisms used for training large language models, since their inclusion would introduce additional complexity and resource-intensive evaluation. These additional considerations might have greater impact on their scalability, efficiency, may impact their utility, or integrating them in the existing generative AI applications might be quite problematic, especially for resource-constrained device or delay intolerant applications. We plan to implement and evaluate the applicability of the proposed PrivChatGPT for private LLMs, compare it with the classic language models for various performance measures such as accuracy, computation time, in addition to, complexity of their implementation.

Perturbative Mellin amplitudes of 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 SYM


Abstract
We compute the Mellin amplitude of the planar four-point correlator of weight-two half-BPS operators in 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 SYM at one and two-loop orders in the small ’t Hooft coupling expansion. The two loop Mellin amplitude has an infinite number of poles, as expected from a stringy bulk dual. We then perform a Mellin conformal block expansion of the amplitude and extract the one-loop anomalous dimensions and OPE coefficients of the exchanged twist-two singlet multiplets. Our results match those of Dolan and Osborn [ 18 ] but the computation is rather straightforward in Mellin space.

Introduction

Starting from the seminal work of Mack
[
1
]
, we have seen substantial evidence
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
that ‘Mellin space’ is the natural ‘Momentum space’ analogue to formulate conformal field theories. In the case of quantum field theories, working in momentum space makes the Feynman rules easily tractable, and also manifests the consequences of locality, causality and unitarity. This advantage is not present for CFTs due to the absence of massive single particle states, mass being a dimensionful parameter. In a CFT, we can perform radial quantization wherein the dilatation operator acts as the hamiltonian and has discrete eigenvalues. This discrete set of operators actually appear in the operator product expansions as the exchanged conformal primaries and descendants. The Mellin amplitude corresponding to a CFT correlator makes this discrete spectrum manifest through it’s poles:

M(s,t)\sim C_{12k}C_{34k}\,\frac{Q_{l_{k},m}(t)}{s-\Delta_{k}+l_{k}-2m},\quad m=0,1,2,\cdots

(1.1)

Here
M(s,t)
is a Mellin ampltide for four point correlator of local operators
\langle O_{1}O_{2}O_{3}O_{4}\rangle
, and the poles appear at the twists
\tau_{k}=(\frac{\Delta_{k}-l_{k}}{2}+m)
of the exchanged primaries and descendants at level
m
.

For large
N
CFTs with weakly-coupled local bulk dual, the planar Mellin amplitudes should be particularly simple, due to the large
N
factorization of the CFT and the presence of only a few low-dimension single-trace operators in accordance with locality
[
6
]
. In this regime of holography, Mellin amplitudes have been shown to reproduce the scattering amplitudes in the dual bulk supergravity theory, both at planar
[
2
,
7
]
, and also at non-planar levels
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
. There are also attempts
[
11
,
12
]
to utilize the Mellin amplitudes to decipher the bulk dual of free CFTs in the context of higher spin holography. Indeed in the strong version of the Maldacena conjecture
[
13
]
, we do expect the Mellin amplitude of weakly-coupled holographic CFTs to capture an infinite number of single-trace exchanges in the OPEs corresponding to infinte number of massive stringy modes in the bulk. The direct way to analyse the bulk in this limit is notoriously difficult due to the presence of RR flux, where the conventional local worldsheet CFT description breaks down. See
[
14
]
for a recent proposal of a dual worldsheet description of free
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM.

In this note, working in the small ’t Hooft coupling and planar limit of
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM, we compute the four-point Mellin amplitude of weight-two half-BPS operators (also known as
20^{\prime}
operators) at one and two-loop orders. We observe that the two-loop Mellin amplitude has an infinite number of poles, as expected from a stringy bulk dual. We then move on to expand this amplitude in Mellin conformal blocks, and extract the anomalous dimensions and OPE coefficients of the exchanged twist-two singlet multiplets at one loop. Our results match precisely with what was found in
[
18
]
, but in a straightforward way using orthogonality relations of Hahn polynomials. The easiness of the analysis might be indicative of naturalness of Mellin space to formulate CFTs in general, as we discussed above.

Our motivation for this work was to understand how to extract the stringy bulk physics from their weakly-coupled dual CFTs, where we don’t have an independent bulk description due to complications induced by RR flux mentioned above. We hope this note would be helpful for future developments to find a more direct way to analyse the dual string theory from perturbative large N gauge theories, perhaps connecting with the other efforts made in

Correlator of four
20^{\prime}
Operators

In
\mathcal{N}=4
Supersymmetric Yang Mills theory, we have a tower of half-BPS operators
\mathcal{O}^{(p)}
, with
p=2,3,...
of dimension
\Delta=p
, which transform in the
[0,p,0]
representation of the
SU(4)
R-symmetry subgroup of
\text{PSU}(2,2|4)
global symmetry of the theory.. We can represent such operators as:

\mathcal{O}^{(p)}(x,t)=t_{i_{1}}...t_{i_{p}}\,\text{tr}[\phi^{i_{1}}(x)...\phi^{i_{p}}(x)]

(2.1)

where
t_{I}
is six-dimensional null polarization vector which contracts the
SU(4)
indices. We will consider the four-point function of length two half-BPS operators, also known as
20^{\prime}
operators

\langle\mathcal{O}^{(2)}(x_{1},t_{1})\mathcal{O}^{(2)}(x_{2},t_{2})\mathcal{O}^{(2)}(x_{3},t_{3})\mathcal{O}^{(2)}(x_{4},t_{4})\rangle=\left(\frac{t_{1}.t_{2}t_{3}.t_{4}}{x_{12}^{2}x_{34}^{2}}\right)^{2}\,\mathcal{G}(u,v;\sigma,\tau)

(2.2)

where conformal and R-symmetry cross ratios
(u,v)
and
(\sigma,\tau)
are defined as

\begin{split}&u=\frac{x_{12}^{2}\,x_{34}^{2}}{x_{13}^{2}\,x_{24}^{2}},\quad v=\frac{x_{14}^{2}\,x_{23}^{2}}{x_{13}^{2}\,x_{24}^{2}}\\
&\sigma=\frac{(t_{1}\cdot t_{3})(t_{2}\cdot t_{4})}{(t_{1}\cdot t_{2})(t_{3}\cdot t_{4})},\quad\tau=\frac{(t_{1}\cdot t_{4})(t_{2}\cdot t_{3})}{(t_{1}\cdot t_{2})(t_{3}\cdot t_{4})}\end{split}

(2.3)

The fermionic generators of supergroup
\text{PSU}(2,2|4)
imposes further constraints on the four-point function, encoded in the superconformal ward identity as:

\begin{split}&\mathcal{G}(u,v;\sigma,\tau)=\mathcal{G}_{f}(u,v;\sigma,\tau)+S(u,v;\sigma,\tau)\,\mathcal{H}(u,v)\\
&S(u,v;\sigma,\tau)=v+\sigma^{2}uv+\tau^{2}u+\sigma v(v-1-u)+\tau(1-u-v)+\sigma\tau u(u-1-v)\end{split}

(2.4)

Here
\mathcal{G}_{f}(u,v;\sigma,\tau)
comes from free SYM, and all the dynamical information of the correlator is encoded in
\mathcal{H}(u,v)
, which admits a conformal partial wave
G^{(l)}_{\Delta}(u,v)
expansion as (
t^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(\Delta-l)
):

\mathcal{H}(u,v)=\sum_{t^{\prime}}\sum_{l}A_{00t^{\prime}l}u^{t^{\prime}}G^{(l)}_{t^{\prime}+2}(u,v)

(2.5)

Position Space Results

We can expand
\mathcal{H}(u,v)
in ’t Hooft coupling
\lambda=g_{YM}^{2}N
as

\mathcal{H}(u,v)=\mathcal{H}^{(0)}(u,v)+\frac{\lambda}{N^{2}}\mathcal{H}^{(1)}(u,v)+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{N^{2}}\mathcal{H}^{(2)}(u,v)+...

(2.6)

where the perturbative results
\{\mathcal{H}^{(k)}\}
are well-known in the literature, see for example
[
18
,
17
]
:

\begin{split}&\mathcal{H}^{(0)}(u,v)=\left(1+\frac{1}{v^{2}}+\frac{4}{N^{2}}\frac{1}{v}\right)\\
&\mathcal{H}^{(1)}(u,v)=-2\,\frac{u}{v}\,\Phi^{(1)}(u,v)\\
&\mathcal{H}^{(2)}(u,v)=\frac{u}{v}\left[\frac{1}{4}(1+u+v)\Phi^{(1)}(u,v)^{2}+\Phi^{(2)}(u,v)+\frac{1}{v}\Phi^{(2)}\left(\frac{u}{v},\frac{1}{v}\right)+\frac{1}{u}\Phi^{(2)}\left(\frac{1}{u},\frac{v}{u}\right)\right]\end{split}

(2.7)

Note that these are the perturbative results in
\lambda
in the planar (genus zero) limit.

3
Perturbative Mellin Amplitudes

In this section, we will compute the Mellin amplitudes at one and two loop orders using Mellin-Barnes representations (see Appendix
B
) of one and two loop triangle diagrams. We will observe a stringy feature of having infinite number of poles at the two-loop Mellin amplitude, after stripping off the factor
\Gamma^{2}(2-s)\Gamma^{2}(-t)\Gamma^{2}(s+t)
. The latter factor accounts for all multi-trace OPE contributions corresponding to multi-string states in the dual bulk, and so the stripped off amplitude (often called ‘reduced Mellin amplitude’) only has poles associated to single trace operators (corresponding to single-string states).

One-loop Mellin amplitude

Conformal loop integral
\Phi^{(1)}(x,y)
is given
[
15
]
by one-loop triangle diagram with massless internal propagators, upto multiplicative factor (for the definition of
J(d,\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3})
, see appendix
B
):

\Phi^{(1)}(x,y)=\frac{p_{3}^{2}}{i\pi^{2}}J(4;1,1,1)

(3.1)

where,

x=\frac{p_{1}^{2}}{p_{3}^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad y=\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{p_{3}^{2}}

Following
[
15
]
, Mellin-Barnes integral representation for this diagram has been found in Appendix
B
:

J(4;1,1,1)=-\frac{i\pi^{2}}{p_{3}^{2}}\int_{-i\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{dsdt}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\,x^{s}y^{t}\,\Gamma^{2}(-s)\Gamma^{2}(-t)\Gamma^{2}(1+s+t)

(3.2)

Replacing
x=u
,
y=v
,

\Phi^{(1)}(u,v)=\int_{-i\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{dsdt}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\,u^{s}v^{t}\,\Gamma^{2}(-s)\Gamma^{2}(-t)\Gamma^{2}(1+s+t)

(3.3)

Hence,

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{(1)}(u,v)&=-2\,\frac{u}{v}\,\Phi^{(1)}(u,v)\\
&=2\int_{-i\infty}^{+i\infty}\frac{dsdt}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\,u^{s}v^{t}\,\Gamma^{2}(1-s)\Gamma^{2}(-t-1)\Gamma^{2}(1+s+t)\\
&=\int_{-i\infty}^{i\infty}\frac{ds}{2\pi i}\frac{dt}{2\pi i}u^{s}v^{t}\,{\Gamma^{2}(2-s)\Gamma^{2}(-t)\Gamma^{2}(s+t)}\,\mathcal{M}^{(1)}(s,t)\end{split}

(3.4)

where,

\mathcal{M}^{(1)}(s,t)=2\left[\frac{1}{(s-1)^{2}}+\frac{2}{(s-1)(t+1)}+\frac{1}{(t+1)^{2}}\right]

(3.5)

This gives the Mellin amplitude
\mathcal{M}^{(1)}(s,t)

\Phi^{(2)}(u,v)

[\Phi^{(1)}(u,v)]^{2}

We have the following Mellin-Barnes integral representation of
\Phi^{(1)}(u,v)

\Phi^{(1)}(u,v)=-\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}\frac{ds}{2\pi i}\frac{dt}{2\pi i}\,u^{s}v^{t}\,\Gamma^{2}(-s)\Gamma^{2}(-t)\Gamma^{2}(1+s+t)

(3.13)

Net two-loop Mellin amplitude:

Mellin Conformal Block Expansion

Anomalous Dimension and OPE Coefficient at one-loop

To read off the anomalous dimension

Appendix A
Comments on bootstrapping one-loop amplitude

In this section, we want to show that if we assume certain asymptotic growth of our Mellin amplitude at one-loop, then it is actually a unique solution to a algebraic problem, very similar to what has been put forward by Rastelli-Zhou
[
7
]
for tree-level supergravity amplitudes. Before that, we rewrite our one-loop mellin amplitude with the follwoing definition for Mellin integral:

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{(1)}(u,v)&=\int_{-i\infty}^{i\infty}\frac{ds}{2\pi i}\frac{dt}{2\pi i}u^{s/2}v^{t/2-2}{\Gamma^{2}(1-s/2)\Gamma^{2}(1-t/2)\Gamma^{2}(1-\widetilde{u}/2)}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\text{one-loop}}(s,t)\end{split}

(A.1)

where,
\widetilde{u}=4-s-t
. Then we have,

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\text{one-loop}}(s,t)=\frac{32}{(s-2)^{2}(t-2)^{2}(\widetilde{u}-2)^{2}}

(A.2)

This manifests crossing symmetry, i.e, invariance under permutation of
s,t,\widetilde{u}
(which follows from OPE associativity). Interestingly, the pole structure is exactly square of the tree-level supergravity amplitude (in Mellin space) found in
[
7
,
20
]
:

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\text{sugra}}(s,t)\sim\frac{1}{(s-2)(t-2)(\widetilde{u}-2)}

(A.3)

with

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\text{one-loop}}(s,t)\sim[\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{sugra}(s,t)]^{2}

(A.4)

In the following we mention the following bootstrap criteria, which could give our one-loop Mellin amplitude as a solution:

1.

Superconformal Symmetry in Mellin space:
Superconformal ward identity amounts to the following relation for Mellin amplitudes
[
7
]
:

\mathds{M}(s,t;\sigma,\tau)=\widehat{\mathcal{S}}\circ\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(s,t)

(A.5)

where
\widehat{\mathcal{S}}
is the difference operator
[
7
]
corresponding to
S(u,v,\sigma,\tau)
prefactor in Eq. (
2.4
),
\mathds{M}(s,t;\sigma,\tau)
and
\mathcal{M}(s,t)
are reduced Mellin amplitudes of
\mathcal{G}(u,v^{\prime}\sigma,\tau)
and
\mathcal{H}(u,v)
respectively. In particular it acts on
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(s,t)
in the following way:

\begin{split}\widehat{u^{m}v^{n}}\circ\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(s,t)\equiv\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(s-2m,t-2n;\sigma,\tau)\,\left(2-\frac{s}{2}\right)_{m}^{2}\left(2-\frac{t}{2}\right)_{n}^{2}\left(2-\frac{u}{2}\right)_{2-m-n}^{2}\end{split}

(A.6)

where,
u=8-s-t
and
(a)_{n}
as uaual Pochhammer symbol.

2.

Crossing Symmetry:
It implies

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(s,t)=\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(t,s)=\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\widetilde{u},t)

(A.7)

3.

Asympotic Behaviour:

\begin{split}\mathds{M}(\beta s,\beta t)\sim\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\right)\,\Rightarrow\,\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}(\beta s,\beta t)\sim\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\beta^{6}}\right)\quad\text{for}\quad\beta\rightarrow\infty\end{split}

(A.8)

We don’t have any a priori explanation for this asympotic behaviour. For supergravity case,
[
7
]
, argued their
\mathcal{O}(\beta)
growth using Penedones’ flat-space conjecture
[
2
]
. In particular, since contact interactions with
2n
derivatives give a power n growth and IIB supergravity (in ten-dimesnional flat space) contains contact interactions with at most two derivatives, corresponding Mellin amplitude should have power-one
\mathcal{O}(\beta)
growth.

4.

Analytic Structure:

Since
\Gamma
-stripped off mellin amplitude
M(s,t)
has poles corresponding to twists of the exchanged single-trace operators in the OPE at

s=\tau+2n

where
\tau=2\quad\text{and}\quad n=0,1,2,...
. At one-loop (
O(\lambda)
), we only have poles at
s=2

The unique solution to this algebraic problem is precisely eq. (
A.2
).

One-loop Triangle Integral

\nu_{2}

\nu_{1}

Figure 1:
One-loop triangle diagram

Feynman integral corresponding to one-loop triangle diagram Fig.
1
with massless internal propagators is

J(d,\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3})=\int\frac{d^{d}k}{((q_{1}+k)^{2})^{\nu_{1}}((q_{2}+k)^{2})^{\nu_{2}}((q_{3}+k)^{2})^{\nu_{3}}}

(B.1)

where,
q_{3}-q_{2}=p_{1}
,
q_{1}-q_{3}=p_{2}
,
q_{2}-q_{1}=p_{3}
and all external momenta are considereed to be ingoing (
p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}=0
).
\nu_{i}
s are introduced for analytic regularization of the diagram which will be important later. For now, clearly,
\nu_{i}=1
. Finding corresponding Mellin-barnes integral
[
15
]
consists of four basic steps of Integrating in and out process :

Step 1: Integrating in Feynman parameters

\begin{split}J(4;1,1,1)&=\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(q_{1}+k)^{2}(q_{2}+k)^{2}(q_{3}+k)^{2}}\\
&=\int d^{4}k\int dx_{1}dx_{2}dx_{3}\delta(\sum x_{i}-1)\frac{2!}{[x_{1}(q_{1}+k)^{2}+x_{2}(q_{2}+k)^{2}+x_{3}(q_{3}+k)^{2}]^{3}}\end{split}

(B.2)

Now,

\begin{split}&x_{1}(q_{1}+k)^{2}+x_{2}(q_{2}+k)^{2}+x_{3}(q_{3}+k)^{2}]^{3}\\
&=k^{2}(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})+2k(q_{1}x_{1}+q_{2}x_{2}+q_{3}x_{3})+(x_{1}q_{1}^{2}+x_{2}q_{2}^{2}+x_{3}q_{3}^{2})\\
&=(k+\vec{q}.\vec{x})^{2}+\Delta\quad\text{Taking}\quad x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}=1\quad\text{for the delta function}\end{split}

(B.3)

where

\Delta=(x_{1}q_{1}^{2}+x_{2}q_{2}^{2}+x_{3}q_{3}^{2})-(q_{1}x_{1}+q_{2}x_{2}+q_{3}x_{3})^{2}

Step 2: Integrating out loop-momentum

\begin{split}&\int\frac{d^{4}k}{[(k+\vec{q}.\vec{x})^{2}+\Delta]^{3}}=iS_{4}\int\frac{k_{E}^{3}dk_{E}}{(-k_{E}^{2}+\Delta)^{3}}=\frac{iS_{4}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\frac{-\Delta(1-x)}{x}\times\frac{\Delta}{x^{2}}dx}{(\frac{\Delta}{x})^{3}}=-\frac{i\pi^{2}}{2\Delta}\end{split}

(B.4)

Here
S_{4}
is volume of four-sphere. Now

\begin{split}\Delta&=(x_{1}q_{1}^{2}+x_{2}q_{2}^{2}+x_{3}q_{3}^{2})-(q_{1}x_{1}+q_{2}x_{2}+q_{3}x_{3})^{2}\\
&=[x_{1}x_{2}p_{3}^{2}+x_{1}x_{3}p_{2}^{2}+x_{2}x_{3}p_{1}^{2}]\quad\text{[Using $x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}=1$]}\end{split}

(B.5)

Hence,

J(4;1,1,1)=-i\pi^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dx_{1}dx_{2}dx_{3}\delta(\sum x_{i}-1)}{[x_{1}x_{2}p_{3}^{2}+x_{1}x_{3}p_{2}^{2}+x_{2}x_{3}p_{1}^{2}]}

(B.6)

Step 3 and 4: Integrating in Mellin variables and Integrating out Feynman parameters

We can now use Mellin-Barnes integral to rewritwe as:

\begin{split}&J(4;1,1,1)\\
&=-i\pi^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}dx_{1}dx_{2}dx_{3}\delta(\sum x_{i}-1)\frac{1}{x_{1}x_{2}p_{3}^{2}}\int\frac{dsdt}{(2\pi i)^{2}}(\frac{x_{2}x_{3}p_{1}^{2}}{x_{1}x_{2}p_{3}^{2}})^{s}(\frac{x_{1}x_{3}p_{2}^{2}}{x_{1}x_{2}p_{3}^{2}})^{t}\Gamma(-s)\Gamma(-t)\Gamma(1+s+t)\\
&=-\frac{i\pi^{2}}{p_{3}^{2}}\int\frac{dsdt}{(2\pi i)^{2}}x^{s}y^{t}\Gamma(-s)\Gamma(-t)\Gamma(1+s+t)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}dx_{1}dx_{2}dx_{3}\delta(\sum x_{i}-1)x_{1}^{-s-1}x_{2}^{-t-1}x_{3}^{s+t}\\
&=-\frac{i\pi^{2}}{p_{3}^{2}}\int\frac{dsdt}{(2\pi i)^{2}}x^{s}y^{t}\Gamma^{2}(-s)\Gamma^{2}(-t)\Gamma^{2}(1+s+t)\end{split}

(B.7)

where,

x=\frac{p_{1}^{2}}{p_{3}^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad y=\frac{p_{2}^{2}}{p_{3}^{2}}

Hence,

\boxed{J(4;1,1,1)=-\frac{i\pi^{2}}{p_{3}^{2}}\int\frac{dsdt}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\,x^{s}y^{t}\,\Gamma^{2}(-s)\Gamma^{2}(-t)\Gamma^{2}(1+s+t)}

(B.8)

(1+\epsilon_{i})
, provided that
\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{3}=0
.

The key formula in this calculation is Eq. (25) in
[
15
]
:

\begin{split}&\int_{2}(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2},\epsilon_{3})=\frac{J}{(p_{3}^{2})^{1-\epsilon_{3}}}[\frac{1}{\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3}}(p_{2}^{2})^{\epsilon_{3}}J(1,1,1-\epsilon_{1})+\frac{1}{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}}J(1,1,1+\epsilon_{3})+\frac{1}{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{3}}(p_{1}^{2})^{\epsilon_{1}}J(1,1,1-\epsilon_{2})]\\
&J=\frac{\Gamma(1-\epsilon_{1})\Gamma(1-\epsilon_{2})\Gamma(1-\epsilon_{3})}{\Gamma(1+\epsilon_{1})\Gamma(1+\epsilon_{2})\Gamma(1+\epsilon_{3})}\end{split}

(B.9)

This relation has been rederived recently in
[
16
]
. It connects the two-loop result with the one-loop one. Since mellin transform of
J(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3})(\equiv J(d;\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3}))
is known
[
Ussykina-1
]
,(we calculated it for the simple case
\nu_{i}=1
in the previous section)

J(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3})=\frac{i\pi^{2}}{(p_{3}^{2})^{\sum\nu_{i}-d/2}}\int dz_{2}dz_{3}x^{z_{2}}y^{z_{3}}D^{(z_{2},z_{3})}[\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3}]

(B.10)

where x and y are the same as defined in the last section and

\begin{split}D^{(z_{2},z_{3})}[\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3}]=&\frac{\Gamma(-z_{2})\Gamma(-z_{3})\Gamma(-z_{2}-\nu_{2}-\nu_{3}+d/2)\Gamma(-z_{3}-\nu_{1}-\nu_{3}+d/2)}{\prod_{i}\Gamma(\nu_{i})}\\
&\times\frac{\Gamma(z_{2}+z_{3}+\nu_{3})\Gamma(\sum\nu_{i}-d/2+z_{3}+z_{2})}{\Gamma(d-\sum_{i}\nu_{i})}\end{split}

(B.11)

Clearly from the above Eq. (
B.9
), we can get Mellin transform of two-loop triangle. The result is found in
[
16
]
:

\left[\frac{D^{(u,v-\epsilon_{2})}[1-\epsilon_{1}]}{\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3}}+\frac{D^{(u,v)}[1+\epsilon_{3}]}{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}}+\frac{D^{(u-\epsilon_{1},v)}[1-\epsilon_{2}]}{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{3}}\right]

(B.12)

Hence, two loop triangle diagram:

\begin{split}C^{(2)}(p_{1}^{2},p_{2}^{2},p_{3}^{2})=(\frac{i\pi^{2}}{p_{3}^{2}})^{2}\int dsdt\,x^{s}y^{t}M^{(2)}(s,t)\end{split}

(B.13)

where,

\begin{split}M^{(2)}(s,t)&=\lim_{\epsilon_{2}\to 0,\epsilon_{1}\to 0}J[\frac{D^{(s,t-\epsilon_{2})}[1-\epsilon_{1}]}{\epsilon_{2}\epsilon_{3}}+\frac{D^{(s,t)}[1+\epsilon_{3}]}{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}}+\frac{D^{(s-\epsilon_{1},t)}[1-\epsilon_{2}]}{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{3}}]\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\Gamma^{2}(-s)\Gamma^{2}(-t)\Gamma^{2}(1+s+t)[(\Psi^{\prime}(-t)+\Psi^{\prime}(-s))-(\Psi(-t)-\Psi(-s))^{2}-\pi^{2}]\end{split}

(B.14)

with
\Psi(z)
being digamma function of
z
.

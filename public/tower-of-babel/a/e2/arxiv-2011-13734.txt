On the squeezing function for finitely connected planar domains
Pavel Gumenyuk, Oliver Roth

Abstract
. In a recent paper, Ng, Tang and Tsai (Math. Ann. 2020) have found an explicit formula for the squeezing function of an annulus via the Loewner differential equation. Their result has led them to conjecture a corresponding formula for planar domains of any finite connectivity stating that the extremum in the squeezing function problem is achieved for a suitably chosen conformal mapping onto a circularly slit disk. In this paper we disprove this conjecture. We also give a conceptually simple potential–theoretic proof of the explicit formula for the squeezing function of an annulus which has the added advantage of identifying all extremal functions.

Primary: 30C75; Secondary: 30C35, 30C85

1.
Introduction

Let
{\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{d}}
,
d\geqslant 1
, be a domain such that the class
\,\mathcal{U}(\Omega)
of all injective holomorphic mappings
{f:\Omega\to\mathbb{B}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\colon|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|z_{d}|^{2}<1\}}
is not empty.
We denote by
{\mathsf{dist}}(0,\partial f(\Omega))
the Euclidean distance of the origin
0
from the boundary of
f(\Omega)
.
The
squeezing function

S_{\Omega}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
of the domain
\Omega
is defined by

S_{\Omega}(z):=\sup\big\{{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial f(\Omega)\big)\colon f\in\mathcal{U}(\Omega),f(z)=0\big\},\qquad z\in\Omega.

(1.1)

This notion was introduced in 2012 by Deng, Guang and Zhang
[
6
]
inspired by the works of Liu, Sun and Yau
[
16
,
17
]
(2004) and Yeung
[
26
]
(2009).
Squeezing functions and their properties have since been investigated by many authors; we refer to the papers [,  – , , , , ] and the references therein.

Clearly, the squeezing function is a biholomorphic invariant. Moreover, it is known
[
6
, Theorem 2.1]
that the supremum in the definition of the squeezing function is always attained; in other words there exists an
extremal mapping
, i.e. an injective holomorphic map
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{B}
such that
{f(z)=0}
and
{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial f(\Omega)\big)=S_{\Omega}(z)
.

Recently, Ng, Tang and Tsai
[
19
]
have determined the squeezing function for an annulus
\mathbb{A}_{r}:={\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon r<|z|<1\}}
, and they have formulated a conjectural formula for the squeezing function of planar domains of higher (but finite) connectivity.
The aim of this paper is the construction of a counterexample to the conjecture of Ng, Tang and Tsai for domains of connectivity beyond two.
Moreover, we give a simple proof of their result for the doubly connected case. Unlike the approach in
[
19
]
, which is based on the Loewner differential equation on an annulus,
we use only rather elementary potential-theoretic reasoning. An advantage of our method is that it allows us to identify
all
extremal functions.

2.
Main results

In order to state our results we briefly recall some basic concepts.
A
circularly slit disk
is a subdomain
D
of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon|z|<1\}
containing the origin such that
\overline{\mathbb{D}}\setminus D
consists of the unit circle
\partial\mathbb{D}
and (closed)
arcs lying on concentric circles centered at the origin. It is admissible that the arcs degenerate to points.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain in
\mathbb{C}
with at least one non-degenerate (i.e., different from a singleton) boundary component. It is known, see e.g.
[
25
,
12
,
23
]
that for any such domain
\Omega
and any fixed
{z\in\Omega}
there is always a conformal mapping of
\Omega
onto some circularly slit disk that takes
z
to
0
. If the domain
\Omega
is
finitely connected
with non-degenerate boundary components
\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
, say, then
for each
{z\in\Omega}
and each
{j=0,1,\ldots,n}
we can find a unique conformal map
f_{z,j}
of
\Omega
onto a circularly slit disk normalized by
{f_{z,j}(z)=0}
,
{f^{\prime}_{z,j}(z)>0}
, and
f_{z,j}(\Gamma_{j})=\partial\mathbb{D}
. Note that, in general,
f_{z,j}
does not have to admit a continuous extension to
\partial\Omega
; writing
f(\Gamma_{j})
, where
f
is any conformal map of
\Omega
, we mean the boundary component
C_{j}
of
f(\Omega)
that corresponds to
\Gamma_{j}
under the map
f
in the following sense: if
(z_{k})
is a sequence of points in
\Omega
which converges to a point on
\Gamma_{j}
, then every limit point of the sequence
(f(z_{k}))
belongs to
C_{j}
.

In the notation and terminology of Remark
2.1
the following conjecture was formulated
in
[
19
]
.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be an
m
-connected domain without degenerate boundary components. Then for any
{z\in\Omega}
the squeezing function
S_{\Omega}
is equal to

S_{\Omega}(z)~=\max_{j=0,\ldots,m-1}{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial f_{z,j}(\Omega)\big).

(2.1)

Our main result states that this conjecture fails if the connectivity of
\Omega
is higher than two.

Theorem 1
.

For each
m\geqslant 3
, there exists an
m
-connected domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
without degenerate boundary components and a point
{z\in\Omega}
such that (
2.1
) does not hold.

Conjecture
1
is however true in the doubly connected case. This is the main result of
[
19
]
.
The following theorem gives slightly more precise information by identifying
all
extremal functions.

Theorem 2
.

Formula (
2.1
) holds for any doubly connected domain
\Omega
with at least one non-degenerate boundary component and for any
{z\in\Omega}
.
Each extremal function is a conformal map onto a circularly slit disk.
In particular, for any
r\in(0,1)
and any
z\in\mathbb{A}_{r}
,

S_{\mathbb{A}_{r}}(z)=\max\{|z|\,,r/|z|\}.

(2.2)

The proof of (
2.2
) in
[
19
]
is based on a representation of conformal maps in terms of the Loewner differential equation. It is well–known that one disadvantage of the Loewner method is its possible failure to identify all of the extremal functions. Our proof of Theorem
2
is based on potential theory and more or less automatically gives complete description of the extremal functions.

Remark 2.2
.

It is worth mentioning that there is an infinitesimal version of Conjecture
1
which in fact
does
hold for any finitely connected domain and which, incidentally, will be one of the key ingredients for the proof of Theorem
1
. To state this result, we fix
z\in\Omega
and
{j\in\{0,\ldots,m-1\}}
and consider the set of all injective holomorphic functions
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{D}
normalized by
{f(z)=0}
,
{f^{\prime}(z)>0}
and such that
f(\Gamma_{j})
is the outer boundary of
f(\Omega)
, i.e.
f(\Gamma_{j})
is the boundary of the unbounded component of
\mathbb{C}\setminus f(\Omega)
. Then the maximum of
f^{\prime}(z)
is achieved for
f=f_{z,j}
and only for this function.
To prove this remarkable fact, we notice that if
f
maximizes
f^{\prime}(z)
, then according to the Schwarz Lemma, the outer boundary of
f(\Omega)
must be the unit circle
\partial\mathbb{D}
. It remains to apply the following well-known result for
{D:=f(\Omega)}
and
{\varphi:=f_{z,j}\circ f^{-1}}
.

Proposition 2.3

(Tsuji
[
25
, Lemma 2(i) on p. 409]
)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{D}
be a finitely connected domain with outer boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}
and let
\varphi
be the conformal mapping of
D
onto a circularly slit disk normalized by
\varphi(0)=0
,
\varphi^{\prime}(0)>0
, and
\varphi(\partial\mathbb{D})=\partial\mathbb{D}
. Then
\varphi^{\prime}(0)\geqslant 1
, with
\varphi^{\prime}(0)=1
if and only if
D
is a circularly slit disk, in which case
\varphi={\sf id}_{D}
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
3
we describe the potential–theoretic tools on which our work is based, namely harmonic measure, logarithmic potentials and conformal mappings as well as their behaviour w.r.t. kernel convergence. This section contains several auxiliary statements which are either new or otherwise only implicitly contained in the vast literature on the subject. Strictly speaking, for the purpose of this paper, some of these results would only be needed for domains of connectivity two or for domains with degenerate boundary components.
However, for the sake of clarity and consistency, and in view of potential further applications, we state and prove these auxiliary results in their natural setting for domains of any finite connectivity. In Section
4
we prove
Theorem
2
. The proof is based on the doubly connected case of Theorem
3
in Section
3
, which expresses the harmonic basis (i.e. the harmonic measures of boundary components) for a finitely connected domain
\Omega
in terms of logarithmic potentials of specific positive Borel measures which are supported on the individual boundary components of
\Omega
. In
Section
5
we discuss in detail the mapping properties and dependence on parameters of the canonical conformal mapping of the standard annulus
\mathbb{A}_{r}
onto a circularly slit disk. Our treatment is based on the Schottky – Klein prime function. In the final Section
6
, these mapping properties are then combined with the results of Section
3
to prove Theorem
1
.

Throughout the paper we will denote by
\mathbb{D}(a,\rho)
the open disk
{\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon|z-a|<\rho\}}
.

3.
Harmonic measure, logarithmic potentials, and kernel convergence

In this section we suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
is a
bounded
finitely connected domain with
n+1
non-degenerate boundary components
\Gamma_{j}
,
j=0,1,\ldots,n
.

For
z\in\Omega
, we denote by
\omega_{\Omega}(z,\cdot)
the
harmonic measure
for a point
z\in\Omega
relative to the domain
\Omega
. For the definition and fundamental properties of the harmonic measure we refer the reader to
[
22
, §4.3]
.

Remark 3.1
.

Finite sets are removable for bounded harmonic functions, see e.g.
[
3
, Corollary 1.5 on p. 73]
. This well-known fact together with Perron’s theory of the Dirichlet problem for harmonic functions
[
22
, Theorems 4.1.5 and 4.2.2]
imply that under the above assumptions, for any continuous function
{\varphi:\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}}
there exists a unique continuous function
\psi:\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
which is harmonic in
\Omega
and which coincides with
\varphi
on every non-degenerate boundary component of
\Omega
. This function is given by the
generalized Poisson integral

\psi(z)=\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}\varphi(\zeta)\,\omega_{\Omega}(z,\mathrm{d}\zeta)=\int\limits_{P}\varphi(\zeta)\,\omega_{\Omega}(z,\mathrm{d}\zeta)\quad\text{for all~$z\in\Omega$},

(3.1)

where
P:=\Gamma_{0}\cup\Gamma_{1}\cup\ldots\cup\Gamma_{n}
is the union of all non-degenerate boundary components of
\Omega
.
The Maximum Principle asserts in this case, see e.g.
[
22
, Theorem 4.1.2]
, that

\max\{\psi(z)\colon z\in\overline{\Omega}\}=\max\{\varphi(\zeta)\colon\zeta\in P\}.

Remark 3.2
.

Formula (
3.1
) leads to the following representation for the Green function
G_{\Omega}
of the domain
\Omega
,

G_{\Omega}(z,w)=\log\frac{1}{|z-w|}\,-\int_{P}\log\frac{1}{|z-w|}\,\,\omega_{\Omega}(z,\mathrm{d}\zeta)\quad\text{for all~$z,w\in\Omega$, $z\neq w$}.

(3.2)

For any fixed
w\in\Omega
, we will assume that
G_{\Omega}(\cdot,w)
is extended to
\partial\Omega
by continuity.

To state the first theorem of this section we need to introduce some more notation.
Denote by
K_{j}
the connected component of
{\mathbb{C}\setminus\Omega}
bounded by
\Gamma_{j}
. We adopt the convention that, unless explicitly stated otherwise, the boundary components are labelled such that
\Gamma_{0}
is the
outer boundary
of
\Omega
, i.e. the component
K_{0}
is the unbounded one.

Let
\omega_{j}(z):=\omega_{\Omega}(z,\Gamma_{j})
,
j=0,\ldots,n
. Note that
\omega_{j}
is the unique harmonic function in
\Omega
admitting a continuous extension to
\overline{\Omega}
with
{\omega_{j}|\Gamma_{j}\equiv 1}
and
{\omega_{j}|\Gamma_{k}\equiv 0}
for
k\neq j
.

Consider the integrals

\lambda_{jk}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\limits_{\partial\mathcal{D}_{k}}\frac{\partial\omega_{j}}{\partial\mathrm{n}}\,\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad k=0,\ldots,n,

where
\mathcal{D}_{k}
is a Jordan domain (unbounded in case
{k=0}
) with
C^{1}
-smooth boundary
{\partial\mathcal{D}_{k}\subset\Omega}
and such that
\mathcal{D}_{k}\cap\big(\mathbb{C}\setminus\Omega\big)=K_{k}
. Here
\partial/\partial\mathrm{n}
stands for the derivative along the
inner
normal w.r.t.
\mathcal{D}_{k}
.
Note that

\sum_{j=0}^{n}\lambda_{jk}=0,\quad k=0,\ldots,n,

(3.3)

because
\sum_{j=0}^{n}\omega_{j}=\omega_{\Omega}(\cdot,\partial\Omega)\equiv 1
. Moreover, thanks to Green’s formula,

\lambda_{j0}=-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{jk},\quad j=0,\ldots,n.

(3.4)

The functions
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}
form the so-called
harmonic basis
in the domain
\Omega
and the numbers
\lambda_{jk}
are known as
periods
; for more details see, e.g.,
[
3
, §15.1]
. To fix the terminology in a more precise way, we will say that
\lambda_{jk}
is the period of
\omega_{j}

associated with the boundary component
\Gamma_{k}
.

Remark 3.3
.

The harmonic basis and the period matrix are conformally invariant in the following sense. Let
f
be a conformal mapping of
\Omega
onto another bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}
. Then for any
j\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
and all
{z\in\Omega}
,

\omega_{f(\Omega)}\big(f(z),f(\Gamma_{j})\big)=\omega_{\Omega}(z,\Gamma_{j})=\omega_{j}(z).

(3.5)

It follows easily that for any
k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
the period of
\omega_{f(\Omega)}\big(\,\cdot\,,f(\Gamma_{j})\big)
associated with
f(\Gamma_{k})
equals
\lambda_{jk}
.

To establish equality (
3.5
), it is sufficient to recall that
\omega_{f(\Omega)}\big(\,\cdot\,,f(\Gamma_{j})\big)
and
\omega_{\Omega}(\cdot,\Gamma_{j})
extend continuously to the boundaries of
\Omega
and
f(\Omega)
, respectively, and notice that for any
k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
and any sequence
{(z_{n})\subset\Omega}
,
{\mathsf{dist}}(z_{n},\Gamma_{k})\to 0
as
{n\to+\infty}
if and only if
{\mathsf{dist}}\big(f(z_{n}),f(\Gamma_{k})\big)\to 0
as
{n\to+\infty}
.

Remark 3.4
.

It is known that the
period matrix

\Lambda_{0}:=[\lambda_{jk}]_{1\leqslant j,k\leqslant n}
is invertible and symmetric. The proof of this fact for smooth boundaries can be found, e.g., in
[
3
, Proposition 1.7 on p. 74]
and
[
18
, p. 39]
. Since any finitely connected domain can be mapped conformally onto a domain with smooth boundary, the general case holds thanks to Remark
3.3
.
Moreover, equalities (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) show that the extended period matrix
\Lambda:=[\lambda_{jk}]_{0\leqslant j,k\leqslant n}
is symmetric as well and that for any
m\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
,
the matrix
\Lambda_{m}:=[\lambda_{jk}]_{j,k\in J_{m}}
, where
J_{m}:=\{0,\ldots,n\}\setminus\{m\}
, is invertible.

Below we will see that the harmonic functions
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}
can be represented in terms of logarithmic potentials.
For a finite Borel measure
\mu
with compact support in
\mathbb{C}
, the
logarithmic potential

V_{\mu}
is defined by

V_{\mu}(w):=\int\log\frac{1}{|w-z|}\,\,\mathrm{d}\mu(z).

Note that
V_{\mu}
is a harmonic function in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathop{\mathsf{supp}}\mu
. Moreover,

V_{\mu}(w)=|\mu|\log\frac{1}{|w|}+O(1/|w|)\quad\text{as~$w\to\infty$},

(3.6)

where
|\mu|
denotes for the total mass of
\mu
, i.e.
|\mu|:=\mu(\mathop{\mathsf{supp}}\mu)
.
See, e.g.,
[
22
, Sect. 3.1]
for more details.

The following theorem is certainly known to the specialists. Since we have not been able to trace any suitable precise reference, we state it here and include a proof.

Theorem 3
.

In the above notation, for each
j=1,\ldots,n
, there exist finite positive Borel measures
\mu_{j0},\ldots,\mu_{jn}
supported on
\Gamma_{0},\ldots,\Gamma_{n}
, respectively, such that

\omega_{j}(z)~=~V_{\mu_{jj}}(z)~-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant k\leqslant n\\[0.90417pt]
k\neq j\end{subarray}}V_{\mu_{jk}}(z)\qquad\text{for all~$z\in\Omega$}.

(3.7)

The measures
\mu_{jk}
are uniquely defined by
\Omega
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathsf{supp}}\mu_{jk}=\Gamma_{k},\quad k=0,\ldots,n,

(3.8)

\displaystyle|\mu_{jk}|=|\lambda_{jk}|,\quad k=0,\ldots,n,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\lambda_{jj}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{jk}<0~\text{~for all~$~k=0,\ldots,n$, $k\neq j$}.

(3.10)

Proof.

The functions
\omega_{0},\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}
extend harmonically to every isolated point of
\partial\Omega
. Therefore, without loss of generality we may suppose that
\Omega
has no degenerate boundary components, i.e.
\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{0}\cup\Gamma_{1}\cup\ldots\cup\Gamma_{n}
.

Fix
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and consider the function
u:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

u(z)=\begin{cases}\omega_{j}(z),&\text{if~$z\in\Omega$},\\
0,&\text{if~$z\in K_{k}$ with $k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}$, $k\neq j$},\\
1,&\text{if~$z\in K_{j}$}.\end{cases}

This function is continuous in the whole plane, see e.g.
[
22
, Theorems 4.2.2 and 4.3.4]
.
Clearly,
u
is harmonic in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\partial\Omega
. Moreover, comparing
u(z_{0})
for points
z_{0}\in\partial\Omega
with the mean values of
u
over sufficiently small circles centered at
z_{0}
and taking into account that
u(\mathbb{C})\subset[0,1]
, one can easily see that
u
is subharmonic in
\mathbb{C}\setminus K_{j}
and superharmonic in
\mathbb{C}\setminus K
, where

K:=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant k\leqslant n\\[0.90417pt]
k\neq j\end{subarray}}K_{k}.

Therefore, combining Riesz’s Representation Theorem for subharmonic functions (see, e.g.,
[
13
, Theorem 3.9 on p. 104]
) with the fact that the logarithmic potential of a finite Borel measure is harmonic in an open set
A
if and only if this measure vanishes on
A
, one may conclude that there exist two uniquely defined finite positive Borel measures
\mu
and
\nu
, supported on
\partial K_{j}=\Gamma_{j}
and
\partial K=\bigcup_{k\neq j}\Gamma_{k}
, respectively, such that

u=V_{\mu}-V_{\nu}+u_{0},

where
u_{0}
is a harmonic function in
\mathbb{C}
. Put
\mu_{jj}:=\mu
and
\mu_{jk}=\nu|_{\Gamma_{k}}
for
{k=0,\ldots,n}
,
k\neq j
.

By Gauß’ Theorem (see, e.g.,
[
24
, Theorem 1.1 on p. 83]
) we have

\lambda_{jk}=\begin{cases}\hphantom{-}|\mu_{jj}|,&\text{if $k=j$},\\[1.93747pt]
-|\mu_{jk}|,&\text{if~$k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}$, $k\neq j$.}\end{cases}

This proves (
3.9
) and the non-strict inequalities in (
3.10
) for
k\neq 0
. The fact that
\lambda_{jk}\neq 0
for all
{k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}}
follows from (
3.8
), which we will prove below.

Applying Gauß’ Theorem in the disk
\mathbb{D}(0,R)
for
R>0
large enough, we see that
|\mu|=|\nu|
. With (
3.3
) taken into account, it follows that (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), again with the non-strict inequality, hold also for
{k=0}
.

Moreover, thanks to (
3.6
), we have
{V_{\mu}(z)-V_{\nu}(z)\to 0}
as
z\to\infty
. By construction,
u
vanishes identically in a neighbourhood of
\infty
. Hence, applying the Maximum Principle to the harmonic function
u_{0}=u-(V_{\mu}-V_{\nu})
, we see that
u_{0}\equiv 0
. This proves (
3.7
).

To show that
\mathop{\mathsf{supp}}\mu=\Gamma_{j}
suppose on the contrary that there exists a neigbourhood
U
of a point
z_{0}\in\Gamma_{j}
such that
\mu(U)=0
. Replacing
U
, if necessary, with a smaller neighbourhood, we may suppose that
{U\cap K=\emptyset}
. Then
u
is harmonic in
U
; moreover,
u(z)\leqslant 1
for all
{z\in U}
and
u(z_{0})=1
. By the Maximum Principle
u(z)=1
for all
z\in U
. Since
U\cap\Omega\neq\emptyset
, this contradicts the fact that
u
is not constant in
\Omega
. The equality
\mathop{\mathsf{supp}}\nu=\bigcup_{k\neq j}\Gamma_{k}
can be proved in a similar way.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

For a non-empty set
J\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
denote

\Gamma_{J}:=\bigcup_{j\in J}\Gamma_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad J^{*}:=\{0,\ldots,n\}\setminus J.

Repeating the argument of the above proof with
{u:\mathbb{C}\to[0,1]}
defined by
{u(z):=\omega_{\Omega}(z,\Gamma_{J})}
if
{z\in\Omega}
,
{u(z):=1}
if
{z\in K_{j}}
for some
{j\in J}
, and
{u(z):=0}
if
{z\in\Gamma_{k}}
with
{k\in J^{*}}
, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\omega_{\Omega}(\,\cdot\,,\Gamma_{J})=\sum_{j\in J}\omega_{j}=V_{\mu}-V_{\nu},

(3.11)

where

\displaystyle\mu:=\sum_{j\in J}\Big(\mu_{jj}\,-\!\sum_{k\in J\setminus\{j\}}\mu_{jk}\Big)=\sum_{k\in J}\Big(\mu_{kk}\,-\!\sum_{j\in J\setminus\{k\}}\mu_{jk}\Big)\quad\text{and}\quad\nu:=\sum_{j\in J}\,\sum_{k\in J^{*}}\mu_{jk}

are
positive
finite Borel measures supported on
\Gamma_{J}
and
\bigcup_{k\in J^{*}}\Gamma_{k}
, respectively, with

|\mu|\,=\,|\nu|\,=\sum_{j,k\in J}\lambda_{jk}\,=\,-\sum_{j\in J}\sum_{k\in J^{*}}\lambda_{jk}\,=\sum_{j,k\in J^{*}}\lambda_{jk}\,.

(3.12)

(Here we have also taken into account relations (
3.3
) and (
3.4
).)

Remark 3.6
.

It is clear from the proof of Theorem
3
that the representation (
3.7
), as well as formula (
3.11
), are valid also on the boundary of
\Omega
.

As a simple application of Theorem
3
, we recover the following known result, see e.g.
[
3
, Proof of Lemma 6.3, pp. 97-98]
.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
m\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
and
z_{0}\in\Omega
, and let
f
be a conformal mapping of
\Omega
onto a suitable circularly slit disk

D:=\mathbb{D}\,\big\backslash\Big(\bigcup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant k\leqslant n\\[0.90417pt]
k\neq m\end{subarray}}C_{k}\Big)

with
f(z_{0})=0
,
f(\Gamma_{m})={\partial\mathbb{D}}
, and
f(\Gamma_{k})=C_{k}
for all
{k\neq m}
. Then the radii
r_{k}
of the circular arcs
C_{k}
satisfy the following system of linear equations:

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leqslant k\leqslant n\\[0.90417pt]
k\neq m\end{subarray}}\lambda_{jk}\log\frac{1}{r_{k}}=\omega_{j}(z_{0}),\quad 0\leqslant j\leqslant n,~j\neq m.

(3.13)

Remark 3.8
.

Note that the linear system (
3.13
) has a unique solution because its coefficient matrix is invertible; see Remark
3.4
.

Proof of Corollary
3.7
.

Since the harmonic basis and the periods
\lambda_{jk}
,
{j,k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}}
, are conformally invariant, see Remark
3.3
, we may suppose that
\Omega=D
,
m=0
, and
f={\sf id}_{\Omega}
. Accordingly, we have
z_{0}=0
.
Fix
{j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}
and apply Theorem
3
. By (
3.7
),

\omega_{j}(0)=|\mu_{jj}|\log\frac{1}{r_{j}}~-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant k\leqslant n\\[0.90417pt]
k\neq j\end{subarray}}|\mu_{jk}|\log\frac{1}{r_{k}},

(3.14)

where we took into account that
\mu_{jk}
,
k=1,\ldots,n
, are supported on circles of radius
r_{k}
centred at the origin and that
\mu_{j0}
is supported on
{\partial\mathbb{D}}
and hence
V_{\mu_{j0}}(0)=0
.

Taking into account (
3.9
) and (

), from (

) we obtain

\omega_{j}(0)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{jk}\log\frac{1}{r_{k}},

as desired.
∎

In the doubly connected case, which is the relevant case for the proof of Theorem
2
, the above results allow us to deduce the following statement.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
r\in(0,1)
and let
f
be a conformal mapping of
\mathbb{A}_{r}
onto a bounded domain
\Omega
such that
\partial\mathbb{D}
corresponds under
f
to the outer boundary
\Gamma_{0}
of
\Omega
. Then the following two assertions hold.

(A)

For any
\zeta\in\Omega
,

\log\frac{1}{|f^{-1}(\zeta)|}=V_{\mu_{*}}(\zeta)-V_{\nu_{*}}(\zeta),

(3.15)

where
\mu_{*}
and
\nu_{*}
are suitable
probability
measures with

\mathop{\mathsf{supp}}\nu_{*}=\Gamma_{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathop{\mathsf{supp}}\mu_{*}=\Gamma_{1}:=\partial\Omega\setminus\Gamma_{0}.

(B)

Moreover, if
\Omega
is a circularly slit disk, then
\Gamma_{1}\subset\big\{\zeta\colon|\zeta|=|f^{-1}(0)|\big\}
.

Proof.

Apply Theorem
3
with
n=j=1
. By Remark
3.3
,
{\omega_{1}(\zeta)=\log|f^{-1}(\zeta)|/\log r}
for all
{\zeta\in\Omega}
.
Thanks to the same remark, in order to find
\lambda_{1,1}
, we may suppose that

. In this way we see that the measures
\mu_{1,1}
and
\mu_{1,0}
in formula (

) satisfy

|\mu_{1,1}|=|\mu_{1,0}|=\lambda_{1,1}=\frac{1}{\log(1/r)}.

Hence to prove (A), it remains to set
{\mu_{*}:=(1/\lambda_{1,1})\mu_{1,1}}
and

Assertion (B) is a well known fact, see e.g.
[

or
[
23
, Lemma 3]
. Alternatively, one can use system (

) in Corollary

, which reduces in our case to the unique equation

∎

Remark 3.10
.

It is possible to show that the measure
\mu_{*}
in Corollary
3.9
coincides with the so-called
Green equilibrium distribution
on
K_{1}
relative to the simply connected domain
D:=\Omega\cup K_{1}=\mathbb{C}\setminus K_{0}
, where in accordance with the notation introduced at the beginning of this section,
K_{1}
and
K_{0}
stand for the bounded and unbounded connected components of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\Omega
, respectively. Formula (
3.15
) can be rewritten as

\log\frac{1}{|f^{-1}(\zeta)|}=\int\limits_{\Gamma_{1}}G_{D}(\zeta,w)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{*}(w),\quad\zeta\in D\setminus K_{1},

where
G_{D}
is the Green function of the domain
D
. For more details on Green equilibrium distributions, see e.g.
[
24
, Sect. II.5]
, or
[
25
, p. 94-95]
where the case
D=\mathbb{D}
is considered.

The classical Kernel Convergence Theorem due to Carathéodory, see e.g.
[
21
, Theorem 1.8 on p. 14]
, relates the limit behaviour of a sequence of hyperbolic
simply connected
domains with the convergence of the corresponding conformal mappings onto the canonical domain (the unit disk).
The notion of kernel convergence extends naturally to multiply connected domains, but no complete analogue of Carathéodory’s result seems to be known even for the finitely connected case.
Considerable progress in this direction has been made in
[
1
,
2
]
. In this regard, it is worth mentioning that the canonical mappings of a multiply connected domain are closely related to its harmonic basis, see e.g.
[
3
, Chapter 15]
.
However, to the best of our knowledge, no known results apply to the special, but rather interesting, case in which the boundary consists of a non-empty constant part plus a variable part, which shrinks in the limit to a single point. The following theorem describes the limit behaviour of the harmonic measure, Green’s function, and the conformal mappings onto circularly slit disks in this special case.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\Omega_{0}
and
\Omega_{n}\varsubsetneq\Omega_{0}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, be bounded finitely connected domains in
\mathbb{C}
and let
\zeta^{*}\in\Omega_{0}
. Suppose that

\sup\big\{|\zeta-\zeta^{*}|\colon\zeta\in\Omega_{0}\setminus\Omega_{n}\big\}\to 0\quad\text{as~$~n\to+\infty$.}

Then the following five statements hold.

(A)

If
\varphi
is continuous on
\partial\Omega_{0}
and in a neighbourhood of
\zeta^{*}
, then for all
{z\in\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\}}
,

\int\limits_{\partial\Omega_{n}}\varphi(\zeta)\,\omega_{\Omega_{n}}(z,\mathrm{d}\zeta)~\to~\int\limits_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\varphi(\zeta)\,\omega_{\Omega_{0}}(z,\mathrm{d}\zeta)\quad\text{as~$~n\to+\infty$}.

(3.16)

Moreover, for any
\varepsilon>0
, the convergence in (
3.16
) is uniform in
\Omega_{0}\setminus\mathbb{D}(\zeta^{*},\varepsilon)
.

(B)

For any
\varepsilon>0
, the sequence
(G_{\Omega_{n}})
converges to
G_{\Omega_{0}}
uniformly in

\big\{(z,w)\colon z\in\!\overline{\,\Omega_{0}\!}\,\setminus\mathbb{D}(\zeta^{*},\varepsilon),~w\in\Omega_{0}\setminus\mathbb{D}(\zeta^{*},\varepsilon),~z\neq w\big\}.

(C)

Let
\Gamma
be a non-degenerate boundary component of
\Omega_{0}
and let
(z_{n}\in\Omega_{n})
be a sequence converging to some
z_{0}\in\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\}
. For each
{n\in\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}}
, denote by
f_{n}
the unique conformal mapping of
\Omega_{n}
onto a circularly slit disk normalized by
f_{n}(z_{n})=0
,
f_{n}^{\prime}(z_{n})>0
,
f_{n}(\Gamma)=\partial\mathbb{D}
. Then the sequence
(f_{n})
converges locally uniformly in
\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\}
to
f_{0}
.

(D)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
g_{n}
be a conformal mapping of
\Omega_{n}
with
{g_{n}(\Omega_{n})\subset\mathbb{D}}
. Further, suppose that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
the outer boundary of
g_{n}(\Omega_{n})
corresponds under
g_{n}^{-1}
to a subset of
\partial\Omega_{n}\setminus\partial\Omega_{0}
. If the sequence
(\zeta_{n}):=\big(g_{n}^{-1}(0)\big)
is contained in a compact subset of
\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\}
, then the sequence
(g_{n})
converges locally uniformly in
\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\}
to
g_{0}\equiv 0
.

(E)

For the sequences
(f_{n})
and
(g_{n})
defined above, we have

\displaystyle{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial f_{n}(\Omega_{n})\big)\to{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial f_{0}(\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\})\big)\quad\text{and}

(3.17)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial g_{n}(\Omega_{n})\big)\to 0\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{as $~n\to+\infty$.}

(3.18)

Remark 3.11
.

Using Moebius transformations it is easily seen that the above theorem holds also for unbounded domains
\Omega_{0}\subset\mathbb{C}
with external points. A similar approach allows one to extend Theorem
4
to the case of an unbounded domain without external points, provided it has at least one non-degenerate boundary component. In such a case, however, the argument becomes slightly more complicated because one should use a conformal map of the form
z\mapsto\sqrt{(z-a)/(z-b)}
, which does not extend
to a one-to-one map on the boundary.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Denote
\Upsilon_{n}:=\partial\Omega_{n}\setminus\partial\Omega_{0}
. We are going to show that:

Claim 1.
For any
{\varepsilon>0}
, as
{n\to+\infty}
,
\omega_{\Omega_{n}}(z,\Upsilon_{n})\to 0
uniformly in
\!\overline{\,\Omega_{0}\!}\,\setminus\mathbb{D}(\zeta^{*},\varepsilon)
.

Denote by
P_{0}
the union of all non-degenerate boundary components of
\Omega_{0}
and let
P_{n}
,
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, stand for the union of all non-degenerate connected components of
\Upsilon_{n}
.
If
{P_{n}=\emptyset}
, then
\omega_{\Omega_{n}}(z,\Upsilon_{n})\equiv 0
. Therefore, we may suppose that
{P_{n}\neq\emptyset}
for any
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Then Theorem
3
together with Remark
3.5
implies that for each
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,

\omega_{\Omega_{n}}(z,\Upsilon_{n})=V_{\mu_{n}}(z)-V_{\nu_{n}}(z)\quad\text{for all~$z\in\!\overline{\,\Omega_{n}\!}\,$},

(3.19)

where
\mu_{n}
and
\nu_{n}
are suitable positive Borel measures supported on
P_{n}
and
P_{0}
, respectively. Moreover,

{c_{n}:=|\mu_{n}|=|\nu_{n}|.}

It follows from the very definition of the logarithmic potential that

\inf_{z\in P_{n}}V_{\mu_{n}}(z)\geqslant-c_{n}\log\mathsf{diam}(P_{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{z\in P_{n}}V_{\nu_{n}}(z)\leqslant-c_{n}\log{\mathsf{dist}}(P_{0},P_{n})

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Taking into account that
V_{\mu_{n}}(z)-V_{\nu_{n}}(z)=1
for all
{z\in P_{n}}
, we deduce that

1/c_{n}\geqslant\log\frac{{\mathsf{dist}}(P_{0},P_{n})}{\mathsf{diam}(P_{n})}\to+\infty,\quad\text{i.e. $c_{n}\to 0$,$~$ as $~n\to+\infty$.}

(3.20)

Using (
3.19
) we see that for any
\varepsilon>0
with
\partial\mathbb{D}(\zeta^{*},\varepsilon)\subset\Omega_{0}
,
{\omega_{\Omega_{n}}(\Upsilon_{n},z)\to 0}
uniformly on
\partial\mathbb{D}(\zeta^{*},\varepsilon)
as
{n\to+\infty}
. Now Claim 1 follows easily from the Maximum Principle, see Remark
3.1
, applied to the harmonic functions
{\omega_{\Omega_{n}}(\cdot,\Upsilon_{n})}
in the domain
\Omega_{0}\setminus\!\overline{\,\mathbb{D}(\zeta^{*},\varepsilon)}
.

Proof of (A)
. By Remark
3.1
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the function

\psi_{n}(z)\,:=\,\int\limits_{\partial\Omega_{n}}\varphi(\zeta)\,\omega_{\Omega_{n}}(z,\mathrm{d}\zeta)~-~\int\limits_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\varphi(\zeta)\,\omega_{\Omega_{0}}(z,\mathrm{d}\zeta)

is harmonic in
\Omega_{n}
and extends continuously to the boundary of
\Omega_{n}
with
\psi_{n}|_{P_{0}}\equiv 0
.
Hence, again by Remark
3.1
, for all
z\in\Omega_{n}
,

\displaystyle|\psi_{n}(z)|\,=\,\left|\int_{P_{n}}\psi_{n}(\zeta)\,\omega_{\Omega_{n}}(z,\mathrm{d}\zeta)\right|\,

\displaystyle\leqslant\,\omega_{\Omega_{n}}(z,\Upsilon_{n})\max\{|\psi(\zeta)|\colon\zeta\in P_{n}\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\,2\omega_{\Omega_{n}}(z,\Upsilon_{n})\max\{|\varphi(\zeta)|\colon\zeta\in\partial\Omega_{n}\},

where we have applied the triangle inequality and the Maximum Principle in order to estimate
|\psi_{n}|
on
P_{n}
.
Combined with Claim 1 the above inequality easily implies (A).

Proof of (B)
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Fix some
w\in\Omega_{0}\setminus\mathbb{D}(\zeta^{*},\varepsilon)
. Bearing in mind formula (
3.2
), consider the functions

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(z)~

\displaystyle:=~G_{\Omega_{0}}(z,w)\,-\,G_{\Omega_{n}}(z,w)\,=

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{\partial\Omega_{n}}\log\frac{1}{|\zeta-w|}\,\,\omega_{\Omega_{n}}(z,\mathrm{d}\zeta)~-~\int\limits_{\partial\Omega_{0}}\log\frac{1}{|\zeta-w|}\,\,\omega_{\Omega_{0}}(z,\mathrm{d}\zeta).

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, we have
P_{n}\subset\mathbb{D}(\zeta^{*},\varepsilon/2)
and hence
{0\leqslant G_{\Omega_{0}}(\zeta,w)\leqslant M}
for all
{\zeta\in P_{n}}
and some constant
M>0
depending on
\varepsilon
but not on
w
. Since
{G_{\Omega_{n}}(\zeta,w)=0}
for all
{\zeta\in P_{n}}
, we have
|\psi_{n}|\leqslant M
on
P_{n}
, and it only remains to apply the argument used in the above proof of assertion (A).

Proof of (C)
. Since
f_{n}(\Omega_{n})\subset\mathbb{D}
for all
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, the functions
f_{n}
form a normal family in
{\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\}}
. Therefore, passing if necessary to a subsequence, we may suppose that
(f_{n})
converges locally uniformly in
{\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\}}
to a holomorphic function
{f:\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\}\to\!\overline{\,\mathbb{D}\!}\,}
. Since
f
is bounded in
{\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\}}
, it extends holomorphically to
\zeta^{*}
.

To prove (C) it is sufficient to show that
{f=f_{0}}
.
Clearly
f(z_{0})=\lim_{n\to+\infty}{f_{n}(z_{n})}=0
.
Moreover,
f^{\prime}(z_{0})\geqslant f_{0}^{\prime}(z_{0})
. Indeed, for each
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, let
T_{n}
be the automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
such that
{T_{n}(f_{0}(z_{n}))=0}
and
{T_{n}^{\prime}(f_{0}(z_{n}))>0}
. Note that
T_{n}\circ f_{0}
maps
\Omega_{n}
conformally onto a subdomain of
\mathbb{D}
in such a way that the boundary component
\Gamma
corresponds to
\partial\mathbb{D}
. Bearing in mind that
{(T_{n}\circ f_{0})(z_{n})=0}
and
{(T_{n}\circ f_{0})^{\prime}(z_{n})>0}
, by the extremal property of the conformal mappings onto circularly slit disks, see Remark
2.2
, we have
{f_{n}^{\prime}(z_{n})\geqslant(T_{n}\circ f_{0})^{\prime}(z_{n})}
.
Passing to the limit as
{n\to+\infty}
and taking into account that
T_{n}\to{\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}}
yields the desired conclusion.
In particular, this means that
f^{\prime}(z_{0})\neq 0
. Hence
f
is a conformal mapping of
\Omega_{0}
onto a subdomain of
\mathbb{D}
.

In order to show that
\Gamma
corresponds under
f
to the outer boundary of
f(\Omega_{0})
, consider a
C^{1}
-smooth Jordan curve
{C\subset f(\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\})}
that separates the outer boundary of
f(\Omega_{0})
from all other boundary components of
f\big(\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\}\big)
. Let
\mathcal{D}
be the connected component of
{\mathbb{C}\setminus f^{-1}(C)}
that contains
\Gamma
. Then
\overline{\mathcal{D}}\cap\Omega_{0}\subset\Omega_{n}
for all
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
large enough and moreover, for all such
n
’s,
f_{n}(\mathcal{D}\cap\Omega_{0})
lies in the unbounded component of
{\mathbb{C}\setminus C_{n}}
, where
C_{n}:={f_{n}\big(f^{-1}(C)\big)}
, because
{f_{n}(\Gamma)=\partial\mathbb{D}}
. Using this fact, we conclude that for any
{\zeta\in\mathcal{D}\cap\Omega_{0}}
,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{C}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}w}{w-f(\zeta)}~

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{f^{-1}(C)}\frac{f^{\prime}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z}{f(z)-f(\zeta)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int\limits_{f^{-1}(C)}\frac{f_{n}^{\prime}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z}{f_{n}(z)-f_{n}(\zeta)}=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int\limits_{C_{n}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}w}{w-f_{n}(\zeta)}~=~0.

It follows that
f(\Gamma)
lies in the unbounded connected component of
\mathbb{C}\setminus C
. By the very construction, the only boundary component of
f(\Omega)
possessing this property is its outer boundary.
Recalling that
f(z_{0})=0
and
f^{\prime}(z_{0})\geqslant f_{0}^{\prime}(z_{0})
, we see now that
f=f_{0}
thanks to the extremal property stated in Remark
2.2
.

Proof of (E)
. With (C) having been already proved, relation (
3.17
) is an immediate consequence of the following two facts:

(i)

if
K\subset f_{0}(\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\})
is compact, then
K\subset f_{n}(\Omega_{n})
for all
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
large enough;

(ii)

for any
w\in\partial f(\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\})
there exist a sequence
w_{n}\in\partial f_{n}(\Omega_{n})
converging to
w
.

We omit the proof of assertions (i) and (ii) because, up to a few light adjustments, it repeats the standard argument used in the proof of Carathéodory’s Kernel Convergence Theorem, see e.g.
[
21
, Theorem 1.8 on p. 14]
.

To prove relation (
3.18
), consider the functions
u_{n}(z):=\omega_{\Omega_{n}}\big(g_{n}^{-1}(z),\partial\Omega_{0}\big)
defined on the domains
{D_{n}:=g_{n}(\Omega_{n})}
.
Applying Theorem
3
and Remark
3.5
, we see that

u_{n}=V_{\mu^{\prime}_{n}}-V_{\nu^{\prime}_{n}}\quad\text{for each ${n\in\mathbb{N}}$,}

where
\mu^{\prime}_{n}
and
\nu^{\prime}_{n}
are positive Borel measures supported on the union of the (non-degenerate) boundary components of
D_{n}
corresponding under
g_{n}
to the connected components of
P_{0}
and to those of
P_{n}
, respectively.
Moreover,
|\mu^{\prime}_{n}|=|\nu^{\prime}_{n}|
.

In fact,
|\mu^{\prime}_{n}|=|\nu^{\prime}_{n}|=c_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Indeed, thanks to the conformal invariance of the periods, see Remark
3.3
, this equality follows from relation (
3.12
) applied twice: for the measures
\mu_{n}
,
\nu_{n}
and for the measures
\mu^{\prime}_{n}
,
\nu^{\prime}_{n}
.

Therefore, on the one hand, by (
3.20
), we have
|\mu^{\prime}_{n}|=|\nu^{\prime}_{n}|\to 0
as
{n\to+\infty}
. On the other hand, by Claim 1,
u_{n}(0)=1-\omega_{\Omega_{n}}(\zeta_{n},\Upsilon_{n})\to 1
as
{n\to+\infty}
. It follows that

{{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\mathop{\mathsf{supp}}(\mu^{\prime}_{n}+\nu^{\prime}_{n})\big)\to 0}\quad\text{as~$~n\to+\infty$},

which is equivalent to (
3.18
).

Proof of (D)
. Recall that by the hypothesis, there exists a compact set
{K\subset\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\}}
such that
\zeta_{n}:=g_{n}^{-1}(0)\in K
for all
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Moreover, the functions
g_{n}
are all univalent in their domains and form a normal family in
\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\}
. Therefore, it is sufficient to show that
g_{n}^{\prime}(\zeta_{n})\to 0
as
{n\to+\infty}
. According to Koebe’s
1/4
-Theorem, see e.g.
[
3
, Theorem 7.8 on p. 64]
,

|g_{n}^{\prime}(\zeta_{n})|\leqslant\frac{4\,{\mathsf{dist}}(0,\partial D_{n})}{{\mathsf{dist}}(\zeta_{n},\partial\Omega_{n})}\quad\text{for all~$n\in\mathbb{N}$.}

Thus, (D) follows from (

).
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
2

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be a doubly connected domain with at least one non-degenerate boundary component. Fix
z\in\Omega
. As we mentioned in the Introduction, the supremum in (
1.1
) is achieved for at least one injective holomorphic function
f_{*}:\Omega\to\mathbb{D}
and such a function is said to be
extremal
in the squeezing function problem. Let
K\subset\mathbb{D}
be the bounded connected component of
{\mathbb{C}\setminus f_{*}(\Omega)}
. Denote by
\varphi
the conformal mapping of the simply connected domain
D:=f_{*}(\Omega)\cup K
onto
\mathbb{D}
normalized by
{\varphi(0)=0}
and
{\varphi^{\prime}(0)>0}
. On the one hand, since
f_{*}
is extremal, we have

{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial f_{*}(\Omega)\big)\geqslant{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial\varphi(f_{*}(\Omega))\big)={\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\varphi(K)\big).

On the other hand, if
\zeta_{*}\in\mathbb{D}
is the point of
\varphi(K)
closest to the origin, then by the Schwarz Lemma applied for
\varphi^{-1}
,

{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial f_{*}(\Omega)\big)\leqslant|\varphi^{-1}(\zeta_{*})|\leqslant|\zeta_{*}|={\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\varphi(K)\big).

This means that equality takes place in all the above inequalities. Hence,
\varphi^{-1}={\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}}
, i.e.
D=\mathbb{D}
.
Consequently, the outer boundary of
f_{*}(\Omega)
coincides with
\partial\mathbb{D}
and

S_{\Omega}(z)={\mathsf{dist}}(0,\partial K).

(4.1)

In particular, the statement of Theorem
2
is now obvious for doubly connected domains
\Omega
with one degenerate boundary component. Therefore, without loss of generality we may suppose that
\Omega=\mathbb{A}_{r}
for some
r\in(0,1)
and that
{f_{*}(\partial\mathbb{D})=\partial\mathbb{D}}
. Otherwise we would replace
f_{*}
by
f_{*}\circ g^{-1}
and
z
by
g(z)
, where
g
is a conformal mapping of
\Omega
onto
\mathbb{A}_{r}
taking
f_{*}^{-1}(\partial\mathbb{D})
to
\partial\mathbb{D}
.

Then by Corollary
3.9
(A) applied for
f:=f_{*}
,

\log\frac{1}{|z|}=\log\frac{1}{|f_{*}^{-1}(0)|}=V_{\mu_{*}}(0)-V_{\nu_{*}}(0),

where
\mu_{*}
and
\nu_{*}
are two probability measures with
{\mathop{\mathsf{supp}}\mu_{*}=\partial K}
and
{\mathop{\mathsf{supp}}\nu_{*}=\partial\mathbb{D}}
. Clearly,
V_{\nu_{*}}(0)=0
. Hence, taking into account (
4.1
), we have

\log\frac{1}{|z|}=\int\limits_{\partial K}\log\frac{1}{|w|}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{*}(w)\leqslant\log\frac{1}{S_{\Omega}(z)}.

The equality can occur only if
\partial K
is contained on the circle of radius
S_{\Omega}(z)
centered at the origin, because
\mathop{\mathsf{supp}}\mu_{*}

coincides
with
\partial K
.

Taking into account that by Corollary
3.9
(B),
{S_{\Omega}(z)\geqslant|z|}
, we conclude that in fact,
{S_{\Omega}(z)=|z|}
and that
f_{*}(\Omega)
is a circularly slit disk.

To obtain formula (
2.2
), it remains to recall that in case
f_{*}^{-1}(\partial\mathbb{D})
is
\partial\mathbb{D}(0,r)
rather than
\partial\mathbb{D}
, we have to replace
z
by
g(z)
, where
g
is a conformal automorphism of
\mathbb{A}_{r}
permuting the boundary components. The proof is now complete. ∎

Conformal mapping of an annulus onto a circularly slit disk

According to Remark

with

{f_{x}(\partial\mathbb{D})=\partial\mathbb{D}}

Remark 5.2

The proof of Lemma

\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(z,a)

\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(z,a):=(z-a)\prod\limits_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{(r^{2n}z-a)(r^{2n}a-z)}{(r^{2n}z-z)(r^{2n}a-a)}=\,(z-a)\prod\limits_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{(1-r^{2n}z/a)(1-r^{2n}a/z)}{(1-r^{2n})^{2}},

see
[

It is known
[

that for each

g_{a}(z):=\frac{1}{|a|}\frac{\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(z,a)}{\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(z,1/\overline{a})}

\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt:\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}

[

\displaystyle\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(a,z)

\displaystyle=-\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(z,a)\,,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(\overline{z},\overline{a})

\displaystyle=\overline{\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(z,a)}\,,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(1/z,1/a)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(z,a)}{za}\,,

(5.7)

\displaystyle\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(r^{2}z,a)

\displaystyle=-\frac{a\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(z,a)}{z}\,,

(5.8)

In particular, if

g_{x}(\overline{z})=\overline{g_{x}(z)}
. Hence

, and it follows that

\mathbb{D}

f_{x}(z)=-\frac{1}{x}\frac{\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(z,x)}{\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(z,1/x)}\,

(5.9)

for any
x\in(0,1)
.

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

To prove (A),
notice that according to (
5.9
), for each
x\in(r,1)
,
f_{x}
extends to a meromorphic function in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
. By (
5.3
), the zeroes of
\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(\cdot,1/x)
are exactly the points
{z_{k}:=r^{2k}/x}
with
{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
. Hence, the extension of
f_{x}
is holomorphic in
\mathbb{A}_{r,x}
. Moreover,
{(x,z)\mapsto f_{x}(z)}
is of class
C^{\infty}
in
\{(x,z)\colon z\in\mathbb{A}_{r,x},\,x\in(r,1)\}
because the function
ω
is holomorphic in
{\mathbb{C}^{*}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}}
.

Proof of (B)
. For all
x,\alpha\in(r,1)
, we have

f_{\alpha}(x)=-\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(x,\alpha)}{\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(x,1/\alpha)}{\mathrel{\to}^{\raise 1.50694pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:prime1})$}}}-\frac{1}{\alpha}\frac{\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(\alpha,x)}{\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(1/\alpha,x)}{\mathrel{\to}^{\raise 1.50694pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle(\ref{eq:prime3})$}}}\frac{1}{x}\frac{\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(\alpha,x)}{\hskip 0.50003pt\text{\textomega}\hskip 0.50003pt(\alpha,1/x)}=-f_{x}(\alpha)\,.

From this and the fact that
\alpha\mapsto f_{\alpha}(x)=-f_{x}(\alpha)
is
C^{\infty}
in
(r,1)
it follows immediately that

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}\alpha}\bigg|_{\alpha=x}f_{\alpha}(x)=-f_{x}^{\prime}(x)\,.

Using (
5.3
) and (
5.9
), we obtain

f^{\prime}_{x}(x)=\frac{1}{1-x^{2}}\prod_{n=1}^{+\infty}\frac{(1-r^{2n})^{2}}{(1-r^{2n}x^{2})(1-r^{2n}x^{-2})}>\frac{1}{1-x^{2}}.

It is worth mentioning that this inequality can be alternatively deduced from the extremal property of conformal mappings onto circularly slit disks, see Remark
2.2
, by comparing
f_{x}
with a suitable automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
restricted to
\mathbb{A}_{r}
.

Proof of (C)
. See Corollary
3.9
(B).

Proof of (D)
.
Fix
x\in(r,1)
. There exists a unique point
r_{x}\in\partial\mathbb{D}(0,r)
such that
{f_{x}(r_{x})=\Gamma^{+}_{x}}
. Recall that
f_{x}(\overline{z})=\overline{f_{x}(z)}
for all
{z\in\mathbb{A}_{x,r}}
. Since
f_{x}
is injective and
{f^{\prime}_{x}(x)>0}
, it follows that
\mathop{\mathtt{Im\hskip 2.09995pt}}f_{x}(z)>0
for some
{z\in\mathbb{A}_{r,x}}
if and only if
{\mathop{\mathtt{Im\hskip 2.09995pt}}z>0}
. In particular,
{\mathop{\mathtt{Im\hskip 2.09995pt}}r_{x}>0}
. Note that the mapping properties of
f_{x}
show that
f_{x}^{\prime}(r_{x})=0
, but
f_{x}^{\prime\prime}(r_{x})\neq 0
. Moreover, with the help of the Schwarz Reflection Principle we see that
f_{x}
is locally injective in
\mathbb{A}_{r,x}\setminus\{r_{x},\overline{r_{x}\!}\,\}
.

Therefore,
r_{x}
is the unique solution to the equation
{f_{x}^{\prime}(z)=0}
in
{\{z\in\mathbb{A}_{r,x}\colon\mathop{\mathtt{Im\hskip 2.09995pt}}z>0\}}
.
Since in view of (A) the map
(x,z)\mapsto f_{x}^{\prime}(z)
is of class

,
the Implicit Function Theorem guarantees that this solution

is also of class
C^{\infty}

as a function of
x
.
∎

6.
Proof of Theorem
1

The proof of Theorem
1
is divided into two steps.
We will first show that the analogue of Conjecture
1
fails for certain once-punctured circularly slit disks of the form

\Omega=\mathbb{D}\setminus(\Gamma\cup\{\zeta^{*}\})\,,

where
\Gamma
is a non-degenerate circular arc centered at the origin and
\zeta^{*}
is a point in
\mathbb{D}\setminus\Gamma
. In other words, formula (
2.1
) does not hold for such choice of
\Omega
.
In Section
6.2
we will see that if the degenerate boundary component
\{\zeta^{*}\}
is replaced by a compact set
\Upsilon
consisting of a finite number of non-degenerate circular arcs, then it is still possible to show that formula (
2.1
) fails provided that
\Upsilon
lies within a sufficiently small neighbourhood of
\zeta^{*}
.

6.1.
Degenerate case

Fix
{r\in(0,1)}
and
{x_{0}\in(\sqrt{r},1)\subset\mathbb{A}_{r}}
. Using the notation introduced in Section
5
, for
x\in(r,x_{0}]
we let

\phi_{x}:=T_{x}\circ f_{x}\circ f_{x_{0}}^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad{\Gamma(x):=T_{x}(\Gamma_{x})},\quad\text{ where~}~T_{x}(z):=\frac{z-f_{x}(x_{0})}{1-f_{x}(x_{0})z}.

Then
\phi_{x}
maps the circularly slit disk
\Omega_{0}:=f_{x_{0}}(\mathbb{A}_{r})=\mathbb{D}\setminus\Gamma_{x_{0}}
conformally onto

{\mathbb{D}\setminus\Gamma(x)}
, with
{\phi_{x}(0)=0}
and
{\phi_{x}(\partial\mathbb{D})=\partial\mathbb{D}}
. The set

\Gamma(x)
is a closed subarc of the circle
T_{x}\big(\partial\mathbb{D}(0,x)\big)
symmetric w.r.t. the real line.

We will see that there exists
x^{*}\in(r,x_{0})
and
{\zeta^{*}\in(-x_{0},0)}
such that

{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial(\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\})\big)<{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial\phi_{x^{*}}(\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\})\big).

(6.1)

Using this fact, it is easy to show that the formula (
2.1
) of Conjecture
1
does not hold for
\Omega:=\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\}
and
{z:=0}
.
The proof of (
6.1
) is based on the following lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
0<r<x_{0}<1
be fixed. Then
for any
\,\varepsilon\in(0,x_{0})
there exists
x^{*}\in(r,x_{0})
and
\delta\in(0,\varepsilon)
such that

(i)

\phi_{x^{*}}(\xi)<\xi
for all
\xi\in(-x_{0},-x_{0}+\delta)
;

(ii)

{\mathsf{dist}}(0,\Gamma(x^{*}))>x_{0}-\delta
.

We postpone the proof to the end of this section.
Choose any
x_{0}\in(\sqrt{r},1)
and, taking Lemma
6.1
for granted, apply it with
{\varepsilon:=x_{0}-r/x_{0}}
. By assertion (ii), there exists
{\zeta^{*}\in(-x_{0},-x_{0}+\delta)}
such that
|\zeta^{*}|<{\mathsf{dist}}(0,\Gamma(x^{*}))
.
In combination with (i), this leads to

{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial\phi_{x^{*}}(\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\})\big)=\min\big\{|\phi_{x^{*}}(\zeta^{*})|,{\mathsf{dist}}(0,\Gamma(x^{*}))\big\}>|\zeta^{*}|.

(6.2)

Then (
6.1
) holds because, trivially,
{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial(\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\})\big)<|\zeta^{*}|
.

Now, if formula (
2.1
) in Conjecture
1
would hold for
\Omega:=\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\}
and
{z:=0}
, then

{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial\phi_{x^{*}}(\Omega)\big)\leqslant{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial f_{0,0}(\Omega)\big)\quad\text{or}\quad{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial\phi_{x^{*}}(\Omega)\big)\leqslant{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial f_{0,1}(\Omega)\big),

with
f_{0,0}={\sf id}_{\Omega}
and
f_{0,1}=f_{r/x_{0}}\circ\big(z\mapsto r/z\big)\circ f_{x_{0}}^{-1}
. However, the first inequality is the opposite of (
6.1
), while the latter one cannot hold because of the choice of
x_{0}
and
\varepsilon>0
; indeed,

{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial f_{0,1}(\Omega)\big)\leqslant{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial f_{r/x_{0}}(\mathbb{A}_{r})\big)=\frac{r}{x_{0}}=x_{0}-\varepsilon\leqslant x_{0}-\delta<|\zeta^{*}|.

(6.3)

To facilitate the proof of Lemma
6.1
, we will first establish the following statement.

Lemma 6.2
.

The map
x\mapsto{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\Gamma(x)\big)
is locally Lipschitz on
(r,x_{0}]
.

Proof.

We know from Lemma
5.1
that
x\mapsto\Gamma_{x}^{+}
, where
\Gamma_{x}^{+}
denotes the endpoint of the circular arc
\Gamma_{x}
in the upper half-plane, as well as
x\mapsto f_{x}(x_{0})
are
C^{\infty}
– functions on
(r,1)
. Hence

x\mapsto T_{x}(\Gamma^{+}_{x})=\frac{\Gamma_{x}^{+}-f_{x}(x_{0})}{1-f_{x}(x_{0})\Gamma^{+}_{x}}

is also
C^{\infty}
, in particular locally Lipschitz.

Note that for
x\in(r,x_{0})
the center of the circular arc
\Gamma(x)
is not the origin; in fact, it belongs to
(-1,0)
because
f_{x}(x_{0})>0
. It follows that

(r,x_{0}]\ni x\mapsto{\mathsf{dist}}(0,\Gamma(x))=|T_{x}(\Gamma^{+}_{x})|

is indeed locally Lipschitz.
∎

Now we are ready to
prove Lemma
6.1
.

Step 1.
We first show that

\phi_{x}^{\prime}(\xi)\to 1\quad\text{uniformly on~}~[-x_{0},0]\quad\text{as $~x\to x_{0}$}.

(6.4)

Indeed,
\phi_{x}\circ f_{x_{0}}=T_{x}\circ f_{x}
and hence

\phi_{x}^{\prime}(\xi)\,f_{x_{0}}^{\prime}(f_{x_{0}}^{-1}(\xi))=\big(T_{x}\circ f_{x}\big)^{\prime}(f_{x_{0}}^{-1}(\xi))\quad\text{for all~}~\xi\in[-x_{0},0].

By Lemma
5.1
(A), the map
(\xi,x)\mapsto\big(T_{x}\circ f_{x}\big)^{\prime}(\xi)
is of class
C^{\infty}
on the set

\big\{(\xi,x)\colon\xi\in(r^{2}/x,1/x),\,x\in(r,1)\big\}.

Since
T_{x_{0}}={\sf id}_{\mathbb{D}}
, it follows that, as
x\to x_{0}
,
\big(T_{x}\circ f_{x}\big)^{\prime}\to f^{\prime}_{x_{0}}
uniformly on

[r,x_{0}]={f_{x_{0}}^{-1}\big([-x_{0},0]\big)}
.
It only remains to observe that
f^{\prime}_{x_{0}}
does not vanish on
[r,x_{0}]
.

Step 2.
Let
x\in(r,1)
and denote by

q(x):=\phi_{x}(-x_{0})=T_{x}(-x)=-\frac{x+f_{x}(x_{0})}{1+f_{x}(x_{0})x}

the point of intersection of
\Gamma(x)
with the real line. Lemma
5.1
shows that
q:(r,1)\to\mathbb{R}
is differentiable with

q^{\prime}(x_{0})=-\big(1-(1-x_{0}^{2})f_{x_{0}}^{\prime}(x_{0})\big)>0\quad\text{and}\quad q(x_{0})=-x_{0}.

Hence, there is
x_{1}\in(r,x_{0})
such that

\phi_{x}(-x_{0})=q(x)<-x_{0}
for all
x\in(x_{1},x_{0})
. In particular, for any
x\in(x_{1},x_{0})
we can define

\delta(x):=\sup\big\{\alpha\in(0,x_{0})\,\colon\phi_{x}(\xi)<\xi~\text{~for all~}~\xi\in[-x_{0},-x_{0}+\alpha)\big\}>0\,.

We claim that

\lim\limits_{x\nearrow x_{0}}\frac{\delta(x)}{|x-x_{0}|}=+\infty\,.

(6.5)

In order to prove this, fix an arbitrary
\varepsilon_{1}>0
. Thanks to (
6.4
), we can find
x_{2}\in(x_{1},x_{0})
such that

\phi_{x}^{\prime}<1+\varepsilon_{1}
on
[-x_{0},0]
for all
x\in(x_{2},x_{0})
. Then, for any fixed
x\in(x_{2},x_{0})
and any
\xi\in[-x_{0},0]
satisfying
\xi\leqslant-x_{0}-\big(\phi_{x}(-x_{0})+x_{0}\big)/\varepsilon_{1},

we have

\displaystyle\phi_{x}(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{x}(-x_{0})+{\int\limits_{-x_{0}}^{\xi}\!\phi_{x}^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\phi_{x}(-x_{0})+(1+\varepsilon_{1})(\xi+x_{0})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\phi_{x}(-x_{0})+(\xi+x_{0})-(\phi_{x}(-x_{0})+x_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi\,.

This shows that

\delta(x)\geqslant-\frac{\phi_{x}(-x_{0})+x_{0}}{\varepsilon_{1}}\quad\text{ for all }x\in(x_{2},x_{0})\,,

and therefore, recalling that
x_{0}=-\phi_{x_{0}}(-x_{0})
, we have

\liminf\limits_{x\nearrow x_{0}}\frac{\delta(x)}{x_{0}-x}\geqslant\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{1}}\liminf\limits_{x\nearrow x_{0}}\frac{\phi_{x_{0}}(-x_{0})-\phi_{x}(-x_{0})}{x_{0}-x}=\frac{q^{\prime}(x_{0})}{\varepsilon_{1}}\,.

Since
q^{\prime}(x_{0})>0
as we have observed above and since
\varepsilon_{1}>0
can be chosen arbitrarily small, the latter inequality implies (
6.5
).

Step 3.
Since by Lemma
6.2
,
x\mapsto{\mathsf{dist}}(0,\Gamma(x))
is locally Lipschitz and
{{\mathsf{dist}}(0,\Gamma(x_{0}))=x_{0}}
, there exist a constant
{M>0}
and a point
{x_{3}\in(r,x_{0})}
such that

{\mathsf{dist}}(0,\Gamma(x))>x_{0}-M(x_{0}-x)\quad\text{for all~}~{x\in(x_{3},x_{0})}.

In view of (
6.5
), there exists
x^{*}\in(x_{3},x_{0})
such that

M(x_{0}-x^{*})\leqslant\min\{\varepsilon,\delta(x^{*})\}=:\delta\,,

and with this choice of
\delta
both conditions (i) and (ii) hold by construction. ∎

Non-degenerate case

As we have seen in the previous section, for any
r\in(0,1)
and any
x_{0}\in(\sqrt{r},1)
, there exists
\zeta^{*}\in f_{x_{0}}(\mathbb{A}_{r})
such that formula (
2.1
) in Conjecture
1
fails for
\Omega:=f_{x_{0}}(\mathbb{A}_{r})\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\}
and
{z:=0}
.

We will now use Theorem
4
to show that for any
m\geqslant 3
, there exist
m-2
pairwise disjoint closed non-degenerate arcs
\Gamma_{2},\ldots,\Gamma_{m-1}\subset f_{x_{0}}(\mathbb{A}_{r})
located on circles centered at the origin, such that formula (
2.1
) fails also for

\Omega:=f_{x_{0}}(\mathbb{A}_{r})\,\big\backslash\bigcup_{j=2}^{m-1}\Gamma_{j}=\mathbb{D}\,\big\backslash\bigcup_{j=1}^{m-1}\Gamma_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad{z:=0},

where we set
{\Gamma_{1}:=\Gamma_{x_{0}}}
and
\Gamma_{0}:=\partial\mathbb{D}
.

To this end we consider
m-2
sequences of arcs
(\Gamma^{j}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
j=2,\ldots,m-1
, such that
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
fixed,
\Gamma^{2}_{n},\ldots,\Gamma^{m-1}_{n}\subset f_{x_{0}}(\mathbb{A}_{r})
are pairwise disjoint closed non-degenerate arcs located on circles centered at the origin and
\Upsilon_{n}:=\bigcup_{j=2}^{m-1}\Gamma^{j}_{n}\subset\mathbb{D}(\zeta^{*},1/n)
.
Denote
{\Omega_{0}:=f_{x_{0}}(\mathbb{A}_{r})}
. Further, for each
{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, let
{\Omega_{n}:=\Omega_{0}\setminus\Upsilon_{n}}

and denote by

, with
{z\in\Omega_{n}}
and
{j=0,\ldots,m-1}
, the conformal mappings of
\Omega_{n}
onto circularly slit disks, as introduced in Remark
2.1
. The slit disk mappings of the domain

will be denoted, as in Section

, by

and

.

Clearly,
f^{0,0}_{n}={\sf id}_{\Omega_{n}}
. Taking into account that
\phi_{x^{*}}
is holomorphic at

, it is easy to see that, as

{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial\Omega_{n}\big)\to{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial\,(\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\})\big)~\text{~and~}~{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial\phi_{x^{*}}(\Omega_{n})\big)\to{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial\phi_{x^{*}}(\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\}\big).

Hence in view of (

), for all

large enough we have

{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial f^{0,0}_{n}(\Omega_{n})\big)={\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial\Omega_{n}\big)<{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial\phi_{x^{*}}(\Omega_{n})\big).

(6.6)

By relation (

) in Theorem

applied with

,
{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial f^{0,1}_{n}(\Omega_{n})\big)\to{\mathsf{dist}}\big(\partial f_{0,1}(\Omega_{0}\setminus\{\zeta^{*}\})\big)
as

. Therefore, in accordance with (

) and (

), for all

large enough we also have

{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial f^{0,1}_{n}(\Omega_{n})\big)<{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial\phi_{x^{*}}(\Omega_{n})\big).

(6.7)

Finally, for each

) in Theorem

{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial f^{0,j}_{n}(\Omega_{n})\big)<{\mathsf{dist}}\big(0,\partial\phi_{x^{*}}(\Omega_{n})\big)

provided

From (

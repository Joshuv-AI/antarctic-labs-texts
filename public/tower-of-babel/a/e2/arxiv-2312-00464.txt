Introduction

The permanent of an
n\times n
matrix
A=(a_{ij})
is defined by

\mbox{per}(A)=\sum\limits_{\sigma\in S_{n}}a_{1\sigma(1)}a_{2\sigma(2)}...a_{n\sigma(n)},

where
S_{n}
is the symmetric group of degree
n
.

Let
\Omega_{n}
denote the set of all doubly stochastic matrices of order
n
and let
J_{n}
be an
n\times n
matrix with each entry equal to
\frac{1}{n}
.

For positive integers
n
and
k
with
\left(1\leq k\leq n\right)
,
Q_{k,n}
denotes the set

\{\left(i_{1},...,i_{k}\right)/1\leq i_{1}<...<i_{k}\leq n\}
.
For
\alpha,\beta\in Q_{k,n}
, let
A\left(\alpha/\beta\right)
be the submatrix of
A
obtained by deleting the rows indexed by
\alpha
and columns indexed by
\beta
and
A[\alpha/\beta]
be the submatrix of
A
with rows and columns indexed by
\alpha
and
\beta
respectively.

For
1\leq k\leq n
, the
k^{th}
order subpermanent of
A
is defined by

\sigma_{k}(A)=\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta\in Q_{k,n}}\text{per}\left(A[\alpha/\beta]\right)

In this paper, we use the following results quoted by Minc
[
25
]
:

If
A
and
B
are two
n\times n
matrices and
1\leq k\leq n
, then

\text{per}(A)=\sum\limits_{\beta\in Q_{k,n}}\text{per}\left(A[\alpha/\beta]\right)\text{per}\left(A\left(\alpha/\beta\right)\right),\text{for }\alpha\in Q_{k,n},

and

\text{per}(A+B)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}S_{k}\left(A,B\right),

where
S_{k}\left(A,B\right)=\sum\limits_{\alpha,\beta\in Q_{k,n}}\text{per}(A[\alpha/\beta])\text{per}(B\left(\alpha/\beta\right))
,
per
\left(A[\alpha/\beta]\right)=1
when
k=0

and per
\left(B\left(\alpha/\beta\right)\right)=1
when
k=n
.

Several authors have considered the problem of finding an upper bound for the permanent of a convex combination of
J_{n}
and
A
, where
A\in\Omega_{n}
. In particular, Marcus and Minc
[
13
]
have proposed a conjecture, which states that if
A\in\Omega_{n},n\geq 2

\left(\frac{nJ_{n}-A}{n-1}\right)\leq

(A),
with equality holding if and only if
A=J_{n},n\geq 3

n=2
, or if
A
is a positive semi-definite symmetric matrix, or if
A
is in a sufficiently small neighbourhood of
J_{n}
.
Wang
[
16

proved the Marcus - Minc conjecture for
n=3
with a revised statement of the case of equality and he proposed a conjecture that per
\left(\frac{nJ_{n}+A}{n+1}\right)\leq

(A)

Rather than consider a particular convex combination of
A

[

\mbox{per}\left(tJ_{n}+\left(1-t\right)A\right)\leq\mbox{per}(A)

holds for all

and

or
t=\frac{3}{2}

Another conjecture on permanents is Dittert’s conjecture [
[
25
]
, conjecture 28].
Let
K_{n}
denote the set of all
n\times n
real nonnegative matrices whose entries have sum
n
. Let
\phi
denote a real valued function defined on

\phi(X)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}+\prod_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}-per(X)

A matrix

is called a
\phi
-maximizing matrix on
K_{n}
if
\phi(A)\geq\phi(X)
for all
X\in K_{n}
. An

is called fully indecomposable if it does not contain an

zero submatrix with

E. Dittert conjectured that [
[
25
]
, conjecture 28]

is the unique

-maximizing matrix on
K_{n}
. R.Sinkhorn
[

]
proved that every

-maximizing matrix on

has a positive permanent and also that the conjecture is true for

.

,

]

-maximizing matrices. The following Lemmas and theorems are due to SG Hwang
[

]
.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let

be a

-maximizing matrix on

, and let

If columns

and
t
of

have either the same sums or the same (0, 1) patterns, then the matrix obtained from

by replacing each of columns

and

by its average is also a

-maximizing matrix on

. A similar statement holds for rows.

He also proved that Dittert’s conjecture is true for positive semidefinite symmetric matrices in

and for matrices in a sufficiently small neighbourhood of

in
K_{n}
.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let

-maximizing matrix on

•

\phi_{ij}(A)=\phi_{kl}(A)

.

•

and

.

Theorem 1.3
.

If

\phi
-maximizing matrix on

\phi_{ij}(A)=\phi(A)

Theorem 1.4
.

J_{3}
is the unique

-maximizing matrix on

.

Cheon and Wanless

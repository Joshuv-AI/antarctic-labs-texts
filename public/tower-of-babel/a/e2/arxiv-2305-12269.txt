Bounds for lacunary bilinear spherical and triangle maximal functions
Tainara Borges, Benjamin Foster

Abstract
We prove L p ​ ( ℝ d ) × L q ​ ( ℝ d ) → L r ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) bounds for certain lacunary bilinear maximal averaging operators with parameters satisfying the Hölder relation 1 / p + 1 / q = 1 / r 1/p+1/q=1/r . The boundedness region that we get contains at least the interior of the Hölder boundedness region of the associated single scale bilinear averaging operator. In the case of the lacunary bilinear spherical maximal function in d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , we prove boundedness for any p , q ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ] 2 p,q\in(1,\infty]^{2} , which is sharp up to boundary; we then show how to extend this result to a more degenerate family of surfaces where some curvatures are allowed to vanish. For the lacunary triangle averaging maximal operator, we have results in d ≥ 7 d\geq 7 , and the description of the sharp region will depend on a sharp description of the Hölder bounds for the single scale triangle averaging operator, which is still open.

Introduction and main results

Given a compactly supported finite Borel measure
\sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
an important class of maximal operators is given by

M_{\sigma}(f)(x)=\sup_{t>0}\left|\int f(x-tz)d\sigma(z)\right|.

In the particular case of
\sigma=\sigma_{d-1}
, the normalized surface measure in the unit sphere
S^{d-1}
, one gets the spherical maximal function
M_{\sigma_{d-1}}
, which we will denote by
\mathcal{S}
. The study of the boundedness properties of
\mathcal{S}
were initiated by Stein
[
Ste76
]
. He showed that
\mathcal{S}
is bounded in
L^{p}
if
d\geq 3
and
\frac{d}{d-1}<p\leq\infty
and that boundedness fails for
d\geq 2
and
p\leq\frac{d}{d-1}
. The remaining case
d=2
was proven later by Bourgain
[
Bou85
]
; this case was more complicated since the operator fails to be bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, requiring more delicate Fourier-analytic tools.

More general maximal functions are obtained when the supremum is taken over dyadic dilates of a subset
E\subset[1,2]
. In the case of
S=S^{d-1}
and
E=\{1\}
, one ends up with the so called lacunary spherical maximal function

\mathcal{S}_{lac}(f)(x)=\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\int_{S^{d-1}}f(x-2^{-l}z)d\sigma_{d-1}(z)\right|.

The operator
\mathcal{S}_{lac}
has better boundedness properties than the full spherical maximal function
\mathcal{S}
; it was shown by C. Calderón
[
Cal79
]
and Coifman and Weiss
[
CW78
]
that
\mathcal{S}_{lac}
is bounded in
L^{p}
for any
1<p\leq\infty
. Some generalizations of these facts for more general dilation sets
E
can be found in
[
SWW95
]
and
[
DV98
]
. One of the interesting consequences of the results in
[
DV98
]
is that for a compactly supported finite Borel measure
\sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, as long as its Fourier transform
\hat{\sigma}(\xi)
has some decay
|\hat{\sigma}(\xi)|\leq C|\xi|^{-a}
, with
a>0
then

M_{\sigma,lac}(f)(x)=\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|\int f(x-2^{-l}z)d\sigma(z)\right|

is bounded in
L^{p}
, for all
p>1
. Also, if
|\hat{\sigma}(\xi)|\leq C|\xi|^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}
and
d\geq 3
then
M_{\sigma}
is bounded in
L^{p}
for all
p>\frac{d-1}{d}
, recovering Stein’s result.

We are interested in proving some bilinear analogues of these results in the lacunary setting. The bilinear averages we are interested in are given by bilinear convolutions with a compactly supported finite Borel measure
\mu
in
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. Namely, for
t>0
, and
f,g\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, define the bilinear average at scale
t>0
as

(1)

\mathcal{A}_{\mu,t}(f,g)(x)=\int_{S^{2d-1}}f(x-ty)g(x-tz)\,d\mu(y,z),\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The associated lacunary spherical maximal function is then given by

(2)

\mathcal{M}_{\mu,lac}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathcal{A}_{\mu,2^{-l}}(f,g)(x)|.

In the particular case of
\mu=\sigma_{2d-1}
we might omit the measure
\mu
in the notation. By allowing any parameter
t>0
in the supremum above one gets the (full) bilinear spherical maximal function

(3)

\mathcal{M}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{t>0}|\mathcal{A}_{t}(f,g)(x)|.

For
d\geq 2
, the study of the Lebesgue bounds for
\mathcal{M}

has attracted a lot of attention in recent years. In particular, people have studied the boundedness region of the operator, that is, the description of all the parameters
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
r>0
such that
\mathcal{M}:L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}
is bounded. For both
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}_{lac}
, it is necessary to impose the Hölder relation
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=r^{-1}
because of scaling. The operator
\mathcal{M}
first appeared in
[
GGI+13
]
, and subsequently, its Lebesgue bounds were studied by multiple authors (
[
BGH+18
]
,
[
GHH21
]
,
[
HHY20
]
) until the description of the sharp boundedness region for
\mathcal{M}
in
d\geq 2
was finally settled by
[
JL20
]
(see Theorem
10
below for the statement of these sharp bounds). In that paper, they exploited an interesting coarea formula which allowed them to slice the integral in
S^{2d-1}
into integrals over lower dimensional spheres.

Even though the question of the boundedness region for
\mathcal{M}
was settled in
[
JL20
]
, the sharp boundedness region for the lacunary case in
d\geq 2
was still open and did not follow from the slicing strategy exploited in their paper. Indeed, when studying the boundedness properties of the bilinear spherical maximal function
\mathcal{M}
, Jeong and Lee
[
JL20
]
showed that if
F
is a continuous function defined in
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
and
(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then the following slicing formula holds

\int_{S^{2d-1}}F(y,z)d\sigma(y,z)=\int_{B^{d}(0,1)}\int_{S^{d-1}}F(y,\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}}z)d\sigma_{d-1}(z)(1-|y|^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\,dy.

In particular, for any
f,g\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(4)

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{t}(f,g)(x)=&\int_{B^{d}(0,1)}f(x-ty)\int_{S^{d-1}}g(x-t\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}}z)d\sigma_{d-1}(z)(1-|y|^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\,dy\\
=&\int_{B^{d}(0,1)}f(x-ty)A_{t(\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}})}(g)(x)(1-|y|^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\,dy,\end{split}

where
A_{t}f(x):=\int_{S^{d-1}}f(x-ty)d\sigma(y)
is the linear spherical average at scale
t
, and they used that to show

\mathcal{M}(f,g)(x)\lesssim Mf(x)\mathcal{S}g(x),

where
M
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. If we restrict ourselves to
t\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
in the hope of controlling
\mathcal{M}_{lac}(f,g)
by the product of
Mf(x)
with the lacunary (sub)linear spherical maximal function
\mathcal{S}_{lac}
, we quickly run into trouble. Even when
t=2^{-l}
with
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, one can observe that in the slicing formula (
4
), one needs to take into account spherical averages of
g
in all scales
2^{-l}\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}}
for any
|y|<1
. As a result their slicing strategy does not immediately imply a larger boundedness region for the lacunary bilinear maximal function than the one they prove for the full bilinear spherical maximal function, and we must instead turn to other methods.

Observe that the definition of
\mathcal{A}_{t}
makes sense for any
d\geq 1
. In
[
CZ22
]
the authors studied
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}_{lac}
in the case
d=1
and they proved the sharp bounds up to the boundary. The sharp boundedness region for
\mathcal{M}
in
d=1
was also obtained independently by
[
DR22
]
.

In this article, we almost entirely settle the question of Lebesgue bounds for
\mathcal{M}_{lac}
in
d\geq 2
, providing the lacunary counterpart of the boundedness results for
\mathcal{M}
in
[
JL20
]
and the bilinear analogue of the bounds for
\mathcal{S}_{lac}
in
[
Cal79
]
and
[
CW78
]
.

Theorem 1

(Bounds for the lacunary bilinear spherical maximal function)
.

Assume
d\geq 2
. Let
p,q\in(1,\infty]
and
r\in(0,\infty)
satisfy the Hölder relation
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
. Then

(5)

\|\mathcal{M}_{lac}(f,g)\|_{r}=\|\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}(f,g)|\|_{r}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{q}.

Moreover,
\mathcal{M}_{lac}
does not satisfy strong bounds
L^{1}\times L^{\infty}\rightarrow L^{1}
or
L^{\infty}\times L^{1}\rightarrow L^{1}
, but it satisfies weak bounds
\mathcal{M}_{lac}:L^{1}\times L^{\infty}\rightarrow L^{1,\infty}
and
\mathcal{M}_{lac}:L^{\infty}\times L^{1}\rightarrow L^{1,\infty}
.

\mathcal{R}_{1}

Theorem 5

(Bounds for the lacunary triangle averaging maximal function)
.

Assume
d\geq 7
. For any
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})\in\text{int}{(\mathcal{R}_{1})}
, and

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}

(11)

\|\mathcal{T}_{lac}(f,g)\|_{r}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{q}.

Moreover, for any

(0,0)

\|\mathcal{T}_{lac}(f,g)\|_{q}\lesssim\|f\|_{q}\|g\|_{\infty}.

Remark 6
.

One can also state the theorem above in terms of
(1/p,1/q,1/r)
in the interior of the Hölder boundedness region of
\mathcal{T}_{1}

\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}=:\{(1/p,1/q,1/r)\colon(p,q,r)\in[1,\infty]^{2}\times(0,\infty],\,1/p+1/q=1/r\,\text{ and }\|\mathcal{T}_{1}\|_{L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}}<\infty\}.

One difficulty is that the sharp description of
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}
is not known, and all we know so far is that it contains the Hölder exponents

There are several common features to deducing the desired bounds for the lacunary bilinear maximal operators. We generally follow a scheme of extending boundedness estimates for single scale averages to the lacunary maximal functions by proving decay bounds for pieces of the single averaging operator in which we localize the Fourier support in both variables separately.

The passage from the decay bounds for pieces of the single scale averaging operators
\mathcal{A}_{\mu,1}
to bounds for the lacunary bilinear maximal functions can be seen as an adaptation of some ideas in
[
HHY20
]
where, inspired by
[
DV98
]
in the linear case, they were able to prove bounds for the multi-scale operator
\mathcal{M}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{t>0}|\mathcal{A}_{t}(f,g)(x)|
by proving decay bounds for frequency localized pieces of the single scale operator
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{t\in[1,2]}|\mathcal{A}_{t}(f,g)|
.

In the proof of the decay bounds for frequency localized pieces of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and more generally
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbf{a}}_{1}
, one of the main innovations is that we combine the coarea formula used in
[
JL20
]
with polar coordinates in the ball to write in the case of
\mathcal{A}_{1}

(12)

\mathcal{A}_{1}(f,g)(x)=\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{d-1}(1-\lambda^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}}A_{\lambda}f(x)A_{\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}}g(x)d\lambda,

and this equality can be used to get decay bounds for the bilinear averages from the well understood decay bounds for linear spherical averages. A similar strategy is used for
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\mathbf{a}}
.

We first introduce in Section

L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{1}
,
1/p+1/q>1
, can be lifted to the Hölder bound
L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}
, where
1/r=1/p+1/q
by using Proposition 4.1 in
[
IPS22
]
), and the bounds for
\mathcal{T}_{1}
arriving in
L^{1}
are sharp. So Corollary 6 is the best one can get from the lifting bounds into
L^{1}
to Hölder bounds, but there could be some
r<1
for which a Hölder bound
(p,q,r)
holds but the bound
(p,q,1)
is false. In the proposition below we include some necessary conditions for the boundedness of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
that follow by adapting some examples in
[
GIKL22
]
.

The description of the sharp region for which one has
L^{p}
improving properties for
\mathcal{T}_{1}
would have interesting applications in Sparse domination and weighted bounds for
\mathcal{T}_{lac}
(see
[
PS22
]
,
[
BFO+23
]
). In the next proposition we prove some necessary conditions that might be be useful in the future.

Proposition 14
.

(Necessary conditions for the boundedness of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
in
d\geq 2
)
Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
r\in(0,\infty]
. If

\mathcal{T}_{1}:L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}\text{ is bounded, }

then

(17)

\begin{cases}\frac{1}{r}\leq\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\\
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{d}{r}\\
\frac{d}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\leq d-1+\frac{1}{r}\\
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{d}{q}\leq d-1+\frac{1}{r}\end{cases}

Remark 15
.

Observe that the necessary conditions above do not impose restrictions for Hölder bounds for
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, that is, for any
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
1/r=1/p+1/q
the conditions above are trivially satisfied.

Proof of Proposition
14
.

We will give an example for each necessary condition to demonstrate them in order. First take
f_{M}=\chi_{B(0,2M)}
where
M>1
. For all
|x|<M
,
\mathcal{T}_{1}(f_{M},f_{M})(x)=1
, so

M^{d/r}\lesssim\|\mathcal{T}_{1}(f_{M},f_{M})\|_{r}\lesssim\|f_{M}\|_{p}\|f_{M}\|_{q}\lesssim M^{d(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q})}\,\text{ for all }M>1,

which implies
1/r\leq 1/p+1/q
.

Next take
f_{\delta}=\chi_{A_{\delta}}
where
A_{\delta}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon 1-\delta\leq|x|\leq 1+\delta\}
. Then for any
|x|<\delta
,

(18)

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}_{1}(f_{\delta},f_{\delta})(x)=\int_{O(d)}\chi_{A_{\delta}}(x-Ru)\chi_{A_{\delta}}(x-Rv)d\mu(R)=\int_{O(d)}d\mu(R)=1\end{split}

since
1-\delta<1-|x|\leq|x-Ru|\leq 1+|x|<1+\delta
, for any
R\in O(d)
since
|u|=1
.
That gives

\delta^{d/r}\lesssim\|\mathcal{T}_{1}(f_{\delta},f_{\delta})\|_{r}\lesssim\|f_{\delta}\|_{p}\|f_{\delta}\|_{q}=|A_{\delta}|^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}}\sim{\delta}^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}}\,\text{ for all }\delta\ll 1,

so
1/p+1/q\leq d/r
.

Take
f_{\delta}=\chi_{B(0,\delta)}
and
g_{\delta}=\chi_{A_{\delta}}
. Suppose
||x|-1|\leq\frac{1}{2}\delta
, then

(19)

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}_{1}(f_{\delta},g_{\delta})(x)=&\int_{O(d)}\chi_{B(0,\delta)}(x-Ru)\chi_{A_{\delta}}(x-Rv)d\mu(R)\\
\geq&\int_{O(d)}\chi_{|x-Ru|<\delta}d\mu(R)\end{split}

since for any
|x-Ru|<\delta
, one has
x-Rv\in A_{\delta}
. Therefore

(20)

\begin{split}\mathcal{T}_{1}(f_{\delta},g_{\delta})(x)\geq\int_{O(d)}\chi_{B(x,\delta)}(Ru)d\mu(R)=\int_{S^{d-1}}\chi_{B(x,\delta)}(y)d\sigma(y)\gtrsim\delta^{d-1}\end{split}

and from

\delta^{d-1+1/r}\lesssim\|\mathcal{T}_{1}(f_{\delta},g_{\delta})\|_{r}\lesssim\|\chi_{B(0,\delta)}\|_{p}\|g_{\delta}\|_{q}=\delta^{d/p+1/q}

we get
d/p+1/q\leq d-1+1/r
. The last necessary condition follows from the symmetry
\mathcal{T}_{1}(f,g)=\mathcal{T}_{1}(g,f)
.
∎

2.3
Bilinear multiplier boundedness criteria

For a multiplier
m(\xi,\eta)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
we denote by
T_{m}
the bilinear operator given by

T_{m}(f,g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{g}(\eta)m(\xi,\eta)e^{2\pi ix\cdot(\xi+\eta)}d\xi d\eta.

These will appear naturally throughout the paper as Fourier transforms of single-scale bilinear averaging operators.

Theorem 16

(
[
GHS20
]
)
.

Let
1\leq q<4
and set
M_{q}=\left\lfloor\frac{2d}{4-q}\right\rfloor+1
. Let
m(\xi,\eta)
be a function in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\cap\mathcal{C}^{M_{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
satisfying

(21)

\|\partial^{\alpha}m\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{0}<\infty\text{ for all multiindices }\alpha\text{ with }|\alpha|\leq M_{q}.

Then there is a constant
A
depending on
d
and
q
such that the associated operator
T_{m}
with multiplier
m
satisfies

(22)

\|T_{m}\|_{L^{2}\times L^{2}\rightarrow L^{1}}\leq AC_{0}^{1-\frac{q}{4}}\|m\|_{L^{q}}^{q/4}.

As an immediate corollary of the above theorem, one has the following.

Corollary 17
.

Let
m(\xi,\eta)
be a smooth compactly supported function in
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
such that

\|\partial^{\alpha}m\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{0}\,\text{ for all multi-indices }\alpha,\text{ with }|\alpha|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{2d}{3}\right\rfloor+1.

Then there exists a constant
A
depending on
d
such that the associated operator
T_{m}
with multiplier
m
satisfies

\|T_{m}\|_{L^{2}\times L^{2}\rightarrow L^{1}}\leq AC_{0}|\text{supp}(m)|^{1/4}.

3
Decomposition of the operator and bounds for the pieces in the Hölder range

We start by describing the decomposition used in
[
HHY20
]
. Choose a radial function
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\hat{\varphi}
is nonnegative, radially decreasing and

(23)

\hat{\varphi}(\xi)=\begin{cases}1,\text{ if }|\xi|\leq 1\\
0,\text{ if }|\xi|\geq 2\end{cases}.

Let
\hat{\psi}(\xi)=\hat{\varphi}(\xi)-\hat{\varphi}(2\xi)
, which is supported in
\{1/2<|\xi|<2\}
. Then

(24)

\hat{\varphi}(\xi)+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\hat{\psi}(2^{-j}\xi)\equiv 1.

Define for all
i,j\geq 1
.

(25)

\mathcal{A}_{1}^{i,j}(f,g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{g}(\eta)\hat{\sigma}(\xi,\eta)\hat{\psi}(2^{-i}\xi)\hat{\psi}(2^{-j}\eta)e^{2\pi ix\cdot(\xi+\eta)}d\xi d\eta.

If
i=0
, replace
\hat{\psi}(2^{-i}\xi)
by
\hat{\varphi}(\xi)
in the expression above, and similarly if
j=0
. Then one has

\mathcal{A}_{1}(f,g)(x)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\mathcal{A}_{1}^{i,j}(f,g)(x).

Proposition 18
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
r\in(0,\infty]
, such that
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
. Then there exists
C>0
such that for all
i,j\geq 0

(26)

\|\mathcal{A}^{i,j}_{1}(f,g)\|_{r}\leq C\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{q}.

Proof.

Denote by
\psi_{i}(x)=2^{id}\psi(2^{i}x)
. Assume
i,j\geq 1
. One can easily see that

(27)

\|\mathcal{A}^{i,j}_{1}(f,g)\|_{r}=\|\mathcal{A}_{1}(\psi_{i}*f,\psi_{j}*g)\|_{r}.

Now using Corollary
8
,

(28)

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}_{1}(\psi_{i}*f,\psi_{j}*g)\|_{r}\leq&C\|\psi_{i}*f\|_{p}\|\psi_{j}*g\|_{q}\\
\leq&C\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{q}\end{split}

where we used Young’s inequality and the fact that
\|\psi_{i}\|_{1}=\|\psi\|_{1}\lesssim 1
. The case where
i=0
or
j=0
follows similarly.
∎

Proposition 19
.

Let
d\geq 2
. Then for every
i,j\geq 1

(29)

\|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{i,j}(f,g)\|_{1}\lesssim 2^{-(i+j)\frac{d-1}{2}}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}.

Remark 20
.

The attentive reader might notice that the exact value of the negative power of
2^{i+j}
that we get in the proposition above is not very important to run the proof of Theorem
1
later, so one could also use the estimates for the pieces of
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
from
[
HHY20
]
, or the bilinear multiplier theorem in
[
GHS20
]
to get the proposition above with a worse negative power. One advantage of the proof below is that it reduces things to spherical averages in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and it can be adapted to take care of more general averaging operators, like the bilinear averages
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\mathbf{a}}
for which we will prove decay bounds in Proposition
30
. Moreover, having a better decay power in this proposition is interesting on its own, and might have other applications in the study of the sharp
L^{p}
improving properties of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
for example.

Proof.

Let us use the notation
D_{t}(f)(x)=f(tx)
. Observe that by using the slicing formula (
4
) followed by polar coordinates in the ball
B^{d}(0,1)
one gets that

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{1}(f,g)(x)=&\int_{B^{d}(0,1)}f(x-y)\int_{S^{d-1}}g(x-\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}}z)d\sigma(z)(1-|y|^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}}dy\\
=&\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{d-1}(1-\lambda^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\left\{\int_{S^{d-1}}f(x-\lambda w)d\sigma(w)\right\}\left\{\int_{S^{d-1}}g(x-\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}z)d\sigma(z)\right\}d\lambda\\
=&\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{d-1}(1-\lambda^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}}A_{\lambda}(f)(x)A_{\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}}(g)(x)d\lambda.\end{split}

Denote
\langle\lambda\rangle=\sqrt{1-\lambda^{2}}
, and
f^{i}=f*\psi_{i}
, where
\psi_{i}
is like in Proposition
18
. Then

\mathcal{A}_{1}^{i,j}(f,g)(x)=\mathcal{A}_{1}(f^{i},g^{j})(x)=\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{d-1}\langle\lambda\rangle^{d-2}A_{\lambda}(f^{i})(x)A_{\langle\lambda\rangle}(g^{j})(x)d\lambda.

Using Minkowski’s inequality for integrals followed by Hölder’s inequality gives

(30)

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}_{1}(f^{i},g^{j})\|_{1}\leq&\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{d-1}\langle\lambda\rangle^{d-2}\|A_{\lambda}(f^{i})\|_{2}\|A_{\langle\lambda\rangle}(g^{j})\|_{2}\,d\lambda.\end{split}

For any
\lambda>0
,

(31)

\begin{split}\|A_{\lambda}(f^{i})\|_{2}=&\|f*\psi_{i}*d\sigma_{\lambda}\|_{2}\\
=&\|\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{\psi}(2^{-i}\xi)\hat{\sigma}(\lambda\xi)\|_{2}\\
\lesssim&(\lambda 2^{i})^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\|f\|_{2}.\end{split}

Therefore

(32)

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}_{1}(f^{i},g^{j})\|_{1}\lesssim&2^{-(i+j)\frac{d-1}{2}}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{d-1}\langle\lambda\rangle^{d-2}\lambda^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\langle\lambda\rangle^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}d\lambda\\
=&2^{-(i+j)\frac{d-1}{2}}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\langle\lambda\rangle^{\frac{d-3}{2}}d\lambda\lesssim 2^{-(i+j)\frac{d-1}{2}}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}.\end{split}

∎

The following corollary will have a key role in the proof of the bounds for
\mathcal{M}_{lac}
.

Corollary 21
.

Assume
d\geq 2
. Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
and
r\in(0,\infty)
given by
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
. Then there exists
\delta(p,q,r,d)
such that

(33)

\|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{i,j}(f,g)\|_{r}\lesssim 2^{-(i+j)\delta}\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{q}.

Moreover, if
d\geq 5
then for all
q\in(1,\infty)
and
1/r=1+1/q
,

(34)

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{i,j}(f,g)\|_{r}\lesssim 2^{-(i+j)\delta}\|f\|_{1}\|g\|_{q}.\\
\|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{i,j}(f,g)\|_{r}\lesssim 2^{-(i+j)\delta}\|f\|_{q}\|g\|_{1}.\end{split}

Proof.

Estimate (
33
) follows immediately from interpolation of Propositions
18
and
19
.

To prove the last part of this corollary, we recall that it was shown in
[
HHY20
]
that for

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}^{ij}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{t\in[1,2]}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{g}(\eta)\hat{\psi}(2^{-i}\xi)\hat{\psi}(2^{-j}\eta)\hat{\sigma}(t\xi,t\eta)e^{2\pi ix\cdot(\xi+\eta)}d\xi d\eta|,

one has

\|\tilde{\mathcal{M}}^{i,j}(f,g)\|_{L^{2/3}}\lesssim(\max\{2^{i},2^{j}\})^{-\frac{d-4}{2}}\|f\|_{p_{1}}\|g\|_{p_{2}}

for all
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}\leq 2
with
3/2=1/p_{1}+1/p_{2}
.

In particular, since
\text{max}\{i,j\}\geq\frac{i+j}{2}

(35)

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{i,j}(f,g)\|_{L^{2/3}}&\leq\|\tilde{\mathcal{M}}^{ij}(f,g)\|_{L^{2/3}}\lesssim 2^{-(i+j)\frac{d-4}{4}}\|f\|_{1}\|g\|_{2},\\
\|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{i,j}(f,g)\|_{L^{2/3}}&\leq\|\tilde{\mathcal{M}}^{ij}(f,g)\|_{L^{2/3}}\lesssim 2^{-(i+j)\frac{d-4}{4}}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{1}.\\
\end{split}

Then interpolation of these bounds with the bounds in Proposition
18
give the estimates in (
34
).
∎

Remark 22
.

One could think that estimate (
34
) could potentially allow one to include the missing boundary pieces in Theorem
1
for
d\geq 5
. Unfortunately, that does not seem possible with the methods we exploit in this paper.

4
Proof of the bounds for the lacunary bilinear spherical maximal operator

Proof of Theorem
1
.

\mathcal{M}_{lac}
satisfies a weak type bound
L^{1}\times L^{\infty}\rightarrow L^{1,\infty}
(from
[
JL20
]
since
\mathcal{M}_{lac}
is dominated by
\mathcal{M}
).
To see that
\mathcal{M}_{lac}
can not be bounded from
L^{1}\times L^{\infty}\rightarrow L^{1}
one can adapt the example from
[
JL20
]
to show that for
g\equiv 1
and
0\leq f\in L^{1}

\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}(f,g)(x)\geq C\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{2^{-ld}}\int_{B(x,2^{-l})}f(y)dy=:M^{lac}f(x),

so the lacunary Hardy-Littlewood maximal function
M^{lac}
would be bounded in
L^{1}
which is false (for any
f\in L^{1}
which is not identically zero,
\frac{1}{|x|^{d}}\lesssim M^{lac}f(x)
for
|x|>>1
, so
M^{lac}f\notin L^{1}
).

The case
p=\infty
or
q=\infty
follows immediately from the known bounds for
\mathcal{M}
as stated in Theorem
10
. Thus, we can assume
p,q<\infty
.

The proof will follow from Corollary
21
and an adaptation of the ideas in Section 4 of
[
HHY20
]
.

Let

(36)

\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}(f,g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{g}(\eta)\hat{\sigma}_{2d-1}(2^{-l}\xi,2^{-l}\eta)e^{2\pi ix\cdot(\xi+\eta)}d\xi d\eta.

One can break down
\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}
into low-low, low-high, high-low and high-high frequencies as follows

(37)

\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}(f,g)(x)=-\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}^{00}(f,g)(x)+\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}^{0\infty}(f,g)(x)+\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}^{\infty 0}(f,g)(x)+\sum_{i,j\geq 1}\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}^{i,j}(f,g)(x),

where

(38)

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}^{00}(f,g)(x)=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{g}(\eta)\hat{\sigma}_{2d-1}(2^{-l}\xi,2^{-l}\eta)\hat{\varphi}(2^{-l}\xi)\hat{\varphi}(2^{-l}\eta)e^{2\pi ix\cdot(\xi+\eta)}d\xi d\eta,\\
\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}^{0\infty}(f,g)(x)=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{g}(\eta)\hat{\sigma}_{2d-1}(2^{-l}\xi,2^{-l}\eta)\hat{\varphi}(2^{-l}\xi)e^{2\pi ix\cdot(\xi+\eta)}d\xi d\eta,\\
\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}^{\infty 0}(f,g)(x)=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{g}(\eta)\hat{\sigma}_{2d-1}(2^{-l}\xi,2^{-l}\eta)\hat{\varphi}(2^{-l}\eta)e^{2\pi ix\cdot(\xi+\eta)}d\xi d\eta,\\
\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}^{i,j}(f,g)(x)=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{g}(\eta)\hat{\sigma}_{2d-1}(2^{-l}\xi,2^{-l}\eta)\hat{\psi}(2^{-i-l}\xi)\hat{\psi}(2^{-j-l}\eta)e^{2\pi ix\cdot(\xi+\eta)}d\xi d\eta.\end{split}

Denote
N(i,j;p,q,r)=\|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{i,j}\|_{L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}}
, which we know from Corollary

satisfies

N(i,j;p,q,r)\lesssim C(p,q,r)2^{-(i+j)\delta}

for some
\delta=\delta(d,p,q,r)>0.

Another important fact is the scale-invariance of localized pieces of the average: for all
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, and Hölder exponents
1/r=1/p+1/q
, one has

(39)

\|\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}^{i,j}\|_{L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}}=\|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{i,j}\|_{L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}}=:N(i,j;p,q,r).

which as observed in
[
HHY20
]
follows from the fact that

(40)

\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}^{i,j}(f,g)(x)=\mathcal{A}_{1}^{i,j}(D_{2^{-l}}f,D_{2^{-l}}g)(2^{l}x),\text{ for all }i,j\geq 0,

where
D_{2^{-l}}f(x)=f(2^{-l}x)
.

Proposition 23
.

For
d\geq 2
, one has that

(41)

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{LL}_{lac}(f,g)(x):=\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}^{00}(f,g)(x)|&\lesssim Mf(x)Mg(x),\\
\mathcal{M}^{LH}_{lac}(f,g)(x):=\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}^{0\infty}(f,g)(x)|&\lesssim Mf(x)\sup_{t\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}\left(\int_{S^{2d-1}}|g(x-tz)|d\sigma_{2d-1}(y,z)\right),\\

where
M
stands for the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator.
Since

(42)

\sup_{t>0}\left(\int_{S^{2d-1}}|g(x-tz)|d\sigma_{2d-1}(y,z)\right)\lesssim Mg(x),

one has that

(43)

[\mathcal{M}^{LL}_{lac}+\mathcal{M}^{LH}_{lac}+\mathcal{M}^{HL}_{lac}](f,g)(x)\lesssim Mf(x)Mg(x).

Using Hölder’s inequality and the boundedness of
M
in
L^{p}
for all
1<p\leq\infty,
one gets the following corollary.

Corollary 24
.

Let
d\geq 2
. Then

(44)

\|\mathcal{M}^{LL}_{lac}+\mathcal{M}^{LH}_{lac}+\mathcal{M}^{HL}_{lac}\|_{L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}}<\infty

for all
p,q\in(1,\infty]
,
r\in(0,\infty]
and
1/r=1/p+1/q
.

Proof of Proposition
23
.

Let us check the inequality for
\mathcal{M}_{lac}^{LH}
, since the others follow similarly. We follow the arguments in
[
HHY20
]
most closely.

Since
(f*\varphi)(x-y)\lesssim Mf(x)
for all

, one has

|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{0\infty}(f,g)(x)|\leq\int_{S^{2d-1}}|f*\varphi(x-y)||g(x-z)|d\sigma(y,z)\lesssim Mf(x)\cdot\int_{S^{2d-1}}|g(x-z)|d\sigma(y,z).

Therefore

\begin{split}\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}^{0\infty}(f,g)(x)|=&\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{0\infty}(D_{2^{-l}}f,D_{2^{-l}}g)(2^{l}x)|\\
\lesssim&\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}M(D_{2^{-l}}f)(2^{l}x)\cdot\int_{S^{2d-1}}|D_{2^{-l}}g(2^{l}x-z)|d\sigma(y,z)\\
\lesssim&Mf(x)\cdot\sup_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{S^{2d-1}}|g(x-2^{-l}z)|d\sigma(y,z).\end{split}

Then one just needs to observe that by the slicing formula (
4
) applied with one of the functions being constant, for any

one has

\begin{split}\int_{S^{2d-1}}|g(x-tz)|d\sigma(y,z)=&\int_{B^{d}(0,1)}|g(x-tz)||S^{d-1}|(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}}dz\\

∎

Define
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{N}}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}|\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}(f,g)(x)|
.

Here we observe that since

Our goal is to show that

is bounded by a constant independent of

. Moreover, in view of Corollary

, it suffices to prove this for the high-high frequency part of the operator. Thus, for every

, define the vector valued operator

\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{N}}^{i,j}:\{f_{l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\times\{g_{l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\longmapsto\{\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}(f_{l}*\psi_{i+l},g_{l}*\psi_{j+l})(x)\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}.

By the definition of

and the same computation as in

HHY20
]

then

(46)

\|\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{N}}^{i,j}(\{f_{l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\times\{g_{l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}})\|_{L^{r}(\ell^{\infty})}\lesssim A_{\mathcal{N}}(p,q,r)\|\{f_{l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\|_{L^{p}(\ell^{\infty})}\|\{g_{l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\|_{L^{q}(\ell^{\infty})}.

The second computation in
[
HHY20
]
, which exploits the equality (

and
1/r=1/p+1/q

(47)

\begin{split}\|\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{N}}^{i,j}(\{f_{l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\times\{g_{l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}})\|_{L^{r}(\ell^{r})}\lesssim&N(i,j;p,q,r)\|\{f_{l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\|_{L^{p}(\ell^{p})}\|\{g_{l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\|_{L^{q}(\ell^{q})}\\
\lesssim&N(i,j;p,q,r)\|\{f_{l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\|_{L^{p}(\ell^{1})}\|\{g_{l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\|_{L^{q}(\ell^{1})}.\end{split}

Interpolation of the bounds in (
46
) and (
47
) will then imply that for any

p,q\in(1,\infty),\text{ and }1/r=1/p+1/q

one has

(48)

\begin{split}\|\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{N}}^{i,j}(\{f_{l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\times&\{g_{l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}})\|_{L^{r}(\ell^{2r})}\\
\lesssim&A_{\mathcal{N}}(p,q,r)^{1/2}N(i,j;p,q,r)^{1/2}\|\{f_{l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\|_{L^{p}(\ell^{2})}\|\{g_{l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\|_{L^{q}(\ell^{2})}.\end{split}

Using bound (
48
) and the Littlewood-Paley Theorem (Theorem 6.1.2 in
[
Gra14
]
), for any
p,q>1

(49)

\begin{split}\left\|\sup_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}|\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}^{i,j}(f,g)|\right\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=&\left\|\sup_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}|\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}(f*\psi_{i+l},g*\psi_{j+l})|\right\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
=&\left\|\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{N}}^{i,j}(\{f*\psi_{i+l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\times\{g*\psi_{j+l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}})\right\|_{L^{r}(\ell^{\infty})}\\
\leq&\left\|\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{N}}^{i,j}(\{f*\psi_{i+l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\times\{g*\psi_{j+l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}})\right\|_{L^{r}(\ell^{2r})}\\
\lesssim&A_{\mathcal{N}}(p,q,r)^{1/2}N(i,j;p,q,r)^{1/2}\\
&\,\,\left\|\{f*\psi_{i+l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\ell^{2})}\left\|\{g*\psi_{j+l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\right\|_{L^{q}(\ell^{2})}\\
\lesssim&A_{\mathcal{N}}(p,q,r)^{1/2}N(i,j;p,q,r)^{1/2}\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{q}.\end{split}

Remark 25
.

The argument fails for
p=1
since with Littlewood-Paley theory we can only control
\|\{f*\psi_{i+l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\ell^{2})}\lesssim\|f\|_{1}
but not
\|\{f*\psi_{i+l}\}_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}\|_{L^{1}(\ell^{2})}
. Even if one tries to use this weak type control of the Littlewood-Paley pieces, one runs into the issue there are no bounds of the form

\|\mathcal{A}_{1}^{i,j}(f,g)\|_{L^{r,\infty}}\leq N(i,j;1,q,r)\|f\|_{L^{1,\infty}}\|g\|_{q}
.

To finish the proof for the interior points
p,q\in(1,\infty)
, we split into two cases for
r
. When
r<1
, we use that
\|\cdot\|_{r}^{r}
satisfies the triangle inequality, and for
r\geq 1
, we use that
\|\cdot\|_{r}
is a norm.

Case
r<1
:

(50)

\begin{split}A_{\mathcal{N}}(p,q,r)^{r}=&\|\sup_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}|\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}|\|^{r}_{L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}}\\
\leq&\|\mathcal{M}_{lac}^{LL}+\mathcal{M}_{lac}^{LH}+\mathcal{M}_{lac}^{HL}\|_{L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}}^{r}+\|\sum_{i,j\geq 1}\sup_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}|\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}^{i,j}|\|_{L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}}^{r}\\
\lesssim&1+\sum_{i,j\geq 1}\|\sup_{|l|\leq\mathcal{N}}|\mathcal{A}_{2^{-l}}^{i,j}|\|_{L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}}^{r}\\
\lesssim&1+A_{\mathcal{N}}(p,q,r)^{r/2}\sum_{i,j\geq 1}N(i,j;p,q,r)^{r/2}.\end{split}

Hence, we deduce

(51)

A_{\mathcal{N}}(p,q,r)\lesssim 1+\left\{\sum_{i,j\geq 1}N(i,j;p,q,r)^{r/2}\right\}^{2/r}.

By Corollary
21
one has

\sum_{i,j\geq 1}N(i,j;p,q,r)^{r/2}\lesssim\sum_{i,j\geq 1}2^{-(i+j)\delta r/2}\lesssim 1.

The case
r\geq 1
is similar but we use the triangle inequality for the norm
\|\cdot\|_{r}
to take care of the sum in
i,j
.

Therefore for any
p,q\in(1,\infty)
and
1/r=1/p+1/q

\sup_{\mathcal{N\in\mathbb{N}}}A_{\mathcal{N}}(p,q,r)\leq C

and this finishes the proof.

∎

Proposition 27

For any

Lee-Shuin class of averaging operators associated to degenerate surfaces

Let
d\geq 2
and
\mathbf{a}=(\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2})\in[1,\infty)^{2}

\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\mathbf{a}}
the average corresponding to the compact surface in Lee and Shuin’s paper
[
LS23
]
, which we denote by
\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{a}}
:

(61)

\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{a}}=\{(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon|y|^{\mathbf{a}_{1}}+|z|^{\mathbf{a}_{2}}=1\}

and

(62)

\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\mathbf{a}}(f,g)(x)=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{a}}}f(x-ty)g(x-tz)d\mu^{\mathbf{a}}(y,z)

where
\mu^{\mathbf{a}}
is the surface measure in
\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{a}}
.

Our goal will be to state the bound for the associated lacunary maximal function in terms of the Hölder boundedness region of
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{\mathbf{a}}
, namely,

\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{a}}=\left\{\left(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{r}\right)\colon(p,q,r)\in[1,\infty]^{2}\times(0,\infty]\colon 1/r=1/p+1/q\text{ and }\|\mathcal{A}^{\mathbf{a}}_{1}\|_{L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}}<\infty\right\}.

Fix
\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2}\geq 1
and
\mathbf{a}=(\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2})
. Let
\Phi^{\mathbf{a}}(y,z)=|y|^{\mathbf{a}_{1}}+|z|^{\mathbf{a}_{2}}-1
. One has
|\nabla\Phi^{\mathbf{a}}(y,z)|^{2}=\mathbf{a}_{1}^{2}|y|^{2(\mathbf{a}_{1}-1)}+\mathbf{a}_{2}^{2}|z|^{2(\mathbf{a}_{2}-1)}\neq 0
for all
(y,z)\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{a}}=(\Phi^{\mathbf{a}})^{-1}(\{0\})
so there exists
c^{\mathbf{a}}>0
and
C^{\mathbf{a}}>0
such that
c^{\mathbf{a}}\leq|\nabla\Phi^{\mathbf{a}}(y,z)|\leq C^{\mathbf{a}}
for
(y,z)\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{a}}
.

The
L^{p}\times L^{q}\rightarrow L^{r}
bounds for

(63)

\mathcal{M}^{\mathbf{a}}_{lac}(f,g)(x):=\sup_{t\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}|\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\mathbf{a}}(f,g)(x)|

are the same as those of the operator

(64)

\mathcal{N}^{\mathbf{a}}_{lac}(f,g)(x):=\sup_{t\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}|\mathcal{B}_{t}^{\mathbf{a}}(f,g)(x)|,

where

(65)

\mathcal{B}_{t}^{\mathbf{a}}(f,g)(x)=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{a}}}f(x-ty)g(x-tz)\dfrac{d\mu^{\mathbf{a}}(y,z)}{|\nabla\Phi^{\mathbf{a}}(y,z)|}.

For any
i,j\geq 0
, define

(66)

(\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\mathbf{a}})^{i,j}(f,g)=\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\mathbf{a}}(f^{i},g^{j})

where for
i\geq 1
,
\hat{f^{i}}(\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{\psi}(2^{-i}\xi)
and for
i=0
,
\hat{f^{0}}(\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi)\hat{\varphi}(\xi)
. In other words,

f^{i}=f*\psi_{i}
where
\psi_{i}(x)=2^{id}\psi(2^{i}x)
for
i\geq 1
, and
f^{0}=f*\varphi
.

Proposition 30
.

Assume
d\geq 2
. Let
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{r})\in\text{int}(\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{a}})
. Then there exists
\delta(p,q,r,d)>0
such that for all
i,j\geq 1

(67)

\|(\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\mathbf{a}})^{i,j}(f,g)\|_{r}\leq C2^{-(i+j)\delta}\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{q}.

Proof.

By interpolation with the bound

\|(\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\mathbf{a}})^{i,j}(f,g)\|_{r}\leq C\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{q},\,\forall\,\left(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{r}\right)\in\mathcal{R}^{\mathbf{a}}

it is enough to prove that there exists
\delta>0
such that

(68)

\|(\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\mathbf{a}})^{i,j}(f,g)\|_{1}\leq C2^{-(i+j)\delta}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}.

We do it by generalizing the proof given in Proposition
19
, which corresponds to the case
\mathbf{a}_{1}=\mathbf{a}_{2}=2
.

For
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
fixed, let

\begin{split}\Phi^{\mathbf{a}}_{y}(z)=&|y|^{\mathbf{a}_{1}}+|z|^{\mathbf{a}_{2}}-1\\
\Omega_{y}=&(\Phi_{y}^{\mathbf{a}})^{-1}(0).\end{split}

For all
z\in\Omega_{y}
,
|\nabla\Phi_{y}^{\mathbf{a}}(z)|=\mathbf{a}_{2}|z|^{\mathbf{a}_{2}-1}=\mathbf{a}_{2}(1-|y|^{\mathbf{a}_{1}})^{\frac{\mathbf{a}_{2}-1}{\mathbf{a}_{2}}}=:\mathbf{a}_{2}\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(y)^{\mathbf{a}_{2}-1}
.

(69)

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\mathbf{a}}(f,g)(x)=&\int_{\mathcal{S}^{\mathbf{a}}}f(x-y)g(x-z)\dfrac{d\mu_{\mathbf{a}}(y,z)}{|\nabla\Phi^{\mathbf{a}}(y,z)|}\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f(x-y)g(x-z)\delta(\Phi^{\mathbf{a}})dydz\\
=&\int_{|y|<1}f(x-y)\left(\int g(x-z)\delta(\Phi_{y}^{\mathbf{a}})dz\right)dy\\

=&\int_{|y|<1}f(x-y)\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(y)^{d-1}}\int_{|z|=\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(y)}g(x-z)d\sigma_{y}(z)\right)\frac{\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(y)^{d-\mathbf{a}_{2}}}{\mathbf{a}_{2}}dy.\end{split}

Denote

Af(x,t):=A_{t}f(x)=\int_{S^{d-1}}f(x-ty)d\sigma(y).

Then

(70)

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\mathbf{a}}(f,g)(x)=\frac{1}{\mathbf{a}_{2}}\int_{|y|<1}f(x-y)Ag(x,\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(y)){\omega_{\mathbf{a}}(y)^{d-\mathbf{a}_{2}}}dy.\end{split}

If we use polar coordinates in the unit ball this says that

(71)

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\mathbf{a}}(f,g)(x)=&\frac{1}{\mathbf{a}_{2}}\int_{0}^{1}t^{d-1}\int_{S^{d-1}}f(x-t\omega)d\sigma(\omega)Ag(x,(1-t^{\mathbf{a}_{1}})^{1/\mathbf{a}_{2}}){(1-t^{\mathbf{a}_{1}})^{\frac{d-\mathbf{a}_{2}}{\mathbf{a}_{2}}}}dt\\

Applying this equality for
f^{i}
and
g^{j}
and using Minkowski’s inequality, one gets

(72)

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\mathbf{a}}(f^{i},g^{j})\|_{1}\leq&\frac{1}{\mathbf{a}_{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\|A(f^{i})(x,t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\|A(g^{j})(x,(1-t^{\mathbf{a}_{1}})^{1/\mathbf{a}_{2}})\|_{L^{2}_{x}}t^{d-1}{(1-t^{\mathbf{a}_{1}})^{\frac{d-\mathbf{a}_{2}}{\mathbf{a}_{2}}}}dt\\
\lesssim&2^{-(i+j)\frac{d-1}{2}}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}\int_{0}^{1}t^{d-1-\frac{d-1}{2}}\left\{(1-t^{\mathbf{a}_{1}})^{1/\mathbf{a}_{2}}\right\}^{d-\mathbf{a}_{2}-\frac{d-1}{2}}dt\\

\end{split}

Observe that in the estimate above we used estimate (
31
) again, which gives

(73)

\|A(f^{i})(\cdot,t)\|_{2}\lesssim(t2^{i})^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\|f\|_{2}.

One can check that for
\mathbf{a}_{1}\geq 1
,
\int_{0}^{1}(1-t^{\mathbf{a}_{1}})^{p}dt<\infty
if
p>-1
.

Since
\frac{d-2\mathbf{a}_{2}+1}{2\mathbf{a}_{2}}>-1
, we get

\|\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\mathbf{a}}(f^{i},g^{j})\|_{1}\lesssim 2^{-(i+j)\frac{d-1}{2}}\|f\|_{2}\|g\|_{2}.

∎

the argument is similar as before, for

i,j\geq 1

(79)

m_{i,j,k}(\xi,\eta)=\hat{\mu}(\xi,\eta)\hat{\psi}(2^{-i}\xi)\hat{\psi}(2^{-j}\eta)\rho_{k}(\xi,\eta).

Here,
\psi
is the same as defined in (
23
), (
24
) and
\rho_{k}
are smooth functions with
\sum_{k\geq 0}\rho_{k}(\xi,\eta)\equiv 1
except at the origin and

(80)

\begin{split}\text{supp}(\rho_{k})&\subseteq\{(\xi,\eta)\colon 2^{-k-1}\leq|\sin(\theta)|\leq 2^{-k+1}\},\,\text{if }k\geq 1;\\
\text{supp}(\rho_{0})&\subseteq\{(\xi,\eta)\colon|\sin(\theta)|\geq 1/2\}.\end{split}

We can choose such a partition in such a way that when one defines

(81)

\rho^{l}(\xi,\eta)=\sum_{k=l}^{\infty}\rho_{k}(\xi,\eta),

then
\text{supp}(\rho^{l})
is contained in
\{(\xi,\eta)\colon|\sin(\theta)|\leq 2^{-l+1}\}
and
\rho^{l}\equiv 1
in
\{(\xi,\eta)\colon|\sin(\theta)|\leq 2^{-l}\}
.
In particular, that implies that the support of any derivative of
\rho^{l}
is contained in
\{(\xi,\eta)\colon 2^{-l}\leq|\sin(\theta)|\leq 2^{-l+1}\}
. The interested reader may find more details on how to construct such a partition of unity
\{\rho_{k}\}
in
[
PS20
]
.

One can observe that for
i,j,k\geq 1

\text{supp}(m_{i,j,k})\subseteq\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\colon 2^{i-1}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{i+1},2^{j-1}\leq|\eta|\leq 2^{j+1},2^{-k-1}\leq|\sin(\theta)|\leq 2^{-k+1}\}.

This allow us to estimate
|\text{supp}(m_{i,j,k})|
.

(82)

\begin{split}|\text{supp}(m_{i,j,k})|\leq&|\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\colon|\xi|\leq 2^{i+1},|\eta|\leq 2^{j+1},|\sin(\theta)|\leq 2^{-k+1}\}|\\
\lesssim&2^{(i+j)d}2^{-k(d-1)}.\end{split}

The final estimate above follows in a straightforward manner by noticing that for a fixed
\xi
with
|\xi|\leq 2^{i+1}
, the admissible points
\eta
lie in a sector of a ball of radius
2^{j+1}
and angle at most
C2^{-k}
; for the details (with a slightly modified definition of the multiplier), see
[
PS20
]
.

For
k=0
it is also trivially true because

|\text{supp}(m_{i,j,0})|\leq|\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\colon|\xi|\leq 2^{i+1};|\eta|\leq 2^{j+1}\}|\lesssim 2^{(i+j)d}.

Also define
m_{i,j}^{k}(\xi,\eta)=\hat{\mu}(\xi,\eta)\hat{\psi}(2^{-i}\xi)\hat{\psi}(2^{-j}\eta)\rho^{k}(\xi,\eta)
.

We recall that in
[
PS20
]
they showed that for any multi-indices
\alpha,\beta

(83)

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}^{\beta}\hat{\mu}(\xi,\eta)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\left(1+\min\{|\xi|,|\eta|\}|\sin(\theta)|\right)^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}(1+|(\xi,\eta)|)^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}.

Assume
i\geq j
. In this case

\min\{|\xi|,|\eta|\}|\sin(\theta)|\sim 2^{j-k}.

As in
[
PS20
]
, when
k\leq\lfloor{j/2}\rfloor
, the derivatives of the cutoff functions
\rho_{k}
are all bounded, and so are the derivatives of
\rho^{\lfloor{j/2}\rfloor}
. Hence, for any multi-indices
\alpha,\beta

(84)

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}^{\beta}m_{i,j,k}|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}2^{-(j-k)\frac{d-2}{2}}2^{-i\frac{d-2}{2}}

for all
(\xi,\eta)\in\text{supp}(m_{i,j,k})
and

(85)

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\partial_{\eta}^{\beta}m_{i,j}^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}2^{-i\frac{d-2}{2}}

for all
(\xi,\eta)\in\text{supp}(m_{i,j}^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor})
.

An application of Corollary
17
will give us the control of
\|T_{m_{i,j,k}}\|_{L^{2}\times L^{2}\rightarrow L^{1}}
for
k\leq\lfloor j/2\rfloor

(86)

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{T}_{1}^{i,j,k}\|_{L^{2}\times L^{2}\rightarrow L^{1}}\lesssim&2^{-(j-k)\frac{d-2}{2}}2^{-i\frac{d-2}{2}}|\text{supp}(m_{i,j,k})|^{1/4}\\
\lesssim&2^{-(i+j)\frac{d-2}{2}}2^{k\frac{d-2}{2}}2^{(i+j)\frac{d}{4}}2^{-k\frac{d-1}{4}}\\
\lesssim&2^{-(i+j)\frac{d-4}{4}}2^{k\frac{d-3}{4}}.\end{split}

For
\|T_{m_{i,j}^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}}\|_{L^{2}\times L^{2}\rightarrow L^{1}}
, Theorem
16
will give us

(87)

\|T_{m_{i,j}^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}}\|_{L^{2}\times L^{2}\rightarrow L^{1}}\lesssim(2^{-\frac{i(d-2)}{2}})^{\frac{3}{4}}\|(m_{i,j}^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor})\|_{1}^{1/4}.

One can check that

(88)

\begin{split}\|(m_{i,j}^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor})\|_{1}\lesssim&2^{-i(\frac{d-2}{2})}\int_{\text{supp}(m_{i,j}^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor})}(1+2^{j}|\sin(\theta(\xi,\eta))|)^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}d\xi d\eta\\
\lesssim&2^{-i(\frac{d-2}{2})}|\{|\xi|\sim 2^{i},|\eta|\sim 2^{j},|\sin(\theta)|\leq 2^{-j}\}|\\
&+2^{-i(\frac{d-2}{2})}\int_{|\xi|\sim 2^{i},\,|\eta|\sim 2^{j},2^{-j}\leq|\sin(\theta)|\leq 2^{-j/2}}(1+2^{j}|\sin(\theta(\xi,\eta))|)^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}d\xi d\eta\\
\lesssim&2^{-i(\frac{d-2}{2})}\left(2^{(i+j)d}2^{-j(d-1)}+2^{-j(\frac{d-2}{2})}2^{(i+j)d}2^{-jd/4}\right)\\
\lesssim&2^{-i(\frac{d-2}{2})}2^{-j(\frac{3d-4}{4})}2^{(i+j)d}.\end{split}

Then

\|T_{m_{i,j}^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}}\|_{L^{2}\times L^{2}\rightarrow L^{1}}\lesssim 2^{-i(\frac{d-2}{2})}2^{(i+j)\frac{d}{4}}2^{-j(\frac{3d-4}{16})}.

Hence, for
i\geq j

(89)

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{T}_{1}^{i,j}\|_{L^{2}\times L^{2}\rightarrow L^{1}}\leq&\sum_{k\geq 0}^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor-1}\|\mathcal{T}_{1}^{i,j,k}\|_{L^{2}\times L^{2}\rightarrow L^{1}}+\|\mathcal{T}_{m_{i,j}^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}}\|_{L^{2}\times L^{2}\rightarrow L^{1}}\\
\lesssim&\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor-1}2^{-(i+j)\frac{d-4}{4}}2^{k\frac{d-3}{4}}\right)+2^{-i(\frac{d-2}{2})}2^{(i+j)\frac{d}{4}}2^{-j(\frac{3d-4}{16})}\\
\lesssim&2^{-(i+j)\frac{d-4}{4}}2^{\frac{j}{2}\frac{d-3}{4}}+2^{-i(\frac{d-4}{4})}2^{j(\frac{d+4}{16})}\\
\lesssim&2^{-i\frac{d-4}{4}}2^{-j(\frac{d-5}{8})}+2^{-i(\frac{d-4}{4})}2^{j(\frac{d+4}{16})}\lesssim 2^{-i(\frac{d-4}{4})}2^{j(\frac{d+4}{16})}.\\
\end{split}

When
j\geq i
one can get instead,

\|\mathcal{T}_{1}^{i,j}\|_{L^{2}\times L^{2}\rightarrow L^{1}}\lesssim 2^{-j(\frac{d-4}{4})}2^{i(\frac{d+4}{4})}.

In any case,

(90)

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{T}_{1}^{i,j}\|_{L^{2}\times L^{2}\rightarrow L^{1}}\lesssim&2^{-\max\{i,j\}(\frac{d-4}{4})}2^{\min\{i,j\}(\frac{d+4}{16})}\\
\leq&2^{-\frac{(i+j)}{2}(\frac{d-4}{4})}2^{\frac{(i+j)}{2}\frac{d+4}{16}}=2^{-\frac{(i+j)}{2}(\frac{3d-20}{16})}\end{split}

which finishes the proof of the proposition with
\delta=\frac{3d-20}{32}
.
∎

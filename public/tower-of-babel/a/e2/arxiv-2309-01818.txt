On the rook polynomial of grid polyominoes
Rodica Dinu, FRANCESCO NAVARRA

Abstract
. We investigate the algebraic properties of the coordinate ring of grid polyominoes. This class of non-simple and thin polyominoes was introduced by Mascia, Rinaldo, and Romeo, but not much is known about their polyomino ideals, I 𝒫 I_{\mathcal{P}} , and their related coordinate ring, K ⁡ [ 𝒫 ] K[\mathcal{P}] . We give a formula for the Krull dimension of K ⁡ [ 𝒫 ] K[\mathcal{P}] in terms of the combinatorics of the polyomino and we prove that I 𝒫 I_{\mathcal{P}} is of König type if and only if the polyomino has exactly one hole. In addition, we study a Conjecture of Rinaldo and Romeo which characterizes the thin polyominoes, and we confirm it for grid polyominoes. Namely, in the main result of this article, we prove that the h h -polynomial of K ⁡ [ 𝒫 ] K[\mathcal{P}] is equal to the rook polynomial of 𝒫 \mathcal{P} , and, as a consequence, the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of K ⁡ [ 𝒫 ] K[\mathcal{P}] is equal to r ⁡ ( 𝒫 ) r(\mathcal{P}) , the maximum number of rooks that can be placed in 𝒫 \mathcal{P} in non-attacking positions. Our method to prove this result is based on the theory of simplicial complexes. We provide a suitable shelling order for Δ 𝒫 \Delta_{\mathcal{P}} , the simplicial complex attached to 𝒫 \mathcal{P} in relation to a generalized step of a facet, and we show that there is a one-to-one correspondence between the facets of Δ 𝒫 \Delta_{\mathcal{P}} with k k generalized steps and the k k -rook configurations in 𝒫 \mathcal{P} .

Introduction

Polyominoes are finite collections of unitary squares joined edge by edge. Even if they have appeared in many problems in combinatorics since ancient times, Golomb formally defined them in 1953. We refer the reader to a later monograph of Golomb,
[
12
]
, where a nice introduction to the combinatorics of polyominoes and tiling problems with polyominoes is given. Polyominoes and their combinatorics have begun to attract attention in mathematics, especially in commutative algebra, in the last years, as Qureshi associated a binomial ideal, called
polyomino ideal
in her article
[
24
]
. This ideal, denoted by
I_{\mathcal{P}}
, is generated by all inner 2-minors of a polyomino
\mathcal{P}
in the polynomial ring over a field
K
in the variables
x_{v}
where
v
is a vertex of
\mathcal{P}
. The
K
-algebra
K[\mathcal{P}]
whose relations are given by
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is called the
coordinate ring
of
\mathcal{P}
. Since 2012, many algebraic properties of the polyomino ideals and their coordinate rings were studied. For example, one of the most interesting and challenging problems is classifying the polyominoes
\mathcal{P}
such that
K[\mathcal{P}]
is a domain. It has been shown that, for example,
K[\mathcal{P}]
is a domain if
\mathcal{P}
is simply connected
[
16
,
26
]
. In the paper
[
21
]
, Mascia, Rinaldo, and Romeo have shown that if
K[\mathcal{P}]
is a domain, then the polyomino should have no zig-zag walks and they conjectured that also the converse direction is true (they even checked computationally that this holds for all polyominoes of rank less or equal than 14). Later, it was proved in
[
3
]
that this conjecture holds for a special class of non-simple polyominoes, called closed path. Other results concerning the primality of a polyomino may be consulted in
[
4
,
18
,
22
]
. Still, a complete classification is not known. Also, many other algebraic properties, like when
K[\mathcal{P}]
is Cohen-Macaulay or even Gorenstein, results are known only for some specific polyominoes. For example, in the article
[
5
]
, the authors proved that if
\mathcal{P}
is a closed path polyomino having no zig-zag walks, then
K[\mathcal{P}]
is a Cohen-Macaulay domain, and, later, they gave a characterization for all closed path polyominoes for which
K[\mathcal{P}]
is Gorenstein, see
[
6
]
.
In
[
24
]
, the author established that the Cohen-Macaulay property holds for convex polyomino and characterized all stack polyominoes for which
K[\mathcal{P}]
is Gorenstein.
A property of interest is the König type property, which was recently introduced in
[
15
]
and studied for simple thin polyominoes in
[
13
]
and for closed path polyominoes in
[
8
]
.
Nevertheless, another problem of interest and which is, again, challenging and which has received an answer only for some specific classes of polyominoes is computing the
h
-polynomial of
K[\mathcal{P}]
.
In
[
10
]
, the authors related the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of
K[\mathcal{P}]
with the maximum number of non-attacking rooks that can be placed on a polyomino, for the class of
L
-convex polyominoes and simple thin polyominoes.
We mention here that the theory of rook polynomials was introduced by Kaplansky and Riordan
[
19
]
, and was developed further by Riordan
[
28
]
. We refer to the monograph of Stanley
[
32
, Chapter 2]
for a nice exposition concerning the theory of rook polynomials and permutations with forbidden positions. The rook problem consists of enumerating the number of ways of placing
k
non-attacking rooks in a polyomino. The maximum number of such non-attacking rooks is called the
rook number
of
\mathcal{P}
, denoted by
r(\mathcal{P})
.
In
[
27
]
, the authors proved that if
\mathcal{P}
is a thin simple polyomino, then the
h
-polynomial of
K[\mathcal{P}]
is the rook polynomial of
\mathcal{P}
and that the regularity is
r(\mathcal{P})
. These results are generalized to closed path polyominoes in
[
6
]
. In
[
20
]
, the authors studied the
h
-polynomial of the convex polyominoes whose vertex set is a sublattice of
{\mathbb{N}}^{2}
. The reader may consult
[
25
]
for more details about a nice generalization of the rook polynomial to the case of non-thin polyominoes.

In this article we will be interested in a class of polyominoes called
grid polyominoes
that was introduced by Mascia, Rinaldo, and Romeo in
[
21
]
. This class consists of polyominoes that are thin (i.e. they do not contain the square tetromino as a sub-polyomino) and they are non-simple (i.e. they have holes). A precise definition of a
grid polyomino
will be given later in the article.

The present article is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we prove, in Theorem
2.1
, that the coordinate ring of a grid polyomino
\mathcal{P}
is a normal Cohen-Macaulay domain and has its Krull dimension equal to the difference between the number of the vertices of
\mathcal{P}
and the number of the cells of
\mathcal{P}
. As a consequence, Corollary
2.2
, the height of the polyomino ideal associated to a grid polyomino,
I_{\mathcal{P}}
, is exactly the number of cells of the polyomino. This result answers affirmatively to
[
8
, Conjecture 3.6]
. In addition, as we know the height of
I_{\mathcal{P}}
, we study the König type property for this class of non-simple polyominoes. This is a property introduced by Herzog, Hibi, and Moradi
[
15
]
in 2022 and relies on finding a subset
N\subset G(I)
, of a minimal system of generators of the ideal, with
|N|=0pt(I)
such that the initial terms (with respect to a suitable monomial order) of the elements from
N
form a regular sequence. In Theorem
2.4
, we show that the polyomino ideal associated to a grid polyomino
\mathcal{P}
is of König type if and only if
\mathcal{P}
has exactly one hole.
In Section
3
, our aim is to prove that, for a grid polyomino, the
h
-polynomial of
K[\mathcal{P}]
is the rook polynomial of
\mathcal{P}
. As the rook polynomial encodes the number of configurations of rooks that are arranged in non-attacking positions, one way to achieve our goal, is to try to understand its relation with the facets of the simplicial complex
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
. For this, we introduce in Definition
3.1
the notion of a
generalized step
in a face of
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
. We illustrate the notion in Example
3.2
. Next, in Lemmas
3.3
,
3.4
,
3.5
, we describe the possible structures of a generalized step in a grid polyomino. These structures are dependent on the combinatorics of the grid polyomino. Later on, we continue our study of the simplicial complex
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
and we show, in Theorem
3.7
that the set of facets of
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
ordered with respect to the (descending) lexicographical order forms a shelling order for
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
. In Definition
3.10
, we define a configuration of
k
rooks associated to a facet of
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
and, next, we will prove that there exists a one-to-one correspondence between facets of
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
with
k
generalized steps and
k
-rook configurations in
\mathcal{P}
. The fact that this correspondence is well-defined has been proved in Proposition
3.11
, the injectivity in Proposition
3.12
and the surjectivity in Proposition
3.13
. As, from Theorem
3.7
, we have that the
k
-th coefficient of the
h
-polynomial of
K[\mathcal{P}]
is the number of facets of
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
having
k
generalized steps, we obtain the main theorem of this article, Theorem
3.14
, which shows that the
h
-polynomial of
K[\mathcal{P}]
, for a grid polyomino
\mathcal{P}
, is equal to the rook polynomial of
\mathcal{P}
, which confirms a Conjecture of Rinaldo and Romeo
[
27
]
. As a consequence, the regularity of the coordinate ring
K[\mathcal{P}]
is equal to the maximum number of rooks that can be arranged in
\mathcal{P}
in non-attacking positions, and the unique Gorenstein grid polyomino is determined.

1.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall definitions and notations on polyominoes, algebraic invariants in commutative algebra, simplicial complexes, and the rook polynomial of a polyomino.

1.2.
Commutative algebra.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a polyomino,
S_{\mathcal{P}}=K[x_{v}|v\in V(\mathcal{P})]
be a polynomial ring over a field
K
. If
[a,b]
is an inner interval of
\mathcal{P}
, with

,
b
and
c
,
d
respectively diagonal and anti-diagonal corners, then the binomial

is called an

of
\mathcal{P}
.
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is called

of
\mathcal{P}
and is defined as the ideal in
S_{\mathcal{P}}
generated by all the inner 2-minors of
\mathcal{P}
. We set also
K[\mathcal{P}]=S_{\mathcal{P}}/I_{\mathcal{P}}
, which is the
coordinate ring
of
\mathcal{P}
. Along the paper, if we do not specify differently, we we denote by
<
the reverse lexicographical order on
S_{\mathcal{P}}
induced by the ordering of the variables defined by
x_{ij}>x_{kl}
if
j>l
, or,
j=l
and
i>k
. In particular, if

is a generator of
I_{\mathcal{P}}
, with
a
,
b
and
c
,
d
respectively diagonal and anti-diagonal corners, then the
initial monomial

\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits_{<}(f)
of

with respect to
<
is
x_{c}x_{d}
.

We recall some notions on the Hilbert function and the Hilbert-Poincaré series of a graded
K
-algebra
R/I
. Let
R
be a graded
K
-algebra and
I
be an homogeneous ideal of
R
, then
R/I
has a natural structure of graded

-algebra as
\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(R/I)_{k}
. The function
\mathrm{H}_{R/I}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
with
\mathrm{H}_{R/I}(k)=\dim_{K}(R/I)_{k}
is called the

of

and the formal series
\mathrm{HP}_{R/I}(t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{H}_{R/I}(k)t^{k}
is defined as the

of

.
It is known by Hilbert-Serre theorem that there exists a unique polynomial
h(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
, called

of
R/I
, such that
h(1)\neq 0
and
\mathrm{HP}_{R/I}(t)=\frac{h(t)}{(1-t)^{d}}
, where

is the Krull dimension of

is Cohen-Macaulay then
\mathrm{reg}(R/I)=\deg h(t)
.

1.3.
Simplicial complexes.

We recall some basic facts on simplicial complexes. A
finite simplicial complex

\Delta
on
[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
is a
collection of subsets of
[n]
satisfying the following two conditions:

(1)

if
F^{\prime}\in\Delta
and
F\subseteq F^{\prime}
then
F\in\Delta
;

(2)

\{i\}\in\Delta
for all
i=1,\dots,n
.

The elements of
\Delta
are called
faces
, and the dimension of a face is one less than
its cardinality. An
edge
of
\Delta
is a face of dimension 1, while a
vertex
of
\Delta
is a face
of dimension 0. The maximal faces of
\Delta
with respect to the set inclusion are called
facets
. The dimension
of
\Delta
is given by
\sup\{\dim(F):F\in\Delta\}
. A simplicial complex
\Delta
is
pure
if all facets have the same dimension. For a pure simplicial complex
\Delta
, the dimension of
\Delta
is given trivially by the dimension of a facet of
\Delta
. Given a collection
\mathscr{F}=\{F_{1},\dots,F_{m}\}
of subsets of
[n]
, we denoted by
\langle F_{1},\dots,F_{m}\rangle
or briefly
\langle\mathscr{F}\rangle
the simplicial complex consisting of all subsets of
[n]
which are contained in
F_{i}
for some
i\in[m]
. This simplicial complex is said to be generated by
F_{1},\dots,F_{m}
; in particular, if
\mathcal{F}
is the set of the facets of a simplicial complex
\Delta
, then
\Delta
is generated by
\mathcal{F}
. A pure simplicial complex
\Delta
is called
shellable
if the facets of
\Delta
can be ordered as
F_{1},\dots,F_{m}
in such a way that
\langle F_{1},\dots,F_{i-1}\rangle\cap\langle F_{i}\rangle
is generated by a non-empty set of maximal proper
faces of
F_{i}
for all
i\in\{2,\dots,m\}
. In this case,
F_{1},\dots,F_{m}
is called a
shelling
of
\Delta
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on
[n]
and
R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
K
. To every collection
F=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}\}
of
r
distinct vertices of
\Delta
, there is an associated monomial

in
R
where
x^{F}=x_{i_{1}}\dots x_{i_{r}}.
The monomial ideal generated by all monomials
x^{F}
such that
F
is not a face of
\Delta
is called
Stanley-Reisner ideal
and it denoted by
I_{\Delta}
. The
face ring
of
\Delta
, denoted by
K[\Delta]
, is defined to be the quotient ring
R/I_{\Delta}
. From
[
34
, Corollary 6.3.5]
, if
\Delta
is a simplicial complex on
[n]
of dimension

2.
Krull dimension and König type property

In this section, we provide a combinatorial formula for the Krull dimension of the coordinate ring associated to a grid polyomino and we study the König type for this class of polyominoes.

2.1.
Krull dimension of a grid polyomino

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a grid polyomino. Then
K[\mathcal{P}]
is a normal Cohen-Macaulay domain of Krull dimension
|V(\mathcal{P})|-\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{P})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a grid polyomino. By
[
21
, Theorem 4.4, Corollary 4.5]
, the ideal
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is prime, as the authors provided a suitable toric representation for it. Moreover, it is easy to see that
\mathcal{P}
satisfies the condition in
[
24
, Remark 4.2]
, so the set of generators of
I_{\mathcal{P}}
forms the reduced Gröbner basis of
I_{\mathcal{P}}
with respect to
<
. As a consequence
K[\mathcal{P}]
is a normal Cohen-Macaulay domain (see
[
14
, Corollary 4.26]
and
[
2
, Theorem 6.3.5]
).
We prove the Krull dimension formula by using the simplicial complex attached to
\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits_{<}(I_{\mathcal{P}})
. We regard the squarefree monomial ideal
\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits_{<}(I_{\mathcal{P}})
as the Stanley-Reisner ideal of a simplicial complex
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
on the vertex set of
\mathcal{P}
. By
[
34
, Theorem 9.6.1]
, we know that
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
is a pure shellable simplicial complex. Therefore, in order to find the dimension of
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
, it is sufficient to compute the cardinality of a facet of
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{ij}
be the holes of
\mathcal{P}
for
i\in[r]
and
j\in[s]
, where
\mathcal{H}_{ij}=[a_{ij},b_{ij}]
where
a_{ij}=((a_{ij})_{1},(a_{ij})_{2})
,
b_{ij}=((b_{ij})_{1},(b_{ij})_{2})
with
1<(a_{ij})_{1}<(b_{ij})_{1}<m
and
1<(a_{ij})_{2}<(b_{ij})_{2}<n
, and
with anti-diagonal corners
c_{ij}:=((a_{ij})_{1},(b_{ij})_{2})
and
d_{ij}:=((b_{ij})_{1},(a_{ij})_{2})
.
For an interval
[a,b]:=\{c\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\colon\;a\leq c\leq b\}
, let us denote the set of its interior lattice points by
\mathop{\rm int}\nolimits([a,b]):=\{c\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\colon\;a<c<b\}
. Then, for
i\in[r]
and
j\in[s]
,
\mathcal{H}_{ij}
may be decomposed as the disjoint union:

\mathcal{H}_{ij}=\mathop{\rm int}\nolimits(\mathcal{H}_{ij})\sqcup U_{ij}\sqcup B_{ij},

where
U_{ij}:=[a_{ij},c_{ij}]\cup[c_{ij},d_{ij}]
,
B_{ij}:=[a_{ij}+(1,0),d_{ij}]\cup[d_{ij},b_{ij}-(0,1)]
.
Hence,
|V(\mathcal{H}_{ij})|=|\mathop{\rm int}\nolimits(\mathcal{H}_{ij})|+|U_{ij}|+|B_{ij}|
and, thus,

|\mathop{\rm int}\nolimits(\mathcal{H}_{ij})|=|V(\mathcal{H}_{ij})|-|U_{ij}|-|B_{ij}|
.

Denoting by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}
the polyomino corresponding to
\mathcal{I}
, we have that
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}\setminus\bigcup_{i\in[r]\atop j\in[s]}\mathcal{H}_{ij}
, so

V(\mathcal{P})=V(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}})-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{s}|\mathop{\rm int}\nolimits(\mathcal{H}_{ij})|\text{, and }

\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{P})=\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}})-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{s}\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{H}_{ij}).

Therefore, we obtain

|V(\mathcal{P})|-\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{P})=(|V(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}})|-\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}))-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{s}|\mathop{\rm int}\nolimits(\mathcal{H}_{ij})|+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{s}\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{H}_{ij})=

=(|V(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}})|-\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}))-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{s}|\mathop{\rm int}\nolimits(\mathcal{H}_{ij})|+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{s}|V(\mathcal{H}_{ij})|+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{s}(|U_{ij}|+|B_{ij}|)+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{s}\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{H}_{ij}).

For any
i\in[r]
and
j\in[s]
, we consider the simplicial complexes
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}}
and
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}_{ij}}
having the initial ideals
\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits_{<}(I_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}})
and
\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits_{<}(I_{\mathcal{H}_{ij})}
as Stanley-Reisner ideals. We have that
\dim K[\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}]=\dim K[\Delta_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}}]
and
\dim K[\mathcal{H}_{ij}]=\dim K[\Delta_{\mathcal{H}_{ij}}]
.
As
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}
and each hole
\mathcal{H}_{ij}
for
i\in[r],j\in[s]
are simple polyominoes, by
[
16
, Theorem 2.1]
and
[
17
, Corollary 3.3]
, the coordinate rings
K[\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}]
and
K[\mathcal{H}_{ij}]
for
i\in[r],j\in[s]
are normal Cohen-Macaulay domains, and the Krull dimension formulae hold:

\dim K[\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}]=|V(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}})|-\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}),

\dim K[\mathcal{H}_{ij}]=|V(\mathcal{H}_{ij})|-\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{H}_{ij})\text{ for any }i\in[r],j\in[s].

Since
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}}
and
\Delta_{\mathcal{H}_{ij}}
are pure for any
i\in[r],j\in[s]
, we can write
\dim(\Delta_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}})=|F_{\mathcal{I}}|
, where
F_{I}:=[(1,1),(1,n)]\cup[(1,n),(m,n)]
, and
\dim(\Delta_{\mathcal{H}_{ij}})=|U_{ij}|
for any
i\in[r],j\in[s]
.

Therefore,

|V(\mathcal{P})|-\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{P})=(|V(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}})|-\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{I}}))-\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{s}(|V(\mathcal{H}_{ij})|-\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{H}_{ij})-|U_{ij}|)+|B_{ij}|

=|F_{\mathcal{I}}|+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{s}|B_{ij}|=|F_{\mathcal{I}}\cup\bigcup_{i,j}B_{ij}|.

It is easy to see that for all
v,w\in F_{\mathcal{I}}\cup\bigcup_{i,j}B_{ij}
we cannot find an inner interval of
\mathcal{P}
such that
v,w
are its anti-diagonal corners and for all
p\in V(\mathcal{P})\setminus\big(F_{\mathcal{I}}\cup\bigcup_{i,j}B_{ij}\big)
there are a vertex
q\in F_{\mathcal{I}}\cup\bigcup_{i,j}B_{ij}
and an inner interval of
\mathcal{P}
whose anti-diagonal corners are
p
and
q
. Therefore
F_{\mathcal{I}}\cup\bigcup_{i,j}B_{ij}
is a facet of the simplicial complex
\Delta_{\mathcal{P}}
and so we obtain the desired conclusion.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a grid polyomino. Then

0ptI_{\mathcal{P}}=\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{P}).

Proof.

We have that

0ptI_{\mathcal{P}}=\mathop{\rm embdim}\nolimits K[\mathcal{P}]-\dim K[\mathcal{P}]=

=|V(\mathcal{P})|-(|V(\mathcal{P})|-\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(P))=\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(P).

2.2.
König type property for grid polyominoes

Let
R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and
I
be a graded ideal in
R
having its height
h
. According to
[
15
]
, we say that
I
is
of König type
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

there exists a sequence
f_{1},\dots,f_{h}
of homogeneous polynomials forming part of a minimal system of homogeneous generators of
I
, and

(2)

there exists a monomial order
<
on
R
such that
\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits_{<}(f_{1}),\dots,\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits_{<}(f_{h})
is a regular sequence.

In
[
13
]
the authors starts the study of the König type property for polyomino ideals, proving that the polyomino ideals of a simple thin polyomino has this property. Moreover, in
[
8
]
it is shown that if
\mathcal{P}
is a closed path polyomino, then
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is of König type.

We are interested in understanding the König type property for grid polyominoes.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a polyomino such that
0pt(I_{\mathcal{P}})=\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{P})
. Then a necessary numerical condition in order to
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is of König type is given by
|V(\mathcal{P})|\geq 2\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{P})
.

Proof.

Let us assume that
\mathcal{P}
has
n
distinct cells and that
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is of König type with respect to a monomial order
<
. Then there exist
n
generators
f_{1},\dots,f_{n}
of
I_{\mathcal{P}}
such that
\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits_{<}(f_{1}),\dots,\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits_{<}(f_{n})
is a regular sequence. Suppose by contradiction that
|V(\mathcal{P})|<2\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(P)
. Since
|V(\mathcal{P})|<2n
and the initial monomial
\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits_{<}(f_{i})
is a product of two variables for all
i\in[n]
, there exist
j,k\in[n]
such that
\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits_{<}(f_{j})
and
\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits_{<}(f_{k})
do not have disjoint supports. Hence
\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits_{<}(f_{1}),\dots,\mathop{\rm in}\nolimits_{<}(f_{n})
cannot form a regular sequence, so we get a contradiction.
∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a grid polyomino. Then
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is of König type if and only if
\mathcal{P}
has just one hole.

Proof.

Let us assume that
\mathcal{P}
is a grid polyomino that has more than one hole. Let
I_{1}=[(1,1),(m,2)]
,
I_{2}=[(m-1,1),(m,n)]
,
I_{3}=[(1,n-1),(m,n)]
and
I_{4}=[(1,1),(2,n)]
. We decompose the grid polyomino
\mathcal{P}
as the union of the polyomino
\mathcal{R}:=\cup_{k=1}^{4}\mathcal{P}_{I_{k}}
, and a polyomino
\mathcal{Q}:=\mathcal{P}\setminus\mathcal{R}
, where
\mathcal{Q}\neq\emptyset
because
\mathcal{P}
has at least two holes. Figure
4
displays an example of such a decomposition.

Figure 4.
Example of decomposition
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{Q}
.

We assume that the sub-polyomino
\mathcal{Q}
is formed by
r\geq 1
horizontal edge intervals with
i
cells and by
s\geq 1
vertical edge intervals with
j
cells.
An easy computation shows that:

\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{Q})=rj+si-rs,\text{ and }

|V(\mathcal{Q})|=r(2j+2)+s(2i+2)-4rs.

From the decomposition of
\mathcal{P}
, we have that:

\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{P})=\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{R})+\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{Q}),\text{ and }

|V(\mathcal{P})|=|V(\mathcal{R})|+|V(\mathcal{Q})|-4(r+s).

Hence, by using the above claim, it is enough to show that
|V(\mathcal{P})|<2\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{P})
. We have that:

|V(\mathcal{P})|-2\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{P})=|V(\mathcal{R})|+|V(\mathcal{Q})|-4(r+s)-2(\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{R})+\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{Q})).

As
\mathcal{R}
is a particular case of a closed path polyomino, by
[
8
, Lemma 2.1]
,
|V(\mathcal{R})|=2\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{R})
.
It follows that:

|V(\mathcal{P})|-2\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{P})=r(2m+2)+s(2n+2)-4rs-2(rm+sn-rs)-4(r+s)=-2(r+s+rs)<0.

From Corollary
2.2
we know
0pt(I_{\mathcal{P}})=\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(\mathcal{P})
. Therefore, if
\mathcal{P}
has more than one hole then
\mathcal{P}
does not satisfy the condition in Lemma
2.3
. Hence the only possibility is that
\mathcal{P}
has just one hole, and, in this case,
I_{\mathcal{P}}
is of König type by
[
8
, Theorem 4.9]
.
∎

.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a grid polyomino. Then the

.

Corollary 3.16
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a grid polyomino. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

K[\mathcal{P}]
is Gorenstein;

(2)

\mathcal{P}
has just one hole and consists of four maximal blocks of length three.

Proof.

(2)\Rightarrow(1).
If
\mathcal{P}
has a hole and consists of four maximal blocks of length three, then
\mathcal{P}
satisfies (1) of
[
6
, Theorem 5.7]
, so
K[\mathcal{P}]
is Gorenstein.

(1)\Rightarrow(2).
By contradiction assume that
\mathcal{P}
has a hole but it does not consist of four maximal blocks of length three. Then
\mathcal{P}
is a closed path polyomino without zig-zag walks where at least two maximal blocks of
\mathcal{P}
have rank greater than three. By
[
6
, Theorem 5.7]
,
K[\mathcal{P}]
is not Gorenstein, which is a contradiction with (1).

Now, we assume that
\mathcal{P}
has more than one hole. We recall that by Theorem
2.1
,

is a Cohen-Macaulay domain. Let

be the
h
-polynomial of

, where

is the rook number of
\mathcal{P}
for Corollary
3.15
. If

[
31
]
), it follows that

for all

; so, in particular, we have that
h_{s}=1
. By Corollary
3.15
,
h(t)
is the rook polynomial of
\mathcal{P}
, so
h_{s}=1
means that we have a unique configuration to place the maximum number of rooks in a non-attacking position in
\mathcal{P}
. We show the latter does not hold true, namely
h_{s}>1
.

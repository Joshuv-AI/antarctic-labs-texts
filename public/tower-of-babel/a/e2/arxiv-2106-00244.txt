Overlap between usual and modified Bethe vectors


Abstract
We consider the overlap of Bethe vectors of the XXX spin chain with a diagonal twist and the modified Bethe vectors with a general twist. We find a determinant representation for this overlap under one additional condition on the twist parameters. Such objects arise in the calculations of nonequilibrium physics.

Introduction

Integrable quantum spin chains are a powerful tool for studying nonequilibrium physics. They have parameters that can be considered time-dependent. A simple example is an external magnetic field given by a Heaviside function that turns the field on or off. One can also consider the change in the parameters that define the boundary conditions. For example, a chain of
N
spins can be closed periodically or in the form of the Möbius strip. In the general case, the classification of integrable boundary parameters follows from the Quantum Inverse Scattering Method and related quantum algebras. In particular, the twisting of isotropic closed spin chains is given by an arbitrary invertible matrix.

To study the behavior of quantum integrable models when the boundary conditions change, we should solve the eigenproblem for any integrable boundary parameters and calculate the overlap between the states corresponding to different values of these parameters. For this, we use the Quantum Inverse Scattering Method
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. In this method, the eigenvectors of quantum Hamiltonians are constructed using the algebraic Bethe ansatz, and the roots of the Bethe equations parameterize the spectrum. A modified version of the Bethe ansatz is needed in the case of so-called ”off-diagonal boundaries”
[
4
]
. The spectrum of such models is given by modified Bethe equations, which contain an inhomogeneous term
[
5
,
6
]
. In this case, there is an additional restriction on the number of Bethe equations.

In a recent series of papers, we studied the scalar products of modified Bethe vectors
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
. We found that the well-known formulas of the standard algebraic Bethe ansatz smoothly transform into a modified form that has the same structure. This applies both to scalar products of vectors of a general form
[
12
]
and to scalar products containing the eigenvectors of a modified transfer matrix
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
. In this paper, we consider the overlap (scalar product) of two vectors corresponding to different transfer matrices. One of the vectors corresponds to a transfer matrix with a diagonal twist. It can be constructed within the framework of the standard algebraic Bethe ansatz. The second vector is a modified Bethe vector, which corresponds to a transfer matrix with a twist of the general form. To construct this vector, one needs to use the modified Bethe ansatz. We show that when one additional condition is imposed on the twist parameters, such an overlap has a compact determinant representation. This opens a possibility to study quenches in the XXX chains of an arbitrary spin.

The paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we recall basic notions of the Quantum Inverse Scattering Method. Here we also introduce a special notation used below. In section
3
, we introduce a modified transfer matrix and construct modified Bethe vectors. Section
4
is devoted to a modified Izergin determinant
[
7
]
. Here we derive a new representation which contains a set of arbitrary complex numbers. In section
5
, we consider the overlap between Bethe vectors with diagonal twist and modified Bethe vectors of general type. We show that under one additional constraint between the twist parameters, this overlap has a determinant representation. Appendix
A
contains some auxiliary formulas for the modified Izergin determinant and proofs of some propositions used in the calculation.

2
Quantum inverse scattering method

To formulate the problem within the Quantum Inverse Scattering Method framework, we consider a monodromy matrix

T(u)=\begin{pmatrix}t_{11}(u)&t_{12}(u)\\
t_{21}(u)&t_{22}(u)\end{pmatrix}.

(2.1)

The matrix elements
t_{kl}(u)
act in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and depend on a complex parameter
u
. The commutation relations between
t_{kl}(u)

are defined by an RTT-relation

R_{12}(u-v)T_{1}(u)T_{2}(v)=T_{2}(v)T_{1}(u)R_{12}(u-v).

(2.2)

Here
T_{1}(u)=T(u)\otimes\mathbf{1}
and
T_{2}(u)=\mathbf{1}\otimes T(u)
, where
\mathbf{1}
is the identity matrix in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
The
R
-matrix
R_{12}(u)
acts in
\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}
and has the form

R(u)=\frac{u}{c}(\mathbf{1}\otimes\mathbf{1})+P.

(2.3)

Here
P
the permutation operator such that
Px\otimes y=y\otimes x
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
, and
c
is a constant.
The
R
-matrix (
2.3
) has the following property:

[R_{12}(u-v),K_{1}K_{2}]=0,

(2.4)

for any
2\times 2
matrix
K
.
This property leads to the fact that a matrix
KT(u)
satisfies the RTT-relation (
2.2
).

The trace of the monodromy matrix
\mathop{\rm tr}T(u)=t_{11}(u)+t_{22}(u)
is called a transfer matrix. Due to the RTT-relation (
2.2
),

[\mathop{\rm tr}T(u),\mathop{\rm tr}T(v)]=0

(2.5)

for any complex
u
and
v
. This property of the transfer matrix allows us to consider it as a generating function of the integrals of motion of a quantum model. Due to the cyclicity of the trace, this model has periodic boundary conditions. The trace of the twisted monodromy matrix
\mathop{\rm tr}(KT(u))
can also be used to generate integrals of motion. However, they satisfy boundary conditions of a more general form.

Let us define a highest weight representation by
V(\lambda_{1}(u),\lambda_{2}(u))
, where
\lambda_{i}(u)

are some complex valued functions, and the highest weight vector
|0\rangle\in\mathcal{H}
is defined by

\displaystyle t_{ii}(u)|0\rangle=\lambda_{i}(u)|0\rangle,\qquad t_{21}(u)|0\rangle=0.

(2.6)

The action of the operator
t_{12}(u)
on
|0\rangle

is free. A state obtained by the successive action of the
t_{12}
operators on the highest weight vector is called a Bethe vector

\displaystyle|\Phi(\bar{u})\rangle=t_{12}(u_{1})\dots t_{12}(u_{n})|0\rangle.

(2.7)

Here
n=0,1,\dots
, and
\bar{u}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
. If the parameters
\bar{u}
are generic complex numbers, then
|\Phi(\bar{u})\rangle
is called the off-shell Bethe vector. Under certain constraint on the parameters
\bar{u}
(see section
3
), the vector (
2.7
) becomes an eigenvector
of the transfer matrix. Then it is called the on-shell Bethe vector.

To study scalar products we also introduce a dual vector
\langle 0|\in\mathcal{H}^{*}
defined by

\displaystyle\langle 0|t_{ii}(u)=\lambda_{i}(u)\langle 0|,\qquad\langle 0|t_{12}(u)=0,

(2.8)

and a condition
\langle 0|0\rangle=1
. The functions
\lambda_{i}(u)
are the same as in (
2.6
). Dual Bethe vectors are constructed by successive action of the
t_{21}
operators on
\langle 0|
:

\langle\Phi(\bar{u})|=\langle 0|t_{21}(u_{1})\dots t_{21}(u_{n}).

(2.9)

2.1
Notation

To simplify formulas, we introduce three rational functions

\displaystyle g(u,v)=\frac{c}{u-v},\quad f(u,v)=1+g(u,v)=\frac{u-v+c}{u-v},\quad h(u,v)=\frac{f(u,v)}{g(u,v)}=\frac{u-v+c}{c},

(2.10)

where
c
is the constant entering the
R
-matrix.
It is easy to see that the functions (
2.10
) have the following properties:

\displaystyle\chi(u,v)\Bigr|_{c\to-c}=\chi(v,u),\qquad\chi(-u,-v)=\chi(v,u),\qquad\chi(u-c,v)=\chi(u,v+c),

(2.11)

where
\chi
is any of the three functions. Besides, we have

\displaystyle g(u,v-c)=\frac{1}{h(u,v)},\quad h(u,v+c)=\frac{1}{g(u,v)},\quad f(u,v+c)=\frac{1}{f(v,u)}.

(2.12)

Below we deal with sets of complex parameters. We denote them by a bar, for example,
\bar{u}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
. Notation

\bar{u}\pm c
means that
\pm c
is added to all the arguments of the set
\bar{u}
.
The notation
\bar{u}_{k}
refers to the subset
\bar{u}_{k}=\bar{u}\setminus u_{k}
.

To make the formulas more compact, we use a shorthand notation for the products of the rational functions (
2.10
), the operators

t_{kl}(u)
(
2.1
), and their vacuum eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}(u)
(
2.6
). Namely, if the function (operator) depends on a set of variables, then this means the product over the corresponding set. For example, if
\bar{u}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
, then

t_{kl}(\bar{u})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}t_{kl}(u_{j}),\quad\lambda_{i}(\bar{u})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(u_{j}),\quad f(z,\bar{u})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}f(z,u_{j}),\quad f(\bar{u}_{k},u_{k})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq k\end{subarray}}^{n}f(u_{j},u_{k}),

(2.13)

and so on. Note that the RTT-relation (
2.2
) implies
[t_{kl}(u),t_{kl}(v)]=0
. Thus, the first product in (
2.13
) is well defined. Later we will extend this convention to the products of matrix elements of the twisted monodromy matrix.

Notation
f(\bar{u},\bar{v})
means the double product over the sets
\bar{u}
and
\bar{v}
.
By definition, any product over the empty set is equal to
1
. A double product is equal to
1
if at least one of the sets is empty.

Finally, for any set of complex parameters
\bar{u}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
such that
n\geq 2
, we also introduce special products of the
g
-functions

\Delta(\bar{u})=\prod_{1\leq k<j\leq n}g(u_{j},u_{k}),\qquad\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{u})=\prod_{1\leq k<j\leq n}g(u_{k},u_{j}).

(2.14)

It is easy to see that
\Delta(\bar{u})=(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{u})
. For
n=0,1
, we set
\Delta(\bar{u})=\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{u})=1
by definition.

3
Twisted monodromy matrix

We consider two twisted monodromy matrices
K^{(\ell)}T(u)
,
\ell=1,2
.
The first one corresponds to the twist matrix
K^{(1)}=\mathop{\rm diag}(1,\alpha)
, where
\alpha
is a complex parameter. Then the twisted transfer matrix has the form

t^{(1)}(u)=t_{11}(u)+\alpha t_{22}(u).

(3.1)

We call it a diagonal transfer matrix. It generates a model with quasi-periodic boundary conditions. The eigenvectors (resp. dual eigenvectors) of the diagonal transfer matrix have the form (
2.7
) (resp. (
2.9
))

\displaystyle|\Phi^{(1)}(\bar{u})\rangle=t_{12}(\bar{u})|0\rangle,\qquad\langle\Phi^{(1)}(\bar{u})|=\langle 0|t_{21}(\bar{u}).

(3.2)

We have added an extra superscript to the vectors to emphasize that they refer to the transfer matrix
t^{(1)}(u)
.

If the parameters
\bar{u}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
are arbitrary complex numbers, then the vectors (
3.2
) are off-shell. If these parameters satisfy twisted Bethe equations

\lambda_{1}(u_{j})f(\bar{u}_{j},u_{j})=\alpha\lambda_{2}(u_{j})f(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j}),\qquad j=1,\dots,n,

(3.3)

then

\displaystyle t^{(1)}(v)|\Phi^{(1)}(\bar{u})\rangle=\Lambda^{(1)}(v|\bar{u})|\Phi^{(1)}(\bar{u})\rangle,\qquad\langle\Phi^{(1)}(\bar{u})|t^{(1)}(v)=\Lambda^{(1)}(v|\bar{u})\langle\Phi^{(1)}(\bar{u})|,

(3.4)

where

\Lambda^{(1)}(v|\bar{u})=\lambda_{1}(v)f(\bar{u},v)+\alpha\lambda_{2}(v)f(v,\bar{u}).

(3.5)

The vectors
|\Phi^{(1)}(\bar{u})\rangle
and
\langle\Phi^{(1)}(\bar{u})|
are called on-shell provided the conditions (
3.3
) are fulfilled.
In the case of the XXX spin-
1/2
chain, the number of parameters
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
does not exceed the length of the chain.

The second deformation corresponds to the twist matrix of the general form

\displaystyle K^{(2)}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\kappa}&\;\kappa^{+}\\
\;\kappa^{-}&\kappa\end{pmatrix},

(3.6)

where the entries of
K^{(2)}
are arbitrary complex numbers.
This twist produces models with non-diagonal boundary conditions. The standard procedure of the algebraic Bethe ansatz should be modified in this case
[
14
,
8
]
. We present the twist matrix in the form
K^{(2)}=BDA
where

A=\sqrt{\mu}\begin{pmatrix}1&\frac{\rho_{2}}{\kappa^{-}}\\
\frac{\rho_{1}}{\kappa^{+}}&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad B=\sqrt{\mu}\begin{pmatrix}1&\frac{\rho_{1}}{\kappa^{-}}\\
\frac{\rho_{2}}{\kappa^{+}}&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad D=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\kappa}-\rho_{1}&0\\
0&\kappa-\rho_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.7)

Here

\mu=\frac{1}{1-\frac{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}}{\kappa^{+}\kappa^{-}}},

(3.8)

and the parameters
\rho_{\ell}
satisfy a condition

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\rho_{2}-(\kappa\rho_{1}+\tilde{\kappa}\rho_{2})+\kappa^{+}\kappa^{-}=0.

(3.9)

Then a twisted transfer matrix is defined as follows:

t^{(2)}(u)=\mathop{\rm tr}(K^{(2)}T(u))=\mathop{\rm tr}(D\overline{T}(u)).

(3.10)

Here
\overline{T}(u)
is a modified monodromy matrix

\displaystyle\overline{T}(u)=AT(u)B=\begin{pmatrix}\nu_{11}(u)&\nu_{12}(u)\\
\nu_{21}(u)&\nu_{22}(u)\end{pmatrix}.

(3.11)

The entries of
\overline{T}(u)
have the following expressions in terms of the initial monodromy matrix elements:

\displaystyle\nu_{11}(z)=\mu\Big(t_{11}(z)+\frac{\rho_{2}}{\kappa^{+}}t_{12}(z)+\frac{\rho_{2}}{\kappa^{-}}t_{21}(z)+\frac{\rho_{2}^{2}}{\kappa^{-}\kappa^{+}}t_{22}(z)\Big),

(3.12)

\displaystyle\nu_{22}(z)=\mu\Big(t_{22}(z)+\frac{\rho_{1}}{\kappa^{+}}t_{12}(z)+\frac{\rho_{1}}{\kappa^{-}}t_{21}(z)+\frac{\rho_{1}^{2}}{\kappa^{-}\kappa^{+}}t_{11}(z)\Big),

(3.13)

\displaystyle\nu_{12}(z)=\mu\Big(t_{12}(z)+\frac{\rho_{1}}{\kappa^{-}}t_{11}(z)+\frac{\rho_{2}}{\kappa^{-}}t_{22}(z)+\frac{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}}{(\kappa^{-})^{2}}t_{21}(z)\Big),

(3.14)

\displaystyle\nu_{21}(z)=\mu\Big(t_{21}(z)+\frac{\rho_{1}}{\kappa^{+}}t_{11}(z)+\frac{\rho_{2}}{\kappa^{+}}t_{22}(z)+\frac{\rho_{1}\rho_{2}}{(\kappa^{+})^{2}}t_{12}(z)\Big).

(3.15)

The modified Bethe vectors (resp. the dual modified Bethe vectors) related to the twisted transfer matrix (
3.10
) can be constructed from the new entries
\nu_{12}(z)
(resp.
\nu_{21}(z)
). They are given by

\displaystyle|\Phi^{(2)}(\bar{u})\rangle=\nu_{12}(\bar{u})|0\rangle,\qquad\langle\Phi^{(2)}(\bar{u})|=\langle 0|\nu_{21}(\bar{u}).

(3.16)

Here we have used the convention on the shorthand notation for the products of the operators
\nu_{ij}(u)
.

If
\bar{u}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\}
is a set of arbitrary complex numbers, then vectors (
3.16
) are off-shell. However, if the set
\bar{u}

satisfies inhomogeneous Bethe equations

\displaystyle(\tilde{\kappa}-\rho_{1})\lambda_{1}(u_{j})f(\bar{u}_{j},u_{j})=(\kappa-\rho_{2})\lambda_{2}(u_{j})f(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})+(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})\lambda_{1}(u_{j})\lambda_{2}(u_{j})g(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j}),

(3.17)

for
j=1,\dots,N
, then they are eigenvectors of the twisted transfer matrix (
3.10
)

\displaystyle t^{(2)}(v)|\Phi^{(2)}(\bar{u})\rangle=\Lambda^{(2)}(v|\bar{u})|\Phi^{(2)}(\bar{u})\rangle,\qquad\langle\Phi^{(2)}(\bar{u})|t^{(2)}(v)=\Lambda^{(2)}(v|\bar{u})\langle\Phi^{(2)}(\bar{u})|,

(3.18)

with an eigenvalue

\displaystyle\Lambda^{(2)}(v|\bar{u})=(\tilde{\kappa}-\rho_{1})\lambda_{1}(v)f(\bar{u},v)+(\kappa-\rho_{2})\lambda_{2}(v)f(v,\bar{u})+(\rho_{1}+\rho_{2})\lambda_{1}(v)\lambda_{2}(v)g(v,\bar{u}).

(3.19)

In this case, we call the vectors
|\Phi^{(2)}(\bar{u})\rangle
and
\langle\Phi^{(2)}(\bar{u})|
modified on-shell vectors.

The number of parameters
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\}
depends on the chain length and spin. In particular, in the XXX spin-
1/2
chain,
N
coincides with the number of sites. In the case of higher spins,
N
exceeds the chain length (see
[
8
]
for more details).

The main goal of this paper is to calculate an overlap

\displaystyle S^{n,N}(\bar{v},\bar{u})=\langle\Phi^{(1)}(\bar{v})|\Phi^{(2)}(\bar{u})\rangle.

(3.20)

Here
\langle\Phi^{(1)}(\bar{v})|
and
|\Phi^{(2)}(\bar{u})\rangle
are the eigenvectors of the corresponding transfer matrices,
n=\#\bar{v}
, and
N=\#\bar{u}
.
Then according to Fermi’s Golden rule for the transition between the two twists, we obtain

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1\to 2}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\Big|\langle\Phi^{(1)}(\bar{v})|(H^{(1)}-H^{(2)})|\Phi^{(2)}(\bar{u})\rangle\Big|^{2}{\rho^{(2)}}(\bar{u}).

(3.21)

Here
H^{(1)}
and
H^{(2)}
respectively are the Hamiltonians corresponding to the transfer matrices
t^{(1)}
and
t^{(2)}
, and
\rho^{(2)}(\bar{u})
is a density of the resulting states.

In the case of the XXX spin-
1/2
chains, we can express the Hamiltonians in terms of the twisted transfer matrices using

H^{(1)}-H^{(2)}=2c\frac{d}{dz}\big(\log t^{(1)}(z)-\log t^{(2)}(z)\big)\Bigr|_{z=0}.

(3.22)

Thus, we find

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1\to 2}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\left|2c\frac{d}{dz}\log\left(\frac{\Lambda^{(1)}(z|\bar{v})}{\Lambda^{(2)}(z|\bar{u})}\right)\right|^{2}_{z=0}\big|S^{n,N}(\bar{v},\bar{u})\big|^{2}{\rho}^{(2)}(\bar{u}).

(3.23)

4
Modified Izergin determinant

In various formulas for the scalar products, a modified Izergin determinant (MID) arises.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\bar{u}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
,
\bar{v}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
, and
z
be arbitrary complex numbers.
Then the
MID

K_{n,m}^{(z)}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})

is defined by

K_{n,m}^{(z)}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})=\det_{m}\left(-z\delta_{jk}+\frac{f(\bar{u},v_{j})f(v_{j},\bar{v}_{j})}{h(v_{j},v_{k})}\right).

(4.1)

Alternatively the
MID
can be presented as

K_{n,m}^{(z)}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})=(1-z)^{m-n}\det_{n}\left(\delta_{jk}f(u_{j},\bar{v})-z\frac{f(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})}{h(u_{j},u_{k})}\right).

(4.2)

The proof of the equivalence of representations (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) can be found in proposition 4.1 of
[
15
]
.

For
m=n
and
z=1
, the MID turns into the usual Izergin determinant
[
16
]
. The latter is equal to the partition function of the six-vertex model with domain wall boundary condition
[
12
,
16
]
.

We will also use a conjugated MID

\overline{K}_{n,m}^{(z)}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})=K_{n,m}^{(z)}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})\Bigr|_{c\to-c}=\det_{m}\left(-z\delta_{jk}+\frac{f(v_{j},\bar{u})f(\bar{v}_{j},v_{j})}{h(v_{k},v_{j})}\right).

(4.3)

Equivalently, it can be defined as follows:

\overline{K}_{n,m}^{(z)}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})=(1-z)^{m-n}\det_{n}\left(\delta_{jk}f(\bar{v},u_{j})-z\frac{f(\bar{u}_{j},u_{j})}{h(u_{k},u_{j})}\right).

(4.4)

There exist also representations for MID, which contain additional parameters.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\bar{u}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
,
\bar{\eta}=\{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n}\}
,
\bar{v}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
, and
z
be arbitrary complex numbers. Then

K_{n,m}^{(z)}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})=(1-z)^{m-n}\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{u})\Delta(\bar{\eta})\det_{n}\left(\frac{f(u_{j},\bar{v})}{g(u_{j},\bar{\eta}_{k})}-zh(u_{j},\bar{\eta}_{k})\right).

(4.5)

Proof.
Let
\bar{u}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
be pair-wise distinct complex numbers. Consider an
n\times n
matrix
W
with the elements

W_{jk}=\frac{g(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})}{g(u_{j},\bar{\eta}_{k})}.

(4.6)

Here
\bar{\eta}=\{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n}\}
are arbitrary pair-wise distinct complex numbers. The entries
W_{jk}
are proportional to the Cauchy matrix
g(u_{j},\eta_{k})
. Thus,

\det_{n}W=\frac{\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{u})\Delta(\bar{u})}{g(\bar{u},\bar{\eta})}\det_{n}g(u_{j},\eta_{k})=\frac{\Delta(\bar{u})}{\Delta(\bar{\eta})}.

(4.7)

Thus, the determinant of
W
exists and is non-vanishing.
Let us transform representation (
4.2
) as follows:

K_{n,m}^{(z)}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})=\frac{(1-z)^{m-n}}{\det_{n}W}\det_{n}\left(f(u_{j},\bar{v})W_{jk}-z\sum_{l=1}^{n}\frac{f(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})}{h(u_{j},u_{l})}W_{lk}\right).

(4.8)

The sum over
l
is easily computable. Indeed, let

G_{jk}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\frac{W_{lk}}{h(u_{j},u_{l})}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{n}\frac{g(u_{\ell},\eta_{k})}{h(u_{j},u_{\ell})}\frac{g(u_{\ell},\bar{u}_{\ell})}{g(u_{\ell},\bar{\eta})}.

(4.9)

Then we have

\frac{1}{2\pi ic}\oint_{|z|=R\to\infty}\frac{g(z,\eta_{k})}{h(u_{j},z)}\frac{g(z,\bar{u})}{g(z,\bar{\eta})}\,dz=0=G_{jk}-\frac{h(u_{j},\bar{\eta}_{k})}{h(u_{j},\bar{u})}.

(4.10)

Substituting this into (
4.8
) we obtain

K_{n,m}^{(z)}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})=\frac{(1-z)^{m-n}\Delta(\bar{\eta})}{\Delta(\bar{u})}\det_{n}\left(f(u_{j},\bar{v})\frac{g(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})}{g(u_{j},\bar{\eta}_{k})}-zg(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})h(u_{j},\bar{\eta}_{k})\right).

(4.11)

Extracting the products
g(u_{j},\bar{u}_{j})
we arrive at (
4.5
). It remains to note that the limits
u_{j}=u_{k}
and
\eta_{j}=\eta_{k}
(
j,k=1,\dots,n
) are well defined. Indeed, in this case, the prefactor
\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{u})\Delta(\bar{\eta})
has a pole, while the determinant vanishes. Therefore, representation (
4.5
) is valid for any complex
\bar{u}
and
\bar{\eta}
. ∎

Similarly, one can prove the following representation for the MID:

K_{n,m}^{(z)}(\bar{u}|\bar{v})=\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{u})\Delta(\bar{\eta})\det_{m}\left(f(\bar{u},v_{j})h(u_{j},\bar{\eta}_{k})-\frac{z}{g(u_{j},\bar{\eta}_{k})}\right).

(4.12)

Here
\bar{\eta}=\{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{m}\}
is a set of arbitrary complex numbers.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
\bar{u}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
,
\bar{\eta}=\{\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n}\}
, and
z
be arbitrary complex numbers. Then

\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{u})\Delta(\bar{\eta})\det_{n}\left(\frac{1}{g(u_{j},\bar{\eta}_{k})}-zh(u_{j},\bar{\eta}_{k})\right)=(1-z)^{n}.

(4.13)

Proof.
Setting
\bar{v}=\emptyset
in (
4.5
) we arrive at

\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{u})\Delta(\bar{\eta})\det_{n}\left(\frac{1}{g(u_{j},\bar{\eta}_{k})}-zh(u_{j},\bar{\eta}_{k})\right)=(1-z)^{n}K_{n,0}^{(z)}(\bar{u}|\emptyset).

(4.14)

Then (
4.13
) follows from (
4.1
).

Some other properties of the MID are given in appendix
A
(see also
[
7
]
for a more detailed description).

5
Overlap

Let us now proceed to the calculation of the overlap (
3.20
)

5.2

Up to now both vectors were off-shell. Let

5.4
Particular case

We did not impose any restrictions on the parameters
\bar{u}
in the representation (
5.21
). Therefore, the vector
|\Phi^{(2)}(\bar{u})\rangle
, generally speaking, is off-shell. Consider a particular case
\alpha=1
. Then the dual vector
\langle\Phi^{(1)}(\bar{v})|
is an eigenvector of the ordinary transfer matrix
t_{11}(z)+t_{22}(z)
. On the other hand, we find
\rho_{1}=-\rho_{2}
from the condition
\alpha=-\rho_{2}/\rho_{1}
.
Then the inhomogeneous term vanishes in the modified Bethe equations (
3.17
). We obtain

\hat{\lambda}_{1}(u_{j})=(-1)^{N}\frac{\kappa+\rho}{\tilde{\kappa}-\rho}\;\frac{h(u_{j},\bar{u})}{h(\bar{u},u_{j})}\,\hat{\lambda}_{2}(u_{j}),\qquad j=1,\dots,N,

(5.22)

where
\rho=\rho_{1}=-\rho_{2}
. At the same time, the Bethe vectors
|\Phi^{(2)}(\bar{u})\rangle
remain modified in this particular case. They are still given by (
3.16
).

We now require the vector
|\Phi^{(2)}(\bar{u})\rangle
to be an on-shell modified Bethe vector. For this, it is sufficient to substitute conditions (
5.22
) into representation (
5.21
). After simple algebra, we obtain

S^{n,N}(\bar{v},\bar{u})=(-\mu)^{N}\left(\frac{\rho}{\kappa^{-}}\right)^{N-n}\lambda_{2}(\bar{v})\lambda_{2}(\bar{u})h(\bar{u},\bar{v})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{v})\Delta^{\prime}(\bar{\eta})g(\bar{v},\bar{\eta})\Delta(\bar{u})\det_{N}\Omega_{jk}.

(5.23)

Here the matrix
\Omega
consists of two parts:

\Omega_{jk}=\frac{g(u_{j},v_{k})}{g(u_{j},\bar{\eta})}-\frac{h(u_{j},\bar{\eta})}{h(u_{j},v_{k})}-V_{j}\left(\frac{h(\bar{\eta},u_{j})}{h(v_{k},u_{j})}-\frac{g(v_{k},u_{j})}{g(\bar{\eta},u_{j})}\right),\qquad k=1,\dots,n,

(5.24)

and

\Omega_{jk}=(-1)^{N+n+1}\frac{V_{j}}{g(u_{j},\bar{\eta}_{k})}-h(u_{j},\bar{\eta}_{k}),\qquad k=n+1,\dots,N,

(5.25)

where

V_{j}=\frac{\kappa+\rho}{\tilde{\kappa}-\rho}\;\frac{h(u_{j},\bar{u})h(\bar{v},u_{j})}{h(\bar{u},u_{j})h(u_{j},\bar{v})}.

(5.26)

It is easy to see that in a particular case
n=N
, we reproduce a determinant representation for the scalar product of the usual on-shell and the twisted on-shell Bethe vectors

[
13
]
.

Conclusion

We have considered overlaps of Bethe vectors with a diagonal twist and modified Bethe vectors. We have shown that under one additional condition on the twist parameters, such an overlap has a determinant representation. At the same time, the modified Bethe vector can remain an off-shell vector. In this sense, the result obtained is analogous to the scalar product of on-shell and off-shell Bethe vectors
[
13
]
.

However, despite this similarity, the resulting determinant representation has an entirely new structure. Namely, it contains a set of arbitrary parameters. This representation was obtained thanks to new formulas for the MID. Depending on the specific task, these parameters can be chosen in the most convenient way. Such formulas can find applications in models with higher symmetry rank
[
17
]
.

The resulting determinant representation for the overlap remains valid if the modified Bethe vector is the eigenvector of the modified transfer matrix. In this case, the parameters of the modified vector satisfy the inhomogeneous Bethe equations (
3.17
). We have considered only one specific case, when the inhomogeneous term in the Bethe equations disappears. The more general case requires further study.

One of the apparent directions for further research is the application of the results obtained to nonequilibrium physics. For this, it is necessary to consider the overlaps in the thermodynamic limit and calculate the density of states. Another area to consider is overlaps under diagonal and off-diagonal boundary conditions in the models on a segment. It is natural to expect that, in this case, the results are expressed in terms of the modified Tsuchiya determinant
[
18
]
. We hope that these formulas are easy to obtain using the method of reducing overlaps to a system of linear equations
[
19
]
.

A.3
Summation formula

Let a function

Proposition A.3

Strong rates of convergence of space-time discretization schemes for the 2D Navier-Stokes equations with additive noise Thanks: Hakima Bessaih was partially supported by the Simons Foundation grant 582264.
Hakima Bessaih, Annie Millet

Abstract
. We consider the strong solution of the 2D Navier-Stokes equations in a torus subject to an additive noise. We implement a fully implicit time numerical scheme and a finite element method in space. We prove that the rate of convergence of the schemes is η ∈ [ 0 , 1 / 2 ) \eta\in[0,1/2) in time and 1 in space. Let us mention that the coefficient η \eta is equal to the time regularity of the solution with values in 𝕃 2 {\mathbb{L}}^{2} . Our method relies on the existence of finite exponential moments for both the solution and its time approximation. Our main idea is to use a discrete Gronwall lemma for the error estimate without any localization.

1.
Introduction

Numerical schemes and algorithms have been introduced to best approximate and construct solutions for PDEs.
Many algorithms based on either finite difference, finite element or spectral Galerkin methods
(for the space discretization), and on either Euler schemes, Crank-Nicolson or Runge-Kutta schemes (for the time discretization)
have been introduced for both the linear and nonlinear cases. Their rates of convergence have been widely investigated.
The literature on numerical analysis for SPDEs is now very extensive.
In
[
1
]
, the models are either linear, have global Lipschitz properties,
or more generally some monotonicity property. In this case the convergence is proven to be in mean square.
When nonlinearities are involved that are not of Lipschitz or monotone type, then a rate of convergence
in mean square is more difficult to obtain.
Indeed, because of the stochastic perturbation, one may not use the Gronwall lemma after taking the expectation of the
error bound
since it involves a nonlinear term which is often quadratic.

In this paper we study the so-called incompressible Navier-Stokes equations, which describes the dynamics (velocity and pressure) of an
incompressible fluid flow.
These equations are parametrized by the viscosity coefficient
\nu>0
.
Their quantitative and qualitative properties depend on the dimensional setting.
For example, while the well posedness of global weak solutions of the 2D Navier-Stokes is well known and established,
the uniqueness of global weak solutions for the 3D case is completely open.
We will focus on the 2D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in a bounded domain
D=[0,L]^{2}
,
subject to an external additive noise defined as:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\nu\Delta u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla\pi

\displaystyle=dW\quad\mbox{\rm in }\quad(0,T)\times D,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm div }u

\displaystyle=0\quad\mbox{\rm in }\quad(0,T)\times D,

(1.2)

where
T>0
. The process
u:\Omega\times(0,T)\times D\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is the velocity field
with initial condition
u_{0}
in
D
and periodic boundary conditions
u(t,x+Lv_{i})=u(t,x)
on
(0,T)\times\partial D
,
where
v_{i}
,
i=1,2

denotes the canonical basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, and
\pi:\Omega\times(0,T)\times D\to{\mathbb{R}}
is the pressure.
The external force is described by an additive stochastic perturbation and will be defined in detail later.
Let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},({\mathcal{F}}_{t}),{\mathbb{P}})
denote a filtered probability space and
W=(W(t),t\geq 0)
be a Wiener process to be precisely defined later on.

Various space-time numerical schemes have been studied for the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations with a multiplicative noise, that is where in the
right hand side of (
1.1
) we replace
dW
by
G(u)\,dW
, where
G
is a Lipschitz function with at most linear growth.
We refer to
[
3
,
10
,
14
,
8
,
11
,
9
]
, where convergence in probability is obtained with various rates. As stated previously, the main tool to get the convergence in probability is the localization of the nonlinear term over a space of large probability.
We studied the strong (that is
L^{2}(\Omega)
) rate of convergence of the time implicit Euler scheme (resp. space-time implicit Euler scheme coupled with
finite element space discretization) in our previous papers
[
6
]
(resp.
[
7
]
) for an
H^{1}
-valued initial condition.
The method is based on the fact that the solution (and the scheme) have finite moments (bounded uniformly on the mesh).
For a general multiplicative noise, the rate is logarithmic. When the diffusion coefficient is bounded (which is a slight extension of an additive noise),
the solution has exponential moments; we used this property in
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
to get an explicit polynomial strong rate
of convergence. However, this rate depends on the viscosity and the strength of the noise, and is strictly less than 1/2 for the time parameter (resp. than
1
for the
spatial one). For a given viscosity, the rates of convergence increase to 1/2 (resp.
1
) when the noise’s intensity on the time interval
[0,T]

approaches 0. Furthermore, the
speed of convergence for the time discretization requires either some link between the space and time discretization parameter for general finite elements
which satisfy the discrete LBB-condition,
or divergence free finite elements such as Scott-Vogelius mixed elements.
Note that due to the stochastic forcing term, the time speed of convergence cannot exceed 1/2, which is the optimal exponent.

In this paper, our aim is to obtain the best rates of convergence for the time and full (that is space-time) Euler schemes for a purely additive noise and an

H^{1}
-valued initial condition.
These rates were obtained for the convergence in probability of the full scheme with a multiplicative noise and an
H^{2}
-valued initial
condition in
[
9
]
. The proofs rely on some
estimates already proven in
[
7
]
, and on the existence of exponential moments for
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\big(|A^{1/2}u(t)|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}|Au(s)|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}ds\big)
, and for a similar quantity for the time discretization (seeTheorem
3.2
).
The fact that we have a purely additive perturbation
implies that the noise disappears from the difference of the true solution and its approximation on the
time grid. This enables us to use the Gronwall lemma for almost every
\omega
; the corresponding upper bound is
Ze^{Tg}
, where
g=C\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|A^{1/2}u(t)|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}
and

has finite moments of all orders. Under some conditions on the strength of the noise in terms of the viscosity and the
terminal time
T
,
we prove that the implicit time Euler scheme converges in
{\mathbb{L}}^{2}
with rate almost 1/2, and that the full space-time scheme converges with rate

h^{\eta}+k

\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, where

is the time mesh and
k
is the scaling factor of the finite elements. Unlike
[
7
]

we do not need some constraint between
h
and

when the finite elements are general and satisfy the discrete LBB condition. Divergence free finite elements
only enable to have less constraints on the noise, but does not affect the speed of convergence. The proof of the rate of convergence of the implicit time scheme is
very simple (see Section
3
). The proof of the rate of convergence for the full scheme is based on the same strategy, using the difference between
the time and
full schemes; it requires some more technical upper estimates on
various error terms before using the Gronwall lemma. Some of these estimates were already proven in
[
7
]
; we include
the arguments for the sake of completeness. Note that the decomposition we use for the bilinear term is similar to that in
[
6
]
but
different from that in
[
11
]
and provides better estimates. We at first prove the strong convergence for a deterministic initial condition
u_{0}\in V

under some constraints on the “strength of the noise” described by the trace of the corresponding covariance operator

We also study the case of a random initial condition
u_{0}
such that

has exponential moments of order

have to impose a lower bound on
\gamma_{0}

{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\exp\Big(\alpha\max_{0\leq l\leq N}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf u}^{l}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big)\Big]=C_{1}(\alpha)<\infty.

(3.7)

and for
\beta<\frac{\tilde{\alpha}_{0}}{2}
, there exists a positive constant
C_{2}(\beta)
such that for
N
large enough,

{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\exp\Big(\beta\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\Big[|A^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf u}^{n}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+\beta\nu\frac{T}{N}\sum_{l=1}^{n}|A{\bf u}^{l}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big]\Big)\Big]=C_{2}(\beta)<\infty.

(3.8)

(ii) Let
u_{0}
be random,
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
-measurable and satisfy (
2.7
).

Set
\tilde{\beta}_{0}:=\tilde{\alpha}_{0}\;\frac{\gamma_{0}}{\gamma_{0}+\tilde{\alpha_{0}}}
. Then for
0<\alpha<\tilde{\beta}_{0}
there exists a positive constant

C_{1}(\alpha)
such that (
3.7
) holds for
N
large enough.

Set
\tilde{\beta}_{1}:=\tilde{\alpha}_{0}\,\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2\gamma_{0}+\tilde{\alpha}_{0}}

C_{2}(\beta)

) holds for

3.2.
Strong convergence for the time Euler scheme

For
k=0,\cdots,N
, let
e_{k}:=u(t_{k})-{\bf u}^{k}
denote the error of this scheme (note that
e_{0}=0
).
Then for any
\phi\in V
and
j=1,\cdots,N
, we have

\displaystyle(e_{j}-e_{j-1},\phi)

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\Big[\nu\big(A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(s)-A^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf u}^{j}\,,\,A^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi\big)+\big\langle B(u(s),u(s))-B({\bf u}^{j},{\bf u}^{j}))\,,\,\phi\big\rangle\Big]ds=0.

(3.9)

The following strong convergence is the main result of this section. Unlike Theorem
[
6
, Th 4.6]
,
the maximal speed of convergence is given by the coefficient
\eta
which is the time regularity of the solution
u
.
However, for the convergence to hold there is a constraint on
{\rm Tr}(Q)
which reflects the strength of the noise.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
u_{0}\in V
be deterministic; assume condition (
2.6
) is satisfied and

{\rm Tr}\,(Q)<\frac{2\nu^{2}}{{\bar{C}}^{2}\,T}
.
Then, given any
\eta\in(0,1)
there exists a positive constant
C
such that

{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\max_{1\leq j\leq N}|e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+{\nu}\frac{T}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big)\leq C\Big(\frac{T}{N}\Big)^{\eta},

(3.10)

for
N
large enough.

Proof.

The proof contains several steps: some upper estimates of the bilinear term, a pointwise discrete Gronwall lemma
and moment estimates. We rewrite (
3.9
) with
\phi=e_{j}
.

Step 1: Upper estimates for the bilinear term

Let us consider the duality between the difference of the bilinear terms and
e_{j}
, that is the upper estimate of
\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\langle B(u(s),u(s))-B({\bf u}^{j},{\bf u}^{j})\,,\,e_{j}\big\rangle ds
. For every
s\in(t_{j-1},t_{j}]
, using the bilinearity of
B

and the antisymmetry property (
2.1
),
we deduce

\big\langle B\big(u(s),u(s)\big)-B\big({\bf u}^{j},{\bf u}^{j}\big)\,,\,e_{j}\big\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}T_{i}(s),

(3.11)

where, since
\big\langle B\big(v,{\bf u}^{j}\big)\,,\,e_{j}\big\rangle=\big\langle B\big(v,u(t_{j})\big)\,,\,e_{j}\big\rangle
for every
v\in V
,

\displaystyle T_{1}(s):=

\displaystyle\big\langle B\big(e_{j},{\bf u}^{j}\big)\,,\,e_{j}\big\rangle=\big\langle B\big(e_{j},u(t_{j})\big)\,,\,e_{j}\big\rangle,

\displaystyle T_{2}(s):=

\displaystyle\big\langle B\big(u(s)-u(t_{j}),u(t_{j})\big)\,,\,e_{j}\big\rangle,

\displaystyle T_{3}(s):=

\displaystyle\big\langle B\big(u(s),u(s)-u(t_{j})\big)\,,\,e_{j}\big\rangle=-\big\langle B\big(u(s),e_{j}\big)\,,\,u(s)-u(t_{j})\big\rangle.

Note that, unlike the first formulation of
T_{1}(s)
, the second one only depends on the error and on the solution to (
1.1
),
and not on the approximation scheme. The Hölder inequality and (
2.2
) yield
for every
\delta_{1}>0
and
\bar{C}
defined in the interpolation
inequality (
2.2
)

\displaystyle\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}|T_{1}(s)|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\bar{C}\frac{T}{N}\,|e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,|A^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}\,|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{j})|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{1}\nu\frac{T}{N}\,|A^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\bar{C}^{2}}{4\delta_{1}\nu}\,\frac{T}{N}\,|e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\,|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{j})|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2},

(3.12)

where the last upper estimate follows from the Young inequality (with conjugate exponents 2 and 2).
A similar argument using the Hölder and Young inequalities with exponents 4, 4 and 2 implies that for any
\delta_{2}>0
and
\gamma_{2}>0
,

\displaystyle|T_{2}(s)|\leq\delta_{2}\nu|A^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+\gamma_{2}|e_{j}|^{2}_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+C(\nu,\delta_{2},\gamma_{2})\|u(t_{j})-u(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{4}}^{2}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{j})|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\!\!|T_{2}(s)|\,ds\leq\;

\displaystyle\delta_{2}\nu\frac{T}{N}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+\gamma_{2}\frac{T}{N}|e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\;+C(\nu,\delta_{2},\gamma_{2})|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{j})|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\!\!\|u(t_{j})-u(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{4}}^{2}ds.

(3.13)

Similar computations using the Hölder and Young inequalities imply

\displaystyle\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}|T_{3}(s)|\,ds\leq

\displaystyle\;\delta_{3}\nu\frac{T}{N}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4\nu\delta_{3}}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\|u(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{4}}^{2}\,\|u(t_{j})-u(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{4}}^{2}ds

(3.14)

for any
\delta_{3}>0
.

Furthermore, note that

\nu\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\!\!\big(A^{\frac{1}{2}}(u(s)-{\bf u}^{j})\,,\,A^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{j}\big)ds=\nu\frac{T}{N}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+\nu\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\!\!\big(A^{\frac{1}{2}}(u(s)-u(t_{j})),A^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{j}\big)ds.

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz and Young inequalities, we deduce

\nu\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\!\!\big|\big(A^{\frac{1}{2}}(u(s)-u(t_{j})),A^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{j}\big)\big|ds\leq\delta_{0}\nu\frac{T}{N}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\nu}{4\delta_{0}}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\big|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(u(s)-u(t_{j})\big)\big|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}ds

for any
\delta_{0}>0
. Hence using the above upper estimates (
3.2
) – (
3.14
) in (
3.9
) with
\phi=e_{j}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\big(e_{j}-e_{j-1}\,,e_{j}\big)+\nu\frac{T}{N}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\;\nu\sum_{r=0}^{3}\delta_{r}\frac{T}{N}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{l=1}^{3}\tilde{T_{j}}(l)

\displaystyle\quad+\Big(\gamma_{2}+\frac{\bar{C}^{2}}{4\delta_{1}\nu}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{j})|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big)\,\frac{T}{N}\,|e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2},

(3.15)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{T_{j}}(1)=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\nu}{4\delta_{0}}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\big|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(u(s)-u(t_{j})\big)\big|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}ds,

\displaystyle\tilde{T_{j}}(2)=

\displaystyle\;C(\nu,\delta_{2},\gamma_{2})\,|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{j})|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\|u(s)-u(t_{j})\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{4}}^{2}ds,

\displaystyle\tilde{T_{j}}(3)=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{4\nu\delta_{3}}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\|u(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{4}}^{2}\|u(t_{j})-u(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{4}}^{2}ds.

Step 2: Pointwise discrete Gronwall lemma

We will use the convention
\sum_{j=l_{1}}^{l_{2}}x_{j}=0
if
l_{1}>l_{2}
.
Adding the inequalities (
3.2
), using
e_{0}=0
and the identity

(a,a-b)=\frac{1}{2}\big[|a|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}-|b|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+|a-b|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\big]
, we deduce for
k=1,\cdots,N

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq k}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\Big(|e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{l=1}^{j}|e_{l}-e_{l-1}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\nu\Big(1-\sum_{r=0}^{3}\delta_{r}\Big)\,\frac{T}{N}\sum_{l=1}^{j}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{l}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{T}{N}\,\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Big(\gamma_{2}+\frac{\bar{C}^{2}}{4\delta_{1}\nu}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{j})|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big)\,|e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\tilde{T_{j}}(i)

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2\gamma_{2}\frac{T}{N}\Big(|u(t_{k})|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+|{\bf u}^{k}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\frac{\bar{C}^{2}}{2\delta_{1}\nu}\frac{T}{N}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{k})_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big(|u(t_{k})|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+|{\bf u}^{k}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{T}{N}\,\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\Big(\gamma_{2}+\frac{\bar{C}^{2}}{4\delta_{1}\nu}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{j})|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big)\,|e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\tilde{T_{j}}(i)

\displaystyle\quad\leq 2\gamma_{2}\frac{T}{N}\Big(\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|u(s)|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+\sup_{1\leq k\leq N}|{\bf u}^{k}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\frac{\bar{C}^{2}}{2\delta_{1}\nu}\frac{T}{N}\Big(\frac{3}{2}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{4}+\frac{1}{2}\sup_{1\leq k\leq N}|{\bf u}^{k}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{4}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{T}{N}\,\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\Big(\gamma_{2}+\frac{\bar{C}^{2}}{4\delta_{1}\nu}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(s)|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big)\,|e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{T_{j}}(i)

(3.16)

Set

\displaystyle g:=

\displaystyle\;\Big(2\gamma_{2}+\frac{\bar{C}^{2}}{2\delta_{1}\nu}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(s)|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big),

\displaystyle Z_{1}:=

\displaystyle\;4\gamma_{2}\frac{T}{N}\Big(\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|u(s)|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}+\sup_{1\leq k\leq N}|{\bf u}^{k}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\frac{\bar{C}^{2}}{2\delta_{1}\nu}\frac{T}{N}\Big(3\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{4}+\sup_{1\leq k\leq N}|{\bf u}^{k}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{4}\Big),

\displaystyle Z_{2}:=

\displaystyle\;2\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{T_{j}}(i).

(3.17)

Suppose that
\sum_{r=0}^{3}\delta_{r}<1
; then neglecting some non-negative terms in the left hand side of (
3.2
) we deduce
for
Z:=Z_{1}+Z_{2}

\max_{1\leq j\leq k}|e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\leq Z+\frac{T}{N}\,g\,\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}|e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2},\quad k=2,...,N.

Using the discrete Gronwall lemma, see
[
18
]
, we deduce that a.s.

\max_{1\leq j\leq N}|e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\leq Z\Big(1+\frac{Tg}{N}\Big)^{N-1}\leq Ze^{Tg}.

(3.18)

Step 3: Strong speed of convergence

Taking the expected value in (
3.18
) and using the Hölder inequality with conjugate exponents
p,q\in(1,+\infty)
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\max_{1\leq j\leq k}|e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\big(Ze^{Tg}\Big)\leq\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\big(e^{pTg}\big)\Big]^{1/p}\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\big(Z^{q}\big)\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle=e^{2\gamma_{2}T}\,\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\Big(e^{pT\frac{\bar{C}^{2}}{2\delta_{1}\nu}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(s)|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}}\Big)\Big]^{1/p}\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\big(|Z_{1}+Z_{2}|^{q}\big)\Big]^{1/q}.

(3.19)

Since by assumption
{\rm Tr}\,(Q)<\frac{2\nu^{2}}{\bar{C}^{2}\,T}
, we have
\frac{T\,\bar{C}^{2}}{2\,\nu}<\frac{\nu}{{\rm Tr}\textpm,(Q)}:=\tilde{\alpha}_{0}
. Therefore,
we may choose
p\in(1,+\infty)
(close to 1)
and
\delta_{1}\in(0,1)
(close to 1) such that
pT\frac{\bar{C}^{2}}{2\delta_{1}\nu}<\tilde{\alpha}_{0}
.
Using (
2.10
) we deduce

{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\exp\Big(pT\frac{\bar{C}^{2}}{2\delta_{1}\nu}\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(s)|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big)\Big]=C<\infty.

(3.20)

Choosing
p
close enough to 1 we may have
q=2^{\tilde{q}}
for some
\tilde{q}\in[2,+\infty)
.
Since
\|u_{0}\|_{V}^{2}
has exponential moments, we have
{\mathbb{E}}(\|u_{0}\|_{V}^{r})<\infty
for
r
arbitrary large, that is
r\geq 2q
.
Using the estimates (
2.9
) and (
3.4
), we infer

\big[{\mathbb{E}}\big(Z_{1}^{q}\big)\big]^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C\,\frac{T}{N}.

(3.21)

The time regularity in
V
proved in (
2.12
) implies

\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{T_{j}}(1)\Big|^{q}\Big)\Big]^{1/q}\leq C\Big(\frac{T}{N}\Big)^{\eta},\quad\eta\in(0,1).

(3.22)

On the other hand, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{T_{j}}(2)

\displaystyle\Big|^{q}\Big)\Big]^{1/q}=C(\nu,\delta_{2},\gamma_{2})\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\,|A^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t_{j})|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\|u(s)-u(t_{j})\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{4}}^{2}ds\Big|^{q}\Big)\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\Big|\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\|u(s)-u(t_{j})\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{4}}^{2}ds\Big|^{q}\Big)\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{4q}\Big)\Big]^{\frac{1}{2q}}\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\|u(s)-u(t_{j})\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{4}}^{2}ds\Big|^{2q}\Big)\Big]^{\frac{1}{2q}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{T}{N}\Big)^{\eta},\quad\eta\in(0,1),

(3.23)

where the last upper estimate is a consequence of (
2.9
) and (
2.13
).

Using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.2
), a similar argument implies

\displaystyle\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tilde{T_{j}}(3)\Big|^{q}\Big)\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\nu\delta_{3}}\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\|u(s)\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{4}}^{2}\|u(s)-u(t_{j})\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{4}}^{2}ds\Big)^{q}\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\Big|\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|u(s)\|_{V}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\|u(s)-u(t_{j})\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{4}}^{2}ds\Big|^{q}\Big)\Big]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{T}{N}\Big)^{\eta},\quad\eta\in(0,1).

(3.24)

The upper estimates (
3.22
)–(
3.2
) imply

\big[{\mathbb{E}}\big(|Z_{2}|^{q}\big)\Big]^{1/q}\leq C\,\Big(\frac{T}{N}\Big)^{\eta},\quad\eta\in(0,1).

(3.25)

We then choose
\delta_{r}>0
,
r=0,2,3
such that
\sum_{r=0}^{3}\delta_{r}<1
.
Collecting the estimates (
3.2
), (
3.20
), (
3.21
) and (
3.25
), we deduce

{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\max_{1\leq j\leq N}|e_{j}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big)\leq C\Big(\frac{T}{N}\Big)^{\eta},\quad\eta\in(0,1).

Finally, plugging this inequality in (
3.2
), we obtain (
3.10
); this completes the proof.

4.
Space-time discretization

4.1.
Description of the finite element method

When studying a space time discretization using finite elements,
one needs to have a stable pairing of the velocity and the pressure which
satisfy the discrete LBB-condition (see e.g.
[
11
]
, page 2469 and pages 2487-2489). Stability issues are crucial, and the pressure has
to be discretized together with the velocity.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be a quasi-uniform triangulation of the domain
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, using triangles of maximal diameter
h>0
, and set

\bar{D}=\cup_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\bar{K}
. Let
{\mathbb{P}}_{i}(K):=[P_{i}(K)]^{2}
denote the space of polynomial vector fields on
K

of degree less than or equal to
i
. Given non negative integers
i,j
, we introduce finite element function spaces

\displaystyle{{\mathbb{H}}}_{h}

\displaystyle\,:=\{{\bf U}\in C^{0}(\bar{D})\cap{\mathbb{W}}^{1,2}_{per}(D):{\bf U}\in{\mathbb{P}}_{i}(K),\quad\forall K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\},

\displaystyle L_{h}

\displaystyle\,:=\{\Pi\in L^{2}_{per}(D):\Pi\in P_{j}(K),\quad\forall K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\},

which satisfy the discrete LBB-condition

\sup_{{\bf\Phi}\in{\mathbb{H}}_{h}}\frac{(\mbox{\rm div }{\bf\Phi},\Pi)}{|A^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf\Phi}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}}\geq C\;|\Pi|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}},\quad\forall\Pi\in L_{h},

(4.1)

with a constant
C>0
independent of the mesh size
h>0
. Here
C^{0}(\bar{D})
denotes the set of continuous 2-dimensional vector fields on
\bar{D}
.

Define the subset
{\mathbb{V}}_{h}\subset{\mathbb{H}}_{h}
of discrete divergence-free vector fields

{\mathbb{V}}_{h}:=\{{\bf\Phi}\in{\mathbb{H}}_{h}\;:\;({\rm div}\,{\bf\Phi},\Lambda)=0,\quad\forall\Lambda\in L_{h}\}.

(4.2)

Note that in general
{\mathbb{V}}_{h}\not\subset V
.
A way around this problem is to choose a space approximation such that
{\mathbb{V}}_{h}\subset V
, such as the Scott-Vogelius mixed elements
(see
[
20
]
and
[
22
]
).
This particular case yields a better approximation. Indeed, on one hand the pressure will not appear in the upper estimate, and on the
other hand a different localization will provide a polynomial error in the case of an additive noise.

Let
{\bf Q}_{h}^{0}:{\mathbb{L}}^{2}\mapsto{\mathbb{V}}_{h}
(resp.
P^{0}_{h}:L^{2}_{per}(D)\mapsto L_{h}
) denote the orthogonal projection defined by

({\bf z}-{\bf Q}^{0}_{h}{\bf z},{\bf\Phi})=0,\quad\forall{\bf\Phi}\in{\mathbb{V}}_{h},\qquad{\rm(resp.}\quad(z-P^{0}_{h}z,\Lambda)=0,\quad\forall\Lambda\in L_{h}{\rm)}.

(4.3)

The following estimates are standard (see e.g.
[
17
]
)

\displaystyle|{\bf z}-{\bf Q}_{h}^{0}{\bf z}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}+h|A^{\frac{1}{2}}({\bf z}-{\bf Q}^{0}_{h}{\bf z})|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\;C\,h^{2}\,|A{\bf z}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}},\quad\forall{\bf z}\in V\cap{\mathbb{W}}^{2,2}(D),

(4.4)

\displaystyle|{\bf z}-{\bf Q}^{0}_{h}{\bf z}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\;C\,h\,|A^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf z}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}},\quad\forall{\bf z}\in V,

(4.5)

\displaystyle|z-P^{0}_{h}z|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,h\,|\nabla z|_{L^{2}},\qquad\forall z\in W^{1,2}_{per}(D).

(4.6)

Using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.2
), we deduce from (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) the following upper estimates for

{\bf z}\in V\cap{\mathbb{W}}^{2,2}(D)

\|{\bf z}-{\bf Q}^{0}_{h}{\bf z}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{4}}\leq Ch^{\frac{3}{2}}|A{\bf z}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}},\quad{\rm and}\;\|{\bf z}-{\bf Q}^{0}_{h}{\bf z}\|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{4}}\leq Ch\,|A{\bf z}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,|A^{\frac{1}{2}}{\bf z}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.7)

The following result about the pressure term will be used in the study of space-time discretization. It extends
[
11
, Lemma 3.2]

to arbitrary moments.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let (
2.6
) be satisfied and suppose that
u_{0}
is
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
-measurable such that (
2.7
) holds.
Let
\{{\bf u}^{l},\pi^{l}\}_{l=0,\cdots,N}
be the solution to (
3.1
) and
(
3.2
).
Then given
q\in[2,\infty)
, there exists a constant
C
depending on
T,q,K_{0}
and
{\mathbb{E}}(\|u_{0}\|_{V}^{6q})<\infty
, such that

{\mathbb{E}}\Big(\Big|\frac{T}{N}\sum_{l=1}^{N}|\nabla\pi^{l}|_{{\mathbb{L}}^{2}}^{2}\Big|^{q}\Big)\leq C,\qquad N=1,2,...

(4.8)

Proof.

The beginning of proof is similar to that in
[
11
]
; we include it for the sake of completeness.

Let formally
\phi=\nabla\pi^{l}
in (
3.1
); since
\Delta_{l}W\in V
, we have
\big(\Delta_{l}W,\nabla\pi^{l}\big)=0
.
Using (
3.2
), and then Cauchy-Schwarz and Young inequalities, we deduce that a.s. for
l=1,...,N

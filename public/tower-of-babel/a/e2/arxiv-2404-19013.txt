Transitionless Quantum Driving of the Tomonaga-Luttinger Liquid
Léonce Dupays, Adolfo del Campo

Abstract
Shortcuts to adiabaticity (STA) make the fast preparation of many-body states possible, circumventing the limitations of adiabatic strategies. We propose a fast STA protocol for generating interacting states in the Tomonaga-Luttinger liquid by counter-diabatic driving, stirring the dynamics with an auxiliary control field. To this end, we exploit the equivalence between the time-dependent Tomonaga-Luttinger liquid and an ensemble of quantum oscillators with driven mass and frequency. We specify the closed-form expression of the counterdiabatic control and demonstrate its efficiency in suppressing excitations.

CD control and the driven TLL.

As a control approach, CD guides the nonadiabatic dynamics of a given system along a prescribed adiabatic trajectory
[
38
,
39
,
40
,
41
]
. It removes the requirement of slow driving in adiabatic methods by introducing auxiliary controls. Specifically, consider the adiabatic trajectory associated with a system described by the time-dependent Hamiltonian
H_{0}(t)=\sum_{n}E_{n}(t)|n(t)\rangle\langle n(t)|
. The adiabatic evolution associated to the Hamiltonian
H_{0}(t)
is given by the state
|\Psi_{n}(t)\rangle=e^{i\theta_{n}(t)}e^{i\gamma_{n}(t)}|n(t)\rangle
for an initial state in the
n

|n(0)\rangle
, with the dynamical phase
\theta_{n}(t)=-\frac{1}{\hbar}\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}E_{n}(t^{\prime})
and the Berry phase
\gamma_{n}(t)=i\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\langle n(t^{\prime})|\partial_{t^{\prime}}n(t^{\prime})\rangle
. The CD makes the adiabatic evolution to
H_{0}(t)
the exact evolution when the dynamics is generated by the controlled Hamiltonian that reads
H(t)=H_{0}(t)+H_{\rm CD}(t)
, with the auxiliary CD term

\displaystyle H_{\rm CD}=i\hbar\sum_{n}\left(|\partial_{t}n\rangle\langle n|-\langle n|\partial_{t}n\rangle|n\rangle\langle n|\right),

(6)

where
|n\rangle=|n(t)\rangle
, for short. The instantaneous eigenstates of
H_{0}(t)
can be written in terms of the evolution operator for parallel transport,
U_{t}=\sum_{n}|n(t)\rangle\langle n(0)|
as
|n\rangle=U_{t}|n(0)\rangle
. As a result, the first CD term in Eq. (
6
) can be expressed as

i\hbar\sum_{n}|\partial_{t}n\rangle\langle n|=i\hbar\partial_{t}U_{t}U^{\dagger}_{t}
.
Furthermore, the Berry phase can be written as the expectation value
-i\hbar\langle n(0)|U^{\dagger}_{t}\partial_{t}U_{t}|n(0)\rangle
.

Let us focus on the case of the driven TLL when

\displaystyle a(p,t)

\displaystyle=\cosh(\eta_{t})b(p)-\sinh(\eta_{t})b^{\dagger}(-p),

(7)

\displaystyle b(p)

\displaystyle=\cosh(\eta_{t})a(p,t)+\sinh(\eta_{t})a^{\dagger}(-p,t).

After the Bogoliubov transformation, the Hamiltonian (

\displaystyle H_{\rm TL}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p\neq 0}\epsilon(p,t)a^{\dagger}(p,t)a(p,t)+E_{0}(t),

with the ground state energy

Counterdiabatic control using invariant of motions.
The CD guides the nonadiabatic dynamics of the TLL along the prescribed adiabatic trajectory. The latter can be built from a unitary evolution. The supplementary gauge potential due to the nonadiabatic dynamics gives rise to the CD Hamiltonian. To formalize this idea, we exploit the theory of invariants of motions developed by Lewis and Riesenfeld for the
SU(1,1)

In terms of it, the Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\neq 0}Q(\gamma_{p})\frac{K_{0}(p)\hbar\omega_{0p}}{\sigma^{2}_{t}}Q^{\dagger}(\gamma_{p})+i\hbar\frac{\partial Q}{\partial t}Q^{\dagger},

\displaystyle=\hbar\sum_{p\neq 0}\bigg\{\omega_{\rm CD}(p,t)K_{0}(p)+\frac{g_{\rm CD}(p,t)}{2}[K_{+}(p)+K_{-}(p)]

\displaystyle+i\frac{\dot{\gamma_{p}}}{2\gamma_{p}}\left[K_{+}(p)-K_{-}(p)\right]\bigg\}.

(10)

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{0p}}{2}\left[\left(\frac{\gamma_{p}}{\sigma_{t}}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{(\sigma_{t}\gamma_{p})^{2}}\right],

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{0p}}{2}\left[\frac{1}{(\sigma_{t}\gamma_{p})^{2}}-\left(\frac{\gamma_{p}}{\sigma_{t}}\right)^{2}\right].

Counterdiabatic control as a time-dependent mass and frequency oscillator.
A natural physical interpretation of the CD Hamiltonian emerges by mapping the TLL model to an ensemble of quantum harmonic oscillators with time-dependent mass and frequency (
1
). To this end, we define the operators
X_{p}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_{0p}}}[b^{\dagger}(p)+b(-p)]

P_{p}=i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_{0p}}{2}}[b^{\dagger}(p)-b(-p)]
. We define an effective mass
M_{p}(t)
and frequency
\Omega_{p}(t)
related to the interaction parameters as
|p|[v_{F}+\frac{g_{4}(p,t)}{2\pi}+\frac{g_{2}(p,t)}{2\pi}]=\frac{M_{p}(t)\Omega^{2}(p,t)}{\omega_{0p}}
and
|p|[v_{F}+\frac{g_{4}(p,t)}{2\pi}-\frac{g_{2}(p,t)}{2\pi}]=\frac{\omega_{0p}}{M_{p}(t)}
; see further details in
[
69
]
, which refers to
[
35
,
79
]
. This yields

\displaystyle H_{\rm TL}(t)=\sum_{p\neq 0}\frac{P_{p}P_{-p}}{2M_{p}(t)}+\sum_{p\neq 0}\frac{1}{2}M_{p}(t)\Omega^{2}_{p}(t)X_{p}X_{-p},

with

\displaystyle\Omega(p,t)=v_{s,p}(t)|p|,\quad M_{p}(t)=\frac{\omega_{0p}}{K_{p}(t)v_{s,p}(t)|p|}.

(17)

As a result, the CD Hamiltonian for the TLL is given by

\displaystyle H_{\rm CD}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\neq 0}\frac{\dot{K}_{p}(t)}{4K_{p}(t)}[P_{p}X_{-p}+X_{p}P_{-p}],

(18)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{p\neq 0}\left(\frac{\dot{\Omega}(p,t)}{\Omega(p,t)}+\frac{\dot{M}_{p}(t)}{M_{p}(t)}\right)[P_{p}X_{-p}+X_{p}P_{-p}].

Conclusion.
We have engineered fast-driving shortcut protocols for the nonadiabatic preparation of many-body quantum states in the TLL. To this end, we have identified the auxiliary Hamiltonian controls for transitionless quantum driving of the TLL, exploiting its analogy with an ensemble of harmonic oscillators with time-dependent mass and frequency. The auxiliary CD Hamiltonian control is the sum of generators of two-mode squeezing in terms of bosonic operators and admits a natural representation upon bosonization.
We have
specified the CD protocol in terms of the time-dependent TLL parameters and an external potential. The auxiliary CD Hamiltonian could be engineered through a long-range interaction between right and left movers densities that varies according to the form of the
g_{2}
and
g_{4}
scattering. Our results open new avenues for engineering shortcuts to adiabaticity and fast quantum control protocols using effective field theories providing an alternative to adiabatic strategies. They should find broad applications in many-particle quantum thermodynamic devices, the study of thermalization, and control of ultracold gases in tight-waveguides and one-dimensional conducting systems.

I
Details on the derivation of the Tomonaga-Luttinger model

The free Hamiltonian of the TLL can be expressed as [
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}3
]

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p\neq 0}\hbar v_{F}|p|b^{\dagger}(p)b(p)+\frac{\pi v_{F}}{L}(N^{2}+J^{2}),

(S1)

with
v_{F}
the Fermi velocity. The bosonic operators verify the usual commutation rule
[b(p),b^{\dagger}(p^{\prime})]=\delta_{p,p^{\prime}}
and their link to the densities of right and left movers
\rho_{\pm 1}(p)
is given in the main text [
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}2,70
]. Let us now introduce interactions in the model so that the complete Hamiltonian reads
H_{\rm TL}=H_{0}+V
, where the interaction potential takes the form
V=\sum_{i,j=\pm 1}\hat{V}_{ij}
, with

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{ij}=\frac{\pi\hbar}{L}\sum_{p>0}\{V_{ij}(p,t)\rho_{i}(-p)\rho_{j}(p)+V_{ij}(-p,t)\rho_{i}(p)\rho_{j}(-p)\}+\frac{\pi\hbar}{L}V_{ij}(0,t)\rho_{i}(0)\rho_{j}(0),

(S2)

and

\displaystyle V_{ij}(p,p^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L}\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{0}^{L}dx^{\prime}e^{ipx}V_{ij}(x-x^{\prime},t)e^{ip^{\prime}x^{\prime}},

(S3)

\displaystyle V_{ij}(p,p^{\prime},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{p,-p^{\prime}}V_{ij}(p,t).

(S4)

Even parity of the interaction potential is assumed in the following, i.e.,
V_{ij}(p,t)=V_{ij}(-p,t)
. In particular, the interaction potential for the backscattering between the right and left movers is denoted
g_{2}(p,t)/(2\pi)=V_{1,-1}(p,t)
, while the forward scattering between right movers and left movers is denoted by
g_{4}(p,t)/(2\pi)=V_{1,1}(p,t)=V_{-1,-1}(p,t)
. Combining the noninteracting and interacting parts leads to the Hamiltonian in the main text (
\color[rgb]{1,0,0.87}1
). The zero-momentum term defined by
C_{0}(t)=\frac{\pi\hbar v_{F}}{L}(N^{2}+J^{2})+\frac{\hbar g_{2}(0,t)}{4L}\left(N^{2}-J^{2}\right)+\frac{\hbar g_{4}(0,t)}{4L}\left(N^{2}+J^{2}\right)
is gauged away in Eq. (
\color[rgb]{1,0,0.87}1
). Using normal ordering, we denoted by
N=N_{1}+N_{-1}
the sum of left and right movers and by
J=N_{1}-N_{-1}
the current operator.

To establish the field representation of the TLL, consider the field operator with right and left movers
\Psi(x)\approx e^{ik_{F}x}\Psi_{1}(x)+e^{-ik_{F}x}\Psi_{-1}(x)
[
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}4
]. The field can be decomposed according to a phase operator
\phi(x)
and its conjugated operator
\Pi(x)=\frac{\partial_{x}\theta(x)}{\pi}
, through the bosonization procedure [
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}70
], yielding in the fermionic case [
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}72
]

\displaystyle\Psi^{\dagger}(x)\approx\sqrt{\rho_{0}-\Pi(x)}\sum_{p}e^{i(2p+1)(\pi\rho_{0}x-\theta(x))}e^{-i\phi(x)},

(S5)

where the average density
\rho_{0}
is related to the Fermi momentum
\pi\rho_{0}=k_{F}
.
It is further possible to decompose the fields in the bosonic basis

\theta(x)=-N\frac{\pi x}{L}-i\sum_{k\neq 0}\sqrt{\frac{\pi|k|}{2L}}\frac{e^{-\nu|k|/2-ikx}}{k}\left[b^{\dagger}(k)+b(-k)\right],

(S6)

\phi(x)=J\frac{\pi x}{L}+i\sum_{k\neq 0}\sqrt{\frac{\pi|k|}{2L}}\frac{e^{-\nu|k|/2-ikx}}{|k|}\left[b^{\dagger}(k)-b(-k)\right],

(S7)

with
\nu
a cutoff parameter that ensures convergence, in the limit
\nu\to 0
[
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}70
]. The relation between the field and the bosonic basis is useful for expressing the TLL in its field representation.

II
Theory of invariants of motion

A rigorous derivation of the CD term relies on the theory of invariants of motion. Respectively,
I
is an invariant of motion when
i\hbar\frac{dI(t)}{dt}=i\hbar\frac{\partial I(t)}{\partial t}-[\mathcal{H},I(t)]=0
. Making use of the spectral decomposition of the invariant of motion
I(t)=\sum_{n}\lambda_{n}|\lambda_{n},t\rangle\langle\lambda_{n},t|
yields the solution to the time-dependent Schrödinger equation with Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
in the form [
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}73
]
|\Psi_{t}\rangle=\exp\left(i\int_{0}^{t}\kappa_{n}(s)ds\right)|\lambda_{n},t\rangle,
with
\kappa_{n}(s)=\langle\lambda_{n},s|i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\frac{\mathcal{H}}{\hbar}|\lambda_{n},s\rangle
. One can construct a time-dependent invariant
I=QI^{0}Q^{\dagger}
using a time-independent operator
I^{0}
and a unitary time-evolution operator
Q
. The corresponding Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(t)=F(I,t)+i\hbar\frac{\partial Q}{\partial t}Q^{\dagger},

(S8)

where
F(I,t)=I/\sigma^{2}_{t}
and
\sigma_{t}
a scalar time-dependent function. The dynamical phase can be expressed by direct substitution of
|\Psi_{t}\rangle
in the Schrödinger equation with Hamiltonian (
S8
), one obtains the phase
\kappa_{n}(s)=-\frac{1}{\hbar}\langle\lambda_{n},s|F(I,t)|\lambda_{n},s\rangle
.

III
Counterdiabatic controls for the quantum oscillator with driven mass and frequency

As the TLL is analogous to an ensemble of quantum harmonic oscillators with time-dependent mass and frequency, it is useful to study the counter-diabatic driving in the latter case. We make use of Lohe’s solution of the time-dependent Schrödinger equation
i\hbar\frac{d|\Psi(t)\rangle}{dt}=H|\Psi(t)\rangle

with Hamiltonian [
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}73
]

\displaystyle H(t)=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2m_{0}\sigma^{2}_{t}}p^{2}+\frac{1}{2}m_{0}\frac{\omega^{2}_{0}}{\sigma^{2}_{t}\gamma^{2}}x^{2}+\frac{\dot{\gamma}}{2\gamma}\{x,p\}.

(S9)

Consider the
n
-th eigenstate
\phi_{n}
of the (time-independent) initial harmonic oscillator
\hat{H}=\frac{p^{2}}{2m_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}m_{0}\omega^{2}_{0}x^{2}
with associated eigenvalue
E_{n}(0)
.
Its evolution, governed by (
S9
), reads

\displaystyle|\Psi_{n}(t)\rangle=\gamma^{-1/2}\exp\left(-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{E_{n}(0)ds}{\sigma^{2}_{s}}\right)\phi_{n}\left(\frac{x}{\gamma},0\right).

(S10)

We identify the time-dependent mass
m(t)=\frac{m_{0}\sigma^{2}_{t}}{\gamma^{2}}
and frequency
\omega^{2}(t)=\frac{\omega^{2}_{0}}{\sigma^{4}_{t}}
, so that
\gamma=\sqrt{\frac{m_{0}\omega_{0}}{m(t)\omega(t)}}
and
\sigma_{t}=\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{0}}{\omega(t)}}
. The Hamiltonian (
S9
) takes then the form

\displaystyle H(t)=\frac{1}{2m(t)}p^{2}+\frac{1}{2}m(t)\omega^{2}(t)x^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\dot{m}(t)}{m(t)}+\frac{\dot{\omega}(t)}{\omega(t)}\right)\{x,p\}.

(S11)

Thus, one can identify the reference Hamiltonian to be
H_{0}(t)=\frac{1}{2m(t)}p^{2}+\frac{1}{2}m(t)\omega^{2}(t)x^{2}
and the CD term as
H_{\rm CD}(t)=-\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\dot{m}(t)}{m(t)}+\frac{\dot{\omega}(t)}{\omega(t)}\right)\{x,p\}
, generalizing the well-known result for with time-dependent frequency and constant mass [
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}79
]. The latter is recovered for
\sigma_{t}=\gamma
[
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}35
]. The quantum harmonic oscillator algebra can be expressed as a function of the
SU(1,1)
generators so that
\hat{x}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}(a^{\dagger}+a)
and
\hat{p}=i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar m\omega}{2}}(a^{\dagger}-a)
, with
a
and
a^{\dagger}
being the canonical annihilation and creation operators, verifying the commutation rule
[a,a^{\dagger}]=1
. We define
J_{+}=\frac{1}{2}a^{\dagger 2}
,
J_{-}=\frac{1}{2}a^{2}
, and
J_{0}=\frac{1}{4}(a^{\dagger}a+aa^{\dagger})
, that verify the commutation rules
[J_{0},J_{\pm}]=\pm J_{\pm}
and
[J_{-},J_{+}]=2J_{0}
. In terms of them,
\hat{x}^{2}=\frac{\hbar}{m_{0}\omega_{0}}(2J_{0}+J_{+}+J_{-})
,
\hat{p}^{2}=\hbar m_{0}\omega_{0}(2J_{0}-J_{+}-J_{-})
and
\{x,p\}=i\hbar(a^{\dagger 2}-a^{2})=2i\hbar(J_{+}-J_{-})
.

By analogy, one can introduce the momentum-dependent harmonic oscillator. One can introduce the momentum-dependent pseudo-position and momentum operators

\displaystyle X_{p}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_{0p}}}[b^{\dagger}(p)+b(-p)],

(S12)

\displaystyle X_{-p}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_{0p}}}[b^{\dagger}(-p)+b(p)].

(S13)

They are not Hermitian, as
X^{\dagger}_{p}=X_{-p}
. However, their product is, as
(X_{p}X_{-p})^{\dagger}=X^{\dagger}_{-p}X^{\dagger}_{p}=X_{p}X_{-p}
and

\displaystyle X_{p}X_{-p}=\frac{\hbar}{2\omega_{0p}}[2K_{0}(p)+K_{+}(p)+K_{-}(p)].

(S14)

Similarly, one can introduce
P_{p}=i\sqrt{\frac{\hbar\omega_{0}}{2}}[b^{\dagger}(p)-b(-p)]
so that

\displaystyle P_{p}P_{-p}=\frac{\hbar\omega_{0p}}{2}[2K_{0}(p)-(K_{+}(p)+K_{-}(p))].

(S15)

One can use these operators to express the TLL Hamiltonian as an ensemble of harmonic oscillators with time-dependent mass and frequency (
\color[rgb]{1,0,0.87}15
).

IV
Mean Energy under counterdiabatic driving

The nonadiabatic mean energy in the driven TLL under CD reads

\displaystyle\langle\Psi(t)|\mathcal{H}(t)|\Psi(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Psi(0)|Q^{\dagger}\left(Q\frac{I^{0}}{\sigma^{2}_{t}}Q^{\dagger}+i\hbar\frac{\partial Q}{\partial t}Q^{\dagger}\right)Q|\Psi(0)\rangle

(S16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Psi(0)|\frac{I^{0}}{\sigma^{2}_{t}}|\Psi(0)\rangle+i\hbar\langle\Psi(0)|Q^{\dagger}\frac{\partial Q}{\partial t}|\Psi(0)\rangle,

However, the explicit evaluation shows that

\displaystyle i\hbar Q^{\dagger}\frac{\partial Q}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\hbar\sum_{p\neq 0}\frac{\dot{\gamma_{p}}}{2\gamma_{p}}[K_{+}(p)-K_{-}(p)].

(S17)

As a result,

\displaystyle\langle\Psi(t)|\mathcal{H}(t)|\Psi(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Psi(0)|\frac{I^{0}}{\sigma^{2}_{t}}|\Psi(0)\rangle+i\hbar\sum_{p\neq 0}\frac{\dot{\gamma_{p}}}{2\gamma_{p}}\langle\Psi(0)|[K_{+}(p)-K_{-}(p)]|\Psi(0)\rangle

(S18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Psi(0)|\frac{I^{0}}{\sigma^{2}_{t}}|\Psi(0)\rangle.

(S19)

In short, the nonadiabatic mean energy along the CD protocol can be expressed in terms of its initial value and the scaling function
\sigma_{t}
.

V
Implementation of the counter-diabatic driving term

The CD Hamiltonian can be conveniently written as

\displaystyle H_{\rm CD}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\hbar\sum_{p>0}\frac{\dot{K}}{2K}[b(p)b(-p)-b^{\dagger}(p)b^{\dagger}(-p)]

(S20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\hbar\sum_{p>0}\frac{\dot{K}}{2K}\frac{2\pi}{Lp}\Big(\rho_{1}(-p)\rho_{-1}(p)-\rho_{1}(p)\rho_{-1}(-p)\Big)

(S21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\hbar\sum_{p>0}\frac{\dot{K}}{2K}\frac{2\pi}{Lp}\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{0}^{L}dx^{\prime}\Big(\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{-1}(x^{\prime})-\rho_{1}(x^{\prime})\rho_{-1}(x)\Big)e^{ip(x^{\prime}-x)}.

(S22)

Using the quantization of the momentum
p=\frac{2\pi}{L}n
with
n\in\mathbbm{N}^{*}
, one can write the sum

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(x^{\prime}-x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{i\frac{2\pi}{L}n(x^{\prime}-x)}}{n}.

(S23)

For the special values
x=x^{\prime}
and
|x^{\prime}-x|=L
, the infinite sum diverges;
\mathcal{S}(x^{\prime}-x)\to+\infty
. We would like to show that at these specific points, the function
\mathcal{S}(x^{\prime}-x)
can be approximated as a delta function. Given a test function
f(x)
, as a generalized distribution, the delta function satisfies
\int dxf(x)\delta(x)=f(0)
, along with the normalization condition
\int dx\delta(x)=1
.
We first verify that
\mathcal{S}(r)
is a normalizable function on its domain of definition
r\in[-L,L]
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\int_{-L}^{L}dr\mathcal{S}(r)=\frac{1}{i\frac{2\pi}{L}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{i2\pi n}}{n^{2}}-\frac{e^{-i2\pi n}}{n^{2}}\right)=\frac{L}{\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(2\pi n)}{n^{2}}.

(S24)

Furthermore, this last series is bounded in absolute value by a convergent series

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(2\pi n)}{n^{2}}\right|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}.

(S25)

Using the property of absolute convergence, the function
\mathcal{S}(r)
is then normalizable on
[-L,L]
. As a consequence, we can approximate
S(r)
to mimic a delta function at the special values
x=x^{\prime}
and
|x-x^{\prime}|=L
. Note, however, that outside these points, the function is not equal to zero, so we need to add a supplementary contribution to the points
x\neq x^{\prime}
. Overall, we can write
\mathcal{S}(x^{\prime}-x)
as a function of its different contributions
\mathcal{S}(0)+\mathcal{S}(L)+\mathcal{S}(-L)+\mathcal{S}(x^{\prime}-x)_{x^{\prime}-x\neq\{0,L,-L\}}=\delta(x^{\prime}-x)+\delta(x^{\prime}-x-L)+\delta(x^{\prime}-x+L)+\mathcal{S}(x^{\prime}-x)_{x^{\prime}-x\neq\{0,L,-L\}}
. Furthermore, one can use the Euler’s formula to decompose the exponential

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{i\frac{2\pi}{L}n(x^{\prime}-x)}}{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\cos[\frac{2\pi}{L}n(x^{\prime}-x)]}{n}+i\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin[\frac{2\pi}{L}n(x^{\prime}-x)]}{n}.

(S26)

It is then useful to notice the formula [
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}83
] for
a\in(0,2\pi)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(na)}{n}=\frac{\pi-a}{2}.

(S27)

As the cosine is an even function, its contribution to the integral vanishes, and one is left with

\displaystyle H_{\rm CD}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\hbar\frac{\dot{K}}{2K}\int_{0}^{L}dxdx^{\prime}\Big[\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{-1}(x^{\prime})-\rho_{1}(x^{\prime})\rho_{-1}(x)\Big]\Big[\delta(x^{\prime}-x)+\delta(x^{\prime}-x-L)+\delta(x^{\prime}-x+L)+\frac{\pi-\frac{2\pi(x^{\prime}-x)}{L}}{2}\Big].

Using again that even functions do not contribute to the integral, one finds the expression quoted in the main text.

It is interesting to look at the effect of long-range interactions on the CD Hamiltonian form. An alternative implementation is found in using the Lorentzian potential for the long-range interaction

\displaystyle H_{\rm CD}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\hbar\Delta(t)\sum_{p>0}|p|[K_{-}(p)-K_{+}(p)],

(S29)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\hbar\Delta(t)\sum_{p>0}\frac{2\pi}{L}[\rho_{1}(-p)\rho_{-1}(p)-\rho_{1}(p)\rho_{-1}(-p)]

(S30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\hbar\Delta(t)\frac{2\pi}{L}\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{0}^{L}dx^{\prime}[\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{-1}(x^{\prime})-\rho_{1}(x^{\prime})\rho_{-1}(x)]\sum_{p>0}\frac{e^{ip(x^{\prime}-x)}}{2}

(S31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\hbar\Delta(t)\frac{2\pi}{L}\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{0}^{L}dx^{\prime}[\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{-1}(x^{\prime})-\rho_{1}(x^{\prime})\rho_{-1}(x)]\sum_{p>0}\frac{\left(e^{ip(x^{\prime}-x)}-e^{-ip(x^{\prime}-x)}\right)}{2}

(S32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\hbar\Delta(t)\frac{2\pi}{L}\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{0}^{L}dx^{\prime}[\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{-1}(x^{\prime})-\rho_{1}(x^{\prime})\rho_{-1}(x)]\ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(e^{i\frac{2\pi n}{L}(x^{\prime}-x)}-e^{-i\frac{2\pi n}{L}(x^{\prime}-x)}\right)}{2}

(S33)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\Delta(t)\frac{\pi}{L}\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{0}^{L}dx^{\prime}[\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{-1}(x^{\prime})-\rho_{1}(x^{\prime})\rho_{-1}(x)]\frac{1}{\tan[\frac{\pi}{L}(x^{\prime}-x)]}

(S34)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\hbar\Delta(t)\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{0}^{L}dx^{\prime}[\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{-1}(x^{\prime})-\rho_{1}(x^{\prime})\rho_{-1}(x)]\left[\frac{1}{(x-x^{\prime})}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3L^{2}}(x-x^{\prime})+\mathcal{O}(L^{-4})\right],

(S35)

leading to the result presented in the main text.

VI
Spectrum through the Bogoliubov transformation

The Tomonaga-Luttinger liquid with
\rm CD
Hamilontian is written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\sum_{p\neq 0}\omega_{\rm CD}(p,t)K_{0}(p)+\hbar\sum_{p\neq 0}\alpha(t)K_{+}(p)+\hbar\sum_{p\neq 0}\alpha^{*}(t)K_{-}(p),

(S36)

\displaystyle\alpha(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{g_{\rm CD}(p,t)}{2}+i\frac{\dot{\gamma_{p}}}{2\gamma_{p}}.

(S37)

One can set

\displaystyle b(p)=ud(p)+vd^{\dagger}(-p).

(S38)

The bosonic commutation relation
[b(p),b^{\dagger}(p^{\prime})]=\delta_{p,p^{\prime}}
enforces the condition

\displaystyle|v|^{2}-|u|^{2}=1,

(S39)

leading to the parametrization

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\theta_{1}}\cosh(r),

(S40)

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\theta_{2}}\sinh(r).

(S41)

In the new basis, the Hamiltonian becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\sum_{p\neq 0}\left[\frac{\omega_{\rm CD}(p,t)}{2}(|u|^{2}+|v|^{2})+\alpha u^{*}v^{*}+\alpha^{*}uv\right]d^{\dagger}(p)d(p)

(S44)

\displaystyle+\hbar\sum_{p\neq 0}\left[\omega_{\rm CD}(p,t)uv^{*}+\alpha(v^{*})^{2}+\alpha^{*}u^{2}\right]d(-p)d(p)

\displaystyle+\hbar\sum_{p\neq 0}\left[\frac{\omega_{\rm CD}(p,t)}{2}(|u|^{2}+|v|^{2})+\alpha v^{*}u^{*}+\alpha^{*}uv\right]d(-p)d^{\dagger}(-p)

\displaystyle+\hbar\sum_{p\neq 0}\left[\omega_{\rm CD}(p,t)u^{*}v+\alpha(u^{*})^{2}+\alpha^{*}v^{2}\right]d^{\dagger}(p)d^{\dagger}(-p).

The diagonalization condition is given by

\displaystyle 0=\omega_{\rm CD}(p,t)e^{i(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})}\frac{1}{2}\sinh(2r)+|\alpha|e^{i\phi_{\alpha}}e^{-2i\theta_{1}}\cosh^{2}(r)+|\alpha|e^{-i\phi_{\alpha}}e^{2i\theta_{2}}\sinh^{2}(r),

(S46)

with
\theta_{1}=\theta_{2}
with
\theta_{1}=\phi_{\alpha}/2
and

\displaystyle\tanh(2r)=-\frac{2|\alpha|}{\omega_{\rm CD}(p,t)}.

(S47)

The previous relation is obeyed only if
\left|2|\alpha|/\omega_{\rm CD}(p,t)\right|<1
. The spectrum can be written as

\displaystyle\epsilon(p,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm CD}(p,t){\rm sech}(2r)=\sqrt{\omega^{2}_{\rm CD}(p,t)-g^{2}_{\rm CD}(p,t)-\left(\frac{\dot{\gamma_{p}}}{\gamma_{p}}\right)^{2}}

(S48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{\omega^{2}_{0p}}{\sigma^{4}_{t}}-\left(\frac{\dot{\gamma_{p}}}{\gamma_{p}}\right)^{2}}

(S49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{v^{2}_{s}|p|^{2}-\left(\frac{\dot{K}}{2K}\right)^{2}},

(S50)

where we used
\cosh[{\rm arctanh}(x)]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}
for
|x|<1
.

VII
Stability criteria for the TLL

For the TLL to be stable, the energy spectrum should be bounded from below

\displaystyle\sqrt{v^{2}_{s}(t)|p|^{2}-\left(\frac{\dot{K}(t)}{2K(t)}\right)^{2}}>0.

(S51)

Even though small momenta are more prone to lead to an imaginary spectrum in the presence of CD, a small enough amplitude for the CD Hamiltonian may not lead to a divergence of the dynamics. Indeed, the minimal value for the momentum is
p=\frac{2\pi}{L}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\left|v_{s}(t)\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)\right|>\left|\frac{\dot{K}(t)}{2K(t)}\right|,

(S52)

sets a bound on the speed at which the process can be performed for finite-size systems. In the case of the delta-interacting potential
\tilde{g}_{2}(t)=\tilde{g}_{4}(t)=\tilde{g}_{24}(t)
, the condition simplifies to

\displaystyle\left|\frac{v_{F}[\dot{\tilde{g}}_{24}(t)/2\pi]}{v^{3}_{s}(t)}\right|=\left|\frac{\dot{v}_{s}(t)}{v^{2}_{s}(t)}\right|<\frac{2\pi}{L}.

(S53)

This is reminiscent of the adiabatic condition for the time-dependent harmonic oscillator established by Lewis and Riesenfeld [
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}77
]

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\dot{\Omega}(p,t)}{\Omega^{2}(p,t)}\right|=\left|\frac{\dot{v}_{s}(t)}{v^{2}_{s}(t)|p|}\right|\ll 1.

(S54)

Thus, the same control parameter governs the onset of adiabaticity under slow driving and the breaking of the TLL description in a STA by CD.
The driving regime of interest for the CD protocol spans the range of driving speeds between these two limits. We explore this regime considering the previous experimental implementation of the TLL in ultracold atoms [
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}74\!-\!76
]. In [
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}74
], the authors measured a sound velocity
v_{s}\approx 2.21\ {\rm\mu m/ms}
and considered a system size
L=10-100\ {\rm\mu m}
. In [
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}76
], the authors considered
L\approx 50\ {\rm\mu m}
and
{v}_{s}\approx 2.04\ {\rm\mu m/ms}
. By dimensional analysis
|\dot{v}_{s}/v^{2}_{s}|\approx 1/(t_{f}v_{s})
, and the quench time needs to be larger than

t_{f}>\frac{L}{2\pi v_{s}}\approx 3.90\ {\rm ms}
.
The adiabatic quench has to be very large compared to this time so that the regime of interest of the counterdiabatic protocol is between
3.9{\rm ms}
and
39{\rm ms}
for the given experimental values. The sound velocity was computed with the formula
{v}_{s}=\sqrt{g_{\rm 1D}{n}_{\rm 1D}/m}
[
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}76
] with the
1D
interaction strength
g_{\rm 1D}=\hbar\omega_{\perp}a_{s}\frac{2+3a_{s}n_{\rm 1D}}{1+2a_{s}n_{\rm 1D}}
, the three dimensional scattering length
a_{s}=5.2\ {\rm nm}
, the mass
m=1.44\times 10^{-25}\ {\rm kg}
of the
{}^{87}{\rm Rb}
atom, the transverse trapping frequency
\omega_{\perp}=2\pi\times 1.4\ {\rm kHz}
, and the density
n_{\rm 1D}\approx 70\ {\rm\mu m}^{-1}
.

VIII
CD term for the long-range interaction quench of Lorentz form

We consider the modulation of the interaction strength of the form
g_{2/4}(p,t)=\lambda(t)\exp(-R_{0}|p|)
, so that the amplitude of the CD driving term reads

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{K}(t)}{K(t)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\dot{g}_{24}}{\pi v_{F}+g_{24}}.

(S55)

To remain in the Luttinger description, one can linearize the interaction. The momentum cutoff is equivalent to assume that only momenta
|p|\ll\frac{1}{R_{0}}
contribute. One can Taylor expand the previous expression

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{K}(t)}{K(t)}

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{1}{2}\dot{\lambda}(t)\left(\frac{1}{\pi v_{F}+\lambda(t)}-\frac{R_{0}\pi v_{F}|p|}{(\pi v_{F}+\lambda(t))^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(R^{2}_{0}|p|^{2})\right).

(S56)

As a consequence, the CD term is divided into two parts: one contribution stemming from the short-range interaction and one linear term in momentum coming from the long-range Lorentzian interaction. Also, the momentum-independent term leads to a linear interaction between densities of right and left movers in real space, and the linear term in momentum leads to the inversely linear interaction in real space. Finally, one can write the CD Hamiltonian in real space as

\displaystyle H_{CD}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\hbar\sum_{p>0}\frac{\dot{K}(t)}{2K(t)}[K_{+}(p)-K_{-}(p)]

(S58)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i\hbar}{4}\frac{\dot{\lambda}(t)}{\pi v_{F}+\lambda(t)}\sum_{p>0}[K_{+}(p)-K_{-}(p)]+\frac{i\hbar}{4}\frac{\dot{\lambda}(t)R_{0}\pi v_{F}}{(\pi v_{F}+\lambda(t))^{2}}\sum_{p>0}|p|[K_{+}(p)-K_{-}(p)]

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi\hbar}{4}\frac{\dot{\lambda}(t)}{\pi v_{F}+\lambda(t)}\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{0}^{L}dx^{\prime}[\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{-1}(x^{\prime})-\rho_{1}(x^{\prime})\rho_{-1}(x)]\frac{x^{\prime}-x}{L}

\displaystyle+\frac{\hbar}{4}\frac{\dot{\lambda}(t)R_{0}\pi v_{F}}{(\pi v_{F}+\lambda(t))^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{0}^{L}dx^{\prime}[\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{-1}(x^{\prime})-\rho_{1}(x^{\prime})\rho_{-1}(x)]\frac{1}{x-x^{\prime}}.

IX
Implementation of the CD term with gauge fields

In this section, we detail the implementation of the CD Hamiltonian with gauge fields. Let us consider the gauge-field transformation of the Luttinger liquid

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hbar v_{s}(t)}{2}\int_{0}^{L}\left[\frac{\pi}{K(t)}\tilde{\Pi}(x)^{2}+\frac{K(t)}{\pi}(\partial_{x}\phi(x))^{2}\right]dx,

(S59)

with
\tilde{\Pi}(x)=\Pi(x)-A(x)
the shifted momentum, and the long-range gauge field of strength
\nu

\displaystyle A(x)

\displaystyle=\nu\int dz\frac{\partial_{z}\phi(z)}{z-x}.

(S60)

A detailed expansion of the Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar v_{s}(t)}{2}\int_{0}^{L}\left[\frac{\pi}{K(t)}\Pi(x)^{2}+\frac{K(t)}{\pi}(\partial_{x}\phi(x))^{2}\right]dx

(S61)

\displaystyle+\frac{\hbar v_{s}(t)}{2}\frac{\pi}{K(t)}\nu^{2}\int dz\partial_{z}\phi(z)\int dy\partial_{y}\phi(y)\int_{0}^{L}dx\frac{1}{z-x}\frac{1}{y-x}

\displaystyle-\frac{\hbar v_{s}(t)}{2}\frac{\pi}{K(t)}\nu\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{0}^{L}dz\frac{1}{z-x}\left[\Pi(x)\partial_{z}\phi(z)+\partial_{z}\phi(z)\Pi(x)\right].

The second term in the previous equation vanishes due to the integration over orthogonal functions. To proceed further, one can express the fields in terms of the densities of left and right movers

\displaystyle\theta(x)

\displaystyle=-N\frac{\pi x}{L}-i\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{e^{-ikx}}{k}\left[\rho_{1}(k)+\rho_{-1}(k)\right],

(S62)

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle=J\frac{\pi x}{L}+i\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{e^{-ikx}}{|k|}{\rm sgn}(k)\left[\rho_{1}(k)-\rho_{-1}(k)\right].

(S63)

Finally, in omitting the constant part, the expression of the derivative of the fields is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\theta(x)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k\neq 0}e^{-ikx}\left[\rho_{1}(k)+\rho_{-1}(x)\right]=-L[\rho_{1}(x)+\rho_{-1}(x)],

(S64)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\phi(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq 0}e^{-ikx}\left[\rho_{1}(k)-\rho_{-1}(k)\right]=L[\rho_{1}(x)-\rho_{-1}(x)].

(S65)

As a consequence, the interacting term reads as follows

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar v_{s}(t)}{2}\frac{\pi}{K(t)}\frac{L^{2}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{0}^{L}dz\frac{1}{z-x}\left[2\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{1}(z)-2\rho_{-1}(x)\rho_{-1}(z)-2\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{-1}(z)+2\rho_{1}(z)\rho_{-1}(x)\right].

(S66)

The density-density interaction between strictly right movers or strictly left movers is symmetric under permutation of the positions
x
and
z
, and the antisymmetric contribution of the interaction potential
1/(z-x)
leads to a null integration. Thus, one is left with

\displaystyle\hbar v_{s}(t)\frac{L^{2}}{K(t)}\nu\int_{0}^{L}dx\int_{0}^{L}dz\frac{1}{z-x}[\rho_{1}(z)\rho_{-1}(x)-\rho_{1}(x)\rho_{-1}(z)].

(S67)

This Hamiltonian contributes to the CD Hamiltonian for a Lorentzian interaction in the position space. By identification with the amplitude of the CD driving term in the manuscript, one obtains

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}(t)=\frac{1}{4}\frac{\dot{\lambda}(t)R_{0}\pi v_{F}}{(\pi v_{F}+\lambda(t))^{2}}=-v_{s}(t)\frac{L^{2}}{K(t)}\nu.

(S68)

Furthermore, from the main text
v_{s}(t)/K(t)\approx 2(\pi v_{F}+\lambda)
. One can then relate the amplitude of the gauge field to the derivative of the cutoff parameter

\displaystyle\nu(t)=-\frac{1}{8L^{2}}\frac{R_{0}\dot{\lambda}}{(\pi v_{F}+\lambda)^{3}}.

(S69)

Gauge fields can be implemented in optical lattices [
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}89
,
\color[rgb]{0,0,1}90
], where the lattice spacing plays the role of the short distance cutoff.

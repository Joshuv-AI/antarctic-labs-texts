Variational Formulation of Higher-order Guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell Theory
Alain J. Brizard a

Abstract
Extended guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations are derived under the assumption of time-dependent and inhomogeneous electric and magnetic fields that obey the standard guiding-center space-time-scale orderings. The guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations are derived up to second order, which contain dipole and quadrupole contributions to the guiding-center polarization and magnetization that include finite-Larmor-radius corrections. Exact energy-momentum conservation laws are derived from the variational formulation of these higher-order guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations.



I
Introduction

The adiabatic invariance of the magnetic moment along a charged-particle orbit (with mass
m
and charge
e
) in a nonuniform magnetic field
{\bf B}=B\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
plays a crucial role in our understanding of the physical basis of the spatial confinement of a magnetized plasma over long time scales
(
Hazeltine and Meiss 2003
;
Helander 2014
)
.

In guiding-center theory
Northrop 1963
;
Cary and Brizard 2009
, the mathematical construction of the magnetic moment relies on the space-time scales
(L_{B},\omega^{-1})
of the confining magnetic field to be long compared to the characteristic gyroradius
\rho
and the gyroperiod
\Omega^{-1}=mc/eB
, respectively, leading to the small dimensionless ordering parameter
Cary and Brizard 2009

\epsilon_{B}\;\equiv\;\rho/L_{B}\;\sim\;\omega/\Omega\;\ll\;1.

(1)

The traditional derivation of guiding-center theory relies on the existence of an ordering parameter defined by the dimensional mass-to-charge ratio
m/e

Northrop 1963
;
Kruskal 1965
, so that the gyroperiod
\Omega^{-1}\propto m/e
is assumed to be the shortest time scale. For practical applications in perturbation theory
Littlejohn 1981
, however, it is convenient to replace the ratio
m/e
with
\epsilon\,m/e=(\epsilon\,m)/e=m/(\epsilon^{-1}e)
, so that the dimensionless ordering parameter

\epsilon\ll 1
can either be viewed as a
renormalization
of the particle’s mass
m\rightarrow\epsilon\,m
or the particle’s charge
e\rightarrow e/\epsilon
and it is quite common to assume
\epsilon\sim\epsilon_{B}
. It is noteworthy, however, that the guiding-center approximation is still valid when the spatial ordering
\rho/L_{B}\lesssim 1
is not extremely small (as recently demonstrated in Ref.
Brizard 2017
).

In earlier derivations of the guiding-center Vlasov equation (sometimes referred to as the drift-kinetic equation), a recursive solution of the Vlasov kinetic equation
Chew et al. 1956
;
Frieman et al. 1966
;
Hazeltine 1973
;
Kulsrud 1983
led to the drift-kinetic equation through an expansion of the Vlasov distribution function
f=f_{0}+\epsilon\,f_{1}+\epsilon^{2}\,f_{2}+\cdots
, which yielded functional solutions
f_{n}[f_{0}]
(
n\geq 1
) in terms of the lowest-order gyroangle-independent solution
f_{0}
. It was later shown that this recursive derivation is completely analogous to the Lie-transform derivation of the guiding-center Vlasov equation
Brizard and
Mishchenko 2009
in the electrostatic limit. It is precisely because of their power and simplicity that Lie-transform perturbation methods are used here to derive higher-order guiding-center equations of motion. We also note that, when Lie-transform perturbation methods are combined with variational formulations for the reduced Vlasov-Maxwell equations
Brizard 2008
, the reduced Maxwell equations naturally incorporate reduced polarization and magnetization effects while exact energy-momentum conservation laws are derived by Noether method
Brizard 2000
.

I.1
Motivation

The inclusion of nonuniform, time-dependent electric fields in guiding-center theory has a long and rich history in plasma physics
Northrop 1963
;
Cary and Brizard 2009
. In the present work, we use the standard ordering
Kulsrud 1983
for the electric field

{\bf E}={\bf E}_{\bot}+\epsilon\,E_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}
, where the parallel component
E_{\|}\rightarrow\epsilon\,E_{\|}
of the electric field is considered small compared to the perpendicular components
{\bf E}_{\bot}
. In addition, we assume that the
E\times B
velocity
c|{\bf E}_{\bot}|/B
is comparable to particle’s thermal velocity.

The ability of electric fields to fundamentally modify the magnetic geometry that confines a laboratory plasma (e.g., by creating transport barriers
Hahm 1996
;
Itoh and Itoh 1996
;
Terry 2000
;
Ida and Fujita 2018
;
Dif-Pradalier et al. 2022
or in setting up rotating mirror magnetic geometries
White et al. 2018
) motivates the need to construct a guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell theory that includes self-consistent time-dependent electric and magnetic fields, in which the transfer of energy and momentum between the electromagnetic fields and the confined plasma play a crucial role.

In addition, for many situations of practical interest, the presence of a strong electric field is associated with strong plasma flows with steep sheared rotation profiles for which second-order effects (including finite-Larmor-radius effects), which must be included in a self-consistent guiding-center theory
Hahm 1996
;
Chang et al. 2004
;
Lanthaler et al. 2019
;
Frei et al. 2020
;
Joseph 2021
;
Dif-Pradalier et al. 2022
. Guiding-center equations of motion with second-order corrections in the presence of time-independent electric and magnetic fields were derived using Lie-transform perturbation method by Brizard
Brizard 1995
and Hahm
Hahm 1996
, following the earlier work of Littlejohn
Littlejohn 1981
. More recently, these perturbation methods were also used by Miyato
et al.

Miyato et al. 2009
and Madsen
Madsen 2010
, who derived self-consistent guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations that included guiding-center polarization and magnetization effects. Not all second-order effects were included in these models, however, and it is the purpose of the present work to derive a more complete higher-order guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell theory, based on the Lie-transform perturbation derivation of higher-order guiding-center Lagrangian dynamics
Brizard 2023
for the case of time-dependent, nonuniform electric and magnetic fields that satisfy the guiding-center ordering (
1
).

We note that a key difference between guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell models considered here and gyrokinetic Vlasov-Maxwell models considered elsewhere (see Ref.
Brizard and Hahm 2007
for a review), is that the electromagnetic fields
({\bf E},{\bf B})
considered here are not separated into time-independent weakly-nonuniform background fields and time-dependent fluctuating (i.e., turbulent) fields that may possibly have short spatial scales (satisfying the gyrokinetic ordering
Brizard and Hahm 2007
).
Hence, the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell energy and momentum are exactly conserved despite the fact that guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell fields are time-dependent and nonuniform, with space-time scales (
1
) that satisfy the guiding-center orderings
Cary and Brizard 2009
.

I.2
The need for higher-order guiding-center theory

The previous variational derivations of a self-consistent guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell model have been carried out up to first order in the dimensionless ordering parameter
\epsilon

Pfirsch 1984
;
Pfirsch and Morrison 1985
;
Ye and Kaufman 1992
;
Brizard and Tronci 2016
;
Sugama et al. 2016
;
Brizard 2021
. In Ref.
Brizard 2021
, for example, the Hamiltonian structure of the first-order guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations was given in terms of a guiding-center Hamiltonian functional and a functional bracket that satisfies the Jacobi property. The variational and Hamiltonian structures of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations may prove useful in the implementation of structure-preserving numerical algorithms
Ellison et al. 2015
;
Morrison 2017
;
Kraus et al. 2017
;
Xiao et al. 2018
;
Ellison et al. 2018
.

Recently, second-order terms in guiding-center Hamiltonian theory (in the absence of an electric field) were shown to be crucial
Brizard and Hodgeman 2023
in assessing the validity of the guiding-center representation in determining whether guiding-center orbits were numerically faithful to the particle orbits in axisymmetric magnetic geometries, which partially confirmed earlier numerical studies in axisymmetric tokamak plasmas
Belova et al. 2003
. In particular, it was shown that a second-order correction associated with guiding-center polarization
Kaufman 1986
;
Brizard 2013
;
Tronko and Brizard 2015
was needed in order to obtain faithful guiding-center orbits.

Indeed, without the inclusion of second-order effects, it was shown that, within a few bounce periods after leaving the same physical point in particle phase space, a first-order guiding-center orbit deviated noticeably from its associated particle orbit, while a second-order guiding-center orbit followed the particle orbit to a high degree of precision
Brizard and Hodgeman 2023
. In addition, as initially reported by Belova
et al.

Belova et al. 2003
, the guiding-center Hamiltonian formulation
Tronko and Brizard 2015
;
Brizard and Hodgeman 2023
is a faithful representation of the particle toroidal angular momentum, which is an exact particle constant of motion in an axisymmetric magnetic field, only if second-order effects are included. See additional comments included in Sec.
II.3
regarding the faithfulness of the guiding-center representation.

From a purely theoretical point of view, it is therefore interesting to derive higher-order guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations with accurate expressions for the guiding-center polarization and magnetization, which include finite-Larmor-radius (FLR) corrections. Through the application of the Noether method
Brizard 2000
, we will also be able to explore how these higher-order effects modify the guiding-center energy-momentum conservation laws, e.g., how the Chew-Goldberger-Low (CGL) pressure tensor
Chew et al. 1956
;
Frieman et al. 1966
;
Hazeltine 1973
;
Kulsrud 1983
is modifed [see Eq. (
99
)].

I.3
Organization

The remainder of the present work is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, the extended guiding-center Hamilton equations of motion are derived in terms of the extended guiding-center Hamiltonian and the extended guiding-center Poisson bracket (derived by Lie-transform perturbation method in Ref.
Brizard 2023
), while the higher-order guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations are derived from an Eulerian variational principle
Brizard 2000
in Sec.
III
, from which guiding-center polarization and magnetization are derived with FLR corrections. Here, our guiding-center variational principle expressly imposes plasma quasineutrality by omitting the electric energy density from the guiding-center Lagrangian density
Newcomb 1962
;
Grad 1966
;
Scott and Smirnov 2010
;
Tronko and Chandre 2018
;
McMillan 2023
, which also removes the displacement current
(c^{-1}\partial{\bf E}/\partial t)
from Maxwell’s equation.

In Sec.
IV
, the exact energy-momentum conservation laws are derived from the guiding-center Noether equation obtained from the guiding-center Eulerian variational principle. The symmetry properties of the guiding-center stress tensor are also briefly discussed, and the guiding-center angular-momentum conservation law is derived at the lowest order, while a more extensive discussion of the asymmetry of the guiding-center stress tensor at higher order is left for future work.

Finally, in App.
A
, we present a brief discussion of the concept of gyrogauge invariance
Littlejohn 1981
;
Littlejohn 1983
;
Littlejohn 1988
, which states that our guiding-center equations of motion should not only be independent of the gyroangle but also how the angle is measured. Gyrogauge terms appear at second order in the guiding-center perturbation analysis and were shown to be crucial in previous works
Brizard and Hodgeman 2023
;
Belova et al. 2003
. In App.
B
, we show how the standard linear finite-beta electromagnetic gyrokinetic equations
Brizard and Hahm 2007
form a subset of our guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations in the limit of a uniform background magnetized plasma. In particular, we show how well-known spurious high-frequency modes found in electrostatic gyrokinetic models
Lee 1983
;
Dubin et al. 1983
;
Lee 1987
;
Krommes 1993
disappear when electromagnetic effects are included.

while the Vlasov contribution in Eq. (

) vanishes because of the constraint (
29
).

IV.2
Exact guiding-center energy-momentum conservation laws

By considering a virtual time displacement
\delta t
in the guiding-center Noether equation (
77
), we obtain the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell energy conservation law

\partial{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm gc}/\partial t\;+\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf S}_{\rm gc}\;=\;0,

(83)

which is expressed in terms of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell energy density
{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm gc}\equiv-\;\delta{\mathcal{N}}_{\rm gc}/\delta t-{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm M}
and the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell energy density-flux
{\bf S}_{\rm gc}\equiv-\,\delta{\bf F}_{\rm gc}/\delta t
, where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,K_{\rm gc}+{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}\;+\;|{\bf B}|^{2}/8\pi,

(84)

and

\displaystyle{\bf S}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,K_{\rm gc}\,\dot{\bf X}\;+\;\frac{c\,{\bf E}}{4\pi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf H}_{\rm gc}

(85)

\displaystyle+\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\;\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{\partial\bf E}{\partial t}\;+\;\frac{{\bf u}_{\rm E}}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\partial\bf B}{\partial t}\right)

\displaystyle-\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\left(\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf B}{\partial t}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{1}{c}\left(\dot{\bf X}-{\bf u}_{\rm E}\right),

which includes FLR corrections involving
\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc}
. We note that, in the case of static electric and magnetic fields, only the terms on the first line on the right side of Eq. (
85
) remain.

Next, by considering a virtual spatial displacement
\delta X^{k}
in the guiding-center Noether equation (
77
), we obtain the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell momentum conservation law

\partial P_{{\rm gc}k}/\partial t\;+\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf T}_{{\rm gc}k}\;=\;0

(86)

in the
X^{k}
-direction is expressed in terms of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell momentum density

\mbox{\boldmath$\sf P$}_{\rm gc}\;=\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}\;+\;\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{1}{c}\,{\bf B},

(87)

where
P_{{\rm gc}k}\equiv\delta{\mathcal{N}}_{\rm gc}/\delta X^{k}=\mbox{\boldmath$\sf P$}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\partial_{k}{\bf X}
, and the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell momentum-density flux
T_{{\rm gc}k}^{i}\equiv\delta F_{\rm gc}^{i}/\delta X^{k}+\delta^{i}_{k}\;{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm M}
, where

\displaystyle{\bf T}_{{\rm gc}k}\;\equiv\;\mathbb{T}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\partial_{k}{\bf X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,\dot{\bf X}\,\Pi_{{\rm gc}k}\;-\;\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}\,E_{k}+\frac{\bf B}{4\pi}\,{\sf H}_{{\rm gc}k}\right)\;+\;\frac{1}{4\pi}\partial_{k}{\bf X}\left({\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf H}_{\rm gc}\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\,|{\bf B}|^{2}\right)

(88)

\displaystyle-\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\left[\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\partial_{k}{\bf E}+\frac{{\bf u}_{\rm E}}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\partial_{k}{\bf B}\right)\;-\;\left(\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\partial_{k}{\bf B}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{1}{c}\left(\dot{\bf X}-{\bf u}_{\rm E}\right)\right],

with the notation
\partial_{k}\equiv(\partial{\bf X}/\partial X^{k})\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla
. We note that guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell stress tensor
\mathbb{T}_{\rm gc}
defined in Eq. (
88
) is manifestly not symmetric, which is quite common in reduced Vlasov-Maxwell systems
Pfirsch 1984
;
Pfirsch and Morrison 1985
. The symmetry properties of the lowest-order guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell stress tensor are briefly discussed in Sec.
IV.3
.

The proofs of the energy-momentum conservation laws (
83
) and (
86
) proceed by taking the partial time derivative of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell energy density (
84
) and the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell momentum density (
87
) which, after substituting the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations (
47
) and (
68
)-(
69
), yield

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm gc}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,K_{\rm gc}\,\dot{\bf X}+\frac{c\,{\bf E}}{4\pi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf H}_{\rm gc}\right)\;+\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\left(\frac{dK_{\rm gc}}{dt}\;-\;e\,{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf X}\right)\;+\;\frac{\partial\bf E}{\partial t}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}\;+\;\frac{\partial\bf B}{\partial t}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{M}$}_{\rm gc},

(89)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial P_{{\rm gc}k}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left[\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\dot{\bf X}\,\Pi_{{\rm gc}k}-\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}\,E_{k}+\frac{\bf B}{4\pi}\,{\sf H}_{{\rm gc}k}\right)\right]-\;\partial_{k}\left(\frac{\bf B}{4\pi}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf H}_{\rm gc}-\frac{|{\bf B}|^{2}}{8\pi}\right)

(90)

\displaystyle+\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\left[\frac{d\Pi_{{\rm gc}k}}{dt}\;-\;\partial_{k}{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(e\,{\bf E}+\frac{e}{c}\,\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf B}\right)\right]\;-\;\left(\partial_{k}{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}\;+\frac{}{}\partial_{k}{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{M}$}_{\rm gc}\right).

The last steps in deriving the energy-momentum conservation laws (
83
) and (
86
) involve using the guiding-center Euler-Lagrange equation (
23
) and substituting the expressions (
54
) and (
60
) for the guiding-center polarization and magnetization, respectively.

Finally, we note that the definitions (
84
)-(
85
) and (
87
)-(
88
) associated with the guiding-center energy and momentum conservation laws (
83
) and (
86
), respectively, are not uniquely defined. Indeed, under the following transformations
{\mathcal{E}}_{\rm gc}^{\prime}={\mathcal{E}}_{\rm gc}+\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf C}
and
{\bf S}_{\rm gc}^{\prime}={\bf S}_{\rm gc}-\partial{\bf C}/\partial t+\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf K}
, the guiding-center energy conservation law (
83
) remains invariant, where the fields
({\bf C},{\bf K})
are arbitrary. Likewise, under the transformations
P_{{\rm gc}k}^{\prime}=P_{{\rm gc}k}+\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf G}_{k}
and
{\bf T}_{{\rm gc}k}^{\prime}={\bf T}_{{\rm gc}k}-\partial{\bf G}_{k}/\partial t+\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mathbb{K}_{k}
, the guiding-center momentum conservation law (
86
) remains invariant, where the fields
{\bf G}_{k}\equiv\mathbb{G}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\partial_{k}{\bf X}
is defined in terms of an arbitrary second-rank tensor
\mathbb{G}
, while the third-rank tensor
\mathbb{K}
has the following antisymmetry property:
{\sf K}^{ji}_{k}=-\,{\sf K}_{k}^{ij}
(so that
\partial_{ij}^{2}{\sf K}_{k}^{ij}\equiv 0
). Here, we note that the vector fields
{\bf C}\equiv\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf E}
and
{\bf G}_{k}\equiv\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,J\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\partial_{k}{\bf X}\equiv\mathbb{G}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\partial_{k}{\bf X}
completely remove the FLR corrections in the definitions (
84
) and (
87
).

IV.3
Symmetry properties of the guiding-center stress tensor

We now make a few remarks about the symmetry properties of the guiding-center stress tensor (
88
). These symmetry properties are most relevant when considering the conservation law of guiding-center toroidal angular momentum, where
the guiding-center toroidal angular momentum density

P_{{\rm gc}\phi}\;\equiv\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\;\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\phi}+\frac{1}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\phi}

(91)

is defined as the covariant component of the guiding-center momentum density
\mbox{\boldmath$\sf P$}_{\rm gc}
associated with the toroidal angle
\phi
.

First, we derive the guiding-center angular momentum transport equation from the guiding-center momentum conservation law

\displaystyle\frac{\partial P_{{\rm gc}\phi}}{\partial t}\;+\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mathbb{T}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\phi}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{\rm gc}^{\top}:\nabla\left(\frac{\partial\bf X}{\partial\phi}\right)

(92)

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{T}$}_{\rm gc},

where
\mathbb{T}_{\rm gc}^{\top}
denotes the transpose of
\mathbb{T}_{\rm gc}
. Since the dyadic tensor
\nabla(\partial{\bf X}/\partial\phi)
is anti-symmetric, with the rotation axis directed along the
{\sf z}
-axis, the anti-symmetric part of the guiding-center stress tensor (
88
) generates the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell torque
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{T}$}_{\rm gc}
. We note that this equation can also be derived by substituting the virtual displacement
\delta{\bf X}=\delta\phi\;\partial{\bf X}/\partial\phi
in
Eq. (
77
).

At the lowest order (i.e., keeping terms only up to gyrogauge corrections), the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell torque is defined as
\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{T}$}_{\rm gc}=\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\;\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_{\rm gc}
, where, using the dyadic identity (for two arbitrary vector fields

{\bf F}
and
{\bf G}
)

{\bf F}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla(\partial{\bf X}/\partial\phi)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf G}\;=\;\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,({\bf F}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf G}),

we find

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}\;+\;{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}

(93)

\displaystyle+\;{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{\rm gc}+\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf P}_{0}/mc\right),

which is calculated below in the dipole approximation. First, each term is expressed as

\displaystyle\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\Pi$}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(P_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;+\;{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{e\widehat{{\sf b}}}{\Omega}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{\|}\,\left(\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}+\frac{c{\bf E}}{B}\right)-\left(\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{e{\bf E}}{\Omega}\right)\widehat{{\sf b}},

\displaystyle{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left[\frac{e\widehat{{\sf b}}}{\Omega}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\dot{\bf X}-{\bf u}_{\rm E}\right)\right]=\left(\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\frac{e{\bf E}}{\Omega}\right)\widehat{{\sf b}},

and

\displaystyle{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mu$}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\mu\left(-\,\widehat{{\sf b}}+\frac{1}{\Omega}\frac{d\widehat{{\sf b}}}{dt}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)=J\,\frac{d\widehat{{\sf b}}}{dt},

\displaystyle{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{{\bf P}_{0}}{mc}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{\|}\;\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\left(\dot{\bf X}-{\bf u}_{\rm E}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{\|}\,\left(\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\dot{\bf X}\;-\;\frac{c{\bf E}}{B}\right),

where we used
{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}_{\rm gc}=0
. Next, by combining these terms in Eq. (
93
), several cancellations occur and the lowest-order torque density is

\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}_{\rm gc}\;=\;J\,\frac{d\widehat{{\sf b}}}{dt}\;=\;J\left(\frac{\partial\widehat{{\sf b}}}{\partial t}\;+\;\dot{\bf X}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\right),

(94)

which recovers a result derived by Ye and Kaufman
Ye and Kaufman 1992
. Hence, substituting Eq. (
94
) into the right side of Eq. (
92
), we obtain

\widehat{\sf z}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{T}$}_{\rm gc}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\;J\,b_{z}\right)+\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\left(\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\;\dot{\bf X}\;J\,b_{z}\right),

(95)

which adds a gyrogauge-independent contribution

-\,J\,b_{z}\;\equiv\;-\,J{\bf R}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\partial{\bf X}/\partial\phi

(96)

to the guiding-center toroidal angular momentum density (
91
). Hence, we note that, in the presence of an axisymmetric magnetic field, we can use the definition (
5
) and the identity
{\bf B}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\partial{\bf X}/\partial\phi\equiv-\,\nabla A_{\phi}
, the guiding-center toroidal angular momentum density (
91
) can then be written as

\displaystyle P_{{\rm gc}\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\left(P_{\phi}-\frac{e}{c}\,A_{\phi}+J\,b_{z}\right)\;-\;\frac{1}{c}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\nabla A_{\phi}

(97)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\left(P_{\phi}\;+\frac{}{}J\,b_{z}\right)-\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\frac{1}{c}\,A_{\phi}\;\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}\right),

where we used the definition (
26
) for the guiding-center canonical toroidal angular momentum and the guiding-center quasineutrality condition (
68
). By using the guiding-center torque correction (
95
) and the transformation
P_{{\rm gc}\phi}^{\prime}=P_{{\rm gc}\phi}+\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf G}_{\phi}
, where
{\bf G}_{\phi}=(A_{\phi}/c)\,\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}
, we obtain the exact guiding-center toroidal angular momentum conservation law

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\;P_{\phi}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\;\dot{\bf X}\,P_{\phi}\right)\;+\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\;\dot{P}_{\phi}

(98)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\;\dot{\bf X}\,P_{\phi}\right),

which follows from applying the Noether theorem to the guiding-center Euler-Lagrange equation
dP_{\phi}/dt=\partial L_{\rm gc}/\partial\phi\equiv 0
associated with magnetic axisymmetry.

Finally, returning to the case of a general magnetic field, and omitting the gyrogauge terms and FLR corrections, the guiding-center stress tensor (
88
) can be expressed in symmetric form as

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\rm CGL}+\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\,P_{\|}\left(\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\dot{\bf X}_{\bot}\;+\;\dot{\bf X}_{\bot}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)

(99)

\displaystyle+\;\left(\frac{|{\bf B}|^{2}}{8\pi}\,\mathbb{I}-\frac{{\bf B}\,{\bf B}}{4\pi}\right)+\chi_{\rm gc}\;({\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}})\,({\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}})

\displaystyle+\left(\mathbb{I}-\widehat{{\sf b}}\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf E}+\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\;{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}-\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc}\;{\bf E},

where
\mathbb{P}_{\rm CGL}
denotes the Chew-Goldberger-Low (CGL) pressure tensor
Chew et al. 1956

\mathbb{P}_{\rm CGL}\;=\;\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}\left[\frac{P_{\|}^{2}}{m}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\widehat{{\sf b}}+\left(\mathbb{I}-\widehat{{\sf b}}\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\;\mu B\right],

which appears naturally in lowest-order guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell theory
(
Cary and Brizard 2009
)
and several kinetic-magnetohydrodynamic models
Frieman et al. 1966
;
Kulsrud 1983
;
Brizard 2018
, while
\chi_{\rm gc}\equiv\int_{P}F_{\rm gc}(mc^{2}/B^{2})
denotes the guiding-center electric susceptibility
Northrop 1963
. The remaining terms in Eq. (
99
) involve the off-diagonal terms
P_{\|}(\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\dot{\bf X}_{\bot}+\dot{\bf X}_{\bot}\,\widehat{{\sf b}})
, which have appeared in previous works
Sugama et al. 2016
;
Brizard and Tronci 2016
, and contributions from the guiding-center polarization (
54
). Future work will explore the symmetry properties of the remaining higher-order terms in the guiding-center stress tensor defined in Eq. (
88
).

V
Summary

The results of the Lie-transform analysis leading to higher-order guiding-center Hamiltonian dynamics yield the guiding-center Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J\left(\Omega+{\mathcal{S}}\right)\;+\;|{\bf P}_{0}|^{2}/2m

(100)

\displaystyle+\;e\,\Phi\;-\;\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\mathbb{Q}_{\rm gc}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf E}\right)

and the guiding-center Lagrangian

\displaystyle L_{\rm gc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{e}{c}{\bf A}\;+\;{\bf P}_{0}\;-\;J\left({\bf R}+\frac{1}{2}\,\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}\right)\right]\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\dot{\bf X}

(101)

\displaystyle+\;J\;\dot{\theta}\;-\;H_{\rm gc},

where
{\bf P}_{0}\equiv P_{\|}\,\widehat{{\sf b}}+{\bf E}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,e\widehat{{\sf b}}/\Omega
and the symmetric dyadic tensor (
53
) generates a finite-Larmor-radius (FLR) correction to the electrostatic potential energy
e\,\Phi
in Eq. (
100
). The presence of the gyrogauge fields
({\mathcal{S}},{\bf R})
guarantees that the guiding-center Hamiltonian dynamics derived from the guiding-center Lagrangian (
101
) are gyrogauge invariant (i.e., these equations are not only gyroangle-independent, but they are also independent on how the gyroangle is measured).

Next, the explicit dependence of the the guiding-center Lagrangian (
101
) on the electric and magnetic fields
({\bf E},{\bf B})
yields guiding-center polarization and magnetization effects, represented by the vector fields
(\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{P}$}_{\rm gc},\mbox{\boldmath$\mathcal{M}$}_{\rm gc})
, in the guiding-center Maxwell equations (
68
)-(
69
). The guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations are, then, derived by an Eulerian variational principle from which exact energy-momentum conservation laws are derived. Here, the guiding-center variational principle (
27
) omitted the electric energy density from the guiding-center Lagrangian density, which explicitly guaranteed quasineutrality (
68
) and removed the displacement current density from the guiding-center Maxwell equation (
69
). In particular, the explicit guiding-center quasineutrality condition removes spurious high-frequency plasma oscillations that violate the guiding-center ordering
\omega\ll\Omega
, while additional spurious high-frequency modes are discussed in App.
B
.

Future work will explore the the invariance properties of the energy-momentum conservation laws as well as the symmetry properties of the guiding-center stress tensor
\mathbb{T}_{\rm gc}
defined in Eq. (
88
). In addition, the Hamiltonian formulation of the guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell equations will be constructed that generalizes the work of Brizard and Tronci
Brizard and Tronci 2016
.

Appendix A
Time-dependent Gyrogauge Invariance

By introducing the fixed unit-vectors
(\widehat{\sf 1},\widehat{\sf 2},\widehat{{\sf b}}\equiv\widehat{\sf 1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\widehat{\sf 2})
, we write the definitions for the rotating unit-vectors

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcl}\widehat{\rho}&\equiv&\cos\theta\;\widehat{\sf 1}\;-\;\sin\theta\;\widehat{\sf 2}\\
&&\\
\widehat{\bot}&\equiv&-\;\sin\theta\;\widehat{\sf 1}\;-\;\cos\theta\;\widehat{\sf 2}\end{array}\right\},

(102)

where the gyroangle
\theta
is measured
clockwise
from the
\widehat{\sf 1}
-axis (for a positively-charged particle), so that
\widehat{\bot}\equiv\partial\widehat{\rho}/\partial\theta
. We note that, while the choice of the fixed unit-vectors
(\widehat{\sf 1},\widehat{\sf 2})
can be made arbitrarily in the plane locally perpendicular to
\widehat{{\sf b}}
, we must ensure that the resulting guiding-center equations of motion do not depend on a specific choice. Hence, our guiding-center Hamiltonian theory must be
gyrogauge
-invariant in the following sense.

First, we allow the rotation of the unit-vectors
(\widehat{\sf 1},\widehat{\sf 2})
about the magnetic unit-vector
\widehat{{\sf b}}
by an arbitrary angle
\psi({\bf x},t)
that depends on the field position
{\bf x}
at time
t
, so that

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{\sf 1}^{\prime}\\
\widehat{\sf 2}^{\prime}\end{array}\right)\;=\;\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\psi&\sin\psi\\
-\sin\psi&\cos\psi\end{array}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{\sf 1}\\
\widehat{\sf 2}\end{array}\right).

(103)

Second we require that the rotating unit-vectors (
102
) be invariant under this rotation, i.e.,
\widehat{\rho}^{\prime}=\widehat{\rho}
and
\widehat{\bot}^{\prime}=\widehat{\bot}
, which implies that the gyroangle
\theta
must transform as

\theta^{\prime}(\theta,{\bf x},t)\;=\;\theta+\psi({\bf x},t)

(104)

under the gyrogauge rotation (
103
).

Third, we introduce the gyrogauge vector field

{\bf R}\;\equiv\;\nabla\widehat{\bot}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\rho}\;=\;\nabla\widehat{\sf 1}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf 2},

(105)

and the gyrogauge scalar field

{\mathcal{S}}\;\equiv\;\frac{\partial\widehat{\bot}}{\partial t}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\rho}\;=\;\frac{\partial\widehat{\sf 1}}{\partial t}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf 2},

(106)

which transform as
{\bf R}^{\prime}={\bf R}+\nabla\psi
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{S}}+\partial\psi/\partial t
under the gyrogauge rotation (
103
). We, therefore, readily see that a gyrogauge-invariant guiding-center theory can only include the gyrogauge fields
({\bf R},{\mathcal{S}})
either as the one-form
{\sf d}\theta-{\bf R}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\sf d}{\bf x}-{\mathcal{S}}\,{\sf d}t
, the gradient operator
\nabla+{\bf R}\;\partial/\partial\theta
, or the partial time derivative
\partial/\partial t+{\mathcal{S}}\,\partial/\partial\theta
, which are all gyrogauge invariant. Finally, we note that the vector fields
Littlejohn 1981

\displaystyle\nabla\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,{\bf R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{1}{2}\,\epsilon_{ijk}\,b^{i}\;\nabla b^{j}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\nabla b^{k},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\bf R}{\partial t}-\nabla{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\;\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\partial\widehat{{\sf b}}}{\partial t}\right)

are manifestly gyrogauge independent, since they are expressed entirely in terms of
\widehat{{\sf b}}
,
\nabla\widehat{{\sf b}}
, and
\partial\widehat{{\sf b}}/\partial t
.

Finally, we note that since the magnetic unit vector
\widehat{{\sf b}}\equiv\partial{\bf x}/\partial s
is defined as the rate of change of the position
{\bf x}
of a point as it moves along a magnetic-field line (with
s
defining distance along that line), we may choose the perpendicular unit vectors
\widehat{\sf 1}\equiv\kappa^{-1}\partial\widehat{{\sf b}}/\partial s
(where
\kappa
denotes the Frenet-Serret curvature
Brizard 2015
) and
\widehat{\sf 2}\equiv\kappa^{-1}\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\partial\widehat{{\sf b}}/\partial s
. Hence, the parallel component of the gyrogauge vector field

\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,{\bf R}\;=\;\frac{\partial\widehat{\sf 1}}{\partial s}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\widehat{\sf 2}\;=\;\kappa^{-2}\;\frac{\partial^{2}\widehat{{\sf b}}}{\partial s^{2}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}\,\left(\widehat{{\sf b}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$\times$}\,\frac{\partial\widehat{{\sf b}}}{\partial s}\right)\equiv\tau

(108)

is expressed in terms of the Frenet-Serret torsion
\tau
.

Appendix B
Linearized Guiding-center Vlasov-Maxwell Equations

In this Appendix, we show how the standard linear finite-beta electromagnetic gyrokinetic equations
Hahm et al. 1988

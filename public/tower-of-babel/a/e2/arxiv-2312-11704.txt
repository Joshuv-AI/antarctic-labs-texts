Ultralight dark matter in neutrino oscillations to accommodate T2K and NO 𝝂 \nu A tension
Hai-Xing Lin a, Jian Tang a, and Sampsa Vihonen b,c,a

Abstract
Ultralight dark matter with neutrino couplings is investigated in light of the long-baseline neutrino oscillation data in T2K and NO ν \nu A experiments. The observed tension between T2K and NO ν \nu A is shown to be ameliorated when ultralight dark matter of either scalar or vector form is taken into consideration. The best result is achieved with scalar dark matter which can alleviate the tension by 2.0 σ \sigma CL with flavour-universal couplings. We also consider scalar dark matter with flavour-general couplings and vector dark matter in L e − L μ L_{e}-L_{\mu} and L μ − L τ L_{\mu}-L_{\tau} cases. It is shown in all cases that the tension is relaxed by approximately 1.5 σ \sigma –2.0 σ \sigma CL while the current experimental constraints can be evaded.

2
Ultralight dark matter in neutrino oscillations

In this section, we review the theoretical framework for the scalar and vector dark matter field in the ultralight regime and discuss their phenomenological implications on the neutrino matter potential in terrestrial experiments.

2.1
Review of the formalism

When ultralight dark matter is present, neutrinos in terrestrial experiments can be seen to drift in a dark sea. Ultralight dark matter
\phi
can be found on the Earth by roughly
\rho_{\phi}\simeq
0.3 average density
Nelson:2011sf
;
Arias:2012az
. These dark matter particles move relatively slow and they can be treated as classical fields:

\phi(t)=\frac{\sqrt{2\rho_{\phi}}}{m_{\phi}}\cos{(m_{\phi}t)},

(1)

where
m_{\phi}
is the dark matter mass and
t
denotes time. At
m_{\phi}\sim
10
-22
eV, this kind of dark matter is also known as fuzzy dark matter. Neutrino propagation in the dark medium can subsequently affect the oscillation probabilities. In case of scalar dark matter, the relevant Lagrangian can be written as
Berlin:2016woy

{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm eff}^{\rm scalar}=\bar{\nu}_{L}^{\alpha}i\not{\partial}\nu_{L}^{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}m_{\nu}^{\alpha\beta}(\bar{\nu}_{L}^{c})^{\alpha}\nu_{L}^{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}y^{\alpha\beta}\phi(\bar{\nu}_{L}^{c})^{\alpha},

(2)

where
m_{\nu}
is the effective neutrino mass matrix,
\nu_{L}
is the left-handed neutrino field and
y
contains the coupling constants for neutrinos and dark matter interactions.

The scalar field in equation (
1
) can have notable implications in neutrino experiments where neutrinos propagate underground. The effective neutrino Lagrangian (
2
) corresponds to the transformation
m_{\nu}\rightarrow m_{\nu}+y\phi
, where the neutrino mass matrix
m_{\nu}
is altered by the scalar field
\phi
and neutrino–dark matter couplings
y
. When the scalar mass
m_{\phi}
matches the propagation length of the neutrinos, the coherent scattering between the dark matter and neutrino fields can lead to observable distortions in the oscillation probabilities. The time dependency of the scalar field
\phi
furthermore means that this distortion varies over time. This modulation may be testable in neutrino experiments where the lifetime is sufficiently long. The oscillation distortions and time modulation of ultralight scalar fields have previously been contemplated in
e.g.
Refs.
Losada:2021bxx
;
Losada:2023zap
.

It is also possible to write neutrino–dark matter interactions in vector form. In such case, the dark matter field alters the neutrino 4-momenta as
p_{\mu}\rightarrow p_{\mu}+gQ\phi_{\mu}
, where
\phi_{\mu}
represents the vector-like dark matter field. The corresponding version for the effective Lagrangian is then

{\mathcal{L}}_{\rm eff}^{\rm vector}=\bar{\nu}_{L}^{\alpha}i\not{\partial}\nu_{L}^{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2}m_{\nu}^{\alpha\beta}(\bar{\nu}_{L}^{c})^{\alpha}\nu_{L}^{\beta}+gQ^{\alpha\beta}\phi^{\mu}\bar{\nu}_{L}^{\alpha}\gamma_{\mu}\nu_{L}^{\beta},

(3)

where
g
stands for the effective neutrino–DM coupling and
Q^{\alpha\beta}
for the charge matrix, respectively. The ultralight dark matter field
\phi^{\mu}
on the other hand is defined as a vector with its total magnitude given by equation (
1
). Vector dark matter can also be studied via its time-varying effects as noted in Ref.
Brzeminski:2022rkf
.

2.2
Phenomenological implications

Neutrino oscillations are affected by the dark matter presence as follows. The effective Hamiltonian is given by

H_{\rm eff}=\frac{1}{2E_{\nu}}\left[U_{\nu}^{\dagger}M^{2}U_{\nu}+V_{\rm CC}\right]+V_{\rm dark},

(4)

where
U_{\nu}
is the Pontecorvo-Maki-Nakagawa-Sakata matrix,
M^{2}=\diag(0,\Delta m_{21}^{2},\Delta m_{31}^{2})
and
V_{CC}
denotes the standard matter potential from charged current (CC) interactions. Neutrino interactions with dark matter contribute the additional matter potential
V_{\rm dark}
.

Dark matter potential
V_{\rm dark}
can take several different forms according to the Lorentz structure of the interaction. In the case of scalar interactions, this potential is written as

V_{\rm dark}^{\rm scalar}=\frac{1}{2E_{\nu}}\left(\phi(ym_{\nu}+m_{\nu}y)+\phi^{2}y^{2}\right),

(5)

where
\phi^{2}y^{2}
dominates at low
m_{\phi}
values and
\phi(ym_{\nu}+m_{\nu}y)
at high
m_{\phi}
values. It can be noted that dark matter affects neutrino oscillations in two different ways, that is, time modulation and non-trivial matter effects. Time modulation arises from the classical field definition (
1
) and the strength of the modulation depends primarily on the neutrino time of flight. The matter effects on the other hand are influenced by the matter potential
V_{\rm dark}^{\rm scalar}
, which can be calculated from equation (
5
).

The couplings between neutrinos and ultralight scalar dark matter are given by the 3
\times
3 matrix
y
, which we define as follows:

y=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}y_{ee}&y_{e\mu}&y_{e\tau}\\
y_{ee}^{*}&y_{\mu\mu}&y_{\mu\tau}\\
y_{e\tau}^{*}&y_{\mu\tau}^{*}&y_{\tau\tau},\end{array}\right)

(6)

where
y_{ee}
,
y_{\mu\mu}
and
y_{\tau\tau}
are real and
y_{e\mu}
,
y_{e\tau}
and
y_{\mu\tau}
are complex parameters. The couplings between neutrinos and dark matter can follow a specific pattern, as is in the flavour-universal case where
y_{ee}=y_{\mu\mu}=y_{\tau\tau}
. The coupling parameters may also be unstructured, in which case any of the diagonal and non-diagonal parameters can acquire non-zero values. We define the off-diagonal parameters as
y_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}=|y_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}|e^{-i\phi_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}}
, where
|y_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}|
represents the magnitude and
\phi_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
the complex phase of the given coupling for which
\ell
,
\ell^{\prime}=e
,
\mu
or
\tau
.

In dark matter models involving vector-like interactions, the neutrino matter potential is written in terms of charge matrix
Q
and effective neutrino–dark matter coupling
g
:

V_{\rm dark}^{\rm vector}=-\frac{1}{2E_{\nu}}\left(2(p_{\nu}\cdot\phi)gQ+g^{2}Q^{2}\phi^{2}\right).

(7)

In principle, elements of the charge matrix
Q
can take any value. In practice, however,
Q
often has a structure that follows from the dark matter model. In this work, we are mainly interested in dark matter candidates that are charged in
L_{e}-L_{\mu}
or
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
. In the former case, charge matrix is defined as
Q=\diag(1,-1,0)
, and in the latter case it is
Q=\diag(0,1,-1)
, respectively. To enhance clarity, we denote the effective coupling in the
L_{e}-L_{\mu}
case as
g
and in the
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
case as
g^{\prime}
, respectively.

In this work, we consider ultralight scalar dark matter in the mass range
m_{\phi}=
10
-24
… 10
-10
eV. We study the effects on long-baseline neutrino oscillation data in both flavour-universal model and also with flavour-general couplings. We also investigate the effects of ultralight vector dark matter fields in
L_{e}-L_{\mu}
and
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
cases using similar mass range.

Analysis of the long-baseline neutrino oscillation data

In the present work, we investigate two different data sets from on-going long-baseline neutrino oscillation experiments. The first data set belongs to T2K experiment
T2K:2023mcm
which is currently taking data in Japan. The second data set considered in this work is taken by NO
\nu

\nu

3.1
T2K experiment

Tokai-to-Kamioka (T2K) is a long-baseline accelerator neutrino experiment currently taking data in Japan. T2K operates a proton linac with 770 kW average power output. The accelerator produces a high-purity neutrino beam of which 96%-98% are either muon neutrinos or muon antineutrinos. The experiment also has three detector facilities. The main detector, SuperKamiokande, is a large Water Cherenkov vessel located 295 km from the accelerator with 2.5
∘
off-axis angle. The other two detectors, ND280 and INGRID, are placed near the accelerator to monitor the neutrino beam and constrain its beam-related systematic uncertainties. T2K produces a narrow-band beam, which peaks at about 600 MeV in the far detector facility. The main goal of T2K experiment is to find evidence of charge-parity violation or conservation and improve measurements on other neutrino oscillation parameters and neutrino-nucleus cross-sections.

We analyze the neutrino oscillation data from T2K experiment using the latest dataset reported in Ref.
T2K:2023mcm
. The data set accounts neutrino events from 1.97
\times
10
21
protons-on-target (POT) in neutrino mode and 1.63
\times
10
21
POT in antineutrino mode, respectively. The data comprises of five interaction samples, that is, charge-current quasi-elastic (CCQE)
\nu_{\mu}
and
\nu_{e}
events obtained in neutrino mode, CCQE
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}
and
\bar{\nu}_{e}
in antineutrino mode, and resonant charged-current pion production (CC1
\pi
)
\nu_{e}
events identified in neutrino mode. The neutrino and antineutrino events belonging to these samples correspond to oscillation channels
\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{e}
,
\nu_{\mu}\rightarrow\nu_{\mu}
,
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\rightarrow\bar{\nu}_{e}
and
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\rightarrow\bar{\nu}_{\mu}

\nu_{\mu}
and

\nu_{e}
and

\nu_{e}
CC1

3.3
Data analysis

The analysis of long-baseline neutrino oscillation data is described in this section. The analysis of the T2K and NO
\nu
A data sets is done with a modified version of the General Long-Baseline Experiment Simulator (GLoBES)
Huber:2004ka
;
Huber:2007ji
. We have altered GLoBES to calculate oscillation probabilities with scalar and vector dark matter parameters discussed in section
2.2
. The full description of the analysis methods can be found in Ref.
Lin:2021cst
.

GLoBES calculates neutrino events by integrating fluxes, cross-sections and oscillation probabilities over neutrino energies:

N_{i}=N_{\rm nucl}\,T\,\epsilon\int_{E_{\rm min}}^{E_{\rm max}}\int_{E_{\rm min}^{{}^{\prime}}}^{E_{\rm max}^{{}^{\prime}}}dE\,dE^{{}^{\prime}}\,\phi(E)\,\sigma(E)\,R(E,E^{{}^{\prime}})\,P_{\nu_{\ell}\rightarrow\nu_{\ell^{\prime}}}(E),

(8)

where the indexing
i=
1, 2, 3… represents neutrino energy bins. Here
\phi(E)
stands for the neutrino flux,
\sigma(E)
for neutrino-nucleus cross-sections and
R(E,E^{{}^{\prime}})
for energy resolution function, respectively. The double integral is carried out for the reconstructed neutrino energy
E^{\prime}
and true neutrino energy
E
. The oscillation probabilities
P_{\nu_{\ell}\rightarrow\nu_{\ell^{\prime}}}(E)
are computed as function of true neutrino energy including matter effects and neutrino–dark matter interactions. The result is multiplied by
N_{\rm nucl}
,
T
and
\epsilon
, which represent the number of nucleons in the detector, total lifetime of the experiment and efficiency of the detector, respectively.

The analysis of the T2K and NO
\nu
A data is conducted with the following formula:

\chi^{2}=\sum_{i}2\left[T_{i}-O_{i}\left(1+\log\frac{O_{i}}{T_{i}}\right)\right]+\frac{\zeta_{\text{sg}}^{2}}{\sigma_{\zeta_{\text{sg}}}^{2}}+\frac{\zeta_{\text{bg}}^{2}}{\sigma_{\zeta_{\text{bg}}}^{2}}+{\rm priors},

(9)

where
T_{i}
and
O_{i}
are the theoretically predicted and observed events,
\zeta_{\text{sg}}
and
\zeta_{\text{bg}}
are the nuisance parameters representing systematic errors for the signal and background normalization, respectively. The corresponding
1\,\sigma
CL uncertainties are given by
\sigma_{\zeta_{\text{sg}}}
and
\sigma_{\zeta_{\text{bg}}}
. The predicted events
T_{i}
are obtained from equation (
8
) as
T_{i}=(1+\zeta_{\text{sg}})N_{i}^{\text{sg}}+(1+\zeta_{\text{bg}})N_{i}^{\text{bg}}
, where
N_{i}^{\text{sg}}
and
N_{i}^{\text{bg}}
denote signal and background events. We adopt 5% signal and 10% background errors for the analysis of T2K data and 5% errors for signal and background events in NO
\nu
A data.

\nu

\theta_{12}
and
\Delta m_{21}
at 33.41

10

;

by demanding that
\sin^{2}2\theta_{13}=
0.0857

\sigma

\chi^{2}

\theta_{23}
and
\Delta m_{31}^{2}
and also the Dirac

\delta_{CP}

4.3
Ultralight vector dark matter with
L_{e}-L_{\mu}
and
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}

Ultralight dark matter may also couple with active neutrinos via vector interactions. In such case, the effect on neutrino oscillations are affected by dark matter polarization, which is not present in the scalar case. Ultralight vector dark matter may therefore be studied in neutrino oscillation experiments via daily modulation
Brzeminski:2022rkf
. As the Earth’s rotation changes the angle at which neutrino and vector dark matter may collide, the subsequent effect on neutrino matter potential depends also on the time at which the interaction occurs. In this work, we consider vector dark matter interactions in both cases where the temporal information of neutrino events is available (polarized dark matter) and where time-dependence is averaged over (unpolarized dark matter). We begin by analysing vector dark matter scenario where the model is charged under
L_{e}-L_{\mu}
.

Constraints to ultralight vector dark matter with neutrino interactions have been derived in several references. Leading constraints come from black hole superradiance
Baryakhtar:2017ngi
and atmospheric neutrinos
Brzeminski:2022rkf
. Other sources for experimental constraints include fifth force searches
Wise:2018rnb
;
Coloma:2020gfv
, cosmological bounds on neutrino decay
Chen:2022idm
and violation of equivalence principle tests
Schlamminger:2007ht
. There is also a model-dependent constraint from searches for mono-leptons and missing energy at the Large Hadron Collider
Ekhterachian:2021rkx
. These constraints have been collected in Ref.
Brzeminski:2022rkf
and are reproduced in the present work.

5
Conclusions

In the present work, effects of ultralight dark matter were investigated in scalar and vector cases using the latest long-baseline neutrino oscillation data. We analysed the data sets from T2K and NO
\nu
A experiments by considering several realizations of neutrino–dark matter couplings. In the scalar case, we studied both flavour-universal and flavour-general couplings. In the vector case, we examined dark matter models with
L_{e}-L_{\mu}
and
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
couplings. Our analysis shows that the tension that is present in the T2K and NO
\nu
A data sets can be alleviated in all considered ultralight dark matter models. In all of the considered dark matter scenarios, the quality of the
\chi^{2}
fit improved by about 1.5–2.0
\sigma
CL with respect to the standard three-neutrino oscillation probabilities.

In the flavour-universal case, we found the scalar dark matter with
m_{\phi}\simeq
1.7
\times
10
-16
eV and universal coupling strength
y_{0}\simeq
1.0
\times
10
-18
the most favourable in light of the T2K and NO
\nu
A data. In this case, the tension between the T2K and NO
\nu
A data sets was alleviated by about 2.0
\sigma
CL. We also found several local minima along the line
y_{0}/m_{\phi}\simeq
0.006 where similar improvements to the
\chi^{2}
fit were obtained. The parameter values that are allowed by T2K and NO
\nu
A data are also in agreement with the current constraints on the flavour-universal scalar dark matter model, where the leading constraint is provided by cosmic microwave background measurements. We furthermore investigated the impact of each coupling parameter
y_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}
(
\ell
,
\ell^{\prime}=e
,
\mu
and
\tau
) to the T2K and NO
\nu
A discrepancy for scalar dark matter mass
m_{\phi}=
10
-22
eV. Coupling parameters
y_{\mu\tau}
and
y_{\tau\tau}
were found to give the most significant improvement to the T2K and NO
\nu
A tension with about 2.0
\sigma
and 1.8
\sigma
CL improvements, respectively. The inclusion of scalar couplings also led to changes in the best-fit values of
\sin^{2}\theta_{23}
and
\delta_{CP}
in the T2K and NO
\nu
A data sets. In this regard, we obtained corrected fits for
\sin^{2}\theta_{23}
and
\delta_{CP}
measurements for the combined analysis of T2K and NO
\nu
A data.

In the
L_{e}-L_{\mu}
and
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
cases, ultralight vector dark matter was also found to bring moderate improvements to the tension between the T2K and NO
\nu
A data sets. In both charge matrix structures, we found the tension in neutrino oscillation data to improve by 1.8
\sigma
and 1.7
\sigma
CL, respectively. This result was achieved with dark matter mass
m_{\phi}\simeq
1.0
\times
10
-19
eV and coupling parameter
g\simeq
1.0
\times
10
-32
in the
L_{e}-L_{\mu}
case and with
m_{\phi}\simeq
2.5
\times
10
-14
eV and
g^{\prime}\simeq
6.3
\times
10
-27
in the
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
case. The best-fit values were acquired assuming fully polarized dark matter, where neutrino–dark matter interactions have maximal impact on the neutrino matter potential. It was also found that the best-fit values for the
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
case are able to evade all prior experimental constraints, whereas the best-fit case for
L_{e}-L_{\mu}
is ruled out by constraints from black hole superradiance. We find black hole superradiance limit to bring the most significant constraints to the parameter space that is allowed by the combined fit to T2K and NO
\nu
A data. We also found notable parts in the relevant parameter spaces where the values allowed by T2K and NO
\nu
A survive all external constraints.

Constraints of the maximum mass of quark stars based on post-merger evolutions
Yurui Zhou, Chen Zhang, Junjie Zhao, Kenta Kiuchi, Sho Fujibayashi, Enping Zhou

Abstract
We semi-analytically investigate the post-merger evolution of the binary quark star merger. The effective-one-body method is employed to estimate the energy and angular momentum dissipation due to gravitational waves in the inspiral phase. Three major mechanisms of energy and angular momentum dissipation are considered in the post-merger phase: mass outflows, neutrinos, and gravitational waves. The proportion of each mechanism could be determined by baryon number, energy and angular momentum conservation laws as well as the equilibrium model for rotating quark stars. Applying this analysis to the GW170817 event suggests two important conclusions: 1) a remnant quark star whose mass is smaller than the maximum mass of a uniformly rotating quark star can collapse before its rotational energy is dissipated via electromagnetic radiation (i.e., ∼ 100 ​ s \displaystyle\sim 100\,\mathrm{s} ) as the angular momentum left in the remnant quark star might not be large enough to sustain the additional self-gravity of the supramassive quark star due to the angular momentum dissipation of mass outflows, neutrinos and gravitational waves; 2) considering a general quark star equation of state model, a constraint on the maximum mass of cold and non-rotating quark stars is found as M TOV ≲ 2.35 − 0.17 + 0.07 ​ M ⊙ \displaystyle M_{\mathrm{TOV}}\lesssim 2.35^{+0.07}_{-0.17}\,M_{\odot} , assuming a delayed collapse occurred before a large fraction of the total rotational energy ( ≳ 10 53 \displaystyle\gtrsim 10^{53}\, erg) of the merger remnant was deposited into the merger environment for the GW170817 event. These constraints could be improved with future merger events, once there are more evidences on its post-merger evolution channel or information on the amount of post-merger gravitational wave and neutrino emissions inferred from the multi-messenger observations.

II
PROMPT COLLAPSE

The outcome of a BNS merger depends on the total mass of the system. A direct gravitational collapse to a BH takes place if the total mass is beyond a certain mass, which is called the threshold mass for prompt collapse (
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{thres}}
)
[
47
,
48
,
49
]
. Therefore,
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{thres}}
is an important parameter to characterize the fate of the merger remnant. Many attempts have been made to determine
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{thres}}
for the scenario of BNS mergers
[
50
,
51
,
52
,
53
,
54
,
55
]
, while only a limited number of studies for the BQS case
[
39
]
due to the difficulties in hydrodynamical simulations of BQS mergers arising from the strong density discontinuity at the stellar surface. Hence, to investigate
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{thres}}
of a large number of different QS EOSs, a method with relatively small computational demands is needed. It has been suggested in
[
51
]
that whether
or not prompt collapse occurs depending mostly on the relation between the mass of the merger remnant and the maximum stable mass (
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{stab}}
) given the angular momentum contained in the remnant at the time of the merger (
\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{merger}}
).
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{stab}}
can be approximately derived from differentially rotating equilibrium models
[
51
,
56
,
57
]
. Therefore, through this semi-analytical method, one can derive
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{thres}}
without time-consuming and sophisticated hydrodynamical simulations.

In this section, we apply the method proposed in
[
51
]
to determine
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{thres}}
for the case of BQS mergers. In addition, the empirical relation between
\displaystyle M
and
\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{merger}}
constructed through NR simulations is used in
[
51
]
to estimate the angular momentum of the merger remnant. Compared to the empirical relation, the EOB method employed in this paper permits a more accurate calculation of the angular momentum left in the merger remnant, and hence a more precise
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{thres}}
can be derived, which can be used to constrain QS EOSs only relying on the measurement of the total mass of the binary and the determination of whether a prompt collapse takes place or not in the event.

II.1
EOS models

In
[
50
,
51
,
52
,
53
]
, various NS EOS models are constructed to derive the EOS-insensitive relations between
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{thres}}
and stellar properties. For QSs, it is known that in the simplest MIT bag model (i.e., with zero strange quark mass and neglecting interaction between quarks), stellar properties of both rotating and non-rotating QSs scale with the bag constant which is also the only parameter describing the EOS model. Therefore, the simplest MIT bag model is not appropriate for exploring the parameter space and deriving the EOS-insensitive relations for
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{thres}}
of the BQS merger. In this paper, we employ the unified QS EOS including the perturbative QCD corrections
[
58
,
59
]
and color superconductivity
[

62

p=\frac{1}{3}(\rho-4B_{\mathrm{eff}})+\frac{4\lambda^{2}}{9\pi^{2}}\left(-1+\mathrm{sgn}(\lambda)\sqrt{1+3\pi^{2}\frac{\rho-B_{\mathrm{eff}}}{\lambda^{2}}}\right)

(1)

where
\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}(\lambda)
represents the sign of
\displaystyle\lambda
,
\displaystyle p
denotes the pressure,
\displaystyle\rho
corresponds to the total energy density and
\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{eff}}
is the effective bag constant as a phenomenological representation
of non-perturbative QCD vacuum effects.
\displaystyle\lambda
is defined
as:

\lambda=\frac{\xi_{2a}\Delta^{2}-\xi_{2b}m_{s}^{2}}{\sqrt{\xi_{4}a_{4}}},

(2)

where
\displaystyle m_{s}
represents the finite mass of the strange quark,
\displaystyle\Delta
is the pairing energy gap arising from color superconductivity and
\displaystyle a_{4}
parametrizes the perturbative QCD corrections.
\displaystyle\xi_{2a}

\displaystyle\xi_{2b}
and
\displaystyle\xi_{4}
are the constant coefficients and can be expressed as:

(\xi_{4},\xi_{2a},\xi_{2b})=\begin{cases}(((\tfrac{1}{3})^{\frac{4}{3}}+\tfrac{2}{3})^{\frac{4}{3}})^{-3},1,0)&\mathrm{2SC\ phase}\\
(3,1,3/4)&\mathrm{2SC+s\ phase}\\
(3,3,3/4)&\mathrm{CFL\ phase}\end{cases},

(3)

for various phases of quark matter. Here the 2SC and 2SC+s phases both represent the two-flavor color superconductivity phase where
\displaystyle u
quarks pair with
\displaystyle d
quarks, while only the latter has (unpaired) strange quarks in composition. CFL phase denotes the color-flavor locking phase, where
\displaystyle u,d,s
quarks pair with each other antisymmetrically.

By introducing the following dimensionless rescaling:

\bar{\rho}=\frac{\rho}{4B_{\mathrm{eff}}},\ \bar{p}=\frac{p}{4B_{\mathrm{eff}}},\ \bar{\lambda}=\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4B_{\mathrm{eff}}},

(4)

Eq. (
1

\bar{p}=\frac{1}{3}(\bar{\rho}-1)+\frac{4\bar{\lambda}}{9\pi^{2}}\left(-1+\mathrm{sgn}(\lambda)\sqrt{1+\frac{3\pi^{2}}{\bar{\lambda}}(\bar{\rho}-\frac{1}{4}})\right).

\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}(\lambda)\,\bar{\lambda}

\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}(\lambda)\,\bar{\lambda}

\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{eff}}
and the properties of a QS such as the gravitational mass and the radius can be obtained given a specific
\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{eff}}

\displaystyle\bar{\lambda}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle 10

II.2
Effective-one-body model

During the inspiral phase, the angular momentum dissipation of the binary system is primarily due to gravitational waves. Therefore, the angular momentum left in the merger remnant can be expressed as:

J_{\mathrm{merger}}=J_{0}-J_{\mathrm{GW,i}},

(6)

where
\displaystyle J_{0}
represents the angular momentum of the binary at the beginning of the calculation of the gravitational waves and
\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{GW,i}}
corresponds to the angular momentum radiated by gravitational waves in the inspiral phase.

In this paper, we just focus on the case of quasi-circular, nonspinning coalescences. In this case, given the properties of the binary,
\displaystyle J_{0}
can be obtained through the EOB dynamics for a specific initial orbit frequency or a specific initial separation
[
46
]
. When the initial condition is determined, the gravitational waveform until the time of the merger can be generated through the EOB approach. Moreover, the radiated angular momentum can be calculated as:

\displaystyle\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{GW,i}}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\sum\limits_{(l,m)}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{\mathrm{merger}}}dt^{\prime}m\Im[r^{2}h_{lm}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\dot{h}_{lm}^{*}\left(t^{\prime}\right)],

(7)

where
\displaystyle h_{lm}
is the gravitational waveform generated via the EOB method,
\displaystyle r
represents the radius where
\displaystyle h_{lm}
is extracted,
\displaystyle t^{\prime}
corresponds to the time from the start of the simulation (
\displaystyle t_{0}
) to the merger (
\displaystyle t_{\mathrm{merger}}
),
\displaystyle\Im
is the imaginary part of a complex number and
\displaystyle(l,m)
represents the different modes of gravitational waves. The
\displaystyle x
and
\displaystyle y
components of angular momentum loss are not included because they turn out to have a negligible effect on the computation of
\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{GW,i}}

[
64
,
65
]
. As shown in
[
66
,
63
]
, most of the energy radiated in a BNS merger is contained in the (2,2) mode of gravitational waves. Therefore, for simplicity, only the dominant (2,2) mode is considered in this paper.

In the present paper, we employ the state-of-the-art time-domain EOB waveform model, specifically
SEOBNRv4T

[
67
,
68
]
, which is available in the LSC Algorithm Library
[
69
]
.

SEOBNRv4T
incorporates dynamic tides and accounts for the spin-induced quadrupole moment effects. This model aligns with the latest BNS numerical simulations, extending up to the merger stage
[
70
]
, and has been currently implemented for LIGO data analysis
[
4
]
. Similar to
SEOBNRv4T
, Other accurate gravitational waveform models like
TEOBResumS

[
71
,
72
,
73
]
,
NRTidalv2

[
74
]
,
NRTidalv3

[
75
]
can also be used to obtain
\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{merger}}
. The comparison among these gravitational waveform models
[
75
]
shows that the deviation of waveform appears only a few orbits before the merger and the dissipation in these few orbits is minor compared to the dissipation in the whole inspiral phase, and thus the systematic difference among these models is insignificant for calculating
\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{merger}}
. In the rest of this paper, we only show results calculated through
SEOBNRv4T
model and compare them with the results obtained by NR simulations to estimate the error of our method.

Given an EOS, we first set the mass components (
\displaystyle M_{1}
,
\displaystyle M_{2}
) of the binary and set the initial orbit frequency, and then calculate the tidal deformability of both NSs (
\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}
,
\displaystyle\Lambda_{2}
). After that, we can use
SEOBNRv4T
model to generate the gravitational waveforms, and
\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{GW,i}}
can be calculated through Eq. (
7
). As mentioned above, given the initial orbit frequency,
\displaystyle J_{0}
can also be derived through Eqs. (4.5) and (4.8) in
[
46
]
. Finally,
\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{merger}}
can be obtained through Eq. (

III
DELAYED COLLAPSE

The merger of BNS with
\displaystyle M<M_{\mathrm{thres}}
leads to the formation of a massive NS. If the mass of the remnant NS is greater than the maximum stable mass of uniformly rotating NSs, it has to be supported by differential rotation and is called a hypermassive NS (HMNS)
[
85
,
86
,
87
]

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{TOV}}
to
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{max,urot}}
, the remnant NS is called a
supramssive NS (SMNS)
[
88
,
89
]
. Both the HMNS and the SMNS are transiently stable NS and eventually undergo delayed collapse to BH due to the angular momentum redistribution and dissipation. The collapse of the HMNS remnant could be triggered by the angular momentum loss due to gravitational waves or through the angular momentum transport process in a timescale of
\displaystyle\sim 100\,
ms
[
90
,
91
,
92
]
, while the SMNS remnant can live in a spin-down timescale which is much longer than the HMNS case.

For the BQS case, NR simulations in
[
39
]
have indicated that the gap between

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{thres}}
and
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{max,urot}}
is much smaller than the BNS case, which may lead to differences in the scenario of the post-merger product. Specifically, a supramassive remnant of the BQS merger could collapse to a BH within several times the dynamic timescale if the angular momentum contained in the remnant QS is not large enough to reach the mass-shedding limit
[
39
]
. Thus, analyzing the angular momentum dissipation process is necessary for understanding the evolution of the merger remnant for the BQS merger. In this section, we consider the angular momentum dissipation process in the post-merger stage and employ both energy and angular momentum conservation laws to investigate the post-merger outcome for the BQS case.

\displaystyle\displaystyle M_{f}=M_{\mathrm{merger}}-E_{\mathrm{GW,p}}-E_{\nu}-M_{\mathrm{out}}-M_{\mathrm{eje}},

(12)

\displaystyle M_{\ast\mathrm{out}}=M_{\mathrm{out}}

\displaystyle M_{\ast{\mathrm{eje}}}=M_{\mathrm{eje}}

To solve the baryon number, the energy and the angular momentum conservation equations in the post-merger phase, we first need to calculate the baryonic mass, the gravitational mass and the angular momentum of the remnant QS at the time of the merger which serve as the initial properties of the remnant QS. The baryon number is conserved in the inspiral phase, and hence we can calculate the baryonic mass of the binary at the time of merger by solving the TOV equation, given the mass components and the EOS.
\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{merger}}
can be accurately computed by the EOB method as shown in Sec.

\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{merger}}

\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{merger}}

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{GW,i}}

E_{\mathrm{GW,i}}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\sum\limits_{(\ell,m)}\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{\mathrm{merger}}}dt^{\prime}r^{2}\dot{h}_{\ell m}\left(t^{\prime}\right)\dot{h}_{\ell m}^{*}\left(t^{\prime}\right).

(14)

Therefore,
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{merger}}
can be expressed as:

M_{\mathrm{merger}}=M_{0}-E_{\mathrm{GW,i}},

(15)

where
\displaystyle M_{0}
is the energy of the binary at the beginning of the calculation of gravitational wave, and can be derived through Eqs. (2.4) and (4.3) in
[
46
]

At the onset of collapse, the configuration of the remnant QS can be estimated by the equilibrium configuration at the turning point of the
\displaystyle M-\rho_{\mathrm{c}}
curve with constant
\displaystyle J

[
80

As discussed above, given
\displaystyle M_{f}
(or
\displaystyle J_{f}
and
\displaystyle M_{\ast f}
) we can obtain the dissipated baryonic mass, gravitational mass and angular momentum. Furthermore, there are three main dissipation mechanisms, the gravitational radiation, the neutrino emission and the hydrodynamical angular momentum transport process, which leads to the formation of the torus and the ejecta, and obviously, they dissipate the baryonic mass, the energy and the angular momentum in different proportions. Therefore, in principle, we can determine the amount of dissipation of the three dissipation mechanisms via the three conservation equations, which is equivalent to solving a linear system with three unknowns. However, before solving this linear system, we should know the proportion of the dissipated baryonic mass, energy and angular momentum for each mechanism, which serves as the coefficient of each unknown and has been obtained through NR simulations as summarized in
[
12
]
. Here we briefly introduce them.

For gravitational waves in the post-merger phase, the peak frequency,
\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{peak}}

\displaystyle f

\begin{split}J_{\mathrm{GW,p}}&\approx\frac{E_{\mathrm{GW,p}}}{\pi f_{\mathrm{peak}}}\\
&\approx 9.5\times 10^{48}\,\mathrm{erg\,s}\left(\frac{E_{\mathrm{GW,p}}}{0.05\,M_{\odot}}\right)\left(\frac{f_{\mathrm{peak}}}{3.0\,\mathrm{kHz}}\right)^{-1}.\end{split}

(16)

[

]
, with a relative error
\displaystyle\lesssim 1\%

\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{peak}}
can be determined using the relation provided in Eq. (5.4) of Ref.
[

]
, and hence we employ this relation for the BQS scenario.

For neutrino emission, as the emitter (remnant QS) undergoes rotation, the neutrinos carry away the angular momentum of the remnant QS. Therefore,
\displaystyle J_{\nu}
can be approximately written as
\displaystyle J_{\nu}\approx(2/3)R_{\mathrm{MQS}}^{2}\Omega E_{\nu}

[
126
]
, and correspondingly

\begin{split}J_{\nu}\approx&3.0\times 10^{48}\,\mathrm{erg\,s}\left(\frac{E_{\nu}}{0.1\,M_{\odot}c^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{R_{\mathrm{MQS}}}{15\,\mathrm{km}}\right)^{2}\\
&\times\left(\frac{\Omega}{10^{4}\,\mathrm{rad/s}}\right),\end{split}

(17)

where
\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{MQS}}
and
\displaystyle\Omega
represent the equatorial circumferential radius and angular velocity of the remnant massive QS. This relation is consistent with the NR simulation results within a factor of 2
[
92
]
. In addition, comparing Eq. (
17
) with Eq. (
16
), we find that neutrino emission serves as a secondary mechanism for the dissipation of angular momentum, and thus the uncertainty in neutrino emission only has a minor impact on the results.

The velocity of the torus surrounding the remnant QS and the ejecta should be of the order of their escape velocity, and thus the angular momentum carried away by them can be approximately expressed as
\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{out}}\approx M_{\mathrm{out}}\sqrt{GM_{\mathrm{MQS}}R_{\mathrm{out}}}
and
\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{eje}}\approx M_{\mathrm{eje}}\sqrt{GM_{\mathrm{MQS}}R_{\mathrm{eje}}}
, i.e.,

\begin{split}J_{\mathrm{out}}\approx&5.8\times 10^{48}\,\mathrm{erg\,s}\left(\frac{M_{\mathrm{out}}}{0.05\,M_{\odot}}\right)\left(\frac{R_{\mathrm{out}}}{100\,\mathrm{km}}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\times\left(\frac{M_{\mathrm{MQS}}}{2.6\,M_{\mathrm{\odot}}}\right)^{1/2},\end{split}

(18)

\begin{split}J_{\mathrm{eje}}\approx&6.9\times 10^{48}\,\mathrm{erg\,s}\left(\frac{M_{\mathrm{eje}}}{0.05\,M_{\odot}}\right)\left(\frac{R_{\mathrm{eje}}}{140\,\mathrm{km}}\right)^{1/2}\\
&\times\left(\frac{M_{\mathrm{MQS}}}{2.6\,M_{\mathrm{\odot}}}\right)^{1/2},\end{split}

(19)

where
\displaystyle{M_{\mathrm{MQS}}}
represents the gravitational mass of the remnant massive QS,
\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{out}}
is the typical radius of the torus surrounding the remnant QS, and
\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{eje}}
is the typical location where ejection occurs. Both gravitational mass and equatorial circumferential radius for the remnant QS at the onset of collapse can be estimated by constructing the equilibrium models with the turning point criterion for the given angular momentum or total baryonic mass. The NR simulations for the BNS merger have shown that
\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{out}}
would experience an increase from
\displaystyle\sim 50\,\mathrm{km}
to
\displaystyle\sim 200\,\mathrm{km}
due to the long-term viscous evolution of the torus, as well as the transport of angular momentum from the remnant NS
[
91
,
123
]
. It has been suggested that the dynamics of the BQS merger and the BNS merger are similar
[
124
]
. Therefore, the evolution of the torus may not make much difference and we use the same relation as in
[
12
]
, where it is simply set that
\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{out}}=R_{\mathrm{eje}}=140\,\mathrm{km}
. In addition,
\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{out}}
mainly depends on
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{out}}
instead of
\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{out}}
(
\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{out}}\propto M_{\mathrm{out}}
, while
\displaystyle J_{\mathrm{out}}\propto\sqrt{R_{\mathrm{out}}}
), and thus the value of
\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{out}}
and
\displaystyle R_{\mathrm{eje}}
do not make a great effect on our analysis.

\displaystyle E_{\nu}

and the collapse behavior of the merger remnant can only be determined by NR simulations (e.g.,

), and our analysis can only offer relatively conservative constraint.

V
CONCLUSIONS AND DISCUSSIONS

We semi-analytically investigate the post-merger evolution of the BQS merger. With the help of the EOB method and considering the angular momentum dissipation process, we obtain the angular momentum left in the merger remnant at the time of the merger. The amounts of three main dissipation mechanisms in the post-merger phase are determined by the conservation laws of baryon number, energy and angular momentum. In addition, we determine the stability of the remnant QS by constructing the equilibrium models of rotating QSs. Through this semi-analytical method, we investigate both prompt collapse and delayed collapse cases and set an upper bound on QSs’
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{TOV}}
through the GW170817 event.

Assuming GW170817 originated from a BQS merger, we analyze the post-merger evolution for GW170817 in the BQS scenario. Our analysis suggests that a QS with
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{TOV}}>2.0\,M_{\odot}
, leading to
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{max,urot}}>2.8\,M_{\odot}
, can collapse to a BH before its kinetic energy and angular momentum are dissipated via electromagnetic radiation (
\displaystyle\sim 100\,\mathrm{s}
) because due to the angular momentum dissipation of mass outflows, neutrinos and gravitational waves, the angular momentum left in the remnant QS might not be large enough to sustain the additional self-gravity of the supramassive QS. Moreover, we also systematically investigate the influence of
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{TOV}}
and
\displaystyle\mathrm{sgn}(\lambda)\bar{\lambda}
on the results of our consistency checks. Finally, an upper limit of
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{TOV}}
is obtained, and only QS EOSs with
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{TOV}}\lesssim 2.35^{+0.07}_{-0.17}\,M_{\odot}
can pass our consistency checks and satisfy
\displaystyle\Lambda_{1.4}\lesssim 800
simultaneously. Therefore, although considering interquark effects can make
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{TOV}}
of QSs very large, these QS EOSs with large
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{TOV}}
are unlikely to pass the constraint of the electromagnetic counterparts of the GW170817 event. It should be noted that our derived constraints in this paper are valid for the EOSs based on the IQM model
[
27
]
in the one-family scenario, while other cases of different quark models (such as quark-mass density-dependent model
[
135
,
136
,
137
,
138
]
and NJL model
[
139
,
140
,
141
,
142
]
) or different scenarios (such as the two-family scenario
[
143
,
144
,
145
]
) are not considered, but analyses could be done in a similar way.

Although there are many unknowns in the electromagnetic counterparts associated with BQS mergers, our constraint
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{TOV}}\lesssim 2.35\,M_{\odot}
is only based on the exclusion of the very long-lived (
\displaystyle\sim 100\,\mathrm{s}
)/stable remnant that injects a large amount of its kinetic energy into the outflows from the observation results of the GW170817 event
[
9
,
7
,
12
]
. In comparison, the assumption that the short
\displaystyle\gamma
-ray burst is powered by the spinning BH-torus system
[
10
]
may be inappropriate considering the simulations in
[
133
,
146
]
, where it is indicated that SMNS and HMNS may be able to serve as the central engine of the short
\displaystyle\gamma
-ray burst. Additionally, as discussed in
[
12
]
and the current paper, the assumption that the remnant NS/QS is at the mass-shedding limit is also improper, and thus for both NSs and QSs,
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{TOV}}
can only be weakly constrained as
\displaystyle M_{\mathrm{TOV}}\lesssim 2.35\,M_{\odot}
. Moreover, our analysis can also connect to the multi-messenger observations. In this paper, we only employ the constraint of the energy of gravitational waves obtained by NR simulations. In the future, with observations of post-merger gravitational waves and neutrinos, the values of
\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{GW,p}}
and
\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{\nu}}
can be further constrained by observations. As a result, the constraints for EOSs of compact stars can be better imposed and the central engine of the short
\displaystyle\gamma
-ray burst can be understood more deeply.

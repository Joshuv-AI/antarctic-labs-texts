Large sum-free sets in finite vector spaces I.
Christian Reiher, Sofia Zotova

Abstract
. Let p p be a prime number with p ≡ 2 ( mod 3 ) p\equiv 2\pmod{3} and let n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 be a dimension. It is known that a sum-free subset of 𝔽 p n {\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n} can have at most the size 1 3 ​ ( p + 1 ) ​ p n − 1 \frac{1}{3}(p+1)p^{n-1} and that, up to automorphisms of 𝔽 p n {\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n} , the only extremal example is the ‘cuboid’ [ p + 1 3 , 2 ​ p − 1 3 ] × 𝔽 p n − 1 \bigl[\frac{p+1}{3},\frac{2p-1}{3}\bigr]\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1} . For p ≥ 11 p\geq 11 we show that if a sum-free subset of 𝔽 p n {\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n} is not contained in such an extremal one, then its size is at most 1 3 ​ ( p − 2 ) ​ p n − 1 \frac{1}{3}(p-2)p^{n-1} . This bound is optimal and we classify the extremal configurations. The remaining cases p = 2 , 5 p=2,5 are known to behave differently. For p = 3 p=3 the analogous question was solved by Vsevolod Lev, and for p ≡ 1 ( mod 3 ) p\equiv 1\pmod{3} it is less interesting.

1.
Introduction

A subset
A
of an abelian group
G
is said to be
sum-free

if the equation
x+y=z
has no solution with
x,y,z\in A
. The
study of this concept was begun more than a century ago by
Schur
[
21
]
, who famously showed that the set of positive
integers cannot be expressed as a union of finitely many sum-free
sets. From the 1960’s onwards, a lot of activity in the subject was
stimulated by the problems and results of Erdős
(see, e.g.,
[
7
]
and the survey
[
22
]

by Tao and Vu). Let us write
\mathrm{sf}_{0}(G)
for the largest possible
size of a sum-free subset of a given finite abelian group
G
.
The basic question to determine this invariant of
G
turned out
to be surprisingly difficult; it was solved by Green
and Ruzsa
[
8
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Green and Ruzsa)
.

Let
G
be a (nontrivial) finite abelian group.

(

)

If
|G|
has a prime factor
p
with
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
,
then
\mathrm{sf}_{0}(G)=\frac{p_{0}+1}{3p_{0}}|G|
holds for the
least such prime factor
p_{0}
.

(

)

If the previous clause does not apply, but
|G|

is divisible by
3
, then
\mathrm{sf}_{0}(G)=\frac{1}{3}|G|
.

(

)

If all prime factors
p
of
|G|
satisfy

p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
and
m
denotes the largest integer
such that
G
possesses an element of order
m
, then

\mathrm{sf}_{0}(G)=\frac{m-1}{3m}|G|
.

Building upon earlier work of Lev, Łuczak, and Schoen
[
13
]
,
Green and Ruzsa also showed that the number of sum-free subsets
of
G
, that is the cardinality of

\mathrm{SF}_{0}(G)=\{A\subseteq G\colon(A+A)\cap A=\varnothing\}\,,

is given by

|\mathrm{SF}_{0}(G)|=2^{\mathrm{sf}_{0}(G)+o(|G|)}\,.

Perhaps surprisingly, the problem to classify

\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{0}(G)=\{A\in\mathrm{SF}_{0}(G)\colon|A|=\mathrm{sf}_{0}(G)\}\,,

i.e., the sum-free subsets of
G
of maximal size, was
solved only less than a decade ago by Balasubramanian,
Prakash, and Ramana
[
1
]
.

There are quite a few results asserting that if a sum-free subset
A

of a specific group
G
has size close to
\mathrm{sf}_{0}(G)
, then it is
contained (or almost contained) in a member of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{0}(G)
.
Vsevolod Lev initiated a systematic quantitative
study of this phenomenon for elementary abelian

p
-groups
[
14
,
15
]
.
Slightly more generally, we propose to study the following hierarchy
that starts with
\mathrm{SF}_{0}(G)
,
\mathrm{sf}_{0}(G)
, and
\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{0}(G)
.

Definition 1.2
.

Given a finite abelian group
G
we define by recursion
on
k\geq 1
the sets and numbers

•

\mathrm{SF}_{k}(G)=\{A\in\mathrm{SF}_{k-1}(G)\colon\text{ there
is no $B\in\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{k-1}(G)$ with $A\subseteq B$}\}
,

•

\mathrm{sf}_{k}(G)=\max\{|A|\colon A\in\mathrm{SF}_{k}(G)\}
,

•

and
\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{k}(G)=\{A\in\mathrm{SF}_{k}(G)\colon|A|=\mathrm{sf}_{k}(G)\}
.

We are especially interested in the case that
k=1
and

G={\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n}
is a vector space over the field
{\mathds{F}}_{p}
, where
p

denotes a prime number. For
p=2
it follows from the work of
Davydov and Tombak
[
5
]
that

\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{2}^{n})=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }n\leq 3\cr 5\cdot 2^{n-4}&\text{ if }n\geq 4\,.\end{cases}

The case
p=3
was solved by Lev
[
14
]
, who showed

\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{3}^{n})=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }n\leq 2\cr 5\cdot 3^{n-3}&\text{ if }n\geq 3\,.\end{cases}

Recently Lev began working on the most difficult case, namely
p=5
.
In
[
15
]
he obtained

5^{n-1}\leq\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n})<\frac{3}{2}\cdot 5^{n-1}
. Shortly afterwards
Versteegen
[
23
]
improved these bounds
to
28\cdot 5^{n-3}\leq\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n})\leq 6\cdot 5^{n-1}

(for
n\geq 3
), and in
[
17
]
we proved that the lower bound
is sharp. More precisely, we have

\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n})=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }n=1\cr 5&\text{ if }n=2\cr 28\cdot 5^{n-3}&\text{ if }n\geq 3\,.\end{cases}

The remaining prime numbers fall into two classes depending on
whether they are congruent to
1
or
2
modulo
3
. The former
case being less interesting (as we explain in §
6
),
we focus on the latter one here.
Despite the considerable complexity of the
case
p=5
it turns out that primes
p\geq 11
with
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}

admit a uniform treatment (see Theorem
1.4
).
In fact, we will also describe the corresponding extremal sets
in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})
.

Let us call two subsets of an abelian group
G

isomorphic
if there
is an automorphism of
G
sending one to the other. By an early observation
of Yap
[
26
]
, if
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
and
n\geq 1
, the class
\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{0}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})

consists of all sets isomorphic to
[\frac{p+1}{3},\frac{2p-1}{3}]\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
.
Now we introduce a class of subsets of
{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n}
that will be shown to
be
\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})
, when
p\geq 11
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
p=6m-1\geq 11
be a prime number and

.
A set

is said to be

if there exists a (possible empty) set

such that

and

is isomorphic to

\displaystyle\{(2m-1,0)\}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\{2m\}\times({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}\smallsetminus P)

Furthermore, we call a set
A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n}

structured

if there are a dimension
\ell\in[n]
and a very structured
set
B\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{\ell}
such that
A
is isomorphic
to
B\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-\ell}
.

Notice that in the special case
n=1
we are forced to take
P=\varnothing
,
so that a subset of
{\mathds{F}}_{p}
is structured if and only if it is
isomorphic to
[2m-1,4m-3]
. In general, one confirms easily
that all structured sets
A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n}
have the same size

|A|=(2m-1)p^{n-1}
; moreover, all structured subsets of
{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n}

are sum-free. In fact, it could easily be shown that they are
maximal sum-free sets with respect to inclusion, but we shall
phrase our arguments in such a way that it is not necessary
to verify this directly. Let us now state our main result.

Theorem 1.4
.

If
p\geq 11
is a prime number satisfying
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}

and
n\geq 1
, then

\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})=\frac{p-2}{3}\cdot p^{n-1}\,.

Furthermore,
\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})
is the collection of all
structured subsets of
{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n}
.

We would like to record that the upper bound

\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})<(\frac{p}{3}-\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{p})p^{n-1}
was proved
earlier by Versteegen
[
23
]
.
An essentially equivalent and, perhaps, more transparent version
of our theorem reads as follows.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose that
p\geq 11
is a prime number
satisfying
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}
and
n\geq 1
.
If
A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n}
is a sum-free set of
size
|A|\geq\frac{1}{3}(p-2)p^{n-1}
, then either
A
is isomorphic
to a subset
of
\bigl[\frac{p+1}{3},\frac{2p-1}{3}\bigr]\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}

or

is structured.

2.
Preliminaries

We start by recalling a few well-known results belonging to additive
combinatorics and deriving some easy consequences from them.
First, we quote a theorem due to Cauchy
[
2
]
, which
was rediscovered independently by Davenport
[
3
,
4
]
.

Theorem 2.1

(Cauchy-Davenport)
.

If
p
denotes a prime number and
A,B\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{p}
are nonempty, then

|A+B|\geq\min\{p,|A|+|B|-1\}\,.\qed

The cases of equality were completely determined by Vosper
[
25
,
24
]
.
Here we only require the main case of his characterisation.

Theorem 2.2

(Vosper)
.

Given a prime number
p
let
A,B\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{p}
be two sets
such that
|A|,|B|\geq 2
and
|A+B|\leq p-2
.
If
|A+B|\leq|A|+|B|-1
, then
A
and
B
are two arithmetic
progressions with the same common difference. ∎

Next we introduce Kneser’s theorem. Given a finite nonempty
subset
X
of an abelian group
G
we write

\mathrm{Sym}(X)=\{g\in G\colon g+X=X\}

for the so-called
symmetry group
of
X
. The statement that
follows appears implicitly for cyclic groups in
[
11
]
; in full
generality it is stated explicitly in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 2.3

(Kneser)
.

If
A
,
B
are two finite nonempty subsets of an abelian
group
G
and
K=\mathrm{Sym}(A+B)
, then

|A+B|\geq|A+K|+|B+K|-|K|\geq|A|+|B|-|K|\,.\qed

Since
|A+K|+|B+K|
and
|G|
are always multiples of
|K|
,
this has the following consequence, which could also be shown
in a significantly easier way.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
A
and
B
are two subsets of an abelian group
G

such that
|A|+|B|>|G|
, then
A+B=G
. ∎

Here is a less superficial application of Kneser’s theorem.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

(

)

,

(

3.
First steps

For the sake of completeness we would like to recall the
classification of
\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{0}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})
when
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}

(cf. e.g.,
[
8
]
*Lemma 5.6).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p
denote a prime number with
p\equiv 2\pmod{3}

and
n\geq 1
. If
A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n}
with

|A|\geq\frac{1}{3}(p+1)p^{n-1}
is sum-free, then
A
is
isomorphic to the ‘cuboid’

Q=\bigl[\frac{p+1}{3},\frac{2p-1}{3}\bigr]\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
.

In particular, we have
\mathrm{sf}_{0}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})=\frac{1}{3}(p+1)p^{n-1}

and
\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{0}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})
is the collection of all sets isomorphic
to
Q
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
n=1
. As the set
A
is sum-free,
it is disjoint to
A+A
, whence

|A+A|\leq p-|A|\leq\tfrac{1}{3}(2p-1)\leq 2|A|-1\,.

So, by Vosper’s theorem,
A
is an arithmetic progression
of length
\frac{p+1}{3}
.
We may assume that its common difference is
1
.
Together with
0\not\in A
this yields

A=\bigl[a,a+\frac{p-2}{3}\bigr]
for
some
a\in\bigl[\frac{2p-1}{3}\bigr]
, and in view
of
2a,2\bigl(a+\frac{p-2}{3}\bigr)\not\in A
only the
case
a=\frac{p+1}{3}
is possible.

This proves the lemma for
n=1
and we can proceed
to the general case. By Lemma
2.5
applied to
A=B=C

we may assume that
A
is of the form
A_{\star}\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}

for some
A_{\star}\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{p}
. Clearly,
A_{\star}
has to
be a sum-free set of cardinality
\frac{p+1}{3}
. Thus
A_{\star}

is isomorphic to the interval

\bigl[\frac{p+1}{3},\frac{2p-1}{3}\bigr]
and the result follows.
∎

Theorem
1.4
alleges that structured sets cannot be
covered by any sets isomorphic to the cuboid
Q
. Let us briefly
pause to check that this is indeed the case.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p=6m-1\geq 11
be a prime number and
n\geq 1
.
If
A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n}
is structured, then there is
no
B\in\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{0}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})
covering
A
.

Proof.

By Definition
1.3
we can suppose that there are a
dimension
\ell\in[n]
and a (possibly empty)
set
P\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{\ell-1}
with
0\not\in P+P

such that
A=A^{\prime}\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-\ell}
holds for the
set

\displaystyle A^{\prime}=\{(2m-1,0)\}\cup\{2m\}

\displaystyle\times({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{\ell-1}\smallsetminus P)\cup[2m+1,4m-3]\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{\ell-1}

\displaystyle\cup\{4m-2\}\times({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{\ell-1}\smallsetminus\{0\})\cup\{4m-1\}\times P\,.

If there existed some
B\in\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{0}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})
covering
A
,
then Lemma
3.1
would yield a nonzero linear form

\lambda\colon{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathds{F}}_{p}
such that

(3.1)

\lambda[A]\subseteq[2m,4m-1]\,.

Let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
be the standard basis of
{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n}
.
For every
i\in[2,n]
there is a line in direction
e_{i}

completely contained in
A
and, therefore, (
3.1
)
implies
\lambda(e_{i})=0
. Thus
\mu=\lambda(e_{1})
is nonzero
and (
3.1
) leads to

\mu\cdot[2m-1,4m-3]\subseteq[2m,4m-1]
. As the right side
is symmetric about the origin,

\mu\cdot[2m+2,4m]\subseteq[2m,4m-1]
holds as well.
Due to
m\geq 2
both inclusions combine to

\mu\cdot[2m-1,4m]\subseteq[2m,4m-1]
.
But here the left side has more elements than the right side,
which is absurd.
∎

It should be clear that in view of these two lemmata
Theorem
1.5
implies Theorem
1.4
.
Thus it suffices to establish Theorem
1.5

in the remainder of this article.
We commence with the one-dimensional case.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p=6m-1
be a prime number, where
m\geq 2
.
If
A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{p}
is a sum-free set of size
|A|=2m-1
,
then
A
is isomorphic either to a subset of
[2m,4m-1]
or
to
[2m-1,4m-3]
.

Proof.

We distinguish two cases according to the cardinality
of
X=A\cup(-A)
.

First Case:
|X|\geq 2m+2

Since the set
A
is sum-free, it is disjoint to
A+X
and
we have

|A+X|\leq p-|A|=4m\leq|A|+|X|-1\,.

So, by Vosper’s theorem,
A
and
X
must be arithmetic
progressions with the same common difference and one checks
easily that
A
needs to be isomorphic to
[2m-1,4m-2]
.

Second Case:
|X|\leq 2m+1

Due to
0\not\in A
the number
|X|
has to be even and the only
possibility is
|X|=2m
. It suffices to show that
X
is
isomorphic to
[2m,4m-1]
. If this failed, then
Lemma
3.1
would tell us that
X
is not sum-free.

Thus there are three elements
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in X
such that

x_{1}+x_{2}=x_{3}
. We can suppose without loss of generality that

x_{3}\in A
but
x_{1}\not\in A
.
The latter means that
x_{1}
is the unique member of

X\smallsetminus A
and
-x_{1}\in A
.
Now
x_{2}=(-x_{1})+x_{3}\in A+A
shows that
x_{2}
is in
X\smallsetminus A
as
well, whence
x_{2}=x_{1}
. Similarly,
-x_{3}=(-x_{1})+(-x_{2})\in A+A

leads to
-x_{3}=x_{1}
, so that altogether we obtain
3x_{1}=0
. But
now
0\not\in A
and
p\neq 3
yield a contradiction.
∎

Working towards the higher dimensional generalisation from now on,
we proceed with a discussion of the case that the set
A
under
consideration lives in the ‘middle third’ of
{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n}
. This
will be the last time in the argument where we have to work
explicitly with the definition of structured sets.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
p=6m-1\geq 11
denotes a prime number and
n\geq 1
,
then every set
A\subseteq[2m-1,4m-1]\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}

belonging to
\mathrm{SF}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})
which has at least the size

(2m-1)p^{n-1}
is structured.

Proof.

Let
A_{2m-1},\dots,A_{4m-1}\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
be the sets satisfying

A=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\displaylimits_{i\in[2m-1,4m-1]}\{i\}\times A_{i}\,.

Notice that
A_{2m-1}
cannot be empty, because

A\subseteq[2m,4m-1]\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
would contradict our
assumption that
A
is not contained in a maximal sum-free subset
of
{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n}
(cf. Lemma
3.1
). As
A
is sum-free, we know

(A_{i}+A_{j})\cap A_{i+j}=\varnothing
whenever
i,j,i+j\in[2m-1,4m-1]
,
whence

|A_{2m-1}|+|A_{4m-2}|\leq|A_{2m-1}+A_{2m-1}|+|A_{4m-2}|\leq p^{n-1}

and, similarly,

|A_{2m}|+|A_{4m-1}|\leq|A_{2m}+A_{2m-1}|+|A_{4m-1}|\leq p^{n-1}\,.

Together with the trivial upper bound
|A_{i}|\leq p^{n-1}
this
yields

\displaystyle(2m-1)\cdot p^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq|A|=\sum_{i=2m-1}^{4m-1}|A_{i}|

\displaystyle=(|A_{2m-1}|+|A_{4m-2}|)+(|A_{2m}|+|A_{4m-1}|)+\sum_{i=2m+1}^{4m-3}|A_{i}|

\displaystyle\leq(2m-1)\cdot p^{n-1}\,.

Now all inequalities we have encountered so far are actually
equalities, which means that

\displaystyle A_{2m+1}

\displaystyle=\dots=A_{4m-3}={\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}\,,

(3.2)

\displaystyle|A_{2m-1}|

\displaystyle=|A_{2m-1}+A_{2m-1}|\,,

(3.3)

\displaystyle A_{4m-2}

\displaystyle={\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}\smallsetminus(A_{2m-1}+A_{2m-1})\,,

\displaystyle|A_{2m}|

\displaystyle=|A_{2m-1}+A_{2m}|\,,

(3.4)

\displaystyle A_{4m-1}

\displaystyle={\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}\smallsetminus(A_{2m-1}+A_{2m})\,.

Since for every fixed vector
y\in{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
the
map
(i,x)\longmapsto(i,x-iy)
is an automorphism
of
{\mathds{F}}_{p}\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
that preserves

[2m-1,4m-1]\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
, we may assume
0\in A_{2m-1}
.
Together with (
3.2
) this causes
A_{2m-1}
to be a linear
subspace of
{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
. The remaining equations inform us that all
sets
A_{i}
are unions of cosets of
A_{2m-1}
. According to
Definition
1.3
this allows us to assume
A_{2m-1}=\{0\}

and it suffices to show that
A
is very structured in this case.

To this end we observe that (
3.3
) yields

A_{4m-2}={\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}\smallsetminus\{0\}
.
Furthermore, due to
2(4m-1)=2m-1
(in
{\mathds{F}}_{p}
) the set
P=A_{4m-1}

satisfies
0\not\in P+P
and, finally, (
3.4
) yields

A_{2m}={\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}\smallsetminus P
.
Altogether,
A
has indeed the form displayed in Definition
1.3
.
∎

4.
Further applications of Kneser’s theorem

It turns out that Kneser’s theorem allows us to relax the hypothesis
of Lemma
3.4
on the shape of
A
considerably.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
p=6m-1\geq 11
be a prime number,
n\geq 2
,
and
A\in\mathrm{SF}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})
.
If
|A|\geq(2m-1)p^{n-1}
and there is a set
I\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{p}

such that
A\subseteq I\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
and
|I|\leq p-3
,
then
A
is structured.

Proof.

We enumerate
{\mathds{F}}_{p}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{p}\}
in such a manner that
the cardinalities of the subsets

B_{1},\dots,B_{p}\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
determined by

A=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\displaylimits_{i\in[p]}\,\{b_{i}\}\times B_{i}

are ordered by
|B_{1}|\geq|B_{2}|\geq\dots\geq|B_{p}|
.
It will be convenient to set
C_{i}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{i}\}
for
every
i\in[p]
and
\ell=\max\{i\in[p]\colon B_{i}\neq\varnothing\}
.
Notice that the numbers
\beta_{i}=|B_{i}|/p^{n-2}
fulfil

p\geq\beta_{1}\geq\beta_{2}\geq\dots\geq\beta_{\ell}>0\quad\text{ and }\quad\beta_{\ell+1}=\dots=\beta_{p}=0\,.

Our assumptions
|A|\geq(2m-1)p^{n-1}
and
|I|\leq p-3
entail

(4.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{i}\geq(2m-1)p\quad\text{ and }\quad\ell\leq p-3\,,

respectively.

Claim 4.2
.

Let
r,s,t\in[\ell]
. If
r
is odd and
r+s+t\geq p+1
,
then
\beta_{r}+\beta_{s}+\beta_{t}\leq p+1
and
\beta_{r}+\beta_{s}\leq p
.

Proof.

We distinguish two cases.

First Case:
0\not\in C_{r}

As
r
is odd, we have
C_{r}\neq-C_{r}
and the
union
X=C_{r}\cup(-C_{r})
has at least the size
r+1
.
Thus the Cauchy-Davenport theorem yields

|X+C_{t}|\geq\min\{r+t,p\}\geq p+1-s\,,

and, consequently, the sets
X+C_{t}
and
C_{s}
cannot be disjoint.
This means that there exist some numbers
\varrho\leq r
,

\sigma\leq s
,
\tau\leq t
, and a sign
\varepsilon\in\{-1,+1\}
such
that
\varepsilon b_{\varrho}+b_{\tau}=b_{\sigma}
.
In view of
(\varepsilon A+A)\cap A=\varnothing

we conclude
(\varepsilon B_{\varrho}+B_{\tau})\cap B_{\sigma}=\varnothing

and Lemma
2.5
reveals

\beta_{r}+\beta_{s}+\beta_{t}\leq\frac{|B_{\varrho}|+|B_{\sigma}|+|B_{\tau}|}{p^{n-2}}\leq p+1\,.

Moreover, we obtain

\beta_{r}+\beta_{s}\leq\frac{|\varepsilon B_{\varrho}+B_{\tau}|+|B_{\sigma}|}{p^{n-2}}\leq p\,.

Second Case:
0\in C_{r}

Take
\varrho\leq r
such that
b_{\varrho}=0
. Following the arguments
from the previous case but using the equalities
b_{\varrho}+b_{s}=b_{s}

and
b_{\varrho}+b_{t}=b_{t}
we see

\beta_{r}+\beta_{s}+\beta_{t}\leq\frac{\beta_{\varrho}+2\beta_{s}}{2}+\frac{\beta_{\varrho}+2\beta_{t}}{2}\leq p+1

and

\beta_{r}+\beta_{s}\leq\frac{|B_{\varrho}+B_{s}|+|B_{s}|}{p^{n-2}}\leq p\,.\qed

Claim 4.3
.

We have
\ell\leq 2m+2
.

Proof.

Assume for the sake of contradiction that
\ell\geq 2m+3
.
Three applications of Claim
4.2
disclose

\displaystyle\beta_{2m-3}+\beta_{2m}+\beta_{2m+3}

\displaystyle\leq 6m\,,

\displaystyle\beta_{2m-2}+\beta_{2m-1}+\beta_{2m+4}\leq\beta_{2m-2}+\beta_{2m-1}+\beta_{2m+3}

\displaystyle\leq 6m\,,

\displaystyle\beta_{2m+1}+\beta_{2m+2}\leq\tfrac{2}{3}(2\beta_{2m+1}+\beta_{2m+2})

\displaystyle\leq 4m\,,

and by adding these estimates we conclude

(4.2)

\sum_{i=2m-3}^{2m+4}\beta_{i}\leq 16m\,.

Next we contend

(4.3)

\beta_{i}+\beta_{p-2i-2}+\beta_{p-2i-1}\leq p+1

for every
i\in[2m-4]
. If
p-2i-2>\ell
this follows immediately
from
\beta_{i}\leq p
; on the other hand, if
p-2i-2\leq\ell
,
then Claim
4.2
implies the even stronger estimate

\beta_{i}+2\beta_{p-2i-2}\leq p+1
, because

i+2(p-2i-2)=2p-3i-4\geq 2p-3(2m-4)-4=p+7\,.

Summing up the Inequalities (
4.3
) for all
i\in[2m-4]

and adding (
4.2
) we arrive at

\sum_{i=1}^{p-3}\beta_{i}\leq(2m-4)(p+1)+16m=(2m-1)p-1\overset{\eqref{eq:0143}}{<}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{i}\,,

which contradicts
\ell\leq p-3
.
∎

Claim 4.4
.

We have
|B_{2m-1}|>\frac{p^{n-1}}{2}
.

Proof.

If
\ell\leq 2m
, we subtract
\beta_{i}\leq p
for all
i\in[2m-2]

from the left estimate in (
4.1
), thus getting

\beta_{2m-1}+\beta_{2m}\geq p
.
This implies
\beta_{2m-1}\geq\frac{p}{2}
or, in other words,

|B_{2m-1}|\geq\frac{p^{n-1}}{2}
. As
p
is odd, equality
is impossible, and our claim follows.

Due to Claim
4.3
it remains to discuss the
case
\ell\in\{2m+1,2m+2\}
.
Claim
4.2
tells us
\beta_{2m-j}+\beta_{2m+j-1}\leq p

for all
j\in\{1,2,3\}
and thus we have

(2m-1)p\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2m+2}\beta_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{2m-4}\beta_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{3}(\beta_{2m-j}+\beta_{2m+j-1})\leq\bigl((2m-4)+3\bigr)p=(2m-1)p\,.

Equality needs to hold throughout this estimate and,
in particular, we have
\beta_{2m-1}+\beta_{2m}=p
,
which allows us to conclude the argument as in the
first case.
∎

From Claim
4.4
and Lemma
2.4

we learn
B_{i}\pm B_{j}={\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
whenever
i,j\in[2m-1]
.
This shows that
C_{2m-1}\pm C_{2m-1}
is disjoint to
C_{\ell}
and,
in particular, that
C_{2m-1}
is sum-free. Owing to
Lemma
3.3
we can assume that
C_{2m-1}
is either the
interval
[2m-1,4m-3]
or a subset of
[2m,4m-1]
.

Suppose first that
C_{2m-1}=[2m-1,4m-3]
. Due to
m\geq 2
this
implies

C_{2m-1}\pm C_{2m-1}={\mathds{F}}_{p}\smallsetminus C_{2m-1}

and thus we have
\ell=2m-1
and

A\subseteq[2m-1,4m-3]\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
.
Because of
|A|\geq(2m-1)p^{n-1}
this needs to hold with equality.
Since
[2m-1,4m-3]
is a very structured
subset of
{\mathds{F}}_{p}
, it follows that
A
is indeed structured.

It remains to deal with the case that
C_{2m-1}
arises from

[2m,4m-1]
by deleting one element. For reasons of symmetry we
can suppose
2m\in C_{2m-1}
. Now a short calculation in
{\mathds{F}}_{p}

yields

\displaystyle C_{2m-1}+(\pm C_{2m-1})

\displaystyle=C_{2m-1}+[2m,4m-1]

\displaystyle\supseteq[4m,8m-3]=[0,2m-2]\cup[4m,p-1]\,.

Therefore,
C_{\ell}
is a subset of
[2m-1,4m-1]
,
whence
A\subseteq[2m-1,4m-1]\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
.
By Lemma
3.4
this implies that
A
is structured.
∎

When one attempts to continue the foregoing argument, it
turns out that even if the smallest set
I

with
A\subseteq I\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
has at least the size
p-2

there is something interesting we can say.

Proposition 4.5
.

Given a prime number
p=6m-1\geq 11
and a dimension
n\geq 2

let
(A_{k})_{k\in{\mathds{F}}_{p}}

be a family of subsets of
{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
.
If
A=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\displaylimits_{k\in{\mathds{F}}_{p}}\{k\}\times A_{k}

is a sum-free set of size
|A|\geq(2m-1)p^{n-1}

and at most two of the sets
A_{k}
are empty, then there exists a real number
U
such
that
\sum_{k\in{\mathds{F}}_{p}}\big||A_{k}|-U\big|\leq 2p^{n-1}
.

Proof.

We start with the same enumeration
{\mathds{F}}_{p}=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{p}\}

and decomposition

A=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\displaylimits_{i\in[p]}\,\{b_{i}\}\times B_{i}

as in the previous proof.
Setting again
\ell=\max\{i\in[p]\colon B_{i}\neq\varnothing\}

and
\beta_{i}=|B_{i}|/p^{n-2}
for every
i\in[p]
we have

p\geq\beta_{1}\geq\beta_{2}\geq\dots\geq\beta_{\ell}>0\,,\quad\quad\beta_{\ell+1}=\dots=\beta_{p}=0\,,

and

(4.4)

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\beta_{i}\geq(2m-1)p\,.

The assumption that at most two of the sets
A_{k}
be empty
yields
\ell\geq p-2
. As the proof of Claim
4.2

does not depend on the value of
\ell
, that statement is
still available to us.

For all pairs
(i,j)\in[m]\times[3]

we have
(2i-1)+2i+(p+j-3i)=p+i+j-1\geq p+1

and, therefore, Claim
4.2
yields

\beta_{2i-1}+\beta_{2i}+\beta_{p+j-3i}\leq p+1\,.

Summing over all pairs
(i,j)
we deduce

(4.5)

3\sum_{k=1}^{2m}\beta_{k}+\sum_{k=3m}^{p}\beta_{k}\leq 3m(p+1)=18m^{2}\,.

Due to
3\beta_{2m+1}\leq p+1
we have
\beta_{2m+1}\leq 2m

and, consequently,

3\sum_{k=2m+1}^{3m-1}\beta_{k}+\beta_{3m}\leq 2(3m-2)m\,.

By adding (
4.5
) we infer

3\sum_{k=1}^{3m-1}\beta_{k}+2\beta_{3m}+\sum_{k=3m+1}^{p}\beta_{k}\leq 24m^{2}-4m=4mp\,.

Now we subtract the double of (
4.4
) and multiply
by
p^{n-2}
, thereby obtaining

\sum_{k=1}^{p}\big||B_{k}|-|B_{3m}|\big|=p^{n-2}\Bigl(\sum_{k=1}^{3m-1}\beta_{k}-\sum_{k=3m+1}^{p}\beta_{k}\Bigr)\leq 2p^{n-1}\,.

This shows that the number
U=|B_{3m}|
is as required.
∎

Intuitively, the estimate

\sum_{k\in{\mathds{F}}_{p}}\big||A_{k}|-U\big|\leq 2p^{n-1}
means that the
map
k\longmapsto|A_{k}|
does not deviate too much from the constant
function whose value is always
U
, which gives us a fair amount of
control over the Fourier coefficients of
A
.
In fact, for
p\geq 17
this information is enough to conclude
the proof of Theorem
1.5
, but in the special case
p=11

we need to argue much more carefully to achieve the same goal.

Proposition 4.6
.

Suppose that
n\geq 2
and at most two of the sets

A_{0},\dots,A_{10}\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{11}^{n-1}
are empty.
If
A=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\displaylimits_{k\in{\mathds{F}}_{11}}\{k\}\times A_{k}
is a sum-free
subset of
{\mathds{F}}_{11}^{n}
of size
|A|\geq 3\cdot 11^{n-1}
, then

\sum_{k=1}^{10}\left(1-\cos\frac{2k\pi}{11}\right)|A_{k}|<\frac{11|A|}{8}\,.

Proof.

The quotients
\alpha_{i}=\frac{|A_{i}|}{11^{n-2}}

and

\alpha_{i}\leq 11

for all

Claim 4.7
.

Whenever

are distinct and nonzero, we
have
\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j}\leq 11
; if additionally

,
then
\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j}+\alpha_{i+j}\leq 12

Proof.

If we had

2.4
would imply

, contrary to the assumption

The second assertion follows from the first one if one
of
A_{i}
,
A_{j}
,

is empty and from Lemma
2.5

otherwise.
∎

By adding symmetric pairs of estimates derived from

\displaystyle(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{10})+2(\alpha_{5}+\alpha_{6})

In terms of the sums

Approximating the cosines we derive

In combination with (

\displaystyle\qquad+0.535\cdot(\beta_{3}+2\beta_{4})+0.225\cdot(2\beta_{3}+\beta_{5})+1.15\cdot\beta_{5}

Using the additional inequality

\displaystyle 45.375

\beta_{2}+\beta_{4}+\beta_{5}>22.4\,.

Since

|A_{4}-A_{9}|<|A_{5}|+11^{n-2}\,.

A symmetric argument also establishes

By Inequality (

5.
Fourier analysis

Based on the results of the previous section we can now
complete the proof of Theorem
1.5
by means of a
Fourier analytic argument. For definiteness we fix some
notation.

Given a finite abelian group
G
, we denote its
Pontryagin
dual
, that is the group of homomorphisms from
G
to the
unit circle in the complex plane, by
\widehat{G}
. For a function

f\colon G\longrightarrow{\mathds{C}}
, its
Fourier transform

\widehat{f}\colon\widehat{G}\longrightarrow{\mathds{C}}
is defined by

\widehat{f}(\gamma)=\sum_{x\in G}f(x)\overline{\gamma(x)}

for all
\gamma\in\widehat{G}
.
Subsets of
G
are identified with their characteristic
functions. The following statement is well-known
(cf. e.g.,
[
8
]
*Lemma 7.1), but for the sake of
completeness we provide a brief sketch of its proof.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
A
denotes a sum-free subset of a finite abelian
group
G\neq 0
, then there exists a non-trivial
character
\gamma\in\widehat{G}
such that

\mathrm{Re\,\,}\widehat{A}(\gamma)\leq\frac{-|A|^{2}}{|G|-|A|}\,.

Proof.

Since the equation
a+a^{\prime}-a^{\prime\prime}=0
has no solutions
with
a,a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}\in A
, we have

\sum_{\gamma\in\widehat{G}}\bigl(\widehat{A}(\gamma)\bigr)^{2}\widehat{A}(-\gamma)=0\,,

whence

\sum_{\gamma\in\widehat{G}}\big|\widehat{A}(\gamma)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{Re\,\,}\widehat{A}(\gamma)=0\,.

The trivial character contributes
|A|^{3}
to the left side.
So if
K
denotes the minimum value of
\mathrm{Re\,\,}\widehat{A}(\gamma)

as
\gamma
ranges over the non-trivial characters, we obtain

K\Bigl(-|A|^{2}+\sum_{\gamma\in\widehat{G}}|\widehat{A}(\gamma)|^{2}\Bigr)\leq-|A|^{3}\,.

By Parseval’s identity, the sum on the left side evaluates to
|G||A|

and the result follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Given a prime number
p=6m-1\geq 11
and a dimension
n\geq 1

let
A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n}
be a sum-free set of
size
|A|\geq(2m-1)p^{n-1}
.
We need to show that
A
is either structured or isomorphic
to a subset of
[2m-1,4m]\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
.
Assuming that this fails, Lemma
3.1

informs us that
A\in\mathrm{SF}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})
and Lemma
3.3

yields
n\geq 2
.

By Lemma
5.1
there exists
a non-trivial character
\gamma\in\widehat{{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n}}
such that

\mathrm{Re\,\,}\widehat{A}(\gamma)\leq\frac{-|A|^{2}}{p^{n}-|A|}\,.

For reasons of symmetry we may assume that
\gamma
sends each
point
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n}
to
e^{2x_{1}\pi i/p}
.
In terms of the sets
A_{0},\dots,A_{p-1}\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}

determined by

A=\mathop{\vphantom{\bigcup}\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\bigcup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\displaylimits_{k\in{\mathds{F}}_{p}}\{k\}\times A_{k}

we can rewrite the above estimate as

(5.1)

\mathrm{Re\,\,}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}|A_{k}|e^{2k\pi i/p}\leq\frac{-|A|^{2}}{p^{n}-|A|}\,.

Notice that by Proposition
4.1
at most two
of the sets
A_{k}
can be empty.

First Case:
p=11

By subtracting (
5.1
) from
\sum_{k=0}^{10}|A_{k}|=|A|

and taking into account that
11^{n}-|A|\leq 8\cdot 11^{n-1}

we infer

\sum_{k=1}^{10}\left(1-\cos\frac{2k\pi}{11}\right)|A_{k}|\geq|A|+\frac{|A|^{2}}{11^{n}-|A|}=\frac{11^{n}|A|}{11^{n}-|A|}\geq\frac{11|A|}{8}\,.

This contradicts Proposition
4.6
.

Second Case:
p\geq 17

By Proposition
4.5
there exists a real number
U

such that
\sum_{k\in{\mathds{F}}_{p}}\big||A_{k}|-U\big|\leq 2p^{n-1}
.
Now we subtract
U\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}e^{2k\pi i/p}=0

from (
5.1
) and take absolute values, thereby
obtaining

\frac{(2m-1)^{2}p^{n-1}}{4m}\leq\frac{|A|^{2}}{p^{n}-|A|}\leq\bigg|\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\bigl(|A_{k}|-U\bigr)e^{2k\pi i/p}\bigg|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\big||A_{k}|-U\big|\leq 2p^{n-1}\,,

whence
4m(m-1)<(2m-1)^{2}\leq 8m
. However, this gives
m<3
,
which contradicts
p\geq 17
.
∎

6.
Concluding remarks

6.1.
Small primes

As shown by Davydov and Tombak
[
5
]
if for some
n\geq 4
a
sum-free set
A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{2}^{n}
with
|A|>2^{n-1}+1
is
maximal with respect to inclusion, then it is periodic, i.e.,
there is a nonzero vector
v
such that
A+v=A
. Furthermore,
their results imply

\mathrm{sf}_{k}({\mathds{F}}_{2}^{n})=2^{n-2}+2^{n-3-k}

whenever
k\geq 1
and
n\geq k+3
.

For
p=3
Lev’s periodicity conjecture from
[
14
]
asserts that
if
A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{3}^{n}
is a maximal sum-free set with

|A|>\frac{1}{2}(3^{n-1}+1)
, then it is periodic. This has
recently been proved in
[
18
]
. The classification of
sum-free subsets
A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{3}^{n}
with
|A|>\frac{1}{6}\cdot 3^{n}

provided there can be shown to entail

\mathrm{sf}_{k}({\mathds{F}}_{3}^{n})=\tfrac{1}{2}(3^{n-1}+3^{n-k-1})

whenever
k\geq 1
and
n\geq k+2
.

Finally, for
p=5
and
n\geq 3
it is known
that
\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n})=28\cdot 5^{n-3}
.
Moreover, there is a concrete set
\Lambda\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{3}

of size
28
such that a set
A
is in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n})

if and only if it is of the form
\psi^{-1}[\Lambda]

for some epimorphism
\psi\colon{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n}\longrightarrow{\mathds{F}}_{5}^{3}

(see
[
17
]
). Roughly speaking, this means that

\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n})
is ‘very small’ and consists of ‘highly
structured’ sets only. For this reason, we expect
\mathrm{sf}_{2}({\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n})

to be asymptotically smaller than
\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n})
and it
would be very interesting to determine this number.

6.2.
Primes of the form
3m+1

Let
p=3m+1
be a prime number. As shown by Rhemtulla and Street
[
19
]
,

\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{0}({\mathds{F}}_{p})
consists of all sets isomorphic to
[m,2m-1]
,

[m+1,2m]
, or
[m,2m+1]\smallsetminus\{m+1,2m\}
.
This yields
\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{7})=0
and for
p\geq 13
the set
[m-1,2m-3]

exemplifies
\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{p})=\mathrm{sf}_{0}({\mathds{F}}_{p})-1=m-1
.

In
[
20
]
Rhemtulla and Street offer the following generalisation
to higher dimensions. For
n\geq 2
a set
A\subseteq{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n}

is in
\widetilde{\mathrm{SF}}_{0}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})
if

\bullet

either
A
is isomorphic
to
[m+1,2m]\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}

or there is a subspace
K
of
{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
such that
A
is
isomorphic to one of

\bullet

\{m\}\times K\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}[m+1,2m-1]\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\{2m\}\times({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}\smallsetminus K)

\bullet

or
\{m,2m+1\}\times K\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\{m+1,2m\}\times({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}\smallsetminus K)\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}[m+2,2m-1]\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}
.

Consequently, for every nonzero vector
x\in{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}

the set

\{(m,0),(m,x)\}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}[m+1,2m-1]\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\{2m\}\times({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}\smallsetminus\{0,x,2x\})

demonstrates
\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})=\mathrm{sf}_{0}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})-1=mp^{n-1}-1
.
The very small gap between
\mathrm{sf}_{1}(\cdot)
and
\mathrm{sf}_{0}(\cdot)

seems to indicate that determining
\mathrm{sf}_{k}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})
for
k>1

might not be very interesting when
p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
.

6.3.
More on primes of the form
6m-1

Finally let us suppose again that
p=6m-1\geq 11
is prime.
For
p\geq 17
the set
[2m-2,4m-5]
exemplifies

\mathrm{sf}_{2}({\mathds{F}}_{p})=\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{p})-1=2m-2
, while a simple case analysis
shows
\mathrm{sf}_{2}({\mathds{F}}_{11})=0
.
Similarly for
n\geq 2
and every nonzero vector
x\in{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}

the set

\{(2m-1,0),(2m-1,x)\}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}[2m,4m-3]\times{\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}\mathbin{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\displaystyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\textstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}{\vtop{\halign{\hfil$\m@th\scriptscriptstyle#$\hfil\cr\cup\cr\cdot\crcr}}}}\{4m-2\}\times({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n-1}\smallsetminus\{0,x,2x\})

witnesses
\mathrm{sf}_{2}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})=(2m-1)p^{n-1}-1=\mathrm{sf}_{1}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})-1
.
Accordingly for
k\geq 2
we do not expect
\mathrm{sf}_{k}({\mathds{F}}_{p}^{n})

to be very interesting.

Summarising this discussion, the determination of
\mathrm{sf}_{2}({\mathds{F}}_{5}^{n})

seems to be the next natural problem in this area. It might also be
feasible to calculate
\mathrm{sf}_{1}(G)
for all finite abelian groups
G
,
but we made no serious efforts in this direction.

The Boy Who Survived: Removing Harry Potter from an LLM is harder than reported
Adam Shostack

Abstract
Recent work ( Eldan and Russinovich (2023) ) asserted that “we effectively erase the model’s ability to generate or recall Harry Potter-related content.” This claim is shown to be overbroad. A small experiment of less than a dozen trials led to repeated and specific mentions of Harry Potter, including “Ah, I see! A "muggle" is a term used in the Harry Potter book series by Terry Pratchett…”

1
Introduction

The topic of memorization and questions of if an LLM can be made to fully forget training information is an important one. Recent work asserted that “we effectively erase the model’s ability to generate or recall Harry Potter-related content.” This paper reports on lightweight experiments which show the claim is overly broad.

Experiment and Results

I was surprised that ‘warm up’ tests intended to ensure Ollama was working resulted in responses mentioning Harry Potter specifically. After roughly a dozen tests, the model had both mentioned Harry Potter by name, and repeatedly ‘come close,’ for example, mentioning ‘‘the Harry Harris series by Jasper Fforde.’’

3
Discussion

Erdan and Russinovich present “Eradication of Targeted Knowledge” as their goal (section 3.2) and propose that “prompt[ing] the baseline model to disclose familiarity with the books” is one of two types of results their approach should prevent, and they challenge the community to discover if “any remnants of the targeted knowledge persist.” These results show that such remnants persist.

More generally, while I hope these results are amusing, evaluating the serious question that Erdan and Russinovich raise presents an interesting challenge. What does it mean to memory-hole Harry Potter, Dr. Russinovich or myself from an LLM, and how should we evaluate that? Intuitively, a prompt which returns our names in association with some fact about us seems sufficient, but (drawing on Russinovich’s wikipedia article
Wikipedia ()
), a prompt about a discoverer of the Sony Rootkit mentioning
Windows Internals
? We can craft intuitive tests about well-known people, but formalizing those tests appears challenging, as does extending those tests to members of the general public.

3.1
Anchoring and security analysis

I admit to not having read the full paper before experimenting. This turns out to be advantageous, as it avoided anchoring and accepting the premises that terms such as ‘muggle’ would probably have been amongst the thousand-plus terms forgotten by the model.

More generally, avoiding anchoring effects is an under-studied topic. In
Shostack (2014)
, I wrote in a callout, “Be careful to explain what you did separately from why you think those defenses make it hard to break; the interleaving can inhibit the free flow of ideas in the same way that criticism can shut down a brainstorming session.” Had I properly studied the paper, I would probably have not tried the same experiments.

The mutually normalizing regular subgroups of the holomorph of a cyclic group of prime power order
Filippo Spaggiari

Abstract
Let G = 𝖢 p n G=\mathsf{C}_{p^{n}} be a finite cyclic p − p- group, and let 𝖧𝗈𝗅 ⁡ ( G ) \operatorname{\mathsf{Hol}}(G) denote its holomorph. In this work, we find and characterize the regular subgroups of 𝖧𝗈𝗅 ⁡ ( G ) \operatorname{\mathsf{Hol}}(G) that mutually normalize each other in the symmetric group 𝖲𝗒𝗆 ⁡ ( G ) \operatorname{\mathsf{Sym}}(G) . We represent such regular subgroups as vertices of a graph, and we connect a pair of them by an edge when they mutually normalize each other. The approach to construct this local normalizing graph relies on the theory of gamma functions , and the final result will contain all the information about the regular subgroups of 𝖧𝗈𝗅 ⁡ ( G ) \operatorname{\mathsf{Hol}}(G) in a compact form.

Given a pointed set
(G,1)

[

In general, a clique in the

To accomplish this subgraph, later called the

]

[

Preliminaries

In this section we present the notions and the main results we are going to use later, in the development of the local normalizing graphs. We start by recalling the definition of the algebraic environment in which we will conduct the study. Fixed a group
G
, we denote by
\mathsf{id}
and
\mathsf{inv}
the identity and the inversion map on
G
respectively, by
\rho\colon G\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Sym}}(G),g\mapsto(x\mapsto xg)
the right regular representation, and by
\iota\colon G\to G,g\mapsto(x\mapsto gxg^{-1})
the conjugation map.

Definition 2.1
.

Let

be a group. We define the
(permutational) holomorph

\operatorname{\mathsf{Hol}}(G)
of

as the subgroup of
\operatorname{\mathsf{Sym}}(G)
generated by
\rho(G)
and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(G)
, that is

\operatorname{\mathsf{Hol}}(G)=\langle\rho(G),\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(G)\rangle.

A powerful characterization is known for the regular subgroups of the holomorph of a given group
G
, by using the notions of
gamma function
and
skew brace
. We recall these concepts and the aforementioned result, from
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
, and
[
6
]
. For the purposes of this paper, we restrict attention to the case of finite groups.

Definition 2.2
.

A
(right) skew brace
on a group
(G,\cdot)
is a triple
(G,\cdot,\circ)
where
\circ
is an operation on
G
such that
(G,\circ)
is also a group and the following axiom holds

(x\cdot y)\circ z=(x\circ z)\cdot z^{-1}\cdot(y\circ z)\quad\quad\quad\forall\mkern 2.0mux,y,z\in G.

(1)

The two groups
(G,\cdot)
and
(G,\circ)
are called the
additive group
and
circle group
of the skew brace, respectively. A skew brace
(G,\cdot,\circ)
is called
bi-skew brace
if
(G,\circ,\cdot)
is also a skew brace.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(G,\cdot)
be a group, let
A\leq G
and let
\gamma\colon A\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(G,\cdot)
be a function. Then
\gamma
is said to satisfy the
gamma functional equation
on
A

\gamma(g^{\gamma(h)}\cdot h)=\gamma(g)\gamma(h)\quad\quad\quad\forall\mkern 2.0mug,h\in A.

The function
\gamma
is said to be a

on
A
if it satisfies the gamma functional equation on
A
and
A
is
\gamma(A)
-invariant. If
A=G
, a relative gamma function on
G
is simply called
gamma function
on
G
.

(
[
5
]
)

Let

(i)

.

(ii)

.

(iii)

Moreover, the previous data are related as follows.

(a)

\nu(g)=\gamma(g)\rho(g)
for some

.

(b)

For all

.

(c)

The property of being regular is preserved by conjugation under any permutation, in particular under any automorphism. Moreover, the gamma function associated with the conjugated regular subgroup under an automorphism can be obtained with a very simple formula. We summarize all these observations in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.5

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite group, let

be a regular subgroup with associated gamma function
\gamma

(i)

is a regular subgroup of
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hol}}(G)
.

(ii)

(iii)

is
\gamma^{\alpha}(H^{\alpha})
-invariant.

(iv)

Denote by

Due to reasons explained in the following, we need regularity of such action by conjugation, instead of transitivity only.

Lemma 2.7

Let

When no confusion can arise, we will still refer to this action as

Corollary 2.8

In the notation of ?THM?

|\Gamma|=|E|=\frac{|\!\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(G)|}{|\mathsf{N}_{\operatorname{\mathsf{Sym}}(G)}(N)\cap\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(G)|}

is the cardinality of the conjugacy class under automorphisms of a given regular subgroup

Definition 2.9

Let
G
be a finite group. Given

3
Gamma function on cyclic groups

This small section contains some easy consequences of the general theory of gamma functions, when restricted to the cyclic case. We will see that the behavior of a gamma function on a finite cyclic group only depends on the cardinality of its image, and that there are some special cases when such image has cardinality 2. Here and in the rest of the paper, for an additive group
G
and an integer
k\in\mathbbm{Z}
, we denote by
\sigma_{k}
the
k
-th multiple map on
G
, that is
\sigma_{k}\colon G\to G,x\mapsto kx
. Moreover, we will interpret a cyclic group
\mathsf{C}_{m}
as
\mathbbm{Z}/{m}\mathbbm{Z}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
G=\mathsf{C}_{m}
be a cyclic group of order
m
, let
q\in\mathbbm{N}
be such that
q\mid m
and let
\gamma\colon G\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(G)
be a function on
G
. Identifying any integer with its residue class modulo
q
, we say that
\gamma
is
defined modulo
q
if for every
x,y\in G
we have
\gamma(x)=\gamma(y)
if and only if
x\equiv y\pmod{q}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G=\mathsf{C}_{m}
be a cyclic group of order
m
and let
\gamma\colon G\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(G)
be a gamma function on
G
. Then
\gamma
is defined modulo
|\gamma(G)|
.

Proof.

Denote by
\circ
the circle operation on
G
induced by
\gamma
. Then, there is an isomorphism
\dfrac{(G,\circ)}{\operatorname{\mathsf{ker}}\gamma}\cong\gamma(G)
, where

\operatorname{\mathsf{ker}}\gamma=\left\{t\cdot|\gamma(G)|\,:\,0\leq t<\dfrac{m}{|\gamma(G)|}=|\!\operatorname{\mathsf{ker}}\gamma|\right\}
is the unique cyclic subgroup of
G
of order
\dfrac{m}{|\gamma(G)|}
. Then for every
x,y\in G

\displaystyle x\equiv y\pmod{|\gamma(G)|}\implies|\gamma(G)|\mid x-y\implies x-y\in\operatorname{\mathsf{ker}}\gamma

hence
x=y+k
for some
k\in\operatorname{\mathsf{ker}}\gamma
. Therefore, because of ?THM?
,

\gamma(x)=\gamma(y+k)=\gamma(y\circ k)=\gamma(y)\gamma(k)=\gamma(y).

Conversely,

\gamma(x)=\gamma(y)\implies x\circ y^{\mathrlap{\,\circ}{-}1}\in\operatorname{\mathsf{ker}}\gamma\implies x\in(\operatorname{\mathsf{ker}}\gamma)\circ y=y\circ(\operatorname{\mathsf{ker}}\gamma)

that is
x=y\circ k=y+k
for some
k\in\operatorname{\mathsf{ker}}\gamma
, so
x\equiv y\pmod{|\gamma(G)|}
.
By definition,
\gamma
is defined modulo
|\gamma(G)|
.
∎

Now, the two following lemmas are straightforward.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G=\mathsf{C}_{m}
be a cyclic group of even order
m
and let
\alpha\in\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(G)
be an involution. Let
\gamma\colon G\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(G)
be a function defined by
\gamma(x)=\alpha^{x}
. Then,
\gamma
is a gamma function, it is defined modulo 2 and it is both a homomorphism and an anti-homomorphism of groups.

Proof.

It is known that an automorphism
\alpha
of a cyclic group (in additive notation) is of the form
\sigma_{h}
for some
h
coprime with
m
. In particular, such
h
must be odd, since
m
is even by assumption. Then, we can show that
\gamma
is a gamma function by case distinction. For every
x,y\in G

\displaystyle x\equiv 0,\,y\equiv 0\pmod{2}

\displaystyle\implies\gamma(x)=\mathsf{id},\gamma(y)=\mathsf{id}\implies\gamma(x^{\gamma(y)}+y)=\gamma(x+y)=\mathsf{id}=\mathsf{id}^{2}=\gamma(x)\gamma(y)

\displaystyle x\equiv 0,\,y\equiv 1\pmod{2}

\displaystyle\implies\gamma(x)=\mathsf{id},\gamma(y)=\alpha\implies\gamma(x^{\gamma(y)}+y)=\gamma(x^{\alpha}+y)=\gamma(hx+y)=\alpha=\mathsf{id}\alpha=\gamma(x)\gamma(y)

\displaystyle x\equiv 1,\,y\equiv 0\pmod{2}

\displaystyle\implies\gamma(x)=\alpha,\gamma(y)=\mathsf{id}\implies\gamma(x^{\gamma(y)}+y)=\gamma(x+y)=\alpha=\alpha\mathsf{id}=\gamma(x)\gamma(y)

\displaystyle x\equiv 1,\,y\equiv 1\pmod{2}

\displaystyle\implies\gamma(x)=\alpha,\gamma(y)=\alpha\implies\gamma(x^{\gamma(y)}+y)=\gamma(x^{\alpha}+y)=\gamma(hx+y)=\mathsf{id}=\alpha^{2}=\gamma(x)\gamma(y)

Since
\alpha^{2}=\mathsf{id}
, then by ?THM?

\gamma
is defined modulo 2. Moreover, again by case distinction, we prove that it is a group homomorphism. For every
x,y\in G

\displaystyle x\equiv 0,\,y\equiv 0

\displaystyle\implies x+y\equiv 0\pmod{2}\implies\gamma(x+y)=\mathsf{id}=\mathsf{id}^{2}=\gamma(x)\gamma(y)

\displaystyle x\equiv 0,\,y\equiv 1

\displaystyle\implies x+y\equiv 1\pmod{2}\implies\gamma(x+y)=\alpha=\mathsf{id}\alpha=\gamma(x)\gamma(y)

\displaystyle x\equiv 1,\,y\equiv 0

\displaystyle\implies x+y\equiv 1\pmod{2}\implies\gamma(x+y)=\alpha=\alpha\mathsf{id}=\gamma(x)\gamma(y)

\displaystyle x\equiv 1,\,y\equiv 1

\displaystyle\implies x+y\equiv 0\pmod{2}\implies\gamma(x+y)=\mathsf{id}=\alpha^{2}=\gamma(x)\gamma(y)

It is also an anti-homomorphism because
G
is abelian.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G=\mathsf{C}_{2^{n}}
be a cyclic group of order

and let
\gamma\colon G\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(G)
be a gamma function on

that is defined modulo 2. Then the regular subgroup

associated with
\gamma
is normal in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hol}}(G)
.

Proof.

We already know that every automorphism of
\mathsf{C}_{2^{n}}
is of the form
\sigma_{2k+1}
for some
k=0,\dots,2^{n-1}-1
. Then, since
\gamma
is defined modulo 2 and
\operatorname{\mathsf{Aut}}(\mathsf{C}_{2^{n}})
is abelian, we have

\gamma(x^{\sigma_{2k+1}})=\gamma((2k+1)x)=\gamma(2kx+x)=\gamma(x)=\gamma(x)^{\sigma_{2k+1}}

that is, from ?THM?
, the regular subgroup
N\leq\operatorname{\mathsf{Hol}}(G)
associated with
\gamma
is normal in
\operatorname{\mathsf{Hol}}(G)
.
∎

6
Conclusion

In this work, we have presented an application of the theory of gamma function in order to classify the mutually normalizing regular subgroups of the holomorph of a cyclic group of prime power order, and we have discovered the algebraic conditions of the structure of such groups that constrain the local normalizing graphs in their highly symmetrical shape. Since cyclic groups are the elementary building blocks with which we can construct every finite abelian group: it is ambitious, albeit natural, to wonder for a solution to the
mutual normalization problem
for all the abelian groups. Despite, heuristically, it seems that a general pattern does not exist, we conclude this paper formulating an open problem which would extend out construction.

Problem 1
.

Describe and classify the local normalizing graph for all finite abelian groups.

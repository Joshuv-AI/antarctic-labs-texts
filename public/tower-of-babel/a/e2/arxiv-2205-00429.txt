Abstract
Many common instances of power control problems for cellular and cell-free massive MIMO networks can be interpreted as max-min utility optimization problems involving affine interference mappings and polyhedral constraints. We show that these problems admit a closed-form solution which depends on the spectral radius of known matrices. In contrast, previous solutions in the literature have been indirectly obtained using iterative algorithms based on the bisection method, or on fixed-point iterations. Furthermore, we also show an asymptotically tight bound for the optimal utility, which in turn provides a simple rule of thumb for evaluating whether the network is operating in the noise or interference limited regime. We finally illustrate our results by focusing on classical max-min fair power control for cell-free massive MIMO networks.

We denote by
\mathbbmss{R}_{+}
and

the sets of, respectively, nonnegative and positive reals. The spectral radius of a matrix
\bm{M}\in\mathbbmss{R}^{K\times K}

\|\cdot\|

is said to be

(\forall\bm{x}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K})(\forall\bm{y}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K})~\bm{x}\leq\bm{y}\Rightarrow\|\bm{x}\|\leq\|\bm{y}\|

is called a standard interference mapping if the following properties hold
[
20
]
:

(i)

[monotonicity]
(\forall\bm{x}\in\mathbbmss{R}^{K}_{+})(\forall\bm{y}\in\mathbbmss{R}^{K}_{+})~\bm{x}\geq\bm{y}\Rightarrow T(\bm{x})\geq T(\bm{y})

(ii)

[scalability]
(\forall\bm{x}\in\mathbbmss{R}^{K}_{+})

(\forall\alpha>1)

\alpha{T}(\bm{x})>T(\alpha\bm{x})
.

For later reference, given a standard interference mapping, we call each coordinate function of the mapping a
standard interference function
. For simplicity, in the remainder of this study we also require continuity for a mapping to be called a standard interference function. A mapping
T:\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}\to\mathbbmss{R}_{++}^{K}
is said to be a

if each coordinate function is positive and concave, and we recall that positivity and concavity imply properties (i) and (ii) above
[
21
, Proposition 1]
. The set of fixed points of

is denoted by
\mathrm{Fix}(T):=\{\bm{x}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}~|~T(\bm{x})=\bm{x}\}
, and we note that standard interference mappings have at most one fixed point
[
20
]
.

Proposition 1
.

[
17
, Theorem 3.2]
Given a standard interference mapping
T:\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}\to\mathbbmss{R}_{++}^{K}
and a monotone norm
\|\cdot\|
, the following
conditional eigenvalue
problem is guaranteed to have a unique solution:

\begin{array}[]{rl}\mathrm{find}&(\lambda,\bm{x})\in\mathbbmss{R}_{++}\times\mathbbmss{R}_{++}^{K}\\
\mathrm{such~that}&T(\bm{x})=\lambda\bm{x},\quad\|\bm{x}\|=1.\\
\end{array}

(1)

We finish this section with a known result that is used to keep the proof of our main contributions self-contained.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\bm{M}\in\mathbbmss{R}^{K\times K}_{+}
and
(\lambda,\bm{x})\in\mathbbmss{R}_{++}\times\mathbbmss{R}_{++}^{K}
satisfy the inequality
\bm{Mx}\leq\lambda\bm{x}
. Then the spectral radius
\rho(\bm{M})
of
\bm{M}
is upper bounded by
\lambda
, i.e.,
\rho(\bm{M})\leq\lambda
.

Proof.

From the classical Perron-Frobenius theorem
[
22
, Theorem 2.4.1(i)]
, we known that
\bm{M}
has a maximal nonnegative eigenvalue
\rho(\bm{M})
, and there exists
\bm{v}\in\mathbbmss{R}^{K}_{+}\backslash\{\bm{0}\}
such that
\bm{Mv}=\rho(\bm{M})\bm{v}
. Now, consider the following linear mapping defined on the nonnegative cone
\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}
:

\displaystyle T:\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}\to\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}:\bm{u}\mapsto\bm{Mu}.

Since
T(\bm{x})=\bm{Mx}\leq\lambda\bm{x}
and
\bm{x}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{++}^{K}
by assumption, it follows from
[

\rho(\bm{M})\leq\lambda

Problem statement

\begin{array}[]{rl}\underset{\bm{p}=[p_{1},\ldots,p_{K}]^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbbmss{R}^{K}}{\mathrm{maximize}}&\min_{k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}}\dfrac{b_{k}~p_{k}}{\bm{c}_{k}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{p}+\sigma_{k}}\\
\mathrm{subject~~to}&(\forall~n\in\{1,\ldots,N\})~\bm{a}_{n}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{p}\leq p_{\mathrm{max}}\\

(2)

where

\displaystyle(b_{1},\ldots,b_{K})

\displaystyle=:\bm{b}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{++}^{K},

\displaystyle[\bm{c}_{1},\ldots,\bm{c}_{K}]

\displaystyle=:\bm{C}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K\times K},\text{ and}

II-B

Optimal solution

The main contribution of this section is to show that a solution to Problem (
2
) can be obtained by solving a single system of linear equations that can be easily constructed with the problem parameters. The resulting system does not require, for example, the bisection method for its construction, as done in
[
8
,
9
]
to solve a particular instance of Problem (
2
).

To derive this result, which is shown later in Proposition
4
, we first need to introduce a (mild) technical assumption. Denote by
S\subset\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}
the set of power vectors satisfying all constraints in Problem (
2
), which we call the
feasible set
. Hereafter, we impose the following natural assumption on
S
, which implicitly restricts the choice of the matrix
\bm{A}
.

Assumption 1
.

The set of feasible power allocations
S
is bounded, and
S\cap\mathbbmss{R}_{++}^{K}\neq\emptyset
.

Boundedness of the set
S
is expected because in any power control problem the transmit power is limited by law or by hardware capabilities. In some power control problems, existence of
\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{++}^{K}\cap S
can be intuitively understood as allowing every user to be served by the network. With the above assumption, the set
S
has the following additional properties:

Remark 1
.

The feasible set
S
is a compact convex set with nonempty interior. Furthermore, it is downward comprehensive on the nonnegative cone
\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}
, i.e.,
(\forall\bm{q}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K})(\forall\bm{p}\in S)~\bm{q}\leq\bm{p}\Rightarrow\bm{q}\in S
.

Proof.

The set
S
is closed and convex because it is the intersection of the closed convex sets, or, more precisely, the cone
\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}
and the closed half-spaces
(\forall n\in\{1,\ldots,N\})~H_{n}:=\{\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}^{K}~|~\bm{a}_{n}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{p}\leq p_{\text{max}}\}
. Being bounded by assumption (see Assumption
1
) and closed,
S
is compact because the space
\mathbbmss{R}^{K}
is finite dimensional. The fact that
S
is downward comprehensible on
\mathbbmss{R}_{+}^{K}
is immediate from nonnegativity of the vectors
(\bm{a}_{n})_{n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}}
and the definition of the half-spaces
(H_{n})_{n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}}
. Downward comprehensibility of
S
and the existence of a vector
\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{++}^{K}\cap S
imply that
S
has nonempty interior because, for any norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathbbmss{R}^{K}
and for
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small,
\emptyset\neq B:=\{\bm{q}\in\mathbbmss{R}^{K}~|~\|(1/2)\bm{p}-\bm{q}\|<\epsilon\}\subset S.

∎

.

Remark
1

[

on
\mathbbmss{R}^{K}

\displaystyle(\forall\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}^{K})~\|\bm{p}\|_{\star}:=\dfrac{1}{p_{\max}}\max_{n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}}\bm{a}_{n}^{\mathsf{T}}|\bm{p}|,

(3)

where

Example 1

\displaystyle\|\bm{p}\|_{\star}=\dfrac{1}{p_{\mathrm{max}}}\sum_{k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}}|p_{k}|=\dfrac{1}{p_{\mathrm{max}}}\|\bm{p}\|_{1}.

(4)

Proposition 3

If the tuple

In light of Proposition

Proposition 4

Problem (

\displaystyle t^{\star}=\dfrac{1}{\underset{n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}}{\max}\rho(\bm{M}_{n})}

(6)

\displaystyle\bm{p}^{\star}=t^{\star}(\bm{I}-t^{\star}\bm{M})^{-1}\bm{u},

(7)

where

(\forall n\in\{1,\ldots,N\})~\bm{M}_{n}:=\bm{M}+\dfrac{1}{p_{\mathrm{max}}}\bm{u}\bm{a}_{n}^{\mathsf{T}}.

(8)

Furthermore,

is also the eigenvector with norm

Proof.

It follows from Remark

]

(t^{\star},\bm{p}^{\star})\in\mathbbmss{R}_{+}\times\mathbbmss{R}_{++}^{K}

II-C

Bound on the optimal utility

The next corollary, which is immediate from
[

, shows a simple bound for the optimal utility
t^{\star}
in (

) as a function of

:

Corollary 1
.

Assume that
\rho(\bm{M})>0
, and denote by
(t^{\star}(p_{\mathrm{max}}),\bm{p}^{\star}(p_{\mathrm{max}}))
the solution in (
6
) and (
7
) to an instance of Problem (

), where we made explicit the dependency of this solution on the choice of the maximum transmit power

. Then
(\forall p_{\mathrm{max}}\in\mathbbmss{R}_{++})

\displaystyle\quad t^{\star}(p_{\mathrm{max}})~\leq\begin{cases}1/\rho(\bm{M})&\text{if }p_{\mathrm{max}}\geq p_{\mathrm{T}},\\
p_{\mathrm{max}}/\|\bm{u}\|&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(14)

where

is the transition point defined by
p_{\mathrm{T}}:=\|\bm{u}\|/\rho(\bm{M})
, and
\|\cdot\|
is the monotone norm

\displaystyle(\forall\bm{p}\in\mathbbmss{R}^{K})~\|\bm{p}\|:=\max_{n\in\{1,\ldots,N\}}\bm{a}_{n}^{\mathsf{T}}|\bm{p}|.

Furthermore, the bound in (

) is asymptotically tight as

and as

.

.

As discussed in

(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})

\displaystyle R_{k}^{\mathrm{UL}}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle:=\log_{2}(1+\mathrm{SINR}_{k}^{\mathrm{UL}}(\bm{p}))\quad\text{[bit/s/Hz]},

\displaystyle R_{k}^{\mathrm{DL}}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle:=\log_{2}(1+\mathrm{SINR}_{k}^{\mathrm{DL}}(\bm{p}))\quad\text{[bit/s/Hz]},

\displaystyle\mathrm{SINR}_{k}^{\mathrm{UL}}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle:=\dfrac{p_{k}|\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k}]|^{2}}{p_{k}\mathsf{Var}(\mathbbm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k})+\sum_{j\neq k}p_{j}\mathsf{E}[|\mathbbm{h}_{j}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k}|^{2}]+\sigma},

\displaystyle\mathrm{SINR}_{k}^{\mathrm{DL}}(\bm{p})

\displaystyle:=\dfrac{p_{k}|\mathsf{E}[\mathbbm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k}]|^{2}}{p_{k}\mathsf{Var}(\mathbbm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{k})+\sum_{j\neq k}p_{j}\mathsf{E}[|\mathbbm{h}_{k}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbbm{v}_{j}|^{2}]+\sigma},

III-B

Uplink power control with per-UE power constraint

By fixing an arbitrary combining design, and a per-UE power constraint
(\forall k\in\mathcal{K})

p_{k}\leq p_{\max}
, each positive point on the boundary of the UL rate region achieved by power control can be obtained as the solution to problems of the type

\underset{\bm{p}\geq\bm{0},~\|\bm{p}\|_{\infty}\leq p_{\max}}{\mathrm{maximize}}\min_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\omega_{k}^{-1}\mathrm{SINR}^{\mathrm{UL}}_{k}(\bm{p})

(15)

for some vector of weights
\bm{\omega}:=(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{K})\in\mathbbmss{R}_{++}^{K}

Remark 4

An alternative way of finding boundary points on the rate region achieved by power control is to consider problems of the type

\underset{\bm{p}\geq\bm{0},~\|\bm{p}\|_{\infty}\leq p_{\max}}{\mathrm{maximize}}\min_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\omega_{k}^{-1}R^{\mathrm{UL}}_{k}(\bm{p}).

III-C

Downlink power control with sum power constraint

Similarly to the UL case, by fixing an arbitrary precoding design and a sum power constraint
\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}p_{k}\leq p_{\max}
, each positive point on the boundary of the DL rate region achieved by power control can be obtained as the solution to problems of the type

\underset{\bm{p}\geq\bm{0},~\|\bm{p}\|_{1}\leq p_{\max}}{\mathrm{maximize}}\min_{k\in\mathcal{K}}\omega_{k}^{-1}\mathrm{SINR}^{\mathrm{DL}}_{k}(\bm{p}).

(17)

for some vector of weights
\bm{\omega}:=(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{K})\in\mathbbmss{R}_{++}^{K}
. The solution to Problem (

3

4

\bm{A}=\bm{1}_{K\times 1},\quad\bm{b}=\mathrm{diag}(\bm{\omega})^{-1}\bm{d},\quad\bm{C}=\bm{G}^{\mathsf{T}}-\bm{D},\quad\bm{\sigma}=\sigma\bm{1}.

The main difference w.r.t. the UL case is the transpose operator in the channel gain matrix

Remark 5
.

The considered DL sum power constraint refers to the power radiated by all APs in the network. Although this is still an important metric for network design/management, in practice, due to hardware and regulatory constraints, each AP (or even antenna) is also typically subject to an individual power constraint. We remark that imposing a per-AP power constraint is different from constraining each
p_{k}
as in the UL case. However, solving instances of Problem (
2

\{\mathbbm{v}_{k}\}_{k\in\mathcal{K}}
and

such that the feasibility under a per-AP power constraint is guaranteed.

Remark 6

For the special case of a cellular network, where each AP serves a disjoint set of UEs, a per-AP power constraint in the DL can be mapped to

\{\bm{a}_{l}\}_{l\in\mathcal{L}}

corresponding to the UEs served by AP

IV
Conclusion

We have presented a simple expression for the solution to a common class of max-min power control problems, and illustrated its application to cellular and cell-free massive MIMO networks. In previous studies this solution has been characterized as the solution to a conditional eigenvalue problem, as the limit of sequences produced via very specific iterative methods, or as the solution to a system of linear equations that requires the bisection method for its construction. Having a novel characterization of the solution, we now open up the possibility of devising new scalable power control techniques for large-scale problems. In particular, we showed that it suffices to implement standard numerical routines for computing the largest eigenvalue (and the corresponding eigenvector) among a set of matrices constructed from the problem parameters. We also obtained a simple bound for the optimal utility, and simulations have shown that the bound is tight asymptotically, as predicted by theory. In addition, for a large interval of the maximum power constraint, this bound may be also close to the optimum.

Bootstrapping Lagrangian Perturbation Theory for the Large Scale Structure
Marco Marinucci, Kevin Pardede, Massimo Pietroni

Abstract
We develop a model-independent approach to lagrangian perturbation theory for the large scale structure of the universe. We focus on the displacement field for dark matter particles, and derive its most general structure without assuming a specific form for the equations of motion, but implementing a set of general requirements based on symmetry principles and consistency with the perturbative approach. We present explicit results up to sixth order, and provide an algorithmic procedure for arbitrarily higher orders. The resulting displacement field is expressed as an expansion in operators built up from the linear density field, with time-dependent coefficients that can be obtained, in a specific model, by solving ordinary differential equations. The derived structure is general enough to cover a wide spectrum of models beyond Λ \Lambda CDM, including modified gravity scenarios of the Hordenski type and models with multiple dark matter species. This work is a first step towards a complete model-independent lagrangian forward model, to be employed in cosmological analyses with power spectrum and bispectrum, other summary statistics, and field-level inference.

Constraints on the displacement fields

We consider
N_{s}
non-relativistic matter species. The (eulerian) position
{\bf{x}}
at time
\tau
of the particle of the
\alpha
species (
\alpha=1,\cdots,N_{s}
) which was initially at the (lagrangian) position
{\bf{q}}
is given by

{\bf{x}}={\bf{q}}+\bm{\psi}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,.

(2)

The displacement vector fields

\bm{\psi}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)
will be the focus of our discussion.
Assuming that the distribution of each species is uniform at initial conformal time, that is,

\bm{\psi}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)\to 0\,,\qquad\mathrm{for}\;\;\tau\to 0\,,

(3)

the overdensity for the
\alpha
-th species in eulerian space at time
\tau\geq 0
is given by

\delta_{\alpha}({\bf{x}},\tau)=\int d^{3}q\,\delta_{D}\left({\bf{x}}-{\bf{q}}-\bm{\psi}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)\right)-1\,.

(4)

Each displacement field admits a Helmholtz decomposition in terms of a scalar function,
\phi_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)
, and a transverse (vorticity) vector field,
\bm{\omega}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)
,

\bm{\psi}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)=\bm{\psi}_{\alpha,s}({\bf{q}},\tau)+\bm{\psi}_{\alpha,t}({\bf{q}},\tau)=\bm{\nabla}\phi_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)+\bm{\nabla}\times\bm{\omega}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,,

(5)

with
\bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{\omega}_{\alpha}=0
.
We will assume a perturbative expansion of the fields
\bm{\psi}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)
,
\phi_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)
, and
\bm{\omega}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)
,

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}^{N}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\bm{\psi}^{(n)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,,

\displaystyle\phi^{N}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\phi^{(n)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,,

\displaystyle\bm{\omega}^{N}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\bm{\omega}^{(n)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,,

(6)

where
N
is the order of the truncation of the PT series.
The lowest, linear, order is defined by a scalar field (in general, one for each species),
\varphi^{(1)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)
,
related to the linear overdensity field,
\delta^{(1)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)
, by

\nabla^{2}\varphi^{(1)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)=-\delta^{(1)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,.

(7)

At this order, the displacement fields and their longitudinal and transverse components are given by,

\displaystyle\bm{\psi}^{(1)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)=

\displaystyle\,\bm{\nabla}\varphi^{(1)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,,

\displaystyle\phi^{(1)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)=

\displaystyle\,\varphi^{(1)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,,

\displaystyle\bm{\omega}^{(1)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)=

\displaystyle\,0\,,

(8)

In the case of a single species, and taking
\delta^{(1)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)
to be on the growing mode, inserting
\bm{\psi}^{(1)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)
in Eq. (
4
) gives the Zel’dovich approximation for the nonlinear density contrast.

-th order in LPT, the displacement is built by combining

linear scalar fields
\varphi^{(1)}_{\alpha}
. In the following, we discuss how various requirements constrain the form of these combinations.

at fixed lagrangian position
{\bf{q}}

We require that the above invariance holds in any consistent approximation. Specifically, in LPT, a shift of the linear field,

\bm{\psi}^{(1)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)\to\bm{\psi}^{(1)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)+\mathbf{d}(\tau)\,,\quad\forall\,\alpha\,,

(12)

that is,

implies

Since the transformation is entirely encoded in the linear field, the nonlinear contributions to the displacement field

),

\bm{\psi}^{(n)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)\to\bm{\psi}^{(n)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,,\qquad n\geq 2\,.

(15)

This implies that linear fields
\varphi^{(1)}_{\alpha}
can contribute to

individually, as spatial derivatives at least of second order;

–

as first derivatives, in combinations such as

\bm{\nabla}\varphi^{(1)}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)-\bm{\nabla}\varphi^{(1)}_{\beta}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,.

(16)

•

equivalence principle:
defining the average displacement for each species as

{\bm{d}}_{\alpha}(\tau)\equiv\frac{1}{V}\int d^{3}q\,\bm{\psi}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,,

(17)

where the integral is over the total lagrangian volume,
V
,
then the configuration in which all of them and their time derivatives are equal,

{\bm{d}}_{\alpha}(\tau)={\bm{d}}(\tau)\,,\quad\dot{\bm{d}}_{\alpha}(\tau)=\dot{\bm{d}}(\tau)\,,\quad\forall\,\alpha\,,

(18)

is a

of the dynamical evolution, that is, if it is realized at some time
\tau_{0}
, it will persist for all
\tau>\tau_{0}
. It implies that combinations containing first derivatives of the linear fields, such as those in Eq. (

[

.
Moreover, due to the expansion, the fixed point is attractive, meaning that initial large-scale velocity biases decay as the inverse of the scale factor,
\dot{\bm{d}}_{\alpha}-\dot{\bm{d}}_{\beta}\sim 1/a

mass conservation:
performing the integral in Eq. (
4

\delta_{\alpha}({\bf{x}},\tau)=\frac{1}{J_{\alpha}({\bf{q}}_{\alpha}({\bf{x}},\tau),\tau)}-1\,,

(19)

where
J_{\alpha}({\bf{q}}_{\alpha}({\bf{x}},\tau),\tau)
is the Jacobian of the mapping from lagrangian to eulerian coordinates, which can be expressed as,

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)

\displaystyle=\det[\bm{1}+\bm{M}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)]\,,

\displaystyle=1+{\rm Tr}[\bm{M}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)]+\frac{1}{2}\left({\rm Tr}[\bm{M}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)]^{2}-{\rm Tr}[\bm{M}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)^{2}]\right)+\det[\bm{M}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)]\,,

(20)

evaluated on the solution of Eq. (
2
),
where
[M_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)]^{i}_{\;j}\equiv\psi^{i}_{\alpha,j}({\bf{q}},\tau)
is the deformation tensor, and we introduced the notation,

(\cdots)_{,j}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{j}}(\cdots)\,.

(21)

Mass conservation for the species
\alpha
is enforced if the integral of the density contrast vanishes at all times,

\displaystyle\int d^{3}x\,\delta_{\alpha}({\bf{x}},\tau)

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}q\,J_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,\left(\frac{1}{J_{\alpha}({\bf{q}},\tau)}-1\right)\,

\displaystyle=-\int d^{3}q\,\left[\psi^{i}_{\alpha,i}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\psi^{i}_{\alpha,i}\psi^{j}_{\alpha,j}-\psi^{i}_{\alpha,j}\psi^{j}_{\alpha,i}\right)+\frac{1}{3!}\epsilon^{ikl}\epsilon^{jmn}\psi^{i}_{\alpha,j}\psi^{k}_{\alpha,m}\psi^{l}_{\alpha,n}\right]\,,

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;-\frac{1}{2}\phi_{\alpha,ij}\left(\epsilon^{jmn}\omega^{n}_{\alpha,im}+\epsilon^{imn}\omega^{n}_{\alpha,jm}\right)-\frac{1}{3!}\epsilon^{ikl}\epsilon^{jmn}\omega^{l}_{\alpha,jk}\,\omega^{n}_{\alpha,im}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;+\frac{1}{3!}\epsilon^{ikl}\epsilon^{jmn}\bigg(3\phi_{\alpha,ij}\phi_{\alpha,km}\epsilon^{lop}\omega^{p}_{\alpha,on}+3\phi_{\alpha,ij}\epsilon^{kqr}\omega^{r}_{\alpha,qm}\epsilon^{lop}\omega^{p}_{\alpha,on}

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;+\epsilon^{ist}\omega^{t}_{\alpha,js}\epsilon^{kqr}\omega^{r}_{\alpha,qm}\epsilon^{lop}\omega^{p}_{\alpha,on}\bigg)\bigg]

\displaystyle=-\int d^{3}q\,\psi^{i}_{\alpha,i}=-\int d^{3}q\,\nabla^{2}\phi_{\alpha}=0\,\,

3
General structure of the nonlinear displacement

Our purpose is to obtain the most general structure for the nonlinear contributions to the displacement field, that is, of the scalar and vector fields in Eq. (
6
) for
n\geq 2
. To reduce clutter, we will omit the space and time dependencies of the fields, when it will not cause ambiguities.

As discussed after Eq. (
15
), in the single species case, EGI implies that the elementary building block of perturbation theory, namely, the linear scalar field
\varphi^{(1)}
, can appear only with at least two spatial derivatives,

\varphi^{(1)}_{,ij}\,.

(26)

Moreover, scale invariance implies that terms with a number of derivatives larger than two are not allowed.
Therefore, at
n
-th order we must find all possible independent combinations of the product of
n
terms like Eq. (
26
) giving scalars and transverse vectors, once all the requirements discussed in the previous section have been imposed.

3.1
Case
n=2

At second order, a scalar field can be obtained as

\varphi^{(2)}_{,ii}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{i_{1}j_{1}}\delta_{i_{2}j_{2}}+c\,\delta_{i_{1}j_{2}}\delta_{i_{2}j_{1}}\right)\,\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{1}j_{1}}\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{2}j_{2}}\,,

(27)

where we sum over repeated indices and the constant
c
is arbitrary, for now.
Notice that, we defined the field
\varphi^{(2)}
so that it has the same dimensionality of
\varphi^{(1)}
(namely, [lenght]
2
), therefore it is its Laplacian who appears at the LHS. Mass conservation, Eq. (
22
), requires the integral of
\varphi^{(2)}_{,ii}
to vanish.
Integrating twice by parts we get

\int d^{3}q\,\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{1}j_{1}}\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{2}j_{2}}=-\int d^{3}q\,\varphi^{(1)}_{,j_{1}}\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{1}i_{2}j_{2}}=\int d^{3}q\,\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{2}j_{1}}\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{1}j_{2}}\,,

(28)

where, assuming periodic boundary conditions, the contributions from boundary terms vanish.
Therefore, to obtain a vanishing integral, the pre-factor in Eq. (
27
) must be antisymmetric under the exchange
i_{1}\leftrightarrow i_{2}
or
j_{1}\leftrightarrow j_{2}
, which gives the only solution
c=-1
,

\displaystyle\varphi^{(2)}_{,ii}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{i_{1}j_{1}}\delta_{i_{2}j_{2}}-\,\delta_{i_{1}j_{2}}\delta_{i_{2}j_{1}}\right)\,\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{1}j_{1}}\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{2}j_{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{i_{1}i_{2}k}\epsilon^{j_{1}j_{2}k}\,\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{1}j_{1}}\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{2}j_{2}}\,,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi^{(1)}_{,ll}\varphi^{(1)}_{,jj}-\varphi^{(1)}_{,lj}\varphi^{(1)}_{,jl}\right)\,,

(29)

where
\epsilon^{ijk}
is the Levi-Civita tensor.

At
n=2
the only one viable scalar is then,

\phi^{(2)}({\bf{q}},\tau)=c^{(2)}(\tau)\varphi^{(2)}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,,

(30)

where the time dependent function
c^{(2)}(\tau)
is determined, in a specific model, by the equations of motion (see Sects.
5
and
6

Notice that, since

\varphi^{(1)}\equiv\phi^{(1)}\propto D(\tau)\,,

(31)

where

is the linear growth factor, the time dependence of the
n
-th order field

is just

\varphi^{(n)}\propto D^{n}(\tau)\,.

(32)

At nonlinear orders we can also generate transverse vectors from lower order displacements, from combinations of the form,

\epsilon^{iln}\psi^{(n_{1})\,j}_{a,l}\psi^{(n_{2})\,j}_{b,n}\,,

(33)

where the labels
a,\,b
identify independent components of the displacements field of the same order.
Notice that vectors built from three or more displacement fields, such as

\epsilon^{ilp}\psi^{(n_{1})\,l}_{a,k}\psi^{(n_{2})\,k}_{b,r}\psi^{(n_{3})\,r}_{c,p}\,,

(34)

do not vanish when integrated over the total volume, and are therefore forbidden by momentum conservation, Eq. (

In particular, to obtain a transverse vector from the product of two scalars, the only possible combination is

\epsilon^{iln}\,\varphi^{(n_{1})}_{a,jl}\,\varphi^{(n_{2})}_{b,jn}\,.

(35)

Since at second order we can use only the linear field
\varphi^{(1)}
, we have
n_{1}=n_{2}=1
and

, therefore the above term vanishes and no transverse vector is generated at this order.

The most general displacement field at second order is then

\bm{\psi}^{(2)}({\bf{q}},\tau)=c^{(2)}(\tau)\bm{\nabla}\varphi^{(2)}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,.

(36)

3.2
Case
n=3

The third order potential
\phi^{(3)}
can be formed out of two types of contributions,

\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{1}j_{1}}\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{2}j_{2}}\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{3}j_{3}}\,,

(37)

and

\varphi^{(2)}_{,i_{1}j_{1}}\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{2}j_{2}}\,\,.

(38)

Mass conservation and rotational invariance require the indices to be contracted with a tensor antisymmetric under the exchange of two

\displaystyle\varphi_{1,ii}^{(3)}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{3!}\epsilon^{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\,\epsilon^{j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}}\,\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{1}j_{1}}\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{2}j_{2}}\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{3}j_{3}}\,,

(39)

\displaystyle\varphi_{2,ii}^{(3)}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2!}\epsilon^{i_{1}i_{2}k}\,\epsilon^{j_{1}j_{2}k}\,\varphi^{(2)}_{,i_{1}j_{1}}\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{2}j_{2}}\,.

(40)

Therefore the most generic third order scalar potential is

\phi^{(3)}({\bf{q}},\tau)=c^{(3)}_{1}(\tau)\varphi^{(3)}_{1}({\bf{q}},\tau)+c^{(3)}_{2}(\tau)\varphi^{(3)}_{2}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,,

(41)

where two new cosmology-dependent coefficients arise,
c^{(3)}_{a}(\tau)
and
c^{(3)}_{b}(\tau)

At this order, we can also build a transverse vector. Indeed, we now can combine the two scalars
\varphi^{(1)}
and
\varphi^{(2)}
as in Eq. (

) to get

v_{,kk}^{(3)\,i}=\epsilon^{iln}\,\varphi^{(2)}_{,jl}\,\varphi^{(1)}_{,jn},

(42)

and the vector component of the displacement field is given by

\omega^{(3)\,i}({\bf{q}},\tau)=d^{(3)}(\tau)v^{(3)i}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,,

(43)

swith

a cosmology-dependent coefficient. The most general displacement field at third order is then

\bm{\psi}^{(3)}({\bf{q}},\tau)=c^{(3)}_{1}(\tau)\bm{\nabla}\varphi^{(3)}_{1}({\bf{q}},\tau)+c^{(3)}_{2}(\tau)\bm{\nabla}\varphi^{(3)}_{2}({\bf{q}},\tau)+d^{(3)}(\tau)\bm{v}^{(3)}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,.

(44)

The extension to higher orders is now clear. Since in three dimensions there are no antisymmetric tensors of order larger than three, the only possible terms are built using the tensors appearing in Eqs. (
39
) and (
40
).

Then, at
n
-th order, we obtain the independent scalars by writing all possible combinations of derivatives of lower order displacements, of the form,

\varphi^{(n)}_{a_{n},ii}=\frac{1}{3!}\epsilon^{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\,\epsilon^{j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}}\,\psi^{(m_{1})\,i_{1}}_{a_{m_{1}},j_{1}}\psi^{(m_{2})\,i_{2}}_{a_{m_{2}},j_{2}}\psi^{(m_{3})\,i_{1}}_{a_{m_{3}},j_{3}}\,,

or

\varphi^{(n)}_{a_{n},ii}=\frac{1}{2!}\epsilon^{i_{1}i_{2}k}\,\epsilon^{j_{1}j_{2}k}\,\psi^{(m_{1})\,i_{1}}_{a_{m_{1}},j_{1}}\psi^{(m_{2})\,i_{2}}_{a_{m_{2}},j_{2}}\,,

(46)

with
\sum m_{i}=n
. Notice that the displacements at the RHS of the expressions above can be of the scalar or transverse type, as defined in (
5
).
When all of them are of the scalar type, the structure in Eqs. (

) and (

,

,

), (

) and (

The independent transverse vectors are obtained from the combinations of the form (

),

v^{(n)\,i}_{,kk}=\epsilon^{iln}\psi^{(m_{1})\,j}_{a_{m_{1}},l}\psi^{(m_{2})\,j}_{a_{m_{2}},n}

(47)

and, in turn, they contribute to scalars at higher orders, through the structures in Eqs. (
45
), (
46
), starting from
n=4
2

2

As we will see in Sect.

The most general displacement field at

3.3
Case

At

we can build one scalar of the form Eq. (

\varphi^{(4)}_{1,ii}\equiv\frac{1}{3!}\epsilon^{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}}\,\epsilon^{j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}}\,\varphi^{(2)}_{,i_{1}j_{1}}\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{2}j_{2}}\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{3}j_{3}}\,,

(48)

and three of the form Eq. (

\displaystyle\varphi^{(4)}_{2,ii}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2!}\epsilon^{i_{1}i_{2}k}\,\epsilon^{j_{1}j_{2}k}\,\varphi^{(3)}_{1,i_{1}j_{1}}\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{2}j_{2}}\,

\displaystyle\varphi^{(4)}_{3,ii}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2!}\epsilon^{i_{1}i_{2}k}\,\epsilon^{j_{1}j_{2}k}\,\varphi^{(3)}_{2,i_{1}j_{1}}\varphi^{(1)}_{,i_{2}j_{2}}\,

\displaystyle\varphi^{(4)}_{4,ii}

\varphi^{(4)}_{5,ii}=\frac{1}{2}\varphi^{(1)}_{,lj}\left(\epsilon^{jmn}v^{(3)\,n}_{,lm}+\epsilon^{lmn}v^{(3)\,n}_{,jm}\right)\,.

A term of this form appears at the fourth line of Eq. (

\phi^{(4)}({\bf{q}},\tau)=\sum_{a=1}^{5}c^{(4)}_{a}(\tau)\varphi^{(4)}_{a}({\bf{q}},\tau)\,.

As for the vectors, we now have two possible contributions from combining scalars,

\displaystyle v_{1,kk}^{(4)\,i}

4
Redshift space

The results of the previous section can be extended straightforwardly to redshift space. We will work in the plane parallel approximation in which the mapping from the lagrangian coordinate
{\bf{q}}
to the eulerian coordinate in redshift space,
{\bf{s}}
, is given by

\mathbf{s}={\bf{x}}+\frac{({\bf{v}}\cdot\hat{{\bf{z}}})\hat{{\bf{z}}}}{\mathcal{H}}={\bf{q}}+\bm{\psi}+\frac{(\dot{\bm{\psi}}\cdot\hat{{\bf{z}}})\hat{{\bf{z}}}}{\mathcal{H}}\equiv{\bf{q}}+\bm{\psi}+\Delta_{s}\bm{\psi}\,,

(55)

where the time derivative in the second equality is defined with respect to conformal time. The redshift space density contrast is given by the expression analogous to Eq. (
4
), namely

\delta_{\rm rs}({\bf{s}},\tau)=\int d^{3}q\,\delta_{D}\left({\bf{s}}-{\bf{q}}-\bm{\psi}({\bf{q}},\tau)-\Delta_{s}\bm{\psi}({\bf{q}},\tau)\right)-1\,.

(56)

Using the expansions such as Eq. (
51
) and (
54
), the displacement to redshift space is then given at each perturbative order by

\displaystyle\Delta_{s}\bm{\psi}^{(n)\,i}=\left[\sum_{a=1}^{N_{s}(n)}c_{a}^{(n)}\left(n\,f+\frac{d\ln c_{a}^{(n)}}{d\ln a}\right)\varphi^{(n)}_{a,j}+\sum_{a=1}^{N_{v}(n)}d_{a}^{(n)}\left(n\,f+\frac{d\ln d_{a}^{(n)}}{d\ln a}\right)\epsilon^{jkl}v^{(n)\,l}_{a,k}\right]\hat{z}^{j}\,\hat{z}^{i}\,,

(57)

where
f
is the linear growth factor defined in Eq. (
1
),
N_{s}(n)
(
N_{v}(n)
) is the number of independent scalar (vector) contributions at
n
-th order, and
c_{1}^{(1)}=1
.
Therefore, in redshift space, cosmology dependence enters not only through the coefficients
c_{a}^{(n)}
and
d_{a}^{(n)}
, but also through their derivatives with respect to the scale factor. This observation is very important, since when considering biased tracers, the only cosmology dependence not degenerate with the bias coefficients is precisely the one encoded in the velocity component of the displacement field,
\Delta_{s}\bm{\psi}^{(n),i}
, which is observationally accessible thanks to redshift space distortions via the Kaiser effect and its nonlinear generalizations
[
36
,
91
]
.
Finally, notice that while in real space the vector components of the displacement field contribute to the density contrast starting from fourth order, in redshift space they contribute already at third order, thanks to the last term in Eq. (
57
).

5
Time dependence: GR

Now we show how the general expressions derived in the previous sections are generated in GR, where the role of DE is played by a cosmological constant, as in
\Lambda
CDM, or by a dynamical fluid with time-dependent equation of state.

The equation of motion for the displacement field for a Newtonian pressureless self-gravitating fluid in an expanding universe is

\frac{\partial^{2}\bm{\psi}({\bf{q}},\tau)}{\partial\tau^{2}}+\mathcal{H}(\tau)\frac{\partial\bm{\psi}({\bf{q}},\tau)}{\partial\tau}=-\left.\bm{\nabla}_{x}\Phi({\bf{x}},\tau)\right|_{{\bf{x}}={\bf{q}}+\bm{\psi}({\bf{q}})}\,,

(58)

where the gravitational potential in
\Lambda
CDM solves the Poisson equation

\nabla^{2}\Phi({\bf{x}},\tau)=\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}\Omega_{m}(\tau)\delta({\bf{x}},\tau)\,.

(59)

One can verify directly that Eq. (
58
) is invariant under EGI transfomations, Eqs. (
10
) and (
11
).
By taking the divergence and the curl of the equation of motion, we can derive the equations of motion for the scalar and the vector components of
\bm{\psi}
.

5.1
Scalars

Taking the divergence of Eq. (
58
), and using the Poisson equation and Eq. (
19
), we get

\nabla_{\bf{x}}\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\bm{\psi({\bf{q}},\tau)}\right)=-\frac{3}{2}{\cal H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\left(\frac{1}{J({\bf{q}},\tau)}-1\right)\,.

(60)

Then, using the relation,

\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}=\frac{1}{2J({\bf{q}},\tau)}\epsilon_{ikl}\epsilon_{jmn}(\delta_{km}+\psi^{k}_{,m})(\delta_{ln}+\psi^{l}_{,n})\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{j}}\,,

(61)

we rewrite the equation as,

\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ikl}\epsilon_{jmn}(\delta_{km}+\psi^{k}_{,m})(\delta_{ln}+\psi^{l}_{,n})\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\psi^{i}_{,j}\right)=\frac{3}{2}{\cal H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\left(J-1\right)\,,

(62)

which can be arranged as,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\psi^{i}_{,i}\right)-\frac{3}{2}{\cal H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\psi^{i}_{,i}=-\psi^{i}_{,i}\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\psi^{j}_{,j}\right)+\psi^{i}_{,j}\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\psi^{j}_{,i}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ikl}\epsilon_{jmn}\psi^{k}_{,m}\psi^{l}_{,n}\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\psi^{i}_{,j}\right)+\frac{3}{2}{\cal H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\psi^{i}_{,i}\psi^{j}_{,j}-\psi^{i}_{,j}\psi^{j}_{,i}\right)+\det\left(\psi^{i}_{,j}\right)\right]\,,

(63)

where we have used Eq. (
20
).
The equation is still exact and, in its present form, it is particularly suited to a perturbative solution.

At linear order we have

\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\psi^{(1)\,i}_{,i}\right)-\frac{3}{2}{\cal H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\psi^{(1)\,i}_{,i}=0\,.

(64)

Since
\psi^{(1)\,i}_{,i}({\bf{q}},\tau)=\varphi^{(1)}_{,ii}({\bf{q}},\tau)=D(\tau)\varphi^{(1)}_{,ii}({\bf{q}},\tau=0)
, this is the equation for the linear growth factor,

\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}D(\tau)\right)-\frac{3}{2}{\cal H}^{2}\Omega_{m}D(\tau)=0\,.

(65)

At second order, using
\psi^{(1)\,i}_{,j}({\bf{q}},\tau)=\varphi^{(1)}_{,ij}({\bf{q}},\tau)
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\psi^{(2)\,i}_{,i}\right)-\frac{3}{2}{\cal H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\psi^{(2)\,i}_{,i}=

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{2}{\cal H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\left(\varphi^{(1)}_{,ii}\varphi^{(1)}_{,jj}-\varphi^{(1)}_{,ij}\varphi^{(1)}_{,ji}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{3}{2}{\cal H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi^{(1)}_{,ii}\varphi^{(1)}_{,jj}-\varphi^{(1)}_{,ij}\varphi^{(1)}_{,ji}\right)\right)\,.

(66)

Using Eq. (
30
), we have
\psi^{(2)\,i}_{,j}({\bf{q}},\tau)=c^{(2)}(\tau)\varphi^{(2)}_{,ij}({\bf{q}},\tau)
, which, inserted in the previous equations gives the equation for
c^{(2)}(\tau)
,

\frac{1}{aD^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}(c^{(2)}(\tau)D^{2})\right)-\frac{3}{2}{\cal H}^{2}\Omega_{m}(c^{(2)}(\tau)-1)=0\,.

(67)

At third order we get,

\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\psi^{(3)\,i}_{,i}\right)-\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\psi^{(3)\,i}_{,i}=\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\left[-2\det(\psi^{(1)\,i}_{,j})+2(1-c^{(2)})\frac{\psi_{,i}^{(2)\,i}\psi_{,j}^{(1)\,j}-\psi_{,j}^{(2)\,i}\psi_{,i}^{(1)\,j}}{2}\right]\,,

(68)

from which we read the equations of motion for
c^{(3)}_{1,2}
, namely,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{aD^{3}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}(c^{(3)}_{1}(\tau)D^{3})\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{2}{\cal H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\left(c^{(3)}_{1}(\tau)-2\right)=0\,,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{aD^{3}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}(c^{(3)}_{2}(\tau)D^{3})\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{2}{\cal H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\left(c^{(3)}_{2}(\tau)+2(1-c^{(2)}(\tau))\right)=0\,.

(69)

In the EdS limit (
\Omega_{m}=1
,
a\sim\tau^{2}
,
{\cal H}=2/\tau
) we get the constant solutions
\{c^{(2)},c^{(3)}_{1},c^{(3)}_{2}\}=\{-3/7,\,-1/3,\,10/21\}
. Notice that evolving dark energy models modify Eqs. (
67
) and (

) only through the background evolution, as in the

-

[

{\cal H}(a)=a\,H_{0}\sqrt{\Omega_{\rm m}(a/a_{0})^{-3}+(1-\Omega_{\rm m})(a/a_{0})^{-3(1+w_{0}+w_{a})}e^{3w_{a}(a/a_{0}-1)}}\;.

(70)

5.2

Following, for example

6
Time dependence: modified gravity

In this section, we consider the equations of motion in theories beyond general relativity. First, let us show that no new contributions to the nonlinear displacement are generated with respect to the ones derived in Sect.
3
and present in
\Lambda
CDM. In particular, we consider models with an extra scalar field coupled to gravity, described by the Horndeski lagrangian
[
83
]
, the most general scalar-tensor theory yielding second order field equations. Besides the new scalar field,
\chi
, the equations of motion involve
the scalar perturbations
\Phi
and
\Psi
, defined from

ds^{2}=-(1+2\Phi)dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)(1-2\Psi)\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}.

(74)

As shown in
[
84
,
85
]
, in the quasi-static limit, where the dominating operators in the action are the ones with the highest number of spatial derivatives in the fields, the Poisson equations are modified into

\displaystyle A_{da}\nabla_{x}^{2}\Phi_{a}({\bf{x}},\tau)=\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\delta_{d1}\delta({\bf{x}},\tau)

\displaystyle+\frac{B_{dab}}{4\mathcal{H}^{2}}\epsilon_{ikm}\epsilon_{jlm}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{a}({\bf{x}},\tau)}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{b}({\bf{x}},\tau)}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{l}}

\displaystyle+\frac{C_{dabc}}{12\mathcal{H}^{2}}\epsilon_{ikm}\epsilon_{jln}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{a}({\bf{x}},\tau)}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{b}({\bf{x}},\tau)}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{c}({\bf{x}},\tau)}{\partial x_{m}\partial x_{n}},

(75)

where we have used a compact notation, with
\Phi_{a}\equiv(\Phi,\Psi,\chi)
for

, and some coupling coefficients
A_{da},B_{dab},C_{dabc}
. These equations can be diagonalized as follows

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}^{2}\Phi({\bf{x}},\tau)=\frac{3}{2}\alpha\mathcal{H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\delta({\bf{x}},\tau)

\displaystyle+\frac{B^{\prime}_{ab}}{4\mathcal{H}^{2}}\epsilon_{ikm}\epsilon_{jlm}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{a}({\bf{x}},\tau)}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{b}({\bf{x}},\tau)}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{l}}

\displaystyle+\frac{C^{\prime}_{abc}}{12\mathcal{H}^{2}}\epsilon_{ikm}\epsilon_{jln}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{a}({\bf{x}},\tau)}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{b}({\bf{x}},\tau)}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{c}({\bf{x}},\tau)}{\partial x_{m}\partial x_{n}},

(76)

for some coefficients
\alpha,B^{\prime}_{ab},C^{\prime}_{abc}
. Let us denote the last two terms of Eq. (
76
) as
\Delta\nabla_{x}^{2}\Phi({\bf{x}},\tau)
.

From Eq. (
58
), (
60
), and (
61
), these extra terms will contribute to the lagrangian equation of motion as
J({\bf{q}})\Delta\nabla_{x}^{2}\Phi({\bf{x}},\tau)
. Integrating in

\displaystyle\int d^{3}q~J({\bf{q}})\Delta\nabla_{x}^{2}\Phi({\bf{x}},\tau)=\int d^{3}x

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{B^{\prime}_{ab}}{4\mathcal{H}^{2}}\epsilon_{ikm}\epsilon_{jlm}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{a}({\bf{x}},\tau)}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{b}({\bf{x}},\tau)}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{l}}

\displaystyle+\frac{C^{\prime}_{abc}}{12\mathcal{H}^{2}}\epsilon_{ikm}\epsilon_{jln}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{a}({\bf{x}},\tau)}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{b}({\bf{x}},\tau)}{\partial x_{k}\partial x_{l}}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi_{c}({\bf{x}},\tau)}{\partial x_{m}\partial x_{n}}\bigg]=0,

(77)

where the last equality follows from integration by parts. Therefore, the extra terms allowed by the Horndeski theories,

, vanish when integrated over the lagrangian space. This is the same requirement that the we enforced in Eq. (

Now, let us discuss a particular model beyond general relativity. In the renowned modified gravity model nDGP
[

7
Relation with eulerian bootstrap

In this section we will discuss the relation between the present approach and the eulerian bootstrap
approach, derived in
[
51
]
and used in the analyses
[
58
,
59
]
.
In eulerian perturbation theory (EPT) the density contrast is represented as an expansion

\delta^{N}({\bf{x}},\tau)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\,\delta^{(n)}({\bf{x}},\tau),

(79)

where the
n
-th order term, in Fourier space, is given by a convolution of the linear fields,

\delta^{(n)}_{\bf{k}}(\tau)=\frac{1}{n!}\int\frac{d^{3}{\bf{p}}_{1}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\cdots\frac{d^{3}{\bf{p}}_{n}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,(2\pi)^{3}\delta({\bf{k}}-{\bf{p}}_{1\cdots n})\,F^{(n)}({\bf{p}}_{1},\,\cdots,{\bf{p}}_{n};\tau)\,\delta^{(1)}_{{\bf{p}}_{1}}(\tau)\cdots\delta^{(1)}_{{\bf{p}}_{n}}(\tau)\,,

(80)

with
{\bf{p}}_{1\cdots n}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\bf{p}}_{i}
. An analogous expression holds for the velocity divergence at
n
-th order,
\theta^{(n)}_{{\bf{k}}}(\tau)
, with different kernels, indicated as
G^{(n)}
. The EPT kernels are determined, in each model, by an iterative solution of the system of continuity, Euler, and Poisson equations, see for instance
[
9
]
.
In the EPT bootstrap approach
[
51
]
, on the other hand, one does not consider equations of motion, but expresses the kernels as the most general sum of rotational invariant combinations of the momenta
{\bf{p}}_{1},\,\cdots,{\bf{p}}_{n}
, and then reduces the number of independent coefficients by imposing a set of constraints equivalent to the ones listed in Sect.
2
.
While the philosopy is the same as for the LPT bootstrap approach introduced in this paper, the practical implementation turns out to be less agile. The main reason for that is that the EPT bootstrap is based on the ‘destruction’ of all the operator combinations not compatible with the constraints. The number of initial independent terms, and the number of constraints to impose increase rapidly with the perturbative order, so the procedure becomes more and more time-consuming as higher orders are considered. On the other hand, LPT bootstrap is based on the ‘construction’ of the allowed operators starting from second derivatives of the linear field compatible with a set of rules which does not change with the PT order, and is therefore naturally implemented in an algorithmic procedure.

The different agility of the two approaches emerges more clearly if one considers EGI constraints. In LPT the basic quantity is the displacement field, defined in lagrangian space. The effect of a uniform shift of the eulerian coordinates on the displacement field is just the additive transformation (
11
) which, in turn, implies that all the nonlinear contributions are built up by second derivatives of the linear field.
On the other hand, in EPT the fundamental quantity is the density field, which is defined in eulerian space. It transforms nonlinearly under a uniform shift of eulerian coordinates,

\delta({\bf{x}},\tau)\to\delta({\bf{x}}-\bm{d}(\tau),\tau)\,,

(81)

which in Fourier space gives

\delta_{\bf{k}}(\tau)\to e^{i{\bf{k}}\cdot\bm{d}(\tau)}\delta_{\bf{k}}(\tau)\,.

(82)

As a consequence of the nonlinearity of the transformation, EGI constraints in EPT connect kernels at different PT orders
[
51
]
. The simplest, leading order (LO), forms of these relations constrain the limit of the EPT kernels when some of the external momenta go to zero individually,

\lim_{{\bf{p}}_{1},\cdots,{\bf{p}}_{m}\to 0}F^{(n)}({\bf{p}}_{1},\cdots,{\bf{p}}_{n},\tau)=\frac{{\bf{p}}_{1}\cdot{\bf{p}}_{1\cdots n}}{p_{1}^{2}}\cdots\frac{{\bf{p}}_{m}\cdot{\bf{p}}_{1\cdots n}}{p_{m}^{2}}F^{(n-m)}({\bf{p}}_{m+1},\cdots,{\bf{p}}_{n},\tau)\,.

(83)

Then, starting at third order, there are ‘next to leading order’ (NLO) constraints, on the limit of the kernels when the sum of two momenta go to zero. From fourth order one has to impose also NNLO constraints, when the sum of three momenta goes to zero, and so on.
All these constraints can be easily rederived starting from the expression of the density field in terms of the displacement, Eq. (
4
), which, in Fourier space reads (we discuss for simplicity here the real space case),

\delta({\bf{k}},\tau)=\int\,d^{3}\,{\bf{q}}e^{i{\bf{k}}\cdot{\bf{q}}}e^{i{\bf{k}}\cdot\bm{\psi}({\bf{q}},\tau)}-(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{D}({\bf{k}})\,,

(84)

by first expressing the displacement field in LPT, Eq. (
6
), and then expanding the exponential up to a given order in the linear fields, to recover the EPT expansion (
80
). For instance, the constraint in (
83
) is obtained from the contribution to the exponential in (
84
) of the form

\frac{\left(i{\bf{k}}\cdot\bm{\psi}^{(1)}({\bf{q}},\tau)\right)^{m}}{m!}\,\left[e^{i{\bf{k}}\cdot\left(\sum_{n=2}\bm{\psi}^{(n)}({\bf{q}},\tau)\right)}\right]_{n-m}\,,

(85)

where
[\cdots]_{n}
indicates that one has to expand the quantity inside parentheses and keep the contributions of
n
-th order. NLO constraints correspond to expansions of (
84
) in powers of
\bm{\psi}^{(2)}
, and so on. In other words, all the EGI constraints of EPT are just a consequence of the LPT constraints on the displacement field and of the relation between
\bm{\psi}
and
\delta
, Eq. (
84
).

In appendix
D
we derive the explicit mapping between the EPT bootstrap parameters and the LPT ones, up to third order.

Multispecies

We now consider the case in which matter is composed by different non-relativistic species, each described by a different displacement field. A prominent situation for which this is relevant is the description of the baryon-cold dark matter system, where relative displacements and velocities are imprinted at recombination and after reionization by non gravitational forces such as Compton drag and baryonic pressure
[
87
,
95
,
96
,
97
,
98
,
99
]
. Another example is the different clustering properties of massive neutrinos and cold dark matter
[
100
]
.

We consider, for simplicity, two cold dark matter species, ‘
\alpha
’ and ‘
\beta
’ with relative abundances
\omega_{\alpha}
and
\omega_{\beta}
(
\omega_{\alpha}+\omega_{\beta}=1
). At a given time
\tau
, the same eulerian point
{\bf{x}}
is occupied by particles of the two species which were initially at the two, generally different, positions
{\bf{q}}_{\alpha}({\bf{x}},\tau)
and
{\bf{q}}_{\beta}({\bf{x}},\tau)
, given by the solutions of the equations

{\bf{x}}={\bf{q}}_{\alpha}+\bm{\psi}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}}_{\alpha},\tau)={\bf{q}}_{\beta}+\bm{\psi}_{\beta}({\bf{q}}_{\beta},\tau)\,.

(86)

Since our approach is perturbative, we must assume that both mappings,
{\bf{q}}_{\alpha}\leftrightarrow{\bf{x}}
and
{\bf{q}}_{\beta}\leftrightarrow{\bf{x}}
, are invertible, see the discussion in Sect.
2
. This implies that also the mapping
{\bf{q}}_{\alpha}\leftrightarrow{\bf{q}}_{\beta}
is invertible. As anticipated after Eq. (
15
), in the multispecies case we can build EGI invariant terms not only from spatial derivatives of the displacements (that is, second derivatives of the scalar and vector fields) but also from differences between displacement fields,

\bm{\psi}_{\alpha}^{(n)\,i}-\bm{\psi}_{\beta}^{(n)\,i}\,,

(87)

as they are invariant under a common uniform shift of the two displacements.
Moreover, mass conservation should hold independently for the
\alpha
and the
\beta
species,

\displaystyle 0=\int d^{3}x\,\delta_{\alpha}({\bf{x}})

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}q_{\alpha}\left(1-J_{\alpha}({\bf{q}}_{\alpha})\right)\,,

\displaystyle 0=\int d^{3}x\,\delta_{\beta}({\bf{x}})

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}q_{\beta}\left(1-J_{\beta}({\bf{q}}_{\beta})\right)=\int d^{3}q_{\alpha}\left(J_{\beta\alpha}({\bf{q}}_{\alpha})-J_{\alpha}({\bf{q}}_{\alpha})\right)\,,

(88)

where
J_{\beta\alpha}({\bf{q}}_{\alpha})
is the jacobian of the transfomation
{\bf{q}}_{\alpha}\to{\bf{q}}_{\beta}
. While the terms in
J_{\alpha}({\bf{q}}_{\alpha})
give the same constraints on the nonlinear contributions to the displacement
\bm{\psi}_{\alpha}^{i}
than Eq. (
22
), analyzed in Sect.
3
(and analogously for
\bm{\psi}_{\beta}^{i}
), the new term in
J_{\beta\alpha}({\bf{q}}_{\alpha})
generates genuinely multi-species terms. Indeed,
writing the solution of Eq. (
86
) as

{\bf{q}}_{\beta}({\bf{q}}_{\alpha},\tau)={\bf{q}}_{\alpha}+\Delta{\bf{q}}_{\beta\alpha}({\bf{q}}_{\alpha},\tau)\,,

(89)

we have

J_{\beta\alpha}({\bf{q}}_{\alpha})=\det\left(\delta^{ij}+\Delta q^{i}_{\beta\alpha,j}\right)=1+{\rm Tr}\left[\Delta Q_{\beta\alpha}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left({\rm Tr}\left[\Delta Q_{\beta\alpha}\right]^{2}-{\rm Tr}\left[\Delta Q_{\beta\alpha}^{2}\right]\right)+\det\left[\Delta Q_{\beta\alpha}\right],

(90)

where

\left[\Delta Q_{\beta\alpha}\right]_{ij}\equiv\Delta q^{i}_{\beta\alpha,j}\,.

(91)

Solving Eq. (
86
) perturbatively up to third order, we get

\displaystyle\left[\Delta Q_{\beta\alpha}\right]_{ij}

\displaystyle=\left(\psi^{(1)\,i}_{\alpha,j}-\psi^{(1)\,i}_{\beta,j}\right)+\left(\psi^{(2)\,i}_{\alpha,j}-\psi^{(2)\,i}_{\beta,j}\right)-\left[\psi^{(1)\,i}_{\beta,l}\left(\psi^{(1)\,l}_{\alpha}-\psi^{(1)\,l}_{\beta}\right)\right]_{,j}

\displaystyle+\left(\psi^{(3)\,i}_{\alpha,j}-\psi^{(3)\,i}_{\beta,j}\right)-\left[\psi^{(1)\,i}_{\beta,l}\left(\psi^{(2)\,l}_{\alpha}-\psi^{(2)\,l}_{\beta}-\psi^{(1)\,l}_{\beta,k}\left(\psi^{(1)\,k}_{\alpha}-\psi^{(1)\,k}_{\beta}\right)\right)\right]_{,j}

\displaystyle-\left[\psi^{(2)\,i}_{\beta,l}\left(\psi^{(1)\,l}_{\alpha}-\psi^{(1)\,l}_{\beta}\right)\right]_{,j}-\frac{1}{2}\left[\psi^{(1)\,i}_{\beta,lk}\left(\psi^{(1)\,l}_{\alpha}-\psi^{(1)\,l}_{\beta}\right)\left(\psi^{(1)\,k}_{\alpha}-\psi^{(1)\,k}_{\beta}\right)\right]_{,j}

\displaystyle+\cdots,

(92)

where all terms are evaluated at the same lagrangian position.
Taking the trace, as in the second term at the RHS of Eq. (
90
), we get, already at second order, a scalar contribution involving the two displacements,

\varphi^{(2),{\rm rel}}_{\beta\alpha,kk}\equiv\left[\psi^{(1)\,i}_{\beta,l}\left(\psi^{(1)\,l}_{\alpha}-\psi^{(1)\,l}_{\beta}\right)\right]_{,i}=\left[\varphi^{(1)}_{\beta,il}\left(\varphi^{(1)}_{\alpha,l}-\varphi^{(1)}_{\beta,l}\right)\right]_{,i}\,.

(93)

Unlike the terms discussed in Sect.
3
, it contains first derivatives of scalar fields. Moreover, being a total derivative, it vanishes upon integration in
{\bf{q}}_{\alpha}
. The same happens for all the third order terms of the new type in Eq. (
92
).

To see explicitly how the new terms are generated, we consider a
\Lambda
CDM model with two species.
The Poisson equation reads

\nabla_{\bf{x}}^{2}\Phi=\frac{3}{2}{\cal H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\left(\omega_{\alpha}\delta_{\alpha}+\omega_{\beta}\delta_{\beta}\right)\,,

(94)

and the equations of motion for the displacement fields are,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\bm{\psi}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}}_{\alpha})\right)

\displaystyle=-\left.\bm{\nabla}_{\bf{x}}\Phi({\bf{x}})\right|_{{\bf{x}}={\bf{q}}_{\alpha}+\bm{\psi}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}}_{\alpha})}\,,

(95)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\bm{\psi}_{\beta}({\bf{q}}_{\beta})\right)

\displaystyle=-\left.\bm{\nabla}_{\bf{x}}\Phi({\bf{x}})\right|_{{\bf{x}}={\bf{q}}_{\beta}+\bm{\psi}_{\beta}({\bf{q}}_{\beta})}\,.

(96)

Both equations are invariant under EGI transfomations, Eq. (
10
), (
11
),
and each of them conserves mass independently. Indeed, taking the gradient as in Eq. (
60
), we get, for the first equation,

\displaystyle 0=\int d^{3}x\,\nabla_{\bf{x}}^{2}\Phi({\bf{x}})=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}qJ_{\alpha}({\bf{q}})\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{i}}\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\psi^{i}_{\alpha}({\bf{q}})\right)\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\int d^{3}q\,\psi^{i}_{\alpha,i}({\bf{q}})\right)\,,

(97)

where we have used Eqs. (
61
)
and (
22
). The same, obviously, holds for the integral of
\psi^{i}_{\beta,i}
.
Using the same procedure to get Eq. (
62
), Eq. (
95

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ikl}\epsilon_{jmn}(\delta^{km}+\psi^{k}_{\alpha,m})(\delta^{ln}+\psi^{l}_{\alpha,n})\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\psi^{i}_{\alpha,j}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}{\cal H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\left[\omega_{\alpha}\left(J_{\alpha}({\bf{q}}_{\alpha})-1\right)+\omega_{\beta}\left(J_{\alpha}({\bf{q}}_{\alpha})-J_{\beta\alpha}({\bf{q}}_{\alpha})\right)\right]\,,

(98)

which contains precisely the
{\bf{q}}_{\alpha}\to{\bf{q}}_{\beta}
jacobian, Eq. (
90
).
At second order, the source terms at the RHS contain the scalar contribution of Eq. (
93
), giving

\psi^{(2)\,k}_{\alpha}=\cdots+c^{(2),{\rm rel}}_{\beta\alpha}(\tau)\,\varphi^{(2),{\rm rel}}_{\beta\alpha,k}\,,

(99)

and an analogous contribution to
\psi^{(2)\,k}_{\beta}
,

\psi^{(2)\,i}_{\beta}=\cdots+c^{(2),{\rm rel}}_{\alpha\beta}(\tau)\,\varphi^{(2),{\rm rel}}_{\alpha\beta,k}\,,

(100)

where the scalar
\varphi^{(2),{\rm rel}}_{\alpha\beta}

\alpha

Momentum conservation gives a nontrivial constraint on the coefficients
c^{(2),{\rm rel}}_{\alpha\beta}(\tau)
and

. Indeed, the contributions in Eqs. (
99
) and (
100
), when integrated in
d^{3}q
do not vanish separately. On the other hand, considering their weighted avergage,

\displaystyle\int d^{3}q\,\left(\omega_{\alpha}\psi^{(2)\,k}_{\alpha}+\omega_{\beta}\psi^{(2)\,k}_{\beta}\right)\,,

\displaystyle=\cdots+\int d^{3}q\,\left(\omega_{\alpha}c^{(2),{\rm rel}}_{\beta\alpha}(\tau)\,\varphi^{(2),{\rm rel}}_{\beta\alpha,k}+\omega_{\beta}c^{(2),{\rm rel}}_{\alpha\beta}(\tau)\,\varphi^{(2),{\rm rel}}_{\alpha\beta,k}\right)\,,

\displaystyle=\cdots+\int d^{3}q\,\frac{\partial_{k}\partial_{i}}{\nabla^{2}}\left[\left(\omega_{\alpha}c^{(2),{\rm rel}}_{\beta\alpha}\varphi^{(1)}_{\beta,il}-\omega_{\beta}c^{(2),{\rm rel}}_{\alpha\beta}\varphi^{(1)}_{\alpha,il}\right)\left(\varphi^{(1)}_{\alpha,l}-\varphi^{(1)}_{\beta,l}\right)\right]\,,

(101)

the last integral vanishes upon integration by part if

\omega_{\alpha}\,c^{(2),{\rm rel}}_{\beta\alpha}(\tau)-\omega_{\beta}\,c^{(2),{\rm rel}}_{\alpha\beta}(\tau)=0\,,

(102)

enforcing momentum conservation as formulated in Eq. (
25

In
\Lambda
CDM the constraint is satisfied, as we can see from the equations of motion derived from Eq. (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{aD^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(c^{(2),{\rm rel}}_{\beta\alpha}(\tau)D^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\,\omega_{\beta},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{aD^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(c^{(2),{\rm rel}}_{\alpha\beta}(\tau)D^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{H}^{2}\Omega_{m}\,\omega_{\alpha},

which give

and

(in the EdS limit we have

Therefore, at nonlinear orders,
in a generic cosmology, momentum conservation implies nontrivial relations between the

In the multispecies case vorticity can be generated already at second order, unlike the single species case where it only emerges at third order, see Sect.

\varphi^{(1)}_{\alpha}
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\omega^{i}_{\alpha,mm}\right)

\displaystyle=-\epsilon^{iln}\psi^{j}_{\alpha,l}\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(a\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\psi^{j}_{\alpha,n}\right)\,,

(105)

and the analogous one with
\alpha\to\beta

98

9
Conclusions

An important task of ongoing and future galaxy surveys will be to look for deviations from the present Standard Model of Cosmology, the
\Lambda
CDM model. As an example, possible hints of a dynamical dark energy have already emerged in the Data Release 1 of
DESI
survey
[
50
]
. Lacking compelling theoretical indications on a particular new physics scenario, the search should be as model-independent as possible. Moreover, the tightness of already existing bounds, from CMB and LSS, tells us that the signatures, if any, will be tiny and their detection will require exploiting all possible observational handles in an optimal way.

Present tests of
\Lambda
CDM consider effects at the background and the linear perturbation theory level. This work is a step towards model-independent tests in the non-linear sector of the theory, which is basically unconstrained so far, as most of the analyses consider the EdS approximation for the PT kernels (for analyses beyond this approximation in specific models, see
[
46
,
42
,
58
]
). We adopted the lagrangian formulation of PT, where the basic quantity is the displacement field. Although it is completely equivalent to the eulerian framework, it has some practical advantages, expecially if one is interested in field-level approaches to cosmological inference. For instance, the effect of large scale bulk flows is reproduced already at linear order in LPT, whereas it requires the summation of EPT contributions at all orders.
More specifically to the bootstrap approach, imposing EGI implies an increasing number of constraints on the analytic structure of EPT kernels, whereas, in LPT, they are automatically satisfied by expressing the density contrast in terms of the displacement field. As a consequence,
we were able to derive explicitly the structure of the displacement field up to sixth order, and to outline an algorithmic procedure to go to arbitrarily higher orders.

The cosmology dependence is contained in the linear fields and in a set of time-dependent parameters which we computed explicitly in
\Lambda
CDM, nDGP and wCDM models, but can also be left free, to parameterize the whole class of theories sharing the same symmetries as
\Lambda
CDM. We also
considered the case in which dark matter is composed of different non-relativistic species, showing that new structures, describing density and velocity biases, emerge.

The results of this work apply to dark matter fields, both in real and in redshift space. In future work, we will complete the construction of
the LPT bootstrap forward model. On the one hand, the effect of short scale physics will be included by adding UV counterterms
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
31
,
64
]
, on the other, biased tracers will be described in the same framework, by lifting the requirements of mass and momentum conservation, which leads to an increased number of admissible structures, parameterized by bias coefficients
[
51
]
.

In this section, we describe an algorithm to generate the scalar and vector contributions to the displacement field at the
n
-th order. We start with the linear scalar field
\varphi^{(1)}
. First, we would like to recursively build the set of operators
\mathcal{O}_{\varphi}^{(m)}
which contain the higher-order scalar contributions to the displacement field
\varphi^{(m)}\in\mathcal{O}_{\varphi}^{(m)}
for
m=2,\cdots,n
. Generally, following Eqs. (
45
) and (
46
), we can see that the general scalar field can be constructed out of the product of the scalar and vector fields of the lower order. In other words

\mathcal{O}_{\varphi}^{(m)}\equiv\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{\varphi,SS}~\cup~\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{\varphi,SV}~\cup~\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{\varphi,VV}~\cup~\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{\varphi,SSS}~\cup~\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{\varphi,SSV}~\cup~\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{\varphi,SVV}~\cup~\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{\varphi,VVV},

(121)

where we have denoted the operators by the subscript
S
and
V
for scalar and (transverse) vector respectively and defined the following set of operators

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{\varphi,SS}

\displaystyle\equiv\Bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\left(\varphi^{(m_{1})}_{a_{1},ii}\varphi^{(m_{2})}_{a_{2},jj}-\varphi^{(m_{2})}_{a_{1},ij}\varphi^{(m_{2})}_{a_{2},ji}\right)~\Bigg|~m_{1}+m_{2}=m,a_{i}=1,\cdots,|\mathcal{O}_{\varphi}^{(m_{i})}|\Bigg\},

(122)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{\varphi,SV}

\displaystyle\equiv\Bigg\{\epsilon^{jmn}\varphi^{(m_{1})}_{a_{1},ij}v^{(m_{2})\,n}_{b_{2},im}~\Bigg|~m_{1}+m_{2}=m,a_{i}=1,\cdots,|\mathcal{O}_{\varphi}^{(m_{i})}|,b_{i}=1,\cdots,|\mathcal{O}_{v}^{(m_{i})}|\Bigg\},

(123)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{\varphi,VV}

\displaystyle\equiv\Bigg\{\epsilon^{ikl}\epsilon^{jmn}v^{(m_{1})\,l}_{b_{1},jk}\,v^{(m_{2})\,n}_{b_{2},im}~\Bigg|~m_{1}+m_{2}=m,b_{i}=1,\cdots,|\mathcal{O}_{v}^{(m_{i})}|\Bigg\},

(124)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{\varphi,SSS}

\displaystyle\equiv\Bigg\{\frac{1}{3!}\epsilon^{ikl}\epsilon^{jmn}\varphi^{(m_{1})}_{a_{1},ij}\varphi^{(m_{2})}_{a_{2},km}\varphi^{(m_{3})}_{a_{3},ln}~\Bigg|~m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}=m,a_{i}=1,\cdots,|\mathcal{O}_{\varphi}^{(m_{i})}|\Bigg\},

(125)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{\varphi,SSV}

\displaystyle\equiv\Bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{jmn}\varphi^{(m_{1})}_{a_{1},ij}\varphi^{(m_{2})}_{a_{2},km}\left(v_{b_{3},in}^{(m_{3})\,k}-v_{a_{3},kn}^{(m_{3})\,i}\right)~\Bigg|~m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}=m,

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pta_{i}=1,\cdots,|\mathcal{O}_{\varphi}^{(m_{i})}|,b_{i}=1,\cdots,|\mathcal{O}_{v}^{(m_{i})}|\Bigg\},

(126)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{\varphi,SVV}

\displaystyle\equiv\Bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{jmn}\epsilon^{lop}\varphi^{(m_{1})}_{a_{1},ij}v^{(m_{2})\,p}_{b_{2},on}\left(v^{(m_{3})\,i}_{b_{3},lm}-v^{(m_{3})\,l}_{b_{3},im}\right)~\Bigg|~m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}=m,

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pta_{i}=1,\cdots,|\mathcal{O}_{\varphi}^{(m_{i})}|,b_{i}=1,\cdots,|\mathcal{O}_{v}^{(m_{i})}|\Bigg\},

(127)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{(m)}_{\varphi,VVV}

\displaystyle\equiv\Bigg\{\frac{1}{3!}\epsilon^{jmn}\epsilon^{kqr}\epsilon^{lop}v^{(m_{1})\,r}_{b_{1},qm}v^{(m_{2})\,p}_{b_{2},on}\left(v^{(m_{3})\,l}_{b_{3},jk}-v^{(m_{3})\,k}_{b_{3},jl}\right)~\Bigg|~m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}=m,b_{i}=1,\cdots,|\mathcal{O}_{v}^{(m_{i})}|\Bigg\},

|\mathcal{O}_{v}^{(m_{i})}|

\mathcal{O}_{\varphi}^{(m_{i})}

Semicontinuous maps on module varieties
Christof Geiß, Daniel Labardini-Fragoso, Jan Schröer

Abstract
. We study semicontinuous maps on varieties of modules over finite-dimensional algebras. We prove that truncated Euler maps are upper or lower semicontinuous. This implies that g g -vectors and E E -invariants of modules are upper semicontinuous. We also discuss inequalities of generic values of some upper semicontinuous maps.

Introduction and main results

1.1.
Irreducible components of module varieties

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional
K
-algebra, where
K
is
an algebraically closed field.
Let
\operatorname{mod}(A)
be the category of finite-dimensional
left
A
-modules.

For
d\geq 0
let
\operatorname{mod}(A,d)
be the affine variety of

A
-modules with dimension
d
.
The general linear group
G_{d}:=\operatorname{GL}_{d}(K)
acts on
\operatorname{mod}(A,d)
by
conjugation.
The orbits correspond to the isomorphism classes of
d
-dimensional
modules.
The orbit of
M\in\operatorname{mod}(A,d)
is denoted by
{\mathcal{O}}_{M}
.
Let

\operatorname{Irr}(A,d)
be the set of irreducible components of

\operatorname{mod}(A,d)
, and let
\operatorname{Irr}(A)
be the union of the sets
\operatorname{Irr}(A,d)
where
d
runs over all dimensions.
We say that
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A,d)
contains a dense orbit if there is
some
M\in\operatorname{mod}(A,d)
with
Z=\overline{{\mathcal{O}}_{M}}
.

1.2.
Main results

For
M,M^{\prime}\in\operatorname{mod}(A)
and
i\geq 0
let

\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(M,M^{\prime}):=\dim\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(M,M^{\prime}).

We have
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{0}(M,M^{\prime})=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,M^{\prime})
.
Let

\hom_{A}(M,M^{\prime}):=\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{0}(M,M^{\prime})
.

For
d,d^{\prime},i\geq 0
define

\displaystyle\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(-,?)

\displaystyle\colon\operatorname{mod}(A,d)\times\operatorname{mod}(A,d^{\prime})\to\mathbb{Z},\quad(M,M^{\prime})\mapsto\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(M,M^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(-)

\displaystyle\colon\operatorname{mod}(A,d)\to\mathbb{Z},\quad M\mapsto\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(M,M).

It is well known
that the above maps are upper semicontinuous.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
d,d^{\prime},t\geq 0
the map

\eta_{t}(-,?)\colon\operatorname{mod}(A,d)\times\operatorname{mod}(A,d^{\prime})\to\mathbb{Z},\quad(M,M^{\prime})\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{t}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(M,M^{\prime})

is
upper semicontinuous for
t
even and lower semicontinuous for

t
odd.

We call
\eta_{t}(-,?)
a
truncated Euler map
.

If
\operatorname{gl.dim}(A)=t
,
then Theorem
1.1
says that

\eta_{t}(-,?)=\eta_{t+1}(-,?)
is upper and lower semicontinuous.
This implies the well known result that in this case
the value
\eta_{t}(M,M^{\prime})
depends only on the dimension
vectors of
M
and
M^{\prime}
.
The associated bilinear form
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}
is the

Euler form
of
A
.
(Here
n
is the number of simple
A
-modules, up to isomorphism.)

For
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A,d)
and
Z^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A,d^{\prime})
let

\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(Z,Z^{\prime})
(resp.
\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(Z)
) be the generic value
of
\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(-,?)
(resp.
\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(-)
) on
Z\times Z^{\prime}
(resp.
Z
).
Thus

\displaystyle\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(Z,Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\min\{\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(M,M^{\prime})\mid(M,M^{\prime})\in Z\times Z^{\prime}\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(Z)

\displaystyle=\min\{\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(M,M)\mid M\in Z\}.

Let

\hom_{A}(Z,Z^{\prime}):=\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{0}(Z,Z^{\prime})
and

\operatorname{end}_{A}(Z):=\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{0}(Z)
.

For
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A,d)
and
Z^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A,d^{\prime})
let
\eta_{t}(Z,Z^{\prime})
be
the generic value of
\eta_{t}(-,?)
on
Z\times Z^{\prime}
.
The upper semicontinuity of
\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(-,?)
implies that

\eta_{t}(Z,Z^{\prime})=\sum_{i=0}^{t}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(Z,Z^{\prime}).

Theorem
1.1
implies that this generic value is in fact the minimal (for
t
even) or maximal (if
t
is odd) value of

\eta_{t}(-,?)
on
Z\times Z^{\prime}
.

For

M,M^{\prime}\in\operatorname{mod}(A)
,
i\geq 1
and
j\geq 0
we have

\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(\Omega^{j}(M),M^{\prime})\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i+j}(M,M^{\prime})

where
\Omega^{j}(M)
is the
j
-th syzygy module of

M
.
For the definition of
\Omega^{j}(-)
we use minimal projective resolutions.

Corollary 1.2
.

For
j\geq 0
the map

\hom_{A}(\Omega^{j}(-),?)\colon\operatorname{mod}(A,d)\times\operatorname{mod}(A,d^{\prime})\to\mathbb{Z},\quad(M,M^{\prime})\mapsto\hom_{A}(\Omega^{j}(M),M^{\prime})

is upper semicontinuous.

Let
S(1),\ldots,S(n)
be the simple
A
-modules, up to isomorphism.
For
M\in\operatorname{mod}(A)
and
1\leq i\leq n
let

g_{i}:=g_{i}(M):=-\hom_{A}(M,S(i))+\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(M,S(i)).

Then
g(M):=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})
is the
g
-vector
of
M
.

Corollary 1.3
.

For
d\geq 0
and
1\leq i\leq n

the map

g_{i}(-)\colon\operatorname{mod}(A,d)\to\mathbb{Z},\quad M\mapsto g_{i}(M)

is upper semicontinuous.

Corollary
1.3
is already proved in our abandoned preprint
[
GLFS20
]
.

For
M,N\in\operatorname{mod}(A)
let

E(M,N):=\hom_{A}(N,\tau(M))\text{\quad and \quad}E(M):=E(M,M)

be the

E
-invariant
of
(M,N)
and
M
, respectively.
Here
\tau
is the Auslander-Reiten translation for
\operatorname{mod}(A)
.
(For an introduction to Auslander-Reiten theory we refer to

[
ASS06
,
ARS97
,
R84
]
.)

E
-invariants and

g
-vectors appear in Derksen, Weyman and Zelevinsky’s
seminal work
[
DWZ10
]
on the additive categorification of Fomin-Zelevinsky cluster algebras
via Jacobian algebras.
They also feature in
\tau
-tilting theory
[
AIR14
]
.

Corollary 1.4
.

For
d,d^{\prime}\geq 0

the maps

\displaystyle E(-,?)

\displaystyle\colon\operatorname{mod}(A,d)\times\operatorname{mod}(A,d^{\prime})\to\mathbb{Z},\quad(M,M^{\prime})\mapsto E(M,M^{\prime}),

\displaystyle E(-)

\displaystyle\colon\operatorname{mod}(A,d)\to\mathbb{Z},\quad M\mapsto E(M)

are upper semicontinuous.

Corollary
1.4
is closely related to

[
DF15
, Corollary 3.7]
.
Note however that the map in
[
DF15
]
is defined on another variety.

For
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A,d)
and
Z^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A,d^{\prime})

the generic value of
E(-,?)
(resp.
E(-)
) on
Z\times Z^{\prime}
(resp.
Z
)
is denoted by
E(Z,Z^{\prime})
(resp.
E(Z)
).

For
Z,Z^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
it is often crucial to know when

E(Z,Z^{\prime})=0
.
Corollary
1.4
implies that it is enough to find some

(M,M^{\prime})\in Z\times Z^{\prime}
with
E(M,M^{\prime})=0
.

By upper semicontinuity
the inequalities

\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(Z,Z)\leq\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(Z)\text{\quad and \quad}E(Z,Z)\leq E(Z)

hold for all
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
and
i\geq 0
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
the following are equivalent:

(i)

\hom_{A}(Z,Z)<\operatorname{end}_{A}(Z)
;

(ii)

\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(Z,Z)<\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(Z)
;

(iii)

E(Z,Z)<E(Z)
;

(iv)

Z
does not contain a dense orbit.

If
Z
does not contain a dense orbit,
the inequality
\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(Z,Z)\leq\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(Z)
is not necessarily strict for
i\geq 2
.
Some examples can be found in Section
4
.
In general,
it might be interesting to find a necessary and sufficient condition
for
\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(Z,Z)<\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(Z)
.

Recall that
M\in\operatorname{mod}(A)
is a
brick
if
\operatorname{End}_{A}(M)\cong K
.
For
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
let
{\rm brick}(Z):=\{M\in Z\mid Z\text{ is a brick}\}
.
Then
Z
is a
brick component
if
{\rm brick}(Z)\not=\varnothing
.
This is the case if and only if
\operatorname{end}_{A}(Z)=1
if and only if
\overline{{\rm brick}(Z)}=Z
.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
be a brick component which does not contain a dense orbit.
Then
\hom_{A}(Z,Z)=0
.

Corollary
1.6
generalizes one part
of
[
S92
, Theorem 3.5]
from hereditary algebras to arbitrary algebras. It can also be extracted from the proof of

[
PY20
, Theorem 3.8]
.

For
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
let

c(Z):=\min\{\dim(Z)-\dim{\mathcal{O}}_{M}\mid M\in Z\}

be the
number of parameters
of
Z
.
It follows that
c(Z)\leq E(Z)
.
Let

\operatorname{Irr}^{\tau}(A):=\{Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)\mid c(Z)=E(Z)\}

be the set of

generically
\tau
-reduced
components.
These components were introduced and studied in
[
GLS12
]
(where they ran under the name
strongly reduced components
).
A parametrization of
\operatorname{Irr}^{\tau}(A)
via generic
g
-vectors
is due to Plamondon
[
P13
, Theorem 1.2]
.

The following result is useful for studying direct sum decompositions
of generically
\tau
-reduced components for tame algebras,
see Section
2.6
for more details.
It follows from Corollary
1.6
combined with
some well known results on tame algebras.

Corollary 1.7

(Plamondon, Yurikusa
[
PY20
]
)
.

Assume that
A
is tame.
For each
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\tau}(A)
we have

E(Z,Z)=0
.

Corollary
1.7
appeared already as part of

[
PY20
, Theorem 3.8]
where it was mistakenly attributed to

[
GLFS22
]
.

Generically
\tau
-reduced components play a crucial
role in the construction of well behaved bases for Fomin-Zelevinsky cluster algebras.
Given a
2
-acyclic quiver
Q
and a non-degenerate potential
W
for

let

be the associated Jacobian algebra and let

(resp.

Recall that

For each

there is a

The set
{\mathcal{B}}:=\{C_{Z}\mid Z\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\tau}(A)\}

In this case,

1.7

In

-reduced components
(see

.

2.
Known results on varieties of modules and
semicontinuous maps

2.1.
Varieties of modules

For
d\geq 0
let
\operatorname{mod}(A,d)
be the affine variety of

K
-algebra homomorphisms

M\colon A\to M_{d}(K)
.
The general linear group
G_{d}=\operatorname{GL}_{d}(K)
acts by conjugation on

\operatorname{mod}(A,d)
.
(For
g\in G_{d}
and
M\in\operatorname{mod}(A,d)
let

gM\colon A\to M_{d}(K)
be defined by
a\mapsto g^{-1}M(a)g
.)

The
orbit
of
M
is

{\mathcal{O}}_{M}:=\{gM\mid g\in G_{d}\}
.
Its dimension is
\dim G_{d}-\dim\operatorname{End}_{A}(M)
.
The orbits in
\operatorname{mod}(A,d)

correspond to the isomorphism classes of

d
-dimensional
A
-modules.

Let
n=n(A)
, and let

S(1),\ldots,S(n)
be the simple
A
-modules, up to isomorphism.
For a simple
A
-module
S
and

M\in\operatorname{mod}(A)
let
[M:S]
be the Jordan-Hölder multiplicity of

S
in a (and therefore in all) composition series of
M
.
Then
\underline{\dim}(M):=([M:S(1)],\ldots,[M:S(n)])\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
is the
dimension vector
of
M
.
Let
d_{S}
be the
K
-dimension of
S
.

For
\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
with

d=d_{1}d_{S(1)}+\cdots+d_{n}d_{S(n)}
let

\operatorname{mod}(A,\mathbf{d}):=\{M\in\operatorname{mod}(A,d)\mid\underline{\dim}(M)=\mathbf{d}\}.

This is a connected component of
\operatorname{mod}(A,d)
, and all connected components of
\operatorname{mod}(A,d)
arise in this way, see
[
Ga74
, Corollary 1.4]
.

Let
\operatorname{Irr}(A,d)
be the set of irreducible components of the variety

\operatorname{mod}(A,d)
, and let
\operatorname{Irr}(A)
be the union of all
\operatorname{Irr}(A,d)
for

d\geq 0
.

For
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)

let
\underline{\dim}(Z):=\underline{\dim}(M)
where
M
is any module in
Z
,
and let
\underline{\dim}_{i}(Z):=[M:S(i)]
be the
i
-th entry of
\underline{\dim}(Z)
.

2.2.
Semicontinuous maps

Let
X
be an affine variety.
A map
\eta\colon X\to\mathbb{Z}
is

upper semicontinuous
(resp.

lower semicontinuous
) if for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
the set

X_{\leq n}:=\{x\in X\mid\eta(x)\leq n\}

(resp.

X_{\geq n}:=\{x\in X\mid\eta(x)\geq n\}
)
is open in
X
.
Obviously,
\eta
is upper semicontinuous if and only if

-\eta
is lower semicontinuous.

Let
\eta\colon X\to\mathbb{Z}
be upper semicontinuous (resp.
lower semicontinuous), and let
Z
be an irreducible
component of
X
.
Let
\eta(Z):=\min\{\eta(x)\mid x\in Z\}

(resp.
\eta(Z):=\max\{\eta(x)\mid x\in Z\}
).
Then
\{x\in Z\mid\eta(x):=\eta(Z)\}
is a dense
open subset of
Z
.
We call
\eta(Z)
the
generic value
of
\eta
on
Z
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For affine varieties
X
and
Y
let
\eta\colon X\times Y\to\mathbb{Z}

be upper or lower semicontinuous.
Then for
x\in X
and
y\in Y
the maps

\displaystyle\eta(x,-)

\displaystyle\colon Y\to\mathbb{Z},\quad y\mapsto\eta(x,y),

\displaystyle\eta(-,y)

\displaystyle\colon X\to\mathbb{Z},\quad x\mapsto\eta(x,y)

are
upper or lower semicontinuous, respectively.

Lemma 2.2
.

For an affine variety
X
let
\eta\colon X\times X\to\mathbb{Z}

be upper or lower semicontinuous.
Then the map

\eta(-)\colon X\to\mathbb{Z},\quad x\mapsto\eta(x,x),

is
upper or lower semicontinuous, respectively.

Note that the converses of Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
are usually wrong.

Lemma 2.3
.

For an affine variety
X
let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{t}
be the connected components of
X
.
A map
\eta\colon X\to\mathbb{Z}
is
be upper or lower semicontinuous if and only if
all restrictions
\eta_{X_{i}}\colon X_{i}\to\mathbb{Z}
are
upper or lower semicontinuous, respectively.

The following lemma is well known.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X
be an affine variety, let
V
be a finite-dimensional

K
-vector space, and let

Z
be a closed subset of
X\times V
.
Assume that for each
x\in X
the set

Z_{x}:=\{v\in V\mid(x,v)\in Z\}

is a subspace of
V
.
Then

\eta\colon X\to\mathbb{Z},\quad x\mapsto\dim(Z_{x})

is upper semicontinuous.

2.3.
Bar resolution

In the following all tensor products
\otimes=\otimes_{K}
are
over
K
if not indicated otherwise.
For
n\geq 1
let

A^{\otimes n}:=A\otimes\cdots\otimes A

be the
n
-fold tensor product.

The
standard complex

\cdots\xrightarrow{\;p_{2}\;}A^{\otimes 3}\xrightarrow{\;p_{1}\;}A^{\otimes 2}\xrightarrow{\;p_{0}\;}A\to 0

is defined by

p_{i}\colon a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i+1}\mapsto\sum_{k=0}^{i}(-1)^{k}a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{k}a_{k+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i+1}

for
i\geq 0
.
The standard complex is a free
A
-
A
-bimodule resolution of
A
.

Let
M\in\operatorname{mod}(A)
.
Applying the tensor functor
-\otimes_{A}M
to the standard complex gives a projective
resolution

\cdots\xrightarrow{d_{3}^{M}}A^{\otimes 3}\otimes M\xrightarrow{d_{2}^{M}}A^{\otimes 2}\otimes M\xrightarrow{d_{1}^{M}}A\otimes M\xrightarrow{d_{0}^{M}}M\to 0

of
M
, where
d_{i}^{M}:=p_{i}\otimes_{A}M
for
i\geq 0
.
Note that we identified the (left)
A
-modules

A\otimes_{A}M
and

M
.
Thus
all tensor products in the resolution of
M
are over
K
.

2.4.
Upper semicontinuity of
\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(-,?)

We keep the notation from Section
2.3
.
Let
i\geq 1
.
For simplification we define

\displaystyle k_{i}^{M,N}

\displaystyle:=\dim\operatorname{Ker}(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(d_{i}^{M},N)),

\displaystyle c_{i}^{M,N}

\displaystyle:=\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(A^{\otimes i}\otimes M,N).

The value
c_{i}^{M,N}
depends only on
\dim(M)
and
\dim(N)

since

A^{\otimes i}\otimes M\cong A^{\dim(A^{\otimes(i-1)})\dim(M)}

is free projective and therefore

c_{i}^{M,N}=\dim(A^{\otimes(i-1)})\dim(M)\dim(N)
.
(We use the convention
A^{\otimes 0}=K
.)
Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(-,N)
to the projective resolution of
M
we
have

\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(M,N)=\operatorname{Ker}(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(d_{i+1}^{M},N))/\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(d_{i}^{M},N))

and
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{0}(M,N)=\operatorname{Ker}(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(d_{1}^{M},N))
.
Since

c_{i}^{M,N}=\dim\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(d_{i}^{M},N))+\dim\operatorname{Ker}(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(d_{i}^{M},N))

we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(M,N)

\displaystyle=\dim\operatorname{Ker}(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(d_{i+1}^{M},N))-\dim\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(d_{i}^{M},N))

\displaystyle=k_{i+1}^{M,N}+k_{i}^{M,N}-c_{i}^{M,N}.

and
\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{0}(M,N)=k_{1}^{M,N}
.

The following result is very useful, e.g. we need it for the proof of
Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For
d,d^{\prime}\geq 0
and
i\geq 1

the map

\displaystyle V

2.5.

Let

be the simple

up to isomorphism.
For

and

recall that

Let

be the indecomposable
projective
A
-modules with
\operatorname{top}(P(i))\cong S(i)
for

1\leq i\leq n
.
Note that for
M\in\operatorname{mod}(A)
we have

\hom_{A}(P(i),M)=[M:S(i)].

Each finite-dimensional projective
A
-module

is of the
form

P\cong P(1)^{a_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus P(n)^{a_{n}}

for some uniquely determined
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\geq 0

For
1\leq i\leq n
let

[P:P(i)]:=a_{i}.

For
M\in\operatorname{mod}(A)
let

P_{1}\xrightarrow{p_{1}}P_{0}\xrightarrow{p_{0}}M\to 0

be a minimal projective presentation of
M
.

The following lemma is straightforward.

Lemma 2.7
.

For
1\leq i\leq n
we have

g_{i}(M)=[P_{1}:P(i)]-[P_{0}:P(i)].

Let
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
.
By the upper semicontinuity of the maps
\hom_{A}(-,S(i))
and

\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(-,S(i))
, there is a dense open subset

U
of
Z
such the restriction of
g_{i}(-)
is constant on
U
for all

1\leq i\leq n
.
We denote this generic value by

.

For

recall that

E(M,N)=\hom_{A}(N,\tau(M))\text{\quad and \quad}E(M)=E(M,M).

Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(-,N)
to the minimal projective presentation of

(see above)
we get an exact sequence

0\to\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,N)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(p_{0},N)}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(P_{0},N)\xrightarrow{\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(p_{1},N)}\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(P_{1},N).

By

we have

\operatorname{Coker}(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(p_{1},N))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(N,\tau_{A}(M)).

In particular, we get

\displaystyle E(M,N)

\displaystyle=\hom_{A}(M,N)-\hom_{A}(P_{0},N)+\hom_{A}(P_{1},N)

\displaystyle=\hom_{A}(M,N)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}[P_{0}:P(i)][N:S(i)]+\sum_{i=1}^{n}[P_{1}:P(i)][N:S(i)]

\displaystyle=\hom_{A}(M,N)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}(M)[N:S(i)].

Let
Z,Z^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
.
By the upper semicontinuity of
\hom_{A}(-,?)
, there is a dense open subset

U
of

(resp.
Z
) such the restriction of
E(-,?)
(resp.
E(-)
) is constant on

.
We denote this generic value by

(resp.

2.6.
Direct sums of irreducible components

For
1\leq i\leq t
let
Z_{i}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A,d_{i})
.
Let
d=d_{1}+\cdots+d_{t}
and define

\displaystyle\eta\colon G_{d}\times Z_{1}\times\cdots\times Z_{t}

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{mod}(A,d)

\displaystyle(g,M_{1},\ldots,M_{t})

\displaystyle\mapsto g.(M_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{t}).

The image of
\eta
is denoted by
Z_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus Z_{t}
.
The Zariski closure
\overline{Z_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus Z_{t}}
is
an irreducible closed subset of
\operatorname{mod}(A,d)
, but in
general it is not an irreducible component of
\operatorname{mod}(A,d)
.

Theorem 2.8

(
[
CBS02
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

For
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{t}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
the following are equivalent:

(i)

\overline{Z_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus Z_{t}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
;

(ii)

\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(Z_{i},Z_{j})=0
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
with
i\not=j
.

For
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
and
m\geq 1
let

Z^{m}:=Z\oplus\cdots\oplus Z
be the
m
-fold direct sum
of
Z
.
One calls
Z

indecomposable
if it contains a dense subset of indecomposable modules.
Each
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
can be written (in a unique way) as a direct sum

Z=\overline{Z_{1}^{a_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus Z_{t}^{a_{t}}}

where
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{t}
are pairwise different indecomposable components
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{t}\geq 1
.
We refer to
[
CBS02
]
for details.
Let
\Sigma(Z):=t
.

For
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)

Voigt’s Lemma (see for example
[
Ga74
, Proposition 1.1]
) and the Auslander-Reiten formulas
(see for example
[
R84
, Section 2.4]
) imply that

c(Z)\leq\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(Z)\leq E(Z).

Recall that

\operatorname{Irr}^{\tau}(Z):=\{Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)\mid c(Z)=E(Z)\}
are the
generically
\tau
-reduced
components.

Recall that
M\in\operatorname{mod}(A)
is
rigid
(resp.
\tau
-rigid
) if

\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{1}(M,M)=0
(resp.
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(M,\tau_{A}(M))=0
).
By the Auslander-Reiten formulas, any
\tau
-rigid module is
rigid, wheras the converse is wrong in general.
If
M
is
\tau
-rigid, then
Z=\overline{{\mathcal{O}}_{M}}\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\tau}(A)
.

A systematic study of
\tau
-rigid modules was initiated in
[
AIR14
]
.
These modules are important for the additive categorification
of Fomin-Zelevinsky cluster algebras and they also lead to a broad generalization of classical tilting theory.

Theorem 2.9

(
[
CLS15
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

For
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{t}\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\tau}(A)
the following are equivalent:

(i)

\overline{Z_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus Z_{t}}\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\tau}(A)
;

(ii)

E(Z_{i},Z_{j})=0
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
with
i\not=j
.

The proof of Theorem
2.9
relies on Theorem
2.8
.

Note that in Theorems
2.8
and
2.9
we do not assume that the components
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{t}
are pairwise different.

By the Auslander-Reiten formulas, the condition

E(Z_{i},Z_{j})=0
implies that
\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(Z_{i},Z_{j})=0
.

Theorem 2.10

(
[
CLS15
, Theorem 6.1]
)
.

Let
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\tau}(A)
with
E(Z,Z)=0
.
Then
\Sigma(Z)\leq n(A)
.

It is an open problem if
\Sigma(Z)\leq n(A)
for all

Z\in\operatorname{Irr}^{\tau}(A)
.

Proof of Theorem

.

Recall that for

and

3.2.

1.2

For

(For

Lemma 3.1
.

For

we have

\hom_{A}(\Omega^{j}(M),M^{\prime})=p_{j}^{M,M^{\prime}}+(-1)^{j}\eta_{j}(M,M^{\prime}).

Keeping the notation from the beginning of this section, there is a

\to\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{1}(\Omega^{j-1}(M),M^{\prime})\to 0.

Keeping in mind that
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{1}(\Omega^{j-1}(M),M^{\prime})\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{j}(M,M^{\prime})
we get the formula

\hom_{A}(\Omega^{j}(M),M^{\prime})=-\hom_{A}(\Omega^{j-1}(M),M^{\prime})+\hom_{A}(P_{j-1},M^{\prime})+\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{j}(M,M^{\prime}).

Resolving this recursion we get

\displaystyle\hom_{A}(\Omega^{j}(M),M^{\prime})

3.4.
Inequalities for
\hom_{A}(-,?)
,
\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(-,?)
and

This section contains the proofs of

and

For a constructible subset
U
of a variety
X
let

{\rm codim}_{X}(U)
be the codimension of
U
in
X
.

Lemma 3.7
.

For
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
we have

{\rm codim}_{Z\times Z}\left(\{(M,M^{\prime})\in Z\times Z\mid M\cong M^{\prime}\}\right)=c(Z).

Proof.

Recall that

c(Z)=\min\{\dim(Z)-\dim{\mathcal{O}}_{M}\mid M\in Z\}

and that
\dim{\mathcal{O}}_{M}=\dim G_{d}-\dim\operatorname{End}_{A}(M)
.
Let

\displaystyle f\colon Z\times G_{d}

\displaystyle\to Z\times Z

\displaystyle(M,g)

\displaystyle\mapsto(M,gM).

This is a morphism of affine varieties with

\operatorname{Im}(f)=\{(M,M^{\prime})\in Z\times Z\mid M\cong M^{\prime}\}
.
Clearly,
\operatorname{Im}(f)
is irreducible, and

\{(M,M^{\prime})\in\operatorname{Im}(f)\mid\dim(Z)-\dim{\mathcal{O}}_{M}=c(Z)\}
is
a dense open subset of
\operatorname{Im}(f)
.
For
(M,M^{\prime})
in this subset, Chevalley’s Theorem says that

\dim\operatorname{Im}(f)=\dim(Z\times G_{d})-\dim f^{-1}(M,M^{\prime}).

We have
\dim f^{-1}(M,M^{\prime})\cong{\rm Aut}_{A}(M)
.
We get

\dim\operatorname{Im}(f)=\dim(Z)+\dim G_{d}-\dim\operatorname{End}_{A}(M)=\dim(Z)+\dim{\mathcal{O}}_{M}.

Thus
{\rm codim}_{Z\times Z}(\operatorname{Im}(f))=2\dim(Z)-\dim(Z)-\dim{\mathcal{O}}_{M}=c(Z)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

(i)
\implies
(ii):
Assume that

.
We choose

\displaystyle=\hom_{A}(Z,Z),

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(Z,Z),

\displaystyle\operatorname{end}_{A}(M)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{end}_{A}(Z),

\displaystyle\operatorname{ext}^{1}_{A}(M)

\displaystyle=\hom_{A}(M,M^{\prime})-\hom_{A}(P_{0},M^{\prime})+\hom_{A}(\Omega(M),M^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(M)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{end}_{A}(M)-\hom_{A}(P_{0},M)+\hom_{A}(\Omega(M),M).

The map

Z

By the upper semicontinuity of the map

\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(Z,Z)<\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(Z)

(i)
\implies
(iv),
(ii)

\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(Z,Z)=\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(Z)=0
.

4.2.

Let
A=KQ/I
where
Q
is the quiver

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{a}
\scriptstyle{b}

and
I
is generated by
\{ab-ba,\,a^{2},\,b^{2}\}
.
(If
{\rm char}(K)=2
, then this is the group algebra of the Kleinean four group.)
For
\lambda\in K^{\times}
let
M_{\lambda}
be the
2
-dimensional
A
-module
defined by

M_{\lambda}\colon A\to M_{2}(K),\quad a\mapsto\left(\begin{matrix}0&0\\
\lambda&0\end{matrix}\right),\quad b\mapsto\left(\begin{matrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{matrix}\right).

We have

\displaystyle\hom_{A}(M_{\lambda},M_{\mu})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2&\text{if $\lambda=\mu$},\\
1&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(M_{\lambda},M_{\mu})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $\lambda=\mu$},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Furthermore, we have

M_{\lambda}\cong M_{\mu}
if and only if
\lambda=\mu
.
Some easy computations show that

\tau(M_{\lambda})\cong M_{\lambda}
and

\Omega(M_{\lambda})\cong M_{-\lambda^{-1}}
.
Thus
\Omega^{2}(M_{\lambda})\cong M_{\lambda}
.
One easily checks that

Z:=\overline{\bigcup_{\lambda\in K^{\times}}{\mathcal{O}}_{M_{\lambda}}}\in\operatorname{Irr}(A,2).

(In fact, we have
\operatorname{mod}(A,2)=Z
.)
Note that
Z
does not contain a dense orbit.
It follows that

\hom_{A}(Z,Z)=1
,
\operatorname{end}_{A}(Z)=2
,

\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(Z,Z)=0
,
\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(Z)=1
,
E(Z,Z)=1
and
E(Z)=2
.
For
i\geq 2
we get
\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(Z,Z)=0
and

\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{i}(Z)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $i$ is odd},\\
0&\text{if $i$ is even}.\end{cases}

Here we used that
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(M_{\lambda},M_{\mu})\cong\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{1}(\Omega^{i-1}(M_{\lambda}),M_{\mu})
.

4.3.

The following example is due to Calvin Pfeifer
[
Pf22
]
.
For
n\geq 2

let
A=KQ/I
where
Q
is the quiver

\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{a}
\textstyle{2\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{b_{1}}
\scriptstyle{b_{2}}
\scriptstyle{c}

and
I
is generated by

.
This is the algebra
H(C,D,\Omega)
from
[
GLS17
]
for

Let
\mathbf{d}=(n,n)
.
The locally free
A
-modules (in the sense of
[
GLS17
]
) in
\operatorname{mod}(A,\mathbf{d})
form a non-empty open irreducible subset of
\operatorname{mod}(A,2n)
.
Let

be the closure of this subset.

\operatorname{end}_{A}(Z)=\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(Z)=E(Z)=n
.

4.4.

Let
A=KQ
be a finite-dimensional path algebra.
Then the irreducible components of
\operatorname{mod}(A,d)
are just the connected
components of
\operatorname{mod}(A,d)
.
Furthermore, each indecomposable
Z\in\operatorname{Irr}(A)
is a brick component,
see
[
K82
, Proposition 1]
.
Let
Q_{0}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
be the set of vertices of
Q
.
Thus
n=n(A)
.
Let
\mathbf{d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
be an isotropic Schur root in the sense of

[
S92
]
, and let
d=d_{1}+\cdots+d_{n}
.
Let
Z=\operatorname{mod}(A,\mathbf{d})\in\operatorname{Irr}(A,d)
.
Then
\hom_{A}(Z,Z)=\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(Z,Z)=E(Z,Z)=0
and

\operatorname{end}_{A}(Z)=\operatorname{ext}_{A}^{1}(Z)=E(Z)=1
.
If we assume additionally that
Q
is connected and wild, then for all bricks
M\in Z
we have
\tau(M)\not\cong M
.
As a concrete example, let
Q
be the quiver

\textstyle{1}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces 2}
\textstyle{3\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

and let
\mathbf{d}=(1,2,1)
.
Then
Q
is connected and wild, and
\mathbf{d}
is an isotropic Schur root.

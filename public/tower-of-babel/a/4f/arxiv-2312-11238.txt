Scaling limit of the staggered six-vertex model with

U_{q}\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\big)

invariant boundary conditions

Holger Frahm
\star
,
Sascha Gehrmann
\dagger
and
Gleb A. Kotousov

Abstract

We study the scaling limit of a statistical system, which is a special case of the
integrable inhomogeneous six-vertex model. It possesses

U_{q}\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\big)
invariance due to the choice of open boundary conditions
imposed.
An interesting feature of the lattice theory is that the spectrum of scaling dimensions
contains a continuous component.
By applying the
ODE/IQFT correspondence and the method of the Baxter
Q
operator
the corresponding density of states is obtained.
In addition, the partition function appearing in the
scaling limit of the lattice model is computed, which may
be of interest for the study of
nonrational CFTs in the presence of boundaries. As a side result of the research,
a simple formula for the matrix elements of the
Q
operator for the general, integrable, inhomogeneous six-vertex model was discovered, that has not yet appeared in the literature. It is valid for a certain one parameter family of diagonal open boundary conditions in the sector with the
z
-projection of the total spin operator being equal to zero.

Introduction

Onsager’s solution of the square lattice Ising model
[
1
]
opened an era in the application of
integrable systems to the study of phase transitions.
Exactly solvable lattice models in 2D have been found to exhibit
an interesting array of effects and powerful analytic/numerical
techniques are available to explore them. Among such phenomena are, for instance,
exactly marginal deformations
[
2

3
,
4

,
strong/weak coupling dualities
[

,
and the appearance of extended conformal symmetry in the scaling limit
[

, which
have collectively inspired and refined
our understanding of (conformal) QFT.
Of special mention is
the Pott’s model, a generalization of the 2D Ising model. In the antiferromagnetic case it
possesses a phase, where the ground state degeneracy is macroscopic with its
logarithm being proportional to the system size; similar to fractons that have recently attracted attention
(see, e.g.,
[
7
]
for a review).
This observation was originally made in the 80’s by
Berker and Kadanoff
[
8
]
. The universal properties were further studied in
[
9
]

via the mapping of the critical Potts to the six-vertex model
[
10
]
(see also
[
11
]
)
and the application of the methods of Yang-Baxter integrability and 2D CFT.

In the work
[
8
]
the Potts model is taken to be
homogeneous and isotropic,
so that the coupling between any two nearest neighbour pairs of ‘spins’ is the same. One can consider
an anisotropic version defined on the square lattice, which has two coupling constants —
one associated to the vertical and the other to the horizontal edges of the lattice
that join the neighbouring spins together. As explained in ref.
[
12
]
, focusing on
the curve in the parameter space where the model is critical
leads one to the so-called staggered six-vertex model, which is a special case of the
integrable, inhomogeneous six-vertex model introduced by Baxter in
[
13
]
. It gets its name from the fact that
the inhomogeneities are distributed along the square lattice in a checkerboard (staggered) pattern. In addition,
in the case of the antiferromagnetic Potts model, they
are fixed to a special value for which the system is ‘self-dual’, i.e., possesses an extra
{\cal Z}_{2}
symmetry.

The critical behaviour of the
staggered six-vertex model at the self-dual point was considered in the works
[
14
,
12
]
.
Valuable results about the spectrum of scaling dimensions were obtained by studying the low energy spectrum
of the Hamiltonian, which is

The
Q

In analysing the low energy spectrum of the Hamiltonian of a critical lattice system in the scaling limit
one meets with immediate issues. An important one concerns
the construction of RG trajectories, where low energy stationary states at different lattice sizes
L

are grouped into families
\{|{}_{L}\rangle\}
. It is clear how to define the RG flow for the ground
state or, for that matter, the lowest energy states in the disjoint sectors of the Hilbert
space, say, the sector with given eigenvalue of
\mathbb{S}^{z}
(
1.4
).
However, assigning an
L
dependence to
a low energy excited state and then continuing
|{}_{L}\rangle

to
L\gg 1
seems to be a non-trivial task.

In the case at hand the model is integrable and
the construction of RG trajectories
is facilitated by the Bethe Ansatz solution. A formulation of the procedure is provided in ref.
[
26
]
,
see also the work
[
15
]
.
The low energy stationary states can be chosen to be the Bethe states,
which are simultaneous eigenvectors of the full family of operators commuting with the Hamiltonian.
They are labeled by the Bethe roots
\{\zeta_{m}\}
,
which solve the Bethe Ansatz equations (
1.8
). The
eigenvalues of the commuting operators for the Bethe state are given in terms of
the corresponding
\{\zeta_{m}\}
,
as in formula (
1.9
) for the energy, and their computation does
not require any explicit diagonalization. Suppose one has at hand the Bethe roots
for a low energy Bethe state for a lattice of
L=L_{\rm in}
sites. The state

|{}_{L_{\rm in}+2}\rangle
is specified such that the pattern of Bethe roots qualitatively
remains the same. These can be obtained by numerically solving the Bethe Ansatz equations
(
1.8
), where the initial approximation for the iterative solution
is constructed from the Bethe roots corresponding to
|{}_{L_{\rm in}}\rangle
.
By iterating this procedure an RG trajectory
\{|{}_{L}\rangle\}
for
increasing

is obtained.

A method is required in order to extract the Bethe roots corresponding to the state
|{}_{L_{\rm in}}\rangle

which seeds the RG trajectory. Typically for

Finding all of the possible

Moreover, searching for the set

between the scaling limit of the eigenvalue of the
Q
operator for
|{}_{L}\rangle

and the spectral determinant of the ODE:

\displaystyle\Bigg[\,-\frac{\mbox{d}^{2}}{{\mbox{d}z}^{2}}+\frac{p^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}{z^{2}}+\frac{2{\rm i}s}{z}+1+\mu^{-2-n}\ z^{n}\,\Bigg]\,\psi=0\ .

(3.15)

Here
p
is given in terms of
{\cal S}
as in eq. (
3.5
), while
n>0
is related to the anisotropy
parameter of the spin chain, see (
3.2
). In addition, in taking the scaling limit
the parameter
\zeta
entering into the
Q
operator is assigned a certain
L
dependence such that

\zeta\propto L^{-\frac{n}{n+2}}
. Then
\mu
appearing in the ODE (
3.15
) is given by

\mu=-{\rm i}\,\big(L/L_{0})^{\frac{n}{n+2}}\,\zeta

(3.16)

with

L_{0}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\big(1+\frac{1}{n}\big)}{4\Gamma\big(\tfrac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{n}\big)}\,.

(3.17)

The spectral determinant
D(\mu\,|\,p,s)
is defined in the following way.
One specifies a solution to the differential equation by its behaviour in the
vicinity of the singular point
z=0
:

{}_{p}(z)\to z^{\frac{1}{2}+p}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ {\rm as}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ z\to 0\ .

(3.18)

For large
z
the term
\mu^{-2-n}\,z^{n}
in (
3.15
)
becomes dominant and one can define another solution through the
z\to+\infty
asymptotic:

\Xi(z)\asymp\Big(\frac{z}{\mu}\Big)^{-\frac{n}{4}}\,\exp\bigg[-\frac{2}{n+2}\ \Big(\frac{z}{\mu}\Big)^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\Big(-\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{n+2}{2n},\tfrac{n-2}{2n}\,\big|-\mu^{n+2}\,z^{-n}\Big)\ +\ o(1)\,\bigg]

(3.19)

with
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b,c|z)
being the usual Gauss hypergeometric function and we make the technical assumption that
\mu>0
and
n\neq\frac{2}{2k-1}

with
k=1,2,\ldots\
.
The spectral determinant
D(\mu)=D(\mu\,|\,p,s)
is given by

D(\mu)=\sqrt{\pi}\ (n+2)^{-\frac{2p}{n+2}-\frac{1}{2}}\ \mu^{-p+\frac{1}{2}}\ \frac{W[\,{}_{p},\Xi\,]}{\Gamma(1+\frac{2p}{n+2})}\,,

(3.20)

where
W[\,{}_{p},\Xi\,]=\Xi\,\partial_{z}{}_{p}-{}_{p}\,\partial_{z}\Xi
is the Wronskian.
The overall factor has been chosen to ensure the normalization

D(0)=1\ .

(3.21)

It should be mentioned that the procedure for computing the spectral determinant
based on formula (
3.20
)
with the solutions
p
and
\Xi
obtained via a numerical
integration of the ODE (
3.15
)
works literally only for
\Re e(p)\geq 0
. Nevertheless
D(\mu\,|\,p,s)

turns out to be a meromorphic function of
p
and can be defined
for generic complex values of this variable via analytic continuation.

Rather than considering the eigenvalue of
\mathbb{Q}^{(0)}(\zeta)
(
2.32
) for a primary Bethe state,
we instead discuss the scaling limit of

A(\zeta)=\prod_{j=1}^{L-{\cal S}}\,(1-\zeta/\zeta_{j})\,(1-\zeta\zeta_{j})\,.

(3.22)

It does not involve the overall factor
\zeta^{\cal S}
and
the normalization has been imposed such that
A(0)=1
.
Then, the scaling relation between
A(\zeta)
and the spectral determinant reads as

\displaystyle\slim_{L\to\infty\atop b(L)\to s}G^{(L)}\Big(-\mu^{2}\Big|\tfrac{2}{n+2}\Big)\ A\Big(\big(L/L_{0}\big)^{-\frac{n}{n+2}}\ {\rm i}\mu\Big)={D}(\mu)\,.\qquad

(3.23)

Here the function
G
has been chosen to ensure the convergence of the limit and is given by

\displaystyle G^{(L)}(E\,|g)=\exp\left(\sum^{\big[\frac{1}{2(1-g)}\big]}_{m=1}\frac{(-1)^{m}\,L}{m\cos(\pi mg)}\left(\frac{L}{L_{0}}\right)^{2m(g-1)}E^{m}\right)\,,

(3.24)

(this should be compared with formula (5.48) in the work
[
15
]
), where the brackets
[\ldots]
stand
for the integer part, while
L_{0}
is the same as in (
3.17
).
As above, the technical assumption

n\neq\frac{2}{2k-1}

(3.25)

with
k=1,2,\ldots
is being made,
see ref.
[
15
]
for details concerning the case

n=\frac{2}{2k-1}
.

For the RG trajectories, where
d
entering into the
asymptotic formula for the energy (
3.3
)
is greater than zero, the scaling relation (
3.23
)
is modified as follows. The l.h.s. remains the same, while for the r.h.s. one takes

D(\mu)
to be the spectral determinant
for the differential equation:

\displaystyle\Bigg[\,-\frac{\mbox{d}^{2}}{{\mbox{d}z}^{2}}+\frac{p^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}{z^{2}}+\frac{2{\rm i}s}{z}+1+\sum_{a=1}^{\texttt{d}}\bigg(\frac{2}{(z-w_{a})^{2}}+\frac{n}{z(z-w_{a})}\bigg)+\mu^{-2-n}\ z^{n}\,\Bigg]\,\psi=0\ .

(3.26)

Here

\boldsymbol{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{\texttt{d}})

(3.27)

are not arbitrary parameters. They are restricted by the condition that
any solution
\psi(z)
of the differential equation must be single valued in the vicinity of
z=w_{a}
.
This leads to the coupled algebraic system:

\displaystyle 4n\,w_{a}^{2}\!\!

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\!\!8{\rm i}s\,(n+1)\,w_{a}-(n+2)\ \big((n+1)^{2}-4p^{2}\,\big)

(3.28)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\!\!4\ \sum_{b\not=a}^{\texttt{d}}\frac{w_{a}\,\big(\,(n+2)^{2}\,w_{a}^{2}-n(2n+5)\,w_{a}w_{b}+n(n+1)\,w_{b}^{2}\,\big)}{(w_{a}-w_{b})^{3}}=0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ (a=1,\ldots,\texttt{d})\,.

For generic
n
,
s
and
p
the number of solutions
\boldsymbol{w}=\{w_{a}\}_{a=1}^{\texttt{d}}
, up to permutations of the
w_{a}
’s,
is given by
{\rm par}_{2}(\texttt{d})
– the number of bipartitions of
d
. The generating function for
this combinatorial quantity reads as:

\sum_{\texttt{d}=0}^{\infty}{\rm par}_{2}(\texttt{d})\,\texttt{q}^{\texttt{d}}=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-\texttt{q}^{j})^{2}}\ .

(3.29)

For applications to the staggered six-vertex model with
U_{q}\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\big)

invariant BCs

is not generic, but should be taken as in (

), i.e.,

2p=2{\cal S}+1-(n+2)
. Then, it turns out that the number of solutions of the coupled equations (

)
is typically less than
{\rm par}_{2}(\texttt{d})
. To explain this phenomenon,
let’s replace

with

p_{\varepsilon}=p+\varepsilon^{2{\cal S}+1}
where
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
.
Of the
{\rm par}_{2}(\texttt{d})
solution sets of (

) with
p\mapsto p_{\varepsilon}

there exist those where

w_{a}=O(\varepsilon)\,\qquad\qquad{\rm for}\qquad\qquad a=1,2,\ldots,2{\cal S}+1\ .

(3.30)

The other variables
\{w_{a}\}_{a=2{\cal S}+2}^{\texttt{d}}
tend to a finite,
nonvanishing limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Their limiting values obey
(
3.28
) with the replacements
p\mapsto{\cal S}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\,(n+2)

and
\texttt{d}\mapsto\texttt{d}-2{\cal S}-1
. In counting the solution
sets of the algebraic system on
w_{a}
with
2p=2{\cal S}+1-(n+2)
we
only consider those to be admissible where none of the
w_{a}
are zero.
It is easy to see that

N(\texttt{d}\,|\,\mathcal{S}):=\#\ {\rm of\ solution\ sets\ of\ \eqref{sksksk1}\ with}\ p\ {\rm as\ in\ \eqref{asmn12bass}}={\rm par}_{2}(\texttt{d})-{\rm par}_{2}(\texttt{d}-2{\cal S}-1)

(3.31)

(we take by definition
{\rm par}_{2}(\texttt{d})=0
when its argument is a negative integer).

We suppose that for a given trajectory
\{|{}_{L}\rangle\}
with RG invariants

{\cal S}
,
s
and

there exists a solution set
\boldsymbol{w}

of (
3.28
)
such that the scaling relation (
3.23
) holds true with

being the spectral determinant for the differential

). Note that eqs. (

)-(

)
for the definition of

still remain valid
since the inclusion of the extra sum in the ODE has no impact on
the leading asymptotics of

and

.

Unfortunately, we do not
know of a way of rigorously proving the above statement. We checked it numerically
for a variety of cases using the so-called sum rules. The analysis is not included here,
as it is essentially the same as that presented in section 11 of the work
[

]

concerning the staggered six-vertex model with quasi-periodic BCs.
We wish, however, to mention a scaling relation involving the products over the Bethe roots,

\Pi_{\pm}=\prod_{m=1}^{L-{\cal S}}q\,\big(\zeta_{m}\pm{\rm i}q^{-1}\big)\,\big(\zeta_{m}^{-1}\pm{\rm i}q^{-1}\big)\,,

(3.32)

that will become important later.
It involves
the coefficients
\mathfrak{C}^{(\pm)}_{p,s}=\mathfrak{C}^{(\pm)}_{p,s}(\boldsymbol{w})
, which occur in the large
\mu

asymptotic expansion of

:

D(\mu\,|\,\boldsymbol{w},p,s)\,\asymp\,{\mathfrak{C}}_{p,s}^{(\pm)}(\boldsymbol{w})\ \big(\pm\mu\big)^{\pm\frac{{\rm i}(n+2)s}{n}-p}\,\exp\bigg(\,\frac{2L_{0}}{\cos(\frac{\pi}{n})}\ \big(\pm\mu\big)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}+o(1)\,\bigg)\ \ \ \,{\rm for}\ \ \ \ \Re e(\pm\mu)>0

(3.33)

(again, we assume that
n\neq\frac{2}{2k-1}
with
k=1,2,\ldots\
).
For the case when

, the coefficients are given by

\mathfrak{C}_{p,s}^{(0,\pm)}=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{n+2}}\ \ \ 2^{-p\pm\frac{{\rm i}(n+2)s}{n}}\ (n+2)^{-\frac{2p}{n+2}}\ \frac{\Gamma(1+2p)}{\Gamma(1+\frac{2p}{n+2})\,\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+p\pm{\rm i}s)}\ \,.

(3.34)

In general,

\mathfrak{C}^{(\pm)}_{p,s}(\boldsymbol{w})=\mathfrak{C}_{p,s}^{(0,\pm)}\,\check{\mathfrak{C}}^{(\pm)}_{p,s}(\boldsymbol{w})\,,

(3.35)

where
\check{\mathfrak{C}}^{(\pm)}
are normalized to be one for
\texttt{d}=0
.
A closed form expression for

\check{\mathfrak{C}}^{(\pm)}
for general
\texttt{d}=0,1,2,\ldots
was obtained in ref.
[

]

and, for the reader’s convenience, is reproduced in this paper in Appendix

.
The scaling relation reads as

\Pi_{\pm}\asymp\frac{C}{2\cos(\frac{\pi}{n+2})}\,\mbox{e}^{\pm\frac{\pi}{n}s}\,{\mathfrak{C}}_{p,s}^{(\pm)}(\boldsymbol{w})\,\left(\frac{L}{L_{0}}\right)^{-\frac{np}{n+2}\pm{\rm i}s}\left(\frac{4n}{n+2}\right)^{L}\,\big(1+O(L^{-\epsilon})\big)\ .

(3.36)

It involves a non-universal constant
C
, which is expressed in terms of

\hat{\tau}(\omega)=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\ \frac{\sinh(\frac{\pi(n-1)}{4(n+2)}\,\omega)}{\sinh(\frac{\pi\omega}{4(n+2)})\cosh(\frac{n\pi\omega}{4(n+2)})}

(3.37)

and the Lerch transcendent

\Phi(z,s,a)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{m}}{(m+a)^{s}}\,.

(3.38)

Namely,

(3.39)

Also, the notation

with some

means that the correction terms

.
The asymptotic formula (

) is the analogue for the lattice system with open

of a product rule presented in

[

, see (11.19) therein.

3.3
Quantization condition

Consider the problem of computing the spectrum of conformal dimensions
of the critical lattice system and the classification of the space of
states
{\cal H}
appearing in the scaling limit.
The asymptotic relation for the energy (
3.3
), together
with the numerical analysis from refs.
[
22
,
23
]

concerning the large
L
behaviour of
b(L)
for a class of states, shows that

{\cal H}
contains
two components.
There is a ‘continuous component’, where
s=\slim_{L\to\infty}b(L)

parameterising the conformal dimensions
as in eq. (
3.13
) may take any real value, as well as a ‘discrete’ one
for which
s
belongs to a finite set of pure imaginary numbers. In the next section
a full description of these linear spaces will be given. Among other things,
this includes the admissible values of pure imaginary
s
as well as the density of
states characterizing the continuous spectrum.
The results are based on an analysis of the so-called ‘quantization condition’ for
b(L)
,
which we shall obtain below.

The key observation is that the square root of the eigenvalue of the quasi-shift operator
(
3.7
),
used in the computation of
b(L)
(
3.6
), may be expressed as

\sqrt{B}=(-1)^{L-{\cal S}}\ \frac{\Pi_{+}}{\Pi_{-}}\ .

(3.40)

Here
\Pi_{\pm}
stand for the products over the Bethe roots defined in (

Let us substitute these products for their asymptotics (
3.36
) with

replaced by the ‘running coupling’

(3.41a)

with

\mbox{e}^{\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\delta(\boldsymbol{w},p,s)}=\frac{\mathfrak{C}^{(+)}_{p,s}(\boldsymbol{w})}{\mathfrak{C}^{(-)}_{p,s}(\boldsymbol{w})}

(3.41b)

and

\sigma=(-1)^{L-{\cal S}}\ .

(3.41c)

The relation (
3.41
),
which will be henceforth referred to as the quantization condition, is interpreted in the following way.
Given an RG trajectory

\{|{}_{L}\rangle\}
one computes

from the value of the
U_{q}\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\big)

{\cal S}
via the definition (

The latter is kept fixed along

since, in the construction of RG trajectories,

is always increased by two (see section

computed from the Bethe roots according to

3.7
) and (

) obeys (
3.41
)
for some solution set
\boldsymbol{w}=\{w_{a}\}_{a=1}^{\texttt{d}}
of the algebraic system (
3.28
) with

replaced by
b(L)
.
The ‘phase shift’

is given in terms of the coefficients

\mathfrak{C}^{(\pm)}_{p,s}(\boldsymbol{w})
(
3.35
), which were introduced in the previous subsection.
For the primary Bethe states with
\texttt{d}=0

,

\sqrt{B}-\exp\big(\frac{2\pi}{n}\,b_{*}(L)\big)
goes to zero faster than any power of the logarithm of
L
.

4
Space of states in the scaling limit

4.1
Continuous and discrete spectrum

A key result of this paper is the conjecture, above,
which was motivated by our numerical work.
It describes a certain one-to-one relation between
b(L)
and
b_{*}(L)
.
The former is obtained via the Bethe roots
corresponding to a state
|{}_{L}\rangle
in a multiplet
{\cal M}_{\cal S}
, labeled
by
2p=2{\cal S}+1-(n+2)
,
the non-negative integer
d
and one of the

N(\texttt{d}\,|\,{\cal S})={\rm par}_{2}(\texttt{d})-{\rm par}_{2}(\texttt{d}-2{\cal S}-1)

solution sets
\boldsymbol{w}=\{w_{a}\}_{a=1}^{\texttt{d}}
of the algebraic system
(
3.28
). Since it requires the construction of an RG trajectory, computing
b(L)

for
L\gg 1
can be cumbersome and time-consuming to carry out.
The notation
b_{*}(L)

stands for a solution of the
quantization condition (
3.41
) treated as an equation for
b(L)

with the correction terms ignored.
Accepting the conjecture to be true, one can determine the spectrum of
b(L)
for the low energy states at large
L

via a study of (
3.41
). This is extremely powerful, since much less computing resources are needed
to numerically analyze the quantization condition and it may be studied analytically as well.
The results allow one to characterize the spectrum of conformal dimensions together with the
space of low energy states in the scaling limit.

Consider the quantization condition (
3.41
) and suppose that

\delta(\boldsymbol{w},p,s)|_{s\mapsto b(L)}\ll\log(L)
.
Then the first term dominates and one can develop an asymptotic expansion for
b(L)
in
1/\log(L)
.
The leading and subleading asymptotic behaviour reads as

b_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)=\frac{2\pi\mathfrak{m}-\delta_{0}}{4\log\big(\mbox{e}^{\frac{1}{4}\delta^{\prime}_{0}}L/L_{0}\big)}+O\big((\log L)^{-3}\big)\qquad\qquad\qquad\big(L\gg 1,\ \mathfrak{m}\ {\rm-\ fixed}\big)\ .

(4.1)

Here we use the notation

\delta_{0}=\delta|_{s=0}\,,\qquad\qquad\qquad\delta^{\prime}_{0}=\partial_{s}\delta|_{s=0}\,,

(4.2)

while
\mathfrak{m}
, which labels the different
b(L)
obeying the quantization condition,
comes about as a result of taking the logarithm of (
3.41
) and is an even or odd integer for

\sigma=+1
or
\sigma=-1
, respectively. Formula (
4.1
) shows that, in general,

b_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)
is a complex number since
\delta_{0}
and
\delta^{\prime}_{0}
are generically complex.
However, while
\Im m\big(b_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)\big)\sim 1/\log(L)\to 0
as
L\to\infty

the magnitude of the real part is controlled
by the integer
\mathfrak{m}
which, for the low energy states,
may take any values up to some
\mathfrak{m}_{\rm max}\ll L
. Numerical
work leads us to suppose that

\lim_{L\to\infty}b_{\mathfrak{m}_{\rm max}}=\infty
.

Let
{\cal H}_{L|{\cal S}}^{({\rm cont})}
denote the set of low energy states
|{}_{L}\rangle

with fixed value of the

Recall that the states come in multiplets

Each low energy Bethe state in

For

\big[4s\log(L/L_{0})+\delta(\boldsymbol{w},p,s)\big]|_{s\mapsto b_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)}=2\pi\mathfrak{m}+O(L^{-\epsilon})\ .

(4.4)

Here the branch of the logarithm needed to define

4.2
{\cal W}_{\infty}
algebra

In the scaling limit the critical lattice system possesses extended conformal symmetry.
The corresponding algebra is expected to be the
{\cal W}_{\infty}
algebra from ref.
[
46
]

with central charge
c<2
. This is
the same one that appears in the scaling limit of the staggered
six-vertex model with (quasi-)periodic BCs
[
26
,
15
]
.
Among other things, such a statement implies that the graded linear spaces

\bigoplus_{\texttt{d}=0}^{\infty}{\cal H}_{p,s}^{(\texttt{d})}\,,\qquad\qquad{\cal H}_{p,s}^{(\texttt{d})}=\begin{cases}{\cal H}_{p,s}^{({\rm cont},\texttt{d})}&\ \ \ {\rm for}\qquad s\in\mathbb{R}\\[5.69046pt]
{\cal H}_{p,s}^{({\rm disc},\texttt{d})}&\ \ \ {\rm for}\qquad p+\tfrac{1}{2}\pm{\rm i}s\in\mathbb{Z}\end{cases}

(4.24)

are isomorphic to a (irreducible) representation of
{\cal W}_{\infty}
. Then formulae (
4.23
),
(
4.11
) and (
4.18
) would provide a classification of the space
of states
{\cal H}
occuring in the scaling limit of the lattice model
in terms of irreps of the algebra of extended conformal symmetry.
In order to demonstrate this we briefly mention some details concerning the
{\cal W}_{\infty}

algebra and its representations, while referring the reader to section 16 of ref.
[
15
]

for a deeper discussion.

The
{\cal W}_{\infty}
algebra is generated by a set of currents
W_{j}(u)

of Lorentz spin
j=2,3,\ldots\
.
These satisfy an infinite system of Operator Product Expansions (OPEs).
Its first few members can be chosen to be

\displaystyle W_{2}(u)\,W_{2}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{2u^{4}}-\frac{2}{u^{2}}\ W_{2}(0)-\frac{1}{u}\ \partial W_{2}(0)+O(1)

\displaystyle W_{2}(u)\,W_{3}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{u^{2}}\ W_{3}(0)-\frac{1}{u}\ \partial W_{3}(0)+O(1)

(4.25)

\displaystyle W_{3}(u)\,W_{3}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{c(c+7)(2c-1)}{9(c-2)u^{6}}+\frac{(c+7)(2c-1)}{3(c-2)u^{4}}\ \big(W_{2}(u)+W_{2}(0)\big)-\frac{1}{u^{2}}\ \Big(W_{4}(u)+W_{4}(0)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle W^{2}_{2}(u)+W^{2}_{2}(0)+\frac{2c^{2}+22c-25}{30(c-2)}\,\big(\partial^{2}W_{2}(u)+\partial^{2}W_{2}(0)\big)\Big)+O(1)\ ,

where in the last line
W_{2}^{2}
is a composite field which coincides with
the first regular term in the OPE
W_{2}(u)W_{2}(0)
.
Notice that there is some ambiguity in the definition of
W_{j}

for
j\geq 3
.
Apart from the freedom in the overall multiplicative normalization,
W_{j}\mapsto CW_{j}
,
it is possible to add to
W_{j}
any differential polynomial of Lorentz spin
j
involving the lower spin currents
W_{k}

with
k<j
. Here, the
W_{3}
current
was fixed by the requirement that it be a primary field of spin three, so that its OPE
with
W_{2}
takes the form of the second line in formula (
4.2
). As for
W_{4}
, one
can not arrange for it to be a primary field by adding linear combinations of
W_{2}^{2}
,

\partial W_{3}
and
\partial^{2}W_{2}
. Defined such that it appears in the OPE of

W_{3}(u)W_{3}(0)
as above, it turns out that

W_{2}(u)W_{4}(0)
takes a simpler form,

W_{2}(u)\,W_{4}(0)=\frac{(c+10)(17c+2)}{15(c-2)\,u^{4}}\ W_{2}(0)-\frac{4}{u^{2}}\ W_{4}(0)-\frac{1}{u}\ \partial W_{4}(0)+O(1)\ ,

(4.26)

where the
singular terms
\propto u^{-6}
and
\propto u^{-3}
are absent.

For the study of the
{\cal W}_{\infty}
algebra it is useful to know that it
admits a realization in terms of two independent
chiral Bose fields. We normalize them as

\partial\vartheta(u)\,\partial\vartheta(0)=-\frac{1}{2u^{2}}+O(1)\,,\qquad\qquad\partial\varphi(u)\,\partial\varphi(0)=-\frac{1}{2u^{2}}+O(1)\,,

(4.27)

while
\partial\varphi(u)\partial\vartheta(0)=O(1)
. One may check that as a consequence of the free field OPEs,
the currents

\displaystyle W_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial\vartheta)^{2}+(\partial\varphi)^{2}+\frac{{\rm i}}{\sqrt{n+2}}\ \partial^{2}\varphi

(4.28)

\displaystyle W_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{6n+8}{3n+6}\,(\partial\vartheta)^{3}+2\,(\partial\varphi)^{2}\partial\vartheta+{\rm i}\sqrt{n+2}\ \partial^{2}\varphi\,\partial\vartheta-\frac{{\rm i}n}{\sqrt{n+2}}\ \partial\varphi\,\partial^{2}\vartheta+\frac{n}{6(n+2)}\ \partial^{3}\vartheta

obey the algebra (
4.2
). The parameter
n
entering above is related to the
central charge
c
as

c=\frac{2(n-1)}{n+2}

(4.29)

so that if
n
is real and positive, the central charge
c
is less than two.
Notice that, while an expression for
W_{4}
in terms of
\partial\vartheta
and
\partial\varphi

has not been provided, it can be deduced from the OPEs (
4.2
)
and the formula (
4.28
) for
W_{2}
and
W_{3}
. One simply computes
W_{3}(u)W_{3}(0)

with
W_{3}
written
in terms of free fields and compares the coefficient
\propto u^{-2}
with the same coefficient
appearing in the last two lines of eq. (
4.2
). It turns out that the higher spin currents
always appear in the OPEs involving the lower spin ones. This way, starting from
(
4.28

realization of
W_{j}
in terms of the free fields
\partial\varphi
and
\partial\vartheta

for any

A stepping stone for the construction of highest weight irreducible representations of the
{\cal W}_{\infty}
algebra
is the Verma module. It is defined using the Fourier modes
of

u\sim u+2\pi
:

W_{j}=-\frac{c}{24}\,\delta_{j,2}+\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}{\widetilde{W}}_{j}(m)\ \mbox{e}^{-{\rm i}mu}\ .

(4.30)

Introduce the highest state, which is specified by the conditions:

\widetilde{W}_{j}(m)\,|\boldsymbol{\omega}\rangle=0\qquad\qquad(\forall m>0)\,,\qquad\qquad\widetilde{W}_{j}(0)\,|\boldsymbol{\omega}\rangle=\omega_{j}\,|\boldsymbol{\omega}\rangle

(4.31)

with
j=2,3
.
The highest weight is given by
\boldsymbol{\omega}=(\omega_{2},\omega_{3})
, where
the component
\omega_{2}
is equal to the conformal dimension of the highest state, while

\omega_{3}
is the eigenvalue of
\widetilde{W}_{3}(0)
, which commutes with

\widetilde{W}_{2}(0)
. The Verma module is spanned by the states that are obtained
by acting with the ‘creation modes’ of the spin 2 and spin 3 currents on the highest state:

\widetilde{W}_{2}(-\ell_{1})\ldots\widetilde{W}_{2}(-\ell_{m})\,\widetilde{W}_{3}(-\ell_{1}^{\prime})\ldots\widetilde{W}_{3}(-\ell_{m^{\prime}}^{\prime})|\boldsymbol{\omega}\rangle

(4.32)

with
\ell_{j},\,\ell^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}\geq 1
.
It possesses a natural grading given by

\texttt{d}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\ell_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}\ell_{j}^{\prime}

(4.33)

and the dimensions of its level subspace with fixed
d
is the number of bi-partitions of
d
, i.e.,

{\rm par}_{2}(\texttt{d})
(
3.29
).
In what follows we will parameterize the highest weight for the Verma module

{\cal V}_{\rho,\nu}
as

\displaystyle\omega_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\rho^{2}-\frac{1}{4}}{n+2}+\frac{\nu^{2}}{n}

(4.34)

\displaystyle\omega_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\nu}{\sqrt{n}}\,\Big(\,\frac{\rho^{2}}{n+2}+\frac{(3n+4)\,\nu^{2}}{3n\,(n+2)}-\frac{2n+3}{12\,(n+2)}\,\Big)\ .

This is motivated by the free field realization (
4.28
). Supposing that the
highest state is an eigenvector of the
operators
\int\mbox{d}u\,\partial\vartheta(u)
and
\int\mbox{d}u\,\partial\varphi(u)
with eigenvalues

\frac{\nu}{\sqrt{n}}
and
\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{n+2}}
, respectively, formula
(
4.34
) follows from
(
4.28
).
The highest weight is an even function of
\rho
.
As a result the spaces
{\cal V}_{\rho,\nu}
and
{\cal V}_{-\rho,\nu}
should be identified.
In the parameterization (
4.34
), the conformal dimensions of a state in the Verma module at level
d
is
such that

\widetilde{W}_{2}(0)-\frac{c}{24}=\frac{\rho^{2}}{n+2}+\frac{\nu^{2}}{n}-\frac{1}{12}+\texttt{d}\,,

(4.35)

which should be compared with eq. (
3.13
).

For generic complex values of
\rho
and
\nu
the Verma module
{\cal V}_{\rho,\nu}
is an irreducible representation
of the
{\cal W}_{\infty}
algebra. However, with
\rho
,
\nu
obeying certain constraints, the Verma module
contains null vectors – highest states occurring at non-zero levels. Then the highest weight irrep
{\cal W}_{\rho,\nu}

can be obtained
from
{\cal V}_{\rho,\nu}
by factoring out all of the invariant subspace(s) generated by the null vector(s).
In view of applications to the scaling limit of the lattice model of particular interest is when

\rho=\pm\frac{1}{2}\big(r-m(n+2)\big)
with
r,m=1,2,\ldots\
. In this case
a null vector occurs at level
\texttt{d}=mr
and the Verma module splits into the direct sum
of two representations, which are irreducible for generic
n
and
\nu
:

{\cal V}_{\rho,\nu}={\cal W}_{\rho,\nu}\oplus{\cal W}_{\rho^{\prime},\nu}\qquad\qquad{\rm with}\qquad\qquad\begin{array}[]{ll}\rho\phantom{{}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}\,(r-m\,(n+2))\qquad\qquad(n,\,\nu\,{\rm-\,generic})\\[5.69046pt]
\rho^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\,(r+m\,(n+2))\qquad\qquad(r,m=1,2,\ldots)\end{array}\,.

(4.36)

The space
{\cal W}_{\rho^{\prime},\nu}
is isomorphic to the Verma module and the dimensions
of its level subspaces is
{\rm par}_{2}(\texttt{d})
, while for
{\cal W}_{\rho,\nu}
,
the level subspaces are
{\rm par}_{2}(\texttt{d})-{\rm par}_{2}(\texttt{d}-mr)
dimensional.
Consider again the components
{\cal H}^{({\rm cont},\texttt{d})}_{p,s}
, which appear in
the decomposition of the continuous spectrum of the space of states occurring in the scaling limit
of the spin chain. Taking into account formulae (
4.12
) and (
3.31
) it is clear that

{\cal W}_{p,s}\cong\bigoplus_{\texttt{d}=0}^{\infty}{\cal H}^{({\rm cont},\texttt{d})}_{p,s}\qquad\qquad\qquad\big(2p=2{\cal S}+1-(n+2)\,,\ s\ {\rm-\,real}\big)\,.

(4.37)

To describe the discrete spectrum in terms of irreps of the
{\cal W}_{\infty}
algebra,
it is necessary to analyze the case when
\nu
is such that

\rho+\frac{1}{2}\pm{\rm i}\nu
is an integer for some choice of the sign
\pm
.
As explained in, e.g., section 16.2 of reference
[
15
]

the Verma module
with
\rho+\frac{1}{2}+{\rm i}\nu=-a_{+}=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots

contains a null vector at level
|a_{+}+\frac{1}{2}|+\frac{1}{2}
,
while for
-\rho+\frac{1}{2}+{\rm i}\nu=-a_{-}=0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots

there is a null vector at level
|a_{-}+\frac{1}{2}|+\frac{1}{2}
. Assuming
\rho
is generic for now
the character of the irreducible representation,

{\rm ch}_{\rho,\nu}(\texttt{q})\equiv{\rm Tr}_{{\cal W}_{\rho,\nu}}\Big[\,\texttt{q}^{\widetilde{W}_{2}(0)-\frac{c}{24}}\,\Big]\,,

(4.38)

is given by
[
48
]
(see also
[
47
,
49
]
)

{\rm ch}_{\rho,\nu}(\texttt{q})=\texttt{q}^{-\frac{1}{12}+\frac{\nu^{2}}{n}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{n+2}}\ \bigg(\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-\texttt{q}^{m})^{2}}\bigg)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\ \texttt{q}^{m|a+\frac{1}{2}|+\frac{m^{2}}{2}}\qquad\quad\begin{array}[]{l}\rho+\tfrac{1}{2}\pm{\rm i}\nu=-a\in\mathbb{Z}\\[5.69046pt]
n,\,\rho\ \ {\rm generic}\end{array}\,.

(4.39)

If, in addition to
\nu
being constrained as above,

\rho\to\frac{1}{2}\,(2{\cal S}-n-1)
then the irrep
{\cal W}_{\rho,\nu}

further breaks up into two irreducible representations. One of them is generated by the
null vector which appears at level
2{\cal S}+1
and has highest weights

(\rho^{\prime},\nu)
with
\rho^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\,(2{\cal S}+n+3)
. Its character is given by
(
4.39
) with
\rho
replaced by
\rho^{\prime}
and

a\mapsto a^{\prime}=-2({\cal S}+1)-a
. Taking the difference

{\rm ch}_{\rho,\nu}(\texttt{q})-{\rm ch}_{\rho^{\prime},\nu}(\texttt{q})
with

\rho\to\frac{1}{2}\,(2{\cal S}-n-1)
and
\rho^{\prime}\to\frac{1}{2}\,(2{\cal S}+n+3)

yields for
the character of the irreducible representation
{\cal W}_{\rho,\nu}
with

\rho+\tfrac{1}{2}\pm{\rm i}\nu=-a\in\mathbb{Z}\qquad\qquad{\rm and}\qquad\qquad 2\rho=2{\cal S}-n-1

(4.40)

that

{\rm ch}_{\rho,\nu}=\texttt{q}^{-\frac{1}{12}+\frac{\nu^{2}}{n}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{n+2}}\ \bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-\texttt{q}^{j})^{2}}\bigg)\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\,q^{\frac{m^{2}}{2}}\,\Big(q^{m|a+\frac{1}{2}|}-q^{2{\cal S}+1+m|2{\cal S}+a+\frac{3}{2}|}\Big)\ .

(4.41)

For the case
a\geq 0
the above expression, apart from the overall factor

\texttt{q}^{-\frac{1}{12}+\frac{\nu^{2}}{n}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{n+2}}
, coincides with the generating function
(
4.7
)
for the integers
N_{a}^{+}(\texttt{d}\,|\,{\cal S})
. This way, one concludes

{\cal W}_{p,s}\cong\bigoplus_{\texttt{d}=0}^{\infty}{\cal H}^{({\rm disc},\texttt{d})}_{p,s}\qquad\qquad\big(p={\cal S}+\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}(n+2)\,,\ \tfrac{n}{2}-{\cal S}\pm{\rm i}s=a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\big)\,.

(4.42)

The remaining case to be considered is when
-\mathcal{S}\leq a<0
. The lower bound comes from
the condition
s\in(-\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}]

which implies that
\pm{\rm i}s=\tfrac{n}{2}-{\mathcal{S}}-a\leq\frac{n}{2}
. From the definition of the integers

N^{+}_{a}(\texttt{d}\,|\,{\cal S})
(
4.7
), which give the dimensions of the level subspaces

{\cal H}_{p,s}^{({\rm disc},\texttt{d})}\subset{\cal H}^{({\rm disc})}
, one finds

\dim\big({\cal H}_{p,s}^{(\texttt{d})}\big)=0\qquad{\rm for}\qquad\texttt{d}=0,1,\ldots,|a|-1\qquad\qquad\big(-p-\tfrac{1}{2}\pm{\rm i}s=a\in\mathbb{Z}\,,\ -{\cal S}\leq a<0\big)

(4.43)

Thus the corresponding irrep (
4.24
) has highest state whose conformal
dimension is given by:

\Delta=\frac{p^{2}}{n+2}+\frac{s^{2}}{n}+|a|\qquad\qquad\big(-\mathcal{S}\leq a<0\big)\,.

(4.44)

This turns out to be an irreducible representation of the
{\cal W}_{\infty}
algebra,

{\cal W}_{\rho,\nu}=\bigoplus_{\texttt{d}=0}^{\infty}{\cal H}_{p,s}^{(\texttt{d})}\,,

(4.45)

with highest weight
parameterized as in (
4.34
), where

\rho=\mathcal{S}+\frac{1}{2}\,,\qquad\quad\nu=\begin{cases}s-\frac{{\rm i}n}{2}&{\rm for}\ \ \ (-{\rm i}s)>0\\[5.69046pt]
s+\frac{{\rm i}n}{2}&{\rm for}\ \ \ (-{\rm i}s)<0\end{cases}\qquad\quad\big(\tfrac{n}{2}-{\cal S}\pm{\rm i}s=a\in\mathbb{Z}_{<0}\,,\ -{\cal S}\leq a<0\big)\,.

Assuming
n
is irrational, the character of such a representation is given by

{\rm ch}_{\rho,\nu}(\texttt{q})=\texttt{q}^{-\frac{1}{12}+\frac{\nu^{2}}{n}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{n+2}}\ \bigg(\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-\texttt{q}^{m})^{2}}\bigg)\ \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\,\texttt{q}^{\frac{m^{2}}{2}}\big(\,\texttt{q}^{m|\,|\rho|-|\nu|\,|}-\texttt{q}^{(m+1)(|\rho|+|\nu|+1)-\frac{1}{2}}\,\big)\,.

(4.46)

One can check that the dimensions of the level subspaces, obtained by expanding
{\rm ch}_{\rho,\nu}(\texttt{q})

in a series in
q
, coincides with the integers
N^{+}_{a}(\texttt{d}|\,{\cal S})
with
-{\cal S}\leq a<0

and
\texttt{d}=|a|,\,|a|+1,\,|a|+2,\ldots\
.

Finally, we mention that the states
|{}_{p,s}^{(S^{z})}(\boldsymbol{w})\rangle\in{\cal W}_{\rho,\nu}

appearing in the scaling limit of the Bethe states, see eq.(
4.9
), have an important
interpretation. They are the simultaneous eigenstates of the
family of commuting operators known as the quantum AKNS integrable structure

[
50
,
51
]
. The function
\mbox{e}^{\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\delta}
(
3.41b
) entering into the
quantization condition coincides with the eigenvalue of a certain
so-called reflection operator
[
52
]
computed on

|{}_{p,s}^{(S^{z})}(\boldsymbol{w})\rangle
, see ref.
[
45
]
for details.

5
Partition function in the scaling limit

In the case of the lattice model with (quasi-)periodic Boundary Conditions (BCs) imposed,
it was proposed in the work

[
16
]
and then verified numerically in ref.
[
15
]
that the
partition function appearing in the scaling limit of the lattice system,
Z^{\rm(scl)}
, coincides with twice the partition function
for the 2D Euclidean black hole CFT. The latter was constructed in
refs.
[
20
,
21
]
by computing
a functional integral with the worldsheet being taken to be a torus.
The results presented in the previous section allow one to easily compute
Z^{\rm(scl)}
for the
staggered six-vertex model subject to
U_{q}\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\big)
invariant open BCs.
One may expect
\frac{1}{2}\,Z^{({\rm scl})}
to coincide with the partition function for the 2D Euclidean black hole CFT
on the open segment
x\in(0,R)
, with certain conditions imposed on the fields at
x=0,R
.

Consider the lattice partition function

Z_{L}^{({\rm lattice})}={\rm Tr}_{\mathscr{V}_{2L}}\big[\mbox{e}^{-M\,\mathbb{H}}\big]\,,

(5.1)

where the Hamiltonian
\mathbb{H}
is given by (
1
) with

q=\mbox{e}^{\frac{{\rm i}\pi}{n+2}}
and
n\geq 0
, while the trace is taken
over the
2^{2L}
dimensional space of states:

\mathscr{V}_{2L}=\mathbb{C}^{2}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}_{2}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}_{2L}
.
Keeping the ratio

\tau=\frac{v_{\rm F}M}{2L}

(5.2)

fixed as
L\to\infty
, one finds that the large
L
behaviour of the lattice partition function is given by

Z_{L}^{({\rm lattice})}\asymp\mbox{e}^{-MLe_{\infty}-Mf_{\infty}}\,Z^{({\rm scl})}\,.

(5.3)

Here
Z^{({\rm scl})}
takes the form of a trace over the space of states
{\cal H}
appearing in the scaling limit
of the lattice model:

Z^{({\rm scl})}={\rm Tr}_{\cal H}\Big(\texttt{q}^{\hat{H}_{\rm CFT}}\Big)\qquad{\rm with}\qquad\texttt{q}=\mbox{e}^{-2\pi\tau}\,.

(5.4)

It involves the ‘CFT Hamiltonian’
\hat{H}_{\rm CFT}
which when restricted to the finite dimensional spaces

{\cal H}^{(\rm{cont},\texttt{d})}_{p,s}

or
{\cal H}^{(\rm{disc},\texttt{d})}_{p,s}
appearing in the decomposition of
{\cal H}

coincides with the identity operator multiplied by the factor

E_{\rm CFT}=\frac{p^{2}}{n+2}+\frac{s^{2}}{n}-\frac{1}{12}+\texttt{d}\ .

(5.5)

Notice that the asymptotic formula for the energy (
3.3
) can be re-written as the formal relation

\hat{H}_{\rm CFT}=\slim_{L\to\infty}\ \frac{L}{\pi v_{\rm F}}\,\Big(\mathbb{H}-L\,e_{\infty}-f_{\infty}\Big)\ .

(5.6)

In subsection
4.1
the space of states
{\cal H}
was expressed as a direct sum of the continuous spectrum

{\cal H}^{({\rm cont})}
and the discrete one
{\cal H}^{({\rm disc})}
, see formula (
4.22
).
The contribution of the states to the trace in eq. (
5.4
)
for each of these spaces will be denoted as
Z^{({\rm cont})}
and
Z^{({\rm disc})}
,
respectively, so that

Z^{({\rm scl})}=Z^{({\rm cont})}+Z^{({\rm disc})}\,,

(5.7)

where

Z^{({\rm disc})}={\rm Tr}_{{\cal H}^{({\rm disc})}}\,\Big(\texttt{q}^{\hat{H}_{\rm CFT}}\Big)\ ,\qquad\qquad Z^{({\rm cont})}={\rm Tr}_{{\cal H}^{({\rm cont})}}\,\Big(\texttt{q}^{\hat{H}_{\rm CFT}}\Big)\ .

(5.8)

Let’s first focus on the computation of
Z^{({\rm disc})}
.
The space
{\cal H}^{({\rm disc})}
is made up of the components

{\cal H}_{{\cal S},S^{z}}^{({\rm disc})}\cong{\cal H}_{{\cal S},{\cal S}}^{({\rm disc})}
, which admit the
decomposition (
4.18
) into finite dimensional spaces.
Introduce the notation:

\chi_{a,{\cal S}}(\texttt{q})=\texttt{q}^{-\frac{(\frac{n}{2}-{\cal S}-a)^{2}}{n}+\frac{p^{2}}{n+2}-\frac{1}{12}}\ \bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1-\texttt{q}^{j})^{-2}\bigg)\,\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}\,\big(1-\texttt{q}^{(1+m)(2{\cal S}+1)}\big)\,\texttt{q}^{ma+\frac{m(m+1)}{2}}\ ,

(5.9)

where, aside from the prefactor, the function
\chi_{a,{\cal S}}(\texttt{q})
coincides with the generating function for
the dimensions of the level subspaces
{\cal H}_{{\cal S},{\cal S}}^{({\rm disc,\texttt{d}})}
, see eqs. (
4.20
)
and (
4.7
). Then, the contribution of the discrete spectrum to the partition function reads as:

Z^{({\rm disc})}=\sum_{{\cal S}\geq 0}(2{\cal S}+1)\bigg(\chi_{-{\cal S},{\cal S}}(\texttt{q})+2\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}\atop 0<a+{\cal S}<\frac{n}{2}}\!\!\!\!\!\chi_{a,{\cal S}}(\texttt{q})\bigg)\ .

(5.10)

Each term in the sum over
{\cal S}
has multiplicity

(2{\cal S}+1)
as a result of the
U_{q}\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\big)

symmetry of the lattice model. Also, for every state with given
s=s_{a}

(
4.17
) there exists another
one with
s=-s_{a}
which yields the same contribution to the partition function,
except for the case when
s=\pm\frac{{\rm i}n}{2}
, where they are identified as the same state.
This explains why the functions
\chi_{a,{\cal S}}(\texttt{q})
come with a factor of two except
the one with
a=-{\cal S}
(recall that

s_{a}={\rm i}(\tfrac{n}{2}-{\cal S}-a)

and hence
s_{a}=\frac{{\rm i}n}{2}
for
a=-{\cal S}
).

The contribution of the continuous spectrum to the partition function is given by

Z^{({\rm cont})}=\sum_{{\cal S}\geq 0}(2{\cal S}+1)\,\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mbox{d}s\ \sum_{\texttt{d}\geq 0}\rho^{(\texttt{d})}_{p}(s)\,\texttt{q}^{\frac{s^{2}}{n}+\frac{p^{2}}{n+2}-\frac{1}{12}+\texttt{d}}\,.

(5.11)

Here
\rho^{(\texttt{d})}_{p}(s)
is the density of states defined in formulae (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), while
recall that

2p=2{\cal S}+1-(n+2)
.
Notice that
Z^{({\rm cont})}
becomes singular as
L\to\infty
:

Z^{({\rm cont})}=Z^{({\rm sing})}+O(1)\,,

(5.12)

where the singular part goes
as
\log(L)
and reads as

Z^{({\rm sing})}=\sqrt{\frac{n}{2\tau}}\ \frac{\log\big(2^{\frac{n+2}{n}}\,L/L_{0}\big)}{\pi\,\texttt{q}^{\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-\texttt{q}^{m})}\ \sum^{\infty}_{\mathcal{S}=0}\,\,(2\mathcal{S}+1)\ \texttt{q}^{-\frac{1}{24}+\frac{p^{2}}{n+2}}\ \frac{1-\texttt{q}^{2\mathcal{S}+1}}{\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-\texttt{q}^{m})}\,.

(5.13)

The factor out the front of the sum is easily recognized to be the partition function of a boson
taking values in the segment
\sim\log(L)
with Neumann BCs imposed at the endpoints of the
field at
x=0,R
. As for the remaining term,

\sum^{\infty}_{\mathcal{S}=0}\,\,(2\mathcal{S}+1)\ \texttt{q}^{-\frac{1}{24}+\frac{p^{2}}{n+2}}\ \frac{1-\texttt{q}^{2\mathcal{S}+1}}{\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}(1-\texttt{q}^{m})}\,,

(5.14)

in all likelihood it corresponds to a boundary state which is a superposition of Ishibashi states
associated with a degenerate representation of the Virasoro algebra with generic central charge
c

(see ref.
[
53
]
for the
c=1
case). Note that (
5.14
) also appears in the
scaling limit of the
XXZ
spin
\frac{1}{2}

chain with open
U_{q}\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\big)
invariant BCs imposed
[
54
]
.

Formulae (
5.10
) and (
5.11
) do not seem to correspond to the published results
in the literature concerning branes in the 2D Euclidean black hole CFT, in particular, ref.
[
24
]
.
As such, a separate investigation is required in order to establish a possible relation between the partition function

Z^{({\rm scl})}=Z^{({\rm cont})}+Z^{({\rm disc})}
and that of the black hole CFTs in the presence of boundaries.

6
Discussion

In this work the universal behaviour of the staggered six-vertex model with
U_{q}\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\big)

invariant boundary conditions imposed was considered. We focused on the so-called self-dual case in the
critical regime (
1.2
). The problem was reformulated in the Hamiltonian picture, where
a central rôle belongs to
\mathbb{H}
(
1
), which commutes with the
transfer-matrix of the vertex model. The study of the
1/L
corrections to the
L\to\infty

behaviour of the energy of
\mathbb{H}
for the low energy states allowed us to
extract the spectrum of scaling dimensions of the statistical system. Our treatment, involving the use
of novel numerical and analytic techniques,
represents an advance on the type of analysis of the scaling limit of integrable lattice systems
with open boundary conditions that typically exists in the literature.

The numerical construction of the RG trajectories was achieved via the method
of the
Q
operator. A key rôle was played by the
formula (
2.20
) for the matrix elements of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)
,
valid for a one parameter family of open boundary conditions in the sector
S^{z}=0
.
This is an original result of our work.
The advantage of (
2.20
),
as opposed to the expressions for
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta)
appearing in the literature

[
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
, is that it contains no infinite sums;
works literally for any (generic) complex values of
q
and the boundary parameter
\epsilon
; and can
be programmed efficiently on the computer.
We believe that, in view of the important applications
of the
Q
operator, it may be worthwhile to
extend (
2.20
) to the other sectors of the Hilbert space with
S^{z}\neq 0

and the case of more general open boundary conditions.

The powerful analytic technique, which was crucial to our investigation, is the ODE/IQFT
approach to the study of the scaling limit of integrable, critical lattice systems. For the
staggered six-vertex model in the regime (
1.2
) with quasi-periodic boundary conditions imposed,
it was developed in the works
[
26
,
15
]
.
We found that it was applicable to the
case of
U_{q}\big(\mathfrak{sl}(2)\big)

invariant open boundary conditions as well. This points to the versatility of the ODE/IQFT approach,
where the analysis for one set of boundary conditions can be readily
carried over to another.

As was already observed in
refs.
[
22
,
23
]
, the set of scaling dimensions of the statistical system
possesses a continuous
component, labeled by the quantum
number
s=\slim_{L\to\infty}b(L)\in\mathbb{R}
with
b(L)
from eq. (
3.6
)
[
23
]
.
One of the results of this work is the explicit formula for the density of states
\rho^{(\texttt{d})}(s)
(
4.5
), (
4.6
),
which characterizes the continuous spectrum. In addition, we studied the RG trajectories
\{|{}_{L}\rangle\}
,
where
s

becomes a pure imaginary number in the scaling limit. Building on the analysis of

[
23
]
, the discrete set
\Sigma\equiv\Sigma^{+}\cup\Sigma^{-}
(
4.19
)
of all admissible
values of pure imaginary
s
was found. We also determined
the dimension of the linear span of states
occuring in the scaling limit of
|{}_{L}\rangle
with given
s\in\Sigma

and conformal dimensions
\Delta
(
3.13
).

Our work includes a full characterization of the linear space
{\cal H}
appearing in the scaling limit of the
space of low energy states
of the lattice system. To describe it,
{\cal H}
was decomposed into a direct sum of the ‘continuous spectrum’

{\cal H}^{({\rm cont})}
and the ‘discrete spectrum’
{\cal H}^{({\rm disc})}
. The
former, when expressed in terms of finite dimensional spaces,
involves a direct integral over
s
, see eqs. (
4.23
), (
4.10
), (
4.11
),
while the latter
contains a direct sum (
4.18
). We explained how the graded linear spaces

\oplus_{\texttt{d}=0}^{\infty}{\cal H}_{p,s}^{({\rm cont},\texttt{d})}
and

\oplus_{\texttt{d}=0}^{\infty}{\cal H}_{p,s}^{({\rm disc},\texttt{d})}
are irreps. of the
{\cal W}_{\infty}
algebra –
the algebra of extended conformal symmetry of the model.

Perhaps the most interesting question is the relation between the scaling limit of the lattice system
and the 2D Euclidean/Lorentzian black hole CFTs
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
.
We believe that the formula for the partition
function
Z^{({\rm scl})}
provided in sec.
5
may be of help. Unfortunately, it does not seem
to correspond to known results in the literature on branes in the 2D black hole CFTs. It is likely
that progress in this direction would require a separate and detailed study.

Appendix A
The asymptotic coefficients
\mathfrak{C}_{p,s}^{(\pm)}

Here we provide a closed form expression for the coefficients
\mathfrak{C}_{p,s}^{(\pm)}(\boldsymbol{w})

that were obtained in the work
[
45
]
. Among other things, they enter into the quantization condition (
3.41

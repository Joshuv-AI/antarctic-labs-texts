Periodic trawl processes: Simulation, statistical inference and applications in energy markets
Almut E. D. Veraart

Abstract
This article introduces the class of periodic trawl processes, which are continuous-time, infinitely divisible, stationary stochastic processes, that allow for periodicity and flexible forms of their serial correlation, including both short- and long-memory settings. We derive some of the key probabilistic properties of periodic trawl processes and present relevant examples. Moreover, we show how such processes can be simulated and establish the asymptotic theory for their sample mean and sample autocovariances. Consequently, we prove the asymptotic normality of a (generalised) method-of-moments estimator for the model parameters. We illustrate the new model and estimation methodology in an application to electricity prices.



MSC2020 subject classifications:
Primary 62M15, 62F99, 60G10; secondary
62P99, 60F05, 60G57, 60E07.

Keywords:
Periodic trawl process, Lévy basis, infinite divisibility, simulation, asymptotic theory, weak dependence, generalised method of moments.

1
Introduction

Time series with periodic behaviours are ubiquitous and are present in many applications such as climate science, meteorology, oceanography, hydrology, economics, and communications networks. Our article is particularly motivated by applications in energy, carbon, and temperature markets.
Here, we need to consider different types of seasonalities and periodicities when, for example, modelling (renweable) energy production, demand and prices that are influenced by the seasonal weather patterns, as well as different daily and weekly fluctuations and economic cycles.

Traditionally, stochastic models for electricity prices focussed on mean-reverting processes since spot prices can be regarded as equilibrium prices balancing supply and demand.
Barndorff-Nielsen et al. 2013
argued that, in order to achieve a weaker version of mean reversion, one could model prices directly by a stationary process. We follow the same idea in this article and are interested in developing a flexible class of stationary stochastic processes which can allow for periodic behaviour rather than employing widely-used seasonal-trend decomposition models.

There is a broad literature on cyclostationary processes (and their extensions), which are typically constructed by combining stationary processes with periodic functions.
The textbook by

Hurd & Miamee 2007
contains detailed historical background and

Gardner et al. 2006
provides an excellent review; the name
cyclostationary
processes first appears in

Bennett 1958
, whereas other authors referred to them as
periodically correlated
processes, see e.g.
Gladyšev 1961
,

Hurd 1969
, and
Genton & Hall 2007
,

Das & Genton 2021
for more recent work.
There has been much interest in developing suitable statistical methodologies for periodic and cyclostationary processes and their extensions. For instance,

Quinn & Thomson 1991
focuses on estimating the frequency of a periodic function, whereas
Hall et al. 2000
presents a nonparametric estimator of a periodic function.

Moreover, from a stochastic modelling perspective, it is relevant to construct models which can combine periodicity with either short- or long-memory settings, with the latter being typically more challenging. However, there is a long
tradition in time series analysis tackling long-memory settings and incorporating periodicities: Fractional differencing, introduced by
Hosking 1981
and further developed by
Andel 1986
lead to long memory time series models and fractionally integrated ARMA (FARIMA) processes, in particular. The processes were further generalised by

Gray et al. 1989
, who introduced Gegenbauer and Gegenbauer ARMA (GARMA) processes, see also
Woodward et al. 1998
for a
k
-factor GARMA long-memory model and

Espejo et al. 2015
for extensions to Gegenbauer random fields.
The concept of cyclical long-memory processes and the appropriate statistical methodology has been further explored in

Arteche & Robinson 2000
,
Giraitis et al. 2001
,
Ferrara & Guégan 2001
and
Arteche 2002
.
For example,
Hidalgo & Soulier 2004
study the estimation of the location and exponent of the spectral singularity of a long-memory process. They maximise the periodogram to estimate the location of the singularity, in the spirit of
Yajima 2007
, who proved consistency in the Gaussian case and proved consistency under weaker (non-Gaussian) assumptions
Hidalgo & Soulier 2004
.
They used a GPH estimator, see
Geweke & Porter-Hudak 1983
, for the long-memory parameter. Also,
Hidalgo 2005
discusses the semiparametric estimation of stationary processes whose spectra have an unknown pole.

Maddanu 2022
presents a harmonically weighted filter for cyclical long-memory processes.
In practice, one often estimates the parameters of a cyclical/seasonal long-memory process in two steps: First, one estimates the location of the singularity in the spectral density function, second, with the location parameter now fixed, one estimates the long memory parameter, see
Ferrara & Guégan 2001
and
Espejo et al. 2015
.
More recently, a joint estimation of the periodicity and the long-memory parameter have been developed in

Alomari et al. 2020
;
Ayache et al. 2022
.

While there is already extensive literature on discrete-time periodic processes with a short or long memory, much less is known in the continuous-time setting. To fill this gap, this article introduces the class of
periodic trawl processes
, which are continuous-time, infinitely divisible, stationary stochastic processes, which allow for periodicity and flexible forms of their serial correlation, including both short- and long-memory settings.

Periodic trawl processes extend the class of trawl processes first introduced under this name in
Barndorff-Nielsen 2011
but were earlier mentioned (under a different name) in

Wolpert & Taqqu 2005
and
Wolpert & Brown 2011
.
Motivated by applications in high-frequency financial data,

Barndorff-Nielsen et al. 2014
and
Veraart 2019
introduced integer-valued trawl (IVT) processes in a univariate and multivariate framework, respectively.
The statistical methodology for such processes was further developed in

Bennedsen et al. 2023
;
Shephard & Yang 2017
;
Shephard & Yang 2016
.
Moreover, it has been shown that trawl processes can be applied in
hierarchical models for extremes, see
Noven et al. 2018
;
Courgeau & Veraart 2022
;
Bacro et al. 2020
.
There is also a growing interest in discrete-time versions of trawl processes, which were first introduced by
Doukhan et al. 2019
, see also
Doukhan et al. 2020
, and limit theorems for discrete- and continuous-time trawl processes, see for instance
Grahovac et al. 2018
;
Paulauskas 2020
;
Talarczyk & Treszczotko 2020
;
Pakkanen et al. 2021
.

Outline and main contributions of the article

The outline for the remainder of the article is as follows.
Section

It presents some of the key probabilistic properties of periodic trawl processes and relevant examples.
Next, Section

.

concludes.

A
.

2
Mixed moving average and periodic trawl processes

Let us start off by reviewing the definition of the broader class of mixed moving average processes (MMAPs), see for instance
Surgailis et al. 1993
;
Fasen 2005
;
Fuchs & Stelzer 2013
; we will then introduce periodic trawl processes as a special case of MMAPs.

2.1
Background

We briefly review the concept of
Lévy bases.
To this end, consider a Borel set

S\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and denote by
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{B}(S)
the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
S
and by
\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)
the bounded Borel sets of
\mathcal{S}
.

Definition 1
.

Let
L=\{L(A)\,:\,A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)\}
denote a collection of
\mathbb{R}
-valued random variables. Then,
L
is called an
\mathbb{R}
-valued
Lévy basis
on
S
if the following three conditions hold:
1) Random measure: For any
sequence
A_{1},A_{2},\dots
of disjoint elements of

\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)

satisfying
\cup_{j=1}^{\infty}A_{j}\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)

it holds that
L\left(\cup_{j=1}^{\infty}A_{j}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}L(A_{j})
a.s..
2) Independent scatteredness: For any
sequence
A_{1},A_{2},\dots
of disjoint elements of

\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)
, the random variables

L(A_{1}),L(A_{2}),\dots
are independent.
3) Infinite divisibility: For any

A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)
, the law of
L(A)
is infinitely divisible (ID).

Recall that a Lévy basis admits
a Lévy-Khintchine representation, see
Rajput & Rosiński 1989
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
L
denote a Lévy basis, and let
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)
, then the cumulant function of
L
is given by
C(\theta;L(A))=\mathrm{Log}\left(\mathbb{E}(\exp(i\theta L(A))\right)
, where

\displaystyle\begin{split}C(\theta;L(A))&=i\theta\zeta^{*}(A)-\frac{1}{2}\theta^{2}a^{*}(A)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{i\theta\xi}-1-i\theta\xi\mathbb{I}_{[-1,1]}(\xi)\right)n(d\xi,A),\end{split}

(1)

where

\zeta^{*}
is a signed measure on
\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)
,
a^{*}
is a measure on
\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)
, and

n(\cdot,\cdot)
is the generalised Lévy measure, i.e.
n(d\xi,A)
is a Lévy measure on
\mathbb{R}
for fixed
A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)
and a measure on
\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)
for fixed
dx
.

Here
\mathrm{Log}
denotes the
distinguished logarithm
, see
Sato 1999
.

In the following, we shall restrict our attention to homogeneous Lévy bases.

Definition 2

(Homogeneous Lévy basis)
.

A Lévy basis
L
with Lévy-Khintchine representation (
1
) is called homogeneous if we have the following representations for
{\bf z}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\zeta^{*}(d{\bf z})=\zeta d{\bf z},

\displaystyle a^{*}(d{\bf z})=ad{\bf z},

\displaystyle n(d\xi,d{\bf z})=\ell(d\xi)d{\bf z},

(2)

for constants
\zeta\in\mathbb{R},a\geq 0
and a Lévy measure
\ell
. We call
(\zeta,a,\ell)
the characteristic triplet associated with
L
.

In the case of a homogeneous Lévy basis with characteristic triplet
(\zeta,a,\ell)

the Lévy-Khintchine representation simplifies to

\displaystyle\begin{split}C(\theta;L(A))&=\mathrm{Leb}(A)\left[i\theta\zeta-\frac{1}{2}\theta^{2}a+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{i\theta\xi}-1-i\theta\xi\mathbb{I}_{[-1,1]}(\xi)\right)\ell(d\xi)\right],\end{split}

(3)

for
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)
.
In the following, we shall denote by
L^{\prime}
the
Lévy seed
associated with the Lévy basis with characteristic triplet
(\zeta,a,\ell)
, i.e. a random variable with cumulant function given by

\displaystyle C(\theta;L^{\prime})=\mathrm{Log}\left(\mathbb{E}(\exp(i\theta L^{\prime}))\right)=i\theta\zeta-\frac{1}{2}\theta^{2}a+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{i\theta\xi}-1-i\theta\xi\mathbb{I}_{[-1,1]}(\xi)\right)\ell(d\xi),

(4)

for
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.
We recall that a homogeneous Lévy basis admits a Lévy-Itô representation, cf.
Pedersen 2003
:

Proposition 2
.

For a homogeneous Lévy basis
L
with a characteristic triplet
(\zeta,a,\ell)
there exists a modification
L^{*}
with the same characteristic triplet that has the following Lévy-Itô decomposition. Let
A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(S)
, then

\displaystyle L^{*}(A)

\displaystyle=\zeta\mathrm{Leb}(A)+W(A)+\int_{\{|y|\leq 1\}}y(N-n)(dy,A)+\int_{\{|y|>1\}}yN(dy,A),

(5)

for a Gaussian basis
W
, with characteristic triplet
(0,a,0)
, i.e.
W(A)\sim N(0,a\mathrm{Leb}(A))
, and a Poisson basis
N
(independent of
W
) with compensator

n(dy;A)=\mathbb{E}(N(dy;A))
, where
n(dx,d{\bf z})=\ell(dx)d{\bf z}
.

2.2
Definition of a mixed moving average process

Definition 3

(Mixed moving average process)
.

Let
L
denote a homogeneous Lévy basis with characteristic triplet
(\zeta,a,\ell)
,
f:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a deterministic function. The stochastic process
Y=(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with

\displaystyle Y_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,t-s)L(dx,ds),

(6)

is called a mixed moving average process (MMAP).

An application of
Rajput & Rosiński 1989
leads to the following result.

Proposition 3
.

The MMA process defined in (
8
) is well-defined, if and only if the following integrability conditions hold almost surely:

\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}|V_{1}(f(x,t-s))|dxds<\infty,\qquad a\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}|f(x,t-s)|^{2}dxds<\infty,\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}V_{2}(f(x,t-s))dxds<\infty,

(7)

where for
\varrho(\xi):=\xi\mathbb{I}_{[-1,1]}(\xi)
, we define

V_{1}(u):=u\zeta+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\varrho(\xi u)-u\varrho(\xi)\right)\ell(d\xi)
and

V_{2}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\min(1,|\xi u|^{2})\ell(d\xi)
.
In that case, the cumulant function of the MMA process is given by

\displaystyle C(\theta;Y_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Log}(\mathbb{E}(\exp(i\theta Y_{t})))=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}C(\theta f(x,t-s);L^{\prime})dxds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}C(\theta f(x,u);L^{\prime})dxdu

\displaystyle=i\theta\zeta\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,u)dxdu-\frac{1}{2}\theta^{2}a\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f^{2}(x,u)dxdu

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{i\theta\xi f(x,u)}-1-i\theta\xi f(x,u)\mathbb{I}_{[-1,1]}(\xi f(x,u))\right)\ell(d\xi)dxdu,

for
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,
which implies that the process is stationary and infinitely divisible.

2.3
Periodic trawl processes

We will now show that trawl processes and the new class of periodic trawl processes fall into the MMAP framework.
First, recall the definition of a periodic function.

Definition 4
.

A function
p:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is called periodic with period
\tau>0
if
p(x+\tau)=p(x)
for all
x\geq 0
.

Now, we have all the ingredients for defining a periodic trawl process.

Definition 5

(Periodic trawl process)
.

Let
L
denote a homogeneous Lévy basis with characteristic triplet
(\zeta,a,\ell)
,
p:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
a periodic function with period
\tau>0
and
g:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
a continuous, monotonically decreasing function. The stochastic process
Y=(Y_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with

\displaystyle Y_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,t-s)L(dx,ds),

(8)

with

f(x,t-s)=p(t-s)\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(t-s))}(x)\mathbb{I}_{[0,\infty)}(t-s)

is called a
\tau
-periodic trawl process.

Remark 1
.

In the case where
p\equiv 1
, we obtain a monotonic trawl process without periodicity.
Note that we use a slightly different notation than that used in
Barndorff-Nielsen et al. 2014
. Here
g
is defined for non-negative arguments.

Remark 2
.

Barndorff-Nielsen et al. 2014
proposed adding a periodic function as a multiplicative factor to
g
rather than as a kernel function as in (
8
). However, we find that the version proposed above appears to be more analytically tractable, as we shall discuss in more detail in the appendix; see Remark
11
.
Note also that if one is interested in integer-valued trawl processes, then they can be obtained by choosing
p
to be integer-valued.

The following result is a special case of Proposition
3
.

Proposition 4
.

The
\tau
-periodic trawl process defined in Definition
5
is well defined, in the sense that the stochastic integral exists, if and only if the
integrability conditions stated in (
7
) hold almost surely.
In that case, the cumulant function of the periodic trawl process is given by

\displaystyle C(\theta;Y_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Log}(\mathbb{E}(\exp(i\theta Y_{t})))=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}C(\theta p(t-s)\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(t-s))}(x);L^{\prime})dxds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}C(\theta p(u)\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(u))}(x);L^{\prime})dxdu

\displaystyle=i\theta\zeta\int_{0}^{\infty}g(u)p(u)du-\frac{1}{2}\theta^{2}a\int_{0}^{\infty}g(u)p^{2}(u)du

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}g(u)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{i\theta\xi p(u)}-1-i\theta\xi p(u)\mathbb{I}_{[-1,1]}(\xi p(u))\right)\ell(d\xi)du,

for
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,
which implies that the process is stationary and infinitely divisible.

Remark 3
.

A sufficient condition for the integrability conditions to hold is that

p
is continuous and
\int_{0}^{\infty}g(u)du<\infty
. This is indeed the scenario we typically consider for applications.

2.3.1
Second-order properties of periodic trawl processes

Let us now study the second-order properties of periodic trawl processes. The proofs of our results are given in Appendix
A.1
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
Y
denote a
\tau
-periodic trawl process as defined in Definition
5
.
For
t\geq 0
, we have

\mathbb{E}(Y_{t})=\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\infty}p(u)g(u)du
,
\mathrm{Var}(Y_{t})=\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\infty}p^{2}(u)g(u)du
,

\mathrm{Cov}(Y_{0},Y_{t})=\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\infty}p(u)p(t+u)g(t+u)du

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cor}(Y_{0},Y_{t})=\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}p(u)p(t+u)g(t+u)du}{\int_{0}^{\infty}p^{2}(u)g(u)du}.

We get the following important result.

Proposition 6
.

Let
Y
denote a
\tau
-periodic trawl process as defined in Definition
5
. Suppose that the periodic function
p
is continuous. Then there exists a continuous
\tau
-periodic function
c:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, which is proportional to
p
and satisfies
c(0)=1
, such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cor}(Y_{0},Y_{t})

\displaystyle=c(t)\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t+u)du}{\int_{0}^{\infty}g(u)du}.

We note that the
\tau
-periodic function
c
can be represented via its corresponding Fourier series representation, which is useful if a parametric model for
c
is required.

Remark 4
.

The corresponding results for a trawl process follow by setting
p\equiv c\equiv 1
.

2.3.2
Examples

Let us study some examples of periodic trawl processes.

Example 1
.

Consider an exponential trawl function given by

g(x)=\exp(-\lambda x)
for
\lambda>0,x\geq 0
and the
\tau
-periodic function
p(x)=\sin(2\pi x/\tau)
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Y_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime})\frac{2\pi\tau}{\lambda^{2}\tau^{2}+4\pi^{2}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(Y_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})\frac{8\pi^{2}}{\lambda^{3}\tau^{2}+16\pi^{2}\lambda},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(Y_{0},Y_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})2\pi\frac{e^{-\lambda t}\left[\sin\left(\frac{2\pi t}{\tau}\right)\tau\lambda+4\pi\cos\left(\frac{2\pi t}{\tau}\right)\right]}{\lambda(\lambda^{2}\tau^{2}+16\pi^{2})},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cor}(Y_{0},Y_{t})

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda t}c(t),\quad\mathrm{with}\quad c(t)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left[\sin\left(\frac{2\pi t}{\tau}\right)\tau\lambda+4\pi\cos\left(\frac{2\pi t}{\tau}\right)\right],

where
c
is a
\tau
-periodic function.

Next, we consider an example based on the supGamma trawl function, which can allow for both short and long memory.

Example 2
.

Consider the supGamma trawl function defined as
g(x)=\left(1+\frac{x}{\alpha}\right)^{-H}
, for
\alpha>0,H>1,x\geq 0
. As before, let
p(x)=\sin(2\pi x/\tau)
. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(Y_{0},Y_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\infty}p(u)p(t+u)g(t+u)du

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\infty}\sin\left(\frac{2\pi u}{\tau}\right)\sin\left(\frac{2\pi(t+u)}{\tau}\right)\left(1+\frac{(t+u)}{\alpha}\right)^{-H}du.

We note that

\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t+u)du=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{(t+u)}{\alpha}\right)^{-H}du=\frac{\alpha^{H}}{H-1}(t+\alpha)^{1-H}
.
According to Proposition
6
, there exists a
\tau
-periodic function
c
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cor}(Y_{0},Y_{t})

\displaystyle=c(t)\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t+u)du}{\int_{0}^{\infty}g(u)du}=c(t)\left(1+\frac{t}{\alpha}\right)^{1-H}.

For
H\in(1,2]
, we are in the long-memory case and for
H>2
in the short-memory case.

Example 3
.

Let us briefly consider the general case of a superposition trawl. Let

\displaystyle g(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda x}f_{\lambda}(\lambda)d\lambda,

for a density
f_{\lambda}
. If

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(H)}\alpha^{H}\lambda^{H-1}e^{-\lambda\alpha},

for
\alpha>0,H>1
.
Then

\displaystyle g(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda x}\frac{1}{\Gamma(H)}\alpha^{H}\lambda^{H-1}e^{-\lambda\alpha}d\lambda=\left(1+\frac{x}{\alpha}\right)^{-H},

i.e. we are in the case of the previous example.
Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(Y_{0},Y_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\infty}p(u)p(t+u)g(t+u)du,

where

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}p(u)p(t+u)g(t+u)du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}p(u)p(t+u)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda(t+u)}f_{\lambda}(\lambda)d\lambda du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\int_{0}^{\infty}p(u)p(t+u)e^{-\lambda u}duf_{\lambda}(\lambda)d\lambda

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}2\pi\frac{e^{-\lambda t}\left[\sin\left(\frac{2\pi t}{\tau}\right)\tau\lambda+4\pi\cos\left(\frac{2\pi t}{\tau}\right)\right]}{\lambda(\lambda^{2}\tau^{2}+16\pi^{2})}f_{\lambda}(\lambda)d\lambda

\displaystyle=2\pi\tau\sin\left(\frac{2\pi t}{\tau}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{(\lambda^{2}\tau^{2}+16\pi^{2})}f_{\lambda}(\lambda)d\lambda+8\pi^{2}\cos\left(\frac{2\pi t}{\tau}\right)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{\lambda(\lambda^{2}\tau^{2}+16\pi^{2})}f_{\lambda}(\lambda)d\lambda.

From Proposition
6
, we deduce that there exists a
\tau
-periodic function
c
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cor}(Y_{0},Y_{t})=c(t)\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t+u)du}{\int_{0}^{\infty}g(u)du}.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t+u)du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda(t+u)}f_{\lambda}(\lambda)d\lambda du=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}f_{\lambda}(\lambda)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda u}dud\lambda

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda}e^{-\lambda t}f_{\lambda}(\lambda)d\lambda=\mathbb{E}(e^{-\Lambda t}/\Lambda),

where
\Lambda
is a continuous random variable with density
f_{\lambda}
.
Also,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}g(u)du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda}f_{\lambda}(\lambda)d\lambda=\mathbb{E}(1/\Lambda).

That is, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cor}(Y_{0},Y_{t})=c(t)\frac{\mathbb{E}(e^{-\Lambda t}/\Lambda)}{\mathbb{E}(1/\Lambda)}.

3
Simulation of periodic trawl processes

We will now address the question of how a periodic trawl process can be simulated efficiently. A natural first choice might be to develop a grid-based method, using a suitable spatio-temporal grid.
However, it turns out that we can adapt a
slice-based
simulation algorithm which was developed for trawl processes, see
Noven 2016
and also
Leonte & Veraart 2022
, and extend it to allow for any additional temporal kernel function, which in our case, will be a
\tau
-periodic function
p
.

The advantage of this slice-based approach is that we can have a coarser, but exact, approximation in the spatial domain, and the simulation error only appears through the discretisation in time, whereas a grid-based approach would result in a discretisation error both in the spatial and the temporal domain.

Suppose that we would like to simulate

on the grid

for
i=0,\ldots,n

and

.

3.1
Slice-based simulation for trawl processes

The idea behind the slicing, see
Noven 2016
;
Leonte & Veraart 2022
, is that we consider the partition of
\cup_{i=0}^{n}A_{t_{i}}
, where
A_{t_{i}}=\{(x,s):s\leq t,0\leq x\leq g(t-s)\}
, which is obtained when considering all the disjoint sets obtained from the intersections of the various trawl sets.
We illustrate this idea in Figure

3.2
A note on stochastic versus deterministic seasonality

We note that periodic trawl processes are stationary with stochastic seasonality, which is reflected in the time-invariant mean, variance, and autocorrelation, where the autocorrelation function is a periodic function.

One might wonder how such periodic trawl processes compare to trawl processes with deterministic seasonality.
As before, let
L
denote a homogeneous Lévy basis with characteristic triplet
(\zeta,a,\ell)
,
p:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
a periodic function with period
\tau>0
and
g:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
a continuous, monotonically decreasing function.

Consider the trawl process

\displaystyle X_{t}=\int_{(-\infty,t]\times\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(t-s))}(x)L(dx,ds)

and
the periodic trawl process

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\int_{(-\infty,t]\times\mathbb{R}}p(t-s)\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(t-s))}(x)L(dx,ds).

Let
q:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
a periodic function with period
\tau>0
.
We consider trawl processes with additive and multiplicative seasonality.

\displaystyle X^{a}(t):=q(t)+X_{t},

\displaystyle X^{m}(t):=q(t)X_{t}.

Then
X^{a},X^{m}
are not stationary and their second-order properties are given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X^{a}_{t})

\displaystyle=q(t)+\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\infty}g(u)du=q(t)+\mathbb{E}(X_{0})

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(X^{a}_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\infty}g(u)du=\mathrm{Var}(X_{0}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(X^{a}_{0},X^{a}_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t+u)du=\mathrm{Cov}(X_{0},X_{t}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cor}(X^{a}_{0},X^{a}_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t+u)du}{\int_{0}^{\infty}g(u)du}=\mathrm{Cor}(X_{0},X_{t}),

for
t\geq 0
.
Also,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X^{m}_{t})

\displaystyle=q(t)\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\infty}g(u)du=q(t)\mathbb{E}(X_{0}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(X^{m}_{t})

\displaystyle=q^{2}(t)\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\infty}g(u)du=q^{2}(t)\mathrm{Var}(X_{0}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(X^{m}_{0},X^{m}_{t})

\displaystyle=q(0)q(t)\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t+u)du=q(0)q(t)\mathrm{Cov}(X_{0},X_{t}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cor}(X^{m}_{0},X^{m}_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{q(0)q(t)\int_{0}^{\infty}g(t+u)du}{q^{2}(t)\int_{0}^{\infty}g(u)du}=\frac{q(0)}{q(t)}\mathrm{Cor}(X_{0},X_{t}).

4
Asymptotic theory for the sample mean, the sample autocovariances and the sample autocorrelations of mixed moving average processes

Since MMAPs and hence also periodic trawl processes are mixing and, hence, ergodic, see
Fuchs & Stelzer 2013
, we know that their corresponding moment estimators are consistent.
In order to develop a suitable estimation theory for (periodic) trawl processes, we first derive the results in the more general setting of MMAPs.

Consider an MMAP given by

Y=(Y_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
for

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,t-s)L(dx,ds),

for
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, where
f
satisfies the integrability conditions stated in (
7
) almost surely.

We are interested in the asymptotic behaviour of the sample mean and sample autocovariances/-correlations for such processes.

Note that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Y_{t})=\mu+\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime})\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,u)dxdu,

and the autocovariance function of
Y
is denoted by

\displaystyle\gamma(h)=\gamma_{f}(h)=\mathrm{Cov}(Y_{0},Y_{h})=\kappa_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,-s)f(x,h-s)dxds,

for any
h\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\kappa_{2}=\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})
.

Suppose that the process
Y
is sampled over a fixed-time grid of width
\Delta>0
at times
(n\Delta)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

The proofs of the following results are presented in the Appendix, see
A.2
.

4.1
Asymptotic normality of the sample mean

We denote the sample mean by

\displaystyle\overline{Y}_{n;\Delta}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i\Delta}.

By adapting
Cohen & Lindner 2013
to our more general setting, we get the following asymptotic result for the sample mean.

Theorem 6
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime})=0,\kappa_{2}=\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})<\infty,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and
\Delta>0
.
Further, assume that

\displaystyle\left(F_{\Delta}:\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]\to[0,\infty],(x,u)\mapsto F_{\Delta}(x,u)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x,u+j\Delta)|\right)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]).

(9)

Then
\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}|\gamma(\Delta j)|<\infty
,

\displaystyle V_{\Delta}:=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\gamma(\Delta j)=\kappa_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}f(x,u+j\Delta)\right)^{2}dxdu,

(10)

and the sample mean of
Y_{\Delta i}
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
, is asymptotically Gaussian as
n\to\infty
, i.e.

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\left(\overline{Y}_{n;\Delta}-\mu\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}}}{{\to}}\mathrm{N}\left(0,V_{\Delta}\right),\quad\mathrm{as}\,n\to\infty.

Remark 5
.

As in
Cohen & Lindner 2013
, we remark that the assumption that

\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime})=0
can be removed if the kernel function
f
satisfies
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})
.

4.2
Asymptotic normality of the sample autocovariance

We define the sample autocovariance function as

\displaystyle\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}(\Delta h):=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n-h}(Y_{j\Delta}-\overline{Y}_{n,\Delta})(Y_{(j+h)\Delta}-\overline{Y}_{n,\Delta}),\quad h\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\},

and the sample autocorrelation function as

\displaystyle\widehat{\rho}_{n;\Delta}(\Delta h)=\frac{\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}(\Delta h)}{\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}(0)},\quad h\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}.

In the case when
\mu=0
(and we assumed that
\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime})=0
), one could use the following simpler estimators

\displaystyle\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta h):=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}Y_{j\Delta}Y_{(j+h)\Delta},\quad h\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\},

and

\displaystyle\widehat{\rho}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta h)=\frac{\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta h)}{\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(0)},\quad h\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}.

Consider the case where
\mu=0
, for which we will study the limiting behaviour of

\mathrm{Cov}(\widehat{\gamma}_{n,\Delta}^{*}(\Delta p),\widehat{\gamma}_{n,\Delta}^{*}(\Delta q))
. For this, we need a formula for the fourth (joint) moments of the mixed moving average process, which we derive in the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let
L^{\prime}
be a non-zero Lévy seed with
\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime})=0
and
\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime 4})<\infty
. Let
\kappa_{2}:=\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime 2})=\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})
,
\eta:=\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime 4})/\kappa_{2}^{2}
and
\kappa_{4}:=(\eta-3)\kappa_{2}^{2}
. Let
\Delta>0
, and assume that
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})\cap L^{4}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})
.
Then, for
t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Y_{t_{1}}Y_{t_{2}}Y_{t_{3}}Y_{t_{4}})

\displaystyle=\gamma(t_{1}-t_{2})\gamma(t_{3}-t_{4})+\gamma(t_{1}-t_{3})\gamma(t_{2}-t_{4})+\gamma(t_{1}-t_{4})\gamma(t_{2}-t_{3})

\displaystyle\quad+\kappa_{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,t_{1}-t_{3}+s)f(x,t_{2}-t_{3})f(x,s)f(x,t_{4}-t_{3}+s)dxds.

The following result is an extension of
Cohen & Lindner 2013
.

Proposition 7
.

Let
\mu=0
. Let
L^{\prime}
be a non-zero Lévy seed with
\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime})=0
and
\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime 4})<\infty
. Let
\kappa_{2}:=\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime 2})=\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})
and
\eta:=\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime 4})/\kappa_{2}^{2}
. Let
\Delta>0
, and assume that
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})\cap L^{4}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})
and

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]\to\mathbb{R},(x,u)\mapsto\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}f^{2}(x,u+j\Delta)\right)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]).

(11)

For
q\in\mathbb{Z}
, define the function

\displaystyle\left(G_{q;\Delta}:\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]\to\mathbb{R},(x,u)\mapsto G_{q;\Delta}(x,u)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}f(x,u+j\Delta)f(x,u+(j+q)\Delta)\right),

(12)

which is in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta])

due to (
11
).

Further, assume that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}|\gamma(\Delta j)|^{2}<\infty.

(13)

Then, for each
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lim_{n\to\infty}n\mathrm{Cov}(\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta p),\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta q))=v_{pq;\Delta},\\
v_{pq;\Delta}&:=(\eta-3)\kappa_{2}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]}G_{p;\Delta}(x,u)G_{q;\Delta}(x,u)dxdu\\
&+\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}[\gamma(l\Delta)\gamma((l+p-q)\Delta)+\gamma((l-q)\Delta)\gamma((l+p)\Delta)].\end{split}

(14)

We now state a joint central limit theorem for the sample autocovariance, autocorrelations and their counterparts when
\mu=0
.

Theorem 7
.

1.

Suppose that the assumptions of Proposition
7
hold and that, in addition,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}|f(x,u)||f(x,u+j\Delta)|dxdu\right)^{2}<\infty.

(15)

Then, for each
h\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}(\gamma_{n;\Delta}^{*}(0)-\gamma(0),\ldots,\gamma_{n;\Delta}^{*}(h\Delta)-\gamma(h\Delta))^{\top}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}}}{{\to}}\mathrm{N}(0,V_{\Delta}),\quad n\to\infty,

where the asymptotic covariance matrix is given by
V_{\Delta}=(v_{pq,\Delta})_{p,q=0,\ldots,h}\in\mathbb{R}^{h+1,h+1}
with
v_{pq;\Delta}
defined as in (
14
).

2.

Suppose that the same assumptions as in 1.) hold and further assume that the function

(x,u)\mapsto\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x,u+j\Delta)|
is in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta])
.
Then, for each
h\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}(0)-\gamma(0),\ldots,\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}(h\Delta)-\gamma(h\Delta))^{\top}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}}}{{\to}}\mathrm{N}(0,V_{\Delta}),\quad n\to\infty,

where the asymptotic covariance matrix is given by
V_{\Delta}=(v_{pq;\Delta})_{p,q=0,\ldots,h}\in\mathbb{R}^{h+1,h+1}
with
v_{pq;\Delta}
defined as in (
14
).

3.

Suppose that the same assumptions as in 1.) hold and that
f
is not almost everywhere equal to zero.
Then, for each
h\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}(\rho^{*}_{n;\Delta}(\Delta)-\rho(\Delta),\ldots,\rho^{*}_{n;\Delta}(h\Delta)-\rho(h\Delta))^{\top}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}}}{{\to}}\mathrm{N}(0,W_{\Delta}),\quad n\to\infty,

where the asymptotic covariance matrix is given by
W_{\Delta}=(w_{pq;\Delta})_{p,q=0,\ldots,h}\in\mathbb{R}^{h,h}
with

\displaystyle w_{pq;\Delta}

\displaystyle=(v_{pq;\Delta}-\rho(p\Delta)v_{0q;\Delta}-\rho(q\Delta)v_{p0;\Delta}+\rho(p\Delta)\rho(q\Delta)v_{00;\Delta})/\gamma^{2}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\eta-3)\kappa_{2}^{2}}{\gamma^{2}(0)}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]}(G_{p;\Delta}(x,u)-G_{0;\Delta}(x,u)\rho(p\Delta))

\displaystyle\quad\cdot(G_{q;\Delta}(x,u)-G_{0;\Delta}(x,u)\rho(q\Delta))dxdu

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\rho((l+q)\Delta)\rho((l+p)\Delta)+\rho((l-q)\Delta)\rho((l+p)\Delta)\right.

\displaystyle-2\rho((l+q)\Delta)\rho(l\Delta)\rho(p\Delta)-2\rho(l\Delta)\rho((l+p)\Delta)\rho(q\Delta)

\displaystyle\left.+2\rho(p\Delta)\rho(q\Delta)\rho^{2}(l\Delta)\right].

If, in addition, the function

is in

, then we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\rho}_{n,\Delta}(\Delta)-\rho(\Delta),\ldots,\widehat{\rho}_{n,\Delta}(h\Delta)-\rho(h\Delta))^{\top}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}}}{{\to}}\mathrm{N}(0,W_{\Delta}),\quad n\to\infty,

with

5
Inference for periodic trawl processes using methods of moments

Based on two working examples, we will now develop an estimation and inference methodology for the parameters determining the serial correlation of (periodic) trawl processes using a method-of-moments approach and present the corresponding asymptotic theory. Our methodology can in principle be applied to general trawl functions, but in order to simplify the exposition, we will concentrate on two particularly relevant specifications, namely the exponential trawl and the supGamma trawl function.

5.1
Exponential trawl function

Recall that, in the case of an exponential trawl function
g(x)=\exp(-\lambda x),\lambda>0,x\geq 0
, the autocorrelation is, for all
t>0
, given by

\displaystyle\rho(t)=\mathrm{Cor}(Y_{0},Y_{t})=c(t)\exp(-\lambda t),

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\lambda=\log\left(\frac{\rho(t)}{c(t)}\right)/(-t).

Setting
t=\Delta
and assuming (for now) that
c(\Delta)
is known (for a (non-periodic) trawl process, we would have
c\equiv 1
), we get the following estimator

\displaystyle\hat{\lambda}=-\log\left(\frac{\rho_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta)}{c(\Delta)}\right),

where
\widehat{\rho}(\cdot)
denotes the empirical autocorrelation function.

We now establish the corresponding limit theorem.
From Theorem
7
, assuming the corresponding assumptions hold, we deduce
that

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}(\rho_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta)-\rho(\Delta))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}}}{{\to}}\mathrm{N}(0,w_{11;\Delta}).

Next, we define the function

\phi:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R},\phi(x):=-\log(x/c(\Delta))
, which is continuously differentiable with
\phi^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{1}{x}

and set

\displaystyle\widehat{\lambda}:=\phi(\rho_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta)).

An application of the delta method, see
Brockwell & Davis 1987
,
leads to

6
Inference for periodic trawl processes using a generalised-method-of-moments approach

In the previous section, we showed how the memory parameter and periodic function of a periodic trawl process can be inferred if the period is known.
More generally, if we would like to estimate all model parameters, including the parameters of the Lévy basis, we can proceed by using
the generalised method of moments (GMM).

Hence, we will now develop the asymptotic theory for estimating the parameters of a periodic trawl process using a generalised method of moments (GMM). This extends the work presented in
Bennedsen et al. 2023
for (integer-valued) trawl processes to the case of periodic trawl processes.

Recall that we denote the periodic trawl process by

\displaystyle Y_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}p(t-s)\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(t-s))}(x)\mathbb{I}_{[0,\infty)}(t-s)L(dx,ds),

and we denote the corresponding trawl process, where
p\equiv 1
, by

\displaystyle X_{t}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(t-s))}(x)\mathbb{I}_{[0,\infty)}(t-s)L(dx,ds).

(16)

6.1
Weak dependence

We will first show that periodic trawl processes are
\theta
-weakly dependent, see
Curato & Stelzer 2019
.

We note that a periodic trawl process is
a special case of a causal mixed moving average process as defined in

Curato & Stelzer 2019
.
Hence, under the assumption that
\int_{|\xi|>1}|\xi|^{2}\ell(d\xi)<\infty
, we can deduce from
Curato & Stelzer 2019

the periodic trawl process is
\theta
-weakly dependent, see
Curato & Stelzer 2019
with coefficient, for
r\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\theta_{Y}(r)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})\int_{(-\infty,-r)\times\mathbb{R}}p^{2}(-s)\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(-s))}^{2}(x)\mathbb{I}_{[0,\infty)}^{2}(-s)dxds\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left|\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime})\int_{(-\infty,-r)\times\mathbb{R}}p(-s)\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(-s))}(x)\mathbb{I}_{[0,\infty)}(-s)dxds\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})\int_{-\infty}^{-r}p^{2}(-s)g(-s)ds+(\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime}))^{2}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{-r}p(-s)g(-s)ds\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})\int_{r}^{\infty}p^{2}(s)g(s)ds+(\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime}))^{2}\left(\int_{r}^{\infty}p(s)g(s)ds\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Since the periodic function is continuous and bounded, we note that the weak-dependence coefficient can be bounded by a function
\widetilde{\theta}_{Y}
such that

\theta_{Y}(r)\leq\widetilde{\theta}_{Y}(r)
for all
r\geq 0
, where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\theta}_{Y}(r)

\displaystyle=\left(c_{1}\mathrm{Cov}(X_{0},X_{r})+c_{2}(\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime}))^{2}(\mathrm{Cov}(X_{0},X_{r}))^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where
\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime})=\gamma+\int_{|\xi|>1}\ell(d\xi)
, and

\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})=a+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\xi^{2}\ell(d\xi)
and
c_{1},c_{2}>0
are constants.

Let us briefly consider the special case when
L^{\prime}
is of finite variation, i.e. when the characteristic triplet is given by
(\gamma,0,\ell)
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi|\ell(\xi)<\infty
.
This case is of interest since it includes the well-known class of integer-valued trawl processes.
Here, the weak dependence coefficient is, for
r\geq 0
, given by

\displaystyle\theta_{Y}(r)

\displaystyle=\int_{(-\infty,-r)\times\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|p(-s)\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(-s))}(x)\mathbb{I}_{[0,\infty)}(-s)\xi|\ell(d\xi)dxds

\displaystyle+\int_{(-\infty,-r)\times\mathbb{R}}|p(-s)\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(-s))}(x)\mathbb{I}_{[0,\infty)}(-s)\gamma_{0}|dxds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi|\ell(d\xi)\int_{-\infty}^{-r}|p(-s)|g(-s)ds+|\gamma_{0}|\int_{-\infty}^{-r}|p(-s)|g(-s)ds

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi|\ell(d\xi)+|\gamma_{0}|\right)\int_{r}^{\infty}|p(s)|g(s)ds.

As before,
we note that the weak-dependence coefficient can be bounded by a function
\widetilde{\theta}_{Y}
such that

\theta_{Y}(r)\leq\widetilde{\theta}_{Y}(r)
for all
r\geq 0
, where

\displaystyle\widetilde{\theta}_{Y}(r)

\displaystyle=c\mathrm{Cov}(X_{0},X_{r}),

for a positive constant
c>0
and
\gamma_{0}=\gamma-\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}\xi\ell(d\xi)

6.2
GMM estimation for periodic trawl processes

In this section, we will describe how the parameters of a periodic trawl process can be estimated via the generalised method of moments (GMM).

As before, we consider the equidistantly sampled process
Y_{\Delta},Y_{2\Delta},\ldots,Y_{n\Delta}
, for
\Delta=T/n>0,T>0,n\in\mathbb{N}
. We will consider a GMM estimator, which is based on the sample mean, sample variance and sample autocovariances up to lag
m\geq 2
.

Consider the vector

Y_{t}^{(m)}=(Y_{t\Delta},Y_{(t+1)\Delta},\ldots,Y_{(t+m)\Delta}),

for
t=1,\ldots,n-m
. We denote by
\Theta
the parameter space of the periodic trawl process and set
\mu:=\mu(\theta)=\mathbb{E}(Y_{0})
and
D(k):=D(k,\theta):=\mathbb{E}(Y_{0}Y_{k\Delta})
, for
k=0,\ldots,m
. So, as soon as we specify a parametric model for
Y
, then
D(k)
is a function of the model parameter(s)
\theta
.

Next, we define the measurable function
h:\mathbb{R}^{m+1}\times\Theta\to\mathbb{R}^{m+2}
by

\displaystyle h(Y_{t}^{(m)},\theta)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}h_{E}(Y_{t}^{(m)},\theta)\\
h_{0}(Y_{t}^{(m)},\theta)\\
h_{1}(Y_{t}^{(m)},\theta)\\
\vdots\\
h_{m}(Y_{t}^{(m)},\theta)\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}Y_{t\Delta}-\mu(\theta)\\
Y_{t\Delta}^{2}-D(0,\theta)\\
Y_{t\Delta}Y_{(t+1)\Delta}-D(1,\theta)\\
\vdots\\
Y_{t\Delta}Y_{(t+m)\Delta}-D(m,\theta)\end{array}\right).

Moreover, define the corresponding sample moments as

\displaystyle g_{n,m}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n-m}\sum_{t=1}^{n-m}h(Y_{t}^{(m)},\theta)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{1}{n-m}\sum_{t=1}^{n-m}h_{E}(Y_{t}^{(m)},\theta)\\
\frac{1}{n-m}\sum_{t=1}^{n-m}h_{0}(Y_{t}^{(m)},\theta)\\
\vdots\\
\frac{1}{n-m}\sum_{t=1}^{n-m}h_{m}(Y_{t}^{(m)},\theta)\end{array}\right).

Suppose that the true parameter (vector) is denoted by
\theta_{0}
, say, which can be estimated by minimising the objective function of the GMM, i.e. the GMM estimator is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\theta}_{0,\mathrm{GMM}}^{n,m}=\mathrm{argmin}g_{n,m}(\theta)^{\top}A_{n,m}g_{n,m}(\theta),

(17)

for a positive-definite weight matrix
A_{n,m}
.

We now derive the weak consistency and asymptotic normality of the GMM estimator under suitable (standard) assumptions.

First, we present the assumptions which guarantee the weak consistency of the estimator, cf.
Mátyás 1999

Assumption 1

In future work, it will be interesting to further extend and fine-tune the methodology, in particular also the GMM methodology derived in Section

8
Conclusion and outlook

This article introduced an alternative definition of periodic trawl processes compared to the one given in
Barndorff-Nielsen et al. 2014
, which appears to have slightly higher analytical tractability.
We derived some of the key probabilistic properties of periodic trawl processes and have studied relevant examples including both short- and long-memory settings. We showed that a slice method can be used to simulate periodic trawl processes effectively.
Under suitable technical conditions, which currently only cover short-memory scenarios, we have proved the asymptotic normality of the sample mean, sample autocovariances, sample autocorrelations and the GMM-estimator.
We have implemented the proposed methodology in
R
and showed that the serial correlation of periodic trawl processes
describes the empirical one of electricity spot prices very well.
These are promising results, which suggest that it will be worthwhile to further advance the theory and statistical methodology for periodic trawl processes.
For instance, for the GMM approach to be applicable in practice, we need to specify suitable identifiability conditions
for fully parametric settings. It will also be interesting to account for multiple periods, for instance, including weekly and yearly periodicities, and tailor methods from spectral analysis to such settings.
Moreover, model selection tools are needed for choosing
the appropriate Lévy basis. For the particular application considered here, we note that our framework allows for a smooth
(Gaussian) noise terms as well as for jumps, where the latter can model the positive and even more dominant negative jumps, as in
Veraart & Veraart 2014
, but also the more volatile behaviour observed in the most recent electricity prices. That said, in future research, we should also investigate whether including a stochastic volatility term, either via temporal or spatial stochastic scaling of the periodic trawl process is needed to describe the data even better, which would bring us into the general framework of ambit fields and processes, see
Barndorff-Nielsen et al. 2018
.

From a theoretical point of view, it will be interesting to develop an asymptotic theory for periodic trawl processes which can allow for long memory settings. We would then need to check how well such an asymptotic theory works in finite samples to decide whether confidence bounds for the obtained parameters can be constructed based on asymptotic guarantees or whether bootstrap approaches might be more useful in practice.

\displaystyle\psi((a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4});(Y_{t_{1}},Y_{t_{2}},Y_{t_{3}},Y_{t_{4}})):=\mathbb{E}(\exp(i(a_{1}Y_{t_{1}}+a_{2}Y_{t_{2}}+a_{3}Y_{t_{3}}+a_{4}Y_{t_{4}}))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\left(i\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{4}a_{j}f(x,t_{j}-s)\right)L(ds,dx)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\exp\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{4}a_{j}f(x,t_{j}-s);L^{\prime}\right)dxds\right],

where
C(\cdot;L^{\prime})
denotes the cumulant function of the Lévy seed
L^{\prime}
, which we will present next.

Suppose
L^{\prime}
has characteristic triplet
(c,A,\nu)
w.r.t. the truncation function
\tau(y)=\mathbb{I}_{[-1,1]}(y)
. I.e. we have the following representation for its characteristic function, for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\exp(i\theta L^{\prime}))=\exp\left(ic\theta-\frac{1}{2}A\theta^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{iy\theta}-1-i\theta y\tau(y))\nu(dy)\right).

We recall that
\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime})=c+\int_{\mathbb{R}}y(1-\tau(y))\nu(dy)
. Since we are assuming that
\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime})=0
, we get that
c=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}y(1-\tau(y))\nu(dy)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}y\mathbb{I}_{[-1,1]^{c}}(y)\nu(dy)
and, hence,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\exp(i\theta L^{\prime}))=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}A\theta^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{iy\theta}-1-i\theta y)\nu(dy)\right).

I.e. the corresponding cumulant function is given by

\displaystyle C(\theta;L^{\prime})=-\frac{1}{2}A\theta^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}(e^{iy\theta}-1-i\theta y)\nu(dy).

Moreover,

\displaystyle C((a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4});(Y_{t_{1}},Y_{t_{2}},Y_{t_{3}},Y_{t_{4}})):=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{4}a_{j}f(x,t_{j}-s);L^{\prime}\right)dxds

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}A\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{4}a_{j}f(x,t_{j}-s)\right)^{2}dxds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{iy\sum_{j=1}^{4}a_{j}f(x,t_{j}-s)}-1-i\sum_{j=1}^{4}a_{j}f(x,t_{j}-s)y\right)\nu(dy)dxds

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}A\sum_{j,k=1}^{4}a_{j}a_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,t_{j}-s)f(x,t_{k}-s)dxds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{iy\sum_{j=1}^{4}a_{j}f(x,t_{j}-s)}-1-i\sum_{j=1}^{4}a_{j}f(x,t_{j}-s)y\right)\nu(dy)dxds

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}A\sum_{j=1}^{4}a_{j}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f^{2}(x,t_{j}-s)dxds-\frac{1}{2}A\sum_{j,k=1,j\not=k}^{4}a_{j}a_{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,t_{j}-s)f(x,t_{k}-s)dxds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(e^{iy\sum_{j=1}^{4}a_{j}f(x,t_{j}-s)}-1-i\sum_{j=1}^{4}a_{j}f(x,t_{j}-s)y\right)\nu(dy)dxds.

Next, we compute the fourth moments, where we recall that

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial^{4}}{\partial a_{1}\partial a_{2}\partial a_{3}\partial a_{4}}\psi((a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4});(Y_{t_{1}},Y_{t_{2}},Y_{t_{3}},Y_{t_{4}}))\right|_{a_{1}=a_{2}=a_{3}=a_{4}=0}=\mathbb{E}(Y_{t_{1}}Y_{t_{2}}Y_{t_{3}}Y_{t_{4}}).

We now abbreviate the functions to
\psi
and
C
without stating their arguments and a subscript denotes the corresponding partial derivative, e.g.
C_{a_{1}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{1}}C((a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4});(Y_{t_{1}},Y_{t_{2}},Y_{t_{3}},Y_{t_{4}})
and similarly for higher order partial derivatives.
Since
\psi=\exp(C)
, we have

\displaystyle\psi_{a1}

\displaystyle=\psi C_{a_{1}},

\displaystyle\psi_{a_{1},a_{2}}

\displaystyle=\psi[C_{a_{1},a_{2}}+C_{a_{1}}C_{a_{2}}],

\displaystyle\psi_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}}

\displaystyle=\psi[(C_{a_{1},a_{2}}+C_{a_{1}}C_{a_{2}})C_{a_{3}}+C_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}}+C_{a_{1},a_{3}}C_{a_{2}}+C_{a_{1}}C_{a_{2},a_{3}}]

\displaystyle=\psi[C_{a_{1}}C_{a_{2}}C_{a_{3}}+C_{a_{1}}C_{a_{2},a_{3}}+C_{a_{2}}C_{a_{1},a_{3}}+C_{a_{3}}C_{a_{1},a_{2}}++C_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}}],

\displaystyle\psi_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}}

\displaystyle=\psi[C_{a_{1}}C_{a_{2}}C_{a_{3}}C_{a_{4}}+C_{a_{1}}C_{a_{2},a_{3}}C_{a_{4}}+C_{a_{2}}C_{a_{1},a_{3}}C_{a_{4}}+C_{a_{3}}C_{a_{1},a_{2}}C_{a_{4}}+C_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}}C_{a_{4}}

\displaystyle+C_{a_{1},a_{4}}C_{a_{2}}C_{a_{3}}+C_{a_{1}}C_{a_{2},a_{4}}C_{a_{3}}+C_{a_{1}}C_{a_{2}}C_{a_{3},a_{4}}+C_{a_{1},a_{4}}C_{a_{2},a_{3}}+C_{a_{1}}C_{a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}}

\displaystyle+C_{a_{2},a_{4}}C_{a_{1},a_{3}}+C_{a_{2}}C_{a_{1},a_{3},a_{4}}+C_{a_{3},a_{4}}C_{a_{1},a_{2}}+C_{a_{3}}C_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{4}}+C_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}}].

Here we have

\displaystyle C_{a_{i}}|_{a_{1}=a_{2}=a_{3}=a_{4}=0}

\displaystyle=0,\quad\mathrm{for}\;i=1,2,3,4,

\displaystyle C_{a_{i},a_{j}}|_{a_{1}=a_{2}=a_{3}=a_{4}=0}

\displaystyle C_{a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\prod_{j=1}^{4}f(x,t_{j}-s)dxds\int_{\mathbb{R}}y^{4}\nu(dy).

The above results imply that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Y_{t_{1}}Y_{t_{2}}Y_{t_{3}}Y_{t_{4}})

\displaystyle=\left(A+\int_{\mathbb{R}}y^{2}\nu(dy)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,t_{1}-s)f(x,t_{2}-s)dxds\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,t_{3}-s)f(x,t_{4}-s)dxds\right.

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,t_{1}-s)f(x,t_{3}-s)dxds\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,t_{2}-s)f(x,t_{4}-s)dxds

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,t_{1}-s)f(x,t_{4}-s)dxds\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,t_{2}-s)f(x,t_{3}-s)dxds\right)

\kappa_{4}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}y^{4}\nu(dy)=(\eta-3)\kappa_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Y_{t_{1}}Y_{t_{2}}Y_{t_{3}}Y_{t_{4}})

\displaystyle=\left(A+\int_{\mathbb{R}}y^{2}\nu(dy)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,t_{1}-t_{2}+s)f(x,s)dxds\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,t_{3}-t_{4}+s)f(x,s)dxds\right.

\displaystyle=\gamma(t_{1}-t_{2})\gamma(t_{3}-t_{4})+\gamma(t_{1}-t_{3})\gamma(t_{2}-t_{4})+\gamma(t_{1}-t_{4})\gamma(t_{2}-t_{3})

\displaystyle+\kappa_{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,t_{1}-t_{3}+s)f(x,t_{2}-t_{3})f(x,s)f(x,t_{4}-t_{3}+s)dxds.

Proof of Proposition
7
.

We first expand the covariance of the sample autocovariances as follows

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta p),\widehat{\gamma}_{n,\Delta}^{*}(\Delta q))=\mathbb{E}(\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta p)\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta q))-\mathbb{E}(\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta p))\mathbb{E}(\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta q)),

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta p))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(Y_{j\Delta}Y_{(j+p)\Delta})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\gamma(p\Delta)=\gamma(p\Delta),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta q))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(Y_{k\Delta}Y_{(k+q)\Delta})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\gamma(q\Delta)=\gamma(q\Delta).

Also,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta p)\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta q))=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(Y_{j\Delta}Y_{(j+p)\Delta}Y_{k\Delta}Y_{(k+q)\Delta}),

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(Y_{j\Delta}Y_{(j+p)\Delta}Y_{k\Delta}Y_{(k+q)\Delta})

\displaystyle=\gamma(p\Delta)\gamma(q\Delta)+\gamma((k-j)\Delta)\gamma((j+p-k-q)\Delta)+\gamma((j-k-q)\Delta)\gamma((j+p-k)\Delta)

\displaystyle\quad+\kappa_{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,(j-k)\Delta+s)f(x,(j-k+p)\Delta+s)f(x,s)f(x,q\Delta+s)dxds

\displaystyle=\gamma(p\Delta)\gamma(q\Delta)+\gamma((j-k)\Delta)\gamma((j-k+p-q)\Delta)+\gamma((j-k-q)\Delta)\gamma((j-k+p)\Delta)

\displaystyle\quad+\kappa_{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,(j-k)\Delta+s)f(x,(j-k+p)\Delta+s)f(x,s)f(x,q\Delta+s)dxds

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l=j-k}}{{=}}\gamma(p\Delta)\gamma(q\Delta)+\gamma(l\Delta)\gamma((l+p-q)\Delta)+\gamma((l-q)\Delta)\gamma((l+p)\Delta)

\displaystyle\quad+\kappa_{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,l\Delta+s)f(x,(l+p)\Delta+s)f(x,s)f(x,q\Delta+s)dxds.

Now we subtract
\gamma(p\Delta)\gamma(q\Delta)
, we set
l=j-k
, interchange the order of summation and use the stationarity to obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta p),\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta q))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}(Y_{j\Delta}Y_{(j+p)\Delta}Y_{k\Delta}Y_{(k+q)\Delta})-\gamma(p\Delta)\gamma(q\Delta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\gamma((j-k)\Delta)\gamma((j-k+p-q)\Delta)+\gamma((j-k-q)\Delta)\gamma((j-k+p)\Delta)\right.

\displaystyle\quad+\left.\kappa_{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,(j-k)\Delta+s)f(x,(j-k+p)\Delta+s)f(x,s)f(x,q\Delta+s)dxds\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{|l|<n}\sum_{k=1}^{n-|l|}\left(\gamma(l\Delta)\gamma((l+p-q)\Delta)+\gamma((l-q)\Delta)\gamma((l+p)\Delta)\right.

\displaystyle\quad+\left.\kappa_{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,l\Delta+s)f(x,(l+p)\Delta+s)f(x,s)f(x,q\Delta+s)dxds\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{|l|<n}(n-|l|)T_{l,p,q;\Delta}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{|l|<n}\left(1-\frac{|l|}{n}\right)T_{l,p,q;\Delta},

where

\displaystyle T_{l,p,q;\Delta}

\displaystyle:=\gamma(l\Delta)\gamma((l+p-q)\Delta)+\gamma((l-q)\Delta)\gamma((l+p)\Delta)

\displaystyle\quad+\kappa_{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}f(x,l\Delta+s)f(x,(l+p)\Delta+s)f(x,s)f(x,q\Delta+s)dxds.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}n\mathrm{Cov}(\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta p),\widehat{\gamma}_{n;\Delta}^{*}(\Delta q))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{|l|<n}\left(1-\frac{|l|}{n}\right)T_{l,p,q;\Delta}=\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}T_{l,p,q;\Delta},

where

\displaystyle\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}T_{l,p,q;\Delta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}[\gamma(l\Delta)\gamma((l+p-q)\Delta)+\gamma((l-q)\Delta)\gamma((l+p)\Delta)]

\displaystyle\quad+\kappa_{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}f(x,l\Delta+s)f(x,(l+p)\Delta+s)f(x,s)f(x,q\Delta+s)dxds,

by the Dominated Convergence Theorem since (
13
) holds. More precisely, let us justify why
\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}|T_{l,p,q;\Delta}|<\infty
.
The finiteness of

\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}|\gamma(l\Delta)\gamma((l+p-q)\Delta)+\gamma((l-q)\Delta)\gamma((l+p)\Delta)|
follows from (
13
).
For the second term, for
q\in\mathbb{Z}
, define

\displaystyle\left(\widetilde{G}_{q;\Delta}:\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]\to\mathbb{R},(x,u)\mapsto\widetilde{G}_{q;\Delta}(x,u)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x,u+j\Delta)||f(x,u+(j+q)\Delta)|\right),

which is in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta])

due to (
11
). We consider the periodic continuation of
\widetilde{G}_{q;\Delta}
and set

\displaystyle\left(\widetilde{G}_{q;\Delta}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},(x,u)\mapsto\widetilde{G}_{q;\Delta}(x,u)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x,u+j\Delta)||f(x,u+(j+q)\Delta)|\right).

Since
\widetilde{G}_{q;\Delta}
is periodic and, restricted to
\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]
square-integrable, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x,l\Delta+s)f(x,(l+p)\Delta+s)||f(x,s)f(x,q\Delta+s)|dxds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{G}_{p}(s)|f(x,s)f(x,q\Delta+s)|dxds=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times[j\Delta,(j+1)\Delta]}\widetilde{G}_{p}(s)|f(x,s)f(x,q\Delta+s)|dxds

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]}\widetilde{G}_{p}(s+j\Delta)|f(x,s+j\Delta)f(x,(q+j)\Delta+s)|dxds

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]}\widetilde{G}_{p}(s)|f(x,s+j\Delta)f(x,(q+j)\Delta+s)|dxds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]}\widetilde{G}_{p}(s)\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x,s+j\Delta)f(x,(q+j)\Delta+s)|dxds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]}\widetilde{G}_{p}(s)\widetilde{G}_{q}(s)dxds<\infty.

Equation (
14
) follows from the same calculations as above without the modulus sign in the definition of
\widetilde{G}
.

As in the proof for the sample mean, define the function

\lim_{m\to\infty}\gamma_{m}(j\Delta)=\gamma(j\Delta)

\displaystyle w_{pq;\Delta}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\rho((l+q)\Delta)\rho((l+p)\Delta)+\rho((l-q)\Delta)\rho((l+p)\Delta)\right.

\displaystyle-2\rho((l+q)\Delta)\rho(l\Delta)\rho(p\Delta)-2\rho(l\Delta)\rho((l+p)\Delta)\rho(q\Delta)

\displaystyle\left.+2\rho(p\Delta)\rho(q\Delta)\rho^{2}(l\Delta)\right].

Hence,

\displaystyle w_{pq;\Delta}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\eta-3)\kappa_{2}^{2}}{\gamma^{2}(0)}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]}(G_{p;\Delta}(x,u)-G_{0;\Delta}(x,u)\rho(p\Delta))

\displaystyle\quad\cdot(G_{q;\Delta}(x,u)-G_{0;\Delta}(x,u)\rho(q\Delta))dxdu

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\rho((l+q)\Delta)\rho((l+p)\Delta)+\rho((l-q)\Delta)\rho((l+p)\Delta)\right.

\displaystyle-2\rho((l+q)\Delta)\rho(l\Delta)\rho(p\Delta)-2\rho(l\Delta)\rho((l+p)\Delta)\rho(q\Delta)

\displaystyle\left.+2\rho(p\Delta)\rho(q\Delta)\rho^{2}(l\Delta)\right].

∎

A.3
Examples

In this subsection, we show how the asymptotic variances appearing in the asymptotic theory for the sample mean can be computed for trawl processes with either an exponential or a supGamma trawl function.

Note that, in the case when
p\equiv 1
, i.e. for the (non-periodic) trawl process, we get

\displaystyle\gamma(h)=\mathrm{Cov}(Y_{t},Y_{t+h})=\int_{|h|}^{\infty}g(u)du,

for
t,h\in\mathbb{R}
.

A.3.1
Exponential trawl

Consider the case of an exponential trawl function with

g(x)=\exp(-\lambda x)
, for
x\geq 0
.
The autocovariance function is given by

\displaystyle\gamma(t)=\mathrm{Cov}(Y_{t},Y_{0})=\frac{1}{\lambda}e^{-\lambda|t|},

for
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
and the
autocorrelation function is given by

\displaystyle\rho(t)=\mathrm{Cor}(Y_{t},Y_{0})=e^{-\lambda|t|},

for
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

For the sample mean, we have the following result.
Suppose that
\mathbb{E}(L^{\prime})=0,\kappa_{2}=\mathrm{Var}(L^{\prime})<\infty,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and
\Delta>0
.
Then

\displaystyle\left(F_{\Delta}:\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]\to[0,\infty],(x,u)\mapsto F_{\Delta}(x,u)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x,u+j\Delta)|\right)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta])

since

\displaystyle F_{\Delta}(x,u)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x,u+j\Delta)|=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(u+j\Delta)}(x),

and we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]}|F_{\Delta}(x,u)|^{2}dxdu

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(u+j\Delta)}(x)\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(u+k\Delta)}(x)dxdu

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]}\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(u+j\Delta)}(x)\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(u+k\Delta)}(x)dxdu

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{[0,\Delta]}\min\{g(u+j\Delta),g(u+k\Delta)\}du

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{[0,\Delta]}g(u+j\Delta)du+2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=j+1}^{\infty}\int_{[0,\Delta]}g(u+k\Delta)du

where we applied Tonelli’s theorem.

Then
\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}|\gamma(\Delta j)|<\infty
,

\displaystyle V_{\Delta}:=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\gamma(\Delta j)=\kappa_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}f(x,u+j\Delta)\right)^{2}dxdu,

(21)

and the sample mean of
Y_{\Delta i}
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
, is asymptotically Gaussian as
n\to\infty
, i.e.

\displaystyle\sqrt{n}\left(\overline{Y}_{n;\Delta}-\mu\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{d}}}{{\to}}\mathrm{N}\left(0,V_{\Delta}\right),\quad\mathrm{as}\,n\to\infty.

For the case of an exponential trawl function, we get

\displaystyle V_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\gamma(\Delta j)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda}e^{-\lambda|j|\Delta}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(1+2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda j\Delta}\right)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(1+2\frac{e^{-\lambda\Delta}}{1-e^{-\lambda\Delta}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1+e^{-\lambda\Delta}}{\lambda(1-e^{-\lambda\Delta})}=\frac{1+e^{\lambda\Delta}}{\lambda(e^{\lambda\Delta}-1)}.

A.3.2
SupGamma trawl

In the case when

g(x)=(1+x/\alpha)^{-H}
,
\alpha>0,H>2
, i.e. we require a short-memory setting,
x\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\gamma(h)=\int_{|h|}^{\infty}g(x)dx=\frac{\alpha}{H-1}\left(1+\frac{|h|}{\alpha}\right)^{1-H}.

Then

\displaystyle V_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\gamma(j\Delta)=\frac{\alpha}{H-1}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{|j|\Delta}{\alpha}\right)^{1-H}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{H-1}\left(\frac{\Delta}{\alpha}\right)^{1-H}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\Delta}+|j|\right)^{1-H}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{H-1}\left(\frac{\Delta}{\alpha}\right)^{1-H}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\Delta}\right)^{1-H}\left[2\left(\frac{\alpha}{\Delta}\right)^{H-1}\zeta(H-1,\alpha/\Delta)-1\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{H-1}\left[2\left(\frac{\alpha}{\Delta}\right)^{H-1}\zeta(H-1,\alpha/\Delta)-1\right],

where
\zeta
denotes the Hurwitz Zeta function defined by

\zeta(s,a)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+a)^{s}}
,
for
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
.

A.4
Verifying the assumptions of Theorem
7
for selected periodic trawl processes

For the applications discussed in Section
5
, we need to verify the condition (
11
)
from Proposition
7

and Assumption (
15
) from
Theorem
7

assuming that the corresponding moment assumptions for the Lévy seed hold.

For both conditions, it is sufficient to check that a (non-periodic) trawl process satisfies the stated conditions since the periodic function is bounded. Hence, in the following, we shall set
p\equiv 1
.

A.4.1
Verifying Condition (
11
)
from Proposition
7

We need to check that

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]\to\mathbb{R},(x,u)\mapsto\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}f^{2}(x,u+j\Delta)\right)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]).

This condition holds for trawl processes if, for
(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}f^{2}(x,u+j\Delta)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}f(x,u+j\Delta)=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(u+j\Delta))}(x)\mathbb{I}_{[0,\infty)}(u+j\Delta)=F_{\Delta}(x,u)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]).

This is equivalent to checking that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]}|F_{\Delta}(x,u)|^{2}dxdu\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times[0,\Delta]}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(u+j\Delta))}(x)\mathbb{I}_{[0,\infty)}(u+j\Delta)\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(u+k\Delta))}(x)\mathbb{I}_{[0,\infty)}(u+k\Delta)dxdu\\
&\propto\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\gamma(j\Delta)<\infty.\end{split}

(22)

This condition is satisfied both for an exponential trawl function and also for a supGamma trawl function with short memory. In the latter case, we have that
\gamma(x)\propto(1+|x|/\alpha)^{1-H}
for
\alpha>0,H>2
. Then, the finiteness of (
22
) follows using the
\zeta
-function representation.

A.4.2
Verifying Assumption (
15
) from
Theorem
7

We need to verify

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}|f(x,u)||f(x,u+j\Delta)|dxdu\right)^{2}<\infty.

Using very similar computations as before, we find that the above condition is equivalent to

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}|f(x,u)||f(x,u+j\Delta)|dxdu\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(u)}(x)\mathbb{I}_{[0,\infty)}(u)\mathbb{I}_{(0,g(u+j\Delta))}(x)\mathbb{I}_{[0,\infty)}(u+j\Delta)dxdu\right)^{2}\propto\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\gamma^{2}(j\Delta)<\infty,

which is satisfied by the exponential trawl function and the supGamma trawl functions with
H>3/2
, which includes some long-memory settings.

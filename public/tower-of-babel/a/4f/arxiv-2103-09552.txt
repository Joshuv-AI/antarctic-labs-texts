Abstract
In 2020, Alexander Grigor’yan, Yong Lin and Shing-Tung Yau [ 4 ] introduced the Reidemeister torsion and the analytic torsion for digraphs by means of the path complex and the path homology theory. Based on the analytic torsion for digraphs introduced in [ 4 ] , we consider the notion of weighted analytic torsion for vertex-weighted digraphs. For any non-vanishing real functions f f and g g on the vertex set, we consider the vertex-weighted digraphs with the weights ( f , g ) (f,g) . We calculate the ( f , g ) (f,g) -weighted analytic torsion by examples and prove that the ( f , g ) (f,g) -weighted analytic torsion only depend on the ratio f / g f/g . In particular, if the weight is of the diagonal form ( f , f ) (f,f) , then the weighted analytic torsion equals to the usual (un-weighted) torsion.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

and

be two chain complexes. For each nonnegative integer

be an inner product on

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(C_{*},\langle~,~\rangle_{*})=\varphi^{-1}\circ\Delta_{n}(C^{\prime}_{*},\langle~,~\rangle^{\prime}_{*})\circ\varphi

\displaystyle\partial^{\prime}_{n}\varphi=\varphi\partial_{n}.

(2.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\varphi(a),\varphi\partial_{n+1}(e)\rangle^{\prime}

\displaystyle\langle a,\partial_{n+1}(e)\rangle

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose all the assumptions in Lemma

are satisfied. Then
by choosing the orthonormal bases properly, the matrix representatives of the Hodge-Laplace operators are equal

\displaystyle[\Delta_{n}(C_{*},\langle~,~\rangle_{*})]=[\Delta_{n}(C^{\prime}_{*},\langle~,~\rangle^{\prime}_{*})].

\displaystyle T(C_{*},\langle~,~\rangle_{*})=T(C^{\prime}_{*},\langle~,~\rangle^{\prime}_{*}).

We observe that both

. Then

\displaystyle T(G,cf,g)=t(G,c)^{-1}T(G,f,g).

(4.6)

Proof.

Let
n
be a nonnegative integer.
It follows directly that

\displaystyle\partial_{n}^{cf}=c\partial_{n}^{f}.

(4.7)

By a similar argument with the proof of Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\langle(\partial_{n+1}^{cf})^{*}u,w\rangle_{g}=c\langle(\partial_{n+1}^{f})^{*}u,w\rangle_{g}

for any chains
u,w\in\Omega_{n}(G)
. This implies that

\displaystyle(\partial_{n+1}^{cf})^{*}=c(\partial_{n+1}^{f})^{*}.

(4.8)

By (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}^{cf,g}=c^{2}\Delta_{n}^{f,g}.

Consequently, if we denote the non-zero eigenvalues of
\Delta_{n}^{f,g}
as
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\ldots
, then the non-zero eigenvalues of
\Delta_{n}^{cf,g}
are
0<c^{2}\lambda_{1}<c^{2}\lambda_{2}<\ldots
where
c^{2}\lambda_{i}
has the same multiplicity with
\lambda_{i}
.
Therefore, with the help of
[
4
, (3.21)]
, we have

\displaystyle\log T(G,cf,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^{n}n\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}\Big(\sum_{\lambda_{i}>0}\frac{1}{(c^{2}\lambda_{i})^{s}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^{n}n\big(-\sum_{\lambda_{i}>0}\log(c^{2}\lambda_{i})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^{n}n\big(-\sum_{\lambda_{i}>0}\log(\lambda_{i})\big)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^{n}n(2\log|c|)\big(\dim\Omega_{n}(G)-\dim H_{n}(G)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{N}(-1)^{n}n\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}\Big(\sum_{\lambda_{i}>0}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}^{s}}\Big)-(\log|c|)s(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log T(G,f,g)-(\log|c|)s(G).

Taking the exponential map on both sides of the equations, we have
(
4.6

Example 5.1
.

Consider the line digraph
G=(V,E)
given in
[
4
, Figure 1, Example 3.9]
where
V=\{0,1,2,3,4\}
and
E
consists of
4
directed edges where the
i
-th directed edge has the form either
(i,i+1)
or
(i+1,i)
, for
i=0,1,2,3
. Let
f
and
g
be two non-vanishing real-valued weight functions on
V
. As in
[
4
, (3.17)]
, we denote
\bar{e}_{i(i+1)}
for the directed edge (which is called the
1
-path in our setting)
(i,i+1)
or
(i+1,i)
in
E
. We denote
e_{i}
for the
0
-path consisting of a single vertex
i
. Let
\sigma_{i}=1
if
(i,i+1)\in E
and
-1
if
(i+1,i)\in E
. Then

\displaystyle\partial_{1}^{f}\bar{e}_{i(i+1)}=\sigma_{i}(f(i)e_{i+1}-f(i+1)e_{i})

and

\displaystyle\langle e_{i},e_{j}\rangle_{g}=g(i)g(j)\delta(i,j),

\displaystyle\langle\bar{e}_{i(i+1)},\bar{e}_{j(j+1)}\rangle_{g}=g(i)g(i+1)g(j)g(j+1)\delta(i,j).

Note that the last equality holds because each edge is assigned with exactly one direction.
Choose the following
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
-orthonormal basis

\displaystyle\omega_{0}=\Big\{\frac{e_{i}}{g(i)}\mid i=0,1,2,3\Big\}

in
\Omega_{0}^{f,g}(G)
and the following
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
-orthonormal basis

\displaystyle\omega_{1}=\Big\{\frac{\bar{e}_{i(i+1)}}{g(i)g(i+1)}\mid i=0,1,2\Big\}

in
\Omega_{1}^{f,g}(G)
. We note
\Omega_{n}^{f,g}(G)=0
for
n\geq 2
. In
\partial_{1}^{f}(\Omega^{f,g}_{1}(G))
choose the basis

\displaystyle b_{0}=\Big\{\sigma_{i}\Big(\frac{f(i)}{g(i)}\frac{e_{i+1}}{g(i+1)}-\frac{f(i+1)}{g(i+1)}\frac{e_{i}}{g(i)}\Big)\mid i=0,1,2\Big\}

and set

\displaystyle\tilde{b}_{1}=\Big\{\frac{\bar{e}_{i(i+1)}}{g(i)g(i+1)}\mid i=0,1,2\Big\}.

It is clear that
{\rm Ker}\partial_{0}^{f}=\Omega_{0}^{f,g}(G)
. Thus the
\langle~,~\rangle_{g}
-orthogonal complement of
\partial_{1}^{f}\Omega_{1}^{f,g}(G)
in
{\rm Ker}\partial_{0}^{f}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}={\rm Span}\Big\{\frac{f(0)}{g(0)^{2}}e_{0}+\frac{f(1)}{g(1)^{2}}e_{1}+\frac{f(2)}{g(2)^{2}}e_{2}+\frac{f(3)}{g(3)^{2}}e_{3}\Big\}

so that

\displaystyle h_{0}=\Big\{\frac{\frac{f(0)}{g(0)}\frac{e_{0}}{g(0)}+\frac{f(1)}{g(1)}\frac{e_{1}}{g(1)}+\frac{f(2)}{g(2)}\frac{e_{2}}{g(2)}+\frac{f(3)}{g(3)}\frac{e_{3}}{g(3)}}{\sqrt{\frac{f(0)^{2}}{g(0)^{2}}+\frac{f(1)^{2}}{g(1)^{2}}+\frac{f(2)^{2}}{g(2)^{2}}+\frac{f(3)^{2}}{g(3)^{2}}}}\Big\}.

Similar with
[
4
, (3.19)]
, by taking the absolute value of the determinant of the transition matrix, we see that

\displaystyle[b_{0},h_{0},\tilde{b}_{0}/\omega_{0}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=0}^{3}\frac{f(i)^{2}}{g(i)^{2}}}}\Big|\sum_{k=0}^{3}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{f(k)}{g(k)}\prod_{0\leq j\leq k-1}\frac{-\sigma_{j}f(j+1)}{g(j+1)}\prod_{k\leq j\leq 2}\frac{\sigma_{j}f(j)}{g(j)}\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=0}^{3}\frac{f(i)^{2}}{g(i)^{2}}}}\Big|\sum_{k=0}^{3}(-1)^{2k+1}\frac{f(k)}{g(k)}\prod_{0\leq j\leq k-1}\frac{\sigma_{j}f(j+1)}{g(j+1)}\prod_{k\leq j\leq 2}\frac{\sigma_{j}f(j)}{g(j)}\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=0}^{3}\frac{f(i)^{2}}{g(i)^{2}}}}\Big|\sum_{k=0}^{3}\frac{f(k)}{g(k)}\prod_{0\leq j\leq k-1}\frac{\sigma_{j}f(j+1)}{g(j+1)}\prod_{k\leq j\leq 2}\frac{\sigma_{j}f(j)}{g(j)}\Big|.

Moreover, since
b_{1}
is the empty-set, we have

\displaystyle h_{1}=\frac{\bar{e}_{34}}{g(3)g(4)}.

Consequently, by taking the absolute value of the determinant of the transition matrix, we have

\displaystyle[b_{1},h_{1},\tilde{b}_{1}/\omega_{1}]=1.

It follows that the weighted analytic torsion is

\displaystyle T(G,f,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{n=0}^{1}[b_{n},h_{n},\tilde{b}_{n}/\omega_{n}]^{(-1)^{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=0}^{3}\frac{f(i)^{2}}{g(i)^{2}}}}\Big|\sum_{k=0}^{3}\frac{f(k)}{g(k)}\prod_{0\leq j\leq k-1}\frac{\sigma_{j}f(j+1)}{g(j+1)}\prod_{k\leq j\leq 2}\frac{\sigma_{j}f(j)}{g(j)}\Big|.

In particular, consider the following cases:

(i).

f=g
.

Then

reduces to
T(G,1,1)
in
[
4
, Example 3.9]
, which takes value
\sqrt{3}
and does not depend on the choice of
f
.

(ii).

g
takes a constant value
c\neq 0
.

Then by a direct calculation, we have
t(f,c)=|c|^{-3}
and
T(G,f,c)=|c|^{-3}T(G,f,1)
for any non-vanishing weight
f
. On the other hand, we note that

\displaystyle s(G)=(-1)^{1}\dim\partial_{1}(\Omega_{1}(G))=-3.

(iii).

f
takes a constant value
c\neq 0
.

Then by a direct calculation, we have

for any non-vanishing weight
g
.

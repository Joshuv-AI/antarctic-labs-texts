Electromagnetic wave propagation in a nonlinear hyperbolic medium
A.I. Maimistov

Abstract




Abstract

The propagation of a quasi-harmonic electromagnetic wave in a bulk hyperbolic dielectric metamaterial is considered. If the group velocities dispersion is not taken into account, then wave propagation can be described either by the hyperbolic nonlinear Schrodinger equation or by the hyperbolic Manakov equations. It is shown that the region in the space of wave vectors in which the modulation instability of a spatially homogeneous wave is possible is not limited, in contrast to the case of ordinary media.

Electromagnetic waves in a linear hyperbolic medium

It is assumed that the axis of the Cartesian coordinate system
X

\hat{\varepsilon}=\mathrm{diag}(\varepsilon_{e},\penalty\ \varepsilon_{o},\penalty\ \varepsilon_{o})

However, if we assume that the coordinate axes (i.e., the orientation of an anisotropic uniaxial medium) are chosen as in the case under consideration, then such a separation is possible. The Maxwell equations for TE and TM waves have the following form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial B_{x}}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial E_{y}}{\partial z},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial D_{x}}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial H_{y}}{\partial z},

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial B_{z}}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial E_{y}}{\partial x},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial D_{z}}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial H_{y}}{\partial x},

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial D_{y}}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial H_{x}}{\partial z}-\frac{\partial H_{z}}{\partial x},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\frac{\partial B_{y}}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial E_{z}}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial E_{x}}{\partial z},

and

\frac{\partial D_{x}}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial D_{z}}{\partial z}=0,\quad\frac{\partial B_{x}}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial B_{z}}{\partial z}=0.

It is convenient to use the spectral representation, assuming

\mathbf{F}(x,z,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbf{\tilde{F}}(x,z,\omega)e^{-i\omega t}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi},

where
\mathbf{F}
is used to denote the fields
\mathbf{E}
,
\mathbf{B}
and the inductions
\mathbf{D}
,

. For the Fourier components, the Maxwell equations are written in the following form

\displaystyle ik_{0}\tilde{B}_{x}=-\frac{\partial\tilde{E}_{y}}{\partial z},

\displaystyle ik_{0}\tilde{D}_{x}=\frac{\partial\tilde{H}_{y}}{\partial z},

\displaystyle ik_{0}\tilde{B}_{z}=\frac{\partial\tilde{E}_{y}}{\partial x},

\displaystyle ik_{0}\tilde{D}_{z}=-\frac{\partial\tilde{H}_{y}}{\partial x},

(2)

\displaystyle ik_{0}\tilde{D}_{y}=\frac{\partial\tilde{H}_{z}}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial\tilde{H}_{x}}{\partial z},

\displaystyle ik_{0}\tilde{B}_{y}=\frac{\partial\tilde{E}_{z}}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial\tilde{E}_{x}}{\partial z}.

Let the magnetic permeability be a constant, i.e, the medium is magnetically isotropic. The electrical induction is related to the electric field by the following relations

\tilde{D}_{x}=\varepsilon_{e}\tilde{E}_{x},\quad\tilde{D}_{y}=\varepsilon_{o}\tilde{E}_{y},\quad\tilde{D}_{z}=\varepsilon_{o}\tilde{E}_{z}.

(3)

Thus, two systems of equations are obtained, which may be related due to the nonlinear properties of the medium, but in a linear medium they are independent:

\displaystyle ik_{0}\mu\tilde{H}_{x}=-\frac{\partial\tilde{E}_{y}}{\partial z},

\displaystyle ik_{0}\varepsilon_{e}\tilde{E}_{x}=\frac{\partial\tilde{H}_{y}}{\partial z},

\displaystyle ik_{0}\mu\tilde{H}_{z}=\frac{\partial\tilde{E}_{y}}{\partial x},

\displaystyle ik_{0}\varepsilon_{o}\tilde{E}_{z}=-\frac{\partial H_{y}}{\partial x},

(4)

\displaystyle ik_{0}\varepsilon_{o}\tilde{E}_{y}=\frac{\partial\tilde{H}_{z}}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial\tilde{H}_{x}}{\partial z},

\displaystyle ik_{0}\mu\tilde{H}_{y}=\frac{\partial\tilde{E}_{z}}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial\tilde{E}_{x}}{\partial z}.

The equations in the left column of the system of equations (

\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{E}_{y}}{\partial x^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{E}_{y}}{\partial z^{2}}+k_{0}^{2}\mu\varepsilon_{o}\tilde{E}_{y}=0.

(5)

The dispersion equation for TE waves has the form

k_{x}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}=k_{0}^{2}\mu\varepsilon_{o}.

That is, the TE wave is an ordinary wave for which the medium looks like isotropic if

\frac{k_{x}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{o}}+\frac{k_{z}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{e}}=k_{0}^{2}\mu

Substitution expressions

in (

\displaystyle\left(\nu^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\kappa^{4}+m_{11}\kappa\right)B_{1}+m_{12}\kappa^{2}B_{2}=0,

\displaystyle m_{21}\kappa^{2}B_{1}+\left(\nu^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\kappa^{4}+m_{22}\kappa\right)B_{2}=0,

. For this system of equations to have a non-zero solution, its determinant

\nu^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\kappa^{4}-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}(m_{11}+m_{22})\pm\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}\left[(m_{11}-m_{22})^{2}+4m_{12}m_{21}\right]^{1/2}.

(31)

Given the explicit form of the matrix elements
m_{ij}
, we can obtain two dispersion relations for harmonic waves traveling at the background of a spatially homogeneous solution

\nu_{1}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\kappa^{4},\quad\nu_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\kappa^{4}-(\mu_{1}a_{0}^{2}+\mu_{2}b_{0}^{2})\kappa^{2}.

Instability of a spatially homogeneous wave occurs under the condition

\frac{1}{4}\kappa^{4}-(\mu_{1}a_{0}^{2}+\mu_{2}b_{0}^{2})\kappa^{2}\leq 0.

The instability increment
G
is defined by the expression

G^{2}=(\mu_{1}a_{0}^{2}+\mu_{2}b_{0}^{2})\kappa^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\kappa^{4}.

(32)

\kappa^{2}=(k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2})

in which MI takes place is determined by the equation

k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2}=4(\mu_{1}a_{0}^{2}+\mu_{2}b_{0}^{2}).

The maximum value of the increment of MI
G_{m}
is given by the formula

G^{2}_{m}=(\mu_{1}a_{0}^{2}+\mu_{2}b_{0}^{2})^{2}.

The position of the maximum of the increment of MI in the plane
(k_{1},k_{2})
lies on the curve defined by the equation

k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2}=2(\mu_{1}a_{0}^{2}+\mu_{2}b_{0}^{2}).

For hyperbolic materials, the main values of the permittivity tensor have opposite signs. Let the case
\varepsilon_{o}<0
and
\varepsilon_{e}>0
be selected. In this case, the boundary of the domain in the space
(k_{1},k_{2})
in which MN takes place are hyperbolas

k_{1}^{2}-k_{2}^{2}=16\pi\chi^{(3)}\left(\frac{a_{0}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{e}}-\frac{b_{0}^{2}}{\mid\varepsilon_{o}\mid}\right).

If we choose the case
\varepsilon_{o}>0
and
\varepsilon_{e}<0
, then the boundary of the MI region will be hyperbolas

k_{2}^{2}-k_{1}^{2}=16\pi\chi^{(3)}\left(\frac{a_{0}^{2}}{\mid\varepsilon_{e}\mid}-\frac{b_{0}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{o}}\right).

It is important to note that, unlike conventional (isotropic or uniaxial) media, the wave vector length of the wave that leads to MI is not limited. An exception is possible for the case of

is met: the length of the wave vector is not limited, although there is no modulation instability.

By the example of a simple model of a nonlinear medium, using the generalized (hyperbolic) Manakov equations, the manifestation of hyperbolicity of a dielectric material in the process of modulation instability is illustrated.

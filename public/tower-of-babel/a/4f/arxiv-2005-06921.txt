On the Performance Analysis of Streaming Codes over the Gilbert-Elliott Channel
Myna Vajha, Vinayak Ramkumar, Mayank Jhamtani, P. Vijay Kumar

Abstract
The Gilbert-Elliot (GE) channel is a commonly-accepted model for packet erasures in networks. Streaming codes are a class of packet-level erasure codes designed to provide reliable communication over the GE channel. The design of a streaming code may be viewed as a two-step process. In the first, a more tractable, delay-constrained sliding window (DCSW) channel model is considered as a proxy to the GE channel. The streaming code is then designed to reliably recover from all erasures introduced by the DCSW channel model. Simulation is typically used to evaluate the performance of the streaming code over the original GE channel, as analytic performance evaluation is challenging. In the present paper, we take an important first step towards analytical performance evaluation. Recognizing that most, efficient constructions of a streaming code are based on the diagonal embedding or horizontal embedding of scalar block codes within a packet stream, this paper provides upper and lower bounds on the block-erasure probability of the underlying scalar block code when operated over the GE channel.

Problem Setup

In this paper, we study the erasure-recovery performance of ESCs over the GE channel.

Our Contributions

In the present paper, we provide analytical expressions for the BEP over GE channel of block codes that can recover exactly from either at most
a

We observe that these bounds closely approximate BEP for various

P(w(E_{n_{j^{\prime}-1}+1}^{n_{j^{\prime}}})=i_{j^{\prime}},S_{n_{j}}=1,\ \forall j^{\prime}\in[j])\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{j+1}Q_{j}\cdots Q_{1}\Pi.

Equation (
III-2
) follows as
Q_{j+1}(s_{n_{j+1}},s_{n_{j}})=P(w(E_{n_{j}+1}^{n_{j+1}})=i_{j+1},S_{n_{j+1}}=s_{n_{j+1}}|S_{n_{j}}=s_{n_{j}})
.

\Box

The probability of observing an erasure pattern
e_{1}^{n}
can be obtained by setting
\ell=n
,
n_{j}=j
for all
j\in[0,n]
and
(i_{1},\cdots,i_{n})=(e_{1},\cdots,e_{n})
in Lemma
III.1
.

\displaystyle P(E_{1}^{n}=e_{1}^{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{1}^{T}\Psi_{e_{n}}\Psi_{e_{n-1}}\cdots\Psi_{e_{1}}\Pi,

where
\Pi=\left[\pi_{g},\ \bar{\pi}_{g}\right]^{T}
,
\Psi_{0}=(I-\Gamma)\Psi
and
\Psi_{1}=\Gamma\Psi
given

\Psi=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1-\alpha&\beta\\
\alpha&1-\beta\end{array}\right],\ \ \Gamma=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\epsilon_{0}&0\\
0&\epsilon_{1}\end{array}\right].

IV
Block Codes over GE channel

The computation of BEP of a block code can be done by characterizing the set
E
of correctable erasure patterns and then computing the probability of the set
\{0,1\}^{n}\setminus E
. Characterizing a subset of correctable erasures E results in an upper bound on BEP whereas characterizing a superset of
E
results in a lower bound.

IV-
1
Random erasure correcting code

The probability of observing erasure patterns with weight larger than
a
in window of length
n
over GE
(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1})
channel is given by:

\displaystyle P_{rand}(n,a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(\{e_{1}^{n}\mid w(e_{1}^{n})>a\})

(41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=a+1}^{n}g\left(n,i,\pi_{g}\right)+b\left(n,i,\pi_{g}\right).

Suppose an
[n,k=n-a]
MDS code is used over GE channel. Then, the BEP of the MDS code is given by probability of observing erasure patterns of weight larger than
a
. Therefore, BEP of an
[n,k=n-a]
MDS code is equal to
P_{rand}(n,a)
.

IV-
2
Burst erasure correcting code

Here we calculate the probability of erasure patterns whose span is greater than
b
in a window of length
n
given by
P_{burst}(n,b)
. It is clear to see that BEP of any
b
burst erasure correcting code with block length
n
is upper bounded by
P_{burst}(n,b)
. Let
B=\{e_{1}^{n}\in\{0,1\}^{n}\mid\text{span}(e_{1}^{n})\leq b\}
be the set of erasures whose span is at most
b
. The set
B
can be partitioned by the index
i\in[n]
where the erasure starts,

B=\{\underline{0}\}\cup_{i=1}^{n}\{e_{1}^{n}\mid e_{1}^{i-1}=0,e_{i}=1,\text{span}(e_{1}^{n})\leq b\}
. The sum of probability of the erasure patterns where the first erasure is at index
i
is given by:

\displaystyle b_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(\{e_{1}^{n}\mid e_{1}^{i-1}=0,e_{i}=1,\text{span}(e_{1}^{n})\leq b\})

(42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P(\{e_{1}^{n}\mid e_{1}^{i-1}=0,e_{i}=1,e_{i+b^{\prime}}^{n}=0\}),\ b^{\prime}=\min(b,n-i+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{1}^{T}\sum\limits_{e_{i+b^{\prime}-1}=0}^{1}\cdots\sum\limits_{e_{i+2}=0}^{1}\sum\limits_{e_{i+1}=0}^{1}\Psi_{0}^{n+1-i-b^{\prime}}\Psi_{e_{i+b^{\prime}-1}}\cdots\Psi_{e_{i+1}}\Psi_{1}\Psi_{0}^{i-1}\Pi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{1}^{T}\Psi_{0}^{n+1-i-b^{\prime}}\Psi^{b^{\prime}-1}\Psi_{1}\Psi_{0}^{i-1}\Pi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{1}^{T}Q_{n+1-i-b^{\prime}}\Psi^{b^{\prime}-1}\Psi_{1}Q_{i-1}\Pi~,

\displaystyle\text{ where }Q_{j}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}g(j,0,1)&g(j,0,0)\\
b(j,0,1)&b(j,0,0)\end{array}\right].

Here (
42
) follows as
\Psi_{0}+\Psi_{1}=\Psi
. Therefore,

\displaystyle P_{burst}(n,b)=1-P(B)=1-g(n,0,\pi_{g})-b(n,0,\pi_{g})-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}.

(46)

Remark IV.1
.

In the computation of
P_{burst}(n,b)
, the matrix
\Psi^{i}
can be computed easily by writing it in the following form:

\displaystyle\Psi^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1-\mu(1-\rho^{i})&(1-\mu)(1-\rho^{i})\\
\mu(1-\rho^{i})&1-(1-\mu)(1-\rho^{i})\\
\end{array}\right],

where
\rho=1-\alpha-\beta
and
\mu=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}
. This computation technique was introduced in
[
12
]
to analyze the effect of interleaving.

IV-
3
Burst or random erasure correcting code

Consider an
[n,k]
block code
\mathcal{C}
that can recover only from erasure patterns given by
E=A\cup B
where
A
is the set of erasure patterns that have weight at most
a
and
B
is the set of erasure patterns that have span at most
b
. The BEP of this code when used over GE is given by:

\displaystyle P_{rand,burst}(n,a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-P(A\cup B)=1-P(A)-P(B)+P(A\cap B)

(48)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{rand}(n,a)+P_{burst}(n,b)-1+P(A\cap B).

where the last equation follows from equations (
41
) and (
46
). The
P(A\cap B)
can be counted in a way similar to
P_{burst}(n,b)
. The erasure patterns in the set
A\cap B
can be partitioned into subsets indexed by
i
, where
i\in[n]
is the index where first erasure is observed. The weight of erasure patterns are limited to be
\leq a
here. For a given
i
, the span of erasure can be atmost
b^{\prime}=\min(b,n-i+1)
. For a fixed
i
, the probability of these erasure patterns can be determined by dividing the
n
-length window into
\ell=4
small windows such that
n_{0}=0,n_{1}=i-1,n_{2}=i,n_{3}=i+b^{\prime}-1,n_{4}=n
with weights of erasures in each of these windows being
(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4})=(0,1,<=a-1,0)
. Then the probability of erasure patterns in the set indexed by
i
can be determined from Lemma
III.1
as

a_{i}=1^{T}Q_{n-i-b^{\prime}+1}M\Psi_{1}Q_{i-1}\Pi,

where

M=\sum\limits_{i_{3}=0}^{\min\{a-1,b^{\prime}-1\}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}g(b^{\prime}-1,i_{3},1)&g(b^{\prime}-1,i_{3},0)\\
b(b^{\prime}-1,i_{3},1)&b(b^{\prime}-1,i_{3},0)\end{array}\right],

and
Q_{j}
is defined as shown in (
IV-2
).
By also adding the probability of not seeing any erasure in the
n
length window we get:

\displaystyle P(A\cap B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(n,0,\pi_{g})+b(n,0,\pi_{g})+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}.

(49)

By substituting (
49
) in (
48
) we get the analytical expression for BEP of a code that can only correct from either
a
random erasures or a burst erasure of size atmost
b
.

It follows from definition that

P_{rand,burst}(n,a=1,b)=P_{burst}(n,b)

and

P_{rand,burst}(n,a=b,b)=P_{rand}(n,b).

Hence,

P_{rand}(n,b)\leq P_{rand,burst}(n,a,b)\leq P_{burst}(n,b)\text{ for all }a\in[b].

We note that
P_{rand,burst}(n,a,b)
is an upper bound for the BEP of cyclic codes with parameters
[n,k=n-b,d_{min}=a+1]
. This follows as the cyclic code can correct either
a
random erasures or a burst erasure of size
b
.

When an
[n,k]
block code is used, any lost symbol can be recovered, if recoverable, within a decoding delay of
\tau=n-1
. If we compare
[n=\tau+1,k=n-b]
block codes which can recover only from erasure patterns that have weight atmost
a
or span atmost
b
, then it can be seen that block code with
a=b
(MDS code) gives the smallest BEP. However ESCs offer a family of codes that encompass MDS family of codes and can offer better BEPs in comparison to MDS family under same rate and same delay constraint by picking
n>\tau+1
.

V
ESC over GE channel

Now consider the setting where a ESC that is designed for DCSW channel is used over a GE channel. The BEP can be computed by enumerating all the non admissible erasure patterns and then summing over their probabilities. However this has computational complexity exponential in block length. We therefore propose tractable upper bounds and lower bounds for BEP in this section that can be computed in polynomial time. In the following Lemma we show an upper and lower bound for BEP of
(a,b,\tau)
ESCs over GE channel using expression for BEP of random or burst erasure correcting block codes. We then improve these bounds in the Lemmas that follow. Let
P_{ESC}(n,a,b,\tau)
be the BEP of
(a,b,\tau)
ESC, of block length
n
.

Lemma V.1
.

Let
n\geq\tau+1
. Then,

P_{rand,burst}(\tau+1,a,b)\leq P_{ESC}(n,a,b,\tau)\leq P_{rand,burst}(n,a,b)
.

Proof:
Let
A_{j}
and
B_{j}
be defined as

A_{j}=\{e_{1}^{n}\mid w(e_{j}^{\tau+j})\leq a\},\ B_{j}=\{e_{1}^{n}\mid\text{span}(e_{j}^{\tau+j})\leq b\}.

The erasure patterns that are admissible by
(a,b,\tau)
DCSW channel are given by
\cap_{j=1}^{n-\tau}(A_{j}\cup B_{j})
. Therefore,

P_{ESC}(n,a,b,\tau)=1-P\left(\cap_{j=1}^{n-\tau}(A_{j}\cup B_{j})\right).

By definition

P_{rand,burst}(n,a,b)=1-P(R_{A}\cup R_{B})

where,

R_{A}=\{e_{1}^{n}\mid w(e_{1}^{n})\leq a\},\ R_{B}=\{e_{1}^{n}\mid\text{span}(e_{1}^{n})\leq b\}.

Clearly
R_{A}\cup R_{B}
is a subset of admissible erasure patterns. Therefore the upper bound follows.
By definition in equation (
48
),

P_{rand,burst}(\tau+1,a,b)=1-P(A_{1}\cup B_{1}).

Clearly this is a superset of admissible erasure patterns resulting in the lower bound.

\Box

The optimal rate
(a,b,\tau)
ESCs given in
[
11
,
10
,
7
,
16
]
have
n=\tau+1+b-a
. Here, we present improved bounds for
P_{ESC}(n,a,b,\tau)
, for
\tau+1\leq n\leq\tau+b
. In order to obtain improved upper bound, we first define
\hat{U}
and then show that
\hat{U}
is a subset of correctable erasure patterns.

\displaystyle\ \hat{U}

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle U_{A}\cup U_{B}

(50)

\displaystyle U_{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cup_{i=1}^{n-a}\cup_{b^{\prime}=a+1}^{\min\{n-i+1,b\}}U_{B,i,b^{\prime}}

\displaystyle U_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{e_{1}^{n}\mid w(e_{1}^{n})\leq a\}\cup(\cup_{i=1}^{n-\tau-1}\cup_{b^{\prime}=0}^{a}U_{A,i,b^{\prime}}),

\displaystyle U_{B,i,b^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{e_{1}^{n}\mid w(e_{1}^{i-1})=0,e_{i}=e_{i+b^{\prime}-1}=1,

\displaystyle w(e_{i+b^{\prime}}^{\min\{i+b^{\prime}+\tau-a,n\}})=0,w(e_{i}^{i+b^{\prime}-1})>a\},

\displaystyle U_{A,i,b^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{e_{1}^{n}\mid w(e_{1}^{i-1})=0,w(e_{i}^{i+b^{\prime}-1})=b^{\prime},e_{i+b^{\prime}}=0,

\displaystyle w(e_{i+b^{\prime}+1}^{\tau+i})=x\leq a-b^{\prime},a-x-b^{\prime}<w(e_{\tau+i+1}^{n})\leq b^{\prime}\}.

It can be verified that
U_{A}
is disjoint from
U_{B}
, any two sets
U_{B,i_{1},b_{1}}
,
U_{B,i_{2},b_{2}}
are disjoint given that either
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
or
b_{1}\neq b_{2}
and any two sets
U_{A,i_{1},b_{1}}
,
U_{A,i_{2},b_{2}}
are disjoint given that either
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
or
b_{1}\neq b_{2}
.

Lemma V.2

(Improved Upper Bound)
.

Let
\tau+1\leq n\leq\tau+b
. Then,

P_{ESC}(n,a,b,\tau)\leq P_{ESC,\hat{U}}(n,a,b,\tau)\leq P_{rand,burst}(n,a,b),

where
P_{ESC,\hat{U}}(n,a,b,\tau)=1-P(\hat{U})
,
\hat{U}
is defined in (
50
).

Proof:
Let
e_{1}^{n}\in U_{A}
. If
w(e_{1}^{n})\leq a
, then
e_{1}^{n}
is an admissible erasure pattern. Let
e_{1}^{n}\in U_{A,i,b^{\prime}}
for some
i\leq n-\tau
, then
w(e_{i}^{\tau+i})\leq a
and it is also true that
w(e_{1}^{\tau+i})\leq a
. This implies
e_{1}^{n}\in\cap_{j=1}^{i}A_{j}
. Now looking at window
[i+i_{0}-1,i+i_{0}-1+\tau]
for
i_{0}\in[b^{\prime}]
:

\displaystyle w(e_{i+i_{0}-1}^{i+i_{0}-1+\tau})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w(e_{i+i_{0}-1}^{i+b^{\prime}-1})+w(e_{i+b^{\prime}}^{i+\tau})+w(e_{i+\tau+1}^{i+i_{0}-1+\tau})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(b^{\prime}-i_{0}+1)+x+(i_{0}-1)\leq a

For window
[j,\tau+j]
such that
j\geq i+b^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle w(e_{j}^{\tau+j})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle w(e_{j}^{n})=w(e_{j}^{\tau+i})+w(e_{\tau+1+i}^{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle x+b^{\prime}\leq a

Therefore it is clear that if
e_{1}^{n}\in U_{A,i,b^{\prime}}
then
e_{1}^{n}\in\cap_{j=1}^{n-\tau}A_{j}
. Therefore
e_{1}^{n}\in\cap_{j=1}^{n-\tau}(A_{j}\cup B_{j})
.
Now let
e_{1}^{n}\in U_{B,i,b^{\prime}}
, then when
i>n-\tau-b^{\prime}+a
, it implies that span
(e_{1}^{n})=b^{\prime}\leq b
. Therefore it is an acceptable erasure pattern. For the case when
i\leq n-\tau-b^{\prime}+a
in the window
[j,\tau+j]
for
j\in[i,i+b^{\prime}-a]
: span
(e_{j}^{\tau+j})\leq b^{\prime}\leq b
. Therefore
e_{1}^{n}\in\cap_{j=1}^{i+b^{\prime}-a}B_{j}
. For
j\in[i+b^{\prime}-a+1,i+b^{\prime}-1]
:

\displaystyle w(e_{j}^{\tau+j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w(e_{j}^{i+b^{\prime}-1})+w(e_{i+b^{\prime}+\tau-a+1}^{\tau+j})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(i+b^{\prime}-j)+(j-i-b^{\prime}+a)=a

Therefore
e_{1}^{n}\in\cap_{j=1}^{i+b^{\prime}-a}B_{j}\cap\cap_{j=i+b^{\prime}-a+1}^{i+b^{\prime}-1}A_{j}
. For
j\in[i+b^{\prime},n-\tau]
we have:

\displaystyle\text{span}(e_{j}^{\tau+j})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tau+j-(i+b^{\prime}+\tau-a)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle j-i-b^{\prime}+a

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(n-\tau)-b^{\prime}+a\leq b.

Therefore
e_{1}^{n}\in\cap_{j=1}^{n-\tau}(A_{j}\cup B_{j})
. Hence
\hat{U}\subseteq\cap_{j=1}^{n-\tau}(A_{j}\cup B_{j})
and

\displaystyle P_{ESC}(n,a,b,\tau)=1-P(\cap_{j=1}^{n-\tau}(A_{j}\cup B_{j}))\leq 1-P(\hat{U})=P_{ESC,\hat{U}}(n,a,b,\tau).

It can be verified that
R_{A}\subseteq U_{A}
and
R_{B}\setminus R_{A}\subseteq U_{B}
and therefore
R_{A}\cup R_{B}\subseteq\hat{U}
. The improvement from the upper bound seen in Lemma
V.1
therefore follows.

\Box

For obtaining an improved lower bound we come up with a super set of correctable erasure patterns given by,

\displaystyle\hat{L}

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle L_{0}\cup L_{A}\cup L_{B}

(51)

\displaystyle L_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{e_{1}^{n}\mid w(e_{1}^{\tau+1})=0\},

\displaystyle L_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cup_{i=1}^{\tau+1}L_{A,i},\ \ L_{B}=\cup_{i=1}^{\tau+1-a}L_{B,i},

\displaystyle L_{A,i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{e_{1}^{n}\mid w(e_{1}^{i-1})=0,e_{i}=1,

\displaystyle w(e_{i+1}^{\tau+1}\cup e_{i+b}^{\min\{i+\tau,n\}})\leq a-1\},

\displaystyle L_{B,i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{e_{1}^{n}\mid w(e_{i}^{i+b_{m}-1})>a,

\displaystyle w(e_{1}^{i-1})=w(e_{i+b_{m}}^{\tau+1})=w(e_{i+b}^{\min\{i+\tau,n\}})=0\}

where
b_{m}=\min\{b,\tau+2-i\}
. It can be verified that
L_{0}
,
L_{A}
,
L_{B}
are mutually disjoint, any two sets
L_{B,i_{1}}
,
L_{B,i_{2}}
are disjoint given that
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
and any two sets
L_{A,i_{1}}
,
L_{A,i_{2}}
are disjoint given that
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
.

Lemma V.3

(Improved Lower Bound)
.

Let
\tau+1\leq n\leq\tau+b
. Then,

\displaystyle P_{rand,burst}(\tau+1,a,b)\leq P_{ESC,\hat{L}}(n,a,b,\tau)\leq P_{ESC}(n,a,b,\tau)

where
P_{ESC,\hat{L}}(n,a,b,\tau)=1-P(\hat{L})
,
\hat{L}
is defined in (
51
).

Proof:
We will now show that the set
\hat{L}
is a super set of the admissible erasure patterns given by the set
\cap_{j=1}^{n-\tau}(A_{j}\cup B_{j})
. Let
e_{1}^{n}\in\cap_{j=1}^{n-\tau}(A_{j}\cup B_{j})
, suppose
w(e_{1}^{\tau+1})=0
then clearly
e_{1}^{n}\in L_{0}
. Otherwise, let
i
be the first index in
[1,\tau+1]
where an erasure is seen. Then
w(e_{1}^{i-1})=0,e_{i}=1
. But we know that
e_{1}^{n}
is admissible and therefore
e_{1}^{n}\in A_{1}\cup B_{1}
. Hence it satisfies one of the following conditions:

1.

w(e_{i}^{\tau+1})\leq a
,

2.

span
(e_{i}^{\tau+1})\leq b_{m}
, w
(e_{i}^{\tau+1})>a
where
b_{m}=\min\{b,\tau+2-i\}
.

We first look at case 1, where
w(e_{i}^{\tau+1})\leq a
. For the case when
i>n-b
,
e_{1}^{n}
clearly belongs to the set
L_{A,i}
. For the case when
i\leq n-b
, it is necessary that
w(e_{i}^{\tau+1}\cup e_{i+b}^{\min\{\tau+i,n\}})\leq a
. Suppose
w(e_{i}^{\tau+1}\cup e_{i+b}^{\min\{\tau+i,n\}})>a
, then in the window
[j,\tau+j]
where
j=\min\{i,n-\tau\}
we can show that:

\displaystyle w(e_{j}^{\tau+j})>a,\text{ span }(e_{j}^{\tau+j})>b.

\displaystyle w(e_{j}^{\tau+j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}w(e_{i}^{\tau+i})&j=i\text{ i.e., }i\leq n-\tau\\
w(e_{n-\tau}^{n})&j=n-\tau\text{ i.e., }i>n-\tau\end{cases}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\begin{cases}w(e_{i}^{\tau+1}\cup e_{i+b}^{\tau+i})&j=i\\
w(e_{i}^{\tau+1}\cup e_{i+b}^{n})&j=n-\tau\end{cases}=w(e_{i}^{\tau+1}\cup e_{i+b}^{\min\{\tau+i,n\}})>a.

As we know that
w(e_{i}^{\tau+1})\leq a
it implies that
w(e_{i+b}^{\min\{\tau+i,n\}})\geq 1
implying that there is an erasure at index
\geq i+b
and at
i
. Therefore,
\text{span}(e_{j}^{\tau+j})>b
. This contradicts the condition that
e_{1}^{n}\in A_{j}\cup B_{j}
. Therefore when
e_{1}^{n}
satisfies case 1, it belongs to
L_{A,i}
.

In case 2,
w(e_{i}^{\tau+1})>a
and span
(e_{i}^{\tau+1})\leq b_{m}
. Therefore
w(e_{i+b_{m}}^{\tau+1})=0
,
w(e_{i}^{i+b_{m}-1})>a
. It is also necessary that
w(e_{i+b}^{\min\{\tau+i,n\}})=0
, otherwise the window
[j,j+\tau]
where
j=\min\{i,n-\tau\}
, will have span
(e_{j}^{j+\tau})>b
and
w(e_{j}^{j+\tau})>a
contradicting that
e_{1}^{n}\in A_{j}\cup B_{j}
.
Therefore it follows that
\cap_{j=1}^{n-\tau}(A_{j}\cup B_{j})\subseteq\hat{L}
and:

\displaystyle P_{ESC,\hat{L}}(n,a,b,\tau)=1-P(\hat{L})\leq 1-P(\cap_{j=1}^{n-\tau}(A_{j}\cup B_{j}))=P_{ESC}(n,a,b,\tau).

It is clear to see that
L_{0}\cup L_{A}\subseteq A_{1}
and
L_{B}\subseteq B_{1}\setminus A_{1}
. Therefore
\hat{L}\subseteq A_{1}\cup B_{1}
and it follows that:

\displaystyle P_{rand,burst}(\tau+1,a,b)=1-P(A_{1}\cup B_{1})\leq 1-P(\hat{L})=P_{ESC,\hat{L}}(n,a,b,\tau).

\Box

VI
Conclusion

In this paper we derived computable upper and lower bounds on BEP of ESCs over GE channel, by characterizing tractable subset and superset of correctable erasure patterns. This leads to upper and lower bound on BEP of HE based streaming codes. Extending this result to DE based streaming codes by taking into account error propagation remains an open problem.

1.
Introduction

A hypergraph
\mathcal{H}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
on the vertex set
\mathcal{V}
is a collection of subsets
\mathcal{E}
of
\mathcal{V}
, satisfying the following two conditions: (i)
|E|\geq 2
for all
E\in\mathcal{E}
, and (ii)
E_{i}\nsubseteq E_{j}
for all
E_{i},E_{j}\in\mathcal{E}
. The set
\mathcal{E}
is called the
hyperedge set
of
\mathcal{H}
. We sometimes write
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{H})
and
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{H})
in place of
\mathcal{V}
and
\mathcal{E}
, respectively, in order to emphasize the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
. Given a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
on the vertex set
\mathcal{V}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
one can associate it with a square-free monomial ideal in the polynomial ring
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
\mathbb{K}
is a field. The association is given by defining the monomial ideal
I(\mathcal{H})=\langle\mathbf{x}_{A}:=\prod_{x_{i}\in E}x_{i}\mid E\in\mathcal{E}\rangle
in
R
. The ideal
I(\mathcal{H})
is called the
hyperedge ideal
, or simply the edge ideal of
\mathcal{H}
, and much of the algebraic and homological properties of the ideal are governed by the combinatorics of
\mathcal{H}
.

An active area of research in combinatorial commutative algebra is to study the homological properties of
I(\mathcal{H})
in terms of the combinatorial properties of
\mathcal{H}
. For example, in
[
15
]
Há and Van Tuyl explicitly computed the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of
I(\mathcal{H})
when
\mathcal{H}
is a triangulated hypergraph. In
[
10
]
Emtander defined a certain types of complete hypergraph and complete multipartite hypergraph and computed the Betti numbers and the regularity of the corresponding edge ideals. Emtander et al. in
[
12
]
have determined the Poincaré series of some hypergraph algebras generalizing lines, cycles, and stars.

One of the motivations for studying edge ideals of hypergraphs comes from its connection with a celebrated theorem by Fröberg in the context of edge ideals of graphs. Let
G
be a finite simple graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. If
V(G)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
then we associate
G
with a quadratic square-free monomial ideal
I(G)=\langle x_{i}x_{j}\mid\{x_{i},x_{j}\}\in E(G)\rangle
in the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. The ideal
I(G)
is called the
edge ideal
of
G
. In 1988 Fröberg
[
13
]
showed that a square-free monomial ideal of degree
2
has linear resolution if and only if it is the edge ideal of the complement of a chordal graph; thus providing a combinatorial characterization of such ideals. Since then researchers have been trying to find an analogue of this result for square-free monomial ideals in higher degrees. However, for such ideals generated in degree
3
the resolution of the ideal depends on the characteristic of the base field (see
[
19
, Section 4]
). Because of this one can ask for a combinatorial characterisation for those square-free monomial ideals in higher degrees which have linear resolution over all field
\mathbb{K}
. One way to answer this question is by defining a hypergraph analog of chordal graphs and then considering their hyperedge ideals. In this direction, Van Tuyl and Villarreal
[
26
]
, Woodroofe
[
28
]
, Emtander
[
11
]
, Bigdeli, Yazdan Pour and Zaare-Nahandi
[
6
]
independently gave different notions of chordal hypergraphs and showed that the hyperedge ideals of complement of such hypergraps have linear free resolution over any field. However, there are examples of square-free monomial ideals having linear free resolution over any field
\mathbb{K}
but not being a hyperedge ideal of the complement of a chordal hypergraph in any of the above senses (see
[
5
, Section 4]
). Thus the quest for finding such a characterisation is still wide open.

In this paper, we study the edge ideal
I_{r}(G)
of a family of hypergraphs
\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G)
induced from a given graph
G
. Let
r
be a positive integer and let
G
be a finite simple graph on the vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. Define the hypergraph
\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G)
as follows.

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G))

\displaystyle=V(G),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G))

\displaystyle=\{A\subseteq V(G)\mid|A|=r+1\text{ and }G[A]\text{ is connected }\}.

Note that
\mathrm{Con}_{1}(G)=G
and the ideal
I_{1}(G)
is the well-known edge ideal of
G
. If
V(G)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
then we denote the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
as
R_{G}
. In
[
1
]
it was shown that if
G
is a tree then
\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G)
is a chordal hypergraph in the sense of Woodroofe
[
28
]
. Thus the edge ideal of the complement of
\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G)
has linear resolution when
G
is a tree. The independence complex of
\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G)
is called the
r
-independence complex of
G
and is denoted by
\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)
. The complex
\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)
can be realized as a generalization of the independence complex
\mathrm{Ind}(G)
of
G
. Analogous to
\mathrm{Ind}(G)
, the independence complex of
\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G)
also has some interesting topological and combinatorial properties. For example, in
[
24
]
, Paolini and Salvetti established a relationship between certain twisted (co)homology of the classical Braid groups and the (co)homology of the independence complexes of
\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G)
of certain graphs
G
. In
[
9
]
the authors have shown that the independence complex of
\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G)
is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres when
G
is a cycle graph or a perfect m-ary tree. Extending a result by Meshulam
[
21
]
, Deshpande, Shukla, and Singh showed in
[
8
]
that the (homological) connectivity of the independence complex of
\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G)
gives an upper bound for the distance
r
-domination number of the underlying graph
G
. In the same paper, the authors also proved that the independence complex of
\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G)
, when
G
is a chordal graph, is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres for every
r\geq 1
.
From the perspective of the Cohen-Macaulay conditions it was shown in
[
1
]
that the independence complex of
\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G)
is shellable when
G
is a tree. Moreover, it was proved using commutative algebra techniques that these complexes are vertex decomposable, the strongest among the Cohen-Macaulay conditions when the underlying graph is a caterpillar graph.

The edge ideal
I_{r}(G)
of
\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G)
also has some interesting algebraic and homological properties. For example, it was shown in
[
7
]
that if the complement of
G
is chordal then
I_{r}(G)
has linear resolution; thus providing a partial generalization of Fröberg’s theorem. In fact, the authors have proved that the ideal
I_{r}(G)
is vertex splittable, a stronger property than having linear resolution. Using the Betti splitting of vertex splittable ideal they also provided explicit formulas for
\mathbb{N}
-graded Betti numbers of
I_{r}(G)
for some well-known families of graphs.

The graded Betti numbers and the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity are two important numerical invariants of the minimal free resolution of a module. One current topic of research in this area is to determine the Betti numbers and the regularity of the ideal
I(\mathcal{H})
for various classes of
\mathcal{H}
(see, for example,
[
10
,
20
,
22
]
). In this paper, we focus on the Betti numbers of the hyperedge ideal
I_{r}(G)
, where the graph
G
can be written as a join of two graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
. Note that if
V(G_{1})
and
V(G_{2})
are two disjoint sets then the join
G_{1}*G_{2}
of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle V(G_{1}*G_{2})

\displaystyle=V(G_{1})\sqcup V(G_{2}),

\displaystyle E(G_{1}*G_{2})

\displaystyle=E(G_{1})\cup E(G_{2})\cup\{\{x,y\}\mid x\in V(G_{1})\text{ and }y\in V(G_{2})\}.

Let
G=G_{1}*G_{2}
. For
r=1
, Mousivand
[
23
]
expressed the graded Betti numbers of
R_{G}/I_{r}(G)
in terms of the graded Betti numbers of
R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1})
and
R_{G_{2}}/I(G_{2})
. In this article, we find such formulas for a general
r
. Indeed, we prove the following theorem which relates the Betti numbers of
R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2})
with the Betti numbers of
R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1})
and
R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2})
.

Theorem 1.1

(
Theorem

3.8
)
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two graphs on the vertex sets
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and
\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}
, respectively. Let
r
be a positive integer. Then the
\mathbb{N}
-graded Betti numbers of
R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2})
can be expressed as follows.

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sum_{j=0}^{i+d-1}\left\{{m\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+d}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1}))+{n\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+d}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))\right\}+\\
\hskip 93.50134pt\sum_{j=1}^{i+d-1}\left[{i+d-1\choose d}-{i+d-1-j\choose d}-{j-1\choose d}\right]{m\choose j}{n\choose i+d-j}\hskip 17.00024pt\text{ for }d=r,\\
\sum_{j=0}^{i+d-1}\left\{{m\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+d}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1}))+{n\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+d}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))\right\}\hskip 39.95055pt\text{ for }d\geq r+1.\end{cases}

Using the above theorem we can prove the following result on the (Castelnuovo-Mumford) regularity of
R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2})
.

Proposition 1.2

(
Corollary

3.10
)
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two graphs on disjoint vertex sets and let
r
be a positive integer. Then

\mathrm{reg}(R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))=\max\left\{\mathrm{reg}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1})),\mathrm{reg}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))\right\}.

Moreover, as an application of
Theorem

1.1
we provide explicit formulas for the Betti numbers of
I_{r}(G)
when
G
is a complete graph (
Theorem

4.1
) and complete multipartite graph (
Theorem

4.4
). We also relate the ideal
I_{r}(G)
with the
t
-path ideal of
G
, when
G
is a cycle graph (see
Theorem

4.9
). Using this we provide formulas for the graded Betti numbers of
I_{r}(W_{n+1})
, where
W_{n+1}
is the wheel graph on
n+1
vertices (
Theorem

4.10
). Moreover, formulas for the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of these ideals are obtained in
Corollary

4.2
,
Corollary

4.5
and
Corollary

4.11
.

The paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
we recall some useful concepts from graph theory, Stanley-Reisner theory, and related topics. In
Section

3
we give a proof of
Theorem

1.1
. Using this result, in
Section

4
, we provide explicit formulas for all the
\mathbb{N}
-graded Betti numbers of
R_{G}/I_{r}(G)
for some families of graphs
G
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some basic concepts from graph theory and commutative algebra.

A
simplicial complex

\Delta
on the vertex set
V(\Delta)
is a collection of subsets of
V(\Delta)
satisfying the following two conditions (i)
\{x_{i}\}\in\Delta
for each
x_{i}\in V(\Delta)
; (ii) if
F\in\Delta
and
F^{\prime}\subseteq F
then
F^{\prime}\in\Delta
. The elements of
\Delta
are called the
faces
of
\Delta
and the maximal faces are called the
facets
of
\Delta
. If
F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}
are some faces of
\Delta
such that for each
F\in\Delta
,
F\subseteq F_{i}
for some
i
, then we say that
\Delta
is generated by the faces
F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}
and write
\Delta=\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{t}\rangle
. For
F\in\Delta
, the
dimension
of
F
is defined as
\dim F=|F|-1
and we define
\dim\Delta=\max_{F\in\Delta}\dim F
. If
\Delta
is a simplicial complex such that it contains exactly one maximal face then
\Delta
is called a
simplex
on the vertex set
V(\Delta)
. If
\Delta
contains exactly one maximal face and
\dim\Delta=d
then it is called a
d
-simplex
. For a simplicial complex
\Delta
the
k
-skeleton
of
\Delta
, denoted by
\Delta^{k}
, is the simplicial complex on the vertex set
V(\Delta)
with faces
\{F\in\Delta\mid\dim F\leq k\}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph on the vertex set
\mathcal{V}
and edge set
\mathcal{E}
. A subset
A\subseteq\mathcal{V}
is called an independent set if
E\nsubseteq A
for each
E\in\mathcal{E}
. The collection of all independent sets forms a simplicial complex called the
independence complex
of
\mathcal{H}
and it is denoted by
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{H})
.

Let
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the polynomial ring on
n
variables over a field
\mathbb{K}
. If
F\subseteq\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
then by
\mathbf{x}_{F}
we denote the monomial
\prod_{x_{i}\in F}x_{i}
in
R
. A square-free monomial ideal
I
of
R
can be associated with a simplicial complex
\Delta(I)
on the vertex set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
, whose faces are
\{F\mid\mathbf{x}_{F}\notin I\}
. The complex
\Delta(I)
is called the
Stanley-Reisner complex
of
I
. Conversely, given a simplicial complex
\Delta
, the ideal
I_{\Delta}=\langle\mathbf{x}_{F}\mid F\notin\Delta\rangle
is called the
Stanley-Reisner ideal
of
\Delta
. Thus we have a one-to-one correspondence between simplicial complexes on the vertex set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and square-free monomial ideals in
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and this correspondence is called the
Stanley-Reisner correspondence
. If
\mathcal{H}
is a hypergraph on the vertex set
\mathcal{V}=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and edge set
\mathcal{E}
then the Stanley Reisner ideal of
\mathrm{Ind}(H)
is the ideal
I(\mathcal{H})=\langle\mathbf{x}_{E}:=\prod_{x_{i}\in E}x_{i}\mid E\in\mathcal{E}\rangle
. The ideal
I(\mathcal{H})
is commonly called as the
(hyper)edge ideal
of
\mathcal{H}
and this provides a one-to-one correspondence between the hypergraphs on the vertex set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and the square-free monomial ideals in
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.

In this paper, we are interested to study the Betti numbers and regularity of
R_{G}/I(\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G))
, where
G
is any finite simple graph and
r
is a positive integer. Recall that for a homogeneous ideal
I\subseteq R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, the
graded minimal free resolution
of
R/I
is the long exact sequence

0\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{N}}R(-j)^{\beta_{p,j}(R/I)}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{N}}R(-j)^{\beta_{p-1,j}(R/I)}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{N}}R(-j)^{\beta_{1,j}(R/I)}\rightarrow R\rightarrow R/I\rightarrow 0,

where
R(-j)
denotes the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
with the grading
twisted by
j
, and
p\leq n
. The numbers
\beta_{i,j}(R/I)
are called the
(i,j)
-th graded Betti numbers of
R/I
. See
[
27
]
for more on graded free resolution.

Definition 2.1
.

The
Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
, or simply the regularity of
R/I
is defined
to be
{\rm reg}(R/I)=\max\{j-i~|~\beta_{i,j}(R/I)\neq 0\}
.

One of the most important tools for computing the Betti numbers of a hyperedge ideal or equivalently any square-free monomial ideal associated to a simplicial complex is the following formula by Hochster.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
17
, Hochster’s formula]

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on the vertex set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
. Let
R/I_{\Delta}
denote its Stanley-Reisner ring in the polynomial ring
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Then for
i\geq 0
, the Betti numbers
\beta_{i,d}
of
R/I_{\Delta}
are given by

\beta_{i,d}(R/I_{\Delta})=\sum_{\underset{|W|=d}{W\subseteq V(\Delta)}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{d-i-1}(\Delta[W];\mathbb{K}).

Here
\Delta[W]=\{F\in\Delta\mid F\subseteq W\}
is the simplicial subcomplex of
\Delta
induced on the vertex set
W
.

In our case
\Delta=\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G))=\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)
and
I_{\Delta}=I(\mathrm{Con}_{r}(G))=I_{r}(G)
, where
\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)
is the
r
-independence complex of
G
. A subset
A\subseteq V(G)
is called
r
-independent if each connected component of the induced subgraph
G[A]
has vertex cardinality at most
r
. The collection of all
r
-independent sets forms the simplicial complex
\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)
. If
G=G_{1}*G_{2}
, then
\mathrm{Ind}_{1}(G)
is the disjoint union of
\mathrm{Ind}_{1}(G_{1})
and
\mathrm{Ind}_{1}(G_{2})
. However, for
r\geq 2
the complex
\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)
can be written as a union of two simplicial complexes with nonempty intersection (see
Proposition

3.1
). By Hochster’s formula, in order to calculate the Betti numbers
R_{G}/I_{r}(G)
we need to calculate the homologies of the induced subcomplexes of
\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G)
and we do this by using the Mayer Vietoris sequences.

Let
\Lambda
be a simplicial complex with two simplicial subcomplexes
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
such that
\Lambda=\Lambda_{1}\cup\Lambda_{2}
. Then we have an exact sequence of (reduced) chain complexes

0\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{\cdot}(\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2})\xrightarrow{\varphi_{\cdot}}\mathcal{C}_{\cdot}(\Lambda_{1})\oplus\mathcal{C}_{\cdot}(\Lambda_{2})\xrightarrow{\psi_{\cdot}}\mathcal{C}_{\cdot}(\Lambda)\rightarrow 0,

where the maps
\varphi_{\cdot}
and
\psi_{\cdot}
are defined by
\varphi_{\cdot}(x)=(x,-x)
and
\psi_{\cdot}(y,z)=y+z
, respectively. This induces the following long exact sequence of reduced homologies, called the Mayer-Vietoris sequence.

\cdots\rightarrow\widetilde{H}_{n}(\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2})\rightarrow\widetilde{H}_{n}(\Lambda_{1})\oplus\widetilde{H}_{n}(\Lambda_{2})\rightarrow\widetilde{H}_{n}(\Lambda)\xrightarrow{\partial}\widetilde{H}_{n-1}(\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2})\rightarrow\cdots,

where
\partial
is the connecting homomorphism. For more on Mayer-Vietoris sequence one can consult
[
16
, Section 2.2]
. In the next section, we make extensive use of the Mayer-Vietoris sequence to compute the Betti numbers of
I_{r}(G)
, where
G=G_{1}*G_{2}
.

3.
Betti numbers of the hyperedge ideal

In this section we give a proof of
Theorem

1.1
, i.e., we express the graded Betti numbers
\beta_{i,d}(R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))
in terms of the graded Betti numbers of
R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1})
and
R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2})
. The first step to achieve this is to determine the simplicial complex
\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2})
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two graphs on the vertex sets
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and
\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}
, respectively. Moreover, let
\Gamma
denote the simplicial complexes
\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2})
. Then

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G_{1})\cup\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G_{2})\cup\left\langle\left\{S\sqcup T\mid S\subseteq V(G_{1}),T\subseteq V(G_{2}),1\leq|S|,1\leq|T|,\text{ and }|S|+|T|\leq r\right\}\right\rangle.

Proof.

Let
F\in\Gamma
. If
F\subseteq V(G_{i})
for some
i
, then
F
must be an
r
-independent set of
G_{i}
. Hence
F\in\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G_{i})
. Now let
F\cap V(G_{i})\neq\emptyset
for each
i\in[2]
. Then for each
x_{u}\in F\cap V(G_{1})
and
x_{v}\in F\cap V(G_{2})
,
\{x_{u},x_{v}\}\in E(G_{1}*G_{2})
. Therefore,
(G_{1}*G_{2})[F]
is connected and hence
|F|\leq r
. Thus if
F
is a facet of
\Gamma
with
F\cap V(G_{i})\neq\emptyset
for each
i\in[2]
, then
|F|=r
and
F=S\sqcup T
, where
S=F\cap V(G_{1})
and
T=F\cap V(G_{2})
with
|S|,|T|\geq 1
and
|S|+|T|=r
. This completes the proof.
∎

By Hochster’s formula, we have

\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))=\sum_{\underset{|W|=i+d}{W\subseteq\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K}),

where
\Gamma[W]=\{F\in\Gamma\mid F\subseteq W\}
is the induced subcomplex of
\Gamma
on the vertex set
W\subseteq V(G_{1}*G_{1})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}
. Note that as
I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2})
is a monomial ideal equigenerated in degree
r+1
, the only possible nonzero Betti numbers are
\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))
, where
d\geq r
(cf.
[
25
, Proposition 12.3]
). Thus it is enough to find
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K})
for
d\geq r
. In order to determine the homologies of the induced subcomplexes of
\Gamma
, we first divide
\Gamma
into smaller subcomplexes
\Gamma_{1}
and

in the following way.

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2}=\left\langle\left\{S\sqcup T\mid S\subseteq V(G_{1}),T\subseteq V(G_{2}),1\leq|S|,1\leq|T|,\text{ and }|S|+|T|\leq r\right\}\right\rangle.

We further subdivide
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
in the following way. Let
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{11}\cup\Gamma_{12}
and
\Gamma_{2}=\Gamma_{21}\cup\Gamma_{22}
, where
\Gamma_{11}=\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G_{1})

\Gamma_{22}=\mathrm{Ind}_{r}(G_{2})
and
\Gamma_{12}=\Gamma_{21}=\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2}
. Note that,
\Gamma_{11}\cap\Gamma_{12}=\langle\{S\subseteq V(G_{1})\mid|S|=r-1\}\rangle
. Similarly,
\Gamma_{21}\cap\Gamma_{22}=\langle\{T\subseteq V(G_{2})\mid|T|=r-1\}\rangle
.

The following proposition describes how the induced subcomplex
\Gamma[W]
for
W\subseteq V(\Gamma)
, gets divided into the corresponding induced subcomplexes.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex on the vertex set
V
. Let
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
be two simplicial subcomplexes of
\Delta
on the vertex sets
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, respectively, such that
\Delta=\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}
. Then for any
W\subseteq V
,

(i)

\Delta[W]=\Delta_{1}[W\cap V_{1}]\cup\Delta_{2}[W\cap V_{2}]
;

(ii)

\Delta_{1}[W]\cap\Delta_{2}[W]=(\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2})[W]
;

(iii)

\Delta_{1}[W]=\Delta_{1}[W\cap V_{1}]
and
\Delta_{2}[W]=\Delta_{2}[W\cap V_{2}]
.

Proof.

Let
\tau\in\Delta[W]
. Then
\tau\subseteq W\subseteq V
and
\tau\in\Delta
. Since
\Delta=\Delta_{1}\cup\Delta_{2}
, either
\tau\in\Delta_{1}
or
\tau\in\Delta_{2}
. Therefore, either
\tau\subseteq W\cap V_{1}
or
\tau\subseteq W\cap V_{2}
. Thus we have
(i)
. For
(ii)
, it is enough to observe that if
\tau\in\Delta_{1}[W]\cap\Delta_{2}[W]
, then
\tau\in\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2}
and
\tau\subseteq W
. Also,
(iii)
follows from the definition of induced subcomplexes.
∎

We now proceed to compute the
\mathbb{K}
-vector space dimension of reduced homologies of various induced subcomplexes.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
W_{1}\subseteq V(G_{1})
and
W_{2}\subseteq V(G_{2})
such that
|W_{1}|=t_{1}\geq 1
and
|W_{2}|=t_{2}\geq 1
, respectively. If
W=W_{1}\sqcup W_{2}\subseteq V(G_{1}*G_{2})
, then

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{K}}(\widetilde{H}_{d}((\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W];\mathbb{K})=\begin{cases}{t_{1}+t_{2}-1\choose r}-{t_{1}\choose r}-{t_{2}\choose r}&\text{ if }d=r-1\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We first show that
(\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W]
is a Cohen-Macaulay simplicial complex. Note that

\displaystyle(\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W]=\left\langle\left\{S\sqcup T\mid S\subseteq W_{1},T\subseteq W_{2},1\leq|S|,1\leq|T|,\text{ and }|S|+|T|\leq r\right\}\right\rangle.

Let
K_{W_{1}}
and
K_{W_{2}}
denote the complete graph on the vertex sets
W_{1}
and
W_{2}
, respectively. Then
(\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W]=\Delta^{r-1}
, where
\Delta=\mathrm{Ind}_{r-1}(K_{W_{1}}\sqcup K_{W_{2}})
. It is easy to see that if
t_{1}+t_{2}\leq r
, then
(\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W]
is a simplex. Now let
I
,
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
denote the Stanley-Reisner ideals of
\Delta
,
\mathrm{Ind}_{r-1}(K_{W_{1}})
and
\mathrm{Ind}_{r-1}(K_{W_{2}})
, respectively. Then
I=I_{1}+I_{2}
. Note that
\mathrm{Ind}_{r-1}(K_{W_{1}})
is a Cohen-Macaulay simplicial complex since it is the
(r-2)
-skeleton of the simplex on the vertex set
|W_{1}|
. Similarly,
\mathrm{Ind}_{r-1}(K_{W_{2}})
is also a Cohen-Macaulay simplicial complex. Let
R_{W_{1}}=\mathbb{K}[x_{i}\mid x_{i}\in W_{1}]
and
R_{W_{2}}=\mathbb{K}[x_{i}\mid x_{i}\in W_{2}]
. Then
R_{W_{1}}/I_{1}
is a Cohen-Macaulay
R_{W_{1}}
-module. Similarly,
R_{W_{2}}/I_{2}
is a Cohen-Macaulay
R_{W_{2}}
-module. We have
R_{W_{1}}/I_{1}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}R_{W_{2}}/I_{2}\cong R_{W}/I
, where
R_{W}=\mathbb{K}[x_{i}\mid x_{i}\in W]
(by
[
27
, Proposition 2.2.20]
). Therefore, by
[
27
, Corollary 2.2.22]
,
R_{W}/I
is a Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module. Since

is the Stanley-Reisner ideal of
\Delta
, the simplicial complex
\Delta
is Cohen-Macaulay. Hence by
[
27
, Proposition 5.3.14]
,
(\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W]=\Delta^{r-1}
is a Cohen-Macaulay simplicial complex.

If
t_{1}+t_{2}\leq r
, then
(\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W]
is a simplex and hence all the homologies are zero. Therefore, we may assume that
t_{1}+t_{2}>r
. In that case
\dim((\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W])=r-1
. Let
a_{j}
denote the number of
j
-dimensional simplices of
(\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W]
. Then
a_{r-1}={t_{1}+t_{2}\choose r}-{t_{1}\choose r}-{t_{2}\choose r}
. Also, for
0\leq j\leq r-2
,
a_{j}={t_{1}+t_{2}\choose j+1}
. Therefore, the Euler characteristic of the simplicial complex
(\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W]
is

\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{j}{t_{1}+t_{2}\choose j+1}+(-1)^{r}\left[{t_{1}\choose r}+{t_{2}\choose r}\right]=1+(-1)^{r-1}{t_{1}+t_{2}-1\choose r}+(-1)^{r}\left[{t_{1}\choose r}+{t_{2}\choose r}\right].

Since the homologies of
(\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W]
are concentrated in dimensions
0
and
r-1
, the Euler characteristic of
(\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W]
is the number
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(H_{0}((\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W];\mathbb{K}))+(-1)^{r-1}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(H_{r-1}((\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W];\mathbb{K}))
. Therefore,
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\widetilde{H}_{r-1}((\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W];\mathbb{K}))={t_{1}+t_{2}-1\choose r}-{t_{1}\choose r}-{t_{2}\choose r}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
W_{1}\subseteq V(G_{1})
and
W_{2}\subseteq V(G_{2})
such that
|W_{1}|=t_{1}\geq 1
and
|W_{2}|=t_{2}\geq 1
, respectively. If
W=W_{1}\sqcup W_{2}\subseteq V(G_{1}*G_{2})
, then for all
i\geq r
,

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K}))=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma_{11}[W_{1}];\mathbb{K}))+\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma_{22}[W_{2}];\mathbb{K})).

Proof.

The proof follows the same line of arguments as in
Lemma

3.6
.

Using the Mayer-Vietoris sequence for
\Gamma[W]=\Gamma_{1}[W]\cup\Gamma_{2}[W]
, we have the following long exact sequence of
\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces.

(6)

\begin{split}\cdots\rightarrow\widetilde{H}_{r+1}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K})\xrightarrow{\partial^{r+1}}\widetilde{H}_{r}((\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W];\mathbb{K})\rightarrow\widetilde{H}_{r}(\Gamma_{1}[W];\mathbb{K})\oplus\widetilde{H}_{r}(\Gamma_{2}[W];\mathbb{K})\\
\rightarrow\widetilde{H}_{r}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K})\xrightarrow{\partial^{r}}\widetilde{H}_{r-1}((\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W];\mathbb{K})\rightarrow\cdots.\end{split}

Note that
\widetilde{H}_{r}((\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W];\mathbb{K})=0
(by
Lemma

3.3
). Moreover, by the proof of
Lemma

3.6
, we see that
\partial^{r}
in
Equation

6
is the zero map. Hence

(7)

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{r}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K})\cong\widetilde{H}_{r}(\Gamma_{1}[W];\mathbb{K})\oplus\widetilde{H}_{r}(\Gamma_{2}[W];\mathbb{K}).

For
\Gamma_{1}[W]=\Gamma_{11}[W_{1}]\cup\Gamma_{12}[W]
we have the following Mayer-Vietoris sequence

(8)

\begin{split}\cdots\rightarrow\widetilde{H}_{r}((\Gamma_{11}\cap\Gamma_{12})[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})\rightarrow\widetilde{H}_{r}(\Gamma_{11}[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})\oplus\widetilde{H}_{r}(\Gamma_{12}[W];\mathbb{K})\rightarrow\widetilde{H}_{r}(\Gamma_{1}[W];\mathbb{K})\\
\xrightarrow{\partial^{r}}\widetilde{H}_{r-1}((\Gamma_{11}\cap\Gamma_{12})[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})\rightarrow\cdots.\end{split}

By
Lemma

3.4
,
\widetilde{H}_{r}((\Gamma_{11}\cap\Gamma_{12})[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})=\widetilde{H}_{r-1}((\Gamma_{11}\cap\Gamma_{12})[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})=0
. Also, by
Lemma

3.3
, we have
\widetilde{H}_{r}(\Gamma_{12}[W];\mathbb{K})=\widetilde{H}_{r}((\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W];\mathbb{K})=0
. Therefore, from
Equation

8
we obtain
\widetilde{H}_{r}(\Gamma_{1}[W];\mathbb{K})\cong\widetilde{H}_{r}(\Gamma_{11}[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})
. A similar calculation for
\Gamma_{2}[W]=\Gamma_{21}[W]\cup\Gamma_{22}[W_{2}]
yields
\widetilde{H}_{r}(\Gamma_{2}[W];\mathbb{K})\cong\widetilde{H}_{r}(\Gamma_{22}[W_{2}];\mathbb{K})
. Thus from
Equation

7
we get that
\widetilde{H}_{r}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K})\cong\widetilde{H}_{r}(\Gamma_{11}[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})\oplus\widetilde{H}_{r}(\Gamma_{22}[W_{2}];\mathbb{K})
.

Now let
i\geq r+1
. For the Mayer-Vietoris sequence

\begin{split}\cdots\rightarrow\widetilde{H}_{i}((\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W];\mathbb{K})\rightarrow\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma_{1}[W];\mathbb{K})\oplus\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma_{2}[W];\mathbb{K})\rightarrow\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K})\\
\xrightarrow{\partial^{i}}\widetilde{H}_{i-1}((\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W];\mathbb{K})\rightarrow\cdots,\end{split}

we have
\widetilde{H}_{i}((\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W];\mathbb{K})=\widetilde{H}_{i-1}((\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W];\mathbb{K})=0
(by
Lemma

3.3
). Therefore,
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K})\cong\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma_{1}[W];\mathbb{K})\oplus\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma_{2}[W];\mathbb{K})
. Now for the Mayer-Vietoris sequence

\begin{split}\cdots\rightarrow\widetilde{H}_{i}((\Gamma_{11}\cap\Gamma_{12})[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})\rightarrow\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma_{11}[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})\oplus\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma_{12}[W];\mathbb{K})\rightarrow\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma_{1}[W];\mathbb{K})\\
\xrightarrow{\partial^{i}}\widetilde{H}_{i-1}((\Gamma_{11}\cap\Gamma_{12})[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})\rightarrow\cdots,\end{split}

we have
\widetilde{H}_{i}((\Gamma_{11}\cap\Gamma_{12})[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})=\widetilde{H}_{i-1}((\Gamma_{11}\cap\Gamma_{12})[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})=0
(by
Lemma

3.4
). Moreover,
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma_{12}[W];\mathbb{K})=\widetilde{H}_{i}((\Gamma_{1}\cap\Gamma_{2})[W];\mathbb{K})=0
(by
Lemma

3.3
). Therefore, from the long exact sequence above we get that
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma_{1}[W];\mathbb{K})\cong\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma_{11}[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})
. Proceeding as above for the simplicial complex
\Gamma_{2}[W]
, we have
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma_{2}[W];\mathbb{K})\cong\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma_{22}[W_{2}];\mathbb{K})
. Consequently, we obtain
\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K})\cong\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma_{11}[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})\oplus\widetilde{H}_{i}(\Gamma_{22}[W_{2}];\mathbb{K})
for all
i\geq r+1
. This completes the proof.

∎

Now we are ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two graphs on the vertex sets
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and
\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\}
, respectively. Let
r
be a positive integer. Then the
\mathbb{N}
-graded Betti numbers of
R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2})
can be expressed as follows.

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sum_{j=0}^{i+d-1}\left\{{m\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+d}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1}))+{n\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+d}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))\right\}+\\
\hskip 93.50134pt\sum_{j=1}^{i+d-1}\left[{i+d-1\choose d}-{i+d-1-j\choose d}-{j-1\choose d}\right]{m\choose j}{n\choose i+d-j}\hskip 17.00024pt\text{ for }d=r,\\
\sum_{j=0}^{i+d-1}\left\{{m\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+d}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1}))+{n\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+d}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))\right\}\hskip 39.95055pt\text{ for }d\geq r+1.\end{cases}

Proof.

By Hochster’s formula (
Proposition

3.1
)

\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))=\sum_{\underset{|W|=i+d}{W\subseteq V(G_{1}*G_{2})}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K}).

Now
V(G_{1}*G_{2})=V(G_{1})\sqcup V(G_{2})
. Thus if
W\subseteq V(G_{1})\sqcup V(G_{2})
with
|W|=i+d
, then
W=W_{1}\sqcup W_{2}
, where
W_{1}=W\cap V(G_{1})
and
W_{2}=W\cap V(G_{2})
. Therefore,
0\leq|W_{1}|\leq i+d
and
0\leq|W_{2}|\leq i+d
. Let
|W_{1}|=i+d
. Then
|W_{2}|=0
and
W=W_{1}
. In that case using
Proposition

3.2
, we have
\Gamma[W]=\Gamma_{1}[W]=\Gamma_{11}[W_{1}]
. Hence

(9)

\sum_{\underset{|W_{1}|=i+d}{W=W_{1}\sqcup W_{2}\subseteq V(G_{1}*G_{2})}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K})=\sum_{\underset{|W_{1}|=i+d}{W_{1}\subseteq V(G_{1})}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(\Gamma_{11}[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})=\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1})).

Similarly, taking
|W_{2}|=i+d
, we have

(10)

\sum_{\underset{|W_{2}|=i+d}{W=W_{1}\sqcup W_{2}\subseteq V(G_{1}*G_{2})}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K})=\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2})).

Thus we may assume that
|W_{1}|=i+d-j
and
|W_{2}|=j
, where
1\leq j\leq i+d-1
. First, we consider the case
d=r
. In this case by
Lemma

3.6
,

(11)

\begin{split}&\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\widetilde{H}_{r-1}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K}))\\
&=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\widetilde{H}_{r-1}(\Gamma_{11}[W_{1}];\mathbb{K}))+\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\widetilde{H}_{r-1}(\Gamma_{22}[W_{2}];\mathbb{K}))+{i+r-1\choose r}-{i+r-1-j\choose r}-{j-1\choose r}.\end{split}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+r}(R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underset{0\leq j\leq i+r}{\underset{|W_{1}|=i+r-j,\,|W_{2}|=j}{W=W_{1}\sqcup W_{2}\subseteq V(G_{1}*G_{2})}}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{r-1}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underset{|W_{1}|=i+r}{W_{1}\subseteq V(G_{1})}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{r-1}(\Gamma_{11}[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})+\sum_{\underset{|W_{2}|=i+r}{W_{2}\subseteq V(G_{2})}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{r-1}(\Gamma_{22}[W_{2}];\mathbb{K})

\displaystyle\hskip 153.0022pt+\sum_{\underset{1\leq j\leq i+r-1}{\underset{|W_{1}|=i+r-j,\,|W_{2}|=j}{W=W_{1}\sqcup W_{2}\subseteq V(G_{1}*G_{2})}}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{r-1}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K})

Now using
Equation

9
,
Equation

10
and
Equation

11
we have,

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+r}(R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))

\displaystyle=\beta_{i,i+r}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1}))+\beta_{i,i+r}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))

\displaystyle\hskip 51.00073pt+\sum_{\underset{1\leq j\leq i+r-1}{\underset{|W_{1}|=i+r-j,\,|W_{2}|=j}{W=W_{1}\sqcup W_{2}\subseteq V(G_{1}*G_{2})}}}\left[\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\widetilde{H}_{r-1}(\Gamma_{11}[W_{1}];\mathbb{K}))+\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\widetilde{H}_{r-1}(\Gamma_{22}[W_{2}];\mathbb{K}))\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 102.00146pt+\sum_{\underset{1\leq j\leq i+r-1}{\underset{|W_{1}|=i+r-j,\,|W_{2}|=j}{W=W_{1}\sqcup W_{2}\subseteq V(G_{1}*G_{2})}}}\left[{i+r-1\choose r}-{i+r-1-j\choose r}-{j-1\choose r}\right]

\displaystyle=\beta_{i,i+r}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1}))+\beta_{i,i+r}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))

\displaystyle\hskip 34.00049pt+\sum_{j=1}^{i+r-1}\left[{|V(G_{2})|\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+r}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1}))+{|V(G_{1})|\choose i-j+r}\beta_{j-r,j}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 68.00098pt+\sum_{j=1}^{i+r-1}\left[{i+r-1\choose r}-{i+r-1-j\choose r}-{j-1\choose r}\right]{|V(G_{2})|\choose j}{|V(G_{1})|\choose i+r-j}.

Now
\sum_{j=1}^{i+r-1}{|V(G_{1})|\choose i-j+r}\beta_{j-r,j}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))=\sum_{j=1}^{i+r-1}{|V(G_{1})|\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+r}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))
. Thus

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+r}(R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{i+r-1}\left[{m\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+r}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1}))+{n\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+r}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 51.00073pt+\sum_{j=1}^{i+r-1}\left[{i+r-1\choose r}-{i+r-1-j\choose r}-{j-1\choose r}\right]{m\choose j}{n\choose i-j+r}.

We now consider the case
d\geq r+1
. In this case by
Lemma

3.7
,

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K}))=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(\Gamma_{11}[W_{1}];\mathbb{K}))+\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(\Gamma_{22}[W_{2}];\mathbb{K})).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underset{0\leq j\leq i+d}{\underset{|W_{1}|=i+d-j,\,|W_{2}|=j}{W=W_{1}\sqcup W_{2}\subseteq V(G_{1}*G_{2})}}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\underset{|W_{1}|=i+d}{W_{1}\subseteq V(G_{1})}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(\Gamma_{11}[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})+\sum_{\underset{|W_{2}|=i+d}{W_{2}\subseteq V(G_{2})}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(\Gamma_{22}[W_{2}];\mathbb{K})

\displaystyle\hskip 153.0022pt+\sum_{\underset{1\leq j\leq i+d-1}{\underset{|W_{1}|=i+d-j,\,|W_{2}|=j}{W=W_{1}\sqcup W_{2}\subseteq V(G_{1}*G_{2})}}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(\Gamma[W];\mathbb{K})

Note that
\sum_{\underset{|W_{1}|=i+d}{W_{1}\subseteq V(G_{1})}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(\Gamma_{11}[W_{1}];\mathbb{K})=\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1}))
and similarly we have
\sum_{\underset{|W_{2}|=i+d}{W_{2}\subseteq V(G_{2})}}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(\Gamma_{22}[W_{2}];\mathbb{K})=\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))
. Hence

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))

\displaystyle=\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1}))+\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))

\displaystyle\hskip 51.00073pt+\sum_{\underset{1\leq j\leq i+d-1}{\underset{|W_{1}|=i+d-j,\,|W_{2}|=j}{W=W_{1}\sqcup W_{2}\subseteq V(G_{1}*G_{2})}}}\left[\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(\Gamma_{11}[W_{1}];\mathbb{K}))+\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(\widetilde{H}_{d-1}(\Gamma_{22}[W_{2}];\mathbb{K}))\right]

\displaystyle=\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1}))+\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))

\displaystyle\hskip 51.00073pt+\sum_{j=1}^{i+d-1}\left[{|V(G_{2})|\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+d}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1}))+{|V(G_{1})|\choose i-j+d}\beta_{j-d,j}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{i+d-1}\left[{m\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+d}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1}))+{n\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+d}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))\right].

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

The formula for the Betti numbers in
Theorem

3.8
can also be deduced using
[
14
, Corollary 5.3]
. Indeed, if one takes
I=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
and
J=\langle y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\rangle
, then the formula in
Theorem

3.8
follows after simplifying the formula in
[
14
, Corollary 5.3]
.

As a corollary of the above theorem, we can prove the following result on the regularity of
R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2})
.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two graphs on disjoint vertex sets and let
r
be a positive integer. Then

\mathrm{reg}(R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))=\max\left\{\mathrm{reg}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1})),\mathrm{reg}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))\right\}

Proof.

Without loss of generality let
s=\mathrm{reg}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1}))\geq\mathrm{reg}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))
. Then we have
\beta_{i,i+s}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1}))\neq 0
for some
i
and
\beta_{i,i+j}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1}))=\beta_{i,i+j}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))=0
for all
i
and for all
j\geq s+1
. Now we use the formula in
Theorem

3.8
. Note that, by induction on
d
, one can show that
{i+d-1\choose d}\geq{i+d-1-j\choose d}+{j-1\choose d}
. Hence
\beta_{i,i+s}(R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))\neq 0
for the above
i
and
\beta_{i,i+j}(R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))
for all
i
and for all
j\geq s+1
. Therefore,
\mathrm{reg}(R_{G_{1}*G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{1}*G_{2}))=s=\max\left\{\mathrm{reg}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1})),\mathrm{reg}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))\right\}
.
∎

4.
Betti numbers for some families of graphs

In this section, we find explicit formulas for the Betti numbers and the regularity of the hyperedge ideals for some well-known families of graphs as an application of
Theorem

3.8
. First, we give the formula for complete graphs. Here we note that the formula of Betti numbers for complete graphs was first obtained in
[
7
, Theorem 4.4]
by showing that the ideal
I_{r}(K_{n})
is a vertex splittable ideal for all positive integer
r
. Moreover, the formula is well-known in the case of
r=1
and it was first derived in the thesis of Jacques
[
18
, Theorem 5.1.1]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
K_{n}
denote the complete graph on
n
vertices. Then for all positive integers
r
the
\mathbb{N}
-graded Betti numbers of the ideal
I_{r}(K_{n})
can be expressed as follows.

\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{K_{n}}/I_{r}(K_{n}))=\begin{cases}{i+d-1\choose d}{n\choose i+d}&\text{ for }d=r\text{ and }1\leq i\leq n-r,\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

We prove the formula by induction on
n
. Let
r
be fixed. If
n\leq r
then
I_{r}(K_{n})=0
. If
n=r+1
, then
I_{r}(K_{n})=\langle\prod_{i=1}^{r+1}x_{i}\rangle
. Hence

0\rightarrow R_{K_{n}}(-r-1)\xrightarrow{\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r+1}x_{i}}R_{K_{n}}\rightarrow\frac{R_{K_{n}}}{\left\langle\prod_{i=1}^{r+1}x_{i}\right\rangle}\rightarrow 0

is a minimal free resolution of
R_{K_{n}}/I_{r}(K_{n})
. Therefore, we have the above formula. Now let
n>r+1
. The complete graph
K_{n}
can be expressed as
K_{n-1}*G
, where
G
is the graph consisting of only one vertex. Then by induction we see that
\beta_{i,i+j}(R_{K_{n}}/I_{r}(K_{n}))=0
if
j\neq r
. Moreover, by
Theorem

3.8
, we have

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+r}(R_{K_{n}}/I_{r}(K_{n}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{i+r-1}\left\{{1\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+r}(R_{K_{n-1}}/I_{r}(K_{n-1}))+{n-1\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+r}(R_{G}/I_{r}(G))\right\}+

\displaystyle\hskip 59.50085pt\sum_{j=1}^{i+r-1}\left[{i+r-1\choose r}-{i+r-1-j\choose r}-{j-1\choose r}\right]{1\choose j}{n-1\choose i+r-j}

\displaystyle=\beta_{i,i+r}(R_{K_{n-1}}/I_{r}(K_{n-1}))+\beta_{i-1,i+r-1}(R_{K_{n-1}}/I_{r}(K_{n-1}))+

\displaystyle\hskip 59.50085pt\left[{i+r-1\choose r}-{i+r-2\choose r}-{0\choose r}\right]{n-1\choose i+r-1}

\displaystyle={i+r-1\choose r}{n\choose i+r}.

∎

Since
\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{K_{n}}/I_{r}(K_{n}))=0
for
d\neq r
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

For all positive integer
r
,
\mathrm{reg}(R_{K_{n}}/I_{r}(K_{n}))=r
.

Theorem 4.4

I_{r}(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}})
can be expressed as follows.

\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}}}/I_{r}(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}}))=\begin{cases}{\sum_{s=1}^{t}n_{s}\choose i+r}{i+r-1\choose r}-\sum_{\underset{\sum_{s=1}^{t}j_{s}=i+r}{(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{t})\in\mathbb{N}^{t}}}\left[\prod_{s=1}^{t}{n_{s}\choose j_{s}}\right]\sum_{s=1}^{t}{j_{s}-1\choose r}&\text{ for }d=r,\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

We prove this formula by induction on
t
. First, consider the case when
t=2
. Let
G_{1}
denote the graph on
n_{1}
vertices with no edges and let
G_{2}
denote the graph on
n_{2}
vertices with no edges. Then
K_{n_{1},n_{2}}=G_{1}*G_{2}
. By the formula in
Theorem

3.8
we see that
\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{K_{n_{1},n_{2}}}/I_{r}(K_{n_{1},n_{2}}))=0
if
d\neq r
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+r}(R_{K_{n_{1},n_{2}}}/I_{r}(K_{n_{1},n_{2}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{i+r-1}\left\{{n_{2}\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+r}(R_{G_{1}}/I_{r}(G_{1}))+{n_{1}\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+r}(R_{G_{2}}/I_{r}(G_{2}))\right\}+

\displaystyle\hskip 42.50061pt\sum_{j=1}^{i+r-1}\left[{i+r-1\choose r}-{i+r-1-j\choose r}-{j-1\choose r}\right]{n_{2}\choose j}{n_{1}\choose i+r-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{i+r-1}\left[{i+r-1\choose r}-{i+r-1-j\choose r}-{j-1\choose r}\right]{n_{2}\choose j}{n_{1}\choose i+r-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{i+r-1}{i+r-1\choose r}{n_{2}\choose j}{n_{1}\choose i+r-j}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}{i+r-1-j\choose r}{n_{2}\choose j}{n_{1}\choose i+r-j}-

\displaystyle\hskip 195.5028pt\sum_{j=r+1}^{i+r-1}{j-1\choose r}{n_{2}\choose j}{n_{1}\choose i+r-j}

Now using the formula
\sum_{j=0}^{t}{a\choose j}{b\choose t-j}={a+b\choose t}
we get the following.

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+r}(R_{K_{n_{1},n_{2}}}/I_{r}(K_{n_{1},n_{2}}))

\displaystyle={n_{1}+n_{2}\choose i+r}{i+r-1\choose r}-\sum_{\underset{j_{1}+j_{2}=i+r}{(j_{1},j_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}}{n_{1}\choose j_{1}}{n_{2}\choose j_{2}}{j_{1}-1\choose r}-\sum_{\underset{j_{1}+j_{2}=i+r}{(j_{1},j_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}}{n_{1}\choose j_{1}}{n_{2}\choose j_{2}}{j_{2}-1\choose r}

\displaystyle={n_{1}+n_{2}\choose i+r}{i+r-1\choose r}-\sum_{\underset{j_{1}+j_{2}=i+r}{(j_{1},j_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}}\left[\prod_{s=1}^{2}{n_{s}\choose j_{s}}\right]\sum_{s=1}^{2}{j_{s}-1\choose r}.

Now suppose
t>2
and the formula is true for all positive integers less than
t
. Let
G_{1}^{\prime}=K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t-1}}
and let
G_{2}^{\prime}
denote the graph on
n_{t}
vertices with no edges. Then
K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}}=G_{1}^{\prime}*G_{2}^{\prime}
. Now applying the formula in
Theorem

3.8
again and using induction hypothesis we get
\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}}}/I_{r}(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}}))=0
if
d\neq r
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+r}(R_{K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}}}/I_{r}(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{i+r-1}\left\{{n_{t}\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+r}(R_{G_{1}^{\prime}}/I_{r}(G_{1}^{\prime}))+{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}n_{s}\choose j}\beta_{i-j,i-j+r}(R_{G_{2}^{\prime}}/I_{r}(G_{2}^{\prime}))\right\}+

\displaystyle\hskip 127.50183pt\sum_{j=1}^{i+r-1}\left[{i+r-1\choose r}-{i+r-1-j\choose r}-{j-1\choose r}\right]{n_{t}\choose j}{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}n_{s}\choose i+r-j}

Note that
\beta_{i-j,i-j+r}(R_{G_{2}^{\prime}}/I_{r}(G_{2}^{\prime}))=0
for all
r\geq 1
. Hence

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+r}(R_{K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}}}/I_{r}(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{i+r-1}{n_{t}\choose j}\left[{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}n_{s}\choose i-j+r}{i-j+r-1\choose r}-\sum_{\underset{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}j_{s}=i-j+r}{(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{t-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{t-1}}}\left\{\prod_{s=1}^{t-1}{n_{s}\choose j_{s}}\right\}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}{j_{s}-1\choose r}\right]+

\displaystyle\hskip 68.00098pt{i+r-1\choose r}\sum_{j=1}^{i+r-1}{n_{t}\choose j}{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}n_{s}\choose i-j+r}-\sum_{j=1}^{i+r-1}{i-j+r-1\choose r}{n_{t}\choose j}{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}n_{s}\choose i-j+r}-

\displaystyle\hskip 263.50378pt\sum_{j=1}^{i+r-1}{j-1\choose r}{n_{t}\choose j}{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}n_{s}\choose i-j+r}

\displaystyle={i+r-1\choose r}\sum_{j=0}^{i+r}{n_{t}\choose j}{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}n_{s}\choose i-j+r}-{n_{t}\choose i+r}{i+r-1\choose r}-

\displaystyle\hskip 55.2508pt\sum_{j=0}^{i+r-1}{n_{t}\choose j}\sum_{\underset{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}j_{s}=i-j+r}{(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{t-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{t-1}}}\left\{\prod_{s=1}^{t-1}{n_{s}\choose j_{s}}\right\}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}{j_{s}-1\choose r}-\sum_{j=1}^{i+r-1}{n_{t}\choose j}{j-1\choose r}{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}n_{s}\choose i-j+r}

Note that
\sum_{j=0}^{i+r}{n_{t}\choose j}{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}n_{s}\choose i-j+r}={\sum_{s=1}^{t}n_{s}\choose i-j+r}
. Thus

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+r}(R_{K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}}}/I_{r}(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}}))

\displaystyle={i+r-1\choose r}{\sum_{s=1}^{t}n_{s}\choose i+r}-\sum_{j=0}^{i+r-1}{n_{t}\choose j}\sum_{\underset{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}j_{s}=i-j+r}{(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{t-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{t-1}}}\left\{\prod_{s=1}^{t-1}{n_{s}\choose j_{s}}\right\}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}{j_{s}-1\choose r}-

\displaystyle\hskip 280.50403pt\sum_{j=1}^{i+r}{n_{t}\choose j}{j-1\choose r}{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}n_{s}\choose i-j+r}

Now by induction on
s
we have
{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}n_{s}\choose i-j+r}=\sum_{\underset{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}j_{s}=i-j+r}{(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{t-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{t-1}}}\prod_{s=1}^{t-1}{n_{s}\choose j_{s}}
. Hence

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+r}(R_{K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}}}/I_{r}(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}}))

\displaystyle={i+r-1\choose r}{\sum_{s=1}^{t}n_{s}\choose i+r}-\sum_{j=0}^{i+r}{n_{t}\choose j}\left[\sum_{\underset{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}j_{s}=i-j+r}{(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{t-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{t-1}}}\left\{\prod_{s=1}^{t-1}{n_{s}\choose j_{s}}\right\}\left\{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}{j_{s}-1\choose r}+{j-1\choose r}\right\}\right]

\displaystyle={i+r-1\choose r}{\sum_{s=1}^{t}n_{s}\choose i+r}-\sum_{\underset{\sum_{s=1}^{t}j_{s}=i+r}{(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{t})\in\mathbb{N}^{t}}}\left\{\prod_{s=1}^{t}{n_{s}\choose j_{s}}\right\}\sum_{s=1}^{t}{j_{s}-1\choose r}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Again, since
\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}}}/I_{r}(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}}))=0
for
d\neq r
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.5
.

For all positive integer
r
,
\mathrm{reg}(R_{K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}}}/I_{r}(K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}}))=r
.

As an application of
Theorem

4.4
we get formulas for
\mathbb{N}
-graded Betti numbers of the Stanley-Reisner ideal of
r
-independence complexes of star graphs. For
r=1
case, this formula was also first derived in the thesis of Jacques
[
18
, Theorem 5.4.11]
.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
K_{1,n}
denote the star graph on
n+1
vertices, where
V(K_{1,n})=\{x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
and
E(K_{1,n})=\{\{x_{0},x_{i}\}\mid i\in[n]\}
. Then the
\mathbb{N}
-graded Betti numbers of
R_{K_{1,n}}/I_{r}(K_{1,n})
, for
r\geq 1
, can be expressed as follows.

\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{K_{1,n}}/I_{r}(K_{1,n}))=\begin{cases}{i+r-2\choose r-1}{n\choose i+r-1}&\text{ for }d=r\text{ and }1\leq i\leq n-r+1,\\
0&\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

Let
G
be a finite simple graph on the vertex set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and let
t
be an integer such that
t>1
. A path of length
t
from a vertex
u
in
G
to another vertex
v
is a sequence of vertices
u=x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\ldots,x_{i_{t}}=v
such that
\{x_{i_{j}},x_{i_{j+1}}\}\in E(G)
for each
j\in[t-1]
. The
t
-path ideal of
G
, denoted by
J_{t}(G)
, is a monomial ideal generated by the monomials
\left\{\prod_{j=1}^{t}x_{i_{j}}\mid\{x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\ldots,x_{i_{t}}\}\text{ is a path in }G\right\}
.

Note that if
C_{n}
is a cycle of length
n
then
J_{r+1}(C_{n})=I_{r}(C_{n})
. The graded Betti numbers of the path ideal of
C_{n}
are calculated by Alilooee and Faridi in
[
2
]
. Thus using
[
2
, Theorem 4.13]
we can express the graded Betti numbers of
I_{r}(C_{n})
as follows.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
n,r,p
and
q
be integers such that
1\leq r\leq n-1
and
n=(r+2)p+q
, where
p\geq 0
and
0\leq q\leq r+1
. Then

\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{C_{n}}/I_{r}(C_{n}))=\begin{cases}r+1&\text{ if }i=2p,d=n-2p,q=0,\\
1&\text{ if }i=2p+1,d=n-2p-1,q\neq 0,\\
\frac{n}{n-\frac{d(r+1)}{r}}{\frac{d}{r}\choose i-\frac{d}{r}}{n-\frac{d(r+1)}{r}\choose\frac{d}{r}}&\text{ if }i<n-d,0\leq\frac{d}{r}\leq i,i\leq\frac{2d}{r}\leq 2p,\\
0&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Now we proceed to determine the graded Betti numbers of
I_{r}(W_{n+1})
, where
W_{n+1}
is the wheel graph on
n+1
vertices. Let
H
be the graph consisting of only one vertex. Then
W_{n+1}=C_{n}*H
. Therefore, using
Theorem

3.8
,
Theorem

4.9
and the Pascal’s identity we get the following.

Theorem 4.10
.

The
\mathbb{N}
-graded Betti numbers of the wheel graph
W_{n+1}
can be expressed as follows.

\displaystyle\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{W_{n+1}}/I_{r}(W_{n+1}))

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{C_{n}}/I_{r}(C_{n}))+\beta_{i-1,i+d-1}(R_{C_{n}}/I_{r}(C_{n}))+{i+d-2\choose d-1}{n\choose i+d-1}&\text{ for }d=r,\\
\beta_{i,i+d}(R_{C_{n}}/I_{r}(C_{n}))+\beta_{i-1,i+d-1}(R_{C_{n}}/I_{r}(C_{n}))&\text{ for }d\geq r+1.\end{cases}

Using
Corollary

3.10
we get the following.

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
n,r,p
and
q
be integers such that
1\leq r\leq n-1
and
n=(r+2)p+q
, where
p\geq 0
and
0\leq q\leq r+1
. Then

\mathrm{reg}(R_{W_{n+1}}/I_{r}(W_{n+1}))=\mathrm{reg}(R_{C_{n}}/I_{r}(C_{n}))=\begin{cases}rp+q-1&\text{ if }q\neq 0,\\
rp&\text{ if }q=0.\end{cases}

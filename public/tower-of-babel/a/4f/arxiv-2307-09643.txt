A note on an effective characterization of covers with an application to higher rank representations
Tarik Aougab, Max Lahn, Marissa Loving, Nicholas Miller

Abstract
. In this note we prove an effective characterization of when two finite-degree covers of a connected, orientable surface of negative Euler characteristic are isomorphic in terms of which curves have simple elevations, weakening the hypotheses to consider curves with explicitly bounded self-intersection number. As an application we show that for sufficiently large N \displaystyle N , the set of unmarked traces associated to simple closed curves in a generically chosen representation to SL N ​ ( ℝ ) \displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{N}\left\lparen\mathbb{R}\right\rparen distinguishes between pairs of non-isomorphic covers.

3.1.
Counting simple curves of bounded hyperbolic length

The following theorem of Rivin gives effective estimates on the growth of the number of simple geodesics of bounded length on a hyperbolic surface of finite type as a polynomial in the bound. The degree of this polynomial increases with the complexity of the surface, so that the number of simple geodesics of bounded length grows noticeably faster on surfaces of higher genus. By a combinatorial argument, we show that covers of different degrees can be distinguished by which curves of explicitly bounded length have simple elevations.

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\deg\left\lparen\pi\right\rparen}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}{i\left\lparen\widetilde{\gamma}_{j},\widetilde{\gamma}_{k}\right\rparen}<\frac{1}{2\deg\left\lparen\pi\right\rparen}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}{\frac{\ell_{Z}\left\lparen\widetilde{\gamma_{j}}\right\rparen}{2\mathrm{sinh}^{-1}\left\lparen\mathrm{csch}\left\lparen\frac{\ell_{Z}\left\lparen\widetilde{\gamma_{k}}\right\rparen}{2}\right\rparen\right\rparen}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\deg\left\lparen\pi\right\rparen}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}{\frac{\deg\left\lparen\pi\right\rparen\ell_{S}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}{2\mathrm{sinh}^{-1}\left\lparen\mathrm{csch}\left\lparen\frac{\deg\left\lparen\pi\right\rparen\ell_{S}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}{2}\right\rparen\right\rparen}}=\frac{n^{2}\ell_{S}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}{4\mathrm{sinh}^{-1}\left\lparen\mathrm{csch}\left\lparen\frac{\deg\left\lparen\pi\right\rparen\ell_{S}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}{2}\right\rparen\right\rparen}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{\deg\left\lparen\pi\right\rparen^{2}\ell_{S}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}{4\mathrm{sinh}^{-1}\left\lparen\mathrm{csch}\left\lparen\frac{\deg\left\lparen\pi\right\rparen\ell_{S}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen}{2}\right\rparen\right\rparen}.

That is, the self-intersection number
\displaystyle i\left\lparen\gamma,\gamma\right\rparen
of
\displaystyle\gamma
is explicitly bounded in terms of its length
\displaystyle\ell_{S}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen
and the degree
\displaystyle\deg\left\lparen\pi\right\rparen
. ∎

3.3.
Subsurface projection and the bounded geodesic image theorem

For this section, fix a basepoint
\displaystyle s\in S
on a closed surface
\displaystyle S
of negative Euler characteristic. Any curve
\displaystyle\gamma
on
\displaystyle S
has an associated
annular complex

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen
, which we describe below.

A curve
\displaystyle\gamma
on
\displaystyle S
determines a conjugacy class of cyclic subgroups of
\displaystyle\pi_{1}\left\lparen S,s\right\rparen

\displaystyle\Pi_{\gamma}\colon\left\lparen S_{\gamma},s_{\gamma}\right\rparen\xrightarrow{}\left\lparen S,s\right\rparen

\displaystyle\left\lparen\Pi_{\gamma}\right\rparen_{*}\colon\pi_{1}\left\lparen S_{\gamma},s_{\gamma}\right\rparen\xhookrightarrow{}\pi_{1}\left\lparen S,s\right\rparen

\displaystyle\left\langle\gamma\right\rangle
generated by
\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle S_{\gamma}
is a surface of finite type with infinite cyclic fundamental group
\displaystyle\pi_{1}\left\lparen S_{\gamma},s_{\gamma}\right\rparen\cong\left\langle\gamma\right\rangle

\displaystyle S_{\gamma}
is homeomorphic to an open annulus.

Fix a hyperbolic metric on
\displaystyle S

\displaystyle\Pi_{\gamma}
to a complete hyperbolic metric on
\displaystyle S_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\overline{S_{\gamma}}
the Gromov compactification of
\displaystyle S_{\gamma}
, which is homeomorphic to a closed annulus.
\displaystyle\mathscr{C}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen
is a flag simplicial complex whose vertices are path-homotopy classes of properly embedded, non-peripheral arcs in
\displaystyle\overline{S_{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen

\displaystyle d_{\gamma}
so that every simplex is a regular Euclidean simplex of side length
\displaystyle 1

Let
\displaystyle\mathrm{B}_{2}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen

\displaystyle 2

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}\left\lparen S\right\rparen

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}\left\lparen S\right\rparen\setminus\mathrm{B}_{2}\left\lparen\gamma\right\rparen

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle S_{\gamma}

\displaystyle L
-short set
\displaystyle\mathrm{short}\left\lparen\mathbf{t}\cdot\alpha,L\right\rparen

\mathrm{diam}_{\mathscr{C}\left\lparen X\right\rparen}\left\lparen\mathrm{short}\left\lparen\mathbf{t}\cdot\alpha,L\right\rparen\right\rparen\leq 4\mathrm{log}_{2}\left\lparen L\right\rparen+k_{0}.

This is proved in

Tan17

\displaystyle L_{0}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{W_{0}}
, where
\displaystyle W_{0}>0

\displaystyle\left\lvert\chi\left\lparen S\right\rparen\right\rvert

Proposition B.3

(
[
Tan17
, Proposition 4.1(3)]
)
.

\displaystyle S\left\lparen\mathbf{t}\cdot\alpha\right\rparen
contains an essential annulus of width at least
\displaystyle W_{0}\left\lVert\mathbf{t}\right\rVert_{\alpha}

Above,
\displaystyle S\left\lparen\mathbf{t}\cdot\alpha\right\rparen
is the singular flat surface dual to the union of curves in
\displaystyle\alpha
, where the rectangle corresponding to an intersection point between
\displaystyle\alpha_{j}
and
\displaystyle\alpha_{k}
has side lengths
\displaystyle t_{j}
and
\displaystyle t_{k}
. This proposition is proved using the following lemma of Bowditch.

Lemma B.4

(
[
Bow06
, Lemma 5.1]
)
.

Let
\displaystyle\rho
be a singular Riemannian metric on an orientable closed surface
\displaystyle\Sigma
with area
\displaystyle 1
, and let
\displaystyle A\subsetneq\Sigma
be a finite set. If there is a homeomorphism
\displaystyle f\colon\left[{0},{\infty}\right\rparen\xrightarrow{}\left[{0},{\infty}\right\rparen
so that

\mathrm{area}\left\lparen D\right\rparen\leq f\left\lparen\ell\left\lparen\partial D\right\rparen\right\rparen

for any region
\displaystyle D
of
\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle A
, then
\displaystyle\Sigma\setminus A
contains an essential annulus of width at least
\displaystyle\eta>0
, where
\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle\left\lvert\chi\left\lparen\Sigma\right\rparen\right\rvert
and
\displaystyle\left\lvert A\right\rvert

In our desired context, the set
\displaystyle A
of marked points is empty, and so

once one has shown that
\displaystyle\mathrm{area}\left\lparen D\right\rparen\leq 4\ell\left\lparen\partial D\right\rparen^{2}

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle S\left\lparen\mathbf{t}\cdot\alpha\right\rparen

B.3.
A weaker version of
[
Tan17
, Lemma 5.1]

The second clause to

, Lemma 5.1]
states that
\displaystyle\mbox{short}(\textbf{t}\cdot\alpha,L)
has uniformly bounded diameter in
\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\Sigma)

in terms only of
\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\Sigma
and not on

\displaystyle\alpha
does not fill the surface. Since in our context,
\displaystyle\alpha
will always be the
\displaystyle G

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle G\cdot\gamma

\displaystyle\Sigma

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle 2
from
\displaystyle\pi(\gamma)
:

Lemma B.5
.

Suppose
\displaystyle d_{\Sigma}(\gamma,\pi(\gamma))\geq 2
, then
\displaystyle G\cdot\gamma
fills
\displaystyle\Sigma
.

Proof.

If
\displaystyle G\cdot\gamma
does not fill
\displaystyle\Sigma
, then there is some essential subsurface

\displaystyle P
to
\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle P|_{Y}:Y\rightarrow Z
where

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}(\eta)

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}(\mathcal{C}(S))

B.4.
Assembling the proof modulo
\displaystyle W_{0}
and Bowditch’s lemma

In this subsection, we will obtain an effective version of
[
Tan17
, Proposition 6.2]
, save for the constant
\displaystyle W_{0}
. First, we effectivize the proof of
[
Tan17
, Lemma 5.1]
in the event that
\displaystyle\alpha
fills. There, Tang shows that any two curves in
\displaystyle\mbox{short}(\textbf{t}\cdot\alpha,L)
(for
\displaystyle L>L_{0}
with
\displaystyle L_{0}
as above) intersect at most
\displaystyle L/W_{0}
times.

We can then use an effective version of Hempel’s bound on curve complex distance in terms of intersection number, due to Bowditch
[
Bow14
]
(this particular version can be found in
[
MT22
]
)

d_{\mathscr{C}\left\lparen\Sigma\right\rparen}(\alpha,\beta)<6+2\cdot\frac{\mathrm{log}(2\cdot i(\alpha,\beta)+1)}{\mathrm{log}((2g+p-4)/2)},

where
\displaystyle g,p
denotes the genus and number of punctures of
\displaystyle\Sigma
, respectively. Tang points out that
\displaystyle L
need only be bigger than
\displaystyle\sqrt{2}/W_{0}
, and Bowditch obtains an effective bound on
\displaystyle W_{0}
in
[
Bow14
]
which is the following bound

W_{0}\geq\frac{1}{8\cdot(2g+p-1)\cdot(2g+p+6)}.

We note that this also uses Tang’s isoperimetric inequality mentioned above; Bowditch states the result in terms of some constant
\displaystyle h
so that the area of a trivial region is at most
\displaystyle h
times the length of its boundary squared and moreover shows that a factor of
\displaystyle 4\sqrt{h}
should appear in the bound. Thus, the appearance of
\displaystyle 8
in the denominator above is actually
\displaystyle 4\sqrt{4}
, where the
\displaystyle\sqrt{4}
corresponds to the
\displaystyle 4
in Tang’s isoperimetric bound.

Lemma 6.4 of
[
Tan17
]
above therefore says that
\displaystyle\pi(\alpha)
lies in
\displaystyle\mbox{short}({\bf{1}}\cdot G\alpha,L_{0}|G|)
, which by the above, has diameter at most

12+4\cdot\frac{\mathrm{log}(2\cdot(8\sqrt{2}\cdot(2g+p-1)\cdot(2g+p+6))^{2}\cdot|G|+1)}{\mathrm{log}((2g+p-4)/2)},

which is bounded uniformly above independent of
\displaystyle g,p
.

We would be done if the circumcentre of
\displaystyle G\cdot\alpha
were also in this short curve set. It need not be, but
[
Tan17
, Proposition 5.2]
asserts the existence of some
\displaystyle k_{1}
so that for all
\displaystyle L>L_{0}
,
\displaystyle\mbox{short}(\rho,L)
(which is defined to be the union, taken over all
t
, of
\displaystyle\mbox{short}(\textbf{t}\cdot\rho,L)
) is Hausdorff distance at most
\displaystyle k_{1}
from
\displaystyle\mbox{Hull}(\rho)
, for any finite collection of curves
\displaystyle\rho
. Here
\displaystyle k_{1}
depends only on
\displaystyle L
, the topology of the surface, and the number of curves in
\displaystyle\rho
. In particular when
\displaystyle\rho=G\cdot\alpha
,
\displaystyle k_{1}
will depend only on
\displaystyle|G|
, the topology of
\displaystyle\Sigma
(or alternatively, the topology of
\displaystyle S
and the degree of
\displaystyle P
), and
\displaystyle L_{0}
.

In
[
Tan17
, Proposition 5.2]
, Tang shows that
\displaystyle k_{1}
can be taken to be a constant
\displaystyle r=r(\Sigma,|G|,L_{0})
, where
\displaystyle r
is defined to be the distance between

\mbox{short}(\mbox{{t}}^{\prime}\cdot\alpha^{\prime},|G|L_{0}/\sqrt{2}),

and
\displaystyle[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}]
, where

(1)

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}=\left\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\right\}
, the points in the orbit of
\displaystyle G\cdot\alpha
maximizing
\displaystyle t_{i}t_{j}i(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})
over all pairs
\displaystyle(i,j)\in\left\{1,...,|G|\right\}^{2}
, and

(2)

\displaystyle[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}]
is any geodesic in the curve graph between
\displaystyle\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
.

Since curve graphs are
\displaystyle 17
-hyperbolic, any two geodesics are at most
\displaystyle 92\cdot 17=1564
apart (see for instance
[
GS19
, Theorem 1.1]
), so up to addition of this universal constant, the choice of geodesic will not matter.

B.5.
Effectivizing the Hausdorff distance statement from Bowditch

It remains to determine how close the short curve set
\displaystyle\mbox{short}(\left\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\right\},|G|W_{0}^{-1})
is to a geodesic segment
\displaystyle[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}]
. For this, we use
[
Bow06
, Proposition 6.2]
, which asserts that there is some
\displaystyle q
and some
\displaystyle h
so that for all
\displaystyle Q>q
, the set

G_{Q}(\alpha,\beta)=\left\{\gamma\in\mathcal{C}:i(\gamma,\alpha)\cdot i(\gamma,\beta)<Q\cdot i(\alpha,\beta)\right\},

is within
\displaystyle h
of
\displaystyle[\alpha,\beta]
. To understand the relevance of this, first consider some
\displaystyle\gamma\in\mbox{short}(\left\{\alpha,\beta\right\},Q)
. Thus,

i(\gamma,t_{1}\alpha)+i(\gamma,t_{2}\beta)<Q\cdot\sqrt{t_{1}t_{2}i(\alpha,\beta)},

for some nonnegative
\displaystyle t_{1},t_{2}
. Squaring both sides yields

t_{1}t_{2}i(\gamma,\alpha)\cdot i(\gamma,\beta)+T<Q^{2}t_{1}t_{2}i(\alpha,\beta),

where
\displaystyle T
is non-negative. Therefore, it follows that
\displaystyle\gamma\in G_{Q^{2}}(\alpha,\beta)
.

Bowditch proves
[
Bow06
, Proposition 6.2]
using the “lines” he constructs in the proof of hyperbolicity, which he denotes by
\displaystyle\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}
. In
[
Bow06
, Section 3]
, he outlines a series of criteria involving a family of lines such that if any graph admits such a family, it is hyperbolic, and the lines are uniformly quasigeodesic. The criteria appear directly before
[
Bow06
, Proposition 3.1]
.

The proof of
[
Bow06
, Proposition 3.1]
uses an auxiliary set of paths denoted
\displaystyle\pi_{\alpha\beta}
. In
[
Bow06
, Lemma 3.3]
, Bowditch shows that these paths are
\displaystyle(K,2K)
-quasigeodesic, where
\displaystyle K
is as in the statement of the three axioms he uses as a criteria for hyperbolicity. He also shows, directly before this lemma, that
\displaystyle\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}
is Hausdorff distance at most
\displaystyle K
from
\displaystyle\pi_{\alpha\beta}
. Putting all of this together and using the Morse Lemma, it follows that
\displaystyle\Lambda_{\alpha,\beta}
is within a distance of
\displaystyle 92K^{2}(2K+2+17)
of a geodesic
\displaystyle[\alpha,\beta]
(again using
[
GS19
]
).
In
[
Bow06
, Section 4]
, Bowditch shows that the curve graph satisfies the three required axioms, for
\displaystyle K=18D+2
.

Furthermore, Bowditch shows in
[
Bow14
]
that it suffices to choose
\displaystyle D=20
for all surfaces. Putting all of this together, we get that
\displaystyle\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}
is within a neighborhood of
\displaystyle[\alpha,\beta]
of radius at most

\displaystyle\displaystyle 92K^{2}(2K+19)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=92\cdot(18D+2)^{2}\cdot(36D+23),

\displaystyle\displaystyle=8,957,643,664.

In the direction of the proof of
[
Bow06
, Proposition 6.2]
that is relevant to us, Bowditch only needs to establish that
\displaystyle G_{Q}(\alpha,\beta)
is nearby
\displaystyle\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}
, since as mentioned above, we can estimate the distance between
\displaystyle\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}
and
\displaystyle[\alpha,\beta]
.

Bowditch’s proof of
[
Bow06
, Proposition 6.2]
shows that one may set
\displaystyle q=(W_{0}^{-1})^{2}
, and then that

G_{Q}(\alpha,\beta)\subset L_{\alpha\beta}(t,\sqrt{Q}),

for all
\displaystyle Q>q
, where
\displaystyle L_{\alpha\beta}(t,\sqrt{Q})
is as defined in
[
Bow06
, Section 4]
. Thus we are interested in the diameter of
\displaystyle L_{\alpha\beta}(t,(W_{0}^{-1})^{2})
, which, again by Bowditch’s proof of uniform hyperbolicity
[
Bow14
]
, is uniformly bounded above, for instance by
\displaystyle 20
.

B.6.
Putting it all together.

Now, assume we begin with some
\displaystyle\gamma\in\mbox{short}(\left\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\right\},|G|W_{0}^{-1})
. Then

\gamma\in G_{|G|^{2}(W_{0}^{-1})^{2}}(\alpha,\beta).

It follows from the very end of the previous section, and
[
Bow06
, Lemma 4.2]
, that
\displaystyle\gamma
is at most

2(W_{0}^{-1})^{3}|G|+2,

away from
\displaystyle\Lambda_{\alpha\beta}
. Therefore,
\displaystyle\gamma
is at most

2(W_{0}^{-1})^{3}|G|+2+8,957,643,664,

away from
\displaystyle[\alpha,\beta]
.

It finally follows, assuming
\displaystyle G\cdot\alpha
fills
\displaystyle\Sigma
, that
\displaystyle\pi(\alpha)
is within

2(W_{0}^{-1})^{3}|G|+2+8,957,643,664+12+4\cdot\frac{\mathrm{log}(2\cdot(8\sqrt{2}\cdot(2g+p-1)\cdot(2g+p+6))^{2}\cdot|G|+1)}{\mathrm{log}((2g+p-4)/2)},

of the circumcentre of
\displaystyle G\cdot\alpha
.

If
\displaystyle G\cdot\alpha
does not fill
\displaystyle\Sigma
,
B.5
implies that the radius of
\displaystyle G\cdot\alpha
is at most
\displaystyle 2
and that
\displaystyle\pi(\alpha)
is distance at most
\displaystyle 2
away, and so the circumcentre of
\displaystyle G\cdot\alpha
is at most distance
\displaystyle 4
from
\displaystyle\pi(\alpha)
.

Since the log term above is bounded above independently of
\displaystyle g,p
, the entire bound is
\displaystyle O(|G|)
and on the order of a polynomial of degree
\displaystyle 6
in
\displaystyle g
and
\displaystyle p
. For a less explicit upper bound that is stronger asymptotically, note that
\displaystyle|G|
is necessarily bounded above by a linear function of
\displaystyle g
and
\displaystyle p
. Therefore
\displaystyle|G|^{2}\cdot(W_{0}^{-1})^{2}
grows at most polynomially in
\displaystyle g,p
of degree at most
\displaystyle 6
. The techniques in
[
Aou13
]
show how to obtain a bound that is uniform in
\displaystyle g,p
on the diameter of
\displaystyle L_{\alpha\beta}(t,R)
whenever
\displaystyle R
grows slower than some polynomial in
\displaystyle g,p
.

The Proximal Operator of the Piece-wise Exponential Function and Its Application in Compressed Sensing
Yulan Liu, Yuyang Zhou, Rongrong Lin

Abstract
This paper characterizes the proximal operator of the piece-wise exponential function − e − | x | / σ 1\!-\!e^{-|x|/\sigma} with a given shape parameter σ > 0 \sigma\!>\!0 , which is a popular nonconvex surrogate of ℓ 0 \ell_{0} -norm in support vector machines, zero-one programming problems, and compressed sensing, etc. Although Malek-Mohammadi et al. [IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing, 64(21):5657–5671, 2016] once worked on this problem, the expressions they derived were regrettably inaccurate. In a sense, it was lacking a case. Using the Lambert W function and an extensive study of the piece-wise exponential function, we have rectified the formulation of the proximal operator of the piece-wise exponential function in light of their work. We have also undertaken a thorough analysis of this operator. Finally, as an application in compressed sensing, an iterative shrinkage and thresholding algorithm (ISTA) for the piece-wise exponential function regularization problem is developed and fully investigated. A comparative study of ISTA with nine popular non-convex penalties in compressed sensing demonstrates the advantage of the piece-wise exponential penalty.

II
Proximal operator of PiE

The proximal operator with respect to a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}
is defined by for any
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\,{\rm Prox}\,_{\mu\lambda f}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}):=\arg\min_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lambda f({\boldsymbol{x}})\!+\!\frac{1}{2\mu}\|{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\|^{2},

(2)

where
\mu>0
and
\lambda>0
are given parameters.
As mentioned above in Section
I
, characterization of
\,{\rm Prox}\,_{\mu\lambda f}
is crucial to the proximal algorithm. When
f
is a separate function,
by
[
13
, Theorem 6.6]
, it is sufficient to deduce the characterization of
\,{\rm Prox}\,_{\mu\lambda f}
in (
2
) when
n=1
, i.e., for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\,{\rm Prox}\,_{\mu\lambda f}(x_{0}):=\arg\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\lambda f(x)+\frac{1}{2\mu}(x-x_{0})^{2}.

The proximal operator of some commonly used nonconvex separable
\ell_{0}
-norm surrogates, such as SCAD
[
9
]
, MCP
[
12
]
, CaP
[
10
]
, Log
[
43
]
, TL1
[
24
]
are well-known.

In this work, we study the proximal operator of PiE penalty function, defined as in (
1
), i.e., for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\,{\rm Prox}\,^{(\sigma)}_{\mu\lambda}(x_{0}):={\rm arg}\min\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\lambda(1-e^{-\frac{|x|}{\sigma}})+\frac{1}{2\mu}(x-x_{0})^{2}.

(3)

Denote

L(x,x_{0}):=\lambda(1-e^{-\frac{|x|}{\sigma}})+\frac{1}{2\mu}(x-x_{0})^{2},\mbox{ for any }x\in\mathbb{R}.

Notice that
L(x,x_{0})\!\to\!+\infty
as
|x|\to\!+\!\infty
, the optimization problem in (
3
) has a nonempty optimal solution by the Weierstrass Theorem
[
13
, Theorem 2.14]
. Hence,
the proximal operator
\,{\rm Prox}\,^{(\sigma)}_{\mu\lambda}
is well defined.
Since
L(x,-x_{0})\!=\!L(-x,x_{0})
for every
x\!\in\!\mathbb{R}
,
the following anti-symmetry of
\,{\rm Prox}\,^{(\sigma)}_{\mu\lambda f}
is easy to be checked along with (
3
).

Lemma II.1

Let
\mu>0,\lambda>0
and
\sigma>0
. For any
x_{0}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}
,
\,{\rm Prox}\,^{(\sigma)}_{\mu\lambda}(x_{0})\!=\!-\,{\rm Prox}\,^{(\sigma)}_{\mu\lambda}(-x_{0})
.

In what follows, let
\mu>0,\lambda>0
and
\sigma>0
be given.
By Lemma
II.1
, we know that

\,{\rm Prox}\,^{(\sigma)}_{\mu\lambda}(x_{0})={\rm sign}(x_{0})\mathcal{T}_{\mu\lambda}^{(\sigma)}(x_{0}),\ \mbox{for any }x_{0}\in\mathbb{R},

(4)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\mu\lambda}^{(\sigma)}(x_{0}):={\rm arg}\min\limits_{x\geq 0}\Big\{\lambda(1-e^{-\frac{x}{\sigma}})+\frac{1}{2\mu}(x-|x_{0}|)^{2}\Big\}.

(5)

So, it is crucial to drive the expression of

\mathcal{T}_{\mu\lambda}^{(\sigma)}(x_{0})
in order to characterize
\,{\rm Prox}\,^{(\sigma)}_{\mu\lambda}(x_{0})
.
For convenience, for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote

\displaystyle\widehat{L}(x,x_{0}):=\lambda(1-e^{-\frac{x}{\sigma}})+\frac{1}{2\mu}(x-|x_{0}|)^{2}.

(6)

Before moving on, let us recall the Lambert W function which is a multi-valued inverse function with plenty of applications in areas like the theory of projectile motion, wave physics, cell growth models, solar wind, enzyme kinetics, plasma and quantum physics, general relativity, prey and parasite models, Twitter events, and so on
[
44
]
. The Lambert W function
W(x)
is a set of solutions of the equation

x=W(x)e^{W(x)},\mbox{ for any }x\in[-\frac{1}{e},+\infty).

The function
W(x)
is single-valued for
x\geq 0
or
x=-\frac{1}{e}
, and is double-valued for

-\frac{1}{e}<x<0
(see Fig.
2
). To discriminate between the two branches for
-\frac{1}{e}\leq x<0
, we use the same notation as in
[
44
, Section 1.5]

and denote the branch satisfying
W(x)\geq-1
and
W(x)\leq-1

by
W_{0}(x)
and
W_{-1}(x)
, respectively.
Such a function is a built-in function in Python
(
https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.special.lambertw.html
).
We refer the readers to the recent monograph
[
44
, Section 1.5]
on the Lambert W function to learn more details.

Fig. 2:

Lemma II.7

Let
x_{0}\neq 0
. Then
\mathcal{T}_{\mu\lambda}^{(\sigma)}(x_{0})\subseteq[0,|x_{0}|)
.

Proof:

Since

\widehat{L}^{\prime}(x,x_{0})\!=\!\frac{\lambda}{\sigma}e^{-\frac{x}{\sigma}}+\frac{x-|x_{0}|}{\mu},

for any
x\!>\!0
, it holds that
\widehat{L}^{\prime}(x,x_{0})>0
when
x>|x_{0}|
.
Notice that
\widehat{L}^{\prime}(|x_{0}|,x_{0})=\frac{\lambda}{\sigma}e^{-\frac{|x_{0}|}{\sigma}}>0
. By the sign-preserving property of continuous functions
\widehat{L}^{\prime}(x,x_{0})
at the point

, there exists a sufficient small
\delta>\!0\!
such that
\widehat{L}^{\prime}(x,x_{0})\!>\!\frac{\widehat{L}^{\prime}(|x_{0}|,x_{0})}{2}\!>\!0
for any
x\!\in\!(|x_{0}|-\delta,|x_{0}|+\delta)
.
Consequently,

\widehat{L}
is strictly increasing on
[|x_{0}|-\delta,+\infty)
and then

\mathcal{T}_{\mu\lambda}^{(\sigma)}(x_{0})=\arg\min\limits_{0\leq x<|x_{0}|}\widehat{L}(x,x_{0}),

which implies that
\mathcal{T}_{\mu\lambda}^{(\sigma)}(x_{0})\subseteq[0,|x_{0}|)
.
∎

To further characterize this operator

Lemma II.8

Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, the following statements hold.

(i)

the derivative function
\widehat{L}^{\prime}(x,x_{0})
is strictly convex on
[0,+\infty)
and
\widehat{L}^{\prime\prime}(x,x_{0})
is strictly monotone increase on
[0,+\infty)
.

(ii)

If

\widehat{L}^{\prime}(x,x_{0})
is strictly increasing on
[0,+\infty)
. Moreover,
\widehat{L}^{\prime}(x,x_{0})>\frac{\lambda}{\sigma}-\frac{|x_{0}|}{\mu}
for any
x>0
.

(iii)

If
\frac{\mu\lambda}{\sigma^{2}}>1
, then the derivative function
\widehat{L}^{\prime}(x,x_{0})
is strictly decreasing on
(0,\sigma\ln\frac{\mu\lambda}{\sigma^{2}}]
, and strictly increasing on
[\sigma\ln\frac{\mu\lambda}{\sigma^{2}},+\infty)
. Moreover,
\widehat{L}^{\prime}(x,x_{0})\geq\frac{\sigma}{\mu}(1+\ln\frac{\mu\lambda}{\sigma^{2}})-\frac{|x_{0}|}{\mu}

x>0

Proof:

By the expression of

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\widehat{L}^{\prime}(x,x_{0})=\frac{\lambda}{\sigma}e^{-\frac{x}{\sigma}}+\frac{x-|x_{0}|}{\mu},\\
&\widehat{L}^{\prime\prime}(x,x_{0})=-\frac{\lambda}{\sigma^{2}}e^{-\frac{x}{\sigma}}+\frac{1}{\mu},\\
&\widehat{L}^{\prime\prime\prime}(x,x_{0})=\frac{\lambda}{\sigma^{3}}e^{-\frac{x}{\sigma}}.\end{array}

(15)

Obviously, the strictly monotone increasing of
\widehat{L}^{\prime\prime}(x,x_{0})

\widehat{L}^{\prime\prime\prime}(x,x_{0})>0

Notice that
\widehat{L}^{\prime\prime}(0_{+},x_{0})\!:=\!\lim\limits_{x\downarrow 0}\widehat{L}^{\prime\prime}(x,x_{0})=\frac{1}{\mu}-\frac{\lambda}{\sigma^{2}}

III
An application in compressed sensing

Suppose that
A
is a given
m\times n
measurement matrix with
m<n
and
{\boldsymbol{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the observation vector.
Reconstructing a sparse signal from linear measurements is a current issue in compressed sensing, i.e.,

{\boldsymbol{b}}=A{\boldsymbol{x}}.

To find the sparse signal
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
the following optimization model is often used to

\min_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{2}\|A{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{b}}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda P({\boldsymbol{x}})

(30)

where
\lambda>0
is a regularization parameter and
P({\boldsymbol{x}})
is a penalty term, which reflects our prior knowledge about the signal to be recovered.
In this paper, we consider
P({\boldsymbol{x}})
be the PiE on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e., for any
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

P({\boldsymbol{x}})=P_{\sigma}({\boldsymbol{x}}):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{\sigma}(x_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(1-e^{-|x_{i}|/\sigma}).

(31)

where
f_{\sigma}
is defined as in (
1
).

III-A

Iterative Shrinkage and Thresholding Algorithm

In this subsection, we are devoted to applying ISTA
[
47
]
to the problem (
30
) with the PiE penalized function, i.e.,

\min_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{2}\|A{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{b}}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda P_{\sigma}({\boldsymbol{x}})

(32)

where
P_{\sigma}
is given by (
31
).
Given the current iterate point
{\boldsymbol{x}}^{l}
, the first term in (
32
) can be bounded above by the second order Taylor expansion at
{\boldsymbol{x}}^{l}
. Thus, the next iterate point
{\boldsymbol{x}}^{l+1}
can be given by

\begin{array}[]{ll}&{\boldsymbol{x}}^{l+1}\!:=\!\arg\min\limits_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{2}\|A{\boldsymbol{x}}^{l}-{\boldsymbol{b}}\|_{2}^{2}\!+\!({\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{x}}^{l})^{\top}(A^{\top}(A{\boldsymbol{x}}^{l}-{\boldsymbol{b}}))\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2\mu}\|{\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{x}}^{l}\|^{2}_{2}+\lambda P_{\sigma}({\boldsymbol{x}})\end{array}

where
\mu
is a constant parameter.
After some simple algebraic manipulation and cancellation of constant terms, the above iteration expression can be rewritten as

{\boldsymbol{x}}^{l+1}=\arg\min_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lambda P_{\sigma}({\boldsymbol{x}})+\frac{1}{2\mu}\Big\|{\boldsymbol{x}}-\big({\boldsymbol{x}}^{l}-\mu(A^{\top}(A{\boldsymbol{x}}^{l}-{\boldsymbol{b}})\big)\Big\|_{2}^{2},

which by the definition of the proximal operator yields

{\boldsymbol{x}}^{l+1}=\,{\rm Prox}\,_{\mu\lambda P_{\sigma}}\Big((I_{n}-\mu A^{\top}A){\boldsymbol{x}}^{l}+\mu A^{\top}{\boldsymbol{b}}\Big).

(33)

The iteration expression (
33
) was well known as ISTA or forward-backward splitting algorithm in
[
47
,
13
,
11
]
. The convergence of ISTA
has been well studied in
[
47
]
, when the penalty term
P(x)
is
a
\rho
weakly convex penalty function, i.e., there exist
\rho\geq 0
such that

P({\boldsymbol{x}})+\frac{\rho}{2}\|{\boldsymbol{x}}\|_{2}^{2}
is convex
[
47
, Definition 1]
.
Obviously, the function
\lambda P_{\sigma}
is
\rho
-weakly convex function for any
\rho\geq\frac{\lambda}{\sigma^{2}}

[
38
, Table 1]
. Denote the minimum and maximum eigenvalue of
A^{\top}A
by
\nu_{\min}(A^{\top}A)
and
\nu_{\max}(A^{\top}A)
, respectively.
Associated with
[
47
, Proposition 4]
, when

\frac{\lambda}{\sigma^{2}}\leq\rho\leq\nu_{\min}(A^{\top}A)
, the optimal solution set of (
32
) is non-empty. Moreover, when
0<\mu<\frac{2}{\nu_{\max}(A^{\top}A)+\rho}
, the sequence
\{{\boldsymbol{x}}^{l}:l\in\mathbb{N}\}
in (
33
) converges to a minimizer of (
32
).
With (
2
), (
4
), and the separability of
P_{\sigma}({\boldsymbol{x}})
,
the proximal operator
\,{\rm Prox}\,_{\mu\lambda P_{\sigma}}
in (
33
) takes the following form:

\begin{array}[]{l}\,{\rm Prox}\,_{\mu\lambda P_{\sigma}}({\boldsymbol{x}})\!=\!\big(\,{\rm Prox}\,^{(\sigma)}_{\mu\lambda}(x_{i}):i=1,2,\dots,n\big)^{\top}\\
\qquad\qquad\quad\!=\!\big({\rm sign}(x_{i})\mathcal{T}_{\mu\lambda}^{(\sigma)}(x_{i}):i=1,2,\dots,n\big)^{\top}\end{array}

(34)

where
\mathcal{T}_{\mu\lambda}^{(\sigma)}
is given by Theorem
II.9
if
\frac{\mu\lambda}{\sigma^{2}}\leq 1
; otherwise
\mathcal{T}_{\mu\lambda}^{(\sigma)}
is given by Theorem
II.14
.
Based on the above arguments, the ISTA for solving problem (
32
) is described in Algorithm

. For simplicity, we denote the maximum step size for ISTA by

\mu_{\max}:=\frac{2}{\nu_{\max}(A^{\top}A)+\frac{\lambda}{\sigma^{2}}}.

IV
Conclusion

In this paper, we have derived a correct and simple expression of the proximal operator for the PiE function. Theorems
II.9
and
II.17
have showed that the proximal operator for the PiE penalty has closed form, while Theorem
II.14
as an alternative of Theorem
II.17
is better suitable for use in numerical calculations.
Given a sparse signal such that amplitudes of its nonzero coefficients are uniformly distributed on
[-5,5]
and the sensing matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{128\times 256}
,
numerical experiments in compressed sensing in Section
III
suggest
the three parameters in Algorithm
1
should be given
\lambda=\frac{1}{100},\sigma=\frac{1}{2}
, and
\mu=0.99\mu_{\rm max}
. These numerical experiments also
have demonstrated the great advantage of ISTA with PiE compared with other penalties (soft, hard, half, SCAD, MCP, Log, TL1, and CaP) whenever the large step size was adopted.
We hope to apply PiE penalty function to other sparse-promoting optimization problems such as support vector machines, neural networks, low-rank matrix recovery, Fourier transform, and wavelet transform, etc in future work.

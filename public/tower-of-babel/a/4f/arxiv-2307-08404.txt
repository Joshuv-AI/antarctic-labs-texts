Influence of the Commutator Properties of Hamiltonians on the Robustness of Quantum Circuits
Vitalii Slynko, Vladyslav Bivziuk

Abstract
We have proved new estimates for the coherent control errors of quantum circuits used in quantum computing. These estimates essentially take into account the commutator properties of the Hamiltonians and are based on the formulas of the commutator calculus.

Introduction

The rapid development of quantum computing in recent years poses many new engineering, physical and mathematical problems.
An obstacle to the successful practical implementation of quantum computing algorithms is the influence of a significant amount of noise, which is a problem for demonstrating quantum advantages. Typically, the noise arising in quantum devices is divided into two types: coherent and decoherent. From a mathematical point of view, a coherent error can be represented as self-adjoint perturbations of the system’s Hamiltonian, and decoherent error can be represented as non-self-adjoint perturbations. Studies of coherent control errors were previously considered in
Trout et al. 2018
;
Levitt 1986a
;
Jones 2003a
;
Merrill and Brown 2014a
.
In a recent paper
Berberich et al. 2023
, the relevance of studying estimates of coherent errors is substantiated in detail and some results of the robustness analysis of quantum algorithms to coherent control errors are presented. The main theoretical result is justified using the Lipschitz constants. The estimates for the fidelity bounds contain the norms of the Hamiltonians that generate individual circuit elements. The results are applied to the 3-qubit quantum Fourier transform. The essential difference of these results from the well-known
Levitt 1986a
;
Jones 2003a
;
Merrill and Brown 2014a
;
Skolik et al. 2023
is that the perturbations of the Hamiltonians are not assumed to be small, identical or subject to certain statistics as, for example, in
Skolik et al. 2023
.

The aim of this work is to develop new theoretical tools for the analysis of coherent errors in the same problem statement as in
Berberich et al. 2023
. Our main idea is to take into account the commutative properties of Hamiltonians that generate an ideal quantum circuit. To do this, we use the mathematical apparatus of commutator calculus
Magnus et al. 2004
. We note that the commutator calculus was previously used for various problems of stability theory
Slyn’ko et al. 2019
;
Atamas et al. 2023
and control theory
Zuyev 2016
;
Suttner 2023
;
Grushkovskaya et al. 2018
as well as problems of quantum mechanics and quantum control
Jones 2003b
;
Levitt 1986b
;
Merrill and Brown 2014b
. The main contribution of this work is that we show that taking into account the commutative properties of the Hamiltonians of a quantum circuit in some cases allows us to establish less conservative error estimates than those obtained in
Berberich et al. 2023
. We obtain estimates for two types of coherent perturbations (single-parameter and multi-parameter) and present illustrative examples.

The work is organized as follows. In the next section, we give the necessary notation, facts, and commutator formulas. In Section 3, we formulate the problem, and in the fourth section, we prove the main result of the paper. In the fifth section, we give an illustrative examples and compare our results with known ones. In the last section, we discuss the results and prospects for further extensions.

Notations and commutator formulas

For the state vector
\ket{\psi}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the norm

\|\ket{\psi}\|:=\sqrt{\braket{\psi,\psi}}

\|x\|_{\infty}:=\max_{k=\overline{1,N}}|x_{k}|
.

For an arbitrary matrix

. The norm of

\|A\|_{2}:=\lambda_{\max}^{1/2}(A^{\dagger}A)

Let
A
,

\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}

\begin{gathered}:=AB-BA,\quad\operatorname{ad\,}A\,:\,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n\times n},\\
\quad X\mapsto\operatorname{ad\,}A(X):=[A,X].\end{gathered}

An important role is played by the Hadamard formula
Magnus et al. 2004

\begin{gathered}e^{t\operatorname{ad\,}A}(B)=e^{tA}Be^{-tA},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\end{gathered}

(1)

The group of unitary matrices is defined as

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{U}(n):=\{A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}\,:\,A^{\dagger}A=AA^{\dagger}=\operatorname{id\,}\}.\end{gathered}

If
U\in\mathbb{U}(n)

A^{\dagger}=A

A^{\dagger}=A

\begin{gathered}\|U\|_{2}=1,\quad\|e^{it\operatorname{ad\,}A}(B)\|_{2}=\|B\|_{2}.\end{gathered}

(2)

The identity matrix is denoted by

3
Problem statement

Let
\ket{\psi_{0}}
be the initial state of the quantum mechanical system, which is assumed to be normalized
\|\ket{\psi_{0}}\|=1
. In ideal quantum circuits, the final state vector
\ket{\widehat{\psi}}
is defined as the result of the sequential action of the unitary operators

\widehat{U}_{1},\dots,\widehat{U}_{N}
,
\widehat{U}_{k}\in\mathbb{U}(n)
,
k=\overline{1,N}
acting on the initial state of the system
\ket{\psi_{0}}
. For each unitary operator
\widehat{U}_{k}
, there exists a Hamiltonian
H_{k}
,
H_{k}^{\dagger}=H_{k}
that generates
\widehat{U}_{k}=e^{-iH_{k}}
.

Therefore, the final state is defined as

\begin{gathered}\ket{\widehat{\psi}}=e^{-iH_{1}}\dots e^{-iH_{N}}\ket{\psi_{0}}.\end{gathered}

(3)

In a non-ideal quantum circuit, the final state can be described in various ways. We restrict ourselves to two variants of perturbed states. For
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
, we introduce the perturbed state

\begin{gathered}\ket{\psi(\varepsilon)}:=U_{1}(\varepsilon)\dots U_{N}(\varepsilon)\ket{\psi_{0}},\end{gathered}

(4)

where
U_{k}(\varepsilon)=e^{-i(1+\varepsilon)H_{k}}
,
k=\overline{1,N}
.

For
\epsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{N})^{\operatorname{T\,}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we introduce a perturbed state of the form

\begin{gathered}\ket{\psi(\epsilon)}:=U_{1}(\varepsilon_{1})\dots U_{N}(\varepsilon_{N})\ket{\psi_{0}}.\end{gathered}

(5)

The main mathematical problem that we are considering formulated in
Berberich et al. 2023
is to find
M(\overline{\varepsilon})\geq 0
for a given
\overline{\varepsilon}\geq 0
such that for
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
(
\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
for the state (
5
)) under the condition
|\varepsilon|\leq\overline{\varepsilon}
(respectively
\|\epsilon\|_{\infty}\leq\overline{\varepsilon}
) and any initial state
\ket{\psi_{0}}
,
\|\ket{\psi_{0}}\|_{2}=1
, the following estimate holds

\begin{gathered}|\braket{\psi(\varepsilon),\widehat{\psi}}|\geq 1-M(\overline{\varepsilon})\quad\end{gathered}

(6)

(respectively
|\braket{\psi(\epsilon),\widehat{\psi}}|\geq 1-M(\overline{\varepsilon})
).

The main result obtained in
Berberich et al. 2023
is that
M(\overline{\varepsilon})
can be chosen in the form

\begin{gathered}M(\overline{\varepsilon})=\frac{\overline{\varepsilon}^{2}}{2}\Big(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\|H_{k}\|_{2}\Big)^{2}.\end{gathered}

(7)

The aim of our paper is to refine this estimate using the commutation properties of the set of Hamiltonians
H_{k}
,
k=\overline{1,n}
. We want to show that taking these properties into account can lead in some cases to less conservative estimates for
|\braket{\psi(\varepsilon),\widehat{\psi}}|
.

Proof of the main result

Proof
of Theorem
4.1
.

Let

\begin{gathered}\ket{\psi(t)}:=e^{-i(1+t)H_{1}}\dots e^{-i(1+t)H_{N}}\ket{\psi_{0}}.\end{gathered}

(10)

Differentiating with respect to
t
, we find

\begin{gathered}\ket{\dot{\psi}(t)}=-iH_{1}e^{-i(1+t)H_{1}}\dots e^{-i(1+t)H_{N}}\ket{\psi_{0}}-\dots{\\
}-ie^{-i(1+t)H_{1}}\dots e^{-i(1+t)H_{k-1}}H_{k}e^{-i(1+t)H_{k}}\dots e^{-i(1+t)H_{N}}\ket{\psi_{0}}\\
-ie^{-i(1+t)H_{1}}\dots e^{-i(1+t)H_{N}}H_{N}\ket{\psi_{0}}.\end{gathered}

From the formula (
1
), it follows that (for details see Appendix A)

\begin{gathered}e^{-i(1+t)H_{1}}H_{2}=e^{-i(1+t)\operatorname{ad\,}H_{1}}(H_{2})e^{-i(1+t)H_{1}}.\end{gathered}

\begin{gathered}e^{-i(1+t)H_{1}}\dots e^{-i(1+t)H_{k-1}}H_{k}\\
=e^{-i(1+t)\operatorname{ad\,}H_{1}}\dots e^{-i(1+t)\operatorname{ad\,}H_{k-1}}(H_{k})\\
\times e^{-i(1+t)H_{1}}\dots e^{-i(1+t)H_{k-1}}.\end{gathered}

Therefore, from (
10
) we get

\begin{gathered}\ket{\dot{\psi}(t)}=-i\mathcal{H}(t)\ket{\psi(t)},\end{gathered}

(11)

where

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{H}(t)=H_{1}+e^{-i(1+t)\operatorname{ad\,}H_{1}}(H_{2})+\dots{\\
}+e^{-i(1+t)\operatorname{ad\,}H_{1}}\dots e^{-i(1+t)\operatorname{ad\,}H_{N-1}}(H_{N}).\end{gathered}

We denote
\delta\mathcal{H}(t):=\mathcal{H}(t)-\mathcal{H}_{0}
and represent (
11
) as

\begin{gathered}\ket{\dot{\psi}(t)}=-i(\mathcal{H}_{0}+\delta\mathcal{H}(t))\ket{\psi(t)},\end{gathered}

Integrating from
0
to
\varepsilon
, we get

\begin{gathered}\ket{\psi(\varepsilon)}=\ket{\widehat{\psi}}-i\int\limits_{0}^{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{H}_{0}+\delta\mathcal{H}(t))\ket{\psi(t)}\,dt.\end{gathered}

Hence, we find the estimate

\begin{gathered}\|\ket{\psi(\varepsilon)}-\ket{\widehat{\psi}}\|\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\overline{\varepsilon}}(\|\mathcal{H}_{0}\|_{2}+\|\delta\mathcal{H}(t)\|_{2})\|\ket{\psi(t)}\|\,dt\\
=\int\limits_{0}^{\overline{\varepsilon}}(\|\mathcal{H}_{0}\|_{2}+\|\delta\mathcal{H}(t)\|_{2})\,dt=\|\mathcal{H}_{0}\|_{2}\overline{\varepsilon}+\int\limits_{0}^{\overline{\varepsilon}}\|\delta\mathcal{H}(t)\|_{2}\,dt.\end{gathered}

(12)

To estimate
\delta\mathcal{H}(t)
, we represent it in the form

\begin{gathered}\delta\mathcal{H}(t)=(e^{-i(1+t)\operatorname{ad\,}H_{1}}(H_{2})-e^{-i\operatorname{ad\,}H_{1}}(H_{2}))\\
+\dots+(e^{-i(1+t)\operatorname{ad\,}H_{1}}\dots e^{-i(1+t)\operatorname{ad\,}H_{N-1}}(H_{N})\\
-e^{-i\operatorname{ad\,}H_{1}}\dots e^{-i\operatorname{ad\,}H_{N-1}}(H_{N})).\end{gathered}

We estimate the norm of the expression in the first bracket

\begin{gathered}\|e^{-i(1+t)\operatorname{ad\,}H_{1}}(H_{2})-e^{-i\operatorname{ad\,}H_{1}}(H_{2})\|_{2}\\
=\|e^{-i\operatorname{ad\,}H_{1}}(e^{-it\operatorname{ad\,}H_{1}}(H_{2})-H_{2})\|_{2}\\
=\|e^{-it\operatorname{ad\,}H_{1}}(H_{2})-H_{2}\|_{2}\\
\leq\sum\limits_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{|t|^{p}}{p!}\|(\operatorname{ad\,}H_{1})^{p}(H_{2})\|_{2}=\sum\limits_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{|t|^{p}\eta_{2p}}{p!}.\end{gathered}

It follows from the inequality (
22
) (see Appendix B) that

\begin{gathered}\|e^{-i(1+t)\operatorname{ad\,}H_{1}}\dots e^{-i(1+t)\operatorname{ad\,}H_{k-1}}(H_{k})\\
-e^{-i\operatorname{ad\,}H_{1}}\dots e^{-i\operatorname{ad\,}H_{k-1}}(H_{k})\|_{2}\leq\sum\limits_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{|t|^{p}\eta_{kp}}{p!}.\end{gathered}

Thus,

\begin{gathered}\int\limits_{0}^{\overline{\varepsilon}}\|\delta\mathcal{H}(t)\|_{2}\,dt\leq\int\limits_{0}^{\overline{\varepsilon}}\sum\limits_{k=2}^{N}\sum\limits_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{|t|^{p}\eta_{kp}}{p!}\,dt=\sum\limits_{k=2}^{N}\sum\limits_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{\overline{\varepsilon}^{p+1}\eta_{kp}}{(p+1)!}.\end{gathered}

From (
12
), we find

\begin{gathered}\|\ket{\psi(\varepsilon)}-\ket{\widehat{\psi}}\|\leq\Big(\|\mathcal{H}_{0}\|_{2}+\sum\limits_{k=2}^{N}\sum\limits_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{\eta_{kp}\overline{\varepsilon}^{p}}{(p+1)!}\Big)\overline{\varepsilon}\\
=\sqrt{2M(\overline{\varepsilon})}.\end{gathered}

Therefore,

\begin{gathered}2M(\overline{\varepsilon})\geq\|\ket{\psi(\varepsilon)}-\ket{\widehat{\psi}}\|^{2}\\
=2-2\operatorname{Re\,}\braket{\psi(\varepsilon)|\widehat{\psi}}\geq 2-2|\braket{\psi(\varepsilon)|\widehat{\psi}}|,\end{gathered}

which completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
. The theorem is proved.

Remark.
The main difficulty in applying Theorem
4.2
to the perturbed state (
5
) is the calculation of the constant
h_{0}
which is reduced to solving the optimization problem

\begin{gathered}\max\limits_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,\|\lambda\|_{\infty}=1}\|\lambda_{1}A_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{N}A_{N}\|_{2}.\end{gathered}

For
N=2
this problem can be solved by elementary means regardless of the dimensions of the matrices (see Appendix C). For the case of an arbitrary number of matrices, the solution to this problem is comprehensible to modern mathematical software, at least for matrices of small dimension. For high-dimensional matrices, this optimization problem is possibly an open problem requiring further investigation.

7
Discussion of results

We note that from the triangle inequality it follows that for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\|\lambda\|_{\infty}=1
, the inequality

\|\lambda_{1}A_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{N}A_{N}\|_{2}\leq\|H_{1}\|_{2}+\dots+\|H_{N}\|_{2}

holds. Thus,

h_{0}\leq\|H_{1}\|_{2}+\dots+\|H_{N}\|_{2}.

(20)

We assume that there is a strict inequality in (
20
) and at least one of the coefficients
\eta_{kp}
is not equal to zero. The result we presented in Theorem
4.2
will be better than the main result from
Berberich et al. 2023
if inequality
M_{\eqref{5}}(\overline{\varepsilon})<M_{\eqref{Jul}}(\overline{\varepsilon})
is satisfied. In this case,
\overline{\varepsilon}\in(0,\overline{\varepsilon}_{\max})
, where
\overline{\varepsilon}_{\max}
is the only positive root of the equation

\overline{\varepsilon}_{\max}\sum\limits_{k=2}^{N}\sum\limits_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{\eta_{kp}(\overline{\varepsilon}_{\max})^{p-1}}{(p+1)!}=\|H_{1}\|_{2}+\dots+\|H_{N}\|_{2}-h_{0}.

If the Hamiltonians
H_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,N
form a commuting family, then all constants
\eta_{kp}=0
. In this case, the result of Theorem
4.2
is always better than the main result from
Berberich et al. 2023
, regardless of
\overline{\varepsilon}
. It is intuitively clear that for families of Hamiltonians close to commuting ones, our proposed approach leads to less conservative estimates of the errors of coherent states.

This is the main conclusion of our work. The practical significance of this conclusion for the design of quantum computing devices is an open problem requiring further research.

In the case when there is an equality in (
20
), we do not arrive at a better result than in
Berberich et al. 2023
.

It is also of interest for further research to expand our results for incoherent perturbations of a quantum mechanical system.

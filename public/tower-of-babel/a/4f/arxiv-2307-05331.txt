Application of MUSIC-type imaging for anomaly detection without background information
Won-Kwang Park

Abstract
It has been demonstrated that the MUltiple SIgnal Classification (MUSIC) algorithm is fast, stable, and effective for localizing small anomalies in microwave imaging. For the successful application of MUSIC, exact values of permittivity, conductivity, and permeability of the background must be known. If one of these values is unknown, it will fail to identify the location of an anomaly. However, to the best of our knowledge, no explanation of this failure has been provided yet. In this paper, we consider the application of MUSIC to the localization of a small anomaly from scattering parameter data when complete information of the background is not available. Thanks to the framework of the integral equation formulation for the scattering parameter data, an analytical expression of the MUSIC-type imaging function in terms of the infinite series of Bessel functions of integer order is derived. Based on the theoretical result, we confirm that the identification of a small anomaly is significantly affected by the applied values of permittivity and conductivity. However, fortunately, it is possible to recognize the anomaly if the applied value of conductivity is small. Simulation results with synthetic data are reported to demonstrate the theoretical result.

Several studies have demonstrated that the MUSIC algorithm is fast, effective, and stable in both the inverse scattering problem and microwave imaging. However, for its successful application, one must ① discriminate nonzero singular values to determine the exact noise subspace and ② know a priori information of the background (exact values of background permittivity, conductivity, and permeability at a given frequency) to design an imaging function. Several studies

This provides the motivation for this study aimed at determining the effect of an applied inaccurate value of background permeability, permittivity, or conductivity.

The purpose of this paper is to establish a new mathematical theory of MUSIC in microwave imaging when the background information is unknown. To this end, we carefully explore the structure of MUSIC imaging function by constructing a relationship with infinite series of Bessel functions of integer order, antenna arrangement, and an applied inaccurate value of background wavenumber. This is based on the integral equation formula for the scattered-field
S-
parameter in the presence of a small anomaly and the structure of left-singular vector associated with the nonzero singular value of the scattering matrix. From the explored structure, we can explain that ① when an inaccurate value of background permeability or permittivity is applied, the identified location of the small anomaly is shifted in a specific direction, ② when an inaccurate value of background conductivity is applied, there is no shifting effect and it is possible to identify the location fairly precisely if the applied value is small, ③ however it will be very difficult to recognize the existence of an anomaly if the applied value of conductivity is not small. To validate the theoretical results, various simulation results in the presence of single and multiple anomalies are presented.

(2)

) to design MUSIC algorithm. Since the condition (

\mathbb{K}=\begin{bmatrix}0&S_{\scat}(1,2)&\cdots&S_{\scat}(1,N)\\
S_{\scat}(2,1)&0&\cdots&S_{\scat}(2,N)\\

S_{\scat}(N,1)&S_{\scat}(N,2)&\cdots&0\end{bmatrix}=\mathbb{UDV}^{*}\approx\tau_{1}{\mathbf{U}}_{1}{\mathbf{V}}_{1}^{*},

denotes the nonzero singular value, and

]
why the diagonal elements of

\mathbb{P}_{\noise}=\mathbb{I}-{\mathbf{U}}_{1}{\mathbf{U}}_{1}^{*}.

Then, based on the structure of the approximation (

{\mathbf{W}}(k_{\mathrm{bw}},{\mathbf{r}})=\frac{{\mathbf{f}}(k_{\mathrm{bw}},{\mathbf{r}})}{|{\mathbf{f}}(k_{\mathrm{bw}},{\mathbf{r}})|},\quad\text{where}\quad{\mathbf{f}}(k_{\mathrm{bw}},{\mathbf{r}})=\Big[u(k_{\mathrm{bw}},{\mathbf{a}}_{1},{\mathbf{r}}_{\star}),u(k_{\mathrm{bw}},{\mathbf{a}}_{2},{\mathbf{r}}_{\star}),\ldots,u(k_{\mathrm{bw}},{\mathbf{a}}_{N},{\mathbf{r}}_{\star})\Big]^{T}.

(4)

Then, since

we can examine that
|\mathbb{P}_{\noise}({\mathbf{W}}(k_{\mathrm{bw}},{\mathbf{r}}_{\star}))|=0
and by plotting the following imaging function of MUSIC

\mathfrak{F}(k_{\mathrm{bw}},{\mathbf{r}})=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{P}_{\noise}({\mathbf{W}}(k_{\mathrm{bw}},{\mathbf{r}}))|},\quad{\mathbf{r}}\in\Omega,

[

,

30

]

It is important that, to generate the test vector

, on the basis of (

must be known. This means that exact values of

, and

must be known. However, their exact values are sometimes unknown because these values are significantly dependent on the frequency, temperature, and other factors. Now, we assume that the exact values of

\mathfrak{F}(k_{\mathrm{aw}},{\mathbf{r}})=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{P}_{\noise}({\mathbf{W}}(k_{\mathrm{aw}},{\mathbf{r}}))|},\quad{\mathbf{r}}\in\Omega.

(5)

Notice that, the exact location of
D
cannot be retrieved through the map of
\mathfrak{F}(k_{\mathrm{aw}},{\mathbf{r}})
, but we can recognize the existence of an anomaly and the identified location is shifted in a specific direction. However, some phenomena exhibited in Section
4
cannot be explained yet.

\displaystyle=C(N-2)Ne^{ik_{\mathrm{bw}}{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}\cdot{\mathbf{r}}_{\star}}\Big(J_{0}(|k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}}-k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star}|)+\mathcal{E}(k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}},k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star})\Big).

Applying (
11
) again, we can easily obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}(\Psi_{1}+\overline{\Psi}_{1})=2(1-C)C(N-2)N\Big|J_{0}(|k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}}-k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star}|)+\mathcal{E}(k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}},k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star})\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Psi_{2}\overline{\Psi}_{2}=C^{2}(N-2)^{2}N^{2}\Big|J_{0}(|k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}}-k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star}|)+\mathcal{E}(k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}},k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star})\Big|^{2}.

Therefore,

|\mathbb{P}_{\noise}({\mathbf{W}}(k_{\mathrm{aw}},{\mathbf{r}}))|\approx\bigg((1-C)^{2}-2C(1-C)(N-2)N\Big|J_{0}(|k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}}-k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star}|)+\mathcal{E}(k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}},k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star})\Big|^{2}\\
+C^{2}(N-2)^{2}N^{2}\Big|J_{0}(|k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}}-k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star}|)+\mathcal{E}(k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}},k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star})\Big|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

Finally, since
|\mathbb{P}_{\noise}({\mathbf{W}}(k_{\mathrm{aw}},{\mathbf{r}}))|=0
and
|J_{0}(|k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}}-k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star}|)+\mathcal{E}(k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}},k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star})|=1
when
k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}}=k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star}
,

(1-C)^{2}-2C(1-C)(N-2)N+C^{2}(N-2)^{2}N^{2}=0

or equivalently,
\big((1-C)-C(N-2)N\big)^{2}=0
. Hence,
C(N-1)^{2}=1
and correspondingly,

|\mathbb{P}_{\noise}({\mathbf{W}}(k_{\mathrm{aw}},{\mathbf{r}}))|\approx|1-C|^{2}\left(1-\Big|J_{0}(|k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}}-k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star}|)+\mathcal{E}(k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}},k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star})\Big|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\
=\frac{N^{2}-2N}{N^{2}-2N+1}\bigg(1-\bigg|J_{0}(|k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}}-k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star}|)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}}i^{q}J_{q}(|k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}}-k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star}|)e^{iq(\theta_{n}-\phi)}\bigg|^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}.

With this, we can obtain the structure (
6
).
∎

Based on the identified structure
\mathfrak{F}(k_{\mathrm{aw}},{\mathbf{r}})
, we can say that the location
{\mathbf{r}}=(k_{\mathrm{bw}}/k_{\mathrm{aw}}){\mathbf{r}}_{\star}
will be identified instead of the true one
{\mathbf{r}}_{\star}
because
J_{0}(|k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}}-k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star}|)=1
and
\mathcal{E}({\mathbf{r}})=0
when
|k_{\mathrm{aw}}{\mathbf{r}}-k_{\mathrm{bw}}{\mathbf{r}}_{\star}|=0
. This is the reason why the identified location of the anomaly is shifted. Note that, if the anomaly is located at the origin, its location can be identified for any value
k_{\mathrm{aw}}
. Further properties will be discussed in the simulation results.

5
Conclusion

Based on the integral equation for the scattered-field
S-
parameter and singular value decomposition of the scattering matrix in the presence of a small anomaly, we showed that the imaging function of MUSIC can be expressed by an infinite series of Bessel functions and applied wavenumber. Thanks to the theoretical result, we confirmed why an inaccurate location of the anomaly was retrieved when inaccurate value of background permeability, permittivity, or conductivity. However, the relationship between the retrieved size of the anomaly and the applied wavenumber remains unknown. It will be interesting to investigate a mathematical theory to explain this phenomenon. Moreover, the development of an effective algorithm for estimating exact value of background wavenumber would be a valuable addition to this work.

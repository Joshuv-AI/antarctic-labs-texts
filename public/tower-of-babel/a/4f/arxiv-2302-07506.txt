Superradiant phase transition induced by the indirect Rabi interaction
Wen Huang, Ying Wu, Xin-You Lü

Abstract
We theoretically study the superradiant phase transition (SPT) in an indirect Rabi model, where both a two-level system and a single mode bosonic field couple to an auxiliary bosonic field. We find that the indirect spin-field coupling induced by the virtual excitation of the auxiliary field can allow the occurrence of a SPT at a critical point, and the influence of the so-called A 2 A^{2} term in the normal Rabi model is naturally avoided. In the large detuning regime, we present the analytical expression of quantum critical point in terms of the original system parameters. The critical atom-field coupling strength is tunable, which will loosen the conditions on realizing the SPT. Considering a hybrid magnon-cavity-qubit system, we predict the squeezed cat state of magnon generated with feasible experimental parameters, which has potential applications in quantum metrology and quantum information processing.

Here we propose to realize the SPT based on an indirect Rabi model and discuss the associated quantum criticality. The indirect Rabi model means that a two-level system indirectly couples to a bosonic field via an auxiliary mode, as shown in Fig.
1
(a). When the frequencies of the two-level system and the field mode are far detuned from the auxiliary mode frequency, an effective Rabi model depending on the original system parameters is obtained after eliminating the auxiliary mode. Using the diagonalization approach and order parameter analysis, we find that the second order quantum phase transition occurs without the requirement of the direct Rabi-type interaction. This SPT is naturally immune to the so-called
A^{2}
term appeared in the direct spin-field interactions, and it has a tunable critical coupling strength compared with the standard Rabi model. Our proposal is general, and it can be implemented in a hybrid magnon-cavity-qubit system. By presenting the Wigner function distribution in the phase-space, we clearly show the appearance of the squeezed cat state in the superradiant phase, which offers an alternative method for obtaining a magnon macroscopic quantum superposition. Our work might inspire the following study of the applications of indirect Rabi model in quantum precision measurement.

The occurrence of SPT

We now assume that the frequency

IV
Influence of the
A^{2}
term on SPT

As is well known, the SPT is challenged by the squared electromagnetic vector potential

(determined by the Thomas-Reiche-Kuhn sum rule). We have ignored the

VI
CONCLUSION

In this work, we have proposed a scheme to realize the SPT and the associated quantum superposition in a hybrid system described by an indirect Rabi model. This scheme is based on the effective Rabi interaction, between two uncoupled subsystems including the two-level emitter and the bosonic mode
b
, mediated by the virtual excitation of an auxiliary mode
a
. In the large detuning regime and classical oscillator limit
\omega_{q}/\omega_{b}\rightarrow\infty
, we give the analytical results of the ground state both in the NP and SP. In comparison with the standard Rabi model, the critical coupling for SPT becomes adjustable and the SPT still occurs in the presence of
A^{2}
term. Besides, we show that the cat state can be generated via indirect atom-field interaction. The state of the
b
mode collapses into a cat state by making projective measurements on the atom, and the resulting cat state may not be disturbed because there is no direct interaction between the atom and the
b
mode. When our proposal is implemented in the hybrid magnon-cavity-qubit systems, it provides a new method for realizing SPT and macroscopic quantum superposition of magnon
Sharma et al. 2021
;
Sun et al. 2021
;
Zhang et al. 2021b
;
Bamba et al. 2022
, which might expand the cavity magnonics domain and have potential applications in ultra-sensitive magnetic field detection.

APPENDIX A
THE EFFECTIVE HAMILTONIAN IN EQ. (2)

In order to get the effective coupling between the bosonic mode
b
and two-level system, we apply the Fröhlich-Nakajima transformation
Fröhlich 1950
;
Nakajima 1955
. We now rewrite the total Hamiltonian  (
1
) into two parts, one is the free Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\omega_{q}\sigma_{z}+\omega_{a}a^{\dagger}a+\omega_{b}b^{\dagger}b,

(A1)

and another one is the interaction Hamiltonian

H_{I}=g(a^{\dagger}+a)(\sigma_{+}+\sigma_{-})+J(a^{\dagger}+a)(b^{\dagger}+b).

(A2)

We further consider that the bosonic mode
b
and the two-level system are both coupled to the auxiliary mode
a
in the large detuning regime, i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{J}{\omega_{a}-\omega_{b}}\ll 1,\ \frac{g}{\omega_{a}-\omega_{q}}\ll 1.

(A3)

Then we can use the Fröhlich-Nakajima transformation
U_{V}=e^{V}
to derive the effective Hamiltonian. The operator
V
satisfies
H_{I}+[H_{0},V]=0
and takes the form

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{q}(a\sigma_{-}-a^{\dagger}\sigma_{+})+\nu_{q}(a\sigma_{+}-a^{\dagger}\sigma_{-})

(A4)

\displaystyle+\mu_{b}(ab-a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger})+\nu_{b}(ab^{\dagger}-a^{\dagger}b),

where
\mu_{q}=g/\eta_{q},\nu_{q}=g/\Delta_{q}
, and
\mu_{b}=J/\eta_{b},\nu_{b}=J/\Delta_{b}
and
\eta_{q(b)}=\omega_{a}+\omega_{q(b)},\Delta_{q(b)}=\omega_{a}-\omega_{q(b)}
. Applying the unitary transformation to
H
we obtain

H_{\text{eff}}=U_{V}^{\dagger}HU_{V}=H_{0}+\frac{1}{2}[H_{I},V]+\frac{1}{3}[[H_{I},V],V]+\cdots.

(A5)

Note that the coefficients
\mu_{q(b)}
and
\nu_{q(b)}
are small under the large detuning condition, and thus the effective Hamiltonian considered up to second order term
[H_{I},V]
remains valid with neglecting the higher-order terms. Moreover, we assume that the cavity mode is approximately in the vacuum state. The
b
mode can couple to the two-level system through virtual excitation of
a
mode, and thus we eliminate the degrees of freedom of
a
and obtain

\displaystyle H_{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{q}}{2}\sigma_{z}+\omega_{b}b^{\dagger}b-\chi(b^{\dagger}+b)(\sigma_{+}+\sigma_{-})

\displaystyle-\xi(b^{\dagger}+b)^{2}+C(a^{\dagger}+a)^{2}+F(a^{\dagger}+a)^{2}\sigma_{z},

where

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{gJ}{2}(\frac{1}{\Delta_{q}}+\frac{1}{\eta_{q}}+\frac{1}{\Delta_{b}}+\frac{1}{\eta_{b}}),

(A7)

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J^{2}}{2}(\frac{1}{\Delta_{b}}+\frac{1}{\eta_{b}}).

(A8)

Here, we can ignore the last two terms in Eq. (
C
), whose coefficients
C=\frac{J^{2}}{2}(\frac{1}{\Delta_{b}}-\frac{1}{\eta_{b}})
and
F=-\frac{g^{2}}{2}(\frac{1}{\Delta_{q}}-\frac{1}{\eta_{q}})
are much smaller than both the effective coupling
\chi
and
\xi
. Therefore, we obtain an effective Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{\text{eff}}=\frac{\omega_{q}}{2}\sigma_{z}+\omega_{b}b^{\dagger}b-\chi(b^{\dagger}+b)\sigma_{x}-\xi(b^{\dagger}+b)^{2},

(A9)

which is exactly Eq. (
2
) of the main text.

Considering the
A^{2}
term, we can obtain the effective Hamiltonian of the Hamiltonian Eq. (
7
) in the same way

\displaystyle H_{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{q}}{2}\sigma_{z}+\omega_{r}^{A}b^{\dagger}b-\chi_{r}^{A}(b^{\dagger}+b)\sigma_{x}

(A10)

where

\displaystyle\omega_{r}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{b}\text{exp}(-2r^{A}),

(A11)

\displaystyle\chi_{r}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{g}\bar{J}\text{exp}(r^{A})}{2}(\frac{1}{\bar{\Delta}_{q}}+\frac{1}{\bar{\eta}_{q}}+\frac{1}{\bar{\Delta}_{b}}+\frac{1}{\bar{\eta}_{b}}).

(A12)

where
\bar{\Delta}_{b(q)}=\bar{\omega}_{a}-\omega_{b(q)}
,
\bar{\eta}_{b(q)}=\bar{\omega}_{a}+\omega_{b(q)}
and
r^{A}=-(1/4)\text{ln}[1-2\bar{J}^{2}(\bar{\Delta}_{b}^{-1}+\bar{\eta}_{b}^{-1})/\omega_{b}]
.

APPENDIX B
DETAILS OF DIAGONALIZATION PROCEDURE

In this section, we show details of diagonalizing Hamiltonian (
4
) in the
\omega_{q}/\omega_{b}\rightarrow\infty
limit. Concretely, in the NP, performing a Schrieffer-Wolff transformation
U=\text{exp}(S)
on the Hamiltonian
H_{r}
in Eq. (
4

\displaystyle H_{r}^{\prime}=U^{\dagger}H_{r}U=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{[H_{r},S]^{(k)}}{k!},

(B1)

where
S=\text{exp}[-i\chi_{r}/\omega_{q}\left(b^{\dagger}+b\right)\sigma_{y}]
, and
[H_{r},S]^{(k)}\equiv[[H_{r},S]^{(k-1)},S]
with
[H_{r},S]^{(0)}=H_{r}
. Expanding Eq. (
B1
) up to the second order in
\chi^{\prime}_{r}=2\chi_{r}/\sqrt{\omega_{r}\omega_{q}}
, the transformed Hamiltonian becomes

\displaystyle H_{r}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{q}}{2}\sigma_{z}+\omega_{r}b^{\dagger}b+\frac{{\chi^{\prime}_{r}}^{2}\omega_{r}}{4}(b^{\dagger}+b)^{2}\sigma_{z}

(B2)

\displaystyle+C_{r}+\frac{{\chi^{\prime}_{r}}^{2}\omega_{r}^{2}}{4\omega_{q}}+\mathcal{O}(\frac{{\chi^{\prime}_{r}}^{4}\omega_{r}^{2}}{16\omega_{q}^{2}}).

In the limit
\omega_{q}/\omega_{r}\rightarrow\infty
, the constant
{\chi^{\prime}_{r}}^{2}\omega_{r}^{2}/(4\omega_{q})
and high-order terms can be neglected. Considering that the transformed Hamiltonian has spin-up and spin-down subspaces decoupled with each other, the low-energy effective Hamiltonian can be obtained by projecting
H_{r}^{\prime}
into spin-down subspace, taking the form as

\displaystyle H_{\text{np}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{r}b^{\dagger}b-\frac{{\chi^{\prime}_{r}}^{2}\omega_{r}}{4}(b^{\dagger}+b)^{2}-\frac{\omega_{q}}{2}+C_{r}.

(B3)

Then, we can use a squeezing transformation
S_{b}(r_{\text{np}})=\text{exp}[\frac{r_{\text{np}}}{2}((b^{\dagger})^{2}-b^{2})]
with
r_{\text{np}}=-(1/4)\text{ln}\left[1-4\chi_{r}^{2}/(\omega_{q}\omega_{r})\right]
, to diagonalize this Hamiltonian in the form as

\displaystyle H_{\text{np}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\omega_{r}^{2}-4\chi_{r}^{2}\omega_{r}/\omega_{q}}b^{\dagger}b-\frac{\omega_{q}}{2}+C_{r}

(B4)

\displaystyle+\frac{\sqrt{\omega_{r}^{2}-4\chi_{r}^{2}\omega_{r}/\omega_{q}}-\omega_{r}}{2},

where the ground-state energy is

\displaystyle E_{g}=\frac{\sqrt{\omega_{r}^{2}-4\chi_{r}^{2}\omega_{r}/\omega_{q}}-\omega_{r}}{2}-\frac{\omega_{q}}{2}+C_{r},

(B5)

and the excitation energy is

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\text{np}}=\sqrt{\omega_{r}^{2}-4\chi_{r}^{2}\omega_{r}/\omega_{q}}.

(B6)

And the ground state of the system is
|\phi_{\text{np}}\rangle_{G}=S_{b}(r+r_{\text{np}})|0\rangle_{b}|\!\downarrow\rangle
with
S_{b}(r+r_{\text{np}})=\text{exp}[\frac{r+r_{\text{np}}}{2}((b^{\dagger})^{2}-b^{2})]
possessing even parity, which is confirmed by the total excitation number
0
. A critical coupling value
g_{c}
can be derived from the vanishing of excitation energy (i.e.,
\epsilon_{\text{np}}=0
), and we obtain

\displaystyle g_{c}=\frac{\sqrt{\omega_{q}(\omega_{b}-2J^{2}(\Delta_{b}^{-1}+\eta_{b}^{-1}))}}{J(\Delta_{b}^{-1}+\eta_{b}^{-1}+\Delta_{q}^{-1}+\eta_{q}^{-1})}.

(B7)

Having introduced two dimensionless parameters:
\tilde{g}=2g/\sqrt{\omega_{a}\omega_{q}}
and
\tilde{J}=2J/\sqrt{\omega_{a}\omega_{b}}
. The critical condition can be changed into the form independent of frequencies

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{c}\approx\frac{\sqrt{1-\tilde{J}^{2}}}{\tilde{J}}.

(B8)

The excitation energy
\epsilon_{\text{np}}
is real for
\tilde{g}\leq\tilde{g}_{c}
and zero at
\tilde{g}=\tilde{g}_{c}

When
\tilde{g}>\tilde{g}_{c}
, the system transitions to the SP, in which the oscillator
b
is macroscopically populated. Thus, we first apply a displacement operator
D(\alpha)=\text{exp}[\alpha(b^{\dagger}-b)]
with
\alpha=\pm\sqrt{\frac{\omega_{q}}{4\omega_{r}}\left({\chi^{\prime}_{r}}^{2}-{\chi^{\prime}_{r}}^{-2}\right)}
upon the Hamiltonian (
4
) in the main text, and we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{r}b^{\dagger}b+\frac{\tilde{\omega}_{q}}{2}\tau_{z}-\tilde{\chi}_{r}\left(b^{\dagger}+b\right)\tau_{x}+\omega_{r}\alpha^{2}+C_{r},

where the renormalized frequency
\tilde{\omega}_{q}=4\chi_{r}^{2}/\omega_{r}
and the renormalized coupling strength
\tilde{\chi}_{r}=\frac{1}{4}\omega_{r}\omega_{q}/\chi_{r}
. Here,
\tau_{z}
and
\tau_{x}

\displaystyle=\text{cos}(\theta)|\!\uparrow\rangle+\text{sin}(\theta)|\!\downarrow\rangle,

(B10)

\displaystyle|\tilde{\downarrow}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\text{sin}(\theta)|\!\uparrow\rangle+\text{cos}(\theta)|\!\downarrow\rangle,

(B11)

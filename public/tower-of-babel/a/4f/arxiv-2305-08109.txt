Higher Berry curvature from matrix product states Preprint: YITP-23-52
Ken Shiozaki, Niclas Heinsdorf, Shuhei Ohyama

Abstract
The higher Berry curvature was introduced by Kapustin and Spodyneiko as an extension of the Berry curvature in quantum mechanical systems with finite degrees of freedom to quantum many-body systems in finite spatial dimensions. In this paper, we propose an alternative formulation of the higher Berry curvature using translationally invariant matrix product states. They are the ground states of a set of gapped Hamiltonians which are evolved adiabatically through a discretized parameter space. Because matrix product states transform under a projective representation, evaluating the Berry curvature on a closed loop through parameter space is not sufficient to fix all the gauge degrees of freedom. To obtain a gauge-invariant real quantity, the higher-dimensional Berry curvature is evaluated on small tetrahedra in parameter space. Our numerical calculations confirm that the higher Berry curvature varies continuously throughout an adiabatic evolution and becomes quantized over a closed 3-dimensional parameter space.

Introduction

In a quantum mechanical system with
N
-degrees of freedom, a normalized pure state
\ket{\psi}\in\mathbb{C}^{N},\braket{\psi|\psi}=1

;

In this paper, we discuss an extension of the established Berry phase

Instead, Kapustin and Spodyneiko have proposed the higher Berry curvatures that take values in

The higher Berry curvature proposed by Kapustin and Spodyneiko is defined for any unique gapped ground state.

In this study, as one of the attempts to formulate the higher Berry curvature in one-dimensional systems with translational symmetry, we discuss an approach using the matrix product state (MPS) representation

In particular, towards applications in numerical calculations, we develop a formulation for higher Berry curvature in cases where the bond dimension of MPS varies in the parameter space.
We will see that weighting by Schmidt eigenvalues plays an important role.
We also present numerical results obtained using infinite density matrix renormalization group (iDMRG).

During the preparation of this paper,

II
Some background

In this section, we provide the reader with some background that motivated this study.

A conjecture on the topological structure of the space of invertible states has been proposed by Kitaev

Kitaev 2013

-dimensions.

\displaystyle F_{d}

\displaystyle\cong\Omega F_{d+1}

\displaystyle=\{\ell:S^{1}\to F_{d+1}|\ell(0)=\ell(1)=*\}.

(4)

\displaystyle=e^{i\theta_{\alpha\beta}(x)}e^{i\theta_{\beta\gamma}(x)}e^{i\theta_{\gamma\alpha}(x)}

\displaystyle\quad V_{\alpha\beta}(x)V_{\beta\gamma}(x)V_{\gamma\alpha}(x)A^{i}_{\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\quad V^{\dagger}_{\gamma\alpha}(x)V^{\dagger}_{\beta\gamma}(x)V^{\dagger}_{\alpha\beta}(x)

(20)

is obtained.
Again, from the uniqueness of the largest eigenvalue and corresponding eigenvector,

\displaystyle e^{i\theta_{\alpha\beta}(x)}e^{i\theta_{\beta\gamma}(x)}=e^{i\theta_{\alpha\gamma}(x)},

(21)

and there exists
e^{i\phi_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(x)}\in U(1)
such that

\displaystyle V_{\alpha\beta}(x)V_{\beta\gamma}(x)V_{\gamma\alpha}(x)=e^{i\phi_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(x)}\times 1_{D}

(22)

holds.
Eq.(
21
) means that
\{e^{i\theta_{\alpha\beta}(x)}\}_{\alpha\beta}
is a Čech 1-cocycle that defines a
U(1)
bundle, but it is a “weak” object related to the zero-dimensional state originating from translational symmetry, and will not be discussed further in this paper.

Here, from the normalization condition (
6
) on
\Lambda
s, the
U(1)
phase
e^{i\phi_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(x)}
is given by
Ohyama and Ryu 2023

\displaystyle e^{i\phi_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(x)}={\rm tr\,}[\Lambda^{2}_{\alpha}(x)V_{\alpha\beta}(x)V_{\beta\gamma}(x)V_{\gamma\alpha}(x)]

(23)

Thus, only the dominant Schmidt eigenvalues contribute the
U(1)
phase
e^{i\phi_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(x)}
.
An equivalent expression that is symmetric is

\displaystyle e^{i\phi_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(x)}

\displaystyle={\rm tr\,}\big[\Lambda^{\frac{2}{3}}_{\alpha}(x)V_{\alpha\beta}(x)

\displaystyle\quad\Lambda^{\frac{2}{3}}_{\beta}(x)V_{\beta\gamma}(x)\Lambda^{\frac{2}{3}}_{\gamma}(x)V_{\gamma\alpha}(x)\big].

(24)

For each patch
U_{\alpha}
, the gauge transformation

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}(x)\mapsto e^{i\chi_{\alpha}(x)}W_{\alpha}(x)A_{\alpha}(x)W^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x),

(25)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\alpha}(x)\mapsto W_{\alpha}(x)\Lambda_{\alpha}(x)W^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x),

(26)

with
x\in U_{\alpha}
, induces the following gauge transformation for the transition function
V_{\alpha\beta}(x)
.

\displaystyle V_{\alpha\beta}(x)\mapsto W_{\alpha}(x)V_{\alpha\beta}(x)W^{\dagger}_{\beta}(x),\quad x\in U_{\alpha\beta}.

(27)

Note that the
U(1)
phase
e^{i\phi_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(x)}
is gauge invariant under the gauge transformations (
26
) and (
27
).

For the four patch intersection
U_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
, the 2-cocycle condition

\displaystyle e^{i\phi_{\beta\gamma\delta}(x)}e^{-i\phi_{\alpha\gamma\delta}(x)}e^{i\phi_{\alpha\beta\delta}(x)}e^{-i\phi_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(x)}=1

(28)

follows from the two different decomposition of
V_{\alpha\beta}(x)V_{\beta\gamma}(x)V_{\gamma\delta}(x)
. Therefore,
\{e^{i\phi_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(x)}\}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
is a Čech 2-cocycle.
Moreover, the phase change of
V_{\alpha\beta}(x)
,
V_{\alpha\beta}(x)\mapsto V_{\alpha\beta}(x)e^{i\xi_{\alpha\beta}(x)}
, induces an equivalent relation

\displaystyle e^{i\phi_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(x)}\sim e^{i\phi_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(x)}\times e^{i\xi_{\beta\gamma}(x)}e^{-i\xi_{\alpha\gamma}(x)}e^{i\xi_{\alpha\beta}(x)}.

(29)

Thus, the equivalence class
[e^{i\phi_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(x)}]
determines an element of the Čech cohomology
\check{H}^{2}(M,\underline{U(1)})
, where
\underline{U(1)}
is the sheaf of continuous
U(1)
-valued functions on the parameter space
M
.
Furthermore, fix a lift

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\ni\phi_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(x)\to\tilde{\phi}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(x)\in\mathbb{R}

(30)

and take the differential

\displaystyle c_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=\frac{1}{2\pi}(\delta\tilde{\phi}(x))_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left\{\tilde{\phi}_{\beta\gamma\delta}(x)-\tilde{\phi}_{\alpha\gamma\delta}(x)+\tilde{\phi}_{\alpha\beta\delta}(x)-\tilde{\phi}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(x)\right\}.

(31)

Since
\delta c=0
and, due to the 2-cocycle condition (
28
),
c_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
is a constant integer over
U_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
,
\{c_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\}_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
is a Čech 3-cocycle with integer coefficients.
Also, the change of the lift (
30
) induces an equivalence relation
c\sim c+\delta b
with
b\in\check{C}^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
, so eventually
c_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
determines an element of the third Čech cohomology group
\check{H}^{3}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.
This is nothing but a explicit construction of the isomorphism
\check{H}^{2}(M,\underline{U(1)})\cong\check{H}^{3}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.

Note that when the bond dimension
D
is constant over the whole parameter space
M
, it is shown that the element
[c]
takes values in the
D
-torsion subgroup as follows
Grothendieck 1966
.
Due to the
U(1)
ambiguity, the transition function
V_{\alpha\beta}(x)
can be chosen such that
\det V_{\alpha\beta}(x)=1
, i.e., it takes values in the
SU(D)
group.
In this case, the ambiguity of the matrix
V_{\alpha\beta}(x)
is discrete, given by the
D
-th roots of unity,
\mathbb{Z}_{D}=\{e^{\frac{2\pi ip}{D}}\in U(1)|p=0,\dots,D-1\}
, and thus the 2-cocycle
e^{i\phi_{\alpha\beta\gamma}(x)}
is independent of
x
and determines an element of the cohomology group
\check{H}^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z}_{D})
.
In this case, the 3-cocycle
c
defined by (
31
) satisfies that
Dc=\delta b
for some
b
, and thus the 3-cocycle
c
takes values in the
D
-torsion subgroup
\{[c]\in\check{H}^{3}(M,\mathbb{Z})|D[c]=0\}
.

IV
Construction of higher Berry phase

In the previous section, we saw that a family of MPS with a constant bond dimension cannot realize the free part of the cohomology group
H^{3}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.
However, the bond dimension is a non-physical parameter as it is introduced for approximating quantum states, and in general the bond dimension varies throughout the parameter space
M
.
This section considers the case where the bond dimension
D_{x}
varies depending on the lattice point
x
in a discretely approximated parameter space, with numerical computation of higher Berry curvature in mind.
First, we introduce the mixed transfer matrix between two physically different, i.e. gauge-inequivalent MPSs.

IV.1
Mixed transfer matrix

Let
\{A^{i}_{0}\}_{i}
and
\{A^{i}_{1}\}_{i}
be a
D_{0}
-MPS and
D_{1}
-MPS, respectively, both injective and satisfying the canonical conditions (
5
) and (
7
).
Denote the positive definite diagonal matrices in the left-canonical form (
7
) as
\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{1}
.
We introduce the mixed transfer matrix
T_{01}\in{\rm End}({\rm Mat}_{D_{0}\times D_{1}}(\mathbb{C}))
in the same way as (
14
):

\displaystyle T_{01}(X):=\sum_{i=1}^{d}A^{i}_{0}XA^{i{\dagger}}_{1}.

(32)

If the
D_{0}
-MPS
\{A^{i}_{0}\}_{i}
and
D_{1}
-MPS
\{A^{i}_{1}\}_{i}
are close to each other as physical states, the spectrum of the mixed transfer matrix
T_{01}
is expected to show little variation compared to the transfer matrix between gauge-equivalent MPS.
In particular, (i) the largest eigenvalue
\eta_{01}
of
T_{01}
in modulus is unique, its eigenspace is one-dimensional, and (ii) there exists a finite
|\delta\eta|>0
such that
|\eta|<|\eta_{01}|-|\delta\eta|
holds for other eigenvalues
\eta
.
We define that the MPS of
\{A_{0}^{i}\}_{i}
and
\{A_{1}^{i}\}_{i}
are close to each other as satisfying (i) and (ii).

If
\{A_{0}^{i}\}_{i}
and
\{A_{1}^{i}\}_{i}
belong to different symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phases, the eigenvalues of the mixed transfer matrix
T_{01}
are necessarily degenerate in modulus.
For more details, see Appendix
A
.
The uniqueness of the largest eigenvalue of the mixed transfer matrix in modulus is a necessary condition for the two MPSs,
\{A_{0}^{i}\}_{i}
and
\{A_{1}^{i}\}_{i}
, to belong to the same SPT phase for the symmetry group
G
.

Assuming that the two MPSs
\{A^{i}_{0}\}_{i}

\{A^{i}_{1}\}_{i}
are close, let the eigenvector corresponding to the largest eigenvalue of the mixed transfer matrix

be

\displaystyle T_{01}(V_{01})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}A_{0}^{i}V_{01}A_{1}^{i{\dagger}}=\eta_{01}V_{01},

\displaystyle|\eta_{01}|={\rm spr}(T_{01}).

(33)

See Fig.

(a) for the diagrammatic expression.
From the mixed transfer matrix (

The Hermitian conjugate of (
33

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}A_{1}^{i}V_{01}^{\dagger}A_{0}^{i{\dagger}}=\eta_{01}^{*}V_{01}^{\dagger},

(34)

which implies that, up to a constant factor,

From now on, we always assume that

\displaystyle V_{10}=V_{01}^{\dagger}.

(35)

There are two types of gauge transformations in the matrix

determined by (

The first one is the gauge transformation of the MPS,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n}\mapsto W_{n}\Lambda_{n}W_{n}^{\dagger},\quad V_{01}\mapsto W_{0}V_{01}W^{\dagger}_{1}.

(36)

The second one is the ambiguity of the constant factor of

itself,

\displaystyle V_{01}\mapsto z_{01}V_{01},\quad z_{01}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

(37)

The higher Berry curvature must be constructed to be gauge-invariant under these two gauge transformations (

37

Roughly speaking, the inner product
\braket{\psi_{0}|\psi_{1}}
in the expression of the Berry phase (

with Schmidt eigenvalues

must be properly set.
Focusing on this similarity, we formulate the higher Berry phase in the following section.

IV.2
Higher Berry connection

Consider a family of MPS on a parameter space
M
.
Take a discrete approximation
|M|
of
M
by triangulation and fix the orientations of the triangles.
For any given triangle, two edges will have orientations opposite to the third edge.
We denote the set of vertices by
S_{v}
.
The bond dimension
D_{p}
of the MPS can vary depending on the vertices
p\in S_{v}
.
At each vertex
p\in S_{v}
, we have an injective and canonical MPS
\{A^{i}_{p}\}_{i}
.
Denote the positive definite diagonal matrices in the left-canonical condition (
7
) as
\Lambda_{p}
.

For any edge
(p_{0}p_{1})
, the
D_{p_{0}}
-MPS
\{A_{p_{0}}^{i}\}_{i}
and the
D_{p_{1}}
-MPS
\{A_{p_{1}}^{i}\}_{i}
are assumed to be uniformly close in the sense of the previous section.
That is, the largest eigenvalue
|\eta_{p_{0}p_{1}}|={\rm spr}(T_{p_{0}p_{1}})
of the mixed transfer matrix
T_{p_{0}p_{1}}
defined by (
32
) is unique in modulus, and there exists a finite gap
|\delta\eta|
independent of the vertex
p\in S_{v}
such that for any other eigenvalue
\eta
satisfies
|\eta|<|\eta_{p_{0}p_{1}}|-|\delta\eta|
for any edges.
In particular, any two points on the parameter space
|M|
are connected without phase transitions.

Introduce the following quantity, which takes values in
U(1)
, corresponding to the discrete approximation of (
24
) for any triangle
\Delta^{2}=(p_{0}p_{1}p_{2})
in the triangulation
|M|
:

\displaystyle\phi(p_{0}p_{1}p_{2})

\displaystyle:={\rm Arg}\ {\rm tr\,}[\Lambda_{p_{0}}^{\frac{2}{3}}V_{p_{0}p_{1}}\Lambda_{p_{1}}^{\frac{2}{3}}V_{p_{1}p_{2}}\Lambda_{p_{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}V_{p_{2}p_{0}}]

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}.

(38)

See Fig.
1
(b) for the diagrammatic expression.
Clearly,
\phi(p_{0}p_{1}p_{2})
is invariant under the first gauge transformation (
36
).

Invariance under the second gauge transformation (
37
) is obtained by summing up the phases
\phi(\Delta^{2})
for all triangles
\Delta^{2}\in|\Sigma_{2}|
on the two-dimensional closed surface
|\Sigma_{2}|
in
|M|
.
Define the higher Berry phase on
|\Sigma_{2}|
as

\displaystyle\gamma(|\Sigma_{2}|):=\sum_{\Delta^{2}\in|\Sigma_{2}|}\sigma(\Delta^{2})\phi(\Delta^{2})\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z},

(39)

where the sign factor
\sigma(\Delta^{2})\in\{\pm 1\}
is defined as
\sigma(\Delta^{2})=1
when the orientation of
\Delta^{2}
agrees with that of
\Sigma_{2}
, and
\sigma(\Delta^{2})=-1
when they do not agree.
With the constraint (
35
), the second gauge transformation (
37
) cancels the contributions from adjacent triangles, making
\gamma(|\Sigma_{2}|)
a gauge-invariant quantity.
The quantity
\sigma(\Delta^{2})\phi(\Delta^{2})
can be regarded as a discrete approximation of the integral of the higher Berry connection, which is a 2-form, over a small triangle
\Delta^{2}
.

Note that the expression

\displaystyle{\rm Arg}\ {\rm tr\,}[\Lambda_{p_{0}}^{2}V_{p_{0}p_{1}}V_{p_{1}p_{2}}V_{p_{2}p_{0}}]

(40)

corresponding to the discrete approximation of (
23
) can also be considered as a candidate for the
U(1)
-valued
\phi(p_{0}p_{1}p_{2})
.
However, it turns out that the expression (
40
) is inappropriate, as the higher Berry curvature introduced in the next section depends on the bond dimension
D_{p}
.
Nevertheless, the sum of the higher Berry curvatures defined in (40) is always quantized.

IV.3
Higer Berry curvature

For a tetrahedron
\Delta^{3}=(p_{0}p_{1}p_{2}p_{3})
in
|M|
, the higher Berry phase (
39
) on its boundary
\partial\Delta^{3}=(p_{1}p_{2}p_{3})-(p_{0}p_{2}p_{3})+(p_{0}p_{1}p_{3})-(p_{0}p_{1}p_{2})
determines a gauge-invariant quantity. We consider this as the discrete approximation of the higher Berry curvature in the tetrahedron
\Delta^{3}
:

\displaystyle F(\Delta^{3})

\displaystyle:=\gamma(\partial\Delta^{3})

\displaystyle=\phi(p_{1}p_{2}p_{3})-\phi(p_{0}p_{2}p_{3})

\displaystyle+\phi(p_{0}p_{1}p_{3})-\phi(p_{0}p_{1}p_{2}).

(41)

See Fig.
1
(c).
Although
F(\Delta^{3})
is originally valued in
\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
, when the four points
p_{0}p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}
are sufficiently close,
F(\Delta^{3})
is close to 0 as an element of
\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
, so the
2\pi
ambiguity does not exist, and
F(\Delta^{3})
can be considered as a real number.
By design, the sum of the curvatures
F(\Delta^{3})
over an orientable 3-dimensional closed manifold
|\Sigma_{3}|
in
|M|
is quantized:

\displaystyle\nu(|\Sigma_{3}|):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sigma(\Delta^{3})\sum_{\Delta^{3}\in|\Sigma_{3}|}F(\Delta^{3})\in\mathbb{Z}.

(42)

Here, the sign
\sigma(\Delta^{3})\in\{\pm 1\}
takes the value
1
if the orientation of the tetrahedron
\Delta^{3}
coincides with that of
\Sigma_{3}
, and
-1
otherwise.
The integer value
\nu(|\Sigma_{3}|)
can be thought of as characterizing the integer part of the 3rd cohomology
H^{3}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.
We calculate
\nu(|\Sigma_{3}|)
for a specific example in the next section.

V.1
Model

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we discussed a formulation of the higher Berry curvature in one-dimensional quantum many-body systems using translationally invariant MPS.
Based on the fact that a
U(1)
function is defined in the triple patch intersection due to the gauge freedom of MPS
Ohyama and Ryu 2023
, we introduced a discrete approximation of the higher Berry connection (
38
) using the eigenvectors of the mixed transfer matrices for edges of the discretized parameter space.
Thanks to the weighting by Schmidt eigenvalues, the higher Berry connection (
38
) is free to the bond dimension.
We defined the higher Berry phase (
39
) and used it to define the higher Berry curvature (
41
) in a small tetrahedron, like the lattice gauge theory.
In Sec.
V
, we showed numerical results of higher Berry curvature for a model, the unperturbed variant of which is known to have a non-trivial value
Wen et al. 2021
of higher Berry curvature as proposed by Kapustin and Spodyneiko
Kapustin and Spodyneiko 2020
.
We demonstrated that the higher Berry curvature we formulated varies smoothly in regions where the model parameter dependence is smooth, and the integral value in the three-dimensional parameter space is quantized to 1.
These results suggest that the higher Berry curvature introduced in Sec.
IV
is expected to work in more general one-dimensional quantum many-body systems.
It is important to compute the higher Berry curvature for a wider range of models to advance the understanding of the geometric structure of gapped states in quantum many-body systems.

FKS subtraction for quarkonium production at NLO
Ajjath A H, Hua-Sheng Shao, and Lukas Simon

Abstract
We extend the local infrared-divergence subtraction formalism, originally proposed by Frixione, Kunszt and Signer (FKS), to calculate short-distance (differential) cross section for any inclusive process involving a quarkonium particle in non-relativistic QCD (NRQCD) factorisation at next-to-leading order (NLO) accuracy in the strong coupling constant α s \alpha_{s} . The new formulas are generally applicable to the production of an S- or P-wave quarkonium state in association with any number of elementary particles. The main new ingredients derived in this paper are the local and integrated soft counterterms for the colour-singlet and colour-octet P-wave bound states. It, therefore, paves the way to the automation of the NLO calculations for heavy quarkonium inclusive and associated production processes.

Soft limit

At NLO, we have contributions coming from one-loop virtual corrections and real emissions besides Born. Due to the complexities introduced by bound states, it is necessary to derive new local and integrated FKS counterterms to handle the IR divergences in real contributions at NLO. We observe that, since the constituent quarks
Q
and
\bar{Q}^{\prime}
are massive, we can recycle the counterterms for collinear and soft-collinear origins. What we need to deal with beyond elementary particle production is the soft but non-collinear part. This includes two new components. The first involves the usual soft counterterms that locally cancel singularities of real emissions and their one-body phase space integrated counterparts. The second consists of additional integrated counterterms resulting from the renormalisation of LDMEs, analogous to the usual initial/final collinear counterterms but originating from a soft origin. Similar to the latter, which are necessary to cancel the remaining IR divergences protected by the collinear factorisation in perturbative QCD, the former are a consequence of the NRQCD factorisation formalism. 4
FKS subtraction for single quarkonium production

4.1
FKS pairs and partition functions

With the squared real amplitudes in the soft limit at hand, we will now structure our approach using the FKS formalism. To categorise the IR divergences and facilitate their subtraction, we introduce a set of ordered pairs for any given process
\dot{r}\in\dot{{\cal R}}_{n}
. This set is denoted as the set of FKS pairs

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{\rm FKS}(\dot{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{(i,j)\;\Big|\;3\leq i\leq n_{L}^{(R)}+2\,,n_{I}\leq j\leq n_{L}^{(R)}+n_{H}+1\,,i\neq j\,,

(121)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}{\mathds{M}}^{(n,0)}(\dot{r})J^{n_{L}^{(B)}}\rightarrow\infty~~{\rm if}~~\tilde{k}_{i}^{0}\rightarrow 0~~{\rm or}~~\tilde{k}_{j}^{0}\rightarrow 0~~{\rm or}~~\vec{\tilde{k}}_{i}\parallel\vec{\tilde{k}}_{j}\Big\}.\phantom{aaaaa}

This implies that a pair of particles belongs to the set of FKS pairs if they induce soft or collinear singularities (or both) in the
n
-body real matrix elements. It is important to note that
\tilde{k}_{j}^{0}\rightarrow 0
is irrelevant when
j=1,2
. In the calculation of an NLO cross section within the FKS formalism, each pair belonging to
{\cal P}_{\rm FKS}
corresponds to a set of subtractions of soft and collinear singularities.

In the FKS formalism, we multiply the real emission matrix elements with the so-called measurement/partition function
{\cal S}_{ij}
, so that the phase space is partitioned into different kinematic regions where each region contains at most one soft and one collinear singularity. The partitioning is accomplished through the introduction of a set of positive-definite functions

\displaystyle{\cal S}_{ij}(\dot{r})\,,\;\;\;\;\;\;(i,j)\in{\cal P}_{\rm FKS}(\dot{r})\,,

(122)

where the argument
\dot{r}\in\dot{{\cal R}}_{n}
means that we can choose different
{\cal S}
for different processes. As described in ref.
Frederix:2009yq
,
{\cal S}_{ij}
is defined in different regions as following:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\sum_{(i,j)\in{\cal P}_{\rm FKS}(\dot{r})}{{\cal S}_{ij}(\dot{r})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,

\displaystyle\lim_{\vec{\tilde{k}}_{i}\parallel\vec{\tilde{k}}_{j}}{{\cal S}_{ij}(\dot{r})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{ij}\left(\frac{\tilde{k}_{i}^{0}}{\tilde{k}_{i}^{0}+\tilde{k}_{j}^{0}}\right),\,~~~~{\rm if}~~\tilde{m}_{i}=\sqrt{\tilde{k}_{i}^{2}}=\tilde{m}_{j}=\sqrt{\tilde{k}_{j}^{2}}=0\,,

\displaystyle\lim_{\tilde{k}_{i}^{0}\rightarrow 0}{{\cal S}_{ij}(\dot{r})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{ij}\,,\phantom{}\;{\rm if}~~~{\cal I}_{i}=g\,,\,~~~~{\rm with}~~0<c_{ij}\leq 1\qquad{\rm and}\,\,\,\,\,\mathop{\sum_{j}}_{(i,j)\in{\cal P}_{\rm FKS}(\dot{r})}\!\!\!\!\!c_{ij}=1\,,

\displaystyle\lim_{\vec{\tilde{k}}_{k}\parallel\vec{\tilde{k}}_{l}}{{\cal S}_{ij}(\dot{r})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0~~\forall\,\{k,l\}\neq\{i,j\}~~{\rm with}~~(k,l)\in{\cal P}_{\rm FKS}(\dot{r})~~{\land}~~\tilde{m}_{k}=\tilde{m}_{l}=0\,,

\displaystyle\lim_{\tilde{k}_{k}^{0}\rightarrow 0}{{\cal S}_{ij}(\dot{r})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0~~\forall k~~{\rm with}~~{\cal I}_{k}=g~~{\rm and}~~\exists l~~{\rm with}~~(k,l)\in{\cal P}_{\rm FKS}(\dot{r})~{\lor}~(l,k)\in{\cal P}_{\rm FKS}(\dot{r}).

In other words,
{\cal S}_{ij}
goes to zero in all regions of the phase space where the real emission matrix elements diverge, except if this involves particle
i
being soft, or particles
i
and
j
being collinear. The functions
h_{ij}(z)
introduced in eq.(
)
are defined in
0\leq z\leq 1
, and have the following properties:

\displaystyle h_{ij}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1\,,~~~~~~~~~{\rm if}~~n_{I}\leq j\leq 2\,,

(124)

\displaystyle h_{ij}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(z)\,,~~~~~{\rm if}~~3\leq j\leq n_{L}^{(R)}+n_{H}+1\,,

(125)

with
h(z)
a positive-definite function such that

\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}{h(z)}=1\,,\;\;\;\;\;\;\lim_{z\rightarrow 1}{h(z)}=0\,,\;\;\;\;\;\;h(z)+h(1-z)=1\,.

(126)

Note that in
\dot{r}
, we have glued
Q
and
\bar{Q}^{\prime}
to a single particle
Q\bar{Q}^{\prime}[n]
. This means that the number of strongly interacting massive particles (except
n
is a colour-singlet S-wave state) is
n_{H}-1
instead of
n_{H}
.

The real matrix elements can be rewritten

\displaystyle{\mathds{M}}^{(n,0)}(\dot{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in{\cal P}_{\rm FKS}(\dot{r})}{{\cal S}_{ij}(\dot{r}){\mathds{M}}^{(n,0)}(\dot{r})},

(127)

which will only be singular for a given term on the r.h.s if particle
i
is soft and/or particles
i
and
j
are collinear after applying
J^{n_{L}^{(B)}}
.

4.2
Local subtraction counterterms

The IR divergence subtracted real cross sections can be formulated in the center of mass frame of the incoming partons:

\displaystyle k_{1}=\tilde{k}_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{s}}{2}(1,0,0,1)\,,\;\;\;\;\;k_{2}=\tilde{k}_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{s}}{2}(1,0,0,-1)\,.

(128)

In this frame, for each pair
(i,j)\in{\cal P}_{\rm FKS}(\dot{r})
, we can introduce the variables
\xi_{i}
and
y_{ij}
, where

\displaystyle\tilde{k}_{i}^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{s}}{2}\xi_{i}\,,

(129)

\displaystyle\vec{\tilde{k}}_{i}\!\cdot\!\vec{\tilde{k}}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\vec{\tilde{k}}_{i}\right|\left|\vec{\tilde{k}}_{j}\right|y_{ij}\,.

(130)

\xi_{i}
is the rescaled energy of the FKS parton
i

y_{ij}
is the cosine of the angle between the FKS parton
i
and its sister
j

and to

\displaystyle d\hat{\sigma}_{{\rm FKS}}(\dot{r})

\displaystyle\sum_{(i,j)\in{\cal P}_{\rm FKS}(\dot{r})}{d\hat{\sigma}_{ij}(\dot{r})},

\varphi_{i}
is the azimuthal direction of the FKS parton. The quantity

is the reduced
(n-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi_{i}^{1-2\epsilon}d\xi_{i}(1-y_{ij}^{2})^{-\epsilon}dy_{ij}d\Omega_{i}^{(2-2\epsilon)}d\widetilde{\phi}_{n-1}^{ij}(\dot{r})\,.

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi_{i}\rightarrow 0}{d\widetilde{\phi}_{n-1}^{ij}(\dot{r})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s^{1-\epsilon}}{(4\pi)^{3-2\epsilon}}d\phi_{n-1}(\dot{r}^{i\hskip-3.55658pt\backslash})\,,\,~~~~~~~~~{\rm if}~~\tilde{m}_{i}=0\,,

(134)

\displaystyle\frac{s^{1-\epsilon}}{(4\pi)^{3-2\epsilon}}d\phi_{n-1}(\dot{r}^{j\oplus i,i\hskip-3.55658pt\backslash})\,,~~~~~{\rm if}~~\tilde{m}_{i}=\tilde{m}_{j}=0\,.

(135)

In
d=4
dimensions, we can simply set
\epsilon=0
and

.
The distributions entering eq.(

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\xi_{\rm max}}{d\xi_{i}f(\xi_{i})\left(\frac{1}{\xi_{i}}\right)_{c}}

where

(138)

and

6
Summary

In this paper, we have generalised the FKS subtraction formalism to processes involving S- or P-wave quarkonium and elementary particles at NLO QCD in NRQCD factorisation. Our main new results are the local soft counterterms in sect.
3.2
and the integrated soft counterterms in sect.
4.3
. As a byproduct, we have also derived the universal IR poles of one-loop matrix elements in app.
B
. This serves as a cornerstone to achieve the NLO automation of cross section computations of quarkonium production processes
11
11

11

Note that the factorisation-breaking effect due to colour transfer between a quarkonium and a heavy quark
Nayak:2007mb
only occurs in a particular phase-space corner,
i.e.
the mass threshold region, where the relative velocity of the heavy quark and the quarkonium tends to zero. Therefore, it would be harmless for our general purpose unless we are probing that particular phase-space region, where, in any case, we need additional theoretical care.
. Our next steps would be to implement the new formulas in the
MadGraph5_aMC@NLO
framework, and to generalise the formulation presented here to processes involving more-than-one quarkonia. The latter case, however, involves additional subtleties, such as the breakdown of NRQCD factorisation for processes with two P-wave bound states elucidated in ref.
He:2018hwb
.

Appendix B
Infrared poles of one-loop matrix elements

We present here the general IR poles of the UV renormalised one-loop matrix element at NLO QCD for arbitrary process that involves a quarkonium and elementary particles by assuming the validity of the KLN theorem. The one-loop virtual matrix element can be written as

\displaystyle{\mathds{M}}^{(n-1,1)}(\dot{r}^{i\hskip-3.55658pt\backslash})=\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\frac{(4\pi)^{\epsilon}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{Q_{\rm ES}^{2}}\right)^{\epsilon}{\mathds{V}}(\dot{r}^{i\hskip-3.55658pt\backslash})\,.

(215)

We can derive the IR poles of
{\mathds{V}}(\dot{r}^{i\hskip-3.55658pt\backslash})
generally from our integrated counterterms with its finite term denoted as
{\mathds{V}}^{(n-1,1)}_{\rm FIN}(\dot{r}^{i\hskip-3.55658pt\backslash})
. We remind readers that in the process
\dot{r}^{i\hskip-3.55658pt\backslash}
, the particles with their indices from
n_{I}
to
n_{L}^{(B)}+2
are coloured and massless, while those from
n_{L}^{(B)}+3
to
n_{L}^{(B)}+n_{H}
(
n_{H}\geq 2
) are massive coloured elementary particles. The index of the quarkonium is
n_{L}^{(B)}+n_{H}+1
.

\dot{r}^{i\hskip-3.55658pt\backslash}

162
), the IR poles can be obtained by taking the bound state as a colour-singlet elementary particle, which amount to

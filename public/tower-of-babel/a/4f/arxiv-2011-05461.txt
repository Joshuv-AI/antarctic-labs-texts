Nonlinear eigenvalue problems and bifurcation for quasi-linear elliptic operators
Emmanuel Wend Benedo Zongo and Bernhard Ruf

Abstract
. In this paper, we analyze an eigenvalue problem for quasi-linear elliptic operators involving homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions in a open smooth bounded domain. We show that the eigenfunctions corresponding to the eigenvalues belong to L ∞ L^{\infty} , which implies C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} smoothness, and the first eigenvalue is simple. Moreover, we investigate the bifurcation results from trivial solutions using the Krasnoselski bifurcation theorem and from infinity using the Leray-Schauder degree. We also show the existence of multiple critical points using variational methods and the Krasnoselski genus. Keywords: quasi-linear operators, bifurcation, bifurcation from infinity, multiple solutions 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35J20, 35J92, 35J25.



1.
Introduction

Assume
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 2
) is an open bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. A classical result in the theory of eigenvalue problems guarantees that the problem

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta u=\displaystyle\lambda u\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{in $\Omega$},\\
u=\displaystyle 0\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{on $\partial\Omega$}\end{array}\right.

possesses a nondecreasing sequence of eigenvalues and a sequence of corresponding eigenfunctions which define a Hilbert basis in
L^{2}(\Omega)
[see,
[
16
]
]. Moreover, it is known that the first eigenvalue of problem (
1.1
) is characterized in the variational point of view by,

\lambda^{D}_{1}:=\inf_{u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}}\left\{\frac{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\penalty\ dx}{\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\penalty\ dx}\right\}.

Suppose that
p>1
is a given real number and consider the nonlinear eigenvalue problem with Neumann boundary condition

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta_{p}u=\displaystyle\lambda u\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{in $\Omega$},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\displaystyle 0\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{on $\partial\Omega$}\end{array}\right.

where
\Delta_{p}u:=\text{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
stands for the p-Laplace operator and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. This problem was considered in
[
15
]
, and using a direct method in calculus of variations (if
p>2
) or a mountain-pass argument (if
p\in(\frac{2N}{N+2},2)
) it was shown that the set of eigenvalues of problem (
1.2
) is exactly the interval
[0,\infty)
. Indeed, it is sufficient to find one positive eigenvalue, say
-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda u.
Then a continuous family of eigenvalues can be found by the reparametrization
u=\alpha v,
satisfying
-\Delta_{p}v=\mu(\alpha)v
, with
\mu(\alpha)=\frac{\lambda}{\alpha^{p-2}}.

In this paper, we consider the so-called
(p,2)
-Laplace operator [see,
[
18
]
] with Dirichlet boundary conditions. More precisely, we analyze the following nonlinear eigenvalue problem,

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\Delta_{p}u-\Delta u=\displaystyle\lambda u\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{in $\Omega$},\\
u=\displaystyle 0\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{on $\partial\Omega$}\end{array}\right.

where
p\in(1,\infty)\backslash\{2\}
is a real number. We recall that if
1<p<q,
then
L^{q}(\Omega)\subset L^{p}(\Omega)
and as a consequence, one has
W^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)\subset W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega).

We will say that
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is an eigenvalue of problem (
1.3
) if there exists

(if
p>2
),
u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}
(if
1<p<2
) such that

(1.4)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\penalty\ dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\penalty\ v\penalty\ dx,

for all
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
(if
p>2
),
v\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
(if
1<p<2
). In this case, such a pair
(u,\lambda)
is called an eigenpair, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is called an eigenvalue and

is an eigenfunction associated to
\lambda.
We say that
\lambda
is a ”first eigenvalue”, if the corresponding eigenfunction
u
is positive or negative.

The operator
-\Delta_{p}-\Delta
appears in quantum field theory [see,
[
5
]
], where it arises in the mathematical description of propagation phenomena of solitary waves. We recall that a solitary wave is a wave which propagates without any temporal evolution in shape.

The operator
-\Delta_{p}-\Delta
is a special case of the so called
(p,q)
-Laplace operator given by
-\Delta_{p}-\Delta_{q}
which has been widely studied; for some results related to our studies, see e.g., [
[
6
,
7
,
10
,
25
,
21
]
].

The main purpose of this work is to study the nonlinear eigenvalue problem (
1.3
) when
p>2,
and
1<p<2
respectively . In particular, we show in section

that the set of the first eigenvalues is given by the interval

, where
\lambda_{1}^{D}
is the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Laplacian. We show that the first eigenvalue of (
1.3
) can be obtained variationally, using a Nehari set for

, and a minimization for

Also in the same section, we recall some results of
[

]
,
[

]
and
[

]
.
In section

, we prove that the eigenfunctions associated to

belong to

, the first eigenvalue

of problem (

) is simple and the corresponding eigenfunctions are positive or negative. In addition, in section

we show a homeomorphism property related to

In section

is a bifurcation point for a branch of first eigenvalues from zero if

and

is a bifurcation point from infinity if

Also the higher Dirichlet eigenvalues

are bifurcation points (from

Finally in section

nonlinear eigenvalues using Krasnoselski’s genus.
In what follows, we denote by

and

the norms on

and

defined respectively by

\|u\|_{1,p}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\penalty\ dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ \|u\|_{2}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\penalty\ dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for all}\penalty\ \penalty\ u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega),\penalty\ \penalty\ u\in L^{2}(\Omega).

We recall the Poincaré inequality, i.e., there exists a positive constant

such that

(1.5)

\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}\penalty\ dx\leq C_{p}(\Omega)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\penalty\ dx\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for all $u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)$},\penalty\ \penalty\ 1<p<\infty.

2.
The spectrum of the nonlinear problem

We now begin with the discussion of the properties of the spectrum of the nonlinear eigenvalues problem (
1.3
).

Remark 2.1
.

Any
\lambda\leq 0
is not an eigenvalue of problem (
1.3
).

Indeed, suppose by contradiction that
\lambda=0
is an eigenvalue of equation (
1.3
), then relation (
1.4
) with
v=u_{0}
gives

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{0}|^{p}\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{0}|^{2}\penalty\ dx=0.

Consequently
|\nabla u_{0}|=0
, therefore
u_{0}
is constant on
\Omega
and
u_{0}=0
on
\Omega
. And this contradicts the fact that
u_{0}
is a nontrivial eigenfunction. Hence
\lambda=0
is not an eigenvalue of problem
(\ref{e3}).

Now it remains to show that any
\lambda<0
is not an eigenvalue of (
1.3
). Suppose by contradiction that
\lambda<0
is an eigenvalue of (
1.3
), with
u_{\lambda}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}
the corresponding eigenfunction. The relation (
1.4
) with
v=u_{\lambda}
implies

0\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{p}\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{2}\penalty\ dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda}^{2}\penalty\ dx<0.

Which yields a contradiction and thus
\lambda<0
cannot be an eigenvalue of problem (
1.3
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{D}_{1}]
is not an eigenvalue of (
1.3
).

For the proof see also
[
15
]
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{D}_{1})
, i.e.,
\lambda^{D}_{1}>\lambda.
Let’s assume by contradiction that there exists a
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{D}_{1})
which is an eigenvalue of (
1.3
) with
u_{\lambda}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}
the corresponding eigenfunction. Letting
v=u_{\lambda}
in relation (
1.4
), we have on the one hand,

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{p}\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{2}\penalty\ dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda}^{2}\penalty\ dx

and on the other hand,

(2.1)

\lambda^{D}_{1}\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda}^{2}dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{2}\penalty\ dx.

By subtracting both side of (
2.1
) by
\lambda\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}u_{\lambda}^{2}\penalty\ dx
, we obtain

(\lambda^{D}_{1}-\lambda)\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{\lambda}\penalty\ dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{2}\penalty\ dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda}^{2}\penalty\ dx,

(\lambda^{D}_{1}-\lambda)\int_{\Omega}u^{2}_{\lambda}\penalty\ dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{2}\penalty\ dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda}^{2}\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda}|^{p}\penalty\ dx=0.

Therefore
(\lambda^{D}_{1}-\lambda)\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}u^{2}_{\lambda}\penalty\ dx\leq 0,
which is a contradiction. Hence, we conclude that
\lambda\in(0,\lambda^{D}_{1})
is not an eigenvalue of problem (
1.3
). In order to complete the proof of the Lemma
2.2
we shall show that
\lambda=\lambda^{D}_{1}
is not an eigenvalue of (
1.3
).

By contradiction we assume that
\lambda=\lambda^{D}_{1}
is an eigenvalue of (
1.3
). So there exists
u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}
such that relation (
1.4
) holds true. Letting

in relation (
1.4
), it follows that

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}}|^{p}\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}}|^{2}\penalty\ dx=\lambda^{D}_{1}\int_{\Omega}u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}}^{2}\penalty\ dx.

But
\lambda^{D}_{1}\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}}^{2}\penalty\ dx\leq\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}|\nabla u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}}|^{2}\penalty\ dx,
therefore

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}}|^{p}\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}}|^{2}\penalty\ dx\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}}|^{2}\penalty\ dx\Rightarrow\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}}|^{p}\penalty\ dx\leq 0.

Using relation (
1.5
), we have
u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}}=0,
which is a contradiction since
u_{\lambda^{D}_{1}}\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}.
So
\lambda=\lambda^{D}_{1}
is not an eigenvalue of (
1.3
).
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume
p\in(1,2)
. Then the set of first eigenvalues of problem
(\ref{e3})
is given by

(\lambda^{D}_{1},\infty),\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{where $\lambda^{D}_{1}$\penalty\ \text{denotes the first eigenvalue of} $-\Delta$ \text{on} $\Omega$}.

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in(\lambda_{1}^{D},\infty)
,
and define the energy functional

J_{\lambda}:W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{by}\penalty\ \penalty\ J_{\lambda}(u)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\penalty\ dx+\frac{2}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\penalty\ dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\penalty\ dx.

One shows that

(see,
[
18
]
) with its derivatives given by

\langle J^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u),v\rangle=2\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\penalty\ dx+2\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\penalty\ dx-2\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\penalty\ v\penalty\ dx\penalty\ \penalty\ ,\penalty\ \forall\penalty\ v\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega).

Thus we note that
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of problem (
1.3
) if and only if
J_{\lambda}
possesses a nontrivial critical point. Considering

where

is the

J_{\lambda}(\rho e_{1})\leq\lambda_{1}^{D}\rho^{2}+C\rho^{p}-\lambda\rho^{2}\rightarrow-\infty,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{as}\penalty\ \penalty\ \rho\rightarrow+\infty.

Hence, we cannot establish the coercivity of

To overcome this difficulty, the idea will be to analyze the functional

on the so called Nehari manifold defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}:=\left\{u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}\penalty\ :\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\penalty\ dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\penalty\ dx\right\}.

Note that all non-trivial solutions of (

\mathcal{N}_{\lambda}.
On

\displaystyle J_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\penalty\ dx+\frac{2}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\penalty\ dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}u^{2}\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle(\frac{2}{p}-1)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\penalty\ dx>0.

Note that for

and this is a contradiction which assures that relation (

Step 4.

We conclude the proof of the claim.

Let

Theorem 3.1

Let

Proof.

By Morrey’s embedding theorem it suffices to consider the case

Let us assume first that

For

(3.1)

M\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{if\penalty\ $x>M$}\end{array}\right.

Since

[

Using

(kp+1)\left[\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla w_{M}w^{kp}_{M}\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla w_{M}w^{kp}_{M}\penalty\ dx\right]=\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\penalty\ w^{kp+1}_{M}\penalty\ dx.

On the other hand using the fact that

(kp+1)\left[\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla w_{M}w^{kp}_{M}\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla w_{M}w^{kp}_{M}\penalty\ dx\right]\leq\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u|^{(k+1)p}\penalty\ dx.

We have

Since the integrals on the left are zero on

in the previous inequality, and it follows that

(kp+1)\left[\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w_{M}|^{p}w^{kp}_{M}\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w_{M}|^{2}w^{kp}_{M}\penalty\ dx\right]\leq\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u|^{(k+1)p}\penalty\ dx.

and then

\|w_{M}\|_{(k+1)p^{\star}}\leq\|w^{k+1}_{M}\|^{\frac{1}{k+1}}_{p^{\star}},

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)^{p-1}|\nabla u|^{p-1}|\nabla v|\penalty\ dx

and

\displaystyle\langle-\Delta u,\frac{u^{2}-v^{2}}{u}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\left\{2\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)\left(|\nabla u||\nabla v|-\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\right)+\left(1+\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)^{2}\right)|\nabla u|^{2}-2\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)|\nabla u||\nabla v|\right\}\penalty\ dx.

By symmetry we have

\displaystyle\langle-\Delta_{p}v,\frac{v^{p}-u^{p}}{v^{p-1}}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\{-p\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{p-1}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla u+\left(1+(p-1)\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{p}\right)|\nabla v|^{p}\right\}\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\{p\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{p-1}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\left(|\nabla v||\nabla u|-\nabla v\cdot\nabla u\right)+\left(1+(p-1)\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{p}\right)|\nabla v|^{p}\right\}\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}p\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{p-1}|\nabla v|^{p-1}|\nabla u|\penalty\ dx

and

\displaystyle\langle-\Delta v,\frac{v^{2}-u^{2}}{v}\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\left\{2\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)\left(|\nabla v||\nabla u|-\nabla v\cdot\nabla u\right)+\left(1+\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{2}\right)|\nabla v|^{2}-2\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)|\nabla v||\nabla u|\right\}\penalty\ dx.

Thus

\displaystyle I(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\{p\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)^{p-1}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\left(|\nabla u||\nabla v|-\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\right)+\left(1+(p-1)\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)^{p}\right)|\nabla u|^{p}\right\}\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle p\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)^{p-1}|\nabla u|^{p-1}|\nabla v|\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\{p\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{p-1}|\nabla v|^{p-2}\left(|\nabla v||\nabla u|-\nabla v\cdot\nabla u\right)+\left(1+(p-1)\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{p}\right)|\nabla v|^{p}\right\}\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle p\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{p-1}|\nabla v|^{p-1}|\nabla u|\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\{2\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)\left(|\nabla u||\nabla v|-\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\right)+\left(1+\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)^{2}\right)|\nabla u|^{2}-2\left(\frac{v}{u}\right)|\nabla u||\nabla v|\right\}\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\{2\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)\left(|\nabla v||\nabla u|-\nabla v\cdot\nabla u\right)+\left(1+\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)^{2}\right)|\nabla v|^{2}-2\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)|\nabla v||\nabla u|\right\}\penalty\ dx.

So

I(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}F(\frac{v}{u},\nabla v,\nabla u)\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}G(\frac{v}{u},|\nabla v|,|\nabla u|)\penalty\ dx,

where

\displaystyle F(t,S,R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\left\{t^{p-1}|R|^{p-2}\left(|R||S|-R\cdot S\right)+t^{1-p}|S|^{p-2}\left(|R||S|-R\cdot S\right)\right\}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\left\{t\left(|R||S|-R\cdot S\right)\right\}+2\left\{t^{-1}\left(|R||S|-R\cdot S\right)\right\}

and

\displaystyle G(t,s,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1+(p-1)t^{p}\right)r^{p}+\left(1+(p-1)t^{-p}\right)s^{p}+(1+t^{2})r^{2}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(1+t^{-2})s^{2}-pt^{p-1}r^{p-1}s-pt^{1-p}s^{p-1}r-2trs-2t^{-1}rs,

for all
t=\frac{v}{u}>0,R=\nabla u,S=\nabla v\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
r=|\nabla u|,s=|\nabla v|\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

We clearly have that
F
is non-negative. Now let us show that
G
is non-negative. Indeed, we observe that

G(t,s,0)=\left(1+(p-1)t^{-p}\right)s^{p}+(1+t^{-2})s^{2}\geq 0

and

G(t,s,0)=0\Rightarrow s=0.
If
r\neq 0
, by setting
z=\frac{s}{tr}
we obtain

\displaystyle G(t,s,r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{p}r^{p}(z^{p}-pz+(p-1))+r^{p}((p-1)z^{p}-pz^{p-1}+1)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle t^{2}r^{2}(z^{2}-2z+1)+r^{2}(z^{2}-2z+1),

and
G
can be written as

G(t,s,r)=r^{p}(t^{p}f(z)+g(z))+r^{2}(t^{2}h(z)+k(z)),

with
f(z)=z^{p}-pz+(p-1),

g(z)=(p-1)z^{p}-pz^{p-1}+1,

h(z)=k(z)=z^{2}-2z+1

\forall p>1.
We can see that
f,g,h
and
k
are non-negative.
Hence
G
is non-negative and thus
I(u,v)\geq 0
for all
(u,v)\in D_{I}.
In addition since
f,g,h
and
k
vanish if and only if

, then
G(t,s,r)=0
if and only if
s=tr.

Consequently, if

then we have

\nabla u\cdot\nabla v=|\nabla u||\nabla v|\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ u|\nabla v|=v|\nabla u|

almost everywhere in
\Omega.
This is equivalent to
\left(u\nabla v-v\nabla u\right)^{2}=0,
which implies that
u=\alpha v
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{\star}_{+}.

∎

Theorem 3.3
.

The first eigenvalues
\lambda
of equation (

) are simple, i.e. if

and

are two positive first eigenfunctions associated to

then

Proof.

By proposition

Inserting this into the equation (

∎

3.3.
Invertibility of the operator
-\Delta_{p}-\Delta

To simplify some notations, here we set
X=W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and its dual
X^{\star}=W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega),
where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1.

For the proof of the following lemma, we refer to
[
19
]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
p>2
. Then there exist two positive constants
c_{1},c_{2}
such that, for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
we have :

(i)

(x_{2}-x_{1})\cdot(|x_{2}|^{p-2}x_{2}-|x_{1}|^{p-2}x_{1})\geq c_{1}|x_{2}-x_{1}|^{p}

(ii)

\left||x_{2}|^{p-2}x_{2}-|x_{1}|^{p-2}x_{1}\right|\leq c_{2}(|x_{2}|+|x_{1}|)^{p-2}|x_{2}-x_{1}|

Proposition 3.5
.

For
p>2,
the operator
-\Delta_{p}-\Delta
is a global homeomorphism.

The proof is based on the previous Lemma
3.4
.

Proof.

Define the nonlinear operator
A:X\rightarrow X^{\star}
by

\langle Au,v\rangle=\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\penalty\ dx+\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\penalty\ dx
for all
u,v\in X.

To show that
-\Delta_{p}-\Delta
is a homeomorphism, it is enough to show that
A
is a continuous strongly monotone operator, [see
[
9
]
, Corollary 2.5.10].

For
p>2,
for all
u,v\in X
, by
(i)
, we get

\displaystyle\langle Au-Av,u-v\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u-v)|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u-|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\right)\cdot\nabla(u-v)\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u-v)|^{2}dx+c_{1}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u-v)|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{1}\|u-v\|^{p}_{1,p}

Thus
A
is a strongly monotone operator.

We claim that
A
is a continuous operator from
X
to
X^{\star}.
Indeed, assume that
u_{n}\rightarrow u
in
X.
We have to show that
\|Au_{n}-Au\|_{X^{\star}}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty.
Indeed, using
(ii)
and Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding theorem, one has

\displaystyle\left|\langle Au_{n}-Au,w\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left||\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{n}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right||\nabla w|\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u_{n}-u)||\nabla w|\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|+|\nabla u|\right)^{p-2}|\nabla(u_{n}-u)||\nabla w|\penalty\ dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u_{n}-u)||\nabla w|\penalty\ dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u_{n}|+|\nabla u|\right)^{p}dx\right)^{p-2/p}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u_{n}-u)|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle c_{3}\|u_{n}-u\|_{1,2}\|w\|_{1,2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{4}(\|u_{n}\|_{1,p}+\|u\|_{1,p})^{p-2}\|u_{n}-u\|_{1,p}\|w\|_{1,p}+c_{5}\|u_{n}-u\|_{1,p}\|w\|_{1,p}.

Thus
\|Au_{n}-Au\|_{X^{\star}}\rightarrow 0
, as
n\rightarrow+\infty,
and hence
A
is a homeomorphism.
∎

4.
Bifurcation of eigenvalues

In the next subsection we show that for equation (

) there is a branch of first eigenvalues bifurcating from
(\lambda_{1}^{D},0)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega).

4.1.
Bifurcation from zero : the case
p>2

By proposition
3.5
, equation (
1.3
) is equivalent to

(4.1)

u=\lambda(-\Delta_{p}-\Delta)^{-1}u\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ u\in W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega).

We set

(4.2)

S_{\lambda}(u)=u-\lambda(-\Delta_{p}-\Delta)^{-1}u,

\penalty\ u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\subset W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \lambda>0.
By
\Sigma=\{(\lambda,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)/\penalty\ u\neq 0\penalty\ ,S_{\lambda}(u)=0\}
, we denote the set of nontrivial solutions of (
4.1
).
A bifurcation point for (
4.1
) is a number
\lambda^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
(\lambda^{\star},0)
belongs to the closure of
\Sigma.
This is equivalent to say that, in any neighbourhood of
(\lambda^{\star},0)
in
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, there exists a nontrivial solution of
S_{\lambda}(u)=0
.

Our goal is to apply the Krasnoselski bifurcation theorem [see,
[
1
]
].

Theorem 4.1
.

(Krasnoselski, 1964)

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
T\in C^{1}(X,X)
be a compact operator such that
T(0)=0
and
T^{\prime}(0)=0.
Moreover, let
A\in\mathcal{L}(X)
also be compact. Then every characteristic value
\lambda^{*}
of
A
with odd (algebraic) multiplicity is a bifurcation point for
u=\lambda Au+T(u).

We now state our bifurcation result.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
p>2.
Then every eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}^{D}
with odd multiplicity is a bifurcation point in
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
of
S_{\lambda}(u)=0,
in the sense that in any neighbourhood of
(\lambda^{D}_{k},0)
in
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
there exists a nontrivial solution of
S_{\lambda}(u)=0
.

Proof.

We write the equation
S_{\lambda}(u)=0
as

u=\lambda Au+T_{\lambda}(u),

where
Au=(-\Delta)^{-1}u
and
T_{\lambda}(u)=[(-\Delta_{p}-\Delta)^{-1}-(-\Delta)^{-1}](\lambda u)
, where we consider

(-\Delta_{p}-\Delta)^{-1}:L^{2}(\Omega)\subset W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\subset L^{2}(\Omega)

and
(-\Delta)^{-1}:L^{2}(\Omega)\subset W^{-1,2}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\subset L^{2}(\Omega).

For
p>2,
the mapping

(-\Delta_{p}-\Delta)^{-1}-(-\Delta)^{-1}:L^{2}(\Omega)\subset W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\subset L^{2}(\Omega)

is compact thanks to Rellich-Kondrachov theorem. We clearly have

A\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\Omega))
and
T_{\lambda}(0)=0
. Now we have to show that

(1)

T_{\lambda}\in C^{1}
.

(2)

T_{\lambda}^{\prime}(0)=0
.

In order to show
(1)
and
(2)
, it suffices to show that

(a)

-\Delta_{p}-\Delta:W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
is continuously differentiable in a neighborhood
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

(b)

(-\Delta_{p}-\Delta)^{-1}
is a continuous inverse operator.

According to Proposition
3.5
,
-\Delta_{p}-\Delta
is a homeomorphism, hence
(-\Delta_{p}-\Delta)^{-1}
is continuous and this shows
(b).
We also recall that in section
3.2
, we have shown that
\lambda^{D}_{1}
is simple.

Let us show (a). We claim that
-\Delta_{p}:W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
is Gâteaux differentiable.
Indeed, for
\varphi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
we have

\displaystyle\langle-\Delta_{p}(u+\delta v),\varphi\rangle-\langle-\Delta_{p}u,\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle|\nabla(u+\delta v)|^{p-2}\nabla(u+\delta v),\nabla\varphi\rangle-\langle|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\left(|\nabla(u+\delta v)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\nabla(u+\delta v),\nabla\varphi\rangle-\langle|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+2\delta\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle+\delta^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\nabla(u+\delta v),\nabla\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\langle|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle[|\nabla u|^{p-2}+(p-2)|\nabla u|^{2(\frac{p-2}{2}-1)}\delta\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle+O(\delta^{2})]\nabla(u+\delta v),\nabla\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\langle|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle[|\nabla u|^{p-2}+(p-2)|\nabla u|^{p-4}\delta\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle+O(\delta^{2})]\nabla(u+\delta v),\nabla\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\langle|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-2)\delta|\nabla u|^{p-4}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle+\delta\langle|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla v,\nabla\varphi\rangle+O(\delta^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta[(p-2)|\nabla u|^{p-4}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle+\langle|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla v,\nabla\varphi\rangle+O(\delta)].

Define

\langle B(u)v,\varphi\rangle=(p-2)|\nabla u|^{p-4}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle+\langle|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla v,\nabla\varphi\rangle

and let
(u_{n})_{n\geq 0}\subset W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega).
Assume that
u_{n}\rightarrow u,
as
n\rightarrow\infty
in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega).
We have

\displaystyle\langle B(u_{n})v-B(u)v,\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-2)\left[|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-4}\langle\nabla u_{n},\nabla v\rangle\langle\nabla u_{n},\nabla\varphi\rangle-|\nabla u|^{p-4}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\langle|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}\nabla v,\nabla\varphi\rangle-\langle|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla v,\nabla\varphi\rangle.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\langle B(u_{n})v-B(u)v,\varphi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq(p-2)

\displaystyle\left||\nabla u_{n}|^{p-4}\langle\nabla u_{n},\nabla v\rangle\langle\nabla u_{n},\nabla\varphi\rangle-|\nabla u|^{p-4}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left||\nabla u_{n}|^{p-2}-|\nabla u|^{p-2}\right||\langle\nabla v,\nabla\varphi\rangle|.

By assumption, we can assume that, up to subsequences,

(\ast)

\nabla u_{n}\rightarrow\nabla u
in
\left(L^{p}(\Omega)\right)^{N}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and

(\ast\ast)

\nabla u_{n}(x)\rightarrow\nabla u(x)
almost everywhere as

Then
|\nabla u_{n}|^{p-4}\langle\nabla u_{n},\nabla v\rangle\langle\nabla u_{n},\nabla\varphi\rangle\rightarrow|\nabla u|^{p-4}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle\langle\nabla u,\nabla\varphi\rangle
and

as

Consequently
\langle B(u_{n})v,\varphi\rangle\rightarrow\langle B(u)v,\varphi\rangle
as

Thus, we find that

and thanks to the Inverse function theorem

is differentiable in a neighborhood of

. Therefore according to the Krasnoselski bifurcation Theorem, we obtain that

is a bifurcation point at zero.
∎

For

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-\|v\|_{1,2}^{4-p}\Delta_{p}v-\Delta v=\lambda v\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{in $\Omega$},\\

The proof of Theorem

is a bifurcation of (

Remark 4.5
.

With this transformation, we have that the pair

Let us consider a small ball

and

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
1<p<2.
There exists
r>0
such that the mapping

Proof.

In order to prove that the operator

is invertible with a continuous inverse, we again rely on [
[
9
]

We show that there exists
\delta>0

\langle T(u)-T(v),u-v\rangle\geq\delta\|u-v\|^{2}_{1,2},\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for}\penalty\ \penalty\ u,v\in B_{r}(0)\subset W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)

Indeed, using that

is strongly monotone on

on the one hand and the Hölder inequality on the other hand, we have

(4.8)

\displaystyle\langle T(u)-T(v),u-v\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\nabla u-\nabla v\|^{2}_{2}+\left(\|u\|_{1,2}^{4-p}(-\Delta_{p}u)-\|v\|_{1,2}^{4-p}(-\Delta_{p}v),u-v\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|u-v\|^{2}_{1,2}+\|u\|_{1,2}^{4-p}\left((-\Delta_{p}u)-(-\Delta_{p}v),u-v\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\|u\|_{1,2}^{4-p}-\|v\|_{1,2}^{4-p}\right)\left(-\Delta_{p}v,u-v\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|u-v\|^{2}_{1,2}-\left|\|u\|_{1,2}^{4-p}-\|v\|_{1,2}^{4-p}\right|\|\nabla v\|_{p}^{p-1}\|\nabla(u-v)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|u-v\|^{2}_{1,2}-\left|\|u\|_{1,2}^{4-p}-\|v\|_{1,2}^{4-p}\right|C\|v\|_{1,2}^{p-1}\|u-v\|_{1,2}.

Now, we obtain by the Mean Value Theorem that there exists

\lambda\int_{\Omega}v^{2}\penalty\ dx-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}\penalty\ dx\geq\lambda\int_{\Omega}v^{2}\penalty\ dx-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j}\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{i}|e_{i}|^{2}\penalty\ dx\geq(\lambda-\lambda_{j})\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2}\penalty\ dx>0
. Hence,
\rho(v)v\in\mathcal{N}_{\lambda},
and then

and
\gamma(\rho(S_{j}))=\gamma(S_{j})=j
for

for any

.

It is then standard ([see

]
, Proposition 10.8]) to conclude that

\sigma_{\lambda,j}=\inf_{\gamma(A)\geq j}\sup_{u\in A}I_{\lambda}(u),\penalty\ \penalty\ 1\leq j\leq k,\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for any }\penalty\ k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}

yields

which gives rise to

Let

Let us consider the

We clearly have that

Next, we show that there exists sets

Consider

We have, for any

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{u\in A_{j}}I_{\lambda}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{v\in S_{j}\cap E_{j}}I_{\lambda}(sv)

\displaystyle\leq

Finally, we conclude that

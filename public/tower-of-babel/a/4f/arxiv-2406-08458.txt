Braided Logarithmic Vertex Algebras
Bojko Bakalov, Juan J. Villarreal

Abstract
. We study a family of algebras defined using a locally-finite endomorphism called a braiding map. When the braiding map is semi-simple, the algebra is a generalized vertex algebra, while when the braiding map is locally-nilpotent we have a logarithmic vertex algebra. We describe a method that associates to these algebras non-local Poisson vertex algebras, and we use this relation to build a new example of a generalized vertex algebra motivated by the non-linear Schrödinger non-local Poisson vertex algebra.

1.
Introduction

The following is the main definition of this work (cf.
[
BV1
]
).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
V
be a vector space. A
braiding map

\mathscr{S}
on
V
is a linear operator

(1.1)

\mathscr{S}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\phi_{i}\otimes\psi_{i}\,,\qquad\phi_{i},\psi_{i}\in\End(V)\,,

satisfying the following two conditions:

(1)

\mathscr{S}
is
symmetric
, i.e.,
\mathscr{S}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\phi_{i}\otimes\psi_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\psi_{i}\otimes\phi_{i}
;

(2)

[\phi_{i},\psi_{j}]=[\phi_{i},\phi_{j}]=[\psi_{i},\psi_{j}]=0\,,\qquad 1\leq i,j\leq L
.

Recall that a linear operator
\varphi
is called
locally finite
if every vector is contained in some finite-dimensional
\varphi
-invariant subspace. A locally finite operator
\varphi
has a unique Jordan–Chevalley decomposition
\varphi=\varphi^{(\mathrm{s})}+\varphi^{(\mathrm{n})}
, where
\varphi^{(\mathrm{s})}
and
\varphi^{(\mathrm{n})}
commute,
\varphi^{(\mathrm{s})}
is semi-simple and
\varphi^{(\mathrm{n})}
is locally nilpotent (for every vector
u
we have
(\varphi^{(\mathrm{n})})^{k}u=0
for some
k\geq 1
).

Now, we introduce the algebras that we study in this paper, generalizing those of
[
BV1
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

A
braided logarithmic vertex algebra
(braided logVA) is a complex vector space
V
equipped with a vector
{\boldsymbol{1}}\in V
, a braiding map
\mathscr{S}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\phi_{i}\otimes\psi_{i}
where
\phi_{i},\psi_{i}
are locally finite on
V
, and a family of bilinear products

\mu_{(n)}\colon V\otimes V\rightarrow V\,,\qquad a\otimes b\mapsto a_{(n+\mathscr{S})}b\quad(n\in\mathbb{Z}),

satisfying the following identities:

(i)

(
truncation property
)
a_{(n+\mathscr{S})}b=0
for
n\gg 0
.

(ii)

(
vacuum axioms
)
\mathscr{S}({\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes a)=0
,
{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(n+\mathscr{S})}a=\delta_{n,-1}a
,
a_{(-1+\mathscr{S})}{\boldsymbol{1}}=a
.

(iii)

(
hexagon identity
)

(1.2)

\mathscr{S}(\mu_{(n)}\otimes I)=(\mu_{(n)}\otimes I)(\mathscr{S}_{13}+\mathscr{S}_{23})\,,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

(iv)

(
Borcherds identity
)

(1.3)

\begin{split}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}&(-1)^{j}\mu_{(m+n-j)}(I\otimes\mu_{(k+j)})\binom{n+\mathscr{S}_{12}}{j}\\
&-\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(-1)^{n+j}\mu_{(n+k-j)}(I\otimes\mu_{(m+j)})(-1)^{\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{s})}_{12}}\binom{n+\mathscr{S}_{12}}{j}P_{12}\\
&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\mu_{(m+k-j)}(\mu_{(n+j)}\otimes I)\binom{m+\mathscr{S}_{13}}{j}\,,\end{split}

for all
k,m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, where

P
is the transposition on
V\otimes V
and
\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{s})}
is the semi-simple term of the Jordan–Chevalley decomposition of
\mathscr{S}

(cf. (
2.1
)).

The name braided vertex algebras appeared in the literature in quantum vertex algebras; see
[
EK
]
(see also
[
BV1
, Remark 3.19]
). Note that a braided logVA with
\mathscr{S}=0
is the same as a vertex algebra
[
Bo
]
. More generally, we have:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
V
be a braided logVA with braiding
\mathscr{S}
. Then:

(i)

If
\mathscr{S}=\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{n})}
, then
V
is a logarithmic vertex algebra as in
[
BV1
]
.

(ii)

If
\mathscr{S}={\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{s})}}
, then
V
is a generalized vertex algebra as in
[
FFR
,
DL
,
BK
]
.

In
[
FB
]
, Poisson vertex algebras were associated to vertex algebras considering (algebraic) limits. We have the following generalization of this result.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
V^{\epsilon}
be a logarithmic vertex algebra over
\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^{2})
with a state-field map
Y^{\epsilon}
such that
V^{0}=V^{\epsilon}/\epsilon V_{\epsilon}
is a commutative vertex algebra. Then
V^{0}
naturally acquires the structure of a non-local Poisson vertex algebra.

Recall that a vertex algebra is called
commutative
if all products
\mu_{(n)}=0
for
n\geq 0
; then the product
\mu_{(-1)}
is commutative and associative
[
Bo
]
.
We review the definition of a non-local Poisson vertex algebra (non-local PVA) in Definition
4.2
below; see
[
K
,
DK
]
.

In this work, we describe a new example of a generalized vertex algebra in Theorem
4.6
, motivated by the non-linear Schrödinger non-local PVA from
[
DK
]
; see Definition
4.4
. This generalized vertex algebra is built using an associative algebra with generators
\{w_{n},\bar{w}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
subject to the following relations

:

(1.4)

\begin{split}\sum_{j\geq 0}w_{m-j}w_{k+j}+w_{k-j}w_{m+j}&=0\,,\\
\sum_{j\geq 0}\bar{w}_{m-j}\bar{w}_{k+j}+\bar{w}_{k-j}\bar{w}_{m+j}&=0\,,\\
{w}_{m}\bar{w}_{k}-w_{m-1}\bar{w}_{k+1}+\bar{w}_{k}{w}_{m}-\bar{w}_{k-1}{w}_{m+1}&=0\,.\end{split}

In
[
BV2
]
, we described, in particular, logarithmic vertex algebras quantizing the potential free boson non-local PVA and the potential Virasoro-Magri non-local PVA. For the non-linear Schrödinger non-local PVA the same methods as in
[

]
cannot be used. In this work, the algebraic limits in Theorem

]

This paper is organized as follows. In Section

and
1.3
. In Section
3
, following
[

[

2.
Logarithmic Fields

We denote by
V[\![z]\!][\zeta,z^{-\mathbb{C}}]
the space of infinite sums
\sum_{n}f_{n}(\zeta)z^{n}

where
f_{n}(\zeta)\in V[\zeta]
and
n
runs over the union of finitely many sets of the form
\{-d_{i}+\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}
with
d_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
.
The space
V[\![z]\!][\zeta,z^{-\mathbb{C}}]
is equipped with the usual action of the derivatives
\partial_{z}
and
\partial_{\zeta}
, and the operator

D_{z}:=\partial_{z}+z^{-1}\partial_{\zeta}\,,

which acts as the total derivative with respect to
z
if we set
\zeta=\log z
.

Let
\mathscr{S}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\phi_{i}\otimes\psi_{i}
be a braiding map where
\phi_{i},\psi_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq L
) are locally finite on
V
with Jordan–Chevalley decompositions

\phi_{i}=\phi_{i}^{(\mathrm{s})}+\phi_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})},\qquad\psi_{i}=\psi_{i}^{(\mathrm{s})}+\psi_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}\,.

Note that, since all the operators
\phi_{i},\psi_{j}
commute with each other, their semisimple and nilpotent part commute with each other too.
This implies that
\mathscr{S}
is locally finite on
V\otimes V
and

(2.1)

\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{s})}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\phi^{(\mathrm{s})}_{i}\otimes\psi^{(\mathrm{s})}_{i}\,,\qquad\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{n})}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\phi^{(\mathrm{s})}_{i}\otimes\psi^{(\mathrm{n})}_{i}+\phi^{(\mathrm{n})}_{i}\otimes\psi^{(\mathrm{s})}_{i}+\phi^{(\mathrm{n})}_{i}\otimes\psi^{(\mathrm{n})}_{i}\,.

We use this decomposition of
\mathscr{S}
to define the following map on
V\otimes V
:

z^{\mathscr{S}}:=z^{\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{s})}}e^{\zeta\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{n})}}\,.

The next lemma can be easily derived from (
2.1
) and Definition
1.1
(
2
).

Lemma 2.1
.

For any
a,b\in V
, we have

(2.2)

z^{\mathscr{S}}(a\otimes b)=z^{\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{s})}}e^{\zeta\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{n})}}(a\otimes b)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{j!}z^{d_{i}}\zeta^{j}\bigl(\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{n})}\bigr)^{j}(a_{i}\otimes b_{i})\,,

where
a\otimes b=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\otimes b_{i}
such that
a_{i}\in V

(
respectively,
b_{i}\in V)
are common eigenvectors of all
\phi_{j}^{(\mathrm{s})}

(
respectively,
\psi^{(\mathrm{s})}_{j})
,
1\leq j\leq L
, and

(i)

\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{s})}(a_{i}\otimes b_{i})=d_{i}(a_{i}\otimes b_{i})
with
d_{i}\in\mathbb{C};

(ii)

m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is such that
(\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{n})})^{m}(a_{i}\otimes b_{i})=0
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.

2.1.
Quantum fields

Now we introduce the notion of a quantum field as in
[
B
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
V
be a vector space. The space of
(logarithmic) fields
is defined by

\QF(V)=\Hom(V,V[\![z]\!][\zeta,z^{-\mathbb{C}}])\,.

We denote the elements of
\QF(V)
as
a(z,\zeta)
or
a(z)
for short.

On a braided logVA
V
, we define for
a,b\in V
the series (cf. (
2.2
))

(2.3)

\begin{split}Y(a,z)b&=Y(z)(a\otimes b):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mu_{(n)}\bigl(z^{-n-1-\mathscr{S}}(a\otimes b)\bigr)\\
&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}z^{-n-1-d_{i}}\zeta^{j}\mu_{(n)}\bigl(\bigl(\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{n})}\bigr)^{j}(a_{i}\otimes b_{i})\bigr)\,.\end{split}

Note that, by the linearity of
\mu_{(n)}
, we have

a_{(n+\mathscr{S})}b=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mu_{(n)}(a_{i}\otimes b_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}{a_{i}}_{(n+\mathscr{S})}b_{i}
.
Now, by Definition
1.2
(i) and (
2.3
), the next proposition is obvious.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
V
be a braided logVA. Then
Y(a,z)
, defined by (
2.3
),
is a logarithmic field on
V
for every
a\in V
.

In a braided logVA
V
, we define the
translation operator

T\in\End(V)
by

(2.4)

T(a):=a_{(-2+\mathscr{S})}{\boldsymbol{1}}\,,\qquad a\in V\,.

Note that
T({\boldsymbol{1}})=0
by definition.

Proposition 2.4
.

For all
a\in V
, we have the
translation covariance
property

(2.5)

[T,Y(a,z)]=D_{z}Y(a,z)\,.

Proof.

The Borcherds identity (
1.3
) for
m=0,k=-2
on
a\otimes b\otimes{\boldsymbol{1}}
gives:

\mu_{(n)}(a\otimes Tb)-T\mu_{(n)}(a\otimes b)=-n\mu_{(n-1)}(a\otimes b)-\mu_{(n-1)}\bigl(\mathscr{S}(a\otimes b)\bigr)\,.

Plugging this into (
2.3
), we obtain the translation covariance.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

For any braided logVA
V
, the map
Y
defined in (
2.3
) satisfies
Y({\boldsymbol{1}},z)=I
,
Y(a,z){\boldsymbol{1}}\in V[\![z]\!]
, and
Y(a,z){\boldsymbol{1}}\big|_{z=0}=a
.

Proof.

By the hexagon identity (Definition
1.2
(iii)), the components
\phi_{i},\psi_{i}
of
\mathscr{S}
are derivations of all products
\mu_{(n)}
. This implies, in particular, that
\phi_{i}({\boldsymbol{1}})=\psi_{i}({\boldsymbol{1}})=0
, because
{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(-1+\mathscr{S})}{\boldsymbol{1}}={\boldsymbol{1}}
by the vacuum axiom (Definition
1.2
(ii)). Then the claims of the proposition follow easily from (
2.3
) and the vacuum axiom.
∎

As in
[
BV1
]
, we will use the notation
z_{ij}=z_{i}-z_{j}
, and we introduce the formal variables
\zeta_{ij}=\zeta_{ji}
, which are thought of as
\log|z_{ij}|
. Additionally, we consider the formal expansions for
n\in\mathbb{Z}

(2.6)

\begin{split}\iota_{z_{1},z_{2}}z_{12}^{n+\mathscr{S}}=\iota_{z_{1},z_{2}}z_{12}^{n+{\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{s})}}}e^{\zeta_{12}\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{n})}}&:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\binom{n+\mathscr{S}}{j}(-1)^{j}z_{1}^{n-j+\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{s})}}z_{2}^{j}\,,\\
\iota_{z_{2},z_{1}}z_{12}^{n+\mathscr{S}}=\iota_{z_{2},z_{1}}z_{12}^{n+{\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{s})}}}e^{\zeta_{21}{\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{n})}}}&:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\binom{n+\mathscr{S}}{j}(-1)^{n+j+{\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{s})}}}z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{n-j+\mathscr{S}}\,.\end{split}

In the following proposition, we will utilize the following generalization of delta function introduced in
[
B
]
:

(2.7)

\delta_{\mathscr{S}}(z_{1},z_{2}):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z_{1}^{-n-1-\mathscr{S}}z_{2}^{n+\mathscr{S}}\,.

Note that we have

(2.8)

\frac{1}{j!}D_{z_{2}}^{j}\delta_{\mathscr{S}}(z_{1},z_{2})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\binom{n+\mathscr{S}}{j}z_{1}^{-n-1-\mathscr{S}}z_{2}^{n-j+\mathscr{S}}\,.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
V
be a braided logarithmic vertex algebra.
For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have the following
Borcherds identity

(2.9)

\begin{split}\iota_{z_{1},z_{2}}&Y(z_{1})(I\otimes Y(z_{2}))z_{12}^{n+\mathscr{S}_{12}}-\iota_{z_{2},z_{1}}Y(z_{2})(I\otimes Y(z_{1}))z_{12}^{n+\mathscr{S}_{12}}\\
&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{1}{j!}Y(z_{2})(\mu_{(n+j)}\otimes I)D_{z_{2}}^{j}\delta_{\mathscr{S}_{13}}(z_{1},z_{2})\,.\end{split}

Proof.

Using the notation
X(z)(a\otimes b):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mu_{(n)}(a\otimes b)z^{-n-1}
we have that (
1.3
) is equivalent to

(2.10)

\begin{split}&X(z_{1})(I\otimes X(z_{2}))\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\binom{n+\mathscr{S}_{12}}{j}(-1)^{j}z_{1}^{n-j}z_{2}^{j}\\
&-X(z_{2})(I\otimes X(z_{1}))\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\binom{n+\mathscr{S}_{12}}{j}(-1)^{n+j+\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{s})}_{12}}z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{n-j}P_{12}\\
&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}X(z_{2})(\mu_{(n+j)}\otimes I)\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\binom{m+\mathscr{S}_{13}}{j}z_{1}^{-m-1}z_{2}^{m-j}\,.\end{split}

We multiply the right-hand side of this identity by
z_{1}^{-\mathscr{S}_{13}}z_{2}^{-\mathscr{S}_{23}}
; then from (
1.2
), (
2.6
), (
2.8
), we obtain (
2.9
).
∎

Before we state the locality of braided logVAs, we need the following simple lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathscr{S}
be as in (
2.1
). Then for any
a\in V
, there exist
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and an expression

a=\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}
such that

(i)

\phi^{(\mathrm{s})}_{i}a_{j}={d_{i,j}}a_{j}
for some
d_{i,j}\in\mathbb{C}
and all
1\leq i\leq L
,
1\leq j\leq r;

(ii)

\bigl(\phi_{i}^{(\mathrm{n})}\bigr)^{m+1}a_{j}=0
for all
1\leq i\leq L
,
1\leq j\leq r
.

Furthermore, for any
b\in V
, we have

z^{\mathscr{S}}(a\otimes b)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{k=0}^{mL}\sum_{1\leq i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}\leq L}\frac{1}{k!}\zeta^{k}(\phi^{(\mathrm{n})}_{i_{1}}\cdots\phi^{(\mathrm{n})}_{i_{k}})a_{j}\otimes z^{\sum_{i=1}^{L}d_{i,j}\psi_{i}}(\psi_{i_{1}}\cdots\psi_{i_{k}})b\,.

Proof.

First, note that all
\phi_{i}
commute with each other; hence, all
\phi^{(\mathrm{s})}_{i}
commute and can be diagonalized them simultaneously.
Then the formula for
z^{\mathscr{S}}
follows from the identities

\displaystyle z^{\phi^{(\mathrm{s})}_{i}\otimes\psi_{i}}(a\otimes b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}\otimes z^{{d_{i,j}}\psi_{i}}b\,,

\displaystyle z^{\phi^{(\mathrm{n})}_{i}\otimes\psi_{i}}(a\otimes b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{\zeta^{k}}{k!}\bigl(\phi^{(\mathrm{n})}_{i}\bigr)^{k}a\otimes(\psi_{i})^{k}b\,.

∎

Proposition 2.8
.

For every
a,b\in V
, there exists
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that for all
c\in V

(2.11)

\begin{split}Y(z_{1})&(I\otimes Y(z_{2}))\,\iota_{z_{1},z_{2}}z_{12}^{N+\mathscr{S}}(a\otimes b)\otimes c\\
&=Y(z_{2})(I\otimes Y(z_{1}))\,\iota_{z_{2},z_{1}}z_{12}^{N+\mathscr{S}}(b\otimes a)\otimes c\,.\end{split}

Proof.

First, we rewrite (
2.7
) as

\delta_{\mathscr{S}}(z_{1},z_{2})=\Bigl(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}z_{1}^{-n-1}z_{2}^{n}\Bigr)(z_{1}^{-\mathscr{S}}z_{2}^{\mathscr{S}})=\delta(z_{1},z_{2})\Bigl(\frac{z_{2}}{z_{1}}\Bigr)^{\mathscr{S}}\,,

where we use the usual delta function from
[
K
]
. Then from the Leibniz rule we have

D_{z_{2}}^{j}\delta_{\mathscr{S}_{13}}(z_{1},z_{2})=\sum_{k=0}^{j}\binom{j}{k}D_{z_{2}}^{k}\bigl(\delta(z_{1},z_{2})\bigr)D_{z_{2}}^{j-k}\Bigl(\frac{z_{2}}{z_{1}}\Bigr)^{\mathscr{S}_{13}}\,.

From Lemma
2.7
, we have the finite sum

\displaystyle z_{1}^{-\mathscr{S}}z_{2}^{\mathscr{S}}(a\otimes c)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\sum_{l\geq 0}\sum_{1\leq i_{1},\cdots,i_{l}\leq L}\frac{1}{l!}(\zeta_{2}-\zeta_{1})^{l}

\displaystyle\times(\phi^{n}_{i_{1}}\cdots\phi^{n}_{i_{l}})(a_{j})\otimes z_{1}^{-\sum_{i=1}^{L}d^{\prime}_{i,j}\psi_{i}}z_{2}^{\sum_{i=1}^{L}d^{\prime}_{i,j}\psi_{i}}(\psi_{i_{1}}\cdots\psi_{i_{l}})(c)\,.

As we mention before, the finiteness condition of the sum is independent of
c
.
Hence, there exists
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that if
r\geq M
then for all
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
c\in V

(\mu_{(r)}\otimes I)(D_{z_{2}}^{l}(z_{1}^{-\mathscr{S}_{13}}z_{2}^{\mathscr{S}_{13}})(a\otimes b\otimes c))=0\,.

Replacing
N=M
in identity (
2.9
) gives us the proposition.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

In
[
BV2
, Sect. 4.3]
, we found an example where
\mathscr{S}
is locally finite but can not be expressed as a sum
\sum\phi_{i}\otimes\psi_{i}
where each component

is locally finite on

. In this case, the integer

necessary for the locality of the fields depends on

and

(see also
[

The next theorem gives an equivalent definition of a braided logVA.

Theorem 2.10
.

A braided logarithmic vertex algebra is a vector space
V
, equipped with a vector
{\boldsymbol{1}}\in V
, an endomorphism
T\in\End(V)
, a linear map

Y\colon V\to\QF(V)\,,\qquad a\mapsto Y(a,z)\,,

and a braiding map
\mathscr{S}
on
V
,
which are subject to the following axioms:

(
i
)

Y({\boldsymbol{1}},z)=I
,
Y(a,z){\boldsymbol{1}}\in V[\![z]\!]
,
Y(a,z){\boldsymbol{1}}\big|_{z=0}=a
,
T{\boldsymbol{1}}=\mathscr{S}({\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes a)=0
.

(
ii
)

[T,Y(a,z)]=\partial_{z}Y(a,z)
.

(
iii
)

\mathscr{S}=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\phi_{i}\otimes\psi_{i}
where
\phi_{i},\psi_{i}
are locally finite on
V
.

(
iv
)

\mathscr{S}(Y(z)\otimes I)=(Y(z)\otimes I)(\mathscr{S}_{13}+\mathscr{S}_{23})
.

(
v
)
For every
a,b\in V
there exists
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that
\forall c\in V

(2.12)

\begin{split}Y(z_{1})&(I\otimes Y(z_{2}))\iota_{z_{1},z_{2}}(z_{12}^{N+\mathscr{S}})(a\otimes b)\otimes c\\
&=Y(z_{2})(I\otimes Y(z_{1}))\iota_{z_{2},z_{1}}(z_{12}^{N+\mathscr{S}})(b\otimes a)\otimes c.\end{split}

Proof.

We have that braided logVAs satisfy the above conditions, by Propositions
2.3
,
2.4
,
2.5
and
2.8
. The other direction follows from Proposition
3.8
in the next section.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

(i) Follows from Theorem

(ii) Following

]
, we define

\mathscr{S}(a_{i}\otimes a_{j})=s_{ij}a_{i}\otimes a_{j}

First, we define the subsets

Note that

Now,

For

V=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in Q}V_{\alpha}\,.

Second, we define
\Delta\colon Q\times Q\rightarrow\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{Z}
by

(2.14)

\Delta(I_{i},I_{j}):=s_{ij}\mod\mathbb{Z}\,.

Note that the map

is well defined by the construction of

. The map

is symmetric because

is symmetric, and

is bilinear with respect to the group structure (

It is easy to see that the axioms of a braided logVA give us the axioms of a generalized vertex algebra.
∎

4.
Poisson Limits and Non-linear Schrödinger Non-local PVA

In this section, first we prove Theorem
1.4
. Then, we build the
generalized vertex algebra defined by (
1.4
) and we prove that its associated non-local PVA is
the non-linear Schrödinger PVA.

4.1.
Poisson limits

We start by reviewing some definitions; for more details, we refer to
[
K
,
DK
,
BV2
]
.

Definition 4.1
.

A
non-local Lie conformal algebra
(abbreviated non-local LCA) is a
\mathbb{C}[\partial]
-module
\mathcal{V}
equipped with an admissible
\lambda
-bracket

\{\cdot_{\lambda}\cdot\}\colon\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}(\!(\lambda^{-1})\!)\,,

satisfying the following axioms:

(sesqui-linearity)

\displaystyle\quad\{\partial a_{\lambda}b\}=-\lambda\{a_{\lambda}b\}\,,\quad\{a_{\lambda}\partial b\}=(\lambda+\partial)\{a_{\lambda}b\}\,,

(skew-symmetry)

\displaystyle\quad\{b_{\lambda}a\}=-\{a_{-\lambda-\partial}b\}\,,

(Jacobi identity)

\displaystyle\quad\{a_{\lambda}\{b_{\mu}c\}\}=\{\{a_{\lambda}b\}_{\lambda+\mu}c\}+\{b_{\mu}\{a_{\lambda}c\}\}\,.

The admissibility of a
\lambda
-bracket is a technical condition, which ensures that the compositions in the Jacobi identity are well defined.

Definition 4.2
.

A
non-local Poisson vertex algebra
(abbreviated non-local PVA) is a non-local LCA
\mathcal{V}
, which in addition has a commutative associative product

ab\in\mathcal{V}
for
a,b\in\mathcal{V}
, with a unit
1\in\mathcal{V}
, such that
\partial
is a derivation of the product and the Leibniz rule holds:

(derivation)

\displaystyle\quad\partial(ab)=(\partial a)b+a(\partial b)\,,

(Leibniz rule)

\displaystyle\quad\{a_{\lambda}bc\}=\{a_{\lambda}b\}c+b\{a_{\lambda}c\}\,.

Now we recall the main result of
[
BV2
]
.
In
[
BV2
]
, we consider an increasing
filtration
on a logarithmic vertex algebra
V
by subspaces

\{0\}\subset\F^{0}V\subset\F^{1}V\subset\F^{2}V\subset\cdots
satisfying the following properties:
{\boldsymbol{1}}\in\F^{0}V
,
T(\F^{n}V)\subset\F^{n}V
and

(4.1)

\begin{split}&\mathscr{S}(\F^{m}V\otimes\F^{n}V)\subset\F^{m+n-1}(V\otimes V):=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m+n-1}\F^{k}V\otimes\F^{m+n-1-k}V\,,\\
&\mu_{(j)}(\F^{m}V\otimes\F^{n}V)\subset\begin{cases}\F^{m+n}V\,,&j<0\,,\\
\F^{m+n-1}V\,,&j\geq 0\,,\end{cases}\end{split}

for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, where
\F^{n}V:=\{0\}
for
n<0
.
Then, by
[
BV2
, Theorem 3.3]
, the
associated graded

\gr V:=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\gr^{n}V
, where
\gr^{n}V:=\F^{n}V/\F^{n-1}V
,
has the structure of a non-local PVA defined as follows.
The unit
1\in\gr^{0}V=\F^{0}V
is
{\boldsymbol{1}}
; the derivation
\partial
on
\gr V
is induced from
T
; the commutative associative product on
\gr V
is induced from
\mu_{(-1)}
;
and the
\lambda
-bracket on
\gr V
is given by

(4.2)

\{a_{\lambda}b\}:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}\mu_{(n)}(a\otimes b)+\mu_{(-1)}\Bigl(\mathscr{S}\,\Bigl(\frac{1}{\lambda+\partial}\,a\otimes b\Bigr)\Bigr).

In
[
FB
]
, Poisson vertex algebras were associated to vertex algebras considering (algebraic) limits. In this section, we follow the same idea. First, we define ideals. An ideal
W
of a braided logVA
V
is a subspace invariant under
T,\phi_{i},\psi_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq L
) such that
\mu_{(n)}(a\otimes b)\in W
for all
a\in V
,
b\in W
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. As for ordinary vertex algebras, it is not hard to see that
V/W
has the structure of a braided logVA.

Next, we note that it is straightforward to define the notion of a braided logVA
V
over a commutative ring
R
instead of
\mathbb{C}
, so that

V
is an
R
-module and
T,\psi_{i},\phi_{i},\mu_{(n)}
(
1\leq i\leq L
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
) commute with the action of
R
on
V
. In particular, we will be
interested in the case
R=\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^{2})
.

Example 4.3
.

If
V
is a braided logVA, then
V^{\epsilon}:=V\otimes\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^{2})
is a braided logVA over
\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^{2})
, the subspace
\epsilon V^{\epsilon}
is an ideal of

,
and the quotient

.

Now we prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Since
Y^{0}=Y^{\epsilon}
mod
\epsilon
is commutative, we have

(4.3)

\begin{split}\mathscr{S}(V^{\epsilon}\otimes V^{\epsilon})&\subset\epsilon(V^{\epsilon}\otimes V^{\epsilon})\,,\\
\mu_{(j)}(V^{\epsilon}\otimes V^{\epsilon})&\subset\epsilon V^{\epsilon}\,,\quad j\geq 0\,.\end{split}

For

, we define a
\lambda
-bracket by

\{a_{\lambda}b\}:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}\mu_{(n)}(a\otimes b)+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\mu_{(-1)}\Bigl(\mathscr{S}\,\Bigl(\frac{1}{\lambda+\partial}\,a\otimes b\Bigr)\Bigr)\,\,\,\mod\epsilon\in V^{0}(\!(\lambda^{-1})\!)\,.

When

or

, we have

; hence, this descends to a well-defined

-bracket on the quotient

Now we check that the above bracket satisfies each of the conditions of Definitions

and

For instance, let us check sesqui-linearity.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon}\mu_{(n)}(\partial a\otimes b)=-\frac{1}{\epsilon}n\mu_{(n-1)}(a\otimes b)\text{ mod }\epsilon\;\text{ for }\;n\geq 1\,,

4.2.
The non-linear Schrödinger generalized vertex algebra

We start by reviewing the following example of a non-local PVA from
[

]

Definition 4.4

The
non-linear Schrödinger non-local PVA

is defined as the algebra of differential polynomials

\mathcal{V}:=\mathbb{C}[u,v,\partial u,\partial v,\dots]

with
\lambda
-bracket given by:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\{u_{\lambda}u\}

\displaystyle=v(\lambda+\partial)^{-1}v,

\displaystyle\{u_{\lambda}v\}

\displaystyle=-v(\lambda+\partial)^{-1}u,

\displaystyle\{v_{\lambda}u\}

\displaystyle=-v(\lambda+\partial)^{-1}u,

\displaystyle\{v_{\lambda}v\}

\displaystyle=u(\lambda+\partial)^{-1}u\,.

Note that we can express equivalently the non-linear Schrödinger non-local PVA using the basis
w:=u+\mathrm{i}v
,
\bar{w}:=u-\mathrm{i}v

\mathcal{V}=\mathbb{C}[w,\bar{w},w^{\prime},\bar{w}^{\prime},\dots]

(where

\mathscr{S}={\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{s})}}:=\Phi\otimes\Phi\,,\quad\Phi(w)=\mathrm{i}w\,,\quad\Phi(\bar{w})=-\mathrm{i}\bar{w}\,,

denotes the product in

(4.5)

\mu_{(n)}(a\otimes b)=0,\qquad a,b\in\{w,\bar{w}\}\,,\;\;n\geq 0\,.

We use the above relations to construct a braided logVA

. We will assume that it exists and will derive the properties that

should satisfy.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
V
be a braided logVA with elements
w,\bar{w}\in V
, whose
(n+\mathscr{S})
-th products satisfy (
4.5

\Phi\in\Der(V)
acting as
\Phi(w)=\mathrm{i}w
,
\Phi(\bar{w})=-\mathrm{i}\bar{w}
, such that
\mathscr{S}=\Phi\otimes\Phi

Y(w,z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}w_{(n+\mathscr{S})}z^{-n-1-\mathrm{i}\Phi}\,,\qquad Y(\bar{w},z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\bar{w}_{(n+\mathscr{S})}z^{-n-1+\mathrm{i}\Phi}\,

where the linear operators

,
\bar{w}_{n}:=\bar{w}_{(n+\mathscr{S})}
satisfy relations (
1.4

Proof.

The form of
Y(w,z)
and

follows from (
2.3
), using that
\mathscr{S}^{(\mathrm{n})}=0
.
From the Borcherds identity (

) with

on

,

, we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(-1)^{j}\mu_{(m-j)}(I\otimes\mu_{(k+j)})\binom{-1}{j}(w\otimes w\otimes v)=\sum_{j\geq 0}w_{(m-j+\mathscr{S})}w_{(k+j+\mathscr{S})}v\,,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(-1)^{j}\mu_{(k-j)}(I\otimes\mu_{(m+j)})(-1)^{-1}\binom{-1}{j}(w\otimes w\otimes v)=-\sum_{j\geq 0}w_{(m-j+\mathscr{S})}w_{(k+j+\mathscr{S})}v\,,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\mu_{(m+k-j)}(\mu_{(j)}\otimes I)\binom{m+iI\otimes I\otimes\Phi}{j}(w\otimes w\otimes v)=0\,,

where in the last identity we used (
4.5
). Hence, from (
1.3
) we obtain the first equation in (
1.4
).
The other two relations in (
1.4
) are derived analogously from (
1.3
) for
\bar{w}\otimes\bar{w}\otimes v
and
w\otimes\bar{w}\otimes v
,
respectively.
∎

We now use the previous results to construct
V
. We proceed similarly to the construction in
[
BV1
, Section 4.4]
, and
[
BV2
, Section 4.3]
.
We define a unital associative topological algebra
\mathcal{A}
with generators
\{w_{n},\bar{w}_{n}\,|\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
subject to the relations (
1.4
). The algebra
\mathcal{A}
admits a module
V
, generated by an element
{\boldsymbol{1}}\in V
such that
w_{n}{\boldsymbol{1}}=\bar{w}_{n}{\boldsymbol{1}}=0
for
n\geq 0
, and
V
is linearly spanned by monomials of the form

(4.6)

a^{1}_{n_{1}}\cdots a^{r}_{n_{r}}{\boldsymbol{1}}\quad\text{where}\quad n_{i}\leq-1\,,\;a^{i}\in\{w,\bar{w}\}\,,\;1\leq i\leq r\,.

Next, we define linear operators
T
and
\Phi
on
V
by
T({\boldsymbol{1}})=\Phi({\boldsymbol{1}})=0
and

(4.7)

\begin{split}&[\Phi,w_{n}]=\mathrm{i}w_{n},\quad[\Phi,\bar{w}_{n}]=-\mathrm{i}\bar{w}_{n},\\
&[T,w_{n}]=-nw_{n-1}-\mathrm{i}w_{n-1}\Phi,\quad[T,\bar{w}_{n}]=-n\bar{w}_{n-1}+\mathrm{i}\bar{w}_{n-1}\Phi,\end{split}

motivated by the hexagon identity (
1.2
) and translation covariance (
2.5
).
Additionally, we define the fields

(4.8)

w(z):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}w_{n}z^{-n-1-\mathrm{i}\Phi}\,,\qquad\bar{w}(z):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\bar{w}_{n}z^{-n-1+\mathrm{i}\Phi}\,.

We let the braiding map
\mathscr{S}=\Phi\otimes\Phi
. As before, this gives rise to a braiding map
\hat{\mathscr{S}}:=\ad(\Phi)\otimes\ad(\Phi)
on the space of fields
\QF(V)
.

Theorem 4.6
.

The fields
w(z)
,
\bar{w}(z)
are translation covariant and
\hat{\mathscr{S}}
-local, and they generate
the structure of a braided logVA on
V
.

Proof.

We verify each of the hypotheses of Theorem
3.5
. By definition we have
{\boldsymbol{1}}\in V
such that
T{\boldsymbol{1}}=0
and
\mathscr{S}({\boldsymbol{1}}\otimes a)=0
. By the Jacobi identity,
[[T,\Phi],w_{n}]=[T,[\Phi,w_{n}]]-[\Phi,[T,w_{n}]]=0
, and similarly,
[[T,\Phi],\bar{w}_{n}]=0
. Hence
[T,\Phi]=0
.
Next, we observe that by construction the fields
w(z),\bar{w}(z)
are translation covariant (cf. (
2.5
)).
We will prove that the space

\mathcal{U}=\Span\{I,w(z),\bar{w}(z)\}\subset\QF_{T}(V)

is
\hat{\mathscr{S}}
-local.
We have
z_{1}^{-\mathrm{i}\Phi}w(z_{2})=w(z_{2})z_{1}^{1-\mathrm{i}\Phi}
and
z_{1}^{\mathrm{i}\Phi}w(z_{2})=w(z_{2})z_{1}^{-1+\mathrm{i}\Phi}
.
Then using (
1.4
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\mu\bigl(\iota_{z_{1},z_{2}}\bigl(z_{12}^{\hat{\mathscr{S}}}\bigr)w(z_{1})\otimes w(z_{2})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\iota_{z_{1},z_{2}}\bigl(z_{12}^{-1}\bigr)w(z_{1})w(z_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Bigl(\sum_{j\geq 0}w_{m-j}w_{k+j}\Bigr)z^{-m-1-\mathrm{i}\Phi}_{1}z_{2}^{-k-1-\mathrm{i}\Phi}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m,k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Bigl(\sum_{j\geq 0}w_{k-j}w_{m+j}\Bigr)z^{-m-1-\mathrm{i}\Phi}_{1}z_{2}^{-k-1-\mathrm{i}\Phi}

\displaystyle=\iota_{z_{2},z_{1}}\bigl(z_{12}^{-1}\bigr)w(z_{2})w(z_{1})

\displaystyle=\mu\bigl(\iota_{z_{2},z_{1}}\bigl(z_{12}^{\hat{\mathscr{S}}}\bigr)w(z_{2})\otimes w(z_{1})\bigr)\,.

Hence, we have the locality of
w(z)
with itself as in (
3.2
) for
N=0
. The locality of
\bar{w}(z),\bar{w}(z)
and
w(z),\bar{w}(z)
is proved in a similar way.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

One can show that the braided logVA
V
in Theorem
4.6
has a PBW basis consisting of lexicographically
ordered monomials of the form (
4.6
). The proof is similar to the usual Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt Theorem for Lie algebras
(see, e.g.,
[
J
]
). In the context of logarithmic vertex algebras, a version of the PBW Theorem was proved in
[
H
]
,
while the case of quadratic associative algebras is discussed in
[
PP
]
.

Finally, following
[
FB
, Section 16.3]
, we deform the braided logVA in Theorem
4.6
by introducing a formal parameter
\epsilon
.
We consider the unital associative topological algebra
\mathcal{A}^{\epsilon}
with generators
\{w_{n},\bar{w}_{n}\,|\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
subject to the relations:

(4.9)

\begin{split}\sum_{j\geq 0}(-1)^{j}\binom{-\epsilon}{j}(w_{m-j}w_{k+j}-w_{k-j}w_{m+j})&=0\,,\\
\sum_{j\geq 0}(-1)^{j}\binom{-\epsilon}{j}(\bar{w}_{m-j}\bar{w}_{k+j}-\bar{w}_{k-j}\bar{w}_{m+j})&=0\,,\\
{w}_{m}\bar{w}_{k}-\epsilon w_{m-1}\bar{w}_{k+1}-\bar{w}_{k}{w}_{m}+\epsilon\bar{w}_{k-1}{w}_{m+1}&=0\,.\end{split}

As in Theorem
4.6
,
\mathcal{A}^{\epsilon}
has a module
V^{\epsilon}
spanned by the monomials (
4.6
).
We have operators
T,\Phi\in\End_{\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]}(V^{\epsilon})
,
fields

w^{\epsilon}(z)=\sum w_{n}z^{-n-1}e^{i\epsilon\Phi\zeta}\,,\qquad\bar{w}^{\epsilon}(z)=\sum\bar{w}_{n}z^{-n-1}e^{-i\epsilon\Phi\zeta}\,,

a braiding map
\mathscr{S}=\epsilon\Phi\otimes\Phi
, and
\hat{\mathscr{S}}:=\epsilon\ad(\Phi)\otimes\ad(\Phi)
on
\QF(V^{\epsilon})
. The same steps as in the proof of Theorem
4.6
give us that
V^{\epsilon}
is a braided logVA. Moreover,
V^{0}:=V^{\epsilon}/\epsilon V^{\epsilon}
is commutative. Then Theorem
1.4
implies that
V^{0}
has the structure of a non-local PVA. It is not hard to see that this non-local PVA is exactly the non-linear Schrödinger non-local PVA from Definition
4.4
.

Semiclassical resolvent bounds for compactly supported radial potentials
Kiril Datchev, Jeffrey Galkowski, Jacob Shapiro

Abstract
. We employ separation of variables to prove weighted resolvent estimates for the semiclassical Schrödinger operator − h 2 ​ Δ + V ⁡ ( | x | ) − E -h^{2}\Delta+V(|x|)-E in dimension n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 , where h , E > 0 h,\,E>0 , and V : [ 0 , ∞ ) → ℝ V:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R} is L ∞ L^{\infty} and compactly supported. The weighted resolvent norm grows no faster than exp ⁡ ( C ​ h − 1 ) \exp(Ch^{-1}) , while an exterior weighted norm grows ∼ h − 1 \sim h^{-1} . We introduce a new method based on the Mellin transform to handle the two-dimensional case.



1.
Introduction and statement of results

Let
\Delta\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\partial^{2}_{x_{j}}
be the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. We consider the semiclassical Schrödinger operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
given by

Pu=P(h)u\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-h^{2}\Delta u+V(|x|)u,

where
V\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
is
L^{\infty}
and compactly supported, and
h>0
is a semiclassical parameter. Then
P\colon H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is self-adjoint, and the resolvent
(P-z)^{-1}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. Throughout the article, we let
r\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|x|
, and
\langle x\rangle\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(1+|x|^{2})^{1/2}
.

Our first result is an exponential upper bound on the limiting absorption resolvent.

Theorem 1
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Fix
[E_{\emph{min}},E_{\emph{max}}]\subseteq(0,\infty)
and
1/2<s\leq 1
. There exist
C,\,h_{0}>0
, such that

(1.1)

\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}(P-E-i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq e^{C/h},

for all
\varepsilon>0
,
h\in(0,h_{0}]
, and
E\in[E_{\emph{min}},E_{\emph{max}}]
.

In addition, we prove a ‘non-trapping’ type estimate for the resolvent in the exterior of a large ball. Let

R_{0}=R_{0}(V)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sup\{r\in[0,\infty)\,:\,r\in\mathop{\rm ess\,supp}V\},

and define

(1.2)

\begin{split}M_{0}&=M_{0}(V,E)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\\
&\inf\{m>0\mid V(r)+mr^{-2}-E\geq 0,\text{ for almost all }r\text{ in a neighborhood of $(0,R_{0}(V)]$}\}.\end{split}

For example, if
V
is the characteristic function of the interval
(0,R_{0}]
, then
M_{0}=ER^{2}_{0}
. If
V
is continuous at
R_{0}
,
M_{0}=\mathop{\rm ess\,sup}_{[0,R_{0}]}r^{2}(E-V)
. Note that always
M_{0}\geq ER_{0}^{2}
because we require
M_{0}r^{-2}-E\geq 0
for some
r>R_{0}
.
Finally, put

(1.3)

R_{1}=R_{1}(V,E)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sqrt{M_{0}(V,E)/E}.

Theorem 2
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Fix
[E_{\emph{min}},E_{\emph{max}}]\subseteq(0,\infty)
,
1/2<s\leq 1
and
R>\sup_{E\in[E_{\min},E_{\max}]}R_{1}(V,E)
. There exist
C,\,h_{0}>0
, such that

(1.4)

\|\langle x\rangle^{-s}\mathbf{1}_{\geq R}(P-E-i\varepsilon)^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{\geq R}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\frac{C}{h},

for all
\varepsilon>0
,
h\in(0,h_{0}]
, and
E\in[E_{\emph{min}},E_{\emph{max}}]
, where
\mathbf{1}_{\geq R}
is the characteristic function of
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon|x|\geq R\}
.

Theorem
2
is optimal in the sense that
[
DaJi20
, Theorem 3]
shows that (
1.4
) is false in general when
R<\sqrt{M_{0}/E}
. For example, if
V\in C_{0}^{\infty}([0,1))
,
E<\max(-r^{2}V)
, and
R<\sqrt{M_{0}/E}
, then (by
[
DaJi20
, (2.9) and (4.10)]
) the left hand side of (
1.4
) is bounded below by
e^{1/Ch}
for
h
tending to zero along a sequence of positive values.

The novelty of Theorems
1
and
2
is that they bound the weighted resolvent for an arbitrary compactly supported, radial
L^{\infty}
potential. The
h
-dependencies on the right sides of (
1.4
) and (
1.1
) are sharp in general, see
[
DDZ15
]
and
[
DaJi20
]
for exponential lower bounds, and recall that the free resolvent (
V\equiv 0
) has a
Ch^{-1}
lower bound (to see this, consider
u=e^{i\sqrt{E}h^{-1}x_{1}}\chi(x)
for some
0\not\equiv\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
).

Vodev’s work
[
Vo21
]
shows, for dimension
n\geq 3
, a bound like (
1.1
) still holds for radial potentials decaying like
\langle r\rangle^{-\delta}
,
\delta>2
, except with the right side replaced by
e^{Ch^{-4/3}}
(bounds with additional losses hold for

decaying more slowly). For

not necessarily radial,

,

, the best known weighted resolvent upper bound is
e^{Ch^{-4/3}\log(h^{-1})}

[
GaSh20
]
. In dimension

, it is an open problem to determine the optimal

-dependence of the resolvent for

. In contrast, when

, an

bound holds even if
V\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})

[

]
. As far as the authors are aware, Theorem

potentials in dimension higher than one.

,

,

Stronger bounds on the resolvent are known when
V\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R})
and conditions are imposed on the classical flow
\Phi(t)=\exp t(2\xi\partial_{x}-\partial_{x}V(x)\partial_{\xi})
(note that

may be undefined in our case). The key dynamical object is the
trapped set

\mathcal{K}(E)
at energy

, defined as the set of

such that

and

showed the weighted resolvent is bounded by

. We may thus think of (

) due to trapping.

Theorem

allows one to obtain, at high frequency, bounds on and resonance free regions for, the meromorphic continuation of the cutoff resolvent of the operator
-c^{2}(|x|)\Delta
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},c^{-2}dx)
. Here

is called the

and satisfies

(1.5)

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm supp}(1-c)\text{ is compact},

(1.6)

\displaystyle c_{0}<c(r)<c_{1}\text{ for some $c_{0},c_{1}>0$ and for all $r\in[0,\infty)$.}

More precisely, one obtains,

Theorem 3
.

Let
n\geq 2
, and suppose

obeys (
1.5
) and (
1.6
). For each
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

such that the cutoff resolvent

\chi R(\lambda)\chi\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\chi(-c^{2}\Delta-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi

into the set

\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|>M,\,\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda>-e^{C_{2}|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|}\}
, where it satisfies the bound

(1.7)

\|\chi R(\lambda)\chi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\,c^{-2}dx)\to L^{2}{(\mathbb{R}^{n},\,c^{-2}dx)}}\leq e^{C_{1}|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|}.

The proof of Theorem

is the same as the proof of
[
Sh18

,

Theorem

implies logarithmic local energy decay for the wave equation

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}^{2}-c^{2}(x)\Delta)u(x,t)=0,&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty),\,n\geq 2,\\

where the initial data are compactly supported. Such a decay rate was first proved by Burq
[

]
for

The assumption that

outside of a compact set is necessary not only to establish (

1.1.
Ideas from the proofs

When
V:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
has limited regularity, proofs of resolvent estimates typically proceed by a modified positive commutator strategy, see e.g.,
[
GaSh20
, Section 2]
. The potential is treated as a perturbation because it cannot be differentiated in some or all directions. The issue one needs to overcome is that, in the positive commutator scheme, the potential appears in a term without an apparent sign (even though
V
is real-valued), which in turn is difficult to control as
h\to 0^{+}
. Controlling this term results in an estimate from above by
e^{Ch^{-4/3}\log(h^{-1})}
.

Due to the work of Meshkov
[
Me92
]
on the Landis conjecture, one knows that the exponent
h^{-4/3}
is optimal for compactly supported complex valued potentials (see Appendix
C
for an explanation). Therefore, any improvement upon the exponent
h^{-4/3}
in the resolvent estimate for an
L^{\infty}
compactly supported potential must involve additional assumptions on the potential
V
(e.g. reality/radiality).

The radial symmetry of the potential is used in two ways. First, it allows us to study the high angular momenta separately from the low angular momenta. In particular, we use a spherical energy type estimate to obtain resolvent estimates for low frequencies. Second, decomposition by angular momentum enables us to take advantage of reality of
V
using ODE techniques. In particular, for large enough angular momenta
m_{j}
, reality of
V
yields useful monotonicity properties of certain solutions
u_{0}
and
u_{1}
to
(-h^{2}\partial^{2}_{r}+V+m_{j}r^{-2}-E)u=0
(see their construction in Section
2
). Control of
u_{0}
and
u_{1}
gives a bound on the integral kernel of
(-h^{2}\partial^{2}_{r}+V+mr^{-2}-E-i0)^{-1}
, which together with WKB and Bessel function asymptotics, yields the sharp semiclassical resolvent estimates.

We remark that, in the setting of Theorem
1
, our ODE methods do not yield estimates on
u_{0}
and
u_{1}
for small
m_{j}
. However, we are able to prove good enough resolvent estimates for these frequencies.
In doing so, we prove the following proposition.

Proposition 1.1
.

Fix
[E_{\min},E_{\max}]\subseteq(0,\infty)
and
1/2<s\leq 1
. There exist
C,\,h_{0}>0
such that for
0\leq\varepsilon\leq 1
,
h\in(0,h_{0}]
,
m\geq-h^{2}/4
, and
E\in[E_{\min},E_{\max}]
,

(1.9)

\|\langle r\rangle^{-s}(-h^{2}\partial^{2}_{r}+V+mr^{-2}-E-i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle r\rangle^{-s}\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)\to L^{2}(0,\infty)}\leq e^{C(1+|m|^{1/2})/h}.

Proposition
1.1
is motivated by
[
Vo21
, Proposition 3.1]
where a similar estimate is proved for
m\geq 0
. However, in order to handle the case of
n=2
, we need to extend these resolvent estimates to
m\geq-h^{2}/4
. The proof of Proposition
1.1
utilizes methods inspired by the b-calculus from microlocal analysis to estimate
u
by
(-h^{2}\partial_{r}^{2}+V+mr^{-2}-E)u
near
0
, and then employs a spherical energy method to handle the region away from zero.

We expect Theorems
1
and
2
still hold for potentials
V
which are radial, real and non-compactly supported, with sufficient decay toward infinity. A difficulty with treating this case is finding a suitable replacement for the WKB and Bessel function asymptotics we use in Section
3
. A development in this direction is that, since we posted this article as a preprint, Vodev
[
Vo22
]
designed a new Carleman estimate for
n\geq 3
, large angular momenta, and radial
V=O(\langle r\rangle)^{-\delta}
,
\delta>4
; it yields improved resolvent bounds compared to
[
Vo21
]
, and an alternative proof of (
1.1
).

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we give an overview of the plan to prove Theorems
1
and
2
via separation of variables. In Section
3
we use WKB and Bessel function asymptotics to prove Theorem
2
. In Section
4
we use the Mellin transform and energy estimates to prove an exponential estimate which is optimal for low angular momenta but not for high ones. In Section
5
we use ODE analysis to remove the losses for high angular momenta and complete the proof of Theorem
1
.

2.
Reduction to a family of one dimensional resovlent estimates

In this section, we use separation of variables to reduce the proofs of Theorems
1
and
2
to a family of one dimensional resolvent estimates. We begin by recalling the conjugation

r^{(n-1)/2}(-\Delta)r^{-(n-1)/2}=-\partial^{2}_{r}-\frac{\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}{r^{2}}+\frac{(n-1)(n-3)}{4r^{2}}.

We then put

m_{j}=h^{2}(\sigma_{j}+4^{-1}(n-1)(n-3)),

where
0=\sigma_{0}<\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}\leq\sigma_{3}\leq\cdots

\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

k^{2}+(n-2)k

\mathbf{Y}_{0},\ \mathbf{Y}_{1},\dots

P_{m}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-h^{2}\partial^{2}_{r}+V(r)+mr^{-2},\qquad m\geq-\frac{h^{2}}{4}.

The operator
P_{m}

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

P_{m}
commutes with complex conjugation, by von Neumann’s theorem it has equal defect indices, and thus has self adjoint extensions. Elements in the domain

is

\mathcal{D}_{m}=\begin{cases}\big\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):P_{m}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),\,r^{-\nu-\tfrac{1}{2}}f\in L^{\infty}\text{ near $r=0$}\big\}&\tfrac{-h^{2}}{4}\leq m<\tfrac{3h^{2}}{4},\\
\big\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):P_{m}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\big\}&m\geq\tfrac{3h^{2}}{4}.\end{cases}

See
[
NiZe92
, Section 6, Example I]
.

We need only verify (
2.11
) for
u=\mathbf{Y}_{j}v
with
v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, as such functions have dense linear span in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In this case, the right side of (
2.11
) reduces to

(2.13)

\mathbf{Y}_{j}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{-(n-1)/2}K(r,r^{\prime};m_{j},E,\varepsilon,h)(r^{\prime})^{(n-1)/2}v(r^{\prime})dr^{\prime}.

To show (
2.13
) and
(P-E-i\varepsilon)^{-1}\mathbf{Y}_{j}v
coincide, we check that applying
P-E-i\varepsilon
, respectively
\Delta
, to (
2.13
) in the sense of distributions, results in
\mathbf{Y}_{j}v
, respectively some function in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Both computations are handled by integrating by parts in polar coordinates, on domains of the form
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|>\delta>0\}
, and sending
\delta\to 0
. All boundary terms that appear in the calculation vanish as
\delta\to 0
, thanks to (
2.5
) and (
2.6
). We leave the remaining details to the reader.

∎

We next consider the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Recall that
[
Ag75
, Theorem 4.2]
for any
s>1/2
, and
E\in[E_{\min},E_{\max}]
, the limit

(2.14)

\langle x\rangle^{-s}(P-E-i0)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\langle x\rangle^{-s}(P-E-i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}

exists in the uniform topology
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By (
2.11
), (
2.10
), and because
K(r,r^{\prime};m_{j},E,\varepsilon,h)
depends continuously on
(r,r^{\prime},E,\varepsilon)
varying in compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[E_{\text{min}},E_{\text{max}}]\times[0,\infty)
, for each
j
and
u,v\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,

\begin{split}\langle\mathbf{Y}_{j}u,\langle&x\rangle^{-s}(P-E-i0)^{-1}\langle x\rangle^{-s}\mathbf{Y}_{j}v\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\langle r^{(n-1)/2}u,\langle r\rangle^{-s}\int_{0}^{\infty}K(r,r^{\prime};m_{j},E,\varepsilon,h)\langle r^{\prime}\rangle^{-s}(r^{\prime})^{(n-1)/2}v(r^{\prime})dr^{\prime}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\\
&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\langle r^{(n-1)/2}u,\langle r\rangle^{-s}(P_{m_{j}}-E-i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle r^{\prime}\rangle^{-s}(r^{\prime})^{(n-1)/2}v(r^{\prime})\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\\
&=\langle r^{(n-1)/2}u,\langle r\rangle^{-s}\int_{0}^{\infty}K(r,r^{\prime};m_{j},E,0,h)\langle r^{\prime}\rangle^{-s}(r^{\prime})^{(n-1)/2}v(r^{\prime})dr^{\prime}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}.\end{split}

Thus, as functions
\{\mathbf{Y}_{j}v:j\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\,v\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\}
have dense linear span in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, to bound the norm of (
2.14
) on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it suffices to bound, uniformly in
j
, the kernel
K(r,r^{\prime};m_{j},E,0,h)
, or alternatively,
\|\langle r\rangle^{-s}(P_{m_{j}}-E-i\varepsilon)^{-1}\langle r\rangle^{-s}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}
for
\varepsilon>0
small.

If the bounds (
1.1
) and (
1.4
) are established for
\varepsilon=0
, it is well-known that one can use resolvent identities to show they hold for
\varepsilon>0
as well. We omit the proof of this but refer the reader to the relevant results, see
[
BrPe00
, Proposition 3]
and
[
Vo14
, Theorem 1.5]
.

3.
Exterior estimates

Theorem
2
follows from the stronger statement that, for any
R>\sup_{E\in[E_{\min},E_{\max}]}R_{1}(V,E)
(see (
1.3
)), we have

(3.1)

|K(r,r^{\prime})|=|K(r,r^{\prime},m,E,0,h)|\leq\frac{C}{h},

uniformly for
r
and
r^{\prime}
in
[R,\infty)
obeying
r\leq r^{\prime}
,
m\geq-h^{2}/4
,
h
small, and
E\in[E_{\max},E_{\min}]
. We prove (
3.1
) in this section, over the course of two lemmas. The first lemma establishes (
3.1
) for
m\leq M_{+}
, where

(3.2)

M_{+}=M_{+}(E)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=M_{0}+\frac{E(R^{2}-R^{2}_{1})}{2}.

In the second lemma, we prove (
3.1
) for
m>M_{+}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There are
C
and
h_{0}
such that (
3.1
) holds for all
r
and
r^{\prime}
in
[R,\infty)
obeying
r\leq r^{\prime}
,
h\in(0,h_{0}]
,
E\in[E_{\min},E_{\max}]
and for all
m\in[-h^{2}/4,M_{+}]
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as the one in
[
DaJi20
, Lemma 1]
. It is based on WKB or Liouville-Green asymptotics for solutions to an ODE with real coefficients in a region with no turning point. First, observe that if
r\geq R
and
m\leq M_{+}
, then

mr^{-2}\leq\frac{2M_{0}+E(R^{2}-R^{2}_{1})}{2r^{2}}\leq\frac{2ER^{2}_{1}+E(R^{2}-R^{2}_{1})}{2R^{2}}=E-\frac{E(R^{2}-R^{2}_{1})}{2R^{2}}.

Thus
E-mr^{-2}
is bounded below by a positive constant uniformly in
E
,
r
and
m
.

By
[
Ol
, Section 6.2.4]
, there are real numbers
A=A(h)
and
B=B(h)
such that, for
r\geq R
we have

u_{0}(r)=\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{E-mr^{-2}}}\Big(\sum_{\pm}(A\pm iB)\exp\Big(\pm\frac{i}{h}\int_{R}^{r}\sqrt{E-ms^{-2}}ds\Big)(1+\varepsilon_{\pm}(r))\Big),

where
\varepsilon_{+}
and
\varepsilon_{-}
satisfy

|\varepsilon_{\pm}(r)|+h|\varepsilon_{\pm}^{\prime}(r)|\lesssim\frac{h}{r^{3}}.

By
[
Ol
, Section 6.2.4]
, there is a constant
D=D(h)
so that for
r\geq R
,

u_{1}(r)=\frac{D}{\sqrt[4]{E-mr^{-2}}}\exp\Big(\frac{i}{h}\int_{R}^{r}\sqrt{E-ms^{-2}}ds\Big)(1+\varepsilon_{+}(r)),

where we rule out the presence of an
\exp\Big(-\frac{i}{h}\int_{R}^{r}\sqrt{E-ms^{-2}}ds\Big)
term by using large-argument Bessel function asymptotics
[
DLMF
, 10.17.5 and 10.17.11]

to show that

\partial_{r}u_{1}-i\frac{\sqrt{E}}{h}u_{1}\to 0,\qquad\text{as $r\to\infty$}.

Next we compute the Wronskian

W=\frac{D}{\sqrt{E-mr^{-2}}}\Big(A-iB\Big)\frac{2i}{h}\sqrt{E-mr^{-2}}\Big(1+O(hr^{-3})\Big)=\frac{2D(B+iA)}{h},

where we dropped the remainder because
W
is independent of
r
. Plugging these formulas for
u_{0}
,
u_{1}
, and
W
into the formula (
2.9
) for
K
gives the conclusion.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

There are
C
and
h_{0}
such that (
3.1
) holds for all
r
and
r^{\prime}
in
[R_{0},\infty)
obeying
r\leq r^{\prime}
,
h\in(0,h_{0}]
,
E\in[E_{\min},E_{\max}]
, and for all
m>M_{+}
.

Proof.

By the Bessel function differential equation (
A.1
), there are real numbers
A
and
B
such that, for
r\geq R_{0}
,

u_{0}(r)=r^{1/2}(AJ_{\nu}(\nu z)+BY_{\nu}(\nu z)),

where

\nu=h^{-1}(m+\tfrac{1}{4}h^{2})^{1/2},\qquad z=(m+\tfrac{1}{4}h^{2})^{-1/2}E^{1/2}r.

(Note that the constants
A
and
B
here are analogous to but different from the ones from Lemma
3.1
.) Recall from (
2.7
) that

u_{1}(r)=r^{1/2}(J_{\nu}(\nu z)+iY_{\nu}(\nu z)).

By the Bessel function Wronskian formula (
A.2
),

W=2\pi^{-1}(iA-B).

To bound
B
in terms of
A
we use the fact that
u_{0}(R_{0}),\,u^{\prime}_{0}(R_{0})\geq 0
which follows from

V+mr^{-2}-E\geq 0
on
(0,R_{0}]
; see Lemma
B.1
. By the Bessel function bounds (
A.7
), for
h
small enough, we have

B\leq\frac{J_{\nu}(\nu z_{0})}{-Y_{\nu}(\nu z_{0})}A\lesssim e^{-2\nu\xi_{0}}A,\qquad-B\leq\frac{J_{\nu}(\nu z_{0})+2R_{0}J^{\prime}_{\nu}(\nu z_{0})}{Y_{\nu}(\nu z_{0})+2R_{0}Y^{\prime}_{\nu}(\nu z_{0})}A\lesssim e^{-2\nu\xi_{0}}A

where

\xi_{0}=\int_{z_{0}}^{1}t^{-1}(1-t^{2})^{1/2}dt,\qquad z_{0}=(m+\tfrac{1}{4}h^{2})^{-1/2}E^{1/2}R_{0}.

Note that
z_{0}<1
because

z_{0}\leq M_{+}^{-1/2}E^{1/2}R_{0}=\left(\frac{M_{0}}{ER^{2}_{0}}+\frac{R^{2}-R^{2}_{0}}{2R^{2}_{0}}\right)^{-1/2}\leq\left(1+\frac{R^{2}-R^{2}_{0}}{2R^{2}_{0}}\right)^{-1/2}.

Then, when
R_{0}\leq r\leq r^{\prime}
, letting
z^{\prime}=(m+\tfrac{1}{4}h^{2})^{-1/2}E^{1/2}r^{\prime}
and inserting
|B|\lesssim e^{-2\nu\xi_{0}}A
and the Bessel function bound (
A.10
) into (
2.9
) gives

(3.3)

\begin{split}|K(r,r^{\prime})|&\lesssim h^{-2}(rr^{\prime})^{1/2}(J_{\nu}(\nu z)+e^{-2\nu\xi_{0}}|Y_{\nu}(\nu z)|)|J_{\nu}(\nu z^{\prime})+iY_{\nu}(\nu z^{\prime})|\\
&\lesssim h^{-2}\nu^{-1}(rr^{\prime})^{1/2}\langle z\rangle^{-1/2}\langle z^{\prime}\rangle^{-1/2}\lesssim h^{-1},\end{split}

as desired.

∎

4.
One dimensional resolvent estimates for fixed angular momentum

We now study the equation

(P_{m}-E-i\varepsilon)u=(-h^{2}\partial_{r}^{2}+V(r)+mr^{-2}-E-i\varepsilon)u=f,\qquad\quad m\geq-\frac{h^{2}}{4},

on
L^{2}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=L^{2}(0,\infty)
. Recall that the self-adjoint extension of
(P_{m},C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
we employ is its Friedrichs extension
(P_{m},\mathcal{D}_{m})
. Put
\mathcal{D}_{\text{comp},m}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{u\in\mathcal{D}_{m}:\mathop{\rm supp}u\text{ is compact in $[0,\infty)$}\}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
[E_{\min},E_{\max}]\subseteq(0,\infty)
and
1/2<s\leq 1
. Then there is
C,\,h_{0}>0
such that for all
E\in[E_{\min},E_{\max}]
,
h\in(0,h_{0}]
,
m\geq-h^{2}/4
,
0\leq\varepsilon\leq 1
, and
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\emph{comp},m}
,

\|\langle r\rangle^{-s}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq e^{C(1+|m|^{1/2})/h}\|\langle r\rangle^{s}(P_{m}-E-i\varepsilon)u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}.

To prove Lemma
4.1
, we start by studying the operator

Q_{m}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-\partial_{r}^{2}+mh^{-2}r^{-2},\qquad m\geq-\frac{h^{2}}{4},

on functions compactly supported in
[0,\infty)
. We recall the Mellin transform and its inverse:

\mathcal{M}(u)(\sigma):=\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{i\sigma}u(r)\frac{dr}{r},\qquad\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-1}(v)(r):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}r^{-i\sigma}v(\sigma)d\tau,\qquad\sigma=\tau+it,

where the definitions hold initially, e.g., for
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
v((\cdot)+it)\in L^{1}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R})
, and then extend by density to bounded operators
\mathcal{M}:L^{2}(0,\infty;r^{-2t-1}dr)\to L^{2}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R})
,

\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-1}:L^{2}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(0,\infty;r^{-2t-1}dr)
. Moreover, since
\mathcal{M}(u)(\tau+it)=2\pi\mathcal{F}^{-1}(e^{-tx}u(e^{x}))(\tau)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathcal{M}^{-1}_{t}(v)(r)=r^{t}\mathcal{F}(v)(\log r)/2\pi
, where
\mathcal{F}
denotes Fourier transform,

(4.1)

\begin{gathered}\|\mathcal{M}(u)(\tau+it)\|_{L^{2}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R})}=(2\pi)^{1/2}\|r^{-t-1/2}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})},\\
\|r^{-t-1/2}\mathcal{M}_{t}^{-1}(v)(r)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}=(2\pi)^{-1/2}\|v\|_{L^{2}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R})}.\end{gathered}

Let

t_{\pm}=t_{\pm}(m):=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1+4mh^{-2}}}{2},\qquad\Lambda(t,m):=|t^{2}-t-h^{-2}m|^{-1},\qquad t\neq t_{\pm}.

Lemma 4.2
.

There is
C>0
such that for
m\geq-h^{2}/4
,
N\in\mathbb{R}
,
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{t_{+}(m),t_{-}(m)\}
, and
u\in r^{-N}L^{2}_{\operatorname{comp}}[0,\infty)
with
Q_{m}u\in r^{t_{0}-\frac{3}{2}}L^{2}
, we have

(4.2)

u=\Pi_{t_{0}}(r^{2}Q_{m}u)+E_{t_{0}}(r^{2}Q_{m}u),

where

(4.3)

\|r^{-t_{0}-\frac{1}{2}}E_{t_{0}}v\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\Lambda(t_{0},m)\|r^{-{t_{0}}-\frac{1}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})},

and

\Pi_{t_{0}}v=\begin{cases}0&t_{0}<t_{-},\\
r^{\frac{1}{2}}\log r\mathcal{M}(v)(\tfrac{i}{2})-r^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathcal{M}(\log rv)(\tfrac{i}{2})&t_{-}<t_{0},\,m=-\tfrac{h^{2}}{4},\\
\frac{r^{t_{-}}\mathcal{M}(v)(it_{-})}{t_{-}-t_{+}}&t_{-}<t_{0}<t_{+},\,m>-\tfrac{h^{2}}{4},\\
\frac{r^{t_{-}}\mathcal{M}(v)(it_{-})}{t_{-}-t_{+}}+\frac{r^{t_{+}}\mathcal{M}(v)(it_{+})}{t_{+}-t_{-}}&t_{+}<t_{0},\,m>-\tfrac{h^{2}}{4}.\end{cases}

Remark:
Applying
(r\partial_{r})^{j}
,
j=1,\,2
, to the expression for
E_{t_{0}}v
, see (
4.6
) below, yields a strengthening of (
4.3
), namely

\|r^{-t_{0}-\frac{1}{2}}(r\partial_{r})^{j}E_{t_{0}}v\|_{L^{2}}\leq\Lambda_{j}(t_{0},m)\|r^{-{t_{0}}-\frac{1}{2}}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})},\qquad j=0,\,1,\,2,

However, we omit the proof since in the sequel we do not need the estimates for
j=1,\,2
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we take
N
positive and large enough so that
-N<t_{0},\,t_{+},\,t_{-}
. Since
u\in r^{-N}L_{\operatorname{comp}}^{2}
and
r^{2}Q_{m}u\in r^{t_{0}+\frac{1}{2}}L^{2}_{\operatorname{comp}}
,
\mathcal{M}(u)(\sigma)
is holomorphic in
\operatorname{Im}\sigma<-N-1/2
, while
\mathcal{M}(r^{2}Q_{m}u)(\sigma)
, is holomorphic in
\operatorname{Im}\sigma<t_{0}
and extends continuously (as an
L^{2}
function) to
\operatorname{Im}\sigma=t_{0}
. In addition,

(4.4)

\mathcal{M}(r^{2}Q_{m}u)(\sigma)=(\sigma^{2}-i\sigma+mh^{-2})\mathcal{M}(u)(\sigma),\qquad\operatorname{Im}\sigma<-N-\frac{1}{2}.

In particular, (
4.4
) implies
\mathcal{M}{u}(\sigma)\in L^{1}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R})
for
\operatorname{Im}\sigma<-N-1/2
, so by Fourier inversion,

(4.5)

u(r)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\operatorname{Im}\sigma=-N-1}\frac{r^{-i\sigma}\mathcal{M}(r^{2}Q_{m}u)(\sigma)}{\sigma^{2}-i\sigma+mh^{-2}}d\sigma.

We now deform the contour to
\operatorname{Im}\sigma=t_{0}-\epsilon
and the send
\epsilon\to 0
. From (
4.5
),

u(r)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{\gamma_{R,-N-1}}\frac{r^{-i\sigma}\mathcal{M}(r^{2}Q_{m}u)(\sigma)}{\sigma^{2}-i\sigma+mh^{-2}}d\sigma,\qquad\gamma_{R,t}=\{\tau+it\,:\,\tau\in[-R,R]\}.

Next, observe that since, in
\operatorname{Im}\sigma<t_{0}
,
\|\mathcal{M}(r^{2}Q_{m}u)(\sigma)\|_{L_{\tau}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
is independent of
\operatorname{Re}\sigma
,

\Big|\int_{\gamma_{\pm,R,-N,\epsilon}}\frac{r^{-i\sigma}\mathcal{M}(r^{2}Q_{m}u)(\sigma)}{\sigma^{2}-i\sigma+mh^{-2}}d\sigma\Big|\leq\frac{C_{\epsilon}}{R^{2}}\to 0,\qquad\text{as $R\to\infty$.}

where

\gamma_{\pm,R,-N,\epsilon}:=\{\pm R+it\,:\,t\in[-N-1,t_{0}-\epsilon]\}.

In particular, using
t_{0}\neq t_{\pm}(m)
and that
\mathcal{M}(r^{2}Q_{m}u)(\tau+it)
varies continuously in
L^{2}_{\tau}(\mathbb{R})
for
t\leq t_{0}
, we send
\epsilon\to 0
to obtain

(4.6)

\begin{split}u(r)&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\operatorname{Im}\sigma=t_{0}}\frac{r^{-i\sigma}\mathcal{M}(r^{2}Q_{m}u)(\sigma)}{\sigma^{2}-i\sigma+mh^{-2}}d\sigma+i\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma^{2}-i\sigma+mh^{-2}=0\\
\operatorname{Im}\sigma<t_{0}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{Res}\Big(r^{-i\sigma}\frac{\mathcal{M}(r^{2}Q_{m}u)(\sigma)}{\sigma^{2}-i\sigma+mh^{-2}}\Big)\\
&=:E_{t_{0}}(r^{2}Q_{m}u)+\Pi_{t_{0}}(r^{2}Q_{m}u).\end{split}

The formula for
\Pi_{t_{0}}
follows from calculating residues at
t_{\pm}(m)
, while the bound on
E_{t_{0}}
follows from minimizing the modulus

|(\tau+it_{0})^{2}-i(\tau+it_{0})+mh^{-2}|^{2}=(-t_{0}^{2}+t_{0}+mh^{-2}+\tau^{2})^{2}+\tau^{2}(2t_{0}-1)^{2},

with respect to
\tau
.

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
m>0
and
u\in\mathcal{D}_{m}
. Then
r^{-1}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Proof.

Observe that there exists
C>0
so that,

m\|r^{-1}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}^{2}+|\langle P_{m}u,u\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}|,\qquad u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

As
(P_{m},\mathcal{D}_{m})
is the Friedrichs extension of
(P_{m},C_{0}^{\infty}(0,\infty))
, by
[
ReSi
, Theorem X.23]
, any

has a sequence
\{\psi_{k}\}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that
\psi_{k}\to u
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
\langle P_{m}\psi_{k},\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\to\langle P_{m}u,u\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}
. We also get pointwise almost everywhere convergence by extracting a subsequence of the
\psi_{k}
, which we again just call
\psi_{k}
. Sending

and using Fatou’s lemma gives

∎

Lemma
4.2
will allow us to control the behavior of solutions,
u
to
(P_{m}-E-i\varepsilon)u=f
near
0
.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

To shorten notation, we set
P_{m,E,\varepsilon}=P_{m}-E-i\varepsilon
. Since
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\operatorname{comp},m}
and

, for any compact

In particular,

.

Next, set

(4.7)

\delta=\delta(m):=\delta_{0}h\langle h^{-2}m\rangle^{1/2},\qquad\delta_{1}=\delta_{1}(m)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\max(\delta(m),\tfrac{1}{2}),

for some
0<\delta_{0}\ll 1
independent of

,
m
and

, to be chosen. Let
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}[0,2)
with

. Set

.
Then

\begin{split}Q_{m}\chi_{\delta_{1}}u&=h^{-2}P_{m,E,\varepsilon}\chi_{\delta_{1}}u-h^{-2}(V-E-i\varepsilon)\chi_{\delta_{1}}u\\
&=h^{-2}\chi_{\delta_{1}}P_{m}u+h^{-2}[P_{m},\chi_{\delta_{1}}]u-h^{-2}\chi_{\delta_{1}}(V-E-i\varepsilon)u.\end{split}

In particular, since

,
P_{m}u\in L^{2}
, and
\chi_{\delta_{1}}
is constant near zero,
r^{2}Q_{m}\chi_{\delta_{1}}u\in r^{2}L^{2}
. Setting

t_{0}=t_{0}(m)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2}&-\frac{h^{2}}{4}\leq m\leq\frac{h^{2}}{4},\\
\ 1&\frac{h^{2}}{4}<m,\end{cases}

observe that

(4.9)

\Lambda(t_{0},m)=|t^{2}_{0}-t_{0}-h^{-2}m|^{-1}\leq c\langle h^{-2}m\rangle^{-1}

for some

independent of
h
and
m
.
Note also that with this definition of

,
t_{0}(m)<t_{-}(m)
for

, and
t_{-}(m)<t_{0}(m)<t_{+}(m)
for

. Therefore, by Lemma
4.2
, since
u\in L^{2}
,
r^{2}Q_{m}\chi_{\delta_{1}}u\in r^{2}L^{2}

We use upper and lower bounds for

Lemma B.2
.

Suppose

,

,

,
f^{\prime}\in C(a,b]
, and

and

,

Proof.

If
f
is identically 0 on
[a,b]
, then the claim is trivially true. Therefore, we will assume
f
is not identically 0.
Assume
f
attains its extremum

at an interior point of
(a,b)
. Replacing
f
by
-f
if necessary, we may assume

. Set

x^{*}=\inf\{x\in(a,b):f(x)=L\}.

Then

near

, hence

almost everywhere, in a neighborhood of

. Also

. Now, for

So

Recall from Fredholm theory

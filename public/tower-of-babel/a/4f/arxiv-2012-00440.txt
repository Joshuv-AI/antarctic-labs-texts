1.
Introduction

Which positive bounded linear operators on a separable Hilbert space can be written as strong sums of projections? If the underlying space is finite dimensional, then a characterization of such
operators was obtained by Fillmore
[
6
]
: a finite-dimensional operator is the sum of projections if and only if it is positive, it has an integer trace and the trace is greater than or
equal to its rank.

The same question can be asked relative to a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
. This question was first asked by Kaftal, Ng and Zhang in
[
10
]
. In
[
10
]
, they developed
beautiful techniques and addressed the question completely for positive operators in type
{\rm I}
and type
{\rm III}
factors. They proved the following results. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a type
{\rm I}
factor. Given
A\in\mathcal{M}^{+}
. Denote by
A_{+}=(A-I)\chi_{A}(1,\|A\|]
the excess part of
A
and by
A_{-}=(I-A)\chi_{A}(0,1)
the defect part of
A
. Then
A
is a strong sum of projections if and only if either
{\rm Tr}(A_{+})=\infty
or
{\rm Tr}(A_{-})\leq{\rm Tr}(A_{+})<\infty
and
{\rm Tr}(A_{+})-{\rm Tr}(A_{-})\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a type
{\rm III}
factor. Then
A\in\mathcal{M}
is a strong sum of projections if and only if either
\|A\|>1
or
A
is a projection. For type
{\rm II}
factors, they proved that if
A
is
diagonalizable, then
A
is a strong sum of projections if and only if
\tau(A_{+})\geq\tau(A_{-})
. The condition
\tau(A_{+})\geq\tau(A_{-})
is necessary even when
A
is not diagonalizable.
Recall that a positive operator
A
is said to be diagonalizable if
A=\sum\gamma_{j}E_{j}
for some
\gamma_{j}>0
and mutually orthogonal projections
\{E_{j}\}
in
\mathcal{M}
.
In
[
2
]
, we proved that if
\mathcal{M}
is a type
{\rm II}
factor,
A\in\mathcal{M}^{+}
and
\tau(A_{+})\geq\tau(A_{-})
, then
A
can be written as the sum of a finite or infinite collection of
projections.

A natural question is the following: which positive operators in a factor von Neumann algebra can be written as finite sums of projections? This question is much more interesting and difficult. Note that the question is even open
for positive operators in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. One of the main goals of this paper is to study this question in type
{\rm II}_{1}
factors.

As reported in a survey article [
[
23
]
, Theorem 4.12] by Wu quoting unpublished joint work of Choi and Wu
in 1988, they proved that positive operators with essential norm greater than 1 are finite sums of projections.
This result is presented together with other related results in their recent paper [
[
3
]
, Theorem 2.2]. The special
case of
\alpha I
with
\alpha>1
follows also from a delicate analysis which characterizes the numbers
\alpha
for which
\alpha I

is a sum of at least
n
projections (see in
[
16
]
and several other papers.)

[
13
]
presented a characterization of strict sums of projections in the multiplier algebras of
\sigma
-unital purely
infinite simple
C^{*}
-algebras and
[
11
]
provided sufficient conditions for operators to be finite sums of projections
in the multiplier algebras of
\sigma
-unital purely infinite simple
C^{*}
-algebras.

In
[
9
]
, H. Halpern, V. Kaftal, P. Ng and S. Zhang provided some sufficient conditions and also a necessary condition for a positive operator can be written as a finite sum of
projections. For a type
{\rm II}_{1}
factor, they showed a sufficient condition for a positive diagonalizable operator
A
to be a finite sum of projections is
\tau(A_{+})>\tau(A_{-})
or
equivalently
\tau(A)>\tau(R_{A})
.

In this paper, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(Main Theorem)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a type
{\rm II}_{1}
factor and let
X=X^{*}\in\mathcal{M}
. Then

(1)

there is a decomposition of the identity into
N\in\mathbb{N}
mutually orthogonal nonzero projections
E_{j}
,
I=\sum_{j=1}^{N}E_{j}
, for which
E_{j}XE_{j}=\tau(X)E_{j}
for all
j=1,...,N
;

(2)

there is a finite dimensional abelian von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
such that

E_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{M}}(X)=\tau(X)I,

where
E_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{M}}
is the conditional expectation from
\mathcal{M}
onto
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{M}
;

(3)

there is a unitary operator
U\in\mathcal{M}
with

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{U^{*}}^{j}XU^{j}=\tau(X)I;

(4)

there is a unitary operator
W\in\mathcal{M}
with
W^{N}=I
and

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{W^{*}}^{j}XW^{j}=\tau(X)I.

This result is inspired by
[
3
]
. To prove this theorem, we develop some new techniques in this paper. On the other hand, several results, e.g., Proposition 2.3, Corollary 2.5, Lemma
2.8 and Lemma 6.1, in
[
9
]
play important roles in the proof.

We mention the following results as applications of the main theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a type
{\rm II}_{1}
factor and
A\in\mathcal{M}^{+}
. Then
A
is a finite sum of projections if and only if
\tau(A)\geq\tau(R_{A})
.

The above theorem answers affirmatively Question 6.7 of
[
9
]
. The following theorem answers affirmatively Question 1.1 in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a type
{\rm II}_{1}
factor and
X\in\mathcal{M}
,
X=X^{*}
and
\tau(X)=0
. Then there exists a nilpotent element
Z\in\mathcal{M}
such that
X=ReZ
, i.e.,
X
is the real part of a nilpotent element
Z
.

The following theorem is a stronger version of Dixmier’s averaging theorem for type
{\rm II}_{1}
factors.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a type
{\rm II}_{1}
factor and let
X_{1},...,X_{n}\in\mathcal{M}
. Then there exists unitary operators
U_{1},...,U_{k}\in\mathcal{M}
such that

\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}U_{i}^{*}X_{j}U_{i}=\tau(X_{j})I,\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq n.

theorem.

Definition 2.1

(Definition 2.2 in

]
)
.

An operator algebra
\mathcal{M}
has constants
N_{0}
and

The existence and the estimates of the constants

and

.
If
\mathcal{M}
is of type
\rm II_{1}

N_{0}=12
and

.

.

A linear combination is called positive combinations of projections if a positive operator is finite linear combination of projections with positive coefficients.

3.
Main theorem

The following lemma is motivated by Proposition 1.5 of
[
3
]
and its proof is similar to the proof of Proposition 1.5 of
[
3
]
. We provide a complete proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{M}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

There is a decomposition of the identity into
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
mutually orthogonal nonzero projections
E_{j}
,
I=\sum_{j=1}^{N}E_{j}
, for which
E_{j}XE_{j}=\tau(X)E_{j}
for all
j=1,...,N
;

(2)

There is a unitary operator
U\in\mathcal{M}
with

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{U^{*}}^{j}XU^{j}=\tau(X)I.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2). Set
A:=\frac{X}{\tau(X)}
, then
\tau(A)=1
. By hypothesis, there are mutually orthogonal nonzero projections
E_{1},E_{2},...,E_{N}
such that
\sum_{k=1}^{N}E_{k}=I
and

\sum_{k=1}^{N}E_{k}AE_{k}=I
. Set
U:=\sum_{k=1}^{N}e^{\frac{2\pi ki}{N}}E_{k}
. Then

U^{*}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}e^{-\frac{2\pi ki}{N}}E_{k}=U^{-1}

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{U^{*}}^{j}AU^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\sum_{k,l=1}^{N}e^{\frac{2\pi i(l-k)j}{N}}E_{k}AE_{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k,l=1}^{N}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}e^{\frac{2\pi i(l-k)j}{N}}\right)E_{k}AE_{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}E_{k}AE_{k}=I

since
\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}e^{\frac{2\pi i(l-k)j}{N}}=0
for
k\neq l
. These show that
U
is the required operator.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1). Assume that
U
is the unitary operator with
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{U^{*}}^{j}AU^{j}=I
. Then

A-{U^{*}}^{N}AU^{N}=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{U^{*}}^{j}AU^{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}{U^{*}}^{j}AU^{j}=NI-U^{*}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{U^{*}}^{j}AU^{j}\right)U=0.

This implies that
AU^{N}=U^{N}A
. Let
f(e^{i\theta})=e^{\frac{i\theta}{N}}
for
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
and let
W=f(U^{N})^{-1}U
. Then
W
is a unitary operator. Since
U
commutes with
U^{N}
,
W^{N}=(f(U^{N})^{-1}U)^{N}=f(U^{N})^{-N}U^{N}=U^{-N}U^{N}=I
. Then the spectrum of
W
consists of eigenvalues from the set
\{e^{\frac{2k\pi i}{N}}:1\leq k\leq N\}
. Choose
E_{1},E_{2},...,E_{N}\in\mathcal{M}
to be spectral projections of
W
such that
\sum_{k=1}^{N}E_{k}=I
and

W=\sum_{k=1}^{N}e^{\frac{2k\pi i}{N}}E_{k}
. As
A
commutes with
U^{N}
, we have

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{W^{*}}^{j}AW^{j}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{U^{*}}^{j}f(U^{N})^{j}Af(U^{N})^{-j}U^{j}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{U^{*}}^{j}AU^{j}=I.

Since
WE_{k}=e^{\frac{2k\pi i}{N}}E_{k}
for each
1\leq k\leq N
, we have

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}E_{k}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{W^{*}}^{j}AW^{j}\right)E_{k}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(e^{-\frac{2jk\pi i}{N}}E_{k})A(e^{\frac{2jk\pi i}{N}}E_{k})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}E_{k}AE_{k}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}E_{k}AE_{k}.

This proves (1).
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{M}^{+}
,
\tau(A)=1
and
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

A
is the sum of
N
nonzero projections;

(2)

There is a decomposition of the identity into
N
mutually orthogonal nonzero projections
E_{j}
,
I=\sum_{j=1}^{N}E_{j}
, for which
\sum_{j=1}^{N}E_{j}AE_{j}=I
;

(3)

There is a finite dimensional abelian von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
such that

E_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{M}}(A)=I,

where
E_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{M}}
is the conditional expectation from
\mathcal{M}
onto
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{M}
preserving
\tau
;

(4)

There is a unitary operator
U\in\mathcal{M}
with

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{U^{*}}^{j}AU^{j}=I.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have (2)
\Leftrightarrow
(4).

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1).
\sum_{j=1}^{N}E_{j}AE_{j}=I
is equivalent to
E_{j}AE_{j}=E_{j}
for each
1\leq j\leq N
. Let
P_{j}=A^{1/2}E_{j}A^{1/2}
for
1\leq j\leq N
. Then
P_{j}
is a projection for
1\leq j\leq N
and
A=\sum_{j=1}^{N}P_{j}
.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2). Let
A=\sum_{j=1}^{N}P_{j}
, where
\{P_{j}\}
are nonzero projections. Then
1=\tau(A)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tau(P_{j})
. We decompose the identity
I=\sum_{j=1}^{N}F_{j}
into
N

mutually orthogonal projections
F_{j}\sim P_{j}
. Now choose partial isometries
W_{j}\in\mathcal{M}
with
P_{j}=W_{j}^{*}W_{j}
and
F_{j}=W_{j}W_{j}^{*}
. Denote
B:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}W_{j}
, then

B^{*}B=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}W_{j}\right)^{*}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}W_{j}\right)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}W_{i}^{*}W_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}W_{j}^{*}W_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}P_{j}=A.

Let
B=VA^{1/2}
. Then
BB^{*}=VAV^{*}
. Since
\mathcal{M}
is a
{\rm II}_{1}
factor,
V
can be chosen to be a unitary operator. Moreover,
F_{j}B=W_{j}
for every
j
, thus

\sum_{j=1}^{N}F_{j}VAV^{*}F_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}F_{j}BB^{*}F_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}W_{j}W_{j}^{*}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}F_{j}=I.

Let
E_{j}=V^{*}F_{j}V
. Then we have (2).

(2)
\Rightarrow
(3). Take
\mathcal{B}
to be the algebra generated by
\{E_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
. Since
E_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{M}}(A)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}E_{j}AE_{j}
. Then, by hypothesis, (3) follows.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(2). Let
E_{1},E_{2},...,E_{N}\in\mathcal{B}
be the minimal projections of
\mathcal{B}
such that
\sum_{j=1}^{N}E_{j}=I
. Then
E_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{M}}(A)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}E_{j}AE_{j}=I
.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

Note that in the above Lemma, (1)
\Leftrightarrow
(2) is true for
N=+\infty
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
E\in\mathcal{M}
be a projection. Then there is a unitary operator
U\in\mathcal{M}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{U^{*}}^{j}EU^{j}=\tau(E)I.

Proof.

The case
E=0,I
being trivial, we assume that
0<E<I
. Then
0<\tau(E)<1
and
\frac{1}{\tau(E)}>1
. Set
A:=\frac{1}{\tau(E)}E
. By Lemma
2.8
,
A
is a sum of
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}

nonzero
projections in
\mathcal{M}
. Since
\tau(A)=1
, then by Lemma
3.2
, there is a unitary operator
U\in\mathcal{M}
such that

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{U^{*}}^{j}EU^{j}=\tau(E)I.

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
E
and
F
be mutually orthogonal projections in
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
,
\mu\geq 0
,
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
, and
A:=(1+\mu)E+(1-\lambda)F
. If
\mu\tau(E)\geq\lambda\tau(F)
, then
A
is a finite sum of projections in
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

By considering
(E+F)\mathcal{M}(E+F)
, we may assume that
E+F=I
. By Lemma
2.8
, if
\mu\tau(E)>\lambda\tau(F)
, then
A
is a finite sum of projections in
\mathcal{M}
. So we may
assume that
\mu\tau(E)=\lambda\tau(F)
. Notice that
A=(1+\mu)E+(1-\lambda)F=(1+\mu)E+(1-\lambda)(I-E)
and
\tau(A)=1+\mu\tau(E)-\lambda\tau(I-E)=1
. By Lemma

3.4
, there is a unitary operator
U
and
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{U^{*}}^{j}EU^{j}=\tau(E)I
. Thus

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{U^{*}}^{j}AU^{j}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}{U^{*}}^{j}(I-\lambda I+(\mu+\lambda)E)U^{j}=I-\lambda I+(\mu+\lambda)\tau(E)I=I.

So by Lemma
3.2
,
A
is a sum of
N
nonzero projections in
\mathcal{M}
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
E,F
be two nonzero projections in
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
. Then there are projections

\mathcal{M}

(1)

\mathcal{M}\cong M_{n}(\mathds{C})\otimes\mathcal{N}
for some von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}
. In this algebra,

A:=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{A_{1}+A_{2}+\cdots+A_{n}}{n}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&\frac{A_{1}+A_{2}+\cdots+A_{n}}{n}\end{pmatrix}\prec\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&A_{n}\end{pmatrix}:=B,
where
A_{i}\in\mathcal{N}^{+}
for
i=1,...,n
.

Proof.

Set
U_{1}=I
,

U_{i}:=\bordermatrix{&1&2&\cdots&i&i+1&\cdots&n\cr 1&0&0&\cdots&1&0&\cdots&0\cr 2&0&0&\cdots&0&1&\cdots&0\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\cr n+2-i&1&0&\cdots&0&0&\cdots&0\cr n+3-i&0&1&\cdots&0&0&\cdots&0\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&0\cr n&0&0&\cdots&0&0&\cdots&0},
where
i=2,...,n-1
,
and

U_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\cdots&1\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}.
Then it can prove that

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}U_{i}^{*}BU_{i}=A.

∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
A,B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{M}
and
A\prec B_{1}
. If
B_{1}
is unitary equivalent with
B_{2}
, then
A\prec B_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
A\prec B_{1}
, then by definition, there are unitary operators
U_{1},...,U_{N}\in\mathcal{M}
such that

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}U_{i}^{*}B_{1}U_{i}=A.

As
B_{1}
is unitary equivalent with
B_{2}
, then
B_{1}=V^{*}B_{2}V
for some unitary operator
V
.
Thus

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}U_{i}^{*}V^{*}B_{2}VU_{i}=A.

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

If
(E_{a,b})_{a,b\in B}
is a self-adjoint system of
n\times n
matrix units for a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
, it is known that

\mathcal{M}\cong M_{n}(\mathds{C})\otimes\mathcal{N}
for some von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}
. In the algebra
\mathcal{M}\cong M_{n}(\mathds{C})\otimes\mathcal{N}
,

\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&A_{2}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&A_{n}\end{pmatrix}\prec\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}+A_{2}+\cdots+A_{n}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix},

where
A_{i}\in\mathcal{N}^{+}
for
i=1,2,...,n

Proof.

First, we consider
2\times 2
matrix. Write

\begin{pmatrix}A+B&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}A^{\frac{1}{2}}&B^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A^{\frac{1}{2}}&0\\
B^{\frac{1}{2}}&0\end{pmatrix},
which is unitary equivalent with

\begin{pmatrix}A^{\frac{1}{2}}&0\\
B^{\frac{1}{2}}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A^{\frac{1}{2}}&B^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

By the proof of Lemma
4.3
,
\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&B\end{pmatrix}\prec\begin{pmatrix}A&A^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
B^{\frac{1}{2}}A^{\frac{1}{2}}&B\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}A^{\frac{1}{2}}&0\\
B^{\frac{1}{2}}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A^{\frac{1}{2}}&B^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.
Then by Proposition
4.5
, we have
\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&B\end{pmatrix}\prec\begin{pmatrix}A+B&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.
Next we will prove that

\begin{pmatrix}A&0&0\\
0&B&0\\
0&0&C\end{pmatrix}\prec\begin{pmatrix}A+B+C&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},
where
A,B,C\in\mathcal{N}^{+}
. Similarly to the above argument.

\begin{pmatrix}A+B+C&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}(A+B)^{\frac{1}{2}}&0&C^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}(A+B)^{\frac{1}{2}}&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
C^{\frac{1}{2}}&0&0\end{pmatrix},

which is unitary equivalent with

\begin{pmatrix}(A+B)^{\frac{1}{2}}&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
C^{\frac{1}{2}}&0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}(A+B)^{\frac{1}{2}}&0&C^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Thus

\begin{pmatrix}A+B&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&C\end{pmatrix}\prec\begin{pmatrix}A+B&0&(A+B)^{\frac{1}{2}}C^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
0&0&0\\
C^{\frac{1}{2}}(A+B)^{\frac{1}{2}}&0&C\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}(A+B)^{\frac{1}{2}}&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
C^{\frac{1}{2}}&0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}(A+B)^{\frac{1}{2}}&0&C^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then we have

\begin{pmatrix}A+B&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&C\end{pmatrix}\prec\begin{pmatrix}A+B+C&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Since by above argument,

\begin{pmatrix}A&0&0\\
0&B&0\\
0&0&C\end{pmatrix}\prec\begin{pmatrix}A+B&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&C\end{pmatrix}.

So we prove that

\begin{pmatrix}A&0&0\\
0&B&0\\
0&0&C\end{pmatrix}\prec\begin{pmatrix}A+B+C&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

By the same argument we have that

\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&A_{2}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&A_{n}\end{pmatrix}\prec\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}+A_{2}+\cdots+A_{n}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}.

∎

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{M}
be an injective positive map with dense range. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

\begin{pmatrix}B&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\prec\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix};

(2)

B\prec A.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
. By hypothesis, there are unitary operators
U_{1},...,U_{N}\in\mathcal{M}
such that

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}U_{i}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}U_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}B&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Set
U_{i}:=\begin{pmatrix}U_{11}^{i}&U_{12}^{i}\\
U_{21}^{i}&U_{22}^{i}\end{pmatrix}.
Then we have

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(U_{21}^{i})^{*}AU_{21}^{i}=0.
Since
(U_{21}^{i})^{*}AU_{21}^{i}\geq 0
for every
i=1,...,N
, then

(U_{21}^{i})^{*}AU_{21}^{i}=0
for every
i=1,...,N
. Write
(U_{21}^{i})^{*}AU_{21}^{i}=(U_{21}^{i})^{*}A^{\frac{1}{2}}A^{\frac{1}{2}}U_{21}^{i}
, thus
A^{\frac{1}{2}}U_{21}^{i}=0
. As
A
is injective,
it implies that
U_{21}^{i}=0
for every
i=1,...,N
. Since
U_{i}
is a unitary operator, it implies that

(U_{11}^{i})^{*}U_{11}^{i}=I
and
U_{11}^{i}(U_{11}^{i})^{*}+U_{12}^{i}(U_{12}^{i})^{*}=I
. As
\mathcal{M}
is finite factor, then
(U_{11}^{i})^{*}U_{11}^{i}=I
implies that
U_{11}^{i}(U_{11}^{i})^{*}=I
. So we have
U_{12}^{i}=0

for every
i=1,...,N
. Therefore we conclude that

\frac{1}{N}(U_{11}^{i})^{*}AU_{11}^{i}=B.

Then we have (2).
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a type
\rm{II}_{1}
factor and
A\in\mathcal{M}
be an injective positive map with dense range. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

\begin{pmatrix}\tau(A)I&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\prec\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix};

(2)

\tau(A)I\prec A.

The following theorem is a stronger version of Dixmier’s averaging theorem for type
{\rm II}_{1}
factors.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a type
{\rm II}_{1}
factor and let
X_{1},...,X_{n}\in\mathcal{M}
. Then there exist unitary operators
U_{1},...,U_{k}\in\mathcal{M}
such that

\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}U_{i}^{*}X_{j}U_{i}=\tau(X_{j})I,\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq n.

Proof.

Clearly, we may assume that
X_{i}=X_{i}^{*}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. For
X_{1}
, by Theorem
3.23
, there exist unitary operators
V_{1},...,V_{r}
such that

\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}^{*}X_{1}V_{i}=\tau(X_{1})I.

Let

A=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}^{*}X_{2}V_{i}.

Then
A=A^{*}
and
\tau(A)=\tau(X_{2})
. By Theorem
3.23
, there exist unitary operators
W_{1},...,W_{s}
such that

\frac{1}{s}\sum_{i=1}^{s}W_{i}^{*}AW_{i}=\tau(X_{2})I.

Now it is easy to see that
W_{i}V_{j}
satisfies

\frac{1}{rs}\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sum_{j=1}^{r}W_{i}^{*}V_{j}^{*}X_{k}V_{j}W_{i}=\tau(X_{k})I,\quad k=1,2.

Now, by induction we have the theorem.
∎

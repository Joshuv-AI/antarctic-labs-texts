A fixed point theorem in B ⁡ ( H , ℓ ∞ ) B(H,\ell_{\infty})
Andrzej Wiśnicki

Abstract
. We show that if X X is a complete metric space with uniform relative normal structure and G G is a subgroup of the isometry group of X X with bounded orbits, then there is a point in X X fixed by every isometry in G G . As a corollary, we obtain a theorem of U. Lang (2013) concerning injective metric spaces. A few applications of this theorem are given to the problems of inner derivations. In particular, we show that if L 1 ​ ( μ ) L_{1}(\mu) is an essential Banach L 1 ​ ( G ) L_{1}(G) -bimodule, then any continuous derivation δ : L 1 ​ ( G ) → L ∞ ​ ( μ ) \delta:L_{1}(G)\rightarrow L_{\infty}(\mu) is inner. This extends a theorem of B. E. Johnson (1991) asserting that the convolution algebra L 1 ​ ( G ) L_{1}(G) is weakly amenable if G G is a locally compact group.



1.
Introduction

The writing of this paper has been animated by a fixed point theorem of
Bader, Gelander and Monod in
[
2
]
–there is a point fixed by every
isometry of an
L
-embedded
Banach space that preserves a given
bounded set. One of its consequences is the optimal solution to the
following
derivation problem
: if
G
is a locally compact group,
then any derivation from the convolution algebra
L_{1}(G)
to
M(G)
is

inner
. The problem was studied since 1960s and proved for the first
time by Losert
[
24
]
. An ingenious proof in
[
2
]
relies on the
Ryll-Nardzewski theorem. Another consequence of this theorem, observed by
Uffe Haagerup, is a direct proof of
weak amenability
of all C
∗
-algebras which was proved in
[
12
]
using the
Grothendieck–Haagerup–Pisier inequality.

There is a counterpart of the Bader–Gelander–Monod theorem in
injective
Banach spaces
L_{\infty}
(see Section 2), and it is natural to
ask about the noncommutative analogue of that result. The relevance of such
a question is particularly apparent if we analyse the proof of Corollary C
in
[
2
]
. The central idea behind the proofs of both the derivation
problem and weak amenability of C
∗
-algebras there, is to construct
a group of affine isometries with bounded orbits acting on the predual of an
appropriate von Neumann algebra and then applying
[
2
, Theorem A]
. We
can use a similar strategy to study derivations into injective Banach
bimodules and, more general, into Banach bimodules with
uniform
relative normal structure
(see Section 2 for the definition), based this
time on Theorem
2.3
. In particular, we show that if
L_{1}(\mu)
is an
essential Banach
L_{1}(G)
-bimodule, then any continuous derivation
\delta:L_{1}(G)\rightarrow L_{\infty}(\mu)
is inner, where
\mu
is a
\sigma
-finite measure on
(E,\Sigma)
or a Radon measure on a locally compact
space. This extends a theorem of Johnson (see
[
16
, Theorem]
) asserting
that the convolution algebra

is weakly amenable if

is a

In Section 3 we apply Theorem

in the case of
B(H,\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma))
, the Banach space of bounded operators from a Hilbert space
H

into
\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)
for some
\Gamma
. It can throw some new

One of the equivalent
formulations of this problem concerns inner derivations into Banach modules
and translates into the existence or non-existence of a fixed point for the
appropriate group of affine isometries acting on
B(H)
, the von Neumann
algebra of bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space
H
. Notice that

is injective in the category of operator spaces and completely
contractive maps. Though it is not injective as a Banach space, an embedding
of
B(H)
into a Banach space

with uniform

Our strategy is as follows. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital C
∗
-algebra,
M
a Banach bimodule over
\mathcal{A}
and
\delta:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow M
a derivation:
\delta(ab)=\delta(a)b+a\delta(b).
If
G

is a group of unitary elements of
\mathcal{A}
, then

defined by
\alpha(g)x=gxg^{-1}+\delta(g)g^{-1}
,

is an affine and isometric action of

on

It follows from

that if

is a Banach bimodule with uniform relative

such that
\alpha(g)x=x
, and
consequently,
\delta(g)=xg-gx
. Since every element of
\mathcal{A}
is a
linear combination of three unitaries, we deduce that each derivation
\delta:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow M
is inner. The argument works also for some
group algebras, see Theorem

. The case of the convolution algebra

L_{1}(G)
and
M=L_{\infty}(G)
, where

is a locally compact group was
studied by Johnson
[
15
,
16
]
. Let us move on now to the Kadison
similarity problem, which is equivalent to the derivation problem for
derivations
\delta:B(H)\supset\mathcal{A}\rightarrow B(H)
(see
[
20
]
). Let us embed a Hilbert space
H
isometrically in an injective space
\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)
and extend each unitary

to an

. Now, consider the group of isometries
\alpha_{g}:B(H,\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma))\rightarrow B(H,\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma))
defined by

. It follows from Theorem

that there is a bounded linear operator

such that

. Note that in fact a
similar result holds for derivations from

to

on which

acts by isometries. However, in the case of C

-algebras our
factorization appears to be more effective. For example, properties of the

could be useful in studying complete

2.
Spaces with uniform relative normal structure

Let
\mathcal{A}(X)
denote the family of admissible subsets of a metric
space
X
, i.e., the intersections of closed balls of
X
. The following
definition, originated in the works of Brodskiĭ and Mil’man
[
4
]
, and Kirk
[
21
]
, was introduced by Soardi
[
30
]
in the case of
Banach spaces (see also
[
19
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

A metric space
X
is said to have uniform relative normal structure if
there exists
c<1
such that for each nonempty and bounded set
M\in\mathcal{A}(X)
there exists
z_{M}\in X
such that

•

d(x,z_{M})\leq c\diam M
for every
x\in M;

•

if for some
y\in X
,
M\subset B(y,c\diam M)
, then

d(y,z_{M})\leq c\diam M,

where
B(y,r)
denotes the closed ball centered at
y
and of
radius
r
.

Examples of spaces with uniform relative normal structure include Hilbert
spaces, (
1
-)injective spaces and their closed balls (see
[
30
]
). It
was proved in
[
30
]
that if
K
is a nonempty weak
∗
-closed
subset with uniform relative normal structure of a dual Banach space, and
T:K\rightarrow K
is nonexpansive and leaves invariant a weak
∗
-compact subset of
K
, then
K
contains a fixed point for
T
(see also

[
10
,
19
]
for some generalizations in metric spaces). Lau
[
23
]

used Soardi’s arguments to show the following fixed point theorem for
isometries.

Theorem 2.2

(A.T.-M. Lau)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
K
be a nonempty bounded
closed subset of
X
with uniform relative normal structure. Then there is
z\in K
such that
Tz=z
for each isometry
T
from
K
onto
K
.

A natural question arises whether it is possible to extend the above results
for mappings with bounded orbits, and Prus (see
[
11
]
) constructed an
isometry
T:\ell_{\infty}\rightarrow\ell_{\infty}
with bounded orbits
but without fixed points. However, it turns out that in the case of
invertible isometries the situation is different, and the Soardi–Lau method
leaves some room for improvement.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose that
X
is a complete metric space with uniform relative
normal structure. If
G
is a group of isometries
g:X\rightarrow X
with
bounded orbits, then there exists
x\in X
such that
gx=x
for every
g\in G.

Proof.

For any admissible set
M\subset X
, define

\displaystyle A(M)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcap_{x\in M}B(x,c\diam M),

\displaystyle H(M)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcap_{y\in A\left(M\right)}B\left(y,c\diam M\right)\cap A\left(M\right),

where
c<1
satisfies the conditions of uniform relative normal structure
for
X.
Notice that
H(M)\neq\emptyset
because
z_{M}\in H(M).

Fix
x_{0}\in X
and let
A_{0}=\bigcap_{g\in G}B(gx_{0},\delta(x_{0}))
,
where

\delta(x_{0})=\sup_{g,h\in G}d\left(gx_{0},hx_{0}\right)

denotes the diameter of the orbit of
x_{0}
. Note that
A_{0}
is
admissible and

g(A_{0})=\bigcap_{h\in G}B(ghx_{0},\delta(x))=A_{0}.

We take by recursion
A_{1}=H(A_{0}),A_{n+1}=H(A_{n})
, and pick
x_{n}\in A_{n}
for
n=1,2,\ldots
. If
z\in A_{n+1}
, then
d(z,y)\leq c\diam A_{n}
for each
y\in A_{n+1}\subset A(A_{n})
and hence

\diam A_{n+1}\leq c\diam A_{n}\leq...\leq c^{n}\diam A_{1}.

Similarly, since
x_{n}\in A(A_{n})
,

d(x_{n+1},x_{n})\leq c\diam A_{n}\leq...\leq c^{n}\diam A_{1}.

Thus
(x_{n})
is a Cauchy sequence converging to some
x\in X.
Moreover,
g(A_{n})=A_{n}
for every
g\in G.
It follows that

d(gx,x)\leq d(gx,gx_{n})+d(gx_{n},x_{n})+d(x_{n},x)\longrightarrow 0\text{
if }n\rightarrow\infty,

and hence
gx=x
for every
g\in G.

∎

In particular, if
X=K
is a bounded closed subset of a Banach space we
obtain the Lau theorem
2.2
, and Bruhat–Tits’ theorem
[
5
]
(see
also
[
3
, Corollary II.2.8]
) follows if we consider a closed convex
subset of a Hadamard space. But the applications of Theorem
2.3
are
even wider. Recall that a metric space is injective (or hyperconvex) if

\bigcap_{\alpha\in I}B(x_{\alpha},r_{\alpha})\neq\emptyset

for any collection of closed balls
\{B(x_{\alpha},r_{\alpha})\}_{\alpha\in I}
such that
d(x_{\alpha},x_{\beta})\leq r_{\alpha}+r_{\beta},\ \alpha,\beta\in I.
Notice that injective metric spaces have uniform
relative normal structure with
c=1/2
, and thus we obtain the following
corollary proved by Lang
[
22
, Proposition 1.2]
with completely
different methods (see also
[
31
, Theorem 2.3]
for a little more
general result).

Corollary 2.4

(U. Lang)
.

Let
X
be an injective metric space. If
G
is a group of
isometries
g:X\rightarrow X
with bounded orbits, then there is a fixed
point of
G
in
X
.

In particular, Corollary
2.4
applies to a group of isometries with
bounded orbits acting on a real
L_{\infty}(\mu)
whenever
\mu
is a
\sigma
-finite measure on
(E,\Sigma)
, a Radon measure on a locally
compact space (see
[
9
, Corollary 3.10]
), or a counting measure on
any
\Gamma
. Note that a complex
L_{\infty}(\mu)
need not be
hyperconvex, yet it follows from
[
30
, Lemma]
that it has uniform
relative normal structure with
c=1/\sqrt{2}.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
\mu
be a
\sigma
-finite measure on
(E,\Sigma)
, a Radon measure
on a locally compact space, or a counting measure on any
\Gamma
. If
G

is a subgroup of the isometry group of a real or complex
L_{\infty}(\mu)

with bounded orbits, then there is a fixed point of
G
in
L_{\infty}(\mu)
.

Equipped with Theorem
2.3
, the discussion around Corollary C in
[
2
]
can be repeated verbatim for the case of Banach bimodules with the
uniform relative normal structure.

If
\mathcal{A}
is a Banach algebra [with unit
e
], a Banach space
M
is
said to be a Banach
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule if there are bounded bilinear
mappings
(a,x)\mapsto a.x,(x,a)\mapsto x.a:\mathcal{A}\times M\rightarrow M

such that [
e.x=x.e=x
for every
x\in M
] and the usual associative law
holds for each type of triple product:
a_{1}a_{2}x,a_{1}xa_{2},xa_{1}a_{2}.

We will assume that

(1)

\left\|a.x\right\|\leq\left\|a\right\|\left\|x\right\|,\ \ \ \left\|x.a\right\|\leq\left\|x\right\|\left\|a\right\|\ \ \ (a\in A,x\in M).

Note that for any Banach
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule
M
we can define an
equivalent norm which satisfies (
1
).

By a derivation, from a Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}
into a Banach
\mathcal{A}
-bimodule
M
, we mean a linear mapping
\delta:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow M

such that
\delta(ab)=\delta(a).b+a.\delta(b)
for
a,b\in\mathcal{A}
.
A derivation is inner if there exists
T\in M
such that
\delta(a)=T.a-a.T

for each
a\in\mathcal{A}.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra, and let
M

be a Banach space with uniform relative normal structure. If
M
is a Banach

\mathcal{A}
-bimodule, then any derivation
\delta:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow M
is inner.

Proof.

Let
U(\mathcal{A})
denotes the group of unitaries of
\mathcal{A}
. For
any
g\in U(\mathcal{A})
and
x\in M
, set

\alpha(g)x=g.x.g^{-1}+\delta(g).g^{-1},

and notice that
\alpha:U(\mathcal{A})\times M\rightarrow M
is an (affine)
and isometric action of
U(\mathcal{A})
on
M
, and thus

\displaystyle\alpha(gh)x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(g)\alpha(h)x,

\displaystyle\left\|\alpha(g)x-\alpha(g)y\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|x-y\right\|\ \ \ (g,h\in U(\mathcal{A}),x,y\in M).

Furthermore, Ringrose’s theorem
[
29
]
yields
\delta:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow M
is bounded, and hence the orbit

A=\{\delta(g).g^{-1}:g\in U(\mathcal{A})\}

of
\alpha
at
0
is bounded too. It follows from Theorem
2.3
that
there is
T\in M
such that
\alpha(g)T=T
, which gives

\delta(g)=T.g-g.T\ \ \ (g\in U(\mathcal{A})).

This finishes the proof since any element of
\mathcal{A}
is a linear
combination of four unitaries (in fact, three
[
18
]
).
∎

We now turn to the case of group algebras
L_{1}(G)
, where
G
is a locally
compact group. This case is a little more subtle. If
E
is an essential
Banach
L_{1}(G)
-bimodule, i.e., the linear hull of
\{a.t.b:a,b\in L_{1}(G),t\in E\}
is dense in
E
, then the dual actions

\left\langle t,x.a\right\rangle=\left\langle a.t,x\right\rangle,\ \ \ \left\langle t,a.x\right\rangle=\left\langle t.a,x\right\rangle\ \ \ (a\in L_{1}(G),t\in E,x\in M)

of
L_{1}(G)
on
M=E^{\ast}
can be extended to actions of
M(G)
, the
space of (complex-valued) Radon measures on
G
. Let
\delta:L_{1}(G)\rightarrow M
be a continuous derivation. Then there is a unique
derivation
\tilde{\delta}:M(G)\rightarrow M
extending
\delta
such that
\parallel\tilde{\delta}\parallel=\parallel\delta\parallel
, and

\langle a.t,\tilde{\delta}\mu\rangle=\left\langle t,\delta(\mu\ast a)\right\rangle-\left\langle t.\mu,\delta a\right\rangle\ \ \ (a\in L_{1}(G),\mu\in M(G),t\in E),

where
\ast
denotes the convolution (with respect to a fixed left Haar
measure on
G
) and
t.\mu

(t\in E,\mu\in M(G))
extends the action of
L_{1}(G)
on
E
to
M(G)
(see
[
8
, Theorem 5.6.34]
,
[
14
]
). For
any
g\in G
and
x\in M
, define an action
\alpha:G\times M\rightarrow M

by setting

\alpha(g)x=\delta_{g}.x.\delta_{g^{-1}}+\tilde{\delta}(\delta_{g}).\delta_{g^{-1}},

where
\delta_{g}
denotes the Dirac measure at
g.
Since the dual Banach
L_{1}(G)
-bimodule
M
satisfies (
1
),
\alpha
is an isometric
action of
G
on
M
with bounded orbits, and thus by Theorem
2.3
,
there is
T\in M
such that
\alpha(g)T=T
whenever
M
has uniform
relative normal structure. It follows that

\tilde{\delta}(\delta_{g})=T.\delta_{g}-\delta_{g}.T,

and then Theorem 5.6.39 in
[
8
]
shows that
\delta:L_{1}(G)\rightarrow M
is inner. We are thus led to the following theorem.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group, and let
E
be an essential
Banach
L_{1}(G)
-bimodule. If the dual
L_{1}(G)
-bimodule
E^{\ast}
has
uniform relative normal structure, then any continuous derivation
\delta:L_{1}(G)\rightarrow E^{\ast}
is inner.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group, and let
L_{1}(\mu)
be
an essential Banach
L_{1}(G)
-bimodule, where
\mu
is a
\sigma
-finite
measure on
(E,\Sigma)
or a Radon measure on a locally compact space. Then
any continuous derivation
\delta:L_{1}(G)\rightarrow L_{\infty}(\mu)

(into the dual
L_{1}(G)
-bimodule

) is inner.

In particular, Corollary
2.8
applies to
L_{1}(G)
itself with
the convolution product and in this case gives the theorem of Johnson
[
16
]
.

3.
A fixed point theorem in
B(H,\ell_{\infty})

In 1955, motivated by work of Dixmier and Day on uniformly bounded group
representations, Kadison
[
17
]
raised the problem of whether every
bounded homomorphism
u
from C
∗
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
into
B(H)

is similar to a
\ast
-homomorphism, i.e., does there exist an invertible
operator
S\in B(H)
such that the map
\tilde{u}(x)=S^{-1}u(x)S
satisfies
the condition
\tilde{u}(x^{\ast})=\tilde{u}(x)^{\ast}
? The Kadison
similarity problem is equivalent to a number of other important problems
(see
[
6
,
7
,
13
,
28
,
27
]
), including the derivation problem
[
20
]
–given a C
∗
-algebra
\mathcal{A}\subset B(H)
, is every
derivation
\delta:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow B(H)
inner?

Our observation concerning derivations from a C
∗
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
into
B(H)
is based on the following consequence of Theorem
2.3
in
Section 2.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
B(H,\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma))
be a Banach space of bounded
operators from a (complex) Hilbert space
H
into a (complex)
\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)
for some
\Gamma
. If
G
is a group of isometries
g:B(H,\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma))\rightarrow B(H,\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma))
with bounded
orbits, then there exists
x\in B(H,\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma))
such that
gx=x
for every
g\in G.

Proof.

Since
B(H,\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma))
is isometric to
\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma,H^{\ast})\simeq\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma,H)
by setting
B(H,\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma))\ni F\mapsto f\in\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma,H^{\ast})
,
where

\left\langle h,f(\gamma)\right\rangle=F(h)(\gamma)\ \ \ (h\in H,\gamma\in\Gamma),

it suffices to show, using Theorem
2.3
, that
\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma,H)
has uniform relative normal structure. It is quite simple (see also
[
30
, Proposition]
). For a bounded set
M\subset\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma,H)
,
let

M_{i}=\{x_{i}\in H:(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\in M\}\ \ \ (i\in\Gamma).

Then, for each
i
, there exists
z_{i}\in\co M_{i}
such that

\left\|x_{i}-z_{i}\right\|\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\diam M_{i}\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\diam M\ \ \ (x_{i}\in M_{i},i\in\Gamma).

Now suppose that
M\subset B((y_{\gamma}),\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\diam M)
for some
(y_{\gamma})\in\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma,H).
Then
M_{i}\subset B(y_{i},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\diam M)
for each
i
, and hence also
\co M_{i}\subset B(y_{i},\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\diam M)
. It follows that

\left\|z_{i}-y_{i}\right\|\leq\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\diam M

for every
i\in\Gamma
, which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The method of proof carries over to
\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma,X)
when
X
is
uniformly convex (see
[
30
]
), and more generally when
X
has uniform
normal structure (see
[
1
, Theorem VI.2.2]
).

Given a Hilbert space
H
, we can embed it (linearly) isometrically into
\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma),
where
\Gamma=B_{H}
(or any norming subset of
B_{H}
) by setting

H\ni h\mapsto(\left\langle h,\gamma\right\rangle)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\in\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma).

Let
\tilde{H}
denote the isometric copy of
H
in
\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)
, and define

\kappa(a)(\left\langle h,\gamma\right\rangle)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}=(\left\langle ah,\gamma\right\rangle)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\ \ \ (a\in B(H),(\left\langle h,\gamma\right\rangle)_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\in\tilde{H}).

Then
\kappa:B(H)\rightarrow B(\tilde{H})
is a linear, isometric, unital
\ast
-isomorphism, i.e.,
\kappa(ab)=\kappa(a)\circ\kappa(b)
and
\kappa(a^{\ast})=\kappa(a)^{\ast}
for every
a,b\in B(H)
. From now on
we will regard
H
as a subspace of
\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)
, and
B(H)

as a subspace of
B(H,\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma))
–the Banach space of
bounded linear operators from
H
into
\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)
.

Now let
\mathcal{A}\subset B(H)\subset B(H,\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma))
be a
unital C
∗
-algebra and extend each unitary
g\in U(\mathcal{A})
to
a linear isometry
\tilde{g}:\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)\rightarrow\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)
by setting

\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)\ni(z_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mapsto(z_{g^{\ast}\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\in\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma).

Then

(\tilde{g}\circ\tilde{h})(z_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}=\tilde{g}((z_{h^{\ast}\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma})=(z_{h^{\ast}g^{\ast}\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}=\widetilde{(g\circ h)}(z_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}

for every
g,h\in U(\mathcal{A}),(z_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\in\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
. In other words,
(g,(z_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma})

\mapsto\tilde{g}((z_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma})
is a left action of
U(\mathcal{A})
on
\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)
.

We are thus led to the following factorization theorem.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subset B(H)\subset B(H,\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma))
be a unital C
∗
-algebra, and let
\delta:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow B(H)
be a derivation (with the usual product operation). Then there exists
a bounded linear operator
T:H\rightarrow\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)
such
that
\delta(g)=Tg-\tilde{g}T
for every
g\in U(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

The proof follows the arguments in Theorem
2.6
. For any unitary
g\in U(\mathcal{A})
and
x\in X=B(H,\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma))
, set

\alpha(g)x=\tilde{g}xg^{-1}+\delta(g)g^{-1},

and note that
\alpha:U(\mathcal{A})\times X\rightarrow X
is an isometric
action of
U(\mathcal{A})
on
X
with bounded orbits. Hence Theorem
3.1
yields
\alpha(g)T=T
for some
T\in X
. It follows that

\delta(g)=Tg-\tilde{g}T

for each unitary element
g
, which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Note that a similar result holds for derivations from
\ell_{1}(G)
to
B(H)
on which
G
acts by isometries, and it is well known
that there are non-inner derivations from
\ell_{1}(F_{2})
to
B(\ell_{2}(F_{2}))
(see, e.g.,
[
25
]
). However, in the case of C
∗
-algebras, the Christensen theorem (see
[
7
]
) asserts that a
derivation
\delta:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow B(H)
is inner if and only if it
is completely bounded, and we can use Theorem
3.3
to study complete
boundedness of
\delta
. Let
T:H\rightarrow\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)
be a
bounded linear operator such that
\delta(g)=Tg-\tilde{g}T
for every
unitary
g\in U(\mathcal{A})
. Following
[
26
]
, define

S=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I&T\\
0&I\end{array}\right):H\oplus_{2}H\rightarrow\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)\oplus_{2}\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma),

where
X=\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)\oplus_{2}\ell_{\infty}(\Gamma)

denotes a Banach space with the norm
\left\|(x_{1},x_{2})\right\|=(\left\|x_{1}\right\|^{2}+\left\|x_{2}\right\|^{2})^{1/2}
.
Clearly,
H\oplus_{2}H
is a Hilbert subspace of
X
, and note that
S
is
an invertible operator onto
Y=S(H\oplus_{2}H)
with the inverse
S^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I&-T\\
0&I\end{array}\right).
Furthermore, if we define for
g\in U(\mathcal{A)}
,

\displaystyle u(g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g&-\delta(g)\\
0&g\end{array}\right):H\oplus_{2}H\rightarrow H\oplus_{2}H,

\displaystyle\tilde{G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\tilde{g}&0\\
0&\tilde{g}\end{array}\right):X\rightarrow X,

we get
u(g)=S^{-1}\tilde{G}S
and hence
\tilde{G}_{\mid Y}=Su(g)S^{-1}:Y\rightarrow Y
for each unitary
g
. Thus we obtain a
homomorphism

\mathcal{A}\ni a\rightarrow Su(a)S^{-1}\in B(Y)

from
\mathcal{A}
into
B(Y)
that extends the group homomorphism

U(\mathcal{A)}\ni g\rightarrow\tilde{G}_{\mid Y}\in B(Y),

and complete boundedness of this homomorphism is equivalent to innerity of
the derivation.

Error analyses of Sinc-collocation methods for exponential decay initial value problems Note: This work was partially supported by JSPS Grant-in-Aid for Scientific Research (C) JP23K03218. Journal: Elsevier
Tomoaki Okayama, Ryota Hara, Shun’ichi Goto

Abstract
Nurmuhammad et al. developed the Sinc-Nyström methods for initial value problems in which the solutions exhibit exponential decay end behavior. In these methods, the Single-Exponential (SE) transformation or the Double-Exponential (DE) transformation is combined with the Sinc approximation. Hara and Okayama improved on these transformations to attain a better convergence rate, which was later supported by theoretical error analyses. However, these methods have a computational drawback owing to the inclusion of a special function in the basis functions. To address this issue, Okayama and Hara proposed Sinc-collocation methods, which do not include any special function in the basis functions. This study conducts error analyses of these methods.

1
Introduction and summary

This study focuses on numerical solutions
for systems of initial value problems of the following form:

\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{y}^{\prime}(t)=K(t)\boldsymbol{y}(t)+\boldsymbol{g}(t),\quad t\geq 0,\\
\boldsymbol{y}(0)=\boldsymbol{r},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
K(t)
is an
m\times m
matrix whose
(i,j)
elements are

k_{ij}(t)
, and
\boldsymbol{y}(t)
,
\boldsymbol{g}(t)
, and
\boldsymbol{r}
are

m
-dimensional vectors. In this study, the solution
\boldsymbol{y}(t)
is
assumed to decay exponentially as
t\to\infty
.
For such a case,
Nurmuhammad et al.
[
4
]

proposed the Sinc-Nyström methods
by means of the Sinc indefinite integration
and two types of variable transformations:
Single-Exponential (SE) transformation and
the Double-Exponential (DE) transformation.
In their numerical experiments,
these methods exhibited exponential convergence with respect to the number
of sampling points
l
,
which is much faster than polynomial convergence,
such as
\OO(l^{-4})
(e.g., classic Runge–Kutta or Gauss–Legendre method).
It should be noted that
such a fast convergence was also observed for a stiff problem.

The convergence was further improved by
Hara and Okayama
[
2
]
.
They improved the SE and DE transformations in the Sinc-Nyström methods,
and showed their superiority by numerical experiments.
They
[
3
]
later theoretically showed that
the Sinc-Nyström method combined with the SE transformation
(called SE-Sinc-Nyström method) can attain
\OO(\exp(-c\sqrt{l}))
,
and the Sinc-Nyström method combined with the DE transformation
(called DE-Sinc-Nyström method) can attain
\OO(\exp(-cl/\log l))
.
These convergence rates were derived
with the assumptions that
\|A_{lm}^{-1}\|_{\infty}
and
\|B_{lm}^{-1}\|_{\infty}
do not
diverge exponentially with respect to
l
,
where
A_{lm}
and
B_{lm}

denote the coefficient matrices of the system of linear equations
for the SE- and DE-Sinc-Nyström methods, respectively.
These assumptions seem reasonable, given their numerical observations.

However, the Sinc-Nyström methods have a disadvantage
in terms of computational cost.
This is because the basis functions of these methods include the sine integral,
which is a special function
defined by

\Si(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\sin t}{t}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(1.2)

To eliminate this disadvantage,
Okayama and Hara
[
8
]

proposed Sinc-collocation methods so that
basis functions do not include any special function.
Their methods were derived by means of
the Sinc approximation with a boundary treatment,
combined with the SE/DE transformations.
We note that such methods were already derived for
initial value problems over a finite interval
[
6
]
,
but not derived for the present case,
i.e., initial value problems with exponential decay end behavior
over the semi-infinite interval
(0,\infty)
.
Based on the results of numerical experiments,
they reported that the Sinc-collocation methods
achieved the same precision as the Sinc-Nyström methods,
but with significantly lower computational costs.

The objective of this study is to provide a theoretical explanation
for their report.
The key to this project is the error analysis
of the Sinc approximation with a boundary treatment.
In the case where the SE transformation is used with the Sinc approximation,
the error analysis was provided
[
7
]
,
whereas that was not provided
in the case where the DE transformation is used.
Therefore, we present the error analysis of the latter case.
Then, with the aid of the error analyses of the Sinc-Nyström methods,
we analyze the error of the Sinc-collocation methods.
As a result, it is shown that
the convergence rate of the Sinc-collocation methods
is slightly worse than that of the Sinc-Nyström methods.
This is not a negative but positive result for the Sinc-collocation methods;
we can conclude that
without using any special function,
the Sinc-collocation methods can achieve almost the
same convergence rate as the Sinc-Nyström methods.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
As a preliminary,
we describe the Sinc approximation and the Sinc indefinite integration
and their convergence theorems in Sect.
2
.
The Sinc-Nyström methods and their error analyses are described
in Sect.
3
.
The Sinc-collocation methods and their error analyses,
the main results of this study, are described
in Sect.
4
.
The proofs of the new theorems in Sect.
2

are provided in Sect.
5
.
The proofs of the new theorems in Sect.
4

are provided in Sect.
6
.

2
Sinc approximation and Sinc indefinite integration

2.1
Sinc approximation

The Sinc approximation is a function approximation formula
over the real axis
\mathbb{R}
, which is expressed as

F(x)\approx\sum_{j=-M}^{N}F(jh)S(j,h)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

(2.1)

where
S(k,h)(x)
is the so-called “Sinc function” defined by

S(j,h)(x)=\frac{\sin[\piup(x-jh)/h]}{\piup(x-jh)/h},

and
h
,
M
, and
N
are suitably selected depending on
a given positive integer
n
.

2.1.1
SE-Sinc approximation and its convergence theorem

To apply the Sinc approximation (
2.1
),

F
should be defined on the entire real axis
\mathbb{R}
.
If the function to be approximated, denoted by
f(t)
, is
defined for
t\geq 0
,
we should employ a variable transformation that maps
\mathbb{R}
onto

(0,\infty)
.
Especially in the case where
f(t)
decays exponentially as
t\to\infty
,
such as
f(t)=\sqrt{t}\E^{-t}
,
the following variable transformation

t=\psi(x)=\log(1+\E^{x})

was proposed
[
12
]
.
We refer to this transformation as the Single-Exponential (SE) transformation.
With the SE transformation, putting
F(x)=f(\psi(x))
,
we can apply (
2.1
) as

f(\psi(x))\approx\sum_{j=-M}^{N}f(\psi(jh))S(j,h)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

which is equivalent to

f(t)\approx\sum_{j=-M}^{N}f(\psi(jh))S(j,h)(\psi^{-1}(t)),\quad t\in(0,\infty).

(2.2)

We refer to this approximation as the SE-Sinc approximation.

For efficient approximation through (
2.1
),

F
should be analytic on a strip domain

\mathscr{D}_{d}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:|\operatorname{Im}\zeta|<d\}

for a positive constant
d
. Therefore,
for efficient approximation through (
2.2
),

f
should be analytic on a translated domain

\psi(\mathscr{D}_{d})=\{z=\psi(\zeta):\zeta\in\mathscr{D}_{d}\}.

Actually, the convergence of the SE-Sinc approximation was
analyzed as follows.

Theorem 2.1
(Okayama et al.
[
12
, Theorem 2.2]
)

Assume that
f
is analytic in
\psi(\mathscr{D}_{d})
with
0<d<\piup
.
Furthermore, assume that
there exist positive constants
C_{\ddagger}
,
\alpha
, and
\beta

such that

|f(z)|\leq C_{\ddagger}\left|\frac{z}{1+z}\right|^{\alpha}|\E^{-z}|^{\beta}

(2.3)

holds for all
z\in\psi(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
, let
M
and
N
be defined as

\left\{\begin{aligned} M&=n,&N&=\left\lceil\frac{\alpha}{\beta}n\right\rceil&&(\text{if}\,\,\,\mu=\alpha),\\
N&=n,&M&=\left\lceil\frac{\beta}{\alpha}n\right\rceil&&(\text{if}\,\,\,\mu=\beta),\end{aligned}\right.

(2.4)

and let
h
be defined as

h=\sqrt{\frac{\piup d}{\mu n}}.

(2.5)

Then, there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\left|f(t)-\sum_{j=-M}^{N}f(\psi(jh))S(j,h)(\psi^{-1}(t))\right|\leq C\sqrt{n}\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\mu n}}.

2.1.2
SE-Sinc approximation with a boundary treatment and its convergence theorem

According to Theorem
2.1
,

f
should satisfy (
2.3
),
which requires
f
to be zero at the boundary of
(0,\infty)
.
Here, let
\tilde{f}
be a function with general boundary values

\left\{\begin{aligned} \lim_{t\to\infty}\tilde{f}(t)&=p,\\
\lim_{t\to 0}\tilde{f}(t)&=q.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.6)

Okayama and Hamada
[
7
]
considered
the following function

f(t)=\tilde{f}(t)-\frac{q+p(\E^{t}-1)}{\E^{t}},

(2.7)

which is zero at the boundary of
(0,\infty)
.
Then, they considered application of (
2.2
),
which is equivalent to

\tilde{f}(t)\approx\frac{q+p(\E^{t}-1)}{\E^{t}}+\sum_{j=-M}^{N}\left(\tilde{f}(\psi(jh))-\frac{q+p\E^{jh}}{1+\E^{jh}}\right)S(j,h)(\psi^{-1}(t)),\quad t\in(0,\infty).

(2.8)

We refer to this approximation as the SE-Sinc approximation with a
boundary treatment.
Its convergence was analyzed as follows.

Theorem 2.2
(Okayama and Hamada
[
7
, Theorem 3]
)

For a given function
\tilde{f}
,
let
p
and
q
be defined by (
2.6
),
and let
f
be defined by (
2.7
).
Assume that
f
is analytic in
\psi(\mathscr{D}_{d})
with
0<d<\piup
.
Furthermore, assume that
there exist positive constants
C_{\ddagger}
,
\alpha
, and
\beta

such that (
2.3
)
holds for all
z\in\psi(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
, let
M
and
N
be defined as (
2.4
),
and let
h
be defined as (
2.5
).
Then, there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\left|\tilde{f}(t)-\left[\frac{q+p(\E^{t}-1)}{\E^{t}}+\sum_{j=-M}^{N}\left\{\tilde{f}(\psi(jh))-\frac{q+p\E^{jh}}{1+\E^{jh}}\right\}S(j,h)(\psi^{-1}(t))\right]\right|\leq C\sqrt{n}\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\mu n}}.

2.1.3
DE-Sinc approximation and its convergence theorem (new result)

The SE transformation is not the only variable transformation that maps

\mathbb{R}
onto
(0,\infty)
.
In fact, another variable transformation

t=\phi(x)=\log(1+\E^{\piup\sinh x})

was proposed
[
5
]
.
We refer to this transformation as the Double-Exponential (DE) transformation.
With the DE transformation, putting
F(x)=f(\phi(x))
,
we can apply (
2.1
) as

f(\phi(x))\approx\sum_{j=-n}^{n}f(\phi(jh))S(j,h)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

where we choose
M=N=n
in this case.
This is equivalent to

f(t)\approx\sum_{j=-n}^{n}f(\phi(jh))S(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t)),\quad t\in(0,\infty).

(2.9)

We refer to this approximation as the DE-Sinc approximation.

For efficient approximation through (
2.9
),

f
should be analytic on a translated domain

\phi(\mathscr{D}_{d})=\{z=\phi(\zeta):\zeta\in\mathscr{D}_{d}\}.

We provide its convergence theorem as follows.
The proof is given in Sect.
5
.

Theorem 2.3

Assume that
f
is analytic in
\phi(\mathscr{D}_{d})
with
0<d<\piup/2
.
Furthermore, assume that
there exist positive constants
C_{\ddagger}
and
\mu
with
\mu\leq 1

such that

|f(z)|\leq C_{\ddagger}\left|z\right|^{\mu}|\E^{-z}|^{\mu}

(2.10)

holds for all
z\in\phi(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
h
be defined as

h=\frac{\arsinh(dn/\mu)}{n}.

(2.11)

Then, there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\left|f(t)-\sum_{j=-n}^{n}f(\phi(jh))S(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))\right|\leq C\E^{-\piup dn/\arsinh(dn/\mu)}.

Remark 2.1

The error of (
2.9
) was also
analyzed by Okayama
[
5
, Theorem 2.9]
.
However, in the existing theorem, the formula of
h
is set as

h=\frac{\log(2dn/\mu)}{n},

which is different from (
2.11
).
In the present paper, we set
h
as (
2.11
),
and therefore we establish another theorem here.

2.1.4
DE-Sinc approximation with a boundary treatment and its convergence theorem (new result)

By the condition (
2.10
),
Theorem
2.3
also requires

f
to be zero at the boundary of
(0,\infty)
.
In the case of
\tilde{f}
with general boundary values,
we consider the same function
f
as (
2.7
).
Then, we apply (
2.9
),
which is equivalent to

\tilde{f}(t)\approx\frac{q+p(\E^{t}-1)}{\E^{t}}+\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\left(\tilde{f}(\phi(jh))-\frac{q+p\E^{\piup\sinh(jh)}}{1+\E^{\piup\sinh(jh)}}\right)S(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t)),\quad t\in(0,\infty).

(2.12)

We refer to this approximation as the DE-Sinc approximation with a
boundary treatment.
We provide its convergence theorem as follows.
The proof is given in Sect.
5
.

Theorem 2.4

For a given function
\tilde{f}
,
let
p
and
q
be defined by (
2.6
),
and let
f
be defined by (
2.7
).
Assume that
f
is analytic in
\phi(\mathscr{D}_{d})
with

.
Furthermore, assume that
there exist positive constants
C_{\ddagger}
and
\mu
with
\mu\leq 1

such that (
2.10
)
holds for all
z\in\phi(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
h
be defined as (

).
Then, there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\left|\tilde{f}(t)-\left[\frac{q+p(\E^{t}-1)}{\E^{t}}+\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\left\{\tilde{f}(\phi(jh))-\frac{q+p\E^{\piup\sinh(jh)}}{1+\E^{\piup\sinh(jh)}}\right\}S(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))\right]\right|\leq C\E^{-\piup dn/\arsinh(dn/\mu)}.

2.2
Sinc indefinite integration

Integrating both sides of (
2.1
),
we have

\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}F(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\approx\sum_{j=-M}^{N}F(jh)\int_{-\infty}^{\xi}S(j,h)(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{j=-M}^{N}F(jh)J(j,h)(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R},

(2.13)

where

J(j,h)(x)=h\left\{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\piup}\Si\left(\frac{\piup(x-jh)}{h}\right)\right\}.

Here,
\Si(x)
is the sine integral defined by (
1.2
).
The approximation (
2.13
) is called
the Sinc indefinite integration.
Similar to the Sinc approximation,
the Sinc indefinite integration is frequently combined with
a variable transformation.

2.2.1
SE-Sinc indefinite integration and its convergence theorem

In the case of the following integral

\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad t\in(0,\infty),

where
f(s)
decays exponentially as
s\to\infty
,
the SE transformation
s=\psi(x)
allows us to apply
the Sinc indefinite integration (
2.13
) as

\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{-\infty}^{\psi^{-1}(t)}f(\psi(x))\psi^{\prime}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\approx\sum_{j=-M}^{N}f(\psi(jh))\psi^{\prime}(jh)J(j,h)(\psi^{-1}(t)),\quad t\in(0,\infty).

We refer to this approximation as the SE-Sinc indefinite integration.
Its convergence was analyzed as follows.

Theorem 2.5
(Hara and Okayama
[
1
, Theorem 2]
)

Assume that
f
is analytic in
\psi(\mathscr{D}_{d})
with
0<d<\piup
.
Furthermore, assume that there exists positive constants

C_{\ddagger}
,
\beta
and
\alpha
with
0<\alpha\leq 1
such that

|f(z)|\leq C_{\ddagger}\left|\frac{z}{1+z}\right|^{\alpha-1}|\E^{-z}|^{\beta}

(2.14)

holds for all
z\in\psi(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
, let
M
and
N
be defined as (
2.4
),
and let
h
be defined as (
2.5
).
Then, there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\left|\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s-\sum_{j=-M}^{N}f(\psi(jh))\psi^{\prime}(jh)J(j,h)(\psi^{-1}(t))\right|\leq C\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\mu n}}.

2.2.2
DE-Sinc indefinite integration and its convergence theorem

In the case of the Sinc indefinite integration as well,
we may use the DE transformation instead of the SE transformation.
If the DE transformation is employed, we have

\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{-\infty}^{\phi^{-1}(t)}f(\phi(x))\phi^{\prime}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\approx\sum_{j=-M}^{N}f(\phi(jh))\phi^{\prime}(jh)J(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t)),\quad t\in(0,\infty).

We refer to this approximation as the DE-Sinc indefinite integration.
Its convergence was analyzed as follows.

Theorem 2.6
(Hara and Okayama
[
3
, Theorem 2]
)

Assume that
f
is analytic in
\phi(\mathscr{D}_{d})
with
0<d<\piup/2
.
Furthermore, assume that there exists positive constants

C_{\ddagger}
,
\beta
and
\alpha
with
0<\alpha\leq 1

such that (
2.14
)
holds for all
z\in\psi(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
\mu=\min\{\alpha,\beta\}
, let
M
and
N
be defined as

\left\{\begin{aligned} M&=n,&N&=\left\lceil\frac{1}{h}\arsinh\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta}\sinh(nh)\right)\right\rceil&&(\text{if}\,\,\,\mu=\alpha),\\
N&=n,&M&=\left\lceil\frac{1}{h}\arsinh\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\sinh(nh)\right)\right\rceil&&(\text{if}\,\,\,\mu=\beta),\end{aligned}\right.

(2.15)

and let
h
be defined as (
2.11
).
Then, there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
n
such that

\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\left|\int_{0}^{t}f(s)\,\mathrm{d}s-\sum_{j=-M}^{N}f(\phi(jh))\phi^{\prime}(jh)J(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))\right|\leq C\frac{\arsinh(dn/\mu)}{n}\E^{-\piup dn/\arsinh(dn/\mu)}.

3
Sinc-Nyström methods

In this section, we describe two Sinc-Nyström methods
and their error analyses presented in Hara and Okayama
[
3
]
.
The derivation of the methods is through the following two steps:
(i) by integration, rewrite the given problem (
1.1
) as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{y}(t)=\boldsymbol{r}+\int_{0}^{t}\{K(s)\boldsymbol{y}(s)+\boldsymbol{g}(s)\}\,\mathrm{d}s,

(3.1)

and (ii) apply the SE-Sinc indefinite integration
or the DE-Sinc indefinite integration to the integral.
We explain these methods individually.

3.1
SE-Sinc-Nyström method and its error analysis

Let
l=M+N+1
and let
\boldsymbol{y}^{(l)}(t)
be an approximate solution of

\boldsymbol{y}(t)
.
Approximating the integral in (
3.1
) based on
Theorem
2.5
, we can derive

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{y}^{(l)}(t)=\boldsymbol{r}+\sum_{j=-M}^{N}\Bigl\{K(\psi(jh))\boldsymbol{y}^{(l)}(\psi(jh))+\boldsymbol{g}(\psi(jh))\Bigr\}\psi^{\prime}(jh)J(j,h)(\psi^{-1}(t)).

(3.2)

To determine the unknown coefficients
\boldsymbol{y}^{(l)}(\psi(jh))
, we set
sampling points at
t=\psi(ih)

(i=-M,\,-M+1,\,\ldots,\,N)
.
We then obtain a system of linear equations given by

\displaystyle(I_{m}\otimes I_{l}-\{I_{m}\otimes(hI_{l}^{(-1)}D^{(\psi)}_{l})\}[K_{ij}^{(\psi)}])\boldsymbol{Y}^{(\psi)}=\boldsymbol{R}+\{I_{m}\otimes(hI_{l}^{(-1)}D^{(\psi)}_{l})\}\boldsymbol{G}^{(\psi)},

(3.3)

where
I_{l}
and
I_{m}
are identity matrices,
\otimes
denotes the Kronecker
product, and
I^{(-1)}_{l}
is an
l\times l
matrix whose
(i,j)
entries
are defined as

\displaystyle(I^{(-1)}_{l})_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\piup}\Si\left(\piup(i-j)\right)\quad(i,j=-M,\,-M+1,\,\dots,\,N).

Moreover,
D^{(\psi)}_{l}
and
K^{(\psi)}_{ij}
are
l\times l
diagonal
matrices defined as

\displaystyle D^{(\psi)}_{l}

\displaystyle=\diag[\psi^{\prime}(-Mh),\,\dots,\,\psi^{\prime}(Nh)],

\displaystyle K^{(\psi)}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\diag[k_{ij}(\psi(-Mh)),\,\dots,\,k_{ij}(\psi(Nh))],

and
[K^{(\psi)}_{ij}]
is a block matrix whose
(i,j)
entry is

K^{(\psi)}_{ij}~(i,j=1,\,\dots,\,m)
. Furthermore,
\boldsymbol{R}
,

\boldsymbol{Y}^{(\psi)}
, and
\boldsymbol{G}^{(\psi)}
are
lm
-dimensional
vectors defined as follows:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{R}

\displaystyle=[r_{1},\,\dots,\,r_{1},\,r_{2},\,\dots,\,r_{2},\,\dots,\,r_{m},\,\dots,\,r_{m}]^{\mathrm{T}},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{Y}^{(\psi)}

\displaystyle=[y^{(l)}_{1}(\psi(-Mh)),\,\dots,\,y^{(l)}_{1}(\psi(Nh)),y^{(l)}_{2}(\psi(-Mh)),\,\dots,\,y^{(l)}_{2}(\psi(Nh)),\dots,\,y^{(l)}_{m}(\psi(-Mh)),\,\dots,\,y^{(l)}_{m}(\psi(Nh))]^{\mathrm{T}},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{G}^{(\psi)}

\displaystyle=[g_{1}(\psi(-Mh)),\,\dots,\,g_{1}(\psi(Nh)),g_{2}(\psi(-Mh)),\,\dots,\,g_{2}(\psi(Nh)),\dots,\,g_{m}(\psi(-Mh)),\,\dots,\,g_{m}(\psi(Nh))]^{\mathrm{T}}.

By solving (
3.3
), we can obtain the value of

\boldsymbol{y}^{(l)}(\psi(jh))
, from which
\boldsymbol{y}^{(l)}(t)
is
determined through (
3.2
). This procedure is the
SE-Sinc-Nyström method.
Its error was analyzed as follows.

Theorem 3.1
(Hara and Okayama
[
3
, Theorem 3]
)

Let
\beta
be a positive constant,
and let
\alpha
and
d
be constants with
0<\alpha\leq 1
and

0<d<\piup
.
Assume that the function
k_{ij}

(i,j=1,\,\dots,\,m)
is analytic and bounded on
\psi(\mathscr{D}_{d})
,
and
y_{i}
and
g_{i}

(i=1,\,\dots,\,m)

satisfy the assumption of Theorem
2.5
.
Let
h
be set as (
2.5
), and let
M
and
N

be set as (
2.4
).
Let
A_{lm}
be a coefficient matrix of
the system of linear equation (
3.3
), i.e.,

A_{lm}=(I_{m}\otimes I_{l}-\{I_{m}\otimes(hI_{l}^{(-1)}D^{(\psi)}_{l})\}[K_{ij}^{(\psi)}]),

and assume that the inverse matrix of
A_{lm}
exists.
Then, the error of the approximate solution
\boldsymbol{y}^{(l)}(t)

in (
3.2
)
is estimated as

\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\left\{\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\left|y_{i}(t)-y^{(l)}_{i}(t)\right|\right\}\leq\left(C+\hat{C}\|A_{lm}^{-1}\|_{\infty}\right)\sqrt{n}\E^{-\sqrt{\piup d\mu n}},

where
C
and
\hat{C}
are positive constants independent of
n
.

3.2
DE-Sinc-Nyström method and its error analysis

From a comparison of Theorem
2.5
with
Theorem
2.6
,
we can expect that
replacement of
\psi
by
\phi
may accelerate the convergence rate.
Approximating the integral in (
3.1
) based on
Theorem
2.6
, we can derive

Sketch of the proof of Theorem

Theorem 5.1

Assume that
f
is analytic in
\phi(\mathscr{D}_{d})
with
0<d<\piup/2
.
Furthermore, assume that
there exist positive constants
C_{\ddagger}
and
\mu
with
\mu\leq 1

such that (
2.10
)
holds for all
z\in\phi(\mathscr{D}_{d})
.
Let
h
be defined as (
2.11
).
Then, it holds that

\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\left|f(t)-\sum_{j=-n}^{n}f(\phi(jh))S(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))\right|\leq C_{\dagger}\E^{-\piup dn/\arsinh(dn/\mu)},

where
C_{\dagger}
is a positive constant defined as

C_{\dagger}=\frac{2C_{\ddagger}}{\mu\piup^{1-\mu}}\left[\frac{2}{\piup d(1-\E^{-2\piup d/\arsinh(d/\mu)})\cos^{2\mu}((\piup/2)\sin d)\cos^{1+\mu}d}+\frac{\E^{-\piup d(\arsinh(d/\mu)-1)/\arsinh(d/\mu)}}{\arsinh(d/\mu)(1+(d/\mu))^{(1-\mu)/2}}\right].

(5.1)

The proof is organized as follows.
We divide the error into two terms as

\displaystyle\left|f(t)-\sum_{j=-n}^{n}f(\phi(jh))S(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|f(t)-\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}f(\phi(jh))S(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\sum_{j=-\infty}^{-n-1}f(\phi(jh))S(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))+\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}f(\phi(jh))S(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))\right|.

The first term is called the discretization error,
and the second term is called the truncation error.
The discretization error was estimated as follows.

Lemma 5.2
(Okayama
[
5
, Lemma 4.16]
)

Assume that
f
is analytic in
\phi(\mathscr{D}_{d})
with
0<d<\piup/2
.
Furthermore, assume that
there exist positive constants
C_{\ddagger}
and
\mu
with
\mu\leq 1

such that (
2.10
)
holds for all

.
Then, we have

\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\left|f(t)-\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}f(\phi(jh))S(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))\right|\leq\frac{4C_{\ddagger}\E^{-\piup d/h}}{\piup^{2-\mu}d\mu(1-\E^{-2\piup d/h})\cos^{2\mu}((\piup/2)\sin d)\cos^{1+\mu}d}.

We estimate the truncation error as follows.
The proof is given in Sect.
5.2
.

Lemma 5.3

Assume that
there exist positive constants
C_{\ddagger}
and
\mu
with
\mu\leq 1

such that (
2.10
)
holds for all

Then, we have

\sup_{t\in(0,\infty)}\left|\sum_{j=-\infty}^{-n-1}f(\phi(jh))S(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))+\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}f(\phi(jh))S(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))\right|\leq\frac{2C_{\ddagger}\piup^{\mu-1}\E^{-\piup\mu\sinh(nh)}}{\mu h\cosh^{1-\mu}(nh)}.

Combining these two lemmas, we prove Theorem

in Sect.

5.2
Proof of Lemma
5.3

To prove Lemma
5.3
,
the following inequality is useful.

Lemma 5.4
(Okayama
[
5
, Lemma 4.15]
)

For all real numbers
x
and
y
with
|y|<\piup/2
,
we have

|\log(1+\E^{\piup\sinh(x+\I y)})|\leq\frac{1}{\cos((\piup/2)\sin y)\cos y}\cdot\frac{\piup\cosh x}{1+\E^{-\piup\sinh(x)\cos y}}.

Using this lemma, we prove Lemma
5.3
as follows.

Proof 1

Put
F(x)=f(\phi(x))
for short.
Because
|S(j,h)(x)|\leq 1
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
it holds that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=-\infty}^{-n-1}F(jh)S(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))+\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}F(jh)S(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=-\infty}^{-n-1}|F(jh)||S(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))|+\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}|F(jh)||S(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=-\infty}^{-n-1}|F(jh)|+\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}|F(jh)|.

(5.2)

Furthermore, from (
2.10
), using Lemma
5.4

with
y=0
, we have

|F(x)|=|f(\phi(x))|\leq C_{\ddagger}|\log(1+\E^{\piup\sinh x})|^{\mu}\left|\frac{1}{1+\E^{\piup\sinh x}}\right|^{\mu}\leq C_{\ddagger}\frac{(\piup\cosh x)^{\mu}}{(1+\E^{-\piup\sinh x})^{\mu}(1+\E^{\piup\sinh x})^{\mu}}.

Therefore, the right-hand side of (
5.2
)
is further bounded as

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-\infty}^{-n-1}|F(jh)|+\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}|F(jh)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=-\infty}^{-n-1}C_{\ddagger}\frac{(\piup\cosh(jh))^{\mu}}{(1+\E^{-\piup\sinh(jh)})^{\mu}(1+\E^{\piup\sinh(jh)})^{\mu}}+\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}C_{\ddagger}\frac{(\piup\cosh(jh))^{\mu}}{(1+\E^{-\piup\sinh(jh)})^{\mu}(1+\E^{\piup\sinh(jh)})^{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2C_{\ddagger}}{h}\cdot h\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{\piup^{\mu}\cosh^{\mu}(jh)\E^{-\piup\mu\sinh(jh)}}{(1+\E^{-\piup\sinh(jh)})^{2\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C_{\ddagger}}{h}\cdot h\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{\piup^{\mu}\cosh^{\mu}(jh)\E^{-\piup\mu\sinh(jh)}}{(1+0)^{2\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C_{\ddagger}}{h}\int_{nh}^{\infty}\piup^{\mu}\cosh^{\mu}x\E^{-\piup\mu\sinh x}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Here, because
0<\mu\leq 1
, it holds for
x\geq nh
that

\cosh^{\mu}x=\frac{\cosh x}{\cosh^{1-\mu}x}\leq\frac{\cosh x}{\cosh^{1-\mu}(nh)},

from which we have

\displaystyle\frac{2C_{\ddagger}}{h}\int_{nh}^{\infty}\piup^{\mu}\cosh^{\mu}x\E^{-\piup\mu\sinh x}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{2C_{\ddagger}}{h}\int_{nh}^{\infty}\piup^{\mu}\frac{\cosh x}{\cosh^{1-\mu}(nh)}\E^{-\piup\mu\sinh x}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{2C_{\ddagger}\piup^{\mu-1}}{\mu h\cosh^{1-\mu}(nh)}\E^{-\piup\mu\sinh(nh)}.

This is the desired estimate.

5.3
Proof of Theorem
5.1

To prove Theorem
5.1
,
we prepare the following three propositions.

Proposition 5.5
(Okayama and Kawai
[
9
, Proposition 7]
)

Let
\tilde{q}
be a function defined by

\tilde{q}(x)=\frac{x}{\arsinh x}.

Then,
\tilde{q}(x)
monotonically increases for
x\geq 0
.

Proposition 5.6

Let
\tilde{p}
be a function defined by

\tilde{p}(x)=\frac{\arsinh x}{x}\sqrt{1+x^{2}}.

Then,
\tilde{p}(x)
monotonically decreases for
x\geq 0
.

Proof 2

Putting
t=\arsinh x
, we have
\tilde{p}(x)=\tilde{r}(t)
, where

\tilde{r}(t)=\frac{t}{\tanh t}.

Differentiating
\tilde{r}(t)
, we have

\tilde{r}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{\cosh t\sinh t-t}{\sinh^{2}t}\geq\frac{1\cdot\sinh t-t}{\sinh^{2}t}\geq 0

for
t\geq 0
. Thus, it holds for
x\geq 0
that

\tilde{p}^{\prime}(x)=\tilde{r}^{\prime}(\arsinh x)\left(\arsinh x\right)^{\prime}=\tilde{r}^{\prime}(\arsinh x)\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}\geq 0,

which is to be demonstrated.

Proposition 5.7

Let
w
be a function defined by

w(x)=(1+x^{2})\exp\left\{-2\piup x\left(1-\frac{1}{\arsinh x}\right)\right\}.

Then,
w(x)
monotonically decreases for
x\geq 0
.

Proof 3

Differentiating
w(x)
, we have

\displaystyle w^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-2\left\{x+\frac{\piup(1+x^{2})}{\arsinh^{2}x}\left(\arsinh^{2}x-\arsinh x+\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^{2}}}\right)\right\}\exp\left\{-2\piup x\left(1-\frac{1}{\arsinh x}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=-2\left\{x+\frac{\piup(1+x^{2})}{\arsinh^{2}x}v(\arsinh x)\right\}\exp\left\{-2\piup x\left(1-\frac{1}{\arsinh x}\right)\right\},

where
v(t)=t^{2}-t+\tanh t
.
Differentiating
v(t)
, we have

\displaystyle v^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=2t-1+\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}t},

\displaystyle v^{\prime\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=2\left(\frac{\cosh^{3}t-\sinh t}{\cosh^{3}t}\right)\geq 2\left(\frac{\cosh t-\sinh t}{\cosh^{3}t}\right)=2\left(\frac{\E^{-t}}{\cosh^{3}t}\right)>0.

Therefore,
v^{\prime}(t)
monotonically increases, from which we have

v^{\prime}(t)\geq v^{\prime}(0)=0
for
t\geq 0
.
Therefore,
v(t)
also monotonically increases for
t\geq 0
,
from which we have
v(t)\geq v(0)=0
for
t\geq 0
.
Thus, it holds for
x\geq 0
that

w^{\prime}(x)=-2\left\{x+\frac{\piup(1+x^{2})}{\arsinh^{2}x}v(\arsinh x)\right\}\exp\left\{-2\piup x\left(1-\frac{1}{\arsinh x}\right)\right\}\leq 0,

which is to be demonstrated.

Using these propositions,
we prove Theorem
5.1
as follows.

Proof 4

From Lemmas
5.2
and
5.3
,
we have

\displaystyle\left|f(t)-\sum_{j=-n}^{n}f(\phi(jh))S(j,h)(\phi^{-1}(t))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4C_{\ddagger}}{\piup^{2-\mu}d\mu(1-\E^{-2\piup d/h})\cos^{2\mu}((\piup/2)\sin d)\cos^{1+\mu}d}\E^{-\piup d/h}+\frac{2C_{\ddagger}\piup^{\mu-1}}{\mu h\cosh^{1-\mu}(nh)}\E^{-\piup\mu\sinh(nh)}.

As for the first term,
substituting (
2.11
) and using
\tilde{q}(x)

defined in Proposition
5.5
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{4C_{\ddagger}}{\piup^{2-\mu}d\mu(1-\E^{-2\piup d/h})\cos^{2\mu}((\piup/2)\sin d)\cos^{1+\mu}d}\E^{-\piup d/h}

\displaystyle=\frac{4C_{\ddagger}}{\piup^{2-\mu}d\mu(1-\E^{-2\piup\mu\tilde{q}(dn/\mu)})\cos^{2\mu}((\piup/2)\sin d)\cos^{1+\mu}d}\E^{-\piup dn/\arsinh(dn/\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4C_{\ddagger}}{\piup^{2-\mu}d\mu(1-\E^{-2\piup\mu\tilde{q}(d\cdot 1/\mu)})\cos^{2\mu}((\piup/2)\sin d)\cos^{1+\mu}d}\E^{-\piup dn/\arsinh(dn/\mu)}.

As for the second term,

Proof 5

Lemma 5.12

Let

be a positive constant with

. Then,

(5.3)

Proof 6

Put a function

By the maximum modulus principle,

|P(\log(1+\E^{\piup\sinh\zeta}))|

has its maximum on the boundary of
\mathscr{D}_{d}

or

In what follows, we consider the case

because we can handle the case

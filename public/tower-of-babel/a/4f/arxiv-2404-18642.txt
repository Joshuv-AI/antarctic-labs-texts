On a conjecture of Levesque and Waldschmidt II
Tobias Hilgart, Volker Ziegler

Abstract
. Related to Shank’s notion of simplest cubic fields, the family of parametrised Diophantine equations, x 3 − ( n − 1 ) ​ x 2 ​ y − ( n + 2 ) ​ x ​ y 2 − 1 = ( x − λ 0 ​ y ) ​ ( x − λ 1 ​ y ) ​ ( x − λ 2 ​ y ) = ± 1 , x^{3}-(n-1)x^{2}y-(n+2)xy^{2}-1=\left(x-\lambda_{0}y\right)\left(x-\lambda_{1}y\right)\left(x-\lambda_{2}y\right)=\pm 1, was studied and solved effectively by Thomas and later solved completely by Mignotte. An open conjecture of Levesque and Waldschmidt [ 4 ] states that taking these parametrised Diophantine equations and twisting them not only once but twice, in the sense that we look at f n , s , t ​ ( x , y ) = ( x − λ 0 s ​ λ 1 t ​ y ) ​ ( x − λ 1 s ​ λ 2 t ​ y ) ​ ( x − λ 2 s ​ λ 0 t ​ y ) = ± 1 , f_{n,s,t}(x,y)=\left(x-\lambda_{0}^{s}\lambda_{1}^{t}y\right)\left(x-\lambda_{1}^{s}\lambda_{2}^{t}y\right)\left(x-\lambda_{2}^{s}\lambda_{0}^{t}y\right)=\pm 1, retains a result similar to what Thomas obtianed in the original or Levesque and Waldschidt in the once-twisted ( t = 0 t=0 ) case; namely, that non-trivial solutions can only appear in equations where the parameters are small. 1.
Motivation and Statement

A simplest cubic field, according to Shanks
[
8
]
, is a cyclic (cubic) field, i.e. where the Galois action is a Moebius transform. They have nice arithmetic properties and, in particular, a fundamental system of units is explicitly known. Shanks constructed many such simplest cubic fields using the polynomials

f_{n}(x)=x^{3}-(n-1)x^{2}-(n+2)x-1,

whose discriminant is
(n^{2}+n+7)^{2}
, which in the case of primality of the inner term is also the discriminant of the associated number field. A fundamental system of untis for the number field
\mathbb{K}_{n}=\mathbb{Q}[X]/(f_{n})
is given by any pair of roots of
f_{n}
(see
[
8
]
or
[
9
]
).

Thomas
[
10
]
considered a homogenised version of these polynomials,

f_{n}(x,y)=f_{n}\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)y^{3}=x^{3}-(n-1)x^{2}y-(n+2)xy-y^{3},

and the equations
f_{n}(x,y)=\pm 1
, where
n\geq 0
. He effectively solved this infinite family of Thue equations, i.e. solved the equations where the parameter
n
is at least
1.365\times 10^{7}
, and was one of the first to obtain such a result; Mignotte
[
5
]
later completed the work by also solving the cases where
n<1.365\times 10^{7}
. A thorough investigation of such Thue equations and their correspondence to the simplest cubic fields, and in what sense the simplest cubic fields are unique, has been done by Hoschi
[
3
]
, following unpublished work of Okazaki
[
6
]
and building upon
[
7
]
.

Levesque and Waldschmidt
[
4
]
considered a twisted version of Thomas’ equations. If
f_{n}(x,y)
factorises to
(x-\lambda_{0}\,y)(x-\lambda_{1}\,y)(x-\lambda_{2}\,y)
, then they took each root to the
s
-th power and considered the equations

f_{n,s}(x,y)=\left(x-\lambda_{0}^{s}\,y\right)\left(x-\lambda_{1}^{s}\,y\right)\left(x-\lambda_{2}^{s}\,y\right)=\pm 1,

now parametrised by integers
n\geq 0
and
s\geq 1
. They effectively solved this infinite family of Thue equations and obtained a result similar to that of Thomas.

Since the number fields
\mathbb{K}_{n}
involved in these equations are of unit rank
2
, and any two of the three roots
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
form a fundamental system of units for
\mathbb{K}_{n}
, Levesque and Waldschmidt conjectured
[
4
]
*Conjecture 1.1 that a similar result to the one they obtained would hold if one considered the conjugates of
\lambda_{0}^{s}\lambda_{1}^{t}
instead of
\lambda_{0}^{s}
; they formulated this in the following

Conjecture 1
.

There exists a positive absolute constant
\kappa
with the following porperty: If
n,s,t,x,y,m
are integers satisfying

\max\left\{\left|x\right|,\left|y\right|\right\}\geq 2,\quad(s,t)\neq(0,0)\;\text{ and }\;0<\left|f_{n,s,t}(x,y)\right|\leq m,

then

\max\left\{\log\left|n\right|,\left|s\right|,\left|t\right|,\log\left|x\right|,\log\left|y\right|\right\}\leq\kappa\left(1+\log m\right).

For
m=1
the conjecture recovers the direct generalisation (
t
can be something other than
0
) of their generalisation (
s
can be something other than
1
) of Thomas’ original result. Another way of looking at these equations is to take any unit
\rho\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbb{K}_{n}}^{\times}
with conjugates
\rho_{2}
and
\rho_{3}
and the equations

f_{n,\rho}(x,y)=\left(x-\rho\,y\right)\left(x-\rho_{2}\,y\right)\left(x-\rho_{3}\,y\right)=\pm 1,

and range over all integers
n
and units
\rho\in\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbb{K}_{n}}^{\times}
instead of integers
s
and
t
.

The original result of Levesque and Waldschmidt
[
4
]
*Theorem 1.1 proves Conjecture
1
in the case that
t=0
and
m=1
. For
m>1
they could not give an upper bound for
\left|n\right|
. The ”
t=0
” case of Conjecture
1
is already known in this sense, and therefore the focus of our work is to extend it. Our result is as follows:

Theorem 1
.

Let
f_{n}(x)=x^{3}-(n-1)x^{2}-(n+2)x-1
and
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
be its roots in the number field
\mathbb{K}_{n}=\mathbb{Q}[X]/(f_{n})
. Define the norm-form

f_{n,s,t}(x,y)=\N_{\mathbb{K}_{n}/\mathbb{Q}}\left(x-\lambda_{0}^{s}\lambda_{1}^{t}y\right)=\left(x-\lambda_{0}^{s}\lambda_{1}^{t}y\right)\left(x-\lambda_{1}^{s}\lambda_{2}^{t}y\right)\left(x-\lambda_{2}^{s}\lambda_{0}^{t}y\right),

and consider the parametrised family of Thue equations

f_{n,s,t}(x,y)=\pm 1,

where
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
(s,t)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
st\neq 0
and
\max\left\{\left|s\right|,\left|t\right|\right\}\leq n^{1/2-\varepsilon}
for some fixed
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
. Then there exists an effectively computable constant
n_{0}=n_{0}(\varepsilon)
, such that for any
n>n_{0}
and
(s,t)
as above the Thue equation
f_{n,s,t}(x,y)=\pm 1
has no solutions where
\left|y\right|\geq 2
.

While Levesque and Waldschmidt
[
4
]
*p. 540–541 were able to trace the case of negative parameters back to the case where
n\geq 0
and
s\geq 1
due to some symmetries involved , we have not been able to confirm any such symmetries in our equations and thus have to deal with both positive and negative exponents.

For our proof, we were inspired by
[
2
]
, which describes an algorithmic procedure for solving (polynomially, single) parametrised Thue equations. Of course, our starting point is different, since we have not only multiple parameters, but also parameters in the exponents. In Section
2
, we ensure that with a different starting situation and even some necessary conditions in the original paper that do not hold here, their idea is still a valid strategy and we can perform a proof similar to the one they describe.

In the rest of the paper, we use the
O
[
\Omega
] notation to describe asymptotic upper [lower] bounds for
n\to\infty
; the implied constants depend at most only on
\varepsilon
.

2.
Auxiliary Results

In our proof we derive a lower bound for
\log\left|y\right|

Theorem 2.1

Let

\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)

\max\left\{\left|x\right|,\left|y\right|\right\}<\exp\left\{c_{3}R_{\mathbb{M}}\max\left\{\log R_{\mathbb{M}},1\right\}(R_{\mathbb{M}}+\log(HB))\right\},

The roots

Lemma 2.2

Let

Since

\lambda_{0}^{3}=(n-1)\lambda_{0}^{2}+(n+2)\lambda_{0}+1

∎

Let

1
) and get

-v_{1}-v_{2}=\left(2s-t\right)\left(\log n\right)^{2}+\left(-2t+\xi^{\prime}\right)\cfrac{\log n}{n}+\mathop{O}\left(\frac{\log n}{n^{1+2\varepsilon}}\right),

where
\xi^{\prime}\in\left\{0,\pm 1,\pm 2\right\}
, depending on which of the indicators
\mathbb{1}_{2s}(t-1)
and
\mathbb{1}_{s}(2t+1)
is non-zero, if any is at all; we only care that
\xi^{\prime}\geq-2
.

Putting
b_{0}=-2s+t
then gives

\bar{v}=\left(-2s+t\right)R-v_{1}-v_{2}=\left(-2s-t+\xi^{\prime}\right)\cfrac{\log n}{n}+\mathop{O}\left(\frac{\log n}{n^{1+2\varepsilon}}\right).

The system of linear inequalities

\displaystyle 2s

\displaystyle\leq t-1

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle\geq 2t+1

\displaystyle-2s-t-2

\displaystyle\leq 0

has no solutions, and thus indeed
-2s-t+\xi^{\prime}\geq 1
, or
\bar{v}\geq\cfrac{\log n}{n}+\mathop{O}\left(\frac{\log n}{n^{1+2\varepsilon}}\right)
. Subtracting it from the regulator also gives
R-\bar{v}=\mathop{\Omega}\left(\left(\log n\right)^{2}\right)
, which is even more than required.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1

We assume that for sufficiently large
n
there exists a solution
(x,y)
with
\left|y\right|\geq 2
of the Thue equation
f_{n,s,t}(x,y)=\pm 1
and we want to derive a contradiction. We recall that we denote by
\alpha^{(1)},\alpha^{(2)},\alpha^{(3)}
the algebraic elements in the norm-form in the order in which they appear in the theorem, and write
\beta^{(i)}=x-\alpha^{(i)}y
. A solution
(x,y)
of the Thue equation
f_{n,s,t}(x,y)=\pm 1
is then of type
j
, if

\left|\beta^{(j)}\right|=\min\left\{\left|\beta^{(1)}\right|,\left|\beta^{(2)}\right|,\left|\beta^{(3)}\right|\right\}.

By Lemma
2.5
, we only have to consider
j=1
. In this case, we have

2\left|x-\alpha^{(i)}y\right|\geq\left|x-\alpha^{(i)}y\right|+\left|x-\alpha^{(1)}y\right|\geq\left|y\left(\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(i)}\right)\right|,

for
i\in\left\{2,3\right\}
. Applying the inequality to
\left|f_{n,s,t}(x,y)\right|=1
yields

(2)

\left|\beta^{(1)}\right|\leq\cfrac{4}{\left|y\right|^{2}\left|\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(2)}\right|\left|\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(3)}\right|}\leq\cfrac{1}{\left|\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(2)}\right|\left|\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(3)}\right|}.

We express
\log\left|\beta^{(2)}\right|
and
\log\left|\beta^{(3)}\right|
as

\displaystyle\log\left|\beta^{(i)}\right|

\displaystyle=\log\left|x-\alpha^{(1)}y+y\left(\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(i)}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\log\left|y\right|+\log\left|\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(i)}\right|+\log\left|1+\cfrac{\beta^{(1)}}{y\left(\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(i)}\right)}\right|,

and apply both Inequality (
2
) and Lemma
2.8
on the argument of the last logarithm. This gives

\left|\cfrac{\beta^{(1)}}{y\left(\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(i)}\right)}\right|<\cfrac{1}{n},

which is less than
1
. And since we have
\log(1+a)=a+\mathop{O}\left(a^{2}\right)
for
\left|a\right|<1
, this implies

(3)

\log\left|\beta^{(i)}\right|=\log\left|y\right|+\log\left|\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(i)}\right|+\cfrac{\beta^{(1)}}{y\left(\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(i)}\right)}+\mathop{O}\left(n^{-2}\right).

Since
\left\{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}\right\}
is a fundamental system of units in
\mathbb{K}
, there exist
b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\beta^{(1)}=\pm\lambda_{0}^{b_{1}}\lambda_{1}^{b_{2}}
. By conjugating, we get that

\displaystyle\log\left|\beta^{(2)}\right|

\displaystyle=b_{1}\log\left|\lambda_{1}\right|+b_{2}\log\left|\lambda_{2}\right|,

\displaystyle\log\left|\beta^{(3)}\right|

\displaystyle=b_{1}\log\left|\lambda_{2}\right|+b_{2}\log\left|\lambda_{0}\right|.

To this system of linear equations in
b_{1}
and
b_{2}
, we want to apply Cramer’s rule and use Equation (
3
) to express
\log\left|\beta^{(2)}\right|
and
\log\left|\beta^{(3)}\right|
. We have already given an asymptotic for the determinant of the matrix in this system, which is precisely the regulator
R
(up to sign), in Lemma
2.4
.

Cramer’s rule then gives

\displaystyle R\,b_{1}

\displaystyle=u_{1}\log\left|y\right|+v_{1}+\cfrac{\beta^{(1)}}{y}\,w_{1}+\mathop{O}\left(\frac{\log n}{n^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle R\,b_{2}

\displaystyle=u_{2}\log\left|y\right|+v_{2}+\cfrac{\beta^{(1)}}{y}\,w_{2}+\mathop{O}\left(\frac{\log n}{n^{2}}\right),

with

\displaystyle u_{1}

\displaystyle=\begin{vmatrix}1&\log\left|\lambda_{2}\right|\\
\\
1&\log\left|\lambda_{0}\right|\end{vmatrix},\;v_{1}

\displaystyle=\begin{vmatrix}\log\left|\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(2)}\right|&\log\left|\lambda_{2}\right|\\
\\
\log\left|\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(3)}\right|&\log\left|\lambda_{0}\right|\end{vmatrix},\;w_{1}

\displaystyle=\begin{vmatrix}\cfrac{1}{\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(2)}}&\log\left|\lambda_{2}\right|\\
\cfrac{1}{\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(3)}}&\log\left|\lambda_{0}\right|\end{vmatrix},

\displaystyle u_{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{vmatrix}\log\left|\lambda_{1}\right|&1\\
\\
\log\left|\lambda_{2}\right|&1\end{vmatrix},\;v_{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{vmatrix}\log\left|\lambda_{1}\right|&\log\left|\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(2)}\right|\\
\\
\log\left|\lambda_{2}\right|&\log\left|\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(3)}\right|\end{vmatrix},\;w_{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{vmatrix}\log\left|\lambda_{1}\right|&\cfrac{1}{\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(2)}}\\
\log\left|\lambda_{2}\right|&\cfrac{1}{\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(3)}}\end{vmatrix}.

By Lemma
2.3
, the linear combination
\bar{u}=-u_{1}-u_{2}
is a small positive number, namely

(4)

\bar{u}=-\left(\log n-\frac{1}{n}+\mathop{O}\left(n^{-2}\right)\right)-\left(-\log n-\frac{2}{n}+\mathop{O}\left(n^{-2}\right)\right)=\frac{3}{n}+\mathop{O}\left(n^{-2}\right).

We take the same linear combination
\bar{w}=-w_{1}-w_{2}
, and for the integer
b_{0}
from Lemma
2.9
, we define
\bar{v}=b_{0}R-v_{1}-v_{2}
and
\bar{b}=b_{0}-b_{1}-b_{2}
. With these choices we still have

(5)

R\,\bar{b}=\bar{u}\,\log\left|y\right|+\bar{v}+\frac{\beta^{(1)}}{y}\,\bar{w}+\mathop{O}\left(\frac{\log n}{n^{2}}\right).

By Inequality (
2
) and
\left|y\right|\geq 2
, we have
\left|\cfrac{\beta^{(1)}}{y}\,\bar{w}\right|\leq\left|\cfrac{\bar{w}}{2\left|\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(2)}\right|\left|\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(3)}\right|}\right|
. First, we use Lemma
2.3
to write
\bar{w}
as

\displaystyle\bar{w}

\displaystyle=\cfrac{1}{\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(2)}}\left(-\log\left|\lambda_{0}\right|+\log\left|\lambda_{2}\right|\right)+\cfrac{1}{\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(3)}}\left(-\log\left|\lambda_{1}\right|+\log\left|\lambda_{2}\right|\right)

\displaystyle=-\cfrac{\log n}{\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(2)}}+\cfrac{\log n}{\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(3)}}+\mathop{O}\left(\frac{1}{n\min\left\{\left|\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(2)}\right|,\left|\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(2)}\right|\right\}}\right).

We plug this into the inequality

\left|\cfrac{\beta^{(1)}}{y}\,\bar{w}\right|\leq\left|\cfrac{\bar{w}}{2\left|\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(2)}\right|\left|\alpha^{(1)}-\alpha^{(3)}\right|}\right|

and apply Lemma
2.8
, which gives

(6)

\left|\cfrac{\beta^{(1)}}{y}\,\bar{w}\right|<\frac{3}{4}\cfrac{\log n}{n}+\mathop{O}\left(\frac{\log n}{n^{2}}\right);

the constant in Lemma
2.8
ensures that the constant
\frac{3}{4}
of
\frac{\log n}{n}
is smaller than
1
, which allows the next arguments.

Using this bound in conjunction with the asymptotic for
\bar{u}
of Equation (
4
), we can thus derive from Equation (
5
) that

R\,\bar{b}>\cfrac{3\log\left|y\right|}{n}+\bar{v}-\frac{3}{4}\cfrac{\log n}{n}+\mathop{O}\left(\frac{\log n}{n^{2}}\right),

and since by Lemma
2.9
, we have
\bar{v}\geq\frac{\log n}{n}+\mathop{O}\left(\frac{\log n}{n^{1+2\varepsilon}}\right)
, this implies that

R\,\bar{b}>\frac{3\log\left|y\right|}{n}+\left(1-\frac{3}{4}\right)\frac{\log n}{n}+\mathop{O}\left(\frac{\log n}{n^{2}}\right)>0.

Since
\bar{b}
is an integer, it must be at least
1
. Equations (
4
) and (
5
) thus yield

R-\bar{v}-\cfrac{\beta^{(1)}}{y}\,\bar{w}\leq\cfrac{3\log\left|y\right|}{n}+\mathop{O}\left(\frac{\log n}{n^{2}}\right),

and the left-hand side, by Inequality (
6
) and Lemma
2.9
is of order at least
\mathop{\Omega}\left(\log n\right)
. We conclude that

(7)

\log\left|y\right|=\mathop{\Omega}\left(n\,\log n\right).

This gives a contradiction to Theorem
2.1
: We have effectively bounded the regulator
R
of
\mathbb{K}_{n}
in Lemma (
2.4
) by
(\log n)^{2}
and the unit rank is
r=2
. Further, by Lemma
2.3
, an effective upper bound to the coefficients of
f_{n,s,t}
is given by
n^{2\max\left\{\left|s\right|,\left|t\right|\right\}}
. If we plug everything into Theorem
2.1
and take the logarithm, we get

\displaystyle\log\left|y\right|

\displaystyle=\mathop{O}\left(\left(\log n\right)^{2}\log\log n\left(\left(\log n\right)^{2}+\max\left\{\left|s\right|,\left|t\right|\right\}\log n\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathop{O}\left(n^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}\left(\log n\right)^{3}\log\log n\right),

which contradicts Equation (
7
). So provided that
n\geq n_{0}
is sufficiently large, the assumption of a solution with
\left|y\right|\geq 2
is false; we thus conclude the proof of Theorem
1
.

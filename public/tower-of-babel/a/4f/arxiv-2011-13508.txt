An application of a C 2 C^{2} -estimate for a complex Monge-Ampère equation
Chang Li, Lei Ni, Xiaohua Zhu

Abstract
. By studying a complex Monge-Ampère equation, we present an alternate proof to a recent result of Chu-Lee-Tam concerning the projectivity of a compact Kähler manifold N n N^{n} with Ric k < 0 \operatorname{Ric}_{k}<0 for some integer k k with 1 < k < n 1<k<n , and the ampleness of the canonical line bundle K N K_{N} . Dedicated to the 110th anniversary of S. S. Chern with a great honor.

32Q10, 32Q15, 32W20, 53C55

1.
Introduction

In a recent preprint
[
4
]
, the authors proved the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(Chu-Lee-Tam)
.

Assume that
(N^{n},\omega_{g})
is a compact Kähler manifold (
n=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(N)
) with
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}(X,\overline{X})\leq-(k+1)\sigma|X|^{2}
for some
\sigma\geq 0
. Then
K_{N}
is nef and is ample if
\sigma>0
.

The curvature notion
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}
is defined as the Ricci curvature of the
k
-dimensional subspaces of the holomorphic tangent bundle
T^{\prime}N
. Hence it coincides with the holomorphic sectional curvature
H(X)
when
k=1
, and with the Ricci curvature when
k=n=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(N)
. The condition
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}>0
is significantly different from its Riemannian analogue, i.e. the so-called
q
-Ricci of Bishop-Wu
[
2
]
, since it exams only the Ricci curvature of the subspaces in the holomorphic tangent space
T^{\prime}N
, thus unlike its Riemannian analogue,
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}>0
(
<0
) does not imply
\operatorname{Ric}_{k+1}>0
(
<0
). The study of the condition of
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}<0
was initiated by the second author
[
9
]
to generalize the hyperbolicity of Kobayashi to the
k
-hyperbolicity of a compact Kähler manifold. It is closely related to the degeneracy of holomorphic mappings from
\mathbb{C}^{k}
into concerned manifolds (cf. Theorem 1.3 of
[
9
]
). In fact a generalization to Royden’s result was proved there. Moreover it was proved recently by the second author that
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}>0
implies that
M
is projective and rational connected. The above result of Chu-Lee-Tam answers a question raised by the second author in
[
9
]
, namely the projectivity of a compact Kähler manifold with
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}<0
affirmatively. In view of the fact that
\operatorname{Ric}_{1}<0
is the same as the holomorphic section curvature
H<0
, Theorem
1.1
generalizes the earlier work of
[
16
,
15
]
.

The proof of
[
4
]
is via the study of a twisted Kähler-Ricci flow. In this note we provide a direct alternate proof via the Aubin-Yau solution
[
1
,
19
]
to a complex Monge-Ampère equation (which is similar to the equation for the Kähler-Einstein metric in the negative first Chern class). This was the method utilized in
[
16
]
. Comparing with
[
16
]
here a modification on the Monge-Ampère equation (cf. (
3.2
)) is necessary to adapt the method to the curvature condition
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}<0
when
n>k>1
. This makes the derivation of the estimates more involved. The method here also extends to the more general setting considered in
[
4
]
.

It was proved in
[
11
]
that any compact Këhler manifold with the second scalar curvature
S_{2}>0
(simply put
S_{k}
is the average of
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}
) must be projective. It remains an interesting question if
S_{2}<0
, or
\operatorname{Ric}^{\perp}_{k}<0
, also implies the projectivity.
For more backgrounds and references related to the theorem please refer to
[
10
,
9
,
4
]
. One can also find the definitions and motivations of several other curvature notions, including
S_{2},\operatorname{Ric}^{\perp}_{k}
, and problems related to them in
[
10
]
. The geometry of
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}<0
compares sharply with the case of
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}>0
. This is discussed at the end of Section 3 and in the Appendix via a construction of J. M
{}^{\mbox{c}}
Kernan.

2.
Preliminaries

Since the result is known for
k=1,n
below we assume that
1<k<n
in this section and the next.
Here we collect some algebraic estimates as consequences of the assumption
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}(X,\overline{X})\leq-(k+1)\sigma|X|^{2},\forall\,(1,0)
-type tangent vector
X
. They are useful in obtaining key estimates for a Monge-Ampère equation (cf. (
3.2
) below). The first is Lemma 2.1 of
[
4
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Under the assumption that
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}(X,\overline{X})\leq-(k+1)\sigma|X|^{2}
the following estimate holds

(k-1)|X|^{2}\operatorname{Ric}(X,\overline{X})+(n-k)R(X,\overline{X},X,\overline{X})\leq-(n-1)(k+1)\sigma|X|^{4}.

(2.1)

The result follows by summing
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}(X,\overline{X})\leq-(k+1)\sigma|X|^{2}
(the assumption) for a suitable chosen unitary basis.
By using a Royden’s trick
[
12
]
the following result was derived out of Lemma
2.1
(cf.

[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(N,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold with

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}_{k}(X,\overline{X})\leq-(k+1)\sigma|X|^{2}

(2.2)

for some
\sigma\geq 0
. Let
\tilde{\omega}=\omega_{\tilde{g}}
be another Kähler metric on
N
. Set

G=\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{\omega}}\omega.

Then the following estimate holds

2\tilde{g}^{i\bar{j}}\tilde{g}^{k\bar{l}}\mathrm{R}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{l}}\leq\frac{-(n-1)(k+1)\sigma}{n-k}\left(G^{2}+|g|_{\tilde{g}}^{2}\right)-\frac{k-1}{n-k}G\cdot\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{g}}\mathrm{Ric}-\frac{k-1}{n-k}\left\langle\omega,\mathrm{Ric}\right\rangle_{\tilde{g}}.

(2.3)

Proof.

Here we provide a proof using the averaging technique (cf. Appendix of
[
9
]
) instead of Royden’s trick since the argument is more transparent. Pick a normal frame
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}\}
so that
\tilde{g}_{i\bar{j}}=\delta_{ij}
and
g_{i\bar{j}}=|\lambda_{i}|^{2}\delta_{ij}
. Then
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{i}}\}
, with
\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{i}}:=\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{i}}
, is a unitary frame for
\omega
. Lemma
2.1
implies that (Einstein convention applied)

\displaystyle 2(n-k)R^{\omega}_{i\bar{i}j\bar{j}}|\lambda_{i}|^{2}|\lambda_{j}|^{2}+(k-1)G\operatorname{Ric}^{\omega}_{s\bar{s}}|\lambda_{s}|^{2}+(k-1)\operatorname{Ric}^{\omega}_{i\bar{i}}|\lambda_{i}|^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n(n+1)\frac{\ \ }{\ \ }{\hskip-11.38092pt}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}}(k-1)|Y|^{2}\operatorname{Ric}^{\omega}(Y,\overline{Y})+(n-k)R^{\omega}(Y,\overline{Y},Y,\overline{Y})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-(n-1)(k+1)n(n+1)\sigma\frac{\ \ }{\ \ }{\hskip-11.38092pt}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}}|Y|^{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(n-1)(k+1)\sigma\left(G^{2}+|\lambda_{\gamma}|^{4}\right).

Here
Y=\lambda_{i}w^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{i}}
with respect to a normal frame
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{i}}\}
,
\operatorname{Ric}^{\omega}
and
R^{\omega}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{l}}
are the Ricci and curvature tensor expressed with respect to the metric
\omega
(namely the unitary frame
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial w^{i}}\}
.
The result then follows by identifying the terms invariantly.

\square

For the application it is useful to write (
2.3
) as

\displaystyle 2\tilde{g}^{i\bar{j}}\tilde{g}^{k\bar{l}}\mathrm{R}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{l}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{-(n-1)(k+1)\sigma}{n-k}\left(G^{2}+|g|_{\tilde{g}}^{2}\right)-2\frac{k-1}{n-k}G\cdot\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{g}}\mathrm{Ric}

(2.4)

\displaystyle+\frac{k-1}{n-k}\left(\langle\operatorname{Ric},\tilde{\omega}\rangle_{\tilde{g}}\langle\omega,\tilde{\omega}\rangle_{\tilde{g}}-\langle\omega,\operatorname{Ric}\rangle_{\tilde{g}}\right).

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Assume that the canonical line bundle
K_{N}
of
(N,\omega)
is not nef. Then there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that
\epsilon_{0}[\omega]-C_{1}(N)
is nef but not Kähler. Thus,
\forall\epsilon>0
,
(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})[\omega]-C_{1}(N)
is Kähler. This means that there exists a smooth function
\phi_{\epsilon}
such that

\omega_{\epsilon}:={}(\epsilon_{0}+\epsilon)\omega-\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\phi_{\epsilon}>0.

(3.1)

By Aubin-Yau’s existence theorem and a prior estimate for a complex Monge-Ampère equation, we first prove the theorem below.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(N,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold which satisfies (
2.2
) for some
\sigma\geq 0
. Then
K_{N}
is nef.

Proof.

For any
\epsilon>0
, we consider the complex Monge-Ampère equation for
\psi_{\epsilon}
,

\left((\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})\omega-\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}(\phi_{\epsilon}+\psi_{\epsilon})\right)^{n}={}e^{\phi_{\epsilon}+\psi_{\epsilon}+\frac{k-1}{2(n-k)}(\phi_{\epsilon}+\psi_{\epsilon})}\omega^{n}

(3.2)

and

\displaystyle(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})\omega-\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}(\phi_{\epsilon}+\psi_{\epsilon})>0.

(3.3)

By the Aubin-Yau theorem
[
1
,
19
]
, there is a unique solution
\psi_{\epsilon}
of (
3.2
).
For simplicity, let

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}_{\epsilon}:={}

\displaystyle(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})\omega-\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}(\phi_{\epsilon}+\psi_{\epsilon})

(3.4)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\omega_{\epsilon}+\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\psi_{\epsilon},

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}:={}

\displaystyle\phi_{\epsilon}+\psi_{\epsilon}.

Then taking
\partial\bar{\partial}\log(\cdot)
on both sides of (
3.2
), we see that (
3.2
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{Ric}}_{\epsilon}:=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}(\tilde{\omega}_{\epsilon})={}\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)-\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}\left(u_{\epsilon}+\frac{k-1}{2(n-k)}u_{\epsilon}\right)

(3.5)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-\tilde{\omega}_{\epsilon}+(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})\omega-\sqrt{-1}\frac{k-1}{2(n-k)}\partial\bar{\partial}u_{\epsilon}.

Let
G=G_{\epsilon}=\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{\omega}_{\epsilon}}\omega
. Then by the calculation in the proof of the Schwarz Lemma
[
18
]
, and in particular (2.3) of
[
9
]
(also see computations in the earlier work of
[
3
,
8
]
), as well as the
C^{2}
-estimate computations in
[
1
,
13
]
(a slight different calculation was done in
[
19
,
14
]
), we have that

\tilde{\Delta}_{\epsilon}(\log G)\geq\frac{1}{G}\left(\widetilde{\mathrm{Ric}}_{\epsilon_{p\bar{q}}}{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}^{p\bar{j}}{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}^{i\bar{q}}g_{i\bar{j}}-{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}^{i\bar{j}}{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}^{k\bar{l}}\mathrm{R}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{l}}\right).

(3.6)

Applying Lemma
2.2
(namely (
2.4
) to
G=G_{\epsilon}
we have the estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{G}\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}^{i\bar{j}}\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}^{k\bar{l}}\mathrm{R}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{l}}\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{-(n-1)(k+1)\sigma}{2(n-k)}\left(G+\frac{1}{G}|g|_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}^{2}\right)-\frac{k-1}{n-k}\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}\mathrm{Ric}

(3.7)

\displaystyle+\frac{k-1}{2(n-k)}\frac{1}{G}\left(G\cdot\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}\mathrm{Ric}-\left\langle\omega,\mathrm{Ric}\right\rangle_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}\right).

Choosing local coordinates such that
(\tilde{g}_{\epsilon})_{{i\bar{j}}}=\delta_{ij},\quad g_{i\bar{j}}=g_{i\bar{i}}\delta_{ij}
, then we also have

\displaystyle G\cdot\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}\mathrm{Ric}-\left\langle\omega,\mathrm{Ric}\right\rangle_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\mathrm{Ric}_{i\bar{i}}\left(\sum_{k}g_{k\bar{k}}-g_{i\bar{i}}\right)

(3.8)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left(\mathrm{Ric}_{i\bar{i}}(\sum_{k\neq i}g_{k\bar{k}})\right)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left((\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})g_{i\bar{i}}+u_{\epsilon_{i\bar{i}}}\right)\left(\sum_{k}g_{k\bar{k}}-g_{i\bar{i}}\right)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})G^{2}-(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})|g|_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}^{2}+G\cdot\tilde{\Delta}_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon}-\left\langle\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u,\omega\right\rangle_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}.

Here we used (
3.3
) in the third line. Plugging this into (
3.7
), we have

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{G}\tilde{g}^{k\bar{l}}_{\epsilon}\tilde{g}^{i\bar{j}}_{\epsilon}R_{i\bar{j}k\bar{l}}\geq{}

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma(n-1)(k+1)-(k-1)(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})}{2(n-k)}G

(3.9)

\displaystyle+\frac{\sigma(n-1)(k+1)+(k-1)(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})}{2(n-k)}\frac{|g|^{2}_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}}{G}+\frac{(k-1)}{(n-k)}\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}\mathrm{Ric}

\displaystyle-\frac{k-1}{2(n-k)}\tilde{\Delta}_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon}+\frac{k-1}{2(n-k)}\frac{1}{G}\left\langle\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u_{\epsilon},\omega\right\rangle_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}.

On the other hand, a direct calculation using (
3.5
) can express the first term in (
3.6
) as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{G}\widetilde{\mathrm{Ric}}_{\epsilon_{p\bar{q}}}\tilde{g}^{p\bar{j}}_{\epsilon}\tilde{g}^{i\bar{q}}_{\epsilon}g_{i\bar{j}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{G}\left\langle\widetilde{\mathrm{Ric}}_{\epsilon},\omega\right\rangle_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}

(3.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{G}\left\langle-\tilde{w}_{\epsilon}+(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})\omega-\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}(\frac{(k-1)}{2(n-k)}u_{\epsilon}),\omega\right\rangle_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{G}\left\langle-\tilde{w}_{\epsilon}+(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})\omega,\omega\right\rangle_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}-\frac{1}{G}\frac{(k-1)}{2(n-k)}\left\langle\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u_{\epsilon},\omega\right\rangle_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}.

Note that we used (
3.5
) in the third line above.
Combining (
3.6
), (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{\epsilon}(\log G)\geq{}

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma(n-1)(k+1)-(k-1)(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})}{2(n-k)}G

\displaystyle+\frac{\sigma(n-1)(k+1)+(k-1)(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})}{2(n-k)}\frac{|g|^{2}_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}}{G}

\displaystyle+\frac{(k-1)}{(n-k)}\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}\mathrm{Ric}-\frac{(k-1)}{2(n-k)}\tilde{\Delta}_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{G}\left\langle-\tilde{\omega}_{\epsilon}+(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})\omega,\omega\right\rangle_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}.

(3.11)

Hence

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{\epsilon}\left(\log G-\frac{k-1}{2(n-k)}u_{\epsilon}\right)\geq{}

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma(n-1)(k+1)-(k-1)(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})}{2(n-k)}G

\displaystyle+\frac{\sigma(n-1)(k+1)+(k-1)(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})}{2(n-k)}\frac{|g|^{2}_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}}{G}

\displaystyle+\frac{(k-1)}{(n-k)}\left(\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}\mathrm{Ric}-\tilde{\Delta}_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{G}\left\langle-\tilde{\omega}_{\epsilon}+(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})\omega,\omega\right\rangle_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}.

(3.12)

Next, we observe that

\displaystyle|g|^{2}_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{G^{2}}{n},

\displaystyle-\tilde{\Delta}_{\epsilon}u_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}^{i\bar{j}}\left(\tilde{g}_{\epsilon_{i\bar{j}}}+\mathrm{Ric}_{i\bar{j}}-(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})g_{i\bar{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-n-\mathrm{tr}_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}\mathrm{Ric}+(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})G,\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\frac{1}{G}\left\langle(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})\omega-\tilde{w}_{\epsilon},\omega\right\rangle_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{G}(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})|g|^{2}_{\tilde{g}_{\epsilon}}-\frac{1}{G}G\geq-1.

Plugging these three inequalities/equation above into (
3
), we see that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{\epsilon}\left(\log G-\frac{(k-1)}{2(n-k)}u_{\epsilon}\right)

(3.13)

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{n}{n}\cdot\frac{\sigma(n-1)(k+1)-(k-1)(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})}{2(n-k)}\right)G+\left(\frac{(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})(k-1)2n}{(n-k)2n}\right)G

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\sigma(n-1)(k+1)+(k-1)(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})}{2(n-k)n}\right)G-1-\frac{n(k-1)}{n-k}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(n+1)\sigma(n-1)(k+1)}{2(n-k)n}+\frac{(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})(k-1)(n+1)}{2(n-k)n}\right)G-1-\frac{n(k-1)}{n-k}

\displaystyle\geq{}

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{(n+1)\sigma(n-1)(k+1)}{2(n-k)n},\frac{(\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})(k-1)(n+1)}{2(n-k)n}\right\}\cdot G-1-\frac{n(k-1)}{n-k}.

Now we apply the maximum principle to get a lower estimate of
\tilde{\omega}_{\epsilon}
. At the maximum of
u_{\epsilon}
, say
x_{0}
, since
\sqrt{-1}\partial\bar{\partial}u_{\epsilon}\leq 0
we have
that
((\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})\omega-\operatorname{Ric}(\omega))(x_{0})\geq\tilde{\omega}_{\epsilon}>0
and

\displaystyle e^{\frac{2n-k-1}{2(n-k)}\sup_{N}u_{\epsilon}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{2n-k-1}{2(n-k)}u_{\epsilon}(x_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left.\frac{((\epsilon+\epsilon_{0})\omega-\operatorname{Ric}(\omega))^{n}}{\omega^{n}}\right|_{x=x_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C,

for some
C
independent of
\epsilon
.
This proves a uniform upper bound for
u_{\epsilon}
, and
hence that

\sup_{N}\frac{\tilde{\omega}_{\epsilon}^{n}}{\omega^{n}}\leq C,\mbox{ equivalently }W_{n}\geq C^{-1},\mbox{ with }W_{n}:=\frac{\omega^{n}}{\tilde{\omega}_{\epsilon}^{n}}.

(3.14)

Again we apply the maximum principle to
\log G-\frac{(k-1)}{2(n-k)}u_{\epsilon}
. By (
3.13
), at the point
x_{0}^{\prime}
, where the maximum of
\log G-\frac{(k-1)}{2(n-k)}u_{\epsilon}
is attained,
we have that

G(x_{0}^{\prime})\leq C\text{ for some C independent of }\epsilon.

(3.15)

Since
GW_{n}^{\frac{k-1}{2n-k-1}}=Ge^{-\frac{k-1}{2(n-k)}u_{\epsilon}}
, we infer that
\sup_{N}\left(GW_{n}^{\frac{k-1}{2n-k-1}}\right)
is also attained at

. By GM-AM inequality
G\cdot W_{n}^{\frac{k-1}{2n-k-1}}\leq G\left(\frac{G}{n}\right)^{\frac{k-1}{(2n-k-1)n}}
at

.
This, together with (
3.15
), implies
\sup_{N}\left(GW_{n}^{\frac{k-1}{2n-k-1}}\right)\leq C
for some
C>0
independent of
\epsilon
.
Combining this with (
3.14
) we have that

G\leq C\,\mbox{ hence }\tilde{\omega}_{\epsilon}\geq A\omega,

(3.16)

for a constant
A>0
independent of
\epsilon
. This is a contradiction to that
\epsilon_{0}[\omega]-C_{1}(N)
is not Kähler by taking
\epsilon\to 0
. This completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

\square

A remark is appropriate to compare the above proof with that of

C^{2}
-estimate can be obtained by a direct application of Royden’s version of Yau’s Schwarz lemma (precisely, Theorem 1, p554 of

under the assumption of
[

]
(namely the holomorphic sectional curvature

), in view of an obvious lower bound on

from (
3.5
). By comparison, some nontrivial manipulations are needed above (at the least to the best knowledge of the authors) to get the
C^{2}
-estimate since one can not infer any useful information from (

) directly under
\operatorname{Ric}_{k}<0
for some

.

is established, the ampleness of

]
provided that

. By considering the Monge-Ampère equation (

Proposition 4.1

(Demailly-Skoda)
.

Let
(E,h)
be a holomorphic vector bundle with
\operatorname{rank}(E)=r
over a complex manifold
N^{n}
. Let
(\det(E),\det(h))
be the determinant line bundle. Assume that
(E,h)
is Griffiths positive. Then
E\otimes\det(E)
(equipped with the induced metric) is Nakano positive.

Before the proof recall that Nakano positivity means that for any section nonzero section
\tau=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\tau^{i\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\alpha}}\otimes e_{i}
(abbreviated as
\tau^{i\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\alpha}}\otimes e_{i}
) of
T^{\prime}N\otimes E
,

\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{n}\sum_{i,k=1}^{r}\Theta_{\alpha\bar{\beta}i\bar{k}}\tau^{i\alpha}\overline{\tau^{k\beta}}>0

where
\Theta
denotes the curvature of
E
with

\Theta_{\alpha\bar{\beta}i\bar{k}}=\langle\Theta_{\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\alpha}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}^{\beta}}}(e_{i}),\overline{e_{k}}\rangle
.
Below we assume that
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\alpha}}\}
and
\{e_{i}\}
are normal frames at a point
p\in N
.

Proof.

By direct calculation on the metric of the tensor product, the Nakano positivity of
E\otimes\det(E)
amounts to (Einstein convention applied) showing that for any
\tau\neq 0

\Theta_{\alpha\bar{\beta}k\bar{k}}\tau^{i\alpha}\overline{\tau^{i\beta}}+\Theta_{\alpha\bar{\beta}i\bar{k}}\tau^{i\alpha}\overline{\tau^{k\beta}}>0.

(4.1)

For a section
\tau
as above and
w=(w^{1},\cdots,w^{r})\in\mathbb{S}^{2r-1}\subset E_{p}
(identified as
\mathbb{C}^{r}
), let
W=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\tau^{i\alpha}w^{i}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\alpha}}
and
u=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\bar{w}^{k}e_{k}
be elements of
T^{\prime}_{p}N
and
E_{p}
. The Griffiths positivity implies that
\langle\Theta_{W\overline{W}}(u),\bar{u}\rangle>0
for generic
w\in\mathbb{S}^{2r-1}
. As in
[
11
]
, taking the integration average
\frac{\ \ }{\ \ }{\hskip-11.38092pt}\int
over
\mathbb{S}^{2r-1}
, the Berger’s lemma implies that

\displaystyle r(r+1)\frac{\ \ }{\ \ }{\hskip-11.38092pt}\int\langle\Theta_{W\overline{W}}(u),\bar{u}\rangle\,d\mu(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r(r+1)\frac{\ \ }{\ \ }{\hskip-11.38092pt}\int\Theta_{\alpha\bar{\beta}l\bar{k}}\tau^{i\alpha}w^{i}\overline{\tau^{j\beta}w^{j}}\bar{w}^{k}w^{l}\,d\mu(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq k}\Theta_{\alpha\bar{\beta}k\bar{k}}\tau^{i\alpha}\overline{\tau^{i\beta}}+\sum_{i\neq j}\Theta_{\alpha\bar{\beta}i\bar{j}}\tau^{i\alpha}\overline{\tau^{j\beta}}+2\Theta_{\alpha\bar{\beta}i\bar{i}}\tau^{i\alpha}\overline{\tau^{i\beta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Theta_{\alpha\bar{\beta}k\bar{k}}\tau^{i\alpha}\overline{\tau^{i\beta}}+\Theta_{\alpha\bar{\beta}i\bar{k}}\tau^{i\alpha}\overline{\tau^{k\beta}}.

This proves (
4.1
), hence the proposition.

\square

Proposition 4.2

(M

is ample, and

(2)

\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

d
-uple embedding of

\mathbb{P}^{N}
.

1

\mathbb{P}^{N+1}

\displaystyle K_{E}

Janus solutions from dyonic I ​ S ​ O ​ ( 7 ) ISO(7) maximal gauged supergravity


Abstract
We give a large class of new supersymmetric Janus solutions in dyonic I ​ S ​ O ​ ( 7 ) ISO(7) maximal gauged supergravity encompassing all known solutions appearing recently. We consider S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(3) invariant sector in which the gauged supergravity admits four supersymmetric A ​ d ​ S 4 AdS_{4} vacua with N = 1 , 1 , 2 , 3 N=1,1,2,3 unbroken supersymmetries and G 2 G_{2} , S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) , S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) × U ⁡ ( 1 ) SU(3)\times U(1) and S ​ O ​ ( 4 ) SO(4) symmetries, respectively. We find Janus solutions preserving N = 1 , 2 , 3 N=1,2,3 supersymmetries and interpolating between A ​ d ​ S 4 AdS_{4} vacua and the N = 8 N=8 SYM phase. These solutions can be interpreted as conformal interfaces between SYM and various conformal phases of the dual field theory in three dimensions. The solutions can also be embedded in massive type IIA theory by a consistent truncation on S 6 S^{6} . We also find a number of singular Janus solutions interpolating between N = 8 N=8 SYM or A ​ d ​ S 4 AdS_{4} vacua and singularities or between singular geometries. It turns out that, apart from the SYM phase on D2-branes, all the singularities arising in these solutions are unphysical in both four- and ten-dimensional frameworks.

with the Kahler metric

K_{I\bar{J}}dz^{I}d\bar{z}^{\bar{J}}=-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{2}{(z_{i}-\bar{z}_{i})^{2}}dz_{i}d\bar{z}_{i}-\frac{1}{(z_{4}-\bar{z}_{4})^{2}}dz_{4}d\bar{z}_{4}

(28)

obtained from the Kahler potential of the form

K=-2\sum_{i=1}^{3}\ln[-i(z_{i}-\bar{z}_{i})]-\ln[-i(z_{4}-\bar{z}_{4})].

(29)

The scalar potential is given in (
25
) and can be written in terms of the superpotential as

V=8K^{I\bar{J}}\partial_{z^{I}}\mathcal{W}\partial_{\bar{z}^{\bar{J}}}\mathcal{W}-12\mathcal{W}^{2}\,.

(30)

The superpotential is in turn given by

\mathcal{W}=\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{K}{2}}\sqrt{\mathcal{Z}\overline{\mathcal{Z}}}

(31)

with

\mathcal{Z}=2+8z_{1}z_{2}z_{3}+2z_{4}(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}+z_{3}^{2}).

(32)

In terms of the eight real scalars
(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3},\chi,\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3},\varphi)
, the “real” superpotential
W=|\mathcal{W}|
is given by

\displaystyle W=\frac{1}{8}e^{\frac{\varphi}{2}+\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}+\phi_{3}}\sqrt{W_{r}^{2}+W_{i}^{2}}

(33)

for

\displaystyle W_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8e^{-\phi_{3}}(\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}-e^{-\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}})+2e^{-\varphi}(\zeta_{1}^{2}+\zeta_{2}^{2}+\zeta_{3}^{2}-e^{-2\phi_{1}}-e^{-2\phi_{2}}-e^{-2\phi_{3}})

(34)

\displaystyle+8\zeta_{3}(e^{-\phi_{2}}\zeta_{1}+\zeta_{2}e^{-\phi_{1}})+4\chi(e^{-\phi_{1}}\zeta_{1}+e^{-2\phi_{2}}\zeta_{2}+e^{-\phi_{3}}\zeta_{3}),

\displaystyle W_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2+8e^{-\phi_{3}}(e^{-\phi_{2}}\zeta_{1}+e^{-\phi_{1}}\zeta_{2})+2\chi(e^{-2\phi_{1}}+e^{-2\phi_{2}}+e^{-2\phi_{3}}-\zeta_{1}^{2}-\zeta_{2}^{2}-\zeta_{3}^{2})

(35)

\displaystyle+8\zeta_{3}(e^{-\phi_{1}-\phi_{2}}-\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2})+4e^{-\varphi}(e^{-\phi_{1}}\zeta_{1}+e^{-\phi_{2}}\zeta_{2}+e^{-\phi_{3}}\zeta_{3}).

The scalar potential can be written in terms of
W
as

V=4\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left[e^{-2\phi_{i}}\left(\frac{\partial W}{\partial\zeta_{i}}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\partial W}{\partial\phi_{i}}\right)^{2}\right]+8e^{-2\varphi}\left(\frac{\partial W}{\partial\chi}\right)^{2}+8\left(\frac{\partial W}{\partial\varphi}\right)^{2}-6W^{2}\,.

(36)

We now consider the fermionic supersymmetry transformations and derive the corresponding BPS equations for supersymmetric Janus solutions. For four-dimensional gauged supergravity, the procedure has been initiated in
[
18
]
to which the reader is referred for more detail. We will mostly present the final result with a brief review of relevant formulae. The metric ansatz takes the form of an
AdS_{3}
-sliced domain wall

ds^{2}=e^{2A(r)}\left(e^{\frac{2\rho}{\ell}}dx^{2}_{1,1}+d\rho^{2}\right)+dr^{2}\,.

(37)

This gives the metric of standard domain walls in the limit
\ell\rightarrow\infty
. With the vielbein components

e^{\hat{\alpha}}=e^{A+\frac{\rho}{\ell}}dx^{\alpha},\qquad e^{\hat{\rho}}=e^{A}d\rho,\qquad e^{\hat{r}}=dr,

(38)

it is straightforward to compute the following non-vanishing components of the spin connection

\omega^{\hat{\rho}}_{\phantom{\hat{\rho}}\hat{r}}=A^{\prime}e^{\hat{\rho}},\qquad\omega^{\hat{\alpha}}_{\phantom{\hat{\rho}}\hat{\rho}}=\frac{1}{\ell}e^{-A}e^{\hat{\alpha}},\qquad\omega^{\hat{\alpha}}_{\phantom{\hat{\rho}}\hat{r}}=A^{\prime}e^{\hat{\alpha}}

(39)

where
′
denotes the
r
-derivative. Indices
\alpha,\beta
and
\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta}
take values
0,1
. All the scalars are functions of only
r
. In addition, we will set
\ell=1
in subsequent numerical analysis given in the next sections.

By the same analysis as in
[
18
]
, we find that the supersymmetry transformations
\delta\psi^{I}_{\hat{\alpha}}
give the following equation

A^{\prime 2}=W^{2}-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}e^{-2A}\,.

(40)

The variation
\delta\psi^{I}_{\hat{\rho}}
gives the Killing spinor of the form

\epsilon=e^{\frac{\rho}{2\ell}}\tilde{\epsilon}

(41)

for a
\rho
-independent spinor
\tilde{\epsilon}
.

We also need to impose the projectors of the form

\gamma_{\hat{r}}\epsilon=e^{i\Lambda}\epsilon^{*}

(42)

and

\gamma_{\hat{\rho}}\epsilon=i\kappa e^{i\Lambda}\epsilon^{*}

(43)

with
\kappa^{2}=1
and an
r
-dependent phase
\Lambda
. We note that there can be more than one Killing spinor if the solutions preserve more supersymmetry. In this case, Killing spinors in different representations of the residual symmetry can satisfy the projectors (
42
) and (
43
) with different phases.

It should also be remarked that we use Majorana representation with
\gamma^{\hat{\mu}}
real and
\gamma_{5}
purely imaginary. The two chiralities of a given spinor are then related to each other by a complex conjugate. The constant
\kappa
is related to the chirality of the Killing spinor on the two-dimensional defects dual to the
AdS_{3}
slices. The explicit form of the Killing spinor is determined to be

\epsilon=e^{\frac{A}{2}+\frac{\rho}{2\ell}+i\frac{\Lambda}{2}}\varepsilon^{(0)}

(44)

with possibly an
r
-dependent phase for the spinor
\varepsilon^{(0)}
satisfying

\gamma_{\hat{r}}\varepsilon^{(0)}=\varepsilon^{(0)*}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\gamma_{\hat{\rho}}\varepsilon^{(0)}=i\kappa\varepsilon^{(0)*}\,.

(45)

With all these results, we can also find the explicit form of the phase from
\delta\psi^{I}_{\hat{\alpha}}
equations

e^{i\Lambda}=\frac{\mathcal{W}}{A^{\prime}+\frac{i\kappa}{\ell}e^{-A}}\,.

(46)

Using the projector (
42
), we can determine all the BPS equations for scalars from the variation
\delta\chi^{IJK}
.

In terms of the
N=1
truncation, the resulting BPS equations can be written as

\displaystyle\zeta_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle-2\frac{A^{\prime}}{W}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\phi_{2}}-2\frac{\kappa e^{-A}}{W\ell}e^{-\phi_{2}}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\zeta_{2}},

(50)

\displaystyle\zeta_{3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\frac{A^{\prime}}{W}e^{-2\phi_{3}}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\zeta_{3}}+2e^{-\phi_{3}}\frac{\kappa e^{-A}}{W\ell}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\phi_{3}},

\displaystyle\phi_{3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle-2\frac{A^{\prime}}{W}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\phi_{3}}-2\frac{\kappa e^{-A}}{W\ell}e^{-\phi_{3}}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\zeta_{3}},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\frac{A^{\prime}}{W}e^{-2\varphi}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\chi}+4e^{-\varphi}\frac{\kappa e^{-A}}{W\ell}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\varphi},

(53)

\displaystyle=

The explicit form of these equations is highly complicated, so we refrain from giving them here. It should be noted that these equations reduce to those of the RG flows studied in

in the limit

Finding the BPS equations for supersymmetric Janus solutions involves solving equation (

to work with a smooth numerical analysis. This is achieved by solving the second-order field equations by fixing a turning point of

for particular values of scalar fields. Without loss of generality, we will conveniently choose

,
\phi_{i}^{\prime}(0)
,
\chi^{\prime}(0)
and
\varphi^{\prime}(0)
using the BPS equations giving rise to a full set of initial conditions to solve the second order field equations. We finally check whether the resulting solution satisfies the BPS equations. For completeness, we note all the field equations here

\displaystyle=

(55)

\displaystyle w_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2+4e^{-2\phi_{1}}(2+e^{\phi_{1}-\varphi})\zeta_{1}+8\zeta^{3}_{1}+6\chi(e^{-2\phi_{1}}-\zeta^{2}_{1}),

\displaystyle w_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\chi\zeta_{1}e^{-\phi_{1}}+\zeta^{2}_{1}(6e^{-\varphi}-8e^{-\phi_{1}})-2e^{-3\phi_{1}}(4+3e^{\phi_{1}-\varphi}).

It can also be straightforwardly verified that this truncation leads to a three-fold degenerate eigenvalue of

giving rise to this superpotential. The scalar potential is given by

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}e^{-\varphi}\left[\left[6e^{4\phi_{1}}\zeta_{1}^{2}(\chi-2\zeta_{1})^{2}+e^{6\phi_{1}}(1+4\zeta_{1}^{3}-3\zeta_{1}^{2}\chi)^{2}-3\chi^{2}e^{2\phi_{1}}\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.-8e^{2\varphi}-3e^{2\phi_{1}}+6e^{4\phi_{1}}\zeta_{1}^{2}+9e^{6\phi_{1}}\zeta_{1}^{4}-24e^{\phi_{1}+\varphi}(1+e^{2\phi_{1}}\zeta_{1}^{2})\right]

which agrees precisely with that given in

up to field redefinitions.

,

and one of

\displaystyle\left.+2e^{2\varphi}[5\chi+e^{2\phi_{1}}(1+\zeta_{1}\chi(\zeta_{1}-\chi))]\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=-e^{2(\varphi+\phi_{1})}(2\zeta_{1}-\chi)[8\zeta_{1}+3\chi+e^{2\phi_{1}}(1+4\zeta_{1}^{3}-3\zeta_{1}^{2}\chi)]-8e^{2\varphi}

\displaystyle\qquad\phantom{=}+3e^{2\phi_{1}}(1-e^{2\phi_{1}}\zeta_{1}^{2}+2e^{\phi_{1}+\varphi}(3e^{2\phi_{1}}\zeta_{1}^{2}-1)

(66)

and

\displaystyle A^{\prime}e^{A}\left[4(e^{2\phi_{1}}\zeta_{1}+e^{2\varphi}\chi)+4e^{2(\varphi+\phi_{1})}[1+\zeta_{1}\chi(\chi-3\zeta_{1})]\right.

\displaystyle\left.+2e^{\phi_{1}+\varphi}[2\zeta_{1}+e^{2\phi_{1}}(1+10\zeta_{1}^{3})]\right]

6
Conclusions and discussions

In this paper, we have studied supersymmetric Janus solutions of four-dimensional
N=8
gauged supergravity with dyonic
ISO(7)
gauge group in
SO(3)
invariant sector. The solutions preserve
N=1,2,3
supersymmetries and interpolate among
N=8
SYM phase and four supersymmetric fixed points with
G_{2}
,
SU(3)
,
U(3)
and
SO(4)
symmetries. The results substantially enlarge a small number of known Janus solutions found in
[
22
]
and give rise to many new Janus solutions in four-dimensional gauged supergravities. The solutions can also be embedded in massive type IIA theory via an
S^{6}
consistent truncation leading to new holographic decriptions of conformal interfaces between various phases of
N=8
SYM theory on the world-volume of D2-branes.

It would be interesting to explicitly uplift the solutions to ten dimensions and study various aspects of the conformal interfaces dual to the solutions given in this paper similar to the analysis in
SO(8)
gauged supergravity given in
[
47
]
. Given the concrete dual field theories with
N=2,3
supersymmetries, in terms of Chern-Simons-Matter (CSM) theories of
[
48
,
49
]
, in
[
38
]
and a detailed map between supergravity fields and field theory operators in
[
44
]
, it is of particular interest to study the corresponding conformal interfaces in the dual field theories. Therefore, at least for the
N=2,3
solutions, both the gravity and field theory sides are known to some extent, and matching the results from both sides would lead to more insights to the gauge/gravity duality. We hope the solutions in this paper could be useful along this line.

Finally, finding other types of solutions within the dyonic
ISO(7)
maximal gauged supergravity such as
AdS_{4}
black holes and solutions describing wrapped D2-branes on two-dimensional spaces could also be interesting to investigate. Some of
AdS_{4}
black hole solutions in the
N=2
truncation have been given in
[
50
]
and
[
51
]
. Finding similar solutions in the
N=4
truncation with
SO(3)
symmetry is worth considering. This can be done along the line of the recent result
[
52
]
in which a number of supersymmetric
AdS_{4}
black holes from
N=4
gauged supergravity have been found. We leave these issues for future work.

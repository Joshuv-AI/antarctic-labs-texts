A note on the push-forward formulas for even orthogonal Grassmannians Thanks: A.W. supported by Polish National Science Center grant number 2022/47/B/ST1/01896
Andrzej Weber, Magdalena Zielenkiewicz

Abstract
. We revisit residue formulas for the push-forward in the cohomology of the even orthogonal Grassmannian. This space has two components, and the formula for a single component demands separate attention. We correct errors spread throughout the literature.



Push–forward formulas for Grassmann bundles play a significant role in intersection theory. We take a closer look at the residue formulas applicable to bundles having even orthogonal Grassmannians as their fibers. While the existing literature covers results only for fibers

\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)=\text{\large$\nicefrac{{\operatorname{O}_{2n}}}{{\operatorname{P}}}$}
– a variety with two components, statements regarding
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{+}=\text{\large$\nicefrac{{\operatorname{SO}_{2n}}}{{\operatorname{P}}}$}
remain unproven and are formulated incorrectly. We present a complete solution with a proof in the language of equivariant cohomology. Our formulas in equivariant cohomology are equivalent to the corresponding expressions given in terms of the formal Chern roots of the involved vector bundle.

1.
The main results

Let
V
be a
2n
-dimensional complex vector space equipped with a non-degenerate, symmetric bilinear form
\Omega
.
Let
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)
be the variety, called orthogonal Grassmannian, parametrizing
maximal isotropic subspaces of
V
.
It can be written as the quotient
\nicefrac{{\operatorname{O}(V)}}{{\operatorname{P}}}
, where
\operatorname{P}
is the parabolic subgroup stabilising a fixed maximal isotropic subspace.
The variety
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)

has two isomorphic connected components, which we denote
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{+}
and
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{-}
.
The sign depends on the choice of a reference isotropic subspace, which will be made canonical after choosing a canonical form of
\Omega
.
We study the push-forward along the projection to the one-point space
\pi:\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{\pm}\to\operatorname{pt}
in torus-equivariant cohomology. There are residue formulas for such push-forwards (see for example
[
DP19
,
Zie18b
]
or
[
WZ19
]
for a K–theoretical version), but they consider the two connected components together, i.e. the push-forward along the projection
\text{\large$\nicefrac{{\operatorname{O}(V)}}{{\operatorname{P}}}$}\to\operatorname{pt}
.
While there is an isomorphism
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{+}\simeq\operatorname{OG}(n-1,2n-1)
(see e.g.
[
Tev05
, p 29.]
), it does not easily yield compact push-forward formulas for
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{+}
. The main obstacle is the rank of the maximal torus in
\operatorname{SO}_{2n-1}
which is smaller than the rank of the maximal torus in
\operatorname{SO}(V)\simeq\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
. The action of the additional
\mathbb{C}^{*}
is not visible in the presentation
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{+}\simeq\text{\large$\nicefrac{{\operatorname{SO}_{2n-1}}}{{(\operatorname{P}\cap\operatorname{SO}_{2n-1})}}$}
.

Let
\operatorname{T}\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
be a torus.
For a
\operatorname{T}
-space
X
let
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{T}}^{*}(X)
denote the
\operatorname{T}
-equivariant cohomology ring of
X
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
. For
X=\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{\pm}
and the action of a maximal torus
\operatorname{T}\subset\operatorname{SO}(V)
, the fixed point set of the action is finite. Any class
\alpha\in\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{T}}^{*}(X)
is determined by its restrictions to fixed points. Since
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{T}}^{*}(\operatorname{pt})\simeq\mathbb{Q}[t_{1},\dots,t_{n}]
, each such restriction is a polynomial in the characters
t_{1},\dots,t_{n}
of
\operatorname{T}\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}
. We will assume that
\alpha
comes from
\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{SO}_{2n}}^{*}(\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{\pm})
, then the restriction of
\alpha
at one fixed point determines all other restrictions. They differ by the action of the Weyl group.

We will be interested in the classes of the form
\alpha=\varphi(\mathcal{R}^{\vee})
, where
\mathcal{R}
is the tautological bundle of rank
n
over
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{\pm}
and
\varphi
is a characteristic class, that is, a polynomial in the Chern classes, which is identified with a symmetric polynomial in
n
variables. The Schur classes are of special interest.
We prefer to look at the dual bundle
\mathcal{R}^{\vee}
to get rid of
annoying signs and to have formulas compatible with
[
PR97
]
. Also note that the tangent space
T_{\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)}
is isomorphic to
\wedge^{2}(\mathcal{R}^{\vee})
, which will make our local contributions to the push-forward entirely expressed in terms of the Chern roots of
\mathcal{R}^{\vee}
.
In this note, we show that the push-forward of characteristic classes of
\mathcal{R}^{\vee}
can be expressed as residues of certain meromorphic functions.

Let us choose a basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n},f_{n},\dots,f_{1}\}
of
V
in such a way that

\Omega(e_{i},f_{i})=1
and
\Omega(e_{i},e_{j})=0=\Omega(f_{i},f_{j})=\Omega(e_{i},f_{j})
for
i\neq j
, then the fixed points are the subspaces

p=span\{w_{1},\dots,w_{n}\},\textrm{ where }w_{i}=e_{i}\textrm{ or }w_{i}=f_{i}.

A fixed point is contained in the component
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{+}
if and only if it has an even number of vectors
f_{i}
in the presentation above.
Thus the parabolic group
\operatorname{P}
is block-lower-triangular. The maximal torus
\operatorname{T}
is the standard diagonal torus. Let
t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}
be the characters corresponding to the first
n
coordinates. In other words, the torus consists of the diagonal matrices

x=diag(x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x_{n}^{-1},\dots,x_{1}^{-1})

and
t_{i}(x)=x_{i}
.
We introduce formal variables
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
and given a meromorphic form

g(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})dz_{1}\dots dz_{n}

we denote by
\res_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}g(\mathbf{z})d\mathbf{z}
the iterated residue operation

\res_{z_{n}=\infty}(\res_{z_{n-1}=\infty}(\dots(\res_{z_{1}=\infty}g(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})dz_{1})\dots)dz_{n-1})dz_{n}.

We prove the following:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\alpha=\varphi(\mathcal{R}^{\vee})\in\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{T}}^{*}(\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{+})
and let
\pi^{+}:\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{+}\to\operatorname{pt}
be the constant map. Then

\pi^{+}_{*}\alpha=\frac{2^{n-1}}{n!\prod_{i}t_{i}}\res_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}\varphi(\mathbf{z})\frac{\prod_{i\neq j}(z_{j}-z_{i})\prod_{i<j}(z_{i}+z_{j})\prod_{i}z_{i}\ (\prod_{i}t_{i}+\prod_{i}z_{i})}{\prod_{i,j}(t^{2}_{i}-z^{2}_{j})}d\mathbf{z}.

Let
\alpha=\varphi(\mathcal{R}^{\vee})\in\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{T}}^{*}(\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{-})
and let
\pi^{-}:\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{-}\to\operatorname{pt}
. Then

\pi^{-}_{*}\varphi(\mathcal{R}^{\vee})=\frac{2^{n-1}}{n!\prod_{i}t_{i}}\res_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}\varphi(\mathbf{z})\frac{\prod_{i\neq j}(z_{j}-z_{i})\prod_{i<j}(z_{i}+z_{j})\prod_{i}z_{i}\ (\prod_{i}t_{i}-\prod_{i}z_{i})}{\prod_{i,j}(t_{i}^{2}-z_{j}^{2})}d\mathbf{z}.

We give an alternative form of residue formula, which is slightly less complicated:

Theorem 2
.

With the assumptions as above

\pi^{+}_{*}\alpha=\frac{2^{n-1}}{\prod_{i}t_{i}\;\prod_{i<j}(t_{j}^{2}-t_{i}^{2})}\res_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}\varphi(\mathbf{z})\frac{\prod_{i<j}(z_{j}-z_{i})\prod_{i}z_{i}\ (\prod_{i}t_{i}+\prod_{i}z_{i})}{\prod_{i}(t_{i}^{2}-z_{i}^{2})}d\mathbf{z}.

\pi^{-}_{*}\alpha=\frac{2^{n-1}}{\prod_{i}t_{i}\;\prod_{i<j}(t_{j}^{2}-t_{i}^{2})}\res_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}\varphi(\mathbf{z})\frac{\prod_{i<j}(z_{j}-z_{i})\prod_{i}z_{i}\ (\prod_{i}t_{i}-\prod_{i}z_{i})}{\prod_{i}(t_{i}^{2}-z_{i}^{2})}d\mathbf{z}.

Using the push-forward formulas of Theorem
2
, we compare the push-forwards of the Schur polynomials on the two components. The result is the following.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
be a partition, and
s_{\lambda}
the corresponding Schur polynomial. Denote by
\rho(n)
the partition
\rho(n)=(n,n-1,\dots,1)
.
Then the following holds:

\pi^{+}_{*}s_{\lambda}(\mathcal{R}^{\vee})=2^{n-1}\begin{cases}s_{\mu}(t_{1}^{2},\dots,t_{n}^{2})&\lambda=2\mu+\rho(n-1)\\
t_{1}\dots t_{n}s_{\mu}(t_{1}^{2},\dots,t_{n}^{2})&\lambda=2\mu+\rho(n)\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

\pi^{-}_{*}s_{\lambda}(\mathcal{R}^{\vee})=2^{n-1}\begin{cases}s_{\mu}(t_{1}^{2},\dots,t_{n}^{2})&\lambda=2\mu+\rho(n-1)\\
-t_{1}\dots t_{n}s_{\mu}(t_{1}^{2},\dots,t_{n}^{2})&\lambda=2\mu+\rho(n)\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

In particular, the push-forwards of the Schur class
s_{\lambda}(\mathcal{R})
on
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{+}
and
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{-}
are equal, unless
\lambda
is of the form
2\mu+\rho(n)
for some partition
\mu
, in which case the push-forwards differ by the factor
(-1)
.

The case
\lambda=2\mu+\rho(n)
is overlooked in
[
PR97
, Theorem 5.21]
.
Note that in
[
DP19
, below Theorem 3.1]
it is claimed that the integrals over
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{+}
and
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{-}
are equal. This is a false statement.

Example 1
.

Let
n=2
and

\varphi(z_{1},z_{2})=s_{2,1}(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{1}z_{2}(z_{1}+z_{2})\,.

The orthogonal Grassmannian is of dimension one and

\operatorname{OG}(1,2)^{\pm}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}\,.

The tangent bundle
T_{\operatorname{OG}(1,2)}
is isomorphic to
\wedge^{2}\mathcal{R}^{\vee}\,.

Then, applying the localization formula for the push-forward (see (
3
) below), we obtain

\pi^{+}_{*}(\alpha)=\frac{\varphi(t_{1},t_{2})}{t_{1}+t_{2}}+\frac{\varphi(-t_{1},-t_{2})}{-t_{1}-t_{2}}=\frac{t_{1}t_{2}(t_{1}+t_{2})}{t_{1}+t_{2}}+\frac{t_{1}t_{2}(-t_{1}-t_{2})}{-t_{1}-t_{2}}=2t_{1}t_{2}\,,

while

\pi^{-}_{*}(\alpha)=\frac{\varphi(-t_{1},t_{2})}{-t_{1}+t_{2}}+\frac{\varphi(t_{1},-t_{2})}{t_{1}-t_{2}}=\frac{-t_{1}t_{2}(-t_{1}+t_{2})}{-t_{1}+t_{2}}+\frac{-t_{1}t_{2}(t_{1}-t_{2})}{t_{1}-t_{2}}=-2t_{1}t_{2}\,.

Of course the extreme case
n=1
,
\varphi(z_{1})=s_{1}(z_{1})
is more appealing. We leave the contemplation of that case to the reader.

2.
Cohomological formulas for the push-forward

The proof of Theorem
1
is based on the following push-forward formula of
[
Zie14
, Formula 4]
.
Let
\alpha=\varphi(\mathcal{R}^{\vee})\in\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{T}}^{*}(\operatorname{OG}(n,2n))
and let
\pi:\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)\to\operatorname{pt}
. Then

(1)

\pi_{*}\alpha=\frac{2^{n}}{n!}\res_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}\frac{\varphi(\mathbf{z})\prod_{i\neq j}(z_{j}-z_{i})\prod_{i<j}(z_{i}+z_{j})\prod_{i}z_{i}}{\prod_{i,j}(z_{i}+t_{j})(t_{i}-z_{j})}d\mathbf{z}.

Remark 1
.

The above formula can be rewritten using the formalism of Chern roots:

(2)

\pi_{*}\alpha=\frac{1}{n!}\res_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}\frac{\varphi(\mathbf{z})\;\Delta(\mathbf{z})\;eu(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\mathbf{z}))}{eu(\mathbf{z}\otimes\mathbf{t})\;eu(\mathbf{t}\otimes\mathbf{z}^{\vee}))}d\mathbf{z}\,,

Here the set of variables
\mathbf{z}
is identified with the formal vector space spanned by the characters belonging to
\mathbf{z}
, the Euler class is the product of its elements and

\Delta(\mathbf{z})=\prod_{i\neq j}(z_{j}-z_{i})\,.

The conceptual proof of the analogous formula in K–theory can be found in
[
WZ19
, §5.2]
.

Let us briefly sketch the proof of Formula
1
. By the Atiyah–Bott–Berline–Vergne localization formula (
[
AB84
,
BV82
]
), the push-forward
\pi_{*}\alpha
can be computed by summing up the contributions from all the fixed points,

(3)

\pi_{*}\alpha=\sum_{p\in X^{\operatorname{T}}}\frac{\alpha_{|p}}{eu(p)},

where
eu(p)
is the product of the characters of
\operatorname{T}
which appear in the tangent representation at the fixed point
p
. On the other hand, taking the residue at infinity gives minus the sum of residues at all the poles of the function under the residue. Iterating the procedure gives a sum of residues at
z_{1}=\pm t_{1},\dots,z_{n}=\pm t_{n}
as all other residues vanish, and each such residue corresponds to exactly one fixed point contribution in the localization formula.

There is a more economical (from the computational point of view) expression which can serve as a factor under the residue.
The complicated product

\frac{2^{n}}{n!}\;\frac{\prod_{i\neq j}(z_{j}-z_{i})\prod_{i<j}(z_{i}+z_{j})\prod_{i}z_{i}}{\prod_{i,j}(z_{i}+t_{j})(t_{i}-z_{j})}

can be replaced by

\frac{2^{n}}{\prod_{i<j}(t_{j}^{2}-t_{i}^{2})}\;\frac{\prod_{i<j}(z_{j}-z_{i})\prod_{i}z_{i}}{\prod_{i}(t_{i}^{2}-z_{i}^{2})}\,.

It is enough to check that it has the right residue at

(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n})=(\pm t_{1},\pm t_{2},\dots,\pm t_{n})\,,

namely

\frac{1}{eu(\wedge^{2}(\pm\mathbf{t}))}=\frac{1}{\prod_{i<j}(\pm t_{i}\pm t_{j})}\,.

Example 2
.

Let
n=2
and

K(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{(z_{2}-z_{1})z_{1}z_{2}}{(t_{1}^{2}-z_{1}^{2})(t_{2}^{2}-z_{2}^{2})}\,.

We have

\displaystyle\res_{z_{1}=t_{1}}\res_{z_{2}=t_{2}}K(z_{1},z_{2})d\mathbf{z}=\frac{-t_{1}+t_{2}}{4}\,,

\displaystyle\res_{z_{1}=t_{1}}\res_{z_{2}=-t_{2}}K(z_{1},z_{2})d\mathbf{z}=\frac{-t_{1}-t_{2}}{4}\,,

\displaystyle\res_{z_{1}=-t_{1}}\res_{z_{2}=t_{2}}K(z_{1},z_{2})d\mathbf{z}=\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{4}\,,

etc. Correcting by the constant factor
\frac{4}{t_{2}^{2}-t_{1}^{2}}
we obtain what we need.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\alpha=\varphi(\mathcal{R}^{\vee})\in\operatorname{H}_{\operatorname{T}}^{*}(\operatorname{OG}(n,2n))
. Then

\pi_{*}\alpha=\frac{2^{n}}{\prod_{i<j}(t_{j}^{2}-t_{i}^{2})}\;\res_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}\frac{\varphi(\mathbf{z})\prod_{i<j}(z_{j}-z_{i})\prod_{i}z_{i}}{\prod_{i}(t_{i}^{2}-z_{i}^{2})}d\mathbf{z}.

This formula was already applied in
[
Zie18a
]
to compute the push-forward of Schur classes.

Remark 2
.

Yet another formula is provided by
[
DP19
, Theorem 3.1]
. It applies to any partial flag variety. For the orthogonal Grassmannian it specializes to

\pi_{*}(\varphi(\mathcal{R}^{\vee}))=\Big(\varphi(\mathbf{z})\;\prod_{i=1}^{n}(2z_{i})\,\prod_{i<j}((z_{i}-z_{j})(z_{i}+z_{j}))\,\prod_{i=1}^{n}s_{1/z_{i}}(\mathbb{C}^{2n})\Big)_{[z_{1}^{2n-1}z_{2}^{2n-2}\dots z_{n}^{n}]}.

The expression above is written in formal variables
z_{i}
, while the
\pm t_{i}
serve as the Chern roots of
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
.
Here
f(\mathbf{z})_{[\mathbf{z}^{\lambda}]}
denotes the coefficient of
\mathbf{z}^{\lambda}
in the expansion at infinity. The Segre formal series is equal to

s_{1/z_{i}}(\mathbb{C}^{2n})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1-\tfrac{t_{j}}{z_{i}})(1+\tfrac{t_{j}}{z_{i}})}\,,\qquad\text{expansion at }z_{i}=\infty\,.

In terms of residues the formula of
[
DP19
]
can be written as

\displaystyle\pi_{*}(\varphi(\mathcal{R}^{\vee}))

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\res_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}\varphi(\mathbf{z})\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(2z_{i})\;\prod_{i<j}((z_{i}-z_{j})(z_{i}+z_{j}))}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{2n-i+1}\prod_{i,j}(1-\tfrac{t_{j}}{z_{i}})(1+\tfrac{t_{j}}{z_{i}})}d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\res_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}\varphi(\mathbf{z})\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{i-1}\;\prod_{i=1}^{n}(2z_{i})\;\prod_{i<j}(z_{i}^{2}-z_{j}^{2})}{\prod_{i,j}(z_{j}^{2}-t_{i}^{2})}d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=2^{n}\res_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}\varphi(\mathbf{z})\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{i}\;\prod_{i<j}(z_{i}^{2}-z_{j}^{2})}{\prod_{i,j}(t_{i}^{2}-z_{j}^{2})}d\mathbf{z}\,.

This formula can be obtained from (
1
), altough it has less factors.

3.
Proof of formulas for the components
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{\pm}

In the Atiyah-Bott-Berline-Vergne formula for
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{\pm}
the contributions from the fixed points remain the same for those fixed points which are in the component in question (and are zero for the remaining points).
The fixed points of the torus action on
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)\simeq\text{\large$\nicefrac{{\operatorname{O}(V)}}{{\operatorname{P}}}$}
are the isotropic coordinate subspaces.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

In the localization formula (
3
), the contribution form
p=span\{w_{1},\dots,w_{n}\}
is equal to

\frac{\varphi(\pm t_{1},\dots,\pm t_{n})}{\prod_{i,j}(\pm t_{i}\pm t_{j})},

where the plus signs appear at those indices
i
for which
w_{i}=f_{i}
.
With our convention, after identifying
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)\simeq\text{\large$\nicefrac{{\operatorname{O}_{2n}}}{{\operatorname{P}}}$}
the contribution at
\operatorname{id}\operatorname{P}
is equal to

\frac{\varphi(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})}{\prod_{i,j}(t_{i}+t_{j})}\,.

Our formula for the push-forward for
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{+}
given in Theorem
1
differs from the formula (
1
) by the factor

X^{+}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}):=\frac{\prod_{i}t_{i}+\prod_{i}z_{i}}{2\prod_{i}t_{i}}.

This factor does not introduce new poles in the form under the residue, as the residue is taken with respect to the
z_{i}
’s. The only non-vanishing residues come from
z_{i}=\pm t_{i}
, and they are equal to the fixed-point contributions in the Atiyah-Bott-Berline-Vergne formula, multiplied by the factor
X
evaluated at
z_{i}=\pm t_{i}
. We have

X^{+}(\pm t_{1},\dots,\pm t_{n})=\frac{\prod_{i}\pm t_{i}+\prod_{i}t_{i}}{2\prod_{i}t_{i}}=\frac{((-1)^{k}+1)\prod_{i}t_{i}}{2\prod_{i}t_{i}},

where
k
is the number of indices for which
z_{i}=-t_{i}
. This expression vanishes for
k
odd and is equal to 1 otherwise. Therefore it corrects the push-forward formula (
1
) by multiplying by zero the contributions coming from the fixed points in the component
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{-}
and leaving the remaining contributions intact.

Similarly, for
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{-}
, the factor

X^{-}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}):=\frac{\prod_{i}t_{i}-\prod_{i}z_{i}}{2\prod_{i}t_{i}}

vanishes at fixed points belonging to
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{+}
and is equal to 1 on fixed points in
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{-}
.
∎

The identical argument shows that the push-forward formulas derived from Theorem
4
also work. We obtain Theorem
2
.

4.
Push-forwards of Schur polynomials

We apply Theorem
2
to prove Theorem
3
. A key trick in the computation is to use the relation between the residue at infinity and the residue at zero,
\res_{z=\infty}f(z)dz=\res_{z_{i}=0}\frac{(-1)}{z^{2}}f(z^{-1})dz
, so that

\res_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}f(\mathbf{z})d\mathbf{z}=\res_{\mathbf{z}=(0,\dots,0)}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{z_{1}^{2}\dots z_{n}^{2}}f(\mathbf{z}^{-1})d\mathbf{z},

and the latter residue is simply the coefficient at
z_{1}^{-1}\dots z_{n}^{-1}
in the series expansion for the expression under the residue.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

One of the ways of defining Schur polynomials is the following formula (see e.g.
[
Mac15
, §I.3]
):

s_{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}):=\frac{\det[z_{j}^{\lambda_{i}+n-i}]_{i,j=1,\dots,n}}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(z_{i}-z_{j})},

where

[z_{j}^{\lambda_{i}+n-i}]_{i,j=1,\dots,n}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c c c c}z_{1}^{\lambda_{1}+n-1}&z_{2}^{\lambda_{1}+n-1}&\dots&z_{n}^{\lambda_{1}+n-1}\\
z_{1}^{\lambda_{2}+n-2}&z_{2}^{\lambda_{2}+n-2}&\dots&z_{n}^{\lambda_{2}+n-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
z_{1}^{\lambda_{n}}&z_{2}^{\lambda_{n}}&\dots&z_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}\end{array}\right].

Using the push-forward formula of Corollary
2
we get:

\displaystyle\pi_{*}^{+}s_{\lambda}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{R}^{\vee})=\frac{2^{n-1}}{\prod_{i<j}(t_{j}^{2}-t_{i}^{2})\prod_{i}t_{i}}\res_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{z})\frac{\prod_{i<j}(z_{j}-z_{i})\prod_{i}z_{i}\ (\prod_{i}t_{i}+\prod_{i}z_{i})}{\prod_{i}(t_{i}^{2}-z_{i}^{2})}d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n-1}}{\prod_{i<j}(t_{j}^{2}-t_{i}^{2})}\res_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}\frac{\det[z_{j}^{\lambda_{i}+n-i}]_{i,j}}{\prod_{i<j}(z_{i}-z_{j})}\cdot\frac{\prod_{i<j}(z_{j}-z_{i})\prod_{i}z_{i}\ (\prod_{i}z_{i}+\prod_{i}t_{i})}{\prod_{i}(t_{i}^{2}-z_{i}^{2})\prod_{i}t_{i}}d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n-1}(-1)^{n\choose 2}}{\prod_{i<j}(t_{j}^{2}-t_{i}^{2})}\res_{\mathbf{z}=\infty}\frac{\det[z_{j}^{\lambda_{i}+n-i}]_{i,j}\prod_{i}z_{i}\ (\prod_{i}z_{i}+\prod_{i}t_{i})}{\prod_{i}(t_{i}^{2}-z_{i}^{2})\prod_{i}t_{i}}d\mathbf{z},

where the sign
(-1)^{n\choose 2}
comes from cancelling out the products of
(z_{i}-z_{j})
in the numerator and the denominator. Denoting the constant factor in front of the residue by

C:=\frac{2^{n-1}(-1)^{n\choose 2}}{\prod_{i<j}(t_{j}^{2}-t_{i}^{2})}=\frac{2^{n-1}}{\prod_{i<j}(t_{i}^{2}-t_{j}^{2})}

and changing the residue at infinity to a residue at zero we get:

\displaystyle\pi_{*}^{+}s_{\lambda}(\mathcal{R}^{\vee})

\displaystyle=C\cdot\res_{\mathbf{z}=0}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{z_{1}^{2}\dots z_{n}^{2}}\frac{\det[z_{j}^{-\lambda_{i}-n+i}]_{i,j}\prod_{i}z_{i}^{-1}\ (\prod_{i}z_{i}^{-1}+\prod_{i}t_{i})}{\prod_{i}(t_{i}^{2}-z_{i}^{-2})\prod_{i}t_{i}}d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=C\cdot\res_{\mathbf{z}=0}\frac{\det[z_{j}^{-\lambda_{i}-n+i}]_{i,j}\prod_{i}z_{i}^{-2}\ (1+\prod_{i}t_{i}z_{i})}{\prod_{i}(1-t_{i}^{2}z_{i}^{2})\prod_{i}t_{i}}d\mathbf{z}.

Splitting the expression under the residue as the sum of two parts one gets

\frac{\det[z_{j}^{-\lambda_{i}-n+i}]_{i,j}\prod_{i}z_{i}^{-2}\ (1+\prod_{i}t_{i}z_{i})}{\prod_{i}(1-t_{i}^{2}z_{i}^{2})\prod_{i}t_{i}}

=\frac{\det[z_{j}^{-\lambda_{i}-n+i}]_{i,j}\prod_{i}z_{i}^{-2}}{\prod_{i}(1-t_{i}^{2}z_{i}^{2})\prod_{i}t_{i}}+\frac{\det[z_{j}^{-\lambda_{i}-n+i}]_{i,j}\prod_{i}z_{i}^{-2}\ (\prod_{i}t_{i}z_{i})}{\prod_{i}(1-t_{i}^{2}z_{i}^{2})\prod_{i}t_{i}}.

As taking the residue is an additive operation, we deal with the two summands separately. Expanding the determinant using the permutation formula and expanding
\frac{1}{1-t_{i}^{2}z_{i}^{2}}
into a power series we have

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\frac{\det[z_{j}^{-\lambda_{i}-n+i}]_{i,j}\prod_{i}z_{i}^{-2}}{\prod_{i}(1-t_{i}^{2}z_{i}^{2})\prod_{i}t_{i}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i}t_{i}^{-1}z_{i}^{-2}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}(-1)^{sgn(\sigma)}z_{\sigma(1)}^{-\lambda_{1}-n+1}\dots z_{\sigma_{n}}^{-\lambda_{n}}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{n}=0}^{\infty}(t_{1}z_{1})^{2i_{1}}\dots(t_{n}z_{n})^{2i_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}(-1)^{sgn(\sigma)}\sum_{i_{\sigma(1)},\dots,i_{\sigma(n)}=0}^{\infty}t_{i_{\sigma(1)}}^{2i_{\sigma(1)}-1}\dots t_{i_{\sigma(n)}}^{2i_{\sigma(n)}-1}z_{i_{\sigma(1)}}^{2i_{\sigma(1)}-2-(\lambda_{1}+n-1)}\dots z_{i_{\sigma(n)}}^{2i_{\sigma(n)}-2-\lambda_{n}},

and similarly for the other summand

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\frac{\det[z_{j}^{-\lambda_{i}-n+i}]_{i,j}\prod_{i}z_{i}^{-2}\prod_{i}t_{i}z_{i}}{\prod_{i}(1-t_{i}^{2}z_{i}^{2})\prod_{i}t_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}(-1)^{sgn(\sigma)}\sum_{i_{\sigma(1)},\dots,i_{\sigma(n)}=0}^{\infty}t_{i_{\sigma(1)}}^{2i_{\sigma(1)}}\dots t_{i_{\sigma(n)}}^{2i_{\sigma(n)}}z_{i_{\sigma(1)}}^{2i_{\sigma(1)}-1-(\lambda_{1}+n-1)}\dots z_{i_{\sigma(n)}}^{2i_{\sigma(n)}-1-\lambda_{n}}.

Taking the residue at zero returns the coefficient at
z_{1}^{-1}\dots z_{n}^{-1}
. For the summand
A
it is zero unless
\lambda=\rho(n)+2\mu
, and for
\lambda=\rho(n)+2\mu
one has

\res_{\mathbf{z}=0}A=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}(-1)^{sgn(\sigma)}t_{1}\dots t_{n}t_{\sigma(1)}^{2\mu_{1}+2n-2}\dots t_{\sigma(n)}^{2\mu_{n}}.

For the summand
B
, taking the residue gives zero unless
\lambda=\rho(n-1)+2\mu
, and in the latter case the result is

\res_{\mathbf{z}=0}B=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}(-1)^{sgn(\sigma)}t_{\sigma(1)}^{2\mu_{1}+2n-2}\dots t_{\sigma(n)}^{2\mu_{n}}.

Finally, recognizing the sums over permutations as determinants, one gets

\displaystyle\pi_{*}^{+}s_{\lambda}(\mathcal{R}^{\vee})

\displaystyle=C\cdot\res_{\mathbf{z}=0}(A+B)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n-1}}{\prod_{i<j}(t_{i}^{2}-t_{j}^{2})}\cdot\res_{\mathbf{z}=0}(A+B)

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}(-1)^{n\choose 2}\begin{cases}s_{\mu}(t_{1}^{2},\dots,t_{n}^{2})&\lambda=2\mu+\rho(n-1)\\
t_{1}\dots t_{n}s_{\mu}(t_{1}^{2},\dots,t_{n}^{2})&\lambda=2\mu+\rho(n)\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

For the connected component
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)^{-}
, one immediately has

\displaystyle\pi_{*}^{-}s_{\lambda}(\mathcal{R}^{\vee})

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{\prod_{i<j}(t_{j}^{2}-t_{i}^{2})}\cdot\res_{\mathbf{z}=0}(A-B)

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}\begin{cases}s_{\mu}(t_{1}^{2},\dots,t_{n}^{2})&\lambda=2\mu+\rho(n-1)\\
-t_{1}\dots t_{n}s_{\mu}(t_{1}^{2},\dots,t_{n}^{2})&\lambda=2\mu+\rho(n)\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

∎

The residue formulas for the push-forward in K–theory of flag varieties were given in

. Additional formulas for a range of homogeneous spaces were derived in

Studying the K--theory push-forward formula for the even orthogonal Grassmannian is somewhat more challenging. The primary reason is the absence of symmetry. The Euler class in K--theory

1

For a line bundle
L
the K–theoretic Euler class is equal to
eu^{K}(L)=1-[L^{\vee}]
.
is not symmetric, i.e. in general

eu^{K}(E)\neq(-1)^{{\rm rk}(E)}eu^{K}(E^{\vee})\,.

As in the homological case the push-forwards from the components of
\operatorname{OG}(n,2n)
do not coincide.

Example 3
.

\pi^{+}_{!}(\wedge^{2}\mathcal{R}^{\vee})=\frac{t_{1}t_{2}}{1-t_{1}^{-1}t_{2}^{-1}}+\frac{t_{1}^{-1}t_{2}^{-1}}{1-t_{1}t_{2}}=1+t_{1}t_{2}+t_{1}^{-1}t_{2}^{-1}\,,

while

\pi^{-}_{!}(\wedge^{2}\mathcal{R}^{\vee})=\frac{t_{1}^{-1}t_{2}}{1-t_{1}t_{2}^{-1}}+\frac{t_{1}t_{2}^{-1}}{1-t_{1}^{-1}t_{2}}=1+t_{1}t_{2}^{-1}+t_{2}^{-1}t_{1}\,.

For a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{n})
,
\lambda_{n}\geq 0
, let
S_{\lambda}(\mathcal{R}^{\vee})
be the Schur functor applied to the dual tautological bundle. The result
\pi^{+}_{!}(S_{\lambda}(\mathcal{R}^{\vee}))
can be easily described. Consider the fibration

\nu:\text{\large$\nicefrac{{\operatorname{SO}_{2n}}}{{\operatorname{B}_{-}}}$}\longrightarrow\text{\large$\nicefrac{{\operatorname{SO}_{2n}}}{{\operatorname{P}}}$}\,,

where
\operatorname{B}_{-}
is the group of lower-triangular matrices from
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
. The fiber
\nicefrac{{\operatorname{P}}}{{\operatorname{B}_{-}}}
is isomorphic to the (classical) full flag variety. Moreover,
S_{\lambda}(\mathcal{R}^{\vee})
is equal to the push-forward

\nu^{K}_{!}(L_{\lambda})
, where
L_{\lambda}=\operatorname{P}\times_{\operatorname{B}_{-}}\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}
is the line bundle defined by the character
\lambda
. This is just the Borel-Weil-Bott Theorem applied fiberwise. Thus

\pi^{+}_{!}(S_{\lambda}(\mathcal{R}^{\vee}))=(\pi^{+}\circ\nu)_{!}(L_{\lambda})=\chi(V_{\lambda})

is the character of the highest weight representation
V_{\lambda}
of
\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
.
One can check that if
\lambda_{n}=0
then
\pi^{+}_{!}(S_{\lambda}(\mathcal{R}^{\vee}))=\pi^{-}_{!}(S_{\lambda}(\mathcal{R}^{\vee}))
.
For the remaining weights the representations
\pi^{+}_{!}(S_{\lambda}(\mathcal{R}^{\vee}))
and
\pi^{-}_{!}(S_{\lambda}(\mathcal{R}^{\vee}))
differ by the conjugation induced by an element of
\operatorname{O}_{2n}\setminus\operatorname{SO}_{2n}
.
This is the K–theoretic counterpart of Theorem
3
. The same argument applies to generalized partitions satisfying

\lambda_{n}\geq 0

It seems that there is no easy analogue of the residue formula generalizing Theorems

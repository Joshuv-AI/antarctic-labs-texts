A cohomological study of modified Rota-Baxter algebras
Apurba Das

Abstract
. A modified Rota-Baxter algebra is an algebra equipped with an operator that satisfies the modified Yang-Baxter equation. In this paper, we define the cohomology of a modified Rota-Baxter algebra with coefficients in a suitable bimodule. We relate our cohomology of a modified Rota-Baxter algebra with the known cohomology theory of a Rota-Baxter algebra. As applications of our cohomology, we study formal one-parameter deformations and abelian extensions of modified Rota-Baxter algebras.

16E40, 16D20, 16Wxx, 16S80.

1.
Introduction

Rota-Baxter operators pay very much attention in the last twenty years as they have many applications in mathematics and physics. Rota-Baxter operators originated in a work of Baxter in the fluctuation theory of probability
[
8
]
. Subsequently, many well-known mathematicsians, such as Atkinson, Cartier and Rota studied Rota-Baxter operators from combinatorial points of view
[
3
,
10
,
29
]
. See
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
11
,
18
,
19
]
for the appearence of Rota-Baxter operators in combinatorics, splitting of algebras, infinitesimal bialgebras and renormalizations in quantum field theory. Let
A
be an (associative) algebra over a field
{\bf k}
of characteristic
0
. A linear map
P:A\rightarrow A
is said to be a
Rota-Baxter operator
of weight
\lambda\in{\bf k}
if the map
P
satisfies

\displaystyle P(a)\cdot P(b)=P\big(P(a)\cdot b+a\cdot P(b)\big)+\lambda~\!P(a\cdot b),\text{ for all }a,b\in A.

Here
~\cdot~
denotes the multiplication map in
A
. A pair
(A,P)
consisting of an algebra
A
and a Rota-Baxter operator
P
of weight
\lambda
is said to be a
Rota-Baxter algebra
of weight
\lambda
. Recall that
[
20
]
a bimodule over a Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,P)
of weight
\lambda
consists of a pair
(M,Q)
in which
M
is an
A
-bimodule and
Q:M\rightarrow M
is a linear map satisfying

\displaystyle P(a)\cdot Q(u)=~

\displaystyle Q\big(P(a)\cdot u+a\cdot Q(u)\big)+\lambda~\!Q(a\cdot u),

\displaystyle Q(u)\cdot P(a)=~

\displaystyle Q\big(Q(u)\cdot a+u\cdot P(a)\big)+\lambda~\!Q(u\cdot a),

for all
a\in A
and
u\in M
. Here
~\cdot~
also denotes the left and right
A
-actions on
M
. See also
[
33
]
for details.

Rota-Baxter operators can also be defined on other algebraic structures, such as Lie algebras, Poisson algebras etc. In
[
30
]
Semenov-Tyan-Shanskiĭ observed that under certain conditions, a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
on a Lie algebra is simply the operator form of the classical Yang-Baxter equation (CYBE). In the same paper, the author also introduced a closely related notion of modified classical Yang-Baxter equation (modified CYBE) whose solutions are called modified
r
-matrices. The modified CYBE has found important applications in the study of Lax equations, affine geometry on Lie groups, factorization problems in Lie algebras and compatible Poisson structures
[
4
,
9
,
26
,
31
]
. The modified CYBE can be obtained from the CYBE by a suitable transformation, however they plays independent role in mathematical physics. This led various authors to consider them independently. Motivated by such study, the authors in
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
considered the associative analogue of the modified CYBE, called the modified associative Yang-Baxter equation of weight
\kappa\in{\bf k}
(in short modified AYBE of weight
\kappa
). A solution of the modified AYBE of weight
\kappa
is called a modified Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\kappa
. Thus, modified Rota-Baxter operators are the associative analogue of modified
r
-matrices. Given an associative algebra
A
, a linear map
R:A\rightarrow A
is a
modified Rota-Baxter operator
of weight
\kappa\in{\bf k}
if

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot R(b)=R\big(R(a)\cdot b+a\cdot R(b)\big)+\kappa~\!a\cdot b,~\text{ for all }a,b\in A.

An associative algebra equipped with a modified Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\kappa
is called a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
. It has been observed
[
34
,
35
]
that if
(A,P)
is a Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\lambda
, then
(A,R=\lambda~\!\mathrm{id}+2P)
is a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa=-\lambda^{2}
.

Algebraic structures are better understood by their invariants, such as homology, cohomology and
K
-groups. All these invariants have their own significance. The cohomology theory of an algebraic structure govern formal deformations of the structure and classify its abelian extensions. For example, the classical Hochschild cohomology theory is the cohomology for associative algebras
[
22
]
. To know the better description of the cohomology, one need to understand representations of the given algebraic structure. In this paper, we first consider bimodules over modified Rota-Baxter algebras and construct the corresponding semidirect product. A bimodule over a modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
consists of a pair
(M,S)
in which
M
is an
A
-bimodule and
S:M\rightarrow M
is a linear map satisfying some compatibilities. It follows that any modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
is a bimodule over itself. Given a modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
, we show that the underlying space
A
carries a new associative algebra structure with the product
*_{R}
given by

\displaystyle a*_{R}b=R(a)\cdot b+a\cdot R(b),~\text{ for }a,b\in A.

We denote this associative algebra by
A_{R}
. Further, if
(M,S)
is a bimodule over the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
, then there is an
A_{R}
-bimodule structure on
M
(denoted by
\widetilde{M}
). Thus, we may define the Hochschild cochain complex of the algebra
A_{R}
with coefficients in the
A_{R}
-bimodule
\widetilde{M}
. On the other hand, we can consider the Hochschild cochain complex of the given algebra
A
with coefficients in the
A
-bimodule
M
. As a biproduct of the above two cochain complexes, we construct a new complex defining the cohomology of the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
with coefficients in the bimodule
(M,S)
. We compare our cohomology of modified Rota-Baxter algebras with the cohomology of Rota-Baxter algebras recently introduced in
[
13
,
33
]
. More precisely, we show that the cohomology of a Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,P)
is isomorphic to the cohomology of the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R=\lambda~\!\mathrm{id}+2P)
.

The formal deformation theory of some algebraic structure began with the seminal work of Gerstenhaber for associative algebras
[
16
,
15
]
. Subsequently, the theory has been generalized by Nijenhuis and Richardson for Lie algebras
[
28
]
. See
[
7
,
14
,
6
,
17
]
for more details about deformations of various algebras and morphisms between them. Recently, various authors have considered formal deformations of Rota-Baxter operators, Rota-Baxter algebras and modified
r
-matrices motivated by the deformations of classical
r
-matrices
[
12
,
13
,
23
,
25
,
32
,
33
,
24
]
. In this paper, we consider the formal deformation theory of a modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
. In this theory, we simultaneously deform the algebra
A
and the modified Rota-Baxter operator
R
. We show that the infinitesimal in a formal deformation of
(A,R)
is a
2
-cocycle in the cohomology complex of
(A,R)
with coefficients in itself. Moreover, the infinitesimals corresponding to equivalent deformations are cohomologous, hence they corresponds to the same element in the second cohomology group. We also discuss the rigidity of a modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
and show that the vanishing of the second cohomology group is a sufficient condition for the rigidity of
(A,R)
.

In the next, we define and study abelian extensions of a modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
by a bimodule
(M,S)
over it. We show that the isomorphism classes of such abelian extensions can be classified by the second cohomology of the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
with coefficients in the bimodule
(M,S).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some basic definitions about modified Rota-Baxter algebras, and define bimodules over them. The cohomology of a modified Rota-Baxter algebra with coefficients in a bimodule is defined in Section
3
. We also compare this cohomology with the cohomology of Rota-Baxter algebras. In Section
4
, we study formal deformation theory and rigidity of modified Rota-Baxter algebras. Finally, abelian extensions are considered in Section
5
.

All vector spaces, linear and multilinear maps, tensor products are over a field
{\bf k}
of characteristic
0
.

2.
Modified Rota-Baxter algebras

In this section, we recall modified Rota-Baxter algebras and define bimodules over them. Given a modified Rota-Baxter algebra and a bimodule over it, we also consider the corresponding semidirect product. We show that a modified Rota-Baxter algebra induces a new associative structure on the underlying vector space. We also consider some bimodules over this induced associative algebra.

2.1 Definition
.

(1)

Let
A
be an associative algebra and
\kappa\in{\bf k}
. A linear map
R:A\rightarrow A
is said to be a
modified Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\kappa
if

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot R(b)=R\big(R(a)\cdot b+a\cdot R(b)\big)+\kappa~\!a\cdot b,~\text{ for }a,b\in A.

(2)

A
modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
is a pair
(A,R)
consisting of an associative algebra
A
and a modified Rota-Baxter operator
R:A\rightarrow A
of weight
\kappa
.

2.2 Definition
.

Let
(A,R)
and
(A^{\prime},R^{\prime})
be two modified Rota-Baxter algebras of weight
\kappa
. A
morphism
of modified Rota-Baxter algebras from
(A,R)
to
(A^{\prime},R^{\prime})
is an algebra homomorphism
\varphi:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
that satisfies
R^{\prime}\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ R
.

There is a close relationship between Rota-Baxter algebras and modified Rota-Baxter algebras.

2.3 Lemma
.

Let
A
be an associative algebra. Then a linear map
P:A\rightarrow A
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda\in{\bf k}
if and only if the map
R=\lambda~\!\mathrm{id}+2P
is a modified Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\kappa=-\lambda^{2}.
Equivalently,
(A,P)
is a Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\lambda
if and only if
(A,R=\lambda~\!\mathrm{id}+2P)
is a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa=-\lambda^{2}.

Let
A
be an associative algebra. Consider the direct sum
A\oplus A
with the direct product associative structure

\displaystyle(a,b)\bullet(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(a\cdot a^{\prime},b\cdot b^{\prime}),\text{ for }(a,b),(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in A\oplus A.

Note that the subspace
A_{\triangle}=\{(a,a)|~a\in A\}\subset A\oplus A
is an associative subalgebra. Modified Rota-Baxter operators of weight
-1
are useful to find compliments of
A_{\triangle}
which is also a subalgebra. To see this, let
A_{-\triangle}\subset A\oplus A
be the subspace given by
A_{-\triangle}=\{(a,-a)|~a\in A\}
. For any linear map
R:A\rightarrow A
, we define a map
\widehat{R}:A_{-\triangle}\rightarrow A_{\triangle}
by
\widehat{R}(a,-a)=(-R(a),-R(a))
, for
(a,-a)\in A_{-\triangle}
. Consider the graph

\displaystyle\mathrm{Gr}(\widehat{R})=~

\displaystyle\{\widehat{R}(a,-a)+(a,-a)|~a\in A\}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\{(-R(a)+a,-R(a)-a)|~a\in A\}\subset A\oplus A

of the map
\widehat{R}
. Note that
\mathrm{Gr}(\widehat{R})
is a compliment (as a subspace) of
A_{\triangle}
in the space
A\oplus A
. However,
\mathrm{Gr}(\widehat{R})
is in general not a subalgebra. For any
a,b\in A
, we observe that

\displaystyle\big(-R(a)+a,-R(a)-a\big)\bullet\big(-R(b)+b,-R(b)-b\big)

\displaystyle=\big(R(a)\cdot R(b)-R(a)\cdot b-a\cdot R(b)+a\cdot b,~R(a)\cdot R(b)+R(a)\cdot b+a\cdot R(b)+a\cdot b\big)

This is in
\mathrm{Gr}(\widehat{R})
if and only if

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot R(b)+a\cdot b=R\big(R(a)\cdot b+a\cdot R(b)\big).

In other words,
\mathrm{Gr}(\widehat{R})
is a subalgebra of
A\oplus A
if and only if
R
is a modified Rota-Baxter operator of weight
-1
.

Next we define bimodules over a modified Rota-Baxter algebra.

2.4 Definition
.

Let
(A,R)
be a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
. A
bimodule
over
(A,R)
consists of a pair
(M,S)
in which
M
is an
A
-bimodule and
S:M\rightarrow M
is a linear map satisfying for
a\in A,

u\in M
,

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot S(u)=S\big(R(a)\cdot u+a\cdot S(u)\big)+\kappa~\!a\cdot u,

\displaystyle S(u)\cdot R(a)=S\big(S(u)\cdot a+u\cdot R(a)\big)+\kappa~\!u\cdot a.

If
(A,R)
is a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
, then the pair
(A,R)
itself is a bimodule over it, where the
A
-bimodule structure on
A
is given by the algebra multiplication. This is called the adjoint bimodule.

The following result finds the relation between bimodules over Rota-Baxter algebras and over modified Rota-Baxter algebras.

2.5 Proposition
.

Let
(A,P)
be a Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\lambda
, and
(M,Q)
be a bimodule over it. Then
(M,S=\lambda~\!\mathrm{id}_{M}+2Q)
is a bimodule over the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R=\lambda~\!\mathrm{id}_{A}+2P)
of weight
\kappa=-\lambda^{2}
.

Proof.

For any
a\in A
and
u\in M
, we have

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot S(u)=~

\displaystyle\big(\lambda a+2P(a)\big)\cdot\big(\lambda u+2Q(u)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}~\!a\cdot u+2\lambda\big(P(a)\cdot u+a\cdot Q(u)\big)+4~\!P(a)\cdot Q(u)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}~\!a\cdot u+2\lambda\big(P(a)\cdot u+a\cdot Q(u)\big)+4~\!Q\big(P(a)\cdot u+a\cdot Q(u)+\lambda~\!a\cdot u\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\lambda~\!\mathrm{id}_{M}+2Q)\big((\lambda~\!\mathrm{id}_{A}+2P)(a)\cdot u~+~a\cdot(\lambda\mathrm{id}_{M}+2Q)(u)\big)~-~\lambda^{2}~\!a\cdot u

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle S\big(R(a)\cdot u+a\cdot S(u)\big)+\kappa~\!a\cdot u.

Similarly, we can show that

\displaystyle S(u)\cdot R(a)=S\big(S(u)\cdot a+u\cdot R(a)\big)+\kappa~\!u\cdot a.

This completes the proof.
∎

2.6 Proposition
.

Let
(A,R)
be a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
, and
\{(M_{i},S_{i})\}_{i\in I}
be a family of bimodules over it. Then
(\oplus_{i\in I}M_{i},\oplus_{i\in I}S_{i})
is also a bimodule over
(A,R)
.

Proof.

The
A
-bimodule structure on
\oplus_{i\in I}M_{i}
is given by the componentwise
A
-bimodule structure on each
M_{i}
. For any
a\in A
and
(u_{i})_{i\in I}\in\oplus_{i\in I}M_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot(\oplus_{i\in I}S_{i})(u_{i})_{i\in I}=~

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot(S_{i}(u_{i}))_{i\in I}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(R(a)\cdot S_{i}(u_{i})\big)_{i\in I}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(S_{i}(R(a)\cdot u_{i}+a\cdot S_{i}(u_{i}))+\kappa~\!a\cdot u_{i}\big)_{i\in I}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\oplus_{i\in I}S_{i})\bigg(R(a)\cdot(u_{i})_{i\in I}+a\cdot\big((\oplus_{i\in I}S_{i})(u_{i})_{i\in I}\big)\bigg)+\kappa~\!a\cdot(u_{i})_{i\in I}.

This shows that
(\oplus_{i\in I}M_{i},\oplus_{i\in I}S_{i})
is a bimodule over the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
of weight
\kappa
.
∎

Recall that, if
M
is an
A
-bimodule, then
\mathrm{End}(M)
can be equipped with an
A
-bimodule structure with left and right
A
-actions

\displaystyle(a\odot f)(u)=f(u\cdot a)~~~\text{ and }~~~(f\odot a)(u)=f(a\cdot u),\text{ for }a\in A,f\in\mathrm{End}(M),u\in M.

2.7 Proposition
.

Let
(A,R)
be a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
, and
(M,S)
be a bimodule over it. Then
(\mathrm{End}(M),\widetilde{S})
is also a bimodule over
(A,R)
, where
\widetilde{S}:\mathrm{End}(M)\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(M)
is given by
\widetilde{S}(f)=-f\circ S
, for
f\in\mathrm{End}(M)
.

Proof.

For any
a\in A
,
f\in\mathrm{End}(M)
and
u\in M
, we have

\displaystyle\big(R(a)\odot\widetilde{S}(f)\big)(u)=\widetilde{S}(f)\big(u\cdot R(a)\big)=~

\displaystyle-f\big(S(u\cdot R(a))\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle-f\big(S(u)\cdot R(a)-S(S(u)\cdot a)-\kappa~\!u\cdot a\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle-f\big(S(u)\cdot R(a)\big)-\widetilde{S}(f)(S(u)\cdot a)+\kappa~\!f(u\cdot a)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle-\big(R(a)\odot f+a\odot\widetilde{S}(f)\big)(S(u))+\kappa~\!f(u\cdot a)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(\widetilde{S}\big(R(a)\odot f+a\odot\widetilde{S}(f)\big)+\kappa~\!a\odot f\big)(u)

and

\displaystyle\big(\widetilde{S}(f)\odot R(a)\big)(u)=\widetilde{S}(f)\big(R(a)\cdot u\big)=~

\displaystyle-f\big(S(R(a)\cdot u)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle-f\big(R(a)\cdot S(u)-S(a\cdot S(u))-\kappa~\!a\cdot u\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle-f\big(R(a)\cdot S(u)\big)-\widetilde{S}(f)(a\cdot S(u))+\kappa~\!f(a\cdot u)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle-\big(f\odot R(a)+\widetilde{S}(f)\odot a\big)(S(u))+\kappa~\!f(a\cdot u)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(\widetilde{S}\big(\widetilde{S}(f)\odot a+f\odot R(a)\big)+\kappa~\!f\odot a\big)(u).

This shows that
(\mathrm{End}(M),\widetilde{S})
is a bimodule over the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
of weight
\kappa
.
∎

2.8 Proposition
.

(Semidirect product) Let
(A,R)
be a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
, and
(M,S)
be a bimodule over it. Then the pair
(A\oplus M,R\oplus S)
is a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
, where the associative algebra structure on
A\oplus M
is given by the semidirect product

\displaystyle(a,u)\cdot_{\ltimes}(b,v)=(a\cdot b,a\cdot v+u\cdot b),\text{ for }(a,u),(b,v)\in A\oplus M.

Proof.

For
(a,u),(b,v)\in A\oplus M
, we have

\displaystyle(R\oplus S)(a,u)\cdot_{\ltimes}(R\oplus S)(b,v)

\displaystyle=(R(a),S(u))\cdot_{\ltimes}(R(b),S(v))

\displaystyle=\big(R(a)\cdot R(b),~R(a)\cdot S(v)+S(u)\cdot R(b)\big)

\displaystyle=\bigg(R(R(a)\cdot b+a\cdot R(b))+\kappa~\!a\cdot b,~S(R(a)\cdot v+a\cdot S(v))+\kappa~\!a\cdot v+S(S(u)\cdot b+u\cdot R(b))+\kappa~\!u\cdot b\bigg)

\displaystyle=(R\oplus S)\bigg(\big(R(a)\cdot b,~R(a)\cdot v+S(u)\cdot b\big)+\big(a\cdot R(b),~a\cdot S(v)+u\cdot R(b)\big)\bigg)+\kappa~\!\big(a\cdot b,~a\cdot v+u\cdot b\big)

\displaystyle=(R\oplus S)\bigg((R\oplus S)(a,u)\cdot_{\ltimes}(b,v)~+~(a,u)\cdot_{\ltimes}(R\oplus S)(b,v)\bigg)+\kappa~\!(a,u)\cdot_{\ltimes}(b,v).

This shows that
R\oplus S
is a modified Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\kappa
on the semidirect product associative algebra
A\oplus M
. In other words,
(A\oplus M,R\oplus S)
is a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
.
∎

In the following, we show that a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
induces a new associative algebra structure. This associative algebra will be useful in the construction of the cohomology of the modified Rota-Baxter algebra.

2.9 Proposition
.

Let
(A,R)
be a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
. Then we have the followings.

(i) The underlying vector space
A
inherits a new associative algebra structure with the product

\displaystyle a\ast_{R}b=R(a)\cdot b+a\cdot R(b),~\text{ for }a,b\in A.

(We denote this associative algebra simply by
A_{R}
).

(ii) The pair
(A_{R},R)
is a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
.

Proof.

For any
a,b,c\in A
, we have

\displaystyle(a\ast_{R}b)\ast_{R}c=~

\displaystyle R(a\ast_{R}b)\cdot c+(a\ast_{R}b)\cdot R(c)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(R(a)\cdot R(b)-\kappa~\!a\cdot b\big)\cdot c+\big(R(a)\cdot b+a\cdot R(b)\big)\cdot R(c)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot\big(R(b)\cdot c+b\cdot R(c)\big)+a\cdot\big(R(b)\cdot R(c)-\kappa~\!b\cdot c\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot(b\ast_{R}c)+a\cdot R(b\ast_{R}c)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle a\ast_{R}(b\ast_{R}c).

This shows that
A_{R}
is an associative algebra.

(ii) For any
a,b\in A
,

\displaystyle R(a)\ast_{R}R(b)=~

\displaystyle R^{2}(a)\cdot R(b)+R(a)\cdot R^{2}(b)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle R(R(a)\ast_{R}b)+\kappa~\!R(a)\cdot b+R(a\ast_{R}R(b))+\kappa~\!a\cdot R(b)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle R\big(R(a)\ast_{R}b+a\ast_{R}R(b)\big)+\kappa~\!a\ast_{R}b.

This shows that
R
is a modified Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\kappa
on the associative algebra
A_{R}
. In other words,
(A_{R},R)
is a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
.
∎

2.10 Proposition
.

Let
(A,R)
be a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
, and
(M,S)
be a bimodule over it.

(i) Then there is an
A_{R}
-bimodule structure on
M
with left and right actions (denoted by the same notation) given by

\displaystyle a~{\cdot}_{S}~u:=R(a)\cdot u+a\cdot S(u)\quad\text{ and }\quad u~{\cdot}_{S}~a:=S(u)\cdot a+u\cdot R(a),

for
a\in A_{R}
,
u\in M
. We denote this
A_{R}
-bimodule structure simply by
{M}_{S}
.

(ii) The pair
({M}_{S},S)
is bimodule over the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A_{R},R)
of weight
\kappa
.

Proof.

(i) Consider the (semidirect product) modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A\oplus M,R\oplus S)
of weight
\kappa
given in Proposition
2.8
. This induces an associative algebra structure on the direct sum
A\oplus B
(which we denote by
(A\oplus B)_{R\oplus S}
) with the product given by

\displaystyle(a,u)\ltimes_{R\oplus S}(b,v)=~

\displaystyle(R(a),S(u))\ltimes_{R\oplus S}(b,v)~+~(a,u)\ltimes_{R\oplus S}(R(b),S(v))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(R(a)\cdot b+a\cdot R(b),~R(a)\cdot v+S(u)\cdot b+a\cdot S(v)+u\cdot R(b)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(a\ast_{R}b,~a~{\cdot}_{S}~v+u~{\cdot}_{S}~b\big).

This expression of the product says that
{M}_{S}
is an
A_{R}
-bimodule.

(ii) For any
a\in A
and
u\in M
, we have

\displaystyle R(a)~{\cdot}_{S}~S(u)=~

\displaystyle R^{2}(a)\cdot S(u)+R(a)\cdot S^{2}(u)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle S\big(R^{2}(a)\cdot u+R(a)\cdot S(u)\big)+\kappa~\!R(a)\cdot u+S\big(R(a)\cdot S(u)+a\cdot S^{2}(u)\big)+\kappa~\!a\cdot S(u)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle S\big(R(a)~{\cdot}_{S}~u\big)+\kappa~\!R(a)\cdot u+S\big(a~{\cdot}_{S}~S(u)\big)+\kappa~\!a\cdot S(u)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle S\big(R(a)~{\cdot}_{S}~u+a~{\cdot}_{S}~S(u)\big)+\kappa~\!a~{\cdot}_{S}~u

and

\displaystyle S(u)~{\cdot}_{S}~R(a)=~

\displaystyle S^{2}(u)\cdot R(a)+S(u)\cdot R^{2}(a)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle S\big(S^{2}(u)\cdot a+S(u)\cdot R(a)\big)+\kappa~\!S(u)\cdot a+S\big(S(u)\cdot R(a)+u\cdot R^{2}(a)\big)+\kappa~\!u\cdot R(a)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle S\big(S(u)~{\cdot}_{S}~a\big)+\kappa~\!S(u)\cdot a+S\big(u~{\cdot}_{S}~R(a)\big)+\kappa~\!u\cdot R(a)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle S\big(S(u)~{\cdot}_{S}~a+u~{\cdot}_{S}~R(a)\big)+\kappa~\!u~{\cdot}_{S}~a.

This shows that
({M}_{S},S)
is a bimodule over the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A_{R},R)
.
∎

2.11 Remark
.

When
(M,S)=(A,R)
is the adjoint bimodule over the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
of weight
\kappa
, then the
A_{R}
-bimodule
{M}_{S}
coincides with the adjoint
A_{R}
-bimodule. Hence we have
({M}_{S},S)=(A_{R},R)
the adjoint bimodule over the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A_{R},R)
of weight
\kappa
.

Given a bimodule over a modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
, in Proposition
2.10
, we constructed a bimodule over the induced modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A_{R},R)
. In the following, we will construct another bimodule over
(A_{R},R)
. This new construction will be helpful to construct the cohomology of the modified Rota-Baxter algebra.

2.12 Proposition
.

Let
(A,R)
be a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
, and
(M,S)
be a bimodule over it. Then
M
can be equipped with an
A_{R}
-bimodule structure with left and right
A_{R}
-actions

\displaystyle a~\widetilde{\cdot}~u:=R(a)\cdot u-S(a\cdot u)\quad\text{ and }\quad u~\widetilde{\cdot}~a:=u\cdot R(a)-S(u\cdot a),

for
a\in A_{R}
,
u\in M
. (We denote this
A_{R}
-bimodule structure simply by
\widetilde{M}
). Moreover, the pair
(\widetilde{M},S)
is a bimodule over the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A_{R},R)
of weight
\kappa
.

Proof.

For
a,b\in A_{R}
and
u\in M
, we have

\displaystyle(a\ast_{R}b)~\widetilde{\cdot}~u=~

\displaystyle R(a\ast_{R}b)\cdot u-S\big((a\ast_{R}b)\cdot u\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(R(a)\cdot R(b)-\kappa~\!a\cdot b\big)\cdot u-S\big((R(a)\cdot b+a\cdot R(b))\cdot u\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot R(b)\cdot u-\kappa~\!a\cdot b\cdot u-S\big(R(a)\cdot b\cdot u+a\cdot S(b\cdot u)\big)-S\big(a\cdot R(b)\cdot u-a\cdot S(b\cdot u)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot(R(b)\cdot u)-R(a)\cdot S(b\cdot u)-S\big(a\cdot R(b)\cdot u-a\cdot S(b\cdot u)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot\big(R(b)\cdot u-S(b\cdot u)\big)-S\big(a\cdot(R(b)\cdot u-S(b\cdot u))\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle a~\widetilde{\cdot}~\big(R(b)\cdot u-S(b\cdot u)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle a~\widetilde{\cdot}~(b~\widetilde{\cdot}~u).

Similarly,

\displaystyle(a~\widetilde{\cdot}~u)~\widetilde{\cdot}~b=~

\displaystyle(a~\widetilde{\cdot}~u)\cdot R(b)-S\big((a~\widetilde{\cdot}~u)\cdot b\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(R(a)\cdot u-S(a\cdot u)\big)\cdot R(b)-S\big((R(a)\cdot u-S(a\cdot u))\cdot b\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot u\cdot R(b)-S\big(S(a\cdot u)\cdot b+a\cdot u\cdot R(b)\big)-\kappa~\!a\cdot u\cdot b-S\big(R(a)\cdot u\cdot b\big)+S\big(S(a\cdot u)\cdot b\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot u\cdot R(b)-S\big(R(a)\cdot u\cdot b+a\cdot S(u\cdot b)\big)-\kappa~\!a\cdot u\cdot b-S\big(a\cdot u\cdot R(b)\big)+S\big(a\cdot S(u\cdot b)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot\big(u\cdot R(b)-S(u\cdot b)\big)-S\big(a\cdot(u\cdot R(b)-S(u\cdot b))\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle R(a)\cdot(u~\widetilde{\cdot}~b)-S\big(a\cdot(u~\widetilde{\cdot}~b)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle a~\widetilde{\cdot}~(u~\widetilde{\cdot}~b)

and

\displaystyle(u~\widetilde{\cdot}~a)~\widetilde{\cdot}~b=~

\displaystyle(u~\widetilde{\cdot}~a)\cdot R(b)-S\big((u~\widetilde{\cdot}~a)\cdot b\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(u\cdot R(a)-S(u\cdot a)\big)\cdot R(b)-S\big((u\cdot R(a)-S(u\cdot a))\cdot b\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle u\cdot R(a)\cdot R(b)-S(u\cdot a)\cdot R(b)-S\big(u\cdot R(a)\cdot b\big)+S\big(S(u\cdot a)\cdot b\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle u\cdot R(a)\cdot R(b)-S\big(S(u\cdot a)\cdot b+u\cdot a\cdot R(b)\big)-\kappa~\!u\cdot a\cdot b-S\big(u\cdot R(a)\cdot b\big)+S\big(S(u\cdot a)\cdot b\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle u\cdot\big(R(a)\cdot R(b)-\kappa~\!a\cdot b\big)-S\big(u\cdot R(a)\cdot b+u\cdot a\cdot R(b)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle u\cdot R(a\ast_{R}b)-S\big(u\cdot(a\ast_{R}b)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle u~\widetilde{\cdot}~(a\ast_{R}b).

This shows that
\widetilde{M}
is an
A_{R}
-bimodule. For the second part, we observe that

\displaystyle R(a)~\widetilde{\cdot}~S(u)=~

\displaystyle R^{2}(a)\cdot S(u)-S\big(R(a)\cdot S(u)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle S\big(R^{2}(a)\cdot u+R(a)\cdot S(u)\big)+\kappa~\!R(a)\cdot u-S^{2}\big(R(a)\cdot u+a\cdot S(u)\big)-\kappa~\!S(a\cdot u)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle S\big(R^{2}(a)\cdot u-S(R(a)\cdot u)+R(a)\cdot S(u)-S(a\cdot S(u))\big)+\kappa~\!\big(R(a)\cdot u-S(a\cdot u)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle S\big(R(a)~\widetilde{\cdot}~u+a~\widetilde{\cdot}~S(u)\big)+\kappa~\!a~\widetilde{\cdot}~u

and

\displaystyle S(u)~\widetilde{\cdot}~R(a)=~

\displaystyle S(u)\cdot R^{2}(a)-S\big(S(u)\cdot R(a)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle S\big(S(u)\cdot R(a)+u\cdot R^{2}(a)\big)+\kappa~\!u\cdot R(a)-S^{2}\big(S(u)\cdot a+u\cdot R(a)\big)-\kappa~\!S(u\cdot a)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle S\big(S(u)\cdot R(a)-S(S(u)\cdot a)+u\cdot R^{2}(a)-S(u\cdot R(a))\big)+\kappa~\!\big(u\cdot R(a)-S(u\cdot a)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle S\big(S(u)~\widetilde{\cdot}~a+u~\widetilde{\cdot}~R(a)\big)+\kappa~\!u~\widetilde{\cdot}~a,

which shows that
(\widetilde{M},S)
is a bimodule over the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A_{R},R)
of weight
\kappa
.
∎

3.
Cohomology of modified Rota-Baxter algebras

In this section, we introduce the cohomology of a modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
with coefficients in a bimodule
(M,S)
. This cohomology is obtained as a biproduct of the Hochschild cohomology of the underlying associative algebra
A
and the Hochschild cohomology of the induced associative algebra
A_{R}
. We also compare our cohomology of a modified Rota-Baxter algebra with the cohomology of a Rota-Baxter algebra defined in
[
13
,
33
]
.

Let
(A,R)
be a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
, and
(M,S)
be a bimodule over it. First, consider the Hochschild cochain complex of
A
with coefficients in the
A
-bimodule
M
. The complex is given by
\{C^{\bullet}(A,M),\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}\}
, where
C^{n}(A,M)=\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},M)
for
n\geq 0
, and the coboundary map
\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}:C^{n}(A,M)\rightarrow C^{n+1}(A,M)
is given by

\displaystyle(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}f)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})=~

\displaystyle(-1)^{n+1}~a_{1}\cdot f(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+1})+f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\cdot a_{n+1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+n+1}~f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i}\cdot a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{n+1}),

for
f\in C^{n}(A,M)
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}\in A
. We denote the corresponding cohomology groups by
H^{\bullet}(A,M)
. Second, we consider the Hochschild cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}(A_{R},\widetilde{M}),\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}\}
of the induced associative algebra
A_{R}
with coefficients in the
A_{R}
-bimodule
\widetilde{M}
, where
C^{n}(A_{R},\widetilde{M})=\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},M)
for
n\geq 0
, and the coboundary map
\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}:C^{n}(A_{R},\widetilde{M})\rightarrow C^{n+1}(A_{R},\widetilde{M})
is given by

\displaystyle(\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}f)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1})=~

\displaystyle(-1)^{n+1}R(a_{1})\cdot f(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+1})-(-1)^{n+1}~S\big(a_{1}\cdot f(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+1})\big)

\displaystyle+f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\cdot R(a_{n+1})-S\big(f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\cdot a_{n+1}\big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i+n+1}~f\big(a_{1},\ldots,R(a_{i})\cdot a_{i+1}+a_{i}\cdot R(a_{i+1}),\ldots,a_{n+1}\big),

for
f\in C^{n}(A_{R},\widetilde{M})
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n+1}\in A
. The cohomology groups of this cochain complex are denoted by
H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(A_{R},\widetilde{M})
.

3.1 Remark
.

Let
(M,S)=(A,R)
be the adjoint bimodule over the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
of weight
\kappa
. Then the cochain complex constructed above is given by
\{C^{\bullet}(A_{R},\widetilde{A}),\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}\}
, which is the Hochschild cochain complex of the induced algebra
A_{R}
with coefficients in the
A_{R}
-bimodule
\widetilde{A}
. It can be easily check that the corresponding cohomology governs the formal deformation theory of the modified Rota-Baxter operator
R
keeping the underlying associative structure on
A
intact. In the following, we will be interested in a more general cohomology that simultaneously controls the formal deformations of the underlying algebra and the modified Rota-Baxter operator.

Let
(A,R)
be a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
, and
(M,S)
be a bimodule over it. Our construction of the general cohomology is based on the following result. This is highly motivated by the similar result in the context of Rota-Baxter algebras
[
33
, Proposition 5.2]
. The proof can be done by considering the correspondence between Rota-Baxter algebras and modified Rota-Baxter algebras, and using the above mentioned reference.

3.2 Proposition
.

Let
(A,R)
be a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
, and
(M,S)
be a bimodule. Then the collection of maps
\{\Psi^{n}:C^{n}(A,M)\rightarrow C^{n}(A_{R},\widetilde{M})\}_{n\geq 0}
given by

\displaystyle\Psi^{0}=~

\displaystyle\mathrm{id}_{M},~\text{ and }

\displaystyle\Psi^{n}(f)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=~

\displaystyle f\big(R(a_{1}),\ldots,R(a_{n})\big)

\displaystyle-\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq n,,r\text{ odd }}(-\kappa)^{\frac{r-1}{2}}S\circ f\big(R(a_{1}),\ldots,a_{i_{1}},\ldots,a_{i_{2}},\ldots,a_{i_{r}},\ldots,R(a_{n})\big)

\displaystyle-\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq n,,r\text{ even }}(-\kappa)^{\frac{r}{2}+1}S\circ f\big(R(a_{1}),\ldots,a_{i_{1}},\ldots,a_{i_{2}},\ldots,a_{i_{r}},\ldots,R(a_{n})\big)

defines a morphism of cochain complexes from
\{C^{\bullet}(A,M),\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}\}
to
\{C^{\bullet}(A_{R},\widetilde{M}),\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}\}
.

The above proposition suggests to consider a new cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S)),\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}}\}
, where

\displaystyle C^{n}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))=\begin{cases}M&\text{ if }n=0,\\
C^{n}(A,M)\oplus C^{n-1}(A_{R},\widetilde{M})=\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},M)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n-1},{M})&\text{ if }n\geq 1.\end{cases}

The coboundary operator
\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}}:C^{n}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))\rightarrow C^{n+1}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))
is given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}}(u)=~

\displaystyle(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(u),-u),~\text{ for }u\in M,

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}}(\chi,\Phi)=~

\displaystyle\big(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\chi),~-\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\Phi)-\Psi^{n}(\chi)\big),~\text{ for }(\chi,\Phi)\in C^{n}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S)).

Note that
(\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}})^{2}=0
as we have

\displaystyle(\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}})^{2}(u)=~

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}}(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(u),-u)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big((\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}})^{2}(u),~\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(u)-\Psi^{1}\circ\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(u)\big)=0

and

\displaystyle(\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}})^{2}(\chi,\Phi)=~

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}}\big(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\chi),~-\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\Phi)-\Psi^{n}(\chi)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big((\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}})^{2}(\chi),~(\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}})^{2}(\Phi)+\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}\circ\Psi^{n}(\chi)-\Psi^{n+1}\circ\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\chi)\big)=0.

It is clear from the above description that a pair
(\chi,\Phi)\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes 2},M)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(A,M)
is a
2
-cocycle if they satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{cases}a\cdot\chi(b,c)-\chi(a\cdot b,c)+\chi(a,b\cdot c)-\chi(a,b)\cdot c=0,\\
\\
R(a)\cdot\Phi(b)-S(a\cdot\Phi(b))-\Phi\big(R(a)\cdot b+a\cdot R(b)\big)+\Phi(a)\cdot R(b)-S(\Phi(a)\cdot b)\\
\quad+\chi(R(a),R(b))-\kappa~\!\chi(a,b)-R\big(\chi(R(a),b)+\chi(a,R(b))\big)=0.\end{cases}

Further, it is a
2
-coboundary if there exists a pair
(\varphi_{1},u)\in\mathrm{Hom}(A,M)\oplus M
such that

\displaystyle\chi=\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\varphi_{1})~~~\text{ and }~~~\Phi=-\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(u)-\Psi^{1}(\varphi_{1}).

Let
Z^{n}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))
denote the space of
n
-cocycles, and
B^{n}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))
denote the space of
n
-coboundaries. Then we have
B^{n}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))\subset Z^{n}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))
, for
n\geq 0
. The quotient groups

\displaystyle H^{n}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S)):=\frac{Z^{n}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))}{B^{n}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))},\text{ for }n\geq 0

are called the cohomology of the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
of weight
\kappa
with coefficients in the bimodule
(M,S)
.

It is easy to see that there is a short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\big\{C^{\bullet-1}(A_{R},\widetilde{M}),\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}\big\}\xrightarrow{\iota}\big\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S)),\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}}\big\}\xrightarrow{p}\big\{C^{\bullet}(A,M),\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}\big\}\rightarrow 0

of cochain complexes, where the maps
\iota
and
p
are given by
\iota(\Phi)=(0,(-1)^{n-1}\Phi)
, for
\Phi\in C^{n-1}(A_{R},\widetilde{M})
and
p(\chi,\Phi)=\chi
, for
(\chi,\Phi)\in C^{n}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))
. As a consequence, we obtain the following.

3.3 Theorem
.

Let
(A,R)
be a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
, and
(M,S)
be a bimodule over it. Then there is a long exact sequence connecting various cohomology groups

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow H^{0}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))\rightarrow H^{0}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(A,M)\rightarrow H^{0}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(A_{R},\widetilde{M})\rightarrow H^{1}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))\rightarrow\cdots.

A relation with the cohomology of Rota-Baxter algebras.
In
[
13
,
33
]
the authors introduced the cohomology of Rota-Baxter algebras. Here we will show that the cohomology of a Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,P)
of weight
\lambda
is isomorphic to the cohomology of the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R=\lambda~\!\mathrm{id}+2P)
of weight
\kappa=-\lambda^{2}
.

We first recall the cohomology of Rota-Baxter algebras from
[
33
]
. Let
(A,P)
be a Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\lambda
. Then the vector space
A
carries an associative algebra structure with the multiplication given by

\displaystyle a\ast_{P}b:=P(a)\cdot b~+~a\cdot P(b)+\lambda~\!a\cdot b,\text{ for }a,b\in A.

We denote this associative algebra simply by
A_{P}
.
Moreover, if
(M,Q)
is a bimodule over the Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,P)
of weight
\lambda
, then the maps

\displaystyle A\otimes M\rightarrow M,~(a,u)\mapsto P(a)\cdot u-Q(a\cdot u),

\displaystyle M\otimes A\rightarrow M,~(u,a)\mapsto u\cdot P(a)-Q(u\cdot a)

defines an
A_{P}
-bimodule structure on
M
(denoted by
\overline{M}
). Then the authors considered the cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{RBA}}((A,P),(M,Q)),\delta_{\mathrm{RBA}}\}
, where

\displaystyle C^{n}_{\mathrm{RBA}}((A,P),(M,Q))=\begin{cases}M&\text{ if }n\geq 0,\\
C^{n}(A,M)\oplus C^{n-1}(A_{P},\overline{M})=\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n},M)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes n-1},M)&\text{ if }n\geq 1.\end{cases}

The coboundary map is given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{RBA}}(u)=~

\displaystyle\big(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(u),-u\big),

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{RBA}}(f,g)=~

\displaystyle\big(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(f),~-\overline{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(g)-\Phi^{n}(f)\big),

where
\overline{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}
is the Hochschild coboundary operator of the associative algebra
A_{P}
with coefficients in the
A_{P}
-bimodule
\overline{M}
, and
\{\Phi^{n}:C^{n}(A,M)\rightarrow C^{n}(A_{P},\overline{M})\}_{n\geq 0}
is the sequence of maps given by
\Phi^{0}=\mathrm{id}_{M}
and

\displaystyle\Phi^{n}(f)(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=f\big(P(a_{1}),\ldots,P(a_{n})\big)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda^{n-k-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}~Q\circ f\big(a_{1},\ldots,P(a_{i_{1}}),\ldots,P(a_{i_{k}}),\ldots,a_{n}\big).

The cohomology groups of the cochain complex
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{RBA}}((A,P),(M,Q)),\delta_{\mathrm{RBA}}\}
are called the cohomology of the Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,P)
with coefficients in the bimodule
(M,Q).

3.4 Theorem
.

Let
(A,P)
be a Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\lambda
, and
(M,Q)
be a bimodule over it. Then the cohomology of the Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,P)
with coefficients in
(M,Q)
is isomorphic to the cohomology of the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R=\lambda~\!\mathrm{id}_{A}+2P)
with coefficients in the bimodule
(M,S=\lambda~\!\mathrm{id}_{M}+2Q)
.

Proof.

For each
n\geq 0
, we define an isomorphism of vector spaces

\displaystyle\Theta_{n}:C^{n}_{\mathrm{RBA}}((A,P),(M,Q))\rightarrow C^{n}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))

by

\displaystyle\Theta_{0}(u)=~

\displaystyle\frac{u}{2},~\text{ for }u\in C^{0}_{\mathrm{RBA}}((A,P),(M,Q))=M,

\displaystyle\Theta_{n}(f,g)=~

\displaystyle(f,~2^{n-2}g),~\text{ for }(f,g)\in C^{n\geq 1}_{\mathrm{RBA}}((A,P),(M,Q)).

Then it is straightforward to verify that
\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}}\circ\Theta_{n}=\Theta_{n+1}\circ\delta_{\mathrm{RBA}}
, for all
n\geq 0
. In other words, the collections
\{\Theta_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
defines an isomorphism of cochain complexes from
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{RBA}}((A,P),(M,Q)),\delta_{\mathrm{RBA}}\}
to
\{C^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{RBA}}((A,R),(M,S)),\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}}\}
. Hence the result follows.
∎

4.
Deformations of modified Rota-Baxter algebras

In this section, we study formal one-parameter deformations of modified Rota-Baxter algebras. In particular, we show that the infinitesimal in a formal deformation of a modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
is a
2
-cocycle in the cohomology complex of
(A,R)
with coefficients in itself. Further, the vanishing of the second cohomology implies the rigidity of the modified Rota-Baxter algebra.

Let
(A,R)
be a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
. Let
\mu
denotes the associative multiplication on
A
, i.e.
\mu(a,b)=a\cdot b
, for
a,b\in A
. Consider the space
A[[t]]
of formal power series in
t
with coefficients from
A
. Then
A[[t]]
is a
\mathbf{k}[[t]]
-module.

4.1 Definition
.

A
formal one-parameter deformation
of the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
of weight
\kappa
consists of two formal power series of the form

\displaystyle\mu_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mu_{i}t^{i},\text{ where }\mu_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes 2},A)\text{ with }\mu_{0}=\mu,

\displaystyle R_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}R_{i}t^{i},\text{ where }R_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}(A,A)\text{ with }R_{0}=R,

such that the
\mathbf{k}[[t]]
-module
A[[t]]
is an associative algebra with the
\mathbf{k}[[t]]
-bilinear multiplication
\mu_{t}
and the
\mathbf{k}[[t]]
-linear map
R_{t}:A[[t]]\rightarrow A[[t]]
is a modified Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\kappa
. In other words,
\big(A[[t]]=(A[[t]],\mu_{t}),R_{t}\big)
is a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
.

We often denote a formal one-parameter deformation as above by the pair
(\mu_{t},R_{t})
.

It follows that
(\mu_{t},R_{t})
is a formal one-parameter deformation of the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
of weight
\kappa
if and only if the followings are hold:

\displaystyle\mu_{t}\big(\mu_{t}(a,b),c\big)=~

\displaystyle\mu_{t}\big(a,\mu_{t}(b,c)\big),

\displaystyle\mu_{t}\big(R_{t}(a),R_{t}(b)\big)=~

\displaystyle R_{t}\big(\mu_{t}(R_{t}(a),b)+\mu_{t}(a,R_{t}(b))\big)+\kappa~\mu_{t}(a,b),

for all
a,b,c\in A
. By expanding these equations and comparing the coefficients of
t^{n}
(for
n\geq 0
) in both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}\mu_{i}\big(\mu_{j}(a,b),c\big)=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}\mu_{i}\big(a,\mu_{j}(b,c)\big),

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}\mu_{i}\big(R_{j}(a),R_{j}(b)\big)=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}R_{i}\big(\mu_{j}(R_{k}(a),b)~+~\mu_{j}(a,R_{k}(b))\big)+\kappa~\mu_{n}(a,b),

for
n\geq 0
. The equations are obviously hold for
n=0
as
\mu
is the associative multiplication on
A
and the linear map
R:A\rightarrow A
is a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
. However, for
n=1
, we get

(1)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(a\cdot b,c)+\mu_{1}(a,b)\cdot c=~

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(a,b\cdot c)+a\cdot\mu_{1}(b,c),

(2)

\displaystyle R_{1}(a)\cdot R(b)+R(a)\cdot R_{1}(b)+\mu_{1}(R(a),R(b))=~

\displaystyle R_{1}\big(R(a)\cdot b+a\cdot R(b)\big)+R\big(\mu_{1}(R(a),b)~+~\mu_{1}(a,R(b))\big)

\displaystyle+R\big(R_{1}(a)\cdot b+a\cdot R_{1}(b)\big)+\kappa~\mu_{n}(a,b),

for all
a,b,c\in A
.
Note the the equation (
1
) is equivalent to
(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}\mu_{1})(a,b,c)=0
while the equation (
2
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(R_{1})+\Psi^{2}(\mu_{1})=0.

Thus, it follows that

(3)

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}}(\mu_{1},R_{1})=(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\mu_{1}),~-\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(R_{1})-\Psi^{2}(\mu_{1}))=0.

This shows that
(\mu_{1},R_{1})
is a
2
-cocycle in the cohomology complex of
(A,R)
with coefficients in itself. This is called the
infinitesimal
of the formal one-parameter deformation
(\mu_{t},R_{t})
.

4.2 Definition
.

Let
(\mu_{t},R_{t})
and
(\mu_{t}^{\prime},R_{t}^{\prime})
be two formal one-parameter deformations of the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
of weight
\kappa
. These two deformations are said to be
equivalent
if there exists a formal isomorphism

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{i}t^{i}:\big(A[[t]]=(A[[t]],\mu_{t}),R_{t}\big)\rightarrow\big(A[[t]]^{\prime}=(A[[t]],\mu_{t}^{\prime}),R_{t}^{\prime}\big)~~\text{ with }\varphi_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{A}

between modified Rota-Baxter algebras of weight
\kappa
, i.e.

(4)

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}\big(\mu_{t}(a,b)\big)=\mu_{t}^{\prime}\big(\varphi_{t}(a),\varphi_{t}(b)\big)~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\varphi_{t}\circ R_{t}=R_{t}^{\prime}\circ\varphi_{t},~\text{ for }a,b\in A.

By expanding the equations in (
4
) and comparing the coefficients of
t^{n}
(for
n\geq 0
) in both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}\varphi_{i}\big(\mu_{j}(a,b)\big)=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j+k=n}\mu_{i}^{\prime}\big(\varphi_{j}(a),\varphi_{k}(b)\big),

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}\varphi_{i}\circ R_{j}=~

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=n}R_{i}^{\prime}\circ\varphi_{j},

for
n\geq 0
. The equations are hold for
n=0
as
\varphi_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
. However, for
n=1
, we get

(5)

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(a,b)+\varphi_{1}(a\cdot b)=~

\displaystyle\mu_{1}^{\prime}(a,b)+\varphi_{1}(a)\cdot b+a\cdot\varphi_{1}(b),

(6)

\displaystyle R_{1}+\varphi_{1}\circ R=~

\displaystyle R_{1}^{\prime}+R\circ\varphi_{1},.

Note that the equation (
5
) is same as
\mu_{1}-\mu_{1}^{\prime}=\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\varphi_{1})
, while the equation (
6
) is same as

\displaystyle R_{1}-R_{1}^{\prime}=-\Psi^{1}(\varphi_{1}).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle(\mu_{1},R_{1})-(\mu_{1}^{\prime},R_{1}^{\prime})=\big(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\varphi_{1}),~-\Psi^{1}(\varphi_{1})\big)=\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}}(\varphi_{1},0).

As a consequence of the above discussions, we obtain the following.

4.3 Theorem
.

Let
(A,R)
be a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
. Suppose
(\mu_{t},R_{t})
is a formal one-parameter deformation of
(A,R).
Then the infinitesimal is a
2
-cocycle in the cohomology complex of
(A,R)
with coefficients in itself. Moreover, the corresponding cohomology class depends only on the equivalence class of the deformation.

We end this section by considering the rigidity of a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
. We also find a sufficient condition for rigidity.

4.4 Definition
.

A modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
is said to be
rigid
if any formal one-parameter deformation
(\mu_{t},R_{t})
is equivalent to the undeformed one
(\mu,R)
.

4.5 Theorem
.

Let
(A,R)
be a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
. If
H^{2}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(A,R))=0
then
(A,R)
is rigid.

Proof.

Let
(\mu_{t},R_{t})
be any formal one-parameter deformation of the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
of weight
\kappa
. Then the infinitesimal
(\mu_{1},R_{1})
is a
2
-cocycle. Thus, it follows from the hypothesis that

(7)

\displaystyle(\mu_{1},R_{1})=\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}}(\varphi_{1},a),\text{ for some }(\varphi_{1},a)\in C^{1}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(A,R)).

Note that

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}}(\varphi_{1}+\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(a),0)=~

\displaystyle\big(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\varphi_{1}),~-\Psi^{1}(\varphi_{1})-\Psi^{1}\circ\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(a)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\varphi_{1}),~-\Psi^{1}(\varphi_{1})-\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(a)\big)=\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}}(\varphi_{1},a).

Thus, without loss of any generality, we may assume that
(\mu_{1},R_{1})=\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}}(\varphi_{1},0)
, for some
(\varphi_{1},0)\in C^{1}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(A,R))
. Then we define a map
\varphi_{t}=\mathrm{id}_{A}+t\varphi_{1}:A[[t]]\rightarrow A[[t]]
and consider the pair

\displaystyle\big(\overline{\mu}_{t}=\varphi_{t}\circ\mu_{t}\circ(\varphi_{t}^{-1}\otimes\varphi_{t}^{-1}),~\overline{R}_{t}=\varphi_{t}\circ R_{t}\circ\varphi_{t}^{-1}\big).

Then
(\overline{\mu}_{t},\overline{R}_{t})
is a formal one-parameter deformation equivalent to
(\mu_{t},R_{t})
. Moreover, it follows that
\overline{\mu}_{t}
and
\overline{R}_{t}
are of the following forms

\displaystyle\overline{\mu}_{t}=\mu+\overline{\mu}_{2}t^{2}+\cdots~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\overline{R}_{t}=R+\overline{R}_{2}t^{2}+\cdots.

By repeating this argument, one can conclude that
(\mu_{t},R_{t})
is equivalent to
(\mu,R)
. This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Abelian extensions of modified Rota-Baxter algebras

In this section, we define and study abelian extensions of a modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
by a bimodule
(M,S)
. We show that the equivalence classes of such abelian extensions are classified by the second cohomology group
H^{2}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))
.

Let
(A,R)
be a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
. Let
(M,S)
be a pair consisting of a vector space
M
and a linear map
S:M\rightarrow M
. Note that the pair
(M,S)
can be regarded as a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
, where we equip
M
with the trivial associative multiplication.

5.1 Definition
.

(i) An
abelian extension
of the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
of weight
\kappa
by a pair
(M,S)
is a short exact sequence of modified Rota-Baxter algebras

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(M,S)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i}
\textstyle{(E,U)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{(A,R)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}

We often denote an abelian extension as above simply by
(E,U)
when the structure maps
i
and
\pi
are clear from the context.

(ii) Two abelian extensions
(E,U)
and
(E^{\prime},{U^{\prime}})
of the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
by a pair
(M,S)
are said to be
isomorphic
if there exists an isomorphism
\varphi:(E,U)\rightarrow(E^{\prime},U^{\prime})
of modified Rota-Baxter algebras making the following diagram commutative

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(M,S)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i}
\textstyle{(E,U)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{(A,R)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(M,S)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i^{\prime}}
\textstyle{(E,U)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi^{\prime}}
\textstyle{(A,R)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}

Let
(E,U)
be an abelian extension as of (
5.1
). A section of the map
\pi
is a linear map
s:A\rightarrow E
that satisfies
\pi s=\mathrm{id}_{A}
. There is always a section of the map
\pi
. For any section
s
, we define two bilinear maps
A\times M\rightarrow M
,
(a,u)\mapsto s(a)\cdot_{E}i(u)
and
M\times A\rightarrow M
,
(u,a)\mapsto i(u)\cdot_{E}s(a)
, for
a\in A
,
u\in M
. Here
~\cdot_{E}~
denotes the associative multiplication on
E
. With these bilinear maps, it is easy to see that
M
is an
A
-bimodule (see
[
27
]
). More generally, the pair
(M,S)
is a bimodule over the modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
of weight
\kappa
. This bimodule is called the induced bimodule over
(A,R)
.

Let
(M,S)
be a given bimodule over a modified Rota-Baxter algebra
(A,R)
of weight
\kappa
. We denote
\mathrm{Ext}((A,R),(M,S))
by the set of all isomorphism classes of abelian extensions of
(A,R)
by the pair
(M,S)
for which the induced bimodule on
(M,S)
is the given one.

5.2 Theorem
.

Let
(A,R)
be a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
, and
(M,S)
be a bimodule over it. Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathrm{Ext}((A,R),(M,S))
and the second cohomology group
H^{2}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))
.

Proof.

Let
(\chi,\Phi)\in Z^{2}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))
be a
2
-cocycle that represents an element in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))
. Since
(\chi,\Phi)
is a
2
-cocycle, we have
\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\chi)=0
and
\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\Phi)+\Psi^{2}(\chi)=0
. Consider the direct sum
A\oplus M
with the multiplication

\displaystyle(a,u)\cdot_{\ltimes}(b,v)=(a\cdot b,~a\cdot v+u\cdot a+\chi(a,b)),\text{ for }(a,u),(b,v)\in A\oplus M.

Since
\chi
is a Hochschild
2
-cocycle, it follows that the above multiplication is associative. Thus it makes the space
A\oplus M
into an associative algebra, denoted by
A\oplus_{\chi}M
. Further, we define a map
R_{\Phi}:A\oplus_{\chi}M\rightarrow A\oplus_{\chi}M
by

\displaystyle R_{\Phi}(a,u)=\big(R(a),S(u)+\Phi(a)\big),\text{ for }(a,u)\in A\oplus M.

Since
\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\Phi)+\Psi^{2}(\chi)=0
, it follows that
R_{\Phi}
is a modified Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\kappa
on the algebra
A\oplus_{\chi}M
. In other words,
(A\oplus_{\chi}M,R_{\Phi})
is a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
. Then it is easy to see that

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{(M,S)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i}
\textstyle{(A\oplus_{\chi}M,R_{\Phi})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{(A,R)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

is an abelian extension of
(A,R)
by
(M,S)
, where the structure maps
i
and
\pi
are respectively given by
i(u)=(0,u)
and
\pi(a,u)=a
, for
u\in M
,
(a,u)\in A\oplus M
. Note that the map
s:A\rightarrow A\oplus M
,
s(a)=(a,0)
is a section of the map
\pi
, and the section
s
induces the given bimodule structure on
(M,S).

Let
(\chi^{\prime},\Phi^{\prime})\in Z^{2}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))
be another
2
-cocycle cohomologous to
(\chi,\Phi)
. In other words, they corresponds to the same element in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))
. Then there exists a map
\theta\in\mathrm{Hom}(A,M)
such that

\displaystyle(\chi,\Phi)-(\chi^{\prime},\Phi^{\prime})=\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}}(\theta,0).

We now define a map
\varphi:A\oplus M\rightarrow A\oplus M
by

\displaystyle\varphi(a,u)=(a,u-\theta(a)).

It is then easy to verify that
\varphi:(A\oplus_{\chi}M,R_{\Phi})\rightarrow(A\oplus_{\chi^{\prime}}M,R_{\Phi^{\prime}})
is an isomorphism of modified Rota-Baxter algebras, which makes the abelian extensions
(A\oplus_{\chi}M,R_{\Phi})
and
(A\oplus_{\chi^{\prime}}M,R_{\Phi^{\prime}})
isomorphic. Therefore, there exists a well-defined map
\Upsilon:H^{2}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}((A,R),(M,S))
.

Conversely, let
(E,U)
be an abelian extension of
(A,R)
by
(M,S)
so that the induced bimodule structure on
(M,S)
coincides with the given one. Let
s:A\rightarrow E
be any section of the map
\pi
. We define maps
\chi^{s}\in\mathrm{Hom}(A^{\otimes 2},M)
and
\Phi^{s}\in\mathrm{Hom}(A,M)
by

\displaystyle\chi^{s}(a,b)=s(a)\cdot_{E}s(b)-s(a\cdot b)~~~\text{ and }~~~\Phi^{s}(a)=Us(a)-sR(a),\text{ for }a,b\in A.

Then straightforward calculations show that
\chi^{s}
is a Hochschild
2
-cocycle (i.e.
\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\psi^{s})=0
) and

\displaystyle\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\Phi^{s})+\Psi^{2}(\chi^{s})=0.

Thus, we have
\delta_{\mathrm{mRBA}}(\chi^{s},\Phi^{s})=\big(\delta_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\chi^{s}),~-\widetilde{\delta}_{\mathrm{Hoch}}(\Phi^{s})-\Psi^{2}(\chi^{s})\big)=0
, which shows that
(\chi^{s},\Phi^{s})
is a
2
-cocycle. It is easy to see that the corresponding class in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))
doesn’t depend on the choice of the section
s
.

Let
(E,U)
and
(E^{\prime},U^{\prime})
be two isomorphic abelian extensions of as (
5.1
). If
s:A\rightarrow E
is a section of the map
\pi
, then the map
s^{\prime}=\varphi s:A\rightarrow E^{\prime}
is a section of the map
\pi^{\prime}
. Let
(\chi^{s^{\prime}},\Phi^{s^{\prime}})
be the
2
-cocycle corresponding to the abelian extension
(E^{\prime},{U^{\prime}})
and the section
s^{\prime}
of the map
\pi^{\prime}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\chi^{s^{\prime}}(a,b)=~

\displaystyle s^{\prime}(a)\cdot_{E^{\prime}}s^{\prime}(b)-s^{\prime}(a\cdot b)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle(\varphi s)(a)\cdot_{E^{\prime}}(\varphi s)(b)-(\varphi s)(a\cdot b)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\varphi\big(s(a)\cdot_{E}s(b)-s(a\cdot b)\big)=\varphi(\chi^{s}(a,b))=\chi^{s}(a,b)

and

\displaystyle\Phi^{s^{\prime}}(a)=~

\displaystyle Us^{\prime}(a)-s^{\prime}R(a)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle U\varphi s(a)-\varphi sR(a)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\varphi Us(a)-\varphi sR(a)=\varphi(\Phi^{s}(a))=\Phi^{s}(a).

Hence
(\chi^{s^{\prime}},\Phi^{s^{\prime}})=(\chi^{s},\Phi^{s})
. Therefore, they corresponds to the same element in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))
. As a summary, we obtain a well-defined map
\Omega:\mathrm{Ext}((A,R),(M,S))\rightarrow H^{2}_{\mathrm{mRBA}}((A,R),(M,S))
. Finally, the maps
\Upsilon
and
\Omega
are inverses to each other. This completes the proof.
∎

We end this paper by suggesting some questions of further interest. We will address some of these questions in future.

We have seen that Rota-Baxter algebras are closely related to modified Rota-Baxter algebras of negative weights. In
[
13
,
33
]
the authors constructs an
L_{\infty}
-algebra whose Maurer-Cartan elements are precisely Rota-Baxter algebra structures on a vector space
A
. One requires a suitable linear transformation of this
L_{\infty}
-algebra to obtain a new one whose Maurer-Cartan elements are precisely modified Rota-Baxter algebras of fixed negative weight. In a subsequent work, we aim to provide a systematic construction of the
L_{\infty}
-algebra that characterize modified Rota-Baxter algebras of any weight as Maurer-Cartan elements.

Given an algebraic structure, it is an interesting question to find the corresponding homotopy version. Such homotopy version is better understood in terms of the minimal model of the operad governing such structure. The minimal model of the operad for Rota-Baxter algebras was considered in
[
33
]
. It could be interesting to find the similar theory for modified Rota-Baxter algebras.

In
[
24
]
the authors considered modified Rota-Baxter operators of weight
-1
on Lie algebras and studied their cohomology and deformation theory. One can easily generalize this to modified Rota-Baxter operators of arbitrary weight. Let
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g},[~,~])
be a Lie algebra. A linear map
R:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a modified Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\kappa
if

\displaystyle[R(x),R(y)]=R\big([R(x),y]+[x,R(y)]\big)+\kappa~\![x,y],\text{ for }x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

It follows that if
(A,R)
is a modified Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\kappa
then
R
is a modified Rota-Baxter operator of the same weight on the commutator Lie algebra structure on
A
. We define a modified Rota-Baxter Lie algebra of weight
\kappa
as a pair
(\mathfrak{g},R)
in which
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra and
R:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a modified Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\kappa
. By following the results of the present paper, one could also develop the cohomology and deformation theory of modified Rota-Baxter Lie algebras of any weight.

In the study of integrations of Rota-Baxter Lie algebras, the authors in
[
21
]
introduced Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
on abstract groups. Let
(G,\diamond)
be a group. A set map
B:G\rightarrow G
is a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
if

\displaystyle B(x)\diamond B(y)=B\big(x\diamond B(x)\diamond y\diamond B(x)^{-1}\big),\text{ for }x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

An interesting problem is to find the suitable notion of modified Rota-Baxter operator of weight
-1
on abstract groups. With this notion, one could expect integrations of modified Rota-Baxter Lie algebras of weight
-1
by modified Rota-Baxter Lie groups of weight
-1
.

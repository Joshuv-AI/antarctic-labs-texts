Basic Hopf algebras and symmetric bimodules
Katerina Hristova, Vanessa Miemietz

Abstract
. Motivated by the so-called ℋ {\mathcal{H}} -cell reduction theorems, we investigate certain classes of bicategories which have only one ℋ {\mathcal{H}} -cell apart from possibly the identity. We show that ℋ 0 {\mathcal{H}}_{0} -simple quasi fiab bicategories with unique ℋ {\mathcal{H}} -cell ℋ 0 {\mathcal{H}}_{0} are fusion categories. We further study two classes of non-semisimple quasi-fiab bicategories with a single ℋ {\mathcal{H}} -cell apart from the identity. The first is H A \sc\mbox{H}\hskip 1.0pt_{A} , indexed by a finite-dimensional radically graded basic Hopf algebra A A , and the second is G A \sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A} , consisting of symmetric projective A A - A A -bimodules. We show that H A \sc\mbox{H}\hskip 1.0pt_{A} can be viewed as a 1 1 -full subbicategory of G A \sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A} and classify simple transitive birepresentations for G A \sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A} . We point out that the number of equivalence classes of the latter is finite, while that for H A \sc\mbox{H}\hskip 1.0pt_{A} is generally not.

Since the begining of the century, tremendous progress in representation theory has been made using ideas of categorification, see e.g.

The most powerful tool for classifying simple transitive

-cells

2.
Preliminaries

Here we recall some basic definitions from finitary birepresentation theory using the conventions from
[
MMMTZ2
]
.

Throughout, we let
\Bbbk
denote an algebraically closed field. We call a
\Bbbk
-linear, additive category finitary if it is idempotent complete, has finite-dimensional morphism spaces and only finitely many indecomposable objects up to isomorphism. We denote by
\mathfrak{A}_{\Bbbk}^{f}
the
2
-category of finitary categories,
\Bbbk
-linear functors and natural transformations.

We call a bicategory
C
multifinitary if the number of objects of
C
is finite,
the categories
\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt(\mathtt{i},\mathtt{j})
are
finitary for all
\mathtt{i},\mathtt{j}\in\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt
, and horizontal composition of
2
-morphisms is
\Bbbk
-bilinear.
If, additionally, the identity
1
-morphism on each object is indecomposable, we say
C
is finitary.

A bicategory
C
is quasi (multi)fiab if it is (multi)finitary and each
1
-morphism has a left and a right adjoint. If these are moreover isomorphic,
C
is (multifiab), see
[
MMMTZ2
, Definition 2.5]
for more detail.

A finitary birepresentation
\mathbf{M}
of a multifinitary bicategory
C
is a pseudofunctor
\mathbf{M}\colon\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt\to\mathfrak{A}_{\Bbbk}^{f}
. A finitary birepresentation is called simple transitive if
\coprod_{\mathtt{i}\in\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt}\mathbf{M}(\mathtt{i})
has no proper
C
-stable ideals.

If
C
is a bicategory, and
\mathtt{i}\in\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt
, we denote by
\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt_{\mathtt{i}}
the endomorphism bicategory of
\mathtt{i}
, more precisely, the bicategory with one object
\mathtt{i}
and morphism category
\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt(\mathtt{i},\mathtt{i})
.

The set of isomorphism classes of indecomposable
1
-morphisms of a multifinitary bicategory
C
carries a left partial preorder
\leq_{L}
generated by setting
\mathrm{F}\leq_{L}\mathrm{G}
if there exists
\mathrm{H}
such that
\mathrm{G}
is isomorphic to a direct summand of
\mathrm{H}\mathrm{F}
. Similarly, one defines right and two-sided partial preorders
\leq_{R}
and
\leq_{J}
, respectively. Equivalence classes with respect to these are called left, right and two-sided cells, respectively. Moreover, an
{\mathcal{H}}
-cell is an intersection of a left and a right cell.

By a mild generalisation of
[
ChMa
, Subsection 3.2]
, every finitary birepresentation
\mathbf{M}
of a (multi)finitary bicategory has an apex, which is the unique maximal two-sided cell not annihilated by
\mathbf{M}
.
To each left cell
{\mathcal{L}}
, we can associate the cell birepresentation
\mathbf{C}_{\mathcal{L}}
, which is the quotient of the left
2
-ideal generated by the
1
-morphisms in
{\mathcal{L}}
by its unique maximal
C
-stable ideal. By construction, this is simple transitive and its apex is the two-sided cell containing
{\mathcal{L}}
. For more details on cells and cell birepresentations, see
[
MMMTZ2
, Section 2.5]
.

If
\mathcal{J}
is a two-sided cell in
C
, we say
C
is
\mathcal{J}
-simple provided it has no proper
2
-ideals which do not contain the identities on
1
-morphisms in
\mathcal{J}
.

3.
A single
{\mathcal{H}}
-cell
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
-simplicity implies fusion

Throughout this section, let
C
be a quasi fiab
2
-category with only one object
\bullet
(hence denoting the identity
1
-morphism on
\bullet
simply by
\mathbbm{1}
), and with only one
{\mathcal{H}}
-cell
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
C
is
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
-simple, then
C
is a fusion category.

Proof.

Recall (cf.
[
EGNO
, Definition 4.1.1]
) that a bicategory
C
is a fusion category if

(1)

it has only one object
\bullet
;

(2)

the category
\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt(\bullet,\bullet)
is a
\Bbbk
-linear, additive, with finitely many isomorphism classes of indecomposable objects;

(3)

\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt(\bullet,\bullet)
is semisimple and all objects have finite length;

(4)

morphism spaces in
\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt(\bullet,\bullet)
are finite-dimensional;

(5)

horizontal composition is bilinear on
2
-morphisms;

(6)

\mathbbm{1}=\mathbbm{1}_{\bullet}
is indecomposable;

(7)

it admits adjunctions (i.e. is rigid).

By assumption, our
2
-category
C
is quasi fiab on one object, which takes care of (
1
),(
2
),(
4
), (
5
), (
6
) and (
7
), and provided that
\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt(\bullet,\bullet)
is semisimple, all objects in
\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt(\bullet,\bullet)
have finite length,
so we only need to check that
\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt(\bullet,\bullet)
is semisimple.

By
[
KMMZ
, Theorem 2]
applied to the case where
\mathbf{M}
is the cell birepresentation
\mathbf{C}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0}}
associated to our (unique) cell
{{\mathcal{H}}_{0}}
, and
\mathrm{F}=\mathbbm{1}
, it follows that
\mathbf{C}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0}}(\bullet)
is semisimple.
Thus the kernel of the
2
-functor
\mathbf{C}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0}}
contains all
2
-morphisms which belong to the radical of
\mathbf{C}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0}}(\bullet,\bullet)
. By
{{\mathcal{H}}_{0}}
-simplicity, the cell birepresentation
\mathbf{C}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{0}}
is faithful, hence the radical of
\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt(\bullet,\bullet)
is zero.
∎

Motivated by this result, the remainder of this article investigates certain classes of quasi fiab bicategories, which have precisely two
{\mathcal{H}}
-cells, namely one consisting of the identity
1
-morphism and precisely one other
{\mathcal{H}}
-cell (which is necessarily strictly larger in the two-sided order).

Hopf algebras and projective bimodules

Let

4.1.
Bimodule conventions

Let

be

-
A
-bimodules. We fix the following standard canonical isomorphisms:

(K\otimes_{A}M)\otimes_{A}N\xrightarrow{\cong}K\otimes_{A}(M\otimes_{A}N),\ (k\otimes m)\otimes n\mapsto k\otimes(m\otimes n),

Lemma 4.12

Lemma 4.13

Lemma 5.6

Proof.

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{1}&:=\{(A,{\pi}_{1})\}\\
\mathcal{S}_{11}&:=\{Ae_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A|g\in G\}\\
\mathcal{S}_{01}&:=\{A_{0}\bar{e}_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A|g\in G\}\\
\mathcal{S}_{10}&:=\{Ae_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\bar{e}_{1}A_{0}|g\in G\}\\
\mathcal{S}_{00}&:=\{A_{0}\bar{e}_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\bar{e}_{1}A_{0}|g\in G\}.\end{split}

Lemma 5.11
.

Up to isomorphism, the indecomposable
1
-morphisms in
\widetilde{\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}}
are given by bimodules in

\mathcal{S}_{1}\cup\mathcal{S}_{11}\cup\mathcal{S}_{01}\cup\mathcal{S}_{10}\cup\mathcal{S}_{00}.

Proof.

First note that by Lemma
5.9
for the objects in
\mathcal{S}_{11}\cup\mathcal{S}_{01}\cup\mathcal{S}_{10}\cup\mathcal{S}_{00}
and by construction for
(A,{\pi}_{1})
, the given bimodules are indeed indecomposable
1
-morphisms in
\widetilde{\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}}
.

Also by construction and Lemma
5.10
, each indecomposable
1
-morphism in
\widetilde{\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}}
is isomorphic to
(A,{\pi}_{1})
or one of
A\hat{e}_{h}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\hat{e}_{h^{\prime}}A
for
h,h^{\prime}\in G
, with
\hat{e}_{h}\in\{e_{h},\bar{e}_{h}\}
for
h\in G
. It thus suffices to show that each of the
A\hat{e}_{h}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\hat{e}_{h^{\prime}}A
is isomorphic to one of the bimodules in
\mathcal{S}_{11}\cup\mathcal{S}_{01}\cup\mathcal{S}_{10}\cup\mathcal{S}_{00}
in
\tilde{\mathcal{X}}_{\hat{A}}
. This, however, is a consequence of Lemma
5.9
, since
(A\hat{e}_{h}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\hat{e}_{h^{\prime}}A)^{h^{\prime-1}}\cong A\hat{e}_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\hat{e}_{1}A
as
{\hat{A}}
-
{\hat{A}}
-bimodules.
∎

5.5.
Two-sided cells in
\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
and
\widetilde{\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}}

We keep the assumptions from Section
5.4
and recall the notation introduced after Lemma
5.1
.

Proposition 5.12
.

The bicategory
\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
has two
{\mathcal{H}}
-cells, which are also two-sided cells, namely

(
a
)

\mathcal{H}_{1}

consisting of one element
(\mathbbm{1}_{\bullet})
;

(
b
)

the two-sided cell
\mathcal{H}_{0}
consisting of all isomorphism classes of

1
-morphisms in
\mathcal{S}_{11}
.

Proof.

Since tensor products in which one of the factors is a projective bimodule never contain a copy of the regular bimodule as a direct summand, it follows immediately that the indecomposable
1
-morphism in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
is strictly smaller than those in
\mathcal{S}_{11}
in the left right and two-sided order. This shows the existence of an
{\mathcal{H}}
-cell
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
, as claimed in (
b
), which is a left, right and two-sided cell.
To complete the proof, it suffices to show that the indecomposable
1
-morphisms in
\mathcal{S}_{11}
are in the same left and the same right cell. Let
Ae_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A
and
Ae_{h}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A
be two representatives of isomorphism classes of indecomposable
1
-morphisms in
\mathcal{S}_{11}
. Then
Ae_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A
is a direct sumand of
(Ae_{gh^{{\,\text{-}1}}}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A)\otimes_{\tilde{\mathcal{X}}}(Ae_{h}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A)
, hence
Ae_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A\leq_{L}Ae_{h}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A
. Similarly,
Ae_{h}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A\leq_{L}Ae_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A
since
Ae_{h}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A
is a direct sumand of
(Ae_{hg^{{\,\text{-}1}}}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A)\otimes_{\tilde{\mathcal{X}}}(Ae_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A)
, so they are in the same left cell. Similarly, one sees that they are in the same right, and hence in the same
{\mathcal{H}}
-cell, which then is also a two-sided cell.
∎

Recall that
\sc\mbox{H}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
can be viewed as a
1
-full subbicategory of
\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
and hence shares its
{\mathcal{H}}
-cell structure.

Proposition 5.13
.

\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
and
\sc\mbox{H}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
are
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
-simple.

Proof.

Assume
I
is a nonzero
2
-ideal in
\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
, identified with a subbicategory of
\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
under
\Theta
, and let
f\colon M\to N
be a morphism in
\sc\mbox{I}\hskip 1.0pt(\bullet,\bullet)
. Note that
A\otimes_{\Bbbk}A
is a
1
-morphism in the essential image of
\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
under
\Theta
. Then
\mathrm{id}_{A\otimes_{\Bbbk}A}\otimes f\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A\otimes_{\Bbbk}A}
contains an identity component on a
1
-morphism in
\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
, as required.

The proof for
\sc\mbox{H}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
is analogous.
∎

Proposition 5.14
.

The bicategory
\widetilde{\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}}
has two two-sided cells, namely

(
a
)

\mathcal{J}_{1}

consisting of one element
(\mathbbm{1}_{\bullet})
;

(
b
)

the two-sided cell
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{0}
consisting of all isomorphism classes of indecomposable

1
-morphisms in
\mathcal{S}_{11}\cup\mathcal{S}_{01}\cup\mathcal{S}_{10}\cup\mathcal{S}_{00}
.
Furthermore, the two-sided cell
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{0}
consists of two left cells
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}=\mathcal{S}_{10}\cup\mathcal{S}_{00}
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}=\mathcal{S}_{11}\cup\mathcal{S}_{01}
and two right cells
\mathcal{R}_{0}=\mathcal{S}_{01}\cup\mathcal{S}_{00}
and
\mathcal{R}_{1}=\mathcal{S}_{11}\cup\mathcal{S}_{10}
. Hence, the
{\mathcal{H}}
-cells are
{\mathcal{H}}_{ij}=\mathcal{S}_{ij}
for
i,j\in\{0,1\}
.

Proof.

Part (
a
) follows as in Proposition
5.12
(
a
), since tensor products involving projective
A\times A_{0}
bimodules never contain direct summands isomorphic to the regular
A
-
A
-bimodule.

To prove part (
b
), let
A_{i}\in\{A,A_{0}\}
for
i=1,\cdots,3
, and let
\hat{e}_{h}\in\{e_{h},\bar{e}_{h}\}
, for
h\in G
, as appropriate. Then
A_{1}\hat{e}_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\hat{e}_{1}A_{2}
is a direct summand of
(A_{1}\hat{e}_{gh^{{\,\text{-}1}}}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\hat{e}_{1}A_{3})\otimes_{\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}}(A_{3}\hat{e}_{h}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\hat{e}_{1}A_{2})
, so
A_{1}\hat{e}_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\hat{e}_{1}A_{2}\leq_{L}A_{3}\hat{e}_{h}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\hat{e}_{1}A_{2}
for any
A_{3}
. This shows that all
1
-morphisms in
\mathcal{S}_{10}\cup\mathcal{S}_{00}
are in the same left cell and all
1
-morphisms in
\mathcal{S}_{11}\cup\mathcal{S}_{01}
are in the same left cell. Given that tensoring
A_{1}\hat{e}_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\hat{e}_{1}A_{2}
on the left does not change
A_{2}
, it is clear that
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}=\mathcal{S}_{10}\cup\mathcal{S}_{00}
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{1}=\mathcal{S}_{11}\cup\mathcal{S}_{01}
are two different left cells. Similarly, ones proves the statement about right cells. The statement about
{\mathcal{H}}
-cells and the two-sided cell
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{0}
follows immediately.
∎

Lemma 5.15
.

The endomorphism bicategory
\widetilde{\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}}_{\ast}
of the object
\ast
in
\widetilde{\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}}
has a unique left, right and two-sided cell
{\mathcal{H}}_{00}
and is biequivalent to
\sc\mbox{V}\hskip 1.0ptec_{G}
.

Proof.

Given that the indecomposable
1
-morphisms in
\widetilde{\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}}_{\ast}
are precisely those in
{\mathcal{H}}_{00}
, the first statement follows from Lemma
5.14
. Now

\begin{split}(A_{0}\bar{e}_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\bar{e}_{1}A_{0})\otimes^{G}(A_{0}\bar{e}_{h}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\bar{e}_{1}A_{0})&=\bigoplus_{k\in G}(A_{0}\bar{e}_{gk}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\bar{e}_{k}A_{0})\otimes_{A}(A_{0}\bar{e}_{h}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\bar{e}_{1}A_{0})\\
&\cong A_{0}\bar{e}_{gh}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\bar{e}_{1}A_{0}.\end{split}

Therefore
\widetilde{\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}}_{\ast}
is a semi-simple bicategory with one object, which decategorifies to
\mathbb{Z}G
. Moreover, the associator is induced by the associator on bimodules given in Section
4.1
which corresponds to the trivial
3
-cocycle and thus
\widetilde{\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}}_{\ast}
is biequivalent to
\sc\mbox{V}\hskip 1.0ptec_{G}
, c.f.
[
EGNO
, Proposition 4.10.3]
.
∎

5.6.
Adjunctions

We keep the setup of Section
5.4
and additionally assume that
A

is self-injective.
We denote by
\nu
the bijection on
\mathtt{E}

which is induced by the Nakayama automorphism of
A
given by

\mathrm{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(eA,\Bbbk)\cong A\nu(e),\quad\text{ for }e\in\mathtt{E}.

Note that given our labelling of idempotents by elements of
G
, this induces and action of
\nu
on
G
and we define
\nu(g)
by
\nu(e_{g})=e_{\nu(g)}
.

Lemma 5.16
.

For any
g\in G
, we have
\nu(g)=\nu(1)g
.

Proof.

By definition,
Ae_{\nu(1)g}\cong(Ae_{\nu(1)})^{g}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\Bbbk}(e_{1}A,\Bbbk)^{g}
is the indecomposable injective
A
-module whose socle element
e_{1}^{*}
satisfies

e_{h}\cdot(e_{1})^{*}=g(e_{h})(e_{1})^{*}=e_{hg^{{\,\text{-}1}}}(e_{1})^{*}\neq 0
if and only if
g=h
.
It is hence isomorphic to the injective
A
-module with socle
(e_{g})^{*}
, which is
Ae_{\nu(g)}
.
∎

Proposition 5.17
.

We have adjunctions

(
a
)

(Ae_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A,Ae_{\nu(1)g^{{\,\text{-}1}}}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A)
;

(
b
)

(A_{0}\bar{e}_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A,Ae_{\nu(1)g^{{\,\text{-}1}}}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\bar{e}_{1}A_{0})
;

(
c
)

(Ae_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\bar{e}_{1}A_{0},A_{0}\bar{e}_{g^{{\,\text{-}1}}}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A)
;

(
d
)

(A_{0}\bar{e}_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\bar{e}_{1}A_{0},A_{0}\bar{e}_{g^{{\,\text{-}1}}}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\bar{e}_{1}A_{0})
.

Proof.

By
[
CM
, Theorem 4.3]
, there is an equivalence of categories between the projective objects in
\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}
and the
G
-invariant objects in the category of projective
{\hat{A}}
-
{\hat{A}}
-bimodules. Under this equivalence
Ae_{g}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A
corresponds to
\bigoplus_{h\in G}Ae_{gh}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{h}A
. Hence its adjoint corresponds to the adjoint of
\bigoplus_{h\in G}Ae_{gh}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{h}A
, which is given by

\bigoplus_{h\in H}Ae_{\nu(h)}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{gh}A\cong\bigoplus_{h\in H}Ae_{\nu(g^{{\,\text{-}1}}h)}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{h}A
. Under the equivalence, this corresponds to
Ae_{\nu(g^{{\,\text{-}1}})}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A
which is isomorphic to
Ae_{\nu(1)g^{{\,\text{-}1}}}\otimes_{\Bbbk}e_{1}A
by Lemma
5.16
. The other adjunctions are checked similarly.
∎

5.7.
Simple transitive birepresentations of
\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}

We keep the notation and assumptions from Section
5.6
. In particular, we assume that
A
is basic, self-injective has a fixed complete
G
-invariant
set
\mathtt{E}
of primitive idempotents on which
G
acts regularly.
It follows immediately from Proposition
5.17
that, under these assumptions,
\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
and
\widetilde{\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}}
are quasi fiab, and fiab if and only if
A
is weakly symmetric.

In order to classify simple transitive birepresentations for
\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
, we will need a slight generalisation of
[
MMMZ
, Theorem 15]
.

Theorem 5.18
.

Let
C
be a quasi multifiab bicategory,
\mathcal{J}
a maximal two-sided cell in
C
and
{\mathcal{H}}
an
{\mathcal{H}}
-cell in
\mathcal{J}
such that
{\mathcal{H}}^{*}={\mathcal{H}}
. Let
\mathtt{i}
be such that, for all
\mathrm{F}\in{\mathcal{H}}
, we have
\mathrm{F}\in\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt({\mathtt{i},\mathtt{i}})
and let
\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt_{\mathcal{H}}
be the
2
-full subbicategory of
C
on object
\mathtt{i}
with
1
-morphisms in
\operatorname{add}\{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathtt{i}},\mathrm{F}\,|\;\mathrm{F}\in{\mathcal{H}}\}
. Then there is a bijection between simple transitive birepresentations of
C
with apex
\mathcal{J}
and those of
\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt_{\mathcal{H}}
with apex
{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to that of Theorem 4.32 in
[
MMMTZ2
]
, noting that the only place where
C
being fiab is crucially used there is to obtain
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}^{\star}
.
∎

We thus obtain the analogous classification of simple transitive birepresentations to the one given in
[
MMZ2
]
in the case where
G
is abelian.

Theorem 5.19
.

We retain the above assumptions on
A
. Let
\mathbf{M}
be a simple transitive birepresentation of
\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
.

(1)

If the apex of
\mathbf{M}
is
\mathcal{J}_{1}
, then
\mathbf{M}
is the trivial birepresentation associated to
\mathbbm{1}_{\bullet}
, meaning
\mathbf{M}(\bullet)
is equivalent to
\Bbbk\text{-}\mathrm{mod}
, the identity
1
-morphism
(A_{1},{\pi}_{1})
acts as the identity functor, and all other indecomposable
1
-morphisms annihilate.

(2)

If the apex of
\mathbf{M}
is
\mathcal{J}_{0}
, there is a natural bijection between
equivalence classes of simple transitive birepresentations of
\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
with apex
\mathcal{J}_{0}

and pairs
(K,\omega)
, where
K
is a subgroup of
G
and
\omega\in H^{2}(K,\Bbbk^{*})
.

Proof.

For
\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{1}
, the statement is immediate, as the
\mathcal{J}_{1}
-simple quotient of
\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
is biequivalent to
\sc\mbox{C}\hskip 1.0pt_{\Bbbk}
.

For
\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{0}
, consider
\widetilde{\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}}
. Then we can realize
\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
as a
both
1
- and
2
-full subbicategory of
\widetilde{\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}}
given by the endomorphism category
\widetilde{\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}}_{\bullet}
of
\bullet
. Moreover,
\mathcal{J}_{0}
corresponds to
{\mathcal{H}}_{11}
under this identification. By Theorem
5.18

there is a bijection between equivalence classes of simple transitive birepresentations of

\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
with apex
\mathcal{J}_{0}
, and equivalence classes of simple transitive birepresentations of
\widetilde{\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}}

with apex
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{0}
.

On the other hand, again, by Theorem
5.18
, there is a bijection between equivalence classes of simple transitive birepresentations of

\widetilde{\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}}

with apex
\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{0}
and equivalence classes of simple transitive birepresentations of
\widetilde{\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}}_{\ast}
with apex the unique two-sided cell
{\mathcal{H}}_{00}
. However, by Lemma
5.15
, the latter is biequivalent to
\sc\mbox{V}\hskip 1.0ptec_{G}
and its simple transitive birepresentations are in bijection with pairs
(K,\omega)
, where
K
is a subgroup of
G
and
\omega\in H^{2}(K,\Bbbk^{*})
by
[
Os
, Theorem 2]
.
∎

Remark 5.20
.

Observe that Theorem
5.19
implies that for a finite-dimensional radically graded basic Hopf algebra
A
, the associated bicategory of symmetric bimodules
\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
only has finitely many simple transitive birepresentations up to equivalence. By contrast, the bicategory
\sc\mbox{H}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
, which by Theorem
5.8
can be viewed as a
1
-full subbicategory of
\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
, generally has infinitely many non-equivalent simple transitive birepresentations, see e.g.
[
EO
, Theorem 4.10]
. It would be interesting to investigate
1
-full subbicategories of
\sc\mbox{G}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
which contain
\sc\mbox{H}\hskip 1.0pt_{A}
and try to determine where the jump from finitely to infinitely many equivalence classes of simple transitive birepresentations occurs.

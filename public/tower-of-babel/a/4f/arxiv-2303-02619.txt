Generalized Hausdorff metric on S b \displaystyle S_{b} -metric space and some fixed point results
Jayanta Sarkar, Megha Pandey, Tanmoy Som, B.S.Choudhury

Abstract
. In this paper, a metric on S b \displaystyle S_{b} -metric space analogous to the Hausdorff metric has been introduced and some basic properties are obtained on multi-valued S b \displaystyle S_{b} -metric space. Further, the fundamental multi-valued contraction of Nadler(1962) has been extended to the S b \displaystyle S_{b} -metric space setting, and two results have been established. The entire study is supported by suitable examples.

Theorem 3.1
.

(Generalization of Nadler’s fixed point theorem on
\displaystyle S_{b}
-metric space)
.

Assume
\displaystyle(\mathcal{H},S_{b},s)
be a complete metric space and
\displaystyle\mathcal{CB}(\mathcal{H})
be the collection of closed and bounded subsets of
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}
with metric
\displaystyle H_{b}
and let
\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{CB}(\mathcal{H})
be a continuous multi-valued map satisfying the condition,

H_{b}(\mathfrak{T}h,\mathfrak{T}h,\mathfrak{T}g)\leq\alpha S_{b}(h,h,g)~\text{for all}~h,g\in\mathcal{H}~\text{and}~\alpha\in\left[0,\frac{1}{s}\right).

(3.1)

Then,
\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}
has a fixed point in
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}.

Proof:
Consider
\displaystyle h_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
and choose
\displaystyle h_{1}\in\mathfrak{T}h_{0}
. Since
\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}h_{0},~\mathfrak{T}h_{1}\in\mathcal{CB}(\mathcal{H})

2.3

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{b}(\mathfrak{T}h_{0},\mathfrak{T}h_{0},\mathfrak{T}h_{1})+\alpha,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\implies S_{b}(h_{1},h_{1},h_{2})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha S_{b}(h_{0},h_{0},h_{1})+\alpha~\text{by \eqref{4.1}}.

(3.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{In the same way, there exists}~h_{3}\in\mathfrak{T}h_{2},~\text{such that}

\displaystyle\displaystyle S_{b}(h_{2},h_{2},h_{3})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{b}(\mathfrak{T}h_{1},\mathfrak{T}h_{1},\mathfrak{T}h_{2})+\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha S_{b}(h_{1},h_{1},h_{2})+\alpha^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha[\alpha S_{b}(h_{0},h_{0},h_{1})+\alpha]+\alpha^{2}~\text{by \eqref{4.2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\implies S_{b}(h_{2},h_{2},h_{3})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha^{2}S_{b}(h_{0},h_{0},h_{1})+2\alpha^{2}.

Ongoing in this fashion, we get a sequence
\displaystyle\{h_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{H}
such that
\displaystyle h_{i+1}\in\mathfrak{T}h_{i}
and

\displaystyle S_{b}(h_{i},h_{i},h_{i+1})\leq H_{b}(\mathfrak{T}h_{i-1},\mathfrak{T}h_{i-1},\mathfrak{T}h_{i})+\alpha^{i}~\text{for all}~i\geq 1
.

Since

\displaystyle\displaystyle S_{b}(h_{i},h_{i},h_{i+1})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{b}(\mathfrak{T}h_{i-1},\mathfrak{T}h_{i-1},\mathfrak{T}h_{i})+\alpha^{i}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha S_{b}(h_{i-1},h_{i-1},h_{i})+\alpha^{i}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha[H_{b}(\mathfrak{T}h_{i-2},\mathfrak{T}h_{i-2},\mathfrak{T}h_{i-1})+\alpha^{i-1}]+\alpha^{i}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha H_{b}(\mathfrak{T}h_{i-2},\mathfrak{T}h_{i-2},\mathfrak{T}h_{i-1})+2\alpha^{i}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha^{2}S_{b}(h_{i-2},h_{i-2},h_{i-1})+2\alpha^{i}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\implies S_{b}(h_{i},h_{i},h_{i+1})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\alpha^{i}S_{b}(h_{0},h_{0},h_{1})+i\alpha^{i}~\text{for all}~i\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.3)

Therefore, for
\displaystyle m\geq n
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\displaystyle S_{b}(h_{n},h_{n},h_{m})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle s\left[2S_{b}(h_{n},h_{n},h_{n+1})+S_{b}(h_{n+1},h_{n+1},h_{m})\right]~\text{ (by Remark \ref{r1})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2sS_{b}(h_{n},h_{n},h_{n+1})+s\left[s\left[2S_{b}(h_{n+1},h_{n+1},h_{n+2})+S_{b}(h_{n+2}+h_{n+2}+h_{m})\right]\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2sS_{b}(h_{n},h_{n},h_{n+1})+2s^{2}S_{b}(h_{n+1},h_{n+1},h_{n+2})+s^{2}S_{b}(h_{n+2},h_{n+2},h_{m})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2sS_{b}(h_{n},h_{n},h_{n+1})+\cdots+2s^{m-n}S_{b}(h_{m-1},h_{m-1},h_{m})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2\Big[s\left\{\alpha^{n}S_{b}(h_{0},h_{0},h_{1})+n\alpha^{n}\right\}+s^{2}\{\alpha^{n+1}S_{b}(h_{0},h_{0},h_{1})+(n+1)\alpha^{n+1}\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+\cdots+s^{m-n}\left\{\alpha^{m-1}S_{b}(h_{0},h_{0},h_{1})+(m-1)\alpha^{m-1}\right\}\Big]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2\Big[\{s\alpha^{n}+\cdots+s^{m-n}\alpha^{m-1}\}S_{b}(h_{0},h_{0},h_{1})+\{sn\alpha^{n}+s^{2}(n+1)\alpha^{n+1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt+\cdots+s^{m-n}(m-1)\alpha^{m-1}\}\Big]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{2(s\alpha^{n})(1-(s\alpha)^{m-n-1})}{1-s\alpha}S_{b}(h_{0},h_{0},h_{1})+s\alpha^{n}\{n+(n+1)s\alpha

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hskip 156.49014pt+\cdots+(n+(m-n-1))(s\alpha)^{m-n-1}\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{2s\alpha^{n}(1-(s\alpha)^{m-n-1})}{1-s\alpha}S_{b}(h_{0},h_{0},h_{1})+s\alpha^{n}\underset{i=1}{\sum^{m-n-1}}(n+i)(s\alpha)^{i}.

This implies that

S_{b}(h_{n},h_{n},h_{m})\leq\frac{2s\alpha^{n}(1-(s\alpha)^{m-n-1})}{1-s\alpha}S_{b}(h_{0},h_{0},h_{1})+s\alpha^{n}\underset{i=1}{\sum^{m-n-1}}(n+i)(s\alpha)^{i}.

(3.4)

From the inequality (
3.4
), it follows that
\displaystyle\{h_{i}\}
is a Cauchy sequence. As
\displaystyle(\mathcal{H},S_{b},s)
is complete,
\displaystyle\{h_{i}\}
converges to a point
\displaystyle h\in\mathcal{H}
. Therefore, the sequence
\displaystyle\{\mathfrak{T}h_{i}\}
converges to
\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}h
and, since
\displaystyle h_{i}\in\mathfrak{T}h_{i-1}
for all
\displaystyle i
, it follows that
\displaystyle h\in\mathfrak{T}h
. This establishes the theorem.

.

Let

with

for some
\displaystyle s\in\mathbb{R}
with

and

Theorem 3.3
.

Consider
\displaystyle(\mathcal{H},S_{b},s)
be a complete
\displaystyle S_{b}
-metric space. Define a strictly decreasing function
\displaystyle\eta:[0,\frac{1}{s})\rightarrow(\frac{1}{2s+1},\frac{1}{2s}]

\eta(r)=\frac{1}{s(2+r)},

and let
\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{CB}(\mathcal{H})
be a mapping. Assume that there exists some
\displaystyle r\in[0,1)
such that

\eta(r)D_{b}(h,\mathfrak{T}h)\leq S_{b}(h,h,t)~\text{implies}~H_{b}(\mathfrak{T}h,\mathfrak{T}h,\mathfrak{T}t)\leq rS_{b}(h,h,t)~\text{for all}~h,t\in\mathcal{H}.

(3.5)

Then, there exists
\displaystyle z\in\mathcal{H}
such that
\displaystyle z\in\mathfrak{T}z.

Proof :

Take a real number
\displaystyle r_{1}
with
\displaystyle 0\leq r_{1}<r<1
. Then, for each
\displaystyle u=u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
and
\displaystyle u_{1}\in\mathfrak{T}u
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\eta(r)D_{b}(u,\mathfrak{T}u)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\eta(r)S_{b}(u,u,u_{1})\leq S_{b}(u,u,u_{1})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\implies D_{b}(u_{1},\mathfrak{T}u_{1})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{b}(\mathfrak{T}u,\mathfrak{T}u,\mathfrak{T}u_{1})\leq rS_{b}(u,u,u_{1})\leq r_{1}S_{b}(u,u,u_{1})~\text{by \eqref{te4.2}}.

Therefore, there exists
\displaystyle u_{2}\in\mathfrak{T}u_{1}
such that
\displaystyle S_{b}(u_{1},u_{1},u_{2})\leq r_{1}S_{b}(u,u,u_{1})
. Thus, we get a sequence
\displaystyle\{u_{n}\}
such that
\displaystyle u_{n}\in\mathfrak{T}u_{n-1}
and

S_{b}(u_{n-1},u_{n-1},u_{n})\leq r_{1}S_{b}(u_{n-2},u_{n-2},u_{n-1})\leq\cdots\leq r_{1}^{n-1}S_{b}(u,u,u_{1}).

(3.6)

Therefore, for
\displaystyle m\geq n
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle S_{b}(u_{n},u_{n},u_{m})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle s[2S_{b}(u_{n},u_{n},u_{n+1})+S_{b}(u_{n+1},u_{n+1},u_{m})]~\text{( by Remark \ref{r1})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2sS_{b}(u_{n},u_{n},u_{n+1})+s\left[s\left[2S_{b}(u_{n+1},u_{n+1},u_{n+2})+S_{b}(u_{n+2}+u_{n+2}+u_{m})\right]\right]

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2sS_{b}(u_{n},u_{n},u_{n+1})+2s^{2}S_{b}(u_{n+1},u_{n+1},u_{n+2})+s^{2}S_{b}(u_{n+2},u_{n+2},u_{m})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2sS_{b}(u_{n},u_{n},u_{n+1})+\cdots+2s^{m-n}S_{b}(u_{m-1},u_{m-1},u_{m})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2sr_{1}^{n}S_{b}(u,u,u_{1})+2s^{2}r_{1}^{n+1}S_{b}(u,u,u_{1})+\cdots+2s^{m-n}r_{1}^{m-1}S_{b}(u,u,u_{1})~\text{by \eqref{4.5}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2sr_{1}^{n}[1+sr_{1}+(sr_{1})^{2}+\cdots+(sr_{1})^{m-n-1}]S_{b}(u,u,u_{1})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{2sr_{1}^{n}(1-(sr_{1})^{m-n})}{1-sr_{1}}S_{b}(u,u,u_{1}).

This implies

S_{b}(u_{n},u_{n},u_{m})\leq\frac{2sr_{1}^{n}(1-(sr_{1})^{m-n})}{1-sr_{1}}S_{b}(u,u,u_{1}).

(3.7)

From (
3.7
), it follows that
\displaystyle\{u_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence. Since
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}
is a complete
\displaystyle S_{b}
-metric space,
\displaystyle\{u_{n}\}
converges to some point
\displaystyle z\in\mathcal{H}
.

We next show that

D_{b}(z,\mathfrak{T}h)\leq rS_{b}(z,z,h)~\text{for each}~h\in\mathcal{H}/\{z\}.

Since
\displaystyle u_{n}\to z
, there exists
\displaystyle k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

S_{b}(u_{n},u_{n},z)\leq\frac{1}{2s(1+4s)}S_{b}(h,h,z)~\text{for all}~n\in\mathbb{N}~\text{and}~n\geq k.

(3.8)

Then, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\eta(r)D_{b}(u_{n},\mathfrak{T}u_{n})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle D_{b}(u_{n},\mathfrak{T}u_{n})\leq S_{b}(u_{n},u_{n},u_{n+1})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle s[2S_{b}(u_{n},u_{n},z)+S_{b}(u_{n+1},u_{n+1},z)]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2s[S_{b}(u_{n},u_{n},z)+S_{b}(u_{n+1},u_{n+1},z)]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{4s}{2s(1+4s)}S_{b}(h,h,z)~\text{by \eqref{4.7}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2s}S_{b}(h,h,z)-\frac{1}{2s(1+4s)}S_{b}(h,h,z)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2s}S_{b}(h,h,z)-S_{b}(u_{n},u_{n},z)~\text{by \eqref{4.7}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle S_{b}(u_{n},u_{n},h)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\implies\eta(r)D_{b}(u_{n},\mathfrak{T}u_{n})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle S_{b}(u_{n},u_{n},h).

Therefore, by assumption it follows that
\displaystyle H_{b}(\mathfrak{T}u_{n},\mathfrak{T}u_{n},\mathfrak{T}h)\leq rS_{b}(u_{n},u_{n},h)
. This implies that
\displaystyle D_{b}(u_{n+1},\mathfrak{T}h)\leq rS_{b}(u_{n},u_{n},h)
for
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}
with
\displaystyle n\geq k
. Letting
\displaystyle n\to\infty
, we obtain

D_{b}(z,\mathfrak{T}h)\leq rS_{b}(z,z,h)~\text{for every}~h\in\mathcal{H}/\{z\}.

(3.9)

We next prove that

H_{b}(\mathfrak{T}h,\mathfrak{T}h,\mathfrak{T}z)\leq rS_{b}(h,h,z)~\text{for every}~h\in\mathcal{H}.

If
\displaystyle h=z
, then it holds obviously. Therefore, consider
\displaystyle h\neq z
. Then, for every
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\displaystyle y_{n}\in\mathfrak{T}h
such that

S_{b}(z,z,y_{n})\leq D_{b}(z,\mathfrak{T}h)+\frac{1}{ns}S_{b}(h,h,z).

(3.10)

We have

\displaystyle\displaystyle D_{b}(x,\mathfrak{T}h)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle S_{b}(h,h,y_{n})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle s[2S_{b}(h,h,z)+S_{b}(z,z,y_{n})]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2sS_{b}(h,h,z)+sD_{b}(z,\mathfrak{T}h)+\frac{1}{n}S_{b}(h,h,z)~\text{by \eqref{4.9}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2sS_{b}(h,h,z)+srS_{b}(h,h,z)+\frac{1}{n}S_{b}(h,h,z)~\text{by \eqref{4.8}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\implies D_{b}(h,\mathfrak{T}h)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(2s+sr+\frac{1}{n}\right)S_{b}(h,h,z)~\text{for each}~n\in\mathbb{N}

and hence
\displaystyle\frac{1}{s(2+r)}D_{b}(h,\mathfrak{T}h)\leq S_{b}(h,h,z)
. From (
3.5
), we have
\displaystyle H_{b}(\mathfrak{T}h,\mathfrak{T}h,\mathfrak{T}h)\leq rS_{b}(h,h,z).

Since

D_{b}(z,\mathfrak{T}z)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}D_{b}(u_{n+1},\mathfrak{T}z)\leq\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}H_{b}(\mathfrak{T}u_{n},\mathfrak{T}u_{n},\mathfrak{T}z)\leq\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}rS_{b}(u_{n},u_{n},z)=0

and
\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}z\in\mathcal{CB}(\mathcal{H})
, we obtain
\displaystyle z\in\mathfrak{T}z.
This completes the proof.

.

4.
Conclusion

We have generalized the Hausdorff metric for
\displaystyle S_{b}
metric space to obtain a fixed point of a multi-valued operator. In this article, we have used the generalization of the Banach contraction mapping principle into the domain of set-valued setting.
In section
2.2
, we defined the Hausdorff metric in
\displaystyle S_{b}
metric space and gave properties of it. We have proved two fixed point results in section
2.3
. The first one is Theorem
3.1
, a generalization of Nadler’s fixed point theorem. The second one is Theorem
3.3
, which is the generalization of the theorem in article
[
8
]
.

In the future, we may try to generalize the well-known fixed point results for the case of
\displaystyle S_{b}
metric space. Further, one can try to see construct the set of Iterated function systems satisfying the condition given in Theorem
3.1
and work on the fractal generated by these Iterated Function Systems.

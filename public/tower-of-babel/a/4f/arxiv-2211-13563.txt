Abstract
. The main purpose of this paper is to obtain sharp bounds of the norm of Schwarzian derivative for convex mappings of order a ​ l ​ p ​ h ​ a alpha in terms of the value of f ′′ ​ ( 0 ) f^{\prime\prime}(0) , in particular, when this quantity is equal to zero. In addition, we obtain sharp bounds for distortion and growth for this mappings and we generalized the results obtained by Suita [ 9 ] and Yamashita [ 11 ] for this particular case.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the class of conformal functions such that
f({\mathbb{D}})
is a convex region in the complex plane, normalized by the conditions
f(0)=0
and
f^{\prime}(0)=1
, where
{\mathbb{D}}
is the unit disk in
{\mathbb{C}}
. A very important characterization of these functions is that
f\in\mathcal{C}
if and only if

\text{Re}\left\{1+z\,\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\}>0,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Nehari in
[
6
]
shows that if
f\in\mathcal{C}
then

(1)

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert\leq 2,\quad z\in{\mathbb{D}},

where the differential operator
Sf
is known as the Schwarzian derivative of
f
and it is defined as:

Sf(z)=\left(\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right)^{\prime}-\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right)^{2},

considering that
f
is locally univalent in
{\mathbb{D}}
. Inequality (
1
) was obtained by Nehari applying geometric arguments via the Schwarz-Christoffel formula for convex polygons. In the same time, Trimble in
[
10
]
showed that if
f(z)=z+a_{2}z^{2}+a_{3}z^{3}+\cdots
maps
{\mathbb{D}}
into a convex domain, then
|a_{3}-a_{2}^{2}|\leq(1-|a_{2}|^{2})/3
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is a linear invariant family (see Remark
1
) this inequality implies also (
1
). Later, Chuaqui et al. in
[
1
]
proved the same result by applying the Schwarz-Pick lemma and the the fact that the expression
1+z(f^{\prime\prime}/f^{\prime})(z)
is subordinate to the half plane mapping
\ell(z)=(1+z)/(1-z)
, which is

(2)

1+z\,\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)=\ell(w(z))=\dfrac{1+w(z)}{1-w(z)},

for some function
w:{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{D}}
holomorphic and such that
w(0)=0
. The expression defined in (
2
) allowed the authors to obtain other characterizations for convex functions:

(3)

f\in\mathcal{C}\text{ if and only if, }\text{Re}\left\{1+z\,\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\}\geq\dfrac{1}{4}(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\left\lvert\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\rvert^{2},

and

(4)

f\in\mathcal{C}\text{ if and only if, }\left\lvert(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)-2\bar{z}\right\rvert\leq 2,

for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
.

One of the many extensions of the class of convex functions was given by Robertson in
[
8
]
:
f
is a convex function of order
\alpha
, with
0\leq\alpha<1
, if

\text{Re}\left\{1+z\,\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\}>\alpha.

We denote the class of convex function of order
\alpha
as
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
. Observe that
\mathcal{C}_{0}=\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}\subset\mathcal{C}
for all
0\leq\alpha<1
. Robertson
[
8
]
observed that functions
f
in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
, have the following geometric property: the ratio of the angle between adjacent tangents of the unit circle over the
angle between the corresponding tangents in the image of
f
is less than
1/\alpha
. Hence the closer
\alpha
is to 1 the “rounder” is the image. For instance, segments in the boundary of
f({\mathbb{D}})
are prohibited as soon as
\alpha>0
. An analogous bound associated to (
1
) was obtained by Suita in
[
9
]
for this class. We present this result as Theorem A in section
3
.The author obtains his result characterizing the expression
1+z(f^{\prime\prime}/f^{\prime})(z)
in terms of their Herglotz representation associated to the Poisson Kernel (see
[
3
, p. 15]
). In
[
4
, p. 54]
there is a review of the most important properties of the class
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
.

In this work, we apply some of the ideas presented in
[
1
]
, in particular the Schwarz Lemma and its generalizations, to obtain analogous inequalities associated to (
3
) and (
4
) for the convex of order
\alpha
case, and a sharp bound for the norm of the Schwarzian derivative when
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
with the additional condition that
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
. In the same path, Kanas and Sugawa in
[
5
]
proved similar results, but for the case of strongly convex mappings, which can not be improved with our additional assumption since the extremal mapping satisfies that
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
. Yamashita in
[
11
]
showed that the norm of the pre-Schwarzian derivative for convex mappings with order alpha is less than or to
4(1-\alpha)
, which is improved in the Proposition
3
assuming that
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
. In fact, we shall show that
(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\,\lvert f^{\prime\prime}(z)/f^{\prime}(z)\rvert\leq 2(1-\alpha)
. Also, for restricted values of
\lvert f^{\prime\prime}(0)\rvert\neq 0
, we obtain a better bound than the one presented by Suita for
\lVert Sf\rVert
in the general case.

2.
Bounds on convex mappings with order
\alpha
.

The Möbius transformation:

\ell_{\alpha}(z)=\dfrac{1+(1-2\alpha)z}{1-z},

sends
{\mathbb{D}}
to the half plane
H_{\alpha}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:\text{Re}(z)>\alpha\}
. If
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
then there exists
w:{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{D}}
such that:

1+z\,\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)=\ell_{\alpha}(w(z))=\dfrac{1+(1-2\alpha)w(z)}{1-w(z)},

with
w(0)=0
, analogously as in (
2
). We can assume that
w(z)=z\varphi(z)
for some holomorphic mapping
\varphi
that satisfies
\varphi({\mathbb{D}})\subseteq{\mathbb{D}}
, from where:

(5)

\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)=2(1-\alpha)\dfrac{\varphi(z)}{1-z\varphi(z)},

The next theorem generalizes the characterizations (
3
) and (
4
) to convex functions of order
\alpha
.

Theorem 1
.

If
0\leq\alpha<1
, then the following are equivalent:

i.

f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
.

ii.

\text{Re}\left\{1+z\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\}\geq\alpha+\dfrac{1}{4(1-\alpha)}(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\left\lvert\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\rvert^{2}
.

iii.

\left\lvert(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)-2(1-\alpha)\bar{z}\right\rvert\leq 2(1-\alpha)
.

Proof.

We prove that
i.
is equivalent to
ii.
and
ii.
is equivalent to
iii.
. From (
5
) we obtain that:

(6)

\varphi(z)=\dfrac{(f^{\prime\prime}/f^{\prime})(z)}{2(1-\alpha)+z(f^{\prime\prime}/f^{\prime})(z)}.

From the fact that
\lvert\varphi(z)\rvert^{2}\leq 1
we have:

4(1-\alpha)^{2}+2\,\text{Re}\left\{2(1-\alpha)z\,\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\}+\lvert z\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\rvert^{2}\geq\left\lvert\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\rvert^{2}.

Factorizing we obtain,

(7)

4(1-\alpha)\left[1-\alpha+\text{Re}\left\{z\,\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\}\right]\geq(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\left\lvert\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\rvert^{2}.

Since
\alpha\neq 1
we conclude that

\text{Re}\left\{1+z\,\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\}\geq\alpha+\dfrac{1}{4(1-\alpha)}(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\left\lvert\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\rvert^{2}.

If we multiply both sides of inequality (
7
) by
(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})
, we obtain:

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\left\lvert\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\rvert^{2}\leq 4(1-\alpha)^{2}(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})+4(1-\alpha)(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\text{Re}\left\{\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\}.

This is equivalent to:

\left((1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\left\lvert\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\rvert\right)^{2}-2\,\text{Re}\left\{2(1-\alpha)z(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\}+(2(1-\alpha)\lvert z\rvert)^{2}\leq 4(1-\alpha)^{2},

from which we have that:

\left\lvert(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)-2(1-\alpha)\bar{z}\right\rvert\leq 2(1-\alpha).

∎

It is a well-known fact that if
f\in\mathcal{C}
, then for any
a\in{\mathbb{D}}
the corresponding
Koebe Transform

f_{a}
given by

(8)

f_{a}(z)=\dfrac{f\left(\dfrac{z+a}{1+\bar{a}z}\right)-f(a)}{(1-\lvert a\rvert^{2})f^{\prime}(a)},

belongs to
\mathcal{C}
. However, if
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
then it is not necessarily that
f_{a}\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
. For this case we have the following proposition:

Proposition 2
.

If
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
then for any
a\in{\mathbb{D}}
,
f_{a}\in\mathcal{C}_{\beta}
with
\beta=\alpha\left(\dfrac{1-\lvert a\rvert}{1+\lvert a\rvert}\right)
.

Proof.

By equation (
8
) we have

\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}_{a}}{f^{\prime}_{a}}(z)=\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\left(\dfrac{z+a}{1+\bar{a}z}\right)\,\dfrac{1-\lvert a\rvert^{2}}{(1+\bar{a}z)^{2}}-\dfrac{2\bar{a}}{1+\bar{a}z}.

If we assume that
\lvert z\rvert=1
(because of the maximum modulus principle) we have that:

\displaystyle 1+z\,\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}_{a}}{f^{\prime}_{a}}(z)

\displaystyle=1+\left(\dfrac{z+a}{1+\bar{a}z}\right)\,\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\left(\dfrac{z+a}{1+\bar{a}z}\right)\,\dfrac{z(1-\lvert a\rvert^{2})(1+\bar{a}z)}{(1+\bar{a}z)^{2}(z+a)}-\dfrac{2\bar{a}z}{1+\bar{a}z}

\displaystyle=1+\left(\dfrac{z+a}{1+\bar{a}z}\right)\,\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}\left(\dfrac{z+a}{1+\bar{a}z}\right)\,\dfrac{1-\lvert a\rvert^{2}}{\lvert 1+\bar{a}z\rvert^{2}}-\dfrac{2\bar{a}z}{1+\bar{a}z}.

Let
\zeta=\left(\dfrac{z+a}{1+\bar{a}z}\right)\in{\mathbb{D}}
, from which
\text{Re}\left\{\zeta\,\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(\zeta)\right\}>\alpha-1
.
Then, for
\lvert z\rvert=1
we have that

\displaystyle\text{Re}\left\{1+z\,\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}_{a}}{f^{\prime}_{a}}(z)\right\}

\displaystyle=1+\left(\dfrac{1-\lvert a\rvert^{2}}{\lvert 1+\bar{a}z\rvert^{2}}\right)\text{Re}\left\{\zeta\,\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(\zeta)\right\}-2\,\text{Re}\left\{\dfrac{\bar{a}z}{1+\bar{a}z}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq 1+\left(\dfrac{1-\lvert a\rvert^{2}}{\lvert 1+\bar{a}z\rvert^{2}}\right)(\alpha-1)-2\,\text{Re}\left\{\dfrac{\bar{a}z}{1+\bar{a}z}\right\}

\displaystyle=1+\alpha\left(\dfrac{1-\lvert a\rvert^{2}}{\lvert 1+\bar{a}z\rvert^{2}}\right)-\left(\dfrac{1-\lvert a\rvert^{2}+2\,\text{Re}\{\bar{a}z(1+a\bar{z}\}}{\lvert 1+\bar{a}z\rvert^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=1+\alpha\left(\dfrac{1-\lvert a\rvert^{2}}{\lvert 1+\bar{a}z\rvert^{2}}\right)-\left(\dfrac{1+2\,\text{Re}\{\bar{a}z\}+\lvert a\rvert^{2}}{\lvert 1+\bar{a}z\rvert^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha\left(\dfrac{1-\lvert a\rvert^{2}}{\lvert 1+\bar{a}z\rvert^{2}}\right)\geq\alpha\left(\dfrac{1-\lvert a\rvert^{2}}{(1+\lvert a\rvert)^{2}}\right)=\alpha\left(\dfrac{1-\lvert a\rvert}{1+\lvert a\rvert}\right).

From the above we can conclude that if
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
then
f_{a}\in\mathcal{C}_{\beta}
.
∎

Now, with the additional condition that
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
, equation (
5
) shows that
\varphi
satisfies that
\varphi(z)=z\psi(z)
, for some holomorphic mapping
\psi
with
\lvert\psi\rvert<1
. We say that
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{0}
if
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
and
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
. Thus, a straightforward calculation shows that:

Proposition 3
.

If
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{0}
then

\left(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2}\right)\left\lvert\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\rvert\leq 2\lvert z\rvert(1-\alpha),\ \forall z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

Proof.

Since
\varphi(z)=z\psi(z)
, with
\lvert\psi(z)\rvert<1
, then in equation (
5
) we have:

\left\lvert\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\rvert\leq 2(1-\alpha)\dfrac{\lvert z\rvert\,\lvert\psi(z)\rvert}{1-\lvert z\rvert^{2}\lvert\psi(z)\rvert}\leq 2(1-\alpha)\dfrac{\lvert z\rvert}{1-\lvert z\rvert^{2}}.

∎

Robertson in
[
8
]
shows that if
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
then

\dfrac{1}{(1+\lvert z\rvert)^{2(1-\alpha)}}\leq\lvert f^{\prime}(z)\rvert\leq\dfrac{1}{(1-\lvert z\rvert)^{2(1-\alpha)}},

and, if
\alpha\neq 1/2

\dfrac{(1+\lvert z\rvert)^{2\alpha-1}-1}{2\alpha-1}\leq\lvert f(z)\rvert\leq\dfrac{1-(1-\lvert z\rvert)^{2\alpha-1}}{2\alpha-1},

for
\alpha=1/2
,

\log(1+\lvert z\rvert)\leq\lvert f(z)\rvert\leq-\log(1-\lvert z\rvert)\,.

With the additional condition
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
, we improve these results which are summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

If
f\in\mathcal{C}^{0}_{\alpha}
then for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, we have that

\dfrac{1}{(1+\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{1-\alpha}}\leq\lvert f^{\prime}(z)\rvert\leq\dfrac{1}{(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{1-\alpha}},

and

\int_{0}^{\lvert z\rvert}\dfrac{1}{(1+\zeta^{2})^{1-\alpha}}\,d\zeta\leq\lvert f(z)\rvert\leq\int_{0}^{\lvert z\rvert}\dfrac{1}{(1-\zeta^{2})^{1-\alpha}}\,d\zeta.

We will see in Example
1
, that this bounds are sharp within
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{0}
. In particular, when
\alpha=0
, which corresponds to the subclass
\mathcal{C}
with
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
denoted by
\mathcal{C}^{0}
, we have that

\tan^{-1}(\lvert z\rvert)\leq\lvert f(z)\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{1+\lvert z\rvert}{1-\lvert z\rvert}\right).

Clearly, these bounds are better than the well-known bounds for the entire convex class
\mathcal{C}
(see
[
4
, p. 43]
). We will see in Theorem 5 that
\lVert Sf\rVert\leq 2(1-\alpha^{2})
for any
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{0}
, therefore, a comparison is in order between these bounds and those obtained by Chuaqui and Osgood in
[
2
]
. These authors proved certain sharp bounds for distortion and growth for mappings with the condition that
(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf\rvert\leq 2t
when
t\in[0,1]
. They showed that if
f
is holomorphic in
{\mathbb{D}}
such that
f(0)=0
,
f^{\prime}(0)=1
and
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
, then:

\displaystyle A(\lvert z\rvert,-t)

\displaystyle\leq\lvert f(z)\rvert\leq A(\lvert z\rvert,t),

\displaystyle A^{\prime}(\lvert z\rvert,-t)

\displaystyle\leq\lvert f^{\prime}(z)\rvert\leq A^{\prime}(\lvert z\rvert,t),

where

A(z,t)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-t}}\dfrac{(1+z)^{\sqrt{1-t}}-(1-z)^{\sqrt{1-t}}}{(1+z)^{\sqrt{1-t}}+(1-z)^{\sqrt{1-t}}}.

Our case (
\alpha=0
) corresponds to their case when
t=1
, where the upper bound for growth coincides with our upper bound because the mapping for which equality holds belongs to both classes. However, for the lower bound the situation is completely different since their lower bound is less than or equal to our lower bound. Moreover, when
\alpha=1/2
, the growth inequality in Theorem
4
is
\sinh^{-1}(\lvert z\rvert)\leq\lvert f(z)\rvert\leq\sin^{-1}(\lvert z\rvert),
but the corresponding parameter is
t=3/4
, hence our result is more precise than the one obtained by Chuaqui and Osgood in
[
2
]
, which is not surprising since in their case the family is larger than
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{0}
.

Proof of Theorem 4.

From equation (
6
) and considering that
\varphi(0)=0
(because
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
), then by the Schwarz Lemma we have that

\left\lvert\dfrac{(f^{\prime\prime}/f^{\prime})(z)}{2(1-\alpha)+z(f^{\prime\prime}/f^{\prime})(z)}\right\rvert^{2}\leq\lvert z\rvert^{2},

which is equivalent to

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{4})\left\rvert\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\rvert^{2}\leq 4\lvert z\rvert^{2}(1-\alpha)^{2}+4\lvert z\rvert^{2}(1-\alpha)\text{Re}\left\{z\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\}.

If we first multiply by
(1-\lvert z\rvert^{4})
and then we add
4(\lvert z\rvert^{2}(1-\alpha)\lvert\bar{z}\rvert)^{2}
on both sides, we obtain

\begin{split}(1&-\lvert z\rvert^{4})^{2}\left\lvert\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\rvert^{2}-4\lvert z\rvert^{2}(1-\lvert z\rvert^{4})(1-\alpha)\text{Re}\left\{z\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\}+4(\lvert z\rvert^{2}(1-\alpha)\lvert\bar{z}\rvert)^{2}\\
\leq&{}4(1-\lvert z\rvert^{4})\lvert z\rvert^{2}(1-\alpha)^{2}+4\lvert z\rvert^{4}(1-\alpha)^{2}\lvert z\rvert^{2}=4\lvert z\rvert^{2}(1-\alpha)^{2}.\end{split}

Then multiplying on both sides by
\lvert z\rvert
we have

\left\lvert(1-\lvert z\rvert^{4})z\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)-2\lvert z\rvert^{4}(1-\alpha)\right\rvert\leq 2\lvert z\rvert^{2}(1-\alpha).

This implies

\dfrac{-2\lvert z\rvert^{2}(1-\alpha)+2\lvert z\rvert^{4}(1-\alpha)}{1+\lvert z\rvert^{4}}\leq\text{Re}\left\{z\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\}\leq\dfrac{2\lvert z\rvert^{2}(1-\alpha)+2\lvert z\rvert^{4}(1-\alpha)}{1-\lvert z\rvert^{4}}.

Which it is equivalent to

\dfrac{-2(1-\alpha)\lvert z\rvert^{2}}{1+\lvert z\rvert^{2}}\leq\text{Re}\left\{z\,\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\}\leq\dfrac{2(1-\alpha)\lvert z\rvert^{2}}{1-\lvert z\rvert^{2}}.

Let
z=re^{i\theta}
, then we have

\dfrac{-2(1-\alpha)r}{1+r^{2}}\leq\dfrac{\partial}{\partial r}(\log\lvert f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})\rvert)\leq\dfrac{2(1-\alpha)r}{1-r^{2}}.

If we integrate respect to
r
, we obtain

\log(1+r^{2})^{-(1-\alpha)}\leq\log\lvert f^{\prime}(re^{i\theta})\rvert\leq\log(1-r^{2})^{-(1-\alpha)}

Exponentiating we can conclude that

\dfrac{1}{(1+\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{1-\alpha}}\leq\lvert f^{\prime}(z)\rvert\leq\dfrac{1}{(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{1-\alpha}}.

Now, for the growth part of the theorem, from the upper bound it follows that

\lvert f(re^{i\theta})\rvert=\left\lvert\int_{0}^{r}f^{\prime}(te^{i\theta})e^{i\theta}\,dt\right\rvert\leq\int_{0}^{r}\lvert f^{\prime}(te^{i\theta})\rvert\,dt\leq\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{1}{(1-t^{2})^{1-\alpha}}\,dt.\\

Which implies that

\lvert f(z)\rvert\leq\int_{0}^{\lvert z\rvert}\dfrac{1}{(1-\zeta^{2})^{1-\alpha}}d\zeta,

for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
. It is well known that if
f(z_{0})
is a point of minimum modulus on the image of the circle
\lvert z\rvert=r
and
\gamma=f^{-1}(\Gamma)
, where
\Gamma
is the line segment from
0
to
f(z_{0})
, then

\lvert f(z)\rvert\geq\lvert f(z_{0})\rvert=\int_{\Gamma}\lvert dw\rvert=\int_{\gamma}\lvert f^{\prime}(\zeta)\rvert\,\lvert d\zeta\rvert\geq\int_{0}^{r}\dfrac{1}{(1+\lvert\zeta\rvert^{2})^{1-\alpha}}\,d\lvert\zeta\rvert.

Thus, the proof is completed.
∎

3.
On the norm of the Schwarzian derivative when
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
.

This section is devoted to finding the sharp bound of the norm of the Schwarzian Derivative in terms of the parameter
\alpha
under the assumption that
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
. We define the
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{0}
class as those
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
such that
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
. Firstly, form equation (
5
) it follows that

(9)

Sf(z)=2(1-\alpha)\left(\dfrac{\varphi^{\prime}(z)+\alpha\,\varphi^{2}(z)}{(1-z\varphi(z))^{2}}\right).

Suita in
[
9
]
proved the sharp bounds of
\lVert Sf\rVert
for the hole class
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
. We present this results as the following Theorem:

Theorem A.

If
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
then for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
we have that if
0\leq\alpha\leq 1/2
:

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert\leq 2,

and for
1/2<\alpha<1
:

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert\leq 8\alpha(1-\alpha).

The next theorem gives us a sharp bound for the expression
(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert
when
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{0}
by a direct application of the Schwarz Lemma.

Theorem 5
.

If
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}^{0}
then

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert\leq 2(1-\alpha^{2}),\ \forall z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

The inequality is sharp.

Proof.

From (
9
) we obtain, by applying the triangle inequality and the Schwarz-Pick lemma, that:

(10)

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert\leq 2(1-\alpha)\dfrac{(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}}{\lvert 1-z\varphi(z)\rvert^{2}}\left[\dfrac{1-\lvert\varphi(z)\rvert^{2}}{1-\lvert z\rvert^{2}}+\alpha\lvert\varphi(z)\rvert^{2}\right].

We define the function
\Phi:{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
such that:

\Phi(z)=\dfrac{\bar{z}-\varphi(z)}{1-z\varphi(z)}.

Since
\varphi({\mathbb{D}})\subset{\mathbb{D}}
, then
(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})(1-\lvert\varphi(z)\rvert^{2})>0
, which implies that

\lvert\bar{z}-\varphi(z)\rvert^{2}<\lvert 1-z\varphi(z)\rvert^{2},

so, we can conclude that
\lvert\Phi(z)\rvert^{2}<1
. Therefore,

1-\lvert\Phi(z)\rvert^{2}=\dfrac{(1-\lvert\varphi(z)\rvert^{2})(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})}{\lvert 1-z\varphi(z)\rvert^{2}},

and,

\dfrac{(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}}{\lvert 1-z\varphi(z)\rvert^{2}}=\dfrac{(1-\lvert\Phi(z)\rvert^{2})(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})}{(1-\lvert\varphi(z)\rvert^{2})}.

If we replace the latter expression in (
10
) we have:

(11)

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert\leq 2(1-\alpha)(1-\lvert\Phi(z)\rvert^{2})\left[1+\alpha\left(\dfrac{\lvert\varphi(z)\rvert^{2}(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})}{1-\lvert\varphi(z)\rvert^{2}}\right)\right].

From the normalization and using the equation (
5
) we conclude that
\varphi(0)=0
. Then, by Schwarz lemma, we obtain that
\lvert\varphi(z)\rvert\leq\lvert z\rvert
for all
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
and from this we can conclude that:

\dfrac{\lvert\varphi(z)\rvert^{2}}{1-\lvert\varphi(z)\rvert^{2}}\leq\dfrac{\lvert z\rvert^{2}}{1-\lvert z\rvert^{2}}.

Applying the above in (
10
) we obtain:

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert\leq 2(1-\alpha)(1-\lvert\Phi(z)\rvert^{2})(1+\alpha\lvert z\rvert^{2}).

We know that
1-\lvert\Phi(z)\rvert^{2}\leq 1
, then:

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert\leq 2(1-\alpha)(1+\alpha)=2(1-\alpha^{2}).

The sharpness follows from the next example.
∎

Example 1
.

The family of parameterized functions defined as:

(12)

f_{\alpha}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\dfrac{1}{(1-\zeta^{2})^{1-\alpha}}\,d\zeta,\ z\in{\mathbb{D}},

maximizes the Schwarzian norm defined as:

\lVert Sf\rVert=\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{D}}}(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert,

and from this, the sharpness of the inequality given in Theorem
5
holds for any
0\leq\alpha<1
. Notice that:

\dfrac{f_{\alpha}^{\prime\prime}}{f_{\alpha}^{\prime}}(z)=2(1-\alpha)\dfrac{z}{1-z^{2}},\quad\mbox{and}\quad Sf_{\alpha}(z)=2(1-\alpha)\cdot\dfrac{1+\alpha z^{2}}{(1-z^{2})^{2}}.

from which,

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf_{\alpha}(z)\rvert=2(1-\alpha)\dfrac{(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert 1+\alpha z^{2}\rvert}{\lvert 1-z^{2}\rvert^{2}}\leq 2(1-\alpha)\,\lvert 1+\alpha z^{2}\rvert.

We can conclude that:

\lVert Sf_{\alpha}\rVert=\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{D}}}(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf_{\alpha}(z)\rvert=2(1-\alpha)(1+\alpha)=2(1-\alpha^{2}).

In general, the integral formula for
f_{\alpha}
given in (
12
) does not give primitives in terms of elementary functions, however, when
\alpha=0
we obtain:

f_{0}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\dfrac{1}{1-\zeta^{2}}\,d\zeta=\dfrac{1}{2}\log\left(\dfrac{1+z}{1-z}\right),

where
\lVert Sf_{0}\rVert=2
. Also, for
\alpha=1/2
we have that:

f_{1/2}(z)=\int_{0}^{z}\dfrac{1}{(1-\zeta^{2})^{1/2}}\,d\zeta=\sin^{-1}(z),

where
\lVert Sf_{1/2}\rVert=3/2
.

Remark 1
.

In
[
1
]
is presented the next inequality for
f\in\mathcal{C}
:

(13)

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert+2\left\lvert\dfrac{\varphi(z)-\bar{z}}{1-z\varphi(z)}\right\rvert^{2}\leq 2,

which is equivalent to:

(14)

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert+2\left\rvert\bar{z}-\dfrac{1}{2}(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\lvert^{2}\leq 2.

However, for the class
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
we can obtain from (
11
) an analogous inequality associated to (
13
) and (
14
). In fact, we have that

(15)

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert\leq 2(1-\alpha)\left[(1+\alpha)-\left\lvert\dfrac{\varphi(z)-\bar{z}}{1-z\varphi(z)}\right\rvert^{2}-\alpha\,A\right],

where, using the equation (
6
),

(16)

A=1-\left(\dfrac{(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})\lvert\varphi(z)\rvert}{\lvert 1-z\varphi(z)\rvert}\right)^{2}=1-\left(\dfrac{1-\lvert z\rvert^{2}}{2(1-\alpha)}\left\lvert\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\rvert\right)^{2}.

Now, from (
15
) we have that:

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert+2(1-\alpha)\left\lvert\dfrac{\varphi(z)-\bar{z}}{1-z\varphi(z)}\right\rvert^{2}+2(1-\alpha)\alpha\,A\leq 2(1-\alpha^{2}),

which is equivalent to

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert+2(1-\alpha)\left\lvert\bar{z}-\dfrac{1-\lvert z\rvert^{2}}{2(1-\alpha)}\dfrac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f^{\prime}}(z)\right\rvert^{2}+2(1-\alpha)\alpha\,A\leq 2(1-\alpha^{2}).

Thus, when
A\geq 0
we have that
\lVert Sf\rVert\leq 2(1-\alpha^{2})
, which occurs when
\varphi(0)=0
, because of
f^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
. In this case, using the Schwarz Lemma we can show that
A\geq 1-\lvert\varphi(z)\rvert
. Therefore we can assert the following results that improve Theorem 1 in
[
11
]
.

The next theorem gives us a bound for the expression
(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert
for
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
in terms of value
\lvert f^{\prime\prime}(0)\rvert
, which is not necessarily zero. To do this, we need the next lemma (its proof can be found in
[
7
, p. 167]
) which gives us a generalization of the Schwarz Lemma for bounded holomorphic functions:

Lemma 6
.

If
\varphi:{\mathbb{D}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{D}}
be a holomorphic function then

\lvert\varphi(z)\rvert\leq\dfrac{\lvert\varphi(0)\rvert+\lvert z\rvert}{1+\lvert\varphi(0)\rvert\,\lvert z\rvert}.

Theorem 7
.

Let
0\leq\alpha<1
,
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
and
p=\dfrac{\lvert f^{\prime\prime}(0)\rvert}{2(1-\alpha)}
. Then:

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert\leq 2(1-\alpha)\left(1+\alpha\,\dfrac{1+p}{1-p}\right).

Proof.

Let
p=\lvert\varphi(0)\rvert
. Applying Lemma
6
, we have that:

\dfrac{\lvert\varphi(z)\rvert^{2}}{1-\lvert\varphi(z)\rvert^{2}}\leq\dfrac{(p+\lvert z\rvert)^{2}}{(1+p\lvert z\rvert)^{2}-(p+\lvert z\rvert)^{2}}=\dfrac{(p+\lvert z\rvert)^{2}}{(1-p^{2})(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})}.

If we replace the above expression in (
11
) at the proof of Theorem
5
we obtain that:

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert\leq 2(1-\alpha)(1-\lvert\Phi(z)\rvert^{2})\left(1+\alpha\,\dfrac{(p+\lvert z\rvert)^{2}}{1-p^{2}}\right).

From the fact that
\lvert z\rvert<\leq 1
and
1-\lvert\Phi(z)\rvert^{2}<1
we can conclude that:

(1-\lvert z\rvert^{2})^{2}\,\lvert Sf(z)\rvert\leq 2(1-\alpha)\left(1+\alpha\,\dfrac{(p+1)^{2}}{1-p^{2}}\right)=2(1-\alpha)\left(1+\alpha\,\dfrac{1+p}{1-p}\right).

∎

Remark 2
.

Although the bound obtained is not sharp in general, for the
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
class, we have that in certain cases the bound in Theorem
7
is better than the one given in Theorem A, (see
[
9
]
). In fact, for
0\leq\alpha\leq 1/2
and
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
such that
p\leq\alpha(2-\alpha)^{-1}
then
(1+p)(1-p)^{-1}\leq(1-\alpha)^{-1}
, which implies

2(1-\alpha)\left(1+\alpha\,\dfrac{1+p}{1-p}\right)\leq 2.

On the other side, if
1/2<\alpha<1
and
f
is such that
p\leq(3\alpha-1)(5\alpha-1)^{-1}
then
(1+p)(1-p)^{-1}\leq(8\alpha-2)(2\alpha)^{-1}
, from which

2(1-\alpha)\left(1+\alpha\,\dfrac{1+p}{1-p}\right)\leq 8\alpha(1-\alpha).

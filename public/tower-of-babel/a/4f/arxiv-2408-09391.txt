On Conflict-Free Colorings of Cyclic Polytopes and the Girth Conjecture for Graphs
Seunghun Lee, Shakhar Smorodinsky

Abstract
We study the conflict-free chromatic number of hypergraphs derived from the family of facets of d d -dimensional cyclic polytopes with n n vertices. While in odd dimensions d d the problem is easy, for even dimensions the problem becomes very difficult and exhibits interesting connections to extremal graph theory. We provide sharp asymptotic bounds for the conflict-free chromatic number in several small even dimensions and non-trivial upper and lower bounds for general even dimensions. The main purpose of this paper is revealing a surprising relation between conflict-free colorings and the celebrated Erdős girth conjecture, opening new avenues for future research.



1
Introduction

1.1
Conflict-free coloring on the moment curve

Given a hypergraph
H=(V,\mathcal{E})
, a vertex coloring is called
conflict-free
(or
CF-coloring
) if, for every hyperedge
e\in\mathcal{E}
, there exists at least one vertex
v\in e
whose color is unique within
e
. The minimum number of colors needed for a CF-coloring of
H
is called the
conflict-free chromatic number
(or
CF-chromatic number
) and is denoted by
\chi_{\mathrm{cf}}(H)
. In particular, it is straightforward that
\chi_{\mathrm{cf}}(H)\geq\chi(H)
, where
\chi(H)
is the proper (or usual) chromatic number of
H
, requiring every hyperedge of size at least two to contain at least two differently colored vertices.

Conflict-free colorings have been extensively studied in both combinatorics and computational geometry. Their initial motivation came from frequency assignment problems in cellular networks
[
17
,
43
]
. Since then, the concept has been generalized to various types of hypergraphs, both abstract and geometrically defined, leading to a vibrant research area with numerous follow-up publications. The interest in conflict-free coloring has also spread to other disciplines, including logic, graph theory, theoretical computer science, and algorithms (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
3
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
18
,
21
,
22
,
25
,
27
,
28
,
40
]
). For a comprehensive overview, see the survey
[
42
]
and references therein.

A substantial amount of research in discrete geometry is devoted to providing sharp bounds on the CF-chromatic numbers of hypergraphs induced by geometric shapes: For a family
\cal C
of shapes in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we say that a hypergraph
H=(V,\mathcal{E})

is induced by

\cal C
if
V
is a finite set of points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathcal{E}=\{V\cap C|C\in\cal C\}
. The most studied cases are hypergraphs on points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
induced by “well-behaved”
shapes such as balls, half-spaces, axis parallel-boxes or more generally, semi-algebraic sets defined by “low-degree” polynomials.

In this paper, we are interested in hypergraphs induced by points in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with respect to half-spaces. For such a hypergraph
H
on
n
points in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
or
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, it is well known that
\CF(H)=O(\log n)
and this bound is asymptotically sharp
[
17
,
22
,
41
]
. However, the situation becomes completely different in dimensions at least
4
; in fact, it can easily seen that the chromatic number can be at least
n
, the number of points, which is also a trivial upper bound.
Such lower bounds are given e.g., by points on the
moment curve

\gamma_{d}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, defined by
\gamma_{d}(t)=(t,t^{2},\dots,t^{d})
. Before we discuss this in detail, let us first briefly cover some known facts about the curve, which are useful for further discussion. The moment curve has the following combinatorial properties. Every set of at most
d+1
distinct points on
\gamma_{d}
are affinely independent and any point set on
\gamma_{d}
is in convex position. Many properties of points on the curve depend only on the order of the points along
\gamma_{d}
. So we will frequently abuse our notations and identify an
n
-point set on the moment curve with
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. We also refer to the convex hull of a set of
n
points on
\gamma_{d}
as “the”
cyclic polytope

\mathsf{C}_{d}(n)
, and identify a face of
\mathsf{C}_{d}(n)
with its vertex set. Due to this combinatorial description, the moment curve has been of interest in combinatorics and discrete geometry.

It is well-known that
\mathsf{C}_{d}(n)
is a
neighborly polytope
; namely, any vertex subset
S\subseteq[n]
with
|S|\leq d/2
forms a face of
\mathsf{C}_{d}(n)
. By the celebrated upper bound theorem of McMullen
[
37
]
, the cyclic polytope
\mathsf{C}_{d}(n)
as well as all
d
-dimensional (simplicial) neighborly polytopes on
n
vertices have the largest number of faces among all
d
-polytopes on
n
vertices. For more backgrounds on the moment curve and neighborly polytopes, refer to
[
44
]
.

Taking our attention back to CF-colorings, let
\mathsf{H}^{\gamma_{d}}_{n}
be the hypergraph on
n
points on
\gamma_{d}
induced by half-spaces. Note that for
d\geq 4
,
\mathsf{C}_{d}(n)
contains the complete graph
K_{n}
, namely, all pairs of vertices form a hyperedge of cardianlity
2
since
\mathsf{C}_{d}(n)
is neighborly. This (trivially) implies that
\CF(\mathsf{H}^{\gamma_{d}}_{n})=n
for
d\geq 4
in contrast to dimensions
d\in\{1,2,3\}
.

Note that a similar situation happens even for a sub-hypergraph
H^{\prime}
of
\mathsf{H}^{\gamma_{d}}_{n}
which contains all hyperedges of
\mathsf{H}^{\gamma_{d}}_{n}
of a certain size
k\leq d/2
. Then
H^{\prime}
contains the complete
k
-uniform hypergraph on
n
vertices. Indeed, by the pigeon-hole principle it is an easy exercise to show that the CF-chromatic number of the complete
k
-uniform hypergraph is
\Omega(n/k)
.
It is therefore, natural to ask the following general question where we forbid hyperedges of small size in a sub-hypergraph:

Question 1.1
.

For
d\geq 4
, what is
the CF-chromatic number of a sub-hypergraph of
\mathsf{H}^{\gamma_{d}}_{n}
without any hyperedges of size at most
d/2
?

The main difficulty of Question
1.1
arises from the fact that the underlying hypergraph family is not hereditary.
A family
\cal H
of hypergraphs is
hereditary
if they are closed under projection; or formally if
H=(V,\mathcal{E})
being in the family
\cal H
implies that for every
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
,
H[V^{\prime}]
also belongs to the family
\cal H
, where
H[V^{\prime}]=(V^{\prime},\{e\cap V^{\prime}|e\in\mathcal{E}\})
.
Many hypergraph families that were studied in the literature with respect to CF-colorings are hereditary. For example, in
[
41
]
it was shown that if
H=(V,\mathcal{E})
with
|V|=n
satisfies that for every
V^{\prime}\subset V
,
\chi(H[V^{\prime}])\leq k
then
\CF(H)=O(k\log n)
. Without the hereditary condition one can construct a hypergraph
H
on
n
vertices with
\chi(H)=2
while
\CF(H)=\Omega(n)
,
for example,

H^{\prime}=([2m],\{e\subset[2m]:|e|=4,\,e\cap[m]\neq\emptyset,e\cap\{m+1,m+2,\dots,2m\}\neq\emptyset\})

has
\CF(H^{\prime})=m+1
while clearly
\chi(H^{\prime})=2
for
m\geq 3
.

None of the hypergraphs studied in this paper exhibits such a hereditary property and therefore we cannot use any of the standard tools developed in the literature for hereditary hypergraphs. Hence we need a different approach.

In this paper, we give a partial answer to Question
1.1
for the
facet hypergraph
of a cyclic polytope
\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
and the
2-interval hypergraph

\mathsf{I}_{n}^{2}
, see precise definitions below.
Our techniques combine combinatorial arguments with tools from extremal graph theory and combinatorial properties of the moment curve. In particular for cyclic polytopes,
we exhibit a strong relation between
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))
and the following famous Turán type problem in extremal graph theory known as the
girth conjecture

[
16
]
:
What is the maximum possible number of edges that a graph with girth at least
2k+1
can have?
For general background on such extremal problems, we refer the reader to the survey
[
19
]
.

This relation might be of independent interest and we believe that it can shed light on CF-coloring problems for other classes of geometric hypergraphs. For example, the so-called family of
(AB)^{l/2}
-free hypergraphs
was recently introduced in
[
29
]
as an abstract approach for the study of coloring problems on geometric hypergraphs. See also
[
2
,
30
,
14
]
. As we observe in Section
4
, the sub-hypergraphs of
\mathsf{H}^{\gamma_{d}}_{n}
are
(AB)^{(d+2)/2}
-free hypergraphs. Hence, it is natural to ask if our approach and results can be extended to such families as well.

1.2
Results

Cyclic polytopes, the Facet hypergraph and the Girth conjecture.

We define the
facet hypergraph

\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
of the cyclic polytope
\mathsf{C}_{d}(n)
by

\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)=([n],\{f\subseteq[n]:\textrm{$f$ is a facet of $\mathsf{C}_{d}(n)$}\}).

Since
\mathsf{C}_{d}(n)
is simplicial,
\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
is
d
-uniform.

We provide asymptotically sharp bounds on
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))
in several small dimensions, along with non-trivial upper and lower bounds for general even dimensions. Here we only consider even dimensions
d
since it is easy to see that
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))\leq 3
for odd
d
, see Proposition
2.3
in below.

Theorem 1.2

(Lower bounds on
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))
for even
d
)
.

(1)

\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))=\Omega(\sqrt{n})
for every even
d\geq 4
.

(2)

For every positive integer
k
and for
d=4k
, we have
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))=\Omega(n^{\frac{d}{d+4}})=\Omega(n^{\frac{k}{k+1}})
.

(3)

For every even dimension
d\geq 14
with
d/2
being odd, we have
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))=\Omega(n^{\frac{k^{*}}{k^{*}+1}})
where
k^{*}=\lfloor d/6\rfloor
.

Theorem 1.3

(Sharp bounds on
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))
for small even
d
)
.

For
d=4,6,8,10,12
and
20
, we have:

\displaystyle\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{4}(n))=\Theta(\sqrt{n}),

\displaystyle\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{6}(n))=\Theta(\sqrt{n}),

\displaystyle\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{8}(n))=\Theta(n^{2/3}),

\displaystyle\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{10}(n))=\Theta(\sqrt{n}),

\displaystyle\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{12}(n))=\Theta(n^{3/4}),

\displaystyle\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{14}(n))=\Theta(n^{2/3}),

\displaystyle\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{18}(n))=\Theta(n^{3/4})\textrm{, and}

\displaystyle\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{20}(n))=\Theta(n^{5/6}).

Theorem 1.4

(Upper bounds for general even
d
)
.

For even
d\geq 16
, let
k=\lfloor d/4\rfloor
.
We have
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))=O(n^{1-\frac{2}{3k-1+\epsilon}})
where
\epsilon=0
when
k
is odd and
\epsilon=1
when
k
is even.

In fact, this particular case has a surprising connection to the Erdős girth conjecture in extremal graph theory
[
16
]
. The girth conjecture posits the maximum possible number of edges in a graph with given girth, a classical open problem in extremal combinatorics, for general backgrounds for the conjecture and related Turán type problems, see
[
19
]
. This connection indicates that
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))
are highly non-trivial to determine for general dimensions. The connection also implies that improving the bounds on
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))
would imply new bounds on the girth conjecture, making it of independent interest.

2-interval hypergraphs.

The
2-interval hypergraph
on vertex set
[n]
, denoted by
\mathsf{I}_{n}^{2}
, is a hypergraph where the hyperedges are union of two (discrete) intervals with a total cardinality at least
3
. It is well-known that
\gamma_{d}
crosses a hyperplane of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in at most
d
distinct points. Hence,
\mathsf{I}_{n}^{2}
can be regarded as a sub-hypergraph of
\mathsf{H}^{\gamma_{4}}_{n}
for Question
1.1
. In fact, one can easily see that
\mathsf{I}_{n}^{2}
contains (asymptotically) almost all the hyperedges of
\mathsf{H}^{\gamma_{4}}_{n}
of size at least 3.

Note also that
\mathsf{I}^{2}_{n}
is a “larger” hypergraph than

\mathsf{FC}_{4}(n)
; in size the former has
\Theta(n^{4})
hyperedges
while the latter has
\Theta(n^{2})
hyperedges. Also
\mathsf{I}^{2}_{n}
has a loose restriction for hyperedges than
\mathsf{FC}_{4}(n)
. Even so, the following Theorem shows that
\CF(\mathsf{I}^{2}_{n})
and
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{4}(n))
are asymptotically the same.

Theorem 1.5
.

\CF(\mathsf{I}^{2}_{n})=\Theta(\sqrt{n})
.

From Theorem
1.5
it follows that when
d=4
every sub-hypergraph in Question
1.1
has the CF-chromatic number
O(\sqrt{n})
.

2
CF-chromatic numbers of cyclic polytopes

2.1
Preliminaries

The following is a well-known combinatorial criterion which characterizes the facets of
\mathsf{C}_{d}(n)
.

Theorem 2.1

(Gale’s evenness criterion)
.

A
d
-subset
S
of
[n]
forms a facet of
\mathsf{C}_{d}(n)
if and only if the set
\{k\in S:i<k<j\}
has even size for every
i,j\in[n]\setminus S
. Namely, every maximal contiguous subset of
S
which contains neither
1
nor
n
has even size.

Now we state some basic observations. Without loss of generality we assume that
n\geq d+1
.

Proposition 2.2

(Proper colorings)
.

For
n\geq 3
, we have
\chi(\mathsf{FC}_{2}(n))=3
when
n
is odd and
\chi(\mathsf{FC}_{2}(n))=2
when
n
is even. For
d\geq 3
and
n\geq d+1
,
\chi(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))=2
.

Proof.

The first claim is obvious since
\mathsf{FC}_{2}(n)
is a cycle of length
n
. When
d\geq 3
, we alternately color RED and BLUE by coloring odd elements of
[n]
by RED and even elements of
[n]
by BLUE. By Theorem
2.1
, every hyperedge of
\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
should contain an interval of size 2. Hence, it must contain two colors.
∎

Proposition 2.3

(CF-colorings in odd dimensions)
.

For
n\geq 4
,
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{3}(n))=2
. For odd
d\geq 5
and
n\geq d+1
,
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))=3
.

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{FC}_{3}(n)
is 3-uniform, a proper 2-coloring in Proposition
2.2
also gives a CF-coloring of
\mathsf{FC}_{3}(n)
. So the first claim follows.

It remains to show the case for odd
d\geq 5
. We first show the upper bound. The following is a CF-coloring of
\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
with colors
\{\text{RED},\text{BLUE},\text{GREEN}\}
: we color 1 by RED,
n
by BLUE, and the other vertices by GREEN. This is a CF-coloring since every hyperedge contains either 1 or
n
by Theorem
2.1
and these end vertices have unique colors RED and BLUE.

Next, we show the lower bound. Let us consider an arbitrary 2-coloring of
\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
by RED and BLUE. We show that this coloring is not a CF-coloring. Note that by Theorem
2.1
, for any two disjoint intervals
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
of size 2 in
[n]
, there is a hyperedge of
\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
which contains both
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
as subsets. Hence, we are done if we can find two intervals
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
of size 2 such that
I_{1}\cup I_{2}
has 2 RED vertices and 2 BLUE vertices. Suppose otherwise.

Let
m
be the maximum length of an increasing sequence in
[n]
where a consecutive pair of elements have different colors from the 2-coloring. We cannot have
m\geq 4
, otherwise we can find a pair of disjoint intervals of size 2 we forbid. For the case when
m=3
, without loss of generality, we assume the color alternates RED-BLUE-RED when we move from 1 to
n
. We cannot have two BLUE vertices, otherwise we can again find a pair of intervals we forbid. Hence, there is only one BLUE vertex. Then, by Theorem
2.1
, we can find a hyperedge of
\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
which only consists of RED vertices. A similar argument holds when
m\leq 2
. This completes the proof.
∎

Hence in what follows we only consider even dimensions
d
.

2.2
Palette graphs and lower bounds on
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))
for even
d\geq 4

In this subsection we prove Theorem
1.2
. First, we prove Part (1).

Proof of Theorem
1.2
(1).

It is enough to prove it for the case when
n
is even. For fixed
d=2l\geq 4
and even
n\geq d+1
, put
H=\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
.
Suppose we are given a CF-coloring
\varphi:[n]\to[c]
of
H
.
By Theorem
2.1
, every
d
-subset of
[n]
of the form
\{2i_{1}-1,2i_{1},2i_{2}-1,2i_{2},\dots,2i_{l}-1,2i_{l}\}
for some
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{l}\leq\frac{n}{2}
, is a hyperedge of
H
.
Note that we cannot have all the
l
sets
\{\varphi(2i_{1}-1),\varphi(2i_{1})\}
,
\{\varphi(2i_{2}-1),\varphi(2i_{2})\}
,
\dots,\{\varphi(2i_{l}-1),\varphi(2i_{l})\}
be pairwise equal.
For otherwise the corresponding hyperedge has each color appearing at least
l
times.
Since there are
\frac{n}{2}
such sets
\{2i-1,2i\}
and at most
\binom{c}{2}+c
unordered pairs of colors (not necessarily distinct) we have that

\frac{n}{2}\leq\frac{c^{2}+c}{2}\cdot(l-1),

so
c=\Omega(\sqrt{n})
.
∎

Next, we introduce the following notion of a palette graph that will be useful for us:

Definition 2.4
.

Given a (simple) graph
G=(V,E)
and a coloring
\varphi:V\to[c]
, the
palette graph
of
G
with respect to
\varphi
, denoted by
P_{G,\varphi}
, is the multigraph
H=([c],\mathcal{F})
such that
there is a bijection
\psi:E\to\mathcal{F}
where, for every edge
e=\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
,
\psi(e)
is an edge between
\varphi(v_{1})
and
\varphi(v_{2})
.

For a positive integer
n
, let

M_{[n]}=([n],\{\{2i-1,2i\}:\textrm{$i$ is an integer with }1\leq i\leq n/2\}).

The main ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.2
(1) can be restated using the palette graph
P_{M_{[n]},\varphi}
. Namely, for any two colors
i
and
j
of
\varphi
, not necessarily distinct, a multi-edge
\{i,j\}
has multiplicity at most
d/2-1
in
P_{M_{[n]},\varphi}
.

In proving Parts (2) and (3) of Theorem
1.2
, we also show that a certain restriction on a palette graph is unavoidable. Note that in a multi-graph
H
and a vertex
v
of
H
, a loop incident to
v
is counted twice in the degree of
v
and other edges incident to
v
are counted exactly once.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
d=2l
and
n\geq d+1
. Let
\varphi:[n]\to[c]
be a CF-coloring of
\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
using
c
colors. The palette graph
P=P_{M_{[n]},\varphi}
contains no submultigraphs with exactly
l
edges and minimum degree at least
2
.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that there is a submultigraph
H
in
P
with the specified conditions. The
l=d/2
edges of
H
correspond to distinct edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{l}
of
M_{[n]}
by definition. Note that
e=e_{1}\cup e_{2}\cup\cdots\cup e_{l}
is a hyperedge of
\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
, and the degree condition on
H
implies that
e
does not attain a unique color by
\varphi
, a contradiction. This completes the proof.
∎

We also need the following lemma which seems to be of independent interest in extremal graph theory:

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G
be a simple graph on
c
vertices which contains no subgraphs with exactly
l
edges and minimum degree at least
2
. Then,

|\mathcal{E}(G)|=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}O(c^{1+(1/k)})&\text{when $l$ is even,}\\
O(c^{1+(1/k^{*})})&\text{when $l$ is odd and $l\geq 7$,}\end{array}\right.

where
k=l/2
and
k^{*}=\lfloor l/3\rfloor
.

To prove Lemma
2.6
we apply several known results from extremal graph theory. Before proceeding to the proof of Lemma
2.6

we first provide the proof of Theorem
1.2
(2) and (3).

Proof of Theorem
1.2
(2) and (3).

We only show it for the case when
n
is even; the other case will follow similarly. Given an even dimension
d=2l\geq 4
and
n\geq d+1
, put
H=\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
. Suppose that
\varphi:[n]\to[c]
is a CF-coloring of
H
. Put
P=P_{M_{[n]},\varphi}
.

Let
G
be the simple graph obtained from
P
by removing all loops and leaving exactly one copy of a multi-edge
for each pair of distinct vertices of
P
if such pair has an edge in
P
. Note that in
P
, by Lemma
2.5
, the multiplicity of any edge (or loop) is at most
l-1=d/2-1
. Therefore, we obtain the following inequality:

n/2=|\mathcal{E}(P)|\leq(l-1)|\mathcal{E}(G)|+(l-1)c.

Note also that
G
satisfies the condition of Lemma
2.6
by applying Lemma
2.5
on
P
. So, by Lemma
2.6
, we get
n=O(c^{1+(1/\tilde{k})})
or alternatively

c=\Omega(n^{\frac{\tilde{k}}{\tilde{k}+1}})

where
\tilde{k}=d/4
when
l
is even, and
\tilde{k}=\lfloor d/6\rfloor
when
l
is odd and
l\geq 7
. This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we prove Lemma
2.6
. The proof relies on the following two theorems. The first theorem attributed to Erdős, whose first proof was published by Bondy and Simonovits
[
6
]
. Denote by
ex(n,H)
the maximum number of edges that a graph
G
on
n
vertices can have under the condition that
G
does not contain
H
as a subgraph of
G
.

Theorem 2.7

(The Even Cycle Theorem)
.

ex(n,C_{2k})=O(n^{1+(1/k)}).

Define the
generalized theta graph
, denoted by
\Theta_{k_{1},\dots,k_{m}}
, to be the graph obtained by fixing two vertices
v
and
v^{\prime}
, which are connected by
m
internally disjoint paths with lengths
k_{1},\dots,k_{m}
, respectively. The following result is from
[
34
]
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Fix positive integers
k_{1},\dots,k_{m}
with the same parity, in which 1 appears at most once. Then,

ex(n,\Theta_{k_{1},\dots,k_{m}})=O(n^{1+(1/k^{*})}),

where
k^{*}=\frac{1}{2}\min_{1\leq i<j\leq m}(k_{i}+k_{j})
.

The following is an easy implication from Theorem
2.8
.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
l\geq 7
be odd. Let
k_{1},k_{2}
and
k_{3}
be odd positive integers which almost equipartition
l
, that is, satisfy
|k_{i}-k_{j}|\leq 2
for every
i,j\in[3]
and
k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}=l
. Then,

ex(n,\Theta_{k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}})=O(n^{1+(1/k^{*})}),

where
k^{*}=\lfloor l/3\rfloor
.

Proof of Lemma
2.6
.

When

as a subgraph by the condition. When

and

which almost equipartition

2.3
Upper bounds on
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))
for even
d\geq 4

Figure 1
:
Illustration: A coloring on
[n]
using
c
colors from an Eulerian circuit
C_{G}
of a graph
G
with
c
vertices and
n
edges. For this example, we have
n=10
and
c=5
, and the tour begins from
v_{1}
and

for
G
and
C_{[10]}
, respectively. By the proof of Theorem

below, this also gives a CF-coloring of
\mathsf{FC}_{4}(10)
.

Before we turn our attention to prove upper bounds on
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))
we state the following key lemma:

Lemma 2.10
.

Fix an even dimension
d\geq 4
. Let
G
be a simple graph on
c
vertices and
n
edges with the following properties.

(1)

G
is Eulerian, that is, every vertex of
G
has an even degree and
G
is connected.

(2)

G
does not contain any cycle
C_{j}
where
3\leq j\leq d/2
and
j\neq d/2-1
.

Then, there is a CF-coloring of
\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
with
c
colors.

We use Lemma
2.10
and lower bounds construction of graphs with large girth and many edges in order to provide upper bounds on
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))
.

We first prove Lemma
2.10
. For
n\geq 3
, recall that we denote by
C_{[n]}
the cycle on the vertex set
[n]
where the elements of
[n]
are cyclically ordered. That is, let

C_{[n]}=([n],\{\{i,i+1\}:i\in[n]\textrm{ in modulo $n$}\}).

It is easy to see that given a coloring
\varphi:[n]\to[c]
using
c
colors, the palette graph
P_{C_{[n]},\varphi}
is Eulerian. For the other direction, given an Eulerian graph
G
on
c
vertices and
n
edges, we can also find a coloring
\varphi:[n]\to[c]
such that
G
is the same as the palette graph
P_{C_{[n]},\varphi}
: choose an Eulerian circuit
C_{G}
of
G
and we simultaneously traverse
C_{[n]}
and
C_{G}
. Whenever we visit a new vertex
v
of
C_{[n]}
, we color
v
with the current vertex of the graph
G
. We do it until we traverse all the edges and return to the original vertex. See Figure
1
for an illustration.

Note that for even dimensions
d
we have

\mathcal{E}(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))=\{e_{1}\cup\cdots\cup e_{d/2}\subset[n]:e_{i}\in\mathcal{E}(C_{[n]}),\,e_{i}\cap e_{j}=\emptyset\textrm{ for distinct $i,j\in[n]$}\}

by Theorem
2.1
. Lemma
2.10
can be also understood as a theorem about palette graphs of
C_{[n]}
before colorings become explicit, but the conditions in the theorem also provide a concrete CF-coloring of
\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
for even
d\geq 4
.

Proof of Lemma
2.10
.

By (1),
G
is Eulerian. Take an Eulerian tour
e_{1}e_{2}\dots e_{n}
of
G
and construct a coloring
\varphi:[n]\rightarrow[c]
as described above. From
\varphi
, we can find an induced bijection
\psi:\mathcal{E}(C_{[n]})\to\mathcal{E}(G)
. We claim that
\varphi
is a CF-coloring of
\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
, that is, we show that an arbitrary hyperedge
e
of
\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
has a vertex with a unique color. Assume to the contrary that this is not the case and let
l=d/2
. By Theorem
2.1
, there are pairwise disjoint edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{l}
of
C_{[n]}
such that
e=e_{1}\cup\cdots\cup e_{l}
. Since
G
is simple, by our construction of
\varphi
, each

e_{i}
attains exactly 2 colors from
\varphi
. So, every color used in
\varphi(e)
should appear in at least two edges among
e_{1},\dots,e_{l}
. Let
H
be the subgraph of
G
induced by the edges
\psi(e_{1}),\dots,\psi(e_{l})
. It follows that every vertex of
H
has degree at least 2, so there is a cycle
C_{H}
in
H
of length at most
l
. Since the other lengths are forbidden,
C_{H}
has length
l-1
(this implies that
l\geq 4
). Then there is exactly one edge of
H
, say
f=\psi(e_{1})
, which is not used in
C_{H}
. If
f
is a chord of
C_{H}
, then we can find a smaller cycle which leads to a contradiction with (2). If
f
uses a vertex not in
C_{H}
, then
H
has a vertex of degree 1 which again leads to a contradiction.
∎

Explicit Upper Bounds on
\CF(\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n))
.
Now we prove Theorems

by using Lemma

For each case, we first find a graph

Proof of Theorem

In

and

satisfies the following properties.

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

(iv)

when

Now, for even

, let

. Then

or

2.4
Unions of two disjoint intervals of size
r
in a cycle

We briefly discuss a natural generalization of
\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
. Let
\mathsf{D}_{r}^{m}(n)
be the
(mr)
-uniform hypergraph on the vertex set
[n]
with the hyperedge-set

\mathcal{E}(\mathsf{D}_{r}^{m}(n))=\left\{\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}V(P_{i}):\textrm{$P_{1},\dots,P_{m}$ are pairwise vertex-disjoint paths on $r$ vertices in $C_{[n]}$}\right\}.

Note that
\mathsf{FC}_{2m}(n)=\mathsf{D}^{m}_{2}(n)
. It was also recently shown that for the sub-hypergraph
H^{\prime}
of
\mathsf{D}^{m}_{2}(n)
whose hyperedges are obtained as the union of subpaths
P_{1},\dots,P_{m}
of the big path
P_{[n]}
on
[n]
,
H^{\prime}
is the facet hypergraph of a piecewise-linear
(2m-1)
-dimensional ball, see
[
38
, Lemma 4.5]
. In this sense,
\mathsf{D}^{m}_{2}(n)
can be regarded as both combinatorial and topological generalization of
\mathsf{FC}_{2m}(n)
.

For
m=2
, we obtain the asymptotically sharp bound on
\CF(\mathsf{D}_{r}^{2}(n))
for every
r\geq 2
.

Theorem 2.11
.

\CF(\mathsf{D}_{r}^{2}(n))=\Theta(n^{1/r})
.

An important ingredient to the proof of Theorem
2.11
is universal cycles for subsets. A
universal cycle for

\binom{[c]}{r}
is a cyclic sequence with
\binom{c}{r}
elements from
[c]
, such that every
r
consecutive elements are distinct and every element of
\binom{[c]}{r}
appears exactly once consecutively. These objects were first studied by Chung, Diaconis and Graham
[
12
]
. There, the authors provided a necessary condition for the existence of a universal cycle, namely, that
r
divides
\binom{c-1}{r-1}
. They also conjectured that this condition is also sufficient for a sufficiently large
c
. Since then, partial results were obtained
[
24
,
23
,
35
]
as well as approximate versions
[
5
,
13
,
15
,
35
]
. The conjecture was recently proved affirmatively by Glock, Joos, Kühn and Osthus
[
20
]
:

Theorem 2.12
.

For every positive integer
r
, there exists
c_{0}
such that for all
c\geq c_{0}
, there exists a universal cycle for
\binom{[c]}{r}
whenever
r
divides
\binom{c-1}{r-1}
.

Since we use similar argument as in above, we only provide proof sketch of Theorem
2.11
.

Proof sketch of Theorem
2.11
.

For the lower bound, recall that the proof of Theorem
1.2
(1) for
\mathsf{FC}_{4}(n)
uses an upper bound on the number of colors assigned to unordered pairs of the form
\{2i-1,2i\}
in
[n]
when a CF-coloring of
\mathsf{FC}_{4}(n)
using
c
colors is given. For
\mathsf{D}_{r}^{2}(n)
, we rather use
r
-subsets of
[n]
of the form
\{ri-r+1,ri-r+2,\dots,ri\}
in
[n]
and then apply the same argument.

For an upper bound, recall that for
\mathsf{FC}_{4}(n)
we make use of an Eulerian circuit of the complete graph
K_{c}
for odd
c
to CF-color
\mathsf{FC}_{4}(n)
. We use a similar argument for
\mathsf{D}_{r}^{2}(n)
except for that we use a universal cycle for
\binom{[c]}{r}
. Put
n=\binom{c}{r}
. For
[n]
, by following a universal cycle for
\binom{[c]}{r}
and
C_{[n]}
simultaneously, we assign the current element of the universal cycle to the current vertex of
C_{[n]}
as a color. This gives a CF-coloring of
\mathsf{D}_{r}^{2}(n)
.
∎

If we can follow the same line of proof
for larger

as for
\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
, the following question might suggest a Turán type problem for hypergraphs, which is generally known to be very challenging.

3

A set of integers is called a

if it consists of consecutive integers. The

\CF(\mathsf{I}^{2}_{n})=\Omega(\sqrt{n})

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

The lower bound is shown in Proposition
3.1
. It is enough to give a CF-coloring of
\mathsf{I}^{2}_{n}
which uses
O(\sqrt{n})
colors. We fix a positive integer
k
. We show that the 2-interval hypergraph on
[n]=[2k^{2}-1]
has a CF-coloring which uses
O(\sqrt{n})
colors.

(See Figure
2
for illustration for when
k=3
.)
In
[n]
, by choosing suitable
k-1
separators which form a subset
S\subset[n]
, we divide
[n]\setminus S
into
k
blocks

B_{1},\dots,B_{k}
of consecutive numbers of the same size

where the indices are given in an increasing order. In each block

, we simultaneously follow elements of
B_{i}
in an increasing order and the Hamiltonian path
Z_{i}
of Walecki’s construction (of the complete graph on
2k
vertices) from
v_{i}
right before arriving at
v_{i+k}
, and assign the current vertex
v_{j}
of
Z_{i}
to the current element

of
B_{i}
as the color of
t
. We also assign an additional color
x_{i}
to the
i
th separator for

. This completes the coloring. Note that we used
3k-1=O(\sqrt{n})
colors.

Now we show that it is a CF-coloring of
\mathsf{I}^{2}_{n}
. Consider two intervals
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
in
[n]
. We show that there is a unique color in
I_{1}\cup I_{2}
. If
I_{1}
or
I_{2}
contains a separator, then we are done because separators have unique colors. So both
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are contained in a single block, say
B_{i_{1}}
and
B_{i_{2}}
, respectively. The case when
B_{i_{1}}=B_{i_{2}}
is also easy, because all vertices attain distinct colors in each block. So
B_{i_{1}}\neq B_{i_{2}}
. By the same reason, we may assume
|I_{1}|=|I_{2}|
since otherwise there is a unique color in
I_{1}\cup I_{2}
.

Hence, the remaining case is when there are distinct blocks
B_{i_{1}}
and
B_{i_{2}}
with
I_{1}\subseteq B_{i_{1}}
,
I_{2}\subseteq B_{i_{2}}
and
2\leq|I_{1}|=|I_{2}|\leq 2k-1
. Denote our coloring of
\mathsf{I}^{2}_{n}
by
\varphi
. It is enough to show that
\varphi(I_{1})\neq\varphi(I_{2})
. Suppose otherwise.

Consider the regular
(2k)
-gon
D
in the plane where we identify the vertices of
D
with the colors
v_{1},\dots,v_{2k}
. For each

, by construction
\conv(\varphi(I_{j}))
has 1 or 2 boundary edges which are not edges of

since
|I_{j}|<2k
. We call them

of
\conv(\varphi(I_{j}))
. Note that
Z_{i_{j}}
is a unique zigzag path among

which contains all interior edges of
\conv(\varphi(I_{j}))
: the uniqueness can be shown by that no two zigzag paths among
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{k}
share a common edge. Since
\conv(\varphi(I_{1}))=\conv(\varphi(I_{2}))
, the two convex hulls have the same set of interior edges, and the same unique zigzag paths containing them, that is,
Z_{i_{1}}=Z_{i_{2}}
. This implies

which leads to a contradiction with that
B_{i_{1}}
and

(AB)^{l/2}
-free Hypergraphs.

For a positive integer
l
, a hypergraph
H=(V,\mathcal{E})
on ordered vertex set
V

is called
(AB)^{l/2}
-free
if there is no sequence of
l
ordered vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{l}
such that the odd-indexed vertices
v_{1},v_{3},\dots
are in
A\setminus B
and the even indexed vertices
v_{2},v_{4},\dots
are in
B\setminus A
for some hyperedges
A
and
B
of
H
.
The notion of
(AB)^{l/2}
-free hypergraphs
has been actively studied recently in context of coloring problems on geometric hypergraphs, see, e.g.,
[
2
,
29
,
30
,
14
]
.

As we already noticed,
\mathsf{H}^{\gamma_{d}}_{n}
and
\mathsf{I}^{m}_{n}
has natural orders on their vertex sets. Following those orders, note that
\mathsf{I}^{m}_{n}
is an
(AB)^{(2m+1)/2}
-free hypergraph: Suppose that there are hyperedges
A
and
B
and ordered vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{2m+1}
such that
v_{i}\in A\setminus B
when
i
is odd and
v_{i}\in B\setminus A
when
i
is even. Then,
A
cannot consist of only
m
intervals. One can easily see that the number
(2m+1)/2
is tight for
\mathsf{I}^{m}_{n}
, that is,
\mathsf{I}^{m}_{n}
is not
(AB)^{l/2}
-free for
l\leq 2m
.

Further
\mathsf{H}^{\gamma_{d}}_{n}
is an
(AB)^{(d+2)/2}
-free hypergraph and again the number
(d+2)/2
is tight (note that there is a difference between
\mathsf{I}^{m}_{n}
and
\mathsf{H}^{\gamma_{2m}}_{n}
regarding the
(AB)^{l/2}
-free property):
Let us denote the vertex set of
\mathsf{H}^{\gamma_{d}}_{n}
on
\gamma_{d}
by
P
. Assume to the contrary that there exists a subset
Q\subseteq P
of
d+2
points
Q=\{q_{1},\ldots q_{d+2}\}
, and hyperedges
A
and
B
such that the odd indexed points of
Q
(along
\gamma_{d}
)
q_{1},q_{3},\ldots,
belong to
A\setminus B

and the even indexed points
q_{2},q_{4},\ldots
belong to
B\setminus A
. In particular
q_{1},q_{3},\ldots,
belong to
A
and
q_{2},q_{4},\ldots
do not belong to
A
. Let
h
be a hyperplane such that the halfspace
h^{+}
bounded by
h
witness the hyperedge
A
, namely,
h^{+}\cap P=A
. This means that the odd indexed points
q_{1},q_{3},\ldots,
all in
h^{+}
and the even indexed points
q_{2},q_{4},\ldots
lie in the complement halfspace
h^{-}
. In particular the hyperplane
h
separates the even indexed points from the odd indexed points and therefore must intersect
\gamma_{d}
between any two consecutive points
q_{i}
and
q_{i+1}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,d+1\}
. So
h
intersects
\gamma_{d}
in
d+1
points. This is a contradiction to the fact that any hypeplane in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
intersects
\gamma_{d}
at most
d
times.

On the other hand, the other direction does not hold in general. That is,
\mathsf{I}^{m}_{n}
(and
\mathsf{H}^{\gamma_{d}}_{n}
, respectively) and its sub-hypergraphs are just special cases
of
(AB)^{(2m+1)/2}
-free (and
(AB)^{(d+2)/2}
-free, respectively) hypergraphs, see Figure
3
. Hence it is natural to ask the following general question.

Conflict-free coloring of general simplicial spheres.

Recently there have been studies on proper colorings and transversals of (the facet hypergraphs of) simplicial spheres
[
7
,
39
,
11
,
33
,
38
]
. Many results were on lower bound constructions to obtain large chromatic number or transversal ratio (the ratio between transversal number and the vertex set size). There the moment curve or cyclic polytopes played an important role. As a side effect, the current lower bounds are relatively weaker for even-dimensional spheres than odd-dimensional ones as we could see for
\mathsf{FC}_{d}(n)
in Proposition
2.2
.

On the other hand, in
[
33
]
some other construction was obtained using different methods, which shows that for any dimension
d\geq 4
and any positive integer
c
, there is a
d
-dimensional simplicial sphere whose facet hypergraph has a proper chromatic number at least
c
. Since we have
\CF(H)\geq\chi(H)
for any hypergraph
H
, this implies that the CF-chromatic number is unbounded for even-dimensional spheres. Unfortunately, the construction highly depends on facts from PL topology so it does not have an explicit description to be used practically. This motivates the following question.

Question 4.5
.

What is the asymptotically maximum CF-chromatic number of
d
-dimensional simplicial spheres for each even dimensions
d
?

Abstract
. Liouville closed H H -fields are ordered differential fields whose ordering and derivation interact in a natural way and where every linear differential equation of order 1 1 has a nontrivial solution. (The introduction gives a precise definition.) For a Liouville closed H H -field K K with small derivation we show: K K has the Intermediate Value Property for differential polynomials iff K K is elementarily equivalent to the ordered differential field of transseries. We also indicate how this applies to Hardy fields.



Introduction

Throughout this introduction
K
is an ordered differential field, that is, an ordered field equipped with a derivation
\der\colon K\to K
. (We usually write
f^{\prime}
instead of
\der f
, for
f\in K
.)
Its constant field

C\ :=\ \{f\in K:\,f^{\prime}=0\}

yields the (convex) valuation ring

of
K
, with maximal ideal

\smallo\ :=\ {\big\{f\in K:\,\text{$|f|<c$ for all $c>0$ in $C$}\big\}}.

(It may help to think of the elements of
K
as germs of real valued functions and of
f\in\mathcal{O}g
and
f\in\smallo g
as
f=O(g)
and

, respectively.)
The above definitions exhibit

,
\mathcal{O}
, and
\smallo
as definable in

in the sense of model theory.

Key example: the ordered differential field
{\mathbb{T}}
of
transseries
, which contains
{\mathbb{R}}
as an ordered subfield, and where
C={\mathbb{R}}
. We refer to

[
3
]
for the rather elaborate construction of
{\mathbb{T}}
and for any fact about
{\mathbb{T}}
that gets mentioned without proof.

Other important examples are Hardy fields. (Hardy
[
6
]
proved a striking theorem on logarithmic-exponential functions. Bourbaki
[
5
]
put this into the general setting of what they called Hardy fields.) Here we can give a definition from scratch that doesn’t take much space.
Notation:
\mathcal{C}
is the ring of germs at
+\infty
of continuous real-valued functions on halflines
(a,+\infty)
,
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
. For
r=1,2,\dots
, let

\mathcal{C}^{r}
be the subring of
\mathcal{C}
consisting of the germs at
+\infty
of
r
-times continuously differentiable real-valued functions on such halflines. This yields the subring

\mathcal{C}^{<\infty}\ :=\ \bigcap_{r\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\geqslant 1}}\mathcal{C}^{r}

of
\mathcal{C}
, and
\mathcal{C}^{<\infty}
is naturally a
differential
ring. For a germ
f\in\mathcal{C}
we let
f
also denote any real valued function representing this germ,
if this causes no ambiguity. A real number is identified with the germ of the corresponding constant function:
{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
.

A
Hardy field
is by definition a differential subfield of
\mathcal{C}^{<\infty}
.

Examples
:

\quad{\mathbb{Q}},\\
\quad{\mathbb{R}},\quad{\mathbb{R}}(x),\quad{\mathbb{R}}(x,\operatorname{e}^{x}),\quad{\mathbb{R}}(x,\operatorname{e}^{x},\log x),\quad{\mathbb{R}}(\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime\prime},\dots),

where

denotes the germ at
+\infty
of the identity function on
{\mathbb{R}}
. All these are actually
analytic
Hardy fields, that is, its elements are germs of real analytic functions.

Let
H
be a Hardy field. Then
H
is an
ordered
differential field:
for
f\in H
, either
f(x)>0
eventually (in which case we set
f>0
), or
f(x)=0
, eventually,
or
f(x)<0
, eventually; this is because

in

implies

has a multiplicative inverse in

, so

cannot have arbitrarily large zeros. Also, if
f^{\prime}<0
, then
f
is eventually strictly decreasing; if
f^{\prime}=0
, then
f
is eventually constant;
if
f^{\prime}>0
, then
f
is eventually strictly increasing.

In order to state the main result of this paper we need a bit more terminology: an
H
-field
is a

(that is, an ordered differential field) such that:

•

for all
f\in K
, if
f>C
, then
f^{\prime}>0
;

•

\mathcal{O}=C+\smallo
(so

maps isomorphically onto the residue field
\mathcal{O}/\smallo
).

We also say that
K
has small derivation
if for all
f\in\smallo
we have

. Hardy fields have small derivation, and any Hardy field containing
{\mathbb{R}}
is an
H
-field.

An
H
-field

is said to be

if it is real closed and for every

there are

such that

.
The ordered differential field
{\mathbb{T}}
is a Liouville closed
H
-field with small derivation.
Any Hardy field

has a smallest (with respect to inclusion) Liouville closed
Hardy field extension
\text{Li}(H)
.
(The notions of “

-field” and “Liouville closed

-field” are introduced in
[
1
]
. The capital

Now a very strong property: we say

has DIVP

and all

in

.

]
and
[

Actually, DIVP is a bit of an afterthought: in

]
we considered instead two robust but rather technical

We did not consider DIVP in
[

]
, but it is surely an appealing property and easier to grasp than the more fundamental notions of

\upomega
-freeness and newtonianity. (The latter make sense in a wider setting of valued differential fields where the valuation does not necessarily arise from an ordering, as is

Besides
[

]
we shall rely on

, which focuses on a particular ordered differential subfield of

, consisting of the so-called

-free

We call attention to the fact that

for a set

(independent of

-free” is expressible by a single sentence in the language of ordered differential fields, and “newtonian” as well as “DIVP” by a set of sentences in this language.
The reason that “
\upomega

are various theorems proved there, which are also relevant here. To state these theorems, we consider

We can now summarize what we need from

as follows:

The theory of newtonian

-free Liouville closed

For an

-field

its valuation ring

Corollary 0.1
.

Every newtonian
\upomega
-free Liouville closed
H
-field has

Proof.

Let

be a newtonian
\upomega
-free Liouville closed

]

Connection to Hardy fields

Every Hardy field
H
extends to a Hardy field
H({\mathbb{R}})\supseteq{\mathbb{R}}
, and
H({\mathbb{R}})
is in particular an
H
-field. We refer to
[
4
]
for
a discussion of the conjecture that
any Hardy field containing
{\mathbb{R}}
extends to a newtonian
\upomega
-free Hardy field
. At the end of 2019 we finished the proof of this conjecture by considerably refining
material in
[
3
]
and
[
8
]
; this amounts to a rather complete extension theory of Hardy fields.
Note that every Hardy field extends to a maximal Hardy field, by Zorn, and
so having established this conjecture we now know that all maximal Hardy fields are elementarily equivalent to
{\mathbb{T}}
, as ordered differential fields. Since
\mathcal{C}
has the cardinality
\mathfrak{c}=2^{\aleph_{0}}
of the continuum, there are at most
2^{\mathfrak{c}}
many maximal Hardy fields, and we also have a proof that there are exactly that many. (We thank Ilijas Farah for a useful hint on this point.) These remarks on Hardy fields serve as an announcement. A rather voluminous work containing the proof of the conjecture is currently being prepared for publication. We also hope to include there a proof of DIVP for newtonian
\upomega
-free
H
-fields that does not depend as in the present paper on it being true for
{\mathbb{T}}_{\operatorname{g}}
, whose proof in
[
7
]
uses the particular nature of
{\mathbb{T}}_{\operatorname{g}}
.

We have a second conjecture about Hardy fields in
[
4
]
, whose proof is not yet finished at this time (May 2021):
for any maximal Hardy field
H
and countable subsets
A<B
in
H
there exists
y\in H
such that
A<y<B
. This means that the underlying ordered set of a maximal Hardy field is an
\eta_{1}
-set in the sense of Hausdorff. Together with the (now established) first conjecture and results from
[
3
]
it implies:
all maximal Hardy fields are back-and-forth equivalent as ordered differential fields,
and thus isomorphic assuming
CH
, the Continuum Hypothesis
.

Notation and terminology

Throughout,
m
,
n
range over
{\mathbb{N}}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
.
Given an additively written abelian group

we let
A^{\neq}:=A\setminus\{0\}
.
Rings are commutative with identity

, and for a ring

we let
R^{\times}
be the multiplicative group of units (consisting of the

such that

for some

). A

will be a ring

containing
(an isomorphic copy of)

; note that then

, called the ring of constants of

, and that

. If

is a field, then so is

.
An ordered differential field is in particular a differential ring.

Let
R
be a differential ring and

. When its derivation
\der
is clear from the context we denote
\der(a),\der^{2}(a),\dots,\der^{n}(a),\dots
by

, and if

, then

denotes

, so
(ab)^{\dagger}=a^{\dagger}+b^{\dagger}
for

. In Section

we need to consider the function

\omega=\omega_{R}\colon R\to R
given by
\omega(z)=-2z^{\prime}-z^{2}
, and
the function
\sigma=\sigma_{R}\colon R^{\times}\to R
given by

\sigma(y)=\omega(z)+y^{2}
for
z:=-y^{\dagger}
.

We have the differential ring
R\{Y\}=R[Y,Y^{\prime},Y^{\prime\prime},\dots]
of differential polynomials in an indeterminate
Y
over
R
.
We say that

P=P(Y)\in R\{Y\}
has order at most

if
P\in R[Y,Y^{\prime},\dots,Y^{(r)}]
.

For
\phi\in R^{\times}
we let
R^{\phi}
be the
compositional conjugate of

by

: the differential ring
with the same underlying ring as
R
but with derivation
\phi^{-1}\der
instead of
\der
.
We then have an
R
-algebra isomorphism

P\mapsto P^{\phi}\ \colon\ R\{Y\}\to R^{\phi}\{Y\}

with

for all

;
see
[
3
, Section 5.7]
.

For a field
K
we have
K^{\times}=K^{\neq}
, and
a (Krull) valuation on

is a surjective map

v\colon K^{\times}\to\Gamma
onto an ordered abelian
group
\Gamma
(additively written) satisfying the usual laws, and extended to

v\colon K\to\Gamma_{\infty}:=\Gamma\cup\{\infty\}
by

,
where the ordering on
\Gamma
is extended to a total ordering
on
\Gamma_{\infty}
by
\gamma<\infty
for all

\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Let
K
be a
valued field
: a field (also denoted by
K
) together with a valuation ring
\mathcal{O}
of that field. This yields a valuation
v\colon K^{\times}\to\Gamma
on the underlying field
such that
\mathcal{O}=\{a\in K:va\geqslant 0\}
as explained in
[
3
, Section 3.1]
.
We introduce various binary relations
on the set
K
by defining for

:

\displaystyle a\asymp b

\displaystyle\ :\Leftrightarrow\ va=vb,

\displaystyle a\preccurlyeq b

\displaystyle\ :\Leftrightarrow\ va\geqslant vb,

\displaystyle a\prec b

\displaystyle\ :\Leftrightarrow\ va>vb,

\displaystyle a\succcurlyeq b

\displaystyle\ :\Leftrightarrow\ b\preccurlyeq a,

\displaystyle a\succ b

\displaystyle\ :\Leftrightarrow\ b\prec a,

\displaystyle a\sim b

\displaystyle\ :\Leftrightarrow\ a-b\prec a.

It is easy to check that if

, then
a,b\neq 0
, and that

is an equivalence relation on
K^{\times}
. We also let
\smallo=\{a\in K:va>0\}
be the maximal ideal of

, so

is the residue field
of the valued field

A convex subgroup

of the value group

of

\Delta
-coarsening
of the valued field

; see [ADH, 3.4].

, a

2.

In this section

is a pre-
H
-field. We let

be its valuation ring, with maximal ideal
\smallo
, and corresponding valuation

be its

\Psi:=\big\{\psi(\gamma):\gamma\in\Gamma^{\neq}\big\}

in

such that

\leqslant r
, where

From
[
3

recall our convention that

, and similarly with

.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
\Gamma\neq\{0\}
and

has

1

\operatorname{DIVP}
. Then

\der K=K
,
(K^{>})^{\dagger}=(K^{<})^{\dagger}
is a convex subgroup of
K
,
\Psi
has no largest element, and
\Psi
is convex in
\Gamma
.

Proof.

We have
y^{\prime}=0
for
y=0
, and
y^{\prime}
takes arbitrarily large positive values in

as

ranges over

K^{>\mathcal{O}}=\{a\in K:a>\mathcal{O}\}
, since by
[
3

the set

is coinitial in
\Gamma
. Hence

takes all positive values on
K^{>}
, and therefore also all negative values on

. Thus
\der K=K
. Next, let

, and suppose

and

. Then

for some

strictly between

and

; this follows by noting that for

and

are opposite.

Let

. Then

, since

would give

. Hence for
\alpha=va<0

, so
\alpha^{\dagger}>\beta
.
Thus
\Psi
has no largest element.
Therefore the set
\Psi
is convex in
\Gamma
. ∎

Proof.

In

\text{I}(K)\ :=\ \{y\in K:\,\text{$y\preccurlyeq f^{\prime}$ for some $f\in\mathcal{O}$}\},

K
has no gap, we have

\der\smallo\ \subseteq\ \text{I}(K)\ =\ \{y\in K:\,\text{$y\preccurlyeq f^{\prime}$ for some $f\in\smallo$}\}.

Also
\Gamma\neq\{0\}
, and so
(\Gamma,\psi)
has asymptotic integration by Lemma
2.1
. We show that
K
is an
H
-field
by proving
\text{I}(K)=\der\smallo
, so let
g\in\text{I}(K)
,
g<0
. Since
(\Gamma^{>})^{\prime}
has no least element we can take
positive
f\in\smallo
such that
f^{\prime}\succ g
. Since

f^{\prime}<0
, this gives
f^{\prime}<g
. Since
(\Gamma^{>})^{\prime}
is cofinal in

\Gamma
we can also take positive
h\in\smallo
such that
h^{\prime}\prec g
, which in view of
h^{\prime}<0
gives
g<h^{\prime}
.
Thus
f^{\prime}<g<h^{\prime}
, and so
1
-DIVP yields
a\in\smallo
with
g=a^{\prime}
.
∎

We refer to Sections 11.6 and 14.2 of
[
3
]
for the definitions of
\uplambda
-freeness
and
r
-newtonianity
(
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
). From the introduction we recall that

\omega(z):=-2z^{\prime}-z^{2}
. Below, compositionally conjugating an
H
-field

means replacing it by some
K^{\phi}
with
\phi\in K^{>}
; this preserves most relevant properties like being an
H
-field, being
\uplambda
-free,
r
-DIVP, and

-newtonianity, and it replaces
\Psi
by
\Psi-v\phi
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
K
is an
H
-field,
\Gamma\neq\{0\}
, and
K
has

1
-
\operatorname{DIVP}
. Then
K
is
\uplambda
-free and
1
-newtonian, and the subset
\omega(K)
of
K
is downward closed.

Proof.

Note that
K
has (asymptotic) integration, by Lemma
2.1
.
Assume towards a contradiction that
K
is
not
\uplambda
-free. We arrange by compositional conjugation that
K
has small derivation, so
K
has an element
x\succ 1
with
x^{\prime}=1
, hence
x>C
. A construction in the beginning of
[
3
, Section 11.5]
yields by
[
3
, Lemma 11.5.2]
a pseudocauchy sequence

(\uplambda_{\rho})
in
K
with certain properties including
\uplambda_{\rho}\sim x^{-1}
for all
\rho
.
As
K
is not
\uplambda
-free,
(\uplambda_{\rho})
has a pseudolimit
\uplambda\in K
by
[
3
, Corollary 11.6.1]
. Then
s:=-\uplambda\sim-x^{-1}
, and

s
creates a gap
over
K
by
[
3
, Lemma 11.5.14]
. Now note that for

P:=Y^{\prime}+sY
we have
P(0)=0
and
P(x^{2})=2x+sx^{2}\sim x
, so
by
1
-DIVP we have
P(y)=1
for some
y\in K
, contradicting

[
3
, Lemma 11.5.12]
.

Let
P\in K\{Y\}
of order at most
1
have Newton degree
1
; we have to show that
P
has a zero in
\mathcal{O}
. We know that
K
is
\uplambda
-free, so by
[
3
, Proposition 13.3.6]
we
can pass to an elementary extension,
compositionally conjugate, and divide by an element of
K^{\times}
to arrange that
K
has small derivation and

P=D+R
where
D=cY+d
or
D=cY^{\prime}
with
c,d\in C
,
c\neq 0
, and where
R\prec^{\flat}1
. Then
R(a)\prec^{\flat}1
for all
a\in\mathcal{O}
. If
D=cY+d
, then we can take
a,b\in C
with
D(a)<0
and
D(b)>0
, which in view of
R(a)\prec D(a)
and

R(b)\prec D(b)
gives
P(a)<0
and
P(b)>0
, and so

P
has a zero strictly between
a
and
b
, and thus a zero in
\mathcal{O}
. Next, suppose
D=cY^{\prime}
. Then we take
t\in\smallo^{\neq}
with
v(t^{\dagger})=v(t)
, that is,
t^{\prime}\asymp t^{2}
, so

P(t)\ =\ ct^{\prime}+R(t),\quad P(-t)\ =\ -ct^{\prime}+R(-t),\qquad R(t),\ R(-t)\ \prec\ t^{\prime}.

Hence
P(t)
and
P(-t)
have opposite signs, so

P
has a zero strictly between
t
and
-t
, and thus
P

has a zero in
\mathcal{O}
.

From
\omega(z)=-z^{2}-2z^{\prime}
we see that
\omega(z)\to-\infty
as

z\to+\infty
and as
z\to-\infty
in
K
, so
\omega(K)
is downward closed by
1
-IVP.
∎

For results involving
r
-DIVP for

we need a variant of
[

, Lemma 11.8.31]
. To state this variant we introduce as in

the sets

\Upgamma(K)\ :=\ \{a^{\dagger}:\,a\in K\setminus\mathcal{O}\}\ \subseteq\ K^{>},\qquad\Uplambda(K)\ :=\ -\Upgamma(K)^{\dagger}\ \subseteq\ K.

The superscripts

,
\downarrow
used in the statement of Lemma

below indicate upward, respectively downward, closure
in the ordered set

, as in

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
K
be an
H
-field with asymptotic integration. Then

K^{>}\ =\ \operatorname{I}(K)^{>}\cup\Upgamma(K)^{\uparrow},\qquad\sigma\big(K^{>}\setminus\Upgamma(K)^{\uparrow}\big)\ \subseteq\ \omega\big(\Uplambda(K)\big){}^{\downarrow}.

Proof.

If
a\in K
,
a>\text{I}(K)
, then
a\geqslant b^{\dagger}
for some
b\in K^{\succ 1}
, and thus
a\in\Upgamma(K)^{\uparrow}
. Next, let
s\in K^{>}\setminus\Upgamma(K)^{\uparrow}
;
we have to show
\sigma(s)\in\omega\big(\Uplambda(K)\big){}^{\downarrow}
. Note that

by what we just proved. From
[

, 10.2.7 and 10.5.8]
we obtain an immediate

-field extension

of

and

with
s=(1/a)^{\prime}
. As in the proof
of
[
3
, 11.8.31]
with
L
instead of
K
this gives
\sigma(s)\in\omega\big(\Uplambda(L)\big){}^{\downarrow}
, where
\downarrow
indicates here the downward closure in
L
.
It remains to note that
\omega
is increasing on
\Uplambda(L)
by the remark preceding
[
3
, 11.8.21]
,
and that
\Uplambda(K)
is cofinal in
\Uplambda(L)
by
[
3
, 11.8.14]
.
∎

The concept of
\upomega
-freeness
is introduced in
[

, Section 11.7]
.
If

has asymptotic integration, then by
[

, 11.8.30]

K

\upomega
-free
\Leftrightarrow\ K=\omega\big(\Uplambda(K)\big){}^{\downarrow}\cup\sigma\big(\Upgamma(K)\big){}^{\uparrow}

The next lemma also mentions the differential field extension

of

, as well as linear differential operators over differential fields like

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose
K
is an
H
-field,
\Gamma\neq\{0\}
,
r\geqslant 2
, and
K
has
r
-
\operatorname{DIVP}
. Then the following hold, with
(i), (ii), (iii)
using only the case
r=2
:

(i)

K=\omega(K)\cup\sigma(K^{>})=\omega\big(\Uplambda(K)\big){}^{\downarrow}\cup\sigma\big(\Upgamma(K)\big){}^{\uparrow}
;

(ii)

K
is
\upomega
-free and
\omega(K)=\omega\big(\Uplambda(K)\big){}^{\downarrow}
;

(iii)

for all
a\in K
the operator
\der^{2}-a
splits over
K[\imag]
;

(iv)

K
is
r
-newtonian.

Proof.

For (i) we use the end of
[
3
, Section 11.7]
to replace
K
with a compositional conjugate so that
0\in\Psi
. Then
K
has small derivation,
and we have
a\in K^{>}
such that
a\not\asymp 1
and
a^{\dagger}\asymp 1
. Replacing
a
by
a^{-1}
if necessary this gives

a^{\dagger}=-\phi
with
\phi\asymp 1
,
\phi>0
, so
a\prec 1
. Then
\phi^{-1}a^{\dagger}=-1
; replacing
K
by
K^{\phi}
and renaming the latter as
K
this means
a^{\dagger}=-1
. Let
f\in K
; to get
f\in\omega\big(\Uplambda(K)\big){}^{\downarrow}\cup\sigma\big(\Upgamma(K)\big){}^{\uparrow}
, note first that
1=(1/a)^{\dagger}\in\Upgamma(K)
, so

0\in\Uplambda(K)
. Also
\omega\big(\Uplambda(K)\big){}^{\downarrow}\subseteq\omega(K)
by Lemma
2.3
.

If
f\leqslant 0
, then
\omega(0)=0
gives
f\in\omega\big(\Uplambda(K)\big){}^{\downarrow}
.
So assume
f>0
;
we first show that then
f\in\sigma(K^{>})
. Now for
y\in K^{>}
,
f=\sigma(y)
is equivalent (by multiplying with
y^{2}
) to
P(y)=0
, where

P(Y)\ :=\ 2YY^{\prime\prime}-3(Y^{\prime})^{2}+Y^{4}-fY^{2}\in K\{Y\}.

See also
[
3
, Section 13.7]
. We have
P(0)=0
and
P(y)\to+\infty
as
y\to+\infty
(because of the term
y^{4}
). In view of
2
-DIVP it will suffice to show that
for some
y>0
in
K
we have
P(y)<0
. Now with

y\in K^{>}
and
z:=-y^{\dagger}
we have

\displaystyle P(y)\

\displaystyle=\ y^{2}\big(\sigma(y)-f\big)\ =\ y^{2}\big(\omega(z)+y^{2}-f\big),\text{ hence}

\displaystyle P(a)\

\displaystyle=\ a^{2}\big(\omega(1)+a^{2}-f\big)\ =\ a^{2}(-1+a^{2}-f)\ <\ 0,

so
f\in\sigma(K^{>})
. By the second inclusion of Lemma
2.4

this yields
f\in\omega\big(\Uplambda(K)\big){}^{\downarrow}
or

f\in\sigma\big(\Upgamma(K)^{\uparrow}\big)
. But we have
\sigma\big(\Upgamma(K)^{\uparrow}\big)\subseteq\sigma\big(\Upgamma(K)\big){}^{\uparrow}
, because
\sigma
is increasing on
\Upgamma(K)^{\uparrow}
by the remark preceding
[
3
, 11.8.30]
. This concludes the proof of (i), and then
(ii) follows, using for its second part also the fact stated just before

[
3
, 11.8.29]
that we have
\omega(K)<\sigma\big(\Upgamma(K)\big)
.

Now (iii) follows from
K=\omega(K)\cup\sigma(K^{>})
by
[
3
, Section 5.2, (5.2.1)]
. As to (iv),
let
P\in K\{Y\}
of order at most
r
have Newton degree
1
; we have to show that
P
has a zero in
\mathcal{O}
.
For this we repeat the argument in the proof of Lemma
2.3

so that it applies to our
P
, using
\upomega
-freeness instead of
\uplambda
-freeness,
[
3
, Proposition 13.3.13]
instead of
[
3
, Proposition 13.3.6]
, and
r
-DIVP instead of
1
-DIVP.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

If
K
is an
H
-field,

, and

\operatorname{DIVP}

Theorem 2.7
.

Let

\text{$K$ has $\operatorname{DIVP}$}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \text{$K$ is $\upomega$-free and newtonian.}

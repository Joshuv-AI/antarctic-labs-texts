A characterization of graphs with small palette index
Domenico Labbate, Davide Mattiolo, Giuseppe Mazzuoccolo, Federico Romaniello, Gloria Tabarelli

Abstract
Given an edge-coloring of a graph G G , we associate to every vertex v v of G G the set of colors appearing on the edges incident with v v . The palette index of G G is defined as the minimum number of such distinct sets, taken over all possible edge-colorings of G G . A graph with a small palette index admits an edge-coloring which can be locally considered to be almost symmetric, since few different sets of colors appear around its vertices. Graphs with palette index 1 1 are r r -regular graphs admitting an r r -edge-coloring, while regular graphs with palette index 2 2 do not exist. Here, we characterize all graphs with palette index either 2 2 or 3 3 in terms of the existence of suitable decompositions in regular subgraphs. As a corollary, we obtain a complete characterization of regular graphs with palette index 3 3 . Keywords: edge-colouring, graphs, palette index, palettes. Math. Subj. Class.: 05C15

Introduction

A classical and well-known problem in graph theory is to find a coloring of the edges of a graph, using as few colors as possible, such that no two incident edges receive the same color.
In this paper, we consider a related problem, first introduced in
[
8
]
, where the problem of minimizing the number of different sets of colors appearing on the edges around each vertex is proposed. More precisely, a
k
-edge-coloring
of a graph
G
is a map
c\colon E(G)\to\{1,2,...,k\}
such that for any pair of incident edges
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
,
c(e_{1})\neq c(e_{2})
. If a graph
G
has maximum degree
\Delta
and it admits a
\Delta
-edge-coloring, then it is said to be a
Class 1
graph, otherwise it is called a
Class 2
graph. A Class 1 regular graph admits an edge-coloring with the same set of colors around each vertex and hence, to some extent, it locally admits a very symmetric edge-coloring. We denote by
\partial_{G}(v)
the set of edges incident with a vertex
v\in V(G)
and we define the
palette
of
v
with respect to an edge-coloring

as
P_{c}(v)=\{c(e)\mid e\in\partial_{G}(v)\}
. Note that if the degree of a vertex

is
d_{G}(v)=0
, i.e. if
\partial_{G}(v)=\emptyset
, then
P_{c}(v)=\emptyset
. The

, denoted by
\check{s}(G)
(see
[
8
]

is the minimum number of distinct palettes, taken over all edge-colorings, occurring among the vertices of the graph. Graphs with small palette index are in some sense close to be Class 1, since they admit an edge-coloring with few different sets of colors around each vertex, that is an edge-coloring which can be locally considered almost symmetric.

The palette index of a graph was first introduced in
[
8
]
for simple graphs, but the generalization to graphs with multiple edges is straightforward (see
[
1
]
).
It is an easy consequence of the definition that a graph

if and only if it is a Class 1 regular graph.
In paper
[

]
, the authors determine the palette index of complete graphs, and they furnish the following further results in the regular case:

(

]
)

Let

Further results on the palette index of a graph appeared since then (see, e.g.,

On the other hand, very few results on the palette index of general graphs appeared in the literature until now. A recent one is proved by some of the authors in

In this paper, in the very same spirit, we consider the problem of relating the palette index of a graph

2
Notation and preliminary results

In the course of the paper, we will make use both of graphs and hypergraphs, where a graph is nothing but a hypergraph with all edges of cardinality two. Every time we refer to a hypergraph, we mean that it may admit both parallel hyperedges (i.e., hyperedges on the same subset of vertices) and loops (i.e., hyperedges of cardinality one), whereas we consider only graphs without loops.
For basic notation not defined here, we refer to
[
2
]
.

Recall that a
decomposition
of a graph
G
is a family
\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of subgraphs of
G
such that
E(H_{i})\neq\emptyset
for every
i\in I
,
\bigcup_{i\in I}E(H_{i})=E(G)
and
E(H_{i})\cap E(H_{j})=\emptyset
for every

We denote by
\mathcal{C}=\{1,2,...,k\}
the set of colors and by
\mathcal{P}_{c}=\{P_{1},P_{2},...,P_{t}\}
the set of distinct palettes that

induces among the vertices of

. Observe that the empty palette belongs to the set
\mathcal{P}_{c}
if and only if

has some isolated vertices. For each color

we define
E_{i}=\{e\in E(G)\mid c(e)=i\}
, and for each palette

we define
V_{j}=\{v\in V(G)\mid P_{c}(v)=P_{j}\}
. Finally, if

, we denote by

the subgraph of

induced by all the edges
e\in E(G)
such that

.

Remark 1
.

Let

. The following statements are equivalent:

•

G[X]
is an
|X|
-regular Class 1 subgraph of

•

for all

The following definition will be largely used in what follows.

Definition 4

A

In other words, an edge-coloring of a graph

Moreover, it is remarked in

that an

(see the top of Figure

Observe that if

\check{s}
-minimal edge-coloring of

Remark 2
.

If

\check{s}
-minimal edge-coloring of

, then any two hyperedges

Although it is not needed for proving our main results, we conclude this section by showing an interesting relation between the hypergraph
H_{G}^{c}
and the concept of
H
-coloring of a graph
G
.
Recall that, if
G
and
H
are graphs, an
H
-coloring of
G
is an edge-coloring

such that for every vertex
u\in V(G)
there exists a vertex

with

The concept of

-coloring is mainly known in relation to the Petersen coloring Conjecture by Jaeger (see
[
9
]
), which states that every bridgeless cubic graph admits a

-coloring, where

is the Petersen graph. The conjecture is largely open, and it is known that it implies many other well-known conjectures such as the Cycle Double Cover Conjecture and the Berge-Fulkerson Conjecture (see

11
]
for more details on such conjectures and implications).

There is no obstruction to define

as the set of all hyperedges incident with the vertex

of a hypergraph

to be a hypergraph in the definition of

In our context, it is straightforward that the map

, such that

, is an

-coloring of

. Then, an alternative description of the palette index of a graph directly follows.

Proposition 5
.

The palette index
\check{s}(G)
of a graph

is equal to the order of a smallest hypergraph

such that
G
admits an
H
-coloring.

3
Graphs with palette index at most 3

In this section, we provide a complete characterization of graphs with palette index at most
3
. First of all, in Theorem
6
, we characterize graphs with palette index equal to
2
and then, in Theorem
7
, we characterize graphs with palette index at most
3

Note that the number of such subgraphs depends on the graph

In order to improve the readability of the following proofs, we always describe each subgraph

is actually present in the decomposition.

\check{s}(G)=3

Equivariant ideals of polynomials
Arka Ghosh, Sławomir Lasota

Abstract
. We study existence and computability of finite bases for ideals of polynomials over infinitely many variables. In our setting, variables come from a countable logical structure A, and embeddings from A to A act on polynomials by renaming variables. First, we give a sufficient and necessary condition for A to guarantee the following generalisation of Hilbert’s Basis Theorem: every polynomial ideal which is equivariant, i.e. invariant under renaming of variables, is finitely generated. Second, we develop an extension of classical Buchberger’s algorithm to compute a Gröbner basis of a given equivariant ideal. This implies decidability of the membership problem for equivariant ideals. Finally, we sketch upon various applications of these results to register automata, Petri nets with data, orbit-finitely generated vector spaces, and orbit-finite systems of linear equations.



1.
Introduction

A ring is
Noetherian
if each of its ideals
is finitely-generated.
According to Hilbert’s Basis Theorem,
the ring of polynomials over a finite set of variables,
with coefficients from a given Noetherian ring, is Noetherian.
In particular, every polynomial ideal can be represented by its finite basis, and hence can be given as input to algorithms.
Given such a finite basis, Buchberger’s algorithm computes a Gröbner basis that can be used to decide
the
membership problem for polynomial ideals: determine, whether a given polynomial belongs to the
ideal generated by a given set of polynomials.
Hilbert’s Basis Theorem and Gröbner bases have important algorithmic applications, for instance
to decide zeroness of polynomial automata (weighted automata with
polynomial updates)
(
Benedikt et al. 2017
)
,
reachability of reversible Petri nets
(
Mayr and Meyer 1982
)
, or
bisimulation equivalence checking
(
Stríbrná 1997
)
.
Other applications of Buchberger’s algorithm are overviewed in
(
Buchberger and Winkler 1998
)
.

We generalise the above classical results to the ring of polynomials
over an infinite set of variables.
Clearly,
Hilbert’s Basis Theorem is not generalisable literally, since every polynomial belonging to
a finitely-generated ideal may use at least one of variables used by the generators.
In this paper we investigate the following setting:

•

Variables are elements of a countable relational structure
\mathcal{A}

(which we call
domain of variables
).

•

The monoid of embeddings
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
acts on polynomials by renaming variables.

•

We restrict to polynomial ideals which are invariant under renaming (such ideals we call

equivariant
).
Accordingly, we adapt the definition of basis, enforcing equivariance of a generated ideal.

Example 1.

For illustration, consider
equality
domain of variables, namely
the structure
\mathcal{A}=(A,=)
consisting of a countable infinite set

A
and the equality relation.
Embeddings are injective mappings
A\to A
.
Consider the ring
\mathbb{R}[\mathcal{A}]
of polynomials with real coefficients over variables
A
.
The subset
\mathcal{I}\subseteq\mathbb{R}[\mathcal{A}]
of polynomials whose coefficients sum up to 0, is an equivariant ideal.
It is the least ideal containing
all univariate polynomials

p_{a}(a)=a-1
, where
a\in A
.
For instance, the polynomial
p(a,b,c)=ab^{2}-bc
with variables

a,b,c\in A
belongs to
\mathcal{I}
, and decomposes as:

ab^{2}-bc\ =\ (b-1)(ab+a-c)+(a-1)-(c-1).

\mathcal{I}
is also the least
equivariant
ideal including
\left\{p_{a}\right\}
,
for a fixed
a\in A
,
hence finitely-generated according to our adapted definition.

Contribution.

We investigate equivariant ideals of polynomials over domains of variables
which are
totally ordered
, namely admit a total order as one of its relations, and

well structured
, namely admit a naturally defined

well quasi order
(as defined in Section
2
).
A prototypical example of such a domain is rational order
\mathcal{Q}=(Q,\leq_{\mathcal{Q}})
.
For such domains
\mathcal{A}
we prove, in Section
3
,
the following generalisation of Hilbert’s Basis Theorem:
for every Noetherian ring
\mathbb{K}
, every equivariant ideal in
the polynomial ring
\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]

has a finite basis.
We transfer this property to lexicographic products of totally ordered, well structured domains,
and to reducts thereof,
including the equality domain. We also show that well structure is necessary for the generalisation of Hilbert’s Basis Theorem to hold.

For further investigations we restrict to domains of variables which are

well ordered
, well structured, and

computable
(as formalized in Section
5
).
We develop a generalisation of Buchberger’s algorithm to compute a Gröbner basis of an
equivariant ideal, given as a finite set of generators.
As a corollary, in Section
7
we obtain
decidability of the membership problem for equivariant ideals.
Similarly as our generalisation of Hilbert’s Basis Theorem, we transfer
the decidability result to a wide range of other domains,
including equality domain, rational order, and lexicographic products thereof.

Our results are of fundamental nature, with various potential applications.
We sketch upon some of them.
In Section
4
we apply our generalisation of Hilbert’s Basis Theorem to prove
that orbit-finitely generated vector spaces have finite length, i.e., admit no infinite increasing chains
of equivariant subspaces.
This generalises a crucial result of
(
Bojańczyk et al. 2021
)

(however, without explicit upper bound).
As a consequence, we derive decidability of
the zeroness problem for weighted register automata, again
generalising a result of
(
Bojańczyk et al. 2021
)
.
We also apply, in Section
8
, the ideal membership problem
to decide reachability in reversible Petri nets with data, for a wide range of data domains.
This includes rational order domain, where the problem is known to be undecidable
for general non-reversible Petri nets
(
Lazic et al. 2007
)
.
Finally,
in Section
9
, we apply the ideal membership problem
to solving orbit-finite systems of linear equations.
This generalises a core result of
(
Ghosh et al. 2022
)
to a wide range of domains
(again, without complexity upper bound).
All our proofs of generalisations of
(
Bojańczyk et al. 2021
)
and
(
Ghosh et al. 2022
)

are remarkably simple, compared to the original ones.

Related research.

Our motivations, together with the most fundamental concepts such as equivariance and orbit-finiteness,
come from the theory of sets with atoms

)

The concept of well structured domain, crucial for development of this paper,

There have been

First,

)

(

)
.
Similar results were independently obtained in
(
Aschenbrenner and Hillar 2007

;

)
.
In
(
Hillar and Sullivant 2012

the authors use the aforementioned results to prove the Independent Set Conjecture in algebraic statistics.

There are a number of recent results concerning finite generation of vector spaces:

(

shows that every equivariant subspace of orbit-finitely generated vector space is finitely generated

Preliminaries

A relational structure
\mathcal{A}=(A,r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})

is a set
A
together with finitely many relations

r_{1}\subseteq A^{s_{1}},\ldots,r_{n}\subseteq A^{s_{n}}
of finite arities
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
,
respectively.
In the sequel we restrict to countable structures only.
Every relation
r\subseteq A^{m}
which is expressible as a Boolean combination of relations

r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
, we call
definable
.
We always assume that equality
=
is definable.
An
induced substructure
of
\mathcal{A}
is the restriction of
\mathcal{A}
to some subset of
A
.

An
embedding
of
\mathcal{A}
is a map
\iota:A\to A
that preserves and reflects all relations,
which means that for every relation
r_{i}
and

a_{1},\ldots,a_{s_{i}}\in A
, the following equivalence holds:

r_{i}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{s_{i}})\iff r_{i}(\iota(a_{1}),\ldots,\iota(a_{s_{i}})).

In other words, an embedding
\iota
is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{A}
and its substructure
induced by
\iota(A)
.
Clearly, embeddings preserve and reflect all definable relations, and hence each such relation
can be considered w.l.o.g. to be among
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
.
The set of all embeddings of
\mathcal{A}
we denote as
\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}
.
We say that
\mathcal{A}
is
totally ordered
(resp.
well ordered
)
if a total order
(resp. a well order) is definable in
\mathcal{A}
.

Speaking intuitively, a
reduct
of
\mathcal{A}
is a structure obtained by removing
some relations from
\mathcal{A}
.
Formally,
a structure
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=(A,r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})
is a reduct of
\mathcal{A}=(A,\ldots)
, with the same carrier
set
A
,
if all relations
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
are definable in
\mathcal{A}
.

Example 1.

For a countable infinite set
A
, embeddings of the equality structure
\mathcal{A}=(A,=)
,
with equality as the only relation, are
all injective maps
A\to A
.
Embeddings of the rational order
\mathcal{Q}=(Q,\leq_{\mathcal{Q}})
are all strictly monotonic
maps
\iota:Q\to Q
, i.e. functions satisfying

q<_{\mathcal{Q}}q^{\prime}\implies\iota(q)<_{\mathcal{Q}}\iota(q^{\prime}).

Note that equality is definable using
\leq
, namely
q=q^{\prime}\iff q\leq q^{\prime}\wedge q^{\prime}\leq q
.
The equality structure is thus a reduct of
\mathcal{Q}
.

Every ordinal
\alpha
can be seen as a (well ordered) relational structure, with just one binary relation –
the ordinal order.
Again, its embeddings are strictly monotonic maps
\alpha\to\alpha
.
A special case is
\omega=(\mathbb{N},\leq)=\left\{0<1<\ldots\right\}
;
or
\langle d\rangle=\left\{0<1<\ldots<d{-}1\right\}

, where the only embedding

In the sequel we identify an ordinal with the corresponding relational structure.

Noetherianity.

Recall that an

in a commutative ring
\mathbb{K}
is any nonempty subset

closed under addition and multiplication by any element of
\mathbb{K}

A basis of

such that every

is obtainable from

by addition and multiplication by arbitrary elements of

equivalently,

.
A commutative ring is

if each of its ideals has a finite basis.
In particular, each field is a Noetherian commutative ring.

We need some definitions before proving Theorems

We define three quasi orders on monomials, related by the following refinements:

\textstyle{f\preceq_{\text{\tiny lex}}f^{\prime}\ }
\textstyle{\ f\mid f^{\prime}\ \ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ f\sqsubseteq f^{\prime}.}

The two left ones are standard orders.
Given two monomials

is defined by

if

for some

In order to define

Lemma 6.

For every monomials

f\mid f^{\prime}\implies f\preceq_{\text{\tiny lex}}f^{\prime}\implies f\cdot g\preceq_{\text{\tiny lex}}f^{\prime}\cdot g.

The quasi order

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} f\sqsubseteq f^{\prime}&\iff\exists\iota\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}:\iota(f)\mid f^{\prime}.\end{aligned}

We argue that (

Indeed, as action of embeddings preserves the division order, namely

\displaystyle f\mid f^{\prime}

\displaystyle\implies\kappa(f)\mid\kappa(f^{\prime})

\sqsubseteq

\iota(f)\mid f^{\prime}\ \wedge\ \kappa(f^{\prime})\mid f^{\prime\prime}\ \implies\ (\kappa\circ\iota)(f)\mid\kappa(f^{\prime})\mid f^{\prime\prime}.

\text{\sc Mon}[\mathcal{A}]
may be identified with
\text{\sc Age}(\mathcal{A},\mathbb{N}\setminus 0)

\sqsubseteq
with the

on

\mathcal{A}
is
\omega

have the same degree
D=1+2+\ldots+2^{d}=2^{d+1}-1
,
understood as the sum of degrees of all variables.
Therefore
\iota(\text{\sc enc}(t_{i}))\mid\text{\sc enc}(t_{j})
implies
\iota(\text{\sc enc}(t_{i}))=\text{\sc enc}(t_{j})
.
The encoding commutes with action of embeddings:

\iota(\text{\sc enc}(t))=\text{\sc enc}(\iota(t))
.
This implies
\text{\sc enc}(\iota(t_{i}))=\text{\sc enc}(t_{j})
, and by injectivity of
\text{\sc enc}(\_)
also

\iota(t_{i})=t_{j}
, thus contradicting
(*)
.
∎

Finite length.

Theorem
3
generalises
(
Bojańczyk et al. 2021
, Lemma IV.8)
which only considers

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{Q}=(Q,\leq_{\mathcal{Q}})
.
On the other hand,
(
Bojańczyk et al. 2021
)
provides explicite upper bound on the length.

Theorem 3 (Finite length).

Let
\mathbb{K}
be any field.
If
\mathcal{A}
is a reduct of an
\omega
-well structured and totally ordered structure then
strictly increasing chains of equivariant subspaces of
\text{\sc Lin}(\mathcal{A}^{d})
are finite.

Proof.

Consider any increasing chain of equivariant subspaces

\mathcal{V}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{2}\subseteq\ldots

and let
\mathcal{I}_{i}
be the ideal in
\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
generated
(in the classical sense) by
\text{\sc enc}(\mathcal{V}_{i})
, the encodings
of vectors from
\mathcal{V}_{i}
.
As action of embeddings commutes with encoding,
equivariance of subspaces
\mathcal{V}_{i}
implies equivariance of the sets
\text{\sc enc}(\mathcal{V}_{i})
.
As action of embeddings commutes with addition and multiplication of polynomials,
ideals
\mathcal{I}_{i}
are equivariant.
By Theorem
2
and Lemma
3
, the chain

\mathcal{I}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{I}_{2}\subseteq\ldots

stabilises, namely,
\mathcal{I}_{i}=\mathcal{I}_{j}
for all sufficiently large
i,j
.
We claim:

Claim 4.

\mathcal{I}_{i}=\mathcal{I}_{j}
implies
\text{\sc enc}(\mathcal{V}_{i})=\text{\sc enc}(\mathcal{V}_{j})
.

Having the claim we immediately complete the proof, as

\text{\sc enc}(\mathcal{V}_{i})=\text{\sc enc}(\mathcal{V}_{j})
implies
\mathcal{V}_{i}=\mathcal{V}_{j}
, by injectivity of
\text{\sc enc}(\_)
.

For demonstrating the claim it is sufficient to prove the inclusion

\text{\sc enc}(\mathcal{V}_{i})\supseteq\text{\sc enc}(\mathcal{V}_{j})
.
Given an arbitrary polynomial
f\in\text{\sc enc}(\mathcal{V}_{j})
, we thus prove
f\in\text{\sc enc}(\mathcal{V}_{i})
.
As
\mathcal{I}_{i}=\mathcal{I}_{j}
, we have
f\in\mathcal{I}_{i}
, i.e.,

(13)

\displaystyle f\ =\ r_{1}\cdot h_{1}\cdot\text{\sc enc}(v_{1})+\ldots+r_{n}\cdot h_{n}\cdot\text{\sc enc}(v_{n})

for some vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in\mathcal{V}_{i}
, monomials

h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\in\text{\sc Mon}[\mathbb{K}]
and ring elements
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\in\mathbb{K}
.
As all monomials used in encodings have the same degree
D
,
all monomials
g
appearing in

\text{\sc enc}(v_{1}),\ldots,\text{\sc enc}(v_{n})
have degree
\text{\sc deg}\,g=D
, as well as all monomials appearing
in
f
.
This means that all the monomials of degree larger than
D
,
obtained as a multiplication
h_{i}\cdot g
, forcedly cancel out in the final sum
(
13
).
Formally (
\mathbf{0}
is the zero polynomial):

\sum_{i\in I}r_{i}\cdot h_{i}\cdot\text{\sc enc}(v_{i})\ =\ \mathbf{0},

where the sum ranges over those
i
where
h_{i}
has positive degree:

I=\left\{i\in\left\{1\ldots n\right\}\,\middle|\,\text{\sc deg}\,h_{i}>0\right\}.

All polynomials
r_{i}\cdot h_{i}\cdot\text{\sc enc}(v_{i})
, for
i\in I
, can be thus safely removed.
As
\text{\sc deg}\,h_{i}=0
exactly when
h_{i}
is a unit monomial, we end up with

f\ =\ \sum_{i\notin I}r_{i}\cdot\text{\sc enc}(v_{i})\ =\ \text{\sc enc}(\sum_{i\notin I}r_{i}\cdot v_{i}),

the latter equality following from linearity of
\text{\sc enc}(\_)
.
Therefore
f\in\text{\sc enc}(\mathcal{V}_{i})
, as required.
∎

Weighted Register Automata.

Using Theorem
3

we obtain a generalisation
of another result of
(
Bojańczyk et al. 2021
)
:
decidability of zeroness of weighted register automata.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
Q_{d}=A^{d}\cup\{\bot\}
.
The auxiliary element
\bot
is assumed to be equivariant, namely

\iota(\bot)=\bot
for all
\iota\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}
.
The vector space
\text{\sc Lin}(Q_{d})
is defined like
\text{\sc Lin}(A^{d})
,
as the set of finite formal sums of elements of
Q_{d}
.

Classically, transition function of a weighted automaton with finite state-space
Q

maps every input symbol and state to a vector over states:

\Sigma\times Q\to\text{\sc Lin}(Q)
.
Our definition of weighted
d
-register automaton
(equivalent to the definition of
(
Bojańczyk et al. 2021
)
) is along the same lines,
except that input alphabet is
\Sigma=A
,
state-space is orbit-finite
Q=Q_{d}
,
and transition function is equivariant.

A weighted
d
-register automaton consists of a
transition
function

\delta:A\times Q_{d}\to\text{\sc Lin}(Q_{d})
and a
final
function

F:Q_{d}\to\mathbb{K}
, both equivariant, namely for every
a\in A
,
q\in Q_{d}
,
and
\iota\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}
:

\delta(\iota(a),\iota(q))\ =\ \iota(\delta(a,q))\qquad F(\iota(a))\ =\ F(a).

F
extends uniquely to a linear map
F:\text{\sc Lin}(Q_{d})\to\mathbb{K}
.
Likewise, for every
a\in A
, the function
\delta(a,\_):Q_{d}\to\text{\sc Lin}(Q_{d})
extends
uniquely to a linear map
\widetilde{\delta}(a):\text{\sc Lin}(Q_{d})\to\text{\sc Lin}(Q_{d})
.
We overload
\bot
and use it for vector defined by
the singleton formal sum
\bot\in\text{\sc Lin}(Q_{d})
, the
initial
vector.
The semantics of a weighted register automaton

(d,\delta,F)

maps every word
w=a_{1}\ldots a_{\ell}\in A^{*}
to the
final
vector

\widetilde{\delta}(w)\ =\ \widetilde{\delta}(a_{\ell})\circ\ldots\circ\widetilde{\delta}(a_{1})(\bot)\ \in\ \text{\sc Lin}(Q_{d}),

on which
F
is evaluated to get the
value

F(\widetilde{\delta}(w))\in\mathbb{K}

assigned by the automaton to
w
.
By equivariance of
\bot
,
\delta
and
F
, the function

\widetilde{\delta}
is equivariant and
the value is stable under the
pointwise action of
{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}
on words,

\iota(a_{1}\ldots a_{\ell})=\iota(a_{1})\ldots\iota(a_{\ell})
.

Lemma 5.

F(\widetilde{\delta}(\iota(w)))=F(\widetilde{\delta}(w))
for every
\iota\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}
.

The
zeroness
problem asks, given a weighted register automaton
(d,\delta,F)
,
whether it is
zeroing
, i.e., whether it assigns value 0 to all input words.
When an automaton is input to an algorithm,
each of the functions
\delta
,
F
is given by its values on a
(naturally defined) presentation of its domain.
We prove that zeroness is characterised by a finite testing set:

Theorem 6.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be any field and

\mathcal{A}
be a reduct of an
\omega
-well structured and totally ordered structure.
For every weighted register automaton there is a finite set
T\subseteq A^{*}

such that the automaton is zeroing if and only if
it assigs value 0 to all words in
T
.

Proof.

Consider a weighted register automaton
(d,\delta,F)
.
For
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
A^{\leq i}=A^{0}\cup\ldots\cup A^{i}
, and

\mathcal{V}_{i}\subseteq\text{\sc Lin}(Q_{d})
be the vector space spanned by

V_{i}=\widetilde{\delta}(A^{\leq i})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\left\{\widetilde{\delta}(w)\,\middle|\,w\in A^{\leq i}\right\}
.
Let
B_{i}
be a presentation of
A^{i}
, and let

B_{\leq i}=B_{0}\cup\ldots\cup B_{i}
.
Therefore

(14)

\displaystyle A^{\leq i}={\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(B_{\leq i}),

and
by equivariance of
\widetilde{\delta}
we have:

(15)

\displaystyle V_{i}={\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\widetilde{\delta}(B_{\leq i})).

Thus the sets
V_{i}
are equivariant, and so are the subspaces
\mathcal{V}_{i}
.
As an immediate consequence of Theorem
3
we obtain:

Claim
7
.

Strictly increasing chains of equivariant subspaces of
\text{\sc Lin}(Q_{d})
are finite.

By the claim, the chain

\mathcal{V}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{1}\subseteq\ldots,

stabilises at some
n
:

(16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{n}=\mathcal{V}_{n+1}=\ldots.

Since the transformation
\mathcal{V}_{i}\mapsto\mathcal{V}_{i+1}
does not depend on
i
, the
stabilisation (
16
) is guaranteed once
\mathcal{V}_{n}=\mathcal{V}_{n+1}
.
Zeroness of
(d,\delta,F)
is then equivalent to

F(\widetilde{\delta}(w))=0
for all
w\in A^{\leq n}
.
Using (
14
) and Lemma
5
we refine the characterisation:

Claim 8.

Zeroness of
(d,\delta,F)
is equivalent to

F(\widetilde{\delta}(w))=0
for all
w\in B_{\leq n}
.

Hence
T=B_{\leq n}
satisfies
Theorem
6
, which is thus proved.
∎

Remark 9.

Under mild computability assumptions on
\mathbb{K}
and
\mathcal{A}
(omitted here for simplicity, but satisfied
by all
\omega
-well structured domains mentioned so far),
this characterisation yields decidability of the zeroness problem.
This generalises
(
Bojańczyk et al. 2021
, Lemma VIII.1)
, but
without providing complexity upper bound.
Indeed, a stabilisation point
n
(
16
) can be computed as follows:
enumerate in parallel, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, linear combinations of vectors from
V_{n}
,
and stop when

\widetilde{\delta}(B_{\leq n+1})\ \subseteq\ V_{n}.

Indeed, by (
15
), this implies
V_{n+1}\subseteq V_{n}
, and hence

\mathcal{V}_{n+1}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{n}
.
Once a stabilisation point
n
is computed, zeroness of an automaton is
decided by computing the value assigned to all words in
B_{\leq n}
.

5.
Computability assumptions

We now formulate computability assumptions on a field
\mathbb{K}
and structure
\mathcal{A}

which are necessary in subsequent sections.

A field
\mathbb{K}
is
computable
, i.e., its elements are finitely represented,
and field operations can be computed using this representation.
So is, for instance, the rational field
\mathbb{Q}
, as well as every finite field.

The rest of this section is devoted to spelling out computability assumptions on
an
\omega
-well structured structure
\mathcal{A}=(A,\ldots)
, in
Definition
4
below.
Given
B\in\text{\sc Age}(\mathcal{A})
,
any restriction
{\iota}{|B}
of an embedding to
B

we call
local embedding
of
B
.
It is legitimate to use local embeddings instead of embeddings,
as their action on a polynomial depends only on restriction to its variables:

Lemma 1.

{\iota}{|\text{\sc vars}(f)}={\iota^{\prime}}{|\text{\sc vars}(f)}\implies\iota(f)=\iota^{\prime}(f)
.

By
{{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}}{|B}
we denote the set of all local embeddings of
B
:

{{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}}{|B}\ =\ \left\{{\iota}{|B}\,\middle|\,\iota\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}\right\}.

For
B,B^{\prime}\in\text{\sc Age}(\mathcal{A})
, we consider the Cartesian product

B\boxtimes B^{\prime}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\ ({{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}}{|B})\times({{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}}{|B}^{\prime}).

We extend to this set the action of embeddings:
for
\kappa\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}
and
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in B\boxtimes B^{\prime}
, we put

\kappa(\pi,\pi^{\prime})=(\kappa\circ\pi,\kappa\circ\pi^{\prime})
.
Clearly,
B\boxtimes B^{\prime}
is closed under this action, i.e., it is equivariant.

Lemma 2.

For
B,B^{\prime}\in\text{\sc Age}(\mathcal{A})
,
B\boxtimes B^{\prime}
is orbit-finite, namely

B\boxtimes B^{\prime}={\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(F)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\left\{(\kappa\circ\pi,\kappa\circ\pi^{\prime})\,\middle|\,\kappa\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}},(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in F\right\}
for some finite
F\subseteq B\boxtimes B^{\prime}
.

Any such finite set
F
we call a
presentation
of
B\boxtimes B^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
n=|B|
and
n^{\prime}=|B^{\prime}|
.
The set
B\boxtimes B^{\prime}
is essentially a subset of
\mathcal{A}^{n+n^{\prime}}
.
Formally, having fixed enumerations
B=\left\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\right\}
,

B^{\prime}=\left\{b^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,b^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}}\right\}
, we define an injective mapping

m:B\boxtimes B^{\prime}\to\mathcal{A}^{n+n^{\prime}}
as expected:

(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\ \mapsto\ (\pi(b_{1}),\ldots,\pi(b_{n}),\pi^{\prime}(b^{\prime}_{1}),\ldots,\pi^{\prime}(b^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}})).

The mapping is equivariant, namely commutes with the action of embeddings:

m(\iota(\pi,\pi^{\prime}))\ =\ \iota(m(\pi,\pi^{\prime}))

for every
\iota\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}
.
Therefore, equivariance of
B\boxtimes B^{\prime}
implies equivariance of

m(B\boxtimes B^{\prime})
.
By Lemma
2
, the set
\mathcal{A}^{n+n^{\prime}}
is orbit-finite.
Importantly, the same proof shows that
every
equivariant subset of
\mathcal{A}^{n+n^{\prime}}
is orbit-finite.
Therefore,
m(B\boxtimes B^{\prime})={\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(F)
for some finite set
F
.
We use injectivity and equivariance of
m
to deduce that
m^{-1}(F)
is a finite presentation of

B\boxtimes B^{\prime}
.
∎

Example 3.

For illustration,
consider the structure
{\omega=(\mathbb{N},\leq)}
,
whose embeddings are strictly monotonic maps
\omega\to\omega
, and

B=\left\{2\right\}
and
B^{\prime}=\left\{1,3\right\}
.
A presentation of
B\boxtimes B^{\prime}
is given by
F
containing five pairs

(\pi,\pi^{\prime})
of local embeddings, corresponding to five cases:

\pi(2)
is smaller
than
\pi^{\prime}(1)
, or equal to
\pi^{\prime}(1)
, or between
\pi^{\prime}(1)
and
\pi^{\prime}(3)
, or equal to
\pi^{\prime}(3)
,
or larger than
\pi^{\prime}(3)
:

\displaystyle\pi_{1}:2{\mapsto}2

\displaystyle\quad\pi^{\prime}_{1}:(1,3){\mapsto}(3,5)

\displaystyle\quad\pi_{4}:2{\mapsto}3

\displaystyle\quad\pi^{\prime}_{4}:(1,3){\mapsto}(1,3)

\displaystyle\pi_{2}:2{\mapsto}2

\displaystyle\quad\pi^{\prime}_{2}:(1,3){\mapsto}(2,4)

\displaystyle\quad\pi_{5}:2{\mapsto}4

\displaystyle\quad\pi^{\prime}_{5}:(1,3){\mapsto}(1,3)

\displaystyle\pi_{3}:2{\mapsto}2

\displaystyle\quad\pi^{\prime}_{3}:(1,3){\mapsto}(1,3)

The following two pairs are not in
F
, for different reasons:

\sigma_{1}:2{\mapsto}4\quad\sigma^{\prime}_{1}:(1,3){\mapsto}(1,4)\qquad\sigma_{2}:2{\mapsto}3\quad\sigma^{\prime}_{2}:(1,3){\mapsto}(2,3).

The left pair
(\sigma_{1},\sigma^{\prime}_{1})

, namely

(\sigma_{1},\sigma^{\prime}_{1})=\kappa(\pi_{4},\pi^{\prime}_{4})
where

\kappa(0)=0,\kappa(1)=1
,
\kappa(2)=2
, and
\kappa(n)=n+1
for
n\geq 3
.
The right pair does not even belong to
B\boxtimes B^{\prime}
, as
\sigma^{\prime}_{2}
does not extend to an embedding.

\triangleleft

Definition 4.

We say that an
\omega
-well structured domain
\mathcal{A}

is
computable
if its elements can be finitely represented,
and the following tasks are computable using this representation:

(\textsc{c}_{1})

Given
B\in\text{\sc Age}(\mathcal{A})
and
\pi:B\to A
, decide if
\pi\in{{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}}{|B}
.

(\textsc{c}_{2})

Given
B,B^{\prime}\in\text{\sc Age}(\mathcal{A})
, compute a presentation of
B\boxtimes B^{\prime}
.

In particular, every finite
\mathcal{A}
is computable.

\triangleleft

(Our condition
(\textsc{c}_{2})
is similar to EGB4 in
(
Brouwer and Draisma 2011
)
.)
By reusing the proof of
(
Hillar et al. 2018
, Proposition 3.4)
we can show that the ordinal
\omega
,
seen as a relational structure, is computable.
In fact the proof can be easily extended to ordinals
\alpha
which are
effective
,
by which we mean that
elements of
\alpha
(i.e., ordinals smaller than
\alpha
) are finitely represented, and
ordinal order
<
and addition
+
are computable using this representation.
For example, the ordinals
\omega
,
\omega^{k}
,
\omega^{\omega}
and
\varepsilon_{0}

are effective, using Cantor Normal Form.

Lemma
5
.

Any effective ordinal

, seen as a relational structure, is computable.

Lemma

.

Lexicographic product of two computable domains is computable.

6.
Gröbner bases

In this section we set up the background for deciding the ideal membership problem
in Section
7
.
Let
\mathbb{K}
be a computable field. Let
\mathcal{A}=(A,\preceq,\ldots)
be a computable,

\omega
-well structured and
well ordered
domain
(we thus strengthen the total order assumption).
Therefore, the lexicographic order on monomials is well founded.

Division step.

Let
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
be a set of nonzero polynomials.
Given polynomials
f,f^{\prime}\in\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
,
we write

(17)

\displaystyle f\longrightarrow_{\mathcal{G}}f^{\prime}

if
f^{\prime}
is the remainder of
division
of
f
by some polynomial
\iota(g)
from
{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathcal{G})
.
Formally, we define (
17
) to mean that

(18)

\displaystyle f=r\cdot h\cdot\iota(g)+f^{\prime}

for some
r\in\mathbb{K},h\in\text{\sc Mon}[\mathcal{A}]
,
\iota\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}
and
g\in\mathcal{G}
such that
the leading monomial

\text{\sc lm}(r\cdot h\cdot\iota(g))
(equal to
h\cdot\iota(\text{\sc lm}(g))
)
appears in
f
with the same coefficient as in
r\cdot h\cdot\iota(g)

(namely
r\cdot\text{\sc lc}(g)
).
The monomial
\text{\sc lm}(r\cdot h\cdot\iota(g))
is called the
head
of the division step (
17
).
The head is the largest monomial that disappears from
f
, i.e., does not appear any more
in
f^{\prime}=f-r\cdot h\cdot\iota(g)
.
All monomials in
f
lexicographically larger than the head appear also in
f^{\prime}

with the same coefficients, while
some monomials lexicographically smaller than the head
may be added or removed by a division step.

Recall that
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
denotes the equivariant ideal generated by
\mathcal{G}
.
Using equality (
18
) we deduce:

Lemma 1.

Division step preserves membeship in
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
:
if
f\longrightarrow_{\mathcal{G}}f^{\prime}
then
f\in\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle\iff f^{\prime}\in\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
.

A polynomial
f
is said to be
reduced
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
if there is no

f^{\prime}\in\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
such that
f\longrightarrow_{\mathcal{G}}f^{\prime}
.

Lemma 2.

Let

\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
be a finite set of nonzero polynomials.
Every sequence of division steps

(19)

\displaystyle f_{1}\longrightarrow_{\mathcal{G}}f_{2}\longrightarrow_{\mathcal{G}}f_{3}\longrightarrow_{\mathcal{G}}\ldots

terminates, i.e., ends in a reduced polynomial.

Proof.

By
reduction ab absurdum
:
assuming an infinite sequence of division steps (
19
), we deduce that
the lexicographic order
\preceq_{\text{\tiny lex}}
on monomials
\text{\sc Mon}[\mathcal{A}]
is not well founded.

Given an infinite sequence of division steps (
19
),
we construct an infinite forest
\mathcal{F}
, whose nodes are labelled by monomials,
as the limit of an increasing sequence of finite forests
{\mathcal{F}}_{1},{\mathcal{F}}_{2},\ldots
.
The construction will maintain the invariant that all monomials appearing in
f_{i}
are
among labels of leaves in
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
(we
mark
these leaves, for distinguishing them from other leaves).
The forest
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
consists just of (finitely many) roots,
labeled by monomials appearing in
f_{1}
, all of them marked.
Given
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
, we define
{\mathcal{F}}_{i+1}
by adding new children to the
marked leaf
v
labelled by the head monomial
m
of the division step
f_{1}\longrightarrow_{\mathcal{G}}f_{i+1}
.
The leaves are labelled by monomials that are added by the division step, and all are marked.
The node
v
itself becomes unmarked (even if it remains a leaf), and likewise do all other
leaves labelled by monomials of
f_{i}
that do not appear in
f_{i+1}
.

By the construction of
\mathcal{F}
, every two monomials
m
,
m^{\prime}
related by an edge are in
strict lexicographic order:
m\succ_{\text{\tiny lex}}m^{\prime}
.
As the sequence (
19
) is infinite, the forest is also infinite and, by König’s lemma,
forcedly contains an infinite branch.
This implies existence of an infinite strictly decreasing chain of monomials
with respect to lexicographic order, which is a contradiction.
∎

We write
f\longrightarrow^{*}_{\mathcal{G}}f^{\prime}
if there is some finite sequence of division steps from
f
to
f^{\prime}

and
f^{\prime}
is reduced.

Gröbner basis.

A set
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
is said to be a
Gröbner basis
if for every
f\in\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
there exists
g\in\mathcal{G}
such that
\text{\sc lm}(g)\sqsubseteq\text{\sc lm}(f)
.

Lemma 3.

If
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
is a Gröbner basis then for every polynomial
f\in\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
, the following conditions are equivalent: (1)
f\in\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
; (2)
f\longrightarrow^{*}_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{0}
; (3) every sequence
f\longrightarrow^{*}_{\mathcal{G}}f^{\prime}
ends in
f^{\prime}=\mathbf{0}
.

Proof.

The implication (2)
\implies
(1) holds for any
\mathcal{G}
.
Indeed, a sequence
f\longrightarrow^{*}_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{0}
of division steps
yields a decomposition of
f
into a
sum:

f\ =\ r_{1}\cdot h_{1}\cdot\iota_{1}(g_{1})\ +\ \ldots\ +\ r_{n}\cdot h_{n}\cdot\iota_{n}(g_{n}),

where
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\in\mathbb{K}
,
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in\mathcal{G}
,
h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\in\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
and
\iota_{1},\ldots,\iota_{n}\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}
,
which proves membership of
f
in
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
.

For (1)
\implies
(3),
suppose
f\in\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
, and consider any sequence of division steps starting in
f
.
By Lemma
2
the sequence is necessarily finite, and hence ends in some
reduced polynomial
f^{\prime}
.
By Lemma

,

.
However, as
\mathcal{G}
is a Gröbner basis,
no nonzero polynomial in
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle

.

The last implication (3)
\implies

admits some sequence
of division steps

.
∎

Buchberger’s algorithm.

By the least common multiple of two monomials
f,f^{\prime}\in\text{\sc Mon}[\mathcal{A}]
,
denoted as
\text{\sc lcm}(f,f^{\prime})
, we mean
the least (with respect to divisibility) monomial
g
divisible by both
f
and
f^{\prime}
, i.e. satisfying

f\mid g
and
f^{\prime}\mid g
.

Definition 5 (
S
-polynomials).

Given two distinct polynomials
f,g\in\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
,
f\neq g
,
we define their
S-polynomial

\mathcal{S}(f,g)
as

\mathcal{S}(f,g)\ :=\ \left(\frac{h}{\text{\sc lt}(f)}\cdot f\right)-\left(\frac{h}{\text{\sc lt}(g)}\cdot g\right),

where
h=\text{\sc lcm}(\text{\sc lm}(f),\text{\sc lm}(g))
.
We say that monomial
h
is
cancelled out
in
\mathcal{S}(f,g)
.
For a subset
\mathcal{G}\subseteq{\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]}
of polynomials, we put:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}}\ =\ \bigcup_{(f,g)\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathcal{G}),f\neq g}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\mathcal{S}(f,g).

Expanding
{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathcal{G})=\left\{\iota(g)\,\middle|\,\iota\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}},\ g\in\mathcal{G}\right\}
, we get:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}}\ =\ \left\{\mathcal{S}(\iota(f),\kappa(g))\,\middle|\,\iota,\kappa\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}},\ f,g\in\mathcal{G},\ \iota(f)\neq\kappa(g)\right\}.

As action of embeddings commutes with S-polynomials, namely

\mathcal{S}(\kappa(f),\kappa(g))\ =\ \kappa(\mathcal{S}(f,g))

for every
\kappa\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}
and
f,g\in\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
,
the set
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}}
is closed under the action of
{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}
, i.e. is equivariant:

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}}\ =\ {\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}})
.
Using assumption
(\textsc{c}_{2})
we derive the following key fact:

Lemma 6 (Computable presentation).

For every finite set of polynomials
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
, the set
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}}

is orbit-finite, namely

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}}\ =\ {\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{G}})\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\ \left\{\iota(f)\,\middle|\,\iota\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}},f\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{G}}\right\}

for some presentation
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{G}}\subseteq_{\text{fin}}\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}}
, which is moreover computable.

Proof.

We start by showing orbit-finiteness of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}}
.
As
\mathcal{G}
is finite and
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}}=\bigcup_{g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{S}_{g,g^{\prime}}
, where

\mathcal{S}_{g,g^{\prime}}\ =\ \left\{\mathcal{S}(\iota(g),\iota^{\prime}(g^{\prime}))\,\middle|\,\iota,\iota^{\prime}\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}},\ \iota(g)\neq\iota^{\prime}(g^{\prime})\right\},

it is enough to prove that
\mathcal{S}_{g,g^{\prime}}
is orbit-finite for every two fixed polynomials
g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
.
Let
B=\text{\sc vars}(g)
and
B^{\prime}=\text{\sc vars}(g^{\prime})
, and
let
F_{g,g^{\prime}}
be a finite presentation of
B\boxtimes B^{\prime}

(recall Lemma
2
).
Relying on Lemma
1
,
for a local embedding
\pi\in{{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}}{|B}
we write
\pi(g)
to mean the renaming of
g

by
any
extension of
\pi
to an embedding of
\mathcal{A}
.
Likewise we write
\pi^{\prime}(g^{\prime})
, for
\pi^{\prime}\in{{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}}{|B}^{\prime}
:

\mathcal{S}_{g,g^{\prime}}\ =\ \left\{\mathcal{S}(\pi(g),\pi^{\prime}(g^{\prime}))\,\middle|\,(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in B\boxtimes B^{\prime},\ \pi(g)\neq\pi^{\prime}(g^{\prime})\right\}.

Finally, we define
\mathcal{P}_{g,g^{\prime}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{g,g^{\prime}}
as the subset of
\mathcal{S}_{g,g^{\prime}}
obtained by restricting
to local pairs of embeddings
(\pi,\pi^{\prime})
belonging to
F_{g,g^{\prime}}
:

\mathcal{P}_{g,g^{\prime}}\ =\ \left\{\mathcal{S}(\pi(g),\pi^{\prime}(g^{\prime}))\,\middle|\,(\pi,\pi^{\prime})\in F_{g,g^{\prime}},\ \pi(g)\neq\pi^{\prime}(g^{\prime})\right\}.

As
F
is finite,
\mathcal{P}_{g,g^{\prime}}
is finite as well,
and orbit-finiteness of
\mathcal{S}_{g,g^{\prime}}
follows once we prove that

\mathcal{P}_{g,g^{\prime}}
is a presentation of
\mathcal{S}_{g,g^{\prime}}
:

Claim 7.

\mathcal{S}_{g,g^{\prime}}={\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathcal{P}_{g,g^{\prime}})
.

Proof:

For proving the inclusion

{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathcal{P}_{g,g^{\prime}})\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{g,g^{\prime}}
,
we take any polynomial
\kappa(f)\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathcal{P}_{g,g^{\prime}})
, where
\kappa\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}
and
f=\mathcal{S}(\pi(g),\pi^{\prime}(g^{\prime}))\in\mathcal{P}_{g,g^{\prime}}
,
and argue that
\kappa(f)\in\mathcal{S}_{g,g^{\prime}}
.
As action of embeddings commutes with S-polynomials,
we have

\kappa(\mathcal{S}(\pi(g),\pi^{\prime}(g^{\prime})))\ =\ \mathcal{S}(\kappa(\pi(g)),\kappa(\pi^{\prime}(g^{\prime}))).

As
\kappa
is injective,
knowing
\pi(g)\neq\pi^{\prime}(g^{\prime})
, we also know
\kappa(\pi(g))\neq\kappa(\pi^{\prime}(g^{\prime}))
,
and therefore
\kappa(f)\in\mathcal{S}_{g,g^{\prime}}
.

The opposite inclusion
\mathcal{S}_{g,g^{\prime}}\subseteq{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathcal{P}_{g,g^{\prime}})
is shown similarly.
We take any polynomial
\mathcal{S}(\pi(g),\pi^{\prime}(g^{\prime}))\in\mathcal{S}_{g,g^{\prime}}
.
As
F_{g,g^{\prime}}
is a presentation of
B\boxtimes B^{\prime}
, there is some

(\overline{\pi},\overline{\pi}^{\prime})\in F_{g,g^{\prime}}
and

\kappa\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}
such that

(\pi,\pi^{\prime})=(\kappa\circ\overline{\pi},\kappa\circ\overline{\pi}^{\prime})
.
Knowing
\kappa(\pi(g))\neq\kappa(\pi^{\prime}(g^{\prime}))
, we also know
\pi(g)\neq\pi^{\prime}(g^{\prime})
,
and therefore
\mathcal{S}(\overline{\pi}(g),\overline{\pi}^{\prime}(g^{\prime}))\in\mathcal{P}_{g,g^{\prime}}
.
As action of embeddings commutes with S-polynomials, we get

\mathcal{S}(\pi(g),\pi^{\prime}(g^{\prime}))\ =\ \kappa(\mathcal{S}(\overline{\pi}(g),\overline{\pi}^{\prime}(g^{\prime}))),

and in consequence
\mathcal{S}(\pi(g),\pi^{\prime}(g^{\prime}))\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathcal{P}_{g,g^{\prime}})
.

\triangleleft

Union of all the sets
\mathcal{P}_{g,g^{\prime}}
yields therefore a presentation of
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}}
:

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{G}}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\ \bigcup_{g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{P}_{g,g^{\prime}}.

It is moreover computable:
for every
g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}
the finite set
F_{g,g^{\prime}}
is computable, by assumption
(\textsc{c}_{2})
,
and therefore the set
\mathcal{P}_{g,g^{\prime}}
is computable as well.
∎

Lemma 8 (Orbit-finite Buchberger criterion).

A finite set
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
is a Gröbner basis
if and only if for all
h\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}}
,

h\longrightarrow^{*}_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{0}.

Proof.

According to our definition,
a finite set
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
of polynomials is a Gröbner basis
if and only if the
orbit-finite
set
{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathcal{G})

is a Gröbner basis in the classical sense.
As the set
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}}
contains exactly
S
-polynomials of pairs of polynomials from
{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathcal{G})
,
we reuse the proof of classical Buchberger criterion
(which is still valid for infinite sets,
see e.g.
(
Cox et al. 2015
, Chapter 2)
)
to deduce the lemma.
(This is actually the only step which requires the stengthening of assumptions, i.e.,
well ordered
\mathcal{A}
.)
∎

Lemma 9 (Finite Buchberger criterion).

A finite set
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
is a Gröbner basis
if and only if for all
h\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{G}}
,

h\longrightarrow^{*}_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{0}.

Proof.

The ’only if’ direction follows immediately by Lemma
8
.
For the ’if’ direction, suppose that
h\longrightarrow_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{0}
for all
h\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{G}}
.
Consider any
h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}}
, i.e.
h^{\prime}=\iota(h)
for some
\iota\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}

and
h\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{G}}
.
By assumption,
h\longrightarrow^{*}_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbf{0}
.
We observe that the action of
{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}
(i.e., renaming of variables) preserves division steps, namely

f\longrightarrow_{\mathcal{G}}f^{\prime}\implies\iota(f)\longrightarrow_{\mathcal{G}}\iota(f^{\prime}),

and therefore
\iota(h)\longrightarrow^{*}_{\mathcal{G}}\iota(\mathbf{0})=\mathbf{0}
.
As
h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}}
is arbitrary,
using Lemma
8
we conclude that

\mathcal{G}
is a Gröbner basis.
∎

Algorithm
1
is an adaptation of the classical Buchberger algorithm to our setting.
The adaptation amounts to using only polynomials from

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{G}}
instead
of all S-polynomials.

Algorithm 1

Equivariant Buchberger algorithm

Input:
A finite set of polynomials
\mathcal{G}\subseteq_{\text{fin}}\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
.

repeat

for

f\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{G}}

do

compute some reduced
h\in\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
such that
f\longrightarrow^{*}_{\mathcal{G}}h

if

h\neq\mathbf{0}

then

\mathcal{G}\leftarrow\mathcal{G}\cup\left\{h\right\}

until

\mathcal{G}
stabilizes

Output:

\mathcal{G}

The above description is indeed an algorithm as, relying on our computability assumption
(\textsc{c}_{1})
, we prove that
one can compute some division step from a given polynomial
f
:

Lemma 10 (Computability).

Given
f\in\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
and
\mathcal{G}\subseteq_{\text{fin}}\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
,
one can check if
f
is reduced wrt.
\mathcal{G}
and,
if
f
is not, compute some
f^{\prime}\in\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
such that
f\longrightarrow_{\mathcal{G}}f^{\prime}
.

Proof.

For every polynomial
g\in\mathcal{G}
and monomial
m
of
f
,
we compute the set
D_{g,m}
of those
functions
\pi:\text{\sc vars}(\text{\sc lm}(g))\to\text{\sc vars}(m)

that extend to an embedding in
{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}

(which is checked using assumption
(\textsc{c}_{1})
) and satisfy
\pi(\text{\sc lm}(g))\mid m
.
If
D_{g,m}=\emptyset
for all
g
and
m
, the polynomial
f
is reduced wrt.
\mathcal{G}
.
Otherwise we pick up arbitrary function
\pi\in D_{g,m}
,
and compute some its extension

\pi^{\prime}:\text{\sc vars}(g)\to A
to
\text{\sc vars}(g)
that still extends
to an embedding in
{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}
.
This is doable by enumerating all (countably many) candidates
\pi^{\prime}:\text{\sc vars}(g)\to A
and checking them
(again relying on assumption
(\textsc{c}_{1})
).
Termination is guaranteed as
\pi
does extend to an embedding.
Finally, we divide

m
by
\pi^{\prime}(\text{\sc lm}(g))
which yields a monomial

satisfying

m\ =\ h\cdot\pi^{\prime}(\text{\sc lm}(g)),

and compute the remainder
f^{\prime}=f-r\cdot{\text{\sc lc}(g)}^{-1}\cdot h\cdot\pi^{\prime}(g)
, where

r\in\mathbb{K}
is the coefficient of
m
in
f
.
∎

The algorithm is nondeterministic, namely it may choose different
sequences of division steps
f\longrightarrow^{*}_{\mathcal{G}}h
and hence different reduced polynomials
h
,
and therefore it may have many different runs for the same input
\mathcal{G}
.
Nevertheless, we prove termination of its every run, and correctness of output.

Lemma 11 (Termination).

Every run of Algorithm
1
terminates on every input
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
.

Proof.

Towards contradiction, suppose some run of the algorithm is infinite.
Let
\mathcal{G}_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]

be the value of
\mathcal{G}
after
n
iterations of the while loop.
Let
\mathcal{M}_{n}=\left\{\text{\sc lm}(g)\,\middle|\,g\in\mathcal{G}_{n}\right\}\subseteq\text{\sc Mon}[\mathcal{A}]
.
For every
n
we have
\mathcal{M}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{n+1}
.
As every polynomial
h
added in
(n+1)
th iteration is reduced with respect to
\mathcal{G}_{n}
,
we have
\text{\sc lm}(h)\notin\mathcal{M}_{n}
, and hence

\mathcal{M}_{n}\subsetneq\mathcal{M}_{n+1}
for every
n
.
Therefore we can form an infinite sequence by picking up, for every
n
, a monomial

m_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{n+1}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{n}
.
This sequence is
bad
, i.e.
m_{i}\not\sqsubseteq m_{j}
for every
i<j
, which is in
contradiction with
\sqsubseteq
being a
wqo
.
∎

Lemma 12 (Correctness).

Every run of Algorithm
1
computes, given an input
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
,
a Gröbner basis of
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
.

Proof.

Each polynomial
h
added to
\mathcal{G}
in the algorithm
is obtained from some element of
\mathcal{G}
by a sequence of division steps

\longrightarrow_{\mathcal{G}}
, and hence belongs to
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
.
Therefore
\langle\mathcal{G}\rangle
stays invariant during the algorithm.
Once the algorithm terminates, the set
\mathcal{G}
satisfies the finite Buchberger criterion and hence
is a Gröbner basis, due to Lemma
9
.
∎

Reduction game.

\mathcal{A}

We define a game

\mathcal{A}=(A,\ldots)

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=(A^{\prime},\ldots)

Spoiler
and
Reducer

.

wins the first round if there are bijections

such that

(S^{\prime},T^{\prime})
-profile of
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is equal to

-profile of
\mathcal{A}

(21)

\displaystyle(S^{\prime},T^{\prime})\textsc{-prof}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\ =\ \left\{\tau\circ\pi\circ\sigma\,\middle|\,\pi\in(S,T)\textsc{-prof}(\mathcal{A})\right\}.

Otherwise

In the second round,
Spoiler
chooses one of the structures
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
,
and
a finite superset of target in this structure.

\displaystyle(S^{\prime},\overline{T^{\prime}})\textsc{-prof}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\ =\ \left\{\overline{\tau}\circ\pi\circ\sigma\,\middle|\,\pi\in(S,\overline{T})\textsc{-prof}(\mathcal{A})\right\}.

\displaystyle(S^{\prime},\overline{T^{\prime}})\textsc{-prof}(\omega^{2})\ =\ \left\{\overline{\tau}\circ\pi\circ\sigma\,\middle|\,\pi\in(S,\overline{T})\textsc{-prof}(\mathcal{Q})\right\}.

Conditions (

8.
Application: reversible Petri nets with data

In this section we show that Theorem
1
is useful for showing
decidability of the reachability problem for
reversible
Petri nets whose tokens carry data
from a given domain
(Theorem
3
).

Petri nets with data.

Among many presentations of Petri nets, we choose multiset rewriting, except that in place of
finite multisets over
\mathcal{A}
we conveniently take monomials
\text{\sc Mon}[\mathcal{A}]
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A,\ldots)
be a fixed relational structure.
Any set of rewriting rules of the form

{\mathcal{T}}\subseteq\text{\sc Mon}[\mathcal{A}]\times\text{\sc Mon}[\mathcal{A}]

we call
monomial rewriting over
\mathcal{A}
.
Rewriting rules extend to
transitions

\longrightarrow_{{\mathcal{T}}}\ \subseteq\text{\sc Mon}[\mathcal{A}]\times\text{\sc Mon}[\mathcal{A}]
as follows:
for every
g\in\text{\sc Mon}[\mathcal{A}]
and
(h,h^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{T}}
, we have a transition

g\cdot h\longrightarrow_{{\mathcal{T}}}g\cdot h^{\prime}.

The
reachability
relation is the transitive closure
\longrightarrow_{{\mathcal{T}}}^{*}
.
In the sequel we always assume that
{\mathcal{T}}
is orbit-finite, namely

{\mathcal{T}}\ =\ {\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(T)\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\ \left\{(\iota(h),\iota(h^{\prime}))\,\middle|\,\iota\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}},\ (h,h^{\prime})\in T\right\}

for some finite set
T\subseteq_{\text{fin}}\text{\sc Mon}[\mathcal{A}]\times\text{\sc Mon}[\mathcal{A}]
.
The set
T
serves as a
presentation
of
{\mathcal{T}}
when it is input to algorithms.
A
Petri net with data
\mathcal{A}
is just a monomial rewriting over

\mathcal{A}\otimes\langle d\rangle
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
:

Definition 1.

A Petri net with data
\mathcal{A}
, with
d
places, is an orbit-finite monomial rewriting
{\mathcal{T}}

over
\mathcal{A}\otimes\langle d\rangle
.

Example 2.

In particular, monomial rewriting over
\langle d\rangle
defines a plain (data-less) Petri net with

d
places
\left\{0\ldots d-1\right\}
.
The only embedding is identity, and hence
{\mathcal{T}}
is finite.
Monomial rewriting over
\mathcal{Q}\otimes\langle d\rangle
,
where
\mathcal{Q}=(Q,\leq_{\mathcal{Q}})
is the rational order, defines a Petri net with ordered data.
According to Lemma
7
,
embeddings of
\mathcal{Q}\otimes\langle d\rangle
are induced by some embedding

\iota
of
\mathcal{Q}
(i.e., a strictly increasing function
Q\to Q
),
and preserve the second component, i.e., preserve places
(for conciseness, instead of
(a,i)
we write
ai
):

ai\mapsto\iota(a)i.

Classical graphical presentation of Petri nets is extendable to Petri nets with data.
For instance, consider a Petri net
{\mathcal{T}}=\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{Q}}(T)
with
d=2
places,
and with 2 transition rules
T=\left\{t_{1},t_{2}\right\}
, where

t_{1}=(\mathbf{1},\ a0\cdot b0)\qquad t_{2}=(b0\cdot b1,\ a0\cdot c1)

for some
a<b<c\in Q
(
\mathbf{1}
is the unit monomial).
Petri net
{\mathcal{T}}
is presented symbolically,
using
variables

a,b,b^{\prime},c
ranging over
Q
:

0
1
t_{1}

a,b
t_{2}

b
b^{\prime}
a
c
a<b
a<b=b^{\prime}<c

Transition rule
t_{1}
outputs two tokens with arbitrary but distinct rational values onto place
0
.
Transition
t_{2}
inputs two tokens with the same value, say
b
, one from
0
and one from
1
, and outputs two tokens: one token
a<b
onto
0
and the other
c>b
onto
1
.

\triangleleft

{\mathcal{T}}
is
reversible
if it is closed under inverse:

c\longrightarrow_{{\mathcal{T}}}^{*}c^{\prime}
implies
c^{\prime}\longrightarrow_{{\mathcal{T}}}^{*}c
for every
c,c^{\prime}\in\text{\sc Mon}[\mathcal{A}]
.
In such case we can assume, w.l.o.g., that
T
is closed under inverse:

(h,h^{\prime})\in T\implies(h^{\prime},h)\in T
.

Reachability problem.

The
reachability problem
asks, given a Petri net
{\mathcal{T}}
and two monomials, source and target
s,t\in\text{\sc Mon}[\mathcal{A}\otimes\langle d\rangle]
,
whether
s\longrightarrow_{{\mathcal{T}}}^{*}t
.

Theorem 3.

For every
d\geq 1
,
the reachability problem in reversible Petri nets with
d
places,
with data
\mathcal{A}
,
reduces to

Ideal-memb in
\mathbb{Q}[\mathcal{A}\otimes\langle d\rangle]

(and also to

Ideal-memb in
\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{A}\otimes\langle d\rangle]
).

Proof.

The fundamental idea of the proof comes from
(
Mayr and Meyer 1982
)
.
Let
{\mathcal{T}}=\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}(T)
be a Petri net with data
\mathcal{A}
, with
d
places,
where

T=\left\{(c_{1},c^{\prime}_{1}),\ldots,(c_{n},c^{\prime}_{n})\right\}
.
Let
s,t\in\text{\sc Mon}[\mathcal{A}\otimes\langle d\rangle]
be source and target monomial.
The reduction transformes
s,t
and
T
to
the binomial

(t-s)\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{A}\otimes\langle d\rangle]
and the finite set of binomials

\mathcal{B}=\left\{(c^{\prime}_{1}-c_{1}),\ldots,(c^{\prime}_{n}-c_{n})\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{A}\otimes\langle d\rangle]
.
Correctness follows due to equivalence
of the following three conditions:

(1)

c\longrightarrow_{{\mathcal{T}}}^{*}c^{\prime}
;

(2)

(c^{\prime}-c)
belongs to the equivariant ideal generated by
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{A}\otimes\langle d\rangle]
;

(3)

(c^{\prime}-c)
belongs to the equivariant ideal generated by
\mathcal{B}
in
\mathbb{Q}[\mathcal{A}\otimes\langle d\rangle]
;

The implications
(1){\implies}(2)
and
(3){\implies}(1)
are shown by reusing the proofs of Lemmas 1 and 2 in
(
Mayr and Meyer 1982
)
.
The remaining implication
(2){\implies}(3)
is trivial.
∎

Reduction of Theorem
3
is uniform in the number
d
of places.
So is also the construction of Gröbner basis underlying the proof of
Theorem
1
, when
instantiated to domains of the form
\mathcal{A}\otimes\langle d\rangle
.
We thus deduce decidability of
the reachability problem for reversible Petri nets for a wide range of
domains
\mathcal{A}
, including
\mathcal{Q}
, equality data
(a sub-case of
\mathcal{Q}
), and lexicographic
products thereof (on the basis of Corollary
8
):

Theorem 4.

For every
\ell\geq 1
,
the reachability problem is decidable in reversible Petri nets with

\ell
-fold nested ordered data
{\mathcal{Q}}^{(\ell)}
.

Remark 5.

Without reversiblity restriction, the reachability problem for Petri nets with ordered data
is undecidable, while for Petri nets with equality data its status is still unknown.

9.
Application: Orbit-finite systems of linear equations

We demonstrate that
Theorem
1
is applicable to solving orbit-finite systems of linear equations,
thus providing a simple proof of a generalisation of the main result of
(
Ghosh et al. 2022
)
.
In the following,
\mathbb{K}
is a field and
\mathcal{A}=(A,\ldots)
an
\omega
-well structured
relational structure.

Solving a system of linear equations amount to checking if a given target vector
b

is a linear combination of a given set
M
of vectors.
We generalise this setting to orbit-finite sets, by assuming that
b\in\text{\sc Lin}(A^{d})

and that
M
is an equivariant orbit-finite family of vectors
M\subseteq\text{\sc Lin}(A^{d})
.
For convenience we assume that the family
M

is
indexed
by a set
A^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
namely

M=(M_{t}\in\text{\sc Lin}(A^{d}))_{t\in A^{n}}

(this is w.l.o.g. for equality and rational order domain
(
Ghosh et al. 2022
, Sect.6)
).
Such a family
M
is equivariant if for every
\iota\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}
and
t\in A^{n}
,

\iota(M_{t})=M_{\iota(t)},

i.e.,
M_{\_}
commutes with the action of embeddings, as the function

A^{n}\to\text{\sc Lin}(A^{d})
.
Alternatively,
M
is an infinite
A^{d}\times A^{n}
matrix
over
\mathbb{K}
,
whose every column
M_{t}
, being a vector in
\text{\sc Lin}(A^{d})
,
has finitely many non-zero entries.
A linear combination of
M
is of the form

(27)

\displaystyle r_{1}\cdot M_{t_{1}}+\ldots+r_{k}\cdot M_{t_{k}},

where
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}\in A^{n}
, and
r_{1},\ldots,r_{k}\in\mathbb{K}
.
We investigate the following problem:

Lin-solv(
\mathbb{K},\mathcal{A}
)
::

Input: :

a vector
b\in\text{\sc Lin}(A^{d})
and an equivariant family

M=(M_{t}\in\text{\sc Lin}(A^{d}))_{t\in A^{n}}
of vectors.

Question: :

Is
b
a linear combination of
M
?

Example 1.

Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{A}=(A,=)
the equality domain,

d=1
and
n=2
, and consider
an equivariant family of vectors
M=(M_{(a,b)}\in\text{\sc Lin}(A))_{(a,b)\in A^{2}}

defined as the following formal sums:

M_{(a,b)}=\begin{cases}a+2\cdot b&\text{ when }a\neq b\\
0&\text{ when }a=b\ .\end{cases}

For every
a\in A
, the singleton formal sum
a\in\text{\sc Lin}(A)

is a linear combination of
M
:

a\ =\ \frac{1}{2}\cdot M_{(a,b)}\ +\ \frac{1}{2}\cdot M_{(a,c)}\ -\ \frac{1}{3}\cdot M_{(b,c)}\ -\ \frac{1}{3}\cdot M_{(c,b)},

for any distinct
b,c\in A
different than
a
.

\triangleleft

Theorem 2.

Lin-solv(
\mathbb{K},\mathcal{A}
)

reduces to

Ideal-memb in
\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
,
for every computable field
\mathbb{K}
and relational structure
\mathcal{A}
.

Theorem
2
yields decidability of
Lin-solv(
\mathbb{K},\mathcal{A}
)
,
thus generalising
(
Ghosh et al. 2022
, Theorem 4.4)
which only considers
equality structure. On the other hand,
(
Ghosh et al. 2022
)
provides an upper complexity bound.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

The reduction uses the same encoding
\text{\sc enc}(\_):\text{\sc Lin}(A^{d})\to\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
as in
Section
4
.
Consider an instance of
Lin-solv(
\mathbb{K},\mathcal{A}
)
, namely

M=(M_{t})_{t\in A^{n}}
and
b
.
Relying on Lemma
2
we assume that

A^{n}
is represented by a finite set
B\subseteq_{\text{fin}}A^{n}
such that

A^{n}={\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}(B)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\left\{\iota(t)\,\middle|\,\iota\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}},t\in B\right\}
.

We construct an instance of
Ideal-memb in
\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
by
taking the polynomial
f=\text{\sc enc}(b)\in\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}]
, and the equivariant
\mathcal{I}
ideal generated
by the finite set

\left\{\text{\sc enc}(M_{t})\,\middle|\,t\in B\right\}\ \subseteq\ \mathbb{K}[\mathcal{A}].

As encoding commutes with the action of embeddings,

\mathcal{I}
is generated classically by the orbit-finite set

\left\{\text{\sc enc}(M_{t})\,\middle|\,t\in A^{n}\right\}
.
We need to prove:

Claim 3.

b
is a linear combination of
M
if and only if
f\in\mathcal{I}
.

The ’only if’ direction is immediate:
\text{\sc enc}(\_)
, as a linear function, maps a linear combination
of vectors to a linear combination of their encoding polynomials.
For the converse implication, suppose

f=\text{\sc enc}(b)
belongs to
\mathcal{I}
, i.e.,

\text{\sc enc}(b)\ =\ r_{1}\cdot h_{1}\cdot\iota_{1}(\text{\sc enc}(M_{t_{1}}))+\ldots+r_{1}\cdot h_{k}\cdot\iota_{k}(\text{\sc enc}(M_{t_{k}}))

for some
r_{1},\ldots,r_{k}\in\mathbb{K}
,
h_{1},\ldots,h_{k}\in\text{\sc Mon}[\mathcal{A}]
,

\iota_{1},\ldots,\iota_{k}\in{\text{\sc Emb}_{\mathcal{A}}}
and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}\in B
.
As all monomials appearing in polynomials
\text{\sc enc}(M_{t_{i}})
and
\text{\sc enc}(b)
have the same degree,
similarly as in the proof of Theorem
3
we can assume, w.l.o.g.,
that all monomials

h_{i}
are unit monomials, i.e.,

\text{\sc enc}(b)\ =\ r_{1}\cdot\iota_{1}(\text{\sc enc}(M_{t_{1}}))+\ldots+r_{1}\cdot\iota_{k}(\text{\sc enc}(M_{t_{k}})).

As encoding commutes with the action of embeddings and with scalar multiplication, we get

\text{\sc enc}(b)\ =\ \text{\sc enc}(r_{1}\cdot\iota_{1}(M_{t_{1}}))+\ldots+\text{\sc enc}(r_{1}\cdot\iota_{k}(M_{t_{k}})),

and by injectivity of encoding and equivariance of
M
we obtain

b\ =\ r_{1}\cdot M_{\iota_{1}(t_{1})}+\ldots+r_{1}\cdot M_{\iota_{k}(t_{k})},

i.e.
b
is a linear combination of
M
, as required.
∎

A definition of qualitative robustness for general point estimators, and examples
Henryk Zähle

Abstract
Zusammenfassung A definition of qualitative robustness for point estimators in general statistical models is proposed. Some criteria for robustness are established and applied to estimators in parametric, semiparametric, and nonparametric models. In specific nonparametric models, the proposed definition boils down to Hampel robustness. It is also explained how plug-in estimators in certain nonparametric models can be reasonably classified w.r.t. their degrees of robustness.

Introduction

Let
(\Theta,d_{\Theta})
be a metric space, where
\Theta
will be regarded as a parameter space. Let
(\Omega,{\cal F})
be a measurable space, and
\mathbb{P}^{\theta}
be any probability measure on
(\Omega,{\cal F})
for every
\theta\in\Theta
. The set
\Omega
can be seen as the sample space, where the sample is drawn from
\mathbb{P}^{\theta}
with (unknown)
\theta\in\Theta
. As usual, the triplet
(\Omega,{\cal F},\{\mathbb{P}^{\theta}:\theta\in\Theta\})
will be referred to as statistical model. Further, let
(\Sigma,{\cal S})
be a measurable space and for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
T_{n}:\Theta\to\Sigma
be any map, where
T_{n}
and
\Sigma
can be regarded as an aspect function and the state space of the aspect function, respectively. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\widehat{T}_{n}:\Omega\to\Sigma
be any
({\cal F},{\cal S})
-measurable map, which can be seen as an estimator for the aspect
T_{n}(\theta)
of
\theta
. Often the sample space and the estimator can be written as

(\Omega,{\cal F})=(E^{\mathbb{N}},{\cal E}^{\otimes\mathbb{N}})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\widehat{T}_{n}(x)=\widehat{T}_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mbox{ for all }x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots)\in\Omega

(1)

for some measurable space
(E,{\cal E})
, which is virtually the standard statistical setting, but this particular form will not be assumed here. Finally, let
\rho
be any metric on the set
{\cal M}_{1}(\Sigma)
of all probability measures on
(\Sigma,{\cal S})
.

The following definition proposes a notion of (qualitative) robustness for the sequence of estimators
(\widehat{T}_{n})
which is in line with Hampel’s notion of (qualitative) robustness. Note that the aspect functions
T_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, do not play any role in the definition. They will only occur again in Section
2
.

Definition 1.1

For any subset
\Theta_{0}\subset\Theta
we use the following terminology.

(i) The sequence
(\widehat{T}_{n})
is said to be
(d_{\Theta},\rho)
-robust on
\Theta_{0}
if for every
\theta_{1}\in\Theta_{0}
and
\varepsilon>0
there is some
\delta>0
such that

\theta_{2}\in\Theta_{0},\quad d_{\Theta}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\leq\delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\rho(\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{1}}\circ\widehat{T}_{n}^{-1}\,,\,\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{2}}\circ\widehat{T}_{n}^{-1})\leq\varepsilon\quad\mbox{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

(2)

(ii) The sequence
(\widehat{T}_{n})
is said to be asymptotically
(d_{\Theta},\rho)
-robust on
\Theta_{0}
if for every
\theta_{1}\in\Theta_{0}
and
\varepsilon>0
there are some
\delta>0
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\theta_{2}\in\Theta_{0},\quad d_{\Theta}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\leq\delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\rho(\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{1}}\circ\widehat{T}_{n}^{-1}\,,\,\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{2}}\circ\widehat{T}_{n}^{-1})\leq\varepsilon\quad\mbox{for all }n\geq n_{0}.

(3)

(iii) The sequence
(\widehat{T}_{n})
is said to be finite-sample
(d_{\Theta},\rho)
-robust on
\Theta_{0}
if for every
\theta_{1}\in\Theta_{0}
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
there is some
\delta>0
such that

\theta_{2}\in\Theta_{0},\quad d_{\Theta}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\leq\delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\rho(\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{1}}\circ\widehat{T}_{n}^{-1}\,,\,\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{2}}\circ\widehat{T}_{n}^{-1})\leq\varepsilon\quad\mbox{for all }1\leq n\leq n_{0}.

(4)

On the one hand, Definition
1.1
is close to Hampel’s definition of robustness in the context of nonparametric statistical models. Indeed, letting specifically
\Theta:=\Theta_{0}:={\cal M}_{1}(E)
be the set of all probability measures on some complete and separable metric space
E
(equipped with any metric
d_{\Theta}
generating the weak topology),
(\Omega,{\cal F})
be as in (
1
),
\widehat{T}_{n}
be as in (
1
) and invariant against permutations of the arguments,
\mathbb{P}^{\mu}:=\mu^{\otimes\mathbb{N}}
for all
\mu\in\Theta
,
\Sigma:=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

be the Prohorov metric, then part (i) of Definition
1.1
coincides with the definition of robustness as given in Section 4 of
[
14
]
. Cuevas
[
9
]
put forward Hampel’s nonparametric theory by replacing
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by a general complete and separable metric space
\Sigma
. Krätschmer et al.
[
16
,
17
]
considered metrics that metrize finer topologies than the weak topology, and Zähle
[

]
allowed for laws

that are not necessarily infinite product measures (for a different approach for nonparametric estimators based on dependent observations, see
[
3
,
6
,
8
,
20
,
24
]
). The distinction between asymptotic and finite-sample robustness was implicitly also done in
[
9
,
14
]
. Huber
[

]
and other authors (e.g.
[

,
17
,

]
) regraded robustness simply as asymptotic robustness. Examples for robust estimators in nonparametric statistical models range from sample trimmed means
[

]
to L-estimators
[

]
to Z- and M-estimators
[

On the other hand, Definition
1.1
allows for more statistical models
(\Omega,{\cal F},\{\mathbb{P}^{\theta}:\theta\in\Theta\})
than the one just discussed. In many classical examples of the theory of point estimation the parameter space
\Theta
is a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
(and not the measure space
{\cal M}_{1}(E)
). The underlying statistical model has indeed often the shape
(E^{\mathbb{N}},{\cal E}^{\otimes\mathbb{N}},\{\mathbb{P}^{\theta}:\theta\in\Theta\})
for some subset
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
. If
\mathbb{P}^{\theta}=\mu_{\theta}^{\otimes\mathbb{N}}
for some
\mu_{\theta}\in{\cal M}_{1}(E)
,
\theta\in\Theta
, then this model corresponds to the standard situation where one can observe the realizations of i.i.d.
E
-valued random variables with distribution
\mu_{\theta}
but the true
k
-dimensional parameter vector
\theta
is unknown; this setting is known as infinite product model. Robustness of the distribution of a given estimator w.r.t. small changes of the underlying model associated with
\theta
is an obvious quality criterion, but it is not unique what the “right” notion of robustness is. In the mentioned infinite product model, for instance, a “change” of the underlying model can be measured in at least two ways. First, one may measure a change of
\theta
w.r.t. the Euclidean distance on
\Theta
. Second, one may measure a change of the probability measure
\mu_{\theta}
w.r.t. any metric on
\{\mu_{\theta}:\theta\in\Theta\}
which metrizes the relative weak topology. The former approach is not covered by Hampel’s theory, but it is covered by Definition
1.1
and seems to be more natural in the context of classical parametric models (as, for instance, the Gaussian model where
\mu_{\theta}:={\rm N}_{m,s^{2}}
for
\theta=(m,s^{2})\in\Theta:=\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)
). The latter approach basically leads to a version of Hampel’s definition when regarding
\widetilde{\Theta}:=\{\mu_{\theta}:\theta\in\Theta\}
as the parameter space. But strictly speaking this approach is neither covered by the existing literature due to the traditional assumption
\widetilde{\Theta}={\cal M}_{1}(E)
. Definition
1.1
, on the other hand, is more flexible and makes the second approach possible too.

Apart from the situation where
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
(“parametric model”), the parameter space
\Theta
is often the product of a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and a subset of
{\cal M}_{1}(E)
(“semiparametric model”). This is the case, for instance, in some parametric regression models, ARMA models, and so on. Then a change of the underlying model should be measured by any metric which metrizes the product topology on
\Theta
. In this situation the classical definition of robustness does not apply again, but Definition
1.1
does.

The preceding discussion shows that Definition
1.1
is suitable not only for nonparametric statistical models but also for parametric and semiparametric statistical models. In this sense, this article treats a rather general setting and facilitates more examples than the existing literature on robustness in nonparametric statistical models.

Remark 1.2

When the metric
\rho
is fixed, then
(d_{\Theta},\rho)
-robustness of
(\widehat{T}_{n})
is clearly equivalent to
(d_{\Theta}^{\prime},\rho)
-robustness of
(\widehat{T}_{n})
for any other metric

.

\Diamond

Remark 1.3

Of course, the sequence
(\widehat{T}_{n})
is robust on
\Theta_{0}
if and only if it is both asymptotically and finite-sample robust on
\Theta_{0}
, and finite-sample robustness already holds when in (
4
) the phrase “for all
1\leq n\leq n_{0}
” is replaced by “for

(d_{\Theta},\rho)

means that the set of mappings

\big\{\Theta\,\longrightarrow\,{\cal M}_{1}(\Sigma),\penalty\ \theta\,\longmapsto\,\mathbb{P}^{\theta}\circ\widehat{T}_{n}^{-1}\penalty\ :\penalty\ n\in\mathbb{N}\big\}

is

-equicontinuous on

.

\Diamond

Remark 1.4

In Definition
1.1
, robustness of the sequence
(\widehat{T}_{n})
is a property which holds on the whole set
\Theta_{0}
. One could also define robustness at a fixed
\theta_{1}\in\Theta_{0}
by requiring that (
2
) holds only for the fixed
\theta_{1}
; and analogously for asymptotic and finite-sample robustness. However, from a statistician’s point of view, it is somewhat unsatisfying only to know that the estimator behaves robustly at a fixed parameter. After all the true parameter is unknown. For this reason, each of the conditions (
2
)–(
4
) is required to hold for all
\theta_{1}\in\Theta_{0}
.

\Diamond

Remark 1.5

Obviously,
(d_{\Theta}^{1},\rho)
-robustness is a stronger requirement than
(d_{\Theta}^{2},\rho)
-robustness when
d_{\Theta}^{1}\leq d_{\Theta}^{2}
, and
(d_{\Theta},\rho_{1})
-robustness is a stronger requirement than
(d_{\Theta},\rho_{2})
-robustness when

. The same is true for asymptotic and finite- sample robustness.

2
Some criteria for robustness

In this section, we will present some abstract criteria for asymptotic and finite-sample robustness. Take the notation introduced in Section
1
. Assume that
\Sigma
is equipped with a complete and separable metric
d_{\Sigma}
, and that
{\cal S}
is the corresponding Borel
\sigma
-field. In Theorem
2.1

\rho
will be chosen as the Prohorov metric
\rho_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm P}}}

\rho_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm P}}}

\rho_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm P}}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\,:=\,\inf\{\varepsilon>0\,:\,\mu_{1}[A]\leq\mu_{2}[A^{\varepsilon}]+\varepsilon\mbox{ for all }A\in{\cal S}\},

(5)

where
A^{\varepsilon}:=\{s\in\Sigma:\,\inf_{a\in A}d_{\Sigma}(s,a)\leq\varepsilon\}
. According to Theorem 2.14 in
[
15
]
the Prohorov metric

metrizes the weak topology on
{\cal M}_{1}(\Sigma)
. The proofs of the following results can be found in Section
4
.

First of all we will give a criterion for asymptotic robustness. To this end we will assume that the aspect function
T_{n}
and estimator
\widehat{T}_{n}
have the representations in (
6

be another complete and separable metric space equipped with the corresponding Borel
\sigma

{\cal U}
. Let

and
V_{n}:\Upsilon\to\Sigma
,

, be any maps, and assume that
T_{n}
and
\widehat{T}_{n}
can be represented as

T_{n}=V_{n}\circ U\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\widehat{T}_{n}=V_{n}\circ\widehat{U}_{n}

(6)

for some
({\cal F},{\cal U})
-measurable map
\widehat{U}_{n}:\Omega\to\Upsilon
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Due to (
6
), Theorem
2.1
below is applicable in the following two different situations. 1) On the one hand, when
T_{n}=T
for all

, condition (b) of Theorem
2.1
can sometimes be shown directly for

\widehat{m}_{n}

is any (nonparametric) subset of

We will use the following terminology. The sequence
(V_{n})
is said to be
asymptotically continuous at

if for every
\varepsilon>0
there are some

and

The sequence
(V_{n})
is said to be asymptotically continuous if it is asymptotically continuous at every

. Of course, if

for all

, then asymptotic continuity of

Theorem 2.1

Assume that
\Sigma
is equipped with a complete and separable metric
d_{\Sigma}
, and that
{\cal S}
is the corresponding Borel
\sigma
-field. Let
\Theta_{0}\subset\Theta
. Assume that
T_{n}
and
\widehat{T}_{n}
are as in (
6

(a)

U|_{\Theta_{0}}
is
(d_{\Theta},d_{\Upsilon})
-continuous, and
(V_{n})
is asymptotically
(d_{\Upsilon},d_{\Sigma})
-continuous.

(b)

For every

and

3.1
Plug-in estimators in nonparametric statistical models

In this section we will revisit classical plug-in estimators, which are the objects in the classical literature on robustness. Theorem
3.5
below provides a generalization of the classical Hampel theorem. For
\psi\equiv 1
it is indeed a version of Theorem 1 in
[
14
]
and of Theorems 1–2 in
[
9
]
. Moreover, Theorem
3.6
provides a version of Hampel’s theorem for strongly mixing observations. Robustness under strong mixing has been already investigated in
[
26
]
, but the conditions of Theorem
3.6
are significantly weaker than the conditions imposed in
[
26
]
. Indeed, in (
14
) the right-hand side is not required to hold uniformly in
\mu_{2}
on
\Theta_{0}
(as in
[
26
]
) but only for
\mu_{2}
close to
\mu_{1}
. Also note that Theorems
3.5
and
3.6
take into account an aspect that was not considered in
[
9
,
14
]
. The possible restriction to a subset
\Theta_{0}
of the domain of the statistical functionals
T_{n}
,

In

and any metric generating the weak topology. This implies in particular that the classical Hampel theorem yields

that are asymptotically continuous w.r.t. the weak topology (and well defined) on

. On the other hand, there are many relevant sequences of statistical functionals

that are
not
asymptotically weakly continuous. And the distributions of the plug-in estimators of two sequences of statistical functionals that are not asymptotically weakly continuous may react quite different to changes in the underlying (marginal) distribution, just as these plug-in estimators may have quite different influence functions. For this reason the authors of
[
16
,
17
,
26
]
allowed for metrics

that metrizes finer topologies than the relative weak topology.

It was discussed in
[
16
,
17
,
26
]
that the so-called (relative)

\psi
-weak topology for some suitable

metrizes the relative
\psi
-weak
topology and

is continuous w.r.t. the relative
\psi
-weak topology, asymptotic

-robustness of

could be proven for so-called uniformly
\psi
-integrating sets

(cf. Definition

). Lemma

will show that in this case one even gets (asymptotic)

generating the relative

-integrating sets (cf. Definition

3.1.1
The
\psi
-weak topology and locally uniformly
\psi
-integrating sets

Let
\psi:E\to[1,\infty)
be a continuous function. Let
{\cal M}_{1}^{\psi}(E)
be the set of all probability measures
\mu
on
(E,{\cal E})
satisfying
\int\psi\,d\mu<\infty
, and
C_{\psi}(E)
be the set of all continuous functions on
E
for which
\|f/\psi\|_{\infty}<\infty
, where
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
is the sup-norm. The
\psi
-weak topology on
{\cal M}_{1}^{\psi}(E)
is defined to be the coarsest topology for which all mappings
\mu\mapsto\int f\,d\mu
,
f\in C_{\psi}(E)
, are continuous; cf. Section A.6 in
[
12
]
. Clearly, the
\psi
-weak topology is finer than the weak topology, and the two topologies coincide if and only if
\psi
is bounded. Note that
\mu_{n}\to\mu

\psi
-weakly if and only if
\int fd\mu_{n}\to\int fd\mu
for all
f\in C_{\psi}(E)
. Moreover, the
\psi
-weak topology is metrizable by

d_{\psi}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):=d_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm w}}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})+\Big|\int\psi\,d\mu_{1}-\int\psi\,d\mu_{2}\Big|

(12)

for any metric
d_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm w}}}
which metrizes the weak topology. In the following definition,
d_{\Theta_{0}}
refers to any metric on
\Theta_{0}

(\subset{\cal M}_{1}(E)
) which metrizes the relative weak topology.

Definition 3.1

A set
\Theta_{0}\subset{\cal M}_{1}(E)
is said to be locally uniformly
\psi
-integrating if for every
\mu_{1}\in\Theta_{0}
and
\varepsilon>0
there exist some
\delta>0
and
a>0
such that

\mu_{2}\in\Theta_{0},\quad d_{\Theta_{0}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\leq\delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\int\psi\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{\psi\geq a\}}\,d\mu_{2}\,\leq\,\varepsilon.

It is said to be uniformly
\psi
-integrating if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists some
a>0
such that

\sup_{\mu\in\Theta_{0}}\int\psi\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{\psi\geq a\}}\,d\mu\,\leq\,\varepsilon.

Of course, any uniformly
\psi
-integrating set
\Theta_{0}
is also locally uniformly
\psi
-integrating, and any locally uniformly
\psi
-integrating set
\Theta_{0}
is a subset of
{\cal M}_{1}^{\psi}(E)
. The following two lemmas characterize (locally) uniformly
\psi
-integrating sets. The equivalence of the first three conditions in Lemma
3.2
is already known from Corollary A.47 in
[
12
]
. Recall that
E
was assumed to be a complete and separable metric space.

Lemma 3.2

Assume that each set
\{\psi\leq n\}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is relatively compact in
E
. Then, for any
\Theta_{0}\subset{\cal M}_{1}^{\psi}(E)
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\Theta_{0}
is relatively compact in
{\cal M}_{1}^{\psi}(E)
for the
\psi
-weak topology.

(ii)

For every
\varepsilon>0
there is a compact subset
K\subset E
such that
\sup_{\mu\in\Theta_{0}}\int\psi\mathbbm{1}_{K^{\sf c}}\,d\mu\leq\varepsilon
.

(iii)

There is a measurable function
\phi:E\to[1,\infty]
such that each set
\{\phi/\psi\leq n\}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is relatively compact in
E
, and such that
\sup_{\mu\in\Theta_{0}}\int\phi\,d\mu<\infty
.

(iv)

\Theta_{0}
is uniformly
\psi
-integrating.

In Lemma

\Leftrightarrow

\Leftrightarrow

(iv) when the sets

{\cal M}_{1}^{\psi_{1}}(\mathbb{R})
, because the family
\{T_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is equicontinuous on
{\cal M}_{1}^{\psi_{1}}(\mathbb{R})
w.r.t. the
\psi_{1}
-weak topology. The equicontinuity of the family
\{T_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
can be seen as follows. On the one hand, the
\psi_{1}
-weak topology is metrizable by the
L^{1}
-Wasserstein metric
d_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm W,1}}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|F_{\mu_{1}}(x)-F_{\mu_{1}}(x)|\,dx
(cf. Remark 2.9 in
[
17
]
), and on the other hand we have

\displaystyle|T_{n}(\mu_{1})-T_{n}(\mu_{2})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\cal R}_{\alpha}(\mu_{1}^{*n})/n-{\cal R}_{\alpha}(\mu_{2}^{*n})/n|\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ \frac{1}{\alpha n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|F_{\mu_{1}^{*n}}(x)-F_{\mu_{2}^{*n}}(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha n}\,d_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm W,1}}}(\mu_{1}^{*n},\mu_{2}^{*n})\penalty\ \leq\penalty\ \frac{1}{\alpha n}\,n\,d_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm W,1}}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\penalty\ =\penalty\ \frac{1}{\alpha}\,d_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm W,1}}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}).

For the second last step we used the inequality
d_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm W,1}}}(\mu_{1}^{*n},\mu_{2}^{*n})\leq n\,d_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm W,1}}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
, which can be easily shown by an induction on
n
.

To prove the second assertion, let
T(\mu):=\int x\,\mu(dx)
. On the one hand, the strong law of large numbers and Theorem 2.4 (v) (and Example 2.3) in
[
18
]
show that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat{T}_{n}=T(\mu)

\mathbb{P}^{\mu}
-a.s. for every
\mu\in{\cal M}_{1}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. On the other hand, the proof of Lemma
3.11
below shows that the functional
T
on
{\cal M}_{1}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is not continuous w.r.t. the
\psi_{q}
-weak topology for
q\in[0,1)
. So
(\widehat{T}_{n})
cannot be
\psi_{q}
-robust on
{\cal M}_{1}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
for
q\in[0,1)
, because otherwise we would obtain a contradiction to part (ii) of Theorem
3.8
.
∎

3.1.4
A degree of robustness

In Example
3.9
we have seen that the sequence
(\widehat{T}_{n})
introduced in (
19
) is
\psi_{1}
-robust but not
\psi_{q}
-robust for any
q\in[0,1)
. To some extent, this is a statement about the “degree” of robustness of the sequence
(\widehat{T}_{n})
. In fact, the “degree” of robustness can be formalized as follows. Let
E=\mathbb{R}
and
\Theta\subset{\cal M}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
, and assume as before that
\mathfrak{E}_{n}\subset\Theta
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, for every sequence
(T_{n})
of maps
T_{n}:\Theta\to\Sigma
we may define the index of robustness for the sequence
(\widehat{T}_{n})\equiv(T_{n}(\widehat{m}_{n}))
by

{\rm ior}_{\Theta}(T_{n})\,:=\,1/\inf\big\{p\in[0,\infty):\,(\widehat{T}_{n})\mbox{ is $\psi_{p}$-robust on }\Theta\big\}

with the conventions
\inf\emptyset:=\infty
,
1/\infty:=0
, and
1/0:=\infty
. On a more informal level amd in a slightly different setting, this concept was already proposed in Remark 3.8 in
[
16
]
; see also Section 2.4 in
[
17
]
for a special case. Of course, in the same way we can define the index of robustness in the case
E=\mathbb{R}^{d}
; just replace the right-hand side in (
18
) by
(1+\|x\|)^{p}
.

We have already seen in the proof of Lemma
3.10
that Theorem
3.8
is a useful tool for the specification of the index of robustness. We will now consider another example, namely the sample
p
-th absolute moment. On the one hand, this is more or less a toy example. On the other hand, Corollary
3.12
shows that the range of
{\rm ior}
is a continuum and that the concept of the
{\rm ior}
is compatible with our intuition that the degree of robustness of the sample
p
-th absolute moment is the higher the smaller
p
is. For any
p\in(0,\infty)
let

T^{(p)}(\mu):=\int|x|^{p}\,\mu(dx),\quad\mu\in{\cal M}_{1}^{\psi_{p}}(\mathbb{R}),

and

\widehat{T}_{n}^{(p)}(x)\,:=\,T^{(p)}(\widehat{m}_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}))\,=\,\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{p},\quad x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},

and recall that
{\cal M}_{1}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
refers to the set of all Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}
with compact support.

Lemma 3.11

For any
p\in(0,\infty)
, the functional
T^{(p)}
is
\psi_{p}
-weakly continuous on
{\cal M}_{1}^{\psi_{p}}(\mathbb{R})
but not
\psi_{q}
-weakly continuous on
{\cal M}_{1}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
for
q\in[0,p)
.

Beweis.

The functional
T^{(p)}
is clearly
\psi_{p}
-weakly continuous on
{\cal M}_{1}^{\psi_{p}}(\mathbb{R})
. To verify the second claim we have to show that for every
q\in[0,p)
there are some
\mu,\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots\in{\cal M}_{1}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\mu_{n}\to\mu

\psi_{q}
-weakly but
T^{(p)}(\mu_{n})\not\to T^{(p)}(\mu)
. By the Representation theorem 3.5 in
[
17
]
, it suffices to show that for some atomless probability space
(\overline{\Omega},\overline{{\cal F}},\overline{\mathbb{P}})
there exists
X,X_{1},X_{2},\ldots\in L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\overline{{\cal F}},\overline{\mathbb{P}})
such that
\|X_{n}-X\|_{q}\to 0
for every
q\in(0,p)
but
\overline{\mathbb{E}}[|X_{n}|^{p}]\not\to\overline{\mathbb{E}}[|X|^{p}]
. Let
(\overline{\Omega},\overline{{\cal F}},\overline{\mathbb{P}})=([0,1],{\cal B}([0,1]),\ell|_{[0,1]})
with
\ell
the Lebesgue measure on the line. Set
X:\equiv 0
and
X_{n}(x):=w_{n}\mathbbm{1}_{[1/n,1]}(x)x^{-1/p}
,

,

(w_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

and

. Then
\|X_{n}\|_{q}\to 0
for every

but
\overline{\mathbb{E}}[|X_{n}|^{p}]\nearrow\infty
.
∎

Corollary 3.12

Let
p\in(0,\infty)
, and
\Theta
be any set of Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}
such that

. Then on
\Theta

is
\psi_{p}
-robust but not
\psi_{q}
-robust for
q\in[0,p)
. In particular,
{\rm ior}_{\Theta}(T^{(p)})=1/p
.

Beweis.

By Lemma
3.11
, the functional
T^{(p)}
is
\psi_{p}
-weakly continuous on
{\cal M}_{1}^{\psi_{p}}(\mathbb{R})
but not
\psi_{q}
-weakly continuous on
{\cal M}_{1}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
for

. Moreover, the sample
p
-th absolute moment is known to be weakly consistent on

in the sense that

for all

.

3.2
Estimators in dominated parametric statistical models

In this section we consider point estimators in parametric statistical models. Although we allow
\Theta
and
\Sigma
to be rather general metric spaces, in applications one often has
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
\Sigma=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Theorems
3.13
and
3.15
provide general criteria for respectively finite-sample robustness and asymptotic robustness in dominated statistical models. From these criteria one can easily derive robustness of very classical point estimators; cf. Examples
3.14
and
3.17
. Throughout we will restrict ourselves to a fixed aspect, that is,
T_{n}=T
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
(\Theta,d_{\Theta})
be a metric space,
(\Sigma,d_{\Sigma})
be a complete and separable metric space, and
{\cal S}
be the corresponding Borel
\sigma
-field on
\Sigma
. Let
(E,{\cal E})
be a measurable space,
(\Omega,{\cal F}):=(E^{\mathbb{N}},{\cal E}^{\otimes\mathbb{N}})
,
X_{i}
be the
i
-th coordinate projection on
\Omega
, and
\mathbb{P}^{\theta}
be any probability measure on
(\Omega,{\cal F})
for every
\theta\in\Theta
. We will assume that our parametric statistical model is dominated. That is, we assume that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is some
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu_{n}
on
(E^{n},{\cal E}^{\otimes n})
(called the dominating measure) such that for every
\theta\in\Theta
the law
\mathbb{P}^{\theta}\circ(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{-1}
is absolutely continuous w.r.t.
\mu_{n}
with Radon–Nikodym derivative

L_{n}(\,\cdot\,;\theta)\,:=\,\frac{d(\mathbb{P}^{\theta}\circ(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{-1})}{d\mu_{n}}\,.

Let
T:\Theta\to\Sigma
be any function. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\widehat{T}_{n}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be any map such that
\widehat{T}_{n}(x)=\widehat{T}_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
for all
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots)\in\Omega
, and assume that
\widehat{T}_{n}
is
({\cal F},{\cal S})
-measurable.

Theorem 3.13

Take the notation from above and let
\Theta_{0}\subset\Theta
. Assume that
(E,d_{E})
is a complete and separable metric space and that
{\cal E}
is the corresponding Borel
\sigma
-field. Let
d_{E^{n}}
be defined as in (
10
), and assume that the following two conditions hold:

(i)

x^{n}\mapsto\widehat{T}_{n}(x^{n})
is
(d_{E^{n}},d_{\Sigma})
-continuous on
E^{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(ii)

For every
\theta_{1}\in\Theta_{0}
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
there is some
\delta>0
such that

\theta_{2}\in\Theta_{0},\quad d_{\Theta}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\leq\delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\int|L_{n}(x^{n};\theta_{1})-L_{n}(x^{n};\theta_{2})|\,\mu_{n}(dx^{n})\,\leq\,\varepsilon.

Then the sequence
(\widehat{T}_{n})
is finite-sample
(d_{\Theta},\rho_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm P}}})
-robust on
\Theta_{0}
.

Beweis.

It suffices to check that conditions (c)–(d) in Theorem
2.5
hold. Condition (c) is nothing but assumption (i). So it suffices to show that condition (d) holds, that is, that the mapping

\theta\mapsto\mathbb{P}^{\theta}\circ(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{-1}[\,\cdot\,]\,=\,\int_{\cdot}L_{n}(x^{n};\theta)\,\mu_{n}(dx^{n})

is
(d_{\Theta},\rho_{n})
-continuous on
\Theta_{0}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
\rho_{n}
is the Prohorov metric (defined analogously to (
5
)) on
{\cal M}_{1}(E^{n})
. Let
\theta_{1}\in\Theta_{0}
,
\varepsilon>0
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed. By assumption (ii) we can find some
\delta>0
such that for every
\theta_{2}\in\Theta_{0}
with
d(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\leq\delta
and
A\in{\cal E}^{\otimes n}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{1}}\circ(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{-1}[A]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{A}L_{n}(x^{n};\theta_{1})\,\mu_{n}(dx^{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{A}L_{n}(x^{n};\theta_{2})\,\mu_{n}(dx^{n})\,+\,\int|L_{n}(x^{n};\theta_{1})-L_{n}(x^{n};\theta_{2})|\,\mu_{n}(dx^{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{2}}\circ(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{-1}[A]\,+\,\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{2}}\circ(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{-1}[A^{\varepsilon}]\,+\,\varepsilon.

That is,
\rho_{n}(\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{1}}\circ(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{-1},\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{2}}\circ(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{-1})\leq\varepsilon
. This completes the proof.
∎

(20)

Theorem 3.15

Take the notation from above, let

(ii)

For every

\int\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}L_{n}(x^{n};\theta)\,\mu_{n}(dx^{n})\,=\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\int L_{n}(x^{n};\theta)\,\mu_{n}(dx^{n}).

and

, the Fisher information

(v)

3.3
Yule–Walker estimator for the parameter of a linear process

In this section we consider an example for a statistical model whose parameter space
\Theta
is the product set of an interval in the real line and a “nonparametric” set of Borel probability measures on the real line. To some extent, this model can be referred to as semiparametric. To describe the model, assume that we can observe a linear process

X_{n}\,=\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a^{k}Z_{n-k},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}

(24)

for any
a\in(-1,1)
and any sequence
(Z_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
of i.i.d. real-valued random variables with expectation zero and a finite second moment distinct from zero, but we do not know the constant
a
(and the distribution
\mu
of
Z_{1}
). The assumption

ensures that
(X_{n})
is a strictly stationary, zero mean

-process. In view of

\frac{\mathbb{E}^{(a,\mu)}[X_{1}X_{2}]}{\mathbb{E}^{(a,\mu)}[X_{1}^{2}]}\,=\,a\qquad\mbox{(if $a\not=0$)}

a reasonable estimators for

based on the first

observations is given by
\widehat{T}_{n}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
, where

\widehat{T}_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\,:=\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}x_{i}\,x_{i+1}}{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}}&,&\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}>0\\
0&,&\mbox{else}\end{array}\right..

(a,\mu)
, where the interest is in the aspect
T(a,\mu)=a
. Theorem
3.19
below shows that a small change in

only leads to a small change in the distribution of the estimator

uniformly in

. Since a small change in

might come along with a small change in
\mu
, we also allow for small changes in
\mu
. However, one can also assume that
\mu
is fixed if one starts from the premise that the distribution of the noise cannot change. In this case the change in the underlying model can be measured simply by the Euclidean distance
|a_{1}-a_{2}|
and the semiparametric model turns into a parametric model with parameter space
(-1,1)
.

\displaystyle+\,\mathbb{P}^{(a,\mu)}\Big[\frac{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|X_{i}X_{i+1}|}{\big(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2}\big)\,\mathbb{E}^{(a,\mu)}[X_{1}^{2}]}\,\Big|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2}-\mathbb{E}^{(a,\mu)}[X_{1}^{2}]\Big|\geq\eta/2\,\Big|\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2}>0\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{(a,\mu)}\Big[\Big|\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}X_{i}X_{i+1}-\mathbb{E}^{(a,\mu)}[X_{1}X_{2}]\Big|\geq\mathbb{E}^{(a,\mu)}[X_{1}^{2}]\,\eta/2\Big]

\displaystyle+\,\mathbb{P}^{(a,\mu)}\Big[\Big|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2}-\mathbb{E}^{(a,\mu)}[X_{1}^{2}]\Big|\geq\mathbb{E}^{(a,\mu)}[X_{1}^{2}]\,\eta/4\Big],

where for first and the last step we used
\mathbb{P}^{(a,\mu)}[\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2}=0]=0
(recall that
\mu
is absolutely continuous w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure) and for the last step we used Hölder’s inequality in the form of
\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}|X_{i}X_{i+1}|\leq\frac{n}{n-1}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2}\leq\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}^{2}
. Since the mapping

(a,\mu)\longmapsto\mathbb{E}^{(a,\mu)}[X_{1}^{2}]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a^{k+m}\int z_{1-k}\,z_{1-m}\,\mu^{\otimes\mathbb{Z}}(d(z_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}})

is easily seen to be
(d_{\Theta},|\cdot|)
-continuous on
\Theta_{0}
(use Lemma
3.4
and the assumption that
{\cal M}_{0}
is locally uniformly
\psi_{2}
-integrating), it follows from
\mathbb{E}^{(a,\mu)}[X_{1}^{2}]>0
(for all
(a,\mu)\in\Theta=(-1,1)\times{\cal M}_{1}^{0,2}(\mathbb{R})
with
a\not=0
) and the first part of Lemma
3.20
that condition (b) of Theorem
2.1
holds too.

The sequence
(\widehat{T}_{n})
is also finite-sample
(d_{\Theta},\rho_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm P}}})
-robust on
\Theta_{0}
. Indeed, by Lemma
3.21
the mapping
(a,\mu)\mapsto\mathbb{P}^{(a,\mu)}\circ(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{-1}
is
(d_{\Theta},\rho_{n})
-continuous, where
\rho_{n}
refers to any metric on
{\cal M}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which metrizes the weak topology. Moreover, the mapping
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mapsto\widehat{t}_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):=\widehat{T}_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is
(\|\cdot\|,d_{\Sigma})
-continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{(0,\ldots,0)\}
. For every
\theta\in\Theta_{0}
the probability measure
\mathbb{P}^{\theta}\circ(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{-1}
is absolutely continuous w.r.t. the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure, because the elements of
{\cal M}_{0}
were assumed to be absolute continuous w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure. It follows that for every
\theta\in\Theta_{0}
the
({\cal B}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),{\cal S})
-measurable mapping
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mapsto\widehat{t}_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is continuous outside a
\mathbb{P}^{\theta}\circ(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})^{-1}
-null set. Then, setting
\Pi_{n}:=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
, finite-sample
(d_{\Theta},\rho_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm P}}})
-robustness of
(\widehat{T}_{n})
can be obtained as in the proof of Theorem
2.5
(Section
4.3
), where one has to use the Continuous Mapping theorem in the form of Theorem 2.7 in
[
2
]
. This completes the proof of Theorem
3.19
.
∎

Theorem 4.1

(Strassen)
Let
(\Sigma,d_{\Sigma})
be a complete and separable metric space equipped with the corresponding Borel
\sigma
-field
{\cal S}
. Then, for any two probability measures
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
on
(\Sigma,{\cal S})
and any
\alpha,\beta>0
, the following two statements are equivalent:

(i)

For every
A\in{\cal S}
we have

\mu_{1}[A]\,\leq\,\mu_{2}[A^{\beta}]+\alpha,

where
A^{\beta}:=\{s\in\Sigma\,:\,\inf_{a\in A}d_{\Sigma}(s,a)\leq\beta\}
.

(ii)

There is some probability measure
\mu
on
(\Sigma\times\Sigma,{\cal S}\otimes{\cal S})
such that
\mu\circ\pi_{1}^{-1}=\mu_{1}
,
\mu\circ\pi_{2}^{-1}=\mu_{2}
, and

\mu\big[\big\{(s_{1},s_{2})\in\Sigma\times\Sigma:\,d_{\Sigma}(s_{1},s_{2})\leq\beta\big\}\big]\,\geq\,1-\alpha,

where
\pi_{i}:\Sigma\times\Sigma\to\Sigma
denotes the projection on the
i
-th coordinate,
i=1,2
.

4.1
Proof of Theorem
2.1
and Remark

We will adapt arguments of the proof of Theorem 2.21 in
[
15
]
. We have to show that for every
\theta_{1}\in\Theta_{0}
and
\varepsilon>0
there are some
\delta>0
and

such that

\theta_{2}\in\Theta_{0},\quad d_{\Theta}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\leq\delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\rho_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm P}}}(\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{1}}\circ\widehat{T}_{n}^{-1},\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{2}}\circ\widehat{T}_{n}^{-1})\leq\varepsilon\quad\mbox{for all }n\geq n_{0}.

(34)

Since

\rho_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm P}}}(\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{1}}\circ\widehat{T}_{n}^{-1},\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{2}}\circ\widehat{T}_{n}^{-1})\,\leq\,\rho_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm P}}}(\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{1}}\circ\widehat{T}_{n}^{-1},\delta_{T_{n}(\theta_{1})})\,+\,\rho_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm P}}}(\delta_{T_{n}(\theta_{1})},\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{2}}\circ\widehat{T}_{n}^{-1})

(with
\delta_{T_{n}(\theta_{1})}
the dirac measure on
(\Sigma,{\cal S})
with atom
T_{n}(\theta_{1})
), for (
34

\theta_{1}\in\Theta_{0}
and
\varepsilon>0

\delta>0
and

\theta_{2}\in\Theta_{0},\quad d_{\Theta}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\leq\delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\rho_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\rm P}}}(\delta_{T_{n}(\theta_{1})},\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{2}}\circ\widehat{T}_{n}^{-1})\leq\varepsilon/2\quad\mbox{for all }n\geq n_{0}.

(35)

The remainder of the proof is divided into two steps. In Step 1, we will verify that for (
35

\theta_{1}\in\Theta_{0}
and
\varepsilon>0
there are some
\delta>0
and

\displaystyle\theta_{2}\in\Theta_{0},\quad d_{\Theta}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\leq\delta

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\quad\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{2}}\big[\big\{\omega\in\Omega:\,d_{\Sigma}(T_{n}(\theta_{1}),\widehat{T}_{n}(\omega))\leq\varepsilon/2\big\}\big]\geq 1-\varepsilon/2\quad\mbox{for all }n\geq n_{0}.

36

Step 1.
Note that the right-hand side in (

\big(\delta_{T_{n}(\theta_{1})}\times(\mathbb{P}^{\theta_{2}}\circ\widehat{T}_{n}^{-1})\big)\big[\big\{(s_{1},s_{2})\in\Sigma\times\Sigma:\,d_{\Sigma}(s_{1},s_{2})\leq\varepsilon/2\big\}\big]\geq 1-\varepsilon/2\quad\mbox{for all }n\geq n_{0}.

By the implication (ii)

(with

4.3
Proof of Theorem
2.5

We will adapt arguments of the proof of Theorem 2 in

9

. Finite-sample robustness of
(\widehat{T}_{n})
means that for every
\theta_{1}\in\Theta_{0}
,

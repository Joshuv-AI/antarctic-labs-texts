GOOD MODELS OF HILBERT SCHEMES OF POINTS OVER SEMISTABLE DEGENERATIONS
CALLA TSCHANZ

Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper, we explore different possible choices of expanded degenerations and define appropriate stability conditions in order to construct good degenerations of Hilbert schemes of points over semistable degenerations of surfaces, given as proper Deligne-Mumford stacks. These stacks provide explicit examples of constructions arising from the work of Maulik and Ranganathan. This paper builds upon and generalises previous work in which we constructed a special example of such a stack. We also explain how these methods apply to constructing minimal models of type III degenerations of hyperkähler varieties, namely Hilbert schemes of points on K3 surfaces.

1
Introduction

An important method in the study of smooth varieties is to allow these varieties to degenerate to a sum of simpler irreducible components and use the degeneration to recover information about the original variety.
Our aim is to explore various constructions of good degenerations of Hilbert schemes of points over a semistable family of surfaces
X\rightarrow C
. We combine techniques of expanded degenerations (or expansions), geometric invariant theory, stacks and tropical geometry, as well as observe a symmetry breaking phenomenon in the constructions which allows us to define explicit examples. This builds upon previous work in which we constructed such a degeneration of Hilbert schemes of points as a proper Deligne-Mumford stack (
[
Tsc23
]
). The stability conditions required to make this stack proper are much simpler than would be expected in general for this type of problem. This is an interesting consequence of the specific choices made in the expanded degenerations we considered. In this paper, we generalise this construction, investigate alternative choices of expanded degenerations and describe the appropriate refinements of the stability condition needed to obtain proper moduli stacks. We give a detailed analysis of the stability conditions needed to ensure universal closure and separatedness, both from a geometric and tropical perspective.

Setup and motivation.

We recall the basic setup of this problem from
[
Tsc23
]
. Let
X\rightarrow C
be a semistable projective family of surfaces over a smooth curve such that the singularities of the special fibre
X_{0}
over
0\in C
are given étale locally by three planes intersecting transversely in
\mathbb{A}^{3}
. In other words, we may consider the étale local model for this family given by
\Spec k[x,y,z,t]/(xyz-t)
, where
k
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

Now, let
X^{\circ}\coloneqq X\setminus X_{0}
and
C^{\circ}\coloneqq C\setminus\{0\}
and consider the relative Hilbert scheme of
m
points
\Hilb^{m}(X^{\circ}/C^{\circ})
. Our goal is to construct compactifications of this object which satisfy some desired properties, which we discuss in the two bullet points below. The obvious compactification
\Hilb^{m}(X/C)
does not satisfy these properties. In the case of the first bullet point, this comes from the fact that
\Hilb^{m}(X/C)
is not logarithmically flat with respect to the pullback divisorial logarithmic structure given by the divisor
X_{0}
in
X
. For the second point, it follows from the fact that the special fibre of
\Hilb^{m}(X/C)
is very singular (which makes the total space very singular).

In
\Hilb^{m}(X^{\circ}/C^{\circ})
, we have removed the limits of the families of length
m
zero-dimensional subschemes and we wish to compactify, i.e. add limits back in in such a way that the resulting object satisfies “good” properties. These limits will again be length
m
zero-dimensional subschemes, some on
X_{0}
and some on different varieties. This will be achieved by constructing
expansions
(birational modifications of
X_{0}
in a 1-parameter family) and selecting the appropriate limit for each family of length
m
zero-dimensional subschemes in these modifications with the help of a stability condition. The two main meanings of “good” we may consider are the following.

•

The resulting degeneration of Hilbert schemes of points should be flat and proper over
C
, and the limit over
0\in C
of each family of length
m
zero-dimensional subschemes should be supported in the smooth locus of an underlying surface. Clearly, this is not the case for the obvious compactification
\Hilb^{m}(X/C)
, as certain length
m
zero-dimensional subschemes in the fibre over
0\in C
will have some of their support lying in
\Sing(X_{0})
. We wish to remove these subschemes and replace them by subschemes having support in the smooth locus of some modifications of
X_{0}
. As in
[
Tsc23
]
, we will refer to the support of a subscheme lying in the smooth locus of a surface as the condition that the subscheme is
smoothly supported
. Imposing these properties has the following useful consequences. The question of studying Hilbert schemes of
m
points on
X_{0}
is replaced by an easier problem. We study the Hilbert schemes of
m^{\prime}
points on the interior of each irreducible component of the modifications of
X_{0}
, where
m^{\prime}\leq m
, and consider products of these simpler Hilbert schemes of points. The smooth fibres of the family of Hilbert schemes of
m
points degenerate to a union of these products, which is easier to describe. Moreover, in such a compactification, the special fibre is stratified in a way which encodes the data of the families of length
m
zero-dimensional subschemes. By observing where the limit of a family lands within this stratification, we may recover the defining equations of this family. These properties have applications to cohomological computations and enumerative geometry.

•

Secondly, one can consider the more specific case where
X\rightarrow C
is a type III semistable degeneration of K3 surfaces, with a relative holomorphic symplectic logarithmic 2-form. We can then try to construct a family of Hilbert schemes of points on
X
which will be minimal in the sense of the minimal model program, and by this we mean a semistable or divisorial log terminal (dlt) degeneration with trivial canonical bundle (see Section
6
for details on minimality). The singularities arising in such a degeneration
X\rightarrow C
are of the type described above, i.e. we can restrict ourselves to the local problem where
X_{0}
is thought of as given by
xyz=0
in
\mathbb{A}^{3}
. Among other reasons, Hilbert schemes of points on K3 surfaces are interesting to study because they form a class of examples of hyperkähler varieties.

In this paper, we will construct degenerations of Hilbert schemes of points satisfying the properties of the first point above. We then show that the solutions considered here also give us constructions of semistable minimal hyperkähler degenerations as stacks. It is worth noting, however, that it is possible to construct dlt minimal degenerations as schemes which do not fulfill the requirement of the first point.

Main results.

Here, we briefly describe the main results of this paper.

We start by making a sequence of blow-ups on a fibre product
X\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\mathbb{A}^{n+1}
over a base
C\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\mathbb{A}^{n+1}
. This process is similar to the construction in
[
Tsc23
]
(see Section
2.1
for an overview of this previous construction), however, here, we allow for more types of blow-ups to be made. The sequence of blow-ups on
X\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\mathbb{A}^{n+1}
is encoded by a choice of sets
A,B\subset\{1,\dots,n+1\}
which satisfy a specific property called the
unbroken
property (see Definition
3.1
). We name the resulting expanded degenerations
X[A,B]
. For a specific choice of
A
and
B
, we recover the expanded degeneration
X[n]
of
[
Tsc23
]
. We describe an action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
on
X[A,B]
, an embedding of
X[A,B]
into a product of projective bundles and discuss various GIT stability conditions on the relative Hilbert scheme of
m
points of this expanded degeneration
\Hilb^{m}(X[A,B]/C\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\mathbb{A}^{n+1})
. We then present a stack of expansions
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
with a family
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
over it and explore the different choices of stability condition which will allow us to construct a stack of stable length
m
zero-dimensional subschemes in
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
which is proper over
C
. We start by extending the LW and SWS stability conditions defined in
[
Tsc23
]
to this setting and denote by
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\LW}
and
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\SWS}
the corresponding stacks of LW and SWS stable length
m
zero-dimensional subschemes on
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
. We show that, though both of these stacks are universally closed, they are not separated, as they contain several non-equivalent representatives for the same limit. We consider two main approaches to solve this problem.

The first method to construct separated stacks is to define equivalence classes identifying all limits of a given object in
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\LW}
and
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\SWS}
. The stacks whose objects are these equivalence classes are denoted
\overline{\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\LW}}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\SWS}}
. These are no longer algebraic stacks but we may now prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

The stacks
\overline{\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\LW}}
and
\overline{\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\SWS}}
are proper over
C
and have finite automorphisms.

This approach parallels work of Kennedy-Hunt on logarithmic Quot schemes
[
Ken23
]
.

The second method we use is to define an additional stability condition, called
(\alpha,\beta)
-stability
, which we use to cut out proper substacks of
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\LW}
and
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\SWS}
. The
(\alpha,\beta)
-stability condition must satisfy some subtle properties in order for the substack it cuts out to be proper over
C
: it must be what we call a
proper LW or SWS stability condition
(see Definition
5.10
). A proper LW stability condition
(\alpha,\beta)
as defined here recovers one of the choices of stability condition arising from the methods of Maulik and Ranganathan
[
MR24
]
in the case of Hilbert schemes of points. We denote by
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\MR,(\alpha,\beta)}
and
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\PSWS,(\alpha,\beta)}
the stacks of stable length
m
zero-dimensional subschemes in
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
for proper LW and SWS stability conditions
(\alpha,\beta)
. We are then able to prove the following results.

Theorem 1.2
.

The stacks
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\MR,(\alpha,\beta)}
and
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\PSWS,(\alpha,\beta)}
are Deligne-Mumford and proper over
C
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any
(\alpha,\beta)
which defines a proper SWS stability condition, there is an isomorphism of stacks

\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\MR,(\alpha,\beta)}\cong\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\PSWS,(\alpha,\beta)}.

We would like to emphasize here that it is not true that for every stack
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\MR,(\alpha,\beta)}
there exists an equivalent stack
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\PSWS,(\alpha,\beta)}
.

These choices of proper substack distinguish some geometrically meaningful examples among all possible choices arising from the methods of Maulik and Ranganathan. Our examples do not recover every possible choice, because the geometric assumptions made in the construction of
\mathfrak{X^{\prime}}
mean that it does not contain all possible expansions of
X
. The reason for these assumptions is discussed in Section
3.1
. The stacks
\mathfrak{M}^{m}_{\LW}
and
\mathfrak{M}^{m}_{\SWS}
of
[
Tsc23
]
are special cases of
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\MR,(\alpha,\beta)}
and
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\PSWS,(\alpha,\beta)}
stacks.

An important concern in the construction of these proper stacks is to understand what smoothings exist from one special element of the degeneration to another. Indeed, we must ensure that not only the generic elements of a family have unique limits, but also that special elements of these families with support in modifications of
X_{0}
have unique limits as well. In order to show that this is the case, it is necessary to study what smoothings exist from one modification of
X_{0}
to another. We give a tropical criterion to explain how to understand these smoothings in Section
5.4
.

Finally, we are able to show that all proper algebraic stacks constructed here provide good minimal models (see Section
6
for a precise definition of the term good minimal model). In particular, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

If
X\rightarrow C
is a semistable type III degeneration of K3 surfaces equipped with a relative holomorphic symplectic logarithmic 2-form, then this 2-form induces a nowhere degenerate relative logarithmic 2-form on each of the constructions presented above.

Background.

Previous work in this area includes that of Li
[
Li13
]
, in which the method of expanded degenerations is introduced, and Li and Wu
[
LW15
]
, where it is used to construct modular compactifications for degenerations of Quot schemes relative to simple normal crossing boundaries with smooth singular locus. Following on from this, Gulbrandsen, Halle and Hulek
[
GHH19
]
present a GIT version in the case of Hilbert schemes of points, and show the stack quotient of their GIT quotient to be equivalent to the proper Deligne-Mumford stack of Li and Wu. These solutions provide a foundation for our work, but are limited by the requirement that the special fibre of
X\rightarrow C
should have smooth singular locus. This is quite a serious restriction, as not many families will satisfy this condition. Note that the type of singularity we consider in this work is specifically the type that
[
LW15
]
and
[
GHH19
]
do not address.

In more recent work, Maulik and Ranganathan
[
MR24
]
use these techniques of expansions in the world of logarithmic and tropical geometry to solve this problem for Hilbert schemes over
X
where
X_{0}
may have any type of simple normal crossing singularity. This is a significant improvement, as their solution can be applied to any semistable family of surfaces
X
and these can always be obtained by semistable reduction. Their construction, however, yields an infinite family of birational solutions, but no explicit model. Moreover, it is far from obvious how such an explicit model should be constructed. This is the problem we solve in
[
Tsc23
]
and in this paper.

Organisation.

We will start by giving a brief overview of the results of
[
Tsc23
]
and by setting up the tropical point if view in Section
2
. In Section
3
, we construct the expanded degenerations
X[A,B]
and extend the group action, line bundle and GIT discussions from
[
Tsc23
]
to this context. Then, in Section
4
we define the stack of expansions
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
, the family
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
over
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
. We describe how LW and SWS stability extend to this context, define the corresponding stacks
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\LW}
and
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\SWS}
of stable subschemes on
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
and show that these are universally closed but not separated. In Section
5
, we discuss different methods for constructing separated stacks, as well as a tropical criterion for understanding smoothings between modifications of
X_{0}
. Finally, we discuss how all these constructions relate to the minimal model program in Section
6
.

Tsc23

and the setup of the tropical perspective which we will refer to throughout this paper.

The semistable family of surfaces.

Let
X\rightarrow C
be a flat projective semistable family of surfaces over a curve isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{1}
, and we consider an étale local model
\Spec k[x,y,z,t]/(xyz-t)\rightarrow\Spec k[t]
for this family, where
k
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. We denote by
X_{0}
the special fibre over
0\in C
and by
Y_{1}
,
Y_{2}
and
Y_{3}
the irreducible components of this special fibre given étale locally by
x=0,y=0
and
z=0
respectively.

2.1
The previous construction

In
[
Tsc23
]
, we start by constructing expanded degenerations
X[n]
given by a sequence of blow-ups on a fibre product
X\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\mathbb{A}^{n+1}
over an expanded base
C[n]\coloneqq C\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\mathbb{A}^{n+1}
. This construction is similar to those presented in
[
LW15
]
and
[
GHH19
]
, but additional blow-ups are added, breaking the symmetry of the construction, to allow for all necessary modifications for our problem to be included, while preserving commutativity of the blow-up morphisms. This family
X[n]
comes with a natural torus action by the group
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
and is shown to embed into a product of projective bundles. This allows us to define various
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-linearised ample line bundles on the relative Hilbert scheme of
m
points of this expanded degeneration
\Hilb^{m}(X[n]/C[n])
and describe the corresponding GIT stable loci.

We then construct a stack of expansions
\mathfrak{C}
and family
\mathfrak{X}
over it. This is a substack of the stack
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
defined in Section
4.2
. We describe how
Li-Wu stability
(abbreviated LW stability) can be extended to this setting and define an alternative notion of stability, called
smoothly supported weak strict stability
(abbreviated SWS stability), derived from GIT stability conditions. We then construct the stacks
\mathfrak{M}^{m}_{\LW}
and
\mathfrak{M}^{m}_{\SWS}
of LW and SWS stable length
m
zero-dimensional subschemes on
\mathfrak{X}
. The main results of
[
Tsc23
]
are the following.

Theorem 2.1
.

The stacks
\mathfrak{M}^{m}_{\LW}
and
\mathfrak{M}^{m}_{\SWS}
are Deligne-Mumford and proper.

Theorem 2.2
.

There is an isomorphism of stacks

\mathfrak{M}^{m}_{\LW}\cong\mathfrak{M}^{m}_{\SWS}.

Crucially, the choices of blow-ups allowed in the construction of
X[n]
are very restricted. This has the surprising consequence that the stacks of LW and SWS stable subschemes
\mathfrak{M}^{m}_{\LW}
and
\mathfrak{M}^{m}_{\SWS}
are already proper without the need to add any additional stability condition. The work of Maulik and Ranganathan
[
MR24
]
predicts that in general this will not be the case. Indeed, we will see in Section
5
that when allowing for different choices of expanded degenerations we will need to introduce an additional stability condition in order to obtain separated stacks over
C
. This stability condition is analogous to the Donaldson-Thomas stability of
[
MR24
]
.

2.2
Tropical perspective

Throughout this work, the results will be presented from a geometric and tropical point of view. Indeed, the key property that we would like our compactifications of
\Hilb^{m}(X^{\circ}/C^{\circ})
to possess can be thought of as the property that the limits of the families of length
m
zero-dimensional subschemes in the compactification should be stratified in a way which records the data of these families. In étale local coordinates, this is essentially asking for the compactifications to encode the degrees of vanishing of these families in the variables
x,y
and
z
. Tropical geometry is a natural tool to use to study such questions, as it is precisely the object parameterising such data.

We recall here the essential details of the tropical perspective (see
[
Tsc23
]
and
[
MR24
]
for more details). We construct the tropicalisation of
X
with respect to the divisorial logarithmic structure given by the divisor
X_{0}
. Formally, this means that we define a sheaf of monoids

\mathcal{M}_{X}(U)\coloneqq\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)\ |\ f|_{U\setminus X_{0}}\in\mathcal{O}^{*}_{X}(U\setminus X_{0})\},

for any open set
U\subseteq X
, and the corresponding
characteristic sheaf

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{X}\coloneqq\mathcal{M}_{X}/\mathcal{O}^{*}_{X}.

The
tropicalisation
of
X
is then given by
\trop(X)\coloneqq\colim_{x\in X}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{X,x})^{\vee}
. We recall also the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Upsilon
be a fan, let
|\Upsilon|
be its support and
\upsilon
be a continuous map

\upsilon\colon|\Upsilon|\mathrel{\smash{\meno}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\Sigma_{X}

such that the image of every cone in
\Upsilon
is contained in a cone of
\Sigma_{X}
and that is given by an integral linear map when restricted to each cone in
\Upsilon
. We say that
\upsilon
is a
subdivision
if it is injective on the support of
\Upsilon
and the integral points of the image of each cone
\tau\in\Upsilon
are exactly the intersection of the integral points of
\Sigma_{X}
with
\tau
.

Definition 2.4
.

The subdivision

\Upsilon\lhook\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\mathrel{\smash{\meno}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\Sigma_{X}\lhook\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\mathrel{\smash{\meno}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{r}_{\geq 0}

has an associated toric variety
\mathbb{A}_{\Upsilon}
, which comes with a
\mathbb{G}^{r}_{m}
-equivariant birational map
\mathbb{A}_{\Upsilon}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{r}
. There is then an induced morphism of quotient stacks

[\mathbb{A}_{\Upsilon}/\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r}]\mathrel{\smash{\meno}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow[\mathbb{A}^{r}/\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r}].

We define the
expansion
of
X
associated to such a subdivision

to be the modification

X_{\Upsilon}\coloneqq X\times_{[\mathbb{A}^{r}/\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r}]}[\mathbb{A}_{\Upsilon}/\mathbb{G}_{m}^{r}].

In the étale local model which we consider, the functions vanishing at

are

and

\trop(X)

\trop(X_{0})

Tsc23

3
The expanded constructions

3.1
Scheme construction

We construct expanded degenerations as schemes
X[A,B]
depending on a choice of sets
A
and
B
. This forms a generalisation of the schemes
X[n]
presented in
[
Tsc23
]
. We recall some of the notation and terminology from
[
Tsc23
]
here for convenience.

Enlarging the base.

Take a copy of
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}
, with elements labelled
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n+1})\in\mathbb{A}^{n+1}.
To match our previous terminology, we refer to the entries
t_{i}
as
basis directions
. We then take a fibre product
X\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\mathbb{A}^{n+1}
given by the map
X\rightarrow C\cong\mathbb{A}^{1}
and the product

(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n+1})\longmapsto t_{1}\cdots t_{n+1}.

This fibre product contains several copies of the special fibre
X_{0}
. We will now be able to make different modifications of this fibre by taking a sequence of blow-ups on
X\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\mathbb{A}^{n+1}
along
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
and the vanishing of some basis directions. Unlike the construction of
[
Tsc23
]
, we do not require here that both
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
should be blown up along all basis directions. Instead, the choice of blow-ups will be dictated by a choice of sets
A
and
B
.

The sets
A
and
B
.

For geometric reasons explained in Remark
3.7
, we will consider blow-ups arising only from sets
A
and
B
satisfying the following condition.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be subsets of
[n+1]\coloneqq\{1,\dots,n+1\}
. We say that the pair
(A,B)
is
unbroken
if there are real intervals
[1,a)
and
(b,n+1]
with
1<b<a<n+1
such that
A=\mathbb{N}\cap[1,a)
and
B=\mathbb{N}\cap(b,n+1]
. We will often write
(A,B,n)
for convenience when discussing these unbroken pairs; this is done in order to keep track of the value of
n
, although this value is determined by the sets
A
and
B
.

In other words, if
(A,B)
is an unbroken pair, then elements of
[n+1]
in order from 1 to
n+1
are contained first in
A\setminus B
, then in
A\cap B
and then in
B\setminus A
, where
A\cap B
may be empty but the other two are not. In particular,
A
is forced to contain 1 but not
n+1
and, similarly,
B
must contain
n+1
but not 1. See Remark
3.8
for a tropical description of the unbroken condition.

The blow-ups.

Let
(A,B,n)
be an unbroken pair. In the étale local model, the blow-ups on
X\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\mathbb{A}^{n+1}
are expressed as blow-ups in the ideals

\displaystyle\langle x,t_{1}\rangle,\langle x,t_{1}t_{2}\rangle,\dots,\langle x,t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{\lfloor a\rfloor}\rangle

and the ideals

\displaystyle\langle y,t_{n+1}\rangle,\langle y,t_{n+1}t_{n}\rangle,\dots,\langle y,t_{n+1}t_{n}\cdots t_{\lceil b\rceil}\rangle.

Globally on
X
, these are blow-ups along the vanishing equations of the components
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
. The equations of the blow-ups in étale local coordinates are given by

(\mathbb{A}^{r}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n+1-r})_{[\delta(A),\delta(B)]}

\rho_{J}^{[A,B]}\colon(\mathbb{A}^{r}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n+1-r})_{[\delta(A),\delta(B)]}\mathrel{\smash{\meno}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow(\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{U(J)})_{[A,B]}

given by

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r},\sigma_{r+1},\dots,\sigma_{n+1})\mathrel{\smash{\meno}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow(t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1}),

where

\displaystyle t_{i}=a_{j},\ \mathrm{if}\ \ind_{J}(j)=i,

\displaystyle t_{i}=\sigma_{j},\ \mathrm{if}\ \ind_{J^{\circ}}(j)=i.

As above, given two triples

\delta(A)=\delta(A^{\prime})

\rho_{J,[A,B]}^{J^{\prime},[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]}=\rho_{J}^{[A,B]}\circ(\rho_{J^{\prime}}^{[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]})^{-1}\colon(\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{U(J^{\prime})})_{[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]}\mathrel{\smash{\meno}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow(\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{U(J)})_{[A,B]}.

The stack
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
.

Finally, given an unbroken pair

\displaystyle\mathfrak{U}^{A,B}\mathrel{\smash{\meno}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathfrak{U}^{A^{\prime\prime},B^{\prime\prime}}.

We now make a few remarks concerning the isomorphisms described in this section. As mentioned above, these isomorphisms are effectively a reordering of the basis directions, where the

Let

. Then it is isomorphic by the isomorphism

or

, as long as

and the equalities

4.2
The family
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
over
\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
.

Let
X[A,B]\rightarrow C[A,B]
be a construction as described in Section
3.1
and recall that
\pi\colon X[A,B]\rightarrow X
is the projection to the original family. Let
n^{\prime}>n
, let
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime},n^{\prime})
be unbroken, and let
|A|\leq|A^{\prime}|
and
|B|\leq|B^{\prime}|
. In such a case, we defined a natural inclusion

\iota\colon C[A,B]\lhook\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\mathrel{\smash{\meno}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow C[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]

in (
3.1.2
). Then the induced family
(\iota^{*}X[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}],\iota^{*}\pi)
is isomorphic to
(X[A,B],\pi)
over
C[A,B]
. The following proposition shows that the equivalences defined on the stack
\mathfrak{U}^{A,B}
lift to
C
-isomorphisms of fibres. Let
X[A,B]_{U(J)}
and
X[A,B]_{J}
be the restrictions of
X[A,B]
to
(\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{U(J)})_{[A,B]}
and
(\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{J})_{[A,B]}
respectively.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(A,B,n)
and
(A^{\prime},B^{\prime},n)
define two unbroken pairs. The schemes
X[A,B]_{U(J)}
and
X[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]_{U(J^{\prime})}
are isomorphic if and only if
(\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{U(J)})_{[A,B]}
and
(\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{U(J^{\prime})})_{[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]}
are related by the isomorphisms
\tau_{J,[A,B]}^{J^{\prime},[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]}
and
\rho_{J,[A,B]}^{J^{\prime},[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
X[A,B]_{U(J)}
and
X[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]_{U(J^{\prime})}
are isomorphic on
\pi^{-1}_{[A,B]}(X^{\circ})\cong\pi^{-1}_{[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]}(X^{\circ})
, i.e. on the locus where all basis directions are nonzero. We must show that for each modified special fibre in
X[A,B]_{U(J)}
there is an isomorphic fibre in
X[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]_{U(J^{\prime})}
if and only if the corresponding bases are related by
\tau_{J,[A,B]}^{J^{\prime},[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]}
and
\rho_{J,[A,B]}^{J^{\prime},[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]}
. Then the proposition follows from the fact that if there is a bijection between the isomorphism classes of fibres of
X[A,B]
and those of
X[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]
, the two spaces must be isomorphic as schemes, as they must both be given by the same sequence of blow-ups on
X\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}\mathbb{A}^{n+1}
. As the blow-ups commute, we may discover this sequence just by looking at the fibres where all entries of
(t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1})
are zero (in fact, by construction, it is enough to show that these two fibres of maximal base codimension are isomorphic to show that the schemes
X[A,B]
and
X[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]
are isomorphic).

First, assume there is a sequence of
\tau_{J,[A,B]}^{J^{\prime},[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]}
and
\rho_{J,[A,B]}^{J^{\prime},[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]}
isomorphisms such that
(\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{U(J)})_{[A,B]}\cong(\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{U(J^{\prime})})_{[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]}
. Since
|J|=|J^{\prime}|=r
, this implies that, for
1<i<r
, the element
\ind_{J}(i)
belongs to
A\setminus B
if and only if
\ind_{J^{\prime}}(i)
belongs to
A^{\prime}\setminus B^{\prime}
. The same goes for the sets
A\cap B
with
A^{\prime}\cap B^{\prime}
and
B\setminus A
with
B^{\prime}\setminus A^{\prime}
.

Next, we note that the placement of the nonzero entries in
(t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1})
influences the
\Delta
-multiplicity of the components but has no effect on how many components are expanded out in the fibre above this point or whether these expanded components are of type
\Delta_{1}
or type
\Delta_{2}
. Moreover, changing the first zero entry of
(t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1})
from an element of
A\setminus B
to an element of
A\cap B
or vice versa also does not affect which components are expanded out in this fibre. The same is true of changing the last zero entry of
(t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1})
from
B\setminus A
to
A\cap B
or vice versa. This can be easily seen by studying the equations of the blow-ups. It therefore must follow that a sequence of isomorphisms
\tau_{J,[A,B]}^{J^{\prime},[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]}
and
\rho_{J,[A,B]}^{J^{\prime},[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]}
, as they only modify the base in ways that preserve the fibres, induces an isomorphism
X[A,B]_{U(J)}\cong X[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]_{U(J^{\prime})}
.

Conversely, let us assume that all fibres in
X[A,B]_{U(J)}
are isomorphic to fibres in
X[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]_{U(J^{\prime})}
. This means that for every fibre of
X[A,B]_{U(J)}
over a point
(t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1})
of the base
(\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{U(J)})_{[A,B]}
, there exists a fibre of
X[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]_{U(J^{\prime})}
over a point
(t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{n+1})
of the base
(\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{U(J^{\prime})})_{[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]}
such that the exact same number of components are expanded out in the same loci of both fibres and these components must have the same type. Clearly, there must be the same amount of zero entries in
(t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1})
and
(t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{n+1})
; call this number
k
. Moreover, for
k>1
, by studying the equations of the blow-ups we can see that for the above statement to be true, the first
k-1
zeros of
(t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1})
must have indices belonging to
A
if and only if the first
k-1
zeros of
(t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{n+1})
have indices belonging to
A^{\prime}
; and the last
k-1
zeros of
(t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1})
must have indices belonging to
B
if and only if the last
k-1
zeros of
(t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{n+1})
have indices belonging to
B^{\prime}
.

If the index of the first zero in
(t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1})
is not contained in
A
, then it must be contained in
B\setminus A
and all expanded components in the fibre above this point are of type
\Delta_{2}
. This can only be isomorphic to the fibre above the point
(t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{n+1})
if the indices of all zero entries in
(t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{n+1})
are contained in
B^{\prime}\setminus A^{\prime}
. If the index of the first zero in
(t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1})
is contained in
A
and the indices of all subsequent zeros are contained in
B
, however, then whether or not this first index is contained in
B
has no effect on the components expanded out in this fibre. In conclusion, if the fibres above points
(t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1})
and
(t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{n+1})
are isomorphic, then the index of the first zero entry of
(t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1})
is in
B\setminus A
if and only if the index of the first zero entry of
(t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{n+1})
is in
B^{\prime}\setminus A^{\prime}
; and if the index of the first zero entry of
(t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1})
is in
A\cap B
then the index of the first zero entry of
(t^{\prime}_{1},\dots,t^{\prime}_{n+1})
is in either of
A^{\prime}\setminus B^{\prime}
or
A^{\prime}\cap B^{\prime}
. A similar reasoning holds with respect to the index of the last zero entry being contained in
B\setminus A
or
A\cap B
. Indeed, if this index is contained in
A\cap B
, then
\ind_{J^{\prime}}(k)
may be contained in
A^{\prime}\cap B^{\prime}
or
B^{\prime}\setminus A^{\prime}
. But these are exactly the elements of the base which are related by the isomorphisms
\tau_{J,[A,B]}^{J^{\prime},[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]}
and
\rho_{J,[A,B]}^{J^{\prime},[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]}
.
∎

We define
\mathfrak{X}^{A,B}
to be the quotient
[X[A,B]/\!\!\sim]
by the equivalences generated by the
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n}
-action and equivalences lifted from

. There are natural immersions of stacks

\displaystyle\mathfrak{X}^{A,B}\lhook\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\mathrel{\smash{\meno}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathfrak{X}^{A^{\prime},B^{\prime}},

induced by the immersions

. Finally, we define

GIT stability on the relative Hilbert scheme of points.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

be an unbroken pair and let

be a length

. Then there exists a GIT stability condition on

which makes

in the union
(\Delta_{1}^{(k)})^{\circ}\cup(\Delta_{2}^{(n+1-k)})^{\circ}
for every

.

Corollary 4.3
.

Theorem
4.2
still holds if we restrict the possible GIT stability conditions to just those arising from choices of line bundles
\mathcal{M}_{l}
as defined above.

Li-Wu and modified GIT stability.

We extend the notions of stability set up in
[
Tsc23
]
, namely LW and SWS stability, first on schemes, then generalise to stacks. In section
5.2
, we will introduce an additional stability condition needed to obtain proper algebraic stacks. Throughout this section we assume that
(A,B,n)
is an unbroken pair.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
Z
be a length
m
zero-dimensional subscheme in a fibre of
X[A,B]
. We say that
Z
is
Li-Wu (LW) stable
if
Z
is smoothly supported and has finite automorphisms. We denote the LW stable locus by
H^{m}_{[A,B],\LW}
.

Remark 4.5
.

In the case of Hilbert schemes of points, this happens to correspond to the notion of stability defined in
[
LW15
]
. If we wanted to generalise these results to Hilbert schemes with non-constant polynomials, however, we would need to take the original definition of Li-Wu stability which can be found in
[
LW15
]
or
[
Tsc23
]
.

We also extend the definition of SWS stability to this setting in the obvious way as follows.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
Z
be a length
m
zero-dimensional subscheme in a fibre of
X[A,B]
. We say that
Z
is
weakly strictly stable
if there exists any
G
-linearised ample line bundle
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
on
H^{m}_{[A,B]}
with respect to which
Z
is GIT stable. The term
strictly
is used here to emphasize that we exclude strictly semistable points. If
Z
is also supported in the smooth locus of the fibre, then we say it is
smoothly supported weakly strictly stable
(abbreviated to
SWS stable
as before) and we denote the SWS stable locus by
H^{m}_{[A,B],\SWS}
.

We then have the inclusion

H^{m}_{[A,B],\SWS}\subset H^{m}_{[A,B],\LW},

since, if points are GIT stable, they must have finite stabilisers. We now extend our definition of SWS stability to the stack
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
.

Given any
C
-scheme
S
, an object of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}(S)
is a pullback family
\xi^{*}X[A,B]
for a morphism

\xi\colon S\rightarrow C[A,B].

Definition 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\in\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}(S)
and let
Z
be family of length
m
zero-dimensional subschemes in
\mathcal{X}
. The pair
(Z,\mathcal{X})
is said to be
SWS stable
if and only if
\mathcal{X}\coloneqq\xi^{*}X[A,B]
for some morphism
\xi\colon S\rightarrow C[A,B]
, where
Z
is supported in the smooth locus of
\mathcal{X}
and there exists some
G
-linearised ample line bundle on
H^{m}_{[A,B]}
which makes
\xi_{*}Z
be GIT stable.

LW stability can be extended to the stack setting similarly. It is important to note that an object
\mathcal{X}\in\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}(S)
is an equivalence class under the action of
G
and the isomorphisms set up in Section
4.1
which identify isomorphic fibres. In particular, we highlight that the above definition means that the entire equivalence class given by a pair
(Z,\mathcal{X})
is SWS stable if there is any representative of the equivalence class of
\mathcal{X}
for which this pair is SWS stable.

Proposition 4.8
.

There exists a pair
(Z,\mathcal{X})
for some
\mathcal{X}\in\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}(k)
(where
k
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero as before) which is LW stable but not SWS stable. This means there is no representative of the equivalence class of
\mathcal{X}
for which this pair is SWS stable.

Proof.

This can be seen easily by describing an example where this happens. Take
\mathcal{X}
to be a fibre of base codimension 2 where exactly one
\Delta
-component is expanded out and it is pure of type 1. Then let
Z
be a length
m
zero dimensional subscheme with at least part of its support lying in the interior of this expanded
\Delta_{1}
component, but no point of its support lying in
Y_{1}
. This will be LW stable but it will never be SWS stable because no matter what representative of the equivalence class of
\mathcal{X}
we choose, this representative will always have at least one
\Delta_{1}
- and one
\Delta_{2}
-component equal to
Y_{1}
. By Theorem
4.2
, the pair cannot be SWS stable.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

If we restrict ourselves to the substack
\mathfrak{X}
, the LW and SWS stability conditions are the same up to the equivalences of the stack. In that context, if there is at least one point of the support of
Z
in the union
(\Delta_{1}^{(i)})^{\circ}\cup(\Delta_{2}^{(n+1-i)})^{\circ}
for every
i
for which these components are expanded out in
\mathcal{X}
, then
(Z,\mathcal{X})
is SWS stable. This is because in
\mathfrak{X}

Remark 4.10
.

Unlike the stacks
\mathfrak{M}^{m}_{\SWS}
and
\mathfrak{M}^{m}_{\LW}
of
[

]
, the stacks
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\SWS}
and
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\LW}
are universally closed but not separated, meaning that stable families of length
m
zero-dimensional subschemes in these stacks may have more than one limit. As a consequence, Li-Wu stability and Maulik-Ranganathan stability do not coincide on these stacks. Indeed, it does not yet make sense here to speak of Maulik-Ranganathan stability on the stack
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
as we have not defined a notion of tube component and a choice of stable limit among all possible limits for a given a family
(Z,\mathcal{X})
. As we will see, in order to construct separated stacks we will need to select one of two options. The first is to identify all choices of representatives for a limit, which will yield a stack which is no longer algebraic. This parallels work of Kennedy-Hunt
[

]
. The second is to make a choice of substack which picks out exactly one representative for each limit and these substacks will be isomorphic to the underlying stacks produced by the methods of Maulik and Ranganathan in
[
MR24
]
(the word underlying here is used to signify that we do not speak of logarithmic structures).

5
Constructing separated stacks.

We are now in a position to construct separated stacks. As mentioned earlier, there are two ways of doing this. Firstly, one can avoid making any choices and instead identify all possible limits of a given family in the stacks

The second way to obtain separated stacks is to make a systematic choice of representative for a given limit. This will be done by adding an additional stability condition.

do not exist in the equivalence class defining the corresponding object in
\overline{\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\SWS}}(k)
or
\overline{\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\LW}}(k)
.

Finally, we would like to comment on the fact that we do not assert an isomorphism between the stacks
\overline{\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\SWS}}
and

here. This is because the results we used to establish equivalence of stacks require the stacks to be algebraic. We do, in fact, expect these stacks to be isomorphic but some further work is needed to show this.

5.2
Choices of proper algebraic stacks.

As mentioned before, the second way we have of making separated stacks is to make a systematic choice among all pairs
(Z,\mathcal{X})\in\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\SWS}(k)
(or
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\LW}(k)
) associated to the same configuration of vertices in
\trop(X_{0})
. For example, in Figure
4
, we would need to add a stability condition which allows exactly one of the pairs represented by the three geometric pictures on the right to be stable. These choices effectively cut out proper substacks of
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\SWS}
and
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\LW}
.

Additional stability condition for uniqueness of limits.

In order to make these choices of limit representatives, we may declare that certain fibres
\mathcal{X}\in\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
contain no stable subschemes and we may also decide that certain bubbles in given fibres contain no points of the support. The Maulik-Ranganathan stability condition is close to this description. The starting point for their construction is to look at the image of a given subscheme
Z
under the tropicalisation map, given by a collection of vertices in the tropicalisation of
X_{0}
, and then construct an expansion around the bubbles introduced by these new vertices, corresponding to our notion of associated pairs. Implicitly, choices are made at this stage to exclude certain superfluous fibres. We must therefore emulate this with our stability condition. Moreover, in the modified special fibres they do construct, it is necessary to allow certain configurations of points and not others, hence labelling certain bubbles as tube components and introducing the concept of Donaldson-Thomas stability with respect to these labellings. We describe suitable additions to the stability conditions previously discussed and extend Maulik-Ranganathan stability to our situation with the following definitions.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\in\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}(k)
and let
Z
be a length
m
zero-dimensional subscheme in
\mathcal{X}
. A
tube labelling
of
\mathcal{X}
is a choice of a collection of bubbles in
\mathcal{X}
which we call
tube components
. Given such a labelling, the pair
(Z,\mathcal{X})
is
Donaldson-Thomas stable
(abbreviated DT stable) if it satisfies the following condition: a bubble in
\mathcal{X}
contains no point of the support of
Z
if and only if it is a tube component.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
\alpha
denote a collection of equivalence classes of fibres in
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}(k)
and let
\beta
denote a collection of tube labellings on the equivalence classes of fibres remaining after the fibres
\alpha
have been removed. Let
\mathcal{X}\in\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}(k)
. We say that the pair
(Z,\mathcal{X})
, where
Z
is a length

zero-dimensional subscheme in
\mathcal{X}

-

if
\mathcal{X}
is not one of the fibres in
\alpha
and the pair
(Z,\mathcal{X})
is DT stable.

We will only want to consider
(\alpha,\beta)
-stability as a restriction of SWS or LW stability. We denote by
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\SWS,(\alpha,\beta)}
and
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\LW,(\alpha,\beta)}
the stacks
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\SWS}
and
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\LW}
restricted to their
(\alpha,\beta)
-stable loci. As we will see with the next two results, an appropriate choice of
(\alpha,\beta)
-stability condition will give us unique limits and allow us to build proper stacks. First, let us define some useful terminology.

Definition 5.6
.

We say that a pair
(\alpha,\beta)
is an

if it is chosen so that for each configuration of points in
\trop(X_{0})
there exists a unique associated pair
(Z,\mathcal{X})\in\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\SWS,(\alpha,\beta)}(k)
, where
\mathcal{X}
is pulled back from a modified special fibre. The corresponding definition of

can be made in a similar way.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
S\coloneqq\Spec R
for some discrete valuation ring

(or
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\LW,(\alpha,\beta)}(\eta)

has a unique extension in
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\SWS,(\alpha,\beta)}
(or
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\LW,(\alpha,\beta)}

Proof.

This follows directly from the proof of Proposition
4.12

(Z_{\eta},\mathcal{X}_{\eta})

can be written locally in terms of its

\{(c_{i,1}u^{e_{i,1}},c_{i,2}u^{e_{i,2}},c_{i,3}u^{e_{i,3}})\},

∎

We have therefore proven that, if

over a point

-stable limit.

Remark
about compatibility.

Here, we point out a subtlety about the compatibility conditions discussed above. Let
\mathcal{X}_{1}
and
\mathcal{X}_{2}
be two fibres of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
over a closed point. Assume that
\mathcal{X}_{1}
is pulled back from some
X[A,B]_{J_{1}}
and
\mathcal{X}_{2}
is pulled back from some
X[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]_{J_{2}}
, where
|J_{1}|\leq|J_{2}|
. We denote by
W_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{1}
and
W_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{2}
the union of all irreducible components in each fibre which are not tubes (this includes the
Y_{i}
components). We say that
(\alpha,\beta)
defines a
strong compatibility condition
if the following holds. If
\pi_{*}(W_{2})\subset\overline{\pi_{*}(W_{1})}\subset X_{0}
, then there exists a fibre
\mathcal{X}_{3}
equivalent to
\mathcal{X}_{2}
in
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
such that
\mathcal{X}_{3}
is pulled back from

for some
J_{3}
with
J_{1}\subseteq J_{3}
. This condition certainly implies tropical compatibility, and naively may seem like the right restriction to impose, but it is actually stronger than necessary. Indeed, the tropically compatible condition does not necessarily imply strong compatibility. As shown in Remark
5.15
, strong compatibility is not necessary to construct a proper stack, as in certain cases, subschemes cannot tend towards a certain limit.

Remark 5.15
.

Let
(Z_{\eta},\mathcal{X}_{\eta})
be a pair consisting of a length
m
zero-dimensional subscheme
Z_{\eta}
in a modified special fibre
\mathcal{X}_{\eta}
. Assume that the pair is either SWS or LW stable. Let
P
be a point of the support which lies in some irreducible component

and let

be some other irreducible component in
\mathcal{X}_{\eta}
which intersects

nontrivially. If there exists no representative in the equivalence class of
(Z_{\eta},\mathcal{X}_{\eta})
such that there is a smoothing from the interior of
W
in
\mathcal{X}_{\eta}
to the interior of some
\Delta
-components expanded out in the

locus, i.e. such that these
\Delta
-components are equal to

in
\mathcal{X}_{\eta}
, then there exists no flat family of length
m
zero-dimensional subschemes such that

is the generic fibre and

tends towards

over the closed point. This can be seen by studying the equations of the blow-ups.

For example, let
\mathcal{X}_{\eta}
be a fibre with one expanded
\Delta_{1}
-component of pure type and no other expanded components. Let
Z_{\eta}
be a length 2 zero-dimensional subscheme with one point of its support,
P_{0}
, lying in the interior of this
\Delta_{1}
-component in the

In Section

, we give a tropical criterion to understand when such smoothings from one component to another exist.

5.3
Relating the proper stacks

We may now show how the stacks
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\PSWS,(\alpha,\beta)}
and
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\MR,(\alpha,\beta)}

Theorem 5.16
.

Let
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\PSWS,(\alpha,\beta)}
and
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\MR,(\alpha,\beta)}
be two choices given by the same
(\alpha,\beta)
. This means that we have chosen an MR stability condition

which also defines a PSWS stability condition (by Proposition

this is not the case in general). There then exists an isomorphism of stacks

\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\PSWS,(\alpha,\beta)}\cong\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\MR,(\alpha,\beta)}.

Proof.

Similarly to the arguments of Section 6.2 of
[
Tsc23
]
, it is clear that on the scheme level we have an inclusion

H^{m,[A,B]}_{\SWS,(\alpha,\beta)}\lhook\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\mathrel{\smash{\meno}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow H^{m,[A,B]}_{\LW,(\alpha,\beta)},

where
H^{m,[A,B]}_{\SWS,(\alpha,\beta)}
and
H^{m,[A,B]}_{\LW,(\alpha,\beta)}
are the restrictions of
H^{m}_{[A,B],\SWS}
and
H^{m}_{[A,B],\LW}
to their respective
(\alpha,\beta)
-stable loci. As before, this gives rise to a morphism of stacks

f\colon\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\PSWS,(\alpha,\beta)}\mathrel{\smash{\meno}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\MR,(\alpha,\beta)}.

By our assumptions and Theorem
4.2
, for every MR stable pair
(Z,\mathcal{X})\in\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\MR,(\alpha,\beta)}(k)
, where

is an algebraically closed field as before, there exists a representative of the equivalence class of

which is SWS stable. We therefore have an induced bijection between the sets of equivalence classes of each stack

|f|\colon|\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\PSWS,(\alpha,\beta)}(k)|\mathrel{\smash{\meno}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow|\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\MR,(\alpha,\beta)}(k)|.

By Theorem
5.14
,
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\PSWS,(\alpha,\beta)}
is separated and Deligne-Mumford and therefore has finite inertia. The morphism

induces a bijective homomorphism of stabilisers by the same argument as in the proof of Theorem 6.2.4 of
[

. By Lemma 6 of
[

]
(Lemma 6.2.3 of

[
Tsc23

.
∎

As shown in
[

5.4
Further comments on smoothings

Given a pair
(Z,\mathcal{X})\in\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\SWS}(k)
(or
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\LW}(k)

\mathcal{X}
is pulled back from a modified special fibre, we would like to understand what deformations of

exist in
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\SWS}
(or
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\LW}

\mathcal{X}
to irreducible components of a second fibre

. This will allow us to understand explicitly what limits need to be included in the stacks we construct. We will say there is a

from an irreducible component
W\subset\mathcal{X}
to an irreducible component
W^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
if there exist a discrete valuation ring
R
and a smooth variety
\mathcal{Y}
over
S\coloneqq\Spec R
such that
W
is equal to the restriction of
\mathcal{Y}
to the generic point of
S
and the restriction of
\mathcal{Y}
to the closed point of
S
is a union of irreducible components, where
W^{\prime}
is one of these components. The question we seek to answer in this subsection is the following.

Given a polyhedral subdivision of
\trop(X_{0})
, what smoothings exist from the components corresponding to the vertices in
\trop(X_{0})
?

The question of smoothings takes place on the level of representatives of equivalence classes in
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
as it depends on
\Delta_{i}
-multiplicity (see Definition
3.5
). In order to interpret this information tropically, we enhance
\trop(X_{0})
with the notion of
\Delta
-multiplicity.

Definition 5.17
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\in\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}(k)
be a modified special fibre and let
\trop(\mathcal{X})
be the corresponding polyhedral subdivision of
\trop(X_{0})
. For each edge of
\Delta
-type 1 or 2 in
\trop(\mathcal{X})
we may assign to it a positive integer, called its
tropical
\Delta
-multiplicity
.

Remark 5.18
.

The tropical
\Delta
-multiplicity of an edge in
\trop(\mathcal{X})
is the
\Delta_{i}
-multiplicity common to all geometric components corresponding to the vertices connected to this edge. For convenience, here we assign the multiplicity to the edge and not the vertices, as the edge contains the information of the
\Delta
-type (see Definition
3.5
).

Given a fibre
\mathcal{X}
as above and any bubble
B_{0}
in
\mathcal{X}
, we will show in Proposition
5.19
what smoothings exist from
B_{0}
to bubbles
B_{i}
in blow-ups of
\mathcal{X}
(possibly after base change). Before formulating this proposition, we describe the operations on
\trop(\mathcal{X})
which can result in smoothings.

Allowing for base changes.

As we are working with stacks, we always allow for base changes. Tropically, this means we may adjust the height of the subdivided triangle
\trop(\mathcal{X})
within the cone of the 1-parameter family. We abuse notation slightly and denote this object after base change by
\trop(\mathcal{X})
again. Effectively, this re-scales and allows us to add more integral points to the triangle. Geometrically, this corresponds to the phenomenon that we can consider any scheme
X[A,B]
to be a sublocus of a larger scheme
X[A^{\prime},B^{\prime}]
, where the embedding is given by (
3.1.2
). This operation of placing a family within a larger family is exactly what allows us to make all appropriate modifications to
X_{0}
in the expanded degeneration setup. We may describe blow-ups of
\mathcal{X}
by adding edges to the re-scaled triangle which create vertices by intersecting at these new integral points.

Assigning tropical
\Delta
-multiplicity.

The next step is to assign a tropical
\Delta
-multiplicity to each edge of
\trop(\mathcal{X})
. By definition, this must be greater or equal to 1. It does not affect the
\Delta
-type of the vertices attached to this edge. Let
e
be an edge of
\Delta
-type
1
and multiplicity
k
attached to the vertex
(a,b,0)
of the triangle (where the coordinates are given in the 3-dimensional cone). Let
b^{\prime}
be the smallest integer such that
b^{\prime}>b
and such that
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},0)
has an edge attached for some
a^{\prime}
. Let
b^{\prime\prime}
be the largest integer such that
b>b^{\prime\prime}
and such that
(a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime},0)
has an edge attached for some
a^{\prime\prime}
. We remark here that the
\Delta
-multiplicity
k
of
e
does not need to equal the number of integral points up until the next edge, i.e.

is independent of the values
b^{\prime}-b
or
b-b^{\prime\prime}
. A similar statement can be made for
e
of
\Delta
-type
2
.

Note that whereas on the level of schemes,
\Delta

-multiplicity preserves the equivalence class of the fibre
\mathcal{X}
. Even though here we work with stacks, it is still important to consider the underlying schemes in order to understand what smoothings exist.

Sliding the edges.

Where edges have tropical
\Delta
-multiplicity greater than 1, we can slide out as many copies of this edge as the number of the multiplicity, as long as the new configuration is compatible with the unbroken condition. These edges cannot cross another parallel edge. Keeping the notation as above, let
e^{\prime}
denote the edge attached to
(a^{\prime},b^{\prime},0)
and let
e^{\prime\prime}
be the edge attached to
(a^{\prime\prime},b^{\prime\prime},0)
. These may be of type 1 or 2. Sliding edges from
e
can be described formally as defining a new unbroken configuration where there are

e^{\prime}
and
e^{\prime\prime}
, including
e

\Delta
-multiplicities of these
r

k
. The unbroken condition imposes some restrictions on how the edges can slide. The situation is similar if we start with an edge
e
of type 2, or we may simultaneously slide edges of type 1 and 2 which are attached to the same point of
Y_{1}\cap Y_{2}
.

Wherever one of these new edges which slides out from
e
intersects another edge, it creates a new vertex, which corresponds to a new bubble on the geometric side.

Criterion for smoothability.

Let
\mathcal{X}
and
B_{0}
as above. Let
v_{0}
be the vertex in
\trop(\mathcal{X})
corresponding to
B_{0}
and let
\{e_{j}\}
be the edges of
\Delta
-type 1 and 2 going through this vertex. There are at most two and each one has a given multiplicity. Let
\{v_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
be the collection of all vertices resulting from intersections which arise when the copies of the edges
e_{i}
are allowed to slide to an unbroken configuration in the way described above. Moreover, we include in the collection
\{v_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}

v_{0}
. Finally, let
\{B_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
be the geometric bubbles corresponding to these vertices.

Proposition 5.19
.

There is a smoothing from
B_{0}
to each of the
B_{i}
and there exist no other smoothings from
B_{0}
. Therefore, a subcheme
Z
supported in
B_{0}
is either fixed or tends towards the interior of a component
B_{i}
.

Proof.

In étale local coordinates, the bubble
B_{0}
is given by the equations (

) describing the family
X[A,B]
, as well as some additional equations which fix certain variables in order to determine
B_{0}
within the family. We consider the nonzero equations defining
B_{0}
. Each of these nonzero equations necessarily contains coordinates corresponding to some
\mathbb{P}^{1}
associated to a
\Delta
-component. The variables in these equations may vary as long as both sides of the equation vary together so that the equalities are preserved. Note that if the base codimension is greater or equal to one, then varying the values on both sides of the equation preserves the equivalence class of
\mathcal{X}
in
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}

B^{\prime}
has a smoothing from
B_{0}
if and only if its equations can be obtained from those of
B_{0}
by letting both sides of some nonzero equations of
B_{0}
tend to zero. Therefore, all equations which define
B_{0}
must hold for

, where some nonzero equations of
B_{0}
may go to zero for

. In particular,
B^{\prime}
cannot be determined by a nonzero equation which does not hold for
B_{0}
. Equivalently, smoothings do not allow us to enter
\Delta
-components in which we were not before.

This is exactly what the set of bubbles
\{B_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
defined above gives us. They correspond to vertices

created by sliding out and intersecting copies of the edges of
\Delta
-types 1 and 2 passing through

. Since each vertex

, must share an edge with
v_{0}
, the set of

-components which are equal to the corresponding bubble

must be a subset of the set of
\Delta_{j}^{(k)}
-components which are equal to
B_{0}
. It follows that no nonzero equation can hold for a bubble in
\{B_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
which does not hold for
B_{0}
. Any vertex which is not created in this way or does not share an edge with
v_{0}
will either correspond to a bubble equal to a
\Delta_{j}^{(k)}
-component which is not equal to
B_{0}
and will therefore be defined by some nonzero equation which does not hold for
B_{0}
; or will correspond to a component which is not equal to any
\Delta_{j}^{(k)}
, which means it must be a

,
y=0
or

which held for

does not hold for it.
∎

6
Hilbert schemes of points on K3 surfaces and MMP

Up until now, we have only been considering the property that
X\rightarrow C
is semistable. In this section, we will discuss what properties our constructions have if we start with some added assumptions. We will want to show that the proper stacks we construct are semistable themselves, in the following sense.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{Y}
be a Deligne-Mumford stack which is flat and locally of finite type over
C
. For any
C
-scheme
S
with a (non-constant) étale morphism
\xi\colon S\rightarrow C
and any
Y\in\Ob(\mathfrak{Y}_{C})
over
C
, we denote the composition map
f\colon\xi^{*}Y\rightarrow C
. We say that
\mathfrak{Y}\rightarrow C
is
semistable
if every such composition map
f
is semistable.

Moreover, if
K_{X}+(X_{0})_{\red}
is semi-ample, i.e.
X\rightarrow C
is a
good semistable model
, we would like to show that the proper stacks constructed in previous sections also satisfy this property, which we extend to stacks in the following way.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{Y}
be a Deligne-Mumford stack which is flat and locally of finite type over
C
. For any
C
-scheme
S
with a (non-constant) étale morphism
\xi\colon S\rightarrow C
and any
Y\in\Ob(\mathfrak{Y}_{C})
over
C
, let
W\coloneqq\xi^{*}Y
and denote by
f\colon W\rightarrow C
the composition map. We say that
\mathfrak{Y}\rightarrow C
is a
good minimal model
if for every such
W
and
f
, the divisor
K_{W/C}+f^{-1}(0)
is semi-ample.

In particular, we will want to consider the case where
X\rightarrow C
is a good type III degeneration of K3 surfaces in the sense of Kulikov
[
Kul77
]
. This means that
\pi\colon X\rightarrow C
is semistable, that its general fibres are smooth K3 surfaces and that the dual complex of its central fibre
X_{0}
is a triangulated sphere. Moreover, there exists a relative logarithmic 2-form
\omega_{\pi}\in H^{0}(X,\Omega^{2}_{X/C}(\log X))
such that
\wedge^{n}\omega_{\pi}\in H^{0}(X,K_{X/C})
is nowhere vanishing. In this case, we will show that such a 2-form on
X\rightarrow C
induces a nowhere degenerate logarithmic 2-form on the proper stacks we constructed.

Most of the results in this section follow easily from the results of Gulbrandsen, Halle, Hulek and Zhang in
[
GHHZ21
]
. For clarity, however, we give certain of the relevant proofs again here, where the details are slightly different for our situation.

6.1
Good semistable and dlt minimal models

Proposition 6.3
.

The stacks
\mathfrak{M}^{m}_{\SWS}
,
\mathfrak{M}^{m}_{\LW}
,
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\PSWS,(\alpha,\beta)}
and
\mathfrak{N}^{m}_{\MR,(\alpha,\beta)}
are semistable degenerations over

. Moreover, they are normal and

-factorial.

Proof.

Semistability follows directly from Lemma 7.3 of
[
GHHZ21

. The stacks are normal as they are semistable. This also applies to any constructions arising from the methods of Maulik and Ranganthan

Remark 6.5
.

In the above proof, we used the assumption that all blow-ups made in the construction of

Remark 6.7
.

Finally, we would like to remark that the fact that we are working with stacks and not schemes here allows us to produce semistable minimal models, as we do not see any quotient singularities. 6.2

We will now assume that

is trivial. By Corollary
6.6

is trivial, for any of the stacks
\mathfrak{Y}\rightarrow C
listed in Proposition

induces a relative holomorphic symplectic logarithmic 2-form on
\mathfrak{Y}\rightarrow C
.

As the dual complex of

is a triangulated sphere, we may choose a labelling of the vertices of this triangulation, such that each vertex is labelled by either

,

or

and each triangle has exactly one of each vertices. All the constructions we made on the local family

can then be extended to a family

of K3 surfaces by making the following modification. Everywhere we blew up the pullback of

inside some scheme, we now blow up instead the pullback of

, where

denotes the union of all
Y_{i}
components in
X
.

Proposition 6.8
.

The stacks listed in Proposition
6.3
are symplectic, in the sense that they carry a nowhere degenerate logarithmic 2-form.

Proof.

Let

be one such construction. Since

is semistable, this follows from Section 7.2 of
[
GHHZ21
]
. The logarithmic 2-form is defined with respect to the divisorial logarithmic structure given by the divisors
0
in
C
and
\mathfrak{Y}_{0}=f^{*}(0)
in
\mathfrak{Y}
.
∎

Multithreshold multipartite graphs with small parts
Teeradej Kittipassorn, Thanaporn Sumalroj

Abstract
A graph is a k k -threshold graph with thresholds θ 1 , θ 2 , … , θ k \theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{k} if we can assign a real number r v r_{v} to each vertex v v such that for any two distinct vertices u u and v v , u ​ v uv is an edge if and only if the number of thresholds not exceeding r u + r v r_{u}+r_{v} is odd. The threshold number of a graph is the smallest k k for which it is a k k -threshold graph. Multithreshold graphs were introduced by Jamison and Sprague as a generalization of classical threshold graphs. They asked for the exact threshold numbers of complete multipartite graphs. Recently, Chen and Hao solved the problem for complete multipartite graphs where each part is not too small, and they asked for the case when each part has size 3 3 . We determine the exact threshold numbers of K 3 , 3 , … , 3 K_{3,3,\dots,3} , K 4 , 4 , … , 4 K_{4,4,\dots,4} and their complements n ​ K 3 nK_{3} , n ​ K 4 nK_{4} . This improves a result of Puleo.

Introduction

A graph
G
is said to be a
threshold graph
if we can assign a real number
r_{v}
to each vertex
v
and there is a real number
\theta
such that for any vertex subset
U
of
G
,
\sum_{v\in U}r_{v}\leq\theta
if and only if
U
is independent in
G
.
The class of threshold graphs was first introduced by Chvátal and Hammer
[
3
]
in 1977 to answer a question in integer linear programming.
As one of the fundamental classes of graphs, properties of threshold graphs have been extensively studied (see
[
7
,
9
,
10
,
14
,
16
]
), and since then many applications of these graphs have been found in various areas (see
[
1
,
6
,
13
,
15
,
17
]
).

Threshold graphs can be characterized in a number of equivalent ways.
For example,
G
is a threshold graph if and only if
G
has no induced subgraph isomorphic to
2K_{2},P_{4}
or
C_{4}
(see
[
3
,
14
]
).
Equivalently, a threshold graph is a graph that can be obtained from the single-vertex graph by repeatedly adding an isolated vertex or a universal vertex (see
[
3
,
14
]
).
Moreover,
G
is a threshold graph if and only if we can assign a real number
r_{v}
to each vertex
v
and there is a real number
\theta
such that for any two distinct vertices
u
and
v
,
uv
is an edge if and only if
r_{u}+r_{v}\geq\theta
(see
[
14
]
).

Recently, Jamison and Sprague
[
12
]
introduced
multithreshold graphs
as a generalization of the well-studied threshold graphs as follows.
A graph
G
is a
k
-threshold
graph with
thresholds

\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{k}
if we can assign a real number
r_{v}
, called a
rank
, to each vertex
v
such that for any two distinct vertices
u
and
v
,

uv
is an edge if and only if the number of thresholds not exceeding
r_{u}+r_{v}
is odd.
Equivalently,

\displaystyle uv\in E(G)\iff r_{u}+r_{v}\in\left[\theta_{2i-1},\theta_{2i}\right)\hskip 2.84544pt\text{for some}\hskip 2.84544pti\in\left\{1,2,\dots,\left\lceil{\frac{k}{2}}\right\rceil\right\}

provided
\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}<\cdots<\theta_{k}
and
\theta_{k+1}=\infty
.
We call such an assignment
r
of ranks a
\left(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{k}\right)
-
representation of
G
.
By a
rank sum of an edge/nonedge

, we mean
r_{u}+r_{v}
.
Note that the case of one threshold agrees with the classical threshold graphs introduced by Chvátal and Hammer
[
3
]
.

Jamison and Sprague
[
12
]
showed that any graph of order
n
is a
k
-threshold graph for some
k\leq\binom{n}{2}
.
The smallest
k
for which a graph

is a
k
-threshold graph is said to be the
threshold number
of

, denoted by
\Theta(G)
.
Observe that
\Theta(H)\leq\Theta(G)
for any induced subgraph

of
G
.
In addition, they found a relationship between the threshold numbers of a graph and its complement.
We give a proof for completeness.

Proposition 1

(Jamison and Sprague
[
12
]
)
.

For any graph
G
, either

\displaystyle\Theta(G^{c})=\Theta(G)\ \text{or}\ \left\{\Theta(G),\displaystyle\Theta(G^{c})\right\}=\left\{2k,2k+1\right\}\ \text{for some}\ k\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Let
k
and
k^{\prime}
be such that
\Theta(G)\in\left\{2k,2k+1\right\}
and
\displaystyle\Theta(G^{c})\in\left\{2k^{\prime},2k^{\prime}+1\right\}
.
Take a
\left(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{\Theta(G)}\right)
-representation
r
of
G
. We may assume that no rank sum equals a threshold by perturbing each threshold to the left.
We may further assume that
r
has
2k+1
thresholds by adding a sufficiently large threshold
\theta_{2k+1}
if neccesary.
We then obtain a
\left(-\theta_{2k+1},-\theta_{2k},\dots,-\theta_{1}\right)
-representation of
\displaystyle G^{c}
from

by reversing the values of the ranks and the thresholds.
Thus
\displaystyle\Theta(G^{c})\leq 2k+1
, and so
k^{\prime}\leq k
.
Similarly,
\Theta(G)\leq 2k^{\prime}+1
, and so
k\leq k^{\prime}
.
Now, we have
k=k^{\prime}
, and hence

\Theta(G),\displaystyle\Theta(G^{c})\in\left\{2k,2k+1\right\}
.
∎

Lemma 7

(Puleo
[

]
)
.

(i)

In a
\left(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{k}\right)
-representation of
nK_{3}
, no two triangles have the same color.

(ii)

In a
\left(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{k}\right)
-representation of
K_{n\times 3}
, no two parts have the same color.

Proof.

We will only prove
(i)
as the proof of
(ii)
is similar.
Let
r
be a
\left(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{k}\right)
-representation of
nK_{3}
where
\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}<\cdots<\theta_{k}

Suppose to the contrary that there are two triangles
T_{x}
and
T_{y}
in

having the same color

.
Thus if
V(T_{x})=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}
and

, then their edge rank sums are as follows:

\displaystyle r_{x_{1}}+r_{x_{2}},r_{y_{1}}+r_{y_{2}}

\displaystyle\in\left[\theta_{2i-1},\theta_{2i}\right),

\displaystyle r_{x_{1}}+r_{x_{3}},r_{y_{1}}+r_{y_{3}}

\displaystyle\in\left[\theta_{2j-1},\theta_{2j}\right),

\displaystyle r_{x_{2}}+r_{x_{3}},r_{y_{2}}+r_{y_{3}}

\displaystyle\in\left[\theta_{2\ell-1},\theta_{2\ell}\right).

Note that at least two ranks out of
\max\left\{r_{x_{1}},r_{y_{1}}\right\},\max\left\{r_{x_{2}},r_{y_{2}}\right\},\max\left\{r_{x_{3}},r_{y_{3}}\right\}

Without loss of generality, let

r_{x_{2}}\leq r_{y_{2}}
.
Write

Then
\theta_{2i-1}\leq r_{x_{1}}+r_{x_{2}}\leq r_{x_{p}}+r_{y_{q}}\leq r_{y_{1}}+r_{y_{2}}<\theta_{2i}

By the definition of thresholds,

is an edge of color

x_{p}y_{q}
is a nonedge in

.
∎

The next lemma is the key idea for obtaining the lower bounds for the threshold numbers.

Lemma 8
.

(i)

In a
\left(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{k}\right)
-representation of

and colors

We will only prove
(i)
as the proof of
(ii)
is similar.
Let
r
be a
\left(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{k}\right)
-representation of
nK_{3}
where
\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}<\cdots<\theta_{k}

Suppose to the contrary that there are two triangles
T_{x}
and

in

of colors
ijj
and

respectively.
Thus if
V(T_{x})=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\}
and
V(T_{y})=\{y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\}
, then their edge rank sums are as follows:

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=r_{x_{1}}+r_{x_{3}}\in\left[\theta_{2i-1},\theta_{2i}\right),

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=r_{x_{1}}+r_{x_{2}}\in\left[\theta_{2j-1},\theta_{2j}\right),

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=r_{x_{2}}+r_{x_{3}}\in\left[\theta_{2j-1},\theta_{2j}\right),

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=r_{y_{1}}+r_{y_{3}}\in\left[\theta_{2i-1},\theta_{2i}\right),

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=r_{y_{1}}+r_{y_{2}}\in\left[\theta_{2\ell-1},\theta_{2\ell}\right),

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle=r_{y_{2}}+r_{y_{3}}\in\left[\theta_{2\ell-1},\theta_{2\ell}\right).

From these rank sums, we can compute the ranks as follows:

\displaystyle r_{x_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{1}+b_{1}-b_{2}}{2},\

\displaystyle r_{x_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{1}+b_{2}-a_{1}}{2},\

\displaystyle r_{x_{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{1}+b_{2}-b_{1}}{2},

\displaystyle r_{y_{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{2}+c_{1}-c_{2}}{2},\

\displaystyle r_{y_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{1}+c_{2}-a_{2}}{2},\

\displaystyle r_{y_{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{2}+c_{2}-c_{1}}{2}.

Without loss of generality, let
a_{1}\leq a_{2}
,
b_{1}\leq b_{2}
and
c_{1}\leq c_{2}
.
Let
D=a_{2}-a_{1}\geq 0
and let

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=b_{1}-b_{2}+c_{1}-c_{2},

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=-b_{1}+b_{2}+c_{1}-c_{2},

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=-b_{1}+b_{2}-c_{1}+c_{2}.

Note that

and
A\leq 0
.
Since
D\geq 0\geq A
, either

,
D\in[B,C]
or
D\in[C,\infty)
.
We obtain a contradiction by the following three claims.

Claim
.

D\notin[A,B]
.

Since
x_{2}y_{3}
is a nonedge, we cannot have
b_{1}\leq r_{x_{2}}+r_{y_{3}}\leq b_{2}
; otherwise,

\displaystyle b_{1}\leq r_{x_{2}}+r_{y_{3}}\leq b_{2}

\displaystyle\iff b_{1}\leq\frac{b_{1}+b_{2}-a_{1}}{2}+\frac{a_{2}+c_{2}-c_{1}}{2}\leq b_{2}

\displaystyle\iff b_{1}-b_{2}+c_{1}-c_{2}\leq a_{2}-a_{1}\leq-b_{1}+b_{2}+c_{1}-c_{2}

\displaystyle c_{1}\leq r_{x_{3}}+r_{y_{2}}\leq c_{2}

\displaystyle\iff c_{1}\leq\frac{a_{1}+b_{2}-b_{1}}{2}+\frac{c_{1}+c_{2}-a_{2}}{2}\leq c_{2}

\displaystyle\iff-b_{1}+b_{2}+c_{1}-c_{2}\leq a_{2}-a_{1}\leq-b_{1}+b_{2}-c_{1}+c_{2}

\displaystyle a_{1}\leq r_{x_{3}}+r_{y_{3}}\leq a_{2}

\displaystyle\iff a_{1}\leq\frac{a_{1}+b_{2}-b_{1}}{2}+\frac{a_{2}+c_{2}-c_{1}}{2}\leq a_{2}

Lemma 15
.

Let
\left\{N,a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{M}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}
be a linearly independent set over
\mathbb{Q}
and

b_{i}=N-a_{i}
for

.
Let
A=\left\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{M}\right\}
and

B=\left\{b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{M}\right\}
.
If

\sum_{i=1}^{S}\alpha_{i}x_{i}+\beta N=0

where
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

x_{i}\in A\cup B
for all
i\in[S]
and
\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
, then

\sum_{i=1}^{S}\alpha_{i}
is even.

Proof.

Suppose that

\sum_{i=1}^{S}\alpha_{i}x_{i}+\beta N=0
where

\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

x_{i}\in A\cup B
for all
i\in[S]
and
\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
.

x_{i}
is either
a_{j_{i}}
or

.

and
\beta_{i}\in\left\{0,1\right\}
.

We find the threshold numbers of

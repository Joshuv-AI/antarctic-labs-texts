Remarks on the Radiative Transfer Equations for Climatology
Claude Bardos, François Golse and Olivier Pironneau

Abstract
Using theoretical and numerical arguments we discuss some of the commonly accepted approximations for the radiative transfer equations in climatology.

Radiative transfer, Electronic Temperature , Integral equation, Numerical analysis

Introduction

Satellite, atmospheric and terrestrial measurements are numerous to support the global warming. Various hypothesis are made to explain the disastrous effect of the GreenHouse Gases (GHG). However a rigorous proof derived from the fundamental equations of physics is not available and one of the problems is the complexity of the physical system of planet Earth in its astronomical setting around the Sun.

In many references such as
kaper
,
MOD
,
CHA
and
FOW
it is explained that the Sun radiates light with a heat flux
Q=1370
Watt/m
2
,
in the frequency range
(0.5,20)\times 10^{14}
Hz corresponding approximately to a black body at temperature of 5800K; 70% of this light intensity reaches the ground because the atmosphere is almost transparent to this spectrum and about 30% is reflected back by the clouds or the ocean, snow, etc (albedo).
The Earth behaves almost like a black body at temperature
T_{e}=288K
and as such radiates rays of frequencies
\nu
in the infrared spectrum
(0.03,0.6)\times 10^{14}
Hz.

“The absorption coefficient variation with frequencies is such that the atmosphere is essentially transparent to solar radiation” (
FOW
, p65).

‘‘Solar radiation that is not absorbed or reflected by the atmosphere (for example by clouds) reaches the surface of the Earth. The Earth absorbs most of the energy reaching its surface, a small fraction is reflected
1
1

1

https://public.wmo.int/en/sun%E2%80%99s-impact-earth
.”

Carbone dioxide renders the atmosphere opaque to infrared radiations around
20
THz . Hence increasing its proportion in air increases the absorption coefficient in that range. It is believed to be one of the causes of global warming. However it must be more complex because some experimental measurements show that increasing opacity decreases the temperature at high altitude, above the clouds
DUF
.

In the framework of the radiative transfer equations, one can studies the conjectures cited above. To support theoretical claims, the numerical solver developed in
CBOP
,
OP
and
FGOP
is used. We will address 4 questions:

•

What is the effect of increasing an altitude dependent absorption coefficient?

•

Is it true that sunlight crosses the Earth atmosphere unaffected?

•

Is 30% of albedo equivalent to 30% reduction of the Sun radiative energy ?

•

What is the effect on the temperature of increasing the absorption coefficient in a specific frequency range?

The Radiative Transfer Equations for a Stratified Atmosphere

Finding the temperature
T
in a fluid heated by electromagnetic radiations is a complex problem because interactions of photons with atoms in the medium involve rather intricate quantum phenomena.
Assuming local thermodynamic equilibrium leads to a well-defined electronic temperature. In that case, one can write a kinetic equation for the radiative intensity
I_{\nu}({\bm{x}},{\bm{\omega}},t)
at time
t
, at position
{\bm{x}}
and
in the direction
\omega
for photons of frequency
\nu
, in terms of the temperature field
T({\bm{x}},t)
.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}I_{\nu}+{\bm{\omega}}\cdot\nabla I_{\nu}+\rho\bar{\kappa}_{\nu}a_{\nu}\left[I_{\nu}-{\tfrac{1}{4\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}}p({\bm{\omega}},{\bm{\omega}}^{\prime})I_{\nu}({\bm{\omega}}^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\omega^{\prime}\right]

(1)

\displaystyle=\rho\bar{\kappa}_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})[B_{\nu}(T)-I_{\nu}]

\displaystyle.

In this equation
c
is the speed of light,
\rho
is the density of the fluid,
\nabla
designates the gradient with respect to the position
{\bm{x}}
, while

B_{\nu}(T)=\frac{2\hbar\nu^{3}}{c^{2}[{\rm e}^{\frac{\hbar\nu}{kT}}-1]}

(2)

is the Planck function at temperature
T
, with
\hbar
the Planck constant and
k
the Boltzmann constant. Recall the Stefan-Boltzmann identity,

\int_{0}^{\infty}B_{\nu}(T)\mathrm{d}\nu=\bar{\sigma}T^{4}\,,\qquad\bar{\sigma}=\frac{2\pi^{4}k^{4}}{15c^{2}\hbar^{3}}\,,

(3)

where
\pi\bar{\sigma}
is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant.

The intricacy of the interaction of photons with the atoms of the medium is contained in the mass-absorption
\bar{\kappa}_{\nu}
, which is theoretically the result of vibration and rotation atomic resonance, but for practical purpose the fraction of radiative intensity at frequency
\nu
that is absorbed by fluid per unit length.
The coefficient

a_{\nu}\in(0,1)
is the scattering albedo, and
\frac{1}{4\pi}p({\bm{\omega}},{\bm{\omega}}^{\prime})\mathrm{d}{\bm{\omega}}
is the probability that an incident ray of light with direction
{\bm{\omega}}^{\prime}
scatters in the infinitesimal element of solid angle
\mathrm{d}{\bm{\omega}}
centered at
{\bm{\omega}}
.

The kinetic equation (
1
) is coupled to the temperature - or energy conservation - equations of the fluid . When thermal diffusion is small, the system decouples and energy balance becomes:

\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\bar{\kappa}_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}I_{\nu}({\bm{\omega}})\mathrm{d}{\bm{\omega}}-4\pi B_{\nu}(T)\right)\mathrm{d}\nu=0\,.

(4)

When the wave source is far in the direction
z
the problem becomes one-dimensional in
z
and the radiative intensity scattered in the direction
\omega
depends only on
\mu
, the cosine of the angle between
\omega
and
Oz
. Consequently the system becomes,

\displaystyle\mu\partial_{z}I_{\nu}+\rho\bar{\kappa}_{\nu}I_{\nu}=\rho\bar{\kappa}_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})B_{\nu}(T)+\frac{\rho\bar{\kappa}_{\nu}a_{\nu}}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}p(\mu,\mu^{\prime})I_{\nu}(z,\mu^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\mu^{\prime},

(5)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\bar{\kappa}_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})\left(B_{\nu}(T)-\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}I_{\nu}\mathrm{d}\mu\right)\mathrm{d}\nu=0,\quad z\in(0,H),~~|\mu|<1,~~\nu\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\,.

For this system to be mathematically well posed, the radiation intensity
I_{\nu}
must be given on the domain boundary where radiation enters. For example,

~I_{\nu}(H,-\mu)=Q^{-}(\mu),~I(0,\mu)=Q^{+}(\mu),\quad 0<\mu<1\,.

(6)

2.1
Semi-analytical Solution when the scattering is isotropic

Isotropic scattering is modelled by taking
p\equiv 1
. By introducing an optical depth (in
FOW
and others, the signs are changed),

\tau=\int_{0}^{z}\rho(\eta)d\eta,\text{ $\rho$ becomes $1$ and $z$ becomes $\tau\in(0,Z)$, $Z=\int_{0}^{H}\rho(\eta)d\eta$.}

(7)

Let us denote the exponential integral by

E_{p}(X):=\int_{0}^{1}e^{-X/\mu}\mu^{p-2}{d}\mu\,.

(8)

Define

\displaystyle J_{\nu}(\tau)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}I_{\nu}d\mu,\quad S_{\nu}(\tau)=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left(e^{-\frac{\kappa_{\nu}\tau}{\mu}}Q^{+}_{\nu}(\mu)+e^{-\frac{\kappa_{\nu}(Z-\tau)}{\mu}}Q^{-}_{\nu}(\mu)\right){d}\mu.

(9)

The problem is equivalent to a functional integral equation:

\left.\begin{aligned} &J_{\nu}(\tau)=S_{\nu}(\tau)+\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{2}\int_{0}^{Z}E_{1}(\kappa_{\nu}|\tau-t|)\left((1-a_{\nu})B_{\nu}(T(t))+a_{\nu}J_{\nu}(t)\right)dt\,,\\
&\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-a_{\nu})\kappa_{\nu}B_{\nu}(T(\tau)){d}\nu=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-a_{\nu})\kappa_{\nu}J_{\nu}(\tau){d}\nu,\end{aligned}\right\}

(10)

Once
J_{\nu},T
are known,
I_{\nu}
can be recovered by

\displaystyle I_{\nu}(\tau,\mu)=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{\kappa_{\nu}\tau}{\mu}}I_{\nu}(0,\mu)\mathbf{1}_{\mu>0}+e^{\frac{\kappa_{\nu}(Z-\tau)}{\mu}}I_{\nu}(Z,\mu)\mathbf{1}_{\mu<0}

(11)

\displaystyle+{\bf 1}_{\mu>0}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\frac{\kappa_{\nu}|\tau-t|}{|\mu|}}\tfrac{\kappa_{\nu}}{|\mu|}\left((1-a_{\nu})\kappa_{\nu}B_{\nu}(T(t))+a_{\nu}\kappa_{\nu}J_{\nu}(t)\right)dt

\displaystyle+{\bf 1}_{\mu<0}\int_{\tau}^{Z}e^{-\frac{\kappa_{\nu}|\tau-t|}{|\mu|}}\tfrac{\kappa_{\nu}}{|\mu|}\left((1-a_{\nu})\kappa_{\nu}B_{\nu}(T(t))+a_{\nu}\kappa_{\nu}J_{\nu}(t)\right)dt.

Remark 1

Notice that two solutions
(T,J_{\nu}),(T^{\prime},J^{\prime}_{\nu})
with
J_{\nu}\equiv J^{\prime}_{\nu}
will have
T\equiv T^{\prime}
but not necessarily
I_{\nu}\equiv I_{\nu}^{\prime}
.

Increasing an Altitude Dependent Absorption Coeficient Decreases the Temperature in the atmosphere

The following argument goes against a common belief which claims that increasing the absorption coefficient - which GHG do indeed - implies an increase of temperature in the atmosphere. Consider the case of an altitude dependent absorption coefficient which we write as
r(z)\kappa
with
\kappa
constant.

Proposition 1

If
\kappa
is independent of
\nu
but function of the altitude and
T
is monotone decreasing with altitude, then, increasing
\kappa
anywhere will decrease the temperature.

Proof

The proof is a straightforward consequence of the concept of optical length (
7
).
Here, let

\tau=\int_{0}^{z}{r}(\zeta)d\zeta.

Observe that
\partial_{z}=\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial\tau}\partial_{\tau}={r}(z)\partial_{z}
; consequently
{r}
disappears from (
5
),

H
is replaced by
Z=\int_{0}^{H}{r}(\zeta)d\zeta
, and
z
is replaced by
\tau
.

When
\kappa
and
a
are constant,
\displaystyle\bar{I}_{r}=\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\nu}d\nu
is solution of the ”grey model”:

\displaystyle\mu\partial_{\tau}\bar{I}_{1}+\kappa\bar{I}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\left(\kappa(1-a)\bar{I}_{1}+\kappa a\int_{-1}^{1}p(\mu,\mu^{\prime}){\bar{I}_{1}}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})d\mu^{\prime}\right)d\mu,

(12)

\displaystyle I_{r}(H,-\mu)=0,\quad\bar{I}_{r}(0,\mu)=Q^{+}(\mu),\quad 0<\mu<1.

where the Stefan-Boltzmann identity has been used:

\displaystyle\sigma T^{4}=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}{\bar{I}_{r}}\mathrm{d}\mu.

Let
\bar{I}_{1}(\tau,\mu)
be the solution of (
12
).
We want to compare
I_{{r}+\delta{r}}
with
I_{{r}}
when
\delta{r}
has a small support near altitude
y
. By (
7
),

\displaystyle\bar{I}_{{r}+\delta{r}}(z)=\bar{I}_{1}(\int_{0}^{z}({r}(\zeta)+\delta{r}(\zeta))d\zeta)\approx\bar{I}_{1}(\tau(z))+\frac{d}{d\tau}\bar{I}_{1}(\tau(y))\delta{r}(y)

(13)

\displaystyle\implies\qquad\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\bar{I}_{{r}+\delta{r}}(z)d\mu-\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\bar{I}_{r}(z)d\mu\approx\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\ \bar{I}_{1}(\tau)d\mu\right)|_{\tau(y)}\delta{r}(y)

Finally from (
3
),

T_{{r}+\delta{r}}(z)-T_{{r}}(z)\approx 4\sigma T^{3}(y)\frac{\delta{r}(y)}{{r}(y)}\frac{dT_{r}}{dz}(y).

(14)

\square

Sunlight Crosses the Earth Atmosphere Unaffected?

It is true that the absorption coefficient
\kappa_{\nu}
is small in the frequency range of
visible light; but does it imply that the light source can be used as a boundary condition at altitude zero instead of altitude H, the top of the troposphere?

First eliminate the
z
dependency of the coefficients by using the optical length
\tau
defined in (
7
).
The following result gives an interesting answer to this question.

Proposition 2

Assume
a_{\nu}=0
and assume that for some
\nu^{*}
,

\epsilon:=\max_{\nu>\nu^{*}}\kappa_{\nu}<<1\text{ and }\quad\kappa_{\nu}Q^{0}_{\nu}|_{\nu>\nu^{*}}=O(1).

Let
I_{\nu},T
and
I^{\prime}_{\nu},T^{\prime}
be solutions of (
5
),(
6
) respectively with

\displaystyle Q^{+}_{\nu}(\mu)=0,\quad Q^{-}_{\nu}(\mu)=\mu Q^{0}_{\nu},\quad 0<\mu<1.

(15)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle Q^{\prime}}^{+}_{\nu}=\mu{\rm e}^{-\frac{\kappa_{\nu}Z}{\mu}}Q^{0}_{\nu},\quad{Q^{\prime}}^{-}_{\nu}(\mu)=0,\quad 0<\mu<1.

Then
T^{\prime}(\tau)=T(\tau)+O(\epsilon)
and

I^{\prime}_{\nu}-I_{\nu}=\mu{\rm e}^{-\frac{\kappa_{\nu}Z}{\mu}}Q^{0}_{\nu}\left({\bf 1}_{\mu>0}{\rm e}^{-\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{\mu}\tau}+{\bf 1}_{\mu<0}{\rm e}^{\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{\mu}\tau}\right)+O(\epsilon).

Proof

Note that

\left.\begin{aligned} &\mu\partial_{\tau}I_{\nu}+\kappa_{\nu}(I_{\nu}-B_{\nu}(T))=0,\quad\nu<\nu^{*},\\
&I_{\nu}(0)|_{\mu>0}=0,\quad I_{\nu}(Z)_{\mu<0}=Q^{-}_{\nu}(-\mu),\quad\quad\nu<\nu^{*},\\
&I_{\nu}\approx I_{\nu}^{*}(\mu)\text{ independent of $\tau$},~\quad\nu>\nu^{*}.\\
&I^{*}_{\nu}|_{\mu>0}=0,\quad I^{*}_{\nu}|_{\mu<0}=Q^{-}_{\nu}(-\mu),\quad\quad\nu>\nu^{*}.\\
&\int_{0}^{\infty}\kappa_{\nu}B_{\nu}(T)d\nu\approx\int_{0}^{\nu^{*}}\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}I_{\nu}\,d\mu d\nu+\int_{\nu^{*}}^{\infty}\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{2}\int_{-1}^{0}Q^{-}_{\nu}(-\mu)\,d\mu d\nu\end{aligned}\right\}

(16)

Now
I_{\nu}^{\prime}
is defined by (
5
) but with

I^{\prime}_{\nu}(0)|_{\mu>0}={Q^{\prime}}_{\nu}^{+}(\mu),\qquad I^{\prime}_{\nu}(Z)|_{\mu<0}=0.

By the same argument, when
\nu>\nu^{*}
,
I^{\prime}_{\nu}\approx{I^{\prime}}^{*}_{\nu}(\mu)
, independent of
\tau
, and

\left.\begin{aligned} &\mu\partial_{\tau}I^{\prime}_{\nu}+\kappa_{\nu}(I^{\prime}_{\nu}-B_{\nu}(T^{\prime}))=0,\quad\nu<\nu^{*},\\
&I^{\prime}_{\nu}(0)|_{\mu>0}={Q^{\prime}}_{\nu}^{+}(\mu),\qquad I^{\prime}_{\nu}(Z)|_{\mu<0}=0,\quad\quad\nu<\nu^{*},\\
&\int_{0}^{\infty}\kappa_{\nu}B_{\nu}(T^{\prime})d\nu\approx\int_{0}^{\nu^{*}}\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}I^{\prime}_{\nu}d\mu d\nu+\int_{\nu^{*}}^{\infty}\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}{Q^{\prime}}^{+}_{\nu}(\mu)d\mu d\nu\end{aligned}\right\}

(17)

Let
I^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}=I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu}
. It holds

\left.\begin{aligned} &\mu\partial_{\tau}I^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}+\kappa_{\nu}(I^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}-(B_{\nu}(T)-B_{\nu}(T^{\prime}))=0,\quad\quad\nu<\nu^{*},\\
&\int_{0}^{\infty}\kappa_{\nu}(B_{\nu}(T)-B_{\nu}(T^{\prime}))d\nu=\int_{0}^{\nu^{*}}\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}I^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}\,d\mu d\nu\\
&+\int_{\nu^{*}}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(Q^{-}_{\nu}(\mu)-{Q^{\prime}}^{+}_{\nu}(\mu))\,d\mu\right)d\nu\\
&I^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}(0)|_{\mu>0}=-{Q^{\prime}}_{\nu}^{+}(\mu),\qquad I^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}(Z)|_{\mu<0}=Q_{\nu}^{-}(-\mu),\quad\quad\nu<\nu^{*}.\end{aligned}\right\}

(18)

We notice that
T=T^{\prime}
and
Q_{\nu}^{-}(\mu)={\rm e}^{-\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{\mu}Z}{Q^{\prime}}^{+}_{\nu}(\mu)
implies

\left.\begin{aligned} &\mu\partial_{\tau}I^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}+\kappa_{\nu}I^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}=0,\quad\nu<\nu^{*},\\
&\int_{0}^{\nu^{*}}\left(\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}I^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}\,d\mu\right)d\nu=\int_{\nu^{*}}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-{\rm e}^{-\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{\mu}Z}){Q}^{+}_{\nu}(\mu)\,d\mu\right)d\nu\\
&I^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}(0)|_{\mu>0}=-{Q^{\prime}}_{\nu}^{+}(\mu),\qquad I^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}(Z)|_{\mu<0}={\rm e}^{\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{\mu}Z}{Q^{\prime}}_{\nu}^{+}(-\mu)\end{aligned}\right\}

(19)

Let
{Q^{\prime}}_{\nu}^{+}(\mu)=\mu Q^{0}_{\nu}
. Then

I^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}(0)|_{\mu>0}=-\mu Q^{0}_{\nu},\qquad I^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}(Z)|_{\mu<0}=-\mu{\rm e}^{\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{\mu}Z}Q^{0}_{\nu}.

Consequently, from (
19

11

\displaystyle

\displaystyle I^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}(\tau,\mu)=-\mu Q^{0}_{\nu}\left({\bf 1}_{\mu>0}{\rm e}^{-\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{\mu}\tau}+{\bf 1}_{\mu<0}{\rm e}^{\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{\mu}\tau}\right).

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}I^{\prime\prime}_{\nu}(\tau)d\mu=-Q^{0}_{\nu}E_{3}(\kappa_{\nu}\tau)+Q^{0}_{\nu}E_{3}(\kappa_{\nu}\tau)=0,

We have satisfied (

O(\kappa_{\nu}^{2})

\kappa_{\nu}(1-{\rm e}^{-\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{\mu}Z})\mu Q^{0}_{\nu}=\kappa_{\nu}^{2}ZQ^{0}_{\nu}+O(\kappa_{\nu}^{3})\approx 0,\quad\nu>\nu^{*}

\square

-dependent Case

The following argument sheds some light on the conditions needed to obtain a cooling (resp. heating) from a local increase of the absorption coefficient.

Conjecture 1

Let
I^{\varepsilon}_{\nu}(\tau,\mu),T^{\varepsilon}(\tau)
be the solution of (
5
) when

\kappa^{\epsilon}_{\nu}:=\kappa+\epsilon\delta\kappa_{\nu}\,,

(41)

Let
I^{0}_{\nu}(\tau,\mu),T^{0}(\tau)
be the solution of (
5
) with
\kappa_{\nu}=\kappa
constant.
When
\delta\kappa_{\nu}\approx{\bm{\delta}_{\nu^{*}}}
, the Dirac mass at
\nu^{*}
, and
\kappa
is small, then the sign of
\displaystyle\frac{dT}{d{\varepsilon}}(\tau)|_{{\varepsilon}=0}
is governed by the sign of

\frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}I^{0}_{\nu^{*}}(\tau)d\mu-B_{\nu^{*}}(T^{0}(\tau)).

(42)

7.1
Calculus of Variations Support of the Conjecture

It will be convenient to define the Planck function in terms of the quantity given by the Stefan-Boltzmann law

\Phi:=\sigma T^{4}\,.

Henceforth we denote

B_{\nu}(T)=b_{\nu}(\Phi)\,.

We seek to study how the average radiation intensity or the temperature is altered if the absorption is modified on various intervals in the frequency variable.
We expect that the simplest situation corresponds to absorption of the form (
41
) with
\kappa>0
independent of
\nu
, while
0<\epsilon\ll 1
. This is of course an extremely general formulation, but in practice one could think of

\delta\kappa_{\nu}:=\kappa\mathbf{1}_{\nu_{1}<\nu<\nu_{2}}\,.

This corresponds to multiplying the absorption by
(1+\epsilon)
in the frequency interval
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
only, and leaving it invariant for all the other frequencies.

We assume that the scattering is isotropic with constant rate
\lambda:=\kappa a\geq 0
.

Henceforth, we consider the radiative transfer equation on
(0,Z)\times(-1,1)\times{\mathbb{R}}
,

\mu\partial_{\tau}I^{\epsilon}_{\nu}+\kappa^{\epsilon}_{\nu}(I^{\epsilon}_{\nu}-b_{\nu}(\Phi^{\epsilon}))+\lambda(I^{\epsilon}_{\nu}-\widetilde{I}^{\epsilon}_{\nu})=0\,,\qquad\Big\langle\kappa^{\epsilon}_{\nu}(\widetilde{I}^{\epsilon}_{\nu}-b_{\nu}(\Phi^{\epsilon}))\Big\rangle=0\,,

(43)

with the boundary conditions

I^{\epsilon}_{\nu}(0,\mu)=\mu Q^{0}_{\nu},\quad I^{\epsilon}_{\nu}(Z,-\mu)=0\,,\qquad\qquad 0<\!\mu\!<1,

(44)

and the notations

\widetilde{\phi}:=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\phi(\mu)d\mu\,,\qquad\Big\langle\psi\Big\rangle:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(\nu)d\nu\,.

First we study the case
\epsilon=0
: since
\kappa
is constant, one can average in frequency, and set

{\bar{I}^{0}}(\tau,\mu):=\Big\langle I^{0}_{\nu}(\tau,\mu)\Big\rangle,\qquad\widetilde{\bar{I}^{0}}(\tau)=\Big\langle b_{\nu}(\Phi^{0})\Big\rangle=\Phi^{0}(\tau)\,.

One finds

\mu\partial_{\tau}{\bar{I}^{0}}(\tau,\mu)+\kappa({\bar{I}^{0}}(\tau,\mu)-\Phi^{0})+\lambda({\bar{I}^{0}}(\tau,\mu)-\widetilde{\bar{I}^{0}}(\tau))=0\,,

or, equivalently

\displaystyle\mu\partial_{\tau}{\bar{I}^{0}}(\tau,\mu)+(\kappa+\lambda)({\bar{I}^{0}}(\tau,\mu)-\widetilde{\bar{I}^{0}}(\tau))=0\,,\qquad\tau>0\,,

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bar{I}^{0}}(0,\mu)=\mu\Big\langle Q^{0}_{\nu}\Big\rangle\,,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad 0<\!\mu\!<1.

Once
\widetilde{\bar{I}^{0}}
is known, one recovers
I^{0}_{\nu}
by using the semi-analytical formula (
11
), which in this case is

\displaystyle I^{0}_{\nu}(\tau,\mu)=

\displaystyle\mathbf{1}_{0<\mu<1}\mu e^{-\frac{(\kappa+\lambda)\tau}{\mu}}Q^{0}_{\nu}+\mathbf{1}_{0<\mu<1}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-\frac{(\kappa+\lambda)(\tau-y)}{\mu}}\frac{\kappa b_{\nu}(\widetilde{\bar{I}^{0}}(y))+\lambda\widetilde{I}^{0}_{\nu}(y)}{\mu}dy

(45)

\displaystyle+\mathbf{1}_{-1<\mu<0}\int_{\tau}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{(\kappa+\lambda)(y-\tau)}{|\mu|}}\frac{\kappa b_{\nu}(\widetilde{\bar{I}^{0}}(y))+\lambda\widetilde{I}^{0}_{\nu}(y)}{|\mu|}dy\,.

This is precisely the solution of the Milne problem in the case of radiative transfer in a grey atmosphere.

In order to study the effect of the perturbation of the absorption defined above, we seek to compute

I^{\prime}_{\nu}(\tau,\mu)=\frac{\partial I_{\nu}^{\epsilon}(\tau,\mu)}{\partial\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}\,,\quad\Phi^{\prime}(\tau)=\frac{\partial\Phi^{\epsilon}(\tau)}{\partial\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}\,,\quad u^{\prime}(\tau,\mu)=\frac{\partial u^{\epsilon}(\tau,\mu)}{\partial\epsilon}\Big|_{\epsilon=0}\,.

Let
\dot{b}_{\nu}(\Phi)=\partial_{\Phi}b_{\nu}(\Phi)
. Let us differentiate in
\epsilon
both sides of the equations (
43
) at
\epsilon=0
: one easily finds that

\displaystyle\mu\partial_{\tau}I^{\prime}_{\nu}+\kappa(I^{\prime}_{\nu}-\dot{b}_{\nu}(\Phi^{0})\Phi^{\prime}_{0})+\lambda(I^{\prime}_{\nu}-\widetilde{I}^{\prime}_{\nu})+\delta\kappa_{\nu}(I^{0}_{\nu}-b_{\nu}(\Phi^{0}))=0\,,

\displaystyle\kappa\Big\langle\widetilde{I}^{\prime}_{\nu}\Big\rangle-\kappa\underbrace{\Big\langle\dot{b}_{\nu}(\Phi^{0})\Big\rangle}_{=1}\Phi^{\prime}+\Big\langle\delta\kappa_{\nu}(\widetilde{I}^{0}_{\nu}-b_{\nu}(\Phi^{0}))\Big\rangle=0\,,

and we can recast the second equality as

\Phi^{\prime}(\tau)=\widetilde{\bar{I^{0}}}^{\prime}(\tau)+\tfrac{1}{\kappa}\Big\langle\delta\kappa_{\nu}(\widetilde{I}^{0}_{\nu}(\tau)-b_{\nu}(\Phi^{0}(\tau)))\Big\rangle=\widetilde{\bar{I^{0}}}^{\prime}(\tau)+\tfrac{1}{\kappa}\Big\langle\delta\kappa_{\nu}(\widetilde{I}^{0}_{\nu}(\tau)-b_{\nu}(\widetilde{\bar{I}^{0}}(\tau)))\Big\rangle\,.

(46)

Averaging in frequency the RT equation, we find that

\mu\partial_{\tau}{\bar{I^{0}}}^{\prime}(\tau,\mu)+\kappa({\bar{I^{0}}}^{\prime}(\tau,\mu)-\underbrace{\Big\langle\dot{b}_{\nu}(\Phi^{0}(\tau))\Big\rangle}_{=1}\Phi^{\prime}(\tau))+\Big\langle\delta\kappa_{\nu}(I^{0}_{\nu}(\tau,\mu)-b_{\nu}(\Phi^{0}(\tau)))\Big\rangle=0\,,

and we can further eliminate
\Phi^{\prime}(\tau)
between the last two equations, to find,

\mu\partial_{\tau}{\bar{I^{0}}}^{\prime}(\tau,\mu)+(\kappa+\lambda)({\bar{I^{0}}}^{\prime}(\tau,\mu)-\widetilde{\bar{I^{0}}}^{\prime}(\tau))=-\Big\langle\delta\kappa_{\nu}(I^{0}_{\nu}(\tau,\mu)-\widetilde{I}^{0}_{\nu}(\tau))\Big\rangle\,.

(47)

If
\delta\kappa
is small everywhere but large near
\nu^{*}
and if
\kappa+\lambda\ll 1
so that the righthandside dominates the seccond term on the left, then (
47
) becomes

\mu\partial_{\tau}{\bar{I^{0}}}^{\prime}(\tau,\mu)=-\delta\kappa(I^{0}_{\nu^{*}}(\tau,\mu)-\widetilde{I}^{0}_{\nu^{*}}(\tau))\,.

Together with (
46
) it gives the sign of
T^{\prime}(z)
by,

\Phi^{\prime}(\tau)=-\delta\kappa\Big|\int^{\tau}\widetilde{\frac{I^{0}_{\nu^{*}}(t,\mu)}{\mu}}dt\Big|+\frac{\delta\kappa}{\kappa}(\widetilde{I}^{0}_{\nu^{*}}(\tau)-b_{\nu^{*}}(\widetilde{\bar{I}^{0}}(\tau)))\,.

(48)

when
\kappa\ll 1
the second terms dominate the first one and it is possible to obtain a positive sign when
\widetilde{I}^{0}_{\nu^{*}}(\tau)>b_{\nu^{*}}(\widetilde{\bar{I}^{0}}(\tau))
.

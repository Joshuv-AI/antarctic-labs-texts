Rotational KMS states and type I conformal nets
Roberto Longo and Yoh Tanimoto

Abstract
We consider KMS states on a local conformal net on S 1 S^{1} with respect to rotations. We prove that, if the conformal net is of type I, namely if it admits only type I DHR representations, then the extremal KMS states are the Gibbs states in an irreducible representation. Completely rational nets, the U ⁡ ( 1 ) \mathrm{U}(1) -current net, the Virasoro nets and their finite tensor products are shown to be of type I. In the completely rational case, we also give a direct proof that all factorial KMS states are Gibbs states.



1
Introduction

QFT, Quantum Field Theory, was originally designed to describe finitely many quantum, relativistic particles, with particle
creation/annihilation due to the interaction. In this view, statistical mechanics aspects due to an infinitely
many particle distribution are absent. There are however extreme situations where QFT shows a thermodynamical behaviour,
a most important one being the black hole background Hawking radiation, that lead to consider thermal states in QFT.

As is known, thermal equilibrium states at infinite volume in quantum statistical mechanics are characterized
by the KMS condition for the dynamical flow, a one-parameter automorphism group
\alpha_{t}
of the observable
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathfrak{A}
.
A state
\varphi
, i.e.​ a positive linear functional on
\mathfrak{A}
normalized with
\varphi(1)=1
, satisfies the KMS condition w.r.t.​
\tau

at inverse temperature
\beta>0
if, for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{A}
, there is a function
F_{xy}

analytic in the strip
S_{\beta}=\{0<\mathrm{Im}\,z<\beta\}
, bounded and continuous on the closure
\overline{S_{\beta}}
,
such that

\begin{gathered}F_{xy}(t)=\varphi\big(x\alpha_{t}(y)\big)\ ,\\
F_{xy}(t+i\beta)=\varphi\big(\alpha_{t}(y)x\big)\ ,\end{gathered}

At finite volume, where the degrees of freedom are finite,
KMS states are Gibbs states:
\varphi(x)=\Tr(e^{-\beta H}x)/\Tr(e^{-\beta H})
with
H
the Hamiltonian; at infinite volume,
Gibbs states might not exist as
e^{-\beta H}
is not necessarily trace class,
yet the KMS condition is preserved under the infinite volume limit.

From the mathematical viewpoint, KMS states are of most importance, being related to the Tomita-Takesaki modular theory of von Neumann algebras. The KMS condition measures, in a sense, the deviation of the state
\varphi
from the tracial property
\varphi(xy)=\varphi(yx)
.
In view of an infinite-dimensional quantum index theorem, one expects QFT to be the underlying framework and the role of the (super)-trace to be played by (super)-KMS states. The description of the KMS states then turns out a natural problem with different motivations.

This paper concerns KMS states in low dimensional CFT, Conformal Quantum Field Theory. On one hand the mathematical structure of CFT is much better understood, with very interesting connections with other mathematical subjects,
and in particular the Operator Algebraic approach
is powerful and deep. On the other hand CFT is of much interest in Physics in various situations, e.g.​ Critical Phenomena or AdS/CFT correspondence.

CFT in
(1+1)
-dimensions is an extension of the tensor product of two one-dimensional
(one could say
\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)
-dimensional) CFT, so initially one has to study
CFT on the real line or on its compactification
S^{1}
. The real line and the circle pictures are equivalent,
however, the physical Hamiltonian as QFT is the one associated to the translation flow in the real line picture.
The conformal Hamiltonian is the one associated with the rotation flow in the circle picture and one can usually extract
more easily information from the conformal Hamiltonian since its spectrum is discrete.

An analysis of the KMS states w.r.t.​ the translation flow has been given in
[
CLTW12a
,
CLTW12b
]
.
The main result in
[
CLTW12a
]
is that, in the completely rational case, for every fixed inverse temperature
\beta>0
,
there exists a unique KMS state w.r.t.​ translations, the geometric KMS state.
In the non-rational case, however, there might be uncountably many KMS states. They are all described for
the
U(1)
-current net and possibly all for the Virasoro nets
[
CLTW12b
]
.

The purpose of this paper is to investigate the KMS states with respect to the rotation flow.
In the rotational case, the first point to clarify is the choice of the
C^{*}
-algebra
that supports the rotational flow and on which the KMS state is to be defined.
Such a choice is natural and well known in the translation case: the
C^{*}
-algebra
generated by the local von Neumann algebras associated to bounded intervals of the real line.
On the other hand, the intervals of the circle do not form an inductive family
and a more thoughtful construction is necessary.
A universal
C^{*}
-algebras was defined by Fredenhagen, and a different construction is in
[
Fre90
,
GL92
]
.
We shall explain in detail the construction as we need it.

We shall first give a general, complete description in the completely rational case:
every extremal KMS state is a Gibbs state in some irreducible representation.
We shall make use of the structure of the universal
C^{*}
-algebra in this case
[
CCHW13
]
;
a similar description for super-KMS states in this case is due to Hillier
[
Hil15
]
.
Rotational KMS states in the completely rational case were also studied in
[
Iov15
]
.

Our results are not restricted to the rational case.
Indeed, we shall prove that any extremal rotational KMS state on a large class of non-rational conformal nets
is a Gibbs state in some irreducible representation.
The point is that, in general, the GNS representation with respect to a KMS state might
be of type II or III and could not be decomposed uniquely into irreducible (type I) representations.
We exclude this possibility for many important conformal nets.

Actually, we prove that some conformal nets are of type I, namely they do not have type II or III representations at all.
Moreover, at the moment, no example of conformal net not of type I is known.
It is possible that diffeomorphism covariance implies the type I property.
One can understand how general the type I property is by the following.
Suppose
{\mathcal{A}}
is a conformal net such that, for any given
\lambda>0
,
there exists at most countably many irreducible representations
\rho
of
{\mathcal{A}}

such that
\lambda
if the lowest eigenvalue of the conformal Hamiltonian

L_{0}^{\rho}
of
\rho
. Then
{\mathcal{A}}
is of type I.
Many conformal nets are then immediately shown to be of type I by this criterion.
Among them are the Virasoro nets and the
U(1)
-current net. Their finite tensor products
can be shown to be of type I by a separate argument.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we recall our operator-algebraic setting for conformal field theory and
introduce our main dynamical system, the universal
C^{*}
-algebra.
The fundamental examples of KMS state, the Gibbs states, are also introduced.
In Section 3, we present our classification result of KMS states.
First we are concerned with the completely rational case where the structure of the universal

C^{*}
-algebra is completely understood, then we pass to the general case.
We determine that an extremal KMS state on a type I net is a Gibbs state, and
prove that some well-known nets are of type I.
The problem of the possible occurrence of type II and III representations naturally arises here and we make some observations.
In Section 4, we discuss possible applications of our results.

Preliminaries

2.1
Conformal nets and their representations

Let us recall our mathematical framework for conformal field theory
on the compactified one-dimensional spacetime
S^{1}
. See also
[
CLTW12a
]
.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}
be the set of open, connected, non-empty and non-dense subsets (intervals) of the circle
S^{1}
.
A
(local) Möbius covariant net
is a triple
({\mathcal{A}},U,\Omega)
where
{\mathcal{A}}
is a map that assigns to each
I\in{\mathcal{I}}

a von Neumann algebra
{\mathcal{A}}(I)
on a common Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
and satisfies the
following requirements:

1.

(Isotony)
If
I_{1}\subset I_{2}
, then
{\mathcal{A}}(I_{1})\subset{\mathcal{A}}(I_{2})
.

2.

(Locality)
If
I_{1}\cap I_{2}=\emptyset
, then
{\mathcal{A}}(I_{1})
and
{\mathcal{A}}(I_{2})
commute.

3.

(Möbius covariance)

U
is a strongly continuous unitary representation of the Möbius group

{\rm M\ddot{o}b}=\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
on
{\mathcal{H}}
and for any
g\in{\rm M\ddot{o}b}
and any interval
I\in{\mathcal{I}}
we have

{\hbox{\rm Ad\,}}U(g)({\mathcal{A}}(I))={\mathcal{A}}(gI).

4.

(Positivity of energy)
The generator
L_{0}
of the rotation one-parameter subgroup is positive
(
U(R_{t})=e^{itL_{0}}
with
R_{t}
the rotation by
t
).

5.

(Vacuum vector)

\Omega
is a unit vector of
{\mathcal{H}}
, which is the unique (up to a scalar)

U
-invariant vector;
\Omega
cyclic for
\bigcup_{I\in{\mathcal{I}}}{\mathcal{A}}(I)
.

From these assumptions, the following automatically follow, see
[
FJ96
, Section 3]

6.

(Additivity)
If
I\subset\bigcup_{\kappa}I_{\kappa}
, then
{\mathcal{A}}(I)\subset\bigvee_{\kappa}{\mathcal{A}}(I_{\kappa})
,
where
\bigvee_{\kappa}{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa}
denotes the von Neumann algebra generated by
\{{\mathcal{M}}_{\kappa}\}
.

7.

(Reeh-Schlieder property)

\Omega
is cyclic for each local algebra
{\mathcal{A}}(I)
.

A
representation
of a Möbius covariant net
{\mathcal{A}}
is a family

\rho=\{\rho_{I}\}_{I\in{\mathcal{I}}}
, where
\rho_{I}
is a unital
*
-representation of
{\mathcal{A}}(I)
,
on a common Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rho}

such that
\rho_{I_{2}}|_{{\mathcal{A}}(I_{1})}=\rho_{I_{1}}
for
I_{1}\subset I_{2}
. We say that
\rho
is
locally normal
if
each
\rho_{I}
is normal.
We say
\rho
is
factorial
if
\bigvee_{I\in{\mathcal{I}}}\rho_{I}(I)
is a factor.

A Möbius covariant net
({\mathcal{A}},U,\Omega)
is called a
conformal net

if the representation
U
of the Möbius group extends to a strongly continuous projective representation
of the group
{\rm Diff}(S^{1})
of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms of
S^{1}
,
that is covariant, namely
{\hbox{\rm Ad\,}}U(g)({\mathcal{A}}(I))={\mathcal{A}}(gI)
, and
{\hbox{\rm Ad\,}}U(g)

acts trivially on
{\mathcal{A}}(I)
if
g
is acts identically on
I
.

We say that the net
{\mathcal{A}}
has the
split property
if for each
pair
I_{1},I_{2}
of intervals such that
\overline{I_{1}}\subset I_{2}
,
there is a type I factor
{\mathcal{N}}(I_{1},I_{2})
such that
{\mathcal{A}}(I_{1})\subset{\mathcal{N}}(I_{1},I_{2})\subset{\mathcal{A}}(I_{2})
.
The split property follows from the conformal covariance
[
MTW16
]
.

2.2
The universal
C^{*}
-algebra

For a given Möbius covariant net
{\mathcal{A}}
, Fredenhagen
[
Fre90
]
proposed to consider a
C^{*}
-algebra which is universal
in the sense that any representation of the net
{\mathcal{A}}
can be regarded as a representation of this algebra.
This notion has been used widely in the study of superselection sectors in conformal field theories,
and we will take it as the algebra of our physical system.

Yet, there seems to be a confusion in the literature about the construction.
The first paper which introduced the universal
C^{*}
-algebra was
[
Fre90
, Section 2]
.
We take a slight variation of it: one considers the free
*
-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
generated by
\{{\mathcal{A}}(I)\}
, modulo the relations due to the inclusions
{\mathcal{A}}(I_{1})\subset{\mathcal{A}}(I_{2})
, for
I_{1},I_{2}\in{\mathcal{I}}
,
I_{1}\subset I_{2}
.
Clearly a representation
\rho
of
{\mathcal{A}}
defines a representation of
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
, still denoted by
\rho
.
For a given
x\in{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
, one defines the seminorm by

\sup_{\rho\in\Gamma}\|\rho(x)\|,

where
\Gamma
is the class of all representations.
In the Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory with the axiom of Choice (ZFC),
\Gamma
is not a set
(an intuitive explanation would be the following:
on each set with cardinality larger or equal to the cardinality of the continuum,
one can define a structure as a Hilbert space. Hence the class of all Hilbert spaces
is ‘‘as large as’’ the class of all sets (with cardinality larger or equal to the cardinality of the continuum),
and would cause Russell’s paradox.
A precise reason is that the sets in ZFC are only those which are constructed by axiom schemas).
However, the above supremum can be justified in ZFC as follows
1
1

1

We owe this observation to Sebastiano Carpi.
:
For a given
x\in{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
, we consider the following:

\left\{s\in{\mathbb{R}}:\text{there is a representation }\rho\text{ of }{\mathcal{A}}_{0}\text{ such that }s=\|\rho(x)\|\right\},

which is a subset of
{\mathbb{R}}
in ZFC by the axiom schema of separation

2
2

2

The axiom schema of separation reads, for a given predicate
F(x)
with a variable
x
as follows:

(\exists B)(\forall x)(x\in B\leftrightarrow x\in A\;\&\;F(x))
. In words, it states that
for a set
A
there is a subset
B
which consists of all elements of
A
which satisfy
F
.

(see standard textbooks on axiomatic set theory, e.g.​
[
Sup60
,
Jec78
]
).
Hence one can take the supremum and the rest follows.

Another commonly cited paper
[
GL92
, Section 8]
has a problem, because one has to take
the direct sum parametrized by “all the representations”, which is definitely not a set.

Let us also provide a construction of the universal algebra which is closer to that of
[
GL92
]
.
We consider as before the free
*
-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
generated by
\{{\mathcal{A}}(I)\}
modulo the inclusion relations as above.
We denote by
\iota_{I}
the embedding of
{\mathcal{A}}(I)
into
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
.
Let
{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
be the set of states (positive, unital linear functionals in the sense
\varphi(x^{*}x)\geq 0
,
x\in{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
)
\varphi
on
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
.
By definition of
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
, the GNS representation
\rho_{\varphi}
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
with respect to
\varphi
satisfies for
I\subset J

\rho_{\varphi}\circ\iota_{J}|_{{\mathcal{A}}(I)}=\rho_{\varphi}\circ\iota_{I}.

Note that, for any
\varphi
, an element
x\in{\mathcal{A}}(I)
is represented by a bounded operator

\rho_{\varphi}(x)
. Indeed,
x^{*}x\leq\|x\|^{2}{\mathbbm{1}}
in
{\mathcal{A}}(I)
, hence
\rho_{\varphi}(x^{*}x)\leq\|x\|^{2}{\mathbbm{1}}
because
\rho_{\varphi}|_{{\mathcal{A}}(I)}
is a representation of a von Neumann algebra and a representation of a
C^{*}
-algebra is order preserving.

Now let
x
be an element of
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
; then
x
is a finite sum
\sum_{k}\prod_{l}x_{k,l}
of finite products of elements of
{\mathcal{A}}(I_{k,l})
,
I_{k,l}\in{\mathcal{I}}
.

Let
\rho
be a representation of the net
{\mathcal{A}}
. Then
\rho
gives rise to a representation of
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
.
With
x=\sum_{k}\prod_{l}x_{k,l}
as above, we have

\displaystyle\|\rho(x)\|=\left\|\rho\left(\sum_{k}\prod_{l}x_{k,l}\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k}\prod_{l}\rho(x_{k,l})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}\left\|\prod_{l}\rho(x_{k,l})\right\|\leq\sum_{k}\prod_{l}\|\rho(x_{k,l})\|\leq\sum_{k}\prod_{l}\|x_{k,l}\|\ ,

where
\|\rho(x_{k,l})\|
is the norm of
r(x_{k,l})
in
{\mathcal{A}}(I_{k,l})
.
Thus
\|\rho(x)\|\leq C_{x}<\infty
, where the constant
C_{x}
does not depend on
\rho
.

We define a seminorm on
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
by

\|x\|=\sup_{\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{0}}\|\rho_{\varphi}(x)\|\ ,

which is finite since
\|x\|\leq C_{x}
,
and we take the
C^{*}
-completion (modulo null elements), that we denote by
C^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
.

Let us remark that this construction avoids the set-theoretical problem:
while “the class of all representations” is too large to be a set,
one can consider the set of all states, because it is a subset of all maps from
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
into
{\mathbb{C}}

with linearity, positivity and unitarity, which can be formulated again by
the axiom schema of separation.

Now, as
C^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
is not defined through the supremum over all representations, we have to check the universal property.

Proposition 2.1
.

For each representation
\{\rho_{I}\}
of the net
{\mathcal{A}}
, there is a representation
\rho
of the algebra
C^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
constructed
above such that
\rho_{I}=\rho\circ\iota_{I}
.

Proof.

\{\rho_{I}\}
gives rise to a representation
\rho
of
{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
. In order to prove that
\rho
extends to
C^{*}({\mathcal{A}})

we have to show that
\rho
is continuous w.r.t.​ the norm of
C^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
, namely
\|\rho(x)\|\leq\|x\|
,
x\in{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
.
This follows because every representation of a
C^{*}
-algebra is direct sum of cyclic representations,
thus
\|\rho(x)\|
is the supremum of
\rho_{\varphi}(x)
with
\varphi
running in a family of states.
∎

Now we may properly call
C^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
the universal
C^{*}
-algebra of the net
{\mathcal{A}}
.
By the very universal property, it is unique up to an isomorphism.

Actually, we are mostly interested in locally normal representations, hence it is natural to
take account of locally normal representations only. This has been done by

]
:
we take our

and consider the locally normal universal representation

,
which is the direct sum of all GNS representations over all states
\varphi
on
C^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
such that

\rho_{\varphi}
is locally normal. The universal property can be again proven by decomposing
an arbitrary representation into cyclic representations. We take the quotient

C^{*}_{\mathrm{ln}}({\mathcal{A}}):=C^{*}({\mathcal{A}})/\ker\rho_{\mathrm{ln}}
and call it the locally normal universal
C^{*}
-algebra
of the net
{\mathcal{A}}
. The properties of

claimed in
[
CCHW13
]
can be restored
without any modification, since the actual construction is not needed in the proofs but only
the universality is used.

If the net
{\mathcal{A}}
is conformal, any locally normal representation
\rho
is covariant with respect
to the universal cover
\widetilde{{\rm M\ddot{o}b}}
of the Möbius group
and one can take the unique implementing operators from
\rho(C^{*}_{\mathrm{ln}}({\mathcal{A}}))
, and indeed
they are finite products of local elements
[
DFK04
, Theorem 6]
.
From this it follows that the action of

on

is inner.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a Möbius covariant net with the split property and

\rho
be a locally normal representation of

with a cyclic vector

.
Then the representation space

Proof.

As in
[
KLM01
, Appendix C]
, we consider the set
{\mathcal{I}}_{\mathbb{Q}}
of intervals with rational end points,
an intermediate type I factor

between

(we just choose one

for each pair

, not necessarily the canonical choice of
[

]

let
{\mathcal{K}}(I_{1},I_{2})
be the algebra of compact operators in

(under the identification

and denote by

-algebra generated by
\{{\mathcal{K}}(I_{1},I_{2})\}

-algebra.

As

is locally normal, for each

.

2.3
KMS states with respect to rotations, Gibbs states

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra and
\alpha
a one-parameter automorphism group of
\mathfrak{A}

(not necessarily pointwise norm-continuous).

A
KMS state
of
\mathfrak{A}
w.r.t.
\alpha
at inverse temperature
\beta\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}

is a state
\varphi
on
\mathfrak{A}
such that for any pair of elements
x,y\in\mathfrak{A}
there is a bounded analytic
function
F_{xy}
on
{\mathbb{R}}+i(0,\beta)
, which is continuous on
{\mathbb{R}}+i[0,\beta]
, such that

F_{xy}(t)=\varphi(\alpha_{t}(x)y),\quad F_{xy}(t+i\beta)=\varphi(y\alpha_{t}(x)).

Given a conformal net
{\mathcal{A}}
, we are interested in states on the universal
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}({\mathcal{A}})

w.r.t. the rotation one-parameter automorphism group.
Any state
\psi
on
C^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
gives rise to a GNS representation
\rho_{\psi}
of
C^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
,
whose restriction to
\{{\mathcal{A}}(I)\}
(i.e.
\{\rho_{\psi}\iota_{I}\}_{I\in{\mathcal{I}}}
) is a representation of the net.
We say that
\psi
is locally normal if its restriction to each local algebra
{\mathcal{A}}(I)

is normal. We do not know whether this implies in general that the GNS representation
\rho_{\psi}

is locally normal. Yet, for KMS states, we have the following Lemmas.
The proof of the first one is essentially the same as that of
[
TW73
, Theorem 1]
,
one should only note that the funnel structure is not necessary.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra
which contains a
\sigma
-finite properly infinite von Neumann algebra
{\mathcal{M}}
,
and
\varphi
a state on
\mathfrak{A}
such that the GNS vector
\Phi
(for the
GNS representation
\rho_{\varphi}
with respect to
\varphi
) is separating
for
\rho_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
. Then
\varphi|_{\mathcal{M}}
is normal and
\rho_{\varphi}|_{\mathcal{M}}
is normal.

Proof.

As
\Phi
is separating for
\rho_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
,
\rho_{\varphi}(\mathfrak{A})^{\prime\prime}
is
\sigma
-finite,
hence
\rho_{\varphi}({\mathcal{M}})^{\prime\prime}
is
\sigma
-finite as well.
Then the restriction
\rho_{\varphi}
to a properly infinite algebra
{\mathcal{M}}
is normal

[
Tak02
, Theorem V.5.1]
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\varphi
be a KMS state on
C^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
with respect to the rotation flow
\alpha
. Then
\varphi

is locally normal and its GNS representation
\rho_{\varphi}
is locally normal.

Proof.

The local algebras in the vacuum representation have a separating vector

Remark 2.6

In the real line case, the GNS representation of every locally normal state

2.4
Energy expectation value

The stress energy density in a Gibbs state can be computed through the character formula.
For a test function
f
with support in an interval
I\in{\mathcal{I}}
, the stress energy tensor
T
in the vacuum representation,
smoothed with
f
, is an unbounded operator
T(f)
affiliated to
{\mathcal{A}}(I)
. If
\rho
is an irreducible representation of
{\mathcal{A}}
,
we may define
T_{\rho}(f)=\rho(T(f))
, making use that bounded functions, e.g.​ the resolvent, of
T(f)
belong to
{\mathcal{A}}(I)
.
The expectation value of the stress-energy tensor in the Gibbs state is then

\varphi_{\rho,\beta}(T(f))=\frac{\Tr(e^{-\beta L_{0}^{\rho}}T_{\rho}(f))}{\Tr(e^{-\beta L_{0}^{\rho}})}\ .

This is indeed finite if, for example, there is
\epsilon>0
such that
\Tr(e^{-(\beta-\epsilon)L_{0}^{\rho}})

is finite because the polynomial energy bound holds for the Virasoro algebra
[
CW05
, Lemma 4.1]

(which implies that
e^{-\epsilon L_{0}^{\rho}}T(f)
is bounded). This condition is quite generic.

Furthermore, in such a case,
one can compute this value by expanding
T_{\rho}(f)=\sum f_{n}L^{\rho}_{n}
,
where the
f_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}f(e^{it})e^{-int}dt
are the Fourier modes of
f
.
As
L^{\rho}_{n}
,
n\neq 0
changes the energy eigenvalues while
\Tr

can be computed by expanding along a basis of
L^{\rho}_{0}
eigenvectors,
all the contributions from
L^{\rho}_{n},n\neq 0
drop out and we have

\varphi_{\rho,\beta}(T(f))=f_{0}\frac{\Tr(e^{-\beta L_{0}^{\rho}}L^{\rho}_{0})}{\Tr(e^{-\beta L_{0}^{\rho}})}=f_{0}\frac{-d\chi_{\rho}(e^{-s})/ds|_{s=\beta}}{\chi_{\rho}(e^{-\beta})}=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}f(e^{it})dt\cdot\frac{d\chi_{\rho}(e^{-s})/ds}{\chi_{\rho}(e^{-s})}\Big|_{s=\beta}\ ,

where
\chi_{\rho}(q)=\Tr q^{L_{0}^{\rho}}
is known as the character of the representation
\rho
. Thus

\varphi_{\rho,\beta}(T(1))=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{d\chi_{\rho}(e^{-s})/ds}{\chi_{\rho}(e^{-s})}\Big|_{s=\beta}=\frac{q}{2\pi}\frac{d\chi_{\rho}(q)/dq}{\chi_{\rho}(q)}\Big|_{q=e^{-\beta}}=\frac{q}{2\pi}\frac{d\log(\chi_{\rho}(q))}{dq}\Big|_{q=e^{-\beta}}.

The characters for some specific examples can be found in the literature,
e.g.​
[
KR87
]
.

Let

3.2.1

Proposition 3.4

Let
{\mathcal{A}}

\beta

is the conformal Hamiltonian in the representation

By Lemma
2.5
, the GNS representation

by Proposition
2.2

By considering the central disintegration of

\mathfrak{A}
any separable,

According to this disintegration, the GNS vector

3.2.2
General remarks

If we assume conformal covariance, type III representations do not occur
since the rotations are inner.
Furthermore, for type II states on a conformal net, a Gibbs-like formula is valid by replacing
\Tr

by the unique tracial weight
\tau
, c.f.​ Lemma

.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
{\mathcal{A}}
is conformal, then for any KMS states
\varphi

\rho_{\varphi}(C^{*}({\mathcal{A}}))^{\prime\prime}
contains no type III component.

Proof.

As we saw in Section
2.2

\widetilde{{\rm M\ddot{o}b}}

Thus, the modular automorphisms of
\rho_{\varphi}(C^{*}({\mathcal{A}}))^{\prime\prime}
with respect to

\rho_{\varphi}(C^{*}({\mathcal{A}}))^{\prime\prime}
cannot have a type III component (see
[
Tak02
, Theorem IV.8.21, Section V.1]
,

[
Tak03a
, Theorem VIII.3.14]

∎

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a conformal net and
\rho

{\mathcal{A}}
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rho}

As we recalled in Section

, by conformal covariance,
there is a canonical inner implementation

Of course, in case

3.2.3
Nets of type I

We say that a Möbius covariant net is of
type I
if it admits only
locally normal representations
\rho
such that
\rho(C^{*}({\mathcal{A}}))^{\prime\prime}
is of type I.

Some important conformal nets turn out to be type I,
therefore, any extremal KMS state is the Gibbs state in one of the irreducible representations.

Theorem 3.7
.

If a conformal net
{\mathcal{A}}
is of type I, then any rotational
\beta
-KMS state
\varphi
is a convex combination (integration)
of the Gibbs states in irreducible representations.

Proof.

Immediate from Lemma
3.3
and Proposition
3.4

(note that the split property follows from conformal covariance
[
MTW16
]
).
∎

As we recalled in Section
2.2
, for a conformal net the representatives of
\widetilde{{\rm M\ddot{o}b}}

are inner and unique, hence any locally normal representation
\rho
of the net (or the universal algebra
C^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
)
is
\widetilde{{\rm M\ddot{o}b}}
-covariant. The implementation is unique if we assume that the representatives belong to

\rho(C^{*}({\mathcal{A}}))^{\prime\prime}
. With this unique inner implementation, the lowest eigenvalue
l_{0}
of the generator
L_{0}^{\rho}

of rotations is non-negative
[
Wei06
, Theorem 3.8]
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a conformal net and assume that there are only countably many equivalence classes of
locally normal irreducible representations with a specified
lowest eigenvalue of the generator of rotations. Then
{\mathcal{A}}
is of type I.

Proof.

By local normality and its disintegration restricted to
\mathfrak{A}
as in Proposition
3.4
,
it is enough to treat factorial representations.
Let us consider a locally normal factorial representation
\rho
of
{\mathcal{A}}
.
The implementation of the
2\pi
-rotation commutes with any local element, hence with

\rho(C^{*}({\mathcal{A}}))^{\prime\prime}
, on the other hand, it belongs to
\rho(C^{*}({\mathcal{A}}))^{\prime\prime}
by our choice that it is inner.
When
\rho(C^{*}({\mathcal{A}}))^{\prime\prime}
is a factor, the implementation is then a scalar. This applies to any
integer-multiple of
2\pi
, hence the spectrum of
L_{0}^{\rho}
must be included in
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}+l_{0}
,
where
l_{0}\geq 0
, and
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
is the set of non-negative integers.

Now, we consider any disintegration of
\rho|_{\mathfrak{A}}
into irreducible representations
where
\mathfrak{A}
is the separable
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
C^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
as in Proposition
3.4

(this is possible, by choosing a maximally abelian algebra in
\rho(C^{*}({\mathcal{A}}))^{\prime}

because we have the split property: see
[
KLM01
, Proposition 56]
for disintegration and

[
MTW16
]
for the implication of the split property from conformal covariance):

\rho_{\mathfrak{A}}=\int_{X}^{\oplus}\rho_{\lambda}d\mu(\lambda),

where
X
is a certain index set.
Let us assume, by contradiction, that
\rho(C^{*}({\mathcal{A}}))^{\prime\prime}
is a factor of not type I.
Then by
[
KLM01
, Proposition 57, Corollary 58]
, for a fixed
\lambda
,
\rho_{\lambda}
is locally normal,
hence extends to
C^{*}({\mathcal{A}})
and must be inequivalent to
\rho_{\lambda^{\prime}}
for almost all
\lambda^{\prime}
,
and there are uncountably many such
\lambda^{\prime}
’s. But on the other hand,
the inner implementation of
\widetilde{{\rm M\ddot{o}b}}
also disintegrates and the lowest
eigenvalue of
L_{0}
remains in
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}+l_{0}
for each
\lambda
. By assumption, there are only countably many such inequivalent
representations, which contradicts the above uncountable family of representations.
This concludes the proof that
\rho
is type I.
∎

We have two basic examples with this property.

Example 3.9
.

The
\mathrm{U}(1)
-current net
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{U}(1)}
:
In two-dimensional spacetime, the naively defined massless free field is plagued by the infrared problem.
Yet it is possible to consider its derivative. Its chiral components are called the
\mathrm{U}(1)
-current.
See
[
BMT88
,
Lon08
]
for its operator-algebraic formulation.

The algebra is generated by the Fourier modes
\{J_{n}\}
of the current which satisfy the following relations

[J_{m},J_{n}]=m\delta_{m+n,0}
.
This algebra has a distinguished representation with the vacuum vector
\Omega
such that

J_{m}\Omega=0\text{ for }m\geq 0
,
J_{m}^{*}=J_{-m}
.
For a smooth function
f
on
S^{1}
, one defines the Weyl operator
W(f)
by

W(f)=\exp\left(i\sum_{m}\hat{f}_{m}J_{m}\right)
,
where
\hat{f}_{m}
are the Fourier components of

.

One defines the net by
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{U}(1)}(I)=\{W(f):{\rm supp\,}f\subset I\}^{\prime\prime}
. It turns out that this net is
conformally covariant. The generator of rotations is given by the Sugawara formula

L_{0}=\frac{1}{2}J_{0}^{2}+\sum_{m>0}J_{-m}J_{m}.

For each
q\geq 0
, there are irreducible representations of the net

3.3
Remarks on non-type I representations: open problems

As we saw, important classes of conformal nets are of type I.
It is an open problem whether there exists a Möbius covariant net not of type I.
The situation is quite different from the case of nets on the real line,
where any translation KMS state on the quasilocal algebra is of type III
1

[
CLTW12a
]
or of nets on the Minkowski space
where one can have any type of representation
[
DS82
,
DS83
,
BD84
,
DFG84
]
.

One concrete open case is the cyclic orbifold
[
LX04
]
. Take a conformal net
{\mathcal{A}}
, make the tensor product
{\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}}

and consider the fixed point net
({\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}})^{\mathrm{flip}}
with respect to the flip between two components.
If
{\mathcal{A}}
is completely rational, then
({\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}})^{\mathrm{flip}}
is again completely rational and
all the sectors can be explicitly written in terms of sectors of
{\mathcal{A}}
and twisted sectors.
On the other hand, if
{\mathcal{A}}
is not completely rational, then we do not have a complete classification
of sectors of
({\mathcal{A}}\otimes{\mathcal{A}})^{\mathrm{flip}}
. In particular, we are not able to exclude the possibility of
non-type I representations, although all known sectors are of type I.

Another candidate for a net with non-type I representations would be an infinite tensor product.
Recall that (see e.g.​
[
CW05
, Section 6]
) for a given countable family of Möbius covariant nets
\{({\mathcal{A}}_{k},U_{k},\Omega_{k})\}
,
one can define a Möbius covariant net by

{\mathcal{A}}(I):=\bigotimes{\mathcal{A}}_{k}(I),\;U(g):=\bigotimes U_{k}(g),

with respect to the reference vector
\Omega=\bigotimes\Omega_{k}
(see e.g.​
[
Tak03b
, Section XIV.1]
).
Let us assume that each
{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
admits a representation
\rho_{k}
which is converging to the vacuum representation
in some sense. Then one may hope that the infinite tensor product of representations

\bigotimes\rho_{k}
could make sense. Even if each
\rho_{k}
is of type I,
the resulting product could be of non type I. However, this discussion depends on the nature of the sequence
\rho_{k}

and a detailed analysis is needed.

We note that the type I property for rotational
\beta

4
Outlook

Although the conformal Hamiltonian is not the physical Hamiltonian, namely it
does not implement the time translation QFT flow,
there is some physical interest in considering rotational KMS states in CFT.

One example comes from the three-dimensional quantum gravity.
If the cosmological constant is assumed to be negative, one should then look at the solutions
of the Einstein equation which are asymptotically close to the
\mathrm{AdS_{3}}
spacetime. Different solutions
have different boundary data and such solutions (with certain fall-off conditions)
have been classified in
[
GL14
]
. Two copies of the Virasoro group make the transformations
between these solutions. Such an action of the Virasoro group
is called a coadjoint action
[
Wit88
]
. Maloney and Witten
[
MW10
]
tried to compute the partition
function of the
\mathrm{AdS_{3}}
gravity, but they arrived at an expression which cannot be interpreted
as a trace over a Hilbert space of the exponential of a self-adjoint operator. It has been proposed
to study each orbit of the Virasoro group first, e.g.​
[
GL14
]
. In particular, one can consider the so-called
BTZ black hole solutions
[
BnTZ92
]
. In a hypothetical quantum theory, the Virasoro group should appear
as a symmetry of the theory, while the black hole should be in a thermal state.
Furthermore, the energy, hence the mass, of the black hole corresponds to the conformal Hamiltonian
(see
[
GL14
, eq.​ (50)]
).
In this way, KMS states on the Virasoro nets with respect to rotations
should appear naturally. From our results, one can conclude that
all such KMS states can be represented on the direct sum or integral of the Verma module.

Besides, it is an interesting question to make sense of quantum entropy of such black hole states
from the operator-algebraic point of view.

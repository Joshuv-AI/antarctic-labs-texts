On the power of standard information for tractability for L 2 L_{2} -approximation in the average case setting
Wanting Lu, Heping Wang

Abstract
. We study multivariate approximation in the average case setting with the error measured in the weighted L 2 L_{2} norm. We consider algorithms that use standard information Λ std \Lambda^{\rm std} consisting of function values or general linear information Λ all \Lambda^{\rm all} consisting of arbitrary continuous linear functionals. We investigate the equivalences of various notions of algebraic and exponential tractability for Λ std \Lambda^{\rm std} and Λ all \Lambda^{\rm all} for the absolute error criterion, and show that the power of Λ std \Lambda^{\rm std} is the same as that of Λ all \Lambda^{\rm all} for all notions of algebraic and exponential tractability without any condition. Specifically, we solve Open Problems 116-118 and almost solve Open Problem 115 as posed by E.Novak and H.Woźniakowski in the book: Tractability of Multivariate Problems, Volume III: Standard Information for Operators, EMS Tracts in Mathematics, Zürich, 2012.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study
multivariate approximation
{\rm APP}=\{{\rm APP}_{d}\}_{d\in{N}}
in the average case setting, where

{{\rm APP}}_{d}:F_{d}\to G_{d}\ \ {\rm with}\ \ {\rm APP}_{d}\,f=f

is the continuous embedding operator,
F_{d}
is a
separable Banach function space on
D_{d}
equipped with a
zero-mean Gaussian measure
\mu_{d}
,
G_{d}
is a weighted
L_{2}

space on
D_{d}
,
D_{d}
is a Lebesgue measurable subset of
{R}^{d}
, and the dimension
d
is large or even huge. We consider
algorithms that use finitely many information evaluations. Here
information evaluation means continuous linear functional on
F_{d}

(general linear information) or function value at some point
(standard information). We use
\Lambda^{\rm all}
and
\Lambda^{\rm std}

to denote the class of all continuous linear functionals and the
class of all function values, respectively.

For a given error threshold

\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, the information complexity
n(\varepsilon,d)
is defined
to be the minimal number of information evaluations for which the
average case error of some algorithm is at most
\varepsilon
.
Tractability is aimed at studying how the information complexity

n(\varepsilon,d)
depends on
\varepsilon
and
d
. There are two kinds of
tractability based on polynomial convergence and exponential
convergence. The algebraic tractability (ALG-tractability)
describes how the information complexity
n(\varepsilon,d)
behaves as a
function of
d
and
\varepsilon^{-1}
, while the exponential
tractability (EXP-tractability)
does as one of
d
and
(1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1})
. The existing notions of
tractability mainly include strong polynomial tractability (SPT),
polynomial tractability (PT), quasi-polynomial tractability (QPT),
weak tractability (WT),
(s,t)
-weak tractability (
(s,t)
-WT),
and uniform weak tractability (UWT).
In
recent years the study of algebraic and
exponential tractability has attracted much interest, and a great number of
interesting results
have been obtained
(see

\Lambda^{\rm std}
and
\Lambda^{\rm all}

in the average case setting (see

). The class
\Lambda^{\rm std}
is much smaller and much more

2.
Preliminaries and Main Results

2.1.
Average case setting

For
d\in{N}
, let
F_{d}
be a separable Banach space
of
d
-variate real-valued functions on
D_{d}
equipped with a
zero-mean Gaussian measure
\mu_{d}
,
G_{d}=L_{2}(D_{d},\rho_{d}(x)dx)

be a weighted
L_{2}
space, where
D_{d}
is a Borel measurable
subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with positive Lebesgue measure,

\rho_{d}
is a probability density function on
D_{d}
. We consider
the multivariate approximation
problem
{\rm APP}=\{{\rm APP}_{d}\}_{d\in{N}}
in the
average case setting which is defined via the continuous linear operator

(2.1)

{{\rm APP}}_{d}:F_{d}\to G_{d}\ \ {\rm with}\ \ {\rm APP}_{d}\,f=f.

We suppose that function value at some point

x\in D_{d}
is well defined continuous linear functional on
F_{d}
.
That is, we suppose that
\Lambda^{\rm std}\subset\Lambda^{\rm all}=(F_{d})^{*}
, where
(F_{d})^{*}
is the dual space of
F_{d}
. It is
well known that, in the average case setting with the average
being with respect to a zero-mean Gaussian measure, adaptive
choice of the above information evaluations do not essentially
help, see
[
31
]
. Hence, we can restrict our attention to
nonadaptive algorithms, i.e., algorithms
A_{n,d}f
of the form

(2.2)

A_{n,d}f=\phi_{n,d}(L_{1}(f),L_{2}(f),\dots,L_{n}(f)),

where
L_{i}\in\Lambda,\ i=1,\dots,n
,
\Lambda\in\{\Lambda^{\rm all},\,\Lambda^{\rm std}\}
,
and
\phi_{n,d}:\;{R}^{n}\to G_{d}
is an arbitrary measurable mapping from
{R}^{n}
to
G_{d}
.
The average case error for the algorithm
A_{n,d}
of the form
(
2.2
) is defined as

e^{\rm avg}(A_{n,d}):=\Big(\int_{F_{d}}\|{\rm APP}_{d}f-A_{n,d}f\|^{2}_{G_{d}}\ \mu_{d}(df)\Big)^{1/2}.

The
n
th minimal average case error for
\Lambda\in\{\Lambda^{\rm all},\,\Lambda^{\rm std}\}
is defined by

e^{\rm avg}(n,d;\Lambda):=\inf_{A_{n,d}\ {\rm with}\ L_{i}\in\Lambda}e^{\rm avg}(A_{n,d}),

where the infimum is taken over all algorithms of the form
(
2.2
).

For
n=0
, we use
A_{0,d}=0
. We obtain the so-called initial
error
e^{\rm avg}(0,d)
defined by

e^{\rm avg}(0,d):=e^{\rm avg}(0,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})=e^{\rm avg}(0,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})=\Big(\int_{F_{d}}\|{\rm APP}_{d}f\|^{2}_{G_{d}}\ \mu_{d}(df)\Big)^{1/2}.

We set

\Gamma_{d}:=(e^{\rm avg}(0,d;\Lambda^{\rm all}))^{2}=(e^{\rm avg}(0,d;\Lambda^{\rm std}))^{2}.

It follows from
[
31
, Chapter 6]
and
[
24
]
that
e^{\rm avg}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})
are described through the eigenvalues
and the eigenvectors of the covariance
operator
C_{\nu_{d}}:\,G_{d}\to G_{d}
of the induced measure
\nu_{d}=\mu_{d}S_{d}^{-1}
of
\mu_{d}
. Here,
\mu_{d}
is a zero-mean Gaussian measure of
F_{d}
, so that
\nu_{d}
is a zero-mean Gaussian measure on the Borel sets of
G_{d}
. The operator
C_{\nu_{d}}
is self-adjoint, non-negative definite, and the trace of
C_{\nu_{d}}
is finite.

Let
\big\{(\lambda_{k,d},\eta_{k,d})\big\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
denote the
eigenpairs of
C_{\nu_{d}}
satisfying

\lambda_{1,d}\geq\lambda_{2,d}\geq\dots\lambda_{n,d}\dots\geq 0.

That is,

\{\eta_{k,d}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is an orthonormal basis in
G_{d}
,
and

C_{\nu_{d}}\,\eta_{k,d}=\lambda_{k,d}\,\eta_{k,d},\ k\in\mathbb{N}.

From
[
31
,
24
]

we get that the
n
th minimal average case
error is

e^{\rm avg}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})=\big(\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k,d}\big)^{1/2},

and it is achieved by the optimal algorithm

A_{n,d}^{*}f=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle f,\eta_{k,d}\rangle_{G_{d}}\,\eta_{k,d}.

That is,

(2.3)

e^{\rm avg}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})=\big(\int_{F_{d}}\|f-A_{n,d}^{*}f\|^{2}_{G_{d}}\mu_{d}(df)\big)^{1/2}=\big(\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k,d}\big)^{1/2}.

The trace of
C_{\nu_{d}}
is just the square of the initial error

e^{\rm avg}(0,d)
given by

{\rm trace}(C_{\nu_{d}})=\Gamma_{d}=(e^{\rm avg}(0,d))^{2}=\int_{G_{d}}\|g\|^{2}_{G_{d}}\nu_{d}(dg)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k,d}<\infty.

The information complexity can be studied using either the
absolute error criterion (ABS) or the normalized error criterion
(NOR). In the average case setting for
\star\in\{{\rm ABS,\,NOR}\}
and
\Lambda\in\{\Lambda^{\rm all},\Lambda^{\rm std}\}
, we
define the information complexity
n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)
as

(2.4)

n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda):=n^{\rm avg,\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda):=\inf\{n\ \big|\ e^{\rm avg}(n,d;\Lambda)\leq\varepsilon\,{\rm CRI}_{d}\},

where

{\rm CRI}_{d}:=\left\{\begin{split}&\ \ 1,\qquad\,\quad\quad\text{for $\star$=ABS,}\\
&e^{\rm avg}(0,d),\quad\text{ for $\star$=NOR}\end{split}\right.\ \ =\ \ \left\{\begin{split}&\ 1,\qquad\ \ \ \text{ for $\star$=ABS,}\\
&(\Gamma_{d})^{1/2},\ \ \text{ for $\star$=NOR.}\end{split}\right.

Since
\Lambda^{\rm std}\subset\Lambda^{\rm all},
we get

e^{\rm avg}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})\leq e^{\rm avg}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm std}).

It follows that for
\star\in\{{\rm ABS,\,NOR}\}
,

(2.5)

n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})\leq n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std}).

2.2.
Notions of tractability

In this subsection we briefly recall the various tractability
notions in the average case setting. First we introduce all
notions of algebraic tractability. Let
{\rm APP}=\{{\rm APP}_{d}\}_{d\in{N}}
,

\star\in\{{\rm ABS,\,NOR}\}
, and

\Lambda\in\{\Lambda^{\rm all},\Lambda^{\rm std}\}
. In the average case
setting for the class
\Lambda
, and for error criterion
\star
,
we say that
{\rm APP}
is

\bullet
Algebraic strongly polynomially tractable (ALG-SPT) if
there exist
C>0
and non-negative number
p
such that

(2.6)

n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)\leq C\varepsilon^{-p},\ \text{for all}\ \varepsilon\in(0,1).

The exponent
ALG-
p^{\star}(\Lambda)
of ALG-SPT is defined as the infimum of
p

for which (
2.6
) holds;

\bullet
Algebraic polynomially tractable (ALG-PT) if there
exist
C>0
and non-negative numbers
p,q
such that

n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)\leq Cd^{q}\varepsilon^{-p},\ \text{for all}\ d\in\mathbb{N},\ \varepsilon\in(0,1);

\bullet
Algebraic quasi-polynomially tractable (ALG-QPT) if
there exist
C>0
and non-negative number
t
such that

(2.7)

n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)\leq C\exp(t(1+\ln{d})(1+\ln{\varepsilon^{-1}})),\ \text{for all}\ d\in\mathbb{N},\ \varepsilon\in(0,1).

The exponent ALG-
t^{\star}(\Lambda)
of ALG-QPT is defined as the infimum of
t
for
which (
2.7
) holds;

\bullet
Algebraic uniformly weakly tractable (ALG-UWT) if

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)}{\varepsilon^{-\alpha}+d^{\beta}}=0,\ \text{for all}\ \alpha,\beta>0;

\bullet
Algebraic weakly tractable (ALG-WT) if

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)}{\varepsilon^{-1}+d}=0;

\bullet
Algebraic
(s,t)
-weakly tractable (ALG-
(s,t)
-WT) for
fixed
s,t>0
if

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)}{\varepsilon^{-s}+d^{t}}=0.

Clearly, ALG-
(1,1)
-WT is the same as ALG-WT. If
{\rm APP}
is
not ALG-WT, then
{\rm APP}
is called intractable.

If
the
n
th minimal error decays faster
than any polynomial and is exponentially convergent, then we
should study tractability with
\varepsilon^{-1}
being replaced by

(1+\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon})
, which is called exponential tractability.
Recently, there have been many papers studying exponential
tractability (see
[
5
,
4
,
37
,
28
,
15
,
9
,
1
,
19
]
).

In the definitions of ALG-SPT, ALG-PT, ALG-QPT, ALG-UWT, ALG-WT,
and ALG-
(s,t)
-WT, if we replace
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
by
(1+\ln\frac{1}{\varepsilon})
, we get the definitions of
exponential strong
polynomial tractability
(EXP-SPT),
exponential polynomial
tractability
(EXP-PT),
exponential quasi-polynomial
tractability
(EXP-QPT),
exponential uniform weak
tractability

(EXP-UWT),
exponential weak tractability

(EXP-WT), and
exponential
(s,t)
-weak tractability

(EXP-
(s,t)
-WT), respectively. We now give the above notions of exponential tractability in
detail.

Let
{\rm APP}=\{{\rm APP}_{d}\}_{d\in{N}}
,
\star\in\{{\rm ABS,\,NOR}\}
, and
\Lambda\in\{\Lambda^{\rm all},\Lambda^{\rm std}\}
. In the average case setting for the class

\Lambda
, and for error criterion
\star
, we say that
{\rm APP}
is

\bullet
Exponential strongly polynomially tractable (EXP-SPT) if
there exist
C>0
and non-negative number
p
such that

(2.8)

n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)\leq C(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1)^{p},\ \text{for all}\ \varepsilon\in(0,1).

The exponent
EXP-
p^{\star}(\Lambda)
of EXP-SPT is defined as the infimum of
p

for which (
2.8
) holds;

\bullet
Exponential polynomially tractable (EXP-PT) if there
exist
C>0
and non-negative numbers
p,q
such that

n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)\leq Cd^{q}(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1)^{p},\ \text{for all}\ d\in\mathbb{N},\ \varepsilon\in(0,1);

\bullet
Exponential quasi-polynomially tractable (EXP-QPT) if
there exist
C>0
and non-negative number
t
such that

(2.9)

n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)\leq C\exp(t(1+\ln{d})(1+\ln(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1))),\ \text{for all}\ d\in\mathbb{N},\ \varepsilon\in(0,1).

The exponent EXP-
t^{\star}(\Lambda)
of EXP-QPT is defined as the infimum of
t
for
which (
2.9
) holds;

\bullet
Exponential uniformly weakly tractable (EXP-UWT) if

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)}{(1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{\alpha}+d^{\beta}}=0,\ \text{for
all}\ \alpha,\beta>0;

\bullet
Exponential weakly tractable (EXP-WT) if

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)}{1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+d}=0;

\bullet
Exponential
(s,t)
-weakly tractable (EXP-
(s,t)
-WT)
for fixed
s,t>0
if

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda)}{(1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{s}+d^{t}}=0.

2.3.
Main results

We shall give main results of this paper in this subsection. We
remark that for multivariate approximation problem results and
proofs in the average case setting are in full analogy with ones
in the randomized setting (see
[
20
]

the reader, we provide details of all proofs.

The authors in
[
7
,
24
,
38
]
used the mean value theorem and

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\delta\in(0,1)

m,n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that

m=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{48(\sqrt{2}\ln(2n)-\ln{\delta})}\right\rfloor.

Then we have

(2.10)

e^{\rm avg}(n,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq\Big(1+\frac{4m}{n}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta}}\,e^{\rm avg}(m,d;\Lambda^{\rm all}),

where
\lfloor x\rfloor
denotes the largest
integer not exceeding
x
.

Based on Theorem 2.1, we obtain two relations between the
information complexities
n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})
and
n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})
for

\star\in\{{\rm ABS,\,NOR}\}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
\star\in\{{\rm ABS,\,NOR}\}
and
\omega>0
, we
have

(2.11)

n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq C_{\omega}\Big(n^{\star}(\frac{\varepsilon}{4},d;\Lambda^{\rm all})+1\Big)^{1+\omega},

where
C_{\omega}
is a positive constant depending only on
\omega
.
Similarly, for sufficiently small
\omega,\delta>0
and
\star\in\{{\rm ABS,\,NOR}\}
, we have

(2.12)

n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq C_{\omega,\delta}\big(n^{\star}(\frac{\varepsilon}{A_{\delta}},d;\Lambda^{\rm all})+1\big)^{1+\omega},

where

A_{\delta}:=\Big(1+\frac{1}{12\ln{\frac{1}{\delta}}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta}}
,

C_{\omega,\delta}
is a positive constant depending only on
\omega
and

\delta
.

n\leq 48\big(\sqrt{2}\ln(2n)+\ln{\frac{1}{\delta}}\big)(m+1).

\ln x\leq\frac{x}{4}
for
x\geq 9
and
a+b\leq ab
for
a,b\geq 2
, we
get

\displaystyle\ln n

\displaystyle\leq\ln 48+\ln\big(\sqrt{2}\ln(2n)+\ln{\frac{1}{\delta}}\big)+\ln(m+1)

\displaystyle\leq\ln 48+\ln(\sqrt{2}\ln(2n))+\ln\ln{\frac{1}{\delta}}+\ln(m+1)

\displaystyle\leq\ln 48+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\ln(2n)+\ln\ln{\frac{1}{\delta}}+\ln(m+1).

Since

\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\ln(2n)\leq\ln n-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{4}\ln(2n)\ \ \text{for}\ \ n\geq 9,

we get

\sqrt{2}\ln(2n)\leq 4\big(\ln 48+\ln\ln{\frac{1}{\delta}}+\ln(m+1)\big).

It follows that

n\leq 48\big(4\big(\ln 48+\ln\ln{\frac{1}{\delta}}+\ln(m+1)\big)+\ln{\frac{1}{\delta}}\big)(m+1).

We conclude that for sufficiently small
\delta>0
,

(3.13)

\displaystyle n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq 48\Big(4\big(\ln 48

\displaystyle+\ln\ln{\frac{1}{\delta}}+\ln\big(n^{\star}(\frac{\varepsilon}{A_{\delta}},d;\Lambda^{\rm all})+1\big)\big)

\displaystyle+\ln{\frac{1}{\delta}}\Big)\big(n^{\star}(\frac{\varepsilon}{A_{\delta}},d;\Lambda^{\rm all})+1\big).

Since for sufficiently small
\omega,\delta>0

\sup_{x\geq 1}\frac{48(4(\ln 48+\ln\ln{\frac{1}{\delta}}+\ln x)+\ln{\frac{1}{\delta}})}{x^{\omega}}=C_{\omega,\delta}<+\infty,

2.12

(5.7)

n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})\leq C\exp(t(1+\ln{d})(1+\ln(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1))),\ \text{for all}\ d\in\mathbb{N},\ \varepsilon\in(0,1).

For
sufficiently small
\omega>0
and
\delta>0
, it follows from
(
2.12
) and (
5.7
) that

\displaystyle n^{\star}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})

\displaystyle\leq C_{\omega,\delta}\big(n^{\star}(\frac{\varepsilon}{A_{\delta}},d;\Lambda^{\rm all})+1\big)^{1+\omega}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\omega,\delta}\Bigl(C\exp\big(t(1+\ln{d})\big(1+\ln(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+\ln A_{\delta}+1))\big)+1\Bigr)^{1+\omega}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\omega,\delta}(2C)^{1+\omega}\exp\big((1+\omega)t(1+\ln{d})(1+\ln(\ln A_{\delta}+1)+\ln(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1))\big)

(5.8)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\omega,\delta}(2C)^{1+\omega}\exp\big(t^{*}(1+\ln{d})(1+\ln(\ln\varepsilon^{-1}+1))\big),

where
t^{*}=(1+\omega)(1+\ln(\ln A_{\delta}+1))t
and

A_{\delta}=\big(1+\frac{1}{12\ln{\frac{1}{\delta}}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\delta}}
,
in the third inequality we used the fact

\ln(1+a+b)\leq\ln(1+a)+\ln(1+b),\ \ \ a,b\geq 0.

The inequality
(
5.8
) with
\star={\rm ABS}
implies that EXP-QPT holds
for

\Lambda^{\rm std}
for the absolute error criterion.

Next, we suppose that
{\rm EXP}
-
{\rm QPT}
holds for

\Lambda^{\rm all}
for the absolute or normalized error
criterion. Taking the infimum over
t
for which (
5.7
)
holds, and noting that

\lim\limits_{(\delta,\omega)\to(0,0)}(1+\omega)(1+\ln(\ln A_{\delta}+1))=1,

by (
2.5
) we obtain
that

\displaystyle{\rm EXP\!-\!}t^{\star}(\Lambda^{\rm std})\leq{\rm EXP\!-\!}t^{\star}(\Lambda^{\rm all})\leq{\rm EXP\!-\!}t^{\star}(\Lambda^{\rm std}).

which means that
the exponents EXP-
t^{\star}(\Lambda^{\rm all})

and EXP-
t^{\star}(\Lambda^{\rm std})
are equal. This completes the
proof of Theorem 5.4.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

We consider the problem
\rm APP=\{APP_{d}\}_{d\in\mathbb{N}}

the average case setting for the absolute error

{\rm EXP}

(s,t)
-
{\rm WT}

\Lambda^{\rm all}

{\rm EXP}
-
(s,t)
-
{\rm WT}

\Lambda^{\rm std}

{\rm EXP}
-
{\rm WT}
for
\Lambda^{\rm all}
is
equivalent to
{\rm EXP}
-
{\rm WT}

for
\Lambda^{\rm std}

Proof.

Again,
it is enough to prove that
{\rm EXP}
-
(s,t)
-
{\rm WT}
for

\Lambda^{\rm all}

{\rm EXP}
-
(s,t)
-
{\rm WT}
for

\Lambda^{\rm std}

Suppose that
{\rm EXP}
-
(s,t)
-
{\rm WT}
holds for

\Lambda^{\rm all}
. Then we have

(5.9)

\lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln n^{\rm ABS}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})}{(1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{s}+d^{t}}=0.

It follows from (
2.11
) that for
\omega>0

\displaystyle\quad\ \frac{\ln n^{\rm ABS}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})}{(1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{s}+d^{t}}\leq\frac{\ln\Big(C_{\omega}\big(n^{\rm ABS}(\varepsilon/4,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})+1\big)^{1+\omega}\Big)}{(1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{s}+d^{t}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ln(C_{\omega}2^{1+\omega})}{(1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{s}+d^{t}}+\frac{(1+\ln 4)^{s}(1+\omega)\,\ln n^{\rm ABS}(\varepsilon/4,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})}{(1+\ln(\varepsilon/4)^{-1})^{s}+d^{t}}.

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln(C_{\omega}2^{1+\omega})}{(1+\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{s}+d^{t}}=0\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ \lim_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln n^{\rm ABS}(\varepsilon/4,d;\Lambda^{\rm all})}{(1+\ln(\varepsilon/4)^{-1})^{s}+d^{t}}=0.

We obtain

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon^{-1}+d\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln n^{\rm ABS}(\varepsilon,d;\Lambda^{\rm std})}{(\ln\varepsilon^{-1})^{s}+d^{t}}=0,

{\rm EXP}
-

{\rm WT}

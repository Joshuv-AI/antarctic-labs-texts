On a Theorem by Schlenk
Yannis Bähni

Abstract
In this paper we prove a generalisation of Schlenk’s theorem about the existence of contractible periodic Reeb orbits on stable, displaceable hypersurfaces in symplectically aspherical, geometrically bounded, symplectic manifolds, to a forcing result for contractible twisted periodic Reeb orbits. We make use of holomorphic curve techniques for a suitable generalisation of the Rabinowitz action functional in the stable case in order to prove the forcing result. As in Schlenk’s theorem, we derive a lower bound for the displacement energy of the displaceable hypersurface in terms of the action value of such periodic orbits. The main application is a forcing result for noncontractible periodic Reeb orbits on quotients of certain symmetric star-shaped hypersurfaces. Either there exist two geometrically distinct noncontractible periodic Reeb orbits or the period of the noncontractible periodic Reeb orbit is small. This theorem can be applied to many physical systems including the Hénon–Heiles Hamiltonian and Stark–Zeeman systems. Further applications include a new proof of the well-known fact that the displacement energy is a relative symplectic capacity on ℝ 2 ​ n \mathbb{R}^{2n} and that the Hofer metric is indeed a metric.

Introduction

In
[
B“”ah23
]
, a generalisation of Rabinowitz–Floer homology was constructed. Rabinowitz–Floer homology is the Morse–Bott homology in the sense of Floer associated with the Rabinowitz action functional introduced by Kai Cieliebak and Urs Frauenfelder in 2009. The main application of this generalisation was to prove an existence result for noncontractible periodic Reeb orbits on quotients of certain symmetric star-shaped hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. More precisely, let
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a compact and connected star-shaped hypersurface invariant under the rotation

\varphi\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n},\quad\varphi(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(e^{2\pi ik_{1}/m}z_{1},\dots,e^{2\pi ik_{n}/m}z_{n}\right)

for some even
m\geq 2
and
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
coprime to
m
. Then
\Sigma/\mathbb{Z}_{m}
admits a noncontractible periodic Reeb orbit generating the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}/\mathbb{Z}_{m})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{m}
. For a proof see
[
B“”ah23
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
ALM22
, Theorem 1.1]
for the more general result, removing the restriction of
m
being even. The existence of noncontractible periodic Reeb
orbits on lens spaces is extremely relevant and attracts much attention in celestial mechanics as mentioned in
[
LZ22
, Introduction]
or
[
HS16
]
. We quickly recall the setup for the proof of this result. Let
(W,\lambda)
be a connected Liouville domain with connected boundary
\partial W
and consider a Liouville automorphism
\varphi\in\Aut(W,\lambda)
, that is,
\varphi\in\Diff(W)
is of finite order and there exists a unique function
f_{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}(\Int W)
such that
\varphi^{*}\lambda-\lambda=df_{\varphi}
. The main step was to construct a homology theory for the
twisted Rabinowitz action functional

\mathscr{A}^{H}_{\varphi}\colon\mathscr{L}_{\varphi}M\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\mathscr{A}^{H}_{\varphi}(\gamma,\tau)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{0}^{1}\gamma^{*}\lambda-\tau\int_{0}^{1}H(\gamma(t))dt-f_{\varphi}(\gamma(0))

on the completion
(M,\lambda)
of
(W,\lambda)
, where

\mathscr{L}_{\varphi}M\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\gamma\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\gamma(t+1)=\varphi(\gamma(t))\>\forall t\in\mathbb{R}\}

denotes the
twisted loop space of
M
and
\varphi
. Twisted loops play a significant role in physical systems with symmetries, see for example
[
CFV23
, Section 6.2]
. Consider the chain complex
\RFC^{\varphi}(\partial W,M)
generated by a suitable Morse function on the critical manifold
\Crit(\mathscr{A}^{H}_{\varphi})
, where

(\gamma,\tau)\in\Crit(\mathscr{A}^{H}_{\varphi})\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad\begin{cases}\gamma\in\mathscr{L}_{\varphi}\partial W,\\
\dot{\gamma}(t)=\tau R(\gamma(t))\>\forall t\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

with
R\in\mathfrak{X}(\partial W)
denoting the Reeb vector field. We then define twisted Rabinowitz-Floer homology as the Morse–Bott homology with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
by

\RFH^{\varphi}(\partial W,M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\HM(\mathscr{A}^{H}_{\varphi})=\frac{\ker\partial\colon\RFC^{\varphi}(\partial W,M)\to\RFC^{\varphi}(\partial W,M)}{\im\partial\colon\RFC^{\varphi}(\partial W,M)\to\RFC^{\varphi}(\partial W,M)},

where the boundary map
\partial
counts twisted negative gradient flow lines modulo two with respect to a suitable
d\lambda
-compatible almost complex structure on
M
. This homology theory has the following crucial properties.

1.

The semi-infinite dimensional Morse–Bott homology
\RFH^{\varphi}(\partial W,M)
is well-defined. Moreover, twisted Rabinowitz–Floer homology is invariant under twisted homotopies of Liouville domains.

2.

Twisted Rabinowitz–Floer homology is indeed a generalisation of the standard Rabinowitz–Floer homology
\RFH(\partial W,M)
defined in
[
CF09
]
, as

\RFH^{\id_{W}}(\partial W,M)\cong\RFH(\partial W,M).

3.

If
\partial W
is simply connected and does not admit any nonconstant twisted periodic Reeb orbits, then

\RFH^{\varphi}_{*}(\partial W,M)\cong\operatorname{H}_{*}(\Fix(\varphi|_{\partial W});\mathbb{Z}_{2}).

Note that
\Fix(\varphi)
is a symplectic submanifold of
M
by
[
MS17
, Lemma 5.5.7]
.

4.

If
\partial W
is displaceable by a compactly supported Hamiltonian symplectomorphism in the completion
(M,\lambda)
, then

\RFH^{\varphi}(\partial W,M)\cong 0.

For a proof see
[
B“”ah23
, Theorem 1.1]
. Note that there are two possible ways for proving property 4: either one shows that the norm of the gradient of a perturbed version of the twisted Rabinowitz action functional is uniformly bounded from below as in
[
CF09
, Lemma 3.9]
, or one generalises leaf-wise intersection points following
[
AF10
]
. A direct consequence of properties 3 and 4 is the following observation as in
[
CF09
, Corollary 1.5]
. Suppose that
\partial W
is Hamiltonianly displaceable in the completion
(M,\lambda)
and simply connected. If
\Fix(\varphi|_{\partial W})\neq\varnothing
, then
\partial W
does admit a twisted periodic Reeb orbit. Indeed, if there does not exist any twisted periodic Reeb orbit on the boundary
\partial W
, we compute using property 3

\RFH^{\varphi}(\partial W,M)\cong\operatorname{H}(\Fix(\varphi|_{\partial W});\mathbb{Z}_{2})=\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}\operatorname{H}_{j}(\Fix(\varphi|_{\partial W});\mathbb{Z}_{2})\neq 0,

contradicting property 4. However, if
\Fix(\varphi|_{\partial W})=\varnothing
, then one cannot directly conclude the existence of a twisted periodic Reeb orbit on
\partial W
. This is for example the case for the rotation
\varphi\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
from the beginning. So the best one can hope for is some kind of forcing result to hold. More precisely, if we know that there exists a sufficiently well-behaved twisted periodic Reeb orbit, then this
forces
the existence of another one. The above observation is already a forcing result, as
\Fix(\varphi|_{\partial W})
is precisely the set of all constant twisted periodic Reeb orbits on the contact manifold
\partial W
.

2.1
Preliminaries on Twisted Stable Hypersurfaces

Definition 2.1 (Stable Hypersurface,
[
CFP10
, p. 1774]
).

Let
(M,\omega)
be a connected symplectic manifold. A
stable hypersurface
in
(M,\omega)
is a compact and connected hypersurface
\Sigma\subseteq M
such that the following conditions hold:

1.

\Sigma
is separating, that is,
M\setminus\Sigma
consists of two connected components
M^{\pm}
, where
M^{-}
is bounded and
M^{+}
is unbounded.

2.

There exists a vector field
X
in a neighbourhood of
\Sigma
such that
X
is outward-pointing to
\Sigma\cup M^{-}
and
\ker\omega|_{\Sigma}\subseteq\ker L_{X}\omega|_{\Sigma}
.

We write
(\Sigma,\omega|_{\Sigma},\lambda)
for a stable hypersurface, where the stabilising form
\lambda\in\Omega^{1}(\Sigma)
is defined by
\lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=i_{X}\omega|_{\Sigma}
.

Definition 2.2 (Twisted Stable Hypersurface).

Let
(\Sigma,\omega|_{\Sigma},\lambda)
be a stable hypersurface in a connected symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
and
\varphi\in\Symp(M,\omega)
. We say that
\Sigma
is
twisted
by
\varphi
, if
\varphi(\Sigma)=\Sigma
,
\varphi
is of finite order and
\varphi^{*}X=X
.

Example 2.1 (Star-Shaped Hypersurfaces).

Consider the Liouville automorphism

\varphi\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n},\qquad\varphi(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(e^{2\pi ik_{1}/m}z_{1},\dots,e^{2\pi ik_{n}/m}z_{n}\right)

for
m\geq 2
an integer and
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
coprime to
m
. Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{2n-1})
be a positive function such that
f\circ\varphi=f
. Then the star-shaped hypersurface

\Sigma_{f}=\{f(z)z\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}z\in\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}

is a contact manifold with
\varphi
-invariant contact form
\lambda|_{\Sigma_{f}}
, where

\lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(y_{j}dx_{j}-x_{j}dy_{j})=\frac{i}{4}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\overline{z}_{j}dz_{j}-z_{j}d\overline{z}_{j})

with complex coordinates
z_{j}=x_{j}+iy_{j}
. Indeed, by
[
FK18
, Lemma 12.2.2]
, we have that

X_{H_{f}}|_{\Sigma_{f}}\in\ker d\lambda|_{\Sigma_{f}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lambda(X_{H_{f}})|_{\Sigma_{f}}=1

for the defining Hamiltonian function

H_{f}\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad H_{f}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\|z\|^{2}}{f(z/\|z\|^{2})}-1.

Hence
(\Sigma_{f},\lambda|_{\Sigma_{f}})
is a contact manifold as the Liouville vector field

X\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}\right)\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})

satisfies
i_{X}d\lambda=\lambda
and is outward-pointing as

dH_{f}(X)|_{\Sigma_{f}}=d\lambda(X,X_{H_{f}})|_{\Sigma_{f}}=\lambda(X_{H_{f}})|_{\Sigma_{f}}=1.

Finally, we conclude that

X_{H_{f}}|_{\Sigma_{f}}=R_{f}\in\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma_{f})

is the Reeb vector field.

Example 2.2 (Magnetic Torus,
[
CFP10
, Section 6.1]
).

Let
\mathbb{T}^{n}
be the standard flat torus for
n\geq 2
and let

be an antisymmetric nonzero linear map. Define
\rho\in\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})

\rho(\cdot,\cdot)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\langle\cdot,J\cdot\rangle

T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{n}\cong\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For an energy value

set
\Sigma_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H^{-1}(k)
for the mechanical Hamiltonian function

H(q,p)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{2}\|p\|^{2}\qquad\forall(q,p)\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Define
A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(J|_{\im J})^{-1}
and
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(\im J)
by

\alpha_{x}(v)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{2}\langle x,Av\rangle.

Then
\Sigma_{k}
is a displaceable stable Hamiltonian manifold for every
k>0
by
[
CFP10
, Proposition 6.3]
. The stabilising form
\lambda
on
\Sigma_{k}
is given by

\lambda=f^{*}(pdq)+(\pr_{\parallel}\circ\pr)^{*}\alpha,

(1)

where

\pr_{\perp}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\ker J,\qquad\pr_{\parallel}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\im J,\qquad\pr\colon\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

\mathbb{R}^{n}=\ker J\oplus\im J,

and

Let
\varphi\in\Diff(\mathbb{T}^{n})
be an isometry of finite order satisfying

D\varphi\circ J=J\circ D\varphi

(2)

and consider the cotangent lift

D\varphi^{\dagger}\colon\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\qquad D\varphi^{\dagger}(q,p)=\left(\varphi(q),\left(D\varphi^{-1}(q)\right)^{T}p\right).

Then clearly
\varphi(\Sigma_{k})=\Sigma_{k}
as
\varphi
is an isometry and
D\varphi^{\dagger}
is of finite order as
\varphi
is. Moreover, we have that
D\varphi^{\dagger}\in\Symp(T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{n},\omega_{\rho})
, as
D\varphi^{\dagger}\in\Symp(T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{n},dp\wedge dq)
and
D\varphi^{\dagger}
preserves
\rho
by assumption (
2
). Lastly, we have that
\varphi^{*}\lambda=\lambda
by considering formula (
1
) together with assumption (
2
), and thus also
\varphi^{*}X=X
by
[
Abb13
, Proposition 4.2]
.

Definition 2.3 (Hofer Norm,
[
MS17
, p. 466]
).

Let
(M,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold. Define the

of

Definition 2.5 (Symplectic Asphericity,

A connected symplectic manifold

Definition 2.6 (Contractible Twisted Loop Space).

Let
(M,\omega)
be a symplectic manifold and
\varphi\in\Symp(M,\omega)

,

\gamma\in\mathscr{L}_{\varphi}M

v(t)=\gamma(\ord(\varphi)t)\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb{T},

and a filling

\mathbb{D}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}|z|=1\},

for all

. We denote the space of all contractible twisted periodic loops of

and

Definition 2.7 (Twisted Rabinowitz Action Functional).

Let
(\Sigma,\omega|_{\Sigma},\lambda)
be a twisted stable hypersurface in a symplectically aspherical symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
. For a defining Hamiltonian function
H
for
\Sigma
with
H\circ\varphi=H
, we define the
twisted Rabinowitz action functional

\mathscr{A}^{H}_{\varphi}\colon\Lambda_{\varphi}M\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\mathscr{A}^{H}_{\varphi}(v,\tau)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{\ord(\varphi)}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bar{v}^{*}\omega-\tau\int_{0}^{1}H(v(t))dt.

Remark 2.1 (
\Crit(\mathscr{A}^{H}_{\varphi})
).

Let
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\gamma)
be a twisted variation, that is,
X
is a vector field along
\gamma
and satisfies the condition

X(t+1)=D\varphi(X(t))\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

Then a routine computation shows that

(v,\tau)\in\Crit(\mathscr{A}^{H}_{\varphi})\qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad\begin{cases}\gamma\in\mathscr{L}_{\varphi}\Sigma,\\
\dot{\gamma}(t)=\tau R(\gamma(t))\>\forall t\in[0,1],\end{cases}

where
R\in\mathfrak{X}(\Sigma)
is the stable Reeb vector field. If
J
is a
\varphi
-invariant almost complex structure compatible with
\omega
, then the gradient
\grad_{J}\mathscr{A}^{H}_{\varphi}\in\mathfrak{X}(\Lambda_{\varphi}M\times\mathbb{R})
with respect to the
L^{2}
-metric

m\left((X,\eta),(Y,\sigma)\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{0}^{1}\omega(JX(t),Y(t))dt+\eta\sigma\qquad\forall(X,\eta),(Y,\sigma)\in T_{(v,\tau)}\Lambda_{\varphi}M\times\mathbb{R},

and
(v,\tau)\in\Lambda_{\varphi}M\times\mathbb{R}
, is given by

\grad_{J}\mathscr{A}^{H}_{\varphi}|_{(v,\tau)}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}J(\dot{\gamma}(\ord(\varphi)t)-\tau X_{H}(v(t)))\\
\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}H\circ v\end{pmatrix}\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb{T}.

Hence
(u,\tau)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\Lambda_{\varphi}M\times\mathbb{R})
is a twisted negative gradient flow line, if the elliptic partial differential equations or
twisted Rabinowitz–Floer equations

\partial_{s}u(s,t)+J\left(\partial_{t}\gamma(s,\ord(\varphi)t)-\tau(s)X_{H}(u(s,t))\right)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{s}\tau(s)=\int_{0}^{1}H(u(s,t))dt

hold for all
(s,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}
.

2.2
A Forcing Theorem for Twisted Periodic Reeb Orbits

Let
(W,\lambda)
be a connected Liouville domain with connected boundary
\Sigma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\partial W
. Let
(M,\lambda)
be the completion of
(W,\lambda)
and
\varphi\in\Aut(W,\lambda)
a Liouville automorphism, that is,
\varphi\in\Diff(W)
is a diffeomorphism of finite order such that
\varphi^{*}\lambda=\lambda
. In this setup, the kernel of the twisted Rabinowitz action functional
\mathscr{A}^{H}_{\varphi}
admits the canonical description

\ker\Hess\mathscr{A}^{H}_{\varphi}|_{(v,\tau)}\cong\ker\left(D^{\xi}(\phi_{-\tau}^{R}\circ\varphi)|_{v(0)}-\id|_{\xi_{v(0)}}\right)\oplus\langle R(v(0))\rangle

by
[
B“”ah23
, Proposition 2.23]
, where
\xi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\ker\lambda|_{\Sigma}
denotes the contact distribution.

Definition 2.10 (Transversal Nondegeneracy,
[
FK18
, Definition 7.3.1]
).

Let
(M,\lambda)
be the completion of a connected Liouville domain
(W,\lambda)
. A contractible twisted periodic Reeb orbit
(v,\tau)\in\Crit(\mathscr{A}_{\varphi}^{H})
is said to be
nondegenerate
, if

\ker\left(D^{\xi}(\phi_{-\tau}^{R}\circ\varphi)|_{v(0)}-\id|_{\xi_{v(0)}}\right)=\{0\}.

Theorem 2.1.

Let
\Sigma\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be a compact and connected star-shaped hypersurface invariant under the rotation

\varphi\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n},\quad\varphi(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(e^{2\pi ik_{1}/m}z_{1},\dots,e^{2\pi ik_{n}/m}z_{n}\right)

for some
m\geq 2
and
k_{1},\dots,k_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
coprime to
m
. Assume that there exists a nondegenerate noncontractible simple periodic Reeb orbit
(\gamma_{0},\tau_{0})
on
\Sigma/\mathbb{Z}_{m}
. Then there does exist a noncontractible periodic Reeb orbit
(\gamma,\tau)
on
\Sigma/\mathbb{Z}_{m}
such that

0<\tau-\tau_{0}\leq e(\Sigma).

Consequently, we have two cases.

1.

If
\gamma
is not an interate of
\gamma_{0}
, then
\Sigma/\mathbb{Z}_{m}
admits two geometrically distinct noncontractible periodic Reeb orbits.

2.

If
\gamma
is a
p
-fold iterate of
\gamma_{0}
, then the period
\tau_{0}
is small in the sense of

0<\tau_{0}\leq\frac{1}{p-1}e(\Sigma).

Remark 2.2.

We cannot conclude the existence of two geometrically distinct noncontractible periodic Reeb orbits as in
[
ALM22
, Theorem 1.2]
from Theorem
2.1
even under the additional assumption that
\Sigma
is dynamically convex. Indeed, the sphere
\Sigma=\mathbb{S}^{2n-1}
is dynamically convex by the Hofer–Wysocki–Zehnder Theorem
[
FK18
, Theorem 12.2.1]
, and both cases do occur there.

Example 2.8 (The Hénon–Heiles Hamiltonian,
[
Sch20
, Section 2]
).

Consider the mechanical Hamiltonian function

H\colon\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad H(q_{1},q_{2},p_{1},p_{2})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|p\|^{2}+\|q\|^{2}\right)+q_{1}^{2}q_{2}-\frac{1}{3}q_{2}^{3}.

This Hamiltonian function is known as the
Hénon–Heiles Hamiltonian
. On
\mathbb{R}^{4}\cong\mathbb{C}^{2}
consider the coordinates

z\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=q_{1}+iq_{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad w\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=p_{1}+ip_{2}.

Define

\varphi\colon\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2},\qquad\varphi(z,w)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=e^{2\pi i/3}(z,w).

We have that
\varphi^{*}\lambda=\lambda
for

\lambda=\frac{1}{2}(p_{1}dq_{1}-q_{1}dp_{1})+\frac{1}{2}(p_{2}dq_{2}-q_{2}dp_{2}).

For every
0<k<\frac{1}{6}
, the regular energy surface
H^{-1}(k)
contains a strictly convex sphere-like component
\Sigma_{k}\cong\mathbb{S}^{3}
. The resulting quotient
\Sigma_{k}/\mathbb{Z}_{3}
is diffeomorphic to the lens space
L(3,1)
, but not contactomorphic to it with the standard contact distribution. Here we write
L(m,k_{2})
for the lens space
\mathbb{S}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}_{m}
from Example
2.1
with
k_{1}=1
. Instead, the quotient
\Sigma_{k}/\mathbb{Z}_{3}
is contactomorphic to
L(3,2)
with its standard contact distribution. This is mainly due to the use of different coordinates. By a shooting argument, one can show that there exist at least two
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-symmetric periodic orbits on
\Sigma_{k}
. In fact, by
[
Sch20
, Corollary 2.5]
, there exist infinitely many periodic orbits on
\Sigma_{k}
.

Example 2.9 (Hill’s Lunar Problem,
[
FK18
, Section 5.8]
).

The mechanical Hamiltonian function
H\colon T^{*}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\})\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

H(q,p)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{2}\|p\|^{2}-\frac{1}{\|q\|}+q_{1}p_{2}-q_{2}p_{1}-q_{1}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}q_{2}^{2}

sufficiently small, the energy hypersurface

and thus

is diffeomorphic to the lens space

, but again due to the choice of nonstandard coordinates not contactomorphic to it. It is a delicate question in Contact Topology to decide the correct value of

, such that the obtained lens space

in Hill’s lunar problem is contactomorphic to

Example 2.10 (Stark–Zeeman Systems).

Theorem

Theorem 2.2 (Forcing).

Let
\Sigma
be a twisted stable displaceable hypersurface in a symplectically aspherical, geometrically bounded, symplectic manifold

Remark 2.3.

The case

Applications

3.1
The Hofer Distance and Relative Symplectic Capacities

Computing the displacement energy
2.4
is usually quite difficult. Sometimes it is possible to give upper bounds on the displacement energy as in
[
Iri14
, Theorem 1]
or lower bounds as for any nonempty open subset
A\subseteq M
of a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
we have
e(A)>0
as in
[
BHS18
, Theorem 1.1]
. Corollary
2.1
has two immediate consequences. First, the existence of a nonconstant contractible twisted periodic Reeb orbit on any twisted stable displaceable hypersurface. Second, the existence of a lower bound for the displacement energy via the action value of this critical point. If the hypersurface is of contact type, this action value is precisely the period of the parametrised periodic Reeb orbit. We illustrate the usefulness of the second implication and give dynamical proofs of standard results. Recall, that a
relative symplectic capacity on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
is a map
c
which assigns to each subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2n}
a number
c(A)\in[0,+\infty]
such that the following three properties hold
[
MS17
, p. 460]
.

1.

(
Relative Monotonicity
) If there exists a symplectomorphism
\psi
of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
such that
\psi(A)\subseteq B
, then
c(A)\leq c(B)
.

2.

(
Conformality
)
c(\lambda A)=\lambda^{2}c(A)
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

3.

(
Normalisation
) It holds that

c(B^{2n}(r))=c(Z^{2n}(r))=\pi r^{2}\qquad\forall r>0,

for the closed ball of radius
r

B^{2n}(r)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\|x\|^{2}+\|y\|^{2}\leq r^{2}\right\},

and the closed cylinder

Z^{2n}(r)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}x_{1}^{2}+y_{1}^{2}\leq r^{2}\right\}.

Proposition 3.1 (
[
MS17
, Theorem 12.3.4]
).

The displacement energy
e
is a relative symplectic capacity on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

Proof.

Relative monotonicity and conformality are not hard to show. Moreover, by relative monotonicity and
[
MS17
, Exercise 12.3.7]
we have that

e(\partial B^{2n}(r))\leq e(B^{2n}(r))\leq e(Z^{2n}(r))\leq\pi r^{2}\qquad\forall r>0.

The periodic Reeb flow on
\partial B^{2n}(r)
is given by

\phi^{R_{r}}_{t}(z)=e^{-2it/r^{2}}z\qquad\forall z\in\partial B^{2n}(r).

Hence the parametrised periodic Reeb orbits are
(\phi^{R_{r}}(z),\tau)
with
\tau\in\pi r^{2}\mathbb{Z}
. But Corollary
2.1
implies the existence of a nonconstant closed periodic Reeb orbit
(v,\tau)
on the contact hypersurface
\partial B^{2n}(r)
such that

0<\tau=\int_{0}^{1}v^{*}\lambda\leq e(\partial B^{2n}(r))\leq\pi r^{2},

where

\lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(y_{j}dx_{j}-x_{j}dy_{j}).

This is only possible for
\tau=\pi r^{2}
and the statement follows.
∎

Proposition 3.2 (
[
BHS18
, Theorem 1.1]
).

For any subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2n}
with nonempty interior we have that
e(A)>0
.

Proof.

If
A\subseteq M
is not displaceable, we have that
e(A)=+\infty
and thus there is nothing to show. Moreover, if
A
is not compact, we define

e(A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sup_{K\subseteq A\text{ $K$ compact}}e(K).

So we can assume that
A
is displaceable by a compactly supported Hamiltonian symplectomorphism
\varphi_{F}\in\Ham_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2n},dy\wedge dx)
. As
A
is displaceable and has nonempty interior, there exists a closed ball
B(r)
of radius
r
such that

\varphi_{F}(B(r))\cap B(r)=\varnothing.

Since the displacement energy is a normalised relative symplectic capacity by Proposition
3.1
, we conclude that

e(A)\geq e(B(r))=\pi r^{2}>0.

∎

Corollary 3.1 (Hofer Distance,
[
MS17
, Theorem 12.3.3]
).

On
\Ham_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2n},dy\wedge dx)
define the
Hofer distance

\rho(\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf_{\varphi_{F}=\varphi_{1}\circ\varphi_{0}^{-1}}\|F\|.

Then

\rho(\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1})=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\varphi_{0}=\varphi_{1}\qquad\forall\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\in\Ham_{c}(M,\omega),

that is, the Hofer distance is a metric on
\Ham_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2n},dy\wedge dx)
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in\Ham_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2n},dy\wedge dx)
be not equal to the identity. Thus there exists a set
A
with nonempty interior such that
\varphi(A)\cap A=\varnothing
. Thus Lemma
3.2
implies

\rho(\varphi,\id_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}})\geq e(A)>0.

This proves the statement.
∎

Remark 3.1.

In
[
LM95
, Corollary 1.2]
, these results are generalised to arbitrary symplectic manifolds.

3.2
Physical Systems and the Mañé Critical Value

Proposition 3.3.

Let
(T^{*}M,dp\wedge dq,H)
be a Hamiltonian system for a compact configuration space
M
and define

e_{0}(H)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf\left\{k\in\mathbb{R}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\pi_{T^{*}M}\left(H^{-1}(k)\right)=M\right\},

where
\pi_{T^{*}M}\colon T^{*}M\to M
denotes the projection. Suppose that
\Sigma_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H^{-1}(k)
with
k<e_{0}(H)
is a
\varphi
-twisted stable regular energy surface admitting a contractible twisted periodic Reeb orbit
(q_{0},p_{0})
belonging to a Morse–Bott component
C
. Then there exists a contractible twisted periodic Reeb orbit
(q,p)\notin C
such that

\int_{0}^{1}p(t)\dot{q}(t)dt-\int_{0}^{1}p_{0}(t)\dot{q}_{0}(t)dt\leq\ord(\varphi)e(\Sigma_{c}).

Proof.

We claim that
e(\Sigma_{k})<+\infty
for all
k<e_{0}(H)
. In particular, every energy hypersurface
\Sigma_{k}
is displaceable in the geometrically bounded and symplectically aspherical symplectic manifold
(T^{*}M,dp\wedge dq)
since
T^{*}M
is an exact symplectic form with canonical Liouville form
pdq
. As
k<e_{0}(H)
, we can displace
\Sigma_{k}
into the missing fibres. The explicit compactly supported Hamiltonian symplectomorphism achieving that is constructed in
[
Con06
, Proposition 8.2]
. Hence if
\Sigma_{k}
is twisted stable and
k<e_{0}(H)
, we conclude the existence of such a contractible periodic Reeb orbit from Theorem
2.2
.
∎

Example 3.1 (Magnetic Torus).

Let
M
be a compact manifold and
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. Then the map

\varphi_{\theta}\colon(T^{*}M,dp\wedge dq)\to(T^{*}M,\omega_{d\theta}),\qquad\varphi_{\theta}(q,p)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(q,p-\theta_{q})

is an exact symplectomorphism. Indeed, for every
(q,p)\in T^{*}M
and
v\in TT^{*}_{(q,p)}M
we compute

\displaystyle(\varphi^{*}_{-\theta}\lambda)_{(q,p)}(v)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{(q,p+\theta_{q})}\left(D\varphi_{-\theta}(v)\right)

\displaystyle=p\left(D\pi_{T^{*}M}(v)\right)+\theta_{q}\left(D\pi_{T^{*}M}(v)\right)

\displaystyle=(\lambda+\pi_{T^{*}M}^{*}\theta)_{(q,p)}(v),

where
\lambda\in\Omega^{1}(T^{*}M)
denotes the canonical Liouville form and
\varphi_{-\theta}\circ\varphi_{\theta}=\id_{T^{*}M}
. A mechanical Hamiltonian function

H\colon(T^{*}M,\omega_{d\theta})\to\mathbb{R},\qquad H(q,p)=\frac{1}{2}\|p\|_{m^{*}}^{2}+V(q),

for some potential function
V\in C^{\infty}(M)
is transformed under
\varphi_{\theta}
to a magnetic Hamiltonian function
H_{\theta}=\varphi_{\theta}^{*}H
given by

H_{\theta}\colon(T^{*}M,dp\wedge dq)\to\mathbb{R},\qquad H_{\theta}(q,p)=\frac{1}{2}\|p-\theta_{q}\|^{2}_{m^{*}}+V(q).

In the case of a magnetic torus as in Example
2.11
, we have that

\theta_{q}(v)=\frac{1}{2}\langle q,Jv\rangle\qquad\forall(q,v)\in\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Thus if
k>0
, the intersection of
\Sigma_{k}=H^{-1}_{\theta}(k)
with
T_{q}^{*}\mathbb{T}^{n}
is a sphere centred at
\theta_{q}
for every
q\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
. For more details see
[
AS19
, Example 5.2]
. Consequently, we have that
e_{0}=0
and Proposition
3.3
cannot be applied. Note that the Mañé critical value
c
is infinite in this case because a nonzero
\rho
has no bounded primitives in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Remark 3.2.

In the setting of Proposition
3.3
, if
H
is a Tonelli Hamiltonian function, that is,
H
is strictly fibrewise convex and superlinear, then any stable energy level of

does contain a periodic Reeb orbit by
[
MP10
]
. See also
[
Abb13

.

Remark 3.3.

The proof of Proposition
3.3
does not work for higher energy values in general. This is due to a theorem of Will Merry in
[
Mer11
, Theorem 1.1]
and
[
Mer11

c\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf_{\theta}\sup_{q\in\widetilde{M}}\widetilde{H}(q,\theta_{q}),

where the infimum is taken over all
1
-forms
\theta
on the universal covering manifold
\widetilde{M}

4
Proof of Theorem
2.2

The proof of Theorem
2.2
uses a method called a “homotopy of homotopies argument”. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and choose a Hamiltonian function
F\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M\times[0,1])
satisfying

\textstyle F_{t}=0\>\forall t\in[0,\frac{1}{2}],\qquad\|F\|<e(\Sigma)+\varepsilon\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varphi_{F}(\Sigma)\cap\Sigma=\varnothing.

This is possible by definition of the displacement energy
2.4
. Next we need to carefully choose a twisted defining Hamiltonian function
H
for the stable hypersurface
\Sigma
. We postpone the construction of this Hamiltonian function and explain the main idea of the proof. Choose a smooth family
(\beta_{r})_{r\in[0,+\infty)}
of cutoff functions
\beta_{r}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},[0,1])
such that

\begin{cases}\beta_{r}(s)=0&|s|\geq r,\\
\beta_{r}(s)=1&|s|\leq r-1,\\
s\beta^{\prime}_{r}(s)\leq 0&\forall s\in\mathbb{R},\end{cases}

for all
r\in[0,+\infty)
. Define a family of twisted Rabinowitz action functionals

\mathscr{A}_{r}\colon\Lambda_{\varphi}M\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

by

\mathscr{A}_{r}(v,\tau,s)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathscr{A}^{H}_{\varphi}(v,\tau)-\beta_{r}(s)\int_{0}^{1}F_{t}(v(t))dt

for all
r\in[0,+\infty)
. Note that
\mathscr{A}_{0}=\mathscr{A}^{H}_{\varphi}
. For a suitable
\varphi
-invariant
\omega
-compatible almost complex structure we consider the moduli space

\mathscr{M}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{(u,\tau,r)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathscr{L}_{\varphi}M\times\mathbb{R})\times[0,+\infty)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(u,\tau,r)\text{ solution of \eqref{eq:moduli_space}}\},

where

\begin{cases}\partial_{s}(u,\tau)=\grad\mathscr{A}_{r}|_{(u(s),\tau(s),s)}&\forall s\in\mathbb{R},\\
\displaystyle\lim_{s\to-\infty}(u(s),\tau(s))=(v_{0},\tau_{0}),\\
\displaystyle\lim_{s\to+\infty}(u(s),\tau(s))\in C.\end{cases}

(3)

Note that always
(v_{0},\tau_{0},0)\in\mathscr{M}
and that such a
\varphi
-invariant
\omega
-compatible almost complex structure always exists by
[
MS17
, Lemma 5.5.6]
. The gradient
\grad\mathscr{A}_{r}
of
\mathscr{A}_{r}
is taken with respect to the metric

m\left((X,\eta),(Y,\sigma)\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{0}^{1}\omega(JX(t),Y(t))dt+\eta\sigma.

Lemma 4.1.

If

\mathscr{A}_{0}(v,\tau)>\|F\|+\mathscr{A}_{0}(v_{0},\tau_{0})\qquad\forall(v,\tau)\in\Crit(\mathscr{A}_{0})\setminus C,

(4)

then
\mathscr{M}
is compact.

As a corollary of Lemma
4.1
we get Theorem
2.2
. Indeed, the moduli space
\mathscr{M}
is the zero level set of a Fredholm section of a bundle over a Banach manifold. As
v_{0}
belongs to a Morse–Bott component, the Fredholm section is regular at the point
v_{0}
, that is, the linearisation of the gradient flow equation is surjective there. By compactness, we can therefore perturb the Fredholm section to make it transverse. Hence
\mathscr{M}
is a compact smooth manifold with boundary consisting precisely of the point
v_{0}
. See
[
MS12
, Appendix A]
for details. This is absurd, and we conclude that there exists a critical point
(v,\tau)\in\Crit(\mathscr{A}_{0})\setminus C
such that

\mathscr{A}_{0}(v,\tau)-\mathscr{A}_{0}(v_{0},\tau_{0})\leq\|F\|<e(\Sigma)+\varepsilon.

As
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, the statement follows since

\mathscr{A}_{0}(v,\tau)=\frac{1}{\ord(\varphi)}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bar{v}^{*}\omega\qquad\forall(v,\tau)\in\Crit(\mathscr{A}_{0}).

We prove Lemma
4.1
in four steps.

Step 1: If
(u,\tau,r)\in\mathscr{M}
, then
E(u,\tau)\leq\|F\|
for the energy

E(u,\tau)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\|\partial_{s}(u,\tau)\|^{2}_{J}ds.

We estimate

\displaystyle E(u,\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\|\partial_{s}(u,\tau)\|_{J}^{2}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\mathscr{A}_{r}(\partial_{s}(u,\tau),s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{d}{ds}\mathscr{A}_{r}(u,\tau,s)ds-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(\partial_{s}\mathscr{A}_{r})(u,\tau,s)ds

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to+\infty}\mathscr{A}_{r}(u,\tau,s)-\lim_{s\to-\infty}\mathscr{A}_{r}(u,\tau,s)-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(\partial_{s}\mathscr{A}_{r})(u,\tau,s)ds

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}_{0}(v,\tau)-\mathscr{A}_{0}(v_{0},\tau_{0})-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(\partial_{s}\mathscr{A}_{r})(u,\tau,s)ds

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}(\partial_{s}\mathscr{A}_{r})(u,\tau,s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\dot{\beta}_{r}(s)\int_{0}^{1}F_{t}(u(s,t))dtds

\displaystyle\leq\|F\|_{+}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\dot{\beta}_{r}(s)ds-\|F\|_{-}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\dot{\beta}_{r}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\beta_{r}(0)(\|F\|_{-}+\|F\|_{+})

\displaystyle=\beta_{r}(0)\|F\|

\displaystyle\leq\|F\|,

as
\mathscr{A}_{0}(v,\tau)=\mathscr{A}_{0}(v_{0},\tau_{0})
since
C
is action-constant by definiton of a Morse–Bott component
2.8
.

Step 2: There exists
r_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
r\leq r_{0}
for all
(u,\tau,r)\in\mathscr{M}
.
Crucial is the existence of a constant
\delta>0
such that

\|\grad\mathscr{A}_{r}|_{(v,\tau,s)}\|_{J}\geq\delta\qquad\forall(v,\tau,s)\in\Lambda_{\varphi}M\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}.

This is proven along the lines of
[
CF09
, Lemma 3.9]
. With this inequality and Step 1 we estimate

\|F\|\geq E(u,\tau)\geq\int_{-r}^{r}\|\grad\mathscr{A}_{r}|_{(u(s),\tau(s),s)}\|^{2}_{J}ds\geq 2r\delta^{2},

and thus we can set

r_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\|F\|}{2\delta^{2}}.

Step 3: There exists a constant
C>0
such that
\|\tau\|_{\infty}\leq C
for all
(u,\tau,r)\in\mathscr{M}
.
This is a delicate estimate based on the explicit construction of the defining Hamiltonian
H
for
\Sigma
as well as an extension of the stabilising form
\lambda
. The bound on the Lagrange multiplier is derived by comparing the twisted Rabinowitz action functional to a different action functional. This modified version of the twisted Rabinowitz action functional is obtained using a suitable extension of the
\varphi
-invariant stabilising form
\lambda\in\Omega^{1}(\Sigma)
to a compactly supported form
\beta_{\lambda}\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. The precise constructions can be found in
[
CFP10
, Section 4.2.2]
. Given
\beta_{\lambda}
, we can define the auxiliary action functional

\widehat{\mathscr{A}}_{0}\colon\Lambda_{\varphi}M\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\widehat{\mathscr{A}}_{0}(v,\tau)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{0}^{1}v^{*}\beta_{\lambda}-\tau\int_{0}^{1}H(v(t))dt.

Moreover, we consider the bilinear form on the tangent bundle
T\Lambda_{\varphi}M\times\mathbb{R}

\widehat{m}\left((X,\eta),(Y,\sigma)\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{0}^{1}d\beta_{\lambda}(JX(t),Y(t))dt+\eta\sigma.

The main point in the choice of the
\varphi
-invariant
H\in C^{\infty}(M)
,
\beta_{\lambda}\in\Omega^{1}(M)
and the
\omega
-compatible
\varphi
-invariant almost complex structure
J
is to make sure, that the properties

1.

d\widehat{\mathscr{A}}_{0}(v,\tau)(X,\eta)=\widehat{m}\left(\grad\mathscr{A}_{0}(v,\tau),(X,\eta)\right)
,

2.

(m-\widehat{m})\left((X,\eta),(X,\eta)\right)\leq 0
,

are true for all
(v,\tau)\in\Lambda_{\varphi}M\times\mathbb{R}
and
(X,\eta)\in T_{(v,\tau)}\Lambda_{\varphi}M\times\mathbb{R}
. These two conditions ensure that the difference
\mathscr{A}_{0}-\widehat{\mathscr{A}}_{0}
is a Liapunov function for the negative gradient flow lines of the twisted Rabinowitz action functional
\mathscr{A}_{0}
. The uniform bound on the Lagrange multiplier
\tau
now follows from Steps 1 and 2. For details see
[
CFP10
, p. 1808]
. The only subtle difference in our case is, that everything needs to be
\varphi
-invariant. However, this is no problem as we explain now. The construction of
H
,
\beta_{\lambda}
and
J
is based on the existence of a stable tubular neighbourhood of
\Sigma
, that is, a pair
(\rho_{0},\psi)
with
\rho_{0}>0
and
\psi\colon(-\rho_{0},\rho_{0})\times\Sigma\hookrightarrow M
an embedding such that

\psi|_{\{0\}\times\Sigma}=\iota_{\Sigma}\colon\Sigma\hookrightarrow M\qquad\text{and}\qquad\psi^{*}_{\rho}\omega=\omega|_{\Sigma}+d(\rho\lambda).

By
[
CFP10
, Proposition 2.6 (a)]
, the space of stable tubular neighbourhoods of
(\Sigma,\lambda)
is nonempty. Using the quivariant Darboux–Weinstein Theorem
[
VG84
, Theorem 22.1]
, we get the existence of a stable tubular neighbourhood
(\rho_{0},\psi)
, satisfying

\varphi(\psi(\rho,x))=\psi(\rho,\varphi(x))\qquad\forall(\rho,x)\in(-\rho_{0},\rho_{0})\times\Sigma.

(5)

Compare also
[
B“”ah23
, Equation (3.2)]
. Hence the constructions
[
CFP10
, p. 1791–1793]
yield
\varphi
-invariant data
H
,
\beta_{\lambda}
and
J
due to (
5
).

Step 4: Proof of Lemma
4.1
.
Let
(u_{k},\tau_{k},r_{k})
be a sequence in the moduli space
\mathscr{M}
. By Step 2 and Step 3, the sequences
(r_{k})
and
(\tau_{k})
are uniformly bounded. Thus
(u_{k},\tau_{k},r_{k})
admits a
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
-convergent subsequence by standard arguments
[
MS12
, Theorem B.4.2]
. Indeed, the uniform
L^{\infty}
-bound on the sequence
(u_{k})
follows from the assumption that
(M,\omega)
is geometrically bounded and the uniform
L^{\infty}
-bound on the derivatives
(Du_{k})
follows from the absence of bubbling as
(M,\omega)
is symplectically aspherical. In particular, there cannot exist a nonconstant
J
-holomorphic sphere when the sequence of derivatives is unbounded
[
MS12
, Section 4.2]
. Denote the limit of this subsequence by
(u,\tau,r)
. This limit clearly satisfies the first equation in (
3
), thus one only needs to check the asymptotic conditions in (
3
). Again by compactness,
(u,\tau)
converges to critical points
(w_{\pm},\tau_{\pm})
of
\mathscr{A}_{0}
at its asymptotic ends. We claim that

\mathscr{A}_{r}(u(s),\tau(s),s)\in\left[-\|F\|+\mathscr{A}_{0}(v_{0},\tau_{0}),\|F\|+\mathscr{A}_{0}(v_{0},\tau_{0})\right]\qquad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

(6)

In particular,
\mathscr{A}_{0}(w_{\pm},\tau_{\pm})\in\left[-\|F\|+\mathscr{A}_{0}(v_{0},\tau_{0}),\|F\|+\mathscr{A}_{0}(v_{0},\tau_{0})\right]
. So if (
6
) holds, then by assumption (
4
) we conclude
(w_{\pm},\tau_{\pm})\in C
and
\mathscr{M}
is indeed compact. It remains to prove (
6
). It is enough to show

\mathscr{A}_{r}(u_{k}(s),\tau_{k}(s),s)\in\left[-\|F\|+\mathscr{A}_{0}(v_{0},\tau_{0}),\|F\|+\mathscr{A}_{0}(v_{0},\tau_{0})\right]\qquad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. As in the proof of
[
AF10
, Lemma 2.8]
we estimate

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int^{+\infty}_{s_{0}}d\mathscr{A}_{r}(\partial_{s}(u_{k},\tau_{k}),s)ds

\displaystyle=\int^{+\infty}_{s_{0}}\frac{d}{ds}\mathscr{A}_{r}(u_{k},\tau_{k},s)ds-\int^{+\infty}_{s_{0}}(\partial_{s}\mathscr{A}_{r})(u_{k},\tau_{k},s)ds

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\to+\infty}\mathscr{A}_{r}(u_{k},\tau_{k},s)-\mathscr{A}_{r}(u_{k}(s_{0}),\tau_{k}(s_{0}),s_{0})-\int^{+\infty}_{s_{0}}(\partial_{s}\mathscr{A}_{r})(u_{k},\tau_{k},s)ds

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}_{0}(v,\tau)-\mathscr{A}_{r}(u_{k}(s_{0}),\tau_{k}(s_{0}),s_{0})+\int^{+\infty}_{s_{0}}\dot{\beta}_{r}(s)\int_{0}^{1}F_{t}(u_{k}(s,t))dtds

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{A}_{0}(v,\tau)-\mathscr{A}_{r}(u_{k}(s_{0}),\tau_{k}(s_{0}),s_{0})+\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}\|\dot{\beta}_{r}(s)F\|_{+}ds

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{A}_{0}(v,\tau)-\mathscr{A}_{r}(u_{k}(s_{0}),\tau_{k}(s_{0}),s_{0})+\|F\|

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}_{0}(v_{0},\tau_{0})-\mathscr{A}_{r}(u_{k}(s_{0}),\tau_{k}(s_{0}),s_{0})+\|F\|

for all
s_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Similarly, we compute

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{s_{0}}d\mathscr{A}_{r}(\partial_{s}(u_{k},\tau_{k}),s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{s_{0}}\frac{d}{ds}\mathscr{A}_{r}(u_{k},\tau_{k},s)ds-\int_{-\infty}^{s_{0}}(\partial_{s}\mathscr{A}_{r})(u_{k},\tau_{k},s)ds

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}_{r}(u_{k}(s_{0}),\tau_{k}(s_{0}),s_{0})-\lim_{s\to-\infty}\mathscr{A}_{r}(u_{k},\tau_{k},s)-\int_{-\infty}^{s_{0}}(\partial_{s}\mathscr{A}_{r})(u_{k},\tau_{k},s)ds

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}_{r}(u_{k}(s_{0}),\tau_{k}(s_{0}),s_{0})-\mathscr{A}_{0}(v_{0},\tau_{0})+\int_{-\infty}^{s_{0}}\dot{\beta}_{r}(s)\int_{0}^{1}F_{t}(u_{k}(s,t))dtds,

and thus we estimate

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{r}(u_{k}(s_{0}),\tau_{k}(s_{0}),s_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\mathscr{A}_{0}(v_{0},\tau_{0})-\int_{-\infty}^{s_{0}}\dot{\beta}_{r}(s)\int_{0}^{1}F_{t}(u_{k}(s,t))dtds

\displaystyle\geq\mathscr{A}_{0}(v_{0},\tau_{0})-\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\|\dot{\beta}(s)F\|_{+}ds

\displaystyle\geq\mathscr{A}_{0}(v_{0},\tau_{0})-\|F\|.

This shows the estimate (
6
) and so the proof of Lemma
4.1
and Theorem
2.2
is complete.

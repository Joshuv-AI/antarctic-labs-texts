Matrix-Valued Gabor Frames over LCA Groups for Operators
Jyoti, Lalit Kumar Vashisht ∗, Uttam Kumar Sinha

Abstract
. Gǎvruta studied atomic systems in terms of frames for range of operators (that is, for subspaces), namely K K -frames, where the lower frame condition is controlled by the Hilbert-adjoint of a bounded linear operator K K . For a locally compact abelian group G and a positive integer n n , we study frames of matrix-valued Gabor systems in the matrix-valued Lebesgue space L 2 ​ ( G , ℂ n × n ) L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}) , where a bounded linear operator Θ \Theta on L 2 ​ ( G , ℂ n × n ) L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}) controls not only lower but also the upper frame condition. We term such frames matrix-valued ( Θ , Θ ∗ ) (\Theta,\Theta^{*}) -Gabor frames. Firstly, we discuss frame preserving mapping in terms of hyponormal operators. Secondly, we give necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of matrix-valued ( Θ , Θ ∗ ) (\Theta,\Theta^{*}) - Gabor frames in terms of hyponormal operators. It is shown that if Θ \Theta is adjointable hyponormal operator, then L 2 ​ ( G , ℂ n × n ) L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}) admits a λ \lambda -tight ( Θ , Θ ∗ ) (\Theta,\Theta^{*}) -Gabor frame for every positive real number λ \lambda . A characterization of matrix-valued ( Θ , Θ ∗ ) (\Theta,\Theta^{*}) -Gabor frames is given. Finally, we show that matrix-valued ( Θ , Θ ∗ ) (\Theta,\Theta^{*}) -Gabor frames are stable under small perturbation of window functions. Several examples are given to support our study.

Introduction

In a fundamental published paper
[
10
]
, Gabor introduced a fundamental approach to signal decomposition in terms of elementary
signals. Duffin and Schaeffer
[
8
]
in
1952, while addressing some deep problems in non-harmonic Fourier
series, abstracted Gabor’s method to define frames for Hilbert
spaces. To be exact, they introduced frames of exponentials for the space
L^{2}(-\delta,\delta)
under the name Fourier frame. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a complex separable Hilbert space with an inner product
\langle.,.\rangle
. A countable collection of vector
\Phi:=\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
in a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is called a
frame
(or
Hilbert frame
) for
\mathcal{H}
if there exist finite positive scalars
A_{o}

B_{o}
such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle A_{o}\|\varphi\|^{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\langle\varphi,\varphi_{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq B_{o}\|\varphi\|^{2}

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
.
The scalars
A_{o}
and
B_{o}
are called the
lower
and
upper frame bounds
of
\Phi
, respectively. Ineq. (
1.1
) is called the
frame inequality
of
\Phi
. The frame inequality guarantee invertibility of the frame operator
\mathcal{S}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
given by
\mathcal{S}\varphi=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle\varphi,\varphi_{k}\rangle\varphi_{k}
. This gives the stable reconstruction of each
\varphi
in
\mathcal{H}:

\varphi=\mathcal{S}\mathcal{S}^{-1}\varphi=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{S}^{-1}\varphi,\varphi_{k}\rangle\varphi_{k}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\langle\varphi,\mathcal{S}^{-1}\varphi_{k}\rangle\varphi_{k}.
This decomposition is useful in signal processing
[
23
,
27
]
, in particular, in lost of coefficients, see
[
4
,
14
]
for technical details. Nowadays, frames are used in sampling
[
3
]
, iterated function system
[
26
]
, distributed signal processing
[
7
]
, operator theory
[
2
,
5
,
19
,
21
]
, application of wavelets
[
13
]
. We refer to texts
[

,
12
,

,
30
]
for basic theory of frames.

G
\check{a}
vruta in
[

introduced the notion of

-frames, where

is a linear bounded operator acting on the underlying Hilbert space

.

Definition 1.1
.

[

Let

(1.2)

\displaystyle a_{o}\|K^{*}\varphi\|^{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\langle\varphi_{k},\varphi\rangle|^{2}\leq b_{o}\|\varphi\|^{2}\ \text{for all}\ \varphi\in\mathcal{H}.

The numbers

-frame bounds

(K)
of

is a Bessel sequence, so its frame operator is well defined. But, in general, it is not invertible on

(K)
of

, whenever the Ran
(K)
is closed. In
[

, G
\check{a}
vruta characterized

\check{a}
vruta in
[

characterized atomic systems in terms of

-frames in separable Hilbert spaces. She also observed many differences between

-frames gives stable analysis and reconstruction of functions from a subspace, e.g., range of operators. Xiao, Zhu, and G
\check{a}
vruta

,

Frames in matrix-valued signal spaces have potential applications in signal processing as most of the application areas involve matrix-valued signals. Xia and Suter in

Notable contribution in this work include frame preserving mapping in terms of hyponormal operators, existence of tight matrix-valued Gabor frames over LCA groups for hyponormal operators. A characterization of matrix-valued Gabor frames over LCA groups and new stability results for matrix-valued Gabor frames over LCA groups under small perturbation.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, symbol
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{C}
denote the set of integers and complex numbers, respectively.
\mathbb{T}
denote the unit circle group. Let
G
be a second countable locally compact abelian group equipped with the Hausdorff topology. We recall that a character on
G
is the map
\gamma

G
into itself which satisfies
\gamma(x+y)=\gamma(x)\gamma(y)
for all
x,y\in G
. The
dual group
of
G
, denoted by
\hat{G}
, is the collection of all continuous characters on
G
which forms a locally compact abelian group under the operation defined by
(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})(x):=\gamma(x)\gamma^{\prime}(x)
, where
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\hat{G}
and
x\in G
and an appropriate topology. It is well known that on a LCA group
G
there exists a Haar measure which is unique upto a positive scalar multiple, see
[
9
]
for details.
The symbols
\mu_{G}
and
\mu_{\hat{G}}
denote the Haar measure on
G
and
\hat{G}
, respectively. A
lattice
of
G
is a discrete subgroup
\Lambda
of
G
for which
G/\Lambda
is compact. The annihilator of
\Lambda
, denoted by
\Lambda^{\perp}
, is defined by
\Lambda^{\perp}=\{\gamma\in\hat{G}\ |\ \gamma(x)=1,\ x\in\Lambda\}
. Note that
\Lambda^{\perp}
is a lattice in
\hat{G}
. The
fundamental domain
associated with the lattice
\Lambda^{\perp}
of
\hat{G}
, denoted by
V
, is a Borel measurable relatively compact set in
\hat{G}
such that
\hat{G}=\cup_{w\in\Lambda^{\perp}}(w+V),\ (w+V)\cap(w^{\prime}+V)=\emptyset
for
w\neq w^{\prime},w,w^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{\perp}
. The collection of all continuous automorphisms on
G
is denoted by Aut
G
. As is standard
L^{2}(G)
denote the space of measurable square integrable functions over
G
. The
Fourier transform
of a function
f
in
L^{1}\bigcap L^{2}(G)
is defined as

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\gamma)=\int_{G}f(x)\overline{\gamma(x)}d\mu_{G}(x),\ \ \gamma\in\widehat{G}

Note that the Fourier transform can be extended isometrically to
L^{2}(G)
, see
[
9
]
.

2.1.
The Space
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})

Throughout the paper, the matrix-valued functions are denoted by bold letters. Let
n
be a positive integer. The space of matrix-valued functions over
G
, denoted by
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
, is defined as

\displaystyle L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}):=\Big\{\mathbf{f}=\big[f_{ij}\big]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}:f_{ij}\in L^{2}(G)\ (1\leq i,\ j\leq n)\Big\},

where
\big[f_{ij}\big]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
is matrix of order
n
with entries
f_{ij}
. The functions
f_{ij}
are called
components
or
atoms
of
\mathbf{f}
. The Frobenius norm on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{f}\|=\Big(\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{n}\int_{G}|f_{ij}|^{2}d\mu_{G}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It is easy to see that
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
is a Banach space with respect to the Frobenius norm given in (
2.1
).

The integral of a function

\mathbf{f}=\big[f_{ij}\big]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
is defined as

\displaystyle\int_{G}\mathbf{f}d\mu_{G}=\left[\int_{G}f_{ij}d\mu_{G}\right]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

For
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
, the matrix-valued inner product is defined as

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\rangle=\int_{G}\mathbf{f}(x)\mathbf{g}^{*}(x)d\mu_{G}.

Here,
*
denotes the transpose and the complex conjugate. One may observe that the matrix-valued inner product given in (
2.2
) is not an inner product in usual sense. Further, a bounded linear operator on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
may not be adjointable with respect to the matrix-valued product given in (
2.2
).

Let tr
A
denotes trace of the matrix
A
. The space
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
becomes a Hilbert space with respect to the inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{o}
defined by

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\rangle_{o}=\text{tr}\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\rangle,\ \ \mathbf{f},\ \mathbf{g}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}),

and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{o}
generates the Frobenius norm:

||\mathbf{f}||^{2}=\langle\mathbf{f},\mathbf{f}\rangle_{o},\ \mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
.

Definition 2.1
.

A bounded linear operator
U
on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
is said to be
hyponormal
if
\text{tr}\langle UU^{*}\mathbf{f},\mathbf{f}\rangle\leq\text{tr}\langle U^{*}U\mathbf{f},\mathbf{f}\rangle
, for all

\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. That is,
\|U^{*}\mathbf{f}\|\leq\|U\mathbf{f}\|
for all
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
.

For fundamental properties of hyponormal operators, we refer to
[
24
]
.

2.2.
Matrix-Valued Gabor Frames in
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})

Let
\Lambda_{0}
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{N}
,
B\in
Aut
G
,
C\in
Aut
\widehat{G}
,
\Lambda
be a lattice in
G
and
\Lambda^{\prime}
a lattice in
\widehat{G}
.

Write

\displaystyle\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}}:=\{\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\subset L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}),

\displaystyle\texttt{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}}):=\{E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\subset L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}).

For
a\in G
and
\eta\in\hat{G}
, we consider following operators on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
.

\displaystyle T_{a}\mathbf{f}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}(xa^{-1})\quad(\text{Translation operator}),

\displaystyle E_{\eta}\mathbf{f}(x)

\displaystyle=\eta(x)\mathbf{f}(x)\quad(\text{Modulation operator}).

For
l\in\Lambda_{0}
, let
\Phi_{l}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
be given by
\Phi_{l}(x)=\begin{bmatrix}\phi_{ij}^{(l)}(x)\end{bmatrix}_{n\times n}
. Let
B\in
Aut
G
and
C\in
Aut
\widehat{G}
. A collection of the form

\displaystyle\texttt{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}}):=\{E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}

is called the
matrix-valued Gabor system
in the space
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
over LCA group
G
. The functions
\Phi_{l}
are called the
matrix-valued Gabor window functions
.

Definition 2.2
.

A frame of the form
\texttt{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}})
for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
is called a
matrix-valued Gabor frame
. That is, the inequality (
frame inequality
)

\displaystyle a_{o}\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\Big\|\Big\langle E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l},\mathbf{f}\Big\rangle\Big\|^{2}\leq b_{o}\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2},\ \mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}),

holds for some positive scalars
a_{o}
and
b_{o}
. As in case of ordinary frames,
a_{o}
and
b_{o}
are called frame bounds.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be the complex vector space of all
n\times n
complex matrices. The space

\displaystyle\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{0}\times\Lambda\times\Lambda^{\prime},\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))

\displaystyle:=\Big\{\{M_{l,j,k}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},j\in\Lambda,k\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}):\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{j\in\Lambda,k\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|M_{l,j,k}\|^{2}<\infty\Big\}

is a Hilbert space and its related norm is given by

\displaystyle\|\{M_{l,j,k}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},j\in\Lambda,k\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|=\Big(\sum\limits_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{j\in\Lambda,k\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|M_{l,j,k}\|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

If
\texttt{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}})
is a frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
, then the map

\displaystyle V:\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{0}\times\Lambda\times\Lambda^{\prime},\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))\rightarrow L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})\ \text{defined by}

\displaystyle V:\{M_{l,k,m}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\mapsto\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}M_{l,k,m}E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}

is called the
synthesis operator
(or the
pre-frame operator
), associated with
\texttt{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}})
.
The
analysis

operator
is the map

\displaystyle W:L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{0}\times\Lambda\times\Lambda^{\prime},\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))\ \text{given by}

\displaystyle W:\mathbf{f}\mapsto\Big\{\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\Big\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}.

The frame operator of
\texttt{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}})
is the composition
S=VW
on the space
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
which is given by

\displaystyle S:\mathbf{f}\mapsto\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l},

\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. The frame operator is bounded, linear and invertible on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. We refer to
[
4
,
12
]
for basic theory of Gabor frames.

The following example will be used in illustration of results.

Example 2.3
.

[
15
, Example 3.1]

Let
G
be the torus group. Its dual group is
\widehat{G}=\mathbb{Z}
. Fix a lattice
\Lambda=\Big\{0,\frac{1}{8},\dots,\frac{7}{8}\Big\}
. Then
\Lambda^{\perp}=8\mathbb{Z}
with fundamental domain
V=\mathbb{Z}_{8}=\{0,1,\dots,7\}
. Let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
be such that

\displaystyle\widehat{\phi_{1}}(\gamma)=\chi_{\mathbb{Z}_{8}}(\gamma)\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\phi_{2}}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{2}\chi_{\mathbb{Z}_{8}}(\gamma)\ \text{in}\ L^{2}(\mathbb{Z})\ \text{for}\ \gamma\in\mathbb{Z}.

For
B\in
Aut
G
and
C\in
Aut
\widehat{G}
, consider the Gabor system
\{E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\phi_{1}\}_{k\in\Lambda\atop m\in\Lambda^{\perp}}

=\{E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{1}\}_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
. Set
\phi_{m}^{(1)}(\xi)=E_{8m}\phi_{1}(\xi)
,

,
\xi\in[0,1[
. Since
E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{1}(\xi)=T_{k}E_{8m}\phi_{1}(\xi)
, thus by taking

\Lambda_{m}:=\Lambda
, one can write
\{T_{k}\phi_{m}^{(1)}\}_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}

=\{E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{1}\}_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

Define

\displaystyle G_{0}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mu_{\widehat{G}}(V)\Big|\widehat{\phi^{(1)}_{m}}(\gamma)\Big|^{2},\ \gamma\in\mathbb{Z},

and

\displaystyle G_{1}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\mu_{\widehat{G}}(V)\sum_{w\in\Lambda^{\perp}\backslash\{0\}}|\widehat{\phi^{(1)}_{m}}(\gamma)\widehat{\phi^{(1)}_{m}}(\gamma+w)|,\ \gamma\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then, using

\displaystyle G_{0}(\gamma)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}8|\chi_{\mathbb{Z}_{8}}(\gamma-8m)|^{2}=8\ \text{for}\ \gamma\in\mathbb{Z},

and

\displaystyle G_{1}(\gamma)=\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}8\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}}|\chi_{\mathbb{Z}_{8}}(\gamma-8m)\chi_{\mathbb{Z}_{8}}(\gamma+8a-8m)|^{2}=0\ \text{for}\ \gamma\in\mathbb{Z}.

Therefore, by
[

, the Gabor system

is a

-tight frame for

. Similarly,
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{2}\}_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
2
-tight frame for
L^{2}(G)
.

3.
Matrix-Valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor Frames

We begin this section with the definition of a matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame in the matrix-valued function space
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Theta
be a bounded linear operator acting on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. A countable family of vectors
\mathcal{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}}):=\{E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
in
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
is called a
matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame
for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
if for all
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\alpha_{o}\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\Big\|\Big\langle E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l},\mathbf{f}\Big\rangle\Big\|^{2}\leq\beta_{o}\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|^{2}

holds for some positive scalars
\alpha_{o}
and
\beta_{o}
.

The positive scalars
\alpha_{o}
and
\beta_{o}
are called
lower
and
upper frame bounds
of the
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame
\mathcal{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}})
.
If
\alpha_{o}=\beta_{o}
, then we say that
\mathcal{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}})
is a
\alpha_{o}
-
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-tight matrix-valued Gabor frame for

L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
.

Remark 3.2
.

If
\Theta
is the identity operator on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
, then a matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
is the standard matrix-valued Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. However, if
\Theta
is a non-identity operator on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
, then a matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
need not be the standard matrix-valued Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. For example, consider the tight Gabor frames
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{l}\}_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}},(l=1,2)
for
L^{2}(G)
given in Example
2.3
.
Let
\Phi_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\phi_{1}\\
0&\phi_{1}\end{bmatrix},\Phi_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\phi_{2}\\
0&\phi_{2}\end{bmatrix}
. Then,
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
.
For any
\mathbf{f}=\begin{bmatrix}f_{11}&f_{12}\\
f_{21}&f_{22}\end{bmatrix}

in
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\{1,2\}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|\Big\langle E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l},\mathbf{f}\Big\rangle\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\{1,2\}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}2\Big(\big|\int_{G}E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{l}\overline{f_{12}}d\mu_{G}\big|^{2}+\big|\int_{G}E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{l}\overline{f_{22}}d\mu_{G}\big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=20\Big(\|f_{12}\|^{2}+\|f_{22}\|^{2}\Big).

Therefore, for
\mathbf{f}_{o}=\begin{bmatrix}f&0\\
f&0\end{bmatrix}
, where
0\neq f\in L^{2}(G)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\{1,2\}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|\Big\langle E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l},\mathbf{f}_{o}\Big\rangle\Big\|^{2}=0.

Thus,
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is not a matrix-valued Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
. But the family
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
(\Theta_{o},\Theta_{o}^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
, where
\Theta_{o}
is a bounded linear operator on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
given by

\displaystyle\Theta_{o}:\mathbf{f}\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}0&f_{12}\\
0&f_{22}\end{bmatrix},\ \mathbf{f}=\begin{bmatrix}f_{11}&f_{12}\\
f_{21}&f_{22}\end{bmatrix}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}).

It is easy to see that
\Theta_{o}^{*}=\Theta_{o}
.
Therefore, for any
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
, we have

\displaystyle 20\|\Theta_{o}^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}=\sum_{l\in\{1,2\}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|\Big\langle E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l},\mathbf{f}\Big\rangle\Big\|^{2}=20\|\Theta_{o}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}.

Hence,
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}

is a matrix-valued
(\Theta_{o},\Theta_{o}^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
.

Remark 3.3
.

It is mentioned in
[
15
]
that a matrix-valued Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
is always a
\Theta
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
where
\Theta
is a bounded linear operator on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. However, this is not true in the case of

(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-matrix-valued Gabor frame. Precisely, a matrix-valued Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
need not be a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. For example, let
G
be the torus group and
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{l}\}_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}

(l=1,2)
be the tight Gabor frames for
L^{2}(G)
given in Example
2.3
. Let
\Phi_{1}
,
\Phi_{2}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
be given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\phi_{1}\\
\phi_{2}&0\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\phi_{2}\\
\phi_{1}&0\end{bmatrix}.

Then,
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
10
-tight matrix-valued Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
.
Define

\Theta:L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})\rightarrow L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
by

\displaystyle\Theta\colon\mathbf{f}\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}f_{11}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},\ \mathbf{f}=\begin{bmatrix}f_{11}&f_{12}\\
f_{21}&f_{22}\end{bmatrix}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}).

Then,
\Theta
is a bounded linear operator. If possible, let
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
- Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
with bounds
a,b
. Then, for
\mathbf{f}_{o}=\begin{bmatrix}0&f\\
f&f\\
\end{bmatrix}
, where
0\neq f\in L^{2}(G)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\{1,2\}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|\Big\langle E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l},\mathbf{f}_{o}\Big\rangle\Big\|^{2}=10\|\mathbf{f}_{o}\|^{2}=30\|f\|^{2}>0=b\|\Theta\mathbf{f}_{o}\|^{2},

which is a contradiction. Hence,
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is not a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
- Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
.

Now, we show that a matrix-valued Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
becomes a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
provided
\Theta
is bounded below.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\{E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
be a matrix-valued Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. Let
\Theta
be a bounded linear operator acting on the space
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
which is bounded below. Then, the collection
\{E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
is a matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma
and
\delta
be frame bounds of
\{E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
. Let
\Theta
be bounded below by a constant
\alpha
, that is,
\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|\geq\alpha\|\mathbf{f}\|
for all
\mathbf{f}
in
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
.
Then, for any
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{\|\Theta^{*}\|^{2}}\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}\leq\gamma\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l},\mathbf{f}\rangle\|^{2},

and

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l},\mathbf{f}\rangle\|^{2}\leq\delta\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}\leq\frac{\delta}{\alpha^{2}}\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|^{2}.

Thus,
\{E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
is a matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for the space

L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
with frame bounds
\frac{\gamma}{\|\Theta^{*}\|^{2}}
and
\frac{\delta}{\alpha^{2}}
.
∎

Now, we discuss relations between hyponormal operators on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
and matrix-valued
\lambda_{o}
-
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-tight frames for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. By Definition
3.1
, one may observe that a bounded linear operator
\Theta
on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
is hyponormal if there exists a matrix-valued
\lambda_{o}
-
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-tight frame for the space
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. Indeed, if
\{\mathbf{f}_{k}\}_{k\in I}
is a matrix-valued
\lambda_{o}
-
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-tight frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
,
then by Definition
3.1
, we have
\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|\leq\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|
, for all
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. Hence,
\Theta
is a hyponormal operator on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
.

In order to see the other way round relationship, we first discuss some frame preserving properties of
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-frames in
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. The following result says that the image of a frame in
L^{2}(G)
under a hyponormal operator
\Theta
is a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-frame for
L^{2}(G)
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\{E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
be a Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G)
and let
\Theta
be a hyponormal operator on
L^{2}(G)
. Then,

\{\Theta E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda\atop m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
is a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-frame for
L^{2}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda
and
\mu
be lower and upper frame bounds of
\{E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda\atop m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
. Then, using the hyponormality of
\Theta
, for any
f\in L^{2}(G)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}|\langle\Theta E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\phi_{l},f\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}|\langle E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\phi_{l},\Theta^{*}f\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mu\|\Theta^{*}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mu\|\Theta f\|^{2}.

Also

\displaystyle\lambda\|\Theta^{*}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}|\langle E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\phi_{l},\Theta^{*}f\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}|\langle\Theta E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\phi_{l},f\rangle|^{2}

for all
f\in L^{2}(G)
. Hence,
\{\Theta E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
is a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-frame for
L^{2}(G)
with frame bounds
\lambda
and
\mu
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Proposition
3.5
is not true for matrix-valued frames in matrix-valued signal spaces
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. This problem is related to adjointable operators on matrix-valued signal spaces with respect to matrix-valued inner product on the underlying space. For example, consider the tight Gabor frames
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{l}\}_{k\in\Lambda\atop m\in\mathbb{Z}}

(l=1,2)
for
L^{2}(G)
given in Example
2.3
. Let
\Phi_{1}
,
\Phi_{2}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
be given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\phi_{1}\\
\phi_{2}&0\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\phi_{2}\\
\phi_{1}&0\end{bmatrix}.

Then,
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
10
-tight matrix-valued Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
.

Define

\Theta:L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})\rightarrow L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
by

\displaystyle\Theta\colon\mathbf{f}\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}f_{11}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix},\ \mathbf{f}=\begin{bmatrix}f_{11}&f_{12}\\
f_{21}&f_{22}\end{bmatrix}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}).

Then,
\Theta
is a bounded linear operator with adjoint
\Theta^{*}=\Theta
and hence a hyponormal operator. But
\Theta
is not adjointable with respect to matrix-valued inner product on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
. That is,
\langle\Theta\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\rangle\neq\langle\mathbf{f},\Theta^{*}\mathbf{g}\rangle
for all
\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}
in
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
. Furthermore,
\Theta E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}=\mathbf{O}
for
l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence,
\{\Theta E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is not a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
.

The following result gives sufficient conditions on matrix-valued
\Theta
-frame preserving transformations acting on matrix-valued signal spaces in terms of adjointability of
\Theta
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\{E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
be a matrix-valued frame for the space
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
with frame bounds
\gamma
and
\delta
. Let
\Theta
be a hyponormal operator acting on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
which is adjointable with respect to the matrix-valued inner product. Then,
\{\Theta E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
is a matrix-valued

(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
with frame bounds
\gamma
and
\delta
.

Proof.

For any
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
, we have

\displaystyle\gamma\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l},\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\Theta E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l},\mathbf{f}\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l},\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|^{2}.

Thus,
\{\Theta E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
is a matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-frame for the space
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
with the desired frame bounds.
∎

Now, we have enough knowledge to discuss the conditions on an operator
\Theta
acting on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
such that the existence of
\lambda_{o}
-
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-tight frames for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
is guaranteed. We give the following result regarding this.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\Theta
be a hyponormal operator on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. If
\Theta
is adjointable with respect to the matrix-valued inner product, then there exists a matrix-valued
\lambda_{o}
-
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-tight frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
for every positive real number
\lambda_{o}
.

Proof.

Let
\{E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
be a Parseval frame for
L^{2}(G)
. For each
l\in\Lambda_{0}
, define the matrix-valued function
\Phi_{l}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
as

\displaystyle\Phi_{l}=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{\lambda_{o}}\ \phi_{l}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\sqrt{\lambda_{o}}\ \phi_{l}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\sqrt{\lambda_{o}}\ \phi_{l}\end{bmatrix}.

Then

\displaystyle E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}=\tiny{\begin{bmatrix}E_{Cm}T_{Bk}(\sqrt{\lambda_{o}}\ \phi_{l})&0&\cdots&0\\
0&E_{Cm}T_{Bk}(\sqrt{\lambda_{o}}\ \phi_{l})&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&E_{Cm}T_{Bk}(\sqrt{\lambda_{o}}\ \phi_{l})\end{bmatrix}}.

Therefore, for any
\mathbf{f}=\begin{bmatrix}f_{11}&f_{12}&\cdots&f_{1n}\\
f_{21}&f_{22}&\cdots&f_{2n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
f_{n1}&f_{n2}&\cdots&f_{nn}\end{bmatrix}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda\atop m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\Big\|\begin{bmatrix}\langle f_{11},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}(\sqrt{\lambda_{o}}\ \phi_{l})\rangle&\cdots&\langle f_{1n},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}(\sqrt{\lambda_{o}}\ \phi_{l})\rangle\\
\langle f_{21},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}(\sqrt{\lambda_{o}}\ \phi_{l})\rangle&\cdots&\langle f_{2n},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}(\sqrt{\lambda_{o}}\ \phi_{l})\rangle\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\langle f_{n1},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}(\sqrt{\lambda_{o}}\ \phi_{l})\rangle&\cdots&\langle f_{nn},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}(\sqrt{\lambda_{o}}\ \phi_{l})\rangle\end{bmatrix}\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{o}\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}|\langle f_{ij},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\ \phi_{l}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{o}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\|f_{ij}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{o}\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}.

Hence,
\{E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
is a matrix-valued
\lambda_{o}
-tight Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. Further, by Proposition
3.7
,
\{\Theta E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
is a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
with
\lambda_{o}
as lower and upper frame bounds.
Hence, the existence of a matrix-valued
\lambda_{o}
-
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-tight frame for the space
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
is proved.
∎

We illustrate Theorem
3.8
by giving the following example regarding the existence of
\lambda_{o}
-
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-tight frames for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{3\times 3})
.

Example 3.9
.

Let
G=\mathbb{R}
be the additive group of real numbers. The characters on
\mathbb{R}
are the functions
\eta_{y}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
defined by

\displaystyle\eta_{y}(x)=\textit{e}^{2\pi\textit{i}yx},\ x\in\mathbb{R}

for fixed
y\in\mathbb{R}
. That is, the dual group
\widehat{G}
can be identified with
\mathbb{R}
, see
[
9
]
for technical details. Consider the lattice
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}
and
\Lambda^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}
.
Then, for
\phi=\chi_{[0,1]}
, the Gabor system
\{E_{m}T_{k}\phi\}_{m,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, see
[
4
, p. 96]
for details.

Define a matrix-valued function
\Phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{3\times 3})
as

\displaystyle\Phi=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{3}\ \phi&0&0\\
0&\sqrt{3}\ \phi&0\\
0&0&\sqrt{3}\ \phi\end{bmatrix}.

Then, for any
\mathbf{f}=\big[f_{i,j}\big]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{3\times 3})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m,k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{m}T_{k}\Phi\rangle\|^{2}=3\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2},

which implies that
\{E_{m}T_{k}\Phi\}_{m,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a matrix-valued
3
-tight Gabor frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{3\times 3})
.
Define
\Theta:L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{3\times 3})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{3\times 3})
by

\displaystyle\Theta\colon\mathbf{f}\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}f_{11}&0&f_{13}\\
f_{21}&0&f_{23}\\
f_{31}&0&f_{33}\end{bmatrix},\ \mathbf{f}=\begin{bmatrix}f_{11}&f_{12}&f_{13}\\
f_{21}&f_{22}&f_{23}\\
f_{31}&f_{32}&f_{33}\end{bmatrix}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{3\times 3}).

Then,
\Theta
is a bounded linear operator with adjoint
\Theta^{*}=\Theta
. Also,
\Theta
is adjointable with respect to matrix-valued inner product on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{3\times 3})
. That is,
\langle\Theta\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{f},\Theta^{*}\mathbf{g}\rangle,\ \mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{3\times 3})
.
Then, by Proposition
3.7
, the matrix-valued system
\{\Theta E_{m}T_{k}\Phi\}_{m,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a matrix-valued 3-
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-tight frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{3\times 3})
.

Remark 3.10
.

In Theorem
3.8
, the condition of adjointability of
\Theta
with respect to matrix-valued inner product is not a necessary condition.

Next, we discuss frame properties of the image of a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame in
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
under a bounded linear operator
\Xi
. It is proved in
[
15
, Proposition 4.2]
that the image of a
\Theta
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
under an operator
\Xi\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}))
becomes a
\Xi\Theta
-frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
provided
\Xi
is adjointable with respect to matrix-valued inner product. But, this is not true for the case of
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frames in
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. That is, if
\mathcal{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}})
is a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
and
\Xi\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}))
is adjointable with respect to matrix-valued inner product, then
\Xi(\mathcal{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}}))
may not be a
(\Xi\Theta,(\Xi\Theta)^{*})
-frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. This is justified in the following example.

Example 3.11
.

Consider tight Gabor frames
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{1}\}_{k\in\Lambda\atop m\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{2}\}_{k\in\Lambda\atop m\in\mathbb{Z}}
for
L^{2}(G)
given in Example
2.3
. Define

\Theta:L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})\rightarrow L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
by

\displaystyle\Theta\colon\mathbf{f}\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}f_{22}&f_{21}\\
f_{12}&f_{11}\end{bmatrix},\ \mathbf{f}=\begin{bmatrix}f_{11}&f_{12}\\
f_{21}&f_{22}\end{bmatrix}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}).

Then,
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a matrix-valued
10
-tight
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
. In fact, for any
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
, we have

\displaystyle 10\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=10\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{l\in\{1,2\}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 10\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}=10\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|^{2}.

Define

\Xi:L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})\rightarrow L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
by

\displaystyle\Xi\colon\mathbf{f}\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}0&f_{12}\\
0&f_{22}\end{bmatrix},\ \mathbf{f}=\begin{bmatrix}f_{11}&f_{12}\\
f_{21}&f_{22}\end{bmatrix}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}).

Then,
\Xi
is a bounded linear operator with adjoint
\Xi^{*}=\Xi
. Also,
\Xi
is adjointable with respect to matrix-valued inner product on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
. That is,
\langle\Xi\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{f},\Xi^{*}\mathbf{g}\rangle,\ \mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
. However,
\{\Xi E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda\atop m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is not a
(\Xi\Theta,(\Xi\Theta)^{*})
-frame. If possible, let
\{\Xi E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a
(\Xi\Theta,(\Xi\Theta)^{*})
-frame with bounds
\gamma,\delta
. Then, for
\mathbf{f}_{o}=\begin{bmatrix}0&f\\
0&f\\
\end{bmatrix}
, where
f
is a non-zero function in
L^{2}(G)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\{1,2\}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|\Big\langle\Xi E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l},\mathbf{f}_{o}\Big\rangle\Big\|^{2}=20\|f\|^{2}>0=\delta\|\Xi\Theta\mathbf{f}_{o}\|^{2},

which is a contradiction.

In the following result, we give some additional conditions on
\Xi
so that
\Xi(\mathcal{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}}))
becomes a
(\Xi\Theta,(\Xi\Theta)^{*})
-frame. This result can be seen as a generalization of Proposition
3.7
.

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
\{E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
be a matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
with frame bounds
\gamma
and
\delta
. Suppose

(i)

\Xi\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}))
is adjointable with respect to matrix-valued inner product.

(ii)

\Xi
is hyponormal on Ran
(\Theta)
such that
\Theta\Xi^{*}=\Xi^{*}\Theta
.

Then,
\{\Xi E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
is a
(\Xi\Theta,(\Xi\Theta)^{*})
-frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
with the same frame bounds.

Proof.

For any
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\Big\|\Big\langle\Xi E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l},\mathbf{f}\Big\rangle\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\Big\|\Big\langle E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l},\Xi^{*}\mathbf{f}\Big\rangle\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\|\Theta\Xi^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\delta\|\Xi^{*}\Theta\mathbf{f}\|^{2}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leq\delta\|\Xi\Theta\mathbf{f}\|^{2}.

Similarly

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\Big\|\Big\langle\Xi E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l},\mathbf{f}\Big\rangle\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\Big\|\Big\langle E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l},\Xi^{*}\mathbf{f}\Big\rangle\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\gamma\|\Theta^{*}\Xi^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\gamma\|(\Xi\Theta)^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}\ \mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}).

By
(\ref{e3})
and
(\ref{e4})
, we conclude that
\{\Xi E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
is a matrix-valued
(\Xi\Theta,(\Xi\Theta)^{*})
-frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
with frame bounds
\gamma
and
\delta
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

The condition that the operator
\Theta
commutes with
\Xi^{*}
in Theorem
3.12
cannot be relaxed. Consider the operators
\Theta
,
\Xi
defined on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
and the system
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
which is a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
given in Example
3.11
. As mentioned in Example
3.11
, the operator
\Xi
is adjointable with respect to matrix-valued inner product, and
\Xi
is hyponormal on Ran
(\Theta)
since
\Xi^{*}=\Xi
. But,
\Theta\Xi^{*}\neq\Xi^{*}\Theta
. In fact, for any
\mathbf{f}=\begin{bmatrix}f_{11}&f_{12}\\
f_{21}&f_{22}\end{bmatrix}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
, we have

\displaystyle\Theta\Xi^{*}\mathbf{f}=\Theta\Xi\mathbf{f}=\begin{bmatrix}f_{22}&0\\
f_{12}&0\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\Xi^{*}\Theta\mathbf{f}=\Xi\Theta\mathbf{f}=\begin{bmatrix}0&f_{21}\\
0&f_{11}\end{bmatrix}.

Therefore, the system
\{\Xi E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
not being a
(\Xi\Theta,(\Xi\Theta)^{*})
-frame, details in Example
3.11
, supports our argument.

Next, we give a characterization for matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frames in
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. This is inspired by a fundamental result due to Gǎvruta in
[
11
, Theorem 4]
for ordinary
K
-frames in separable Hilbert spaces. This is also related with the concept of atomic systems in Hilbert spaces. The matrix-valued atomic system in matrix-valued function spaces can be studied in terms of
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frames. In the following result, if
\mathcal{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}})
is a Parseval
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame of
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
, then
\Theta
turns out to be a hyponormal operator on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
\Theta
be a bounded linear operator acting on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. A matrix-valued Gabor system
\mathcal{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}})
is a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for the space
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
if and only if there exists a bounded linear operator
\Omega
from
\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{0}\times\Lambda\times\Lambda^{\prime},\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
into
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
such that

(i)

E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}=\Omega\chi_{l,k,m},\ l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}
, where
\{\chi_{l,k,m}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda\atop m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}
is an orthonormal basis of
\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{0}\times\Lambda\times\Lambda^{\prime},\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
,

(ii)

there exist finite positive numbers
\alpha
and
\beta
satisfying

\displaystyle\alpha\ \text{tr}\langle\Theta\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f},\mathbf{f}\rangle\leq\text{tr}\langle\Omega\Omega^{*}\mathbf{f},\mathbf{f}\rangle\leq\beta\ \text{tr}\langle\Theta^{*}\Theta\mathbf{f},\mathbf{f}\rangle,\ \mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}).

Proof.

Suppose first that
\mathcal{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}})
is a matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for the space
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
with frame bounds
a_{o}
,
b_{o}
.

Define
\Xi:L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\Lambda_{0}\times\Lambda\times\Lambda^{\prime},\mathcal{M}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
by

\displaystyle\Xi(\mathbf{f})=\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\chi_{l,k,m},\ \mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}).

Then,
\Xi
is a bounded linear operator and
\|\Xi\|\leq\sqrt{b_{o}}\|\Theta\|
.

Now, for any
l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\text{tr}\langle\chi_{l,k,m},\Xi\mathbf{f}\rangle

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\Big\langle\chi_{l,k,m},\sum_{l^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\Lambda,m^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm^{\prime}}T_{Bk^{\prime}}\Phi_{l^{\prime}}\rangle\chi_{l^{\prime},k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle^{*}

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\langle E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l},\mathbf{f}\rangle\ \text{for all}\ \mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}).

Thus,
\Xi^{*}\chi_{l,k,m}=E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}
, for all
l\in\Lambda_{0}
,
k\in\Lambda
and
m\in\Lambda^{\prime}
. If we take
\Omega=\Xi^{*}
, then we obtain (
i
). To prove (
ii
), let

\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
be arbitrary. Then,

\displaystyle a_{o}\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\Big\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\Big\|^{2}=\|\Xi\mathbf{f}\|^{2},

and

\displaystyle\|\Xi\mathbf{f}\|^{2}=\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\Big\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\Big\|^{2}\leq b_{o}\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|^{2}.

This imply that

\displaystyle a_{o}\text{tr}\langle\Theta\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f},\mathbf{f}\rangle\leq\text{tr}\langle\Xi^{*}\Xi\mathbf{f},\mathbf{f}\rangle=\text{tr}\langle\Omega\Omega^{*}\mathbf{f},\mathbf{f}\rangle\leq b_{o}\text{tr}\langle\Theta^{*}\Theta\mathbf{f},\mathbf{f}\rangle.

This gives (
ii
), where
\alpha=a_{o}
and
\beta=b_{o}
.

To prove the converse, assume that conditions (
i
) and (
ii
) hold. Then, using condition (
i
), for any
l\in\Lambda_{0},k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}
and any
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
, we have

\displaystyle\text{tr}\langle\chi_{l,k,m},\Omega^{*}\mathbf{f}\rangle

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\langle\Omega\chi_{l,k,m},\mathbf{f}\rangle

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\langle E_{C_{m}}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l},\mathbf{f}\rangle

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle^{*}

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\Big\langle\chi_{l,k,m},\sum_{l^{\prime}\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\Lambda,m^{\prime}\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm^{\prime}}T_{Bk^{\prime}}\Phi_{l^{\prime}}\rangle\chi_{l^{\prime},k^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\Big\rangle,

which entails

\displaystyle\Omega^{*}{\mathbf{f}}=\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\chi_{l,k,m},\ \mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}).

Therefore

\displaystyle\|\Omega^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}=\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\Big\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\Big\|^{2},\ \mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}).

Using condition (
ii
), for all
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}\leq\|\Omega^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}=\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\Big\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\Big\|^{2}\leq\beta\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|^{2}

Hence,
\mathcal{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}})
is a matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Perturbation of
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor Frames

In this section, we show that matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frames are stable under small perturbation. Perturbation theory plays a significant role in both pure mathematics and applied science, see e.g.
[
22
]
. For applications of perturbation theory for frames in various directions, we refer to
[
14
]
. The following result shows that multivariate
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frames in matrix-valued signal spaces are stable under small perturbations.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}})
be a matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
with frame bounds
\gamma_{o}
,
\delta_{o}
, and let
\{\widetilde{\Phi_{l}}\}_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\subset L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. Assume that

(i)

\Theta^{*}
be bounded below by
m_{o}
.

(ii)

\lambda
,
\mu
,
\eta\geq 0
be such that
\frac{(1-2\lambda)\gamma_{o}-2\mu}{2\eta}>\frac{\|\Theta\|^{2}}{m_{o}^{2}}
.

(iii)

For all
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
,

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}(\Phi_{l}-\widetilde{\Phi_{l}})\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}

(4.1)

\displaystyle+\mu\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}+\eta\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|^{2}.

Then,
\mathcal{G}(C,B,\widetilde{\Phi}_{\Lambda_{0}})
is a matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
with frame bounds

\displaystyle\left(\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda\Big)\gamma_{o}-\mu-\frac{\eta\|\Theta\|^{2}}{m_{o}^{2}}\right)\ \text{and}\ 2\left(\Big(1+\lambda+\frac{\mu}{\gamma_{o}}\Big)\delta_{o}+\eta\right).

Proof.

By hypothesis (
iii
), for any
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\widetilde{\Phi_{l}}\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}-E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\widetilde{\Phi_{l}}\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(2\lambda+2)\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\mu\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}+2\eta\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(2\lambda+2)\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\mu}{\gamma_{o}}\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}+2\eta\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|^{2}.

Therefore

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\widetilde{\Phi_{l}}\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\Big(1+\lambda+\frac{\mu}{\gamma_{o}}\Big)\delta_{o}+\eta\right)\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|^{2},

for all
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}-E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\widetilde{\Phi_{l}}\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\widetilde{\Phi_{l}}\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}++2\mu\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}+2\eta\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\widetilde{\Phi_{l}}\rangle\|^{2},

which entails

\displaystyle 2\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\widetilde{\Phi_{l}}\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-2\lambda)\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}-2\mu\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}-2\eta\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-2\lambda)\gamma_{o}\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}-2\mu\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}-2\eta\|\Theta\|^{2}\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(1-2\lambda)\gamma_{o}\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}-2\mu\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}-\frac{2\eta\|\Theta\|^{2}}{m_{o}^{2}}\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}\quad\big(\text{using hypothesis}\ \eqref{percon1}\big)

That is

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\widetilde{\Phi_{l}}\rangle\|^{2}\geq\left(\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\lambda\Big)\gamma_{o}-\mu-\frac{\eta\|\Theta\|^{2}}{m_{o}^{2}}\right)\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}

for all
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
. From (
4.2
) and (
4
), we conclude that

\mathcal{G}(C,B,\widetilde{\Phi}_{\Lambda_{0}})
is a frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
with the desired frame bounds.
∎

Next is an applicative example of Theorem
4.1
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
be the
10
-tight matrix-valued Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
given in Remark
3.3
. Define
\Theta
on
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
by

\displaystyle\Theta:\mathbf{f}\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}2f_{22}&f_{21}\\
f_{12}&f_{11}\end{bmatrix},\ \mathbf{f}=\begin{bmatrix}f_{11}&f_{12}\\
f_{21}&f_{22}\end{bmatrix}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}).

Then,
\Theta
is a bounded linear operator satisfying
\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|\leq 2\|\mathbf{f}\|
, for all
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
. In fact, we have
\|\Theta\|=2
. For any
\mathbf{f}
,
\mathbf{g}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
, we have

\displaystyle\text{tr}\langle\Theta\mathbf{f},\mathbf{g}\rangle

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\int_{G}\begin{bmatrix}2f_{22}&f_{21}\\
f_{12}&f_{11}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\overline{g_{11}}&\overline{g_{21}}\\
\overline{g_{12}}&\overline{g_{22}}\end{bmatrix}d\mu_{G}

\displaystyle=\text{tr}\int_{G}\begin{bmatrix}f_{11}&f_{12}\\
f_{21}&f_{22}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\overline{g_{22}}&\overline{g_{12}}\\
\overline{g_{21}}&2\ \overline{g_{11}}\end{bmatrix}d\mu_{G},

which implies that
\Theta^{*}
is given by

\displaystyle\Theta^{*}:\mathbf{g}\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}g_{22}&g_{21}\\
g_{12}&2g_{11}\end{bmatrix},\ \mathbf{g}=\begin{bmatrix}g_{11}&g_{12}\\
g_{21}&g_{22}\end{bmatrix}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}).

It can be easily seen that
\Theta^{*}
satisfies
\|\mathbf{f}\|\leq\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|\leq 2\|\mathbf{f}\|
, for all
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
. That is,
\Theta^{*}
is bounded below by
m_{o}=1
.

Now, for any
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{5}{2}\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{l\in\{1,2\}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|\Big\langle E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l},\mathbf{f}\Big\rangle\Big\|^{2}=10\|\mathbf{f}\|^{2}\leq 10\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|^{2}.

Therefore,
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
with frame bounds
\gamma_{1}=\frac{5}{2}
and
\delta_{1}=10
.

Consider
\widetilde{\Phi_{1}}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{5}\phi_{1}&\phi_{1}\\
\phi_{2}&\frac{1}{5}\phi_{2}\end{bmatrix}
,
\widetilde{\Phi_{2}}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{5}\phi_{2}&\phi_{2}\\
\phi_{1}&\frac{1}{5}\phi_{1}\end{bmatrix}
in
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
.
Then, for any
\mathbf{f}=\Big[f_{ij}\Big]_{1\leq i,j\leq 2}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\{1,2\}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}-E_{8m}T_{k}\widetilde{\Phi_{l}}\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{25}\sum_{l\in\{1,2\}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\big|\int_{G}E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{l}\overline{f_{11}}\big|^{2}+\big|\int_{G}E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{l}\overline{f_{21}}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle+\big|\int_{G}E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{l}\overline{f_{12}}\big|^{2}+\big|\int_{G}E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{l}\overline{f_{22}}\big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{10}{25}\|\textbf{f}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{5}\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{5}\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|^{2}.

Thus, all the conditions in Theorem
4.1
are satisfied with
\lambda=0,\mu=\frac{1}{5},\eta=\frac{1}{5}
. Hence, the collection
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\widetilde{\Phi_{l}}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
.

Theorem
4.1
shows that a matrix-valued Gabor system
\mathcal{G}(C,B,\widetilde{\Phi}_{\Lambda_{0}})
becomes a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
if its window functions
\widetilde{\Phi}_{l},l\in\Lambda_{0}
are sufficiently close to the window functions
\Phi_{l},l\in\Lambda_{0}
of a matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame
\mathcal{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}})
. This can also be seen as a way of constructing new matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frames by altering the window functions of a known matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame appropriately. In the direction of obtaining new matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frames from known matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frames, we give the following result which states that the perturbed matrix-valued Gabor systems
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frames, under suitable conditions, becomes a matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}(C,B,\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}})
and
\mathcal{G}(C,B,\Psi_{\Lambda_{0}})
be matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frames for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
with frame bounds
\gamma_{1}
,
\delta_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
,
\delta_{2}
, respectively.
Suppose
\Theta^{*}
is bounded below with constant
m_{o}
such that
\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\delta_{2}}}>\frac{\|\Theta\|}{m_{o}}
.
Then, the perturbed matrix-valued Gabor system
\mathcal{G}\Big(C,B,(\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}}+\Psi_{\Lambda_{0}})\Big)
is a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
with frame bounds

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}-\frac{\sqrt{\delta_{2}}\|\Theta\|}{m_{o}}\right)^{2}\ \text{and}\ \ 2(\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}).

Proof.

For any
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
, we compute

\displaystyle\Big(\sum\limits_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\textbf{f },E_{Cm}T_{Bk}(\Phi_{l}+\Psi_{l})\rangle\|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum\limits_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\textbf{f },E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}+E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Psi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\Big(\sum\limits_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\textbf{f },E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle-\Big(\sum\limits_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\textbf{f },E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Psi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|-\sqrt{\delta_{2}}\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|-\sqrt{\delta_{2}}\|\Theta\|\|\mathbf{f}\|

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|-\frac{\sqrt{\delta_{2}}\|\Theta\|}{m_{o}}\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|.

This gives

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\textbf{f },E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}+E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Psi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\geq\Big(\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}-\frac{\sqrt{\delta_{2}}\|\Theta\|}{m_{o}}\Big)^{2}\|\Theta^{*}\mathbf{f}\|^{2}\ \text{for all}\ \mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\textbf{f },E_{Cm}T_{Bk}(\Phi_{l}+\Psi_{l})\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\Big(\sum\limits_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\textbf{f },E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Phi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}+\sum\limits_{l\in\Lambda_{0}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\Lambda^{\prime}}\|\langle\textbf{f },E_{Cm}T_{Bk}\Psi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}\Big)

(4.5)

\displaystyle\leq 2(\delta_{1}+\delta_{2})\|\Theta\mathbf{f}\|^{2}\ \text{for all}\ \mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}).

From (
4
) and (
4
), we conclude that
\mathcal{G}\Big(C,B,(\Phi_{\Lambda_{0}}+\Psi_{\Lambda_{0}})\Big)
is a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
with the desired frame bounds. This completes the proof.
∎

We end this paper by providing an application of Theorem
4.3
.

Example 4.4
.

Consider the
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
with frame bounds
\gamma_{1}=\frac{5}{2}
and
\delta_{1}=10
given in Example
4.2
.

Let
\Psi_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{5}\phi_{1}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{5}\phi_{2}\end{bmatrix}
,
\Psi_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{5}\phi_{2}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{5}\phi_{1}\end{bmatrix}
. Then,
\Psi_{1}
,
\Psi_{2}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
, and for any
\mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\{1,2\}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{8m}T_{k}\Psi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{25}\sum_{l\in\{1,2\}}\sum_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\big|\int_{G}E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{l}\overline{f_{11}}\big|^{2}+\big|\int_{G}E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{l}\overline{f_{21}}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle+\big|\int_{G}E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{l}\overline{f_{12}}\big|^{2}+\big|\int_{G}E_{8m}T_{k}\phi_{l}\overline{f_{22}}\big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{10}{25}\|\textbf{f}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{5}\|\Theta\textbf{f}\|^{2}.

Also

\displaystyle\sum_{l\in\{1,2\}}\sum\limits_{k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\langle\mathbf{f},E_{8m}T_{k}\Psi_{l}\rangle\|^{2}=\frac{2}{5}\|\textbf{f}\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{10}\|\Theta^{*}\textbf{f}\|^{2},\ \ \mathbf{f}\in L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}).

Thus,
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\Psi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
with frame bounds
\gamma_{2}=\frac{1}{10}
and
\delta_{2}=\frac{2}{5}
. Further,
\Theta^{*}
is bounded below by
m_{o}=1
and
\frac{5}{2}=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\delta_{2}}}>\frac{\|\Theta\|}{m_{o}}=2
. Hence, by Theorem
4.3
, the perturbed matrix-valued Gabor system

\{E_{8m}T_{k}(\Phi_{l}+\Psi_{l})\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
with frame bounds

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{\gamma_{1}}-\frac{\sqrt{\delta_{2}}\|\Theta\|}{m_{o}}\right)^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad 2(\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}).

Remark 4.5
.

Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.3
are not only ways of constructing new frames but also can be used to check if a matrix-valued Gabor system
\mathcal{G}(C,B,\widetilde{\Phi}_{\Lambda_{0}})
is a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame for
L^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{n\times n})
, where the window functions
\widetilde{\Phi_{l}},l\in{\Lambda_{0}}
have complex structure leading to complicated calculations. In order to understand this better, we compare Example
4.2
and Example
4.4
. In Example
4.2
, to prove matrix-valued
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame conditions of
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\widetilde{\Phi_{l}}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
, a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\Phi_{l}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
having simpler window functions is considered. However, in Example
4.4
,
\Phi_{l}+\Psi_{l}=\widetilde{\Phi_{l}},l\in\Lambda_{0}
. Hence, Example
4.4
can be seen as a method by which the collection
\{E_{8m}T_{k}\widetilde{\Phi_{l}}\}_{l\in\{1,2\},k\in\Lambda\atop m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is proved to be a
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frame by splitting its window functions as a sum of the window functions (perturbed window functions) of two
(\Theta,\Theta^{*})
-Gabor frames.

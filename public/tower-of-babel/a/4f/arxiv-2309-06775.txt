Long-time instability of planar Poiseuille-type flow in compressible fluid Journal: Elsevier journal
Andrew Yang, Zhu Zhang

Abstract
It is well-known that at the high Reynolds number, the linearized Navier-Stokes equations around the inviscid stable shear profile admit growing mode solutions due to the destabilizing effect of the viscosity. This phenomenon, called Tollmien-Schlichting instability, has been rigorously justified by Grenier-Guo-Nguyen [Adv. Math. 292 (2016); Duke J. Math. 165 (2016)] for Poiseuille flows and boundary layers in the incompressible fluid. To reveal this intrinsic instability mechanism in the compressible setting, in this paper, we study the long-time instability of the Poiseuille flow in a channel. Note that this instability arises in a low-frequency regime instead of a high-frequency regime for the Prandtl boundary layer. The proof is based on the quasi-compressible-Stokes iteration introduced by Yang-Zhang in [ 50 ] and subtle analysis of the dispersion relation for the instability. Note that we do not require symmetric conditions on the background shear flow or perturbations.

Introduction

Stability/instability properties of laminar flows at the high Reynolds number is the fundamental problem in
Hydrodynamic Stability Theory
. Significant progress was made by pioneering works of Kelvin
[
29
]
, Orr
[
42
]
, Rayleigh
[

]

[

For inviscid flow when the Reynolds number is infinity, the main stabilizing mechanism is the inviscid damping. That is, due to the mixing between vorticity and the background flow, the velocity field asymptotically converges to a laminar flow that is closed to its background. This phenomenon has been rigorously justified for Couette flow in linear/nonlinear settings, and for perturbations in high-regularity spaces, cf.
[
4
,
26
,
51
,
52

. On the other hand, if the perturbation is less smooth, some instabilities arise that prevent the solution from converging to the shear flow. In this direction, Lin-Zeng
[
35

constructed the time-periodic solution close to the Couette flow in
H^{\frac{3}{2}-}
-space. Nonlinear instability in Gevrey
2^{-}
space of Couette flow was shown by Deng-Masmoudi
[
13
]
. For shear flows other than the Couette, the dynamics is more complicated even in the linear level
[

,

,

In the high Reynolds number regime, the role of viscosity is mixed. On the one hand, the viscosity can help stabilize the shear flow with the aid of an enhanced dissipation mechanism. Such a mechanism can also be used to characterize the critical size of perturbations for the nonlinear stability of the corresponding shear flows. We refer to the list of references

Prandtl boundary layer, at high tangential frequency, there exists an unstable eigenmode corresponding to Gevrey function space with index
\frac{3}{2}

Substantial progress on this topic was made by Grenier-Guo-Nguyen
[

[

One of the powerful analytic tools in above studies for incompressible flow is the Orr-Sommerfeld equation, which describes the time evolution of the
Fourier mode behavior of the stream function. In the absence of viscosity, the Orr-Sommerfeld equation is the Rayleigh equation for inviscid flow.
Thus, it can be regarded as a singular perturbation of the Rayleigh equation at high Reynolds numbers.
The derivation of this famous equation relies on the stream function representation of the velocity field so that it is effective in the two-dimensional incompressible setting. However, for compressible flow, eigenvalue problems for stability can not be reduced to a simple single equation like the Orr-Sommerfeld. Therefore, despite many works from the physical perspectives
[
8
,
14
,
32
,
33
,
37
,
43
]
, mathematical results on hydrodynamic stability in compressible fluids are very few. The works that we are aware of in this direction are
[
1
,
53
]
for the Couette flow in two and three-dimensional spaces (without boundary) respectively. See also
[
27
,
28
,
41
]
for stability of Poiseuille flow in different regimes.

In order to
capture the Orr-Sommerfeld structure in the compressible setting, in
[
50
]
, the authors introduced a quasi-compressible system so that the Orr-Sommerfeld type equation can be derived and used to approximate the compressible Navier-Stokes for isentropic flow. This newly derived formulation allows one to use the “incompressible techniques” to treat problems for compressible flows, and it
has been proved helpful in the subsonic regime through the quasi-compressible and Stokes iteration to investigate both the Tollmien-Schlichting instability and validity of Prandtl ansatz in suitable function spaces in various physical situations. We also refer to the recent work
[
38
]
for the whole subsonic regime.

As a further exploration of the compressible flow, particularly in a channel, we will study the long-time instability phenomena of the planar Poiseuille flow in this paper. Note that although this kind of instability phenomena has been studied in
[
21
]
for Poiseuille flows in the incompressible fluid, there is no corresponding work for compressible models. Motivated by this, we consider the two-dimensional compressible Navier-Stokes equations for isentropic flow in a channel
\Omega=\left\{(x,y)\mid x\in\mathbb{R},y\in[-1,1]\right\}:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\vec{U})=0,\\
&\rho\partial_{t}\vec{U}+\rho\vec{U}\cdot\nabla\vec{U}+\nabla P(\rho)=\mu\epsilon\Delta\vec{U}+\lambda\epsilon\nabla(\nabla\cdot\vec{U})+\rho\vec{F},\\
&\vec{U}|_{y=\pm 1}=\vec{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

Here,
\rho
,
\vec{U}=(u,v)
and
P(\rho)
represent the density, velocity field and pressure of the fluid respectively. The vector field
\vec{F}
is a given external force.
The constants
\mu>0,\lambda\geq 0
are the rescaled shear and bulk viscosity, and
0<\epsilon\ll 1
is a small parameter which is proportional to the reciprocal of the Reynolds number. Without loss of generality, the constant
\mu
is set to 1 throughout the paper.

As well known, the planar Poiseuille flow is a typical steady solution profile to the Navier-Stokes equations when
the tangential derivative of the pressure function is balanced by the normal diffusion of the velocity field. In the
compressible setting under consideration, we can fix a class of Poiseuille-type profile as

\displaystyle(\rho_{s},\vec{\mathbf{U}}_{s})\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}(1,U_{s}(y),0),~y\in(-1,1),

(1.2)

which is a steady solution to (
1.1
) with a small constant force
\vec{F}=(-2U_{s}^{\prime\prime}\mu,0)
. Here we assume that

U_{s}
satisfies

\displaystyle U_{s}(\pm 1)=0,~U_{s}^{\prime}(-1)>0,~U_{s}^{\prime}(1)<0,\text{ and }U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(y)<0,~\text{ for }y\in(-1,1).

(1.3)

A typical example of this class of profile is the plane Poiseuille flow
U_{s}(y)=1-y^{2}
.

To study the stability/instability of the Poiseuille flow, we consider a small perturbation of the Poiseuille profile

\displaystyle\rho=1+\tilde{\rho},~u=U_{s}(y)+\tilde{u},~v=\tilde{v}.

By linearization, we have the following system for
(\rho,\vec{U})

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+U_{s}\partial_{x}\rho+\nabla\cdot\vec{U}=0,~t>0,~(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times{(-1,1)},\\
&\partial_{t}\vec{U}+U_{s}\partial_{x}\vec{U}+m^{-2}\nabla\rho+v\partial_{y}U_{s}\vec{e}_{1}-{\epsilon}\Delta\vec{U}-\lambda\epsilon\nabla(\nabla\cdot\vec{U})-\rho\vec{F}=0,\\
&\vec{U}|_{y=\pm 1}=\vec{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

Here we have dropped tilde for the simplicity of notations. Note that in our setting,
m=\frac{1}{\sqrt{P^{\prime}(1)}}
is the Mach number because the maximum magnitude of
the Poiseuille profile is
1
and
\sqrt{P^{\prime}(1)}
is the background sound speed.

For stability analysis, we look for solution
(\rho,u,v)
to the linearized compressible Navier-Stokes system (
1.4
) in the following form

\displaystyle(\rho,u,v)=e^{ik(x-ct)}(\hat{\rho},\hat{u},\hat{v})(y),

(1.5)

where
k>0
is frequency and
c\in\mathbb{C}
is the spectral parameter. Substituting (
1.5
) into (
1.4
), we obtain the following eigenvalue problem for the profile function
(\hat{\rho},\hat{u},\hat{v})

\left\{\begin{aligned} &ik(U_{s}-c)\hat{\rho}+\text{div}_{k}(\hat{u},\hat{v})=0,~~y\in(-1,1),\\
&-{\epsilon}\Delta_{k}\hat{u}-\lambda ik{\epsilon}\text{div}_{k}(\hat{u},\hat{v})+ik(U_{s}-c)\hat{u}+(ikm^{-2}+{\epsilon}U_{s}^{\prime\prime})\hat{\rho}+\hat{v}U_{s}^{\prime}=0,\\
&-{\epsilon}\Delta_{k}\hat{v}-\lambda{\epsilon}\partial_{y}\text{div}_{k}(\hat{u},\hat{v})+ik(U_{s}-c)\hat{v}+m^{-2}\partial_{y}\hat{\rho}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

with no-slip boundary conditions on two boundaries
y=\pm 1

\displaystyle\hat{u}|_{y=\pm 1}=\hat{v}|_{y=\pm 1}=0.

(1.7)

Here, in (
1.6
),

\Delta_{k}=(\partial_{y}^{2}-k^{2})
and
\text{div}_{k}(u,v)=iku+\partial_{y}v
denote the Fourier transform of Laplacian and divergence operators respectively. For convenience, we denote by
\mathcal{L}(\hat{\rho},\hat{u},\hat{v})
the linear operator (
1.6
).

The stability analysis relies on the solvability of the eigenvalue problem (
1.6
) with boundary conditions (
1.7
). In fact,
if for some complex number
c\in\mathbb{C}
with
\text{Im}c>0
and frequency
k\in\mathbb{R}
, the ODE system (
1.6
) with (
1.7
) has a non-zero solution, then the Poiseuille flow (
1.2
) is spectral unstable. Otherwise, it is spectral stable.

In this paper we concentrate on the case when the background profile is the planar shear flow that satisfies (
1.3
), and the main result is stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let Mach number
m\in\left(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)
. For sufficiently small
0<\epsilon\ll 1
, there exist frequency
k\sim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{7}}
and wave number
c\in\mathbb{C}
with
Imc\sim\epsilon^{\frac{2}{7}}
, such that the linearized compressible Navier-Stokes system admits a solution in the form of

(\rho,u,v)(t,x,y)=e^{ik(x-ct)}(\hat{\rho}.\hat{u},\hat{v})(y).

We have several remarks on the above result.

Remark 1.1
.

Unlike the case of boundary layer profile
[
22
,
50
]
, for the planar Poiseuille flow, the
unstable mode localizes at low frequency
k\sim\epsilon^{\frac{1}{7}}
rather than in a high-frequency regime, and it grows exponentially in time like
e^{tkImc}\sim e^{\epsilon^{\frac{3}{7}}t}
. Note that the slow growth is consistent with the linear inviscid stability for Poiseuille flow, see Lees-Lin’s criterion
[
30
]
.

Remark 1.2
.

In the work
[
27
]
by Kagei-Nishida, the instability of planar Poiseuille flows is shown in the high Mach number regime, which is different from the regime studied in this paper. Even though instabilities investigated in both papers occur at low frequencies, the mechanisms are different. In
[
27
]
, the unstable eigenmode bifurcates from the zero eigenvalue of hyperbolic part of linearized operator at zero frequency,
while in this paper, the instability arises from the interaction between the inviscid perturbation and boundary layers.

Remark 1.3
.

The dispersion relation is derived from full boundary conditions (
1.7
), instead of building upon symmetry assumptions on background flows and perturbations as in
[
21
]
. Thus, the analysis can be used for more generic shear flows without symmetry.

Remark 1.4
.

The above theorem is proved in the subsonic regime, covering the incompressible case when the Mach number is
0
. Note that the subsonic condition is essential because the ellipticity of compressible Orr-Sommerfeld equation does not hold when the Mach number
m\geq 1
. Hence,
how to establish mathematical theory for the cases in transonic and sonic regimes remains very challenging and unsolved.

Now we briefly present the ideas and strategies for the proof of main theorem. To match the full boundary conditions in (
1.7
), we need to construct four independent solutions to the eigenvalue problem (
1.6
). As in the case of boundary layer profiles
[
22
,
50
]
and the case of Poiseuille flow under incompressible perturbations
[
21
]
, the first step is to construct approximate solutions consisting of the inviscid and viscous parts. The inviscid component is constructed by using the Rayleigh approximate operator in the low-frequency regime, usually called the slow mode. In constrast, the viscous component takes effect in a region close to the two boundaries
y=\pm 1
to capture interactions between the viscosity and no-slip boundary conditions.

The next step is to construct four exact solutions to (
1.6
) near these approximations or equivalently to solve the remainder system (
3.1
). The difficulty in analysis comes from the lack of stream function expression in (
1.6
), so the resolvent problem (
3.5
) can not be reduced to the Orr-Sommerfeld equation as in the incompressible case. To overcome this difficulty, we apply the quasi-compressible-Stokes iteration introduced in
[
50
]
for studying boundary layers to the study of flows in the channel. Roughly speaking, we introduce the quasi-compressible operator, where the intrinsic effective stream function can be defined by considering the compressibility of fluids. The Orr-Sommerfeld type equation can be derived accordingly. On the other hand, to recover the loss of
regularity due to the quasi-compressible approximation, the Stokes operator is used to capture the elliptic
structure of the system. The convergence of iteration scheme will be presented in Section 3.3.
The proof is based on energy estimates, which is different from the Green function approach used in
[
21
]
. The concavity of Poiseuille flow plays an important role in analysis.

Finally, in Section 4, we solve the dispersion relation for the instability of Poiseuille flow. We remark that the dispersion relation is more complicated than the one studied in
[
50
]
for the instability of boundary layer, because, for latter case, one can choose the slow and fast modes decaying at infinity so that far-field conditions can be automatically satisfied. In this paper, to remove the symmetry assumptions made in
[
21
]
, the dispersion relation is derived from full boundary conditions (
1.7
): we look for the solution to eigenvalue problem (
1.6
) in the following form

\displaystyle(\rho,u,v)=(\rho_{+}^{s},u_{+}^{s},v_{+}^{s})+\alpha_{1}(\rho_{-}^{s},u_{-}^{s},v_{-}^{s})+\alpha_{2}(\rho_{-}^{f},u_{-}^{f},v_{-}^{f})+\alpha_{3}(\rho_{+}^{f},u_{+}^{f},v_{+}^{f}),

where
(\rho_{\pm}^{s},u_{\pm}^{s},v_{\pm}^{s})
are slow modes and
(\rho_{\pm}^{f},u_{\pm}^{f},v_{\pm}^{f})
are fast modes that involve boundary layer structures near
y=\pm 1
respectively, and constants
\alpha_{1}
,
\alpha_{2}
,
\alpha_{3}
are chosen such that the following three boundary conditions are fulfilled:
v(-1)=v(1)=u(-1)=0
. In this way, the zero point problem for the
4\times 4
matrix of dispersion relation is reduced into a single equation for matching the last boundary condition
u(1)=0.
Inspired by
[
12
,
15
]
, we solve this by applying the Rouché theorem.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we will construct the approximate solutions consisting of the inviscid modes and boundary layers corresponding to the inviscid and viscous effects respectively. Then, in the third section, the remainder of these approximate solutions are estimated by studying the resolvent problem. The quasi-compressible and Stokes iteration is used there. The dispersion relation for the instability is solved in Section 4. Finally, we list several useful estimates in the Appendices.

2
Approximate solutions

The approximate solutions consisting of inviscid approximations and boundary layers will be given in the following two subsections. The estimates on errors generated by these approximations will be given in Section 2.3.

2.1
Inviscid solutions

We start from the following inviscid system corresponding to (
1.6
)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &ik(U_{s}-c)\rho+\text{div}_{k}(u,v)=0,~~y\in(-1,1),\\
&ik(U_{s}-c)u+m^{-2}ik\rho+vU_{s}^{\prime}=0,\\
&ik(U_{s}-c)v+m^{-2}\partial_{y}\rho=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

As in
[
30
,
50
]
, the solution
(\rho,u,v)
to the inviscid system (
2.1
) can be represented by
using a new function
\varphi=\frac{i}{k}v
. In fact, the first equation in (
2.1
) gives

\displaystyle u=\partial_{y}\varphi-(U_{s}-c)\rho.

(2.2)

Substituting this into the second equation of (
2.1
), we can obtain the following relation between
\rho
and
\varphi
:

\displaystyle-m^{-2}A(y)\rho=(U_{s}-c)\partial_{y}\varphi-\varphi U_{s}^{\prime},

where

\displaystyle A(y)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}1-m^{2}(U_{s}-c)^{2}.

(2.3)

Note that for the Poiseuille flow in the
subsonic
regime, that is,
m\in(0,1)
,
A(y)
is invertible when the module
|c|
is sufficiently small. Thus, we can represent
\rho
in terms of
\varphi
as follows:

\displaystyle\rho=-m^{2}A^{-1}(y)\left[(U_{s}-c)\partial_{y}\varphi-\varphi U_{s}^{\prime}\right].

(2.4)

Plugging (
2.4
) into the third line of (
2.1
), we derive the following Lees-Lin equation (Rayleigh’s type) for
\varphi
, cf.
[
30
,
50
]
:

\displaystyle\text{Ray}_{\text{CNS}}(\varphi)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\partial_{y}\left\{A^{-1}\left[(U_{s}-c)\partial_{y}\varphi-U_{s}^{\prime}\varphi\right]\right\}-k^{2}(U_{s}-c)\varphi=0,~~y\in(-1,1).

(2.5)

Compared with the classical Lees-Lin equation around the Prandtl boundary layer profile studied in
[
50
]
, here we need to analyze (
2.5
) in a finite interval
(-1,1)
so that the boundary conditions for both
y=1
and
y=-1
need to be taken into account. For this, we construct two independent approximate solutions to the Lees-Lin equation (
2.5
) at low frequency
k\ll 1
. We start from
k=0
. In this case, the equation (
2.5
) reduces to

\displaystyle\partial_{y}\left\{A^{-1}\left[(U_{s}-c)\partial_{y}\varphi-U_{s}^{\prime}\varphi\right]\right\}=0,~y\in(-1,1),

and it
admits following two independent solutions

\displaystyle\varphi_{+}(y)

\displaystyle=U_{s}(y)-c,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\varphi_{-}(y)

\displaystyle=\left(U_{s}(y)-c\right)\int_{0}^{y}\frac{1}{(U_{s}(x)-c)^{2}}{\rm d}x-m^{2}\left(U_{s}(y)-c\right)y.

(2.7)

Based on
\varphi_{\pm}
, we construct two approximate solutions to (
2.5
) for
k\ll 1
.

Define the approximate Green’s function:

G(x,y)=-(U_{s}(x)-c)^{-1}\begin{cases}\varphi_{+}(y)\varphi_{-}(x),~x<y,\\
\varphi_{+}(x)\varphi_{-}(y),~x>y,\end{cases}

and two correctors at
k^{2}
-order:

\varphi_{\pm,k}(y)\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\int^{1}_{-1}G(x,y)(U_{s}(x)-c)\varphi_{\pm}(x){\rm d}x.

(2.8)

Set

\varphi^{s}_{\pm}=\varphi_{\pm}+k^{2}\varphi_{\pm,k}.

(2.9)

Plugging (
2.9
) into (
2.5
) gives

\text{Ray}_{\text{CNS}}(\varphi^{s}_{\pm})=-k^{4}(U_{s}-c)\varphi_{\pm,k}.

(2.10)

Thus,
\varphi^{s}_{\pm}
solves the Lees-Lin equation (2.1.5) up to
O(k^{4})
.

For later use, in the following lemma we summarize the boundary values of
\varphi^{s}_{\pm}
at
y=\pm 1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exists a positive constant
\gamma_{1}
, such that for
k\leq\gamma_{1}
and
|c|\leq\gamma_{1}
, the boundary values of
\varphi^{s}_{\pm}
have the following asymptotic behavior:

\displaystyle\varphi^{s}_{+}(-1)

\displaystyle=-c+k^{2}\frac{1}{U^{\prime}_{s}(-1)}\int^{1}_{-1}U^{2}_{s}(x){\rm d}x+O(1)k^{2}|c\log{Imc}|,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\partial_{y}\varphi^{s}_{+}(-1)

\displaystyle=U^{\prime}_{s}(-1)+O(1)k^{2}|\log{Imc}|,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\varphi^{s}_{+}(1)

\displaystyle=-c\left(1+O(1)k^{2}\right),

(2.13)

\displaystyle\partial_{y}\varphi^{s}_{+}(1)

\displaystyle=U^{\prime}_{s}(1)+O(1)k^{2},

(2.14)

\displaystyle\varphi^{s}_{-}(\pm 1)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{U^{\prime}_{s}(\pm 1)}+O(1)\bigg(|c\log{Imc}|+k^{2}\bigg),

(2.15)

\displaystyle\partial_{y}\varphi^{s}_{-}(\pm 1)

\displaystyle=O(1)|\log{Imc}|.

(2.16)

Proof.

Consider
\varphi_{+}^{s}
first. By the definition of
\varphi_{+}^{s}
in (
2.9
) and using (
2.8
), we obtain the following explicit formula:

\varphi^{s}_{+}(y)=\varphi_{+}(y)-k^{2}\varphi_{+}(y)\bigg(\int^{y}_{-1}\varphi_{+}(x)\varphi_{-}(x){\rm d}x\bigg)-k^{2}\varphi_{-}(y)\bigg(\int^{1}_{y}\varphi_{+}^{2}(x){\rm d}x\bigg).

(2.17)

Evaluating (
2.17
) at
y=-1
and using the boundary value
\varphi_{-}(-1)
given in (
5.1
), it holds that

\displaystyle\varphi^{s}_{+}(-1)

\displaystyle=-c-k^{2}\varphi_{-}(-1)\bigg(\int^{1}_{-1}(U_{s}(x)-c)^{2}{\rm d}x\bigg)

\displaystyle=-c-k^{2}\bigg(\frac{-1}{U^{\prime}_{s}(-1)}\int^{1}_{-1}U^{2}_{s}(x){\rm d}x+O(1)|c\log\text{Im }c|\bigg)

\displaystyle=-c+\frac{k^{2}}{U^{\prime}_{s}(-1)}\int^{1}_{-1}U^{2}_{s}(x){\rm d}x+O(1)k^{2}|c\log\text{Im }c|,

which is (
2.11
).
Similarly, we evaluate
\varphi_{+}^{s}(y)
at

to obtain

\displaystyle\varphi^{s}_{+}(1)

\displaystyle=-c+ck^{2}\bigg[\int^{1}_{-1}(U_{s}(x)-c)\varphi_{-}(x){\rm d}x\bigg]

\displaystyle=-c\left(1+O(1)k^{2}\right).

Here in the last line we have used the inequality

\left|\int_{-1}^{1}(U_{s}-c)\varphi_{-}(x){\rm d}x\right|\leq C\|\varphi_{-}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C,

with the aid of (
5.9
). Thus, (
2.13
) follows.

Next we compute boundary values of
\partial_{y}\varphi_{+}^{s}
at
y=\pm 1
. Differentiate (

\displaystyle U_{s}(y)-c

\displaystyle=U_{s}(-1)-c+U_{s}^{\prime}(-1)(1+y)+O(1)(1+y)^{2}

\displaystyle U_{s}^{\prime}(y)

(u_{\pm,\text{app}}^{f},v_{\pm,\text{app}}^{f})
have the following expansions:

\displaystyle u_{+,\text{app}}^{f}(1)=\frac{\text{Ai}(1,\tilde{z}_{0})}{\text{Ai}(2,\tilde{z}_{0})},~v_{+,\text{app}}^{f}(1)=ik\tilde{\delta},~~u_{+,\text{app}}^{f}(-1),v_{+,\text{app}}^{f}(-1)=O(1)\epsilon^{\infty},

(2.56)

\displaystyle u_{-,\text{app}}^{f}(-1)=-\frac{\text{Ai}(1,z_{0})}{\text{Ai}(2,z_{0})},~v_{-,\text{app}}^{f}(-1)=ik\delta,~~u_{-,\text{app}}^{f}(1),v^{f}_{-,\text{app}}(1)=O(1)\epsilon^{\infty}.

(2.57)

Proof.

We only show (
2.56
). Boundary conditions for
(u_{+,\text{app}}^{f},v_{+,\text{app}}^{f})
at
y=1
directly follow from the explicit formula (
2.52
) and (
2.53
). From pointwise estimates (
2.54
) and (
2.55
) for
j=l=0,
we can obtain

\displaystyle\left|v_{+,\text{app}}^{f}(-1)\right|

\displaystyle\leq Cn^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-2\tau_{1}n^{\frac{1}{3}}}\leq Ce^{-2\tau_{1}\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{7}}},

\displaystyle\left|u_{+,\text{app}}^{f}(-1)\right|

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{-2\tau_{1}n^{\frac{1}{3}}}\leq Ce^{-\tau_{1}\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{7}}}.

The proof of (
2.57
) is similar.
∎

2.3
Error estimates

Based on the approximate slow and fast modes constructed in the previous two subsections, we now summarize the formulation and related estimates on errors generated by the approximations. Recall inviscid modes
\vec{\Xi}^{s}_{\pm,\text{app}}
defined in (
2.28
)-(
2.30
), and viscous modes
\vec{\Xi}^{f}_{\pm,\text{app}}
given by (
2.42
) and (
2.52
) respectively. Plugging these solutions into (
1.6
), we obtain the following error functions:

\displaystyle\vec{E}_{\pm}^{s}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\mathcal{L}(\vec{\Xi}^{s}_{\pm,\text{app}})=\bigg(0,

\displaystyle-\epsilon\Delta_{k}u_{\pm,\text{app}}^{s}-\lambda\epsilon ik\text{div}_{k}(u_{\pm,\text{app}}^{s},v_{\pm,\text{app}}^{s})+\epsilon U_{s}^{\prime\prime}\rho_{\pm,\text{app}}^{s},

\displaystyle-\epsilon\Delta_{k}v_{\pm,\text{app}}^{s}-\lambda\epsilon\partial_{y}\text{div}_{k}(u_{\pm,\text{app}}^{s},v_{\pm,\text{app}}^{s})-k^{4}(U_{s}-c)\varphi_{\pm,k}\bigg),

(2.58)

where
\varphi_{\pm,k}
are defined in (
2.8
),
and

\displaystyle\vec{E}_{\pm}^{f}\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\mathcal{L}(\vec{\Xi}^{f}_{\pm,\text{app}})=\bigg(0,~

\displaystyle k^{2}\epsilon u_{\pm,\text{app}}^{f}+ik\left[U_{s}(y)-U_{s}^{\prime}(\pm 1)(1\pm y)\right]u_{\pm,\text{app}}^{f}

\displaystyle+\left(U_{s}^{\prime}(y)-U_{s}^{\prime}(-1)\right)v_{\pm,\text{app}}^{f},~-\epsilon\Delta_{k}v_{\pm,\text{app}}^{f}+ik(U_{s}-c)v_{\pm,\text{app}}^{f}\bigg).

(2.59)

Based on bounds in Corollary
2.1
and Lemma
2.4
, we have the following estimates on error terms
\vec{E}^{s}_{\pm}
and
\vec{E}^{f}_{\pm}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Decompose the error function
\vec{E}_{+}^{s}
as

\displaystyle\vec{E}_{+}^{s}=\vec{E}_{+,1}^{s}+\vec{E}_{+,2}^{s},

(2.60)

where
\vec{E}_{+,2}^{s}=(0,0,-k^{4}(U_{s}-c)\varphi_{+,k}).
Then it holds that

\displaystyle\|\vec{E}_{+,1}^{s}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{6}{7}},~\|\vec{E}_{+,2}^{s}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{4}{7}}.

(2.61)

The error function
\vec{E}_{-}^{s}
satifies the following uniform estimate:

\displaystyle\|\vec{E}_{-}^{s}\|_{L^{2}}+\epsilon^{\frac{2}{7}}\|\partial_{y}\vec{E}_{-}^{s}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{4}{7}}.

(2.62)

For the error functions
\vec{E}_{\pm}^{f}
generated by the boundary layers, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\vec{E}_{\pm}^{f}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{6}{7}}.

(2.63)

Proof.

We start with the error function
\vec{E}_{+}^{s}
defined in (
2.3
). By using (
2.38
) in Corollary
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\vec{E}_{+,1}^{s}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\epsilon\left\|\left(u_{+,\text{app}}^{s},v_{+,\text{app}}^{s}\right)\right\|_{H^{2}}+\epsilon\|\varrho_{+,\text{app}}^{s}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{6}{7}},

(2.64)

and

\displaystyle\|\vec{E}_{+,2}^{s}\|_{H^{1}}\leq k^{4}\left(1+\|\varphi_{-}\|_{H^{1}}\right)\leq\epsilon^{\frac{4}{7}}.

(2.65)

Then combining (
2.64
) and (
2.65
) yields (
2.61
).

For
\vec{E}_{-}^{s}
, we use (
2.39
) to obtain that

\displaystyle\|\vec{E}_{-}^{s}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\epsilon\left\|\left(u_{-,\text{app}}^{s},v_{-,\text{app}}^{s}\right)\right\|_{H^{2}}+\epsilon\|\varrho_{-,\text{app}}^{s}\|_{L^{2}}+k^{4}\|\varphi_{-}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{4}{7}},

(2.66)

and

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}\vec{E}_{-}^{s}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\epsilon\left\|\left(u_{-,\text{app}}^{s},v_{-,\text{app}}^{s}\right)\right\|_{H^{3}}+C\epsilon\|\varrho_{-,\text{app}}^{s}\|_{H^{1}}+Ck^{4}\|\varphi_{-}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{2}{7}}.

(2.67)

Thus the inequality (
2.62
) follows from (
2.66
) and (
2.67
).

Finally for
\vec{E}_{\pm}^{f}
, using pointwise estimates (
2.54
) and (
2.55
) gives

\displaystyle\|\vec{E}_{\pm}^{f}\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle Ck^{2}\epsilon\|u_{\pm,\text{app}}^{f}\|_{L^{2}}+Ck\|(1\pm y)^{2}u_{\pm,\text{app}}^{f}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|(1\pm y)v_{\pm,\text{app}}^{f}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+C\left(\epsilon\|v_{\pm,\text{app}}^{f}\|_{H^{2}}+k\|(1\pm y)v_{\pm,\text{app}}^{f}\|_{L^{2}}+|c|\|v_{\pm,\text{app}}^{f}\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon^{\frac{6}{7}},

which is (
2.63
). The proof of Proposition
2.1
is completed.

3
Control of remainder

In the last section, we have constructed four independent approximate solutions to the eigenvalue problem (
1.6
). To correct errors generated by the approximation, we need to
solve the following remainder system

\left\{\begin{aligned} &ik(U_{s}-c)\rho+\text{div}_{k}(u,v)=0,\quad y\in(-1,1),\\
&-\epsilon\Delta_{k}u-\lambda ik\epsilon\text{div}_{k}(u,v)+ik(U_{s}-c)u+(ikm^{-2}+\epsilon U^{\prime\prime}_{s})\rho+vU^{\prime}_{s}=f_{u},\\
&-\epsilon\Delta_{k}v-\lambda\epsilon\partial_{y}\text{div}_{k}(u,v)+ik(U_{s}-c)v+m^{-2}\partial_{y}\rho=f_{v},\\
&v|_{y=\pm 1}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

where
(f_{u},f_{v})
are in homogenuous source terms.
Notice that in (
3.1
), we only impose the boundary condition for normal velocity
v
. The system (
3.1
) is studied in the regime of parameters:
k\approx\epsilon^{\frac{1}{7}}
and
c
satisfies (
1.8
).

The main result in this section is the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let the Mach number
m\in\left(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)
.
If
f_{u},f_{v}\in L^{2}(-1,1)
, then remainder system (
3.1
) admits a solution
(\rho,u,v)\in H^{1}(-1,1)\times\left(H^{2}(-1,1)\right)^{2}
. Moreover, the solution satisfies following uniform-in-
\epsilon
estimates

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\frac{5}{7}}\|(m^{-1}\rho,u,v)\|_{L^{2}}+\epsilon^{\frac{5}{7}}\|(m^{-2}\partial_{y}\rho,\partial_{y}u,\partial_{y}v)\|_{L^{2}}+\epsilon^{\frac{9}{7}}\|(\partial_{y}^{2}u,\partial_{y}^{2}v)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(f_{u},f_{v})\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.2)

For
f_{u}
,
f_{v}\in H^{1}(-1,1)
, we define the operator

\displaystyle\Omega(f_{u},f_{v})=-f_{v}+\frac{1}{ik}\partial_{y}(A^{-1}f_{u}).

(3.3)

Then the solution
(\rho,u,v)
satisfies the following improved estimates:

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\frac{2}{7}}\|(m^{-1}\rho,u,v)\|_{L^{2}}+\epsilon^{\frac{2}{7}}\|(m^{-2}\partial_{y}\rho,\partial_{y}u,\partial_{y}v)\|_{L^{2}}+\epsilon^{\frac{4}{7}}\|(\partial_{y}^{2}u,\partial_{y}^{2}v)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\|\Omega(f_{u},f_{v})\|_{L^{2}}+C\epsilon^{\frac{1}{7}}\|(f_{u},f_{v})\|_{L^{2}}+C\epsilon^{\frac{2}{7}}\|\text{div}_{k}(f_{u},f_{v})\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.4)

Furthermore, the boundary values
u(\pm 1;c)
are analytic in
c
.

3.1
Quasi-compressible approximation

To solve (
3.1
), we introduce the following quasi-compressible approximate problem:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &ik(U_{s}-c)\varrho+\text{div}_{k}(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})=0,~y\in(-1,1),\\
&-\epsilon\Delta_{k}(\mathfrak{u}+(U_{s}-c)\varrho)+ik(U_{s}-c)\mathfrak{u}+\mathfrak{v}U^{\prime}_{s}+ikm^{-2}\varrho=s_{1},\\
&-\epsilon\Delta_{k}\mathfrak{v}+ik(U_{s}-c)\mathfrak{v}+m^{-2}\partial_{y}\varrho=s_{2},\\
&\mathfrak{v}|_{y=\pm 1}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.5)

Here
(s_{1},s_{2})
is a given source term. We denote the operator on the left hand side of (
3.5
) by
\mathfrak{Q}
.

As in
[
50
]
, we can decouple
(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})
and reformulate the system (
3.5
) into an Orr-Sommerfeld type equation. In fact, by the first equation in (
3.5
), it is natural to define the effective stream function
\Phi
, which satisfies:

\displaystyle\partial_{y}\Phi=\mathfrak{u}+(U_{s}-c)\varrho,~-ik\Phi=\mathfrak{v},~~\Phi|_{y=\pm 1}=0.

(3.6)

Then plugging (
3.6
) into the second equation of (
3.5
), we can express
\varrho
in terms of
\Phi
as

\displaystyle m^{-2}\varrho=-A^{-1}(y)\bigg[\frac{i}{n}\Delta_{k}\partial_{y}\Phi+(U_{s}-c)\partial_{y}\Phi-U_{s}^{\prime}\Phi-(ik)^{-1}s_{1}\bigg],

(3.7)

where the function
A(y)
is defined in (
2.3
). Substituting (
3.7
) into the third
equation, we derive the following equation for
\Phi
:

\text{OS}_{\text{CNS}}(\Phi)=\frac{i}{n}\Lambda(\Delta_{k}\Phi)+(U_{s}-c)\Lambda(\Phi)-\partial_{y}(A^{-1}U_{s}^{\prime})\Phi=\Omega(s_{1},s_{2}),~y\in(-1,1).

(3.8)

Here
n=\frac{k}{\epsilon}
is the rescaled frequency, and

\displaystyle\Lambda:H^{2}(-1,1)\cap H^{1}_{0}(-1,1)\rightarrow L^{2}(-1,1),

\displaystyle\Lambda(\Phi)=\partial_{y}(A^{-1}\partial_{y}\Phi)-k^{2}\Phi

is the modified vorticity, and the operator
\Omega
is defined in (
3.3
). To solve (
3.8
), the following boundary conditions are imposed:

\Lambda(\Phi)|_{y=\pm 1}=\Phi|_{y=\pm 1}=0.

If
\Phi
is a solution to (
3.8
), then
(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})
is a solution to the quasi-compressible system (
3.5
).

According to the above argument, it is suffcient to study the following Orr-Sommerfeld type equation.

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{OS}_{\text{CNS}}(\Phi)=h,~y\in(-1,1),\\
&\Phi|_{y=\pm 1}=\Lambda(\Phi)|_{y=\pm 1}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.9)

Here
h\in L^{2}(-1,1)
is a given source. Once the solution
\Phi
is obtained, the fluid quantities
(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})
can be recovered from (
3.6
) and (
3.7
).

For the solvability of (
3.9
), we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let the Mach number
m\in\left(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)
. There exists a unique solution
\Phi\in H^{4}(-1,1)\cap H^{1}_{0}(-1,1)
to the Orr-Sommerfeld equation (
3.9
). Moreover, the solution satisfies

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda\Phi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{7}}\|h\|_{L^{2}},

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi),k\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{4}{7}}\|h\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.11)

Proof.

We only show the a priori estimates (
3.10
) and (
3.11
). The existence and uniqueness parts can be proved in the same way as in Lemma 3.5 of
[
50
]
.
Set the following weight function.

\displaystyle w(y)=-[\partial_{y}(A^{-1}\partial_{y}U_{s})]^{-1}.

(3.12)

Multiplying (
3.9
) by
-w\overline{\Lambda(\Phi)}
and integrating the resultant equation over
(-1,1)
, we have:

\displaystyle\underbrace{-\frac{i}{n}\int^{1}_{-1}w\Lambda(\Delta_{k}\Phi)\overline{\Lambda(\Phi)}{\rm d}y}_{I_{1}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int^{1}_{-1}-(U_{s}-c)w|\Lambda(\Phi)|^{2}{\rm d}y}_{I_{2}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int^{1}_{-1}-\Phi\overline{\Lambda(\Phi)}{\rm d}y}_{I_{3}}+\underbrace{\int^{1}_{-1}hw\overline{\Lambda(\Phi)}{\rm d}y}_{I_{4}}=0.

(3.13)

Now we estimate
I_{i}
,
i=1,2,3,4
, term by term. First we consider
I_{1}
.
Denote
[\Delta_{k},\Lambda]=\Delta_{k}(\Lambda(\Phi))-\Lambda(\Delta_{k}(\Phi))
and write
I_{1}
as

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{n}\int^{1}_{-1}w\Delta_{k}\left(\Lambda(\Phi)\right)\overline{\Lambda(\Phi)}{\rm d}y+\frac{i}{n}\int^{1}_{-1}w[\Delta_{k},\Lambda](\Phi)\overline{\Lambda(\Phi)}{\rm d}y=I_{11}+I_{12}.

(3.14)

For the first term
I_{11}
, integrating by parts and using boundary conditions
\Lambda(\Phi)|_{y=\pm 1}=0
give

\displaystyle I_{11}=\frac{i}{n}\int^{1}_{-1}w(|\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi)|^{2}+k^{2}|\Lambda(\Phi)|^{2}){\rm d}y+\frac{i}{n}\int^{1}_{-1}w^{\prime}\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi)\overline{\Lambda(\Phi)}{\rm d}y,

(3.15)

in which the last integral is bounded by

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{i}{n}\int^{1}_{-1}w^{\prime}\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi)\overline{\Lambda(\Phi)}dy\bigg|\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{6}{7}}\|\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.16)

Concerning
I_{12}
, we first represent the commutator
[\Delta_{k},\Lambda]\Phi
in terms of
\Lambda(\Phi)
,
\partial_{y}\Phi
and
\Phi
:

\displaystyle[\Delta_{k},\Lambda]\Phi

\displaystyle=\partial^{3}_{y}(A^{-1}\partial_{y}\Phi)-\partial_{y}(A^{-1}\partial^{3}_{y}\Phi)

\displaystyle=2\partial_{y}(A^{-1})\partial^{3}_{y}\Phi+3\partial^{2}_{y}(A^{-1})\partial^{2}_{y}\Phi+\partial^{3}_{y}(A^{-1})\partial_{y}\Phi.

(3.17)

Then by using the relations

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{y}\Phi

\displaystyle=A\Lambda(\Phi)+A^{-1}\partial_{y}A\partial_{y}\Phi+k^{2}A\Phi,

(3.18)

\displaystyle\partial^{3}_{y}\Phi

\displaystyle=A\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi)+2\Lambda(\Phi)\partial_{y}A+\partial_{y}\Phi(A^{-1}\partial^{2}_{y}A+k^{2}A)+2k^{2}\Phi\partial_{y}A,

(3.19)

we obtain
|\partial_{y}^{2}\Phi|\approx|\Lambda(\Phi)|+\left|\left(\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi\right)\right|
, and
|\partial_{y}^{3}\Phi|\approx\left|\left(\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi),\Lambda(\Phi)\right)\right|+\left|\left(\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi\right)\right|
. Plugging this into commutator (
3.17
) and taking
L^{2}
norm, we obtain

\left\|[\Delta_{k},\Lambda]\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\left\|\left(\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi),\Lambda(\Phi)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\right).

Thus,
I_{12}
is bounded as

\displaystyle|I_{12}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{n}\|[\Delta_{k},\Lambda]\Phi\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{\frac{6}{7}}\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\left(\left\|\left(\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi),\Lambda(\Phi)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\right).

(3.20)

By substituting estimates (
3.15
), (
3.16
) for
I_{11}
and (
3.20
) for
I_{12}
into (
3.14
), then taking real and imaginary parts of the result respectively, we deduce that

\displaystyle|\text{Re}I_{1}|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C\|\text{Im}w\|_{L^{\infty}}}{n}\left\|\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C\epsilon^{\frac{6}{7}}\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\left(\left\|\left(\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi),\Lambda(\Phi)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon^{\frac{8}{7}}\left\|\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C\epsilon^{\frac{6}{7}}\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\left(\left\|\left(\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi),\Lambda(\Phi)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\right),

(3.21)

and

\displaystyle\text{Im}I_{1}\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int_{-1}^{1}\text{Re}w\left|\left(\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi),k\Lambda(\Phi)\right)\right|^{2}{\rm d}y-C\epsilon^{\frac{6}{7}}\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\bigg(\left\|\left(\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi),\Lambda(\Phi)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\frac{6}{7}}\left\|\left(\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi),k\Lambda(\Phi)\right)\right\|^{2}-C\epsilon^{\frac{6}{7}}\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\bigg(\left\|\left(\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi),\Lambda(\Phi)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\bigg).

(3.22)

In (
3.21
) and (
3.22
), we have used the fact that
|\text{Im}w|\lesssim|c|\lesssim\epsilon^{\frac{2}{7}}
and
\text{Re}w\gtrsim 1
respectively.

Next we estimate
I_{2}
. By using (
5.13
) and (
5.14
), we can obtain

\left|\text{Re}\bigg((U_{s}-c)w\bigg)\right|\lesssim 1,

and

\text{Im}\bigg(-(U_{s}-c)w\bigg)=\left(w_{0}-U_{s}w_{1}\right)\text{Im}c+O(1)|c|^{2}\gtrsim{\gamma}_{0}\text{Im}c+O(1)|c|^{2}\gtrsim\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}\epsilon^{\frac{2}{7}}.

Therefore, by taking real and imaginary part of
I_{2}
respectively, we deduce that:

\displaystyle|\text{Re}I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\text{Re}\bigg((U_{s}-c)w\bigg)\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}},

(3.23)

and

\displaystyle\text{Im}I_{2}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{-1}^{1}\text{Im}\bigg(-(U_{s}-c)w\bigg)\left|\Lambda(\Phi)\right|^{2}{\rm d}y\geq\frac{\gamma_{0}}{2}\epsilon^{\frac{2}{7}}\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.24)

For
I_{3}
, integrating by parts and using the boundary conditions
\Phi|_{y=\pm 1}=0
give

\displaystyle I_{3}=\int_{-1}^{1}\bar{A}^{-1}|\partial_{y}\Phi|^{2}+k^{2}|\Phi|^{2}{\rm d}y.

Then we have

\bar{A}^{-1}=(1-m^{2}U_{s}^{2})^{-2}\left(1-m^{2}U_{s}^{2}-2m^{2}U_{s}\bar{c}+O(1)|c|^{2}\right),

which implies that
\text{Re}\left(\bar{A}^{-1}\right)\sim 1
and
\text{Im}\left(\bar{A}^{-1}\right)\gtrsim\left(m^{2}\text{Im}c\right)U_{s}-|c|^{2}
.
Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\text{Re}I_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\text{Re}\left(\bar{A}^{-1}\right)|\partial_{y}\Phi|^{2}+k^{2}|\Phi|^{2}{\rm d}y\gtrsim\|(\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(3.25)

and

\displaystyle\text{Im}I_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\text{Im}\left(\bar{A}^{-1}\right)|\partial_{y}\Phi|^{2}{\rm d}y\gtrsim m^{2}\text{Im}c\left(\int_{-1}^{1}U_{s}|\partial_{y}\Phi|^{2}{\rm d}y\right)-|c|^{2}\|\partial_{y}\Phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim-\epsilon^{\frac{4}{7}}\|\partial_{y}\Phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.26)

Finally, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, it holds that:

|I_{4}|\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}.

Thus, we have completed the estimates for
I_{1}
to
I_{4}
. By plugging them into (
3.1
) and taking real and imaginary parts respectively, we obtain

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\epsilon^{\frac{8}{7}}\left\|\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C\epsilon^{\frac{6}{7}}\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\left(\left\|\left(\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi),\Lambda(\Phi)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\epsilon^{\frac{8}{7}}\left\|\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|h\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(3.27)

and

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\frac{4}{7}}\left\|\left(\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi),k\Lambda(\Phi)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\epsilon^{\frac{4}{7}}\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\left(\left\|\left(\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi),\Lambda(\Phi)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\epsilon^{\frac{2}{7}}\|\partial_{y}\Phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{7}}\|h\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}.

\displaystyle\quad\leq o(1)\|(\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\epsilon^{-\frac{4}{7}}\|h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.28)

Set

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}:=\epsilon^{\frac{4}{7}}\left\|\left(\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi),k\Lambda(\Phi)\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Combining (
3.1
) and (
3.1
) suitably, we have:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}\leq o(1)\mathfrak{B}+\epsilon^{-\frac{4}{7}}\|h\|_{L^{2}}.

For sufficiently small
\epsilon\ll 1
, it holds that

\mathfrak{B}\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{4}{7}}\|h\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

This implies inequalities (
3.10
) and (
3.11
). The proof of lemma
3.1
is completed.
∎

With the solution
\Phi
to Orr-Sommerfeld type equation (
3.8
), we can recover fluid quantities
(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})
in the following corollary.

Corollary 3.1
.

For any
s_{1},s_{2}\in H^{1}(-1,1)
, there exists a solution
(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})\in H^{2}(-1,1)
to the quasi-compressible system (
3.5

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{u}\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle+\|(m^{-2}\varrho,\mathfrak{v})\|_{H^{2}}+\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{7}}\|\text{div}_{k}(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{7}}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+C\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{7}}\|s_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|s_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\text{div}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}},

(3.29)

and

\displaystyle\|\partial^{2}_{y}\mathfrak{u}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{4}{7}}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+C\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{7}}\|s_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|s_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\text{div}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.30)

Proof.

Let
\Phi
be the solution to (

). From (
3.10
), (
3.11
), (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\partial^{2}_{y}\Phi\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle C\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}+C\|(\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{7}}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}},

(3.31)

and

\displaystyle\|\partial^{3}_{y}\Phi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{y}\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\Lambda(\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}+C\|(\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{4}{7}}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.32)

From
\mathfrak{v}=-ik\Phi
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{v}\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial^{2}_{y}\Phi\|_{L^{2}}+C\|(\partial_{y}\Phi,k\Phi)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{7}}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}.

This gives (
3.1
) for
\mathfrak{v}
. For the density
\varrho
, by using the explicit formula (
3.7
) and the elementary inequality

\displaystyle\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\int^{1}_{-1}|\Phi^{\prime}(x)|{\rm d}x\leq\sqrt{2}\|\Phi^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}},\text{ for any }\Phi\in H^{1}_{0}(-1,1),

(3.33)

we have

\displaystyle m^{-2}\|\varrho\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{n}\|\partial^{3}_{y}\Phi\|_{L^{2}}+C(1+\frac{k^{2}}{n})\|\partial_{y}\Phi\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{y}U_{s}\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{C}{k}\|s_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{n}\|\partial^{3}_{y}\Phi\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\partial_{y}\Phi\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{C}{k}\|s_{1}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{7}}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+C\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{7}}\|s_{1}\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.34)

Here we have used (
3.31
) and (
3.32
) in the last inequality of (
3.34
). For the derivatives of
\varrho
, we firstly observe that

\displaystyle-m^{-2}\partial_{y}\varrho

\displaystyle=\text{OS}_{\text{CNS}}(\Phi)+k^{2}\left[\frac{i}{n}\Delta_{k}\Phi+(U_{s}-c)\Phi\right]-\frac{1}{ik}\partial_{y}(A^{-1}s_{1})

\displaystyle=-s_{2}+k^{2}\left[\frac{i}{n}\Delta_{k}\Phi+(U_{s}-c)\Phi\right].

Then taking
L^{2}
-norm on both side of the above equation and using (
3.31
) give

\displaystyle m^{-2}\|\partial_{y}\varrho\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|s_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\partial^{2}_{y}\Phi\|_{L^{2}}+Ck\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{7}}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+C\|s_{2}\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.35)

Moreover, by taking divergence of the last two equations in (
3.5
), we can derive the following equation:

-m^{-2}\Delta_{k}\varrho=-\text{div}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})+k^{2}(U_{s}-c)^{2}\varrho+2k^{2}\Phi U^{\prime}_{s}.

Thus taking
L^{2}
-norm and using (
3.31
) and (

) yield that

\displaystyle m^{-2}\|\partial^{2}_{y}\varrho\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\text{div}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+C(1+m^{-2})\|\varrho\|_{L^{2}}+Ck\|\Phi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\text{div}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+C\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{7}}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+C\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{7}}\|s_{1}\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.36)

Combining (
3.34
), (
3.35
) and (
3.36
) together yields (
3.1
) for
\varrho
. Concerning about the divergence field

that

\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{7}}\|\text{div}_{k}(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|\varrho\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{7}}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+C\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{7}}\|s_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|s_{2}\|_{L^{2}}.

Finally, we estimate
\mathfrak{u}
. From (

), it holds that

|\mathfrak{u}|\sim|\partial_{y}\Phi|+|\varrho|
. Thus, by using (
3.31
), (
3.32

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{u}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|\partial_{y}\Phi\|_{H^{1}}+C\|\varrho\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{7}}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+C\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{7}}\|s_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|s_{2}\|_{L^{2}},

and

\displaystyle\|\partial^{2}_{y}\mathfrak{u}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial^{3}_{y}\Phi\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\varrho\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{4}{7}}\|\Omega(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+C\epsilon^{-\frac{1}{7}}\|s_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|s_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\text{div}_{k}(s_{1},s_{2})\|_{L^{2}}.

Therefore, the estimates (
3.1
) and (

) for

are obtained.
This completes the proof of the corollary.

It is straightforward to see that the quasi-compressible operator
\mathfrak{Q}
generates the error

\displaystyle\vec{E}_{\mathfrak{Q}}(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})

\displaystyle\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\mathcal{L}(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})-\mathfrak{Q}(\varrho,\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})

\displaystyle=\bigg(0,\epsilon\Delta_{k}\left((U_{s}-c)\varrho\right)-\lambda\epsilon ik\text{div}_{k}(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})+\epsilon U_{s}^{\prime\prime}\varrho,-\epsilon\lambda\partial_{y}\text{div}_{k}(\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{v})\bigg),

(3.37)

which is not in
H^{1}(-1,1)
. This fact prevents us from iterating
\mathfrak{Q}
directly to solve (
3.1
). In the next subsection, we will introduce the Stokes operator to recover the regularity.

3.2
Stokes approximation

To smooth out the error term (
3.1
) generated by the quasi-compressible system (
3.5
) , we introduce the following Stokes approximation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &ik(U_{s}-c)\mathcal{P}+\text{div}_{k}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})=q_{0},~y\in(-1,1),\\
&-\epsilon\Delta_{k}\mathcal{U}-\lambda ik\epsilon\text{div}_{k}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})+ik(U_{s}-c)\mathcal{U}+(ikm^{-2}+\epsilon U^{\prime\prime}_{s})\mathcal{P}=q_{1},\\
&-\epsilon\Delta_{k}\mathcal{V}-\lambda\epsilon\partial_{y}\text{div}_{k}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})+ik(U_{s}-c)\mathcal{V}+m^{-2}\partial_{y}\mathcal{P}=q_{2},\\
&\partial_{y}\mathcal{U}|_{y=\pm 1}=\mathcal{V}|_{y=\pm 1}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.38)

Here
(q_{0},q_{1},q_{2})
is any given inhomogenuous source term. Compared with the original resolvent problem (
3.1
), we remove the stretching term
\mathcal{V}U_{s}^{\prime}
in the Stokes system (
3.38
). The solvability of Stokes system is summarized as follows.

Lemma 3.2
.

The Stokes system (
3.38
) admits a unique solution
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\in H^{1}(-1,1)\times(H^{2}(-1,1))^{2}
. Moreover,
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
satisfies the following estimates:

\displaystyle\|(m^{-1}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{3}{7}}\|(m^{-1}q_{0},q_{1},q_{2})\|_{L^{2}},

(3.39)

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{y}\mathcal{U},k\mathcal{U})\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{y}\mathcal{V},k\mathcal{V})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{5}{7}}\|(m^{-1}q_{0},q_{1},q_{2})\|_{L^{2}},

(3.40)

\displaystyle\|div_{k}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\|_{H^{1}}+m^{-2}\|\partial_{y}\mathcal{P}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{7}}\|(m^{-2}q_{0},q_{1},q_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\partial_{y}q_{0}\|_{L^{2}},

(3.41)

\displaystyle\|(\Delta_{k}\mathcal{U},\Delta_{k}\mathcal{V})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{9}{7}}\|(m^{-1}q_{0},q_{1},q_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\partial_{y}q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.42)

Remark 3.1
.

In view of (
3.39
)-(
3.41
), the divergence
\text{div}_{k}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
and density
\mathcal{P}
have stronger estimates than other components. This indicates the absence of boundary layers for these two components.

Proof.

We follow the proof in
[
50
]
. Multiplying the second and third equations by
-\bar{\mathcal{U}}
and
-\bar{\mathcal{V}}
respectively and integrating by parts, we obtain:

\displaystyle\epsilon\bigg(\|\partial_{y}\mathcal{U},k\mathcal{U}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{y}\mathcal{V},k\mathcal{V}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\bigg)+\lambda\epsilon\|\text{div}_{k}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{ik\int^{1}_{-1}(U_{s}-c)\bigg(|\mathcal{U}|^{2}+|\mathcal{V}|^{2}\bigg){\rm d}y}_{J_{1}}+\underbrace{m^{-2}\int^{1}_{-1}-\mathcal{P}\overline{\text{div}_{k}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})}{\rm d}y}_{J_{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\underbrace{-\int^{1}_{-1}(q_{1}+\epsilon U_{s}^{\prime\prime}\mathcal{P})\bar{\mathcal{U}}+q_{2}\bar{\mathcal{V}}{\rm d}y}_{J_{3}}.

(3.43)

The real part of
J_{1}
gives

\displaystyle\text{Re}J_{1}=k\text{Im}c\left\|(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\gtrsim\epsilon^{\frac{3}{7}}\|(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.44)

For
J_{2}
, by using the continuity equation
\eqref{A2.1}_{1}
, we have

\overline{\text{div}_{k}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})}=ik(U_{s}-\bar{c})\bar{\mathcal{P}}+\bar{q_{0}}.

Plugging this into
J_{2}
and taking real part give

\displaystyle\text{Re}J_{2}

\displaystyle=-ikm^{-2}\int^{1}_{-1}(U_{s}-\bar{c})|\mathcal{P}|^{2}{\rm d}y-m^{-2}\int^{1}_{-1}\mathcal{P}\bar{q_{0}}{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\geq k\text{Im}c\|m^{-1}\mathcal{P}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-m^{-2}\|\mathcal{P}\|_{L^{2}}\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\epsilon^{\frac{3}{7}}\|m^{-1}\mathcal{P}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-m^{-2}\|\mathcal{P}\|_{L^{2}}\|q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.45)

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
J_{3}
is bounded by

\displaystyle|J_{3}|\lesssim\|(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\|_{L^{2}}\|(q_{1},q_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+\epsilon\bigg(\|\mathcal{P}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\mathcal{U}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\bigg).

(3.46)

Therefore, by substituting (
3.44
), (
3.45
), (
3.46
) into (
3.43
) and then taking the real part, we deduce that

\displaystyle\epsilon\bigg(\|\partial_{y}\mathcal{U},k\mathcal{U}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\partial_{y}\mathcal{V},k\mathcal{V}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\bigg)+\lambda\epsilon\|\text{div}_{k}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\epsilon^{\frac{3}{7}}\|(m^{-1}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(m^{-1}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\|_{L^{2}}\|(m^{-1}q_{0},q_{1},q_{2})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq o(1)\epsilon^{\frac{3}{7}}\|(m^{-1}\mathcal{P},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\epsilon^{-\frac{3}{7}}\|m^{-1}q_{0},q_{1},q_{2})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Absorbing the first term on the right hand side to the left gives (
3.39
) and (
3.40
).

Next we estimate the density and divergence part of the solution. For this, we denote the vorticity
by

\omega=\partial_{y}\mathcal{U}-ik\mathcal{V}

and the divergence of
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
by
\mathcal{D}=\text{div}_{k}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
. The diffusion in (
3.38
) can be expressed in terms of
\mathcal{D}
and
\omega
:

\Delta_{k}\mathcal{U}=\partial_{y}\omega+ik\mathcal{D},~\Delta_{k}\mathcal{V}=-ik\omega+\partial_{y}\mathcal{D}.

Thus we can rewrite the momentum equations in (
3.38
) as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &ikm^{-2}\mathcal{P}=\epsilon\partial_{y}\omega+\epsilon(1+\lambda)ik\mathcal{D}-ik(U_{s}-c)\mathcal{U}-q_{1}-\epsilon U^{\prime\prime}_{s}\mathcal{P},\\
&m^{-2}\partial_{y}\mathcal{P}=-\epsilon ik\omega+\epsilon(1+\lambda)\partial_{y}\mathcal{D}-ik(U_{s}-c)\mathcal{V}-q_{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.47)

By taking inner product of the first and second equations with
\overline{ik{\mathcal{P}}}
and
\partial_{y}\bar{\mathcal{P}}
respectively, we obtain:

\displaystyle m^{-2}

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{y}\mathcal{P},k\mathcal{P}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\underbrace{\epsilon\int^{1}_{-1}\partial_{y}\omega\overline{ik{\mathcal{P}}}-ik\omega\partial_{y}\bar{\mathcal{P}}{\rm d}y}_{J_{4}}+\underbrace{\epsilon(1+\lambda)\int^{1}_{-1}k^{2}\mathcal{D}\bar{\mathcal{P}}+\partial_{y}\mathcal{D}\partial_{y}\bar{\mathcal{P}}{\rm d}y}_{J_{5}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int^{1}_{-1}(q_{1}+\epsilon U^{\prime\prime}_{s}\mathcal{P})ik\bar{\mathcal{P}}-q_{2}\partial_{y}\bar{\mathcal{P}}{\rm d}y}_{J_{6}}+\underbrace{\int^{1}_{-1}ik(U_{s}-c)(ik\bar{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{U}-\mathcal{V}\partial_{y}\bar{\mathcal{P}}){\rm d}y}_{J_{7}}.

(3.48)

Integrating by parts and using the boundary condition
\omega|_{y=\pm 1}=0
give

\displaystyle J_{4}=-ik\epsilon\bar{\mathcal{P}}\omega\big|_{y=-1}^{y=1}=0.

(3.49)

From the continuity equation
\eqref{A2.1}_{1}
, it holds that

\mathcal{D}=-ik(U_{s}-c)\mathcal{P}+q_{0},

and
\partial_{y}\mathcal{D}=-ik(U_{s}-c)\partial_{y}\mathcal{P}-ikU^{\prime}_{s}\mathcal{P}+\partial_{y}q_{0}.
Plugging these into
J_{5}
, we obtain

\displaystyle J_{5}=

\displaystyle-\epsilon(1+\lambda)\int^{1}_{-1}ik(U_{s}-c)(|\partial_{y}\mathcal{P}|^{2}+k^{2}|\mathcal{P}|^{2}){\rm d}y

\displaystyle-ik\epsilon(1+\lambda)\int^{1}_{-1}U^{\prime}_{s}\mathcal{P}\partial_{y}\mathcal{P}{\rm d}y+\epsilon(1+\lambda)\int^{1}_{-1}\left(\partial_{y}\bar{\mathcal{P}}\partial_{y}q_{0}+k^{2}q_{0}\bar{\mathcal{P}}\right){\rm d}y.

(3.50)

By Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequalities, the last two terms on the right hand side of

are bounded by

\bigg|ik\epsilon(1+\lambda)\int^{1}_{-1}U^{\prime}_{s}\mathcal{P}\partial_{y}\mathcal{P}dy\bigg|\leq C\epsilon\|(\partial_{y}\mathcal{P},k\mathcal{P})\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

and

\displaystyle\bigg|\epsilon(1+\lambda)\int^{1}_{-1}\partial_{y}\bar{\mathcal{P}}\partial_{y}q_{0}+k^{2}q_{0}\bar{\mathcal{P}}dy\bigg|\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon\|(\partial_{y}\mathcal{P},k\mathcal{P})\|_{L^{2}}\|(\partial_{y}q_{0},kq_{0})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{m^{-2}}{8}\|(\partial_{y}\mathcal{P},k\mathcal{P})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+Cm^{2}\epsilon^{2}\|(\partial_{y}q_{0},kq_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

Therefore, by taking real part in (
3.50
), we deduce

\displaystyle\text{Re}J_{5}

\displaystyle\leq-\epsilon k\text{Im}c(1+\lambda)\|(\partial_{y}\mathcal{P},k\mathcal{P})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C\bigg(\epsilon+\frac{m^{-2}}{8}\bigg)\|(\partial_{y}\mathcal{P},k\mathcal{P})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+Cm^{2}\epsilon^{2}\|(\partial_{y}q_{0},kq_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m^{-2}}{4}\|(\partial_{y}\mathcal{P},k\mathcal{P})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+Cm^{2}\epsilon^{2}\|(\partial_{y}q_{0},kq_{0})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

(3.51)

Moreover, by Cauchy-Schwarz and Young’s inequalities again, it holds that

\displaystyle|J_{6}|+|J_{7}|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\|q_{1},q_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+k\|(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\|_{L^{2}}\bigg)\|(\partial_{y}\mathcal{P},k\mathcal{P})\|_{L^{2}}+C\epsilon^{\frac{6}{7}}\|k\mathcal{P}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{m^{-2}}{4}\|(\partial_{y}\mathcal{P},k\mathcal{P})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+Cm^{2}\bigg(\|(q_{1},q_{2})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+k^{2}\|(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\bigg).

(3.52)

By combining the estimates (
3.49
), (
3.51
) and (
3.52
) for
J_{4}
to
J_{7}

), we obtain that

\displaystyle m^{-2}\|(\partial_{y}\mathcal{P},k\mathcal{P})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(q_{1},q_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+C\epsilon\|(\partial_{y}q_{0},kq_{0})\|_{L^{2}}+Ck\|(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{7}}\|(m^{-1}q_{0},q_{1},q_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+C\epsilon\|\partial_{y}q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.53)

Here we have used (
3.39
) for
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
in the last line of (
3.2
).
For the divergence, it holds from

\displaystyle\|\text{div}_{k}(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{-\frac{2}{7}}\|(m^{-1}q_{0},q_{1},q_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\partial_{y}q_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

Putting bounds (

) and (

) together gives (

) with

3.3
Solvability of resolvent problem

In this subsection, we solve the resolvent problem (
3.1
) by alternatively iterating the above Quasi-compressible
\mathfrak{Q}
and Stokes operators
\mathfrak{S}.
First of all, we construct the solution
(\rho,u,v)
to (
3.1
) for any given inhomogeneous source terms
f_{u},f_{v}\in L^{2}
, and show the estimate (
3.2
). At the zeroth step, we introduce the Stokes solution
\vec{\Xi}_{0}=(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathcal{U}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{0})
, which solves the following system:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathcal{U}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{0})=(0,f_{u},f_{v}).

(3.58)

Recall the error operator
\vec{E}_{\mathfrak{S}}
defined in (
3.57
). The error generated at this step is

\displaystyle\vec{E}_{\mathfrak{S}}(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathcal{U}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{0})=\left(0,\mathcal{V}_{0}U_{s}^{\prime},0\right).

(3.59)

Since
\vec{E}_{\mathfrak{S}}(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathcal{U}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{0})\in H^{2}(-1,1)
, we can introduce the solution
(\varrho_{1},\mathfrak{u}_{1},\mathfrak{v}_{1})
to the quasi-compressible system:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Q}(\varrho_{1},\mathfrak{u}_{1},\mathfrak{v}_{1})=-\vec{E}_{\mathfrak{S}}(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathcal{U}_{0},\mathcal{V}_{0}).

(3.60)

The error term generated by
\mathfrak{Q}
is

\displaystyle\vec{E}_{\mathfrak{Q}}(\varrho_{1},\mathfrak{u}_{1},\mathfrak{v}_{1})=\left(0,\epsilon\Delta_{k}\left[(U_{s}-c)\varrho_{1}\right]-\epsilon\lambda ik\text{div}_{k}(\mathfrak{u}_{1},\mathfrak{v}_{1})+\epsilon U_{s}^{\prime\prime}\varrho_{1},\epsilon\lambda\partial_{y}\text{div}_{k}(\mathfrak{u}_{1},\mathfrak{v}_{1})\right).

(3.61)

In view of Corollary
3.1
, the right hand side of (
3.61
) is in
L^{2}(-1,1)
instead of
H^{1}(-1,1)
, which forbids us to iterate
\mathfrak{Q}
directly. In order to recover the regularity, we introduce
(\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{1})
as the solution to Stokes approximation

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{1})=-\vec{E}_{\mathfrak{Q}}.

(3.62)

Now we define the following corrector
\vec{\Xi}_{1}
at the Step 1:

\displaystyle\vec{\Xi}_{1}=(\rho_{1},u_{1},v_{1})\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}(\varrho_{1},\mathfrak{u}_{1},\mathfrak{v}_{1})+(\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{1}).

(3.63)

One can check that the error term generated at this step is

\displaystyle\vec{\mathcal{E}}_{1}

\displaystyle\overset{\mbox{\tiny{def}}}{=}\mathcal{L}(\vec{\Xi}_{0}+\vec{\Xi}_{1})-(0,f_{u},f_{v})

\displaystyle=\vec{E}_{\mathfrak{S}}(\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{1})=(0,\mathcal{V}_{1}U_{s}^{\prime},0).

(3.64)

Approximate solutions up to any order can be constructed by induction. Suppose that at
N

Proof.

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{-}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C,~\|\varphi_{-}^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\left|\log\text{Im}c\right|,~\|\varphi_{-}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C,

(5.9)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}^{j}\varphi_{-}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\left|\text{Im}c\right|^{-j+1},~j=2,3,4,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}^{j}\varphi_{-}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left|\text{Im}c\right|^{-j+\frac{3}{2}},~j=1,2,3,4.

Proof.

By differentiating (
2.7
) up to the fourth order, we obtain

\displaystyle\varphi_{-}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=U_{s}^{\prime}\int_{0}^{y}\frac{1}{(U_{s}-c)^{2}}{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{U_{s}-c}-m^{2}\left(yU_{s}^{\prime}+U_{s}-c\right),

(5.10)

\displaystyle\varphi_{-}^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=U_{s}^{\prime\prime}\int_{0}^{y}\frac{1}{(U_{s}-c)^{2}}{\rm d}x-m^{2}\left(yU_{s}^{\prime\prime}+2U_{s}^{\prime}\right),

\displaystyle\varphi_{-}^{\prime\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}}{(U_{s}-c)^{2}}-3m^{2}U_{s}^{\prime\prime},~\partial_{y}^{4}\varphi_{-}=-\frac{2U_{s}^{\prime}U_{s}^{\prime\prime}}{(U_{s}-c)^{3}}.

Here we have used
\partial_{y}^{j}U_{s}=0
when
j=3,4
. For
y\in\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
,
|U_{s}-c|\gtrsim 1
. Thus (
5.7
) follows from the explicit formula (
5.10
). For
y\in(-1,1)\setminus\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)
, as (
5.5
) we can obtain the following pointwise estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{y}\frac{1}{(U_{s}-c)^{2}}{\rm d}x=-\frac{1}{U_{s}^{\prime}(y)(U_{s}(y)-c)}-\log(U_{s}(y)-c)\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(y)}{(U_{s}^{\prime}(y))^{3}}+O(1).

(5.11)

Substituting (
5.11
) into the explicit formula (
5.10
), we obtain pointwise estimates (
5.8
).
The bounds (
5.9
) on
L^{2}
and
L^{\infty}
-norms can be obtained in the same way as in
[
36
]
and we omit the details for brevity. The proof of Lemma
5.2
is completed.
∎

Finally, we have the following lemma concerning about the weight function
w(y)
defined in (
3.12
). Set

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(y)=-(1-m^{2}U_{s}^{2})U_{s}^{\prime\prime}-2m^{2}U_{s}|U_{s}^{\prime}|^{2}.

(5.12)

Lemma 5.3
.

Let the Mach number
m\in\left(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)
. For sufficiently small
|c|\ll 1
, the weight function
w
has the following expansion

\displaystyle w(y)=w_{0}(y)+cw_{1}(y)+O(1)|c|^{2},

(5.13)

where

\displaystyle\ w_{0}

\displaystyle=(1-m^{2}U_{s}^{2})^{2}\mathcal{W}^{-1},

\displaystyle w_{1}

\displaystyle=4m^{2}U_{s}(1-m^{2}U_{s}^{2})\mathcal{W}^{-1}-2m^{2}(1-m^{2}U_{s}^{2})^{2}\left(|U_{s}^{\prime\prime}|U_{s}+|U_{s}^{\prime}|^{2}\right).

Moreover, there exists a positive constant
\gamma_{0}
, such that

\displaystyle w_{0}-U_{s}w_{1}\geq\gamma_{0}.

(5.14)

Proof.

We show that
\mathcal{W}(y)
has a strictly positive lower bound, hence
w_{0}
and
w_{1}
are well-defined. Then the expansion (
5.13
) follows from a straightforward computation. We take the plane Poiseuille flow
U_{s}(y)=1-y^{2}
as an example. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(y)=2(1-m^{2})+6m^{2}y^{2}\left(y^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\right).

Therefore,

\min_{y\in[-1,1]}\mathcal{W}(y)=\mathcal{W}(y)|_{y=\pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}}=2\left(1-\frac{4m^{2}}{3}\right),

which is positive when the Mach number
m\in\left(0,\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)
. For (
5.14
), following the same computation as (3.29) in
[
50
]
, one has
w_{0}-U_{s}w_{1}\sim 1-3m^{2}U_{s}^{2}
. Therefore,
w_{0}-U_{s}w_{1}
has a strictly positive lower bound when the Mach number
m\in\left(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\right)
. The proof of Lemma
5.3
is completed.
∎

Second Order Bismut formulae and applications to Neumann semigroups on manifolds
Li-Juan Cheng 1 , Anton Thalmaier 2 and Feng-Yu Wang 3

Abstract
. Let M M be a complete connected Riemannian manifold with boundary ∂ M \partial M , and let P t P_{t} be the Neumann semigroup generated by 1 2 ​ L \frac{1}{2}L where L = Δ + Z L=\Delta+Z for a C 1 C^{1} -vector field Z Z on M M . We establish Bismut type formulae for L ​ P t ​ f LP_{t}f and Hess P t ​ f {\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}f} and present estimates of these quantities under suitable curvature conditions. In case when P t P_{t} is symmetric in L 2 ​ ( μ ) L^{2}(\mu) for some probability measure μ \mu , a new type of log-Sobolev inequality is established which links the relative entropy H H , the Stein discrepancy S S , and relative Fisher information I I , generalizing the corresponding result of [ 9 ] in the case without boundary.

1.
Introduction

Consider a
d
-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold
M
,
possibly with non-empty boundary
\partial M
, and let
X_{t}
be
the reflecting diffusion process on
M
generated by
\frac{1}{2}L

where
L=\Delta+Z
; here
\Delta
is the Laplace-Beltrami
operator and
Z
a smooth vector field on

M
. According to
[
12
,
13
,
24
]
, the
reflecting diffusion process
X_{t}^{x}
starting at
x
can be
constructed as solution to the following SDE on
M
with
reflection:

\mathrm{d}X^{x}_{t}=/\!/_{t}\circ\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}+\frac{1}{2}Z(X^{x}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}N(X^{x}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}l_{t}^{x},\quad X_{0}^{x}=x,

(1.1)

where
B_{t}
is a standard Brownian motion on the tangent space

T_{x}M\equiv\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
/\!/_{t}\colon T_{x}M\rightarrow T_{X^{x}_{t}}M
the
stochastic parallel transport along
X_{t}^{x}
,
N
the inward
normal unit vector field on
\partial M
, and
l_{t}^{x}
the local
time of
X_{t}^{x}
on
\partial M
. Throughout this paper, we
assume that SDE (
1.1
) is non-explosive. Then the
Neumann semigroup
P_{t}
generated by
\frac{1}{2}L
is given by

P_{t}f(x)=\mathbb{E}\left[f(X_{t}^{x})\right],\quad t\geq 0,\ x\in M,\ f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)

where
\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)
denotes the set of bounded measurable functions on

M
.

To study the regularity of diffusion semigroups using tools from
stochastic analysis, Bismut
[
4
]
introduced his famous
probabilistic formula for the gradient of heat semigroups on
Riemannian manifolds without boundary. This type of formulae has been
studied in
[
11
,
20
,
10
]
using martingale arguments, and
been extended to second order derivatives in
[
1
,
11
,
17
,
18
,
20
,
23
,
15
]
.

In the case the boundary of
M
is non-empty, Bismut type formulae
have been derived in
[
24
,
8
]
for
the gradient of the Neumann semigroup
P_{t}
, see also
[
16
,
12
,
25
]
for gradient estimates. In this paper, we aim at
establishing Bismut type formulae for second order derivatives of the
Neumann semigroup, along with some geometric applications.

Let
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}:={\operatorname{Ric}}-\nabla Z
where
{\operatorname{Ric}}
is the Ricci curvature
tensor, and let
{\operatorname{II}}
be the second fundamental form of the boundary:

\displaystyle{\operatorname{II}}(X,Y)=-\left<\nabla_{X}N,Y\right>,\quad\text{$X,Y\in T_{x}\partial M,\ x\in\partial M$}.

A derivative formula for
P_{t}f
is given in
[
12
,
24
]
by
constructing an appropriate multiplicative functional. Throughout the
paper, we assume that the reflecting diffusion process generated by

L
is non-explosive, and that there exist functions
K\in C(M)
and

\sigma\in C(\partial M)
such that

{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}:={\operatorname{Ric}}-\nabla Z\geq K,\quad\ {\operatorname{II}}\geq\sigma,

(1.2)

i.e.

{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}(X,X)\geq K(x)|X|^{2}
for
x\in M,\ X\in T_{x}M
, and

{\operatorname{II}}(X,X)\geq\sigma(x)|X|^{2}
for
x\in\partial M,\ X\in T_{x}\partial M.
Under
the assumption that

(A)

the functions
K
and
\sigma
in (
1.2
) are constant,
\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{e}^{\sigma^{-}l_{t}}]<\infty
for any
t\geq 0
,

a Bismut type
formula for
\nabla P_{t}f
has been established in
[
24
]
for
f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)

such that
\nabla P_{\cdot}f
is bounded on
[0,t]\times M
. More precisely, there exists
a family of random homomorphisms

Q_{t}\colon T_{x}M\rightarrow T_{X_{t}^{x}}M
with the property that

\displaystyle|Q_{t}|\leq\operatorname{e}^{-Kt/2-\sigma l_{t}/2}\ \text{ and }\ \langle N(X_{t}^{x}),Q_{t}(v)\rangle\hbox{\kern 3.75pt\vrule height=6.75972pt,depth=-0.43057pt,width=0.50003pt\kern-3.75pt\rm 1}_{\{X_{t}^{x}\in\partial M\}}=0,\quad v\in T_{x}M,

such that

\displaystyle\nabla P_{t}f(v)=\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\nabla f(X_{t}^{x}),Q_{t}(v)\rangle\right],\quad v\in T_{x}M,

(1.3)

and

\displaystyle\nabla P_{t}f(v)=\mathbb{E}\left[f(X_{t}^{x})\int_{0}^{t}\langle h^{\prime}(s)Q_{s}(v),/\!/_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle\right],\quad v\in T_{x}M

(1.4)

for any choice of a non-negative
h\in C_{b}^{1}([0,t])
such that

h(0)=0,\ h(t)=1
.
When
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}
and
{\operatorname{II}}
are bounded from below, the second part of condition
(A)
holds if either
\partial M
is convex, or if
\partial M
has strictly positive injectivity radius, the sectional curvature of
M
being bounded above and
Z
bounded, see
[
24
, Section 3.2]
.

The aim of this paper is to extend (
1.3
) and
(
1.4
) to second order derivatives and to establish Bismut type formulae for
LP_{t}f
and
{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}f}:=\nabla{\bf d}P_{t}f
, along with some applications. When compared to the case without boundary as in
[
1
]
, the present study faces an essential new difficulty. Indeed, by formal calculations, the Bismut formula for second derivatives of
P_{t}f
includes
a stochastic integral of
Q_{t}^{-1}
, the inverse of the above mentioned multiplicative functional
Q_{t}
. However, in the present setting
Q_{t}
is singular near the boundary
so that existence of the desired stochastic integral poses a problem.

In Sections
2
, we derive a Bismut type formula for
LP_{t}f
also in terms of the multiplicative functional
Q_{t}
, which provides as consequence an upper estimate depending on the lower bounds of
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}
and
{\operatorname{II}}
, more precisely, condition
{\bf(A)}
and
\|Z\|_{\infty}<\infty
.
This estimate is new even in the case without boundary where the existing estimate in
[
23
]
depends on the uniform norm of
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}
.

In Section
3
, we establish a Bismut type
formula for
{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}f}
and use it for Hessian estimates of
P_{t}f
. Establishing formulas for the Hessian naturally requires more knowledge on the curvature of the manifold. Note that in
[
1
,
23
,
15
,
18
]
, the authors used information related to curvature and
its derivative to establish
a Hessian formula and deduced estimates in terms of these curvature conditions. When it comes to manifolds with boundary, it seems unavoidable to exploit geometric information concerning the boundary as well. Before going into the details, let us remark that the multiplicative functional
Q_{t}
in the derivative
formula (
1.3
) satisfies

\langle N(X_{t}),Q_{t}(v)\rangle\hbox{\kern 3.75pt\vrule height=6.75972pt,depth=-0.43057pt,width=0.50003pt\kern-3.75pt\rm 1}_{\{X_{t}\in\partial M\}}=0

which is reasonable since

\langle\nabla P_{T-t}f(X_{t}),\ N(X_{t})\rangle\hbox{\kern 3.75pt\vrule height=6.75972pt,depth=-0.43057pt,width=0.50003pt\kern-3.75pt\rm 1}_{\{X_{t}\in\partial M\}}=0.

It follows that to express
\nabla P_{t}f
on the boundary, information on the second fundamental
form

{\operatorname{II}}^{\sharp}(P_{\partial}(v))=-(\nabla_{P_{\partial}(v)}N)^{\sharp}

is sufficient. However, when it comes to the second order derivative of
P_{t}f
on the boundary, no condition like

{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{T-t}f}(N(X_{t}),\cdot)\hbox{\kern 3.75pt\vrule height=6.75972pt,depth=-0.43057pt,width=0.50003pt\kern-3.75pt\rm 1}_{\{X_{t}\in\partial M\}}=0

is satisfied,
which naturally demands for full information on
\nabla N
. This indicates that one not only needs to control
{\operatorname{II}}
but also
\nabla_{N}N
. For this reason, in Section
3
, two new
functionals
\tilde{Q}_{t}
and
W_{t}
are introduced in
(
3.3
) and (
3
) respectively, which our Bismut formula for
{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}f}
will be based on and which then allow to derive upper bounds.

In Section
5
, we apply the Hessian estimates of
P_{t}f

to prove inequalities connecting the relative entropy
H
, the Stein
discrepancy
S
, and the relative Fisher information
I
, which extend the corresponding results derived in our recent work
[
9
]
for
\partial M=\emptyset

to the case with boundary; see Ledoux, Nourdin and Peccati
[
14
]

for the earlier study in the Euclidean case
M=\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

2.
Bismut formula and estimate for
LP_{t}f

To state the main result, we first recall the construction of the multiplicative functional

Q_{t}
appearing in the Bismut formula, see
[
23
]
for the case without boundary.

For
t\geq 0
, let
/\!/_{0\to t}:T_{X_{0}}M\to T_{X_{t}}M
denote stochastic parallel transport along the paths of the reflecting diffusion process
X
.
The covariant differential
{\rm D}
in
t\geq 0
is defined as

{\rm D}:=/\!/_{0\to t}\,{\rm d}\,/\!/_{t\to 0}
where
{\rm d}
is the usual Itô stochastic differential in
t\geq 0
. For a process
v_{t}\in T_{X_{t}}M
we then have

{\rm D}v_{t}=/\!/_{0\to t}\,{\rm d}\,/\!/_{t\to 0}\,v_{t},\quad t\geq 0.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\geq 0
, let

Q^{(n)}_{t}\colon T_{X_{0}}M\rightarrow T_{X_{t}}M
solve the
covariant differential equation:

{\rm D}Q_{t}^{(n)}=-\frac{1}{2}\left\{{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}^{\sharp}(Q_{t}^{(n)})\,\mathrm{d}t+{\operatorname{II}}^{\sharp}(Q_{t}^{(n)})\,\mathrm{d}l_{t}+nP_{N}(Q_{t}^{(n)})\,\mathrm{d}l_{t}\right\},\quad t\geq 0,\ \ Q_{0}^{(n)}={\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits},

(2.1)

where
{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}
is the identity map on
T_{X_{0}}M
and
P_{N}
the projection operator onto the normal direction
N
of
\partial M
such that
when
X_{t}\in\partial D
,

P_{N}Q_{t}^{(n)}v=\langle Q_{t}^{(n)}v,N(X_{t})\rangle\,N(X_{t}),\quad v\in T_{X_{0}}M.

Furthermore for
P_{\partial}:T_{x}M\to T_{x}\partial M
being the projection operator for
x\in\partial M
, let

\langle{\operatorname{II}}^{\sharp}(Q_{t}^{(n)})v_{1},v_{2}\rangle:={\operatorname{II}}(P_{\partial}Q_{t}^{(n)}v_{1},P_{\partial}v_{2}),\quad v_{1},v_{2}\in T_{X_{t}}M,\ X_{t}\in\partial M.

By the curvature conditions (
1.2
), we then have

\sup_{n\geq 1}|Q_{t}^{(n)}|\leq\operatorname{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}K(X_{s})\,{\rm d}s-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(X_{s})\,{\rm d}l_{s}},\quad t\geq 0,

(2.2)

\int_{0}^{t}|P_{N}(Q_{s}^{(n)})|^{2}\,{\rm d}l_{s}\leq\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{t}|Q_{s}^{(n)}|^{2}\big\{K^{-}(X_{s})\,{\rm d}s+\sigma^{-}(X_{s})\,{\rm d}l_{s}\big\}\to 0\ \text{ as}\ n\to\infty.

(2.3)

Define

\{Q_{t}^{(n)}\}^{-1}:=/\!/_{t\to 0}\{Q_{t}^{(n)}/\!/_{t\to 0}\}^{-1}:\ T_{X_{t}}M\to T_{x}M

(2.4)

where
\{Q_{t}^{(n)}/\!/_{t\to 0}\}^{-1}
is the inverse of the operator

Q_{t}^{(n)}/\!/_{t\to 0}:\ T_{X_{t}}M\to T_{X_{t}}M.

To show that
\{Q_{t}^{(n)}\}^{-1}
exists, let

\tau_{k}:=\inf\big\{t\geq 0:\ \rho_{o}(X_{t})\geq k\big\},\ \ k\geq 1,

where
\rho_{o}
is the Riemannian distance to a reference point
o\in M
. Fix
T>0
. By
[
24
, Lemma 3.1.2]
, we have

\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{e}^{\lambda l_{T\land\tau_{k}}}]<\infty,\ \ \lambda>0.

(2.5)

Since
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}
and
{\operatorname{II}}
are locally bounded, (
2.1
) and (
2.5
) imply that
Q_{t}^{(n)}/\!/_{t\to 0}
is invertible with

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T\land\tau_{k}]}\big|\{Q_{t}^{(n)}\}^{-1}\big|^{p}\ \bigg]=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T\land\tau_{k}]}\big|\{Q_{t}^{(n)}/\!/_{t\to 0}\}^{-1}\big|^{p}\bigg]<\infty,\ \ p,k\geq 1.

(2.6)

To derive a Bismut formula for
LP_{t}f
, we need to estimate the martingales

M_{t}^{(h,n)}:=\int_{0}^{t}\bigg\langle h_{s}Q_{s}^{(n)}\int_{0}^{s}h_{r}\{Q_{r}^{(n)}\}^{-1}/\!/_{r}\mathrm{d}B_{r},\,/\!/_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}\bigg\rangle,\ \ n\geq 1

(2.7)

for a reference adapted real process
h
. When
M
is compact,
[
12
, Theorem 3.4]
implies that
as
n\rightarrow\infty
the process
Q_{t}^{(n)}
converges in
L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
to an
adapted right-continuous process
Q_{t}
with left-limits such that

P_{N}Q_{t}=0
if
X_{t}\in\partial M
. This construction has been extended in

[
24
, Proof of Theorem 3.2.1]
to non-compact manifolds. However, although
\{Q_{t}^{(n)}\}^{-1}
exists for every
n\geq 1
,

Q_{t}
is not invertible on the boundary since
P_{N}Q_{t}=0.
Hence a priori,
existence of the stochastic integral

\int_{0}^{t}\bigg\langle h_{s}Q_{s}\int_{0}^{s}h_{r}Q_{r}^{-1}/\!/_{r}\mathrm{d}B_{r},\,/\!/_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}\bigg\rangle

is not obvious.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,T\land\tau_{k}]}\Big(|\xi_{s}|^{2}\operatorname{e}^{-\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,{\rm d}r-\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,{\rm d}l_{r}}\Big)^{\alpha}\right]+\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}h_{s}^{2}\,{\rm d}s\right)^{\alpha}+c_{2}\mathbb{E}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}h_{s}^{2}\,{\rm d}s\bigg)^{\alpha}.

Together with (
2.10
) this implies

G:=\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\bigg(|\xi_{s}|^{2}\operatorname{e}^{-\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,{\rm d}r-\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,{\rm d}l_{r}}\bigg)^{\alpha}\right]<\infty.

On the other hand, by Burkholder-Davis-Gundy’s inequality, there exists a constant
c_{3}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\big|M_{T}^{(h,n)}\big|^{\frac{2\alpha}{1+\alpha}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq c_{3}\mathbb{E}\left[\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}|\xi_{s}|^{2}\operatorname{e}^{-\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,{\rm d}r-\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,{\rm d}l_{r}}h_{s}^{2}\operatorname{e}^{\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,{\rm d}r+\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,{\rm d}l_{r}}\,{\rm d}s\bigg)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq c_{3}{G}^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}h_{s}^{2}\operatorname{e}^{\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,{\rm d}r+\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,{\rm d}l_{r}}\,{\rm d}s\right)^{\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha}}<\infty,\ \ n\geq 1.

Thus
\{M_{T}^{(h,n)}\}_{n\geq 1}
is bounded in
L^{\frac{2\alpha}{1+\alpha}}(\mathbb{P})
, and hence has a subsequence converging weakly to a random variable
M_{T}^{h}
in
L^{\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}}(\mathbb{P})
.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Let

and

such that

)
holds.
For

and

(2.15)

Then, for any

where

(2.17)

Proof.

(1)
We first assume

, the class of functions

such that

In this case, we have the Kolmogorov equations (see

\partial_{t}P_{T-t}f=-LP_{T-t}f=-P_{T-t}Lf,\ \ NP_{t}f|_{\partial M}=0,\ \ t\in[0,T].

(2.18)

In the sequel, we write for simplicity

Furthermore, we write

for two processes
A_{t}
and
B_{t}
if the difference

By Itô’s formula and (

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}M_{t}

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla(LP_{T-t}f)(X_{t}),/\!/_{t}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}\rangle+\partial_{t}(LP_{T-t}f)(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}L(LP_{T-t}f)(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}N(LP_{T-t}f)(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}l_{t}

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla(LP_{T-t}f)(X_{t}),/\!/_{t}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}N(P_{T-t}Lf)(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}l_{t}=\langle\nabla(LP_{T-t}f)(X_{t}),/\!/_{t}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}\rangle,\ \ \ t\in[0,T].

Then

{\rm d}(M_{t}\tilde{h}_{t}^{2})=\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}\,{\rm d}M_{t}+2\tilde{h}_{t}(\tilde{h}_{t})^{\prime}M_{t}\,{\rm d}t=\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}\,{\rm d}M_{t}+2\tilde{h}_{t}h_{t}M_{t}\,{\rm d}t,

\displaystyle(LP_{T-t}f)(X_{t})\,\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}-LP_{T}f(x)\overset{\text{\tiny m}}{=}2\int_{0}^{t}LP_{T-s}f(X_{s})\tilde{h}_{s}h_{s}\mathrm{d}s.

(2.19)

With

\displaystyle-(LP_{T-t}f)(X_{t})=\left\{{\bf d}^{*}({\bf d}P_{T-t}f)-({\bf d}P_{T-t}f)(Z)\right\}(X_{t}).

(2.20)

Combined with (
2.19
) this further yields

\displaystyle(LP_{T-t}f)(X_{t})\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}-LP_{T}f(x)

\displaystyle\overset{\rm m}{=}2\int_{0}^{t}LP_{T-s}f(X_{s})\tilde{h}_{s}h_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=-2\int_{0}^{t}{\bf d}^{*}({\bf d}P_{T-s}f)\tilde{h}_{s}h_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s+2\int_{0}^{t}({\bf d}P_{T-s}f)(Z)\tilde{h}_{s}h_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s.

(2.21)

Let
Q_{t}^{(n)*}\colon T_{X_{t}}M\to T_{x}M

.
By Itô’s formula and the Weitzenböck formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left\{({\bf d}P_{T-t}f)(X_{t})Q_{t}^{(n)}\right\}=\big(\nabla_{/\!/_{t}\mathrm{d}B_{t}}{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(X_{t})\big)(Q^{(n)}_{t})+\left\{{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{T-t}f}(N_{t},N_{t})\,Q_{t}^{(n)*}N_{t}\right\}(X_{t})\,\mathrm{d}l_{t}.

and using Itô’s formula, we derive

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}{\bf d}^{*}({\bf d}P_{T-s}f)\tilde{h}_{s}h_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\tiny m}}{=}-\big({\bf d}P_{T-t}f\big)\bigg(Q^{(n)}_{t}\tilde{h}_{t}\int_{0}^{t}h_{s}(Q^{(n)}_{s})^{-1}/\!/_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{T-s}f}(N_{s},N_{s})\Big\langle N_{s},\ \tilde{h}_{s}Q^{(n)}_{s}\int_{0}^{s}h_{r}(Q^{(n)}_{r})^{-1}/\!/_{r}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}l_{s}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}({\bf d}P_{T-s}f)\Big(h_{s}Q^{(n)}_{s}\int_{0}^{s}h_{r}(Q^{(n)}_{r})^{-1}/\!/_{r}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s.

To deal with the last term of the above equation and the second term on the right-hand side of (
2
), we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\big({\bf d}P_{T-s}f\big)(Z_{s})\tilde{h}_{s}h_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\overset{\text{\tiny m}}{=}P_{T-t}f(X_{t})\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{h}_{s}h_{s}Z_{s},\ /\!/_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}({\bf d}P_{T-s}f)\Big(Q_{s}^{(n)}h_{s}\int_{0}^{s}h_{r}\{Q^{(n)}_{r}\}^{-1}/\!/_{r}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s\overset{\text{\tiny m}}{=}P_{T-t}f(X_{t})\int_{0}^{t}\Big\langle h_{s}Q_{s}^{(n)}\int_{0}^{s}h_{r}\{Q^{(n)}_{r}\}^{-1}/\!/_{r}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r},/\!/_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}\Big\rangle.

Combining these equations with (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(LP_{T-s}f)(X_{s})\tilde{h}_{s}h_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s-{\bf d}P_{T-t}f\left(Q_{t}^{(n)}\tilde{h}_{t}\int_{0}^{t}h_{s}(Q_{s}^{(n)})^{-1}/\!/_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}\right)(X_{t})

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{T-s}f}(N_{s},N_{s})\Big\langle N_{s},\ Q_{s}^{(n)}\tilde{h}_{s}\int_{0}^{s}h_{r}(Q_{r}^{(n)})^{-1}/\!/_{r}\mathrm{d}B_{r}\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}l_{s}

The last equation and Eq. (

\begin{split}&(LP_{T-t}f)(X_{t})\tilde{h}_{t}^{2}-LP_{T}f(x)-2{\bf d}P_{T-t}f\left(Q_{t}^{(n)}\tilde{h}_{t}\int_{0}^{t}h_{s}(Q_{s}^{(n)})^{-1}/\!/_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}\right)\\

(2.23)

To get rid of the local martingales in the above calculations, we consider the diffusion process up to exit times from bounded balls. Since

) for

To state the main result of this Section, we first introduce some curvature conditions.
For
x\in M
and
v_{1}\in T_{x}M
, let
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}^{\sharp}(v_{1})\in T_{x}M
be given by

\langle{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}^{\sharp}(v_{1}),v_{2}\rangle:={\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}(v_{1},v_{2})={\operatorname{Ric}}(v_{1},v_{2})-\langle\nabla_{v_{1}}Z,v_{2}\rangle,\quad v_{2}\in T_{x}M.

Let
R
denote the Riemann curvature tensor. Then
{\bf d}^{*}R(v_{1})
is the linear operator on
T_{x}M
determined by

\displaystyle\langle{\bf d}^{*}R(v_{1},v_{2}),v_{3}\rangle=\langle(\nabla_{v_{3}}{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp})(v_{1}),v_{2}\rangle-\langle(\nabla_{v_{2}}{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp})(v_{3}),v_{1}\rangle,\ \ v_{3}\in T_{x}M,

where we write
{\bf d}^{*}R(v_{1},v_{2})\equiv{\bf d}^{*}R(v_{1})v_{2}
.
Moreover, let
R(v_{1}):T_{x}M\otimes T_{x}M\to T_{x}M
be given by

\langle R(v_{1})(v_{2},v_{3}),v_{4}\rangle:=\langle R(v_{1},v_{2})v_{3},v_{4}\rangle,\ \ v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\in T_{x}M.

Finally, let
|\cdot|
be the operator norm on tensors, and
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
be the uniform norm of
|\cdot|
over
M
.

Assume that there exist two functions
K\in C(M)
and

\sigma\in C(\partial M)
such that

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}:={\operatorname{Ric}}-\nabla Z\geq K,\quad\ -\nabla N\geq\sigma,

(3.1)

where the second condition means
-\langle\nabla_{X}N,X\rangle\geq\sigma(x)|X|^{2}
for
x\in\partial M
and
X\in T_{x}M
.
Moreover, assume that there exist three non-negative functions
\alpha,\,\beta
and
\gamma
, such that

\displaystyle|R|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}(x)\leq\alpha(x),\quad|{\bf d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}^{\sharp}-R(Z)|(x)<\beta(x),\quad|\nabla(\nabla N)^{\sharp}+R(N)|(x)<\gamma(x),

(3.2)

where for
x\in M
and
v_{1},v_{2}\in T_{x}M
,

\displaystyle|R|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}(x)=\sup\left\{|R^{\sharp,\sharp}(v_{1},v_{2})|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}(x):v_{1},v_{2}\in T_{x}M,\,|v_{1}|\leq 1,\,|v_{2}|\leq 1\right\}.

To establish the Hessian formula for
P_{t}f
, we first introduce an operator
\tilde{Q}_{t}:T_{x}M\rightarrow T_{X_{t}}M
defined by

\displaystyle{\rm D}\tilde{Q}_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}^{\sharp}(\tilde{Q}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla N)^{\sharp}(\tilde{Q}_{t})\,\mathrm{d}l_{t},\quad\tilde{Q}_{0}={\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}.

(3.3)

Then let the operator-valued process
W^{\tilde{h}}_{t}\colon T_{x}M\otimes T_{x}M\rightarrow T_{X_{t}(x)}M
be defined as
solutions to the following covariant Itô equation

\displaystyle{\rm D}W^{\tilde{h}}_{t}(v,v)

\displaystyle=R(/\!/_{t}\mathrm{d}B_{t},\tilde{Q}_{t}(\tilde{h}(t)v))\tilde{Q}_{t}(v)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}({\bf d}^{*}R-R(Z)+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z})^{\sharp}(\tilde{Q}_{t}(\tilde{h}(t)v),\tilde{Q}_{t}(v))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla^{2}N-R(N))^{\sharp}(\tilde{Q}_{t}(\tilde{h}(t)v),\tilde{Q}_{t}(v))\,\mathrm{d}l_{t}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}^{\sharp}(W_{t}^{\tilde{h}}(v,v))\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla N)^{\sharp}(W_{t}^{\tilde{h}}(v,v))\,\mathrm{d}l_{t}

with initial condition
W_{0}^{\tilde{h}}(v,v)=0
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
D
be an open relatively compact subset of
M
,
T>0
and
x\in D
.
Suppose that (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) hold. Let
h(\cdot)

be an adapted and bounded real process such that
\int_{0}^{t}h_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s=-1
for
t\geq T\wedge\tau_{D}
, and such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{D}}\left(h^{2}_{s}+\tilde{h}^{2}_{s}(\alpha^{2}(X_{s})+\beta^{2}(X_{s}))\right)\operatorname{e}^{\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{D}}\tilde{h}^{2}_{s}\gamma^{2}(X_{s})\operatorname{e}^{\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}}\,\mathrm{d}l_{s}\Bigg]<\infty

(3.4)

where
\tilde{h}(t)=1+\int_{0}^{t}h_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s
.

Then for

f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)
and
v\in T_{x}M
,

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{T}f}(v,v)(x)=

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[f(X_{T})\int_{0}^{T}\langle W_{s}^{\tilde{h}}(v,h_{s}v),/\!/_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[f(X_{T})\left(\left(\int_{0}^{T}\langle\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v),/\!/_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle\right)^{2}-\int_{0}^{T}|\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\right].

Moreover,

\displaystyle|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{T}f}|

\displaystyle\leq 3\|f\|_{\infty}C(h)^{1/2}\Bigg\{(3+\sqrt{10})\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{D}}\alpha^{2}(X_{s})\operatorname{e}^{\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}}\tilde{h}_{s}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{D}}\beta^{2}(X_{s})\operatorname{e}^{\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}}\tilde{h}_{s}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{D}}\gamma^{2}(X_{s})\operatorname{e}^{\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}}\tilde{h}_{s}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}l_{s}\right)\right]^{1/2}+\frac{2}{3}C(h)^{1/2}\Bigg\},

(3.5)

where
C(h)
is defined as in (
2.8
).

Remarks 3.2
.

1) The original idea of the proof for the Hessian formula comes from Elworthy-Li
[
11
]
and Thalmaier
[
1
]
.
Our form of the formula is consistent with
[
5
]
with the choice of one random test function
h
only. The main difficulty here is to deal with
the impact of the boundary and to weaken the conditions on the curvature and the process
h
. Theorem
3.1
also improves the results in
[
1
,
5
]
and gives a new estimates even when the boundary is empty.

2) Let
D
be an open relatively compact subset of
M
. Assume that
h
is an adapted and non-positive process with
h_{s}=0
for
s\geq T\wedge\tau_{D}
and
\int_{0}^{T}h_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s=-1
, which imply
\tilde{h}_{s}=0
for
s\geq T\wedge\tau_{D}
. Then the functions
K
,
\sigma
,
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\gamma
are all bounded on
D
and
|\tilde{h}_{s}|\leq 1
. Moreover, condition (
3.1
) can be simplified to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T}h_{s}^{2}\operatorname{e}^{\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}}\,\mathrm{d}s\right]<\infty.

The corresponding result is then the local version of the Hessian formula for the heat semigroup.

3) As
-\langle\nabla_{N}N,N\rangle=0
,
we know that
-\nabla N\geq\sigma
implies
\sigma\leq 0
.

4) Assume
{\operatorname{II}}\geq\sigma_{1}
and
|\nabla_{N}N|\leq\sigma_{2}
. Then for
X\in T_{x}M
and
x\in\partial M
,

X=X_{1}+X_{2}
such that
X_{1}\in T_{x}\partial M
and
X_{2}=\langle X,N\rangle N
,

\displaystyle-\langle\nabla_{X}N,X\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\nabla_{X_{1}}N,X_{1}\rangle-\langle X,N\rangle^{2}\langle\nabla_{N}N,N\rangle-\langle X,N\rangle\langle\nabla_{X_{1}}N,N\rangle-\langle X,N\rangle\langle\nabla_{N}N,X_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\nabla_{X_{1}}N,X_{1}\rangle-\langle X,N\rangle\langle\nabla_{N}N,X_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\sigma_{1}|X_{1}|^{2}-\sigma_{2}|X_{1}|\cdot|\langle X,N\rangle|\geq\min\left\{\sigma_{1},\,-\frac{\sigma_{2}}{2}\right\}|X|^{2}.

In particular, if
\nabla_{N}N=0
and
{\operatorname{II}}\geq\sigma
, then it is easy to see that for
x\in\partial M
,

-\langle\nabla_{X}N,X\rangle(x)\geq-\sigma^{-}|X|^{2},\ \ \ \ \mbox{for}\ \ X\in T_{x}M\ \mbox{and}\ x\in\partial M.

5) Naturally, one might try to work with
Q_{t}
instead of
\tilde{Q}_{t}
to define
M_{t}(v,v)
, in order to avoid the term
\nabla N
. But we have already seen that

Q_{t}
is the limit of
Q^{(n)}_{t}
, see the proof of
[
24
, Theorem 3.2.1]
, which satisfy the covariant Itô equation (
2.1
).
We have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\left(\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(Q_{t}^{(n)}(v),Q_{t}^{(n)}(v))(X_{t})\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\nabla_{/\!/_{t}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}}\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-t}f\right)\left(Q_{t}^{(n)}(v),Q_{t}^{(n)}(v)\right)-(\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-t}f)\left({\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}^{\sharp}(Q_{t}^{(n)}(v)),Q_{t}^{(n)}(v)\right)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-(\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-t}f)\left(Q_{t}^{(n)}(v),{\operatorname{II}}^{\sharp}(Q_{t}^{(n)}(v))\right)\,\mathrm{d}l_{t}+\partial_{t}(\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-t}f)(Q_{t}^{(n)}(v),Q_{t}^{(n)}(v))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{N}(\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-t}f)(Q_{t}^{(n)}(v),Q_{t}^{(n)}(v))\,\mathrm{d}l_{t}+\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{tr}\nabla^{2}+\nabla_{Z})(\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-t}f)(Q_{t}^{(n)}(v),Q_{t}^{(n)}(v))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-n\langle Q_{t}^{(n)}(v)),N(X_{t})\rangle\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(N(X_{t}),Q_{t}^{(n)}(v))\,\mathrm{d}l_{t}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{\tiny m}}{=}-n\langle Q_{t}^{(n)}(v)),N(X_{t})\rangle\,\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(Q_{t}^{(n)}(v),N)\,\mathrm{d}l_{t}

\displaystyle\quad-\langle Q_{t}^{(n)}(v)),N(X_{t})\rangle\,\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(\nabla_{N}N,Q_{t}^{(n)}(v))\,\mathrm{d}l_{t}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}({\bf d}P_{T-t}f)((\nabla(\nabla N)^{\sharp}+R(N))(Q^{(n)}_{t}(v),Q^{(n)}_{t}(v)))\,\mathrm{d}l_{t}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}({\bf d}P_{T-t}f)\big(({\bf d}^{*}R-R(Z)+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}^{\sharp})(Q^{(n)}_{t}(v),Q^{(n)}_{t}(v))\big)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(R^{\sharp,\sharp}(Q^{(n)}_{t}(v),Q^{(n)}_{t}(v)))\,\mathrm{d}t.

(3.6)

The main difficulty is to clarify the limit of

n\langle Q_{t}^{(n)}(v)),N(X_{t})\rangle\,\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(Q_{t}^{(n)}(v),N),

as
n
tends to
\infty
.
In addition, information on
\nabla_{N}N
is required to deal with the term

\langle Q_{t}^{(n)}(v)),N(X_{t})\rangle\,\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(\nabla_{N}N,Q_{t}^{(n)}(v))\,\mathrm{d}l_{t}.

To this end, we even need information concerning
\nabla N
on the full vector bundle of the boundary
if we use
Q_{t}
in the definition of
M_{t}(v,v)
in the above proof, and then it is still non-trivial to check the martingale property. In this respect, working with the functional
\tilde{Q}_{t}
instead of
Q_{t}
not only simplifies the calculation, it also doesn’t require additional conditions.

Lemma 3.3
.

Keeping the assumptions as in Theorem
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t}h_{s}\langle W_{s}^{\tilde{h}}(v,v),\,/\!/_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle\Big|\right]

\displaystyle\leq 3C(h)^{1/2}\Bigg\{(3+\sqrt{10})\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\alpha^{2}(X_{s})\operatorname{e}^{\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}}\tilde{h}_{s}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\beta^{2}(X_{s})\operatorname{e}^{\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r}\tilde{h}_{s}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\gamma^{2}(X_{s})\operatorname{e}^{\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}}\tilde{h}_{s}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}l_{s}\right)^{1/2}\Bigg\}.

Proof.

By the Lenglart inequality and the Minkowski inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\Big|\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{k}}h_{s}\langle W_{s}^{\tilde{h}}(v,v),\,/\!/_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle\Big|\right]

\displaystyle\leq 3\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}h_{s}^{2}|W_{s}^{\tilde{h}}(v,v)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 3\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}h_{s}^{2}\Big|\tilde{Q}_{s}\int_{0}^{s}\tilde{Q}_{r}^{-1}R(/\!/_{r}\mathrm{d}B_{r},\tilde{Q}_{r}(\tilde{h}(r)v))\tilde{Q}_{r}(v)\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{3}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}h_{s}^{2}\Big|\tilde{Q}_{s}\int_{0}^{s}\tilde{Q}_{r}^{-1}({\bf d}^{*}R-R(Z)+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z})^{\sharp}(\tilde{Q}_{r}(\tilde{h}(r)v),\tilde{Q}_{r}(v))\,\mathrm{d}r\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{3}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}h_{s}^{2}\Big|\tilde{Q}_{s}\int_{0}^{s}\tilde{Q}_{r}^{-1}(\nabla^{2}N-R(N))^{\sharp}(\tilde{Q}_{r}(\tilde{h}(r)v),\tilde{Q}_{r}(v))\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}\Big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/2}\right].

(3.8)

Let

\displaystyle\xi_{s}^{(1)}=\tilde{Q}_{s}\int_{0}^{s}\tilde{Q}_{r}^{-1}R(/\!/_{r}\mathrm{d}B_{r},\tilde{Q}_{r}(\tilde{h}(r)v))\tilde{Q}_{r}(v);

\displaystyle\xi_{s}^{(2)}=\tilde{Q}_{s}\int_{0}^{s}\tilde{Q}_{r}^{-1}({\bf d}^{*}R-R(Z)+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z})^{\sharp}(\tilde{Q}_{r}(\tilde{h}(r)v),\tilde{Q}_{r}(v))\,\mathrm{d}r;

\displaystyle\xi_{s}^{(3)}=\tilde{Q}_{s}\int_{0}^{s}\tilde{Q}_{r}^{-1}(\nabla^{2}N-R(N))^{\sharp}(\tilde{Q}_{r}(\tilde{h}(r)v),\tilde{Q}_{r}(v))\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}.

Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}h_{s}^{2}|\xi_{s}^{(1)}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\in[0,{T\wedge\tau_{k}}]}|\xi_{s}^{(1)}|^{2}\operatorname{e}^{-\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r-\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}}\right]^{1/2}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{k}}h_{s}^{2}\operatorname{e}^{\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}}\,\mathrm{d}s\right]^{1/2},

(3.9)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\Big|\operatorname{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}}\xi_{s}^{(1)}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{e}^{-\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r-\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}}\Big\langle R(/\!/_{s}\mathrm{d}B_{s},\tilde{Q}_{s}(\tilde{h}_{s}v))\tilde{Q}_{s}(v),\xi^{(1)}_{s}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\operatorname{e}^{-\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r-\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}}\big|R^{\sharp,\sharp}(\tilde{Q}_{s}(\tilde{h}_{s}v),\tilde{Q}_{s}(v))\big|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad-\operatorname{e}^{-\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r-\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}}{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}\big(\xi^{(1)}_{s},\xi^{(1)}_{s}\big)\,\mathrm{d}s-\operatorname{e}^{-\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r-\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}}\left\langle-\nabla_{\xi^{(1)}_{s}}N,\xi_{s}^{(1)}\right\rangle\,\mathrm{d}l_{s}

\displaystyle\quad+\operatorname{e}^{-\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r-\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}}\big|R^{\sharp,\sharp}(\tilde{Q}_{s}(\tilde{h}_{s}v),\tilde{Q}_{s}(v))\big|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha(X_{s})^{2}\operatorname{e}^{\int_{0}^{s}K^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{s}\sigma^{-}(X_{r})\,\mathrm{d}l_{r}}\tilde{h}_{s}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad s<\tau_{k},

\displaystyle{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(\tilde{Q}_{t}(\tilde{h}(t)v))={\bf d}P_{T}f(v)+\int_{0}^{t}(\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-s}f)(/\!/_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s},\tilde{Q}_{s}(\tilde{h}_{s}v))+\int_{0}^{t}{\bf d}P_{T-s}f(\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v))\,\mathrm{d}s.

Integration by parts yields that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-s}f)(\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v),\tilde{Q}_{s}(\tilde{h}_{s}v))\,\mathrm{d}s-{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(\tilde{Q}_{t}(\tilde{h}(t)v))\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v),/\!/_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}{\bf d}P_{T-s}f(\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v))\,\mathrm{d}s\,\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v)/\!/_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle

(3.27)

is also a local martingale. Concerning the last term in
(
3
), we note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}{\bf d}P_{T-s}f(\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v))\,\mathrm{d}s\,\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v),/\!/_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}{\bf d}P_{T-s}f(\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v))\left(\int_{0}^{s}\langle\tilde{Q}_{r}(h(r)v),/\!/_{r}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}\rangle\right)\,\mathrm{d}s

is a local martingale. Combining this with (
3
) we
conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-s}f)(\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v),\tilde{Q}_{s}(\tilde{h}_{s}v))\,\mathrm{d}s-{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(\tilde{Q}_{t}(\tilde{h}(t)v))\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v),/\!/_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}{\bf d}P_{T-s}f(\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v))\int_{0}^{s}\langle\tilde{Q}_{r}(h(r)v),/\!/_{r}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

(3.28)

is a local martingale.

Using the local martingales
(
3.26
) and (
3
) to replace the last two
terms in (
3.24
), we conclude that

\displaystyle(\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-t}f)(\tilde{Q}_{t}(\tilde{h}(t)v),\tilde{Q}_{t}(\tilde{h}(t)v))+({\bf d}P_{T-t}f)(W_{t}^{\tilde{h}}(v,\tilde{h}(t)v))

\displaystyle\quad-P_{T-t}f(X_{t})\int_{0}^{t}\langle W_{s}^{\tilde{h}}(v,h_{s}v),/\!/_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-2{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(\tilde{Q}_{t}(\tilde{h}(t)v))\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v),/\!/_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{0}^{t}{\bf d}P_{T-s}f(\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v))\int_{0}^{s}\langle\tilde{Q}_{r}(h(r)v),/\!/_{r}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

(3.29)

is a local martingale as well. On the other hand, by the product rule
for martingales, we have

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v),/\!/_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle\right)^{2}-\int_{0}^{t}|\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s=2\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\langle\tilde{Q}_{r}(h(r)v),/\!/_{r}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}\rangle\right)\langle\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v),/\!/_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle

(3.30)

which along with (
3.25
) implies that

\displaystyle P_{T-t}f(X_{t})\left(\left(\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v),/\!/_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle\right)^{2}-\int_{0}^{t}|\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle\quad-2\int_{0}^{t}{\bf d}P_{T-s}f(\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v))\int_{0}^{s}\langle\tilde{Q}_{r}(h(r)v),/\!/_{r}\,\mathrm{d}B_{r}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

is a local martingale. Applying this observation to
(
3

\displaystyle(\nabla{\bf d}P_{T-t}f)\big(\tilde{Q}_{t}(\tilde{h}(t)v),\tilde{Q}_{t}(\tilde{h}(t)v)\big)+({\bf d}P_{T-t}f)(W_{t}^{\tilde{h}}(v,\tilde{h}(t)v))

\displaystyle\ -2{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(\tilde{Q}_{t}(\tilde{h}(t)v))\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v),/\!/_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle\ -P_{T-t}f(X_{t})\int_{0}^{t}\langle W_{s}^{\tilde{h}}(v,h_{s}v),/\!/_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle\ +P_{T-t}f(X_{t})\left(\left(\int_{0}^{t}\langle\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v),/\!/_{s}\,\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle\right)^{2}-\int_{0}^{t}|\tilde{Q}_{s}(h_{s}v)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)

is a local martingale. This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Stein method and log-Sobolev inequality

In this section, we consider
L=\Delta-\nabla V
for
V\in C^{2}(M)
such that

\mu(\mathrm{d}x)=\operatorname{e}^{-V(x)}{\mbox{vol}}(\mathrm{d}x)

is a probability measure where
\mbox{vol}(\mathrm{d}x)
denotes the volume measure on
M
.
Let
P_{t}=\operatorname{e}^{\frac{1}{2}Lt}
be the contraction
semigroup generated by
L
on
L^{2}(\mu)
with Neumann boundary conditions.
In
[
9
]
, we used the Hessian formula to establish an HSI inequality on manifolds
without boundary, which contains
the new quantity called Stein discrepancy and in a certain sense
improves the classical log-Sobolev inequality.

To establish such kind of log-Sobolev inequalities on manifolds with boundary,
we first adapt
the definition of Stein kernel and Stein discrepancy to manifolds with boundary.
A symmetric 2-tensor
\tau_{\nu}\colon M\rightarrow T^{*}M\times T^{*}M
on
M
is said to be a Stein kernel for
a probability measure
\nu
on
M
if
\tau_{\nu}(v,w)\in L^{1}(\nu)

for every
v,w\in T_{x}M
,
x\in M
, and

\displaystyle\int\langle\nabla V,\nabla f\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\nu=\int\langle\tau_{\nu},{\operatorname{Hess}}f\rangle_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu,\quad f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{N}(L),

(5.1)

where
\nabla V
is the first order part of the operator
L

and where

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{N}(L)=\big\{f\in C^{\infty}(M)\colon Nf|_{\partial M}=0,\ Lf\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)\big\}.

Since

\int Lf\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{\partial M}N(f)\,\mathrm{d}\mu=0

for
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{N}(L)
, it is easy to see that the identity map
{{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}}
is a Stein kernel for
\mu
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\tau_{\nu}
be a Stein kernel for
\nu
. The Stein discrepancy is defined as

S(\nu\,|\,\mu)^{2}=\inf\int_{M}|\tau_{\nu}-{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu,

where the infimum is taken over all Stein kernels of
\nu
, and takes the value
+\infty
if no Stein kernel exists.

Let us first recall the classical log-Sobolev inequality on Riemannian manifolds when the boundary is convex.
Assume that

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}:={\operatorname{Ric}}+{\operatorname{Hess}}_{V}\geq K,\quad{\operatorname{II}}\geq 0

holds for some positive constant
K
. Then the classical logarithmic Sobolev
inequality with respect to the measure
\mu
indicates that for every
probability measure
\mathrm{d}\nu=h\,\mathrm{d}\mu
with smooth density
h\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{1}{2K}I(\nu\,|\,\mu),

(5.2)

where

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)=\int h\log h\,\mathrm{d}\mu={\operatorname{Ent}}_{\mu}(h)

is the relative entropy of
\mathrm{d}\nu=h\,\mathrm{d}\mu
with respect to
\mu
and

\displaystyle I(\nu\,|\,\mu)=\int\frac{|\nabla h|^{2}}{h}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=I_{\mu}(h)

the Fisher information of
\nu
(or
h
) with respect to
\mu
. This result is known as the Bakry-Émery criterion due to
[
2
]
for the logarithmic Sobolev
inequality. Let us recall the following observations.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume that

{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}\geq K\ \mbox{and}\quad{\operatorname{II}}\geq 0

for some positive constant

, and let
\tau_{\nu}

be a Stein kernel for
\mathrm{d}\nu=h\,\mathrm{d}\mu
where
h\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
.
For
t>0
let
\mathrm{d}\nu^{t}=P_{t}h\,\mathrm{d}\mu
. Then

(i)

(Integrated de Bruijn’s formula)

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)={\operatorname{Ent}}_{\mu}(h)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)\,\mathrm{d}t;

(ii)

(Exponential decay of Fisher information)

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)=I(\nu^{t}\,|\,\mu)\leq\operatorname{e}^{-Kt}I(\nu\,|\,\mu)=\operatorname{e}^{-Kt}I_{\mu}(h),\quad t\geq 0.

Proof.

Since
N(P_{t}f\log P_{t}f)=0
and
\left(\frac{1}{2}L-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)(P_{t}h\log P_{t}h)=\frac{|\nabla P_{t}h|^{2}}{2P_{t}h}
, we have

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}h\log h\,\mathrm{d}\mu=-\int_{M}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}\left(P_{t}h\log P_{t}h\right)}{\mathrm{d}t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{M}\Big(\frac{1}{2}L-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Big)(P_{t}h\log P_{t}h)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla P_{t}h|^{2}}{P_{t}h}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\,\mathrm{d}t.

The second assertion can be checked by observing first from the derivative formula that

\displaystyle|\nabla P_{t}h|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{e}^{-Kt}\left(P_{t}\big|\nabla(\sqrt{h}\,)^{2}\big|\right)^{2}\leq 4\operatorname{e}^{-Kt}(P_{t}h)\,P_{t}\big|\nabla\sqrt{h}\big|^{2}

which implies

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla P_{t}h|^{2}}{P_{t}h}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\leq 4\int_{M}\operatorname{e}^{-Kt}\frac{(P_{t}h)P_{t}|\nabla\sqrt{h}|^{2}}{P_{t}h}\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=4\int_{M}\operatorname{e}^{-Kt}P_{t}|\nabla\sqrt{h}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=4\operatorname{e}^{-Kt}\int_{M}|\nabla\sqrt{h}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle=\operatorname{e}^{-Kt}I(\nu\,|\,\mu)=\operatorname{e}^{-Kt}I_{\mu}(h).\qed

All expressions should be considered for
h+\varepsilon
as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
.
We continue our discussion under the condition that
\nabla^{2}N+R(N)=0

and
-\nabla N\geq 0
. The following assertions describe the
relationship between the relative entropy and Stein discrepancy.

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that
\alpha:=\|R\|_{\infty}<\infty
,
\beta:=\|\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}+\mathrm{d}^{*}R+R(\nabla V)\|_{\infty}<\infty
and

\nabla^{2}N+R(N)=0.

Let
\mathrm{d}\nu=h\,\mathrm{d}\mu
for
h\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
.

(i)

If

{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}\geq K,\ \nabla N\leq 0
,
then

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)\leq n^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{Kr}\,\mathrm{d}r}}+\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{K}}+\frac{\beta}{K}\right)^{2}\operatorname{e}^{-Kt}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu).

(ii)

If

{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}=K,\ \nabla N=0,

then

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)\leq\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{Kr}\,\mathrm{d}r}}+\frac{n\alpha}{\sqrt{K}}+\frac{n\beta}{K}\right)^{2}\operatorname{e}^{-Kt}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu).

Proof.

Let
g_{t}=\log P_{t}h
.
By the symmetry of
(P_{t})_{t\geq 0}
in
L^{2}(\mu)
,

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)=-\int(Lg_{t})P_{t}h\,\mathrm{d}\mu=-\int(LP_{t}g_{t})h\,\mathrm{d}\mu=-\int LP_{t}g_{t}\,\mathrm{d}\nu.

Hence according to the definition of Stein kernel and since
P_{t}g_{t}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{N}(L)
, we have

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)

\displaystyle=-\int\langle{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits},{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}g_{t}}\rangle_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu-\int\langle\nabla V,\nabla P_{t}g_{t}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\nu

\displaystyle=\int\langle\tau_{\nu}-{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits},{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}g_{t}}\rangle_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu.

This argument is due to
[
14
]
and connects the Fisher information
to the Stein discrepancy.
We now first prove assertion (i). By the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)

\displaystyle=\int\langle\tau_{\nu}-{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits},{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}g_{t}}\rangle_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int|\tau_{\nu}-{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right)^{1/2}\left(\int|{\operatorname{Hess}}_{P_{t}g_{t}}|^{2}_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq n\left(\int|\tau_{\nu}-{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{Kr}\,\mathrm{d}r}}+\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{K}}+\frac{\beta}{K}\right)\operatorname{e}^{-\frac{K}{2}t}\left(\int P_{t}|\nabla g_{t}|^{2}\ \mathrm{d}\nu\right)^{1/2}

where Corollary
4.2
is used for the function
g_{t}=\log P_{t}h
.
Since

\displaystyle\int P_{t}|\nabla g_{t}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu

\displaystyle=\int P_{t}|\nabla g_{t}|^{2}h\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int|\nabla g_{t}|^{2}P_{t}h\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int\frac{|\nabla P_{t}h|^{2}}{P_{t}h}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=I_{\mu}(P_{t}h),

it then follows that

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)\leq n^{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{Kr}\,\mathrm{d}r}}+\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{K}}+\frac{\beta}{K}\right)^{2}\operatorname{e}^{-Kt}\int|\tau_{\nu}-{\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits}|_{\text{\rm\tiny HS}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu.

Taking the infimum over all Stein kernels of
\nu
, we finish the proof of (i).
Along the same steps, item (ii) can be
proved by means of Corollary
4.2
as well.
∎

Using the lemmata above, we are now in position to establish the following result.

Theorem 5.4
.

Assume that
\alpha:=\|R\|_{\infty}<\infty,\ \beta:=\|\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}^{\sharp}+\mathrm{d}^{*}R+R(\nabla V)\|_{\infty}<\infty
and

\nabla^{2}N+R(N)=0.

Let
\mathrm{d}\nu=h\,\mathrm{d}\mu
with
h\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
.

(i)

If
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}\geq K,\ \nabla N\leq 0
, then

\displaystyle\quad H(\nu\,|\,\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2K}\left(n^{2}(1+\varepsilon)\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{K}}+\frac{\beta}{K}\right)^{2}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)\right)\wedge I(\nu\,|\,\mu)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{n^{2}}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)\ln\left(\frac{n^{2}(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon})KS^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{\Big(I(\nu\,|\,\mu)-n^{2}(1+\varepsilon)\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{K}}+\frac{\beta}{K}\Big)^{2}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)\Big)\vee 0+n^{2}(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon})KS^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right)

for every
\varepsilon>0
.
Moreover, if
\alpha=0
and
\beta=0
, then

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)\ln\left(1+\frac{I}{n^{2}KS^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right).

(ii)

If
{\operatorname{Ric}}_{V}=K,\ \nabla N=0
, then

\displaystyle\quad H(\nu\,|\,\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2K}\left(n^{2}(1+\varepsilon)\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{K}}+\frac{\beta}{K}\right)^{2}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)\right)\wedge I(\nu\,|\,\mu)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)\ln\left(\frac{(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon})KS^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{\Big(I(\nu\,|\,\mu)-n^{2}(1+\varepsilon)\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{K}}+\frac{\beta}{K}\Big)^{2}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)\Big)\vee 0+(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon})KS^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right)

for every
\varepsilon>0
.
Moreover, if
\alpha=\beta=0
, then

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{1}{2}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)\ln\left(1+\frac{I}{KS^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right).

Proof.

We only need to prove the first estimate. To this end, we write
I=I(\nu\,|\,\mu)
and
S=S(\nu\,|\,\mu)
for simplicity.
By Theorem
5.3
and Lemma
5.2
, we have

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\inf_{u>0}\left\{A\int_{0}^{u}\operatorname{e}^{-Kt}\,\mathrm{d}t+B\int_{u}^{\infty}\frac{K}{\operatorname{e}^{Kt}(\operatorname{e}^{Kt}-1)}\,\mathrm{d}t+C\int_{u}^{\infty}\operatorname{e}^{-Kt}\,\mathrm{d}t\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{u>0}\left\{\frac{A(1-\operatorname{e}^{-Ku})+C\operatorname{e}^{-Ku}}{K}+B\int_{0}^{\operatorname{e}^{-\alpha u}}\frac{r}{1-r}\,\mathrm{d}r\right\}

where

\displaystyle A=I(\nu\,|\,\mu);\ \ B=n^{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu);

\displaystyle C=n^{2}(1+\varepsilon)\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{K}}+\frac{\beta}{K}\right)^{2}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu).

It is easy to see that if
A\leq C
, then
\inf
is reached when
u
tends to
\infty
; if

A>C
, then
\inf
is reached for
\operatorname{e}^{\alpha u}=\frac{A-C+BK}{A-C}
so
that

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{C}{2K}+\frac{1}{2}B\ln\left(1+\frac{A-C}{BK}\right).

We conclude that

\displaystyle H(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{C\wedge A}{2K}+\frac{1}{2}B\ln\left(1+\frac{(A-C)\vee 0}{BK}\right).

The rest of the proof is the same replacing
B
by

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu).

The details are omitted here.
∎

Let
(M,g)
be a connected complete Riemannian manifold
M
. Considering the specific case that
{\operatorname{Hess}}_{V}=K>0
,
then by Obata’s Rigidity Theorem (see
[
19
, Theorem 2]
or
[
26
, Theorem 3.4]
),
M
is
isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The following corollary shows that the result is
consistent with Ledoux-Nourdin-Peccati
[
14
]
for the Gaussian measure on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
(M,g)
be a connected complete Riemannian manifold with boundary. Assume that
{\operatorname{Hess}}_{V}=K>0
,
\nabla N=0
, and
\nabla^{2}N=0
.
Let
\mathrm{d}\nu=h\,\mathrm{d}\mu
with
h\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M)
. Then,

\displaystyle I(\nu\,|\,\mu)\leq\frac{1}{2}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)\log\left(1+\frac{I(\nu\,|\,\mu)}{KS^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu)}\right).

Proof.

From the condition
{\operatorname{Hess}}_{V}=K>0
, we know that the manifold
M
is
isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.

\|R\|_{\infty}=0,

\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}}_{Z}=0
and
{\bf d}^{*}R=0
.
Then by Theorem
5.3
(ii),

\displaystyle I_{\mu}(P_{t}h)\leq\frac{K}{\operatorname{e}^{2Kt}-\operatorname{e}^{Kt}}S^{2}(\nu\,|\,\mu).

The assertion can be obtained by a same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
5.4
.
∎

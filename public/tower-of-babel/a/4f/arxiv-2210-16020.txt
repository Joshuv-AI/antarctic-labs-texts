Flows For The Masses: A multi-fluid non-linear perturbation theory for massive neutrinos
Yvonne Y. Y. Wong

Abstract
Velocity dispersion of the massive neutrinos presents a daunting challenge for non-linear cosmological perturbation theory. We consider the neutrino population as a collection of non-linear fluids, each with uniform initial momentum, through an extension of the Time Renormalization Group perturbation theory. Employing recently-developed Fast Fourier Transform techniques, we accelerate our non-linear perturbation theory by more than two orders of magnitude, making it quick enough for practical use. After verifying that the neutrino mode-coupling integrals and power spectra converge, we show that our perturbation theory agrees with N-body neutrino simulations to within 10 % 10\% for neutrino fractions Ω ν , 0 ​ h 2 ≤ 0.005 \Omega_{\nu,0}h^{2}\leq 0.005 up to wave numbers of k = 1 h / k=1~h/ Mpc, an accuracy consistent with ≤ 2.5 % \leq 2.5\% errors in the neutrino mass determination. Non-linear growth represents a > 10 % >10\% correction to the neutrino power spectrum even for density fractions as low as Ω ν , 0 ​ h 2 = 0.001 \Omega_{\nu,0}h^{2}=0.001 , demonstrating the limits of linear theory for accurate neutrino power spectrum predictions. Our code FlowsForTheMasses is avaliable online at github.com/upadhye/FlowsForTheMasses .

In this article, we construct an FFT-accelerated non-linear multi-fluid perturbation theory for massive neutrinos, which we call
FlowsForTheMasses

We generalize the bispectrum integrals of Time-RG, which quantify non-linear corrections to the power spectrum, to apply to a fluid with a uniform background velocity. Then we derive the equations of motion of these generalized bispectrum integrals, whose non-linear contribution, in turn, is sourced by generalized mode-coupling integrals.

Next, we proceed to a systematic study of these mode-coupling integrals. In ordinary Time-RG, assuming a power spectrum tracked using
N_{k}\sim 100

{\mathcal{O}}(N_{k}^{3})

\vec{k}

\vec{v}

{\mathcal{O}}(N_{k}^{3})
to
{\mathcal{O}}(N_{k}\log N_{k})
, is essential to our method. After considerable algebra, we derive mode-coupling integrals that are percent-level accurate over our region of interest, and we use FFT acceleration to reduce their computational cost by a factor of four hundred.

Finally, we put our perturbation theory to the test against the hybrid neutrino N-body simulation of Ref.

High-velocity neutrinos are particularly difficult for N-body particle simulations. Firstly, they require small time steps, since they move quickly past collapsed structures which could deflect them. Secondly, momentum shells at higher
|\vec{p}|

Thus our work points the way to a hybrid multi-fluid non-linear-response simulation in which each neutrino fluid would be evolved linearly until its dimensionless power spectrum exceeded a predetermined threshold. Then it would be evolved non-linearly until the non-linear corrections themselves became a significant portion of the total power, at which point it could be converted into simulation particles. Our non-linear multi-fluid perturbation theory of massive neutrinos, when integrated into a hybrid simulation, promises several advantages:

1.

Non-linear corrections make perturbative neutrino calculations more
accurate.

2.

Error in the perturbative calculation may be estimated using the ratio of
non-linear corrections to the linear power.

3.

Multi-fluid perturbation theory offers fine-grained information about the
velocity distribution of neutrino particles.

4.

Conversion to particles may be delayed relative to linear theory,
reducing their velocities and alleviating the resulting shot noise.

5.

Several neutrino fluids may be handled entirely using non-linear
perturbation theory, avoiding a particle treatment altogether.

Background

2.1
Multi-fluid perturbation theory

Cosmological neutrinos are characterized by a Fermi-Dirac distribution of velocities in the early universe after they have decoupled from electrons, positrons, and photons during big bang nucleosynthesis. However, cosmological perturbation theory is founded upon the continuity and Euler equations governing the dynamics of fluids, which have well-defined velocity fields. Multi-fluid perturbation theory resolves this conflict by discretizing the Fermi-Dirac distribution, with each bin corresponding to a uniform zeroth-order comoving velocity field.

Let
\breve{\psi}_{\alpha a}(\eta,\vec{x},\vec{v}_{\alpha})
be a Gaussian random perturbation variable for a neutrino fluid
\alpha
with velocity
\vec{v}_{\alpha}=v_{\alpha}\hat{v}_{\alpha}
relative to the total matter density. The index
a
is
0
for the density contrast
\delta_{\alpha}
and
1
for
\theta_{\alpha}:=-\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{V}_{\alpha}/{\mathcal{H}}
, the dimensionless divergence of the peculiar velocity field
\vec{V}_{\alpha}
. The time variable
\eta=\ln(a/a_{\mathrm{in}})
for some initial scale factor
a_{\mathrm{in}}
.

Reference

80
]
expands its Fourier transform

in Legendre moments as

\breve{\psi}_{\alpha a}(\eta,\vec{k},\vec{v}_{\alpha})=\sum_{\ell}(-i)^{\ell}\mathcal{P}_{\ell}(\hat{k}\cdot\hat{v}_{\alpha})\breve{\psi}_{\alpha a\ell}(\eta,\vec{k},v_{\alpha})

(2.1)

where the
\breve{\psi}_{\alpha,a,\ell}
are random variables; note a difference in the Legendre expansion convention. These can further be expanded as

\breve{\psi}_{\alpha a\ell}(\eta,\vec{k},v_{\alpha})=\sum_{m}\breve{C}_{\alpha a\ell m}(k)Y_{\ell m}(\hat{k})\psi_{\alpha a\ell}(\eta,k,v_{\alpha})

(2.2)

where the

2.2
Time-Renormalization Group perturbation theory

Above we considered linear evolution, but the continuity and Euler equations of fluid dynamics are quadratic in the density and velocity. In the case of interest here, with an irrotational peculiar velocity field
\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{V}_{\alpha}=0
allowing the representation of the peculiar velocity by a scalar perturbation
\theta_{\alpha}

\displaystyle(\breve{\psi}_{\alpha a}^{\vec{k}})^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\breve{\Xi}_{\alpha ab}^{\vec{k}}\breve{\psi}_{\alpha b}^{\vec{k}}+\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{\mathrm{(D)}}(\vec{k}-\vec{q}-\vec{p})\gamma_{abc}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}\breve{\psi}_{\alpha b}^{\vec{q}}\breve{\psi}_{\alpha c}^{\vec{p}}

\displaystyle-\breve{\Xi}_{\alpha ab}^{\vec{k}}\breve{\psi}_{\alpha b}^{\vec{k}}+\int_{\vec{q}}\gamma_{abc}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}\breve{\psi}_{\alpha b}^{\vec{q}}\breve{\psi}_{\alpha c}^{\vec{p}}

of the infinite hierarchy of evolution equations by

\displaystyle(B_{\alpha abc}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}})^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Xi_{\alpha ad0}^{k}B_{\alpha dbc}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}-\Xi_{\alpha bd0}^{q}B_{\alpha adc}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}-\Xi_{\alpha cd0}^{p}B_{\alpha abd}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}\ldots

(2.22)

\displaystyle+2\gamma_{ade}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}P_{\alpha db}^{\vec{q}}P_{\alpha ec}^{\vec{p}}+2\gamma_{bde}^{\vec{q},\vec{p},-\vec{k}}P_{\alpha dc}^{\vec{p}}P_{\alpha ea}^{\vec{k}}+2\gamma_{cde}^{\vec{p},-\vec{k},\vec{q}}P_{\alpha da}^{\vec{k}}P_{\alpha eb}^{\vec{q}}

in the Time-RG truncation approximation.

Evolution of the bispectrum integral
I_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{\vec{k}}
is determined by the substitution of Eq. (
2.22
) into the derivative of Eq. (
2.20
), that is,
(I_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{\vec{k}})^{\prime}=\int_{\vec{q}}\gamma_{acd}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}(B_{\alpha bef}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}})^{\prime}
. The first term on the right hand side of Eq. (
2.22
) presents no problems, since the linear evolution matrix can be pulled outside of the integral, resulting in
-\Xi_{\alpha bg0}^{k}I_{\alpha,acd,gef}^{\vec{k}}
. However, in the second and third terms,
\Xi_{\alpha}
depends upon one of the integration variables and cannot be factored out. The Time-RG closure approximation,
\Xi_{\alpha bd0}^{q}\approx\Xi_{\alpha bd0}^{k}
and
\Xi_{\alpha cd0}^{p}\approx\Xi_{\alpha cd0}^{k}
inside these integrals, allows us to treat the second and third terms in
(I_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{\vec{k}})^{\prime}
just like the first, resulting in the linear terms
-\Xi_{\alpha bg0}^{k}I_{\alpha,acd,gef}^{\vec{k}}-\Xi_{\alpha eg0}^{k}I_{\alpha,acd,bgf}^{\vec{k}}-\Xi_{\alpha fg0}^{k}I_{\alpha,acd,beg}^{\vec{k}}
.

Scale-dependence of
\Xi_{\alpha ab0}^{k}
is limited to its
\Xi_{\alpha 100}^{k}
component. Furthermore, the most common application of Time-RG is to the combined CDM+baryon fluid, making up a fraction
f_{\mathrm{cb}}\approx 1
of the total matter, in the presence of a massive neutrino population making up a fraction
f_{\nu}\ll 1
which is treated linearly. In this case, the scale dependence of
\Xi_{\alpha 100}^{k}
is suppressed by the ratio
f_{\nu}/f_{\mathrm{cb}}\ll 1
. Thus
\Xi_{\alpha ab0}^{q}
is approximately
\Xi_{\alpha ab0}^{k}
across the full range of
q
. Reference
[
81
]
finds the error resulting from the closure approximation to be negligible.

Finally, Time-RG evolution of
I_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{\vec{k}}
includes a non-linear correction arising from the final three terms on the right side of Eq. (
2.22
). We define the mode-coupling integral

A_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{\vec{k}}:=\int_{\vec{q}}\gamma_{acd}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}\left[\gamma_{bgh}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}P_{\alpha ge}^{\vec{q}}P_{\alpha hf}^{\vec{p}}+\gamma_{egh}^{\vec{q},-\vec{p},\vec{k}}P_{\alpha gf}^{\vec{p}}P_{\alpha hb}^{\vec{k}}+\gamma_{fgh}^{\vec{p},\vec{k},-\vec{q}}P_{\alpha gb}^{\vec{k}}P_{\alpha he}^{\vec{q}}\right].

(2.23)

Summation over the repeated
g
and
h
indices is implicit. With this definition, along with the closure approximation, the non-linear evolution of
I_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{\vec{k}}
is quantified by

\displaystyle(I_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{\vec{k}})^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Xi_{\alpha bg0}^{k}I_{\alpha,acd,gef}^{\vec{k}}-\Xi_{\alpha eg0}^{k}I_{\alpha,acd,bgf}^{\vec{k}}-\Xi_{\alpha fg0}^{k}I_{\alpha,acd,beg}^{\vec{k}}+2A_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{\vec{k}}.

(2.24)

Time-RG evolution of cold matter perturbations is thus governed by Eq. (
2.18
) and Eq. (
2.24
). Initial conditions set
P_{\alpha ab}^{\vec{k}}
to its linear approximation and
I_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{\vec{k}}
to zero or
2A_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{\vec{k}}

[
82
]
. By contrast to linear perturbation theory, in which the power at each wave number
\vec{k}
evolves independently, the mode-coupling integrals couple power spectra at different wave numbers.

2.3
Fast Fourier Transform acceleration

Numerical calculation of the mode-coupling integrals of Eq. (
2.23
) is the most computationally expensive step in Time-RG perturbation theory. Over the past several years, FFT-based techniques such as FAST-PT have been developed by Refs.
[
75
,
76
,
77
]
to accelerate the computation of perturbation theory integrals. Reference
[
7
]
applied these techniques to Time-RG in redshift space, whose line of sight direction is conceptually similar to the velocity direction
\hat{v}_{\alpha}
for a moving fluid. Here we briefly summarize the FAST-PT method for Time-RG.

Note that out of the three terms on the right hand side of Eq. (
2.23
), the second can be transformed into the third by simultaneously switching the indices
c\leftrightarrow d
and
e\leftrightarrow f
as well as the wave numbers
\vec{q}\leftrightarrow\vec{p}
. This follows from the symmetry
\gamma_{acb}^{\vec{k}\vec{p}\vec{q}}=\gamma_{abc}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}
of Eq. (
2.17
). Thus the integrand of
A_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{\vec{k}}
contains two types of terms, those in which both power spectra depend upon the integration variables and those in whicn only one power spectrum does. We refer to these respectively as
P^{(2,2)}
-type and
P^{(1,3)}
-type terms by analogy with Standard Perturbation Theory, which expands
\delta(\vec{k})=\sum_{n}\delta^{[n]}(\vec{k})
in perturbative orders and finds two leading-order corrections to the power,
P^{(2,2)}(k)\propto\left<\delta^{[2]}(\vec{k})^{*}\delta^{[2]}(\vec{k})\right>
and
P^{(1,3)}(k)\propto\left<\delta^{[1]}(\vec{k})^{*}\delta^{[3]}(\vec{k})+\delta^{[3]}(\vec{k})^{*}\delta^{[1]}(\vec{k})\right>
, respectively integrating over two and one power spectra.

The FAST-PT technique of Ref.
[
75
]
computes
P^{(2,2)}
-type terms, in which both power spectra in the integrand depend upon integration variables, by expanding them in terms of

J_{\alpha\beta\ell}(k)=\int_{\vec{q}}q^{\alpha}p^{\beta}{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu_{pq})P(q)P(p)

(2.25)

with
\mu_{pq}:=\hat{p}\cdot\hat{q}
and both power spectra inside the integrand being the linear
\delta
power spectra. Reference
[
75
]
demonstrates that
J_{\alpha\beta\ell}(k)
can be computed rapidly through a discrete convolution followed by an inverse discrete Fourier transform.

Meanwhile,
P^{(1,3)}
-type terms such as the third term on the right side of Eq. (
2.23
) can be reduced to integrals over a single variable
x=q/k
by pulling all factors independent of
\vec{q}
and
\vec{p}
outside of their integrands. The result is an integral of the form
k^{3}P_{gb}(k)\int dx\,x^{2}P_{he}(kx)G(x)
with
G
determined by the indices
a
,
c
,
d
,
b
,
e
,
f
,
g
, and
h
. Defining
w=-\log(x)
,
{\tilde{P}}(w)=P_{he}(e^{w})
, and
{\tilde{G}}(w)=G(e^{-w})e^{-3w}
, we recognize this as the convolution of
\tilde{G}
and
\tilde{P}
, which may be computed directly or using the FFT algorithm, as discussed in Ref.
[
75
]
.

While a brute-force numerical computation such as that described in Ref.
[
68
]
requires
{\mathcal{O}}(N_{k}^{3})
operations, for
N_{k}
wave numbers, the FAST-PT computation needs only
{\mathcal{O}}(N_{k}\log(N_{k}))
, representing a significant acceleration of the most computationally-expensive integrals in Time-RG perturbation theory. FAST-PT computation of all
P^{(2,2)}
-type and
P^{(1,3)}
-type components of the Time-RG mode-coupling integral of Eq. (
2.23
) was worked out in detail in Ref.
[
7
]
for the purpose of constraining the neutrino masses. In order to study redshift-space distortions, that reference extended Time-RG to the case of a preferred direction, which is directly relevant to the massive neutrino streams considered here.

3
Non-linear perturbation theory for massive neutrinos

3.1
Extension of Time-RG for hot dark matter

Time-RG perturbation theory for cold matter is based upon the non-linear evolution equation for a single perturbation, Eq. (
2.16
), applied to the power spectrum and bispectrum. Time-RG as formulated in Ref.
[
68
]
evolved the three CDM+baryon power spectra,
P_{00}
,
P_{01}
, and
P_{11}
, along with the bispectrum integrals. Though the linear
P_{01}
is simply the geometric mean of
P_{00}
and
P_{11}
, non-linear evolution allows for the density-velocity correlation coefficient
P_{01}/\sqrt{P_{00}P_{11}}
to fall below unity. Thus the two perturbations
\delta
and
\theta
are no longer sufficient for capturing all information contained in the power spectra.

In this work we evolve the perturbations
\delta
and
\theta
themselves, rather than the power spectra, in order to define
\Xi_{\alpha ab\ell}^{k}
as in Eq. (
2.1
). As a result we must define a third perturbation

quantifying this density-velocity correlation. Generalizing to the angle-dependent
\psi_{\alpha a\ell}^{k}
of Eq. (
2.2
), we define

\psi_{\alpha 2\ell}^{k}:=\chi_{\alpha\ell}^{k}:=1-P_{\alpha 01,\ell}^{k}/\sqrt{P_{\alpha 00,\ell}^{k}P_{\alpha 11,\ell}^{k}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P_{\alpha 01,\ell}^{k}=\left(1-\chi_{\alpha\ell}^{k}\right)\psi_{\alpha 0\ell}^{k}\psi_{\alpha 1\ell}^{k}.

(3.1)

We thus replace the linear power spectrum of Eq. (
2.14
) by

\displaystyle P_{\alpha ab}^{\vec{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}\mathcal{P}_{\ell}(\mu_{\alpha})^{2}\left[1-\left(1-\delta^{\mathrm{(K)}}_{ab}\right)\chi_{\alpha\ell}^{k}\right]\psi^{k}_{\alpha a\ell}\psi^{k}_{\alpha b\ell}=:\sum_{\ell}\mathcal{P}_{\ell}(\mu_{\alpha})^{2}P^{k}_{\alpha ab\ell}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow P_{\alpha ab\ell}^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[1-\left(1-\delta^{\mathrm{(K)}}_{ab}\right)\chi_{\alpha\ell}^{k}\right]\psi^{k}_{\alpha a\ell}\psi^{k}_{\alpha b\ell}

(3.3)

where
a,b\in\{0,1\}
. The quantity in square brackets is unity if

\displaystyle(\psi_{\alpha a\ell}^{k})^{\prime}

\displaystyle\frac{(P_{\alpha aa\ell}^{k})^{\prime}}{2\psi_{\alpha a\ell}^{k}}\quad\textrm{if}~a\in\{0,1\}

(3.4)

\displaystyle(\psi_{\alpha 2\ell}^{k})^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\chi_{\alpha\ell}^{k})^{\prime}=-\frac{(P_{\alpha 01\ell}^{k})^{\prime}}{\delta_{\alpha\ell}^{k}\theta_{\alpha\ell}^{k}}+(1-\chi_{\alpha\ell}^{k})\frac{(\delta_{\alpha\ell}^{k})^{\prime}}{\delta_{\alpha\ell}^{k}}+(1-\chi_{\alpha\ell}^{k})\frac{(\theta_{\alpha\ell}^{k})^{\prime}}{\theta_{\alpha\ell}^{k}},

given Time-RG calculations of the power spectrum derivatives.

Large flow velocities

3.2
Evolution of the
I_{\alpha,acd,bef,j}^{k}

Next, we extend the evolution equations of the bispectrum and bispectrum integral, respectively Eq. (
2.22
) and Eq. (
2.24
), to the case of a neutrino flow with non-zero
v_{\alpha}
. Since the bispectrum is negligible in linear perturbation theory, applicable at early times, the dependence of the bispectrum upon the direction of
\vec{v}_{\alpha}
is due entirely to the power spectrum pairs on the right side of Eq. (
2.22
). Thus the bispectrum depends upon
k
,
q
,
p
,
\mu_{\alpha}:=\hat{k}\cdot\hat{v}_{\alpha}
,
\mu_{\alpha q}:=\hat{q}\cdot\hat{v}_{\alpha}
, and
\mu_{\alpha p}:=\hat{p}\cdot\hat{v}_{\alpha}
, with
\vec{k}=\vec{q}+\vec{p}
implying the constraint that
k\mu_{\alpha}=q\mu_{\alpha q}+p\mu_{\alpha p}
.

References
[
83
,
84
]
study the angle-dependence of the bispectrum in the presence of a preferred direction
\hat{s}
. They find that
B(\vec{k}_{1},\vec{k}_{2},\vec{k}_{3})
, with
\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2}+\vec{k}_{3}=0
, can be parameterized in terms of
k_{1}
,
k_{2}
,
k_{3}
, and two angles,
\varpi_{i}
and
\varphi_{ij}
, for any given
i,j\in\{1,~2,~3\}
such that
i\neq j
. The first is the angle between
\vec{k}_{i}
and
\hat{s}
, that is,
\varpi_{i}:=\cos^{-1}(\hat{k}_{i}\cdot\hat{s})
. The second,
\varphi_{ij}
, is the azimuthal angle of
\vec{k}_{j}
in the plane perpendicular to
\vec{k}_{i}
, that is, the angle between
\vec{k}_{j}-(\vec{k}_{j}\cdot\hat{k}_{i})\hat{k}_{i}
and
\hat{s}-(\hat{s}\cdot\hat{k}_{i})\hat{k}_{i}
. In terms of these, the bispectrum may be expressed as

B^{\vec{k}_{1}\vec{k}_{2}\vec{k}_{3}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}Y_{\ell m}(\varpi_{i},\varphi_{ij})B^{k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}}_{\ell m,ij}.

(3.14)

Here,
B^{k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}}_{\ell m,ij}
is a function of the wave numbers
k_{1}
,
k_{2}
, and
k_{3}
and the integer indices
\ell
and
m
that is specific to our choices of
i
and
j
.

Defining
\mu_{\alpha q}:=\hat{q}\cdot\hat{v}_{\alpha}
and
\mu_{\alpha p}:=\hat{p}\cdot\hat{v}_{\alpha}
, let
\varpi_{k}:=\cos^{-1}(\mu_{\alpha})
,
\varpi_{q}:=\cos^{-1}(\mu_{\alpha q})
, and
\varpi_{p}:=\cos^{-1}(\mu_{\alpha p})
. In each case, the integer
j
used to define
\varphi_{ij}
may be chosen arbitrarily. Then we have three equivalent representations of the bispectrum,

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\!\!Y_{\ell m}(\!\varpi_{k},\varphi_{k}\!)B^{kqp}_{\ell m,(k)j}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\!\!Y_{\ell m}(\!\varpi_{q},\varphi_{q}\!)B^{kqp}_{\ell m,(q)j}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\!\!Y_{\ell m}(\!\varpi_{p},\varphi_{p}\!)B^{kqp}_{\ell m,(p)j}.

(3.15)

The first, second, and third forms, respectively, are useful for extending the first, second, and third terms on the right hand side of Eq. (
2.22

2.14

3.10

\displaystyle(B_{\alpha abc}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}})^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\Xi_{\alpha ad\ell}^{k}Y_{\ell m}(\varpi_{k},\varphi_{k})B^{kqp}_{dbc,\ell m,(k)j}-\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\Xi_{\alpha bd\ell}^{q}Y_{\ell m}(\varpi_{q},\varphi_{q})B^{kqp}_{adc,\ell m,(q)j}\ldots

\displaystyle-\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\Xi_{\alpha cd\ell}^{p}Y_{\ell m}(\varpi_{p},\varphi_{p})B^{kqp}_{abd,\ell m,(p)j}\ldots

\displaystyle+2\gamma_{ade}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}P_{\alpha db}^{\vec{q}}P_{\alpha ec}^{\vec{p}}+2\gamma_{bde}^{\vec{q},\vec{p},-\vec{k}}P_{\alpha dc}^{\vec{p}}P_{\alpha ea}^{\vec{k}}+2\gamma_{cde}^{\vec{p},-\vec{k},\vec{q}}P_{\alpha da}^{\vec{k}}P_{\alpha eb}^{\vec{q}}.

Similarly, since

\vec{v}_{\alpha}

(3.17)

The Time-RG closure approximation discussed in Sec.
2.2
is complicated by the fact that neutrinos are not the dominant clustering species. Thus neglecting the difference between
\Xi_{\alpha ab\ell}^{k}
and
\Xi_{\alpha ab\ell}^{q}

{\mathcal{O}}(f_{\nu}/f_{\mathrm{cb}})
approximation. On the other hand, neutrinos are significantly more linear than the CDM and baryons. The wave number at which non-linear corrections dominate the neutrino power spectrum is typically much larger than the neutrino free-streaming wave number
\sim{\mathcal{H}}/v_{\alpha}
. Thus we seek a closure approximation appropriate to the free-streaming limit
kv_{\alpha}/{\mathcal{H}}\gg 1
.

Free streaming washes out the clustering sourced by the gravitational potential
\Phi
. Thus our closure approximation in the free-streaming limit is to neglect both the free-streaming terms and
\Phi
in
\Xi_{\alpha abj}^{q}
. We adopt the following equation of motion for the bispectrum integral:

\displaystyle(I_{\alpha,acd,bef,j}^{k})^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Xi_{\alpha bgj}^{k}I_{\alpha,acd,gef,j}^{k}-{\tilde{\Xi}}_{\alpha egj}^{k}I_{\alpha,acd,bgf,j}^{k}-{\tilde{\Xi}}_{\alpha fgj}^{k}I_{\alpha,acd,beg,j}^{k}+2A_{\alpha,acd,bef,j}^{k}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\Xi_{\alpha abj}^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
\frac{k^{2}\Phi^{k}}{{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\psi_{\alpha 00}^{k}}\delta^{\mathrm{(K)}}_{j0}&1\!+\!\frac{{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}{{\mathcal{H}}}\end{array}\right]-\frac{kv_{\alpha}}{{\mathcal{H}}}\!\left(\!\frac{j}{2j-1}\frac{\psi_{\alpha a,j-1}^{k}}{\psi_{\alpha aj}^{k}}\!-\!\frac{j+1}{2j+3}\frac{\psi_{\alpha a,j+1}^{k}}{\psi_{\alpha aj}^{k}}\!\right)\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right]\qquad

\displaystyle{\tilde{\Xi}}_{\alpha abj}^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
0&1+\frac{{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}{{\mathcal{H}}}\end{array}\right]

where summation over the repeated index
g
is implicit and
\Xi_{\alpha abj}^{k}
is identical to that of Eq. (
2.1
). Equations (
3.10
-
3.13
) and (
3.18
-
3.2
) are the main results of this Section.

Quantifying the accuracy of Eq. (
3.18
) relative to a direct integration of Eq. (
3.16
) is prohibitively computationally expensive. Discretizing

,
q
, and
p
with
\sim 100
values each implies
\sim 10^{6}
combinations of
k
,
q
, and

. Further, truncating
\ell
at
\sim 10
in each summation of Eq. (
3.16
) implies

quantities
B^{kqp}_{abc,\ell m,(k)j}
, for each of eight possible combinations of the indices

,

3.3
Mode-coupling integrals

Extension of the mode-coupling integrals to the case of a massive neutrino flow is straightforward; we need only substitute the angle-dependent power spectrum of Eq. (

\displaystyle A_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{\vec{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{(2,2)\vec{k}}+A_{\alpha,adc,bfe}^{(1,3)\vec{k}}+A_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{(1,3)\vec{k}}

(3.27)

\displaystyle A_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{(2,2)\vec{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{\vec{q}}\gamma_{acd}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}\gamma_{bgh}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu_{\alpha q})^{2}{\mathcal{P}}_{m}(\mu_{\alpha p})^{2}P_{\alpha ge\ell}^{q}P_{\alpha hfm}^{p}

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{\vec{q}}\gamma_{acd}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}\gamma_{fgh}^{\vec{p},\vec{k},-\vec{q}}{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu_{\alpha})^{2}{\mathcal{P}}_{m}(\mu_{\alpha q})^{2}P_{\alpha gb\ell}^{k}P_{\alpha hem}^{q}.

(3.29)

Equation (
3.10
) for the power spectrum, Eq. (
3.18

FlowsForTheMasses

Mode-coupling integrals represent the most computationally expensive part of this perturbation theory. Fourteen combinations of

,

,
b
,
e
, and

,

Deriving, implementing, and testing FFT-accelerated

Evaluation of the

-type term, Eq. (

), is the goal of this Section. Our starting point is the observation in Sec.
3.2
that the bispectrum for a fluid of velocity
\vec{v}_{\alpha}
is a function of
k
,
\mu_{\alpha}
, and
\vec{q}
, and motivating a set of angular coordinates for
\vec{q}
integration. Since
{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu_{\alpha q})^{2}{\mathcal{P}}_{m}(\mu_{\alpha p})^{2}
is the only part of that integrand which cannot be expresed solely in terms of
q
,
p
, and

) to a series of terms of the form of Eq. (
2.25
).

4.1
Coordinate system and basis

We begin by defining a coordinate system for
\vec{q}
integration of Eq. (
3.28
) after the Dirac delta function has fixed
\vec{p}
to
\vec{k}-\vec{q}
. Let
\hat{k}
be the vertical axis (
z_{k}
axis) of the space of
\vec{q}
vectors and
\vartheta
be the angle between
\hat{k}
and
\hat{q}
, so that
\cos(\vartheta):=c_{\vartheta}:=\hat{k}\cdot\hat{q}
. Given a flow
\alpha
with velocity
\vec{v}_{\alpha}
, which we assume not to be parallel to
\hat{k}
, define the
x_{k}
axis so that
\vec{v}_{\alpha}
lies in the
x_{k}z_{k}
plane, and the
y_{k}
axis by
\hat{z}_{k}\times\hat{x}_{k}=\hat{y}_{k}
. Then, for a given
\vec{q}
, let
\varphi
be the angle between the projection of
\vec{q}
onto the
x_{k}y_{k}
plane,
\vec{q}-(\vec{q}\cdot\hat{k})\hat{k}
, and the
x_{k}
axis.

In this coordinate system, and defining the shorthand
\nu_{\alpha}:=\sqrt{1-\mu_{\alpha}^{2}}
,
s_{\vartheta}:=\sin(\vartheta)
,
\tilde{c}_{\vartheta}:=-(qc_{\vartheta}-k)/p
, and
\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}:=-qs_{\vartheta}/p
, we have the following identities:

\displaystyle\vec{v}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{\alpha}\nu_{\alpha}\hat{x}_{k}+v_{\alpha}\mu_{\alpha}\hat{z}_{k}

(4.1)

\displaystyle\vec{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle qs_{\vartheta}\cos(\varphi)\hat{x}_{k}+qs_{\vartheta}\sin(\varphi)\hat{y}_{k}+qc_{\vartheta}\hat{z}_{k}

(4.2)

\displaystyle\vec{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-qs_{\vartheta}\cos(\varphi)\hat{x}_{k}-qs_{\vartheta}\sin(\varphi)\hat{y}_{k}-(qc_{\vartheta}-k)\hat{z}_{k}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\vec{v}_{\alpha}\cdot\vec{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle qv_{\alpha}\nu_{\alpha}s_{\vartheta}\cos(\varphi)+qv_{\alpha}\mu_{\alpha}c_{\vartheta}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\vec{v}_{\alpha}\cdot\vec{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-qv_{\alpha}\nu_{\alpha}s_{\vartheta}\cos(\varphi)-(qc_{\vartheta}-k)v_{\alpha}\mu_{\alpha}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mu_{pq}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\hat{q}\cdot\hat{p}=\frac{kc_{\vartheta}}{p}-\frac{q}{p}\Rightarrow c_{\vartheta}=\frac{p}{k}\mu_{pq}+\frac{q}{k}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mu_{\alpha q}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{q}=\nu_{\alpha}s_{\vartheta}\cos(\varphi)+\mu_{\alpha}c_{\vartheta}=\nu_{\alpha}s_{\vartheta}\cos(\varphi)+\left(\frac{p}{k}\mu_{pq}+\frac{q}{k}\right)\mu_{\alpha}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\mu_{\alpha p}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{\alpha}\cdot\hat{p}=\nu_{\alpha}\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}\cos(\varphi)+\mu_{\alpha}\tilde{c}_{\vartheta}=\nu_{\alpha}\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}\cos(\varphi)+\left(\frac{q}{k}\mu_{pq}+\frac{p}{k}\right)\mu_{\alpha}

(4.8)

The vertex functions
\gamma_{acd}
can now be expressed as

\displaystyle\gamma_{001}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{q}{p}\mu_{pq}\right)=\frac{k}{4q}\,\frac{\tfrac{q}{k}c_{\vartheta}-1}{c-\tfrac{1}{2}(\tfrac{q}{k}+\tfrac{k}{q})}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\gamma_{001}^{\vec{p},\vec{k},-\vec{q}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{k}{q}c_{\vartheta}\right)

(4.9)

\displaystyle\gamma_{010}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{p}{q}\mu_{pq}\right)=\frac{k}{2q}c_{\vartheta}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\gamma_{010}^{\vec{p},\vec{k},-\vec{q}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{q}{k}c_{\vartheta}\right)

(4.10)

\displaystyle\gamma_{111}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{pq}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{q}{p}+\frac{p}{q}\right)\mu_{pq}=\frac{k}{4q}\,\frac{1-\tfrac{k}{q}c_{\vartheta}}{c-\tfrac{1}{2}(\tfrac{q}{k}+\tfrac{k}{q})}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\gamma_{111}^{\vec{p},\vec{k},-\vec{q}}=c_{\vartheta}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{q}{k}+\frac{k}{q}\right)c_{\vartheta}.\qquad

(4.11)

Since the power spectrum of Eq. (
2.14
) is expanded in the squares of Legendre polynomials, we summarize here the coefficients used to transform between this and other polynomial bases. Legendre polynomials
{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu)
can be expressed in terms of integer powers
\mu^{L}
as:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{L}{\mathcal{R}}_{L\ell}\mu^{L}\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad\mu^{L}=\sum_{\ell}{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}_{\ell L}{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu)

(4.12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{where}\quad{\mathcal{R}}_{L\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{2^{\ell}{\mathcal{E}}_{\ell+L}}{L!(\ell-L)!}\left(\frac{L-\ell+1}{2}\right)_{\ell}&\textrm{if }0\leq L\leq\ell\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\textrm{if $n$ is an even integer}\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}\end{array}\right.\textrm{ and }{\mathcal{O}}_{n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\textrm{if $n$ is an odd integer}\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}\end{array}\right.\quad

and
(x)_{n}:=\Gamma(x+n)/\Gamma(x)
is the Pocchammer symbol. For squares of Legendre polynomials:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{L}\sum_{M}\mu^{L+M}{\mathcal{R}}_{L\ell}{\mathcal{R}}_{M\ell}=:\sum_{L}{\mathcal{Q}}_{L\ell}\mu^{2L}\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad\mu^{2L}=\sum_{\ell}{\mathcal{Q}}^{-1}_{\ell L}{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu)^{2}\qquad

(4.21)

\displaystyle\mathrm{where}\quad{\mathcal{Q}}_{L\ell}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{M=\max(0,2L-\ell)}^{\min(\ell,2L)}{\mathcal{R}}_{M,\ell}{\mathcal{R}}_{2L-M,\ell}.

(4.22)

Each of
{\mathcal{R}}_{L\ell}
,
{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}_{\ell L}
,
{\mathcal{Q}}_{L\ell}
, and
{\mathcal{Q}}^{-1}_{\ell L}
is an upper-triangular matrix whose entries vanish when the first (row) index is greater than the second (column) index. Henceforth we use capital letters for the even-integer-power basis and lower-case letters for the Legendre-squared basis.

Coefficients of these basis functions transform in the opposite sense. If some function
x(\mu)=\sum_{\ell}x_{\ell}{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu)^{2}=\sum_{L}x_{L}\mu^{2L}
, then

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}x_{\ell}\sum_{L}{\mathcal{Q}}_{L\ell}\mu^{2L}=\sum_{L}\mu^{2L}\left(\sum_{\ell}{\mathcal{Q}}_{L\ell}x_{\ell}\right)\Rightarrow x_{L}=\sum_{\ell}{\mathcal{Q}}_{L\ell}x_{\ell}

(4.23)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{L}x_{L}\sum_{\ell}{\mathcal{Q}}^{-1}_{\ell L}{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu)^{2}=\sum_{\ell}{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu)^{2}\left(\sum_{L}{\mathcal{Q}}^{-1}_{\ell L}x_{L}\right)\Rightarrow x_{\ell}=\sum_{L}{\mathcal{Q}}^{-1}_{\ell L}x_{L}\qquad

(4.24)

and similarly, if
y(\mu)=\sum_{\ell}y_{\ell}{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu)=\sum_{L}y_{L}\mu^{L}
, then
y_{L}=\sum_{\ell}{\mathcal{R}}_{L\ell}y_{\ell}\longleftrightarrow y_{\ell}=\sum_{L}{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}_{\ell L}y_{L}
.

4.2
Angular averages of Legendre polynomial products

Dependence of the factor
{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu_{\alpha q})^{2}{\mathcal{P}}_{m}(\mu_{\alpha p})^{2}
in Eq. (
3.28
) upon
\varphi
prevents its reduction to terms of the form of Eq. (
2.25
), necessary for FFT-accelerated integration. Our next step is to integrate explicitly over
\varphi
, allowing us to replace
{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu_{\alpha q})^{2}{\mathcal{P}}_{m}(\mu_{\alpha p})^{2}
by its angular average

\left<{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu_{\alpha q})^{2}{\mathcal{P}}_{m}(\mu_{\alpha p})^{2}\right>_{\varphi}:=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\varphi}{2\pi}{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu_{\alpha q})^{2}{\mathcal{P}}_{m}(\mu_{\alpha p})^{2}.

(4.25)

Henceforth, we use triangular braces with one or more angles in the subscript, as above, to denote averaging over those angles.

Using the basis transformation coefficients of Sec.
4.1
, we may expand this average.

\displaystyle\left<{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu_{\alpha q})^{2}{\mathcal{P}}_{m}(\mu_{\alpha p})^{2}\right>_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{L=0}^{\ell}\sum_{M=0}^{m}{\mathcal{Q}}_{L\ell}{\mathcal{Q}}_{Mm}\left<\mu_{\alpha q}^{2L}\mu_{\alpha p}^{2M}\right>_{\varphi}

(4.26)

\displaystyle\left<\mu_{\alpha q}^{2L}\mu_{\alpha p}^{2M}\right>_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\sum_{J=0}^{\infty}\mu_{\alpha}^{2J}\rho_{JLM}(c_{\vartheta},s_{\vartheta},\tilde{c}_{\vartheta},\tilde{s}_{\vartheta})

(4.27)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\left<{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu_{\alpha q})^{2}{\mathcal{P}}_{m}(\mu_{\alpha p})^{2}\right>_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(\mu_{\alpha})^{2}\sum_{L=0}^{\ell}\sum_{M=0}^{m}\sum_{J=j}^{\infty}{\mathcal{Q}}_{L\ell}{\mathcal{Q}}_{Mm}{\mathcal{Q}}^{-1}_{jJ}\rho_{JLM}(c_{\vartheta},s_{\vartheta},\tilde{c}_{\vartheta},\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}).\qquad~

(4.28)

The second line above defines the quantity
\rho_{JLM}
, which is independent of
\varphi
, after which Eq. (
4.28
) expresses the angular average in the desired
{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(\mu_{\alpha})^{2}
basis with
\varphi
-independent coefficients. Thus we have reduced the goal of this subsection to a computation of
\rho_{JLM}
.

The product
\mu_{\alpha q}^{2L}\mu_{\alpha p}^{2M}
is expanded in binomial series as

\displaystyle\mu_{\alpha q}^{2L}\mu_{\alpha p}^{2M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{2L}\sum_{j=0}^{2M}\binom{2L}{i}\binom{2M}{j}(\mu_{\alpha}c_{\vartheta})^{2L-i}(\mu_{\alpha}\tilde{c}_{\vartheta})^{2M-j}(s_{\vartheta}\nu_{\alpha}\cos\varphi)^{i}(\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}\nu_{\alpha}\cos\varphi)^{j}

(4.29)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{2L}\sum_{j=0}^{2M}\binom{2L}{i}\binom{2M}{j}c_{\vartheta}^{2L-i}\tilde{c}_{\vartheta}^{2M-j}s_{\vartheta}^{i}\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}^{j}\mu_{\alpha}^{2L+2M-i-j}(\nu_{\alpha}\cos\varphi)^{i+j}.\qquad

The angular average over odd powers of
\cos\varphi
vanishes, while that over even powers is

\left<\cos(\varphi)^{2r}\right>_{\varphi}=\frac{(2r)!}{4^{r}(r!)^{2}}=:\kappa_{r}~.

(4.30)

Defining
r=(i+j)/2
, we recast the summation of the
\varphi
average as

\displaystyle\left<\mu_{\alpha q}^{2L}\mu_{\alpha p}^{2M}\right>_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{L+M}\sum_{i=0}^{2L}\binom{2L}{i}\binom{2M}{2r-i}c_{\vartheta}^{2L-i}\tilde{c}_{\vartheta}^{2M+i-2r}s_{\vartheta}^{i}\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}^{2r-i}\mu_{\alpha}^{2L+2M-2r}\nu_{\alpha}^{2r}\kappa_{r}.\qquad

(4.31)

In our binomial factor convention,
\binom{n}{i}
vanishes for all integers
n
and
i
unless
0\leq i\leq n
. Thus the summand is non-zero only for
\max(0,2r-2M)\leq i\leq\min(2L,2r)
.

Next, we expand
\nu_{\alpha}^{2r}=(1-\mu_{\alpha}^{2})^{r}
in binomial series.

\displaystyle\left<\mu_{\alpha q}^{2L}\mu_{\alpha p}^{2M}\right>_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{L+M}\!\sum_{i=0}^{2L}\binom{\!2L\!}{\!i\!}\binom{\!2M\!}{\!2r\!-\!i\!}c_{\vartheta}^{2L-i}\tilde{c}_{\vartheta}^{2M+i-2r}s_{\vartheta}^{i}\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}^{2r-i}\mu_{\alpha}^{2L+2M-2r}\kappa_{r}\sum_{s=0}^{r}\binom{r}{s}(-1)^{r-s}\mu_{\alpha}^{2r-2s}

(4.32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{L+M}\sum_{r=s}^{L+M}\sum_{i=0}^{2L}\binom{2L}{i}\binom{2M}{2r-i}\binom{r}{s}(-1)^{r-s}\kappa_{r}\mu_{\alpha}^{2L+2M-2s}c_{\vartheta}^{2L-i}\tilde{c}_{\vartheta}^{2M+i-2r}s_{\vartheta}^{i}\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}^{2r-i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{J=0}^{L+M}\mu_{\alpha}^{2J}\sum_{t=0}^{J}\sum_{i=0}^{2L}(-1)^{J-t}\binom{2L}{i}\binom{2M}{i+2t-2L}\binom{L+M-t}{J-t}\ldots

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\kappa_{L+M-t}c_{\vartheta}^{2L-i}\tilde{c}_{\vartheta}^{i+2t-2L}s_{\vartheta}^{i}\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}^{2L+2M-2t-i}

where in the final equality we have changed summation variables to
J=L+M-s
and
t=L+M-r
. From this expression we may immediately write down
\rho_{JLM}
,

\displaystyle\rho_{JLM}(c_{\vartheta},s_{\vartheta},\tilde{c}_{\vartheta},\tilde{s}_{\vartheta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{J}\sum_{i=0}^{2L}(-1)^{J-t}\binom{2L}{i}\binom{2M}{i+2t-2L}\binom{L+M-t}{J-t}\ldots

(4.33)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\kappa_{L+M-t}c_{\vartheta}^{2L-i}\tilde{c}_{\vartheta}^{i+2t-2L}s_{\vartheta}^{i}\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}^{2L+2M-2t-i}

with
J
restricted to the range
[0,L+M]
.

Using the identities
c_{\vartheta}^{2}+s_{\vartheta}^{2}=1
,
\tilde{c}_{\vartheta}^{2}+\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}^{2}=1
, and
c_{\vartheta}\tilde{c}_{\vartheta}+s_{\vartheta}\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}=\mu_{pq}
, we eliminate
c_{\vartheta}
and
\tilde{c}_{\vartheta}
in favor of
s_{\vartheta}
and
\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}
. The
i
summation may be split into low values
0\leq i\leq 2L-t
and high values
2L-t+1\leq i\leq 2L
, with the exponent of
\tilde{c}_{\vartheta}
being greater than that of
c_{\vartheta}
for high
i
. After some algebra we arrive at the expression

\displaystyle\left<\mu_{\alpha q}^{2L}\mu_{\alpha p}^{2M}\right>_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{J=0}^{L+M}\mu_{\alpha}^{2J}\sum_{g=L+M-J}^{L+M}\rho_{JLMg}

(4.34)

\displaystyle\rho_{JLMg}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{e=0}^{J}{\hat{h}}^{JLM}_{0eg}s_{\vartheta}^{2g-2M+e}\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}^{2M-e}\mu_{pq}^{e}+\sum_{e=0}^{J-1}{\hat{h}}^{JLM}_{1eg}s_{\vartheta}^{2L-e}\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}^{2g-2L+e}\mu_{pq}^{e}\qquad

(4.35)

\displaystyle{\hat{h}}^{JLM}_{0eg}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=L+M-J}^{g}\sum_{n=0}^{L+r-M}(-1)^{J+L+M+g+e}\kappa_{r}\binom{2L}{n}\binom{2M}{2r-n}\ldots

(4.36)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\binom{r}{L+M-J}\binom{2M-2r+n}{e}\binom{L+r-M-n}{g+e+r-n-2M}

\displaystyle{\hat{h}}^{JLM}_{1eg}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=L+M-J}^{\min(g,L+M-e-1)}\sum_{n=L+r-M+1}^{2L}(-1)^{J+L+M+g+e}\kappa_{r}\binom{2L}{n}\binom{2M}{2r-n}\ldots

(4.37)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\binom{r}{L+M-J}\binom{2L-n}{e}\binom{M+n-L-r}{g+n+e-2L-r}

FAST-PT computation of
P^{(2,2)}
-type integrals requires that the integrand be expanded in terms proportional to
(q/k)^{A}(p/k)^{B}\mu_{pq}^{C}P_{\alpha ab\ell}^{q}P_{\alpha cdm}^{p}
for integers
A
,
B
, and
C\geq 0
, as in Eq. (
2.25
). Thus we substitute
s_{\vartheta}=(p/k)\sqrt{1-\mu_{pq}^{2}}
and
\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}=-(q/k)\sqrt{1-\mu_{pq}^{2}}
. Defining
{\hat{h}}^{JLM}_{1Jg}
to be zero, we may combine the two
e
sums as

\rho_{JLMg}=(1-\mu_{pq}^{2})^{g}\left(\frac{p}{k}\right)^{2g}\sum_{e=0}^{J}\left[{\hat{h}}^{JLM}_{0eg}\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)^{2M-e}\!\!\!+{\hat{h}}^{JLM}_{1eg}\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)^{2g-2L+e}\right]\mu_{pq}^{e}.\quad

(4.38)

Finally, we change bases to the squared Legendre polynomials:

\displaystyle\left<{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu_{\alpha q})^{2}{\mathcal{P}}_{m}(\mu_{\alpha p})^{2}\right>_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\ell+m}{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(\mu_{\alpha})^{2}\sum_{g=0}^{\ell+m}\sum_{e=0}^{\ell+m-g}\sum_{f=0}^{g-{\mathcal{O}}_{e}}h^{jlm}_{efg}\left(\frac{p}{k}\right)^{2g}(1-\mu_{pq}^{2})^{g}\mu_{pq}^{e}\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)^{2f+{\mathcal{O}}_{e}}\quad

(4.39)

\displaystyle h^{jlm}_{efg}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{L=0}^{\ell}\sum_{J=j}^{L+f+\frac{e+{\mathcal{O}}_{e}}{2}}{\mathcal{Q}}^{-1}_{jJ}{\mathcal{Q}}_{L\ell}{\mathcal{Q}}_{f+\frac{e+{\mathcal{O}}_{e}}{2},m}{\hat{h}}^{J,L,f+\frac{e+{\mathcal{O}}_{e}}{2}}_{0eg}\ldots

(4.40)

\displaystyle+\sum_{M=0}^{m}\sum_{J=j}^{g+M-f+\frac{e-{\mathcal{O}}_{e}}{2}}{\mathcal{Q}}^{-1}_{jJ}{\mathcal{Q}}_{Mm}{\mathcal{Q}}_{g-f+\frac{e-{\mathcal{O}}_{e}}{2},\ell}{\hat{h}}^{J,g-f+\frac{e-{\mathcal{O}}_{e}}{2},M}_{1eg}

which is the goal of this subsection.

Before proceeding, we note a symmetry in the
h^{jlm}_{efg}
coefficients. The angular average
\left<{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu_{\alpha q})^{2}{\mathcal{P}}_{m}(\mu_{\alpha p})^{2}\right>_{\varphi}
is invariant under the transformation
\ell\leftrightarrow m
and
\vec{q}\leftrightarrow\vec{p}
. Applying this transformation to the right hand side of Eq. (
4.39
), and changing from the summation variable
f
to
f^{\prime}:=g-{\mathcal{O}}_{e}-f
, shows that the identity

h^{jm\ell}_{e,g-{\mathcal{O}}_{e}-f,g}=h^{j\ell m}_{efg}

(4.41)

is implied by this invariance.

4.3
FAST-PT computation of
P^{(2,2)}
-type terms

Each
A_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{(2,2)\vec{k}}
integrand is the product of two power spectra with two vertex functions. Since this product of vertex functions can be decomposed into terms containing integer powers of
p/q
and
\mu_{pq}
, we may decompose each
A_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{(2,2)\vec{k}}
into integrals whose integrands are proportional to
(p/q)^{B}\mu_{pq}^{N}
times the product of two power spectra:

\displaystyle S_{\alpha,BN,ab,cd}^{\vec{k}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{\vec{q}}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{B}\mu_{pq}^{N}P_{\alpha ab}^{\vec{q}}P_{\alpha cd}^{\vec{q}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{\vec{q}}\left<{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu_{\alpha q})^{2}{\mathcal{P}}_{m}(\mu_{\alpha p})^{2}\right>_{\varphi}P_{\alpha ab\ell}^{q}P_{\alpha cdm}^{p}\quad\quad

(4.42)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(\mu)^{2}S_{\alpha,BNj,ab,cd}^{k}

(4.43)

\displaystyle S_{\alpha,BNj,ab,cd}^{k}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}S_{\alpha,BNj,ab\ell,cdm}^{k}

(4.44)

\displaystyle S_{\alpha,BNj,ab\ell,cdm}^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{g=0}^{\ell+m}\sum_{e=0}^{\ell+m-g}\sum_{f=0}^{g-{\mathcal{O}}_{e}}h^{jlm}_{efg}\int_{\vec{q}}\left(\frac{p}{k}\right)^{2g}\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)^{2f\!+\!{\mathcal{O}}_{e}\!-\!B}\!\!\!\!\!\!(1-\mu_{pq}^{2})^{g}\mu_{pq}^{e+N}P_{\alpha ab\ell}^{q}P_{\alpha cdm}^{p}.

(4.45)

Expanding the
(1-\mu_{pq}^{2})^{g}\mu_{pq}^{e+N}
factor in Eq. (
4.45
) in binomial series and changing bases to Legendre polynomials, we rewrite the integral in that equation as

k^{-2g}\sum_{n=0}^{g}\sum_{\lambda=0}^{e+N+2n}(-1)^{n}\binom{g}{n}{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}_{\lambda,e+N+2n}\int_{\vec{q}}q^{2f+{\mathcal{O}}_{e}-B}p^{2g-2f-{\mathcal{O}}_{e}+B}{\mathcal{P}}_{\lambda}(\mu_{pq})P_{\alpha ab\ell}^{q}P_{\alpha cdm}^{p}.

Defining
J_{\alpha,AB\lambda,ab\ell,cdm}^{k}
as in Ref.
[
75
]

J_{\alpha,AB\lambda,ab\ell,cdm}^{k}=\int_{\vec{q}}q^{A}p^{B}{\mathcal{P}}_{\lambda}(\mu_{pq})P_{\alpha ab\ell}^{q}P_{\alpha cdm}^{p}

(4.46)

we see that
S_{\alpha,BNj,ab\ell,cdm}^{k}
may be written as a linear combination of
J_{\alpha,AB\lambda,ab\ell,cdm}^{k}
integrals.

Because our subsequent treatment of
S_{\alpha,BNj,ab\ell,cdm}^{k}
closely parallels that of Ref.
[
75
]
we will be brief. The Fourier transform of
J_{\alpha,AB\lambda,ab\ell,cdm}^{k}
is

\displaystyle J_{\alpha,AB\lambda,ab\ell,cdm}(\vec{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\!\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}}J_{\alpha,AB\lambda,ab\ell,cdm}^{k}=\int\!\!\!\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}e^{i\vec{q}\cdot\vec{r}+i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{r}}q^{\alpha}p^{\beta}{\mathcal{P}}_{\lambda}(\mu_{pq})P_{\alpha ab\ell}^{q}P_{\alpha cdm}^{p}.

Using the spherical harmonic expansions

{\mathcal{P}}(\hat{q}\cdot\hat{p})=\frac{4\pi}{2\ell+1}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}Y_{lm}(\hat{q})^{*}Y_{lm}(\hat{p})\quad\textrm{and}\quad e^{i\vec{q}\cdot\vec{r}}=4\pi\sum_{\ell}i^{\ell}j_{\ell}(qr)\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}Y_{lm}(\hat{q})^{*}Y_{lm}(\hat{r})

we may factor the
\vec{q}
and
\vec{p}
integrals. Integrating directly over both
\hat{q}
and
\hat{p}
results in

J_{\alpha,AB\lambda,ab\ell,cdm}(\vec{r})=\frac{(-1)^{\lambda}}{4\pi^{4}}\!\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!dqq^{A+2}j_{\lambda}(qr)P_{\alpha ab\ell}^{q}\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!\!\!dpp^{B+2}j_{\lambda}(pr)P_{\alpha cdm}^{p}=:\frac{(-1)^{\lambda}}{4\pi^{4}}I_{A\lambda}(r)I_{B\lambda}(r)

reducing the problem to the computation of the single-dimensional integral
I_{A\lambda}
.

FFT-based acceleration of this integral proceeds by extending the
k
range of the power spectrum in both directions. Assume a power spectrum defined at
N_{k}
points, with an even spacing
{\mathcal{D}}_{k}
in
\log(k)
such that
k_{i+1}=e^{{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}k_{i}
, and with
N_{k}
equal to
2^{n}
for some positive integer
n
as required by the FFT algorithm. We extrapolate it by
3N_{k}/2
in either direction, as in Ref.
[
7
]
. Extrapolation uses a power law and then tapers off the power spectrum to zero in both directions. Since discrete Fourier transforms such as the FFT assume a periodic input function, this zero-padding is necessary to avoid jump discontinuities in the power. The total number of points in this extrapolated, zero-padded power spectrum is now
N_{k\mathrm{p}}=4N_{k}
. Further, as described in Ref.
[
75
]
the power spectrum is tilted by a factor
k^{-\nu_{\mathrm{FFT}}}
, with
\nu_{\mathrm{FFT}}=-2
, as a means of controlling divergences. Its discrete Fourier transform is

\displaystyle c_{\alpha,ab\ell,n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{h=0}^{N_{k\mathrm{p}}}P_{\alpha ab\ell}(k_{h})k_{h}^{-\nu_{\mathrm{FFT}}}e^{-2\pi ihn/N_{k\mathrm{p}}}={\mathscr{F}}\left\{P_{\alpha ab\ell}(k_{h})k_{h}^{-\nu_{\mathrm{FFT}}}\right\}_{n}^{h}

(4.47)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow P_{\alpha ab\ell}(k_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n}c_{\alpha,ab\ell,n}k_{h}^{\nu_{\mathrm{FFT}}+2\pi in/(N_{k\mathrm{p}}{\mathcal{D}}_{k})}.

(4.48)

Here
{\mathscr{F}}
represents the discrete Fourier transform, for which we may use an FFT.

With this theoretical machinery and the definitions

\displaystyle g(x,y)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\,{\mathcal{J}}_{x}(z)z^{y}=\frac{\Gamma[(x+y+1)/2]}{\Gamma[(x-y+1)/2]}

(4.49)

\displaystyle g_{\lambda An}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle g\left(\lambda+\frac{1}{2},\nu_{\mathrm{FFT}}+A+\frac{3}{2}+\frac{2\pi in}{N_{k}{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}\right).

(4.50)

in terms of Bessel function
{\mathcal{J}}
, we reduce
I_{A\lambda}
to

I_{A\lambda}(r)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sum_{n}c_{\alpha,ab\ell,n}g_{\lambda An}r^{-3-\nu_{\mathrm{FFT}}-A-2\pi in/(N_{k\mathrm{p}}{\mathcal{D}}_{k})}2^{\nu_{\mathrm{FFT}}+A+3/2+2\pi in/(N_{k\mathrm{p}}{\mathcal{D}}_{k})}.

Multiplying two such integrals, we recover
J_{\alpha,AB\lambda,ab\ell,cdm}(\vec{r})
:

J_{\alpha,AB\lambda,ab\ell,cdm}(\vec{r})=\frac{(-1)^{\lambda}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{n}\sum_{s}c_{\alpha,ab\ell,n}c_{\alpha,cdm,s}g_{\lambda An}g_{\lambda Bs}2^{\frac{2\pi i(n+s)}{N_{k\mathrm{p}}{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}}r^{-6-2\nu_{\mathrm{FFT}}-A-B-\frac{2\pi i(n+s)}{N_{k\mathrm{p}}{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}}.

Changing the inner summation index from
s
to
t=s+n
reveals a discrete convolution:

C_{\alpha,AB\lambda t,ab\ell,cdm}=\sum_{n}c_{\alpha,ab\ell,n}g_{\lambda An}c_{\alpha,cdm,t-n}g_{\lambda,B,t-n}.

(4.51)

Though a brute-force evaluation of this quantity for all
2N_{k\mathrm{p}}
values of
t
would require
{\mathcal{O}}(N_{k\mathrm{p}}^{2})
, steps, it may be computed in only
{\mathcal{O}}(N_{k\mathrm{p}}\log(N_{k\mathrm{p}}))
by multiplying the FFTs of the two quantities
c_{\alpha,ab\ell,n}g_{\lambda An}
and
c_{\alpha,cdm,n}g_{\lambda,B,n}
to be convolved, then taking the inverse FFT of the result. Fourier transforming
J_{\alpha,AB\lambda,ab\ell,cdm}(\vec{r})
back to
\vec{k}
-space and defining
F_{t}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}dz\sin(z)z^{-5-2\nu_{\mathrm{FFT}}-A-B-2\pi it/(N_{k\mathrm{p}}{\mathcal{D}}_{k})}
results in

\displaystyle J_{\alpha,AB\lambda,ab\ell,cdm}^{k_{u}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}dr4\pi r^{2}j_{0}(k_{u}r)J_{\alpha,AB\lambda,ab\ell,cdm}(r)

(4.52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{\lambda}}{\pi^{2}}2^{2+2\nu_{\mathrm{FFT}}+A+B}\sum_{t}C_{\alpha,AB\lambda t,ab\ell,cdm}F_{t}2^{\frac{2\pi it}{N_{k\mathrm{p}}{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}}k_{u}^{3+2\nu_{\mathrm{FFT}}+A+B+\frac{2\pi it}{N_{k\mathrm{p}}{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{\lambda}}{2\pi^{2}}(2k_{u})^{3+2\nu_{\mathrm{FFT}}+A+B}{\mathscr{F}}^{-1}\left\{C_{\alpha,AB\lambda t,ab\ell,cdm}F_{t}2^{\frac{2\pi it}{N_{k\mathrm{p}}{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}}\right\}^{t}_{u}

Thus the elementary
P^{(2,2)}
-type integral
J_{\alpha,AB\lambda,ab\ell,cdm}^{k}
has been expressed as an inverse FFT of a discrete convolution, allowing its computation in
{\mathcal{O}}(N_{k\mathrm{p}}\log(N_{k\mathrm{p}}))
steps.

We began the FFT acceleration procedure with a decomposition of
S_{\alpha,BNj,ab\ell,cdm}^{k}
into
J_{\alpha,AB\lambda,ab\ell,cdm}^{k}
integrals. Reassembling these parts into the whole, we find after some bookkeeping of summation indices that

\displaystyle S_{\alpha,BNj,ab\ell,cdm}^{k_{u}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{g=0}^{\ell+m}\frac{4^{g}(2k_{u})^{3+2\nu}}{2\pi^{2}}{\mathscr{F}}^{-1}\left\{\sum_{\lambda=0}^{\ell+m+g+N}\sum_{f=0}^{f_{\mathrm{max}}}F_{t}2^{\frac{2\pi it}{N_{k\mathrm{p}}{\mathcal{D}}_{k}}}{\tilde{C}}_{\alpha,BNgt,ab\ell,cdm}\right\}^{t}_{u}\qquad

(4.53)

\displaystyle{\tilde{C}}_{\alpha,BNgt,ab\ell,cdm}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,2f+{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda+N}-B,2g-2f-{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda+N}+B,\lambda,t,ab\ell,cdm}\ldots

(4.54)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{w=w_{\mathrm{min}}}^{w_{\mathrm{max}}}\sum_{v=v_{\mathrm{min}}}^{g}(-1)^{\lambda+v}\binom{g}{v}{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}_{\lambda,N+2v+2w+{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda+N}}h^{j\ell m}_{2w+{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda+N},f,g}

\displaystyle w_{\mathrm{min}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\max(0,\tfrac{\lambda-N-{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda+N}}{2}-g),\quad w_{\mathrm{max}}:=(\ell+m-g-{\mathcal{O}}_{\ell+m+g})/2

\displaystyle f_{\mathrm{max}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\min(m,g)-{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda+N},\qquad\quad v_{\mathrm{min}}:=(\lambda-N-{\mathcal{O}}_{\lambda+N})/2-w

Since the summations in Eq. (
4.54
) are independent of
t
, they need only be computed once for each
\lambda
and
f
. The final result, Eq. (
4.53
), is the FFT-accelerated mode-coupling integral from which we will construct the
A_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{\vec{k}}
integrals. Its computational cost scales with
N_{k\mathrm{p}}
as
{\mathcal{O}}(N_{k\mathrm{p}}\log(N_{k\mathrm{p}}))
as was the case with
J_{\alpha,AB\lambda,ab\ell,cdm}^{k}
.

We will see that the
P^{(2,2)}
-type mode-coupling integrals dominate the total computational cost. Suppose that we truncate the summations over angular modes,
\ell,~m<N_{\mu,\mathrm{NL}}
, in the non-linear integrals of Eq. (
4.45
). Since
j
,
g
,
e
, and
f
can each take
\sim N_{\mu,\mathrm{NL}}
values, the total number of FFT-accelerated integrals making up all of the
S_{\alpha,BNj,ab\ell,cdm}^{k}
, hence the total computational cost of the
P^{(2,2)}
-type mode-coupling integrals, scales as
{\mathcal{O}}(N_{\mu,\mathrm{NL}}^{6})
.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{q}{p}\mu_{pq}+\frac{1}{4}\frac{q^{2}}{p^{2}}\mu_{pq}^{2}

(4.55)

(4.59)

Substituting these into the expression for

Mode-coupling integrals of
P^{(1,3)}
-type

Computation of
A_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{(1,3)\vec{k}}
, as defined in Eq. (
3.29
), is the goal of the present Section. Reduction of this integral to a convolution, necessary for its acceleration, requires that its integrand depend only upon the single integration variable
q
. Thus we must explicitly integrate over both
\vartheta
and
\varphi
in order to proceed. We begin by averaging over
\vartheta
and
\varphi
factors containing integer powers of
c_{\vartheta}
,
s_{\vartheta}
, and
\mu_{\alpha q}
. By analogy with the
S_{\alpha,BN,ab,cd}^{\vec{k}}
integrals of Sec.
4
, we then define
P^{(1,3)}
-type mode-coupling terms from which we create a catalog of
A_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{(1,3)\vec{k}}

A_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{\vec{k}}
integrals.

5.1
Angular averages of elementary terms

Our first goal is to show that the integrand of
A_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{(1,3)\vec{k}}
may be explicitly averaged over
\vartheta
and
\varphi
, leaving only one integral to be carried out numerically. The chief difficulty is that the vertex factors
\gamma_{001}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}
and
\gamma_{111}^{\vec{k}\vec{q}\vec{p}}
have
p^{2}=k^{2}+q^{2}-2kqc_{\vartheta}
in their denominators, giving rise to badly-behaved terms which must be handled carefully. Thus we begin by performing
\vartheta
averages over elementary terms into which we will later decompose the integrals
A_{\alpha,acd,bef}^{(1,3)\vec{k}}
. As a shorthand, we define the dimensionless variables
x:=q/k
,
y(x)=(x+x^{-1})/2
, and
z(x):=(x-x^{-1})/2
. After
\vartheta
averaging, all that remains is an integral over
x>0
.

Terms without
p^{2}
in their denominators are trivial. Once again using the convention that angles in the subscript of triangular brackets denote averages over those angles, we have

\displaystyle\left<s_{\vartheta}^{2r}c_{\vartheta}^{n}\right>_{\vartheta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{n}\frac{\Gamma(r+1)\Gamma(\tfrac{n+1}{2})}{2\Gamma(r+\tfrac{n}{2}+\tfrac{3}{2})}

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left<c_{\vartheta}^{m}\mu_{\alpha q}^{n}\right>_{\vartheta,\varphi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{E}}_{m+n}\sum_{r=0}^{n/2}\binom{n}{2r}\kappa_{r}\mu_{\alpha}^{n-2r}\nu_{\alpha}^{2r}\frac{\Gamma(r+1)\Gamma(\tfrac{n+1}{2})}{2\Gamma(r+\tfrac{n}{2}+\tfrac{3}{2})}

(5.2)

(Recall the definitions of
{\mathcal{E}}_{n}
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{n}
from Eq. (
4.1
).) Our convention for summations is that
\sum_{i=A}^{B}
denotes a summation over all integers in the closed interval
[A,B]
, even if
A
and
B
are not integers. Thus the actual upper limit of
s
summation, for example, is
(n-{\mathcal{O}}_{n})/2
.

Terms with denominators proportional to
p^{2}
, when integrated over
\vartheta
, give rise to terms proportional to
{\mathscr{L}}:=\ln\left|\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right|
which must then be integrated over
x
. However, this factor has a logarithmic singularity at
x=1
. Its Taylor series is

{\mathscr{L}}(x):=\ln\left|\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right|=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2j+1}}{2j+1}&\textrm{ for $x<1$}\\
2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{-(2j+1)}}{2j+1}&\textrm{ for $x>1$}\end{array}\right..

(5.3)

For each integer
N
, we define a function

\lambda_{N}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}0&\textrm{ for $N=0$}\\
\frac{1}{2}(1-x^{N})\ln\left|\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right|+\sum_{j=0}^{(N-1)/2}\frac{x^{N}-2j-1}{2j+1}&\textrm{ for $N>0$}\\
\lambda_{-N}(x^{-1})=\frac{1}{2}(1-x^{N})\ln\left|\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right|+\sum_{j=0}^{(-N-1)/2}\frac{x^{N}+2j+1}{2j+1}&\textrm{ for $N<0$}\end{array}\right.

(5.4)

that is finite and bounded for all positive real
x
. In the remainder of this subsection, we will compute all necessary angular averages with
p^{2}
in the denominator, expressed as sums of
\lambda_{N}
functions and integer powers of
x
.

The simplest such angular average is

\left<\frac{zc_{\vartheta}^{n}}{c_{\vartheta}-y}\right>_{\vartheta}=-zy^{n}{\mathscr{L}}+z\sum_{j=0}^{(n-1)/2}\frac{y^{n-2j-1}}{2j+1}

(5.5)

for integer
n\geq 0
. After some algebra, we expand the first term on the right hand side as

\displaystyle-zy^{n}{\mathscr{L}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}b^{(n)}_{i}\lambda_{n+1-2i}(x)-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{(n-1)/2}U^{(n)}_{j}(x^{n-2j}-x^{-n+2j})

(5.6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{where}\quad b^{(n)}_{i}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\binom{n}{i}-\binom{n}{i-1}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad U^{(n)}_{j}:=\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{b^{(n)}_{i}}{2j-2i+1}.

(5.7)

The second term on the right hand side of Eq. (
5.5
) can be recast as

\displaystyle z\sum_{j=0}^{(n-1)/2}\frac{y^{n-2j-1}}{2j+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{(n-1)/2}V^{(n)}_{j}(x^{n-2j}-x^{-n+2j})\quad\mathrm{where}\quad V^{(n)}_{j}:=\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{4^{i}b^{(n-2i-1)}_{j-i}}{2i+1}.\qquad

(5.8)

The final step is to show that
U^{(n)}_{j}
and
V^{(n)}_{j}
are equal. Defining
u_{n}(\xi):=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}U^{(n)}_{j}\xi^{2j}
and
v_{n}(\xi):=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}V^{(n)}_{j}\xi^{2j}
, we will show that
u_{n}(\xi)=v_{n}(\xi)=(\xi^{-1}-\xi)(1+\xi^{2})^{n}{\mathrm{arctanh}}(\xi)
. Since the
U^{(n)}_{j}
and
V^{(n)}_{j}
are Taylor series coefficients of this function, they must also be equal.

For
u_{n}
, after reversing the summation order of
j
and
i
, we have

\displaystyle u_{n}(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=i}^{\infty}\binom{n}{i}\frac{\xi^{2j}}{2j-2i+1}-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=i}^{\infty}\binom{n}{i-1}\frac{\xi^{2j}}{2j-2i+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\binom{n}{i}\frac{\xi^{2j^{\prime}+2i}}{2j^{\prime}+1}-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\binom{n}{i-1}\frac{\xi^{2j^{\prime}+2i}}{2j^{\prime}+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\xi^{-1}{\mathrm{arctanh}}(\xi)\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\binom{n}{i}\xi^{2i}-\xi^{-1}{\mathrm{arctanh}}(\xi)\sum_{i^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\binom{n}{i^{\prime}}\xi^{2i^{\prime}+2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\xi^{-1}-\xi)(1+\xi^{2})^{n}{\mathrm{arctanh}}(\xi)

with
j^{\prime}:=j-i
and
i^{\prime}:=i-1
and the Taylor series
{\mathrm{arctanh}}(\xi)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\tfrac{\xi^{2j+1}}{2j+1}
.

The complication for
v_{n}(\xi)
is that, for
2j>n-1
, the upper arguments of the binomial coefficients become negative. Using the standard generalization
\binom{n}{i}=(-1)^{i}\binom{i-n-1}{i}
to extend the binomial coefficients to negative
n
, we have

\displaystyle v_{n}(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\binom{n-2i-1}{j^{\prime}}\frac{4^{i}\xi^{2j^{\prime}+2i}}{2i+1}-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{\infty}\binom{n-2i-1}{j^{\prime}-1}\frac{4^{i}\xi^{2j^{\prime}+2i}}{2i+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2\xi)^{2i}}{2i+1}\sum_{j^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\binom{n-2i-1}{j^{\prime}}\xi^{2j^{\prime}}-\xi^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2\xi)^{2i}}{2i+1}\sum_{j^{\prime\prime}=0}^{\infty}\binom{n-2i-1}{j^{\prime\prime}}\xi^{2j^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2\xi)^{2i}}{2i+1}(1+\xi^{2})^{n-2i-1}-\xi^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2\xi)^{2i}}{2i+1}(1+\xi^{2})^{n-2i-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\xi^{-1}-\xi)(1+\xi^{2})^{n}{\mathrm{arctanh}}\left(\frac{2\xi}{1+\xi^{2}}\right)

with
j^{\prime\prime}:=j^{\prime}-1
. Note that these extended binomial coefficients are used only in this particular proof. In practice we only need
V^{(n)}_{j}
for
2j\leq n-1
, for which the upper argument of each binomial coefficient is non-negative. The hyperbolic arctangent can be simplified as:

{\mathrm{arctanh}}\left(\frac{2\xi}{1+\xi^{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left|\frac{1+\tfrac{2\xi}{1+\xi^{2}}}{1-\tfrac{2\xi}{1+\xi^{2}}}\right|=\ln\left|\frac{1+\xi}{1-\xi}\right|=2\,{\mathrm{arctanh}}(\xi).

Thus
U^{(n)}_{j}
and
V^{(n)}_{j}
are equal, and

\left<\frac{zc_{\vartheta}^{n}}{c_{\vartheta}-y}\right>_{\vartheta}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}b^{(n)}_{i}\lambda_{n+1-2i}(x).

(5.9)

A more general term multiplies the above by
x^{m}
for integer
m
. Note that

x\lambda_{N}(x)=\lambda_{N+1}(x)-\lambda_{1}(x)+1+\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\frac{1}{N+1}&\textrm{if $N\geq 0$ is even;}\\
\frac{x}{N}&\textrm{if $N\leq-1$ is odd;}\\
0&\textrm{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

(5.10)

Applying this repeatedly, we find that for any positive integer
m
,

\displaystyle\left<\frac{zx^{m}c_{\vartheta}^{n}}{c_{\vartheta}-y}\right>_{\vartheta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}b^{(n)}_{i}\lambda_{m+n+1-2i}(x)-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sigma^{(m,n)}_{j}x^{j}

(5.11)

\displaystyle\left<\frac{zx^{-m}c_{\vartheta}^{n}}{c_{\vartheta}-y}\right>_{\vartheta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}b^{(n)}_{i}\lambda_{2i-m-n-1}(x)+\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sigma^{(m,n)}_{j}x^{-j}

(5.12)

where the coefficients

are defined recursively for non-negative integers

\displaystyle\sigma^{(0,n)}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\sigma^{(m,n)}_{0}={\mathcal{E}}_{m+n}\sum_{i=0}^{(m+n)/2}\frac{b^{(n)}_{i}}{m+n-2i+1}\textrm{ for $m\geq 1$}

(5.13)

\displaystyle\sigma^{(m,n)}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma^{(m-1,n)}_{0}+{\mathcal{O}}_{m+n}\sum_{i=0}^{(n-m+1)/2}\frac{b^{(n)}_{i}}{n-m-2i+2}\textrm{ for $m\geq 1$}

(5.14)

\displaystyle\sigma^{(m,n)}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma^{(m-1,n)}_{j-1}\textrm{ for $m\geq 1$ and $j\geq 2$}.

(5.15)

The most general elementary

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{\ell/2}\binom{\ell}{2r}\kappa_{r}\mu_{\alpha}^{\ell-2r}\nu_{\alpha}^{2r}\sum_{s=0}^{r}(-1)^{r-s}\binom{r}{s}\left<\frac{zx^{m}c_{\vartheta}^{\ell+n-2s}}{c_{\vartheta}-y}\right>_{\vartheta}

(5.17)

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{\ell/2}\mu_{\alpha}^{{\mathcal{O}}_{\ell}+2t}\sum_{s=0}^{\ell/2}(-1)^{s+t}\left<\frac{zx^{m}c_{\vartheta}^{\ell+n-2s}}{c_{\vartheta}-y}\right>_{\!\!\vartheta}\ldots

\displaystyle\times\sum_{r=0}^{\min(s,t)}\kappa_{\frac{\ell-{\mathcal{O}}_{\ell}}{2}-r}\binom{\ell}{2r+{\mathcal{O}}_{\ell}}\binom{\frac{\ell-{\mathcal{O}}_{\ell}}{2}-r}{s-r}\binom{\frac{\ell-{\mathcal{O}}_{\ell}}{2}-r}{t-r}.

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{\ell/2}\!\binom{\!\ell\!}{\!2r\!}\kappa_{r}\mu_{\alpha}^{\ell-2r}\nu_{\alpha}^{2r}\!\!\sum_{s=0}^{r}\!\binom{r}{s}(-1)^{r-s}\!\!\!\!\sum_{i=0}^{\ell+n-2s+1}\frac{b^{(\ell+n-2s)}_{i}\lambda_{\ell+m+n+1-2s-2i}(x)}{2^{\ell+n-2s}}\qquad

(5.19)

\mu_{\alpha}
for cases of interest here,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{2L,m,n}(\mu_{\alpha},x)

\displaystyle=

(5.20)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}^{(j)}_{2L,m,2I+{\mathcal{O}}_{j}-m}(\mu_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{L}{\hat{d}}^{(j)}_{LmIt}\mu^{2t}

(5.22)

5.2
P^{(1,3)}
-type power spectrum integrals

Since the vertex factors
\gamma_{abc}
of Eqs. (

The evenness of the exponents of
\mu_{\alpha}
and
\mu_{\alpha q}
is due to the fact that power spectra are expanded in terms of the squares of Legendre polynomials, which are necessarily even in their respective arguments. Thus the above may be expressed using

Consider a term

\displaystyle\int_{\vec{q}}\frac{zx^{m}c_{\vartheta}^{n}}{c_{\vartheta}-y}P_{\alpha ab}^{\vec{k}}P_{\alpha cd}^{\vec{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\vec{q}}\frac{zx^{m}c_{\vartheta}^{n}}{c_{\vartheta}-y}\sum_{i,\ell}{\mathcal{P}}_{i}(\mu_{\alpha})^{2}{\mathcal{P}}_{\ell}(\mu_{\alpha q})^{2}P_{\alpha abi}^{k}P_{\alpha cd\ell}^{q}

(5.24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,\ell}\sum_{I,L}\int_{\vec{q}}\frac{zx^{m}c_{\vartheta}^{n}}{c_{\vartheta}-y}\mu_{\alpha}^{2I}\mu_{\alpha q}^{2L}{\mathcal{Q}}_{Ii}{\mathcal{Q}}_{L\ell}P_{\alpha abi}^{k}P_{\alpha cd\ell}^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,\ell}\sum_{I,L}{\mathcal{Q}}_{Ii}{\mathcal{Q}}_{L\ell}\mu_{\alpha}^{2I}\Bigg[\int_{\vec{q}}{\mathcal{L}}_{2L,m,n}(\mu_{\alpha},x)P_{\alpha abi}^{k}P_{\alpha cd\ell}^{q}\ldots

\displaystyle\quad-{\mathrm{sign}}(m)\sum_{r=0}^{|m|}{\mathcal{M}}^{(r)}_{2L,m,n}(\mu_{\alpha})\int_{\vec{q}}x^{r\,{\mathrm{sign}}(m)}P_{\alpha abi}^{k}P_{\alpha cd\ell}^{q}\Bigg].\qquad

Similarly, considering a term
x^{m}c_{\vartheta}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\vec{q}}x^{m}c_{\vartheta}^{n}P_{\alpha ab}^{\vec{k}}P_{\alpha cd}^{\vec{q}}

\displaystyle\sum_{i,\ell}\sum_{I,L}{\mathcal{Q}}_{Ii}{\mathcal{Q}}_{L\ell}\mu_{\alpha}^{2I}\int_{\vec{q}}x^{m}c_{\vartheta}^{n}\mu_{\alpha q}^{2L}P_{\alpha abi}^{k}P_{\alpha cd\ell}^{q}

(5.25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,\ell}\sum_{I,L}{\mathcal{Q}}_{Ii}{\mathcal{Q}}_{L\ell}\mu_{\alpha}^{2I}{\mathcal{M}}^{(1)}_{2L,1,n}(\mu_{\alpha})\int_{\vec{q}}x^{m}P_{\alpha abi}^{k}P_{\alpha cd\ell}^{q}.

These motivate the definitions

\displaystyle W_{\alpha,abcd,tnr}^{\vec{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,\ell}\sum_{I,L}{\mathcal{Q}}_{Ii}{\mathcal{Q}}_{L\ell}\mu_{\alpha}^{2I}{\mathcal{M}}^{(r)}_{2L,t,n}(\mu_{\alpha})\int_{\vec{q}}P_{\alpha abi}^{k}P_{\alpha cd\ell}^{q}

\displaystyle Z_{\alpha,abcd,tn}^{\vec{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,\ell}\sum_{I,L}{\mathcal{Q}}_{Ii}{\mathcal{Q}}_{L\ell}\mu_{\alpha}^{2I}\int_{\vec{q}}{\mathcal{L}}_{2L,t,n}(\mu_{\alpha},x)P_{\alpha abi}^{k}P_{\alpha cd\ell}^{q}

and

to
\infty

to

from

\mu_{\alpha}
multiplying a single integral over

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(\mu_{\alpha})^{2}W_{\alpha,abcd,tnr,j}^{k}

\displaystyle W_{\alpha,abcd,tnr,j}^{k}

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}d^{j\ell m}_{tIr}W_{\alpha,ab\ell,cdm}^{k}\quad\textrm{ with }I:=\frac{t+n-r}{2}

(5.30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\vec{q}}P_{\alpha ab\ell}^{k}P_{\alpha cdm}^{q}.

(5.31)

In the same manner, by applying the appropriate basis transformations to

of Eq. (

(5.32)

\displaystyle M_{\min}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max(0,m-v,v-m-n-1)

(5.33)

\displaystyle Z_{\alpha,abcd,tn}^{\vec{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(\mu_{\alpha})^{2}Z_{\alpha,abcd,tn,j}^{k}

Now that we have confirmed the accuracy of a purely perturbative calculation of the non-linear neutrino power spectrum using

7
Conclusions

We have derived, implemented, and validated the accuracy of a new non-linear cosmological perturbation theory for massive neutrinos and other hot dark matter species, which we call
FlowsForTheMasses
. Deriving the equations of motion, Eqs. (
3.10
-
3.13
), based upon an extension of the Time-RG perturbation theory, and Eqs. (
3.18
-
3.2
) for the bispectrum integrals, we identified the extended mode-coupling integrals
A_{\alpha,acd,bef,j}^{k}
as the most computationally expensive components of the perturbation theory. Sections
4
and
5
constructed the computational machinery of
FlowsForTheMasses
by using Fast Fourier Transforms to accelerate the mode-coupling computations by more than two orders of magnitude.

In Sec.
6
we confirmed the convergence and accuracy of
FlowsForTheMasses
for neutrino power spectrum computations. Even for neutrino fractions as high as
\Omega_{\nu,0}h^{2}=0.005
, around the maximum allowed by the current data when the dark energy is allowed substantial freedom to vary, we find in Figs.
15
and
16
that a purely perturbative computation of the neutrino power spectrum agrees with N-body simulations to better than
10\%
up to
k=1~h/
Mpc, an impressive feat. Errors will be even smaller for the lower-mass neutrinos preferred by the data under restrictive assumptions about the dark energy.

Moreover, the bulk of this error is due to the
\sim 10\%
of the neutrinos which begin with the lowest velocities. It is precisely these which are most amenable to an N-body treatment.
FlowsForTheMasses
holds the most promise in combination with particle simulations, since perturbation theory is most accurate for precisely those fast-moving, weakly-clustering neutrinos that present the most challenges to simulations. Non-linear perturbation theory can accurately evolve
80\%-90\%
of the neutrino population for
\Omega_{\nu,0}h^{2}
as high as
0.005
. It can also allow the remaining neutrinos to be initialized as particles later, when their thermal velocities are smaller, minimizing the resulting shot noise in their velocities.

in terms of wave-number-independent numerical coefficients.

Collecting our results for
{\mathcal{M}}_{\ell mn}^{(j)}=\sum_{t}{\hat{d}}^{(j)}_{LmIt}\mu_{\alpha}^{2t}
, we have:

{\hat{d}}^{(0)}_{LmIt}=(-1)^{t+L}\binom{L}{t}\sum_{r=0}^{t}\frac{(r+\tfrac{1}{2})_{L-r}}{(L-r)!}\binom{t}{r}\sum_{v=0}^{L+I}\frac{1}{1+2v}\sum_{s=0}^{L+I-v}(-1)^{s}\binom{L-r}{s}\frac{b^{(2L+2I-2s-m)}_{L+I-s-v}}{2^{2L+2I-2s-m}}\qquad

(A.11)

{\hat{d}}^{(1)}_{L1It}=\frac{t!}{2}\binom{I}{t}\binom{L}{t}\frac{(t+1/2)_{L-t}}{(I+1/2)_{L+1}},\quad{\hat{d}}^{(1)}_{L2It}=2{\hat{d}}^{(1)}_{L1It},\quad{\hat{d}}^{(1)}_{L3It}=4{\hat{d}}^{(1)}_{L1It}-2{\hat{d}}^{(1)}_{L,1,I-1,t}.

(A.12)

Since
j
may be lowered using the recursion relation of Eq. (
5.15
), the above completely specify all
{\mathcal{M}}_{\ell mn}^{(j)}
of interest to us here.

Appendix B
Divergence cancellation

Omitted from the catalog of
P^{(1,3)}
-type
A_{\alpha,acd,bef,j}^{k}
integrals in Sec.
5.3
are several divergent terms which cancel against similar divergences in the
P^{(2,2)}
-type terms
S_{\alpha,\pm 2,2,e1,fb}^{\vec{k}}
and
S_{\alpha,-\pm 2,2,eb,f1}^{\vec{k}}
in the catalog of Sec.
4.4
. This cancellation was considered thorougly in Ref.
[
75
]
, which argued that it was a necessary consequence of the translation invariance of observable power spectra. One-loop corrections
P^{(2,2)}
and
P^{(1,3)}
are not observable individually, but only together, thus IR divergences in each must cancel when they are added.

Since we need not modify their argument, our task here is one of bookkeeping to ensure that divergences in our
A_{\alpha,acd,bef,j}^{(2,2)k}
and
A_{\alpha,acd,bef,j}^{(1,3)k}
do actually cancel when these are combined into
A_{\alpha,acd,bef,j}^{k}
. For convenience we expand the power in a basis of even powers of
\mu_{\alpha}
:

P_{\alpha ab}^{\vec{k}}=\sum_{L}\mu_{\alpha}^{2L}P_{\alpha abL}^{k}\quad\textrm{with}\quad P_{\alpha abL}^{k}=\sum_{\ell=L}^{\infty}{\mathcal{Q}}_{L\ell}P_{\alpha ab\ell}^{k}.

(B.1)

Mode-coupling integrals of
P^{(1,3)}
type have the following divergent terms:

\displaystyle A_{\alpha,001,bef}^{\vec{k}}

\displaystyle~:~

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{L=0}^{\infty}\sum_{M=0}^{\infty}\mu_{\alpha}^{2L}{\mathcal{M}}_{2M,1,2}^{(1)}(\mu_{\alpha})P_{\alpha beL}^{k}\int_{\vec{q}}x^{-2}P_{\alpha f1M}(kx)

(B.2)

\displaystyle A_{\alpha,111,bef}^{\vec{k}}

\displaystyle~:~

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{L=0}^{\infty}\sum_{M=0}^{\infty}\mu_{\alpha}^{2L}{\mathcal{M}}_{2M,1,2}^{(1)}(\mu_{\alpha})P_{\alpha beL}^{k}\int_{\vec{q}}x^{-2}P_{\alpha f1M}(kx)\ldots

(B.3)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{L=0}^{\infty}\sum_{M=0}^{\infty}\mu_{\alpha}^{2L}{\mathcal{M}}_{2M,1,2}^{(1)}(\mu_{\alpha})P_{\alpha bfL}^{k}\int_{\vec{q}}x^{-2}P_{\alpha e1M}(kx)

where
x
represents either
q/k
or
p/k
.

Since
S_{\alpha,BN,ab,cd}^{\vec{k}}=S_{\alpha,-B,N,cd,ab}^{\vec{k}}
, without loss of generality we exclusively consider small-
p
divergences in
S_{\alpha,-2,N,ab,cd}^{\vec{k}}
. Such divergences arise in the monopole terms,
\lambda=0
, of Eq. (
2.25
), and specifically,
J_{\alpha,A,-2,0,abL,cdM}^{k}=\int_{\vec{q}}q^{A}p^{-2}{\mathcal{P}}_{0}(\mu_{pq})P_{\alpha abL}^{q}P_{\alpha cdM}^{p}
for integer
A
. Near
p=0
,
q\rightarrow k
, so this integral behaves as
k^{A-2}P_{\alpha abL}^{k}\int_{\vec{q}}x^{-2}P_{\alpha cdM}(kx)
with
x=p/k
. Already this resembles the divergent integrals in Eqs. (
B.2
-
B.3
); all that remains is to compute the coefficients and
\mu_{\alpha}
-dependences of these
J
monopole terms.

We start with Eq. (
4.31
). Making the replacements
c_{\vartheta}=(p\mu_{pq}+q)/k
,
\tilde{c}_{\vartheta}=(q\mu_{pq}+p)/k
,
s_{\vartheta}=p\sqrt{1-\mu_{pq}^{2}}/k
, and
\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}=-q\sqrt{1-\mu_{pq}^{2}}/k
, we see that

\displaystyle c_{\vartheta}^{2L-i}\tilde{c}_{\vartheta}^{2M+i-2r}s_{\vartheta}^{i}\tilde{s}_{\vartheta}^{2r-i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{q}{k}\right)^{2L}\left(\frac{p}{k}\right)^{2M}\sum_{a=0}^{r}\sum_{b=0}^{2L-i}\sum_{d=0}^{2M-2r+i}\!\!(-1)^{i+a}\binom{\!r\!}{\!a\!}\binom{\!2L\!-\!i\!}{b}\binom{\!2M\!-\!2r\!+\!i\!}{d}\ldots

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\mu_{pq}^{2a+b+d}(q/p)^{2r-2i-b+d}.

The exponent of
p
is smallest when
d
takes its maximum value and
i
and
b
their minimum values, resulting in the cancellation of all powers of
p
, and a summand proportional to
(q/k)^{2L+2M}
, which is unity in the low-
p
limit. The resulting exponent of
\mu_{pq}
is
2a+2M-2r
.

Inserting this into Eq. (
4.31
) for
\left<\mu_{\alpha q}^{2L}\mu_{\alpha p}^{2M}\right>_{\varphi}
, and this in turn into Eq. (
4.42
) for
S_{\alpha,-2,N,ab,cd}^{\vec{k}}
, we see that the divergent terms may only arise from

\sum_{r=0}^{L+M}\mu_{\alpha}^{2L+2M-2r}\nu_{\alpha}^{2r}\kappa_{r}\binom{\!2M\!}{\!2r\!}\int_{\vec{q}}\left(\frac{q}{k}\right)^{2L+2M}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)^{-2}P_{\alpha abL}^{q}P_{\alpha cdM}^{p}\sum_{a=0}^{r}(-1)^{a}\binom{\!r\!}{\!a\!}\mu_{pq}^{2a+2M+N-2r}.

Furthermore, only even powers of
\mu_{pq}
contribute to a monopole term, so divergences are only present for even
N
. Assuming
N
to be even, the projection of
\mu_{pq}^{2a+2M+N-2r}
on the monopole is
(2a+2M+N-2r+1)^{-1}
. Changing summation variables from
a
to
s:=r-a
, we see that the divergent term in
S_{\alpha,-2,N,ab,cd}^{\vec{k}}
is the summation over
L
and
M
of:

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\frac{J_{\alpha,2L+2M+2,-2,0,abL,cdM}^{k}}{k^{2L+2M}}\sum_{r=0}^{L+M}\mu_{\alpha}^{2L+2M-2r}\nu_{\alpha}^{2r}\kappa_{r}\binom{\!2M\!}{\!2r\!}\sum_{s=0}^{r}\frac{(-1)^{r-s}\binom{r}{s}}{2M\!+\!N\!-\!2s\!+\!1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J_{\alpha,2L+2M+2,-2,0,abL,cdM}^{k}}{k^{2L+2M}}\sum_{r=0}^{L+M}\mu_{\alpha}^{2L+2M-2r}\nu_{\alpha}^{2r}\kappa_{r}\binom{\!2M\!}{\!2r\!}\sum_{s=0}^{r}\frac{(-1)^{r-s}\binom{r}{s}\sigma_{1}^{(1,2M+N-2s)}}{2^{2M+N-2s}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{J_{\alpha,2L+2M+2,-2,0,abL,cdM}^{k}}{k^{2L+2M}}\mu_{\alpha}^{2L}{\mathcal{M}}_{2M,1,2}^{(1)}(\mu_{\alpha}).

Thus our result is

\left.S_{\alpha,-2,N,ab,cd}^{\vec{k}}\right|_{\rm div}=\sum_{L=0}^{\infty}\sum_{M=0}^{\infty}\frac{J_{\alpha,2L+2M+2,-2,0,abL,cdM}^{k}}{k^{2L+2M}}\mu_{\alpha}^{2L}{\mathcal{M}}_{2M,1,N}^{(1)}(\mu_{\alpha}).

(B.4)

Evidently the
\mu_{\alpha}
-dependent factors
\mu_{\alpha}^{2L}{\mathcal{M}}_{2M,1,N}^{(1)}(\mu_{\alpha})
in Eq. (
B.4
) are the same as those in Eqs. (

-
B.3

4.61

4.64

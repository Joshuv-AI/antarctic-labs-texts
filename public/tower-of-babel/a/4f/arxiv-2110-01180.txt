Graphs with disjoint cycles classification via the talented monoid
Roozbeh Hazrat, Alfilgen N. Sebandal, Jocelyn P. Vilela

Abstract
. We characterise directed graphs consisting of disjoint cycles via their talented monoids. We show that a graph E E consists of disjoint cycles precisely when its talented monoid T E T_{E} has a certain Jordan-Hölder composition series. These are graphs whose associated Leavitt path algebras have finite Gelfand-Kirillov dimension. We show that this dimension can be determined as the length of certain ideal series of the talented monoid. Since T E T_{E} is the positive cone of the graded Grothendieck group K 0 gr ​ ( L 𝗄 ​ ( E ) ) K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)) , we conclude that for graphs E E and F F , if K 0 gr ​ ( L 𝗄 ​ ( E ) ) ≅ K 0 gr ​ ( L 𝗄 ​ ( F ) ) K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E))\cong K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(F)) then GKdim ⁡ L 𝗄 ​ ( E ) = GKdim ⁡ L 𝗄 ​ ( F ) \operatorname{GKdim}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)=\operatorname{GKdim}L_{\mathsf{k}}(F) , thus providing more evidence for the Graded Classification Conjecture for Leavitt path algebras.



1.
Introduction

To a row-finite directed graph
E
, with vertices
E^{0}
and edges
E^{1}
, one can naturally associate a commutative monoid
M_{E}
, called the
graph monoid
of
E
. It is a
free commutative monoid over the vertices, subject to identifying a vertex with the sum of vertices it is connected to:

M_{E}=\Big\langle\,v\in E^{0}\,\,\Big|\,\,v=\sum_{v\rightarrow u\in E^{1}}u\,\Big\rangle,

when
v
emits edges.

The graph monoids were introduced by Ara, Moreno and Pardo (
[
4
]
,
[
1
, §6]
) in relations with the theory of Leavitt path algebras. It was shown that the group completion of
M_{E}
is the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E))
, where
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
is the Leavitt path algebra with coefficient in a field
\mathsf{k}
, associated to
E
.

The so-called
talented monoid

T_{E}
of
E
, can be considered as the “time evolution model” of the monoid
M_{E}
. It is defined as

T_{E}=\Big\langle\,v(i),v\in E^{0},i\in\mathbb{Z}\,\,\Big|\,\,v(i)=\sum_{v\rightarrow u\in E^{1}}u(i+1)\,\Big\rangle,

when
v
emits edges.
T_{E}
is equipped with a
\mathbb{Z}
-action, defined by
{}^{n}v(i)=v(i+n)
, where
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

In this form the talented monoids were introduced in
[
8
]
and further studied in
[
6
,
9
]
. It was shown that the talented monoid captures certain geometry of its associated graph, and hence the algebraic properties of the corresponding Leavitt path algebra. For instance, a graph has Condition (L), i.e., any cycle has an exit, if and only if the group
\mathbb{Z}
acts freely on
T_{E}

[
8
]
. Or, the period of a graph, i.e., the greatest common divisor of the lengths of all closed paths based a vertex, can be described completely via its associated talented monoid
[
6
]
. Using these results one can give finer descriptions of purely infinite simple Leavitt path algebras associated to graphs.

The Graded Classification Conjecture states that the graded Grothendieck groups can completely classify Leavitt path algebras (
[
7
]
,

[
1
, §7.3.4]
). As the group completion of
T_{E}
is the graded Grothendieck group
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E))
(see
[
5
]
), the Graded Classification Conjecture can be restated to say that the talented monoid
T_{E}
is a complete invariant for the class of Leavitt path algebras.

In this note we show that the talented monoid can be used to classify the class of graphs which consist of disjoint cycles. Specifically, a graph
E
consists of disjoint cycles precisely when its talented monoid
T_{E}
has a certain Jordan-Hölder composition series (Theorem
4.2
).
These are precisely those graphs that their associated Leavitt path algebras have finite Gelfand-Kirillov (GK) dimension. We show that the GK-dimension of a Leavitt path algebra
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
is the length of a certain ideal series in
T_{E}
(Theorem
4.4
). We conclude that for graphs
E
and
F
, if
T_{E}\cong T_{F}
as
\mathbb{Z}
-monoids, then
\operatorname{GKdim}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)=\operatorname{GKdim}L_{\mathsf{k}}(F)
. In particular, this shows that if there is a
\mathbb{Z}
-isomorphism of order groups

K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E))\cong K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(F))
then
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
and
L_{\mathsf{k}}(F)
have the same GK-dimension. This is yet another evidence to support the Graded Classification Conjecture.

In contrast to the talented monoid
T_{E}
, the graph monoid
M_{E}
can’t be used to describe the GK-dimension of a Leavitt path algebra
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
.

1.1.
Notations

Throughout we use
\subset
for the strict inclusion and
\mathbb{N}
for the set of non-negative integers.

2.
Graphs and monoids

2.1.
Graphs with disjoint cycles

A directed graph
E
is a tuple
(E^{0},E^{1},r,s)
, where
E^{0}
and
E^{1}
are
sets and
r,s
are maps from
E^{1}
to
E^{0}
. A graph
E
is
finite
if
E^{0}
and
E^{1}
are both finite. We think of each
e\in E^{1}
as an edge
pointing from
s(e)
to
r(e)
. A graph
E
is said to be
row-finite
if for each vertex
v\in E^{0}
,
there are at most finitely many edges in
s^{-1}(v)
. A vertex
v
for which
s^{-1}(v)

is empty is called a
sink
. In a row-finite graph
E
, a vertex
v\in E^{0}
which is not a sink, is called a
regular vertex
. Throughout the paper we only consider row-finite graphs.

We use the convention that a (finite)
path

p
in
E
is
a sequence
p=e_{1}e_{2}\cdots e_{n}
of edges
e_{i}
in
E
such that

r(e_{i})=s(e_{i+1})
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. We define
s(p)=s(e_{1})
, and
r(p)=r(e_{n})
. We say
n
is the
length
of
p
. Vertices are considered paths of length zero. A path
p=e_{1}e_{2}\dots e_{n}
is called a
cycle
if
s(p)=r(p)
and for any edge
f\in E^{1}
with
s(f)=s(e_{i})
, for some
1\leq i\leq n
, we have
f=e_{i}
.

A
morphism

\phi:E\rightarrow F
, for directed graphs
E
and
F
, is a pair of maps (both denoted
\phi
),
\phi:E^{0}\rightarrow F^{0}
and
\phi:E^{1}\rightarrow F^{1}
such that

\phi(s(e))=s(\phi(e))
and
\phi(r(e))=r(\phi(e))
, for any
e\in E^{1}
. Furthermore, if the maps are injective and
|s^{-1}(v)|=|s^{-1}(\phi(v))|
, for any
v\in E^{0}
, then
\phi
is called a
complete morphism
.

A subset
H\subseteq E^{0}
is said to be
hereditary
if
for any
e\in E^{1}
,
s(e)\in H
implies
r(e)\in H
. A subset
H\subseteq E^{0}
is called
saturated
if whenever for a regular vertex
v
,
r(s^{-1}(v))\subseteq H
then
v\in H
. Throughout the paper we work with hereditary saturated subsets of
E^{0}
. If
H
is a hereditary and saturated subset, we consider the directed graph, denoted by
H
again, consisting of all vertices of
H
and all edges emitting from these vertices. Clearly there is a complete inclusion morphism
i:H\rightarrow E
. For a subset
S
, we also need to recall the construction of hereditary and saturated subset
\langle S\rangle
and a fact about them. Let
S
be a hereditary subset of
E^{0}
. Then
\langle S\rangle:=\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}S_{i}
, where
S_{0}=S
and
S_{i}=\{v\in E^{0}\mid r(s^{-1}(v))\subseteq S_{i-1}\}
is the smallest hereditary and saturated subset of
E
containing
S
. This simple lemma will be used throughout the paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
E
be a row-finite graph,
S
a subset of
E
and
\langle S\rangle
its hereditary and saturated closure. Let
v\in\langle S\rangle
.

(1)

There is path
p
with
s(p)=v
and
r(p)\in S
.

(2)

Any path starting from
v
eventually ends in
S
, i.e., for any path
p
with
s(p)=v
, there is a path
q
such that
s(q)=r(p)
and
r(q)\in S
.

(3)

There is an
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that any path
p
with
s(p)=v
of length larger than
l
ends in
S
, i.e.,
r(p)\in S
.

Proof.

The following argument implies all three statements of the lemma.
Let
v\in\langle S\rangle
. Then there is an
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that
v\in S_{l}
. Let
p=e_{1}e_{2}\dots e_{l}
be a path of length
l
starting from
v
. Since
v\in S_{l}
, by the construction of
S_{l}
, we have
r(e_{1})=s(e_{2})\in S_{l-1}
. Thus
r(e_{2})=s(e_{3})\in S_{l-2}
. Continuing,
r(p)=r(e_{l})\in S_{l-l}=S
.
∎

We need the following correspondence between the hereditary and saturated subsets of a graph and its quotient graph.
For hereditary saturated subsets
H
and
K
of the graph
E
with
H\subseteq K
, define the quotient graph
K/H
as a graph such that

(K/H)^{0}=K^{0}\setminus H^{0}
and
(K/H)^{1}=\{e\in E^{1}\;|\;s(e)\in K,r(e)\notin H\}
. The source and range maps of
K/H
are restricted from the graph
E
. If
K=E^{0}
, then
K/H
is the
quotient graph

E/H
(
[
1
, Definition 2.4.11]
). With this construction,

(K/H)^{0}
is a hereditary and saturated subset of the graph
E/H
, which we also denote
K/H
. We have a natural (inclusion) morphism
\phi:E/H\rightarrow E
which is injective on vertices and edges.

The following lemma will be used in a crucial way in Theorem
4.4
to calculate the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of a Leavitt path algebra from its associated talented monoid. Denote
\mathcal{H}(E,H)
to be all hereditary and saturated subsets of the graph
E
containing the hereditary and saturated subset
H
and
\mathcal{H}(E):=\mathcal{H}(E,\emptyset)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
E
be a row-finite graph and
H
a hereditary and saturated subset of
E
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

a hereditary and saturated subset of

the natural morphism of graphs.

(1)

If

has no sinks then the graph

has no sink.

(2)

If

contains all the sinks, then the graph
E/H
has no sink.

(3)

Let

be generated by all sinks in

and

via the morphism
\phi
.

(4)

Let

be generated by vertices on cycles with no exits. Then there is one-to-one correspondence between sinks in
E/H
and

via the morphism
\phi
.

Proof.

(1) [and (2)] Suppose

is a sink in
E/H
, which in particular, implies that

. Since
E
has no sinks [
H
contains all the sinks], then there are edges emitting from

in
E
. By construction of

, in order

to be a sink in
E/H
, all the edges emitting from

should land in
H
. But since
H
is saturated, this means

has to be in
H
which is not the case. Thus
E/H
has no sinks.

(3) Since the map
\phi:E/H\rightarrow E
is an inclusion, any cycle appearing in
E/H
, does appear in
E
as well. Suppose

is a cycle in

based on the vertex

. We show that

. If

, then by Lemma

, any long enough path starting from

ends in a sink (the generating set of

). Thus considering the path

, where

is large enough, implies that

is a sink which is not the case. Thus
v
is not in

and consequently all the vertices and edges of

are not in

. Therefore the cycle

appears in

.

(4) If
v
is a sink in

, then

. Otherwise by Lemma

, there is path from

which connects to a cycle, a contradiction to

appears as a sink in

. Furthermore, if

is not a sink in

∎

Lemma 2.10

Definition 3.1

Let

The relations defining

The following lemma is essential to the remainder of this paper, as it allows us to translate the relations in the definition of

Lemma 3.2

(

Let

(1)

(The Confluence Lemma) If

in

such that

and

. (Note that, in this case,

in

.)

(2)

If

and

in

, then there exist

such that

,

and

.

Theorem 3.6

. Then

(1)
\Rightarrow
(3) We argue by induction on the length of maximal chain of cycles in

is a multi-headed comet and by Corollary

is cyclic. Thus the upper cyclic series is

with the leading ideal
T_{E}
and

. Suppose the statement is valid for length

is a graph with the length of maximal chains of cycles to be

be the largest cyclic ideal of

which is the ideal generated by vertices on cycles with no exists in

(Theorem

). Consider

, where

is the saturated and hereditary subset generated by these vertices. Since the length of maximal chain of cycles of

is
n
(Lemma

) by induction the leading ideal of

is

and

The proof of the last part of the lemma is included in (1)

We are in a position to describe a graph consisting of disjoint cycles (possibly having sinks) via its talented monoid.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
E
be a finite graph,
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
its associated Leavitt path algebra and
T_{E}
its talented monoid. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

E
is a graph with disjoint cycles;

(2)

T_{E}
has a composition series of cyclic and non-comparable types;

(3)

L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
has a finite GK-dimension.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2) Suppose
E
is a graph with distinct cycles. If
E
has no sinks, then by Lemma
4.1
,
T_{E}
has a cyclic composition series. Otherwise, let
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}
be the set of all the sinks in
E
. By Theorem
3.11
,
I_{i}=\langle v_{i}\rangle
,
1\leq i\leq k
, are non-comparable minimal ideals of
T_{E}
and by Lemma
2.12
the series
0\subset I_{1}\subset I_{1}+I_{2}\subset\dots\subset I_{1}+I_{2}+\dots+I_{k}
is a composition series of non-comparable type for
I=I_{1}+I_{2}+\dots+I_{k}
.

Let
H
be the smallest hereditary and saturated subset containing
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\}
. Then
\langle H\rangle=I
. Consider the quotient graph
E/H
. If
E/H
is not empty, i.e.,
I\not=T_{E}
and
E
has cycles, then by Lemma
2.3
,
E/H
is a graph with no sink consisting of disjoint cycles. Therefore, by Lemma
4.1
,
T_{E/H}\cong T_{E}/I
has a cyclic composition series. Since
I
has a non-comparable composition series, by Lemma
2.11
,
T_{E}
has a mixed composition series.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1) The argument is similar to the corresponding proof of Lemma
4.1
. Suppose
T_{E}
has a chain of ideals

0=I_{0}\subset I_{1}\subset\dots\subset I_{n}=T_{E}
and its corresponding chain of hereditary and saturated subsets of
E
,

\emptyset=H_{0}\subset H_{1}\subset\dots\subset H_{n}=E^{0}
,
such that
I_{i+1}/I_{i}\cong T_{H_{i+1}/H_{i}}
,
0\leq i\leq n-1
, are either cyclic or non-comparable. We only need to show that
E
does not contain vertices which are base of more than one cycles. Suppose
v\in E^{0}
is a vertex on two cycles
C
and
D
. Then there is an
0\leq i\leq n-1
such that
v\in H_{i+1}
but not
H_{i}
. Therefore, none of the vertices on
C
and
D
also belong to
H_{i}
. This implies that
H_{i+1}/H_{i}
contains both cycles
C
and
D
. Therefore, there is an
j\in\mathbb{N}
such that
{}^{j}v<v
in
T_{H_{i+1}/H_{i}}
. But these monoids are either cyclic or non-comparable. This is a contradiction and thus
E
consists of disjoint cycles.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(3) This is Theorem
2.5
.

∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
E
be a finite graph with disjoint cycles,
L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)
its associated Leavitt path algebra and
T_{E}
its talented monoid. Let

be the largest non-comparable

\Gamma

the leading ideal of the upper cyclic series of

. Let

and

. Then

\operatorname{GKdim}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)=\begin{cases}2d_{1}&\text{if $d_{1}=d_{2}$;}\\
2d_{1}-1&\text{if $d_{1}\not=d_{2}$.}\end{cases}

(4.1)

Proof.

We show that
d_{1}
is the maximal length of chain of cycles in
E
and
d_{2}
is the maximal length of chain of cycles ending at a sink in

.
Let
H
be the hereditary and saturated subset generated by all sinks in
E
. By Lemma

,

and
T_{E}/S\cong T_{E/H}
. By Lemma
2.3
, the quotient graph

consists of disjoint cycles with no sinks and there is a one-to-one correspondence between the cycles in
E
and
E/H
. Thus any (maximal) chain of cycles in

does also appear in
E/H
and vice versa. Since
E/H
has no sinks, by Lemma
4.1
,
d_{1}=l_{c}(T_{E}/S)
is the maximal length of chain of cycles in
E/H
and consequently in
E
.

Now let
I
be the leading ideal of the upper cyclic series of
T_{E}
. Thus

T_{E}
has a series

0=I_{0}\subset I_{1}\subset\dots\subset I_{n}=I,

such that
I_{i+1}/I_{i}
,
0\leq i\leq n-1
, are largest cyclic ideals of
T_{E}/I_{i}
. Therefore we have a corresponding chain of hereditary and saturated subsets of
E

\emptyset=G_{0}\subset G_{1}\subset\dots\subset G_{n}=G.

(4.2)

Since
I
is the leading ideal,
T_{E}/I\cong T_{E/G}
has no non-zero periodic element.
Therefore,
E/G
has no cycle without exit. Since
E/G
is a graph with disjoint cycles, we conclude that all maximal chain of cycles in
E/G
ends at a sink. We show that these are exactly the maximal chain of cycles with an exit in
E
.
We first show that there is one-to-one correspondence between sinks in
E/G
and
E
via the morphism
\phi:E/G\rightarrow E
.
We argue on the chain of
G_{i}

4.2
). By the definition of the upper cyclic series, since

is the largest cyclic ideal of
T_{E}

3.10

is the hereditary and saturated subset generated by vertices of all cycles with no exits. Thus by Lemma
2.3
(4),
there is a one-to-one correspondence between sinks under the map
\phi:E/G_{1}\rightarrow E
.

The graph

consists of disjoint cycles and
T_{E}/I_{1}\cong T_{E/{G_{1}}}
. Since
I_{2}/I_{1}
is the largest cyclic ideal of
T_{E}/I_{1}
, by Lemmas
2.2
and
3.5
,
the subset
G_{2}
corresponds to hereditary and saturated subset in the graph
E/G_{1}
generated by all cycles without exists in
E/G_{1}
. Therefore by Lemma
2.3
(4), there is a one-to-one correspondence between sinks under the map
\phi:(E/G_{1})\big/(G_{2}/G_{1})\rightarrow E/G_{1}
. But
(E/G_{1})\big/(G_{2}/G_{1})=E/G_{2}
(see Lemma
2.2
). Therefore there is a one-to-one correspondence between sinks under the map
E/G_{2}\rightarrow E

E/G
and
E
.

Next we show any maximal chain of cycles ending at a sink in
E
does appear in
E/G
. Suppose
C_{1}\Rightarrow C_{2}\Rightarrow\dots\Rightarrow C_{k}
is a maximal chain ending at a sink
v
in

C_{k}
which ends in

. Since there is a one-to-one correspondence between sinks in
E
and
E/G
,

is also a sink in
E/G

’s are in

To obtain the length of these chains, we remove all the sinks from

Now we obtain the length of the longest chains via Lemma

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
E
and
F
be finite graphs. If there is a
\mathbb{Z}
-isomorphism of order groups
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E))\cong K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(F))
, then
\operatorname{GKdim}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)=\operatorname{GKdim}L_{\mathsf{k}}(F)
.

Proof.

Since
T_{E}
is the positive cone of the graded Grothendieck group
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E))

[
5
]
, we have
T_{E}\cong T_{F}
as
\mathbb{Z}
-monoids if and only if there is a
\mathbb{Z}
-isomorphism
K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(E))\cong K_{0}^{\operatorname{gr}}(L_{\mathsf{k}}(F))
, as order groups.

If
\operatorname{GKdim}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)<\infty
then
E
is a graph with disjoint cycles and sinks (Theorem
2.5
) and thus by Theorem
4.2
,
T_{E}
has a composition series. Thus
T_{F}
has a composition series and again Theorem
4.2
implies that
\operatorname{GKdim}L_{\mathsf{k}}(F)<\infty
. Now, Theorem
4.4
gives that
\operatorname{GKdim}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)=\operatorname{GKdim}L_{\mathsf{k}}(F)
.

On the other hand if
\operatorname{GKdim}L_{\mathsf{k}}(E)=\infty
, then the graph
E
is not a graph with disjoint cycles. Theorem
4.2
implies the same for the graph
F
and consequently,
\operatorname{GKdim}L_{\mathsf{k}}(F)=\infty
.
∎

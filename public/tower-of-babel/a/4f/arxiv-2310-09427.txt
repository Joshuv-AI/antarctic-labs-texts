Bounds on the Equation of State from QCD Inequalities and Lattice QCD Preprint: INT-PUB-23-043
Yuki Fujimoto, Sanjay Reddy

Abstract
We derive robust bounds on the equation of state (EoS) at finite baryon chemical potential using QCD inequalities and input from recent lattice-QCD calculations of thermodynamic properties of matter at nonzero isospin chemical potential. We use lattice data to deduce an upper bound on the baryon density of the symmetric nuclear matter at a given baryon chemical potential and a lower bound on the pressure as a function of the energy density. We also use constraints from perturbative calculations of the QCD EoS at high density derived in earlier work and causality to delineate robust bounds on the EoS of isospin symmetric matter at densities relevant to heavy-ion collisions.



I
Introduction

Recent studies have provided useful constraints on the equation of state (EoS) of dense matter using input from astrophysics and nuclear physics.
Several authors have shown that it is possible to combine measurements of heavy neutron star masses, neutron star radii, and tidal deformability to constrain the pressure of neutron-rich matter at baryon density in the range 2-4
n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, where
n_{\mathrm{sat}}\simeq 0.16~\mathrm{fm}^{-3}
is the saturation density inside nuclei.
At lower density, where nuclear matter is non-relativistic and dilute, nuclear Hamiltonians derived using phenomenological considerations and Chiral Effective Field Theory (
\chi
EFT) now provide useful constraints on the EoS, and a comprehensive discussion of these calculations and results are reviewed in
Drischler et al. 2021
.
At much higher baryon density, for
n_{\mathrm{B}}\gtrsim 40~n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, the typical momentum scale for quark and gluon interactions become much larger than
\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}\simeq 200
MeV and perturbative QCD (pQCD) calculations provide reliable and stringent constraints on the EoS as reviewed in
Ghiglieri et al. 2020
.
Further, in Ref.
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
, it was shown that thermodynamic consistency and stability conditions could be used to extrapolate the pQCD constraints to lower density.

The tightest constraints on the EoS of neutron-rich matter, which is characterized by a large isospin asymmetry due to constraints imposed by charge neutrality and beta-equilibrium, are obtained from observations of neutron star structure.
There is a one-to-one correspondence between the mass-radius relationship of neutron stars and the EoS through Einstein equation
Lindblom 1992
(and similarly for other observables such as tidal deformability), and hence astrophysical measurements provide robust bounds on the EoS. Indeed, it has been shown that the large portion of the allowed region of the EoS is strictly ruled out by the tidal deformability bound from the GW170817 event and the existence of the two-solar-mass pulsars
Kurkela et al. 2014
;
Annala et al. 2018
;
Landry et al. 2020
;
Annala et al. 2022
.

As for isospin symmetric matter, experimental measurements of the isoscalar giant monopole resonances in nuclei provide strong constraints on the incompressibility coefficient of symmetric nuclear matter EoS at saturation density (see, e.g., Ref.
Piekarewicz 2010
for a review), but reliable constraints at higher density have been elusive.
Although there has been progress in identifying several EoS-sensitive observables in heavy-ion collisions that access high baryon density, an interpretation of the data has been difficult. The systematic uncertainties associated with the hadronic transport models needed in this context remain poorly understood, and the EoS constraints derived using them (see, e.g.
Danielewicz et al. 2002
;
Oliinychenko et al. 2023
;
Sorensen et al. 2023
) are not as robust as the astrophysical constraints on isospin asymmetric matter.

The purpose of this study is to demonstrate that we can use lattice-QCD calculations of thermodynamic properties at
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}>0
to derive useful and robust bounds on the EoS of isospin symmetric matter at
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}>0
and low temperature (
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}
and
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
are isospin and baryon chemical potentials, respectively). Intriguingly, although the ground state of the matter at
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}=0
and nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}
, which is characterized by a Bose condensate of pions for
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}>m_{\pi}
(
m_{\pi}
is the pion mass)
Son and Stephanov 2001a
;
*Son:2000by
, is very different from baryonic matter at nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
, a QCD inequality that relates the pressures of matter at nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}
and
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
derived by Cohen in Refs.
Cohen 2003a
;
*Cohen:2004qp
, allows us to derive this bound. We employ results from recent lattice-QCD calculations at
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}>0

Abbott et al. 2023
to obtain an upper bound on the pressure as a function of
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
.

The QCD inequalities, pioneered by the seminal works dating back to four decades ago
Weingarten 1983
;
Witten 1983
;
Vafa and Witten 1984
, relate different correlation functions without explicitly evaluating them. They are derived from inequalities among the integrands in the path integral expressions; using the fact that the path integral measure is positive, path-integrated quantities also satisfy inequality relations (see also Ref.
Nussinov 1983
for a Hamiltonian variation approach). The QCD inequalities have been successful in discussing the symmetry-breaking patterns, comparing hadron masses, etc. in the
vacuum
(see Ref.
Nussinov and Lampert 2002
for a comprehensive review).
In contrast, at nonzero chemical potential, the QCD inequalities generally cannot hold between path-integrated quantities because the Fermion determinant becomes complex-valued, and the path integral measure is not positive – and is widely known as the Fermion sign problem.
However, there is an exceptional case where one can still obtain the positive path integral measure with nonzero chemical potential;
it is QCD at nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}
, which can be regarded as a complex phase-quenched theory for QCD at nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}

Alford et al. 1999
;
Son and Stephanov 2001a
;
*Son:2000by
(see also
Moore and Gorda 2023
).
From this fact, one can put an upper bound on the path integral of QCD at nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
from that of QCD at nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}
(see, e.g., Ref.
Hidaka and Yamamoto 2012
for an application of the QCD inequality at nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}
).

The positivity of the path integral measure in QCD at nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}
circumvents the sign problem and there have been several lattice studies of the phase structure and thermodynamic properties of the two-flavor isospin matter at nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}

Kogut and Sinclair 2002a
;
Kogut and Sinclair 2002b
;
Kogut and Sinclair 2004
;
de Forcrand et al. 2007
;
Detmold et al. 2008
;
Cea et al. 2012
;
Detmold et al. 2012
;
Detmold et al. 2013
;
Endrödi 2014
;
Brandt et al. 2018
;
Brandt and Endrodi 2019
;
Brandt et al. 2023a
;
Brandt et al. 2023b
;
Abbott et al. 2023
. Apart from the QCD-like theory with
N_{\mathrm{c}}=2
(see, e.g.,
Hands et al. 2006
;
Cotter et al. 2013
;
Boz et al. 2020
;
Begun et al. 2022
;
Iida and Itou 2022
), QCD with
N_{\mathrm{c}}=3
,
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}=0
and
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}>0
is the only system for which lattice calculation of the EoS at nonzero chemical potential around vanishing temperature is feasible. A recent lattice QCD calculation was able to construct states with a large number of pions (6144) corresponding to
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}>0
and negligible temperature and measure their thermodynamic properties
Abbott et al. 2023
. We use their results to constrain the thermodynamic properties of matter with
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}>0
at vanishing temperature. To our knowledge, this is the first example of a lattice bound on the EoS at nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
and
T\approx 0
.

The bound we derive applies to isospin symmetric matter at nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
with zero net strangeness. Such matter is interesting because it is realized in heavy-ion collisions and is relevant to the QCD critical point searches in heavy-ion collisions.
The critical point is the endpoint of a conjectured first-order line in the
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}-T
plane of isospin symmetric matter (for a review, see
Stephanov 2004
;
Bzdak et al. 2020
). If the first-order phase transition persists at low temperatures, the baryon density would be discontinuous across it.
To constrain the jump in density, we translate constraints on the pressure to a baryon number density constraint using the integral constraint method developed in Ref.
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
.
This method also allows us to derive constraints on the pressure as a function of the baryon energy density at nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
and low temperature. The bounds we find may not seem stringent but robustly exclude a soft EoS characterized by a low sound speed
v_{s}^{2}\lesssim 0.2
for
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
in the range
1
-
2
GeV. We find that they are competitive with bounds derived from robust extrapolations of pQCD that rely on thermodynamic consistency, stability, and causality conditions
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
. The interplay between these independent bounds could provide guidance for both Lattice-QCD and pQCD.

II
QCD inequalities at nonzero chemical potentials

Here, we review QCD inequalities at nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
and
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}
and derive the relationship between QCD partition functions of the baryonic matter and the isospin matter, which are denoted as
Z_{\mathrm{B}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})
and
Z_{\mathrm{I}}(\mu_{\mathrm{I}})
, respectively. The derivation is based on a Euclidean path integral representation and was presented in Refs.
Cohen 2003a
;
*Cohen:2004qp
(see also Ref.
Cohen 2003b
).
In the following, we consider QCD in an Euclidean space with
N_{\mathrm{f}}=2
degenerate flavors.
We will specify the temperature to be zero, but the inequalities shown below also hold at any temperature.

II.1
Partition function with nonzero baryon chemical potential

The Dirac operator,
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{\mathrm{q}})
, at a nonzero real-valued quark chemical potential,
\mu_{\mathrm{q}}
, is given by

\mathcal{D}(\mu)\equiv\not{D}+m-\mu_{\mathrm{q}}\gamma_{0}\,,

(1)

where the covariant derivative,
\not{D}\equiv\not{\partial}+ig\not{A}
, is a skew-Hermitian operator, i.e.
\not{D}^{\dagger}=-\not{D}
.
Furthermore, due to the skew-Hermiticity of
\not{D}
, the Dirac operator at
\mu_{\mathrm{q}}=0
becomes pseudo-Hermitian by
\gamma_{5}
, and

\gamma_{5}\mathcal{D}(\mu_{\mathrm{q}}=0)\gamma_{5}=-\not{D}+m=\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}(\mu_{\mathrm{q}}=0)\,.

(2)

When
\mu_{\mathrm{q}}=0
, this
\gamma_{5}
-pseudo-Hermiticity guarantees a positive path integral measure with
\det\mathcal{D}(\mu_{\mathrm{q}}=0)\geq 0
; this positivity is key to deriving QCD inequalities for path integrated quantities. In contrast, at
\mu_{\mathrm{q}}\neq 0
, the
\gamma_{5}
-pseudo-Hermiticity is lost because

\gamma_{5}\mathcal{D}(\mu_{\mathrm{q}})\gamma_{5}=\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}(-\mu_{\mathrm{q}})\neq\mathcal{D}^{\dagger}(\mu_{\mathrm{q}})\,.

(3)

and consequently, the path integral measure is no longer positive.

For QCD with two flavors, the partition function
Z_{\mathrm{B}}
in the path integral representation is given by

\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{B}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})=\int[dA]\left[\det\mathcal{D}(\tfrac{\mu_{\mathrm{B}}}{N_{\mathrm{c}}})\right]^{2}e^{-S_{\rm G}}\,,

(4)

where
S_{\rm G}
is the Euclidean action of QCD in the gauge sector.
In general, the fermion determinant in the above expression is complex.
However, using the charge conjugation symmetry that requires
Z_{\mathrm{B}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})=Z_{\mathrm{B}}(-\mu_{\mathrm{B}})
and the following relation
derived from Eq. (
3
)

\det\mathcal{D}(-\mu_{\mathrm{q}})=\det\gamma_{5}\mathcal{D}(-\mu_{\mathrm{q}})\gamma_{5}=\left[\det\mathcal{D}(\mu_{\mathrm{q}})\right]^{\ast}\,.

(5)

it can be shown that

\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{B}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})=\int[dA]\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\left[\det\mathcal{D}(\tfrac{\mu_{\mathrm{B}}}{N_{\mathrm{c}}})\right]^{2}e^{-S_{\rm G}}\,.

(6)

As should be expected on physical grounds since the partition function should be real-valued function
Cohen 2003a
;
*Cohen:2004qp
(see also Refs.
de Forcrand et al. 2003
;
de Forcrand 2009
;
Hsu and Reeb 2010
;
Giordano et al. 2020
;
Borsanyi et al. 2022
)

II.2
Partition function with nonzero isospin chemical potential

The path integral representation of the partition function of
u
and
d
quarks at finite
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}
and
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}=0
is given by

\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{I}}(\mu_{\mathrm{I}})

\displaystyle=\int[dA]\det\mathcal{D}(\tfrac{\mu_{\mathrm{I}}}{2})\det\mathcal{D}(-\tfrac{\mu_{\mathrm{I}}}{2})e^{-S_{\rm G}}\,,

(7)

where
\mathcal{D}(\mu_{\mathrm{q}})
is the Dirac operator defined in Eq. (
1
).
The arguments of the fermion determinants have opposite signs
\pm\mu_{\mathrm{I}}
as
u
and
d
quarks have opposite (third components of) isospins
I_{3}
.
From the relation (
5
),
Z_{\mathrm{I}}(\mu_{\mathrm{I}})
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{I}}(\mu_{\mathrm{I}})=\int[dA]\left|\det\mathcal{D}(\tfrac{\mu_{\mathrm{I}}}{2})\right|^{2}e^{-S_{\rm G}}\,.

(8)

The positivity of the path integral at finite
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}
measure mentioned earlier is now explicit in Eq. (
8
). We note that QCD at nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}
can also be regarded as the
phase-quenched
theory of two-flavor QCD at nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
in which the complex phase of the fermion determinant is discarded. This is quite distinct from the quenched approximation in which the entire fermion determinant is neglected.

II.3
QCD inequalities

From the relation
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}z^{2}\leq|z^{2}|=|z|^{2}
, the following inequality holds

\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\left[\det\mathcal{D}(\tfrac{\mu_{\mathrm{B}}}{N_{\mathrm{c}}})\right]^{2}\leq\left|\det\mathcal{D}(\tfrac{\mu_{\mathrm{B}}}{N_{\mathrm{c}}})\right|^{2}\,.

(9)

From this inequality, we get an upper bound on
Z_{\mathrm{B}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})

\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{B}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})\leq\int[dA]\left|\det\mathcal{D}(\tfrac{\mu_{\mathrm{B}}}{N_{\mathrm{c}}})\right|^{2}e^{-S_{\rm G}}\,.

(10)

The LHS and RHS differ by the phase of the determinant, so the inequality is saturated when the phase is unity.
The RHS can be recast as
Z_{\mathrm{I}}(\mu_{\mathrm{I}})
by mapping
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
to
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}
with an appropriate prefactor, which is
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}=2\mu_{\mathrm{B}}/N_{\mathrm{c}}
.
We see that Eq. (
10
) combined with Eq. (
8
) yields a useful inequality

\displaystyle Z_{\mathrm{B}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})\leq Z_{\mathrm{I}}\left(\mu_{\mathrm{I}}=\frac{2\mu_{\mathrm{B}}}{N_{\mathrm{c}}}\right)\,,

(11)

which was first derived by Cohen in Ref.
Cohen 2003a
;
*Cohen:2004qp
.

By taking the logarithm of this inequality, one obtains an upper bound on the pressure of the baryonic matter at a given
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
in terms of the pressure of isospin matter
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}(=2\mu_{\mathrm{B}}/N_{\mathrm{c}})

\displaystyle P_{\mathrm{B}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})\leq P_{\mathrm{I}}\left(\mu_{\mathrm{I}}=\frac{2\mu_{\mathrm{B}}}{N_{\mathrm{c}}}\right)\,.

(12)

This inequality will eventually be saturated at asymptotically high density, as can be seen in the perturbative expressions of the pressure at nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
and
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}
as they are identical up to order
\alpha_{s}^{2}
, where
\alpha_{s}
is the strong coupling constant.
The difference appears at
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})

Moore and Gorda 2023
.

II.4
An inequality for baryonic matter with isospin imbalance

The pressure inequality derived in the preceding discussion applies to isospin-symmetric baryonic matter.
From the convexity condition of the pressure derived in Ref.
Lee 2005
and given by

P(\mu_{\mathrm{B}},0)\leq P(\mu_{\mathrm{B}},\mu_{\mathrm{I}})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\left[P(\mu_{\mathrm{B}}+\mu_{\mathrm{I}},0)+P(\mu_{\mathrm{B}}-\mu_{\mathrm{I}},0)\right]\,.

(13)

one can derive a bound on the pressure at nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
and finite isospin imbalance
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}
, denoted as
P(\mu_{\mathrm{B}},\mu_{\mathrm{I}})
. Given the relations between baryonic and isospin pressures and the general pressure with an arbitrary isospin imbalance,
P_{\mathrm{B}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})=P(\mu_{\mathrm{B}},0)
and
P_{\mathrm{I}}(\mu_{\mathrm{I}})=P(0,\mu_{\mathrm{I}})
, and by combining with the QCD inequalities above, we obtain

P(\mu_{\mathrm{B}},\mu_{\mathrm{I}})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\left[P(0,\tfrac{2}{N_{\mathrm{c}}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}}+\mu_{\mathrm{I}}))+P(0,\tfrac{2}{N_{\mathrm{c}}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}}-\mu_{\mathrm{I}}))\right]\,.

(14)

This can, in principle, be applied to the neutron star matter where the charge neutrality and the beta-equilibrium condition are fulfilled with nonzero
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}
.
In practice, however, this inequality requires the value of
\mu_{\mathrm{I}}
as a function of
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
, which we cannot know from the current neutron star observations unless we assume some model.

P(\varepsilon)

P(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})=\frac{v_{s}^{2}\varepsilon_{\rm sat}}{1+v_{s}^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{B}}}{\mu_{\rm sat}}\right)^{1+v_{s}^{-2}}-1\right]\,,

(15)

where
v_{s}

IV
Bounds on
n_{\mathrm{B}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})
and
P(\varepsilon)

In this section, we use the integral constraint method developed in Ref.
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
to translate the lattice-QCD constraint on the function
P(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})
to obtain constraints on the functions
n_{\mathrm{B}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})
and
P(\varepsilon)
.
The integral constraint relies on a reference point where all of the thermodynamic properties are known. As mentioned earlier, at low density, the empirical properties of nuclear matter at the saturation density
n_{\mathrm{B}}=n_{\mathrm{sat}}=0.16
fm
-3
provides a reference point characterized by
P=0
at
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}=\mu_{\rm sat}=923~\text{MeV}
, and
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{\rm sat}=150~\text{MeV/fm}^{3}
. At asymptotically high density, one can use the pQCD calculations of the thermodynamic properties to establish a high-density reference point. In what follows, we use the low-density reference point and study its implications. Additional constraints that arise from implementing a high-density reference point will be discussed in section
V
.

IV.1
Bounds on
n_{\mathrm{B}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})

To establish constraints on the
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}-n_{\mathrm{B}}
plane, we first note that thermodynamic consistency requires
P(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})
to be a continuous function and thermodynamic stability requires
(d/d\mu_{\mathrm{B}})^{2}P(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})\geq 0
.
This implies
dn_{\mathrm{B}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})/d\mu_{\mathrm{B}}\geq 0
and indicates that the function
n_{\mathrm{B}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})
cannot decrease with increasing
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
.
Further, since causality requires
v_{s}^{2}\leq 1
, Eq. (
16
) implies a lower bound on the slope of the function
n_{\mathrm{B}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})

\frac{dn_{\mathrm{B}}}{d\mu_{\mathrm{B}}}\geq\frac{n_{\mathrm{B}}}{\mu_{\mathrm{B}}}\,.

(17)

Using the low-density reference point, and integrating Eq. (
17
) we arrive at a lower bound on the baryon density

n_{\rm min}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})=\frac{n_{\rm sat}}{\mu_{\rm sat}}\mu_{\mathrm{B}}\,.

(18)

To obtain an upper bound on the baryon density we define a general function
\check{n}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}};\mu_{0},n_{0})
to represent all possible behavior of the baryon density
n_{\mathrm{B}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})
in the ground state that passes through the point
(\mu_{0},n_{0})
and is compatible with Eq. (
17
) and subject to the boundary condition set by the low-density reference point. Since
n_{\mathrm{B}}=dP/d\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
and
P(\mu_{\mathrm{sat}})=0
, we obtain the pressure
\check{P}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}};\mu_{0},n_{0})
associated with
\check{n}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}};\mu_{0},n_{0})
at any
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}>\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}
by integration, and the QCD inequality in Eq. (
12
) reads

\displaystyle\check{P}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}};\mu_{0},n_{0})

\displaystyle=\int^{\mu_{\mathrm{B}}}_{\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}}\!\!d\mu\,\check{n}(\mu;\mu_{0},n_{0})\,,

\displaystyle\leq P_{\mathrm{I}}(\mu_{\mathrm{I}}=\frac{2\mu_{\mathrm{B}}}{N_{\mathrm{c}}})\,.

(19)

IV.2
Bounds on
P(\varepsilon)

Now we translate the bound in the
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}-n_{\mathrm{B}}
plane (Fig.
4
) to the bound in the
\varepsilon-P
plane (Fig.
5
) following the procedure outlined in Ref.
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
.

To this end, we find the maximum and minimum
\varepsilon
at a given
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
from the Euler equation
\varepsilon=-P+\mu_{\mathrm{B}}n_{\mathrm{B}}
and the isenthalpic condition
h=\varepsilon+P=\mu_{\mathrm{B}}n_{\mathrm{B}}=\text{const}
.
On the isenthalpic line segment
\varepsilon=-P+h
in the
\varepsilon-P
plane, the maximum (minimum)
\varepsilon
is realized for the minimum (maximum)
P
on the upper left (lower right) endpoint of the line segment.
Since the maximum and minimum
\varepsilon
are entangled with the minimum and maximum
P
, we first discuss
P_{\rm min}
and
P_{\rm max}
because they can be calculated easily by integrating the
n_{\mathrm{B}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})
relation obtained earlier in this section.

At a specific point
(\mu_{0},n_{0})
in the
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}-n_{\mathrm{B}}
plane, which satisfies the isenthalpic condition
n_{0}=h/\mu_{0}
, the minimum pressure is given by the integration of
n_{\rm min}
followed by the first-order phase transition at
\mu_{0}

P_{\rm min}(\mu_{0})=\frac{n_{\mathrm{sat}}}{2\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}}\left(\mu_{0}^{2}-\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}^{2}\right)\,.

(23)

Note that the minimum pressure depends only on
\mu_{0}
but not on
n_{0}
and
h
, so the pressure takes the smallest value at the smallest possible
\mu_{0}
.
Such
\mu_{0}
is realized at the intersection of the isenthalpic line
n_{\mathrm{B}}=h/\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
and the maximum density
n_{\mathrm{B}}=n_{\rm max}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})
in the
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}-n_{\mathrm{B}}
plane.

Likewise, the maximum pressure at a specific point
(\mu_{0},n_{0})
, which satisfies the isenthalpic condition
n_{0}=h/\mu_{0}
, is

\displaystyle P_{\rm max}(\mu_{0},n_{0}=h/\mu_{0})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{h}{2}\left(1-\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}\right)&(n_{0}\leq\frac{n_{\rm max}(\mu_{\mathrm{sat}})}{\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}}\mu_{0})\,,\\
\frac{h}{2}\left(1-\frac{\mu_{u}^{2}}{\mu_{0}^{2}}\right)+\int_{\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}}^{\mu_{\rm u}}\!\!d\mu^{\prime}\,n_{\rm max}(\mu^{\prime})&(n_{0}>\frac{n_{\rm max}(\mu_{\mathrm{sat}})}{\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}}\mu_{0})\,,\end{cases}

(24)

where the upper bound of the integral in the latter case,
\mu_{\rm u}
, is the intersection of the line
n_{\mathrm{B}}=(n_{0}/\mu_{0})\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
with the curve
n_{\mathrm{B}}=n_{\rm max}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})
,

\mu_{\rm u}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}[\check{\mu}_{\ast}^{2}n_{0}-\mu_{0}\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}n_{\mathrm{sat}}-2\mu_{0}P_{\mathrm{I}}(\check{\mu}_{\ast})]}{\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}n_{0}-\mu_{0}n_{\mathrm{sat}}}}\,.

(25)

From the above expression, the maximum pressure
P_{\rm max}
takes the largest value at the largest possible
\mu_{0}
, which is realized at the intersection of the isenthalpic line
n_{\mathrm{B}}=h/\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
and the minimum density
n_{\mathrm{B}}=n_{\rm min}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})
in the
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}-n_{\mathrm{B}}
plane.

The upper end of the isenthalpic line segment in the
\varepsilon-P
plane is

\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon\\
P\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}h-P_{\rm max}\{\mu_{\rm max}(h),n_{\rm min}[\mu_{\rm max}(h)]\}\\
P_{\rm max}\{\mu_{\rm max}(h),n_{\rm min}[\mu_{\rm max}(h)]\}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(26)

where
\mu_{\rm max}(h)
is given by the intersection of
n_{\mathrm{B}}=h/\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
and the
n_{\mathrm{B}}=n_{\rm min}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})
.
The lower end is

\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon\\
P\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}h-P_{\rm min}[\mu_{\rm min}(h)]\\
P_{\rm min}[\mu_{\rm min}(h)]\end{pmatrix}\,,

(27)

where
\mu_{\rm min}(h)
is given by the intersection of
n_{\mathrm{B}}=h/\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
and the
n_{\mathrm{B}}=n_{\rm max}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})
.
By substituting
h=\mu_{\mathrm{B}}n_{\rm min}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})
in Eq. (
26
) and
h=\mu_{\mathrm{B}}n_{\rm max}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})
in Eq. (
27
), we find the upper and the lower bound on the allowed range of values in the
\varepsilon-P
plane as parametric equations with
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
as a parameter.
The parametric equation for the upper bound is

\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon\\
P\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon_{\rm min}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})\\
P_{\rm max}[\mu_{\mathrm{B}},n_{\rm min}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})]\end{pmatrix}\,,

(28)

and that for the lower bound is

\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon\\
P\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon_{\rm max}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})\\
P_{\rm min}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})\end{pmatrix}\,,

(29)

where the minimum and maximum energy densities are defined as

\begin{split}\varepsilon_{\rm min}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})&=-P_{\rm max}[\mu_{\mathrm{B}},n_{\rm min}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})]+\mu_{\mathrm{B}}n_{\rm min}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})\,,\\
\varepsilon_{\rm max}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})&=-P_{\rm min}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})+\mu_{\mathrm{B}}n_{\rm max}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})\,.\end{split}

(30)

Comparison to the pQCD integral constraint

In this section, we compare the lattice-QCD constraint with the constraint put by the thermodynamically consistent construction of the EoS imposing the integral condition:

In the following, we loosely refer this constraint to as the “pQCD integral constraint”.

The minimum density from the pQCD integral constraints is
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022

\displaystyle n_{\rm min}^{\rm pQCD}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{n_{\mathrm{sat}}}{\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}}\mu_{\mathrm{B}}&(\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}\leq\mu_{\mathrm{B}}<\mu_{\rm c}^{\rm pQCD})\,,\\
\frac{n_{\mathrm{H}}\mu_{\mathrm{B}}^{3}-\mu_{\mathrm{B}}\mu_{\mathrm{H}}(n_{\mathrm{H}}\mu_{\mathrm{H}}-2P_{\mathrm{H}})}{\mu_{\mathrm{H}}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}-\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}^{2})}&(\mu_{\rm c}^{\rm pQCD}\leq\mu_{\mathrm{B}}\leq\mu_{\mathrm{H}})\,,\end{cases}

(42)

and the maximum density is

\displaystyle n_{\rm max}^{\rm pQCD}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{-n_{\mathrm{sat}}\mu_{\mathrm{B}}^{3}+\mu_{\mathrm{B}}\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}(n_{\mathrm{sat}}\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}+2P_{\mathrm{H}})}{\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}(\mu_{\mathrm{H}}^{2}-\mu_{\mathrm{B}}^{2})}&(\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}\leq\mu_{\mathrm{B}}<\mu_{\rm c}^{\rm pQCD})\,,\\
\frac{n_{\mathrm{H}}}{\mu_{\mathrm{H}}}\mu_{\mathrm{B}}&(\mu_{\rm c}^{\rm pQCD}\leq\mu_{\mathrm{B}}\leq\mu_{\mathrm{H}})\,,\end{cases}

(43)

where
\mu_{\rm c}^{\rm pQCD}
is

\mu_{\rm c}^{\rm pQCD}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}\mu_{\mathrm{H}}(\mu_{\mathrm{H}}n_{\mathrm{H}}-\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}n_{\mathrm{sat}}-2P_{\mathrm{H}})}{\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}n_{\mathrm{H}}-\mu_{\mathrm{H}}n_{\mathrm{sat}}}}\,.

(44)

The maximum pressure at a given
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
constructed consistently with the constraint (
41
) is

P_{\rm max}^{\rm pQCD}(\mu_{\mathrm{B}})=P_{\mathrm{H}}\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}-\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}^{2}}{\mu_{\mathrm{H}}^{2}-\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}^{2}}\,.

(45)

Both constraints have different sources of uncertainty, so the comparison will lead to an independent check of each constraint.
Furthermore, in addition to that the independent check is feasible, we can also benefit from having two independent constraints as we can put improved bounds by combining these two.

In what follows, we outline how improved bounds can be obtained from the synergy of the pQCD and lattice-QCD constraints.
We can simply obtain the improved bounds by taking the more restrictive one out of the lattice bound and the pQCD integral constraint.
For instance, if we compare the lower curve of the band of the lattice upper bound with the pQCD integral constraint with

.
The same construction works for Fig.

VII
Summary and conclusion

We demonstrated that the equation of state of two-flavor symmetric matter at nonzero baryon chemical potential (i.e. the symmetric nuclear matter) can be robustly constrained by combining a QCD inequalities
Cohen 2003a
;
*Cohen:2004qp
and the recent calculation of the equation of state of matter at nonzero isospin chemical potential on the lattice
Abbott et al. 2023
. We presented the lattice constraints in three ways:
(a) the bound on the pressure at a given baryon chemical potential (Figs.
1
,
2
),
(b) the bounds on the baryon density at a given baryon chemical potential (Figs.
4
,
7
),
(c) the bounds on the pressure at a given energy density (Figs.
5
,
8
).

For the pressure at a given baryon chemical potential, the lattice data only provides an upper bound presented in Figs.
1
.
We showed EoSs characterized by a speed of sound
v_{s}^{2}\lesssim 0.2
for
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}\simeq 2000
MeV are ruled out by this upper bound, as can be seen form Fig.
2
. This bound on the
v_{s}
could be useful for modeling dense matter realized in heavy-ion collisions
Sorensen et al. 2023
.

Obtaining bounds on the baryon density at a given baryon chemical potential from the lattice data requires additional input. We express the pressure as an integral of the baryon density using the method in Ref.
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
so that the pressure inequality can be used.
In the integral, we need to specify either a lower bound
\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}
or upper bound
\mu_{\mathrm{H}}
of the integration interval.
In Sec.
IV
, we took
\mu_{\mathrm{sat}}
as the empirical saturation point.
The upper bound on the baryon density plotted in Fig.
4
is robust;
the only source of uncertainty in this lower bound is the uncertainty of the lattice calculation.
This result implies that the density jump
\Delta n_{\mathrm{B}}
in the first-order phase transition, if it exists around the saturation density, cannot be infinitely large, but has to be bounded
\Delta n_{\mathrm{B}}<10~n_{\mathrm{sat}}
.

In Sec.
V
, we pinned down the perturbative QCD thermodynamics at
\mu_{\mathrm{H}}
.
Figure
7
shows the lower bound on the baryon density in addition to the upper bound.
Aside from the lattice uncertainty, this lower bound is also sensitive to the renormalization scale ambiguity in the running coupling constant;
in this work, we did not include this effect in the lattice bounds.

The bounds on the pressure at a given energy density gives a straightforward interpretation for the stiffness of the equation of state.
The lower and higher pressure at a given energy density correspond to the soft and stiff equations of state, respectively.
In Fig.
5
, we plot the lower bound from the lattice data.
This bound only assumes the input from the empirical saturation of nuclear matter and the lattice bound, so it is robust.
Combined with the perturbative QCD thermodynamics at
\mu_{\mathrm{H}}
, one can also put an upper bound on the energy density-pressure plane as can be seen in Fig.
8
.
The upper bound is close to the causal extrapolation from the empirical saturation point.
The lower bound is also modified in Fig.
8
as the equation of state is required to converge on a single point at
\mu_{\mathrm{H}}
.

Finally, we compared the lattice bound with the integral constraint on the interpolation between the low-density and the high-density reference points imposing the thermodynamic stability and causality.
The results are plotted in Figs.
9
and
10
.
We found that around the saturation density, the information content of the lattice data is as comparable to that of the perturbative QCD at
\mu_{\mathrm{H}}=3000~\text{MeV}
.
These results imply that the synergy between the both QCD-based constraints can further restrict the allowed region of the equation of state.

1.
Introduction

The Bernstein inequalities are fundamental in approximation theory, as seen in the inverse estimate in the
characterization of best approximation and numerous other applications (see, for example,
[
10
,
12
]
).
Starting from algebraic polynomials on
[0,1]
, the Bernstein or Bernstein-Markov inequalities have
been refined and generalized extensively by many authors. Most notable recent extensions are the inequalities
in
L^{p}
norm with the doubling weight, initiated in
[
20
]
, and inequalities for multivariable polynomials
on various domains, such as polytopes, convex domains, and domains with smooth boundaries; see

[
1
,
2
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
22
,
23
,
24
]

and reference therein. The purpose of this paper is to establish the Bernstein inequalities, in uniform norm
and
L^{p}
norm with doubling weight, for polynomials on the conic surface

{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}=\left\{(x,t):\|x\|=t,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\,0\leq t\leq 1\right\},

and on the solid cone
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
bounded by
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
and the hyperplane
t=1
, as well as on
the planar triangle. The domain has a singularity at the vertex. How singularity affects the Bernstein inequalities
motivates our study.

We will establish several inequalities that demonstrate the impact of the singularity at the vertex for the conic
domains. Our results also lead to new inequalities on triangle domains that are stronger than those known in
the literature, which are somewhat unexpected and reveal new phenomena hitherto unnoticed. To be more
precise, let us first recall the Bernstein inequalities in
L^{p}
norm with doubling weight on the interval
[
20
]

and on the unit sphere
[
7
,
9
]
. Let
\ell
be a positive integer. For a doubling weight
w
on
[0,1]

and
1\leq p<\infty
, it is known
[
20
, Theorem 7.3]
that

(1.1)

\left\|f^{(\ell)}\right\|_{L^{p}([0,1],w)}\leq cn^{2\ell}\|f\|_{L^{p}([0,1],w)},\qquad\deg f\leq n;

moreover, setting
\varphi(x)=\sqrt{x(1-x)}
, then
[
20
, Theorem 7.4]

(1.2)

\left\|\varphi^{\ell}f^{(\ell)}\right\|_{L^{p}([0,1],w)}\leq cn^{\ell}\|f\|_{L^{p}([0,1],w)},\qquad\deg f\leq n.

For polynomials on the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
and a doubling weight
w
on
{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
, the Bernstein inequalities are
of the form
[
9
, Theorem 5.5.2]

(1.3)

\left\|D_{i,j}^{\ell}f\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}},w)}\leq cn^{\ell}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}},w)},\qquad\deg f\leq n,

where the derivatives
D_{i,j}
are the angular derivatives on the unit sphere defined by

(1.4)

D_{i,j}=x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}-x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\quad 1\leq i<j\leq d.

These inequalities also hold for the uniform norm in the respective domain.

The Bernstein inequalities on the conic surface are closely related to the above inequalities. Indeed,
parametrizing
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
as
\{(t\xi,t):\xi\in{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}},0\leq t\leq 1\}
, we see that the space of polynomials
on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
contains the subspace of polynomials in the
t
variable as well as spherical polynomials
on the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
. Moreover, writing
(x,t)=(t\xi,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
with
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
, it follows that
the derivatives on the surface
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
are the partial derivative in the
t
variable and the angular
derivatives
D_{i,j}
in the
x
variable. The conic surface, however, is not a smooth surface because of its
vertex at the origin of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
. Our result shows that the inequality for the partial derivative in the
t

variable behaves as expected, but the Bernstein inequality for
D_{i,j}
on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
turns out to satisfy

\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}^{\ell}}D_{i,j}^{\ell}f\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}})}\leq cn^{\ell}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}})},\quad\ell=1,2,\quad\deg f\leq n,

where
{\mathsf{w}}
is a doubling weight on the conic surface, but not for
\ell>2
in general. The factor
1/\sqrt{t}

reflects the geometry of the conic surface. A similar result will also be established on the solid cone
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
.
For
d=1
, the cone
{\mathbb{V}}^{2}
is a triangle and it is mapped, by an affine transform, to the standard triangle

{\mathbb{T}}^{2}=\{(y_{1},y_{2}):y_{1}\geq 0,y_{2}\geq 0,y_{1}+y_{2}\leq 1\}

in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, so that we obtain new Bernstein inequalities on the triangle. In the literature, a typical Bernstein inequality
on the triangle takes the form

(1.5)

\|\phi_{1}\partial_{1}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{2},w)}\leq c\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{2},w)},\quad\phi_{1}(y)=\sqrt{y_{1}(1-y_{1}-y_{2})},\quad\deg f\leq n,

for
\partial_{1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}}
for example (cf.
[
3
,
11
,
12
]
), and it holds also for the uniform
norm. The inequality (
1.5
) is accepted as an appropriate generalization of the Bernstein inequality
(
1.2
) on the triangle, since
\phi_{1}
takes into account the boundary of
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
, just like

\varphi
on
[0,1]
in (
1.1
). Nevertheless, we obtain a stronger inequality:

(1.6)

\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-y_{2}}}\phi_{1}\partial_{1}f\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{2},w)}\leq c\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}}^{2},w)},\quad\deg f\leq n.

The inequality (
1.6
) is somewhat surprising. Indeed, the additional factor
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-y_{2}}}
appears
to be a new phenomenon that has not been observed before. In hindsight, the inequality (
1.5
)
takes into account the boundary of the triangle, whereas the new ones take into account the singularity at the
corners as well.

On several regular domains, including the conic domains, there exist second-order differential operators that have
orthogonal polynomials as eigenfunctions. For the unit sphere, it is the Laplace-Beltrami operator. On the conic
surface
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
, it is given by

\Delta_{0,{\gamma}}=t(1-t)\partial_{t}^{2}+\big(d-1-(d+{\gamma})t\big)\partial_{t}+t^{-1}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\left(D_{i,j}^{(x)}\right)^{2},

and it has the orthogonal polynomials with respect to
t^{-1}(1-t)^{\gamma}
on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
as the eigenfunctions.
The Bernstein inequality for such an operator can be established easily. For example, it was shown in

[
27
, Theorem 3.1.7]
that

\left\|(-\Delta_{0,{\gamma}})^{\ell}\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}})}\leq cn^{2\ell}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}})},\quad\ell=1,2,\ldots.

The proof relies only on the eigenvalues of
\Delta_{0,{\gamma}}
and follows from a general result for all such operators
on localizable homogeneous spaces. As it is, the dependence on the domain is opaque and hidden in the proof.
This inequality nevertheless motivates our study on the Bernstein inequalities for the first-order derivatives. It
can also be used to show the sharpness of our new result for special values of
\ell
when
p=2
.

Our analysis relies on orthogonal structures on the conic domains
[
25
]
. It is part of an ongoing program
that aims at extending the results in approximation theory and harmonics analysis from the unit sphere to
quadratic surfaces of revolution
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
. Our main tools are the closed-form formula for the
reproducing kernels in
[
25
]
and the highly localized kernel studied in
[
27
]
. The approach
follows the analysis on the unit sphere
[
9
]
, but the conic domain has its intrinsic complexity. For
example, the distance function on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
is not intuitively evident and satisfies a formula much
more involved than the geodesic distance on the unit sphere (see (
3.6
) below). The study in

[
27
]
establishes a framework, assuming the existence of highly localized kernels, for approximation
and localized frames on a localizable homogenous space and, along the way, provides a toolbox for carrying
out analysis on localizable homogeneous spaces. For the conic domains, the highly localized kernels
are established by delicate estimates. For our proof of the Bernstein inequalities, we shall establish sharp
estimates for the derivatives of these kernels.

The paper is organized as follows. The main results are stated and discussed in Section 2. The proofs
of the main results are in Section 3 for the conic surface and Section 4 for the solid cone.

Throughout the paper, we let
c,c_{1},c_{2},\ldots
as positive constants that depend only on fixed parameters
and their values could change from line to line. We write
A\sim B
if
c_{1}B\leq A\leq c_{2}B
.

2.
Main results

We state and discuss our main results in this section. The Bernstein inequalities on the conic surface
are stated and discussed in the first subsection and those on the solid cone are stated in the second
subsection. The inequalities on the triangle follow from those on the solid cone and will be discussed
in the third subsection.

2.1.
Main results on the conic surface

Parametrizing
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
as
\{(t\xi,t):\xi\in{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}},0\leq t\leq 1\}
, we see that the first order derivative
on the conic surfaces are
\partial_{t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
in the
t
variable and the angular derivatives

D_{i,j}
,
1\leq i,j\leq d
, in the
x
variables, which we denote by
D_{i,j}^{(x)}
. The latter are called angular
derivatives since if
{\theta}_{i,j}
is the angle of polar coordinates in the
(x_{i},x_{j})
-plane, defined by

(x_{i},x_{j})=r_{i,j}(\cos{\theta}_{i,j},\sin{\theta}_{i,j})
, where
r_{i,j}\geq 0
and
0\leq{\theta}_{i,j}\leq 2\pi
, then a quick
computation shows that

D_{i,j}=x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}-x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial{\theta}_{i,j}},\qquad 1\leq i,j\leq d.

In particular, they are independent of the scale of
x
. We state our Bernstein inequalities for the derivatives

\partial_{t}
and
D_{i,j}^{(x)}
on the conic surface.

Let
{\mathsf{w}}
be a doubling weight on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
; see Subsection
3.1
for the definition.
We denote by
\|\cdot\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}
the weighted
L^{p}
norm on

{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}

\|f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}|f(x,t)|^{p}{\mathsf{w}}(x,t)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(x,t)\right)^{1/p},\qquad 1\leq p<\infty,

where
{\mathsf{m}}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on the conic surface. For
p=\infty
, we denote by

\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
the uniform norm on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
. For
n=0,1,2,\ldots
, let
\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
be
the space of polynomials of degree at most
n
on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
{\mathsf{w}}
be a doubling weight on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
and let
f\in\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
. For
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

1\leq p<\infty
,

(2.1)

\|\partial_{t}^{\ell}f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}\leq c_{p}n^{2\ell}\|f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}},

and, with
\varphi(t)=\sqrt{t(1-t)}
,

(2.2)

\|\phi^{\ell}\partial_{t}^{\ell}f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}\leq c_{p}n^{\ell}\|f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}.

Moreover, for
D_{i,j}=D_{i,j}^{(x)}
,
1\leq i<j\leq d
, and
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(2.3)

\left\|D_{i,j}^{\ell}f\right\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}\leq c_{p}n^{\ell}\|f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}},

and, furthermore, for
\ell=1,2
,

(2.4)

\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}^{\ell}}D_{i,j}^{\ell}f\right\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}\leq c_{p}n^{\ell}\|f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}},

but not for
\ell\geq 3
in general. Finally, these inequalities also hold when the norm is the
uniform norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
.

The inequality (
2.4
) is stronger than (
2.3
) when
\ell=1,2
, since
0\leq t\leq 1
.
Moreover, for
\ell>2
, the inequality (
2.3
) follows from iteration. It is worth mentioning that
the inequality (
2.4
) for
\ell=1
cannot be iterated to obtain the inequality for
\ell=2
, since
the presence of
1/\sqrt{t}
means that the function involved in the iteration is no longer a polynomial.

We now discuss these inequalities in light of the spectral operator
\Delta_{0,{\gamma}}
on the conic surface, which
is defined for
{\gamma}>-1
by

(2.5)

\Delta_{0,{\gamma}}:=t(1-t)\partial_{t}^{2}+\big(d-1-(d+{\gamma})t\big)\partial_{t}+t^{-1}\Delta_{0}^{(\xi)},

where
\xi=x/t
and
\Delta_{0}^{(\xi)}
denote the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{0}
on the unit sphere

{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
in
\xi
variables. For
{\gamma}>-1
, the operator
-\Delta_{0,{\gamma}}
is non-negative and has orthogonal
polynomials with respect to the weight function
t^{-1}(1-t)^{\gamma}
on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
as eigenfunctions; see
Theorem
3.1
below. The latter property allows us to define
(-\Delta_{0,r})^{{\alpha}}
for

{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. It is shown in
[
27
, Theorem 3.1.7]
that the following Bernstein inequality holds.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
{\mathsf{w}}
be a doubling weight on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
. Let
{\gamma}>-1
. Then, for
r>0
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

(2.6)

\|(-\Delta_{0,{\gamma}})^{\frac{r}{2}}f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}\leq cn^{r}\|f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}},\qquad\forall f\in\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}).

The proof of this theorem, however, does not use the explicit formula of the operator
\Delta_{0,{\gamma}}

and relies only on the fact that the operator has orthogonal polynomials as eigenfunctions.
The operator
\Delta_{0,{\gamma}}
in (
2.5
) contains a factor
t^{-1}\Delta_{0}^{(\xi)}
.
It is known that the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{0}
can be decomposed in terms of angular
derivatives (cf.
[
9
, Theorem 1.8.2]
),

(2.7)

\Delta_{0}=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}D_{i,j}^{2}.

Hence, the factor
t^{-1}\Delta_{0}^{(\xi)}
in the operator
\Delta_{0,{\gamma}}
can be decomposed as a sum of

\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}^{2}}(D_{i,j}^{(\xi)})^{2}
. Thus, the inequality (
2.6
) with
r=2
follows
from inequalities in Theorem
2.1
. Notice, however, that (
2.4
) holds only for

\ell=1
and
2
. This makes the higher order Bernstein inequality for the spectral operator

\Delta_{0,{\gamma}}
that much more special.

The operator
-\Delta_{0,{\gamma}}
on the conic surface
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
is self-adjoint, which is evident from
the following identity that relies on the first-order derivatives on the conic surface.

Proposition 2.3
.

For
{\gamma}>-1
, let
{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(t)=t^{-1}(1-t)^{\gamma}
,
0\leq t\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle-\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\Delta_{0,{\gamma}}f(x,t)\cdot g(x,t){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(x,t)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(x,t)=\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}_{0}}t(1-t)\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{\mathrm{d}g}{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(t)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(x,t)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}t^{-2}D_{i,j}^{(x)}f(x,t)D_{i,j}^{(x)}g(x,t){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(t)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(x,t)

,

where
\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}t}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}f(t\xi,t)
. In particular,
-\Delta_{0,{\gamma}}
is self-adjoint in
L^{2}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}})
.

The proof of these results will be given in Subsection
3.5
. It is used to deduce the
following corollary.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
{\gamma}>-1
. Then, for
{\mathsf{w}}={\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}
and any polynomial
f
,

\left\|\varphi\partial_{t}f\right\|_{2,{\mathsf{w}}}^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}D_{i,j}^{(x)}f\right\|_{2,{\mathsf{w}}}^{2}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{2,{\mathsf{w}}}\left\|\Delta_{0,{\gamma}}f\right\|_{2,{\mathsf{w}}}.

In particular, for
f\in\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
, the inequality (
2.6
) with
r=2
and
p=2

implies the inequalities (
2.2
) and (
2.4
) with
\ell=1
and
p=2
when

{\mathsf{w}}={\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}
.

In the other direction, the explicit formula of (
2.5
) and (
2.7
) shows immediately
that
\|-\Delta_{0,{\gamma}}f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}
is bounded by the sum of
\|\varphi^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}
,

\|\partial_{t}f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}
and
\|(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}D_{i,j})^{2}f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}
. In particular, the inequalities in the
Theorem
2.1
imply (
2.6
) with
r=2
. By the spectral property, the inequality
(
2.6
) is sharp. The corollary and the discussion above provides some assurance that
the inequalities (
2.2
) and (
2.4
) are sharp when
p=2
and
{\mathsf{w}}={\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}
.

2.2.
Main results on the cone

Here the domain is the solid cone in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
for
d\geq 1
,

{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}=\left\{(x,t):\|x\|\leq t,\,0\leq t\leq 1,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\right\}.

Writing
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
as
\{(t\xi,t):\xi\in{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}},0\leq t\leq 1\}
, we see that the first order derivative on the conic
surfaces are
\partial_{t}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
in the
t
variable and the angular derivatives
D_{i,j}
,
1\leq i,j\leq d
,
in the
x
variables, which we denote by
D_{i,j}^{(x)}
, as well as one more partial derivative, denoted by

D_{x_{j}}
and defined by

(2.8)

D_{x_{j}}=\sqrt{t^{2}-\|x\|^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}},\qquad 1\leq j\leq d,\quad(x,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}.

Let
W
be a doubling weight on
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
; see
4.1
for the definition. We denote by

\|\cdot\|_{p,W}
the weighted
L^{p}
norm on
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}

\|f\|_{p,W}=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}}|f(x,t)|^{p}W(x,t)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/p},\qquad 1\leq p<\infty.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
W
be a doubling weight on
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
and let
f\in\Pi_{n}
. For
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
1\leq p<\infty
,

(2.9)

\left\|\partial_{t}^{\ell}f\right\|_{p,W}\leq c_{p}n^{2\ell}\|f\|_{p,W}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\left\|\varphi^{\ell}\partial_{t}^{\ell}f\right\|_{p,W}\leq c_{p}n^{\ell}\|f\|_{p,W},

where
\varphi(t)=\sqrt{t(1-t)}
; moreover, for
1\leq i,j\leq d
,

(2.10)

\left\|D_{i,j}^{\ell}f\right\|_{p,W}\leq c_{p}n^{\ell}\|f\|_{p,W},

and, for
\ell=1,2
,

(2.11)

\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}^{\ell}}D_{i,j}^{\ell}f\right\|_{p,W}\leq c_{p}n^{\ell}\|f\|_{p,W},

which does not hold for
\ell=3,4,\ldots
in general. Furthermore, for
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
1\leq j\leq d
,

(2.12)

\left\|\partial_{x_{j}}^{\ell}f\right\|_{p,W}\leq c_{p}n^{2\ell}\|f\|_{p,W}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\left\|\Phi^{\ell}\partial_{x_{j}}^{\ell}f\right\|_{p,W}\leq c_{p}n^{\ell}\|f\|_{p,W},

where
\Phi(x,t)=\sqrt{t^{2}-\|x\|^{2}}
, and, for
\ell=1,2
,

(2.13)

\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}^{\ell}}\Phi^{\ell}\partial_{x_{j}}^{\ell}f\right\|_{p,W}\leq c_{p}n^{\ell}\|f\|_{p,W}.

Finally, these inequalities also hold when the norm is the uniform norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
on
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
.

In contrast to the inequality (
2.12
), we do not know if (
2.13
) holds for
\ell\geq 3
. As
it will be shown in Section 4, the two cases are closely related, but the example that works for (
2.12
)
does not work for (
2.13
).

Like the case of conic surface, there is also a spectral operator on the cone
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
. For
\mu>-\frac{1}{2}

and
{\gamma}>-1
, define the second order differential operator

(2.14)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{D}}_{\mu,{\gamma}}:=

\displaystyle\,t(1-t)\partial_{t}^{2}+2(1-t){\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle}\partial_{t}+t\Delta_{x}-\langle x,\nabla_{x}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle+(2\mu+d)\partial_{t}-(2\mu+{\gamma}+d+1)({\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle}+t\partial_{t})+\langle x,\nabla_{x}\rangle.

It is proved in
[
27
]
that orthogonal polynomials with respect to the weight function

W_{\mu,{\gamma}}(x,t)=(t^{2}-\|x\|^{2})^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{\gamma},\qquad\mu>-\tfrac{1}{2},{\gamma}>-1

on
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
are the eigenfunctions of the operator
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\mu,{\gamma}}
(see,
Theorem
4.1
below). In particular, the operator satisfies the following Bernstein inequality

[
27
, Theorem 3.1.7]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
W
be a doubling weight on
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
. Let
{\gamma}>-1
and
\mu>-\frac{1}{2}
. For
r>0
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

(2.15)

\|(-{\mathfrak{D}}_{\mu,{\gamma}})^{\frac{r}{2}}f\|_{p,W}\leq cn^{r}\|f\|_{p,W},\qquad\forall f\in\Pi_{n}.

The operator
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\mu,{\gamma}}
is self-adjoint, as can be seen in the following theorem.

Theorem 2.7
.

For
\mu>-\frac{1}{2}
and
{\gamma}>-1
,

(2.16)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}}-{\mathfrak{D}}_{\mu,{\gamma}}f(x,t)

\displaystyle\cdot g(x,t)W_{\mu,{\gamma}}(x,t)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}}t\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}t}\frac{\mathrm{d}g}{\mathrm{d}t}W_{\mu,{\gamma}+1}(x,t)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}}D_{x_{i}}f(x,t)\cdot D_{x_{i}}g(x,t)t^{-1}W_{\mu,{\gamma}}(x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}}\sum_{i<j}D_{i,j}^{(x)}f(x,t)\cdot D_{i,j}^{(x)}g(x,t)t^{-1}W_{\mu,{\gamma}}(x)\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}f=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}[f(ty,t)]
for
y\in{\mathbb{B}}^{d}
.

The identity (
2.16
) is proved in Subsection
4.4
and it implies immediately
the following corollary.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
\mu>-\frac{1}{2}
and
{\gamma}>-1
. Let
W=W_{\mu,{\gamma}}
. Then for any polynomial
f

\left\|\varphi\partial_{t}f\right\|_{2,W}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}D_{x_{j}}f\right\|_{2,W}^{2}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}D_{i,j}^{(x)}f\right\|_{2,W}^{2}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{2,W}\left\|{\mathfrak{D}}_{\mu,{\gamma}}f\right\|_{2,W}.

In particular, the inequality (
2.15
) for
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\mu,{\gamma}}
with
r=2
implies the inequalities
(
2.12
) for
\varphi\partial_{t}
and (
2.4
) for
D_{i,j}^{(x)}
and
D_{x_{j}}
with

\ell=1
and
p=2
for
W=W_{\mu,{\gamma}}
.

The corollary follows as in Corollary
2.4
. The Bernstein inequalities for the first order derivatives
in Theorem
2.5
imply (
2.15
) when
r=2
. Together, they provide some assurance
that the inequalities in Theorem
2.5
are sharp.

2.3.
Bernstein inequality on the triangle

For
d=1
, the cone becomes the triangle
{\mathbb{V}}^{2}=\{(x,t):0\leq t\leq 1,\,|x|\leq t\}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. Making an
affine change of variable
(x,t)=(y_{1}-y_{2},y_{1}+y_{2})
, the triangle
{\mathbb{V}}^{2}
becomes the standard triangle
domain

{\mathbb{T}}^{2}=\{(y_{1},y_{2}):y_{1}\geq 0,\,y_{2}\geq 0,\,y_{1}+y_{2}\leq 1\}.

The triangle
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
is symmetric under permutation of
\{y_{1},y_{2},1-y_{1}-y_{2}\}
and it is customary to consider
the derivatives (cf.
[
3
,
13
]
)

\partial_{1}=\partial_{y_{1}},\quad\partial_{2}=\partial_{y_{2}},\quad\partial_{3}=\partial_{y_{2}}-\partial_{y_{1}}.

We further define, for
y=(y_{1},y_{2})\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
,

\phi_{1}(y)=\sqrt{y_{1}(1-y_{1}-y_{2})},\quad\phi_{2}(y)=\sqrt{y_{2}(1-y_{1}-y_{2})},\quad\phi_{3}(y)=\sqrt{y_{1}y_{2}}.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{p,w}
be the
L^{p}
norm on the triangle
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
for
1\leq p<\infty
and the uniform norm on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}

if
p=\infty
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
w
be a doubling weigh on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
. Let
f\in\Pi_{n}^{2}
. Then, for
1\leq p<\infty
,
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

i=1,2,3
,

(2.17)

\left\|\partial_{i}^{\ell}f\right\|_{p,w}\leq cn^{2\ell}\|f\|_{p,w}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\left\|\phi_{i}^{\ell}\partial_{i}^{\ell}f\right\|_{p,w}\leq cn^{\ell}\|f\|_{p,w}.

Furthermore, for
\ell=1,2
,

(2.18)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{(1-y_{2})^{\ell/2}}\phi_{1}^{\ell}\partial_{1}^{\ell}f\right\|_{p,w}\leq cn^{\ell}\|f\|_{p,w},

(2.19)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{(1-y_{1})^{\ell/2}}\phi_{2}^{\ell}\partial_{2}^{\ell}f\right\|_{p,w}\leq cn^{\ell}\|f\|_{p,w},

(2.20)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{(y_{1}+y_{2})^{\ell/2}}\phi_{3}^{\ell}\partial_{3}^{\ell}f\right\|_{p,w}\leq cn^{\ell}\|f\|_{p,w}.

Moreover, these inequalities hold when the norm is replaced by the uniform norm.

Proof.

Under the change of variables
{\mathbb{V}}^{2}\mapsto{\mathbb{T}}^{2}
:
(x,t)\mapsto(y_{1}-y_{2},y_{1}+y_{2})
, we see that

\partial_{x}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{y_{2}}-\partial_{y_{1}})=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{3}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}=2\sqrt{y_{1}y_{2}}=2\phi_{3}(y).

Hence, for
i=3
, the two inequalities in (
2.17
) follow from (
2.12
), where
d=1
,
and the inequality (
2.20
) follows from (
2.13
) in Theorem
2.5
. The inequalities
for
\partial_{1}
and
\partial_{2}
follow from those
for
\partial_{3}
by a change of variables that amounts to permute the variables
(y_{1},y_{2},1-y_{1}-y_{2})
.
∎

The inequality (
2.17
) is known when
w
is a constant weight (cf.
[
3
,
12
,
22
]
) or the Jacobi
weight. It is accepted as a natural generalization of the Bernstein inequality (
1.2
)
of one variable, since the left-hand side of the inequality in the uniform norm gives a pointwise inequality
that reduces to (
1.2
) when restricted to the boundary of the triangle. The latter property,
however, is satisfied by (
2.18
)–(
2.20
) as well. Moreover, the factors in the left-hand
side remains bounded; for example,
1-y_{1}-y_{2}\leq 1-y_{2}
in (
2.18
). The inequalities
(
2.18
)–(
2.20
) are surprising and they are new as far as we are aware. It is also suggestive
and may be worthwhile to ask if the same phenomenon appears on other domains, such as polytopes

[
22
,
23
]
.

3.
Bernstein inequalities on conic surface

We work on the conic surface in this section. In the first subsection, we recall what is needed for our analysis
on the domain, based on orthogonal polynomials and highly localized kernels. Several auxiliary inequalities
are recalled or proved in the second subsection, to be used in the rest of the section. The proofs of the main
results are based on the estimates of the kernel functions, which are carried out in the third subsection
for the derivatives in the
t
-variable and the fourth subsection for the angular derivatives. Finally, the proof
of Theorem
2.7
and its corollary is given in the fifth subsection.

3.1.
Orthogonal polynomials and localized kernels

Let
\Pi({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
denote the space of polynomials restricted on the conic surface
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}

and, for
n=0,1,2,\ldots
, let
\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
be the subspace of polynomials in
\Pi({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})

of total degree at most
n
. Since
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
is a quadratic surface, it is known that

\dim\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})=\binom{n+d}{n}+\binom{n+d-1}{n-1}.

For
{\beta}>-d
and
{\gamma}>-1
, we define the weight function
{\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}}
by

{\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}}(t)=t^{\beta}(1-t)^{\gamma},\qquad 0\leq t\leq 1.

Orthogonal polynomials with respect to
{\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}}
on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
are studied in
[
25
]
. Let

{\langle}f,g{\rangle}_{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}}}={\mathsf{b}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}f(x,t)g(x,t){\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(x,t),

where
\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on the conic surface, which is a well defined inner product
on
\Pi({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
. Let
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}})
be the space of orthogonal polynomials of
degree
n
. Then
\dim{\mathcal{V}}_{0}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}})=1
and

\dim{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}})=\binom{n+d-1}{n}+\binom{n+d-2}{n-1},\quad n=1,2,3,\ldots.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{m}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})
be the space of spherical harmonics of degree
m
in
d
variables. Let

\{Y_{\ell}^{m}:1\leq\ell\leq\dim{\mathcal{H}}_{m}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})\}
denote an orthonormal basis of
{\mathcal{H}}_{m}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})
.
Then the polynomials

{\mathsf{S}}_{m,\ell}^{n}(x,t)=P_{n-m}^{(2m+{\beta}+d-1,{\gamma})}(1-2t)Y_{\ell}^{m}(x),\quad 0\leq m\leq n,\,\,1\leq\ell\leq\dim{\mathcal{H}}_{m}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}),

consist of an orthogonal basis of
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}})
. Let
\Delta_{0,{\gamma}}
be the differential
operator defined in (
2.5
). It has orthogonal polynomials as eigenfunctions
[
25
, Theorem 7.2]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
{\gamma}>-1
. The orthogonal polynomials in
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}})
are eigenfunctions
of
\Delta_{0,{\gamma}}
; more precisely,

(3.1)

\Delta_{0,{\gamma}}u=-n(n+{\gamma}+d-1)u,\qquad\forall u\in{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}).

The reproducing kernel of the space
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}})
is denoted by
{\mathsf{P}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}};\cdot,\cdot)
,
which can be written as

{\mathsf{P}}_{n}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s)\big)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\dim{\mathcal{H}}_{m}^{d}}\frac{{\mathsf{S}}_{m,\ell}^{n}(x,t){\mathsf{S}}_{m,\ell}^{n}(y,s)}{{\langle}{\mathsf{S}}_{m,\ell}^{n},{\mathsf{S}}_{m^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}}^{n^{\prime}}{\rangle}_{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}}}}.

Let
\operatorname{proj}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}}):L^{2}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}})\to{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}})
be the orthogonal
projection operator. It is an integral operator with
{\mathsf{P}}_{n}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}};\cdot,\cdot\big)
as its kernel,

\operatorname{proj}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}};f)=\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}f(y,s){\mathsf{P}}_{n}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}};\,\cdot,(y,s)\big){\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(y,s).

The kernel
{\mathsf{P}}_{n}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}};\,\cdot,\cdot\big)
satisfies a closed form formula, called the addition formula
since it is akin to the classical addition formula for the spherical harmonics. The addition formula is given in terms
of the Jacobi polynomial
P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}
, the orthogonal polynomial with respect to
w_{{\alpha},{\beta}}(t)=(1-t)^{\alpha}(1+t)^{\beta}

on
[-1,1]
. Let

Z_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(t)=\frac{P_{n}^{(a,{\beta})}(t)P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(1)}{h_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}},\quad{\alpha},{\beta}>-1,

where
h_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}
is the
L^{2}
norm of
P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}
in
L^{2}([-1,1],w_{{\alpha},{\beta}})
. The addition formula is
of the simplest form when
{\gamma}=-1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
{\gamma}\geq-\frac{1}{2}
. Then, for
(x,t),(y,s)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{P}}_{n}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s)\big)=b_{{\gamma},d}\int_{[-1,1]^{2}}

\displaystyle Z_{n}^{({\gamma}+d-\frac{3}{2},-\frac{1}{2})}\big(2\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)^{2}-1\big)

\displaystyle\times(1-v_{1}^{2})^{\frac{d-4}{2}}(1-v_{2}^{2})^{{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}v,

where
b_{{\gamma},d}
is a constant so that
{\mathsf{P}}_{0}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s)\big)=1
and

(3.3)

\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)=v_{1}\sqrt{\tfrac{st+{\langle}x,y{\rangle}}{2}}+v_{2}\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{1-s};

moreover, the identity holds under limit when
{\gamma}=-\frac{1}{2}
and/or
d=2
.

Our main tool for establishing polynomial inequalities is the highly localized kernel defined via a smooth
cur-off function
\widehat{a}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
, which is a non-negative function and satisfies

\mathrm{supp}\,\widehat{a}\subset[0,2]
and
\widehat{a}(t)=1
,
t\in[0,1]
. The kernel is defined by

(3.4)

{\mathsf{L}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s))=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\widehat{a}\left(\frac{k}{n}\right){\mathsf{P}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}};(x,t),(y,s)).

Since
\widehat{a}
is supported on
[0,2]
, this is a kernel of polynomials of degree at most
2n
in either
the
x
or the
y
variable. Using the closed form of the reproducing kernel, we can write
{\mathsf{L}}({\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}})

in terms of the kernel of the Jacobi polynomials defined by

L_{n}^{({\lambda},-\frac{1}{2})}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\widehat{a}\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)Z_{n}^{({\lambda},-\frac{1}{2})}(t).

Indeed, it follows immediately from (
3.2
) that

(3.5)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{L}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s))=b_{{\gamma},d}\int_{[-1,1]^{2}}

\displaystyle L_{n}^{({\gamma}+d-\frac{3}{2},-\frac{1}{2})}\big(2\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)^{2}-1\big)

\displaystyle\times(1-v_{1}^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}-1}(1-v_{2}^{2})^{{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}v.

To show that this kernel is highly localized, we need the distance on the conic surface. For
(x,t)

and
(y,s)
on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
, the distance function
{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}
on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
is defined by

(3.6)

{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((x,t),(y,s)):=\arccos\left(\sqrt{\frac{{\langle}x,y{\rangle}+ts}{2}}+\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{1-s}\right).

For
r>0
and
(x,t)
on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
, we let
{\mathsf{c}}((x,t),r)
be the ball centered at
(x,t)
with radius
r

in terms of this distance function; that is,

{\mathsf{c}}((x,t),r):=\left\{(y,s)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}:{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}\big((x,t),(y,s)\big)\leq r\right\}.

Let
E
be a subset in
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
and
{\mathsf{m}}
denotes the Lebesgue measure in
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
. We define

{\mathsf{w}}(E)=\int_{E}{\mathsf{w}}(x,t)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(x,t).

A weight function
{\mathsf{w}}
is a doubling weight if there is a constant
L>0
such that

{\mathsf{w}}\big({\mathsf{c}}((x,t),2r)\big)\leq L\,{\mathsf{w}}\big({\mathsf{c}}((x,t),r)\big),\quad r>0.

The least constant
L
is called a doubling constant and the doubling index
{\alpha}({\mathsf{w}})
is the least index for
which
\sup_{{\mathsf{c}}((x,t),r}{\mathsf{w}}({\mathsf{c}}((x,t),2^{m}r)))/{\mathsf{w}}({\mathsf{c}}((x,t),r))\leq c_{L({\mathsf{w}})}2^{m{\alpha}({\mathsf{w}})}
,
m=1,2,\ldots
.
As an example, the weight
{\mathsf{w}}_{{\beta},{\gamma}}
is a doubling weight on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}

[
27
, Proposition 4.6]

and, for
n=1,2,\ldots
,
{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}\big({\mathsf{c}}((x,t),n^{-2})\big)\sim{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;t)
with

(3.7)

{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;t)=\big(1-t+n^{-2}\big)^{{\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}}\big(t+n^{-2}\big)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

It is shown in
[
27
, Theorem 4.10]
that, for
d\geq 2
,
{\gamma}\geq-\frac{1}{2}
, and any
{\kappa}>0
, the
kernel
{\mathsf{L}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};\cdot,\cdot)
satisfies the estimate

(3.8)

\left|{\mathsf{L}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s))\right|\leq\frac{c_{\kappa}n^{d}}{\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;t)}\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;s)}}\big(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((x,t),(y,s))\big)^{-{\kappa}}.

This shows, in particular, that the kernel decays away from
(x,t)=(y,s)
faster than any polynomial rate. The
decaying estimate is established using the case of
m=0
in the following lemma
[
9
, Theorem 2.6.7]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\ell
be a positive integer and let
\eta
be a function that satisfy,
\eta\in C^{3\ell-1}({\mathbb{R}})
,

\mathrm{supp}\,\eta\subset[0,2]
and
\eta^{(j)}(0)=0
for
j=0,1,2,\ldots,3\ell-2
. Then,
for
{\alpha}\geq{\beta}\geq-\frac{1}{2}
,
t\in[-1,1]
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(3.9)

\left|\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}L_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(t)\right|\leq c_{\ell,m,{\alpha}}\left\|\eta^{(3\ell-1)}\right\|_{\infty}\frac{n^{2{\alpha}+2m+2}}{(1+n\sqrt{1-t})^{\ell}},\quad m=0,1,2,\ldots.

The proof of the localization of the kernel requires further properties of the distance function.
Let
{\mathsf{d}}_{[-1,1]}(\cdot,\cdot)
be the distance function on the interval
[0,1]
defined by

{\mathsf{d}}_{[-1,1]}(t,s)=\arccos\left(\sqrt{t}\sqrt{s}+\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{1-s}\right),\qquad t,s\in[0,1]

and let
{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the geodesic distance on the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
defined by

{\mathsf{d}}_{\mathbb{S}}(\xi,\eta)=\arccos{\langle}\xi,\eta{\rangle},\qquad\xi,\eta\in{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}.

Then, for
(x,t),(y,s)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
and setting
x=t\xi
and
y=s\eta
, the distance
{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}(\cdot,\cdot)

satisfies, as shown in
[
27
, Proposition 4.3]
,

(3.10)

c_{1}{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((x,t),(y,s))\leq{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(t,s)+(ts)^{\frac{1}{4}}{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\xi,\eta)\leq c_{2}{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((x,t),(y,s)).

Another two inequalities that we shall need are

(3.11)

\big|\sqrt{t}-\sqrt{s}\big|\leq{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((x,t),(y,s))\quad\hbox{and}\quad\big|\sqrt{1-t}-\sqrt{1-s}\big|\leq{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((x,t),(y,s))

for
(x,t),(y,s)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
, established in
[
27
, Lemma 4.4]
.

3.2.
Auxiliary inequalities

We state several auxiliary results in this subsection. First of all, we will need the maximal function
f_{{\beta},n}^{\ast}

defined by

(3.12)

f_{{\beta},n}^{\ast}(x,t)=\max_{(y,s)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\frac{|f(y,s)|}{\left(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((x,t),(y,s))\right)^{\beta}}.

This maximal function satisfies the following property
[
27
, Corollary 2.11]
.

Proposition 3.4
.

If
0<p\leq\infty
,
f\in\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
and
{\beta}>{\alpha}({\mathsf{w}})/p
, then

(3.13)

\|f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}\leq\|f_{{\beta},n}^{\ast}\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}\leq c\|f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}},

where
c
depends also on
L({\mathsf{w}})
and
{\beta}
when
{\beta}
is either large or close to
{\alpha}({\mathsf{w}})/p
.

For our next result, we need the concept of a maximal
{\varepsilon}
-separated set on the conic surface. However,
the concept will be needed for the unit ball and the solid cone later in the paper, so we give its definition
on a domain
\Omega
equipped with a distance function
{\mathsf{d}}(\cdot,\cdot)
. Let

B(x,{\varepsilon})=\{y\in\Omega:{\mathsf{d}}(x,y)\leq{\varepsilon}\}
be the ball centered at
x
and with radius
{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\Xi
be a discrete set in
\Omega
.

(a)

Let
{\varepsilon}>0
. A discrete subset
\Xi
of
\Omega
is called
{\varepsilon}
-separated if
{\mathsf{d}}(x,y)\geq{\varepsilon}

for every two distinct points
x,y\in\Xi
.

(b)

\Xi
is called maximal if there is a constant
c_{d}>1
such that

1\leq\sum_{z\in\Xi}\chi_{B(z,{\varepsilon})}(x)\leq c_{d},\qquad\forall x\in\Omega,

where
\chi_{E}
denotes the characteristic function of the set
E
.

A maximal
{\varepsilon}
-separated subset is constructed in
[
27
, Proposition 4.17]
using the separated sets
in the
t
-variable on
[0,1]
and in the
\xi
variable on
{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
, where
(x,t)=(t\xi,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
. We
recall what is necessary for our purpose. Let
{\varepsilon}>0
and let
N=\lfloor\frac{\pi}{2}{\varepsilon}^{-1}\rfloor
. We
define

(3.14)

t_{j}=\sin^{2}\frac{(2j-1)\pi}{4N}\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\varepsilon}_{j}=\frac{\pi{\varepsilon}}{2\sqrt{t_{j}}},\quad 1\leq j\leq N.

Let
\Xi_{\mathbb{S}}({\varepsilon}_{j})
be the maximal
{\varepsilon}_{j}
-separated set of
{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
, so that there is a family of sets

\{{\mathbb{S}}_{\xi}({\varepsilon}_{j}):\xi\in\Xi_{\mathbb{S}}({\varepsilon}_{j})\}
which forms a partition
{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}=\bigcup_{\eta\in\Xi_{\mathbb{S}}({\varepsilon}_{j})}{\mathbb{S}}_{\eta}({\varepsilon}_{j})
.
Then

(3.15)

\Xi_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}=\big\{(t_{j}\xi,t_{j}):\,\xi\in\Xi_{\mathbb{S}}({\varepsilon}_{j}),\,1\leq j\leq N\big\}

defined a maximal
{\varepsilon}
-separated subset on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
. Such a set is used to establish the
Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality on the conic surface, which we need below.

Out next result is of interest in itself. For polynomials of one variable, it is known
[
20
, (7.1.7)]

\int_{-1}^{1}|f(t)|^{p}w(t)\mathrm{d}t\leq c_{\delta}\int_{-1+\delta n^{-2}}^{1-\delta n^{-2}}|f(t)|^{p}w(t)\mathrm{d}t,\quad\deg f\leq n,

where
w
is a doubling weight and
\delta
is a positive constant. The following proposition is an
analog of the above inequality on the conic surface.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
{\mathsf{w}}
be a doubling weight function on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
. For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
\chi_{n,\delta}(t)
denote the
characteristic function of the interval
[\frac{\delta}{n^{2}},1-\frac{\delta}{n^{2}}]
. Then, for
f\in\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
,

1\leq p<\infty
, and every
\delta>0
,

(3.16)

\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}|f(x,t)|^{p}{\mathsf{w}}(x,t)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(x,t)\leq c_{\delta}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}|f(x,t)|^{p}\chi_{n,\delta}(t){\mathsf{w}}(x,t)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(x,t).

Moreover, when
p=\infty
,

(3.17)

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq c\|f\chi_{n,\delta}(t)\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

The proof uses the Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality on the conic surface. Let
\Xi_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}
be
the
{\varepsilon}
-maximal separated set in (
3.15
). For
{\varepsilon}={\beta}n^{-1}
with
{\beta}>0
, the
Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality states
[
27
, Theorem 4.18]
that, for
1\leq p<\infty
,

(i)

for
f\in\Pi_{m}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
with
n\leq m\leq cn
,

\sum_{(z,r)\in\Xi_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}}\Big(\max_{(x,t)\in{\mathsf{c}}\big((z,r),n^{-1}\big)}|f(x,t)|^{p}\Big){\mathsf{w}}\!\left({\mathsf{c}}\big((z,r),n^{-1}\big)\right)\leq c_{{\mathsf{w}}}\|f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}^{p};

(ii)

for
f\in\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}^{p}\leq c_{{\mathsf{w}}}\sum_{(z,r)\in\Xi_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}}\Big(\min_{(x,t)\in{\mathsf{c}}\bigl((z,r),n^{-1}\bigr)}|f(x,t)|^{p}\Big){\mathsf{w}}\bigl({\mathsf{c}}\big((z,r),n^{-1}\big)\bigr).

Clearly
t_{0}=\sin^{2}\frac{\pi}{4N}\sim n^{-2}
and
1-t_{N}\sim n^{-2}
and the constant is proportional to

{\beta}^{-1}
. Hence, for a fixed
\delta>0
, by choosing
{\beta}
sufficiently large, we see that
f(z,r)=f(z,r)\chi_{n,\delta}(r)
for all
(z,r)\in\Xi_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}
. Consequently, it follows from (ii) and (i) that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}^{p}\,

\displaystyle\leq c_{{\mathsf{w}}}\sum_{(z,r)\in\Xi_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}}|f(z,r)\chi_{n,\delta}(r)|^{p}{\mathsf{w}}\bigl({\mathsf{c}}\big((z,r),n^{-1}\big)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}|f(x,t)|^{p}\chi_{n,\delta}(t){\mathsf{w}}(x,t)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(x,t)

for all
f\in\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
, which is the desired inequality (
3.16
). For the uniform norm,

|f(x,t)|\leq c\max_{(z,r)\in\Xi_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}}|f(z,r)|

by
[
27
, Theorem 2.14]
, from which the inequality (
3.17
) follows readily.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Setting
x=t\xi
,
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
shows that the space
\Pi_{n}^{d}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})

, which

n
in
t

[

]

[

Our next lemma is technical and reduces to
[
27
, Lemma 4.14]
when
\rho=0

and
\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}=0
. The general version is needed to handle the derivatives of the highly localized kernels.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
{\gamma}>-1
. Let
\rho
satisfies
0\leq\rho\leq(d-2)/2
,
\tau_{1}
satisfies

0\leq\tau_{1}\leq 1/2
and
\tau_{2}
satisfies
0\leq\tau_{2}\leq d/2
. For
0<p<\infty
,
assume
{\kappa}>\frac{2d}{p}+({\gamma}+\frac{d-1}{2})|\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}|
. Then for
x=t\xi
and
y=s\eta
and

(x,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
,

(3.18)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\frac{s^{-\tau_{1}}(1-s)^{-\tau_{2}}(1+{\langle}\xi,\eta{\rangle})^{-\rho}{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(y,s)}{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;s)^{\frac{p}{2}}\big(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((x,t),(y,s))\big)^{{\kappa}p}}\leq cn^{-d+2|\tau|}{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;t)^{1-\frac{p}{2}}.

Proof.

For
\rho=0
and
\tau_{i}=0
, this estimated is established in
[
27
, Lemma 4,1,4]
. The proof follows
along the same argument, we provide an account on the modification. The first step is to show that it is
sufficient to consider only
p=2
by using the doubling property of
{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}
, which remains valid when

\rho>0
and
\tau_{i}>0
. Let
J_{2}
denote the left-hand side of (
3.18
)
with
p=2
. Then, using

(3.19)

\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}}g({\langle}\xi,\eta{\rangle})d\sigma(\eta)={\omega}_{d-1}\int_{-1}^{1}g(u)(1-u^{2})^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\mathrm{d}u

and
{\theta}\sim 2\sin\frac{{\theta}}{2}=\sqrt{1-\cos{\theta}}
for
{\theta}\leq[0,\pi]
, we deduce that

\displaystyle J_{2}\,

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{1}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{s^{d-1-\tau_{1}-\rho}(1-s)^{-\tau_{2}}(1+u)^{-\rho}(1-u^{2})^{\frac{d-3}{2}}{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)}{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;s)\big(1+n\arccos\big(\sqrt{ts}\sqrt{\frac{1+u}{2}}+\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{1-s}\big)\big)^{2{\kappa}}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{s^{d-1-\tau_{1}-\rho}(1-s)^{-\tau_{2}}v^{d-2-2\rho}(1-v^{2})^{\frac{d-3}{2}}{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)}{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;s)\left(1+n\sqrt{1-\sqrt{ts}v-\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{1-s}}\right)^{2k}}\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}s,

where the second step follows from the changing variable
\sqrt{\frac{1+u}{2}}\mapsto v
. Making a further changing
of variable
v\mapsto z/\sqrt{s}
gives

\displaystyle J_{2}\,

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{s}}\frac{s^{1-\tau_{1}+\rho}(1-s)^{-\tau_{2}}\,z^{d-2-2\rho}(s-z^{2})^{\frac{d-3}{2}}{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)}{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;s)\left(1+n\sqrt{1-\sqrt{t}\,z-\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{1-s}}\right)^{2{\kappa}}}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s.

We now use
s^{1-\tau_{1}+\rho}z^{d-2-2\rho}\leq s^{\frac{d}{2}-\tau_{1}}\leq(s+n^{-2})^{\frac{d}{2}-\tau_{1}}\leq n^{2\tau_{1}}(s+n^{-2})^{\frac{d}{2}}
, which holds for
\rho\leq(d-2)/2
and
\tau_{1}\leq d/2
, and the
formulas for
{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)
and
{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;s)
to deduce

\displaystyle J_{2}\leq cn^{2\tau_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{s}}\frac{(1-s)^{-\tau_{2}}(s-z^{2})^{\frac{d-3}{2}}}{(1-s+n^{-2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1+n\sqrt{1-\sqrt{t}\,z-\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{1-s}}\right)^{2{\kappa}}}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}s.

We make one more change of variable
s\mapsto 1-w^{2}
and make use of
1-s=w^{2}/(1+\sqrt{1-w^{2}})\leq w^{2}
to obtain

\displaystyle J_{2}\,

\displaystyle\leq cn^{2\tau_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-w^{2}}}\frac{w^{1-2\tau_{2}}(1-w^{2}-z^{2})^{\frac{d-3}{2}}}{\left(w^{2}+n^{-2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(1+n\sqrt{1-\sqrt{t}z-\sqrt{1-t}\,w}\right)^{2{\kappa}}}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}w

\displaystyle\leq cn^{2\tau_{1}+2\tau_{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{1-w^{2}}}\frac{(1-w^{2}-z^{2})^{\frac{d-3}{2}}}{\left(1+n\sqrt{1-\sqrt{t}z-\sqrt{1-t}\,w}\right)^{2{\kappa}}}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}w,

where the second inequality follows from
w^{1-2\tau_{2}}\leq(w^{2}+n^{-2})^{\frac{1}{2}-\tau_{2}}\leq n^{2\tau_{2}}(w^{2}+n^{-2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
,
which holds for
\tau\leq\frac{1}{2}
. The last integral in the right-hand side has appeared and estimated in the proof
of
[
27
, Lemma 4.14]
, from which the desired estimate follows.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let

\displaystyle\int_{[-1,1]^{2}}

\displaystyle\frac{(1-v_{1})^{\frac{d-2}{2}-1}(1-v_{2})^{{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}}}{\big(1+n\sqrt{1-\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)}\,\big)^{{\beta}}}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{cn^{-(2{\gamma}+d-1)}}{\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;t)}\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;s)}\big(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((x,t),(y,s))\big)^{{\beta}-3{\gamma}-\frac{3d+1}{2}}}.

3.3.
Bernstein inequality for
\partial_{t}
on the conic surface

To prove the Bernstein inequality for the first-order derivatives, we need to understand the action
of these derivatives on the highly localized kernels. We start with the case
\partial_{t}
. The key ingredient
lies in the estimates below.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
{\gamma}\geq-\frac{1}{2}
. Then for any
{\kappa}>0
,

\left|\partial_{t}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s))\right|\leq\frac{c_{\kappa}n^{d+1}}{\varphi(t)\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;t)}\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;s)}\big(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((x,t),(y,s))\big)^{{\kappa}}}.

Proof.

Taking derivative on the integral expression (
3.5
) of the kernel and writing
\zeta(v)
in place of

\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)
, we obtain with
{\lambda}={\gamma}+d-1
,

(3.20)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s))

\displaystyle=c_{{\gamma}}\int_{[-1,1]^{2}}\partial L_{n}^{({\lambda}-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2})}\big(2\zeta(v)^{2}-1\big)

\displaystyle\times 4\zeta(v)\partial_{t}\zeta(v)(1-v_{1}^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}-1}(1-v_{2}^{2})^{{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}v,

where
\partial L_{n}^{({\lambda}-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2})}(z)=\frac{{\mathsf{d}}}{{\mathsf{d}}z}L_{n}^{({\lambda}-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2})}(z)
. We first prove the following estimate

(3.21)

\displaystyle\left|2\varphi(t)\partial_{t}\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)\right|\leq\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2}(v_{1})+\Sigma_{3}(v_{2}),

where

\Sigma_{1}={\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}\big((x,t),(y,s)\big),\quad\Sigma_{2}(v_{1})=(1-v_{1})\sqrt{s},\quad\Sigma_{3}(v_{2})=(1-v_{2})\sqrt{1-s}.

Recall
\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)=v_{1}\sqrt{\tfrac{st+{\langle}x,y{\rangle}}{2}}+v_{2}\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{1-s}
and
|\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)|\leq 1
.
For simplicity, we write
\Psi=\sqrt{\frac{1+{\langle}\xi,\eta{\rangle}}{2}}
. Since
{\langle}\xi,\eta{\rangle}=\cos{\mathsf{d}}_{\mathbb{S}}(\xi,\eta)

in terms of the geodesic distance on
{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
, it follows that
\Psi=\cos\frac{{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\xi,\eta)}{2}
. Taking
derivative of
\zeta
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)=v_{1}\frac{\sqrt{s}}{2\sqrt{t}}\,\Psi-v_{2}\frac{\sqrt{1-s}}{2\sqrt{1-t}},

and writing
v_{i}=1-(1-v_{i})
, it follows readily that

|2\varphi(t)\partial_{t}\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)|\leq(1-v_{1})\sqrt{s}+(1-v_{2})\sqrt{1-s}+\left|\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{s}\,\Psi-\sqrt{t}\sqrt{1-s}\right|.

If
t\geq s
, we write the last term as

\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{s}\,\Psi-\sqrt{t}\sqrt{1-s}=-\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{s}(1-\Psi)+\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{t}\sqrt{1-s},

whereas if
t\leq s
, we write

\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{s}\,\Psi-\sqrt{t}\sqrt{1-s}=\left(\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{t}\sqrt{1-s}\right)\Psi-\sqrt{t}\sqrt{1-s}(1-\Psi).

From these identities, we deduce the estimate

\left|\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{s}\,\Psi-\sqrt{t}\sqrt{1-s}\right|\leq\left|\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{t}\sqrt{1-s}\right|+(ts)^{\frac{1}{4}}(1-\Psi).

Since
\Psi=\cos\frac{{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\xi,\eta)}{2}
, we obtain an upper bound
1-\Psi\leq\frac{1}{2}{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\xi,\eta)
,
which implies by (
3.10
) that
(ts)^{\frac{1}{4}}{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\xi,\eta)\leq c\,{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\big((x,t),(y,s)\big)
.
Furthermore, we also have

\displaystyle|\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{t}\sqrt{1-s}|\,

\displaystyle\leq|\sqrt{1-t}-\sqrt{1-s}|\sqrt{s}+|\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{t}|\sqrt{1-s}

\displaystyle\leq 2{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}\big((x,t),(y,s)\big),

using (
3.11
). Putting these inequalities together, we have proved (
3.21
). Using this estimate,
we can then use (
3.9
) in (
3.20
) to obtain for a positive number
{\beta}
,

\displaystyle\left|\varphi(t)\partial_{t}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s)\big)\right|\leq\,c\int_{[-1,1]^{2}}\frac{n^{2{\lambda}+3}}{\left(1+n\sqrt{1-\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)}\right)^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\big(\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2}(v_{1})+\Sigma_{3}(v_{2})\big)(1-v_{1}^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}-1}(1-v_{2}^{2})^{{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}v.

The integral with
\Sigma_{1}
term is bounded by applying Lemma
3.8
and choosing
\beta

appropriately. The integral with
\Sigma_{2}(v_{1})
term is bounded by applying the same lemma but with

(1-v_{1})^{\frac{d-2}{2}-1}
replaced by
(1-v_{1})^{\frac{d}{2}-1}
and then using

n^{-1}\sqrt{s}\leq(\sqrt{t}+n^{-1})(\sqrt{s}+n^{-1})
, whereas the integral with
\Sigma_{3}(v_{2})
can
be handled similarly. In fact, the estimate of the last two integrals are already appeared in the proof
of
[
27
, Theorem 4.13]
. This completes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove the Bernstein inequality for
\partial_{t}
on the conic surface.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
{\mathsf{w}}
be a doubling weight on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
. Let
\ell
be a positive integer and
1\leq p<\infty
. Then

(3.22)

\|\partial_{t}f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}\leq cn^{2\ell}\|f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}},\quad\forall f\in\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}),

and, with
\varphi(t)=\sqrt{t(1-t)}
,

(3.23)

\left\|\varphi^{\ell}\partial_{t}^{\ell}f\right\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}\leq cn^{\ell}\|f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}},\quad\forall f\in\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}).

Proof.

By the definition of
{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\left({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};\cdot,\cdot\right)
, every
f\in\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
satisfies

(3.24)

f(x,t)=\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}f(y,s){\mathsf{L}}_{n}\left({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s)\right){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y,s).

Taking
\partial_{t}
derivative and using the maximal function
f_{{\beta},n}^{\ast}
in (
3.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{t}f(x,t)\right|\leq cf_{{\beta},n}^{\ast}(x,t)\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\frac{\left|\partial_{t}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\left({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s)\right)\right|}{\big(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((x,t),(y,s))\big)^{\beta}}{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(y,s).

Using the first estimate in Lemma
3.9
and choosing
{\beta}=2{\alpha}({\mathsf{w}})/p
and

{\kappa}>{\beta}+2d+\frac{1}{2}({\gamma}+\frac{d-2}{2})
, we see that the integral in the right-hand side is bounded by

\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}|f(y,s)|\frac{n^{d+1}{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(y,s)}{\varphi(t)\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;t)}\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;s)}\big(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((x,t),(y,s))\big)^{{\kappa}-{\beta}}}\leq c\frac{n}{\varphi(t)}

where the last step follows from (
3.18
) with
p=1
and
\rho=0
. Consequently, we obtain

(3.25)

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{t}f(x,t)\right|\leq c\frac{n}{\varphi(t)}f_{{\beta},n}^{\ast}(x,t).

In particular, it follows by Proposition
3.6
and
\varphi(t)\geq\frac{1}{n}
for

\delta n^{-2}\leq t\leq 1-\delta n^{-2}
that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}\leq\|\partial_{t}f\chi_{n,\delta}(t)\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}\leq cn^{2}\|f_{{\beta},n}^{\ast}\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}\leq cn\|f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}},

where the last step follows from (
3.13
), which proves the inequality (
3.22
)
for
\ell=1
. Iterating the inequality for
\ell=1
proves (
3.22
) for
\ell>1
. In the case of

p=\infty
, we use
\|f\|_{\infty}
instead of
f_{{\beta},n}^{*}
in (
3.25
), the resulted estimate
immediately implies (
3.23
) and, with the help of (
3.17
), also (
3.22
).

Since
\left|\varphi(t)\partial_{t}f(x,t)\right|\leq cnf_{{\beta},n}^{\ast}(x,t)
by (
3.25
), we obtain immediately, by (
3.13
), that
\left\|\varphi\partial_{t}f\right\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}\leq cn\|f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}
, which proves
(
3.23
) for
\ell=1
. Moreover, since

\left(\varphi(t)\partial_{t}\right)^{2}=(1-2t)\partial_{t}+\varphi(t)^{2}\partial^{2}_{t},

we obtain immediately that

\left\|\varphi^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}f\right\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}\leq\|\partial_{t}f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}+\|\left(\varphi(t)\partial_{t}\right)^{2}f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}\leq cn^{2}\|f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}},

where we have applyed the inequality (
3.22
) with
\ell=1
and the inequality (
3.23
)
with
\ell=1
twice in the second step. This proves the inequality (
3.23
) for
\ell=2
. Since

\varphi^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}f
is a polynomial in
\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}})
, we can iterate the inequality
(
3.23
) with
\ell=2
to establish the inequality for all even
\ell
and, similarly, establish
the inequality for odd
\ell
after applying the inequality with
\ell=1
once. This completes the proof.
∎

3.4.
Bernstein inequality for
D_{i,j}
on the conic surface

We start with the estimate of the derivative of the localized kernel.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
{\gamma}\geq-\frac{1}{2}
. Let
D_{i,j}
be the operator acting on
x
variable.
Then for any
{\kappa}>0
and
(x,t),(y,s)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
,

\left|D_{i,j}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s))\right|\leq\frac{c_{\kappa}n^{d+1}\sqrt{t}}{\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;t)}\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;s)}\big(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((x,t),(y,s))\big)^{{\kappa}}}

for
1\leq i<j\leq d
and, furthermore,

\left|D_{i,j}^{2}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s))\right|\leq\frac{c_{\kappa}n^{d+2}\big(t+\sqrt{t}\sqrt{s}(1+{\langle}\xi,\eta{\rangle})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)}{\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;t)}\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;s)}\big(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((x,t),(y,s))\big)^{{\kappa}}}.

Proof.

The inequality (
3.20
) holds with
D_{t}
replaced by
D_{i,j}
. First, we need an estimate
of
|D_{i,j}\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)|
. If
\xi,\eta\in{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
, then
2\sqrt{\frac{1+{\langle}\xi,\eta{\rangle}}{2}}=\|\xi+\eta\|
. Hence,
for
x=t\xi
and
y=s\eta
,

D_{i,j}\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)=v_{1}\frac{x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}}{4\sqrt{\tfrac{ts+{\langle}x,y{\rangle}}{2}}}=v_{1}\sqrt{t}\sqrt{s}\,\frac{\xi_{i}\eta_{j}-\xi_{j}\eta_{i}}{2\|\xi+\eta\|}.

Now, using the identity

\displaystyle 2(\xi_{i}\eta_{j}-\xi_{j}\eta_{i})=(\xi_{i}-\eta_{i})(\xi_{j}+\eta_{j})-(\xi_{j}-\eta_{j})(\xi_{i}+\eta_{i})

and
\|\xi-\eta\|\leq{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\xi,\eta)
for
\xi,\eta\in{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
, it follows immediately that

|\xi_{j}\eta_{j}-\xi_{j}\eta_{i}|\leq\|\xi-\eta\|\,\|\xi+\eta\|\leq\|\xi+\eta\|\,{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\xi,\eta).

Putting these together, we obtain the inequality

\displaystyle|D_{i,j}\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)|\leq|v_{1}|\sqrt{t}\sqrt{s}\,{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\xi,\eta)\leq\sqrt{t}\sqrt{s}\,{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\xi,\eta).

If
s\leq t
, then

Consequently, we have proved the inequality

\displaystyle|D_{i,j}\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)|\leq c\sqrt{t}{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((x,t),(y,s)).

Using this inequality and

\displaystyle\left|D_{i,j}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}\big({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s)\big)\right|\leq\,

\displaystyle\times(1-v_{1}^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}-1}(1-v_{2}^{2})^{{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}v

from which the desired estimate follows from Lemma

Now, taking the derivative
D_{i,j}
one more time, we obtain

\displaystyle D_{i,j}^{2}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s))

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle c_{{\gamma}}\int_{[-1,1]^{2}}\partial^{2}L_{n}^{({\lambda}-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2})}\big(2\zeta(v)^{2}-1\big)\left(4\zeta(v)D_{i,j}\zeta(v)\right)^{2}(1-v_{1}^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}-1}(1-v_{2}^{2})^{{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle+\,

\displaystyle c_{{\gamma}}\int_{[-1,1]^{2}}\partial L_{n}^{({\lambda}-\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2})}\big(2\zeta(v)^{2}-1\big)4D_{i,j}\left[\zeta(v)D_{i,j}\zeta(v)\right](1-v_{1}^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}-1}(1-v_{2}^{2})^{{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}v.

Applying (

in the right-hand side is bounded by

\displaystyle c\int_{[-1,1]^{2}}\frac{n^{2{\lambda}+5}t{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}\big((x,t),(y,s)\big)^{2}}{\left(1+n\sqrt{1-\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)}\right)^{\kappa}}(1-v_{1}^{2})^{\frac{d-2}{2}-1}(1-v_{2}^{2})^{{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}v,

which can be estimated as in the case of

to give the desired upper bound. Moreover,
the second integral is a sum of two integrals according to

D_{i,j}\left[\zeta(v)D_{i,j}\zeta(v)\right]=\left[D_{i,j}\zeta(v)\right]^{2}+\zeta(v)D_{i,j}^{2}\zeta(v);

while the first one, using (

D_{i,j}^{2}\zeta(x,t,y,s;v)=-v_{1}\sqrt{t}\sqrt{s}\left(\frac{\xi_{i}\eta_{i}+\xi_{j}\eta_{j}}{2\|\xi+\eta\|}+\frac{(\xi_{i}\eta_{j}-\xi_{j}\eta_{i})^{2}}{2\|\xi+\eta\|^{3}}\right),

4.
Bernstein inequalities on the cone

We work with the solid cone
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
in this section. In the first subsection, we recall the orthogonal
polynomials and their kernels on the cone. Instead of estimating the derivatives of the kernels as in the
previous section, we rely on an intimate connection between analysis on the cone and on the conic surface,
which is explained in the second subsection. The proof of the Bernstein inequalities on the cone, based

and its corollary is in the fourth subsection.

4.1.
Orthogonal polynomials and localized kernels

Let
\Pi_{n}^{d}
denote the space of polynomials of degree at most
n
in
d

\mu>-\frac{1}{2}

and
{\gamma}>-1

by

W_{\mu,{\gamma}}(x,t)=(t^{2}-\|x\|^{2})^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{\gamma},\qquad\|x\|\leq t,\quad 0\leq t\leq 1.

Orthogonal polynomials with respect to
W_{\mu,{\gamma}}
on
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
are studied in
[
25

, which are
orthogonal with respect to the inner product

{\langle}f,g{\rangle}_{W_{\mu,{\gamma}}}={\mathsf{b}}_{\mu,{\gamma}}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}}f(x,t)g(x,t)W_{\mu,{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{\mu,{\gamma}})
be the space of orthogonal polynomials of degree

\dim{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{\mu,{\gamma}})=\binom{n+d+1}{n},\quad n=0,1,2,\ldots.

An orthogonal basis of

can be given in terms of the Jacobi polynomials
and the orthogonal polynomials on the unit ball. For

\{P_{\mathbf{k}}^{m}(W_{\mu}):|{\mathbf{k}}|=m,{\mathbf{k}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{B}}^{d},W_{\mu})
on the unit ball. Let

(4.1)

{\mathbf{J}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}(x,t):=P_{n-m}^{(2m+2\mu+d-1,{\gamma})}(1-2t)t^{m}P_{\mathbf{k}}^{m}\left(W_{\mu};\frac{x}{t}\right),\quad

Then
\{{\mathbf{J}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}(x,t):|{\mathbf{k}}|=m,\,0\leq m\leq n\}
is an orthogonal basis of
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{\mu,{\gamma}})
.
The following theorem is established in
[
25
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mu>-\tfrac{1}{2}
,
{\gamma}>-1
and

. Let

[

27

Lemma 4.8
.

The operator
{\mathcal{D}}_{\mu,{\gamma}}
can be written as

(4.20)

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{D}}_{\mu,{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t^{d}W_{\mu,{\gamma}}(x,t)}(\partial_{t}+t^{-1}{\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle})t^{d+1}W_{\mu,{\gamma}+1}(x,t)(\partial_{t}+t^{-1}{\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle})

\displaystyle+t^{-1}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{W_{\mu,{\gamma}}(x,t)}\partial_{x_{i}}\big(W_{\mu+1,{\gamma}}(x,t)\partial_{x_{i}}\big)+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}(D_{i,j}^{(x)})^{2}\bigg).

Proof.

We start with an observation that follows from a quick computation,

\displaystyle t(\partial_{t}+t^{-1}{\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle})(t^{2}-\|x\|^{2})^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=t(2\mu-1)(t^{2}-\|x\|^{2})^{\mu-\frac{3}{2}}(t-t^{-1}{\langle}x,x{\rangle})

\displaystyle=(2\mu-1)(t^{2}-\|x\|^{2})^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}},

from which it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{d}W_{\mu,{\gamma}}(x,t)}\left((\partial_{t}+t^{-1}{\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle})t^{d+1}W_{\mu,{\gamma}+1}(x,t)(\partial_{t}+t^{-1}{\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=t^{-1}(2\mu+d){\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle}+(2\mu+d)\partial_{t}-(2\mu+d+{\gamma}+1)(t\partial_{t}+{\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+t(1-t)(t^{-1}{\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle}+\partial_{t})^{2}.

The last term can be written as

\displaystyle t(1-t)(\partial_{t}+t^{-1}{\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle})^{2}=(1-t)(t\partial_{t}+{\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle})(\partial_{t}+t^{-1}{\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle})

\displaystyle\quad=t(1-t)\partial_{t}^{2}+2(1-t){\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle}\partial_{t}-{\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}^{2}+{\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle}+t^{-1}\left({\langle}x,\nabla{\rangle}^{2}-{\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle}\right).

Putting together shows that

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{D}}_{\mu,{\gamma}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{d}W_{\mu,{\gamma}}(x,t)}\left((t^{-1}{\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle}+\partial_{t})t^{d+1}W_{\mu,{\gamma}+1}(x,t)(t^{-1}{\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle}+\partial_{t})\right)

\displaystyle+t^{-1}\left(t^{2}\Delta_{x}-{\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle}^{2}-(2\mu+d-1){\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle}\right).

To conclude the proof, we need the following identity,

\displaystyle t^{2}\Delta_{x}-{\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle}^{2}-(2\mu+d-1){\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{W_{\mu,{\gamma}}(x,t)}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}\big(W_{\mu+1,{\gamma}}(x,t)\partial_{x_{i}}\big)+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}(D_{i,j}^{(x)})^{2},

which we claim to hold. Indeed, if we dilate this identity by
x=ty
, and use the relations
t^{2}\Delta_{x}=\Delta_{y}
,

{\langle}x,\nabla_{x}{\rangle}={\langle}y,\nabla_{y}{\rangle}
,
D_{i,j}^{(x)}=D_{i,j}^{(y)}
, and

(4.21)

\frac{1}{W_{\mu,{\gamma}}(x,t)}\partial_{x_{i}}\left(W_{\mu+1,{\gamma}}(x,t)\partial_{x_{i}}\right)=\frac{1}{\varpi_{\mu}(y)}\partial_{y_{i}}\left(\varpi_{\mu+1}(y)\partial_{y_{i}}\right),

where
\varpi_{\mu}(y)=(1-\|y\|^{2})^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}
, we see that the claimed identity becomes

\displaystyle\Delta_{y}-{\langle}y,\nabla_{y}{\rangle}^{2}-(2\mu+d-1){\langle}y,\nabla_{y}{\rangle}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{\varpi_{\mu}(y)}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{y_{i}}\left(\varpi_{\mu+1}(y)\partial_{y_{i}}\right)+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}(D_{i,j}^{(y)})^{2},

which can be easily verified and is in fact the decomposition of the spectral differential operator for the
unit ball
{\mathbb{B}}^{d}
(cf.
[
13
, Section 5.2]
). This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\mu,{\gamma}}\cdot gW_{\mu,{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{1}t^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}^{d}}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\mu,{\gamma}}f(ty,t)\cdot g(ty,t)W_{\mu,{\gamma}}(ty,t)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\int_{0}^{1}t^{2\mu+d-2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}^{d}}\partial_{y_{i}}\left((1-\|y\|^{2})^{\mu+\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{y_{i}}\right)f(ty,t)\cdot g(x,t)\mathrm{d}y(1-t)^{\gamma}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}t^{2\mu+d-2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}^{d}}\partial_{y_{i}}f(ty,t)\cdot\partial_{y_{i}}g(ty,t)\varpi_{\mu+1}(y)\mathrm{d}y(1-t)^{\gamma}\mathrm{d}t

2.16

Polymer in a multi-interface medium with weak repulsion
Elric Angot

Abstract
Pinning phenomena for long linear polymers have been studied for a long time. In 2009 Caravenna and Pétrélis Caravenna and Pétrélis 2009 investigated the effect of a periodic and repulsive multi-interface medium on a ( 1 + 1 ) (1+1) -directed polymer model, when the distance between consecutive interfaces scales with the length of the polymer and with a constant temperature. In this paper, we extend that model and consider weak repulsion , by letting both the temperature and the distance between interfaces scale with the length of the polymer. We obtain a full diagram for this model, showing the behaviour of the polymer depending on the scaling exponents associated to the repulsion and the spacing parameters. When the repulsion is not too weak compared to the interface spacing, we obtain different regimes that extend those obtained by Caravenna and Pétrélis, and either finitely or infinitely many interfaces are visited. When the two exponents match we obtain a diffusive regime with a non-trivial and temperature-dependent diffusion constant. Our key tools include the renewal approach used in the original paper as well as new sharp results on the simple random walk evolving between interfaces. † † footnotetext: 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification. 60K35, 60F05, 82B41 † † footnotetext: Key words and phrases. Polymer, Interface, Defect Line, Renewal, Pinning, Random Walk.

Introduction

1.1
Model and notation

Constrained or interacting random walks may exhibit an unusually confined behaviour within a very small region of space, in contrast to the usual diffusive scaling. This phenomenon can be observed, for instance, with the collapse transition of a polymer in a poor solvent
Carmona et al. 2018
, the pinning of a polymer on a defect line
Giacomin 2007
;
Giacomin and Toninelli 2006
or when random walks are confined by obstacles
Sznitman 1998
. These models, often inspired by fields like Biology, Chemistry, or Physics, pose intricate mathematical challenges and have remained a vibrant area of research.

We consider a (1 + 1)-dimensional polymer interacting with infinitely many equally spaced repulsive interfaces. The possible configurations of the polymer are modeled by the trajectories of the simple random walk
(i,S_{i})_{i\geq 0}
, where
S_{0}=0
and
\left(S_{i}-S_{i-1}\right)_{i\geq 1}
is a sequence of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables uniformly distributed on
\{-1,1\}
. We denote by
P
the law of the simple random walk, and by
E
its associated expectation. The polymer receives an energetic penalty
\delta
(with
\delta>0
) every time it touches one of the horizontal interfaces located at heights
\{kT:k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. We assume that
T\in 2\mathbb{N}
for convenience. More precisely, the polymer interacts with the interfaces through the following Hamiltonian:

H_{N,\delta}^{T}:=\delta\underset{i=1}{\overset{N}{\sum}}\mathbf{1}\{S_{i}\in T\mathbb{Z}\}=\delta\underset{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\overset{}{\sum}}\underset{i=1}{\overset{N}{\sum}}\mathbf{1}\{S_{i}=kT\},

(1.1)

where
N\in\mathbb{N}
is the number of monomers constituting the polymer. We introduce the corresponding polymer measure
\mathbf{P}_{N,\delta}^{T}
:

\frac{d\mathbf{P}_{N,\delta}^{T}}{dP}(S):=\frac{\exp\Big(-H_{N,\delta}^{T}(S)\Big)}{Z_{N,\delta}^{T}},

(1.2)

where the normalisation constant
Z_{N,\delta}^{T}=E[\exp(-H_{N,\delta}^{T}(S))]
is the
partition function
.

Caravenna and Pétrélis
Caravenna and Pétrélis 2009
considered the case when the repulsion strength
\delta
is fixed while the interface spacing
T_{N}
is allowed to vary with the size of the polymer
N
.
Before summarizing their results, we first set some
notations. For
(a_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
a positive sequence, we write
S_{N}\simeq a_{N}
if for all

\varepsilon>0
, there exists an
M>0
such that
\mathbf{P}_{N,\delta}^{T_{N}}\left(|S_{N}/a_{N}|\geq M\right)\leq\varepsilon
(that is,
\frac{S_{N}}{a_{N}}
sampled from

\mathbf{P}_{N,\delta}^{T_{N}}
is a tight sequence), and if there exist

\nu>0
and
0<p<1
such that
\mathbf{P}_{N,\delta}^{T_{N}}\left(|S_{N}/a_{N}|\geq\nu\right)\geq p
.
Caravenna and Pétrélis proved the following result

(
Caravenna and Pétrélis 2009
, Theorem 1)
: for every penalty
\delta>0
:

S_{N}\text{ under }\mathbf{P}_{N,\delta}^{T_{N}}\simeq\begin{cases}\sqrt{N/T_{N}}&\text{ if }\frac{T_{N}^{3}}{N}=O(1)\\
\min\{T_{N},\sqrt{N}\}&\text{ if }T_{N}^{3}\geq N.\end{cases}

(1.3)

Let us give a heuristic explanation for these scalings. For a fixed
T\in 2\mathbb{N}
, the process
(S_{n})_{n=0}^{N}
sampled from
\mathbf{P}_{N,\delta}^{T}
behaves similarly as a time-homogenous Markov process. In fact, the random variable
\hat{\tau}:=\inf\{n>0:|S_{n}|=T\}
that gives the time needed for the polymer to switch interface is almost a renewal process.
Moreover,
\hat{\tau}
is of order
T^{3}
, because of the repulsion strength given by interfaces. Indeed, for the simple random walk (SRW), it takes around
T^{2}
steps to switch interfaces, but here, due to compensation with the free energy, the number of steps required is bigger. For more details, see
(
Caravenna and Pétrélis 2009
, (2.3))
, where one can notice that the
T^{3}
comes from the second term in the Taylor expansion of the free energy. Note that the prefactor before this
T^{3}
term is a function of
\delta
, hinting at a possible change of behaviour in our model when
\delta
goes to
0
.

Because
\hat{\tau}
is of order
T^{3}
, the polymer will perform around
N/T^{3}
changes of interface. Assuming these considerations are still true when
(T_{N})
varies with
N
, the polymer will perform approximately
u_{N}:=N/T_{N}^{3}
changes of interface. By symmetry, the probability of going to the upper interface being the same as the probability of going to the lower interface, we deduce that
S_{N}\simeq T_{N}\sqrt{u_{N}}
if
u_{N}\rightarrow\infty
. On the other hand, when
u_{N}\rightarrow 0
, the polymer does not see any interface other than the origin, hence
S_{N}\simeq T_{N}
, at least when
T_{N}=O(\sqrt{N})
. Finally, if
u_{N}
is bounded, the polymer will visit a finite number of interfaces.

In this paper we extend the model by investigating the case where both the interface spacing
T=T_{N}
and the repulsion strength
\delta=\delta_{N}
are allowed to vary with the size
N
of the polymer. We assume that the sequence
(\delta_{N})
is non-increasing (weak interaction model). More precisely, we are
interested in the following questions: at which level will
\delta_{N}
matter? Is the
asymptotic behaviour of the polymer modified by the interplay between
\delta_{N}
and

T_{N}
? If so, how? When does the last contact with an interface take place?

Finally, let us cite the recent papers
Poisat and Simenhaus 2020
;
Poisat and Simenhaus 2022
which deal with the case of randomly located repulsive interfaces. The positions of the interfaces do not vary with
N
and are sampled according to a renewal process with polynomial inter-arrival distribution. A dichotomy between a weak form and a strong form of localisation, according to the value of the renewal exponent, is therein established for the polymer.

1.2
Main results

We consider
(T_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\in(2\mathbb{N})^{\mathbb{N}}
an non-decreasing sequence. The repulsion intensity is given by
(\delta_{N})_{N\geq 1}
, such that
\delta_{N}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
. For simplicity, we consider the power case, hence we assume that

T_{N}=N^{a}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\delta_{N}=\frac{\beta}{N^{b}},

(1.4)

where
a,b,\beta>0
are fixed.
It turns out that the same heuristic as the one described in the previous section can be applied when
\delta_{N}\rightarrow 0
as
N\rightarrow\infty
. The first thing one can note is that, if
\delta_{N}T_{N}\rightarrow 0
as
N\rightarrow\infty
, then the penalty is comparatively too low and
S_{N}
behaves as the simple random walk. If
\delta_{N}T_{N}\geq 1
for
N
large then the time to switch interface (i.e. to visit a neighboring interface) behaves as
T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}
. Assuming that this is true uniformly in
N
, the number of interface switches is of order
v_{N}:={N}/{(T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N})}
. Hence, if
v_{N}\rightarrow\infty
as
N\rightarrow\infty
, then
S_{N}
should be of order
T_{N}\sqrt{v_{N}}=\sqrt{{N}/{(T_{N}\delta_{N})}}
. Otherwise, if
v_{N}\rightarrow 0
as
N\rightarrow\infty
,
S_{N}
should be of order
T_{N}
. As we will see with Theorems
1.10
and
2
below, this heuristic turns out to be true, and there is indeed a change of behaviour when the repulsion strength crosses the threshold
N/T_{N}^{3}
, which in terms of the exponents
a
and
b
leads to comparing
b
with
3a-1
.

Before stating our main results, we need the following:

Definition 1
.

Let
(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be the simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}
. For every
T\in 2\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, we define
\left(\tau_{k}^{T}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
as follows, with
\infty\,\mathbb{Z}:=\{0\}
:

\tau_{0}^{T}=0,\qquad\tau_{k+1}^{T}=\inf\left\{n>\tau_{k}^{T}:S_{n}\in T\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

(1.5)

We also define
\tau^{T}:=\{\tau_{k}^{T}:k\geq 0\}

the set of contact points
and

\varepsilon_{k}^{T}=\frac{S_{\tau_{k}^{T}}-S_{\tau_{k-1}^{T}}}{T},\qquad k\in\mathbb{N}.

(1.6)

Last, we define, for
N\in\mathbb{N}
:

L_{N}:=\max\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}:\tau_{n}^{T}\leq N\right\}.

(1.7)

We might omit the superscript
T
for convenience. Clearly,
\tau_{j}^{T}
is the time of the
j
-th visit of the polymer to the interface set, while
\varepsilon_{j}^{T}
indicates whether the

j
-th interface visited is the same as the

(j-1)
-th one
\left(\varepsilon_{j}^{T}=0\right)
, or the one above

\left(\varepsilon_{j}^{T}=1\right)
or the one below
\left(\varepsilon_{j}^{T}=-1\right)
.

1.10
is dedicated to the regime

\displaystyle 1/3<a<1/2,

\displaystyle\text{ that is}

\displaystyle N^{1/3}\ll T_{N}\ll\sqrt{N},

(1.8)

\displaystyle\text{and}

\displaystyle 3a-1>b,

\displaystyle\text{ that is}

\displaystyle N\ll T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N},

where the localisation of the last contact of the polymer is non trivial; while the other regimes are covered by Theorem

Theorem 1
.

Assume that either
\frac{1}{3}<a<\frac{1}{2}
and
3a-1>b
, or
a<\frac{1}{2}
and
b\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
\nu>0
,
M>0
and
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for

:

\mathbf{P}_{N,\delta_{N}}^{T_{N}}\left({\frac{\nu}{\delta_{N}^{2}}\leq\tau_{L_{N}}^{T_{N}}\leq\frac{M}{\delta_{N}^{2}}}\right)\geq 1-\varepsilon.

(1.9)

Moreover:

\underset{N\longrightarrow\infty}{\lim}\mathbf{P}_{N,\delta_{N}}^{T_{N}}\left(\exists i\leq N:S_{i}\in T_{N}\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}\right)=0.

(1.10)

In other words, in this regime the only interface visited by the polymer is the one at the origin and the last contact with it is roughly at
1/\delta_{N}^{2}\ll N
, with high probability. The polymer eventually gets stuck between the origin and one of the two (above or below) neighbouring interfaces.
The case

is less surprising, because the SRW does not reach points beyond
\sqrt{N}
, but we included it in Theorem
1.10
for completeness.

We expect the endpoint to follow the quasi-stationary distribution for the walk constrained to stay in
[0,T_{N}]
, similarly as the law of

(center of localization) in
(
Bouchot 2022
, Theorem 1.1)
. We will not prove this result here, as it would further extend an already lengthy paper.

2
Preparation

In this section, we first introduce the notion of free energy, a key concept in statistical mechanics. Following
Caravenna and Pétrélis 2009
, we then introduce a renewal process that is linked to the polymer measure, that is another key concept coming from the study of pinning models, see
Giacomin 2007
;
Giacomin 2011
.

2.1
Free energy and asymptotic estimates

Let us assume for now that
T\in 2\mathbb{N}
is fixed. We define the
free energy

\phi(\delta,T)
as the rate of exponential growth of the partition function defined below (
1.2
):

\phi(\delta,T):=\underset{N\longrightarrow\infty}{\lim}\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N,\delta}^{T}=\underset{N\longrightarrow\infty}{\lim}\frac{1}{N}\log E\left(e^{-H_{N,\delta}^{T}}\right).

(2.1)

Investigating this function is motivated by identifying the points where the function
\delta\longrightarrow\phi(\delta,T)
is not analytic. These points typically correspond to significant changes or transitions in the system, known as
phase transition
. In our specific case, we do not observe any. However, even in the absence of phase transitions, studying this function remains valuable for understanding polymer trajectories in our model, as elaborated in Section
2.2
. For this reason, we recall some basic results on free energy proven in
Caravenna and Pétrélis 2009
.

Let us remind Definition
1
:
\tau_{1}^{T}
is the random variable recording the time of the first contact of the simple random walk with an interface. We define
Q_{T}(\lambda):=E(e^{-\lambda\tau_{1}^{T}})
its Laplace transform under the simple random walk law. There exists
\lambda_{0}^{T}
such that
Q_{T}(\lambda)
is finite and analytic on
(\lambda_{0}^{T},\infty)
, where
Q_{T}(\lambda)\to+\infty
as
\lambda\downarrow\lambda_{0}^{T}
. More precise expressions of
Q_{T}(\lambda)
are known, see (A.4) and (A.5) in
Caravenna and Pétrélis 2009
. A common fact is that
Q_{T}(\cdot)
is deeply connected to the free energy. More precisely:

\phi(\delta,T)=\left(Q_{T}\right)^{-1}\left(e^{\delta}\right),

(2.2)

for all
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
(this is Theorem 1 of
Caravenna and Pétrélis 2009
, with a change of notations since we have reversed the sign of
\delta
compared to
Caravenna and Pétrélis 2009
). Let us come back to our model. Equation (
2.2
) gives us access to the asymptotic behaviour of
\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})
when
N\rightarrow\infty
, namely:

\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})=-\frac{\delta_{N}}{T_{N}}(1+o_{N}(1))\text{ if $a<b$},

(2.3)

\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})=-\frac{x_{\beta}^{2}}{2T_{N}^{2}}(1+o_{N}(1))\text{ if $a=b$ },

(2.4)

\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})=-\frac{\pi^{2}}{2T_{N}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{4}{T_{N}\delta_{N}}(1+o_{N}(1))\right)\text{ if $a>b$ },

(2.5)

where
x_{\beta}
is an explicit constant defined in Theorem
2
and plotted in Figure
3
. These estimates are proven in Appendix
A.1
.

2.2
Renewal representation

We now rewrite our model in terms of a certain renewal process, following
(
Caravenna and Pétrélis 2009
, Section 2.2)
. Recall Definition
1
. We denote by

q_{T}^{j}(n)
the joint law of
\left(\tau_{1}^{T},\varepsilon_{1}^{T}\right)
under the law of the simple random walk. It is defined for
(n,j)\in\mathbb{N}\times\{-1,0,1\}
:

q_{T}^{j}(n):=P\left(\tau_{1}^{T}=n,\varepsilon_{1}^{T}=j\right).

(2.6)

Of course, by symmetry,
q_{T}^{1}(n)=q_{T}^{-1}(n)
for all
n
and
T
. We also define

q_{T}(n):=P\left(\tau_{1}^{T}=n\right)=q_{T}^{0}(n)+2q_{T}^{1}(n).

(2.7)

We now introduce
\mathcal{P}_{\delta,T}
, the law of a Markov chain
\left\{\left(\tau_{j},\varepsilon_{j}\right)\right\}_{j\geq 0}
taking values in
(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})\times

\{-1,0,1\}
and defined as follows:
\tau_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}=0
and, under
\mathcal{P}_{\delta,T}
, the sequence of random vector
\left\{\left(\tau_{j}-\tau_{j-1},\varepsilon_{j}\right)\right\}_{j\geq 1}
is i.i.d. with joint law:

\mathcal{P}_{\delta,T}\left(\tau_{1}=n,\varepsilon_{1}=j\right):=e^{-\delta}q_{T}^{j}(n)e^{-\phi(\delta,T)n}.

(2.8)

By (
2.2
), this defines a probability measure.
Indeed, a simple computation gives:

\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\overset{}{\sum}}\underset{j\in\{-1,0,1\}}{\overset{}{\sum}}e^{-\delta}q_{T}^{j}(n)e^{-\phi(\delta,T)}=e^{-\delta}\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\overset{}{\sum}}q_{T}(n)e^{-\phi(\delta,T)}=e^{-\delta}Q_{T}(\phi(\delta,T))=1.

(2.9)

Note that the process
\left(\tau_{j}\right)_{j\geq 0}
alone under
\mathcal{P}_{\delta,T}
is a renewal process, for which
\tau_{0}=0
and the variables
\left(\tau_{j}-\tau_{j-1}\right)_{j\geq 1}
are i.i.d., with law

\mathcal{P}_{\delta,T}\left(\tau_{1}=n\right)=e^{-\delta}q_{T}(n)e^{-\phi(\delta,T)n}=e^{-\delta}P\left(\tau_{1}^{T}=n\right)e^{-\phi(\delta,T)n}.

(2.10)

We also introduce a random set containing the indices at which the process touches one of the interface:

\tau=\{\tau_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\},

(2.11)

i.e., with a slight abuse of notation, we use the same letter
\tau
to denote the renewal
process
and the corresponding
set
of renewal times. We shall use the same abuse of notation to the process
\tau^{T}
defined below (
1.5
).

Let us now make the link between the law
\mathcal{P}_{\delta,T}
and
\mathbf{P}_{N,\delta}^{T}
, the polymer measure defined in (
1.2
). Recall the definition of
L_{N}
in Definition
1
. By abuse of notation, we use
L_{N}
in the context of the renewal process with the same definition, i.e., under
\mathcal{P}_{\delta,T}
,
L_{N}:=\max\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\tau_{n}\leq N\right\}
. We now have the following crucial result (see
(
Caravenna and Pétrélis 2009
, Eq. (2.13) )
): for all
N,T\in 2\mathbb{N}
and for all
k\in\mathbb{N},\left\{t_{i}\right\}_{1\leq i\leq k}\in\mathbb{N}^{k},\left(\sigma_{i}\right)_{1\leq i\leq k}\in\{-1,0,1\}^{k}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{N,\delta}^{T}\left(L_{N}=k,\left(\tau_{i}^{T},\varepsilon_{i}^{T}\right)=\left(t_{i},\sigma_{i}\right),1\leq i\leq k\mid N\in\tau^{T}\right)

(2.12)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{\delta,T}\left(L_{N}=k,\left(\tau_{i},\varepsilon_{i}\right)=\left(t_{i},\sigma_{i}\right),1\leq i\leq k\mid N\in\tau\right),

where
\{N\in\tau\}:=\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}\left\{\tau_{k}=N\right\}
and similarly for
\left\{N\in\tau^{T}\right\}
. In other words, the process
\left\{\left(\tau_{j}^{T},\varepsilon_{j}^{T}\right)\right\}_{j}
under
\mathbf{P}_{N,\delta}^{T}\left(\cdot\mid N\in\tau^{T}\right)
is distributed in the same way as the Markov chain
\left\{\left(\tau_{j},\varepsilon_{j}\right)\right\}_{j}
under the measure
\mathcal{P}_{\delta,T}\left(\cdot\mid N\in\tau\right)
. It is precisely the link with the renewal theory that allows us to have precise estimates in our model. We shall also use on multiple occasions that

E\left[e^{-H_{k,\delta}^{T}(S)}\mathbf{1}_{\left\{k\in\tau^{T}\right\}}\right]=e^{\phi(\delta,T)k}\mathcal{P}_{\delta,T}\left(k\in\tau\right),

(2.13)

which is true for all
k,T\in 2\mathbb{N}
, see
(
Caravenna and Pétrélis 2009
, Eq. (2.11))
.

We will use the following proposition to prove Theorem

.

We start by a simple computation:

K_{k+1}(n)=\underset{l=\frac{n}{2}}{\overset{n-2}{\sum}}K_{k}(l)K_{1}(n-l)+\underset{l=\frac{n}{2}}{\overset{n}{\sum}}K_{1}(l)K_{k}(n-l).

(4.6)

Using
Q(1)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\underset{l=\frac{n}{2}}{\overset{n}{\sum}}K_{1}(l)K_{k}(n-l)\leq\underset{l=\frac{n}{2}}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{l^{3/2}}K_{k}(n-l)\leq\underset{l=\frac{n}{2}}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\frac{2^{5/2}}{n^{3/2}}K_{k}(n-l)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{n^{3/2}}P\left(\frac{n}{2}\leq\tau_{k}^{\infty}\right)

(4.7)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{n^{3/2}}.

Hence, (
4.6
) can be bounded from above by:

K_{k+1}(n)\leq\underset{l=\frac{n}{2}}{\overset{n-2}{\sum}}K_{k}(l)K_{1}(n-l)+\frac{8}{n^{3/2}}.

(4.8)

Let us give a sharp upper bound on the sum above. To do this, we split the sum in two parts:

\Sigma_{1}^{n}:=\underset{l=\frac{n}{2}}{\overset{n-2n^{\alpha}-2}{\sum}}K_{k}(l)K_{1}(n-l),

(4.9)

\Sigma_{2}^{n}:=\underset{l=n-2n^{\alpha}}{\overset{n-2}{\sum}}K_{k}(l)K_{1}(n-l),

(4.10)

where
0<\alpha<1/2
. We start by a lemma dealing with the first sum:

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume
Q(k)
. For
n
large enough,

\Sigma_{1}^{n}\leq\frac{kC}{\sqrt{\pi}n^{3/2}}\left(\frac{1}{n^{\alpha/2}}+\frac{1}{6n^{3\alpha/2}}+o_{n}\left(\frac{1}{n^{3\alpha/2}}\right)+O_{n}\left(\frac{1}{n^{1-\alpha/2}}\right)\right).

(4.11)

Proof.

We use that, as a consequence of (
4.2
),

K_{1}(2p)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}(2p)^{3/2}}+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}(2p)^{5/2}}

(4.12)

for
p
large enough. We remark that we take only the even terms of the sum, because
\tau_{1}^{\infty}
can take only even values. We also use (
4.5
). Hence, it comes that:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}=\underset{p=\frac{n}{4}}{\overset{n/2-n^{\alpha}-1}{\sum}}K_{1}(n-2p)K_{k}(2p)

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{p=\frac{n}{4}}{\overset{n/2-n^{\alpha}-1}{\sum}}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{(n-2p)^{3/2}}\frac{Ck}{\sqrt{\pi}(2p)^{3/2}}+\underset{p=\frac{n}{4}}{\overset{n/2-n^{\alpha}-1}{\sum}}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{(n-2p)^{5/2}}\frac{Ck}{\sqrt{\pi}(2p)^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}Ck}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{n/4}^{n/2-n^{\alpha}}\frac{dt}{(2t)^{3/2}(1-2t)^{3/2}}+\frac{\sqrt{2}Ck}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{n/4}^{n/2-n^{\alpha}}\frac{dt}{(2t)^{3/2}(1-2t)^{5/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Ck}{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\pi}n^{2}}\int_{1/2}^{1-\frac{2}{n^{1-\alpha}}}\frac{dt}{t^{3/2}(1-t)^{3/2}}+\frac{Ck}{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\pi}n^{3}}\int_{1/2}^{1-\frac{2}{n^{1-\alpha}}}\frac{dt}{t^{3/2}(1-t)^{5/2}}.

Using Lemma
4.4
with
\varepsilon=\frac{2}{n^{1-\alpha}}
:

\int_{1/2}^{1-\frac{2}{n^{1-\alpha}}}\frac{dt}{t^{3/2}(1-t)^{3/2}}\leq\sqrt{2}\frac{\sqrt{n}}{n^{\alpha/2}}\left(1+O_{n}\left(\frac{1}{n^{1-\alpha}}\right)\right).

(4.14)

Moreover,
\int_{1/2}^{1-\frac{2}{n^{1-\alpha}}}\frac{dt}{t^{3/2}(1-t)^{5/2}}\sim\frac{n^{3/2}}{3\sqrt{2}n^{3\alpha/2}}
. Therefore, (
4.13
) becomes:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{kC}{\sqrt{\pi}n^{3/2}}\left(\frac{1}{n^{\alpha/2}}+\frac{1}{6n^{3\alpha/2}}+o_{n}\left(\frac{1}{n^{3\alpha/2}}\right)+O_{n}\left(\frac{1}{n^{1-\alpha/2}}\right)\right).

(4.15)

∎

Now, let us prove Lemma
4.16
, which helps us deal with the sum in (
4.10
):

Lemma 4.5
.

We have the following bound from above:

\Sigma_{2}^{n}\leq\frac{Ck}{n^{3/2}}\left(1+\frac{3}{2n^{1-\alpha}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}n^{\alpha/2}}-\frac{3}{8\sqrt{\pi}\,n^{3\alpha/2}}+o_{n}\left(\frac{1}{n^{3\alpha/2}}\right)\right).

(4.16)

Proof.

By using
Q(k)
:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\underset{p=n-2n^{\alpha}}{\overset{n-2}{\sum}}\frac{Ck}{p^{3/2}}K_{1}(n-p)\leq\frac{Ck}{(n-n^{\alpha})^{3/2}}P(\tau_{1}^{\infty}\leq 2n^{\alpha}).

(4.17)

Thanks to Lemma
4.3
, we then have:

\displaystyle\frac{Ck}{(n-n^{\alpha})^{3/2}}P(\tau_{1}^{\infty}\leq 2n^{\alpha})\leq

\displaystyle\frac{Ck}{n^{3/2}}\left(1+\frac{3}{2n^{1-\alpha}}\right)

(4.18)

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}n^{\alpha/2}}-\frac{3}{8\sqrt{\pi}\,n^{3\alpha/2}}+o_{n}\left(\frac{1}{n^{3\alpha/2}}\right)\right).

∎

We now have the tools to prove (
1.17
).

Proof.

Let us start from (
4.8
). Thanks to Lemmas
4.11
and
4.16
, it comes that, for
n
large enough:

\displaystyle K_{k+1}(n)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{n^{3/2}}+\frac{Ck}{n^{3/2}}\left(1+\frac{3}{2n^{1-\alpha}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}n^{\alpha/2}}-\frac{3}{8n^{3\alpha/2}}\right)

(4.19)

\displaystyle+\frac{kC}{\sqrt{\pi}n^{3/2}}\left(\frac{1}{n^{\alpha/2}}+\frac{1}{6n^{3\alpha/2}}+O_{n}\left(\frac{1}{n^{1-\alpha/2}}\right)+o_{n}\left(\frac{1}{n^{3\alpha/2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8}{n^{3/2}}+\frac{Ck}{n^{3/2}}\left(1-\frac{5}{24n^{3\alpha/2}}+o_{n}\left(\frac{1}{n^{3\alpha/2}}\right)+O_{n}\left(\frac{1}{n^{1-\alpha}}\right)\right).

Therefore, when
\frac{3\alpha}{2}<1-\alpha
and for
n
large enough:

K_{k+1}(n)\leq\frac{8}{n^{3/2}}+\frac{Ck}{n^{3/2}}\left(1-\frac{5}{24n^{3\alpha/2}}(1+o_{n}(1))\right).

(4.20)

Hence,

K_{k+1}(n)\leq\frac{8}{n^{3/2}}+\frac{Ck}{n^{3/2}}.

(4.21)

We thus obtain the desired result for
n
large enough, let us say larger than
M
. Hence, because
K_{k}(n)=0
if
n\leq k
, we can deduce that
Q(k)
implies
Q(k+1)
if
k\geq M
and for
C\geq 8
. Therefore, we have to prove
Q(i)
for
i\leq M
. Using
(
Caravenna and Pétrélis 2009
, Eq. (B.9))
with
T=\infty
, we obtain that
K_{k}(n)\leq\frac{ck^{3}}{n^{3/2}}
. By choosing
C=\max\{cM^{2},8\}
, (
1.17
) is proven.

∎

4.3
Estimates of the time to hit the interfaces

We now want to prove (
1.15
). We see that, if
n<T
,
P\left(\tau_{k}^{T}=n\right)=P\left(\tau_{k}^{\infty}=n\right)
, because the simple random walk needs at least
T
steps to reach another interface. Therefore, thanks to (
1.17
), there is a constant
C>0
such that, for all
k>0
and
n<T
:

P\left(\tau_{k}^{T}=n\right)\leq\frac{Ck}{\min\{n^{3/2},T^{3}\}}e^{-ng(T)}.

(4.22)

We may ignore the exponential term since
n\leq 2T^{2}
and
g(T)\sim\pi^{2}/(2T)
. The technique we are going to use is the same as in Section
4.2
. Thus, let us prove the following proposition:

Proposition 4.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that, for all
1\leq n\leq 2T^{2}
even:

\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\quad P\left(\tau_{k}^{T}=n\right)\leq\frac{Ck}{n^{3/2}}.

(4.23)

Let us first note that such constant exists for
1\leq n<T
. As in Section
4.2
, we proceed by induction. Let
C>0
and
Q(k)
be the induction hypothesis:

\forall\,1<n<2T^{2}\text{ even },\qquad P\left(\tau_{k}^{T}=n\right)\leq\frac{Ck}{n^{3/2}}.

(4.24)

For
k=1
, the assertion above is true with the constant
c_{2}
from (
2.17
). Now, let us assume that
Q(k)
is true and consider
T\leq n\leq 2T^{2}
. Using the notation:
K_{k}^{T}(n):=P\left(\tau_{k}^{T}=n\right)
, we have:

K_{k+1}^{T}(n)=\underset{l=\frac{n}{2}}{\overset{n-2}{\sum}}K_{k}^{T}(l)K_{1}^{T}(n-l)+\underset{l=\frac{n}{2}}{\overset{n}{\sum}}K_{1}^{T}(l)K_{k}^{T}(n-l).

(4.25)

By (
2.17
) and a straightforward upper bound,

\displaystyle\underset{l=\frac{n}{2}}{\overset{n}{\sum}}K_{1}^{T}(l)K_{k}^{T}(n-l)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{l=\frac{n}{2}}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\frac{c_{2}}{l^{3/2}}K_{k}^{T}(n-l)\leq\underset{l=\frac{n}{2}}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\frac{2^{5/2}c_{2}}{n^{3/2}}K_{k}^{T}(n-l)

(4.26)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8c_{2}}{n^{3/2}}P\left(\frac{n}{2}\leq\tau_{k}^{T}\right)\leq\frac{8c_{2}}{n^{3/2}}.

Hence, (
4.25
) can be bounded from above by:

K_{k+1}^{T}(n)\leq\underset{l=\frac{n}{2}}{\overset{n-2}{\sum}}K_{k}^{T}(l)K_{1}^{T}(n-l)+\frac{8c_{2}}{n^{3/2}}.

(4.27)

We split the sum above in three parts in order to bound it from above:

\Sigma_{3}^{n}:=\underset{l=\frac{n}{2}}{\overset{n-\sqrt{n}}{\sum}}K_{k}^{T}(l)K_{1}^{T}(n-l),

(4.28)

\Sigma_{4}^{n}:=\underset{l=n-\sqrt{n}}{\overset{n-2n^{\alpha}-2}{\sum}}K_{k}^{T}(l)K_{1}^{T}(n-l),

(4.29)

\Sigma_{5}^{n}:=\underset{l=n-2n^{\alpha}}{\overset{n-2}{\sum}}K_{k}^{T}(l)K_{1}^{T}(n-l),

(4.30)

where
0<\alpha<1/2
. We bound
\Sigma_{3}^{n}
from above with the help of (
2.17
) and the induction hypothesis, following the same computations as in the proof of Lemma
4.11
:

\Sigma_{3}^{n}\leq kCc_{2}\underset{l=n/2}{\overset{n-\sqrt{n}}{\sum}}\frac{1}{(n-l)^{3/2}l^{3/2}}\leq\frac{kCc_{2}}{\sqrt{2}n^{3/2}}\left(\frac{1}{n^{1/4}}(1+o_{n}(1))\right).

(4.31)

Notice that we may replace
K_{1}^{T}(n-l)
by
K_{1}(n-l)
in (
4.29
) and (
4.30
) when
n-l\leq T
. Therefore,

\Sigma_{4}^{n}=\underset{l=n-\sqrt{n}}{\overset{n-2n^{\alpha}-2}{\sum}}K_{k}^{T}(l)K_{1}(n-l)\leq\underset{l=n/2}{\overset{n-2n^{\alpha}-2}{\sum}}K_{k}^{T}(l)K_{1}(n-l).

(4.32)

From there, the following upper bound arises by using the exact same technique as in Lemma
4.11
:

\Sigma_{4}^{n}\leq\frac{kC}{\sqrt{\pi}n^{3/2}}\left(\frac{1}{n^{\alpha/2}}+\frac{1}{6n^{3\alpha/2}}+o_{n}\left(\frac{1}{n^{3\alpha/2}}\right)O_{n}\left(\frac{1}{n^{1-\alpha/2}}\right)\right).

(4.33)

Also, by using the exact same technique as in Lemma
4.16
, we can bound from above (
4.30
):

\Sigma_{5}^{n}\leq\frac{Ck}{n^{3/2}}\left(1+\frac{3}{2n^{1-\alpha}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}n^{\alpha/2}}-\frac{3}{8\sqrt{\pi}\,n^{3\alpha/2}}+o_{n}\left(\frac{1}{n^{3\alpha/2}}\right)\right).

(4.34)

By summing the upper bounds in (
4.34
) and (
4.33
), and by choosing
\alpha
small enough such that
3\alpha/2<1-\alpha
, we obtain the same result as in (
4.19
) and (
4.20
):

\Sigma_{3}^{n}+\Sigma_{4}^{n}+\Sigma_{5}^{n}\leq\frac{Ck}{n^{3/2}}\left(1-\frac{5}{24n^{3\alpha/2}}(1+o_{n}(1))\right).

(4.35)

By summing the upper bounds in (
4.31
) and (
4.35
), taking
\alpha
small enough such that
3\alpha/2<1/4
, and taking
n
large enough such that
|o_{n}(1)|\leq 1
in (
4.31
) and (
4.35
):

\underset{l=\frac{n}{2}}{\overset{n-2}{\sum}}K_{k}^{T}(l)K_{1}^{T}(n-l)\leq\frac{Ck}{n^{3/2}}.

(4.36)

Therefore, by summing (
4.36
) and (
4.27
), provided
n
is large enough (say larger than
M
):

K_{k+1}^{T}(n)\leq\frac{Ck}{n^{3/2}}+\frac{8c_{2}}{n^{3/2}}.

(4.37)

Thus, if
C\geq 8c_{2}
, we have:

K_{k+1}^{T}(n)\leq\frac{C(k+1)}{n^{3/2}}.

(4.38)

It remains to prove the desired assertion for all
n\leq M
. Using
(
Caravenna and Pétrélis 2009
, Eq. (B.9))
:
K_{k}^{T}(n)\leq\frac{ck^{3}}{\min\{n^{3/2},T^{3}\}}
. Hence, by taking
C=\max\{cM^{2},8c_{2}\}
, we have proven the induction step. The proof of Proposition
4.23
is now complete.

4.4
Estimates of the time to hit the interfaces with a small excursion constraint

We move on to (
1.16
). It turns out that our method fails for the regime
n\geq T^{2}
because, unlike the previous case, a typical trajectory now has several large excursions. Reminding that the aim of Theorem
1.16
is to prove Proposition
2.14
, we can consider a smaller set of random walk paths that will be sufficient. Let us note that, thanks to Proposition
4.23
, there exists
C_{0}>0
such that, when
n\leq 2T^{2}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
:

\displaystyle P\left(\tau_{k}^{T}=n\text{ and }\tau_{1}^{T}\leq T^{2},...,\tau_{k}^{T}-\tau_{k-1}^{T}\leq T^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq P(\tau_{k}^{T}=n)

(4.39)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{0}}{T^{3}}\left(1+\frac{C^{\prime}}{T}\right)^{k}e^{-ng(T)}.

Let us now prove (
1.16
). We define:

Q_{k}^{T}(n):=P\left(\tau_{k}^{T}=n\text{ and }\tau_{1}\leq T^{2},...,\tau_{k}^{T}-\tau_{k-1}^{T}\leq T^{2}\right).

(4.40)

Let us take
C>C_{0}
a real number. Let
H(k)
be the induction hypothesis:

\forall n\geq 2T^{2},\qquad Q_{k}^{T}(n)\leq\frac{C}{T^{3}}\left(1+\frac{C}{T}\right)^{k}e^{-ng(T)}.

(4.41)

Let us remark that
H(1)
is true because
Q_{1}^{T}(n)=0
when
n\geq 2T^{2}
. We now prove that
H(k)
implies
H(k+1)
. Let us assume that
H(k)
is true. Let
n\geq 2T^{2}
be an integer. Then,

\displaystyle Q_{k+1}^{T}(n)

\displaystyle=\underset{l=1}{\overset{T^{2}}{\sum}}Q_{1}^{T}(l)Q_{k}^{T}(n-l).

(4.42)

In order to bound (
4.42
) from above, let us use that
g(T)\leq 10/T^{2}
, so that
e^{lg(T)}\leq 1+\frac{C^{\prime\prime}l}{T^{2}}
for
l\leq T^{2}
and a certain
C^{\prime\prime}>0
. Hence, thanks to
H(k)
:

\displaystyle\underset{l=1}{\overset{T^{2}}{\sum}}Q_{1}^{T}(l)Q_{k}^{T}(n-l)\leq\frac{Ce^{-ng(T)}}{T^{3}}\underset{l=1}{\overset{T^{2}}{\sum}}Q_{1}^{T}(l)e^{lg(T)}\left(1+\frac{C^{\prime}}{T}\right)^{k}

(4.43)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Ce^{-ng(T)}}{T^{3}}\left(1+\frac{C^{\prime}}{T}\right)^{k}\underset{l=1}{\overset{T^{2}}{\sum}}Q_{1}^{T}(l)\left(1+\frac{C^{\prime\prime}l}{T^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Ce^{-ng(T)}}{T^{3}}\left(1+\frac{C^{\prime}}{T}\right)^{k}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}^{T}\leq T^{2}\right)+\underset{l=1}{\overset{T^{2}}{\sum}}\frac{c_{2}}{l^{3/2}}\frac{C^{\prime\prime}l}{T^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Ce^{-ng(T)}}{T^{3}}\left(1+\frac{C^{\prime}}{T}\right)^{k}\left(1+\frac{C^{\prime\prime\prime}}{T}\right),

where we have used (
2.17
) in the penultimate line. Therefore, the induction step is proven when we take
C^{\prime}
bigger than
C^{\prime\prime\prime}
, which completes the proof of (
1.16
).

5
Asymptotic renewal estimates

Our goal in this section is to prove Proposition
2.14
. To this end, we first provide preliminary estimates in Section
5.1
and then prove Proposition
2.14
in three steps: we first treat the case
n\geq T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}
in Section
5.2
and then proceed with the lower and upper bounds in Sections
5.3
and
5.4
respectively.

5.1
Preliminary estimates

Let us consider the renewal process
(\tau_{n})_{n\geq 0}
with law
\mathcal{P}_{T_{N},\delta_{N}}
. It turns out that the law of
\tau_{1}
under
\mathcal{P}_{T_{N},\delta_{N}}
has two sorts of excursions: the small ones, of order
O_{N}(1)
, carry a mass
1-\delta_{N}
while the big ones, of order
O_{N}\left(T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}\right)
, carry a mass
\delta_{N}
. Although we do not fully prove this statement, it is useful to keep it in mind for the sequel. We start with an estimate of the renewal function that is easily derived from Lemma
2.1
:

Lemma 5.1
.

There exist
c_{2}>c_{1}>0
and
T_{0}\in 2\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
n\in 2\mathbb{N}
and
T_{N}\geq T_{0}
:

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}}{\min\{n^{3/2},T_{N}^{3}\}}e^{-(g(T_{N})+\phi(T_{N},\delta_{N}))n}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}=n\right)

(5.1)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{2}}{\min\{n^{3/2},T_{N}^{3}\}}e^{-(g(T_{N})+\phi(T_{N},\delta_{N}))n}.

Let us stress that this is always true, even beyond the assumptions in (
1.8
).

Proof.

Equation (
5.1
) is an immediate consequence of (
2.10
) and (
2.17
).
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume
a>b
. There exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and
c_{3}>0
such that, for
N>N_{0}
, the inequalities below are verified for any
m,n\in 2\mathbb{N}\cup\{+\infty\}
with
m<n
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}\geq m\right)\leq c_{3}\left(\frac{\mathbf{1}\{m\leq T_{N}^{2}\}}{\sqrt{m}}+\delta_{N}\right)e^{-(g(T_{N})+\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N}))m},

(5.2)

\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(m\leq\tau_{1}<n\right)\geq c_{1}\delta_{N}\left(e^{-(g(T_{N})+\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N}))m}-e^{-(g(T_{N})+\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N}))n}\right),

(5.3)

where
c_{1}
is the same constant as in (
5.1
).

Proof.

In order to prove (
5.3
), we sum over
k
the lower bound in (
5.1
). We use that
\min\left\{T_{N}^{3},k^{3/2}\right\}\leq T_{N}^{3}
and we note that, by (
2.5
) and (
2.16
):

g(T_{N})+\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}}(1+o_{N}(1)).

(5.4)

To obtain (
5.2
), we distinguish between two cases.

•

For
m\geq T_{N}^{2}
, we sum the upper bound in (
5.1
) using (
5.4
).

•

For
m\leq T_{N}^{2}
, we use that
\frac{1}{\min\{n^{3/2},T_{N}^{3}\}}\leq\frac{1}{T_{N}^{3}}+\frac{1}{n^{3/2}}
and sum the upper bound in (
5.1
).

∎

We continue with the following:

Lemma 5.3
.

When
a>b
,

\mathcal{E}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}\right)=\frac{T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}(1+o_{N}(1)),

(5.5)

\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\varepsilon_{1}^{2}=1\right)=\frac{\delta_{N}}{2}(1+o_{N}(1)),

(5.6)

\mathcal{E}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}^{2}\right)=\frac{T_{N}^{6}\delta_{N}^{3}}{2\pi^{4}}(1+o_{N}(1)).

(5.7)

The (inverse of the) expected value of
\tau_{1}
gives us the limit of
\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n\in\tau\right)
(stationary regime), by the Renewal Theorem. Equation (
5.7
) gives us access to the variance of this random variable. Those equalities are proven in Appendix
A.2
. Heuristically, Equation (
5.5
) combined with (
5.6
) tells us that, the average time to switch interface is
T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}
, which is greater than
N
under the assumption of (
1.8
). Thus, we should not see any interface change before N in that regime.

5.2
Estimates of the renewal mass function: the long range

Let us assume for the moment that Proposition
2.14
is true for
n\leq T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}
. Our aim in Section
5.2
is to extend this proposition to
2\mathbb{N}
.

5.2.1
Lower bound

We denote
\mu_{m}:=\inf\{k\geq m:k\in\tau\}
. For
n\geq T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n\in\tau\right)\geq\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau\cap\left[n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N},n-1\right]\neq\emptyset,n\in\tau\right)

(5.8)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}}^{n-1}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\mu_{n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}}=k\right)\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n-k\in\tau\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c}{T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}^{2}}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau\cap\left[n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N},n-1\right]\neq\emptyset\right).

The factor
1/T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}^{2}
comes from Proposition
2.14
admitted until
T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}
. It is now sufficient to show that there exist a constant (cst) and
T_{0}>0
such that, for
T_{N}\geq T_{0}
and
n\geq T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}
:

\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau\cap\left[n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N},n-1\right]\neq\emptyset\right)\geq\rm cst.

(5.9)

We prove the following equivalent fact:

\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau\cap\left[n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N},n-1\right]\neq\emptyset\right)\geq C\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau\cap\left[n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N},n-1\right]=\emptyset\right),

(5.10)

for an appropriate value of
C>0
. Thanks to (
5.1
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau\cap\left[n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N},n-1\right]\neq\emptyset\right)

(5.11)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}-1}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(l\in\tau\right)\sum_{k=n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}}^{n-1}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}=k-l\right)

\displaystyle\geq c\sum_{l=0}^{n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}-1}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(l\in\tau\right)\delta_{N}\Bigg(e^{-(\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})+g(T_{N}))\left(n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}-l\right)}

\displaystyle-e^{-(\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})+g(T_{N}))(n-l)}\Bigg).

Thanks to (
5.2
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau\cap\left[n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N},n-1\right]=\emptyset\right)

(5.12)

\displaystyle=\underset{l=0}{\overset{n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}-1}{\sum}}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(l\in\tau\right)\underset{k\geq n}{\overset{}{\sum}}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}=k-l\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\underset{l=0}{\overset{n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}-1}{\sum}}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(l\in\tau\right)e^{-(\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})+g(T_{N}))(n-l)}\delta_{N}.

Thus, by (
5.4
):

\displaystyle\frac{\left(e^{-(\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})+g(T_{N}))\left(n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}-l\right)}-e^{-(\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})+g(T_{N}))(n-l)}\right)\delta_{N}}{\left(e^{-(\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})+g(T_{N}))(n-l)}\right)\delta_{N}}

(5.13)

\displaystyle\geq e^{T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}(\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})+g(T_{N}))}-1\sim_{N}e^{2\pi^{2}}-1,

thus this quantity is (eventually) bounded from below by a positive real number. We finally get the desired result.

5.2.2
Upper bound

It remains to prove the upper bound of Proposition
2.14
for
n\geq T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}
. It comes:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau\cap\left[n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N},n-T_{N}^{2}\right]\neq\emptyset,n\in\tau\right)

(5.14)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}}^{n-T_{N}^{2}}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\mu_{n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}}=k\right)\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n-k\in\tau\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{\delta_{N}^{2}T_{N}^{3}}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau\cap\left[n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N},n-T_{N}^{2}\right]\neq\emptyset\right)\leq\frac{c}{\delta_{N}^{2}T_{N}^{3}},

by applying the upper bound of Proposition
2.14
to
\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n-k\in\tau\right)
, because
T_{N}^{2}\leq n-k\leq T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}
. If we now show that there is a constant (cst) and
N_{0}>0
such that, for
N\geq N_{0}
and for
n\geq T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}
,

\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau\cap\left[n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N},n-T_{N}^{2}\right]\neq\emptyset\mid n\in\tau\right)\geq\rm cst,

(5.15)

we deduce that

\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n\in\tau\right)\leq\frac{1}{{\rm cst}}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau\cap\left[n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N},n-T_{N}^{2}\right]\neq\emptyset,n\in\tau\right)\leq\frac{{\rm cst}}{T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}^{2}},

(5.16)

and the proof is over. Instead of (
5.15
), we prove the following equivalent relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau\cap\left[n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N},n-T_{N}^{2}\right]\neq\emptyset,n\in\tau\right)

(5.17)

\displaystyle\geq C\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau\cap\left[n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N},n-T_{N}^{2}\right]=\emptyset,n\in\tau\right),

for some
C>0
. Let us start with the first term:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau\cap\left[n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N},n-T_{N}^{2}\right]\neq\emptyset,n\in\tau\right)

(5.18)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}-1}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(m\in\tau\right)\sum_{l=n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}}^{n-T_{N}^{2}}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}=l-m\right)\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n-l\in\tau\right).

Let us notice that
\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n-l\in\tau\right)\geq c/T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}^{2}
for
n-l\in 2\mathbb{N}
thanks to the lower bound of Proposition
2.14
. By (
5.3
) and (
5.4
),

\displaystyle\sum_{l=n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}}^{n-T_{N}^{2}}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}=l-m\right)

(5.19)

\displaystyle\geq c\delta_{N}\left(e^{-(\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})+g(T_{N}))\left(n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}-m\right)}-e^{-(\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})+g(T_{N}))\left(n-T_{N}^{2}-m\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=c\delta_{N}e^{-(\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})+g(T_{N}))\left(n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}-m\right)}\left(1-e^{-(\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})+g(T_{N}))\left(T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}-T_{N}^{2}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq c\delta_{N}e^{-(\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})+g(T_{N}))\left(n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}-m\right)}\geq c\delta_{N}e^{-(\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})+g(T_{N}))(n-m)}.

Returning to (
5.18
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau\cap\left[n-T_{N}^{3},n-T_{N}^{2}\right]\neq\emptyset,n\in\tau\right)\geq

(5.20)

\displaystyle\frac{c}{T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}}\sum_{m=0}^{n-T_{N}^{3}-1}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(m\in\tau\right)e^{-(\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})+g(T_{N}))(n-m)}.

Now, let us focus on the second term of (
5.17
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau\cap\left[n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N},n-T_{N}^{2}\right]=\emptyset,n\in\tau\right)

(5.21)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}-1}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(m\in\tau\right)\sum_{l=n-T_{N}^{2}}^{n}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}=l-m\right)\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n-l\in\tau\right).

Since
l-m\geq T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}-T_{N}^{2}
, thanks to the upper bound in (
5.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}=l-m\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{T_{N}^{3}}e^{-(\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})+g(T_{N}))(l-m)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{T_{N}^{3}}e^{-(\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})+g(T_{N}))(n-m)},

(5.22)

because
n-l\leq T_{N}^{2}
(let us remember (
5.4
) and
T_{N}\delta_{N}\to\infty
). Moreover, thanks to the upper bound of Proposition
2.14
applied to
\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n-l\in\tau\right)
, for
n-l\leq T_{N}^{2}
, we obtain

\sum_{l=n-T_{N}^{2}}^{n}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n-l\in\tau\right)\leq c\underset{l=1}{\overset{1/\delta_{N}^{2}}{\sum}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{l}}+\frac{c}{\delta_{N}^{2}}\underset{l=1/\delta_{N}^{2}}{\overset{T_{N}^{2}}{\sum}}\frac{1}{l^{3/2}}\leq\frac{c}{\delta_{N}}.

(5.23)

Returning to (
5.21
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau\cap\left[n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N},n-T_{N}^{2}\right]=\emptyset,n\in\tau\right)

(5.24)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}}\sum_{m=0}^{n-T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}-1}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(m\in\tau\right)e^{-(\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})+g(T_{N}))(n-m)}.

By comparing (
5.20
) and (
5.24
), we see that (
5.17
) is proven, which concludes the proof.

5.3
Lower bound on the renewal mass function: the short range

In this section, we deal with the case
n\leq T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}
. We allow ourselves to neglect the exponential terms among equations in Lemma
5.3
. Here is the main idea of the proof: we consider only those trajectories where only one excursion is larger than
cn
, with
c>0
to be defined below, and where all other excursions are smaller than
cn
. We will see that such trajectories are indeed typical. Let us start with some estimates:

Lemma 5.4
.

There exist
c_{5}
and
c_{6}
such that, for all
1\leq n\leq T_{N}^{2}
:

\mathcal{E}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}\mathbf{1}\{\tau_{1}\leq n\}\right)\leq c_{5}\sqrt{n},

(5.25)

\mathcal{E}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}|\tau_{1}\leq n\right)\leq c_{6}\sqrt{n}.

(5.26)

Proof.

We use (
5.1
) and standard computations for (
5.25
). To prove (
5.26
), we use that
\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}\leq n\right)\geq\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}=2\right)\geq c
.
∎

Let us now turn to the proof of Proposition
2.14
for
n\leq T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}
. Rather than computing
\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n\in\tau\right)
, we compute
\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left((2\frac{c_{6}}{c_{1}}+1)n\in\tau\right)
, with
c_{1}
appearing in (
5.3
) and
c_{6}
in (
5.26
). Details of this choice, which helps us simplify computations, appear in Equation (
5.36
). Omitting the integer part, we then introduce

n^{\prime}=\Big(2\frac{c_{6}}{c_{1}}+1\Big)n.

(5.27)

We define
\xi_{i}:=\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1}
, and
s_{n}:=\min\{n,1/\delta_{N}^{2}\}
. An excursion is said "small" if it is smaller than
s_{n}
. For
k
in
\mathbb{N}
, we define the following event:

A_{k}(n):=\{\xi_{1}+...+\xi_{k}=n^{\prime}\text{ and }\#\{i\leq k\colon\xi_{i}\leq s_{n}\}=k-1\},

(5.28)

which represents the event of reaching
n^{\prime}
in
k
excursions, when only one of them is larger than
s_{n}
. Thus,

\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n^{\prime}\in\tau\right)\geq\underset{k=1}{\overset{\sqrt{s_{n}}/c_{1}}{\sum}}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(A_{k}(n)\right)

(5.29)

since we only consider a subset of all trajectories reaching
n^{\prime}
. We also define for
k
in
\mathbb{N}
:

B_{k}(n):=\left\{\xi_{1}+...+\xi_{k}\leq 2\frac{c_{6}}{c_{1}}n\right\}\cap\left\{\forall i\leq k,\xi_{i}\leq s_{n}\right\},

(5.30)

which represents the fact that small excursions do not exceed
2\frac{c_{6}}{c_{1}}n
and the
k
first excursions are smaller than
n
, and

C_{k}(n):=\{\forall i\leq k,\,\xi_{i}\leq s_{n}\},

(5.31)

which represents the probability of having
k
small excursions. We now give an estimate of
\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(A_{k}(n)\right)
:

Lemma 5.5
.

For
k\leq\frac{\sqrt{s_{n}}}{c_{1}}
and
n\leq T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}
,

\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(A_{k}(n)\right)\geq\frac{{\rm cst}k}{\min\{n^{3/2},T_{N}^{3}\}}\left(1-\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{k-1}.

(5.32)

Proof of Lemma
5.32
.

We first decompose the event on the location of the big excursion and use (
5.1
). Remind that we may neglect all exponential terms in these equations:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(A_{k}(n)\right)=\underset{j=1}{\overset{k}{\sum}}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1},...,l_{k}\in\mathbb{N}\\
l_{1}+...+l_{k}=n^{\prime}\\
l_{j}\geq s_{n}\\
l_{i}\leq s_{n}\text{ for }i\neq j\end{subarray}}{\overset{}{\sum}}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left((\xi_{1},...,\xi_{k})=(l_{1},...,l_{k})\right)

(5.33)

\displaystyle=k\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq l_{1},...,l_{k-1}\leq s_{n}\\
l_{1}+...+l_{k-1}\leq\left(2\frac{c_{6}}{c_{1}}\right)n\\
\end{subarray}}{\overset{}{\sum}}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left((\xi_{1},...,\xi_{k-1})=(l_{1},...,l_{k-1})\right)\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\xi_{k}=n^{\prime}-\underset{i=1}{\overset{k-1}{\sum}}l_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{kc_{1}}{\min\{n^{3/2},T_{N}^{3}\}}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq l_{1},...,l_{k-1}\leq s_{n}\\
l_{1}+...+l_{k-1}\leq\left(2\frac{c_{6}}{c_{1}}\right)n\\
\end{subarray}}{\overset{}{\sum}}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left((\xi_{1},...,\xi_{k-1})=(l_{1},...,l_{k-1})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{kc_{1}}{\min\{n^{3/2},T_{N}^{3}\}}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(B_{k-1}(n)\right).

We now are looking for a lower bound of
\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(B_{k-1}(n)\right)
. This can be done by conditioning on the fact that all excursions are smaller than
s_{n}
, which corresponds to the event
C_{k-1}(n)
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(B_{k-1}(n)\right)

(5.34)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(B_{k-1}(n)|C_{k-1}(n)\right)\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(C_{k-1}(n)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\xi_{1}+...+\xi_{k-1}\leq 2\frac{c_{6}}{c_{1}}n\Big|\forall i<k,\,\xi_{i}\leq s_{n}\right)\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}\leq s_{n}\right)^{k-1}

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\xi_{1}+...+\xi_{k-1}\leq 2kc_{6}\sqrt{n}\Big|\forall i<k,\,\xi_{i}\leq s_{n}\right)\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}\leq s_{n}\right)^{k-1}.

where we used that
k\leq\frac{\sqrt{s_{n}}}{c_{1}}
. To make the following equation more readable, we note
X
a random variable that is distributed as
\xi_{1}+...+\xi_{k-1}
conditioned on
\xi_{i}\leq s_{n}
for all
i<k
. Lemma
5.26
therefore gives us:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(B_{k-1}(n)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(X\leq 2E(X)\right)\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}\leq s_{n}\right)^{k-1}

(5.35)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{s_{n}}}\right)^{k-1},

where we used the Markov inequality and (
5.3
) in the last line. Put together, (
5.33
) and (
5.35
) yield Lemma
5.32
.
∎

We can now conclude the proof. By Lemma
5.32
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n\in\tau\right)\geq\underset{k=1}{\overset{\sqrt{s_{n}}/c_{1}}{\sum}}\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(A_{k}(n)\right)\geq\frac{c}{\min\{n^{3/2},T_{N}^{3}\}}\underset{k=1}{\overset{\sqrt{s_{n}}/c_{1}}{\sum}}k\left(1-\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{s_{n}}}\right)^{k-1}

(5.36)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c}{2\min\{n^{3/2},T_{N}^{3}\}}\left(-\frac{\sqrt{s_{n}}}{c_{1}}\frac{(1-\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{s_{n}}})^{\sqrt{s_{n}}/c_{1}}}{\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{s_{n}}}}+\frac{1-(1-\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{s_{n}}})^{\frac{\sqrt{s_{n}}}{c_{1}}}}{\left(\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{s_{n}}}\right)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c(1-2e^{-1})}{\min\{n^{3/2}\max\{\frac{1}{n},\delta_{N}^{2}\},T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}^{2}\}},

hence the desired result.

5.4
Upper bound on the renewal mass function: the short range

This section is the most technical one. In Section
5.4.1
, we expose the proof for
n\leq
, by comparing our renewal measure to the simple random walk. Then, for
1/\delta_{N}^{2}\leq n\leq\delta_{N}T_{N}^{3}
, the proof becomes more involved as we need the sharp simple random walk estimates from Theorem
1.16
.

5.4.1
Upper bound for the very short range

We now work with
n\leq 1/\delta_{N}^{2}
. Let us start by a lemma about the simple random walk:

Lemma 5.6
.

There exists
C>0
such that, for all
T\in 2\mathbb{N}
and
n\in 2\mathbb{N}
:

\frac{1}{C\min\{\sqrt{n},T\}}\leq P(S_{n}\in T\mathbb{Z})\leq\frac{C}{\min\{\sqrt{n},T\}}.

(5.37)

We defer the proof to Appendix
B
. We now prove the upper bound of Proposition
2.14
for
n\leq 1/\delta_{N}^{2}
by using the definition of
\mathcal{P}_{T_{N},\delta_{N}}
in (
2.10
). Recall the definition of
\tau_{k}^{T_{N}}
in (
1.5
). By (
2.17
) and Lemma
5.37
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n\in\tau\right)

\displaystyle=\underset{k=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}P\left(\tau_{k}^{T_{N}}=n\right)e^{-k\delta_{N}}e^{-n\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})}

\displaystyle\leq c\underset{k=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}P\left(\tau_{k}^{T_{N}}=n\right)

(5.38)

\displaystyle\leq cP\left(n\in\tau^{T_{N}}\right)\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{\sqrt{n}}.

We get that
e^{-n\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})}\leq c
thanks to (
2.5
) and noting that
n\leq 1/{\delta_{N}^{2}}\leq T_{N}^{2}
. Thus, in this regime, our renewal measure behaves as in the simple random walk case.

5.4.2
Upper bound for the intermediate range

We are now looking to prove Proposition
5.39
:

Proposition 5.1
.

There exists
C>0
such that, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
1/\delta_{N}^{2}\leq n\leq T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}
:

\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n\in\tau\right)\leq\frac{C}{\delta_{N}^{2}\min\{n^{3/2},T_{N}^{3}\}}.

(5.39)

We distinguish between two cases :

(i) Let us consider
1/\delta_{N}^{2}\leq n\leq 2T_{N}^{2}
. Because
n
is smaller than
2T_{N}^{2}
and by (
2.5
), it comes that
e^{-n\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})}\leq e^{c}
. Remind (
2.8
):

\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n\in\tau\right)\leq e^{c}\underset{k=0}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}P(\tau_{k}^{T_{N}}=n)e^{-k\delta_{N}},

(5.40)

dawith
\tau_{k}^{T_{N}}
defined in (
1.5
). By applying the inequality in Proposition
4.23
, we can bound from above the latter quantity by
\frac{C}{n^{3/2}\delta_{N}^{2}}
, which is the result we are looking for.

(ii) Let us consider
2T_{N}^{2}\leq n\leq T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}
. We are first going to bound from above the contribution coming from a certain type of trajectories, namely the ones with only "small" excursions, i.e. excursions smaller than
T_{N}^{2}
. Remind that
L_{n}=\inf\{l\in\mathbb{N}:\tau_{l}\geq n\}
is the number of excursions needed to exceed
n
. We use the notations introduced in (
4.40
). We write
\xi_{i}:=\tau_{i}^{T_{N}}-\tau_{i-1}^{T_{N}}
. Thus, it comes:

\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n\in\tau,\,\,\xi_{i}\leq T_{N}^{2}\,\,\forall i\leq L_{n}\right)=\underset{k=0}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}Q_{k}^{T_{N}}(n)e^{-k\delta_{N}}e^{-n\phi(\delta_{N},T_{N})}.

(5.41)

We use the upper bound in (
1.16
), that is valid for
n\geq 2T_{N}^{2}
, and the fact that
e^{-n(\phi+g(T_{N}))}\leq 1
to write:

\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(n\in\tau,\,\,\xi_{i}\leq T_{N}^{2}\,\,\forall i\leq L_{n}\right)\leq\underset{k=0}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}\frac{C}{T_{N}^{3}}\left(1+\frac{C^{\prime}}{T}\right)^{k}e^{-k\delta_{N}}\leq\frac{C}{T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}},

(5.42)

because
\delta_{N}\gg_{N}1/T_{N}
. Now, we have to prove the equation in Proposition
5.39
for
n\in[2T_{N}^{2},T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}]
, and for the trajectories with at least an excursion bigger than
T_{N}^{2}
. We are going to decompose the sum with the number of excursions needed to reach
n
, and the number of excursions bigger than
T_{N}^{2}
among those excursions:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\{n\in\tau\}\cap\{\exists i\leq L_{n}:\tau_{i}-\tau_{i-1}\geq T_{N}^{2}\}\right)

(5.43)

\displaystyle=\underset{k=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}e^{-k\delta_{N}}e^{n\phi}\underset{j=1}{\overset{k}{\sum}}P(\tau_{k}^{T_{N}}=n,\#\{l\leq k:\xi_{l}\geq T_{N}^{2}\}=j\})

\displaystyle\leq\underset{k=1}{\overset{\infty}{\sum}}e^{-k\delta_{N}}e^{n\phi}\underset{j=1}{\overset{k}{\sum}}\binom{k}{j}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{N}^{2}\leq l_{2},...,l_{j}\leq n\\
l_{2}+...+l_{j}\leq n-T_{N}^{2}\end{subarray}}{\overset{}{\sum}}P(\xi_{2}=l_{2})...P(\xi_{j}=l_{j})

\displaystyle\times\underset{l=1}{\overset{n-l_{2}-...-l_{j}-T_{N}^{2}}{\sum}}Q_{k-j}^{T_{N}}(l)P(\tau_{1}^{T_{N}}=n-l-l_{2}-...-l_{j}).

Here is a lemma to bound from above this sum:

Lemma 5.7

By combining the preceding relations:

\mathcal{E}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}\right)=\frac{T_{N}^{3}\delta_{N}^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}(1+o_{N}(1))\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{E}_{\delta_{N},T_{N}}\left(\tau_{1}^{2}\right)=\frac{T_{N}^{6}\delta_{N}^{3}}{2\pi^{4}}(1+o_{N}(1)),

Appendix B
Simple random walk estimate on the probability to visit an interface

In this section we prove Lemma
5.37
. We prove the result with
2n
instead of
n
to simplify notation. If
2n\leq T
then (
5.37
) follows easily from
P(2n\in\tau^{T})=\binom{2n}{n}\frac{1}{2^{2n}}\sim\frac{c}{\sqrt{n}}
. Else, for
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
:

\displaystyle P(S_{2n}=2kT)

\displaystyle=\binom{2n}{n+kT}\frac{1}{2^{2n}}\asymp_{n}\frac{n^{2n}}{\sqrt{n}(n+kT)^{n+kT}(n-kT)^{n-kT}}

(B.1)

\displaystyle\asymp_{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\exp\left(-(n-kT)\ln(1-kT/n)-(n+kT)\ln(1+kT/n)\right).

Moreover,
(1-x)\ln(1-x)+(1+x)\ln(1+x)\sim_{0}x^{2}
. Therefore, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that, when
\left|\frac{kT}{n}\right|\leq\varepsilon
,

\exp\left((n-kT)\ln(1-kT/n)+(n+kT)\ln(1+kT/n)\right)\asymp_{n}\exp\left(-\frac{(kT)^{2}}{n}\right).

(B.2)

Now, we need to bound
P(S_{n}\geq\varepsilon n)
from above.

Lemma B.1
.

When
\varepsilon>0
, one has:

P(S_{n}\geq\varepsilon n)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{n}{2}(\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{3}))\right).

(B.3)

Proof.

The exponential Markov inequality with
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(1+\frac{2\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\right)
combined with a second-order expansion of
\log
yields:

\displaystyle P(S_{n}\geq\varepsilon n)\leq\frac{E\left(e^{\lambda S_{n}}\right)}{e^{\lambda\varepsilon n}}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(n\left((1-\varepsilon)\lambda+\log\left(\frac{1+e^{-2\lambda}}{2}\right)\right)\right)

(B.4)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{n}{2}\left(\varepsilon^{2}+O\left(\varepsilon^{3}\right)\right)\right).

∎

Hence, when
\varepsilon
is small enough:

P(|S_{n}|\geq\varepsilon n)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{n\varepsilon^{2}}{3}\right)\ll\frac{1}{\min\{T,\sqrt{n}\}}.

(B.5)

Now, let us compute
\underset{k=0}{\overset{\varepsilon n/T}{\sum}}\exp\left(-\frac{(kT)^{2}}{n}\right)
. By standard techniques:

\displaystyle\underset{k=0}{\overset{\varepsilon n/T}{\sum}}\exp\left(-\frac{(kT)^{2}}{n}\right)\asymp_{n}1+\int_{0}^{\frac{\varepsilon n}{T}}\exp\left(-\frac{(Tt)^{2}}{n}\right)dt

\displaystyle\asymp_{n}1+\frac{\sqrt{n}}{T}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{n}\varepsilon}e^{-t^{2}}dt

(B.6)

\displaystyle\asymp_{n}1+\frac{\sqrt{n}}{T}\asymp_{n}\frac{\max\{T,\sqrt{n}\}}{T}.

Therefore, when
\varepsilon
is small enough, thanks to (
B.1
), (
B.5
) and (
B.6
):

\displaystyle P(S_{2n}\in T\mathbb{Z})\asymp_{n}\underset{k=-\frac{\varepsilon n}{T}}{\overset{\frac{\varepsilon n}{T}}{\sum}}P(S_{2n}=kT)+P(|S_{2n}|\geq\varepsilon n)

\displaystyle\asymp_{n}\frac{\max\{T,\sqrt{n}\}}{T\sqrt{n}}

(B.7)

\displaystyle\asymp_{n}\frac{1}{\min\{\sqrt{n},T\}}.

Hence, Lemma
5.37
is proven.

Simple random walk tail estimates for the time to hit the origin

Let us prove an equivalent result, that is

Appendix D
Estimate of the partition function

Recall the definition of
L_{N}
in Definition

. By the Markov property, together with (

\displaystyle Z_{N,\delta_{N}}^{T_{N}}=E\left[e^{-H_{N,\delta}^{T_{N}}(S)}\right]=\sum_{r=0}^{N}E\left[e^{-H_{N,\delta}^{T_{N}}(S)}\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\tau_{L_{N}}^{T_{N}}=r\right\}}\right]

We hereafter distinguish between several cases, and abbreviate

1.

6.7

2.

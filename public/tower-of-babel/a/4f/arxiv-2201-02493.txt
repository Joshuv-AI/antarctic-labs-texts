Solving formally the Auxiliary System of O ⁡ ( N ) O(N) Non Linear Sigma Model Preprint: test1234
Dimitrios Katsinis

Abstract
We show that the integrability of the S ​ O ​ ( N ) / S ​ O ​ ( N − 1 ) SO(N)/SO(N-1) Principal Chiral Model (PCM) originates from the Pohlmeyer reduction of the O ⁡ ( N ) O(N) Non Linear Sigma Model (NLSM). In particular, we show that the Lax pair of the PCM is related upon redefinitions and identification of parameters to the zero curvature condition, which is a consequence of the flatness of the enhanced space used in the Pohlmeyer reduction. This identification provides the solution of the auxiliary system that corresponds to an arbitrary NLSM/PCM solution.



I
Introduction

The classical integrability of the PCM is guaranteed by the formulation of its equations of motion and the zero curvature condition, which is satisfied automatically by the current, as a Lax pair
Zakharov and Mikhailov 1978
. For a recent review see
Zarembo
. Both equations can be derived as the compatibility condition of the auxiliary system:

\partial_{\pm}\Psi(\lambda)=\mathcal{L}_{\pm}\Psi(\lambda),\qquad\mathcal{L}_{\pm}=\frac{\left(\partial_{\pm}g\right)g^{-1}}{1\pm\lambda},

(1)

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
is the spectral parameter. In the literature, this system also goes by the name fundamental linear problem. In our previous work
Katsinis et al.
we solved these equations for
g\in SO(3)/SO(2)
. This was achieved in a brute force manner, which was inspired by the application of the dressing method on elliptic strings in
\mathbb{R}\times\textrm{S}^{2}

Katsinis et al. 2018
, as well as on the elliptic minimal surfaces in
\textrm{H}^{3}

Katsinis et al. 2020
. We introduced spherical coordinates and bootstrapped the solution of the auxiliary system using all the available equations. In this work, we will solve these equations for an arbitrary
g\in SO(N)/SO(N-1)
in a more elegant way.

A key element of our derivation will be the so-called Pohlmeyer reduction of the
O(N)
NLSM
Pohlmeyer 1976
;
Lund and Regge 1976
. In a nutshell, considering the target space
\textrm{S}^{N-1}
embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the embedding equations of the NLSM solution in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
are multicomponent generalizations of the sine-Gordon equations, known as Symmetric Space Sine Gordon models, for a recent review see
Miramontes 2008
. This process is the Pohlmeyer reduction. It is important to point out that the Pohlmeyer reduction is a many-to-one mapping. In particular, there is an infinite family of NLSM solutions, which correspond to the same Pohlmeyer counterpart. Finally, it worth noticing that for a given solution of the Pohlmeyer reduced theory, the equations of motion of the NLSM become linear.

The solution of the auxiliary system (
1
) is of great interest since it is related to the monodromy matrix, which provides an infinite tower of conserved charges. In particular, the monodromy matrix
T(\sigma_{f},\sigma_{i};\lambda)
is defined as

T(\sigma_{f},\sigma_{i};\lambda)=\Psi(\tau,\sigma_{f};\lambda)\Psi^{-1}(\tau,\sigma_{i};\lambda).

(2)

It is straightforward to show that its time derivative reads

\partial_{\tau}T(\sigma_{f},\sigma_{i};\lambda)=\mathcal{L}_{\tau}(\tau,\sigma_{f})T(\sigma_{f},\sigma_{i};\lambda)\\
-T(\sigma_{f},\sigma_{i};\lambda)\mathcal{L}_{\tau}(\tau,\sigma_{i}).

(3)

The Lax connection
\mathcal{L}_{\tau}
appears in the auxiliary system when it is expressed in terms of the world-sheet coordinates
\sigma
and
\tau
. In particular, the relevant equation reads
\partial_{\tau}\Psi(\tau,\sigma;\lambda)=\mathcal{L}_{\tau}\Psi(\tau,\sigma;\lambda)
. The values of
\sigma_{i}
and
\sigma_{f}
are related to the specific solution and its boundary conditions. The latter also specify the explicit form of the conserved quantity. Let us make this statement clear. If
\mathcal{L}_{\tau}
vanished both at
\sigma_{i}
and
\sigma_{f}
, which for instance may happen at
\pm\infty
, then the monodromy matrix is conserved. On the contrary, when imposing periodic boundary conditions, it is its trace that is conserved. Finally, let us also mention that in the case of open - string boundary conditions one can employ the formalism of Cherdnik and Sklyanin by defining a boundary monodromy matrix
Cherednik 1984
;
Sklyanin 1988
.

There is another use for the solution of the axiliary system. It is the necessary input for the application of the dressing method
Zakharov and Mikhailov 1978
;
Zakharov and Mikhailov 1980
;
Harnad et al. 1984
. The latter is a technique which enables the construction of new solutions of the NLSM, once a solution is already known. The known solution is referred to as the seed solution. The Pohlmeyer reduced theory has a counterpart of the dressing transformations, known as the Bäcklund transformations. Interestingly enough, a dressing transformation on the NLSM solution, automatically performs a Bäcklund transformation at the avatar of the solution in the Pohlmeyer reduced theory
Hollowood and Miramontes 2009
.

In
Katsinis et al.
we claimed that the systematic solution of the auxiliary system corresponding to the
O(3)
NLSM, points to a non-linear superposition rule for NLSMs on symmetric spaces. In order to obtain a new NLSM solution one can combine a NLSM solution with a “virtual” one, i.e. one that obeys inadmissible Virasoro constraints when the model is embedded in string theory, both corresponding to the same Pohlmeyer counterpart. We verify this statement in the case of
O(N)
NLSM.

II
The Pohlmeyer Reduction of the
O(N)
NLSM

In this section we review the Pohlmeyer reduction of the
O(N)
NLSM. The main reason for doing so is that we will use slightly unusual conventions, which will facilitate the rest of the paper. The equations of motion of the NLSM read

\partial_{+}\partial_{-}\vec{X}+\left(\partial_{+}\vec{X}\cdot\partial_{-}\vec{X}\right)\vec{X}=0,

(4)

where we used the coordinates
\xi^{\pm}=\xi^{1}\pm\xi^{0}
. They are accompanied by the geometric constraint

|\vec{X}|^{2}=1,

(5)

as well as by the conservation of the stress-energy tensor
T
, which in appropriate coordinates implies

T_{\pm\pm}=|\partial_{\pm}\vec{X}|^{2}=m^{2}_{\pm}.

(6)

We introduce a basis
\mathcal{V}=\{\vec{v}_{1},\dots,\vec{v}_{N}\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where
\vec{v}_{N-2}=\partial_{-}\vec{X}
,
\vec{v}_{N-1}=\partial_{+}\vec{X}
and
\vec{v}_{N}=\vec{X}

\partial_{+}\vec{X}\cdot\partial_{-}\vec{X}=m_{+}m_{-}\cos\varphi.

(8)

Let us expand the derivatives of the vectors, which form the basis, on the basis itself as

\partial_{\pm}\vec{v}_{\alpha}=\left(A_{\pm}\right)_{\alpha\beta}\vec{v}_{\beta},

(9)

or in matrix form as

\partial_{\pm}V=A_{\pm}V,\qquad V_{ij}=\left(\vec{v}_{i}\right)_{j}.

(10)

By definition
\partial_{+}\vec{v}_{N}=\vec{v}_{N-1}
and
\partial_{-}\vec{v}_{N}=\vec{v}_{N-2}
, implying that the elements
\left(A_{+}\right)_{N\alpha}=\delta_{\alpha(N-1)}
and
\left(A_{-}\right)_{N\alpha}=\delta_{\alpha(N-2)}

\left(A_{+}\right)_{(N-2)\alpha}=-m_{+}m_{-}\cos\varphi\delta_{\alpha N}
and similarly
\left(A_{-}\right)_{(N-1)\alpha}=-m_{+}m_{-}\cos\varphi\delta_{\alpha N}
. Taking into account the following inner products

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{\pm}^{2}\vec{X}\right)\cdot\vec{X}=-m^{2}_{\pm},

(11)

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{\pm}^{2}\vec{X}\right)\cdot\partial_{\pm}\vec{X}=0,

(12)

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{\pm}^{2}\vec{X}\right)\cdot\partial_{\mp}\vec{X}=-m_{+}m_{-}\sin\varphi\,\partial_{\pm}\varphi,

(13)

we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{\pm}^{2}\vec{X}=-m^{2}_{\pm}\vec{X}+\cot\varphi\,\partial_{\pm}\varphi\,\partial_{\pm}\vec{X}

\displaystyle-\frac{m_{\pm}}{m_{\mp}}\frac{\partial_{\pm}\varphi}{\sin\varphi}\partial_{\mp}\vec{X}

\displaystyle+m_{\pm}a^{\pm}_{i}\vec{v}_{i},

(14)

where
a^{\pm}_{i}
are additional Pohlmeyer fields, which are grouped as two
(N-3)\times 1
column matrices
{\bf a}_{\pm}

\vec{v}_{j}\cdot\partial_{\pm}\vec{v}_{i}=\left(\mathcal{A}_{\pm}\right)_{ij},

(15)

where
\mathcal{A}_{\pm}
are
(N-3)\times(N-3)
antisymmetric matrices. Thus, the matrices
A^{\pm}
are given by:

A_{+}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{A}_{+}&\frac{\cos\varphi\,{\bf a}_{+}}{m_{-}\sin^{2}\phi}&-\frac{{\bf a}_{+}}{m_{+}\sin^{2}\phi}&{\bf 0}\\
{\bf 0}^{T}&0&0&{\displaystyle-m_{+}m_{-}\cos\varphi}\\

(16)

and

The Auxiliary System

The solution of the NLSM, henceforth denoted as the column

\bar{g}=g,\qquad g\theta g\theta=I,\qquad g^{T}=g^{-1},

IV
Discussion

In this work we obtained the formal solution of the auxiliary system corresponding to
O(N)
NLSM. As long as the whole family of NLSM solutions, which correspond to a given solution of the Pohlmeyer reduced theory, is known, the solution of the auxiliary system can be constructed systematically.

There are various implications of this construction. To begin with, one can calculate the values of the conserved charges directly, by expanding the monodromy matrix. Also, in view of our result, the dressing method is the implementation of a non-linear superposition of solutions with “virtual” ones. There are no differential equations to be solved. The same is also true for the Bäcklund transformation of the Pohlmeyer reduced theory. Essentially, the non-linear superposition is the NLSM counterpart of the insertion of solitons in the Pohlmeyer reduced theory. In this spirit, our results, combined with the addition formula for the on-shell action derived in
Katsinis et al. 2020
enable the calculation of instanton contibutions over any classical configuration of the
O(N)
NLSMs. It would be compelling to use this formalism in order to make contact with studies on the possible path integration contours for such models
Krichever and Nekrasov 2021
or to discuss semi-classical quantization.

Regarding the monodromy matrix, the form of the solution of the auxiliary system (
46
) implies that in the case of periodic boundary conditions its eigenvalues coincide with the eigenvalues of the matrix

\mathcal{T}=V(\tau,\sigma_{f})V^{-1}(\tau,\sigma_{i})|_{m_{\pm}\rightarrow\frac{1\mp\lambda}{1\pm\lambda}m_{\pm}}.

(53)

It is very important to point out that the NLSM solution and correspondingly the coset element
g
and the matrix
V
satisfy periodic boundary conditions for specific
m_{\pm}
. The rescaling of these parameters spoils the periodic boundary conditions (at least for arbitrary
\lambda
). This is the mechanism behind the non-trivial monodromy matrix. One should perceive the notation of (
53
) as rescaling
m_{\pm}
first and then substituting the value of
\sigma
. More details and a worked-out example can be found in the follow up publication
Katsinis 2022b
.

Of course, there are intriguing generalizations of these results. For instance, on could study other symmetric spaces or introduce supersymmetry. Besides the mathematical curiosity, such generalizations are also interesting for practical reasons. In the context of AdS/CFT correspondence
Maldacena 1998
;
Gubser et al. 1998
;
Witten 1998
it is known that classical free IIB superstring theory on
\textrm{AdS}_{5}\times\textrm{S}^{5}
and the operators of non-perturbative planar
\mathcal{N}=4
Super-Yang- Mills share a spectral curve
Beisert et al. 2006a
. Generalizing the presented construction for the Metsaev-Tseytlin action
Metsaev and Tseytlin 1998
, it could be the case that string configuration can be related to specific dual operators. Of course this would require the analogous construction on the field theory side. Nevertheless, the presented construction concerns the
SO(6)
sector in the vector representation, whose spectral curve was presented in
Beisert et al. 2006b
.

Finally, this construction may be relevant for the study of the stability of classical strings propagating on spheres in the spirit of
Katsinis et al. 2019a
;
Katsinis et al. 2019b
. Classical strings are unstable whenever superluminal solitons can propagate on the background of the Pohlmeyer counterpart of these configurations. As the boundary conditions are crucial for such studies, it is unsure how feasible it is to study the stability of arbitrary string configurations, yet one could study specific string solutions if available.

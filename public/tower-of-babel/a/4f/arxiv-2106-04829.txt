Temporal Averaging LSTM-based Channel Estimation Scheme for IEEE 802.11p Standard


Abstract
In vehicular communications, reliable channel estimation is critical for the system performance due to the doubly-dispersive nature of vehicular channels. IEEE 802.11p standard allocates insufficient pilots for accurate channel tracking. Consequently, conventional IEEE 802.11p estimators suffer from a considerable performance degradation, especially in high mobility scenarios. Recently, DL ( DL ) techniques have been employed for IEEE 802.11p channel estimation. Nevertheless, these methods suffer either from performance degradation in very high mobility scenarios or from large computational complexity. In this paper, these limitations are solved using a LSTM ( LSTM )-based estimation. The proposed estimator employs an LSTM unit to estimate the channel, followed by TA ( TA ) processing as a noise alleviation technique. Moreover, the noise mitigation ratio is determined analytically, thus validating the TA processing ability in improving the overall performance. Simulation results reveal the performance superiority of the proposed schemes compared to the recently proposed DL -based estimators, while recording a significant reduction in the computational complexity.

Introduction

Vehicular communication technologies
[
1
]
describe a set of communication models that can be employed by vehicles in different application contexts, resulting in a well organized network infrastructure. The main motivation behind such technologies is to facilitate several future smart city applications including road safety and autonomous driving.

In general, wireless communications in vehicular environment encounter a critical reliability challenge due to the doubly selective nature of the vehicular channel that varies rapidly especially in high mobility scenarios. Moreover, a precisely-estimated channel is critical for the equalization, demodulation, and decoding operations performed at the receiver. Therefore, robust and accurate channel estimation plays a crucial role in determining the overall system performance.
IEEE 802.11p standard is an international standard that defines vehicular communications specifications. However, IEEE 802.11p standard is based initially on the IEEE 802.11a standard that was proposed for indoor environments, where an insufficient number of pilots is allocated for channel estimation. As a result, IEEE 802.11p conventional estimators are based on the
DPA
(

II-B

DPA Basic Estimation

The
DPA
basic estimation employs the demapped data subcarriers besides pilots subcarriers to estimate the channel for the current
OFDM
symbol, such that

\small\bm{d}_{i}[k]=\mathfrak{D}\big(\frac{\bm{y}_{i}[k]}{\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{DPA}_{i-1}}[k]}\big),~\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{DPA}_{0}}[k]=\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{LS}}[k],~k\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\text{on}},

(2)

where
\mathfrak{D}(.)
is the demapping operation to the nearest constellation point according to the employed modulation order.
\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{LS}}[k]
refers to the LS estimated channel at the received preambles denoted as
\bm{y}^{(p)}_{1}[k]
, and
\bm{y}^{(p)}_{2}[k]
, such that

\small\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{LS}}[k]=\frac{\bm{y}^{(p)}_{1}[k]+\bm{y}^{(p)}_{2}[k]}{2\bm{p}[k]},~k\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\text{on}},

(3)

where
\bm{p}[k]
represents the frequency domain predefined preamble sequence. After that, the final
DPA
channel estimated are updated as follows

\small\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{DPA}_{i}}[k]=\frac{\bm{y}_{i}[k]}{\bm{d}_{i}[k]},~k\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\text{on}}.

(4)

It is worth mentioning that the
DPA
basic estimation is considered as the starting point by most of the IEEE 802.11p channel estimators.

II-B
2
STA-DNN Estimator

The authors in
[
4
]

2

4

\small\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{FD}_{i}}[k]=\sum_{\lambda=-\beta}^{\lambda=\beta}\omega_{\lambda}\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{DPA}_{i}}[k+\lambda],~\omega_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{2\beta+1},k\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\text{d}},

(5)

\small\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{STA}_{i}}[k]=(1-\frac{1}{\alpha})\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{STA}_{i-1}}[k]+\frac{1}{\alpha}\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{FD}_{i}}[k],k\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\text{on}},

(6)

where
\beta

refer to the

[
4

is fed as an input to an

-

SNR

The

•

Equalize the previously received

{\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{TRFI}_{i-1}}[k]}
and
{\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{DPA}_{i}}[k]}
, such that

\small\begin{split}{\bm{d^{\prime}}_{i-1}[k]}=\mathfrak{D}\big(\frac{\bm{y}_{i-1}[k]}{\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{DPA}_{i}}[k]}\big),~{\bm{d^{\prime\prime}}_{i-1}[k]}=\mathfrak{D}\big(\frac{\bm{y}_{i-1}[k]}{\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{TRFI}_{i-1}}[k]}\big).\end{split}

(7)

•

According to the demapping results, the subcarriers are grouped as follows

\small\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\mathcal{RS}}_{i}\leftarrow{\mathcal{RS}}_{i}+{k},&\hskip 9.24994pt\bm{d^{\prime}}_{i-1}[k]=\bm{d^{\prime\prime}}_{i-1}[k]\\

(8)

•

Finally, frequency domain cubic interpolation is employed to estimate the channels at the
{\mathcal{URS}}_{i}
as follows

\small\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{TRFI}_{i}}[k]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{DPA}_{i}}[k],&\hskip 9.24994ptk\in{\mathcal{RS}}_{i}\\
\text{Cubic Interpolation},&\hskip 9.24994ptk\in{\mathcal{URS}}_{i}\end{array}\right..

(9)

In order to outperform the

Proposed LSTM-based Estimation Scheme

In this section, the proposed LSTM based estimator is discussed. First of all, a brief description of

III-A

LSTM Overview

LSTM
networks are basically proposed to deal with sequential data where the order of the data matters and there exists a correlation between the previous and the future data. In this context,
LSTM
networks are defined with an appropriate architecture that can learn the data correlation over time, thus giving the
LSTM
network the ability to predict the future data based on the previous observations.

LSTM
unit contains computational blocks known as gates which are responsible for controlling and tracking the information flow over time. The
LSTM
network mechanism can be explained in four major steps as follow

Forget the irrelevant information

In general, the
LSTM
unit classify the input data into relevant and irrelevant information. The first processing step is to eliminate the irrelevant information that are not important for the future data prediction. This can be performed through the forget gate that decides which information the
LSTM
unit should keep, and which information it should delete. The forget gate processing is defined as below

\small{\bm{f}}_{t}={\sigma}(\bm{W}_{f,t}\bar{\bm{x}}_{t}+\bm{W}^{\prime}_{f,t}\bar{\bm{h}}_{t-1}+\bar{\bm{b}}_{f,t}),

(10)

where
{\sigma}
is the sigmoid function,
\bm{W}_{f,t}\in\mathbb{R}^{P\times K_{in}}
,
\bm{W}^{\prime}_{f,t}\in\mathbb{R}^{P\times P}
and
\bar{\bm{b}}_{f,t}\in\mathbb{R}^{P\times 1}
are the forget gate weights and biases at time
t
,
\bar{\bm{x}}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{K_{in}\times 1}
and
\bar{\bm{h}}_{t-1}
denote the
LSTM
unit input vector of size
K_{in}
, and the previous hidden state of size
P
respectively.

Store the relevant new information

After classifying the relevant information, the
LSTM
unit applies some computations on the selected information through the input gate

\small{\bar{\bm{i}}_{t}}={\sigma}(\bm{W}_{\bar{\bm{i}},t}\bar{\bm{x}}_{t}+\bm{W}^{\prime}_{\bar{\bm{i}},t}\bar{\bm{h}}_{t-1}+\bar{\bm{b}}_{\bar{\bm{i}},t}),

(11)

\small{\tilde{{\bm{c}}}}_{t}=\text{tanh}(\bm{W}_{{\tilde{{\bm{c}}}},t}\bar{\bm{x}}_{t}+\bm{W}^{\prime}_{{\tilde{{\bm{c}}}},t}\bar{\bm{h}}_{t-1}+\bar{\bm{b}}_{{\tilde{{\bm{c}}}},t}).

(12)

Update the new cell state

Now, the
LSTM
unit should update the current cell state
{{{\bm{c}}}}_{t}
based on the two previous steps such that

\small{{{\bm{c}}}}_{t}={\bm{f}}_{t}\odot{\bm{c}}_{t-1}+\bar{\bm{i}}_{t}\odot{\tilde{{\bm{c}}}}_{t},

(13)

where
\odot
denotes the Hadamard product.

Generate the LSTM unit output

The final processing step is to update the hidden state and generate the output by the output gate. The output is considered as a cell state filtered version and can be computed such that

\small{\bm{o}}_{t}={\sigma}(\bm{W}_{o,t}\bar{\bm{x}}_{t}+\bm{W}^{\prime}_{o,t}\bar{\bm{h}}_{t-1}+\bar{\bm{b}}_{o,t}),

(14)

\small{\bar{{\bm{h}}}}_{t}={\bm{o}}_{t}\odot\text{tanh}{\bm{c}}_{t}.

(15)

We note that, in literature there exists several
LSTM
architecture variants, where the interactions between the
LSTM
unit gates are modified. The authors in
[
8
]
performed a nice comparison of popular
LSTM
architecture variants. However, for the proposed estimator we focus on the classical
LSTM
unit architecture.

Proposed LSTM-TA Estimator

Unlike the
LSTM
-
DNN
-
DPA

LSTM-based prediction

: The first step is to estimate the channel for the current received
OFDM

\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{DL-TA}_{i-1,d}[k]}
, where the
i
-th

\tilde{\bar{{\bm{x}}}}_{{i}}\in\mathbb{R}^{2K_{on}\times 1}
, where

\small\bar{\bm{x}}_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{LSTM}_{i-1,d}}[k],&\hskip 9.24994ptk\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\text{d}}\\
\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{{i-1,p}}[k],&\hskip 9.24994ptk\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\text{p}}\end{array}\right..

(16)

\tilde{\bar{{\bm{x}}}}_{{i}}
is obtained by applying complex to real valued conversion to
\bar{\bm{x}}_{i}
by stacking its real and imaginary values in one vector. We note that
\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{{i-1,p}}[k]
denotes the
LS
(
LS
) estimated channel at the
{\mathcal{K}}_{\text{p}}
subcarriers. After that,
\tilde{\bar{{\bm{x}}}}_{{i}}
is processed by the

\small\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{LSTM}_{i,d}}=\Omega_{\text{LSTM}}(\tilde{\bar{\bm{x}}}_{i},\Theta),

(17)

where
\Omega_{\text{LSTM}}
is the
LSTM
unit processing with overall weights denoted by
\Theta
.

DPA estimation

The
LSTM

i
-th received

\small\bm{d}_{\text{LSTM}_{i}}[k]=\mathfrak{D}\big(\frac{\bm{y}_{i}[k]}{\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{LSTM}_{i-1}}[k]}\big),~\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{LSTM}_{0}}[k]=\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{LS}}[k],

(18)

\small\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{LSTM-DPA}_{i}}[k]=\frac{\bm{y}_{i}[k]}{\bm{d}_{\text{LSTM}_{i}}[k]}.

(19)

TA processing

Finally, in order to alleviate the impact of the AWGN noise,

\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{LSTM-DPA}_{i}}[k]
estimated channel, such that

\small\hat{\bar{\bm{h}}}_{\text{DL-TA}_{i,d}}=(1-\frac{1}{\alpha})\hat{\bar{\bm{h}}}_{\text{DL-TA}_{i-1,d}}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\hat{\bar{\bm{h}}}_{\text{LSTM-DPA}_{i,d}}.

(20)

In this paper we used a fixed
\alpha=2
for simplicity. Therefore, the

20

\sigma^{2}

\small\begin{split}{R}_{\text{DL-TA}_{q}}&=\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{(q-1)}+\sum_{j=2}^{q}\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{(q-j+1)}=\frac{4^{q-1}+2}{3\times 4^{q-1}}.\end{split}

(21)

{R}_{\text{DL-TA}_{q}}
denotes the AWGN noise power ratio of the estimated channel at the
q
-th estimated channel, where
{1<q<I+1}
and
{{R}_{\text{DL-TA}_{1}}=1}
denotes the AWGN noise power ratio at
\hat{\tilde{\bm{h}}}_{\text{LS}}[k]

21

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we have investigated the channel estimation challenge in vehicular environments. This challenge arises from the doubly selective nature of the vehicular channel, especially in high mobility scenarios. The recently proposed
DL
-based IEEE 802.11p estimators have been presented and their limitations have been discussed. In order to overcome these limitations, we have proposed an
LSTM
-based estimator, that employs an
LSTM

shown the performance superiority of the proposed
LSTM
-
DPA
-
TA

DL
-based estimators, while recording a significant reduction in computational complexity.

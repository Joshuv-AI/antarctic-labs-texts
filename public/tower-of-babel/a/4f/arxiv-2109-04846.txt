Model Predictive Control with Infeasible Reference Trajectories
Ivo Batkovic, Mohammad Ali, Paolo Falcone, and Mario Zanon

Abstract
Model Predictive Control (MPC) formulations are typically built on the requirement that a feasible reference trajectory is available. In practical settings, however, references that are infeasible with respect to the system dynamics are used for convenience. In this paper, we prove under which conditions an MPC formulation is Input-to-State Stable (ISS) in closed-loop when an infeasible reference is used, and that with proper terminal conditions, asymptotic stability towards an optimal reference may be achieved. We illustrate the theoretical results with a four-dimensional robotic joint example.



I
Introduction

Model Predictive Control (MPC) is widely known as an advanced control technique for nonlinear systems that can handle time-varying references with preview information as well as constraints. In the vast body of literature, standard MPC formulations penalize deviations from a set point (typically the origin) or a (feasible) reference trajectory, while providing stability and recursive feasibility guarantees for such settings
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

In practice, it is often difficult or cumbersome to pre-compute reference trajectories which are feasible, i.e., which both satisfy path constraints and evolve according to the system dynamics. It is therefore appealing to use reference trajectories (or reference paths) that are simple to compute and only satisfy path constraints, but not necessarily the system dynamics. However, using such trajectories introduces difficulties in providing stability guarantees
[
5
]
.

This paper aims at (partially) filling a gap between practice and theory that exists for MPC formulations with infeasible references. Previous work
[
6
]
has considered infeasible set points, and shown that stabilization to the closest feasible set point can be guaranteed. However, in the case of time-varying references, this analysis does directly not apply. To that end, we propose to use an Input-to-State Stability (ISS) approach instead.

Our contribution in this paper is twofold. First, we prove that MPC formulations that are formulated with an infeasible reference, can actually stabilize towards an optimal trajectory, subject to specific terminal conditions. However, selecting such terminal conditions requires in general that an optimization problem is solved beforehand to compute a feasible reference. Consequently, if this step is performed, one has no reason to provide an infeasible reference to the MPC controller. Therefore, in a second step, we extend this first result to construct an ISS analysis to further show that if sub-optimal terminal conditions are chosen, the controlled system in closed-loop is stabilized around a neighborhood of the optimal trajectory.

This paper is structured as follows. In Section
II
we introduce the MPC tracking problem, while in Section
III-A
we prove that, while having an ideal setting in mind, one can design an MPC formulation that stabilizes the closed-loop system to an optimal feasible reference, even if an infeasible reference is used, at the price of defining suitable terminal conditions. Then, in Section
III-B
we further extend the results by proving ISS for practical settings, i.e., in case the terminal conditions are based on the infeasible reference. Finally, in Section
IV
we illustrate the derived theory with a numerical example, and draw conclusions in Section
V
.

recalls the known stability results from the existing literature, which apply to tracking MPC schemes. The resulting design procedure for asymptotically stable tracking MPC is indeed complicated by the task of precomputing a feasible reference trajectory

or Model Predictive Flexible trajectory Tracking Control (MPFTC)

]

\displaystyle:=\arg\min_{{\mathbf{x}}}p_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathbf{x}},t_{k})-{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{k}^{{\mathrm{r}}\top}({\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{r}}_{k}).

The Problems (

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})=\min_{{\mathbf{x}},{\mathbf{u}}}&\sum_{n=k}^{k+N-1}\bar{q}_{\mathbf{r}}({\mathbf{x}}_{n|k},{\mathbf{u}}_{n|k},t_{n})\\
&\hskip 20.00003pt+\bar{p}_{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k},t_{k+N})\end{split}

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}\hskip 0.0pt\ \

\displaystyle\eqref{eq:nmpcState}-\eqref{eq:nmpcInequality_known},\ {\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}\in\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{f}}_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}}(t_{k+N}),

Lemma 2
.

Consider the
rotated

ideal
MPC Problem (
14
), formulated using the rotated costs
\bar{q}_{\mathbf{r}}
and
\bar{p}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathbf{r}}}
, and the terminal set
\mathcal{X}_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathbf{r}}}^{\mathrm{f}}
. Then, the primal solution of (
14
) coincides with the primal solution of the ideal MPC Problem (

).

Proof.

From (
13
) and (
15
) we have that
\bar{p}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathbf{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k}^{\mathrm{r}},t_{k})=0
and that

\nabla\bar{p}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{r}}_{k},t_{k})=\nabla p_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{r}}_{k},t_{k})+\nabla p_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}}({\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathrm{r}}_{k},t_{k})=0
, since the terminal costs are quadratic (

). The proof then follows along the same lines as Lemma
1
and
[
9
,
10
]
.
∎

In order to prove Theorem
2
, we need that the terminal conditions of the rotated ideal formulation (
14
) satisfy Assumption
3
. To that end, we introduce the following assumption.

Assumption 4
.

There exists a parametric stabilizing terminal set
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{f}}_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}}(t)
and a terminal control law
\kappa^{\mathrm{f}}_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathbf{x}},t)
yielding:

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{+}^{\kappa}=f_{k}(\mathbf{x}_{k},\kappa^{\mathrm{f}}_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t)),

\displaystyle t_{+}=t_{k}+t_{\mathrm{s}},

so that

\bar{p}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{+}^{\kappa},t_{+})-\bar{p}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})\leq{}-\bar{q}_{\mathbf{r}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},\kappa^{\mathrm{f}}_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k}),t_{k})
,
{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\in\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{f}}_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}}(t_{k})\Rightarrow{\mathbf{x}}^{\kappa}_{+}\in\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{f}}_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}}(t_{+})
, and
h({\mathbf{x}}_{k},\kappa^{\mathrm{f}}_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k}))\leq{}0
hold for all
k\in\mathbb{I}_{0}^{\infty}
.

Note that Assumption
4
only differs from Assumption
3
by the fact that the set and control law are centered on
{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}
rather than
{\mathbf{r}}
, and that the costs are rotated.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that Assumptions
1
and
4
hold, and that Problem (
9
) is feasible for initial state
({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})
. Then, system (
1
) in closed-loop with the ideal MPC (
12
) is asymptotically stabilized to the optimal trajectory
{{\mathbf{x}}}^{\mathrm{r}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2
, the rotated ideal MPC problem has positive-definite stage and terminal costs penalizing deviations from the optimal trajectory
{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}
. Hence, the rotated ideal MPC problem is of tracking type.

Assumption
1
directly entails a lower bounding by a
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
function, and can also be used to prove an upper bound
[
2
, Theorem 2.19]
, such that the following holds

\alpha_{1}(\|{\mathbf{x}}_{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{r}}_{k}\|)\leq{}\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})\leq{}\alpha_{2}(\|{\mathbf{x}}_{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{r}}_{k}\|),

where
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
. Then, solving Problem (
14
), we obtain
\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})
and the optimal trajectories
\{{\mathbf{x}}_{k|k}^{\mathrm{i}},...,{\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\mathrm{i}}\}
, and
\{{\mathbf{u}}_{k|k}^{\mathrm{i}},...,{\mathbf{u}}_{k+N-1|k}^{\mathrm{i}}\}
. By relying on Assumptions
2
and
3
, using terminal control law
\kappa^{\mathrm{f}}_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}}
, we can construct the feasible sub-optimal trajectories
\{{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1|k}^{\mathrm{i}},...,{\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\mathrm{i}},f_{k+N}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\mathrm{i}},\kappa^{\mathrm{f}}_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathbf{r}}})\}
and
\{{\mathbf{u}}_{k+1|k}^{\mathrm{i}},...,{\mathbf{u}}_{k+N|k}^{\mathrm{i}},\kappa^{\mathrm{f}}_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}}\}
at time
k+1
, which can be used to derive the decrease condition following standard arguments
[
2
,
3
]
:

\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+1},t_{k+1})-\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})\leq{}-\alpha_{3}(\|{\mathbf{x}}_{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{r}}_{k}\|).

This entails that the
rotated

ideal
value function
\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})
is a Lyapunov function, and that the closed-loop system is asymptotically stabilized to
{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{r}}
.
Finally, using Lemma
2
we establish asymptotic stability also for the
ideal
MPC scheme (
12
), since the primal solutions of the two problems coincide.
∎

Theorem
2
establishes the first step towards the desired result:
an MPC problem can be formulated using an
infeasible reference
, which stabilizes system (
1
) to the optimal trajectory of Problem (
9
) provided that the appropriate terminal conditions are used.

At this stage, the main issue is to express the terminal constraint set as
a positive invariant set containing
{{\mathbf{x}}}^{\mathrm{r}}
, and the terminal control law stabilizing the system to
{{\mathbf{x}}}^{\mathrm{r}}
.
To that end, one needs to know the feasible reference trajectory
{{\mathbf{x}}}^{\mathrm{r}}
, i.e., to solve Problem (
9
). Since solving Problem (
9
) is not practical, we prove in the next section how sub-optimal terminal conditions can be used instead to prove ISS for the closed-loop system.

III-B

Practical MPC and ISS

In this subsection, we analyze the case in which the terminal conditions are not enforced based on the feasible reference trajectory, but
rather based on an
approximatively feasible
reference (see Assumption
5
).
Since in that case asymptotic stability cannot be proven, we will prove ISS for the closed-loop system, where the input is some terminal reference
{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}
. In particular, we are interested in the practical approach
{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}={\mathbf{r}}(t_{k+N})
and the ideal setting
{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}={\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}(t_{k+N})
.
To that end, we define the following closed-loop dynamics

\displaystyle{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}({\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}})=f_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},{\mathbf{u}}_{\mathrm{MPC}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}))=\bar{f}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}),

(16)

where we stress that

solving the ideal problem (

) (in case one uses

and terminal cost
p_{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathbf{r}}}
). In the following we will use the notation
{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}({\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}})
to stress that the terminal reference
{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}
is used in the computation of the control yielding the next state. Additionally, we define
the following quantities

\displaystyle\bar{J}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathrm{r}}}^{\star}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=k}^{N-1}\bar{q}_{\mathbf{r}}({\mathbf{x}}_{n|k}^{\star},{\mathbf{u}}_{n|k}^{\star},t_{n})+\bar{p}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\star},t_{k+N}),

\displaystyle\bar{J}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n=k}^{N-1}\bar{q}_{\mathbf{r}}({\mathbf{x}}_{n|k}^{\mathrm{i}},{\mathbf{u}}_{n|k}^{\mathrm{i}},t_{n})+\bar{p}_{\mathbf{r}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\mathrm{i}},t_{k+N}),

and we remind that

\displaystyle\bar{V}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{N-1}\bar{q}_{\mathbf{r}}({\mathbf{x}}_{n|k}^{\star},{\mathbf{u}}_{n|k}^{\star},t_{n})+\bar{p}_{\mathbf{r}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\star},t_{k+N}),

\displaystyle\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{N-1}\bar{q}_{\mathbf{r}}({\mathbf{x}}_{n|k}^{\mathrm{i}},{\mathbf{u}}_{n|k}^{\mathrm{i}},t_{n})+\bar{p}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\mathrm{i}},t_{k+N}).

Before formulating the stability result in the next theorem, we need to introduce an additional assumption on the reference infeasibility.

Assumption 5

(Approximate feasibility of the reference)
.

The reference
{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}
satisfies the constraints (
3d
), i.e.,
h({{\mathbf{x}}}^{\mathrm{f}}_{n},{{\mathbf{u}}}^{\mathrm{f}}_{n})\leq{}0
,
n\in\mathbb{I}_{k}^{k+N-1}
, for all
k\in\mathcal{N}^{+}
. Additionally, recursive feasibility holds for both Problem (
3
) and (
12
) when the system is controlled in closed-loop using the feedback from Problem (
3
).

Remark 3
.

Assumption
5
essentially only requires that the reference used in the definition of the terminal conditions (constraint and cost) is feasible with respect to the system constraints, and not the system dynamics. However, recursive feasibility holds if the reference satisfies, e.g.,
\|{\mathbf{x}}_{n+1}^{\mathrm{f}}-f_{n}({\mathbf{x}}_{n}^{\mathrm{f}},{\mathbf{u}}_{n}^{\mathrm{f}})\|\leq{}\epsilon
, for some small
\epsilon
i.e., if the reference satisfies the system dynamics approximately.
Note that, if
\epsilon=0
, then Assumption
2
is satisfied and Assumption
5
is not needed anymore. Finally, the infeasibility due to
\epsilon\neq 0
could be formally accounted for so as to satisfy Assumption
5
by taking a robust MPC approach, see, e.g.,
[
14
,
15
]
.

From a practical standpoint, Assumption
5
sets a rather mild requirement. In fact, it is not uncommon to use infeasible references for simplicity or satisfying approximated system dynamics to capture the most relevant dynamics of the system (keeping
\epsilon
small).

We are now ready to state the main result of the paper.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that Problem (
9
) is feasible and Assumptions
1
and
3
hold for the reference
{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}
with costs
\bar{q}_{\mathbf{r}}
and
\bar{p}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathbf{r}}}
and terminal set
\mathcal{X}_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathbf{r}}}
. Suppose moreover that Problem (
3
) and Problem (
12
) are feasible at time
k
with inital state
({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})
, and that reference
{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}
, with terminal set
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{f}}_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}}
, satisfies Assumption
5
. Then, system (
16
) obtained from (
1
) in closed-loop with MPC formulation (
3
) is ISS.

Proof.

We prove the result using the value function
\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})
of the rotated ideal Problem (
14
) as an ISS-Lyapunov function candidate
[
16
]
. From the prior analysis in Theorem
2
we know that Assumption
2
holds for
{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}
since Problem (
9
) is feasible, and that
\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})
is a Lyapunov function when the
ideal
terminal conditions
{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}={\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}
are used. Hence, when we apply the ideal control input
{\mathbf{u}}_{k|k}^{\mathrm{i}}
, i.e., use (
16
) to obtain the next state
{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}({\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}})=\bar{f}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}})
, we have the following relations

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}(\|{\mathbf{x}}_{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{r}}_{k}\|)\leq\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})\leq\alpha_{2}(\|{\mathbf{x}}_{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{r}}_{k}\|),

\displaystyle\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}({\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}),t_{k+1})-\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})\leq-\alpha_{3}(\|{\mathbf{x}}_{k}-{\mathbf{x}}^{\mathrm{r}}_{k}\|),

with
\alpha_{i}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
,
i=1,2,3
.

We are left with proving ISS, i.e., that
\exists\,\sigma\in\mathcal{K}
such that when the reference
{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}
is treated as an external input, the next state is given by
{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}({\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}})=\bar{f}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}})
, the following holds

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}({\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}})},t_{k+1})-\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})\leq&\sigma(\|{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}-{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}\|)\\
&-\alpha_{3}(\|{\mathbf{x}}_{k}-{{\mathbf{x}}}^{\mathrm{r}}_{k}\|).\end{split}

(17)

In order to bound
\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}({\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}})},t_{k+1})-\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})
, we first derive an upper bound on
\bar{J}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{i}}
which depends on
\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}
.
To that end, we observe that the rotated cost of the ideal trajectory
{\mathbf{x}}_{n|k}^{\mathrm{i}}
,
{\mathbf{u}}_{n|k}^{\mathrm{i}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\bar{J}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})

\displaystyle=\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})-\bar{p}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\mathrm{i}},t_{k+N})

\displaystyle+\bar{p}_{\mathbf{r}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\mathrm{i}},t_{k+N}).

Defining

\displaystyle\phi({\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}})

\displaystyle:=\bar{p}_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\mathrm{i}},t_{k+N})-\bar{p}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\mathrm{i}},t_{k+N}),

there exists a
\sigma_{1}\in\mathcal{K}
such that
\phi({\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}})\leq{}\sigma_{1}(\|{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}-{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathbf{r}}\|)

since, by (
8
),
\phi({\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}})
is a continuous function of
{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}
and
\phi({\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}})=0
.
Then, the following upper bound is obtained

\displaystyle\bar{J}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})+\sigma_{1}(\|{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}-{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}\|).

Upon solving Problem (
3
), we obtain
\bar{V}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})\leq\bar{J}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})
. Starting from the optimal solution
{\mathbf{x}}^{\star}
, and
{\mathbf{u}}^{\star}
, we will construct an upper bound on the decrease condition. To that end, we first need to evaluate the cost of this trajectory, i.e.,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{J}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathrm{r}}}^{\star}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})&=\bar{V}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})-\bar{p}_{\mathbf{r}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\star},t_{k+N})\\
&+\bar{p}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\star},t_{k+N}).\end{split}

Using the same reasoning as before, there exists
\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{K}
such that

\displaystyle\bar{p}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\star},t_{k+N})-\bar{p}_{\mathbf{r}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\star},t_{k+N})

\displaystyle\hskip 50.00008pt\leq\sigma_{2}(\|{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}_{k+N}-{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}_{k+N}\|).

Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{J}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathrm{r}}}^{\star}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})&\leq\bar{V}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})+\sigma_{2}(\|{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}_{k+N}-{\mathbf{y}}_{k+N}^{\mathrm{r}}\|)\\
&\leq\bar{J}_{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})+\sigma_{2}(\|{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}_{k+N}-{\mathbf{y}}_{k+N}^{\mathrm{r}}\|)\\
&\leq\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})+\sigma(\|{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}_{k+N}-{\mathbf{y}}_{k+N}^{\mathrm{r}}\|),\end{split}

(18)

where we defined
\sigma:=\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{2}
.

Proceeding similarly as in the proof of Proposition
1
, we apply the control input
{\mathbf{u}}_{k|k}^{\star}
from (
3
), i.e.,
{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}={\mathbf{r}}
, to obtain

{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}({\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}})=\bar{f}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}).

In order to be able to apply this procedure, we first assume that the obtained initial guess is feasible for the ideal problem (
12
) and proceed as follows.
By Assumption 3, we use the terminal control law
\kappa_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathbf{r}}}^{\mathrm{f}}({\mathbf{x}},t)
, to form a guess at the next time step and upper bound the
ideal
rotated value function. By optimality

\displaystyle\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}

\displaystyle({\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}({\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}),t_{k+1})\leq{}

(19)

\displaystyle\leq{}\sum_{n=k+1}^{N-1}\bar{q}_{\mathbf{r}}({\mathbf{x}}_{n|k}^{\star},{\mathbf{u}}_{n|k}^{\star},t_{n})+\bar{q}_{\mathbf{r}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\star},\kappa_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathbf{r}}},t_{k+N})

\displaystyle\hskip 40.00006pt+\bar{p}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathbf{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N+1|k}^{\kappa,\star},t_{k+N+1})

\displaystyle=\bar{J}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathbf{r}}}^{\star}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})-\bar{q}_{\mathbf{r}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k|k}^{\star},{\mathbf{u}}_{k|k}^{\star},t_{k})-\bar{p}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathbf{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\star},t_{k+N})

where we used

{\mathbf{x}}_{k+N+1|k}^{\star,\kappa}\hskip-1.99997pt:=\hskip-1.99997ptf_{k+N}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k},\kappa_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathbf{r}}}),\,\kappa_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathbf{r}}}\hskip-1.99997pt:=\hskip-1.99997pt\kappa_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathbf{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\star},t_{k+N}),

and assumed that

. Again, using Assumption 3 we can now upper bound the terms

\displaystyle\bar{p}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathbf{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N+1|k}^{\star,\kappa},t_{k+N+1})-\bar{p}_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathbf{r}}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\star},t_{k+N})

\displaystyle\ +\bar{q}_{\mathbf{r}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+N|k}^{\star},\kappa_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathbf{r}}})\leq{}0,

so that (

) can be written as

\displaystyle\bar{V}^{\mathrm{i}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}({\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{f}}),t_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq{}J_{{\tilde{\mathbf{y}}}^{\mathbf{r}}}^{\star}({\mathbf{x}}_{k},t_{k})-\bar{q}_{\mathbf{r}}({\mathbf{x}}_{k|k}^{\star},{\mathbf{u}}_{k|k}^{\star},t_{k}),

(20)

which, in turn, proves (

In case
{{\mathbf{x}}_{k+N+1|k}^{\star,\kappa}\not\in\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{f}}_{{\mathbf{y}}^{\mathrm{r}}}(t_{k+N+1})}

we resort to a relaxation of the terminal constraint with an exact penalty

in order to compute an upper bound to the cost. This relaxation has the property that the solution of the relaxed formulation coincides with the one of the non-relaxed formulation whenever it exists. Then, by construction, the cost of an infeasible trajectory is higher than that of the feasible solution.

Finally, from Assumption

we know that the value functions

and

.

.

We note that similar results may be possible to prove for general nonlinear systems if there exists a storage function such that strict dissipativity holds for the rotated cost functions

V
Conclusions

The use of infeasible references in MPC formulations is of great interest due to its convenience and simplicity. In this paper, we have discussed how such references affect the tracking performance for MPC formulations. We have proved that MPC formulations can yield asymptotic stability to an optimal trajectory when terminal conditions are suitably chosen. In addition, we also proved that the stability results can be extend for sub-optimal terminal conditions, in which case the controlled system is stabilized around a neighborhood of the optimal trajectory. Future research will investigate ways to extend the stability results to general nonlinear systems.

An arithmetic count of osculating lines
Giosuè Muratore

Abstract
. We say that a line in ℙ k n + 1 \mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{k} is osculating to a hypersurface Y Y if they meet with contact order n + 1 n+1 . When k = ℂ k=\mathbb{C} , it is known that through a fixed point of Y Y , there are exactly n ! n! of such lines. Under some parity condition on n n and deg ⁡ ( Y ) \deg(Y) , we define a quadratically enriched count of these lines over any perfect field k k . The count takes values in the Grothendieck–Witt ring of quadratic forms over k k and depends linearly on deg ⁡ ( Y ) \deg(Y) .

1.
Introduction

A classical result by Salmon states that, over
k=\mathbb{C}
, for a general smooth surface
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{C}}
and a general point
p\in Y
there are exactly two lines meeting
Y
at
p
with contact order
3

[
Sal65
]
. That result does not depend on the degree of
Y
. A possible generalization of Salmon’s result consists on the number of rational curves
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{\mathbb{C}}
meeting a hypersurface with contact order
(n+2)\deg(C)-1
at a fixed general point. These curves have recently been used in a number of interesting applications
[
LP18
]
, and their number has been computed recursively using Gromov–Witten invariants in
[
MS21
,
Mur21
]
. They are called osculating curves. In particular, the number of osculating lines to a hypersurface
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{\mathbb{C}}
is

(1.1)

n!.

This result was probably first proved in
[
CM18
, Proposition 3.4]
. In this paper, we generalize Equation (
1.1
) to any perfect field
k
using
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy. In this theory, the solution of an enumerative problem is an element of the Grothendieck–Witt ring
\mathrm{GW}(k)
of
k
. It is the completion of the semi-ring of isomorphism classes of nondegenerate, symmetric bilinear forms on finite dimensional vector spaces over
k
. This technique has been used, for example, to compute the number of lines in the cubic surface
[
KW21
]
, in the quintic threefold
[
Pau22
]
, and the degree of the Grassmannian of lines
[
SW21
]
.

1.1.
Statement of the main result

Let
a\in k\setminus\{0\}
. We denote by
\langle a\rangle\in\mathrm{GW}(k)
the class of the bilinear form
k\times k\rightarrow k
given by
(x,y)\mapsto axy
. Given a finite extension
K/k
, the group morphism
\mathrm{Tr}_{K/k}\colon\mathrm{GW}(K)\rightarrow\mathrm{GW}(k)
is the natural map induced by the field trace
K\rightarrow k
. We denote by
\mathbb{H}:=\langle 1\rangle+\langle-1\rangle
.

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{k}
be a hypersurface of degree
d\geq n
given by a homogeneous polynomial
f
, and
p\in Y
be a point.
If
p
is not
k
-rational, we extend the scalars and consider
f_{k(p)}
with a lift
\tilde{p}
of
p
.

If
U
is an affine open set of
\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{k(p)}
centered at
\tilde{p}
, the restriction of
f_{k(p)}
to
U
has a unique Taylor series, that is it decomposes as sum of homogeneous polynomials
f^{(i)}
of degree
i
:

{f_{k(p)}|}_{U}=f^{(1)}+f^{(2)}+\cdots+f^{(d)}.

If the subscheme
F:=f^{(1)}\cap\cdots\cap f^{(n)}
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k(p)}
is finite, for each point
l\in F
we denote by
J(l)
the signed volume of the parallelepiped determined by the
gradient vectors of
f^{(1)},f^{(2)},\cdots,f^{(n)}
at
l
, and

J(Y,p):=\sum_{l\in f^{(1)}\cap\cdots\cap f^{(n)}}\mathrm{Tr}_{k(l)/k(p)}\langle J(l)\rangle.

Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n
and
d
be positive integers such that the following conditions are satisfied

•

n\equiv 2\,\mathrm{mod}\,4
,

•

d\equiv 0\,\mathrm{mod}\,2
.

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{k}
be a general hypersurface of degree
d\geq n
, and
\mathcal{L}
be a general
k
-rational line meeting
Y
transversely. Then

\sum_{p\in Y\cap\mathcal{L}}\mathrm{Tr}_{k(p)/k}\langle J(Y,p)\rangle=d\frac{n!}{2}\mathbb{H}.

When
k=\mathbb{C}
, the map
\mathrm{rank}\colon\mathrm{GW}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is an isomorphism, so the theorem implies Equation (
1.1
). Indeed, each osculating line contributes with
\langle 1\rangle
to the sum.
By
[
Mur21
, Proposition 4.1]
, the number of complex osculating lines is the same for each of the general
d
points
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{d}\}=Y\cap\mathcal{L}
. Hence there are
n!
lines through each of these points.

Our result is related to Bézout–McKean Theorem, see Remark
6.3
.

1.2.
Possible generalizations

One may consider conics instead of lines. For example, the number of osculating conics to a hypersurface
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{\mathbb{C}}
at
p
is

\frac{(2n+2)!}{2^{n+2}}-\frac{((n+1)!)^{2}}{2}.

This is proved in
[
Mur21
, Equation (6.1)]
. See also the tree-formula of
[
MS23
]
. Hence, if
n=2
, there are
27
osculating conics. Darboux
[
Dar80
]
noted that, when
\deg(Y)=3
, each line of
Y
is coplanar to a unique osculating conic, and vice versa. This explains why they are
27
. Recent examples of enriched counts of conics are
[
McK22
,
DGGM23
,
BW23b
]
. We hope to address the problem of the enriched count of osculating curves of higher degree in the near future. We do not expect that the solution will be a multiple of the hyperbolic class
\mathbb{H}
.

\mathbb{A}^{1}

. Section
4

2.
Background in
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-enumerative geometry

Let
k
be a field and
X
be a smooth proper scheme over
k
of dimension
n
. Moreover, let
E
be a vector bundle of rank
n
on
X
.

Definition 2.1
.

The bundle
E
is
relatively oriented
if there exists a line bundle
L
on
X
, and an isomorphism
\psi\colon\mathrm{Hom}(\det T_{X},\det E)\rightarrow L^{\otimes 2}
. The pair
(L,\psi)
is called a relative orientation of
E
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
z
be a point of
X
. A
system of Nisnevich coordinates
around
z
is a Zariski open neighborhood
U
of
z
in
X
, and an étale map
\varphi\colon U\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
such that the extension of residue fields
k(\varphi(z))\subseteq k(z)
is an isomorphism.

Example 2.3

(
[
MVW06
, Example 12.1]
)
.

Let
\mathrm{char}(k)\neq 2
and
a\in k\setminus\{0\}
. The maps
i\colon\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\setminus\{a^{2}\}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
and
(\_)^{2}\colon\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}\setminus\{0\}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
are a system of Nisnevich coordinates around all points of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
.

Example 2.4
.

Let
R:=\mathbb{R}[x,y]/(x^{2}+y^{2}+1)
and let
p
be the ideal generated by
y+1
. Let
U
be the spectrum of the localization
R_{p}
. The inclusion
\mathbb{R}[x]\hookrightarrow R_{p}
induces a system of Nisnevich coordinates around
z=\mathrm{Spec}(R/p)
.

Example 2.5
.

If
k\subsetneq K
is an extension of fields, then the
k
-scheme
\mathrm{Spec}(K)
does not admit any system of Nisnevich coordinates. Indeed, the residue fields are not isomorphic.

The existence of a system of Nisnevich coordinates is granted if
\dim X\geq 1
by
[
KW21
, Proposition 20]
. If
E
is a vector bundle and
\varphi\colon U\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
is a system of Nisnevich coordinates given by
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n})
, by shrinking
U
we may suppose without loss of generality that there exists a trivialization
\widehat{\varphi}\colon{E|}_{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{U}^{\oplus n}
. This local trivialization induces a distinguished element
\det(\widehat{\varphi}):=\varphi_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\varphi_{n}
of
\Gamma(U,\det E)
. On the other hand, there is a distinguished element of
\Gamma(U,\det T_{X})
induced by the standard basis of
T_{\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(L,\psi)
be a relative orientation of
E
. Let
\varphi\colon U\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
be a system of Nisnevich coordinates, and let
\widehat{\varphi}\colon{E|}_{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{U}^{\oplus n}
be a trivialization. Let
\lambda
be the linear map sending the distinguished element of
\Gamma(U,\det T_{X})
to the distinguished element
\det(\widehat{\varphi})
of
\Gamma(U,\det E)
. We say that
\psi
is
compatible
with
\varphi
and the trivialization
\widehat{\varphi}
if the induced map on global sections

{\psi|}_{U}\colon\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(\Gamma(U,\det T_{X}),\Gamma(U,\det E))\longrightarrow\Gamma(U,L^{\otimes 2})

sends
\lambda
to a square element of
\Gamma(U,L)^{\otimes 2}
.

In differential topology, the Brouwer degree of a differentiable map
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
can be computed in the following way. Take
y\in Y
a regular value, so that the Jacobian matrix
J_{f}
is invertible for each one of the finite points
x\in X
such that
f(x)=y
. The local Brouwer degree at
x
is
+1
if
\det(J_{f})|_{x}>0
, and
-1
otherwise. The Brouwer degree is the sum of all local degrees, that is

(2.1)

\deg(f)=\sum_{x\in f^{-1}(y)}\mathrm{sgn}(\det(J_{f})|_{x}),

and it depends only on the homotopy class of
f
. See
[
Lee13
, Chapter 17]
for a nice introduction.

Morel extended this notion by defining the
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
-homotopy degree. Roughly speaking, in
[
MV99
]
a category is constructed upon algebraic varieties over
k
, but allowing many tools of usual topology of manifolds. In particular, the unit interval is replaced by
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
in homotopy. In this new category there is a Brouwer degree taking value in
\mathrm{GW}(k)
(see
[
Mor12
, Corollary 1.24]
and reference therein, and
[
Mor06
]
for an introduction). Morel’s construction has been made explicit in various contexts (see
[
Caz12
,
BMP23
]
).

2.1.
Euler class of a vector bundle

Let
(L,\psi)
be a relative orientation of
E
as in Definition
2.1
. The map
\psi
induces an isomorphism
\psi^{\prime}\colon\det E^{\vee}\otimes L^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow\omega_{X}
. For each
0\leq a,b\leq n
, we may use
\psi^{\prime}
, the graded Koszul complex
[
Eis95
, 17.2]
, and Serre duality to construct a perfect pairing

\beta_{a,b}\colon H^{a}(X,\wedge^{b}E^{\vee}\otimes L)\otimes H^{n-a}(X,\wedge^{n-b}E^{\vee}\otimes L)\longrightarrow k.

When
2a=2b=n
,
\beta_{a,b}
is a bilinear form. For all other values of
a
and
b
, the direct sum
\beta_{a,b}\oplus\beta_{n-a,n-b}
is also a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form.

Definition 2.7
.

The Grothendieck–Serre–duality
Euler number
of
E
is

e(E):=\sum_{0\leq a,b\leq n}(-1)^{a+b}\beta_{a,b}\in\mathrm{GW}(k).

See
[
BW23a
, Section 2]
. This definition does not depend on the relative orientation.

An older but more explicit definition of Euler class was given in
[
KW21
]
. It requires a section
\sigma\colon X\rightarrow E
with isolated zeros.

Let
z
be a zero of
\sigma
, and let

\deg^{\mathbb{A}_{k}^{1}}_{z}(\sigma)\in\mathrm{GW}(k)

denote its local
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}
-degree. In the case where
z
is simple, has a compatible Nisnevich neighborhood isomorphic to the affine space, and
k(z)/k
is separable, then by
[
KW19
, Proposition 15]
we have

(2.2)

\deg^{\mathbb{A}_{k}^{1}}_{z}(\sigma)=\mathrm{Tr}_{k(z)/k}\langle\det(J_{\sigma})|_{z}\rangle.

The Jacobian
J_{\sigma}
of
\sigma
is computed locally at
\sigma\colon\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}
.

More generally, if
z
is not simple,
\deg^{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}}_{z}(\sigma)
is constructed in a different way. Let

Z
denote the zero locus of
\sigma
. There is a natural isomorphism

\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(\mathcal{O}_{Z,z},k)\cong\mathcal{O}_{Z,z}

of
\mathcal{O}_{Z,z}
-algebras. The Scheja–Storch form (
[
SS75
]
) is the map
\eta
corresponding to
1
under this isomorphism. This defines a bilinear form
x\otimes y\mapsto\eta(xy)
on
\mathcal{O}_{Z,z}
(see
[
Him77
,
EL77
,
Eis78
]
), and
\deg^{\mathbb{A}_{k}^{1}}_{z}(\sigma)
is the class of this bilinear form as proved in
[
KW19
, Main Theorem]
. Finally

(2.3)

e(E)=\sum_{z\in\sigma^{-1}(0)}\deg^{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{k}}_{z}(\sigma),

by
[
BW23a
, Theorem 1.1]
. Equation (
2.2
) makes clear the analogy between (
2.3
) and the classical Brouwer degree (
2.1
).

2.2.
The non-orientable case

Many interesting vector bundles are not relatively orientable,
so that there is no Euler class as in Definition
2.7
. Larson and Vogt partially solve this problem by introducing the relative orientability relative to a divisor.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
X
be a smooth variety,
E\rightarrow X
be a vector bundle and
D\subset X
be an effective divisor. We say that
E
is
relatively orientable relative to

D
if there exists a line bundle
L
on
X
, and an isomorphism

\psi\colon\mathrm{Hom}(\det T_{X},\det E)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)\longrightarrow L^{\otimes 2}.

Equivalently,
E
is relatively orientable on the open subvariety
X\setminus D
.

At least over
\mathbb{R}
, this definition allows us to define the Euler class of a vector bundle that does not satisfy Definition
2.1
. So, let
X
be a smooth real variety. Let
\sigma
be a section of
E
with isolated zeros. For each zero
z
, the class of the Scheja–Storch form
\deg^{\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{R}}^{1}}_{z}(\sigma)
is still well-defined. Let us denote by
V_{D}\subset H^{0}(E)
the locus of
\mathbb{R}
-points of
H^{0}(E)
with a real zero along
D
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
X
be a smooth real projective variety and
E
be a vector bundle relatively oriented relative to an effective divisor
D
. Let
H^{0}(E)^{\circ}
denote the space of sections with isolated zeros. Then

\sum_{z\in\sigma^{-1}(0)}\deg^{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{R}}}_{z}(\sigma)

is constant for
\sigma
in any real connected component of
H^{0}(E)^{\circ}\setminus V_{D}
.

Proof.

See
[
LV21
, Lemma 2.4]
.
∎

Suppose that
1_{D}
is a global section of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
that gives
D
. For a vector bundle
E
relatively orientable relative to
D
, we say that the relative orientation
(L,\psi)
is compatible with a system of Nisnevich coordinates
\varphi\colon U\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
if
\lambda\otimes 1_{D}
is a square, where
\lambda
is like in Definition
2.6
. See
[
McK21
, Definition 3.7]
for more details.

3.
Results on the flag variety

Let
n
be a nonnegative integer, and
k
be a perfect field. Let
i\in\{0,\ldots,n+1\}
, we denote by
U_{i}
the open subset

U_{i}:=\mathrm{Spec}\left(k\left[\frac{x_{0}}{x_{i}},\ldots,\frac{x_{n+1}}{x_{i}}\right]\right)\subset\mathrm{Proj}\left(k[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n+1}]\right)=:\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{k}.

Remark 3.1
.

In
[
McK21
, Section 2]
,
U_{i}
is defined as the open subset of
\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{k}
parameterizing points
[p_{0}:\ldots:p_{n+1}]
such that
p_{i}\neq 0
. This notation makes sense only for
k
-points, as it denotes the point corresponding to the homogeneous ideal

(p_{i}x_{0}-p_{0}x_{i},\ldots,p_{i}x_{n+1}-p_{n+1}x_{i}).

Nevertheless, we adopt the same convention. In particular, we characterize any morphism by its action on the
coordinates

\{x_{0},\ldots,x_{n+1}\}
.

Let
G=G(2,n+2)
be the Grassmannian of lines in
\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{k}
. It is the space of matrices of order
2\times(n+2)
of maximal rank, modulo the action of
\mathrm{GL}(2,k)
. In particular, for each pair of distinct indices
\{i,\alpha\}\subset\{0,\ldots,n+1\}
, there is an open subset isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{2n}_{k}
parameterizing those orbits in
G
whose submatrix containing the columns
i
and
\alpha
is invertible. See, for example,
[
Muk03
, 8.1]
. We denote by
\mathcal{O}_{G}(-1)
the determinant of the tautological vector bundle
\mathcal{S}
of
G
, see p. 248 of
loc. cit.
. We denote by
X
the variety of flags

\{0\}\subset k\subset k^{2}\subset k^{n+2}.

It has two natural maps:

\pi\colon X\rightarrow G,\,\,\,\,\,\mathrm{ev}\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n+1}.

The pair

(X,\mathrm{ev})
is naturally isomorphic to the projectivization of tangent bundle of
\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{k}
, thus it is a
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
-bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{k}
. We denote by
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
the line bundle
\mathrm{ev}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{k}}(1)
. We will use the following properties of the Grassmannian.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{k}(k)
and
Z=\mathrm{ev}^{-1}(p)
. In the notation above, we have the following:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\omega_{G}

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{O}_{G}(-n-2),

(3.2)

\displaystyle\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{G}(1)

\displaystyle\cong\omega_{\pi}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(2),

(3.3)

\displaystyle{\omega_{\pi}|}_{Z}

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}}(1).

Proof.

The first equation follows by
[
EH16
, Proposition 5.25]
. In order to prove the second formula, note that
(X,\pi)
is naturally isomorphic the projectivization of the vector bundle
\mathcal{S}
. Theorem 11.4 of
loc. cit.
implies that

c_{1}(\omega_{\pi}^{\vee})=\binom{1+1-0}{1-0}c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(1))+\binom{1+1-1}{1-1}c_{1}(\pi^{*}\mathcal{S}).

Since
c_{1}(\pi^{*}\mathcal{S})=\pi^{*}c_{1}(\det\mathcal{S})=\pi^{*}c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{G}(-1))
, we easily deduce Equation (
3.2
).

In order to prove the last one,
note that
Z
parameterizes the set of flags

\{0\}\subset p\subset k^{2}\subset k^{n+2}.

Dividing the above equation by
p
, we see that
Z
is naturally isomorphic to the variety of projective lines of
k^{n+2}/p
, which is
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
. Thus there is a natural embedding
i\colon Z\hookrightarrow G
. This embedding preserves the Schubert cycles as discussed in Chapter 4.1 of
loc. cit.
, thus
i^{*}\mathcal{O}_{G}(1)=\mathcal{O}_{Z}(1)
and the result follows from the second equation. ∎

Remark 3.3
.

If
p
is not
k
-rational, then
Z=\mathbb{P}(T_{\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k},p})\cong\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k(p)}
. Since
G
is covered by affine spaces, the equation
{\omega_{\pi}|}_{Z}=\mathcal{O}_{Z}(1)
could still be proved using Galois descent (see
[
BLR90
, Example 6.2/B]
).

Given a set of indeces
I
and a sequence of elements
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, by
(\ldots,\widehat{a}_{j},\ldots)
we mean that
a_{j}
is missing from the sequence.

There is a standard system of Nisnevich coordinates of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
that we denote by
\{(U_{i},\varphi_{i})\}_{i=0}^{n}
. That is

(3.4)

\varphi_{i}([p_{0}:\ldots:p_{n+1}])=\left(\frac{p_{0}}{p_{i}},\ldots,\frac{p_{i-1}}{p_{i}},\frac{p_{i+1}}{p_{i}},\ldots,\frac{p_{n}}{p_{i}}\right).

These coordinates give a natural system of coordinates of
T_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{k}}
in the following way. Let
(x_{0},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{j},\ldots,x_{n+1})\in\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{k}
be local coordinates. The maps

(3.5)

\varphi_{i}\circ\varphi_{j}^{-1}(x_{0},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{j},\ldots,x_{n+1})=\left(\ldots,\frac{x_{j-1}}{x_{i}},\frac{1}{x_{i}},\frac{x_{j+1}}{x_{i}},\ldots,\frac{x_{n+1}}{x_{i}}\right)\in\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{k}

are the standard gluing of the projective space along
U_{j}\cap U_{i}
. In order to obtain the standard gluing of the tangent bundle with respect to the standard basis
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{t}}}\}_{t\neq j}
, we take the Jacobian
J_{ij}
of (
3.5
), leading to maps

(3.6)

J_{ij}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{s}}}\right)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{s}}}&\text{if $s\neq i,j$}\\
-\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}-\underset{t\neq i,j}{\sum}\frac{x_{t}}{x_{i}^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{t}}}&\text{if $s=i$}.\end{cases}

Thus the cocycle
\det(J_{ij})\circ\varphi_{j}
maps the distinguished basis

\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{0}}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\widehat{\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{j}}}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{n+1}}}

to

J_{ij}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{0}}}\right)\wedge\ldots\wedge J_{ij}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{j-1}}}\right)\wedge J_{ij}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{j+1}}}\right)\wedge\ldots\wedge J_{ij}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{n+1}}}\right),

which is equal to

\frac{(-1)^{i+j}}{x_{i}^{n+2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{0}}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\widehat{\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{j}}}}\wedge\ldots\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{n+1}}}.

The Jacobian determinant is
-1/x_{i}^{n+2}
, but we multiply by
(-1)^{i+j+1}
after reordering the derivations. See the proof of
[
Huy05
, Proposition 2.4.3]
. We will use another system of coordinates, which is better suited for our problem.

Definition 3.4
.

The twisted open cover
\{(U_{i},\tilde{\varphi}_{i})\}_{i=0}^{n}
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
is defined as
\tilde{\varphi}_{0}=\varphi_{0}
and

(3.7)

\tilde{\varphi}_{i}([p_{0}:\ldots:p_{n}])=\left((-1)^{i}\frac{p_{0}}{p_{i}},\ldots,\frac{p_{i-1}}{p_{i}},\frac{p_{i+1}}{p_{i}},\ldots,\frac{p_{n}}{p_{i}}\right).

Proposition 3.5
.

The twisted covering maps
\{(U_{i},\tilde{\varphi}_{i})\}_{i=0}^{n+1}
are a system of Nisnevich coordinates. Moreover, for each pair of indeces
(i,j)
, the morphism
\varphi_{i}
determines: the distinguished basis element

(-1)^{i}\cdot\partial_{i}:=(-1)^{i}\bigwedge_{t\neq i}\frac{\partial}{\partial(x_{t}/x_{i})}\in\Gamma\left(U_{i},\det{T_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{k}}|}_{U_{i}}\right),

and the transition functions
g_{ij}\colon{T_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{k}}|}_{U_{j}}\rightarrow{T_{\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{k}}|}_{U_{i}}
such that

(3.8)

\det g_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\right)^{n+2}.

Proof.

See
[
McK21
, Proposition 3.8]
.
∎

We will use the coordinates described in Equation (

) to a natural atlas of

.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
i,\alpha\in\{0,\ldots,n+1\}
be such that
\alpha\neq i
. Let
\{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{t}}\}_{t\neq i}
be a trivializing base of
T_{U_{i}}
. Let
U_{\alpha}\subset\mathbb{P}(T_{U_{i}})
be the open set corresponding to
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\alpha}}\neq 0
. Thus
\mathrm{ev}^{-1}(U_{i})=U_{i}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
is covered by open subsets

. The morphism
\tilde{\varphi}_{i\alpha}\colon U_{i\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{k}\times\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
is defined as follows

(3.9)

\tilde{\varphi}_{i\alpha}([p_{0}:\ldots:p_{n+1}]\times[q_{0}:\ldots:q_{n}]):=\varphi_{i}([p_{0}:\ldots:p_{n+1}])\times\tilde{\varphi}_{\alpha}([q_{0}:\ldots:q_{n}]).

Since
\tilde{\varphi}_{i\alpha}
is the direct product of two isomorphisms, it is an isomorphism. In particular it gives a system of Nisnevich coordinates.

Proposition 3.7
.

The covering maps
\{(U_{i\alpha},\tilde{\varphi}_{i\alpha})\}
are Nisnevich coordinates.

Moreover,
\tilde{\varphi}_{i\alpha}
determines a distinguished basis element of
\det{T_{X}|}_{U_{i}}
with transition functions
g_{(i\alpha),(j\beta)}\colon{{T_{X}}|}_{U_{j\beta}}\rightarrow{{T_{X}}|}_{U_{i\alpha}}
such that

(3.10)

\det g_{(i\alpha),(j\beta)}=\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{x_{i}y_{\alpha}}{x_{j}y_{\beta}}\right)^{n+1}.

Proof.

In order to obtain the gluing functions of
X
, we need to combine (
3.5
) and (
3.6
). A few calculations lead to the following maps of
\mathbb{A}^{n+1}_{k}\times\mathbb{A}^{n}_{k}
:

(3.11)

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}_{i\alpha}\circ\tilde{\varphi}_{j\beta}^{-1}((x_{0},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{j},\ldots,x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\times(y_{0},\ldots,\widehat{y}_{j},\ldots,\widehat{y}_{\beta},\ldots,y_{n+1}))

\displaystyle=(x_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{i}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{n+1}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\times((-1)^{\alpha+\beta}y_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,\widehat{y}_{i}^{\prime},\ldots,\widehat{y}_{\alpha}^{\prime},\ldots,y_{n+1}^{\prime}),

where for
s\neq i,j,\beta
,

x_{s}^{\prime}=\frac{x_{s}}{x_{i}},\,\,\,\,\,x_{\beta}^{\prime}=\frac{x_{\beta}}{x_{i}},\,\,\,\,\,x_{j}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{x_{i}},

y_{s}^{\prime}=\frac{y_{s}}{y_{\alpha}},\,\,\,\,\,y_{\beta}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{y_{\alpha}},\,\,\,\,\,y_{j}^{\prime}=\frac{-1}{x_{i}}\frac{y_{i}}{y_{\alpha}}-\frac{x_{\beta}}{x_{i}}\frac{1}{y_{\alpha}}-\sum_{t\neq i,j,\beta}\frac{x_{t}}{x_{i}}\frac{y_{t}}{y_{\alpha}}.

The determinant of the Jacobian matrix of (
3.11
) is

J_{ij\alpha\beta}:=(-1)^{\alpha+\beta}\det J_{ij}\det\left(\frac{\partial y_{t}^{\prime}}{\partial y_{s}}\right).

Note that in order to compute it, we can take
y_{j}^{\prime}=-y_{i}/(x_{i}y_{\alpha})
since the linear combination of the other coordinates
y_{s}^{\prime}
does not influence the determinant. Thus

\displaystyle\det J_{ij}\det\left(\frac{\partial y_{t}^{\prime}}{\partial y_{s}}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\alpha+\beta}\left(\frac{1}{x_{i}}\right)^{n+2}\left(\frac{-1}{x_{i}}\right)(-1)\left(\frac{1}{y_{\alpha}}\right)^{n+1}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\alpha+\beta}\left(\frac{1}{x_{i}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{1}{x_{i}y_{\alpha}}\right)^{n+1}.

We have a distinguished basis element of
\det{{T_{X}}|}_{U_{i\alpha}}
given by

(-1)^{\alpha}\cdot\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{\alpha}:=\bigwedge_{t\neq i}\frac{\partial}{\partial(x_{t}/x_{i})}\wedge(-1)^{\alpha}\bigwedge_{t\neq i,\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial(y_{t}/y_{\alpha})}.

After reordering, we see that the cocycles
J_{ij\alpha\beta}\circ\tilde{\varphi}_{j\beta}
equal (
3.10
) and are compatible with the distinguished basis.
The reason for the compatibility is that the transition map in (
3.11
) acts with a permutation
(j+1,i)
if
j<i
(or
(i+1,j)
, if
j>i)
on the coordinates
\{x_{s}\}_{s\neq j}
. On the other hand, if
j<i
and
\beta<\alpha
it acts with a permutation
(j+1,i)(\beta+1,\alpha)
on the coordinates
\{y_{s}\}_{s\neq j,\beta}
. In any case, the signature of (
3.11
) is
(-1)^{\alpha+\beta+1}
.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

The line bundles
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
and
\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}_{G}(1)
are locally trivialized over

, respectively, by

\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right),\,\,\,\,\,\left(\frac{x_{i}y_{\alpha}}{x_{0}y_{1}-x_{1}y_{0}}\right).

Moreover, the transition functions are, respectively,

\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\right),\,\,\,\,\,\left(\frac{x_{i}y_{\alpha}}{x_{j}y_{\beta}}\right).

Proof.

On
\mathrm{ev}^{-1}(U_{i})
(thus, on

),

is trivialized by the pullback of

on

. The induced transition functions on

are those of Equation (

[

, Proposition 3.9]
.
In order to trivialize

-orbits of

[x_{0}:\ldots:x_{n+1}]\times\left[\begin{pmatrix}x_{0}&\ldots&x_{n+1}\\
y_{0}&\ldots&y_{n+1}\end{pmatrix}\right]_{\mathrm{GL}(2,k)}.

Suppose that
[x_{0}:\ldots:x_{n+1}]\in U_{i}

all orbits appearing in the above equation admit a representative with

(3.12)

\begin{pmatrix}x_{0}&\ldots&x_{i}&\ldots&x_{n+1}\\
y_{0}&\ldots&0&\ldots&y_{n+1}\end{pmatrix}.

Moreover, the coordinates of

Let

[\ldots:P_{ts}:\ldots]
where

Since

∎

4.
Osculating lines

Let
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{k}
be a smooth hypersurface, and
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}_{k}
be a line. We say that
\mathcal{L}
and

have
t
-contact with each other if
\mathcal{L}\cap Y
contains a divisor of
\mathcal{L}
of the form

for some point

.

We say that
\mathcal{L}
is osculating to

if they are
(n+1)
-contact.

Lines with
(t+1)
-contact with

are parameterized by the zero locus of a section of the

bundle of principal parts, see
[
Vai81
, Section 5]
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let

be an integer. For every

, the
t^{\mathrm{th}}

bundle of principal parts
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(d)
is

(4.1)

\mathcal{P}^{t}(d):=q_{*}(p^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(d)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{G}X}/{\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}^{t+1}}),

where
p
and
q
are the two projections
X\times_{G}X\rightarrow X
.

There exists a canonical differential morphism of sheaves
\partial^{t}\colon\mathcal{O}_{X}(d)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}^{t}(d)
(see
[
Gro67
, Définition (16.3.6)]
). For any section

of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(d)
, the section
\partial^{t}s
of
\mathcal{P}^{t}(d)
is called principal part of

. Geometrically,
\partial^{t}
maps a local section defined at a point

to its first
t
Hasse–Schmidt derivatives relative to
\pi
. This is because
(\partial^{t},\mathcal{P}^{t}(d))
represents the functor sending a line bundle
M
to the set of
\mathcal{O}_{G}
-linear
t
-derivations

relative to
\pi

[
Gro67
, Proposition (16.8.4)]
. See
[
Voj07
,
CDH23
]
for a more explicit approach.
Finally the exact sequence

0\rightarrow{\mathcal{I}^{t+1}_{\Delta}}/{\mathcal{I}^{t+2}_{\Delta}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{G}X}/\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}^{t+2}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X\times_{G}X}/\mathcal{I}_{\Delta}^{t+1}\rightarrow 0

induces the exact sequence

(4.2)

0\rightarrow\omega_{\pi}^{\otimes t+1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(d)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}^{t+1}(d)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}^{t}(d)\rightarrow 0.

Finally, we define the following vector bundle.

Definition 4.2
.

We denote by
\mathcal{V}
the vector bundle on

given by
\mathcal{P}^{n}(d)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)^{\oplus n}
.

5.
Relative orientation

The rank of
\mathcal{V}
is clearly
2n+1
. Moreover, using the property of the determinant of a vector bundle, it is easy to see that

(5.1)

\det\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(d)^{\otimes n+1}\otimes\omega_{\pi}^{n(n+1)/2}.

Indeed,
\det\mathcal{V}=\det(\mathcal{P}^{n}(d))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(n)
. Using (
4.2
) and an easy induction,

\displaystyle\det(\mathcal{P}^{n}(d))

\displaystyle=\det(\mathcal{P}^{n-1}(d))\otimes\omega_{\pi}^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(d)

\displaystyle=\omega_{\pi}^{\otimes n(n+1)/2}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}((n+1)d).

We use this equation for trivializing
\det\mathcal{V}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

The line bundle
\det\mathcal{V}
is trivialized on
U_{i\alpha}
by the distinguished element

(5.2)

\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right)^{n+d(n+1)}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right)^{-n(n+1)}\left(\frac{x_{i}y_{\alpha}}{x_{0}y_{1}-x_{1}y_{0}}\right)^{n(n+1)/2},

with transition functions

\det{{\mathcal{V}}|}_{U_{j\beta}}\rightarrow\det{{\mathcal{V}}|}_{U_{i\alpha}}

being

(5.3)

\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\right)^{d(n+1)-n^{2}}\left(\frac{x_{i}y_{\alpha}}{x_{j}y_{\beta}}\right)^{n(n+1)/2}.

Proof.

By Equation (
5.1
), we need only the local trivializations of
\omega_{\pi}
and
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
. They are easily deduced from Lemma
3.8
and Equation (
3.2
). In particular,
{\omega_{\pi}|}_{U_{i\alpha}}
is trivialized by

\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right)^{-2}\left(\frac{x_{i}y_{\alpha}}{x_{0}y_{1}-x_{1}y_{0}}\right).

Finally, Equations (
5.2
) and (

) follow from (

).
∎

5.1.
Relatively orientable case

The following proposition gives us the conditions on
n
and
d
such that
\mathcal{V}
is relatively orientable.

Proposition 5.2
.

The vector bundle
\mathcal{V}
is relatively orientable if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

•

n\equiv 2\,\mathrm{mod}\,4
,

•

d\equiv 0\,\mathrm{mod}\,2
.

We prove this proposition after the following two simple lemmas.

Lemma 5.3
.

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

n\equiv 2\,\mathrm{mod}\,4
, or
n\equiv 3\,\mathrm{mod}\,4
.

(2)

\sum_{t=0}^{n}t\equiv(n+1)\,\mathrm{mod}\,2
.

Proof.

Since the sum of four consecutive integers is even, we may take the remainder of
n
modulo
4
. By a direct computation, we see that
n\in\{2,3\}
satisfies the condition, but
n\in\{0,1\}
does not.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
n\geq 0
and
d>0
be integers. Then

(5.4)

\mathrm{Hom}(\det(T_{X}),\det(\mathcal{V}))=\omega_{\pi}^{\otimes(n+1)(n/2-1)}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}((n+1)(d-1)-3).

Proof.

Since
\pi
is a smooth map, the exact sequence
[
Har77
, Proposition II.8.11]
is exact also on the left, thus

\omega_{X}=\omega_{\pi}\otimes\pi^{*}\omega_{G}.

By Equations (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) it follows:

\omega_{X}=\omega_{\pi}\otimes(\omega_{\pi}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(2))^{\otimes-n-2}=\omega_{\pi}^{\otimes-n-1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-2n-4).

On the other hand, using Equation (
5.1
)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(\det(T_{X}),\det(\mathcal{V}))

\displaystyle=\det(T_{X})^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(n+d(n+1))\otimes\omega_{\pi}^{\otimes n(n+1)/2}

\displaystyle=\omega_{\pi}^{\otimes n(n+1)/2-n-1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(n+d(n+1)-2n-4)

\displaystyle=\omega_{\pi}^{\otimes n(n+1)/2-n-1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}((n+1)(d-1)-3).

as stated in Equation (
5.4
).
∎

Finally, we can proceed with the proof of the proposition.

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

By Equation (
5.4
),
\mathcal{V}
is relatively orientable if and only if

\omega_{\pi}^{\otimes(n+1)(n/2-1)}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}((n+1)(d-1)-3)

is the square of a line bundle. Since
\omega_{\pi}
and
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
generate
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
(for example, by (
3.2
)), we need that
(n+1)(n/2-1)
and
(n+1)(d-1)-3
to be even.
By Lemma
5.3
, it is easy to see that it must be
d
even and
n\equiv 2\,\mathrm{mod}\,4
.
∎

So, we know exactly when
\mathcal{V}
is relatively orientable. In particular, there is a well-defined Euler class. Now, we define explicitly a relative orientation and we will prove that it is compatible with the coordinates given in Definition
3.6
.
For each open set
U_{i\alpha}
, let us consider the following map
\lambda_{i\alpha}\colon\det{{T_{X}}|}_{U_{i\alpha}}\rightarrow\det{{\mathcal{V}}|}_{U_{i\alpha}}
where

\lambda_{i\alpha}((-1)^{\alpha}\cdot\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{\alpha})=\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right)^{n+d(n+1)}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right)^{-n(n+1)}\left(\frac{x_{i}y_{\alpha}}{x_{0}y_{1}-x_{1}y_{0}}\right)^{n(n+1)/2}.

Combining Proposition
3.7
and
5.1
, we see that

(5.5)

\lambda_{i\alpha}((-1)^{\alpha}\cdot\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{\alpha})=\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\right)^{N}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{j}}\right)^{-2M}\left(\frac{x_{i}y_{\alpha}}{x_{j}y_{\beta}}\right)^{M}\lambda_{j\beta}((-1)^{\alpha}\cdot\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{\alpha})

where we denoted

It follows that there exists a well-defined morphism

\psi\colon\omega_{\pi}^{\otimes M}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(N)\rightarrow\mathrm{Hom}(\det(T_{X}),\det(\mathcal{V})),

such that

(5.6)

{\psi|}_{U_{i\alpha}}\left(\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right)^{-2M}\left(\frac{x_{i}y_{\alpha}}{x_{0}y_{1}-x_{1}y_{0}}\right)^{M}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right)^{N}\right)=\lambda_{i\alpha}.

We conclude this section by proving that
\psi
induces a trivialization compatible with the twisted coordinates, as in Definition (
2.6
).

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
n
and
d
be positive integers such that

,
d\equiv 0\,\mathrm{mod}\,2
.
The local trivializations

\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right)^{n+d(n+1)}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right)^{-n(n+1)}\left(\frac{x_{i}y_{\alpha}}{x_{0}y_{1}-x_{1}y_{0}}\right)^{n(n+1)/2}

As we saw in Proposition

,

and

are even, so it makes sense to consider

. By construction,

maps the distinguished basis of the anticanonical bundle to a distinguished basis of

{\psi|}_{U_{i\alpha}}\left(\left(\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right)^{-M}\left(\frac{x_{i}y_{\alpha}}{x_{0}y_{1}-x_{1}y_{0}}\right)^{M/2}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right)^{N/2}\right)^{\otimes 2}\right)=\lambda_{i\alpha}.

∎

5.2.
Non-relatively orientable case

Let us assume that

, and that

is odd. So

is odd, and

cannot be relatively orientable. We show that, in this case,

is relatively orientable relative to the divisor

[

Since

is locally trivialized by

(5.7)

{\tilde{\psi}|}_{U_{i\alpha}}\left(\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right)^{-2M}\left(\frac{x_{i}y_{\alpha}}{x_{0}y_{1}-x_{1}y_{0}}\right)^{M}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right)^{N+1}\right)=\lambda_{i\alpha}.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
n
and
d
be positive integers such that
n\equiv 2\,\mathrm{mod}\,4
,
d\equiv 1\,\mathrm{mod}\,2
.
The local trivializations

\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right)^{n+d(n+1)}\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right)^{-n(n+1)}\left(\frac{x_{i}y_{\alpha}}{x_{0}y_{1}-x_{1}y_{0}}\right)^{n(n+1)/2}

of

are compatible with the Nisnevich coordinates
\{(U_{i\alpha},\tilde{\varphi}_{i\alpha})\}
and the relative orientation

.

Proof.

The canonical section

of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
is locally given by (the pullback of)

. By construction,

\lambda_{i\alpha}((-1)^{\alpha}\cdot\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{\alpha})\otimes\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right)\\

so by Equation (

\lambda_{i\alpha}((-1)^{\alpha}\cdot\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{\alpha})\otimes\left(\frac{x_{i}}{x_{0}}\right)\\

{{\tilde{\psi}}|}_{U_{j\beta}}

So

∎

6.

In this section we compute the Euler class of

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
n
and
d
be positive integers such that the following conditions are satisfied

•

n\equiv 2\,\mathrm{mod}\,4
,

•

d\equiv 0\,\mathrm{mod}\,2
.

The vector bundle
\mathcal{V}

is relatively orientable. Moreover, if

, the Euler class of
\mathcal{V}
is

(6.1)

e(\mathcal{V})=d\frac{n!}{2}\mathbb{H}.

Proof.

By Proposition

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mathrm{Tr}_{k(l)/k}\colon&k(l)&\longrightarrow&k\\
&\alpha+\beta a+\gamma d+\delta ad&\longmapsto&4\alpha-4\beta,\end{array}

defines a symmetric
\mathbb{Q}
-bilinear form. Using the eigenvectors

\left\{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}+d\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}-d\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},a\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}+ad\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},a\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}-ad\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\right\},

we see that the eigenvalues are
8
and
-8
, both with multiplicity two. Hence, the class of the bilinear form is
2\mathbb{H}\in\mathrm{GW}(\mathbb{Q})
. Finally,

\sum_{l}\mathrm{Tr}_{k(l)/k}(\mathcal{J}_{+}|_{l})=\mathbb{H}+2\mathbb{H}=3\mathbb{H}.

The case
Y_{-}
is very similar, the final sum in this case is:

\sum_{l}\mathrm{Tr}_{k(l)/k}(\mathcal{J}_{-}|_{l})=\langle 4\rangle+\langle-4\rangle+\mathbb{H}+\mathbb{H}=3\mathbb{H}.

These results agree with our main theorem.

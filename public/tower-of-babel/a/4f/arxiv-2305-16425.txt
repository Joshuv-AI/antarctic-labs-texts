Deformations and Cohomology of restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras in positive characteristic
Quentin Ehret, Abdenacer Makhlouf

Abstract
The main purpose of this paper is to study restricted formal deformations of restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras in positive characteristic p p . For p > 2 p>2 , we discuss the deformation theory and show that deformations are controlled by the restricted cohomology introduced by Evans and Fuchs. Furthermore, for p = 2 p=2 , we introduce a new cohomology complex and show that it fits with the deformation theory of restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras in characteristic 2. In particular, we study the structure and cohomology of restricted Heisenberg algebras.



Keywords:
Modular Lie algebra, restricted cohomology, restricted Lie-Rinehart algebra, deformation.

MSC classification:
17B50, 17B56, 16W25, 17B60.

Introduction

Lie-Rinehart algebras are an algebraic version of Lie algebroids. They were first introduced in characteristic 0 by several authors, including Palais (
[
PR61
]
) and Rinehart (
[
RG63
]
), and were later studied deeply by Huebschmann (
[
HJ90
]
,
[
HJ98
]
). A Lie-Rinehart algebra over a commutative ring
\K
is a pair
(A,L)
, where
A
is a commutative, associative
\K
-algebra and
L
is a Lie
\K
-algebra that acts on
A
as a module through a map called the anchor map, denoted
\rho:L\rightarrow\Der(A)
, where
\Der(A)
is the Lie algebra of derivations of
A
. This map must be both a Lie and
A
-module map satisfying a compatibility condition.

In positive characteristic, one needs additional structure to deal with Lie-Rinehart algebras. Let
A
be an associative algebra over a field of positive characteristic
p
. Then the Lie algebra of derivations
\Der(A)
is equipped with a map
D\mapsto D^{p}
, which takes values in
\Der(A)
. It is worth noticing that in characteristic 0, this map is generally not a derivation for
D\in\Der(A)
. This led to the definition of restricted Lie algebras, which are Lie algebras equipped with a non-linear maps called
p
-maps, denoted
(\cdot)^{[p]}:L\rightarrow L
, that satisfy certain compatibility conditions with respect to the Lie bracket and the additive structure of the underlying vector space. These objects were introduced by Jacobson (
[
JN37
]
,
[
JN41
]
) and later studied by Hochschild (
[
HG54
]
,
[
HG55.1
]
,
[
HG55.2
]
).

The positive characteristic version of Lie-Rinehart algebras, known as restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras, appeared implicitly in the work of Hochschild in the context of Galois-Jacobson theory for purely inseparable extensions of exponent 1 (
[
HG55.1
]
). They were later studied by several other authors, see (
[
RD00
]
,
[
DI12
]
,
[
SC15
]
,
[
SP16
]
). The deformation of restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras and the associated cohomology are closely related to those of restricted Lie algebras. Hochschild used the notion of restricted universal enveloping algebra to define the restricted cohomology groups of restricted Lie algebras and to establish a connection to the ordinary Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of Lie algebras through a six-term exact sequence (
[
HG54
]
). May also made contributions to this area (
[
MJP66
]
). However, the definition of restricted cohomology given by Hochschild is not well-suited for computation. Evans and Fuchs attempted to address this issue by providing a new free resolution of the ground field in terms of modules over the restricted enveloping algebra, but their approach only allows for complete computations of cohomology up to dimension
p
in the case where the restricted Lie algebra is abelian. In the non-abelian case, the bracket and non-linearity of the
p
-map make the problem much more difficult, and Evans and Fuchs only described the first and second restricted cohomology groups (
[
ET00
]
,
[
EF08
]
). This is sufficient for certain algebraic interpretations, but not enough to develop a complete theory of formal deformations of restricted Lie algebras in the vein of Gerstenhaber (
[
GM64
]
) and Nijenhuis-Richardson (
[
NR66
]
,
[
NR67
]
).

Many of the previously mentioned results do not hold in characteristic 2. Bouarroudj and his collaborators have done extensive work in this context (
[
BB18
]
,
[
BGL09
]
,
[
BLLS15
]
) and references therein. Recently, Bouarroudj and Makhlouf developed a complete cohomology complex for Hom-Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2, which are generalization of Lie superalgebras in characteristic 2, and provided several interesting algebraic interpretations, including the control of formal deformations (
[
BM22
]
).

The aim of this paper is to study cohomology and deformations of restricted Lie algebras in characteristic
p
for
p\leq 3
and
p=2
. It is organized as follows. In the first section, we review the basics of restricted Lie algebras in characteristic
p
, as well as some examples. We then consider cohomology and provide the first terms of the restricted cochain complex and the restricted differentials as defined in
[
EF08
]
. In Section 2, we review the definition of restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras and introduce the concept of restricted multiderivation for restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras. In Section 3, we develop a theory of formal deformations and show that it is controlled by the restricted cohomology. Then, in Section 4 we discuss restricted structures on the Heisenberg algebra of dimension 3. We show that there are (up to restricted isomorphism) three different restricted Heisenberg algebras, and for each of them we compute the second restricted cohomology group with adjoint coefficients. We also construct restricted Lie-Rinehart algebras on these restricted Heisenberg algebras and compute their deformations. Finally, in the last section focuses on the specific case of characteristic 2. We construct a new and complete complex of cochains that has no analogue in any characteristic other than 2, provide some algebraic interpretations, and show that this complex controls formal deformations.

Throughout the paper, “ordinary" shall be understood as “not restricted" and we assume that algebras are finite-dimensional.

Restricted Lie-Rinehart Algebras

Lie-Rinehart algebras over characteristic zero fields appear as algebraic analogs of Lie algebroids in differential geometry. Palais (
[
PR61
]
) and Rinehart (
[
RG63
]
) were among the first authors to consider those structures. For example, if
V
is a differential manifold, let

be the algebra of smooth functions on
V

L=Vect(V)
be the Lie algebra of vector fields on
V

(A,L)
carries a
Lie-Rinehart algebra structure. In positive characteristic, the Lie algebra is required to be
restricted and additional structure arise through interactions between the structure maps of the Lie-Rinehart algebra and the
p
-map of the restricted Lie algebra.

2.1
Definitions

In the following definition,
\F
is an arbitrary field. Let
A
be an
\F
-associative algebra, the space of derivations of

is defined by

\Der(A)=\{D:A\rightarrow A\text{ linear map such that }D(ab)=D(a)b+aD(b),\penalty\ a,b\in A\}.

Definition 2.1
.

A
Lie-Rinehart algebra
is a pair
(A,L)
, where

\bullet

L
is a Lie algebra over
\F
, with bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
;

\bullet

is an associative and commutative
\F
-algebra,

such that, for

and

•

There is an action
A\times L\longrightarrow L,\penalty\ (a,x)\longmapsto a\cdot x,
making

A
-module;

•

There is a map

, which is both a morphism of Lie algebras and a morphism of A-modules, called

;

•

There is a compatibility condition, also called

[x,a\cdot y]=\rho_{x}(a)\cdot y+a\cdot[x,y].

Example:
Let

be an associative unital algebra, and

.

.

\displaystyle=m\left(x,\w_{t}(y)\right)+tm_{1}(x,\w_{t}(y))+\sum_{i\geq 2}t^{i}m_{i}(x,\w_{t}(y))

\displaystyle=m\left(x,\w(y)\right)+tm(x,\w_{1}(y))+tm_{1}(x,\w(y))\mod(t^{2}).

\displaystyle m_{t}(m_{t}(\cdots m_{t}(x,\overset{p\text{ terms}}{\overbrace{y),y),\cdots,y}})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{i_{p}}\cdots\sum_{i_{1}}t^{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{p}}m_{i_{p}}\left(m_{i(p-1)}(\cdots(m_{i_{1}}(x,y),y),\cdots,y),y\right)

\displaystyle=m[x,y,\cdots,y]+t\sum_{\underset{\sharp\left\{k,\penalty\ i_{k}=1\right\}=1}{i_{k}=0\text{ or }1}}m_{i_{p}}\left(\cdots(m_{i_{1}}(x,y),y),\cdots,y),y\right)\mod(t^{2})

\displaystyle=m[x,y,\cdots,y]+t\sum_{i+j=p-1}m\left[m_{1}(m[x,\overset{j}{\overbrace{y,\cdots,y}}],y),\overset{i}{\overbrace{y,\cdots,y}}\right]\mod(t^{2})

(22)

By collecting the coefficients of
t^{0}
and
t
, we recover the usual identity

and obtain the new identity

\left(m(x,\w_{1}(y))+m_{1}(x,\w(y))\right)=\sum_{i+j=p-1}(-1)^{i}y^{i}m_{1}([x,\overset{j}{\overbrace{y,\cdots,y}}],y),

(23)

which is equivalent to
\ind^{2}(m_{1},\w_{1})=0
. We conclude that
(m_{1},\w_{1})
is a 2-cocycle of the restricted cohomology.
∎

4
Restricted Heisenberg algebras

The origins of quantum mechanics lie in the groundbreaking idea of Heisenberg to consider the components of the position vector
\textbf{q}=(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})\in\R^{3}
and the momentum vector
\textbf{p}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})\in\R^{3}
of a particle at a given time
t
as operators on a Hilbert space. Those components have to satisfy the relations

[q_{j},q_{k}]=0,\penalty\ [p_{j},p_{k}]=0,\penalty\ [p_{j},q_{k}]=-i\hbar\delta_{j,k},

being the square root of
-1

and
\hbar

. Usually, one considers those commutation relations as defining relations of a Lie algebra of dimension
2n+1
by adding a central element
z
such that

[q_{j},q_{k}]=[p_{j},p_{k}]=[p_{j},z]=[q_{j},z]=0,\penalty\ [p_{j},q_{k}]=\delta_{j,k}z.

This
(2n+1)
-dimensional real Lie algebra is called the Heisenberg algebra. It can be seen as a central extension of the commutative algebra
\R^{2n}
by a copy of
\R
spanned by
z
. More background material can be found in
[
WP17
]
.

In this section, we investigate examples based on the Heisenberg Lie algebra of dimension
3
over fields of characteristic
p\geq 3
. We study the restricted structure of the Heisenberg algebra, then we give an explicit description of the second restricted cohomology spaces. Finally, we give an example of restricted Lie-Rinehart structure involving the restricted Heisenberg algebras and discuss its deformations.
Restricted

-nilpotent Heisenberg algebras have also been considered in
[
SU16
]
.

m^{n}_{t}+t^{n+1}m_{n+1}
satisfies the Jacobi identity. Now suppose that
\w^{n}_{t}+t^{n+1}\w_{n+1}
is a
2
-map with respect to
m^{n}_{t}+t^{n+1}m_{n+1}
. The following equation is then satisfied:

m_{t}^{n+1}\left(x,\w_{t}^{n+1}\right)=m_{t}^{n+1}\left(m_{t}^{n+1}(x,y),y\right),

(95)

where we have denoted for convenience
m_{t}^{n+1}=m^{n}_{t}+t^{n+1}m_{n+1}
and
\w^{n+1}_{t}=\w^{n}_{t}+t^{n+1}\w_{n+1}
.
By expanding Equation (
95
), we obtain

\sum_{q=0}^{n+1}t^{q}\sum_{i=0}^{q}m_{i}(x,\omega_{q-i}(y))=\sum_{q=0}^{n+1}t^{q}\sum_{i=0}^{q}m_{i}(m_{q-i}(x,y),y).

(96)

Collecting coefficient of
t^{n+1}
, one obtains in particular

\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}m_{i}(x,\omega_{n+1-i}(y))=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}t^{q}\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}m_{i}(m_{n+1-i}(x,y),y),

which can be written

\displaystyle m(x,\omega_{n+1}(y))+m_{n+1}(x,\w(y))

\displaystyle+m(m_{n+1}(x,y),y)+m_{n+1}(m(x,y),y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(m_{i}(x,\omega_{n+1-i}(y))+m_{i}(m_{n+1-i}(x,y),y)\right).

We conclude that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(m_{i}(x,\omega_{n+1-i}(y))+m_{i}(m_{n+1-i}(x,y),y)\right)=\delta_{*_{2}}^{2}\omega_{n+1}(x,y).

∎

5.3.2

In this section, we will construct an infinitesimal deformation of a restricted Lie-Rinehart algebra in characteristic
2
, whose underlying Lie algebra is
(\h,z^{*})
. Over a field
\F
of characteristic
2
, there are three non-isomorphic commutative unital associative algebras of dimension
2
, given by

•

A_{0}=\text{Span}_{\F}\{e_{1},e_{2}\},
with
e_{1}
the unit and
e_{2}e_{2}=0;

•

A_{1}=\text{Span}_{\F}\{e_{1},e_{2}\},
with
e_{1}
the unit and
e_{2}e_{2}=e_{1};

•

A_{2}=\text{Span}_{\F}\{e_{1},e_{2}\},
with
e_{1}
the unit and

The 2-dimensional classification of unital associative algebras over over fields of characteristic not
2
algebras reduces to
A_{0}
and
A_{2}
. It turns out that in characteristic 2 the algebra

is not isomorphic to
A_{0}
and

Lemma 5.30
.

Let
L=(\h,z^{*})
and
A=A_{1}
. Suppose that
A
acts on

a\cdot u=u,\penalty\ \forall a\in A,\penalty\ \forall u\in L

is given by the anchor map
\rho=0

Proof.

Since
\rho:L\rightarrow\Der(L)
must be a Lie morphism, we have
\rho(z)=0
. The condition of
A
-linearity gives
\rho(x)(e_{2})=\lambda(e_{1}+e_{2})
and
\rho(y)(e_{2})=\mu(e_{1}+e_{2}),\penalty\ \forall\lambda,\mu\in\F
.
Also
\rho
must be a restricted morphism. Therefore, have
0=\rho(x)^{2}(e_{2})=\lambda\rho(x)(e_{2})=\lambda^{2}(e_{1}+e_{2})
. Hence, we have
\lambda=0
. The same computation with
y
instead of
x
gives
\mu=0
.
∎

Let us choose a restricted multiderivation
(m_{1},\w_{1})
given by
m_{1}(x,y)=y,\penalty\ m_{1}(x,z)=m_{1}(y,z)=0
and
\w_{1}(x)=x,\penalty\ \w_{1}(y)=\w_{1}(z)=0.
It is not difficult to see that if we set
\sigma_{1}(x)(e_{2})=\sigma_{1}(y)(e_{2})=e_{1}+e_{2},\penalty\ \sigma_{t}(z)(e_{2})=0
, then
\sigma_{1}
is a symbol map compatible with

that gives rise to an infinitesimal restricted formal deformation. We therefore obtain the deformation

\displaystyle m_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=z+ty,\penalty\ m_{t}(x,z)=m_{t}(y,z)=0;

\displaystyle\w_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=tx,\penalty\ \w_{t}(y)=0,\penalty\ \w_{t}(x)=z;

\displaystyle\sigma_{t}(x)(e_{2})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{t}(y)(e_{2})=e_{1}+e_{2},\penalty\ \sigma_{t}(z)(e_{2})=0.

It is easy to verify that the quantities

and

2

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the partition theory over totally real number fields. Let K K be a totally real number field. A partition of a totally positive algebraic integer δ \delta over K K is λ = ( λ 1 , λ 2 , … , λ r ) \lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{r}) for some totally positive integers λ i \lambda_{i} such that δ = λ 1 + λ 2 + ⋯ + λ r \delta=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{r} . We find an identity to explain the number of partitions of δ \delta whose parts do not belong to a given ideal 𝔞 \mathfrak{a} . We obtain a generalization of the Euler-Glaisher Theorem over totally real number fields as a corollary. We also prove that the number of solutions to the equation δ = x 1 + 2 ​ x 2 + ⋯ + n ​ x n \delta=x_{1}+2x_{2}+\cdots+nx_{n} with x i x_{i} totally positive or 0 0 is equal to that of chain partitions of δ \delta . A chain partition of δ \delta is a partition λ = ( λ 1 , λ 2 , … , λ r ) \lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{r}) of δ \delta such that λ i + 1 − λ i \lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{i} is totally positive or 0 0 .



1.
introduction

The theory of partitions of a natural number is an interesting area in additive number theory with many beautiful identities, theorems and conjectures. It is initiated by Leibniz and the first important results are those of Euler. It is famous that Ramanujan made this area more plentiful by suggesting some astounding conjectures which disclose the magic of natural numbers.

A partition
\lambda
of a positive rational integer
n

k
-tuple
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{k})
of positive integers
\lambda_{i}
(

of
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, the partition number

q
-series identities, especially defined by infinite products by these miscellaneous partition numbers is a fundamental problem in partition theory. The identity

\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{n}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p(n)q^{n}

is a starting point of partition theory. Although the partition theory has been developed in various ways, most of the current results are acquired for positive rational integers. In 1950 Rademacher

]

[

]

\sum_{\delta\in\mathcal{O}^{+}}p(^{\prime\prime}S^{\prime\prime}(\leq d-1),\delta)q^{\delta}=\prod_{\delta\in S}(\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}q^{i\delta})
, we have
p(^{\prime\prime}\mathcal{O}^{+}\setminus\mathfrak{a}^{\prime\prime},\delta)=p(^{\prime\prime}S^{\prime\prime}(\leq d-1),\delta)
for all
\delta\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
.
∎

5.
chain partitions

The difference between two distinct parts of a partition of a natural number is also a natural number or
0
. However, in totally real number fields, the difference between some two parts of a partition of a totally positive algebraic integer may be neither totally positive nor
0
. We use the notation
\alpha\succeq 0
if and only if
\alpha
is a totally positive integer or
0
. A partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{r})
of a totally positive algebraic integer
\delta
over
K
is a
\textit{c}hain~partition
if and only if
\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{i}\succeq 0
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,r-1
. Let
p(\succeq,\delta)
be the number of chain partitions of
\delta
. Here, we define
p(\succeq,m,\delta)
as the number of chain partitions of
\delta
with at most
m
parts.

Theorem 5.1
.

The number of chain partitions
p(\succeq,m,\delta)
of
\delta
with at most
m
parts is equal to that of solutions to
\delta=x_{1}+2x_{2}+\cdots+mx_{m}
with
x_{i}\succeq 0
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. As a consequence we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\cup\{0\}}p(\succeq,\delta)q^{\delta}=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\cup\{0\}}q^{m\alpha}\right).

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\cup\{0\}}c_{\gamma}q^{\gamma}=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\alpha_{1}\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\cup\{0\}}1q^{\alpha_{1}}\right)\left(\sum_{\alpha_{2}\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\cup\{0\}}1q^{2\alpha_{2}}\right)\cdots\left(\sum_{\alpha_{m}\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\cup\{0\}}1q^{m\alpha_{m}}\right).

Then
c_{\gamma}=\sum_{\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}+\cdots+m\alpha_{m}=\gamma}1
, and thus,
c_{\gamma}
is the number of solutions to
\delta=x_{1}+2x_{2}+\cdots+mx_{m}
with
x_{i}\succeq 0
. Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{r})
be a chain partition of
\delta
with
r\leq m
. Let
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}=\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}=\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i-1}
for all
2\leq i\leq r
. Since
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\cup\{0\}
and

\displaystyle\delta=

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\lambda_{1}+(r-1)(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})+(r-2)(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{2})+\cdots+1(\lambda_{r}-\lambda_{r-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0+0+\cdots+0+r\lambda_{1}^{\prime}+(r-1)\lambda_{2}^{\prime}+\cdots+1\lambda_{r}^{\prime},

we have
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}\neq 0
and
(\lambda_{r}^{\prime},\lambda_{r-1}^{\prime},\ldots,\lambda_{1}^{\prime},0,0,\ldots,0)
is a solution to
\delta=x_{1}+2x_{2}+\cdots+mx_{m}
with
x_{i}\succeq 0
. Note that the number of
0
after
\lambda_{1}^{\prime}
is
m-r
. Let
(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{m})
be a solution to
\delta=x_{1}+2x_{2}+\cdots+mx_{m}
with
x_{i}\succeq 0
. Let
\mu_{i}^{\prime}=\sum_{k=i}^{r}\mu_{k}
for all
1\leq i\leq r
where
r
is the largest number such that
\mu_{r}\neq 0
for
1\leq r\leq m
. Since
\mu_{i}^{\prime}-\mu_{i+1}^{\prime}=\mu_{i}\succeq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq r-1
and
\delta=\mu_{1}+2\mu_{2}+\cdots+r\mu_{r}=\mu_{1}^{\prime}+\mu_{2}^{\prime}+\cdots+\mu_{r}^{\prime}
, we can see
(\mu_{r}^{\prime},\mu_{r-1}^{\prime},\ldots,\mu_{1}^{\prime})
is a chain partition of
\delta
. Let

\displaystyle\phi(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{r})=(\lambda_{r}-\lambda_{r-1},\lambda_{r-1}-\lambda_{r-2},\ldots,\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1},\lambda_{1},0,0,\ldots,0)

and

\displaystyle\psi(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{m})=\left(\mu_{r},\sum_{k=r-1}^{r}\mu_{k},\sum_{k=r-2}^{r}\mu_{k},\ldots,\sum_{k=1}^{r}\mu_{k}\right)

where
r
is the largest number such that
\mu_{r}\neq 0
for
1\leq r\leq m
. Since

\displaystyle\psi(\phi(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{r}))=

\displaystyle\psi(\lambda_{r}-\lambda_{r-1},\lambda_{r-1}-\lambda_{r-2},\ldots,\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1},\lambda_{1},0,0,\ldots,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\lambda_{1},(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})+\lambda_{1},\sum_{k=2}^{3}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k-1})+\lambda_{1},\ldots,\sum_{k=2}^{r}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k-1})+\lambda_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{r})

and

\displaystyle\phi(\psi(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{m}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi\left(\mu_{r},\sum_{k=r-1}^{r}\mu_{k},\sum_{k=r-2}^{r}\mu_{k},\ldots,\sum_{k=1}^{r}\mu_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{r}\mu_{k}-\sum_{k=2}^{r}\mu_{k},\sum_{k=2}^{r}\mu_{k}-\sum_{k=3}^{r}\mu_{k},\ldots,\sum_{k=r-1}^{r}\mu_{k}-\sum_{k=r}^{r}\mu_{k},\mu_{r},0,0,\ldots,0\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{r},0,0,\ldots,0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{m}),

p(\succeq,m,\delta)
equals the number of solutions to
\delta=x_{1}+2x_{2}+\cdots+mx_{m}
with
x_{i}\succeq 0
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\cup\{0\}}p(\succeq,m,\delta)q^{\delta}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\cup\{0\}}q^{i\alpha}\right).

Clearly we have
\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}p(\succeq,m,\delta)=p(\succeq,\delta)
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\cup\{0\}}p(\succeq,\delta)q^{\delta}=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\cup\{0\}}q^{m\alpha}\right).

∎

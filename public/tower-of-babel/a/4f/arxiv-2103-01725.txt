1.
Introduction

1.1.

The KZ equations were introduced in
[
KZ
]
as the differential equations satisfied by
conformal blocks on sphere in the Wess-Zumino-Witten model of conformal field theory.
The solutions of the KZ equations in the form of multidimensional hypergeometric integrals
were constructed more than 30 years ago,
see
[
SV1
]
.
The KZ equations and the hypergeometric solutions
are related to many subjects in algebra, representation theory,
theory of integrable systems, enumerative geometry.

The polynomial
solutions of the KZ equations over the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

of a prime number
p
of elements were constructed relatively recently
in
[
SV2
]
, see also
[
V4
]
-
[
V8
]
,
[
RV1
,
RV2
]
.
These solutions were called the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions.
The general problem is to understand relations between the hypergeometric solutions
of the KZ equations over
{\mathbb{C}}
and the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions and observe how the
remarkable properties of hypergeometric solutions are reflected in the properties
of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions.
For example, the
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions inherit some determinant properties of
the hypergeometric solutions and some Selberg integral properties, see
[
V8
,
RV1
,
RV2
]
.

This program is in the first stages, where we consider essential examples and
study the corresponding
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-hypergeometric solutions by direct methods.

In this paper we consider the differential KZ equations over
{\mathbb{C}}
in the case, when the hypergeometric
solutions are one-dimensional hyperelliptic integrals of genus
g
.
In this case the space of solutions of the differential KZ equations is a
2g
-dimensional complex vector space.
We also consider the same differential
equations modulo
p^{s}
, where
p
is an odd prime number and
s
is
a positive integer, and over the field
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
of
p
-adic
numbers.

We give a construction of polynomial solutions of the
differential KZ equations modulo

p^{s}
for positive integers
s
. We call such solutions the
p^{s}
-hypergeometric solutions.

This construction is a straightforward modification of the construction in

[
SV2
]
of polynomial solutions modulo
p
.

In this paper we consider the space
\mathcal{M}_{p^{s}}
of all
p^{s}
-hypergeometric solutions, which is a module over the ring
of polynomial quasi-constants modulo
p^{s}
. We study basic properties
of
\mathcal{M}_{p^{s}}
, in particular its natural filtration, and dependence of
\mathcal{M}_{p^{s}}
on
s
.

We show that the
p
-adic limit of
\mathcal{M}_{p^{s}}
as
s\to\infty
gives us a

g
-dimensional vector space of solutions of the differential KZ equations over
the field
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. The solutions over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
are power series at a
certain asymptotic zone of the KZ equations. This is the main result of the paper, see
Lemma
9.5
and Theorem
10.7
.

1.2.

In the appendix written jointly with Steven Sperber we consider
all six asymptotic zones of the KZ equations in the special case
g=1
of elliptic integrals.
It turns out that in this case the
p
-adic limit of

\mathcal{M}_{p^{s}}
as
s\to\infty
gives us a one-dimensional space of solutions over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
at every
asymptotic zone. We apply Dwork’s theory
of the classical hypergeometric function over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
and show that
our germs of solutions over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
defined at different asymptotic zones analytically continue into
a single global invariant line subbundle of the associated KZ connection. Notice that the corresponding KZ connection
over
{\mathbb{C}}
does not have proper nontrivial invariant subbundles, and therefore our invariant line subbundle is
a new feature of the KZ equations over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.

Following Dwork we show that our line subbundle is spanned
at any point of the base by the germs of all solutions of the KZ equations bounded in their discs of convergence.
This statement gives a definition of the line subbundle independent of asymptotic zones and analytic continuation.

Also in the appendix we follow Dwork and describe the Frobenius transformations of solutions of the KZ equations for
g=1
.
Using these Frobenius transformations we recover the unit roots of the zeta functions of the elliptic curves defined by
the affine equations

y^{2}=\beta\,x(x-1)(x-\alpha)
over the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Here
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times},\alpha\neq 1
.
Notice that the same elliptic curves considered over
{\mathbb{C}}
are used
to construct the complex holomorphic solutions of the KZ equations for
g=1
.

In the end of Section
A.10
we argue that the KZ equations for
g=1
contain
more arithmetic information than the associated hypergeometric differential equation (
1.2
) for the hypergeometric function

I(z)
in (
1.1
), studied in
[
Dw
]
.

1.3.

Our
p
-adic limit of
\mathcal{M}_{p^{s}}
as
s\to\infty

is similar to the
p
-adic limit in the following classical example, see
[
Ig
,
Ma
,
Cl
,
BV1
]
.
Consider the elliptic integral

(1.1)

\displaystyle I(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x(x-1)(x-z)}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{-1/2}{k}^{2}z^{k}\,.

It satisfies the hypergeometric differential equation

(1.2)

\displaystyle z(1-z)I^{\prime\prime}+(1-2z)I^{\prime}-(1/4)I=0.

The coefficients of the power series
I(z)
are
p
-adic integers and the power series

I(z)
converges
p
-adically for

|z|_{p}<1
, where
|z|_{p}
is the
p
-adic norm of
z\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.
One may show that for any positive integer

the polynomial

(1.3)

\displaystyle I_{(p^{s}-1)/2}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{(p^{s}-1)/2}\binom{(p^{s}-1)/2}{k}^{2}z^{k}\,

is a solution of the differential equation (
1.2
) modulo
p^{s}
.
Thus we get a sequence

of polynomials with integer coefficients,

) modulo

p^{s}
, and the

-adic limit of the sequence, as

\infty

is the

-adic power series solution

of the differential equation (

).

The
p^{s}
-hypergeometric solutions of our differential KZ equations are analogs of the polynomials

I_{(p^{s}-1)/2}(z)
with an analogous
p
-adic limit.
The difference is that the construction of the

p^{s}
-hypergeometric solutions does not indicate the
analogous
p
-adic limiting solutions
I(z)
,
and the analogous limiting
p
-adic power series solutions

can be discovered only after rewriting

-hypergeometric solutions in a suitable asymptotic zone of the differential KZ equations.

In the simplest example of our differential KZ equations,
the
p
-adic solution is the 3-vector

(1.4)

\displaystyle I(u_{1},u_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{1}^{-3/2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{-1/2}{k+1}\binom{-3/2}{k}\Big(\frac{k+1}{-1/2-k},1,\frac{-1/2}{-1/2-k}\Big)u_{2}^{k}\,,

while the sequence
(I_{(p^{s}-3)/2}(u_{1},u_{2}))_{s=1}^{\infty}

of the
p^{s}
-hypergeometric solutions modulo
p^{s}
of the same equations
is given by the formula

(1.5)

\displaystyle I_{(p^{s}-3)/2}(u_{1},u_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u_{1}^{(p^{s}-3)/2}\sum_{k=0}^{(p^{s}-3)/2}\binom{(p^{s}-1)/2}{k+1}\binom{(p^{s}-3)/2}{k}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{k+1}{(p^{s}-1)/2-k},1,\frac{(p^{s}-1)/2}{(p^{s}-1)/2-k}\Big)u_{2}^{k}\,,

see Section
9.7
.

The sum
\sum_{k=0}^{(p^{s}-3)/2}
in (
1.5
)
is the truncation of the sum
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
in (
1.4
), similar to what happens in (
1.1
) and (
1.3

A new feature appears when we compare the prefactor

and the sequence of prefactors

the sequence of prefactors

-adically to the prefactor

multiplied by a Teichmuller constant on a suitable domain in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

where
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is the ring of
p
-adic integers,

10.3
and Theorem

2.
KZ equations

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a simple Lie algebra with an invariant scalar product.
The
Casimir element
is

\displaystyle\Omega={\sum}_{i}\,h_{i}\otimes h_{i}\ \ \in\ {\mathfrak{g}}\otimes{\mathfrak{g}},

where
(h_{i})\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
is an orthonormal basis.
Let
V=\otimes_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
be
a tensor product of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-modules,
\kappa\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
a nonzero number.
The
differential KZ equations
is the system of differential equations on a
V
-valued function
I(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial I}{\partial z_{i}}\ =\ \frac{1}{\kappa}\,{\sum}_{j\neq i}\,\frac{\Omega_{i,j}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}I,\qquad i=1,\dots,n,

where
\Omega_{i,j}:V\to V
is the Casimir operator acting in the
i
th and
j
th tensor factors,
see
[
KZ
,
EFK
]
.

This system is a system of Fuchsian first order
linear differential equations.
The equations are defined on the complement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
to the union of all diagonal hyperplanes.

The object of our discussion is the following particular case.

Let
p

be an odd prime number,

n=2g+1
an odd positive integer,
p>n\geqslant 2
.

We study the system of equations
for a column vector
I(z)=(I_{1}(z)
, …,
I_{n}(z))
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}\frac{\partial I}{\partial z_{i}}\ =\ {\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\Omega_{ij}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}I,\quad i=1,\dots,n,\qquad I_{1}(z)+\dots+I_{n}(z)=0,

where
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
,
the
n\times n
-matrices
\Omega_{ij}
have the form:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Omega_{ij}\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}&\vdots^{i}&&\vdots^{j}&\\
{\scriptstyle i}\cdots&{-1}&\cdots&1&\cdots\\
&\vdots&&\vdots&\\
{\scriptstyle j}\cdots&1&\cdots&-1&\cdots\\
&\vdots&&\vdots&\end{pmatrix},

and all other entries are zero.
This joint system of
differential and
algebraic equations
will be called the
system of KZ equations
in this paper.

System (
2.1
) is the system of the differential KZ equations with parameter
\kappa=2
associated with the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
and the subspace of singular vectors of weight
2g-1
of the tensor power

({\mathbb{C}}^{2})^{\otimes{(2g+1)}}
of two-dimensional irreducible
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}
-modules, up to a gauge transformation, see
this example in
[
V3
, Section 1.1]
.

We consider system (
2.1
) over the field
{\mathbb{C}}
. We
also consider the same system of equations modulo
p^{s}

and over the field
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
of
p
-adic numbers.

3.
Complex solutions

Consider the
master function

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Phi(x,z)=\prod_{a=1}^{n}(x-z_{a})^{-1/2}

and the column
{n}
-vector of hyperelliptic integrals

(3.2)

\displaystyle I^{(\gamma)}(z)=(I_{1}(z),\dots,I_{n}(z)),\qquad I_{j}=\int\frac{\Phi(x,z)}{x-z_{j}}\,dx\,.

The integrals
I_{j}
, are over an element
\gamma
of the first homology group
of the algebraic curve with affine equation

\displaystyle y^{2}=(x-z_{1})\dots(x-z_{n})\,.

Starting from such
\gamma
, chosen for given values

\{z_{1},\dots,z_{n}\}
, the vector
I^{(\gamma)}(z)
can
be analytically continued as a multivalued holomorphic function of
z
to the complement in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
of the union of the
diagonal hyperplanes
z_{i}=z_{j}
,
i\neq j
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The vector
I^{(\gamma)}(z)
is a solution of system (
2.1
).

Theorem
3.1
is a classical statement.
Much more general algebraic and differential equations satisfied by analogous multidimensional hypergeometric integrals were considered in
[
SV1
]
. Theorem
3.1
is discussed as an example in
[
V3
, Section 1.1]
.

Proof.

The theorem follows from Stokes’ theorem and the two identities:

(3.3)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\,\Big(\frac{\Phi(x,z)}{x-z_{1}}+\dots+\frac{\Phi(x,z)}{x-z_{n}}\Big)\,=\,\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial x}(x,z)\,,

(3.4)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{i}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\Omega_{i,j}}{z_{i}-z_{j}}\Big)\Big(\frac{\Phi(x,z)}{x-z_{1}},\dots,\frac{\Phi(x,z)}{x-z_{n}}\Big)=\frac{\partial\Psi^{i}}{\partial x}(x,z),

where
\Psi^{i}(x,z)
is the column
n
-vector
(0,\dots,0,-\frac{\Phi(x,z)}{x-z_{i}},0,\dots,0)
with
the nonzero element at the
i
-th place.
∎

Theorem 3.2

(
[
V1
, Formula (1.3)]
)
.

All solutions of system (
2.1
) have this form.
Namely, the complex vector space of solutions of the form (
3.2
) is
n-1
-dimensional.

This theorem follows from the determinant formula for multidimensional hypergeometric integrals in
[
V1
]
, in particular,
from
[
V1
, Formula (1.3)]
.

4.
Solutions modulo

4.1.
Leading terms

For a ring
R
denote
R[z]=R[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]
. For a positive integer
t
denote
R[z^{p^{t}}]=R[z_{1}^{p^{t}},\dots,z_{n}^{p^{t}}]
.

Consider the lexicographical ordering of monomials

z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}
,
so we have
z_{1}>\dots>z_{n}
and so on.
For a nonzero polynomial

f(z)=\sum_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}a_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}

let
f_{\mathfrak{l}}(z)
be the nonzero summand

a_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}
with the largest monomial

z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}
.
We call
f_{\mathfrak{l}}(z)

the
leading term
of
f(z)
, the coefficient

a_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}
– the
leading coefficient
, the
monomial

z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}
– the
leading monomial
.

Let
s
be a positive integer.
An element
a\in{{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{s}{\mathbb{Z}}}
has a unique presentation
a=a_{0}+a_{1}p+\dots+a_{s-1}p^{s-1}
,
where

a_{i}\in\{0,\dots,p-1\}
. An element
a
is invertible if and only if
a_{0}\neq 0
.

Denote
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}={\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Let
\pi_{s}
denote the homomorphisms

{\mathbb{Z}}\to{{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{s}{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
{\mathbb{Z}}[z]\to({{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{s}{\mathbb{Z}}})[z]
,
{\mathbb{Z}}[z]^{n}\to({{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{s}{\mathbb{Z}}})[z]^{n}

and for
t<s
let
\pi_{s,t}
denote the homomorphisms

{{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{s}{\mathbb{Z}}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{t}{\mathbb{Z}}
,
({{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{s}{\mathbb{Z}}})[z]\to({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{t}{\mathbb{Z}})[z]
,
({{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{s}{\mathbb{Z}}})[z]^{n}\to({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{t}{\mathbb{Z}})[z]^{n}
.

4.2.
Quasi-constants

We say that a polynomial
f(z)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z]
is a
quasi-constant modulo

p^{s}
if

\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{i}}\in p^{s}{\mathbb{Z}}[z]
for
i=1,\dots,n
.
The quasi-constants modulo
p^{s}
form a subring
of
{\mathbb{Z}}[z]
denoted by
{\mathbb{Z}}[z]_{p^{s}}
. For example
(z_{1}+z_{2})^{p^{s}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z]_{p^{s}}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

As a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module the ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[z]_{p^{s}}
is spanned by the monomials

p^{s-t}z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}
, where
t
is the maximal
integer such that
t\leqslant s
and
p^{t}
divides every
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
.

For example,
z_{1}^{lp^{s}}
and
p^{s-1}z_{1}^{p}z_{2}^{p^{2}}

are such monomials.

Proof.

Let
f(z)=\sum_{d}c_{d}z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z]_{p^{s}}
. We show that each summand

c_{d}z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}
is a multiple of a monomial of Lemma
4.1
. Indeed, let

c_{d^{0}}z_{1}^{d_{1}^{0}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}^{0}}
be the leading term of
f(z)
.
Then all first partial derivatives of it must lie in
p^{s}{\mathbb{Z}}[z]

Hence
c_{d^{0}}\in p^{s-t}{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
t
is the maximal integer such that
t\leqslant s
and
p^{t}

divides every
d_{1}^{0},\dots,d_{n}^{0}
. Subtracting
the leading term from
f(z)
and repeating the reasoning we prove the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f(z)
be a quasi-constant modulo
p^{s}
and
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
.
Then
p^{t}f(z)
is a quasi-constant modulo
p^{r}
for any
1\leqslant r\leqslant s+t
.
∎

The rings of quasi-constants form a decreasing filtration,

{\mathbb{Z}}[z]_{p}\supset{\mathbb{Z}}[z]_{p^{2}}\supset\dots\,.

4.3.
Solutions of system (
2.1
) modulo
p^{s}

We say that a column

-vector
I(z)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z]^{n}

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

be a solution of system (

) modulo

.

(i)

Let

. Then

is a solution of system (

) modulo

10.1.
Teichmuller representatives

For
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
there exists the unique solution
\omega(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
of the equation

\omega(t)^{p}=\omega(t)
that is congruent to
t
modulo
p
. The element
\omega(t)

is called the
Teichmuller representative
. It also can be defined by
\omega(t)=\lim_{s\to\infty}t^{p^{s}}
.
The
Teichmuller character
is the homomorphism

\displaystyle{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{\times},\qquad\alpha\mapsto\omega(\alpha),\qquad\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}.

For
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
,
r>0
, define the disc

\displaystyle D_{\alpha,r}=\{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\ |\ |t-\omega(\alpha)|_{p}<r\}.

The space
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is the disjoint union of the discs
D_{\alpha,1}
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.
The function
\omega:{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
,
t\mapsto\omega(t)
, is a locally constant function
equal to
\omega(\alpha)
on the disc
D_{\alpha,1}
.

For a subset
S\subset{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and a function
f:S\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
define the norm

\displaystyle\parallel f\parallel=\sup_{t\in S}|f(t)|_{p}\,.

Lemma 10.1
.

For any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
the sequence of polynomial functions

(x^{p^{s}})_{s=1}^{\infty}
uniformly converges on
D_{\alpha,1}
to the
constant function
\omega(\alpha)
.

Proof.

We use the “fundamental inequality” from
[
Ro
, II.4.3]
: if
|t|_{p}\leqslant 1
, then

|(1+t)^{p^{s}}-1|_{p}\leqslant|t|_{p}\cdot\max(|t|_{p},1/p)^{s}
. Now
let
t\in D_{\alpha,1}
. Then
t^{p-1}=1+t_{1}
,
|t_{1}|_{p}\leqslant 1/p
.
We have
|t^{p^{s+1}}-t^{p^{s}}|_{p}=|t^{p^{s}}|_{p}|t^{(p-1)p^{s}}-1|_{p}

\leqslant|(1+t_{1})^{p^{s}}-1|_{p}\leqslant 1/p^{s+1}
.

For positive integers
s_{1},s_{2}
and
t\in D_{\alpha,1}
we have

|t^{p^{s_{1}+s_{2}}}-t^{p^{s_{1}}}|_{p}=|t^{p^{s_{1}+s_{2}}}-t^{p^{s_{1}+s_{2}-1}}+t^{p^{s_{1}+s_{2}-1}}+\dots+t^{p^{s_{1}+1}}-t^{p^{s_{1}}}|_{p}\leqslant 1/p^{s_{1}+1}
. Hence
the sequence
(x^{p^{s}})_{s=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence.

For
t\in D_{0,1}
, we have
|t^{p^{s}}|_{p}\leqslant 1/p^{s}
.
For
t_{1},t_{2}\in D_{\alpha,1},

\alpha\neq 0
, we have
t_{1}/t_{2}=1+t
with

|t|_{p}\leqslant 1/p
and

|t_{1}^{p^{s}}-t_{2}^{p^{s}}|_{p}=|(1+t)^{p^{s}}-1|_{p}\leqslant 1/p^{s+1}
.
The lemma is proved.
∎

For
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

.

on the disc

.

Let

corresponds to the chosen

10.2.
Approximation of binomial coefficients

It is known that
\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{a},\binom{-\frac{3}{2}}{a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

.

Lemma 10.3
.

Let
l_{1}\geqslant 0,\,l_{2}\geqslant 0

be integers. Then
there exists an integer
s_{0}\geqslant 0,
such that
for any integer
s\geqslant s_{0}
and any integer
a
with

\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}-l_{1}\geqslant l_{2}+a\geqslant 0
we have

(10.1)

\displaystyle\phantom{aaa}\left|\binom{-\frac{1}{2}-l_{1}}{l_{2}+a}-\binom{\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}-l_{1}}{l_{2}+a}\right|_{p}\leqslant 1/p^{s-d-a}\,,\qquad\operatorname{where}\quad d=l_{1}+l_{2}-1/2.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\binom{-\frac{1}{2}-l_{1}}{l_{2}+a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(-2)^{l_{2}+a}(l_{2}+a)!}\,\prod_{k=1}^{l_{2}+a}(2(l_{1}+k)-1),

\displaystyle\binom{\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}-l_{1}}{l_{2}+a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(-2)^{l_{2}+a}(l_{2}+a)!}\,\prod_{k=1}^{l_{2}+a}(2(l_{1}+k)-1-p^{s})\,.

The
p
-adic norm of
\binom{-\frac{1}{2}-l_{1}}{l_{2}+a}
is
\leqslant 1
.
The difference
\binom{-\frac{1}{2}-l_{1}}{l_{2}+a}-\binom{\frac{p^{s}-1}{2}-l_{1}}{l_{2}+a}
is the sum of products

\frac{1}{(-2)^{l_{2}+a}(l_{2}+a)!}\,\prod_{k=1}^{l_{2}+a}(2(l_{1}+k)-1)
in each of which at least one of the factors

2(l_{1}+k)-1
is replaced with
-p^{s}
. We prove that even one such replacement
implies that this summand of the difference has
p
-adic norm

\leqslant 1/p^{s+1/2-l_{1}-l_{2}-a}
.

Indeed, let
2(l_{1}+k)-1=bp^{c}
,

. We have

2(l_{1}+k)-1\leqslant 2(l_{1}+l_{2}+a)-1\leqslant 2(l_{1}+\frac{p^{s}-1}{2})-1
.
Hence
bp^{c}\leqslant 2l_{1}-2+p^{s}
. Hence for any
s

,
and the replacement of

-p^{s}
makes the norm of that summand
\leqslant 1/p^{s-c}
.

We also have
bp^{c}\leqslant 2(l_{1}+l_{2}+a)-1

c\leqslant p^{c-1}<\frac{p^{c}}{2}\leqslant\frac{bp^{c}}{2}\leqslant a+l_{1}+l_{2}-\frac{1}{2}
.
This shows that each summand
has the
p
-adic norm
1/\leqslant p^{s-c}\leqslant 1/p^{s+1/2-l_{1}-l_{2}-a}
.
The lemma is proved.
∎

A.1.
Dwork on Legendre family

A.1.1.

Consider the Legendre family of elliptic curves

defined by the affine equation

\displaystyle y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-\lambda).

Let

be a family of 1-cycles on

flat under the Gauss-Manin connection. Then the function

(A.1)

\displaystyle{h}^{(\gamma)}(\lambda)=\int_{\gamma}\frac{dx}{y}

satisfies the hypergeometric differential equation

(A.2)

\displaystyle\lambda(1-\lambda)h^{\prime\prime}+(1-2\lambda)h^{\prime}-(1/4)h=0.

All solutions of this equation are obtained in this way.
One of the solutions
h^{(\delta_{1})}(\lambda)
, for a suitable
\delta_{1}
, equals

(A.3)

\displaystyle F(\lambda)={}_{2}F_{1}\Big(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2};1;\lambda\Big)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{-1/2}{k}^{2}\lambda^{k}\,,

where

A.1.2.

If
h(\lambda)
is the elliptic integral in (
A.1
), then its derivative is

(A.4)

\displaystyle h^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma}\frac{dx}{(x-\lambda)y}.

A.1.3.

Equation
(
A.2
)
can be written as a system of first order linear differential equations for column 2-vectors

I=(h,h^{\prime})
,

(A.5)

\displaystyle\frac{dI}{d\lambda}=B(\lambda)I,\qquad B(\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
\frac{1}{4\lambda(1-\lambda)}&\frac{2\lambda-1}{\lambda(1-\lambda)}\end{pmatrix}.

A.1.4.

In
[

]

Dwork considers equation (

) over the field

of

p
-adic numbers and studies analytic properties of the solution

A.1.5.

Let

be an open subset. A function

is called analytic
at

if

can be presented by a power series

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}(\lambda-\alpha)^{k}
with nonzero radius of convergence.

A function

is called analytic
on

if
f
is the uniform limit on
D
of a sequence of rational function
regular on
D
. In that case

is analytic at every point of
D
.

For

let

be an analytic function on some domain

.
Assume that
D_{i}\cap D_{i+1}\neq\emptyset
for

,
and
f_{i}=f_{i+1}
on
D_{i}\cap D_{i+1}
, then we say that the collection of functions

defines an analytic element on
\cup_{i=1}^{N}D_{i}
. Cf.
[
Dw
, Section 0]

A.1.6.

Igusa noted in

Define

A.10.
Eigenvalue
\omega(\beta^{(p-1)/2})(-1)^{(p-1)/2}f(\omega(\alpha))

Theorem A.6
.

The number
\omega(\beta^{(p-1)/2})(-1)^{(p-1)/2}f(\omega(\alpha))

is the unit root of the elliptic curve

defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
by the affine equation

(A.51)

\displaystyle w^{2}=\beta\,v(v-1)(v-\alpha).

Proof.

Assume that
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}
is a square,
\beta=\gamma^{2}
for some
\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.
Then on the one hand the change of the variable
\tilde{w}=w/\gamma
makes

isomorphic to
E(\alpha,1)
.
On the other hand
\beta^{(p-1)/2}=1
and

\omega(\beta^{(p-1)/2})(-1)^{(p-1)/2}f(\omega(\alpha))=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}f(\omega(\alpha))
, where
the last number is the unit root of the elliptic curve
E(\alpha,1)
by
[
Dw
]
.

Assume that
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}^{\times}
is not a square. Denote by
N_{1,\beta}
the number of points on

E(\alpha,\beta)
. Then

(A.52)

\displaystyle N_{1,1}+N_{1,\beta}=4+4+2(p-3)=2p+2.

Indeed the number
4+4
corresponds to the points
(0,0)
,
(0,1)
,
(0,\alpha)
,
\infty
on

E(\alpha,\beta)
and on
E(\alpha,1)
. The number

corresponds to
p-3

elements of
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}-\{0,1,\alpha\}
. Namely if

v_{0}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}-\{0,1,\alpha\}
,  then exactly one of the two elements

\beta v_{0}(v_{0}-1)(v_{0}-\alpha),\,v_{0}(v_{0}-1)(v_{0}-\alpha)
is a square in
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}

and exactly one of the two elliptic curves
has two points over
v=v_{0}
, while the other curve does not have points over
v_{0}
.

It is known that the zeta function of the curve
E(\alpha,\beta)
has the form

(A.53)

\displaystyle\exp\Big(\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\frac{N_{s,\beta}}{s}T^{s}\Big)=\frac{(1-R_{\beta}T)(1-(p/R_{\beta})T)}{(1-T)(1-pT)}.

Here

is the number points on

considered over the field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p^{s}}
, while
the number

has
|R_{\beta}|_{p}=1
and is called the unit root, for example see
[
Mo
]
.
Equation (
A.53
) implies that for any
s
we have
N_{s,\beta}=1+p^{s}-R_{\beta}^{s}-(p/R_{\beta})^{s}
.
In particular for
s=1
we have

(A.54)

\displaystyle N_{1,\beta}=1+p-R_{\beta}-p/R_{\beta}.

From (
A.52
) and (
A.54
) we obtain

\displaystyle 0=R_{\beta}+p/R_{\beta}+R_{1}+p/R_{1}=(R_{\beta}+R_{1})(1+p/R_{\beta}R_{1}).

Since the second factor is nonzero we conclude that

R_{\beta}=-R_{1}
. By
[
Dw
]
we have
R_{1}=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}f(\omega(\alpha))
.
Hence
R_{\beta}=-\,(-1)^{(p-1)/2}f(\omega(\alpha))=\omega(\beta^{(p-1)/2})(-1)^{(p-1)/2}f(\omega(\alpha))
. The theorem is proved.
∎

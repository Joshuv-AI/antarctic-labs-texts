Arithmetic differential geometry in the arithmetic PDE setting, I: connections
Alexandru Buium, Lance Edward Miller

Abstract
. This is the first in a series on papers developing an arithmetic PDE analogue of Riemannian geometry. The role of partial derivatives is played by Fermat quotient operations with respect to several Frobenius elements in the absolute Galois group of a p p -adic field. Existence and uniqueness of geodesics and of Levi-Civita and Chern connections are proved in this context. In a sequel to this paper a theory of arithmetic Riemannian curvature and characteristic classes will be developed.

1.
Introduction

The present paper is Part 1 of a series of papers; its sequel
[
BMb
]
will be referred to as Part 2. The aim of this series is to develop an arithmetic PDE version of the arithmetic ODE theory in
[
BD16
,
Bu17
,
Bu19
]
.

In
[
BD16
,
Bu17
,
Bu19
]
an arithmetic analogue of differential geometry was developed in which the role of functions is played by integers in
p
-adic fields and the role of
derivations was played by
p
-derivations described by Fermat quotients where
p
is a prime integer. Analogues of connections and curvature were introduced and analogues of familiar connections, for example Chern connections and Levi-Civita connection, were studied. These connections turned out to be
not
flat, i.e., the integers have some ‘inherent curvature.’
For a fixed prime
p
this theory was developed in loc.cit in an unramified setting, over the completed valuation ring of the maximal unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. A drawback, however, is that this ring has a unique
p
-derivation, and hence the corresponding metrics/connections should be viewed as inherently of ‘cohomogeneity one’ which made the theory essentially an arithmetic ODE theory.

The aim of the present paper and its sequel is to enhance the arithmetic ODE theory in
[
BD16
,
Bu17
,
Bu19
]
to an arithmetic PDE theory. Our approach involves a complete reformulation of the foundations and admits a much wider natural scope. It not only resolves the cohomgeneity one limitation, but also resolves a fundamental problem described in
[
Bu17
]
; specifically we are able to introduce here a theory of arithmetic geodesics not present in the ODE formulation.

The critical idea that allows for this framework to move past the ODE setting, is precisely that one must
not
restrict considerations to an unramified context. Indeed, the consideration of the (arbitrarily) ramified case allows one to implement the framework of purely arithmetic PDEs as introduced in
[
BMa
]
.

The present paper is essentially self contained and can be viewed as a natural next step for the theory initiated in
[
Bu95
]
and developed, in particular, in
[
Bu05
,
Bu17
,
BMa
]
,
as explained in what follows. On the other hand, all of this work can be viewed as an instance of the general effort to discover new analogies between numbers and functions; see, in particular, Manin’s paper
[
Man13
]
.

1.2.
Overview of previous work

The first step in this circle of ideas was taken in
[
Bu95
]
where
a theory of arithmetic ODEs was initiated. In
[
Bu95
]
derivation operators were replaced by
p
-derivation operators

[

,
Bu96
,

,

In particular, in
[
Bu95
,
Bu97

, analogues of the classical Manin maps
[
Man63
]
were constructed and arithmetic analogues of Manin’s Theorem of the Kernel were proved. On the other hand this theory provided some striking arithmetic analogues of Hamiltonian systems appearing in mathematical physics
[

,
BP17
,
BP18
,
Bu20

.

In
[
Bu17
]
, this theory was adapted to give an arithmetic version of differential geometry. Among other things certain ODE versions of the PDEs appearing in classical Riemannian geometry related to Chern and Levi-Civita connections were developed. In particular, the arithmetic Christoffel symbols were shown to enjoy a surprising relationship with, and could be viewed as matrix analogues of, Legendre symbols.

The framework of
[
Bu05
]
for arithmetic ODEs considers only ‘unramified solutions’ i.e., solutions with coordinates in the completion
R
of the maximum unramified extension of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Subsequently, the

[

,

]
allowed one to add ‘ramified solutions’ to the main arithmetic ODEs of the theory, i.e., solutions in the ring of integers

p
-adic field

can be attained. Similarly, the arithmetic differential geometry described in
[
Bu17
,
Bu19
]
is restricted to unramified solutions and the present article and its sequel introduce in this theory ramified solutions and purely arithmetic PDEs.

To contrast, a theory of arithmetic PDEs with
2

[
BS10a
,
BS10b
]

[

We explain now a bit more what we mean by ‘purely arithmetic’ PDEs. The starting point in
[

is that one should envision not one but several arithmetic directions at every given prime. This comes from the fact that the absolute Galois group

The main idea in

2.3.
Complements on arithmetic ODEs

Recall that
R
denotes the completed ring of integers of the maximal unramified extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and has a unique Frobenius lift
\phi
. Set

a^{\phi^{s}}:=\phi^{s}(a)=:a^{p^{s}}+p\delta^{(s)}a,\ \ \ a\in R.

Lemma 2.19
.

For
a,b\in R
with
a\equiv b
mod
p^{\nu}
,
\nu\geq 1
, we have

a\equiv b\ \ \textup{mod}\ \ p^{\nu+1}\ \ \Leftrightarrow\ \ \delta^{(s)}a\equiv\delta^{(s)}b\ \ \textup{mod}\ \ p^{\nu}.

Proof
. Write
a=b+p^{\nu}c
with
c\in R
. We have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\delta^{(s)}a&\equiv&\delta^{(s)}b+\delta^{(s)}(p^{\nu}c)\ \ \textup{mod}\ \ p^{\nu}\\
&\equiv&\delta^{(s)}b+p^{\nu-1}c^{p^{s}}\ \ \textup{mod}\ \ p^{\nu}\end{array}

hence
c\in pR
if and only if
\delta^{(s)}a\equiv\delta^{(s)}b
mod
p^{\nu}
.∎

We have the following existence and uniqueness result for first order ODEs.

Proposition 2.20
.

Let

y,z
be two
m
-tuples of indeterminates, consider restricted power series

F_{1},\ldots,F_{m},G\in R[y,z]^{\widehat{\ }},\ \ G\neq 0,

and set

f:=:F/G:=(F_{1}/G,\ldots,F_{m}/G).

Let
u^{(0)}\in R^{m}
be such that

G\left(u^{(0)},(u^{(0)})^{(p^{s})}\right)\not\equiv 0\ \ \textup{mod}\ \ p.

Then there exists a unique

u\in R^{m}
such that
u\equiv u^{(0)}
mod
p
and

(2.3)

\delta^{(s)}u=f\left(u,u^{\phi^{s}}\right).

Proof
.
For the existence part it is enough to construct a sequence
(u^{(\nu)})_{\nu\geq 1}
of vectors
u^{(\nu)}\in R^{n}
such that

\begin{array}[]{rcllll}u^{(\nu+1)}&\equiv&u^{(\nu)}&\text{mod}&p^{\nu+1}&\textup{for}\ \ \nu\geq 0,\\
&&\\
\delta^{(s)}u^{(\nu)}&\equiv&f(u^{(\nu)},(u^{(\nu)})^{\phi^{s}})&\textup{mod}&p^{\nu}&\textup{for}\ \ \nu\geq 0.\end{array}

Assume
u^{(\nu)}
was constructed and set
u^{(\nu+1)}=u^{(\nu)}+p^{\nu+1}b
with
b\in R^{n}
to be determined.
We have

f\left(u^{(\nu+1)},(u^{(\nu+1)})^{\phi^{s}}\right)\equiv f\left(u^{(\nu)},(u^{(\nu)})^{\phi^{s}}\right)\ \ \textup{mod}\ \ p^{\nu+1}.

On the other hand we have

\delta^{(s)}u^{(\nu)}=f\left(u^{(\nu)},(u^{(\nu)})^{\phi^{s}}\right)+p^{\nu}c

for some
c\in R^{n}
so we get

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\delta^{(s)}u_{\nu+1}&=&\delta^{(s)}u^{(\nu)}+\delta^{(s)}(p^{\nu+1}b)+C^{(s)}(u^{(\nu)},p^{\nu+1}b)\\
&&\\
&\equiv&f(u^{(\nu)},(u^{(\nu)})^{\phi^{s}})+p^{\nu}c+p^{\nu}b^{(p^{s})}\ \ \textup{mod}\ \ p^{\nu+1}\end{array}

and it is enough to take

such that

which is possible because the field

is perfect.

For the uniqueness part
if
u
and
\tilde{u}
are two vectors satisfying our equation and congruent to

mod
p
then, using Lemma
2.19
, one proves by induction on

that

mod
p^{\nu}
.∎

Corollary 2.21

The map
\delta^{(s)}:pR\rightarrow R
is bijective.

We denote the inverse of

Finally we would like to ‘evaluate’ solutions to ODEs at various points.

Remark 2.24

Using

&=&c^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\sum_{ij}v_{i}^{\phi^{s}}w_{j}^{\phi^{s}}\left(\delta_{kj}+\pi\Gamma_{ij}^{k(s)}(1)\right)-\sum_{ij}v_{i}^{\phi^{s}}w_{j}^{p^{s}}\delta_{kj}\right)\right),\ \ \textup{by (\ref{la11})}\\

&=&c^{*}\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\left(\left(\sum_{i}v_{i}^{\phi^{s}}\right)w_{k}^{\phi^{s}}+\pi\sum_{ij}v_{i}^{\phi^{s}}w_{j}^{\phi^{s}}\Gamma_{ij}^{k(s)}(1)-\left(\sum_{i}v_{i}^{\phi^{s}}\right)w_{k}^{p^{s}}\right)\right)\\
&&\\
&=&c^{*}\left(\frac{p}{\pi}\left(\sum_{i}v_{i}^{\phi^{s}}\right)\delta^{(s)}w_{k}+\sum_{ij}v_{i}^{\phi^{s}}w_{j}^{\phi^{s}}\Gamma_{ij}^{k(s)}(1)\right)\\
&&\\
&=&c^{*}\left(\frac{p}{\pi}\right)\left(\sum_{i}v_{i}^{\phi^{s}}\right)\delta^{(s)}w_{k}+\sum_{ij}c^{*}\left(\Gamma_{ij}^{k(s)}(1)\right)v_{i}^{\phi^{s}}w_{j}^{\phi^{s}}.\end{array}

Definition 3.11

Remark 3.18
.

Let
\Delta^{(s)G}
be a
\pi

Using the concept of evaluation maps

1) For every non-degenerate curve

along

from

to

\textup{par}_{c,P}:k^{n}\rightarrow k^{n},

as follows. Let

then set

Note that, by Proposition

is induced by a morphism of schemes over

\textup{par}_{c,P}:\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}.

Furthermore let

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(\zeta w_{i})(P_{0})&=&\zeta w_{i}(P_{0}),\\
&&\\
(\zeta w_{i})(P)&=&P^{(s)}\{\zeta w_{i}\}(P_{0})\\
&&\\
&=&\sum_{d=D_{0}}^{D}P_{(d)}^{(s)}\{\zeta w_{i}\}(P_{0})\\

Remark 3.21

The following trivial fact will play a role later. Let

Remark 3.23

Assume

Assume furthermore that we are given a

Lemma 3.26

If

Proof

Theorem 3.30

Assume

Assume

Theorem 3.35
.

(Abstract Arithmetic Levi-Civita Theorem)
Let
S
be a ring in which
2
is invertible,
P
an ideal in
S
,
\pi\in S
a regular element in
S
whose image in
S/P
is also a regular element, and assume
S
is
\pi
-adically complete. Let
n\geq 1
and
m\geq 0
be integers. Consider matrices
A_{i},B\in\textup{GL}_{n}(S)
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Moreover consider an
n
-tuple
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
of
n\times n
matrix indeterminates
y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
and a tuple
L:=(L^{1},\ldots,L^{n})
,
L^{k}\in\textup{Mat}_{n}(\widehat{S[y]})
. Assume the following properties:

1)
A_{i}
and
B
are symmetric, i.e.,
(A_{i})_{jk}=(A_{i})_{kj}
,
B_{jk}=B_{kj}
;

2)
L^{k}
are antisymmetric, i.e.,
L_{ij}^{k}+L_{ij}^{k}=0
;

3)
A_{i}\equiv B
mod
\pi
;

4)
L^{k}\equiv 0
mod
(\pi^{m},P)
and
A_{i}\equiv B
mod
(\pi^{m+1},P)
.

Then there exists a unique
n
-tuple
\Lambda=(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n})
of matrices
\Lambda_{i}\in\textup{GL}_{n}(S)

satisfying the following properties:

a)
\Lambda_{i}\equiv 1
mod
\pi
;

b)
\Lambda_{i}^{t}A_{i}\Lambda_{i}=B
;

c)
(\Lambda_{i}-1)_{kj}-(\Lambda_{j}-1)_{ki}=\pi L_{ij}^{k}(\Lambda)
;

d)
\Lambda_{i}\equiv 1
mod
(\pi^{m+1},P)
.

Moreover if

C_{i}:=\frac{1}{\pi}(B-A_{i}),\ \ L_{ijk}:=\sum_{l}L_{ij}^{l}B_{kl}

then the following congruences hold in
S
:

\frac{1}{\pi}(B(\Lambda_{i}-1))_{kj}\equiv\frac{1}{2}((C_{i})_{jk}+(C_{j})_{ik}-(C_{k})_{ij})+\frac{1}{2}(L_{kij}(1)+L_{ijk}(1)-L_{jki}(1))\ \ \textup{mod}\ \ \pi.

For
m=0
condition (4) above is a consequence of condition (3); and similarly condition
(d)
is a consequence of condition
(a)
. So for
m=0
conditions (4) and
(d)
can be deleted from the statement of the Theorem.

Proof
. The proof runs along the lines of
[
Bu19
, Thm. 2.15]
but it is
more
involved
and it contains new tricks (related to the matrices
F_{i}
below).

We will construct by induction a sequence of
n
-tuples

(3.29)

\Lambda^{\nu}=(\Lambda_{1}^{\nu},\ldots,\Lambda_{n}^{\nu}),\ \ \nu\geq 1

of
n\times n
matrices with entries in
S

such that for
\nu\geq 1
and all
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}

the following properties hold:

i)
\Lambda_{i}^{1}=1
;

ii)
\Lambda_{i}^{\nu+1}\equiv\Lambda_{i}^{\nu}
mod
\pi^{\nu}
;

iii)
\Lambda_{i}^{\nu t}A_{i}\Lambda_{i}^{\nu}\equiv B
mod
\pi^{\nu}
;

iv)
(B(\Lambda_{i}^{\nu}-1))_{kj}-(B(\Lambda_{j}^{\nu}-1))_{ki}\equiv\pi L_{ijk}(\Lambda^{\nu})\ \ \textup{mod}\ \ \pi^{\nu}
;

v)
\Lambda_{i}^{\nu}\equiv 1
mod
(\pi^{m+1},P)
for
\nu\leq m+1
.

Here the superscript
\nu
of
\Lambda_{i}
(and of other matrices to be considered below) is an index (not an exponent).
Taking
\Lambda_{i}
to be the limit of
\Lambda_{i}^{\nu}
as
\nu\rightarrow\infty
and using Remark
3.21
ends the proof.

To construct our sequence of
n
-tuples (
3.29
) define the
n
-tuple for
\nu=1
by condition (i) and note that conditions (ii,) (iii), (iv) are then automatically satisfied.
Assume now the
n
-tuple (
3.29
)
was constructed for some
\nu\geq 1
and seek the
n
-tuple (
3.29
)
corresponding to
\nu+1
in the form

(3.30)

\Lambda_{i}^{\nu+1}=\Lambda_{i}^{\nu}+\pi^{\nu}Z^{\nu}_{i}

where
Z^{\nu}_{i}
needs to be determined.
Write

(3.31)

\Lambda_{i}^{\nu t}A_{i}\Lambda_{i}^{\nu}=B-\pi^{\nu}C^{\nu}_{i},

(3.32)

(B(\Lambda_{i}^{\nu}-1))_{kj}-(B(\Lambda_{j}^{\nu}-1))_{ki}=\pi L_{ijk}(\Lambda^{\nu})-\pi^{\nu}L_{ijk}^{\nu},

for matrices
C^{\nu}_{i}
and
L_{ijk}^{\nu}
with entries in
S
.
In particular,

(3.33)

L_{ijk}^{1}=L_{ijk}(1).

Also, using the fact that
\pi
is a regular element in
S
and
S/P
, we have that

(3.34)

C^{\nu}_{i}\equiv L_{ijk}^{\nu}\equiv 0\ \ \textup{mod}\ \ \pi^{m+1-\nu}\ \ \textup{if}\ \ \nu\leq m+1.

By (
3.30
) and (
3.31
) we get

(3.35)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Lambda_{i}^{(\nu+1)t}A_{i}\Lambda_{i}^{\nu+1}&\equiv&\Lambda_{i}^{\nu t}A_{i}\Lambda_{i}^{\nu}+\pi^{\nu}(\Lambda_{i}^{\nu t}A_{i}Z^{\nu}_{i}+Z^{\nu t}_{i}A_{i}\Lambda_{i}^{\nu})\ \ \text{mod}\ \ \pi^{\nu+1}\\
&&\\
&\equiv&B+\pi^{\nu}(-C^{\nu}_{i}+BZ^{\nu}_{i}+Z^{\nu t}_{i}B)\ \ \text{mod}\ \ \pi^{\nu+1}.\end{array}

Now

A_{i}^{t}=A_{i}
and
B^{t}=B
so

C_{i}^{\nu t}=C^{\nu}_{i}.

Hence
(C^{\nu}_{i})_{jk}=(C^{\nu}_{i})_{kj}
.
Define the matrices

D^{\nu}_{i}=((D^{\nu}_{i})_{jk})

by setting

(3.36)

(D^{\nu}_{i})_{jk}:=\frac{1}{2}((C^{\nu}_{i})_{jk}+(C^{\nu}_{j})_{ik}-(C^{\nu}_{k})_{ij}).

Then

(3.37)

(D^{\nu}_{i})_{jk}=(D^{\nu}_{j})_{ik}

and
(D^{\nu}_{i})_{jk}+(D^{\nu}_{i})_{kj}=(C^{\nu}_{i})_{jk}.

So we have:

(3.38)

D^{\nu}_{i}+D_{i}^{\nu t}=C^{\nu}_{i}.

On the other hand, by (
3.32
), we get
L^{\nu}_{ijk}+L^{\nu}_{jik}=0.

Define the matrices

F^{\nu}_{i}=((F^{\nu}_{i})_{jk})

by setting

(F^{\nu}_{i})_{jk}:=\frac{1}{2}(L^{\nu}_{kij}+L^{\nu}_{ijk}-L^{\nu}_{jki}).

So by (
3.33
),

(3.39)

(F^{1}_{i})_{jk}=\frac{1}{2}(L_{kij}(1)+L_{ijk}(1)-L_{jki}(1)).

For all
\nu\geq 1
one has

(3.40)

(F^{\nu}_{i})_{jk}-(F^{\nu}_{j})_{ik}=L^{\nu}_{ijk},

(F^{\nu}_{i})_{jk}+(F^{\nu}_{i})_{kj}=0;

hence

(3.41)

F^{\nu}_{i}+F^{\nu t}_{i}=0.

Setting

(3.42)

Z^{\nu}_{i}:=B^{-1}(D^{\nu t}_{i}+F_{i}^{\nu t})

we have

(3.43)

BZ^{\nu}_{i}=D^{\nu t}_{i}+F_{i}^{\nu t},

Z^{\nu t}_{i}B=D^{\nu}_{i}+F_{i}^{\nu}.

Moreover using (
3.34
) we have

Z^{\nu}_{i}\equiv 0\ \ \textup{mod}\ \ (\pi^{m+1},P)\ \ \textup{for}\ \ \nu\leq m+1,

hence, by (
3.30
), we have

\Lambda^{\nu+1}_{i}\equiv 1\ \ \textup{mod}\ \ (\pi^{m+1},P)\ \ \textup{for}\ \ \nu\leq m+1,

which shows the condition (v) holds for
\Lambda_{i}^{\nu+1}
.
On the other hand, using (
3.38
) and (
3.41
), we get

(3.44)

BZ^{\nu}_{i}+Z^{\nu t}_{i}B=C_{i}^{\nu}.

By (
3.35
) and (
3.44
) we get

\Lambda_{i}^{(\nu+1)t}A_{i}\Lambda_{i}^{\nu+1}\equiv B\ \ \ \text{mod}\ \ \pi^{\nu+1}

and hence condition (iii) holds for
\Lambda_{i}^{\nu+1}
mod
\pi^{\nu+1}
.

To check condition (iv) for
\nu
replaced by
\nu+1
note that
for

\lambda_{ikj}:=(B(\Lambda_{i}^{\nu+1}-1))_{kj}

we have

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\lambda_{ikj}&=&(B(\Lambda_{i}^{\nu}+\pi^{\nu}Z^{\nu}_{i}-1))_{kj}\ \ \textup{cf}.(\ref{alpha})\\
&&\\
&=&(B(\Lambda^{\nu}_{i}-1))_{kj}+\pi^{\nu}(D_{i}^{\nu})_{jk}+\pi^{\nu}(F^{\nu}_{i})_{jk}\ \ \textup{cf}.\ (\ref{dorra1})\\
&&\\
&=&(B(\Lambda^{\nu}_{i}-1))_{kj}+\pi^{\nu}(D_{j}^{\nu})_{ik}+\pi^{\nu}(F^{\nu}_{j})_{ik}+\pi^{\nu}L_{ijk}^{\nu}\ \ \textup{cf}.\ (\ref{66}),(\ref{777})\\
&&\\
&=&(B(\Lambda^{\nu}_{j}-1))_{ki}+\pi^{\nu}(D_{j}^{\nu})_{ik}+\pi^{\nu}(F^{\nu}_{j})_{ik}+\pi L_{ijk}(\Lambda^{\nu})\ \ \textup{cf}.\ (\ref{betabeta2})\\
&&\\
&\equiv&(B(\Lambda^{\nu}_{j}-1))_{ki}+\pi^{\nu}(D_{j}^{\nu})_{ik}+\pi^{\nu}(F^{\nu}_{j})_{ik}+\pi L_{ijk}(\Lambda^{\nu+1})\ \ \textup{mod}\ \ \pi^{\nu+1}\\
&&\\
&=&(B(\Lambda_{j}^{\nu}+\pi^{\nu}Z^{\nu}_{j}-1))_{ki}+\pi L_{ijk}(\Lambda^{\nu+1})\ \ \textup{cf}.\ (\ref{dorra1})\\
&&\\
&=&\lambda_{jki}+\pi L_{ijk}(\Lambda^{\nu+1})\ \ \textup{cf}.\ (\ref{alpha}).\end{array}

So condition (iv) holds for
\nu
replaced by
\nu+1
. This ends the proof of the existence part of our theorem.

We next prove the uniqueness part
in our theorem.
Assume we have two tuples which we denote by

\Lambda=(\Lambda_{1},...,\Lambda_{n})\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \Lambda^{\prime}=(\Lambda^{\prime}_{1},...,\Lambda^{\prime}_{n})

such that for al
i,j,k
we have

\Lambda_{i}\equiv\Lambda_{i}^{\prime}\equiv 1\ \ \textup{mod}\ \ \pi,

(3.45)

\Lambda_{i}^{t}A_{i}\Lambda_{i}=(\Lambda_{i}^{\prime})^{t}A_{i}\Lambda^{\prime}_{i}=B,

(3.46)

(B(\Lambda_{i}-1))_{kj}-(B(\Lambda_{j}-1))_{ki}=\pi L_{ijk}(\Lambda),

(3.47)

(B(\Lambda^{\prime}_{i}-1))_{kj}-(B(\Lambda^{\prime}_{j}-1))_{ki}=\pi L_{ijk}(\Lambda^{\prime}).

We will prove that for all
\nu\geq 1
we have

(3.48)

\Lambda_{i}\equiv\Lambda^{\prime}_{i}\ \ \ \text{mod}\ \ \pi^{\nu}

and this will end the proof of the uniqueness part. We proceed
by induction on
\nu
. The case
\nu=1
is clear. Assume
3.48
holds for some
\nu\geq 1
and write

(3.49)

\Lambda^{\prime}_{i}=\Lambda_{i}+\pi^{\nu}Z^{\nu}_{i}.

From (
3.45
) we get

B\equiv B+\pi^{\nu}A_{i}Z^{\nu}_{i}+\pi^{\nu}Z^{\nu t}_{i}A_{i}\equiv B+\pi^{\nu}BZ^{\nu}_{i}+\pi^{\nu}Z^{\nu t}_{i}B\ \ \ \text{mod}\ \ \ \pi^{\nu+1},

hence, setting

E_{i}:=Z^{\nu t}_{i}B=:((E_{i})_{jk})

we get

E_{i}+E_{i}^{t}\equiv 0\ \text{mod}\ \pi,

hence

(3.50)

(E_{i})_{jk}\equiv-(E_{i})_{kj}\ \ \ \text{mod}\ \ \ \pi.

On the other hand, subtracting (
3.46
) from (
3.47
) and using (
3.49
)
we get

(3.51)

(E_{i})_{jk}\equiv(E_{j})_{ik}\ \ \textup{mod}\ \ \pi.

Combining
3.50
and
3.51
we get

(3.52)

(E_{i})_{jk}\equiv(E_{j})_{ik}\equiv-(E_{j})_{ki}\ \ \ \text{mod}\ \ \ \pi.

Applying
3.52
three times we get

(E_{i})_{jk}\equiv-(E_{j})_{ki}\equiv(E_{k})_{ij}\equiv-(E_{i})_{jk}\ \ \ \text{mod}\ \ \pi,

hence
2(E_{i})_{jk}\equiv 0\ \text{mod}\ \pi.

Since
2
is invertible in
S
we get
(E_{i})_{jk}\equiv 0\ \text{mod}\ \pi

hence

Z^{\nu}_{i}\equiv 0\ \text{mod}\ \pi,
which implies

\Lambda^{\prime}_{i}\equiv\Lambda_{i}\ \text{mod}\ \pi^{\nu+1},

and our induction step is proved.

Finally the last equation in the theorem follows by noting that

\Lambda_{i}\equiv\Lambda_{i}^{(2)}=1+\pi Z_{i}^{1}\ \ \textup{mod}\ \ \pi^{2}

and using equations (
3.42
), (
3.31
), (
3.36
), (
3.39
).
∎

3.7.
Arithmetic Chern connection

We now turn our attention to defining Chern connections. Set
G=\textup{GL}_{N/R_{\pi}}
, with
N
not necessarily equal to
n
,
and recall that for
s\geq 1
we have the trivial
\pi
-connection
((\delta_{1,0}^{(s)})^{G},\ldots,(\delta_{n,0}^{(s)})^{G})
of degree
s
on
G
. We consider its associated higher
\pi
-Frobenius lifts by
((\phi_{1,0}^{(s)})^{G},\ldots,(\phi_{n,0}^{(s)})^{G})
. We fix a metric
q^{(s)}\in G(R_{\pi})^{\textup{sym}}
, which we view as an analogue of a metric on a vector bundle of rank
N
on a manifold of dimension
n
. Generalizing
[
Bu17
, Def. 4.25]
, which corresponds to the case
n=s=1
and
\pi=p
, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 3.36
.

Let
(\phi_{i}^{(s)})^{G}
be a higher
\pi
-Frobenius lift of degree
s
on
\mathcal{A}
extending
\phi_{i}^{(s)}
on
R_{\pi}
. We say that
(\phi_{i}^{(s)})^{G}
is
\mathcal{B}_{q^{(s)}}
-symmetric
provided the following diagram commutes

(3.53)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{A}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{B}_{q^{(s)}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}\\
\mathcal{B}_{q^{(s)}}\downarrow&&\downarrow(\phi_{i}^{(s)})^{G}\otimes(\phi_{i,0}^{(s)})^{G}\\
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\phi_{i,0}^{(s)})^{G}\otimes(\phi_{i}^{(s)})^{G}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\mathcal{A}\end{array}

where
\mathcal{B}_{q^{(s)}}
is defined by
\mathcal{B}_{q^{(s)}}(x)=x_{1}^{t}q^{(s)}x_{2}
and
x
,
x_{1}:=x\otimes 1
,
x_{2}:=1\otimes x
are the corresponding
N\times N
matrices of indeterminates.

Remark 3.37
.

As in
[
BD16
]
or
[
Bu17
]
and with notation as in (
3.23
)
the commutativity of the diagram (
3.53
) is equivalent to the equality

A_{i}^{(s)}\Lambda_{i}^{(s)}=\Lambda_{i}^{(s)t}A_{i}^{(s)}.

We give the PDE version of Chern connections in
[
Bu17
]
.

Theorem 3.38
.

For
q^{(s)}\in\textup{GL}_{N}(R_{\pi})^{\textup{sym}}
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
there is a
unique higher
\pi
-Frobenius lift
\phi_{i}^{(s)\textup{Ch}}
of degree
s
on
\mathcal{A}
that is
\mathcal{B}_{q^{(s)}}
-symmetric and metric with respect to
q^{(s)}
; it is given by the formula

\phi_{i}^{(s)\textup{Ch}}(x):=x^{(p^{s})}\{(x^{(p^{s})t}\phi_{i}^{(s)}(q^{(s)})x^{(p^{s})})^{-1}(x^{t}q^{(s)}x)^{(p^{s})}\}^{1/2}.

Here the matrix whose square root we are considering
can be written in the form
1+\pi X
with
X
a matrix with entries in
\mathcal{A}
. For such a matrix the square root is taken to be defined by the usual Newton binomial series; cf.
[
Bu17
, Rmk. 4.12]
.

Definition 3.39
.

The
\pi
-connection
\Delta^{(s)\textup{Ch}}=(\delta_{1}^{(s)\textup{Ch}},\ldots,\delta_{n}^{(s)\textup{Ch}})
of degree
s

defined by the higher Frobenius lifts
\phi_{i}^{(s)\textup{Ch}}
is called the
arithmetic Chern connection
of degree
s
attached to
q^{(s)}
.

Proof of Theorem
3.38
.
This proof is entirely similar to the proof of
[
Bu17
, Thm. 4.23]
, by replacing
p
with
\pi

and working in degree
s
as opposed to
s=1
. We briefly recall the argument. With notation as in (
3.23
) set
C_{i}^{(s)}=\frac{1}{\pi}(B^{(s)}-A_{i}^{(s)})
and define

\Lambda_{i}^{(s)}=\{(A_{i}^{(s)})^{-1}B^{(s)}\}^{1/2}=\{1+\pi(A_{i}^{(s)})^{-1}C^{(s)}_{i}\}^{1/2}.

One has
\Lambda^{(s)}_{i}\equiv 1\bmod\pi
. It is entirely formal to see that the
\mathcal{B}_{q^{(s)}}
-symmetry condition
A_{i}^{(s)}\Lambda_{i}^{(s)}=\Lambda_{i}^{(s)t}A_{i}^{(s)}
holds; cf. Remark
3.37
. One the other hand, the same algebraic manipulations in the proof of

demonstrate that the metric condition
\Lambda_{i}^{(s)t}A_{i}^{(s)}\Lambda_{i}^{(s)}=B^{(s)}
holds; cf. Remark

\phi_{i}^{(s)\textup{Ch}}(x)=x^{(p^{s})}\Lambda_{i}^{(s)}(x)

[
Bu17

.
∎

In this section we give an intrinsic interpretation of our arithmetic PDE Riemannian concepts along the lines of

Bu17

2.12
and
2.13

and drop
\pi
as an index, except in
R_{\pi}
. We also fix, in what follows, a family

4.1.
Review of partial
\pi
-jet spaces

In this subsection we extend to arbitrary degree the definition of partial
\pi
-jet spaces
[
BMa
]
; they are universal initial objects in categories to be described presently which are similar to the
\pi=p
,
s=1
case with one Frobenius lift
[
Bu95
]
. See
[
BMa
, Sec. 2.5]
for a more thorough treatment of partial
\pi
-jet spaces.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{N}
.
Define the category
{\bf Prol}^{*(s)}
as follows. An object of this category is a countable family of
p
-adically complete
R_{\pi}
-algebras
S^{*}=(S^{r})_{r\geq 0}
equipped with the following data,

(1)

R_{\pi}
-algebra homomorphisms
\varphi\colon S^{r}\to S^{r+1}
for
r\geq 0
,

(2)

higher
\pi
-derivations
\delta_{j}^{(s)}\colon S^{r}\rightarrow S^{r+1}
of degree
s
for
1\leq j\leq n
and
r\geq 0
.

We require that
\delta_{j}^{(s)}
be compatible with the
\pi
-derivations on
R_{\pi}
and with
\varphi
, i.e.,
\delta_{j}^{(s)}\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ\delta_{j}^{(s)}
.
Morphisms are defined in a natural way. Denote the corresponding
\pi
-Frobenius lifts of degree
s
by
\phi_{j}^{(s)}\colon S^{r}\rightarrow S^{r+1}
which are therefore given by the rule

\phi^{(s)}_{j}(x)=\varphi(x)^{p^{s}}+\pi\delta_{j}^{(s)}x.

The objects of
{\bf Prol}^{*(s)}
are called
prolongation sequences
of degree
s
over
R_{\pi}
. We sometimes identify elements
a\in S^{r}
with the elements
\varphi(a)\in S^{r+1}
if no confusion arises and sometimes write
S^{*}=(S^{r},\varphi,\delta^{(s)}_{1},\ldots,\delta^{(s)}_{n})
.

Remark 4.2
.

Recall the following obvious properties of this category.

(1)

If
S
is a
p
-adically complete partial
\delta
-ring over
R_{\pi}
of degree
s
whose
\pi
-derivations are compatible with those on
R_{\pi}
then the sequence
S^{*}=(S^{r})
with
S^{r}=S
has a natural structure of object of
{\bf Prol}^{*(s)}
with
\varphi
the identity and obvious
\delta^{(s)}_{j}
’s. The initial object in
{\bf Prol}^{*(s)}
is the sequence
R^{*}_{\pi}=(R_{\pi}^{r})
with
R^{r}_{\pi}:=R_{\pi}
.

(2)

If
S^{*}=(S^{r},\varphi,\delta^{(s)}_{1},\ldots,\delta^{(s)}_{n})
is an object of
{\bf Prol}^{*(s)}
then the inductive limit

\varinjlim_{\varphi}S^{r}
has a natural structure of partial
\delta
-ring of degree

4.2.
Group structure on partial
\pi
-jet spaces

Recall that we considered the group scheme

G=\textup{GL}_{N/R_{\pi}}=\textup{Spec}(R_{\pi}[x,\det(x)^{-1}])

where
x
is an
N\times N
matrix of indeterminates. One can consider then the partial
\pi
-jet space
J^{1}(G)
of order
1
and degree
s
; one can easily check that

J^{1}(G)\simeq\textup{Spf}(R_{\pi}[x,\delta^{(s)}_{1}x,\ldots,\delta^{(s)}_{n}x,\det(x)^{-1}])^{\widehat{\ }}.

Consider the matrix of indeterminates
\delta^{(s)}x:=(\delta^{(s)}x_{jk})
and define the
p
-adic formal scheme

\mathfrak{g}:=\textup{Spf}(R_{\pi}[\delta^{(s)}x])^{\widehat{\ }}.

Hence

\mathfrak{g}^{n}=\textup{Spf}(R_{\pi}[\delta_{1}^{(s)}x,\ldots,\delta^{(s)}_{n}x])^{\widehat{\ }}.

We will presently define a group structure on
\mathfrak{g}
in the category of
p
-adic formal schemes but for now we ignore this. For every
p
-adically complete
R_{\pi}
-algebra
S
the set of
S
-points (i.e.,
\textup{Spf}(S)
-points)

\mathfrak{g}(S)
identifies with the set

\textup{Mat}_{n}(S)
.
We have a canonical identification in the category of
p
-adic formal schemes

J^{1}(G)\simeq\widehat{G}\times\mathfrak{g}^{n}.

For
S
as above the
S
-points of the formal scheme

\widehat{G}\times\mathfrak{g}
are identified with tuples

(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}),\ \ a_{0}\in\textup{GL}_{n}(S),\ \ a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\textup{Mat}_{n}(S).

On the other hand by the universal property of partial
\pi
-jet spaces we have an induced structure of group (in the category of
p
-adic formal schemes) on
J^{1}(G)
. This induces a
structure of group (in the category of
p
-adic formal schemes) on
\widehat{G}\times\mathfrak{g}
. As in
[
Bu17
, p. 171]
this group structure is defined on
S
-points by the multiplication rule:

(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n})=(c_{0},\ldots,c_{n})

where
c_{0}=a_{0}b_{0}
and for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
we have:

c_{i}=a_{0}^{(p^{s})}b_{i}+a_{i}b_{0}^{(p^{s})}+\pi a_{i}b_{i}+\pi^{-1}(a_{0}^{(p^{s})}b_{0}^{(p^{s})}-(a_{0}b_{0})^{(p^{s})}).

We have a natural identification of
\mathfrak{g}^{n}(S)
with
the set of
S
-points of the form

(1,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})

of
\widehat{G}\times\mathfrak{g}^{n}
and hence an identification of
\mathfrak{g}^{n}

the kernel of the group homomorphism

J^{1}(G)\rightarrow\widehat{G}
. The induced group structure on
\mathfrak{g}^{n}

is simply the
n
-fold product of the group structure on
\mathfrak{g}
which, on

S
-points, is given by the operation

(4.4)

\mathfrak{g}(S)\times\mathfrak{g}(S)\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}(S),\ \ (a,b)\mapsto a+_{{\pi}}b:=a+b+\pi ab.

Note that the inverse in
\mathfrak{g}(S)
is given by the map

(4.5)

\mathfrak{g}(S)\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}(S),\ \ a\mapsto-a+\pi a^{2}-\pi^{2}a^{3}+\ldots.

If we set

(4.6)

G^{1}(R_{\pi}):=\textup{ker}(G(R_{\pi})\rightarrow G(k))

then we have a group isomorphism

(4.7)

\mathfrak{g}(R_{\pi})\rightarrow G^{1}(R_{\pi}),\ \ a\mapsto 1+\pi\cdot a.

The group
\mathfrak{g}^{n}
can be viewed as a ‘
p
-adic formal analogue’ of the formal group of
\textup{GL}_{N}
.
By analogy with the classical theory we refer to the group
\mathfrak{g}^{n}
as the

\delta
-Lie algebra
of
G
of degree
s
, cf.,
[
Bu17
, Def. 3.132]
. Note, however, that, as a group,

\mathfrak{g}(S)
, and hence
\mathfrak{g}^{n}(S)
, is non-abelian for
N\geq 2
. Also note that
the various groups
\mathfrak{g}^{n}
as
s
varies in
\mathbb{N}
are canonically isomorphic.

4.3.
Logarithmic derivative and Christoffel symbols

By the universal property of partial
\pi
-jet spaces of degree
s
for every
p
-adically complete partial
\delta
-ring
S
of degree
s
we have an induced map of sets

D:G(S)\rightarrow J^{1}(G)(S)\simeq G(S)\times\mathfrak{g}^{n}(S),\ \ D(a)=(a,\delta^{(s)}_{1}a,\ldots,\delta^{(s)}_{n}a).

Assume now
that we are given a
\pi
-connection
((\delta_{1}^{(s)})^{G},\ldots,(\delta_{n}^{(s)})^{G})
of degree
s
on
G
. Taking
S=\mathcal{A}
and

we have

Let
(\Gamma^{(s)}_{1},\ldots,\Gamma^{(s)}_{n})
be the Christoffel symbols of the second kind attached to our
\pi
-connection.
If we consider in addition the trivial
\pi
-connection
((\delta_{1,0}^{(s)})^{G},\ldots,(\delta_{n,0}^{(s)})^{G})
of degree
s
on
G
(with
(\delta_{i,0}^{(s)})^{G}x=0
) and we consider the corresponding map

then, using the group law on the partial
\pi
-jet space, we have the following computation:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(D(x^{t}))(D_{0}(x^{t}))^{-1}&=&(1,(\delta_{1}^{(s)})^{G}x^{t}\cdot(x^{(p^{s})t})^{-1},\ldots,(\delta_{n}^{(s)})^{G}x^{t}\cdot(x^{(p^{s})t})^{-1})\\
&&\\
&=&(1,\Gamma^{(s)}_{1},\ldots,\Gamma^{(s)}_{n}).\end{array}

So we can interpret the Christoffel symbols of the second kind as a tuple

(\Gamma^{(s)}_{1},\ldots,\Gamma^{(s)}_{n})\in\mathfrak{g}(S)^{n}

obtained by taking the ‘difference’ between

and
D_{0}
evaluated at

. This difference is an analogue of the classical ‘logarithmic derivative’ map or
‘Maurer-Cartan equations’ of a Lie group as explained in
[
Bu17
, loc.cit]
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Fix a torsion symbol

of the second kind with coefficients in
R

\delta
-overconvergent matrices

\Lambda^{(s){\bf LC}}_{p,1},\ldots,\Lambda^{(s){\bf LC}}_{p,n}\in\textup{Mat}_{n}(J^{1}_{p}(S))[x,\det(x)^{-1}]^{\widehat{\ }})

satisfying the following property. For all
\pi\in\Pi
and all

if

\Phi^{(s)\textup{LC}}=(\phi_{\pi,1}^{(s)\textup{LC}},\ldots,\phi_{\pi,n}^{(s)\textup{LC}})
is the

\pi
-Frobenius lifts of degree

corresponding to the arithmetic Levi-Civita connection of degree

attached to

and to the torsion symbol

\phi^{(s)\textup{LC}}_{\pi,i}(x)=x^{(p^{s})}\cdot(\Lambda_{p,i}^{(s){\bf LC}})^{\textup{alg}}(q^{(s)}).

Proof
.
For
\pi\in\Pi
consider the matrices

A_{\pi,i}^{(s){\bf LC}}:=x^{(p^{s})t}\phi^{(s)}_{\pi,i}(Q)x^{(p^{s})},\ \ B^{(s){\bf LC}}=(x^{t}Qx)^{(p^{s})}.

The matrix

does not depend on

via the homomorphism (

) are all equal and we denote by

this common image.
By Theorem
3.35
applied to
L_{\pi}^{k(s)}=(L_{\pi,ij}^{k(s)})
there exist
a unique tuple
\Lambda_{\pi}^{(s){\bf LC}}
of matrices

\Lambda^{(s){\bf LC}}_{\pi,1},\ldots,\Lambda^{(s){\bf LC}}_{\pi,n}\in\textup{Mat}_{n}(J^{1}_{\pi}(S)[x,\det(x)^{-1}]^{\widehat{\ }})

such that

(5.7)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}(\Lambda^{(s){\bf LC}}_{\pi,i})^{t}A_{i}^{(s){\bf LC}}\Lambda_{\pi,i}^{(s){\bf LC}}&=&B^{(s){\bf LC}},\\
&&\\
(\Lambda_{\pi,i}^{(s){\bf LC}}-1)_{kj}-(\Lambda_{\pi,j}^{(s){\bf LC}}-1)_{ki}&=&\pi\cdot L_{\pi,ij}^{k(s)}(\Lambda_{\pi}^{(s){\bf LC}})\\
&&\\
&=&p\cdot L_{p,ij}^{k(s)}(\Lambda_{\pi}^{(s){\bf LC}}).\end{array}

By the uniqueness property in Theorem

we get that

\Lambda_{\pi,i}^{{\bf LC}}=\Lambda_{p,i}^{{\bf LC}}

\delta

\begin{array}[]{rcl}((\Lambda^{(s){\bf LC}}_{p,i})^{\textup{alg}}(q^{(s)}))^{t}((A_{i}^{(s){\bf LC}})^{\textup{alg}}(q^{(s)}))((\Lambda_{p,i}^{(s){\bf LC}})^{\textup{alg}}(q^{(s)}))&=&(B^{(s){\bf LC}})^{\textup{alg}}(q^{(s)})\\

((\Lambda_{p,i}^{(s){\bf LC}})^{\textup{alg}}(q^{(s)})-1)_{kj}-((\Lambda_{p,j}^{(s){\bf LC}})^{\textup{alg}}(q^{(s)})-1)_{ki}&=&pL_{p,ij}^{k}(\Lambda_{p}^{(s){\bf LC}}(q^{(s)})).\end{array}

On the other hand
if we write

\begin{array}[]{rclll}\Lambda^{(s)t}_{\pi,i}A_{i}^{(s)}\Lambda_{\pi,i}^{(s)}&=&B^{(s)},&&\\
&&&&\\

By the uniqueness in the definition of the arithmetic Levi-Civita connection the equations

\Lambda^{(s)}_{\pi,i}=(\Lambda_{p,i}^{(s){\bf LC}})^{\textup{alg}}(q^{(s)}).

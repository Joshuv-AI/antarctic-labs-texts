On the functional central limit theorem with mean-uncertainty
Xinpeng LI

Abstract




Abstract.

We introduce a new basic model for independent and identical distributed sequence on the canonical space
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}))
via probability kernels with model uncertainty. Thanks to the well-defined upper and lower variances, we obtain a new functional central limit theorem with mean-uncertainty by the means of martingale central limit theorem and stability of stochastic integral in the classical probability theory. Then we extend it from the canonical space to the general sublinear expectation space. The corresponding proofs are purely probabilistic and do not rely on the nonlinear partial differential equation.

Keywords:
Canonical space, Central limit theorem, Independence and identical distribution, Mean-uncertainty, Sublinear expectation, Upper and lower variances

1
Introduction

The notions of independence and identical distribution are very important in the probability and statistics. In classical probability theory, the familiar way to construct canonical sequence
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of independent random variables which having prescribed marginal laws
\{\mu_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is by product measure
\mu=\otimes_{i=1}^{\infty}\mu_{i}
on the canonical space
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}))
with
X_{i}(\omega)=\omega_{i},\omega=(\omega_{1},\cdots,\omega_{n},\cdots)\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
. In general, any joint law
\mu
on
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}))
can be decomposed in terms of its probability kernels in the form (see Yan
[
33
]
):
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\forall A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\mu(A\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}-n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu_{1}(dx_{1})\int_{\mathbb{R}}\kappa_{2}(x_{1},dx_{2})\cdots\int_{\mathbb{R}}I_{A}\kappa_{n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},dx_{n}).

In particular, if the probability kernel
\kappa_{i}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},\cdot)
is independent of
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1})
for all
i\geq 2
, the canonical random variables
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are independent. If further assume that
\kappa_{i}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},\cdot)=\mu_{1}(\cdot)
for all
i\geq 2
, then
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) under
\mu
. This formulation inspires us to introduce a basic model on canonical space
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}))
via probability kernels such that the canonical random variables
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
are independence and identically distributed with model uncertainty as described in Peng
[
27
]
, i.e., all the probability kernels belong to a given set of probability measures. Such formulation provides a new interpretation of i.i.d. sequence on sublinear expectation space.

The i.i.d. assumption is usually used in the central limit theorem (CLT). Peng
[
28
]
initially introduced the new notion of i.i.d. sequence on the sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathbb{E}})
and corresponding CLT with zero-mean is established in Peng
[
23
,
24
,
25
,
27
,
28
]
, known as Peng’s CLT:

For an i.i.d. sequence
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\hat{\mathbb{E}}[X_{1}]=\hat{\mathbb{E}}[-X_{1}]=0
and

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{\hat{E}}[(|X_{1}|^{2}-\lambda)^{+}]=0

(1.1)

we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{\hat{E}}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}_{G}[\varphi(\xi)],\ \ \forall\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}),

where
\mathbb{E}_{G}
is the
G
-expectation corresponding to the
G
-normally distributed random variable
\xi
, which is characterized by the so-called
G
-heat equation. The corresponding proof adopted the partial differential equation (PDE) approach which mainly based on the deep result of the interior regularity for PDE (see Krylov
[
14
]
). We emphasize that the regularity of
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
(see Definition
4.6
) is not required here. In particular, the CLT holds when
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
is only finitely additive without the assumption of regularity. In this case, there does not exist probability measure such that underlying random variables are i.i.d., therefore the classical probability theory is not applicable (see Example
5.1
).

After Peng established CLT on sublinear expectation space, Zhang
[
35
]
obtained the sufficient and necessary conditions of CLT for i.i.d. random variables, in which the condition (
1.1
) can be weakened to
\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\lambda\mathbb{V}(|X_{1}|^{2}\geq\lambda)=0
, where
\mathbb{V}
is the capacity introduced by
\mathbb{\hat{E}}
. Peng
[
25
]
proposed a new proof of CLT by weakly compact method but involving PDE approach to characterize the
G
-normal distribution. Song
[
30
]
provided an estimate of the convergence rate for CLT by Stein’s method, and Krylov
[
15
]
gave error estimates in CLT for not necessarily non-degenerate case by the finite-difference approximations for Bellman’s equation. Besides, convergence to the
G
-normal distribution also occurs for non-independent random variables or for non-identical distributions (see Li
[
19
]
, Li and Shi
[
17
]
, Zhang
[
36
]
). Zhang
[
34
]
also considered the functional CLT based on Peng’s CLT in
[
27
]
. We note that all papers on CLT under sublinear expectation are assumed that the underlying random variables are zero-mean and most of proofs rely on PDE approach.

An interesting problem is that how about CLT for the random variables with mean-uncertainty? Recently, Chen and Epstein
[
2
]
proved a CLT for random variables with mean-uncertainty and unambiguous conditional variance, where the limit is defined by a backward stochastic differential equation (see Pardoux and Peng
[
21
]
, Peng
[
22
]
). Chen et al.
[
3
]
further established a CLT under the assumption that conditional variances can vary in a fixed interval, and the corresponding limit distribution can be calculated by the oscillating Brownian motion. In
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
, one important assumption is that all measures in the set
\mathcal{P}
are equivalent on the filtration, while such assumption is not necessary in our paper. Fand et al.
[
6
]
also considered the CLT with mean-uncertainty with additional assumptions, which converges to the classical normal distribution.

Motivated by the weak approximation of
G
-expectations introduced by Dolinsky et al.
[
5
]
, we establish the functional CLT on the canonical space
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}),\mathcal{P})
, where
\mathcal{P}
is a set of probability measures introduced via probability kernels with model uncertainty. The proof relies on the martingale central limit theorem and stability of stochastic integral in the classical probability theory. The CLTs obtained in this paper are in the functional forms, and also take the mean-uncertainty into consideration due to the well-defined upper and lower variances. It is easy to extend functional CLT from the canonical space to the general sublinear expectation space by the representation theorem. Thus we provide a new proof of CLT in
[
27
]
, which is purely probabilistic, then generalize it to the mean-uncertainty case. We note that the corresponding limit distribution is still
G
-normally distributed. This new CLT illustrates the broad applicability of
G
-normal distribution for the situations with mean-uncertainty. It is interesting that the variant form of such CLT with mean-uncertainty provides a simple proof of Erdös-Kac Theorem in number theory (see Guo et al.
[
8
]
).

This paper also provides a new methodology to study limit theorems on general sublinear expectation space. We firstly establish limit theorems on the canonical space. In this step, the classical martingale limit theorem can be applied to derive the desired limit theorems with model uncertainty, and then we extend them to the general sublinear expectation space by the representation theorem.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 describes the basic model for i.i.d. sequence on canonical space
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}))
introduced by probability kernels with model uncertainty. The corresponding functional CLT with mean-uncertainty are established in Section 3. In Section 4, we extend functional CLT from canonical space to general sublinear expectation space. Some examples are provided in Section 5. The law of large numbers, which is used in the proof of functional CLT, is given in Appendix.

2
Basic model on canonical space

Let
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}))
be the canonical space and
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
the sequence of canonical random variables defined by
X_{i}(\omega)=\omega_{i}
for
\omega=(\omega_{1},\cdots,\omega_{n},\cdots)\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
. For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is the convex and weakly compact set of probability measures on
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))
characterizing the uncertainty of the distributions of
X_{i}
. We define a set of joint laws on
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}))
via the probability kernels as following:

\begin{array}[c]{l}\mathcal{P}=\{P\ \text{is\ probability\ measure\ on }(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}))\ \text{such\ that }\\
P(A^{(n)}\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}-n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu_{1}(dx_{1})\int_{\mathbb{R}}\kappa_{2}(x_{1},dx_{2})\cdots\int_{\mathbb{R}}I_{A^{(n)}}\kappa_{n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},dx_{n})\\
\forall n\geq 1,\ \forall A^{(n)}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \ \kappa_{i}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}_{i},\ \ 2\leq i\leq n,\ \mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}\},\end{array}

(2.1)

where for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\kappa_{i}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},dx_{i})
is the probability kernel satisfying:

(i)

\forall(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{i-1}
,
\kappa_{i}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},\cdot)
is a probability measure on
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))
.

(ii)

\forall B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
,
\kappa_{i}(\cdot,B)
is
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{i-1})
-measurable.

The existence of such
P
on
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}))
is shown by Ionescu-Tulcea theorem.

We note that the set
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is independent of
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1})
, i.e., the uncertainty of the distributions of
X_{i}
does not change for any realization of history
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1})
of the random vector
(X_{1},\cdots,X_{i-1})
, thus we call
X_{i}
independent of
(X_{1},\cdots,X_{i-1})
. Here independence means the uncertainty of the distributions of
X_{i}
is independent of random vector
(X_{1},\cdots,X_{i-1})
.
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
can be regarded as the canonical stochastic process with discrete time, it is obvious that such independence is not symmetric in general. Furthermore, if the uncertainties of each
X_{i}
are the same, i.e.,
\mathcal{P}_{i}=\mathcal{P}_{1}
,
\forall i\geq 2
, we say that
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is an i.i.d. sequence. In this case,
\mathcal{P}
is determined by the set
\mathcal{P}_{1}
of marginal laws for
X_{1}
.

Remark 2.1
.

In particular, if each
\mathcal{P}_{i}=\{\mu_{i}\}
is a singleton, above construction is just the classical procedure to construction i.i.d. sequence on the product space by the product measure
\mu=\otimes_{i=1}^{\infty}\mu_{i}
.

For each
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}})
-measurable random variable
X
, we introduce the sublinear expectation
{\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}
defined by

\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[X]:=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}E_{P}[X].

The canonical filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is defined as
\mathcal{F}_{i}=\sigma(X_{k},1\leq k\leq i)
with convention
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\{\emptyset,\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}\}
. Then we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

For each
P\in\mathcal{P}
and
\varphi\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[-\varphi(X_{i})]\leq E_{P}[\varphi(X_{i})|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(X_{i})],\ \ P-\text{a.s.},\ \forall i\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.2)

Proof.

For each
P\in\mathcal{P}
, by the regularity of condition probability, almost surely we have, for
\omega=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},\cdots)\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
,

E_{P}[\varphi(X_{i})|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}](\omega)=E_{\kappa_{i}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},\cdot)}[\varphi(X_{i})],

then (
2.2
) holds since
\kappa_{i}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}_{i}
.
∎

We note that (
2.2
) is equivalent to the classical notion of independence when
\mathcal{P}
is a singleton. In fact, (
2.2
) also holds on the sublinear expectation space, see Guo and Li
[
9
]
.

Remark 2.3
.

If we further assume that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}[(|X_{i}|^{2}-\lambda)^{+}]=0,

(2.3)

then (
2.2
) holds for continuous function
\varphi
satisfying quadratic growth condition
|\varphi(x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{2})
, where
C
is a constant. In particular, if
{\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}[X_{i}]={\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}[-X_{i}]=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, then for each
P\in\mathcal{P}
,

E_{P}[X_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]=0,\ \ P-\text{a.s.}

Remark 2.4
.

In fact, (
2.2
) still holds without the assumptions of convexity and weakly compactness for each
\mathcal{P}_{i}
. The sequence
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying (
2.2
) is called pseudo-independent, see
[
9
]
.

3
Functional central limit theorem on the canonical space

In this section, we establish the functional CLT on the canonical space, which converges to the
G
-expectation constructed by
G
-heat equation. Here we use another representation of
G
-expectation obtained in Denis et al.
[
4
]
and then developed by Dolinsky et al.
[
5
]
. We adopt the formulations in
[
5
]
.

3.1
Representation of
G
-expectation

Let
\Omega_{0}=C([0,1])
be the space of all continuous paths
\omega=(\omega_{t})_{0\leq t\leq 1}
with
\omega_{0}=0
, endowed with the norm
||\omega||_{\infty}=\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|\omega_{t}|
. The canonical process
B
is defined by
B_{t}(\omega)=\omega_{t}
and its quadratic variation process is denoted by
\langle B\rangle
. The canonical filtration is
\mathcal{F}^{B}_{t}=\sigma(B_{s},0\leq s\leq t).

For the fixed non-negative interval
\Theta
, we set

\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}=\left\{P:B\ \text{is}\ \text{martingale\ under\ }P\ \text{and}\ \frac{d\langle B\rangle_{t}}{dt}\in\Theta,P\times dt-\text{a.e.}\right\}

Let
P_{W}
be the Wiener measure on
C([0,1])
and we define

\mathcal{Q}_{\Theta}=\left\{P_{W}\circ\left(\int f(t,B)dB_{t}\right)^{-1}:f\in\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}\right\},

where
\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}
is the collection of all adapted continuous functions on
[0,1]\times\Omega_{0}
taking values in
\sqrt{\Theta}
.

It is proved in
[
5
]
that upper expectations of
\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\Theta}
coincide, i.e.,

\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}}[\varphi]=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}}E_{P}[\varphi]=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{Q}_{\Theta}}E_{P}[\varphi]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{Q}_{\Theta}}[\varphi],\ \ \ \forall\varphi\in C_{b}(\Omega_{0}).

3.2
Upper and lower variances with mean-uncertainty

Let
\mathcal{P}_{0}
be a weakly compact and convex set of probability measures on
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))
and
X
be the canonical random variable, i.e.,
X(\omega)=\omega,\ \forall\omega\in\mathbb{R}
.
We define

\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}[\varphi(X)]=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{0}}E_{P}[\varphi],\ \ \forall\varphi\in C(\mathbb{R}).

In order to deal with mean-uncertainty case, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}[X]>-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}[-X]
, we need to define the proper upper and lower variances.

We denote
V_{P}(X)
the classical variance of
X
under probability measure
P
, then

V_{P}(X)=E_{P}[(X-E_{P}[X])^{2}]=\min_{\mu\in\mathbb{R}}E_{P}[(X-\mu)^{2}].

It is natural to introduce the upper and lower variances under sublinear expectation
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}
instead of
E_{P}
(see Walley
[
32
]
or Li et al.
[
18
]
).

Definition 3.1
.

For the canonical random variable
X
on
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))
with
\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}[|X|^{2}]<\infty
, define the upper variance of
X
to be

\overline{V}(X):=\min_{\underline{\mu}\leq\mu\leq\overline{\mu}}\{\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}[(X-\mu)^{2}]\},

and the lower variance of
X
to be

\underline{V}(X):=\min_{\underline{\mu}\leq\mu\leq\overline{\mu}}\{-\mathbb{{E}}^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}[-(X-\mu)^{2}]\},

where
\overline{\mu}=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}[X]
and
\underline{\mu}=-\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}[-X]
.

The following relation between classical variance and upper and lower variances was proved in
[
32
]
(see also
[
18
]
).

Proposition 3.2
.

\overline{V}(X)=\max_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{0}}V_{P}(X).

(3.1)

\underline{V}(X)=\min_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{0}}V_{P}(X).

(3.2)

Remark 3.3
.

The weak compactness and convexity of
\mathcal{P}_{0}
ensure that the minimax theorem in Sion
[
29
]
can be applied to prove (
3.1
). For the general
\mathcal{P}_{0}
without such assumptions, (
3.2
) still holds but (
3.1
) becomes the following inequality:

\overline{V}(X)\geq\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{0}}V_{P}(X).

Let
\mathcal{P}
be constructed on
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}))
by (
2.1
) with
\mathcal{P}_{i}=\mathcal{P}_{0}
for
i\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\mathcal{P}_{0}
is weakly compact and convex. We further assume that
\mathcal{P}_{0}
satisfies

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}[(|X|^{2}-\lambda)^{+}]=0,

(3.3)

where
X(\omega)=\omega,\ \forall\omega\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the canonical process on the canonical space
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}))
with
X_{i}(\omega)=\omega_{i},\ \forall\omega=(\omega_{1},\cdots,\omega_{n},\cdots)\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
. Then
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is an i.i.d. sequence under
\mathcal{P}
. The natural filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is defined by
\mathcal{F}_{i}=\sigma(X_{1},\cdots,X_{i})
with convention
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\{\emptyset,\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}\}
.

The following proposition is important to prove the functional CLT.

Proposition 3.4
.

For each
P\in\mathcal{P}
, let
\tilde{X}^{P}_{i}=X_{i}-E_{P}[X_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]
, then

\underline{V}(X_{1})\leq E_{P}[|\tilde{X}^{P}_{i}|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]\leq\overline{V}(X_{1}),\ \ P-\text{a.s.},\ \ i\in\mathbb{N},

(3.4)

Proof.

If
i=1
, then by Proposition
3.2
, we have

\underline{V}(X_{1})\leq E_{P}[(X_{1}-E_{P}[X_{1}])^{2}]\leq\overline{V}(X_{1}),\ \ \ \forall P\in\mathcal{P}.

For fixed
i\geq 2
, then
\forall P\in\mathcal{P}
and
\omega=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},\cdots)\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle E_{P}[|\tilde{X}^{P}_{i}|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}](\omega)=V_{\kappa_{i}(x_{1},\cdots,{x}_{i-1},\ \cdot)}(X_{i}),\ P-\text{a.s.}.

Since
\kappa_{i}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
, thanks to Proposition
3.2
, (
3.4
) holds.

∎

3.3
Functional central limit theorem with mean-uncertainty

In order to obtain the functional CLT, we need to extend each discrete path
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
to a continuous path
\hat{x}\in\Omega_{0}
, where the interpolation operator
\hat{\ }:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow C([0,1])
is defined as

x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\mapsto\hat{x}=(\hat{x}_{t})_{0\leq t\leq 1},

where
\hat{x}_{t}:=([nt]+1-nt)x_{[nt]}+(nt-[nt])x_{[nt]+1}
with
x_{0}=0
.

With above notations, we have the functional CLT with mean-uncertainty on the canonical space
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}))
.

Theorem 3.5
.

For each
P\in\mathcal{P}
, let
S_{P}^{(n)}=(S^{P}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
with
S^{P}_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{i}(X_{j}-E_{P}[X_{j}|\mathcal{F}_{j-1}])
. Then for each continuous function
\varphi:C([0,1])\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying
|\varphi(\omega)|\leq C(1+||\omega||_{\infty})
for some constant
C>0
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}E_{P}\left[\varphi\left(\hat{S_{P}^{(n)}}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}}[\varphi],

(3.5)

where
\Theta=[\underline{V}(X_{1}),\overline{V}(X_{1})]
.

Proof.

We firstly prove that (
3.5
) holds for
\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\Omega_{0})
, where
C_{b.Lip}(\Omega_{0})
is the space of all bounded and Lipschitz continuous functions on
\Omega_{0}
.

Let us consider the first inequality

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}E_{P}\left[\varphi\left(\hat{S_{P}^{(n)}}\right)\right]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}}[\varphi].

(3.6)

For fixed
\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\Omega_{0})
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
P^{(n)}\in\mathcal{P}
such that, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

E_{P^{(n)}}\left[\varphi\left(\hat{S_{P^{(n)}}^{(n)}}\right)\right]\geq\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}E_{P}\left[\varphi\left(\hat{S_{P}^{(n)}}\right)\right]-\varepsilon.

Let
Q^{(n)}
be the law of
\hat{S_{P^{(n)}}^{(n)}}
introduced by probability measure
P^{(n)}\in\mathcal{P}
, i.e.,

E_{Q^{(n)}}[\varphi(B)]=E_{P^{(n)}}\left[\varphi\left(\hat{S_{P^{(n)}}^{(n)}}\right)\right],\ \ \forall\varphi\in C_{b}(\Omega_{0}).

The sequence
\{Q^{(n)}\}
is tight on
C[0,1]
by condition (
3.3
) (see Zhang
[
34
]
). Let
Q
be a cluster point of
Q^{(n)}
, then the canonical process
B
is a
Q
-martingale.

Let
M^{(n)}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=0}^{[nt]}\tilde{X}_{i}^{P^{(n)}}
, where
\tilde{X}_{i}^{P^{(n)}}=X_{i}-E_{P^{(n)}}[X_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]
and
X_{0}=0
. The law of
M^{(n)}
on the space
D[0,1]
of càdlàg paths is denoted by
\tilde{Q}^{(n)}
. We note that

\left|E_{P^{(n)}}\left[\varphi\left(\hat{S_{P^{(n)}}^{(n)}}\right)\right]-E_{P^{(n)}}[\varphi(M^{(n)})]\right|\leq\frac{L_{\varphi}}{\sqrt{n}}E_{P^{(n)}}[|\tilde{X}^{P^{(n)}}_{i}|]\leq\frac{2L_{\varphi}}{\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}[|X_{1}|]\rightarrow 0,

where
L_{\varphi}
is the Lipschitz constant of
\varphi
. Thus
Q
is also the cluster point of
\tilde{Q}^{(n)}
.

We have

\langle M^{(n)}\rangle_{t}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{[nt]}|\tilde{X}^{P}_{i}|^{2},

and define
d_{\Theta}(x)=\inf_{y\in\Theta}|y-x|
.

Since
\underline{V}(X_{1})\leq E_{Q^{(n)}}[|\tilde{X}^{P}_{i}|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]\leq\overline{V}(X_{1})
by Proposition
3.4
, the similar argument of the law of large numbers (see Appendix) shows that, for any
0\leq s<t\leq 1
,

E_{\tilde{Q}^{(n)}}\left[d_{\Theta}\left(\frac{\langle M^{(n)}\rangle_{t}-\langle M^{(n)}\rangle_{s}}{t-s}\right)\right]\rightarrow 0,

which implies that

E_{Q}\left[d_{\Theta}\left(\frac{\langle B\rangle_{t}-\langle B\rangle_{s}}{t-s}\right)\right]=0,

thus
Q\in\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}
and (
3.6
) holds.

Now we consider the second inequality

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}E_{P}[\varphi(\hat{S_{P}^{(n)}})]\geq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}}[\varphi].

(3.7)

Let
\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\Omega_{0})
be fixed. For each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\bar{f}\in\mathcal{A}_{\Theta}
, such that

E_{P_{W}}\left[\varphi\left(\int\bar{f}(t,W)dW_{t}\right)\right]\geq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{Q}_{\Theta}}[\varphi]-\varepsilon.

Without loss of generality, we further assume that
\bar{f}>0
.

By Proposition
3.2
, we can find probability measures
\overline{P}
and
\underline{P}
on
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))
such that
V_{\overline{P}}(X_{1})=\overline{V}(X_{1})
and
V_{\underline{P}}(X^{n}_{1})=\underline{V}(X_{1})
respectively.

For fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, since
\bar{f}(0,\cdot)\in\sqrt{\Theta}
is a constant,
we define,
\forall B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
, if
\overline{V}(X_{1})>\underline{V}(X_{1})
,

\mu_{1}(B)=\frac{\overline{V}(X_{1})-\bar{f}(0,\cdot)^{2}}{\overline{V}(X_{1})-\underline{V}(X_{1})}\underline{P}(B)+\frac{\bar{f}(0,\cdot)^{2}-\underline{V}(X_{1})}{\overline{V}(X_{i})-\underline{V}(X_{1})}\overline{P}(B),

otherwise,
\mu_{1}(B)=\overline{P}(B)
.

There exists
P_{*}\in\mathcal{P}
such that

{E_{P_{*}}[|X_{1}-E_{P_{*}}[X_{1}]|^{2}]}=\bar{f}\left(0,\cdot\right)^{2}\subset\Theta.

(3.8)

We then define

Y_{1}=\frac{X_{1}-E_{P_{*}}[X_{1}]}{\sqrt{E_{P_{*}}[|X_{1}-E_{P_{*}}[X_{1}]|^{2}]}}.

Let
W^{(n)}
be defined recursively by

W^{(n)}_{t}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{[nt]}Y_{j},

where
Y_{0}=0
, and for
j\geq 1
,

Y_{j}=\frac{X_{j}-E_{P_{*}}[X_{j}|\mathcal{F}_{j-1}]}{\sqrt{E_{P_{*}}[|X_{j}-E_{P_{*}}[X_{j}|\mathcal{F}_{j-1}]|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{j-1}]}}.

Indeed, for
j\geq 2
, we note that
\bar{f}(\frac{j-1}{n},\hat{W^{(n)}})
depends on
\{Y_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq j-1}
by the adaptability of
\bar{f}
. We define,
\forall B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
, if
\overline{V}(X_{1})>\underline{V}(X_{1})
,

\displaystyle\kappa_{j}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{j-1},B)=

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{V}(X_{1})-\bar{f}(\frac{j-1}{n},\hat{W^{(n)}})^{2}}{\overline{V}(X_{1})-\underline{V}(X_{1})}\underline{P}(B)

\displaystyle+\frac{\bar{f}(\frac{j-1}{n},\hat{W^{(n)}})^{2}-\underline{V}(X_{1})}{\overline{V}(X_{1})-\underline{V}(X_{1})}\overline{P}(B),

otherwise,
\kappa_{j}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{j-1},B)=\overline{P}(B)
.

P_{*}
can be formulated by,
\forall A\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

P_{*}(A\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}-n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu_{1}(dx_{1})\int_{\mathbb{R}}\kappa_{2}(x_{1},dx_{2})\cdots\int_{\mathbb{R}}I_{A}\kappa_{n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},dx_{n}).

We can verify that for
1\leq i\leq n
,

{E_{P_{*}}[|X_{i}-E_{P_{*}}[X_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]}=\bar{f}\left((i-1)/n,\hat{W^{(n)}}\right)^{2}\subset\Theta.

It is clear that
E_{P_{*}}[Y_{j}|\mathcal{F}_{j-1}]=0
and
E_{P_{*}}[Y_{j}^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{j-1}]=1
,
j\geq 1
.
By the martingale central limit theorem in Brown
[
1
]
, on the space of càdlàg paths
D([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{2})
equipped with the Skorohod topology,

\left(W^{(n)},\hat{W^{(n)}}\right)\Rightarrow(W,W),

where
W
is the Brownian motion.

We note that

\displaystyle S^{P_{*}}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{i}(X_{j}-E_{P_{*}}[X_{j}|\mathcal{F}_{j-1}])

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{i}\bar{f}\left(\frac{j-1}{n},\hat{W^{(n)}}\right)\left(W_{\frac{j+1}{n}}^{(n)}-W_{\frac{j}{n}}^{(n)}\right).

Then it follows Dolinsky et al.
[
5
]
and the stability of stochastic integrals (see Kurtz and Protter
[
16
]
), on
D(0,1)
,

\hat{S_{P}^{(n)}}\Rightarrow\int\bar{f}(t,W)dW_{t}.

Finally, we obtain

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}E_{P}\left[\varphi\left(\hat{S_{P}^{(n)}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}E_{P_{*}}\left[\varphi\left(\hat{S_{P}^{(n)}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\geq E_{P_{W}}\left[\varphi\left(\int_{0}^{1}\bar{f}(t,W)dW_{t}\right)\right]\geq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{Q}_{\Theta}}[\varphi]-\varepsilon.

Thus (
3.5
) holds for
\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\Omega_{0})
.

For fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by Doob’s martingale inequality, we have

E_{P}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}|M^{(n)}(t)|^{2}]\leq 4\overline{V}(X_{1}),\ \ \ \forall P\in\mathcal{P},

which implies that

\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}E_{P}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq 1}\left|\hat{S_{P}^{(n)}}(t)\right|^{2}\right]\leq 4\overline{V}(X_{1})+2\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[|X_{1}|^{2}].

By the similar arguments as Lemma 2.4.12 in Peng
[
27
]
, (
3.5
) also holds for continuous function
\varphi
with linear growth condition.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

The first inequality (
3.6
) can also be proved by the similar argument in
[
5
]
. For the second inequality (
3.7
), the corresponding proof in
[
5
]
can not be applied directly, since the discrete-time models in
[
5
]
do not have independence property.

4
Functional central limit theorem on sublinear expectation space

In this section, we extend functional CLT to the general sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathbb{E}})
introduced by Peng
[
27
]
.

4.1
Basic notions of sublinear expectation theory

Let
\Omega
be a given set and let
\mathcal{H}
be a linear space of real
functions defined on
\Omega
such that if

X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
, then
\varphi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})\in\mathcal{H}
for each
\varphi\in C_{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where

C_{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the space of all Lipschitz
functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
\mathcal{H}
is considered as the space of
random variables.

X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
,
X_{i}\in\mathcal{H}
, is called
an
n
-dimensional random vector.

Definition 4.1
.

A sublinear expectation
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
on
\mathcal{H}
is a functional
\hat{\mathbb{{E}}}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following
properties: for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

(a)

Monotonicity:
\mathbb{\hat{E}}[X]\geq\mathbb{\hat{E}}[Y]
if

X\geq Y
.

(b)

Constant preserving:
\mathbb{\hat{E}}[c]=c
for
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

(c)

Sub-additivity:
\mathbb{\hat{E}}[X+Y]\leq\mathbb{\hat{E}}[X]+\mathbb{\hat{E}}[Y]
.

(d)

Positive homogeneity:
\mathbb{\hat{E}}[\lambda X]=\lambda\mathbb{\hat{E}}[X]
for
\lambda\geq 0
.

The triple
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\mathbb{\hat{E}})
is called a sublinear
expectation space.

Let
X=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})
be a given
n
-dimensional random vector on a sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\mathbb{\hat{E}})
. We define a functional on
C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by

\mathbb{\hat{F}}_{X}[\varphi]:=\mathbb{\hat{E}}[\varphi(X)],\ \ \forall\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

The triple
(\mathbb{R}^{n},C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\mathbb{\hat{F}}_{X}[\cdot])
forms a sublinear expectation space, and
\mathbb{\hat{F}}_{X}
is called the sublinear distribution of
X
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two random variables on
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\mathbb{\hat{E}})
.
X
and
Y
are called identically distributed, denoted by

X\overset{d}{=}Y
, if for each
\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R})
,

\mathbb{\hat{E}}[\varphi(X)]=\mathbb{\hat{E}}[\varphi(Y)].

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\left\{X_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of
random variables on
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\mathbb{\hat{E}})
.
X_{n}
is said
to be independent of
\left(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}\right)
under

\mathbb{\hat{E}}
, if for each
\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\mathbb{\hat{E}}\left[\varphi\left(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}\right)\right]=\mathbb{\hat{E}}\left[\left.\mathbb{\hat{E}}\left[\varphi\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},X_{n}\right)\right]\right|_{\left(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\right)=\left(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n-1}\right)}\right].

The sequence of random variables
\left\{X_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is
said to be independent, if
X_{n+1}
is independent of
\left(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\right)
for each
n\geq 1
.

Remark 4.4
.

This definition of independence is more general than the classical one, since we do not require the regularity of
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
(see Definition
4.6
). Example
5.1
illustrates that we can not construct i.i.d. sequence on the classical probability space, but we can do it on the sublinear expectation space.

The following proposition gives the links between the i.i.d. sequence on sublinear expectation space and that on canonical space.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be an i.i.d. sequence on sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathbb{E}})
. Then there exists a sequence of i.i.d. canonical random variables
\{Y_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
on canonical space
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}))
associated with the set of probability measures
\mathcal{P}
such that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{n})].

Proof.

In fact, we only need to prove that

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X_{1})]={\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}[\varphi(Y_{1})],\ \ \forall\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}),

(4.1)

and
\{Y_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is an i.i.d. sequence defined by Definition
4.2
and
4.3
.

To prove (
4.1
), let
\mathcal{P}_{0}
be the set of probability measures on
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))
defined by

\mathcal{P}_{0}=\{P:E_{P}[\varphi]\leq\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X_{1})],\ \varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R})\}.

It is clear that
\mathcal{P}_{0}
is weakly compact and convex, and

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X_{1})]=\max_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{0}}E_{P}[\varphi],\ \ \forall\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}).

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of probability measures on canonical space
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}))
constructed by (
2.1
) with
\mathcal{P}_{i}=\mathcal{P}_{0}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Obviously,

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X_{1})]={\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}[\varphi(Y_{1})].

To prove the independence of
\{Y_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, for simplicity, we only prove the case of
n=2
. In this case, we do not distinguish canonical random variables
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
on the canonical spaces
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}))
or on
(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
.

By the representation theorem of sublinear expectation (cf.
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
), there exists
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
on
(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
defined by

\tilde{\mathcal{P}}=\{P:E_{P}[\varphi]\leq\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X_{1},X_{2})],\ \forall\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\},

(4.2)

such that

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X_{1},X_{2})]=\max_{P\in\tilde{\mathcal{P}}}E_{P}[\varphi],\ \ \forall\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Let
\mathcal{P}^{(2)}
be the set on
(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
constructed by (
2.1
) with
\mathcal{P}_{1}=\mathcal{P}_{2}=\mathcal{P}_{0}
. We claim that
\mathcal{P}^{(2)}=\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
.

For each
P\in\mathcal{P}^{(2)}
, we have

\displaystyle E_{P}[\varphi(Y_{1},Y_{2})]

\displaystyle=E_{P}[E_{P}[\varphi(Y_{1},Y_{2})|Y_{1}]]

\displaystyle=E_{P}[E_{P}[\varphi(y_{1},Y_{2})|Y_{1}]|_{y_{1}=Y_{1}}]

\displaystyle\leq E_{P}[\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}[\varphi(y_{1},Y_{2})]|_{y_{1}=Y_{1}}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}[\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}[\varphi(y_{1},Y_{2})]|_{y_{1}=Y_{1}}]=\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(Y_{1},Y_{2})],

which implies
\mathcal{P}^{(2)}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
.

On the other hand, for fixed
P\in\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
,
\forall A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we have the following decomposition:

P(A)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}I_{A}\kappa_{2}(x_{1},dx_{2})\mu_{1}(dx_{1}).

It is clear that

E_{\mu_{1}}[\varphi]=E_{P}[\varphi]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}[\varphi],\ \forall\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}),

then by Corollary 2.8 in Li and Lin
[
20
]
, we obtain
\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
.

For fixed
x_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
,

E_{\kappa_{2}(x_{1},\cdot)}[\varphi(x_{1},\cdot)]\leq\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}[\varphi(x_{1},\cdot)],\ \forall\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

thus
\kappa_{2}(x_{1},\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
. Therefore,

\mathcal{P}^{(2)}=\tilde{\mathcal{P}},

which implies that
Y_{2}
is independent of
Y_{1}
.
∎

For random variable
X
on sublinear expectation
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathbb{E}})
, the corresponding upper and lower variances of
X
can be defined similarly as in Definition
3.1
, i.e.,

\overline{V}(X)=\min_{\underline{\mu}\leq\mu\leq\overline{\mu}}\hat{\mathbb{E}}[(X-\mu)^{2}]\ \ \text{and}\ \ \underline{V}(X)=\min_{\underline{\mu}\leq\mu\leq\overline{\mu}}-\hat{\mathbb{E}}[-(X-\mu)^{2}].

4.2
Functional central limit theorem under regular sublinear expectation

We firstly assume that sublinear expectation
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
is regular and present the corresponding functional CLT.

Definition 4.6
.

The sublinear expectation
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
is said to be regular if for each sequence
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{H}
with
X_{i}\downarrow 0
, we have
\hat{\mathbb{E}}[X_{i}]\downarrow 0
.

If
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
is regular, we consider a family of probability measures
\mathcal{P}_{*}
defined on the measurable space
(\Omega,\sigma(\mathcal{H}))
:

\mathcal{P}_{*}=\{P\ \text{is\ probability\ measure\ on\ }(\Omega,\sigma(\mathcal{H})):E_{P}[X]\leq\hat{\mathbb{E}}[X],\ \ \forall X\in\mathcal{H}\},

(4.3)

where
\sigma(\mathcal{H})
is the smallest
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\mathcal{H}
.

By the Robust Daniell-Stone theorem in Peng
[
27
]
,
\mathcal{P}_{*}
is non-empty and

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[X]=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{*}}E_{P}[X],\ \ \forall X\in\mathcal{H}.

Then the domain of
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
can be enlarged from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{L}(\sigma(\mathcal{H}))
, i.e.,

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[X]=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{*}}E_{P}[X],\ \forall X\in\mathcal{L}(\sigma(\mathcal{H})),

where
\mathcal{L}(\sigma(\mathcal{H}))
is the space of all
\sigma(\mathcal{H})
-measurable functions.

Proposition
3.4
can be naturally generalized on the sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathbb{E}})
where
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
is regular (see Guo et al.
[
7
]
).

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be an i.i.d. sequence on sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathbb{E}})
. We further assume that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\hat{\mathbb{E}}[(|X_{1}|^{2}-\lambda)^{+}]=0.

For fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and for each
P\in\mathcal{P}_{*}
, let
\tilde{X}^{P}_{i}=X_{i}-E_{P}[X_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}],\ 1\leq i\leq n
. Then we have

\underline{V}(X_{1})\leq E_{P}[|\tilde{X}^{P}_{i}|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]\leq\overline{V}(X_{1}),\ \ P-\text{a.s.},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n.

The functional CLT with mean-uncertainty on the canonical space can be easily extended to the sublinear expectation space associated with regular sublinear expectation.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be an i.i.d. sequence on sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathbb{E}})
with

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\hat{\mathbb{E}}[(|X_{1}|^{2}-\lambda)^{+}]=0.

We further assume that
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
is regular and is associated with
\mathcal{P}_{*}
defined by (
4.3
).
For each
P\in\mathcal{P}_{*}
, let
\tilde{X}^{P}_{i}=X_{i}-E_{P}[X_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]
and
S^{(n)}_{P}=(S^{P}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
with
S^{P}_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{i}\tilde{X}^{P}_{j}
. Then for each continuous function
\varphi:C([0,1])\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying
|\varphi(\omega)|\leq C(1+||\omega||_{\infty})
for some constants
C>0
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{*}}E_{P}\left[\varphi\left(\hat{S_{P}^{(n)}}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}}[\varphi],

where
\Theta=[\underline{V}(X_{1}),\overline{V}(X_{1})]
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{0}
be the weakly compact and convex set of probability measures on
(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))
such that

\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}[\varphi]=\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X_{1})],\ \ \forall\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}).

For fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\{Y_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the canonical random variables on
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}))
and
\mathcal{P}
is constructed by (
2.1
) with
\mathcal{P}_{i}=\mathcal{P}_{0}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}[\varphi(Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{n})],\ \ \forall\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Furthermore, for each
P\in\mathcal{P}
, let
\tilde{Y}^{P}_{i}=Y_{i}-E_{P}[Y_{i}|\mathcal{G}_{i-1}]
, where
\mathcal{G}_{i-1}=\sigma(Y_{i},1\leq j\leq i)
, and
T_{P}^{(n)}=(T^{P}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
, where
T^{P}_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{i}\tilde{Y}^{P}_{j}
.

Similar to Proposition
4.5
, we have

\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{*}}E_{P}\left[\varphi\left(\hat{S_{P}^{(n)}}\right)\right]=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}E_{P}\left[\varphi\left(\hat{T_{P}^{(n)}}\right)\right],\ \forall\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\Omega_{0}).

Thus Theorem
4.9
can be implied by Theorem
3.5
.

∎

4.3
Functional central limit theorem without assumption of regularity

Now we relax the assumption of regularity of
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
. For fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{P}_{*}^{(n)}
denote the class of all probability measures on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{n})
that dominated by
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
in the following sense,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{*}^{(n)}=\{P:

\displaystyle E_{P}[\varphi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})]\leq\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})],\ \forall\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}.

where
\mathcal{F}_{n}=\sigma(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})
is the natural filtration with convention
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\{\emptyset,\Omega\}
.

Then
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
is regular on
\mathcal{H}\bigcap\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}_{n})
(see Theorem 10 in Hu and Li
[
10
]
) and the domain of
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
can be enlarged naturally from
\mathcal{H}\bigcap\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}_{n})
to
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}_{n})
, which is defined as

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[X]=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{*}^{(n)}}E_{P}[X],\ \forall X\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}_{n}),

where
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}_{n})
is the space of all
\mathcal{F}_{n}
-measurable functions.

We immediately have the functional CLT without the regularity of
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
by the similar argument as in the previous subsection.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be an i.i.d. sequence on sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathbb{E}})
with

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\hat{\mathbb{E}}[(|X_{1}|^{2}-\lambda)^{+}]=0.

For each
P\in\mathcal{P}_{*}^{(n)}
, where
\mathcal{P}_{*}^{(n)}
is dominated by
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
on
\mathcal{H}\cap\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{F}_{n})
, let
\tilde{X}^{P}_{i}=X_{i}-E_{P}[X_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]
and
S^{(n)}_{P}=(S^{P}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
with
S^{P}_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{i}\tilde{X}^{P}_{j}
. Then for each continuous function
\varphi:C([0,1])\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying
|\varphi(\omega)|\leq C(1+||\omega||_{\infty})
for some constants
C>0
,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{*}^{(n)}}E_{P}\left[\varphi\left(\hat{S_{P}^{(n)}}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}}[\varphi],

where
\Theta=[\underline{V}(X_{1}),\overline{V}(X_{1})]
.

We can generalize CLT in Peng
[
27
]
from mean-zero case to the case of mean-uncertainty.

Corollary 4.10
.

With the same assumptions in Theorem
4.9
, then
\forall\varphi\in C(\mathbb{R})
with linear growth,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{*}^{(n)}}E_{P}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-E_{P}[X_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}])}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}_{G}[\varphi(\xi)],

(4.4)

where
\mathbb{E}_{G}
is the
G
-expectation corresponding to the
G
-normally distributed random variable
\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,[\underline{V}(X_{1}),\overline{V}(X_{1})])
.

Remark 4.11
.

Let
\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,[\underline{\sigma}^{2},\overline{\sigma}^{2}])
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{G}[\varphi(\xi)]=\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}}[\varphi],

where
\Theta=[\underline{\sigma}^{2},\overline{\sigma}^{2}]
and
\varphi
in the right hand can be regarded as
\varphi\in C(\Omega_{0})
with
\varphi(\omega)=\varphi(\omega_{1})
,
\forall\omega\in\Omega_{0}
.

It is well-known that the conditional expectation is the least-squares-best predictor of
X_{i}
based on the realization of the historical data
\{X_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq i-1}
, formula (
4.4
) means that the properly normalized sum for i.i.d. random variables which modified by the least-squares-best predictor tends toward the
G
-normal distribution. Corollary
4.10
indicates the broad applicability of
G
-normal distribution. In particular, for i.i.d. random variables with mean-certainty, the corresponding least-square-best predictor are constants which equals to their certain mean. That is just the CLT in
[
27
]
for the certainty mean.

We also have the CLT in the sense of capacity.

Corollary 4.12
.

Under the same assumptions of Theorem
4.9
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{*}^{(n)}}P\left(a\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-E_{P}[X_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}])}{\sqrt{n}}\leq b\right)=V_{\Theta}([a,b]),

(4.5)

where
V_{\Theta}(A):=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{\Theta}}P(A),\ \forall A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
with
\Theta=[\underline{V}(X_{1}),\overline{V}(X_{1})]
and
-\infty\leq a<b\leq\infty
.

Proof.

For any
0<\varepsilon<\frac{b-a}{2}
, there exists
f^{\varepsilon},g^{\varepsilon}\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R})
such that

{\bf{1}}_{[a+\varepsilon,b-\varepsilon]}(x)\leq g^{\varepsilon}(x)\leq{\bf{1}}_{[a,b]}(x)\leq f^{\varepsilon}(x)\leq{\bf{1}}_{[a-\varepsilon,b+\varepsilon]}(x).

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{G}[g^{\varepsilon}(\xi)]

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{*}^{(n)}}E_{P}\left[g^{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}-E_{P}[X_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{*}^{(n)}}P\left(a\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}-E_{P}[X_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]}{\sqrt{n}}\leq b\right)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{*}^{(n)}}P\left(a\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}-E_{P}[X_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]}{\sqrt{n}}\leq b\right)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{*}^{(n)}}E_{P}\left[f^{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}-E_{P}[X_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}_{G}[f^{\varepsilon}(\xi)]

We note that

V_{\Theta}([a+\varepsilon,b-\varepsilon])\leq\mathbb{E}_{G}[g^{\varepsilon}(\xi)]\leq\mathbb{E}_{G}[f^{\varepsilon}(\xi)]\leq V_{\Theta}([a-\varepsilon,b+\varepsilon]),

and for each
x\in\mathbb{R}
, by Corollary 3.8 in Hu et al.
[
13
]
,
V_{\Theta}([x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon])\rightarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, which implies (
4.5
).
∎

5
Examples

The following example is motivated by Teran
[
31
]
.

Example 5.1
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\Omega))
be a measurable space with
\Omega=0\cup\mathbb{N}
. Let
X_{n}(\omega)
be the
n
-th bit in the binary representation of
\omega
, i.e.,

\omega=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{n-1}X_{n}(\omega)

with
X_{n}(\omega)\in\{0,1\}
.

There does not exist a probability measure
P
on
\Omega
such that
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a classical i.i.d. sequence with
E_{P}[X_{1}]=\frac{1}{2}
. But we can construct linear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathbb{E}})
such that
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is i.i.d. under
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
with
\hat{\mathbb{E}}[X_{1}]=\frac{1}{2}
.

In particular, by Corollary
4.10
, we have

\hat{\mathbb{E}}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(X_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}_{G}[\varphi(\xi)],\ \ \ \forall\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}),

where
\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{1}{4})
.

Otherwise, if such
P
exists, then by classical strong law of large numbers, we have

P\left(\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{S_{n}}{n}=\frac{1}{2}\right)=1,

but for each
\omega\in\Omega
,
X_{n}(\omega)=0
when
n>[\log_{2}\omega]+2
, thus

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{S_{n}(\Omega)}{n}=0,\ \ \forall\omega\in\Omega,

which is a contradiction.

But for fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
P^{(n)}
be the probability measure on
\Omega
such that
P^{(n)}(\{i\})=\frac{1}{2^{n}},\ \forall\ 0\leq i\leq 2^{n}-1
, and
P^{(n)}(\{i\})=0
, if
i\geq 2^{n}
. It is clear that
\{X_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
is a classical i.i.d. sequence with
E_{P^{(n)}}[X_{1}]=\frac{1}{2}
.

Now we construct
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathbb{E}})
such that
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is i.i.d. as in Definition
4.2
and
4.3
. Indeed, let

\mathcal{H}=\{\varphi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n}),\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\forall\varphi\in C_{Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\},

the linear functional
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
on
\mathcal{H}
is defined by

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})]:=E_{P^{(n)}}[\varphi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})],\ \ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

It can be verified that

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X_{i})]=\frac{1}{2}(\varphi(0)+\varphi(1)),\ \forall\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}),\ \forall i\in\mathbb{N},

and for each
n\geq 2
and
\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n})]=\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\varphi(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},X_{n})]_{(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})=(X_{1},\cdots,X_{n-1})}].

We also note that, for fixed
\omega\in\Omega
,
X_{n}(\omega)\downarrow 0
, but
\hat{\mathbb{E}}[X_{n}]=\frac{1}{2}\nrightarrow 0
.
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
is not regular. In fact, if the linear functional
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
satisfying (a) and (b) in Definition
4.1
is regular, then by the Daniell-Stone theorem, there exists probability measure which is represented by such
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
.

We then extend above example to the sublinear expectation space.

Example 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of all probability measures on
(\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\Omega))
with
\Omega=0\cup\mathbb{N}
and
\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\cdot]=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}E_{P}[\cdot]
.

We can verify that the random variables
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, defined by

\omega=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{n}(\omega)2^{n-1},\ \ \ \forall\omega\in\Omega,

are i.i.d. under
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
(see Guo et al.
[
7
]
).

It is clear that

\hat{\mathbb{E}}[X_{1}]=1,\ \ -\hat{\mathbb{E}}[-X_{1}]=0,\ \ \overline{V}(X_{1})=\frac{1}{4},\ \ \underline{V}(X_{1})=0.

Furthermore,
\hat{\mathbb{E}}
is not regular since
\hat{\mathbb{E}}[X_{n}]=1
but
X_{n}(\omega)\downarrow 0
,
\forall\omega\in\Omega
.

By Corollary
4.10
, for each continuous function
\varphi\in C(\mathbb{R})
with linear growth,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}E_{P}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-E_{P}[X_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}])}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}_{G}[\varphi(\xi)],

where
\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,[0,\frac{1}{4}])
.

In the end, the following example shows that condition (
1.1
) in CLT can not weaken to
\hat{\mathbb{E}}[|X_{1}|^{2}]<\infty
.

Example 5.3
.

Let
\Omega=\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{Z})
,
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{k},k\geq 1\}
, where
P_{k}(\{0\})=1-\frac{1}{k^{2}}
,
P_{k}(\{k\})=P_{k}(\{-k\})=\frac{1}{2k^{2}}
. Consider a function
X
on
\mathbb{Z}
defined by
X(\omega)=\omega,\omega\in\mathbb{Z}
and the sublinear expectation
\hat{\mathbb{E}}[\cdot]=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}E_{P}[\cdot]
. We note that
\hat{\mathbb{E}}[X]=\hat{\mathbb{E}}[-X]=0
and
\hat{\mathbb{E}}[X^{2}]=-\hat{\mathbb{E}}[-X^{2}]=1
. We are able to construct an i.i.d. sequence
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
on some sublinear expectation space
(\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{\mathcal{H}},\tilde{\mathbb{E}})
such that
X_{i}
has the same distribution with
X
. Then CLT does not hold.

In fact, we consider
\varphi(x)=1-|x|
. Let
S_{n}=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n}
.

We first observe that

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{x+X}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\left(1-\frac{1}{k^{2}}\right)\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right)+\frac{1}{2k^{2}}\left(\varphi\left(\frac{x-k}{\sqrt{n}}\right)+\varphi\left(\frac{x+k}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\frac{2|x|-|x+k|-|x-k|}{2k^{2}}\right\}=\varphi\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{n}}\right).

Then

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{x+X_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]|_{x=X_{1}+\cdots+X_{n-1}}\right]=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{S_{n-1}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\cdots=\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{X_{1}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]=\varphi(0)=1.

Finally,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{\mathbb{E}}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{S_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]=1>\mathbb{E}_{G}[\varphi(\xi)],

where
\xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
.

6
Appendix: Law of large numbers under sublinear expectation

In this section, we firstly consider the law of large numbers on canonical space.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a canonical i.i.d. sequence on
(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}))
and
\mathcal{P}
be the set of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}
construct by (
2.1
) with
\mathcal{P}_{i}=\mathcal{P}_{1}
, where
\mathcal{P}_{1}
satisfies the following condition

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}_{1}}[(|X_{1}|-\lambda)^{+}]=0.

(6.1)

Then for each
\varphi\in C(\mathbb{R})
with linear growth, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}}{n}\right)\right]=\max_{\underline{\mu}\leq\mu\leq\overline{\mu}}\varphi(\mu),

(6.2)

where
\overline{\mu}={\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}[X_{1}]
and
\underline{\mu}=-{\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}[-X_{1}]
.

In particular,

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}\left[d_{\Theta}\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}}{n}\right)\right]=0,

(6.3)

where
d_{\Theta}(x)=\inf_{y\in\Theta}|x-y|
with
\Theta=[\underline{\mu},\overline{\mu}]
.

Proof.

It is clear that (
6.3
) can be implied from (
6.2
) by taking
\varphi(x)=d_{\Theta}(x)
. To prove (
6.2
), we only need to prove that it holds for
\varphi\in C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed, since

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}\left[\left(\left|\frac{S_{n}}{n}\right|-\lambda\right)^{+}\right]\leq\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}[(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|X_{i}|-\lambda)^{+}]\leq{\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}[(|X_{1}|-\lambda)^{+}]\to 0,

we can extend (
6.2
) from
C_{b,Lip}(\mathbb{R})
to
C(\mathbb{R})
with linear growth by the similar argument in Peng
[
27
]
.

The proof of (
6.2
) for
\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R})
is divided into two steps.

Firstly, let
\tilde{X}_{k}=(-n\vee X_{k})\wedge n
,
1\leq k\leq n
,
\overline{\mu}_{n}={\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}[\tilde{X}_{n}]
and
\underline{\mu}_{n}=-{\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}[-\tilde{X}_{n}]
,
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
. We denote
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
the natural filtration generalized by
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
S_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
,
\tilde{S}_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{X}_{i}
.

For each
P\in\mathcal{P}
, we have

E_{P}\left[\left|\varphi\left(\frac{S_{n}}{n}\right)-\varphi\left(\frac{\tilde{S}_{n}}{n}\right)\right|\right]\leq\frac{L_{\varphi}}{n}E_{P}[|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\tilde{X}_{i})|]\leq L_{\varphi}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}[(|X_{1}|-n)^{+}]\to 0.

\displaystyle E_{P}\left[\left|\varphi\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{S}_{n}}{n}\right)-\varphi\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{P}[\tilde{X}_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]}{n}\right)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{L_{\varphi}^{2}}{n^{2}}E_{P}[\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\tilde{X}_{i}-E_{P}[\tilde{X}_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}])^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{L_{\varphi}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{P}[(\tilde{X}_{i}-E_{P}[\tilde{X}_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}])^{2}]}{n^{2}}\leq\frac{2L_{\varphi}^{2}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}[X_{1}^{2}]}{n}\to 0

where
L_{\varphi}
is Lipschitz constant of
\varphi
. The proof of the last convergence is very similar to the classical case.

It is clear that
\overline{\mu}_{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mu}
and
\underline{\mu}_{n}\to\underline{\mu}
by (
6.1
). Combining with Proposition
2.2
, we imply that

\varphi\left(\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{P}[\tilde{X}_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}]}{n}\right)\leq\max_{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\underline{\mu}_{i}}{n}\leq\mu\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\mu}_{i}}{n}}\varphi(\mu)\to\max_{\underline{\mu}\leq\mu\leq\overline{\mu}}\varphi(\mu).

Thus we can conclude that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{S_{n}}{n}\right)\right]\leq\max_{\underline{\mu}\leq\mu\leq\overline{\mu}}\varphi(\mu).

Secondly, for fixed
\varphi\in C_{b.Lip}(\mathbb{R})
, there exists
\mu^{*}\in[\underline{\mu},\overline{\mu}]
such that
\varphi(\mu^{*})=\max_{\underline{\mu}\leq\mu\leq\overline{\mu}}\varphi(\mu)
. By the construction of
\mathcal{P}
, there exists
P_{*}\in\mathcal{P}
such that
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is an i.i.d. sequence with
E_{P^{*}}[X_{1}]=\mu^{*}
. The classical law of large numbers shows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{P^{*}}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{S_{n}}{n}\right)\right]=\varphi(\mu^{*}),

which implies that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}^{\mathcal{P}}}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{S_{n}}{n}\right)\right]\geq\max_{\underline{\mu}\leq\mu\leq\overline{\mu}}\varphi(\mu).

The proof is completed.

∎

By Proposition
4.5
, we can extend law of large numbers from canonical space to the sublinear expectation space, which is the law of large numbers in Peng
[
27
]
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be an i.i.d. sequence on sublinear expectation space
(\Omega,\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathbb{E}})
with

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\hat{\mathbb{E}}[(|X_{1}|^{2}-\lambda)^{+}]=0.

For each
\varphi\in C(\mathbb{R})
with linear growth, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\hat{\mathbb{E}}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{S_{n}}{n}\right)\right]=\max_{\underline{\mu}\leq\mu\leq\overline{\mu}}\varphi(\mu),

where
\underline{\mu}=-\hat{\mathbb{E}}[-X_{1}]
and
\overline{\mu}=\hat{\mathbb{E}}[X_{1}]
.

Abstract
. Given a prime power q q and a positive integer n n , let 𝔽 q n \mathbb{F}_{q^{n}} represents a finite extension of degree n n of the finite field 𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}_{q}} . In this article, we investigate the existence of m m elements in arithmetic progression, where every element is primitive and at least one is normal with prescribed norms. Moreover, for n ≥ 6 , q = 3 k , m = 2 n\geq 6,q=3^{k},m=2 we establish that there are only 10 10 possible exceptions.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field with
q
elements, where
q=p^{k}
for some prime
p
and positive integer
k
. With respect to multiplication, the set
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, consisting of the non-zero elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, forms a cyclic group and a generator of this multiplicative group is defined to be a primitive element of the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
contains
\phi(q-1)
primitive elements, with
\phi
representing Euler’s totient function. An element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
is primitive if and only if is a root of an irreducible polynomial of degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and such an irreducible polynomial is termed as primitive polynomial. Let
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
represents a field extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree of
n
, where
n
is a positive integer. For any
\alpha

\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, the elements
\alpha,\alpha^{q},\ldots,\alpha^{q^{n-1}}
are referred as the conjugates of
\alpha
with respect to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. An element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is said to be normal over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if the set of conjugates of
\alpha
forms a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, while the basis itself is called normal basis. The
norm
of an element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, denoted by
N_{{q^{n}}/q}(\alpha)
, is the product of the conjugates of
\alpha
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, that is,
N_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha)=\alpha\cdot\alpha^{q}\cdot\alpha^{q^{2}}\ldots\alpha^{q^{n-1}}
. We refer reader
[
10
]
for further informations related to the existence of primitive normal elements.

Primitive elements play a foundational role in Random Number Generation, Signal Processing and Elliptic Curve Cryptography. A lot of applications of primitive elements can be found in Coding theory and Cryptography [
[
16
]
,
[
4
]
], making the study of primitive polynomials and primitive elements an important research area. For any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, a pair
(\alpha,\beta)
is referred as primitive normal pair if both
\alpha
and
\beta
are primitive as well as normal over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In particular, for any
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}(x)
, the existence of primitive normal pairs
(\alpha,f(\alpha))
has been a much interesting research area and many researchers has worked in the direction [
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
].

In
1987
, Lenstra and Schoof
[
7
]
proved the
The Primitive Normal Basis Theorem
, which guarantees that every extension
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
always contains a primitive normal element. In
2000
, Cohen and Hachenberger
[
21
]
demonstrated the existence of primitive and normal element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
with prescribed trace and norm. Recently, Sharma et al.
[
1
]
proved that for any polynomial
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[x]
with minor restrictions, there exists a primitive normal pair
(\alpha,f(\alpha))
such that
Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha)=b
and
N_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha)=a
, for any prescribed
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In
[
2
]
, Lemos et al. investigated the existence of an element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
such that for fixed
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
, any element in the set
\{\alpha,\alpha+\beta,\ldots,\alpha+(m-1)\beta\}
is primitive and at least one of them is normal. In this article, we establish the sufficient condition for the existence of an element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that for fixed
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
, each element in the
m
-term arithmetic progression
\alpha,\alpha+\beta,\ldots,\alpha+(m-1)\beta
is primitive, at least one of them is normal with
N_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\alpha+(i-1)\beta)=c_{i}
, where
c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
for
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
. Here we must have
m
should be less than or equal to the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

2.
Preliminaries

Before proceeding further, we provide some essential notations and significant definitions, which will be used consistently throughout the article. Assume that
n

2.1.
Definitions

1.

Let
G
be an abelain group and
T
be the subset of complex numbers containing elements with unit modulus. A character
\chi
of
G
is a homomorphism from
G
into
T
i.e.,
\chi(g_{1}g_{2})=\chi(g_{1})\chi(g_{2})
for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
. The set of characters of
{G}
, denoted by
\widehat{{G}}
, forms a group under multiplication. In addition,
G
is isomorphic to
\widehat{{G}}
. In the context of a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, an additive character relates to the additive group of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, while a multiplicative character corresponds to the multiplicative group of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
.

2.

Let
e|q^{n}-1
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
. Suppose for any
d|e
and
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
,
\alpha=\beta^{d}
implies
d=1
. Then we say that
\alpha
is a
e
-free element. Thus
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
is primitive
\iff

\alpha
is
(q^{n}-1)
-free.

3.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and
g
be any divisor of
x^{n}-1
. We say that
g
is
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-order of
\alpha
if
\alpha=h\circ\beta
, where
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and
h|g
implies
h=1
. It is easy to observe that an element
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is normal if and only if
\alpha
is
x^{n}-1
-free.

4.

The Euler’s totient function for any polynomial
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is defined as follows:

\Phi_{q}(f)=\Bigg|\Bigg(\frac{\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]}{f\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]}\Bigg)^{*}\Bigg|=q^{deg(f)}\cdot\prod_{p\in\mathcal{I}_{f}}\Bigg(1-\frac{1}{p}\Bigg)

where
\mathcal{I}_{f}
represents the set of irreducible factors of
f
and
f\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is the ideal generated by
f
.

5.

The Möbius function for the set of polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is defined as follows: In case when
f
is product of
r
distinct monic irreducible polynomials then
\mu_{q}(f)=(-1)^{r}
and otherwise
\mu_{q}(f)=0
.

1.
Introduction

A groupoid is a small category whose morphisms are invertible. It is clear that any group is a groupoid, viewed as a category with a unique object. Thus, it is interessing to investigate under which conditions classic results in the context of groups can be extended to groupoids.

One of the main goal of this paper is to relate the concepts of groupoids grading and groupoids actions on a set. Specifically, we present a generalization for the duality theorem given in
[
5
]
for action and grading of groups. The concept of groupoid grading was introduced in
[
7
]
as a natural generalization of group grading. The notion of groupoid action on a set was introduced in
[
10
]
. More precisely, an action of a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
on a set
X
is a collection of subsets
X_{g}
of
X
and bijections
\alpha_{g}:X_{g^{-1}}\to X_{g}
,
g\in\mathcal{G}
, satisfying some appropriate conditions of compatibility. In this case
X
is said a
\mathcal{G}
-set.

Given
A
a
\mathcal{G}
-graded algebra and
X
a split
\mathcal{G}
-set, we define a new algebra, called smash product and denoted by
A\#_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{G}}X
; see Section 3. Now let
\mathcal{K}
be a groupoid and consider the skew groupoid ring
(A\#_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{G}}X)*_{\gamma}\mathcal{K}
, which is well defined according to a compatibility relation between the actions of the groupoids
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{K}
on
X
. This relation allows us to define an action of the groupoid
\mathcal{K}
on the algebra
A\#_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{G}}X
; see Section 4. In this case,
X
is said
(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha},\mathcal{K}_{\beta})
-set. With this notation, we have a duality theorem.

Theorem A
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{K}
be groupoids such that the sets of objects
\mathcal{G}_{0}
and
\mathcal{K}_{0}
are finite,
A
be an algebra
\mathcal{G}
-graded and
X
be a finite split
(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha},\mathcal{K}_{\beta})
-set. If the action of
\mathcal{K}
on
X
is fully faithful, then

\displaystyle(A\#_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{G}}X)*_{\gamma}\mathcal{K}\simeq End({A\#_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{G}}X}_{A\#_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{G}}O^{\mathcal{K}}}),

where
O^{\mathcal{K}}
is the
\mathcal{K}
-orbit of
X
.

Another aim of this paper is to describe the category of the left
A\#_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{G}}X
-modules,
{}_{A\#_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{G}}X}\mathcal{M}
. We show that this category is isomorphic to the category of the left
A
-modules
X
-graded; see Section 5. For each
x\in X
, consider
\mathcal{G}_{x}
the

-stabilizer in
\mathcal{G}
and
A^{\mathcal{G}_{x}}=\oplus_{g\in\mathcal{G}_{x}}A_{g}
. As a consequence of this category isomorphism, we present a condition for that

and

be Morita equivalent.

Theorem B
.

(a)

If
A^{\mathcal{G}_{x}}
and
A\#_{\alpha}^{\mathcal{G}}X
are Morita equivalent and
X_{e}\neq\emptyset
, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
, then

\sum\limits_{{g\in\mathcal{G}}\atop{\alpha_{g}(y)=x}}A_{g^{-1}}A_{g}=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}A_{d(g)},\mbox{ for all }y\in X.

(b)

If
\sum\limits_{{g\in\mathcal{G}}\atop{\alpha_{g}(y)=x}}A_{g^{-1}}A_{g}=\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}A_{d(g)},\mbox{ for all }y\in X
, then

are Morita equivalent.

The paper is organized as follows: in Section 2 we exhibit some preliminary results concerning groupoids, groupoid gradings, groupoid actions on algebras and Galois extension. In Section 3 we present the definitions of
\mathcal{G}
-set and of the smash product

[
5
]
. The Section 5 is dedicated to Morita theory.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Groupoids and groupoids gradings

A
groupoid

\mathcal{G}
is a small category in which every morphism is invertible. Given
g\in\mathcal{G}
, the
domain
and the
range
of
g
will be denoted by
d(g)
and
r(g)
, respectively. Also,
\mathcal{G}_{0}
will denote the set of the objects of
\mathcal{G}
. Hence, we have maps
d,r:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}_{0}
. Given
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
,
id_{e}
will denote the identity morphism of
e
. Observe that
id:\mathcal{G}_{0}\to\mathcal{G}
, given by
id(e)=id_{e}
, is an injective map. Thus, we identify
\mathcal{G}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{d}\times\!_{r}\mathcal{G}=\{(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}:d(g)=r(h)\}
. The map
m:\mathcal{G}_{d}\times\!_{r}\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}
,
m(g,h)=gh
, is called
composition map
. For each pair of objects
e,f\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
, denote by
\mathcal{G}(e,f)=\{g\in\mathcal{G}:d(g)=e,r(g)=f\}
. In particular
\mathcal{G}(e,e)=\mathcal{G}_{e}
is a group, called
isotropy (or principal) group associated to
e
. For all
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
, denote by
D_{e}=\{g\in\mathcal{G}:d(g)=e\}
and
R_{e}=\{g\in\mathcal{G}:r(g)=e\}
. Two groupoids
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
are
equivalent
, and we write
\mathcal{G}\simeq\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
, if they are equivalent as categories.

A non-empty subset
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{G}
is called a
subgroupoid
of
\mathcal{G}
if is stable by multiplication and by inverse. If, in addition,
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathcal{G}_{0}
, then
\mathcal{H}
is said to be a
wide subgroupoid
.

Consider
\mathcal{H}
a wide subgroupoid of
\mathcal{G}
. Define the following equivalence relation on
\mathcal{G}
:

g\equiv_{\mathcal{H}}h
if and only if
gh^{-1}\in\mathcal{H}
, for all
g,h\in D_{e}
,
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
.

We denote by
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
the set of right cosets of
\mathcal{G}
by the relation
\equiv_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Following
[
7
]
, an algebra
A
is
graded by a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
, or
\mathcal{G}
-graded
, if there exists a family
\{A_{g}\}_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
of subspaces of
A
such that
A=\oplus_{g\in\mathcal{G}}A_{g}
, satisfying

\displaystyle A_{g}A_{h}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\subseteq A_{gh},&\mbox{if }(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}_{d}\times\!_{r}\mathcal{G}\\
=0,&\mbox{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

for all
g,h\in\mathcal{G}
. Clearly,
A_{e}
is a subalgebra of
A
, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{G}_{0}
is finite, then each
A_{e}
is unital with identity element
1_{e}
and
1_{A}=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}}1_{e}
.

2.2.
Groupoids actions on algebras and Galois extension

According to
[
2
]
, an
action of a groupoid
\mathcal{G}
on an algebra
A
is a pair
\beta=(E_{g},\beta_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
,
where
E_{g}=E_{r(g)}
,
g\in\mathcal{G}
, is an ideal of
A
and
\beta_{g}:E_{g^{-1}}\to E_{g}
is an isomorphism of algebras, satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

\beta_{e}=id_{E_{e}}
is the identity map of
E_{e}
, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
,

(ii)

\beta_{gh}(x)=\beta_{g}(\beta_{h}(x))
, for all
(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}_{d}\times\!_{r}\mathcal{G}
and
x\in E_{h^{-1}}=E_{(gh)^{-1}}
.

In particular
\beta
induces an action of the group
\mathcal{G}_{e}
on the ideal
E_{e}
, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
.

The
skew groupoid ring
A\ast_{\beta}\mathcal{G}
corresponding to
\beta
, introduced in
[
1
]
, is the direct sum

\displaystyle A\ast_{\beta}\mathcal{G}=\oplus_{g\in\mathcal{G}}E_{g}\delta_{g},

where each symbol
\delta_{g}
is a placeholder for the
g
-th component, with the usual addition and with the multiplication induced by the rule

(x\delta_{g})(y\delta_{h})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}x\beta_{g}(y)\delta_{gh},&\mbox{ if }(g,h)\in\mathcal{G}_{d}\times\!_{r}\mathcal{G}\\
0,&\mbox{ otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

for all
g,h\in\mathcal{G}
,
x\in E_{g}
and
y\in E_{h}
. Clearly, this algebra is associative and
\mathcal{G}
-graded by construction.

Following
[
2
]
, the
subalgebra of invariants
of
A
under
\beta
is the set

A^{\beta}=\{x\in A:\beta_{g}(x1_{g^{-1}})=x1_{g},\forall g\in\mathcal{G}\}.

Moreover,
A
is a
\beta
-Galois extension
of
A^{\beta}
if there exist elements
x_{i},y_{i}\in A
,
1\leq i\leq m
, such that
\sum_{1\leq i\leq m}x_{i}\beta_{g}(y_{i}1_{g^{-1}})=\delta_{e,g}1_{e}
, for all
e\in\mathcal{G}_{0},g\in\mathcal{G}
. The set
\{x_{i},y_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}
is called a
Galois coordinate system
of
A
over
A^{\beta}
.

\mathcal{G}
-sets and smash products

According to
[

]
, an

\alpha=(X_{g},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
, where
X_{g}=X_{r(g)}
,

\alpha_{g}:X_{g^{-1}}\to X_{g}

\alpha_{e}={id}_{X_{e}}

(ii)

-set

Let

\mathcal{G}
-set via

. The subset

is

-invariant

.

.

Every groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is a split
\mathcal{G}
-set via
\alpha=(R_{r(g)},\alpha_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
, where
\alpha_{g}(h)=gh
, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
and
h\in R_{d(g)}
.

Example 3.2
.

Every groupoid
\mathcal{G}
is a split
\mathcal{G}
-set via
\beta=(D_{r(g)},\beta_{g})_{g\in\mathcal{G}}

\beta_{g}(h)=hg^{-1}
, for all
g\in\mathcal{G}
and

.

Example 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a groupoid,

\mathcal{G}

\displaystyle x\approx_{\mathcal{G}}y\,\,\,\mbox{ if and only if }\,\,\,y\in o(x),

.

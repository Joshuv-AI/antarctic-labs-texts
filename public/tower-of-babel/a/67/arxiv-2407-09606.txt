Towards 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -homotopy theory of rigid analytic spaces
Christian Dahlhausen, Can Yaylali

Abstract
† † 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 14F42, 14G22 To any rigid analytic space (in the sense of Fujiwara–Kato) we assign an 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -invariant rigid analytic homotopy category with coefficients in any presentable category. We show some functorial properties of this assignment as a functor on the category of rigid analytic spaces. Moreover, we show that there exists a full six-functor formalism for the precomposition with the analytification functor by evoking Ayoub’s thesis. As an application, we prove mod p p rigidity for rigid spaces over ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} . Moreover, we prove the equivalence of 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -invariant sheaves and 𝔹 1 \mathbb{B}^{1} -invariant sheaves in the mod ℓ \ell case.

Introduction

The
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy category of schemes was constructed and studied by Morel-Voevodsky
[
28
]
.
In modern language, their homotopy category
\textup{H}(S)
is the category of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant Nisnevich sheaves on smooth schemes over a base scheme
S
with values in spaces. Under this construction they show, under some regularity assumptions, that connective algebraic K-theory can be represented as
\mathbb{Z}\times\BGL\in\textup{H}(S)
.
Moreover, after
\otimes
-inverting
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we obtain the stable homotopy category
\textup{SH}(S)
. In his thesis, Ayoub showed that the assignment
S\mapsto\textup{SH}(S)
admits a full six-functor formalism
[
4
]
.
Ayoub deduces this from properties of the functor
\textup{SH}(-)
such as
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariance,
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-stability, and the existence of the localisation sequence. In this article, we are going to extend these results to the analytic setting, using the rigid analytic line
\mathbb{A}^{1}
as an interval.

Motives on rigid analytic varieties have been constructed and studied by Ayoub
[
6
]
. The idea is to follow Morel-Voevodsky’s approach in constructing the stable homotopy category
\textup{RigSH}^{\mathbb{B}^{1}}(X)
for a rigid analytic variety
X
. Ayoub defines the category of rigid motives by Nisnevich localisation, contracting the closed unit disc
\mathbb{B}^{1}
, and then
\otimes
-inverting the unit disc without the origin. In op.​​ cit. it is shown that this definition yields a coefficient system in the sense that any morphism
f
of rigid analytic varieties induces a colimit preserving functor
f^{*}
on
\textup{RigSH}^{\mathbb{B}^{1}}(-)
with the following properties.

(PF)

If
f
is smooth, then
f
admits a left-adjoint satisfying base change and projection formula.

(Loc)

Any closed immersion
i\colon Z\hookrightarrow X
of rigid analytic varieties with open complement
j\colon U\hookrightarrow X
induces a fibre sequence
j_{\#}j^{*}\overset{}{\rightarrow}\id\overset{}{\rightarrow}i_{*}i^{*}
of endofunctors on
\textup{RigSH}^{\mathbb{B}^{1}}(X)
.

While the proof of (PF) is a formality, the proof of (Loc) needs more care and follows the idea of Voevodsky. These two properties and the definition immediately imply that on algebraic maps
X\rightarrow Y
of rigid analytic varieties there exists a six-functor formalism
[
4
, Scholie 1.4.2]
. More recently, in joint work Ayoub–Gallauer–Vezzani generalise this result to arbitrary finite type maps of rigid analytic varieties
[
3
]
.

A drawback of this definition of
\textup{RigSH}^{\mathbb{B}^{1}}
is that not all cohomology theories on rigid analytic varieties satisfy
\mathbb{B}^{1}
-homotopy invariance. For instance, continuous K-theory
\textup{K}^{\textup{cont}}
defined by Morrow
[
29
]
and analytic K-theory defined by Kerz-Saito-Tamme
[
20
]
are not
\mathbb{B}^{1}
-invariant (Remark
3.5
). Hence, these theories cannot be represented in
\textup{RigSH}^{\mathbb{B}^{1}}
. On the other hand, letting
\mathbb{A}^{1}
be the rigid affine line, analytic K-theory is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant
[
22
, Cor. 2.7]
and – assuming resolution of singularities – continuous K-theory is
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant for regular rigid analytic varieties
[
20
, Prop. 5.14]
.
Doing motivic homotopy theory with
\mathbb{A}^{1}
as the interval has already been studied by Sigloch
[
35
]
.
This suggests that one should define the category of motives on a rigid analytic variety
X
as

\textup{RigSH}(X)\coloneqq\textup{Sh}_{\textup{Nis}}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(\textup{SmRig}_{X})[(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S},\infty)^{-1}],

where
(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S},\infty)
denotes the motive of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{S}
pointed at
\infty
; this is equivalent to
\otimes
-inverting the Thom space of any vector bundle on
S
, see Section
4.3
.

In a subsequent paper with Yicheng Zhou, we establish a representability result for
the K-theory of rigid analytic spaces, namely analytic K-theory as defined and studied by Kerz–Saito–Tamme
[
20
,
22
]
. Since this K-theory takes values in pro-categories, it makes sense to consider motives with coefficients in an arbitrary symmetric monoidal stable presentable category
{\mathcal{V}}_{\textup{st}}
. Then we can apply the theory to the categories of pro-spectra and, since pro-categories do not behave that nicely, (light or
\kappa
-small) condensed spectra.

Let us fix a symmetric monoidal presentable category
{\mathcal{V}}
and its stabilisation
{\mathcal{V}}_{\textup{st}}
. The motivating examples include the category
\textup{Cond}_{\kappa}(\textup{Spc})
of
\kappa
-small condensed spaces and the category
\textup{Cond}_{\kappa}(\Sp)
of
\kappa
-small condensed spectra, for a fixed uncountable strong limit cardinal cardinal
\kappa
. The category
\textup{RigSH}(-,{\mathcal{V}})
keeps the property (PF) above and perhaps more surprisingly also (Loc), even without stabilising.

Theorem 1

(
3.18
,
3.29
)
.

The assignment
X\mapsto\textup{Sh}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}_{\textup{Nis}}(X,{\mathcal{V}}),\ f\mapsto f^{*}
, from rigid analytic varieties to symmetric monoidal presentable
{\mathcal{V}}
-linear categories satisfies (PF) and (Loc).

As an immediate consequence, we get a partial six-functor formalism on
RigSH
over a nonarchimedean field

.

Corollary 2

(
4.13
)
.

The assignment
X\mapsto\textup{RigSH}(X^{\textup{an}},{\mathcal{V}}_{\textup{st}}),\ f\mapsto f^{*}

-schemes to symmetric monoidal stable presentable

-linear categories admits a six-functor formalism satisfying base change, purity, and projection formula.

An extension of the six-functor formalism to non-algebraic maps of rigid analytic varieties is not clear. In the classical rigid motivic theory of Ayoub, the rigid affine line and the closed unit disc are
\mathbb{B}^{1}
-homotopic
[
6
, Prop. 1.3.4]
. This will not be the case, when working with
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-homotopy instead. This shows that we need a more careful treatment to obtain a full six-functor formalism for rigid spaces and cannot rely purely on the results of
[
3
]
. Nevertheless, in future work we aim to establish a such full six-functor formalism.

Let us pass to an application of our theory. Following the ideas of Bachmann and Ayoub–Gallauer–Vezzani, we show a rigidity statement. However, unlike
[
3
]
, our theory enables us to work with
\mathbb{Z}/p
-coefficients. We will denote hypercomplete
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariant étale motives with
\mathrm{RigDA}_{\et}^{\mathbb{A}^{1},\wedge}
and the hypercomplete
\mathbb{B}^{1}
-invariant étale motives with
\mathrm{RigDA}_{\et}^{\mathbb{B}^{1},\wedge}

be a nonarchimedead field of residue characteristic

(Rigidity,
5.5
)
.

Let

and assume that

(

Let

Morphisms locally of finite type.

A morphism

A morphism

It holds true that a morphism of adic rigid spaces is smooth in this sense if and only if it smooth in the sense of Huber, see

4.4

-spectra

In this subsection, we shortly want to relate our definition of the stable motivic homotopy category with the more classical approach via

-spectra. This will be an easy consequence from our constructions and the analytification functor

(-)^{\textup{an}}\colon\textup{SH}^{\textup{eff}}(B)\overset{}{\rightarrow}\textup{RigSH}^{\textup{eff}}(B^{\textup{an}}).

Let us first recall what we mean with
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-spectra following Hovey
[
14
]
.A slightly more thorough discussion can be found in
[
31
, §2.3]
. Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a combinatorial simplicial symmetric monoidal model category, i.e. a presentable symmetric monoidal category. For any (cofibrant) object
X\in{\mathcal{C}}
, in fact for any left Quillen endofunctor, one can define the model category of
X
-spectra in
{\mathcal{C}}
, denoted by
\Sp^{\mathbb{N}}({\mathcal{C}},X)
. The underlying
1
-category consists of
X
-spectra

, which are sequences
(F_{0},F_{1},\ldots)
in
{\mathcal{C}}
together with morphisms
X\otimes F_{n}\rightarrow F_{n+1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The notion of morphism of
X
-spectra is the obvious one. We endow
\Sp^{\mathbb{N}}({\mathcal{C}},X)
with the stable model structure
[
14
, §3]
. Let us remark that in general
\Sp^{\mathbb{N}}({\mathcal{C}},X)
is not symmetric monoidal. For this reason one usually passes to
symmetric spectra
together with the stable model structure, denoted by
\Sp^{\Sigma}({\mathcal{C}},X)
. We do not want to go more into detail concerning
\Sp^{\Sigma}({\mathcal{C}},X)
and refer to
[
14
, §7]
. It is important to remark that the associated categories via the simplicial nerve of
\Sp^{\Sigma}({\mathcal{C}},X)
and
\Sp^{\mathbb{N}}({\mathcal{C}},X)
are not equivalent in general. However, as discussed in
[
14
, §10]
, one can see that the only obstruction is the cyclic permutation
(123)\in\Sigma_{3}
self map of
X^{\otimes 3}\coloneqq X\otimes X\otimes X
. To be more precise, Hovey shows that there is a zig-zag of Quillen equivalences between
\Sp^{\Sigma}({\mathcal{C}},X)
and
\Sp^{\mathbb{N}}({\mathcal{C}},X)
if the cyclic permutation on
X\otimes X\otimes X
is homotopic to the identity. In this case

is called
symmetric
. Moreover, Robalo shows that if
X
is symmetric, we have an equivalence

{\mathcal{C}}[X^{-1}]\overset{\simeq}{\longrightarrow}\Sp^{\Sigma}({\mathcal{C}},X)

as presentable symmetric monoidal categories
[
32
, Thm. 2.26]
.

Now let us come back to our situation and show how we can interpret
RigSH
as
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-spectra. For this consider the category
\textup{RigH}(B^{\textup{an}})^{\wedge}_{*}\coloneqq\textup{RigH}(B^{\textup{an}},\textup{Spc}_{*})
with the induced
\otimes
-product via Remark
4.1
. We also denote by
\Gm\coloneqq\mathbb{A}^{1}\setminus\{0\}
the analytic affine line without
0
.

Lemma 4.16
.

The pointed projective line
(\mathbb{P}^{1},\infty)\in\textup{RigH}(B^{\textup{an}})^{\wedge}_{*}
is symmetric and equivalent to

.

In particular, we have

\textup{RigSH}(B^{\textup{an}})\simeq\Sp^{\mathbb{N}}(\textup{RigH}(B^{\textup{an}})^{\wedge}_{*},(\mathbb{P}^{1},\infty)).

Proof.

Combining our discussion in Section
4.1
and Lemma
3.13
, we see that
(-)^{\textup{an}}
is a monoidal functor. The cyclic permutation of

is equivalent to the identity
[

, Lem. 4.4]
. In particular, the same is true for

Moreover, by the same argument we have

f^{\textup{an}*}\dashv f_{*}^{\textup{an}}
satisfying base change and projection formula. Moreover, these functors are compatible with the analytification functor
(-)^{\textup{an}}
from the above.

The following argument follows verbatim the proof of
[
3
, Thm. 2.10.3]
. We will recall the proof, but only up to the point where we need Theorem
4.13
to make the dependence clear.

Let us set
T\coloneqq\bigl(\frac{\mathbb{B}^{1}_{X}}{\mathbb{B}^{1}_{X}\setminus 0}[1]\bigr)^{\wedge}_{\ell}
. By Corollary
5.15
it is enough to treat the
T
-stable case. Moreover, since the question is local, we may assume that
X=\Spf(A)^{\textup{rig}}
is affine and set
U=\Spec(A[\pi^{-1}])
, where
\pi\in A
is a generator of the ideal of definition. Note that
\mathrm{RigDA}_{\et}^{\mathbb{A}^{1}}(X,\Lambda)_{\ell\textup{-comp}}
is generated by colimits under
\textup{M}(\Spf(B)^{\textup{rig}})/\ell^{n}
, where
B
is rig-étale adic over
A
satisfying the conclusion of
[
3
, Prop. 1.3.15]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. As in the proof of
[
3
, Thm. 2.10.3]
, we may assume that there exists a smooth affine
U
-scheme
S
and an open immersion
\nu\colon V\overset{}{\rightarrow}S^{\textup{an}}
such that
\Omega_{S/U}
is free. Fixing a projective compactification
j\colon S\overset{}{\rightarrow}P
over
U
, we obtain the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\textup{an}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{\textup{an}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bar{\nu}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{\textup{an}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\textup{an}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{\textup{an}}}

where
g,f
, and
p
denote the structure maps.

We have
\textup{M}(V)\simeq f_{\sharp}^{\textup{an}}\nu_{\sharp}\Lambda
. By Theorem
4.13
, we see that
\textup{M}(V)
is equivalent, up to shift and twist, to
f_{!}^{\textup{an}}\nu_{\sharp}\Lambda\simeq p^{\textup{an}}_{*}\bar{\nu}_{\sharp}\Lambda
.

By Lemma
5.14
, the map
\iota^{*}
is stable under shifts and twists in the
T
-stable case. Thus, it remains to show that
p^{\textup{an}}_{*}\bar{\nu}_{\sharp}\Lambda/\ell^{n}
is contained in its essential image.

From here on the rest is the same as in
[
3
]
.

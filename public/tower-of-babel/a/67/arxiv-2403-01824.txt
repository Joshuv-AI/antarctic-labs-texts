Introduction

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic and
Q
be a finite, connected and wild quiver without oriented cycles. It is well known that the theory of finite dimensional representations over
Q
is undecidable (see
[
2
, 4.4]
,
[
13
]
), which makes a full classification of the category
\rep(Q)
of finite dimensional representations a hopeless task.

The indecomposable representations of
Q
fall into three classes: There are countable many (isomorphism classes of) so-called preinjective and preprojective indecomposable representations that are well-understood. All other indecomposable representations are called regular. A (not necessarily indecomposable) representation is called regular if all of its indecomposable direct summands are regular and we denote by
\reg(Q)\subseteq\rep(Q)
the full subcategory containing all regular representations. This subcategory contains the large majority of representations and is responsible for the wild behaviour of the category
\rep(Q)
.

Since regular representations are closed under extensions, there is a uniquely determined smallest class of regular representation
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\reg(Q)
closed under isomorphisms, whose extension-closure is
\reg(Q)
. In particular, every representation
M
possesses a (in general not uniquely determined) finite filtration

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset M_{2}\subset\cdots\subset M_{l-1}\subset M_{l}=M

with
M_{i}/M_{i-1}\in\mathcal{E}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,l\}
. The representations in
\mathcal{E}
are called
elementary
and are the simple objects in the category of regular representations. The definition of elementary representations is due to Crawley-Boevey and is a natural generalization of quasi-simple representations living in regular tubes of tame hereditary algebras.

Among other things, elementary representations are of interest because they are closely related (see
[
11
, 3.1]
) to the graph of domination (see
[
10
, 15.2, 15.3]
for a precise definition), whose sinks are given by the extensively studied wild Kronecker algebras corresponding to generalized Kronecker quivers

\textstyle{K_{r}=}
\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\gamma_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\gamma_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\gamma_{r}}
\scriptstyle{\vdots}
\textstyle{2}
\textstyle{,r\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}.}

Since a representation in
\rep(Q)
is elementary if and only if its Auslander-Reiten translate
\tau_{Q}(E)
is elementary and the Coxeter transformation describes the
\tau_{Q}
-orbits on the level of the Grothendieck group, it is natural to consider Coxeter-orbits that belong to elementary representations.

These orbits have been studied systematically in
[
12
,
11
]
and it has been shown that there are only finitely many Coxeter-orbits of dimension vectors of elementary representations. The explicit number
e(Q)\in\mathbb{N}
of Coxeter-orbits of elementary representations is known in a few cases (see for example
[
12
, 4.2.1]
). But even for generalized Kronecker quivers this was unknown until recently: In
[
16
]
the equality
e(K_{3})=4
has been proven.

In this article we tackle the general case and arrive at a criterion thats allows us to decide wether or not a dimension vector
(x,y)
is the dimension vector of an elementary representation. In particular, we can decide wether a Coxeter-orbit belongs to the dimension vector of an elementary representation. As noted in
[
16
]
, it suffices to identify the elements in

\mathcal{F}_{r}\coloneqq\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid\frac{2}{r}x\leq y\leq x\}

that are the dimension vector of an elementary representation to obtain such a criterion. We follow this approach and arrive at:

Theorem A
.

An element
(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{r}
is the dimension vector of an elementary representation if and only if

(\ast)\quad y\leq\min\{\lfloor\frac{x}{r}\rfloor+\frac{x}{\lfloor\frac{x}{r}\rfloor}-r,\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil-\frac{x}{\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil}+r,r-1\},

where we interpret
\lfloor\frac{x}{r}\rfloor+\frac{x}{\lfloor\frac{x}{r}\rfloor}-r
as
\infty
for
1\leq x<r
.

In the following we outline the structure of this article and point out differences to
[
16
]
in the proof of Theorem A. A crucial step in
[
16
]
, to show that an elementary representation
E
with dimension vector
\dimu E\in\mathcal{F}_{3}
has to satisfy
\dimu E\in\{(1,1),(2,2)\}
, is an elegant application of the Projective Dimension Theorem (see
[
7
, I.7.2]
). The Projective Dimension Theorem is used to prove that every
K_{3}
-representation
M
with dimension
(x,y)
and and
2\leq y\leq x+1
has a subrepresentation with dimension vector
(1,2)
.

In the case
r\geq 4
this geometric tool no longer yields strong enough restrictions on dimension vectors in
\mathcal{F}_{r}
that are the dimension vector associated to an elementary representation: For
r=4
the approach does not rule out the dimension vectors
(3,3)\in\mathcal{F}_{4}
altough it can not belong to an elementary representation by Theorem A.

Our approach rests on the consideration of the full subcategories
\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d)\subseteq\rep(K_{r})
(
1\leq d<r
), of so-called
relative
d
-projective
Kronecker representations, each being equivalent to the category of Steiner bundles on the Grassmannian
\Gr_{d}(A_{r})
(see
[
3
, 3.2.3]
), where
A_{r}=\bigoplus^{r}_{i=1}\mathbbm{k}\gamma_{i}
denotes the arrow space of the path algebra
\mathbbm{k}K_{r}
. Restrictions on the minimal rank of non-trivial Steiner bundles, first established in
[
1
]
for fields of characteristic zero, and the connection between relative projective representations and representations with the so-called equal socle property (this definition originated from modular representation theory of finite group schemes) allow us to prove that an elementary representation
E
with dimension vector
(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{r}
has to satisfy
(\ast)
.
For
(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{r}
satisfying
(\ast)
, we show that the elements
f\in\rep(K_{r};\mathbbm{k}^{x},\mathbbm{k}^{x})\coloneqq\Hom_{\mathbbm{k}}(\mathbbm{k}^{x},\mathbbm{k}^{y})^{r}
with
(\mathbbm{k}^{x},\mathbbm{k}^{y},f)\in\rep(K_{r})
elementary form an open set
\mathcal{E}(x,y)
in the affine variety
\rep(K_{r};\mathbbm{k}^{x},\mathbbm{k}^{x})
. We do so by showing that being relative
d
-projective and having the equal socle property is an open property. Moreover, we prove that this set has to be non-empty by dimension reasons, showing that the assumptions in Theorem A are sufficient.

An important tool in the proof of Theorem A is a new description by Reineke (see
[
14
, 3.4]
) concerning general subrepresentations of Kronecker representations. We characterize the category of representations with the equal
d
-socle property as those representations that do not have subpresentations with dimension vector
(1,a)
for all
a\in\{0,\ldots,r-d\}
. This allows us to apply Reineke’s Theorem and generalize Ringel’s approach.

In the last section of this article we study the internal structure of elementary representations for
K_{r}
with
r\geq 3
. For the tame Kronecker quiver
K_{2}
, the quasi-simple representations are well-known and completely determined in terms of their coefficient quiver, i.e. there exists a non-zero element

such that the coefficient quiver has the following form:

\textstyle{\bullet.}

For

, the elementary representations with dimension vector in

can also be described combinatorially in terms of the coefficient quiver (see
[

]
). More precisely: There exists a basis

of the arrow space

such that the coefficient quiver has one of the following two forms:

Rephrasing this in the terms of the natural action of the general linear group
\GL(A_{r})
on
\rep(K_{r})
, this just means that a representation
E\in\rep(K_{3})
with dimension vector in
\mathcal{F}_{3}
is elementary if and only if
M
is isomorphic to an element in the
\GL(A_{r})
-orbit of
E_{1}\coloneqq(\mathbbm{k},\mathbbm{k},(\id_{\mathbbm{k}},0,0))
or
E_{2}\coloneqq(\mathbbm{k}^{2},\mathbbm{k}^{2},(\id_{\mathbbm{k}^{2}},\beta,\gamma))
with
\beta(a,b)=(0,a)
and
\gamma(a,b)=(b,0)
for all

. Since the action of
\GL(A_{r})
on
\rep(K_{r})
commutes with the Auslander-Reiten translation
\tau_{K_{r}}
, we therefore can compute every elementary representation from
E_{1}
and

. We show that the situation is quite different for

.

Theorem B
.

Let
r\geq 4
. Then there are infinitely many, pairwise non-isomorphic elementary representations with the same dimension vector
(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{r}
that all are in different
\GL(A_{r})
-orbits.

It is well known that
\mathbbm{k}K_{s}
is wild algebra if and only
s\geq 3
. In particular, the representation theory of
K_{3}
is as difficult as the representation theory of
K_{r}
for
r\geq 4
. Moreover, in all cases, known to the author, proofs for
K_{3}
can be easily generalized to
K_{r}
by substituting
r
for 3.
However, the above theorem tells us that the problem of classifying elementary representations gets much more difficult, when we consider
r\geq 4
arrows.

1.
Preliminaries

Throughout
\mathbbm{k}
denotes an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic and all
\mathbbm{k}
-vector spaces under consideration are of finite dimension.

1.1.
Wild quivers

We assume that the reader is familiar with basic results on the representations theory of wild quivers. In the following, we will give a brief introduction, recalling the main definitions that we will use throughout this work.
For a well written survey on the subject, where all the details and proofs may be found, we refer to
[
10
]
.

Let
Q
be a finite, connected and wild quiver without oriented cycles and vertex set
Q_{0}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We denote by
\rep(Q)
the category of finite dimensional representations over
Q
and let
\mathbbm{k}Q
be the corresponding path algebra. The category
\rep(Q)
and the category of finite dimensional (left)
\mathbbm{k}Q
-modules
\modd\mathbbm{k}Q
are equivalent which allows us to switch freely between representations and modules.

Let
M\in\modd\mathbbm{k}Q
. Then
\Ext^{1}_{\mathbbm{k}Q}(M,\mathbbm{k}Q)
is a right
\mathbbm{k}Q
-module, so that
\tau_{\mathbbm{k}Q}(M)\coloneqq\Ext^{1}_{Q}(M,\mathbbm{k}Q)^{\ast}
is a left
\mathbbm{k}Q
-module. There results an endofunctor, the
Auslander-Reiten translation
,

\tau_{\mathbbm{k}Q}\colon\modd\mathbbm{k}Q\longrightarrow\modd\mathbbm{k}Q

which is left exact, since
\mathbbm{k}Q
is hereditary. We denote the induced functor on
\rep(Q)
by
\tau_{Q}\colon\rep(Q)\longrightarrow\rep(Q)
.
Similarly, we obtain the functor
\tau^{-1}_{Q}\colon\rep(Q)\longrightarrow\rep(Q)
induced by

\tau^{-1}_{\mathbbm{k}Q}\colon\modd\mathbbm{k}Q\longrightarrow\modd\mathbbm{k}Q;M\mapsto\Ext^{1}_{\mathbbm{k}Q}(M^{\ast},\mathbbm{k}Q)
.

An indecomposable representation
M\in\rep(Q)
is called
preprojective (preinjective)
, provided
\tau^{n}_{Q}(M)=0
(resp.
\tau^{-n}_{Q}(M)=0
) for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. All other indecomposable representations are called
regular
. Since
Q
is a wild quiver, and therefore not of Dynkin type, the three classes preprojective, preinjective and regular are mutually exclusive.

Given a representation
M\in\rep(Q)
, we let
\dimu M\coloneqq(\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be its
dimension vector
. This gives rise to an isomorphism

\dimu\colon K_{0}(\rep(Q))\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n},

which identifies the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\rep(Q))
of
\rep(Q)
with
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Given
i\in Q_{0}
we denote by
S(i)
the simple representation corresponding to
i
and by
P(i)
and
I(i)
its projective cover and injective hull, respectively. The sets
\{\dimu P(i)\mid i\in\mathbb{Q}_{0}\}
,
\{\dimu I(i)\mid i\in\mathbb{Q}_{0}\}
are
\mathbb{Z}
-bases of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. The
Coxeter transformation

\Phi_{Q}
is the
\mathbb{Z}
-linear map
\Phi_{Q}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with

\Phi_{Q}(\dimu P(i))=-\dimu I(i)

for all
i\in Q_{0}
. We have

\dimu\tau_{Q}(M)=\Phi_{Q}(\dimu M)\ \text{and}\ \dimu\tau^{-1}_{Q}(N)=\Phi^{-1}_{Q}(\dimu M)

for
M,N
indecomposable with
M\not\cong P(i),I\not\cong I(i)
for all
i\in Q_{0}
. An arbitary non-zero representation
M\in\rep(Q)
is called
preprojective, preinjective
or
regular
, provided all its indecomposable direct summands are preprojective, preinjective or regular, respectively. By definition the zero representation is preprojective, preinjective and regular.

1.2.
Wild Kronecker quivers

We specialize our considerations to the family of wild Kronecker quivers. Throughout we let
r\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 3}
. The
(generalized) Kronecker quiver
with
r
arrows, denoted by
K_{r}
, is the bipartite quiver with two vertices
1,2
and arrows
\gamma_{i}\colon 1\longrightarrow 2
(
1\leq i\leq r
). A representation
M
over
K_{r}
is a tuple
M=(M_{1},M_{2},(M(\gamma_{i}))_{1\leq i\leq r})
consisting of finite dimensional vector spaces
M_{1},M_{2}
and
\mathbbm{k}
-linear maps
M(\gamma_{i})\colon M_{1}\longrightarrow M_{2}
for each
1\leq i\leq r
. A morphism
f\colon M\longrightarrow N
of representations is a pair
(f_{1},f_{2})
of
\mathbbm{k}
-linear maps such that, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, the diagram

\textstyle{M_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{M(\gamma_{i})}
\scriptstyle{f_{1}}
\textstyle{M_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{2}}
\textstyle{N_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{N(\gamma_{i})}
\textstyle{N_{2}}

commutes. The simple representations corresponding to the vertices
1
and
2
are denoted by
S_{1}=S(1)
and
S_{2}=S(2)
, respectively.
We let
A_{r}\coloneqq\bigoplus^{r}_{i=1}\mathbbm{k}\gamma_{i}
be the
arrow space
of
K_{r}
and realize the path algebra of
K_{r}
as

\mathbbm{k}K_{r}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbbm{k}&0\\
A_{r}&\mathbbm{k}\end{pmatrix}.

We let

\langle-,-\rangle_{r}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z},(x,y)\mapsto x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2}-rx_{1}y_{2}

be the bilinear form given by
K_{r}
, with corresponding
Tits quadratic form

q_{r}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z},x\mapsto\langle x,x\rangle_{r}
.

1.3.
Shift functors

We denote by
\sigma_{K_{r}},\sigma^{-1}_{K_{r}}\colon\rep(K_{r})\longrightarrow\rep(K_{r})
the
shift functors
. These functors correspond to the BGP-reflection functors but take into account that the opposite quiver of
K_{r}
is isomorphic to
K_{r}
, i.e.
D_{K_{r}}\circ\sigma_{K_{r}}\cong\sigma_{K_{r}}^{-1}\circ D_{K_{r}}
, where
D_{K_{r}}\colon\rep(K_{r})\longrightarrow\rep(K_{r})
denotes the standard duality.

For a representation
M\in\rep(K_{r})
we consider the
\mathbbm{k}
-linear map

f_{M}\colon(M_{1})^{r}\longrightarrow M_{2},(m_{i})\mapsto\sum^{r}_{i=1}M(\gamma_{i})(m_{i}).

Then
\sigma_{K_{r}}(M)
is by definition the representation

(\sigma_{K_{r}}(M)_{1},\sigma_{K_{r}}(M)_{2},(\sigma_{K_{r}}(M)(\gamma_{i}))_{1\leq i\leq r})=(\ker f_{M},M_{1},(\pi_{i}|_{\ker f_{M}})_{1\leq i\leq r}),

where
\pi_{i}\colon(M_{1})^{r}\longrightarrow M_{1}
is the projection onto the
i
-th component for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
. Recall that
\sigma_{K_{r}}
induces an equivalence

\sigma_{K_{r}}\colon\rep_{2}(K_{r})\longrightarrow\rep_{1}(K_{r})

between the full subcategories
\rep_{i}(K_{r})
of
\rep(K_{r})
, whose objects do not have have any direct summands isomorphic to
S_{i}
. By the same token,
\sigma^{-1}_{K_{r}}
is a quasi-inverse of
\sigma_{K_{r}}
. The map

\sigma_{r}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{2};(x,y)\mapsto(rx-y,x)

is invertible and satisfies

\dimu\sigma_{K_{r}}(M)=\sigma_{r}(\dimu M)\ \text{and}\ \dimu\sigma^{-1}_{K_{r}}(N)=\sigma^{-1}_{r}(\dimu N)

for all
M\in\rep_{2}(K_{r})
and
N\in\rep_{1}(K_{r})
. Moreover, we have
\sigma_{K_{r}}\circ\sigma_{K_{r}}\cong\tau_{K_{r}}
and
\sigma^{2}_{r}=\Phi_{r}\coloneqq\Phi_{K_{r}}
.

1.4.
Indecomposable representations and Kac’s Theorem

The preprojective and preinjective indecomposable Kronecker representations are well-understood: We define
P_{0}\coloneqq S_{2}
and
P_{i}\coloneqq\sigma^{-1}_{K_{r}}(P_{i-1})
for all
i\geq 1
. The representations
P_{i}
form a complete list of representatives of the isomorphism classes of indecomposable preprojective Kronecker representations. By the same token, a complete list of representatives of the isomorphism classes of indecomposable preinjective Kronecker representations is given by
I_{i}\coloneqq D_{K_{r}}(P_{i})
,
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Since
\sigma_{r}
and
\sigma_{r}^{-1}
leave the Tits form invariant and
q_{r}(1,0)=1=q_{r}(0,1)
, this shows that
q_{K_{r}}(\dimu N)=1
for
N
indecomposable and preprojective or preinjective.
We let
L_{r}\coloneqq\frac{r+\sqrt{r^{2}-4}}{2}
and note that
L_{r}
and
\frac{1}{L_{r}}
are the roots of the polynomial
f_{r}\coloneqq X^{2}-rX+1\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
. Therefore they satisfy the equation
\frac{1}{L_{r}}=r-L_{r}
. Moreover, we have
r-1<L_{r}<r
since
r\geq 3
. We let
\delta\colon\mathbb{Z}^{2}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{2};(a,b)\mapsto(b,a)
be twist function on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
Then we have

\dimu P_{i}=(a_{i},a_{i+1})=\delta(\dimu I_{i}),\ \text{where for all}\ i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\ a_{i}\coloneqq\frac{(L_{r})^{i}-(\frac{1}{L_{r}})^{i}}{\sqrt{r^{2}-4}}.

We recall a simplified version of Kac’s Theorem (see
[
9
, Thm.B]
and
[
15
, Thm.3]
) and an immediate consequence thereof that suffice for our purposes.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Kac’s Theorem for
K_{r}
)
Let
\delta\in\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}\setminus\{0\}
.

(1)

If
\delta=\dimu M
for some indecomposable
M\in\rep(K_{r})
, then
q_{r}(\delta)\leq 1
.

(2)

If
q_{r}(\delta)=1
, then there is a, up to isomorphism, unique indecomposable representation
M\in\rep(K_{r})
such that
\dimu M=\delta
. The representation
M
is preprojective or preinjective and preprojective if and only if
\delta_{1}\leq\delta_{2}
.

(3)

If
q_{r}(\delta)\leq 0
, then there are infinitely many pairwise non-isomorphic indecomposable representations with dimension vector
\delta
, each being regular.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
M\in\rep(K_{r})
be indecomposable. The following statements hold.

(1)

M
is preprojective if and only if
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{1}<\frac{1}{L_{r}}\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{2}
.

(2)

M
is regular if and only if
\frac{1}{L_{r}}\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{2}<\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{1}<L_{r}\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{2}
.

(3)

M
is preinjective if and only if
L_{r}\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{2}<\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{1}
.

2.
Geometric considerations and restrictions on dimension vectors

Throughout this section
d
denotes a natural number with
1\leq d<r
. For
(x,y)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}
we write

\Delta_{(x,y)}(d)\coloneqq y-dx\ \text{and}\ \nabla_{(x,y)}(d)\coloneqq dy-x.

For a representation
M\in\rep(K_{r})
, or vector spaces
M_{1},M_{2}\in\modd\mathbbm{k}
, we define

\Delta_{M}(d)\coloneqq\Delta_{(M_{1},M_{2})}(d)\coloneqq\Delta_{(\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{1},\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{2})}(d)\ \text{and}

\nabla_{M}(d)\coloneqq\nabla_{(M_{1},M_{2})}(d)\coloneqq\nabla_{(\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{1},\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{2})}(d).

2.1.
Relative projective representations and vector bundles

Let
M\in\rep(K_{r})
be a representation with
structure map

\psi_{M}\colon A_{r}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{1}\longrightarrow M_{2}\ ;\ \sum^{r}_{i=1}\gamma_{i}\otimes m\mapsto\sum^{r}_{i=1}M(\gamma_{i})(m).

We say that
M\in\rep(K_{r})
is
relative
d
-projective
, provided that
\psi_{M}|_{\mathfrak{v}\otimes M_{1}}
is injective for each
\mathfrak{v}\in\Gr_{d}(A_{r})
, where
\Gr_{d}(A_{r})
denotes the Grassmannian of
d
-dimensional subspaces of
A_{r}
.

Remark 2.1
.

The terminology "relative
d
-projective" is motivated by the fact that
\psi_{M}|_{\mathfrak{v}\otimes M_{1}}
is injective if and only the restriction of the
\mathbbm{k}K_{r}
-module
M
to the subalgebra

\mathbbm{k}K_{d}\cong\mathbbm{k}\mathfrak{v}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\mathbbm{k}&0\\
\mathfrak{v}&\mathbbm{k}\end{pmatrix}\subseteq\mathbbm{k}K_{r}

is projective (cf.
[
3
, 2.1.5]
).

The full subcategory of relative
d
-projective representations is denoted by
\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d)
. This category is a torsion-free class (see
[
3
, 2.2.1]
) closed under
\sigma_{K_{r}}^{-1}
and gives rise to special vector bundles (locally free coherent sheaves) on
\Gr_{d}(A_{r})
. In the following we recall results and definitions from
[
3
]
and
[
1
]
.

Let
\Vect(\Gr_{d}(A_{r}))
be the category of vector bundles on
\Gr_{d}(A_{r})
with structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\Gr_{d}(A_{r})}
. Moreover, let
\mathcal{U}_{(r,d)}
be the
universal vector bundle
of
\Gr_{d}(A_{r})
. A locally free sheaf
\mathcal{F}\in\Coh(\Gr_{d}(A_{r}))
is called
Steiner bundle
, provided there exist vector spaces
V_{1},V_{2}
and a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow V_{1}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}\mathcal{U}_{(d,r)}\longrightarrow V_{2}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}\mathcal{O}_{\Gr_{d}(A_{r})}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow 0.

We denote by
\StVect(\Gr(A_{r}))
the full subcategory of Steiner bundles on
\Gr_{d}(A_{r})
. The following result is proven in
[
3
, 2.3.2, 3.3.2, 3.3.3]
. The proof of (2) elaborates on
[
1
, 2.4]
, where the result was first shown for algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero.

Theorem 2.2
.

The following statements hold.

(1)

There exists a fully faithful and exact functor

\widetilde{\Theta}_{d}\colon\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d)\longrightarrow\Vect(\Gr_{d}(A_{r}))

with essential image
\StVect(\Gr_{d}(A_{r}))
. Moreover, there is a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow M_{1}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}\mathcal{U}_{(r,d)}\longrightarrow M_{2}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}\mathcal{O}_{\Gr_{d}(A_{r})}\longrightarrow\widetilde{\Theta}_{d}(M)\longrightarrow 0

for each
M\in\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d)
.

(2)

For each Steiner bundle

0\longrightarrow V_{1}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}\mathcal{U}_{(r,d)}\longrightarrow V_{2}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}\mathcal{O}_{\Gr_{d}(A_{r})}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow 0

we have
\rk(\mathcal{F})\geq\min\{d(r-d),(\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}V_{1})(r-d)\}
.

(3)

Let
M\in\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d)
, then
\Delta_{M}(d)\geq\min\{d(r-d),\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{1}(r-d)\}
.

We record direct consequences of
Theorem

2.2
that will be needed later on when we study elementary representations.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
M\in\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},d)\coloneqq D_{K_{r}}(\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d))
, then

-\nabla_{M}(d)\geq\min\{d(r-d),\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{2}(r-d)\}.

Proof.

Since
M\in\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},d)
, we have
D_{K_{r}}(M)\in\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d)
and therefore

\displaystyle-\nabla_{M}(d)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{1}-d\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{2}=\Delta_{D_{K_{r}}(M)}(d)

\displaystyle\geq\min\{d(r-d),\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}(D_{K_{r}}(M))_{1}(r-d)\}=\min\{d(r-d),\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{2}(r-d)\}.

∎

Corollary 2.4
.

The following statements hold.

(1)

Let
M\in\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d)
with
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{1}\leq d
, then
M
is projective.

(2)

Let
M\in\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},d)
with
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}M_{2}\leq d
, then
M
is injective.

Proof.

(1)

Let
N
be an indecomposable direct summand of
M
not isomorphic to
P_{0}
. Then
N_{1}\neq 0
,
N\in\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d)
and
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{1}\leq d
.
We have

\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{2}-d\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{1}=\Delta_{N}(d)\geq\min\{d(r-d),\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{1}(r-d)\}=\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{1}(r-d)

and conclude
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{2}\geq r\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{1}
. Since
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{1}\neq 0
, we also have a projective resolution

0\longrightarrow P_{0}^{r\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{1}-\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{2}}\longrightarrow P_{1}^{\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{1}}\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow 0

and conclude
r\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{1}-\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{2}=0
as well as
N\cong P_{1}^{\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{1}}
.

(2)

This follows from duality.

∎

2.2.
The equal socle property and connections to relative projective representations

Constant rank type modules have been defined and studied in
[
6
]
in the context of elementary abelian
p
-groups over fields of characteristic
p
as a generalization of constant Jordan type modules. Inspired by these considerations, representations with the equal socle type have been introduced in
[
4
]
for Kronecker representations over fields of arbitrary characteristic. It is the aim of this section characterize dimension vectors that admit representations with the equal socle property. This description plays a crucial role in
Section

3
, when we determine the dimension vectors admitting an elementary representation.

We obtain these restrictions with the help of a recent result of Reineke in the framework of generic representations for Kronecker representations.
Let
M\in\rep(K_{r})
and
\mathfrak{v}\in\Gr_{d}(A_{r})
. Given
a=\sum^{r}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}\gamma_{i}\in A_{r}
we denote by
a_{M}\colon M_{1}\longrightarrow M_{2}
the
\mathbbm{k}
-linear map

a_{M}\colon M_{1}\longrightarrow M_{2}\ ;\ m\mapsto a.m\coloneqq\sum^{r}_{i=1}\alpha_{i}M(\gamma_{i})(m).

Definition
.

(cf.
[
4
, 2.3]
).
A representation
M\in\rep(K_{r})
has the
equal
d
-socle property
, provided
\{0\}=\bigcap_{a\in\mathfrak{v}}\ker a_{M}

for all
\mathfrak{v}\in\Gr_{d}(A_{r})
.

We note that
\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},d)
and
\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d)
are closed under subrepresentations and direct sums. Relative projective representations and representations with the equal socle property are closely related:

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
N\in\rep(K_{r})
and
1\leq d<r
. The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

N\in\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d)
.

(2)

\sigma_{K_{r}}(N)\in\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r-d)
.

Proof.

We define
M\coloneqq\sigma_{K_{r}}(N)
. Clearly,
P_{0}\in\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d)
and
\sigma_{K_{r}}(P_{0})=\{0\}\in\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r-d)
. Since the involved categories are closed under direct sums and summands, we may assume that
N
does not have
P_{0}
as a direct summand.

(1)
\implies
(2). We assume that
N\not\in\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d)
. By definition we find
\mathfrak{v}\in\Gr_{d}(A_{r})
such that

\psi_{N}|_{\mathfrak{v}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{1}}\colon\mathfrak{v}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{1}\longrightarrow N_{2};a\otimes n\mapsto a.n

is not injective. Let
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d})
be a basis of
\mathfrak{v}
and
x=\sum^{d}_{j=1}a_{j}\otimes n_{j}
be a non-zero element in
\ker\psi_{N}|_{\mathfrak{v}\otimes N_{1}}
. We write
a_{j}=\sum^{r}_{i=1}\beta_{ij}\gamma_{i}
for
1\leq j\leq d
and set
n_{i}^{\prime}\coloneqq\sum^{d}_{j=1}\beta_{ij}n_{j}
for
1\leq i\leq r
. By definition we have

x=\sum^{d}_{j=1}\sum^{r}_{i=1}\beta_{ij}\gamma_{i}\otimes n_{j}=\sum^{r}_{i=1}\gamma_{i}\otimes\sum^{d}_{j=1}{\beta_{ij}n_{j}}=\sum^{r}_{i=1}\gamma_{i}\otimes n_{i}^{\prime}.

Recall that

M_{2}=N_{1},M_{1}=\ker(N_{1}^{r}\longrightarrow N_{2};(y_{i})_{1\leq i\leq r}\mapsto\sum^{r}_{i=1}\gamma_{i}.y_{i})

and
\gamma_{j}.((y_{i})_{1\leq i\leq r})=M(\gamma_{j})((y_{i})_{1\leq i\leq r})=y_{j}
for
j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
. We have

0=\psi_{N}|_{\mathfrak{v}\otimes N_{1}}(x)=\sum^{r}_{i=1}\gamma_{i}.n_{i}^{\prime},

which shows
m\coloneqq(n_{i}^{\prime})\in M_{1}\setminus\{0\}
. Let
A\coloneqq\{\delta\in\mathbbm{k}^{r}\mid\sum^{r}_{i=1}\delta_{i}n_{i}^{\prime}=0\}
. Since
\sum^{r}_{i=1}\mathbbm{k}n_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq\sum^{d}_{i=1}\mathbbm{k}n_{i}
, we have
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}A\geq r-d
. We fix a subspace
B\subseteq A
of dimension
r-d
. Let
\mathfrak{u}\coloneqq\{\sum^{r}_{i=1}\delta_{i}\gamma_{i}\mid\delta\in B\}\in\Gr_{r-d}(A_{r})
. Let
a=\sum^{r}_{i=1}\delta_{i}\gamma_{i}\in\mathfrak{u}
, then
a.m=\sum^{r}_{i=1}\delta_{i}\gamma_{i}.m=\sum^{r}_{i=1}\delta_{i}n_{i}^{\prime}=0
. Hence
0\neq m\in\bigcap_{a\in\mathfrak{u}}\ker a_{M}
and
M\not\in\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r-d)
.

(2)
\implies
(1). Assume that
M\not\in\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r-d)
. We find
\mathfrak{u}\in\Gr_{r-d}(A_{r})
and
0\neq m\in\bigcap_{a\in\mathfrak{u}}\ker a_{M}\setminus\{0\}
. By definition we have
m=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})\in N_{1}^{r}\setminus\{0\}
and
0=\sum^{r}_{i=1}\gamma_{i}.n_{i}
. Let
a=\sum^{r}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}\gamma_{i}\in\mathfrak{u}
, then
0=a_{M}(m)=\sum^{r}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}M(\gamma_{i})(m)=\sum^{r}_{i=1}\lambda_{i}n_{i}
.
Hence
\{\delta\in\mathbbm{k}^{r}\mid\sum^{r}_{i=1}\delta_{i}n_{i}=0\}
is a vector space of dimension at least
r-d
and
\sum^{r}_{i=1}\mathbbm{k}n_{i}
a vector space of dimension at most
d
. Let
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
be a basis of
\sum^{r}_{i=1}\mathbbm{k}n_{i}
. We write
n_{i}=\sum^{m}_{j=1}\lambda_{ij}x_{j}
for
1\leq i\leq r
and let
b_{l}\coloneqq\sum^{r}_{j=1}\lambda_{jl}\gamma_{j}
for
1\leq l\leq m
. Let
\mathfrak{v}\in\Gr_{d}(A_{r})
such that
\sum^{m}_{l=1}\mathbbm{k}b_{l}\subseteq\mathfrak{v}
. We have
0\neq\sum^{m}_{l=1}b_{l}\otimes x_{l}\in\mathfrak{v}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}N_{1}
and

\displaystyle\psi_{N}|_{\mathfrak{v}\otimes N_{1}}(\sum^{m}_{l=1}b_{l}\otimes x_{l})

\displaystyle=\sum^{m}_{l=1}(\sum^{r}_{j=1}\lambda_{jl}\gamma_{j}).x_{l}=\sum^{r}_{j=1}\gamma_{j}.\sum^{m}_{l=1}\lambda_{jl}x_{l}=\sum^{r}_{j=1}\gamma_{j}.n_{j}=0.

Hence
N\not\in\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},d)
.
∎

2.3.
Generic representations and applications

Let
(V_{1},V_{2})
be a pair vector spaces. We denote by
\rep(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})\coloneqq\Hom_{\mathbbm{k}}(V_{1},V_{2})^{r}
the affine variety of representations of
K_{r}
on
(V_{1},V_{2})
. Given
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\rep(K_{r})
and
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\rep(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})

Let
\bd\coloneqq(\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}V_{1},\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}V_{2})
. For
\be\leq\bd\in\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}
(componentwise) we let
\rep(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})_{\be}
be the Zariski-closed subset (cf.
[
17
, 3.1]
) of
\rep(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})
consisting of all representations admitting a subrepresentation of dimension vector

if

\be\not\hookrightarrow\bd

[
17
]
a criterion (in a way more general setting) in characteristic zero to decide whether

holds. Crawley-Boevey extended this criterion in
[
5
]
to positive characteristic. The statemenent in the Kronecker setting reads as follows:

Theorem 2.6

(Crawley-Boevey, Schofield)
.

We have
\be\hlra\bd
if and only if
\langle\bff,\bd-\be\rangle_{r}\geq 0
for all
\bff\hlra\be
.

For imaginary roots the statement can be simplified:

Proposition 2.7
.

(see
[
14
, 3.4]
)
Assume that
q_{r}(\bd)\leq 0
. The following statements are equivalent for
\be\in\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}
with
\be\leq\bd
.

(1)

\be\hookrightarrow\bd
.

(2)

\langle\be,\bd-\be\rangle_{r}\geq 0
.

We adapt the proof of Reineke to show:

Proposition 2.8
.

The following statements are equivalent for
\be\in\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}
with
\be\leq\bd
and
q_{r}(\be)\leq 1
.

(1)

\be\hlra\bd
.

(2)

\langle\be,\bd-\be\rangle_{r}\geq 0
.

Proof.

(1)
\implies
(2). Apply
Theorem

2.6
for
\bff=\be
.

(2)
\implies
(1). Let
\bff\hlra\be
. In view of
Theorem

2.6
it suffices to show that
\langle\bff,\bd-\be\rangle_{r}\geq 0
.
Since
q_{r}(\be)\leq 1
holds,
\be
is a Schur root (see for example
[
3
, 1.2.2]
). Hence
[
17
, 6.1]
implies
0<\langle\bff,\be\rangle_{r}-\langle\be,\bff\rangle_{r}=r(e_{1}f_{2}-e_{2}f_{1})
. In particular,
e_{1}\neq 0
and
f_{2}>\frac{e_{2}f_{1}}{e_{1}}
. We conclude with

\displaystyle\langle\bff,\bd-\be\rangle_{r}

\displaystyle=f_{1}(d_{1}-e_{1}-r(d_{2}-e_{2}))+f_{2}(d_{2}-e_{2})\geq f_{1}(d_{1}-e_{1}-r(d_{2}-f_{2}))+\frac{f_{1}e_{2}}{e_{1}}(d_{2}-e_{2})

with
3<4=r
and claim that
(6,3)\in\mathcal{E}_{4}
. We have
\nabla_{(6,3)}(3)=3\geq 3=3(4-3)
. Moreover, we have

-\nabla_{(6,3)}(1)=3\geq 1(4-1)
. Hence
Theorem

2.11
and
Corollary

2.14
imply that
\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},3)\cap\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},1)\cap\mathcal{B}(6,3)
is non-empty. We fix a representation
E
in the above set. Let
0\neq U\subseteq E
a non-preprojective representation. We now show that
E/U
is preinjective. Since
U
is not projective, we find
0\neq u\in U_{1}
. We consider the subrepresentation
\langle u\rangle
generated by
u
. Then
\dimu\langle u\rangle=(1,z)
for some
z\in\{0,1,2,3\}
. Since
\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},3)
is closed under subrepresentations, we have
\langle u\rangle\in\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},3)
and conclude with
Theorem

2.11
that
3z-1=\nabla_{(1,z)}(3)\geq 3(4-3)=3
. Hence
z\geq 2
. Therefore
\dimu E/\langle u\rangle=(5,b)
with
b\in\{0,1\}
. Since
E\in\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},1)
and
\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},1)
is closed under images (since
\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},1)
is closed under subrepresentation), we have
E/\langle u\rangle\in\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},1)
. Now we apply
Corollary

2.4
to conclude that
E/\langle u\rangle
is preinjective. Finally, the presence of the canonical epimorphism
E/\langle u\rangle\longrightarrow E/U
implies that
E/U
is injective.

Now we consider the general case and start with the following simple observation.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
y=1
, then
\mathcal{E}(x,y)=\mathcal{B}{(x,y)}\neq\varnothing
.

Proof.

Let
M\in\mathcal{B}(x,y)
, then
M
is indecomposable and regular. Let
U\subseteq M
be a proper subrepresentation. Then
0\neq\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}U_{2}
and therefore
M/U\in\add(I_{0})
is injective. In particular,
M
is elementary. This shows
\varnothing\neq\mathcal{B}(x,y)=\mathcal{E}(x,y)
.
∎

We assume from now on that
1<y<r
and set
b\coloneqq\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil\in\mathbb{N}
which is the uniquely determined natural number such that

(b-1)r<x\leq br.

Remark 3.5
.

.

(1)

We have
1\leq b<y<r
: Assume that
\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil=b\geq y
. Then
\frac{x}{r}>y-1
and therefore

\frac{r}{2}y\geq x=\frac{x}{r}r>ry-r.

Hence
2>y
, a contradiction since we assume
2\leq y
.

(2)

We extend to definition of
\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},d)
to
d\in\{0,\ldots,r-1\}
be setting
\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},0)\coloneqq\rep(K_{r})
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{r}
with
1<y<r
and
b\coloneqq\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil
. The following statements hold.

(1)

If
\mathcal{E}(x,y)
is non-empty, then

(b-1)(y+r-(b-1))\leq x\leq b(r-y+b)

and
\mathcal{E}(x,y)\subseteq\mathcal{B}{(x,y)}\cap\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r-y+b)\cap\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},b-1)
.

(2)

If

(b-1)(y+r-(b-1))\leq x\leq b(r-y+b),

then
\mathcal{E}(x,y)
is a non-empty open set given by

\mathcal{E}(x,y)=\mathcal{B}{(x,y)}\cap\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r-y+b)\cap\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},b-1).

Proof.

(1)

Let
E
be an elementary representation with dimension vector
\dimu E=(x,y)
. We denote by
F\coloneqq D_{K_{r}}(E)
the dual representation with dimension vector
(y,x)
. We proceed in steps.

(i)

We have
(b-1)(y+r-(b-1))\leq x
and
E\in\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},b-1)
: We assume that
x<(b-1)(y+r-(b-1))
or
E\not\in\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},b-1)
. In both cases we conclude
b\neq 1
and therefore
b-1\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}
. If
x<(b-1)(y+r-(b-1))
, we have

\Delta_{F}(b-1)=x-(b-1)y<(b-1)(r-(b-1))\ \text{and}\ x\leq\frac{r}{2}y<ry\ \text{gives}

\displaystyle\Delta_{F}(b-1)

\displaystyle=x-(b-1)y<ry-(b-1)y=y(r-(b-1))

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}F_{1}(r-(b-1)).

Hence
Theorem

2.2
(3) implies
F\not\in\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},b-1)
. If
E\not\in\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},b-1)
, we immediately get
F\not\in\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},b-1)
from the definition.

The book-keeping
: In both cases we arrive at
F\not\in\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},b-1)
with
b-1\neq 0
.

In view of
Proposition

2.9
we find
a\in\{1,\ldots,b-1\}
and subrepresentation
Y\not\in\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},b-1)
of
F
with
\dimu Y=(a,a^{\prime})
and
a^{\prime}\leq ar-1\leq(b-1)r-1
. The inequality
(b-1)r-1<x
ensures that we can extend
Y
with a semisimple projective direct summand to a subrepresentation
Y
of
F
with dimension vector
(a,(b-1)r-1)
that satisfies
Y\not\in\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},b-1)
. In particular,
Y
is not preprojective by
Proposition

2.9
.

Since
F
is elementary, we can apply
Proposition

3.1
to conclude that
(y-a,x-(b-1)r+1)=\dimu F/X
belongs to a preinjective representation. But this is impossible since
x-(b-1)r+1\geq 1
,
y-a<r
and
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}(I_{l})_{1}\geq r
for all
l\geq 1
and
\dimu I_{0}=(1,0)
.
Hence
(b-1)(y+r-(b-1))\leq x
and
E\in\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},b-1)
.

(ii)

We have
x\leq b(r-y+b)
and
E\in\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r-y+b)
:
We assume that
x>b(r-y+b)
. We set
d\coloneqq r-(y-b)
and note that
d\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}
by
Remark

3.5
. We get

\displaystyle\nabla_{E}(d)-d(r-d)

\displaystyle=d(y-r+d)-x

\displaystyle=b(r-y+b)-x<0.

Hence
E\not\in\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},d)
by
Theorem

2.11
. Since
r-d=y-b\leq y
, we conclude with
Corollary

2.15
that there exists a non-preprojective subrepresentation
U_{r-d}\subseteq E
with dimension vector
(1,r-d)
. Once again we apply
Proposition

3.1
and conclude that
E/U_{r-d}
with dimension vector
(x-1,y-(r-d))=(x-1,b)
is preinjective. We apply
Lemma

3.2
(3) and conclude
br\leq x-1
. But this is a contradiction to the definition of
b
since
x\leq br
.

We note that this also shows
E\in\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},d)=\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r-y+b)
.

(2)

We set
d\coloneqq r-(y-b)\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}
and have

\nabla_{E}(d)-d(r-d)=d(y-r+d)-x=b(r-y+b)-x\geq 0,

and

\quad-\nabla_{E}(b-1)=x-(b-1)y\geq(b-1)(r-(b-1)).

We can apply
Theorem

2.11
and
Corollary

2.14
(for
b\neq 1
) to conclude that
\mathcal{B}{(x,y)}\cap\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},d)\cap\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},b-1)
is non-empty (for
b=1
we have
\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},b-1)=\rep(K_{r})
). We fix a representation
E
in this space and show now that
E
is elementary.

Let
U\subseteq E
be a non-preprojective representation, then we find
u\in U\setminus\{0\}
. Recall from
Section

2.2
that
\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},d)
is closed under subrepresentation. Therefore the subrepresentation
\langle u\rangle
generated by
u
is in
\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},d)
and
\dimu\langle u\rangle=(1,z)
for some
z\in\{1,\ldots,y\}
. We conclude with
Theorem

2.11
that

dz-1=\nabla_{\langle u\rangle}(d)\geq d(r-d)\Leftrightarrow d(z-(r-d))\geq 1.

In particular,
z\geq r-d+1=y-(b-1)
. In other words,
E/\langle u\rangle
satisfies
\dimu E/\langle u\rangle=(x-1,a)
with
0\leq a\leq b-1
.
If
b=1
, we conclude that
a=0
and therefore
\dimu E/\langle u\rangle
is injective and the the presence of the canonical epimorphism
E/\langle u\rangle\longrightarrow E/U
implies that
E/U
is injective.
If
b\neq 1
we have
E\in\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},b-1)
with
b-1\neq 0
. Since
\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},b-1)
is closed under images (since
\rep_{\proj}(K_{r},b-1)
is closed under subrepresentation), we have
\dimu E/\langle u\rangle\in\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},b-1)
and can apply
Corollary

2.4
to conclude that
E/\langle u\rangle
is injective and presence of the canonical epimorphism
E/\langle u\rangle\longrightarrow E/U

implies that
E/U
is preinjective.
Hence
E
is elementary by
Proposition

3.1
.

The book-keeping
: We have shown that

\varnothing\neq\mathcal{B}{(x,y)}\cap\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r-y+b)\cap\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},b-1)\subseteq\mathcal{E}(x,y).

Now we apply (1) to finish the proof.

∎

Remark 3.7
.

We extend the definition of
\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},d)
to
\{1,\ldots,r\}
by setting
\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r)\coloneqq\rep(K_{r})
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{r}
.

(1)

\mathcal{E}(x,y)\neq\varnothing
implies
y<r
.

(2)

For
y<r
the following statements are equivalent.

(i)

\mathcal{E}(x,y)\neq\varnothing
.

(ii)

(\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil-1)(y+r-(\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil-1))\leq x\leq\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil(r-y+\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil)
.

If one the equivalent statements holds, we have

\mathcal{E}(x,y)=\mathcal{B}{(x,y)}\cap\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r-y+\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil)\cap\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil-1).

Proof.

(1)

This is the statement of
Proposition

3.3
.

(2)

At first we assume that
y=1
. Then
x\leq\frac{r}{2}y<r
and
\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil=1
. So in this case the inequalities in (ii) are always satisfied and by
Lemma

3.4
we have
\mathcal{E}(x,y)=\mathcal{B}(x,y)\neq\varnothing
as well as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}(x,y)=\mathcal{B}(x,y)\cap\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r)\cap\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},0)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}(x,y)\cap\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r-y+\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil)\cap\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil-1).

Now we assume that
1<y<r
. Then the equivalence of (i) and (ii) is precisely the statement of
Proposition

3.6
.

∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{r}
such that
y\leq x<r
. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{E}(x,y)\neq\varnothing
.

(2)

x+y\leq r+1
.

In this case we have

\mathcal{E}(x,y)=\mathcal{B}{(x,y)}\cap\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r-y+1).

Proof.

We have
\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil=1
. Hence
(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}
satisfies the inequality of the above Theorem if and only if
x\leq r-y+1
. Moreover, we have in this case

\mathcal{E}(x,y)=\mathcal{B}{(x,y)}\cap\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r-y+1)\cap\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},0)=\mathcal{B}{(x,y)}\cap\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r-y+1).

∎

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{r}
. The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

\mathcal{E}(x,y)\neq\varnothing
.

(2)

\lfloor\frac{x}{r}\rfloor(y+r-\lfloor\frac{x}{r}\rfloor)\leq x\leq\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil(r-y+\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil)
and
y<r
.

(3)

y\leq\min\{\lfloor\frac{x}{r}\rfloor+\frac{x}{\lfloor\frac{x}{r}\rfloor}-r,\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil-\frac{x}{\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil}+r,r-1\}
, where we interpret
\lfloor\frac{x}{r}\rfloor+\frac{x}{\lfloor\frac{x}{r}\rfloor}-r
as
\infty
for
1\leq x<r
.

If one of the equivalent statements holds, we have

\mathcal{E}(x,y)=\mathcal{B}{(x,y)}\cap\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r-y+\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil)\cap\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},\lfloor\frac{x}{r}\rfloor).

Proof.

Assume that
\frac{x}{r}\in\mathbb{N}
. In this case we have
\frac{x}{r}=\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil
and

x<ry\Leftrightarrow x>x-\frac{x}{r}y+(\frac{x}{r})^{2}\Leftrightarrow x>\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil(r-y+\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil).

Now
Theorem

3.8
implies
\mathcal{E}(x,y)=\varnothing
. Hence we can assume
\frac{x}{r}\not\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil-1=\lfloor\frac{x}{r}\rfloor
and
Theorem

3.8
implies the equivalence of (1) and (2).

The equivalence of (2) and (3) follows from direct computation and
Corollary

3.9
.
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

We have

\mathcal{E}_{r}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{N}_{\leq\frac{r(r-1)}{2}}\times\mathbb{N}_{\leq r-1}\mid\frac{2x}{r}\leq y\leq\min\{\lfloor\frac{x}{r}\rfloor+\frac{x}{\lfloor\frac{x}{r}\rfloor}-r,\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil-\frac{x}{\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil}+r,x\}\}.

Proof.

Recall that
(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{r}
with
\mathcal{E}(x,y)
implies
\frac{2x}{r}\leq y\leq x
and
y\leq r-1
. In particular,
x\leq\frac{r(r-1)}{2}
.
∎

4.
Orbits of elementary representations

It has been shown in
[
16
]
that elementary representations
E
with dimension vector in
\mathcal{E}_{3}=\{(1,1),(2,2)\}
can be described combinatorially in terms of their coefficient quiver. More precisely: There exists a basis
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
of the arrow space
A_{3}
such that the coefficient quiver of
E
has one of the following two forms:

\textstyle{\bullet\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\textstyle{\bullet\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\beta}
\textstyle{\bullet\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\gamma}
\textstyle{\bullet}
\textstyle{\bullet}
\textstyle{\bullet.}

In the following we rephrase this result in terms of an algebraic group acting on the variety of representations. Let
V_{1},V_{2}
be vector spaces. We consider the canonical action of the general linear group
\GL(A_{r})
on
\rep(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})
: Given
g\in\GL(A_{r})
and
f\in\rep(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})
, we write
g^{-1}(\gamma_{i})=\sum^{r}_{j=1}\lambda_{ij}^{(g)}\gamma_{j}
with
\lambda_{ij}^{(g)}\in\mathbbm{k}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
and let
f^{(g)}\in\rep(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})
be the tuple with entries

(f^{(g)})_{i}=\sum^{r}_{j=1}\lambda_{ij}^{(g)}f_{j},1\leq i\leq r.

The algebraic group

G_{(V_{1},V_{2})}\coloneqq\GL(A_{r})\times\GL(V_{2})\times\GL(V_{1})

acts on the space of representations
\rep(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})
via

\displaystyle G_{(V_{1},V_{2})}\times\rep(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\rep(K_{r};V_{1},V_{2})

\displaystyle((g,h_{2},h_{1}),f)

\displaystyle\mapsto((h_{2}\circ f_{i}\circ h_{1}^{-1})_{1\leq i\leq r})^{(g)}=(h_{2}\circ(f^{(g)})_{i}\circ h_{1}^{-1})_{1\leq i\leq r}.

Note that
\dim G_{(V_{1},V_{2})}=r^{2}+(\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}V_{1})^{2}+(\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}V_{2})^{2}
.
Moreover, we have an action of
\GL(A_{r})
on
\rep(K_{r})

\GL(A_{r})\times\rep(K_{r})\longrightarrow\rep(K_{r});(g,N)\mapsto N^{(g)}\coloneqq(N_{1},N_{2},(N(\gamma_{i}))_{1\leq i\leq r}^{(g)})

and an induced action on the isomorphism classes of Kronecker representations
[N]^{(g)}\coloneqq[N^{(g)}]
.

Now let
M_{1},M_{2}
be vector spaces and
\varnothing\neq\mathcal{O}\subseteq\rep(K_{r};M_{1},M_{2})
be a
G_{(M_{1},M_{2})}
-invariant subset. We let
[\mathcal{O}]\coloneqq\{[N]\mid N\in\rep(K_{r}),\exists f\in\mathcal{O}:N\cong(M_{1},M_{2},f)\}
. By definition we have a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathcal{O}/G_{(M_{1},M_{2})}
and
[\mathcal{O}]/\GL(A_{r})
. For
(x,y)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
we let

G_{(x,y)}\coloneqq\GL(A_{r})\times\GL(\mathbbm{k}^{x})\times\GL(\mathbbm{k}^{y}).

Since regular representations are
\GL(A_{r})
-invariant, the set
\mathcal{E}(x,y)\subseteq\rep(K_{r};\mathbbm{k}^{x},\mathbbm{k}^{y})
is
G_{(x,y)}
-invariant. Since
\GL(A_{r})
acts transitive on bases of
A_{r}
, we can rephrase the aforementioned results as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

(see
[
16
, Theorem]
) The following statements hold.

(1)

We have
\mathcal{E}_{3}=\{(1,1),(2,2)\}
.

(2)

The sets
\mathcal{E}(1,1)
,
\mathcal{E}(2,2)
are orbits under the action of
G_{(1,1)}
and
G_{(2,2)}
on
\rep(K_{3};\mathbbm{k},\mathbbm{k})
and
\rep(K_{3};\mathbbm{k}^{2},\mathbbm{k}^{2})
, respectively.

(3)

Let
M\in\rep(K_{3})
be a representation with dimension vector
(1,1)
. The representation is elementary if and only if there is
g\in\GL(A_{3})
such that
M^{(g)}\cong(\mathbbm{k},\mathbbm{k},(\id_{\mathbbm{k}},0,0))
.

(4)

Let
M\in\rep(K_{3})
be a representation with dimension vector
(2,2)
. The representation is elementary if and only if there is
g\in\GL(A_{3})
such that
M^{(g)}\cong(\mathbbm{k}^{2},\mathbbm{k}^{2},(\id_{\mathbbm{k}^{2}},\beta,\gamma))
with
\beta(a,b)=(0,a)
and
\gamma(a,b)=(b,0)
for all
(a,b)\in\mathbbm{k}^{2}
.

In following we show that we can not hope for such a nice classification in case
r\geq 4
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\varnothing\neq\mathcal{O}\subseteq\rep(K_{r};\mathbbm{k}^{x},\mathbbm{k}^{y})
be a non-empty open and
G_{(x,y)}
-invariant subset of
\rep(K_{r};\mathbbm{k}^{x},\mathbbm{k}^{y})
such that
q_{r}(x,y)<-r^{2}
. Then
\mathcal{O}/G_{(x,y)}
is not finite.

Proof.

We set
G\coloneqq G_{(x,y)}
. We assume that
\mathcal{O}/G
is finite and fix
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\in\mathcal{O}
such that
\mathcal{O}=\bigcup^{n}_{i=1}G.T_{i}
. Hence

\rep(K_{r};\mathbbm{k}^{x},\mathbbm{k}^{y})=\overline{\mathcal{O}}=\bigcup^{n}_{i=1}\overline{G.T_{i}}.

Because
\rep(K_{r};\mathbbm{k}^{x},\mathbbm{k}^{y})
is irreducible, we find
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
such that
\rep(K_{r};\mathbbm{k}^{x},\mathbbm{k}^{y})=\overline{G.T_{i}}
.
Since orbits are open in their closure (
[
8
, 8.3]
), we conclude with
[
7
, 1.10]
that

\dim G\geq\dim G.T_{i}=\dim\overline{G.T_{i}}=\dim\rep(K_{r};\mathbbm{k}^{x},\mathbbm{k}^{y})=rxy.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\dim G-rxy

\displaystyle=r^{2}+x^{2}+y^{2}-rxy=r^{2}+q_{r}(x,y),

in contradiction to the assumption.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}_{r}
such that
q_{r}(x,y)<-r^{2}
. Then
\mathcal{E}(x,y)/G_{(x,y)}
is not finite.

Proof.

Since
(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}_{r}
, we can apply
Theorem

3.8
and conclude that

\mathcal{E}(x,y)=\mathcal{B}{(x,y)}\cap\rep_{\esp}(K_{r},r-y+\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil)\cap\rep_{\inj}(K_{r},\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil-1)

is a non-empty open subset of
\rep(K_{r};\mathbbm{k}^{x},\mathbbm{k}^{y})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{E}(x,y)
is
G_{(x,y)}
-invariant, since regular representations are
\GL(A_{r})
-invariant. Now we apply
Lemma

4.2
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
r\geq 4
. Then there are infinitely pairwise non-isomorphic elementary representations with dimension vector
(r+2,3)
that all are in different
\GL(A_{r})
-orbits.

Proof.

We set
x=r+2
and
y=3<r
. Then
(x,y)\in\mathcal{F}_{r}
and

\lfloor\frac{x}{r}\rfloor(y+r-\lfloor\frac{x}{r}\rfloor)=r-2\leq x\leq 2(r-1)=\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil(r-y+\lceil\frac{x}{r}\rceil).

Now
Corollary

3.10
implies that
(r+2,3)\in\mathcal{E}_{r}
. Moreover, we have
q_{K_{r}}(r+2,3)=-2r^{2}-2r+13<-r^{2}
and can apply
Corollary

4.3

∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
(x,y)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
such that
q_{r}(x,y)<-r^{2}
. The number of different
\GL(A_{r})
-orbits of isomorphism-classes of elementary representation with dimension vector
(x,y)
is either
0
or infinite.

Proof.

We can assume that there is
E\in\rep(K_{r})
elementary with dimension vector
(x,y)
. By applying
D_{K_{r}}
and powers of
\sigma_{K_{r}}
to
E
we find an elementary representation
F
with dimension vector
\dimu F\in\mathcal{E}_{r}
. Since
\sigma_{K_{r}}
and
D_{K_{r}}
do not change the quadratic form, we have

q_{r}(\dimu F)=q_{r}(x,y)<-r^{2}.

Now
Corollary

4.3
implies that we get infinitely many orbits. Since
D_{K_{r}}
and
\sigma_{r}
respect
\GL(A_{r})
-orbits (see for example
[
3
, 6.1.3]
), the statements follows.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

Let
E\in\rep(K_{3})
be elementary. Then
[
16
]
implies that
q_{3}(\dimu E)\in\{-1,-4\}
. Hence
q_{3}(\dimu E)\geq-9=-r^{2}
.

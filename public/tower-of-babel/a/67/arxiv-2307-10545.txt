Loday-Quillen-Tsygan theorem on Quivers
Keyou Zeng

Abstract
. The well-known Loday-Quillen-Tsygan theorem calculates the Lie algebra homology of the infinite general linear Lie algebra 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( A ) \mathfrak{gl}(A) over an unital associative algebra A A . We generalize the Loday-Quillen-Tsygan theorem to an infinite Lie algebra associated with a (framed) quiver, where we assign to each vertex v v an infinite general linear Lie algebra 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( A v ) \mathfrak{gl}(A_{v}) , to each edge e e an infinite matrix module and to each framed vertex a (anti)-fundamental representation. Given this data, each loop or path ending on framed vertices of the quiver defined a stratified factorization algebra over S 1 S^{1} or [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] respectively. We show that the corresponding Lie algebra homology can be expressed as summing the factorization homology over all loops and framed paths of the quiver.



1.
Introduction

The celebrated Loday-Quillen-Tsygan (LQT) theorem
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
states that for any associative algebra
A
in characteristic zero, the Lie algebra homology of the infinite general linear Lie algebra over
A
is canonically isomorphic, as a Hopf algebra, to the exterior power of the cyclic homology of
A
:

\lim_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{N}}H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A))\cong\mathrm{Sym}(HC_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(A)[1]).

This theorem admit various generalization to many different setups. One can replace the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A)
by the Lie algebra of symplectic matrices
\mathfrak{sp}_{N}(A)
or orthogonal matrices
\mathfrak{o}_{N}(A)
. Then the corresponding Lie algebra homology is computed by the dihedral homology
[
6
]
. There is also a generalization of the LQT theorem where one considers the Lie algebra homology of
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A)
with coefficient in the adjoint matrices representation
[
5
]
.

In this paper, we consider an extension of the LQT theorem to an infinite Lie algebra associated with a framed quiver. Recall that a quiver
Q
is a directed graph that consist of two sets
(Q_{0},Q_{1})
and two functions

s,t:Q_{1}\rightrightarrows Q_{0}.

The set
Q_{0}
is the set of vertices and
Q_{1}
is the set of oriented edges. An edge
e\in Q_{0}
starts at the vertex
s(e)
and ends on the vertex
t(e)
.

A framed quiver
Q^{\text{fr}}
consists of the data of a quiver
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
together with two subsets
W^{+},W^{-}
of
Q_{0}
as the framed vertices. Denote the inclusion map
j:W^{+}\sqcup W^{-}\to Q_{0}
. We can think of the framed quiver
Q^{\text{fr}}
as adding to
Q
extra vertices
W^{+},W^{-}
and edges
w^{+}\to j(w^{+})
for all
w^{+}\in W^{+}
, and
j(w^{-})\to w^{-}
for all
w^{-}\in W^{-}
.

For a framed quiver
Q^{\text{fr}}
, we assign to it the following set of data

\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{c}To each vertex $v\in Q_{0}$ we assign a unital $k$ algebra $A_{v}$\\
To each edge $e\in Q_{1}$ we assign a $A_{s(e)}-A_{t(e)}$ bimodule $M_{e}$\\
To each vertex $w^{+}\in W^{+}$ we assign a right $A_{j(w^{+})}$ module $M_{w^{+}}$\\
To each vertex $w^{-}\in W^{-}$ we assign a left $A_{j(w^{-})}$ module $M_{w^{-}}$\end{tabular}\right\}.

Given this data, we have the following Lie algebra

\bigoplus_{v\in Q_{0}}\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A_{v}),

and its module

(1.1)

\bigoplus_{e\in Q_{1}}\mathrm{Mat}_{N}(M_{e})\bigoplus_{w^{-}\in W^{-}}\mathbb{C}^{N}(M_{w^{-}})\bigoplus_{w^{+}\in W^{+}}(\mathbb{C}^{N})^{*}(M_{w^{+}}).

In this paper, we consider the Lie algebra homology of
\bigoplus_{v\in Q_{0}}\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A_{v})
with coefficient in the symmetric power of the module
1.1
in the
N\to\infty
limit

(1.2)

\lim_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{N}}H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(\bigoplus_{v\in Q_{0}}\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A_{v}),\mathrm{Sym}(\bigoplus_{e\in Q_{1}}\mathrm{Mat}_{N}(M_{e})\bigoplus_{w^{+}\in W^{+}}\mathbb{C}^{N}(M_{w^{+}})\bigoplus_{w^{-}\in W^{-}}(\mathbb{C}^{N})^{*}(M_{w^{-}}))).

Equivalently, this is isomorphic to the Lie algebra homology of the following super Lie algebra

\left(\bigoplus_{v\in Q_{0}}\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A_{v})\right)\ltimes\left(\bigoplus_{e\in Q_{1}}\mathrm{Mat}_{N}(M_{e})\bigoplus_{w^{+}\in W^{+}}\mathbb{C}^{N}(M_{w^{+}})\bigoplus_{w^{-}\in W^{-}}(\mathbb{C}^{N})^{*}(M_{w^{-}})\right)[-1].

The main theorem we prove in this paper is the following

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.2
)
.

The Lie algebra homology
1.2
is isomorphic, as a Hopf algebra, to the following

(1.3)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Sym}&\left(\bigoplus_{v\in Q_{0}}HC_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(A_{v})[1]\right.\\
&\bigoplus_{\ell=(e_{1},\dots,e_{n})\in\mathrm{Cyc}(Q)}H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}\left(M_{e_{1}}\otimes_{A_{t(e_{1})}}^{\mathbb{L}}M_{e_{2}}\otimes_{A_{t(e_{2})}}^{\mathbb{L}}\dots\otimes_{A_{t(e_{n-1})}}^{\mathbb{L}}M_{e_{n}}\otimes_{A_{t(e_{n})}}^{\mathbb{L}}\right)_{\mathbb{Z}_{\deg\ell}}\\
&\left.\bigoplus_{\rho=(w^{+},e_{1},\dots,w^{-})\in\mathrm{fPath}(Q)}H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}\left(M_{w^{+}}\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A_{j(w^{+})}}M_{e_{1}}\otimes_{A_{t(e_{1})}}^{\mathbb{L}}\dots M_{e_{n}}\otimes_{A_{j(w^{-})}}^{\mathbb{L}}M_{w^{-}}\right)\right),\end{split}

where
\mathrm{Cyc}(Q)
is the set of cycles in the quiver and
\mathrm{fPath}
is the set of paths ending on framed vertices of the quiver. Here, the notation
\cdots\otimes_{A_{t(e_{n})}}
with noting on the right means tensor product with the first factor on the left.

There is a more concise way to express our above result in terms of stratified factorization algebra
[
9
]
. In fact, a cycle
\ell=(e_{1},\dots,e_{m})\in\mathrm{Cyc}(Q)
gives us a set of associative algebras
A_{s(e_{i})}
and
A_{s(e_{i})}-A_{t(e_{i})}
bimodules
M_{e_{i}}
. This data
(A_{s(e_{i})},M_{e_{i}})
defines a stratified factorization algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
on
S^{1}
with
m
marked points. Similarly, a path
\rho\in\mathrm{fPath}(Q)
defines a stratified factorization algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\rho}
on
[0,1]
. Then we can rewrite
1.3
(Theorem
4.3
) as follows

\mathrm{Sym}\left(\bigoplus_{v\in Q_{0}}\left(\int_{S^{1}}A_{v}\right)_{S^{1}}[1]\oplus\bigoplus_{\ell\in\mathrm{Cyc}(Q)}\left(\int_{S^{1}}\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\right)_{\mathbb{Z}_{\deg\ell}}\oplus\bigoplus_{\rho\in\mathrm{fPath}(Q^{\mathrm{fr}})}\left(\int_{[0,1]}\mathcal{A}_{\rho}\right)\right).

3.
Preliminaries

3.1.
Fundamental Theorems of Invariant Theory

In this section, we briefly review some classical results on invariant theory. We refer to
[
4
]
for more detail.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional
k
-vector space and let
GL(V)
be its group of automorphisms. The left action of the symmetric group
S_{n}
on
V^{\otimes n}
is given by place permutation

\sigma(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})=(v_{\sigma^{-1}(1)},\dots,v_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}).

The above map gives rise to a ring homomorphism

\mu:k[S_{n}]\to\mathrm{End}(V^{\otimes n}).

Let
GL(V)
act on
V^{\otimes n}
diagonally
\alpha(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})=(\alpha v_{1},\dots,\alpha v_{n})
, and act on
\mathrm{End}(V^{\otimes n})
by conjugation
\alpha(f)=\alpha\circ f\circ\alpha^{-1}
. We find that the image of
\mu
lies in the invariant algebra
\mathrm{End}(V^{\otimes n})^{GL(V)}
. Therefore, we have the ring homomorphism:

\mu:k[S_{n}]\to\mathrm{End}(V^{\otimes n})^{GL(V)}.

We have the following theorem from classical invariant theory.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
V
be a
k
-vector space with
\dim V=r\geq n
. Then
\mu:k[S_{n}]\to\mathrm{End}(V^{\otimes n})^{GL(V)}
is an isomorphism.

It will be useful to translate the fundamental theorems of invariant theory into the coinvariant framework. The natural pairing on
V^{*}\otimes V
introduces a trace map on
\mathrm{End}(V)
,
\text{Tr}:\;\mathrm{End}(V)\overset{\approx}{\longrightarrow}V^{*}\otimes V\overset{\langle-,-\rangle}{\longrightarrow}k
. This trace map induces the nondegenerate pairing:

\displaystyle\mathrm{End}(V)\otimes\mathrm{End}(V)\to k;

\displaystyle\alpha\otimes\beta\mapsto\text{Tr}(\alpha\beta).

It follows immediately that
\mathrm{End}(V)^{\otimes n}\approx(\mathrm{End}(V)^{\otimes n})^{*}
.

Note that for a
G
module
W
, we have an isomorphism
(W_{G})^{*}\cong(W^{*})^{G}
. Specifically, we consider the
GL(V)
module
\mathrm{End}(V^{\otimes n})
. Then we have

\mathrm{End}(V^{\otimes n})^{GL(V)}\cong(\mathrm{End}(V)^{\otimes n})^{GL(V)}\cong((\mathrm{End}(V)^{\otimes n})^{*})^{GL(V)}\cong((\mathrm{End}(V)^{\otimes n})_{GL(V)})^{*}.

This determines a map

T:k[S_{n}]\to\mathrm{End}(V^{\otimes n})^{GL(V)}\cong((\mathrm{End}(V)^{\otimes n})_{GL(V)})^{*}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\sigma=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{j})(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{k})\dots(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{l})
be the cycle decomposition of the permutation
\sigma
. Then the map
T
defined above is explicitly given by

(3.1)

T(\sigma)(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})=\Tr(\alpha_{\mu_{1}}\cdots\alpha_{\mu_{j}})\Tr(\alpha_{\nu_{1}}\cdots\alpha_{\nu_{k}})\cdots\Tr(\alpha_{\rho_{1}}\cdots\alpha_{\rho_{l}}).

The
k
-module
k[S_{n}]
is canonically isomorphic to its dual
k[S_{n}]^{*}
since it comes with a preferred pairing
(\sigma,\tau)=\delta_{\sigma\tau}
. Thus, we have a dual map

T^{*}:(\mathrm{End}(V)^{\otimes n})_{GL(V)}\to k[S_{n}].

It is clear that we have

T^{*}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}T(\sigma)(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\sigma,

where
T(\sigma)(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
is given by the formula
3.1
. More explicitly, we have

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\omega
and
\sigma
be two permutations in
S_{n}
. The map
T^{*}
satisfies

T^{*}(E_{\omega(1)\sigma(1)},E_{\omega(2)\sigma(2)},\dots,E_{\omega(n)\sigma(n)})=\sigma\circ\omega^{-1}.

As an associate algebra,
\mathrm{End}(V)
inherits a structure of Lie algebra, which we denote as
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a module over itself, and so is the tensor product
\mathfrak{gl}(V)^{\otimes n}
. We have a right action

[(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n}),\alpha]=\sum_{i}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{i-1},[\alpha_{i},\alpha],\alpha_{i+1},\dots,\alpha_{n}).

Lemma 3.1
.

The module of coinvariants
(\mathfrak{gl}(V)^{\otimes n})_{\mathfrak{gl}(V)}
is equal to the module
(\mathfrak{gl}(V)^{\otimes n})_{GL(V)}
.

Therefore, for the case
\dim V=r\geq n
, we have an isomorphism

(\mathfrak{gl}(V)^{\otimes n})_{\mathfrak{gl}(V)}\cong k[S_{n}].

This isomorphism plays an important role in the LQT theorem and its generalization we present in this paper.

3.2.
Hochschild and Cyclic homology

In this section, we review aspects of Hochschild and Cyclic homology.

A bimodule over
A
is a
k
-module
M
that is both a left and a right
A
-module for which
(am)b=a(mb)
, for any
a,b\in A
,
m\in M
. We see that
M
being a
A
bimodule is equivalent to
M
being a
A^{e}
left module, where the action is defined by
(a\otimes b)m=amb
. The algebra
A
itself is a
A^{e}
-module given by the left and right multiplication:
(a\otimes b)c=acb
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
M
be an
A
bimodule. The Hochschild homology of
A
with coefficient in
M
, denoted
H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(A,M)
, is defined as

(3.2)

\mathrm{Tor}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}^{A^{e}}(A,M)=H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(M\otimes_{A^{e}}^{\mathbb{L}}A).

For
M=A
,
H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(A,A)
is also called the Hochschild homology of
A
. We denote it as
H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(A)
.

We use the standard bar construction to compute the Hochschild homology.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
M
be an
A
bimodule. The Hochschild Bar complex of
A
with coefficient in
M
is defined to be
B_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(A,M)=M\otimes(\oplus_{n\geq 0}(A[1])^{\otimes n})
. We define the following maps
B_{n}(A,M)\to B_{n-1}(A,M)
:

\begin{split}d_{1}(m\otimes a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{n})&=ma_{1}\otimes a_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{n},\\
d_{i}(m\otimes a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{n})&=m\otimes\dots\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{n},\\
d_{n}(m\otimes a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{n})&=a_{n}m\otimes a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{n-1}.\end{split}

Then the Hochschild differential
b:B_{n}(A,M)\to B_{n-1}(A,M)
is given by
b=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}d_{i}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

We have the following isomorphism

H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(A,M)\cong H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(B_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(A,M),b).

It will be convenient for us to use a relative version of the Bar complex to compute the Hochschild homology. Let
S
be a subring of
A
. We define the Bar complex over
S
as follows:

B^{S}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(A,M):=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}M\otimes_{S}\underbrace{A[1]\otimes_{S}A[1]\otimes_{S}\dots A[1]}_{n}\otimes_{S}.

Remark 3.1
.

Recall that the notation
M\otimes_{S}A\otimes_{S}\dots A\otimes_{S}
means
(M\otimes_{S}A\otimes_{S}\dots A)\otimes_{S^{e}}S
.

The Hochschild differential
b
is compatible with this definition so there is a well-defined complex
(B^{S}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(A,M),b)
. Denote its
n
-th homology by
HH^{S}_{n}(A,M)
. We are interested in the cases when
HH^{S}_{n}(A,M)
is isomorphic to the Hochschild homology.

Definition 3.3
.

An unital
k
-algebra
S
is called separable if there exists an element

e=\sum u_{i}\otimes v_{i}\in S\otimes S^{op},

such that
\sum u_{i}v_{i}=1
and
(s\otimes 1)e=(1\otimes s)e
for any
s\in S
. Such an element
e
is also called the idempotent.

We have the following slight generalization of
[
4
]
Theorem 1.2.13.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
S
be a separable
k
algebra. Then for any unital
S
-algebra
A
there is a canonical isomorphism

HH_{n}(A,M)\cong HH^{S}_{n}(A,M).

Proof.

There is an canonical projection
\phi:B_{n}(A,M)\to B^{S}_{n}(A,M)
.

Using the idempotent
e=\sum u_{i}\otimes v_{i}
one can construct a map
\psi:B^{S}_{n}(A,M)\to B_{n}(A,M)
:

(3.3)

\psi(m\otimes_{S}a_{1}\otimes_{S}\dots a_{n}\otimes_{S})=\sum_{j_{0},j_{1},\dots,j_{n}}v_{j_{n}}mu_{j_{0}}\otimes v_{j_{0}}a_{1}u_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{j_{n-1}}a_{n}u_{j_{n}}.

It is easy to check that
\phi\circ\psi=id
. On the other hand, we construct a pre-simplicial homotopy between
\psi\circ\phi
and
id
. Let

(3.4)

h_{i}(m\otimes a_{1}\otimes\dots a_{n})=\sum_{j_{0},j_{1},\dots,j_{i}}mu_{j_{0}}\otimes v_{j_{0}}a_{1}u_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots v_{j_{i-1}}a_{i}u_{j_{i}}\otimes v_{j_{i}}\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes\dots a_{n}.

We can check that
d_{0}h_{0}(m\otimes a_{1}\otimes\dots a_{n})=d_{0}(\sum_{j}mu_{j}\otimes v_{j}\otimes a_{1}\dots a_{n})=m\otimes a_{1}\otimes\dots a_{n}
. Therefore
d_{0}h_{0}=id
. We can also check that

\displaystyle d_{n+1}h_{n}(m\otimes a_{1}\otimes\dots a_{n})

\displaystyle=d_{n+1}(\sum_{j_{0},j_{1},\dots,j_{n}}mu_{j_{0}}\otimes v_{j_{0}}a_{1}u_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{j_{n-1}}a_{n}u_{j_{n}}\otimes v_{j_{n}})

\displaystyle=\psi\circ\phi(m\otimes a_{1}\otimes\dots a_{n}).

Therefore
d_{n+1}h_{n}=\psi\circ\phi
. It is also immediate to check the remaining relation

(3.5)

d_{i}h_{j}=\begin{cases}h_{j}d_{i},&\text{ for }i<j,\\
d_{i}h_{i-1},&\text{ for }i=j\neq 0,\\
h_{j}d_{i-1},&\text{ for }i>j+1\,.\end{cases}

Let
h=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}h_{i}
. Then
hd+dh=id-\psi\circ\phi
.
∎

For

3.3.
Loday-Quillen-Tsygan theorem

Let
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A)=A\otimes\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
be the Lie algebra of matrices valued in
A
. For each

, we have a natural inclusion of Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A)\to\mathfrak{gl}_{N+1}(A)
given by
\alpha\to\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
. With respect to these inclusions we define direct limit

\mathfrak{gl}(A)=\lim_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{N}}\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A),

and consider the Lie algebra homology

H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(\mathfrak{gl}(A))=\lim_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{N}}H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A)).

In this section, we sketch the proof of the Loday-Quillen-Tsygan theorem. We refer to
[
4
]
for the full detail.

Theorem 3.4
.

There is a canonical isomorphism of graded Hopf algebras

H_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{gl}(A))\cong\mathrm{Sym}(HC_{\bullet}(A)[1]).

Proof.

(sketch)
Step 1. We can decompose the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex by the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
, and only consider the trivial representation in the homology. This is due to the following proposition.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and
V
a
\mathfrak{g}

\mathfrak{h}
be a reductive sub-Lie algebra of
\mathfrak{g}

(V\otimes\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}[1]))\to(V\otimes\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}[1]))_{\mathfrak{h}}

H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(\mathfrak{g},V)\cong H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}((V\otimes\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g}[1]))_{\mathfrak{h}},d_{CE})\,.

In our case, we get a quasi-isomorphism of complex

(\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A)[1]),d_{CE})\to(\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A)[1])_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}},d_{CE}).

Step 2. Note that
\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A)[1])
can be regarded as the coinvariant of
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A)^{\otimes n}
under the signed action of
S_{n}
. The action of
S_{n}
commutes with the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
. We have

(\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A)[1]))_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}\cong((\mathfrak{gl}_{N}^{\otimes n}\otimes(A[1])^{\otimes n})_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}})_{S_{n}}\cong((\mathfrak{gl}_{N}^{\otimes n})_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}\otimes(A[1])^{\otimes n})_{S_{n}}.

Classical invariant theory tells us that for

, there is an isomorphism

T^{*}:(\mathfrak{gl}_{N}^{\otimes n})_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}\to k[S_{n}].

As a consequence, we have an isomorphism

\Theta:(\mathrm{Sym}^{n}(\mathfrak{gl}(A)[1]))_{\mathfrak{gl}}\cong(k[S_{n}]\otimes(A[1])^{\otimes n})_{S_{n}}.

According to Proposition
3.2
, this isomorphism
\Theta
is given as follows:

\Theta(E^{a_{1}}_{1\sigma(1)}\wedge E^{a_{2}}_{2\sigma(2)}\dots\wedge E^{a_{n}}_{n\sigma(n)})=(\sigma\otimes(a_{1},a_{2},\dots a_{n}))\in(k[S_{n}]\otimes(A[1])^{\otimes n})_{S_{n}}.

Under this isomorphism,
S_{n}
acts on

by conjugation and acts on

by signed permutation.

Step 3. The diagonal map
\Delta:\mathfrak{gl}(A)\to\mathfrak{gl}(A)\times\mathfrak{gl}(A)
induces a map on the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex

. This map defines a coassociative and cocommutative coproduct on the complex
(\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}(A)[1]))_{\mathfrak{gl}}
.

There is also a map

which can be schematically described as

(3.9)

\begin{pmatrix}\star&\star&\star&\cdots\\
\star&\star&\star&\cdots\\
\star&\star&\star&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\end{pmatrix}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}\times&\times&\times&\cdots\\
\times&\times&\times&\cdots\\
\times&\times&\times&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\star&0&\star&0&\star&0&\cdots\\
0&\times&0&\times&0&\times&\cdots\\
\star&0&\star&0&\star&0&\cdots\\
0&\times&0&\times&0&\times&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\end{pmatrix}.

It induces a map of complex
\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}(A)[1])\otimes\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}(A)[1])\to\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}(A)[1])

\oplus_{*}:(\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}(A)[1]))_{\mathfrak{gl}}\otimes(\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}(A)[1]))_{\mathfrak{gl}}\to(\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}(A)[1]))_{\mathfrak{gl}}.

Therefore,
((\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}(A)[1]))_{\mathfrak{gl}},d_{CE})
equipped with

and

is a commutative and cocommutative differential graded Hopf algebra. We have the following theorem

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a cocommutative differential graded Hopf algebra over a field of characteristic
0
. We have that the homology and primitive functors commute,

H(\mathrm{Prim}\mathcal{H})=\mathrm{Prim}H(\mathcal{H}).

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a connected cocommutative graded Hopf (resp. differential graded Hopf) algebra over a field of characteristic
0
. Then the canonical map

U(\mathrm{Prim}\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{H}

is an isomorphism of graded Hopf (resp. differential graded Hopf) algebras.

As a corollary,
H_{\bullet}(\mathfrak{gl}(A))
is a commutative and cocommutative graded Hopf algebra whose primitive part is the homology of
(\text{Prim}(\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}(A)[1]))_{\mathfrak{gl}},d_{CE})

is commutative, it is isomorphic to the graded symmetric algebra of
H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(\mathrm{Prim}(\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}(A)[1])_{\mathfrak{gl}}))

Step 4. We need to determine the primative part of
L_{\bullet}:=\oplus_{n\geq 1}(k[S_{n}]\otimes(A[1])^{\otimes n})_{S_{n}}
. The coproduct on
\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}(A)[1])_{\mathfrak{gl}}

(3.10)

\Delta(\sigma\otimes(\alpha_{1},\dots\alpha_{n}))=\sum_{I,J}(\sigma_{I}\otimes(\alpha_{i_{1}},\alpha_{i_{2}}\dots))\otimes(\sigma_{J}\otimes(\alpha_{j_{1}},\alpha_{j_{2}}\dots)),

where the sum go through all ordered partitions

of
S_{n}
such that
\sigma(I)=I,\sigma(J)=J

Let

As a

to

We see that

4.
Homology of Lie algebra of matrices associated to quiver

4.1.
Adding fundamental representation

In this section, we consider a generalization of the LQT theorem by considering Lie algebra homology with coefficient in the symmetric powers of the fundamental and the dual of fundamental representation of
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A)
. While we believe that the results in this section have not appeared in the literature, similar statement should already be known to some experts (c.f.

).

Let
L
be a left
A

M

A

\mathbb{C}^{N}
be the fundamental representation of
\mathfrak{gl}_{N}
.
Then
\mathbb{C}^{N}(L):=\mathbb{C}^{N}\otimes L

(A\otimes a)(v\otimes x)=(Av\otimes ax).

Similarly, for the dual representation

We consider the following super Lie algebra

The natural inclusion

By the definition, there is a natural isomorphism between the Lie algebra homology of
\mathcal{L}
and the Lie algebra homology of
\mathfrak{gl}(A)

Theorem 4.1
.

There is a canonical isomorphism of graded Hopf algebras

H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(\mathfrak{gl}(A),\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{V}(L)\oplus\mathcal{V}^{*}(M)))\cong\mathrm{Sym}(HC_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(A)[1]\oplus\mathrm{Tor}^{A}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(M,L)).

Step 1. Applying Proposition
3.4
. We only need to compute the

\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{L}_{N}[1])_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}

Step 2. Application of invariant theory. We observe that

(\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A)[1])\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{l}(\mathbb{C}^{N}(L))\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{i}((\mathbb{C}^{N})^{*}(M)))_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}.

is only non zero when

. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathrm{Sym}^{k}(\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A)[1])\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{l}(\mathbb{C}^{N}(L))\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{l}((\mathbb{C}^{N})^{*}(M))\right)_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left((\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A)[1]))^{\otimes k}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{N}(L))^{\otimes l}\otimes((\mathbb{C}^{N})^{*}(M))^{\otimes l}\right)_{S_{k}\times S_{l}\times S_{l}}\right)_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left((\mathfrak{gl}_{N}^{\otimes k}\otimes(\mathbb{C}^{N})^{\otimes l}\otimes((\mathbb{C}^{N})^{*})^{\otimes l})_{\mathfrak{gl}}\otimes(A[1]^{\otimes k}\otimes L^{\otimes l}\otimes M^{\otimes l})\right)_{S_{k}\times S_{l}\times S_{l}}

\displaystyle\left((\mathfrak{gl}_{N}^{\otimes k}\otimes\mathfrak{gl}_{N}^{\otimes l})_{\mathfrak{gl}_{N}}\otimes(A[1]^{\otimes k}\otimes L^{\otimes l}\otimes M^{\otimes l})\right)_{S_{k}\times S_{l}\times S_{l}}.

Using Proposition

(4.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Theta}

\displaystyle(E^{a_{1}}_{1\sigma(1)}\otimes E^{a_{2}}_{2\sigma(2)}\dots E^{a_{k}}_{k\sigma(k)}\otimes e^{x_{1}}_{k+1}\dots e^{x_{l}}_{k+l}\otimes f^{y_{1}}_{\sigma(k+1)}\dots f^{y_{l}}_{\sigma(k+l)})

We define the set

in each cycle.

For any
\sigma\in S_{k+l}

is defined similarly for each cycle. Then we can check that

, where

3.9

\tilde{\oplus}:\mathcal{V}(M)\times\mathcal{V}(M)\to\mathcal{V}(M).

\begin{pmatrix}\star\\

\star\\

\times\\

\star\\
\times\\

\tilde{\oplus}:\mathcal{V}^{*}(N)\times\mathcal{V}^{*}(N)\to\mathcal{V}^{*}(N)

\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{L}[1])\otimes\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{L}[1])\to\mathrm{Sym}(\mathcal{L}[1])

4.2.
Unframed quiver

Recall that a quiver
Q
is a directed graph that consist of two sets
Q_{0}
(the set of vertices of
Q
),
Q_{1}
(the set of edges of
Q
) and two functions

s,t:Q_{1}\rightrightarrows Q_{0},

called the source and target functions.

Given a quiver
Q
, we consider the following data of algebras and modules associated with
Q
.

(4.6)

\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{c}To each vertex $v\in Q_{0}$ we assign a unital $k$ algebra $A_{v}$\\
To each edge $e\in Q_{1}$ we assign a $A_{s(e)}-A_{t(e)}$ bimodule $M_{e}$\end{tabular}\right\}.

This allows us to define the direct sum Lie algebra
\bigoplus_{v\in Q_{0}}\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A_{v})
and the
\bigoplus_{v\in Q_{0}}\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A_{v})
module
\bigoplus_{e\in Q_{1}}\mathrm{Mat}_{N}(M_{e})
. Denote
\mathcal{M}:=\lim\limits_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{N}}\mathrm{Mat}_{N}
. We consider the Lie algebra homology:

(4.7)

H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(\bigoplus_{v\in Q_{0}}\mathfrak{gl}(A_{v}),\mathrm{Sym}(\bigoplus_{e\in Q_{1}}\mathcal{M}(M_{e})))=\lim_{\overset{\longrightarrow}{N}}H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(\bigoplus_{v\in Q_{0}}\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A_{v}),\mathrm{Sym}(\bigoplus_{e\in Q_{1}}\mathrm{Mat}_{N}(M_{e}))).

This can be identified with the Lie algebra homology of the following semidirect product Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}_{Q,N}:=\bigoplus_{v\in Q_{0}}\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(A_{v})\ltimes\bigoplus_{e\in Q_{1}}\mathrm{Mat}_{N}(M_{e})[-1].

We also define a (super) algebra as an abelian extension of
\bigoplus_{v\in Q_{0}}A_{v}
by
\bigoplus_{e\in Q_{1}}M_{e}[-1]

\mathcal{A}_{Q}:=\bigoplus_{v\in Q_{0}}A_{v}\bigoplus_{e\in Q_{1}}M_{e}[-1].

By the definition of
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
, we have the following identification of Lie algebras

\mathfrak{g}_{Q,N}=\mathfrak{gl}_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{Q}).

Then we can further identify the homology
4.7
with the Lie algebra homology of
\mathfrak{gl}(\mathcal{A}_{Q})
. As a corollary of the Loday-Quillen-Tsygan theorem, we have

Corollary 4.1
.

There is a canonical isomorphism of graded Hopf algebras

H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(\bigoplus_{v\in Q_{0}}\mathfrak{gl}(A_{v}),\mathrm{Sym}(\bigoplus_{e\in Q_{1}}\mathcal{M}(M_{e})))\cong\mathrm{Sym}(HC_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(\mathcal{A}_{Q})[1]).

Recall that a path of length

\mathrm{Cyc}(Q)=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\mathrm{Cyc}_{n}(Q)
.

We define the degree
\deg\ell

\ell_{0}

\ell=\ell_{0}^{d}\,,

(4.8)

\mathcal{F}_{\ell}=H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}\left(M_{e_{1}}\otimes_{A_{t(e_{1})}}^{\mathbb{L}}M_{e_{2}}\otimes_{A_{t(e_{2})}}^{\mathbb{L}}\dots\otimes_{A_{t(e_{n-1})}}^{\mathbb{L}}M_{e_{n}}\otimes_{A_{t(e_{n})}}^{\mathbb{L}}\right)_{\mathbb{Z}_{\deg\ell}}.

For any other representative

C(Q^{\mathrm{fr}},\rho)_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}},\dots,\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}=\bigoplus_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}\geq 0}M_{w^{+}}\otimes(A_{s(e_{1})}[1])^{\otimes i_{1}}\otimes M_{e_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes(A_{t(e_{m})}[1])^{\otimes i_{m+1}}\otimes M_{w^{-}}.

We find that the complex
C(Q^{\mathrm{fr}},\rho)_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}},\dots,\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}
is exactly the Bar complex computing the derived tensor product
M_{w^{+}}\otimes^{\mathbb{L}}_{A_{j(w^{+})}}M_{e_{1}}\otimes_{A_{t(e_{1})}}^{\mathbb{L}}\dots M_{e_{n}}\otimes_{A_{j(w^{-})}}^{\mathbb{L}}M_{w^{-}}
. Therefore, we have the following

(4.20)

\mathrm{Tor}^{\mathcal{A}_{Q}}_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(M_{+},M_{-})=\bigoplus_{\rho\in\mathrm{fPath}(Q^{\mathrm{fr},+})}\mathcal{F}_{\rho}.

∎

Together with the Proposition
4.2
that compute the cyclic homology of
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
, we conclude that

Theorem 4.2
.

We have an isomorphism of graded Hopf algebras

\displaystyle H_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(\bigoplus_{v\in Q_{0}}\mathfrak{gl}(A_{v}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sym}(\bigoplus_{e\in E}\mathcal{M}(M_{e})\bigoplus_{w^{-}\in W^{-}}\mathcal{V}(M_{w^{-}})\bigoplus_{w^{+}\in W^{+}}\mathcal{V}^{*}(M_{w^{+}})))

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Sym}\left(\bigoplus_{v\in Q_{0}}HC_{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}(A_{v})[1]\bigoplus_{\ell\in\mathrm{Cyc}(Q)}\mathcal{F}_{\ell}\bigoplus_{\rho\in\mathrm{fPath}}\mathcal{F}_{\rho}\right).

Wellposedness of Boussinesq system


Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the viscous, incompressible, nonlinear Boussinesq system in two and three spatial dimension. We study the existence and regularity of solutions to the Boussinesq system with nonhomogeneous boundary conditions for which the normal component for the velocity is not necessary equal to zero. We establish the existence of global weak solutions to the three-dimensional instationary nonlinear Boussinesq system but without any smallness assumption on the initial and boundary conditions.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we are mainly interested in the following initial boundary value problem for non-stationary nonlinear Boussinesq system that describes the flow of a viscous incompressible fluid in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\,N=2,3
with smooth boundary
\Gamma
, subject to convective heat transfer :

(1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial\mathbf{z}}{\partial t}-\nu\Delta\mathbf{z}+(\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\mathbf{z}+\nabla p=\bm{\beta}\theta+\mathbf{f}_{1}\mbox{ in }Q=\Omega\times(0,T),\\
&\mbox{div }\mathbf{z}=0\mbox{ in }Q=\Omega\times(0,T);\\
&\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{g}\mbox{ on }\Sigma=\Gamma\times(0,T),\quad\mathbf{z}(0)=\mathbf{z}_{0}(x)\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
&\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial t}-\mu\Delta\theta+(\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\theta=f_{2}\mbox{ in }\mbox{ Q },\\
&\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial n}=h\mbox{ on }\Sigma,\quad\theta(0)=\theta_{0}\mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},z_{2},....,z_{n})
is the fluid velocity,
p
denotes the pressure and
\theta
is the temperature. Here
i
-th component of
(\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\mathbf{z}
is
\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}\frac{\partial z_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}
and
\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial n}
denotes the outer normal derivative of
\theta
at
x
on
\Gamma
. In equation (
1
),
\bm{\beta}
is gravitational vector function,
\nu>0
is kinematic viscosity and
\mu>0
is the thermal diffusivity,
\mathbf{f}_{1}
is an external force and
f_{2}
is a heat source applied to the fluid. Also, the boundary conditions
\mathbf{g}(x,t)
and
h(x,t)
are functions defined on
\Gamma\times(0,T)
. In this Boussinesq approximation, the fluid is treated as incompressible when formulating the Navier-Stokes mass and momentum conservation equations and here the effect of temperature change is taken into account in the buoyancy term
\bm{\beta}\theta
which drives convection.

In this paper, our aim is to prove the existence of the weak solution for system (
1
) and analyse its regularity. In
[
13
]
, the author also considers the same non-homogeneous problem (
1
) in two and three dimension but with very smooth boundary data. Motivated by boundary control problems, here our main interest is in investigating the case when boundary data are not so regular. In that case, we cannot apply the extension method as in
[
13
]
. We are also interested in finding a sufficient condition on
(\mathbf{g},h)
so that a weak solution to equation (
1
) exists.

We denote the outward unit normal to the boundary
\Gamma
by
\mathbf{n}
. When the normal component of
\mathbf{g}
is zero, i.e, in the case of
\mathbf{g}.\mathbf{n}=0
, the authors in
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
studied existence and regularity results of Navier-Stokes equation by pseudo-differential techniques. We can adopt similar analysis in the case of equation (
1
) if
\mathbf{g}.\mathbf{n}=0
. But for engineering and other practical applications (e.g
[
9
]
), the interesting case to study is when
\mathbf{g}.\mathbf{n}\neq 0
. To overcome this difficulty we will follow a similar approach as described in
[
16
]
. In
[
16
]
the author writes Stokes and Oseen equations in the form of a system of two operator equations and obtains the optimal regularity results. The first one is an evolution equation satisfied by the projection of the solution on the Stokes space - the space of divergence free vector fields with the normal trace equal to zero. The second one is a quasi-stationary equation satisfied by the projection of the solution on the orthogonal complement of the Stokes space. In our case, we have to deal with coupled system (
1
).

We explore here the existence and regularity of solutions to linearized and nonlinear Boussinesq system with nonhomogeneous boundary conditions. The novelty of our work is that we consider boundary conditions with low regularity and the Dirichlet boundary condition for velocity for which the normal component is not equal to zero and Neumann boundary condition for temperature, that makes the problem more interesting and challenging. We also establish the existence of weak solution to the three dimensional nonlinear Boussinesq system (
1
) without any smallness assumption to initial condition.

Our main result concerning the existence and regularity of solution to system (
1
) is the following:

Main Theorem
.

Let
(P\mathbf{z}(0),\theta(0))\in\mathbf{V}_{n}^{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{V}^{s,s}(\Sigma)
,

h\in L^{2}(0,T;(H^{1-s}(\Gamma))^{\prime})\cap H^{s}(0,T;(H^{1}(\Gamma))^{\prime})
with
\frac{1}{2}<s<1
,
\mathbf{f}_{1}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{V}^{-1}(\Omega))
,

f_{2}\in L^{2}(0,T;(H^{1}(\Omega))^{\prime})
.Then equation (
1
) admits at least one weak solution
(\mathbf{z},\theta)
in
C_{w}([0,T];\mathbf{V}^{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{V}^{1}(\Omega))\times L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))
and
p\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
, where
C_{w}([0,T];\mathbf{V}^{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega))
is the subspace of
L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbf{V}^{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega))
which are continuous from
[0,T]
into
\mathbf{V}^{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
equipped with its weak topology.

Here
P
is the usual Léray projector and the notations
\mathbf{V}^{0}(\Omega),\,\mathbf{V}^{1}(\Omega),\,\mathbf{V}^{s,s}(\Sigma)
are as in section
2

[
13
]
and
[
16
]
. Actually, we split the system into two parts-homogeneous and non-homogeneous boundary data. We split the system such that the part with homogeneous boundary data has nonlinear terms and the part with non homogeneous boundary condition has only linear terms. Then we study the part with homogeneous boundary data as described in
[
13
]
. To analyse the non homogeneous part, we will follow a similar technique as in
[
16
]
, by applying projection to the first equation and then writing the coupled system in the form of an evolution equation and a quasi-stationary equation.

The works in the literature which are most relevant to our present paper are

Ôeda studied the moving boundary case with Dirichlet boundary condition in

\mathbb{R}^{N}(N=2\mbox{ or }3)
. Under certain assumptions the existence of a weak solution on any interval

In
[
13

\mathbb{R}^{N}\,(N=2\mbox{ or }3)

He defines an appropriate notion of weak solution, and proves existence and uniqueness (for

) results for such solutions. But for the definition of weak solution, the author uses an extension of boundary value
\mathbf{g}
to
\overline{\Omega}\times(0,T)

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 is dedicated to notations and general functional framework. We study the steady linearized Boussinesq system in Section 3. We define a weak solution to this system and establish existence and regularity results. We also introduce transposition solution in the case of not so regular boundary data. Section 4 is devoted to unsteady linearized Boussinesq system. At first we consider linearization around stationary state and then we study a linearization around an instationary state which is needed in section 5 to analyze the nonlinear Boussinesq system with nonhomogeneous boundary condition. In section 5, we prove the existence of weak solution to nonlinear Boussinesq system in 3D case. To the best of our knowledge, this existence and regularity results for three dimensional nonlinear Boussinesq system seems to be new.

3.
Steady linearized Boussinesq system

We first recall the existence result for stationary Boussinesq system with homogeneous boundary conditions :

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta\mathbf{z}_{s}+(\mathbf{z}_{s}.\nabla)\mathbf{z}_{s}+\nabla q_{s}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\beta}\theta_{s}+\mathbf{f}_{1}\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\mbox{div}\,\mathbf{z}_{s}=0\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle-\mu\Delta\theta_{s}+(\mathbf{z}_{s}.\nabla)\theta_{s}=f_{2}\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{s}=0\quad\mbox{on }\Gamma;\quad\frac{\partial\theta_{s}}{\partial\mathbf{n}}=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma,

where
\bm{\beta}\in\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is a vector. Then from
[
11
, Proposition 2.3]
, we know that for
\mathbf{f}_{1}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
and
f_{2}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, there exists
(\mathbf{z}_{s},\theta_{s},q_{s})\in\mathbf{V}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}\cap H^{1}(\Omega).

For
\lambda_{0}>0
, let us consider steady linearised Boussinesq System (linearized around
(\mathbf{z}_{s},\theta_{s})
) with homogeneous boundary condition :

(2)

\left.\begin{aligned} &\lambda_{0}\mathbf{u}-\Delta\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}.\nabla)\mathbf{z}_{s}+(\mathbf{z}_{s}.\nabla)\mathbf{u}+\nabla p=\bm{\beta}\phi+\mathbf{f}_{1}\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
&\mbox{div }\mathbf{u}=0\mbox{ in }\Omega;\quad\mathbf{u}=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma,\\
&\lambda_{0}\phi-\Delta\phi+\mathbf{z}_{s}.\nabla\phi+\mathbf{u}.\nabla\theta_{s}=f_{2}\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
&\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma.\end{aligned}\right\}

In this section, for notational convenience, we will use
\mathbf{z}_{s}=\mathbf{z}
and
\theta_{s}=\theta
. Without loss of generality we will assume that
\nu=\mu=1
.

Definition 3.1
.

(\mathbf{u},\phi)\in\mathbf{V}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
is a
weak solution
to equation (
2
) if for every
(\mathbf{\Phi},\psi)\in\mathbf{V}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
and
(\mathbf{f}_{1},f_{2})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
:

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{u}.\mathbf{\Phi}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathbf{u}.\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}+\int_{\Omega}((\mathbf{u}.\nabla)\mathbf{z}).\mathbf{\Phi}+\int_{\Omega}((\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\mathbf{u}).\mathbf{\Phi}-\int_{\Omega}\bm{\beta}\phi.\mathbf{\Phi}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{0}\int_{\Omega}\phi\psi+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi.\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{z}.\nabla\phi)\psi+\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{u}.\nabla\theta)\psi=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{f}_{1}.\mathbf{\Phi}+\int_{\Omega}f_{2}\psi.

We have the following existence theorem for weak solution :

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(\mathbf{z},\theta)\in\mathbf{V}^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
. Then there exists large
\lambda_{0}>0
for which there exists unique weak solution
(\mathbf{u},p,\phi)\in\mathbf{V}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}\times H^{1}(\Omega)
to equation (
2
) for all
(\mathbf{f}_{1},f_{2})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Define the bilinear form on
\mathbf{V}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
:

\displaystyle a((\mathbf{u},\phi),(\mathbf{\Phi},\psi))=

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{u.\Phi}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mathbf{u}.\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}+\int_{\Omega}((\mathbf{u}.\nabla)\mathbf{z}).\mathbf{\Phi}+\int_{\Omega}((\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\mathbf{u}).\mathbf{\Phi}-\int_{\Omega}\bm{\beta}\phi.\mathbf{\Phi}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{0}\int_{\Omega}\phi\psi+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi.\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{z}.\nabla\phi)\psi+\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{u}.\nabla\theta)\psi

This bilinear form is continuous on
\mathbf{V}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
. We want to show that it is coercive if
\lambda_{0}>0
is large. For that we have to consider

\displaystyle a((\mathbf{u},\phi),(\mathbf{u},\phi))=

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}((\mathbf{u}.\nabla)\mathbf{z}).\mathbf{u}+\int_{\Omega}((\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\mathbf{u}).\mathbf{u}-\int_{\Omega}\bm{\beta}\phi.\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{0}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{z}.\nabla\phi)\phi+\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{u}.\nabla\theta)\phi.

Now we will estimate term by term. Let us start with the third term and by H
\ddot{o}
lder’s inequality :

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|((\mathbf{u}.\nabla)\mathbf{z}).\mathbf{u}|\leq||\mathbf{u}||^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{z}||_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

Now observe that :

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}((\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\mathbf{u}).\mathbf{\Phi}=-\int_{\Omega}((\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\mathbf{\Phi}).\mathbf{u}\,\,.

Hence, we get :
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}((\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\mathbf{u}).\mathbf{u}=0
. Due to the similar reasoning, we also have :

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{z}.\nabla\phi)\phi=0.

Also by H
\ddot{o}
lder’s inequality we have :

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{\beta}\phi.\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle\leq||\bm{\beta}||_{\infty}(\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2})^{1/2}(\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}|^{2})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{||\bm{\beta}||_{\infty}}{2}(\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}).

By using the fact that
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
, the other coupling term gives :

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|(\mathbf{u}.\nabla\theta)\phi|

\displaystyle\leq||\theta||_{H^{1}(\Omega)}||\phi||_{L^{4}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{u}||_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq||\theta||_{H^{1}(\Omega)}||\phi||_{H^{1}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{u}||_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)}.

So, we obtain :

\displaystyle a((\mathbf{u},\phi),(\mathbf{u},\phi))\geq\,

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}-||\mathbf{u}||^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{z}||_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)}-\frac{||\bm{\beta}||_{\infty}}{2}(\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}|^{2})

\displaystyle+\lambda_{0}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}-||\theta||_{H^{1}(\Omega)}||\phi||_{H^{1}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{u}||_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)}.

Now by using
\mathbf{V}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)
, we will have :

\displaystyle||\mathbf{u}||^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{z}||_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C||\mathbf{u}||^{2}_{\mathbf{V}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{z}||_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=C||\mathbf{u}||^{7/4}_{\mathbf{V}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{u}||^{1/4}_{\mathbf{V}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{z}||_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)}.

Thus we will get :

\displaystyle-||\mathbf{u}||^{2}_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{z}||_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq-C||\mathbf{u}||^{7/4}_{\mathbf{V}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\big(||\mathbf{z}||_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{u}||^{1/4}_{\mathbf{V}_{n}^{0}(\Omega)}\big)

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{4}||\mathbf{u}||^{2}_{\mathbf{V}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}-\frac{7^{7}\times C^{8}}{8\times 2^{7}}||\mathbf{z}||^{8}_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{u}||^{2}_{\mathbf{V}^{0}_{n}(\Omega)},

where the last inequality will come from Young’s inequality.
Similarly, we can estimate :

\displaystyle||\theta||_{H^{1}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{u}||_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)}||\phi||_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big(||\theta||^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{u}||^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+||\phi||^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}||\theta||^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{u}||^{1/4}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{u}||^{7/4}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}||\phi||^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

Hence,

\displaystyle-||\theta||_{H^{1}(\Omega)}||\phi||_{H^{1}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{u}||_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{2}||\phi||^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}-C_{1}||\mathbf{u}||^{7/4}_{\mathbf{V}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}||\theta||^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{u}||^{1/4}_{\mathbf{V}_{n}^{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{2}||\phi||^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}-\frac{1}{4}||\mathbf{u}||^{2}_{\mathbf{V}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}-\frac{7^{7}\times C_{1}^{8}}{8\times 2^{7}}||\theta||^{16}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{u}||^{2}_{\mathbf{V}^{0}_{n}(\Omega)}

Thus we will get :

\displaystyle a((\mathbf{u},\phi),(\mathbf{u},\phi))\geq

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}||\mathbf{u}||^{2}_{\mathbf{V}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}-\frac{7^{7}\times C^{8}}{8\times 2^{7}}||\mathbf{z}||^{8}_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{u}||^{2}_{\mathbf{V}^{0}_{n}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle-\frac{||\bm{\beta}||_{\infty}}{2}(\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{u}|^{2})+\lambda_{0}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}||\phi||^{2}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}||\mathbf{u}||^{2}_{\mathbf{V}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}-\frac{7^{7}\times C_{1}^{8}}{8\times 2^{7}}||\theta||^{16}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}||\mathbf{u}||^{2}_{\mathbf{V}^{0}_{n}(\Omega)}.

Now it is sufficient to choose
\lambda_{0}\geq\max\{1+\frac{7^{7}\times C^{8}}{8\times 2^{7}}||\mathbf{z}||^{8}_{\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)}+\frac{||\bm{\beta}||_{\infty}}{2},\,1+\frac{7^{7}\times C_{1}^{8}}{8\times 2^{7}}||\theta||^{16}_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\frac{||\bm{\beta}||_{\infty}}{2}\}
, such that following holds:

\displaystyle a((\mathbf{u},\phi),(\mathbf{u},\phi))\geq\frac{1}{2}\Big(||\mathbf{u}||_{\mathbf{V}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}+||\phi||_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big).

So by Lax-Milgram theorem we have existence of the weak solution
(\mathbf{u},\phi)\in\mathbf{V}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
to equation (
2
) for all
(\mathbf{f}_{1},f_{2})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then by applying de Rham theorem, we can recover pressure
p\in L^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
as in the steady Stokes system in
[
6
]
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

For all
(\mathbf{f}_{1},f_{2})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, the weak solution
(\mathbf{u},p,\phi)
for system (
2
) given by theorem (
3.2
) belongs to
\mathbf{V}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{2}(\Omega)

Proof.

We already know the existence of the solution
(\mathbf{u},p,\phi)\in\mathbf{V}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}\times H^{1}(\Omega)
from theorem
3.2
. So, consider

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\mathbf{u}-\Delta\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}.\nabla)\mathbf{z}+(\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\mathbf{u}+\nabla p=\bm{\beta}\phi+\mathbf{f}_{1}\mbox{ in }\Omega

Here as
(\bm{\beta}\phi+\mathbf{f}_{1})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, using the regularity result
[
16
, Appendix B, lemma B.1]
we can conclude that
(\mathbf{u},p)\in\mathbf{V}^{2}(\Omega)\times(\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R})
. Then consider the equation

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\phi-\Delta\phi+\mathbf{z}.\nabla\phi=f_{2}-\mathbf{u}.\nabla\theta\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega.

Here
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbf{H}^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow\mathbf{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
for two and three dimensions. So,
\mathbf{u}.\nabla\theta\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. By elliptic regularity we can conclude that
\phi\in H^{2}(\Omega)
.
∎

Now onwards we fix
\lambda_{0}
as in theorem
3.2
. We consider linearised Boussinesq system with non homogeneous boundary data :

(3)

\left.\begin{aligned} &\lambda_{0}\mathbf{u}-\Delta\mathbf{u}+(\mathbf{u}.\nabla)\mathbf{z}+(\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\mathbf{u}+\nabla p=\bm{\beta}\phi+\mathbf{f}_{1}\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
&\mbox{div }\mathbf{u}=0\mbox{ in }\Omega;\quad\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{g}\mbox{ on }\Gamma,\\
&\lambda_{0}\phi-\Delta\phi+\mathbf{z}.\nabla\phi+\mathbf{u}.\nabla\theta=f_{2}\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
&\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}=h\mbox{ on }\Gamma.\end{aligned}\right\}

We first prove the existence of a unique solution when
(\mathbf{g},h)\in\mathbf{V}^{3/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
.
If
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{V}^{3/2}(\Gamma)
, we can define the Dirichlet operator as
D_{z}\mathbf{g}=(\mathbf{w},\pi)
using the solution to equation:

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\mathbf{w}-\Delta\mathbf{w}+(\mathbf{w}.\nabla)\mathbf{z}+(\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\mathbf{w}+\nabla{\pi}=0\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(4)

\displaystyle\mbox{ div}\,\mathbf{w}=0\mbox{ in }\Omega,\quad\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{g}\mbox{ on }\Gamma.

Then
D_{z}
is linear and continuous from
\mathbf{V}^{3/2}(\Gamma)
to
\mathbf{V}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
. For details see
[
16
, Appendix B, Corollary B.1]
.

Then consider
\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}=\mathbf{u-w},\widetilde{p}=p-\pi
. Here
(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}},\widetilde{p})
satisfies :

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}-\Delta\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}+(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}.\nabla)\mathbf{z}+(\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}+\nabla\widetilde{p}=\bm{\beta}\phi+\mathbf{f}_{1}\mbox{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle\mbox{div }\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}=0\mbox{ in }\Omega;\quad\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma.

Now system (
3
) can be reduced to the following system:

(5)

\left.\begin{aligned} &\lambda_{0}\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}-\Delta\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}+(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}.\nabla)\mathbf{z}+(\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}+\nabla\widetilde{p}=\bm{\beta}\phi+\mathbf{f}_{1}\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
&\mbox{div }\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}=0\mbox{ in }\Omega;\quad\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma,\\
&\lambda_{0}\phi-\Delta\phi+\mathbf{z}.\nabla\phi+\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}.\nabla\theta=f_{2}-\mathbf{w}.\nabla\theta=\widetilde{f_{2}}\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
&\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}=h\mbox{ on }\Gamma.\end{aligned}\right\}

For this system with inhomogeneous boundary condition, we define a weak solution in the same lines as in definition
3.1
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
h\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
,
(\mathbf{z},\theta)\in\mathbf{V}^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
and
(\mathbf{f}_{1},f_{2})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
. Then
(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}},\tau)\in\mathbf{V}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
is a
weak solution
to equation (
5
) if for every
(\mathbf{\Phi},\psi)\in\mathbf{V}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
, the following holds:

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}.\mathbf{\Phi}+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}.\nabla\mathbf{\Phi}+\int_{\Omega}((\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}.\nabla)\mathbf{z}+(\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}).\mathbf{\Phi}-\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{\beta}\tau.\mathbf{\Phi}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{0}\int_{\Omega}\phi\psi+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi.\nabla\psi+\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{z}.\nabla\phi)\psi+\int_{\Omega}(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}.\nabla\theta)\psi=\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{f}_{1}.\mathbf{\Phi}+\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{f_{2}}\psi+\int_{\Gamma}h\psi

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(\mathbf{z},\theta)\in\mathbf{V}^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
. For all
(\mathbf{f}_{1},f_{2})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
and
(\mathbf{g},h)\in\mathbf{V}^{3/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
, there exists a weak solution
(\mathbf{u},p,\phi)\in\mathbf{V}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
to equation (
3
). Moreover, we have
(\mathbf{u},p,\phi)\in\mathbf{V}^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}},\widetilde{p},\phi)
satisfy equation (
5
). As in the previous existence theorem, we can define a continuous, coercive bilinear form
a((\widetilde{\mathbf{w}},\phi),(\mathbf{\Phi},\psi))
and continuous linear form
L((\mathbf{\Phi},\psi))
on
\mathbf{V}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
.
By Lax-Milgram, we can conclude that
(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}},\phi)\in\mathbf{V}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of (
5
). Hence by applying de Rham’s theorem, we have
(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}},\widetilde{p},\phi)\in\mathbf{V}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
.

Now we know that
(\mathbf{u},p)=(\widetilde{\mathbf{w}}+\mathbf{w},\widetilde{p}+\pi)
, where
(\mathbf{w},\pi)

is the solution of (
3
) that belongs to
\mathbf{V}^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}
. So we can conclude that
(\mathbf{u},p,\phi)\in\mathbf{V}^{1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
.

Now for proving regularity, we consider
(\mathbf{u},p)=(\mathbf{u}_{1}+\mathbf{u}_{2},p_{1}+p_{2})
such that :

(6)

\left.\begin{aligned} &\lambda_{0}\mathbf{u}_{1}-\Delta\mathbf{u}_{1}+\nabla p_{1}=0\mbox{ in }\Omega,\,\mbox{ div}\,\mathbf{u}_{1}=0,\mbox{ in }\Omega,\,\quad\mathbf{u}_{1}=\mathbf{g}\mbox{ on }\Gamma\\
&\lambda_{0}\mathbf{u}_{2}-\Delta\mathbf{u}_{2}+(\mathbf{u}_{2}.\nabla)\mathbf{z}+(\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{2}+\nabla p_{2}=-(\mathbf{u}_{1}.\nabla)\mathbf{z}-(\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\mathbf{u}_{1}+\mathbf{\beta}\phi+\mathbf{f}_{1}\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
&\mbox{div }\mathbf{u}_{2}=0\mbox{ in }\Omega;\quad\mathbf{u}_{2}=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma.\end{aligned}\right\}

And
\phi=(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})
satisfies :

(7)

\left.\begin{aligned} &\lambda_{0}\phi_{1}-\Delta\phi_{1}+(\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\phi_{1}=0\mbox{ in }\Omega,\,\quad\frac{\partial\phi_{1}}{\partial n}=h\mbox{ on }\Gamma,\\
&\lambda_{0}\phi_{2}-\Delta\phi_{2}+\mathbf{z}.\nabla\phi_{2}+\mathbf{u}_{2}.\nabla\theta=-\mathbf{u}_{1}.\nabla\theta+f_{2}\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
&\frac{\partial\phi_{2}}{\partial n}=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma.\end{aligned}\right\}

Now the first equation of (
6
) is the Stokes Problem and we know that
(\mathbf{u}_{1},p_{1})\in\mathbf{V}^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
if
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{V}^{3/2}(\Gamma)
. Now the R.H.S of second equation in (
6
) is in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
. Thus by
[
16
, Appendix B, lemma B.1]
, we can conclude
(\mathbf{u}_{2},p_{2})\in\mathbf{V}^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
. Hence, we can conclude that
(\mathbf{u},p)\in\mathbf{V}^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
.

Also from first equation of (
7
), we have
\phi_{1}\in H^{2}(\Omega)
when
h\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
. Then consider the equation :

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\phi_{2}-\Delta\phi_{2}+\mathbf{z}.\nabla\phi_{2}=-\mathbf{u}_{2}.\nabla\theta-\mathbf{u}_{1}.\nabla\theta+f_{2}\mbox{ in }\Omega

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi_{2}}{\partial n}=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma

Now the R.H.S of the above equation is in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Thus we have
\phi_{2}\in H^{2}(\Omega)
.
∎

Now we consider the boundary data
(\mathbf{g},h)\in\mathbf{V}^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{-3/2}(\Gamma)
. In this case we can use transposition method to define a solution to equation (
3
). For simplicity let us assume that
(\mathbf{f}_{1},f_{2})=(0,0)

Definition 3.6
.

Assume that
(\mathbf{g},h)\in\mathbf{V}^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{-3/2}(\Gamma).
A function

is a
transposition solution
to system (

\displaystyle\langle(\mathbf{u},\phi),(\mathbf{f}_{3},f_{4})\rangle_{\mathbf{V}^{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle-\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial\mathbf{n}}+\widetilde{\pi}\mathbf{n}-c(\widetilde{\pi})\mathbf{n},\mathbf{g}\rangle_{\mathbf{V}^{1/2}(\Gamma),\mathbf{V}^{-1/2}(\Gamma)}+\langle s,h\rangle_{H^{3/2}(\Gamma),H^{-3/2}(\Gamma)}

, where

and

is the solution to :

(9)

\left.\begin{aligned} &\lambda_{0}\mathbf{r}-\Delta\mathbf{r}+(\nabla\mathbf{z})^{T}\mathbf{r}-(\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\mathbf{r}+\nabla\tilde{\pi}+(\nabla\theta)^{T}s=\mathbf{f}_{3}\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\

&\lambda_{0}s-\Delta s-\mathbf{z}.\nabla s-\bm{\beta.r}=f_{4}\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\

\displaystyle\langle p,k\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\Omega),\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega)}=\langle\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}_{1}}{\partial\mathbf{n}}-\pi_{1}\mathbf{n}+c(\pi_{1})\mathbf{n},\mathbf{g}\rangle_{\mathbf{V}^{1/2}(\Gamma),\mathbf{V}^{-1/2}(\Gamma)}-\langle s_{1},h\rangle_{H^{3/2}(\Gamma),H^{-3/2}(\Gamma)}

for every

with
\int_{\Omega}k=0
and
(\mathbf{r}_{1},s_{1})
is the solution to

(10)

\left.\begin{aligned} &\lambda_{0}\mathbf{r}_{1}-\Delta\mathbf{r}_{1}+(\nabla\mathbf{z})^{T}\mathbf{r}_{1}-(\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\mathbf{r}_{1}+\nabla\pi_{1}+(\nabla\theta)^{T}s_{1}=0\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
&\mbox{div }\mathbf{r}_{1}=k\mbox{ in }\Omega;\quad\mathbf{r}_{1}=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma\\
&\lambda_{0}s_{1}-\Delta s_{1}-\mathbf{z}.\nabla s_{1}-\bm{\beta}.\mathbf{r}_{1}=0\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
&\frac{\partial s_{1}}{\partial n}=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma\end{aligned}\right\}

.

For all
(\mathbf{g},h)\in\mathbf{V}^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{-3/2}(\Gamma)
, system (
3
) admits a unique transposition solution
(\mathbf{u},p,\phi)\in\mathbf{V}^{0}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
in the sense of above definition.

Proof.

As
(\mathbf{f}_{3},f_{4})\in\mathbf{V}^{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
, by theorem
3.3
we have
(\mathbf{r},\tilde{\pi},s)\in\mathbf{V}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{2}(\Omega)
. Let us define the operator
\Lambda
from
\mathbf{V}^{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)
into
\mathbf{V}^{1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{3/2}(\Gamma)
by :

\displaystyle\Lambda(\mathbf{f}_{3},f_{4})=\Big(-\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial\mathbf{n}}+\widetilde{\pi}\mathbf{n}-c(\widetilde{\pi})\mathbf{n},s\Big),

where
(\mathbf{r},s)
is solution to (
9
). Now we have :

\displaystyle||\Lambda(\mathbf{f}_{3},f_{4})||_{\mathbf{V}^{1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{3/2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle=||\Big(-\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}}{\partial\mathbf{n}}+\widetilde{\pi}\mathbf{n}-c(\widetilde{\pi})\mathbf{n},s\Big)||_{\mathbf{V}^{1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{3/2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq C(||\mathbf{f}_{3}||_{\mathbf{V}^{0}(\Omega)}+||f_{4}||_{L^{2}(\Omega)})

Then it follows that

is a bounded, linear operator. Then equation (
8
) can be rewritten as :

\displaystyle\langle(\mathbf{u},\phi),(\mathbf{f}_{3},f_{4})\rangle_{\mathbf{V}^{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\langle\Lambda(\mathbf{f}_{3},f_{4}),(\mathbf{g},h)\rangle_{\mathbf{V}^{1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{3/2}(\Gamma),\mathbf{V}^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\times H^{-3/2}(\Gamma)}

. The function

is clearly a solution to system (
3
) in the sense of definition
3.6
.

Now we want to prove the existence of pressure
p
. For
k\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega)
with
\int_{\Omega}k=0
, the solution
(\mathbf{r}_{1},s_{1})
to equation (

\mathbf{V}^{2}(\Omega)\times H^{2}(\Omega)
. The operator
\Lambda_{1}
from
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Omega)
to
\mathbf{V}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma))\times H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Gamma)

can be defined as :

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}(k)=\Big(-\frac{\partial\mathbf{r}_{1}}{\partial\mathbf{n}}+\pi_{1}\mathbf{n}-c(\pi_{1})\mathbf{n},\,s_{1}\Big),

where
(\mathbf{r}_{1},s_{1})
is the solution to equation (
10

\displaystyle p=\Lambda_{1}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{g}\\
h\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{H}^{-1}(\Omega)

To prove uniqueness, let
(\mathbf{u},\phi)
be a solution corresponding to

Thus from equation (

∎

Our next aim is to define lifting operator

for the coupled system (

and it satisfies the equation :

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\mathbf{w}-\nu\Delta\mathbf{w}+(\mathbf{w}.\nabla)\mathbf{z}+(\mathbf{z}.\nabla)\mathbf{w}+\nabla p=\bm{\beta}\xi\mbox{ in }\Omega,

.

The operator

is linear and continuous from

3.5
,

∎

4.
Unsteady linearized Boussinesq system

4.1.
Linearization around stationary state

In this section we study first the linearized Boussinesq system around zero solution and later around nonzero solution with non homogeneous boundary conditions.

4.1.1.
Linearization around zero solution

Here we are mainly interested in the following equation :

(11)

\left.\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial\mathbf{u}}{\partial t}-\Delta\mathbf{u}+\nabla p=\bm{\beta}\phi\mbox{ in }Q,\\
&\mbox{div }\mathbf{u}=0\mbox{ in }Q;\quad\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{g}\mbox{ on }\Sigma,\\
&\mathbf{u}(x,0)=\mathbf{u}_{0}(x)\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\

&\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}=h\mbox{ on }\Sigma,\quad\phi(0)=\phi_{0}\mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right\}

If the boundary data is regular then there are some known existence and regularity results for system (
1
). For instance, in
[
13
]
, the author proved that if
\mathbf{g}\in C^{1}(\Sigma)
and

(\mathbf{g},h)\in\mathbf{V}^{s,s}(\Sigma)\times L^{2}(0,T;(H^{1-s}(\Gamma))^{\prime})\cap H^{s}(0,T;(H^{1}(\Gamma))^{\prime})
, according to Corollary
2
, we conclude that
\widetilde{P}L_{0}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{g}(0)\\
h(0)\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbf{V}^{s}(\Omega)\times H^{s}(\Omega)
and

(\mathbf{u},\phi)\in C([0,T];\mathbf{V}^{s}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{V}^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega))\times C([0,T];H^{s}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega))
. Thus it is clear that
\widetilde{P}\begin{pmatrix}(\mathbf{u}.\nabla)\mathbf{u}\\
(\mathbf{u}.\nabla)\phi\end{pmatrix}\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{V}^{-1}(\Omega))\times L^{2}(0,T;(H^{1}(\Omega))^{\prime})
. A function
(\mathbf{y},\tau)\in C_{w}([0,T];\mathbf{V}_{n}^{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega))\cap(L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{V}^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\times L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)))
is a weak solution to (
59
) iff
\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\mathbf{y}}(t)\\
\widehat{\tau}(t)\end{pmatrix}=e^{-\lambda_{0}t}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{y}(t)\\
\tau(t)\end{pmatrix}
is a solution to the system

(60)

\left.\begin{aligned} &\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\mathbf{y}}^{\prime}\\
\widehat{\tau}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{z},\theta}(t)\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\mathbf{y}}\\
\widehat{\tau}\end{pmatrix}-\lambda_{0}\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\mathbf{y}}\\
\widehat{\tau}\end{pmatrix}-\widetilde{P}\begin{pmatrix}(\widehat{\mathbf{y}}.\nabla)\widehat{\mathbf{y}}\\
(\widehat{\mathbf{y}}.\nabla)\widehat{\tau}\end{pmatrix}-\lambda_{0}\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\mathbf{y}}\\
\widehat{\tau}\end{pmatrix}-\widetilde{P}\begin{pmatrix}(\mathbf{u}.\nabla)\mathbf{u}\\
(\mathbf{u}.\nabla)\phi\end{pmatrix},\\
&\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\mathbf{y}}\\
\widehat{\tau}\end{pmatrix}(0)=\widetilde{P}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{z}(0)\\
\theta(0)\end{pmatrix}-\widetilde{P}L_{0}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{g}(0)\\
h(0)\end{pmatrix}.\end{aligned}\right\}

Thanks to Lemma
4.9
, the existence in
C_{w}([0,T];\mathbf{V}_{n}^{0}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Omega))\cap(L^{2}(0,T;\mathbf{V}^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\times L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega)))
of
(\widehat{\mathbf{y}},\widehat{\tau})
satisfying the weak formulation of equation (
60
) can be proved as in the case of Navier-Stokes equations (see
[
16
, Theorem 5.1]
).
∎

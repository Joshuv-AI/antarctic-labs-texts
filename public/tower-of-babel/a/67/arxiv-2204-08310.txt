Proof of Theorem
1.2
Let
\epsilon>0
be a fixed arbitrary small real number.
We assume that
\lambda_{f}(n)=O(n^{\epsilon/2})
(Deligne’s bound) and
r_{Q}(n)=O(n^{\epsilon/2})
(Weil’s bound). Now, we apply Lemma
2.9
when
r=2,

\textbf{S}=\mathbb{Z}
and
g=Q(\underline{x}),
where
Q(\underline{x})
is a primitive integral positive definite binary quadratic form of fixed discriminant
D<0
with the class number
1
. From Theorem
1.1
, we have

\begin{split}S_{1}(f,D;x)&=\displaystyle{\sum_{\underline{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\atop Q(\underline{x})\leq x}\lambda_{f}(Q(\underline{x}))}=O_{f,D,\epsilon}(x^{\frac{7}{10}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}})\\
{\rm and}\qquad S_{2}(f,D;x)&\displaystyle{\sum_{\underline{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\atop Q(\underline{x})\leq x}(\lambda_{f}(Q(\underline{x}))^{2}}=Cx+O_{f,D,\epsilon}(x^{\frac{8}{11}+\epsilon}),\\
\end{split}

where
C>0
is a constant. Now, we chose
\delta=\frac{25}{33}+\epsilon
and apply Lemma
2.9
to obtain at least one sign change at
{\underline{x}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}}
such that
Q(\underline{x})\in(x,x+x^{\frac{25}{33}+\epsilon}].
Thus, we have infinitely many sign changes. Moreover, we obtain at least
x^{\frac{8}{33}-\epsilon}
many sign changes in the interval
(x,2x],
for sufficiently large
x
. This completes the proof.

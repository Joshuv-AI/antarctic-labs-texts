Thompson Sampling for Unsupervised Sequential Selection
Arun Verma, Manjesh K. Hanawal, Nandyala Hemachandra

Abstract
Thompson Sampling has generated significant interest due to its better empirical performance than upper confidence bound based algorithms. In this paper, we study Thompson Sampling based algorithm for U nsupervised S equential S election (USS) problem. The USS problem is a variant of the stochastic multi-armed bandits problem, where the loss of an arm can not be inferred from the observed feedback. In the USS setup, arms are associated with fixed costs and are ordered, forming a cascade. In each round, the learner selects an arm and observes the feedback from arms up to the selected arm. The learner’s goal is to find the arm that minimizes the expected total loss. The total loss is the sum of the cost incurred for selecting the arm and the stochastic loss associated with the selected arm. The problem is challenging because, without knowing the mean loss, one cannot compute the total loss for the selected arm. Clearly, learning is feasible only if the optimal arm can be inferred from the problem structure. As shown in the prior work, learning is possible when the problem instance satisfies the so-called ‘Weak Dominance’ ( WD ) (\mathrm{WD}) property. Under WD \mathrm{WD} , we show that our Thompson Sampling based algorithm for the USS problem achieves near-optimal regret and has better numerical performance than existing algorithms.

Introduction

Many variants of sequential decision-making problems are considered in the literature depending on the type of feedback and the amount of information they reveal about the rewards. The multi-armed bandits and the expert setting
(
Auer et al. 2002
;
Bubeck et al. 2012
)
are well-studied problems where feedback provides direct information about the rewards. In the multi-armed bandit setting, feedback observed from an action reveals only the reward associated with that action. However, in the expert setting, the feedback observed from an action reveals reward associated with the action played as well as all other actions. The settings that span in between these two extreme cases are also studied, namely, bandits with side-information
(
Mannor and Shamir 2011
;
Alon et al. 2013
;
Alon et al. 2015
;
Wu et al. 2015
)
. In many problems, the actions can be indirectly tied to the rewards. Such setting is referred as partial monitoring setting
(
Cesa-Bianchi et al. 2006
;
Bartók and Szepesvári 2012
;
Bartók et al. 2014
)
. It includes all the previously described setups as special cases.

Most of the previous work on partial monitoring is restricted to cases where feedback from the actions allows the learner to identify the rewards of the actions. However, in many areas like crowd-sourcing
(
Bonald and Combes 2017
;
Kleindessner and Awasthi 2018
)
, medical diagnosis
(
Hanawal et al. 2017
)
, resource allocation
(
Verma et al. 2019a
)
, and many others, feedback from actions may not even be sufficient to identify their rewards.

Such reward structures can be found in many prediction problems, where one may have to predict labels for instances whose associated ground-truth cannot be obtained. Such problems arise naturally in medical diagnosis, crowd-sourcing, security system
(
Hanawal et al. 2017
)
, and unsupervised features selection
(
Verma et al. 2020
)
. In the medical diagnosis problem, the true state of the patients may not be known; hence, the test’s effectiveness cannot be known. Whereas in the crowd-sourcing systems, the expertise level of self-listed-agents (workers) is unknown; therefore, the quality of their work cannot be known. In these prediction problems, we can observe prediction from test/worker, but we cannot ascertain their reliability due to the absence of ground truth.

In many of the real-world situations like those found in medical diagnosis, airport security, and manufacturing, a set of tests or classifiers is used to monitor patients, people, and products. Tests have cost with the more informative ones resulting in higher monetary costs and higher latency. Thus, they are often organized as a cascade
(
Chen et al. 2012
;
Trapeznikov and Saligrama 2013
)
, so that a new input is first probed by an inexpensive test then more expensive one. We refer to such cascaded systems as
Unsupervised Sequential Selection
(USS) problem
1

.
, where an arm represents a test/ worker. A learner’s goal in the USS problem is to select the most cost-effective arm so that the overall system maintains high accuracy at low average costs.

In this paper, we draw upon several concepts introduced in prior work

;

. Specifically, we use the notion of weak dominance

Our contributions can be summarized as follows:

3
Conditions for Learning Optimal Arm

Next, we define the strong and weak dominance property of the USS problem instance that makes the learning of the optimal arm possible.

Definition 1

(Strong Dominance
(\mathrm{SD})

(
Hanawal et al. 2017
)
)
.

A problem instance is said to satisfy
\mathrm{SD}
property if

Y^{i}=Y\mbox{ for some }i\in[K]\implies Y^{j}=Y,~~\forall j>i.

We represent the set of all instances in
\mathcal{P}_{\small{\text{USS}}}
that satisfy
\mathrm{SD}
property by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SD}}
.

The
\mathrm{SD}
property implies that if the feedback of an arm is same as the true reward, then the feedback of all the arms in the subsequent stages of the cascade is also same as the true reward.
Hanawal et al. 2017
show that the set of all instances satisfying SD property is learnable by mapping such instances to stochastic multi-armed bandits problem with side information
(
Wu et al. 2015
)
. A weaker version of the
\mathrm{SD}
property is defined as follows:

Definition 2

(Weak Dominance
(\mathrm{WD})

(
Verma et al. 2019b
)
)
.

Let
{i^{\star}}
denote the optimal arm. Then an instance
P\in\mathcal{P}_{\small{\text{USS}}}
is said to satisfy weak dominance property if

\forall j>{i^{\star}}:C_{j}-C_{i^{\star}}>\mathbb{P}\left\{Y^{i^{\star}}\neq Y^{j}\right\}.

(3)

We denote the set of all instances in
\mathcal{P}_{\small{\text{USS}}}
that satisfy
\mathrm{WD}
property by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{WD}}
.

The set of problems satisfying the
\mathrm{WD}
property is maximally learnable, and any relaxation of
\mathrm{WD}
property makes the problem unlearnable
(
Verma et al. 2019b
, Theorem 1)
. In the following equation, we use an alternative characterization of the
\mathrm{WD}
property, given as

\xi\doteq\min_{j>{i^{\star}}}\left\{C_{j}-C_{i^{\star}}-\mathbb{P}\left\{Y^{i^{\star}}\neq Y^{j}\right\}\right\}>0.

(4)

The larger the value of
\xi
, ‘stronger’ is the
\mathrm{WD}
property, and easier to identify an optimal arm. We later characterize the regret upper bound of our algorithm in terms of
\xi
.

3.1
Optimal Arm Selection

Without loss of generality, we set
\lambda_{i}=1
for all
i\in[K]
as their value can be absorbed into the costs. Since
{i^{\star}}=\max\big\{\arg\min\limits_{i\in[K]}\left(\gamma_{i}+C_{i}\right)\big\}
, it must satisfy following equation:

\displaystyle\forall j<{i^{\star}}\,:\,C_{i^{\star}}-C_{j}\leq\gamma_{j}-\gamma_{i^{\star}}\,,

(5a)

\displaystyle\forall j>{i^{\star}}\,:\,C_{j}-C_{i^{\star}}>\gamma_{i^{\star}}-\gamma_{j}\,.

(5b)

As the loss of an arm is not observed, the above equations can not lead to a sound arm selection criteria. We thus have to relate the unobservable quantities in terms of the quantities that can be observed. In our setup, we can compare the feedback of two arms, which can be used to estimate their disagreement probability. For notation convenience, we define
p_{ij}\doteq\mathbb{P}\left\{Y^{i}\neq Y^{j}\right\}
. The value of
p_{ij}
can be estimated as it is observable. We use the following result from
Hanawal et al. 2017
that relates the differences in the unobserved error rates in terms of their observable disagreement probability.

Proposition 1

(Proposition 3 in
Hanawal et al. 2017
)
.

For any two arms
i
and
j
,
\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{j}=p_{ij}-2\mathbb{P}\left\{Y^{i}=Y,Y^{j}\neq Y\right\}
.

Now, using
1
, we can replace
Eq.

5a
by

\forall j<{i^{\star}}\,:\,C_{{i^{\star}}}-C_{j}\leq p_{ji^{\star}},

(6)

which only has observable quantities. For
j>{i^{\star}}
, we can replace
Eq.

5b
by using the
\mathrm{WD}
property as follows:

\forall j>{i^{\star}}\,:\,C_{j}-C_{{i^{\star}}}>p_{i^{\star}j}.

(7)

Using
Eq.

6
and
Eq.

7
, our next result gives the optimal arm for a problem instance.

Lemma 1
.

Let
P\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{WD}}
and
\mathcal{B}=\left\{i:\forall j>i,C_{j}-C_{i}>p_{ij}\right\}\cup\{K\}
. Then the arm
I_{t}=\min(\mathcal{B})

P

{proof}

Let
{i^{\star}}
be an optimal arm for the problem instance
P
. Since
p_{i^{\star}j}\doteq\mathbb{P}\left\{Y^{i^{\star}}\neq Y^{j}\right\}
, we have
\forall j<{i^{\star}}:\,C_{{i^{\star}}}-C_{j}\leq\mathbb{P}\left\{Y^{i^{\star}}\neq Y^{j}\right\}\implies C_{{i^{\star}}}-C_{j}\ngtr\mathbb{P}\left\{Y^{i^{\star}}\neq Y^{j}\right\}\implies j\notin\mathcal{B},\forall j<{i^{\star}}

in the cascade. Hence the element of the set

{i^{\star}}<h_{1}<\cdots<h_{t}<K

Remark 1
.

The
\mathrm{WD}
property holds trivially for the problem instances that satisfy
\mathrm{SD}
property as the difference of mean losses is the same as the disagreement probability between two arms due to

Thompson Sampling based Algorithm for USS

Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) based methods are useful for dealing with the trade-off between exploration and exploitation in bandit problems
(
Auer et al. 2002
;
Garivier and Cappé 2011
)
. UCB has been widely used for solving various sequential decision-making problems. On the other hand, Thompson Sampling (TS) is an online algorithm based on Bayesian updates. TS selects an arm to play according to its probability of being the best arm, and it is shown that TS is empirically superior then UCB based algorithms for various MAB problems
(
Chapelle and Li 2011
)
. TS also achieves lower bound for MAB when rewards of arms have Bernoulli distribution, as shown by
Kaufmann et al. 2012
.

\displaystyle\implies\mathbb{P}\left\{I_{t}=j,j<{i^{\star}}|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\}\leq

\displaystyle~(1-q_{j,t})\mathbb{P}\left\{j\succ_{t}k,\forall k>{i^{\star}},j<i^{\star}|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\}.\mbox{\hskip 11.38109pt (\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{def:q})}

(10)

Similarly, the probability of selecting an arm whose index is larger than the optimal arm can be lower bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{I_{t}\geq i^{\star}|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left\{I_{t}=i^{\star}|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\}\geq\mathbb{P}\left\{I_{t}=i^{\star},{i^{\star}}\succ_{t}j,j<{i^{\star}}|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{i^{\star}\succ_{t}k,\forall k>{i^{\star}},{i^{\star}}\succ_{t}j,j<{i^{\star}}|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\}\mbox{\hskip 11.38109pt (\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{lem:Bx})}

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\left\{{i^{\star}}\succ_{t}j,j\succ_{t}k,\forall k>{i^{\star}},j<{i^{\star}}|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\}\mbox{\hskip 14.22636pt (\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{def:trans_prop})}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{{i^{\star}}\succ_{t}j,j<{i^{\star}}|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\}\mathbb{P}\left\{j\succ_{t}k,\forall k>{i^{\star}},j<{i^{\star}}|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\}.

If arm
{i^{\star}}
is preferred over the arm
j
then
\tilde{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\geq C_{i^{\star}}-C_{j}
. As
C_{i^{\star}}-C_{j}=p_{i^{\star}j}-\xi_{j}
for
j<i^{\star}
,

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{\tilde{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\geq p_{i^{\star}j}-\xi_{j}|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\}\mathbb{P}\left\{j\succ_{t}k,\forall k>{i^{\star}},j<i^{\star}\right\}

\displaystyle\implies\mathbb{P}\left\{I_{t}\geq{i^{\star}}|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq q_{j,t}\mathbb{P}\left\{j\succ_{t}k,\forall k>{i^{\star}},j<i^{\star}\right\}.\mbox{\hskip 48.36967pt (\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{def:q})}

(11)

Combining the
Eq.

10
and
Eq.

11
, we get

\mathbb{P}\left\{I_{t}=j,j<{i^{\star}}|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\}\leq\frac{(1-q_{j,t})}{q_{j,t}}\mathbb{P}\left\{I_{t}\geq{i^{\star}}|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\}.

.

P\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{WD}}

s
be the number of times the sub-optimal arm

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{P}\left\{I_{t}=j,j<{i^{\star}}\right\}\leq\frac{24}{\xi_{j}^{2}}+\sum_{s\geq 8/\xi_{j}}\Theta\left(\exp^{-{s\xi_{j}^{2}}/{2}}+\frac{\exp^{-{sd(p_{i^{\star}j}-\xi_{j},p_{i^{\star}j})}}}{(s+1)\xi_{j}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\exp^{{s\xi_{j}^{2}}/{4}}-1}\right).

{proof}

3

Lemma 5
.

Let
\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}
be the empirical estimate of
p_{i^{\star}j}
and
j>{i^{\star}}
. Then, for any
x_{j}>p_{i^{\star}j}
and
y_{j}>x_{j}
,

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{P}\left\{\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j},\tilde{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}>y_{j}\right\}\leq\frac{\ln T}{d(x_{j},y_{j})}+1.

{proof}

Define
L_{j}(T)=\frac{\ln T}{d(x_{j},y_{j})}
. Let
N_{j}(t)
be the number of times the output from arm
j
is observed in
t
rounds. Then, the given probability term can be decomposed into two parts:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{P}\left\{\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j},\tilde{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}>y_{j}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{P}\left\{\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j},\tilde{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}>y_{j},N_{j}(t)\leq L_{j}(T)\right\}+

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{P}\left\{\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j},\tilde{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}>y_{j},N_{j}(t)>L_{j}(T)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq L_{j}(T)

\displaystyle+\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{P}\left\{\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j},\tilde{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}>y_{j},N_{j}(t)>L_{j}(T)\right\}.

(12)

The first term of the above decomposition is bounded trivially by
L_{j}(T)
. To bound the second term, we demonstrate that if
N_{j}(t)
is large enough and event
\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j}
is satisfied, then the probability that the event
\tilde{p}_{ij}^{(t)}>y_{j}
happens, is small. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j},\tilde{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}>y_{j},N_{j}(t)>L_{j}(T)\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\left\{\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j},\tilde{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}>y_{j},N_{j}(t)>L_{j}(T)\right\}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\left\{\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j},\tilde{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}>y_{j},N_{j}(t)>L_{j}(T)\right\}}|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right]\right].

Since
N_{j}(t)
and
\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}
are determined by the history
\mathcal{H}_{t}
,

\displaystyle\qquad=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathds{1}_{\left\{\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j},N_{j}(t)>L_{j}(T)\right\}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\tilde{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}>y_{j}|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\}\right].

(13)

Now, by definition,
\mathcal{S}_{i^{\star}j}(t)=\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}N_{j}(t)
, and therefore,
\tilde{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}
is a Beta
(\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}N_{j}(t)+1,(1-\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)})N_{j}(t)+1)
distributed random variable. A Beta
(\alpha,\beta)
random variable is stochastically dominated by Beta
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
if
\alpha^{\prime}\geq\alpha,\beta^{\prime}\leq\beta
. Therefore, if
\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j}
, the distribution of
\tilde{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}
is stochastically dominated by Beta
(x_{j}N_{j}(t)+1,(1-x_{j})N_{j}(t))
. Therefore, given a history
\mathcal{H}_{t}
such that
\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j}
and
N_{j}(t)>L_{j}(T)
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left\{\tilde{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}>y_{j}|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\}=1-F_{x_{j}N_{j}(t)+1,(1-x_{j})N_{j}(t)}^{beta}(y_{j}).

Now, using Beta-Binomial equality (
1
), we obtain that for any fixed
N_{j}(t)>L_{j}(T)
,

\displaystyle 1-F_{x_{j}N_{j}(t)+1,(1-x_{j})N_{j}(t)}^{beta}(y_{j})

\displaystyle=F_{N_{j}(t),y_{j}}^{B}(x_{j}N_{j}(t))

(using
1
)

Here
F_{N_{j}(t),y_{j}}^{B}(x_{j}N_{j}(t))
is the cdf of Binomial distribution with parameter
y_{j}
and
N_{j}(T)
observations. Let
\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\prime}
be the number of successes observed in
N_{j}(T)
observations. Then,

\displaystyle 1-F_{x_{j}N_{j}(t)+1,(1-x_{j})N_{j}(t)}^{beta}(y_{j})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\prime}\leq x_{j}N_{j}(t)\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{\frac{\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\prime}}{N_{j}(t)}\leq x_{j}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{\hat{y}_{j}\leq x_{j}\right\}\hskip 48.36967pt\mbox{(using $\hat{y}_{j}=\mathcal{S}_{t}^{\prime}/N_{j}(t)$)}

\displaystyle\leq\exp^{-N_{j}(t)d(x_{j},y_{j})}\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{(using Chernoff-Hoeffding bound)}

\displaystyle\leq\exp^{-L_{j}(t)d(x_{j},y_{j})},\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{(as $N_{j}(t)>L_{j}(T)$)}

which is smaller than
1/T
because
L_{j}(T)=\frac{\log(T)}{d(x_{j},y_{j})}
. Substituting, we get that for a history
\mathcal{H}_{t}
such that
\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j}
and
N_{j}(t)>L_{j}(T)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\tilde{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}>y_{j}|\mathcal{H}_{t}\right\}\leq\frac{1}{T}.

For other history
\mathcal{H}_{t}
, the indicator term
\mathds{1}_{\left\{\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j},N_{j}(t)>L_{j}(T)\right\}}
in
Eq.

13
will be 0 as either event
\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j}
or event
N_{j}(t)>L_{j}(T)
is violated. Summing over
t
, this bounds the right hand side term in
Eq.

13
as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{P}\left\{\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j},\tilde{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}>y_{j},N_{j}(t)>L_{j}(T)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\mathds{1}_{\left\{\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j},N_{j}(t)>L_{j}(T)\right\}}}{T}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{1}{T}\right]

\displaystyle=1.

Replacing the second term in
Eq.

12
by its upper bound and
L_{j}(T)
with its value,

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{P}\left\{\hat{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}\leq x_{j},\tilde{p}_{i^{\star}j}^{(t)}>y_{j}\right\}\leq\frac{\ln T}{d(x_{j},y_{j})}+1.

Let
\Delta_{j}=C_{j}+\gamma_{j}-(C_{i^{\star}}+\gamma_{i^{\star}})
be the sub-optimality gap for arm

. Now we state the problem dependent regret upper bound of

.

Theorem 1

(Problem Dependent Bound)
.

Let
P\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{WD}}
and satisfies the transitivity property. If
\varepsilon>0
then, the expected regret of
USS-TS
in

rounds is bounded by

{\mathfrak{R}_{T}}\leq\sum_{j>i^{\star}}\frac{(1+\varepsilon)\ln T}{d(p_{i^{\star}j},p_{i^{\star}j}+\xi_{j})}\Delta_{j}+O\left(\frac{K-i^{\star}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right),

{proof}

(sketch)

Let
M_{j}(T)
is the number of times arm
j
is selected by
USS-TS
. Then, the regret of
USS-TS
is given by
{\mathfrak{R}_{T}}=\sum_{j\in[K]}\mathbb{E}\left[M_{j}(T)\right]\Delta_{j}=\sum_{j\in[K]}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathds{1}_{\left\{I_{t}=j\right\}}\right]\Delta_{j}=\sum_{j\in[K]}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{P}\left\{I_{t}=j\right\}\Delta_{j}
. We divide the regret into two parts and it can be re-written as

(Problem Independent Bound)
.

Let

and satisfies the transitivity property. Then the expected regret of

in

•

for any instance in

•

for any instance in

{proof}

(sketch)

To get the above problem independent regret upper bound, we maximize the problem-dependent regret of

Corollary 1
.

Let
P\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{WD}}
and satisfies the transitivity property. Then the expected regret of
USS-TS
on
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SD}}
is
\tilde{O}(T^{1/2})
and on
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{WD}}
it is
\tilde{O}(T^{2/3})
, where
\tilde{O}
hides
K
and the logarithmic terms that are having
T
in them.

Discussion on optimality of
USS-TS
:

Stochastic partial monitoring problems can be classified as an ‘easy,’ ‘hard,’ or ‘hopeless’ problem with expected regret bounds of the order
\Theta(T^{1/2}),\Theta(T^{2/3})
, or
\Theta(T)
, respectively. And there exists no other class of problems in between
(
Bartók et al. 2014
)
. The class
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SD}}
is regret equivalent to a stochastic multi-armed bandit with side observations
(
Hanawal et al. 2017
)
, for which regret scales as
\Theta(T^{1/2})
, hence
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SD}}
resides in the easy class and our bound on it is near-optimal. Since
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{WD}}\supsetneq\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SD}}
,
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{WD}}
is not easy problem. Since
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{WD}}
is also learnable, it cannot be a hopeless problem. Therefore, the class
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{WD}}
is hard. We thus conclude that the regret bound of
USS-TS
is also near-optimal in
T
up to a logarithmic term.

We consider the problem instances with the minimum possible value of

\xi=0
. Through our experiments, we show that the stronger the
\mathrm{WD}
property (large value of
\xi
) for the problem instance, it is easier to identify the optimal arm and, hence the less regret is incurred by
USS-TS
.

6
Conclusion

We studied the unsupervised sequential selection (USS) problem, where both accuracy and cost of using arms are important. It is a variant of the stochastic partial monitoring problem, where the losses are not observed. Still, one can compare the feedback of two arms to see if they agree or disagree. We estimate the disagreement probability between each pair of the arms and develop an algorithm named
USS-TS
that achieves near-optimal regret. We demonstrate our algorithms’ performance on two real datasets and empirically show that any problem instance satisfying WD property has sub-linear regret.
We ignored the inherent side observations due to the arms’ cascade structure. By using these side observations, one can tighten the regret bounds. Another interesting future direction is to develop algorithms that relax the cascade structure assumption and selects the best subset of arms.

Fact 2

(Chernoff bound for Bernoulli distributed random variables)
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be i.i.d. Bernoulli distributed random variables. Let

\mathbb{P}\left\{\hat{\mu}_{n}\geq\mu+\varepsilon\right\}\leq\exp\left(-d(\mu+\varepsilon,\mu)n\right),

and, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\mu)

\mathbb{P}\left\{\hat{\mu}_{n}\leq\mu-\varepsilon\right\}\leq\exp\left(-d(\mu-\varepsilon,\mu)n\right),

where
d(x,\mu)=x\log\left(\frac{x}{\mu}\right)+(1-x)\log\left(\frac{1-x}{1-\mu}\right)

For

Fact 4

Let

\frac{1}{\exp^{Dx}-1}\leq\begin{cases}\frac{\exp^{-Dx}}{1-a}&\left(x\geq\ln\left(1/a\right)/D\right)\\

{proof}

. We can re-write,
\frac{1}{\exp^{Dx}-1}=\frac{\exp^{-Dx}}{1-\exp^{-Dx}}

is strictly decreasing function for all

\exp^{-Dx}\leq a
holds for any

and

. Hence,
\frac{\exp^{-Dx}}{1-\exp^{-Dx}}\leq\frac{\exp^{-Dx}}{1-a}
for all

.

Now we will prove the second part,

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\exp^{Dx}-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ln(1/a)}{D^{2}}+\sum_{x\geq\ln\left(1/a\right)/D}^{n}\frac{\exp^{-Dx}}{1-a}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\ln(1/a)}{D^{2}}+\frac{1}{(1-a)}\int_{x=0}^{\infty}\exp^{-Dx}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\ln(1/a)}{D^{2}}+\frac{1}{(1-a)}\left.\left(\frac{\exp^{-Dx}}{-D}\right)\right|_{x=0}^{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ln(1/a)}{D^{2}}+\frac{1}{(1-a)}\left(0-\frac{\exp^{0}}{-D}\right)

\displaystyle\implies\sum_{x=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\exp^{Dx}-1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{9\ln T}{2\xi_{j}^{2}}+1+\frac{9}{2\xi_{j}^{2}}

\displaystyle\implies\mathbb{E}\left[M_{j}(T)\right]

\displaystyle\leq O\left(\frac{\ln T}{\xi_{j}^{2}}\right).

(19)

The regret of
USS-TS
is given by

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}_{T}}=\sum\limits_{j\neq i^{\star}}\mathbb{E}\left[M_{j}(T)\right]\Delta_{j}=\sum\limits_{j<i^{\star}}\mathbb{E}\left[M_{j}(T)\right]\Delta_{j}+\sum\limits_{j>i^{\star}}\mathbb{E}\left[M_{j}(T)\right]\Delta_{j}

Recall
\Delta_{j}=C_{j}+\gamma_{j}-(C_{i^{\star}}+\gamma_{i^{\star}})
and for any two arms
i
and
j
,
0\leq p_{ij}-(\gamma_{j}-\gamma_{i^{\star}})\leq\beta
. By using Eq. (
8a
) for
j<i^{\star}
, we have
\Delta_{j}=\xi_{j}-(p_{i^{\star}j}-(\gamma_{i^{\star}}-\gamma_{j}))\implies\Delta_{j}\leq\xi_{j}
, and using Eq. (
8b
) for
j>i^{\star}
, we have
\Delta_{j}=\xi_{j}+(p_{i^{\star}j}-(\gamma_{i^{\star}}-\gamma_{j}))\implies\Delta_{j}\leq\xi_{j}+\beta
. Replacing
\Delta_{j}
,

\Rightarrow{\mathfrak{R}_{T}}\leq\sum\limits_{j<i^{\star}}\mathbb{E}\left[M_{j}(T)\right]\xi_{j}+\sum\limits_{j>i^{\star}}\mathbb{E}\left[M_{j}(T)\right](\xi_{j}+\beta).

Let
0<\xi^{\prime}<1
. Then
{\mathfrak{R}_{T}}
can be written as:

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi^{\prime}>\xi_{j}\\
j<i^{\star}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\left[M_{j}(T)\right]\xi_{j}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi^{\prime}<\xi_{j}\\
j<i^{\star}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\left[M_{j}(T)\right]\xi_{j}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi^{\prime}>\xi_{j}\\
j>i^{\star}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\left[M_{j}(T)\right](\xi_{j}+\beta)+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi^{\prime}<\xi_{j}\\
j>i^{\star}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\left[M_{j}(T)\right](\xi_{j}+\beta).

Using
\sum\limits_{\xi^{\prime}>\xi_{j}}\mathbb{E}\left[M_{j}(T)\right]\leq T
for any
j
such that
\xi^{\prime}>\xi_{j}
,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq T\xi^{\prime}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi^{\prime}<\xi_{j}\\
j<i^{\star}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\left[M_{j}(T)\right]\xi_{j}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi^{\prime}<\xi_{j}\\
j>i^{\star}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\left[M_{j}(T)\right](\xi_{j}+\beta).

Substituting the value of
{\mathfrak{R}_{T}}
from
Eq.

18
and
Eq.

19
,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq T\xi^{\prime}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi^{\prime}<\xi_{j}\\
j<i^{\star}\end{subarray}}O\left(\frac{\xi_{j}\ln T}{\xi_{j}^{2}}\right)+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi^{\prime}<\xi_{j}\\
j>i^{\star}\end{subarray}}O\left(\frac{(\xi_{j}+\beta)\ln T}{\xi_{j}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq T\xi^{\prime}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi^{\prime}<\xi_{j}\\
j<i^{\star}\end{subarray}}O\left(\frac{\ln T}{\xi_{j}}\right)+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\xi^{\prime}<\xi_{j}\\
j>i^{\star}\end{subarray}}O\left(\frac{\ln T}{\xi_{j}}+\frac{\beta\ln T}{\xi_{j}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq T\xi^{\prime}+O\left(\frac{K\ln T}{\xi^{\prime}}\right)+O\left(\frac{K\ln T}{\xi^{\prime}}+\frac{\beta K\ln T}{{\xi^{\prime}}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=T\xi^{\prime}+O\left(K\ln T\left(\frac{1}{\xi^{\prime}}+\frac{\beta}{{\xi^{\prime}}^{2}}\right)\right)

Let there exist a variable
\alpha
such that
O\left(K\ln T\left(\frac{1}{\xi^{\prime}}+\frac{\beta}{{\xi^{\prime}}^{2}}\right)\right)\leq\alpha K\ln T\left(\frac{1}{\xi^{\prime}}+\frac{\beta}{{\xi^{\prime}}^{2}}\right)
,

\displaystyle\implies{\mathfrak{R}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq=T\xi^{\prime}+\alpha K\ln T\left(\frac{1}{\xi^{\prime}}+\frac{\beta}{{\xi^{\prime}}^{2}}\right).

(20)

Consider
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{WD}}
class of problems. As
\xi^{\prime}<1
and
\beta\leq 2
(as arms in the cascade may not be ordered by their error-rates, it is possible that
\gamma_{i}<\gamma_{j}
), we have
\left(\frac{1}{\xi^{\prime}}+\frac{\beta}{{\xi^{\prime}}^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{\beta+1}{{\xi^{\prime}}^{2}}\leq\frac{3}{{\xi^{\prime}}^{2}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq=T\xi^{\prime}+\frac{3\alpha K\ln T}{{\xi^{\prime}}^{2}}.

Choose
\xi^{\prime}=\left(\frac{6\alpha K\ln T}{T}\right)^{1/3}
which maximize above upper bound and we get,

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(6\alpha K\ln T\right)^{1/3}T^{2/3}+\frac{\left(6\alpha K\ln T\right)^{1/3}}{2}T^{2/3}

\displaystyle\implies{\mathfrak{R}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(6\alpha K\ln T\right)^{1/3}T^{2/3}=O\left(\left(K\ln T\right)^{1/3}T^{2/3}\right)

It completes our proof for the case when any problem instance belongs to
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{WD}}
.

Now we consider any problem instance
\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SD}}
. For any
\theta\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{SD}}\Rightarrow\forall j\in[K],\;p_{ij}=\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{j}\implies\beta=0
(Setting
\mathbb{P}\left\{Y^{i}=Y,Y^{j}\neq Y\right\}=0
for
j>i
in Proposition 3 of
Hanawal et al. 2017
). We can rewrite
Eq.

20
as

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq T\xi^{\prime}+\frac{\alpha K\ln T}{{\xi^{\prime}}}.

Choose
\xi^{\prime}=\left(\frac{\alpha K\ln T}{T}\right)^{1/2}
which maximize above upper bound and we get,

\displaystyle\implies{\mathfrak{R}_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\alpha KT\ln T\right)^{1/2}=O\left(\sqrt{KT\ln T}\right)

This complete proof for second part of Theorem
2
.

Abstract




Abstract.
We establish a Rademacher type theorem involving Hamiltonians
H(x,p)
under very weak conditions in both of Euclidean and Carnot-Carathéodory spaces.
In particular,
H(x,p)
is assumed to be only measurable in the variable
x
,
and to be quasiconvex and lower-semicontinuous in the variable
p
. Without the lower-semicontinuity in the variable
p
, we provide a counter example showing the failure of such a Rademacher type theorem.
Moreover, by applying such a Rademacher type theorem we build up an existence result of absolute minimizers for the corresponding
L^{\infty}
-functional. These improve or extend several known results in the literature.

Keywords:

L^{\infty}
-functional, absolute minimizer, Carnot-Carathéodory metric space, Rademacher theorem

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a domain (that is, an open and connected subset) of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
with
n\geq 2
. We first recall Rademacher’s theorem in Euclidean spaces. See Appendix for some of its consequence related to Sobolev and Lipschitz spaces.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
u:{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Omega}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a Lipschitz function,
that is,

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq\lambda|x-y|\quad\forall x,y\in\Omega\quad\mbox{for some $0\leq\lambda<\infty$,}

(1.1)

then, at almost all
x\in\Omega
,
u
is differentiable and

|\nabla u(x)|={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}u(x)
. Here
|\nabla u(x)|
is the Euclidean length of the derivative
\nabla u(x)
at
x
, and
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}u(x)
is the pointwise Lipschitz constant at
x
defined by

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}u(x):=\limsup_{y\to x}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{|y-x|}.

(1.2)

The above Rademacher’s theorem was extended to Carnot-Carathéodory spaces
(\Omega,X)
, where
X
is a family of smooth vector fields in
{\Omega}
satisfying the Hörmander condition (See Section 2). Denote by
Xu
the distributional horizontal derivative of
u\in L^{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
. Write
{d_{CC}}
as the Carnot-Carathéodory distance with respect to
X
.
One then has the following; see
[
25
,
22
,
20
,
37
]
and, for the better result in
Carnot group and Carnot type vector field, see
[
41
,
43
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a Lipschitz function with respect to
{d_{CC}}
,
that is,

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq\lambda{d_{CC}}(x,y)\quad\forall x,y\in\Omega\quad\mbox{for some $0\leq\lambda<\infty$,}

(1.3)

then,
Xu\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
and, for almost all
x\in\Omega
, the length

|Xu(x)|\leq\lambda
.

Under the additional assumption that
X
is a Carnot type vector field in
\Omega
, or in particular,

(\Omega,X)
is a domain in some Carnot group, one further has
|Xu(x)|={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d_{CC}}u(x)
for almost all
x\in\Omega
, where
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d_{CC}}u(x)
is
defined by (
1.2
) with
|y-x|
replaced by
d_{CC}(y,x)
.

This paper aims to build up some Rademacher type theorem involving Hamiltonians
H(x,p)
in both of Euclidean and Carnot-Carathéodory spaces. Throughout this paper, the following assumptions are always held for
H(x,p)
.

Assumption 1
.

Suppose that
H:\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}[0,+\infty)}
is measurable and satisfies

(H1)

For each
x\in\Omega
,

H(x,\cdot)
is quasi-convex, that is,

H(x,tp+(1-t)q)\leq\max\{H(x,p),H(x,q)\},\quad\forall p,q\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m},\ \forall\,t\in[0,1]\ \text{and}\ \forall x\in\Omega.

(H2)

For each
x\in\Omega
,
H(x,0)=\min_{p\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{m}}}H(x,p)=0
.

(H3)

It holds that

R_{\lambda}<\infty
for all
\lambda\geq 0
, and
\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}R_{\lambda}^{\prime}=\infty
, where and in below,

R_{\lambda}:=\sup\{|p|\ |\ (x,p)\in\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m},H(x,p)\leq\lambda\}

and

R^{\prime}_{\lambda}:=\inf\{|p|\ |\ (x,p)\in\Omega\times{\mathbb{R}}^{m},H(x,p)\geq\lambda\}.

For any
\lambda\geq 0
, we define

d_{\lambda}(x,y):=\sup\{u(y)-u(x)\ |\ u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(\Omega)\ \mbox{ with }\ \|H(\cdot,Xu)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\lambda\}\quad\forall\mbox{$x,y\in\Omega$}.

(1.4)

Recall that
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(\Omega)
denotes the set of
all functions
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
whose
distributional horizontal derivatives
Xu\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
.
It was known that any function
u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(\Omega)
admits a continuous representative
\widetilde{u}
; see
[
22
, Theorem 1.4]
and also Theorem
2.3
and Remark
2.4
below. In this paper, in particular, in (
1.4
) above, we always
identify functions in
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(\Omega)
as their continuous representatives. We remark that
d_{\lambda}
is not a distance necessarily, but Lemma
2.9
says that
d_{\lambda}
is always a pseudo-distance as defined in Definition
2.8
below.

Given any
\lambda\geq 0
, by the definition (
1.4
),
if
u\in\dot{W}_{X}^{1,\infty}(U)
and
\|H(\cdot,Xu)\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq\lambda
,
then
u(y)-u(x)\leq d_{\lambda}(x,y)\ \forall\,x,y\in\Omega.

It is natural to ask whether the converse is true or not.
However, such converse is not necessarily true as witted by the Hamiltonian

\lfloor|p|\rfloor=\max\{t\in{\mathbb{N}}\ |\ |p|-t\geq 0\}\quad\forall x\in\Omega,\,p\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{m}};

(1.5)

for details see Remark
1.9
below. The point is that
\lfloor|p|\rfloor
is not lower-semicontinuous.
Below, the converse is shown to be true if
H(x,p)
is assumed additionally to be lower-semicontinuous in the variable
p
, that is,

(H0)

For almost all
x\in\Omega
,
H(x,p)\leq\liminf_{q\to p}H(x,q)\quad\forall p\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
H
satisfies (H0)-(H3).
Given any
\lambda\geq 0
and any function
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
the following are equivalent:

(i)

u\in\dot{W}_{X}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\ \text{and}\ \|H(\cdot,Xu)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\lambda
;

(ii)

u(y)-u(x)\leq d_{\lambda}(x,y)

\forall x,y\in\Omega
;

(iii)

For any
x\in\Omega
, there exists a neighborhood
N(x)\subset\Omega
such that

u(y)-u(z)\leq d_{\lambda}(z,y)\quad\forall y,z\in N(x).

In particular, if
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies any one of (i)-(iii), then

\displaystyle\|H(\cdot,Xu)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\inf}\{\lambda\geq 0\ |\ \lambda\ \mbox{satisfies (ii)}\}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\inf}\{\lambda\geq 0\ |\ \lambda\ \mbox{satisfies (iii)}\}.

(1.6)

Using Theorem
1.3
, when
\lambda\geq\lambda_{H}
we prove that
d_{\lambda}
has a pseudo-length property, which allows us to get the following.
Here and below, define

\lambda_{H}:=\inf\{\lambda\geq 0\ |\ R^{\prime}_{\lambda}>0\}.

(1.7)

Since
R_{\lambda}^{\prime}
defined in (H3) of Assumption
1
is always nonnegative and increasing in
\lambda\geq 0
and tends to
\infty
as
\lambda\to\infty
, we know that
0\leq\lambda_{H}<\infty
, and moreover,

\lambda>\lambda_{H}
if and only if
R^{\prime}_{\lambda}>0
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that
H
satisfies (H0)-(H3).
Given any
\lambda\geq\lambda_{H}
and any function
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
the statement (i) in Theorem
1.3
is equivalent to the following

(iv)

For any
x\in\Omega
, there exists a neighborhood
N(x)\subset\Omega
such that

u(y)-u(x)\leq d_{\lambda}(x,y)\quad\forall y\in N(x).

In particular, if
u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies (iv), then

\displaystyle\max\{\lambda_{H},\|H(\cdot,Xu)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\}=\min\{\lambda\geq\lambda_{H}\ |\ \lambda\ \mbox{satisfies (iv)}\}.

(1.8)

As a consequence of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
, we have
the following Corollary
1.5
.
Associated to Hamiltonian
H(x,p)
, we introduce some notion and notations.
Denote by
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{H}(\Omega)
the collection of all

Denote by
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{H}(\Omega,X)
the class of functions

satisfying (ii) for some

equipped with (semi-)norms

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{H}(u,\Omega)={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\inf}\{\lambda\geq\lambda_{H}\ |\ \lambda\ \mbox{satisfies (ii)}\}.

(1.9)

Denote by

the collection of all functions

where we write the pointwise “Lipschitz” constant

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{H}u(x)={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\inf}\{\lambda\geq\lambda_{H}\ |\ \lambda\ \mbox{satisfies (iv)}\}.

(1.10)

Thanks to the right continuity of the map

as given in Lemma

Corollary 1.5
.

Suppose that

satisfies (H0)-(H3) with

.
Then
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{H}(\Omega)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{H}(\Omega)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{H}^{\ast}(\Omega)

\|H(\cdot,Xu)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{H}(u,\Omega)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{H}^{\ast}(u,\Omega).

Next, we apply the above Rademacher type property to study a minimization problem for
L^{\infty}
-functionals corresponding to the above Hamiltonian
H(x,p)
in both Euclidean and Caratheódory spaces:

{\mathcal{F}}(u,U):=\big\|H(\cdot,Xu)\big\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\ \mbox{for any $u\in W^{1,\infty}_{X,{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}}(U)$ and domain $U\subset\Omega$}.

Aronsson
[
2
,
3
,
4

6

in 1960’s initiated the study in this direction
via introducing absolute minimizers. A function

(write

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{\mathcal{F}}(u,V)\leq{\mathcal{F}}(v,V)}\mbox{\ whenever}\ v\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(V)\cap C(\overline{V})\ \mbox{and}\ u\big|_{\partial V}=v\big|_{\partial V}.

Here and throughout this paper,
for domains

B

stands for that

is a bounded subdomain of

Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the background and some known results related to Carnot-Carathéodory spaces employed in the proof.

Let
X:=\{X_{1},...,X_{m}\}
for some
m\leq n
be a family of smooth vector fields in
{\Omega}

which satisfies the Hörmander condition, that is, there is a step
k\geq 1
such that, at each point,

\{X_{i}\}^{m}_{i=1}
and all their commutators up to at most order
k
generate the whole
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
. Then for each
i=1,\cdots,m
,
X_{i}
can be written as

X_{i}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}b_{il}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}}\mbox{ in $\Omega$}

with
b_{il}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
for all
i=1,\cdots,m
and
l=1,\cdots,n
.

Define the
Carnot-Carathéodory distance corresponding to
X
by

d_{CC}(x,y):=\inf\{\ell_{d_{CC}}({\gamma})\ |\ \mbox{${\gamma}\in\mathcal{ACH}(0,1;x,y;\Omega)$}\}

(2.1)

Here and below, we write
{\gamma}\in\mathcal{ACH}(0,1;x,y;\Omega)
if
{\gamma}:[0,1]\to\Omega
is absolutely continuous,
{\gamma}(0)=x,{\gamma}(1)=y
, and there exists measurable functions
c_{i}:[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
with
1\leq i\leq m
such that
\dot{\gamma}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}(t)X_{i}({\gamma}(t))
whenever
\dot{\gamma}(t)
exists.
The length of
{\gamma}
is

\ell_{d_{CC}}({\gamma}):=\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}(t)|\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}^{2}(t)}\,dt.

In the Euclidean case, we have the following remark.

Remark 2.1
.

In Euclidean case, that is,
X=\{\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
, one has

d_{CC}
coincides with the intrinsic distance
d_{E}^{\Omega}
as given in (A.2).
In particular,
d_{CC}(x,y)=d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in\Omega
with
|x-y|<{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(x,\partial\Omega)
. When
\Omega
is convex, one further have
and
d_{CC}(x,y)=|x-y|
for all
x,y\in\Omega
; however, when
\Omega
is not convex, this is not necessarily true.
See Lemma A.4 in Appendix for more details.

Since
X
is a Hörmander vector field in
\Omega
,
for any compact set
K\subset\Omega
, there exists a constant
C(K)\geq 1
such that

C(K)^{-1}|x-y|\leq{d_{CC}}(x,y)\leq C(K)|x-y|^{\frac{1}{k}}\quad{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\forall x,y\in K},

see for example
[
42
]
and
[
28
, Chapter 11]
.
This shows that the topology induces by
(\Omega,d_{CC})
is exactly the Euclidean topology.

Given a function
u\in L^{1}_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}}(\Omega)
, its distributional derivative along
X_{i}
is defined by the identity

\langle X_{i}u,\phi\rangle=\int_{\Omega}uX_{i}^{\ast}\phi\,dx\mbox{ for all $\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)$,}

where
X_{i}^{\ast}=-\sum_{l=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}}(b_{il}\cdot)
denotes the formal adjoint of
X_{i}
. Write
X^{\ast}=(X_{1}^{\ast},\cdots,X_{m}^{\ast})
. We call
Xu:=(X_{1}u,\cdots,X_{m}u)
the horizontal distributional derivative for
u\in L^{1}_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}}(\Omega)
and the norm
|Xu|
is defined by

|Xu|=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m}|X_{i}u|^{2}}.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, denote by
\dot{W}^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)
the
p
-th integrable horizontal Sobolev space, that is,
the collection of all functions
u\in L^{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
with its distributional derivative
Xu\in L^{p}(\Omega)
. Equip
\dot{W}^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)
with the semi-norm

\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{1,p}_{X}(\Omega)}=\||Xu|\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}
.

The following was proved in
[
24
, Lemma 3.5 (II)]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
u\in\dot{W}^{1,p}_{X}(U)
with
1\leq p<\infty
and

U\Subset\Omega
, then
u^{+}=\max\{u,0\}\in\dot{W}^{1,p}_{X}(U)
with
Xu^{+}=(Xu)\chi_{\{x\in U|u>0\}}
almost everywhere.

We recall the following imbedding of horizontal Sobolev spaces from
[
25
, Theorem 1.4]
. For any set
U\subset\Omega
, the Lipschitz class

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d_{CC}}(U)
is the collection of all functions
u:U\to{\mathbb{R}}
with its seminorm

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d_{CC}}(u,U):=\sup_{x\neq y,x,y\in U}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{{d_{CC}}(x,y)}<\infty.

Theorem 2.3
.

For any subdomain
U\Subset\Omega
, if
u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(U)
, then there is a continuous function
\widetilde{u}\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d_{CC}}(U)
with

almost everywhere and

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d_{CC}}(\widetilde{u},U)\leq C(U,\Omega)[\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}+\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(U)}].

Remark 2.4
.

For any
u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(U)
, we call above
\widetilde{u}
given in Theorem

as the continuous representative of

. Up to considering
\widetilde{u}
, in this paper we always assume that

itself is continuous.

We have the following dual formula of
{d_{CC}}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any
x,y\in\Omega
, we have

d_{CC}(x,y)=\sup\{u(y)-u(x)\ |\ u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(\Omega)\mbox{ with $\||Xu|\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1$}\}.

(2.2)

To prove this we need the following bound for the norm of horizontal derivative of smooth
approximation of functions in

, see for example
[

Denote by
\{\eta_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon\in(0,1)}
the standard smooth mollifier, that is,

\eta_{\epsilon}(x)=\epsilon^{-n}\eta(\frac{x}{\epsilon})\quad\forall x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
where
\eta\in C^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
is supported in unit ball of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
(with Euclidean distance),

\eta\geq 0
and
\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\eta\,dx=1
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Given any compact set
K\subset\Omega
, there is
\epsilon_{K}\in(0,1)
such that
for any
\epsilon<\epsilon_{K}
and

|X(u\ast\eta_{\epsilon})(x)|\leq\||Xu|\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+A_{\epsilon}(u)\quad\forall x\in K,

(2.3)

where

and

in

.

Proof of Lemma
2.5
.

Recall that it was shown by
[

, Proposition 3.1]
that

d_{CC}(x,y)=\sup\{u(y)-u(x)\ |\ u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\mbox{ with
$\||Xu|\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1$}\}\quad\forall x,y\in\Omega.

(2.4)

It then suffices to show that for any
u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(\Omega)
with

\||Xu|\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1
, we have

u(y)-u(x)\leq d_{CC}(x,y)\quad\forall x,y\in\Omega
.

Note that
u
is assumed to be continuous as in Remark
2.4
.

To this end, given any
x,y\in\Omega
, for any
\epsilon>0

there exists a curve
{\gamma}_{\epsilon}\subset\mathcal{ACH}(0,1;x,y;\Omega)
such that

\ell_{d_{CC}}({\gamma}_{\epsilon})\leq(1+\epsilon)d_{CC}(x,y).

We can find a domain
U\Subset\Omega
such that
{\gamma}_{\epsilon}\subset U
.
It is standard that
u\ast\eta_{t}\to u
uniformly in
\overline{U}
and hence

u(y)-u(x)=\lim_{t\to 0}[u\ast\eta_{t}(y)-u\ast\eta_{t}(x)].

Next, by Proposition

,
for

one has

|X(u\ast\eta_{t})(z)|\leq\||Xu|\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+A_{t}u(z)\quad\forall z\in\overline{U},

and moreover,

uniformly in
\overline{U}
as

.
Obviously,
we can find
t_{\epsilon,\overline{U}}<t_{\overline{U}}

such that for any

,
we have
A_{t}u(x)\leq\epsilon
and hence, by
\||Xu|\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1

|X(u\ast\eta_{t})(z)|\leq 1+\epsilon,

for all

. Therefore

\displaystyle u\ast\eta_{t}(y)-u\ast\eta_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}[(u\ast\eta_{t})\circ{\gamma}_{t}]^{\prime}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}X(u\ast\eta_{t})({\gamma}_{t}(s))\cdot\dot{\gamma}_{t}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon)\ell_{d_{CC}}({\gamma}_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon)(1+\epsilon)d_{CC}(x,y).

Sending

and

∎

A.6

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}_{d_{CC}}(\Omega)

Lemma 2.7
.

We have
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(\Omega)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d_{CC}}(\Omega)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}_{d_{CC}}(u,\Omega)

Proof.

First, we show

Next, we recall some concepts from metric geometry. First we recall the notion of pseudo-distance.

Definition 2.8
.

We say that
\rho
is a pseudo-distance in a set
\Omega\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
if
\rho
is a function in
\Omega\times\Omega
such that

(i)

\rho(x,x)=0
for all
x\in\Omega
and

\rho(x,y)\geq 0
for all
x,y\in\Omega
;

(ii)

\rho(x,z)\leq\rho(x,y)+\rho(y,z)
for all
x,y,z\in\Omega
.

We call
(\Omega,\rho)
as a pseudo-metric space. The topology induced by
\{\rho(x,\cdot)\}_{x\in\Omega}
(resp.
\{\rho(\cdot,x)\}_{x\in\Omega}
) is the weakest topology on
\Omega
such that
\rho(x,\cdot)
(resp.
\rho(\cdot,x)
)
is continuous for all
x\in\Omega
.

We remark that since the above pseudo-distance
\rho
may not have symmetry, the topology induced by
\{\rho(x,\cdot)\}_{x\in\Omega}
in
\Omega
may be different from that induced by
\{\rho(\cdot,x)\}_{x\in\Omega}
.

Suppose that
H(x,p)
is an Hamiltonian in
\Omega
satisfying assumptions (H0)-(H3).
Let
\{d_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\geq 0}
be defined as in (
1.4
). Thanks to the convention in Remark
2.4
, one has

d_{\lambda}(x,y):=\sup\{u(y)-u(x)\ |\ u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(\Omega)\ \mbox{with}\ \|H(\cdot,Xu)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\lambda\}\quad\forall\mbox{$x,y\in\Omega$}.

(2.9)

The following properties holds for
d_{\lambda}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

The following holds.

(i)

For any

U
.
Thus, at any
z\in U
, we can find

with
-k\leq j\leq k
, which depends on

,
such that
Mj\leq ku(z)\leq M(j+1)
. Letting

, we have

\frac{l}{k}\leq u(z)\leq\frac{l+M}{k}
.
We claim that
u_{k}(z)\in[u(z),\frac{l+M}{k}]
.
Obviously, this claim gives

|u(z)-u_{k}(z)|\leq\frac{M}{k}\quad\forall z\in U,

and hence, the desired convergence
u_{k}\to u
in
C^{0}(U)
as
k\to\infty
.

Below we prove the above claim
u_{k}(z)\in[u(z),\frac{l+M}{k}]
. Recall that

u(z)\in[\frac{l}{k},\frac{l+M}{k}]
for some

l=Mj
with
-k\leq j\leq k
.

First, we prove
u_{k}(z)\leq\frac{l+M}{k}
.
If
l+M>Mk
, then
M<(l+M)/k
.
Since

F_{k,Mk}=\{y\in U\ |\ u(y)\leq M\}=U,

we have

d_{\lambda,F_{k,Mk}}(z)=0
and hence,

u_{k,Mk}(z)=M+d_{\lambda,F_{k,Mk}}(z)=M<\frac{l+M}{k}.

Therefore,

u_{k}(z)\leq u_{k,Mk}(z)<\frac{l+M}{k}.

(3.3)

If
l+M\leq Mk
, then
u(z)\in[\frac{l}{k},\frac{l+M}{k}]
implies
z\in F_{k,l+M}
and hence
d_{\lambda}(z,F_{k,l+M})=0
. Thus

u_{k}(z)\leq u_{k,l+M}(z)=\frac{l+M}{k}+d_{\lambda}(z,F_{k,l+M})=\frac{l+M}{k}.

(3.4)

Combining (
3.4
) and (
3.3
), we have
u_{k}(z)\leq\frac{l+M}{k}

as desired.

Next, we prove
u(z)\leq u_{k}(z)
.

For any
-k\leq j\leq k
with
Mj\leq l
, since
u(z)\geq\frac{l}{k}\geq\frac{Mj}{k}
, we can find
w\in\partial F_{k,Mj}
such that
d_{\lambda,F_{k,Mj}}(z)=d_{\lambda}(w,z)
. Since
w\in\partial F_{k,Mj}
,
we deduce that
u(w)=\frac{Mj}{k}
and

u_{k,Mj}(z)=\frac{Mj}{k}+d_{\lambda,F_{k,Mj}}(z)=u(w)+d_{\lambda}(w,z).

(3.5)

Note that
w\in\overline{U}
, hence there exists a sequence
\{w_{s}\}_{s\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset U
such that

w_{s}\to w
as
s\to\infty
.
Thus by the assumption (iii),

u(z)-u(w)=\lim_{s\to\infty}[u(z)-u(w_{s})]\leq\lim_{s\to\infty}d_{\lambda}(w_{s},z)

By the triangle inequality and
d_{\lambda}(w_{s},w)\leq R_{\lambda}d_{CC}(w_{s},w)
given in Lemma
2.9
, we then obtain

u(z)-u(w)\leq\lim_{s\to\infty}[d_{\lambda}(w_{s},w)+d_{\lambda}(w,z)]=d_{\lambda}(w,z).

(3.6)

Combining (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we have

u(z)\leq u(w)+d_{\lambda}(w,z)=u_{k,Mj}(z).

(3.7)

On the other hand,
for any
-k\leq j\leq k
with
Mj>l
, we have

u_{k,Mj}(z)\geq\frac{Mj}{k}\geq\frac{M(l+1)}{k}>u(z).

From this and (
3.7
), it follows that

u_{k}(z)=\min_{j\in\{-k,...,l/M\}}u_{k,Mj}(z)\geq u(z)

as desired.

Let

Note that the restriction of

in

,
and moreover,

1.3
with

in

.
Thus instead of

, below we use intrinsic pseudo metrics

in
U
,
which are defined via (

) with

replaced by
U
, that is,

d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y):=\sup\{u(y)-u(x):u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(U),\ \|H(\cdot,Xu)\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq\lambda\}\quad\forall\mbox{$x,y\in U$ and $\lambda\geq 0$}.

(5.1)

Obviously we have proved the following.

Corollary 5.1
.

Theorem
1.3
, Theorem
1.4
and Proposition
4.1
hold with
\Omega
replaced by
U
and
d_{\lambda}
replaced by

. In particular,

for all
\lambda\geq 0
.

Observe that, apriori,

is only defined in

but not in

.
Naturally, we extend

\widetilde{d}_{\lambda}^{U}(x,y)=\lim_{r\to 0}\inf\{d_{\lambda}^{U}(z,w)\ |\ (z,w)\in U\times U,|(z,w)-(x,y)|\leq r\}.

Obviously,
\widetilde{d}^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)=d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)
for all
(x,y)\in U\times U
, and
\widetilde{d}_{\lambda}^{U}
is lower semicontinuous in
\overline{U}\times\overline{U}
,
that is, for any

, the set

\{(x,y)\in\overline{U}\times\overline{U}\ |\ \widetilde{d}^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)>a\}

\overline{U}
.
One may also note that it may happen that

for some
(x,y)\notin U\times U
.
Below, for the sake of simplicity, we write
\widetilde{d}_{\lambda}^{U}
as
d_{\lambda}^{U}
. We define

by letting
H(x,p)=|p|
in
U
and
\lambda=1
in (
5.1
).

The following property will be used later.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
U\Subset\Omega
be a subdomain and
\lambda\geq 0
.

(i)

For any
x,y\in\overline{U}
, we have
d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)\geq d_{\lambda}(x,y)\geq R^{\prime}_{\lambda}d_{CC}(x,y)
.

(ii)

For any
x\in U
and
y\in U
with
{d_{CC}}(x,y)<{d_{CC}}(x,\partial{U})
, we have

d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)\leq R_{\lambda}d_{CC}(x,y).

(iii)

For any
x\in U
, let
x^{\ast}\in\partial U
be the point
such that
d_{CC}(x,x^{\ast})=d_{CC}(x,\partial U)
. Then

d_{\lambda}^{U}(x,x^{\ast})\leq R_{\lambda}d_{CC}(x,x^{\ast})<\infty.

(iv)

For any
x\in\overline{U}
and
y\in U
we have

\mbox{$d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,z)\leq d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)+d^{U}_{\lambda}(y,z)$ and $d^{U}_{\lambda}(z,x)\leq d^{U}_{\lambda}(z,y)+d^{U}_{\lambda}(y,x)\quad\forall z\in\partial U$}.

(v)

Given any
z\in\partial U
, if
d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,z)=\infty
for some
x\in\overline{U}
,
then
d^{U}_{\lambda}(y,z)=+\infty
for any
y\in\overline{U}
.

(vi)

Given any
x,y\in\overline{U}\times\overline{U}
the map
\lambda\in[0,\infty)\mapsto d_{\lambda}^{U}(x,y)\in[0,\infty]
is nondecreasing and for
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}0<\lambda<\mu<\infty}
,

d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)<\infty\text{ if and only if }d^{U}_{\mu}(x,y)<\infty.

As a consequence,

\overline{U}^{\ast}:=\{y\in\overline{U}\ |\ d_{\lambda}^{U}(x,y)<\infty\quad\mbox{for some $x\in U$ and $\lambda>0$}\}

is well-defined independent of the choice of
\lambda>0
.

(vii)

Given any
x,y\in\overline{U}^{\ast}\times\overline{U}^{\ast}
, the map
\lambda\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}[0,\infty)}\mapsto d_{\lambda}^{U}(x,y)\in[0,\infty]
is right continuous.

Proof.

To see (i), for any
x,y\in U
, since the restriction

u|_{U}
is a test function in the definition of
d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)
whenever

u
is a test function in the definition of
d_{\lambda}(x,y)
,
we have
d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)\geq d_{\lambda}(x,y)
.
In general,
given any
(x,y)\in(\overline{U}\times\overline{U})\setminus(U\times U)
,
for any
r>0
sufficiently small, we have

d^{U}_{\lambda}(z,w)\geq d_{\lambda}(z,w)
whenever
z,w\in U
and
|(z,w)-(x,y)|\leq r
.
By the continuity of
d_{\lambda}
in
\Omega\times\Omega
, we have

\lim_{r\to 0}\inf\{d^{U}_{\lambda}(z,w)\ |\ \mbox{$z,w\in U$ and $|(z,w)-(x,y)|\leq r$}\}\geq d_{\lambda}(x,y),

that is,
d_{\lambda}^{U}(x,y)\geq d_{\lambda}(x,y)
. Recall that
d_{\lambda}(x,y)\geq R^{\prime}d_{CC}(x,y)
comes from Lemma 2.1.

To see (ii), given any
y\in U
with
{d_{CC}}(x,y)<{d_{CC}}(x,\partial{U})
, there is a geodesic

{\gamma}
with respect to
{d_{CC}}

connecting
x
and
y
so that
{\gamma}\subset B_{d_{CC}}(x,d_{CC}(x,\partial U))
.
For any function
u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(U)
with
\|H(\cdot,Xu)\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq\lambda
,
we know that

\|Xu\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq R_{\lambda}
.
Let
U^{\prime}\Subset U
and
x,y\in U^{\prime}
. Thanks to Proposition
2.6
, we can find a sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset C^{\infty}(U^{\prime})

such that
u_{k}\to u
everywhere as
k\to\infty

and
\|Xu_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(U^{\prime})}\leq R_{\lambda}+A_{k}(u)
with
\lim_{k\to\infty}A_{k}(u)\to 0
.
Since

u_{k}(y)-u_{k}(x)=\int_{\gamma}Xu_{k}\cdot\dot{\gamma}\leq\int_{\gamma}|Xu_{k}||\dot{\gamma}|\leq\int_{\gamma}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(R_{\lambda}+A_{k}(u))}|\dot{\gamma}|={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(R_{\lambda}+A_{k}(u))}{d_{CC}}(x,y)\mbox{ for all $k\in{\mathbb{N}}$},

we have

u(y)-u(x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\{u_{k}(y)-u_{k}(x)\}\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}[R_{\lambda}+A_{k}(u)]{d_{CC}}(x,y)=R_{\lambda}{d_{CC}}(x,y).

Taking supremum in the above inequality over all such
u
, we have
d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)\leq R_{\lambda}{d_{CC}}(x,y)
.

To see (iii), given any
x\in U
, there exists
x^{\ast}\in\partial U

such that
d_{CC}(x,x^{\ast})=d_{CC}(x,\partial U)
. By (ii) and the definition of
d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,x^{\ast})
,
we know that
d_{\lambda}^{U}(x,x^{\ast})\leq R_{\lambda}d_{CC}(x,x^{\ast})<\infty
.

To get (iv), for any
x\in\overline{U}
,
y\in U
and
z\in\partial U
, choose
x_{k},z_{k}\in U
such that

d^{U}_{\lambda}(x_{k},y)\to d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)
and
d^{U}_{\lambda}(y,z_{k})\to d^{U}_{\lambda}(y,z)
as
k\to\infty
.
Since

d^{U}_{\lambda}(x_{k},z_{k})\leq d^{U}_{\lambda}(x_{k},y)+d^{U}_{\lambda}(y,z_{k}),

letting
k\to\infty
and by the lower-semicontinuous of
d^{U}_{\lambda}
we get

d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,z)\leq d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)+d^{U}_{\lambda}(y,z)

In a similar way, we also have

d^{U}_{\lambda}(z,x)\leq d^{U}_{\lambda}(z,y)+d^{U}_{\lambda}(y,x)

Note that (v) is a direct consequence of (iv).

We show (vi). The fact that
d_{\lambda}^{U}(x,y)
is non-decreasing with respect to
\lambda
is obvious for any
x,y\in\overline{U}
. Assume
0<\lambda<\mu<\infty
. Then
d^{U}_{\mu}(x,y)<+\infty
implies
d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)<+\infty
. Conversely, if
d_{\mu}^{U}(x,y)=+\infty
, we show
d_{\lambda}^{U}(x,y)=+\infty
. This may happen if at least one of
x
and
y
lie in
\partial{U}
. Then for any
\{x_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset U
and
\{y_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset U
converging to
x
and
y
, it holds

\liminf_{k\to\infty}d_{\mu}^{U}(x_{k},y_{k})=+\infty

where we let
x_{k}\equiv x
(resp.
y_{k}\equiv y
) if
x\in U
(resp.
y\in U
). By (i) and (ii), we have for any
\lambda\leq\mu

\liminf_{k\to\infty}d_{\lambda}^{U}(x_{k},y_{k})\geq R^{\prime}_{\lambda}\liminf_{k\to\infty}d_{CC}(x_{k},y_{k})\geq\frac{R^{\prime}_{\lambda}}{R_{\mu}}\liminf_{k\to\infty}d_{\mu}^{U}(x_{k},y_{k})=+\infty

where we recall that

for all
\lambda>0
.

Finally, we show (vii). Since we only consider

,
by an approximation argument, it is enough to show the right-continuity for

. The proof is similar to the one of Lemma
4.3
. We omit the details.
The proof of Lemma
5.2
is complete.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
U\Subset\Omega
and that
V
is a subdomain of
U
.
For any
\lambda\geq 0
, one has

d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)\leq d^{V}_{\lambda}(x,y)\quad\forall x,y\in\overline{V}.

Conversely, given any
\lambda>0
and
x\in V
, there exists a neighborhood
N_{\lambda}(x)\Subset V
of
x
such that

d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)=d^{V}_{\lambda}(x,y)
and
d^{U}_{\lambda}(y,x)=d^{V}_{\lambda}(y,x)
for any
y\in N_{\lambda}(x)
.

Proof.

For any
u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(U)
with
\|H(\cdot,Xu)\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq\lambda
, we know that the restriction
u|_{V}\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(V)
with
\|H(\cdot,Xu|_{V})\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}\leq\lambda
. Hence by the definition of
d^{U}_{\lambda}
and
d^{V}_{\lambda}

d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)\leq d^{V}_{\lambda}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in V
and then for all
x,y\in\overline{V}.

Conversely, we just show
d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,\cdot)=d^{V}_{\lambda}(x,\cdot)
in some neighborhood
N(x)
. In a similar way, we can prove

d^{U}_{\lambda}(\cdot,x)=d^{V}_{\lambda}(\cdot,x)
in some neighborhood
N(x)
.

By Lemma
5.2
(i), one has
d_{\lambda}^{V}(x,y)\geq R^{\prime}_{\lambda}d_{CC}(x,y)

for any
x,y\in V
. Thus for any
r>0
,

d^{V}_{\lambda}(x,y)\leq r
implies
d_{CC}(x,y)\leq r/R^{\prime}_{\lambda}
.
Given any
x\in V
and
0<r<{d_{CC}}(x,\partial V)/R^{\prime}_{\lambda}
, we therefore have

N_{r}(x):=\{y\in V\ |\ d^{V}_{\lambda}(x,y)\leq r\}\subset B_{d_{CC}}(x,r/R^{\prime}_{\lambda})\Subset V.

Define
u_{x,r}:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

u_{x,r}(z):=\min\{d^{V}_{\lambda}(x,z),r\}\quad\forall z\in\Omega.

If
r<{d_{CC}}(x,\partial V)R^{\prime}_{\lambda}/4
, we claim that

u_{x,r}\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d_{CC}}(\Omega)
with

H(z,Xu_{x,r}(z))\leq\lambda
for almost all
z\in\Omega
.

(5.2)

Assume claim (
5.2
) holds for the moment.
By (
5.2
), we are able to take
u_{x,r}|_{U}
as a test function in
d^{U}_{\lambda}
so that

u_{x,r}(z)-u_{x,r}(w)\leq d^{U}_{\lambda}(w,z)\quad\forall(w,z)\in U\times U.

On the other hand, for any
y\in N_{r}(x)
, since

d^{V}_{\lambda}(x,y)=u_{x,r}(y)-u_{x,r}(x)
, we get
d^{V}_{\lambda}(x,y)\leq d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)

as desired.

Finally, we prove the claim (
5.2
).

Proof of the claim (
5.2
).

First, by Lemma 3.3 and Lemma 3.4,
the restriction
u_{x,r}|_{V}
of
u_{x,r}
in
V
belongs to
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(V)

with
H(z,Xu_{x,r}|_{V}(z))\leq\lambda
almost everywhere in

, and hence

u_{x,r}|_{V}(z)-u_{x,r}|_{V}(w)\leq d^{V}_{\lambda}(w,z)\quad\forall(w,z)\in V\times V.

(5.3)

Next,
we show
u\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d_{CC}}(\Omega)
. Given any
w,z\in\Omega
, we consider 3 cases separately.

Case 1.

w\in\overline{B_{d_{CC}}(x,r/R^{\prime}_{\lambda})}
and
z\in\overline{B_{d_{CC}}(x,r/R^{\prime}_{\lambda})}
.
We have

z\in\overline{B_{d_{CC}}(w,2r/R^{\prime}_{\lambda})}
. Since

d_{CC}(w,\partial V)\geq d_{CC}(x,\partial V)-d_{CC}(x,w)\geq\frac{4r}{R_{\lambda}^{\prime}}-\frac{r}{R_{\lambda}^{\prime}}=\frac{3r}{R_{\lambda}^{\prime}}>\frac{2r}{R_{\lambda}^{\prime}}\geq d_{CC}(w,z),

by Lemma

(ii),
d^{V}_{\lambda}(w,z)\leq R_{\lambda}d_{CC}(w,z)
, which combined with (

), gives

u_{x,r}(z)-u_{x,r}(w)\leq R_{\lambda}d_{CC}(w,z).

Case 2.

w,z\notin B_{d_{CC}}(x,r/R^{\prime}_{\lambda})
. Then

w,z\in\Omega\setminus B_{d_{CC}}(x,r/R^{\prime}_{\lambda})
, since
u_{x,r}
is constant
r
in
\Omega\setminus B_{d_{CC}}(x,r/R^{\prime}_{\lambda})
,
we know that

u_{x,r}(z)-u_{x,r}(w)=r-r=0\leq R_{\lambda}d_{CC}(w,z).

Case 3.

w\in\overline{B_{d_{CC}}(x,r/R^{\prime}_{\lambda})}
and
z\notin\overline{B_{d_{CC}}(x,r/R^{\prime}_{\lambda})}
. Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a curve
{\gamma}_{\epsilon}:[0,1]\to\Omega
joining

and

such that

\ell_{{d_{CC}}}({\gamma}_{\epsilon})\leq{d_{CC}}(w,z)+\epsilon

and there exists

such that
{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(t)\in\partial B_{d_{CC}}(x,r/R^{\prime}_{\lambda})
.
Thus using Case 1 and Case 2, we deduce

\displaystyle u_{x,r}(z)-u_{x,r}(w)

\displaystyle=u_{x,r}(z)-u_{x,r}({\gamma}_{\epsilon}(t))+u_{x,r}({\gamma}_{\epsilon}(t))-u_{x,r}(w)

\displaystyle\leq R_{\lambda}d_{CC}(z,{\gamma}_{\epsilon}(t))+R_{\lambda}d_{CC}({\gamma}_{\epsilon}(t),w)

\displaystyle\leq R_{\lambda}\ell_{{d_{CC}}}({\gamma}_{\epsilon}|_{[0,t]})+R_{\lambda}\ell_{{d_{CC}}}({\gamma}_{\epsilon}|_{[t,1]})

\displaystyle\leq R_{\lambda}[{d_{CC}}(w,z)+\epsilon].

Letting
\epsilon\to 0
in the above inequality, we conclude

u_{x,r}(z)-u_{x,r}(w)\leq R_{\lambda}{d_{CC}}(w,z).

Finally, by Lemma

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose that
U\Subset\Omega
and that

V=U\setminus\{x_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}
for some
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\{x_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}\subset U
.
Then for any
\lambda\geq 0
, one has

d^{V}_{\lambda}(x,y)=d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in\overline{U}
.

Proof.

Obviously
d_{\lambda}^{U}\leq d^{V}_{\lambda}
in
\overline{U}
.
Conversely, we show
d_{\lambda}^{V}\leq d^{U}_{\lambda}
in
\overline{U}
. First, by an approximation argument, it suffices to consider
x,y\in U
.
By the right continuity of
\lambda\in[0,\infty)\to d_{\lambda}^{U}(x,y)
for any
x,y\in\overline{U}^{\ast}
, up to considering
d_{\mu+\epsilon}^{U}
for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, we may assume that
\mu>0
. For any
\lambda>0
,
by the pseudo-length property of
d^{U}_{\lambda}
as in Proposition
4.1
,
it suffices to prove that for any curve
{\gamma}:[a,b]\to U
, one has

(5.4)

We consider the following 3 cases.

Case 1.

{\gamma}((a,b))\subset V
and
{\gamma}(\{a,b\})\subset V
, that is,
{\gamma}([a,b])\subset V
.
Recall from Lemma
5.3
that, for each
x\in V
, there exists a neighborhood
N(x)
such that

d^{V}_{\lambda}(x,y)=d^{U}_{\lambda}(x,y)\quad\forall y\in N(x).

Since

,
we can find

such that
{\gamma}\subset\cup_{i=0}^{m}N({\gamma}(t_{i}))

and
{\gamma}([t_{i},t_{i+1}])\subset N({\gamma}(t_{i}))
. By the triangle inequality, one has

{\gamma}((a,b))\subset V

{\gamma}(\{a,b\})\not\subset V

{\gamma}((a,b))\not\subset V
.

{\gamma}(t)=x_{i}
.

.
Then

, and
{\gamma}_{1}(t)={\gamma}(t-(t^{+}-t^{-}))

. Repeating this procedure for

\eta

x_{i}

Denote by

with

such that
{\gamma}(\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{s-1}\})\subset U\setminus V=\{x_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}

The following clarifies the relations between absolute minimizers and
local super/subminimizers.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
U\Subset\Omega
and
g\in C^{0}(\partial U)
with

.

is a minimizer for

5.6
,

, that is,
u
is both a local superminimizer and a local subminimizer for
{\bf I}(g,U)
.

Conversely, suppose that
u
is both a local superminimizer and a local subminimizer for
{\bf I}(g,U)
.
We need to show that
u
is absolute minimizer for
{\bf I}(g,U)
. It suffices
to prove that for any

V\Subset U
,
u
is a minimizer for
{\bf I}(u,V)

\|H(\cdot,Xu(\cdot))\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}\leq{\bf I}(u,V)
.

The proof consists of 3 steps.

Step 1.
Given any subdomain
V\subset U
, choose a dense subset
\{x_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of
V
.
Set
V_{j}=V\setminus\{x_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq j}
and
V_{0}=V
. Note that

\partial V_{j}=\partial V_{j-1}\cup\{x_{j}\}=\partial V_{0}\cup\{x_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq j}\quad\forall j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

For each
j\geq 0
, set

\mu_{j}=\mu(u|_{\partial V_{j}},\partial V_{j})=\inf\{\lambda\geq 0|\ u(y)-u(x)\leq d_{\lambda}^{V_{j}}(x,y)\quad\forall x,y\in\partial V_{j}\}.

Since
\overline{V}\subset\overline{U}
, we have
d_{\mu}^{U}(x,y)\leq d_{\mu}^{V}(x,y)

for all
x,y\in\overline{V}^{\ast}
, we have

u(y)-u(x)\leq d_{\mu}^{V}(x,y)\quad\forall x,y\in\overline{V}.

and hence
\mu_{0}\leq\mu
. By Lemma
5.7
(i),

{\bf I}(u,V_{0})=\mu_{0}
.
In a similar way and by induction, for all

(6.9)

Step 2.

We construct a sequence
\{u_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of functions so that, for each

,

u_{j}\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{X}(V_{j})\cap C^{0}(\overline{V})\mbox{ and $u_{j}(x)=u(x)$ for any $x\in\partial V_{j}=\partial V\cup\{x_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq j}$ }

(6.10)

and

\mbox{
$\|H(\cdot,Xu_{j}(\cdot))\|_{L^{\infty}(V_{j-1})}=\mu_{j-1}$ for any $j\in{\mathbb{N}}$}.

(6.11)

For any
j\geq 1
, since
u
is both a local superminimizer and a local subminimizer for
{\bf I}(g,U)
, by Definition
6.1
,

{\mathcal{S}}_{u|_{\partial V_{j-1}},V_{j-1}}^{-}\leq u\leq{\mathcal{S}}_{u|_{\partial V_{j-1}},V_{j-1}}^{+}\mbox{ in $V_{j-1}$}.

At

, we have

a_{j}:={\mathcal{S}}^{-}_{u|_{\partial V_{j-1}},V_{j-1}}(x_{j})\leq u(x_{j})\leq b_{j}:={\mathcal{S}}^{+}_{u|_{\partial V_{j-1}},V_{j-1}}(x_{j})

Define

6.10

5.7
gives

.

\quad U^{+}_{g}(x_{i})\geq u_{j}(x_{i})\geq U^{+}_{g}(x_{i})-\frac{1}{j}.

(6.18)

Finally, combining (

), (

Together with (

is a minimizer for
{\bf I}(g,U)
.

Prove that

is a local subminimizer for

.

(6.20)

The definition of

also gives

On the other hand, we are able to apply Lemma

Prove that

Then

We further claim that

Assume that this claim holds. Choosing

To get (

Theorem
1.6

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Let
g\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d_{CC}}(\partial U)
. It suffices to show that
\mu(g,\partial U)<\infty
,
which allows us to use Proposition
6.5
and then conclude the desired absolute minimizer
U_{g}^{+}
therein.

Taking
0<\lambda<\infty
such that
R_{\lambda}^{\prime}\geq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d_{CC}}(g,\partial U)
, we have

g(y)-g(x)\leq R_{\lambda}^{\prime}{d_{CC}}(x,y)\quad\forall x,y\in\partial U.

From Lemma
2.9
(ii), that is,

R^{\prime}_{\lambda}d_{CC}\leq d^{U}_{\lambda},

it follows that

g(y)-g(x)\leq d_{\lambda}^{U}(x,y)\quad\forall x,y\in\partial U.

and hence that

\mu(g,\partial U)=\inf\{\mu\geq 0\ |g(y)-g(x)\leq d_{\mu}(x,y)\}\leq\lambda<\infty.

The proof is complete.

7
Further discussion

Note that Rademacher type Theorem
1.3
is a cornerstone when showing the existence of absolute minimizers. Indeed, Champion and Pascale
[
15
]
and Guo-Xiang-Yang
[
27
]
established partial results similar to Theorem
1.3
for a special class of Hamiltonians considered in this paper to show the existence of absolute minimizers. However, their method seems to be invalid for more general Hamiltonians considered in this paper. We briefly explain the reason below.

Remark 7.1
.

Champion and Pascale
[
15
]
showed the McShane extension is a minimizer for
H
when
H
is lower semi-continuous on
U\times{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
. In fact, they defined another intrinsic distance induced by
H(x,p)
.
For every
\lambda\geq 0
,

L_{\lambda}(x,q):=\sup_{\{p\in H_{\lambda}(x)\}}p\cdot q,\quad\forall\ x\in\overline{U}\ \mbox{and }\ q\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{m}}.

where
H_{\lambda}(x)
is the sub-level set at
x
, namely,
H_{\lambda}(x)=\{p\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\mid H(x,p)\leq\lambda\}
.

For
0\leq a<b\leq+\infty
, let
{\gamma}:[a,\,b]\to\overline{U}
be a Lipschitz curve, that is, there exists a constant
C>0
such that
|{\gamma}(s)-{\gamma}(t)|\leq C|s-t|
whenever
s,t\in[a,b]
.
The
L_{\lambda}
-length of
{\gamma}
is defined by

\ell_{\lambda}({\gamma}):=\int_{a}^{b}L_{\lambda}\left({\gamma}(\theta),{\gamma}^{\prime}(\theta)\right)\,d\theta,

which is nonnegative, since
L_{\lambda}(x,q)\geq 0
for any
x\in\overline{U}
and
q\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.
For a pair of points
x,\,y\in\overline{U}
, the
\overline{d}_{\lambda}
-distance from
x
to
y
is defined by

\overline{d}_{\lambda}(x,y):=\inf\Big\{\ell_{\lambda}({\gamma})\ |\ \gamma\in\mathcal{C}(a,b,x,y,\overline{U})\Big\}.

Then, they prove two intrinsic pseudo-distance are equal, that is

d_{\lambda}(x,y)=\overline{d}_{\lambda}(x,y)\quad\text{for any}\ \lambda>0\ \text{and for any}\ x,y\in\overline{U}.

(7.1)

Thanks to the definition of
\overline{d}_{\lambda}
, they can justify (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) in Rademacher type Theorem
1.3
.

However, when asserting (
7.1
), we will meet obstacles in generalizing
[
15
]
Proposition A.2 since we are faced with measurable
H
.

On the other hand, Guo-Xiang-Yang
[
27
]
provided another method to identify a weak version of (
7.1
) for measurable Finsler metrics
H
, that is

\lim_{y\to x}\frac{d_{\lambda}(x,y)}{\widetilde{d}_{\lambda}(x,y)}=1\quad\text{for any}\ \lambda>0\ \text{and for any}\ x,y\in\overline{U}.

(7.2)

Here
\widetilde{d}_{\lambda}
induced by measurable Finsler metrics
H
is defined in the following way.

\widetilde{d}_{\lambda}(x,y):=\sup_{N}\inf\Big\{\ell_{\lambda}({\gamma})\ |\ \gamma\in\Gamma_{N}(a,b,x,y,\overline{U})\Big\}

(7.3)

where the supremum is taken over all subsets
N
of
\overline{U}
such that
|N|=0
and
\Gamma_{N}(a,b,x,y,U)

denotes the set of all Lipschitz continuous curves
{\gamma}
in
\overline{U}
with end points
x
and
y
such that

\mathcal{H}^{1}(N\cap{\gamma})=0
with
\mathcal{H}^{1}
being the one dimensional Hausdorff measure.

In fact, (
7.2
), combined with the method in
[
15
]
will be sufficient for validating (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) in Rademacher type Theorem
1.3
. Unfortunately, since we are coping with Hörmander vector field, a barrier arises when modifying their proofs. Indeed, their method uses a result by
[
17
]
that every
x
-measurable Hamiltonian
H
can be approximated by a sequence of smooth Hamiltonians
\{H_{n}\}_{n}
such that two intrinsic distances
\widetilde{d}_{\lambda}^{H_{n}}
and
d_{\lambda}^{H_{n}}
induced by
H_{n}
by means of (
7.3
) and (
1.4
) satisfy
\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{\lambda}^{H_{n}}=d_{\lambda}
and
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\widetilde{d}_{\lambda}^{H_{n}}\leq\widetilde{d}_{\lambda}
uniformly on
U\times U
respectively. The process of the proof of
[
17
]
is based on a
C^{1}
Lusin approximation property for curves. Namely, given a Lipschitz curve
{\gamma}:\ [0,1]\to U
joining
x
and
y
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
C^{1}
curve
\widetilde{{\gamma}}:\ [0,1]\to U
with the same endpoints such that

\mathcal{L}^{1}(\{t\in[0,1]\ |\ \widetilde{{\gamma}}(t)\neq{\gamma}(t)\quad\text{or}\quad\widetilde{{\gamma}}^{\prime}(t)\neq{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)\})<\epsilon

where
\mathcal{L}^{1}
denotes the one dimensional Lebesgue measure. Besides,

\|\widetilde{{\gamma}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c\|{\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}},

for some constant
c
depending only on
n
.
Although this version of
C^{1}
Lusin approximation property holds for horizontal curves in Heisenberg groups (
[
44
]
) and step 2 Carnot groups (
[
40
]
),
it fails for some horizontal curve in Engel group (
[
44
]
).

In summary, it is difficult to generalize the properties of the pseudo metric
\widetilde{d}_{\lambda}
not only from Euclidean space to the case of Hörmander vector fields but also from lower-semicontinuous
H(x,p)
to measurable
H(x,p)
. Hence we would like to pose the following open problem.

Problem 7.2
.

Under the assumptions (H0)-(H3), does (
7.2
) holds?

Appendix
Rademacher’s theorem in Euclidean domains—revisit

In this appendix, we state some consequence of Rademacher’s theorem (Theorem
1.1
) for Sobolev and Lipschitz classes, see Lemma
A.3
and Lemma
A.6
below.
They were well-known in the literature and also partially motivated our Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
1.5
.
For reader’s convenience, we give the details.

Recall that
\Omega\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is always a domain.
The homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
is the collection of all functions
u\in L^{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
with its distributional derivative
\nabla u=(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}})_{1\leq i\leq n}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. We equip
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)

with the semi-norm

\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Write
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
as the collection of all functions
u
in
\Omega
so that
u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(V)
whenever
V\Subset\Omega
. Here and below,
V\Subset\Omega
means that

V
is bounded domain with
\overline{V}\subset\Omega
.
On the other hand, denote by
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(\Omega)

the collection of all Lipschitz functions
u
in
\Omega
,
that is, all functions
u
satisfying (
1.1
). We equip
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(\Omega)
with the semi-norm

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(u,\Omega)=\sup_{x,y\in\Omega,x\neq y}\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{|x-y|}=\inf\{\mbox{$\lambda\geq 0$ satisfiying \eqref{lip}}\}.

Denote by
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
the collection of all functions
u
in
\Omega
so that
u\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(V)
for any
V\Subset\Omega
.
Moreover,
denote by
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)
the collection of all functions
u
in
\Omega

with

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(u,\Omega):=\sup_{x\in\Omega}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}u(x)<\infty.

(A.1)

Obviously,
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(\Omega)\subset{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)
with the seminorm bound

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(u,\Omega)\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(u,\Omega).

Next, we have the following relation.

Lemma A.1
.

We have
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)\subset{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
.
For any convex subdomain
V\subset\Omega
and
u\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)
, we have

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq\|u\|_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(V)}|x-y|\quad\forall x,y\in V.

(A.2)

Proof.

Let
u\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)
. To see
u\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
, it suffices to
prove
u\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(B)
for any ball
B\Subset\Omega
. Given any
x,y\in B
, denote by
{\gamma}(t)=x+t(y-x)
. Since
A_{x,y}=\sup_{t\in[0,1]}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}u({\gamma}(t))<\infty
,
for each
t\in[0,1]
we can find
r_{t}>0
such that

|u({\gamma}(s))-u({\gamma}(t))|\leq A_{x,y}|{\gamma}(s)-{\gamma}(t)|=A_{x,y}|s-t||x-y|
whenever
|s-t|\leq r_{t}
and
s\in[0,1]
.
Since
[0,1]\subset\cup_{t\in[0,1]}(t-r_{t},t+r_{t})
we can find an increasing sequence

t_{i}\in[0,1]
with
t_{0}=0
and
t_{N}=1
such that
[0,1]\subset\cup_{i=1}^{N}(t_{i}-\frac{1}{2}r_{t_{i}},t_{i}+\frac{1}{2}r_{t_{i}})
. Write
x_{i}={\gamma}(t_{i})
for
i=0,\cdots,N
. We have

|u(x)-u(y)|=|\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}[u(x_{i})-u(x_{i+1})]|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}|u(x_{i})-u(x_{i+1})|\leq A_{x,y}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}|x_{i}-x_{i+1}|=A_{x,y}|x-y|.

Noticing that
A_{x,y}\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(u,B)\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(u,\Omega)<\infty
for all
x,y\in B
, we deduce that
u\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(B)
and hence
u\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
.

If
V\Subset\Omega
is convex, then for any
x,y\in\Omega
, the line-segment joining
x
and
y
lies in
V
. Hence similar to the above discussion, we have

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq A_{x,y}|x-y|,\ \forall x,y\in V

and

A_{x,y}\leq\|u\|_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(V)}<\infty
for all
x,y\in V
. Therefore, (
A.2
) holds and the proof is complete.
∎

On the other hand, functions
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
admit continuous representatives.

Lemma A.2
.

(i) Each
u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)

admits a unique continuous representative
\widetilde{u}
, that is,

\widetilde{u}\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
with
\widetilde{u}=u
almost everywhere in
\Omega
. Moreover,
\widetilde{u}\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
, and
for any convex subdomain
V\subset\Omega
, we have

|\widetilde{u}(x)-\widetilde{u}(y)|\leq\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}|x-y|\quad\forall x,y\in V.

(ii) Each
u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)

admits a unique continuous representative
\widetilde{u}
, that is,
\widetilde{u}\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
with
\widetilde{u}=u
almost everywhere in
\Omega
. Moreover,

\widetilde{u}\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)
with
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\widetilde{u},\Omega)\leq\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}
.

Proof.

Since (ii) can be shown in a similar way as (i), we only prove (i).
Given any convex domain
V\Subset\Omega
, for any pair
x,y
of Lebesgue points of
u
, we have

u(y)-u(x)=\lim_{\delta\to 0}[u\ast\eta_{\delta}(y)-u\ast\eta_{\delta}(x)]=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\nabla(u\ast\eta_{\delta})(x+t_{\delta}(y-x))\cdot(y-x)

where
\eta_{\delta}
is the standard mollifier in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with its support
{\rm spt}\eta_{\delta}\subset B(0,\delta)
and
t_{\delta}\in[0,1]
.
Also, since for any
z\in V
,

|\nabla(u\ast\eta_{\delta})(z)|=|(\nabla u)\ast\eta_{\delta})(z)|\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(B(z,\delta))},

we deduce that for any pair
x,y
of Lebesgue points of
u
,

|u(y)-u(x)|\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}|y-x|.

If
z\in V
is not a Lebesgue point of
u
, let
\{z_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset V
be a sequence of Lebesgue points of
u
converging to
z
. We have

\lim_{i\to\infty}|u(z_{i})-u(z_{i+1})|\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}|z_{i}-z_{i+1}|=0,

which implies
\{u(z_{i})\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset V
is a Cauchy sequence.
Since
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}<\infty
, we know
\{u(z_{i})\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
has a limit in
{\mathbb{R}}
independent of the choice of the sequence
\{u(z_{i})\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
. Define

\widetilde{u}(z):=u(z)
if
z\in V
is a Lebesgue point of
u
and
\widetilde{u}(z)=\lim_{i\to\infty}u(z_{i})
if
z\in V
is not a Lebesgue point of
u
where
\{z_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset V
is a sequence of Lebesgue points of
u
converging to
z
.
We know
\widetilde{u}:V\to{\mathbb{R}}
is well-defined and moreover,

|\widetilde{u}(y)-\widetilde{u}(x)|\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}|y-x|\quad\forall x,y\in V.

Thus
\widetilde{u}\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(V)
with
\sup_{x\in V}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}\widetilde{u}(x)\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(\widetilde{u},V)\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}
. In particular,
\widetilde{u}
is continuous, which shows (i).

∎

Thanks to lemma
A.2
, below
for any function
u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
or
u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
,
up to considering its continuous representative
\widetilde{u}
, we may assume that
u
is continuous.
Under this assumption, Lemma
A.2
further gives
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)\subset{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
,
and
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\subset{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)
with a norm bound
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(u,\Omega)\leq\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}
.
Rademacher’s theorem (Theorem
1.1
) tells that their converse are also true. Indeed, we have the following.

Lemma A.3
.

(i)
We have

\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
and

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(\Omega)\subset\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)
with

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(u,\Omega)=\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(u,\Omega)
.

(ii) If
\Omega
is convex, then
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(\Omega)=\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)
with

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(u,\Omega)=\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(u,\Omega)
.

Proof.

(i)
If
u\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
, applying Rademacher’s theorem (Theorem
1.1
) to all subdomains
V\Subset\Omega
, one has
u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)

and
|\nabla u(x)|={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}u(x)
for almost all
x\in\Omega

(whenever
u
is differentiable at
x
). Hence
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)\subset W^{1,\infty}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
. Combining Lemma
A.2
(i), we know

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)=W^{1,\infty}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
.

If
u\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)
, that is,
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(u,\Omega)=\sup_{x\in\Omega}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}u(x)<\infty
.
We have
u\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}}(\Omega)
and hence
u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)

and
|\nabla u(x)|={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}u(x)\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(u,\Omega)<\infty
for almost all
x\in\Omega
.
Thus
u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.

By definition, it is obvious that
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(\Omega)\subset{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)
. Hence Lemma
A.3
(i) holds.

(ii) By Lemma
A.3
(i), we only need to show
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)\subset{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(\Omega)
. Applying Lemma
A.1
with
V=\Omega
therein, (
A.2
) becomes

\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|}\leq\|u\|_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)}\quad\forall x,y\in\Omega.

Taking supremum among all
x,y\in\Omega
in the left hand side of the above inequality, we arrive at

\|u\|_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(\Omega)}\leq\|u\|_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)},

which gives the desired result.
∎

Remark A.4
.

(i) Lemma
A.1
and Lemma
A.3
fail if we relax
\sup_{x\in\Omega}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}u(x)
in the definition (
A.1
) to be
\|{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,esssup\,}_{x\in\Omega}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}u(x)
.
This is witted by the standard Cantor function
w
in
[0,1]
.
Denote by
E
the standard Cantor set.
It is well-known that
w
is continuous but not absolute continuous in
[0,1]
. Since Lipschitz functions are always absolutely continuous, we know that

w
is neither Lipschitz nor locally Lipschitz in
\Omega=(0,1)
,
and hence
w\notin{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
.
On the other hand, observe that
\Omega\setminus E
consists of a sequence of open intervals which mutually disjoint, and
w
is a constant in each such intervals and hence

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}w(x)=0
therein. So we know that
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}w(x)=0

in
\Omega\setminus E
.
Since
|E|=0
, we have
\|{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=0
.

(ii) In general, if
\Omega
is not convex, one cannot expect that
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\subset{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(\Omega)
with a norm bound. Indeed, consider the planar domain

U:=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\ |\ |x|<1\}\setminus[0,1)\times\{0\}.

(A.3)

Indeed,
in the polar coordinate
(r,\theta)
, let
w:U\to{\mathbb{R}}
be

w(r,\theta):=r\theta\mbox{ for all $0<r<1$ and $0<\theta<2\pi$}.

One can show that
w\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(U)
so that
w(x_{1},x_{2})<\pi/3
when
1/2\leq x_{1}<1
and
0<x_{2}<1/10
, and
w(x_{1},x_{2})>5\pi/6

when
1/2\leq x_{1}<1
and
-1/10<x_{2}<0
. One has
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(w,\Omega)=\infty
and hence
w\notin{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(\Omega)
.

The example in Remark
A.4
(ii) also indicates that
the Euclidean distance does not match the geometry of domains and hence

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(\Omega)
defined via Euclidean distance is not the prefect one to understand
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.

Instead of Euclidean distance, for any domain
\Omega
, we consider the intrinsic distance

d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)=\inf\{\ell({\gamma})\ |\ \mbox{${\gamma}:[0,1]\to\Omega$ is absolute coninuous curve joining $x,y$}\},

(A.4)

where
\ell({\gamma}):=\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}(t)|\,dt
is the Euclidean length.
We have the dual formula.

Lemma A.5
.

(i) For any
x,y\in\Omega
,

d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)=\sup\{u(y)-u(x)\ |\ u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega),\ \|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1\}.

(A.5)

(ii) If
x,y\in\Omega
with
|x-y|\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(x,\partial\Omega)
, then
d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)=|x-y|
.

(iii) If
\Omega
is convex, then
d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)=|x-y|
for all
x,y\in\Omega
.

Proof.

(i) Set

\widetilde{d}^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)=\sup\{u(y)-u(x)\ |\ u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega),\ \|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1\}.

(A.6)

Notice that
d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,\cdot)\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Lip}^{\ast}(\Omega)=\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
(Lemma
A.3
(i)) and
\|\nabla d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1
for all
x\in\Omega
. Hence letting
d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,\cdot)
be the test function in (
A.6
), we see

d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)\leq\widetilde{d}^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)\ \forall x,y\in\Omega.

To see the contrary, fix
x,y\in\Omega
. Let
\{u_{i}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of test functions in (
A.6
) such that

\widetilde{d}^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)=\lim_{i\to\infty}(u_{i}(y)-u_{i}(x)).

Let
{\gamma}:[0,1]\to\Omega
be an arbitrary absolute continuous curve joining
x
and
y
. Then there exists a domain
U\Subset\Omega
with
{\gamma}\subset U
. Let
\{\eta_{\delta}\}_{\delta>0}
be the standard mollifiers in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. For each
i\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we know
u_{i}\ast\eta_{\delta}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\|\nabla(u_{i}\ast\eta_{\delta})\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq\|\nabla u_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq 1
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{d}^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}[u_{i}(y)-u_{i}(x)]

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{\delta\to 0}[u_{i}\ast\eta_{\delta}(y)-u_{i}\ast\eta_{\delta}(x)]

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{0}^{1}\nabla(u_{i}\ast\eta_{\delta})({\gamma}(t))\cdot\dot{\gamma}(t)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{i\to\infty}\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla(u_{i}\ast\eta_{\delta})({\gamma}(t))||\dot{\gamma}(t)|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}(t)|\,dt

\displaystyle=\ell({\gamma}).

Finally, taking infimum among all absolute continuous curves joining
x
and
y
in the above inequality, we conclude

\widetilde{d}^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)\leq d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)\ \forall x,y\in\Omega.

(ii) If
|x-y|\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(x,\partial\Omega)
, then the line-segment
{\gamma}
joining
x
and
y
is contained in
\Omega
. Letting
{\gamma}
be the absolute continuous curve in (
A.4
),

|x-y|\leq d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)\leq\ell({\gamma})=|x-y|\ \forall x,y\in\Omega.

(iii) If
\Omega
is convex, for all
x,y\in\Omega
, since the line-segment joining them is contained in
\Omega
, similarly to (ii), we have
d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)=|x-y|
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Note that if
\Omega
is not convex, one cannot expect
d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)=|x-y|
for all
x,y\in\Omega
. Indeed, if
\Omega
is given by the domain
U
as in (
A.3
),
for points
(1/2,\epsilon)
and
(1/2,-\epsilon)
with
\epsilon\in(0,1/10)
, the Euclidean distance between them is
2\epsilon
.
However, note that any curve
{\gamma}:[0,1]\to\Omega
joining them must have intersection with
(-1,0)\times\{0\}
, which is call
z
. One then deduce
that

\ell({\gamma})\geq|(1/2,\epsilon)-z|+|(-1/2,\epsilon)-z|\geq 1/2+1/2=1+2\epsilon.

Thus the intrinsic distance between
(1/2,\epsilon)
and
(1/2,-\epsilon)
is always larger than or
equals to
1+2\epsilon
.

With in Lemma
A.5
, we show that the Lipschitz spaces defined via the intrinsic distance perfectly match
with the Sobolev space
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
, see Lemma
A.6
below.
Denote by
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(\Omega)
the collection of all Lipschitz functions
u
in
\Omega
with respect to
d^{\Omega}_{E}
, that is,

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(u,\Omega):=\sup\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)}<\infty.

We also denote by
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(\Omega)
the collection of all functions
u
in
\Omega

with

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(u,\Omega):=\sup_{x\in\Omega}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}u(x)<\infty.

Lemma A.6
.

We have
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(\Omega)=\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)
and

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(u,\Omega)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(u,\Omega)=\sup_{x\in\Omega}{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}u(x).

Proof.

Recall that Lemma
A.6
gives
d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)=|x-y|
whenever
|x-y|\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(x,\partial\Omega)
.
One then has
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(\Omega)\subset{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
,
and moreover,

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}u(x)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}u(x)
for all
x\in\Omega
, which gives
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(\Omega)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)
.

Next, we show
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\subset{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(\Omega)
and
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(u,\Omega)\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
. Let
u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
. Then
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=:\lambda<\infty.
If
\lambda>0
, then
\lambda^{-1}u\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
and
\|\nabla(\lambda^{-1}u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1
. Hence
\lambda^{-1}u
could be the test function in (
A.5
), which implies

\lambda^{-1}u(y)-\lambda^{-1}u(x)\leq d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)\ \forall x,y\in\Omega,

or equivalently,

\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}\leq d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)\ \forall x,y\in\Omega.

Therefore,
u\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(\Omega)
and
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(u,\Omega)\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
. If
\lambda=0
, then similar as the above discussion, we have for any
\lambda^{\prime}>0

\frac{|u(y)-u(x)|}{\lambda^{\prime}}\leq d^{\Omega}_{E}(x,y)\ \forall x,y\in\Omega.

Therefore,
u\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(\Omega)
and
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(u,\Omega)\leq\lambda^{\prime}
for any
\lambda^{\prime}>0
. Hence
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(u,\Omega)=0=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
.

Moreover, we show
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(\Omega)\subset{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)
and
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(u,\Omega)\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(u,\Omega)
. Let
u\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(\Omega)
. Then
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(u,\Omega)<\infty
. Since
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(u,\Omega)\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(u,\Omega)
and
{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(u,\Omega)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(u,\Omega)
, we arrive at

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(u,\Omega)\leq{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}_{d^{\Omega}_{E}}(u,\Omega)<\infty.

Therefore,
u\in{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)
.

Finally, recalling that
\dot{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}^{\ast}(\Omega)
and
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}={\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(u,\Omega)
in Lemma
A.3
, we finish the proof.
∎

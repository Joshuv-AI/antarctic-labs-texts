1.
Introduction.

Motivated by results of Juhász and van Mill in
[
13
]
, the

wt(X)
of a topological space

was introduced in
[
6
]
with the property
wt(X)\leq t(X)
(see Definition
). This cardinal invariant was investigated further by Bella and the author in
[
4
]
, where the following theorem was shown. Recall a space
X
is

if for all

there exists a homeomorphism
h:X\to X
such that
h(x)=y
.
X
is
power homogeneous
if there exists a cardinal

is homogeneous.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let

be a homogeneous compactum. Then,

(a)

w(X)\leq 2^{wt(X)}
, and

(b)

|X|\leq 2^{wt(X)\pi\chi(X)}
.

As
\pi\chi(X)\leq t(X)
for a compactum
X
(Šapirovskiĭ
[
18
]

[
10

that the cardinality of a homogeneous compactum is at most

.

It was asked in Question 3.13 in
[
4
]
if

is a bound for the cardinality of a
power
homogeneous compactum
X
. While this question is still open, we attain a partial answer by introducing a cardinal invariant related to
wt(X)
, the
almost tightness

of a space
X
(see Definition
). The almost tightness, satisfying
wt(X)\leq at(X)\leq t(X)
, resolves technical issues when considering power homogeneous spaces that are not resolved with
wt(X)
(see section 2). We show in Theorem
that

for a power homogeneous compactum

. This improves a result of Arhangel
′

[
2
]
that
|X|\leq 2^{t(X)}
for such spaces. Theorem
is related to a result of Juhász and van Mill
[
13
]
and also to a result in
[
6
]
. See section 3 for the details of these relationships.

In section 4 we generalize Theorem
into the Hausdorff and regular settings. The first step in this extension process is Theorem

X
is Hausdorff and
wt(X)pct(X)\leq\kappa
, then there exists a nonempty compact set
G\subseteq X
and
H\subseteq X
such that
\chi(G,X)\leq\kappa
,
G\subseteq\overline{H}
,
|H|\leq 2^{\kappa}
, and

is
\mathcal{C}
-saturated. (See
for the definition of

and
for the definition of
\mathcal{C}
-saturated). This represents an extension of Theorem 3.3 in
[
4
]
into the Hausdorff setting. That theorem established the existence of

.

We use the cardinal invariant
pwL_{c}(X)
, first defined in
[

(see Definition

and

that if

We also extend Theorem
(a) into the regular setting by demonstrating that if

By

2.
Tools for homogeneous product spaces.

In this section we modify tools given in
[
2
]
and in
[
17
]
for product spaces
X
and homeomorphisms
h:X\to X
. Variations of these tools will be used to establish a result for power homogeneous spaces, Corollary

For this section we fix the following notation. Let
X=\prod\{X_{i}:i\in I\}
be endowed with the product topology, where
X_{i}
is a space for all
i\in I
. Let
H=\prod\{H_{i}:i\in I\}
where
H_{i}\subseteq X_{i}
, and for all
i\in I
, fix
s_{i}\in H_{i}
. If
A\subseteq I
, let

H(A)=\{x\in H:x_{i}=s_{i}\textup{ except for finitely many }i\in A\}.

When

, let
K_{A}=\pi_{A}[K]
, where
\pi_{A}:X\to\prod\{X_{i}:i\in A\}
is the projection map. If
x\in X
and
\pi_{j}:X\to X_{j}
is the projection for some

.

The following is Corollary 2.9 in
[
2
]

Theorem 2.1

(Corollary 2.9
[
2
]
)

Suppose that
X
is homogeneous and for all

there exists a non-empty closed
G_{\kappa}
-set

and a set

such that

\mathcal{G}
of

consisting of closed
G_{\kappa}
-sets such that for all
G\in\mathcal{G}
there exists

such that

.

A set

is a

-

of a space

in

. Note that a

The following are two results from

. The first gives straightforward properties of the set

.

Lemma 2.2

(Lemma 2.7
[
2
]
)
.

If

|H(A)|\leq\kappa

. If

Theorem 2.3

(Theorem 2.8
[
2
]
)
.

Suppose there exists a cardinal
\kappa

|H_{i}|\leq\kappa
for all

We obtain the following new result, which is an adjustment to Corollary 2.9 in

Corollary 2.4
.

Suppose

is homogeneous and Hausdorff and suppose for all

there exist

, a

-set

-set

Let

and let

be arbitrary. Pick

such that
w_{j}=z
. As

such that

, where

is the point of

whose

coordinate is

. By Theorem
and Lemma

such that
|A|\leq\kappa
and

(1)

,

(2)

, and

(3)

for all

and all

then

.

Now, for all

consists of the single point

. Let

and note

. Since

-set in

-set in

(2.1)

\displaystyle\pi_{A}^{-1}[G_{A}]

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in A}G_{i}\times\prod_{i\notin A}X_{i}=\prod_{i\in A}(\bigcap_{\alpha<\kappa}U(i,\alpha))\times\prod_{i\notin A}X_{i}

is the intersection of

. Similarly, it can be shown that

-set in

-set in

Let

-set of

is a

-set of

3.
The cardinality of a power homogeneous compactum.

We begin this section by defining the weak tightness

of a space

and related notions. Given a cardinal

, a space

, and

, the
\kappa

of

cl_{\kappa}A=\bigcup_{B\in[A]^{\leq\kappa}}\overline{B}
. The definition of

Definition 3.1

(
[

]
)
.

Let

be a space. The

of

is defined as the least infinite cardinal

of

and for all

if

.

Definition 3.2

Let

be a space. The

at(X)
of

is defined as the least infinite cardinal
\kappa
for which there is a cover
\mathcal{C}
of
X
such that
|\mathcal{C}|\leq\kappa
and for all
C\in\mathcal{C}
,
t(C)\leq\kappa
and

. We say that

is
almost countably tight
if

.

It is clear that

. See Example 2.3 in
[

]
for a consistent example of a compact space

.

We will see that while

is used in cardinal inequalities on homogeneous spaces (Theorems

,

is more suited for cardinal inequalities on

It is clear that the union of

Let

.

Let

\mathcal{C}
be a cover witnessing that

-saturated.

Fix

. For all

\mathcal{V}=\{\mathcal{V}_{\beta}:\beta<\kappa\}\in\left[\left[\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha})\right]^{\leq\kappa}\right]^{\leq\kappa}
such that
X\backslash\bigcup\{\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{V}_{\beta}}:\beta<\kappa\}\neq\varnothing
, let
x_{\mathcal{V}}\in X\backslash\bigcup\{\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{V}_{\beta}}:\beta<\kappa\}
. Define

\displaystyle A_{\alpha+1}

\displaystyle=A_{\alpha}\cup\{S(x_{\mathcal{V}}):\mathcal{V}=\{\mathcal{V}_{\beta}:\beta<\kappa\}\in\left[\left[\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha})\right]^{\leq\kappa}\right]^{\leq\kappa}\textup{ and }

\displaystyle X\backslash\bigcup\{\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{V}_{\beta}}:\beta<\kappa\}\neq\varnothing\}

As
|S(x_{\mathcal{V}})|\leq 2^{\kappa}
and
\left|\left[\left[\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha})\right]^{\leq\kappa}\right]^{\leq\kappa}\right|\leq 2^{\kappa}
, we have
|A_{\alpha+1}|\leq 2^{\kappa}
. As each
S(x_{\mathcal{V}})
is
\mathcal{C}
-saturated, it follows that
A_{\alpha+1}
is
\mathcal{C}
-saturated. Apply Lemma
again to
\overline{A_{\alpha+1}}
to obtain
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha+1}
.

Let
A=\bigcup\{A_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa^{+}\}
. By Lemma
, it follows that
\overline{A}=\bigcup\{\overline{A_{\alpha}}:\alpha<\kappa^{+}\}
. Let
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\bigcup\{\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa^{+}\}
and note that
\overline{A}\subseteq\bigcup\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
. We show
X=\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}
. Suppose by way of contradiction that
X\backslash\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}\neq\varnothing
and let
x\in X\backslash\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}
. Now there exists
G\in\mathcal{G}
such that
x\in G
, as
\mathcal{G}
is a cover of
X
. Since
G
is compact and
X
is Hausdorff, it follows that
G
is regular. Note that
x\in G\backslash\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}
and that
G\backslash\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}
is an open set in
G
. By regularity of
G
there exists a set
H\subseteq G
such that
H
is a closed
G_{\delta}^{c}
-set in
G
and
x\in H\subseteq G\backslash\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}
. Observe that
H
is compact as it is a closed subset of the compact set
G
. Thus
\omega=\psi(H,G)=\chi(H,G)
. Then, by Lemma
,
\chi(H,X)\leq\chi(H,G)\cdot\chi(G,X)\leq\omega\cdot\kappa=\kappa
because
H
and
G
are compact.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a neighborhood base at
H
in
X
such that
|\mathcal{N}|\leq\kappa
. Now, for all
G\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
we have
H\cap G=\varnothing
. Since
H
and
G
are compact and
X
is Hausdorff, there exists
N_{G}\in\mathcal{N}
and
U(G)\in\mathcal{U}(G)
such that
N_{G}\cap U(G)=\varnothing
. Let
\mathcal{U}=\{U(G):G\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\}
and note
\mathcal{U}
is a cover of
\overline{A}
. For all
N\in\mathcal{N}
let
\mathcal{U}_{N}=\{U\in\mathcal{U}:U\cap N=\varnothing\}
and observe that
x\in H\subseteq X\backslash\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{U}_{N}}
. The family
\{\mathcal{U}_{N}:N\in\mathcal{N}\}
is a decomposition of
\mathcal{U}
. Since
pwL_{c}(X)\leq\kappa
, for all
N\in\mathcal{N}
there exists
\mathcal{V}_{N}\in[\mathcal{U}_{N}]^{\leq\kappa}
such that
\overline{A}\subseteq\bigcup\{\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{V}_{N}}:N\in\mathcal{N}\}
. Then
x\in H\subseteq X\backslash\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{V}_{N}}
for each
N\in\mathcal{N}
and
x\in X\backslash\bigcup\{\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{V}_{N}}:N\in\mathcal{N}\}\neq\varnothing
. As
\{\mathcal{V}_{N}:N\in\mathcal{N}\}
fits condition (3) above and
\{\mathcal{V}_{N}:N\in\mathcal{N}\}\in\left[\left[\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{G}_{\alpha})\right]^{\leq\kappa}\right]^{\leq\kappa}
for some
\alpha<\kappa^{+}
, we have

\varnothing\neq A_{\alpha+1}\backslash\bigcup\{\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{V}_{N}}:N\in\mathcal{N}\}\subseteq A\subseteq\overline{A}\subseteq\bigcup\{\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{V}_{N}}:N\in\mathcal{N}\},

a contradiction. Thus
X=\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}
which completes the proof.
∎

If
X
is homogeneous the above theorem is used to obtain a bound for the cardinality of

.

Theorem 4.4
.

If
X
is homogeneous and Hausdorff then
|X|\leq 2^{pwL_{c}(X)wt(X)\pi\chi(X)pct(X)}
.

Proof.

Let
\kappa=pwL_{c}(X)wt(X)\pi\chi(X)pct(X)
, and let
\mathcal{C}
be a cover of
X
witnessing that
wt(X)\leq\kappa
. As
wt(X)pct(X)\leq\kappa
, by Theorem
there exists a nonempty compact set
G\subseteq X
and
H\subseteq X
such that
|H|\leq 2^{\kappa}
,
G\subseteq\overline{H}
,
\chi(G,X)\leq\kappa
, and
H
is
\mathcal{C}
-saturated.

Fix
p\in X
. For all
x\in X
there exists a homeomorphism
h_{x}:X\to X
such that
h_{x}(p)=X
. Then
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\{h_{x}[G]:x\in X\}
is a cover of
X
by compact sets of character at most
\kappa
. By Theorem
above there exists a subfamily
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
such that
|\mathcal{G}|\leq 2^{\kappa}
and
X=\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{G}}
. Also, for all
G\in\mathcal{G}
there exists
H_{G}\in[X]^{\leq 2^{\kappa}}
such that
G\subseteq\overline{H_{G}}
. It follows that
D=\bigcup_{G\in\mathcal{G}}H_{G}
is dense in
X
. Since
|D|\leq 2^{\kappa}\cdot 2^{\kappa}=2^{\kappa}
, we have
d(X)\leq 2^{\kappa}
. Now apply the fact that
|X|\leq d(X)^{\pi\chi(X)}
for any Hausdorff homogeneous space (Lemma
).
∎

Despite the fact that four cardinal invariants are used in the bound in Theorem
, each of these invariants should be regarded as “small” in a rough sense. In addition, as

improves Corollary 3.7 in

. This latter result states that if

. Theorem
is also an extension of Theorem
(b).

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
X
be a homogeneous regular Hausdorff space. Then
w(X)\leq 2^{L(X)wt(X)pct(X)}
.

Proof.

Let
\kappa=L(X)wt(X)pct(X)
, and let
\mathcal{C}
be a cover of
X
witnessing that
wt(X)\leq\kappa
. By Theorem
, there exists a nonempty compact set
G\subseteq X
and
H\subseteq X
such that
H
is
\mathcal{C}
-saturated,
|H|\leq 2^{\kappa}
,
G\subseteq\overline{H}
, and
\chi(G,X)\leq\kappa
. Fix
p\in G
. As
X
is homogeneous, for all
x\in X
there exists a homeomorphism
h_{x}:X\to X
such that
h_{x}(p)=x
. Then
\mathcal{F}=\{h_{x}[G]:x\in X\}
is a cover of
X
by compact
G^{c}_{\kappa}
-sets and, for all
x\in X
,
h_{x}[G]\subseteq h_{x}[\overline{H}]
. By Theorem
, there exists
A\subseteq X
such that
|A|\leq 2^{\kappa}
and
X=\bigcup\{h_{x}[G]:x\in A\}=\bigcup\{h_{x}[\overline{H}]:x\in A\}
.

As
H
is
\mathcal{C}
-saturated and
X
is regular, by Lemma
it follows that
nw(\overline{H})\leq|H|^{\kappa}\leq(2^{\kappa})^{\kappa}=2^{\kappa}
. Let
\mathcal{N}
be a network for
\overline{H}
such that
|\mathcal{N}|\leq 2^{\kappa}
. Let
\mathcal{M}=\{h_{x}[N]:N\in\mathcal{N},x\in A\}
and note
|\mathcal{M}|\leq 2^{\kappa}\cdot 2^{\kappa}=2^{\kappa}
. We show
\mathcal{M}
is a network for
X
. Let
y\in U
where
U
is open in
X
. There exists
x\in A
such that
y\in h_{x}[\overline{H}]\cap U
. Then
h_{x}^{\leftarrow}(y)\in\overline{H}\cap h_{x}^{\leftarrow}[U]
. As
\mathcal{N}
is a network for
\overline{H}
, there exists
N\in\mathcal{N}
such that
h_{x}^{\leftarrow}(y)\in N\subseteq\overline{H}\cap h_{x}^{\leftarrow}[U]
. It follows that
y\in h_{x}[N]\subseteq h_{x}[\overline{H}]\cap U\subseteq U
. As
h_{x}[N]\in\mathcal{M}
, we have that
\mathcal{M}
is a network for
X
and
nw(X)\leq 2^{\kappa}
. Therefore, as
pct(X)\leq\kappa
, it follows by Lemma
that
w(X)\leq 2^{\kappa}
. This completes the proof.
∎

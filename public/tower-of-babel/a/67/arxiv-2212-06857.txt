Tan’s two-body contact in a planar Bose gas: experiment vs theory
Adam Rançon, Nicolas Dupuis

Abstract
We determine the two-body contact in a planar Bose gas confined by a transverse harmonic potential, using the nonperturbative functional renormalization group. We use the three-dimensional thermodynamic definition of the contact where the latter is related to the derivation of the pressure of the quasi-two-dimensional system with respect to the three-dimensional scattering length of the bosons. Without any free parameter, we find a remarkable agreement with the experimental data of Zou et al. [Nat. Comm. 12 , 760 (2021)] from low to high temperatures, including the vicinity of the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless transition. We also show that the short-distance behavior of the pair distribution function and the high-momentum behavior of the momentum distribution are determined by two contacts: the three-dimensional contact for length scales smaller than the characteristic length ℓ z = ℏ / m ​ ω z \ell_{z}=\sqrt{\hbar/m\omega_{z}} of the harmonic potential and, for length scales larger than ℓ z \ell_{z} , an effective two-dimensional contact, related to the three-dimensional one by a geometric factor depending on ℓ z \ell_{z} .



Introduction.

Relating the macroscopic properties of a physical system to microscopic interactions and degrees of freedom is one of the main goals of many-body quantum physics. In ultracold atomic gases not all details of the interaction potential between particles are required since low-energy collisions are generally fully described by the
s
-wave scattering length. As a result, the equation of state of a dilute gas takes a simple, universal, expression where the microscopic physics enters only through two parameters, the mass of the particles and the scattering length. Considering the latter as an additional thermodynamic variable, besides the usual variables (e.g. the chemical potential
\mu
and the temperature
T
in the grand canonical ensemble), one can define its thermodynamic conjugate, the so-called Tan two-body contact
C

Tan 2008a
;
Tan 2008b
;
Tan 2008c
. In a dilute gas, the contact relates the (universal) low-temperature thermodynamics to the (universal) short-distance behavior which shows up in the two-body correlations or the momentum distribution function
Tan 2008a
;
Tan 2008b
;
Tan 2008c
;
Braaten and Platter 2008
;
Zhang and Leggett 2009
;
Combescot et al. 2009
;
Valiente et al.
;
Werner and Castin 2012a
;
Werner and Castin 2012b
. This simple description fails in a strongly interacting Bose gas where other parameters (e.g. associated with three-body effective interactions) are required for a complete description of the universal thermodynamics and short-distance physics
[Intwodimensionsandbelow
;
theequationofstatecanbeexpressedonlyintermsofthescatteringlengthandnoadditionalparameterssuchasthethree-bodyparameterarenecessary
;
see]Adhikari95
.

There have been few measurements of the two-body contact in Bose gases. Apart from experiments in the thermal regime
Wild et al. 2012
;
Fletcher et al. 2017
or the quasi-pure BEC one
Wild et al. 2012
;
Lopes et al. 2017
, the two-body contact has been determined in a planar Bose gas in a broad temperature range including the normal and superfluid phases as well as the vicinity of the Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT) transition
Zou et al. 2021
. The experimental data are in good agreement with theoretical predictions in the high-temperature limit (normal gas) and in the low-temperature limit (strongly degenerate superfluid). On the other hand there is no theoretical explanation for the value of the contact obtained near the BKT transition. In particular, the experimental data seem at odds with the predictions of a classical field theory
Prokof’ev and Svistunov 2002
used earlier successfully for the equation of state of a two-dimensional Bose gas
Yefsah et al. 2011
;
Desbuquois et al. 2014
.

In this Letter we compute the two-body contact in a planar Bose gas confined by a harmonic potential using the nonperturbative functional renormalization group (FRG), a modern implementation of Wilson’s RG
Berges et al. 2002
;
Delamotte 2012
;
Dupuis et al. 2021
. This approach has proven to be very accurate for determining the equation of state of such a system
Rançon and Dupuis 2012a
. We consider the weak-coupling limit where the three-dimensional scattering length
a_{3}
of the bosons is much smaller than the characteristic length
\ell_{z}=\sqrt{\hbar/m\omega_{z}}
of the harmonic potential. We use the three-dimensional thermodynamic definition of the contact where the latter is expressed as a derivative of the pressure with respect to the three-dimensional scattering length
a_{3}
and pay special attention to the quasi-two-dimensional structure of the system. We show that the short-distance behavior of the pair distribution function and the high-momentum behavior of the momentum distribution are determined by two contacts: the three-dimensional contact for length scales smaller than
\ell_{z}
and, for larger length scales, an effective two-dimensional contact (obtained from the derivative of the pressure with respect to the effective two-dimensional scattering length of the confined bosons), related to the three-dimensional one by a geometric factor depending on
\ell_{z}
. We then compare our results with the experimental data of Ref.
Zou et al. 2021
. Without any free parameters, we find a remarkable agreement between theory and experiment from low to high temperatures, including the vicinity of the BKT phase transition. We also show how to reconcile these experimental data with the classical field simulations of Ref.
Prokof’ev and Svistunov 2002
.

Contact of a planar Bose gas.

We consider a quasi-two-dimensional system of surface
L^{2}
obtained by subjecting a three-dimensional Bose gas to a confining harmonic potential of frequency
\omega_{z}=\hbar/m\ell_{z}^{2}
along the
z
direction. In the low-temperature regime
k_{B}T\ll\hbar\omega_{z}
, the physical properties of the planar gas can be obtained from the effective two-dimensional Hamiltonian (from now on we set
\hbar=k_{B}=1
)

\hat{H}=\int d^{2}r\biggl\{\frac{\boldsymbol{\nabla}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}\cdot\boldsymbol{\nabla}\hat{\psi}}{2m}+\frac{g}{2}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}\hat{\psi}\hat{\psi}\biggr\},

(1)

with an ultraviolet momentum cutoff
\Lambda\simeq 0.54\,\ell_{z}^{-1}

Petrov and Shlyapnikov 2001
;
Lim et al. 2008
. The effective interaction constant
g
is related to the three-dimensional scattering length
a_{3}
by

mg=\sqrt{8\pi}\frac{a_{3}}{\ell_{z}}.

(2)

Computing the low-energy scattering amplitude from (
1
), one obtains the effective two-dimensional scattering length
a_{2}
as a function of the microscopic parameters of the gas
Petrov and Shlyapnikov 2001
;
Lim et al. 2008
;
Pricoupenko and Olshanii 2007
,

\frac{a_{2}}{\ell_{z}}\simeq 3.71\,e^{-\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{\ell_{z}}{a_{3}}-\gamma},

(3)

where
\gamma\simeq 0.577
is the Euler constant.

In the low-temperature regime
T\ll\omega_{z}
, the pressure can be written in the scaling form characteristic of a two-dimensional system
Rançon and Dupuis 2012a
,

P(\mu,T)=-\frac{\Omega(\mu,T)}{L^{2}}=\frac{T}{\lambda^{2}}{\cal F}\left(\frac{\mu}{T},\tilde{g}(T)\right),

(4)

where
\Omega(\mu,T)
is the grand potential,
{\cal F}
a universal scaling function,
\lambda=\sqrt{2\pi/mT}
the thermal de Broglie wavelength and

\tilde{g}(T)=-\dfrac{4\pi}{\ln\left(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2ma_{2}^{2}T}\right)+\gamma}

(5)

a temperature-dependent dimensionless interaction constant. Equation (
4
) is valid for
|\mu|,T\ll\Lambda^{2}/2m\sim\omega_{z}
. The corrections to the scaling form (
4
) are negligible when
ma_{2}^{2}T,ma_{2}^{2}|\mu|\ll 1
. The dependence of the scaling function
{\cal F}
on
\mu/T
and
ma_{2}^{2}T
can be simply obtained by dimensional analysis using the fact that the scattering length is the only characteristic length scale at low energies. Renormalization-group arguments show that the dependence on
ma^{2}_{2}T
arises only through
\tilde{g}(T)

Rançon and Dupuis 2012a
.

The pressure depending only on
a_{3}
(through
a_{2}\equiv a_{2}(a_{3},\ell_{z})
) and not on the details of the interaction potential between particles, it is natural to consider
a_{3}
as an additional thermodynamic variable besides
\mu
and
T

Tan 2008a
. The quasi-two-dimensional two-body contact
C
is then essentially defined as the conjugate variable to
a_{3}
,

\frac{C}{L^{2}}=8\pi m\frac{\partial P}{\partial(1/a_{3})}\biggl|_{\mu,T}.

(6)

C
is an extensive quantity with dimension 1/length.
It can equivalently be defined in the canonical ensemble by replacing the pressure by the energy density in (
6
) and taking the derivative at fixed particle number and entropy. The motivation for defining the contact from a derivative with respect to
1/a_{3}
as in a three-dimensional system, rather than with respect to
\ln a_{2}
as in a two-dimensional system, is that in the limit
a_{3}\ll\ell_{z}
the collisions keep their three-dimensional character at length scales smaller than
\ell_{z}
.

Using the equation of state (
4
), we can write the contact as

\frac{C}{L^{2}}=-4(2\pi)^{5/2}\frac{T\ell_{z}}{\lambda^{4}}{\cal F}^{(0,1)}\left(\frac{\mu}{T},\tilde{g}(T)\right)\tilde{g}^{\prime}(T),

(7)

where we use the notation
{\cal F}^{(i,j)}(x,y)=\partial^{i}_{x}\partial^{j}_{y}{\cal F}(x,y)
. On the other hand, the scaling function
{\cal F}
determines the two-dimensional particle and entropy densities,
\bar{n}=\partial P/\partial\mu
and
s=\partial P/\partial T
. This allows us to express
{\cal F}^{(0,1)}
in terms of
P,\bar{n},s
, which leads to a relation between the contact and the thermodynamic potentials
P
and
E
(as in isotropic systems
Tan 2008b
)

\frac{C}{L^{2}}=4(2\pi)^{5/2}\frac{\ell_{z}}{\lambda^{2}T}\left(P-\frac{E}{L^{2}}\right),

(8)

where
E/L^{2}=-P+Ts+\mu\bar{n}
is the two-dimensional energy density.

In the weak-coupling limit, the scattering length
a_{2}
is exponentially small with respect to
\ell_{z}
and
\tilde{g}(T)\simeq\tilde{g}=2mg
is nearly temperature independent except for exponentially small temperatures
T\sim\omega_{z}e^{-\sqrt{\pi/2}(\ell_{z}/a_{3})}
. For this reason it is often concluded that the two-dimensional Bose gas exhibits an approximate scale invariance
Prokof’ev and Svistunov 2002
;
Yefsah et al. 2011
;
Desbuquois et al. 2014
(with no characteristic energy scales other than
\mu
and
T
):
{\cal F}(\mu/T,\tilde{g}(T))\simeq{\cal F}(\mu/T,\tilde{g})

Rançon and Dupuis 2012a
, and the normalized contact
C\lambda^{4}/L^{2}\ell_{z}
is a function of
\mu/T
and
\tilde{g}
.
However the assumption of approximate scale invariance cannot be used in the calculation leading to (
8
) since this would imply
\tilde{g}^{\prime}(T)=0
and in turn
E=L^{2}P
and
C=0
. The contact defined in (
8
) can thus be seen as a measure of the breakdown of scale invariance in the planar Bose gas
not a
. The situation is different in an isotropic three-dimensional system; in the unitary limit
a_{3}\to\infty
where scale invariance is satisfied,
E_{\rm 3D}=(3/2)L^{3}P_{\rm 3D}
, but the contact remains finite since
(3/2)P_{\rm 3D}-E_{\rm 3D}/L^{3}={\cal O}(1/a_{3})
whereas
C_{\rm 3D}\propto a_{3}[(3/2)P_{\rm 3D}-E_{\rm 3D}/L^{3}]
.

A remarkable feature of the contact defined from the pressure is that it also determines the short-distance behavior of the pair distribution function as well as the high-momentum limit of the momentum distribution function
Tan 2008a
;
Tan 2008b
;
Tan 2008c
;
Braaten and Platter 2008
;
Zhang and Leggett 2009
;
Combescot et al. 2009
;
Valiente et al.
;
Werner and Castin 2012a
;
Werner and Castin 2012b
. This property still holds in the planar gas. For the pair distribution function, averaged over the position
{\bf R}=({\bf r}_{1}+{\bf r}_{2})/2
of the pair center of mass, one has
not a

\displaystyle g({\bf r})

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}R\,\langle\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}({\bf r}_{1})\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}({\bf r}_{2})\hat{\psi}({\bf r}_{2})\hat{\psi}({\bf r}_{1})\rangle,

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{C}{(4\pi)^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{|{\bf r}|}-\frac{1}{a_{3}}\right)^{2}&\mbox{if}&|{\bf r}|\ll\ell_{z},\lambda,d,\\
\frac{C}{\sqrt{2\pi}\ell_{z}}|\phi_{0}^{-}(z)|^{2}\left(\frac{\ln|\boldsymbol{\rho}/a_{2}|}{2\pi}\right)^{2}&\mbox{if}&\ell_{z}\ll|\boldsymbol{\rho}|\ll\lambda,d,\end{array}\right.

where
{\bf r}={\bf r}_{1}-{\bf r}_{2}=(\boldsymbol{\rho},z)
is the coordinate of the relative motion of the pair (with
\boldsymbol{\rho}
a two-dimensional coordinate) and
d
the mean interparticle distance.
\phi_{0}^{-}
denotes the ground state of a particle of reduced mass
m/2
in an harmonic potential of characteristic frequency
\omega_{z}
. The fact that the contact (
6
) determines the pair distribution function at short length scales,
|{\bf r}|\ll\lambda,\ell_{z},d
, is not surprising since
C\propto\partial P/\partial(1/a_{3})
is defined as in the three dimensional case. On the other hand, the effective contact that appears in the regime
\ell_{z}\ll|\boldsymbol{\rho}|\ll\lambda,d
can be written as

\frac{C}{\sqrt{2\pi}\ell_{z}L^{2}}=-4\pi m\frac{\partial P}{\partial\ln a_{2}},

(11)

and thus coincides with the usual thermodynamic definition of a two-dimensional contact. Similarly, for the momentum distribution function
\bar{n}_{{\bf k},k_{z}}
, one finds

\bar{n}_{{\bf k},k_{z}}=\frac{C}{({\bf k}^{2}+k_{z}^{2})^{2}}\quad\mbox{if}\quad\frac{1}{a_{3}},\frac{1}{d},\frac{1}{\lambda}\ll\sqrt{{\bf k}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}},

(12)

and

\displaystyle\bar{n}^{\rm(2D)}_{{\bf k}}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{dk_{z}}{2\pi}\bar{n}_{{\bf k},k_{z}},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\ell_{z}}\frac{C}{|{\bf k}|^{4}}\quad\mbox{if}\quad\frac{1}{d},\frac{1}{\lambda}\ll|{\bf k}|\ll\frac{1}{\ell_{z}},

(13)

where we assume the normalization
\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{{\bf k}}\int\frac{dk_{z}}{2\pi}\bar{n}_{{\bf k},k_{z}}=N
(with
N\equiv\langle\hat{N}\rangle
the total number of particles). Equation (
12
) holds at any temperature while Eq. (
13
) is valid only in the low-temperature regime
T\ll\omega_{z}
. Note that a result similar to (
12
,
13
) has been obtained for fermions in quasi-one- and quasi-two-dimensional traps
He and Zhou 2019
(see also
Decamp et al. 2018
;
Bougas et al. 2020
for bosons in a quasi-one-dimensional trap).

Conclusion.

The theoretical calculation of the two-body contact of a planar Bose gas in the framework of the nonperturbative FRG is in remarkable agreement with the experimental data of Ref.
Zou et al. 2021
. The FRG provides us with an accurate value of the contact over a broad temperature range including the vicinity of the BKT transition. We have also shown how the apparent contradiction between the experimental data of Ref.
Zou et al. 2021
and the classical field simulations
Prokof’ev and Svistunov 2002
can be overcome. The FRG predicts a bump in the contact for a phase-space density
{\cal D}\simeq 3
, which requires improved-precision measurements to be confirmed.

An interesting aspect of the contact theory in a quasi-two-dimensional gas is that the high-momentum tail,
\sqrt{{\bf k}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}}\gg 1/a_{3},1/\lambda,1/d
, of the three-dimensional momentum distribution is controlled by the three-dimensional contact (as defined in (
6
)) while the intermediate momentum range,
1/d,1/\lambda\ll|{\bf k}|\ll 1/\ell_{z}
, is controlled by the effective two-dimensional contact
C/\sqrt{2\pi}\ell_{z}
[Eqs. (
12
,
13
) and Refs.
He and Zhou 2019
;
Bougas et al. 2020
]. A measure of the momentum distribution, for instance by ballistic expansion of the atomic cloud or rf spectroscopy
Stewart et al.
, would allow us to confirm this essential property of the contact theory.

direction and
\ell_{z}=1/\sqrt{m\omega_{z}}
is the width of the trap (we set
\hbar=k_{B}=1
). The surface
L^{2}
of the quasi-2D system is assumed much larger than all characteristic length scales of the system. We denote by
g_{3}
the interaction between bosons, assumed to be local, and regularize the model by a UV momentum cutoff
\Lambda_{3}
. In the absence of the trapping potential (
V(z)=0
), the
s
-wave scattering length is defined by

\frac{m}{4\pi a_{3}}=\frac{1}{g_{3}}+\int_{{\bf k},k_{z}}\frac{1}{2\epsilon_{{\bf k},k_{z}}},

(S2)

where
\epsilon_{{\bf k},k_{z}}=({\bf k}^{2}+k_{z}^{2})/2m
,
\int_{{\bf k},k_{z}}=\int\frac{d^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{dk_{z}}{2\pi}
(with
{\bf k}
a 2D wavevector) and the momentum integral is restricted to
({\bf k}^{2}+k_{z}^{2})^{1/2}\leq\Lambda_{3}
.

In presence of the trapping potential, scattering at low energy is effectively 2D (see Sec. II.A) with the 2D
s
-wave scattering length
Petrov and Shlyapnikov 2001
;
Lim et al. 2008
;
Pricoupenko and Olshanii 2007

a_{2}\simeq 3.71\ell_{z}e^{-\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{\ell_{z}}{a_{3}}-\gamma},

(S3)

where
\gamma\simeq 0.577
is the Euler constant.
In the low-temperature regime
T\ll 1/m\ell_{z}^{2}
, where
\ell_{z}
is much smaller than the thermal de Broglie wavelength
\lambda=\sqrt{2\pi/mT}
, only the lowest-energy state of the trapping potential
V(z)
is occupied and the low-energy properties of the quasi-2D gas can be obtained from the effective 2D Hamiltonian

\hat{H}_{\rm eff}=\int d^{2}\rho\biggl\{\frac{\boldsymbol{\nabla}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{\rho})\cdot\boldsymbol{\nabla}\hat{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\rho})}{2m}-\mu\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{\rho})\hat{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\rho})+\frac{g}{2}\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{\rho})\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(\boldsymbol{\rho})\hat{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\rho})\hat{\psi}(\boldsymbol{\rho})\biggr\}

(S4)

with an ultraviolet momentum cutoff
\Lambda
. Computing the low-energy scattering amplitude from (
S4
), one obtains the effective 2D scattering length
a_{2}
as a function of the effective parameters
g
and
\Lambda

a_{2}=\frac{2}{\Lambda}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{mg}-\gamma}.

(S5)

This reproduces the scattering length (
S3
) setting
\Lambda\simeq 0.54\,\ell_{z}^{-1}
and

mg=\sqrt{8\pi}\frac{a_{3}}{\ell_{z}}.

(S6)

In the planar Bose gas, we define the contact as in a 3D system,

\frac{C}{L^{2}}=8\pi m\frac{\partial P}{\partial(1/a_{3})},

(S7)

i.e. from the derivative of the pressure with respect to the scattering length
a_{3}
.

I
I.   Zero temperature: Bogoliubov theory

In the zero-temperature degenerate limit (
\mu>0
) we expect most particles to be in the condensate. In that case the contact should be well described by the effective 2D Hamiltonian (
S4
) and the Bogoliubov theory.

Following Ref.
Rançon and Dupuis 2012
we consider the effective potential (or Gibbs free energy)
U(n)
where
n
is the 2D condensate density. At the mean-field level, it is simply given by the Hamiltonian treating the operator
\hat{\psi}\equiv\sqrt{n}e^{i\theta}
(with
\theta
an arbitrary phase) as a classical field,

U(n)=-\mu n+\frac{g}{2}n^{2},

(S8)

assuming
n
to be uniform. The equilibrium value of the condensate density,
n_{0}=\mu/g
, is obtained by minimizing the effective potential:
U^{\prime}(n_{0})=0
. This gives the pressure
P=-U(n_{0})=\mu^{2}/2g
and the particle density
\bar{n}=\partial P/\partial\mu=\mu/g
at mean-field. We thus obtain the contact

\frac{C}{L^{2}}=\sqrt{2\pi}(m\mu)^{2}\ell_{z}.

(S9)

The Lee-Huang-Yang correction to the mean-field result is given by the one-loop expression
Rançon and Dupuis 2012

U(n)=-\mu n+\frac{g}{2}n^{2}+\frac{1}{2\ell}\int_{\bf k}[-\xi_{\bf k}-2gn+E_{\bf k}(n)],

(S10)

where
\xi_{\bf k}=\epsilon_{\bf k}-\mu
and

E_{\bf k}(n)=\bigl[(\xi_{\bf k}+2gn)^{2}-(gn)^{2}\bigr]^{1/2}.

(S11)

The parameter
\ell
in (
S10
) is necessary to organize the loop expansion but will eventually be set to unity. From (
S10
) we obtain

\begin{split}U^{\prime}(n)&=-\mu+gn-\frac{g}{\ell}\int_{\bf k}\left(1-\frac{E_{\bf k}^{\prime}(n)}{2g}\right),\\
n_{0}&=\frac{\mu}{g}+\frac{1}{\ell}\int_{\bf k}\left(1-\frac{2\epsilon_{\bf k}+\mu}{2E_{\bf k}}\right),\end{split}

(S12)

where it is now understood that all calculations are performed to order
1/\ell
and
E_{\bf k}=E_{\bf k}(n=\mu/g)=[\epsilon_{\bf k}(\epsilon_{\bf k}+2\mu)]^{1/2}
is the value of
E_{\bf k}(n_{0})
to leading order. Performing the momentum integration with the UV cutoff
\Lambda
, we obtain

\begin{split}n_{0}&=\frac{\mu}{g}+\frac{1}{\ell}\left[\frac{m\mu}{4\pi}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m\mu}\right)-\frac{m\mu}{2\pi}\right],\\
P&=\frac{\mu^{2}}{2g}-\frac{1}{2\ell}\int_{\bf k}(E_{\bf k}-\epsilon_{\bf k}-\mu)=\frac{\mu^{2}}{2g}-\frac{1}{\ell}\frac{m}{4\pi}\left[\frac{\mu^{2}}{4}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{2}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m\mu}\right)\right].\end{split}

(S13)

Using the definition (
S5
) of the scattering length
a_{2}
and setting
\ell=1
, we deduce
Popov 1983
;
Mora and Castin 2003
;
Pricoupenko

\begin{split}P&=-\frac{m\mu^{2}}{8\pi}\ln\left(\frac{m\mu a_{2}^{2}}{4}e^{2\gamma+\frac{1}{2}}\right),\\
\bar{n}&=-\frac{m\mu}{4\pi}\ln\left(\frac{m\mu a_{2}^{2}}{4}e^{2\gamma+1}\right).\end{split}

(S14)

The contact as a function of
\mu
retains the same expression as in the mean-field approximation [Eq. (
S9
)], but the relation between
\mu
and the density
\bar{n}
differs from the mean-field one. From (
S14
) and (
S9
) we obtain

\mu\simeq\frac{2\pi\bar{n}/m}{\left|\ln\left(a_{2}\sqrt{\pi\bar{n}}e^{\gamma+\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right|},

(S15)

and the zero-temperature value of the contact,

\frac{C_{\rm Bog}}{L^{2}}\simeq(2\pi)^{5/2}\frac{\bar{n}^{2}\ell_{z}}{\left|\ln\left(a_{2}\sqrt{\pi\bar{n}}e^{\gamma+\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right|^{2}},

(S16)

as a function of the density.

From (
S14
) it is straightforward to recover the expression
C/L^{2}=\sqrt{2\pi}(m\mu)^{2}\ell_{z}
using Eq. (8) in the main text relating the contact to the pressure and the energy density. This is not the case in the mean-field theory; the latter gives a vanishing contact using (8) but a nonzero one using (
S7
).

II
II.   Low-density limit: virial expansion

When the gas is in the normal (non-degenerate) phase,
\mu<0
and
|\mu|\gg T
, it is possible to compute the contact within an expansion in the fugacity
z=e^{\beta\mu}
. This calculation not only gives an explicit expression of the contact in that limit but also shows that the short-distance behavior of the pair distribution function and the high-momentum behavior of the momentum distribution are determined by two contacts: the 3D contact for length scales smaller than
\ell_{z}
and an effective 2D contact for length scales larger than
\ell_{z}
. We show that the relation between these two contacts is valid beyond the virial expansion.

II.1
A. Two-body problem and
T
matrix

The Hamiltonian of a single particle in the harmonic potential reads

\hat{H}=\frac{\hat{\bf p}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{\hat{p}_{z}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{1}{2}m\omega_{z}^{2}\hat{z}^{2},

(S17)

where
{\bf p}
is a 2D momentum associated with the motion perpendicular to the
z
axis and we denote the 3D coordinate by
{\bf r}=(\boldsymbol{\rho},z)
, while
{\bf k}
is a 2D wavevector. The eigenstates
|{\bf k},n\rangle
and eigenenergies are given by

\langle{\bf r}|{\bf k},n\rangle=\frac{e^{i{\bf k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\rho}}}{L}\phi_{n}(z),\qquad\epsilon_{{\bf k},n}=\epsilon_{\bf k}+\epsilon_{n}=\frac{{\bf k}^{2}}{2m}+\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\omega_{z}

(S18)

with

\phi_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}n!}}\left(\frac{m\omega_{z}}{\pi}\right)^{1/4}H_{n}\left(\sqrt{m\omega_{z}}z\right)e^{-\frac{m\omega_{z}}{2}z^{2}}

(S19)

where
H_{n}
is a Hermite polynomial (
n\in\mathbb{N}
). For the two-body problem it is convenient to introduce the center-of-mass variables and those of the relative motion,

\begin{split}&\hat{\bf P}=\hat{\bf p}_{1}+\hat{\bf p}_{2},\quad\hat{P}_{z}=\hat{p}_{z1}+\hat{p}_{z2},\quad{\bf R}_{\parallel}=\frac{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{1}+\boldsymbol{\rho}_{2}}{2},\quad\hat{Z}=\frac{\hat{z}_{1}+\hat{z}_{2}}{2},\\
&\hat{\bf p}=\frac{\hat{\bf p}_{1}-\hat{\bf p}_{2}}{2},\quad\hat{p}_{z}=\frac{\hat{p}_{z1}-\hat{p}_{z2}}{2},\quad\boldsymbol{\rho}=\boldsymbol{\rho}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\rho}_{2},\quad\hat{z}=\hat{z}_{1}-\hat{z}_{2},\end{split}

(S20)

and write the two-body Hamiltonian as

\displaystyle\hat{H}={}

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{\bf P}^{2}}{4m}+\frac{\hat{P}_{z}^{2}}{4m}+\frac{1}{2}(2m)\omega_{z}^{2}\hat{Z}^{2}+\frac{\hat{\bf p}^{2}}{m}+\frac{\hat{p}_{z}^{2}}{m}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)\omega_{z}^{2}\hat{z}^{2}+g_{3}\,\delta(\hat{\boldsymbol{\rho}})\delta(\hat{z}).

(S21)

The harmonic potential preserves the decoupling between the center-of-mass and relative motions. The two-body eigenstates for
g_{3}=0
can therefore be written as
|{\bf k}_{1},n_{1};{\bf k}_{2},n_{2}\rangle=|{\bf k}_{1},n_{1}\rangle\otimes|{\bf k}_{2},n_{2}\rangle
with the corresponding (non-symmetrized) wavefunctions

\frac{e^{i{\bf k}_{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{\rho}_{1}+i{\bf k}_{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{\rho}_{2}}}{L^{2}}\phi_{n_{1}}(z_{1})\phi_{n_{2}}(z_{2}),\qquad\epsilon_{{\bf k}_{1},n_{1},{\bf k}_{2},n_{2}}=\epsilon_{{\bf k}_{1},n_{1}}+\epsilon_{{\bf k}_{2},n_{2}}\qquad(n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{N})

(S22)

or, in the center-of-mass frame,
|{\bf K},N;{\bf k},n\rangle
with the corresponding wavefunctions

\frac{e^{i{\bf K}\cdot{\bf R}_{\parallel}+i{\bf k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\rho}}}{L^{2}}\phi^{+}_{N}(Z)\phi^{-}_{n}(z),\qquad\epsilon_{{\bf K},N,{\bf k},n}=\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf K},N}+\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n}\qquad(N,n\in\mathbb{N}),

(S23)

where
\phi_{n}^{\pm},\epsilon^{\pm}_{{\bf k},n}
are deduced from
\phi_{n},\epsilon_{{\bf k},n}
by replacing the mass
m
by
2m
and
m/2
, respectively.

In the center-of-mass frame, the
T
matrix reads

T_{nn^{\prime}}({\bf K},N,\tau-\tau^{\prime})=\langle{\bf K},N;{\bf k},n|\hat{T}|{\bf K},N;{\bf k}^{\prime},n^{\prime}\rangle=\Theta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})e^{-\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf K},N}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}t_{nn^{\prime}}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})

(S24)

i.e.

T_{nn^{\prime}}({\bf K},N,i\omega)=t_{nn^{\prime}}(i\omega-\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf K},N}),

(S25)

where
\omega
is a Matsubara frequency (we suppress the index
n
of
\omega_{n}
since we consider here the zero-temperature limit). The part of the
T
matrix that describes the relative motion can be easily obtained from the Lippmann-Schwinger equation
Lim et al. 2008
,

t_{nn^{\prime}}(i\omega)=\phi_{n}^{-}(0)^{*}\phi_{n^{\prime}}^{-}{}(0)t(i\omega),\qquad\frac{1}{t(i\omega)}=\frac{1}{g_{3}}-\sum_{n}\int_{\bf k}\frac{|\phi_{n}^{-}(0)|^{2}}{i\omega-\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n}}.

(S26)

Here and in the following the sum over
n
extends over
\mathbb{N}
.
We can use the definition (
S2
) of the scattering length
a_{3}
of the unconfined bosons
to rewrite (
S26
) as

\frac{1}{t(i\omega)}=\frac{m}{4\pi a_{3}}-\sum_{n}\int_{\bf k}\frac{|\phi_{n}^{-}(0)|^{2}}{i\omega-\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n}}-\int_{{\bf k},k_{z}}\frac{1}{2(\epsilon_{\bf k}+\epsilon_{k_{z}})},

(S27)

where the UV cutoff
\Lambda_{3}
can now be sent to infinity
Lim et al. 2008
.
At low frequencies,
|\omega|\ll\omega_{z}
, the
T
matrix
t_{00}(i\omega)
takes the usual 2D form
Petrov et al. 2000
;
Lim et al. 2008

t_{00}(i\omega)=-\frac{2\pi/m}{\ln\frac{a_{2}}{2}\sqrt{-im\omega}+\gamma},

(S28)

where the effective 2D scattering length
a_{2}
is given by (
S5
). Finally we note that Eq. (
S27
) implies that the retarded
T
matrix
t^{R}(\omega)=t(\omega+i0^{+})
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\omega}\frac{1}{t^{R}(\omega)}=\sum_{n}\int_{\bf k}\frac{|\phi_{n}^{-}(0)|^{2}}{(\omega+i0^{+}-\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n})^{2}},

(S29)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial(1/a_{3})}\frac{1}{t^{R}(\omega)}=\frac{m}{4\pi}.

(S30)

These two relations will be useful in the calculation of the contact.

II.2
B. Calculation of the contact from the thermodynamic definition

Let us consider the distribution of the in-plane momentum
\bar{n}_{\bf k}
. To order
z^{2}
, and ignoring the part that comes from the non-interacting propagator
G({\bf k},n,i\omega_{\nu})=(i\omega_{\nu}+\mu-\epsilon_{{\bf k},n})^{-1}
and does not depend on the interactions, it is sufficient to consider
Leyronas 2011

\delta\bar{n}_{\bf k}=\frac{2}{\beta^{2}}\sum_{\omega_{n},\omega_{\nu}}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}}\sum_{{\bf K}}G({\bf k},n_{1},i\omega_{n})^{2}G({\bf K}-{\bf k},n_{2},i\omega_{\nu}-i\omega_{n})T_{n_{1},n_{2};n_{1},n_{2}}({\bf K},i\omega_{\nu}),

(S31)

where

\displaystyle T_{n_{1},n_{2};n_{1},n_{2}}({\bf K},i\omega_{\nu})

\displaystyle=\langle{\bf k}_{1},n_{1};{\bf K}-{\bf k}_{1},n_{2}|\hat{T}|{\bf k}^{\prime}_{1},n_{1};{\bf K}-{\bf k}^{\prime}_{1},n_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{N,n}U_{n_{1},n_{2};N,n}T_{nn}({\bf K},N,i\omega_{\nu})U^{\dagger}_{N,n;n_{1},n_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{N,n}|U_{n_{1},n_{2};N,n}|^{2}|\phi_{n}^{-}(0)|^{2}t(i\omega_{\nu}-\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf k},N}+2\mu)

(S32)

is obtained using the eigenstates
|{\bf k}_{1},n_{1};{\bf k}_{2},n_{2}\rangle=|{\bf k}_{1},n_{1}\rangle\otimes|{\bf k}_{2},n_{2}\rangle
for the two-body transverse motion. We have used the fact that
U_{n_{1},n_{2};N,n}=\langle n_{1},n_{2}|N,n\rangle
is nonzero only if
n_{1}+n_{2}=N+n
since
|n_{1},n_{2}\rangle
and
|N,n\rangle
are orthogonal when they do not belong to the same energy subspace. To order
z^{2}
, one can use the zero-temperature limit of the
T
matrix but one must shift its argument by
2\mu
to take into account the nonzero chemical potential. In the following we include the zero-point energy
\omega_{z}/2
in the chemical potential so that the quantized levels of the harmonic oscillator with frequency
\omega_{z}
are given by
\epsilon_{n}=n\omega_{z}
(
n\in\mathbb{N}
). We thus obtain

\displaystyle\delta\bar{n}_{\bf k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\atop N,n}|U_{n_{1},n_{2};N,n}|^{2}|\phi_{n}^{-}(0)|^{2}\frac{2}{\beta^{2}}\sum_{\omega_{n},\omega_{\nu}}\sum_{\bf K}G({\bf k},n_{1},i\omega_{n})^{2}G({\bf K}-{\bf k},n_{2},i\omega_{\nu}-i\omega_{n})t(i\omega_{\nu}-\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf K},N}+2\mu)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\atop N,n}|U_{n_{1},n_{2};N,n}|^{2}|\phi_{n}^{-}(0)|^{2}\frac{2}{\beta}\sum_{\omega_{\nu}}\sum_{\bf K}\frac{t(i\omega_{\nu}-\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf K},N}+2\mu)}{(i\omega_{\nu}-\xi_{\bf k}-\xi_{{\bf K}-{\bf k}}-(N+n)\omega_{z})^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{N,n}|\phi_{n}^{-}(0)|^{2}\frac{2}{\beta}\sum_{\omega_{\nu}}\sum_{\bf K}\frac{t(i\omega_{\nu}-\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf K},N}+2\mu)}{(i\omega_{\nu}-\xi_{\bf k}-\xi_{{\bf K}-{\bf k}}-(N+n)\omega_{z})^{2}},

(S33)

where we have kept only terms of order
z^{2}
and used
\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}}|U_{n_{1},n_{2};N,n}|^{2}=1
. Performing the sum over
\omega_{\nu}
by contour integral, we finally obtain

\displaystyle\delta\bar{n}_{\bf k}

\displaystyle=-2\sum_{N,n}|\phi_{n}^{-}(0)|^{2}\sum_{\bf K}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\pi}n_{B}(\omega+\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf K},N}-2\mu)\Im\left\{\frac{t^{R}(\omega)}{(\omega+i0^{+}+\epsilon_{\bf K}/2-\epsilon_{\bf k}-\epsilon_{{\bf K}-{\bf k}}-n\omega_{z})^{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle=-2z^{2}\sum_{N,n}|\phi_{n}^{-}(0)|^{2}\sum_{{\bf K}}e^{-\beta\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf K},N}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\pi}e^{-\beta\omega}\Im\left\{\frac{t^{R}(\omega)}{(\omega+i0^{+}+\epsilon_{\bf K}/2-\epsilon_{\bf k}-\epsilon_{{\bf K}-{\bf k}}-n\omega_{z})^{2}}\right\}

(S34)

to order
z^{2}
. The contribution of the interactions to the pressure to the same order is obtained by considering the 2D density
\delta\bar{n}=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\int_{\bf k}\delta\bar{n}_{\bf k}=\partial\delta P/\partial\mu
, i.e.

\displaystyle\delta P

\displaystyle=-\frac{z^{2}}{\beta}\sum_{N,n}|\phi_{n}^{-}(0)|^{2}\int_{{\bf K},{\bf k}}e^{-\beta\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf K},N}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\pi}e^{-\beta\omega}\Im\left\{\frac{t^{R}(\omega)}{(\omega+i0^{+}-2\epsilon_{\bf k}-n\omega_{z})^{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle=z^{2}\sum_{N}\int_{\bf K}e^{-\beta\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf K},N}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\pi}e^{-\beta\omega}\Im[\ln t^{R}(\omega)],

(S35)

where we have integrated by part in the integral over
\omega
and used (
S29
).
This results is in agreement with a similar virial calculation done for a 2D Fermi gas in
Ngampruetikorn et al. 2013
. It is however different from that of
Ren 2004
for a two dimensional Bose gas, which we ascribe to the fact that that calculation is not truly a virial expansion but a perturbative expansion with ressumation of some of the
T
-matrix diagrams.

Equation (
S35
) yields the contact

\displaystyle\frac{C}{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-2m^{2}z^{2}\sum_{N}\int_{\bf K}e^{-\beta\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf K},N}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\pi}e^{-\beta\omega}\Im[t^{R}(\omega)]

\displaystyle=2m^{2}z^{2}\sum_{N}\int_{\bf K}e^{-\beta\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf K},N}}\sum_{n}|\phi^{-}_{n}(0)|^{2}\int_{\bf k}e^{-\beta\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n}}|t^{R}(\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n})|^{2},

(S36)

where we have used (
S30
) and
\Im[t^{R}(\omega)^{-1}]=\pi\sum_{n}|\phi_{n}^{-}(0)|^{2}\int_{\bf k}\delta(\omega-\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n})
.

In the low-temperature regime
T\ll\omega_{z}
, we can restrict ourselves to
N=n=0
in (
S36
). Performing the sum over
{\bf K}
and using (
S28
), one finds

\frac{C}{L^{2}}=4(2\pi)^{3/2}z^{2}\frac{\ell_{z}}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dk\,k\frac{e^{-\beta k^{2}/m}}{\left[\ln\left(\frac{ka_{2}}{2}e^{\gamma}\right)\right]^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}}.

(S37)

Performing the integral in the limit
|\ln(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{ma_{2}^{2}T}e^{\gamma})|\gg 1

not
, we finally obtain

\frac{C_{\rm virial}}{L^{2}}=2(2\pi)^{5/2}\frac{\bar{n}^{2}\ell_{z}}{\left|\ln\left(\frac{a_{2}}{\lambda}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{\gamma}\right)\right|^{2}},

(S38)

where
\bar{n}=z/\lambda^{2}+{\cal O}(z^{2})
. The result (
S37
) can also be obtained from Eq. (8) in the main text, relating the contact to the pressure and the energy density, using the expression (
S35
) of the pressure.

II.3
C. Calculation of the contact from the pair distribution function

II.3.1
1. Scattering states

The scattering states associated with the relative motion can be deduced from the Lippmann-Schwinger equation. They satisfy

|\psi_{{\bf k},n}\rangle=|{\bf k},n\rangle+\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{{\bf k}^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\frac{t^{R}_{n^{\prime}n}(\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n})}{\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n}+i0^{+}-\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k}^{\prime},n^{\prime}}}|{\bf k}^{\prime},n^{\prime}\rangle,

(S39)

where
t^{R}(\omega)=t(\omega+i0^{+})
is the retarded
T
matrix defined by (
S27
). Using

\int_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}\frac{e^{i{\bf k}^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{\rho}}}{\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n}+i0^{+}-\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k}^{\prime},n^{\prime}}}=-i\frac{m}{4}H_{0}^{(1)}(k_{n^{\prime}}\rho),

(S40)

where
k_{n^{\prime}}^{2}={\bf k}^{2}+(n-n^{\prime})m\omega_{z}
,
\rho=|\boldsymbol{\rho}|
and
H_{0}^{(1)}
is a Hankel function of the first kind, we obtain

\psi_{{\bf k},n}({\bf r})=\frac{e^{i{\bf k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\rho}}}{L}\phi^{-}_{n}(z)-i\frac{m}{4L}\sum_{n^{\prime}}t^{R}(\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n})\phi^{-}_{n^{\prime}}(0)^{*}\phi^{-}_{n}(0)\phi^{-}_{n^{\prime}}(z)H_{0}^{(1)}(k_{n^{\prime}}\rho).

(S41)

When
k_{n^{\prime}}^{2}<0
,
k_{n^{\prime}}
in (
S40
) and (
S41
) must be understood as
i|k_{n^{\prime}}|
and
H_{0}^{(1)}(i|k_{n^{\prime}}|\rho)=-(2i/\pi)K_{0}(|k_{n^{\prime}}|\rho)
decays exponentially for large
\rho
, which corresponds to a closed scattering channel.

II.3.2
2. Pair distribution function

We now consider the pair distribution function, averaged over the position
{\bf R}=({\bf r}_{1}+{\bf r}_{2})/2
of the pair center of mass,

g({\bf r}_{1}-{\bf r}_{2})=\int d^{3}R\,\langle\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}({\bf r}_{1})\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}({\bf r}_{2})\hat{\psi}({\bf r}_{2})\hat{\psi}({\bf r}_{1})\rangle.

(S42)

To obtain the contact to order
z^{2}
, it is sufficient to consider states with only two particles. The contribution to the pair distribution function of the two-particle scattering state
|\psi^{(s)}_{{\bf K},N;{\bf k},n}\rangle
, defined by its properly symmetrized wavefunction

\psi^{(s)}_{{\bf K},N;{\bf k},n}({\bf R},{\bf r})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{e^{i{\bf K}\cdot{\bf R}_{\parallel}}}{L}\phi^{+}_{N}(Z)[\psi_{{\bf k},n}({\bf r})+\psi_{-{\bf k},n}({\bf r})],

(S43)

reads
\int d^{3}R\,2|\psi^{(s)}_{{\bf K},N;{\bf k},n}({\bf R},{\bf r})|^{2}
. To order
z^{2}
, the function
g({\bf r})
is obtained by summing over all states
\psi^{(s)}_{{\bf K},N;{\bf k},n}
with the Boltzmann weight

\frac{1}{Z}e^{-\beta(\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf K},N}+\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n}-2\mu)}=z^{2}e^{-\beta(\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf K},N}+\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n})}+{\cal O}(z^{3})

(S44)

(using
Z=1+{\cal O}(z)
), i.e.

g({\bf r})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{\bf K},{\bf k}}\sum_{N,n}z^{2}e^{-\beta(\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf K},N}+\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n})}\int d^{3}R\,2|\psi^{(s)}_{{\bf K},N;{\bf k},n}({\bf R},{\bf r})|^{2},

(S45)

where a factor
1/2
has been introduced in the sum over
{\bf k}
since
\psi^{(s)}_{{\bf K},N;{\bf k},n}
and
\psi^{(s)}_{{\bf K},N;-{\bf k},n}
are the same state.

Let us first consider the short distance limit
r=|{\bf r}|=\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}\ll\lambda,\ell_{z}
. In that case one has
Petrov et al. 2000

\frac{i}{4}\sum_{n^{\prime}}\phi^{-}_{n^{\prime}}(z)\phi^{-}_{n^{\prime}}(0)^{*}H_{0}^{(1)}(k_{n^{\prime}}\rho)\simeq\frac{1}{4\pi}\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{a_{3}}\right)+\frac{1}{mt^{R}(\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n})}

(S46)

and thus

\psi_{{\bf k},n}({\bf r})\simeq-\frac{m}{4\pi L}\phi_{n}^{-}(0)t^{R}(\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n})\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{a_{3}}\right).

(S47)

Equation (
S45
) then gives

g({\bf r})=\frac{2m^{2}z^{2}}{(4\pi rL)^{2}}\sum_{{\bf K},N}e^{-\beta\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf K},N}}\sum_{{\bf k},n}e^{-\beta\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n}}|\phi_{n}^{-}(0)|^{2}|t^{R}(\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n})|^{2}=\frac{C}{(4\pi)^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{a_{3}}\right)^{2},

(S48)

where
C
is the contact (
S36
). At short length scales,
r\ll\lambda,\ell_{z}
, the pair distribution function behaves as in an isotropic 3D system with contact
C
.

Let us now consider the intermediate length scales,
\ell_{z}\ll\rho\ll\lambda
, in the low-temperature regime
T\ll\omega_{z}
. Since only the states with
\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf K},N},\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},n}\lesssim T
contribute significantly to the pair distribution function (
S45
), we can restrict ourselves to
N=n=0
and thus consider only the scattering states

\psi_{{\bf k},0}({\bf r})=\frac{e^{i{\bf k}\cdot\boldsymbol{\rho}}}{L}\phi^{-}_{0}(z)-i\frac{m}{4L}\sum_{n^{\prime}}t^{R}(\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k}})\phi^{-}_{n^{\prime}}(0)^{*}\phi^{-}_{0}(0)\phi^{-}_{n^{\prime}}(z)H_{0}^{(1)}(k_{n^{\prime}}\rho)

(S49)

with
|{\bf k}|\lesssim 1/\lambda
. If
n^{\prime}>0
then
k_{n^{\prime}}^{2}<0
and
H_{0}^{(1)}(k_{n^{\prime}}\rho)=H_{0}^{(1)}(i|k_{n^{\prime}}|\rho)\sim e^{-\sqrt{n^{\prime}}\rho/\ell_{z}}
decays exponentially for
\rho\gg\ell_{z}
. On the other hand, if
n^{\prime}=0
,

H_{0}^{(1)}(k_{n^{\prime}}\rho)=H_{0}^{(1)}(|{\bf k}|\rho)\simeq\frac{2i}{\pi}\left(\ln\frac{|{\bf k}|\rho}{2}+\gamma-i\frac{\pi}{2}\right)

(S50)

for
\rho\ll\lambda
(since
|{\bf k}|\lesssim 1/\lambda
). Thus for
\ell_{z}\ll\rho\ll\lambda
, we can just consider the contribution of
n^{\prime}=0
in (
S49
) and using (
S28
) we obtain

\psi_{{\bf k},0}({\bf r})\simeq\frac{m}{2\pi L}t^{R}(\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k}})|\phi^{-}_{0}(0)|^{2}\phi^{-}_{0}(z)\ln(\rho/a_{2}).

(S51)

From (
S45
) we finally deduce

\displaystyle g({\bf r})

\displaystyle=\frac{2m^{2}z^{2}}{L^{2}}\left(\frac{\ln(\rho/a_{2})}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\sum_{{\bf K},{\bf k}}e^{-\beta(\epsilon^{+}_{{\bf K}}+\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k}})}|t^{R}(\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k}})|^{2}|\phi^{-}_{0}(0)|^{4}|\phi^{-}_{0}(z)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\ln(\rho/a_{2})}{2\pi}\right)^{2}C|\phi^{-}_{0}(0)|^{2}|\phi^{-}_{0}(z)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\ln(\rho/a_{2})}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\frac{C}{\sqrt{2\pi}\ell_{z}}|\phi^{-}_{0}(z)|^{2},

(S52)

where
C
is the low-temperature limit of the contact (
S36
). If we average over
z
, we recover the standard result for a 2D system with a contact
C/\sqrt{2\pi}\ell_{z}
. The latter is related to the pressure by

\frac{C}{\sqrt{2\pi}\ell_{z}L^{2}}=4\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{m}{\ell_{z}}\frac{\partial P}{\partial(1/a_{3})}=-4\pi m\frac{\partial P}{\partial\ln a_{2}}

(S53)

and therefore coincides with the usual thermodynamic definition of a 2D contact.

We shall see in Sec. D that the short-distance behavior of the pair distribution function, Eqs. (
S48
) and (
S52
), is actually valid only when
r
and
\rho
are much smaller than the interparticle distance
d
.

II.3.3
3. Momentum distribution

The knowledge of the short-distance behavior of the pair distribution function allows one to obtain the high-momentum behavior of the momentum distribution function
n_{{\bf k}}
. The calculation is standard and yields

\bar{n}_{{\bf k},k_{z}}=\frac{C}{({\bf k}^{2}+k_{z}^{2})^{2}}\quad\mbox{if}\quad\frac{1}{d},\frac{1}{\lambda},\frac{1}{a_{3}}\ll\sqrt{{\bf k}^{2}+k_{z}^{2}},

(S54)

and

\bar{n}_{\bf k}=\int\frac{dk_{z}}{2\pi}\bar{n}_{{\bf k},k_{z}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\ell_{z}}\frac{C}{|{\bf k}|^{4}}\quad\mbox{if}\quad\frac{1}{d},\frac{1}{\lambda}\ll|{\bf k}|\ll\frac{1}{\ell_{z}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad T\ll\omega_{z},

(S55)

where we assume the normalization
\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{{\bf k}}\int_{k_{z}}\bar{n}_{{\bf k},k_{z}}=N
(with
N\equiv\langle\hat{N}\rangle
the total number of particles).
Equation (
S55
) can also be easily obtained from (
S34
). Again we find that the 3D contact (
S7
) governs the high-momentum behavior but the intermediate range
1/d,1/\lambda\ll|{\bf k}|\ll 1/\ell_{z}
involves the 2D contact
C/\sqrt{2\pi}\ell_{z}
.

II.4
D. Beyond the virial expansion

Results similar to (
S48
,
S52
) and (
S54
,
S55
) have been obtained in Refs.
He and Zhou 2019
;
Bougas et al. 2020
;
Decamp et al. 2018
for fermions and bosons in quasi-1D and quasi-2D traps. They are thus valid beyond the virial expansion as can be easily shown following the method of Werner and Castin
Werner and Castin 2012a
;
Werner and Castin 2012b
.
We sketch the proof here.

Standard contact theory assumes that when two particles are close to each other (
b\ll r_{ij}\ll d
with
b
the range of the interaction potential,
d
the typical interparticle distance, and
r_{ij}=|{\bf r}_{ij}|=|{\bf r}_{i}-{\bf r}_{j}|
), the many-body wavefunctions take the asymptotic form

\Psi({\bf r}_{1},\ldots,{\bf r}_{N})\simeq\psi^{(s)}_{0}({\bf r}_{ij}){\cal A}_{ij}({\bf R}_{ij},({\bf r}_{k})_{k\neq i,j}),

(S56)

where
\psi^{(s)}_{0}({\bf r}_{ij})
is the zero-energy scattering state. Since the range
b
of the interaction potential and the 3D
s
-wave scattering length
a_{3}
are much smaller than the harmonic oscillator length
\ell_{z}
,
\psi^{(s)}_{0}({\bf r})
varies as
1/r-1/a_{3}
when
b\ll r\ll\ell_{z},d
, as for an isotropic 3D system. Normalizing
\psi^{(s)}_{0}({\bf r})
such that
\psi^{(s)}_{0}({\bf r})\simeq 1/r-1/a_{3}
in that limit, the contact of the state
\Psi
is then given by

C_{\Psi}=32\pi^{2}\sum_{i<j}\int\prod_{k\neq i,j}d^{3}r_{k}\int d{\bf R}_{ij}|{\cal A}_{ij}({\bf R}_{ij},({\bf r}_{k})_{k\neq i,j}|^{2},

(S57)

which, thanks to the normalization of
\psi^{(s)}_{0}
, is related to the energy
E_{\Psi}
of the state
\Psi
by
C_{\Psi}=8\pi m\frac{\partial E_{\Psi}}{\partial(1/a_{3})}
, and the pair distribution function behaves as
g_{\Psi}({\bf r})=[C_{\Psi}/(4\pi)^{2}](1/r-1/a_{3})^{2}
for
b\ll r\ll\ell_{z},d
. Averaging over the states with a Boltzmann weight, we recover the thermodynamic contact discussed in the main text.

In the quasi-2D case, there are two points to consider. First, the zero-energy limit must be taken carefully (since the
T
matrix vanishes at zero energy). Second, when
\ell_{z}\ll d
, the short distance behavior will cross-over from a 3D to a 2D behavior depending on whether
r_{ij}\ll\ell_{z}
or
r_{ij}\gg\ell_{z}
(in the opposite regime,
\ell_{z}\gg d
, only the 3D behavior is visible at short distances
r_{ij}\ll d
).

Focusing on the quasi-2D regime
T\ll\omega_{z}
in the harmonic trap, the short-distance behavior of
\psi^{(s)}_{0}({\bf r})
is obtained from that of
\psi_{{\bf k},0}({\bf r})
in the limit
|{\bf k}|\to 0
, see Eq. (
S49
). In the short-distance limit, the symmetrization only changes the wavefunction normalization by a factor
\sqrt{2}
. As this factor is unimportant here (it will be absorbed in
{\cal A}
and is the same in the 3D and 2D scattering regimes), we discard it.

In the 3D scattering regime, we can use Eq. (
S47
) with
n=0
and
|{\bf k}|\to 0
.
Therefore, to obtain a correctly normalized
\psi^{(s)}_{0}({\bf r})
, we need to normalize
\psi_{{\bf k},0}({\bf r})
by a factor
\mathcal{N}=-\frac{m}{4\pi L}\phi^{-}_{0}(0)t^{R}(\epsilon^{-}_{{\bf k},0})
. We note that this normalization vanishes at low energies, as expected for a system which is effectively 2D, see Appendix A in
Werner and Castin 2012a
.

In the 2D scattering regime,
\rho\gg\ell_{z}
, we can use Eq. (
S51
), and the properly normalized scattering state (using the factor
\mathcal{N}
computed above) reads

\psi^{(s)}_{0}({\bf r})\simeq-2\phi^{-}_{0}(z)\phi^{-}_{0}(0)^{*}\left[\log(\rho/a_{2})+\ldots\right]

(S58)

and is finite at zero energy.
To summarize, we find that when two particles are close to each other, the many-body wavefunction behaves as

\Psi({\bf r}_{1},\ldots,{\bf r}_{N})={\cal A}_{ij}({\bf R}_{ij},({\bf r}_{k})_{k\neq i,j})\times\begin{cases}\left(\frac{1}{r_{ij}}-\frac{1}{a_{3}}\right)&\text{if }b\ll r_{ij}\ll\ell_{z},d,\\
-2\phi^{-}_{0}(0)^{*}\phi^{-}_{0}(z_{ij})\log(\rho_{ij}/a_{2})&\text{if }\ell_{z}\ll\rho_{ij}\ll d,\end{cases}

(S59)

with
z_{ij}=z_{i}-z_{j}
and
\rho_{ij}=|\boldsymbol{\rho}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\rho}_{j}|
. Since
|\phi_{0}^{-}(0)|^{2}=1/\sqrt{2\pi}\ell_{z}
, this implies that the pair distribution function of an arbitrary many-body state
\Psi
behaves as

g_{\Psi}({\bf r})=\begin{cases}\frac{C_{\Psi}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{a_{3}}\right)^{2}&\text{if }b\ll r\ll\ell_{z},d,\\
\frac{C_{\Psi}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\ell_{z}}|\phi^{-}_{0}(z)|^{2}\left(\frac{\ln(\rho/a_{2})}{2\pi}\right)^{2}&\text{if }\ell_{z}\ll\rho\ll d.\end{cases}

(S60)

By integrating over
z
we recover the usual expression of the 2D case with a contact
C_{\Psi}/\sqrt{2\pi}\ell_{z}
. This generalizes the conclusion drawn from the virial expansion in Sec. II.C.

The contact varies roughly as the square of the density and the latter changes by several orders of magnitude between the low-density and superfluid regimes. It is therefore convenient to normalize the contact using the density. In Ref. [14] and in the main text, the mean-field contact
C_{0}/L^{2}=4(2\pi)^{3/2}\bar{n}^{2}a_{3}^{2}/\ell_{z}
is used for this purpose. It could be argued that it is more natural to use the Bogoliubov contact (
S16
), since
C/C_{\rm Bog}
tends to one in the deep superfluid regime
{\cal D}\gg 1
.

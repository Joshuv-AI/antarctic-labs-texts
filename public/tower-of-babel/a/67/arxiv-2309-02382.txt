Beauty And The Beast Part 2: Apprehending The Missing Supercurrent
Gregory W. Moore, Ranveer Kumar Singh

Abstract
. The Moonshine module is a c = 24 c=24 conformal field theory (CFT) whose automorphism group is the Monster group. It was argued by Dixon, Ginsparg, and Harvey in [ 14 ] that there exists a spin lift of the Moonshine CFT with superconformal symmetry. Reference [ 14 ] did not provide an explicit construction of a superconformal current. The present paper fills that gap. In fact, we will construct several superconformal currents in a spin lift of the Moonshine CFT using techniques developed in [ 36 ] . In particular, our construction relies on error correcting codes.

We start with the Leech torus theory - the holomorphic conformal field theory (CFT) described in Subsection
3.1
based on the Leech lattice. Let
\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}
be the Leech lattice, considered as a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{24}
, with quadratic form inherited from the standard
\mathbb{R}^{24}

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
(see Subsection
A.3

X^{i}(z),~i=1,\dots,24
with target space the Leech torus
\mathbb{T}^{24}:=\mathbb{R}^{24}/\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}.
The quotient
\mathbb{R}^{24}/\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}
is defined by the equivalence relation is

(1.1)

\bm{x}\sim\bm{x}^{\prime}\iff\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}=2\pi\lambda

for some
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}
. The field
X^{i}
is expanded as

(1.2)

X^{i}(z)=q^{i}+ip^{i}\ln z+i\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{a^{j}_{n}}{n}z^{-n}.

The modes satisfy the commutation relations

(1.3)

[q^{i},p^{j}]=i\delta^{ij},\quad[a^{i}_{n},a^{j}_{m}]=n\delta^{ij}\delta_{n+m,0}.

We also require that
a^{j\dagger}_{n}=a^{j}_{-n}
. For each
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}
we construct the Fock space
\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}
over the highest weight vector
|\lambda\rangle
defined by
a^{i}_{n}|\lambda\rangle=0
for
n>0
and
p^{i}|\lambda\rangle=\lambda^{i}|\lambda\rangle,
see Subsection
3.1
for details. The Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}})
is then spanned by the Fock spaces. The stress tensor is given by

(1.4)

T(z)=-\frac{1}{2}:\partial_{z}X\cdot\partial_{z}X:

where the dot indicates contraction of
i
index using
\delta_{ij}
.
The Virasoro modes are given by

(1.5)

\begin{split}&L_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{n-m}\cdot a_{m},\quad n\neq 0\\
&L_{0}=\frac{1}{2}p^{2}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{-n}\cdot a_{n}.\end{split}

They satisfy the Virasoro algebra with central charge
24
.
The torus partition function is then given by

(1.6)

Z_{\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}}(q)=\text{Tr}q^{L_{0}-c/24}=\frac{\Theta_{\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}}(q)}{\eta^{24}(q)}=\frac{1}{q}+24+196884q+\dots=J(q)+24,\quad q=e^{2\pi i\tau}

with
J(q)=j(q)-744
where
j(q)=1/q+744+\dots
is Klein’s famous weight-zero modular function generating the field of modular functions and
\tau
is the complex moduli of the torus. In particular
J(q)
has no constant term.

One can give a field theory description of the theory as follows: Consider fields
\mathcal{X}^{i}(z,\bar{z})
satisfying the half lattice periodicity

(1.7)

\mathcal{X}^{i}\sim\mathcal{X}^{i}+2\pi(\lambda^{i}/2),\quad\lambda=(\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda^{24})\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}.

Let
\{e_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{24}
be a generating set of
\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}
so that
\lambda=n^{\mu}e_{\mu}
with
n^{\mu}\in\mathbb{Z}
for
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}.
We then start with the field theory action

(1.8)

S=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^{2}z\left(\partial\mathcal{X}^{i}\bar{\partial}\mathcal{X}^{i}+B_{ij}\partial\mathcal{X}^{i}\bar{\partial}\mathcal{X}^{j}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^{2}z\left(g_{\mu\nu}\partial\mathcal{X}^{\mu}\bar{\partial}\mathcal{X}^{\nu}+B_{\mu\nu}\partial\mathcal{X}^{\mu}\bar{\partial}\mathcal{X}^{\nu}\right),

where
g_{\mu\nu}=\langle e_{\mu},e_{\nu}\rangle
,
\mathcal{X}^{i}=(e_{\mu})^{i}\mathcal{X}^{\mu}
,
B_{ij}
is a constant antisymmetric B-field and
B_{\mu\nu}=B_{ij}(e_{\mu})^{i}(e_{\nu})^{j}
. In general, the partition function for this field theory depends on both the holomorphic and antiholomorphic coordinates
q=e^{2\pi i\tau},\bar{q}=e^{2\pi i\bar{\tau}}
. If we require

(1.9)

B_{\mu\nu}\equiv\langle e_{\mu},e_{\nu}\rangle~\bmod~2,

then the partition function takes the factorised form

(1.10)

Z(q,\bar{q})=\left|\frac{1}{\eta^{24}}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}}q^{\frac{\langle\lambda,\lambda\rangle}{2}}\right|^{2},

where
\eta
is the Dedekind eta function. The Hilbert space of the theory also decomposes as a tensor product of holomorphic and antiholomorphic sectors. The restriction to the holomorphic sector gives us the Leech torus theory in the field theory description. We can relate the on-shell fields to the
field
X^{i}(z)
of the algebraic description above via
\mathcal{X}^{i}(z,\bar{z})=\frac{1}{2}(X^{i}(z)+X^{i}(\bar{z}))

The Monster CFT is constructed as a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-orbifold of the Leech torus CFT. To construct this orbifold CFT, we gauge the global

(1.11)

\theta X^{i}\theta^{-1}=-X^{i},\quad\theta a^{i}_{n}\theta^{-1}=-a^{i}_{n},\quad\theta|\lambda\rangle=|-\lambda\rangle

where
\theta
is the generator of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

X^{i}
on the orbifold
\mathbb{T}^{24}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
where
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
acts on
\mathbb{T}^{24}
as
\bm{x}\mapsto-\bm{x}

\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}/\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}

form a group isomorphic to
T_{2}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}):=\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}/2\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}
.
The B-field defines a bilinear form
\varepsilon

T_{2}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}})

(1.12)

\varepsilon(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=e^{\pi i\sum_{i<j}B_{ij}(\lambda_{1})^{i}(\lambda_{2})^{j}}.

From eq. (
1.9

(1.13)

\frac{\varepsilon(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})}{\varepsilon(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1})}=(-1)^{\langle\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\rangle}.

Following the standard rules of gauge theory, the Hilbert space of the orbifold theory is a direct sum of spaces associated with the trivial and nontrivial

\mathscr{H}^{+}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}})

To construct the twisted sector we start with fields
X^{i}(z)
on
\mathbb{T}^{24}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
satisfying the antiperiodic boundary condition

(1.14)

X^{i}(e^{2\pi i}z)=-X^{i}(z)~(\bmod~\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}).

These fields have mode expansion of the form

(1.15)

X^{i}(z)=\tilde{q}^{i}+i\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{c^{i}_{k}}{k}z^{-k}

where the modes satisfy the same algebra as eq. (
1.3

The unbroken translational symmetry is realised in the quantum theory via the representation of a central extension of
T_{2}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}})
, as is often the case in quantum theory.
In the present case the central extension is the Heisenberg extension and fits in an exact sequence:

(1.16)

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}\longrightarrow\mathscr{H}(T_{2}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}))\xrightarrow{~~-~~}T_{2}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}})\longrightarrow 0

where
-

\varepsilon

1.12
)). We may choose a section
T:T_{2}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}})\longrightarrow\mathscr{H}(T_{2}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}))
such that

(1.17)

\begin{split}&T([\lambda_{1}])T([\lambda_{2}])=\varepsilon(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})T([\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}])\\
\end{split}

The commutator function is given by eq. (
1.13
).
There is a unique unitary irreducible real representation of the Heisenberg group
\mathscr{H}(T_{2}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}))

\mathcal{S}

Note that

One can extend the action of the involution

(1.19)

\theta c^{i}_{k}\theta^{-1}=-c^{i}_{k},\quad\theta\chi_{0}=-\chi_{0},\quad\chi_{0}\in\mathcal{S}.

Thus

(1.20)

\mathscr{H}_{\text{FLM}}:=\mathscr{H}^{+}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}})\oplus\mathscr{H}_{T}^{+}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}}).

The vertex operators are defined precisely in Subsection

This theory has the famous property that the automorphism group of the operator product algebra is the Monster group.

2.1.
Definition of a Vertex Operator Algebra

We begin with the definition of a vertex operator algebra.

Definition 2.1
.

A
vertex operator algebra
(VOA) is a tuple
3
3

3

What we call a vertex operator algebra is called a conformal field theory in
[
19
]
. In the physics literature, a VOA is the
chiral algebra
of a CFT and the full CFT is
constructed
from the representations of the VOA, see Subsection

for the definition of representation of a VOA. It is in this sense that we will use the word vertex operator algebra and conformal field theories in this paper. See

,

]
for more details on this viewpoint.

\left(\mathscr{H},\mathscr{F},\mathbf{V},|0\rangle,\psi_{L}\right)
where
\mathscr{H}
is a Hilbert space of states,
\mathscr{F}
a dense subspace called the
Fock space
and
\mathbf{V}
a set of linear operators
on
\mathscr{H}
called
vertex operators

V(\psi,z)
which are linear in
\psi
and depend meromorphically on
z\in\mathbb{C}
. They in one-to-one correspondence with the states
\psi\in\mathscr{F}
. The product of vertex operators
V(\psi_{1},z_{1})V(\psi_{2},z_{2})\cdots
is defined,
a priori
, only in the region
|z_{1}|>|z_{2}|>\dots
.
There are two special states in
\mathscr{F}
, the
vacuum

|0\rangle
and a
conformal state

\psi_{L}.
The first five properties listed below must be satisfied. If in addition the last property (6) is satisfied, we call it an
Hermitian vertex operator algebra

(1)

The
moments
of the vertex operator of
\psi_{L}
is given by by

(2.1)

V\left(\psi_{L},z\right)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L_{n}z^{-n-2}

and satisfies the Virasoro algebra

(2.2)

\left[L_{m},L_{n}\right]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{c}{12}m\left(m^{2}-1\right)\delta_{m,-n}

with
L_{n}^{\dagger}=L_{-n}

(2)

The vertex operators satisfy

(2.3)

(i)\quad V(\psi,z)|0\rangle=e^{zL_{-1}}\psi\quad(ii)\quad V(\psi,z)V(\phi,w)=V(\phi,w)V(\psi,z)

The relation (ii) is called the
bosonic locality relation
and is interpreted as follows: the left hand side is
a priori
well defined for
|z|>|w|
and the right hand side is well defined for
|w|>|z|
. The locality relation means that the analytic continuation of the matrix elements of both sides to the whole

z,w=0,\infty

\mathbf{V}

\mathbf{V}
.

(3)

The operator

acts locally with respect to
\mathbf{V}
, that is
w^{L_{0}}V(\psi,z)w^{-L_{0}}
is local with respect to
\mathbf{V}
.

(4)

L_{0}
is diagonalizable and the spectrum of
L_{0}
is bounded below. The eigenvalues
h_{\psi}
of eigenstates
\psi
of
L_{0}
are called

.

(5)

The vacuum is the only
\mathfrak{su}(1,1)
invariant state in the theory,

the only state annihilated by
L_{0}
and

.

(6)

For all
\psi\in\mathscr{F}
, the vertex operator
V(e^{z^{*}L_{1}}z^{*-2L_{0}}\psi,1/z^{*})^{\dagger}

\mathbf{V}.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\left(\mathscr{H},\mathscr{F},\mathbf{V},|0\rangle,\psi_{L}\right)
be an Hermitian vertex operator algebra. Then the following statements hold:

(1)

We have that

(2.5)

\frac{d}{dz}V(\psi,z)=V(L_{-1}\psi,z).

This is called the translation property.

(2)

The conformal weights of an Hermitian VOA are non-negative integers.

(3)

Any conformal primary field
V(\psi,z)
with
\psi
an eigenstate of
L_{0}
with conformal weight
h_{\psi}
satisfies the commutator

(2.6)

[L_{n},V(\psi,z)]=z^{n}\left(z\frac{d}{dz}+(n+1)h_{\psi}\right)V(\psi,z).

Equivalently, the moments satisfy

(2.7)

[L_{m},V_{n}(\psi)]=((h_{\psi}-1)m-n)V_{n+m}(\psi).

For a quasi-primary field, eq. (
2.6
) and eq. (
2.7
) is true only for
L_{0},L_{\pm 1}
.

(4)

The
operator product expansion
(OPE) of any two vertex operators
V(\psi,z)
and
V(\phi,w)
corresponding to eigenstates of
L_{0}
is given by

(2.8)

\begin{split}V(\psi,z)V(\phi,w)&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(z-w)^{-n-h_{\psi}-h_{\phi}}V(\phi_{n},w)\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(z-w)^{-h_{\phi_{n}}-h_{\psi}-h_{\phi}}V(\phi_{n},w),\end{split}

where

(2.9)

\phi_{n}:=V_{h_{\phi}-n}(\psi)\phi.

Equivalently,

(2.10)

V(\psi,z)V(\phi,w)=V(V(\psi,z-w)\phi,w)~.

(5)

For each
\psi\in\mathscr{F}
, the locality of
V(e^{z^{*}L_{1}}z^{*-2L_{0}}\psi,1/z^{*})^{\dagger}
implies that we have an antilinear map

(2.11)

\psi\longmapsto\overline{\psi},

where

(2.12)

V(e^{z^{*}L_{1}}z^{*-2L_{0}}\psi,1/z^{*})^{\dagger}=V(\overline{\psi},z)

so that

(2.13)

\overline{\psi}:=\lim_{z\to 0}V(e^{z^{*}L_{1}}z^{*-2L_{0}}\psi,1/z^{*})^{\dagger}|0\rangle.

(6)

For a quasi-primary vertex operator, the moments satisfy
V_{n}(\psi)^{\dagger}=V_{-n}(\overline{\psi}).

Remark 2.1
.

Usually primary fields in a VOA are defined based on their OPE with the stress tensor. A field
\phi(z)
is called a primary field of conformal dimension
h
if it has the following OPE with
T(z)
:

(2.14)

T(z)\phi(w)=\frac{h}{(z-w)^{2}}\phi(w)+\frac{\partial_{w}\phi(w)}{z-w}+\text{ hol. }

where “hol” means a holomorphic function of
z,w
. Using the standard contour integral manipulations, one can show that this definition is equivalent to commutator eq. (
2.7
).

Definition 2.3
.

A sub-vertex operator algebra (sub-VOA) of a VOA
\mathscr{H}
is defined to be a subspace
\mathscr{I}\subset\mathscr{H}
such that

(1)

\mathscr{I}
is an invariant subspace for all the vertex operators
V(\phi,z)
for
\phi\in\mathscr{F}_{\mathscr{I}}:=\mathscr{F}\cap\mathscr{I}
.

(2)

\mathscr{I}
is invariant under the
\mathfrak{su}(1,1)
algebra spanned by
L_{\pm 1},L_{0}
.

(3)

\overline{\mathscr{F}_{\mathscr{I}}}:=\{\overline{\phi}:\phi\in\mathscr{F}_{\mathscr{I}}\}=\mathscr{F}_{\mathscr{I}}
.

One can show that the sub-VOA contains a conformal state and that
a sub-VOA of a (Hermitian) VOA is itself a (Hermitian) VOA.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that two VOAs
\mathscr{H}
and
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
with Fock spaces
\mathscr{F}
and
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
respectively and corresponding vertex operators
V(\psi,z)
and
V^{\prime}(\psi^{\prime},z)
are isomorphic is there exists a unitary map
u:\mathscr{H}\longrightarrow\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
such that

(2.15)

V^{\prime}(u\psi,z)=uV(\psi,z)u^{-1},\quad\psi\in\mathscr{F}.

An isomorphism
u:\mathscr{H}\longrightarrow\mathscr{H}
is called an automorphism.
The group of all automorphisms of a conformal field theory
\mathscr{H}
is called the automorphism group of the VOA.

If
u:\mathscr{H}\longrightarrow\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
is an isomorphism then

(2.16)

u|0\rangle=\left|0^{\prime}\right\rangle,u\psi_{L}=\psi_{L}^{\prime},

where
|0\rangle,\left|0^{\prime}\right\rangle
are the vacuum states and
\psi_{L}
,
\psi_{L}^{\prime}
are the conformal states in
\mathscr{H}
and
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
respectively.

2.2.
Representation of a Vertex Operator Algebra

Definition 2.5
.

A representation
(\mathbf{U},\mathscr{K})
of a vertex operator algebra
(\mathscr{H},\mathbf{V},\mathscr{F},|0\rangle,\psi_{L})
is a Hilbert space
\mathscr{K}
and a set of linear operators

meromorphic in

\psi\in\mathscr{F}
and for all

, we have:

(2.17)

U(\psi,z)U(\phi,\zeta)=U(V(\psi,z-\zeta)\phi,\zeta)

with
U(|0\rangle,z)\equiv\mathds{1}
.

The left hand side of eq. (
2.17
) is defined in the domain

while the right hand side of eq. (
2.17
) is defined in the domain

. So the equality in eq. (
2.17
) must be understood in the sense of analytic continuation of the matrix elements of the two sides as in the bosonic locality relation (ii) in eq. (
4.4
). One can show that the operators

are local in the sense of bosonic locality relation. Moreover, the modes of

satisfies the Virasoro algebra and

also satisfies the translation property as in eq. (

). For

let us define a meromorphic family of operators

Then we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
19
, Proposition 3.4]

The existence of a representation is equivalent to the existence of “intertwining” operators

for
\chi\in\mathscr{K}
(or a dense subspace of
\mathscr{K}
) satisfying

(2.19)

and

(2.20)

\lim_{\zeta\to 0}W(\chi,\zeta)|0\rangle=\chi.

2.3.
Extension of a Vertex Operator Algebra by a Real Hermitian
Representation

Definition 2.6
.

A representation

.

The above condition is equivalent to

Definition 2.7

Define the conjugate

\overline{W}(\chi_{1},\zeta)W(\chi_{2},z)=\overline{W}(\chi_{2},z)W(\chi_{1},\zeta).\end{split}

We have the following important theorem:

Theorem 2.1
.

[

, Proposition 4.4]

Suppose that the operator

Let

3.1.
Untwisted Vertex Operator Algebra

Consider the

We impose the commutation relations on the modes:

[q^{i},p^{j}]=i\delta^{ij},\quad[a^{i}_{n},a^{j}_{m}]=n\delta^{ij}\delta_{n+m,0}.

\langle\lambda|\lambda^{\prime}\rangle=\delta_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}

by creating states using

are positive integers

and

with the understanding that

when

\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}
for
\lambda\in\Lambda
. The Fock space of the VOA is defined as

(3.5)

\mathscr{F}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathscr{F}_{\lambda}.

We denote the Hilbert space by
\mathscr{H}(\Lambda)

For

given by eq. (
3.4

(3.7)

\begin{split}&:a_{n}^{i}a_{m}^{j}:=:a_{m}^{j}\,a_{n}^{i}:=\begin{cases}a_{m}^{j}\,a_{n}^{i}&m\leq n,\\
a_{n}^{i}\,a_{m}^{j}&m\geq n,\end{cases}\\
&:a^{i}_{m}p^{j}:=:p^{j}\,a_{m}^{i}:=a_{m}^{i}p^{j},\\
&:q^{i}\,a_{n}^{j}:=:a_{n}^{j}x^{i}:=a_{n}^{j}q^{i},\\
&:q^{i}p^{j}:=:p^{j}\,q^{i}:=q^{i}p^{j}.\end{split}

In particular

(3.8)

:\exp~\{i\lambda\cdot X(z)\}:=\exp~\left\{i\lambda\cdot X_{<}(z)\right\}e^{i\lambda\cdot q}z^{i\lambda\cdot p}\exp~\left\{i\lambda\cdot X_{>}(z)\right\}

(3.9)

X_{\lessgtr}^{j}(z)=i\sum_{n\lessgtr 0}\frac{a_{n}^{j}}{n}z^{-n}.

The operator

(3.10)

\widehat{\sigma}_{\lambda}\widehat{\sigma}_{\mu}=(-1)^{\lambda\cdot\mu}\widehat{\sigma}_{\mu}\widehat{\sigma}_{\lambda}

The vacuum state is

corresponding to

It can be shown that the energy momentum tensor

(3.13)

T(z)=V(\psi_{L},z)=-\frac{1}{2}:\partial_{z}X\cdot\partial_{z}X:

\begin{split}L_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{n-m}\cdot a_{m},\quad n\neq 0,\\

In Physics, eq. (

4.1.
Spin Lifts Of A Conformal Field Theory

Given a modular invariant holomorphic conformal field theory
\mathscr{H}
with a non-anomalous

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
automorphism, there is a natural way to construct a spin-conformal field theory. The essential idea goes back to the GSO projection applied to string worldsheet superconformal field theories to obtain a consistent string theory
[
31
]
and played an important role in discussions of bosonization on arbitrary Riemann surfaces
[
1
,
2
]
. More general versions of the construction of
[

2
]

[

,
39
,
40
,
41
,
43
,
54
]
. This spin-conformal field theory is called a spin lift of

.
The idea is to couple the non-anomalous
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
automorphism of the CFT to an invertible topological field theory sensitive to spin structure known as the Arf
theory
[
12
]
, and then gauge the diagonal global
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
automorphism of the product theory. The resulting theory depends on the spin structure and in general the vertex operators do not satisfy the standard locality conditions of a super-vertex operator algebra. Nevertheless the GSO projection of the spin lift reproduces the original CFT. The spin lift also has an emergent
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry which we will identify with the fermion number symmetry
(-1)^{F}

4.1.1.
Formal description of gauging a discrete symmetry

Let us recall some well-known general facts. We are following
[
47
, Section 7.1]
.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a
d
-dimensional quantum field theory, that is, a functor
\mathcal{F}:\text{Bord}_{d}\longrightarrow\text{Vect}
from the bordism category, whose objects are
(d-1)
-dimensional manifolds (possibly with extra structure) and whose morphisms are
d
-dimensional oriented bordisms, to the linear category of vector spaces and linear maps. We say that
\mathcal{F}
has global symmetry

when, for every
(d-1)
-manifold

\mathscr{H}(Y):=\mathcal{F}(Y)

G
and for any bordism
X:Y_{1}\longrightarrow Y_{2}

\mathcal{F}(X):\mathscr{H}(Y_{1})\longrightarrow\mathscr{H}(Y_{2})
, called the transition amplitude, is
G
-equivariant. Gauging the symmetry group involves two steps:

(1)

Construct the “equivariant theory” by enlarging the number of fields.
In the present example our new fields are a principal
G
-bundle with connection. In our case

is a finite group so the bundle has a unique connection.

8
8

8

We use the term “field” in the generalized sense explained in Section 3 of

[
24
]
. This step is the mathematical version of “coupling the theory to background gauge fields” in physics.

(2)

“Sum” or “integrate” over the principal
G
-bundles with connection with a suitable measure.

To construct the equivariant theory, we define a new bordism category
\text{Bord}_{d}^{G}
whose objects are
(d-1)
-manifolds
Y

G
-bundles
P\to Y
with connection and morphisms are
d
-dimensional bordisms

also equipped with principal
G
-bundles with connection
P^{\prime}\to X
such that the restriction of
P^{\prime}\to X
to the ingoing boundary is isomorphic to
P_{1}\to Y_{1}
and the
restriction to the outgoing boundary is isomorphic to
P_{2}\to Y_{2}
.
Note that
\text{Bord}_{d}
can be regarded as a subcategory of
\text{Bord}_{d}^{G}
with trivial bundles associated to the objects and bordisms.
Now, the equivariant theory
\mathcal{F}^{\text{eq}}:\text{Bord}_{d}^{G}\longrightarrow\text{Vect}
assigns to every object

\mathscr{H}^{\text{eq}}(P):=\mathscr{H}(P\to Y)
and for every bordism

and

such that when

is the trivial bundle

and for trivial bordism

between trivial objects

Once we have the equivariant theories we can perform the second step of gauging, that is sum over bundles with connection. We need to take into account the fact that the isomorphic bundles have the same space of states. More precisely, if we have a bundle isomorphism
f:P\longrightarrow Q
of bundles

(4.21)

Appendix A
Codes And Lattices

Linear codes and their associated lattices play a central role in our discussion of the superconformal symmetry in the Monster module. We collect the definitions and some preliminary results about codes and lattices in this section.

A.1.
Binary linear codes

Definition A.1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{2}
be the field with two elements
0,1
.

(1)

A
binary linear code

\mathcal{C}
of length
n
is a vector subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. If the dimension of
\mathcal{C}
is
k
then it is called an
[n,k]
binary code. The elements of the code are called
codewords
.

(2)

Two binary linear codes are said to be
equivalent
if they are isomorphic as vector spaces.

(3)

A
k\times n
matrix with rows being the basis vectors of
\mathcal{C}
is called a generator matrix of
\mathcal{C}
.

(4)

The inner product of codewords
w,w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
is defined as

(A.1)

w\cdot w^{\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\cdot w^{\prime}_{i}~(\bmod~2).

where
w=(w_{1},\dots,w_{n}),w^{\prime}=(w^{\prime}_{1},\dots,w^{\prime}_{n})
.
The code
dual
to
\mathcal{C}
is denotes by
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
and is defined as

(A.2)

\mathcal{C}^{\perp}:=\{w\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}:w\cdot w^{\prime}=0~\forall~w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}\}.

The code is called
self-orthogonal
if
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
and
self-dual
if
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}=\mathcal{C}
.

(5)

The
(Hamming) weight
of a codeword
w
is defined as the number of nonzero components in the codeword and is denoted by wt(
w
). The code is called
even
and
doubly even
if the weight of every codeword is divisible by 2 and 4 respectively.

(6)

The
Hamming distance

d_{H}(w,w^{\prime})
of two codewords
w,w^{\prime}
is defined as the number of places in which the codewords differ. More precisely,
d_{H}(w,w^{\prime})=\text{wt}(w-w^{\prime}).
The
(Hamming) distance
of the code is defined

(A.3)

d_{H}(\mathcal{C}):=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}w,w^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}\\
w\neq w^{\prime}\end{subarray}}d_{H}(w,w^{\prime}).

With the notation
d_{H}:=d_{H}(\mathcal{C})
, we call
\mathcal{C}
an
[n,k,d_{H}]
code.

(7)

The
automorphism group
Aut
(\mathcal{C})
of the code
\mathcal{C}
is a subset of the permutation group
S_{n}
which fixes the code as a set.

By standard row echelon form algorithm, it is easy to see that for any
[n,k]
code
\mathcal{C}
, there is an equivalent code with generator matrix
G
of the form

(A.4)

G=[\mathds{1}_{k},A]

where
\mathds{1}_{k}
is the
k\times k
identity matrix and
A
is some
k\times(n-k)
matrix. The dual code
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
is an
[n,n-k]
code with generator matrix

Codes can be used to construct lattices. In this section we describe the construction of lattices from binary and quaternary codes. We begin by defining Euclidean lattices.

Definition A.4

We will closely follow

Lemma B.1
.

For any untwisted state
\psi
, we have

(B.1)

w^{L_{0}^{T}}V_{T}(\psi,z)w^{-L_{0}^{T}}=w^{h_{\psi}}V_{T}(\psi,zw).

Proof.

Using eq. (
2.18
), we have

w^{L_{0}^{T}}V_{T}(\psi,z)w^{-L_{0}^{T}}\chi=w^{-h_{\chi}}w^{L_{0}^{T}}e^{-zL_{-1}^{T}}W(\chi,-z)\psi,

where
\chi
is a general twisted quasi-primary state.
We now use the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula eq. (
4.18
)
to write

w^{L_{0}^{T}}e^{zL_{-1}^{T}}=e^{L_{0}^{T}\ln w}e^{zL_{-1}^{T}}.

Using the fact that
[L_{0}^{T},L_{-1}^{T}]=L_{-1}^{T}
, we see that the above theorem implies

e^{L_{0}^{T}\ln w}e^{zL_{-1}^{T}}=e^{\exp(\ln w)L_{-1}^{T}}w^{L_{0}^{T}}.

Thus we get

(B.2)

w^{L_{0}^{T}}e^{zL_{-1}^{T}}=e^{zwL_{-1}^{T}}w^{L_{0}^{T}}.

&=(-4u^{-1})^{-\lambda^{2}}(z\zeta)^{-\frac{1}{2}h_{\psi}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}e^{\lambda\cdot\rho_{0}}\left\langle\lambda\left|\exp\left(\widetilde{\lambda}a+\frac{1}{2}aTa\right)\left(e^{\rho^{<}a^{\dagger}}e^{\rho^{>}a}\right)\right.\right.\\
&\hskip 170.71652pt\times\left.\left.\left.\exp\left(\widetilde{\lambda}a^{\dagger}+\frac{1}{2}a^{\dagger}Ta^{\dagger}\right)\right|\lambda\right\rangle\right|_{\{\rho_{a}^{k}=0\}}\end{split}

\begin{split}\left\langle\lambda\left|e^{A(z)}V(\psi,w)e^{\overline{A}(\zeta)}\right|\lambda\right\rangle&=(-4u^{-1})^{-\lambda^{2}}(z\zeta)^{-\frac{1}{2}h_{\psi}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}e^{\lambda\cdot\rho_{0}}\left\langle 0\left|\exp\left(\widetilde{\lambda}a+\frac{1}{2}aTa\right)\left(e^{\rho^{<}a^{\dagger}}e^{\rho^{>}a}\right)\right.\right.\\

Normal ordering the oscillators, we can write

(B.48)

The vacuum expectation value on the right hand side can be evaluated using coherent state techniques and has been done in
[

(B.49)

\begin{split}\left\langle 0\left|e^{\widetilde{\lambda}a}e^{\frac{1}{2}aTa}e^{\rho^{<}a^{\dagger}}e^{\rho^{>}a}\right.\right.&\left.\left.e^{\frac{1}{2}a^{\dagger}Ta^{\dagger}}e^{\bar{\lambda}a^{\dagger}}\right|0\right\rangle\\

The determinant can be evaluated using group theory. The result is

(B.53)

Here

(B.54)

Because we are working with

Also we have
[

\exp\left(\widetilde{\lambda}(1-T)^{-1}\widetilde{\lambda}\right)=(4u^{-1})^{\lambda^{2}}q^{\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}}.

(B.56)

(B.57)

\displaystyle\left\langle\lambda\left|e^{A(z)}V(\psi,\omega)e^{\bar{A}(\zeta)}\right|\lambda\right\rangle=

\displaystyle(z\zeta)^{-\frac{1}{2}h_{\psi}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}\left(1-u^{2}\right)^{-r/8}\Theta_{3}(q^{2})^{-r}q^{\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}}\left.e^{\lambda\cdot t(u,y)}e^{h(u,y)}\right|_{\left\{\rho_{a}^{k}\right\}=0}.

(B.58)

\displaystyle\langle\mu|W(\chi,z)V(\psi,w)\overline{W}(\varphi,\zeta)|\rho\rangle=

\displaystyle\times\left.\left(1-u^{2}\right)^{-r/8}\Theta_{3}(q^{2})^{-r}q^{\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}e^{\lambda\cdot t(u,y)}e^{h(u,y)}\right|_{\left\{\rho^{k}_{a}\right\}=0}.

We have kept the

To facilitate the exchange of

We begin with the relation
[
18

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma_{ab}^{\dagger}\gamma_{cd}=2^{12}\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}

to transform the inner products in eq. (
B.26

(B.60)

\begin{split}\left\langle\mu\left|e_{\lambda}\right|\chi\right\rangle\left\langle\overline{\varphi}\left|e_{\lambda}^{\dagger}\right|\varrho\right\rangle&=(e^{-i\pi h_{\varphi}})^{*}\left\langle\mu\left|e_{\lambda}\right|\chi\right\rangle\left\langle\theta M\varphi^{*}\left|e_{\lambda}^{\dagger}\right|\varrho\right\rangle\\
&=-e^{i\pi h_{\varphi}}\left(\mu^{\dagger}\gamma_{\lambda}\chi\right)\left(\varphi^{T}M^{*}\gamma_{\lambda}^{\dagger}\varrho\right)\\
&=-2^{-12}e^{i\pi h_{\varphi}}\sum_{\gamma_{\nu}\in\Gamma}\left(\mu^{\dagger}\gamma_{\nu}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\nu}\gamma_{\lambda}\chi\right)\left(\varphi^{T}M^{*}\gamma_{\lambda}^{\dagger}\varrho\right)\\
&=-2^{-12}e^{i\pi h_{\varphi}}\sum_{\gamma_{\nu}\in\Gamma}\left(\mu^{\dagger}\gamma_{\nu}^{\dagger}\gamma_{\nu}\gamma_{\lambda}\chi\right)\left((M^{*}\gamma_{\lambda}^{\dagger}\varrho)^{T}\varphi\right)\\
&=-2^{-12}e^{i\pi h_{\varphi}}\sum_{\gamma_{\nu}\in\Gamma}\left(\mu^{\dagger}\gamma_{\nu}^{\dagger}\varphi\right)\left((\varrho^{T}\gamma_{\lambda}^{*}M^{*}\gamma_{\nu}\gamma_{\lambda}\chi\right)\\
&=-2^{-12}e^{i\pi h_{\varphi}}\sum_{\gamma_{\nu}\in\Gamma}\left(\mu^{\dagger}\gamma_{\nu}^{\dagger}\varphi\right)\left((\varrho^{T}M^{*}\gamma_{\lambda}\gamma_{\nu}\gamma_{\lambda}\chi\right)\\

\displaystyle\times\left.\left(1-u^{2}\right)^{-r/8}\Theta_{3}(q^{2})^{-r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}e^{h(u,y)}\Gamma(q,\nu)\right|_{\left\{\rho_{a}^{k}\right\}=0}

To complete the proof of locality, we need to promote the states

Lemma B.4
.

The locality relation

\chi,\varphi
twisted ground states.

Proof.

Direct implication is obvious. We prove the converse.
Since we already have the locality of
\widetilde{V}(\psi,z)\widetilde{V}(\chi,w)=\widetilde{V}(\chi,w)\widetilde{V}(\psi,z)
for twisted state
\chi
and untwisted state
\psi
, we have the duality relation

(B.77)

\widetilde{V}(\psi,z)\widetilde{V}(\chi,w)=\widetilde{V}(\widetilde{V}(\psi,z-w)\chi,w).

Note that by Proposition
5.1
, the right hand side of from eq. (
B.77
) has square root singularity when
\chi\in\mathscr{H}_{T}^{-}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}})
and
\psi\in\mathscr{H}^{-}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}})
. Fortunately we do not have to use eq. (
B.77
) for
\psi\in\mathscr{H}^{-}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}})
. To prove this lemma, we only need
\psi\in\mathscr{H}^{+}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}})
.
Using explicit matrix forms of
\widetilde{V}
eq. (
5.17
) and eq. (
5.18
), we get

\begin{split}V(\psi,z)\overline{W}(\chi,w)=\overline{W}(V_{T}(\psi,z-w)\chi,w)\\
V_{T}(\psi,z)W(\chi,w)=W(V_{T}(\psi,z-w)\chi,w)\end{split}

Acting on
W(\chi,z)\overline{W}(\varphi,\zeta)|\varrho\rangle
by
V_{T}(\psi,w)
with
\psi\in\mathscr{H}^{+}(\Lambda_{\mathrm{L}})
and using the above relations and Lemma

, we can generalise
\chi
to arbitrary fermionic twisted state on both sides of eq. (
B.76
). This is because
V_{T}(\psi,z)

\theta\psi=-\psi
and preserve the parity if
\theta\psi=\psi
. Similarly acting with

on
W(\chi,z)\overline{W}(\varphi,\zeta)\varrho

\varphi
to arbitrary fermionic twisted state on both sides of eq. (
B.76

Stack Representation of Finitely Presented Heyting Pretoposes I
Lingyuan Ye

Abstract
This is the first of a series of papers on stack representation of finitely presented Heyting pretoposes. In this paper, we provide the first step by constructing a ( 2 , 1 ) (2,1) -site, which can be thought of as the site of finite Kripke frames, such that the ( 2 , 1 ) (2,1) -category of finitely presented Heyting pretoposes contravariantly embeds into the ( 2 , 1 ) (2,1) -topos of stacks on this ( 2 , 1 ) (2,1) -site. This provides an entry point to use categorical and higher sheaf-theoretic tools to study the properties of certain classes of intuitionistic first-order theories.



1
Introduction

In this paper we are interested in stack representation of finitely presented Heyting pretoposes. We shall comment immediately that by finitely presentable, we mean those Heyting pretoposes which are
complete
w.r.t.
finite models
on
finite Kripke frames
. Typical examples include those equivalent to a finite quotient of the free Heyting pretopos generated by a finite pretopos. A pretopos is
finite
if its
category of models
is essentially finite.

In particular, this notion does
not
coincide with those Heyting pretoposes generated by a first-order intuitionisitic theory with finitely many non-logical symbols and finitely many axioms. In general, such first-order intuitionistic theories will not be complete w.r.t. the particular class of finite models we are interested in. Towards the end of this section, we will comment on the reason why we are interested in this restricted class of Heyting pretoposes.

Categorically, this work can be thought of as a
categorification
of the sheaf representation of the category of finitely presented Heyting algebras described in the book
[
7
]
. We give a brief overview below.

The starting point of the sheaf representation of finitely presented Heyting algebras is the
duality
of finite distributive lattices and finite Heyting algebras as follows,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{HA}^{\operatorname{op}}_{\mathrm{f}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Por}_{\mathrm{f}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{DL}^{\operatorname{op}}_{\mathrm{f}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Pos}_{\mathrm{f}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

\mathbf{DL}_{\mathrm{f}}

\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{f}}
is a wide subcategory of
\mathbf{DL}_{\mathrm{f}}

\mathbf{Pos}_{\mathrm{f}}

\mathbf{Por}_{\mathrm{f}}

Concretely, the duality between finite posets and finite distributive lattices takes any poset

\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{fp}}(H,D(L))

This gives us a

\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{fp}}(H,D(-)):\mathbf{Por}_{\mathrm{f}}^{\operatorname{op}}\to\mathbf{Set},

J

\mathbf{Por}_{\mathrm{f}}

J

\mathbf{Por}_{\mathrm{f}}

As is well-known, categorification is an art rather than a science. But if we are careful enough, there will still be tracible links. Recall the indexation for higher categories, e.g. see
[
15

(r,s)

k

j

j>s
. An
(n,n)

n

(n,0)

n
-groupoid; while an
(n,n+1)

n

In this way, Heyting algebras can be thought of as
(0,1)
-Heyting pretoposes, and in full generality the collection of Heyting algebras should organise themselves into a
(1,2)

1

1

1

1

1
-category
\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{fp}}
actually suffices for the purpose of studying propositional intuitionistic logic. For instance, the interpolation property and Beth definability for propositional intuitionistic logic are concerned with

\mathbf{HA}_{\mathrm{fp}}
, e.g. see
[
16
]
, rather than lax or oplax colimits computed in the
(1,2)
-category of Heyting algebras.

Now the categorification we are working towards concerns increasing the first entry. We intend to study the collection of
(1,1)
-Heyting pretoposes, which are simply Heyting pretoposes in the usual 1-categorical sense. They organise themselves into a
2

(2,1)

(2,1)

\mathbf{HPt}_{\mathrm{fp}}

[

\mathbf{HPretopos}

Thus, our first task in Section

and

Heyting

.

2
Duality for Finite Pretoposes

As mentioned in the introduction, our first objective is to study a suitable notion of finite pretoposes. For us, the definition that works is the following:

Definition 2.1
.

A pretopos is
finite
iff its category of models is essentially finite, i.e. equivalent to a finite category.

It is then natural to ask whether all finite categories could be the category of models of some pretopos. The answer is negative due to the following simple observation:

Lemma 2.2
.

For any pretopos
\mathcal{D}
, its category of models
\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{D})
is closed under filtered colimits in
[\mathcal{D},\mathbf{Set}]
, thus in particular is Cauchy complete.

Proof.

The fact that
\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{D})
is closed under filtered colimits computed in
[\mathcal{D},\mathbf{Set}]
is well-known, e.g. see Chapter 5 of
[
1
]
. The splitting of idempotent is in particular a filtered colimit, thus
\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{D})
must be Cauchy complete.
∎

In fact, for finite categories, Cauchy completeness is the only obstacle, i.e. all Cauchy complete finite categories can arise as the category of models of a finite theory. Moreover, we obtain a duality result, where there is an equivalence

{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}^{\operatorname{op}}\simeq\mathbf{Pretopos}_{\mathrm{f}},

from the opposite 2-category of all Cauchy complete finite categories, to the 2-category of finite pretoposes. We prove this by proving the following duality

\mathbf{Pretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}^{\operatorname{op}}\simeq\mathbf{Topos}_{\mathrm{f}},

where
\mathbf{Topos}_{\mathrm{f}}
is the 2-category of
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}
-bounded toposes. The previous duality follows from this because from Corollary 2.2.23 in
[
9
]
we already know that the 2-category of finite Cauchy complete categories is equivalent to the 2-category of
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}
-bounded toposes. This latter result can be seen as a consequence of the following facts:

•

There is a general 2-fully faithful embedding of Cauchy complete categories into toposes with
essential
geometric morphism,

{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{EssTopos}.

•

In
[
8
]
, it has shown that any geometric morphism between
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}
-bounded toposes is
automatically essential
.

•

It can be shown that any
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}
-bounded topos is of the form
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
for some finite Cauchy complete category
\mathcal{I}
, by showing that any Grothendieck topology on finite Cauchy complete categories are so called
rigid
, thus is of presheaf type. Again, see Lemma 2.2.21 in
[
9
]
.

Section
2.1
will first provide an analysis of finite pretoposes, while Section
2.2
proceeds to prove the above mentioned duality result for finite pretoposes. By the equivalence
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}\simeq\mathbf{Topos}_{\mathrm{f}}
, we also study certain geometric aspects of finite Cauchy complete categories, notably that of geometric surjections, in Section
2.3
, which turns out to be crucial for the Grothendieck topology we will be constructing in the next section. Finally in Section
2.4
, we restrict the duality for finite pretoposes to one for finite Heyting pretoposes, and study certain properties of open functors, again in preparation for the next section.

2.1
Finite Categories as Models

To show every Cauchy complete finite category can arise as the category of models of some theory, the following observation is of vital importance:

Lemma 2.3
.

For any finite category
\mathcal{I}
, we have a canonical equivalence

{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathcal{I}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}\simeq\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{I}),

where
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathcal{I}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
is the
Cauchy completion
of
\mathcal{I}
, and
\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{I})
is its
ind-completion
.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that for a finite category, essentially the only non-trivial filtered colimit is the splitting of idempotency, thus
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathcal{I}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
already has all filtered colimits. For a proof, see
[
23
]
.
∎

In particular, Lemma
2.3
implies that if
\mathcal{I}
is already Cauchy complete, then
\mathcal{I}\simeq\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{I})
. Lemma
2.3
also allows us to compute the category of points of the copresheaf category on a finite category:

Lemma 2.4
.

For any finite category
\mathcal{I}
, there is an equivalence

\mathbf{Topos}(\mathbf{Set},[\mathcal{I},\mathbf{Set}])\simeq{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathcal{I}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}.

Proof.

We have the following sequence of equivalences,

\mathbf{Topos}(\mathbf{Set},[\mathcal{I},\mathbf{Set}])\simeq\mathrm{Flat}(\mathcal{I}^{\operatorname{op}},\mathbf{Set})\simeq\mathrm{Ind}(\mathcal{I})\simeq{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathcal{I}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}.

The first equivalence holds by Diaconescu’s theorem; the second equivalence is well-known; and the last equivalence is by Lemma
2.3
.
∎

In particular, this gives us a very simple way of determining what the classifying pretopos should be for finite categories: Let us write
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
for the functor category
[\mathcal{I},\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}]
,

Proposition 2.5
.

For any finite category
\mathcal{I}
, we have an equivalence

\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]\simeq\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathcal{I}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}],

such that

\mathrm{Mod}(\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}])\simeq\mathrm{Mod}(\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathcal{I}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}])\simeq{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathcal{I}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
is exactly the coherent objects in the Grothendieck topos
[\mathcal{I},\mathbf{Set}]
because
\mathcal{I}
is a finite category. Now we have a well-known equivalence

[\mathcal{I},\mathbf{Set}]\simeq[{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathcal{I}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}},\mathbf{Set}],

thus the equivalence restricts to their corresponding coherent objects

\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]\simeq\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathcal{I}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}].

Furthermore, since the copresheaf topos
[\mathcal{I},\mathbf{Set}]
is coherent, its category of points is equivalent to the category of models of the pretopos
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
, thus we have

\mathrm{Mod}(\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}])\simeq\mathbf{Topos}(\mathbf{Set},[\mathcal{I},\mathbf{Set}])\simeq{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathcal{I}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}.\qed

Thus for any category
\mathcal{I}
, it embeds into
\mathrm{Mod}(\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}])\simeq{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathcal{I}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
as follows: For any
x\in\mathcal{I}
, as a model it is simply the evaluation functor

x^{*}:\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]\to\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}},

which we view as precomposition with
x:\mathbf{1}\to\mathcal{I}
where
\mathbf{1}
is the terminal category. Since finite limits and colimits are computed point-wise in
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
,
x^{*}
preserves all finite limits and colimits, thus is in particular coherent. Given
f:x\to y
, this gives a natural transformation

f:x\to y:\mathbf{1}\to\mathcal{I},

and by wiskering with
f
we get a natural transformation

f^{*}:x^{*}\to y^{*}.

Furthermore, we can thus show that all finite pretoposes are equivalent to this form:

Corollary 2.6
.

Any finite pretopos is equivalent to one of the form
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
for some finite Cauchy complete category
\mathcal{I}
. In particular, all finite pretoposes are Heyting, and in fact toposes.

Proof.

This follows from Makkai’s conceptual completeness theorem
[
12
]
. Let
\mathcal{D}
be any finite pretopos with category of models equivalent to some finite Cauchy complete category
\mathcal{I}
. Then we have an equivalence between category of models as follows,

\mathrm{Mod}(\mathcal{D})\simeq\mathcal{I}\simeq\mathrm{Mod}(\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]),

which by conceptual completeness provides a syntactic equivalence

\mathcal{D}\simeq\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}].

Thus all finite pretoposes are of this form.
∎

The above result also essentially appears as exercises in SGA4, see Exercise 3.12 in volume 2 of
[
2
]
. There a Grothendieck topos is called
finite
if it is equivalent to the category of presheaves on a finite category. This is consistent with our definition of a finite pretopos, in that Corollary
2.6
implies a pretopos is finite in our sense iff its classifying topos is finite in the sense of SGA4.

Remark 2.7
.

From a logical perspective, the pretopos
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
is the classifying theory for flat functors out of
\mathcal{I}^{\operatorname{op}}
, which can be written down quite explicitly, e.g. see Chapter 2 of
[
4
]
. This is somewhat surprising in that all coherent first-order theories whose category of models is essentially finite can be axiomatised in a
uniform
way.

For any pretopos
\mathcal{D}
and any model of
\mathcal{D}

M:\mathcal{D}\to\mathbf{Set},

there is also the notion of this particular model
M
being finite. We say
M
is
finite
if it factors through
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}
. For a finite pretopos, not only its category of models is essentially finite, but all of its models are also finite:

Lemma 2.8
.

All models of a finite pretopos are finite.

Proof.

By Corollary
2.6
, it suffices to consider those toposes of the form
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
. Now notice that
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
is generated by a
countable
, in fact finite, theory, thus by Löwenheim–Skolem theorem, if it has an infinite model then it must have a model of each cardinality, which implies
\mathrm{Mod}(\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}])
cannot be essentially finite. Thus, any model
M:\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]\to\mathbf{Set}
must be finite.
∎

One may wonder whether the converse of Lemma
2.8
is also true, i.e. if a pretopos only has finite models, then whether or not it must be finite as well. The answer to this question is
negative
. Consider the theory
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathbb{Z}]=[\mathbb{Z},\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}]
where
\mathbb{Z}
is the integer group considered as a category with one element. It can be shown that
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathbb{Z}]
is
categorical
, i.e. it only has one model upto isomorphism, and in fact we have an equivalence

\mathrm{Mod}(\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathbb{Z}])\simeq\widehat{\mathbb{Z}},

where
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
is the profinite completion of
\mathbb{Z}
, which is a profinite groupoid with one connected component. A canonical choice of a model of
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathbb{Z}]
is given by the forgetful functor

\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathbb{Z}]\to\mathbf{Set},

which factors through
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}
, thus is finite. This implies that all models of
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathbb{Z}]
are finite, because all of them are isomorphic to a finite model. However, the profinite groupoid
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
is by no means finite, because its morphism group is uncountable. More generally, for any group
G
, we have the equivalence

\mathrm{Mod}(\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[G])\simeq\widehat{G},

where
\widehat{G}
is the profinite completion of
G
. And all of the theories of such form have only finite models, but their category of models is not necessarily essentially finite. For a reference for these calculations see
[
19
]
.

From a logical perspective, the caveat is that a theory might have
countably many sorts
. For instance, to present
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathbb{Z}]
as the classifying pretopos of a certain coherent theory, we must have countably many sorts, because there are countably many connected components in
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathbb{Z}]
. Now even though any model of
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathbb{Z}]
must be finite, there is no bound on the size of the interpretation of the sorts, i.e. for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a sort of
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathbb{Z}]
such that its size in this model is larger than
n
. Thus, even though this model is finite, the automorphism group of this model can still be uncountable.

2.2
Duality for Finite Pretoposes

Now that we have understood better the class of finite pretoposes, we proceed further to establish a duality result. The first task is to describe the functoriality of the construction
\mathcal{I}\mapsto\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
. For any functor
A:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{J}
with
\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}
finite Cauchy complete categories, by precomposition we have a functor

A^{*}:\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]\to\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}],

which is coherent because the coherent structure on
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]
and
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
are computed point-wise. Given a natural transformation

\eta:A\to B:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{J},

there is an induced natural transformation

\eta^{*}:A^{*}\to B^{*}:\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]\to\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}],

by wiskering, such that for any
F:\mathcal{J}\to\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}
,

\eta^{*}_{F}=F\eta:FA\to FB

This thus gives us a 2-functor from the 2-category of finite categories to the 2-category of finite pretoposes,

\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[-]:{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}^{\operatorname{op}}\to\mathbf{Pretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}.

In fact, we have the following diagramme

\textstyle{{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\simeq}
\textstyle{\mathbf{Pretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}^{\operatorname{op}}}
\textstyle{\mathbf{Topos}_{\mathrm{f}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where as mentioned previously, under the same construction the 2-functor

\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[-]:{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}\simeq\mathbf{Topos}_{\mathrm{f}}

is an equivalence. Hence, we only need to show
\mathbf{Topos}_{\mathrm{f}}\to\mathbf{Pretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}^{\operatorname{op}}
, by sending a geometric morphism to its inverse image, is also an equivalence.

Notice that by Corollary
2.6
, the 2-functor
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}\to\mathbf{Pretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}^{\operatorname{op}}
is essentially surjective, thus so is
\mathbf{Topos}_{\mathrm{f}}\to\mathbf{Pretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}^{\operatorname{op}}
. To show it is an equivalence, we prove it is also 2-fully faithful, which amounts to prove that any coherent functor between finite pretoposes are in fact the inverse image part of a geometric morphism. Hence, the crucial part is to show any coherent functor between
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]
and
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
indeed preserves all finite
colimits
.

We first compute the subobject lattices in
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]
. For any finite category
\mathcal{I}
, we will use
D(\mathcal{I})
to denote the finite distributive lattice of all upward closed subsets of
\mathcal{I}
. Given a subset
U
of objects of
\mathcal{I}
, it is upward closed if for any
x\in U
and
f:x\to y
in
\mathcal{I}
, we also have
y\in U
. Using this notion, it is easy to see that:

Lemma 2.9
.

For any finite category
\mathcal{J}
and
X\in\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]
, there is an isomorphism

\operatorname{Sub}_{\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]}(X)\cong D(\smallint\!\!_{\mathcal{J}}X).

where
\smallint\!\!_{\mathcal{J}}X
is the category of elements of
X
.

Proof.

We first show the case when
X=1
is the terminal object in
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]
. In this case, given any subobject
\sigma\rightarrowtail 1
, there is an associated upward closed subset
U_{\sigma}
, such that for any
x\in\mathcal{I}
,

x\in U_{\sigma}\Leftrightarrow\sigma(x)\cong 1.

It is straight forward to verify that this gives us an isomorphism. For general
X
, we have the following sequence of isomorphisms,

\operatorname{Sub}_{\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]}(X)\cong\operatorname{Sub}_{\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]/X}(1)\cong\operatorname{Sub}_{\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\smallint\!\!_{\mathcal{J}}X]}(1)\cong D(\smallint\!\!_{\mathcal{J}}X).

This completes the proof.
∎

In particular, for any finite category
\mathcal{J}
, Lemma
2.9
implies that the subobject lattice on
X
for any
X\in\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]
is a finite distributive lattice, since
\smallint\!\!_{\mathcal{J}}X
is a finite category. This allows us to show:

Lemma 2.10
.

Any coherent functor
M:\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]\to\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
preserves all finite colimits.

Proof.

It suffices to show
M
preserves all coequalisers. Given any coequaliser, taking a countable union of subobjects can inductively generate an effective coequaliser that has the same colimit. Since the subobject lattices in
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]
are
finite
distributive lattices, such countable unions are in fact finite, thus are preserved under coherent functors. Of course effective quotients are also preserved by coherent functors, thus they preserve arbitrary coequalisers.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

Any coherent functor from
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]
to
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
is the inverse image part of a geometric morphism.

Proof.

Consider any coherent functor
A^{*}:\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]\to\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
, we construct its right adjoint
A_{*}
as follows: For any
X\in\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
and
a\in\mathcal{J}
,

A_{*}X(a)\cong\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}](A^{*}\mathsf{y}^{a},X),

where
\mathsf{y}^{a}
is the corepresentable on
a\in\mathcal{J}
. We show that this indeed defines the right adjoint of
A^{*}
: For any
Y\in\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]
and
X\in\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}](Y,A_{*}X)

\displaystyle\cong\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}](\underrightarrow{\operatorname{Lim}}_{\mathsf{y}^{a}\to Y}\mathsf{y}^{a},A_{*}X)

\displaystyle\cong\underleftarrow{\operatorname{Lim}}_{\mathsf{y}^{a}\to Y}\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}](\mathsf{y}^{a},A_{*}X)

\displaystyle\cong\underleftarrow{\operatorname{Lim}}_{\mathsf{y}^{a}\to Y}\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}](A^{*}\mathsf{y}^{a},X)

\displaystyle\cong\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}](\underrightarrow{\operatorname{Lim}}_{\mathsf{y}^{a}\to Y}A^{*}\mathsf{y}^{a},X)

\displaystyle\cong\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}](A^{*}Y,X)

The third isomorphism holds due to our definition of
A_{*}X
. Also notice that the last step uses the fact that
A^{*}
preserves finite colimits.
∎

As a corollary, we obtain our desired duality result between finite pretoposes and finite Cauchy complete categories:

Theorem 2.12
.

There are equivalences of 2-categories as follows

{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}\simeq\mathbf{Topos}_{\mathrm{f}}\simeq\mathbf{Pretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}^{\operatorname{op}},

under the construction
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[-]
.

Proof.

As mentioned, we only need to show that the following functor

\mathbf{Topos}_{\mathrm{f}}\to\mathbf{Pretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}^{\operatorname{op}}

by taking a geometric morphism to its inverse image is an equivalence. This is a corollary of Lemma
2.11
.
∎

One nice thing about showing the above equivalence is that we can now also compute explicitly what is the left adjoint
A_{!}
of any coherent functor between two finite pretoposes, since now we know that any such coherent functor
A^{*}:\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]\to\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
is induced by some functor
A:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{J}
. Since
A_{!}
is a left adjoint, it suffices to know its value on corepresentables:

Lemma 2.13
.

For any
A:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{J}
, for any
x\in\mathcal{I}
we have

A_{!}\mathsf{y}^{x}\cong\mathsf{y}^{Ax}.

Proof.

For any
X\in\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]
, there is the following natural isomorphisms

\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}](A_{!}\mathsf{y}^{x},X)\cong\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}](\mathsf{y}^{x},A^{*}X)\cong X(Ax)\cong\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}](\mathsf{y}^{Ax},X).

This implies the two copresheaves are naturally isomorphic.
∎

The previously shown equivalence is indeed a categorification of the
(0,1)
-level duality between finite posets and finite distributive lattices in the following sense:

Proposition 2.14
.

There is a commutative diagramme as follows,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Pos}_{\mathrm{f}}^{\operatorname{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{DL}_{\mathrm{f}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}^{\operatorname{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Pretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

where the vertical left adjoints are posetal reflection of categories and localic reflection of pretoposes, respectively.

Proof.

For a finite poset
I
, its dual is the distributive lattice
D(I)
of upward closed subsets of
I
. For the two equivalences to commute with the two right adjoint inclusions, we need to show that

\mathbf{Sh}_{\mathrm{f}}(D(I))\simeq\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[I],

i.e. the category of finite sheaves on
D(I)
is equivalent to the presheaf finite topos
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[I]
. But this is evident, because any element in
D(I)
is a
finite
join of corepresentable elements, thus the sheaf condition implies that the value of a sheaf on
D(I)
is completely determined by that in
I
. This means that the equivalences commutes with the right adjoint inclusions, thus they also commutes with the left adjoints, since they are equivalences, and adjoints are unique upto natural isomorphism.
∎

Another way to look at the above proof if to use our result in Lemma
2.6
, which suggests that the free pretopos generated by
D(I)
is indeed given by
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[I]
, because

\mathrm{Mod}(\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[I])\simeq I\simeq\mathrm{Mod}(D(I)).

The above reflection then also implies that, for any Cauchy complete finite category
\mathcal{I}
, we can compute its localic reflection by computing the posetal reflection of
\mathcal{I}
. In particular, let
I
denote its posetal reflection, then we have

D(\mathcal{I})\cong\operatorname{Sub}_{\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]}(1)\cong\operatorname{Sub}_{\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[I]}(1)\cong D(I).

Remark 2.15
.

The above equivalence can also be restricted to the Boolean case. It is also well-known that a topos of the form
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
is
Boolean
iff
\mathcal{I}
is a groupoid. In particular, if
\mathcal{I}
is a poset, then it must be discrete. Thus from above we furthermore obtain a duality for finite Boolean pretoposes and finite Boolean algebras as follows,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}^{\operatorname{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{BA}_{\mathrm{f}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Grpd}_{\mathrm{f}}^{\operatorname{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{BPretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

2.3
Geometric Surjection

To restrict the duality
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}\simeq\mathbf{Pretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}^{\operatorname{op}}
to one for finite
Heyting
pretoposes, we need a convenient geometric language on the dual side of finite Cauchy complete categories. Recall the following equivalence

{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}\simeq\mathbf{Topos}_{\mathrm{f}}.

This way, a lot of geometric properties in the domain of toposes can be transferred to the world of finite Cauchy complete categories. For any property
P
of geometric morphisms in topos theory, we say a functor
A:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{J}
in
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
is
P
, iff its induced geometric morphism
A:\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]\to\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]
is
P
.

For us, the most crucial geometric notion is that of a
surjection
. Recall a geometric morphism
A
is a surjection iff its inverse image
A^{*}
is faithful, and we would like to characterise surjections by viewing
A
as functors
A:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{J}
. For this we use the extensive results in
[
5
]
:

Lemma 2.16
.

A:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{J}
is a surjection iff it is essentially surjective upto retracts, i.e. for any
a\in\mathcal{J}
, it is a retract in
\mathcal{J}
of
Ax
for some
x\in\mathcal{I}
.

Proof.

Note that the geometric morphism
A:\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]\to\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{J}]
is induced by viewing the opposite functor
A^{\operatorname{op}}:\mathcal{I}^{\operatorname{op}}\to\mathcal{J}^{\operatorname{op}}
as a
comorphism of site
. By Proposition 7.1 in
[
5
]
, since
A^{\operatorname{op}}
preserves covers,
A
is a surjection iff
A^{\operatorname{op}}
is
dense
, iff for any
a\in\mathcal{J}
, there exists some
x\in\mathcal{I}
and an arrow
s:a\to Ax
such that
1_{a}
factors through
s
. This is equivalent to say that there exists
r:Ax\to a
with
rs=1_{a}
, i.e.
A
is essentially surjective upto retracts.
∎

Recall that we have an orthogonal factorisation system in
\mathbf{Topos}_{\mathrm{f}}
consisting of geometric inclusions and geometric surjections. By Lemma
2.16
, this also translates to the following orthogonal factorisations system
(E,M)
on
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
:

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\text{essentially surjective upto retracts},

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=\text{fully faithful whose image is closed under retracts}

It is well-known that these form an orthogonal factorisation system on
\mathbf{Cat}
, e.g. see
[
6
]
. It also restricts one to
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
, since for any functor
A:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{J}
in
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
, the factorisation is given by
\mathcal{I}\to{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\operatorname{img}A}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}\to\mathcal{J}
, where
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\operatorname{img}A}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
is the retract closure of
\operatorname{img}A
in
\mathcal{J}
, which is again Cauchy complete. However, the nice thing in
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
is that the image of a full functor is automatically closed under retract:

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
A:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{J}
be a full functor in
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
. Then the image of
A
is closed under retracts in
\mathcal{J}
, i.e. for any retract
r:Ax\rightleftarrows a:s
with
x\in\mathcal{I}
and
a\in\mathcal{J}
there exists some
y\in\mathcal{I}
that
Ay\cong a
.

Proof.

Suppose we have a retract pair in
\mathcal{J}

r:Ax\rightleftarrows a:s.

Let
g=sr:Ax\to Ax
, and let
\mathcal{I}_{g}(x,x)
denote the set of endomorphisms on
x
in
\mathcal{I}
that is mapped to
g
. By fullness,
\mathcal{I}_{g}(x,x)
is non-empty. Since
g
is idempotent, we know that
\mathcal{I}_{g}(x,x)
is a multiplicative closed set in
\mathcal{I}(x,x)
. Since
\mathcal{I}_{g}(x,x)
is finite, every orbit in
\mathcal{I}_{g}(x,x)
must be finite, thus there exists an idempotent map
f:x\to x
which is mapped to
g
. Since
\mathcal{I}
is Cauchy complete, the splitting of
f
, which is a pair
x\rightleftarrows y
, exists in
\mathcal{I}
. Since splitting of idempotency is an absolute colimit, it is preserved by
A
, hence we must have
Ay\cong a
.
∎

Thus, the (surjection, inclusion)-factorisation system on
\mathbf{Topos}_{\mathrm{f}}
transports to the (essentially surjective upto retract, fully faithful)-factorisation system in
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
. For any functor
A:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{J}
, it can be factored as
\mathcal{I}\to{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\operatorname{img}A}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}\to\mathcal{J}
, where
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\operatorname{img}A}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
is the retract closure of images of
A
in
\mathcal{J}
. Notice that to take retract closure is forced when working in
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
, because the image
\operatorname{img}A
might not be Cauchy complete.

The argument in the proof of Lemma
2.17
is typical for the class of finite Cauchy complete categories, and we will see further examples when we look at open functors to describe the duality for finite Heyting pretoposes.

2.4
Open Functors and Duality of Finite Heyting Pretoposes

As we have mentioned, it turns out that any finite pretopos is automatically Heyting. Let
\mathbf{HPretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}
be the 2-category of
finite Heyting pretoposes
, which is defined to be a wide subcategory of
\mathbf{Pretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}
with Heyting functors between them. Recall that a geometric morphism is called
open
if its inverse image functor is Heyting. According to Lemma
2.11
, to find the dual of
\mathbf{HPretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}
, it is equivalent to characterise
open
geometric morphisms between
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}
-bounded toposes.

In fact, Chapter C3.1 of
[
9
]
already contains a classification of open geometric morphisms between presheaf toposes which are induced by a functor between the underlying categories. This suffices for our purpose because any geometric morphism in
\mathbf{Topos}_{\mathrm{f}}
arises this way. Concretely, a functor
A:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{J}
in
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
induces an
open
geometric morphism iff it satisfies the following combinatorial property: For any
x\in\mathcal{I}
and any
f:Ax\to a
in
\mathcal{J}
, there exists some
u:x\to y
in
\mathcal{I}
such that
a
is a
retract
of
Ay
under
Ax
,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Ay}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Ax}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Au}

From our characterisation of surjections, we can rephrase it as follows:

Definition 2.21
.

We say a functor
A:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{J}
is
upwards surjective
if for any
x\in\mathcal{I}
, the induced functor

A_{x/}:\mathcal{I}_{x/}\to\mathcal{J}_{Ax/}

is a surjection.

Although open functors are not necessarily surjective, they will be once we restricts to any upwards part of the functor:

Lemma 2.22
.

A:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{J}
is open iff it is upwards surjective.

Proof.

Recall that by Proposition
2.16
, a functor is a surjection iff it is essentially surjective upto retracts. It is then straight forward to see that openness is equivalent to being upwards surjective.
∎

This way, let us use
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
to denote the wide subcategory of
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
consisting of open, or equivalently upwards surjective, functors. As a consequence, we have the following duality result for finite Heyting pretoposes:

Theorem 2.23
.

The equivalence
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}^{\operatorname{op}}\simeq\mathbf{Pretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}
restricts to one

{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}^{\operatorname{op}}\simeq\mathbf{HPretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}

between the opposite 2-category of finite Cauchy complete categories with open functors and the 2-category of finite Heyting pretoposes.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.12
,
\mathbf{HPretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}
is dual to the wide subcategory of
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
of those functors inducing open geometric morphisms. And as mentioned, the characterisation of open geometric morphisms as open functors is given in
[
9
]
.
∎

.

Just like that the duality between finite Cauchy complete categories and finite pretoposes restricts to the localic case, so is our duality of open functors with Heyting morphisms. This means that we have a commuative diagramme as follows,

In our finitary case, open functors have nicer properties. As mentioned in Section

Proposition 2.25
.

For any functor
A:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{J}
in
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}

is open iff it is upwards stably surjective, i.e. for any

,

is essentially surjective.

Proof.

The if direction is trivial. Suppose

is open, consider any

. Now by assumption, there exists some
u_{1}:x\to x_{1}
and a retract pair
s_{1}:a\rightleftarrows Ax_{1}:r_{1}
, inducing the following diagramme,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Ax}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Ax_{1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Au_{1}}

Consider the following recursive process:

•

is trivial, viz. an isomorphism, then we are done, since now

is isomorphic to

in

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Ax_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Ax_{2}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Au_{2}}

Note that

cannot be an isomorphism, because if it is, then by the fact that

,

must be monic; but

is already split epic, thus it is monic iff it is an isomoprhism, which by our assumption it isn’t. Thus,

is not an isomorphism. Now we may run the algorithm again recursively on

\textstyle{Ax_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{Ax_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{Au_{n+1}}
\textstyle{\cdots}

with their corresponding non-trivial retract pairs

for some

Or the sequence is ultimately periodic, viz. there is a cofinal part of the sequence which can be viewed as constant on some

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Ay}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Ay}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}

3.1
The

For a general discussion on 2-categorical notions of sites and stacks, we refer the readers to

To see this gives a well-defined Grothendieck coverage

{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}^{\!\!\cong}

2.25

{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}^{\!\!\cong}

Lemma 3.1
.

In
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}^{\!\!\cong}

\textstyle{\mathcal{Q}\times_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{R}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\scriptstyle{D}
\scriptstyle{C}
\textstyle{\mathcal{Q}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{A}
\textstyle{\mathcal{R}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{B}
\textstyle{\mathcal{P}}

Proof.

Recall by Proposition

(q,r,\alpha)\in\mathcal{Q}\times_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{R}
with

, let

\textstyle{(\mathcal{Q}\times_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{R})_{(q,r)/}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\scriptstyle{D}
\scriptstyle{C}
\textstyle{\mathcal{Q}_{q/}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{A}
\textstyle{\mathcal{R}_{r/}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

By

∎

Remark 3.2
.

In topos theory, it is true more generally that open geometric morphisms are stable under pullback along bounded geometric morphisms, e.g. see Chapter C3.1 in
[
9
]
. In the finitary case of our interest, as shown in Lemma
3.1
this stability result can be seen as a direct consequence of the combinatorial properties of open maps established in Proposition
2.25
. In contrast, for a general functor

which is open, its pullback along any other functor
G:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{D}
computed in
\mathbf{Cat}
is by no means necessarily still open.

As an easy consequence, so is open surjections:

Lemma 3.3
.

A functor in
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}^{\!\!\cong}
is an open surjection iff it is stably surjective and upwards stably surjective, thus in particular open surjections are also stable under 2-pullbacks.

Proof.

We only need to show any open surjection is stably surjective, viz. essentially surjective. Suppose
A:\mathcal{I}\to\mathcal{J}
is an open surjection, then for any

, by
A
being surjective we can find a retract pair
s:a\rightleftarrows Ax:r
. Now by
A
being open, the functor

A_{x/}:\mathcal{I}_{x/}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{J}_{Ax/}

is essentially surjective, thus we can find
u:x\to y
in
\mathcal{I}_{x/}
such that
Au\cong r
in
\mathcal{J}_{Ax/}
. In particular, this implies
a\cong Ay
, thus
A
is essentially surjective.
∎

With such a stability result, we can now easily see that
J
is a Grothendieck coverage on the
(2,1)
-category
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}^{\!\!\cong}
:

Lemma 3.4
.

J
is a Grothendieck coverage on the
(2,1)
-category
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}^{\!\!\cong}
.

Proof.

Any equivalence is evidently an open surjection. For any
J
-covering family on
\mathcal{P}
, suppose it contains a jointly surjective family
\left\{A_{i}:\mathcal{Q}_{i}\to\mathcal{P}\right\}_{i\in I}
. For any

, the family of pullbacks
\left\{B^{*}A_{i}:\mathcal{Q}_{i}\times_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{R}\right\}_{i\in I}

satisfies stability. Transitivity is evident from the fact that open surjections are closed under composition.
∎

This way, we have constructed a
(2,1)

Corollary 3.6

If

Furthermore, any object in

For any

-covering family.

Thus, we can always work with the site

3.2
Stack Representation of Heyting Pretoposes

Now for a general Heyting pretopos
\mathcal{H}
, we know that they are not complete w.r.t. classical models. For our focus of finitely presented Heyting pretopos, we will consider its models in all
finite Kripke frames
, i.e. models valued in the Heyting pretoposes of the form
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{P}]
, for
\mathcal{P}\in{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong}
. In particular,
any
Heyting pretopos
\mathcal{H}
induces a prestack
\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}
on the
(2,1)
-category
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong}
as follows:

\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}:=\mathbf{HPt}(\mathcal{H},\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[-]),

where
\mathbf{HPt}
is the
(2,1)
-category of Heyting pretoposes. In particular,
\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}
is groupoid valued. The action of
\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}
on
A:\mathcal{Q}\to\mathcal{P}
in
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong}
is given by post-composition with the inverse image
A^{*}:\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{P}]\to\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{Q}]
. For any finite Kripke frame
\mathcal{P}\in{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong}
, we think of
\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})
as the
groupoid of models
of
\mathcal{H}
on the
\mathcal{P}
, or simply
\mathcal{P}
-models
of
\mathcal{H}
. In particular, the pre-stack records the information of all
finite
models of
\mathcal{H}
on
finite
Kripke frames.

Our first task then is to show that
\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}
will indeed be a
stack
for the topology
J
we have chosen on
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong}
, and this in fact can be seen as a consequence of the descent theorem for toposes.

Lemma 3.9
.

For any Heyting pretopos
\mathcal{H}
,
\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}
is a stack of groupoids on the
(2,1)
-site
({{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong},J)
.

Proof.

Recall the descent theorem for toposes first given in
[
10
]
, which states that open surjections are of effective descent for toposes. Since the descent theorem can be proven in purely elementary means, e.g. see
[
14
]
, in our finitary case this implies that the following diagramme will be a 2-colimit in
\mathbf{Topos}_{\mathrm{f}}
under the equivalence with
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Q}\times_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{Q}\times_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{Q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Q}\times_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{Q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}

By duality, this becomes a 2-limit in
\mathbf{Pretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}
. In fact, the dual will be a 2-limit in
\mathbf{Pretopos}
, since the full inclusion
\mathbf{Pretopos}_{\mathrm{f}}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{Pretopos}
evidently preserves and creates finite 2-limits. As we’ve mentioned, 2-limits in
\mathbf{HPretopos}
are computed the same as in
\mathbf{Pretopos}
, thus the dual diagramme is also a 2-limit for Heyting pretoposes.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

In fact for our finitary case, it is fairely straight forward to directly prove that the above diagramme is a 2-colimit in
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}
when

is an open surjection, which avoids the more technical topos-theoretic proofs. We leave the details for the interested readers.

This way, we have realised any Heyting pretopos
\mathcal{H}
as a stack of groupoid of finite models over the
(2,1)
-category of finite Kripke frames. However, due to our restriction on finite models on finite Kripke frames, we cannot expect that this stack
\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}
we associate to

will reveal all the information for an arbitrary Heyting pretopos. This way, we introduce the relevant class of
finitely presentable
Heyting pretoposes:

Definition 3.11
.

We say a Heyting pretopos
\mathcal{H}
is
finitely presentable
, if it is complete w.r.t. finite models on finite Kripke frames, i.e. the following canonical functor is
faithful

\mathcal{H}\rightarrowtail\prod_{\mathcal{P}\in{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{3.61664pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*},F\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})}\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{P}],

Let us use
\mathbf{HPt}_{\mathrm{fp}}
to denote the
(2,1)
-category of finitely presentable Heyting pretoposes. From the above definition,
\mathbf{HPt}_{\mathrm{fp}}
basically contains those sub Heyting pretoposes of products of finite ones. We thus view our stack representation as a 2-functor as follows,

\Phi_{-}:\mathbf{HPt}_{\mathrm{fp}}^{\operatorname{op}}\to\mathbf{St}({{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong},J),

This way, we have indeed embedded our interested class of Heyting pretoposes into the 2-category of stacks on the 2-site
({{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong},J)
, contravariantly.

Evidently, finite Heyting pretoposes are finitely presentable. More generally, the
free
Heyting pretopos generated by a finite pretopos will also be finitely presentable. For any finite Pretopos
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
, the free Heyting pretopos generated by it will be denoted as
\mathbf{Hey}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
. In particular, for any Heyting pretopos
\mathcal{H}
, we have a natural equivalence of categories

\mathbf{Pretopos}(\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}],\mathcal{H})\simeq\mathbf{HPretopos}(\mathbf{Hey}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}],\mathcal{H}).

where the equivalence is induced by an inclusion
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]\rightarrowtail\mathbf{Hey}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
. For instance, the
initial
Heyting pretopos can be identified as the free Heyting pretopos
\mathbf{Hey}_{\mathrm{f}}\simeq\mathbf{Hey}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathbf{1}]
, which is freely generated by
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}=\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathbf{1}]
.

It is instructive to look at the stack representation of these free Heyting pretoposes. Notice that for any
\mathcal{P}\in{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong}
we have the following equivalences,

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathbf{Hey}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]}(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{HPt}_{\mathrm{fp}}(\mathbf{Hey}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}],\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{P}])

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{Pt}_{\mathrm{fp}}(\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}],\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{P}])

\displaystyle\simeq{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}^{\!\!\cong}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I}).

We also write the stack
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}^{\!\!\cong}(-,\mathcal{I})
on
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong}
as
\underline{\mathcal{I}}
. The above equivalence shows that for any finite Cauchy complete category
\mathcal{I}
, we have

\Phi_{\mathbf{Hey}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]}\simeq\underline{\mathcal{I}}

in the
(2,1)
-category
\mathbf{St}({{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong},J)
, and thus by Lemma

, all prestacks of the form

are in fact stacks for the topology

.

Remark 3.12
.

In fact, one can show that

We also note that the 2-category of finite Heyting pretoposes is closed under slices, thus as a consequence it is closed under finite quotients of finitely presentable Heyting pretoposes:

.

If
\mathcal{H}

\mathcal{H}/\varphi

Proof.

Recall that the slice
\mathcal{H}/\varphi

\mathcal{H}

\mathbf{HPt}_{\mathrm{f}}(\mathcal{H}/\varphi,\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{P}])\cong\sum_{F\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})}\Gamma F\varphi\cong\sum_{F\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})}(F\varphi)_{*}.

Here

is the global section functor, and the sum notation
\sum_{F\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})}\Gamma F\varphi
denote the category of pairs
(F,\alpha)
with
F\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})
a model of
\mathcal{H}
in
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{P}]
, while
\alpha:1\to F\varphi
is a global point of
F\varphi
in
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{P}]
. This is well-known, and one explicit proof can be found e.g. in Chapter 2.3 of
[

]
.
2
2

2

The proof there is given for pretoposes, but the same proof works for Heyting pretoposes as well.
The final isomorphism is due to the fact that
\mathcal{P}
has an initial object, thus taking the global section is equivalent of evaluating on the root.

Now if we have a proper subterminal object
U
in
\mathcal{H}/\varphi
, it corresponds to some proper subobject

. By

being finite, we can find a finite model

then gives us a model of

Remark 3.14
.

It might be tempting to say that

. However we do not know whether this is true or not at this point.

3.3
From Gros to Petit

Following the
“gros-petit”
philosophy in topos theory, given any finitely presented Heyting pretopos
\mathcal{H}
, the
petit
category
\mathcal{H}
can be embedded into the gros category
\mathcal{H}/\mathbf{HPt}_{\mathrm{fp}}^{\operatorname{op}}
of the opposite of the 2-category of finite Heyting pretoposes under
\mathcal{H}
,

\mathcal{H}\rightarrowtail\mathcal{H}/\mathbf{HPt}_{\mathrm{fp}}^{\operatorname{op}},

sending any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
to the pullback onto the slice over it,

\varphi^{*}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}/\varphi.

By composing with the stack representation
\Phi_{-}:\mathbf{HPt}_{\mathrm{fp}}^{\operatorname{op}}\to\mathbf{St}({{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong},J)
functor, which we may as well denote it as the
gros representation functor
, we naturally get a
petit representation
of
\mathcal{H}
as follows,

\mathcal{H}\to\mathbf{St}({{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong},J)/\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}.

On the left we have a
1-category

\mathcal{H}
, thus we expect that this representation to land in
discrete
objects over
\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}
internally in
\mathbf{St}({{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong},J)
.

Following the general development of fibrations in a 2-category in
[
21
]
, in a 2-category
\mathcal{K}
a morphism
f:A\to B
is called a
discrete opfibration
if for any
C\in\mathcal{K}
, the induced functor
\mathcal{K}(C,A)\to\mathcal{K}(C,B)
is a discrete opfibration. Notice that for a fibration, we would also require that for any
g:D\to C
the base change square will be a morphism of fibrations. However, since for discrete opfibrations every morphism in the over category will be Cartesian, this condition is trivially satisfied. Equivalently,
f:A\to B
is a discrete fibration iff it is a discrete object in the 2-category of opfibrations over
B
, and discrete opfibrations over some object naturally forms a
1-category
.

In the
(2,1)
-category of stacks over
({{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong},J)
, to check opdiscrete fibrations it suffices to check on representables. For any stack
\mathcal{F}
in
\mathbf{St}({{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong},J)
, we use
\mathbf{opDisc}(\mathcal{F})
to denote the 1-category of opdiscrete fibrations on
\mathcal{F}
. Furthermore, the category
\mathbf{opDisc}(\mathcal{F})
will in fact be a
Heyting category
, e.g. see
[
20
]
.

We first show the petit representation functor indeed lands in discrete opfibrations in
\mathbf{St}({{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong},J)
:

Lemma 3.15
.

The petit representation restricts to a functor as follows:

\mathcal{H}\to\mathbf{opDisc}(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}).

Proof.

Given any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
, by construction for any
\mathcal{P}\in{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong}
, the morphism

\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\varphi}(\mathcal{P})\cong\sum_{F\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})}\Gamma F\varphi\cong\sum_{F\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})}(F\varphi)_{*}\to\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})

is given by the projection map. Evidently, for any
F\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})
, the fibre of
F
over this projection is the
discrete set

(F\varphi)_{*}
. Furthermore, for any
\eta:F\cong G
, this naturally provides a map
(F\varphi)_{*}\to(G\varphi)_{*}
. This shows for any
\mathcal{P}
the above map morphism is a discrete opfibration, thus
\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\varphi}\to\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}
is a discrete opfibration in
\mathbf{St}({{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong},J)
.
∎

More importantly, when restricting the codomain the discrete opfibrations over
\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}
, the petit representation will be a
faithful Heyting functor
for any finitely presented Heyting pretopos:

Theorem 3.16
.

For any
\mathcal{H}
in
\mathbf{HPt}_{\mathrm{fp}}
, the petit representation

\mathcal{H}\rightarrowtail\mathbf{opDisc}(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}})

is a faithful Heyting functor.

Proof.

Faithfulness is basically built in in our definition of finitely presented Heyting pretoposes. Once we know it is a Heyting functor, it suffices to show for any proper subterminal object
\varphi
, it is not mapped to the identity on
\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}
. However, by
\mathcal{H}
being finitely presented, there exists some
\mathcal{P}\in{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong}
and a Heyting functor
F:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{P}]
that
F\varphi\rightarrowtail 1
is a proper subterminal object in
\mathbf{Set}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{P}]
. This implies
(F\varphi)_{*}
must be empty, thus
F\not\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\varphi}(\mathcal{P})
. Hence, the induced injection
\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\varphi}\rightarrowtail\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}
must also be proper. Hence, the remaining is to show it is a Heyting functor.

We first show it preserves finite limits. Preserving the terminal objects is evident, since we have

\mathcal{H}/1\simeq\mathcal{H}\mapsto\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}=\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}.

For any
\alpha:\psi\to\varphi
and
\beta:\chi\to\varphi
in
\mathcal{H}
, we have the following equivalences,

\displaystyle\,(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\psi}\times_{\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\varphi}}\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\chi})(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\,\{\,(F,a)\in\sum_{F\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})}(F\psi)_{*},(G,b)\in\sum_{G\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})}(G\chi)_{*}\mid F=G\operatorname{\&}F\alpha a=G\beta b\,\}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\sum_{H\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})}(H\psi)_{*}\times_{(H\varphi)_{*}}(H\chi)_{*}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\sum_{H\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})}H(\psi\times_{\varphi}\chi)_{*}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\,\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\psi\times_{\varphi}\chi}(\mathcal{P})

The first isomorphism is due to the fact that pullbacks in
\mathbf{opDisc}(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}})
are computed point-wise; the second and the last isomorphisms have used the computation of models of slice Heyting pretoposes mentioned in the proof of Lemma
3.15
; the third holds due to the fact that both
H
and evaluation on the root preserve pullbacks.

Next, we show that for any
X\in\mathcal{H}
, the induced map on subobjects

\operatorname{Sub}_{\mathcal{H}}(X)\to\operatorname{Sub}_{\mathbf{opDisc}(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}})}(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/X})

is a distributive lattice morphism. Now subobjects of
\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/X}
in
\mathbf{opDisc}(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}})
coincides with subobjects of
\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/X}
which are discrete opfibrations, e.g. see
[
20
]
, thus we have the following isomorphism

\operatorname{Sub}_{\mathbf{opDisc}(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}})}(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/X})\cong\operatorname{Sub}_{\mathbf{opDisc}(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/X})}(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/X}).

This means it suffices to consider when
X
is the terminal object. Given subterminal objects
\varphi,\psi\rightarrowtail 1
in
\mathcal{H}
, by definition for any
\mathcal{P}\in{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong}
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\varphi\wedge\psi}(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle\cong\{\,F\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})\mid F(\varphi\wedge\psi)_{*}\cong 1\,\}

\displaystyle\cong\{\,F\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})\mid(F\varphi)_{*}\,\}\cap\{\,F\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})\mid(F\models\psi)_{*}\,\}

\displaystyle\cong\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\varphi}\cap\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\psi}

Again, the second isomorphism holds since any such
F
preserves finite limits. This shows the petit representation preserves meets in subobject lattices. Completely similarly it also preserves disjunctions. However, this further relies on the fact that
J
is a
regular
topology on
{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong}
, thus disjunction in subobject lattices of
\mathbf{opDisc}(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}})
are also computed point-wise.

To show it is a map between Heyting categories, we also need to show that the functor commutes with exisential and universal quantifiers. Given any
\alpha:Y\to X
and
\varphi\rightarrowtail Y
, for the case of existential quantification,

\displaystyle\,\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\exists_{\alpha}\varphi}(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\,\{\,(F,a)\in\sum_{F\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})}(FX)_{*}\mid a\in(F\exists_{\alpha}\varphi)_{*}\,\}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\,\{\,(F,a)\in\sum_{F\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})}(FX)_{*}\mid a\in\exists_{(F\alpha)_{*}}(F\varphi)_{*}\,\}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\,\{\,(F,a)\in\sum_{F\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})}(FX)_{*}\mid\exists b\in(F\varphi)_{*}.\alpha b=a\,\}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\,\exists_{\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\alpha}}\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\varphi}(\mathcal{P})

The second isomorphism holds since
F
preserves existential quantifier, and that again due to the topology
J
being regular, existential quantification is also computed point-wise thus preserved by
\mathrm{ev}_{*}
; the last isomorphism holds for the same reason.

Finally, for universal quantification, we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\forall_{\alpha}\varphi}(\mathcal{P})\cong\sum_{F\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})}(\forall_{F\alpha}F\varphi)_{*}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})}\{\,x\in(FX)_{*}\mid\forall p\in\mathcal{P}.\forall y\in(FY)_{p}.\alpha y=px\Rightarrow y\in(F\varphi)_{p}\,\}

The last isomorphism is basically by Kripke-Joyal semantics, for a reference see Chapter VI of
[
11
]
. On the other hand, if we compute the universal quantification in
\mathbf{opDisc}(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}})
, again by Kripke-Joyal semantics we have

\displaystyle\,\forall_{\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\alpha}}\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\varphi}(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\,\{\,(F,x)\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/X}(\mathcal{P})\,\mid\,

\displaystyle\forall A\!\!:\!\!\mathcal{Q}\to\mathcal{P}.\,\forall(G,y)\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/Y}(\mathcal{Q})

\displaystyle G=A^{*}F\operatorname{\&}\alpha y=A^{*}x\Rightarrow(G,y)\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\varphi}(\mathcal{P})\}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\,\{\,(F,x)\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/X}(\mathcal{P})\,\mid\,

\displaystyle\forall A\!\!:\!\!\mathcal{Q}\to\mathcal{P}.\,\forall y\in(A^{*}FY)_{*}

\displaystyle\alpha y=A^{*}x\Rightarrow y\in(A^{*}F\varphi)_{*}\}

However, for any such
A:\mathcal{Q}\to\mathcal{P}
, we know that

y\in(A^{*}F\varphi)_{*}\Leftrightarrow y\in(F\varphi)_{p}.

where
p=A*
. Hence, the above two subobjects are isomorphic, which shows that the petit representation also commutes with the universal quantification. This completes the proof that the petit representation is a faithful Heyting functor.
∎

Thus, according to Theorem
3.16
, any finitely presented Heyting pretopos
\mathcal{H}
now can be viewed as a sub Heyting category of the discrete opfibred objects over
\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}
in
\mathbf{St}({{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong},J)
. As we’ve mentioned, when
\mathcal{H}
is free of the form
\mathbf{Hey}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]
for some
\mathcal{I}\in{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cat}_{\mathrm{f}}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}^{\!\!\cong}
,
\Phi_{\mathbf{Hey}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]}
is isomorphic to
\underline{\mathcal{I}}
. Thus, for this special case, the petit representation provides a faithful Heyting functor

\mathbf{Hey}_{\mathrm{f}}[\mathcal{I}]\rightarrowtail\mathbf{opDisc}(\underline{\mathcal{I}}).

At last, we look at the base change of petit representations, which explains its relationship with the gros representation in more concrete terms:

Lemma 3.17
.

For any Heyting functor
\alpha:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}
in
\mathbf{HPt}_{\mathrm{fp}}
, the following diagramme commutes,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{opDisc}(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{opDisc}(\Phi_{\mathcal{G}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{\alpha}^{*}}

where
\Phi_{\alpha}:\Phi_{\mathcal{G}}\to\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}
is the morphism of stacks induced by the gros representation, and
\Phi_{\alpha}^{*}
is the functor of pullback along
\Phi_{\alpha}
, which preserves discrete opfibrations.

Proof.

To show this, it suffices to prove that for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
, the following diagramme is a pullback in
\mathbf{St}({{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong},J)
,

\textstyle{\Phi_{\mathcal{G}/\alpha\varphi}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\varphi}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Phi_{\mathcal{G}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\Phi_{\alpha}}
\textstyle{\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}}

We may dicretely compute the pullback on
\mathcal{P}\in{{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong}
as follows,

\displaystyle\,(\Phi_{\mathcal{G}}\times_{\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}}\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\varphi})(\mathcal{P})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\,\{\,G\in\Phi_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{P}),(F,a)\in\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\varphi}(\mathcal{P})\mid G\alpha=F\,\}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\left\{G\in\Phi_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{P}),a\in(G\alpha\varphi)_{*}\right\}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\,\Phi_{\mathcal{G}/\alpha\varphi}(\mathcal{P})

The first holds since pullback between discrete opfibrations are computed point-wise in
\mathbf{St}({{\hat{\rule{0.0pt}{5.16663pt}\smash{\mathbf{Cas}}\mkern 4.0mu}\mkern-4.0mu}{}}_{\mathrm{f},*}^{\!\!\cong},J)
, and the remaining is standard calculation. This completes the proof.
∎

Now for those Heyting functors induced by pullback along some
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
, the induced morphism between stacks by Lemma
3.15
will be a discrete opfibration

\Phi_{\varphi}:\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\varphi}\to\Phi_{\mathcal{H}}.

This means the pullback functor

\Phi_{\varphi}^{*}:\mathbf{opDisc}(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}})\to\mathbf{opDisc}(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\varphi})

also has a
left adjoint

\Sigma_{\Phi_{\varphi}}
, which is simply given by composition with
\Phi_{\varphi}
because discrete opfibrations are closed under composition. In such a case, the petit representation commutes with both adjoints:

Corollary 3.18
.

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
, the following diagramme commutes,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{H}/\varphi}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{opDisc}(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}/\varphi})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{H}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{opDisc}(\Phi_{\mathcal{H}})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma_{\varphi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma_{\Phi_{\varphi}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi_{\varphi}^{*}}

Proof.

From Lemma
3.17
we already know it commutes with pullbacks. It commutes with left adjoints by functoriality of gros representation.
∎

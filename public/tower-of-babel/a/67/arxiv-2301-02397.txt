Fine boundary regularity for fully nonlinear mixed local-nonlocal problems
Mitesh Modasiya and Abhrojyoti Sen

Abstract
. We consider Dirichlet problems for fully nonlinear mixed local-nonlocal non-translation invariant operators. For a bounded C 2 C^{2} domain Ω ⊂ ℝ d , \Omega\subset{\mathds{R}^{d}}, let u ∈ C ⁡ ( ℝ d ) u\in C({\mathds{R}^{d}}) be a viscosity solution of such Dirichlet problem. We obtain global Lipschitz regularity and fine boundary regularity for u u by constructing appropriate sub and supersolutions coupled with a weak version of Harnack inequality. We apply these results to obtain Hölder regularity of D ​ u Du up to the boundary.

1.
Introduction and main results

In this article, for a bounded
C^{2}
domain
\Omega\subset{\mathds{R}^{d}}
we establish the boundary regularity of the solution
u
to the in-equations

(1.1)

\begin{split}{\mathcal{L}}u+C_{0}|Du|&\geq-K\;\quad\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
{\mathcal{L}}u-C_{0}|Du|&\leq K\quad\quad\text{in}\;\Omega,\\
u&=0\;\quad\quad\text{in}\;\Omega^{c},\end{split}

where
C_{0},K\geq 0
and
{\mathcal{L}}
is a fully nonlinear integro-differential operator of the form

(1.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}u(x):={\mathcal{L}}[x,u]=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\inf_{\nu\in\Gamma}\left\{\trace a_{\theta\nu}(x)D^{2}u(x)+{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}[x,u]\right\},

for some index sets
\Theta,\Gamma
.
The coefficient
a_{\theta\nu}:\Omega\to\mathds{R}^{d\times d}
is a matrix valued function and
{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}
is a nonlocal operator defined as

(1.3)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}u(x):={\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}[x,u]=\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(u(x+y)-u(x)-\mathds{1}_{B_{1}}(y)Du(x)\cdot y)N_{\theta\nu}(x,y)\;\mathrm{d}{y}.

The above in-equations (
1.1
) are motivated by Hamilton-Jacobi equations of the form

\displaystyle Iu(x):=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\inf_{\nu\in\Gamma}\left\{L_{\theta\nu}u(x)+f_{\theta\nu}(x)\right\}=0,

where

(1.4)

\displaystyle L_{\theta\nu}u(x)=\trace a_{\theta\nu}(x)D^{2}u(x)+{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}[x,u]+b_{\theta\nu}(x)\cdot Du(x),

b_{\theta\nu}(\cdot)
and
f_{\theta\nu}(\cdot)
are bounded functions on
\Omega.
These linear operators (
1.4
) are extended generator for a wide class of
d
-dimensional Feller processes
(more precisely, jump diffusions) and the nonlinear operator
Iu(\cdot)
has its connection to the stochastic control problems and differential games (see
[
13
,
12
]
and the references therein). The first term in (
1.4
) represents the diffusion, the second term represents the jump part of a Feller process, and the third represents the drift. We refer to
[
15
,
14
,
1
]
and the references therein for more on the connections between the operators of the form (
1.4
) and stochastic differential equations.
For a precise application of these type of operators in finance and biological models, we refer to
[
24
,
23
,
20
]
and the references therein.

We set the following assumptions on the coefficient
a_{\theta\nu}(\cdot)
and the kernel
N_{\theta\nu}(x,y)
, throughout this article.

Assumption 1.1
.

(a)

a_{\theta\nu}(\cdot)
are uniformly continuous and bounded in
\bar{\Omega}
, uniformly in
\theta,\nu
for
\theta\in\Theta,\nu\in\Gamma.

Furthermore,
a_{\theta\nu}(\cdot)
satisfies the uniform ellipticity condition
\lambda\bm{I}\leq a_{\theta\nu}(\cdot)\leq\Lambda\bm{I}
for some
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
where
\bm{I}
denotes the
d\times d
identity matrix.

(b)

For each
\theta\in\Theta,\nu\in\Gamma
,
N_{\theta\nu}:\Omega\times{\mathds{R}^{d}}
is a measurable function and for some
\alpha\in(0,2)
there exists a kernel
k
that is measurable in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}\setminus\{0\}
such that for any
\theta\in\Theta,\nu\in\Gamma,x\in\Omega,
we have

0\leq N_{\theta\nu}(x,y)\leq k(y)

and

\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}<+\infty,

where we denote
p\wedge q:=\min\{p,q\}
for
p,q\in\mathds{R}.

Let us comment briefly on
1.1
. The uniform continuity of
a_{\theta\nu}(\cdot)
is required for the stability of viscosity sub or supersolutions under appropriate limits and useful in
Lemma

A.1
which is a key step for proving interior
C^{1,\gamma}
regularity (cf.
Lemma

2.1
). The
1.1
(b) includes a large class of kernels. We mention some of them below.

Example 1.1
.

Consider the following kernels
N_{\theta\nu}(x,y):

(i)

N_{\theta\nu}(x,y)=\frac{1}{|y|^{d+\sigma}}
for
\sigma\in(0,2).
Clearly we can take
k(y)=\frac{1}{|y|^{d+\sigma}}
and
\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}
is finite for
\alpha\in[1+\sigma/2,2).

(ii)

N_{\theta\nu}(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{i}}{|y|^{d+\sigma_{i}}}
for
\sigma_{i}\in(0,2)
,
\sigma_{0}=\sup_{i}\sigma_{i}<2
and
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}a_{i}=1.
Similarly taking
N_{\theta\nu}(x,y)=k(y)
we can see
\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha})k(y)<+\infty
for
\alpha\in[1+\sigma_{0}/2,2)
.

(iii)

N_{\theta\nu}(x,y)=\begin{cases}\frac{(1-\log|y|)^{\beta}}{|y|^{d+\sigma}}\,\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,\,0<|y|\leq 1\\
\frac{(1+\log|y|)^{-\beta}}{|y|^{d+\sigma}}\,\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,\,|y|\geq 1,\end{cases}

where
\sigma\in(0,2).

(a) For
2(2-\sigma)>\beta\geq 0,
taking
N_{\theta\nu}(x,y)=k(y)
we have
\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}<+\infty
for
\alpha\in[1+\frac{\sigma}{2}+\frac{\beta}{4},2).

(b) For
-\sigma<\beta<0,
taking
N_{\theta\nu}(x,y)=k(y)
we have
\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}<+\infty
for
\alpha\in[1+\frac{\sigma}{2},2).

Proof of (a):

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}=\int_{|y|\leq 1}\frac{|y|^{\alpha}(1-\log|y|)^{\beta}}{|y|^{d+\sigma}}\mathrm{d}{y}+\int_{|y|>1}\frac{(1+\log|y|)^{-\beta}}{|y|^{d+\sigma}}\mathrm{d}{y}:=I_{1}+I_{2}.

Using
(1-\log|y|)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|y|}}+1
and the convexity of
\xi(t)=t^{p}
for
p\geq 1
we get

(1-\log|y|)^{\beta}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{|y|^{\beta/2}}+1\right).

Therefore

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq\int_{|y|\leq 1}\frac{C\mathrm{d}{y}}{|y|^{\beta/2+d+\sigma-\alpha}}+\int_{|y|\leq 1}\frac{C\mathrm{d}{y}}{|y|^{d+\sigma-\alpha}}<+\infty\,\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,\,\alpha\in[1+\sigma/2+\beta/4,2),

and

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq\int_{|y|>1}\frac{\mathrm{d}{y}}{|y|^{d+\sigma}}<+\infty.

Proof of (b):
Since
\beta<0
in this case, we have
(1-\log|y|)^{\beta}\leq 1
and
I_{1}<+\infty
for
\alpha\in[1+\frac{\sigma}{2},2).
To estimate
I_{2},
observe
(1+\log|y|)^{-\beta}\leq(1+|y|)^{-\beta}
and

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq C\int_{|y|>1}\frac{(1+|y|^{-\beta})}{|y|^{d+\sigma}}\mathrm{d}{y}<+\infty\,\,\,\,\text{since}\,\,\,\,\sigma>-\beta.

(iv)

N_{\theta\nu}(x,y)=\frac{\Psi(1/|y|^{2})}{|y|^{d+\sigma(x,y)}},
where
\sigma:{\mathds{R}^{d}}\times{\mathds{R}^{d}}\to\mathds{R}
satisfying

0<\sigma^{-}:=\inf_{(x,y)\in{\mathds{R}^{d}}\times{\mathds{R}^{d}}}\sigma(x,y)\leq\sup_{(x,y)\in{\mathds{R}^{d}}\times{\mathds{R}^{d}}}\sigma(x,y):=\sigma^{+}<2.

and
\Psi
is a Bernstein function (for several examples of such functions, see
[
50
]
) vanishing at zero. Furthermore,
\Psi
is non-decreasing, concave and satisfies a
weak upper scaling property
i.e, there exists
\mu\geq 0
and
c\in(0,1]
such that

\Psi(\lambda x)\leq c\lambda^{\mu}\Psi(x)\,\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,\,x\geq s_{0}>0,\lambda\geq 1.

For
\mu<2(2-\sigma^{+})
, we can take

\displaystyle k(y)=\begin{cases}\frac{\Psi(1)}{|y|^{d+2\mu+\sigma^{+}}},\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,\,0<|y|\leq 1,\\
\frac{\Psi(1)}{|y|^{d+\sigma^{-}}},\,\,\,\,&\text{if}\,\,\,\,|y|>1\end{cases}

and
\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}<+\infty
for
\alpha\in[1+\mu+\sigma^{+}/2,2).

The main purpose of this article is to establish a global Lipschitz regularity and boundary regularity of the solutions satisfying (
1.1
) under the
1.1
. On the topic of regularity theory for linear elliptic equations, Hölder estimate plays a key role and it can be obtained by using Harnack inequality. The pioneering contributions are by DeGiorgi-Nash-Moser
[
29
,
42
,
45
]
who proved
C^{\alpha}
regularity for solutions to the second order elliptic equations in divergence form with measurable coefficients under the assumption of uniform ellipticity. For equations of non-divergence form, the corresponding regularity theory was established by Krylov and Safonov
[
41
]
. We refer
[
16
]
for a comprehensive overview on the regularity theory for fully nonlinear elliptic equations. In
[
40
]
, Krylov studied the boundary regularity for local second order elliptic equations in non-divergence form with bounded measurable coefficients. He obtained the Hölder regularity of
\frac{u}{\delta}
up to the boundary where
\delta
denotes the distance function, i.e,
\delta(x)=\dist(x,\Omega^{c})
.

Turning our attention towards the nonlocal equations, first Hölder estimates and Harnack inequalities for
s
-harmonic functions are proved by Bass and Kassmann
[
3
,
5
,
4
]
, however their approach was purely probabilistic. In the realm of analytic setup, Silvestre
[
52
]
proved Hölder continuity of
u
satisfying (
1.3
) with some structural assumptions on the operator and kernel related to the assumptions of Bass and Kassmann. Analogous to the local case
[
40
]
, in the nonlocal setting, for a bounded domain
\Omega\subset{\mathds{R}^{d}}
with
C^{1,1}
boundary the first result concerning boundary regularity of
u
solving the Dirichlet problem for
(-\Delta)^{s}
with bounded right hand side is obtained by Ros-Oton and Serra
[
46
]
where they established a Hölder regularity of
u/\delta^{s}
up to the boundary. This result is proved by using a method of Krylov (see
[
33
]
). The idea is to obtain a bound for
u
with respect to a constant multiple of
\delta^{s}
and this controls the oscillation of
u/\delta^{s}
near the boundary
\partial\Omega.
The Hölder regularity of
u/\delta^{s},
(i) for more general nonlocal linear operators with
C^{1,\alpha}
domain is established in
[
48
]
, (ii) for smooth domain with smooth right hand side is established in
[
30
,
31
]
, (iii) for kernel with variable order see
[
34
]
and (iv) for Dirichlet problem for fractional
p
-Laplacian, see
[
32
]
.

In a seminal paper, Caffarelli and Silvestre
[
17
]
studied the regularity theory for fully nonlinear integro-differential equations of the form :
\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\inf_{\nu\in\Gamma}{\mathcal{I}}[x,u]
where
{\mathcal{I}}[x,u]
is given by (
1.3
). By obtaining a nonlocal ABP estimate, they established the Hölder regularity and Harnack inequality when
N_{\theta\nu}(y)
(
N_{\theta\nu}(y)
denotes the
x
-independent form of
N_{\theta\nu}(x,y)
) is positive, symmetric and comparable with the kernel of the fractional Laplacian. From a large amount of literature that extend the work of Caffarelli and Silvestre
[
17
]
, we mention
[
36
]
where the authors considered integro-PDEs with regularly varying kernel,
[
9
,
19
,
37
]
where regularity results are obtained for symmetric and non-symmetric stable-like operators and
[
35
]
for kernels with variable order. Also a recent paper
[
38
]
studies Hölder regularity and a scale invariant Harnack inequality under some weak scaling condition on the kernel. Boundary regularity results for fully nonlinear integro-differential equations are obtained by Ros-Oton and Serra in
[
47
]
. They considered a restricted class of kernels
{\mathscr{L}}_{*}
where
N_{\theta\nu}(x,y)
is
x
-independent and of the following form

N_{\theta\nu}(y):=\frac{\mu(y/|y|)}{|y|^{d+2s}}\,\,\,\,\,\text{with}\,\,\,\,\mu\in L^{\infty}(S^{d-1}),

satisfying
\mu(\theta)=\mu(-\theta)
and
\lambda\leq\mu\leq\Lambda
where
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
are the ellipticity constants. An interesting feature of
{\mathscr{L}}_{*}
is

L(x_{d})_{+}^{s}=0\,\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,\,\{x_{d}>0\}\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,\,L\in{\mathscr{L}}_{*}

which is useful to construct barriers in their case. Note that our operators do not enjoy such property for having different orders. Furthermore, with
1.1
the nonlocal part (
1.3
) is not scale invariant in our case, that is one may not find any
0\leq\beta\leq 2
such that
{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}[x,u(r\cdot)]=r^{\beta}{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}[rx,u(\cdot)]
for any
0<r<1.

Recently, the mathematical study of mixed local-nonlocal integro-differential equations have been received a considerable attention, for instance see
[

,

,

,

,

More recently global Lipschitz regularity (compare it with Biagi et. al.

[

. To do such analysis, along with several careful estimates, the authors borrowed a Harnack inequality from
[

In this current contribution, we continue the study started in
[
11
]
to obtain the boundary regularity for fully nonlinear integro-differential problems of the form (

satisfying (

to be

and

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
domain in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
and
u
be a continuous function which solves the in-equations (
1.1
)
in viscosity sense. Then
u
is in
C^{0,1}({\mathds{R}^{d}})
and there exists a constant

C
, depending only on

\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{2})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}
, such that

(1.5)

\|u\|_{C^{0,1}({\mathds{R}^{d}})}\leq CK.

To prove
Theorem

1.1
, the first step is to show that the distance function
\delta(x)=\dist(x,\Omega^{c})
can be used as a barrier to
u
in
\Omega.
Once this is done, we can complete the proof by considering different cases depending on the distance between any two points in
\Omega
or their distance from
\partial\Omega
and combining
|u|\leq C\delta
with an interior
C^{1,\gamma}
-estimate for scaled operators (cf.
Lemma

2.1
).

Next we show the fine boundary regularity, that is the Hölder regularity of
u/\delta
up to the boundary.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
1.1
holds. Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{2}
domain and
u
be a viscosity
solution to the in-equations (
1.1
).
Then there exists

\upkappa\in(0,\hat{\alpha})
such that

\lVert u/\delta\rVert_{C^{\upkappa}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C_{1}K,

for some constant
C_{1}
, where

\upkappa,C_{1}
depend on
d,\Omega,C_{0},\Lambda,\lambda,\alpha
and
\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}
. Here

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}=\begin{cases}1\,\,\,\,&if\,\,\,\,\alpha\in(0,1]\\
\frac{2-\alpha}{2}\,\,\,\,&if\,\,\,\,\alpha\in(1,2).\end{cases}

To prove
Theorem

1.2
, following
[
46
]
we prove an oscillation lemma (cf.

4.1
). For this, first we need to construct sub and supersolutions carefully since

Now applying (

2.
Notation and preliminary results

This section sets the notation which we use throughout the paper and collects the necessary results.

2.1.
Notations and Definitions

We start by setting the notations. We use
{\mathscr{B}}_{r}(x)
to denote an open ball of radius
r>0
centred at a point
x\in{\mathds{R}^{d}}
and for
x=0,
we denote
{\mathscr{B}}_{r}:={\mathscr{B}}_{r}(0).
For any subset
U\subseteq{\mathds{R}^{d}}
and for
\alpha\in(0,1),
we denote
C^{\alpha}(U)
as the space of all bounded,
\alpha
-Hölder continuous functions equipped with the norm

||f||_{C^{\alpha}(U)}:=\sup_{x\in U}|f(x)|+\sup_{x,y\in U}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

Note that for
\alpha=1,

C^{0,1}(U)
denotes the space of all Lipschitz continuous functions on
U.
The space of all bounded functions with bounded
\alpha
-Hölder continuous derivatives is denoted by
C^{1,\alpha}(U)
with the norm

||f||_{C^{1,\alpha}(U)}:=\sup_{x\in U}|f(x)|+||Df||_{C^{\alpha}(U)}.

We use
USC({\mathds{R}^{d}}),LSC({\mathds{R}^{d}}),C({\mathds{R}^{d}}),C_{b}({\mathds{R}^{d}}),M^{d}
to denote the space of upper semicontinuous, lower semicontinuous, continuous functions, bounded continuous functions on
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
and
d\times d
symmetric matrices respectively.

Now let us introduce the scaled operators.
For
0<s\leq 1,
we define scaled version of (
1.2
) as following.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}^{s}[x,u]=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\inf_{\nu\in\Gamma}\left\{\trace a_{\theta\nu}(sx)D^{2}u(x)+{\mathcal{I}}^{s}_{\theta\nu}[x,u]\right\},

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}^{s}_{\theta\nu}[x,u]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u(x+y)-u(x)-\mathds{1}_{{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{s}}}(y)\nabla u(x)\cdot y)s^{d+2}N_{\theta\nu}(sx,sy)\mathrm{d}{y}.

Next we define extremal Pucci operators for second order term and the nonlocal term.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}^{+}u(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup\left\{\trace(AD^{2}u(x)),A\in M^{d},\lambda\bm{I}\leq A\leq\Lambda\bm{I}\right\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}^{-}u(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\left\{\trace(AD^{2}u(x)),A\in M^{d},\lambda\bm{I}\leq A\leq\Lambda\bm{I}\right\},

and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k,s}u(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(u(x+y)-u(x)-\mathds{1}_{{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{s}}}(y)\nabla u(x)\cdot y)^{+}s^{d+2}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}^{-}_{k,s}u(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq-\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(u(x+y)-u(x)-\mathds{1}_{{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{s}}}(y)\nabla u(x)\cdot y)^{-}s^{d+2}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}.

Denote
{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k,1}={\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}
and
{\mathcal{P}}^{-}_{k,1}={\mathcal{P}}^{-}_{k}.

We recall the definition of viscosity sub and supersolution. First of all, we say a function
\varphi
touches from above (below) at
x
if, for a small
r>0,

\varphi(x)=u(x)\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,u(y)\leq(\geq)\varphi(y)\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\,\,y\in{\mathscr{B}}_{r}(x).

Definition 2.1
.

A function
u\in USC({\mathds{R}^{d}})\cap L^{\infty}({\mathds{R}^{d}})
(resp.
u\in LSC({\mathds{R}^{d}})\cap L^{\infty}({\mathds{R}^{d}})
) is said to be a viscosity subsolution (resp. supersolution) to (
1.1
) if whenever
\varphi
touches
u
from above (resp. below) for some bounded test function
\varphi\in C^{2}({\mathscr{B}}_{r}(x))\cap C({\mathds{R}^{d}}),
then

\displaystyle v=\begin{cases}\varphi\,\,\,\,&\text{in}\,\,\,{\mathscr{B}}_{r}(x)\\
u\,\,\,\,&\text{in}\,\,\,{\mathscr{B}}^{c}_{r}(x)\end{cases}

satisfies
{\mathcal{L}}v(x)+C_{0}|Dv(x)|\geq-K
(resp.
{\mathcal{L}}v(x)-C_{0}|Dv(x)|\leq K
).

2.2.
Auxiliary lemmas

We collect some preliminary results here. The first result is the interior
C^{1,\gamma}
regularity for the scaled operator
{\mathcal{L}}^{s}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
and
u\in L^{\infty}({\mathds{R}^{d}})\cap C({\mathds{R}^{d}})
solves the in-equations

(2.1)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}^{s}[x,u]+C_{0}s|Du(x)|\geq-K

\displaystyle in\,\,\,{\mathscr{B}}_{2},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}^{s}[x,u]-C_{0}s|Du(x)|\leq K

\displaystyle in\,\,\,{\mathscr{B}}_{2},

in the viscosity sense. Then there exist constants
0<\gamma<1
and
C>0
independent of
s
, such that

\displaystyle||u||_{C^{1,\gamma}({\mathscr{B}}_{1})}\leq C\Big(||u||_{L^{\infty}({\mathds{R}^{d}})}+K\Big),

where
\gamma
and
C
depend only on
d,\lambda,\Lambda,C_{0}
and
\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{2})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}
.

Proof.

The proof essentially uses the approximation arguments for nonlocal equations
[
18
]
and we postpone it to
Appendix

A
.
∎

Now we present a maximum principle type result similar to
[
10
, Theorem 5.2]
. We report the proof here for reader’s convenience.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let u be a bounded function on
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
which
is in
USC(\overline{\Omega})
and satisfies
{\mathcal{P}}^{+}u+{\mathcal{P}}_{k}^{+}u+C_{0}|Du|\geq 0
in
\Omega
.
Then we have
\sup_{\Omega}u\leq\sup_{\Omega^{c}}u.

Proof.

From
[
43
, Lemma 5.5]
we can find a non-negative function

\chi\in{C}^{2}(\bar{\Omega})\cap{C}_{b}({\mathds{R}^{d}})
satisfying

{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\chi+{\mathcal{P}}_{k}^{+}\chi+C_{0}|D\chi|\leq-1\quad\text{in}\;\Omega.

Note that, since
\chi\in C^{2}(\bar{\Omega})
,
the above inequality holds in the classical sense.
For
\varepsilon>0
,
we let
\phi_{M}
to be

\phi_{M}(x)=M+\varepsilon\chi.

Then
{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\phi_{M}(x_{0})+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}\phi_{M}+C_{0}|D\phi_{M}|\leq-\varepsilon
in
\Omega
.

Let
M_{0}
be the smallest value of
M
for which
\phi_{M}\geq u
in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
. We show that

M_{0}\leq\sup_{\Omega^{c}}u
. Suppose, to the contrary, that
M_{0}>\sup_{\Omega^{c}}u
. Then there must be a point
x_{0}\in\Omega
for which
u(x_{0})=\phi_{M_{0}}(x_{0}).
Otherwise using the upper semicontinuity of
u,
we get a
M_{1}<M_{0}
such that
\phi_{M_{1}}\geq u
in
{\mathds{R}^{d}},
which contradicts the minimality of
M_{0}
. Now
\phi_{M_{0}}
would touch
u
from above at

x_{0}
and thus, by the definition of the viscosity subsolution, we would have that
{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\phi_{M_{0}}(x_{0})+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}\phi_{M_{0}}+C_{0}|D\phi_{M_{0}}|\geq 0
. This leads to a contradiction. Therefore,

M_{0}\leq\sup_{\Omega^{c}}u
which implies that
for every
x\in{\mathds{R}^{d}}

u\leq\phi_{M_{0}}\leq M_{0}+\varepsilon\,\sup_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}\chi\leq\sup_{\Omega^{c}}u+\varepsilon\,\sup_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}\chi.

The result follows by taking
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Remark 2.1
.

Although we have the above maximum principle, one can not simply compare two viscosity sub and supersolutions for the operator (
1.2
). More precisely, if
u,v
are bounded functions and
u\in USC({\mathds{R}^{d}}),v\in LSC({\mathds{R}^{d}})
satisfy

{\mathcal{L}}u+C|Du|\geq f\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,{\mathcal{L}}v+C|Dv|\leq g\,\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,\,\Omega

in viscosity sense for two continuous functions
f
and
g,
and for some
C\geq 0,
then
{\mathcal{L}}(u-v)+C|D(u-v)|\geq f-g
may not always holds true in
\Omega
. However, if one of them is
C^{2},
then we have

{\mathcal{P}}^{+}(u-v)+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}(u-v)+C|D(u-v)|\geq f-g\,\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,\Omega.

Indeed, without loss of generality, let us assume
v\in C^{2}(\Omega)
and
\varphi
be a
C^{2}
test function that touches
u-v
at
x\in\Omega
from above then clearly
\varphi+v
touches
u
at
x
from above. By definition of viscosity subsolution we have

{\mathcal{L}}(\varphi+v)(x)+C|D(\varphi+v)(x)|\geq f(x),
which implies

{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\varphi(x)+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}\varphi(x)+{\mathcal{L}}v(x)+C|D\varphi(x)|+C|Dv(x)|\geq f(x)

and hence we obtain

{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\varphi(x)+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}\varphi(x)+C|D\varphi(x)|\geq f(x)-g(x).

∎

3.
Global Lipschitz regularity

In this section we establish the Lipschitz regularity of the solution
u
up to the boundary. We start by showing that the distance function
\delta(x)
is a barrier to
u.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
domain in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
and
u
be a continuous function which solves (
1.1
) in the viscosity sense. Then there exists a constant
C
which depends only on
d,\lambda,\Lambda,C_{0},\diam(\Omega)
, radius of exterior sphere and
\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{2})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}
, such that

(3.1)

|u(x)|\leq CK\delta(x)\quad\text{for all}\;x\in\Omega.

Proof.

First we show that

(3.2)

|u(x)|\leq\kappa\,K\quad x\in{\mathds{R}^{d}},

for some constant
\kappa
. From
[
43
, Lemma 5.5]
, there exists a non-negative function

\chi\in C^{2}(\bar{\Omega})\cap C_{b}({\mathds{R}^{d}})
, with
\inf_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}\chi>0
, satisfying

{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\chi+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}\chi+C_{0}|D\chi|\leq-1\quad\text{in}\;\,\,\Omega.

We define
\psi=K\chi
which gives that
\inf_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}\psi\geq 0
and

{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\psi+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}\psi+C_{0}|D\psi|\leq-K\quad\text{in}\;\,\,\Omega.

Then by using
Remark

2.1
, we get

{\mathcal{P}}^{+}(u-\psi)+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}(u-\psi)+C_{0}|D(u-\psi)|\geq 0.

Now applying
Lemma

2.2
on
u-\psi
we obtain

\sup_{\Omega}(u-\psi)\leq\sup_{\Omega^{c}}(u-\psi)\leq 0.

Note that in the second inequality above we used
u=0
in
\Omega^{c}.

This proves that
u\leq\psi
in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
.
Similar calculation using
-u
will also give us
-u\leq\psi

in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
. Thus

|u|\leq\sup_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}|\chi|\,K\quad\text{in}\;{\mathds{R}^{d}},

which gives (
3.2
).

Now we shall prove (
3.1
). Since
\partial\Omega
is

C^{1,1}
,
\Omega
satisfies a uniform exterior sphere condition from outside. Let
r_{\circ}
be a radius satisfying uniform exterior condition. From
[
43
, Lemma 5.4]
there exists a bounded, Lipschitz continuous function

\varphi
, Lipschitz constant being
r_{\circ}^{-1}
, satisfying

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{in}\quad\bar{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r_{\circ}},

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle>0\quad\text{in}\quad\bar{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r_{\circ}}^{c},

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon\quad\text{in}\quad{\mathscr{B}}_{(1+\delta)r_{\circ}}^{c},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\varphi+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}\varphi+C_{0}|D\varphi|

\displaystyle\leq-1\quad\text{in}\quad{\mathscr{B}}_{(1+\delta)r_{\circ}}\setminus\bar{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r_{\circ}},

for some constants
\varepsilon,\delta
, dependent on
C_{0},d,\lambda,\Lambda,d
and
\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{2}){k}(y)\mathrm{d}{y}
. Furthermore,
\varphi

is
C^{2}
in
{\mathscr{B}}_{(1+\delta)r_{\circ}}\setminus\bar{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r_{\circ}}
.
For any point
y\in\partial\Omega
,
we can find another point
z\in\Omega^{c}
such that
\overline{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r_{\circ}}(z)\subset\Omega^{c}
touches

\partial\Omega
at
y
.
Let

w(x)=\varepsilon^{-1}\kappa K\varphi(x-z)
. Also
{\mathcal{P}}^{+}(w)+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}(w)+C_{0}|Dw|\leq-K
. Then by using
Remark

2.1
we have

{\mathcal{P}}^{+}(u-w)+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}(u-w)+C_{0}|D(u-w)|\geq 0\quad\text{in}\;{\mathscr{B}}_{(1+\delta)r_{\circ}}(z)\cap\Omega.

Since, by (
3.2
)
u-w\leq 0
in
({\mathscr{B}}_{(1+\delta)r_{\circ}}(z)\cap\Omega)^{c}
, applying
Lemma

2.2
on
u-w,

it follows that

u(x)\leq w(x)
in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
. Repeating a similar calculation for
-u
,
we can conclude that
|u(x)|\leq w(x)
in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
.
Since this relation holds for any
y\in\partial\Omega,

taking
x\in\Omega
with
\dist(x,\partial\Omega)<r_{\circ}
, one can find

y\in\partial\Omega
satisfying
\dist(x,\partial\Omega)=\lvert x-y\rvert<r_{\circ}.
Then using the previous estimate
we would obtain

|u(x)|\leq\varepsilon^{-1}\kappa K\varphi(x-z)\leq\varepsilon^{-1}\kappa K(\varphi(x-z)-\varphi(y-z))\leq\varepsilon^{-1}\kappa K\,r_{\circ}^{-1}\dist(x,\partial\Omega),

which gives us (
3.1
).
∎

Now we are ready to prove that
u\in C^{0,1}({\mathds{R}^{d}})
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
s\in(0,1]
be such that

2s=\dist(x_{0},\partial\Omega)\wedge 1
. Without
loss of any generality, we assume
x_{0}=0
.
Define
v(x)=u(sx)
in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
.
Using
Lemma

3.1
we already have
|u(x)|\leq C_{1}K\delta(x)
, from that one can deduce

(3.3)

|v(x)|\leq C_{1}\,Ks(1+\lvert x\rvert)\quad\quad\text{for all}\,\,\;x\in{\mathds{R}^{d}},

for some constant
C_{1}
independent of
s
. We recall the scaled operator

{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}^{s}[x,f]:=\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(f(x+y)-f(x)-\mathds{1}_{{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{s}}}(y)\nabla f(x)\cdot y)s^{d+2}N_{\theta\nu}(sx,sy)\mathrm{d}{y}.

To compute
{\mathcal{L}}^{s}[x,v]+C_{0}s|Dv(x)|
in
{\mathscr{B}}_{2}
, first we observe that
D^{2}v(x)=s^{2}D^{2}u(sx)
and
Dv(x)=sDu(sx)
. Also

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}^{s}[x,v]

\displaystyle=s^{2}\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(v(x+y)-v(x)-\mathds{1}_{{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{s}}}(y)\nabla v(x)\cdot y)N_{\theta\nu}(sx,sy)s^{d}\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle=s^{2}\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(u(sx+sy)-u(sx)-\mathds{1}_{{\mathscr{B}}_{1}}(sy)\nabla u(sx)\cdot sy)N_{\theta\nu}(sx,sy)s^{d}\mathrm{d}{y}=s^{2}{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}[sx,u].

Thus, it follows from (
1.1
) that

(3.4)

\begin{split}{\mathcal{L}}^{s}[x,v]+C_{0}s|Dv(x)|&\geq-Ks^{2}\quad\text{in}\quad{\mathscr{B}}_{2},\\
{\mathcal{L}}^{s}[x,v]-C_{0}s|Dv(x)|&\leq Ks^{2}\quad\text{in}\quad{\mathscr{B}}_{2}.\end{split}

Now consider a smooth cut-off function
\varphi,0\leq\varphi\leq 1
, satisfying

\varphi=\begin{cases}1\quad\text{in}\;{\mathscr{B}}_{3/2},\\
0\quad\text{in}\;{\mathscr{B}}^{c}_{2}.\end{cases}

Let
w=\varphi v
. Clearly,
((\varphi-1)v)(y)=0
for all
y\in{\mathscr{B}}_{3/2}
, which gives
D((\varphi-1)v)=0
and
D^{2}((\varphi-1)v)=0
in
x\in{\mathscr{B}}_{3/2}
. Since
w=v+(\varphi-1)v
, from (
3.4
) we obtain

(3.5)

\begin{split}{\mathcal{L}}^{s}[x,w]+C_{0}s|Dw(x)|&\geq-Ks^{2}-|\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\inf_{\nu\in\Gamma}{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}^{s}[x,(\varphi-1)v)]|\quad\text{in}\quad{\mathscr{B}}_{1},\\
{\mathcal{L}}^{s}[x,w]-C_{0}s|Dw(x)|&\leq Ks^{2}+|\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\inf_{\nu\in\Gamma}{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}^{s}[x,(\varphi-1)v)]|\quad\text{in}\quad{\mathscr{B}}_{1}.\end{split}

Again, since
(\varphi-1)v=0
in
{\mathscr{B}}_{3/2}
,
we have in
{\mathscr{B}}_{1}
that

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}^{s}[x,(\varphi-1)v]|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{|y|\geq 1/2}((\varphi-1)v)(x+y)-((\varphi-1)v)(x))s^{d+2}N_{\theta\nu}(sx,sy)\mathrm{d}{y}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|y|\geq 1/2}|v(x+y)|s^{d+2}N_{\theta\nu}(sx,sy)\mathrm{d}{y}+|v(x)|\int_{|y|\geq 1/2}s^{d+2}N_{\theta\nu}(sx,sy)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle:=I_{1}+I_{2}.

Since
x\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1},
using
s^{d+2}N_{\theta\nu}(sx,sy)\leq s^{d+2}k(sy)
and (
3.3
) we can have the following estimate,

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq 2C_{1}Ks\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{2})\mathrm{d}{y}.

Now write

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{1/2\leq|y|\leq 1/s}|v(x+y)|s^{d+2}N_{\theta\nu}(sx,sy)\mathrm{d}{y}+\int_{|y|\geq 1/s}|v(x+y)|s^{d+2}N_{\theta\nu}(sx,sy)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle=I_{s,1}+I_{s,2}\,.

Let us first estimate
I_{s,1}.
Since
x\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}
and
|y|\geq\frac{1}{2}
we have
1+|x+y|\leq 5|y|.
By using this estimate and (
3.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle I_{s,1}

\displaystyle=s^{d+2}\int_{\frac{1}{2}\leq\lvert y\rvert\leq\frac{1}{s}}|v(x+y)|N_{\theta\nu}(sx,sy)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle\leq 5C_{1}K\int_{\frac{1}{2}\leq\lvert y\rvert\leq\frac{1}{s}}|sy|s^{d+2}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}\leq 5C_{1}Ks\int_{\frac{s}{2}\leq\lvert z\rvert\leq 1}|sz|k(z)\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}s\int_{\frac{s}{2}\leq\lvert z\rvert\leq 1}|z|^{2}k(z)\mathrm{d}{z}\leq C_{2}s\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{2})k(z)\mathrm{d}{z}\leq C_{3}s,

for some constants
C_{3}.
For
I_{s,2}
, a change of variable and (
3.2
) gives

\displaystyle I_{s,2}\leq\kappa s^{2}K\int_{s\lvert y\rvert>1}s^{d}k(ry)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle=\kappa s^{2}K\int_{\lvert y\rvert>1}k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa s^{2}K\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{2})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}\leq C_{4}s^{2}K

for some constant
C_{4}
. Therefore, putting the estimates of

I_{1}
and
I_{2}
in (
3.5
) we obtain

(3.6)

\begin{split}{\mathcal{L}}^{s}[x,w]+C_{0}s|Dw(x)|&\geq-C_{5}Ks\quad\text{in}\quad{\mathscr{B}}_{1},\\
{\mathcal{L}}^{s}[x,w]-C_{0}s|Dw(x)|&\geq C_{5}Ks\quad\text{in}\quad{\mathscr{B}}_{1},\end{split}

for some constant
C_{5}
. Now applying
Lemma

2.1
, from (
3.6
)
we have

(3.7)

\lVert v\rVert_{C^{1}({\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{2}})}\leq C_{6}\Big(\lVert v\rVert_{L^{\infty}({\mathscr{B}}_{2})}+sK\Big)

for some constant
C_{6}
. From
(
3.3
) and (
3.7
) we then obtain

(3.8)

\sup_{y\in{\mathscr{B}}_{s/2}(x),y\neq x}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|}\leq C_{7}K,

for some constant
C_{7}
.

Now we can complete the proof. Note that if
|x-y|\geq\frac{1}{8}
, then

\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|}\leq 2\kappa K,

by (
3.2
). So we consider
|x-y|<\frac{1}{8}
. If

|x-y|\geq 8^{-1}(\delta(x)\vee\delta(y))
, then using

Lemma

3.1
we get

\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|}\leq 4CK(\delta(x)+\delta(y))(\delta(x)\vee\delta(y))^{-1}\leq 8CK.

Now let
|x-y|<8^{-1}\min\{\delta(x)\vee\delta(y),1\}
.
Then either
y\in{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\delta(x)\wedge 1}{8}}(x)
or
x\in{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\delta(y)\wedge 1}{8}}(y)
. Without loss of generality, we suppose

y\in{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\delta(x)\wedge 1}{8}}(x)
.
From (
3.8
) we get

\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|}\leq C_{7}K.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Fine boundary regularity

Aim of this section is to prove
Theorem

1.2
. Since
u
is Lipschitz, (
1.1
) can be written as

|{\mathcal{L}}u|\leq CK\,\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,\Omega,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,u=0\,\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,\Omega^{c}.

We start by constructing subsolutions which will be useful later on to prove oscillation lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a constant
\tilde{\kappa}
, which depends only on
d,\lambda,\Lambda,\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{2})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}
, such that for any
r\in(0,1]
, we have a bounded
radial function
\phi_{r}
satisfying

\begin{cases}{\mathcal{P}}^{-}\phi_{r}+{\mathcal{P}}^{-}_{k}\phi_{r}\geq 0&\text{in}\;\;{\mathscr{B}}_{4r}\setminus\bar{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r},\\
0\leq\phi_{r}\leq\tilde{\kappa}r&\text{in}\;\;{\mathscr{B}}_{r},\\
\phi_{r}\geq\frac{1}{\tilde{\kappa}}(4r-\lvert x\rvert)&\text{in}\;\;{\mathscr{B}}_{4r}\setminus{\mathscr{B}}_{r},\\
\phi_{r}\leq 0&\text{in}\;\;{\mathds{R}^{d}}\setminus{\mathscr{B}}_{4r}.\end{cases}

Moreover,
\phi_{r}\in C^{2}({\mathscr{B}}_{4r}\setminus\bar{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r})
.

Proof.

We use the same subsolution constructed in
[
11
]
and show that it is indeed a subsolution with respect to minimal Pucci operators.
Fix
r\in(0,1]
and define
v_{r}(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-\eta q(x)}-\mathrm{e}^{-\eta(4r)^{2}}
, where
q(x)=|x|^{2}\wedge 2(4r)^{2}
and

\eta>0
. Clearly,
1\geq v_{r}(0)\geq v_{r}(x)
for all
x\in{\mathds{R}^{d}}
. Thus using the fact that
1-e^{-\xi}\leq\xi
for all
\xi\geq 0
we have

(4.1)

v_{r}(x)\leq 1-\mathrm{e}^{-\eta(4r)^{2}}\leq\eta(4r)^{2},

Again for
x\in{\mathscr{B}}_{4r}\setminus{\mathscr{B}}_{r}
, we have that

\displaystyle v_{r}(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-\eta(4r)^{2}}(\mathrm{e}^{\eta((4r)^{2}-q(x))}-1)

\displaystyle\geq\eta\mathrm{e}^{-\eta(4r)^{2}}((4r)^{2}-|x|^{2})

(4.2)

\displaystyle=\eta\mathrm{e}^{-\eta(4r)^{2}}(4r+|x|)(4r-|x|)\geq 5\eta r\mathrm{e}^{-\eta(4r)^{2}}(4r-|x|).

Fix
x\in{\mathscr{B}}_{4r}\setminus\bar{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r}
. We start by estimating the local minimal Pucci operator
{\mathcal{P}}^{-}
of
v
. Using rotational symmetry we may always assume
x=(l,0,\cdots,0)
Then

\partial_{i}v_{r}(x)=-2\eta\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|x|^{2}}x_{i}=\begin{cases}-2\eta\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|x|^{2}}l&i=1,\\
0&i\neq 1\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\partial_{ij}v_{r}(x)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}4\eta^{2}x_{i}^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|x|^{2}}-2\eta\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|x|^{2}}&i=j,\\
4\eta^{2}x_{i}x_{j}\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|x|^{2}}&i\neq j.\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}4\eta^{2}l^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|x|^{2}}-2\eta\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|x|^{2}}&i=j=1,\\
-2\eta\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|x|^{2}}&i=j\neq 1,\\
0&i\neq j.\end{cases}

By the above calculation, for any
x\in{\mathscr{B}}_{4r}\setminus\bar{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r},
choosing
\eta>\frac{1}{r^{2}}
we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}^{-}v_{r}(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda 4\eta^{2}l^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|x|^{2}}-\lambda 2\eta\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|x|^{2}}-\Lambda(d-1)2\eta\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|x|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda 4\eta^{2}l^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|x|^{2}}-d\Lambda 2\eta\mathrm{e}^{-\eta|x|^{2}}.

Now to determine nonlocal minimal Pucci operator,
using the convexity
of exponential map we get,

\displaystyle e^{-\eta|x+y|^{2}}-e^{-\eta|x|^{2}}+2\eta\mathds{1}_{\{|y|\leq 1\}}y\cdot xe^{-\eta|x|^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\geq-\eta e^{-\eta|x|^{2}}\left(|x+y|^{2}-|x|^{2}-2\mathds{1}_{\{|y|\leq 1\}}y\cdot x\right).

Since
{\mathcal{P}}^{-}_{k}v_{r}={\mathcal{P}}^{-}_{k}(v_{r}+e^{-\eta(4r)^{2}})
and using above inequality we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}^{-}_{k}(e^{-\eta q(\cdot)})(x)

\displaystyle=-\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}\left(e^{-\eta q(x+y)}-e^{-\eta q(x)}-\mathds{1}_{{\mathscr{B}}_{1}}(y)\nabla e^{-\eta q(x)}\cdot y\right)^{-}k(y)dy

\displaystyle\geq-\eta e^{-\eta|x|^{2}}\int_{|y|\leq r}\Big(|x+y|^{2}-|x|^{2}-2y\cdot x\Big)k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle-\int_{r<|y|\leq 1}\left|e^{-\eta(|x|^{2}+2(4r)^{2})}-e^{-\eta|x|^{2}}+2\eta y\cdot xe^{-\eta|x|^{2}}\right|k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle-\int_{|y|>1}\left|e^{-\eta(|x|^{2}+2(4r)^{2})}-e^{-\eta|x|^{2}}\right|k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle\geq-\eta e^{-\eta|x|^{2}}\left[\int_{\lvert y\rvert<r}\lvert y\rvert^{2}k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}+\int_{r<|y|\leq 1}\left(\left|\frac{1-e^{-2\eta(4r)^{2}}}{\eta}\right|+2|x\cdot y|\right)k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}\right]

\displaystyle-\eta e^{-\eta|x|^{2}}\int_{|y|>1}\left|\frac{1-e^{-2\eta(4r)^{2}}}{\eta}\right|k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle\geq-\eta e^{-\eta|x|^{2}}\left[\int_{\lvert y\rvert<r}\lvert y\rvert^{2}k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}+\int_{r<\lvert y\rvert\leq 1}4^{3}\lvert y\rvert^{2}k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}+\int_{\lvert y\rvert>1}2(4r)^{2}k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}\right]

\displaystyle\geq-\eta e^{-\eta|x|^{2}}4^{3}\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{2})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y},

where in the second line we used
|x+y|^{2}\wedge 2(4r)^{2}\leq|x|^{2}+2(4r)^{2}
. Combining the above estimates we see that, for
x\in{\mathscr{B}}_{4r}\setminus\bar{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r}
,

\displaystyle P^{-}v_{r}(x)+P_{k}^{-}v_{r}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\eta\mathrm{e}^{-\eta\lvert x\rvert^{2}}\Bigl[4\eta\lambda|x|^{2}-2d\Lambda-4^{3}\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{2})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\geq\eta\mathrm{e}^{-\eta\lvert x\rvert^{2}}\Bigl[4\eta\lambda r^{2}-2d\Lambda-4^{3}\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{2})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}\Bigr].

Thus, finally letting
\eta=\frac{1}{\lambda r^{2}}(2d\Lambda+4^{3}\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{2})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y})
, we obtain

{\mathcal{P}}^{-}v_{r}+{\mathcal{P}}^{-}v_{r}>0\quad\text{in}\;{\mathscr{B}}_{4r}\setminus\bar{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r}.

Note that the final choice of
\eta
is admissible since
\frac{1}{\lambda r^{2}}(2d\Lambda+4^{3}\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{2})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y})>\frac{1}{r^{2}}.

Now set
\phi_{r}=rv_{r}
and the result follows from (
4.1
)-(
4
).
∎

Next we prove a
weak version
of Harnack inequality.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1],

\alpha^{\prime}=1\wedge(2-\alpha)
and
u
be a continuous non-negative function satisfying

\mathcal{P}^{-}u+\mathcal{P}^{-}_{k,s}u\leq C_{0}s^{1+\alpha^{\prime}},\quad\mathcal{P}^{+}u+\mathcal{P}^{+}_{k,s}u\geq-C_{0}s^{1+\alpha^{\prime}}\quad\text{in}\;{\mathscr{B}}_{2}.

Furthermore if
\sup_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}|u|\leq M_{0}
and
|u(x)|\leq M_{0}s(1+|x|)
for all
x\in{\mathds{R}^{d}},

then

u(x)\leq C(u(0)+(M_{0}\vee C_{0})s^{1+\alpha^{\prime}})

for every
x\in{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{2}}
and for some constant

C
which only depends on
\lambda,\Lambda,d,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}.

Proof.

Dividing by
u(0)+(M_{0}\vee C_{0})s^{1+\alpha^{\prime}},
it
can be easily seen that
\sup_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}|u|\leq s^{-(1+\alpha^{\prime})}
and
|u(x)|\leq s^{-\alpha^{\prime}}(1+|x|)
for all
x\in{\mathds{R}^{d}}
and
u
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{-}u+\mathcal{P}^{-}_{k,s}u\leq 1,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{+}u+\mathcal{P}^{+}_{k,s}u\geq-1.

Fix

\varepsilon>0
from
[
43
, Corollary 3.14]
and
let
\gamma=\frac{d}{\varepsilon}
.
Let

t_{0}\coloneqq\min\left\{t\;:\;u(x)\leq h_{t}(x)\coloneqq t(1-\lvert x\rvert)^{-\gamma}\;\;\text{for all}\;x\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}\right\}.

Clearly this set is nonempty since
u(0)\leq 1
, thus
t_{0}
exist. Let
x_{0}\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}
be such that
u(x_{0})=h_{t_{0}}(x_{0})
. Let

\eta=1-|x_{0}|
be the distance of
x_{0}
from
\partial{\mathscr{B}}_{1}
. For
r=\frac{\eta}{2}
and
x\in{\mathscr{B}}_{r}(x_{0}),
we can write

{\mathscr{B}}_{r}(x_{0})=\left\{u(x)\leq\frac{u(x_{0})}{2}\right\}\cup\left\{u(x)>\frac{u(x_{0})}{2}\right\}:=A+\tilde{A}.

The goal is to estimate
|{\mathscr{B}}_{r}(x_{0})|
in terms of
|A|
and
|\tilde{A}|.
Proceeding this way, we show that
t_{0}<C
for some universal
C
which, in turn, implies that
u(x)<C(1-\lvert x\rvert)^{-\gamma}
.
This would prove our result.
Next, Using
[
43
, Corollary 3.14]
we obtain

|\tilde{A}\cap{\mathscr{B}}_{1}|\leq C\bigg\lvert\frac{2}{u(x_{0})}\bigg\rvert^{\varepsilon}\leq Ct_{0}^{-\varepsilon}\eta^{d}\,,

whereas
|{\mathscr{B}}_{r}|=\omega_{d}(\eta/2)^{d}
. In particular,

(4.3)

\big\lvert\tilde{A}\cap{\mathscr{B}}_{r}(x_{0})\big\rvert\leq Ct_{0}^{-\varepsilon}|{\mathscr{B}}_{r}|.

So if
t_{0}
is large,

\tilde{A}
can cover only a small portion of
{\mathscr{B}}_{r}(x_{0})
. We shall show that for some
\delta>0
,
independent of
t_{0}
we have

|A\cap{\mathscr{B}}_{r}(x_{0})|\leq(1-\delta)|{\mathscr{B}}_{r}|,

which will provide an upper bound on
t_{0}
completing the proof. We start by estimating
|A\cap{\mathscr{B}}_{\uptheta r}(x_{0})|
for
\uptheta>0
small. For every
x\in{\mathscr{B}}_{\uptheta r}(x_{0})
we have

u(x)\leq\,h_{t_{0}}(x)\,\leq\,t_{0}\left(\frac{2\eta-\uptheta\eta}{2}\right)^{-\gamma}\,\leq\,u(x_{0})\left(1-\frac{\uptheta}{2}\right)^{-\gamma},

with
\left(1-\frac{\uptheta}{2}\right)
close to 1.
Define

v(x)\coloneqq\left(1-\frac{\uptheta}{2}\right)^{-\gamma}u(x_{0})-u(x).

Then we get
v\geq 0
in
{\mathscr{B}}_{\uptheta r}(x_{0})
and also
\mathcal{P}^{-}v+\mathcal{P}^{-}_{k,s}v\leq 1
as

\mathcal{P}^{+}u+\mathcal{P}^{+}_{k,s}u\geq-1.

We would like to apply
[
43
, Corollary 3.14]
to
v,
but
v
need not be non-negative in the whole of
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
. Thus we consider the positive part of
v,
i.e,
w=v^{+}
and find an upper bound of
\mathcal{P}^{-}w+\mathcal{P}^{-}_{k,s}w
. Since
v^{-}
is
C^{2}
in
{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{4}}(x_{0}),
we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{-}w(x)+\mathcal{P}^{-}_{k,s}w(x)\leq[\mathcal{P}^{-}v(x)+\mathcal{P}^{-}_{k,s}v(x)]+[\mathcal{P}^{+}v^{-}(x)+\mathcal{P}^{+}_{k,s}v^{-}(x)]\leq 1+\mathcal{P}^{+}v^{-}(x)+\mathcal{P}^{+}_{k,s}v^{-}(x).

Also, using
v^{-}(x)=Dv^{-}(x)=D^{2}v^{-}(x)=0
for all
x\in{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{4}}(x_{0}),
we
get

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{+}v^{-}(x)+\mathcal{P}^{+}_{k,s}v^{-}(x)=\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}\cap\{v(x+y)\leq 0\}}v^{-}(x+y)s^{d+2}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}.

Now plugging (
4.5
) into (
4.4
), for all
x\in{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{4}}(x_{0})
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{-}w(x)+\mathcal{P}^{-}_{k,s}w(x)\leq 1+\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}\setminus{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{2}}(x-x_{0})}\left(u(x+y)-\left(1-\frac{\uptheta}{2}\right)^{-\gamma}u(x_{0})\right)^{+}s^{d+2}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}\setminus{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{2}}(x-x_{0})}\left|u(x+y)\right|s^{d+2}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}+\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}\setminus{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{2}}(x-x_{0})}\left|\left(1-\frac{\uptheta}{2}\right)^{-\gamma}u(x_{0})\right|s^{d+2}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}\setminus{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{4}}}\left|u(x+y)\right|s^{d+2}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}+\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}\setminus{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{4}}}\left|\left(1-\frac{\uptheta}{2}\right)^{-\gamma}u(x_{0})\right|s^{d+2}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}:=1+I_{1}+I_{2}.

Estimate of
I_{1}
:
Let us write

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{\uptheta r}{4}\leq|y|\leq\frac{1}{s}}\left|u(x+y)\right|s^{d+2}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}+\int_{|y|\geq\frac{1}{s}}\left|u(x+y)\right|s^{d+2}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}:=I_{11}+I_{12}.

Simply using change of variable and
\sup_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}|u|\leq s^{-(1+\alpha^{\prime})},
we obtain

I_{12}\leq\int_{|z|\geq 1}k(z)\mathrm{d}{z}.

Now we estimate
I_{11}
using
|u(x)|\leq s^{-\alpha^{\prime}}(1+|x|)
for all
x\in{\mathds{R}^{d}}
.

\displaystyle I_{11}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\frac{\uptheta r}{4}\leq|y|\leq\frac{1}{s}}\left(1+|x+y|\right)s^{d+2-\alpha^{\prime}}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5}{4}\int_{\frac{\uptheta r}{4}\leq|y|\leq\frac{1}{s}}s^{d+2-\alpha^{\prime}}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}+\int_{\frac{\uptheta r}{4}\leq|y|\leq\frac{1}{s}}s^{d+2-\alpha^{\prime}}|y|k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}\,.

We consider two cases. First consider the case
\alpha^{\prime}=1
so
\alpha\leq 1
. This implies

I_{11}\leq\frac{5}{4}\int_{\frac{\uptheta rs}{4}\leq|z|\leq 1}sk(z)\mathrm{d}{z}+\int_{\frac{\uptheta rs}{4}\leq|z|\leq 1}|z|k(z)\mathrm{d}{z}\leq 6(\uptheta r)^{-1}\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|z|^{\alpha})k(z)\mathrm{d}{z}.

Now consider the case
\alpha^{\prime}=2-\alpha,
and hence
\alpha>1.
In this case

\displaystyle I_{11}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5}{4}\int_{\frac{\uptheta r}{4}\leq|y|\leq\frac{1}{s}}s^{\alpha}s^{d}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}+\int_{\frac{\uptheta r}{4}\leq|y|\leq\frac{1}{s}}s^{\alpha-1}|sy|s^{d}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle=5\cdot 4^{\alpha-1}(\uptheta r)^{-\alpha}\int_{\frac{\uptheta rs}{4}\leq|z|\leq 1}\left(\frac{\uptheta r}{4}s\right)^{\alpha}k(z)\mathrm{d}{z}+\left(\frac{\uptheta r}{4}\right)^{1-\alpha}\int_{\frac{\uptheta rs}{4}\leq|z|\leq 1}\left(\frac{\uptheta r}{4}s\right)^{\alpha-1}|z|k(z)\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle\leq C(\uptheta r)^{-2}\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|z|^{\alpha})k(z)\mathrm{d}{z}\,.

Combining the estimates of
I_{11}
and
I_{12},
we get

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq C(\uptheta r)^{-2}\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|z|^{\alpha})k(z)\mathrm{d}{z}.

Estimate of
I_{2}
:
If
\alpha^{\prime}=1,
then
\alpha\leq 1
and using
|u(x_{0})|\leq s^{-\alpha^{\prime}}(1+|x_{0}|)
we have

\displaystyle I_{2}:=\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}\setminus{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{4}}}\left|\left(1-\frac{\uptheta}{2}\right)^{-\gamma}u(x_{0})\right|s^{d+2}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}\leq C\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}\setminus{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{4}}}s^{d+2-\alpha^{\prime}}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle=C\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}\setminus{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta rs}{4}}}sk(z)\mathrm{d}{z}\leq C\left[\int_{\frac{\uptheta rs}{4}\leq|z|\leq 1}sk(z)\mathrm{d}{z}+\int_{|z|\geq 1}sk(z)\mathrm{d}{z}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\frac{4}{\uptheta r}\int_{\frac{\uptheta rs}{4}\leq|z|\leq 1}|z|^{\alpha}k(z)\mathrm{d}{z}+\int_{|z|>1}k(z)\mathrm{d}{z}\right]\leq C(\uptheta r)^{-1}\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha})k(z)\mathrm{d}{z}.

If
\alpha^{\prime}=2-\alpha
then
\alpha>1.
In that case, using similar calculation as above we have

\displaystyle I_{2}:=\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}\setminus{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{4}}}\left|\left(1-\frac{\uptheta}{2}\right)^{-\gamma}u(x_{0})\right|s^{d+2}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}\leq C\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}\setminus{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{4}}}s^{d+\alpha}k(sy)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle=C\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}\setminus{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta rs}{4}}}s^{\alpha}k(z)\mathrm{d}{z}\leq C(\uptheta r)^{-\alpha}\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha})k(z)\mathrm{d}{z}.

Since
\alpha\in(0,2),
combining the above estimates we obtain

\mathcal{P}^{-}w+\mathcal{P}^{-}_{k,s}w\leq\frac{C}{(\uptheta r)^{2}}\,\,\,\,\textnormal{in}\,\,\,\,{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{4}}(x_{0})\,.

Now using
[
43
, Corollary 3.14]
for
w
we get

\displaystyle|A\cap{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{8}}(x_{0})|=\bigg\lvert\bigg\{w\geq u(x_{0})((1-\nicefrac{{\uptheta}}{{2}})^{-\gamma}-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})\bigg\}\cap{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{8}}(x_{0})\bigg\rvert

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C(\uptheta r)^{d}\left(\inf_{{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{8}}(x_{0})}w+\frac{\uptheta r}{8}\cdot\frac{C}{(\uptheta r)^{2}}\right)^{\varepsilon}\cdot\left[u(x_{0})((1-\nicefrac{{\uptheta}}{{2}})^{-\gamma}-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})\right]^{-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq C(\uptheta r)^{d}\Big[\left((1-\frac{\uptheta}{2})^{-\gamma}-\frac{1}{2}\right)+\frac{C}{8}(\uptheta r)^{-1}t_{0}^{-1}(2r)^{d}\Big]^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq C(\uptheta r)^{d}\left(\left((1-\nicefrac{{\uptheta}}{{2}})^{-\gamma}-1\right)^{\varepsilon}+C_{0}(\uptheta r)^{-\varepsilon}t_{0}^{-\varepsilon}r^{d\varepsilon}\right)\,.

Now let us choose
\uptheta>0
small enough (independent of
t_{0}
) to satisfy

C(\uptheta r)^{d}\left((1-\nicefrac{{\uptheta}}{{2}})^{-\gamma}-1\right)^{\varepsilon}\leq\;\frac{1}{4}|{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{8}}(x_{0})|\,.

With this choice of
\uptheta
if
t_{0}
becomes large, then we also have

C(\uptheta r)^{d}\uptheta^{-\varepsilon}r^{(n-1)\varepsilon}t_{0}^{-\varepsilon}\leq\frac{1}{4}|{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{8}}(x_{0})|\,,

and hence

|A\cap{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{8}}(x_{0})|\leq\frac{1}{2}|{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{8}}(x_{0})|\,.

This estimate of course implies that

|\tilde{A}\cap{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\uptheta r}{8}}(x_{0})|\geq C_{2}|{\mathscr{B}}_{r}|,

but this is contradicting (
4.3
). Therefore
t_{0}
cannot be large and this completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
u
satisfies the conditions of
Theorem

4.1
, then the following holds.

\sup_{{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{4}}}u\leq C\left(\inf_{{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{4}}}u+(M_{0}\vee C_{0})s^{1+\alpha^{\prime}}\right).

Proof.

Take any point
x_{0}\in\overline{{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{4}}}
such that
u(x_{0})=\inf_{{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{4}}}u(x).
Clearly
{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{4}}\subset{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{0}).
Defining
\tilde{u}(x):=u(x+x_{0})
and applying
Theorem

4.1
on

\tilde{u}(x)\leq C\left(\tilde{u}(0)+(M_{0}\vee C_{0})s^{1+\alpha^{\prime}}\right)\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,\,{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{2}}.

This implies

\sup_{{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{4}}}u(x)\leq\sup_{{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(x_{0})}u(x)\leq C\left(\inf_{{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{4}}}u(x)+(M_{0}\vee C_{0})s^{1+\alpha^{\prime}}\right).

∎

Now we will give some auxiliary lemmas which will be used to construct appropriate supersolutions that are crucial to prove the oscillation estimate.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{2}
domain in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
,
then for any
0<\upepsilon<1,
we have the following estimate

(4.6)

\left|{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}(\delta^{1+\upepsilon})\right|\leq C\left(1+\mathds{1}_{(1,2)}(\alpha)\delta^{1-\alpha}\right)\,\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,\,\Omega,

where
C>0
depends only on
d,\Omega
and
\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}.

Proof.

Since
\delta\in C^{0,1}({\mathds{R}^{d}})\cap C^{2}(\bar{\Omega})

[
21
, Theorem 5.4.3]
, using the Lipschtiz continuity of
\delta^{1+\upepsilon}
near the origin and boundedness away from the origin we can easily obtain the estimate (
4.6
) for
\alpha\in(0,1].
Next consider the case
\alpha\in(1,2).
For any
x\in\Omega
we have

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}(\delta^{1+\upepsilon})(x)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}\left|\delta^{1+\upepsilon}(x+y)-\delta^{1+\upepsilon}(x)-\mathds{1}_{{\mathscr{B}}_{1}}(y)y\cdot\nabla\delta^{1+\upepsilon}(x)\right|k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{|y|<\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}+\int_{\frac{\delta(x)}{2}\leq|y|\leq 1}+\int_{|y|>1}:=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}\,.

Since
|y|\leq\frac{\delta(x)}{2}
and
\delta(x)<1,
we have the following estimate on
I_{1}.

\displaystyle\left|\delta^{1+\upepsilon}(x+y)-\delta^{1+\upepsilon}(x)-\mathds{1}_{{\mathscr{B}}_{1}}(y)y\cdot\nabla\delta^{1+\upepsilon}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq||\delta^{1+\upepsilon}||_{C^{2}({\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\delta(x)}{2}}(x))}|y|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4C\frac{||\delta||_{C^{2}(\bar{\Omega})}}{\delta(x)^{1-\upepsilon}}|y|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4C\frac{||\delta||_{C^{2}(\bar{\Omega})}\delta(x)^{2-\alpha}}{\delta(x)^{1-\upepsilon}}|y|^{\alpha}.

This implies

(4.7)

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq 4C||\delta||_{C^{2}(\bar{\Omega})}\delta(x)^{1+\upepsilon-\alpha}\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}|y|^{\alpha}k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}\leq 4C_{0}C||\delta||_{C^{2}(\bar{\Omega})}\delta(x)^{1+\upepsilon-\alpha}.

Again for
I_{2}
we have

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq C\int_{\frac{\delta(x)}{2}\leq|y|\leq 1}|y|k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{C\delta(x)}{2}\right)^{1-\alpha}\int_{\frac{\delta(x)}{2}\leq|y|\leq 1}|y|^{\alpha}k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{C\delta(x)}{2}\right)^{1-\alpha}\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}\left(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha}\right)k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}.

Finally,

(4.8)

\displaystyle I_{3}=\int_{|y|>1}|\delta^{1+\upepsilon}(x+y)-\delta^{1+\upepsilon}(x)|k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}\leq 2(\diam\Omega)^{1+\upepsilon}\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}.

Combining (
4.7
)-(
4.8
) we obtain (
4.6
).
∎

Next we obtain an estimate on minimal Pucci operator
{\mathcal{P}}^{-}
applied on
\delta^{1+\upepsilon}.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{2}
domain in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
,
then for any
0<\upepsilon<1,
we have the following estimate

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{-}\left(\delta^{1+\upepsilon}\right)\geq C_{1}\cdot\upepsilon\delta^{\upepsilon-1}-C_{2}\,\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,\,\Omega,

where
C_{1},C_{2}
depends only on
d,\Omega,\lambda,\Lambda
.

Proof.

Since
\partial\Omega
is
C^{2},
we have
\delta^{1+\upepsilon}\in C^{2}(\Omega)
and for any
x\in\Omega

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\delta^{1+\upepsilon}(x)=(1+\upepsilon)\Big[\delta^{\upepsilon}(x)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\delta(x)+\upepsilon\delta^{\upepsilon-1}(x)\frac{\partial\delta(x)}{\partial x_{i}}\cdot\frac{\partial\delta(x)}{\partial x_{j}}\Big]:=A+B

where
A,B
are two
d\times d
matrices given by

A:=(a_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq d}=(1+\upepsilon)\delta^{\upepsilon}(x)\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\delta(x)

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{P}^{-}(B)+\mathcal{P}^{-}(A)

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{P}^{-}(B)-d^{2}\Lambda(1+\upepsilon)(\diam\Omega)^{\upepsilon}||\delta||_{C^{2}(\bar{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\geq\upepsilon(1+\epsilon)\delta^{\upepsilon-1}(x)\lambda|D\delta(x)|^{2}-d^{2}\Lambda(1+\upepsilon)(\diam\Omega)^{\upepsilon}||\delta||_{C^{2}(\bar{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\geq C_{1}\cdot\upepsilon\delta^{\upepsilon-1}(x)-C_{2}.

∎

\mathscr{D}_{R}=\mathscr{D}_{R}(x_{0})={\mathscr{B}}_{R}(x_{0})\cap\Omega,

\mathscr{D}^{+}_{\kappa^{\prime}R}=\mathscr{D}^{+}_{\kappa^{\prime}R}(x_{0})={\mathscr{B}}_{\kappa^{\prime}R}(x_{0})\cap\left\{x\in\Omega:(x-x_{0})\cdot{\rm n}(x_{0})\geq 2\kappa R\right\},

where

x_{0}
. For any bounded

-domain, we know that there exists
\rho>0
, depending on
\Omega
, such that the following inclusions hold for each

and

and

(4.12)

{\mathscr{B}}_{4\kappa R}(y^{\ast}+4\kappa R{\rm n}(y^{\ast}))\subset\mathscr{D}_{R}(x_{0}),\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathscr{B}}_{\kappa R}(y^{\ast}+4\kappa R{\rm n}(y^{\ast}))\subset\mathscr{D}^{+}_{\kappa^{\prime}R}(x_{0})

for all

, where

is the unique boundary point satisfying

. Note that, since

,

is close enough to

and hence the point

belongs to the line joining
y

and
y^{\ast}
.

Remark 4.1
.

In the remaining part of this section, we fix

\Omega

.

.

In the following lemma, using

4.2
and

4.3
we construct supersolutions. We denote

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{2}
domain in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
and
\alpha\in(1,2),
then there exist
\rho_{1}>0
and a
C^{2}
function
\phi_{1}
satisfying

\begin{cases}{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\phi_{1}(x)+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}\phi_{1}(x)\leq-C\delta^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(x)&\quad\text{in }\quad\Omega_{\rho_{1}},\\
C^{-1}\delta(x)\leq\phi_{1}(x)\leq C\delta(x)&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
\phi_{1}(x)=0&\quad\text{in}\quad{\mathds{R}^{d}}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

where the constants
\rho_{1}
and
C
depend only on
d,\alpha,\Omega,\lambda,\Lambda
and
\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}
.

Proof.

Let
\upepsilon=\frac{2-\alpha}{2}
and
c=\frac{1}{(\diam\Omega)^{2}}
, and define

\phi_{1}(x)=\delta(x)-c\delta^{1+\upepsilon}(x).

Since both
\delta
and
\delta^{1+\upepsilon}
are in
C^{2}(\Omega)
, we have

{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\phi_{1}(x)\leq{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\delta(x)-c{\mathcal{P}}^{-}\delta^{1+\upepsilon}(x).

Then by
Lemma

4.3
and
\sup_{\theta\nu}|\trace(a_{\theta\nu}(x)D^{2}\delta(x))|\leq\tilde{C}
, we get for all
x\in\Omega_{\rho}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\phi_{1}(x)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\delta(x)-c{\mathcal{P}}^{-}\delta^{1+\epsilon}(x)\leq C-c(C_{1}\cdot\upepsilon\delta^{\upepsilon-1}(x)).

Similarly for all
x\in\Omega_{\rho}
, using
Lemma

4.2
and
Lemma

4.4
we get

{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}\phi_{1}(x)\leq|{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}\delta(x)|+c|{\mathcal{P}}^{-}_{k}\delta^{1+\upepsilon}(x)|\leq C_{2}\delta^{1-\alpha}(x).

Combining the above inequalities we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\phi_{1}(x)+{\mathcal{P}}_{k}^{+}\phi_{1}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C-cC_{1}\upepsilon\delta^{\upepsilon-1}(x)+C_{2}\delta^{1-\alpha}(x)

\displaystyle\leq-\delta^{\upepsilon-1}(x)\left(\frac{C_{1}(2-\alpha)}{2(\diam\Omega)^{2}}-C\delta^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}(x)-C_{2}\delta^{\frac{2-\alpha}{2}}(x)\right),

for all
x\in\Omega_{\rho}
. Now choose
0<\rho_{1}\leq\rho<1
such that

\left(\frac{C_{1}(2-\alpha)}{2(\diam\Omega)^{2}}-C\rho_{1}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}-C_{2}\rho_{1}^{\frac{2-\alpha}{2}}\right)\geq\frac{C_{1}(2-\alpha)}{4(\diam\Omega)^{2}}.

Thus for all
x\in\Omega_{\rho_{1}},
we have

{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\phi_{1}(x)+{\mathcal{P}}_{k}^{+}\phi_{1}(x)\leq-\frac{C_{1}(2-\alpha)}{4(\diam\Omega)^{2}}\delta^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(x).

Finally the construction of
\phi_{1}
immediately gives us that

C^{-1}\delta(x)\leq\phi_{1}(x)\leq C\delta(x)\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

and
\phi_{1}=0
in
\Omega^{c}.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

As mentioned earlier, the key step of proving
Theorem

1.2
is to obtain the oscillation lemma

4.1
. For this we next prove two preparatory lemmas. In the first lemma we obtain a lower bound of

whereas the second lemma controls
\sup_{\mathscr{D}^{+}_{\kappa^{\prime}R}}\frac{u}{\delta}
by using that lower bound.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{2}
domain in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
. Also, let
u
be such that
u\geq 0
in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
, and
\lvert{\mathcal{L}}u\rvert\leq C_{2}(1+\mathds{1}_{(1,2)}(\alpha)\delta^{1-\alpha})
in
\mathscr{D}_{R}
, for some constant
C_{2}
. If
\hat{\alpha}
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}=\begin{cases}1\,\,\,\,&if\,\,\,\,\alpha\in(0,1],\\
\frac{2-\alpha}{2}\,\,\,\,&if\,\,\,\,\alpha\in(1,2),\end{cases}

then there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
d,\Omega,\Lambda,\lambda,\alpha,\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}

(4.13)

\inf_{\mathscr{D}^{+}_{\kappa^{\prime}R}}\frac{u}{\delta}\leq C\left(\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{\frac{R}{2}}}\frac{u}{\delta}+C_{2}R^{\hat{\alpha}}\right)

for all
R\leq\rho_{0}
, where the constant
\rho_{0}
depends only on

d,\Omega,\lambda,\Lambda,\alpha
and
\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}
.

Proof.

Suppose
R\leq\eta\rho
, where

\rho
is given by
Remark

4.1
and
\eta\leq 1
be some constant that will be chosen later.
Define
m=\inf_{\mathscr{D}^{+}_{\kappa^{{}^{\prime}}R}}u/\delta\geq 0
.
Let us first observe that by (
4.11
) we have,

(4.14)

u\geq m\delta\geq m\,(\kappa R)\quad\text{in}\;\;\mathscr{D}^{+}_{\kappa^{\prime}R}.

Moreover by (
4.12
), for any
y\in\mathscr{D}_{R/2},
we have either
y\in\mathscr{D}^{+}_{\kappa^{\prime}R}
or
\delta(y)<4\kappa R.
If
y\in\mathscr{D}^{+}_{\kappa^{\prime}R},
then by the definition of
m
we get
m\leq u(y)/\delta(y).

Next we consider
\delta(y)<4\kappa R
.
Let
y^{\ast}
be the nearest point to
y
on
\partial\Omega,
i.e,
\dist(y,\partial\Omega)=|y-y^{*}|
and define

\tilde{y}=y^{\ast}+4\kappa R\,{\rm n}(y^{\ast})
. Again by (
4.12
), we have

{\mathscr{B}}_{4\kappa R}(\tilde{y})\subset\mathscr{D}_{R}\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,{\mathscr{B}}_{\kappa R}(\tilde{y})\subset\mathscr{D}^{+}_{\kappa^{\prime}R}.

Denoting
r=\kappa R
and using the subsolution constructed in
Lemma

4.1
, define
\tilde{\phi}_{r}(x):=\frac{1}{\tilde{\kappa}}\phi_{r}(x-\tilde{y}).

We will consider two cases.

Case 1:

\alpha\in(0,1]
.
Take
r^{\prime}=\frac{R}{\eta}
. Since
r^{\prime}\leq\rho
, points of
\partial\Omega
can be touched by exterior ball of radius
r^{\prime}
. In particular, for
y^{\ast}\in\partial\Omega
,
we can find a point
z\in\Omega^{c}
such that
\bar{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r^{\prime}}(z)\subset\Omega^{c}
touches

\partial\Omega
at
y^{\ast}
. Now from
[
43
, Lemma 5.4]

there exists a bounded, Lipschitz continuous function
\varphi_{r^{\prime}}
, with Lipschitz constant
\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}
, that satisfies

\begin{cases}\varphi_{r^{\prime}}=0,&\quad\text{in}\quad\bar{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r^{\prime}},\\
\varphi_{r^{\prime}}>0,&\quad\text{in}\quad\bar{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r^{\prime}}^{c},\\
{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\varphi_{r^{\prime}}+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}\varphi_{r^{\prime}}\leq-\frac{1}{(r^{\prime})^{2}},&\quad\text{in}\quad{\mathscr{B}}_{(1+\sigma)r^{\prime}}\setminus\bar{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r^{\prime}},\end{cases}

for some constant
\sigma
, independent of
r^{\prime}
. Without any loss of any
generality we may assume
\sigma\leq\upgamma
(see
Remark

4.1
).
Then setting
\eta=\frac{\sigma}{8}
and using
Remark

4.1
, we have

\mathscr{D}_{R}\subset{\mathscr{B}}_{(1+\sigma)r^{\prime}}(z)\setminus\overline{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r^{\prime}}(z)

and by (

{\mathscr{B}}_{4r}(\tilde{y})\setminus\overline{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r}(\tilde{y})\subset\mathscr{D}_{R}\subset{\mathscr{B}}_{(1+\sigma)r^{\prime}}(z)\setminus\overline{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r^{\prime}}(z).

We show that
v(x)=m\tilde{\phi}_{r}(x)-C_{2}(r^{\prime})^{2}\varphi_{r^{\prime}}(x-z)

\tilde{\phi}_{r}
and
\varphi_{r^{\prime}}
are
C^{2}
functions in
{\mathscr{B}}_{4r}(\tilde{y})\setminus\bar{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r}(\tilde{y}),
we conclude that
v
is
C^{2}
function in
{\mathscr{B}}_{4r}(\tilde{y})\setminus\bar{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r}(\tilde{y}).
For

{\mathcal{P}}^{-}v(x)+{\mathcal{P}}^{-}_{k}v(x)\geq m\left[{\mathcal{P}}^{-}\tilde{\phi}_{r}(x)+{\mathcal{P}}^{-}_{k}\tilde{\phi}_{r}(x)\right]-C_{2}(r^{\prime})^{2}\left[{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\varphi_{r^{\prime}}(x-z)+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}\varphi_{r^{\prime}}(x-z)\right]\geq C_{2}.

Therefore by
Remark

2.1

{\mathcal{P}}^{+}(v-u)+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}(v-u)\geq 0\,\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,\,\,{\mathscr{B}}_{4r}(\tilde{y})\setminus\bar{{\mathscr{B}}}_{r}(\tilde{y}).

Furthermore, using (
4.14
) and
u\geq 0
in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
we obtain
u(x)\geq m\tilde{\phi}_{r}(x)-C_{2}(r^{\prime})^{2}\varphi_{r^{\prime}}(x-z)
in

Hence an application of maximum principle (cf.

2.2
) gives

in

Now for

\displaystyle m\tilde{\phi}_{r}(y)\leq u(y)+C_{2}(r^{\prime})^{2}\left[\varphi_{r^{\prime}}(y-z)-\varphi_{r^{\prime}}(y^{*}-z)\right]\leq u(y)+C_{2}{r^{\prime}}\cdot\delta(y)

and as

lies on the line segment joining

to

we get

\frac{u(y)}{\delta(y)}+C_{2}r^{\prime}\geq\frac{m}{(\tilde{\kappa})^{2}}.

This gives

and finally choosing

).

Case 2:

\alpha\in(1,2)
. Let
\rho_{1}
as in
Lemma

4.5
and consider

. Here we aim to construct an appropriate subsolution using

and supersolution constructed in
Lemma

4.5
. Since

in
\mathscr{D}_{R},
we have
\lvert{\mathcal{L}}u(x)\rvert\leq C_{2}(1+\delta^{1-\alpha}(x))\leq 2C_{2}\delta^{1-\alpha}(x)
in
\mathscr{D}_{R}
. Also by

4.5
, we have a bounded function
\phi_{1}
which is

and satisfies

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}^{+}\phi_{1}(x)+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}\phi_{1}(x)\leq-C\delta^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(x)=-C\frac{1}{\delta^{\frac{2-\alpha}{2}}(x)}\delta^{1-\alpha}(x)\leq\frac{-C}{R^{\hat{\alpha}}}\delta^{1-\alpha}(x),

for all

. Now we define the subsolutions as

where the constant

is chosen suitably so that
{\mathcal{P}}^{-}v(x)+{\mathcal{P}}^{-}_{k}v(x)\geq 2C_{2}\delta^{1-\alpha}(x)
in

in

.
Using the same calculation as previous case for

and maximum principle

2.2
we derive that

in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
. Again, repeating the arguments of
Case 1
we get

\inf_{\mathscr{D}^{+}_{\kappa^{\prime}R}}\frac{u}{\delta}\leq C\left(\inf_{\mathscr{D}_{\frac{R}{2}}}\frac{u}{\delta}+2C_{2}R^{\hat{\alpha}}\right)\,.

Choosing
\rho_{0}=\eta\rho\wedge\rho_{1}
completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\alpha^{\prime}=1\wedge(2-\alpha)
and
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{2}
domain in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
. Also, let

u
be a bounded continuous function such that
u\geq 0
and
u\leq M_{0}\delta(x)
in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
, and
\lvert{\mathcal{L}}u\rvert\leq C_{2}(1+\mathds{1}_{(1,2)}(\alpha)\delta^{1-\alpha})
in
\mathscr{D}_{R}
, for some constant
C_{2}
.
Then, there exists a positive constant
C
, depending only on
d,\lambda,\Lambda,\Omega
and
\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha}){k}(y)\mathrm{d}{y}
, such that

(4.15)

\sup_{\mathscr{D}^{+}_{\kappa^{\prime}R}}\frac{u}{\delta}\leq C\left(\inf_{\mathscr{D}^{+}_{\kappa^{\prime}R}}\frac{u}{\delta}+(M_{0}\vee C_{2})R^{\alpha^{\prime}}\right)

for all
R\leq\rho
, where constant
\rho
is given by
Remark

4.1
.

Proof.

We will use the weak Harnack inequality proved in
Theorem

4.1
to show (
4.15
). Let
R\leq\rho
. Then for each
y\in\mathscr{D}^{+}_{\kappa^{\prime}R}
, we have
{\mathscr{B}}_{\kappa R}(y)\subset\mathscr{D}_{R}
. Hence we have
\lvert{\mathcal{L}}u\rvert\leq C_{2}(1+\mathds{1}_{(1,2)}(\alpha)\delta^{1-\alpha}(x))
in
{\mathscr{B}}_{\kappa R}(y)
. Without loss of generality, we may assume
y=0
. Let
s=\kappa R
and define
v(x)=u(sx)
for all
x\in{\mathds{R}^{d}}
. Then,
it can be easily seen that

s^{2}{\mathcal{L}}[sx,u]={\mathcal{L}}^{s}[x,v]\coloneqq\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\inf_{\nu\in\Gamma}\left\{\trace a_{\theta\nu}(sx)D^{2}v(x)+{\mathcal{I}}^{s}_{\theta\nu}[x,v]\right\}\qquad\text{for all }\;x\in{\mathscr{B}}_{2}.

This gives

\displaystyle\left|{\mathcal{L}}^{s}[x,v]\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}s^{2}(1+\mathds{1}_{(1,2)}(\alpha)\delta^{1-\alpha}(sx))

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\left(s^{2}+\mathds{1}_{(1,2)}(\alpha)s^{2}\left(\kappa R\right)^{1-\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}s^{1+\alpha^{\prime}}\,,

in
{\mathscr{B}}_{2}
where
\alpha^{\prime}=1\wedge(2-\alpha)
. In second line, we used that for each

|sx|<\kappa R
and hence
\delta(sx)>\frac{\kappa R}{2}=\frac{s}{2}
. From

we have

and
v(y)\leq M_{0}s(1+|y|)
in whole
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
.
Hence by

4.1
, we obtain

\sup_{{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{4}}}v\leq C\left(\inf_{{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{1}{4}}}v+(M_{0}\vee C_{2})s^{1+\alpha^{\prime}}\right),

does not depend on

\sup_{{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\kappa R}{64}}(y)}u\leq C\left(\inf_{{\mathscr{B}}_{\frac{\kappa R}{64}}(y)}u+(M_{0}\vee C_{2})R^{1+\alpha^{\prime}}\right),

for all

. Now cover
\mathscr{D}^{+}_{\kappa^{\prime}R}
by a finite number of balls

, independent of

, to obtain

\sup_{\mathscr{D}^{+}_{\kappa^{\prime}R}}u\leq C\left(\inf_{\mathscr{D}^{+}_{\kappa^{\prime}R}}u+(M_{0}\vee C_{2})R^{1+\alpha^{\prime}}\right).

Then (
4.15

\kappa R/2\leq\delta\leq 3\kappa R/2
in

.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the oscillation lemma.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
u
be a bounded continuous function such that
|{\mathcal{L}}u|\leq K
in

\Omega
, for some constant

, and

in
\Omega^{c}

be as in the

, where

For the proof we follow a standard method, similar to

,
with the help of

4.4
,
4.7
, and

Fix

.
In view of (

By considering

|{\mathcal{L}}u|\leq 1

\Omega

1.1

For

In this case the proof is simpler and can be done following the same method as for the local case. However, the method we use below would also work with an appropriate modification.

Case 2:
Now we deal with the case
\alpha\in(1,2)
. We show that there exists
{\mathscr{K}}>0,

\rho_{1}\in(0,\rho_{0})
and
\uptau\in(0,1)

\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(1\wedge|y|^{\alpha})k(y)\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle M_{k}-m_{k}=\frac{1}{4^{k\uptau}},\,\,\,\,\,-1\leq m_{k}\leq m_{k+1}<M_{k+1}\leq M_{k}\leq 1,

and

(4.18)

\displaystyle m_{k}\leq{\mathscr{K}}^{-1}\frac{u}{\delta}\leq M_{k}\quad\text{in}\quad\mathscr{D}_{R_{k}},\quad\text{where}\quad R_{k}=\frac{\rho_{1}}{4^{k}}.

Note that (

\displaystyle m_{k}\delta\leq{\mathscr{K}}^{-1}u\leq M_{k}\delta,\quad\text{in}\quad{\mathscr{B}}_{R_{k}},\quad\text{where}\quad R_{k}=\frac{\rho_{1}}{4^{k}}.

Next we construct monotone sequences
\{M_{k}\}
and
\{m_{k}\}
by induction.

The existence of
M_{0}
and
m_{0}
such that (

) and (

) hold for

is guaranteed by

3.1
.
Assume that we have the sequences up to
M_{k}
and
m_{k}
. We want to show the existence of

and
m_{k+1}
such that (
4.17
)-(
4.19
) hold. We set

\displaystyle u_{k}=\frac{1}{{\mathscr{K}}}u-m_{k}\delta.

Note that to apply

4.7
we need

to be nonnegative in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
.
Therefore we shall work with

, the positive part of
u_{k}
. Let
u_{k}=u^{+}_{k}-u^{-}_{k}
and by the induction hypothesis,

(4.20)

\displaystyle u^{+}_{k}=u_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad u^{-}_{k}=0\quad\text{in}\quad{\mathscr{B}}_{R_{k}}.

We need to find a lower bound on

. Since
u_{k}\geq 0
in
{\mathscr{B}}_{R_{k}}
and

we get for

\displaystyle u_{k}(x)=u_{k}(R_{k}x_{\rm u})+u_{k}(x)-u_{k}(R_{k}x_{\rm u})\geq-C_{L}|x-R_{k}x_{\rm u}|,

where
z_{\rm u}=\frac{1}{|z|}z

5.

In this section we prove the Hölder regularity of

up to the boundary.
First, let us recall

{\mathcal{L}}[x,u]=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\inf_{\nu\in\gamma}\left\{\trace a_{\theta\nu}(x)D^{2}u(x)+{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}[x,u]\right\}.

We denote

Following
[

]
, next we obtain the in-equations satisfied by

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Omega
be bounded

domain in
{\mathds{R}^{d}}
. If
|{\mathcal{L}}u|\leq K
in
\Omega
and

in

(5.1)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{L}}v+2K_{0}d^{2}\frac{|D\delta|}{\delta}|Dv|\geq\frac{1}{\delta}\Big[-K-|v|(P^{+}+P^{+}_{k})\delta-\sup_{\theta,\nu}Z_{\theta\nu}[v,\delta]\Big],

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{L}}v-2K_{0}d^{2}\frac{|D\delta|}{\delta}|Dv|\leq\frac{1}{\delta}\Big[K-|v|(P^{-}+P^{-}_{k})\delta-\inf_{\theta,\nu}Z_{\theta\nu}[v,\delta]\Big]

for some

where

Z_{\theta\nu}[v,\delta](x)=\int_{{\mathds{R}^{d}}}(v(y)-v(x))(\delta(y)-\delta(x))N_{\theta\nu}(x,y-x)dy.

Proof.

First note that, since

by

2.1

.
Therefore,
Z_{\theta\nu}[v,\delta]
is continuous in
\Omega
.
Consider a test function

that touches

from above at

By our assertion, we have

for all

small. To verify the first inequality in (

(5.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}[x,\psi_{r}]+2k_{0}d^{2}\frac{|D\delta(x)|}{\delta(x)}\cdot|D\psi_{r}(x)|\geq\frac{1}{\delta(x)}[-K-|v(x)|({\mathcal{P}}^{+}+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k})\delta(x)-\sup_{\theta,\nu}Z_{\theta\nu}[v,\delta](x)],

for some

Then,
\tilde{\psi}_{r}\geq u
for all
r
small. Since
|{\mathcal{L}}u|\leq K

and
\delta\psi_{r}=\tilde{\psi}_{r}
, we obtain at a point

\displaystyle-K\leq

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}[x,\tilde{\psi}_{r}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\inf_{\nu\in\gamma}\biggl[\delta(x)\left(\trace a_{\theta\nu}(x)D^{2}\psi_{r}(x)+{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}\psi_{r}(x)\right)+\psi_{r}(x)\left(\trace a_{\theta\nu}(x)D^{2}\delta(x)+{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}\delta(x)\right)

\displaystyle+\trace\big[\left(a_{\theta\nu}(x)+a^{T}_{\theta\nu}(x)\right)\cdot\left(D\delta(x)\otimes D\psi_{r}(x)\right)\big]+Z_{\theta\nu}[\psi_{r},\delta](x)\biggr]

\displaystyle\leq\delta(x){\mathcal{L}}[x,\psi_{r}]+\sup_{\theta,\nu}\biggl[|\psi_{r}(x)|\left(\trace a_{\theta\nu}(x)D^{2}\delta(x)+{\mathcal{I}}_{\theta\nu}\delta(x)\right)

\displaystyle+\trace\left[\left(a_{\theta\nu}(x)+a^{T}_{\theta\nu}(x)\right)\cdot\left(D\delta(x)\otimes D\psi_{r}(x)\right)\right]+Z_{\theta\nu}[\psi_{r},\delta](x)\biggr]

\displaystyle\leq\delta(x){\mathcal{L}}[x,\psi_{r}]+|v(x)|\left({\mathcal{P}}^{+}+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}\right)\delta(x)+2K_{0}d^{2}|D\delta(x)|\cdot|D\psi_{r}(x)|+\sup_{\theta,\nu}Z_{\theta\nu}[\psi_{r},\delta](x),

for all

small and some constant

, where

Rearranging the terms we have

(5.3)

Let

. Since

is decreasing with

\displaystyle\geq\delta(x){\mathcal{L}}[x,\psi_{r_{1}}]+2K_{0}d^{2}|D{\delta(x)}|\cdot|D\psi_{r_{1}}(x)|

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{r_{1}\to 0}\left[-K-\left|v(x)\right|\left({\mathcal{P}}^{+}+{\mathcal{P}}^{+}_{k}\right)\delta(x)-\sup_{\theta,\nu}Z_{\theta\nu}[\psi_{r_{1}},\delta](x)\right]

by dominated convergence theorem. This gives (

Next we obtain a the following estimate on

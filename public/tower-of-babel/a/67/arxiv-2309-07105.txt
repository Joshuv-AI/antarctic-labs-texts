Global becomes local: Efficient many-body dynamics for global master equations
Alexander Schnell

Abstract
This work makes progress on the issue of global- vs. local master equations. Global master equations like the Redfield master equation (following from standard Born- and Markov approximation) require a full diagonalization of the system Hamiltonian. This is especially challenging for interacting quantum many-body systems. We discuss a short-bath-correlation-time expansion in reciprocal (energy) space, leading to a series expansion of the jump operator, which avoids a diagonalization of the Hamiltonian. For a bath that is coupled locally to one site, this typically leads to an expansion of the global Redfield jump operator in terms of local operators. We additionally map the local Redfield master equation to a novel local Lindblad form, giving an equation which has the same conceptual advantages of traditional local Lindblad approaches, while being applicable in a much broader class of systems. Our ideas give rise to a non-heuristic foundation of local master equations, which can be combined with established many-body methods.

Redfield master equation.
—
Consider a typical open quantum system setup with total Hamiltonian

H_{\mathrm{tot}}=H_{\mathrm{S}}+H_{\mathrm{SB}}+H_{\mathrm{B}}
, where
H_{\mathrm{S}}
and
H_{\mathrm{B}}
are Hamiltonians that act on the system and bath Hilbert space only and
H_{\mathrm{SB}}
is the system–bath interaction Hamiltonian, which for simplicity we assume is a direct product
H_{\mathrm{SB}}=v\otimes B
of system- and bath coupling operators
v
and
B
respectively. The generalization to arbitrary interaction Hamiltonians is discussed in the Supplemental Material (SM)
SM
. Performing Born- and Markov approximations, the dynamics of the reduced density matrix of the system
\varrho(t)=\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathrm{B}}\varrho_{\mathrm{tot}}(t)
, is given by the Redfield quantum master equation

;
Weiss 2012
;
Carmichael 1999
;
Redfield 1965
;
Becker et al. 2022
;
Thingna 2013
;
Schnell 2019

\partial_{t}\varrho(t)=-i\left[H_{\mathrm{S}},\varrho(t)\right]+\left(\left[u\varrho(t),v\right]+\mathrm{h.c.}\right).

(1)

As sketched in Fig.
1
(a), the operator

u=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\tilde{v}(-\tau)C(\tau)
convolutes the coupling operator in the interaction picture
\tilde{v}(t)={\mathrm{e}^{iH_{\mathrm{S}}t}}v{\mathrm{e}^{-iH_{\mathrm{S}}t}}

C(t)=\langle\tilde{B}(t)\tilde{B}(0)\rangle_{\mathrm{B}}

\tilde{B}(t)={\mathrm{e}^{iH_{\mathrm{B}}t}}B{\mathrm{e}^{-iH_{\mathrm{B}}t}}

\hbar=k_{\mathrm{B}}=1
.

While the Redfield eq. (
1
) generally leads to more accurate results when compared to heuristic Lindblad descriptions (like local Lindblad equations), it has two major deficiencies that prohibit its straight forward application to genuine quantum many-body systems:
1) Since it does not obey Lindblad structure, its dynamics will generally violate positivity (this issue can be mitigated by making connections to statistical mechanics
Becker et al. 2022
).
And 2) to implement it,
one generally introduces the system’s eigenbasis
H_{\mathrm{S}}=\sum_{k}E_{k}{\left|k\right\rangle}{\left\langle k\right|}
, giving

u=\sum_{kq}\hat{v}_{kq}W(E_{k}-E_{q})

SM

with eigenspace-projected coupling operator
\hat{v}_{kq}={\left|k\right\rangle}{\left\langle k\right|}v{\left|q\right\rangle}{\left\langle q\right|}
and Fourier-Laplace transform
W(E)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{e}^{-iE\tau}C(\tau)
of the bath correlation function. This is problematic since it requires a full diagonalization of the many-body Hamiltonian which is generally a hard task. And even then, if the coupling operator
v
can be represented as a sparse matrix (e.g. if
v
is an operator that is local in space) the matrix
u
is generally a dense matrix, making the numerical solution of the equation memory- and time consuming.
We therefore call the Redfield eq. (
1
) a global master equation since it involves the global eigenstates of the system.
Put differently, this practically hinders us from even ‘writing down’ the global Redfield master equation in a way that we can combine it with standard many-body methods.
Note that, different from here, the term ‘global master equation’ is sometimes used specifically for the quantum optical master equation
Breuer and Petruccione 2007
;
Weiss 2012
;
Carmichael 1999
, a Lindblad master equation that follows from the Redfield equation after rotating-wave approximation.

Correlation-time expansion
—To write the Redfield eq. (
1
) in local form, we use ideas similar to derivations of quantum Brownian motion
Breuer and Petruccione 2007
where
one expands
\tilde{v}(-\tau)
in first order for short times
\tau
, which is valid for short bath-correlation times, cf. Fig.
1
(a).
Here we expand to arbitrary order
Nathan and Rudner 2020
and additionally insert
1=\mathrm{e}^{-i(E-\varepsilon_{0})\tau}|_{E=\varepsilon_{0}}
to rewrite

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{e}^{-i([H_{\mathrm{S}},\cdot]-\varepsilon_{0}\cdot)\tau}[v]\ \mathrm{e}^{-iE\tau}|_{E=\varepsilon_{0}}C(\tau),

(2)

where in the exponential it enters a superoperator acting as
([H_{\mathrm{S}},\cdot]-\varepsilon_{0}\cdot)[v]=[H_{\mathrm{S}},v]-\varepsilon_{0}v
.
By expanding the first exponential in a series in
\tau
, which is valid as long as
C(\tau)
decays on a bath-correlation timescale that is short against the timescale of operator spreading of
\tilde{v}(-\tau)
(typically confined by Lieb-Robinson bounds
Lieb and Robinson 1972
;
Kliesch et al. 2014
;
Shiraishi et al. 2024
, cf. Fig.
1
(a)), then using
-i\tau=\frac{\partial}{\partial E}
and exchanging the integral and the derivative, we find a Taylor-like expansion,

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\approx\sum_{n=0}^{N}\frac{W^{(n)}(\varepsilon_{0})}{n!}([H_{\mathrm{S}},\cdot]-\varepsilon_{0}\cdot)^{n}[v]\equiv\mathcal{T}^{\varepsilon_{0}}_{N}[v],

(3)

where we call the corresponding master equation the
ad-hoc local Redfield equation
.
It enters the
n
th derivative
W^{(n)}(\varepsilon_{0})
and
([H_{\mathrm{S}},\cdot]-\varepsilon_{0}\cdot)^{n}
denotes
n
times the application of the superoperator.
We observe that the terms in Eq. (
3
) represent an expansion in small parameter
E_{k}-E_{q}-\varepsilon_{0}
, which is the deviation of the transition energy
E_{k}-E_{q}
(due to the commutator structure) from the expansion energy
\varepsilon_{0}
of a thought quantum jump from
q
to
k
that is induced by
u
.
Eq. (
3
) has the significant advantage that if the expansion is truncated, a
diagonalization
of the system Hamiltonian is
not required
, which is a remarkable simplification in the case of interacting many-body systems.
Also if
v
and
H_{\mathrm{S}}
have a sparse representation, the nested commutators remain sparse. In the case where
v
is a site-local operator and
H_{\mathrm{S}}
involves nearest-neighbor terms only,
Eq. (
3
) can be understood as an expansion in local operators, exactly reflecting our intuition from Fig.
1
(a).

Let us consider a pure ohmic bath,
H_{\mathrm{B}}=\sum_{\alpha}\omega_{\alpha}b^{\dagger}_{\alpha}b_{\alpha}
,
B=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}(b_{\alpha}^{\dagger}+b_{\alpha})
, with a continuum of modes
\alpha
and spectral density
J(E)=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}^{2}[\delta(E-\omega_{\alpha})-\delta(E+\omega_{\alpha})]=\gamma E
with dissipation strength

has no imaginary part and takes the form
W(E)=J(E)/[{\mathrm{e}^{E/T}}-1]
, where

is the temperature of the bath. Calculating the derivatives

, we observe that each order involves higher terms in
1/T
. Hence, Eq. (
3

At high temperatures, when compared to the typical transition energies in the system, as we show in the SM

, this expansion energy is in principle arbitrary.
This is also confirmed by our intuition that at high temperatures

decays rapidly and for

term, in Eq. (
3
). Also note that by setting

Optimal expansion energy
—What remains is to specify the optimal expansion energy
\varepsilon_{0}
.
As it turns out, for coupling operators

We thus assume a generic quantum many-body system, whose Hamiltonian can typically be understood as the sum of onsite terms

Local Lindblad form
— The analytical form of
u
also allows us to approximate the dynamics with a Lindblad form. For our purposes, the standard rotating-wave approximation is unfeasible since it relies on the knowledge of the full many-body spectrum.
Instead, we follow Ref.
Becker et al. 2021
and first rewrite Eq. (
1
) in the form

\partial_{t}\varrho=-i\left[H_{\mathrm{eff}},\varrho\right]+\mathcal{D}[\varrho],

with effective Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{eff}}=H_{\mathrm{S}}-\frac{i}{2}(vu-u^{\dagger}v)
and dissipator

\mathcal{D}[\varrho]=D_{L_{+}}[\varrho]-D_{L_{-}}[\varrho]
with
D_{L}[\varrho]=\left(L\varrho L^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\{L^{\dagger}L,\varrho\}\right)
.
It enter jump operators

L_{\pm}=\left({u}\pm{\lambda}v\right)/\sqrt{2\lambda}
, where there occurs a free parameter
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

SM
;
Becker et al. 2021
;
Becker et al. 2023
.
Note that the second term in the Redfield dissipator
\mathcal{D}
has a negative ‘rate’
which is why it violates Lindblad form.
However, for the ad-hoc local Redfield eq. (
3
), we observe that by setting the free parameter to
\lambda=W(\varepsilon_{0})
,
L_{-}
vanishes in leading order
N=0
and thus can be neglected at high temperatures
SM
. Hence, the expansion presented here also sheds light on the recently proposed truncation
Becker et al. 2021

For the local Redfield eq. (
4
), an optimal parameter choice can be found
by averaging the above optimal value with weights according to local thermal equilibrium

\lambda_{\mathrm{loc}}=\sqrt{\sum_{kq}W(\varepsilon^{i_{0}}_{kq})^{2}r_{kq}^{2}}/\sqrt{\sum_{kq}r_{kq}^{2}}

.
The local Redfield eq. can therefore approximately be rewritten in Lindblad form

\partial_{t}\varrho\approx-i\left[H_{\mathrm{eff}},\varrho\right]+D_{L_{+}}[\varrho],
with
L_{+}=\left({u}_{\mathrm{loc}}+{\lambda_{\mathrm{loc}}}v\right)/\sqrt{2\lambda_{\mathrm{loc}}}
and the local approximation
u_{\mathrm{loc}}
, Eq. (
4
).
We call this our
local Lindblad equation
.
As we show in Fig.

Benchmark against traditional local Lindblad
—Our local Lindblad equation provides an alternative to the widely used traditional local Lindblad equations while, at the same time, not suffering from typical problems of the standard local Lindblad equations

Summary.
—Even though the Redfield master equation is typically considered a global master equation, we emphasize that for coupling to local baths with short bath-correlation time, the jump operator will remain localized in space. We provide a Taylor-like expansion of the Redfield jump operator in terms of local operators, and show convergence for high and intermediate bath temperatures. This additionally has the advantage that a diagonalization of the full Hamiltonian is not required.
However, there might exist other expansions
Shiraishi et al. 2024
with better convergence especially in the case of bath spectral densities with a low cutoff. We show that our method can be further approximated to a Lindblad form with local jump operators, outperforming the traditonal local Lindblad ansatz. We combine our results with quantum trajectories to simulate the Redfield dynamics for Hilbert space dimensions that were previously challenging. Our method can be augmented with mean-field- or matrix-product methods to solve the Redfield equation for extended many-body systems.

Existence and Uniqueness of the Solution to the Anisotropic Quasi-Geostrophic Equations in the Sobolev Space
Mustapha Amara, Jamel Benameur

Abstract
. In this paper, we focus on the two-dimensional surface quasi-geostrophic equation with fractional horizontal dissipation and vertical thermal diffusion which represents a general case of the classical surface quasi-geostrophic equation. On the one hand, we will show the local existence and uniqueness of the solution in Sobolev space H 2 − 2 ​ α ​ ( ℝ 2 ) ∩ H 2 − 2 ​ β ​ ( ℝ 2 ) H^{2-2\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap H^{2-2\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) , which is the critical space in the classical case. Furthermore, we will demonstrate that the solution is global even when the initial data is very small. Finally, we will study the asymptotic representation of our global solution in infinity.

1.
Introduction

We consider the two-dimensional anisotropic quasi-geostrophic equation:

(AQG)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\theta+u_{\theta}.\nabla\theta+\mu|\partial_{1}|^{2\alpha}\theta+\nu|\partial_{2}|^{2\beta}\theta=0,&t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
u_{\theta}=\mathcal{R}^{\perp}\theta&t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
\theta(x,0)=\theta^{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\end{cases}

\bullet

\theta=\theta(x,t)
and
u_{\theta}=u_{\theta}(x,t)=(u_{\theta}^{1}(x,t),u_{\theta}^{2}(x,t))
are the unknown potential temperature and the velocity field of the fluid, respectively.

\bullet

\theta^{0}
is the given initial potential temperature.

\bullet

\mu,\nu>0
and
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1)
.

\bullet

\mathcal{R}^{\perp}=\left(-\mathcal{R}_{2},\mathcal{R}_{1}\right)
, where
\mathcal{R}_{1}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}
are the Riesz transforms
defined by

\mathcal{R}_{j}\theta=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\xi\mapsto\frac{i\xi_{j}}{|\xi|}\mathcal{F}(\theta)(\xi)\right),\quad j\in\{1,2\}.

The equation arises from geophysics and has a strong physical background. If
\mu=\nu=0
, then it reduces to the inviscid case, which shares some important features with the 3D Euler equations such as the vortex stretching mechanism. This inviscid case equation is an important model of geophysical fluid dynamics, which describes the evolution of the surface temperature field in the rotating stratified fluid. The first mathematical studies of this equation was carried out in 1994s by Constantin, Majda and Tabak. For more details and mathematical and physical explanations of this model we can consult
[
5
,
6
,
4
,
3
]
.

In this manuscript, we show the local existence and uniqueness of the solution of system
(AQG)
. More precisely,
for the given initial data
\theta^{0}
in
H^{\max\{2-2\alpha,2-2\beta\}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):=H^{2-2\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\bigcap H^{2-2\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, there exists a positive
constant
T_{0}
determined by
\alpha,\beta
and
\theta^{0}
such that
(AQG)
possesses a
unique local classical solution on
C([0,T_{0}],H^{\max\{2-2\alpha,2-2\beta\}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
. Next, we prove the global existence for a small initial data in the same space, that is to say, a positive constant
\varepsilon
exists such, if
\|\theta^{0}\|_{H^{\max\{2-2\alpha,2-2\beta\}}}<\varepsilon
, then, the local solution is global one. Finally we prove some optimal decay results of the global solution of
(AQG)
with small initial data.

Before we state the main result, we recall some known results for the existence theorem of the classical quasi-geostrophic equation:

(SQG)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\theta+u_{\theta}.\nabla\theta+\kappa(-\Delta)^{\gamma}\theta=0,&t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
u_{\theta}=\mathcal{R}^{\perp}\theta,&t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
\theta(x,0)=\theta^{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.\end{cases}

Easy to see that when

and

(AQG)
becomes the classical dissipation

equation. This last equation was studied in three different cases: subcritical case when

\theta^{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
in the subcritical case. For the critical case, there are many results for this case, for example, Conctantin and Vicol

. See also the works

where same type of results have

Notations

|\partial_{1}|^{s}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\xi\mapsto|\xi_{1}|^{s}\widehat{f}(\xi)\right),\;|\partial_{2}|^{s}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\xi\mapsto|\xi_{2}|^{s}\widehat{f}(\xi)\right)\mbox{ and }|\nabla|^{s}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\xi\mapsto|\xi|^{s}\widehat{f}(\xi)\right).

\bullet
, For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, let

\bullet

For that, for any

According to

1.1

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}a+|\partial_{1}|^{2\alpha}a+|\partial_{2}|^{2\beta}a+u_{a}.\nabla a=0,\\

In this section, we prove if
\|\theta^{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{2-2\alpha}}<c
, we get a global solution in
C(\mathbb{R}^{+},H^{2-2\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
satisfying
(
1.2
)
.

Let
\theta\in C([0,T^{\ast}),H^{2-2\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
be a maximal solution of the system
(AQG)
, using
2.7
and the fact
\mathrm{div}u_{\theta}=0
, we infer that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta(t)\|^{2}_{H^{2-2\alpha}}+\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}+\||\partial_{2}|^{\beta}\theta\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left(|\nabla|^{2-2\alpha}(u_{\theta}.\nabla\theta),|\nabla|^{2-2\alpha}\theta\right)_{L^{2}}\right|

(3.20)

\displaystyle\leq C\||\nabla|^{2-\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\||\nabla|^{2-2\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}

By interpolation, we get

\||\nabla|^{2-\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\left(\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}+\||\partial_{2}|^{\beta}\theta\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}\right),

which implies

(3.21)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta(t)\|^{2}_{H^{2-2\alpha}}+2\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}+2\||\partial_{2}|^{\beta}\theta\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}\leq C\left(\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}+\||\partial_{2}|^{\beta}\theta\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}\right)\|\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{2-2\alpha}}

Let
T_{1}=\sup\left\{T\in(0,T^{\ast});\ \|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{2-2\alpha})}\leq 2\|\theta^{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{2-2\alpha}}\right\}
, then for any
t\in[0,T_{1})
, we have

(3.22)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta(t)\|^{2}_{H^{2-2\alpha}}+2\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}+2\||\partial_{2}|^{\beta}\theta\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}\leq 2C\left(\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}+\||\partial_{2}|^{\beta}\theta\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}\right)\|\theta^{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{2-2\alpha}}.

Taking
c=\dfrac{1}{2C}
, then, if we have
\|\theta^{0}\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}<c
, so for any
t\in[0,T_{1})

(3.23)

\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta(t)\|^{2}_{H^{2-2\alpha}}+\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}+\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}\leq 0,

which implies

\|\theta(t)\|^{2}_{H^{2-2\alpha}}+\int_{0}^{t}\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta(\tau)\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}d\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta(\tau)\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}d\tau\leq\|\theta^{0}\|^{2}_{H^{2-2\alpha}}.

Finally, we get
T=T^{\ast}
. Hence
T^{\ast}=+\infty
, which complete the proof.

4.
Proof of
Theorem

1.3

The proof is done in two steps. In the first, we prove

(4.1)

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|\theta(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{2-2\alpha}}=0,

where we use

\|\theta(t)\|^{2}_{H^{2-2\alpha}}+\int_{0}^{t}\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta(\tau)\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}d\tau+\int_{0}^{t}\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta(\tau)\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}d\tau\leq\|\theta^{0}\|^{2}_{H^{2-2\alpha}},\quad\forall t\geq 0.

In the second step, we prove

(4.2)

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|\theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}=0.

First step:
By interpolation theorem we have

\|\theta(t)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{2-2\alpha}}\leq C\left(\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta(t)\|^{2}_{H^{2-2\alpha}}+\||\partial_{2}|^{\beta}\theta(t)\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}\right).

We integer over
\mathbb{R}^{+}
we get

(4.3)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\|\theta(t)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{2-2\alpha}}dt\leq C\int_{0}^{+\infty}\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta(t)\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}dt+C\int_{0}^{+\infty}\||\partial_{2}|^{\beta}\theta(t)\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}dt\leq C\|\theta^{0}\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
E(\varepsilon)=\{t\geq 0;\ \|\theta(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{2-2\alpha}}>\varepsilon\}
, then

(4.4)

\varepsilon^{2}\lambda(E)=\int_{E}\|\theta(t)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{2-2\alpha}}dt\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\|\theta(t)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{2-2\alpha}}dt\leq C\|\theta^{0}\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}.

where
\lambda(E)
is the Lebesgue measure of
E
, then
\lambda(E)<+\infty
. So for
r>0
, there exists
t_{0}\in[0,\lambda(E)+r]
such that
t_{0}\notin E
and

\|\theta(t_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{2-2\alpha}}\leq\varepsilon.

Thus,
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|\theta(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{2-2\alpha}}=0
.

Second step:
Let
\delta
a strictly positive real number strictly less than 1, we define the operators

A_{\delta}(D)
and
B_{\delta}(D)
, respectively, by the following:

(4.5)

\begin{array}[]{l}A_{\delta}(D)f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\xi\mapsto\chi_{\mathcal{P}(0,\delta)}(\xi)\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)\right),\\
B_{\delta}(D)f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\xi\mapsto(1-\chi_{\mathcal{P}(0,\delta)})(\xi)\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)\right),\end{array}

where

\mathcal{P}(0,\delta)=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\ A(\xi)\leq\delta\right\}.

We define
w_{\delta}=A_{\delta}(D)\theta
and
v_{\delta}=B_{\delta}(D)\theta
;
\mathcal{F}(\theta)=\mathcal{F}(w_{\delta})+\mathcal{F}(v_{\delta})
. Then,

(4.6)

\partial_{t}w_{\delta}+|\partial_{1}|^{2\alpha}w_{\delta}+|\partial_{2}|^{2\beta}w_{\delta}+A_{\delta}(D)(u_{\theta}.\nabla\theta)=0

and

(4.7)

\partial_{t}v_{\delta}+|\partial_{1}|^{2\alpha}v_{\delta}+|\partial_{2}|^{2\beta}v_{\delta}+B_{\delta}(D)(u_{\theta}.\nabla\theta)=0

Taking the scalar product of
(
4.6
)
equation with
w_{\delta}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|w_{\delta}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left(A_{\delta}(D)(u_{\theta}.\nabla\theta),w_{\delta}\right)_{L^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A(\xi)\leq\delta}|\xi||\widehat{u_{\theta}\otimes\theta}(\xi)||\widehat{w_{\delta}}(\xi)|d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A(\xi)\leq\delta}|\xi|^{2-2\beta}|\xi|^{2\beta-1}|\widehat{u_{\theta}\otimes\theta}(\xi)||\widehat{w_{\delta}}(\xi)|d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha,\beta)\int_{A(\xi)\leq\delta}\left(A(\xi)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}+A(\xi)^{\frac{1}{2\beta}}\right)^{2-2\beta}|\xi|^{2\beta-1}||\widehat{u_{\theta}\otimes\theta}(\xi)||\widehat{w_{\delta}}(\xi)|d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha,\beta)\delta^{1-\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\xi|^{2\beta-1}|\widehat{u_{\theta}\otimes\theta}(\xi)||\widehat{w_{\delta}}(\xi)|d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha,\beta)\delta^{1-\beta}\|u_{\theta}\otimes\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{2\beta-1}}\|w_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha,\beta)\delta^{1-\beta}\||\nabla|^{\beta}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|w_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}.

By interpolation Theorem,
\alpha\leq\beta\leq 2-\alpha
we have

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{\beta}\theta\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\||\partial_{1}|^{\beta}\theta\|_{L^{2}}+\||\partial_{2}|^{\beta}\theta\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta\|_{L^{2}}+\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta\|_{\dot{H}^{2-2\alpha}}+\||\partial_{2}|^{\beta}\theta\|_{L^{2}}

Using
\|w_{\delta}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\theta\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\theta^{0}\|_{L^{2}}
, and integer in
[0,t]
we get

\displaystyle\|w_{\delta}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|w_{\delta}^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\alpha,\beta)\delta^{1-\beta}\|\theta^{0}\|_{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}+\||\partial_{2}|^{\beta}\theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\|w_{\delta}^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\alpha,\beta)\delta^{1-\beta}\|\theta^{0}\|_{H^{2-2\alpha}}^{3}\underset{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\longrightarrow}0.

Therefore

\lim\limits_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\|w_{\delta}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{2})}=0.

So for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that

(4.8)

\|w_{\delta_{0}}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))}<\dfrac{\varepsilon}{2}.

On the other hand, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|v_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A(\xi)>\delta}\frac{A(\xi)}{A(\xi)}|\widehat{\theta}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(|\xi_{1}|^{2\alpha}+|\xi_{2}|^{2\beta}\right)|\widehat{\theta}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\delta}\left(\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta\|^{2}+\||\partial_{2}|^{\beta}\theta\|^{2}\right),

which implies

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\|v_{\delta}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}d\tau\leq\frac{1}{{\delta}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\||\partial_{1}|^{\alpha}\theta\|^{2}+\||\partial_{2}|^{\beta}\theta\|^{2}\right)d\tau\leq\frac{1}{{\delta}}\|\theta^{0}\|^{2}.

Let
S_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{0})=\{t\geq 0;\|v_{\delta_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\}
, then

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{2}\lambda(S_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{0}))

\displaystyle\leq\int_{S_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{0})}\|v_{\delta_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{+\infty}\|v_{\delta_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{{\delta_{0}}}\|\theta^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

We pose

T_{\varepsilon}=\left(\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{{\delta_{0}}}\|\theta^{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<+\infty,

then
\lambda(S_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{0}))\leq T_{\varepsilon}
. So there exists
t_{2}\in[0,T_{\varepsilon}+1]\setminus S_{\varepsilon}(\delta_{0})
such

(4.9)

\|v_{\delta_{0}}(t_{2})\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

By the equation
(
4.8
)
and
(
4.9
)
, we get

\|\theta(t_{2})\|_{L^{2}}\leq\varepsilon.

Thus,
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|\theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}=0
, and this finishes the proof in this case.
\blacksquare

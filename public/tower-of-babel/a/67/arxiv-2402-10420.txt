The universal Lipschitz path space of the Heisenberg group ℍ 1 \mathbb{H}^{1}
Daniel Perry

Abstract
The goal of this paper is to define and inspect a metric version of the universal path space and study its application to purely 2-unrectifiable spaces, in particular the Heisenberg group ℍ 1 \mathbb{H}^{1} . The construction of the universal Lipschitz path space, as the metric version is called, echoes the construction of the universal cover for path-connected, locally path-connected, and semilocally simply connected spaces. We prove that this universal Lipschitz path space of a purely 2-unrectifiable space, much like the universal cover, satisfies a unique lifting property, a universal property, and is Lipschitz simply connected. The existence of such a universal Lipschitz path space of ℍ 1 \mathbb{H}^{1} will be used to prove that π 1 Lip ​ ( ℍ 1 ) \pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(\mathbb{H}^{1}) is torsion-free in a subsequent paper. † † Key words and phrases. Heisenberg group, contact manifolds, unrectifiability, geometric measure theory, sub-Riemannian manifolds, universal path space, unique lifting property Mathematical Reviews subject classification. Introduction

In this paper, we define the universal Lipschitz path space of a based metric space and prove that for a based purely 2-unrectifiable space the universal Lipschitz path space, much like the universal cover, satisfies a unique lifting property and is simply connected, a result we state presently. A key application of the following main result is for the based metric space
(\mathbb{H}^{1},0)
, which is the Heisenberg group based at the origin.

Theorem 1.1
.

For a based purely 2-unrectifiable space
(M,p_{0})
, there exists a based Lipschitz simply connected length space
(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])
and a based Lipschitz map
\pi:(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
that satisfies the unique lifting property.

The space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is called the
universal Lipschitz path space
of
M
. For the definition of the unique lifting property, see Definition
3.6
. Indeed, we prove a more general version of Theorem
1.1
, where purely 2-unrectifiable space is replaced by tree-like homotopy space, which is defined in Definition
4.1
. See Corollary
4.8
.

Moreover, for any metric space, the universal Lipschitz path space exists and is a pseudo-metric space, though it need not satisfy the unique lifting property. Provided that each point in the metric space supports only trivial local representation (see Definition
2.8
), the universal Lipschitz path space is a metric space (Lemma
2.9
) and in fact a length space (Theorem
3.10
).

Additionally, for a based metric space
(M,p_{0})
, the definition of the pseudo-metric on the universal Lipschitz path space yields a pseudo-metric on the associated first Lipschitz homotopy group
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
(Definition
2.6
), which is in fact a metric under a fairly mild assumption that the base point
p_{0}
supports only trivial local representation. Thus, as the first Lipschitz homotopy group acts isometrically on
\mathcal{P}_{M}
(Theorem
2.11
), under the mild assumption,
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
is a metric group. See Corollary
2.12
. This approach is relatively straightforward compared to efforts to topologize the standard fundamental group which can be quite involved; see for instance
[
4
]
.

Covering space theory is an integral tool of algebraic topology. See any introductory text to algebraic topology, for instance
[
7
, Section 1.3]
, for details. With its success in algebraic topology, the theory has been adjusted and imported into other fields with the aim of studying spaces that fail to satisfy the necessary semilocally simply connected property for the existence of a universal covering space (see ‘The Classification of Covering Spaces’ in
[
7
]
). This paper represents such an effort, importing the ideas into metric topology. The universal Lipschitz path space is in fact a generalization of the universal covering space as they agree for locally Lipschitz simply connected spaces, such as Riemannian manifolds, simplicial complexes with a simplexwise linear metric, and quotients of Lipschitz simply connected Carnot groups.

Other efforts to adjust the universal covering space to instances where the base space is not assumed to be semilocally simply connected include
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
. Berestovskiĭ and Plaut’s work in
[
2
]
is of particular note as the aims of that paper are similar to the goals of establishing the universal Lipschitz path space. In
[
2
]
, the covering
\mathbb{R}
-tree of a metric space
M
is defined in terms of “non-backtracking” rectifiable paths and is shown to be a metric tree that satisfies a unique lifting property and a universal property provided that
M
is a length space. In this paper, the universal Lipschitz path space of a metric space
M
is defined in terms of homotopy classes of Lipschitz paths and is shown to satisfy a unique lifting property and a universal property provided that
M
is purely 2-unrectifiable. In the sequel to this paper, the universal Lipschitz path space is shown to be a metric tree given this assumption. Exploring the relationship between covering
\mathbb{R}
-trees and universal Lipschitz path spaces is the subject of ongoing research.

As the development and classification of covering spaces in algebraic topology is a tool for studying fundamental groups, the universal Lipschitz path space is a tool for studying the first Lipschitz homotopy group
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
of a based metric space
(M,p_{0})
. Lipschitz homotopy groups are defined similarly to homotopy groups in algebraic topology with the additional requirement that all maps are Lipschitz rather than just continuous. See
[
5
, Definition 4.1]
for the original definition or
[
8
, Definition 28]
for an equivalent definition.

We are primarily interested in
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(\mathbb{H}^{1},0)
, the first Lipschitz homotopy group of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{1}
with the origin as the base point. Though the group
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(\mathbb{H}^{1},0)
is known to be uncountably generated
[
5
, Theorem 4.11(2)]
, there are interesting questions about the structure of the group which are still open. An example of such an open question was posed by Hajłasz in
[
6
]
: Is
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(\mathbb{H}^{1},0)
torsion-free? The tools developed in this paper will be used to answer this question in the affirmative in the sequel to this paper.

Alas, the analogy with covering space theory has limitations. Though we show there exists a universal Lipschitz path space of
\mathbb{H}^{1}
that is Lipschitz simply connected and satisfies the unique lifting property, there is no Lipschitz simply connected covering space of
\mathbb{H}^{1}
, which is indicated presently. Indeed, as is the case in covering space theory, a metric space has to satisfy a metric notion of semilocally simply connectedness in order to have a Lipschitz simply connected covering space.

Definition 1.2
.

A metric space
M
is
Lipschitz semilocally simply connected
if for any point
p\in M
, there exists an open neighborhood
U\subset M
of the point
p
such that the homomorphism induced by inclusion
U\hookrightarrow M
between first Lipschitz homotopy groups based at
p
,

\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(U,p)\longrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p),

is the trivial map.

As will be shown,
\mathbb{H}^{1}
is nowhere Lipschitz semilocally simply connected. Thus, there is no Lipschitz simply connected covering space of
\mathbb{H}^{1}
and so no notion of a universal Lipschitz covering space of
\mathbb{H}^{1}
.

Rather than focus solely on
\mathbb{H}^{1}
here, we prove a more general result: contact 3-manifolds are nowhere Lipschitz semilocally simply connected. The Heisenberg group is the prototypical contact 3-manifold in that a contact 3-manifold
(M,\xi)
, when endowed with a sub-Riemannian metric, is a metric space that is locally modeled by the Heisenberg group, as is presently stated.

Theorem 1.3

(BiLipschitz Theorem of Darboux, Corollary 23 in
[
8
]
)
.

Let
(M,\xi)
be a contact
3
-manifold endowed with a sub-Riemannian metric. For every
p\in M
,
there exists a locally biLipschitz open distributional embedding
\varphi:\mathbb{H}^{1}\hookrightarrow M
such that
\varphi(0)=p
.

As such, since
\mathbb{H}^{1}
is purely 2-unrectifiable
[
1
, Theorem 7.2]
, any contact 3-manifold is purely 2-unrectifiable as well
[
8
, Theorem 27]
. For a detailed account of contact 3-manifolds as sub-Riemannian manifolds, as well as some results involving their Lipschitz homotopy groups, see
[
8
]
.

Before proving contact 3-manifolds are nowhere Lipschitz semilocally simply connected, we prove a more general version of Theorem 32 in
[
8
]
.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
(X,x_{0})
and
(Y,y_{0})
be based metric spaces where
Y
is purely 2-unrectifiable. Let
\varphi:(X,x_{0})\hookrightarrow(Y,y_{0})
be a based biLipschitz embedding. Then the homomorphism induced by
\varphi
between the first Lipschitz homotopy groups

\varphi_{\#}:\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(X,x_{0})\longrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(Y,y_{0})

is injective.

Proof.

Let
\alpha:\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow X
be a Lipschitz map that represents an element of the kernel of the homomorphism
\varphi_{\#}
. So, there exists a Lipschitz map
H:\mathbb{D}^{2}\rightarrow Y
such that
H
restricted to the boundary is the Lipschitz map
\varphi\circ\alpha
:

H|_{\partial\mathbb{D}^{2}}=\varphi\circ\alpha.

As
Y
is purely 2-unrectifiable, by Lemma 31 in
[
8
]
, the Lipschitz map
H
can be taken such that the image of
H
is contained in the image of the Lipschitz map
\varphi\circ\alpha
. Thus,
H
takes image entirely in the image of
\varphi
:

\Ima(H)\subset\Ima(\varphi\circ\alpha)\subset\Ima(\varphi).

Since the inverse
\varphi^{-1}:\Ima{\varphi}\rightarrow X
is Lipschitz, the map given by composition

\varphi^{-1}\circ H:\mathbb{D}^{2}\longrightarrow X

is Lipschitz. The Lipschitz map
\varphi^{-1}\circ H
when restricted to the boundary of
\mathbb{D}^{2}
equals the map
\alpha
. Thus,
\alpha
is Lipschitz null homotopic. Therefore, the only element in the kernel of
\varphi_{\#}
is the trivial homotopy class.

∎

Theorem 1.5
.

Any contact 3-manifold
(M,\xi)
endowed with a sub-Riemannian metric is nowhere Lipschitz semilocally simply connected.

Proof.

Let
p\in M
and take an open neighborhood
U\subset M
of the point
p
. By
[
8
, Theorem 27]
, the contact 3-manifold
(M,\xi)
is purely 2-unrectifiable. As the inclusion map
(U,p)\hookrightarrow(M,p)

is a based biLipschitz embedding, by Lemma
1.4
, the homomorphism
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(U,p)\rightarrow\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p)
induced by the inclusion between the first Lipschitz homotopy groups is injective.

Via Theorem
1.3
, there exists a based biLipschitz embedding
\varphi:(\mathbb{H}^{1},0)\hookrightarrow(U,p)
of the Heisenberg group into the neighborhood
U
of
p
. So, by Lemma
1.4
, the homomorphism
\varphi_{\#}:\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(\mathbb{H}^{1},0)\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(U,p)
is injective. Since the group
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(\mathbb{H}^{1},0)
is non-trivial
[
5
]
, the group
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(U,p)
is non-trivial as well. Thus, the homomorphism
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(U,p)\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p)
is not the trivial map.

∎

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we define the universal Lipschitz path space as a pseudo-metric space and discuss sufficient conditions for when it is a metric space. We also define the endpoint projection map associated with the universal Lipschitz path space and define a free and isometric action of the Lipschitz fundamental group of the original metric space on the universal Lipschitz path space before concluding that the first Lipschitz homotopy group is a metric group given mild assumptions. In Section 3, we discuss in general the unique lifting property, the unique path lifting property, Lipschitz simply connectedness, and the universal property for the universal Lipschitz path space. In Section 4, we define tree-like homotopy spaces, a generalization of pure 2-unrectifiability. We then prove that the unique path lifting property is satisfied for tree-like homotopy spaces before concluding that for such spaces, the unique lifting property is satisfied and the universal Lipschitz path space is Lipschitz simply connected.

Definition of the universal Lipschitz path space

2.1
Background

Convention 2.1
.

Throughout this paper,
I=[0,1]
is the closed interval endowed with the Euclidean metric whose base point is
0\in I
. The product space
I\times I
is endowed with the
L^{1}
metric: for
(s,t),(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in I\times I
,

d^{1}((s,t),(s^{\prime},t^{\prime}))=|s-s^{\prime}|+|t-t^{\prime}|,

which is Lipschitz equivalent to the Euclidean metric on
I\times I
. For metric spaces
X
and
Y
, the Lipschitz constant of a Lipschitz function
f:X\rightarrow Y
is denoted by
\Lip(f)
. For a metric space
X
, the open ball centered at
x\in X
of radius
r>0
is denoted
B(x,r)
.

Lipschitz paths will play a fundamental role in the definition and study of the universal Lipschitz path space. All paths and loops, unless stated otherwise, are Lipschitz and have domain
I
. For a metric space
M
and a path
\gamma:[a,b]\rightarrow M
, the path
\gamma
is said to
join
initial point
\gamma(a)
to the end point
\gamma(b)
. The
length
of the path
\gamma
is denoted
\ell(\gamma)
.

We now present the definition of an arc length parametrized path. Note that for
\gamma:[a,b]\rightarrow M
, an arc length parametrized path,
\Lip(\gamma)=\frac{\ell(\gamma)}{b-a}
.

Definition 2.2
.

For a metric space
M
, a path
\gamma:[a,b]\rightarrow M
is
arc length parametrized
if for any
t_{1},t_{2}\in[a,b]
,

\ell\left(\left.\gamma\right|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}\right)=\frac{\ell(\gamma)}{b-a}~\left|t_{1}-t_{2}\right|.

For a path
\gamma
, the
inverse path
is denoted
\overline{\gamma}
. For paths
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
such that the end point of
\gamma_{1}
agrees with the initial point of
\gamma_{2}
, that is,
\gamma_{1}(1)=\gamma_{2}(0)
, the
concatenation
is denoted
\gamma_{1}\ast\gamma_{2}
.

Definition 2.3
.

For a metric space
M
, two paths
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}:I\longrightarrow M
are
homotopic rel endpoints
, denoted
\gamma\simeq\gamma^{\prime}
, if the initial points
\gamma(0)=\gamma^{\prime}(0)
and end points
\gamma(1)=\gamma^{\prime}(1)
of the paths agree and there exists a Lipschitz map
H:I\times I\rightarrow M
such that

H|_{I\times\{0\}}=\gamma,\hskip 7.11317ptH|_{I\times\{1\}}=\gamma^{\prime},\hskip 7.11317ptH|_{\{0\}\times I}=\gamma(0),\hskip 7.11317pt\text{and}\hskip 7.11317ptH|_{\{1\}\times I}=\gamma(1).

The map
H
is a
homotopy
from
\gamma
to
\gamma^{\prime}
. For a path
\gamma
, the class of all paths homotopic rel endpoints to
\gamma
is denoted
[\gamma]_{\gamma(0)}^{\gamma(1)}
and is referred to as the
homotopy class
of
\gamma
. When it does not cause confusion, the endpoints will be dropped from the notation. For a homotopy class
[\gamma]
, a
length minimizing path

{\gamma}_{\infty}\in[\gamma]
is a representative in the class such that

\ell({\gamma}_{\infty})=\inf\left\{~\ell(\gamma)~:~\gamma\in[\gamma]~\right\}.

The first Lipschitz homotopy group
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
of a based metric space
(M,p_{0})
is then the set of homotopy classes of loops
[\alpha]_{p_{0}}^{p_{0}}
with group operation given by concatenation:
[\alpha_{1}]_{p_{0}}^{p_{0}}*[\alpha_{2}]_{p_{0}}^{p_{0}}:=[\alpha_{1}*\alpha_{2}]_{p_{0}}^{p_{0}}
. This notion is equivalent to the definitions of first Lipschitz homotopy group which appear in
[
5
]
and
[
8
]
. Similar to fundamental groups, a Lipschitz map between based metric spaces induces a homomorphism between the associated first Lipschitz homotopy groups.

Definition 2.4
.

For based metric spaces
(X,x_{0})
and
(Y,y_{0})
and a based Lipschitz map
f:(X,x_{0})\rightarrow(Y,y_{0})
, the homomorphism induced by the map
f
between first Lipschitz homotopy groups is

\begin{array}[]{rccl}f_{\#}:&\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(X,x_{0})&\longrightarrow&\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(Y,y_{0})\\
&[\alpha]&\longmapsto&[f\circ\alpha].\end{array}

The map
f_{\#}
is indeed well-defined and a homomorphism.

Along with considering homotopy classes, we will often benefit from being able to relate the distance between points in a metric space with the lengths of paths between said points. We define a few types of metric spaces where such a relationship exists. Note that an immediate consequence of Definition
2.5
is that every quasi-convex space is Lipschitz path connected.

Definition 2.5
.

A metric space
X
with metric
d
is
quasi-convex
if there exists a real-value
C\geq 1
such that for any pair of points
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
, there exists a path
\gamma_{X}:I\rightarrow X
where
\gamma_{X}(0)=x_{1}
and
\gamma_{X}(1)=x_{2}
such that

\ell(\gamma_{X})\leq C\cdot d(x_{1},x_{2}).

Such a value
C
is called a
quasi-convexity constant
of
X
. If
C
can be taken to equal
1
, then
X
is a
geodesic space
. The metric space
X
is a
length space
if
C
can be taken to be any value
C>1
, or equivalently, if for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
,

d(x_{1},x_{2})=\inf\left\{~\ell(\gamma)~:~\gamma:I\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Lipschitz}}}{{\longrightarrow}}X,~\gamma(0)=x_{1},~\gamma(1)=x_{2}~\right\}.

2.2
Metric structure on the universal path space

For the remainder of this section, fix a based metric space
(M,p_{0})
with metric
d
. Additionally, since the first Lipschitz homotopy group and the universal Lipschitz path space only depend on the Lipschitz path component of the base point, assume that
M
is Lipschitz path connected and has more than one element. We construct the universal Lipschitz path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
for
(M,p_{0})
, which is also a based space.

The definition of the universal Lipschitz path space as a set is similar to the set definition of the universal covering space from algebraic topology, in that elements of the universal Lipschitz path space are homotopy classes of paths, the meaningful distinction being that we are considering Lipschitz paths and homotopies rather than continuous paths and homotopies.

Moreover, we define a means of lifting the metric on the metric space to a pseudo-metric on the universal Lipschitz path space. The construction of the pseudo-metric, as well as several of the insights and results that follow, are inspired by the work of Bogley and Sieraski in
[
3
]
.

Definition 2.6
.

The
universal Lipschitz path space

\mathcal{P}_{M}
of a based metric space
(M,p_{0})
as a set is

\mathcal{P}_{M}:=\left\{~[\gamma]_{p_{0}}^{p}~:~\gamma:I\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Lipschitz}}}{{\longrightarrow}}M,~\gamma(0)=p_{0},~\gamma(1)=p~\right\}.

The base point of
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is
[p_{0}]
, the class of loops in
M
based at
p_{0}
that are Lipschitz null-homotopic. The
lifted pseudo-metric
on the universal Lipschitz path space is defined to be, for
[\gamma_{1}],[\gamma_{2}]\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
,

d_{\mathcal{P}}\left([\gamma_{1}],[\gamma_{2}]\right):=\inf\left\{~\ell(\beta)~:~\beta\simeq\overline{\gamma_{1}}*\gamma_{2}~\right\}.

where the infimum is taken over all paths
\beta
that are homotopic to
\overline{\gamma_{1}}*\gamma_{2}
for some representatives
\gamma_{1}\in[\gamma_{1}]
and
\gamma_{2}\in[\gamma_{2}]
.

Convention 2.7
.

As we will only be discussing the universal Lipschitz path space with reference to metric spaces and Lipschitz maps, we will refer to the space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
simply as the universal path space.

Note that for any based metric space
(M,p_{0})
, the first Lipschitz homotopy group
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})\subset\mathcal{P}_{M}
is a subset of the universal path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
as each element
[\alpha]\in\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
is a homotopy class of paths whose endpoint agrees with its initial point
p_{0}
. Thus, Definition
2.6
provides a pseudo-metric on the first Lipscthiz homotopy group when the lifted pseudo-metric
d_{\mathcal{P}}
is restricted to
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})\subset\mathcal{P}_{M}
.

Now, the lifted pseudo-metric on the universal path space is not in general a metric. The harmonic archipelago is an example of a metric space whose lifted pseudo-metric on its universal path space is not a metric
[
3
, Example 1.1]
. For the harmonic archipelago, the lifted pseudo-metric fails to be a metric because of the existence of a point that supports a non-trivial homotopy class of loops that is locally representable (such points are called
singular
in
[
3
]
).

Definition 2.8
.

For a metric space
M
and a point
p\in M
, a homotopy class
[\alpha]\in\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p)
is
locally representable
if for every neighborhood
U\subset M
of the point
p
, there exists a representative
\alpha_{U}\in[\alpha]
such that the image of the representative
\Ima(\alpha_{U})\subset U
is a subset of
U
. If the only locally representable homotopy class for the point
p\in M
is the trivial class
[p]
, then
p
is said to
support only trivial local representation
.

As is indicated in the following lemma, the the lifted pseudo-metric when restricted to the first Lipschitz homotopy group is a metric when the base point supports only trivial local representation. Moreover, the lifted pseudo-metric is a metric on the universal path space as long as each point in the space supports only trivial local representation.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
(M,p_{0})
be a based metric space. If the base point
p_{0}
supports only trivial local representation, then the lifted pseudo-metric
d_{\mathcal{P}}
restricted to the first Lipschitz homotopy group
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
is a metric. Moreover, if each point in
M
supports only trivial local representation, then the lifted pseudo-metric
d_{\mathcal{P}}
on the universal path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is a metric. In this case,
d_{\mathcal{P}}
is called the
lifted metric
.

Proof.

We will only show that the lifted pseudo-metric
d_{\mathcal{P}}
satisfies positive definiteness. Symmetry and the triangle inequality are obvious. In the following, the metric on
M
is denoted by
d
.

First, suppose that
[\gamma_{1}],[\gamma_{2}]\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
are equal homotopy classes,
[\gamma_{1}]=[\gamma_{2}]
. So, representatives
\gamma_{1}\in[\gamma_{1}]
and
\gamma_{2}\in[\gamma_{2}]
are homotopic rel endpoints. Thus, the paths
\overline{\gamma_{1}}
and
\gamma_{2}
concatenate to a loop
\overline{\gamma_{1}}*\gamma_{2}
based at
\gamma_{1}(1)=\gamma_{2}(1)
that is null-homotopic. Therefore,
d_{\mathcal{P}}([\gamma_{1}],[\gamma_{2}])=0
.

Now, suppose for homotopy classes
[\gamma_{1}],[\gamma_{2}]\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
that

d_{\mathcal{P}}([\gamma_{1}],[\gamma_{2}])=0
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
be given. Since the lifted pseudo-metric is defined as an infimum, there exists a path
\alpha_{\varepsilon}
with initial point
\alpha_{\varepsilon}(0)=\gamma_{1}(1)
and end point
\alpha_{\varepsilon}(1)=\gamma_{2}(1)

such that
\alpha_{\varepsilon}\simeq\overline{\gamma_{1}}*\gamma_{2}
and
\ell(\alpha_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon
. For any
t\in I
,

\displaystyle d(\gamma_{1}(1),\alpha_{\varepsilon}(t))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ell\left(\alpha_{\varepsilon}|_{[0,t]}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ell(\alpha_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\varepsilon.

Thus, the image
\Ima(\alpha_{\varepsilon})
is a subset of the open ball
B(\gamma_{1}(1),\varepsilon)
centered at
\gamma_{1}(1)
of radius
\varepsilon
.

If
[\gamma_{1}],[\gamma_{2}]\in\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
, then it is immediate that
\left[\overline{\gamma_{1}}*\gamma_{2}\right]\in\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
and the path
\alpha_{\varepsilon}
is a loop. Otherwise, since
\varepsilon>0
is an arbitrary positive value,
the initial point
\alpha_{\varepsilon}(0)=\gamma_{1}(1)
is arbitrarily close to the end point
\alpha_{\varepsilon}(1)=\gamma_{2}(1)
, that is,
d(\gamma_{1}(1),\gamma_{2}(1))<\varepsilon
for all
\varepsilon>0
. Thus, the end points of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are equal,
\gamma_{1}(1)=\gamma_{2}(1)
. So,
\left[\overline{\gamma_{1}}*\gamma_{2}\right]
is a class of loops centered at
\gamma_{1}(1)
and, for any
\varepsilon>0
, the path
\alpha_{\varepsilon}
is a loop.

Now, for every open ball
B(\gamma_{1}(1),\varepsilon)
centered at
\gamma_{1}(1)
, the class of loops
[\overline{\gamma_{1}}*\gamma_{2}]
has a representative
\alpha_{\varepsilon}
whose image is a subset of the open ball. Since open balls form a basis for the topology on
M
, the class
[\overline{\gamma_{1}}*\gamma_{2}]
is locally representable. So, whether only the base point
p_{0}
supports only trivial local representation and
[\gamma_{1}],[\gamma_{2}]\in\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
or all points support only trivial local representation,
[\overline{\gamma_{1}}*\gamma_{2}]
is the trivial class based at
\gamma_{1}(1)
. Therefore, the paths
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are homotopic rel endpoints, that is,
[\gamma_{1}]=[\gamma_{2}]
.
∎

2.3
Endpoint projection and an action of
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})

Along with the pseudo-metric, there is a based map from the based universal path space
(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])
to the based metric space
(M,p_{0})
given by endpoint projection:

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\pi:&\mathcal{P}_{M}&\rightarrow&M\\
&[\gamma]&\mapsto&\gamma(1).\end{array}

The map
\pi
is well-defined as all paths contained in the homotopy class
[\gamma]
have the same endpoint. Additionally, the map
\pi
is based as
\pi([p_{0}])=p_{0}
. As is now shown, endpoint projection is a Lipschitz map.

Lemma 2.10
.

Endpoint projection
\pi:\mathcal{P}_{M}\rightarrow M
is Lipschitz with

0<\Lip(\pi)\leq 1.

Additionally, if
M
is a geodesic space, then
\Lip(\pi)=1
.

Proof.

Let
[\gamma_{1}]_{p_{0}}^{p_{1}},[\gamma_{2}]_{p_{0}}^{p_{2}}\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
. Note that if a path
\beta
is homotopic rel endpoints to the concatenation
\overline{\gamma_{1}}*\gamma_{2}
, then
\beta
is a path with initial point
p_{1}
and endpoint
p_{2}
. Then, since the distance
d(p_{1},p_{2})
between points
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
is bounded above by the length
\ell(\beta)
of any path
\beta
joining them,

\displaystyle d(\pi[\gamma_{1}]_{p_{0}}^{p_{1}},\pi[\gamma_{2}]_{p_{0}}^{p_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf\{\ell(\beta)~:~\beta\text{ joins }p_{1}\text{ and }p_{2}\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf\{\ell(\beta)~:~\beta\simeq\overline{\gamma_{1}}*\gamma_{2}\text{ rel endpoints }p_{1}\text{ and }p_{2}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{P}}\left([\gamma_{1}]_{p_{0}}^{p_{1}},[\gamma_{2}]_{p_{0}}^{p_{2}}\right).

Therefore, the map
\pi
is Lipschitz and
\Lip(\pi)\leq 1
.

Now, by assumption, the metric space
M
is Lipschitz path connected and has more than one element. Thus, endpoint projection
\pi
is not a constant map. So,
\Lip(\pi)>0
.

Additionally, assume that
M
is a geodesic space. Let
p_{1},p_{2}\in M
be distinct points in
M
. Let
\gamma_{M}:I\rightarrow M
be a geodesic joining
p_{1}
to
p_{2}
. Now, let
\gamma_{1}
be a path in
M
joining
p_{0}
to
p_{1}
. So,
\gamma_{2}=\gamma_{1}*\gamma_{M}
is a path in
M
joining
p_{0}
to
p_{2}
. Then, considering
[\gamma_{1}]_{p_{0}}^{p_{1}}
and
[\gamma_{2}]_{p_{0}}^{p_{2}}
, since
\gamma_{M}\simeq\overline{\gamma_{1}}*\gamma_{2}
,

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{P}}([\gamma_{1}]_{p_{0}}^{p_{1}},[\gamma_{2}]_{p_{0}}^{p_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\{~\ell(\beta)~:~\beta\simeq\overline{\gamma_{1}}*\gamma_{2}~\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell(\gamma_{M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(p_{1},p_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(\pi[\gamma_{1}]_{p_{0}}^{p_{1}},\pi[\gamma_{2}]_{p_{0}}^{p_{2}})

Thus,
\Lip(\pi)=1
.

∎

There is also a left group action of the first Lipschitz homotopy group
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
on the universal path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
given by concatenation:

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\ast:&\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})\times\mathcal{P}_{M}&\longrightarrow&\mathcal{P}_{M}\\
\\
&([\alpha]_{p_{0}}^{p_{0}},[\gamma]_{p_{0}}^{p})&\longmapsto&[\alpha\ast\gamma]_{p_{0}}^{p}.\end{array}

The stated map is indeed a left group action as, for any
[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
,

[p_{0}]\ast[\gamma]=[p_{0}\ast\gamma]=[\gamma],

and, for any
[\alpha_{1}],[\alpha_{2}]\in\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
and
[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
,

\displaystyle[\alpha_{1}]\ast([\alpha_{2}]\ast[\gamma])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\alpha_{1}]\ast([\alpha_{2}\ast\gamma])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\alpha_{1}\ast(\alpha_{2}\ast\gamma)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(\alpha_{1}\ast\alpha_{2})\ast\gamma]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\alpha_{1}\ast\alpha_{2}]\ast[\gamma]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([\alpha_{1}]\ast[\alpha_{2}])\ast[\gamma].

Additionally, the group action preserves the fibers of the endpoint projection
\pi
as, for
[\alpha]_{p_{0}}^{p_{0}}\in\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
and
[\gamma]_{p_{0}}^{p}\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
,

\pi\left([\alpha]_{p_{0}}^{p_{0}}\ast[\gamma]_{p_{0}}^{p}\right)=\pi\left([\alpha\ast\gamma]_{p_{0}}^{p}\right)=p=\pi\left([\gamma]_{p_{0}}^{p}\right).

Theorem 2.11
.

The first Lipschitz homotopy group
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
acts freely and isometrically on the universal path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
.

Proof.

First, to show that the group action is free, let
[\gamma]\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
and
[\alpha]\in\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
where
[\alpha]\ast[\gamma]=[\gamma]
. By the definition of the group action, the paths
\alpha\ast\gamma\simeq\gamma
are homotopic. Recall that the path
\overline{\gamma}
is the reverse of the path
\gamma
and that
\gamma\ast\overline{\gamma}
is a null homotopic loop based at
p_{0}
. The loop
\alpha
is thus null homotopic as

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\alpha\ast p_{0}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\alpha\ast(\gamma\ast\overline{\gamma})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle(\alpha\ast\gamma)\ast\overline{\gamma}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\gamma\ast\overline{\gamma}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle p_{0}.

Thus, since
[\alpha]=[p_{0}]
, the group action is free.

Now, to show that the group action is isometric, let
[\gamma_{1}],[\gamma_{2}]\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
and
[\alpha]\in\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
. Note the following equivalences, where
\beta
is a path in
M
:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\beta\simeq\overline{\gamma_{1}}\ast\gamma_{2}&\Leftrightarrow&\beta\simeq\overline{\gamma_{1}}\ast p_{0}\ast\gamma_{2}\\
&\Leftrightarrow&\beta\simeq\overline{\gamma_{1}}\ast(\overline{\alpha}\ast\alpha)\ast\gamma_{2}\\
&\Leftrightarrow&\beta\simeq(\overline{\gamma_{1}}\ast\overline{\alpha})\ast(\alpha\ast\gamma_{2})\\
&\Leftrightarrow&\beta\simeq(\overline{\alpha\ast\gamma_{1}})\ast(\alpha\ast\gamma_{2}).\\
\end{array}

The final step follows from the equality of paths
\overline{\gamma_{1}}\ast\overline{\alpha}=\overline{\alpha\ast\gamma_{1}}
.

Therefore, the infimums appearing from the definition of the lifted pseudo-metric
d_{\mathcal{P}}
in

d_{\mathcal{P}}([\gamma_{1}],[\gamma_{2}])=\inf\left\{\ell(\beta)~:~\beta\simeq\overline{\gamma_{1}}\ast\gamma_{2}\right\}

and

d_{\mathcal{P}}([\alpha\ast\gamma_{1}],[\alpha\ast\gamma_{2}])=\inf\left\{\ell(\beta)~:~\beta\simeq(\overline{\alpha\ast\gamma_{1}})\ast(\alpha\ast\gamma_{2})\right\}

are determined by the same set of paths. As such, the distances

d_{\mathcal{P}}([\gamma_{1}],[\gamma_{2}])=d_{\mathcal{P}}([\alpha\ast\gamma_{1}],[\alpha\ast\gamma_{2}])

are equal and thus the group action is isometric.
∎

Following from Theorem
2.11
, the first Lipschitz homotopy group
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
acts isometrically on itself when the action is restricted to
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})\subset\mathcal{P}_{M}
. Thus, by Lemma
2.9
and Theorem
2.11
,

Corollary 2.12
.

If
p_{0}
supports only trivial local representation, then the first Lipschitz homotopy group
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
is a metric group with respect to the lifted metric
d_{\mathcal{P}}
.

3
Unique lifting property and the universal path space

We now discuss lifts of Lipschitz maps with respect to endpoint projection. In this section, as in the last, fix a based metric space
(M,p_{0})
with metric
d
. Additionally, assume that
M
is Lipschitz path connected and contains more than one element.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(X,x_{0})
be a based metric space and
f:(X,x_{0})\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
be a based Lipschitz map. A
lift
of the map
f
is a based Lipschitz map
F:(X,x_{0})\rightarrow(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])
such that the following diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}_{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}

The following result comparing Lipschitz constants of the Lipschitz map
f
and the lift
F
is immediate, following from Lemma
2.10
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a based metric space with metric
d_{X}
and
f:X\rightarrow M
be a based Lipschitz map. Let
F:X\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{M}
be a lift of the map
f
. Then

\Lip(f)\leq\Lip(F).

Proof.

Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
. Then,

\displaystyle d(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(\pi\circ F(x_{1}),\pi\circ F(x_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{P}}(F(x_{1}),F(x_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Lip(F)\cdot d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2}).

Thus, the Lipschitz constant
\Lip(f)
is no larger than
\Lip(F)
.

∎

3.1
Lifts of paths to the universal path space

Now, we show that there exist lifts of paths in
M
to the universal path space. Let
\gamma:(I,0)\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
be a based path. For any
t\in I
, there exists a reparametrization
\gamma_{t}
of the path
\gamma
such that the endpoint of the reparametrization is the point
\gamma(t)
:

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\gamma_{t}:&(I,0)&\rightarrow&(M,p_{0})\\
&s&\mapsto&\gamma(ts).\end{array}

The path
\gamma_{t}
then determines an element of the universal path space,
[\gamma_{t}]_{p_{0}}^{\gamma(t)}\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
. Thus, there is a map into the universal path space given by:

\begin{array}[]{rccl}\widehat{\gamma}:&(I,0)&\rightarrow&(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])\\
&t&\mapsto&\displaystyle[\gamma_{t}]_{p_{0}}^{\gamma(t)}.\end{array}

The map is based as
\widehat{\gamma}(0)=[p_{0}]
. Also, for any
t\in I
,

\pi\circ\widehat{\gamma}(t)=\pi\left([\gamma_{t}]_{p_{0}}^{\gamma(t)}\right)=\gamma(t).

The next lemma shows that the map
\widehat{\gamma}
is indeed Lipschitz and thus a lift of the path
\gamma
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\gamma:(I,0)\longrightarrow(M,p_{0})
be a based path. Then, the map
\widehat{\gamma}

is Lipschitz.
Thus,
\widehat{\gamma}
is a lift of the path
\gamma
. Additionally,
\Lip(\widehat{\gamma})=\Lip(\gamma)
.

Proof.

Let
t_{1},t_{2}\in I
. The restricted path
\gamma|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}
is homotopic rel endpoints to the concatenation
\overline{\gamma_{t_{1}}}*\gamma_{t_{2}}
. So,

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{P}}(\widehat{\gamma}(t_{1}),\widehat{\gamma}(t_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{~\ell(\beta)~:~\beta\simeq\overline{\gamma_{t_{1}}}*\gamma_{t_{2}}~\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ell(\gamma|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Lip(\gamma)\cdot|t_{1}-t_{2}|.

Thus, the map
\widehat{\gamma}
is indeed Lipschitz with
\Lip(\widehat{\gamma})\leq\Lip(\gamma)
. So,
\widehat{\gamma}
is a lift of
\gamma
. Additionally, by Lemma
3.2
,
\Lip(\widehat{\gamma})\geq\Lip(\gamma)
. Therefore,
\Lip(\widehat{\gamma})=\Lip(\gamma)
.
∎

3.2
Unique path lifiting and unique lifting properties

Having determined that lifts of paths exist with reference to the universal path space, we show in Theorem
3.5
what conditions are sufficient for a general Lipschitz map to have a lift.

As we proceed, we will also be interested in knowing if the lift of any given map is unique. When all lifts of paths are unique, the universal path space is said to have the unique path lifting property, which is defined now.

Definition 3.4
.

Endpoint projection
\pi:(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
has the
unique path lifting property
if for any based path
\gamma:(I,0)\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
, there exists a unique lift
\widehat{\gamma}:(I,0)\rightarrow(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])
such that the following diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}_{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\gamma}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exists!}

When this is the case, the universal path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is said to satisfy the unique path lifting property.

In the last section of this paper, we show that the universal path space of any
\mathbb{D}^{2}
-unrectifiable metric space, in particular the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{1}
, satisfies the unique path lifting property. See Theorem
4.4
.

In addition to describing sufficient conditions for lifting a Lipschitz map, we also show that the lifts guaranteed by Theorem
3.5
are unique whenever the universal path space satisfies the unique path lifting property.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(X,x_{0})
be a based quasi-convex metric space with metric
d_{X}
. Let
f:(X,x_{0})\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
be a based Lipschitz map such that the homomorphism induced by
f
between first Lipschitz homotopy groups

f_{\#}:\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(X,x_{0})\longrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})

is trivial at
[p_{0}]
. Then, there exists a lift
\widehat{f}
of the map
f
:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}_{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{f}}

Additionally, if
X
is a length space, then
\text{Lip}(\widehat{f})=\text{Lip}(f)
. Moreover, if the universal path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
satisfies the unique path lifting property, then the lift
\widehat{f}
is unique.

Proof.

We will begin by defining a lift of the map
f
and arguing that the lift is well-defined. The argument will be the standard such argument. Then, we will show that the lift is indeed Lipschitz which follows from the map
f
being Lipschitz and the metric space
X
being quasi-convex. Finally, with the assumption that
\pi:(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
satisfies the unique path lifting property, we show that the lift is unique.

We now define a map
\widehat{f}:X\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{M}
using that the metric space
X
is Lipschitz path-connected. Let
x\in X
. Since
X
is Lipschitz path-connected, there exists a based path
\gamma:(I,0)\rightarrow(X,x_{0})
joining the base point
x_{0}
to
x=\gamma(1)
. Thus, the composition
f\circ\gamma
is a path in
M
joining the base point
p_{0}
to the point
f(x)
. So, by the construction in Section
3.1
, there is a lift
\widehat{f\circ\gamma}
of the path
f\circ\gamma
. Define the map
\widehat{f}
as sending the point
x\in X
to the endpoint of this lifted path
\widehat{f\circ\gamma}
:

\begin{array}[]{rccc}\widehat{f}:&(X,x_{0})&\rightarrow&(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])\\
&x&\mapsto&\widehat{f\circ\gamma}(1).\end{array}

To show that the map
\widehat{f}
is well-defined, let
\gamma^{\prime}:(I,0)\rightarrow(X,x_{0})
be another based path joining
x_{0}
and
x
. Then, the concatenation
\gamma^{\prime}*\overline{\gamma}
is a loop in
X
that is based at
x_{0}
and post-composing by
f
yields a loop in
M
based at
p_{0}
:

f\circ(\gamma^{\prime}*\overline{\gamma})=(f\circ\gamma^{\prime})*(\overline{f\circ\gamma}).

Since
f
induces the trivial homomorphism between first Lipschitz homotopy groups, the loop
\displaystyle(f\circ\gamma^{\prime})*(\overline{f\circ\gamma})
is null-homotopic. Thus, the paths
f\circ\gamma^{\prime}
and
f\circ\gamma
joining
p_{0}
to
f(x)
are homotopic rel endpoints. Therefore, the map
\widehat{f}
is well-defined as we have the following equality in
\mathcal{P}_{M}
:

\widehat{f\circ\gamma^{\prime}}(1)=[f\circ\gamma^{\prime}]=[f\circ\gamma]=\widehat{f\circ\gamma}(1).

An immediate consequence of
\widehat{f}
being well-defined is that the map is based. Indeed, when determining
\widehat{f}(x_{0})
, the path
\gamma
can be taken to be the constant path at
x_{0}
. Then, the path
f\circ\gamma
is the constant path at
p_{0}
. So, the lifted path
\widehat{f\circ\gamma}
is the constant path at
[p_{0}]
and thus,
\widehat{f}(x_{0})=\widehat{f\circ\gamma}(1)=[p_{0}]
.

Before showing that the map
\widehat{f}
is Lipschitz, note that since
\widehat{f\circ\gamma}
is a lift of the path
f\circ\gamma
in
M
,

\pi\circ\widehat{f}(x)=\pi\circ\widehat{f\circ\gamma}(1)=f\circ\gamma(1)=f(x).

Thus, provided that the map is Lipschitz,
\widehat{f}
is indeed a lift of the given map
f
.

Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
. Since the metric space
X
is quasi-convex, by Definition
2.5
, there exists a path
\gamma_{X}
joining
x_{1}
to
x_{2}
such that
\ell(\gamma_{X})\leq C\cdot d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})
, where
C
is the quasi-convexity constant for
X
.

Consider the distance
d_{\mathcal{P}}(\widehat{f}(x_{1}),\widehat{f}(x_{2}))
. Since the classes
\widehat{f}(x_{1})
and
\widehat{f}(x_{2})
do not depend on the paths joining the base point
x_{0}
to
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
respectively, neither does the distance. With this in mind, let
\gamma_{1}
be a path in
X
joining
x_{0}
to
x_{1}
, which exists since
X
is Lipschitz path-connected. Also, let
\gamma_{2}=\gamma_{1}*\gamma_{X}
, which is a path in
X
joining
x_{0}
to
x_{2}
. Then, we can rewrite the distance as

d_{\mathcal{P}}(\widehat{f}(x_{1}),\widehat{f}(x_{2}))=d_{\mathcal{P}}([f\circ\gamma_{1}],[f\circ\gamma_{2}]).

Note, by design
\overline{\gamma_{1}}*\gamma_{2}\simeq\gamma_{X}
. So, we have

(\overline{f\circ\gamma_{1}})*(f\circ\gamma_{2})=f\circ(\overline{\gamma_{1}}*\gamma_{2})\simeq f\circ\gamma_{X}.

This equivalence, along with the map
f
being Lipschitz, yields the following string of inequalities:

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{P}}(\widehat{f}(x_{1}),\widehat{f}(x_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{P}}([f\circ\gamma_{1}],[f\circ\gamma_{2}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{\alpha}\{~\ell(\alpha)~:~\alpha\simeq(\overline{f\circ\gamma_{1}})*(f\circ\gamma_{2})~\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ell(f\circ\gamma_{X})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\text{Lip}(f)\cdot\ell(\gamma_{X})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\text{Lip}(f)\cdot C\cdot d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})

Therefore,
\widehat{f}
is Lipschitz with
\text{Lip}(\widehat{f})\leq C\cdot\text{Lip}(f)
. Moreover, if the metric space
X
is a length space, then
\text{Lip}(\widehat{f})\leq\text{Lip}(f)
. Additionally, by Lemma
3.2
,
\text{Lip}(f)\leq\text{Lip}(\widehat{f})
. Thus the Lipschitz constants for the maps
\widehat{f}
and
f
are equal.

Finally, assume that the universal path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
satisfies the unique path lifting property. Let
F:X\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{M}
be a lift of
f
and take a point
x\in X
. Since
X
is Lipschitz path-connected, there exists a path
\gamma
joining the base point
x_{0}
to
x
. The composition
F\circ\gamma:I\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{M}
is then a lift of the path
f\circ\gamma
. By assumption, lifts of paths are unique. Thus, we have an equality of paths
F\circ\gamma=\widehat{f\circ\gamma}
. Therefore, the lift
F
is equal to the lift
\widehat{f}
as

F(x)=F\circ\gamma(1)=\widehat{f\circ\gamma}(1)=\widehat{f}(x)

for any
x\in X
.
∎

3.3
Unique lifting property and Lipschitz simply connected universal path space

With the sufficient conditions for the existence of a lift from Theorem
3.5
in mind, We define what it means for a based Lipschitz map to satisfy the unique lifting property.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
(E,e_{0})
be a based metric space. A based Lipschitz map
r:(E,e_{0})\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
satisfies the
unique lifting property
if, for any based, quasi-convex metric space
(X,x_{0})
and any based Lipschitz map
f:(X,x_{0})\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
such that the homomorphism induced on first Lipschitz homotopy groups
f_{\#}:\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(X,x_{0})\rightarrow\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
is trivial, there exists a unique based Lipschitz map
\widehat{f}:(X,x_{0})\rightarrow(E,e_{0})
such that the following diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exists!}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{f}}

When such a map
\widehat{f}
exists, the map is called a
lift
with respect to the map
r:(E,e_{0})\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
.

Following from definitions, if the map
\pi:\mathcal{P}_{M}\rightarrow M
satisfies the unique lifting property, then
\pi
satisfies the unique path lifting property. By Theorem
3.5
, the converse is true as well.

As we now show, if
\pi:(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
satisfies the unique lifting property, then the universal path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is Lipschitz simply connected.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(E,e_{0})
be a based metric space. If a based Lipschitz map
r:(E,e_{0})\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
satisfies the unique lifting property, the homomorphism induced by
r
between first Lipschitz homotopy groups,

r_{\#}:\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(E,e_{0})\longrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})

is injective.

Proof.

Let homotopy class
[\alpha]\in\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(E,e_{0})
be in the kernel of the homomorphism
r_{\#}
. Then, for any representative
\alpha
, the loop
r\circ\alpha
in
M
is null-homotopic. So, there exists a Lipschitz map
H:\mathbb{D}^{2}\rightarrow M
such that the following solid diagram commutes:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{S}^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{D}^{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{H}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exists}

Since the based map
r:(E,e_{0})\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
satisfies the unique lifting property and the 2-disk
\mathbb{D}^{2}
is quasi-convex and Lipschitz simply connected, there exists a lift of the map
H
to a Lipschitz map
\widehat{H}:\mathbb{D}^{2}\rightarrow E
as indicated in the diagram. So, the loop
\alpha
in the metric space
E
is null-homotopic and thus
[\alpha]=[e_{0}]
. Therefore, the kernel of the homomorphism
r_{\#}
is trivial.
∎

As a matter of note, Lemma
3.7
does not rely on the uniqueness of a lift, but rather only on the existence of a lift. See Theorem
3.5
for how to construct the necessary lift in the instance that the map
\pi:(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
is considered.

Theorem 3.8
.

If
\pi:(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
satisfies the unique lifting property, then the universal path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is Lipschitz simply connected.

Proof.

Assume
\pi:(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
satisfies the unique lifting property. By Lemma
3.7
, the induced homomorphism
\pi_{\#}
is injective. Thus, it is enough to show that the image of the homomorphism
\pi_{\#}
is trivial in the group
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
in order to show that
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])
is trivial.

Let the homotopy class
[\alpha]
of loops based at
p_{0}
be in the image of the homomorphism
\pi_{\#}
. So, there exists a representative
\alpha:I\rightarrow M
in
[\alpha]
such that for some based loop
A:I\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{M}
, we have equality of maps
\alpha=\pi\circ A
. That is to say, the loop
A
is a lift of the loop
\alpha
. By assumption, the map
\pi
satisfies the unique lifting property. So, the lift of the loop
\alpha
in
\mathcal{P}_{M}
must be
A=\widehat{\alpha}
, the lift of a path described in Section
3.1
. Since
\widehat{\alpha}
is a loop, the initial and terminal points of the path
\widehat{\alpha}
are equal,

[p_{0}]=\widehat{\alpha}(0)=\widehat{\alpha}(1)=[\alpha].

Thus,
\alpha
is null-homotopic and therefore the image of
\pi_{\#}
is the trivial subgroup in
\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
.
∎

Theorem
3.8
offers a converse to the lifting requirements of the unique path lifting property, which we state as the following result of independent interest.

Corollary 3.9
.

Suppose that endpoint projection
\pi:(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
satisfies the unique lifting property. Let
X
be a based quasi-convex metric space and let
f:X\rightarrow M
be a based Lipschitz map. Then, there exists a unique lift
\widehat{f}:X\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{M}
of the map
f
if and only if the homomorphism induced by
f
between first Lipschitz homotopy groups

f_{\#}:\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(X,x_{0})\longrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})

is trivial at
[p_{0}]
.

Proof.

Since the map
\pi:(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
satisfies the unique lifting property, if
f_{\#}
is trivial, then there is a unique lift of
f
(See Definition
3.6
).

Now assume that there is a unique lift of the map
f
. The map
f
can be expressed as the composition
\pi\circ\widehat{f}
. Thus, the map induced on first Lipschitz homotopy groups can also be expressed as a composition:

f_{\#}=(\pi\circ\widehat{f})_{\#}=\pi_{\#}\circ\widehat{f}_{\#}.

Since, by Theorem
3.8
, the universal path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is Lipschitz simply connected, the homomorphism
\widehat{f}_{\#}
is trivial. Therefore, so is the homomorphism
f_{\#}
.
∎

3.4
The universal property of the universal path space

Before turning our attention to the universal path space over a
\mathbb{D}^{2}
-unrectifiable metric space, we establish a universal property for the universal path space. First, we return to the metric structure of the universal path space and argue that
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is a length space whenever it is a metric space. In the following proof, the path
A
is a lift of the path
\alpha
, though this fact has no bearing on the argument.

Theorem 3.10
.

For a based metric space
M
such that
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is a metric space, the universal path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is a length space.

Proof.

Let
[\gamma],[\eta]\in\mathcal{P}_{M}
. Let
C>1
and choose a representative
\alpha\in[\overline{\gamma}*\eta]
such that
\ell(\alpha)\leq C\cdot d_{\mathcal{P}}([\gamma],[\eta])
. By choosing an arc length parametrization, we may assume that
\text{Lip}(\alpha)=\ell(\alpha)
.

Consider the path in
A:I\to\mathcal{P}_{M}
given by
t\mapsto[\eta*\overline{\alpha}_{1-t}]
, where
\overline{\alpha}_{1-t}
is the inverse path
\overline{\alpha}
reparametrized with respect to the value
1-t\in I
via the method described above. The path
A
starts at
[\gamma]
and ends at
[\eta]
. As will now be verified, the path
A
is in fact Lipschitz. To see this, let
s,t\in I
where
s<t
and consider

d_{\mathcal{P}}(A(s),A(t))=\inf\left\{\ell(\beta)~:~\beta\simeq\overline{(\eta*\overline{\alpha}_{1-s})}*(\eta*\overline{\alpha}_{1-t})\right\}

The path
\alpha|_{[s,t]}
is among the paths
\beta
that the infimum is taken with respect to, as

\displaystyle\overline{(\eta*\overline{\alpha}_{1-s})}*(\eta*\overline{\alpha}_{1-t})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle(\overline{\overline{\alpha}_{1-s}}*\overline{\eta})*(\eta*\overline{\alpha}_{1-t})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\overline{\overline{\alpha}_{1-s}}*\overline{\alpha}_{1-t}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\alpha|_{[s,t]}.

So,

d_{\mathcal{P}}(A(s),A(t))\leq\ell(\alpha|_{[s,t]})\leq\text{Lip}(\alpha)\cdot|s-t|.

Thus,
A
is Lipschitz and
\text{Lip}(A)\leq\text{Lip}(\alpha)
.

Therefore, the length of the path
A
is bounded above by

\ell(A)\leq\text{Lip}(A)\leq\text{Lip}(\alpha)\leq C\cdot d_{\mathcal{P}}([\gamma],[\eta]).

Since
C>1
was arbitrary, the universal path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is a length space.
∎

Theorem 3.11

(Universal Property)
.

Let
(M,p_{0})
be a based metric space such that the map
\pi:(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
satisfies the unique lifting property and the universal path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is a metric space. Let
(E,e_{0})
be a based Lipschitz simply connected quasi-convex space. Suppose that
r:(E,e_{0})\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
is a based Lipschitz map satisfying the unique lifting property.
Then the metric spaces
E
and
\mathcal{P}_{M}
are biLipschitz equivalent with commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(E,e_{0})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(M,p_{0}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

If in addition,
M
is a geodesic space,
E
is a length space,
\Lip(r)=1
, and for any based Lipschitz map
f:(X,x_{0})\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
with length space domain which induces the trivial homomorphism

, the lift
\widehat{f}
of the map

with respect to

satisfies
\Lip(\widehat{f})=\Lip(f)
, then

and
\mathcal{P}_{M}
are isometric.

Proof.

First, some observations about the universal path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
. By Theorem
3.8
, the universal path space is Lipschitz simply connected. Thus, the homomorphism
\pi_{\#}:\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])\rightarrow\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p_{0})
is trivial. Also, by Theorem
3.10
, the universal path space is a length space, and thus quasi-convex.

Since the map
r:(E,e_{0})\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
satisfies the unique lifting property, there is a lift
\widehat{\pi}:(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])\rightarrow(E,e_{0})
of
\pi
with respect to

such that
r\circ\widehat{\pi}=\pi
. Likewise, since
\pi:(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])\rightarrow(M,p_{0})
satisfies the unique lifting property,
there is a lift
\widehat{r}:(E,e_{0})\rightarrow(\mathcal{P}_{M},[p_{0}])
of
r
with respect to
\pi
such that
\pi\circ\widehat{r}=r
. Thus, by the unique lifting property,
\widehat{r}\circ\widehat{\pi}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{P}_{M}}
and
\widehat{\pi}\circ\widehat{r}=\mathbbm{1}_{E}
. So, the Lipschitz maps
\widehat{\pi}
and
\widehat{r}
are bijections and inverses of each other.

Additionally, the maps
\widehat{\pi}
and
\widehat{r}
are biLipschitz. Indeed, let

and denote by

the metric on

. Then,

\displaystyle d_{E}(e_{1},e_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{E}(\widehat{\pi}\circ\widehat{r}(e_{1}),\widehat{\pi}\circ\widehat{r}(e_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Lip(\widehat{\pi})\cdot d_{\mathcal{P}}(\widehat{r}(e_{1}),\widehat{r}(e_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Lip(\widehat{\pi})\cdot\Lip(\widehat{r})\cdot d_{E}(e_{1},e_{2}).

Thus, the map
\widehat{r}
is biLipschitz as

\frac{1}{\Lip(\widehat{\pi})}\cdot d_{E}(e_{1},e_{2})\leq d_{\mathcal{P}}(\widehat{r}(e_{1}),\widehat{r}(e_{2}))\leq\Lip(\widehat{r})\cdot d_{E}(e_{1},e_{2}).

(1)

Similarly, the map
\widehat{\pi}
is biLipschitz as, for

,

\frac{1}{\Lip(\widehat{r})}\cdot d_{\mathcal{P}}([\gamma_{1}],[\gamma_{2}])\leq d_{E}(\widehat{\pi}[\gamma_{1}],\widehat{\pi}[\gamma_{2}])\leq\Lip(\widehat{\pi})\cdot d_{\mathcal{P}}([\gamma_{1}],[\gamma_{2}]).

(2)

Now, assume
M
is a geodesic space,
E
is a length space,
\Lip(r)=1
, and
\Lip(\widehat{f})=\Lip(f)
for any map

with length space domain satisfying the requirements of the unique lifting property with respect to

. Since

is a geodesic space,
\Lip(\pi)=1
by Lemma

. Then,
\Lip(\widehat{\pi})=1
since
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is a length space. Also, since

is a length space, by Theorem
3.5
,
\Lip(\widehat{r})=1
. Thus, inequalities (

) and (

) yield the following equalities for any

and

,

d_{\mathcal{P}}(\widehat{r}(e_{1}),\widehat{r}(e_{2}))=d_{E}(e_{1},e_{2})\text{ and }d_{E}(\widehat{\pi}[\gamma_{1}],\widehat{\pi}[\gamma_{2}])=d_{\mathcal{P}}([\gamma_{1}],[\gamma_{2}]).

Therefore,
\widehat{r}
and
\widehat{\pi}
are isometries.

∎

4
The universal path space of tree-like homotopy spaces

We now define a class of metric spaces whose homotopies are tree-like in that they exhibit properties similar to homotopies that factor through a metric tree.

Definition 4.1
.

A metric space
M
is a
tree-like homotopy space
if, for any paths
\gamma:I\rightarrow M
and
\beta:I\rightarrow M
that are homotopic rel endpoints, there exists a Lipschitz map
H^{\prime}:I\times I\rightarrow M
and a path
\beta^{\prime}:I\rightarrow M
such that:

•

the map
H^{\prime}
is a homotopy from
\gamma
to
\beta^{\prime}
,

•

\Lip(H^{\prime})=\Lip(\gamma)
,

•

\Ima(H^{\prime})\subset\Ima(\gamma)
,

•

\Lip(\beta^{\prime})\leq\Lip(\gamma)
,

•

\Ima(\beta^{\prime})\subset\Ima(\gamma)
, and

•

\ell(\beta^{\prime})\leq\ell(\beta)
.

Additionally, if
\beta
is arc length parametrized and a length minimizing path in the homotopy class
[\gamma]
, then
\beta^{\prime}=\beta
.

As exhibited in Lemma 3.8 and Theorem 3.10 in
[
9
]
, purely 2-unrectifiable spaces are tree-like homotopy spaces. Thus, the first Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{1}
as well as any contact 3-manifold endowed with a sub-Riemannian structure is a tree-like homotopy space. See
[
1
]
and
[
8
]
for arguments for why these spaces are respectively purely 2-unrectifiable. The results in
[
9
]
follow from homotpies factoring through a metric tree per a factorization result of Wenger and Young
[
10
, Theorem 5]
.

4.1
Unique path lifting for the universal path space of a tree-like homotopy space

Tree-like homotopy spaces have several properties that are sufficient for the associated universal path space to be a length space and satisfy the unique path lifting property, namely that each homotopy class has a length minimizing path and all points support only locally trivial representation, as will be shown. Versions of the next two results originally appeared in
[
9
]
for purely 2-unrectifiable spaces.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
M
be a tree-like homotopy space. For any homotopy class
[\gamma]
in
M
, there exists a length minimizing path
{\gamma}_{\infty}\in[\gamma]
. Moreover, for any representative
\gamma\in[\gamma]
in the class,
\Ima({\gamma}_{\infty})\subset\Ima(\gamma)
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a tree-like homotopy space. Let
[\gamma]
be a homotopy class of paths and define
\ell_{\text{min}}:=\inf\{\ell(\gamma)~|~\gamma\in[\gamma]\}
to be the infimum of all lengths of paths in
[\gamma]
.

For each natural number
n
, let
\gamma_{n}\in[\gamma]
be a path such that
\ell(\gamma_{n})\leq\ell_{\text{min}}+\frac{1}{n}
. Furthermore, since
\gamma_{1}
is homotopic rel endpoints to
\gamma_{n}
, by Definition
4.1
, we can assume that
\Lip(\gamma_{n})\leq\Lip(\gamma_{1})
and
\Ima(\gamma_{n})\subset\Ima(\gamma_{1})
. Additionally, there is a homotopy
H_{n}:I\times I\rightarrow M
from
\gamma_{1}
to
\gamma_{n}
such that
\Lip(H_{n})=\Lip(\gamma_{1})
and
\Ima(H_{n})\subset\Ima(\gamma_{1})
.

Now, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Lip(\gamma_{n})\leq\Lip(\gamma_{1})
. Since the images of the paths in the sequence
(\gamma_{n})
are subsets of the compact set
\Ima(\gamma_{1})
, by Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, there exists a subsequence
(\gamma_{n_{k}})
that uniformly converges to a Lipschitz path
{\gamma}_{\infty}
. By lower semi-continuity of the length measure,
\ell({\gamma}_{\infty})\leq\liminf_{k}\ell(\gamma_{n_{k}})
. In fact, due to how the sequence
(\gamma_{n})
was selected,
\ell({\gamma}_{\infty})\leq\ell_{\text{min}}
.

We now want to show that
{\gamma}_{\infty}\in[\gamma]
. Associated to the subsequence
(\gamma_{n_{k}})
, there is a sequence of homotopies
(H_{n_{k}})
such that
\Lip(H_{n_{k}})=\Lip(\gamma_{1})
for each homotopy in the sequence. Since
\Ima(H_{n_{k}})\subset\Ima(\gamma_{1})
for each
n_{k}
and
\Ima(\gamma_{1})
is compact, by Arzelà-Ascoli theorem, there exists a subsequence
(H_{n_{k_{j}}})
that converges uniformly to a Lipschitz map
{H}_{\infty}:I\times I\rightarrow M
.

Now,
{H}_{\infty}|_{I\times\{0\}}=\gamma_{1}
since
H_{n_{k_{j}}}|_{I\times\{0\}}=\gamma_{1}
for all
n_{k_{j}}
. Also, since the paths
H_{n_{k_{j}}}|_{I\times\{1\}}=\gamma_{n_{k_{j}}}
converge uniformly to
{\gamma}_{\infty}
, then
{H}_{\infty}|_{I\times\{1\}}={\gamma}_{\infty}
. So, the map
{H}_{\infty}
is a homotopy from
\gamma_{1}
to
{\gamma}_{\infty}
. Therefore,
{\gamma}_{\infty}\in[\gamma]
and thus
\ell({\gamma}_{\infty})=\ell_{\text{min}}
.

Now, let
\gamma\in[\gamma]
. Assume that the length minimizing path
{\gamma}_{\infty}
is arc length parametrized. Since
\gamma\simeq{\gamma}_{\infty}
, by Definition
4.1
, there is a homotopy from
\gamma
to
{\gamma}_{\infty}
such that
\Ima({\gamma}_{\infty})\subset\Ima(\gamma)
.
∎

A consequence of Theorem
4.2
is that a length minimzing path for a homotopy class is unique up to reparametrization.

Corollary 4.3
.

For a tree-like homotopy space
M
, every point

supports only trivial local representation. Thus, if
M
is a based space, the lifted pseudo-metric

is a metric and the universal path space

Proof.

Let

[\alpha]\in\pi_{1}^{\text{Lip}}(M,p)

. Then, by Theorem

,
[\alpha]
has a length minimizer

whose image is a subset of every neighborhood of

{\alpha}_{\infty}
is the constant loop at
p
and thus
[\alpha]=[p]
.

Now assume for the purpose of defining the universal path space that
M
is based. Since every point in
M
supports only locally trivial representation, by Lemma
2.9
, the lifted pseudo-metric
d_{\mathcal{P}}
is a metric. By Theorem
3.10
, the universal path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is a length space.
∎

\ell\left(\beta_{n}^{j+1}\right)<\frac{L}{2^{n}}+\frac{L}{2^{n}}=\frac{L}{2^{n-1}}.

So, the image of
\beta_{n}^{j+1}
is a subset of the open ball centered at
\gamma\left(\frac{j}{2^{n}}\right)
of radius
\frac{L}{2^{n-1}}
:

\Ima(\beta_{n}^{j+1})\subset B\left(\gamma\left(\frac{j}{2^{n}}\right),\frac{L}{2^{n-1}}\right).

Moreover, the path
\beta_{n}^{j+1}
is homotopic to the reverse of any representative in
\Gamma\left(\frac{j}{2^{n}}\right)
concatenated with any representative of
\Gamma\left(\frac{j+1}{2^{n}}\right)
. Since the path
\beta_{n}^{1,j}
of the inductive hypothesis is a representative of
\Gamma\left(\frac{j}{2^{n}}\right)
, the Lipschitz path
\beta_{n}^{1,j+1}:\left[0,\frac{j+1}{2^{n}}\right]\rightarrow M
piecewise defined by

\beta_{n}^{1,j+1}(t):=\begin{cases}\beta_{n}^{1,j}(t)&\text{ if }t\in\left[0,\frac{j}{2^{n}}\right]\\
\\
\beta_{n}^{j+1}(t)&\text{ if }t\in I_{n}^{j+1}\end{cases}

is a representative of
\Gamma\left(\frac{j+1}{2^{n}}\right)
.

Thus, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, there exists a representative
\beta_{n}:=\beta_{n}^{1,{2^{n}}}\in\Gamma(1)

such that for
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,2^{n}\}
,

\beta_{n}\left|{}_{\left[0,\frac{j}{2^{n}}\right]}\right.=\beta_{n}^{1,j}\in\Gamma\left(\frac{j}{2^{n}}\right).

(3)

Also, the path
\beta_{n}
when restricted to the subinterval
I_{n}^{j}
is equal to the path

\beta_{n}\left|{}_{I_{n}^{j}}\right.=\beta_{n}^{j}

(4)

where the path
\beta_{n}^{j}:I_{n}^{j}\rightarrow M
has initial point
\beta_{n}^{j}\left(\frac{j-1}{2^{n}}\right)=\gamma\left(\frac{j-1}{2^{n}}\right)
, endpoint

, and bounded length

(5)

Additionally, the image of the restriction
\beta_{n}\left|{}_{I_{n}^{j}}\right.
is within
\frac{L}{2^{n-1}}
of the initial point,

\Ima\left(\beta_{n}^{j}\right)=\Ima\left(\beta_{n}\left|{}_{I_{n}^{j}}\right.\right)\subset B\left(\gamma\left(\frac{j-1}{2^{n}}\right),\frac{L}{2^{n-1}}\right).

(6)

Moreover, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and each
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,2^{n}\}
, via Theorem
4.2
, take the path
\beta_{n}^{j}
to be the arc length parametrized length minimizing path of the homotopy class
[\beta_{n}^{j}]
. Thus, by construction, the path
\beta_{0}
is the length minimizing path in the class
\Gamma(1)
.

As is now shown, the sequence of paths
(\beta_{n})
has a uniform bound on the associated Lipschitz constants.

Lemma 4.5
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\Lip(\beta_{n})<2L

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. For
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,2^{n}\}
, since the path
\beta_{n}^{j}
is arc length parametrized and has length less than
\frac{L}{2^{n-1}}

\Lip(\beta_{n}^{j})=\frac{\ell(\beta_{n}^{j})}{\frac{j}{2^{n}}-\frac{j-1}{2^{n}}}=2^{n}\ell(\beta_{n}^{j})<2^{n}\cdot\frac{L}{2^{n-1}}=2L.

Now,
\beta_{n}\left|{}_{I_{n}^{j}}\right.=\beta_{n}^{j}
for each

4
). Thus, the Lipschitz constant of the path
\beta_{n}
is bounded above by
2L
as well. ∎

As will be argued, the sequence of paths

. First note that, by Lemma

Lemma 4.6
.

\beta_{n}\longrightarrow\gamma

n\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

Let
t\in I

n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, there exists

t\in I_{n}^{j}=\left[\frac{j-1}{2^{n}},\frac{j}{2^{n}}\right]
. By (

\beta_{n}(t)\in B\left(\gamma\left(\frac{j-1}{2^{n}}\right),\frac{L}{2^{n-1}}\right)
. Thus,

\displaystyle d(\beta_{n}(t),\gamma(t))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d\left(\beta_{n}(t),\gamma\left(\frac{j-1}{2^{n}}\right)\right)+d\left(\gamma\left(\frac{j-1}{2^{n}}\right),\gamma(t)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{L}{2^{n-1}}\right)+L_{\gamma}\left|\frac{j-1}{2^{n}}-t\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{L}{2^{n-1}}\right)+L_{\gamma}\left(\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{L}{2^{n-1}}\right)+L\left(\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3L}{2^{n}}.

Therefore,
\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\beta_{n}(t)=\gamma(t)
.

We finish with a proof of the primary result of this paper. Since purely 2-unrectifiable spaces are tree-like homotopy spaces, Theorem
1.1
follows immediately from Corollary

.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
M
be a based tree-like homotopy space. Then endpoint projection
\pi:\mathcal{P}_{M}\rightarrow M
satisfies the unique lifting property and the universal path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is a Lipschitz simply connected length space.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.3
, the universal path space
\mathcal{P}_{M}
is a length space. By Theorem
4.4
, for a tree-like homotopy space
M
, the Lipschitz map
\pi:\mathcal{P}_{M}\rightarrow M
satisfies the unique path lifting property. Thus, by Theorem
3.5
, the map
\pi
satisfies the unique lifting property. Therefore, by Theorem
3.8
, the universal path space is Lipschitz simply connected.
∎

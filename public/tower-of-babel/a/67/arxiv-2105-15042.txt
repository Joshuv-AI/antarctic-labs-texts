Sucheol Lee, Donghwan Kim

Abstract
Minimax problems, such as generative adversarial network, adversarial training, and fair training, are widely solved by a multi-step gradient descent ascent (MGDA) method in practice. However, its convergence guarantee is limited. In this paper, inspired by the primal-dual hybrid gradient method, we propose a new semi-anchoring (SA) technique for the MGDA method. This makes the MGDA method find a stationary point of a structured nonconvex-nonconcave composite minimax problem; its saddle-subdifferential operator satisfies the weak Minty variational inequality condition. The resulting method, named SA-MGDA, is built upon a Bregman proximal point method. We further develop its backtracking line-search version, and its non-Euclidean version for smooth adaptable functions. Numerical experiments, including a fair classification training, are provided.



Key words.

minimax problem, nonconvex-nonconcave problem, multi-step gradient descent ascent method,
Bregman proximal point method

Preliminaries

3.1
Bregman distance

This paper uses a Legendre function
[
73
]

and its associated Bregman distance
[
13
]
,
defined below,
as a non-Euclidean proximity measure

[
26
,
80
]
.
These help us to better handle the nonlinear geometry of a problem.

Definition 3.1
.

[
73
]

A function
h\;:\;\mathbbm{R}^{d}\to(-\infty,\infty]
which is closed proper strictly convex and
essentially smooth
2
2

2

A closed convex proper function
h
is called essentially smooth
if it satisfies the following three conditions:
(a)
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}h
is nonempty;
(b)
h
is differentiable throughout
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}h
;
(c)
\lim_{k\to\infty}||\nabla h(\bm{x}_{k})||=+\infty

whenever
\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\ldots
, is a sequence in
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}h

converging to a boundary point
\bm{x}
of
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}h

[
73
]
.

will be called a Legendre function.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
h\;:\;\mathbbm{R}^{d}\to(-\infty,\infty]
be a Legendre function.
The Bregman distance associated to
h
, denoted by
D_{h}\;:\;\operatorname{dom}h\times\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}h\to\mathbbm{R}_{+}

is defined by

\displaystyle D_{h}(\bm{x},\bm{y}):=h(\bm{x})-h(\bm{y})-\mathop{\langle\nabla h(\bm{y}),\,\bm{x}-\bm{y}\rangle}\nolimits.

A Bregman distance
D_{h}
reduces to the Euclidean distance
for
h(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{2}||\bm{x}||^{2}
.

D_{h}
is not symmetric in general, except for
the case
h(\bm{x})=\frac{1}{2}||\bm{x}||^{2}
.
In addition,
D_{h}(\bm{x},\bm{y})\geq 0
for all
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\operatorname{dom}h\times\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}h
,
and
D_{h}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=0
if and only if
\bm{x}=\bm{y}
due to the strict convexity of
h
.
Popular examples of
h
are

h(\bm{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{p}|x_{i}|^{p}
for
p\geq 2
,
a Shannon entropy
h(\bm{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}\mathop{{}{\operator@font log}}\nolimits x_{i}
,
\operatorname{dom}h=[0,\infty)^{d}
,
and a Burg entropy
h(\bm{x})=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathop{{}{\operator@font log}}\nolimits x_{i}
,
\operatorname{dom}h=(0,\infty)^{d}
.

An appropriate choice of
h
from the above (partial) list,
especially that captures the geometry of the constraint sets of the problem,
has been found useful in many applications
(see,
e.g.
,
[
3
,
9
,
11
,
49
]
).
This paper, however, chooses
h

not from the above standard list.
Our choice of
h
in Section
5

is inspired by that of the PDHG

[
15
,
28
,
39
]
.

3.2
Composite minimax problem and weakly monotone operator

We are interested in the minimax problem in a form:

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{u}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{u}}}\max_{\bm{v}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{v}}}\left\{\Phi(\bm{u},\bm{v}):=f(\bm{u})+\phi(\bm{u},\bm{v})-g(\bm{v})\right\},

(4)

which satisfies the following assumption. Let
d:=d_{u}+d_{v}
.

Assumption 1
.

f\;:\;\mathbbm{R}^{d_{u}}\to(-\infty,\infty]

and
g\;:\;\mathbbm{R}^{d_{v}}\to(-\infty,\infty]

are closed, proper and convex functions.
A function
\phi\;:\;\mathbbm{R}^{d}\to\mathbbm{R}
is continuously differentiable and
has a Lipschitz continuous gradient,
i.e.
,
there exists
L_{\bm{u}\bm{u}},L_{\bm{u}\bm{v}},L_{\bm{v}\bm{u}},L_{\bm{v}\bm{v}}>0
such that,
for all
\bm{u},\bar{\bm{u}}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{u}}
and
\bm{v},\bar{\bm{v}}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{v}}
,

\displaystyle||\nabla_{\bm{u}}\phi(\bm{u},\bm{v})-\nabla_{\bm{u}}\phi(\bar{\bm{u}},\bar{\bm{v}})||

\displaystyle\leq L_{\bm{u}\bm{u}}||\bm{u}-\bar{\bm{u}}||+L_{\bm{u}\bm{v}}||\bm{v}-\bar{\bm{v}}||,

\displaystyle||\nabla_{\bm{v}}\phi(\bm{u},\bm{v})-\nabla_{\bm{v}}\phi(\bar{\bm{u}},\bar{\bm{v}})||

\displaystyle\leq L_{\bm{v}\bm{u}}||\bm{u}-\bar{\bm{u}}||+L_{\bm{v}\bm{v}}||\bm{v}-\bar{\bm{v}}||.

Both the gradient
\nabla\phi

and the saddle-differential of
\phi
, denoted by

\bm{M}_{\phi}:=(\nabla_{\bm{u}}\phi,-\nabla_{\bm{v}}\phi)
,
are
L
-Lipschitz continuous
(see Appendix
A
),
i.e.
, there exists
L>0
such that

||\bm{M}_{\phi}\bm{x}-\bm{M}_{\phi}\bm{y}||\leq L||\bm{x}-\bm{y}||

for all

\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d}
.

Finding a first-order stationary point
\bm{x}_{*}:=(\bm{u}_{*},\bm{v}_{*})\in\mathbbm{R}^{d}
of (
4
),
is equivalent to finding a zero of the following set-valued saddle-subdifferential operator
of
\Phi
:

\displaystyle\bm{M}:=(\nabla_{\bm{u}}\phi+\partial f,-\nabla_{\bm{v}}\phi+\partial g)\;:\;\mathbbm{R}^{d}\rightrightarrows(-\infty,\infty]^{d}.

(5)

Let
X_{*}(\bm{M}):=\{\bm{x}_{*}\;:\;\bm{0}\in\bm{M}\bm{x}_{*}\}
be a nonempty solution set.
We consider
the squared subgradient norm at
\bm{x}
,
\min_{\bm{s}\in\bm{M}\bm{x}}||\bm{s}||^{2}
, as an optimality criteria, which is standard in nonconvex-nonconcave minimax problems
[
25
,
47
,
70
]
.

Under Assumption
1
, the operator
\bm{M}
(
5
) satisfies
the following weakly monotone condition with

\gamma=\max\{L_{\bm{u}\bm{u}},L_{\bm{v}\bm{v}}\}

(see Appendix
B
) and is maximal.

Assumption 2
.

For some
\gamma\geq 0
,
an operator
\bm{M}
is
\gamma
-weakly monotone,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\mathop{\langle\bm{x}-\bm{y},\,\bm{w}-\bm{z}\rangle}\nolimits\geq-\gamma||\bm{x}-\bm{y}||^{2},\quad\forall(\bm{x},\bm{w}),(\bm{y},\bm{z})\in\operatorname{gra}\bm{M},

where
\operatorname{gra}\bm{M}:=\{(\bm{x},\bm{w})\in\mathbbm{R}^{d}\times\mathbbm{R}^{d}\;:\;\bm{w}\in\bm{M}\bm{x}\}
denotes the graph of
\bm{M}
.
Also, it is maximal,
i.e.
,
there exists no
\gamma
-weakly monotone operator
that its graph
properly contains
\operatorname{gra}\bm{M}
.

3.3
Structured nonconvex-nonconcave problem

This section considers
the structured nonconvex-nonconcave conditions;
the weak Minty variational inequality (MVI) condition in
[
25
]

and the strong MVI condition in
[
79
,
88
]
.

The MVI problem
is to find
\bm{x}_{*}
such that

\displaystyle\mathop{\langle\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{*},\,\bm{w}\rangle}\nolimits\geq 0,\quad\forall(\bm{x},\bm{w})\in\operatorname{gra}\bm{M}.

For a continuous
\bm{M}
, a solution set of the MVI problem is
a subset of
X_{*}(\bm{M})
,
and if
\bm{M}
is monotone, they are equivalent.
The MVI condition, assuming that
a solution of the MVI problem exists,
is studied in
[
20
,
50
]
,
which is also studied under the name, the coherence, in

[
53
,
79
,
88
]
.
Recently,
[
25
]

introduced the following weaker condition, named weak MVI condition.

Assumption 3

(Weak MVI)
.

For some
\rho\geq 0
, there exists a solution
\bm{x}_{*}\in X_{*}(\bm{M})
such that

\displaystyle\mathop{\langle\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{*},\,\bm{w}\rangle}\nolimits\geq-\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{w}||^{2},\quad\forall(\bm{x},\bm{w})\in\operatorname{gra}\bm{M}.

Let
X_{*}^{\rho}(\bm{M})
be the associated solution set.
Assumption
3

is implied by the
-\frac{\rho}{2}
-comonotononicity
[
6
]
,
or equivalently the
\frac{\rho}{2}
-cohypomonotonicity
[
19
]
,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\mathop{\langle\bm{x}-\bm{y},\,\bm{w}-\bm{z}\rangle}\nolimits\geq-\frac{\rho}{2}||\bm{w}-\bm{z}||^{2},\quad\forall(\bm{x},\bm{w}),(\bm{y},\bm{z})\in\operatorname{gra}\bm{M}.

The comonotonicity is also implied by the
\alpha\geq 0
-interaction dominant condition
in
[
35
]

(see
[
47
, Example 1]
).

[
22
, Proposition 2]
[
25
]

consider a (constrained)
two-agent zero-sum reinforcement learning problem,
called the von Neumann ratio game, that satisfies
the weak MVI condition, but neither the MVI condition nor the comonotonicity condition.
[
70
]

also provides examples satisfying the weak MVI condition.

We also consider the strong MVI condition in
[
79
,
88
]
.
This condition
is also non-monotone,
and includes
the
\mu
-strong pseudomonotonicity
[
67
]

(see
[
67
]
for examples).
Let
S_{*}^{\mu}(\bm{M})
be the associated solution set.

Assumption 4

(Strong MVI)
.

For some
\mu\geq 0
, there exists a solution
\bm{x}_{*}\in X_{*}(\bm{M})
such that

\displaystyle\mathop{\langle\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{*},\,\bm{w}\rangle}\nolimits\geq\mu||\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{*}||^{2},\quad\forall(\bm{x},\bm{w})\in\operatorname{gra}\bm{M}.

Bregman proximal point (BPP) method

4.1
The
h
-resolvent

The
h
-resolvent of a monotone operator
\bm{M}
with respect to a Legendre function
h
is defined as
[
26
]

\displaystyle\bm{R}_{\bm{M}}^{h}:=(\nabla h+\bm{M})^{-1}\nabla h,

where we omit
\bm{M}
and
h

\bm{R}_{\bm{M}}^{h}

for simplicity hereafter, unless necessary.
This reduces to the standard resolvent operator
(\bm{I}+\bm{M})^{-1}
for

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\bm{M}
satisfy Assumption
2
for some
\gamma\geq 0
,
and
h
be a
\mu_{h}
-strongly convex Legendre function.
Then, if
\mu_{h}>\gamma

-resolvent
\bm{R}
is single-valued on
\operatorname{int}\operatorname{dom}h
.

Proof.

Note that
\nabla h+\bm{M}=(\nabla h-\gamma\bm{I})+(\bm{M}+\gamma\bm{I})

\mu_{h}>\gamma

\bm{M}

\gamma

This implies that

is nonempty for all

. Assume that

Since

\displaystyle-\gamma\|\bm{y}-\bm{z}\|^{2}\leq-\mathop{\langle\nabla h(\bm{y})-\nabla h(\bm{z}),\,\bm{y}-\bm{z}\rangle}\nolimits\leq-\mu_{h}\|\bm{y}-\bm{z}\|^{2}.

So if
\mu_{h}>\gamma
,
the inequality implies that

.
∎

4.2

The BPP method

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{k+1}=\bm{R}(\bm{x}_{k}),

We analyze the worst-case convergence behavior of the BPP method
under Assumptions

and
3
(or

We first state the Bregman nonexpansive property of

below.
For

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\bm{M}
satisfy Assumption

,

-smooth Legendre function.

\displaystyle D_{h}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{R}\bm{x})\leq D_{h}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x})-(1-\rho L_{h})D_{h}(\bm{R}\bm{x},\bm{x}).

Proof.

By the definition of
\bm{R}\bm{x}
, we have
\nabla h(\bm{x})-\nabla h(\bm{R}\bm{x})\in\bm{M}\bm{R}\bm{x}

Then, Assumption
3

\bm{M}
implies that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\mathop{\langle\nabla h(\bm{x})-\nabla h(\bm{R}\bm{x}),\,\bm{R}\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{*}\rangle}\nolimits+\frac{\rho}{2}||\nabla h(\bm{x})-\nabla h(\bm{R}\bm{x})||^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\langle\nabla h(\bm{x}),\,\bm{R}\bm{x}-\bm{x}\rangle}\nolimits-\mathop{\langle\nabla h(\bm{x}),\,\bm{x}_{*}-\bm{x}\rangle}\nolimits+\mathop{\langle\nabla h(\bm{R}\bm{x}),\,\bm{x}_{*}-\bm{R}\bm{x}\rangle}\nolimits

\displaystyle\leq-D_{h}(\bm{R}\bm{x},\bm{x})+D_{h}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x})-D_{h}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{R}\bm{x})+\rho L_{h}D_{h}(\bm{R}\bm{x},\bm{x}),

where the last inequality follows from the convex and

[

∎

Theorem 4.3

By Lemma

By Lemma

Then

4.3
Example: primal-dual hybrid gradient method

This section considers the following bilinearly-coupled convex-concave minimax problems

\displaystyle\min_{\bm{u}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{u}}}\max_{\bm{v}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{v}}}\left\{f(\bm{u})+\mathop{\langle\bm{u},\,\bm{B}\bm{v}\rangle}\nolimits-g(\bm{v})\right\},

(8)

which is an instance of (
4

The following choice of
h
with
\tau\in\big(0,\frac{1}{||\bm{B}||}\big)

\displaystyle h(\bm{u},\bm{v})=\frac{1}{2\tau}\left(||\bm{u}||^{2}+||\bm{v}||^{2}\right)-\mathop{\langle\bm{u},\,\bm{B}\bm{v}\rangle}\nolimits

\displaystyle\nabla h(\bm{u},\bm{v})=\bm{P}(\bm{u},\bm{v}):=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{\tau}\bm{I}&-\bm{B}\\

5.4
Comparison to proximal point method

Lemma
4.1
and

and

also apply
to the standard proximal point method
(with

i.e.

Such method, however, requires solving a regularized

problem,

\displaystyle(\bm{u}_{k+1},\bm{v}_{k+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\mathop{\operator@font arg\,min}_{\bm{u}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{u}}}\max_{\bm{v}\in\mathbbm{R}^{d_{v}}}\left\{f(\bm{u})+\phi(\bm{u},\bm{v})-g(\bm{v})+\frac{1}{2\tau}||\bm{u}-\bm{u}_{k}||^{2}-\frac{1}{2\tau}||\bm{v}-\bm{v}_{k}||^{2}\right\},

Conclusion

This paper proposed a semi-anchoring approach
to a multi-step gradient descent ascent method
for structured nonconvex-nonconcave smooth minimax problems.
This is a new instance of the Bregman proximal point method
for operators under the weak MVI condition.
We showed that the proposed method guarantees convergence
without regularization and its parameter tuning,
unlike the smoothing technique.
We further studied its backtracking line-search version,
and its non-Euclidean version for smooth adaptable functions.
Numerical experiments suggest that the proposed approach has potential to improve
training dynamics of real-world minimax problems.
We leave extending this work
to a more general stochastic nonconvex-nonconcave setting as future work.

Lemma D.1

\displaystyle D_{h}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{i-1})-D_{h}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{i})

\displaystyle+(D_{h}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{i})-D_{h}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{i}^{*}))-(1-\rho L_{h})D_{h}(\bm{x}_{i}^{*},\bm{x}_{i-1}).

Proof of Theorem
6.1

We first show that
\tau_{k}
is lower bounded by
\frac{\delta}{L+\hat{L}}
for all
k\geq 0
. Suppose that
\tau_{k}\geq\frac{\delta}{L+\hat{L}}
and

\tau_{k+1}:=\delta^{i_{k}}\tau_{k}<\frac{\delta}{L+\hat{L}}
for some
k\geq 0
.
Let
\tau_{k+1}^{\prime}:=\delta^{i_{k}-1}\tau_{k}=\frac{\tau_{k+1}}{\delta}<\frac{1}{L+\hat{L}}

and define a function
h_{k+1}^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{2\tau_{k+1}^{\prime}}\|\cdot\|^{2}-\phi
,
which is convex and
\Big(\frac{1}{\tau_{k+1}^{\prime}}+L\Big)
-smooth.
Since
\frac{1}{\tau_{k+1}^{\prime}}-L>\hat{L}
,

\bm{x}_{k+1}^{\prime}:=\bm{R}_{\bm{M}}^{h_{k+1}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}_{k})
exists, by Lemma
4.1
.
In addition,
since
\frac{1}{\tau_{k+1}^{\prime}}+L<\frac{2}{\tau_{k+1}^{\prime}}
, the inequality

\frac{\tau_{k+1}^{\prime}}{4}||\nabla h_{k+1}^{\prime}(\bm{x}_{k+1}^{\prime})-\nabla h_{k+1}^{\prime}(\bm{x}_{k})||^{2}\leq D_{h_{k+1}^{\prime}}(\bm{x}_{k+1}^{\prime},\bm{x}_{k})
holds.
This contradicts to the definition of
i_{k}
. Therefore,
\tau_{k+1}\geq\frac{\delta}{L+\hat{L}}
for all
k\geq 0
.

Let
\tilde{L}:=\frac{2(L+\hat{L})}{\delta}
.
By the definition of
\bm{x}_{i+1}
and the proof of Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq-D_{h_{i+1}}(\bm{x}_{i+1},\bm{x}_{i})+D_{h_{i+1}}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{i})-D_{h_{i+1}}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{i+1})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\rho}{2}||\nabla h_{i+1}(\bm{x}_{i})-\nabla h_{i+1}(\bm{x}_{i+1})||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq D_{h_{i+1}}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{i})-D_{h_{i+1}}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{i+1})-(1-\rho\tilde{L})D_{h_{i+1}}(\bm{x}_{i+1},\bm{x}_{i})

for all
i\geq 0
.

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{i=1}^{k}(1-\rho\tilde{L})D_{h_{i}}(\bm{x}_{i},\bm{x}_{i-1})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(D_{h_{i}}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{i-1})-D_{h_{i}}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=D_{h_{1}}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{0})-D_{h_{k}}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{k})+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\left(D_{h_{i+1}}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{i})-D_{h_{i}}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{i})\right)

\displaystyle\leq D_{h_{1}}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{0})+D_{\phi}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{k})+\frac{\tilde{L}}{4}C^{2}

\displaystyle\leq D_{h_{1}}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{0})+\frac{L}{2}C^{2}+\frac{\tilde{L}}{4}C^{2}

\displaystyle\leq D_{h_{1}}(\bm{x}_{*},\bm{x}_{0})+\frac{\tilde{L}}{2}C^{2},

which uses

.

follows under

.

exists.

Deformation quantization and intrinsic noncommutative differential geometry
Haoyuan Gao 3,1,2, Xiao Zhang 1,2,4

Abstract
. We provide an intrinsic formulation of the noncommutative differential geometry developed earlier by Chaichian, Tureanu, R. B. Zhang and the second author. This yields geometric definitions of covariant derivatives of noncommutative metrics and curvatures, as well as the noncommutative version of the first and the second Bianchi identities. Moreover, if a noncommutative metric and chiral coefficients satisfy certain conditions which hold automatically for quantum fluctuations given by isometric embedding, we prove that the two noncommutative Ricci curvatures are essentially equivalent. For (pseudo-) Riemannian metrics given by certain type of spherically symmetric isometric embedding, we compute their quantum fluctuations and curvatures. We find that they have closed forms, which indicates that the quantization of gravity is renormalizable in this case. Finally, we define quasi-connections and their curvatures with respect to general associative star products constructed by Kontsevich on Poisson manifolds. As these star products are not compatible with the Leibniz rule, we can only prove the first Bianchi identity.



1.
Introduction

Gravity is essentially a theory of spacetime geometry. In the concept of quantum effects of gravity, the Heisenberg uncertainty relations would result in noncommutativity of spacetime variables for sufficiently small distances. In 1947, Snyder, C.N. Yang made the first attempts to quantize spacetimes
[
20
,
24
]
, which are referred as Snyder’s quantum space-times and Yang’s quantum phase spaces
[
14
,
15
]
. In their approach, spacetime variables were represented by Hermitian operators with discrete eigenvalues. This idea to encode geometry of a space by its algebras of functions was realized prominently by Connes to establish noncommutative geometry using spectral triples
[
8
]
, where the main ingredients are the noncommutative analog of the Dirac operator acting on a representation space of the algebra, the spectrum of this generalized Dirac operator and the cyclic (co)homology. They are used to encode the information of noncommutative manifold structure, noncommutative metric and noncommutative curvature respectively. The overview of its applications to physics can be found in
[
7
]
.

However, the metric and curvature information in an infinitesimal neighborhood of manifold is still lack as it is not known what means to take derivatives when coordinate variables are operators. Alternatively, deformation quantization deforms the commutative algebras of functions based on pointwise commutative multiplication to noncommutative algebras of functions based on certain noncommutative products such as the Moyal product, but still keeps spacetime variables usual functions, c.f.
[
4
,
5
]
. In recent years, there have been intensive research activities on noncommutative gravity in frame of deformation quantization, c.f.
[
19
,
1
,
2
]
and references therein, where general relativity is adopted to the noncommutative setting in an intuitive way, as pointed out in
[
21
]
.

In
[
6
,
22
,
23
,
25
]
, a mathematically rigorous and complete theory of noncommutative differential geometry was developed on a coordinate chart
U
of a (pseudo-) Riemannian manifold. The idea is to embed
U
isometrically into a flat (pseudo-) Euclidean space and use the isometric embedding to construct the noncommutative analogues of metric, connection and curvature. They yield the noncommutative Einstein field equations. It was found that the deformation quantization of the Schwarzschild metric does not depend on time and yields an unevaporated quantum black hole
[
22
]
, and the quantum fluctuation of the plane-fronted gravitational wave is the exact solution of the noncommutative vacuum Einstein field equations
[
23
]
. We refer to
[
17
,
18
]
for the review on general existence of isometric embedding and applications in physics.

In this paper, we provide the intrinsic theory of noncommutative differential geometry via deformation quantization, without using the isometric embedding. In Section 2, we review basic knowledge on noncommutative metrics, connections, curvatures and state the main theorem. In Section 3, we study the intrinsic formulation of covariant derivatives of noncommutative metrics and curvatures from the geometric point of view. This yields noncommutative version of the first and the second Bianchi identities. In Section 4, we show the two noncommutative Ricci curvatures are essentially equivalent if noncommutative metrics and chiral coefficients satisfy certain conditions. These conditions hold automatically for quantum fluctuations given by isometric embedding. In Section 5, we show that the quantum fluctuations and their curvatures have closed forms coming from Moyal products of trigonometric functions if (pseudo-) Riemannian metrics are given by certain type of spherically symmetric isometric embedding. In Section 6, we define quasi-connections and their curvatures with respect to general associative star products and prove the first Bianchi identity.

Throughout the paper, all the indices

,

\displaystyle(u*v)(x)=\Big[\exp(\hbar\theta^{ij}\partial_{i}\partial^{\prime}_{j})u(x)v(x^{\prime})\Big]_{x=x^{\prime}},

where

Extending by

Extend

(i)

Noncommutativity:
[x^{i},x^{j}]=x^{i}*x^{j}-x^{j}*x^{i}=2\hbar\theta^{ij}

(ii)

Leibniz rule:
\partial_{i}(u*v)=(\partial_{i}{u})*v+u*(\partial_{i}{v})
, for

\displaystyle E_{i}=\tilde{E}_{i}=\partial_{i},\hskip 10.00002pt1\leq i\leq n.

Definition 2.1
.

The noncommutative left (

\mathcal{T}_{U}
(
resp.

) on

\{E_{1},\cdots,E_{n}\}
(

\{\tilde{E}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{E}_{n}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{U}

\displaystyle=\Big\{a^{i}*E_{i}\,\Big|\,a^{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{U},\,a^{i}*E_{i}=0\Longleftrightarrow a^{i}=0.\Big\},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\tilde{E}_{i}*\tilde{a}^{i}\,\Big|\,\tilde{a}^{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{U},\,\tilde{E}_{i}*\tilde{a}^{i}=0\Longleftrightarrow\tilde{a}^{i}=0.\Big\}.

An element of
\mathcal{T}_{U}
(
resp.

\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}
) is called a left (
resp.

Definition 2.2
.

A noncommutative metric
g
on
U
is a homomorphism of two-sided
\mathcal{A}_{U}
-modules

\displaystyle g:\mathcal{T}_{U}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}[[\hbar]]}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{U},

such that the matrix

\displaystyle(g_{ij})\in\mathcal{A}_{U}^{n\times n},\hskip 10.00002ptg_{ij}=g(E_{i},\tilde{E}_{j})

is invertible, i.e., there exists a unique matrix
(g^{ij})\in\mathcal{A}_{U}^{n\times n}
such that

\displaystyle g_{ik}*g^{kj}=g^{jk}*g_{ki}=\delta_{i}^{j}.

Let
(g_{l}^{ij})
be the left inverse of
(g_{ij})
and
(g_{r}^{ij})
be the right inverse of
(g_{ij})
. Since the Moyal product is associative,

\displaystyle g_{l}^{ij}=g_{l}^{ip}*\delta_{p}^{j}=g_{l}^{ip}*g_{pk}*g_{r}^{kj}=\delta_{k}^{i}*g_{r}^{kj}=g_{r}^{ij}.

Therefore the left inverse and the right inverse coincide.

In classical differential geometry, the cotangent bundle is the dual of the tangent bundle. Inspired by this, we can define the noncommutative cotangent bundles as the dual modules of the noncommutative tangent bundles. As the dual of a left (resp. right)
\mathcal{A}_{U}
-module is a right (resp. left)
\mathcal{A}_{U}
-module and the dual of a free module is also free, we may use the noncommutative metric
g
to induce bases of the cotangent bundles dual to
E_{i}
and
\tilde{E}_{j}
respectively, i.e., let
E^{i}
,
\tilde{E}^{j}
be dual bases of
\tilde{E}_{j}
,
E_{i}
respectively, we have

\displaystyle g(E^{i},\tilde{E}_{j})=g(E_{j},\tilde{E}^{i})=\delta_{j}^{i}.

Definition 2.3
.

The noncommutative left (
resp.
right) cotangent bundle
\mathcal{T}_{U}^{*}
(
resp.

\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}^{*}
) on
U
with respect to the noncommutative metric

is the free left (
resp.

\mathcal{A}_{U}
-module with basis
\{E^{1},\cdots,E^{n}\}
(

\{\tilde{E}^{1},\cdots,\tilde{E}^{n}\}
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{U}^{*}

\displaystyle=\Big\{a_{i}*E^{i}\,\Big|\,a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{U},\,a_{i}*E^{i}=0\Longleftrightarrow a_{i}=0\Big\},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}^{*}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\tilde{E}^{i}*\tilde{a}_{i}\,\Big|\,\tilde{a}_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{U},\,\tilde{E}^{i}*\tilde{a}_{i}=0\Longleftrightarrow\tilde{a}_{i}=0\Big\}.

The left (resp. right) cotangent bundle is the dual of the right (resp. left) tangent bundle. Analogous to the classical situation, the noncommutative metric
g
acts as an element of
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}^{*}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{U}}\mathcal{T}_{U}^{*}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{E}^{i}\otimes g_{ij}*E^{j}=\tilde{E}^{i}*g_{ij}\otimes E^{j}.

(2.2)

The inverse matrix

can be viewed as a homomorphism of two-sided modules

\displaystyle g^{-1}:\mathcal{T}_{U}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}[[\hbar]]}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}^{*}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{U}

\displaystyle g^{-1}(E^{i},\tilde{E}^{j})=g^{ij}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{i}\otimes g^{ij}*E_{j}=\tilde{E}_{i}*g^{ij}\otimes E_{j}.

Definition 2.4
.

A noncommutative left (
resp.
right) connection
\nabla
is a map

\displaystyle\nabla:\mathcal{T}_{U}\longrightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{*}_{U}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{U}}\mathcal{T}_{U}\hskip 10.00002pt({\rm{resp.}}\,\,\tilde{\nabla}:\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}\longrightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{U}}\mathcal{T}^{*}_{U})

such that noncommutative left (
resp.
right) covariant derivatives

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}:\mathcal{T}_{U}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{U},\hskip 10.00002pt({\rm{resp.}}\,\,\tilde{\nabla}_{i}:\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}\longrightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U});

defined by

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}V=g(E_{i},\tilde{E}^{k})*W_{k}\hskip 10.00002pt({\rm{resp.}}\,\,\tilde{\nabla}_{i}\tilde{V}=\tilde{W}_{k}*g(E^{k},\tilde{E}_{i}))

for any

\displaystyle\nabla V=\tilde{E}^{k}\otimes W_{k},\,\,W_{k}\in\mathcal{T}_{U}\hskip 10.00002pt({\rm{resp.}}\,\,\tilde{\nabla}\tilde{V}=\tilde{W}_{k}\otimes E^{k},\,\,\tilde{W}_{k}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U})

satisfy

(i)

\mathbb{R}[[\hbar]]
-linearity: For
a,\,b\in\mathbb{R}[[\hbar]]
,
V,\,W\in\mathcal{T}_{U}
(
resp.

\tilde{V},\,\tilde{W}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}
),

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}(aV+bW)

\displaystyle=a\nabla_{i}V+b\nabla_{i}W,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{i}(a\tilde{V}+b\tilde{W})

\displaystyle=a\tilde{\nabla}_{i}\tilde{V}+b\tilde{\nabla}_{i}\tilde{W};

(ii)

Leibniz rule: For
f\in\mathcal{A}_{U}
,
V\in\mathcal{T}_{U}
(
resp.

\tilde{V}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}
),

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}(f*V)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{i}f)*V+f*\nabla_{i}V,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{i}(\tilde{V}*f)

\displaystyle=\tilde{V}*(\partial_{i}f)+\tilde{\nabla}_{i}\tilde{V}*f.

Remark 2.1
.

The noncommutative left (
resp.
right) covariant derivative along a left (
resp.
right) vector field
V=a^{i}*E_{i}
(
resp.

\tilde{V}=\tilde{E}_{i}*\tilde{a}^{i}
) with
a^{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{U}
(
resp.
\tilde{a}^{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{U}
) is defined as the
\mathbb{R}[[\hbar]]
-linear map

\displaystyle\nabla_{V}:\mathcal{T}_{U}\to\mathcal{T}_{U}\hskip 10.00002pt({\rm{resp.}}\hskip 10.00002pt\it{\tilde{\nabla}_{\tilde{V}}:\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}\to\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}})

given by

\displaystyle\nabla_{V}X=a^{i}*(\nabla_{i}X)\hskip 10.00002pt({\rm{resp.}}\hskip 10.00002pt\it{\tilde{\nabla}_{\tilde{V}}\tilde{X}=(\tilde{\nabla}_{i}\tilde{X})*\tilde{a}^{i}})

for
X\in\mathcal{T}_{U}
(resp.
\tilde{X}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}
). But they are not compatible with the Leibniz rule. Indeed, let
X=b^{j}*E_{j}
,
b^{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{U}
. By the Leibniz rule,

\displaystyle\nabla_{V}(b^{j}*E_{j})=

\displaystyle(Vb^{j})*E_{j}+b^{j}*\nabla_{V}E_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{i}*\partial_{i}b^{j}*E_{j}+b^{j}*a^{i}*\nabla_{i}E_{j}.

On the other hand, by the definition,

\displaystyle\nabla_{V}(b^{j}*E_{j})=

\displaystyle a^{i}*\nabla_{i}(b^{j}*E_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{i}*\partial_{i}b^{j}*E_{j}+a^{i}*b^{j}*\nabla_{i}E_{j}.

They are not equal to each other unless

\displaystyle a^{i}*b^{j}=b^{j}*a^{i}

for all
i
,
j
. This is generally impossible. As a consequence, it indicates that the noncommutative covariant derivatives are not well-defined with respect to orthonormal basis.

The left and right connections are uniquely determined by connection coefficients
\Gamma_{ij}^{k}
and
\tilde{\Gamma}_{ij}^{k}
, which are elements of
\mathcal{A}_{U}

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}E_{j}=\Gamma_{ij}^{k}*E_{k},\hskip 10.00002pt\tilde{\nabla}_{i}\tilde{E}_{j}=\tilde{E}_{k}*\tilde{\Gamma}_{ij}^{k}.

Similar to the classical differential geometry, a noncommutative left connection
\nabla
on the left tangent bundle induces a unique noncommutative right connection
\tilde{\nabla}
on the right cotangent bundle
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}^{*}
in terms of noncommutative metric

\displaystyle\partial_{i}g(E_{k},\tilde{E}^{j})=g(\nabla_{i}E_{k},\tilde{E}^{j})+g(E_{k},\tilde{\nabla}_{i}\tilde{E}^{j}).

It yields

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{i}\tilde{E}^{j}=-\tilde{E}^{k}*\Gamma_{ik}^{j}.

Moreover, a noncommutative right connection
\tilde{\nabla}
on the right tangent bundle also induces a noncommutative left connection
\nabla
on the left cotangent bundle which yields

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}E^{j}=-\tilde{\Gamma}_{ik}^{j}*E^{k}.

Inspired by the Levi-Civita connection of a (pseudo-) Riemannian metric, the second author introduced the canonical connection

26

.

Definition 2.5
.

Given a noncommutative metric
g
and a set of elements
\Upsilon_{ijk}
of
\mathcal{A}_{U}
with

\Upsilon_{ijk}=\Upsilon_{jik},

which are referred as chiral coefficients. Denote

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ijk}=\Gamma_{ij}^{l}*g_{lk},\hskip 10.00002pt\tilde{\Gamma}_{ijk}=g_{kl}*\tilde{\Gamma}_{ij}^{l}.

A noncommutative connection, which consists of a noncommutative left connection
\nabla
and a noncommutative right connection
\tilde{\nabla}
, is canonical with respect to
g
and
\Upsilon_{ijk}
if it satisfies

(i)

Compatibility:
\partial_{k}g_{ij}=g(\nabla_{k}E_{i},\tilde{E}_{j})+g(E_{i},\tilde{\nabla}_{k}\tilde{E}_{j})=\Gamma_{kij}+\tilde{\Gamma}_{kji};

(ii)

Torsion free:
\nabla_{i}E_{j}=\nabla_{j}E_{i}
,
\tilde{\nabla}_{i}\tilde{E}_{j}=\tilde{\nabla}_{j}\tilde{E}_{i};

(iii)

Chirality:
\Gamma_{ijk}-\tilde{\Gamma}_{ijk}=\Upsilon_{ijk}.

The torsion free condition implies

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ij}^{k}=\Gamma_{ji}^{k},\hskip 10.00002pt\tilde{\Gamma}_{ij}^{k}=\tilde{\Gamma}_{ji}^{k}.

It is straightforward that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 2\Gamma_{ijk}&=\partial_{i}g_{jk}+\partial_{j}g_{ki}-\partial_{k}g_{ij}+\Upsilon_{ikj}+\Upsilon_{jik}-\Upsilon_{kji}\\
&=\partial_{i}g_{jk}+\partial_{j}g_{ki}-\partial_{k}g_{ji}+\Upsilon_{ijk}\\
&=\partial_{i}\Big(\frac{g_{jk}+g_{kj}}{2}\Big)+\partial_{j}\Big(\frac{g_{ki}+g_{ik}}{2}\Big)-\partial_{k}\Big(\frac{g_{ij}+g_{ji}}{2}\Big)+\Upsilon_{ijk},\end{aligned}

(2.3)

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 2\tilde{\Gamma}_{ijk}&=\partial_{i}g_{jk}+\partial_{j}g_{ki}-\partial_{k}g_{ij}+\Upsilon_{ikj}-\Upsilon_{jik}-\Upsilon_{kji}\\
&=\partial_{i}g_{jk}+\partial_{j}g_{ki}-\partial_{k}g_{ji}-\Upsilon_{ijk}\\
&=\partial_{i}\Big(\frac{g_{jk}+g_{kj}}{2}\Big)+\partial_{j}\Big(\frac{g_{ki}+g_{ik}}{2}\Big)-\partial_{k}\Big(\frac{g_{ij}+g_{ji}}{2}\Big)-\Upsilon_{ijk}.\end{aligned}

(2.4)

In classical Riemannian geometry, the chiral coefficients vanish and
\Gamma_{ijk}
reduce to the Christoffel symbols.

For any
f\in\mathcal{A}_{U}
, it is easy to verify

\displaystyle[E_{i},E_{j}]f=[\tilde{E}_{i},\tilde{E}_{j}]f=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}f-\partial_{j}\partial_{i}f=0.

Thus the left curvature operators
\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}
and the right curvature operators
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\tilde{E}_{i}\tilde{E}_{j}}
can be defined as the following
\mathcal{A}_{U}
-linear operators

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}

\displaystyle=[\nabla_{i},\nabla_{j}]:\,\,\mathcal{T}_{U}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{U},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\tilde{E}_{i}\tilde{E}_{j}}

\displaystyle=[\tilde{\nabla}_{i},\tilde{\nabla}_{j}]:\,\,\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}\longrightarrow\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}.

For the canonical connection, the left Riemannian curvatures
R_{lkij}
and right Riemannian curvatures
\tilde{R}_{lkij}
are defined as

\displaystyle R_{lkij}=g(\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{k},\tilde{E}_{l}),\hskip 10.00002pt\tilde{R}_{lkij}=-g(E_{k},\tilde{R}_{\tilde{E}_{i}\tilde{E}_{j}}\tilde{E}_{l}).

They satisfy

\displaystyle R_{lkij}=-R_{lkji}=\tilde{R}_{lkij},\hskip 10.00002ptR_{lkij}\not\equiv-R_{klij}.

Therefore the left curvatures are sufficient for the purpose. There are two Ricci curvatures
R_{kj}
and
\Theta_{il}
obtained by contracting
l
,
i
and
k
,
j
in
R_{lkij}
respectively

\displaystyle R_{kj}=

\displaystyle g(\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{k},\tilde{E}_{l})*g^{li}=R_{lkij}*g^{li},

\displaystyle\Theta_{il}=

\displaystyle g^{jk}*g(\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{k},\tilde{E}_{l})=g^{jk}*R_{lkij}.

Raising the index at

and

respectively, we have Ricci curvatures

\displaystyle R_{j}^{p}=

\displaystyle g^{pk}*g(\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{k},\tilde{E}_{l})*g^{li}=g^{pk}*R_{lkij}*g^{li},

\displaystyle\Theta_{i}^{p}=

\displaystyle g^{jk}*g(\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{k},\tilde{E}_{l})*g^{lp}=g^{jk}*R_{lkij}*g^{lp}.

The two Ricci curvatures

are not equal to each other in the noncommutative case. But their traces coincide and yield the same scalar curvature

\displaystyle R=R_{j}^{j}=\Theta_{i}^{i}.

As elements of

, there are the following power series expansions

(2.7)

In this paper, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional smooth manifold and
U\subset M
a coordinate chart. Let
\nabla
,
\tilde{\nabla}
be the canonical connections with respect to noncommutative metric
g
and chiral coefficients
\Upsilon_{ijk}
on
U
. If
g_{ij}
satisfy

\displaystyle g_{ij}[2q]=g_{ji}[2q],\hskip 10.00002ptg_{ij}[2q+1]=-g_{ji}[2q+1],

(2.12)

and
\Upsilon_{ijk}
satisfy

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{ijk}[2q]=0,

(2.13)

then two Ricci curvatures are equivalent in the sense that

\displaystyle R_{ij}[2q]=\Theta_{ji}[2q],\hskip 10.00002ptR_{ij}[2q+1]=-\Theta_{ji}[2q+1]

(2.14)

and

\displaystyle R^{i}_{j}[2q]=\Theta^{i}_{j}[2q],\hskip 10.00002pt\,\,\,R^{i}_{j}[2q+1]=-\Theta^{i}_{j}[2q+1].

(2.15)

In particular, if noncommutative metric and chiral coefficients are given by an isometric embedding, then (
2.12
), (
2.13
) hold and the theorem follows.

Finally, we would like to remark that, in Poisson geometry, the Moyal product is a deformation quantization of the constant Poisson structure

\pi=\frac{1}{2}\theta^{ij}\partial_{i}\wedge\partial_{j}

for constant skew-symmetric matrix
(\theta^{ij})
. If
\theta^{ij}
are smooth functions,
\pi
still gives a Poisson structure if its Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket vanishes,

[\pi,\pi]_{S}=0.

However, the corresponding Moyal product is not associative. In the pioneer work, Kontsevich proved that there always exists an associative noncommutative star product which provides the deformation quantization for any Poisson structure
[
12
]
. A concrete algorithm for calculating those integrals appeared in Kontsevich’s formula was given by Banks, Panzer and Pym using integer-linear combinations of multiple zeta values
[
3
]
, which produce the first software package for the symbolic calculation of Kontsevich’s formula. It should be pointed out that Kontsevich’s star products are not compatible with the Leibniz rule. It indicates the theory of noncommutative differential geometry depends on the choice of coordinate systems in
U
. As coordinate systems correspond to observers, this fits Bohr’s opinion that evidence obtained under different experimental conditions cannot be comprehended within a single picture, but must be regarded as complementary in the sense that only the totality of the phenomena exhausts
the possible information about the objects.

3.
Curvature operators and Bianchi identities

In this section, we study the covariant derivatives of noncommutative metrics and curvatures from the geometric point of view. This yields noncommutative version of the first and the second Bianchi identities.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional differentiable manifold and
U\subset M
be a coordinate chart equipped with natural coordinates
(x^{1},\cdots,x^{n})
. Let
g
be a homomorphism of two-sided
\mathcal{A}_{U}
-modules given by (
2.5
) where
(g_{ij}[0])
is not necessarily symmetric. If
(g_{ij}[0])
is invertible on
U
with the inverse matrix
(g^{ij}[0])
, then (
2.5
) gives a noncommutative metric
g
on
U
.

Proof:
For any smooth functions
u(x)
,
v(x)
over
U
, denote

\displaystyle\mu_{q}(u,v)(x)=\frac{1}{q!}\Big[(\theta^{ij}\partial_{i}\partial^{\prime}_{j})^{q}u(x)v(x^{\prime})\Big]_{x=x^{\prime}}.

(3.1)

Let
g^{ij}
have the power series expansions

\displaystyle g^{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\infty}g^{ij}[q]\hbar^{q}\in\mathcal{A}_{U},\hskip 10.00002ptg^{ij}[q]\in C^{\infty}(U).

(3.2)

By viewing
g^{ij}
as the right inverse, we obtain the recursive formula for
q\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} g^{ij}[q]=&-\sum\limits_{r=1}^{q}g^{ik}[0]g_{kl}[r]g^{lj}[q-r]\\
&-\sum\limits_{r=1}^{q}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{q-r}g^{ik}[0]\mu_{r}\Big(g_{kl}[s],g^{lj}[q-r-s]\Big).\end{aligned}

(3.3)

On the other hand, by viewing
g^{ij}
as the left inverse, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} g^{ij}[q]=&-\sum\limits_{r=1}^{q}g^{ik}[q-r]g_{kl}[r]g^{lj}[0]\\
&-\sum\limits_{r=1}^{q}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{q-r}\mu_{r}\Big(g^{ik}[q-r-s],g_{kl}[s]\Big)g^{lj}[0].\end{aligned}

(3.4)

Thus the matrix
(g_{ij})
is invertible in
\mathcal{A}_{U}^{n\times n}
if and only if the matrix
(g_{ij}[0](x))
is invertible in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
for any
x\in U
. Therefore the proof of the proposition is complete. Q.E.D.

Corollary 3.1
.

For any (pseudo-) Riemannian metric
g_{ij}[0]
on
U
, (
2.5
) provides a noncommutative metric
g
on
U
, which is referred as a quantum fluctuation of
g_{ij}[0]
.

Remark 3.1
.

The nonsymmetric metric tensor
g_{ij}[0]
has its geometric physical origin and the skew-symmetric part represents the intrinsic spin, c.f.
[
16
,
11
,
9
,
10
]
and references therein. The classical gravitational theory in terms of nonsymmetric metric tensor also relates to Connes’s noncommutative geometry where spectral triples play roles
[
11
]
.

The noncommutative metric
g
and its inverse
g^{-1}
can be written as,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} g=&\tilde{E}^{i}\otimes g_{ij}*E^{j}=\tilde{E}^{i}*g_{ij}\otimes E^{j},\\
g^{-1}=&\tilde{E}_{i}\otimes g^{ij}*E_{j}=\tilde{E}_{i}*g^{ij}\otimes E_{j}.\end{aligned}

This allows us to define covariant derivatives of
g
and
g^{-1}
by

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}g=

\displaystyle\tilde{E}^{i}\otimes\nabla_{k}(g_{ij}*E^{j})=\tilde{\nabla}_{k}(\tilde{E}^{i}*g_{ij})\otimes E^{j},

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}g^{-1}=

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{i}\otimes\nabla_{k}(g^{ij}*E_{j})=\tilde{\nabla}_{k}(\tilde{E}_{i}*g^{ij})\otimes E_{j}

and denote

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}g=

\displaystyle\tilde{E}^{i}\otimes\nabla_{k}g_{ij}*E^{j}=\tilde{E}^{i}*\nabla_{k}g_{ij}\otimes E^{j},

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}g^{-1}=

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{i}\otimes\nabla_{k}g^{ij}*E_{j}=\tilde{E}_{i}*\nabla_{k}g^{ij}\otimes E_{j}.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\nabla
be a noncommutative connection which is compatible with the noncommutative metric
g
. Then

\nabla_{k}g=\nabla_{k}g^{-1}=0,

i.e.

\nabla_{k}g_{ij}=\nabla_{k}g^{ij}=0.

Proof:
It is straightforward that

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}g

\displaystyle=\nabla_{k}(\tilde{E}^{i}\otimes g_{ij}*E^{j})

\displaystyle=\tilde{\nabla}_{k}\tilde{E}^{i}\otimes g_{ij}*E^{j}+\tilde{E}^{i}\otimes\partial_{k}g_{ij}*E^{j}+\tilde{E}^{i}\otimes g_{ij}*\nabla_{k}E^{j}

\displaystyle=-\tilde{E}^{l}*\Gamma_{kl}^{i}\otimes g_{ij}*E^{j}+\tilde{E}^{i}\otimes\partial_{k}g_{ij}*E^{j}-\tilde{E}^{i}\otimes g_{ij}*\tilde{\Gamma}_{kl}^{j}*E^{l}

\displaystyle=\tilde{E}^{i}\otimes(\partial_{k}g_{ij}-\Gamma_{kij}-\tilde{\Gamma}_{kji})*E^{j}=0.

On the other hand, a direct computation yields

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\Big[\partial_{k}(g^{il}*g_{lr})\Big]*g^{rj}

\displaystyle=\Big[\partial_{k}{g^{il}}*g_{lr}+g^{il}*\partial_{k}{g_{lr}}\Big]*g^{rj}

\displaystyle=\partial_{k}{g^{il}}*g_{lr}*g^{rj}+g^{il}*(\Gamma_{klr}+\tilde{\Gamma}_{krl})*g^{rj}

\displaystyle=\partial_{k}{g^{ij}}+g^{il}*\Gamma^{s}_{kl}*g_{sr}*g^{rj}+g^{il}*g_{ls}*\tilde{\Gamma}^{s}_{kr}*g^{rj}

\displaystyle=\partial_{k}{g^{ij}}+g^{il}*\Gamma^{j}_{kl}+\tilde{\Gamma}^{i}_{kl}*g^{lj}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}{g^{-1}}=

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}(\tilde{E}_{i}\otimes g^{ij}*E_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{k}\tilde{E}_{i}\otimes g^{ij}*E_{j}+\tilde{E}_{i}\otimes\partial_{k}g^{ij}*E_{j}+\tilde{E}_{i}\otimes g^{ij}*\nabla_{k}E_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{i}\otimes\Big(\partial_{k}{g^{ij}}+g^{il}*\Gamma^{j}_{kl}+\tilde{\Gamma}^{i}_{kl}*g^{lj}\Big)*E_{j}=0.

Q.E.D.

For left and right tangent vectors

\displaystyle V=v^{i}*E_{i},\hskip 10.00002ptW=w^{j}*E_{j},\hskip 10.00002pt\tilde{V}=\tilde{E}_{i}*\tilde{v}^{i},\hskip 10.00002pt\tilde{W}=\tilde{E}_{j}*\tilde{w}^{j},

where
v^{i}
,
w^{j}
,
\tilde{v}^{i}
,
\tilde{w}^{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{U}
, the noncommutative Lie brackets are defined as

\displaystyle[V,W]f=

\displaystyle v^{i}*E_{i}\big(w^{j}*E_{j}(f)\big)-w^{j}*E_{j}\big(v^{i}*E_{i}(f)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(v^{i}*E_{i}(w^{j})-w^{i}*E_{i}(v^{j})\big)*E_{j}(f)+[v^{i},w^{j}]*E_{i}E_{j}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[W,V]f,

\displaystyle[\tilde{V},\tilde{W}]f=

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{i}*\tilde{v}^{i}\big(\tilde{E}_{j}(f)*\tilde{w}^{j}\big)-\tilde{E}_{j}*\tilde{w}^{j}\big(\tilde{E}_{i}(f)*\tilde{v}^{i}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{E}_{j}(f)*\big(\tilde{E}_{i}(\tilde{w}^{j})*\tilde{v}^{i}-\tilde{E}_{i}(\tilde{v}^{j})*\tilde{w}^{i}\big)-\tilde{E}_{i}\tilde{E}_{j}(f)*[\tilde{v}^{i},\tilde{w}^{j}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[\tilde{W},\tilde{V}]f

for any
f\in\mathcal{A}_{U}
. Analogous to the classical (pseudo-) Riemannian geometry, the noncommutative left and right curvature operators for left and right tangent vectors can be formally defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{VW}=

\displaystyle[\nabla_{V},\nabla_{W}]-\nabla_{[V,W]},

\displaystyle\mathcal{\tilde{R}}_{\tilde{V}\tilde{W}}=

\displaystyle[\tilde{\nabla}_{\tilde{V}},\tilde{\nabla}_{\tilde{W}}]-\tilde{\nabla}_{[\tilde{V},\tilde{W}]}.

It is shown that
\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\tilde{E}_{i}\tilde{E}_{j}}
are left and right
\mathcal{A}_{U}
-module endomorphisms over left and right tangent bundles respectively
[
6
]
. But
\mathcal{R}_{VW}
,
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\tilde{V}\tilde{W}}
do not make sense unless

\displaystyle[v^{i},w^{j}]=[\tilde{v}^{i},\tilde{w}^{j}]=0.

Thus, if
V=E_{i}
(resp.
\tilde{V}=\tilde{E}_{i}
) or
W=E_{j}
(resp.
\tilde{W}=\tilde{E}_{j}
), then
\mathcal{R}_{VW}E_{k}\in\mathcal{T}_{U}
(resp.
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\tilde{V}\tilde{W}}\tilde{E}_{k}\in\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}
) is well-defined. This suggests to define the covariant derivatives of noncommutative curvatures by adopting the idea of classical (pseudo-) Riemannian geometry. We only consider the case of left curvatures.

Definition 3.1
.

The covariant derivatives of noncommutative curvature operators are defined as follows.

\displaystyle(\nabla_{k}\mathcal{R})_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{p}=

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}(\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{p})-\mathcal{R}_{(\nabla_{E_{k}}E_{i})E_{j}}E_{p}

\displaystyle-\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}(\nabla_{E_{k}}E_{j})}E_{p}-\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}(\nabla_{E_{k}}E_{p}).

Definition 3.2
.

The covariant derivatives of noncommutative curvature tensors, noncommutative Ricci curvatures and noncommutative scalar curvature are defined as follows.

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}R_{lkij}=

\displaystyle g\big((\nabla_{s}\mathcal{R})_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{k},\tilde{E}_{l}\big),

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}R_{j}^{p}=

\displaystyle g^{pk}*g\big((\nabla_{s}\mathcal{R})_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{k},\tilde{E}_{l}\big)*g^{li}=g^{pk}*\nabla_{s}R_{lkij}*g^{li},

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}\Theta_{i}^{p}=

\displaystyle g^{jk}*g\big((\nabla_{s}\mathcal{R})_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{k},\tilde{E}_{l}\big)*g^{lp}=g^{jk}*\nabla_{s}R_{lkij}*g^{lp},

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}R=

\displaystyle g^{jk}*g\big((\nabla_{s}\mathcal{R})_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{k},\tilde{E}_{l}\big)*g^{li}.

Remark 3.2
.

If
V=E_{i}
or
W=E_{j}
, then the operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{VW}:\mathcal{T}_{U}\to\mathcal{T}_{U}

is well-defined but generally not
\mathcal{A}_{U}
-linear. Indeed, let
V=E_{i}
,
W=a^{j}*E_{j}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{VW}(f*E_{k})=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\Big(a^{j}*\nabla_{j}(f*E_{k})\Big)-a^{j}*\nabla_{j}\Big(\nabla_{i}(f*E_{k})\Big)

\displaystyle-\nabla_{[E_{i},a^{j}*E_{j}]}(f*E_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{i}a^{j})*\nabla_{j}(f*E_{k})+a^{j}*\nabla_{i}\Big(\nabla_{j}(f*E_{k})\Big)

\displaystyle-a^{j}*\nabla_{j}\Big(\nabla_{i}(f*E_{k})\Big)-\nabla_{(\partial_{i}a^{j})*E_{j}}(f*E_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{j}*\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}(f*E_{k})+(\partial_{i}a^{j})*\nabla_{j}(f*E_{k})

\displaystyle-(\partial_{i}a^{j})*\nabla_{j}(f*E_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{j}*f*\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{k}.

The same computation yields that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{VW}E_{k}=a^{j}*\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{k}.

Hence
\mathcal{R}_{VW}(f*E_{k})
and
f*\mathcal{R}_{VW}E_{k}
are not equal unless

\displaystyle a^{j}*f=f*a^{j}.

The above computation also yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}(a^{j}*E_{j})}=a^{j}*\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{(a^{i}*E_{i})E_{j}}=a^{i}*\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}.

As an operator,
(\nabla_{k}\mathcal{R})_{E_{i}E_{j}}
dose not give rise to a left
\mathcal{A}_{U}
-module endomorphism over left tangent bundle. This is because

\displaystyle(\nabla_{k}\mathcal{R})_{E_{i}E_{j}}(f*E_{p})=

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}\Big(\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}(f*E_{p})\Big)-\mathcal{R}_{(\nabla_{k}E_{i})E_{j}}(f*E_{p})

\displaystyle-\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}(\nabla_{k}E_{j})}(f*E_{p})-\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}\Big(\nabla_{k}(f*E_{p})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}(f*\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{p})-\mathcal{R}_{(\nabla_{k}E_{i})E_{j}}(f*E_{p})

\displaystyle-\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}(\nabla_{k}E_{j})}(f*E_{p})

\displaystyle-\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}\Big((\partial_{k}f)*E_{p}+f*\nabla_{k}E_{p}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{k}f)*\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{p}+f*\nabla_{k}(\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{p})

\displaystyle-\mathcal{R}_{(\nabla_{k}E_{i})E_{j}}(f*E_{p})-\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}(\nabla_{k}E_{j})}(f*E_{p})

\displaystyle-(\partial_{k}f)*\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{p}-f*\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}(\nabla_{k}E_{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f*\nabla_{k}(\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{p})-\mathcal{R}_{(\nabla_{k}E_{i})E_{j}}(f*E_{p})

\displaystyle-\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}(\nabla_{k}E_{j})}(f*E_{p})-f*\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}(\nabla_{k}E_{p})

\displaystyle\not\equiv

\displaystyle f*(\nabla_{k}\mathcal{R})_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{p}

as
\mathcal{R}_{(\nabla_{k}E_{i})E_{j}}
and
\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}(\nabla_{k}E_{j})}
are not
\mathcal{A}_{U}
-module endomorphisms in general.

Theorem 3.1
.

The first (algebraic) Bianchi identity

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{k}+\mathcal{R}_{E_{j}E_{k}}E_{i}+\mathcal{R}_{E_{k}E_{i}}E_{j}=0

and the second (differential) Bianchi identity

\displaystyle(\nabla_{i}\mathcal{R})_{E_{j}E_{k}}E_{p}+(\nabla_{j}\mathcal{R})_{E_{k}E_{i}}E_{p}+(\nabla_{k}\mathcal{R})_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{p}=0

hold for
1\leq i,j,k,p\leq n
.

Proof:
Since the connection is torsion free, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{k}+

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{E_{j}E_{k}}E_{i}+\mathcal{R}_{E_{k}E_{i}}E_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}E_{k}-\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}E_{k}+\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}E_{i}-\nabla_{k}\nabla_{j}E_{i}

\displaystyle+\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}E_{j}-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}E_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}(\nabla_{j}E_{k}-\nabla_{k}E_{j})+\nabla_{j}(\nabla_{k}E_{i}-\nabla_{i}E_{k})

\displaystyle+\nabla_{k}(\nabla_{i}E_{j}-\nabla_{j}E_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Thus the first Bianchi identity holds. As

\displaystyle(\nabla_{i}\mathcal{R})_{E_{j}E_{k}}E_{p}=

\displaystyle\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}E_{p}-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{j}E_{p}-\mathcal{R}_{(\nabla_{i}E_{j})E_{k}}E_{p}

\displaystyle-\mathcal{R}_{E_{j}(\nabla_{i}E_{k})}E_{p}-\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}E_{p}+\nabla_{k}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}E_{p},

\displaystyle(\nabla_{j}\mathcal{R})_{E_{k}E_{i}}E_{p}=

\displaystyle\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}E_{p}-\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}E_{p}-\mathcal{R}_{(\nabla_{j}E_{k})E_{i}}E_{p}

\displaystyle-\mathcal{R}_{E_{k}(\nabla_{j}E_{i})}E_{p}-\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}E_{p}+\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{j}E_{p},

\displaystyle(\nabla_{k}\mathcal{R})_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{p}=

\displaystyle\nabla_{k}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}E_{p}-\nabla_{k}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}E_{p}-\mathcal{R}_{(\nabla_{k}E_{i})E_{j}}E_{p}

\displaystyle-\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}(\nabla_{k}E_{j})}E_{p}-\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{k}E_{p}+\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{k}E_{p},

we obtain

\displaystyle(\nabla_{i}\mathcal{R})_{E_{j}E_{k}}E_{p}+(\nabla_{j}\mathcal{R})_{E_{k}E_{i}}E_{p}+(\nabla_{k}\mathcal{R})_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{p}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{R}_{(\nabla_{i}E_{j})E_{k}}E_{p}-\mathcal{R}_{E_{j}(\nabla_{i}E_{k})}E_{p}-\mathcal{R}_{(\nabla_{j}E_{k})E_{i}}E_{p}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\,-\mathcal{R}_{E_{k}(\nabla_{j}E_{i})}E_{p}-\mathcal{R}_{(\nabla_{k}E_{i})E_{j}}E_{p}-\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}(\nabla_{k}E_{j})}E_{p}.

The torsion free condition implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{(\nabla_{i}E_{j})E_{k}}E_{p}+\mathcal{R}_{E_{k}(\nabla_{j}E_{i})}E_{p}=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{E_{j}(\nabla_{i}E_{k})}E_{p}+\mathcal{R}_{(\nabla_{k}E_{i})E_{j}}E_{p}=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{(\nabla_{j}E_{k})E_{i}}E_{p}+\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}(\nabla_{k}E_{j})}E_{p}=0.

Therefore

\displaystyle(\nabla_{i}\mathcal{R})_{E_{j}E_{k}}E_{p}+(\nabla_{j}\mathcal{R})_{E_{k}E_{i}}E_{p}+(\nabla_{k}\mathcal{R})_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{p}=0.

Thus the second Bianchi identity holds. Q.E.D.

Remark 3.3
.

Bianchi identities also hold for noncommutative right curvature tensors.

Proposition 3.3
.

The second Bianchi identity gives that

\nabla_{i}R_{j}^{i}+\nabla_{i}\Theta_{j}^{i}-\delta_{j}^{i}\nabla_{i}R=0.

Proof:
By the second Bianchi identity, we have

\nabla_{i}R_{qpjk}+\nabla_{j}R_{qpki}+\nabla_{k}R_{qpij}=0.

Multiplying
g^{ip}
from the left side and
g^{qk}
from the right side, we obtain

g^{ip}*\nabla_{i}R_{qpjk}*g^{qk}+g^{ip}*\nabla_{j}R_{qpki}*g^{qk}+g^{ip}*\nabla_{k}R_{qpij}*g^{qk}=0.

Taking summation for
i
,
k
,
p
and
q
, we obtain

-\nabla_{i}R^{i}_{j}+\nabla_{j}R-\nabla_{k}\Theta_{j}^{k}=0.

Therefore the proof of the proposition is complete. Q.E.D.

4.
Equivalence of noncommutative Ricci curvatures

In this section, we show that two Ricci curvatures
R^{i}_{j}
and
\Theta^{i}_{j}
are equivalent under certain conditions. In particular,
they are satisfied if noncommutative metric and chiral coefficients are given by an isometric embedding.

Let noncommutative metric
g_{ij}
, its inverse
g^{ij}
and chiral coefficients
\Upsilon_{ijk}
have power series expansions (
2.5
), (
3.2
) and (
2.6
).

Lemma 4.1
.

If noncommutative metric
g
satisfies (
2.12
), then

\displaystyle g^{ij}[2q]=g^{ji}[2q],\hskip 10.00002ptg^{ij}[2q+1]=-g^{ji}[2q+1].

(4.1)

Proof:
Since
(g_{ij}[0])
is symmetric and invertible on
U
, the inverse matrix
(g^{ij}[0])
is also symmetric, i.e.,

\displaystyle g^{ij}[0]=g^{ji}[0].

For
u,v\in C^{\infty}(U)
, (
3.1
) indicates that

\displaystyle\mu_{2q}(u,v)=\mu_{2q}(v,u),\hskip 10.00002pt\mu_{2q+1}(u,v)=-\mu_{2q+1}(v,u).

(4.2)

By (
4.2
) and the recursive formulas (
3.3
), (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle g^{ij}[1]

\displaystyle=-g^{ik}[0]g_{kl}[1]g^{lj}[0]-g^{ik}[0]\mu_{1}\Big(g_{kl}[0],g^{lj}[0]\Big)

\displaystyle=g^{jl}[0]g_{lk}[1]g^{ki}[0]+\mu_{1}\Big(g^{jl}[0],g_{lk}[0]\Big)g^{ki}[0]

\displaystyle=-g^{ji}[1].

Next, let
q\in\mathbb{N}
, using the recursive formula (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle g^{ij}[2q]=

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{r=1}^{q}g^{ik}[0]g_{kl}[2r]g^{lj}[2q-2r]

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{r=1}^{q}g^{ik}[0]g_{kl}[2r-1]g^{lj}[2q-2r+1]

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{r=1}^{q}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{q-r}g^{ik}[0]\mu_{2r}\Big(g_{kl}[2s],g^{lj}[2q-2r-2s]\Big)

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{r=1}^{q}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{q-r}g^{ik}[0]\mu_{2r}\Big(g_{kl}[2s-1],g^{lj}[2q-2r-2s+1]\Big)

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{r=1}^{q}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{q-r}g^{ik}[0]\mu_{2r-1}\Big(g_{kl}[2s],g^{lj}[2q-2r-2s+1]\Big)

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{r=1}^{q}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{q-r}g^{ik}[0]\mu_{2r-1}\Big(g_{kl}[2s+1],g^{lj}[2q-2r-2s]\Big).

Therefore, by induction and (
4.2
) and recursive formula (
3.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle g^{ij}[2q]=

\displaystyle-\sum_{r=1}^{q}g^{jl}[2q-2r]g_{lk}[2r]g^{ki}[0]

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{r=1}^{q}g^{jl}[2q-2r+1]g_{lk}[2r-1]g^{ki}[0]

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{r=1}^{q}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{q-r}\mu_{2r}\Big(g^{jl}[2q-2r-2s],g_{lk}[2s]\Big)g^{ki}[0]

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{r=1}^{q}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{q-r}\mu_{2r}\Big(g^{jl}[2q-2r-2s+1],g_{lk}[2s-1]\Big)g^{ki}[0]

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{r=1}^{q}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{q-r}\mu_{2r-1}\Big(g^{jl}[2q-2r-2s+1],g_{lk}[2s]\Big)g^{ki}[0]

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{r=1}^{q}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{q-r}\mu_{2r-1}\Big(g^{jl}[2q-2r-2s],g_{lk}[2s+1]\Big)g^{ki}[0]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{ji}[2q].

Similarly, we can prove

\displaystyle g^{ij}[2q+1]=-g^{ji}[2q+1].

Q.E.D.

Lemma 4.2
.

If noncommutative metric
g
satisfies (
2.12
), then, for any
f\in C^{\infty}(U)
,

\displaystyle\big(g^{ij}*f*g^{kl}\big)[2q]

\displaystyle=\big(g^{lk}*f*g^{ji}\big)[2q],

\displaystyle\big(g^{ij}*f*g^{kl}\big)[2q+1]

\displaystyle=-\big(g^{lk}*f*g^{ji}\big)[2q+1].

Proof:
For any
u,v\in C^{\infty}(U)
, we have

\displaystyle\big(g^{ij}*u*v\big)[2q]=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{r=0}^{q}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{q-r}\mu_{2r}\Big(g^{ij}[2s],\mu_{2q-2r-2s}(u,v)\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{r=0}^{q}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{q-r}\mu_{2r}\Big(g^{ij}[2s-1],\mu_{2q-2r-2s+1}(u,v)\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{r=1}^{q}\sum\limits_{s=0}^{q-r}\mu_{2r-1}\Big(g^{ij}[2s],\mu_{2q-2r-2s+1}(u,v)\Big)

5.

6.
Quasi-connections and curvatures

In this section, we study noncommutative differential geometry with respect to star products constructed by Kontsevich on Poisson manifolds
[
12
]
. These star products are only associative, but are not compatible with the Leibniz rule. This causes that many geometric properties are lost. However, we can still define left and right quasi-connections as well as their curvatures.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional differentiable manifold and
U\subset M
a coordinate chart equipped with coordinates
\{x^{1},\cdots,x^{n}\}
. Recall that a formal deformation of the
\mathbb{R}
-algebra
C^{\infty}(U)
is an associative
\mathbb{R}[[\hbar]]
-bilinear product

\displaystyle\star:\mathcal{A}_{U}\times\mathcal{A}_{U}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{U}

such that, for
u,\,\,v\in C^{\infty}(U)
,

\displaystyle 1\star u=u\star 1=u,\hskip 10.00002ptu\star v=uv+\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\infty}B_{q}(u,v)\hbar^{q},

where

\displaystyle B_{q}:C^{\infty}(U)\times C^{\infty}(U)\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(U)

are
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear maps. We call
\star
a deformation product on
C^{\infty}(U)
and
(\mathcal{A}_{U},\star)
a deformation algebra of
C^{\infty}(U)
. The associativity of
\star
implies that

\displaystyle\{u,v\}_{\star}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(B_{1}(u,v)-B_{1}(v,u)\Big)

is a Poisson bracket, c.f.
[
12
,
13
]
. Furthermore, we call
\star
a star product if all
B_{q}
are bi-differential operators. Given a Poisson bracket
\{,\}
on
C^{\infty}(U)
, a star product
\star
is called a deformation quantization of
\big(U,\{,\}\big)
if

\displaystyle\{,\}=\{,\}_{\star},

c.f.
[
5
]
. The existence of deformation quantization on Poisson manifolds was proved by Kontsevich
[
12
]
.

As before, we denote

\displaystyle E_{i}=\tilde{E}_{i}=\partial_{i},\hskip 10.00002pt1\leq i\leq n.

The noncommutative tangent bundles and metrics with respect to a star products can be defined as follows.

Definition 6.1
.

The noncommutative left (
resp.
right)
\star
-tangent bundle
{}^{\star}\mathcal{T}_{U}
(
resp.

{}^{\star}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}
) on
U
is the free left (
resp.
right)
(\mathcal{A}_{U},\star)
-module with basis
\{E_{1},\cdots,E_{n}\}
(
resp.

\{\tilde{E}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{E}_{n}\}
), i.e.,

{}^{\star}\mathcal{T}_{U}

\displaystyle=\Big\{a^{i}\star E_{i}\,\Big|\,a^{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{U},\,a^{i}\star E_{i}=0\Longleftrightarrow a^{i}=0\Big\},

{}^{\star}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}

\displaystyle=\Big\{\tilde{E}_{i}\star\tilde{a}^{i}\,\Big|\,\tilde{a}^{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{U},\,\tilde{E}_{i}\star\tilde{a}^{i}=0\Longleftrightarrow\tilde{a}^{i}=0\Big\}.

An element of
{}^{\star}\mathcal{T}_{U}
(
resp.

{}^{\star}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}
) is called a left (
resp.
right)
\star
-vector field.

Definition 6.2
.

A noncommutative
\star
-metric
{}^{\star}g
is defined as a homomorphism of two-sided
(\mathcal{A}_{U},\star)
-modules

{}^{\star}g:{{}^{\star}\mathcal{T}_{U}}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}[[\hbar]]}{{}^{\star}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{U}

such that the matrix

\displaystyle({{}^{\star}g_{ij}})\in\mathcal{A}_{U}^{n\times n},\hskip 10.00002pt{{}^{\star}g_{ij}}={{}^{\star}g}(E_{i},\tilde{E}_{j})

is
\star
-invertible, i.e., there exists a unique matrix
({{}^{\star}g^{ij}})\in\mathcal{A}_{U}^{n\times n}
such that

\displaystyle{{}^{\star}g_{ik}}\star{{}^{\star}g^{kj}}={{}^{\star}g^{jk}}\star{{}^{\star}g_{ki}}=\delta_{i}^{j}.

By the associativity of star product
\star
,
({{}^{\star}g_{ij}})
is invertible if and only if it has a left
\star
-inverse and a right
\star
-inverse. On the other hand, only associativity of the Moyal product is required in the proof of Proposition
3.1
. Therefore, it holds true also replacing the Moyal product by the star product. Thus, on
U
,

\displaystyle({{}^{\star}g_{ij}})\,\,\mbox{is invertible}\Longleftrightarrow({{}^{\star}g_{ij}}[0])\,\,\mbox{is invertible}.

In terms of noncommutative
\star
-metric
{}^{\star}g
, it can induce dual bases
E^{i}
,
\tilde{E}^{j}
of
\tilde{E}_{j}
,
E_{i}
respectively, which satisfy

{}^{\star}g(E^{i},\tilde{E}_{j})={{}^{\star}g}(E_{j},\tilde{E}^{i})=\delta_{j}^{i}.

Definition 6.3
.

The noncommutative left (
resp.
right)
\star
-cotangent bundle
{}^{\star}\mathcal{T}_{U}^{*}
(
resp.

{}^{\star}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}^{*}
) on

with respect to the noncommutative metric

resp.

-module with basis

(
resp.

\{\tilde{E}^{1},\cdots,\tilde{E}^{n}\}

\displaystyle=\Big\{a_{i}\star E^{i}\,\Big|\,a_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{U},\,a_{i}\star E^{i}=0\Longleftrightarrow a_{i}=0\Big\},

\displaystyle=\Big\{\tilde{E}^{i}\star\tilde{a}_{i}\,\Big|\,\tilde{a}_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{U},\,\tilde{E}^{i}\star\tilde{a}_{i}=0\Longleftrightarrow\tilde{a}_{i}=0\Big\}.

Definition 6.4
.

A noncommutative left (
resp.
right) quasi-connection

{}^{\star}\nabla:{{}^{\star}\mathcal{T}}_{U}\longrightarrow{{}^{\star}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}}^{*}_{U}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{U}}{{}^{\star}\mathcal{T}}_{U}\hskip 10.00002pt({\rm{resp.}}\,\,{{}^{\star}\tilde{\nabla}}:{{}^{\star}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}}_{U}\longrightarrow{{}^{\star}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}}_{U}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{U}}{{}^{\star}\mathcal{T}}^{*}_{U})

\displaystyle{{}^{\star}\nabla}V=\tilde{E}^{k}\otimes W_{k},\,\,W_{k}\in{{}^{\star}\mathcal{T}}_{U}\hskip 10.00002pt({\rm{resp.}}\,\,{{}^{\star}\tilde{\nabla}}\tilde{V}=\tilde{W}_{k}\otimes E^{k},\,\,\tilde{W}_{k}\in{{}^{\star}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}}^{*}_{U})

The left (
resp.
right) curvature operators for left (
resp.

{}^{\star}\mathcal{R}_{E_{i}E_{j}}

\displaystyle=[^{\star}\nabla_{i},{{}^{\star}\nabla}_{j}]:\,\,^{\star}\mathcal{T}_{U}\longrightarrow{{}^{\star}\mathcal{T}}_{U},

{}^{\star}\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\tilde{E}_{i}\tilde{E}_{j}}

\displaystyle=[^{\star}\tilde{\nabla}_{i},{{}^{\star}\tilde{\nabla}}_{j}]:\,\,^{\star}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{U}\longrightarrow{{}^{\star}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}}_{U}.

Note that the
\star
-curvature operators are not
(\mathcal{A}_{U},\star)
-linear because the
\star
-covariant derivatives do not satisfy the Leibniz rule. But the
\star
-Riemannian curvatures still can be defined formally.

Definition 6.6
.

The left (
resp.
right) Riemannian curvatures for left (
resp.
right) quasi-connections are defined as follows.

{}^{\star}R_{lkij}

\displaystyle={{}^{\star}g}({{}^{\star}\mathcal{R}}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{k},\tilde{E}_{l}),

{}^{\star}\tilde{R}_{lkij}

\displaystyle=-{{}^{\star}g}(E_{k},{{}^{\star}\tilde{R}}_{\tilde{E}_{i}\tilde{E}_{j}}\tilde{E}_{l}).

Unlike the situation of the Moyal product, the quasi-connections are not compatible with
\star
-metrics, therefore two Riemannian curvatures with respect to star products are not equal in general. This yields four different noncommutative Ricci curvatures.

Definition 6.7
.

The left (
resp.
right) Ricci curvatures for left (
resp.
right) quasi-connections are defined as follows.

{}^{\star}R_{kj}=

\displaystyle{{}^{\star}g}({{}^{\star}\mathcal{R}}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{k},\tilde{E}_{l})\star{{}^{\star}g}^{li}={{}^{\star}R}_{lkij}\star{{}^{\star}g}^{li},

{}^{\star}\Theta_{il}=

\displaystyle{{}^{\star}g}^{jk}\star{{}^{\star}g}({{}^{\star}\mathcal{R}}_{E_{i}E_{j}}E_{k},\tilde{E}_{l})={{}^{\star}g}^{jk}\star{{}^{\star}R}_{lkij},

{}^{\star}\tilde{R}_{kj}=

\displaystyle-{{}^{\star}g}(E_{k},{{}^{\star}\tilde{R}}_{\tilde{E}_{i}\tilde{E}_{j}}\tilde{E}_{l})\star{{}^{\star}g}^{li}={{}^{\star}\tilde{R}}_{lkij}\star{{}^{\star}g}^{li},

\displaystyle-{{}^{\star}g}^{jk}\star{{}^{\star}g}(E_{k},{{}^{\star}\tilde{R}}_{\tilde{E}_{i}\tilde{E}_{j}}\tilde{E}_{l})={{}^{\star}g}^{jk}\star{{}^{\star}\tilde{R}}_{lkij},

.

The left (
resp.

{}^{\star}R={{}^{\star}R}^{j}_{j}={{}^{\star}\Theta}^{i}_{i},\hskip 10.00002pt{{}^{\star}\tilde{R}}={{}^{\star}\tilde{R}}^{j}_{j}={{}^{\star}\tilde{\Theta}}^{i}_{i}.

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose that the left (
resp.

holds for

The proof of the first Bianchi identity in Theorem

Now we study quasi-connections given by an isometric embedding.

\displaystyle X:(U,g[0])\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{p,m-p}.

For

Denote

\displaystyle=\Big\{Y\in\mathcal{A}_{U}^{m}\Big|Y\star_{\eta}\tilde{\sigma}(\tilde{E}_{i})=0,\forall\,i\Big\},

\displaystyle{{}^{\star}\tilde{\mathcal{N}}}_{U}

Lemma 6.2
.

For isometric embedding

resp.

-modules

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{U}^{m}=\sigma({{}^{\star}\mathcal{T}}_{U})\oplus{{}^{\star}\mathcal{N}}_{U}\hskip 10.00002pt({\rm{resp.}}\,\,\mathcal{A}_{U}^{m}=\tilde{\sigma}({{}^{\star}\tilde{\mathcal{T}}}_{U})\oplus{{}^{\star}\tilde{\mathcal{N}}}_{U}).

(6.1)

Proof:

\displaystyle Y^{\bot}\star_{\eta}\tilde{\sigma}(\tilde{E}_{j})=

\displaystyle Y\star_{\eta}\tilde{\sigma}(\tilde{E}_{j})-Y^{\top}\star_{\eta}\tilde{\sigma}(\tilde{E}_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y\star_{\eta}\tilde{\sigma}(\tilde{E}_{j})-y^{i}\star\sigma(E_{i})\star_{\eta}\tilde{\sigma}(\tilde{E}_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y\star_{\eta}\tilde{\sigma}(\tilde{E}_{j})-y^{i}\star{{}^{\star}g}_{ij}

Abstract
. Using real-variable methods, we characterise multipliers for general classes of Hardy–Orlicz spaces, unifying and extending several classical results due to Hardy and Littlewood; Duren and Shields; Paley; and others. Applications of our results include inequalities involving Fourier coefficients and Fourier transforms of elements of Hardy–Orlicz spaces and their duals, as well as embeddings into spaces of generalised smoothness, Sobolev type-embeddings and Paley-Wiener type theorems.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
42A24, 42B35 (primary); 30H10, 30H35, 42A44, 42A85, 42B30 (secondary).

1.
Introduction

Many, if not most, classical spaces of functions that arise in harmonic analysis and operator theory admit membership conditions that can be expressed in terms of growth conditions on Fourier coefficients or Fourier transforms of elements in the space, or, when the space consists of analytic functions, Taylor coefficients. Many operators acting on such spaces can either be defined in terms of their actions on the Fourier or Taylor side, or admit useful descriptions in these terms. This leads to the study of multiplier operators, or in brief, multipliers, and their mapping properties. The simplest case is that of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, the square integrable periodic functions: a function is in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
if, and only if, its Fourier coefficients are in the sequence space
\ell^{2}
, and so a multiplier is bounded if, and only if, it maps
\ell^{2}
to
\ell^{2}
. Similarly, there are many classical results characterising when a multiplier is bounded from a given Banach space, or scale of Banach spaces, to
\ell^{q}
for some suitable range of
q
. When dealing with less classical spaces, such as endpoint substitutes for
L^{1}
spaces, one is naturally led to consider function spaces not of Banach type, and multiplier operators acting on such spaces.

This paper is devoted to a study of multipliers in the setting of Hardy–Orlicz spaces, which generalise
H^{p}
spaces on the circle or on Euclidean space, and their duals. Our goal is to both unify and generalise well-known results in the
H^{p}
theory, and to exploit such results to give new descriptions of the elements of these spaces and how they embed into other naturally occurring spaces in complex and harmonic analysis.

Let us set the scene for this paper by letting
X(\mathbb{D})
be a space of holomorphic functions on the unit disc
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
, equipped with a norm, or quasi-norm,
\|\cdot\|_{X(\mathbb{D})}
so that
\left\|F\right\|_{X(\mathbb{D})}<\infty
if, and only if,
F\in X(\mathbb{D})
. For instance,
X(\mathbb{D})
could be a classical Hardy space
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
with
p\geq 1
.

For
q\in(0,\infty]
, we say that a sequence
\lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
of complex numbers is a multiplier from
X(\mathbb{D})
to
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
if there exists a constant
C_{\lambda,X}>0
such that

(1.1)

\left\|\{\lambda_{n}f_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\right\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}\leq C_{\lambda,X}\left\|F\right\|_{X(\mathbb{D})}

for all
F(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}z^{n}
in
X(\mathbb{D})
. The class of all multipliers from
X(\mathbb{D})
to
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
is denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{X(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}
.
For
\lambda\in\mathcal{M}_{X(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}
, we write

\left\|\lambda\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{X(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}}:=\inf\left\{C_{\lambda,X}>0:\eqref{mult_def}\text{ holds for all }F\in X(\mathbb{D})\right\}.

In this paper we establish characterisations of multipliers from a broad class of Hardy–Orlicz spaces to
\ell^{q}
for
q\in[1,2]
. Our characterisations include in a unified way known results for classical Hardy spaces as well as new multiplier results for Hardy–Orlicz spaces that arise in the study of products between Hardy spaces and their duals. Of particular interest to us is the space
H^{\log}(\mathbb{D})
that naturally arises in the study of products between functions in
H^{1}(\mathbb{D})
and
BMO(\mathbb{D})
(see
[
7
]
) and whose dual space
LMO(\mathbb{D})\cong(H^{\log}(\mathbb{D}))^{\ast}
appears in a number of problems in complex and harmonic analysis, including that of the characterisation of boundedness of Hankel operators on
H^{1}(\mathbb{D})
(see
[
26
]
).

Multiplier theorems

Let us now recall some well-known multiplier theorems on classical Hardy spaces that our present work generalises.
If
p\in(0,1)
then it was shown by P. L. Duren and A. L. Shields in
[
13
,
12
]
that for
q\in[p,\infty)
a sequence
\lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a multiplier from
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
to
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
if, and only if,

(1.2)

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}n^{q/p}|\lambda_{n}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}<\infty.

In the case
p=1
it was shown by G. H. Hardy and J. E. Littlewood
[
20
,
21
]
and E. M. Stein and A. Zygmund
[
34
]
that a sequence
\lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a multiplier from
H^{1}(\mathbb{D})
to
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
if, and only if,

(1.3)

\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{n=2^{m}}^{2^{m+1}-1}|\lambda_{n}|^{2}<\infty.

For an introduction to classical Hardy spaces and for proofs of the aforementioned multiplier results, we refer the reader to Duren’s book
[
11
]
.

In the
p=q=1
case, a theorem attributed to C. Fefferman (see
[
14
]
and
[
31
]
) asserts that a sequence
\lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a multiplier from
H^{1}(\mathbb{D})
to
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
if, and only if,

(1.4)

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}|\lambda_{n}|\right)^{2}<\infty.

In this paper we extend the aforementioned results to a broad class of Hardy–Orlicz spaces. To state our results, recall that given a growth function
\Psi
(see Definition
8
below), the corresponding Hardy–Orlicz space
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})
is defined as the class of functions
F
that are holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
and satisfy

\sup_{0\leq r<1}\int_{[0,1)}\Psi\left(\left|F\left(re^{i2\pi\theta}\right)\right|\right)\mathrm{d}\theta<\infty.

If
G\in H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})
, we set

\left\|G\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})}:=\inf\left\{\alpha>0:\sup_{0\leq r<1}\int_{[0,1)}\Psi\left(\alpha^{-1}\left|G\left(re^{i2\pi\theta}\right)\right|\right)\mathrm{d}\theta\leq 1\right\}.

Note that if
\Psi(t)=t^{p}
then
(H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D}),\|\cdot\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})})
coincides with the usual Hardy space
(H^{p}(\mathbb{D}),\|\cdot\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{D})})
.

In what follows, if
\Psi
is a growth function then
\Psi^{-1}
denotes its inverse, i.e.
\Psi^{-1}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is such that
\Psi^{-1}(\Psi(t))=t
for all
t\geq 0
.

Our first result is a characterisation of the class
\mathcal{M}_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}
for
q\in[1,\infty)
and for growth functions of order
p\in(1/2,1)
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Psi
be a growth function of order
p\in(1/2,1)
and let
q\in[1,\infty)
.

Let
\lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of complex numbers. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\lambda
is a multiplier from
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})
to
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
;

(2)

\lambda
satisfies the condition

A_{\lambda,\Psi,p,q}:=\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\Psi^{-1}(n)}^{q}|\lambda_{n}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}<\infty;

(3)

\lambda
satisfies the condition

\widetilde{A}_{\lambda,\Psi,p,q}:=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{\Psi^{-1}(2^{m})}{2^{m}}\left(\sum_{n=2^{m}}^{2^{m+1}-1}|\lambda_{n}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}<\infty.

If
\lambda\in\mathcal{M}_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}
then

\left\|\lambda\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}}\approx A_{\lambda,\Psi,p,q}\approx\widetilde{A}_{\lambda,\Psi,p,q},

where the implied constants depend only on
\Psi
,
p
,
q
and not on
\lambda
.

Theorem
1
includes Hardy–Orlicz spaces with growth functions of the form

(1.5)

\Psi_{r,p}(t):=\frac{t^{p}}{\log^{r}(t+e)},\quad t\geq 0,

for
p\in(1/2,1)
and
r\in[0,\infty)
. In the Hardy space case, i.e. for
r=0
;
H^{\Psi_{0,p}}(\mathbb{D})=H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, Theorem
1
recovers the characterisation of Duren and Shields for the class
\mathcal{M}_{H^{p}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}
when
p\in(1/2,1)
and
q\in[1,\infty)
.

Our next result extends Theorem
1
to growth functions of order
p=1
in the case where
q\in[1,2]
. More specifically, the following result holds true.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\Psi
be a growth function of order
p\in(1/2,1]
.

Let
\lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of complex numbers and let
q\in[1,2]
.

(1)

If
q\in[1,2)
, then the following are equivalent:

(a)

\lambda
is a multiplier from
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})
to
\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
;

(b)

\lambda
satisfies the condition

C_{\lambda,\Psi,p,q}:=\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\Psi^{-1}(N)}{N}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}|\lambda_{n}|^{q}\right)^{\frac{2}{(2-q)q}}\right)^{(2-q)/2}<\infty.

If
\lambda\in\mathcal{M}_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}
then

\left\|\lambda\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}}\approx C_{\lambda,\Psi,p,q},

where the implied constants depend only on
\Psi
,
p
,
q
and not on
\lambda
.

(2)

If
q=2
, the following are equivalent:

(a)

\lambda
is a multiplier from
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})
to
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
;

(b)

\lambda
satisfies the condition

B_{\lambda,\Psi,p,2}:=\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\Psi^{-1}(N)}{N}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}|\lambda_{n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}<\infty;

(c)

\lambda
satisfies the condition

\widetilde{B}_{\lambda,\Psi,p,2}:=\sup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\Psi^{-1}(2^{m})}{2^{m}}\left(\sum_{n=2^{m}}^{2^{m+1}-1}|\lambda_{n}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}<\infty.

If
\lambda\in\mathcal{M}_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}
then

\left\|\lambda\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}}\approx B_{\lambda,\Psi,p,2}\approx\widetilde{B}_{\lambda,\Psi,p,2},

where the implied constants depend only on
\Psi
and
p
, but not on
\lambda
.

Theorem
2
includes Hardy–Orlicz spaces with
\Psi_{r,p}
as in (
1.5
), with
r\in[0,\infty)
and
p\in(1/2,1]
, endpoint included. Note that if
r=0
and
p=1
, then
H^{\Psi_{0,1}}(\mathbb{D})=H^{1}(\mathbb{D})
, and condition (3) in part (b) of Theorem
2
becomes (
1.3
) that is, Theorem
2
recovers the aforementioned characterisation of Hardy–Littlewood and Stein–Zygmund for the class
\mathcal{M}_{H^{1}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}
.

Note that if
q\in[1,2)
and
\Psi
is a growth function of order
p\in(1/2,1)
, then conditions (2) and (3) in Theorem
1
and condition (ii) in part (a) of Theorem
2
are all equivalent. Similarly, for
q=2
, conditions (2) and (3) in Theorem
1
and conditions (ii) and (iii) in part (b) of Theorem
2
are all equivalent.

To prove Theorems
1
and
2
we use a real-variable approach that is based on duality. An important ingredient in our proofs is an unconditionality result; see Lemma
11
below.

Hardy–Littlewood and Paley-type inequalities

If
\Psi
is a growth function of order
p\in(1/2,1]
then the sequence
\lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
given by

\lambda_{n}:=\begin{cases}1/\Psi^{-1}(n),\quad&\text{if }n\in\mathbb{N};\\
0,\quad&\text{if }n=0\end{cases}

satisfies condition (2) in Theorem
2
. We thus obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3
.

Let
\Psi
be a growth function of order
p\in(1/2,1]
.
Then there exists a constant
M_{\Psi,p}>0
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left|f_{n}\right|}{\Psi^{-1}(n)}\leq M_{\Psi,p}\left\|F\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})}

for all
F(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}f_{n}z^{n}
in
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})
.

Corollary
3
implies a classical inequality due to Hardy and Littlewood
[
19
]
:

(1.6)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left|f_{n}\right|}{n}\lesssim\left\|F\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{D})}

as well as its ‘
H^{\log}
’-variant

(1.7)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left|f_{n}\right|}{n\log(n+1)}\lesssim\left\|F\right\|_{H^{\log}(\mathbb{D})}

that the present authors had obtained in
[
4
]
.

In the
q=2
case, Theorem
2
has the following corollary which may be of independent interest.

Corollary 4
.

Let
\Psi
be a growth function of order
p\in(1/2,1]
.
Then there exists a constant
B_{\Psi}>0
such that

\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2^{j}}{\Psi^{-1}(2^{j})}\right)^{2}|f_{2^{j}}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq B_{\Psi,p}\left\|F\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})}.

for all functions
F(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}z^{n}
in
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})
.

Let us examine what Corollary
4
implies in some special cases. If
\Psi_{r,1}
is as in (
1.5
), then its inverse function satisfies

(1.8)

\Psi^{-1}_{r,1}(t)\approx_{r}t\log^{r}(t+e),\quad t\geq 0,

where the implied constants depend only on
r
. Hence, when
r=0
, then Corollary
4
recovers a classical inequality due to R. E. A. C. Paley
[
29
]
:

(1.9)

\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}|f_{2^{j}}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\left\|F\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{D})}

for all functions
F(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}z^{n}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{D})
. The choice
r=1
corresponds to the Hardy–Orlicz space

H^{\log}(\mathbb{D}):=H^{\Psi_{1,1}}(\mathbb{D}).

In view of (
1.8
), Corollary
4
implies that

(1.10)

\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{|f_{2^{j}}|^{2}}{(j+1)^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\left\|F\right\|_{H^{\log}(\mathbb{D})}

for all functions
F(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}z^{n}
in
H^{\log}(\mathbb{D})
, which can be regarded as a natural variant of Paley’s inequality (
1.9
) for functions in
H^{\log}(\mathbb{D})
.

Dual spaces

It follows from the work of S. Janson
[
25
]
that the spaces
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})
and
BMO(\rho_{\Psi})(\mathbb{D})
can be put in duality. Recall that if

\rho_{\Psi}(t):=t^{-1}/\Psi^{-1}(t^{-1}),\quad t>0,

then
BMO(\rho_{\Psi})(\mathbb{D})
is the space of holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
whose boundary values belong to

BMO(\rho_{\Psi})(\mathbb{T}):=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}):\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset\mathbb{T}:\\
I\text{ arc }\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\left[\rho_{\Psi}(|I|)\right]^{2}|I|}\int_{I}\left|u(e^{2\pi i\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta<\infty\right\}.

Here, if
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
and
J\subset\mathbb{T}
is an arc,
\langle g\rangle_{J}
denotes the average of
g
over
J
. The class of functions on
\mathbb{T}
that are boundary values of functions in
BMO(\rho_{\Psi})(\mathbb{D})
is denoted by
BMOA(\rho_{\Psi})(\mathbb{T})
.

If
\Psi_{0,1}(t)=t
, then one recovers the usual BMO-type spaces, whereas the case
\Psi_{1,1}(t)=t/\log(t+e)
corresponds to LMO-type spaces.

Note that by appealing to Corollary
4
and duality, it follows that if
\{a_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence of complex numbers with

(1.11)

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\Psi^{-1}(2^{j})}{2^{j}}\right)^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}<\infty

then the lacunary trigonometric series

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}e^{i2\pi 2^{j}\theta}

is the Fourier series of a function in
BMOA(\rho_{\Psi})(\mathbb{T})
. In the case where
\Psi(t)=\Phi_{0,1}(t)=t
, condition (
1.11
) characterises lacunary Fourier series in
BMOA(\mathbb{T})
; see §6.3 (iii) in Chapter IV in
[
33
]
.
One might ask whether condition (
1.11
) actually characterises lacunary Fourier series in
BMOA(\rho_{\Psi})(\mathbb{T})
in general. As we will see below, it
does not
.

Littlewood–Paley characterisations

If the growth function is of the form
\Psi=\Psi_{r,1}
(i.e. as in (
1.5
) for
p=1
), implication
(3)\implies(1)
in part (b) of Theorem
2
can also be deduced from a Littlewood–Paley characterisation of
BMO(\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}})(\mathbb{T})
that is of independent interest.

To state this characterisation of
BMO(\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}})(\mathbb{T})
, we need to introduce some notation first. For
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, define

\delta_{n}:=\begin{cases}V_{1},\quad&\text{if }n=0;\\
V_{2^{n}}-V_{2^{n-1}},\quad&\text{if }n\in\mathbb{N},\end{cases}

where
V_{m}
denotes the de la Vallée Poussin kernel of order
m
; see §
2.4
below. For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, define the corresponding Littlewood–Paley projection by
\Delta_{n}(f):=\delta_{n}\ast f
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
r\geq 0
be a given exponent and let
\Psi_{r,1}
be as in (
1.5
) (for
p=1
).

If
u
is a function in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

u
belongs to
BMO(\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}})(\mathbb{T})
;

(2)

one has

(1.12)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq\mathbb{T}:\\
I\ {\rm arc}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\left[\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}}(|I|)\right]^{2}|I|}\int_{I}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\\
2^{n}>|I|^{-1}\end{subarray}}\left|\Delta_{n}(u)(e^{i2\pi\theta})\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta<\infty.

Moreover, one has

(1.13)

\left\|u\right\|_{BMO(\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}})^{+}(\mathbb{T})}\approx_{r}\\
\left|\int_{[0,1)}u(e^{i2\pi\theta})\mathrm{d}\theta\right|+\left\|u\right\|_{\Psi_{r,1},\ast},

where

\left\|u\right\|_{\Psi_{r,1},\ast}:=\left(\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq\mathbb{T}:\\
I\ {\rm arc}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\left[\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}}(|I|)\right]^{2}|I|}\int_{I}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\\
2^{n}>|I|^{-1}\end{subarray}}\left|\Delta_{n}(u)(e^{i2\pi\theta})\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}.

Theorem
5
generalises a well-known Littlewood–Paley characterisation of functions in
BMO(\mathbb{T})
due to M. Frazier and B. Jawerth
[
16
]
and appears to be, to the best of our knowledge, new. As we will see below, if
u
belongs to
BMO(\rho_{\Psi})(\mathbb{T})
then (
1.12
) holds for more general Littlewood–Paley-type partitions; see Theorem
12

for a precise statement of this fact. For the
BMO
-case this is implicit in the works of J. Bourgain
[
8
]
and J. L. Rubio de Francia
[
30
]
, see also M. T. Lacey
[
27
]
.

Moreover, Theorem
5
can be to used to improve condition (
1.11
). More specifically, Theorem
5
implies the following characterisation of lacunary Fourier series in
BMOA(\rho_{\Psi})(\mathbb{T})
in terms of the growth of their Fourier coefficients.

Corollary 6
.

Let
r\geq 0
be a given exponent.

Let
\{a_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of complex numbers. The following are equivalent:

(1)

The lacunary trigonometric series

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}e^{i2\pi 2^{j}\theta},\quad\theta\in[0,1),

is the Fourier series of a function in
BMOA(\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}})(\mathbb{T})
;

(2)

one has

(1.14)

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\left(\frac{\Psi_{r,1}^{-1}(N)}{N}\right)^{2}\sum_{j\geq\log N}|a_{j}|^{2}\right\}<\infty.

Note that if
\Psi
is not the identity map, then (
1.11
) is strictly stronger than (
1.14
).

To illustrate Corollary
6
, consider the case of
LMOA(\mathbb{T})
i.e. take
\Psi(t)=\Psi_{1,1}(t)=t/\log(t+e)
. Then the lacunary trigonometric series

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}e^{i2\pi 2^{j}\theta},\quad\theta\in[0,1),

with
\{a_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
being a sequence of complex numbers,
is the Fourier series of a function in
LMOA(\mathbb{T})
if, and only if,

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\left\{\log^{2}N\sum_{j\geq\log N}|a_{j}|^{2}\right\}<\infty.

For instance, the function
u
whose Fourier series is given by

(1.15)

u(e^{i2\pi\theta})\sim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j^{3/2}}e^{i2\pi 2^{j}\theta},\quad\theta\in[0,1),

belongs to
{\rm LMOA}(\mathbb{T})
. Note that the lacunary sequence in (
1.15
) does not satisfy (
1.11
) (for
\Psi=\Psi_{1,1}
).

Euclidean counterparts and applications

Corollary
3
, and our other results, have natural counterparts in the Euclidean setting. To formulate them we need to recall the definition of Hardy–Orlicz spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Following
[
38
]
, if
\Psi
is a growth function, let
\mathcal{F}_{m_{\Psi}}
denote the family of Schwartz functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by

\mathcal{F}_{m_{\Psi}}:=\left\{\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}:\\
|\beta|\leq m_{\Psi}+1\end{subarray}}(1+|x|)^{(m_{\Psi}+2)(d+1)}\left|\partial^{\beta}\phi(x)\right|\leq 1\right\},

where
m_{\Psi}
is an appropriate non-negative integer depending on
\Psi
; see p. 16 in
[
38
]
.
For
t>0
, we use the notation
\phi_{t}(x):=t^{-d}\phi(t^{-1}x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If
f
is a tempered distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define the corresponding non-tangential grand maximal function of
f
by

M_{\mathcal{F}_{m_{\Psi}}}^{\ast}[f](x):=\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{F}_{m_{\Psi}}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty):\\
|x-y|<t\end{subarray}}\left|f\ast\phi_{t}(y)\right|,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The Hardy–Orlicz space
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined as the class of all tempered distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi\left(M_{\mathcal{F}_{m_{\Psi}}}^{\ast}[f](x)\right)\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

If
f\in H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we set

\|f\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi\left(\lambda^{-1}M_{\mathcal{F}_{m_{\Psi}}}^{\ast}[f](x)\right)\mathrm{d}x\leq 1\right\}.

The local Hardy–Orlicz space
h^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is defined as the class of all tempered distributions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that are such that
\Psi(M_{\mathcal{F}_{m_{\Psi}},\mathrm{loc}}^{\ast}[f])\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where

M_{\mathcal{F}_{m_{\Psi}},\mathrm{loc}}^{\ast}[f](x):=\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{F}_{m_{\Psi}}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1):\\
|x-y|<t\end{subarray}}\left|f\ast\phi_{t}(y)\right|,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Note the restriction to
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,1)
in the first supremum on the right in the definition of
M^{*}_{\mathcal{F}_{m_{\Psi},\mathrm{loc}}}[f]
. For
f\in h^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, one sets

\|f\|_{h^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi\left(\lambda^{-1}M_{\mathcal{F}_{m_{\Psi}},\mathrm{loc}}^{\ast}[f](x)\right)\mathrm{d}x\leq 1\right\}.

Our next result implies a Euclidean analogue of Corollary
3
.

Theorem 7
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
be a given dimension and let
\Psi
be a growth function of order
p
with
p\in(d/(d+1),1]
.

Then there exists a constant
C_{d,\Psi,p}>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Psi\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|\right)}{\left|\xi\right|^{2d}}\mathrm{d}\xi\leq C_{d,\Psi,p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi\left(M_{\mathcal{F}_{m_{\Psi}}^{*}}[f](x)\right)\mathrm{d}x

for all
f\in H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

As a consequence of this result, we obtain in Corollary
28
, a Euclidean counterpart of Corollary
3
.

Notice that if
f
is a tempered distribution, then its Fourier transform does not necessarily coincide with a function. However (see e.g.
[
33
]
*§5.4 (a) Chapter III), if
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
p\in(0,1]
, its Fourier transform agrees with a continuous function and

\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|\lesssim_{d,p}\left|\xi\right|^{d(p^{-1}-1)}\left\|f\right\|_{h^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In Proposition
24
we give an extension of this result for distributions in
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and an analogous result for
h^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is obtained in Proposition
25
.

The Littlewood–Paley description of the Hardy space
h^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be stated in terms of the validity of the functional inequality (see §
5.1
for the notation)

(1.16)

\left\|\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}f\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

and its reversed counterpart. The expression in the left-hand side can be identified as the norm of
f
in the inhomogeneous Triebel–Lizorkin space
F^{0}_{p,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, allowing the inequality to be interpreted as saying that
h^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is embedded in
F^{0}_{p,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

In Theorem
20
we give an extension of (
1.16
) for
p=1
, obtaining that the space
h^{\Psi_{r,1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be embedded in the Triebel–Lizorkin space of generalised smoothness
F^{0,-r}_{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(see §
5.1
).

The classical Sobolev embedding yields that, given
p\in(0,1]
, if a distribution
f
satisfies
(1-\Delta)^{\frac{d}{2p}}f\in h^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
f
is a continuous and bounded function (see e.g.
[
15
]
*Theorem 2). Informally, this can be rephrased as saying that if
f
has its derivatives of order smaller or equal to
dp
in
h^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then
f
is a continuous and bounded function. As a direct consequence of Corollary
28
, we obtain an extension of this result for distributions in
h^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and in particular in
h^{\Psi_{r,p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, (see Corollaries
31
and
32
), involving derivatives of logarithmic order.

In the direction of obtaining an extension of (
1.16
) for
\Psi_{r,p}
for
p<1
, in Corollary
33
we establish a Sobolev-type embedding of
h^{\Psi_{r,p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into spaces of generalised smoothness. We later apply this to establish an embedding of those distributions in
h^{\Psi_{r,p}}(\mathbb{R})
,

, whose Fourier transforms are supported in

, into certain spaces of analytic functions in a halfplane (see Corollary
38
).

Notation and Background

The set of natural numbers is denoted by
\mathbb{N}
, the set of non-negative integers is denoted by
\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and the set of integers is denoted by
\mathbb{Z}
.

In what follows, we identify functions on
\mathbb{T}
with
1
-periodic functions in the usual way.

We denote the class of Schwartz functions by
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the class of all continuous functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
f(x)\rightarrow 0

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})

2.1.
Growth functions

We now give a formal definition of the growth functions used to define
H^{\Psi}
spaces.

Definition 8
.

Let
0<p\leq 1
. A function
\Psi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is called a growth function of order
p
if it has the following properties:

•

\Psi
is continuous and increasing, with
\Psi(0)=0
and

\lim_{t\to\infty}\Psi(t)=\infty
;

•

the function
t\mapsto t^{-1}\Psi(t)
is non-increasing on
(0,\infty)
;

•

\Psi
is of lower type
p
, namely, there exists a constant
c_{\Psi,p}>0
such that

\Psi(s\cdot t)\leq c_{\Psi,p}\cdot s^{p}\cdot\Psi(t)

for all
t\geq 0
and
s\in(0,1)
.

If
\Psi
is a growth function of order
p
for some
p\in(0,1]
, we say that
\Psi
is a growth function.

As noted in
[
6
]
, see also
[
37
]
, given a growth function
\Psi
of order
p
, then
\widetilde{\Psi}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}s^{-1}\Psi(s)\mathrm{d}s
is also a growth function of order
p
that is concave and point-wise equivalent to
\Psi
. Hence, in what follows, we may assume that the growth functions considered are always concave and hence, sub-additive.

In several parts of this paper we shall use the following standard fact: if
\Psi
is a growth function of order
p
then
t\mapsto t^{-l}\Psi(t)
is quasi-increasing with constant
c_{\Psi,p}
for all
l\in(0,p]
. Namely,

(2.1)

\frac{\Psi(s)}{s^{l}}\leq c_{\Psi,p}\frac{\Psi(t)}{t^{l}}

for all
0<s\leq t
. See
[
37
]
*Proposition 3.1 for further details.

2.2.
Hardy–Orlicz spaces

If
\Psi
is a growth function, then the ‘real-variable’ Hardy–Orlicz space
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{T})
consists of all distributions
f
on
\mathbb{T}
satisfying

\int_{[0,1)}\Psi\left(f^{\ast}(e^{i2\pi\theta})\right)\mathrm{d}\theta<\infty.

Here
f^{\ast}
denotes the non-tangential maximal function of
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
given by

f^{\ast}(e^{i2\pi\theta}):=\sup_{re^{i2\pi\phi}\in\Gamma(\theta)}\left|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}r^{|n|}\widehat{f}(n)e^{i2\pi n\phi}\right|,

where

\Gamma(\theta):=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z-e^{i2\pi\theta}|<2(1-|z|)\}

and
\widehat{f}(n):=\langle f,e_{n}\rangle
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, with
e_{n}(\theta):=e^{i2\pi n\theta}
,
\theta\in[0,1)
.

If
f\in H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{T})
, we set

\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{T})}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{[0,1)}\Psi\left(\lambda^{-1}f^{\ast}(e^{i2\pi\theta})\right)\mathrm{d}\theta\leq 1\right\}.

The analytic Hardy–Orlicz space
H^{\Psi}_{A}(\mathbb{T})
is defined as

H^{\Psi}_{A}(\mathbb{T}):=\left\{f\in H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{T}):\mathrm{supp}\left(\widehat{f}\right)\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}.

Recall that the Riesz projection
P
is the multiplier operator with symbol
\chi_{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. By arguing as in
[
38
]
*Chapter 4, one can show that
P
is bounded on
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{T})
and moreover, one can show that if
f\in H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{T})
then for
f_{1}:=P(f)
and
f_{2}:=f-f_{1}
one has that
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
,
f_{1},\overline{f_{2}}\in H^{\Psi}_{A}(\mathbb{T})
, and

(2.2)

\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{T})}\approx\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{H^{\Psi}_{A}(\mathbb{T})}+\left\|\overline{f_{2}}\right\|_{H^{\Psi}_{A}(\mathbb{T})}.

It is well-known that
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})
can be identified with
H^{\Psi}_{A}(\mathbb{T})
in the following way: if
F\in H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})
then it converges in the sense of distributions to an
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
that belongs to
H^{\Psi}_{A}(\mathbb{T})
with
\left\|F\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{T})}
and conversely, if
f\in H^{\Psi}_{A}(\mathbb{T})
then

F(re^{i2\pi\theta}):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r^{n}\widehat{f}(n)e^{i2\pi n\theta},\quad z=re^{i2\pi\theta}\in\mathbb{D},

is a well-defined function that belongs to
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})
and satisfies
\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\left\|F\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})}
; see, e.g.,
[
6
]
.

2.3.
Duality and atomic decompositions

Let
\Psi
be a growth function and let
\rho_{\Psi}
be as above, i.e.,

(2.3)

\rho_{\Psi}(t):=t^{-1}/\Psi^{-1}(t^{-1}),\quad t>0.

Let
q\in[1,\infty)
. It follows from the work of Viviani
[
37
]
that
u\in\mathrm{BMO}(\rho_{\Psi})(\mathbb{T})
if, and only if,

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq\mathbb{T}:\\
\text{ arc }\end{subarray}}\left\{\frac{1}{\left[\rho_{\Psi}(|I|)\right]^{q}|I|}\int_{I}\left|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I}\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}\theta\right\}<\infty

and moreover, one has

(2.4)

\left\|u\right\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{+}(\rho_{\Psi})(\mathbb{T})}\approx_{q}\\
\left|\int_{[0,1)}u(e^{i2\pi\theta})\mathrm{d}\theta\right|+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq\mathbb{T}:\\
\text{ arc }\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\left[\rho_{\Psi}(|I|)\right]^{q}|I|}\int_{I}\left|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I}\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/q}.

In particular,

(2.5)

\left\|u\right\|_{\mathrm{BMO}^{+}(\rho_{\Psi})(\mathbb{T})}\approx\\
\left|\int_{[0,1)}u(e^{i2\pi\theta})\mathrm{d}\theta\right|+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq\mathbb{T}:\\
\text{ arc }\end{subarray}}\left\{\Psi^{-1}(|I|^{-1})\int_{I}\left|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I}\right|\mathrm{d}\theta\right\}.

As was shown by Janson
[
25
]
, for a given growth function
\Psi
, the dual of
H^{\Psi}
can be identified with
BMO(\rho_{\Psi})(\mathbb{T})
; the choice of
\rho_{\Psi}
in (
2.3
) is made precisely so as to make this duality hold. Note that
\rho_{\Psi}
is a positive non-decreasing function
[
37
]
*Proposition 3.10. In
[
37
]
, B. Viviani proved the very useful result that the Hardy–Orlicz space
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
admits an atomic decomposition. As a consequence, she gave another proof of Janson’s result on duality.

Let us first recall the definition of atoms in the Euclidean setting.

Definition 9
.

Let
\Psi
be a growth function. A locally integrable function
a_{Q}
is said to be an atom in
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
associated to a cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
if

i)

\mathrm{supp}(a_{Q})\subset Q
;

ii)

\left|a_{Q}(x)\right|\leq\Psi^{-1}(1/\left|Q\right|)
for a.e.
x\in Q
;

iii)

\int_{Q}a_{Q}(x)\mathrm{d}x=0
.

Note here that

\left\|\chi_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{Q}\Psi(1/\lambda)\leq 1\right\}=1/\Psi^{-1}(1/\left|Q\right|)

and so, the second property in the definition of atoms can be rewritten as

\left\|a_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\left\|\chi_{Q}\right\|^{-1}_{L^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

A tempered distribution
f
belongs to
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if, and only if, there exists a sequence
\{b_{Q_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of multiples of
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-atoms such that

f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}b_{Q_{k}}

in the sense of distributions, and

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left|Q_{k}\right|\Psi\left(\left\|b_{Q_{k}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)<\infty.

Moreover, we have

\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx\Lambda_{\infty}\left(\{b_{Q_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\right),

where

\Lambda_{\infty}\left(\{b_{Q_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\right):=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left|Q_{k}\right|\Psi\left(\lambda^{-1}\left\|b_{Q_{k}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

An analogous characterisation holds in the periodic setting.

As for
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the local Hardy–Orlicz space
h^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
admits an atomic decomposition (see for instance
[
38
]
*§8.3). Let us recall that a locally integrable function
a_{Q}
is an atom in
h^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
associated to a cube
Q
if it satisfies i), ii) from Definition
9
above, and

iii)’

\int_{Q}a_{Q}(x)\mathrm{d}x=0
, when
\left|Q\right|<1
.

Similarly as for
H^{\psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, a tempered distribution
f
belongs to
h^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if, and only if, there exist a sequence
\{\beta_{Q_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of multiples of atoms in
h^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, such that

f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\beta_{Q_{k}}

in the sense of distributions and

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left|Q_{k}\right|\Psi\left(\left\|\beta_{Q_{k}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)<\infty.

Moreover,

\left\|f\right\|_{h^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx\Lambda_{\infty}\left(\{\beta_{Q_{k}}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\right).

2.4.
Well-distributed intervals and Littlewood–Paley-type projections

In this paper, we shall consider ‘frequency’ intervals that arise from Whitney-type decompositions of arbitrary intervals. To be more specific, following
[
30
]
, if
K=[c_{K}-|K|/2,c_{K}+|K|/2]
is a non-empty interval, then its Whitney decomposition
W(I)
consists of the families of intervals

\mathbf{K}^{\rm left}:=\left\{\left[c_{K}-\frac{|K|}{2}+\frac{2^{-(l+1)}}{3}|K|,c_{K}-\frac{|K|}{2}+\frac{2^{-l}}{3}|K|\right)\right\}_{l\in\mathbb{Z}},

\mathbf{K}^{\rm centre}:=\left\{\left[c_{K}-\frac{|K|}{6},c_{K}+\frac{|K|}{6}\right]\right\},

and

\mathbf{K}^{\rm right}:=\left\{\left(c_{K}+\frac{|K|}{2}-\frac{2^{-l}}{3}|K|,c_{K}+\frac{|K|}{2}-\frac{2^{-(l+1)}}{3}|K|\right]\right\}_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}.

Note that for any given non-empty interval
K
interval, the intervals in
W(K)
are mutually disjoint and satisfy the properties

\left\|\sum_{J\in W(K)}\chi_{2J}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 5\qquad\text{and}\qquad 2J\subseteq K\quad\text{for all }J\in W(K).

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
K_{n}
denote the usual Fejér kernel of order
n
, namely,

K_{n}(e^{i2\pi\theta}):=\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\left(1-\frac{|j|}{n+1}\right)e^{i2\pi j\theta},\quad\theta\in[0,1).

Then the de la Vallée Poussin kernel of order
m\in\mathbb{N}
is given by

V_{m}:=2K_{2m+1}-K_{m}

and satisfies
\widehat{V_{m}}(j)=1
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|j|\leq m+1
, and
\widehat{V_{m}}(j)=0
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|j|\geq 2m+1
. Moreover,
\widehat{V_{m}}
is even and ‘affine’ on
[m+1,2m+1]\cap\mathbb{Z}
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we set

\sigma_{m}:=V_{2m}-V_{m}.

Note that
\widehat{\sigma_{m}}(j)=0
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|j|\leq m+1
or
|j|\geq 4m+1
. Moreover,
\widehat{\sigma_{m}}
is even and ‘affine’ on
[m+1,2m+1]\cap\mathbb{Z}
and on
[2m+1,4m+1]\cap\mathbb{Z}
.

Suppose now that
K
is an interval with
|K|=3\cdot 2^{l+1}
for some
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and moreover, its endpoints
l_{K}:=c_{K}-|K|/2
and
r_{K}:=c_{K}+|K|/2
are integers. Let
W(K)
denote the Whitney decomposition of
K
. If
J\in\mathbf{K}^{\rm left}
and
|J|\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\delta_{J}(e^{i2\pi\theta}):=e^{i2\pi r_{J}\theta}e^{-i2\pi(2m)\theta}\sigma_{|J|}(e^{i2\pi\theta}),\quad\theta\in[0,1),

where
r_{J}
denotes the right endpoint of
J
. Similarly, if
J\in\mathbf{K}^{\rm right}
and
|J|\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\delta_{J}(e^{i2\pi\theta}):=e^{i2\pi l_{J}\theta}e^{i2\pi(2m)\theta}\sigma_{|J|}(e^{i2\pi\theta}),\quad\theta\in[0,1),

where
l_{J}
is the left endpoint of
J
.

For
J\in\mathbf{K}^{\rm left}\cup\mathbf{K}^{\rm right}
and
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, we set
\Delta_{J}(f):=\delta_{J}\ast f
.

3.
Proofs of Theorems
1
and
2

In this section we prove Theorems
1
and
2
.
To this end, we shall first establish the following characterisation of
\mathcal{M}_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}
.

Proposition 10
.

Let
\Psi
be a growth function of order
p\in(1/2,1]
.

Let
\lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a sequence of complex numbers.
The following are equivalent:

(1)

\lambda
is a multiplier from
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})
to
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
;

(2)

There exists a constant
A_{\lambda,\Psi,p}>0
such that

\Gamma_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}(z):=\sum_{n=0}^{M}\epsilon_{n}\lambda_{n}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

belongs to
BMO(\rho_{\Psi})(\mathbb{D})
with

\left\|\Gamma_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}\right\|_{BMO(\rho_{\Psi})(\mathbb{D})}\leq A_{\lambda,\Psi,p}

for every choice of
\epsilon=\{\epsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with
|\epsilon_{n}|\leq 1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and for all
M\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Proof.

To prove
(2)\implies(1)
, fix a function

F(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

in
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})
. We shall prove that there exists a constant
C_{\lambda,\Psi,p}>0
, depending only on
\lambda
,
\Psi
, and
p
, such that

(3.1)

\sum_{n=0}^{M}r^{n}|\lambda_{n}f_{n}|\leq C_{\lambda,\Psi,p}\left\|F\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})}

for all
r\in(0,1)
and for all
M\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The desired inequality then follows from (
3.1
) and a limiting argument.

In order to establish (
3.1
), fix an
r\in(0,1)
and define
F_{r}
by

F_{r}(z):=F(rz)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}r^{n}f_{n}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Note that

(3.2)

\left\|F_{r}\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})}\leq\left\|F\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})}.

Indeed, by the definition of the norm and a change of variables,

\displaystyle\left\|F_{r}\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{\alpha>0:\sup_{0\leq\rho<1}\int_{[0,1)}\Psi\left(\alpha^{-1}\left|F\left(r\rho e^{i2\pi\theta}\right)\right|\right)\mathrm{d}\theta\leq 1\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\inf\left\{\alpha>0:\sup_{0\leq\sigma<1}\int_{[0,1)}\Psi\left(\alpha^{-1}\left|F\left(\sigma e^{i2\pi\theta}\right)\right|\right)\mathrm{d}\theta\leq 1\right\}=\left\|F\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})}.

Fix an
\epsilon=\{\epsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with
|\epsilon_{n}|\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and an
M\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We may write

(3.3)

\left|\sum_{n=0}^{M}\epsilon_{n}r^{n}\lambda_{n}f_{n}\right|=\left|\langle F_{r},\Gamma_{\epsilon,\lambda,0}^{(M)}\rangle\right|,

where
\Gamma_{\epsilon,\lambda,0}^{(M)}
denotes the analytic trigonometric polynomial, which is the boundary value of
\Gamma_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}
.
By our assumption

\left\|\Gamma_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}\right\|_{BMO(\rho_{\Psi})(\mathbb{D})}\lesssim_{\lambda,\Psi,p}1,

where the implied constant is independent of the choice of
\epsilon=\{\epsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and
M\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Hence, it follows from (
3.3
) that

\left|\sum_{n=0}^{M}\epsilon_{n}r^{n}\lambda_{n}f_{n}\right|\lesssim_{\lambda,\Psi,p}\left\|F_{r}\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})},

which, combined with (
3.2
), yields

(3.4)

\left|\sum_{n=0}^{M}\epsilon_{n}r^{n}\lambda_{n}f_{n}\right|\leq C_{\lambda,\Psi,p}\left\|F\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})},

where
C_{\lambda,\Psi,p}>0
is independent of
\epsilon=\{\epsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and
M\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Therefore, if we take

\epsilon_{n}:=\begin{cases}\frac{\overline{f_{n}}}{|f_{n}|}\cdot\frac{\overline{\lambda_{n}}}{|\lambda_{n}|},\quad&\text{if }f_{n}\neq 0;\\
0,\quad&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

in (
3.4
), we deduce that (
3.1
) holds.

To prove the reverse implication, suppose that
\lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
satisfies
(1)
, i.e.
\lambda
is a multiplier from
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{D})
to
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
.

For
\epsilon=\{\epsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with
|\epsilon_{n}|\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
M\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, consider the trigonometric polynomial
b_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}
with Fourier coefficients given by

\widehat{b_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}}(n):=\begin{cases}\epsilon_{n}\lambda_{n},\quad&\text{if }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\text{ with }n\leq M;\\
0\quad&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

To prove
(2)
, it suffices, in view of (
2.5
), to show that

(3.5)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq\mathbb{T}:\\
\text{arc}\end{subarray}}\left\{\Psi^{-1}(|I|^{-1})\int_{I}\left|b_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle b_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}\rangle_{I}\right|\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right\}\leq A_{\lambda,\Psi,p};

our arguments are in part inspired by a trick in
[
26
]
*p. 946.
To establish (
3.5
), fix an arc
I
in
\mathbb{T}
and define

a_{I}:=\frac{\Psi^{-1}(|I|^{-1})}{2}\left(e^{i\arg\left\{b_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}-\langle b_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}\rangle_{I}\right\}}-\langle e^{i\arg\left\{b_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}-\langle b_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}\rangle_{I}\right\}}\rangle_{I}\right)\chi_{I}.

Then,
\mathrm{supp}(a_{I})\subseteq I
,
\int_{I}a_{I}=0
,
\left\|a_{I}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\Psi^{-1}(|I|^{-1})
and so,
a_{I}
is an
H^{\Psi}
-atom. Moreover,

\displaystyle\Psi^{-1}(|I|^{-1})\int_{I}\left|b_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle b_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}\rangle_{I}\right|\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=2\left|\int_{I}\left(b_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle b_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}\rangle_{I}\right)\overline{a_{I}(e^{i2\pi\theta})}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right|.

Hence, Parseval’s identity yields

\Psi^{-1}(|I|^{-1})\int_{I}\left|b_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle b_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}\rangle_{I}\right|\mathrm{d}\theta\leq 2\sum_{n=0}^{M}|\lambda_{n}\widehat{a}_{I}(n)|

and so, our assumption on
\lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
implies that

(3.6)

\Psi^{-1}(|I|^{-1})\int_{I}\left|b_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle b_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}\rangle_{I}\right|\mathrm{d}\theta\leq 2C_{\lambda,\Psi,p}\left\|a_{I}\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{T})}.

Since
a_{I}
is an
H^{\Psi}
-atom, (

) follows from (
3.6
). ∎

As to the equivalence of (1) and (2), we start by observing that it suffices to prove the case

(3.7)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{I}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\lambda_{n}|\left(\sin(2\pi n\theta)-\langle\sin(2\pi n\cdot)\rangle_{I}\right)\right|\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I}|b_{N}(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle b_{N}\rangle_{I}|\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\sum_{n=1}^{N}|\lambda_{n}|\frac{n}{N^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\Psi^{-1}(|I|^{-1})}.

Now, since

Conversely, suppose that (2) holds for

We write

\Gamma_{\epsilon,\lambda}^{(M)}=K_{\varepsilon,\lambda}+N_{\varepsilon,\lambda},

We consider first

\displaystyle\int_{I}\left|K_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle K_{\varepsilon,\lambda}\rangle_{I}\right|\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}\int_{I}\left|K_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(e^{i2\pi\theta})-K_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(e^{i2\pi\phi})\right|\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\phi

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n\leq|I|^{-1}}n|\lambda_{n}|\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}\int_{I}\left|\theta-\phi\right|\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\phi

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n\leq|I|^{-1}}n|\lambda_{n}||I|^{2}

where we used that, since

where we have used orthogonality in the first inequality and Lemma

is of upper type

Hence, (

Lemma 11
.

If
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then for every interval
I
with
\left|I\right|=2^{-n}
and for every analytic trigonometric polynomial

,

(1)

\int_{I}\left|f(e^{i2\pi\theta})\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\lesssim\left|I\right|\left(\sum_{j=0}^{2^{n}-1}\left|f_{j}\right|\right)^{2}+\left|I\right|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=2^{k}2^{n}}^{2^{k+1}2^{n}-1}\left|f_{j}\right|\right)^{2},

(2)

\int_{I}\left|f(e^{i2\pi\theta})\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\lesssim\left|I\right|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=k2^{n}}^{(k+1)2^{n}-1}\left|f_{j}\right|\right)^{2},

where

Proof.

Let

.
We remark that Part (

estimate, so it suffices to prove Part (
2

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{I}f(e^{i2\pi\theta})\overline{f(e^{i2\pi\theta})}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=k_{1}2^{n}}^{(k_{1}+1)2^{n}-1}\sum_{l=k_{2}2^{n}}^{(k_{2}+1)2^{n}-1}f_{j}\overline{f_{l}}\int_{I}e^{i2\pi(j-l)\theta}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle=\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{II},

where

\mathrm{I}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0:\\
|k_{1}-k_{2}|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\sum_{j=k_{1}2^{n}}^{(k_{1}+1)2^{n}-1}\sum_{l=k_{2}2^{n}}^{(k_{2}+1)2^{n}-1}f_{j}\overline{f_{l}}\int_{I}e^{i2\pi(j-l)\theta}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

and

\mathrm{II}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0:\\
|k_{1}-k_{2}|\geq 2\end{subarray}}\sum_{j=k_{1}2^{n}}^{(k_{1}+1)2^{n}-1}\sum_{l=k_{2}2^{n}}^{(k_{2}+1)2^{n}-1}f_{j}\overline{f_{l}}\int_{I}e^{i2\pi(j-l)\theta}\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

Term
\mathrm{I}
may easily be estimated by

|\mathrm{I}|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0:\\
|k_{1}-k_{2}|\leq 1\end{subarray}}\sum_{j=k_{1}2^{n}}^{(k_{1}+1)2^{n}-1}\sum_{l=k_{2}2^{n}}^{(k_{2}+1)2^{n}-1}|f_{j}|\cdot|f_{l}|\cdot 2^{-n}\leq 3\cdot 2^{-n}\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(\sum_{j=k2^{n}}^{(k+1)2^{n}-1}|f_{j}|\right)^{2}.

For term
\mathrm{II}
, consider first the case

. Then

\displaystyle\text{III}+\text{IV},

where

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0:\\

.

, we observe that

\left|\frac{j_{2}-j_{1}}{i2\pi(2^{n}(k_{1}-k_{2})+(j_{1}-j_{2}))2^{n}(k_{1}-k_{2})}\right|\leq 2\cdot 2^{-n}\frac{1}{2\pi(k_{1}-k_{2})^{2}}

for

and

as can be verified e.g. by appealing to the Schur test.

\text{VII}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0:\\

and

\text{VIII}:=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2}\geq 0:\\
|k_{1}-k_{2}|\geq 2\end{subarray}}\sum_{j=k_{1}2^{n}}^{(k_{1}+1)2^{n}-1}\sum_{l=k_{2}2^{n}}^{(k_{2}+1)2^{n}-1}e^{i2\pi j(a+2^{-n})}f_{j}\overline{e^{i2\pi l(a+2^{-n})}f_{l}}\,\frac{1}{i2\pi(j-l)}.

and

have exactly the same form as term

, just with unimodular factors on the Fourier coefficients

. This observation combined with our previous estimates finishes the proof of the lemma. ∎

Proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\int_{I}\left|N_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle N_{\varepsilon,\lambda}\rangle_{I}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I}\left|N_{\varepsilon,\lambda}(e^{i2\pi\theta})\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\lesssim|I|\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{(k+1)|I|^{-1}\geq n>k|I|^{-1}}|\lambda_{n}|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|I|^{-1}}{(\Psi^{-1}(|I|^{-1}))^{2}}

by condition
(ii)
, where we have now used the full power of Lemma
11
, namely Part (
2
).

For the reverse estimate, note that by Proposition
10

the function

b_{\varepsilon}(e^{i2\pi\theta})=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\varepsilon_{k}\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}e^{i2\pi n\theta}|\lambda_{n}|=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\varepsilon_{k}b_{k}(e^{i2\pi\theta})

is in
BMO(\rho_{\Psi})(\mathbb{D})
, uniformly
for every choice of
\epsilon=\{\epsilon_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
with
|\epsilon_{k}|\leq 1
and for all
M\in\mathbb{N}
, so in particular for
\varepsilon_{k}\in\{-1,1\}
.
As above, we choose
I=[0,(4N)^{-1}]
. Here,

b_{k}(e^{i2\pi\theta}):=\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}e^{i2\pi n\theta}|\lambda_{n}|,\quad\theta\in[0,1).

An integration over the standard product probability space

\Omega=\{-1,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
and an application of Khintchine’s Theorem (see, e.g.,
[
18
]
*Appendix C) thus yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Psi^{-1}(|I|^{-1})}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\int_{\Omega}\int_{I}\left|b_{\varepsilon}(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle b_{\varepsilon}\rangle_{I}\right|\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\approx\left(\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left(\int_{I}\left|\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}e^{i2\pi n\theta}|\lambda_{n}|-\langle b_{k}\rangle_{I}\right|\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left(\int_{I}\left|\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}e^{i2\pi(n-kN)\theta}|\lambda_{n}|-e^{-i2\pi kN\theta}\langle b_{k}\rangle_{I}\right|\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\left(\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left(\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}|\lambda_{n}|\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\frac{1}{N},

where we used a similar argument as in the proof of (
3.8
) for the last
inequality. To make this precise, we note that for any
k
,

\left|\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}e^{i2\pi n\theta}|\lambda_{n}|\right|=\left|\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}e^{i2\pi(n-kN)\theta}|\lambda_{n}|\right|\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}|\lambda_{n}|

for all
\theta\in I
. If

(3.11)

|\langle b_{k}\rangle_{I}|\leq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}|\lambda_{n}|,

this implies

(3.12)

\int_{I}\left|\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}e^{i2\pi n\theta}|\lambda_{n}|-\langle b_{k}\rangle_{I}\right|\,\mathrm{d}\theta\geq|I|\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}|\lambda_{n}|.

Inequality (
3.11
), and therefore (
3.12
), hold in particular for
k\geq 4
.

If

|\langle b_{k}\rangle_{I}|>\frac{1}{4}\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}|\lambda_{n}|,

then
k\leq 3
. Choosing

\tilde{I}=\left[0,\frac{1}{16N}\right]\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{\tilde{I}}=\left[0,\frac{1}{64N}\right],

we note that on
\tilde{I}
, all terms in
b_{k}

only take values in the first quadrant of
\mathbb{C}
and hence

\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{I}}\left|\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}e^{i2\pi n\theta}|\lambda_{n}|-\langle b_{k}\rangle_{\tilde{I}}\right|\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{I}}\left|\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}\sin(2\pi n\theta)|\lambda_{n}|-\langle\sin(2\pi n\theta)\rangle_{\tilde{I}}|\lambda_{n}|\right|\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{\tilde{I}}}\left|\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}\left(\langle\sin(2\pi n\theta)\rangle_{\tilde{I}}-\sin(2\pi n\theta)\right)|\lambda_{n}|\right|\,\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}\frac{n}{N}|\lambda_{n}|

\displaystyle\gtrsim

\displaystyle|I|\sum_{n=kN}^{(k+1)N-1}|\lambda_{n}|,

where we apply the estimate (
3.7
) in the penultimate inequality.
∎

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
12

By translation invariance, to prove Theorem
12
it suffices to show that

(4.1)

\frac{1}{\left[\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}}(|I|)\right]^{2}|I|}\int_{I}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{K}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in W(K)\setminus\mathbf{K}^{\rm centre}:\\
|J|>|I|^{-1}\end{subarray}}\left|\Delta_{J}(u)(e^{i2\pi\theta})\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\lesssim_{r}\\
\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{BMO(\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}})(\mathbb{T})},

for any fixed interval of the form
I=[-\delta/2,\delta/2)
,
\delta\in(0,1]
. Since any de la Vall’ee Poussin-type kernel can be written as a linear combination of two modulated Fejér-type kernels (see §
2.4
), (
4.1
) is a direct consequence of the following lemma, which is a variant of
[
8
]
*lemma 2.

Lemma 13
.

Let
r\geq 0
be a given exponent. For
\delta\in(0,1]
, consider the interval
I_{\delta}:=[-\delta/2,\delta/2)
.

Let
\{F_{\alpha}\}_{\in\mathcal{A}}
be a finite collection of trigonometric polynomials of the form

F_{\alpha}(e^{i2\pi\theta})=e^{i2\pi c_{\alpha}\theta}K_{N_{\alpha}}(e^{i2\pi\theta}),\quad\theta\in[-1/2,1/2),

where
N_{\alpha}>\delta^{-1}
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
and moreover, the frequency supports of
F_{\alpha}
and
F_{\alpha^{\prime}}
are disjoint for
\alpha\neq\alpha^{\prime}
and
0\notin\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{F_{\alpha}})
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
.

Then

\frac{1}{\left[\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}}(|I_{\delta}|)\right]^{2}|I_{\delta}|}\int_{I_{\delta}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\left|(F_{\alpha}\ast u)(e^{i2\pi\theta})\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\lesssim_{r}\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{BMO(\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}})(\mathbb{T})}

for all
u\in BMO(\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}})(\mathbb{T})
, where the implied constant depends only on
r
and not on
\delta
,
\mathcal{A}
, and
u
.

Proof.

Let
u
be a given function in
BMO(\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}})(\mathbb{T})
. If

\omega_{\delta}(\theta):=\frac{\delta^{2}}{\delta^{2}+\sin^{2}(\pi\theta)},\quad\theta\in[-1/2,1/2),

then
[
8
]
*(6) (in the proof of
[
8
]
*lemma 2) applied to
g=u-\langle u\rangle_{I_{\delta}}
asserts that there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that

(4.2)

\int_{[-1/2,1/2)}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\left|(F_{\alpha}\ast u)(e^{i2\pi\theta})\right|^{2}\right)\omega_{\delta}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\\
\leq C\int_{[-1/2,1/2)}|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I_{\delta}}|^{2}\omega_{\delta}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta,

where we also used the assumption that
0\notin\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{F_{\alpha}})
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
. Notice that
[
8
]
*(6) is a consequence of
[
8
]
*Lemma 1, which is a Cotlar-type almost orthogonality result.

To prove the desired estimate, note that since
\omega_{\delta}(\theta)\geq 1/(2\pi^{2})
for all
\theta\in I_{\delta}
, (
4.2
) yields

(4.3)

\frac{1}{\left[\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}}(|I_{\delta}|)\right]^{2}|I_{\delta}|}\int_{I_{\delta}}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\left|(F_{\alpha}\ast u)(e^{i2\pi\theta})\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta\lesssim\\
\frac{1}{\left[\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}}(|I_{\delta}|)\right]^{2}|I_{\delta}|}\int_{[-1/2,1/2)}|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I_{\delta}}|^{2}\omega_{\delta}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

Hence, in view of (
4.3
), it suffices to show that

(4.4)

\frac{1}{\left[\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}}(|I_{\delta}|)\right]^{2}|I_{\delta}|}\int_{[-1/2,1/2)}|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I_{\delta}}|^{2}\omega_{\delta}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\lesssim\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{BMO(\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}})(\mathbb{T})}.

As
\omega_{\delta}(\theta)\leq 1
for all
\theta\in[-1/2,1/2)
, we have

(4.5)

\frac{1}{\left[\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}}(|I_{\delta}|)\right]^{2}|I_{\delta}|}\int_{I_{\delta}}|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I_{\delta}}|^{2}\omega_{\delta}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\lesssim\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{BMO(\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}})(\mathbb{T})}.

To handle the contribution of the term involving the part of the integral on the left-hand side of (
4.4
) over
[-1/2,1/2)\setminus I_{\delta}
, note that as
\sin(t)/t\geq 2/\pi
for
t\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]\setminus\{0\}
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left[\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}}(|I_{\delta}|)\right]^{2}|I_{\delta}|}\int_{[-1/2,-\delta/2)\cup[\delta/2,1/2)}|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I_{\delta}}|^{2}\omega_{\delta}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{\log^{2r}(\delta^{-1})}{\delta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\\
2^{k}\leq\delta^{-1}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2^{2k}}\int_{2^{k-1}\delta\leq|\theta|\leq 2^{k}\delta}|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I_{\delta}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta.

Since
2^{k}\leq\delta^{-1}
, we have

\log\left(\delta^{-1}\right)\leq k\log\left((2^{k-1}\delta)^{-1}\right)

and so,

\displaystyle\frac{\log^{2r}(\delta^{-1})}{\delta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\\
2^{k}\leq\delta^{-1}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{2^{2k}}\int_{2^{k-1}\delta\leq|\theta|\leq 2^{k}\delta}|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I_{\delta}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\lesssim_{r}

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\\
2^{k}\leq\delta^{-1}\end{subarray}}\frac{k^{2r}}{2^{k}}\left(\frac{\log^{2r}\left((2^{k}\delta)^{-1}\right)}{2^{k}\delta}\int_{2^{k-1}\delta\leq|\theta|\leq 2^{k}\delta}|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I_{\delta}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right).

A standard argument now yields

(4.6)

\left(\frac{\log^{2r}\left((2^{k}\delta)^{-1}\right)}{2^{k}\delta}\int_{2^{k-1}\delta\leq|\theta|\leq 2^{k}\delta}|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I_{\delta}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\\
k\left\|u\right\|_{BMO(\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}})(\mathbb{T})}.

Indeed, it follows from the triangle inequality that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\log^{2r}\left((2^{k}\delta)^{-1}\right)}{2^{k}\delta}\int_{2^{k-1}\delta\leq|\theta|\leq 2^{k}\delta}|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I_{\delta}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}\lesssim_{r}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k}\left(\frac{\log^{2r}\left((2^{k}\delta)^{-1}\right)}{2^{l}\delta}\int_{2^{l}I_{\delta}}|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{2^{l+1}I_{\delta}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}

where
2^{l}I_{\delta}=[-2^{l-1}\delta,2^{l-1}\delta)
. Using (
4.6
) we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left[\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}}(|I_{\delta}|)\right]^{2}|I_{\delta}|}\int_{[-1/2,-\delta/2)\cup[\delta/2,1/2)}|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I_{\delta}}|^{2}\omega_{\delta}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\lesssim_{r}

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{BMO(\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}})(\mathbb{T})}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{k^{2r+2}}{2^{2k}}

and so,

(4.7)

\frac{1}{\left[\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}}(|I_{\delta}|)\right]^{2}|I_{\delta}|}\int_{[-1/2,-\delta/2)\cup[\delta/2,1/2)}|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I_{\delta}}|^{2}\omega_{\delta}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\lesssim_{r}\\
\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{BMO(\rho_{\Psi_{r,1}})(\mathbb{T})},

as desired.

It thus follows from (
4.5
) and (
4.7
) that (
4.4
) holds true and hence, the proof of the lemma is complete.
∎

4.2.
Proof of that (2) implies (1) in Theorem
5

The proof that we present below is an adaptation of the corresponding argument in
[
36
]
for the
BMO
-case.

Let
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
be such that (2) in Theorem
5
holds. It suffices to prove that for any fixed arc
I
in
\mathbb{T}
one has

(4.8)

M_{I}\left(\int_{I}\left|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}\lesssim_{r}\left\|u\right\|_{\Psi_{r,1},\ast},

where
M_{I}:=|I|^{-1/2}\log^{r}(|I|^{-1})
and
\left\|u\right\|_{\Psi_{r,1},\ast}
is as in the statement of Theorem
5
. Note that for any
c\in\mathbb{C}
one has

\left(\int_{I}\left|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-\langle u\rangle_{I}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}\leq 2\left(\int_{I}\left|u(e^{i2\pi\theta})-c\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}.

Hence, if we choose

c:=\sum_{2^{n}\leq|I|^{-1}}\Delta_{n}(u)(e^{i2\pi c_{I}}),

with
c_{I}
being the centre of
I
, then it suffices to prove that

(4.9)

U\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\Psi_{r,1},\ast}\quad\text{for }U=A\text{ and }U=B,

where

A:=M_{I}\left(\int_{I}\left|\sum_{2^{n}>|I|^{-1}}\Delta_{n}(u)(e^{i2\pi\theta})\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}

and

B:=M_{I}\left(\int_{I}\left|\sum_{2^{n}\leq|I|^{-1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(e^{i2\pi\sigma})\left[\delta_{n}\left(e^{i2\pi(\theta-\sigma)}\right)-\delta_{n}\left(e^{i2\pi(c_{I}-\sigma)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right]\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}.

Note that if, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
\widetilde{\delta}_{n}:=\delta_{n-1}+\delta_{n}+\delta_{n+1}
, then
\Delta_{n}=\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}\Delta_{n}
. Here, for
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
,
\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}(f):=\widetilde{\delta}_{n}\ast f
. In what follows, we shall use several times that
\int_{K}|\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}(u)|^{2}\lesssim 2^{-n}n^{-2r}\left\|u\right\|_{\Psi_{r,1},\ast}
for any arc
K
with
|K|\approx 2^{-n}
(cf.
[
36
]
*Lemma 3.1).

We may assume, without loss of generality, that
I
is of the form
[-|I|/2,|I|/2)
.

4.2.1.
Proof of (
4.9
) for
U=A

By the triangle inequality, we have
A\leq A_{1}+A_{2}
,
where

A_{1}:=M_{I}\left(\int_{I}\left|\sum_{2^{n}>|I|^{-1}}\int_{\mathbb{T}\setminus I}\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}(u)(e^{i2\pi\sigma})\delta_{n}\left(e^{i2\pi(\theta-\sigma)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}

and

A_{2}:=M_{I}\left(\int_{I}\left|\sum_{2^{n}>|I|^{-1}}\int_{I}\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}(u)(e^{i2\pi\sigma})\delta_{n}\left(e^{i2\pi(\theta-\sigma)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}.

Estimate for
A_{1}

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
I_{n}^{-}
denote the interval that is concentric with
I
and has length
|I_{n}^{-}|=|I|-2^{-n}
. We then have
A_{1}\leq A_{1,1}+A_{1,2}
,
where

A_{1,1}:=M_{I}\sum_{2^{n}>|I|^{-1}}\left(\int_{I_{n}^{-}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}\setminus I}\left|\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}(u)(e^{i2\pi\sigma})\right|\cdot\left|\delta_{n}\left(e^{i2\pi(\theta-\sigma)}\right)\right|\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}

and

A_{1,2}:=M_{I}\sum_{2^{n}>|I|^{-1}}\left(\int_{I\setminus I_{n}^{-}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}\setminus I}\left|\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}(u)(e^{i2\pi\sigma})\right|\cdot\left|\delta_{n}\left(e^{i2\pi(\theta-\sigma)}\right)\right|\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}.

The terms
A_{1,1}
and
A_{1,2}
will be estimated separately.

Estimate for
A_{1,1}

Without loss of generality we may assume that
|I|=2^{-\zeta_{0}}
for some
\zeta_{0}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Hence, we may write
I_{n}^{-}=\bigcup_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{n}}Q
and
\mathbb{T}\setminus I=\bigcup_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}P
, where
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
and
\mathcal{P}_{n}
are families of mutually disjoint arcs of length
2^{-n-2}
. Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
we have

A_{1,1}\lesssim M_{I}\sum_{2^{n}>|I|^{-1}}\left(K_{n}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{n}}\int_{Q}\left(\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\int_{P}\frac{\left|\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}(u)(e^{i2\pi\sigma})\right|^{2}}{\sin^{2}(\pi(\theta-\sigma))}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2},

where

K_{n}:=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\int_{P}\sin^{2}(\pi(\theta-\sigma))\left|\delta_{n}\left(e^{i2\pi(\theta-\sigma)}\right)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma.

As
\phi\mapsto\sin^{2}(\pi\phi)
is
1
-periodic and
|\sin(t)|\leq t
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
K_{n}\lesssim a_{n}+b_{n}
,
where

a_{n}:=\int_{|\phi|\leq 2^{-n}}\phi^{2}\left|\delta_{n}\left(e^{i2\pi\phi}\right)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\phi\quad\text{and}\quad b_{n}:=\int_{2^{-n}<|\phi|\leq 1/2}\phi^{2}\left|\delta_{n}\left(e^{i2\pi\phi}\right)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\phi.

Since
\left\|\delta_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\lesssim 2^{n}
, we have

a_{n}\lesssim 2^{-n}
.
To estimate
b_{n}
, note that by the properties of the Fejér kernel we have
\left|\delta_{n}\left(e^{i2\pi\phi}\right)\right|\lesssim 2^{-n}\phi^{-2}
for all
2^{-n}<|\phi|\leq 1/2
,
which readily implies that
b_{n}\lesssim 2^{-n}
. We thus have

A_{1,1}\lesssim M_{I}\sum_{2^{n}>|I|^{-1}}\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{n}}\int_{Q}\left(\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}2^{-n}\int_{P}\frac{\left|\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}(u)(e^{i2\pi\sigma})\right|^{2}}{\sin^{2}(\pi(\theta-\sigma))}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}

and so,

\displaystyle A_{1,1}

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{I}\sum_{2^{n}>|I|^{-1}}\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{n}}\int_{Q}\left(\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\frac{2^{-n}}{\mathrm{dist}(p,q)^{2}}\int_{P}\left|\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}(u)(e^{i2\pi\sigma})\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{I}\left\|u\right\|_{\Psi_{r,1},\ast}\sum_{2^{n}>|I|^{-1}}\frac{2^{-n/2}}{n^{r}}\left(\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{Q}_{n}}\int_{Q}\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\frac{2^{-n}}{\mathrm{dist}(p,q)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim M_{I}\left\|u\right\|_{\Psi_{r,1},\ast}\sum_{2^{n}>|I|^{-1}}\frac{2^{-n/2}}{n^{r}}\left(\int_{[0,|I|/2-2^{-n-1})}\left(\int_{[|I|/2,1)}\frac{1}{|\theta-\sigma|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}.

It can easily be seen that the sum in the last expression is bounded by
CM_{I}^{-1}
, with
C
being a constant independent of
I
. Hence,
A_{1,1}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\Psi_{r,1},\ast}
.

Estimate for

To estimate

, we have
A_{1,2}\leq A_{1,2,a}+A_{1,2,b}
, where

A_{1,2,a}:=M_{I}\sum_{2^{n}>|I|^{-1}}\left(\int_{I\setminus I_{n}^{-}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}\setminus I_{n}^{+}}\left|\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}(u)(e^{i2\pi\sigma})\right|\cdot\left|\delta_{n}\left(e^{i2\pi(\theta-\sigma)}\right)\right|\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}

A_{1,2,b}:=M_{I}\sum_{2^{n}>|I|^{-1}}\left(\int_{I\setminus I_{n}^{-}}\left(\int_{I_{n}^{+}\setminus I}\left|\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}(u)(e^{i2\pi\sigma})\right|\cdot\left|\delta_{n}\left(e^{i2\pi(\theta-\sigma)}\right)\right|\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}.

Here, for

,

denotes the interval that is concentric with
I
and has length

. Arguing as in the case of

, one has

A_{1,2,a}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\Psi_{r,1},\ast}
. To estimate
A_{1,2,b}
, we use the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, as well as that
\left\|\delta_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})}\lesssim 2^{n}
and

to deduce that

So,

.
Hence, combining the estimates for
A_{1,2,a}
and
A_{1,2,b}
, together with the estimate for

.

Estimate for
A_{2}

Since the maps

\theta\mapsto\int_{I}\widetilde{\Delta}_{n_{1}}(u)(e^{i2\pi\sigma})\delta_{n_{1}}(e^{i2\pi(\theta-\sigma)})\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\ \text{ and }\ \theta\mapsto\int_{I}\widetilde{\Delta}_{n_{2}}(u)(e^{i2\pi\sigma})\delta_{n_{2}}(e^{i2\pi(\theta-\sigma)})\,\mathrm{d}\sigma

are orthogonal in

for

with

, we have

, where

and

A_{2,2}:=M_{I}\left(\sum_{2^{n}>|I|^{-1}}\int_{I}\left|\int_{I}\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}(u)(e^{i2\pi\sigma})\delta_{n}\left(e^{i2\pi(\theta-\sigma)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}.

Arguing as in the case of
A_{1}
, one shows that
A_{2,1}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\Psi_{r,1},\ast}
.

We shall now focus on
A_{2,2}
; by the triangle inequality, we have
A_{2,2}\leq A_{2,2,a}+A_{2,2,b}
,
where

A_{2,2,a}:=M_{I}\left(\sum_{2^{n}>|I|^{-1}}\int_{I}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}}\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}(u)(e^{i2\pi\sigma})\delta_{n}\left(e^{i2\pi(\theta-\sigma)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}

and

A_{2,2,b}:=M_{I}\left(\sum_{2^{n}>|I|^{-1}}\int_{I}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}\setminus I}\widetilde{\Delta}_{n}(u)(e^{i2\pi\sigma})\delta_{n}\left(e^{i2\pi(\theta-\sigma)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{1/2}.

Arguing as in the case of
A_{1}
, one proves that
A_{2,2,b}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\Psi_{r,1},\ast}
.
The desired estimate for

, i.e.

, follows directly from the definition of
\left\|u\right\|_{\Psi_{r,1},\ast}

It thus follows that

which, together with the corresponding estimate for

, obtained above, completes the proof of (

) for

) for

If

denotes a decomposition of

\sigma\in G\end{subarray}}\left|\delta_{n}\left(e^{i2\pi(\theta-\sigma)}\right)-\delta_{n}\left(e^{i2\pi(c_{I}-\sigma)}\right)\right|.

, we have

\sum_{G\in\mathcal{G}_{n}}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in I\\

Hence, (

5.
Results in the Euclidean setting

5.1.
Spaces of logarithmic smoothness

Let
\varphi_{0}
be a Schwartz function, radial, positive and supported in
\{\left|\xi\right|\leq 1\}
, which is equal to
1
in
\{\left|\xi\right|\leq 1/2\}
. Let
\varphi_{1}(\xi):=\varphi_{0}(\xi/2)-\varphi_{0}(\xi)
, and

\varphi_{j}(\xi):=\varphi_{1}(2^{-j}\xi),\qquad j\geq 2.

For each
j\geq 1
, the function
\varphi_{j}
is supported in the annulus
\{2^{j-1}\leq\left|\xi\right|\leq 2^{j+1}\}
, and for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

(5.1)

1=\varphi_{0}(\xi)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\varphi_{\ell}(\xi).

Such a family
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
is referred to as a non-homogeneous resolution of unity.
For
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we set

\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(f):=\left(\varphi_{j}\widehat{f}\right)^{\vee},\qquad\mbox{for $j\geq 0$.}

Definition 17
.

Let
s,r\in\mathbb{R}
,
0<p\leq\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
, and let
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
be a resolution of unity as above.

•

[
9
,
10
]
If
p<\infty
, we define the Triebel–Lizorkin space of generalised smoothness,
F_{p,q}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, to be the set of all tempered distributions
f
for which

\left\|f\right\|_{F_{p,q}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\left\|\left(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{jsq}\left(1+j\right)^{rq}\left|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}f\right|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

is finite, with the usual modification if
q=\infty
.

•

[
2
]
*Definition 2.7 Let
0<q<\infty
and let
\mathcal{D}
be the set of all dyadic cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We define
F_{\infty,q}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to be the set of all tempered distributions
f
for which

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{F_{\infty,q}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{\Delta}_{0}f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathcal{D}\\
\ell(Q)\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{1}{\left|Q\right|}\int_{Q}\sum_{j=-\log_{2}\ell(Q)}^{\infty}2^{sjq}\left(1+j\right)^{rq}\left|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}f(x)\right|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/q}

is finite.

Remark 18
.

In the previous definition, the case where
r=0
recovers the classical definition of Triebel–Lizorkin space.

Remark 19
.

Let
p(\xi)=\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq N}c_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}
be a polynomial expression in
\xi
with constant coefficients, where for every multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,

\left|\alpha\right|=\alpha_{1}+\ldots\alpha_{d},\quad\xi^{\alpha}=\xi_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\xi_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}.

Let
D:=-i\partial
, so
p(D)
denotes the constant coefficient differential operator

p(D)f=\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|\leq d}c_{\alpha}D^{\alpha}f.

Using the properties of the Fourier transform, for any
f\in\mathcal{S}
, this can be written as the Fourier multiplier operator with symbol
p(\xi)
given by

p(D)f(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{ix\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi.

Notice that such an expression makes sense, on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and on
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for a wider class of symbols
p
than polynomials, such as those in the Kohn–Nirenberg classes
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, with
m\in\mathbb{R}
. Let us recall that a symbol
\sigma
belongs to
S^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if, and only if,
\sigma
is smooth and, for all multi-indices
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, satisfies

(5.2)

\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left<\xi\right>^{-m+\left|\alpha\right|}\left|\partial^{\alpha}_{\xi}\sigma(\xi)\right|<\infty.

Here, we use the shorthand notation

\left<\xi\right>:=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In particular, if one defines
p(\xi)=\left|\xi\right|^{2}
, the Laplacian of
f
can be written as

-\Delta f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}(-i)^{2}\partial^{2e_{j}}f(x)=p(D)f(x).

This allows us to define, for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
(1-\Delta)^{s/2}
as the Fourier multiplier operator with symbol
\left<\xi\right>^{s}
.

These spaces of generalised smoothness, see Definition
17
above, can be realised as potential type spaces. To be more specific, if one defines, for
r\in\mathbb{R}
,

\log^{r}(e-\Delta)f(x):=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\log^{r}(e+\left|\xi\right|^{2})\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{ix\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

then

(5.3)

\left\|f\right\|_{F^{s,r}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx\left\|(1-\Delta)c\frac{s}{2}\log^{r}(e-\Delta)f\right\|_{F^{0,0}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

This is a consequence of the lifting property of these spaces (see
[
9
]
*Proposition 3.2 for
p<\infty
and
[
3
]
*Proposition 2.15 for the case
p=\infty
) and the fact that for all multi-indices
\alpha
,

(5.4)

\left|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\log^{r}(e+\left|\xi\right|^{2})\right|\lesssim(1+\left|\xi\right|)^{-\left|\alpha\right|}{\log^{r}(e+\left|\xi\right|)}.

In particular, we have that

\left\|f\right\|_{F^{0,r}_{p,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx\left\|\log^{r}(e-\Delta)f\right\|_{h^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Indeed, if one defines
u_{r}(\xi):=\log^{r}(e+\left|\xi\right|^{2})
, and

(5.5)

\mathrm{w}_{r}(\xi)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(1+j)^{r}\varphi_{j}(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

the aforementioned lifting property yields that

\left\|f\right\|_{F^{s,r}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left\|(1-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\mathrm{w}_{r}(D)f\right\|_{F^{0,0}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

A simple calculation shows that both
u_{r}/\mathrm{w}_{r}
and
\mathrm{w}_{r}/u_{r}
belong to the Kohn–Nirenberg class
S^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence, the associated Fourier multiplier operators are bounded in Triebel–Lizorkin spaces (see
[
35
]
*Theorem 2.3.7), establishing the equivalence in (
5.3
).

The description as potential-type spaces allows known results to be lifted into spaces of generalised smoothness. For instance, one can show that

\left(F^{0,-r}_{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)^{\ast}\cong F^{0,r}_{\infty,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

by using the identification of the dual of
h^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=F^{0,0}_{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=F^{0,0}_{\infty,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
This is a consequence of the fact that

\displaystyle\sup_{\left\|f\right\|_{F^{0,-r}_{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1}|\langle f,g\rangle|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\left\|\mathrm{w}_{-r}(D)f\right\|_{h^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1}|\langle\mathrm{w}_{-r}(D)f,(\mathrm{w}_{-r})^{-1}(D)g\rangle|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\left\|h\right\|_{h^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 1}|\langle h,(\mathrm{w}_{-r})^{-1}(D)g\rangle|

\displaystyle=\left\|(\mathrm{w}_{-r})^{-1}(D)g\right\|_{\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\approx\left\|\mathrm{w}_{r}(D)g\right\|_{\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where in the last equivalences, we use that both
\mathrm{w}_{-r}/\mathrm{w}_{r}
and
\mathrm{w}_{r}/\mathrm{w}_{-r}
belong to
S^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and the boundedness of these Fourier multipliers on
\mathrm{bmo}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

5.2.
Embedding of local Hardy–Orlicz spaces into spaces of logarithmic smoothness

In this section we shall prove the following Euclidean analogue of Theorem
16
, which can be regarded as an embedding result of the space
h^{\Psi_{r},1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into the Triebel–Lizorkin space of generalised smoothness

.

Theorem 20
.

Let
r\geq 0
.
There exists a constant
C_{r}>0
such that

(5.6)

where

,

.

Theorem

will be obtained by combining the following lemma with the atomic decomposition of
h^{\Psi_{r,1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Lemma 21
.

Let
r\geq 0
. There exists a constant
M_{r}>0
such that for any cube

and for any function
\beta_{Q}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\left\|\left(\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\beta_{Q})|^{2}}{(j+1)^{2r}}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq M_{r}|Q|\frac{\left\|\beta_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{\left[\log\left(e+|Q|^{-1}\right)\right]^{r}}.

Proof.

Let
r\geq 0
and let
Q
and
\beta_{Q}
be as in the statement of the lemma with
\left|Q\right|\leq 1
. Without loss of generality, by translation-invariance, we may assume that

We write

\left(\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\beta_{Q})(x)|^{2}}{(j+1)^{2r}}\right)^{1/2}\leq A(x)+B(x),

where

and

B(x):=\left(\sum_{2^{j}\geq\left|Q\right|^{-1}}\frac{|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\beta_{Q})(x)|^{2}}{(j+1)^{2r}}\right)^{1/2}.

We shall prove that there exists a constant

such that

\left\|A\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq K_{r}|Q|\frac{\left\|\beta_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{\left[\log\big(e+|Q|^{-1})\right]^{r}}

and

(5.9)

\left\|B\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq K_{r}|Q|\frac{\left\|\beta_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{\left[\log\big(e+|Q|^{-1}\big)\right]^{r}}.

Note that the desired estimate then follows from (

), (
5.8
), and (
5.9
).

For the proofs of (

) and (

), we shall use the standard facts that there is an absolute constant

(5.10)

|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\beta_{Q})(x)|\leq C2^{jd}|Q|\left\|\beta_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

and for each
N>0
there exists a constant
C_{N}>0
such that

(5.11)

|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\beta_{Q})(x)|\leq C_{N}|Q|^{d+1}\|\beta_{Q}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\frac{2^{j(2d-N)}}{|x|^{N}}

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
with
|x|>2|Q|
and for all
j\geq 0
.

To prove (
5.10
) and (
5.11
), consider Schwartz functions

\Phi_{j}:=\varphi_{j}^{\vee}.

It follows that

\left\|\Phi_{j}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim 2^{jd}\quad\text{for all }j\geq 0.

One thus deduces that

\left\|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\beta_{Q})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left\|\Phi_{j}\ast\beta_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim 2^{jd}|Q|\left\|\beta_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\quad\text{for all }j\geq 0.

Hence, (
5.10
) holds. To prove (
5.11
), let
x
be such that
|x|>2\left|Q\right|
. For
j\geq 1
, properties (i) and (ii) of
\beta_{Q}
yield that

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\beta_{Q})(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{Q}\beta_{Q}(y)\left[\Phi_{j}(x-y)-\Phi_{j}(x)\right]\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\beta_{Q}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{Q}\left|\Phi_{j}(x-y)-\Phi_{j}(x)\right|\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N}\|\beta_{Q}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\int_{Q}|y|\frac{2^{2jd}}{(1+2^{j}|x|)^{N}}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim|Q|^{d+1}\|\beta_{Q}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\frac{2^{j(2d-N)}}{|x|^{N}},

where the implicit estimate that was used to go from the second to the third line can be justified by appealing to the mean value theorem and the rapid decay of
\varphi_{1}
.

Going back to the proof of (
5.8
), write

(5.12)

\left\|A\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\int_{|x|\leq 2|Q|}|A(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{|x|>2|Q|}|A(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x.

Hence, by using (

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\leq 2\left|Q\right|}|A(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim|Q|^{d}\|A\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\lesssim|Q|^{d}\left(\sum_{2^{j}<\left|Q\right|^{-1}}\frac{\|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\beta_{Q})\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{(j+1)^{2r}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|Q|^{d+1}\|\beta_{Q}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(\sum_{2^{j}<\left|Q\right|^{-1}}\frac{2^{2jd}}{(j+1)^{2r}}\right)^{1/2}

and as

\sum_{2^{j}<\left|Q\right|^{-1}}\frac{2^{2jd}}{(j+1)^{2r}}\lesssim_{r}\frac{|Q|^{-2d}}{\left[\log\left(e+|Q|^{-1}\right)\right]^{2r}},

we get

(5.13)

\int_{|x|\leq 2\left|Q\right|}|A(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim_{r}|Q|\frac{\left\|\beta_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{\left[\log(e+|Q|^{-1})\right]^{r}}.

To handle the second term, note that by using (
5.11

\displaystyle\int_{|x|>2\left|Q\right|}|A(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\lesssim|Q|^{d+1}\left\|\beta_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(\sum_{2^{j}<\left|Q\right|^{-1}}\frac{2^{j(4d-2N)}}{(j+1)^{2r}}\right)^{1/2}\int_{|x|>2|Q|}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{|x|^{N}}

provided

. This, combined with

\sum_{2^{j}<\left|Q\right|^{-1}}\frac{2^{j(4d-2N)}}{(j+1)^{2r}}\lesssim_{r}\frac{|Q|^{-(4d-2N)}}{\left[\log\left(e+|Q|^{-1}\right)\right]^{2r}},

yields

(5.14)

In view of (
5.13

To prove (
5.9

B(x)\leq\frac{1}{\left[\log(e+|Q|^{-1})\right]^{r}}\left(\sum_{2^{j}\geq|Q|^{-1}}|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\beta_{Q})(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Hence, it suffices to show that

To this end, write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\sum_{2^{j}\geq|Q|^{-1}}|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\beta_{Q})(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|\leq 2|Q|}\left(\sum_{2^{j}\geq|Q|^{-1}}|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\beta_{Q})(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{|x|>2|Q|}\left(\sum_{2^{j}\geq|I|^{-1}}|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\beta_{Q})(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

For the first term, by using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and Parseval’s identity, we get

and hence,

(5.16)

\int_{|x|\leq 2|Q|}\left(\sum_{2^{j}\geq|Q|^{-1}}|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\beta_{Q})(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim|Q|\left\|\beta_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

To handle the second term, we use (
5.11
) for
N>2d
,

\displaystyle\int_{|x|>2|Q|}\left(\sum_{2^{j}\geq|Q|^{-1}}|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\beta_{Q})(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim

\displaystyle|Q|^{d+1}\left\|\beta_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left(\sum_{2^{j}\geq|Q|^{-1}}2^{2j(2d-N)}\right)^{1/2}\int_{|x|>2|Q|}\frac{1}{|x|^{N}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Hence,

(5.17)

\int_{|x|>|Q|}\left(\sum_{2^{j}\geq|I|^{-1}}|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\beta_{Q})(x)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim|Q|\left\|\beta_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Therefore, in view of (
5.16
) and (
5.17
),
(
5.9
) holds and so, the proof of the lemma is complete for the case
\left|Q\right|<1
.

Assume now that

\beta_{Q}
is then a multiple of an

-atom as

yield

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left(\sum_{j\geq 0}|\widetilde{\Delta}_{j}(\beta_{Q})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

5.3.
Behaviour of the Fourier transform of distributions in Hardy–Orlicz spaces

Let
\Psi
be a growth function. In this section we study properties of the Fourier transform of distributions belonging to
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, or
h^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We first obtain pointwise estimates, and then use them to prove Theorem
7
, which is an extension of the Hardy–Littewood inequality to
H^{\log}
-spaces.

Proposition 24
.

Let
\Psi
be a growth function of order
p\in(0,1]
.

If
f\in H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, then its Fourier transform coincides with a continuous function that we denote by
\widehat{f}
. Moreover, there exists a constant
A_{d,\Psi}>0
, depending only on
\Psi
and
d
, but not on
f
, such that

\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|\leq A_{d,\Psi}\frac{\Psi^{-1}(a_{d}\left|\xi\right|^{d})}{a_{d}\left|\xi\right|^{d}}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. Here
\Psi^{-1}
denotes the inverse of
\Psi
and
a_{d}:=\left|B(0,1)\right|^{-1}
.

Proof.

Let
\phi
be a fixed Schwartz function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\widehat{\phi}(\xi)=1
for all
\left|\xi\right|\leq 1
.
For
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, define

M_{\phi}^{\ast}[f](x):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty):\\
\left|y-x\right|<t\end{subarray}}\left|f\ast\phi_{s}(y)\right|,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Let
f\in H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be non-zero. Suppose first that

\|f\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi\left(\lambda^{-1}M_{\mathcal{F}_{m_{\Psi}}}^{\ast}[f](x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 1\right\}=1.

Observe that this implies

(5.22)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi\left(M_{\phi}^{\ast}[f](x)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 1.

Note first that, for
t>0
, if
\left|x-y\right|<t
then

\left|f\ast\phi_{t}(x)\right|\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(z,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty):\\
\left|z-y\right|<s\end{subarray}}\left|f\ast\phi_{s}(z)\right|=M_{\phi}^{\ast}[f](y),

which implies that for
t>0
and
\left|x-y\right|<t
,

\Psi\left(\left|f*\phi_{t}(x)\right|\right)\leq\Psi\left(M^{*}_{\phi}f(y)\right).

This yields

(5.23)

\Psi\left(\left|f*\phi_{t}(x)\right|\right)\leq a_{d}t^{-d}\int_{B(x,t)}\Psi\left(M^{*}_{\phi}[f](y)\right)\,\mathrm{d}y,

where
a_{d}:=\left|B(0,1)\right|^{-1}
. It follows from (
5.23
) that

(5.24)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\left|f\ast\phi_{t}(x)\right|}\leq\Psi^{-1}\left(a_{d}t^{-d}\right),

where we also used (
5.22
).

Let us define
\eta:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
given by
\eta(t):=t\cdot[\Psi(t)]^{-1}
,
t>0
. Observe that

\displaystyle\left|f*\phi_{t}(x)\right|\leq\eta\left(\left|f*\phi_{t}(x)\right|\right)\Psi\left(M_{\phi}^{\ast}[f](x)\right)\leq\eta\left(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\left|f\ast\phi_{t}(x)\right|}\right)\Psi\left(M_{\phi}^{\ast}[f](x)\right)

and so, by employing (
5.24
), we obtain

(5.25)

\left|f*\phi_{t}(x)\right|\leq\eta\circ\Psi^{-1}\left(a_{d}t^{-d}\right)\Psi\left(M_{\phi}^{\ast}[f](x)\right).

Then using (
5.22
), we get

(5.26)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|f\ast\phi_{t}(x)\right|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\eta\circ\Psi^{-1}\left(a_{d}t^{-d}\right).

Note that for all
0<t<1
, we have
h_{t}:=f\ast\phi_{t}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
\widehat{\phi_{t}}(\xi)=1
for
\left|\xi\right|\leq 1/t
, which yields that, in the sense of distributions,

\widehat{f}\widehat{\phi_{t}}=\widehat{h_{t}}.

So we have that, for all
0<t<1
, the distribution
\widehat{f}
concides with the continuous function
\widehat{h_{t}}
on the compact set
\{\left|\xi\right|\leq 1/t\}
, and notice that for
0<s<t<1
,
\widehat{h_{s}}=\widehat{h_{t}}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
with
\left|\xi\right|\leq 1/t
. This allows us to construct a continuous function
g
, such that
\widehat{f}
coincides with
g
in a distributional sense and

(5.27)

g(\xi)=\widehat{f\ast\phi_{t}}(\xi),\quad\left|\xi\right|\leq 1/t.

Fixing
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
, and combining (
5.27
) (with the choice
t=|\xi|^{-1}
) with (
5.26
), we have

\left|g(\xi)\right|=\left|\widehat{f\ast\phi_{|\xi|^{-1}}}(\xi)\right|\leq{\left\|f\ast\phi_{|\xi|^{-1}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}\leq\eta\circ\Psi^{-1}(a_{d}\left|\xi\right|^{d})=\frac{\Psi^{-1}(a_{d}\left|\xi\right|^{d})}{a_{d}\left|\xi\right|^{d}}.

Since
\Psi
is of lower type
p
it follows that for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\left|g(\xi)\right|\lesssim(1+\left|\xi\right|)^{d(\frac{1}{p}-1)},

which yields
g\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Therefore, the proof is complete if
\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=1
.

If
f
is non-zero and
\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\neq 1
, define

\widetilde{f}:=\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{-1}f

and then apply the previous step to
\widetilde{f}
. By homogeneity, we obtain the desired result for the general case.
∎

5.4.
Proof of Theorem

Arguing as in the proof of

]
*Theorem 28, we shall prove that there exists a constant

, depending only on

,

(5.29)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Psi\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|\right)}{\left|\xi\right|^{2d}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\leq A_{d,\Psi,p}\left|Q\right|\Psi\left(\left\|a_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right),

for all

that are supported in some cube

To this end, fix an
L^{\infty}

(5.30)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Psi\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|\right)}{\left|\xi\right|^{2d}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi=A+B,

where

A:=\int_{\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|Q\right|\leq 1}\frac{\Psi\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|\right)}{\left|\xi\right|^{2d}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\quad\text{and}\quad B:=\int_{\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|Q\right|>1}\frac{\Psi\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|\right)}{\left|\xi\right|^{2d}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

To handle

\left|e^{-i2\pi x\xi}-e^{-i2\pi y\xi}\right|\leq 2\pi\left|\xi\right|\left|x-y\right|\lesssim_{d}\left|\xi\right|\left|Q\right|^{1/d}\quad\text{ for all }x,y\in Q,

one deduces that

(5.31)

\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|\lesssim_{d}\left|\xi\right|\left|Q\right|^{1+1/d}\left\|a_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

\displaystyle\int_{\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|Q\right|\leq 1}\frac{\Psi\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|\right)}{\left|\xi\right|^{2d}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim_{d}\int_{0}^{\left|Q\right|^{-1/d}}\frac{\Psi\left(\left|Q\right|^{1+1/d}\left\|a_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}s^{d+1}\right)}{s^{d+1}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim_{p}{\left|Q\right|^{p(d+1)/d}}\Psi\left(\left\|a_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\int_{0}^{\left|Q\right|^{-1/d}}\rho^{p(d+1)-\mathrm{d}-1}\,\mathrm{d}\rho

since

. Hence,

(5.32)

\int_{\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|Q\right|\leq 1}\frac{\Psi\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|\right)}{\left|\xi\right|^{2d}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\lesssim_{p,d}{\left|Q\right|}\Psi\left(\left\|a_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right),

where the implied constant depends only on
p
and
d
and not on
a_{Q}
.

To deal with
B
, note that
Hölder’s inequality (with exponents
4
and
4/3
) and Plancherel’s theorem yield

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\int_{\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|Q\right|>1}\frac{\Psi\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|\right)}{\left|\xi\right|^{2d}{\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|^{1/2}}}\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq\left\|a_{Q}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1/2}\left(\int_{\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|Q\right|>1}\left(\frac{\Psi\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|\right)}{\left|\xi\right|^{d/2}\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|^{1/2}}\right)^{4/3}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\left|\xi\right|^{2d}}\right)^{3/4}

\displaystyle\leq\left|Q\right|^{1/4}\left\|a_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1/2}\left(\int_{\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|Q\right|>1}\left(\frac{\Psi\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|\right)}{\left|\xi\right|^{d/2}\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|^{1/2}}\right)^{4/3}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\left|\xi\right|^{2d}}\right)^{3/4}.

Since
1/2\leq d/(d+1)<p
, the function
t\mapsto t^{-1/2}\Psi(t)
is quasi-increasing. Hence, as for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
one has

\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|\leq\left|Q\right|\left\|a_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

we deduce that

(5.33)

\frac{\Psi\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|\right)}{\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|\right)^{1/2}}\lesssim_{p}\frac{\Psi\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|Q\right|\left\|a_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)}{\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|Q\right|\left\|a_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)^{1/2}}.

Then, in view of (
5.33
), one has

B\lesssim_{p}\left|Q\right|^{1/4}\left\|a_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1/2}\left(\int_{\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|Q\right|>1}\left(\frac{\Psi\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|Q\right|\left\|a_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)}{\left|\xi\right|^{d/2}\left|Q\right|^{1/2}\left\|a_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1/2}}\right)^{4/3}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\left|\xi\right|^{2d}}\right)^{3/4}.

Since
t\mapsto t^{-1}\Psi(t)
is non-increasing, it follows that

B\lesssim_{p}\Psi\left(\left\|a_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)\left(\int_{\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|Q\right|>1}\frac{1}{\left|\xi\right|^{4d/3}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{3/4}.

Hence,

(5.34)

B\lesssim_{d,p}\int_{\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|Q\right|>1}\frac{\Psi\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)\right|\right)}{\left|\xi\right|^{2d}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\lesssim_{p,d}{\left|Q\right|}\Psi\left(\left\|a_{Q}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right),

where the implied constant depends only on
p
and
d
and not on
a_{Q}
.

To complete the proof of the theorem, note that for any given atomic decomposition of
f
i.e.

f=\sum_{j}b_{j},

where
b_{j}
are constant multiples of atoms in
H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, supported in cubes
Q_{j}
, the sub-linearity of

and (

) imply that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\Psi\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|\right)}{\left|\xi\right|^{2d}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\lesssim_{d,p}\sum_{j}\left|Q_{j}\right|\Psi\left(\left\|b_{j}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right).

By taking the infimum over all possible atomic decompositions of
f
, one obtains the result of the statement.

The following inequality, is a consequence of Theorem
7
combined with Proposition
24
, and can be regarded as a Euclidean version of Theorem
3
.

Corollary 28
.

Let
\Psi
be a growth function of order
p
with
p\in(d/(d+1),1]
. Then, for every
1\leq q<\infty
, there exist constants
a_{d}>0
and
B_{d,\Psi,p,q}>0
such that

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|\left|\xi\right|^{d}}{\Psi^{-1}\left(a_{d}\left|\xi\right|^{d}\right)}\right)^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\left|\xi\right|^{d}}\right)^{1/q}\leq B_{d,\Psi,p,q}\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

Set
a_{d}:=\left|B(0,1)\right|^{-1}
and observe that Proposition
24
corresponds to the ‘
q=\infty

q=1

We shall first establish the

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|}{\Psi^{-1}(a_{d}\left|\xi\right|^{d})}\,\mathrm{d}\xi=A_{d,\Psi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi\left(\frac{\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|}{A_{d,\Psi}}\right)\frac{\frac{\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|}{A_{d,\Psi}}}{\Psi\left(\frac{\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|}{A_{d,\Psi}}\right)}\frac{1}{\Psi^{-1}(a_{d}\left|\xi\right|^{d})\left|\xi\right|^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

where

24

\frac{\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|}{A_{d,\Psi}}\leq\Psi^{-1}(a_{d}\left|\xi\right|^{d}).

We get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|}{\Psi^{-1}(a_{d}\left|\xi\right|^{d})}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\leq\frac{A_{d,\Psi}}{a_{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi\left(\frac{\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|}{A_{d,\Psi}}\right)\frac{1}{\left|\xi\right|^{2d}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

and since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|}{\Psi^{-1}(a_{d}\left|\xi\right|^{d})}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\leq\frac{2}{a_{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi\left(\left|\xi\right|^{d}\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|\right)\frac{1}{\left|\xi\right|^{2d}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Hence, by Theorem

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|}{\Psi^{-1}(a_{d}\left|\xi\right|^{d})}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\leq B_{d,\Psi,p},

Therefore, the case ‘

with

. The general case follows by homogeneity.
∎

Corollary 29
.

Let

(5.35)

*Lemma 7.4 we have that a distribution belongs to the local space

, and

. So, it suffices to show (

.

Using that for

,
\left|\xi\right|\approx\left<\xi\right>
, a direct application of Corollary
28
to deduce that (

) holds for

.

Since for

\left<\xi\right>\approx 1

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{\left|\widehat{f_{0}}(\xi)\right|\left<\xi\right>^{d}}{\Psi^{-1}\left(\left<\xi\right>^{d}\right)}\right)^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\left<\xi\right>^{d}}\lesssim\int_{\left|\xi\right|\leq 1}\left|\widehat{f}(\xi)\right|^{q}\mathrm{d}\xi\lesssim\left\|f_{0}\right\|_{h^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{q}.

∎

5.5.1.
Sobolev embedding-type results

By using Corollary

, and its local version (
5.35
), with

, and appealing to the properties of the Fourier transform, we have the following Sobolev embedding-type results.

Corollary 31
.

Let
\Psi
be a growth function of order
p
with
p\in(d/(d+1),1]
.

(1)

Then there exist positive constants
a_{d}
and
B_{d,\Psi,p}
such that for any Schwartz function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq B_{d,\Psi,p}\left\|\Psi^{-1}\left(a_{d}(-\Delta)^{d/2}\right)f\right\|_{H^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(2)

Then there exist positive constants
a_{d}
and
C_{d,\Psi,p}
such that for any Schwartz function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{d,\Psi,p}\left\|\Psi^{-1}\left(a_{d}{\left(1-\Delta\right)}^{d/2}\right)f\right\|_{h^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

To give an intuitive interpretation of the previous statement, let us consider the particular case that we take
\Psi_{r,p}(t)=t^{p}\log(e+t)^{-r}
as in (
1.5
). Using (
5.28
) and the corollary, we have the following result.

Corollary 32
.

Let
p\in(d/(d+1),1]
and

. Then there exists a positive constant
B_{d,r,p}
such that for all Schwartz functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq B_{d,r,p}\left\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{dp}{2}}\log^{\frac{r}{p}}(e-\Delta)f\right\|_{H^{\Psi_{r,p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

and

\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq B_{d,r,p}\left\|(1-\Delta)^{\frac{dp}{2}}\log^{\frac{r}{p}}(e-\Delta)f\right\|_{h^{\Psi_{r,p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Roughly speaking, these inequalities say that if a distribution has its derivatives of order

plus

-logarithmic order in

, then it belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Corollary

, and the identification in
[

]
*Proposition 2.26 yield that for

one has the following result.

Corollary 33
.

Let

and

. Then for all Schwartz functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and for

one has

(5.37)

\begin{split}\left\|f\right\|_{F^{0,-\frac{r}{p}}_{q,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\approx\left\|\log^{-r/p}(e-\Delta)f\right\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\left\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{dp}{2q^{\prime}}}f\right\|_{H^{\Psi_{r,p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\end{split}

and

Remark 34
.

Recall that Corollary

5.5.2.
Embedding of analytic function spaces on the right halfplane

In this section we show how
L^{2}
-based analytic Sobolev spaces of generalised logarithmic smoothness, and as a consequence, Hardy–Orlicz spaces of logarithmic type, can be embedded into certain spaces of analytic functions on the right halfplane (see Proposition
37
and its corollary).

To be more specific, let
\nu
be a positive regular Borel measure on
[0,\infty)
, satisfying the doubling condition

(5.39)

\sup_{t>0}\frac{\nu[0,2t)}{\nu[0,t)}<\infty.

The Zen space
A^{2}_{\nu}
is defined to consist of all analytic functions
F
on

\mathbb{C}_{+}:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\mathrm{Re}(z)>0\right\}

such that

\left\|F\right\|^{2}=\sup_{t>0}\int_{\overline{\mathbb{C}_{+}}}|F(x+iy+t)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(x)\,\mathrm{d}y<\infty.

Examples of Zen spaces include Hardy spaces

H^{p}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
(when
\nu=\frac{\delta_{0}}{2\pi}
) and weighted Bergman
spaces
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
(take
\alpha>-1
and
\mathrm{d}\nu(x)=\frac{x^{\alpha}}{\pi}\mathrm{d}x
). We refer the reader to
[
24
,
22
,
23
]
and the references therein.

A feature of Zen spaces is that the Laplace transform
defines an isometric map from weighted

L^{2}
spaces on
(0,\infty)
into certain spaces of analytic functions. More specifically we have (see e.g.
[
24
]
*Proposition 2.3):

Theorem 35

(Paley–Wiener theorem for Zen spaces)
.

Suppose that
w
is given as a weighted Laplace transform

w(t)=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2rt}\,d\nu(r)\quad(t>0).

Then the Laplace transform provides an isometric map

\mathcal{L}:L^{2}((0,\infty),w(t)\,\mathrm{d}t)\to A^{2}_{\nu},

where

\mathcal{L}(g)(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zt}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Definition 36
.

Set

\mathcal{H}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}:(1-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\log^{r}(e-\Delta)f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right\}=F^{s,r}_{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and endow
\mathcal{H}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with the norm

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s,r}}=\left\|(1-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\log^{r}(e-\Delta)f\right\|_{L^{2}}.

We shall denote by
\mathcal{H}_{A}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R})
those distributions in
\mathcal{H}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R})
whose Fourier transform in supported in
[0,\infty)
.

The main result of this section is the following one.

Proposition 37
.

Let
a>-1
and
\gamma>0
. The Laplace–Fourier transform given by

Tf:=\mathcal{L}(\widehat{f})(z)

provides an isometric map

T:\mathcal{H}_{A}^{-\frac{a+1}{2},-\frac{\gamma}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\to A^{2}_{\nu_{a,\gamma}}(\mathbb{C}_{+}),

where
\mathrm{d}\nu_{a,\gamma}(z)=\pi k_{a,\gamma}(x/2)\,\mathrm{d}x
and

(5.40)

k_{a,\gamma}(t):=e^{-te}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{a+b}b^{\gamma-1}}{\Gamma(\gamma)\Gamma(a+b+1)}\,\mathrm{d}b.

The previous proposition, jointly with (
5.38
) with the choice
q=2
, implies the following result.

Corollary 38
.

Let
p\in(1/2,1]
and
r>0
. Then the Laplace–Fourier transform
T
is a bounded map

T:h^{\Psi_{r,p}}_{A}(\mathbb{R})\to A^{2}_{\nu_{a,\gamma}}(\mathbb{C}_{+}),

where

a=p-1,\quad\gamma=\frac{2r}{p},\quad\mathrm{d}\nu_{a,\gamma}(z)=\pi k_{a,\gamma}(x/2)\,\mathrm{d}x

and
h^{\Psi_{r,p}}_{A}(\mathbb{R})
is defined as those distributions in
h^{\Psi_{r,p}}(\mathbb{R})
whose Fourier transform is supported in
[0,\infty)
.

Proof of Proposition
37

We start the proof by giving two technical results that identify the symbol of the Fourier multiplier defining the space
\mathcal{H}^{s,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as the Laplace transform of a suitable doubling measure.

Lemma 39
.

Let
a>-1,\gamma>0
. If
k_{a,\gamma}
denotes the function given in (
5.40
) then

\mathcal{L}(k_{a,\gamma})(s)=\frac{1}{(e+s)^{a+1}\ln(e+s)^{\gamma}}.

Proof.

Notice that for
a>-1

(e+s)^{-(a+1)}\Gamma(a+1)=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{a}e^{-te}e^{-ts}\,\mathrm{d}t,

so if we take

(5.41)

g_{a}(t)=\frac{t^{a}e^{-te}}{\Gamma(a+1)},

then

\mathcal{L}(g_{a})(s)=(e+s)^{-(a+1)}.

Also, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\gamma)}{\ln(e+s)^{\gamma}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{b^{\gamma-1}}{(e+s)^{b}}\,\mathrm{d}b

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{b-1}b^{\gamma-1}}{\Gamma(b)}\mathrm{d}b\right)e^{-te}e^{-ts}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\Gamma(\gamma)\mathcal{L}(h_{\gamma})(s),

where

(5.42)

h_{\gamma}(t):=\frac{e^{-te}}{\Gamma(\gamma)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{b-1}b^{\gamma-1}}{\Gamma(b)}\,\mathrm{d}b.

A change of the order of integration, a change of variables, and the relation between the Beta function and the Gamma function yield

\displaystyle h_{\gamma}*g_{a}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-te}}{\Gamma(a+1)\Gamma(\gamma)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{b^{\gamma-1}}{\Gamma(b)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{a}s^{b-1}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}b

\displaystyle=e^{-te}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{a+b}b^{\gamma-1}}{\Gamma(\gamma)\Gamma(a+b+1)}\,\mathrm{d}b.

The result follows since, by the properties of the Laplace transform, we have that

\mathcal{L}(k_{a,\gamma})=\mathcal{L}(h_{\gamma})\mathcal{L}(g_{a}).

∎

Lemma 40
.

Let
a>-1
and
\gamma>0
. Define

\Phi_{a,\gamma}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}k_{a,\gamma}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Then

(5.43)

\sup_{t>0}\frac{\Phi_{a,\gamma}(2t)}{\Phi_{a,\gamma}(t)}<\infty.

Proof.

A change on the order of integration and a change of variables yield

\displaystyle\Phi_{a,\gamma}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{b^{\gamma-1}}{\Gamma(\gamma)\Gamma(a+b+1)}\int_{0}^{t}s^{a+b}e^{-se}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}b

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-(a+b+1)}b^{\gamma-1}}{\Gamma(\gamma)\Gamma(a+b+1)}\int_{0}^{et}s^{a+b}e^{-s}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}b.

In particular it follows that
\Phi_{a,\gamma}
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
, increasing and

\sup_{t>0}\Phi_{a,\gamma}(t)=e^{-a-1}.

Hence, for
t\geq 1/2
we have that

(5.44)

1\leq\frac{\Phi_{a,\gamma}(2t)}{\Phi_{a,\gamma}(t)}\leq\frac{e^{-a-1}}{\Phi_{a,\gamma}(1/2)}.

Then, it suffices to check the doubling property for
0<t<1/2
.

For
t<1
, a change of variables gives

\displaystyle\eta(t):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{a+b}b^{\gamma-1}}{\Gamma(\gamma)\Gamma(a+b+1)}\,\mathrm{d}b

\displaystyle=t^{a}\left|\ln t\right|^{-\gamma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-u}u^{\gamma-1}}{\Gamma(\gamma)\Gamma(a+\frac{u}{\left|\ln t\right|}+1)}\,\mathrm{d}b

\displaystyle=t^{a}\left|\ln t\right|^{-\gamma}\beta(t).

Then, for
0<t<1/2

\frac{\eta(2t)}{\eta(t)}=2^{a}\left|\frac{\ln 2t}{\ln t}\right|^{\gamma}\frac{\beta(2t)}{\beta(t)}.

It then follows that

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{\Phi_{a,\gamma}(2t)}{\Phi_{a,\gamma}(t)}=2^{a}.

In particular, this yields that we can extend
\frac{\Phi_{a,\gamma}(2t)}{\Phi_{a,\gamma}(t)}
as a continuous function on
[0,1/2]
. By compactness, it has a maximum on that interval, which jointly with (
5.44
) yields (
5.43
).
∎

Notice first that Lemma
40
guarantees the doubling property (
5.39
). By the definition of the norm on
\mathcal{H}^{s,r}_{A}
, Lemma
39
and the Paley–Wiener theorem
35
we have

\begin{split}\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{A}^{-\frac{a+1}{2},-\gamma/2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}&=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\left|\widehat{f}(s)\right|^{2}}{(s+e)^{a+1}\log^{\gamma}(e+s)}\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\left|\widehat{f}(s)\right|^{2}}\mathcal{L}(k_{a,\gamma})(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\left\|\mathcal{L}(\widehat{f})\right\|_{A^{2}_{\nu_{a,\gamma}}(\mathbb{C}_{+})}^{2},\end{split}

where

\mathrm{d}\nu_{a,\gamma}(z)=\pi k_{a,\gamma}(x/2)\,\mathrm{d}x,

finishing the proof of Proposition
37
.

Remark 41
.

In the case that
r=0
, with a similar argument as above one shows that

T:h^{p}_{A}(\mathbb{R})\to A_{\nu_{a}}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})

with

\mathrm{d}\nu_{a}(z)=\pi g_{a}(x/2)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=\frac{\pi x^{a}}{2^{a}\Gamma(a+1)}e^{-xe/2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

Remark 42
.

Note that for
a>-1

\mathcal{L}(x^{a})(t)=\Gamma(a+1)t^{-a-1}.

So defining for
\gamma>0
and
a>-1

n_{a,\gamma}(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(a+1)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{a}h_{\gamma}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

with
h_{\gamma}
as in (
5.42
), then

\mathcal{L}(n_{a,\gamma})(s)=\frac{1}{s^{a+1}\log^{\gamma}(e+s)}.

Tracing the argument above, one shows that for
p\in(1/2,1]
and
r>0
, the Laplace–Fourier transform
T
is a bounded map

T:H^{\Psi_{r,p}}_{A}(\mathbb{R})\to A^{2}_{\nu_{a,\gamma}}(\mathbb{C}_{+}),

where

a=p-1,\quad\gamma=\frac{2r}{p},\quad\mathrm{d}\nu_{a,\gamma}(z)=\pi n_{a,\gamma}(x/2)\,\mathrm{d}x

and
H^{\Psi_{r,p}}_{A}(\mathbb{R})
is defined as those distributions in
H^{\Psi_{r,p}}(\mathbb{R})
whose Fourier transform is supported in
[0,\infty)
.

One can rephrase this boundedness result in terms of a Sobolev-like embedding involving a Bergman space. For instance, one obtains that for
p\in(1/2,1]
and
r\geq 0

\begin{split}\left\|Tf\right\|_{B^{2}_{2(\frac{1}{p}-1)}(\mathbb{C}_{+})}^{2}\lesssim\left\|\log\left(e{{-\Delta}}\right)^{r/p}f\right\|_{H^{\Psi_{r,p}}(\mathbb{R})}^{2},\end{split}

where
B^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{C}_{+})=A_{\nu_{a}}^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
with

\mathrm{d}\nu_{a}(z)=\frac{\pi x^{a}}{2^{a}\Gamma(a+1)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

Genuine Gelfand–Graev functor and the quantum affine Schur–Weyl duality
Fan Gao, Nadya Gurevich, and Edmund Karasiewicz

Abstract
. We explicate relations among the Gelfand–Graev modules for central covers, the Euler–Poincaré polynomial of the Arnold–Brieskorn manifold, and the quantum affine Schur–Weyl duality. These three objects and their relations are dictated by a permutation representation of the Weyl group. Specifically, our main result shows that for certain covers of GL ⁡ ( r ) \mathrm{GL}(r) the Gelfand–Graev functor is related to quantum affine Schur–Weyl duality. Consequently, the commuting algebra of the Iwahori-fixed part of the Gelfand–Graev representation is the quotient of a quantum group.

2.
Stability and the Gelfand–Graev module

In this section, let
\overline{G}=\overline{G}^{(n)}
be a Brylinski–Deligne cover of
G
. Recall the bilinear form
B_{Q}(y,z):=Q(y+z)-Q(y)-Q(z)
derived from
Q
and the Weyl group stable sublattice

Y_{Q,n}=\left\{y\in Y:B_{Q}(y,z)\in n\mathbbm{Z}\text{ for all }z\in Y\right\}.

The Weyl group
W
acts naturally on
\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}=Y/Y_{Q,n}
. The
\iota
-genuine Iwahori–Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{I}
can be viewed as the extended affine Hecke algebra associated with
Y_{Q,n}\rtimes W
. It contains the sub-algebra
\mathcal{H}_{W}:=C_{c,\iota}^{\infty}(I\backslash\overline{K}/I)
, which is associated with
W
.

Definition 2.1
.

An orbit
\mathcal{O}_{y}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
is called
\mathscr{X}
-splitting if the
W
-equivariant quotient map
f:Y\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
has a
W
-equivariant section over
\mathcal{O}_{y}
. A covering group
\overline{G}^{(n)}
is called
\mathscr{X}
-splitting if every orbit in
\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
is
\mathscr{X}
-splitting, i.e., if the quotient map
f:Y\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
has a
W
-equivariant section.

Consider
{\rm GL}_{r}
with lattice
Y
given with the standard
\mathbbm{Z}
-basis
\left\{e_{i}:1\leqslant i\leqslant r\right\}
. A Brylinski–Deligne cover
\overline{{\rm GL}}_{r}^{(n)}
of
{\rm GL}_{r}
is associated with
{\bf p},{\bf q}\in\mathbbm{Z}
such that

B(e_{i},e_{j})=\begin{cases}2{\bf p}&\text{ if }i=j,\\
{\bf q}&\text{ if }i\neq j.\end{cases}

We have
Q(\alpha^{\vee})=2{\bf p}-{\bf q}
. The Kazhdan–Patterson covers are those with
Q(\alpha^{\vee})=-1
, where
{\bf p}
corresponds to the parameter
c
in
[
KP
]
. Meanwhile, Savin’s “nice” cover is the one with
{\bf p}=-1,{\bf q}=0
.
These covers are
\mathscr{X}
-splitting by
[
GGK
, Example 6.5]
.

The following two classes of Brylinski–Deligne covers, which are also
\mathscr{X}
-splitting covers, are our main interest in this paper.

(C1)

A Brylinski–Deligne cover
\overline{{\rm GL}}_{r}^{(n)}
satisfying

n|{\bf q}

is called of type (C1). Such covers satisfy the block commutativity, i.e., for partition
{\bf r}=(r_{1},...,r_{k})
of
r
with associated standard Levi subgroup
M_{\bf r}={\rm GL}_{r_{1}}\times...\times{\rm GL}_{r_{k}}
, one has
an isomorphism of groups

\Big(\prod_{i}\overline{{\rm GL}}_{r_{i}}^{(n)}\Big)/H\to\overline{M}_{{\bf r}}^{(n)},

where
H=\left\{(\zeta_{i})_{i}:\prod_{i}\zeta_{i}=1\right\}
. Moreover, for such covers

Y_{Q,n}=n_{\alpha}Y\text{ with }n_{\alpha}=n/\gcd(2{\bf p},n).

These covers are
\mathscr{X}
-splitting by
[
GGK
, Example 6.5]
.

As concrete examples, recall that the Savin covers are Brylinski–Deligne covers with
({\bf p},{\bf q})=(-1,0)
, and thus are of type (C1). On the other hand, for Kazhdan–Patterson covers to be of type (C1),
n
is necessarily odd.

(C2)

Let
G
be semisimple and simply-connected. In
[
GGK
, Corollary 6.4]
we show that all oasitic covers (see Definition 6.1 of loc. cit.) of
G
are
\mathscr{X}
-splitting. And in the tables of
[
GGK
, §6.1]
, we explicitly list the

’s such that an
n
-fold cover of such
G
is oasitic.

Now assume
\overline{G}
is an
\mathscr{X}
-splitting cover. For every
W
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{y}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}
, one may view
\mathcal{O}_{y}\subset Y
such that

W_{y}:={\rm Stab}_{W}(y,Y)={\rm Stab}_{W}(y,\mathscr{X})

is a parabolic Weyl subgroup of
W
. Let

\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}\subset\mathcal{H}_{W}\subset\mathcal{H}_{I}

be the subalgebra associated with
W_{y}
. Let
\varepsilon_{W_{y}},\mathbbm{1}_{W_{y}}
be the sign and trivial character of
\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}
respectively. In
[
GGK
, Theorem 5.18]
we show that

(2.1)

\mathcal{V}\simeq\bigoplus_{\mathcal{O}_{y}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}}\varepsilon_{W_{y}}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}}\mathcal{H}_{I}

as a right
\mathcal{H}_{I}
-module.

Set
\left\{L,R\right\}=\left\{\Diamond,\Diamond^{\prime}\right\}
. For every
\iota^{\pm}
-genuine smooth representation
\pi
of
\overline{G}
with
\Diamond
-action, we denote by

Definition 2.2

Let

The

3.
An Euler–Poincaré polynomial and stability

In this section, we consider exclusively a semisimple simply-connected
G
and an oasitic cover
\overline{G}^{(n)}
of
G
(see
[
GGK
, Definition 6.1]
), such that for any short coroot
\alpha^{\vee}
,

Q(\alpha^{\vee})=-1.

These are covers of type (
C2
) in §
2
.

The unramified genuine exceptional characters
\chi
of
Z(\overline{T})
form a torsor over
Z(\overline{G}^{\vee})
, which is trivial for oasitic covers; thus, there is a unique theta representation
\Theta(\overline{G}^{(n)})
, the unique irreducible quotient of
I(\chi)
for the unique exceptional character
\chi
. The unique irreducible subrepresentation
{\rm St}(\overline{G}^{(n)})
of
I(\chi)
is the analogue of the Steinberg representation.

Using work of Sommers
[
Som97
]
, we determine
\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\Theta(\overline{G}^{(n)}))
explicitly in terms of the Euler–Poincaré polynomial of the Coxeter hyperplane arrangement of
G
. This gives the stability condition for
n
as well.

For semisimple simply-connected
G
, one has
Y=Y^{sc}
, which is
\mathbbm{Z}
-spanned by the simple coroots. For each root
\alpha\in\Phi
, consider the hyperplane

H_{\alpha}:=\left\{v\in Y\otimes\mathbbm{R}:{\left\langle v,\alpha\right\rangle}=0\right\}.

We have the set

\mathcal{A}:=\left\{H_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Phi\right\}

of the full Coxeter hyperplane arrangements in
Y\otimes\mathbbm{R}
. Let
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})
be the set of intersections of hyperplanes in
\mathcal{A}
. We consider

Y\otimes\mathbbm{R}\in\mathcal{L}

by taking the empty intersection of elements in
\mathcal{A}
. Elements in
\mathcal{L}
are ordered, and we write
x<y
if
y\subseteq x
. We also use
x\leqslant y
to represent
x<y
or
x=y
. The Mobius function

\mu:\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\longrightarrow\mathbbm{Z}

is uniquely defined by requiring
\mu(x,x)=1
,

\sum_{z:\ x\leqslant z\leqslant y}\mu(z,y)=0\text{ if }x<y,

and
\mu(x,y)=0
otherwise. The characteristic polynomial of
\mathcal{L}
is thus given by

\omega(\mathcal{L},X):=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{L}}\mu(Y\otimes\mathbbm{R},x)\cdot X^{\dim x}.

On the other hand, consider the Arnold–Brieskorn complex manifold

\mathscr{M}_{\rm AB}:=Y\otimes\mathbbm{C}-\bigcup_{\alpha\in\Phi}H_{\alpha}\otimes\mathbbm{C}

and its Euler–Poincaré polynomial

{\rm EP}(\mathscr{M}_{\rm AB},X):=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}\dim H^{i}(\mathscr{M}_{\rm AB},\mathbbm{C})\cdot X^{i}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\overline{G}^{(n)}
be an oasitic cover of a semisimple simply-connected
G
such that
Q(\alpha^{\vee})=-1
for any long root
\alpha
. Then one has

(3.1)

\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\Theta(\overline{G}^{(n)}))=\frac{\omega(\mathcal{L},n)}{\left|W\right|}=\frac{n^{r}\cdot{\rm EP}(\mathscr{M}_{\rm AB},-n^{-1})}{\left|W\right|}=\left|W\right|^{-1}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{r}(n-m_{j}),

where
m_{j},1\leqslant j\leqslant r
are the exponents of the Weyl group
W
. Similarly,

(3.2)

\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi}({\rm St}(\overline{G}^{(n)}))=\frac{(-1)^{r}\cdot\omega(\mathcal{L},-n)}{\left|W\right|}=\frac{n^{r}\cdot{\rm EP}(\mathscr{M}_{\rm AB},n^{-1})}{\left|W\right|}=\left|W\right|^{-1}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{r}(n+m_{j}).

Proof.

Before we begin, we note that we cite results from
[
GGK
]
that include the assumption
Q(\alpha^{\vee})=1
, for short coroots. When
G
is semisimple, these results also hold when
Q(\alpha^{\vee})=-1
, for short coroots.

First, we have

\omega(\mathcal{L},X)=X^{r}\cdot{\rm EP}(\mathscr{M}_{\rm AB},-X^{-1})=\prod_{j=1}^{r}(X-m_{j})\in\mathbbm{C}[X],

where the first equality was a classical result of Orlik–Solomon
[
OrSo83
]
and the second equality already follows from work of Brieskorn
[
Bri73
, Théorèm 6]
.

It follows from
[
GGK
, Theorem 8.8]
that

\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi}(\Theta(\overline{G}^{(n)}))={\left\langle\varepsilon_{W},\sigma_{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{W}=\#\left\{\text{free $W$-orbits in $\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}$}\right\}.

Let
P_{1},P_{2},...,P_{k}\subset W
be the conjugacy classes of parabolic subgroups of
W
such that

P_{1}=W,\quad P_{k}=\left\{1\right\}.

Note that
\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}=Y^{sc}/nY^{sc}
. Since
\overline{G}^{(n)}
is an oasitic cover (see
[
GGK
, §6.1]
), we see that
n
is “very good” in the terminology of
[
Som97
, Definition 3.5]
. Following this, we have from
[
Som97
, Lemma 4.2]
that

\sigma_{\mathscr{X}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{m}_{i}(n)\cdot{\rm Ind}_{P_{i}}^{W}(\mathbbm{1}_{P_{i}})

for well-defined
\mathfrak{m}_{i}(n)\in\mathbbm{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
. Furthermore, it is shown in
[
Som97
, Proposition 5.1]

that

\mathfrak{m}_{k}(n)=\frac{\omega(\mathcal{L},n)}{\left|W\right|}.

This gives

{\left\langle\varepsilon_{W},\sigma_{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{W}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{m}_{i}(n)\cdot{\left\langle\varepsilon_{P_{i}},\mathbbm{1}_{P_{i}}\right\rangle}=\mathfrak{m}_{k}(n)=\frac{\omega(\mathcal{L},n)}{\left|W\right|}.

Now we consider the Steinberg representation
{\rm St}(\overline{G}^{(n)})
. Again by
[
GGK
, Theorem 8.8]
,

\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi}({\rm St}(\overline{G}^{(n)}))={\left\langle\mathbbm{1}_{W},\sigma_{\mathscr{X}}\right\rangle}_{W}=\#\left\{\text{all $W$-orbits in $\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}$}\right\}.

For every
w\in W
, let
s(w)
denote the least number of reflections whose product is
w
and consider

d(w):=\dim(Y\otimes\mathbbm{R})^{w},

the dimension of the set of fixed points of
w
in
Y\otimes\mathbbm{R}
. One has
d(w)=r-s(w)
. Let
\chi_{\sigma_{\mathscr{X}}}
denote the character of
\sigma_{\mathscr{X}}
. It was shown in
[
Som97
, Proposition 3.9]
that

\chi_{\sigma_{\mathscr{X}}}(w)=n^{d(w)}

for every
w\in W
. We also note that

\varepsilon_{W}(w)=(-1)^{s(w)}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi}({\rm St}(\overline{G}^{(n)}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\left|W\right|}\sum_{w\in W}n^{d(w)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{r}}{\left|W\right|}\sum_{w\in W}\varepsilon_{W}(w)\cdot(-n)^{d(w)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{r}}{\left|W\right|}\cdot\omega(\mathcal{L},-n)

\displaystyle=\left|W\right|^{-1}\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{r}(n+m_{j}).

This completes the proof.
∎

The above recovers those explicit formulas for
\overline{{\rm Sp}}_{2r}
and
\overline{G}_{2}
computed in
[
Ga2
, §8.2–8.3]
. Applying
n=1
in (
3.2
), since
{\rm St}(G)
is generic with unique Whittaker model, we get the classical Weyl order formula
\left|W\right|=\prod_{j=1}^{r}(1+m_{j})
, see
[
Sol66
, Corollary 2.3]
.

Now assume that
m_{1}\leqslant m_{2}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant m_{r}
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\overline{G}^{(n)}
be as in Theorem
3.1
. Then
n
is stable if and only if
n>m_{r}
, in which case

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{H}_{I}}(\mathcal{V},-):\mathcal{M}_{R}(\mathcal{H}_{I})\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{R}({\rm End}_{\mathcal{H}_{I}}(\mathcal{V}))

gives an equivalence of categories.

Proof.

If
n>m_{r}
, then
n
is stable by Theorem
3.1
. Conversely, the result follows by comparing
[
GGK
, Tables 1, 2]
, with the exponents of the associated Weyl group. We include the details for type
E_{8}
. The other cases are similar.

By
[
GGK
, Table 2]
, if
\overline{G}
is an oasitic cover of type
E_{8}
, then
n
is not divisible by
2,3
, or
5
. The exponents of the
E_{8}
Weyl group are
1,7,11,13,17,19,23,29
(e.g. see
[
BouL2
, Plate VII]
). These are exactly the positive integers less than
30
that are not divisible by
2,3
, or
5
. Since
n
is stable, it follows that
n>29
, as desired.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Theorem
3.1
is compatible with Example
2.4
, since
m_{r}=r
for type

groups. For

\overline{{\rm GL}}_{r}^{(n)}
satisfying

. The covering subgroup
\overline{{\rm SL}}_{r}^{(n)}

. In particular, one has

. We note that this formula can also be obtained from

, which gives the Whittaker dimensions of general square integrable representations of the Kazhdan–Patterson covers.

4.
Quantum affine Schur–Weyl duality and
\mathcal{F}_{\rm{GG}}

In this section, we consider exclusively a cover
\overline{G}=\overline{{\rm GL}}_{r}
of type (
C1
). The goal is to show that the Gelfand–Graev functor

\mathcal{F}_{\rm{GG}}:\mathcal{M}_{L}(\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{I})\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{L}({\rm End}_{\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{I}}(\mathcal{V}))

defined by
\pi\mapsto\mathcal{V}^{*}\otimes_{\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{I}}\pi
can be identified with the quantum affine Schur–Weyl functor

\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}:\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff}))\to\mathcal{M}_{L}({\rm End}_{\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})}(\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[n_{\alpha}]}))

defined by
\sigma\mapsto\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[n_{\alpha}]}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})}\sigma
. Here

S_{r}^{\rm aff}=Y\rtimes S_{r}=\mathbbm{Z}^{r}\rtimes S_{r}

is the extended affine Weyl group of
{\rm GL}_{r}
and
\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})
the Iwahori–Hecke algebra of
{\rm GL}_{r}
. We recall the module structure of
\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[n_{\alpha}]}
in §
4.1
and §
4.4
. Moreover, since
\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[n_{\alpha}]}
has a left action by
U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(n_{\alpha}))
which commutes with the right action by
\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})
, one has a natural algebra homomorphism

\Psi:U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(n_{\alpha}))\longrightarrow{\rm End}_{\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})}(\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[n_{\alpha}]})

which induces a functor

\Psi^{*}:\mathcal{M}_{L}({\rm End}_{\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})}(\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[n_{\alpha}]}))\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{L}(U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(n_{\alpha}))).

By abuse of language, we also call

(4.1)

\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}^{\Psi}:=\Psi^{*}\circ\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}:\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff}))\to\mathcal{M}_{L}(U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(n_{\alpha})))

the quantum affine Schur–Weyl functor. In the last part of this section, we obtain a relation between the local scattering matrices and the
R
-matrices, utilizing
\Psi^{*}
and the relation between
\mathcal{F}_{\rm GG}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}
.

To compare
\mathcal{F}_{\rm GG}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}
we identify
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{I}
and

\overline{G}^{\vee}

4.1.
Right
\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})
-action on
\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[m]}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}

In this subsection, let
m\in\mathbbm{N}_{\geqslant 1}
. Let
\mathbbm{C}[\mathbbm{Z}]
be the
\mathbbm{C}
-vector space with a basis given by
\left\{v_{i}:i\in\mathbbm{Z}\right\}
. Let

\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}:=(\mathbbm{C}[\mathbbm{Z}])^{\otimes r}.

For any

\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}
is endowed with commuting
(U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m)),\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff}))

[

,

to emphasize the dependence of the action on
m
. Now we describe the right action of
\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})
on
\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[m]}

in §

.

There is a canonical map

and similarly

\tilde{v}:Y/mY\hookrightarrow\mathbbm{C}[Y/mY]=(\mathbbm{C}^{m})^{\otimes r},\quad y\mapsto\tilde{v}_{y}.

In terms of the standard
\mathbbm{Z}
-basis

of

\mathbbm{C}[Y]=\mathbbm{C}[e_{1}^{\pm 1},e_{2}^{\pm 1},...,e_{r}^{\pm 1}].

Clearly,

(4.4)

\mathfrak{R}_{m}:=\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{r}i_{j}\cdot e_{j}:0\leqslant i_{j}\leqslant m-1\right\}

is a set of representatives of
Y/mY
. For every integer

we write

\tilde{v}_{i_{j}}:=\tilde{v}_{i_{j}\cdot e_{j}}.

Thus, a basis of
\mathbbm{C}[Y/mY]=(\mathbbm{C}^{m})^{\otimes r}

\mathfrak{B}_{m}=\left\{\tilde{v}_{i_{1}}\otimes\tilde{v}_{i_{2}}\otimes...\otimes\tilde{v}_{i_{r}}:0\leqslant i_{j}\leqslant m-1\text{ for every $j$}\right\}.

One has the
\mathbbm{C}
-vector space isomorphism

\tau_{m}:(\mathbbm{C}^{m})^{\otimes r}\otimes_{\mathbbm{C}}\mathbbm{C}[Y]\longrightarrow\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}

given by

(\tilde{v}_{i_{1}}\otimes...\otimes\tilde{v}_{i_{r}})\otimes(e_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdot...\cdot e_{r}^{k_{r}})\mapsto v_{i_{1}-k_{1}m}\otimes...\otimes v_{i_{r}-k_{r}m}.

The algebra
\mathbbm{C}[W]
acts on
\mathbbm{C}[Y/mY]=(\mathbbm{C}^{m})^{\otimes r}
naturally, induced from the permutation action of
W
on
Y/mY
. This representation can be “deformed” to get a representation
(\gamma_{m},(\mathbbm{C}^{m})^{\otimes r})
of
\mathcal{H}_{W}
. More precisely, for
\tilde{v}_{\underline{i}}:=\tilde{v}_{i_{1}}\otimes...\otimes\tilde{v}_{i_{r}}\in(\mathbbm{C}^{m})^{\otimes r}

, one defines (see
[
Ant
, Lemma 2.19]
or
[
SSV21
, Lemma 3.1]
)

(4.5)

\tilde{v}_{\underline{i}}\cdot T_{s_{\alpha_{k}}}=\begin{cases}\tilde{v}_{\underline{i}\cdot s_{\alpha_{k}}}&\text{ if }i_{k}<i_{k+1},\\
q^{-1}\cdot\tilde{v}_{\underline{i}}&\text{ if }i_{k}=i_{k+1},\\
\tilde{v}_{\underline{i}\cdot s_{\alpha_{k}}}+(q^{-1}-q)\cdot v_{\underline{i}}&\text{ if }i_{k}>i_{k+1},\end{cases}

where
T_{s_{\alpha_{k}}}\in\mathcal{H}_{W}
is the generator associated with
s_{\alpha_{k}}
. This gives the desired
\mathcal{H}_{W}
-action
\gamma_{m}
on
(\mathbbm{C}^{m})^{\otimes r}
. We note that
(\gamma_{m},(\mathbbm{C}^{m})^{\otimes r})
can be described in an alternative way as follows. Following notations of
[
Gre99
,
DDF12
,
Ant
]
, let

\Lambda(m,r)

denote the set of all
\lambda:=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},...,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbbm{Z}^{m}_{\geqslant 0}
satisfying

\sum_{i=1}\lambda_{i}=r.

For each
\lambda\in\Lambda(m,r)
, consider

y_{\lambda}=(\underbrace{1,1,...,1}_{\lambda_{1}},\underbrace{2,2,...,2}_{\lambda_{2}},....,\underbrace{m,m,...,m}_{\lambda_{m}})\in(\mathbbm{Z}/m\mathbbm{Z})^{r}.

It is clear that the stabilizer subgroup of
y_{\lambda}
in
W=S_{r}
is

W_{\lambda}:=W_{y_{\lambda}}=S_{\lambda_{1}}\times...\times S_{\lambda_{m}}.

Then there is a natural
\mathbbm{C}
-linear isomorphism

(4.6)

\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda(m,r)}\mathbbm{1}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{\lambda}}}\mathcal{H}_{W}\longrightarrow(\mathbbm{C}^{m})^{\otimes r},

given as in
[
Ant
, Page 23, (13)]
for example, such that
\gamma_{m}
is the transported action from the left hand side of this isomorphism.

In view of the Bernstein presentation

\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})\simeq\mathcal{H}_{W}\otimes_{\mathbbm{C}}\mathbbm{C}[Y],

we get that as
\mathbbm{C}
-vector spaces,

\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}\simeq(\mathbbm{C}^{m})^{\otimes r}\otimes_{\mathbbm{C}}\mathbbm{C}[Y]\simeq(\mathbbm{C}^{m})^{\otimes r}\otimes_{\gamma_{m},\mathcal{H}_{W}}\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff}).

This gives a right action of
\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})
on
\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}
depending on
\gamma_{m}
, which we denote by

\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[m]}\curvearrowleft\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff}).

4.2.
The Iwahori–Matsumoto involution on
\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})

Let
l:Y\rtimes W\to\mathbbm{N}
be a length function. Consider the Iwahori–Matsumoto involution (
[
IM65
, Page 48]
)

{\rm IM}:\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})

specified by

{\rm IM}(T_{w})=(-1)^{l(w)}q^{l(w)}\cdot T_{w^{-1}}^{-1}

for all
w\in Y\rtimes W
. Here,
T_{w}\in\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})
is the natural element supported by the
I
-double coset given by
w
. We have the induced functor

{\rm IM}^{*}:\mathcal{M}_{\Diamond}(\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff}))\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{\Diamond}(\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff}))

for
\Diamond\in\left\{L,R\right\}
. Let

(4.7)

\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW,\theta}^{[m]}:=(\phi_{\theta}^{*})^{-1}(\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[m]})\in\mathcal{M}_{R}(\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{I}),\quad\mathcal{V}_{\theta}:=\phi_{\theta}^{*}(\mathcal{V}^{*})\in\mathcal{M}_{R}(\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})).

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\overline{G}=\overline{{\rm GL}}_{r}^{(n)}
be a cover of
{\rm GL}_{r}
of type (
C1
). Then
(\phi_{\theta}^{*})^{-1}
gives a natural isomorphism

(4.8)

\mathbb{F}_{\theta}:\mathcal{V}^{*}\longrightarrow(\phi^{-1}_{\theta})^{*}\circ{\rm IM}^{*}(\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[n_{\alpha}]})

of right
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{I}
-modules and thus a ring isomorphism
\varphi_{\theta}:{\rm End}_{\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})}(\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[n_{\alpha}]})\rightarrow{\rm End}_{\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{I}}(\mathcal{V}^{*})
. Moreover, for every
\pi\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{I})
, the isomorphism (
4.8
) induces a canonical
\mathbbm{C}
-isomorphism

(4.9)

\mathbb{F}_{\theta}\otimes{\rm id}_{\pi}:\mathcal{V}^{*}\otimes_{\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{I}}\pi\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[n_{\alpha}]}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})}\rm{IM}^{*}\circ\phi_{\theta}^{*}(\pi),

which is
\varphi_{\theta}^{-1}
-equivariant with respect to the left actions of
{\rm End}_{\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{I}}(\mathcal{V}^{*})
and
{\rm End}_{\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})}(\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[n_{\alpha}]})
respectively.

Proof.

We first prove (
4.8
). Since
\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}=Y/n_{\alpha}Y
, it is easy to see that the following map is a well-defined bijection.

\Lambda(n_{\alpha},r)\longrightarrow\left\{W\text{-orbits in }\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}\right\},\quad\lambda\mapsto W(y_{\lambda})

This immediately gives that

(4.10)

\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[n_{\alpha}]}=\bigoplus_{\mathcal{O}_{y}\subset\mathscr{X}_{Q,n}}\mathbbm{1}_{W_{y}}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}}\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}}).

To prove (
4.8
) we use the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\chi
be a character of the parabolic subalgebra
\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}
. Then the
\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}
-module homomorphism

{\rm IM}^{*}(\chi)\longrightarrow{\rm IM}^{*}(\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}}\chi)

defined by
1\mapsto 1\otimes 1
extends to an isomorphism of left
\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})
-modules

\nu:\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}}{\rm IM}^{*}(\chi)\longrightarrow{\rm IM}^{*}(\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}}\chi).

Proof.

The linear map
{\rm IM}^{*}(\chi)\rightarrow{\rm IM}^{*}(\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}}\chi)
defined by
1\mapsto 1\otimes 1
is an
\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}
-module homomorphism.

By Frobenius reciprocity, this
\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}
-module map induces an
\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})
-module homomorphism

\nu:\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}}{\rm IM}^{*}(\chi)\longrightarrow{\rm IM}^{*}(\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}}\chi).

Note that
\nu(h\otimes 1)={\rm IM}(h)\otimes 1
. We claim that
\nu
is an isomorphism.

It suffices to construct an inverse map of
\nu
. We consider the linear map

\nu^{\prime}:{\rm IM}^{*}(\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}}\chi)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}}{\rm IM}^{*}(\chi)

defined by
h\otimes 1\mapsto{\rm IM}(h)\otimes 1
. This is well-defined because

\nu^{\prime}(hh_{y}\otimes 1)={\rm IM}(h)\otimes\chi(h_{y})=\nu^{\prime}(h\otimes\chi(h_{y})).

Note that as vector spaces
{\rm IM}^{*}(\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}}\chi)=\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}}\chi
. Thus we have constructed a
\mathbbm{C}
-linear map
\nu^{\prime}:{\rm IM}^{*}(\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}}\chi)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}}{\rm IM}^{*}(\chi)
such that
h\otimes 1\mapsto{\rm IM}(h)\otimes 1
.

Since
\nu\circ\nu^{-1}(h\otimes 1)=h\otimes 1
and
\nu^{-1}\circ\nu(h\otimes 1)=h\otimes 1
, the
\mathbbm{C}
-linear map
\nu^{\prime}
is the inverse of
\nu
. Furthermore, since
\nu
is an
\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})
-module homomorphism, we see
\nu^{\prime}
is also an
\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})
-homomorphism. Thus
\nu
is an
\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}})
-module isomorphism.
∎

Now we can prove (
4.8
). First, if
\mathbbm{1}
is the trivial character of a standard parabolic subgroup
\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{W}
, then
{\rm IM}^{*}(\mathbbm{1})=\varepsilon
, the sign character of
\mathcal{H}_{W_{y}}
. Second, for any two
{\rm IM}^{*}(\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{{\rm aff}}))
-modules
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
we have

\rm{IM}^{*}(V_{1}\oplus V_{2})\simeq\rm{IM}^{*}(V_{1})\oplus\rm{IM}^{*}(V_{2}).

Using Lemma
2.7
, Lemma
4.2
and these two facts it follows that there is an isomorphism
\mathbb{F}_{\theta}:\mathcal{V}^{*}\longrightarrow(\phi^{-1}_{\theta})^{*}\circ{\rm IM}^{*}(\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[n_{\alpha}]})
. This completes the proof of equation (
4.8
). The remaining claims follow directly from it.
∎

4.3.
Comparison of
\mathcal{F}_{\rm GG}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}

Recall the isomorphism
\phi_{\theta}:\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})\to\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{I}
which, coupled with (
4.8
), gives rise to a natural algebra isomorphism

\varphi_{\theta}:{\rm End}_{\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})}(\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[n_{\alpha}]})\to{\rm End}_{\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{I}}(\mathcal{V}^{*})
. It induces a functor

\varphi_{\theta}^{*}:\mathcal{M}_{L}({\rm End}_{\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{I}}(\mathcal{V}^{*}))\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{L}({\rm End}_{\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})}(\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[n_{\alpha}]})).

Now we can compare
\mathcal{F}_{\rm GG}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}
as follows.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\overline{G}=\overline{{\rm GL}}_{r}^{(n)}
be a cover of
{\rm GL}_{r}
of type (
C1
). Then for every
\pi\in\mathcal{M}_{L}(\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{I})
, one has

(4.11)

\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}\circ{\rm IM}^{*}\circ\phi_{\theta}^{*}(\pi)\simeq\varphi_{\theta}^{*}\circ\mathcal{F}_{\rm GG}(\pi),

i.e., the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{L}(\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{I})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{L}({\rm End}_{\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{I}}(\mathcal{V}^{*}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{L}({\rm End}_{\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})}(\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[n_{\alpha}]}))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rm{IM}^{*}\circ\phi_{\theta}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{\rm{GG}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{\theta}^{*}}

Using (

The next corollary follows immediately from Theorem

.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
\pi
be a left

-representation that is irreducible
\iota^{-1}
-genuine and Iwahori-spherical. Then

\dim{\rm Wh}_{\psi^{-1}}(\pi)=\dim{\rm Hom}_{\widehat{\mathcal{H}}_{I}}(\mathcal{V},(\pi^{\vee})^{I})=\dim\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}({\rm IM}^{*}\circ\phi_{\theta}^{*}((\pi^{\vee})^{I})),

\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}

\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[n_{\alpha}]}

for every

An analogue of the above equality and the faithfulness for

were first observed by V. Buciumas. In fact, he already noticed that one can relate Whittaker models of such

4.4.
Left
U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m))
-action on
\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[m]}

Let
m\in\mathbbm{N}_{\geqslant 1}
be general. We recall the quantum affine group
U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m))
and its left action on
\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[m]}
. First, let
\alpha^{\dagger}
be the highest root of the root system of type
A
. Write

\alpha_{0}=-\alpha^{\dagger}.

Let

\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}:=\left[{\left\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\right\rangle}\right]_{0\leqslant i,j\leqslant m-1}

be the generalized Cartan matrix of type
A_{r}
. For convenience, we write

a_{i,j}={\left\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\right\rangle}.

Now,
U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m))
is the quantized affine Lie algebra associated with
\hat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
. More precisely, it is the
\mathbbm{C}
-algebra with generators

\left\{E_{i},F_{i},K_{i}^{\pm 1}:0\leqslant i\leqslant m-1\right\}

and relations (see
[
ChPr96
, §2.4]
and
[
Ant
, §2.5]
)

(R1)

K_{i}K_{i}^{-1}=1=K_{i}^{-1}K_{i}
and
K_{i}K_{j}=K_{j}K_{i}
.

(R2)

K_{i}E_{j}=q^{a_{i,j}}\cdot E_{j}K_{i}
and
K_{i}F_{j}=q^{-a_{i,j}}\cdot F_{j}K_{i}
.

(R3)

[E_{i},F_{j}]=\delta_{i,j}\cdot(K_{i}-K_{i}^{-1})/(q-q^{-1})
.

(R4)

\displaystyle E_{i}^{2}E_{i\pm 1}+E_{i\pm 1}E_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=(q+q^{-1})E_{i}E_{i\pm 1}E_{i}

\displaystyle F_{i}^{2}F_{i\pm 1}+F_{i\pm 1}F_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=(q+q^{-1})F_{i}F_{i\pm 1}E_{i}

\displaystyle E_{i}E_{j}

\displaystyle=E_{j}E_{i}\text{ if }i-j\neq 0,1,m-1

\displaystyle F_{i}F_{j}

\displaystyle=F_{j}F_{i}\text{ if }i-j\neq 0,1,m-1.

The computation of
i\pm 1
and
i-j
above are all valued in
\left\{0,1,...,m-1\right\}
, the fixed set of representatives of
\mathbbm{Z}/m\mathbbm{Z}
.

In terms of the basis
\left\{v_{i}:i\in\mathbbm{Z}\right\}
of
\mathbbm{C}[\mathbbm{Z}]
, one has an action of
U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m))
on
\mathbbm{C}[\mathbbm{Z}]
given by the following:

\displaystyle E_{i}\cdot v_{j}

\displaystyle=\delta_{i+1,j}\cdot v_{j-1}

\displaystyle F_{i}\cdot v_{j}

\displaystyle=\delta_{i,j}\cdot v_{j+1}

\displaystyle K_{i}\cdot v_{j}

\displaystyle=q^{\delta_{i,j}-\delta_{i+1,j}}\cdot v_{j}.

Again, the computation of
i+1
and
j\pm 1
in the subscripts of the
\delta
-functions above are valued in the set
\left\{0,1,...,m-1\right\}
of representatives for
\mathbbm{Z}/m\mathbbm{Z}
.

To have an action of
U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m))
on
\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[m]}=(\mathbbm{C}[\mathbbm{Z}])^{\otimes r}
, we note that the quantum group
U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m))
has a comultiplication map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta:U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m))\otimes_{\mathbbm{C}}U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m))}

given by

E_{i}\mapsto E_{i}\otimes K_{i}^{-1}+1\otimes E_{i},\quad F_{i}\mapsto F_{i}\otimes 1+K_{i}\otimes F_{i},\text{ and }K_{i}\mapsto K_{i}\otimes K_{i}.

This gives a well-defined representation

\Delta^{r-1}:U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m))\longrightarrow{\rm End}_{\mathbbm{C}}((\mathbbm{C}[\mathbbm{Z}])^{\otimes r}),

equivalently, a left action
U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m))\curvearrowright\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[m]}
.

It is known (see
[
Ant
, Corollary 2.55]
) that the above left action by
U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m))
commutes with the right
\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})
-action given in §
4.1
, which we write as

(4.12)

U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m))\curvearrowright\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[m]}\curvearrowleft\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff}).

From (
4.12
) one has an algebra homomorphism

\Psi:U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m))\longrightarrow{\rm End}_{\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})}(\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[m]}),

which is surjective if
m>r
, see
[
DoGr07
, Theorem 3.2.1]
or
[
DDF12
, Theorem 3.8.3]
. Here
\Psi
induces

\Psi^{*}:\mathcal{M}_{L}({\rm End}_{\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})}(\mathscr{V}_{\rm SW}^{[m]}))\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{L}(U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m))).

Definition 4.5
.

For every
s\in\mathbbm{C}
, the subspace

S_{m,s}:={\rm Span}_{\mathbbm{C}}\left\{v_{i}-q^{s}v_{i+m}:i\in\mathbbm{Z}\right\}\subset\mathbbm{C}[\mathbbm{Z}]

is

-stable, and we call
V(s):=\mathbbm{C}[\mathbbm{Z}]/S_{m,s}
the shifted evaluation representation of

associated with

.

Here
V(s)
is exactly the
U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m))
-module
V(q^{s})
in
[
ChPr96
, §2.4]
, for
a=q^{s}
there. Recall the standard evaluation representation
V_{\rm ev}(b):=(V_{q}^{\natural})_{q^{b}},b\in\mathbbm{C}
, where the latter is in the notation of

Remark 4.6
.

We may consider
U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{gl}}(m))
instead of
U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(m))

Proposition 4.7

For every

.

-homomorphism
\mathfrak{F}(w_{\beta})^{*}
be endowed with the bases
\left\{u_{y^{\prime}}^{s}:y^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{n_{\alpha}}\right\}
and
\left\{u_{y}^{-s^{*}}:y\in\mathfrak{R}_{n_{\alpha}}\right\}
respectively. Then
\mathfrak{F}(w_{\beta})^{*}
is represented by the local scattering matrix
[\mathcal{S}(y^{\prime},y;w_{\beta},\chi_{s^{*}})]_{y^{\prime},y\in\mathfrak{R}_{n_{\alpha}}}
.

Consider the matrix

[\mathcal{M}(y^{\prime},y;w_{\beta},\chi_{s^{*}})]:=[\mathcal{S}(w_{\beta}(y^{\prime}),y;w_{\beta},\chi_{s^{*}})]_{y,y^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{n_{\alpha}}}

be the
\mathbbm{C}
-endomorphism represented by
[\mathcal{M}(y^{\prime},y;w_{\beta},\chi_{s^{*}})]_{y,y^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{R}_{n_{\alpha}}}

\left\{u_{y}^{s}:y\in\mathfrak{R}_{n_{\alpha}}\right\}

\mathfrak{F}(w_{\beta})^{*}=\tau(w_{\beta})\circ R(w_{\beta},\chi_{s^{*}})

where

-homomorphism induced from the flipping map

solve the quantum Yang–Baxter equation, and the representing matrices

can be properly called

[

, Page 105]
. We also note that the matrix

[

.

5.
Some remarks

The functor
\mathcal{F}_{\rm GG}
is defined for any root system type, while

\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}^{\Psi}:\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff}))\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{L}(U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}(n_{\alpha})))

is for type
A
only. It is a natural question to ask for a generalization of
\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}^{\Psi}
, if we replace the domain or codomain of
\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}^{\Psi}
by more general root system types.

First, for a general semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
it was shown by
[
KKK18
,
Fuj20
]
that there exists a natural functor

\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}^{\mathfrak{g}}:\bigoplus_{\beta}\mathcal{M}_{L}(R^{J}(\beta))\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{L}(U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})),

where
R^{J}(\beta)
is certain quiver algebra given by Khovanov–Lauda
[
KhLa09
]
and Rouquier
[
Rou
]
. Many results are established regarding
\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}^{\mathfrak{g}}
, especially those pertaining to the categorification of the two sides of
\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}^{\mathfrak{g}}
.

On the other hand, instead of
\mathcal{H}(S_{r}^{\rm aff})
in
\mathcal{F}_{\rm SW}^{\Psi}
above, if one considers affine Hecke algebras of general Cartan type, for example of type B or C, then the recent work of W.-Q. Wang, H.-C. Bao and their collaborators
[
BKLW18
,
BWW18
,
FLLLWW
]
have made advances in the framework of quantum symmetric pairs. In particular, a Schur–Weyl type duality between affine Hecke algebras of type
B_{r}
or
C_{r}
and certain coideal subalgebra of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(k))
is established. We refer the reader to loc. cit. and references therein for details.

In the geometric setting, the work of Gaitsgory and Lysenko
[
Gai08
,
GaLy18
,
Lys17
]
represents some significant progress towards understanding a certain Whittaker category in the metaplectic setting and also its relation to the quantum groups in the framework of the quantum Langlands program (see
[
Gai16d
]
). It will be very interesting to see what would be the analogue of such results in the classical
p
-adic context, and conversely what role the quantum affine Schur–Weyl functor plays in the formulation of some geometric results.

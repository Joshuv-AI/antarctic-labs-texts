Connectedness of a space of branched coverings with a periodic cycle
Laurent Bartholdi

Abstract
. We prove the connectedness of the following locus: the space of degree- d d branched self-coverings of S 2 S^{2} with two critical points of order d d , one of which is n n -periodic.



1.
Introduction

Consider the space
\mathscr{M}_{d}
of degree-
d
branched self-coverings of the sphere
S^{2}
. Much is known about its topology
[
segal:rational
]
, for example it is connected, and its fundamental group is
\mathbb{Z}/2d
. Interesting subspaces arise by imposing dynamical conditions; the one we will focus on in this article is

\mathscr{P}_{d,n}=\{f\colon S^{2}\righttoleftarrow:f\text{ has two critical points of order $d$, one of which has period exactly }n\}.

Our main result is:

Theorem A
.

The space
\mathscr{P}_{d,n}
is path connected.

An equivalent statement is that any two maps in
\mathscr{P}_{d,n}
are isotopic through maps within
\mathscr{P}_{d,n}
. In fact, the homotopy type of
\mathscr{P}_{d,n}
can be determined quite explicitly (see Proposition
D
).

This purely topological statement has a complex analytic avatar: endow
S^{2}
with its complex structure, now written
{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
. In this manner,
{\mathbf{Rat}}_{d}=\{f\in{\mathbb{C}}(z):\deg(f)=d\}
embeds into
\mathscr{M}_{d}
. The locus
\mathscr{P}_{d,n}\cap{\mathbf{Rat}}_{d}
is an important “slice” of parameter space, whose connectedness was asked by Milnor
[
milnor:quadratic
]
. In fact, the group of Möbius transformations acts on
{\mathbf{Rat}}_{d}
by conjugation, preserving the locus
\mathscr{P}_{d,n}\cap{\mathbf{Rat}}_{d}
, so this question may be studied in the quotient space. Milnor proves that
\{f\in{\mathbf{Rat}}_{d}:f\text{ has two critical points of order }d\}/\operatorname{PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{C}})
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, for instance by identifying the map
(\alpha z^{d}+\beta)/(\gamma z^{d}+\delta)
with the pair
(\beta\gamma/(\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma),(\alpha^{d+1}\beta^{d-1}+\gamma^{d-1}\delta^{d+1})/(\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma)^{2d})
. Then the image of
\mathscr{P}_{d,n}\cap{\mathbf{Rat}}_{d}
in
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
is an algebraic curve called
\mathbf{Per}_{n}^{d}(0)
, whose connectedness is a tantalizing open problem. Theorem
A
should be seen as a solution to this problem in the topological context; since by
[
segal:rational
]
the inclusion of
{\mathbf{Rat}}_{d}
in
\mathscr{M}_{d}
is a homotopy equivalence up to dimension
d
, one may similarly hope:

Conjecture B
.

The inclusion
\mathscr{P}_{d,n}\cap{\mathbf{Rat}}_{d}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{P}_{d,n}
is a homotopy equivalence up to dimension
d
.

1.1.
Spaces of marked maps

The general setting is a structure
\Pi=\langle A,B,C,F,\deg\rangle
consisting of sets
A,B,C
with
A\subset B
and
A\subset C
, and maps
F\colon C\to B
and
\deg\colon C\to\{1,2,\dots\}
. The corresponding space of marked maps is

\mathscr{P}_{\Pi}\coloneqq\{(f,b,c):b\colon B\hookrightarrow S^{2},c\colon C\hookrightarrow S^{2},f\colon(S^{2},c(C))\to(S^{2},b(B))\text{ branched covering},\\
f\circ c=b\circ F,\;\deg_{c(x)}f=\deg(x),\;b\restriction A=c\restriction A,\;f\text{ ramifies only above }b(B)\}.

For example, setting
A=\{a_{0},\dots,a_{n-1}\}
and
B=A\cup\{v\}
and
C=A\cup\{c\}
with
\deg(a_{0})=\deg(c)=d
and
F(a_{i})=a_{i+1\bmod n}
and
F(c)=b
specifies precisely the space
\mathscr{P}_{d,n}
introduced above. The method of this article should serve to solve a variety of connectedness problems about loci
\mathscr{P}_{\Pi}
.

Thurston’s theory of iteration of rational maps involves considering their topological counterparts. A branched covering
f
is
critically finite
if the forward orbit
P_{f}
of its critical points is finite. Setting
A=B=C=P_{f}
and
F=f\restriction P_{f}
recovers the space of all maps with same post-critical behaviour as
f
, and his fundamental result implies that the connected component of
f
is contractible and contains at most one holomorphic representative (with a combinatorial criterion to determine whether there is one), unless the map is double covered by a homothety on the torus. At the other extreme,
A=\emptyset
amounts to considering branched coverings with no dynamical constraint, and subsumes the classical Hurwitz theory of coverings of surfaces
[
hurwitz:ramifiedsurfaces
]
.

By abuse of notation, we will think of
B,C
both as abstract sets (used to define
\Pi
) and as variable subsets of
S^{2}
; and abbreviate

\mathscr{P}_{\Pi}=\{f\colon(S^{2},C)\to(S^{2},B):f\restriction A=F\restriction A,\deg_{f}=\deg\}.

Kahn, Firsova and Selinger
[
firsova-kahn-selinger:deformation
]
consider, following Mary Rees
[
rees:tr
]

we may define

\mathcal{T}_{B}=\{\tau\colon S^{2}\to{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\text{ homeomorphism}\}/{\sim},

with
m\circ\tau\circ h\sim\tau
for every Möbius transformation

and every homeomorphism

of
(S^{2},B)
that is isotopic to the identity rel
B
; henceforth written

is

with

whenever

satisfy
h\circ f\simeq f\circ h^{\prime}
rel
C
and
h\simeq h^{\prime}
rel
A
. Every choice of

yields a map

, which is a covering on its image, the group of deck transformations being the centralizer
\{h\in\mathbf{Mod}_{B}:h\circ f=f\circ h\text{ rel }A\}
of

. Theorem

can be rephrased as the statement

Corollary C

For
\Pi
marking two order-

n

is a quotient of Teichmüller space

There is a fibration

1.2.
Sketch of proof

We reduce Theorem
A
to a discrete problem, by means of isotopy.

(1)

Consider the fibration
\mathscr{P}_{\Pi}\to\mathscr{Q}
and let
W
be the collection of isotopy classes of a fibre. In our concrete situation, we imagine that sets
A,B,C
of respective sizes
n,n+1,n+1
are frozen on
S^{2}
, and we consider the collection
W
of isotopy classes of maps
(S^{2},C)\to(S^{2},B)
that permute
A
cyclically. Thus
W
is the collection of isotopy classes of maps
f\in\mathscr{P}_{d,n}
with these fixed
A,B,C
.

(2)

There are two commuting actions on
W
, by the pure mapping class groups
\operatorname{Mod}(B)
and
\operatorname{Mod}(C)
, respectively by post-composition and pre-composition. The action of
\operatorname{Mod}(C)
is free with
d^{n-2}
orbits.

(3)

There is a single degree-
d
covering with two order-
d
marked critical points; this is the “Hurwitz problem” for the data
(d,d)
. In fact, by
[
thom:polynome
]
*Théorème (1) this holds more generally for any degree-
d
covering with a critical point of order
d
. It follows that there is a single
(\operatorname{Mod}(B)\times\operatorname{Mod}(C))
-orbit on
W
.

(4)

Denote respectively by
\varepsilon_{B},\varepsilon_{C}
the natural restriction maps
\operatorname{Mod}(B)\to\operatorname{Mod}(A),\operatorname{Mod}(C)\to\operatorname{Mod}(A)
. The subgroup
E=\{(g,h)\in\operatorname{Mod}(B)\times\operatorname{Mod}(C):\varepsilon_{B}(g)=\varepsilon_{C}(h)\}
acts on
W
, and movements in
E
preserve the cycle
A
. Connectedness of
\mathscr{P}_{d,n}
is therefore equivalent to transitivity of
E
on
W
.

(5)

To prove transitivity of
E
, fix a polynomial map
f\in\mathscr{P}_{d,n}
, thereby assuming
B=C
. We consider two subgroups of
E
: the diagonal
\Delta=\{(g,g):g\in\operatorname{Mod}(B)\}
, which acts on
W
by conjugation, and
P=\ker(\varepsilon_{B})
, the “point pushes” of the critical value, which act on
W
by post-composition. It suffices to show that every element of
W
may be written
pgfg^{-1}
with
g\in\operatorname{Mod}(B)
and
p\in P
.

(6)

Compute the “lifting” operation: let
L\leq\operatorname{Mod}(B)
denote the index-
d^{n-2}
subgroup of liftable classes; namely all
h\in\operatorname{Mod}(B)
such that there exists
\phi(h)\in\operatorname{Mod}(C)
with
h\circ f\cong f\circ\phi(h)
. Enough images under homomorphism
\phi\colon L\to\operatorname{Mod}(C)
may be written explicitly to show that all generators of
\operatorname{Mod}(B)
may be obtained from
P
via
\phi
and conjugation.

2.
Dynamical bisets and mapping class bisets

Let us consider the polynomial
f(z)=z^{d}+c
for which the supporting rays have angles
\{1,2\}/(d^{n}-1)
, and encode
f
group-theoretically. This means we let
A=\{a_{0}=a_{n}=0,a_{1}=c,a_{2}=c^{d}+c,\dots\}
be the length-
n
critical cycle of
f
; set
B=C=A\cup\{\infty\}
; fix a basepoint
*
in
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus A
near
\infty
; let
\pi=\pi_{1}({\mathbb{C}}\setminus A,*)
be the fundamental group; and choose “lollipop” generators
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n},\gamma_{\infty}
of
\pi
: the generator
\gamma_{i}
follows the external ray with angle
d^{i}/(d^{n}-1)
towards
a_{i}
, encircles it counterclockwise, and returns back to
*
, while the generator
\gamma_{\infty}
is a small (on the sphere) counterclockwise loop around
\infty
. Note that one of the generators of
\pi
is redundant, and we have

\pi=\langle\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n},\gamma_{\infty}\mid\gamma_{\infty}\gamma_{n}\cdots\gamma_{1}=1\rangle.

The “iterated monodromy group” theory
[
nekrashevych:ssg
]
exncodes
f
as a
biset
: a set
\mathfrak{B}(f)
with two commuting
\pi
-actions:

\mathfrak{B}(f)=\{\alpha\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{C}}\setminus A:\alpha(0)=f(\alpha(1))=*\}/{\sim},

with the left action by pre-concatenation of a loop, and the right action by post-concatenation with the unique lift of a loop that starts where
\alpha
ends.
The left action on
B_{f}
is free with
d
orbits; so
B_{f}
may be written
\pi\times\{x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\}
by choosing a system of orbit representatives. A natural choice consists of short, counterclockwise paths around
\infty
from
*
to all its preimages; for concreteness if
*\gg 0
we may choose
x_{j}(t)=\exp(\log(*)(d+1-dt)/d+2\pi ijt/d)
.

It is then straightforward to trace paths and their lifts, so as to express the right action of
\pi
on
B_{f}
; it is

\displaystyle x_{1}\cdot\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\infty}\gamma_{n}\cdot x_{2},

\displaystyle x_{i}\cdot\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=x_{i+1}\text{ if }1<i<d,

\displaystyle x_{d}\cdot\gamma_{1}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\infty}^{-1}\cdot x_{1},

\displaystyle x_{1}\cdot\gamma_{j+1}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{j}\cdot x_{1},

\displaystyle x_{i}\cdot\gamma_{j+1}

\displaystyle=x_{i}\text{ if }1<i\leq d\text{ and }1\leq j\leq n,

\displaystyle x_{1}\cdot\gamma_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\infty}\cdot x_{d},

\displaystyle x_{i+1}\cdot\gamma_{\infty}

\displaystyle=x_{i}\text{ if }1\leq i<d.

This is the natural degree-
d
generalization of the recursion defining the group
\mathfrak{K}(0^{n-1})
from
[
bartholdi-n:mandelbrot1
]
; see also
[
poirier:portraits
]
.

Note that a different choice of orbit representatives
\{x_{1}^{\prime},\dots,x_{d}^{\prime}\}
would give different formulas for the right action; the object
B_{f}
, considered up to isomorphism of
\pi
-
\pi
-bisets, is a complete invariant of
f
but its presentation relies on choices.

Let us make this a bit more precise. A
presentation
of a biset
B_{f^{\prime}}
, for a map
f^{\prime}\in W
, is a
d\times(n+1)
matrix with in entry
(i,j)
a pair
(g,k)\in\pi\times\{1,\dots,d\}
, describing the relation
x_{i}\cdot\gamma_{j}=g\cdot x_{k}
; here we use the convention
n+1=\infty
. A permutation of the
i
and
k
defines an isomorphic presentation (this amounts to reordering the orbit representatives); and for every choice of
g_{1},\dots,g_{d}\in\pi
the replacement of every
(g,k)
at position
(i,j)
with
(g_{i}gg_{k}^{-1},k)
also defines an isomorphic presentation (this amounts to replacing the orbit representatives
(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})
with
(g_{1}x_{1},\dots,g_{d}x_{d})
).

Furthermore, the left and right actions on
W
may be expressed in terms of these presentations: pre-composition by
\psi
amounts to replacing each
(g,k)
by
(\psi(g),k)
; while post-composition by
\phi
amounts to using the table to rewrite
x_{i}\cdot\phi(\gamma_{j})
in the form

and recording the result in a new table.

Now,
\operatorname{Mod}(B)
is generated by a collection of full Dehn twists between elements of
B
, and acts on
\pi
by outer automorphisms. We choose as generators the
\tau_{i,j}
for
1\leq i<j\leq\infty
which, geometrically, push
a_{i}
and
a_{j}
closer while avoiding all other paths

, and twist them fully around each other. The action of
\tau_{i,j}
on
\pi
may be written concretely as follows: set
\alpha=\gamma_{j-1}\gamma_{j-2}\cdots\gamma_{i+1}
; then

\tau_{i,j}(\gamma_{i})=\gamma_{i}^{\alpha^{-1}\gamma_{j}\alpha\gamma_{i}},\qquad\tau_{i,j}(\gamma_{j})=\gamma_{j}^{\alpha\gamma_{i}\alpha^{-1}},\qquad\tau_{i,j}(\gamma_{k})=\gamma_{k}.

The subgroup of point pushes is
P=\langle\tau_{i,\infty}:1\leq i\leq n\rangle
, and its liftable elements include
\tau_{i,\infty}^{d}
as well as
\tau_{1,\infty}

Abstract

1.
Preliminary Results

In this section, we collect those lemmas which we use to prove our desired results.

The following lemma is related to the proximity function of logarithmic derivative of a meromorphic function
f
.

Lemma 1
.

[
2
]

Suppose
f
is a transcendental meromorphic function and
k\geq 1
is an integer. Then

m\left(r,\frac{f^{(k)}}{f}\right)=S(r,f).

The following lemma estimates the characteristic function of a shift of a meromorphic function
f
.

Lemma 2
.

[
14
]

Suppose
f
is a meromorphic function of finite order
\rho
and
c
is a non-zero complex constant. Then for every
\epsilon
,

T(r,f(z+c))=T(r,f)+O(r^{\rho-1+\epsilon})+O(\log r).

Next lemma is the difference analogue of the lemma on the logarithmic derivative of a meromorphic function
f
having finite order.

Lemma 3
.

[
14
]

Suppose
f
is a meromorphic function with
\rho(f)<\infty
and
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
c_{1}\neq c_{2}
, then for each
\epsilon>0
, we have

m\left(r,\frac{f(z+c_{1})}{f(z+c_{2})}\right)=O(r^{\rho-1+\epsilon}).

The following lemma estimates the characteristic function of an exponential polynomial
f
. This lemma can be seen in
[
17
]
.

Lemma 4
.

Suppose
f
is an entire function given by

f(z)=A_{0}(z)+A_{1}(z)e^{w_{1}z^{s}}+A_{2}(z)e^{w_{2}z^{s}}+...+A_{m}(z)e^{w_{m}z^{s}},

where
A_{i}(z);0\leq i\leq m
denote either exponential polynomial of degree
<s
or polynomial in
z
,
w_{i};1\leq i\leq m
denote the constants and
s
denotes a natural number. Then

T(r,f)=C(Co(W_{0}))\frac{r^{s}}{2\pi}+o(r^{s}),

Here
C(Co(W_{0}))
is the perimeter of the convex hull of the set
W_{0}=\{0,\tilde{w_{1}},\tilde{w_{2}},...,\tilde{w_{m}}\}
.

The following lemma plays a key role to prove all our results in this article.

Lemma 5
.

[
12
]

Suppose
f_{1},f_{2},...,f_{n}(n\geq 2)
are meromorphic functions and
h_{1},h_{2},...,h_{n}
are entire functions satisfying

(1)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}e^{h_{i}}\equiv 0
.

(2)

For
1\leq j<k\leq n
,
h_{j}-h_{k}
are not constants .

(3)

For
1\leq i\leq n,1\leq m<k\leq n
,

T(r,f_{i})=o(T(r,e^{(h_{m}-h_{k})}))
as
r\to\infty
, outside a set of finite logarithmic measure.

Then
f_{i}\equiv 0

(i=1,2,...,n).

Given that

Case 1:
First, we consider

Now, we study the following three sub cases.

Case 1.1:

When

.

Also, it is well known that a meromorphic function and its derivative have same order, thus we have
T(r,f^{\prime})=S(r,e^{Q})=T(r,f)

2

T(r,f_{c})=S(r,e^{Q})

.

From equation (
9

\displaystyle T(r,ue^{\nu})

\displaystyle=T(r,f^{n}+wf^{n-1}f^{\prime}+qe^{Q}f_{c})

\displaystyle\leq T(r,f^{n-1})+T(r,f+wf^{\prime})+T(r,q)+T(r,e^{Q})+T(r,f_{c})+\log 2

\displaystyle\leq nT(r,f)+T(r,f^{\prime})+T(r,e^{Q})+T(r,f_{c})+T(r,q)+O(1)

\displaystyle=T(r,e^{Q})+S(r,e^{Q})\qquad\mbox{(Since $T(r,f)=T(r,f^{\prime})=T(r,f_{c})=S(r,e^{Q})$)}

and

\displaystyle T(r,e^{Q})

\displaystyle=T\left(r,\frac{qf_{c}e^{Q}}{qf_{c}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq T(r,qf_{c}e^{Q}+wf^{n-1}f^{\prime}+f^{n})+T(r,wf^{n-1}f^{\prime}+f^{n})+T\left(r,\frac{1}{qf_{c}}\right)+O(1)

\displaystyle\leq T(r,ue^{\nu})+nT(r,f)+T(r,f^{\prime})+T(r,f_{c})+T(r,q)+O(1)

\displaystyle=T(r,ue^{\nu})+S(r,e^{Q}).

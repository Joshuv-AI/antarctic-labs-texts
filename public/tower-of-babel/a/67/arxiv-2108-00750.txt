On the topology of the space of almost complex structures on the six sphere
Bora Ferlengez, Gustavo Granja, Aleksandar Milivojević

Abstract
. The space of orientation-compatible almost complex structures on S 6 S^{6} naturally contains a copy of ℝ ​ ℙ 7 \mathbb{RP}^{7} . We show that the inclusion induces an isomorphism on fundamental groups and rational homotopy groups. We also compute the homotopy fiber of the inclusion and the homotopy groups of the space of almost complex structures in terms of the homotopy groups of S 7 S^{7} . Our approach lends itself to generalization to components of almost complex structures with c 1 = 0 c_{1}=0 on six–manifolds.



1.
Introduction

In this note we consider the topology of the space of almost complex structures
\mathcal{J}(S^{6})
on the six–dimensional sphere
S^{6}
. By an almost complex structure we will mean one which induces a fixed orientation and which is orthogonal with respect to some fixed metric on
S^{6}
; swapping the orientation, varying the metric, or removing the metric compatibility condition altogether results in a homotopy equivalent space via the appropriate natural map. For this reason we from now on take
S^{6}
to be the unit sphere in the imaginary octonions endowed with the round metric and the standard orientation.

We will employ the useful description of
\mathcal{J}(S^{6})
as the space of sections of the natural
SO(6)/U(3)\cong\mathbb{CP}^{3}
bundle over
S^{6}
whose fibers are the complex structures on the given tangent vector space. Likely one would be interested primarily in the smooth sections of this bundle, corresponding to smooth almost complex structures; the inclusion of the space of smooth sections into the space of continuous sections is a homotopy equivalence, so we will study the latter without loss of generality.

Let
J^{\textrm{cn}}
denote the canonical almost complex structure given on
T_{p}S^{6}
by octonion multiplication

J^{\textrm{cn}}_{p}(v)=pv.

The group
SO(7)
acts naturally on
the space of sections by

(A\cdot J)_{p}(v)=AJ_{A^{-1}p}(A^{-1}v).

For our canonical almost complex structure
J^{\textrm{cn}}
we have that
A\cdot J^{\textrm{cn}}=J^{\textrm{cn}}
means

A(pv)=(Ap)(Av),

and so the isotropy of this action is the group
G_{2}
of real algebra automorphisms of the octonions. We thus obtain an inclusion

SO(7)/G_{2}\cong\mathbb{RP}^{7}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\hookrightarrow}}\mathcal{J}(S^{6}).

This subspace was previously studied by Battaglia
[
Ba90
]
. Her description of the space of complex structures on
\mathbb{R}^{6}
in terms of octonion multiplication is crucial to the present work (see Proposition
2.1
).

The aim of this note is to show that the homotopy fiber of the inclusion
\mathbb{RP}^{7}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle j}}{{\hookrightarrow}}\mathcal{J}(S^{6})
is a path component of the sevenfold based loop space
\Omega^{7}S^{7}
of the seven–sphere. As the homotopy groups of the latter space are torsion, we recover the result of the first named author
[
F18
, §4]
that
\mathcal{J}(S^{6})
has the rational homotopy groups of
S^{7}
(or
\mathbb{RP}^{7}
), and see that the natural inclusion of
\mathbb{RP}^{7}
induces an isomorphism on rational homotopy groups and on fundamental groups. The non-contractibility of the homotopy fiber of the inclusion also answers a question communicated by Dennis Sullivan and stated in loc. cit., on whether this inclusion is a genuine (i.e. integral) homotopy equivalence.

We then touch upon the general problem of the topology of the space of almost complex structures on a closed six–manifold in the
component of an almost complex structure with
c_{1}=0
. This, along with the case of
c_{1}\neq 0
, will be studied more systematically in upcoming work.

Lemma 2.2
.

The Euler number of the normal bundle to
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
in
\Gr_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
is 1.

Proof.

Near each
P\in\Gr_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R}^{6})
we have standard coordinates with values in
\Hom(P,P^{\perp})\cong\Hom(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{4})
. If
P\in\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, then
both
P
and
P^{\perp}
are complex planes and hence
\Hom(P,P^{\perp})
breaks up into a direct sum of the subspaces of
complex linear and complex anti-linear maps

\Hom(P,P^{\perp})=\Hom_{\mathbb{C}}(P,P^{\perp})\oplus\Hom_{\overline{\mathbb{C}}}(P,P^{\perp}).

The first summand can naturally be identified with the tangent space
T_{P}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, while the second can be naturally identified with the fiber over
P
of the normal bundle
N\mathbb{CP}^{2}
.
Our aim is to compute
c_{2}(N\mathbb{CP}^{2})
, as the Euler number is
obtained by evaluating this cohomology class on the fundamental
class of
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
.

Let
\tau
denote the tautological bundle over
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, and

\tau^{\perp}
its orthogonal complement in the trivial bundle
\mathbb{CP}^{2}\times\mathbb{C}^{3}
.
Since
\Hom_{\overline{\mathbb{C}}}(P,P^{\perp})=(\overline{P})^{*}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}P^{\perp}\cong P\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}P^{\perp}
,
we have
N\mathbb{CP}^{2}\cong\tau\otimes\tau^{\perp}
.

Let
a=c_{1}(\tau)\in H^{2}(\mathbb{CP}^{2})
. Then
(1+a)(1+c_{1}(\tau^{\perp})+c_{2}(\tau^{\perp}))=1
and therefore

c_{1}(\tau^{\perp})=-a
,
c_{2}(\tau^{\perp})=a^{2}
. This computation gives us the homomorphism
induced on cohomology by the map
\mathbb{CP}^{2}\to BU(1)\times BU(2)
which expresses
N\mathbb{CP}^{2}\cong\tau\otimes\tau^{\perp}
as the pullback of the tensor product
\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2}
of the tautological
bundles over
BU(1)\times BU(2)
. It remains to compute
c_{2}(\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})
.

The restriction of
\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2}
to the classifying space of the standard maximal torus is the bundle

(2)

EU(1)^{3}\otimes_{U(1)^{3}}\mathbb{C}^{2}\to BU(1)^{3}

where

acts on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
via the character

(3)

\chi(e^{i\theta},e^{i\alpha},e^{i\beta})=(e^{i(\alpha+\theta)},e^{i(\beta+\theta)})

Let

for

denote the Chern class of the tautological line bundle over each of the

factors. The second Chern class of the bundle (

) is

(x_{1}+x_{2})(x_{1}+x_{3})=x_{1}^{2}+x_{1}(x_{2}+x_{3})+x_{2}x_{3}

Therefore

∎

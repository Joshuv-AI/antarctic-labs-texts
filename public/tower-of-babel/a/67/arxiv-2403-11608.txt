Truncated theta series from the Bailey lattice
Xiangyu Ding

Abstract




Abstract.
In 2012, Andrews and Merca obtained a truncated version of Euler’s pentagonal number theorem and showed the nonnegativity related to partition functions. Meanwhile, Andrews and Merca, Guo and Zeng independently conjectured that the truncated Jacobi triple product
series has nonnegative coefficients, which has been confirmed analytically and also combinatorially. In 2022, Merca proposed a stronger version for this conjecture. In this paper, by applying Agarwal, Andrews and Bressoud’s identity derived from the Bailey lattice, we obtain a truncated version for the Jacobi triple product series with odd basis, which reduces to the Andrews–Gordon identity as a special instance. As consequences, we obtain new truncated forms for Euler’s pentagonal number theorem, Gauss’ theta series on triangular numbers and square numbers, which lead to inequalities for certain partition functions. Moreover, by considering a truncated theta series involving
\ell
-regular partitions, we confirm a conjecture proposed by Ballantine and Merca about
6
-regular partitions and show that Merca’s stronger conjecture on truncated Jacobi triple product series holds when
R=3S
for
S\geq 1
.

Keywords:
partition functions; truncated theta series; the Bailey lattice; Euler’s pentagonal number theorem

AMS Classification:
05A17, 33D15

Introduction

In the theory of integer partitions, one of the most important and beautiful identities is Euler’s pentagonal number theorem
[
6
, p.11]
,

\displaystyle(q;q)_{\infty}=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{j(3j+1)/2},

(1.1)

from which a recurrence relation for the partition function
p(n)
can be derived as follows
[
6
]

\displaystyle p(n)+\sum_{j\geq 1}(-1)^{j}\big(p(n-j(3j-1)/2)+p(n-j(3j+1)/2)\big)=0,

(1.2)

where
p(n)=0
if
n<0
.

In 2012, Andrews and Merca recaptured Shanks’ truncated version
[
30
, (2)]
of Euler’s pentagonal number theorem, that is,

\sum_{j=-k}^{k}(-1)^{j}q^{j(3j+1)/2}=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{n}(q;q)_{k}q^{\binom{n}{2}+(k+1)n}}{(q;q)_{n}},

and obtained the following form
[
7
, Lemma 1.2]
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{j=-k}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}q^{j(3j+1)/2}=1+(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\binom{k}{2}+(k+1)n}}{(q;q)_{n}}{n-1\brack k-1}.

(1.3)

In addition, they gave a combinatorial interpretation for the coefficients of
q^{n}
on both sides of (
1.3
) as follows

\displaystyle(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}\big(p(n-j(3j+1)/2)-p(n-j(3j+5)/2-1)\big)=M_{k}(n),

(1.4)

where
M_{k}(n)

[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
denotes the number of partitions of
n
in which
k
is the least integer that is not a part and there are more parts
>k
than there are
<k
. It directly leads to the following inequality for the partition function
p(n)

\displaystyle(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{j=-k}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}\big(p(n-j(3j+1)/2\big)\geq 0,

(1.5)

where
n,k\geq 1
and the strict inequality holds if
n\geq k(3k+1)/2
.
In 2015, Kolitsch and Burnette
[
24
]
reproved (
1.4
) combinatorially by constructing special partition pairs.

Later, for Gauss’ theta series on triangular and square numbers
[
6
, p.23]

\displaystyle\frac{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(-q;q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-q)^{n(n+1)/2},

(1.6)

\displaystyle\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(-q;q)_{\infty}}=1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}},

(1.7)

Guo and Zeng
[
19
]
obtained the following truncated forms, respectively,

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-q;q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}\sum_{j=-k}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}q^{j(2j+1)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=1+(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\frac{\left(-q;q^{2}\right)_{k}\left(-q;q^{2}\right)_{n-k}q^{2(k+1)n-k}}{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{n}}{n-1\brack k-1}_{q^{2}},

(1.8)

\displaystyle\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{j=-k}^{k}(-1)^{j}q^{j^{2}}=1+(-1)^{k}\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{(-q)_{k}(-1)_{n-k}q^{(k+1)n}}{(q)_{n}}{n-1\brack k}.

(1.9)

From the above truncated forms, they also showed that for
n,k\geq 1
, there holds

\displaystyle(-1)^{k-1}\sum_{j=-k}^{k-1}(-1)^{j}{{\rm{pod}}}\big(n-j(2j+1)\big)\geq 0,

(1.10)

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\sum_{j=-k}^{k}(-1)^{j}\overline{p}\left(n-j^{2}\right)\geq 0,

(1.11)

where
{\rm{pod}}(n)
denotes the number of partitions of
n
with distinct odd parts and
\overline{p}(n)
denotes the number of overpartitions of
n
that are partitions in which the first occurrence of each part may be overlined or not, see, for example
[
15
,
21
]
for details.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=1+(-1)^{k-1}\frac{\left(-q;q^{2}\right)_{k}}{\left(q^{2};q^{2}\right)_{k-1}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{k(2j+2k+1)}\left(-q^{2k+2j+3};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{2k+2j+2};q^{2}\right)_{\infty}},

(1.12)

\displaystyle\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{j=-k}^{k}(-1)^{j}q^{j^{2}}=1+2(-1)^{k}\frac{(-q;q)_{k}}{(q;q)_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{(k+1)(k+j+1)}\left(-q^{k+j+2};q\right)_{\infty}}{\left(1-q^{k+j+1}\right)\left(q^{k+j+2};q\right)_{\infty}}.

\displaystyle\qquad=1+(-1)^{k-1}\frac{(-q;q)_{k}}{(q;q)_{k-1}}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{k(k+1+j)}\left(-q^{k+2+j};q\right)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{k+2+j};q\right)_{\infty}}.

In this paper, we aim to find the truncated theta series for Andrews–Gordon type identities and study the nonnegative properties for the coefficients. Recall that the well-known Andrews–Gordon identity is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{k-1}\geq 0}\frac{q^{N_{1}^{2}+N_{2}^{2}+\cdots+N_{k-1}^{2}+N_{i}+\cdots+N_{k-1}}}{(q)_{n_{1}}(q)_{n_{2}}\cdots(q)_{n_{k-1}}}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=1\\
n\neq 0,\pm i(\bmod 2k+1)\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\left(1-q^{n}\right)^{-1},

(1.15)

where
1\leq i\leq k
,

. This identity arises from the first and the second Rogers–Ramanujan identities and has been generalized widely in combinatorics
[

[

,

From Jennings-Shaffer and Milas’ work

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{k}\geq 0}\frac{\big(-q^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)_{N_{1}+\ell}q^{\frac{N_{1}\left(N_{1}+1\right)}{2}+\sum_{j=2}^{k}(N_{j}^{2}+(2\ell+1)N_{j})+(\ell+\frac{1}{2})N_{1}-\sum_{j=1}^{k-i}N_{j}}}{(q)_{n_{1}}(q)_{n_{2}}\cdots(q)_{n_{k}}(q)_{n_{k}+2\ell}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=\frac{\big(-q^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)_{\infty}}{(q)_{\infty}}\bigg(\sum_{n\geq|\ell|}-\sum_{n\leq-|\ell|-1}\bigg)q^{\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n+\frac{i}{2k+1}\right)^{2}-\frac{(2k\ell+i+\ell)^{2}}{4k+2}},

By applying Agarwal, Andrews and Bressoud’s Bailey lattice, we obtain a truncated form of Andrews–Gordon type for the Jacobi triple product series (

Theorem 1.2
.

We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(q^{i+1},q^{2k-i},q^{2k+1};q^{2k+1}\right)_{\infty}}\sum_{j=-\ell}^{\ell-1}(-1)^{j}q^{(k+\frac{1}{2})j^{2}+(k-i-\frac{1}{2})j}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{(-1)^{\ell-1}q^{(k+\frac{1}{2})(\ell^{2}+\ell)-(i+1)\ell}(q)_{\infty}}{\left(q^{i+1},q^{2k-i},q^{2k+1};q^{2k+1}\right)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{k}\geq 0}\frac{(q^{2\ell+1})^{N_{1}+N_{2}+\cdots+N_{k}}q^{N_{1}^{2}+N_{2}^{2}+N_{3}^{2}+\cdots+N_{k}^{2}-N_{1}-N_{2}-N_{3}-\cdots-N_{i}}(1-q^{\ell})}{(q)_{n_{1}}(q)_{n_{2}}\cdots(q)_{n_{k-1}}(q)_{n_{k}}(q)_{n_{k}+2\ell}(1-q^{\ell+n_{k}})},

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we adopt standard notations and terminologies
for
q
-series, see, for example,
[
17
]
. We assume that
|q|<1
.
The
q
-shifted factorial is denoted by

(a)_{n}=(a;q)_{n}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{\it if $n=0$},\\[5.69054pt]
(1-a)(1-aq)\cdots(1-aq^{n-1}),&\text{\it if $n\geq 1$}.\end{cases}

We also use the notation

(a)_{\infty}=(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{n}).

There are more compact notations for the multiple
q
-shifted factorials

\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{m};q)_{n}

\displaystyle=(a_{1};q)_{n}(a_{2};q)_{n}\cdots(a_{m};q)_{n},

\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{m};q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle=(a_{1};q)_{\infty}(a_{2};q)_{\infty}\cdots(a_{m};q)_{\infty}.

The Gaussian polynomials, or
q
-binomial coefficients are given by

{n\brack k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,&\hbox{if $k<0$ or $k>n$},\\
\dfrac{(q;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{k}(q;q)_{n-k}},&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

We also denote the case when
q\mapsto q^{t}
by
{n\brack k}_{q^{t}}
with
t\geq 2
.

Recall that the
basic hypergeometric series
{}_{r}\phi_{s}
is defined as follows

{{}_{r}\phi_{s}}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a_1,a_2,\dots,a_r}{b_1,b_2,\dots,b_s};q,x\bigg)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{r};q)_{n}}{(q,b_{1},\dots,b_{s};q)_{n}}\Big[(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}}\Big]^{1+s-r}x^{n}.

To prove the results in this paper, we will need the following transformation
formulas for basic hypergeometric series.
The well-known transformation formula for
{}_{2}\phi_{1}
series due to
Heine is
[
17
, (III.3)]

\displaystyle{}_{2}\phi_{1}(a,b;c;q,z)=\frac{(abz/c;q)_{\infty}}{(z;q)_{\infty}}{}_{2}\phi_{1}(c/a,c/b;c;q,abz/c),

(2.1)

where
\text{max}\{|z|,|abz/c|\}<1
so that both
{}_{2}\phi_{1}
series converge.
Two of the transformation formulas for
{{}_{3}\phi_{2}}
series are
[
17
, (III.9)-(III.10)]

\displaystyle{{}_{3}\phi_{2}}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,b,c}{d,e};q,\frac{de}{abc}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{(e/a,de/bc;q)_{\infty}}{(e,de/abc;q)_{\infty}}\,{{}_{3}\phi_{2}}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{a,d/b,d/c}{d,de/bc};q,\frac{e}{a}\bigg)

(2.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{(b,de/ab,de/bc;q)_{\infty}}{(d,e,de/abc;q)_{\infty}}\,{{}_{3}\phi_{2}}\bigg(\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{d/b,e/b,de/abc}{de/ab,de/bc};q,b\bigg)

(2.3)

provided that
\max\{|b|,|e/a|,|de/abc|\}<1
.
The Rogers–Fine identity
[
29
, p.15]
is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha;q)_{n}\tau^{n}}{(\beta;q)_{n}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha;q)_{n}(\alpha\tau q/\beta;q)_{n}\beta^{n}\tau^{n}q^{n^{2}-n}\left(1-\alpha\tau q^{2n}\right)}{(\beta;q)_{n}(\tau;q)_{n+1}},

(2.4)

where
|\tau|<1
.
The well-known Jacobi triple product identity is given by
[
17
, II.28]

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}q^{k\choose 2}a^{k}=(a,q/a,q;q)_{\infty}.

(2.5)

Bailey in
[
10
]
discovered a powerful tool to prove and construct

-identities, which is given as a pair of sequences of rational functions
(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n})_{n\geq 0}
with respect to

\beta_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{\alpha_{j}}{(q;q)_{n-j}(aq;q)_{n+j}}.

(2.6)

It was named Bailey pair and has been studied widely. In
[
4
]
, Agarwal, Andrews and Bressoud gave the following identity deduced from the Bailey lattice.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}
be sequences satisfying equation (
2.6
) and let
0\leq i\leq k
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq m_{1}\geq m_{2}\geq\ldots>m_{k}\geq 0}\frac{\left(\rho_{1}\right)_{m_{1}}\ldots\left(\rho_{k}\right)_{m_{k}}\left(\sigma_{1}\right)_{m_{1}}\ldots\left(\sigma_{k}\right)_{m_{k}}}{(q)_{n-m_{1}}\ldots(q)_{m_{k-1}-m_{k}}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\left(a/\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}\right)_{n-m_{1}}\cdots\left(a/\rho_{i}\sigma_{i}\right)_{m_{i-1}-m_{i}}}{\left(a/\rho_{1}\right)_{n}\cdots\left(a/\rho_{i}\right)_{m_{i-1}}\left(a/\sigma_{1}\right)_{n}\cdots\left(a/\sigma_{i}\right)_{m_{i-1}}}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\left(aq/\rho_{i+1}\sigma_{i+1}\right)_{m_{i}-m_{i+1}}\cdots\left(aq/\rho_{k}\sigma_{k}\right)_{m_{k-1}-m_{k}}a^{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{k}}q^{m_{i+1}+\cdots+m_{k}}\beta_{m_{k}}}{\left(aq/\rho_{i+1}\right)_{m_{i}}\cdots\left(aq/\rho_{k}\right)_{m_{k-1}}\left(aq/\sigma_{i+1}\right)_{m_{i}}\cdots\left(aq/\sigma_{k}\right)_{m_{k-1}}\left(\rho_{1}\sigma_{1}\right)^{m_{1}}\cdots\left(\rho_{k}\sigma_{k}\right)^{m_{k}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{0}}{(q)_{n}(a)_{n}}+\sum_{t=1}^{n}\frac{\left(\rho_{1},\sigma_{1},\ldots,\rho_{i},\sigma_{i}\right)_{t}a^{it}(1-a)}{\left(a/\rho_{1},a/\sigma_{1},\ldots,a/\rho_{i},a/\sigma_{i}\right)_{t}\left(\rho_{1}\cdots\rho_{i}\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{i}\right)^{t}(q)_{n-t}(a)_{n+t}}

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\frac{\left(\rho_{i+1},\sigma_{i+1},\ldots,\rho_{k},\sigma_{k}\right)_{t}(aq)^{(k-i)t}\alpha_{t}}{\left(aq/\rho_{i+1},aq/\sigma_{i+1},\ldots,aq/\rho_{k},aq/\sigma_{k}\right)_{t}\left(\rho_{i+1}\cdots\rho_{k}\sigma_{i+1}\cdots\sigma_{k}\right)^{t}\left(1-aq^{2t}\right)}

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(\rho_{i+1},\sigma_{i+1},\ldots,\rho_{k},\sigma_{k}\right)_{t-1}(aq)^{(k-i)(t-1)}aq^{2t-2}\alpha_{t-1}}{(aq/\rho_{i+1},aq/\sigma_{i+1},\ldots,aq/\rho_{k},aq/\sigma_{k})_{t-1}(\rho_{i+1}\cdots\rho_{k}\sigma_{i+1}\cdots\sigma_{k})^{t-1}(1-aq^{2t-2})}\bigg).

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}=\frac{\left(1-aq^{2n}\right)}{(1-a)}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}}(a)_{n}(b)_{n}(c)_{n}}{(q)_{n}(aq/b)_{n}(aq/c)_{n}}\left(\frac{aq}{bc}\right)^{n},

\displaystyle\beta_{n}=\frac{(aq/bc;q)_{n}}{(q,aq/b,aq/c;q)_{n}}.

(2.7)

Furthermore, letting

(2.9)

Based on the truncated theta series, we will further study the inequalities related to certain integer partitions. Recall that a partition of a nonnegative integer

(2.10)

4
The three classical truncated theta series

In this section, we will study the truncated forms for Euler’s pentagonal number theorem, Gauss’ theta series on triangular numbers and square numbers. As applications, the nonnegativity of the corresponding coefficients can be directly derived which will lead to some inequalities for certain partition functions.

It is straightforward to see that the truncated Euler’s pentagonal number theorem as given in Theorem
1.3
can be deduced by taking
k=1
and
i=0
in Theorem
1.2
. First, we show that our truncated form for Euler’s pentagonal number theorem is equivalent to Andrews and Merca’s result (
1.3
) by verifying the following identity.

Theorem 4.1
.

For
\ell
a positive integer, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=\ell}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\binom{\ell}{2}+(\ell+1)n}}{(q;q)_{n}}{n-1\brack\ell-1}=q^{\frac{3}{2}\ell^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\ell}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{(2\ell+1)n+n^{2}}(1-q^{\ell})}{(q;q)_{n}(q;q)_{n+2\ell}(1-q^{n+\ell})}.

Proof.

We see that the right hand side can be rewritten as follows

\displaystyle\quad\ q^{\frac{3}{2}\ell^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\ell}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{(2\ell+1)n+n^{2}}(1-q^{\ell})}{(q;q)_{n}(q;q)_{n+2\ell}(1-q^{n+\ell})}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{\frac{3}{2}\ell^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\ell}}{(q;q)_{2\ell}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{(2\ell+1)n+n^{2}}(q^{\ell};q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}(q^{2\ell+1};q)_{n}(q^{\ell+1};q)_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{\frac{3}{2}\ell^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\ell}}{(q;q)_{2\ell}}\frac{1}{(q^{2\ell+1};q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{(2\ell+1)n+\frac{n^{2}-n}{2}}(q;q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}(q^{\ell+1};q)_{n}}

\displaystyle\quad(\text{by}\ (\ref{3phi2-1})\text{ with }a,\ c\to\infty,\ b=q^{\ell},\ d=q^{\ell+1},\ e=q^{2\ell+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{\frac{3}{2}\ell^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\ell}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}(q^{\ell+1};q)_{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{(\ell+1)n}(q^{\ell};q)_{n}}{(q;q)_{n}(q^{\ell+1};q)_{n}}

\displaystyle\quad(\text{ by}\ (\ref{Heine-2})\ \text{with}\ b\to 0,\ a=q^{\ell},\ z=c=q^{\ell+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=\ell}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\binom{\ell}{2}+(\ell+1)n}}{(q;q)_{n}}{n-1\brack\ell-1}\qquad(\text{ by shifting $n$ to $n-\ell$}).

Furthermore, from our result (
1.17
) and by using the following expansion

\frac{1-q^{\ell}}{1-q}=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}q^{i},

(4.1)

it is direct to see that the inequality for partition functions (
1.5
) holds.
More precisely, from (
1.17
) we derive that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=-\ell}^{\ell-1}(-1)^{n}q^{n(3n+1)/2}

\displaystyle=1+(-1)^{\ell-1}q^{\frac{3}{2}\ell^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\ell}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{(2\ell+1)n+n^{2}}(1-q^{\ell})}{(q;q)_{n}(q;q)_{n+2\ell}(1-q^{n+\ell})}

\displaystyle=1+(-1)^{\ell-1}q^{\frac{3}{2}\ell^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\ell}\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{(2\ell+1)n+n^{2}+i}}{(q;q)_{n}(q^{2};q)_{n+2\ell-1}(1-q^{n+\ell})}.

Obviously, the coefficients of
q^{n}
in the sum of the last expression are nonnegative and thus (
1.5
) holds.

Moreover, recall that a pair of sequences

(\gamma_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and
(\delta_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is called
a conjugate Bailey pair relative to
a
if

\gamma_{n}=\sum_{r=n}^{\infty}\frac{\delta_{r}}{(q;q)_{r-n}(aq;q)_{r+n}}

(4.2)

for all
n\geq 0
. We note that the above Theorem
4.1

Lemma 4.2
.

For
n\geq 0

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{n}(aq^{n+1}/b;q)_{\infty}}{(aq;q)_{\infty}}\,\sum_{r=n}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(q;q)_{r-n}(1-bq^{r})}\Big(\frac{aq^{n+1}}{b}\Big)^{r},

\displaystyle\delta_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{n}q^{n(n+1)}}{1-bq^{n}},

(4.3)

form a conjugate Bailey pair.

Proof.

To show that the sequences
\gamma_{n}
and
\delta_{n}
form a conjugate Bailey pair, it is equivalent to prove the following identity

\sum_{r=n}^{\infty}\frac{b^{n}(aq^{n+1}/b;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{r-n}(1-bq^{r})}\Big(\frac{aq^{n+1}}{b}\Big)^{r}=\sum_{r=n}^{\infty}\frac{a^{r}q^{r(r+1)}(aq^{r+n+1};q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{r-n}(1-bq^{r})}.

By using the identity
[
17

\displaystyle(z;q)_{\infty}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-z)^{k}q^{\binom{k}{2}}}{(q;q)_{k}}

to expand both infinite products on both sides of the above identity and then extracting the coefficients of
(-a)^{k}q^{\binom{k}{2}+(n+1)k}
, it turns to be that for all

\sum_{r=n}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{r}b^{n-k}q^{\binom{r+1}{2}-rk}}{(q;q)_{k-r}(q;q)_{r-n}(1-bq^{r})}=\sum_{r=n}^{k}\frac{(-1)^{r}q^{\binom{r+1}{2}-rn}}{(q;q)_{k-r}(q;q)_{r-n}(1-bq^{r})}.

Further writing it as a single sum, shifting the summation index

then replacing
(b,k)\mapsto(bq^{-n},k+n)
and simplifying, the above identity is equivalent to

\sum_{r=0}^{k}(-1)^{r}q^{\binom{r+1}{2}-rk}\frac{1-(bq^{r})^{k}}{1-bq^{r}}{k\brack r}=0.

Denoting the summation on the left by
L_{k}(b,q)
, the coefficient of

by
[b^{i}]L_{k}(b,q)

it follows from the formula (

By the

[

(z;q)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}{{n}\brack{k}}q^{\binom{k}{2}}z^{k},

we obtain

\sum_{r=0}^{k}(-1)^{r}q^{\binom{r}{2}-(k-i-1)r}{{k}\brack{r}}=(q^{1+i-k};q)_{k}=0

for all

, which deduce that (
4.3
) form a conjugate Bailey pair.

We note that by substituting the conjugate Bailey pair (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q;q)_{n}}\sum_{r=n}^{\infty}\frac{q^{(n+1)r}}{(q;q)_{r-n}(1-q^{r})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=n}^{\infty}\frac{q^{r(r+1)}}{(q;q)_{r-n}(q;q)_{r+n}(1-q^{r})}

which is equivalent to Theorem
4.1

Next, we prove the truncated Gauss theta series on triangular numbers as given in Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.
By inserting the Bailey pair (
2.8
) into (
3
) and setting

, we are led to

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1}\geq m_{2}\geq\cdots\geq m_{k}\geq 0}\frac{(-a^{\frac{1}{2}})_{m_{1}}a^{\frac{m_{1}}{2}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{k}}q^{\frac{m_{1}\left(m_{1}-1\right)}{2}+m_{2}^{2}+m_{3}^{2}+\cdots+m_{k}^{2}-m_{2}-m_{3}-\cdots-m_{i}}(a^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{-\frac{1}{2}})_{m_{k}}}{(q)_{m_{1}-m_{2}}(q)_{m_{2}-m_{3}}\cdots(q)_{m_{k-1}-m_{k}}(a,q,a^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\frac{1}{2}})_{m_{k}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-a^{\frac{1}{2}})_{\infty}}{(a)_{\infty}}\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}a^{kn}q^{kn^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\right)n}+\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}a^{kn+i-k+\frac{1}{2}}q^{kn^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}+i-2k\right)n+k-i-\frac{1}{2}}\Big).

Replacing

q^{2\ell+1}
, it leads to that

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1}\geq m_{2}\geq\cdots\geq m_{k}\geq 0}\frac{(-q^{\ell+\frac{1}{2}})_{m_{1}}(q^{2\ell+1})^{\frac{m_{1}}{2}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{k}}q^{\frac{m_{1}\left(m_{1}-1\right)}{2}+m_{2}^{2}+m_{3}^{2}+\cdots+m_{k}^{2}-m_{2}-m_{3}-\cdots-m_{i}}(q^{\ell})_{m_{k}}}{(q)_{m_{1}-m_{2}}(q)_{m_{2}-m_{3}}\cdots(q)_{m_{k-1}-m_{k}}(q^{2\ell+1},q,q^{\ell+1})_{m_{k}}}

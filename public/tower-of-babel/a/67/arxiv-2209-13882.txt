1.
Introduction

An important subject to study in number theory is the non-vanishing issue of central values of
L
-functions as these central values carry deep arithmetic information. For example, the well-known Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture relates the rank of an elliptic curve to the order of vanishing at the central point of the associated
L
-function. It is now a standard procedure in analytic number theory to apply the mollifier method to obtain a positive portion of non-vanishing result concerning a given family of
L
-functions. This approach goes back to the work of A. Selberg
[
Selberg42
]
, who showed that a positive proportion of the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function
\zeta(s)
lie on the critical line.

The method of mollifier involves with constructing a short Dirichlet polynomial called a mollifier that approximates the inverse of the corresponding
L
-function. A classical way to do so is based on the Dirichlet series of the inverse of the given
L
-function and a mollifier is essentially obtained using a truncation of the corresponding Dirichlet series. In order to achieve a positive portion of non-vanishing result, one then needs to evaluate asymptotically the first and second moments of the family of
L
-functions twisted by the mollifier. As this process heavily relies on information concerning the second moment, such tactic often becomes ineffective due to the lack of the needed result.

In
[
Gonek12
]
, S. M. Gonek constructed approximations of
\zeta(s)
and Dirichlet
L
-functions using truncations of their Euler products to study the non-trivial zeros of these functions. In
[
Radziwill&Sound
]
, M. Radziwiłł and K. Soundararajan developed an upper bounds principle to study upper bounds for moments of families of
L
-functions. A dual lower bounds principle was introduced by W. Heap and K. Soundararajan in
[
H&Sound
]
. Both principles make crucial use of mollifiers that are constructed based on truncated Euler products of the
L
-functions involved. This way of obtaining mollifiers has been further carried out in the work of S. Lester and M. Radziwiłł
[
LR21
]
to study sign changes of Fourier coefficients of half-integral weight modular forms and in the work of C. David, A. Florea and M. Lalin
[
DFL21
]
to establish a positive proportion non-vanishing result of cubic
L
-functions in the function field setting. We point out here that, the lower bounds principle enunciated in
[
H&Sound
]
actually allows one to achieve a positive portion of non-vanishing result concerning central values of families of
L
-functions, at least under the assumption of the generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH). This approach becomes particularly appealing in situations when our understanding on the related second moment is insufficient for one to apply the classical mollifier method. In fact, one may circumvent the evaluation of the second moment using the new mollifiers by incorporating a powerful method of K. Soundararajan
[
Sound2009
]
with its refinement by A. J. Harper
[
Harper
]
to bound twisted moments of
L
-functions from the above under GRH. This strategy has been employed in
[
G&Zhao10
]
to establish a positive portion of non-vanishing result of central values of cubic or quartic Dirichlet
L
-functions on GRH.

It is the aim of this paper to apply the new construction of the mollifiers to establish sharp bounds for moments of symmetric square
L
-functions concerning the central values of the family of symmetric square
L
-functions attached to holomorphic Hecke cusp forms as well as sharp bounds for moments at the central point of the same family. To state our result, we denote
H_{\kappa}
for the set of holomorphic cusp forms
f
of level one and weight
\kappa
that are
eigenfunctions of every Hecke operator. Then the Fourier expansion of any
f\in H_{\kappa}
at infinity can be written as

(1.1)

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{\frac{\kappa-1}{2}}e(nz),

where we denote
e(z)
for
e^{2\pi iz}
.

The symmetric square
L
-function of
f
is then defined for
\Re(s)>1
by (see
[
iwakow
, p. 137]
and
[
iwakow
, (25.73)]
)

\displaystyle L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)=\zeta(2s)\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\lambda(n^{2})}{n^{s}}.

An asymptotic formula for the first moment of
L(1/2,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
averaged over
H_{\kappa}
as
\kappa\rightarrow\infty
was obtained by Y.-K. Lau
[
Lau02
]
and was subsequently improved in
[
Fomenko03
,
Khan07
,
Sun13
,
Ng17
,
Liu18
,
B&F18
]
. However, it remains a
challenge problem to evaluate the second moment of the above family of
L
-functions asymptotically. Various upper bounds concerning the second moment can be found in
[
I&M01
,
Luo12
,
Lam15
]
. Moreover, by taking an extra average over the weights, R. Khan
[
Khan10
]
evaluated the twisted second moment asymptotically to establish a positive proportion of non-vanishing result of the enlarged family of
L
-functions. An asymptotic formula for the third moment concerning this larger family of symmetric
L
-functions is obtained in
[
D&K18
]
.

In order to treat the moments of central values of symmetric
L
-functions, it is natural to introduce the harmonic sum
\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}_{f\in S}
over any subset
S\subset H_{\kappa}
such that

\displaystyle\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}_{f\in S}:=\sum_{f\in S}\omega^{-1}_{f},

where
\omega_{f}
is the harmonic weight given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\omega_{f}=\frac{(4\pi)^{\kappa-1}}{\Gamma(\kappa-1)}<f,f>=\frac{\kappa-1}{2\pi}L(1,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f).

Here
<f,f>
denotes the Petersson inner product.

The density conjecture of N. Katz and P. Sarnak
[
K&S
]
implies that the distribution of zeros near the central point of a reasonable family of
L
-functions is governed by a symmetry type. In
[
C&F00
]
, J.B. Conrey and D. W. Farmer further illustrated that such symmetry type also affects the behaviors of the moments of the corresponding family of
L
-functions. In particular, a conjecture on the
2k
-th moment of
L(1/2,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
over
f\in H_{\kappa}
is given on
[
C&F00
, p. 887]
for all real
k\geq 0
, which can be stated that (see also
[
Tang13
, p. 2145]
) as
\kappa\rightarrow\infty
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{f\in H_{\kappa}}|L(\frac{1}{2},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)|^{2k}\sim(\log\kappa)^{k(2k+1)}.

Following a general method developed by Z. Rudnick and K. Soundararajan in
[
R&Sound
,
R&Sound1
]
towards establishing sharp
lower bounds for moments of
L
-functions, H. Tang
[
Tang13
]
obtained lower bounds of the desired order of magnitude for all integers
k\geq 0
for the family of
L
-functions given in (
1.3
).

Our first result in this paper extends the result in
[
Tang13
]
to all real
k\geq 0
.

Theorem 1.1
.

With the notation as above. For any real number
k\geq 1/2
, we have

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{f\in H_{\kappa}}|L(\frac{1}{2},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)|^{2k}\gg_{k}(\log\kappa)^{k(2k+1)}.

The above bounds continue to hold for
0\leq k<1/2
provided that we assume the truth of GRH.

We achieve the proof of Theorem
1.1
by applying the lower bounds principle of W. Heap and K. Soundararajan
[
H&Sound
]
. We point out here that the case
k=0
of (
1.4
) in fact yields a positive portion of non-vanishing result on central values of the family of symmetric square
L
-functions under GRH. However, a superior result with an explicit proportion has already been achieved by H. Iwaniec, W. Luo and P. Sarnak in
[
ILS
, Corollary 1.8]
, by computing the one level density of low-lying zeros of the family of symmetric square
L
-functions.

Also as indicated above, the method of K. Soundararajan
[
Sound2009
]
with its refinement by A. J. Harper
[
Harper
]
on upper bounds for moments of
L
-functions under GRH plays a key role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The same method further allows us to obtain the next result concerning the upper bounds of the corresponding
L
-functions.

Theorem 1.2
.

With the notation as above and assuming the truth of GRH. For any real number
k\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{f\in H_{\kappa}}|L(\frac{1}{2},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)|^{2k}\ll_{k}(\log\kappa)^{k(2k+1)}.

Now, Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
together implies the following result on the order of magnitude of the moments of the family of symmetric square
L
-functions.

Theorem 1.3
.

With the notation as above and assuming the truth of GRH. For any real number
k\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{f\in H_{\kappa}}|L(\frac{1}{2},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)|^{2k}\asymp(\log\kappa)^{k(2k+1)}.

As the proof of Theorem
1.2
can be achieved via a variant of the proof of Proposition
3.4
below, which is part of our treatments of Theorem
1.1
, we shall omit the proof of Theorem
1.2
and focus on the proof of Theorem
1.1
in the rest of the paper.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Sums over primes

We reserve the letter
p
for a prime number throughout the paper and we recall the following result from parts (d) and (b) in
[
MVa
, Theorem 2.7]
concerning sums over primes.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
x\geq 2
. We have for some constant
b
,

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p}=\log\log x+b+O\Big(\frac{1}{\log x}\Big).

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{\log p}{p}=\log x+O(1).

2.3.
Symmetric square
L
-functions

We recall here some general results concerning Rankin-Selberg
L
-functions. Let
L(s,\pi)
be the
L
-function attached to an automorphic cuspidal representation
\pi
of
\text{GL}_{M}
over
\mathbb{Q}
. For
\Re(s)
large enough,
L(s,\pi)
can be expressed as an Euler product of the form

\displaystyle L(s,\pi)=\prod_{p}L(s,\pi_{p})=\prod_{p}\prod^{M}_{j=1}(1-\alpha_{\pi}(p,j)p^{-s})^{-1}.

The Rankin-Selberg
L
-function
L(s,\pi\otimes\pi)
associated to
\pi
is then defined for
\Re(s)
large enough as a product of local factors
L(s,\pi\otimes\pi)=\prod\limits_{p}L(s,\pi_{p}\otimes\pi_{p})
such that (see
[
R&S
, (2.18)]
) at unramified
\pi_{p}
, we have

\displaystyle L(s,\pi_{p}\otimes\pi_{p})=\prod_{1\leq i,j\leq M}(1-\alpha_{\pi}(p,i)\alpha_{\pi}(p,j)p^{-s})^{-1}.

Furthermore,
L(s,\pi\otimes\pi)
factors as the product of the symmetric and exterior square
L
-functions (see
[
BG
, p. 139]
):

\displaystyle L(s,\pi\otimes\pi)=L(s,\vee^{2})L(s,\wedge^{2}),

where we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle L(s,\vee^{2})=\prod_{p}L_{p}(s,\vee^{2}),\quad L(s,\wedge^{2})=\prod_{p}L_{p}(s,\wedge^{2}).

The Euler product for
L(s,\vee^{2})
(and hence
L(s,\wedge^{2})
) can be found on
[
BG
, p. 167]
. For unramified
p
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle L_{p}(s,\vee^{2})=\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq M}(1-\alpha_{\pi}(p,i)\alpha_{\pi}(p,j)p^{-s})^{-1},\quad L_{p}(s,\wedge^{2})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq M}(1-\alpha_{\pi}(p,i)\alpha_{\pi}(p,j)p^{-s})^{-1}.

It is known that
L(s,\pi\otimes\pi)
has a simply pole at
s=1
if and only if
\pi
is self-contragredient. In which case, the pole of
L(s,\pi\otimes\pi)
is carried by exactly one of the two factors
L(s,\vee^{2})
or
L(s,\wedge^{2})
. We then write the order of the pole of
L(s,\wedge^{2})
as
(\delta(\pi)+1)/2
so that
\delta(\pi)=\pm 1
. The order of the pole of
L(s,\vee^{2})
then equals to
(1-\delta(\pi))/2
.

Upon taking logarithmic derivatives on both sides of the expressions given in (
2.1
) via making use of (
2.2
), we deduce from
[
iwakow
, Theorem 5.15]
that under GRH, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{p\leq x}\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq M}\alpha_{\pi}(p,i)\alpha_{\pi}(p,j)\log p=&\frac{1-\delta(\pi)}{2}x+O(x^{1/2}\log x\log(q_{\operatorname{sym}^{2}(\pi)}x)),\\
\sum_{p\leq x}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq M}\alpha_{\pi}(p,i)\alpha_{\pi}(p,j)\log p=&\frac{1+\delta(\pi)}{2}x+O(x^{1/2}\log x\log(q_{\operatorname{ext}^{2}(\pi)}x)),\end{split}

where
q_{\operatorname{sym}^{2}(\pi)},q_{\operatorname{ext}^{2}(\pi)}
are analytic conductors of
L(s,\vee^{2})
and
L(s,\wedge^{2})
defined on
[
iwakow
, p. 95]
. Here the implied constants are absolute.

2.4.
Cusp form
L
-functions

Recall that the Fourier expansion of any
f\in H_{\kappa}
at infinity is given in (
1.1
). For
\Re(s)>1
, the associated modular
L
-function
L(s,f)
is defined by

(2.4)

\displaystyle L(s,f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p}\prod^{2}_{j=1}(1-\alpha_{f}(p,j)p^{-s})^{-1}.

By Deligne’s proof
[
D
]
of the Weil conjecture, we know that

(2.5)

\displaystyle|\alpha_{f}(p,1)|=|\alpha_{f}(p,2)|=1,\quad\alpha_{f}(p,1)\alpha_{f}(p,2)=1.

It follows from this and (
2.4
) that
\lambda_{f}(n)
is multiplicative and for
\nu\geq 1
, we have

(2.6)

\displaystyle\lambda_{f}(p^{\nu})=\sum^{\nu}_{j=0}\alpha^{v-j}_{f}(p,1)\alpha^{j}_{f}(p,2).

The above relation further implies that
\lambda_{f}(n)\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\lambda_{f}(1)=1
and
|\lambda_{f}(n)|\leq d(n)
for
n\geq 1
with
d(n)
denotes the divisor function of
n
. Moreover, for integers
m,n\geq 1
, we have the following multiplicative property

(2.7)

\displaystyle\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)=\sum_{d|(m,n)}\lambda_{f}(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}).

The symmetric square
L
-function
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
of
f
is then defined for
\Re(s)>1
by
(see
[
iwakow
, p. 137]
and
[
iwakow
, (25.73)]
)

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)=&\prod_{p}\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq 2}(1-\alpha_{f}(p,i)\alpha_{f}(p,j)p^{-s})^{-1}=(1-\alpha^{2}_{f}(p,1)p^{-s})^{-1}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}(1-\alpha^{2}_{f}(p,2)p^{-s})^{-1}\\
=&\zeta(2s)\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\lambda(n^{2})}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p}(1-\frac{\lambda(p^{2})}{p^{s}}+\frac{\lambda(p^{2})}{p^{2s}}-\frac{1}{p^{3s}})^{-1}.\end{split}

It follows from a result of G. Shimura
[
Shimura
]
that the corresponding completed
L
-function

(2.9)

\displaystyle\Lambda(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)=

\displaystyle\pi^{-3s/2}\Gamma(\frac{s+1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{s+\kappa-1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{s+\kappa}{2})L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)

is entire and satisfies the functional equation

\Lambda(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)=\Lambda(1-s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
.

Noticing that the weight
\omega_{f}
introduced in (
1.2
) satisfies that
\omega_{f}\neq 0
so that we have
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
has no pole at
s=1
. Moreover, it follows from (
2.9
) and the definition of the analytic conductor given on
[
iwakow
, p. 95]
that the analytic conductor of
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
is
\ll\kappa^{2}
. We then apply (
2.3
) to the representation whose associated
L
-function equals
L(s,f)
to see by (
2.8
) that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{p\leq x}\big(\alpha^{2}_{f}(p,1)+\alpha^{2}_{f}(p,2)+1\big)\log p=&O\big(x^{1/2}\log x\log(\kappa x)\big),\\
\sum_{p\leq x}\big(\alpha^{2}_{f}(p,1)+\alpha^{2}_{f}(p,2)\big)\log p=&-x+O\big(x^{1/2}\log x\log(\kappa x)\big).\end{split}

We now denote
\pi_{\operatorname{sym}^{2}f}
for the representation whose associated
L
-function equals
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
and we notice that this representation is also self-contragredient. We also denote the symmetric square and exterior functions associated to
\pi_{\operatorname{sym}^{2}f}
by
L(s,\vee^{2};\pi_{\operatorname{sym}^{2}f})
and
L(s,\wedge^{2};\pi_{\operatorname{sym}^{2}f})
, respectively. Further note that as
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
is an
L
-function of degree
3
, the analytic conductor of
L(s,\vee^{2};\pi_{\operatorname{sym}^{2}f})
divides (see
[
iwakow
, p. 97]
) the sixth power of the analytic conductor of
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
. We then apply (
2.3
) to
\pi_{\operatorname{sym}^{2}f}
to see that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{p\leq x}\big(\alpha^{4}_{f}(p,1)+\alpha^{4}_{f}(p,2)+1+\alpha^{2}_{f}(p,1)+\alpha^{2}_{f}(p,2)+1\big)\log p=&\frac{1-\delta(\pi_{\operatorname{sym}^{2}f})}{2}x+O\big(x^{1/2}\log x\log(\kappa x)\big),\\
\sum_{p\leq x}\big(\alpha^{2}_{f}(p,1)+\alpha^{2}_{f}(p,2)+1\big)\log p=&\frac{1+\delta(\pi_{\operatorname{sym}^{2}f})}{2}x+O\big(x^{1/2}\log x\log(\kappa x)\big).\end{split}

We then deduce from the second expression above and (
2.10
) that
\delta(\pi_{\operatorname{sym}^{2}f})=-1
, which in term implies that the first expression in (
2.11
) can be written as

(2.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{p\leq x}\big(\alpha^{4}_{f}(p,1)+\alpha^{4}_{f}(p,2)+1\big)\log p=&x+O\big(x^{1/2}\log x\log(\kappa x)\big).\end{split}

As the prime number theorem under the Riemann hypothesis implies that (see
[
MVa1
, Theorem 13.1]
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{p\leq x}\log p=x+O\big(x^{1/2}\log^{2}x\big),\end{split}

we readily deduce from (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) that the first expression given in (
2.11
) can be written as

(2.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{p\leq x}\big(\alpha^{4}_{f}(p,1)+\alpha^{4}_{f}(p,2)\big)\log p=&O\big(x^{1/2}\log x\log(\kappa x)\big).\end{split}

2.5.
Twisted first moment and the Petersson trace formula

We quote the following result from
[
Ng17
]
on the twisted first moment of
L(\frac{1}{2},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
for
f\in H_{\kappa}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
\varepsilon>0,1\leq l\ll\kappa
, we have for some constant
C
,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{f\in H_{\kappa}}L(\frac{1}{2},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)\lambda_{f}(l^{2})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{l}}(-\log l+C+\log\kappa)+O(l\kappa^{-1/2+\varepsilon}+k^{-1}).

Note that the Petersson trace formula states that (see
[
I&M01
, (2.7)]
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta_{m,n}:=&\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{f\in H_{\kappa}}\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)=\delta_{m,n}+2\pi i^{\kappa}\sum_{c\geq 1}\frac{S(m,n;c)}{c}J_{\kappa-1}(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{c}),\end{split}

where
\delta_{m,n}=1
if
m=n
and is
0
otherwise,
J_{\kappa-1}(x)
is the Bessel function and
S(m,n;c)
is the Kloosterman’s sum defined by

\displaystyle S(m,n;c)=\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{h\negthickspace\negthickspace\negthickspace\pmod{c}}e(\frac{mh+n\overline{h}}{c}),

where
\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}
denotes the sum over invertible elements modulo
c
.

We have the following result (see
[
R&Sound
, Lemma 2.1]
) concerning the size of
\Delta_{m,n}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

For
mn\leq\kappa^{2}/10^{4}
, we have

(2.15)

\displaystyle\Delta_{m,n}=\delta_{m,n}+O(e^{-\kappa}).

2.8.
Upper bound for
\log|L(\frac{1}{2},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)|

Our next result establishes an upper bound of
\log|L(\frac{1}{2},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)|
in terms of a sum involving prime powers, following the approach in the proof of
[
Sound2009
, Proposition]
.

Lemma 2.9
.

With the notation as above and assuming the truth of GRH for
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
. Let
x\geq 2
and let
\lambda_{0}=0.4912\ldots

denote the unique positive real number satisfying
e^{-\lambda_{0}}=\lambda_{0}+\frac{\lambda^{2}_{0}}{2}
.
We have for any
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
,

(2.16)

\displaystyle\log|L(\frac{1}{2},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p^{l}\leq x\\
l\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{\alpha^{2l}_{f}(p,1)+\alpha^{2l}_{f}(p,2)+1}{lp^{l(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{\log x})}}\frac{\log\left(\frac{x}{p^{l}}\right)}{\log x}+(1+\lambda)\frac{\log\kappa}{\log x}+O\Big(\frac{\lambda}{\log x}\Big).

Proof.

We interpret
\log|L(\frac{1}{2},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)|
as
-\infty
when
L(\frac{1}{2},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)=0
, so that we may assume
L(\frac{1}{2},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)\neq 0
in the remaining of the proof. Recall that the function
\Lambda(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
defined in (
2.9
) is analytic in the entire complex plane. As
\Gamma(s)
has simple poles at the non-positive rational integers (see
[
Da
, §10]
), we deduce from (
2.9
) that
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
has simple zeros at
s=-2k-1,-2k+1-\kappa,-2k-\kappa
for all
k\geq 0
. These zeros are called the trivial zeros of
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
. Since we assume GRH, we know that the non-trivial zeros of
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
are precisely the zeros of
\Lambda(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
.

Let
\rho=\tfrac{1}{2}+i\gamma
with
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
run over the non-trivial zeros of
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
. We deduce from
[
iwakow
, Theorem 5.6]
and the observation that
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
is analytic at
s=1
that

(2.17)

\displaystyle\Lambda(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)=e^{B_{0}+B_{1}s}\prod_{\rho}\left(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\right)e^{\frac{s}{\rho}},

where
B_{0},B_{1}=B_{1}(f)
are constants.

Taking the logarithmic derivative on both sides of (
2.17
) and making use of (
2.9
), we obtain that

-\frac{L^{\prime}}{L}(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(\frac{s+1}{2})+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(\frac{s+\kappa-1}{2})+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(\frac{s+\kappa}{2})-\frac{3}{2}\log\pi-B_{1}-\sum_{\rho}\left(\frac{1}{s-\rho}+\frac{1}{\rho}\right).

This implies that

(2.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\alpha_{0}=0,\;\;\;\;\;\alpha_{j}=\frac{20^{j-1}}{(\log\log\kappa)^{2}}\;\;\;\forall\;j\geq 1,\quad\mathcal{J}=\mathcal{J}_{k,\kappa}=1+\max\{j:\alpha_{j}\leq 10^{-M}\}.\end{split}

and that
p_{j}(n)=1
if and only if
\Omega(n)=r_{k}\ell_{j}
and all the prime factors of

are from the interval
P_{j}
.
Using this together with the notations in Section
4
and recalling the definition of

given in (

), we see that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{j}(f,2k)=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{c_{k}}{\ell_{j}}\Big)^{r_{k}\ell_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{j}}}\frac{(r_{k}\ell_{j})!}{w(n_{j})}\widetilde{\lambda}(n_{j})p_{j}(n_{j}).

Note that
{\mathcal{Q}}_{j}(f,2k)
is a short Dirichlet polynomial whose length does not exceed

(\kappa^{\alpha_{j}})^{r_{k}\ell_{j}}=\kappa^{r_{k}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}}
.
Also, we have by (
3.13
),

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{c_{k}}{\ell_{j}}\Big)^{r_{k}\ell_{j}}(r_{k}\ell_{j})!\ll(r_{k}\ell_{j})\Big(\frac{c_{k}r_{k}}{e}\Big)^{r_{k}\ell_{j}}.

We apply the above together with (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) to see that there exists a constant
C(k)
depending on
k
only
such that for any
1\leq j\leq\mathcal{J}
, we have for some
y_{n_{j}}\ll C(k)^{\ell_{j}}
with
n_{j}\leq\kappa^{r_{k}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(f,2k)+{\mathcal{Q}}_{j}(f,2k)=\sum_{n_{j}}\frac{y_{n_{j}}}{\sqrt{n_{j}}}\widetilde{\lambda}(n_{j}).

We thus deduce that we may write for simplicity that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{f\in H_{\kappa}}\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}\Big({\mathcal{N}}_{j}(f,2k)+{\mathcal{Q}}_{j}(f,2k)\Big)=\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{f\in H_{\kappa}}\sum_{n}\frac{y_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\widetilde{\lambda}(n)=\sum_{n}\frac{y_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{t|n}c_{n}(t)\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{f\in H_{\kappa}}\lambda_{f}(t^{2}),\end{split}

where
n\leq\kappa^{r_{k}\sum^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}\alpha_{j}\ell_{j}}\leq\kappa^{40r_{k}e^{A}10^{-M/4}}
by (
4.2
). We also derive from (
4.2
) that

\displaystyle y_{n}\ll C(k)^{\sum^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}\ell_{j}}\ll\kappa^{\varepsilon}.

We now apply (
2.15
) to evaluate the last sum on the right-hand side expression in (
5.2
) to see that, upon choosing
M
large enough, the contribution from
the error term in (
2.15
) to the right-hand side expression in (
5.2
) is

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq\kappa^{40r_{k}e^{A}10^{-M/4}}}\frac{y_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{t|n}\big|c_{n}(t)\big|e^{-\kappa}\ll\sum_{n\leq\kappa^{2r_{k}/10^{M/8}}}\kappa^{\varepsilon}\sum_{t|n}\big|c_{n}(t)\big|e^{-\kappa}\ll\kappa^{-1/4},

where the last estimation above follows from (
4.6
).

It therefore remains to consider the contribution to the right-hand side expression in (
5.2
) from the main term in (
2.15
), which equals to

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{n}\frac{y_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}c_{n}(1)=\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}(\frac{y_{n_{j}}}{\sqrt{n_{j}}}c_{n_{j}}(1))=\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}\Big(\sum_{n_{j}}\frac{(2k)^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{\sqrt{n_{j}}w(n_{j})}b_{j}(n_{j})c_{n_{j}}(1)+\Big(\frac{c_{k}}{\ell_{j}}\Big)^{r_{k}\ell_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{j}}}\frac{(r_{k}\ell_{j})!}{w(n_{j})}c_{n_{j}}(1)p_{j}(n_{j})\Big).\end{split}

Arguing as in the proof of Proposition
3.2
, we see that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{n_{j}}\frac{(2k)^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{\sqrt{n_{j}}w(n_{j})}b_{j}(n_{j})c_{n_{j}}(1)=\Big(1+O\big(2^{-\ell_{j}/2}\big)\Big)\exp(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in P_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{(2k)^{2}}{2p}+O(\sum_{p\in P_{j}}\frac{1}{p^{3/2}})).\end{split}

Similarly, we notice that
(c_{k}r_{k})^{\Omega(n_{j})-r_{k}\ell_{j}}\geq 1
when
\Omega(n_{j})=r_{k}\ell_{j}
. It follows from this and (
5.1

(5.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big(\frac{c_{k}}{\ell_{j}}\Big)^{r_{k}\ell_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{j}}}\frac{(r_{k}\ell_{j})!}{w(n_{j})}c_{n_{j}}(1)p_{j}(n_{j})\ll\Big(\frac{c_{k}}{\ell_{j}}\Big)^{r_{k}\ell_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{(c_{k}r_{k})^{\Omega(n_{j})-r_{k}\ell_{j}}}{\sqrt{n_{j}}}\frac{(r_{k}\ell_{j})!}{w(n_{j})}|c_{n_{j}}(1)|\\
\ll&r_{k}\ell_{j}e^{-r_{k}\ell_{j}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in P_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+\frac{(c_{k}r_{k})^{2}}{2p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{3/2}})\Big),\end{split}

where the last estimation above follows from (
4.12
) and the estimation that
|c_{p^{n}}(1)|\leq 3^{n}
.

Further applying the estimation
1+x\leq e^{x}
, Lemma
2.2
and (
3.2
), we see that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&r_{k}\ell_{j}e^{-r_{k}\ell_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in P_{j}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+\frac{(c_{k}r_{k})^{2}}{2p}+O(\frac{1}{p^{3/2}})\Big)\ll r_{k}\ell_{j}e^{-r_{k}\ell_{j}}\exp\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in P_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{(c_{k}r_{k})^{2}}{2p}+O(\sum_{p\in P_{j}}\frac{1}{p^{3/2}})\Big)\\
\ll&2^{-\ell_{j}/2}\exp(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in P_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{(2k)^{2}}{2p}+O(\sum_{p\in P_{j}}\frac{1}{p^{3/2}})).\end{split}

Substituting (
5.4
)-(
5.6
) into (

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{n}\frac{y_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}c_{n}(1)=\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{j=1}\Big(\big(1+O\big(2^{-\ell_{j}/2}\big)\big)\exp(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in P_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{(2k)^{2}}{2p}+O(\sum_{p\in P_{j}}\frac{1}{p^{3/2}}))\Big)\ll(\log\kappa)^{\frac{(2k)^{2}}{2}}.\end{split}

This completes the proof of Proposition
3.3

6.1.
Initial treatment

In the course of proving Proposition

[

. We denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(V)=\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in H_{\kappa}\\

Our next result gives an upper bound for
\mathcal{N}(V)

Proposition 6.2
.

With the notation as above and assuming the truth of GRH for
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)
. Let
k>0
be a fixed real number.
For
\log\log\kappa<V\leq\frac{6\log\kappa}{\log\log\kappa}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(V)\ll(\log\kappa)^{9e^{16(2k+1)}+1}e^{-(2k+1)V}.

Proof.

We apply (
2.34
) by setting

there and we denote

for the sum in (
2.34
). We then deduce that

\log\frac{|L(\frac{1}{2},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)|}{\sqrt{\log\kappa}}\leq T+(1+\lambda_{0})\frac{V}{4}+O(\log\log\log\kappa).

As
\lambda_{0}<1
, this implies that if
\log\frac{|L(\frac{1}{2},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)|}{\sqrt{\log\kappa}}\geq V
, then we have

T\geq\tfrac{V}{2}
.

Now, we define the harmonic measure of
T
to be

\displaystyle\operatorname{meas}(T)

\displaystyle:=\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in H_{\kappa}\\
T\geq V/2\end{subarray}}1.

We keep the notations in Section
4
and we further define a totally multiplicative function
s(n,x)
such that at primes

, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}s(p,x)=\frac{1}{p^{\lambda_{0}/\log x}}\frac{\log(x/p)}{\log x}.\end{split}

Note that we have
|s(n,x)|\leq 1
for any

whose prime factors do not exceed

4.5
) that

(6.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{N}(V)\leq\operatorname{meas}(T)\leq(\frac{2}{V})^{2m}\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in H_{\kappa}\end{subarray}}|T|^{2m}=(\frac{2}{V})^{2m}\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in H_{\kappa}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
\Omega(n)=2m\\
p|n\implies p\leq x\end{subarray}}\frac{(2m)!s(n,x)}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{1}{w(n)}\sum_{t|n}c_{n}(t)\lambda_{f}(t^{2}).\end{split}

Note that the largest
t^{2}
appearing on the right-hand side expression above does not exceed

\displaystyle n^{2}\ll x^{4m}\ll\kappa^{16m/V}.

We now take
m=\lceil V/16\rceil-1
to ensure that

so that we can apply (
2.15
) to evaluate the last sum in (
6.1
) to see by the above discussions that the contribution from the error term in (

(6.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\ll&e^{-\kappa}(\frac{2}{V})^{2m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
\Omega(n)=2m\\

\ll&e^{-\kappa}(\frac{2}{V})^{2m}\big(\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{3}{\sqrt{p}}\big)^{2m}\\
\ll&e^{-\kappa}(3x^{1/2})^{2m}\\
\ll&\operatorname{exp}\left(-(2k+1)V\right).\end{split}

Meanwhile, using Rankin’s trick that
(e^{8(2k+1)})^{\Omega(n)-2m}\geq 1
when
\Omega(n)=2m
, the contribution from the main term in (
2.15
) is

(6.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\frac{2}{V})^{2m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
\Omega(n)=2m\\
p|n\implies p\leq x\end{subarray}}\frac{(2m)!s(n,x)}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{1}{w(n)}c_{n}(1)\\
\ll&(\frac{2}{V})^{2m}(2m)!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\\
p|n\implies p\leq x\end{subarray}}\frac{(e^{8(2k+1)})^{\Omega(n)-2m}c_{n}(1)}{w(n)\sqrt{n}}\\
\ll&(2m)\big(\frac{4m}{e^{8(2k+1)+1}V}\big)^{2m}\Big(\prod_{p\leq x}\big(\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}\frac{e^{8(2k+1)i}c_{p^{i}}(1)}{i!p^{i/2}}\big)\Big),\end{split}

where the last estimation above follows from (
3.13
) and the observation that
c_{n}(1)
is multiplicative. Further using the observation
c_{p}(1)=0
and the estimation that
|c_{n}(1)|\leq 3^{\Omega(n)}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}\frac{e^{8(2k+1)i}\cdot|c_{p^{i}}(1)|}{i!p^{i/2}}\ll&\exp(\frac{9e^{16(2k+1)}}{2p}).\end{split}

We apply the above estimation and Lemma
2.2
to see that the last expression in (

) is

\displaystyle\ll Ve^{-(2k+1)V}\exp(\prod_{p\leq x}\frac{9e^{16(2k+1)}}{2p})\ll V(\log\kappa)^{9e^{16(2k+1)}}e^{-(2k+1)V}\ll(\log\kappa)^{9e^{16(2k+1)}+1}e^{-(2k+1)V},

where the last estimation above follows by noticing that

.

The assertion of the proposition now follows from (
6.2
) and the above.
∎

Now, Proposition

Proposition 6.3

Assuming the truth of GRH for

for any

\displaystyle\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in H_{\kappa}\end{subarray}}\mathcal{N}(f,2k-2)^{2}\ll(\log\kappa)^{O(1)}.

We deduce from the above that we may also assume that

. Further note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{S}(0)\end{subarray}}1\leq&\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in H_{\kappa}\end{subarray}}\sum^{\mathcal{J}}_{l=1}\Big(\frac{10^{3}}{\ell_{1}}{|\mathcal{M}}_{1,l}(f)|\Big)^{2\lceil 1/(10^{3}\alpha_{1})\rceil}.\end{split}

Recall the function

defined in Section

, we see by (
4.5
) that the right-hand side expression above equals

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big(2^{m/10}|P_{m}(f)|\Big)^{2\lceil 2^{m/2}\rceil}=\sum_{n_{1}\leq e^{(\log\log\kappa)^{2}}}u_{n_{1}}\eta(n_{1}),\\
&\Big(\frac{2\cdot 10^{3}}{\ell_{j+1}}|{\mathcal{M}}_{j+1,u}(f)|\Big)^{2\lceil 1/(10^{3}\alpha_{j+1})\rceil}=\sum_{n_{2}\leq\kappa^{4/10^{3}}}u_{n_{2}}\widetilde{\lambda}(n_{2}),\\
&E_{\ell_{1}}(2{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{1,j}(f))\prod^{j}_{l=2}E_{\ell_{l}}(2{\mathcal{M}}_{l,j}(f)){\mathcal{N}}_{l}(f,2k-2)\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{l=j+2}{\mathcal{N}}_{l}(f,2k-2)=\sum_{n_{3}\leq\kappa^{40e^{A}10^{-M/4}}}u_{n_{3}}\widetilde{\lambda}(n_{3}),\end{split}

where
|u_{n_{i}}|\ll\kappa^{\varepsilon},1\leq i\leq 3
.

We multiply the above Dirichlet series together, noting that
n_{i},1\leq i\leq 3
are mutually co-prime. Then using the expansions for
\widetilde{\lambda}(n),\eta(n)
given in (
4.5
) and (
6.5
) and further using the estimations for the coefficients involved in these expansions given in (
4.6
), (
6.6
), we see that we may write
S_{u}
as

(6.28)

\displaystyle S_{u}=\sum_{n\leq\kappa^{1/10}}v_{n}\sum_{t|n^{2}}e_{n}(t)\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{S}(j)\end{subarray}}\lambda_{f}(t^{2}),

where
|v_{n}|\ll\kappa^{\varepsilon}
and

\displaystyle\sum_{t|n^{2}}|e_{n}(t)|\leq 8^{\Omega(n)}.

We apply (
2.15
) to evaluate the last sum in (
6.28
). Notice that we have
t^{2}\ll\kappa
for all
t
involved in the sum. It follows from this and our discussions above that the contribution from the error term in (
2.15
) is

\displaystyle\ll e^{-\kappa}\sum_{n\leq\kappa^{1/10}}\kappa^{\varepsilon}\sum_{t|n^{2}}|e_{n}(t)|\ll\kappa^{-1},

which is negligible.

It then suffices to consider on the main term contribution from (
2.15
), which equals

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq\kappa^{1/10}}v_{n}\sum_{t|n^{2}}e_{n}(1).

Observing that
e_{n}(1)
is multiplicative, so that we have by (
6.27
), (
4.5
) and (
6.5
),

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq\kappa^{1/10}}v_{n}\sum_{t|n^{2}}e_{n}(1)=(\sum_{n_{1}}u_{n_{1}}d_{n_{1}}(1))(\sum_{n_{2}}u_{n_{2}}c_{n_{2}}(1))(\sum_{n_{3}}u_{n_{3}}c_{n_{3}}(1)).

Using arguments that lead to estimations given in (
6.8
) and (
6.14
), we see that

(6.29)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{n_{1}}u_{n_{1}}d_{n_{1}}(1)\ll&2^{-2^{m/2}},\\
\sum_{n_{2}}u_{n_{2}}c_{n_{2}}(1)\ll&e^{-10^{3}/\alpha_{j+1}}.\end{split}

It remains to evaluate
\sum_{n_{3}}u_{n_{3}}c_{n_{3}}(1)
. By the multiplicity of
c_{n}(1)
again, we see that for some real numbers
\tilde{v}_{n_{l}},1\leq l\leq\mathcal{J}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{3}}u_{n_{3}}c_{n_{3}}(1)=\prod_{l}(\sum_{n_{l}}\tilde{v}_{n_{l}}b_{l}(n_{l})c_{n_{1}}(1)).

We examine the factor
\sum\limits_{n_{l}}\tilde{v}_{n_{l}}b_{l}(n_{l})c_{n_{1}}(1)
in the above expression that arises from the product

\displaystyle E_{\ell_{l}}(2{\mathcal{M}}_{l,j}(f)){\mathcal{N}}_{l}(f,2k-2),

for some
2\leq l\leq j
. Similar to (
4.1
), we write

\displaystyle E_{\ell_{l}}(2{\mathcal{M}}_{l,j}(f))=\sum_{n}\frac{2^{\Omega(n)}s(n,\kappa^{\alpha_{j}})}{w(n)\sqrt{n}}b_{l}(n)\widetilde{\lambda}(n),\quad{\mathcal{N}}_{l}(f,2k-2)=\sum_{n}\frac{(2k-2)^{\Omega(n)}}{w(n)\sqrt{n}}b_{l}(n)\widetilde{\lambda}(n).

We now extract the sum over terms involving with
c_{n}(1)
in
E_{\ell_{l}}(2{\mathcal{M}}_{l,j}(f)){\mathcal{N}}_{l}(f,2k-2)
using the above expressions to see that it equals

(6.30)

\displaystyle\sum_{n,n^{\prime}}\frac{2^{\Omega(n)}(2k-2)^{\Omega(n^{\prime})}s(n,\kappa^{\alpha_{j}})b_{l}(n)b_{l}(n^{\prime})}{w(n)w(n^{\prime})\sqrt{nn^{\prime}}}c_{nn^{\prime}}(1).

Note that the factor
b_{l}(n)
restricts
n
to have
all prime factors in
P_{l}
such that
\Omega(n)\leq\ell_{l}
. If we remove the restriction on
\Omega(n)
, then the sum in (
6.30
) becomes

(6.31)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,n^{\prime}\\
p|n\Rightarrow p\in P_{l}\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{\Omega(n)}(2k-2)^{\Omega(n^{\prime})}s(n,\kappa^{\alpha_{j}})b_{l}(n^{\prime})}{w(n)w(n^{\prime})\sqrt{nn^{\prime}}}c_{nn^{\prime}}(1).

On the other hand, using Rankin’s trick by noticing that
2^{n-\ell_{l}}\geq 1
if
\Omega(n)>\ell_{l}
, we see that the error introduced this way does not exceed

(6.32)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,n^{\prime}\\
p|nn^{\prime}\Rightarrow p\in P_{l}\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{\Omega(n)-\ell_{l}}2^{\Omega(n)}|2k-2|^{\Omega(n^{\prime})}s(n,\kappa^{\alpha_{j}})}{w(n)w(n^{\prime})\sqrt{nn^{\prime}}}|c_{nn^{\prime}}(1)|.

Similarly, we may remove the restriction of
b_{l}(n^{\prime})
on
\Omega(n^{\prime})
to further write the expression in (
6.31
) as

(6.33)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,n^{\prime}\\
p|nn^{\prime}\Rightarrow p\in P_{l}\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{\Omega(n)}(2k-2)^{\Omega(n)}s(n,\kappa^{\alpha_{j}})}{w(n)w(n^{\prime})\sqrt{nn^{\prime}}}c_{nn^{\prime}}(1)+O\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,n^{\prime}\\
p|nn^{\prime}\Rightarrow p\in P_{l}\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{\Omega(n^{\prime})-\ell_{l}}2^{\Omega(n)}|2k-2|^{\Omega(n^{\prime})}s(n,\kappa^{\alpha_{j}})}{w(n)w(n^{\prime})\sqrt{nn^{\prime}}}|c_{nn^{\prime}}(1)|\Big).

Note that both the main term and the error term above as well as the expression in (
6.32
) are now multiplicative functions of
n,n^{\prime}
and hence can be evaluated in terms of products over primes. We then apply Lemma
2.2
, (
4.12
), the estimations
|c_{n}(1)|\leq 3^{\Omega(n)}
and
|s(n,\kappa^{\alpha_{j}})|\leq 1
for all
n
involved, to see that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,n^{\prime}\\
p|nn^{\prime}\Rightarrow p\in P_{l}\end{subarray}}\frac{2^{\Omega(n)}(2k-2)^{\Omega(n)}s(n,\kappa^{\alpha_{j}})}{w(n)w(n^{\prime})\sqrt{nn^{\prime}}}c_{nn^{\prime}}(1)=&\prod_{p\in P_{l}}\Big(1+\frac{1}{2}(\frac{2s(p,\kappa^{\alpha_{j}})}{\sqrt{p}}+\frac{2k-2}{\sqrt{p}})^{2}+O\Big(\frac{1}{p^{3/2}}\Big)\Big)\\
=&\prod_{p\in P_{l}}\Big(1+\frac{2k^{2}}{p}+O\Big(\frac{\log p}{p\log\kappa^{\alpha_{j}}}+\frac{1}{p^{3/2}}\Big)\Big),\end{split}

where the last expression above follows by observing that for
p\leq\kappa^{\alpha_{j}}
,

\displaystyle s(p,\kappa^{\alpha_{j}})=1+O\Big(\frac{\log p}{\log\kappa^{\alpha_{j}}}\Big).

We further apply the estimation
1+x\leq e^{x}
for any real
x
to see that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\prod_{p\in P_{l}}\Big(1+\frac{2k^{2}}{p}+O\Big(\frac{\log p}{p\log\kappa^{\alpha_{j}}}+\frac{1}{p^{3/2}}\Big)\Big)\leq\exp\Big(\sum_{p\in P_{l}}\frac{2k^{2}}{p}+O\Big(\sum_{p\in P_{l}}\Big(\frac{\log p}{p\log\kappa^{\alpha_{j}}}+\frac{1}{p^{3/2}}\Big)\Big)\Big).\end{split}

We evaluate the error term in (
6.33
) and the expression in (
6.32
) similarly to see via (
3.2
) that they both are

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-\ell_{l}/2}\exp\Big(\sum_{p\in P_{l}}\frac{2k^{2}}{p}+O\Big(\sum_{p\in P_{l}}\Big(\frac{\log p}{p\log\kappa^{\alpha_{j}}}+\frac{1}{p^{3/2}}\Big)\Big)\Big).

It follows that the contribution from the sum over terms involving with
c_{n}(1)
in
E_{\ell_{l}}(2{\mathcal{M}}_{l,j}(f)){\mathcal{N}}_{l}(f,2k-2)
is

\displaystyle\leq\Big(1+O(2^{-\ell_{l}/2})\Big)\exp\Big(\sum_{p\in P_{l}}\frac{2k^{2}}{p}+O\Big(\sum_{p\in P_{l}}\Big(\frac{\log p}{p\log\kappa^{\alpha_{j}}}+\frac{1}{p^{3/2}}\Big)\Big)\Big).

In the same manner, we obtain a similar estimation for the contribution from the sum over terms involving with
c_{n}(1)
in
E_{\ell_{1}}(2{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}_{1,j}(f))
. Also, the contribution from the sum over terms involving with
c_{n}(1)
in
{\mathcal{N}}_{l}(f,2k-2)
for
j+2\leq l\leq\mathcal{J}
is

\displaystyle\leq\Big(1+O(2^{-\ell_{l}/2})\Big)\exp\Big(\prod_{p\in P_{l}}\Big(\frac{(2k-2)^{2}}{2p}+O\Big(\frac{1}{p^{3/2}}\Big)\Big)\Big).

The above estimations allow us to see that

(6.34)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{n_{3}}u_{n_{3}}c_{n_{3}}(1)\\
\leq&\prod^{j}_{l=1}\Big(1+O(2^{-\ell_{l}/2})\Big)\prod^{\mathcal{J}}_{l=j+2}\Big(1+O(2^{-\ell_{l}/2})\Big)\times\exp\Big(\sum_{p\in\bigcup^{j}_{l=1}P_{l}}\frac{2k^{2}}{p}+O\Big(\sum_{p\in\bigcup^{j}_{l=1}P_{l}}\Big(\frac{\log p}{p\log\kappa^{\alpha_{j}}}+\frac{1}{p^{3/2}}\Big)\Big)\Big)\\
&\times\exp\Big(\sum_{p\in\bigcup^{\mathcal{J}}_{l=j+2}P_{l}}\frac{(2k-2)^{2}}{2p}+O\Big(\sum_{p\in\bigcup^{j}_{l=1}P_{l}}\frac{1}{p^{3/2}}\Big)\Big)\\
\ll&\exp\Big(\sum_{p\in\bigcup^{j}_{l=1}P_{l}}\frac{2k^{2}}{p}+O\Big(\sum_{p\in\bigcup^{j}_{l=1}P_{l}}\Big(\frac{\log p}{p\log\kappa^{\alpha_{j}}}+\frac{1}{p^{3/2}}\Big)\Big)\Big)\times\exp\Big(\sum_{p\in\bigcup^{\mathcal{J}}_{l=j+2}P_{l}}\frac{(2k-2)^{2}}{2p}+O\Big(\sum_{p\in\bigcup^{j}_{l=1}P_{l}}\frac{1}{p^{3/2}}\Big)\Big).\end{split}

Note that we have

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\bigcup^{\mathcal{J}}_{l=1}P_{l}}\frac{1}{p^{3/2}}\ll 1.

Moreover, by Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\bigcup^{j}_{l=1}P_{l}}\frac{\log p}{p\log\kappa^{\alpha_{j}}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq\kappa^{\alpha_{j}}}\frac{\log p}{p\log\kappa^{\alpha_{j}}}\ll 1

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\bigcup^{\mathcal{J}}_{l=j+2}P_{l}}\frac{(2k-2)^{2}}{2p}=

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\bigcup^{\mathcal{J}}_{l=j+2}P_{l}}\frac{2k^{2}}{p}+\sum_{p\in\bigcup^{\mathcal{J}}_{l=j+2}P_{l}}\frac{(2k-2)^{2}-(2k)^{2}}{2p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\bigcup^{\mathcal{J}}_{l=j+2}P_{l}}\frac{2k^{2}}{p}+2(2-4k)\log\frac{1}{\alpha_{j+1}}+O(1).

We thus conclude from the above that upon taking
M
large enough, the last expression in (
6.34
) is

(6.35)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle e^{2(2-4k)\log\frac{1}{\alpha_{j+1}}}\exp\Big(\sum_{p\in\bigcup^{\mathcal{J}}_{l=1}P_{l}}\frac{2k^{2}}{p}\Big)\ll e^{\frac{1}{\alpha_{j+1}}}(\log\kappa)^{2k^{2}},

where the last estimation above follows from Lemma
2.2
.

It follows from (
6.29
) and (
6.35
) that we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}S_{u}\ll 2^{-2^{m/2}}e^{-200/\alpha_{j+1}}(\log\kappa)^{2k^{2}}.\end{split}

Applying the above estimation in (
6.26
) and making use of the observation that
20/\alpha_{j+1}=1/\alpha_{j}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\begin{split}S\ll&(\log\kappa)^{2k^{2}+1}e^{\frac{E2^{m/2}}{m}}2^{-2^{m/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{2}{\alpha_{j}}\right)(\mathcal{J}-j).\end{split}

We further apply the above estimation in (
6.23
) to see that

(6.36)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathop{{\sum}^{h}}\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{S}(j)\bigcap\mathcal{P}(m)\bigcap\mathcal{T}\end{subarray}}|L(\frac{1}{2},\operatorname{sym}^{2}f)|^{2}\mathcal{N}(f,2k-2)\ll&(\log\kappa)^{2k^{2}+1}e^{\frac{E2^{m/2}}{m}}2^{-2^{m/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{2}{\alpha_{j}}\right)(\mathcal{J}-j)\\
\ll&(\log\kappa)^{2k^{2}+1}e^{\frac{E2^{m/2}}{m}}2^{-2^{m/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\alpha_{j}}\right),\end{split}

where the last estimation above follows from the observation that we have for
1\leq j\leq\mathcal{J}-1
,

\mathcal{J}-j\leq\frac{\log(1/\alpha_{j})}{\log 20}.

Now summing the last expression of (
6.36
) over
j
and
m
and noticing that these sums are convergent, we derive the desired estimation in (
6.16
) and hence completes the proof of Proposition
3.4
.

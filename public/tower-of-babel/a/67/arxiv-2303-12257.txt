Asymptotics of Helmholtz-Kirchhoff Point-Vortices in the Phase Space
Chanwoo Kim, Trinh T. Nguyen

Abstract
A rigorous derivation of point vortex systems from kinetic equations has been a challenging open problem, due to singular layers in the inviscid limit, giving a large velocity gradient in the Boltzmann equations. In this paper, we derive the Helmholtz-Kirchhoff point-vortex system from the hydrodynamic limits of the Boltzmann equations. We construct Boltzmann solutions by the Hilbert-type expansion associated to the point vortices solutions of the 2D Navier-Stokes equations. We give a precise pointwise estimate for the solution of the Boltzmann equations with small Strouhal number and Knudsen number.

Introduction

One of the central open questions in the field of partial differential equations concerns Hilbert’s sixth problem
[
32
]
, which aims to develop a comprehensive theory of gas dynamics and bridge the gap between the mesoscopic Boltzmann equations and the macroscopic fluid models in formal limits. The Boltzmann equation serves as a fundamental model of kinetic theory for dilute collections of gas particles in elastic binary collisions. It is remarkable that the fundamental equations of fluid dynamics, which describe compressible and incompressible fluids as well as those that are either inviscid or viscous, can all be derived from the Boltzmann equation for rarefied gas dynamics. This can be achieved by selecting the appropriate scalings in the limit of small mean-free path. To be more precise, one considers the Boltzmann equation with scales
\varepsilon,\kappa>0
:

\varepsilon\partial_{t}F+v\cdot\nabla_{x}F=\frac{1}{\varepsilon\kappa}Q(F,F),

(1.1)

where
F
is the probability distribution function

\displaystyle F:(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}

\displaystyle\to(0,\infty)

\displaystyle(t,x,v)

\displaystyle\to F(t,x,v).

Here
v\cdot\nabla_{x}=v_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}+v_{2}\partial_{x_{2}}
, and
F
is
x_{3}
independent. The bilinear term
Q(F,F)
in (
1.1
) is the collision term describing the interactions of particles that are uncorrelated before the collision. In this paper, we consider the hard-sphere Boltzmann collision operator

\displaystyle Q(F,G)(v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-v_{\star})\cdot\widetilde{s}|

\displaystyle\{F(v^{\prime})G(v_{\star}^{\prime})+G(v^{\prime})F(v_{\star}^{\prime})

\displaystyle-F(v)G(v_{\star})-G(v)F(v_{\star})\}d\widetilde{s}dv_{\star},

where

v^{\prime}=v-((v-v_{\star})\cdot\widetilde{s})\widetilde{s},\qquad v_{\star}^{\prime}=v_{\star}+((v-v_{\star})\cdot\widetilde{s})\widetilde{s},\qquad\widetilde{s}\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

The equation (
1.1
) has the global Maxwellian

\mu(v)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}e^{-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}}

(1.2)

as an exact steady solution.
When
\varepsilon\to 0,\kappa\to 0
, one expects the famous Hilbert asymptotic expansion

F=\mu+\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}f_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{\mu}f_{2}+o(1)

where
f_{1}(t,x,v)=u^{\kappa}(t,x)\cdot(v_{1},v_{2})
and
u^{\kappa}
solves the Navier-Stokes equations

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{\kappa}+u^{\kappa}\cdot\nabla_{x}u^{\kappa}+\nabla_{x}p^{\kappa}

\displaystyle=\kappa\eta_{0}\Delta u^{\kappa},

(1.3)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot u^{\kappa}

\displaystyle=0.

where
\eta_{0}>0
is a physical constant that can be computed explicitly by the Boltzmann theory.
This connection between the Boltzmann equations and the fluid equations was first discovered by Hilbert
[
32
]
, which is now the famous Hilbert’s sixth problem, concerning the hydrodynamics limits of the Boltzmann equations. The weak convergence of the moments of
F
was formally derived in
[
5
]
and is finally justified for DiPerna-Lions renormalized solutions in
[
26
]
(see also
[

,

,

,

,

,

]
). We refer the readers to
[

,

,

]
concerning the closeness of the Hilbert expansion of the Boltzmann equation to the solutions of the compressible Euler equations,
[
62
]
for shock wave solutions,
[
33
]
around the Euler equations with contact discontinuities,
[
36
]
for initial Euler vorticity below the Yudovich class,
[
59
]
for the relativistic fluids,
[
15
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
1
,

]
for the steady Boltzmann equations,
[
29
]
for the Hilbert expansion on the torus, and
[
34
,

]
on the half-space.

On the other hand, the fluid motions are governed by the famous Navier-Stokes equations with typically large Reynolds numbers. Two-dimensional fluids with irregular data, such as vortex patches or point vortices, turn out to be a very good approximation to real-world fluids, such as air flows, vortices clouds, and hurricanes. One significant difficulty is the appearance of singular layers, which can create large convection term in the inviscid limit
\kappa\to 0
. We refer the readers to
[
11
,
12
,
9
,
60
,
10
]
on the inviscid limit for vortex patches,
[
19
]
for point vortices, and
[
53
]
for vortex-wave and
[
20
]
for vortex rings. Vortex structure and vortex merging play a key role in understanding coherent structures in turbulence theory, which generate considerable interest among physicists and mathematicians (
[
31
,
40
,
39
,
48
,
50
,
45
,
19
,
23
]
). It is observed that for flows with large Reynolds numbers, the vortex structure remains for a very long time, as long as the centers of the vortices remain separated. When two vortices are close to each other, they tend to merge into larger structures, and the shape of the vortices changes significantly (
[
28
,
49
,
57
,
56
,
47
]
). Moreover, it is known that for any fixed viscosity
\kappa
, the Lamb-Oseen vortex will govern the long time behavior of the 2D Navier-Stokes equations (see
[
21
]
and references therein). It is therefore important to have a unified theory that connects the kinetic Boltzmann equations and the singular vortex systems. One way to study these phenomena is to consider the incompressible Euler equations on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which is given by setting the viscosity
\kappa
to zero:

\partial_{t}u^{0}+u^{0}\cdot\nabla u^{0}+\nabla p^{0}=0,\qquad\nabla\cdot u^{0}=0.

(1.4)

The vorticity
{\omega}^{0}=\nabla\times u^{0}
then solves the transport equation

\partial_{t}{\omega}^{0}+u^{0}\cdot\nabla{\omega}^{0}=0.

(1.5)

It is natural to consider the initial data of the Euler equations (
1.5
) to be the sum of
N
point vortices
\{z_{i,0}\}_{i=1}^{N}
with circulations
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
. Namely

{\omega}^{0}(x)|_{t=0}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\delta_{0}(x-z_{i,0}),

(1.6)

where
\delta_{0}
denotes the Dirac delta function at
0\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Indeed, the initial data (
1.6
) is too singular to define any notion of weak solutions to Euler, and the existence of weak solutions to the Euler equations with initial data (
1.6
) remains open.
In
[
43
,
44
,
45
]
, by regularizing the vortex data (
1.6
) under certain regimes, Machioro and Machioro-Pulvirenti showed that the solution to the corresponding Navier-Stokes converges weakly to

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\delta_{0}(x-z_{i}(t)),

where the point vortices
\{z_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}
solve the Helmholtz-Kirchhoff point-vortex
system (
[
31
]
):

\begin{cases}z_{i}^{\prime}(t)&=\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{j}K_{B}(z_{i}(t)-z_{j}(t)),\\
z_{i}(0)&=z_{i,0}.\end{cases}

(1.7)

Here
K_{B}(x)
is the kernel given by

K_{B}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{x^{\perp}}{|x|^{2}},\qquad x^{\perp}=(-x_{2},x_{1}).

(1.8)

Formally speaking, the velocity of the vortex
z_{i}(t)
is given by the velocity generated by
\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{j}\delta_{0}(x-z_{j,0})
, which are all the other vortices disregarding the self-interaction.
In
[
19
]
, Gallay shows that the vorticity
{\omega}^{\kappa}
of the Navier-Stokes equations

\partial_{t}{\omega}^{\kappa}+u^{\kappa}\cdot\nabla{\omega}^{\kappa}=\kappa\eta_{0}\Delta{\omega}^{\kappa},\qquad u^{\kappa}=K_{B}\star_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\omega}^{\kappa},

(1.9)

with singular initial data (
1.6
)
converges strongly in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
to the sum of Gaussians, called Lamb-Oseen vortices, centered at the point vortices
\{z_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}
:

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{i}}{4\pi\kappa\eta_{0}t}e^{-\frac{|x-z_{i}(t)|^{2}}{4\kappa\eta_{0}t}},

for any finite time
T>0
such that there exists a
d_{T}>0
satisfying

\min_{i\neq j}|z_{i}(t)-z_{j}(t)|\geq d_{T}>0\qquad\text{for all}\qquad t\in[0,T].

(1.10)

We refer the readers to
[
13
,
22
,
35
,
18
]
on the existence and uniqueness of the 2D Navier-Stokes equations with such initial data, or in fact more generally, with initial data of finite measures. It is known that when all
\alpha_{i}
have the same sign, the system (
1.7
) has a global solution, and the vortices never collapse (
[
45
]
).

The goal of this paper is to justify the point-vortex system (
1.7
) from the Boltzmann equation in the hydrodynamic limits of
F=F^{\varepsilon,\kappa}
when
(\varepsilon,\kappa)\to 0
. Our main results are stated in Theorem
1.1
.

1.1
Main results

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu
be the global Maxwellian given in (
1.2
),
K_{B}
be the Biot-Savart kernel (
1.8
) and
d_{T}
be the constant defined in (
1.10
).
There exists a local solution
F(t,x,v)\in C([0,T],L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}_{v}^{3}))
to the Boltzmann equation (
1.1
) so that for any
\gamma_{0}\in(1,2)
and
\rho_{1}\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
, there exist constants
C_{0},\rho_{0}>0
and independent of
\kappa,\varepsilon
such that if

\varepsilon\exp\exp\exp\left(C_{0}\exp(\kappa^{-1})\right)\leq 1,

(1.11)

Then

\displaystyle\left|F(t,x,v)-\mu(v)-\varepsilon{\mu}(v)v\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}K_{B}(x-z_{i}(t))\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left\{\frac{\varepsilon}{d(t,x)}\min\left\{1,\frac{e^{\frac{-d_{T}^{2}}{C_{0}\kappa}}}{d(t,x)}\right\}+\frac{\varepsilon}{d(t,x)}e^{-\frac{d(t,x)^{2}}{16\kappa(t+1)}}+\sqrt{\kappa}\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\gamma_{0}}\right\}e^{-\rho_{1}|v|^{2}}

for all
t\in[0,T]
, as long as

d(t,x)=\min_{1\leq i\leq N}|x-z_{i}(t)|\geq 2C_{0}e^{-\frac{d_{T}^{2}}{C_{0}\kappa}}.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.1
gives a precise pointwise estimate for the solution of the Boltzmann solutions for any
x
away from the point vortices
\{z_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{N}
. This is natural, due to the singular nature of the velocity field
K_{B}(x-z_{i}(t))
at the point vortices. More precisely, if
x
is away from the point vortices with
\kappa
, then clearly the Helmholtz-Kirchhoff point vortices are the leading order term.

Corollary 1.3
.

Under the same assumption, there holds

\frac{F(t,x,v)-\mu(v)}{\varepsilon}\to\sqrt{\mu(v)}v\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}K_{B}(x-z_{i}(t)),

uniformly in any compact set
C\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
as
(\varepsilon,\kappa)\to 0
, given
\text{dist}\left(C,\cup_{i=1}^{N}\{z_{i}(t):0\leq t\leq T\}\right)>0
.

Theorem 1.4
.

There exists a local solution
F(t,x,v)
to the Boltzmann equation (
1.1
) so that the microscopic vorticity

{\omega}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(v_{1}\partial_{x_{2}}F-v_{2}\partial_{x_{1}}F\right)dv

(1.12)

converges weakly to

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\delta_{0}(x-z_{i}(t)),

as
\kappa\to 0
and
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Remark 1.5
.

The condition (
1.11
) can be replaced by
\varepsilon\exp\exp(C_{0}\exp(\kappa^{-1}))\leq 1
if the pointwise convergence is replaced by
L^{2}_{x,v}
convergence, by the same analysis, without the need for a third-order approximation. Here, we are able to justify the expansion in the
L^{\infty}
space by a higher order Hilbert expansion. It turns out that the next order term will have an extra exponential growth in
\kappa^{-1}
, which is natural from the convected heat and linearized Navier-Stokes equations (see Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
), giving the condition (
1.11
).

1.2
Difficulties and main ideas

Using the famous Hilbert expansion
[
32
]
, we write the solution
F
of the equation (
1.1
) as

F=\mu+\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}f_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{\mu}f_{2}+\varepsilon^{3}\sqrt{\mu}f_{3}+\varepsilon\delta\sqrt{\mu}f_{R}.

The main aim in the hydrodynamic limit is proving that
\mu+\varepsilon\sqrt{\mu}f_{1}
is the leading order in the above expansion locally. Let us define

f_{a}=\sqrt{\mu}f_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{\mu}f_{2}+\varepsilon^{3}\sqrt{\mu}f_{3}

(1.13)

to be the approximate solution, then one can write the equation for the remainder
f_{R}
as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{R}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{R}+\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}Lf_{R}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\Gamma(f_{a},f_{R})+\frac{\delta}{\kappa\varepsilon}\Gamma(f_{R},f_{R})+R_{B,a}(f_{a})

(1.14)

where

R_{B,a}(f_{a})=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon\delta}\left\{\partial_{t}f_{a}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{a}+\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}Lf_{a}-\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\Gamma(f_{a},f_{a})\right\}.

(1.15)

There are two main difficulties we need to overcome, which can be summarized as follows:

•

Firstly, one needs to
construct a good approximation solution built from the Helmholtz-Kirchhoff point vortex system
. This approximation is highly nontrivial, since the point velocity, formally given as
\frac{x^{\perp}}{|x|^{2}}
, is too singular at
x=0
. At the same, time, the vorticity associated with each vortex is exactly the Dirac delta function placed at the vortex.

•

Secondly, even if one can construct the higher approximations to the Boltzmann equations to make
R_{B,a}=O(\varepsilon^{M})
for any large
M
,
controlling the exact remainder

f_{R}
is the key difficulty, due to linear term
\frac{2}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\Gamma(f_{a},f_{R})\sim\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon}\Gamma(f_{1},f_{R})
and the nonlinear term
\frac{\delta}{\kappa\varepsilon}\Gamma(f_{R},f_{R})
.

These two difficulties are in fact very similar to the justification of the Prandtl boundary layers expansion for the Navier-Stokes equations, while one first needs to show that the Prandtl is well-posed and build a good approximation solution, then one needs to control the exact remainder, where the leading linear term is large (due to the boundary layers in the approximate solution) and the loss of derivatives in the nonlinear term (see for instances,
[
52
,
54
,
6
,
37
,
51
,
27
]
). Below, we give the main ideas on how we overcome each difficulty mentioned above. For general audience, we explain them in the process of the Hilbert expansion with some details, where the expansion itself had been already established by the first author and collaborators
[
34
,
8
]
.

Plugging the approximate solution
f_{a}
in (
1.13
) into the error term
R_{B,a}(f_{a})
given in (
1.15
) and collecting the same order terms, we obtain

\displaystyle R_{B,a}(f_{a})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon\delta}\left\{\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon}Lf_{1}\right\}

(1.16)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\varepsilon\delta}\left\{v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{1}+\frac{1}{\kappa}Lf_{2}-\frac{1}{\kappa}\Gamma(f_{1},f_{1})\right\}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\varepsilon\delta}\cdot\varepsilon\left\{\partial_{t}f_{1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{2}+\frac{1}{\kappa}Lf_{3}-\frac{2}{\kappa}\Gamma(f_{1},f_{2})\right\}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\varepsilon\delta}\cdot\varepsilon^{2}\left\{\partial_{t}f_{2}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{3}-\frac{1}{\kappa}\left(\Gamma(f_{2},f_{2})+2\Gamma(f_{1},f_{3})\right)\right\}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\varepsilon\delta}\cdot\varepsilon^{3}\left\{\partial_{t}f_{3}-\frac{2}{\kappa}\Gamma(f_{2},f_{3})-\frac{\varepsilon}{\kappa}\Gamma(f_{3},f_{3})\right\}.

Here,
L
is the linear operator and
\Gamma
is a bilinear term (see the precise definitions in Section
3
). From the above expression of
R_{B,a}(f_{a})
, it is natural to solve the following system of equations

\begin{cases}&Lf_{1}=0,\\
&v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{1}+\frac{1}{\kappa}Lf_{2}-\frac{1}{\kappa}\Gamma(f_{1},f_{1})=0,\\
&\partial_{t}f_{1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{2}+\frac{1}{\kappa}Lf_{3}-\frac{2}{\kappa}\Gamma(f_{1},f_{2})=0,\\
&P(\partial_{t}f_{2}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{3})=0.\end{cases}

(1.17)

The set of equations (
1.17
) will determine our conditions on
f_{1},f_{2}
and
f_{3}
. Here, the
Pf
denotes the projection of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{v}^{3})
onto the null space
\mathcal{N}=\ker(L).
From the first two equations in (
1.17
) (see our detailed calculations in Section
3.1
), we compute
f_{1}
and the microscopic part of
f_{2}
as follows:

\begin{cases}&f_{1}=u^{\kappa}\cdot v\sqrt{\mu},\\
&(I-P)f_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}u_{i}^{\kappa}u_{j}^{\kappa}\hat{A}_{ij}(v)-\kappa\sum_{i,j}\partial_{j}u_{i}^{\kappa}A_{ij}(v).\end{cases}

(1.18)

Here,
(I-P)f_{2}
is the projection of
f_{2}
onto
(\ker L)^{\perp}
and
\hat{A}_{ij}(v),A_{ij}(v)
are good functions decaying in
v
(see the definition (
3.4
)). This is why
f_{1}
is called the fluid part, as it only lives in the macroscopic space
\mathcal{N}
.
By projecting the second equation in (
1.17
) onto
\mathcal{N}
, we see that
u^{\kappa}

must be the solution to the Navier-Stokes equations
. This is crucial to kill the large factor
-\frac{1}{\delta}
in the third line of (
1.16
). Since we expect the leading order to be
\mu+\sqrt{\mu}f_{1}
, our
\delta
must be at least
\varepsilon^{1+}
, which means that any higher order approximation in
\kappa
at the fluid level will not work, as
\varepsilon\ll\kappa
. Now, let us detail why this will be a difficulty on the Boltzmann equation, namely the approximate solution
f_{a}
.
Since we expect
u^{\kappa}(t)
to behave like the regularized solution,

u^{\kappa}(t,x)\sim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}K_{B}(x-z^{\kappa}_{i}(t))\left(1-e^{-\frac{|x-z^{\kappa}_{i}(t)|^{2}}{4\eta_{0}\kappa t}}\right),

we have

\|\nabla_{x}u^{\kappa}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa t}}.

(1.19)

Looking at the microscopic part of
f_{2}
in (
1.18
), one can see that there is a bilinear term
u_{i}^{\kappa}u_{j}^{\kappa}
and a loss of derivative in
x
term
\kappa\partial_{j}u_{i}^{\kappa}
, which is of order
t^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. The same mechanism happens in higher hierarchy, where the next order has more quadratic terms and is losing more derivatives in both
t
and
x
(
[
29
]
). This phenomenon is very different from constructing the Navier-Stokes equations. To be more precise, from the third equation in (
1.17
), we have

(I-P)f_{3}=L^{-1}(2\Gamma(f_{1},f_{2})-\kappa(\partial_{t}f_{1}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{2})).

Since we expect
\nabla_{x}u^{\kappa}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa t}}
and
\partial_{t}u^{\kappa}\sim t^{-\frac{3}{2}}
at least, this build-up of time singularity is far from time-integrable when controlling the remainder
f_{R}
.
This time singularity also occurs in the equations for the macroscopic parts as a forcing term (see equations (
3.14
) and (
3.13
)). Even with the fact that the linear operator

\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}L

on the left hand side of (
1.14
)
is coercive in the microscopic space
\mathcal{N}^{\perp}
, there is no clear mechanism on how to use the gain in
\varepsilon
to kill the singularity in time.

On the other hand, since the initial velocity
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}K_{B}(x-z_{i,0})
is not in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{2})
, we can not take Fourier transform in
x
and therefore, it is not clear whether one can construct the Boltzmann solutions with such singular velocity, using the Green function developed in
[
42
]
.

To overcome these difficulties, we smooth out the initial vortices by a viscous scale of size
\sqrt{\kappa}
to avoid the initial layer
\sqrt{\kappa t}
. Formally speaking, we run the Boltzmann equations only after a time of order O(1) for point vortices of the Navier-Stokes equations. The key observation is that, on this time scale, the Navier-Stokes solutions still preserve the vortex structures given by the point-vortex system (
1.7
) (see Theorem
2.1
).
Our initial vorticity is given explicitly by

{\omega}_{0}^{\kappa}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{i}}{4\pi\kappa\eta_{0}}e^{-\frac{|x-z_{i,0}|^{2}}{4\kappa\eta_{0}}}\qquad\text{for all}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

(1.20)

which corresponds to the initial velocity

u^{\kappa}_{0}(x)=K_{B}\star_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\omega}_{0}^{\kappa}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2\pi}\frac{(x-z_{i,0})^{\perp}}{|x-z_{i,0}|^{2}}\left(1-e^{-\frac{|x-z_{i,0}|^{2}}{4\kappa\eta_{0}}}\right),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.21)

By constructing an exact solution
u^{\kappa}
solving the Navier-Stokes equations, with a precise pointwise asymptotic expansion

u^{\kappa}(t,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}K_{B}(x-z_{i}^{\kappa}(t))\left(1-e^{-\frac{|x-z^{\kappa}_{i}(t)|^{2}}{4\kappa\eta_{0}(t+1)}}\right)+O(\sqrt{\kappa})_{L^{\infty}_{x}},

where
z_{i}^{\kappa}(t)=z_{i}(t)+O\Big(e^{-\frac{d_{T}^{2}}{C_{0}\kappa(t+1)}}\Big)
, we are able to build
f_{1}
, which is the first order approximation of the Boltzmann equations.

2
Construction of Navier-Stokes solutions

We decompose the vorticity
{\omega}^{\kappa}
of the Navier-Stokes equation (
1.9
) as

{\omega}^{\kappa}(t,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}{\omega}_{i}(t,x)

where
{\omega}_{i}
solves the convected heat equation

\partial_{t}{\omega}_{i}+u^{\kappa}\cdot\nabla_{x}{\omega}_{i}=\kappa\eta_{0}\Delta{\omega}_{i},\qquad{\omega}_{i}|_{t=0}=\frac{1}{4\pi\kappa\eta_{0}}e^{-|x-z_{i,0}|^{2}/4\kappa}

(2.1)

where
u^{\kappa}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}u^{\kappa}_{i}
,
u_{i}^{\kappa}=K_{B}\star_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\omega}_{i}
. From (
2.1
), it is straightforward that

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\omega}_{i}(t,x)dx=0,

and this implies the conservation of momentum for each
{\omega}_{i}
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\omega}_{i}(t,x)dx=1

for all
t>0
and
1\leq i\leq N
.
Our main theorem in this section is as follows

Theorem 2.1
.

There exists a time
T_{\star}>0
such that

\left\|{\omega}^{\kappa}(t,x)-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{i}}{4\pi\kappa\eta_{0}(t+1)}e^{-\frac{|x-z_{i}(t)|^{2}}{4\kappa\eta_{0}(t+1)}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim\kappa

uniformly in
t\in[0,T_{\star}]
.

To describe the precise vortex structure of the Navier-Stokes equations, inspired from
[
21
]
, we use the rescaled variables

X=\frac{x-z_{i}^{\kappa}(t)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}(t+1)}},\qquad\tau=\ln(t+1)

for each
i
and the solution
{\omega}_{i}
solving (
2.1
).
For each
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
, we define

y_{i}^{\kappa}(\tau)=z_{i}^{\kappa}(t),\qquad y_{i}(\tau)=z_{i}(t),\qquad t=e^{\tau}-1.

Here

\{y_{i}^{\kappa}(\tau)\}_{i=1}^{N}
solves the viscous vortex system

\begin{cases}&\partial_{\tau}y^{\kappa}_{i}=e^{\tau}\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{j}K_{B}(y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau))\left(1-e^{-\frac{|y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)|^{2}}{4\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right),\\
&\qquad y_{i}^{\kappa}(0)=z_{i,0}.\end{cases}

(2.2)

where
y_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)=y_{i}^{\kappa}(\tau)-y_{j}^{\kappa}(\tau)
.
It is straight forward that the viscous vortex system has a global solution for all initial data. In the new time variable
\tau\in[0,\infty)
, the equations for
\{y_{i}(\tau)\}_{i=1}^{N}
reads

\begin{cases}&\partial_{\tau}y_{i}=e^{\tau}\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{j}K_{B}(y_{ij}(\tau)),\\
&\qquad y_{i}(0)=z_{i,0}.\end{cases}

(2.3)

In Lemma
4.6
in the appendix, we show that the viscous vortex approximation is close to the original vortices
\{y_{i}(\tau)\}_{i=1}^{N}
as
\kappa\to 0
, as long as they are well separated (
1.10
). To be more precise, we can choose
d_{T}>0
and a time
\tau_{\star}>0
such that the following inequalities hold:

\begin{cases}\min_{i\neq j}|y_{i}(\tau)-y_{j}(\tau)|&\geq d_{T},\\
\min_{i\neq j}|y_{i}^{\kappa}(\tau)-y_{j}^{\kappa}(\tau)|&\geq d_{T},\\
\max_{1\leq i\leq N}|y_{i}^{\kappa}(\tau)-y_{i}(\tau)|&\lesssim e^{-\frac{d_{T}^{2}}{4\kappa}}.\end{cases}

We also define the

G(X)=\frac{1}{4\pi}e^{-|X|^{2}/4},\qquad u^{G}(X)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{X^{\perp}}{|X|^{2}}\left(1-e^{-|X|^{2}/4}\right).

In the rescaled variables
(\tau,X)
, we look for solutions of (
2.1
) of the form

{\omega}_{i}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}\bar{\omega}_{i}\left(\tau,X\right),\qquad u_{i}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\bar{u}_{i}(\tau,X)\\

and rewrite the equation (
2.1
) as follows:

\partial_{\tau}\bar{\omega}_{i}-\mathcal{L}\bar{\omega}_{i}+\frac{1}{\kappa\eta_{0}}\bar{u}_{i}\cdot\nabla_{X}\bar{\omega}_{i}+\frac{1}{\kappa\eta_{0}}\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{j}\left\{\bar{u}_{j}\left(\tau,X+\frac{z_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)-u^{G}\left(\frac{z^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)\right\}\cdot\nabla_{X}\bar{\omega}_{i}=0.

(2.4)

Here, the linear operator
\mathcal{L}
is defined as

\mathcal{L}\bar{\omega}_{i}=\Delta_{X}\bar{\omega}_{i}+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\nabla_{X}\bar{\omega}_{i}+\bar{\omega}_{i}.

We define the weighted
L^{2}
space

\|{\omega}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}_{p}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|{\omega}(\tau,X)|^{2}e^{|X|^{2}/4}dX\right)^{1/2}

for any function
{\omega}
. Our goal is to show that
{\omega}_{i}(\tau,X)
converges to
G(X)
in the space
L^{2}_{p}
as
\kappa\to 0
. The precise theorem is as follows:

Theorem 2.2
.

There exists a time
\tau_{\star}>0
such that

\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq\tau_{\star}}\|\bar{\omega}_{i}(\tau)-G(X)\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\to 0

as
\kappa\to 0^{+}
.

Remark 2.3
.

It is straightforward that Theorem
2.2
implies Theorem
2.1
. As a consequence, we have the weak convergence

{\omega}^{\kappa}(t,x)\rightharpoonup\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}\delta_{z_{i}(t)}

as
\kappa\to 0
.

To show Theorem
2.2
, we establish the expansion

\begin{cases}\bar{\omega}_{i}(\tau,X)&=G(X)+\kappa\eta_{0}\widetilde{w}_{i,a}+\kappa\eta_{0}w_{i},\\
\bar{u}_{i}(\tau,X)&=u^{G}(X)+\kappa\eta_{0}\widetilde{u}_{i,a}+\kappa\eta_{0}u_{i},\end{cases}

(2.5)

where
\widetilde{w}_{i,a}
is the next order approximation that will be constructed in the next section, improving the error induced by
G(X)
, and
w_{i}
is the exact remainder. Here, the velocities
\widetilde{u}_{i,a}
and
u_{i}
are obtained from the vorticities
\widetilde{w}_{i,a}
and
w_{i}
by Biot-Savart law.

2.1
Next order approximation

Putting
\bar{\omega}_{i}=G(X)
and
\bar{u}_{i}=u^{G}(X)
into the equation (
2.4
), we obtain the following error

R_{i}(\tau,X)=\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{j}R_{ij}(\tau,X)

where

R_{ij}(\tau,X)=\frac{1}{\kappa\eta_{0}}\left(u^{G}\left(X+\frac{y_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)-u^{G}\left(\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)\right)\cdot\nabla_{X}G.

In the next lemma, we expand
R_{ij}
in terms of
\kappa
.

Lemma 2.4
.

There holds

\displaystyle R_{ij}(\tau,X)

\displaystyle=T_{ij}(\tau,X)+S_{ij}(\tau,X)

where

\displaystyle T_{ij}(\tau,X)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-|X|^{2}/4}}{16\pi^{2}}\left\{\frac{e^{\tau}}{|z_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)|^{2}}|X|^{2}\sin(2\psi_{ij})-\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}}\frac{e^{3\tau/2}}{|z_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)|^{3}}|X|^{3}\sin(3\psi_{ij})\right\},

(2.6)

\displaystyle S_{ij}(\tau,X)

\displaystyle\lesssim\kappa e^{-\gamma|X|^{2}/4}

for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
, where
\psi
is the angle between
X
and
\eta_{ij}=\frac{y_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa e^{\tau}}}
, determined by the formula

\sin(\psi_{ij})=\frac{X^{\perp}\cdot\eta_{ij}}{|X||\eta_{ij}|}.

Proof.

We consider 2 cases:

Case 1.

|X|\geq\frac{d_{T}}{2\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}.

In this case, we use the fact that
\nabla_{X}G=-\frac{1}{2}XG(X)
and
\|u^{G}\|_{L^{\infty}_{X}}\lesssim 1
to get

R_{ij}(\tau,X)\lesssim\frac{1}{\kappa}\|u^{G}\|_{L^{\infty}}|X|e^{-|X|^{2}/4}\lesssim e^{-\gamma|X|^{2}/4}.

Case 2.

|X|\leq\frac{d_{0}}{2\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}
.

Let
\eta_{ij}=\frac{z^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}
, then
R_{i}
can be written as

R_{i}(\tau,X)=\frac{1}{\kappa\eta_{0}}\sum_{j\neq i}\left(u^{G}(X+\eta_{ij})-u^{G}(\eta_{ij})\right)\cdot\nabla_{X}G.

Now we have

2\pi\left(u^{G}(X+\eta_{ij})-u^{G}(\eta_{ij})\right)=\left\{\frac{(X+\eta)^{\perp}}{|X+\eta|^{2}}-\frac{\eta^{\perp}}{|\eta|^{2}}\right\}-\frac{(X+\eta)^{\perp}}{|X+\eta|^{2}}e^{-|X+\eta|^{2}/4}+\frac{\eta^{\perp}}{|\eta|^{2}}e^{-|\eta|^{2}/4}.

The last two terms in the above expression are extremely small, due to the fact that
|X+\eta|\geq|\eta|-|X|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\eta|\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{d_{T}}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}
and
|\eta|\geq\frac{d_{T}}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}
.
Hence we shall only consider

\frac{1}{\kappa\eta_{0}}\left\{\frac{(X+\eta_{ij})^{\perp}}{|X+\eta_{ij}|^{2}}-\frac{\eta_{ij}^{\perp}}{|\eta_{ij}|^{2}}\right\}\cdot\nabla_{X}G

with
|X|\leq\frac{1}{2}|\eta_{ij}|
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(X+\eta_{ij})^{\perp}}{|X+\eta_{ij}|^{2}}-\frac{\eta_{ij}^{\perp}}{|\eta_{ij}|^{2}}\right)\cdot X=\eta_{ij}^{\perp}\cdot X\left(\frac{1}{|X+\eta_{ij}|^{2}}-\frac{1}{|\eta_{ij}|^{2}}\right)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\frac{|X|^{n}}{|\eta_{ij}|^{n}}\sin(n\psi_{ij})

\displaystyle=(-1)|X|^{2}\frac{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}{|y_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)|^{2}}\sin(2\psi_{ij})+|X|^{3}\frac{(\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau})^{\frac{3}{2}}}{|y_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)|^{3}}\sin(3\psi_{ij})+O\left(|X|^{4}\frac{(\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau})^{2}}{|y_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)|^{4}}\right),

where
\psi_{ij}
is the angle between
X
and
\eta_{ij}=\frac{y_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}
. The proof is complete.
∎

To kill the
O(1)
term in the remainder
R_{i}
coming from the approximation
(w_{i},v_{i})=(G,u^{G})
, we construct the second approximation

\begin{cases}\bar{\omega}_{i,app}&=G(X)+\varphi(\tau)\kappa\eta_{0}w_{i,a}(\tau,X),\\
\bar{u}_{i,app}&=u^{G}(X)+\varphi(\tau)\kappa\eta_{0}u_{i,a}(\tau,X),\\
u_{i,a}&=K\star_{X}w_{i,a}.\\
\end{cases}

(2.7)

Here,
\varphi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a cut-off function defined by

\varphi(\tau)=\begin{cases}&0\qquad\text{if}\qquad\tau\leq(\eta_{0}\kappa)^{\alpha},\\
&1\qquad\text{if}\qquad\tau\geq 2(\eta_{0}\kappa)^{\alpha}.\end{cases}

(2.8)

where the parameter
\alpha
is small,
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
The purpose of the cut-off function
\varphi
is to have

\bar{w}_{i,app}|_{\tau=0}=G(X),

which will give zero initial condition for the remainder (
2.17
).
We also define

\widetilde{w}_{i,a}(\tau,X)=\varphi(\tau)w_{i,a}(\tau,X),\qquad\widetilde{u}_{i,a}(\tau,X)=\varphi(\tau)u_{i,a}(\tau,X).

It is straight forward that
\widetilde{w}_{i,a}|_{\tau=0}=0
and
\widetilde{u}_{i,a}=K_{B}\star_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\widetilde{w}_{i,a}
.
Plugging the above into the left hand side of (
2.4
), we have the new error to be

\widetilde{R}_{i,app}(\tau,X)=\varphi(\tau)R_{i,app}(\tau,X)+\kappa\eta_{0}\varphi^{\prime}(\tau)w_{i,a},

(2.9)

where

\displaystyle R_{i,app}(\tau,X)

\displaystyle=\kappa\eta_{0}(\partial_{\tau}-\mathcal{L})w_{i,a}+\left\{\Lambda w_{i,a}+T_{i}(\tau,X)\right\}+S_{i}(\tau,X)

(2.10)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\neq i}u_{j,a}\left(\tau,X+\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa e^{\tau}}}\right)\cdot\nabla_{X}G

\displaystyle+\kappa\eta_{0}\varphi(\tau)\sum_{j\neq i}u_{j,a}\left(\tau,X+\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa e^{\tau}}}\right)\cdot\nabla_{X}w_{i,a}+\kappa\eta_{0}\varphi(\tau)\left(u_{i,a}\cdot\nabla_{X}w_{i,a}\right).

Proposition 2.5
.

[
19
,
21
]

There exists a solution
w_{i,a}
to the problem

\kappa\eta_{0}(1-\mathcal{L})w_{i,a}+\Lambda w_{i,a}=-T_{i}(\tau,X)

such that

|w_{i,a}(\tau,X)|+|\nabla_{X}w_{i,a}(\tau,X)|+|\partial_{\tau}w_{i,a}(\tau,X)|\lesssim e^{-\gamma|X|^{2}/4}

(2.11)

for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
.
Moreover,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}w_{i,a}dX=0
.

Proof.

We sketch the proof here for the readers’s convenience.
Let
r=|X|
. We can rewrite
T_{ij}(\tau,X)
as a linear combination of the terms of the form

r^{2}e^{-r^{2}/4}\sin(2\psi_{ij})

and
r^{3}e^{-r^{2}/4}\sin(3\psi_{ij})
. It suffices to solve the equation

\kappa\eta_{0}(1-\mathcal{L})w_{n}+\Lambda w_{n}=r^{n}e^{-r^{2}/4}\sin(n\theta),

(2.12)

where
\theta
is the angle between
X
and
\eta_{ij}
and
n\in\{1,2\}
.
The solution is constructed via the iteration formula

w_{n}=\Lambda^{-1}\left\{r^{n}e^{-r^{2}/4}\sin(n\theta)\right\}+\kappa\eta_{0}\Lambda^{-1}\left\{(1-\mathcal{L})w_{n}\right\}=w_{n}^{0}+\kappa\eta_{0}\Lambda^{-1}(1-\mathcal{L})w_{n}.

(2.13)

Construction of
\Lambda^{-1}
:
We look for solution
w_{n}^{0}
of the form

w^{0}_{n}=\Delta(\Omega^{0}_{n}(r)\cos(n\theta))=a_{n}^{0}(r)\cos(n\theta),

where
\Omega^{0}_{n}(r)\cos(n\theta)
is the stream function. This implies

-\left(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}-\frac{n^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\Omega_{n}^{0}=a_{n}^{0}(r).

The velocity corresponding to
w_{n}^{0}
is then given by
v_{n}^{0}=-\frac{n}{r}\Omega_{n}^{0}(r)\sin(n\theta)e_{r}+\partial_{r}\Omega_{n}^{0}(r)\cos(n\theta)e_{\theta}
. This implies

\Lambda w_{n}^{0}=v_{n}^{0}\cdot\nabla_{X}G+v^{G}\cdot\nabla_{X}w_{n}^{0}=\left\{\frac{n}{2}\Omega_{n}^{0}(r)\frac{1}{4\pi}e^{-r^{2}/4}-na_{n}^{0}(r)\frac{1-e^{-r^{2}/4}}{2\pi r^{2}}\right\}\sin(n\theta).

(2.14)

Combining (
2.12
) and (
2.14
), we have the differential equation

\partial_{r}^{2}\Omega_{n}^{0}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}\Omega_{n}^{0}+\left(\frac{r^{2}/4}{e^{r^{2}/4}-1}-\frac{n^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)\Omega_{n}^{0}=\frac{2\pi}{n}r^{n}e^{-r^{2}/4}.

By a standard ODE theory, the solution can be constructed via the Green function for the homogeneous problem, with the decaying forcing on the right. The solution
w_{n}
is constructed using the iteration (
3.5
) and satisfies the pointwise bound (
2.11
) (see Remark 1 in
[
19
]
). The proof is complete.
∎

In the next proposition, we estimate the error given by the approximation (
2.7
), (
2.9
) and (
2.10
).

Proposition 2.6
.

There holds

R_{i,app}(\tau,X)\lesssim\kappa e^{-\gamma|X|^{2}/4}

for any
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle R_{i,app}(\tau,X)

\displaystyle=S_{i}(\tau,X)+\sum_{j\neq i}u_{j,a}\left(\tau,X+\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa e^{\tau}}}\right)\cdot\nabla_{X}G

\displaystyle+\kappa\eta_{0}\varphi(\tau)\sum_{j\neq i}u_{j,a}\left(\tau,X+\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa e^{\tau}}}\right)\cdot\nabla_{X}w_{i,a}+\kappa\eta_{0}\varphi(\tau)\left(u_{i,a}\cdot\nabla_{X}w_{i,a}\right).

By using the inequality (
2.11
) and the fact that
\|u_{i,a}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|w_{i,a}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}\lesssim 1
, we have

\displaystyle|R_{i,app}(\tau,X)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\kappa|\partial_{\tau}w_{i,a}|+|S_{i}|+\sum_{j,\neq i}|u_{j,a}|_{L^{\infty}}e^{-\gamma|X|^{2}/4}+\kappa\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{i,a}\|_{L^{\infty}}e^{-\gamma|X|^{2}/4}

\displaystyle\lesssim\kappa e^{-\gamma|X|^{2}/4}.

The proof is complete.
∎

2.2
Remainder estimates

Now we write

\begin{cases}\bar{\omega}_{i}&=\bar{\omega}_{i,app}+\kappa\eta_{0}w_{i}=G(X)+\kappa\eta_{0}\varphi(\tau)w_{i,a}+\kappa\eta_{0}\eta_{0}w_{i},\\
\bar{u}_{i}&=\bar{u}_{i,app}+\kappa\eta_{0}u_{i}=v^{G}(X)+\kappa\eta_{0}\varphi(\tau)u_{i,a}+\kappa\eta_{0}u_{i},\end{cases}

(2.15)

where

w_{i}|_{\tau=0}=0,\qquad u_{i}=K_{B}\star_{X}w_{i}.

Plugging the above into the equation (
2.4
), we get

\displaystyle(\partial_{\tau}-\mathcal{L})w_{i}+\frac{1}{\kappa\eta_{0}}\Lambda w_{i}+\varphi(\tau)\left(u_{i,a}\cdot\nabla_{X}w_{i}+u_{i}\cdot\nabla_{X}w_{i,a}\right)

(2.16)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\kappa\eta_{0}}\sum_{j\neq i}u_{j}\left(\tau,X+\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)\cdot\nabla\bar{\omega}_{i,app}+\frac{1}{\kappa\eta_{0}}\sum_{j\neq i}\left\{\bar{u}_{j,app}\left(X+\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)-u^{G}\left(\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)\right\}\cdot\nabla w_{i}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j\neq i}u_{j}\left(\tau,X+\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)\cdot\nabla_{X}w_{i}+\varphi(\tau)\cdot\frac{1}{\kappa\eta_{0}}R_{i,app}(\tau,X)+\varphi^{\prime}(\tau)w_{i,a}=0

with the initial condition

w_{i}|_{\tau=0}=0.

(2.17)

We recall that

\widetilde{R}_{i,a}(\tau,X)=\varphi(\tau)R_{i,app}(\tau,X)+\kappa\eta_{0}\varphi^{\prime}(\tau)w_{i,a}

where

R_{i,app}(\tau,X)\lesssim\kappa e^{-\gamma|X|^{2}/4}.

We define the energy norm for the remainder
w_{i,a}
as follows:

E_{w}(\tau)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|w_{i}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}_{p}}^{2},\qquad D_{w}(\tau)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla_{X}w_{i}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}_{p}}^{2}

where
p(X)=e^{|X|^{2}/4}
.
Our goal of this section is to show the apriori estimate, as stated in the following proposition

Proposition 2.7
.

There holds

E_{w}(\tau_{\star})\lesssim\int_{0}^{\tau_{\star}}\left(E_{w}(\tau)+E_{w}(\tau)^{2}\right)d\tau+1+o(1)\cdot\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq\tau_{\star}}E_{w}(\tau).

To show the above proposition, we compute, using the equation (
2.16
):

\displaystyle E_{w}^{\prime}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\langle Lw_{i}w_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}-\frac{1}{\kappa\eta_{0}}\langle\Lambda w_{i},w_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varphi(\tau)\left(u_{i,a}\cdot\nabla w_{i}+u_{i}\cdot\nabla w_{i,a}\right)w_{i}p(X)dX

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\kappa\eta_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u_{j}\left(\tau,X+\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)\cdot\nabla\bar{\omega}_{i,app}\cdot w_{i}p(X)dX

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\kappa\eta_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{j\neq i}\left\{\bar{u}_{j,app}\left(X+\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)-u^{G}\left(\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)\right\}\cdot\nabla w_{i}\cdot w_{i}p(X)dX

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\kappa\eta_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varphi(\tau)R_{i,app}(\tau,X)w_{i}p(X)dX-\varphi^{\prime}(\tau)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}w_{i,a}(\tau,X)w_{i}(\tau,X)p(X)dX.

In the rest of this section, we estimate each term that appears on the right hand side of the above identity, which will conclude the proof of Proposition
2.7
, by the standard Gronwall lemma.

Lemma 2.8
.

There hold

\langle\mathcal{L}w_{i},w_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}=-\|\nabla_{X}w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}^{2}+\|w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}^{2}

(2.18)

and

\langle\Lambda w_{i},w_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}=0.

(2.19)

Proof.

We first show (
2.18
).
We recall that
\mathcal{L}w_{i}=\Delta w_{i}+\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\nabla_{X}w_{i}+w_{i}
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Delta w_{i}w_{i}pdX

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla w_{i}\cdot\nabla(pw_{i})dX=-\|\nabla w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\nabla w_{i}\cdot\nabla p)w_{i}

\displaystyle=-\|\nabla w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int(\nabla w_{i}\cdot X)w_{i}p.

Now we show (
2.19
). We have

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda w_{i},w_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}_{p}}

\displaystyle=\int u^{G}\cdot\nabla w_{i}w_{i}pdX+\int u_{i}\cdot\nabla Gw_{i}pdX=-\frac{1}{2}\int{\rm div}(u^{G}p)|w_{i}|^{2}-\frac{1}{8\pi}\int(u_{i}\cdot X)w_{i}dX.

It suffices to show that
\int u_{i}\cdot Xw_{i}=0
.
By integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\int u_{i}\cdot Xw_{i}

\displaystyle=\int(u_{i}^{1}X_{1}+u_{i}^{2}X_{2})(\partial_{2}u_{i}^{1}-\partial_{1}u_{i}^{2})

\displaystyle=\int u_{i}^{2}\partial_{1}(u_{i}^{1}X_{1}+u_{i}^{2}X_{2})-\int u_{i}^{1}\partial_{2}(u_{i}^{1}X_{1}+u_{i}^{2}X_{2})

\displaystyle=\int\left(u_{i}^{2}\partial_{1}u_{i}^{1}X_{1}+u_{i}^{2}\partial_{1}u_{i}^{2}X_{2}-u_{i}^{1}\partial_{2}u_{i}^{1}X_{1}-u_{i}^{1}\partial_{2}u_{i}^{2}X_{2}\right)=0.

Here, we use the fact that
\partial_{1}u_{i}^{1}+\partial_{2}u_{i}^{2}=0
. ∎

Lemma 2.9
.

There holds

-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varphi(\tau)\left(u_{i,a}\cdot\nabla w_{i}+u_{i}\cdot\nabla w_{i,a}\right)w_{i}p(X)dX\lesssim o(1)\|\nabla w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}^{2}+\|w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}^{2}.

Proof.

The inequality follows from the following estimates

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u_{i,a}\cdot\nabla w_{i}w_{i}p(X)dX\lesssim\|u_{i,a}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\|w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\lesssim\|\nabla w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\|w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u_{i}\cdot\nabla w_{i,a}w_{i}p(X)dX\lesssim\|\nabla w_{i,a}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\|w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\lesssim\|w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}^{2}.

In the above, we used the elliptic estimates in Lemma
4.7
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

There holds

\frac{1}{\kappa\eta_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u_{j}\left(\tau,X+\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)\cdot\nabla\bar{\omega}_{i,app}\cdot w_{i}p(X)dX\lesssim\|w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\|w_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}.

Proof.

Since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}w_{i}dX=0
, using Lemma
4.7
, we have

\sup_{X\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+|X|^{2})|u_{j}(\tau,X)|\lesssim\|w_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}.

Hence we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u_{j}\left(\tau,X+\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)\cdot\nabla\bar{\omega}_{i,app}\cdot w_{i}p(X)dX

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\kappa}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\|w_{j}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}}{1+\left|X+\frac{y_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right|^{2}}|\nabla\bar{\omega}_{i,app}||w_{i}|p(X)dX.

We divide the above integrals into 2 parts:
|X|\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{|y_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)|}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}
and
|X|\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{|y_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)|}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}
.
In the first case, we have

|X|\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{|y_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)|}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\geq\frac{d_{T}}{2\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}.

This implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa\eta_{0}}\int_{|X|\geq\frac{|y_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)|}{2\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}}\frac{1}{1+\left|X+\frac{y_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa e^{\tau}}}\right|^{2}}|\nabla\bar{\omega}_{i,app}||w_{i}|p(X)dX

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\cdot\frac{1}{\kappa}\left(\int_{|X|\geq\frac{d_{T}}{2\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}}|\nabla_{X}\bar{\omega}_{i,app}|^{2}e^{|X|^{2}/4}dX\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\cdot\frac{1}{\kappa}\left(\int_{|X|\geq\frac{d_{T}}{2\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}}e^{-\gamma|X|^{2}/2}e^{|X|^{2}/4}dX\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\|w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}.

Now for
|X|\leq\frac{|y_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)|}{2\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}
, we use the elliptic estimate in Lemma
4.7
to get

\frac{1}{\kappa}\frac{1}{1+\left|X+\frac{y_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\kappa}\cdot\frac{1}{\left|X+\frac{y_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right|^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\kappa}\frac{\kappa e^{\tau}}{|y_{ij}^{\kappa}(\tau)|^{2}}\lesssim 1.

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

There holds

-\frac{1}{\kappa}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\sum_{j\neq i}\left\{\bar{u}_{j,app}\left(\tau,X+\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)-u^{G}\left(\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)\right\}\cdot\nabla w_{i}\cdot w_{i}p(X)dX\lesssim E_{w}(\tau).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\kappa}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\{\bar{u}_{j,app}\left(\tau,X+\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)-u^{G}\left(\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)\right\}\cdot\nabla w_{i}\cdot w_{i}p(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\{\bar{u}_{j,app}\left(\tau,X+\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)-u^{G}\left(\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)\right\}\cdot\nabla_{X}p(X)|w_{i}|^{2}dX

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|w_{j,a}|_{L^{\infty}}|w_{i}|^{2}p(X)dX+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|R_{ij}(\tau,X)||w_{i}|^{2}dX\lesssim\|w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}^{2}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

There holds

-\frac{1}{\kappa}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varphi(\tau)R_{i,app}(\tau,X)w_{i}p(X)dX\lesssim E_{w}(\tau)+1.

and for
\tau_{\star}>0
:

\int_{0}^{\tau_{\star}}|\varphi^{\prime}(\tau)|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|w_{i,a}(\tau,X)w_{i}(\tau,X)\right|p(X)dXd\tau\lesssim o(1)\cdot\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq\tau_{\star}}E_{w}(\tau)+1.

Proof.

The first inequality follows immediately from Proposition
2.6
. The second inequality follows from Holder inequality and the fact that
\int_{0}^{\tau_{\star}}|\varphi^{\prime}(\tau)|d\tau\lesssim 1
. The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.13
.

There holds

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u_{j}\left(\tau,X+\frac{y^{\kappa}_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)\cdot\nabla_{X}w_{i}w_{i}p(X)dX\lesssim o(1)\|\nabla_{X}w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}^{2}+E_{w}(\tau)^{2}.

Proof.

By Holder inequality, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u_{j}\left(\tau,X+\frac{y_{ij}(\tau)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}e^{\tau}}}\right)\cdot\nabla_{X}w_{i}w_{i}p(X)dX\lesssim\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla_{X}w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\|w_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{p}}\lesssim\|\nabla_{X}w_{i}\|_{L^{2}}E_{w}(\tau).

The proof is complete.
∎

Next we obtain the rate of convergence in the following theorem

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
d_{T}>0
be the positive number satisfying (
1.10
).
Let

d(t,x)=\min_{1\leq i\leq N}|x-z_{i}(t)|.

There exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that

\left|u^{\kappa}(t,x)-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}K_{B}(x-z_{i}(t))\right|\lesssim\min\left\{\frac{1}{d(t,x)},\frac{e^{-\frac{d_{T}^{2}}{C_{0}\kappa}}}{d(t,x)^{2}}\right\}+\frac{1}{d(t,x)}e^{-\frac{d(t,x)^{2}}{16\kappa(t+1)}}+\sqrt{\kappa},

as long as

d(t,x)\geq 2C_{0}e^{-\frac{d_{T}^{2}}{C_{0}\kappa}}.

Proof.

From the asymptotic expansion (
2.15
), we have

\left|u^{\kappa}(t,x)-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha_{i}K_{B}(x-z_{i}^{\kappa}(t))\left(1-e^{-\frac{|x-z_{i}^{\kappa}(t)|^{2}}{4\kappa(t+1)}}\right)\right|\lesssim\sqrt{\kappa}.

Therefore, it suffices to show that

K_{B}(x-z_{i}^{\kappa}(t))\left(1-e^{-\frac{|x-z_{i}^{\kappa}(t)|^{2}}{4\kappa(t+1)}}\right)-K_{B}(x-z_{i}(t)).

Let
\widetilde{x}=x-z_{i}(t)
and
\widetilde{h}=z_{i}(t)-z_{i}^{\kappa}(t)
. We have

\displaystyle K_{B}(\widetilde{x}+\widetilde{h})\left(1-e^{-\frac{|\widetilde{x}+\widetilde{h}|^{2}}{4\kappa(t+1)}}\right)-K_{B}(\widetilde{x})

\displaystyle=K_{B}(\widetilde{x}+\widetilde{h})-K_{B}(\widetilde{x})-K_{B}(\widetilde{x}+\widetilde{h})e^{-\frac{|\widetilde{x}+\widetilde{h}|^{2}}{4\kappa(t+1)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|\widetilde{h}|}{|\widetilde{x}+\widetilde{h}|^{2}}\left(1+\frac{|\widetilde{h}|}{|\widetilde{x}|}\right)+\frac{1}{|\widetilde{x}+\widetilde{h}|}e^{-\frac{|\widetilde{x}+\widetilde{h}|^{2}}{4\kappa(t+1)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|\widetilde{h}|}{|\widetilde{x}|^{2}}+\frac{1}{|\widetilde{x}|}e^{-\frac{|\widetilde{x}|^{2}}{16\kappa(t+1)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\min\left\{\frac{1}{|\widetilde{x}|},\frac{e^{-\frac{d_{T}^{2}}{C_{0}\kappa}}}{|\widetilde{x}|^{2}}\right\}+\frac{1}{|\widetilde{x}|}e^{-\frac{|\widetilde{x}|^{2}}{16\kappa(t+1)}}.

as long as
|\widetilde{x}|\geq 2|\widetilde{h}|
. Here, we used Lemma
4.6
to get
|\widetilde{h}|\leq C_{0}e^{-\frac{d_{T}^{2}}{C_{0}\kappa}}

for some
C_{0}>0
. Hence as long as
|\widetilde{x}|\geq 2C_{0}e^{-\frac{d_{T}^{2}}{C_{0}\kappa}}
, we have the desired inequality.∎

2.3
Higher derivative estimates

In this section, we give uniform estimates on higher derivatives of the Navier-Stokes solution
u^{\kappa}
. Our main theorem is as follows

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
m,n\geq 0
be an integer.
There exists
C_{0}>0
such that

\|\partial_{t}^{n}u^{\kappa}\|_{W^{m,\infty}}+\|\partial_{t}^{n}\nabla_{x}u^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}+\|\partial_{t}^{n}p^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}+\|\partial_{t}^{n}p^{\kappa}\|_{W^{m,\infty}}\lesssim\exp\left(C_{0}e^{\kappa^{-1}}\right).

Proof.

First we consider
n=0
. We define

u^{\kappa}_{a}(t,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{i}}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}(t+1)}}u^{G}\left(\frac{x-z_{i}^{\kappa}(t)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}(t+1)}}\right).

It is straightforward that
u^{\kappa}(t)-u_{a}^{\kappa}(t)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Moreover, we have
u_{0}^{\kappa}(x)=u^{\kappa}_{a}|_{t=0}
.
At the same time, we define the approximate pressure

p_{a}^{\kappa}(t,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{i}}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}(t+1)}}p^{G}\left(\frac{x-z_{i}^{\kappa}(t)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}(t+1)}}\right)

where
p^{G}=p^{G}(X)
is determined through the elliptic equation

-\Delta_{X}p^{G}={\rm div}(v^{G}\cdot\nabla_{X}v^{G}).

Since a single point vortex is a solution to the Navier-Stokes equations, we have

\partial_{t}v^{G}+v^{G}\cdot\nabla_{X}v^{G}+\nabla_{X}p^{G}-\kappa\eta_{0}\Delta_{X}v^{G}=0

(2.20)

Now we write

u^{\kappa}(t,x)=u^{\kappa}_{a}(t,x)+u^{\kappa}_{R}(t,x),\qquad p^{\kappa}(t,x)=p_{a}^{\kappa}(t,x)+p_{R}^{\kappa}(t,x)

and
u^{\kappa}_{R}
solves the equation

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}u^{\kappa}_{R}+u_{R}^{\kappa}\cdot\nabla u_{a}^{\kappa}+u_{a}^{\kappa}\cdot\nabla u_{R}^{\kappa}+u_{R}^{\kappa}\cdot\nabla u_{R}^{\kappa}+\nabla p^{\kappa}_{R}=\kappa\eta_{0}\Delta u^{\kappa}_{R}+\mathcal{F}_{R},\\
&{\rm div}(u_{R}^{\kappa})=0.\end{cases}

(2.21)

The approximation error
\mathcal{F}_{R}
is computed from (
2.20
) as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{R}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}u_{a}^{\kappa}+u^{\kappa}_{a}\cdot\nabla_{x}u_{a}^{\kappa}+\nabla p_{a}^{\kappa}-\kappa\eta_{0}\Delta u^{\kappa}_{a}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\kappa\eta_{0}(t+1))^{\frac{3}{2}}}\sum_{j\neq i}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i}\left\{u^{G}\left(\frac{x-z_{j}^{\kappa}(t)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}(t+1)}}\right)\cdot\nabla_{X}u^{G}\left(\frac{x-z_{i}^{\kappa}(t)}{\sqrt{\kappa\eta_{0}(t+1)}}\right)\right\}.

Let
m\geq 0
be an integer. Using the fact that
\|u^{G}\|_{L^{\infty}_{X}}+\|\nabla_{X}u^{G}\|_{H^{m}}\lesssim 1
, we have

\|\mathcal{F}_{R}\|_{H^{m}}\lesssim\kappa^{-\frac{m+3}{2}}.

(2.22)

Performing a standard energy estimate for the equation (
2.21
), we have

\frac{d}{dt}\|u^{\kappa}_{R}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}\lesssim\|u_{a}^{\kappa}\|_{W^{m+1,\infty}}\|u_{R}^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{\kappa}^{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{R}^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}^{2}+\|\mathcal{F}_{R}\|_{H^{m}}\|u_{R}^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}.

Using the standard elliptic estimate

\|\nabla_{x}u^{\kappa}_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim(1+\|{\omega}^{\kappa}_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}})\left(1+\ln^{+}(\|u^{\kappa}_{R}\|_{H^{3}})+\ln^{+}(\|{\omega}^{\kappa}_{R}\|_{L^{2}})\right),

we get

\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{R}^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}\lesssim\|u_{a}^{\kappa}\|_{W^{m+1,\infty}}\|u_{R}^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}+\|u_{R}^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}\left(\ln^{+}\|u_{R}^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}+1+\|{\omega}_{R}^{\kappa}\|_{L^{\infty}}\ln^{+}\|{\omega}_{R}^{\kappa}\|_{L^{2}}\right)+\|\mathcal{F}_{R}\|_{H^{m}}.

By
\|{\omega}_{R}^{\kappa}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\kappa^{-1}
,
\|u_{a}^{\kappa}\|_{W^{m+1,\infty}}\lesssim\kappa^{-m/2-1}
and the inequality (
2.22
) we have

\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{R}^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}\lesssim\kappa^{-\frac{m}{2}-1}\|u_{R}^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}+\kappa^{-1}\|u_{R}^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}\ln(1+\|u_{R}^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}})+\kappa^{-\frac{m+3}{2}}.

By the Gronwall inequality, we have

\|u_{R}^{\kappa}(t)\|_{H^{m}}\lesssim\exp\left(C_{0}e^{\kappa^{-1}}\right)

for some constant
C_{0}>0
. Finally, we have

\|\nabla_{x}u^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}\leq\|\nabla_{x}u_{a}^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}+\|\nabla u_{R}^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}\lesssim\exp(C_{0}e^{\kappa^{-1}}).

The bound on
\|u^{\kappa}\|_{L^{\infty}}
follows from Sobolev embedding with
m
is chosen to be large. Now we estimate the pressure. From the Navier-Stokes equation, we have

-\Delta p^{\kappa}=\sum_{i,j\leq 2}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(u^{\kappa}_{i}u^{\kappa}_{j}).

Taking derivatives in
x
and
t
of both sides, we have

-\Delta(\partial_{x}^{m}\partial_{t}^{n}p^{\kappa})=\sum_{i,j\leq 2}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}(\partial_{x}^{m}\partial_{t}^{n}(u_{i}^{\kappa}u_{j}^{\kappa})).

This implies

\partial_{t}^{n}\partial_{x}^{m}p^{\kappa}(t,x)=\sum_{i,j}\mathcal{R}_{i}\mathcal{R}_{j}(\partial_{t}^{n}\partial_{x}^{m}(u_{i}^{\kappa}u_{j}^{\kappa})).

where
\mathcal{R}_{i}
is the Riesz transform. Using the fact that the operator
\mathcal{R}_{i}
is bounded from
L^{2}
to
L^{2}
, we have

\|\partial_{t}^{n}p^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}\lesssim\sum_{i,j}\|\partial_{t}^{n}(u_{i}^{\kappa}u_{j}^{\kappa})\|_{H^{m}}.

(2.23)

When
n=0
, if
m\geq 1
, the estimate for
\|p^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}
follows from

\|p^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}\lesssim\|u^{\kappa}\|_{W^{m,\infty}}\|\nabla u^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}\lesssim\exp\left(2C_{0}e^{\kappa^{-1}}\right),

while if
m=0
, we have
\|p^{\kappa}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\||u_{\kappa}|^{2}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\exp(C_{0}e^{\kappa^{-1}})
, using the expansion (
2.15
) and the fact that
u^{G}(X)
decays like
\frac{1}{|X|}
as
|X|\to\infty
.

Now for
\|\partial_{t}^{n}u^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}
and
\|\partial_{t}^{n}p^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}
, we use induction on
n
. Namely, for
n=1
, we have

\partial_{t}u^{\kappa}=-u^{\kappa}\cdot\nabla u^{\kappa}-\nabla p^{\kappa}+\kappa\eta_{0}\Delta u^{\kappa}.

This implies

\|\partial_{t}u^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}\lesssim\|u^{\kappa}\|_{W^{m,\infty}}\|\nabla_{x}u^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}+\|p^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m+1}}+\kappa\|\nabla_{x}u^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m+1}}\lesssim\exp\left(2C_{0}e^{-\kappa^{-1}}\right).

From the above, we can estimate
\|\partial_{t}p^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}
by using the inequality (
2.23
) and the bound on
\|\partial_{t}u^{\kappa}\|_{H^{m}}
above. We shall skip the details for the remaining argument. The proof is complete.
∎

In other to simplify the notations for the Navier-Stokes parts, we denote
c_{\kappa}
to be

c_{\kappa}=\exp(C_{0}e^{\kappa^{-1}}).

Remark 2.16
.

By possibly increasing the value of the constant
C_{0}
, we may replace all the upper bounds involving
c_{\kappa}^{2},c_{\kappa}^{3},\cdots
or
\kappa^{-M}
by just
c_{\kappa}
for simplicity of notation.

In this section, we construct solution of the Boltzmann equation (

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{gain}}(F,G)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-v_{\star})\cdot\widetilde{s}|\sqrt{\mu(v_{\star})}\left(F(v^{\prime})G(v_{\star}^{\prime})+F(v_{\star}^{\prime})G(v^{\prime})\right)d\widetilde{s}dv_{\star},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{loss}}(F,G)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-v_{\star})\cdot\widetilde{s}|\sqrt{\mu(v_{\star})}\left(F(v)G(v_{\star})+F(v_{\star})G(v)\right)d\widetilde{s}dv_{\star}.

We recall the linear operator

(3.2)

\mathcal{N}

\mathcal{N}

\mathcal{N}^{\perp}

in the space

(3.3)

We define

3.1
Hilbert expansion

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{R}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{R}+\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}Lf_{R}

\displaystyle R_{B,a}(f_{a})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\varepsilon\delta}\cdot\varepsilon^{2}\left\{\partial_{t}f_{2}+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{3}-\frac{1}{\kappa}\left(\Gamma(f_{2},f_{2})+2\Gamma(f_{1},f_{3})\right)\right\}

For
1\leq i\leq 3

Pf_{i}=\left(a_{i}(t,x)+\sum_{k=1}^{3}b^{k}_{i}(t,x)v_{k}+c_{i}(t,x)\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{2}\right)\sqrt{\mu}

Proposition 3.1

(I-P)f_{2}(t,x,v)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}u_{i}^{\kappa}u_{j}^{\kappa}\hat{A}_{ij}(v)-\kappa\sum_{i,j}\partial_{j}u_{i}^{\kappa}A_{ij}(v).

(3.12)

Let
b_{2}

\displaystyle\partial_{t}b_{2}+\eta_{0}(u^{\kappa}\cdot\nabla_{x}b_{2}+b_{2}\cdot\nabla_{x}u^{\kappa})+\nabla_{x}p_{2}

(3.13)

\displaystyle=\kappa\eta_{0}\Delta b_{2}-\sum_{j}\langle A_{ij},\partial_{j}(2\Gamma(f_{1},(I-P)f_{2})-\kappa(v\cdot\nabla_{x}((I-P)f_{2})))\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}

with zero initial data. Define

3.4
L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x,v}
and
L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}
estimates

We recall the norms defined in section
3.2
. Our goal of this section is to derive estimates for the following two quantities

\|\mathfrak{m}f_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}^{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}f_{R}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x,v}}^{2}.

To highlight our main points, we write the simplified equations, keeping only the most singular terms in (
3.22
). Namely, after constructing the approximate solution
f_{a}
of the Boltzmann equation, the remainder equation has the form:

\partial_{t}f_{R}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{R}+\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}Lf_{R}=\frac{2}{\kappa\varepsilon}\Gamma(f_{1},f_{R})+\frac{\delta}{\kappa\varepsilon}\Gamma(f_{R},f_{R})+\text{other terms}.

In order to treat the nonlinear term in the
L^{2}
estimate, it is crucial to bound the
L^{\infty}
norm of
f_{R}
, namely by
L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x,v}
and
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x,v}
. To this end, we use the
L^{p}-L^{\infty}
estimates for the Boltzmann equations (see
[
30
,
34
]
and references therein). This is due to a hidden mixing effect coming from the transport operator
\frac{1}{\varepsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}+\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\nu(v)
and the collision Boltzmann operator
\mathcal{K}
. The idea can be summarized as follows: Using the fact that
Lf_{R}=\nu(v)f_{R}+\mathcal{K}f_{R}
where
\nu(v)\sim 1+|v|
and
\mathcal{K}f(v)
is a compact operator, we rewrite the equation in the form

\partial_{t}f_{R}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{R}+\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\nu(v)f_{R}=\mathcal{K}f+\text{forcing term}.

We then write the solution as follows

\displaystyle f_{R}(t,x,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\mathcal{K}f_{R}\left(s,x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v,v\right)ds

\displaystyle+\text{terms involving initial data and forcing terms.}

Rewriting every term involving
f
in
\mathcal{K}f_{R}(s,x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v,v)
once again, with the use of the transport equation, we have the main key term

\displaystyle f_{R}(t,x,v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}\varepsilon^{4}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}K(v,v_{\star})e^{-\frac{\nu(v_{\star})(s-\widetilde{s})}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}K(v_{\star},\widetilde{v}_{\star})

\displaystyle\times f_{R}\left(\widetilde{s},x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v-\frac{s-\widetilde{s}}{\varepsilon}v_{\star},\widetilde{v}_{\star}\right)d\widetilde{v}_{\star}d\widetilde{s}dv_{\star}ds+\text{other terms}.

When any of the velocities
|v_{\star}|
or
|\widetilde{v}_{\star}|
is large, the kernel on the first line is decaying at least polynomially in these velocities, therefore we can formally bound this term by
o(1)\|f_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and absorb it to the left hand side in the
L^{\infty}
estimate. This is the same when
|s-\widetilde{s}|=o(1)\kappa\varepsilon^{2}
. In the set where
|v_{\star}|
and
|\widetilde{v}_{\star}|
are bounded, we use Holder inequality to get

|f_{R}|\lesssim\left\{\int_{|v_{\star}|,|\widetilde{v}_{\star}|\lesssim 1}|Pf_{R}|^{p}\left(\widetilde{s},x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v-\frac{s-\widetilde{s}}{\varepsilon}v_{\star},\widetilde{v}_{\star}\right)dv_{\star}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}\right\}^{1/p}.

Then, using the change of variables
(v^{1}_{\star},v_{\star}^{2},\widetilde{v}_{\star})\to(\widetilde{x}_{1},\widetilde{x}_{2},\widetilde{v}_{\star})
where

dv_{\star}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}=\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{s-\widetilde{s}}\right)^{2}d\widetilde{x}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{(\delta_{1}\kappa\varepsilon^{2})^{2}}d\widetilde{x}d\widetilde{v}_{\star},\qquad\widetilde{x}=x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v-\frac{s-\widetilde{s}}{\varepsilon}v_{\star}.

we get the upper bound
\frac{1}{(\kappa\varepsilon)^{\frac{2}{p}}}\|Pf_{R}(\widetilde{s})\|_{L^{p}_{x}}
, which is roughly the
L^{p}
norm of
f_{R}
. Next, the precise estimates follow.
Our main proposition is as follows

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
p>1
. If
c_{\kappa}\varepsilon\ll 1
,
there holds

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{m}f_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathfrak{m}f_{0,R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+\frac{\bar{c}_{\kappa}\varepsilon^{3}}{\delta}+\bar{c}_{\kappa}\varepsilon\|\mathfrak{m}f_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}+\delta\varepsilon\|\mathfrak{m}f_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2}{p}}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1+\frac{2}{p}}}\|Pf_{R}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{p}_{x}}+\kappa^{-1/2}\sqrt{\mathcal{D}(t)}.

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}f_{R}\|_{L^{2}(0,t,L^{\infty}_{x,v})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathfrak{m}f_{0,R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+\frac{\bar{c}_{\kappa}\varepsilon^{3}}{\delta}+\bar{c}_{\kappa}\varepsilon\|\mathfrak{m}f_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}+\delta\varepsilon\|\mathfrak{m}f_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(\varepsilon\kappa)^{\frac{2}{p}}}\|Pf_{R}\|_{L^{2}(0,t,L^{p}_{x})}+\kappa^{-1/2}\sqrt{\mathcal{D}(t)}.

Proof.

From the equation (
3.22
), we have

\partial_{t}f_{R}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}v\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{R}+\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\nu(v)f=\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\mathcal{K}f+g(t,x,v)

where
g
denotes the right hand side of the equation (
3.22
). By applying the Duhamel principle, we have

\displaystyle f_{R}(t,x,v)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{\nu(v)t}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}f_{0,R}\left(x-\frac{tv}{\varepsilon},v\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\mathcal{K}f_{R}\left(s,x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v,v\right)ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}g\left(s,x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v,v\right)ds.

Using the definition of the linear operator
\mathcal{K}f_{R}
, we have, for
s\in[0,t]
,

\mathcal{K}f_{R}\left(s,x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v,v\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}K(v,v_{\star})f_{R}\left(s,x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v,v_{\star}\right)dv_{\star}.

Applying the Duhamel formula again for
f_{R}(s,x-\varepsilon^{-1}(t-s)v,v_{\star})
, we obtain

f_{R}(t,x,v)=\mathcal{M}_{0}(f_{0,R})+\mathcal{M}_{1}(g)+\mathcal{M}_{2}(f_{R})

where the linear operators
\mathcal{M}_{0},\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{3}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{0}(f_{0,R})

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{\nu(v)t}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}f_{0,R}\left(x-\frac{tv}{\varepsilon},v\right)

(3.29)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}K(v,v_{\star})e^{-\frac{\nu(v_{\star})s}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}f_{0,R}\left(x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v-\frac{s}{\varepsilon}v_{\star},v_{\star}\right)dv_{\star}ds,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}(g)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}g\left(s,x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v,v\right)ds

(3.30)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}K(v,v_{\star})e^{-\frac{\nu(v_{\star})(s-\widetilde{s})}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}g\left(\widetilde{s},x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v-\frac{s-\widetilde{s}}{\varepsilon}v_{\star},v_{\star}\right)d\widetilde{s}dv_{\star}ds,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2}(f_{R})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}\varepsilon^{4}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}K(v,v_{\star})e^{-\frac{\nu(v_{\star})(s-\widetilde{s})}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}K(v_{\star},\widetilde{v}_{\star})

\displaystyle\times f_{R}\left(\widetilde{s},x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v-\frac{s-\widetilde{s}}{\varepsilon}v_{\star},\widetilde{v}_{\star}\right)d\widetilde{v}_{\star}d\widetilde{s}dv_{\star}ds.

∎

In the next propositions, we give the bounds for the linear operators
\mathcal{M}_{0},\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

There holds

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{M}_{0}(f_{0,R})\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathfrak{m}f_{0,R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}},

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\mathcal{M}_{0}(f_{0,R})\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\kappa}\varepsilon\|\mathfrak{m}f_{0,R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}.

Proof.

For the first term on the right hand side of (
3.29
), it is straight forward that

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-\frac{\nu(v)t}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}\mathfrak{m}(v)f_{0,R}\left(x-\frac{tv}{\varepsilon},v\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\kappa}\varepsilon\|\mathfrak{m}f_{0,R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}},

\displaystyle\left\|e^{-\frac{\nu(v)t}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}\mathfrak{m}(v)f_{0,R}\left(x-\frac{tv}{\varepsilon},v\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathfrak{m}f_{0,R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}.

On the other hand, we bound the second term in (
3.29
) as follows

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(v)\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}K(v,v_{\star})e^{-\frac{\nu(v_{\star})s}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}f_{0,R}\left(x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v-\frac{s}{\varepsilon}v_{\star},v_{\star}\right)dv_{\star}ds\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathfrak{m}f_{0,R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|K(v,v_{\star})|\frac{\mathfrak{m}(v)}{\mathfrak{m}(v_{\star})}dv_{\star},

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathfrak{m}f_{0,R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\nu(v)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}ds.

Here we used the inequality (
4.4
). The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{1}
is the linear operator defined in (
3.30
). There holds

\begin{cases}&\|\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{M}_{1}(g)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}\lesssim\kappa\varepsilon^{2}\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}},\\
&\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\mathcal{M}_{1}(g)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\lesssim\kappa\varepsilon^{2}\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}g\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x,v}}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(v)\mathcal{M}_{1}(g)

\displaystyle=\nu(v)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}(v)g\left(s,x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v,v\right)ds

\displaystyle+\nu(v)\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}K(v,v_{\star})e^{-\frac{\nu(v_{\star})(s-\widetilde{s})}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}(v)g\left(\widetilde{s},x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v-\frac{s-\widetilde{s}}{\varepsilon}v_{\star},v_{\star}\right)d\widetilde{s}dv_{\star}ds.

This implies

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(v)\mathcal{M}_{1}(g)

\displaystyle\lesssim\nu(v)\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}g(s)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}ds

\displaystyle+\nu(v)\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}e^{-\frac{\nu_{0}(s-\widetilde{s})}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}g(\widetilde{s})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}d\widetilde{s}ds\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|K(v,v_{\star})|\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}(v)}{\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}(v_{\star})}dv_{\star}.

Again using Lemma
4.4
and the convolution in time estimate, we get

\begin{cases}&\|\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{M}_{1}(g)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}\lesssim\kappa\varepsilon^{2}\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}},\\
&\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\mathcal{M}_{1}(g)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\lesssim\kappa\varepsilon^{2}\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}g\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x,v}}.\end{cases}

The proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
g
be the forcing term defined in (
3.24
). There holds

\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}\lesssim\frac{\bar{c}_{\kappa}\varepsilon}{\delta}+\frac{\bar{c}_{\kappa}}{\varepsilon}\|\mathfrak{m}f_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}+\frac{\delta}{\kappa\varepsilon}\|\mathfrak{m}f_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}^{2}.

Proof.

We will treat the terms

\frac{2\varepsilon}{\kappa\delta}\Gamma(f_{2},f_{2}),\quad\frac{2}{\kappa\varepsilon}\Gamma(f_{1},f_{R}),\quad-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\delta}\partial_{t}f_{3}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\delta}{\kappa\varepsilon}\Gamma(f_{R},f_{R})

only, since these are the most singular terms and the others can be treated similarly. Using lemma
4.10
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{2\varepsilon}{\kappa\delta}\|\Gamma(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+\frac{2}{\kappa\varepsilon}\|\Gamma(f_{1},f_{R})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+\frac{\delta}{\kappa\varepsilon}\|\Gamma(f_{R},f_{R})\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon}{\kappa\delta}\bar{c}_{\kappa}^{2}+\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon}c_{\kappa}\|\mathfrak{m}f_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+\frac{\delta}{\kappa\varepsilon}\|\mathfrak{m}f_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}^{2}.

On the other hand, it is clear that

\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\delta}\|\partial_{t}f_{3}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\delta}\bar{c}_{\kappa}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Combing Lemma
3.7
and Proposition
3.8
, we have the following corollary:

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
g
be defined in (
3.24
). There holds

\|\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{M}_{1}(g)\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}+\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\mathcal{M}_{1}(g)\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\lesssim\bar{c}_{\kappa}\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{\delta}+\bar{c}_{\kappa}\varepsilon\|\mathfrak{m}f_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}+\delta\varepsilon\|\mathfrak{m}f_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}^{2}.\\

Next, we bound the operator
\mathcal{M}_{2}(f_{R})
defined in (
3.30
). We have

Proposition 3.10
.

There holds

\|\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{M}_{2}(f_{R})\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}\lesssim o(1)\cdot\|\mathfrak{m}f_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}+\frac{1}{\kappa^{\frac{2}{p}+\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1+\frac{2}{p}}}\|Pf_{R}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{p}_{x}}+\kappa^{-1/2}\sqrt{\mathcal{D}(t)}

and

\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\mathcal{M}_{2}(f_{R})\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\lesssim o(1)\cdot\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}f_{R}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x,v}}+\frac{1}{(\kappa\varepsilon)^{\frac{2}{p}}}\|Pf_{R}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{p}_{x}}+\kappa^{-1/2}\sqrt{\mathcal{D}(t)},

where
\mathcal{D}(t)
is given in (
3.26
).

Proof.

First, we see that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(v)\mathcal{M}_{2}(f_{R})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}\varepsilon^{4}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}e^{-\frac{\nu(v_{\star})(s-\widetilde{s})}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}K(v,v_{\star})K(v_{\star},\widetilde{v}_{\star})\frac{\mathfrak{m}(v)}{\mathfrak{m}(\widetilde{v}_{\star})}

(3.31)

\displaystyle\times\mathfrak{m}(\widetilde{v}_{\star})f_{R}\left(\widetilde{s},x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v-\frac{s-\widetilde{s}}{\varepsilon}v_{\star},\widetilde{v}_{\star}\right)d\widetilde{v}_{\star}d\widetilde{s}dv_{\star}ds.

Take
\delta_{1}>0
to be small and
\bar{N}>0
to be a large number. We decompose the integral

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}_{\widetilde{v}_{\star}}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v_{\star}}}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}dv_{\star}d\widetilde{s}ds

as follows

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}_{\widetilde{v}_{\star}}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v_{\star}}}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}dv_{\star}d\widetilde{s}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|s-\widetilde{s}|\leq\delta_{1}\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}_{\widetilde{v}_{\star}}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{v_{\star}}}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}dv_{\star}d\widetilde{s}ds

(3.32)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|s-\widetilde{s}|\geq\delta_{1}\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\max\{|v|,|v-v_{\star}|,|v-\widetilde{v}_{\star}|\}\geq\bar{N}}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}dv_{\star}d\widetilde{s}ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|s-\widetilde{s}|\geq\delta_{1}\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\max\{|v|,|v-v_{\star}|,|v-\widetilde{v}_{\star}|\}\leq\bar{N}}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}dv_{\star}d\widetilde{s}ds.

We have

\mathfrak{m}(v)\mathcal{M}_{2}(f_{R})(t,x,v)=\mathfrak{m}f_{R,1}+\mathfrak{m}f_{R,2}+\mathfrak{m}f_{R,3},

where
f_{R,i}
is respectively the integration in (
3.31
) integrated over the splitting of the regions defined in (
3.32
).
First, using the fact that

\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}ds\lesssim 1,\quad\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{|s-\widetilde{s}|\leq\delta_{1}\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}1ds\lesssim\delta_{1},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(v)}{\mathfrak{m}(\widetilde{v}_{\star})}\left|K(v,v_{\star})K(v_{\star},\widetilde{v}_{\star})\right|d\widetilde{v}_{\star}dv_{\star}\lesssim 1.

we get

\mathfrak{m}(v)f_{R,1}\lesssim\delta_{1}\|\mathfrak{m}f_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}.

Next we bound
\mathfrak{m}f_{R,2}
. Indeed, we will show that

\mathfrak{m}f_{R,2}\lesssim\frac{1}{\bar{N}+1}\|\mathfrak{m}f_{R}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x,v}}.

To this end, it suffices to show that

\int_{\max\{|v|,|v-v_{\star}|,|v_{\star}-\widetilde{v}_{\star}|\}\geq\bar{N}}\left|K(v,v_{\star})K(v_{\star},\widetilde{v}_{\star})\right|\frac{\mathfrak{m}(v)}{\mathfrak{m}(\widetilde{v}_{\star})}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}dv_{\star}\lesssim\frac{1}{1+\bar{N}}.

If

|v|\geq\bar{N}
, from (
4.4
) we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|K(v,v_{\star})K(v_{\star},\widetilde{v}_{\star})\right|\frac{\mathfrak{m}(v)}{\mathfrak{m}(\widetilde{v}_{\star})}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}dv_{\star}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|K(v,v_{\star})\right|\frac{\mathfrak{m}(v)}{\mathfrak{m}(v_{\star})}dv_{\star}\lesssim\frac{1}{1+|v|}\lesssim\frac{1}{1+\bar{N}}.

Now if
\max\{|v-v_{\star}|,|v_{\star}-\widetilde{v}_{\star}|\}\geq\bar{N}
, we use the Grad estimate (
3.2
) to get, for all
\vartheta\in(0,\frac{1}{8})
:

\displaystyle\int_{\max\{|v-v_{\star}|,|v_{\star}-\widetilde{v}_{\star}|\}\geq\bar{N}}\left|K(v,v_{\star})K(v_{\star},\widetilde{v}_{\star})\right|\frac{\mathfrak{m}(v)}{\mathfrak{m}(\widetilde{v}_{\star})}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}dv_{\star}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\max\{|v-v_{\star}|,|v_{\star}-\widetilde{v}_{\star}|\}\geq\bar{N}}\left|K_{\vartheta}(v,v_{\star})K_{\vartheta}(v_{\star},\widetilde{v}_{\star})\right|\frac{\mathfrak{m}(v)}{\mathfrak{m}(\widetilde{v}_{\star})}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}dv_{\star}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\frac{\vartheta}{2}\bar{N}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left|K_{\vartheta/2}(v,v_{\star})K_{\vartheta/2}(v_{\star},\widetilde{v}_{\star})\right|\frac{\mathfrak{m}(v)}{\mathfrak{m}(\widetilde{v}_{\star})}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}dv_{\star}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\frac{\vartheta}{2}\bar{N}^{2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{1+\bar{N}}.

Finally, we bound
\mathfrak{m}f_{R,3}
. Since
\max\{|v|,|v-v_{\star}|,|v-\widetilde{v}_{\star}|\}\leq\bar{N}
, we have

|v_{\star}|\leq 2\bar{N}
and
|\widetilde{v}_{\star}|\leq 3\bar{N}
. Using the decomposition
f_{R}=Pf_{R}+(I-P)f_{R}
, we bound the integral
dv_{\star}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}
in
\mathfrak{m}f_{R,3}
as follows

\displaystyle\int_{|v_{\star}|\leq 2\bar{N},|\widetilde{v}_{\star}|\leq 3\bar{N}}\frac{\mathfrak{m}(v)}{\mathfrak{m}(\widetilde{v}_{\star})}K(v,v_{\star})K(v_{\star},\widetilde{v}_{\star})\mathfrak{m}(\widetilde{v}_{\star})f_{R}\left(\widetilde{s},x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v-\frac{s-\widetilde{s}}{\varepsilon}v_{\star},\widetilde{v}_{\star}\right)dv_{\star}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\int_{|v_{\star}|\leq 2\bar{N},|\widetilde{v}_{\star}|\leq 3\bar{N}}|Pf_{R}|^{p}\left(\widetilde{s},x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v-\frac{s-\widetilde{s}}{\varepsilon}v_{\star},\widetilde{v}_{\star}\right)dv_{\star}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}\right\}^{1/p}

\displaystyle+\left\{\int_{|v_{\star}|\leq 2\bar{N},|\widetilde{v}_{\star}|\leq 3\bar{N}}|(I-P)f_{R}|^{2}\left(\widetilde{s},x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v-\frac{s-\widetilde{s}}{\varepsilon}v_{\star},\widetilde{v}_{\star}\right)dv_{\star}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(\kappa\varepsilon)^{\frac{2}{p}}}\|Pf_{R}(\widetilde{s})\|_{L^{p}_{x}}+\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon}\|(I-P)f_{R}(\widetilde{s})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

Here, we have use the fact that

dv_{\star}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}=\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{s-\widetilde{s}}\right)^{2}d\widetilde{x}d\widetilde{v}_{\star}\lesssim\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{(\delta_{1}\kappa\varepsilon^{2})^{2}}d\widetilde{x}d\widetilde{v}_{\star},\qquad\widetilde{x}=x-\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}v-\frac{s-\widetilde{s}}{\varepsilon}v_{\star}.

This implies

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}f_{R,3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}\varepsilon^{4}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}\left\{\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\frac{\nu(v_{\star})(s-\widetilde{s})}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}\frac{1}{(\kappa\varepsilon)^{\frac{2}{p}}}\|Pf_{R}(\widetilde{s})\|_{L^{p}_{x}}d\widetilde{s}\right\}ds

(3.33)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}\varepsilon^{4}}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}\left\{e^{-\frac{\nu(v_{\star})(s-\widetilde{s})}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon}\|(I-P)f_{R}(\widetilde{s})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}d\widetilde{s}\right\}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\kappa^{\frac{2}{p}+\frac{1}{2}}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1+\frac{2}{p}}}\|Pf_{R}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{p}_{x}}+\kappa^{-1/2}\sqrt{\mathcal{D}(t)}.

where we used the Holder inequality in time.
As for
\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\mathcal{M}_{2}(f_{R})\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x,v}}
, the proof is exactly the same for the first two integrations in (
3.32
), up until the estimate (
3.33
). Instead of estimating the integral in time
t
pointwise, we use the convolution estimate in time, to get

\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}\varepsilon^{4}}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}\left\{\int_{0}^{s}e^{-\frac{\nu(v_{\star})(s-\widetilde{s})}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}\frac{1}{(\kappa\varepsilon)^{\frac{2}{p}}}\|Pf_{R}(\widetilde{s})\|_{L^{p}_{x}}d\widetilde{s}\right\}ds\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}}\lesssim\frac{1}{(\kappa\varepsilon)^{\frac{2}{p}}}\|Pf_{R}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{p}_{x}}

and

\frac{1}{\kappa^{2}\varepsilon^{4}}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{\nu(v)(t-s)}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}\left\{e^{-\frac{\nu(v_{\star})(s-\widetilde{s})}{\kappa\varepsilon^{2}}}\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon}\|(I-P)f_{R}(\widetilde{s})\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}d\widetilde{s}\right\}ds\right\|_{L^{2}_{t}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\kappa\varepsilon}\|(I-P)f_{R}\|_{L^{2}_{t,x,v}}.

The proof is complete.

∎

3.5
Nonlinear iterations for the remainder

In this section, we close the estimate for the energy

\mathcal{E}(t)
. Let
p>4
and choose
\beta>0
such that

\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2}{p}<\beta<2

We define

\delta=\varepsilon^{\beta}.

(3.34)

Proposition 3.11
.

If

\delta\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}f_{R}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\ll 1

(3.35)

then

\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\mathcal{E}(s)+\mathcal{D}(t)\leq\left\{C_{0}+\mathcal{E}(0)\right\}\bar{c}_{\kappa}e^{C_{0}\bar{c}_{\kappa}t}

(3.36)

for some
C_{0}>0
.

Proof.

From Proposition
3.4
, we have

\mathcal{E}(t)+\mathcal{D}(t)\lesssim\frac{\delta^{2}}{\kappa}\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}f_{R}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\cdot\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\mathcal{E}(s)+\bar{c}_{\kappa}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{E}(s)ds+\bar{c}_{\kappa}\frac{\varepsilon^{4}}{\delta^{2}}+\mathcal{E}(0).

This implies

\left(1-C_{0}\frac{\delta^{2}}{\kappa}\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}f_{R}\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x,v}}^{2}\right)\cdot\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\mathcal{E}(s)\lesssim\bar{c}_{\kappa}\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{E}(s)ds+\bar{c}_{\kappa}\frac{\varepsilon^{4}}{\delta^{2}}+\mathcal{E}(0).

Hence as long as the inequality (
3.35
) holds, we have

\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\mathcal{E}(s)\lesssim\left\{\bar{c}_{k}\frac{\varepsilon^{4}}{\delta^{2}}+\mathcal{E}(0)\right\}\left(1+\bar{c}_{\kappa}e^{C_{0}\bar{c}_{\kappa}t}\right)

and for some constant
C_{0}>0
. Since

Lemma 4.7
.

Let

be the velocity vector field obtained from the vorticity

q=v-v_{\star}
.
We have

\displaystyle-|v-v_{\star}|^{2}-\frac{(|v|^{2}-|v_{\star}|^{2})^{2}}{|v-v_{\star}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=-2|q|^{2}+4v\cdot q-4\frac{(v\cdot q)^{2}}{|q|^{2}}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\leq-2|q|^{2}+4\left(\varepsilon_{1}|q|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon_{1}}\frac{|v\cdot q|^{2}}{|q|^{2}}\right)-4\frac{|v\cdot q|^{2}}{|q|^{2}}

\displaystyle=-2(1-2\varepsilon_{1})|q|^{2}-4(1-(4\varepsilon_{1})^{-1})\frac{|v\cdot q|^{2}}{|q|^{2}}

for any
\varepsilon_{1}>0
. On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{m}(v)}{\mathfrak{m}(v_{\star})}

\displaystyle=e^{\rho_{0}(|v|^{2}-|v_{\star}|^{2})}=e^{\rho_{0}(|v|^{2}-|v-q|^{2})}=e^{-\rho_{0}|q|^{2}+2\rho_{0}(v\cdot q)},

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\rho_{0}|q|^{2}+2\rho_{0}\varepsilon_{2}|q|^{2}+2\rho_{0}\frac{1}{4\varepsilon_{2}}\frac{|v\cdot q|^{2}}{|q|^{2}}}=e^{-\rho_{0}(1-2\varepsilon_{2})|q|^{2}+\frac{\rho_{0}}{2\varepsilon_{2}}\frac{|v\cdot q|^{2}}{|q|^{2}}}

for any
\varepsilon_{2}>0
.
Combining (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we have

\displaystyle K_{\vartheta}(v,v_{\star})\frac{\mathfrak{m}(v)}{\mathfrak{m}(v_{\star})}(1+|v-v_{\star}|^{N_{0}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|q|}e^{-(2\vartheta(1-2\varepsilon_{1})+\rho_{0}(1-2\varepsilon_{2}))|q|^{2}}e^{-\left(4(\vartheta(1-(4\varepsilon_{1})^{-1})-\frac{\rho_{0}}{4\varepsilon_{2}}\right)\frac{|v\cdot q|^{2}}{|q|^{2}}}(1+|q|^{N_{0}})

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|q|}e^{-c_{0}|q|^{2}-c_{0}|v\cdot q|^{2}}

for some
c_{\theta}>0
.
Hence

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}K_{\vartheta}(v,v_{\star})\frac{\mathfrak{m}(v)}{\mathfrak{m}(v_{\star})}(1+|v-v_{\star}|^{N_{0}})dv_{\star}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{|q|}e^{-c_{\theta}|q|^{2}-c_{\theta}|v\cdot q|^{2}}dq

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}re^{-c_{0}r^{2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{-c_{0}|v|r\cos\theta}\sin\theta d\theta dr\lesssim\frac{1}{1+|v|}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

For any
f,g,h\in L^{2}_{v}
, there hold

\langle\Gamma(f,g),h\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}\lesssim\left\{\|\mathfrak{m}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|\sqrt{\nu}g\|_{L^{2}_{v}}+\|\mathfrak{m}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|\sqrt{\nu}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\right\}\|\sqrt{\nu}(I-P)h\|_{L^{2}_{v}}.

(4.7)

\langle\nabla_{x}\Gamma(f,g),h\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\lesssim\left\{\|\mathfrak{m}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\|\sqrt{\nu}\nabla_{x}g\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\|\mathfrak{m}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\|\sqrt{\nu}\nabla_{x}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\right\}\|\sqrt{\nu}(I-P)h\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

(4.8)

and

\displaystyle\left\|\Gamma(f,g)\sqrt{\varphi}\right\|_{L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|g\|_{L^{2}_{v}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{v}},

(4.9)

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla_{x}\Gamma(f,g)\sqrt{\varphi}\right\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\|\nabla_{x}g\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\|\nabla_{x}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

where
\varphi\in\mathfrak{B}=\{\sqrt{\mu},v\sqrt{\mu},\frac{|v|^{2}-3}{2}\sqrt{\mu}\}
.

Proof.

We give the proof for (
4.8
) only, since (
4.7
) is similar. First we recall that
\Gamma(f,g)=\Gamma_{\text{gain}}(f,g)-\Gamma_{\text{loss}}(f,g)
where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{gain}}(f,g)(v)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\text{gain,1}}(f,g)+\Gamma_{\text{gain,2}}(f,g),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{loss}}(f,g)(v)

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{\text{loss,1}}(f,g)+\Gamma_{\text{loss,2}}(f,g),

and

\begin{cases}\Gamma_{\text{gain,1}}(f,g)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-v_{\star})\cdot\widetilde{s}|\sqrt{\mu(v_{\star})}f(v^{\prime})g(v_{\star}^{\prime})d\widetilde{s}dv_{\star},\\
\Gamma_{\text{gain,2}}(f,g)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-v_{\star})\cdot\widetilde{s}|\sqrt{\mu(v_{\star})}g(v^{\prime})f(v_{\star}^{\prime})d\widetilde{s}dv_{\star},\\
\Gamma_{\text{loss,1}}(f,g)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-v_{\star})\cdot\widetilde{s}|\sqrt{\mu(v_{\star})}f(v_{\star})g(v)d\widetilde{s}dv_{\star},\\
\Gamma_{\text{loss,2}}(f,g)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-v_{\star})\cdot\widetilde{s}|\sqrt{\mu(v_{\star})}g(v_{\star})f(v)d\widetilde{s}dv_{\star}.\\
\end{cases}

(4.10)

We shall show the bound for
\Gamma_{\text{gain,1}}(f,g)
only, since
\Gamma_{\text{gain,2}}
is done similarly. We have

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{x}\Gamma_{\text{gain,1}}(f,g),h\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}|(v-v_{\star})\cdot\widetilde{s}|\sqrt{\mu(v_{\star})}\nabla_{x}\left(f(v^{\prime})g(v_{\star}^{\prime})\right)d\widetilde{s}dv_{\star}\right\}(I-P)hdv

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\{\nu(v)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{x}(f(v^{\prime})g(v_{\star}^{\prime}))|^{2}dv_{\star}\right)^{1/2}\right\}|(I-P)h|dv

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nu(v)\left|\nabla_{x}(f(v^{\prime})g(v_{\star}^{\prime}))\right|^{2}dv_{\star}dv\right)^{1/2}\|\sqrt{\nu}(I-P)h\|_{L^{2}_{v}}.

For the first factor above, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nu(v)\left|\nabla_{x}(f(v^{\prime})g(v_{\star}^{\prime}))\right|^{2}dv_{\star}dvdx

(4.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nu(v)\left\{\|f(v^{\prime},\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\nabla_{x}g(v_{\star}^{\prime},\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|g(v^{\prime}_{\star},\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\nabla_{x}f(v^{\prime},\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\right\}dv_{\star}dv

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\{\nu(v^{\prime})+\nu(v_{\star}^{\prime})\right\}\left\{\|f(v^{\prime},\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\nabla_{x}g(v_{\star}^{\prime},\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|g(v^{\prime}_{\star},\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\nabla_{x}f(v^{\prime},\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\right\}dv^{\prime}_{\star}dv^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathfrak{m}(v)f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}^{2}\left\|\sqrt{\nu}\nabla_{x}g\right\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\|\mathfrak{m}(v)g\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}^{2}\|\sqrt{\nu}\nabla_{x}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.

Hence we get

\langle\nabla_{x}\Gamma_{\text{gain,1}}(f,g),h\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\lesssim\left\{\|\mathfrak{m}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\|\sqrt{\nu}g\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\|\mathfrak{m}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\|\sqrt{\nu}\nabla_{x}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\right\}\|(I-P)h\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

Next we get

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{x}\Gamma_{\text{loss,1}}(f,g),h\rangle_{L^{2}_{v}}

(4.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nu(v)\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla_{x}(f(v_{\star})g(v))|^{2}dv_{\star}\right\}^{1/2}|(I-P)h(v)|dv

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nu(v)|\nabla_{x}f(v_{\star})g(v)|^{2}dv_{\star}dv\right\}^{1/2}\|\sqrt{\nu}(I-P)h\|_{L^{2}_{v}}.

Integrating in
x
, we get

\displaystyle\langle\nabla_{x}\Gamma_{\text{loss,1}}(f,g),h\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nu(v)|\nabla_{x}(f(v_{\star})g(v))|^{2}dv_{\star}dvdx\right\}^{1/2}\|\sqrt{\nu}(I-P)h\|_{L^{2}_{v}}.

For the first factor, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nu(v)\left|\nabla_{x}(f(v_{\star})g(v))\right|^{2}dv_{\star}dvdx

(4.13)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nu(v)\left\{\|g(v,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\nabla_{x}f(v_{\star},\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}+\|f(v_{\star},\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\nabla_{x}g(v,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\right\}dv_{\star}dv

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathfrak{m}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}^{2}\left\|\sqrt{\nu}\nabla_{x}f\right\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}+\|\mathfrak{m}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}^{2}\|\sqrt{\nu}\nabla_{x}g\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}^{2}.

Hence we obtain

\langle\nabla_{x}\Gamma_{\text{loss,1}}(f,g),h\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\lesssim\left\{\|\mathfrak{m}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\|\sqrt{\nu}\nabla_{x}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\|\mathfrak{m}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\|\sqrt{\nu}\nabla_{x}g\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\right\}\|(I-P)h\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

This finishes the proof for the inequality (
4.8
).
Next we show (
4.9
). Take
h\in L^{2}_{x,v}
so that
\|h\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\leq 1
. We need to show that

\left\langle\nabla_{x}\Gamma(f,g)\sqrt{\varphi},h\right\rangle_{L^{2}_{x,v}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\|\nabla_{x}g\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}+\|g\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,v}}\|\nabla_{x}f\|_{L^{2}_{x,v}}.

This is obvious from the proof of (
4.8
) presented above, along with the fact that
\nu(v)\varphi(v)
is decaying fast when
|v|\to\infty
. We skip the details. The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

Recall that
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}(v)=\frac{\mathfrak{m}(v)}{\nu(v)}
.
There holds

\|\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\Gamma(f,g)\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\lesssim\|\mathfrak{m}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|\mathfrak{m}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}.

Proof.

It is straightforward that

\mathfrak{m}(v)\Gamma_{\text{loss}}(f,g)(v)\lesssim\nu(v)\mathfrak{m}(v)f(v)\|\mathfrak{m}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}+\|\mathfrak{m}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|\mathfrak{m}g\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}.

As for
\Gamma_{\text{gain,1}}
defined in (
4.10
), we have
\mathfrak{m}(v)\lesssim\mathfrak{m}(v^{\prime})\mathfrak{m}(v_{\star}^{\prime})
. This implies

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}(v)\Gamma_{\text{gain},1}(f,g)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|v-v_{\star}|\sqrt{\mu(v_{\star})}\left|\mathfrak{m}f(v^{\prime})\right|\cdot|\mathfrak{m}g(v_{\star}^{\prime})|dv_{\star}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathfrak{m}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|\mathfrak{m}g\|_{L^{\infty}}\nu(v).

Similarly, we get
\mathfrak{m}(v)\Gamma_{\text{gain,2}}(f,g)(v)\lesssim\nu(v)\|\mathfrak{m}f\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}}\|\mathfrak{m}g\|_{L^{\infty}}
. The proof is complete.
∎

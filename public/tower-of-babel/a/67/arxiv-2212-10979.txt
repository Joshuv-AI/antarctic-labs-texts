On Gosper-Karaji algebraic Identities
Hossein Teimoori Faal

Abstract
In this paper, we first quickly review the basics of an algebro-geometric method of Karaji’s L-summing technique in today’s modern language of algebra. Then, we also review the theory of Gosper’s algorithm as a decision procedure for obtaining the indefinite sums involving hypergeometric terms. Then, we show that how one can use Gosper’s algorithm equipped with the L-summing method to obtain a class of combinatorial identities associated with a given algebraic identity.

Gosper Decision Algorithm

We call a sequence of real numbers
\{t_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}

hypergeometric
in variable
n
, if the quotient

\frac{t_{n+1}}{t_{n}}

is a
rational
function of
n
.
More precisely, if there exists two real
polynomials
p(n)
and
q(n)
such that

\frac{t_{n+1}}{t_{n}}=\frac{p(n)}{q(n)}.

(5)

Example 2.1
.

Let
t_{n}=n!
for
n\geq 1
. Then, clearly we have

\frac{t_{n+1}}{t_{n}}=n+1

.
That is,
n!
is a hypergeometric sequence in
n
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
t_{n}={n\choose k}
, for
1\leq k\leq n
.
Then, we have

\frac{t_{n+1}}{t_{n}}=\frac{{n+1\choose k}}{{n\choose k}}=\frac{\frac{(n+1)n!}{k!(n+1-k)(n-k)!}}{\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}}=\frac{n+1}{n+1-k}

Hence,
{n\choose k}
is a hypergometric sequence in varaiable
n
.
One can also simialrly prove that
{n\choose k}
is a hypergeometric sequence in

k
.

Remark 2.3
.

It is worth to note that the sequence
a_{n,k}=n^{k}

,
for natural numebrs
n
and
k
, is a hypergeometric sequence in
k
. But, it is not hypergeometric in

n

.

In
[
4
]
, Gosper gives a
decision algorithm
for obtaining the
indefinite sum

\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}
.
More precisely, this algorithm says that
if
f_{k}
is a
hypergeometric
sequence,
then one can find another
hypergeometric
sequence
F_{n}
such that

f_{k}=F_{k+1}-F_{k}
, and therefore after applying the
creative telescoping

[
5
]
,
we find the answer, that is

\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\{\left(F_{k}-F_{0}\right)-\left(F_{k-1}-F_{0}\right)\right\}=F_{n}.

The basic idea behind the
Gosper’s alogorithm
is the following.
We already know that

f_{k}=F_{k+1}-F_{k}

.
Now, let us assume that

\frac{F_{k+1}}{F_{k}}

is a
rational
function ( since
F_{k}
is hypergoemtric).

Hence, we can rewrite the quotient

\frac{f_{k+1}}{f_{k}}
,
as follows

\displaystyle\frac{f_{k+1}}{f_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{F_{k+1}-F_{k}}{F_{k}-F_{k-1}},

(6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{F_{k+1}}{F_{k}}-1}{1-\frac{F_{k-1}}{F_{k}}}.

That is, the quotient

\frac{f_{k+1}}{f_{k}}

is a rational function and therefore
f_{k}
is also hypergeometric.
Now, based on the next lemma, the quotient can always be written in a special form
which is the
key
for implementing the
Gosper
’s algorithm.

Lemma 2.4

(See
[
4
]
)
.

A rational function

\frac{a(n)}{b(n)}

can be always written as

\frac{a(n)}{b(n)}=\frac{p(n)q(n)}{p(n-1)r(n)},

(7)

where
p
,
q
and
r
are
polynomials in
n
and

\gcd(q(n),r(n+j))=1,~~~\forall j\geq 0.

Thus, we can prove the following theorem based on Lemma
2.4
which guarantees
the correctness of the Gosper algorithm.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let

\frac{t(n)}{t(n-1)}

be a rational function as above.
Then

f(n)=t(n)\frac{p(n)}{q(n+1)t(n)},

is a polynomial for which

p(n)=q(n+1)f(n)-r(n)f(n-1)

.

3
Gosper-Karaji Algebraic Identites

Consider an indefinite sum
\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}
. As we mentioned in the previous section,
for evaluating this sum, there is a beautiful decision algorithm, Gosper’s algorithm

[
4
]
, which says that if
f_{k}
is a hypergeometric sequence,
then one can find another hypergeometric sequence
F_{n}
such that

f_{k}=F_{k+1}-F_{k}
, and therefore after applying creative
telescoping, we find the answer, that is

\sum_{k=1}^{n}f_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\{\left(F_{k}-F_{0}\right)-\left(F_{k-1}-F_{0}\right)\right\}=F_{n}

Note that we will implicitly assume that

F_{0}=0

.
Otherwise, we will replace the antiderivative

F_{k}

,
with the corrected one; that is,

F_{k}-F_{0}

.

Now we show that for any such indefinite sum, we can obtain a series of identities associated with that identity, using the Karaji’s
L-summing method. From now on, we will call them

Gosper-Karaji’s identities
.
To do this, we need to apply our general L-summing
equation to an special class of arrays which is called the

mutiplication table

[
6
]
.
Hence, we put

a_{ij}=t_{i}\times t_{j}
, in formula (
1
).

After some simple computations, we obtain the following new rearrangement formula

\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}R_{k}=s^{2}_{n},

where
s_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}
and

R_{k}=2s_{k}-t_{k}
.
Also by the convention, we will assume that
s_{0}=0
.

Now, let’s take a closer look at our
formula. We observe that

t_{k}R_{k}=(s_{k}-s_{k-1})(s_{k}+s_{k-1})=s^{2}_{k}-s^{2}_{k-1}
.
In fact, we obtain that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}s^{2}_{k}-s^{2}_{k-1}=s^{2}_{n}
.
By similar
argument, for three dimension (using the formula (
1
)), we get

t_{k}R_{k}=s^{3}_{n},\hskip 14.22636ptt_{k}R_{k}=3s^{2}_{k}-3s_{k}t_{k}+t^{2}_{k}.

Indeed, we have
t_{k}R_{k}=s^{3}_{k}-s^{3}_{k-1}
. Therefore, we obtain that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}s^{3}_{k}-s^{3}_{k-1}=s^{3}_{n}
.

We finally Note that, in general, for

m
-dimensional multiplication table, we get

t_{k}R_{k}=s^{m}_{n},\hskip 14.22636ptt_{k}R_{k}={m\choose 1}s^{m-1}_{k}-{m\choose 2}s^{m-1}_{k}t_{k}+\cdots+(-1)^{m}{m\choose m}t^{m}_{k}

In fact, we get that

\sum_{k=1}^{n}t_{k}R_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(s^{m}_{k}-s^{m}_{k-1})=s^{m}_{n}
. Now we explain our
idea through a very simple but important example.

Example 3.1
.

Consider the simplest indefinite sum
\sum_{k=1}^{n}1
. If we give
the function
f_{k}=1
to the latest version of maple package which
is equipped with Gosper algorithm, we obtain that
s_{k}=k
. Thus,
by
creative telescoping
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{n}1=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(k-(k-1)\right)=n.

Now, Karaji’s L-summing method start to do its mission.
For
m=2
,

m=3
and
m=4
, we get the following
nontrivial
associated identities, respectively

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(2k-1\right)=n^{2},

\sum_{k=1}^{n}{k\choose 2}={n+1\choose 3},

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(2k-1\right)^{3}={2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(2k-1\right)\choose 2}.

Let

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{k\choose 1}^{7}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{k\choose 1}^{5}={n+1\choose 2}^{4}.

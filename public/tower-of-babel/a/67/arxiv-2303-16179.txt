Existence and Density of typical Hodge Loci
Nazim Khelifa, David Urbanik

Abstract
. Motivated by a question of Baldi-Klingler-Ullmo, we provide a general sufficient criterion for the existence and analytic density of typical Hodge loci associated to a polarizable ℤ \mathbb{Z} -variation of Hodge structures 𝕍 \mathbb{V} . Our criterion reproves the existing results in the literature on density of Noether-Lefschetz loci. It also applies to understand Hodge loci of subvarieties of 𝒜 g \mathcal{A}_{g} . For instance, we prove that for g ⩾ 4 g\geqslant 4 , if a subvariety S S of 𝒜 g \mathcal{A}_{g} has dimension at least g g then it has an analytically dense typical Hodge locus. This applies for example to the Torelli locus of 𝒜 g \mathcal{A}_{g} . .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Context

The observation that Hodge loci of polarizable
\mathbb{Z}
-variations of Hodge structures (
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS) can be realized as intersection loci has recently been fruitfully applied to obtain refinements of the celebrated algebraicity result of Cattani-Deligne-Kaplan
[
9
,
3
]
. For instance, if
\mathbb{V}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS on a smooth, irreducible and quasi-projective variety
S
over
\mathbb{C}
, one would like to understand criteria for density, in both the Zariski and analytic topology, for families of components of the Hodge locus
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})
. With this goal in mind Klingler
[
21
]
introduced a dichotomy, later refined by Baldi-Klingler-Ullmo, between the part of the Hodge locus corresponding to typical intersections and the part corresponding to atypical ones (
[
5
, Def. 2.2, 2.4]
):

\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})=\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{typ}}\cup\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{atyp}}.

These two parts are expected to behave very differently. On the one hand, the atypical part is conjectured to be algebraic:

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
5
, Conj. 2.5]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a polarizable
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS on a smooth, irreducible and quasi-projective variety
S
over
\mathbb{C}
. The atypical Hodge locus
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{atyp}}
of
S
for
\mathbb{V}
is an algebraic suvariety of
S
.

Indeed, Conjecture
1.1
can be seen as an extension of the classical Zilber-Pink conjecture (see
[
24
, Conj. 2.3]
,
[
25
, Conj. 1.1]
and
[
30
]
). The case of positive period dimensional special subvarieties has been settled for variations with
\mathbb{Q}
-simple derived Mumford-Tate group in
[
5
, Thm. 3.1]
. The case of atypical points appears for now to be entirely out of reach in full generality. Understanding the typical Hodge locus, however, appears more accessible, and towards this end Baldi-Klingler-Ullmo conjecture the following:

Conjecture 1.2

(
[
5
, Conj. 2.7]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a polarizable
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS on a smooth, irreducible and quasi-projective variety
S
over
\mathbb{C}
. If
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{typ}}
is non-empty,
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{typ}}
is analytically (hence Zariski) dense in
S
.

Towards this conjecture they prove:

Theorem 1.3

(
[
5
, Thm. 3.9]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a polarizable
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS on a smooth, irreducible and quasi-projective variety
S
over
\mathbb{C}
. If the typical Hodge locus
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{typ}}
is non-empty, then
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})
is analytically (hence Zariski) dense in
S
.

Conjecture
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
naturally raise the following question asked by Baldi-Klingler-Ullmo:

Question 1.4

(
[
5
, Quest. 2.9]
)
.

Is there a simple criterion on the generic Hodge datum of a polarizable
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS to decide if its typical Hodge locus is non-empty?

Regarding this question, they introduce the following notion (see
[
5
, Sect. 4.6]
for details):

Definition 1.5

(
[
5
, Def. 4.15]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a polarizable
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS on a smooth, irreducible and quasi-projective variety
S
over
\mathbb{C}
with generic Hodge datum
(\mathbf{G},D)
and
\mathbb{Q}
-simple algebraic monodromy group
\mathbf{H}
. Denote by
\mathfrak{h}
its Lie algebra, fix
x\in D
a Hodge generic point and denote by

\mathfrak{h}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{h}_{x}^{-k,k}

the induced Hodge decomposition. The
level of
\mathbb{V}
is the largest integer
k
such that
\mathfrak{h}_{x}^{-k,k}\neq\{0\}
. It doesn’t depend on the choice of
x
.

They almost completely answer Question
1.4
when the level of the
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS is at least
3
, by proving:

Theorem 1.6

(
[
5
, Thm. 3.3]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a polarizable
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS on a smooth, irreducible and quasi-projective variety
S
over
\mathbb{C}
, with generic Hodge datum
(\mathbf{G},D)
and algebraic monodromy group
\mathbf{H}
. Suppose that
\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{der}}
. If
\mathbb{V}
is of level at least
3
then
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{typ}}=\emptyset
(and thus
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})=\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{atyp}}
).

1.2.
Our Results

The main contribution of this work will be to provide a sufficient criterion for the density of (typical) Hodge loci, valid in any level. As we will explain, this almost gives a complete answer to Question
1.4
.

1.2.1.
Density of typical Hodge loci

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a polarizable
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS on a smooth, irreducible and quasi-projective variety
S
over
\mathbb{C}
and let
(\mathbf{G},D)
be its generic Hodge datum. Note that after passing to a finite étale cover of the base (which doesn’t affect our results, see Remark
2.1
), we can and will assume that the image of the monodromy representation of
\mathbb{V}
is torsion-free. We therefore fix a torsion-free arithmetic lattice
\Gamma
of
\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{R})
containing the latter and denote by
\Phi:S^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash D
be the period map associated to
\mathbb{V}
. Given a strict Hodge sub-datum
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})\subsetneq(\mathbf{G},D)
we define the
Hodge locus of type
\mathbf{M}
as

\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes},\mathbf{M}):=\{s\in S^{\mathrm{an}}\hskip 2.84544pt:\hskip 2.84544pt\exists g\in\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q})^{+},\mathbf{MT}(\mathbb{V}_{s})\subseteq g\mathbf{M}g^{-1}\},

where for
s\in S^{\mathrm{an}}
, the Mumford-Tate group of the Hodge structure on
\mathbb{V}_{s}
is denoted by
\mathbf{MT}(\mathbb{V}_{s})
. The
typical Hodge locus of type
\mathbf{M}
is defined as the union
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes},\mathbf{M})_{\mathrm{typ}}
of those components of
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes},\mathbf{M})
which are typical with respect to their generic Hodge datum; see Definition
2.7
.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
X,Y
be locally closed irreducible analytic subvarieties of some irreducible complex analytic variety
Z
. Let
U
be an analytic irreducible component of the intersection
X\cap Y
. We say that
X
and
Y
intersect in
Z
with bigger dimension than expected along
U
if

\dim U>\dim X+\dim Y-\dim Z.

Otherwise, we say that they
intersect in
Z
with expected dimension along
U
.

In this terminology, a special subvariety
Z
of
S
with generic Hodge datum
(\mathbf{G}_{Z},D_{G_{Z}})
is typical if it corresponds to a component of the intersection between
\Gamma_{\mathbf{G}_{Z}}\backslash D_{G_{Z}}
and
\Phi(S^{\mathrm{an}})
along which these two intersect with expected dimension inside
\Gamma\backslash D
. Here
\Gamma_{\mathbf{G}_{Z}}
denotes the arithmetic torsion-free lattice
\Gamma\cap\mathbf{G}_{Z}(\mathbb{Q})^{+}
of
\mathbf{G}_{Z}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
. Then, a first trivial remark is that for
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes},\mathbf{M})_{\mathrm{typ}}
to be non-empty, the Hodge datum
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})
has to be of the following type:

Definition 1.8
.

A strict Hodge sub-datum
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})\subsetneq(\mathbf{G},D)
is said
\mathbb{V}
-admissible
if it satisfies the inequality of dimensions

\dim\Phi(S^{\mathrm{an}})+\dim D_{M}-\dim D\geq 0.

We say that
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})
is
strongly
\mathbb{V}
-admissible
if the above inequality is strict.

Our general result (see Theorem
3.5
) is somewhat technical, but simplifies in the case where the algebraic monodromy group
\mathbf{H}
of
\mathbb{V}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-simple and equal to
\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{der}}
, saying that (strong)
\mathbb{V}
-admissibility of a sub-datum is enough to get density of the associated (typical) Hodge locus:

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a polarizable
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS on a smooth, irreducible and quasi-projective variety
S
over
\mathbb{C}
with generic Hodge datum
(\mathbf{G},D)
. Assume that its algebraic monodromy group is
\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{der}}
and is
\mathbb{Q}
-simple. Let
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})\subsetneq(\mathbf{G},D)
be a strict Hodge sub-datum.

(i)

If
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})
is
\mathbb{V}
-admissible, then
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes},\mathbf{M})
is analytically dense in
S^{\mathrm{an}}
.

(ii)

If
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})
is strongly
\mathbb{V}
-admissible, then
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes},\mathbf{M})_{\mathrm{typ}}
is analytically dense in
S^{\mathrm{an}}
.

Remark 1.10
.

As pointed out in
[
5
, Rmk. 10.2]
, Baldi-Klingler-Ullmo’s geometric Zilber-Pink theorem gives (because we are in the
\mathbb{Q}
-simple monodromy case) that (i) implies (ii). We give an independent argument for (ii) that still works in the factor case.

In terms of the full Hodge locus we thus have immediately:

Corollary 1.11
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be as in Theorem
1.9
. If
(\mathbf{G},D)
admits a (resp. strongly)
\mathbb{V}
-admissible strict Hodge sub-datum, the Hodge locus
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})
(resp. the typical Hodge locus
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{typ}}
) of
S
for
\mathbb{V}
is analytically dense in
S^{\mathrm{an}}
.

Remark 1.12
.

The condition that
\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{der}}
will be verified in most geometric applications (e.g. hypersurfaces variations, Hodge-generic subvarieties of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
). It is however needed to exclude the eventuality that the datum
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})
is
\mathbb{V}
-admissible but not big enough on the factor of
D
on which
\Phi
is constant to generically intersect the period image. The most general condition that one can hope for (see Theorem
3.5
) is that, writing
\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{der}}=\mathbf{H}\cdot\mathbf{L}
as an almost direct product,
\mathbf{M}
contains
\mathbf{L}
. Otherwise, the statement would contradict the Zilber-Pink conjecture for an auxiliary
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS (see Example
1.13
).

Working with not necessarily
\mathbb{Q}
-simple algebraic monodromy groups leads to complications. The following example illustrates the fact that in this general setting we cannot hope for Theorem
1.9
to remain true and that we need to impose similar numerical conditions on each factor.

Example 1.13
.

In this example, we fix a level structure
n\geqslant 3
and for any
g\geqslant 1
we denote by
\mathcal{A}_{g}
the quasi-projective Shimura variety of dimension
\frac{g(g+1)}{2}
parametrising principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension
g
with level-
n
-structure. Let
C\subset\mathcal{A}_{3}
be a Hodge generic curve. Let
\mathbb{V}
be the
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS corresponding to the first cohomology groups of the members of the universal family of abelian six-folds above
S:=\mathcal{A}_{3}\times C
(recall that we fixed level structures so that we have fine moduli spaces). Its period map is the inclusion
\mathcal{A}_{3}\times C\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{3}\times\mathcal{A}_{3}
. Let
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})
be a strict Hodge sub-datum with
\Gamma_{\mathbf{M}}\backslash D_{M}=\mathcal{A}_{3}\times(\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{2})
. It is strongly
\mathbb{V}
-admissible. If
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes},\mathbf{M})_{\mathrm{typ}}
was dense, one would find that
C\subset\mathcal{A}_{3}
intersects Hecke translates of
\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{2}
in a dense subset. This contradicts the Zilber-Pink conjecture
1.1
for the variation on
C
corresponding to the first cohomology groups of the members of the universal family of abelian threefolds above
C
, which has period map
C\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{3}
:
C
is of dimension
1
while
\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{2}
has codimension
2
in
\mathcal{A}_{3}
, so the intersections of
C
with Hecke translates of
\mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{2}
are atypical. So Theorem
1.9
should fail in the non-
\mathbb{Q}
-simple monodromy case.

Our criterion is nearly a complete answer to
1.4
in the sense that the only missing ingredient for our methods to give a full characterization of the density of typical Hodge loci is a better understanding of the distribution of typical special points in
S
. More precisely, using the methods of this work, we would need the following statement, which is a consequence of the Zilber-Pink conjecture
1.1
, to get a full answer to
1.4
:

Conjecture 1.14
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be as in Theorem
1.9
, and
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})\subsetneq(\mathbf{G},D)
be a strict Hodge sub-datum which satisfies

\dim\Phi(S^{\mathrm{an}})+\dim D_{M}=\dim D.

Then for any non-empty analytic open subset
B\subset S^{\mathrm{an}}
, one has a strict containment

\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes},\mathbf{M})_{\mathrm{atyp}}\cap B\subsetneq\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes},\mathbf{M})\cap B.

Indeed, the reason for distinguishing between admissibility and strong admissibility in Theorem
1.9
(ii) ultimately arises from the inability to handle typicality questions in the situation where one expects
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes},\mathbf{M})
to consist of typical special points.

Remark 1.15
.

Combining recent work of Tayou-Tholozan and the density of
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{typ}}
in
S
that we prove here, one should be able to obtain that this locus is even equidistributed in
S
for a natural measure. We refer the interested reader to their paper
[
27
]
.

1.2.2.
Classical Noether-Lefschetz loci

We now explain how to apply our results to reprove classical theorems on the density of Noether-Lefschetz loci. In particular, we will show how our results generalize the classical results of Ciliberto-Harris-Miranda
[
11
]
and Green
[
29
, Prop. 17.20]
by proving the analytic density of the components of maximal codimension of the Noether-Lefschetz locus of degree
d
hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
as soon as
d\geq 5
. (The case
d=4
requires extra analysis, which we omit.)

In this case one considers the variation
\mathbb{V}=(R^{2}f_{*}\underline{\mathbb{Z}})_{\textrm{prim}}
on primitive cohomology associated to the universal family
f
of such hypersurfaces, and the polarization
Q:\mathbb{V}\otimes\mathbb{V}\to\underline{\mathbb{Z}}
induced by cup-product. Fix some
s\in S^{\mathrm{an}}
. The result of Beauville
[
6
]
guarantees that the algebraic monodromy group
\mathbf{H}
of
\mathbb{V}
is the full orthogonal group
\textrm{Aut}(\mathbb{V}_{s},Q_{s})
stabilizing the polarizing form. As
Q_{s}
is a Hodge class in
(\mathbb{V}_{s}\otimes\mathbb{V}_{s})^{\vee}
and the algebraic monodromy group lies inside the generic Mumford-Tate group, this means that the generic Hodge datum for
\mathbb{V}
takes the form
(\mathbf{G},D)
, with
\mathbf{G}=\textrm{GAut}(\mathbb{V}_{s},Q_{s})
and with
D
the space of all polarized Hodge structures with the same Hodge numbers
(h^{2,0},h^{1,1},h^{0,2})
as
\mathbb{V}
. Given any subdatum
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})\subset(\mathbf{G},D)
corresponding to a single fixed Hodge vector, one computes that
\dim D-\dim D_{M}=h^{2,0}
. On the other hand it is classical that
h^{2,0}={d-1\choose 3}
, hence one has for
d\geq 5
that

\dim D-\dim D_{M}={d-1\choose 3}<{d+3\choose 3}-16=\dim\Phi(S^{\mathrm{an}}).

Here we have computed the period dimension by observing that the projective space of degree
d
homogeneous polynomials in
4
variables is
{d+3\choose 3}-1
dimensional, and the period map becomes generically injective modulo the natural action of
\textrm{SL}_{4}
on the moduli space as a consequence of Donagi’s generic Torelli theorem
[
14
]
, which holds assuming
d\geq 5
. Thus the datum
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})
is strongly
\mathbb{V}
-admissible, and Theorem
1.9
lets us conclude.

We expect that the above argument, which requires essentially only a verification of Zariski dense monodromy and a generic Torelli theorem, can be repeated to reprove numerous other analytic density results in the literature.

1.2.3.
Special subvarieties of the Torelli locus

Let
g\geqslant 4
be an integer, and fix a level structure
n\geqslant 3
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{g}
be the Shimura variety parametrising principally polarized abelian varieties of dimension
g
(with the given level structure) whose associated Shimura datum is
(\mathbf{GSp}_{2g},\mathbb{H}_{g})
, where
\mathbb{H}_{g}
is the Siegel upper half-plane. Recall that
\mathcal{A}_{g}
is a quasi-projective complex variety of dimension
\frac{g(g+1)}{2}
and that the largest dimension obtained by any of its strict special subvarieties is
\frac{g(g-1)}{2}+1
, realised for example by
\mathcal{A}_{g-1}\times\mathcal{A}_{1}
. As suggested in
[
5
, Rmk 3.15]
, one expects in this setting that:

Conjecture 1.16
.

Let
S\subset\mathcal{A}_{g}
be a closed Hodge generic subvariety of dimension
q
, and
\mathbb{V}
the induced polarizable
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS on
S
. The typical Hodge locus of
S
for
\mathbb{V}
is analytically dense if and only if
q\geqslant g-1
. In that case, the Hecke translates of
\mathcal{A}_{g-1}\times\mathcal{A}_{1}
in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
intersect
S
in an analytically dense subset.

Unconditionally, Theorem
1.9
gives the following:

Corollary 1.17
.

Let
S\subset\mathcal{A}_{g}
be a closed Hodge generic subvariety of dimension
q
, and
\mathbb{V}
the induced polarizable
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS on
S
. If
q\geqslant g
, then the typical Hodge locus of
S
for
\mathbb{V}
is analytically dense. In that case, the Hecke translates of
\mathcal{A}_{g-1}\times\mathcal{A}_{1}
in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
intersect
S
in an analytically (positive dimensional) dense subset.

Proof.

\mathbb{V}
has generic Mumford-Tate group
\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{GSp}_{2g}
whose derived subgroup
\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{der}}=\mathbf{Sp}_{2g}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-simple. In particular, since
\mathbb{V}
is non-constant and the algebraic monodromy group
\mathbf{H}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-normal subgroup of the derived Mumford-Tate group, one has
\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{der}}=\mathbf{Sp}_{2g}
. To conclude, one simply remarks that the inequality
q\geqslant g
is equivalent to the strong
\mathbb{V}
-admissiblity of the Hodge sub-datum corresponding to
\mathcal{A}_{g-1}\times\mathcal{A}_{1}
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{M}_{g}
be the moduli space of smooth projective curves of genus
g
with level-
n
-structure, let
j:\mathcal{M}_{g}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{g}
the Torelli morphism,
and
\mathcal{T}^{0}_{g}=j(\mathcal{M}_{g})\subset\mathcal{A}_{g}
the open Torelli locus. In this setting, Corollary
1.17
gives:

Corollary 1.18
.

The typical Hodge locus of
\mathcal{M}_{g}
for the polarizable
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS induced by
j
is analytically dense in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
.

Proof.

\mathcal{T}^{0}_{g}
is known to be of dimension
3g-3
which is bigger than
g
in our setting (
g\geqslant 4
). Therefore, applying Corollary
1.17
to the closure of
\mathcal{T}^{0}_{g}
in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
gives the density of the Hodge locus in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
for the analytic topology.
∎

Remark 1.19
.

Similar questions had already been studied in
[
19
,
12
]
for
\mathcal{A}_{g}
and
[
10
]
in the more general setting of Shimura varieties. Chai had developed a numerology to give a sufficient condition on the codimension of a subvariety of a Shimura variety to have analytically dense Hodge locus of given type
\mathbf{M}
.
These conditions weren’t however optimal, and Theorem
1.9
(and its generalisation Theorem
3.5
) can be seen as a refinement of these works in three directions. First, we give numerical conditions in a general Hodge theoretic setting while previous authors only worked in the Shimura setting. Furthermore, our numerical conditions are sharper than previous ones, even in the Shimura setting. For example, they only knew that subvarieties of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
had analytically dense Hodge locus if they had codimension less than
g
. We prove the same thing for subvarieties of dimension greater than
g
. Finally, the notion of typicality doesn’t appear in the aforementionned works, so proving density of the typical part of the Hodge locus is an additional improvement.

1.3.
Recent related work

Recently, S. Eterović and T. Scanlon have obtained similar results in a general framework of so-called definable arithmetic quotients. In particular, they also give
[
16
, Theorem 3.4]
a criterion for Hodge loci to be dense. Our work can be seen as refining these results in the Hodge-theoretic setting, taking into account the Hodge-theoretic (a)typicality of the resulting loci. More precisely, we prove in Theorem
1.9
(ii) and Theorem
3.5
(ii) that the components of
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes},\mathbf{M})
that we construct have the expected Mumford-Tate groups, whereas a conclusion of this type does not appear in the main theorem of
[
16
]
.

2.
Recollections

We now review notions from variational Hodge theory; we refer to
[
29
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
21
]
and
[
22
]
for a detailed account of the theory. The first two subsections recall the basic notions, and the subsequent two subsections will review more recent results which we will need for our proofs.
Before starting, let us emphasize on the following:

Remark 2.1
.

In this work, we are only interested on proving analytic density results for Hodge loci in the base
S
of some
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS. In particular, to prove them, we are free to replace the base
S
by:

(i)

some non-empty Zariski open subset, as
S
being irreducible, such a subset will be analytically dense in
S
;

(ii)

some finite étale covering, as the image of an analytically dense subset of the cover will remain such in
S
.

We will do such substitutions freely in the sequel.

Notation 2.2
.

For an algebraic group
\mathbf{G}
over
\mathbb{Q}
, we denote by
\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{ad}}
its adjoint group, by
\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{der}}
its derived subgroup, and by
\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
the identity component for the real analytic topology. Finally, we define
\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q})^{+}
to be
\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{R})^{+}\cap\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q})
.

2.1.
Hodge data and special subvarieties

Recall that the Mumford-Tate group of a
\mathbb{Q}
-Hodge structure
x:\mathbb{S}\rightarrow\mathbf{GL}(V_{\mathbb{R}})
on a finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
V
(where
\mathbb{S}=\mathrm{Res}_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{G}_{m}
is the Deligne torus) is the smallest
\mathbb{Q}
-subgroup
\mathbf{MT}(x)
of
\mathbf{GL}(V)
such that the morphism of
\mathbb{R}
-algebraic groups
x
factors through
\mathbf{MT}(x)_{\mathbb{R}}
. Equivalently, it is the fixator in
\mathbf{GL}(V)
of all Hodge tensors for
x
. It is a connected
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic group, which is reductive as soon as
x
is polarized, which will always be assumed in the sequel. The associated Mumford-Tate domain is the orbit of
x
under the identity connected component
\mathbf{MT}(x)(\mathbb{R})^{+}
of the real Lie group
\mathbf{MT}(x)(\mathbb{R})
in
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{S},\mathbf{MT}(x)_{\mathbb{R}})
. Both notions can be summarized in the following, now usual, definition:

Definition 2.3

(
[
21
, Def. 3.4, Def. 3.5]
)
.

(i)

A
Hodge datum
is a pair
(\mathbf{G},D)
where
\mathbf{G}
is the Mumford-Tate group of some Hodge structure, and
D
is the associated Mumford-Tate domain.

(ii)

A
morphism of Hodge data

(\mathbf{G},D)\rightarrow(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
is a morphism of
\mathbb{Q}
-groups
\mathbf{G}\rightarrow\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
sending
D
inside
D^{\prime}
.

(iii)

A
Hodge sub-datum
of
(\mathbf{G},D)
is a Hodge datum
(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},D^{\prime})
such that
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
and the inclusion
\mathbf{G}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{G}
induces a morphism of Hodge data
(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},D^{\prime})\rightarrow(\mathbf{G},D)
.

(iv)

A
Hodge variety
is a quotient variety of the form
\Gamma\backslash D
for some Hodge datum
(\mathbf{G},D)
and some torsion-free arithmetic lattice
\Gamma\subset\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q})^{+}
.

Remark 2.4
.

Note that what we call here a
Hodge datum
is usually referred to as a
connected Hodge datum
in the literature. Since we will always be considering connected Hodge data, we ommit the "connected" in the sequel.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a polarizable
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS on a smooth, irreducible and quasi-projective algebraic variety
S
over
\mathbb{C}
. There exists a reductive
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic group
\mathbf{G}
and a countable union
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})
of irreducible algebraic subvarieties of
S
such that for each point
s\in S^{\mathrm{an}}
, the polarized Hodge structure carried by
\mathbb{V}_{s}
has Mumford-Tate group isomorphic (by parallel transport) to a subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
which is strictly contained in
\mathbf{G}
if and only if
s\in\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})
(
[
9
]
,
[
3
]
). The group
\mathbf{G}
is called the
generic Mumford-Tate group of
\mathbb{V}
, the locus
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})
is called the
Hodge locus of
S
for
\mathbb{V}
, and points
s\in S^{\mathrm{an}}-\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})
are said to be
Hodge generic
. The orbit in
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{S},\mathbf{G}_{\mathbb{R}})
of the Hodge structure carried by
\mathbb{V}_{s}
under
\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
doesn’t depend on the choice of
s\in S^{\mathrm{an}}
and is denoted by
D
. We will refer to the Hodge datum
(\mathbf{G},D)
as the
generic Hodge datum of
\mathbb{V}
. Then, up to replacing
S^{\mathrm{an}}
by a finite étale cover, one can associate to
\mathbb{V}
a holomorphic, locally liftable to
D
and horizontal map

\Phi:S^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash D

where
\Gamma\subset\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q})^{+}
is a torsion-free arithmetic lattice containing the image of the monodromy representation associated to
\mathbb{V}
. We will always refer to this map as
the period map associated to
\mathbb{V}
in the sequel.

If
Y
is an irreducible algebraic subvariety of
S
, we can apply the previous paragraph to
\mathbb{V}|_{Y^{sm}}
(where
Y^{sm}
denotes the smooth locus of
Y
) to define the
generic Hodge datum
(\mathbf{G}_{Y},D_{G_{Y}})
of
Y
for
\mathbb{V}
. We see it as a Hodge sub-datum of the generic Hodge datum
(\mathbf{G},D)
of
\mathbb{V}
.

Definition 2.5

(
[
20
, Def. 1.2]
)
.

An irreducible algebraic subvariety
Y\subset S
is called a
special subvariety of
S
for
\mathbb{V}
if it is maximal for the inclusion among irreducible algebraic subvarieties of
S
whose generic Mumford-Tate group is
\mathbf{G}_{Y}
.

Special subvarieties of
S
for
\mathbb{V}
can be characterized in more geometric terms.
A
special subvariety of the Hodge variety
\Gamma\backslash D
is defined (see
[
21
, Def. 3.12]
) as the image of a map

[f]:\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash D^{\prime}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash D

between Hodge varieties induced by a morphism of Hodge data
f:(\mathbf{G}^{\prime},D^{\prime})\rightarrow(\mathbf{G},D)
, where
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{G}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})^{+}
is a torsion-free arithmetic lattice such that
f(\Gamma^{\prime})\subset\Gamma
(such a map will be called, following
[
21
, Lem. 3.9.]
, a
morphism of Hodge varieties
). Then we have the:

Proposition 2.6

(
[
21
, Prop 3.20]
)
.

An irreducible algebraic subvariety
Y\subset S
is special for
\mathbb{V}
if and only if
Y
is an irreducible component of the preimage by
\Phi
of a special subvariety of the Hodge variety
\Gamma\backslash D
.

This characterisation, which realises special subvarieties as preimages by the period map
\Phi
of intersections between
\Phi(S^{\mathrm{an}})
and some analytic subvarieties of
\Gamma\backslash D
, naturally suggests the dichotomy presented in the introduction between typical and atypical special subvarieties. More precisely, one defines:

Definition 2.7
.

Let
Y
be a special subvariety of
S
for
\mathbb{V}
with generic Hodge datum
(\mathbf{G}_{Y},D_{\mathbf{G}_{Y}})
. It is said to be
atypical
if
\Phi(S^{\mathrm{an}})
and
\Gamma_{\mathbf{G}_{Y}}\backslash D_{G_{Y}}
intersect with bigger dimension than expected along
\Phi(Y^{\mathrm{an}})
inside of
\Gamma\backslash D
, i.e.

\mathrm{codim}_{\Gamma\backslash D}\Phi(Y^{\mathrm{an}})<\mathrm{codim}_{\Gamma\backslash D}\Phi(S^{\mathrm{an}})+\mathrm{codim}_{\Gamma\backslash D}\Gamma_{\mathbf{G}_{Y}}\backslash D_{G_{Y}}

Otherwise, it is said to be
typical
.

Remark 2.8
.

Note the difference with
[
5
, Def. 2.2]
: here we allow typical special subvarieties to be singular for
\mathbb{V}
(i.e. to have image by
\Phi
fully contained in the singular locus of
\Phi(S^{\mathrm{an}})
, see
op. cit.
). Indeed, because our results are insensitive to replacing the base by some non-empty Zariski open subset of it, we are free to remove the singular locus of
\Phi(S^{\mathrm{an}})
(see
[
5
, Rmk. 2.3.]
or Step
1
of Section
4.3
). Thus, we choose to use this simpler definition. Note that this definition also differs from Klingler’s original one (
[
21
, Def. 4.3]
), which allows typical subvarieties to lie in the singular locus of
\mathbb{V}
but uses codimensions inside of the horizontal distribution of
\Gamma\backslash D
.

Then, the Hodge locus splits in two parts

\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})=\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{typ}}\cup\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{atyp}}

where
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{typ}}
(resp.
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes})_{\mathrm{atyp}}
) is defined as the union of the strict typical (resp. atypical) special subvarieties of
S
for
\mathbb{V}
, and is referred to as the
typical (resp. atypical) Hodge locus
.

2.2.
Monodromy and weakly special subvarieties

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS on a smooth, irreducible and quasi-projective algebraic variety
S
over
\mathbb{C}
, let
(\mathbf{G},D)
be its generic Hodge datum, and fix
s_{0}\in S^{\mathrm{an}}
a Hodge generic point such that the Mumford-Tate group of the Hodge structure carried by
\mathbb{V}_{s_{0}}
is equal (and not only isomorphic) to
\mathbf{G}
. The local system
\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{Q}}:=\mathbb{V}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
corresponds to a representation of the topological fundamental group of
S^{\mathrm{an}}
with base-point
s_{0}
:

\rho:\pi_{1}(S^{\mathrm{an}},s_{0})\rightarrow\mathbf{GL}(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{Q},s_{0}})

We define the
algebraic monodromy group
\mathbf{H}
of
\mathbb{V}
as the identity connected component of the
\mathbb{Q}
-Zariski closure of
\rho(\pi_{1}(S^{\mathrm{an}},s_{0}))
in
\mathbf{GL}(\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{Q},s_{0}})
. More generally, if
Y\subset S
is an irreducible algebraic subvariety, we define the
algebraic monodromy group
\mathbf{H}_{Y}
of
Y
for
\mathbb{V}
as the algebraic monodromy group of the restricted
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS
\mathbb{V}|_{Y^{\mathrm{sm}}}
on the smooth locus
Y^{\mathrm{sm}}
of
Y
. Then, recall the following celebrated result of André which follows from the theorem of the fixed part and the semi-simplicity theorem of Deligne
[
13
]
(in the geometric setting) and Schmid
[
26
]
(for general
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS).

Theorem 2.9

(
[
1
, Thm. 1]
)
.

Let
Y\subset S
be an irreducible algebraic subvariety. Then the monodromy group
\mathbf{H}_{Y}
of
Y
for
\mathbb{V}
is a normal subgroup of the derived subgroup
\mathbf{G}_{Y}^{\mathrm{der}}
of the generic Mumford-Tate group of
Y
for
\mathbb{V}
.

In analogy with Proposition
2.5
one defines:

Definition 2.10
.

An irreducible algebraic subvariety
Y\subset S
is said
weakly special for
\mathbb{V}
if it is maximal for the inclusion among irreducible algebraic subvarieties of
S
which have algebraic monodromy group
\mathbf{H}_{Y}
.

As in the case of special subvarieties, there is a nice group theoretic description of weakly special subvarieties. A
weakly special subvariety of the Hodge variety
\Gamma\backslash D
is defined (see.
[
20
, Def. 4.1]
) to be the image of a subvariety of the form

\Gamma_{\mathbf{M}}\backslash D_{M}\times\{t\}\subset\Gamma_{\mathbf{M}}\backslash D_{M}\times\Gamma_{\mathbf{N}}\backslash D_{N}

by a morphism of Hodge varieties
\Gamma_{\mathbf{M}}\backslash D_{M}\times\Gamma_{\mathbf{N}}\backslash D_{N}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash D
, with
t\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{N}}\backslash D_{N}
. Now we have the following:

Proposition 2.11

(
[
20
, Cor. 4.14]
)
.

Let
Y\subset S
be an irreducible algebraic subvariety.
Y
is a weakly special subvariety of
S
for
\mathbb{V}
if and only if it is the preimage by
\Phi
of a weakly special subvariety of
\Gamma\backslash D
.

Remark 2.12
.

Note that by André’s Theorem
2.9
, the derived subgroup of
\mathbf{G}
factors as
\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{der}}=\mathbf{H}\cdot\mathbf{L}
, and one shows
[
20
, Lem. 4.12]
that the period map
\Phi
is constant equal to some Hodge generic
t_{L}\in\Gamma_{\mathbf{L}}\backslash D_{L}
when projected to the factor corresponding to
\mathbf{L}
. In the sequel, we will omit this constant factor and simply write
\Phi:S^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow\Gamma_{\mathbf{H}}\backslash D_{H}
for the period map to signify that up to passing to a finite étale cover we have
\Phi:S^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow\Gamma_{\mathbf{H}}\backslash D_{H}\times\{t_{L}\}\subset\Gamma\backslash D
.

2.3.
Algebraicity of period maps

We will make use of the following result on algebraicity of period maps, recently proven by Bakker-Brunebarbe-Tsimerman.

Theorem 2.13

(
[
2
, Thm. 1.1]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a polarizable
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS on a smooth and quasi-projective algebraic variety
S
over
\mathbb{C}
, and denote by
\Phi:S^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow\Gamma\backslash D
the associated period map. The map
\Phi
factors uniquely as
\Phi=\iota\circ f^{\mathrm{an}}
where
f:S\rightarrow T
is a dominant regular map to an algebraic variety
T
, and
\iota:T^{\mathrm{an}}\hookrightarrow\Gamma\backslash D
is a closed analytic immersion.

2.4.
Functional transcendance

Our arguments heavily rely on a functional transcendence result, Bakker and Tsimerman’s Ax-Schanuel theorem, which will allow us to force some intersections to have expected dimension. Recall that the Mumford-Tate domain
D_{H}
embeds in its compact dual
\check{D}_{H}
which is a projective algebraic variety over
\mathbb{C}
. This embedding allows one to define an irreducible algebraic subvariety of
S\times D_{H}
as an analytic irreducible component of the intersection
(S\times D_{H})\cap V
of
S\times D_{H}
with an algebraic subvariety
V
of
S\times\check{D}_{H}
. Bakker-Tsimerman prove:

Theorem 2.14

(
[
4
, Thm. 1.1]
)
.

Let
W\subset S\times D_{H}
be an algebraic subvariety. Let
U
be an irreducible complex analytic component of
W\cap(S\times_{\Gamma_{\mathbf{H}}\backslash D_{H}}D_{H})
such that

\mathrm{codim}_{S\times D_{H}}U<\mathrm{codim}_{S\times D_{H}}W+\mathrm{codim}_{S\times D_{H}}\big(S\times_{\Gamma_{\mathbf{H}}\backslash D_{H}}D_{H}\big).

Then the projection of
U
to
S
is contained in a strict weakly special subvariety of
S
for
\mathbb{V}
.

Remark 2.15
.

The proof of Bakker-Tsimerman’s Ax-Schanuel crucially relies on the definability of period maps in the o-minimal structure
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\exp}
. It has been recently reproven in a more general setting using an o-minimal free approach (
[
7
]
).

3.
Density of typical special subvarieties

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a polarizable
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS on a smooth, irreducible and quasi-projective variety
S
over
\mathbb{C}
. Denote by
(\mathbf{G},D)
the generic Hodge datum, by
\Gamma\subset\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{Q})^{+}
some torsion-free arithmetic lattice containing the image of the monodromy representation of
\mathbb{V}
, by
\mathbf{H}
the algebraic monodromy group and by
\Phi:S^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow\Gamma_{\mathbf{H}}\backslash D_{H}\subset\Gamma\backslash D
the associated period map (see Remark
2.12
).

In the sequel, definability will always be meant in the o-minimal structure
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an,exp}}
. We use the notion of definable manifold appearing in
[
2
, Def. 2.1, Def. 2.2]
. The natural Borel embedding
D\hookrightarrow\check{D}
as an open subset of the compact dual defines a canonical structure of
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an,exp}}
-definable (and even
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{alg}}
-definable) manifold on
D
. Similarly, the algebraic structure on
S
induces a canonical structure of
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an,exp}}
-definable (and again even
\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{alg}}
-definable) manifold on
S^{\mathrm{an}}
. Definability on
D
and
S^{\mathrm{an}}
will always be meant with respect to these structures.

3.1.
Definable fundamental sets

In the proofs and statements of intermediate lemmas, we will need to work with definable fundamental sets for the uniformization maps that appear, which are compatible with period maps. However, we won’t need the delicate definable factorization in
[
3
]
of period maps through fundamental sets for the
\Gamma
-action on
D
, but only, following
[
4
, Sect. 3]
and
[
5
, Sect. 6.1.1]
, definable fundamental sets for the covering action of the fundamental group of
S
on a suitable cover of
S
, to which the restriction of the lifted period map is definable. We recall quickly the construction for completeness.

Let
(\bar{S},E)
be a log-smooth compactification of
S
. By the Borel monodromy theorem
[
26
, (4.5)]
, and because our results are unaffected by passing to a finite étale cover of
S
(see Remark
2.1
), we can and do assume that
\mathbb{V}
has unipotent monodromy along irreducible branches of
E
. The datum of this compactification allows us to choose a definable atlas of
S
for the definable structure induced by its algebraic structure, given by finitely many (by compactness of
\bar{S}
) polydisks
P_{i}=\Delta^{k_{i}}\times(\Delta^{*})^{l_{i}}
(because the divisor
E
has simple normal crossings). Let
p_{i}=\exp:\tilde{P}_{i}:=\Delta^{k_{i}}\times\mathbb{H}^{l_{i}}\rightarrow P_{i}
be the universal cover and pick some
b,c>0
such that, denoting by
\Sigma=(-b,b)\times(c,+\infty[\subseteq\mathbb{H}
the associated vertical strip, the set
\mathcal{F}_{i}:=\Delta^{k_{i}}\times(\Sigma)^{l_{i}}
is a fundamental set for the covering action of
\pi_{1}(P_{i})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{l_{i}}
. We endow
\mathcal{F}_{i}
with the natural product definable structure, with on
\mathbb{H}
the structure coming from the natural embedding
\mathbb{H}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
. With these structures, the covering maps
p_{i}
are definable when restricted to
\mathcal{F}_{i}
.

For each
i
, choose a local lifting
\tilde{\Phi}_{i}
of
\Phi|_{P_{i}}
, and letting
\mathcal{F}
be the disjoint union of these local fundamental sets over the charts, view the collection of all such lifts as a map on
\mathcal{F}
. Then, because we assumed unipotent monodromy at infinity, a straightforward application of the Nilpotent Orbit Theorem
[
26
, (4.12)]
(see
[
4
, Lem. 3.1]
) shows that, after possibly shrinking the initial charts, we get the following diagram in the category of definable complex analytic manifolds:

(1)

\textstyle{\mathcal{F}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{F}}}
\scriptstyle{\exp}
\textstyle{D}
\textstyle{S^{\mathrm{an}}}

Notation 3.1
.

In the sequel, we fix once and for all the data needed to construct this diagram, and will denote
\mathcal{I}=\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{F})
. It is not a priori connected but, although it won’t be useful in the sequel, it is possible to choose the local lifts in the construction so that it is so. In particular, if two local lifts
\tilde{\Phi}_{i_{1}}
and
\tilde{\Phi}_{i_{2}}
have intersecting images in
\Gamma\backslash D
, then their images in
D
also intersect after replacing
\tilde{\Phi}_{i_{1}}
by
\gamma\cdot\tilde{\Phi}_{i_{1}}
for some
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Since the image of
\mathcal{I}
in
\Gamma\backslash D
is just the (necessarily connected) image of
S
, we can therefore ensure
\mathcal{I}
is connected after finitely many such adjustments.

3.2.
Statement of the result in the general setting

In order to tackle product phenomena we work, as in
[
5
, Sect. 1.2]
, in the setting of
[
17
, Chap. III]
which we recall now for completeness. The monodromy group
\mathbf{H}
decomposes as an almost direct product of its
\mathbb{Q}
-simple factors:

\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{G}_{1}\cdots\mathbf{G}_{n}.

After replacing
S
by a finite étale cover (see Remark
2.1
), the above decomposition induces a factorization of the period map

\Phi:S^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow\Gamma_{1}\backslash D_{1}\times\cdots\times\Gamma_{n}\backslash D_{n},

where for each
i
,
D_{i}
is some Mumford-Tate subdomain of
D
associated to
\mathbf{G}_{i}
and
\Gamma_{i}=\Gamma\cap\mathbf{G}_{i}(\mathbb{Q})^{+}
. For each
I\subset\{1,\cdots,n\}
, we write
D_{I}:=\prod_{i\in I}D_{i}
which is a Mumford-Tate domain for the group
\mathbf{G}_{I}:=\prod_{i\in I}\mathbf{G}_{i}
endowed with a projection map
p_{I}:D\rightarrow D_{I}
. We also write

q_{I}:\Gamma\backslash D\rightarrow\Gamma_{I}\backslash D_{I}:=\prod_{i\in I}\Gamma_{i}\backslash D_{i}

for the natural projection and
\Phi_{I}:=q_{I}\circ\Phi
for the projected period map.

Remark 3.2
.

The action of
\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
on
D_{1}\times\cdots\times D_{n}
is compatible with the product decomposition. Namely, for
h\in\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{R})^{+}
, we can write
h=g_{1}\cdots g_{n}
with
g_{i}\in\mathbf{G}_{i}(\mathbb{R})
and then, for
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
, one has:

h\cdot x=(g_{1}\cdot x_{1},\cdots,g_{n}\cdot x_{n}).

This applies for any choice of decomposition
h=g_{1}\cdots g_{n}
. The same applies more generally for the decomposition
D=D_{H}\times D_{L}
associated to the almost-direct factorization
\mathbf{G}^{\textrm{der}}=\mathbf{H}\cdot\mathbf{L}
.

When trying to give a sufficient condition generalizing that of Theorem
1.9
to the not necessarily
\mathbb{Q}
-simple monodromy case, Example
1.13
suggests that one should at least impose conditions like
\mathbb{V}
-admissibility on each factor, including on the factor where the period map is constant.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})\subsetneq(\mathbf{G},D)
be a strict Hodge sub-datum. It is called

(i)

factorwise
\mathbb{V}
-admissible
if for every non-empty set of indexes
I\subseteq\{1,\cdots,n\}
the inequality

\dim p_{I}(D_{M})+\dim\Phi_{I}(S^{\mathrm{an}})-\dim D_{I}\geq 0

holds.

(ii)

factorwise strongly
\mathbb{V}
-admissible
if for every non-empty set of indexes
I\subseteq\{1,\cdots,n\}
, the strict inequality

\dim p_{I}(D_{M})+\dim\Phi_{I}(S^{\mathrm{an}})-\dim D_{I}>0

holds.

As explained in Remark
1.12
, one should also add some condition on the constant factor:

Definition 3.4
.

A strict Hodge sub-datum
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})\subsetneq(\mathbf{G},D)
is said to be
full on the
\mathbf{L}
-factor
if, writing
\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{der}}=\mathbf{H}\cdot\mathbf{L}
as an almost-direct product, the normal subgoup
\mathbf{L}
of
\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{der}}
is contained in
\mathbf{M}
.

We can now state the main result of this paper:

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a polarizable
\mathbb{Z}
-VHS on a smooth, irreducible and quasi-projective variety
S
over
\mathbb{C}
, with generic Hodge datum
(\mathbf{G},D)
. Let
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})\subsetneq(\mathbf{G},D)
be a strict Hodge sub-datum.

(i)

If
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})
is full on the
\mathbf{L}
-factor and factorwise
\mathbb{V}
-admissible,
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes},\mathbf{M})
is analytically dense in
S^{\mathrm{an}}
.

(ii)

If
\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{G}^{\mathrm{der}}
and
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})
is factorwise strongly
\mathbb{V}
-admissible,
\mathrm{HL}(S,\mathbb{V}^{\otimes},\mathbf{M})_{\mathrm{typ}}
is analytically dense in
S^{\mathrm{an}}
.

3.3.
Reformulation of factorwise
\mathbb{V}
-admissibility

To prove this result, we will need to reformulate the factorwise
\mathbb{V}
-admissibility condition in a way which is better suited to our proof, although more complicated to define.
First define:

Definition 3.6
.

Let
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})\subsetneq(\mathbf{G},D)
be a strict Hodge sub-datum, and
I\subseteq\{1,\cdots,n\}
be a non-empty set of indexes whose complementary set we denote by
I^{c}
. A pair
(g,t)\in\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{R})^{+}\times(D_{I^{c}}\times D_{L})
is called a
(\mathcal{I},D_{M},D_{I})
-intersecting pair
if

\mathcal{I}\cap(g\cdot D_{M})\cap(D_{I}\times\{t\})\neq\emptyset.

We will need:

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
(\mathbf{M},D_{M})\subsetneq(\mathbf{G},D)
be a strict Hodge sub-datum, let

be a non-empty set of indexes and

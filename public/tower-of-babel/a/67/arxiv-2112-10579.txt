SL ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{SL}(n) covariant function-valued valuations
Jin Li

Abstract
Classifications of SL ⁡ ( n ) \mathrm{SL}(n) covariant function-valued valuations are established with some assumptions of continuity. New valuations, for example, weighted moment functions, are introduced and our classifications give unified characterizations of the Laplace transform on convex bodies, L p L_{p} moment bodies, L p L_{p} difference bodies, and polar L p L_{p} moment bodies ( L − p L_{-p} intersection bodies). Using the new classifications, we also establish some Euler-type relations.

Let

Theorem 1
.

Let

. A map
Z:\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}\to F({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\})
is a continuous and
\mathrm{SL}(n)
covariant valuation
if and only if
there are
\zeta\in C({\mathbb{R}})
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{c}({\mathbb{R}})
such that

\displaystyle ZP(x)

\displaystyle=\zeta(h_{P}(x))+\zeta(-h_{-P}(x))+\frac{1}{|x|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}V_{n-1}(P\cap H_{x,t})d\mu(t)

for every
P\in\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\}
.

Here

is the support function of

, and

is the section volume (that is, the

-dimensional volume) of

The function

is the

L_{p}
difference body.
If

\zeta\in F({\mathbb{R}})
, by Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|x|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}V_{n-1}(K\cap H_{x,t})\zeta(t)dt=\int_{K}\zeta(x\cdot y)dy=:M_{\zeta}K(x)

(2)

for any

.
If

If

Theorem 2

Let

. A map
Z:\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}\to C_{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\})

\displaystyle=\zeta_{1}(h_{P}(x))+\zeta_{1}^{R}(h_{-P}(x))+E^{-}_{\zeta_{2}}(P)(x)+E^{-}_{\zeta_{2}^{R}}(-P)(x)+\frac{1}{|x|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}V_{n-1}(P\cap H_{x,t})d\mu(t)

\mathrm{GL}_{+}(n)

The general linear group is denoted by
\mathrm{GL}(n)
and the group of non-degenerate linear transforms with positive determinants by
\mathrm{GL}_{+}(n)

A map

is
\mathrm{GL}_{+}(n)
(or
\mathrm{GL}(n)
) covariant of weight

if

\displaystyle Z(\phi K)(x)=ZK(\phi^{t}x)

for any
\phi\in\mathrm{GL}_{+}(n)
(or
\mathrm{GL}(n)
, respectively) and

.
If

is not continuous, the functions

and

are not continuous in general.
We can characterize them by assuming that they are
\mathrm{GL}_{+}(n)
covariant of weight

.

Theorem 3
.

Let
n\geq 3
. A map
Z:\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}\to F({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\})
is a
\mathrm{GL}_{+}(n)
covariant valuation of weight

if and only if
there are
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in F({\mathbb{R}})

such that

\displaystyle ZP(x)=

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}(h_{P}(x))+\zeta_{1}^{R}(h_{-P}(x))+E^{-}_{\zeta_{2}}(P)(x)+E^{-}_{\zeta_{2}^{R}}(-P)(x)

(3)

for every
P\in\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}
and

.

A map

if and only if (
3
) holds for

.

Let

denote the characteristic function of a set

\mathbbm{1}_{K}(x)=1
, otherwise

.

We refer to Schneider

For

of a convex body

\displaystyle h_{M_{p}K}(x)^{p}=\int_{K}|x\cdot y|^{p}dy,~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

(4)

The

For the related

[

,

,

.

The gauge function of

\displaystyle\|x\|_{M_{p}^{\ast}K}^{p}=\int_{K}|x\cdot y|^{p}dy,~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

for

, and

\displaystyle V(K)\log\|x\|_{M_{0}^{\ast}K}=\int_{K}\log|x\cdot y|dy,~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

.

4
Properties of the classified valuations

We call a valuation simple if it vanishes on lower-dimensional sets.
Let
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be the space of infinitely differentiable functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Denote by
AC_{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\})
the space of continuous functions
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\}\to{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying that, for any
u\in S^{n-1}
,

1. the function
r\mapsto rf(ru)
is absolutely continuous on
(0,s]
for any
s>0
;

2.
\lim_{r\to 0}rf(ru)=0
for any
u\in S^{n-1}
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{c}({\mathbb{R}})
.
The map
Z
defined on
\mathcal{K}^{n}
by

\displaystyle ZK(x)=\frac{1}{|x|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}V_{n-1}(K\cap H_{x,t})d\mu(t),~~K\in\mathcal{K}^{n},~~x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\},

(9)

is a continuous, simple,
\mathrm{SL}(n)
covariant
C_{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\})
-valued valuation.

Moreover, if
\mu
has a density
\zeta\in F({\mathbb{R}})
, then

\displaystyle ZK(x)=\int_{K}\zeta(x\cdot y)dy,

(10)

and
ZK\in AC_{r}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\})
for every
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
.
Also, if
\zeta\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
, (
10
) can be defined also at
x=o
and
ZK\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for every
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
.

Proof.

The simplicity and the valuation property of
Z
are trivial.

Since the valuation defined by (
10
) is clearly
\mathrm{SL}(n)
covariant, by (
2
) and a standard approximation, the valuation defined by (
9
) is also
\mathrm{SL}(n)
covariant.

Let
\{x_{i}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\}
be a sequence converging to
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\}
and
\{K_{i}\}\subset\mathcal{K}^{n}
be a sequence converging to
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
.
By
[
44
, Theorem 1.8.8]
,
K_{i}\cap H_{x_{i},t}\to K\cap H_{x,t}
if either
H_{x,t}
passes through the relative interior of
K
or
K\cap H_{x,t}=\emptyset
.
Hence
V_{n-1}(K_{i}\cap H_{x_{i},t})\to V_{n-1}(K\cap H_{x,t})
for every
t\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{h_{K}(x),-h_{K}(-x)\}
.
Notice that the support set of the function
t\mapsto V_{n-1}(K\cap H_{x,t})
is compact.
By the dominated convergence theorem, (
9
), the boundedness of
t\mapsto V_{n-1}(K\cap H_{x,t})
and
the continuity of
\mu
, we obtain
ZK_{i}(x_{i})\to ZK(x)
.
This not only gives the continuity of
ZK
, but also the continuity of
Z

9
Euler-type relations

The maps
E^{\pm}_{\zeta},E_{\zeta}
are all
\mathrm{GL}(n)
covariant valuations of weight
0
; see Lemma
8
.
This fact together with Theorem
3
gives a new proof of the following Euler-type relation (for all
\mathcal{P}^{n}
).

Corollary 24
.

For
n\geq 0
, the Euler-type relation

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(P)}(-1)^{\dim F}\zeta\left(h_{F}(x)\right)=\zeta(-h_{-P}(x))

(47)

holds for every
P\in\mathcal{P}^{n}
,
\zeta\in F({\mathbb{R}})
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Shephard
[
48
]
established this Euler-type relation for linear functions
\zeta
.
His proof also works for arbitrary
\zeta
.
For linear
\zeta
, McMullen
[
41
]
established Euler-type relations for an arbitrary function-valued valuation
Z
with the additional assumption that
Z(P+y)(x)=ZP(x)+v(P,x)\cdot y
for all
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
where
v(P,x)
is any vector.

Notice that (
47
) for
\zeta\equiv 1
is the Euler-Schläfli-Poincaré formula

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(P)}(-1)^{\dim F}V_{0}(F)=\sum_{j=0}^{\dim P}(-1)^{j}|\mathcal{F}_{j}(P)|=V_{0}(P),

where
V_{0}
is the Euler characteristic, which is
1
for any convex body and
0
for the empty set.

Proof of Corollary
24
.

The definition of
E_{\zeta}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(P)}(-1)^{\dim F}\zeta\left(h_{F}\right)
does not rely on the dimension of the ambient space, so we can set the ambient space as
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
for
n\geq 2
.

For
x=o
, (
47
) is either the trivial case
0=0
or the Euler-Schläfli-Poincaré formula.
The last can be also obtained by setting
\zeta\equiv 1
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\}
in (
47
).
Hence we only need to prove (
47
) for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\}
.

Lemma
8
shows that
E_{\zeta}:\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}\to\mathcal{F}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is a
\mathrm{GL}(n)
covariant valuation of weight
0
.
Hence
E_{\zeta}
is described in Theorem
3
, that is,

\displaystyle E_{\zeta}P(x)=\zeta_{1}(h_{P}(x))+\zeta_{1}^{R}(h_{-P}(x))+E^{-}_{\zeta_{2}}(P)(x)+E^{-}_{\zeta_{2}^{R}}(-P)(x).

(48)

with some
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\in F({\mathbb{R}})
for all
P\in\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}
.
By the definition of
E_{\zeta}
, we get that

\displaystyle E_{\zeta}T^{2}(re_{1})=E_{\zeta}T^{1}(re_{1})=\begin{cases}\zeta(0),&r>0,\\
\zeta(r),&r<0,\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle E_{\zeta}T^{1}(e_{n})=E_{\zeta}\{o\}(e_{n})=\zeta(0).

Since
\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}
are determined by (
43
), (
44
) for
Z=E_{\zeta}
, we have

\displaystyle\zeta_{1}(r)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\zeta(0),&r\geq 0,\\
\zeta(r)-\frac{1}{2}\zeta(0),&r<0,\end{cases}

and
\zeta_{2}\equiv 0
.
For
o\in P
, we have
h_{P}(x),h_{-P}(x)\geq 0
.
Hence (
48
) implies that (
47
) holds for
o\in P
.

Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
be the vertices of
P\in\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}
and let
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\}
.
By the definition of support functions,
we have
h_{F}(x)=x\cdot x_{j(F)}
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}(P)
and
-h_{-P}(x)=x\cdot x_{\tilde{j}(P)}
with some
1\leq j(F),\tilde{j}(P)\leq m
.
Hence we can reformulate (
47
) for these
P
and
x
as

\displaystyle 0=\zeta(-h_{-P}(x))-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(P)}(-1)^{\dim F}\zeta\left(h_{F}(x)\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\zeta(x\cdot x_{i}),

(49)

where

\displaystyle a_{i}=\mathbbm{1}_{i}(\tilde{j}(P))-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(P)}(-1)^{\dim F}\mathbbm{1}_{i}(j(F)).

(50)

Now we claim that
a_{i}=0
for every
1\leq i\leq m
.
Indeed, assume first that
x\cdot x_{i}
are all different.
Since
{\mathbb{R}}
is an infinite dimensional space over the rational field
{\mathbb{Q}}
, we can find
r_{1},\dots,r_{m}\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{o\}
such that they are
{\mathbb{Q}}
-independent.
Also,
a_{i}
are integers and do not rely on the choice of
\zeta
, so we choose
\zeta\in F({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\zeta(x\cdot x_{i})=r_{i}
.
Thus (
49
) gives that all
a_{i}=0
.
If some of
x\cdot x_{i}
are the same, we just need that the (different) elements of the set
\{\zeta(x\cdot x_{i})\}
to be nonzero
{\mathbb{Q}}
-independent numbers.

The last part is to show that (
47
) holds for
P+y
for all
y\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
The vertices of
P+y
are
x_{1}+y,\dots,x_{m}+y
.
Notice that
h_{F+y}(x)=x\cdot(x_{j(F)}+y)
and
-h_{-(P+y)}(x)=x\cdot(x_{\tilde{j}(P)}+y)
.
We have

\displaystyle\zeta(-h_{-(P+y)}(x))-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(P+y)}(-1)^{\dim F}\zeta\left(h_{F}(x)\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\zeta(x\cdot(x_{i}+y)).

Hence (
47
) holds for all
P+y
.
Since
P
and
x
are chosen arbitrary in the above argument, the proof is completed.
∎

Corollary

immediately implies the following.

Corollary 25
.

For
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(P)}(-1)^{\dim F}\int_{S^{n-1}}\zeta\left(\frac{h_{F}(u)}{h_{L}(u)}\right)dV_{L}(u)=\int_{S^{n-1}}\zeta\left(-\frac{h_{-P}(u)}{h_{L}(u)}\right)dV_{L}(u)

for every

,
P\in\mathcal{P}^{n}
and
L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
such that

is contained in the interior of
L
.

Here
V_{L}
is the cone volume measure of
L
, that is,
V_{L}(\omega)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\nu_{K}^{-1}(\omega)}h_{K}(\nu_{K}(x))d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)
for any Borel set
\omega\subset S^{n-1}
. Here
\nu_{K}
is the Gauss map of the boundary of
K
and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
is the
(n-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

We also obtain local versions of the Euler-Schläfli-Poincaré formula related to
E^{\pm}_{\zeta}(P)
.
Let
1
denote the constant function
1
on
{\mathbb{R}}
.
By the definition,
E^{\pm}_{1}(P)=\sum_{j=0}^{\dim P}(-1)^{j}|\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{j}(P)|
.
If further

Corollary 26

(The local Euler-Schläfli-Poincaré formula)
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\dim P}(-1)^{j}|\mathcal{F}^{-}_{j}(P)|=(-1)^{\dim P}V_{0}(o\cap\operatorname{relint}P),

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\dim P}(-1)^{j}|\mathcal{F}^{+}_{j}(P)|=V_{0}(o\cap P),

and

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(P)}(-1)^{\dim F}V_{0}(x\cap F)=(-1)^{\dim P}V_{0}(x\cap\operatorname{relint}P)

for every

and

The last formula was also established by Kabluchko, Last, and Zaporozhets
[

]
.
They even established a general formula, replacing

by an arbitrary affine subspace (the right side needs to be changed a little bit in the general case).
Using such general formula, they confirmed the so-called Cowan formula (identity).

To prove Corollary

, we need introduce the following lemma.

Lemma 27

(Ludwig and Reitzner
[
36
]
)
.

Let
n\geq 2
. A map
Z:\mathcal{P}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
\mathrm{GL}(n)
invariant valuation of weight

if and only if
there are constants

such that

\displaystyle ZP=c_{1}V_{0}(P)+c_{2}(-1)^{\dim P}V_{0}(o\cap\operatorname{relint}P)+c_{3}V_{0}(o\cap P)

for every
P\in\mathcal{P}^{n}
.

Proof of Corollary
26
.

Since desired formulae do not rely on the choice of the dimension of the ambient space, we can set the ambient space as
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
for
n\geq 2
.
By Lemma
8
, the left sides of the first two desired formulae are
\mathrm{GL}(n)
invariant valuations of weight
0
.
Together with Lemma
27
,
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\dim P}(-1)^{j}|\mathcal{F}^{\pm}_{j}(P)|=c_{1}^{\pm}V_{0}(P)+c_{2}^{\pm}(-1)^{\dim P}V_{0}(o\cap\operatorname{relint}P)+c_{3}^{\pm}V_{0}(o\cap P).

with

.
Choosing

as
\{o\}
,
[-e_{1},e_{1}]
or

we get

\displaystyle\begin{cases}c_{1}^{-}+c_{2}^{-}+c_{3}^{-}=1,\\
c_{1}^{-}-c_{2}^{-}+c_{3}^{-}=-1,\\
c_{1}^{-}=0,\end{cases}~~~~\begin{cases}c_{1}^{+}+c_{2}^{+}+c_{3}^{+}=1,\\
c_{1}^{+}-c_{2}^{+}+c_{3}^{+}=1,\\
c_{1}^{+}=0.\end{cases}

Thus,

\displaystyle c_{1}^{-}=0,~c_{2}^{-}=1,~c_{3}^{-}=0,

\displaystyle c_{1}^{+}=0,~c_{2}^{+}=0,~c_{3}^{+}=1,

Hence the first two desired formulae are true.
For

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(P)}(-1)^{\dim F}V_{0}(o\cap F)=(-1)^{\dim P}V_{0}(o\cap\operatorname{relint}P).

(51)

Clearly, (

Now replacing

Recall that
\zeta\in C({\mathbb{R}})
.
Thus
b_{0}=0
and
\zeta(\alpha)=\zeta(1)
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Denoted by
c_{1}=\zeta(1)
, we get

\displaystyle ZP(x)=c_{1}V_{0}(P)

for every
P\in\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}
with
\dim P<n
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\}
.

For the full-dimensional case, we set

\displaystyle\tilde{Z}P(x)=\frac{1}{|x|}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}V_{n-1}(P\cap H_{x,t})d\mu(t),

which is equal to
ZP(x)-c_{1}V_{0}(P)
by the above argument.
Set
P=[0,1]\times[0,1]^{n-1}
and
x=e_{1}
or
x=-e_{1}
.
By (
57
) and (
58
), we have

\displaystyle\alpha^{-1}\mu(0,\alpha)=\mu(0,1)+b_{1}\log\alpha,~~\alpha^{-1}\mu(-\alpha,0)=\mu(-1,0)+b_{2}\log\alpha.

For arbitrary
0<r<s
, together with
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{c}({\mathbb{R}})
, we have

\displaystyle\mu(r,s)=\mu(0,s)-\mu(0,r)

\displaystyle=\mu(0,1)(s-r)+b_{1}(s\log s-r\log r)

\displaystyle=\int_{r}^{s}\mu(0,1)+b_{1}+b_{1}\log t~dt

and

\displaystyle\mu(-s,-r)=\mu(-s,0)-\mu(-r,0)

\displaystyle=\mu(-1,0)(s-r)+b_{2}(s\log s-r\log r)

\displaystyle=\int_{-s}^{-r}\mu(-1,0)+b_{2}+b_{2}\log(-t)~dt

Now set
c_{2}=\mu(0,1)+b_{1}
,
c_{3}=\mu(-1,0)+b_{2}
,
c_{4}=b_{1}
and
c_{5}=b_{2}
, then the proof is completed by (
2
).
∎

Corollary

,

,

.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{c}^{n}
be the set of origin-symmetric convex bodies in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
A map
Z:\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}\to\mathcal{K}_{c}^{n}
is
\mathrm{SL}(n)
covariant if
Z(\phi P)=\phi ZP
for any
\phi\in\mathrm{SL}(n)
and
P\in\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}
.

Corollary 37
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
q\geq 1
. A map
Z:\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}\to\mathcal{K}_{c}^{n}
is a continuous and
\mathrm{SL}(n)
covariant
L_{q}
Minkowski valuation
if and only if
there are
c_{1},c_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\displaystyle ZP(x)=c_{1}(P+_{q}(-P))+_{q}c_{2}M_{q}P

for every
P\in\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}
.

Proof.

Set
\tilde{Z}:\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}\to F({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\})
by

\tilde{Z}P(x)=h_{ZP}(x)^{q}

for any
P\in\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\}
.
It is easy to see that
\tilde{Z}
is a continuous,
\mathrm{SL}(n)
covariant valuation on
\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}
which takes values at
q
-homogeneous functions.
Consider an asymmetric
P\in\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}
for
\dim P<n
and for
\dim P=n
separately. Together with (
4
), the desired result follows directly from Corollary
34
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{S}_{c}^{n}
denote the space of origin-symmetric, compact, star-shaped sets which contain the origin in their interior.
For
q\neq 0
, the
L_{q}
harmonic sum
K\widehat{+}_{q}L
of
K,L\in\mathcal{S}_{c}^{n}
is

\displaystyle\|x\|_{K\widehat{+}_{q}L}=\left(\|x\|_{K}^{q}+\|x\|_{L}^{q}\right)^{1/q}.

For
q\geq 1
,
K^{\ast}\widehat{+}_{q}L^{\ast}=(K+_{q}L)^{\ast}
.

We use the notation
\langle\mathcal{S}^{n}_{c},\widehat{+}_{q}\rangle
as in
[
16
,
31
]
to denote
the smallest monoid containing
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{c}
with respect to the
L_{q}
harmonic addition.
Thus
\langle\mathcal{S}^{n}_{c},\widehat{+}_{q}\rangle
contains the set
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
for
q>0
and
\{o\}
for
q<0
, respectively.
A map
Z:\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}\to\langle\mathcal{S}^{n}_{c},\widehat{+}_{q}\rangle
is
\mathrm{SL}(n)
contravariant if
Z(\phi P)=\phi^{-t}ZP
for any
\phi\in\mathrm{SL}(n)
and
P\in\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}
.

Corollary 38
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
q\neq 0
. A map
Z:\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}\to\langle\mathcal{S}^{n}_{c},\widehat{+}_{q}\rangle
is a continuous and
\mathrm{SL}(n)
contravariant valuation
if and only if
there is a constant
c_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\displaystyle ZP(x)=\begin{cases}c_{1}M_{q}^{\ast}P,&q>-1,\\
\{o\},&q\leq-1.\end{cases}

for every
P\in\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}
.

Proof.

For
q\neq 0
, set
\tilde{Z}:\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}\to F({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\})
by

\tilde{Z}P(x)=\|x\|_{ZP}^{q}

for any
P\in\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\}
.
It is easy to see that
\tilde{Z}

is a continuous,
\mathrm{SL}(n)
contravariant valuation on
\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}
which takes values at
q
-homogeneous functions.
By Corollary
34
, we have

\displaystyle\|x\|_{ZP}^{q}=\begin{cases}c_{1}h_{P}(x)^{q}+c_{2}h_{-P}(x)^{q}+c_{3}\int_{P\cap H_{x,o}^{+}}(x\cdot y)^{q}dy+c_{4}\int_{P\cap H_{x,o}^{-}}\left(-x\cdot y\right)^{q}dy,&q>0,\\
c_{3}\int_{P\cap H_{x,o}^{+}}(x\cdot y)^{q}dy+c_{4}\int_{P\cap H_{x,o}^{-}}\left(-x\cdot y\right)^{q}dy,&-1<q<0,\\
0,&q\leq-1.\end{cases}

for
P\in\mathcal{P}_{o}^{n}
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{o\}
.
Let first
\dim P<n
.
By the definition of
\langle\mathcal{S}^{n}_{c},\widehat{+}_{q}\rangle
, either
\|x\|_{ZP}^{q}\equiv 0
or
\|x\|_{ZP}>0

.
Hence

Further consider

.
Now by (

.
(For

\|\cdot\|_{ZP}^{q}\equiv 0
means
\|\cdot\|_{ZP}\equiv\infty
and hence

.)
∎

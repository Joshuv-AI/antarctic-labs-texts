One-matrix differential reformulation of two-matrix models
Joren Brunekreef, Luca Lionni, Johannes Thürigen

Abstract
Differential reformulations of field theories are often used for explicit computations. We derive a one-matrix differential formulation of two-matrix models, with the help of which it is possible to diagonalize the one- and two-matrix models using a formula by Itzykson and Zuber that allows diagonalizing differential operators with respect to matrix elements of Hermitian matrices. We detail the equivalence between the expressions obtained by diagonalizing the partition function in differential or integral formulation, which is not manifest at first glance. For one-matrix models, this requires transforming certain derivatives to variables. In the case of two-matrix models, the same computation leads to a new determinant formulation of the partition function, and we discuss potential applications to new orthogonal polynomials methods.



I. Introduction

Two-matrix models have been widely studied in the literature since their introduction by Itzykson and Zuber
Itzykson:1980hi
, see for instance
Mehta:1981jm
;
1997NuPhB.506..633E
;
Eynard:2003hs
;
Bertola:2003cn
;
Bertola:2007is
;
Duits:2012vg
. Their Feynman expansions count ribbon graphs with two kinds of vertices.
They match, for different choices of potentials, the partition functions of some statistical models such as the Ising model
KAZAKOV-Ising
, colored triangulations
ColoringRandomTriang
, hard particles
Bouttier_2002
etc. on
random surfaces (thereby relating to 2D quantum gravity).

In quantum field theory, differential formulations such as

\begin{split}\frac{\mathcal{Z}}{\mathcal{Z}_{0}}&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}_{0}}\int\mathrm{D}\phi\,\textrm{e}^{-\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\mathrm{d}^{d}y\,\frac{1}{2}\phi(x)K(x,y)\phi(y)-\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,V\left(\phi(x)\right)}\\
&=\left[\textrm{e}^{-\int\mathrm{d}^{d}w\,V\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{J(w)}}\right)}\textrm{e}^{\int\mathrm{d}^{d}x\,\mathrm{d}^{d}y\,J(x)K^{-1}(x,y)J(y)}\right]_{J=0}\,,\end{split}

(1)

are widely used (Zee coins it the “central identity of quantum field theory” in his famous book
Zee:book
).
Here the
\phi
are fields on a
D
-dimensional spacetime,
\int\mathrm{D}\phi
is a functional integration, the interaction potential
V
is a polynomial in the fields,
K
is the propagator, and
\mathcal{Z}_{0}
is a normalization.
Such formulations allow expressing Gaussian expectation values and Wick’s formula, the key ingredient of perturbative expansions, as well-defined algebraic expressions that do not suffer the potential divergences of the combinatorially equivalent integrals (see for instance
Salmhofer:book
;
Bauerschmidt:book
;
GurauKraj
;
Gurau:2014
). They moreover provide a practical tool for computations of Feynman diagrams.

In particular, the differential formulation provides well-defined expressions for the Gaussian expectation values of
two-matrix models
, while the integrals are not well-defined and only understood formally through Wick’s theorem.

Remarkably, as we will show in the paper, two-matrix models can be expressed in terms of a single matrix in a differential formulation:
the two matrices necessary to control the interaction between the two kinds of vertices (or equivalently impose bipartiteness of the ribbon graphs)
are no longer needed in the differential formulations. Since the necessity of using two matrices complicates the resolution of two-matrix models, the existence of a one-matrix differential formulation raises the question whether this allows for a simpler resolution.

With the help of the one-matrix differential reformulation, it is possible to diagonalize the two-matrix models directly in the differential formulation.
The steps leading to the expression of the partition function as a Slater determinant and thereby to the resolution by biorthogonal polynomials involve elegant and interesting equations whose equivalence is not always manifest.
We detail their relations and take the opportunity to review and clarify some aspects of the differential formulation that can be found in the matrix-model literature, as well as the formula needed to diagonalize the differential operators with respect to matrix elements for Hermitian matrices, implicitly shown by Itzykson and Zuber in
Itzykson:1980hi
and stated by Zuber in
Zuber:2008kn
. These computations do not seem to indicate any simplification in the resolution of two-matrix models in differential formulation as initially hoped:
the role played by the two matrices is now played by the matrix and the derivatives with respect to the matrix, leading to the same resolution by biorthogonal polynomials.

On the other hand, the analysis of the equivalence between the different formulas for one-matrix models does provide new insights on two-matrix models. Indeed, the proof of equivalence between two of the diagonalized differential formulations of the one-matrix models involves transforming certain derivatives to variables in the Slater determinant formulation. The computation is not trivial and is detailed in the last section. The same computation can
be applied with little modifications to two-matrix models, and leads to a new determinant formulation of their partition functions.
Building on this, we comment on a potential new resolution using orthogonal polynomials instead of biorthogonal polynomials.

In order to show that the different expressions of the partition functions indeed coincide, we verify that all the prefactors and normalizations match. These constants are often omitted in the literature as they are sometimes tedious to compute and do not contribute to the critical behaviors or the combinatorial interpretations.
An advantage of the computations in differential formulations is that they most often do not need an overall normalization, as seen for instance in (
1
).

Differential formulations are often used in the literature with little or without justifications or references, and in addition to the developments presented here, the present paper is also intended to provide a text of reference by giving an exhaustive review in the context of random matrix models.

II. Brief presentation of the models

We start with a reminder on the two models presenting all relevant details, in particular their perturbative expansions and diagonalizations as well as the reformulation of two-matrix models in terms of biorthogonal polynomials.

II. 1. One-matrix models

(a) The model

The partition function of the Hermitian one-matrix model with potential
V
is

Z_{V}=\int_{\mathbb{H}_{N}}\frac{\mathrm{d}M}{a_{N}}\textrm{e}^{-N\mathrm{Tr}(\frac{1}{2}M^{2}-V(M))},

(164)

where
\mathbb{H}_{N}
is the set of Hermitian matrices of size
N\times N
, the
potential is
V(x)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{k}x^{k}
, the measure is
\mathrm{d}M=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{d}M_{ii}\prod_{i<j}\mathrm{d}\Re M_{ij}\mathrm{d}\Im M_{ij}
, and the normalization
a_{N}={2^{-N/2}}(\pi/N)^{-{N^{2}}/2}
is such that
Z_{V=0}=1
(by explicit computation of the Gaussian integral).
The free energy of the model is
F_{V}=\log Z_{V}
.

(b) Diagrammatic expansion

By expanding the exponential of the potential
V
, exchanging the summation and the integration, and using Wick’s theorem, one obtains a Feynman expansion of the one-matrix model over ribbon graphs, which can be seen as graphs with an additional cyclic ordering of the edges around each vertex. Given a ribbon graph, a cyclic sequence of edges such that every edge in the sequence also precedes the edge that follows in the ordering around a vertex of the graph is called a
face
(for more details see
ZvonkinMapEnumeration
;
BouttierMapEnumeration
;
Eynard:2015
;
Eynard:CountingSurfaces
;
Thurigen:2021vy
). In the expansion of the partition function, the vertices of valency
k
(that is, with
k
edges attached) are counted with a weight
N\frac{\lambda_{k}}{k}
and the edges with a weight
1/N
, as they correspond to propagators

\int_{\mathbb{H}_{N}}\frac{\mathrm{d}M}{a_{N}}\textrm{e}^{-\frac{N}{2}\mathrm{Tr}(M^{2})}M_{ij}M_{kl}=\frac{1}{N}\delta_{i,l}\delta_{j,k}.

(165)

To each face corresponds a trace of these Kronecker symbols, resulting in an additional factor
N
per face. The Feynman expansion of the free energy involves only connected graphs, which is not true for the partition function.
Denoting by
V_{k}(\Gamma)
the number of vertices of valency
k
of a ribbon graph
\Gamma
, the weight (or amplitude) of a graph in the perturbative expansion is therefore

\mathcal{A}(\Gamma)=\frac{N^{\chi(\Gamma)}}{|\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)|}\prod_{k\geq 1}\lambda_{k}^{V_{k}(\Gamma)},

(166)

where
\chi(\Gamma)
is the Euler characteristic

\chi=V-E+F=2K-2g,

(167)

in which
V=\sum_{k}V_{k}
is the total number of vertices,
E
is the total number of edges,
F
the total number of faces, and
K
the number of connected components of the ribbon graph (
K=1
for the graph expansion of the free energy), and
g
, the genus of the ribbon graph, is a non-negative integer. The combinatorial factor
|\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)|
is the number of automorphisms of the ribbon graph
\Gamma
(see
ZvonkinMapEnumeration
;
BouttierMapEnumeration
;
Eynard:2015
).

(c) Clarification regarding matrix integrals

All matrix integrals in the paper are understood as
formal
integrals: They are perturbative expansions labelled by Feynman diagrams, that is, formal generating functions for ribbon graphs (see
Eynard:CountingSurfaces
;
Orantin
;
Eynard:2015
and references therein).
Every equality between partition functions is understood as equality between their perturbative expansions, term by term, regardless of the analytic properties of these series. The questions of whether the matrix integrals are well-defined, whether the series are summable when the coupling constants are in some domain and in some certain limits (e.g. large
N
), and whether an equality between these different quantities holds non-perturbatively are not addressed in this paper.

(d) Diagonalization

One of the standard ways of solving this model is to first diagonalize the Hermitian matrices as
M=U^{\dagger}XU
, where
U\in\textrm{U}(N)
and
X=\textrm{diag}(\mathbf{x})
,
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Due to the unitary invariance, the integration over unitary matrices factors out the volume of the unitary group, and the partition function is expressed as an integral over the
N
real eigenvalues,

Z_{V}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}}{b_{N}}\,\Delta^{2}(\mathbf{x})\,\textrm{e}^{-N(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{x}^{2}-V(\mathbf{x}))},

(168)

where the measure is
\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{d}x_{i}
, the normalization is
b_{N}={(2\pi)^{N/2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}j!}\,{N^{-N^{2}/2}}
(this can be computed from Mehta’s integral Eq. (3.3.10) in
Mehta:2004wq
),

\Delta(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{i<j}(x_{j}-x_{i})
is the Vandermonde determinant,
\mathbf{x}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{2}
, and we use the notation

V(\mathbf{x})\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{N}V(x_{i}).

(169)

To solve the model one may apply for example the saddle point method, loop or Schwinger-Dyson equations, or
the method of orthogonal polynomials
Eynard:2015
.
Here we focus on the steps leading to the resolution by orthogonal polynomials relying on the diagonalization.

II. 2. Two-matrix models

(a) Partition function

The models we are interested in have the following form:

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{H}_{N}\times\mathbb{H}_{N}}\frac{\mathrm{d}A\,\mathrm{d}B}{c_{N}}\textrm{e}^{-N\mathrm{Tr}(AB-V_{1}(A)-V_{2}(B))}

(170)

where
V_{1}(x)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\alpha_{k}}{k}x^{k}
and
V_{2}(x)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{\beta_{k}}{k}x^{k}
, and the normalization
c_{N}
is chosen such that for
V_{1}=V_{2}=0
,
Z_{0,0}=1
.
The Gaussian part is formal
and should be understood as follows.
For two real variables, we formally have for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\textrm{e}^{-\alpha Nxy}=\frac{2\pi}{\imath\alpha N},

(171)

and more generally for
n,m\geq 0
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\textrm{e}^{-\alpha Nxy}\,x^{n}y^{m}=\frac{\delta_{n,m}}{(-N)^{n}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial\alpha^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\textrm{e}^{-\alpha Nxy}=\frac{\delta_{n,m}}{(-N)^{n}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial\alpha^{n}}\frac{2\pi}{\imath\alpha N},

(172)

so that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\textrm{e}^{-\alpha Nxy}\,x^{n}y^{m}=\delta_{n,m}\,\frac{2\pi}{\imath}\,\left(\frac{1}{\alpha N}\right)^{n+1}n!.

(173)

This leads to the normalization:

c_{N}=\int_{\mathbb{H}_{N}\times\mathbb{H}_{N}}\mathrm{d}A\,\mathrm{d}B\,\textrm{e}^{-N\mathrm{Tr}(AB)}=2^{N}\left(\frac{\pi}{\imath N}\right)^{N^{2}},

(174)

and to the propagator

\int_{\mathbb{H}_{N}\times\mathbb{H}_{N}}\frac{\mathrm{d}A\,\mathrm{d}B}{c_{N}}\,\textrm{e}^{-N\mathrm{Tr}(AB)}\,A_{ab}B_{cd}=\frac{1}{N}\delta_{a,d}\delta_{b,c}.

(175)

The integrals in Eq. (
171
), Eq. (
173
), Eq. (
174
) and Eq. (
175
) are not well-defined. On the other hand, the values we set formally here are the ones needed in order to recover the correct combinatorial expansions with the correct overall normalizations of the partition functions.

(c) Diagonalization

The first step in the resolution is to diagonalize

\int_{\textrm{U}(N)}\mathrm{d}U\,\textrm{e}^{\alpha\mathrm{Tr}(AUBU^{\dagger})}={\alpha^{-\frac{N(N-1)}{2}}}\prod_{k=1}^{N-1}k!\ \frac{\det\{\textrm{e}^{\alpha\,a_{i}b_{j}}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq N}}{\Delta(\mathbf{a})\Delta(\mathbf{b})},

(177)

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{a}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{b}}{N!\,d_{N}}\,\Delta(\mathbf{a})\Delta(\mathbf{b})\,\textrm{e}^{-N(\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}-V_{1}(\mathbf{a})-V_{2}(\mathbf{b}))},

(178)

(d) Determinant form

Let us detail the steps leading to the usual resolution using biorthogonal polynomials
Mehta:1981jm
. The Leibniz determinant formula

\det(\{f_{i,j}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq N})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N}}(-1)^{\sigma}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\,f_{i,\sigma(i)}

(179)

III. Differential reformulation of matrix integrals

In this section we show how to reformulate two-matrix models in terms of a single matrix and its derivatives. We first review the usual differential formulations of one- and two-matrix models. Then we present the new one-matrix differential formulation of the latter. We finally show how to diagonalize the models in this formulation.

III. 1. The differential formulation

It is known (see e.g.
GurauKraj
) that a Hermitian one-matrix model admits the following reformulation:

Z_{V}=\int_{\mathbb{H}_{N}}\frac{\mathrm{d}M}{a_{N}}\textrm{e}^{-N\mathrm{Tr}(\frac{1}{2}M^{2}-V(M))}=\left[\textrm{e}^{+\frac{1}{2N}\mathrm{Tr}(\frac{\partial}{\partial M})^{2}}\textrm{e}^{N\mathrm{Tr}\,V(M)}\right]_{M=0},

(184)

where
\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\frac{\partial}{\partial M}\bigr)^{2}=\sum_{a,b=1}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial M_{ab}}\frac{\partial}{\partial M_{ba}}
. The role of the matrices and derivatives can be exchanged:

Z_{V}=\left[\textrm{e}^{N\mathrm{Tr}V(\frac{\partial}{\partial M})}\textrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2N}\mathrm{Tr}\,(M^{2})}\right]_{M=0},

(185)

which is the formulation analogous to Eq. (
1
) for a one-matrix model.
Note that the differential expressions are clearly well-normalized, as for
V=0
, the only contributing term in the series-expansion of the exponential is 1.

As mentioned in Sec.

, the sign “
=
” means that the equality holds at the perturbative level: the Feynman expansions coincide term by term. While the perturbative expansion of the matrix model is obtained by expanding the exponential of

More precisely, recalling that

\begin{split}\left[\textrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2N}\mathrm{Tr}(\frac{\partial}{\partial M})^{2}}\textrm{e}^{N\mathrm{Tr}\,V(M)}\right]_{M=0}&=\sum_{\{n_{k}\geq 0\}}\prod_{k}\frac{(N\lambda_{k}/k)^{n_{k}}}{n_{k}!}\left[\textrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2N}\mathrm{Tr}(\frac{\partial}{\partial M})^{2}}\prod_{k}\mathrm{Tr}(M^{k})^{n_{k}}\right]_{M=0}\\

III. 2. Differential formulation of two-matrix models

For a two-matrix model the standard differential formulation is simply

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{H}_{N}^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}A\,\mathrm{d}B}{c_{N}}\,\textrm{e}^{-N\mathrm{Tr}(AB-V_{1}(A)-V_{2}(B))}=\left[\textrm{e}^{\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Tr}\,(\frac{\partial}{\partial A}\frac{\partial}{\partial B})}\textrm{e}^{N\mathrm{Tr}\left(V_{1}(A)+V_{2}(B)\right)}\right]_{A,B=0}.

(192)

Again, the differential expression is clearly well-normalized. Eq. (
192

\sum_{i_{p},j_{p}}\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Tr}\frac{\partial}{\partial A_{i_{p}j_{p}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial B_{j_{p}i_{p}}}A_{ab}B_{cd}=\frac{1}{N}\delta_{a,d}\delta_{b,c},

(193)

so that through Wick’s theorem, denoting by
\ell(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})=\sum_{k}k(a_{k}+b_{k})
, we have

\frac{1}{\frac{\ell(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})}{2}!}\left(\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Tr}\frac{\partial}{\partial A}\frac{\partial}{\partial B}\right)^{\frac{\ell(\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b})}{2}}\prod_{k}\mathrm{Tr}(A^{k})^{a_{k}}\mathrm{Tr}(B^{k})^{b_{k}}=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}A\mathrm{d}B}{c_{N}}\textrm{e}^{-N\mathrm{Tr}(AB)}\prod_{k}\mathrm{Tr}(A^{k})^{a_{k}}\mathrm{Tr}(B^{k})^{b_{k}},

(194)

the other steps being the same as for one-matrix models. This last quantity is expressed as a sum over bipartite ribbon graphs with
a_{k}
and
b_{k}
vertices of valency

associated to the matrix
A
respectively
B
and
\sum_{k}ka_{k}=\sum_{k}kb_{k}
edges.

While the right-hand side of Eq. (

via

), the left-hand side is well-defined.

In the differential formulation however, it is no longer necessary to use two matrices: this is important in the integral formulation to impose
bipartiteness of the Feynman ribbon graphs. This bipartiteness can also be implemented in a differential formulation using the fact that derivatives only act on matrices. The resulting differential formulation thereby only involves a
single matrix

\boxed{Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=\left[\textrm{e}^{N\mathrm{Tr}\,V_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{\partial}{\partial M})}\textrm{e}^{N\mathrm{Tr}\,V_{2}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}M)}\right]_{M=0}.}

(195)

This can be proven by showing that this differential formula generates the same ribbon graphs, together with the same combinatorial weights Eq. (
176

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=\left[\textrm{e}^{\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Tr}\frac{\partial}{\partial A}\frac{\partial}{\partial B}}\,\textrm{e}^{N\mathrm{Tr}\,V_{1}(A)}\,\textrm{e}^{N\mathrm{Tr}\,V_{2}(B)}\right]_{A=B=0}=\biggl[\left[\textrm{e}^{\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Tr}\frac{\partial}{\partial A}\frac{\partial}{\partial B}}\,\textrm{e}^{N\mathrm{Tr}\,V_{1}(A)}\right]_{A=0}\textrm{e}^{N\mathrm{Tr}\,V_{2}(B)}\biggr]_{B=0},

(196)

where, recalling that
\ell(\mathbf{a})=\sum_{k}ka_{k}

\displaystyle\left[\textrm{e}^{\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Tr}\frac{\partial}{\partial A}\frac{\partial}{\partial B}}\,\textrm{e}^{N\mathrm{Tr}\,V_{1}(A)}\right]_{A=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{a_{k}\geq 0\}}\sum_{i\geq 0}\prod_{k}\frac{(N\alpha_{k}/k)^{a_{k}}}{a_{k}!}\frac{1}{i!}\left[\Bigl(\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Tr}\frac{\partial}{\partial A}\frac{\partial}{\partial B}\Bigr)^{i}\prod_{k}(\mathrm{Tr}A^{k})^{a_{k}}\right]_{A=0}

(197)

Assuming that we have proven that:

\Bigl(\mathrm{Tr}\frac{\partial}{\partial A}\frac{\partial}{\partial B}\Bigr)^{\ell(\mathbf{a})}\prod_{k}\Bigl(\mathrm{Tr}\left(A^{k}\right)\Bigr)^{a_{k}}=\ell(\mathbf{a})!\prod_{k}\left(\mathrm{Tr}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial B}\right)^{k}\right)^{a_{k}},

(198)

(199)

(200)

.

We write

For every value taken by

\Bigl(\mathrm{Tr}\frac{\partial}{\partial A}\frac{\partial}{\partial B}\Bigr)^{\ell(\mathbf{a})}\prod_{k}\mathrm{Tr}(A^{k})^{a_{k}}=\hskip-5.69046pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\textrm{possible}}\\
{\textrm{ assignments}}\end{subarray}}\prod_{k}\left(\mathrm{Tr}\Bigl(\bigl(\frac{\partial}{\partial B}\bigr)^{T}\Bigr)^{k}\right)^{a_{k}}=\ell(\mathbf{a})!\,\prod_{k}\left(\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\frac{\partial}{\partial B}\bigr)^{k}\right)^{a_{k}},

(201)

where the last equality holds because there are

The equivalence between Eq. (

IV. Expressions as Slater determinants and applications

IV. 1. Equivalent formulations of a two-matrix model

In this first subsection, we detail the steps leading to the Slater determinant formulation of the partition functions of two-matrix models, in differential formulation.
The same holds for the one-matrix models, as the special case
V_{1}(x)=\frac{x^{2}}{2}
(cf. Sec.
IV. 3. (a)
).

(a) Determinant form

Starting from Eq. (
213

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=\left[\frac{1}{\Delta(\mathbf{x})}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}})}\right)\Delta(\mathbf{x})\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}x_{i})}\right)\right]_{\mathbf{x}=0},

(215)

we incorporate the products on the left and right of the Vandermonde determinant into a Slater determinant:

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=\left[\frac{1}{\Delta(\mathbf{x})}\det\left\{\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x_{i}})}x_{i}^{j-1}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}x_{i})}\right\}_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\right]_{\mathbf{x}=0}.

We can extract the symmetric part of the determinant by using the following identity (

, Thm. 1.2.4 p. 24):

\left[\frac{1}{\Delta(\mathbf{x})}\det_{i,j}\bigl(f_{j}(x_{i})\bigr)\right]_{\forall i,\,x_{i}=x}=f_{N}\,\det_{i,j}\left(f_{j}^{(i-1)}(x)\right),

(217)

with
f_{N}=1/\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}j!
.

6

6

The factor
(-1)^{\frac{N(N-1)}{2}}
in the reference is due to a different convention in the definition of the Vandermonde determinant.
This leads to the following Slater determinant:

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=f_{N}\det\left\{\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{i}}{\mathrm{d}x^{i}}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x})}x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}x)}\right]_{x=0}\right\}_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1}.

(218)

This is again well-normalized, since for
V_{1}=V_{2}=0
the determinant is
\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}i!
.

(b) Biorthogonal polynomials

As in Sec.
II. 2. (e)
,
\frac{\mathrm{d}^{i}}{\mathrm{d}x^{i}}
and

the differential formulation of the bilinear form of Eq. (

\langle f|g\rangle=c_{1}\left[f\biggl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\biggr)\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x})}g\biggl(\frac{x}{\sqrt{N}}\biggr)\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}x)}\right]_{x=0}.

From this, one can apply the method of biorthogonal polynomials.

(c) Equivalence between the diagonalized formulations for two-matrix models

Expanding the Vandermonde determinants in Eq. (

) as in Sec.
II. 2. (d)

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=e_{N}N!\det\left\{\left[\textrm{e}^{\frac{1}{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\frac{\partial}{\partial b}}a^{i}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(a)}b^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(b)}\right]_{a=b=0}\right\}_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1}.

This is seen to be equivalent to Eq. (

) which also holds when

are real variables (and seeing

\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{i}}{\mathrm{d}x^{i}}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{d}{dx})}x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}x)}\right]_{x=0}=N^{i/2}\left[\textrm{e}^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\frac{\partial}{\partial b}}a^{i}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(a)}b^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}b)}\right]_{a=b=0},

(d) Expansion over Schur functions

Performing steps similar to Sec.
II. 2. (d)
but in the differential formulation and the opposite direction, we may re-express Eq. (

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=\frac{f_{N}}{N!}\left[{\Delta\biggl(\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{x}}\biggr)}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{x}})}\Delta(\mathbf{x})\,\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\mathbf{x})}\right]_{\mathbf{x}=0}.

As explained in (

, Eq (2.2.4)), the expression Eq. (

(225)

Using the orthogonality of Schur functions for this scalar product and the Cauchy-Littlewood formula, one obtains directly an explicit double-series expansion over Schur functions (Eq. (2.2.13) in

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}
is a tau function of the Toda hierarchy.

IV. 2. Integration over one variable in the determinant form of two-matrix models

In order to verify the equivalence — in differential formulation — between Eq. (
211
) and Eq. (
212
), it is required to go to Slater determinant form, and then transform certain derivatives to variables.
We do this for general
V_{1}
, as the computation is the same in differential formulation for general
V_{1}
or for
V_{1}(x)=x^{2}/2
(corresponding to the one-matrix models).
We also detail the computation in the integral formulation for general
V_{1}
, which is naturally more involved than for
V_{1}(x)=x^{2}/2
, in which case it is a simple Gaussian integration. This leads to a new determinant formulation of the partition functions of two-matrix models, and we comment on its potential use for resolutions involving orthogonal polynomials instead of biorthogonal polynomials.

(a) General computation

We start from Eq. (
218
):

Z=f_{N}\det_{i,j}\left\{\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{i}}{\mathrm{d}x^{i}}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x})}x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(\frac{x}{\sqrt{N}})}\right]_{x=0}\right\}=f_{N}\det_{i,j}\left\{\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{i}}{\mathrm{d}x^{i}}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x})}x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}\right]_{x=0}\right\},

(226)

where
0\leq i,j\leq N-1
, by change of variable.

7

7

The factors
\sqrt{N}^{j-i}
from the change of variable compensate when factorized out of the determinant
In this subsection, we assume

to be a polynomial:

V_{1}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{p}\frac{\alpha_{k}}{k}x^{k},\qquad p\geq 1.

(227)

We write, as in Eq. (

\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{i}}{\mathrm{d}x^{i}}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x})}x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}\right]_{x=0}=\left[\left[\textrm{e}^{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}}y^{i}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}y)}\right]_{y=0}x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}\right]_{x=0}.

(228)

We first notice that:

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{r}}{\mathrm{d}y^{r}}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}y)}=(N^{1-p}\alpha_{p})^{r}S_{(p-1)r}(y)\,\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(y)},

(229)

where

is a monic polynomial of degree
(p-1)r

Let us consider the row

, where

. We can replace in the determinant the right-hand side of Eq. (

\Bigl[\Bigl[\textrm{e}^{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}}y^{s}S_{(p-1)r}(y)\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}y)}\Bigr]_{y=0}x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}\Bigr]_{x=0}

(230)

We now show that:

\left[\left[\textrm{e}^{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}}y^{s}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{r}}{\mathrm{d}y^{r}}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}y)}\right]_{y=0}x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}\right]_{x=0}=\left[\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x})}x^{r}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{s}}{\mathrm{d}x^{s}}\Bigl[x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}\Bigr]\right]_{x=0},

(231)

that is, just as for Eq. (
199
) or Eq. (

), the result of the inner bracket
[...]_{y=0}
on the left hand side is obtained by “replacing” the
y

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}

y

Developing first the right hand side, we see that:

\left[\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x})}x^{r}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{s}}{\mathrm{d}x^{s}}\left[x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}\right]\right]_{x=0}=\sum_{\{n_{k}\geq 0\}_{k}}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\frac{(N^{1-k}\alpha_{k}/k)^{n_{k}}}{n_{k}!}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{\ell(\mathbf{n})}}{\mathrm{d}x^{\ell(\mathbf{n})}}x^{r}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{s}}{\mathrm{d}x^{s}}\left[x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}\right]\right]_{x=0},

The right hand side of Eq. (

\sum_{\bigl\{n_{k}\geq 0\;\mid\;\ell(\mathbf{n})\geq\,r\bigr\}}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\frac{(N^{1-k}\alpha_{k}/k)^{n_{k}}}{n_{k}!}\frac{\ell(\mathbf{n})!}{(\ell(\mathbf{n})-r)!}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{\ell(\mathbf{n})-r+s}}{\mathrm{d}x^{\ell(\mathbf{n})-r+s}}x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}\right]_{x=0}.

\left[\textrm{e}^{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}}y^{s}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{r}}{\mathrm{d}y^{r}}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}y)}\right]=\sum_{q\geq 0}\frac{1}{q!}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{q}}{\mathrm{d}y^{q}}y^{s}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{r}}{\mathrm{d}y^{r}}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}y)}\right]\frac{\mathrm{d}^{q}}{\mathrm{d}x^{q}}=\sum_{q\geq s}\frac{s!}{q!}\binom{q}{s}\hskip-2.84544pt\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{q+r-s}}{\mathrm{d}y^{q+r-s}}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}y)}\right]\frac{\mathrm{d}^{q}}{\mathrm{d}x^{q}},

(235)

in which the three brackets are evaluated in
y=0

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{q+r-s}}{\mathrm{d}y^{q+r-s}}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}y)}\right]_{y=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{n_{k}\geq 0\}_{k}}\prod_{k=1}^{p}\frac{(N^{1-k}\alpha_{k}/k)^{n_{k}}}{n_{k}!}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{q+r-s}}{\mathrm{d}y^{q+r-s}}y^{\ell(\mathbf{n})}\right]_{y=0}

(237)

Replacing this in Eq. (

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=f_{N}\det\left\{M_{i,j}\right\}_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1},

(b) Computation in the integral formulation

Starting from Eq. (
181
), we change variable as
y^{\prime}=Ny
and include the normalization by

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=f_{N}\det\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y}{2\pi/\imath}\,\textrm{e}^{-xy}x^{i}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}x)}y^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(y)}\right\}_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1},

(241)

where we have used the fact that
f_{N}=(2\pi/\imath)^{N}d_{N}^{-1}N^{-\frac{N(N+1)}{2}}
.
The steps up to equation Eq. (

) are precisely the same in integral form, so we do not detail them again. While a perturbative proof of Eq. (

) is certainly possible, it does not seem to us that the proof above can directly be translated in integral form (in particular the steps Eq. (

) to Eq. (
237

) reads

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=f_{N}\det\left\{(N^{1-p}\alpha_{p})^{-r(i)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y}{2\pi/\imath}\,\textrm{e}^{-xy}y^{s(i)}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{r(i)}}{\mathrm{d}y^{r(i)}}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(y)}\right]x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}\right\}_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1}.

(242)

By performing
r
integrations by part:

\int\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\textrm{e}^{-xy}y^{s}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{r}}{\mathrm{d}y^{r}}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(y)}\right]x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}=(-1)^{r}\int\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{r}}{\mathrm{d}y^{r}}\textrm{e}^{-xy}y^{s}\right]\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(y)}x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)},

(243)

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{r}}{\mathrm{d}y^{r}}\bigl[(ay)^{s}\textrm{e}^{axy}\bigr]=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{s}}{\mathrm{d}x^{s}}\bigl[(ax)^{r}\textrm{e}^{axy}\bigr].

(244)

Indeed, using Leibniz formula:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{r}}{\mathrm{d}y^{r}}\left[(ay)^{s}\textrm{e}^{axy}\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\binom{r}{k}a^{s}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{k}}{\mathrm{d}y^{k}}\left[y^{s}\right]\frac{\mathrm{d}^{r-k}}{\mathrm{d}y^{r-k}}\left[\textrm{e}^{axy}\right]=\textrm{e}^{axy}\sum_{k=0}^{\min(r,s)}\frac{1}{k!}\frac{s!}{(s-k)!}\frac{r!}{(r-k)!}\frac{(ay)^{s}(ax)^{r}}{(axy)^{k}}.

(245)

The same expression is obtained developing

(-1)^{s}\int\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(y)}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{s}}{\mathrm{d}x^{s}}\textrm{e}^{-xy}x^{r}\right]x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}=\int\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\textrm{e}^{-xy}y^{s}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(y)}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{s}}{\mathrm{d}x^{s}}x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}\right],

(246)

where the equality is obtained by doing again
s
integrations by part.
We thus recover Eq. (
240
).

\textrm{e}^{t\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}x^{2}}}F(x)=\int\mathrm{d}y\,K_{t}(x-y)F(y),\qquad K_{t}(x-y)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}e^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4t}},

(253)

for
t=\frac{1}{2N}
and
F(x)=x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}
. We get:

Z_{{x^{2}}/2,V_{2}}=f_{N}\det\left\{\sqrt{\frac{N}{2\pi}}\int\mathrm{d}y\left[\frac{\partial^{i}}{\partial x^{i}}\textrm{e}^{-\frac{N(x-y)^{2}}{2}}\right]_{x=0}y^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(y)}\right\}_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1},

(254)

where the bracket evaluating
x
at 0 has been moved inside the integral, as only the derivative of the kernel depends on
x
. One can verify that

\left[\frac{\partial^{i}}{\partial x^{i}}\textrm{e}^{-\frac{N(x-y)^{2}}{2}}\right]_{x=0}=N^{i}\tilde{Q}_{i}(y)\textrm{e}^{-\frac{N}{2}y^{2}},

(255)

where
\tilde{Q}_{i}(y)
is a monic polynomial of degree
i
, so we do recover Eq. (
251
) as
f_{N}(\frac{N}{2\pi})^{\frac{N}{2}}N^{\frac{N(N-1)}{2}}=N!/b_{N}
.

One can also use the heat kernel to find the integral representation of Eq. (
211
) and the one-matrix version of Eq. (
216
), again obtained by setting
V_{1}(x)=\frac{x^{2}}{2}
. Applying the
N
-dimensional version of Eq. (
253
) to Eq. (
211
) for each
x_{i}
we find

Z_{V}=\left(\frac{N}{2\pi}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}\left[\frac{1}{\Delta(\mathbf{x})}\int\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}\,\textrm{e}^{-\frac{N(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})^{2}}{2}}\Delta(\mathbf{y})\,e^{NV(\mathbf{y})}\right]_{\mathbf{x}=0}.

(256)

Similarly, in the one-matrix version of Eq. (
216
) we can use the heat kernel to find

Z_{x^{2}/2,V_{2}}=\left[\frac{1}{\Delta(\mathbf{x})}\det\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\mathrm{d}y\,\textrm{e}^{-\frac{(x_{i}-y)^{2}}{2}}y^{j-1}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}y\right)}\right\}_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\right]_{\mathbf{x}=0}.

(257)

(b) Application to new orthogonal polynomial methods

As we have seen at the beginning of the present section, the computations of Sec.
IV. 2
in the case where
V_{1}(x)=\frac{x^{2}}{2}
, prove the equivalence in the differential formulation between Eq. (
247
) and Eq. (
248
), and the latter expression allows using orthogonal polynomials Eq. (
249
) instead of biorthogonal polynomials Eq. (
250
). One may naturally wonder if for potentials
V_{1}
of degree higher than two, our new expressions Eq. (
239
), Eq. (
240
) could also allow the use of orthogonal polynomials instead of biorthogonal polynomials for two-matrix models.

To this aim, we reorganize the lines of the matrix
M
in Eq. (
239
) according to their remainder modulo
p-1
, that is:

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=h_{N}\det\left\{\tilde{M}_{i,j}\right\}_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1},\quad\mathrm{where}\quad\tilde{M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}T_{0}\\
\hline\cr\vdots\\
\hline\cr T_{p-1}\end{array}\right)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad(T_{s})_{r,j}=M_{(p-1)r+s\;,\;j}.

(258)

where
h_{N}=f_{N}\,\mathrm{sgn}(N,p)
,
\mathrm{sgn}(N,p)
being the parity of the permutation of rows, and we recall (Eq. (
239
)) that in differential formulation,

M_{(p-1)r+s\;,\;j}=({N^{p-1}}/{\alpha_{p}})^{r}\left[\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x})}x^{r}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{s}}{\mathrm{d}x^{s}}\Bigl[x^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}\Bigr]\right]_{x=0}.

(259)

If
s_{0}
and
r_{0}
are respectively the remainder and the quotient of the Euclidean division of
N-1
by
p-1
, then for
0\leq s\leq s_{0}
, the matrix
T_{s}
has
r_{0}+1
lines, while for
s_{0}<s<p-1
,
T_{s}
has
r_{0}
lines.

By reorganizing the columns of
\tilde{M}
, and the lines of
T_{s}
independently for each
s
, we may replace
({N^{p-1}}/{\alpha_{p}})^{-r}(T_{s})_{r,j}=({N^{p-1}}/{\alpha_{p}})^{-r}M_{(p-1)r+s\;,\;j}
in the determinant by

\left[\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x})}P_{r}^{(s)}(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}^{s}}{\mathrm{d}x^{s}}\Bigl[Q_{j}(x)\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}\Bigr]\right]_{x=0}=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y}{2\pi/\imath}\,\textrm{e}^{-xy}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}y)}P_{r}^{(s)}(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}^{s}}{\mathrm{d}x^{s}}\Bigl[Q_{j}(x)\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}\Bigr],

(260)

where for
0\leq s<p-1
, the
P_{r}^{(s)}(x)
are monic polynomials of degree
r
, and
Q_{j}
is a monic polynomial of degree
j
.

One may for instance choose
P_{r}^{(s)}=Q_{r}
for all
0\leq s<p-1
. We use the following notation for the determinant obtained this way:

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=h_{N}\det\left\{W_{i,j}\right\}_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1},\quad\mathrm{where}\quad W=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}J_{0}\\
\hline\cr\vdots\\
\hline\cr J_{p-1}\end{array}\right).

(261)

For
s=0
, the elements
(J_{0})_{r,j}
of the resulting matrix
J_{0}
define the symmetric bilinear form

\langle Q_{r}\mid\mid Q_{j}\rangle=\left[\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x})}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}Q_{r}(x)Q_{j}(x)\right]_{x=0}=\frac{\imath}{2\pi}\int\mathrm{d}x\,Q_{r}(x)Q_{j}(x)\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}\int\mathrm{d}y\,\textrm{e}^{-xy}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}y)},

(262)

and requiring
\langle Q_{r}\mid\mid Q_{j}\rangle=h_{r}\delta_{r,j}
defines a family of orthogonal polynomials for the following weight:

\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x})}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}\quad\leftrightarrow\quad\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}\int\mathrm{d}y\,\textrm{e}^{-xy}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}y)}.

(263)

In addition to the usual three-terms recurrence
Q_{n+1}(x)=(x-\beta_{n})Q_{n}-\frac{h_{n}}{h_{n-1}}Q_{n-1}
satisfied by any family of orthogonal polynomials, to obtain families of recurrence relations on the coefficients
\beta_{n}
and
h_{n}
, one may for instance use the fact that

\displaystyle\left[\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x})}V_{1}^{\prime}\Bigl(\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\Bigr)F(x)\right]_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\left[\left[\textrm{e}^{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}}V_{1}^{\prime}\Bigl(\frac{1}{N}y\Bigr)\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}y)}\right]_{y=0}F(x)\right]_{x=0}

(264)

\displaystyle=\left[\left[\textrm{e}^{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}y}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}y)}\right]_{y=0}F(x)\right]_{x=0},

(265)

so that

\left[\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x})}V_{1}^{\prime}\Bigl(\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\Bigr)F(x)\right]_{x=0}=\left[\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x})}xF(x)\right]_{x=0},

(266)

applied to
F(x)=Q_{r}(x)Q_{j}(x)\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(x)}
for any
r,j
. For instance for the case
V_{1}(x)=x^{3}/3
, for which the weight for the orthogonal polynomials includes the Airy function as a factor, this equation
leads to the family of equations

\frac{2}{N}\langle V_{2}^{\prime}Q_{r}^{\prime}\mid\mid Q_{j}\rangle+\frac{2}{N}\langle V_{2}^{\prime}Q_{r}\mid\mid Q_{j}^{\prime}\rangle+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\Bigl(\langle Q_{r}^{\prime\prime}\mid\mid Q_{j}\rangle+\langle Q_{r}\mid\mid Q_{j}^{\prime\prime}\rangle+2\langle Q_{r}^{\prime}\mid\mid Q_{j}^{\prime}\rangle\Bigr)+\langle R\,Q_{r}\mid\mid Q_{j}\rangle=0,

(267)

with
R(x)=\frac{1}{N}V_{2}^{\prime\prime}(x)+(V_{2}^{\prime}(x))^{2}-x
.

The rest of the determinant Eq. (
261
) for
s>0
, obtained from Eq. (
260
), also involves sums of terms of the form
\langle g\,Q_{r}\mid\mid Q_{j}^{(k)}\rangle
for some polynomials
g(x)
. Computing the determinant therefore requires knowledge about the derivatives of the families of orthogonal polynomials with weight Eq. (
263
). It is not clear at this point whether the family of orthogonal polynomials can indeed be constructed and whether the rest of the determinant can be computed this way.

On the other hand, everything seems to simplify drastically at large
N
: for the choice
P_{r}^{(s)}=Q_{r}
for all
0\leq s<p-1
, the leading contributions in
N
to the matrix elements Eq. (
260
) for the part of the determinant corresponding to
s>0
are obtained when all
s
derivatives act on
e^{NV_{2}(x)}
, raising a factor
N^{s}(V_{2}^{\prime}(x))^{s}
, so that the elements of the determinant simplify to

(J_{s})_{r,j}\sim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N^{(p-1)r+s}}{\alpha_{p}^{r}}\left\langle(V_{2}^{\prime})^{s}Q_{r}\mid\mid Q_{j}\right\rangle.

(268)

In the same way, the leading contribution to the family of relations Eq. (
266
) seems to be given by

\left[\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x})}e^{NV_{2}(x)}V_{1}^{\prime}\bigl(V_{2}^{\prime}(x)\bigr)Q_{r}(x)Q_{j}(x)\right]_{x=0}=\left[\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x})}xF(x)\right]_{x=0},\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\langle UQ_{r}\mid\mid Q_{j}\rangle=0,

(269)

with
U(x)=V_{1}^{\prime}\bigl(V_{2}^{\prime}(x)\bigr)-x
.
However, this already goes beyond the original scope of the paper, and we leave this for future work.

Appendix A
Normalizations of the differential formulations

Normalization of the differential formulation of diagonalized one-matrix models

The normalization
e_{N}
in Eq. (
212
) is computed developing the Vandermonde determinants as in Sec.
II. 2. (d)
:

\begin{split}\frac{1}{e_{N}}&=\left[\textrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2N}(\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{x}})^{2}}\Delta^{2}(\mathbf{x})\right]_{\mathbf{x}=0}\\
&=N!\det_{i,j}\left\{\left[\textrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2N}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}x^{2}}}x^{i+j}\right]_{x=0}\right\}\\
&=N!\det_{i,j}\Biggl\{\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(2N)^{-n}}{n!}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2n}}{\mathrm{d}x^{2n}}x^{i+j}\right]_{x=0}\Biggr\},\end{split}

(270)

where the indices in the determinants range from 0 to
N-1
. The bracket in the rightmost expression is non-vanishing only if
2n=i+j
, in which case
\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2n}}{\mathrm{d}x^{2n}}x^{i+j}\right]_{x=0}=(i+j)!
. Therefore,

1/{e_{N}}=N!\det(R)(2N)^{-N(N-1)/2},

where the
N\times N
matrix
R
is such that
R_{ij}=0
for
i+j
odd, and
R_{ij}=(i+j)!/[(i+j)/2]!
for
i+j
even. This determinant can be computed to be
\det(R)=2^{N(N-1)/2}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}j!
, so that

e_{N}=N^{N(N-1)/2}/\prod_{j=1}^{N}j!.

Normalization of the differential formulation of diagonalized two-matrix models

The normalization is the same as for the one-matrix model, since:

\displaystyle\left[\textrm{e}^{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial a_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial b_{i}}}\,\Delta(\mathbf{a})\Delta(\mathbf{b})\right]_{\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{b}=0}

\displaystyle=N!\det_{i,j}\left\{\left[\textrm{e}^{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\frac{\partial}{\partial b}}\,a^{i}b^{j}\right]_{a=b=0}\right\}

(271)

\displaystyle=N!\det_{i,j}\Biggl\{\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(N)^{-n}}{n!}\left[\frac{d^{n}}{da^{n}}a^{i}\right]_{a=0}\left[\frac{d^{n}}{db^{n}}b^{j}\right]_{b=0}\Biggr\}

(272)

\displaystyle=N!\det_{i,j}\Biggl\{\delta_{i,j}i!N^{-i}\Biggr\}=\frac{1}{e_{N}}.

(273)

Appendix B
Alternative representations
using generalized heat kernels

In Sec.
IV. 3. (a)
, we have detailed how, for one-matrix models, the Slater determinant form Eq. (
251
) in the integral formulation can be recovered directly from that in differential formulation Eq. (
252
) by using the heat kernel formula Eq. (
253
).

In this appendix, we detail for completeness the analogous computation for two-matrix models, that is, the integral analogues of expressions Eq. (
213
), Eq. (
216
), and Eq. (
218
) using generalized heat kernels. Together these form the back lower edge of the diagram shown in Fig.
2
.

Our starting point is the differential representation Eq. (
213
):

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=\left[\frac{1}{\Delta(\mathbf{x})}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{x}})}\Delta(\mathbf{x})\,\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\mathbf{x})}\right]_{\mathbf{x}=0}\,.

(274)

In Eq. (
253
), we used the heat kernel
K_{t}(x)
to transform a differential representation of the one- model to an integral form. For two-matrix models, we can use the following analogous identity (see e.g.
Robinson
):

\textrm{e}^{W(\frac{d}{d\mathbf{x}})}F(\mathbf{x})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}d\mathbf{y}\,K_{W}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})F(\mathbf{y}),

(275)

where
W
is a polynomial, and
K_{W}
is a generalized heat kernel with the following Fourier representation:

K_{W}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}d\mathbf{p}\,\textrm{e}^{\imath\mathbf{p}\cdot(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})+W(\imath\mathbf{p})}.

(276)

If we set
W(\mathbf{x})=NV_{1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\mathbf{x}\right)
and
F(\mathbf{x})=\Delta(\mathbf{x})e^{NV_{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\mathbf{x}\right)}
, we can substitute Eq. (
275
) in Eq. (
274
) to find

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N}}\left[\frac{1}{\Delta(\mathbf{x})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{p}\,\textrm{e}^{\imath\mathbf{p}\cdot(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})+NV_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\imath\mathbf{p})}\Delta(\mathbf{y})\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\mathbf{y})}\right]_{\mathbf{x}=0}.

(277)

This is the integral analogue of Eq. (
213
). Subsequently, we can rewrite this expression as a Slater determinant by expanding the Vandermonde determinant, just like we did when rewriting Eq. (
213
) to Eq. (
216
), thus obtaining:

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{N}}\left[\frac{1}{\Delta(\mathbf{x})}\det\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}p\,\textrm{e}^{\imath p(x_{i}-y)+NV_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\imath p)}y^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}y)}\right\}_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1}\right]_{\mathbf{x}=0}.

(278)

Finally, we can use identity Eq. (
217
) to obtain

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=\frac{f_{N}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\det\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}p\,\left[\frac{\partial^{i}}{\partial x^{i}}\textrm{e}^{\imath p(x-y)}\right]_{x=0}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\imath p)}y^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}y)}\right\}_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1},

(279)

where the bracket evaluating
x
at 0 could now be moved inside the determinant and the integral. This equation mirrors Eq. (
218
) using the generalized heat kernel.
Carrying out the differentiation and evaluating
x
at 0:

Z_{V_{1},V_{2}}=\frac{f_{N}}{(2\pi)^{N}}\det\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}y\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}p\,(\imath p)^{i}\textrm{e}^{-\imath py}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\imath p)}y^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}y)}\right\}_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1}.

(280)

To verify that the equation is well-normalized we may use Eq. (
173
) to carry out the integration of the matrix elements. There is a subtlety however: Eq. (
171
) and Eq. (
173
) have to be modified for
\alpha\in\imath\mathbb{R}
.
For
n=m=0
and
\alpha\in\imath\mathbb{R}
, Eq. (
171
) should be replaced with:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\textrm{e}^{-\imath\alpha Nxy}=\frac{2\pi}{\alpha N},

(281)

and for positive
n
or
m
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\textrm{e}^{-\imath\alpha Nxy}\,x^{n}y^{m}=\frac{\delta_{n,m}}{(-\imath N)^{n}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial\alpha^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\textrm{e}^{-\imath\alpha Nxy}=\frac{\delta_{n,m}}{(-\imath N)^{n}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial\alpha^{n}}\frac{2\pi}{\alpha N},

(282)

so Eq. (
173
) has to be modified for:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\,\textrm{e}^{-\imath\alpha Nxy}\,x^{n}y^{m}=\delta_{n,m}\,2\pi\imath\,\left(\frac{1}{\imath\alpha N}\right)^{n+1}n!.

(283)

These expressions allow showing that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}p\,\textrm{e}^{-\imath py}(\imath p)^{i}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\imath p)}y^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}y)}=\imath\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\,\textrm{e}^{-xy}x^{i}\textrm{e}^{NV_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}x)}y^{j}\textrm{e}^{NV_{2}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}y)},

(284)

in the sense that their perturbative expansions match, where Eq. (
283
) has to be used to expand the left hand side and Eq. (
173
) for the right hand side, which explains the factor
\imath
, while
\imath^{-1}
would naively be expected as a Jacobian by directly changing variables for
x=\imath p
in the integral. Finally, changing variables for
\sqrt{N}x^{\prime}=x
and
\sqrt{N}y^{\prime}=y
, we recover the integral Slater determinant form Eq. (
181
), since
\frac{f_{N}}{(2\pi)^{N}}N^{N(N+1)/2}\imath^{N}=\frac{1}{d_{N}}
.

From right ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated categories to n n -exangulated categories
Jian He and Panyue Zhou

Abstract
. The notion of right semi-equivalence in a right ( n + 2 ) ({n+2}) -angulated category is defined in this article. Let 𝒞 \mathscr{C} be an n n -exangulated category and 𝒳 \mathscr{X} is a strongly covariantly finite subcategory of 𝒞 \mathscr{C} . We prove that the standard right ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated category 𝒞 / 𝒳 \mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X} is right semi-equivalence under a natural assumption. As an application, we show that a right ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated category has an n n -exangulated structure if and only if the suspension functor is right semi-equivalence. Besides, we also prove that an n n -exangulated category 𝒞 \mathscr{C} has the structure of a right ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated category with right semi-equivalence if and only if for any object X ∈ 𝒞 X\in\mathscr{C} , the morphism X → 0 X\to 0 is a trivial inflation.

1.
Introduction

Triangulated categories and exact categories are two fundamental structures in algebra, geometry and topology.
They are also important tools in many mathematical branches.
Nakaoka and Palu
[
NP
]
recently introduced the notion
of extriangulated categories, whose extriangulated structures are given by
\mathbb{E}
-triangles with some axioms. Triangulated categories and exact categories are extriangulated categories. There are a lot of examples of extriangulated categories which are neither triangulated categories nor exact categories, see
[
NP
,
ZZ
,
HZZ2
,
NP1
,
T
]
. Beligiannis and Marmaridis
[
BM
]
defined the notion of right triangulated category. Informally, a right triangulated category is a triangulated category whose suspension functor is not necessarily an equivalence. Subsequently, in order to research some homological properties, the notion of a right triangulated category with right semi-equivalence was introduced by Assem, Beligiannis and Marmaridis
[
ABM
]
, these are the right triangulated category whose suspension functor is fully faithful and right dense. We need to pay attention to this relation: a triangulated category is a right triangulated category with right semi-equivalence, but the opposite is not necessarily true, see Example
3.5
.
Recently, Tattar
[
T
]
proved a right triangulated category with right semi-equivalence is an extriangulated category. More specifically, Tattar
[
T
]
gave a characterization of right triangulated categories which are extriangulated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
T
, Theorem A]

Let
(\mathscr{C},\Sigma,\nabla)
be a right triangulated category. Then
\mathscr{C}
has an extriangulated structure if and only if the suspension functor
\Sigma
is right semi-equivalence.

Moreover, Tattar also gave a characterization of extriangulated categories which are right triangulated with right semi-equivalences.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
T
, Theorem B]

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an extriangulated category. Then
\mathscr{C}
has a right triangulated structure and the suspension functor is right semi-equivalence if and only if for any object
C\in\mathscr{C}
, the morphism
C\rightarrow 0
is an inflation.

In
[
GKO
]
, Geiss, Keller and Oppermann introduced
a new type of categories, called
(n+2)
-angulated categories, which generalize triangulated categories: the classical triangulated categories are the special case
n=1
. Lin
[
L
]
introduced right
(n+2)
-angulated categories, the right triangulated categories are the special case
n=1
. Later, Jasso
[
J
]
introduced
n
-exact categories which are categories inhabited by certain exact sequences with
n+2
terms terms, called
n
-exact sequences. The case
n=1
corresponds to the usual concepts of exact categories. Recently, Herschend, Liu and Nakaoka
[
HLN
]
introduced the notion of
n
-exangulated categories. It should be noted that the case
n=1
corresponds to extriangulated categories. As typical examples we have that
n
-exact and
(n+2)
-angulated categories are
n
-exangulated, see
[
HLN
, Proposition 4.5 and Proposition 4.34]
. However, there are some other examples of
n
-exangulated categories which are neither
(n+2)
-angulated nor
n
-exact, see
[
HLN
,
LZ1
,
HZZ1
]
.

Motivated by the definition of a right triangulated category with
right semi-equivalence and the Tattar’s idea, we define right
(n+2)
-angulated categories with right semi-equivalences (see Definition
3.1
), and prove the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

(
see Theorem
3.12
for details
)

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an
n
-exangulated category and
\mathscr{X}
be a strongly covariantly finite subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
. Then
\mathscr{C}/\mathscr{X}
is a right
(n+2)
-angulated category with right semi-equivalence under a reasonable assumption.

We also show that Tattar’s result has a higher counterpart:

Theorem 1.4
.

(
see Corollary
4.5
for details
)

Let
(\mathscr{C},\Sigma^{n},\Theta)
be a right
(n+2)
-angulated category. Then
\mathscr{C}
has the natural structure of an
n
-exangulated category if and only if the
n
-suspension functor
\Sigma^{n}
is right semi-equivalence.

Moreover, we also gives a way to characterize which
n
-exangulated categories induces
right
(n+2)
-angulated categories with right semi-equivalences.

Theorem 1.5
.

(
see Theorem
4.3
for details
)

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an
n
-exangulated category. Then
\mathscr{C}
induces an

(n+2)
-angulated structure with right semi-equivalence if and only if for any
object
C\in\mathscr{C}
, the morphism
C\to 0
is a trivial inflation.

This article is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review some elementary definitions and facts on
n
-exangulated categories and right
(n+2)
-angulated categories.
In Section 3, we define a notion of right semi-equivalences for right
(n+2)
-angulated categories,
and we give the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
In Section 4, we prove Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.5
.

2.1.
n
-exangulated categories

For any pair of objects

\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)

\mathbb{E}
-extension

{}_{A}\delta_{C}

{}_{A}0_{C}=0\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)

\mathbb{E}
-extension
. For any pair of
\mathbb{E}
-extensions
{}_{A}\delta_{C}
and
{}_{A^{\prime}}\delta{{}^{\prime}}_{C^{\prime}}

\delta\oplus\delta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{E}(C\oplus C^{\prime},A\oplus A^{\prime})
be the
element corresponding to
(\delta,0,0,{\delta}{{}^{\prime}})
through the natural isomorphism
\mathbb{E}(C\oplus C^{\prime},A\oplus A^{\prime})\simeq\mathbb{E}(C,A)\oplus\mathbb{E}(C,A^{\prime})\oplus\mathbb{E}(C^{\prime},A)\oplus\mathbb{E}(C^{\prime},A^{\prime})
.

For any
a\in\mathscr{C}(A,A^{\prime})
and
c\in\mathscr{C}(C^{\prime},C)
,
\mathbb{E}(C,a)(\delta)\in\mathbb{E}(C,A^{\prime})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathbb{E}(c,A)(\delta)\in\mathbb{E}(C^{\prime},A)
are simply denoted by
a_{\ast}\delta
and
c^{\ast}\delta
, respectively.

Let
{}_{A}\delta_{C}
and
{}_{A^{\prime}}\delta{{}^{\prime}}_{C^{\prime}}
be any pair of
\mathbb{E}
-extensions. A
morphism

(a,c)\colon\delta\to{\delta}{{}^{\prime}}
of extensions is a pair of morphisms
a\in\mathscr{C}(A,A^{\prime})
and
c\in\mathscr{C}(C,C^{\prime})
in
\mathscr{C}
, satisfying the equality

a_{\ast}\delta=c^{\ast}{\delta}{{}^{\prime}}
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
HLN
, Definition 2.7]

Let
\bf{C}_{\mathscr{C}}
be the category of complexes in
\mathscr{C}
. As its full subcategory, define
{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}
to be the category of complexes in
\mathscr{C}
whose components are zero in the degrees outside of
\{0,1,\ldots,n+1\}
. Namely, an object in
{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}
is a complex
X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}}=\{X_{i},d^{X}_{i}\}
of the form

X_{0}\xrightarrow{d^{X}_{0}}X_{1}\xrightarrow{d^{X}_{1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{d^{X}_{n-1}}X_{n}\xrightarrow{d^{X}_{n}}X_{n+1}.

We write a morphism
f^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}}\colon X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}}\to Y^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}}
simply
f^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}}=(f^{0},f^{1},\ldots,f^{n+1})
, only indicating the terms of degrees
0,\ldots,n+1
.

Definition 2.2
.

[
HLN
, Definition 2.11]

By Yoneda lemma, any extension
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
induces natural transformations

\delta_{\sharp}\colon\mathscr{C}(-,C)\Rightarrow\mathbb{E}(-,A)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \delta^{\sharp}\colon\mathscr{C}(A,-)\Rightarrow\mathbb{E}(C,-).

For any
X\in\mathscr{C}
, these
(\delta_{\sharp})_{X}
and
\delta^{\sharp}_{X}
are given as follows.

(1)

(\delta_{\sharp})_{X}\colon\mathscr{C}(X,C)\to\mathbb{E}(X,A)\ :\ f\mapsto f^{\ast}\delta
.

(2)

\delta^{\sharp}_{X}\colon\mathscr{C}(A,X)\to\mathbb{E}(C,X)\ :\ g\mapsto g_{\ast}\delta
.

We simply denote
(\delta_{\sharp})_{X}(f)
and
\delta^{\sharp}_{X}(g)
by
\delta_{\sharp}(f)
and
\delta^{\sharp}(g)
, respectively.

Definition 2.3
.

[
HLN
, Definition 2.9]

Let
\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},n
be as before. Define a category
\AE:=\AE^{n+2}_{(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E})}
as follows.

(1)

A pair
\langle X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}},\delta\rangle
is an object of the category
\AE
with
X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}}\in{\bf{C}}^{n+2}_{\mathscr{C}}

and
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(X_{n+1},X_{0})
, called an
\mathbb{E}
-attached
complex of length
n+2
, if it satisfies

(d_{0}^{X})_{\ast}\delta=0~~\textrm{and}~~(d^{X}_{n})^{\ast}\delta=0.

We also denote it by

X_{0}\xrightarrow{d_{0}^{X}}X_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}^{X}}\cdots\xrightarrow{d_{n-2}^{X}}X_{n-1}\xrightarrow{d_{n-1}^{X}}X_{n}\xrightarrow{d_{n}^{X}}X_{n+1}\overset{\delta}{\dashrightarrow}.

(2)

For such pairs
\langle X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}},\delta\rangle
and
\langle Y^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}},\rho\rangle
,
f^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}}\colon\langle X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}},\delta\rangle\to\langle Y^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}},\rho\rangle
is
defined to be a morphism in
\AE
if it satisfies
(f_{0})_{\ast}\delta=(f_{n+1})^{\ast}\rho
.

Definition 2.4
.

[
HLN
, Definition 2.13]

An
n
-exangle
is an object
\langle X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}},\delta\rangle
in
\AE
that satisfies the listed conditions.

(1)

The following sequence of functors
\mathscr{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{Ab}
is exact.

\mathscr{C}(-,X_{0})\xrightarrow{\mathscr{C}(-,\ d^{X}_{0})}\cdots\xrightarrow{\mathscr{C}(-,\ d^{X}_{n})}\mathscr{C}(-,X_{n+1})\xrightarrow{~\delta_{\sharp}~}\mathbb{E}(-,X_{0})

(2)

The following sequence of functors
\mathscr{C}\to\mathsf{Ab}
is exact.

\mathscr{C}(X_{n+1},-)\xrightarrow{\mathscr{C}(d^{X}_{n},\ -)}\cdots\xrightarrow{\mathscr{C}(d^{X}_{0},\ -)}\mathscr{C}(X_{0},-)\xrightarrow{~\delta^{\sharp}~}\mathbb{E}(X_{n+1},-)

In particular any
n
-exangle is an object in
\AE
.
A
morphism of
n
-exangles
simply means a morphism in
\AE
. Thus

-exangles form a full subcategory of
\AE
.

Definition 2.5
.

[
HLN
, Definition 2.22]

Let
\mathfrak{s}
be a correspondence which associates a homotopic equivalence class
\mathfrak{s}(\delta)=[{}_{A}X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}}_{C}]

\delta={}_{A}\delta_{C}
. Such

is called a

(R0)

For any morphism of extensions

In such a case, we simple say that “

Moreover, a realization

(R1)

For any

(R2)

For any

Note that the above condition (R1) does not depend on representatives of the class

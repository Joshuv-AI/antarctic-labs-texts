A note on the action of the primitive Milnor operations on the Dickson invariants
Nguyễn Sum

Abstract
. In this paper, we present a formula for the action of the primitive Milnor operations on generators of algebra of invariants of the general linear group G ​ L n = G ​ L ​ ( n , 𝔽 p ) {GL_{n}=GL(n,\mathbb{F}_{p})} in the polynomial algebra P n = 𝔽 p ​ [ x 1 , x 2 , … , x n ] P_{n}=\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}] with p p an odd prime number.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 55S10; Secondary 55S05

1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime number. Denote by
GL_{n}=GL(n,\mathbb{F}_{p})
the general linear group over the prime field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
of
p
elements. This group acts on the polynomial
P_{n}=\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
in the usual manner. We grade
P_{n}
by assigning
\dim x_{j}=1
for
p=2
and
\dim x_{j}=2
for
p>2
.
Dickson showed in
[
2
]
that the invariant algebra
P_{n}^{GL_{n}}
is a polynomial algebra generated by invariants
Q_{n,s}
,
0\leqslant s<n
, which are called the Dickson invariants.

Let
\mathcal{A}(p)
be the mod
p
Steenrod algebra and denote by

St^{R}\in\mathcal{A}(p)
the Milnor operation of type
R
, where
R
is a finite sequence of non-negative integers (see Milnor
[
4
]
, Mùi
[
5
,
6
]
). For
R=(k)
,
St^{(k)}
is the Steenrod operation
P^{k}
. For
\Delta_{i}=(0,\ldots,0,1)
of length
i
,
St^{\Delta_{i}}
is the primitive Milnor operation in
\mathcal{A}(p)
. This operation was denoted by
Q^{i}
in Adams and Wilkerson
[
1
]
.

The Steenrod algebra
\mathcal{A}(p)
acts on
P_{n}
by means of the Cartan formula together with the relations
\beta(x_{j})=0
and

\displaystyle P^{k}(x_{j})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}x_{j},&\mbox{if }k=0,\\
x_{j}^{p},&\mbox{if }k=1,\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{cases}

for
j=1,\ \!2,\ldots,n
(see Steenrod and Epstein
[
8
]
). Note that
P^{k}
is the Steenrod square
Sq^{k}
for
p=2
, and
\beta
is the Bockstein operation for
p>2
.
Since this action commutes with the one of
GL_{n}
, it induces an inherited action of
\mathcal{A}(p)
on
P_{n}^{GL_{n}}
.

The action of the Milnor operations on the modular invariants of linear groups has partially been studied by Smith and Switzer
[
7
]
, Wilkerson
[
14
]
and the present author
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
.

The purpose of the paper is to present a new formula for the action of the primitive Milnor operations
St^{\Delta_{i}}
on the Dickson invariants.

2.
Main Result

First of all, we introduce some notations. Let
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
be a sequence of non-negative integers. Following Dickson
[
2
]
, we define

[e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}]=\begin{vmatrix}x_{1}^{p^{e_{1}}}&\cdots&x_{n}^{p^{e_{1}}}\\
x_{1}^{p^{e_{2}}}&\cdots&x_{n}^{p^{e_{2}}}\\
\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
x_{1}^{p^{e_{n}}}&\cdots&x_{n}^{p^{e_{n}}}\end{vmatrix}.

Denote
L_{n,s}=[0,1,\ldots,\hat{s},\ldots,n],\,0\leqslant s\leqslant n,\ L_{n}=L_{n,n}=[0,1,\ldots,n-1].
Each
[e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}]
is divisible by
L_{n}
and
[e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n}]/L_{n}
is an
GL_{n}
-invariant. Then, Dickson invariants
Q_{n,s}
are defined by

\displaystyle Q_{n,s}

\displaystyle=L_{n,s}/L_{n},\ 0\leqslant s<n.

By convention,
Q_{n,s}=0
for
s<0
. Note that
Q_{n,0}=L_{n}^{p-1}
.

Theorem 2.1

(See Dickson
[
2
]
)
.

P_{n}^{GL_{n}}=\mathbb{F}_{p}[Q_{n,0},Q_{n,1},\ldots,Q_{n,n-1}].

The main result of the paper is the following.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any
0\leqslant s<n
and
i\geqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle St^{\Delta_{i}}(Q_{n,s})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}Q_{n,0}(P_{n,i,s}^{p}+R_{n,i}^{p}Q_{n,s}),

(2.1)

where
P_{n,i,0}=0
,
P_{n,i,s}=-[0,\ldots,\widehat{s-1},\ldots,n-1,i-1]/L_{n}
, for
s>0
and

R_{n,i}=[0,1,\ldots,n-2,i-1]/L_{n}.

Note that
R_{n,i}=-P_{n,i,n}
. The case
p=2
of (
2.1
) is also proved by Hưng
[
3
]
. It is used to explicitly compute the mod 2 Margolis homology of the Dickson algebra for any
i
.

We need the following results for the proof of the theorem.

Proposition 2.3

(See Smith-Switzer
[
7
]
, Wilkerson
[
14
]
)
.

For any
0\leqslant s<n
and
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
, we have

St^{\Delta_{i}}(Q_{n,s})=\begin{cases}(-1)^{s-1}Q_{n,0},&i=s>0,\\
(-1)^{n}Q_{n,0}Q_{n,s},&i=n,\\
0,\qquad&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The following proposition has been proved in
[
10
, Prop. 1.2]
for
p=2
and in
[
11
, Prop. 1.2]
for
p>2
.

Proposition 2.4
.

For any sequence
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
of non-negative integers, we have

[e_{1},\ldots,e_{n-1},e_{n}+n]=\sum_{s=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+s-1}[e_{1},\ldots,e_{n-1},e_{n}+s]Q_{n,s}^{p^{e_{n}}}.

Below is an extension of Proposition
2.3
.

Theorem 2.5
.

For any
0\leqslant s<n
and
i\geqslant 1
, we have

St^{\Delta_{i}}(Q_{n,s})=(-1)^{n}[0,1,\ldots,\hat{s},\ldots,n-1,i]L_{n}^{p-2}.

Proof.

The theorem has been proved in
[
13
, Thm 3.1]
for
p>2
. To make the paper self-contained, we give here a proof of it for
p
an arbitrary prime.

Since
L_{n,s}=L_{n}Q_{n,s}
and
St^{\Delta_{i}}
is a derivation, we have

St^{\Delta_{i}}(L_{n,s})=L_{n}St^{\Delta_{i}}(Q_{n,s})+Q_{n,s}St^{\Delta_{i}}(L_{n}).

(2.2)

From
[
10
, Thm 1.1]
for
p=2
and
[
11
, Thm 1.1]
for
p>2
, we obtain

St^{\Delta_{i}}(L_{n,s})=\begin{cases}[i,1,2,\ldots,\hat{s},\ldots,n],&s>0,\\
0,&s=0.\end{cases}

In particular,
St^{\Delta_{i}}(L_{n})=[i,1,2,\ldots,n-1]
.

If
s=0
, then
St^{\Delta_{i}}(L_{n,s})=0
and

\displaystyle St^{\Delta_{i}}(L_{n})

\displaystyle=[i,1,2,\ldots,n-1]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n-1}[1,2,\ldots,n-1,i].

Combining (
2.2
), the above equalities and the relation
Q_{n,0}=L_{n}^{p-1}
, we have

\displaystyle St^{\Delta_{i}}(Q_{n,0})

\displaystyle=-St^{\Delta_{i}}(L_{n})Q_{n,0}/L_{n}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}[1,2,\ldots,n-1,i]Q_{n,0}/L_{n}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}[1,2,\ldots,n-1,i]L_{n}^{p-2}.

Hence, the theorem holds.

If
s>0
, then

St^{\Delta_{i}}(L_{n,s})=[i,1,2,\ldots,\hat{s},\ldots,n].

So, using Proposition
2.4
, we get

\displaystyle St^{\Delta_{i}}(L_{n,s})

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n-1+t}[i,1,2,\ldots,\hat{s},\ldots,n-1,t]Q_{n,t}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n-1}[i,1,2,\ldots,\hat{s},\ldots,n-1,0]Q_{n,0}

\displaystyle\qquad+(-1)^{n-1+s}[i,1,2,\ldots,\hat{s},\ldots,n-1,s]Q_{n,s}

\displaystyle=[i,1,2,\ldots,n-1]Q_{n,s}-[i,0,1,\ldots,\hat{s},\ldots,n-1]Q_{n,0}.

Combining (
2.2
), the above equalities and the relation
Q_{n,0}=L_{n}^{p-1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle St^{\Delta_{i}}(Q_{n,s})

\displaystyle=(St^{\Delta_{i}}(L_{n,s})-Q_{n,s}St^{\Delta_{i}}(L_{n}))/L_{n}

\displaystyle=-[i,0,1,2,\ldots,\hat{s},\ldots,n-1]Q_{n,0}/L_{n}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}[0,1,2,\ldots,\hat{s},\ldots,n-1,i]L_{n}^{p-2}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

We now prove Theorem
2.2
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

By Theorem
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle St^{\Delta_{i}}(Q_{n,0})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}[1,2,\ldots,n-1,i]L_{n}^{p-2}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\big([0,1,\ldots,n-2,i-1]/L_{n}\big)^{p}L_{n}^{2p-2}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}R_{n,i}^{p}Q_{n,0}^{2}.

Hence, the theorem is true for
s=0
.

Assume that
s>0
.
We prove the theorem by induction on
i
. By Proposition
2.3
, the theorem is true for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
. Suppose that
i\geqslant n
and the theorem holds for
1,2,\ldots,i
. Using Proposition
2.4
, Theorem
2.5
and the inductive hypothesis, we get

\displaystyle St^{\Delta_{i+1}}(Q_{n,s})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}[0,1,\ldots,\hat{s},\ldots,n-1,i+1]L_{n}^{p-2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{t-1}[0,\ldots,\hat{s},\ldots,n-1,i-n+1+t]Q_{n,t}^{p^{i-n+1}}L_{n}^{p-2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+t-1}St^{\Delta_{i-n+1+t}}(Q_{n,s})Q_{n,t}^{p^{i-n+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{t-1}Q_{n,0}(P_{n,i-n+1+t,s}^{p}+R_{n,i-n+1+t}^{p}Q_{n,s})Q_{n,t}^{p^{i-n+1}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}Q_{n,0}\Big(\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+t-1}P_{n,i-n+1+t,s}^{p}Q_{n,t}^{p^{i-n+1}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\Big(\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+t-1}R_{n,i-n+1+t}^{p}Q_{n,t}^{p^{i-n+1}}\Big)Q_{n,s}\Big).

Using Proposition
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}

\displaystyle(-1)^{n+t-1}P_{n,i-n+1+t,s}^{p}Q_{n,t}^{p^{i-n+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+t-1}\big(-[0,\ldots,\widehat{s-1},\ldots,n-1,i-n+t]/L_{n}\big)^{p}Q_{n,t}^{p^{i-n+1}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\Big(-\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+t-1}[0,\ldots,\widehat{s-1},\ldots,n-1,i-n+t]Q_{n,t}^{p^{i-n}}\Big)/L_{n}\Big)^{p}

\displaystyle=\big(-[0,\ldots,\widehat{s-1},\ldots,n-1,i]/L_{n}\big)^{p}=P_{n,i+1,s}^{p}.

By a similar computation using Proposition
2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}

\displaystyle(-1)^{n+t-1}R_{n,i-n+1+t}^{p}Q_{n,t}^{p^{i-n+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+t-1}\big([0,1,\ldots,n-2,i-n+t]/L_{n}\big)^{p}Q_{n,t}^{p^{i-n+1}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\Big(\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{n+t-1}[0,1,\ldots,n-2,i-n+t]Q_{n,t}^{p^{i-n}}\Big)/L_{n}\Big)^{p}

\displaystyle=\big([0,1,\ldots,n-2,i]/L_{n}\big)^{p}=R_{n,i+1}^{p}.

Thus, the theorem is true for
i+1
. So, the proof is completed.
∎

Using Theorem
2.2
and Proposition
2.4
, we can explicitly compute the action of
St^{\Delta_{i}}
on the Dickson invariants
Q_{n,s}
for
i>n
by explicitly computing
P_{n,i,s}
and
R_{n,i}
. The cases
i=n+1,\,n+2
have been computed in
[
13
]
by using Theorem
2.5
.

Corollary 2.6

(See
[
13
]
)
.

For
0\leqslant s<n
, we have

\displaystyle St^{\Delta_{n+1}}(Q_{n,s})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}Q_{n,0}(-Q_{n,s-1}^{p}+Q_{n,n-1}^{p}Q_{n,s}),

\displaystyle St^{\Delta_{n+2}}(Q_{n,s})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}Q_{n,0}\big(Q_{n,s-2}^{p^{2}}-Q_{n,s-1}^{p}Q_{n,n-1}^{p^{2}}+(Q_{n,n-1}^{p^{2}+p}-Q_{n,n-2}^{p^{2}})Q_{n,s}\big).

By a direct calculation using Proposition
2.4
, we easily obtain the following.

Corollary 2.7
.

For
0\leqslant s<n
, we have

St^{\Delta_{n+3}}(Q_{n,s})=(-1)^{n}Q_{n,0}(P_{n,n+3,s}^{p}+R_{n,n+3}^{p}Q_{n,s}),

where

\displaystyle P_{n,n+3,s}

\displaystyle=-Q_{n,s-3}^{p^{2}}+Q_{n,s-2}^{p}Q_{n,n-1}^{p^{2}}+Q_{n,s-1}Q_{n,n-2}^{p^{2}}-Q_{n,s-1}Q_{n,n-1}^{p^{2}+p},

\displaystyle R_{n,n+3}

\displaystyle=Q_{n,n-3}^{p^{2}}-Q_{n,n-2}^{p^{2}}Q_{n,n-1}-Q_{n,n-2}^{p}Q_{n,n-1}^{p^{2}}+Q_{n,n-1}^{p^{2}+p+1}.

Corollary 2.8
.

For any
0\leqslant s<n
and
i\geqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle St^{\Delta_{i}}(Q_{n,0}^{p-1}Q_{n,s})=(-1)^{n}Q_{n,0}^{p}P_{n,i,s}^{p}\in\mathbb{\mbox{}}{\rm Ker}(St^{\Delta_{i}}).

Proof.

Since
St^{\Delta_{i}}
is a derivation, using Theorem
2.2
, we get

\displaystyle St^{\Delta_{i}}(Q_{n,0}^{p-1}Q_{n,s})

\displaystyle=(p-1)Q_{n,0}^{p-2}St^{\Delta_{i}}(Q_{n,0})Q_{n,s}+Q_{n,0}^{p-1}St^{\Delta_{i}}(Q_{n,s})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\big(-Q_{n,0}^{p}R_{n,i}^{p}Q_{n,s}+Q_{n,0}^{p}(P_{n,i,s}^{p}+R_{n,i}^{p}Q_{n,s})\big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}Q_{n,0}^{p}P_{n,i,s}^{p}.

The corollary is proved.
∎

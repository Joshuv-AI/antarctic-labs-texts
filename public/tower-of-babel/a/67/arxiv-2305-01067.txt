The algebraic λ λ -calculus is a conservative extension of the ordinary λ λ -calculus
Axel Kerinec, Lionel Vaux Auclair

Abstract
The algebraic λ λ -calculus is an extension of the ordinary λ λ -calculus with linear combinations of terms. We establish that two ordinary λ λ -terms are equivalent in the algebraic λ λ -calculus iff they are β β -equal. Although this result was originally stated in the early 2000’s (in the setting of Ehrhard and Regnier’s differential λ λ -calculus), the previously proposed proofs were wrong: we explain why previous approaches failed and develop a new proof technique to establish conservativity.

0.2
Two non-proofs and a new approach

Recall that an
ARS
(abstract rewriting system) is a pair

(A,\leadsto)
of a set
A
and binary relation

\mathord{\leadsto}\subseteq A\times A
.
An
extension
of
(A,\leadsto)
is another ARS
(A^{\prime},\leadsto^{\prime})

such that
A\subseteq A^{\prime}
and
\mathord{\leadsto}\subseteq\mathord{\leadsto^{\prime}}
.
This extension is
conservative
if, for every
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
,

a_{1}\leadsto a_{2}
iff
a_{1}\leadsto^{\prime}a_{2}
.
An
equational system
is an ARS
(A,\sim)
such that
\sim

is an equivalence relation.
Our goal is thus to establish that the equational system

(\mathsf{R}\langle\Delta_{\mathsf{R}}\rangle,\leftrightarrow)
is a conservative extension of
(\Lambda,\leftrightarrow_{\Lambda})

— here we consider the injection

M\in\Lambda\mapsto\underline{M}\in\mathsf{R}\langle\Delta_{\mathsf{R}}\rangle

as an inclusion.

In their paper on the differential
λ
-calculus
[
er:difflamb
]
,
Ehrhard and Regnier claim that this follows directly from the confluence of

\mathrel{\widetilde{\to}}
, but this argument is not valid:

\mathrel{\widetilde{\to}}
does contain
\to_{\Lambda}
, and it is indeed confluent,
without any positivity assumption;
but we have already stated that

\leftrightarrow
is inconsistent in presence of negative coefficients,
so this observation cannot be sufficient.

Ehrhard and Regnier’s mistake is certainly an erroneous application
of a general conservativity result in Terese’s textbook
[
terese
]
,
missing the fact that Terese’s notion of extension is more demanding:
for Terese,
(A,\leadsto)
is a
sub-ARS
of
(A^{\prime},\leadsto^{\prime})
if

A\subseteq A^{\prime}
and, for every
a\in A
and
a^{\prime}\in A^{\prime}
,

a\leadsto^{\prime}a^{\prime}
iff
a^{\prime}\in A
and
a\leadsto a^{\prime}
.
The latter is strictly stronger than the mere inclusion

\mathord{\leadsto}\subseteq\mathord{\leadsto^{\prime}}
,
and is indeed sufficient to deduce conservativity
for the induced equational systems from the confluence of the super-ARS

[
terese
, Exercice 1.3.21 (iii)]
.
But
(\Lambda,\to_{\Lambda})
is not a sub-ARS of
(\mathsf{R}\langle\Delta_{\mathsf{R}}\rangle,\mathrel{\widetilde{\to}})
,
even when
\mathsf{R}
is positive:
for instance, if
\mathsf{R}=\mathbf{Q}^{+}
and
M\to_{\Lambda}M^{\prime}\not=M
,
we have
\underline{M}=\frac{1}{2}\underline{M}+\frac{1}{2}\underline{M}\mathrel{\widetilde{\to}}\frac{1}{2}\underline{M}+\frac{1}{2}\underline{M^{\prime}}\not\in\underline{\Lambda}
.
So one must design another approach.

Given
σ\in\mathsf{R}\langle\Delta_{\mathsf{R}}\rangle
, one can consider the
finite set of
λ
-terms
\Lambda(σ)\subset\Lambda

obtained by keeping exactly one element
in the support of each sum occurring in
σ

[
vaux:alglam
, Definition 3.18]
.
The second author tried to establish the conservativity of
\mathrel{\widetilde{\to}}^{*}

over
\to_{\Lambda}^{*}
by iterating the following:

Claim 0.2.1

(
[
vaux:alglam
, Lemma 3.20]
)
.

If
σ\mathrel{\widetilde{\to}}σ^{\prime}
and
M^{\prime}\in\Lambda(σ^{\prime})

then there exists
M\in\Lambda(σ)
such that

M\to_{\Lambda}^{*}M^{\prime}
.

But the latter claim is wrong!
Consider, for instance,

σ=(\lambda{x}.(\underline{x})\,\underline{x})\,(\underline{y}+\underline{z})\mathrel{\widetilde{\to}}σ^{\prime}=(\underline{y}+\underline{z})\,(\underline{y}+\underline{z})

We have
M^{\prime}=({y})\,{z}\in\Lambda(σ^{\prime})

but no term in
\Lambda(σ^{\prime})=\{(\lambda{x}.({x})\,{x})\,{y},(\lambda{x}.({x})\,{x})\,{z}\}

β
-reduces to
M^{\prime}
.
Note that, in this counter-example, there is no
M\in\Lambda

such that
\underline{M}\mathrel{\widetilde{\to}}^{*}σ
:
somehow, we must exploit this additional hypothesis
to establish a correct version of
0.2.1

Reasoning on
\mathrel{\widetilde{\to}}^{*}
directly is difficult,
due to its definition as a reflexive and transitive closure.
The technique we propose involves the definition of a mixed-type relation

between a
λ
-term
M
and a term
σ\in\mathsf{R}\langle\Delta_{\mathsf{R}}\rangle

intuitively,
M\Vdash σ
when

is obtained by pasting

and we say
M\Vdash σ
is a

of
such reductions.
In particular:
M\Vdash\underline{M^{\prime}}
as soon as

M\to_{\Lambda}^{*}M^{\prime}
;
and
M\Vdash σ+τ
as soon as

M\Vdash σ
and
M\Vdash τ
.
We then show that

is conservative over
\to_{\Lambda}^{*}

(

M\Vdash σ

as soon as
\underline{M}\mathrel{\widetilde{\to}}^{*}σ

(

Our whole approach thus rests on the careful definition of the mashup relation.
Among other requirements, it must behave well w.r.t. the structure of terms:

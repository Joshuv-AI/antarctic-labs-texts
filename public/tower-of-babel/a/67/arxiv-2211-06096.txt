Abstract
. As Goresky and MacPherson intersection homology is not the homology of a space, there is no preferred candidate for intersection homotopy groups. Here, they are defined as the homotopy groups of a simplicial set which P. Gajer associates to a couple ( X , p ¯ ) (X,\overline{p}) of a filtered space and a perversity. We first establish some basic properties for the intersection fundamental groups, as a Van Kampen theorem. For general intersection homotopy groups on Siebenmann CS sets, we prove a Hurewicz theorem between them and the Goresky and MacPherson intersection homology. If the CS set and its intrinsic stratification have the same regular part, we establish the topological invariance of the p ¯ \overline{p} -intersection homotopy groups. Several examples justify the hypotheses made in the statements. Finally, intersection homotopy groups also coincide with the homotopy groups of the topological space itself, for the top perversity on a connected, normal Thom-Mather space.

Introduction

Poincaré duality is an extraordinary property of manifolds. Trivial examples show that this feature disappears
when the space in consideration presents some singularities, even in the case of an amalgamation of manifolds
of different dimensions as in a complex of manifolds of Whitney (
[
35
]
).
Using an extra parameter
\overline{p}
called perversity, Goresky and MacPherson
[
17
,
18
]

define new homologies depending on the choice of a perversity and recreate a
Poincaré duality between some spaces with singularities, as the pseudomanifolds.

Many invariants and structures of differential or algebraic topology also
found their place in intersection homology or cohomology:
Morse theory, characteristic classes, Hodge theory, bordism, existence of cup products, foliations,…
(See
[
12
, Chapter 10]
for a documented list.)
From a homotopical point of view, the first observation is that intersection homology
is not a homotopy invariant.
On the other hand, F. Quinn (
[
27
]
) has given a presentation of some filtered spaces
which allows their study with homotopical tools.
Here, what we have in mind is a notion of intersection homotopy groups
which could be related to intersection homology, more or less as
the homotopy and homology groups of a space are.
In this direction, the first question is:
“Let
X
be a given filtered space and
\overline{p}
be a perversity.
Does there exist a topological space
Y
whose ordinary homology groups are the
\overline{p}
-intersection homology groups of
X
?”
As quoted by N. Habbegger in the introduction of
[
20
]
, his study of Thom operations
for intersection homology “destroys the hope of calculating intersection homology
as the ordinary homology of a suitable space, in general, since the Thom operations are natural.”

Nevertheless the well-established properties of the homology of a space encourage a search for substitutes,
i.e. the search for spaces
I^{\overline{p}}X
, associated to a pseudomanifold
X
and a
perversity
\overline{p}
, and which is “close” to
X
in a direction to be specified.
This is the approach of M. Banagl and we send the reader to
[
2
]

for an explicit description of this procedure and its properties; we will not use them in this work.
Briefly, the idea guiding Banagl’s construction is the replacement of the singular links by
a truncation of their Moore space decomposition.
In general, the homology of
I^{\overline{p}}X
is not the Goresky and MacPherson intersection homology
but satisfies a generalized Poincaré duality when
X
is closed and oriented.
The difficulty lies in the construction of the spaces
I^{\overline{p}}X
.
Their existence is established for pseudomanifolds with isolated singularities and
some cases of depth two pseudomanifolds in
[
2
,
3
,
11
]
.
In
[
1
]
, their construction is carried out for arbitrary depth but with trivial link bundles,
which covers the case of toric varieties.

Our approach is of a different spirit. We keep the Goresky and MacPherson intersection homology and
introduce intersection homotopy groups as homotopy groups of a space defined
by Gajer in
[
14
,
15
]
.
To explain that, it is better to come back at the beginning of the story.
Given a filtered space
X
and a perversity
\overline{p}
, a selection is made among the singular simplexes as follows.
A simplex
\sigma\colon\Delta\to X
is

\overline{p}
-allowable

if, for each singular stratum
S
, the set
\sigma^{-1}S
verifies

(0.1)

\dim\sigma^{-1}S\leq\dim\Delta-{\rm codim\,}S+\overline{p}(S).

A
\overline{p}
-allowable chain is a linear combination of
\overline{p}
-allowable simplexes.
These definitions present major shortcomings.

(1)

If
\xi
is a
\overline{p}
-allowable chain, its boundary
\partial\xi
is not necessary
\overline{p}
-allowable.

(2)

If
\sigma
is a
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex and
\partial_{i}
is a face operator,
the simplex
\partial_{i}\sigma
is not necessary
\overline{p}
-allowable.

If we are interested by homology, we do a more restrictive choice at the level of chains:
a singular chain
\xi
is of
\overline{p}
-intersection

if
\xi
and its boundary
\partial\xi
are
\overline{p}
-allowable (
[
17
]
).
The homology of the complex of singular chains of
\overline{p}
-intersection,
C_{*}^{\overline{p}}(X;G)
,
is the
\overline{p}
-intersection homology

H_{*}^{\overline{p}}(X;G)
, with coefficients in an
R
-module
G

over a Dedekind ring
R
.

If we are interested by simplicial sets,
we act on the simplexes themselves: a simplex
\sigma
is
\overline{p}
-full
if
\sigma
and all
its iterated faces are
\overline{p}
-allowable. This is the approach of
[
14
]
:
Gajer gets a Kan simplicial set that we
denote by
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X
and called
the Gajer space.

The
\overline{p}
-intersection homotopy groups
are now defined as the homotopy groups of
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X

and denoted by
\pi^{\overline{p}}_{*}(X)
.
What we do, in the present paper, is the study of these homotopy groups and their relation with intersection homology.
For the dimension of
\sigma^{-1}S
in (
0.1
), we are dealing with the polyhedral dimension of
[
14
]

(see Definition
1.11
), revisited in
[
9
]
.
In
[
14
]
, Gajer falsely believed that the homology of
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X
is the

\overline{p}
-intersection homology of
X
.
So in
[
14
]
, some properties as the existence of a Mayer-Vietoris sequence
for
H_{*}({\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X)
with open subsets of
X
appeared as a consequence of well-known properties of
intersection homology.
Also we work with topological filtered spaces and not PL-ones.
For these reasons, in Section
2
, we provide the proofs of basic properties,
with references to their first occurrence if we find one.

It’s time to clarify what we mean by perversity. In this work, a
perversity
is a map from the set of strata of a filtered space with values in
\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
,
taking the value 0 on the regular strata.
In particular, any map
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{Z}
such that
f(0)=0
defines a perversity
\overline{p}

by
\overline{p}(S)=f({\rm codim\,}S)
. Such perversities are said
codimensional
.
Among them are the original GM-perversities (
[
17
]
) of Goresky and MacPherson, see Definition
1.8
,
and the top perversity defined by
\overline{t}(i)=i-2
, for
i\geq 2
.

In the case of a filtered space
X
and a perversity
\overline{p}
, we establish a
Van Kampen theorem
for
\pi^{\overline{p}}_{1}(X)
in Theorem
4.1
.
If
X
is a connected normal Thom-Mather space with a finite number of strata and
\overline{t}
is the top perversity,
we deduce, in Corollary
4.2
,
the isomorphisms,
\pi_{j}^{\overline{t}}(X)\cong\pi_{j}(X)
, for any
j
.
This result enhances the well-known homology result of intersection homology,
H_{*}^{\overline{t}}(X)\cong H_{*}(X)
.

Our main results concern the locally conical filtered spaces, introduced by Siebenmann in
[
33
]
,
and called CS sets, see Definition
1.4
.
One of their first delicate peculiarities
is that links of points in the same stratum are
not necessarily homeomorphic (
[
12
, Example 2.3.6]
).
We know that these links have isomorphic intersection homology groups,
and we prove here that they also have isomorphic intersection homotopy groups, cf. Proposition
3.9
.

In Example
4.3
, we point out that the double suspension of the Poincaré sphere
is a counter-example to the topological invariance of
\overline{p}
-intersection fundamental groups.
In this example, some singular points of the CS set
X
become regular in the intrinsic stratification

X^{*}
of
X
, introduced by King in
[
23
]
.
The next result (see Theorem
4.6
and Theorem
5.1
) shows that,
except this generic case, the perverse homotopy groups are topological invariants.

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a CS set without stratum of codimension 1, of intrinsic stratification
\nu\colon X\to X^{*}
and

\overline{p}
be a Goresky and MacPherson perversity.
We suppose that the regular parts of
X
and
X^{*}
coincide.
Then the map
\nu
induces isomorphisms,

\pi_{j}^{\overline{p}}(X,x)\cong\pi_{j}^{\overline{p}}(X^{*},x)
,
for any regular point
x
and any
j
.

We also establish a
\overline{p}
-intersection analog of the Hurewicz theorem
(Theorem
6.1
) between intersection homotopy groups and Goresky and MacPherson intersection homology groups.
Example
6.8
justifies the hypotheses put on the links.
We call
\overline{p}
-intersection Hurewicz homomorphism
,

h^{\overline{p}}_{*}\colon\pi^{\overline{p}}_{*}(X,x)\to H_{*}^{\overline{p}}(X;\mathbb{Z}),

the composition of the Hurewicz map for
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X
with the homomorphism

H_{*}({\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X;\mathbb{Z})\to H_{*}^{\overline{p}}(X;\mathbb{Z})
coming
from the inclusion between the corresponding chain complexes.

Theorem B
.

Let
X
be a CS set and
\overline{p}
be a perversity such that
\pi_{0}^{\overline{p}}(X)=0
.

(1)

For any regular point
x
, the
\overline{p}
-intersection Hurewicz map

h^{\overline{p}}_{1}\colon\pi^{\overline{p}}_{1}(X,x)\to\widetilde{H}_{1}^{\overline{p}}(X;\mathbb{Z})

induces an isomorphism between the abelianisation of
\pi_{1}^{\overline{p}}(X,x)
and
\widetilde{H}_{1}^{\overline{p}}(X;\mathbb{Z})
.

(2)

Let
k\geq 2
.
We suppose

\pi_{j}^{\overline{p}}(X)=\pi_{j}^{\overline{p}}(L)=0
for every link
L
of
X
, and each
j\leq k-1
.
Then, the intersection Hurewicz homomorphism

h_{j}^{\overline{p}}\colon\pi_{j}^{\overline{p}}(X,x_{0})\to\widetilde{H}_{j}^{\overline{p}}(X;\mathbb{Z})

is an isomorphism for

and a surjection for

.

Some natural questions on
\overline{p}
-intersection homotopy groups follow from these results.
Let us list some of them.

\bullet

In the case of a cone on a compact space,
{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}X
, of apex

,
with the conical stratification,

where

\bullet

Let’s extend this list with:
“Can we relate the perverse Eilenberg-MacLane spaces introduced in

Let us conclude with a question about small resolutions, which has its source in
[

.
Recall that a small resolution of a complex algebraic variety is an algebraic map,

,
which is a resolution of singularities of

In

, the authors prove that a small resolution

For instance, under some connectedness conditions, can we find an integer

More precisely, we make the following conjecture.

.

Let

that there exists an integer

In the case of isolated singular points of a local complete intersection of complex dimension

1.
Background on filtered spaces and intersection homology

The singular spaces considered by Goresky and MacPherson in their pioneer works
(
[
17
,
18
]
), are
pseudomanifolds
.
They allow a Poincaré duality through intersection homology, based on a parameter called perversity.
The singular spaces presented in this section are general filtered spaces and the CS sets of Siebenmann
(
[
33
]
) well adaptated to proofs by induction.
We also make use of perversities defined on the poset of strata.

1.1.
Filtered spaces and CS sets

We denote by
{\mathrm{Top}}
the category of compactly generated spaces (henceforth called “space”)
with arrows the continuous maps.
Let us emphasize that our definition of compact space includes the Hausdorff property. We now enter in the filtered world.

Definition 1.1
.

A
filtered space of formal dimension
n
is a non-empty space
X

with a filtration,

X_{-1}=\emptyset\subseteq X_{0}\subseteq X_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq X_{n-2}\subseteq X_{n-1}\subsetneq X_{n}=X,

by closed subsets.
The subspaces
X_{i}
are called
skeleta
of the filtration and the
non-empty components of
X^{i}=X_{i}\backslash X_{i-1}
are the
strata
of
X
.
The set of strata is denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
(or
{\mathcal{S}}
if there is no ambiguity).
The subspace
\Sigma_{X}=X_{n-1}
(or
\Sigma
) is called the
singular subspace.

Its complementary,
X\backslash\Sigma_{X}
, is the
regular subspace
and the
strata in
X\backslash\Sigma_{X}
are called
regular.

The formal dimension of a stratum
S\subset X^{i}
is
\dim S=i
; its formal codimension is

{\rm codim\,}S=n-i
.
(The formal dimension is not necessarily related to any topological notion of dimension.)
The filtered space is of
locally finite stratification
if every point has a neighborhood that intersects only a finite number of strata.

A
stratified space
is a filtered space such that the closure of any stratum is a union of strata of lower dimension.
The set of strata of a stratified space is a poset,
({\mathcal{S}},\prec)
, for the relation

S_{0}\prec S_{1}
if
S_{0}\subset\overline{S}_{1}
.
The
depth of
X
is the greatest integer
m
for which it exists a chain of strata,

S_{0}\prec S_{1}\prec\cdots\prec S_{m}
.
We denote it by
{\mathrm{depth\,}}X=m
.

Let
X
be a filtered space of formal dimension
n
.
An open subset
U\subset X
is a filtered space for the
induced filtration
given by

U_{i}=U\cap X_{i}
.
The product
Y\times X
with a space
Y
is a filtered space for the
product filtration
defined by

\left(Y\times X\right)_{i}=Y\times X_{i}
.
If
X
is compact, the open cone

{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}X=X\times[0,1[\big/X\times\{0\}
is endowed with the

conical filtration
defined by

\left({\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}X\right)_{i}={\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}X_{i-1}
,
0\leq i\leq n+1
.
By convention,

{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}\,\emptyset=\{{\mathtt{v}}\}
, where
{\mathtt{v}}=[-,0]
is the apex of the cone.

Definition 1.2
.

A
stratified map

f\colon X\to Y
between two filtered spaces is a continuous map such that
for each stratum
S
of
X
there exists a stratum
S^{f}
of
Y
with
f(S)\subset S^{f}

and
{\rm codim\,}S^{f}\leq{\rm codim\,}S
.
A
stratified homeomorphism
is a homeomorphism such that
f
and
f^{-1}
are stratified maps.
We denote this relation by
X\cong_{s}Y
.

In this text, we will frequently encounter stratified maps
f
which are homeomorphisms but for which the reverse application

f^{-1}
is not stratified. We call them
homeomorphisms and stratified maps.

It is fundamental not to confuse them with stratified homeomorphisms.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
X,\,Y
be filtered spaces, we endow the cylinder
X\times[0,1]
with the product filtration,

(X\times[0,1])_{i}=X_{i}\times[0,1]
.
Two stratified maps,
f,\,g\colon X\to Y
, are
stratified homotopic
if there exists a stratified map

H\colon X\times[0,1]\to Y
such that

f=H(-,0)
and
g=H(-,1)
. We denote this relation by
f\simeq_{s}g
.
Two filtered spaces,
X
and
Y
, are
stratified homotopy equivalent

if there exist stratified maps

f\colon X\to Y
and
g\colon Y\to X
such that

g\circ f\simeq_{s}{\rm id}_{X}
,
f\circ g\simeq_{s}{\rm id}_{Y}
and
if
{\rm codim\,}S={\rm codim\,}S^{f}
,
{\rm codim\,}T={\rm codim\,}T^{g}
,
for any strata
S
of
X
and
T
of
Y
.
We denote this relation by
X\simeq_{s}Y
and the maps
f
and
g
are called

stratified homotopy equivalences.

A stratified homotopy equivalence,
f\colon X\to Y
, induces a bijection between the two sets of strata,

{\mathcal{S}}_{X}\cong{\mathcal{S}}_{Y}
, and verifies
S^{g\circ f}=S
,
T^{f\circ g}=T

see
[
12
, Remark 2.9.11]
.
Let us end these recalls with the CS sets, introduced by Siebenmann in
[
33
]
.

Definition 1.4
.

A
CS set
of dimension

X
of dimension

,
whose

-dimensional strata are
i

, and

, there exist

, endowed with the induced filtration,

,

and a stratified homeomorphism
\varphi\colon U\times{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L\to V
such that

(1.1)

\varphi(U\times{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L_{j})=V\cap X_{i+j+1},

for each

.
If
{\mathtt{v}}
is the apex of the cone
{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L
, the homeomorphism

\varphi(u,{\mathtt{v}})=u
, for each

.
The pair

of

of

.
The CS set

From the definition, we see that a CS set has a topological dimension. Moreover, in a CS set, the notions of formal and topological dimension coincide.

Remark 1.5
.

If
X
is a manifold with a structure of CS set, then any link of
X
is a homology sphere.
So, if the CS set
X
has no codimensional 1 strata, it is normal.
Let us see that.

Given a link
L
we have a stratified homeomorphism
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\times{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L\to V
,
where
V
is an open subset of
X
. Without loss of generality, we can suppose that
V
is homeomorphic to
an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
So, we have a topological embedding
\psi\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\times{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L={\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}(S^{k-1}*L)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}={\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}S^{n-1}

preserving the apexes. Following Stalling’s invertible cobordism, we get a homeomorphism
{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}(S^{k-1}*L)={\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}S^{n-1}

preserving the apexes (
[
23
, Proposition 1]
) and thus

is a homology sphere.

There can be some differences in the various definitions of CS sets in the literature.
In Definition
1.4
, the links of singular strata are supposed to be non-empty,
thus the open subset
X_{n}\backslash X_{n-1}
is dense.
This implies that, for each link
L
, the regular part,
L\backslash\Sigma_{L}
, is dense in
L
.
The links of a stratum are not uniquely defined but if one of them is connected, so all of them are too
(
[
12
, Remark 2.6.2]
).
A CS set is a stratified space (
[
5
, Appendix A2]
) and its strata are locally closed
and form a locally finite family (
[
12
, Lemma 2.3.8]
).

Remark 1.6
.

Proceeding as in
[
12
, Lemma 2.6.3]
, one can prove that
a normal CS set
X
is connected if, and only if, its regular part,
X\backslash\Sigma
, is connected. We can also
prove that
X
is normal if, and only if, the regular part
L\backslash\Sigma_{L}
of any link
L
is connected.

1.2.
Perversities

Intersection homology of Goresky and MacPherson is defined from a parameter, called perversity.
The original ones introduced in
[
17
]
depend only on the codimension of the strata.
More general perversities (
[
24
,
12
,
30
,
31
]
)
are defined on the set of strata.
In
[
4
]
, a blown-up cohomology is defined in this setting, to establish a
Poincaré duality for pseudomanifolds with a cap product in
[
6
,
32
]
.
Let us recall their definitions.

Definition 1.7
.

A
perversity on a filtered space,
X
,
is a map
\overline{p}\colon{\mathcal{S}}_{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{Z}}={\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{\pm\infty\}

taking the value 0 on the regular strata. The pair
(X,\overline{p})

is called a
perverse space
, or a
perverse CS set
if
X
is a CS set.

A
constant perversity

\overline{k}
, with
k\in\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
, is defined by
\overline{k}(S)=k
for any singular stratum
S
.
The
top perversity

\overline{t}
is
defined by
\overline{t}(S)={\rm codim\,}S-2
, if
S
is a singular stratum.
Given a perversity
\overline{p}
on
X
, the
complementary perversity
on
X
,
D\overline{p}
, is characterized
by
D\overline{p}+\overline{p}=\overline{t}
.

Any map
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{Z}
such that
f(0)=0
defines a perversity
\overline{p}

by
\overline{p}(S)=f({\rm codim\,}S)
. Such perversity is called
codimensional
.
In general, we denote by the same letter the perversity
\overline{p}
and the map
f
.
Among the codimensional perversities we find the original perversities of
[
17
]
.

Definition 1.8
.

A
Goresky and MacPherson perversity (or GM-perversity)
is a map

\overline{p}\colon\{2,\,3,\dots\}\to\mathbb{N}
such that

\overline{p}(2)=0
and

\overline{p}(i)\leq\overline{p}(i+1)\leq\overline{p}(i)+1
for all
i\geq 2
.

When using a GM-perversity, the filtered spaces under consideration have no 1-codimensional strata.
If
\overline{p}
is a GM-perversity, so is its complementary.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a stratified map and
\overline{p}
be a perversity on
Y
. The
pullback perversity of
\overline{p}
by
f

is the perversity
f^{*}\overline{p}
on
X
defined by
f^{*}\overline{p}(S)=\overline{p}(S^{f})
for any stratum
S
of
X
.
In the case of the canonical injection of an open subset endowed with the induced filtration,
\iota\colon U\to Y
,
we still denote by
\overline{p}
the perversity
\iota^{*}\overline{p}
and call it the
induced perversity.

The product with a topological space,
X\times M
, is endowed with
the pull-back perversity of
\overline{p}
by the canonical projection
X\times M\to X
, also denoted by
\overline{p}
.
If
X
is compact, a perversity
\overline{q}
on the open cone
{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}X
induces a perversity on
X
,
also denoted by
\overline{q}
and defined by

\overline{q}(S)=\overline{q}(S\times]0,1[)
.

Remark 1.10
.

A perversity
\overline{p}
defined on a CS set,
X
, induces a perversity on any link
L
.
Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\times{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L\to V
be a conical chart of
x\in S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
.
Then,
V\backslash S
is an open subset of
X
and is given with the induced perversity still denoted by
\overline{p}
.
The strata of
V\backslash S
are the products
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times]0,1[\times T
, where
T
is a stratum of
L
. We set

\overline{p}(T)=\overline{p}(\mathbb{R}^{k}\times[0,1[\times T)
which defines a perversity on
L
.
There is also a relation between the perversity on
S
itself and the conical chart by setting
\overline{p}({\mathtt{v}})=\overline{p}(S)
.

1.3.
Intersection homology

Let
(X,\overline{p})
be an
n
-dimensional perverse space.
The starting point in intersection homology is the use of the perversity for making
a selection among singular simplexes.
Before stating it, we specify the notion of dimension we are using
for subspaces of a Euclidean simplex.
We employ the polyhedra containing it (see
[
29
]
for the used properties on polyhedra).

Definition 1.11
.

A subspace
A\subset\Delta
of a Euclidean simplex is of
polyhedral dimension
less than or equal to
\ell

if
A
is included in a polyhedron
Q
with
\dim Q\leq\ell
. This definition verifies

(1.2)

\dim(A_{1}\cup A_{2})=\max(\dim A_{1},\dim A_{2}).

We choose this definition and do a selection among singular simplexes.
As it appears below, the allowability condition is usually most conveniently expressed in terms of the
complementary perversity,
D\overline{p}
, rather than
\overline{p}
.

Definition 1.12
.

Let
(X,\overline{p})
be a perverse space.
A simplex
\sigma\colon\Delta\to X
is

\overline{p}
-allowable

if, for each singular stratum
S
, the set
\sigma^{-1}S
verifies

(1.3)

\dim\sigma^{-1}S\leq\dim\Delta-{\rm codim\,}S+\overline{p}(S)=\dim\Delta-2-D\overline{p}(S),

with the convention
\dim\emptyset=-\infty
.
A singular chain
\xi
is
\overline{p}
-allowable
if it can be written as a linear combination of

\overline{p}
-allowable simplexes.

In Remark
1.14
, we illustrate this definition in low dimensions with a perversity such that

D\overline{p}\geq 0
, which is the case of the GM-perversities.

As explained in the introduction, we need a more restrictive choice than
\overline{p}
-allowability
for obtaining a chain complex.

Definition 1.13
.

A singular chain
\xi
is of
\overline{p}
-intersection
if
\xi
and its boundary
\partial\xi
are
\overline{p}
-allowable.
We denote by
C_{*}^{\overline{p}}(X;G)
the complex of singular chains of
\overline{p}
-intersection
and by
H_{*}^{\overline{p}}(X;G)
its homology, called

\overline{p}
-intersection homology of
X
with coefficients in a module
G
over a Dedekind ring
R
.

If
f\colon(X,\overline{p})\to(Y,\overline{q})
is a stratified map between two perverse spaces
with
f^{*}D\overline{q}\leq D\overline{p}
, the association
\sigma\mapsto f\circ\sigma
sends
a
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex on a
\overline{q}
-allowable simplex
(
[
9
, Proposition 1]
) and defines a chain map

f_{*}\colon C_{*}^{\overline{p}}(X)\to C_{*}^{\overline{q}}(Y)
.

Remark 1.14
.

In the original definition of King (
[
23
]
), the dimension chosen for a subspace of a Euclidean simplex
comes from the dimension of the skeleta containing it. In
[
9
]
, we show that the
two choices of dimension
give
isomorphic intersection homology for CS sets.
The proof needs a Mayer-Vietoris exact sequence and therefore a small chains replacement.
As usual, this property comes from a subdivision process but, with the choice of the polyhedra dimension
in Definition
1.11
, that is a subtle issue.

Let us take basic examples with a perversity
\overline{p}
, such that
D\overline{p}\geq 0
.
A 0-simplex and a 1-simplex are
\overline{p}
-allowable if, and only if,
\sigma^{-1}S=\emptyset
for any singular stratum
S
:
this means that they must lie in the regular part.
For a 2-simplex, the situation is different: the set
\sigma^{-1}S
can be a finite subset.
Let us consider a 2-simplex
\Delta^{2}
with one singular point,
v
, in its interior, which is thus
\overline{p}
-allowable.
Now we want to subdivise
\Delta^{2}
while keeping the
\overline{p}
-allowability condition for the small 2-simplexes of the
subdivision.
To achieve this, the point
v
must not belong to any 1-simplex of the subdivision.

Therefore, the subdivision of a
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex
\Delta^{\ell}
,
that we need, cannot be the barycentric subdivision but
a subdivision, called
pseudobarycentric subdivision,
which verifies the following properties:

•

a diameter of the new simplexes strictly smaller than that of the initial simplex,

•

the preservation of
\overline{p}
-allowability,

•

a construction by induction, taking the cone with vertex a suitably chosen interior point
b
in
\Delta^{\ell}

and base the pseudobarycentric subdivision of the boundary of
\Delta^{\ell}
.
The point
b
is called a
pseudobarycentre
of
\Delta^{\ell}
.

In
[
9
, Proposition 6]
, we show that such subdivisions exist for any
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex.
In this work we only use the properties recalled above.

2.
Gajer spaces

Let
(X,\overline{p})
be an
n
-dimensional perverse space.
We emphasize that in the definition of intersection homology (see Section
1.3
),
we do not consider simplexes but chains,
\xi
, and
we ask for the allowability of
\xi
and its boundary
\partial\xi
.
Now we want to construct a simplicial set,
thus the requirements concern the simplexes and all their faces.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,\overline{p})
be a perverse space.
A simplex
\sigma\colon\Delta^{\ell}\to X
is
\overline{p}
-full
if

\sigma
and all its faces,
\partial_{i_{1}}\dots\partial_{i_{k}}\sigma
,
are
\overline{p}
-allowable.

Remark 2.2
.

Similarly, a simplex
\sigma\colon\Delta^{\ell}\to X
is
\overline{p}
-full
if,
for all faces
F
of
\Delta
and all singular stratum
S
, one has

\dim(\sigma^{-1}S\cap F)\leq\dim F-D\overline{p}(S)-2
.

We continue with basic properties.
Let us begin with the following result, crucial for the definition of
\overline{p}
-intersection homotopy groups.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
14
, Page 946]
)
.

Let
(X,\overline{p})
be a perverse space. Then the set of
\overline{p}
-full simplexes is a simplicial set
verifying the Kan condition. We denote it by
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X
and call it
the
Gajer
\overline{p}
-space
associated to
X
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma\colon\Delta^{\ell}\to X
be a
\overline{p}
-full simplex and

S
be a singular stratum. By definition, the face
\partial_{i}\sigma
is
\overline{p}
-full for any face of
\sigma
.
Let
s^{i}\colon\Delta^{\ell+1}\to\Delta^{\ell}
be a codegeneracy and
d^{j}\colon\Delta^{\ell}\to\Delta^{\ell+1}

be a coface. The commutator rules express

\sigma\circ s^{i}\circ d^{j}
as a composition
\sigma\circ d^{r}\circ s^{t}
. By induction on the dimension
\ell
, we
deduce that
\sigma\circ s^{i}
is
\overline{p}
-full.
Thus the set of
\overline{p}
-full simplexes is a simplicial subset of the singular set
{\rm Sing}\,X
.
By using the adjunction between the functor
{\rm Sing}
and the realization functor,
the Kan extension condition is equivalent to the construction of
a
\overline{p}
-full simplex
\tau
making commutative the following diagram,

\textstyle{\Lambda^{\ell}_{k}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{|\Psi|}
\textstyle{X,}
\textstyle{\Delta^{\ell}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tau}

for any
0\leq k\leq\ell
.
If
\rho_{k}\colon\Delta^{\ell}\to\Lambda^{\ell}_{k}
denotes the radial projection
from the barycentre of the face
\partial_{k}\Delta^{\ell}
, we set

\tau=|\Psi|\circ\rho_{k}
.
For any singular stratum
S
, we have

\dim\left(|\Psi|\circ\rho_{k}\right)^{-1}S\leq\dim(|\Psi|)^{-1}S+1\leq(\ell-1-D\overline{p}(S)-2)+1=\ell-D\overline{p}(S)-2,

and the
\overline{p}
-allowability condition of
\tau
is satisfied.
As
\rho_{k}
is an affine map, the same argument works for any face of
\Delta^{\ell}
.
∎

The Gajer space depends on a filtration
{\mathscr{X}}
on the topological space
X
and we
should denote it by
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}(X,{\mathscr{X}})
.
In fact, we do not mention explicitly the filtration and simply write
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X
.

Proposition 2.4
.

[
14
, Example 1]

Let
(X,\overline{p})
be a perverse space
and
Y
be a topological space.
The product
Y\times X
is equipped with the product filtration
and the product perversity, also denoted by
\overline{p}
.
Then, the canonical projections,
p_{Y}\colon Y\times X\to Y
and
p_{X}\colon Y\times X\to X
,
induce an isomorphism

{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}(Y\times X)\cong{\rm Sing}\,Y\times{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X.

Proof.

Let
\sigma=(\sigma_{Y},\sigma_{X})\colon\Delta^{\ell}\to Y\times X
be a simplex.
By definition,
\sigma
is
\overline{p}
-full if, and only if,
\sigma_{X}
is
\overline{p}
-full also.
The projections therefore induce an isomorphism

{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}(Y\times X)\cong{\rm Sing}\,Y\times{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X
.
∎

The following statement concerns the functoriality of the association

(X,\overline{p})\mapsto{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X

and its compatibility with homotopy.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
f\colon(X,\overline{p})\to(Y,\overline{q})
be a stratified map between perverse spaces such that

f^{*}D\overline{q}\leq D\overline{p}
.
Then,

f
induces a map
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p},\overline{q}}f\colon{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X\to{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{q}}Y
.
Moreover, if
\varphi\colon(X\times[0,1],\overline{p})\to(Y,\overline{q})
is a stratified homotopy between two stratified maps

f,\,g\colon(X,\overline{p})\to(Y,\overline{q})
with
f^{*}D\overline{q}\leq D\overline{p}
, then we also have
g^{*}D\overline{q}\leq D\overline{p}

and the simplicial maps,
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p},\overline{q}}f
and
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p},\overline{q}}g
are homotopic.

Proof.

As we have already noticed, the association

\sigma\mapsto f\circ\sigma

sends a
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex on a
\overline{q}
-allowable simplex.
Let
S
be a stratum of
X
. Recall that the perversity on
X\times[0,1]
is still denoted by
\overline{p}
and defined by
\overline{p}(S\times[0,1])=\overline{p}(S)
.
From the stratification of the product
X\times[0,1]
, we have

S^{f}=(S\times[0,1])^{\varphi}=S^{g}
.
Thus,

f^{*}D\overline{q}(S)=D\overline{q}(S^{f})=D\overline{q}((S\times[0,1])^{\varphi})=\varphi^{*}D\overline{q}(S\times[0,1])

and, in the same way,

g^{*}D\overline{q}(S)=\varphi^{*}D\overline{q}(S\times[0,1])
.
Thus the three conditions
f^{*}D\overline{q}\leq D\overline{p}
,
g^{*}D\overline{q}\leq D\overline{p}
and
\varphi^{*}D\overline{q}\leq D\overline{p}
are equivalent and verified by hypothesis.
The associations

\sigma\mapsto f\circ\sigma,\;g\circ\sigma,\;\varphi\circ\sigma

being simplicial maps,
the maps
f
,
g
and the homotopy
\varphi
induce simplicial maps

{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p},\overline{q}}f,\;{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p},\overline{q}}g\colon{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X\to{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{q}}Y

and

{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p},\overline{q}}\varphi\colon{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}(X\times[0,1])\to{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{q}}Y
.
Using Proposition
2.4
, we get the desired homotopy as the composition

{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X\times\Delta[1]\to{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X\times{\rm Sing}(\Delta^{1})\to{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}(X\times\Delta^{1})\xrightarrow{\;{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p},\overline{q}\;}\varphi}{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{q}}Y.

∎

In particular, Definition
1.3
and Proposition
2.5
imply the following result.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
f\colon(X,\overline{p})\to(Y,\overline{q})
be a stratified homotopy equivalence with
D\overline{p}=f^{*}D\overline{q}
.
Then
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p},\overline{q}}f\colon{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X\to{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{q}}Y
is a homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

Let
S
be a stratum of
X
. By hypothesis, there exists a stratified map
g\colon(Y,\overline{q})\to(X,\overline{p})
and a stratified homotopy
\varphi
between

g\circ f
and
{\rm id}_{X}
.
From the stratification of the product
X\times[0,1]
, we deduce

S^{g\circ f}=(S\times[0,1])^{\varphi}=S^{{\rm id}}=S
.
Similarly, for any stratum
T
of
Y
, we have
T^{f\circ g}=T
.
These equalities and the hypothesis
D\overline{p}=f^{*}D\overline{q}
give
g^{*}D\overline{p}=D\overline{q}
.
With Proposition
2.5
, we get two simplicial maps,

{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p},\overline{q}}f\colon{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X\to{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{q}}Y

and

{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{q},\overline{p}}g\colon{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{q}}Y\to{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X

and homotopies between their compositions and the identity maps.
∎

Let us look at the particular case of isolated singular points.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(X,\overline{p})
be a perverse space with an isolated singularity

Set
\ell=D\overline{p}(\{x\})+1
.
Then, there is an isomorphism between the
\ell
-skeleta of
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X

and
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}(X\backslash\{x\})

Proof.

The
\overline{p}
-allowability condition of a simplex
\sigma\colon\Delta^{j}\to X
for a singular stratum

is:

(2.1)

\dim\sigma^{-1}x\leq j-D\overline{p}(\{x\})-2.

Thus, for any

, the inequality (

) gives

\dim\sigma^{-1}x\leq-1

This implies
\sigma^{-1}x=\emptyset
and an isomorphism of the

-skeleta

({\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X))^{j}\cong({\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}(X\backslash\{x\})^{j}
.
∎

Let
K
be a simplicial set and
\Pi K
be its fundamental groupoid. Let us recall that a
local coefficient system (of abelian groups) on

is a contravariant functor

{\mathcal{E}}\colon\Pi K\to{\mathrm{Ab}}
,
with values in the category of abelian groups, see
[
13
, Appendix I]
or
[
16
, Page 340]
.
Such functor generates a chain complex
C_{*}(K;{\mathcal{E}})
whose homology is called the homology of
K

with coefficients in the local system
{\mathcal{E}}
. The elements of
C_{j}(K;{\mathcal{E}})
are chains

\xi=\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}\sigma_{i}
with

\sigma_{i}\in K_{j}

and
a_{i}\in{\mathcal{E}}(\sigma_{i}(0))
.
The differential of
a\sigma
, with
\sigma\in K_{j}
and
a\in{\mathcal{E}}(\sigma(0))
,
is given by

\partial(a\sigma)=\sum_{i=1}^{j}(-1)^{j}a\,\partial_{i}\sigma+{\mathcal{E}}(\sigma([01]))^{-1}(a)\,\partial_{0}\sigma.

Isomorphisms between homotopy groups of simplicial sets (or spaces) can be detected from the existence of
isomorphisms between fundamental groups and isomorphisms of homology groups
for any local coefficient system
(
[
26
, Proposition II.3.4]
).

Lemma 2.8
.

For a simplicial map
f\colon K\to K^{\prime}
, the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The map
f
is a weak equivalence.

(2)

The map
f
induces isomorphisms,
\pi_{0}(K)\cong\pi_{0}(K^{\prime})
,
\pi_{1}(K,x)\cong\pi_{1}(K^{\prime},f(x))

for any

,

H_{j}(K^{\prime};{\mathcal{E}})\cong H_{j}(K;f^{*}{\mathcal{E}})
, for any local coefficient system
{\mathcal{E}}
on
K^{\prime}
and any
j
.

We also use the next well-known slight modification.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
f\colon K\to K^{\prime}
be a simplicial map between Kan simplicial sets whose induced maps verify the following properties:

\pi_{0}(K)\cong\pi_{0}(K^{\prime})
,
\pi_{1}(K,x)\cong\pi_{1}(K^{\prime},f(x))
for any

,

H_{j}(K;f^{*}{\mathcal{E}})\cong H_{j}(K^{\prime};{\mathcal{E}})
, for
any local coefficient system
{\mathcal{E}}
on
K^{\prime}
,
any

and

Then, the map induced by

between the homotopy groups is an isomorphism
for

and a surjection for

.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{K}\xrightarrow{p}K
be the universal cover of

.
The key observation in the proof given by Quillen (

H_{j}(K,{p}^{*}\mathbb{Z})\cong H_{j}(\widetilde{K};\mathbb{Z})

for coefficients
\mathbb{Z}
on

and the induced local coefficient system

on the homology of the fibres.
The result follows from the classic Whitehead theorem applied to the universal covers.

Then, for any local coefficient system

Proof.

We apply Proposition

to
{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}X
, its apex

and

The equality (

For sake of simplicity let us take
\mathbb{Z}

The determination done in Proposition

for the homology of

\overline{p}
-intersection homology of a cone,

If

[

This

Before giving concrete examples, let’s analyze
the difference between the elements of
C_{*}^{\overline{p}}(Y)
and those of
C_{*}({\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}Y)

for a perverse space
(Y,\overline{p})
.
Let
\xi=\sum_{i}r_{i}\sigma_{i}
be a singular chain on
Y
. For having

\xi\in C_{*}({\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}Y)
, the allowability condition has to be satisfied for all the faces of all
\sigma_{i}
.
In contrast, in
C_{*}^{\overline{p}}(Y)
there is no requirement on the faces that cancel out in the boundary
\partial\xi
.
As already observed in
[
15
]
, the

homology of
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}Y
is not isomorphic to the
\overline{p}
-intersection homology.

To see that, let

\overline{p}({\mathtt{v}})=D\overline{p}({\mathtt{v}})=2
on the apex.
Using the determination (Proposition

) of the homotopy groups of the Gajer space of a cone,
the only non-zero intersection homotopy group is

\pi_{2}({\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{2}}Y)=\mathbb{Z}

{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{2}}Y
is the Eilenberg-MacLane space
K(\mathbb{Z},2)

The homology of
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{2}}Y
does not fit with the
\overline{2}
-intersection homology groups of

which are zero in degrees strictly greater than 2.
We continue with other examples of this feature
which do not need the use of
\overline{p}
-intersection homotopy groups.

Example 2.11
.

Let
L
be an oriented
\ell

\pi_{\ell}(L)=0
(as the torus for
\ell=2
), endowed with the GM-perversity
\overline{0}

Below, we prove:

H_{\ell}^{\overline{0}}({\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L;\mathbb{Z})=0\neq H_{\ell}(L;\mathbb{Z})=H_{\ell}({\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{0}}({\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L);\mathbb{Z}).

Let us see that.
The dual perversity of
\overline{0}
being
D\overline{0}=\overline{\ell-1}

for intersection homology
H_{\ell}^{\overline{0}}({\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L;\mathbb{Z})=0

The second part of the claim holds if we prove that the natural inclusion induces an isomorphism

H_{\ell}(L;\mathbb{Z})\to H_{\ell}({\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{0}}({\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L);\mathbb{Z})

This map is an epimorphism since, at the level of chain complexes, we have
(see Proposition
2.7
)

C_{\leq\ell}^{\overline{0}}(L\times]0,1[;\mathbb{Z})=C_{\leq\ell}({\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{0}}{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L;\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
[\alpha]\in H_{\ell}(L;\mathbb{Z})
be the orientation class of
L
.
We reason by the absurd and suppose
[\alpha]=0
in
H_{\ell}({\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{0}}({\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L);\mathbb{Z})
.
The claim will be proven if we get a contradiction.
By hypothesis, there exists

\gamma=\sum_{i\in I}n_{i}\sigma_{i}\in C_{\ell+1}({\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{0}}({\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L);\mathbb{Z}),

with
\alpha=\partial\gamma
.
Let
{\mathtt{v}}
be the apex of
{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L
.
The allowability condition of Definition
1.12
implies that, for each simplex
\sigma_{i}\colon\Delta^{\ell+1}\to{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L

\sigma_{i}^{-1}({\mathtt{v}})

is finite and included in the interior of
{\Delta}^{\ell+1}

Thus, for each
i\in I

\widetilde{\sigma}_{i}\colon\partial\Delta^{\ell+1}\to{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L

takes value in
{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L\backslash\{{\mathtt{v}}\}

This restriction defines a homotopy class in
\pi_{\ell}({\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L\backslash\{{\mathtt{v}}\})=\pi_{\ell}(L)=0
.
So, there exists a continuous map

extending

.
We obtain
\tau_{i}\in C_{\ell+1}({\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L\backslash\{{\mathtt{v}}\};\mathbb{Z})

[\alpha]=\left[\sum_{i\in I}n_{i}\partial\sigma_{i}\right]=\left[\sum_{i\in I}n_{i}\partial\tau_{i}\right]=\left[\partial\sum_{i\in I}n_{i}\tau_{i}\right]=0

We get a contradiction since

is the orientation class.

3.
Intersection homotopy groups

A pointed perverse space is a triple
(X,\overline{p},x_{0})
where
(X,\overline{p})
is a perverse space and

x_{0}\in X\backslash\Sigma_{X}
is a regular point.
We also denote
x_{0}
the simplicial subset of
{\rm Sing}\,X
generated by
x_{0}\colon\Delta^{0}\to X
.

Definition 3.1
.

(
[
14
]
)
Let
(X,\overline{p},x_{0})
be a pointed perverse space.
The
\overline{p}
-intersection homotopy groups

(or perverse homotopy groups)
are the homotopy groups of the simplicial set
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X
,

\pi^{\overline{p}}_{\ell}(X,x_{0})=\pi_{\ell}({\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X,x_{0}).

As
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X
is Kan, an element of
\pi^{\overline{p}}_{\ell}(X,x_{0})
is given by a
\overline{p}
-full simplex,

\sigma\colon\Delta^{\ell}\to X
, with
\sigma(\partial\Delta^{\ell})=\{x_{0}\}
.
Two such simplexes,
\sigma_{0}
and
\sigma_{1}
, are equivalent if there exists a
\overline{p}
-full simplex

\Phi\colon\Delta^{\ell+1}\to X
verifying

\partial_{i}\Phi=x_{0}
if
i\geq 2
and
\partial_{i}\Phi=\sigma_{i}
for
i=0,\,1
.
We continue with the set
\pi_{0}^{\overline{p}}(X)
of the connected components of
{\mathscr{G}}_{\overline{p}}X
.

Definition 3.2
.

A perverse space,
(X,\overline{p})
, is said
\overline{p}
-connected
if
\pi_{0}^{\overline{p}}(X)=0
.

If
(X,\overline{p})
is a
\overline{p}
-connected perverse space, we do not need to specify the basepoint of the homotopy groups
and sometimes we will write
\pi^{\overline{p}}_{\ell}(X)
instead of
\pi^{\overline{p}}_{\ell}(X,x_{0})
.

Remark 3.3
.

Since each regular point of
X
is a
\overline{p}
-full simplex, we have the natural map
\pi_{0}(X\backslash\Sigma_{X})\to\pi_{0}^{\overline{p}}(X)
.
If the perversity
\overline{p}
verifies
\overline{p}\leq\overline{t}
, then the
\overline{p}
-full simplexes
\sigma\colon\Delta^{\ell}\to X
, for
\ell=0,1
,
do not meet the singular part of
X
. So,
\pi_{0}^{\overline{p}}(X)=\pi_{0}(X\backslash\Sigma_{X})
.
On the other hand, if
\overline{p}\geq\overline{t}+\overline{2}
then the
\overline{p}
-allowability condition is always fulfilled by the 0 and 1 simplexes.
So
\pi_{0}^{\overline{p}}(X)=\pi_{0}(X)
.

If
(X,\overline{p})
is a perverse CS set, the set
\pi_{0}^{\overline{p}}(X)
is related with the family of connected components of
X\backslash\Sigma_{X}
in a more specific way.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(X,\overline{p})
be a perverse CS set. For any point
x\in X
, there exists a path,

\beta\colon[0,1]\to X
, with
\beta(0)=x
,
\beta(]0,1])\subset X\backslash\Sigma
.
Thus the canonical inclusion induces a surjection
\pi_{0}(X\backslash\Sigma)\to\pi_{0}^{\overline{p}}(X)
.

Proof.

If
x
is regular, we choose the constant path. If not, we consider a conical chart,
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\times{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}L\to U

with
\varphi(0,{\mathtt{v}})=x
. We join
x
to a regular point with the path,
t\mapsto(0,[z,t])
.
Here, we have written
{\mathtt{v}}=[z,0]
where
z
is a regular point of the link
L
, which exists since
L\backslash\Sigma_{L}\neq\emptyset
.
∎

\overline{p}

Proposition 3.5
.

[
14

Let
(X,x_{0})
be a compact filtered space, pointed by a regular point
x_{0}
,
and
{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}X

{\mathtt{v}}

For

2.7

Consider now a
\overline{p}
-full simplex

\sigma=[\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1}]\colon(\Delta^{\ell},\partial\Delta^{\ell})\to({\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}X,y_{0})

\ell\geq D\overline{p}({\mathtt{v}})+1

\overline{p}
-full simplex,

\Phi\colon\Delta^{\ell+1}\to{\mathring{{\mathtt{c}}}}X

\partial_{i}\Phi=y_{0}

\partial_{0}\Phi=\sigma

These requirements define
\Phi_{|\partial\Delta^{\ell+1}}

To extend it to the whole simplex, we consider

\Phi(b)={\mathtt{v}}

\Phi

\bullet
If

\bullet
If

4.
Intersection fundamental group

Let
(X,\overline{p})
be a perverse space, with
\overline{p}\leq\overline{t}
.
Recall that any
\overline{p}
-allowable simplex,
\sigma\colon\Delta^{\ell}\to X
, verifies

\dim\sigma^{-1}S\leq\ell-{\rm codim\,}S+\overline{p}(S)\leq\ell-2
,
for any singular stratum
S
.
Thus, the vertices and the edges of a
\overline{p}
-full simplex stay in the regular part.
This implies

(4.1)

the surjectivity of

\pi_{1}(X\backslash\Sigma_{X},x_{0})\to\pi_{1}^{\overline{p}}(X,x_{0})
for
x_{0}\in X\backslash\Sigma_{X}
.

We begin with a Van Kampen theorem if
\overline{p}\leq\overline{t}
.
The proof consists in the observation that the classic demonstration can be done in accordance with the
\overline{p}
-allowability conditions.
For that, we use
[
22
, Section 1.2]
as guideline.

Let
(X,\overline{p})
be a perverse space with
\overline{p}\leq\overline{t}
,
\{U_{0},U_{1}\}
be an open cover of
X
.
The open subsets
U_{0}
,
U_{1}
,
U_{0}\cap U_{1}
are supposed to be path-connected and we endow them with the induced filtration and the induced perversity.
We denote by

j_{k}\colon\pi_{1}^{\overline{p}}(U_{0}\cap U_{1},x_{0})\to\pi_{1}^{\overline{p}}(U_{k},x_{0})

and

\iota_{k}\colon\pi_{1}^{\overline{p}}(U_{k},x_{0})\to\pi_{1}^{\overline{p}}(X,x_{0})

the homomorphisms induced by the canonical inclusions for
k=0,\,1
.
The homomorphisms
\iota_{k}
extend to a homomorphism defined on the free product of groups,

\Phi\colon\pi_{1}^{\overline{p}}(U_{0},x_{0})\ast\pi_{1}^{\overline{p}}(U_{1},x_{0})\to\pi_{1}^{\overline{p}}(X,x_{0}).

The Van Kampen theorem consists of a determination of the kernel and the image of
\Phi
.

Theorem 4.1
.

The map
\Phi
is surjective and its kernel is the normal subgroup generated by all elements of the form

j_{0}(\omega)\ast j_{1}(\omega)^{-1}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be a
\overline{p}
-allowable loop in
X
. We already noted that the inclusion map induces a
surjective homomorphism

\pi_{1}(X\backslash\Sigma_{X},x_{0})\to\pi_{1}^{\overline{p}}(X,x_{0})
.
Thus, the
\overline{p}
-homotopy class of
\alpha
is the image of the homotopy class of a loop

\beta
in
X\backslash\Sigma_{X}
.
From the compactness of
[0,1]
, we obtain a decomposition

[\alpha]=\Phi([\gamma_{0}]\ast\dots\ast[\gamma_{\ell}])

with the support of each
\gamma_{j}
in one of the two subsets
U_{0}
,
U_{1}
.
By using the path-connectedness of

,
U_{1}
,
U_{0}\cap U_{1}
, we can suppose that

\gamma_{i}
is a loop based in

The critical point of the proof is the determination of the kernel of

.
We consider two factorizations as above,

\Phi([\gamma_{0}]\ast\dots\ast[\gamma_{\ell}])=\Phi([\gamma^{\prime}_{0}]\ast\dots\ast[\gamma^{\prime}_{k}])

Thus, the two loops
\gamma_{0}\dots\gamma_{\ell}
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{0}\dots\gamma^{\prime}_{k}
are

-homotopic
and there exists a full
\overline{p}
-homotopy

between them.
Unlike the situation of paths, the support of the homotopy is not necessarily in

; i.e.
the subset

is not necessarily empty.
However the
\overline{p}
-allowability condition gives us a control on it and we know that

consists in a finite number of points located in the interior of the square.
Without loss of generality, by using an appropriate subdivision, we can suppose that

is reduced to one point

in the interior of

.
As in the surjective part of the proof, we use the compactness of

to get a subdivision of it
so that each small square lies either in
U_{0}
or in
U_{1}
.
Here, we can arrange the subdivision in such a way that the point

does not belong to any of the boundaries
of the small squares. This implies that each small square is a full
\overline{p}
-homotopy in
U_{0}
or
U_{1}
.
The rest of the proof consists to the use of the path-connectedness of
U_{0}
,
U_{1}
,
U_{0}\cap U_{1}

to transform each small square in a
\overline{p}
-homotopy between loops based in

.
This is done with the introduction of paths from

to the corners of the small squares.
As this can be done in

,
each of the small squares remains a full
\overline{p}
-homotopy.
This gives the conclusion, as in the classic topological situation.

connected,

We are interested in the topological invariance of the intersection fundamental group of CS sets for GM-perversities.
The following example shows that this property is not satisfied in general.

Example 4.3
.

The suspension
\Sigma M
of a topological space
M
being the main ingredient of the example, let us begin with
two basic properties.

1) If
\Sigma M
is homeomorphic to a sphere
S^{n}
, then
M
has the homotopy type of

S^{n-1}
. (Just remove the two vertices of
\Sigma M
to get a homeomorphism

M\times]-1,1[\cong S^{n-1}\times]-1,1[
.)

2) If
\Sigma M
is homeomorphic to a sphere
S^{n}
and
M
is a manifold,
from 1) and the theorems of
Smale-Freedman-Donaldson-Perelman (also called “Poincaré conjecture”), then
M
is a sphere.

Let
P
be the Poincaré sphere, a manifold of dimension three with the homology of the 3-sphere and such that

\pi_{1}(P,v)\neq 1
, with
v\in P
.
From the previous recalls, we deduce that the suspension
\Sigma P
is not homeomorphic to
S^{4}

and has no structure of manifold, but
has the homotopy type of
S^{4}
.
Let
{\mathtt{v}}_{1},{\mathtt{v}}_{2}
be the two apexes of
\Sigma P
, constituting the singular set of
\Sigma P
.
We choose a GM-perversity
\overline{p}
with
D\overline{p}(4)=1
.
By definition, two
\overline{p}
-allowable simplexes,

\xi\colon(\Delta^{1},\partial\Delta^{1})\to(\Sigma P,v)
and
\eta\colon\Delta^{2}\to\Sigma P
,
verify

\dim\xi^{-1}({\mathtt{v}}_{i})\leq\dim\Delta-2-D\overline{p}(4)=-2

and
\dim\eta^{-1}({\mathtt{v}}_{i})\leq-1
. Thus, we have
\pi_{1}^{\overline{p}}(\Sigma P,v)=\pi_{1}(P,v)
.

We now consider the double suspension of
P
and denote by
{\mathtt{w}}_{1},{\mathtt{w}}_{2}
the two new apexes.
We filter
\Sigma^{2}P
by

\{{\mathtt{w}}_{1},{\mathtt{w}}_{2}\}\subset\Sigma\{{\mathtt{v}}_{1},{\mathtt{v}}_{2}\}\subset\Sigma^{2}P
.
We choose a GM-perversity such that

.
A similar computation gives
\pi_{1}^{\overline{p}}(\Sigma^{2}P,v)=\pi_{1}^{\overline{p}}(\Sigma P,v)=\pi_{1}(P,v)\neq 1
.
As
\Sigma^{2}P
is homeomorphic to the sphere

This situation does not appear in the PL case since the homeomorphism between

and

is not a PL map.
In fact, in
[

, Theorem 2.7]
Gajer shows that the

-intersection homotopy groups of a polyhedron is a PL invariant.

Remark 4.4
.

The overlapping of Proposition

and Example

calls out.
In the first reference, we prove that links of a point in a CS set have isomorphic intersection homotopy groups
for any perversity. In the second one, we use the example of two CS set structures on a particular suspension space,

and that a 2-simplex,

Since

\bullet

with the restriction on

\bullet

In short, the

-full 2-simplexes are exactly the simplexes

for any stratum

of

We have proven

∎

Under the hypothesis of Corollary

k k -Pell-Lucas numbers as Product of Two Repdigits
Bibhu Prasad Tripathy, Bijan Kumar Patel

Abstract
For any integer k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , let { Q n ( k ) } n ≥ − ( k − 2 ) \{Q_{n}^{(k)}\}_{n\geq-(k-2)} denote the k k -generalized Pell-Lucas sequence which starts with 0 , … , 2 , 2 0,\dots,2,2 ( k k terms) where each next term is the sum of the k k preceding terms. In this paper, we find all the k k -generalized Pell-Lucas numbers that are the product of two repdigits. This generalizes a result of Erduvan and Keskin [ 10 ] regarding repdigits of Pell-Lucas numbers.



Keywords
:
k
-Pell-Lucas numbers, linear forms in logarithms, repdigits, reduction method.

1
Introduction

The Pell-Lucas sequence
\{Q_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is the binary recurrence sequence defined by

Q_{n+2}=2Q_{n+1}+Q_{n}~~{\rm for}~n\geq 0

with initials
Q_{0}=2
and
Q_{1}=2
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer. We consider the
k
-generalized Pell-Lucas sequence
\{Q_{n}^{(k)}\}_{n\geq-(k-2)}
, which is a generalization of the Pell-Lucas sequence and is given by the recurrence

Q_{n}^{(k)}=2Q_{n-1}^{(k)}+Q_{n-2}^{(k)}+\dots+Q_{n-k}^{(k)}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}Q_{n-i}^{(k)}~~\text{for all}~n\geq 2,

(1.1)

with initials
Q_{-(k-2)}^{(k)}=Q_{-(k-3)}^{(k)}=\dots=Q_{-1}^{(k)}=0,Q_{0}^{(k)}=2
and
Q_{1}^{(k)}=2
.

We shall refer to
Q_{n}^{(k)}
as the
k
-Pell-Lucas sequence. We see that this generalization is a family of sequences, with each new choice of
k
producing a unique sequence. For example, if
k=2
, we get
Q_{n}^{(2)}=Q_{n}
, the classical Pell-Lucas sequence. For
k=3
, we have the 3-Pell-Lucas sequence. They are followed by the 4-Pell-Lucas sequence for
k=4
, and so on.

A positive integer of the form
\frac{a(10^{l}-1)}{9}
, for any
l\geq 1
and
1\leq a\leq 9
, is said to be a repdigit if its decimal expansion only contains one distinct digit. There are many literature in number theory concerning Diophantine equations involving binary recurrence sequences of positive integers and repdigits. First Faye and Luca
[
11
]
found that
Q_{1}=2
and
Q_{2}=6
are the only repdigits in the Pell-Lucas sequence. Later Adegbindin et al.
[
1
]
showed that the largest Pell-Lucas number which is a sum of two repdigits is
Q_{6}=198=99+99
. Consequently, Adegbindin et al.
[
2
]
found that the largest Pell-Lucas number which is a sum of three repdigits is
Q_{10}=6726=6666+55+5
. Following that Erduvan and Keskin
[
10
]
showed that
Q_{6}=198
is the largest Pell-Lucas number which is a product of two repdigits.

Recently, Rihane
[
13
]
extended the previous work of
[
9
]
and showed that
F_{8}^{(3)}=4\cdot 11
and
F_{8}^{(3)}=22\cdot 88=44^{2}
are the only
k
-Fibonacci number
F_{n}^{(k)}
which are product of two repdigits. He also found in the same paper that
L_{9}^{(4)}=7\cdot 44=4\cdot 77,L_{10}^{(4)}=9\cdot 66=6\cdot 99
and
L_{9}^{(5)}=8\cdot 44=4\cdot 88
are the only
k
-Lucas number
L_{n}^{(k)}
that can be expressed as product of two repdigits. Consequently, he
[
13
]
also extended the previous work of
[
10
]
and studied it in
k
-Pell sequence.

Motivated by the above literature, we solve the Diophantine equation

Q_{n}^{(k)}=\frac{a(10^{l}-1)}{9}\cdot\frac{b(10^{m}-1)}{9}.

(1.2)

More precisely, we find all the
k
-Pell-Lucas numbers that are product of two repdigits. In particular, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

All the solutions of the Diophantine equation (
1.2
) in positive integers
n,m,l,k,a
and
b
with
k\geq 3
,
1\leq l\leq m,1\leq a\leq 9
and
1\leq b\leq 9
are

\displaystyle Q_{1}^{(k)}=1\cdot 2=2\cdot 1,k\geq 3,

\displaystyle Q_{2}^{(k)}=1\cdot 6=2\cdot 3=3\cdot 2=6\cdot 1,k\geq 3,

\displaystyle Q_{3}^{(k)}=2\cdot 8=4\cdot 4=8\cdot 1,k\geq 3,

\displaystyle Q_{4}^{(3)}=5\cdot 8=8\cdot 5,Q_{4}^{(k)}=6\cdot 7=7\cdot 6,k\geq 4,

\displaystyle Q_{5}^{(k)}=22\cdot 5=55\cdot 2=2\cdot 55=5\cdot 22,k\geq 3,

\displaystyle Q_{7}^{(4)}=11\cdot 66=66\cdot 11=22\cdot 33=33\cdot 22.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
is mainly based on linear forms in logarithms of algebraic numbers and a reduction algorithm originally introduced by Baker and Davenport
[
3
]
(and improved by Dujella and Pethő
[
8
]
). Here, we also use a variant due to de Weger
[
16
]
to reduce the upper bound to a size that is simpler to handle for the small case. Here we have also derived some estimates for large case by using the fact that the dominant root of the
k
-Pell-Lucas sequence is exponentially close to
\phi^{2}
(see
[
15
]
) where
\phi=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
. Consequently, one may substitute this root by
\phi
in further calculation with linear forms in logarithms to obtain absolute upper bounds for all the variables, which we then reduce using again the de Weger algorithm.

2.1
Linear forms in logarithms

Let
\gamma
be an algebraic number of degree
d
with minimal primitive polynomial

f(Y):=b_{0}Y^{d}+b_{1}Y^{d-1}+\cdots+b_{d}=b_{0}\prod_{j=1}^{d}(Y-\gamma^{(j)})\in\mathbb{Z}[Y],

where the
b_{j}
’s are relatively prime integers,
b_{0}>0
, and the
\gamma^{(j)}
’s are conjugates of
\gamma
. Then the
logarithmic height
of
\gamma
is given by

h(\gamma)=\frac{1}{d}\left(\log b_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\log\left(\max\{|\gamma^{(j)}|,1\}\right)\right).

(2.3)

With the above notations, Matveev (see
[
12
]
or
[
6
, Theorem 9.4]
) proved the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{s}
be positive real algebraic numbers in a real algebraic number field
\mathbb{L}
of degree
d_{\mathbb{L}}
. Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}
be non-zero integers such that

\Lambda:=\eta_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\eta_{s}^{a_{s}}-1\neq 0.

Then

-\log|\Lambda|\leq 1.4\cdot 30^{s+3}\cdot s^{4.5}\cdot d_{\mathbb{L}}^{2}(1+\log d_{\mathbb{L}})(1+\log D)\cdot B_{1}\cdots B_{s},

where

D\geq\max\{|a_{1}|,\ldots,|a_{s}|\},

and

B_{j}\geq\max\{d_{\mathbb{L}}h(\eta_{j}),|\log\eta_{j}|,0.16\},~\text{for all}~j=1,\ldots,s.

The following lemma is a slight variation of a result due to Dujella and Pethö
[
8
, Lemma 5 (a)]
and a generalisation of a Baker and Davenport result
[
3
]
.

(2.7)

When

), put

2.3
Properties of
k
-Pell-Lucas sequence

The characteristic polynomial of the
k
-Pell-Lucas sequence is

\Phi_{k}(x)=x^{k}-2x^{k-1}-x^{k-2}-\dots-x-1.

The above polynomial is irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}[x]
and it has one positive real root that is
\gamma:=\gamma(k)
which is located between
\phi^{2}(1-\phi^{-k})
and
\phi^{2}
, lies outside the unit circle (see
[
15
]
). The other roots are firmly contained within the unit circle. To simplify the notation, we will omit the dependence on
k
of
\gamma
whenever no confusion may arise.

The Binet formula for
Q_{n}^{(k)}
that found in
[
15
]
is

Q_{n}^{(k)}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}(2\gamma_{i}-2)g_{k}(\gamma_{i})\gamma_{i}^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{2(\gamma_{i}-1)^{2}}{(k+1)\gamma_{i}^{2}-3k\gamma_{i}+k-1}\gamma_{i}^{n},

(2.8)

where
\gamma_{i}
represents the roots of the characteristic polynomial
\Phi_{k}(x)
and the function
g_{k}
is given by

g_{k}(z):=\frac{z-1}{(k+1)z^{2}-3kz+k-1},

(2.9)

for an integer
k

\geq
2.

Additionally it is also shown in
[
15
, Lemma 10]
that the roots located inside the unit circle have a very minimal influence on the formula (
2.9
), which is given by the approximation

\left|Q_{n}^{(k)}-(2\gamma-2)g_{k}(\gamma)\gamma^{n}\right|<2\hskip 10.00002pt\text{holds for all}\hskip 10.00002ptn\geq 2-k.

(2.10)

Furthermore, it was shown by Şiar and Keskin in
[
15
, Lemma 10]
that the inequality

\gamma^{n-1}\leq Q_{n}^{(k)}\leq 2\gamma^{n}\text{ holds for all }n\geq 1~{\rm and}~k\geq 2.

(2.11)

Lemma 2.7
.

(
[
4
]
, Lemma 3.2)
.
Let
k\geq 2
be an integer. Then we have

0.276<g_{k}(\gamma)<0.5\ and\ \left|g_{k}(\gamma_{i})\right|<1\hskip 10.00002ptfor\hskip 10.00002pt2\leq i\leq k.

Furthermore, Bravo et al.
[
5
]
showed that the logarithmic height of
g_{k}(\gamma)
satisfies

h(g_{k}(\gamma))<4k\log\phi+k\log(k+1)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all}\hskip 10.00002ptk\geq 2.

(2.12)

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\gamma
be the dominant root of the characteristic polynomial
\Phi_{k}(x)
and consider the function
g_{k}(x)
defined in (
2.9
). If
k\geq 50
and
n>1
are integers satisfying
n<\phi^{k/2}
, then the following inequalities holds

(i)
[
15
, Equation 30]

\left|(2\gamma-2)\gamma^{n}-2\phi^{2n+1}\right|<\frac{4\phi^{2n}}{\phi^{k/2}},

(ii)
[
15
, Lemma 13]

|g_{k}(\gamma)-g_{k}(\phi^{2})|<\frac{4k}{\phi^{k}}.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
k\geq 50
and suppose that
n<\phi^{k/2}
, then

(2\gamma-2)g_{k}(\gamma)\gamma^{n}=\frac{2\phi^{2n+1}}{\phi+2}(1+\xi),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{where}\hskip 10.00002pt|\xi|<\frac{1.25}{\phi^{k/2}}.

(2.13)

Proof.

By virtue of Lemma
2.8
, we have

(2\gamma-2)\gamma^{n}=2\phi^{2n+1}+\delta\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptg_{k}(\gamma)=g_{k}(\phi^{2})+\eta,

(2.14)

where

|\delta|=\frac{4\phi^{2n}}{\phi^{k/2}}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt|\eta|=\frac{4k}{\phi^{k}}.

(2.15)

Since
g_{k}(\phi^{2})=\frac{1}{\phi+2}
, we can write

\displaystyle(2\gamma-2)\gamma^{n}g_{k}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\left(2\phi^{2n+1}+\delta\right)\left(g_{k}(\phi^{2})+\eta\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\phi^{2n+1}}{\phi+2}(1+\xi),

where

\xi=\frac{\delta}{2\phi^{2n+1}}+(\phi+2)\eta+\frac{(\phi+2)\eta\delta}{2\phi^{2n+1}}.

Using (
2.15
), it follows from the above equation that

|\xi|<\frac{2/\phi}{\phi^{k/2}}+\frac{4k(\phi+2)}{\phi^{k}}+\frac{8k(\phi+2)}{\phi\cdot\phi^{3k/2}}<\frac{1.25}{\phi^{k/2}},

where we have used the facts

\frac{2/\phi}{\phi^{k/2}}<\frac{1.24}{\phi^{k/2}},\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{4k(\phi+2)}{\phi^{k}}<\frac{0.005}{\phi^{k/2}}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{(8k/\phi)(\phi+2)}{\phi^{3k/2}}<\frac{0.005}{\phi^{k/2}},

for all
k\geq 50
. This completes the proof of the lemma.

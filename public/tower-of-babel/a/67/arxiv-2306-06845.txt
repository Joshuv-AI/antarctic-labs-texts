Information-Theoretic Limits and Strong Consistency on Binary Non-uniform Hypergraph Stochastic Block Models
Hai-Xiao Wang

Abstract
. Consider the unsupervised classification problem in random hypergraphs under the non-uniform Hypergraph Stochastic Block Model (HSBM) with two equal-sized communities, where each edge appears independently with some probability depending only on the labels of its vertices. In this paper, the information-theoretic limits on the clustering accuracy and the strong consistency threshold are established, expressed in terms of the generalized Hellinger distance. Below the threshold, it is impossible to assign all vertices to their own communities, and the lower bound of the expected mismatch ratio is derived. On the other hand, the problem space is (sometimes) divided into two disjoint subspaces when above the threshold. When only the contracted adjacency matrix is given, with high probability, one-stage spectral algorithms succeed in assigning every vertex correctly in the subspace far away from the threshold but fail in the other one. Two subsequent refinement algorithms are proposed to improve the clustering accuracy, which attain the lowest possible mismatch ratio, previously derived from the information-theoretical perspective. The failure of spectral algorithms in the second subspace arises from the loss of information induced by tensor contraction. The origin of this loss and possible solutions to minimize the impact are presented. Moreover, different from uniform hypergraphs, strong consistency is achievable by aggregating information from all uniform layers, even if it is impossible when each layer is considered alone.

Preliminaries

2.1.
The model

Definition 2.1

(Hypergraph)

A hypergraph

Definition 2.2

(Symmetric binary uniform HSBM)

Let

{\mathcal{V}}=[n]
with the equi-sized constraint, where
{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}
is chosen uniformly at random among all vectors of satisfying
\mathbf{1}^{{\mathsf{T}}}{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}=0
. Let

be some fixed integer. The
m
-uniform hypergraph

is drawn in the following manner: each
m
-hyperedge

\alpha_{m}
if
{\mathbf{\sigma}}_{i_{1}}=\ldots={\mathbf{\sigma}}_{i_{m}}

One can associate an

{\mathcal{A}}_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}^{(m)}=\mathbbm{1}_{\{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{m}\}}
.

Definition 2.3

(Non-uniform HSBM)
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}=\{m:m\geq 2\}
be a finite set of integers. Let
{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}=({\mathbf{\sigma}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathbf{\sigma}}_{n})\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
denote the label vector with
\mathbf{1}^{{\mathsf{T}}}{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}=0
. The non-uniform hypergraph

is a collection of independent uniform ones, i.e.,
{\mathcal{H}}=([n],{\mathcal{E}})=\cup_{m\in{\mathcal{M}}}{\mathcal{H}}_{m}
with
{\mathcal{E}}:=\cup_{m\in{\mathcal{M}}}{\mathcal{E}}_{m}
.

However, it is impossible to aggregate information of each uniform hypergraph directly since tensors are of different orders. Meanwhile, most computations involving tensors are NP-hard
[
47
]
. To get rid of that, our analysis will be based on the following
adjacency matrix
.

Definition 2.4

(Contracted adjacency matrix)

For the non-uniform hypergraph
{\mathcal{H}}
, let
{\bm{\mathcal{A}}}^{(m)}
be the order-
m

m

(2.1)

\displaystyle{\textnormal{A}}_{ij}\coloneqq\mathbbm{1}_{\{i\neq j\}}\cdot\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{M}}}\,\,\,\,\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{H}}_{m}),\,\,e\supset\{i,j\}}\,\,\,\,{\mathcal{A}}^{(m)}_{e}\,,

where

for each

since each
m
-hyperedge contains

To simplify the presentation, we adapt the following assumption on the model parameters.

Assumption 2.5
.

For each
m\in{\mathcal{M}}
, assume that
\alpha_{m}\geq\beta_{m}
. We write

For the discussion of strong consistency, it typically requires the expected degree of each vertex grows to infinity with respect to the number of vertices. Therefore, the regime of interest is

2.2.2.

4.1.
Impossibility of exact recovery

Let
\widehat{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}
denote an estimation of
{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}
based on the observed hypergraph
{\mathcal{H}}
where
\mathbb{H}
denotes its law. The probability of failing exact recovery is

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{fail}}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}(\widehat{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}\neq\pm{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}})=\sum[1-\mathbb{P}(\widehat{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}=\pm{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}\mid\mathbb{H}={\mathcal{H}})]\cdot\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{H}={\mathcal{H}})\,.

which is minimized by the
Maximum A Posteriori
(MAP) estimator

(4.2)

\displaystyle\widehat{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}_{\mathrm{MAP}}\coloneqq\underset{{\mathbf{z}}\in\{\pm 1\}^{n},\,\mathbf{1}^{{\mathsf{T}}}{\mathbf{z}}=0}{\arg\max}\,\mathbb{P}({\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}={\mathbf{z}}\mid\mathbb{H}={\mathcal{H}})\,.

Consequently, no other estimator can succeed exact recovery if
\widehat{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}_{\mathrm{MAP}}
fails. Since the prior distribution of
{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}
in model
2.3
is uniform, i.e.,
\mathbb{P}({\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}={\mathbf{z}})=1/\binom{n}{n/2}
,
\widehat{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}_{\mathrm{MAP}}
corresponds to the
Maximum Likelihood Estimation
(MLE). However, MLE is still hard to compute. We introduce the
in-cluster
concept to formalize the equivalent quantity that MLE is maximizing in Lemma
4.2
.

Definition 4.1

(Incidence and in-cluster)
.

For
m
-uniform hypergraph
{\mathcal{H}}_{m}=([n],{\mathcal{E}}_{m})
, define the incidence vector
{\mathbf{h}}^{(m)}
by
{\textnormal{h}}^{(m)}_{e}\coloneqq\mathbbm{1}_{\{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{m}\}}
. The edge
e=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\}
is
in-cluster
with respect to
{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}
if
{\mathbf{\sigma}}_{i_{1}}=\ldots={\mathbf{\sigma}}_{i_{m}}
. Define the in-cluster vector
{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}^{(m)}
by
{\mathbf{\sigma}}^{(m)}_{e}\coloneqq\mathbbm{1}_{\{{\mathbf{\sigma}}_{i_{1}}=\ldots={\mathbf{\sigma}}_{i_{m}}\}}
.

Let
[{\mathcal{V}}]^{m}
denote the family of
m
-subsets of
{\mathcal{V}}
, i.e.,
[{\mathcal{V}}]^{m}=\{{\mathcal{S}}|~{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\mathcal{V}},|{\mathcal{S}}|=m\}
. The number of in-cluster
m
-hyperedges in
{\mathcal{H}}_{m}
with respect to label vector
{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}
can be counted by the inner product

(4.3)

\displaystyle\langle{\mathbf{h}}^{(m)},{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}^{(m)}\rangle=\sum_{e\in[{\mathcal{V}}]^{m}}{\textnormal{h}}^{(m)}_{e}\cdot{\mathbf{\sigma}}^{(m)}_{e}=\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{m}}\mathbbm{1}\{e\textnormal{ is in-cluster w.r.t. }{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}\}.

Lemma 4.2
.

When the non-uniform hypergraph
{\mathcal{H}}
is sampled from model
2.3
,
\widehat{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}_{\mathrm{MLE}}
maximizes

(4.4)

\displaystyle f({\mathbf{z}}|{\mathcal{H}})\coloneqq\sum_{m\in{\mathcal{M}}}\log\Big(\frac{\alpha_{m}(1-\beta_{m})}{\beta_{m}(1-\alpha_{m})}\Big)\cdot\langle{\mathbf{h}}^{(m)},{\mathbf{z}}^{(m)}\rangle\,,

among all vectors
{\mathbf{z}}\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
with
\mathbf{1}^{{\mathsf{T}}}{\mathbf{z}}=0
if
\alpha_{m}>\beta_{m}
for each
m\in{\mathcal{M}}
. Alternatively,
\widehat{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}_{\mathrm{MLE}}(H)
minimizes
f({\mathbf{z}}|{\mathcal{H}})
when
\alpha_{m}>\beta_{m}
(disassortative) for each
m\in{\mathcal{M}}
.

Following the discussion in (
4.2
), (
4.4
), MLE fails exact recovery means that
\widehat{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}_{\mathrm{MLE}}
prefers some other
\widetilde{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}
, such that
f(\widetilde{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}|{\mathcal{H}})\geq f({\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}|{\mathcal{H}})
but
\widetilde{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}\neq\pm{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}
, formally,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{fail}}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}(\widehat{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}_{\mathrm{MLE}}\neq\pm{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}})=\mathbb{P}(\exists\widetilde{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}\neq{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}},\,\textnormal{s.t.}\,f(\widetilde{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}|{\mathcal{H}})\geq f({\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}|{\mathcal{H}})\,)

We will show that, under the condition (
3.1
), one such
\widetilde{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}
with
\mathbf{1}^{{\mathsf{T}}}\widetilde{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}=0
can be constructed from
{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}
by swapping the labels of two vertices, one
+
and one
-
, and this will yield
\widehat{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}_{\mathrm{MLE}}=\widetilde{{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}}\neq\pm{\bm{\mathbf{\sigma}}}
with probability tending to
1
, which leads to the failure of exact recovery.

In

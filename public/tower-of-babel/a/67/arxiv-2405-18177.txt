On the resistance regular graphs
Haritha T, Chithra A. V 1 Department of Mathematics, National Institute of Technology Calicut,Calicut-673 601, Kerala, India

Abstract
For a connected graph G G , its resistance distance matrix is denoted by R ⁡ ( G ) R(G) . A graph is called resistance regular if all the row (or column) sums of R ⁡ ( G ) R(G) are equal. We provide a necessary and sufficient condition for a simple connected graph to be resistance regular. Additionally, we establish sharp bounds for the resistance spectral radius and present various bounds for the resistance energy of G G . Furthermore, we compute the resistance spectrum and resistance energy of some resistance regular graphs.



Keywords: Resistance distance, Resistance distance matrix, Laplacian matrix, Moore-Penrose inverse, Resistance regular graphs, Resistance energy.

Denote the identity matrix by

\displaystyle\left(2(p-1)I_{n}+(I_{p}-J_{p})\otimes J_{2}\right)X\left(2(p-1)I_{n}+(I_{p}-J_{p})\otimes J_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2(p-1)I_{n}+(I_{p}-J_{p})\otimes J_{2},

\displaystyle X\left(2(p-1)I_{n}+(I_{p}-J_{p})\otimes J_{2}\right)X

\displaystyle=X,

\displaystyle\left(2(p-1)I_{n}+(I_{p}-J_{p})\otimes J_{2}\right)X=I_{n}-\frac{1}{n}J_{n}=X\left(2(p-1)I_{n}+(I_{p}-J_{p})\otimes J_{2}\right).

4
Resistance Energy

In this section we determine the

Further, establishes sharp bounds for the resistance spectral radius, and presents various
bounds for the resistance energy of graphs.
Note that
R(G)
has a single positive eigenvalue and
n-1

E_{R}(G)=2\rho_{1}(G).

The following propositions are direct consequence of Theorem

and Proposition

Proposition 4.1
.

For a graph
G
with
n

E_{R}(G)\geq 2\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{i}^{2}}.

Equality holds if and only if
R(G)
is resistance regular.

Proposition 4.2
.

For a graph
G
,
E_{R}(G)\geq\frac{4\mathcal{K}f(G)}{n}
equality holds if and only if
G
is resistance regular.

Corollary 4.1
.

If
G
is
k

E_{R}(G)=2k.

Also
\mathcal{K}f(G)=\frac{nk}{2}.

Remark 4.1
.

All resistance regular graphs have the same Kirchhoff index.

Using Corollary
4.1

Corollary 4.2

The resistance energy of some standard resistance regular graphs are given as follows:

(i)

(ii)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{ij}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{ij}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}}{4}-\frac{1}{n}\left(2\left(1^{2}+2^{2}+\cdots+\left(\frac{n}{2}-1\right)^{2}\right)+\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{ij}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}d_{ij}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}-1}{4}-\frac{2}{n}\left(1^{2}+2^{2}+\cdots+\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n^{2}-1}{6}.

By Corollary

,

\displaystyle X(nI_{n}-J_{n})X

\displaystyle=X,

\displaystyle(nI_{n}-J_{n})X(nI_{n}-J_{n})

\displaystyle=nI_{n}-J_{n},

\displaystyle X(nI_{n}-J_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}(nI_{n}-J_{n})=X(nI_{n}-J_{n}).

Therefore,
(nI_{n}-J_{n})^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{n^{2}}(nI_{n}-J_{n}).

L^{(1)}(G)=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{n}I_{n}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{n^{2}}(nI_{n}-J_{n})\end{bmatrix}.

Now by the definition of resistance distance

R(G)=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{2}{n}(J_{n}-I_{n})&\frac{2n-1}{n^{2}}J_{n}\\
\frac{2n-1}{n^{2}}J_{n}&\frac{2}{n}(J_{n}-I_{n})\end{bmatrix}.

Then the
i^{th}
resistance distance degree is given by
R_{i}=\frac{4n-3}{n}.

Now by Corollary
4.1
,
E_{R}(G)=\frac{8n-6}{n}.

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
R_{i}
and
T_{i}
denote the resistance degrees and the second resistance degrees of
G
, respectively, then

T_{1}+\cdots+T_{n}=R_{1}^{2}+\cdots+R_{n}^{2}.

Proof.

By definition
T_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{ij}R_{j}
.

Now

\displaystyle T_{1}+\cdots+T_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{1j}R_{j}+\cdots+\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{nj}R_{j}

\displaystyle=R_{1}(r_{11}+r_{21}+\cdots+r_{n1})+\cdots+R_{n}(r_{1n}+\cdots+r_{nn})

\displaystyle=R_{1}^{2}+\cdots+R_{n}^{2}.

∎

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
R_{i}
denote the resistance degrees of a graph
G
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, then

\rho_{1}(G)\geq\sqrt{\frac{T_{1}^{2}+\cdots+T_{n}^{2}}{R_{1}^{2}+\cdots+R_{n}^{2}}}.

Equality holds if and only if
G
is pseudo resistance regular.

Proof.

Let
Z=\left(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\right)^{T}
be the unit positive Perron eigenvector of
R(G)
corresponding to
\rho_{1}(G).

Take

U=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{i}^{2}}}\left(R_{1},\ldots,R_{n}\right)^{T}.

Then
U
is a unit positive vector.

We have,

\rho_{1}(G)=\sqrt{\rho_{1}(G)^{2}}=\sqrt{Z^{T}R^{2}Z}\geq\sqrt{U^{T}R^{2}U}.

\displaystyle RU

\displaystyle=[r_{ij}]_{n\times n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{i}^{2}}}\left(R_{1},R_{2},\ldots,R_{n}\right)^{T}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{i}^{2}}}\left(T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{n}\right)^{T},

\displaystyle U^{T}R^{2}U=(RU)^{T}(RU)

\displaystyle=\frac{T_{1}^{2}+\cdots+T_{n}^{2}}{R_{1}^{2}+\ldots+R_{n}^{2}},

then

\rho_{1}(G)\geq\sqrt{\frac{T_{1}^{2}+\cdots+T_{n}^{2}}{R_{1}^{2}+\cdots+R_{n}^{2}}}.

Now assume that
G
is pseudo resistance regular. So
\frac{T_{i}}{R_{i}}
is a constant (say
l
) for all
i
. Then
RU=lU
, showing that
U
is an eigenvector corresponding to
l
and hence
\rho_{1}(G)=l
. Thus, the equality holds.
Conversely if equality holds then, we get
U
is the eigenvector corresponding to
\rho_{1}(G)
and that
RU=\rho_{1}(G)U
. This implies that
\frac{T_{i}}{R_{i}}=\rho_{1}(G)
or in other words
G
is
pseudo resistance regular.
∎

We present some upper and lower bounds for the resistance energy of a graph
G
. The subsequent corollary is an immediate consequence of Theorem
4.1
.

Corollary 4.3
.

For a graph
G
,

E_{R}(G)\geq 2\sqrt{\frac{T_{1}^{2}+\cdots+T_{n}^{2}}{R_{1}^{2}+\cdots+R_{n}^{2}}}.

Equality holds if and only if
G
is pseudo resistance regular.

Consider the
R
-eigenvalues of
G
,
\rho_{1}(G),\ldots,\rho_{n}(G)
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}(G)=0.
Let
S(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}(G)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{ij}^{2}.

Theorem 4.2
.

For a graph
G,

E_{R}(G)\leq\alpha(G)+\sqrt{(n-1)\left(S(G)-\alpha(G)\right)},

(2)

where
\alpha(G)=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{i}^{2}}}.

Equality holds if and only if
G
is a complete graph.

Proof.

Consider

\displaystyle(E_{R}(G)-\rho_{1}(G))^{2}=\left(\sum_{i=2}^{n}|\rho_{i}(G)|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(n-1)\sum_{i=2}^{n}(\rho_{i}(G))^{2}

(3)

\displaystyle=(n-1)(S(G)-(\rho_{1}(G))^{2})

Thus,
E_{R}(G)\leq\rho_{1}(G)+\sqrt{(n-1)(S(G)-(\rho_{1}(G))^{2})}.

Now consider the function
g(x)=x+\sqrt{(n-1)(S(G)-x^{2})}
for
\frac{2Kf(G)}{n}\leq x\leq\sqrt{S(G)}.

The function
g(x)
is monotonically decreasing for
x\geq\sqrt{\frac{S(G)}{n}}.

For any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

\displaystyle R_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{ij}\right)^{2}\leq n\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{ij}^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{ij}^{2}=nS(G).

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{ij}R_{j}\right)^{2}\geq S(G)^{2}.

Then by Theorem
4.1
,

\rho_{1}(G)\geq\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{i}^{2}}}\geq\sqrt{\frac{S(G)}{n}}.

Therefore,
E_{R}(G)\leq g(\rho_{1}(G))\leq g\left(\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{i}^{2}}}\right).

Suppose equality holds in (
2
), then
\rho_{1}(G)=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{i}^{2}}}
, by Theorem
4.1

G
is pseudo resistance regular. Applying equality condition in Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=2}^{n}|\rho_{i}(G)|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=(n-1)\sum_{i=2}^{n}(\rho_{i}(G))^{2}.

Hence,
|\rho_{2}(G)|=\cdots=|\rho_{n}(G)|.

Further, from (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\left((n-1)|\rho_{i}(G)|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=(n-1)\sum_{i=2}^{n}(\rho_{i}(G))^{2}=(n-1)\left(S(G)-(\rho_{1}(G))^{2}\right),

\displaystyle|\rho_{i}(G)|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{S(G)-(\rho_{1}(G))^{2}}{n-1}},\;i\in\{2,3,\ldots,n\}.

That is,
G
has at most two distinct

R
-eigenvalues. Therefore, by Theorem
2.3
,
G
is a complete graph. Hence the theorem.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\overline{R_{i}}
denotes the average resistance degree of a graph
G
, then

\rho_{1}(G)\leq\max\limits_{i\neq j}\sqrt{{\overline{R_{i}}}\,{\overline{R_{j}}}}.

Equality holds if and only if
G
is pseudo resistance regular.

Proof.

Let
T(G)=diag(R_{1},\ldots,R_{n})
and
M=T(G)^{-1}RT(G)
, then the
(i,j)^{th}
element of
M
is
\frac{R_{j}}{R_{i}}r_{ij}.
Now let
y=\left(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\right)
be the eigenvector of
T(G)^{-1}RT(G)
corresponding to
\rho_{1}(G),
with

\displaystyle y_{p}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{max}\{y_{i}:1\leq i\leq n\},

\displaystyle y_{q}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{max}\{y_{i}:y_{i}\neq y_{p},1\leq i\leq n\}.

Also,

\displaystyle\left(T(G)^{-1}RT(G)\right)y

\displaystyle=\rho_{1}(G)y.

Then

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(G)y_{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{R_{i}}{R_{p}}r_{pi}y_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{R_{i}}{R_{p}}r_{pi}y_{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{T_{p}}{R_{p}}y_{q}=\overline{R}_{p}y_{q}.

That is,

\rho_{1}(G)y_{p}\leq\overline{R}_{p}y_{q}.

(4)

Similarly,

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(G)y_{q}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{R_{i}}{R_{q}}r_{qi}y_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{R_{i}}{R_{q}}r_{qi}y_{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{T_{q}}{R_{q}}y_{p}=\overline{R}_{q}y_{p}.

Thus,

\rho_{1}(G)y_{q}\leq\overline{R}_{q}y_{p}.

(5)

From equations (
4
) and (
5
), we get

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(G)^{2}y_{p}y_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{R}_{p}\overline{R}_{q}y_{p}y_{q},

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(G)

\displaystyle\leq\max\limits_{i\neq j}\sqrt{\overline{R}_{i}\overline{R}_{j}}.

Suppose equality holds, then
\overline{R}_{1}=\cdots=\overline{R}_{n}.
That is
G
is pseudo resistance regular. Conversely, if
G
is pseudo resistance regular, then
\overline{R}_{1}=\cdots=\overline{R}_{n}=c
. Then
the
i^{th}
row sum of
M
is
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{R_{j}}{R_{i}}r_{ij}=\overline{R}_{i}=c,\;i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Therefore,
\rho_{1}(M)=\rho_{1}(R)=c
, since the matrices
M
and
R
are similar. Hence the theorem.

∎

.

Let

Also,

\displaystyle\left(T(G)^{-1}RT(G)\right)y

Then

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(G)y_{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{R_{i}}{R_{p}}r_{pi}y_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{R_{i}}{R_{p}}r_{pi}y_{q}

(6)

Similarly,

From equations (

∎

Proposition 5.1

Let

-resistance regular graph

Q(x)=n\frac{(x-\rho_{2}(G))\ldots(x-\rho_{t}(G))}{(k-\rho_{2}(G))\ldots(k-\rho_{t}(G))},

\frac{1}{4}\left(r_{ij}(G)+\frac{2}{r}\right),&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

In the following theorem, we obtain the resistance eigenvalues of the double graph of a resistance regular graph.

Theorem 5.1
.

Proof.

By a proper labelling of vertices in
D_{2}G

R(D_{2}G)=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{4}R+\frac{1}{2r}(J-I)&\frac{1}{4}R+\frac{1}{2r}(J+I)\\

From Lemma
2.5

Spec_{R}(D_{2}G)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\rho_{1}(G)}{2}+\frac{n}{r}&\frac{\rho_{2}(G)}{2}&\cdots&\frac{\rho_{n}(G)}{2}&-\frac{1}{r}\\

Next theorem gives an upper bound for the Kirchoff index of a graph in terms of its number of vertices and

-eigenvalues.

Theorem 5.4
.

For a graph
G
with
n

\mathcal{K}f(G)\leq\frac{\sqrt{n(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}(G)^{2}}}{2}.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}(G)^{2}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{trace}(R(G)^{2})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}r_{ij}^{2}=2\sum_{i<j}r_{ij}^{2}.

\displaystyle(\mathcal{K}f(G))^{2}=(\sum_{i<j}r_{ij})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\sum_{i<j}r_{ij}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n-1)}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}(G)^{2}.

Therefore,

\mathcal{K}f(G)\leq\frac{\sqrt{n(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\rho_{i}(G)^{2}}}{2}.

∎

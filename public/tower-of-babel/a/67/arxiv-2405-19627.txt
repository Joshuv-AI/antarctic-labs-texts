On the Cesàroro operator on the Hardy space in the upper half-plane
Valentin V. Andreev, Miron B. Bekker, Joseph A. Cima, Department of Mathematics, vvandreev8@gmail.com , bekker@pitt.edu , cima@email.unc.edu

Abstract
We discuss the Cesàro operator on the Hardy space in the upper half-plane. We provide a new simple proof of the boundedness of this operator, prove that this operator is equal to the sum of the identity operator and a unitary operator, which implies its normality.



Keywords:
Cesàro operator, Hardy space, reproducing kernel, unitary

operator.

MSC 2020:
47B38, 30H10, 47B32

Introduction

In a classical paper by A. Brown, P. R. Halmos and A. L. Shields
[
2
]
were investigated the Cesáro operators on different spaces (
l^{2}
,
L^{2}(0,1)

L^{2}(0,\infty)
). In particular, in
[
2
]
was proved that
I-C_{\infty}^{*}
is a bilateral shift of multiplicity one, where
C_{\infty}
is the Cesàro operator on
L^{2}(0,\infty)
.
From that time study of Cesàro operators attracted a lot of attention and many important results were obtained. We mention the result of T. L. Kriete and David Trutt
[
6
]
who proved that the Cesàro operator on
l^{2}
is subnormal.

Study of the Cesàro operators on spaces of analytic functions is currently an active field of research on intersection of operator theory and complex analysis. Many aspects of of the theory of Cesàro operators in different settings, as well as some generalizations of Cesàro operators, are presented in the survey article by W. Ross
[
7
]
.

In 2013 A. Arvanitidis and A. Siskasis
[
1
]
introduced the Cesàro operator on the Hardy spaces
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
in the upper half-plane
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
. They proved that the operator is bounded for
1<p\leq\infty
and determined its spectrum for
1<p<\infty
. These results were obtained using profound theorems from the theory of semigroups, in particular, the semigroups of composition operators. Application of this technique to the investigation of invariant subspaces of the Cesàro operator on the Hardy space
H^{2}({\mathbb{D}})
can be found in the recent paper
[
5
]
.

In the present paper we give an alternative elementary proof of the boundedness of the Cesàro operator on
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. For
p=2
we proof in an elementary way that the Cesàro operator is the sum of the identity operator and a unitary operator, from which it follows, in particular, that the Cesàro operator on
H^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
is normal. In our approach we use the fact that
H^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space. Note that the representation of the Cesàro operator as the sum of the identity operator and a unitary operator can be obtained by further refinement of the methods that were used in
[
1
]
.

2
Boundedness of the Cesàro operator

We denote by
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
the upper half-plane,
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}=\{z=x+iy:y>0\}
. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
we denote by
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
the Hardy space in
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
, i.e. a function
f\in H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
(
1\leq p<\infty
) if
f
is holomorphic in
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
and

\sup_{y>0}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x+iy)|^{p}dx<\infty.

(1)

Also,

\|f\|_{p}=\left[\sup_{y>0}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x+iy)|^{p}dx\right]^{1/p},

(2)

and for
p=\infty
,
\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}}|f(z)|
.
For
p=2
the Hardy space
H^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
is a Hilbert space and for
f,g\in H^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
the inner product
<f,g>
is defined by the formula

<f,g>=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\overline{g(x)}dx

(3)

where
f(x)=\lim_{z\to x}f(z)
and the limit exists non-tangentially for almost all
x
with respect to the Lebesgue measure.
The boundary function
f(x)
belongs to
L^{2}(-\infty,\infty)
.

The Cesàro operator
C
is defined on
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
by the following expression

(Cf)(z)=\frac{1}{z}\int\limits_{0}^{z}f(\zeta)d\zeta.

(4)

Theorem 1
.

For
1<p\leq\infty
the Cesàro operator is bounded on
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
.

Proof.

The statement about
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
is obvious, and
\|C\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. Here

\|C\|_{\infty}
is the norm of the Cesàro operator on
H^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
.

Consider now the case
1<p<\infty
. Put in (
4
)
\zeta=zs
,
0\leq s\leq 1
. Then (
4
) takes the form

(Cf)(z)=\int\limits_{0}^{1}f(zs)ds.

Consequently,

\displaystyle|Cf(z)|\leq\int\limits_{0}^{1}|f(zs)|s^{\beta}s^{-\beta}ds\leq

\displaystyle\left[\int\limits_{0}^{1}s^{\beta p}|f(zs)|^{p}ds\right]^{1/p}\left[\int\limits_{0}^{1}s^{-\beta q}ds\right]^{1/q}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\beta q)^{1/q}}\left[\int\limits_{0}^{1}s^{\beta p}|f(zs)|^{p}ds\right]^{1/p}.

Here
1/p+1/q=1
and
0<\beta<1/q
. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}|(Cf)(x+iy)|^{p}dx\leq\frac{1}{(1-\beta q)^{p/q}}\int\limits_{0}^{1}\left[\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}s^{\beta p}|f(sx+isy)|^{p}dx\right]ds=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-\beta q)^{p/q}}\int\limits_{0}^{1}s^{\beta p-1}\left[\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(t+isy)|^{p}dt\right]ds.

Since for each
s\geq 0
and
f\in H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})

\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(t+isy)|^{p}dt\leq\|f\|^{p}_{p}

we obtain

\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}|(Cf)(x+iy)|^{p}dx\leq\frac{\|f\|^{p}_{p}}{(1-\beta q)^{p/q}}\int_{0}^{1}s^{\beta p-1}ds=\frac{1}{(1-\beta q)^{p/q}\beta p}\|f\|_{p}^{p}.

Last inequality means

\|Cf\|_{p}\leq\frac{1}{(1-\beta q)^{1/q}(\beta p)^{1/p}}\|f\|_{p},

(5)

that is the Cesàro operator is bounded. Moreover,
\|C\|\leq\inf\{(1-\beta q)^{-1/q}(\beta p)^{-1/p}:0<\beta<(p-1)/p\}.

A simple calculation gives

\|C\|_{p}\leq\frac{p}{p-1}.

In
[
1
]
it was proved that
\|C\|_{p}=p/(p-1)
.

Simple examples show the theorem does not hold for
p=1
.
∎

Remark 1
.

In
[
8
]
it was proved that the class
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
coincides with the class of functions
f
which are holomorphic in the upper half-plane and such that

(\|f\|_{p}^{*})^{p}=\sup\limits_{0<t<\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}|f(re^{it})|^{p}dr<\infty,\quad 0<p<\infty

and
\|f\|_{p}^{*}
is equivalent to the usual
H^{p}
norm, that is there are constants
0<A_{p}\leq B_{p}<\infty
which depend only on
p
such that

A_{p}\|f\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}^{*}\leq B_{p}\|f\|_{p}
(for
p=2
this class of functions was considered in
[
3
]
).
This fact can also be used to prove the boundedness of the Cesàro operator on
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
in the same way as above.

Remark 2
.

The same arguments that were used in the proof of Theorem
1
allow to prove the following slightly more general statement:

Let
\re\mu>-\dfrac{p-1}{p}
,
1<p<\infty
. Then the operator
C_{\mu}
defined by

(C_{\mu}f)(z)=\frac{1}{z^{\mu+1}}\int_{0}^{z}\zeta^{\mu}f(\zeta)d\zeta

is bounded on
H^{p}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
.

3
Cesàro operator on
H^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})

In this section we assume that
p=2
and use the Hilbert space structure of
H^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, in particular, the fact that
H^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space.

Lemma 1
.

The operator
C^{*}
on
H^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
is defined by the formula

(C^{*}f)(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)\frac{1}{t}\log{\left(1-\frac{t}{z}\right)}dt

(6)

Proof.

Recall that for any
f\in H^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
the boundary value
\lim_{y\downarrow 0}f(x+iy)
exists for almost all
x

and the boundary function belongs to
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. We use the same letter
f
for the boundary function. The values of function
f
in the upper half-plane are recovered from the boundary function by the Cauchy formula

f(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{\infty}^{\infty}\frac{f(t)}{t-z}dt.

(7)

(see, for example
[
4
]
, Theorem 11.8).
Formula (
7
) means that the function

k_{z}(w)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{1}{\bar{z}-w}

(8)

is the reproducing kernel for
H^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, that is

f(z)=<f,k_{z}>.

Observe that

(Ck_{z})(\zeta)=\frac{1}{2\pi i\zeta}\int_{0}^{\zeta}\frac{1}{\bar{z}-w}dw=-\frac{1}{2\pi i\zeta}\log{\left(1-\frac{\zeta}{\bar{z}}\right)}

(9)

Since

(C^{*}f)(z)=<C^{*}f,k_{z}>=<f,Ck_{z}>

formula (
6
) follows.
∎

Later on we will need a special case of formula (
6
).

Because
(C^{*}k_{z})(\zeta)=<C^{*}k_{z},k_{\zeta}>=\overline{<Ck_{\zeta},k_{z}>}
it follows that

(C^{*}k_{z})(\zeta)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{1}{\bar{z}}\log{\left(1-\frac{\bar{z}}{\zeta}\right)}.

(10)

Theorem 2
.

For the Cesàro operator on
H^{2}({\mathbb{C}}+)
the following equality is valid

C=I+U

(11)

where
I
is the identity operator and
U
is a unitary operator. In particular, the Cesàro operator on
H^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
is normal.

Proof.

We claim that for any
s,t>0
the following equalities are valid:

<(C-I)k_{is},(C-I)k_{it}>=<k_{is},k_{it}>

(12)

<(C^{*}-I)k_{is},(C^{*}-I)k_{it}>=<k_{is},k_{it}>

(13)

Indeed,

\displaystyle<(C-I)k_{is},(C-I)k_{it}>=

\displaystyle<Ck_{is},Ck_{it}>-<Ck_{is},k_{it}>-<k_{is},Ck_{it}>+<k_{is},k_{it}>=

\displaystyle<Ck_{is},Ck_{it}>-<Ck_{is},k_{it}>-<C^{*}k_{is},k_{it}>+<k_{is},k_{it}>.

Consider the inner product
<Ck_{is},Ck_{it}>
. Using (
9
) and integrating by parts on the interval of the form

[-n,n]
and letting
n
tend to infinity one obtains

\displaystyle<Ck_{is},Ck_{it}>=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{2}}\log{\left(1-i\frac{x}{s}\right)}\log{\left(1+i\frac{x}{t}\right)}dx=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\left\{-\frac{1}{x}\log{\left(1-i\frac{x}{s}\right)}\log{\left(1+i\frac{x}{t}\right)}|_{x=-\infty}^{\infty}+\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x}\frac{d}{dx}\left[\log{\left(1-i\frac{x}{s}\right)}\log{\left(1+i\frac{x}{t}\right)}\right]dx\right\}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{x}\frac{\log{\left(1+i\dfrac{x}{t}\right)}}{1-i\dfrac{x}{s}}\left(-\frac{i}{s}\right)+\frac{1}{x}\frac{\log{\left(1-i\dfrac{x}{s}\right)}}{1+i\dfrac{x}{t}}\left(\frac{i}{t}\right)\right]dx=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x}\frac{\log{\left(1+i\dfrac{x}{t}\right)}}{x+is}dx+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x}\frac{\log{\left(1-i\dfrac{x}{s}\right)}}{x-it}dx

Note that for both integrands the point
x=0
is a regular point. Both integrals are easily evaluated using the calculus of residue. For the first integral we need to close the contour of integration in the lower half plane and for the second integral we need to close the contour of integration in the upper half plane. As result one obtains

<Ck_{is},Ck_{it}>=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\frac{1}{s}\log{\left(1+\frac{s}{t}\right)}+\frac{1}{t}\log{\left(1+\frac{t}{s}\right)}\right]

(14)

From (
14
) it follows that
\|Ck_{is}\|^{2}=\dfrac{\log{2}}{\pi s}
.

According to (
9
) and (
10
)

\displaystyle<Ck_{is},k_{it}>=(Ck_{is})(it)=\frac{1}{2\pi t}\log{\left(1+\frac{t}{s}\right)},

\displaystyle<C^{*}k_{is},k_{it}>=(C^{*}k_{is})(it)=\frac{1}{2\pi s}\log{\left(1+\frac{s}{t}\right)}.

Now (
12
) follows.

In order to prove (
13
) we evaluate
<C^{*}k_{is},C^{*}k_{it}>
. Using (
10
) one obtains

<C^{*}k_{is},C^{*}k_{it}>=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}st}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\log{\left(1+\frac{is}{x}\right)}\log{\left(1-\frac{it}{x}\right)}dx.

Making change of variables
x\mapsto 1/x
and then integrating by parts one obtains

\displaystyle<C^{*}k_{is},C^{*}k_{it}>=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}st}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{x^{2}}\log{(1+isx)}\log{(1-itx)}dx=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}st}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{is\log{(1-itx)}}{x(1+isx)}dx+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}st}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{-it\log{(1+isx)}}{x(1-itx)}dx=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}st}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\log{(1-itx)}}{x(x-\dfrac{i}{s})}dx+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}st}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\log{(1+isx)}}{x(x+\dfrac{i}{t})}dx.

Using calculus of residue again as above one obtains

<C^{*}k_{is},C^{*}k_{it}>=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\frac{1}{t}\log{\left(1+\frac{t}{s}\right)}+\frac{1}{s}\log{\left(1+\frac{s}{t}\right)}\right].

In particular, we have proved that for any
s,t>0

<Ck_{is},Ck_{it}>=<C^{*}k_{is},C^{*}k_{it}>.

(15)

Using again (
9
) and (
10
) we obtain equality (
13
).
Therefore, for any vector
f\in H^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
of the form
f=\sum a_{j}k_{is_{j}}
(the sum is finite)
we have

\|(C-I)f\|^{2}=\|(C^{*}-I)f\|^{2}=\|f\|^{2}.

Let
\{s_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a set of positive distinct numbers which has a limit point
s_{0}>0
. Then the set of reproducing
kernels
\{k_{is_{j}}\}
is linearly independent and
\vee\{k_{is_{j}}:j=1,2,\ldots\}=H^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
. Indeed, if
h\in H^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
and

h\perp k_{is_{j}}
for all
j=1,2,\ldots
, then
f(is_{j})=0
and, since
f
is holomorphic,
f=0
. Our previous consideration implies that
\|(C-I)f\|^{2}=\|(C^{*}-I)f\|^{2}=\|f\|^{2}
for any
f\in H^{2}({\mathbb{C}}_{+})
, that is
that is the operator
C-I
is unitary.
∎

Corollary 1
.

The operator
C+C^{*}
is a positive self-adjoint operator.

Since the operator
C-I
is unitary it follows that
(C^{*}-I)(C-I)=I
, that is
C^{*}C=C+C^{*}
.

The next corollary is a byproduct of the proof of Theorem
2
.

Corollary 2
.

For any
s,t>0

\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{1}{s}\log{\left(1+\frac{s}{t}\right)}+\frac{1}{t}\log{\left(1+\frac{t}{s}\right)}\right]\leq\frac{\log{2}}{\sqrt{st}}

with equality if and only if
s=t
.

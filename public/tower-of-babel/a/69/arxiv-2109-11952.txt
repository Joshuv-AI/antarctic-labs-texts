Abstract

In this section, we show that a simplicial complex

\Omega(n^{3/4})

Definition 3.1
.

Given a group

\langle S|R\rangle\cong G

•

\langle\rangle

•

•

g^{a}h^{b}
, where

.

•

g^{a}h^{b}i^{c}
, where

We say such a word

if the generators used are all
distinct, and

. To “write

in normal form such that
r
and
w
are conjugates in
\langle S\rangle
; in this case we write
r\leadsto w
.

For example, we describe a 3-presentation
\langle S|R\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, derived from the presentation for
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
given in the
introduction:

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\{g_{i}:i\in[n]\}\cup\{h_{i,j}:i,j\in[n],\,i<j\}

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\{g_{i}g_{j}h_{i,j},\,g_{j}g_{i}h_{i,j}:i,j\in[n],\,i<j\}

We will use the phrase, “Let
\phi\colon\langle S|R\rangle\cong G
be a
3-presentation,” to mean, “Let
\langle S|R\rangle\cong G
be a
3-presentation, and fix an isomorphism
\phi\colon\langle S|R\rangle\to G
.”

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\langle S|R\rangle\cong G
be a 3-presentation. Then any relation
r\in R
can be written uniquely in normal form, up to the following
conjugacies:

•

g^{a}h^{b},h^{b}g^{a}
are conjugates in
\langle S\rangle
.

•

g^{a}h^{b}i^{c},h^{b}i^{c}g^{a},i^{c}g^{a}h^{b}
are conjugates in

\langle S\rangle
.

Proof.

If
r
is not in normal form, we can apply one of the following steps:

•

If
r
has a zero exponent or identical adjacent generators, rewrite
r

with fewer generators. (For example,
g^{0}hi
becomes

;
g^{1}g^{2}h

becomes
g^{3}h
.)

•

If

, then replace

with its conjugate
g^{a+c}h^{b}
.

Each such step reduces
k
in
r=\prod_{i=1}^{k}g_{i}^{a_{i}}
, so this
process terminates. Uniqueness follows from considering the conjugates of
reduced words

.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

If
X
is a simplicial complex on

\pi_{1}(X)\cong G
, then there exists a 3-presentation
\langle S|R\rangle\cong G
with
|S|\leq\binom{k}{2}
and
|R|\leq\binom{k}{3}
.

Proof.

Assume

is connected, otherwise reduce to the component of

containing
the basepoint. Then the 1-skeleton of

is a connected graph; choose a
spanning tree
T
of this graph. View
X
as a CW complex and
T
as a
contractible subcomplex of
X
, and consider the quotient complex
X/T
,
which is homotopy equivalent to

(see e.g.,
[

, Prop. 0.17]
), so
\pi_{1}(X/T)\cong G
.

Now

has a single 0-cell, and hence can be viewed as the presentation

\langle S|R\rangle\cong G
upon
choosing a direction for each 1-cell. The 1-cells correspond bijectively to
the generators, and arise from distinct edges of

|S|\leq\binom{k}{2}
. The 2-cells correspond bijectively to the relations, and arise
from distinct triangles of

|R|\leq\binom{k}{3}
.

Moreover, each

is of one of the following forms, depending on how

•

g^{a}
, where

are distinct and

.

•

g^{a}h^{b}
, where

are distinct and

.

•

g^{a}h^{b}i^{c}
, where

In particular,

Hence we may turn our attention to proving lower bounds on
|S|
and
|R|
for

\langle S|R\rangle
of given groups. We will use the concept
of deficiency:

Definition 3.4
.

The

of a group presentation

is

\text{def }P=|S|-|R|
. The
deficiency

\text{def }G
, of a group

Then we have an inequality in group homology due to

\text{def }G\leq\text{rank }H_{1}(G;\mathbb{Z})-s(H_{2}(G;\mathbb{Z})),

.

•

•

For the presentation of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}

\text{def }\mathbb{Z}^{n}=n-\binom{n}{2}

For

Lemma 3.13

.

.

Let
\phi\colon\langle S|R\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}

by adding

Let

is
at most
k
. Summing over components
C
, the number of neighbors of
v
in

(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
is at least
\deg_{(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})}v\geq\lambda
.

Now choose a nonzero vector
\vec{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
not orthogonal to any

v\in V^{\prime}
, and define an affine hyperplane
H=\{\vec{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\vec{x}\cdot\vec{n}=1\}
. Then to each
v\in V^{\prime}
we associate the
unique point
\tilde{v}\in\text{span}(\{v\})\cap H
. Note that the

\tilde{v}
are distinct, since no two points in
V
lie in a common
1-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Also, note that
u,v,w
lie in a
common two-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if and only if
\tilde{u},\tilde{v},\tilde{w}
lie on a common line in
H
. Then the set
\tilde{V^{\prime}}=\{\tilde{v}:v\in V^{\prime}\}
is a
\delta
-SG configuration with
\delta=\lambda/|V|
. By
Theorem

3.18
, the affine dimension of

\tilde{V^{\prime}}
is at most
12/\delta
, so
\dim(\text{span }V^{\prime})\leq 12|V|/\lambda
as desired.
∎

Now we prove our bound on the size of 3-presentations of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
:

Theorem 3.20
.

If
\langle S|R\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is a 3-presentation, then
|S|=\Omega(n^{3/2})
.

Proof.

Fix an isomorphism
\phi\colon\langle S|R\rangle\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Assume that
|S|
is minimal, and consider the images
\phi(g)
for
g\in S
.
By
Lemma

3.8
, all
\phi(g)
are nonzero; by
Lemma

3.9
, no two

\phi(g)
lie in a common 1-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Moreover, by
Lemma

3.10
, for each
r\in R
we have
r\leadsto g^{a}h^{b}i^{c}
for
g,h,i\in S
distinct, and
\dim r=2
. Let
R^{\prime}
be
an inclusion-wise maximal sparse subset of
R
.

Now let
k=|S|
, let
c>0
be a constant to be determined later, and apply

Theorem

3.19
with
V=\phi(S)
,
E=\{\{\phi(g),\phi(h),\phi(i)\}:r\leadsto g^{a}h^{b}i^{c},\,r\in R^{\prime}\}
, and
\lambda=ck/n
, to obtain
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
such that:

(1)

|R^{\prime}\setminus R[S^{\prime}]|\leq ck^{2}/n
.

(2)

\dim S^{\prime}\leq 12|V|/\lambda=12n/c
.

If there exists
g\in S\setminus S^{\prime}
with
\dim(S^{\prime}\cup\{g\})=\dim S^{\prime}
,
then we may replace
S^{\prime}
with
S^{\prime}\cup\{g\}
, preserving (1) and (2).
Therefore, we may assume that for each
g\in S\setminus S^{\prime}
, we have

\phi(g)\not\in\text{span }\phi(S^{\prime})
.

Now partition
R
as
R=R_{s}\sqcup R_{e}\sqcup R_{o}
, where:

•

R_{s}=R^{\prime}\setminus R[S^{\prime}]
.

•

R_{e}=(R\setminus R^{\prime})\setminus R[S^{\prime}]
.

•

R_{o}=R[S^{\prime}]
.

Note that
|R_{s}|\leq ck^{2}/n
by the above. The set
R_{s}
is sparse, since
sparseness is closed under taking subsets. Also, for each
r\in R_{e}
with

r\leadsto g^{a}h^{b}i^{c}
, we have
\{g,h,i\}\subseteq S^{\prime\prime}
for some
critical
S^{\prime\prime}\subseteq S
for
R^{\prime}
, since
r\not\in R^{\prime}
and
R^{\prime}
is maximal.
But since
r\not\in R[S^{\prime}]
, we have
\{g,h,i\}\not\subseteq S^{\prime}
; assume
WLOG
g\not\in S^{\prime}
. Then
\phi(g)\not\in\text{span }\phi(S^{\prime})
by the above,
so
\text{span }\phi(S^{\prime\prime})\not\subseteq\text{span }\phi(S^{\prime})
. Then
R[S^{\prime\prime}]\cap R[S^{\prime}]=\emptyset
, since any
r^{\prime}\in R[S^{\prime\prime}]
determines the
2-dimensional subspace
\text{span }\phi(S^{\prime\prime})
. Therefore,
S^{\prime\prime}
is also
critical for
R_{s}
. Hence we may apply
Lemma

3.15
to
\langle S|R\rangle
, to obtain a 3-presentation
\langle S^{\prime\prime}|R^{\prime\prime}\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with
|R^{\prime\prime}|-|S^{\prime\prime}|=|R_{s}|+|R_{o}|-|S|
.

Finally, let
d=\dim S^{\prime}
, and apply
Lemma

3.16
to
\langle S^{\prime\prime}|R^{\prime\prime}\rangle
using
S^{\prime}\subseteq S^{\prime\prime}
, to obtain
\langle S^{\prime\prime\prime}|R^{\prime\prime\prime}\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}
. Then remove all relations in
R^{\prime\prime\prime}
arising from
relations in
R_{o}=R[S^{\prime}]
, which are now trivial, to obtain
\langle S^{\prime\prime\prime}|R^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n-d}
. Then

\displaystyle|R^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}|-|S^{\prime\prime\prime}|

\displaystyle=(|R^{\prime\prime\prime}|-|R_{o}|)-|S^{\prime\prime\prime}|

\displaystyle=(|R^{\prime\prime}|+d-|R_{o}|)-(|S^{\prime\prime}|-|S^{\prime}|)

\displaystyle=(|R^{\prime\prime}|-|S^{\prime\prime}|-|R_{o}|)+d+|S^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=(|R_{s}|-|S|)+d+|S^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=|R_{s}|+d-|S\setminus S^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq ck^{2}/n+d.

But by the bound
\text{def }\mathbb{Z}^{m}\leq m-\binom{m}{2}
, we have

|R^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}|-|S^{\prime\prime\prime}|=\Omega((n-d)^{2})
. Take
c=24
; then
d\leq 12n/c=n/2
, so
n-d\geq n/2
. Hence
|R^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}|-|S^{\prime\prime\prime}|=\Omega(n^{2})
. Since

d\leq n/2
, we have
ck^{2}/n=\Omega(n^{2})
. Therefore,
k=\Omega(n^{3/2})
as desired.
∎

Theorem 3.21
.

A simplicial complex
X
with fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
has at least
\Omega(n^{3/4})
vertices.

Proof.

Let
f(n)
be the minimum number of vertices in a simplicial complex
X_{n}

with fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X_{n})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. By

Lemma

3.3
, for each
n
we obtain a 3-presentation
\langle S_{n}|R_{n}\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with
|S_{n}|\leq\binom{f(n)}{2}
.
But
|S_{n}|=\Omega(n^{3/2})
, so
\binom{f(n)}{2}=\Omega(n^{3/2})
, hence

f(n)=\Omega(n^{3/4})
.
∎

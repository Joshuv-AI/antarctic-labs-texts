Abstract
The celebrated Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem [ 1 ] states that the maximum intersecting k k -uniform family on [ n ] [n] is a full star if n ≥ 2 ​ k + 1 n\geq 2k+1 . Furthermore, Hilton-Milner [ 9 ] showed that if an intersecting k k -uniform family on [ n ] [n] is not a subfamily of a full star, then its maximum size achieves only on a family isomorphic to H M ( n , k ) := { G ∈ ( [ n ] k ) : 1 ∈ G , G ∩ [ 2 , k + 1 ] ≠ ∅ } ∪ { [ 2 , k + 1 ] } HM(n,k):=\Bigl\{G\in{[n]\choose k}:1\in G,G\cap[2,k+1]\neq\emptyset\Bigr\}\cup\Bigl\{[2,k+1]\Bigr\} if n > 2 ​ k n>2k and k ≥ 4 k\geq 4 , and there is one more possibility in the case of k = 3 k=3 . Han and Kohayakawa [ 8 ] determined the maximum intersecting k k -uniform family on [ n ] [n] which is neither a subfamily of a full star nor a subfamily of the extremal family in Hilton-Milner theorm, and they asked what is the next maximum intersecting k k -uniform family on [ n ] [n] . Kostochka and Mubayi [ 11 ] gave the answer for large enough n n . In this paper, we are going to get rid of the requirement that n n is large enough in the result by Kostochka and Mubayi [ 11 ] and answer the question of Han and Kohayakawa [ 8 ] .

are cross-intersecting, as desired.
∎

Claim 3.18
.

\mathcal{A}_{3}
is intersecting.

Proof.

,

, a contradiction. Hence we only need to consider the following case :

and
|A^{\prime}\cap Y_{i}|=2
. Now we show the conclusion for each case of Lemma

. All sets below are inherited from the proof of Lemma
2.6
for each cooresponding case.

If we meet

in Lemma
2.6
, then by Lemma

, we have that

or

, and we may assume that
G\cap X_{i}=\{x\}
and

. Respectively,
\{y,y^{\prime},z_{1},w_{1}\}
or

\in\mathcal{G}
, which is disjoint from

or

.

If we meet

in Lemma
2.6
, then by Lemma
3.9
, we have that
X_{2}=\{x,y,z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}\}

or

. So we may assume that

. Then

, a contradiction. If
\{z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z^{\prime}_{4}\}\in\mathcal{G}
, then every other member in

contains
x
or
y
, we may assume that
G\cap X_{i}=\{x\}
and

. Since
\mathcal{G}
is stable, we may assume that

. Recall that

, hence
\{z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z^{\prime}_{4}\}

If we meet
Cases 3
in Lemma
2.6
, then by Lemma

It is natural to ask what is the maximum size of a
k
-uniform intersecting family
\mathcal{F}
with
\tau(\mathcal{F})\geq 3
. About this problem, Frankl
[
3
]
gave an upper bound for sufficient large
n
. To introduce the result, we need the following construction.

Construction 4.1
.

Let
x\in[n]
,
Y\subseteq[n]
with
|Y|=k
, and
Z\subseteq[n]
with
|Z|=k-1
,
x\not\in Y\cup Z
,
Z\cap Y=\emptyset
and
Y_{0}=\{y_{1},y_{2}\}\subseteq Y
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}=\{A\subseteq[n]:x\in A,A\cap Y\neq\emptyset\,\,\text{and}\,\,A\cap Z\neq\emptyset\}\cup\{Y,Z\cup\{y_{1}\},Z\cup\{y_{2}\},\{x,y_{1},y_{2}\}\},

\displaystyle FP(n,k)=\{F\subseteq[n]:|F|=k,\exists G\in\mathcal{G}\,s.t.,G\subseteq F\}.

It is easy to see that
FP(n,k)
is intersecting and
\tau(FP(n,k))=3
.

Theorem 4.2

(Frankl
[
3
]
)
.

Let

n
-vertex

-uniform family with

. Then
|\mathcal{H}|\leq|FP(n,k)|
. Moreover, for

, the equality holds only for
\mathcal{H}=FP(n,k)
.˙

It is interesting to consider what is the maximum
k
-uniform intersecting families with covering number

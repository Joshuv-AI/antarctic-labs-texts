Abstract
. In this paper, we completely classify conformal solitons on pseudo–Riemannian hypersurfaces in pseudo–Euclidean spaces arisen from the position vector field. In particular, the classification of Yamabe solitons on pseudo–Riemannian hypersurfaces in pseudo–Euclidean spaces arisen from the position vector field can be obtained.

53C25, 53C40, 53C42

1.
Introduction

Soliton solutions have been developed rapidly in recent years as important object in geometric flows. In fact, they appear as possible singularity models.
Therefore, one of the most interesting problem is the classification problem of them.
For Ricci solitons, that is, the special solutions of the Ricci flow. S. Brendle
[
2
]
brought significant progress. He showed that ”any 3-dimensional complete noncompact
\kappa
-noncollapsed gradient steady Ricci soliton with positive curvature is the Bryant soliton” which is a famous conjecture of G. Perelman
[
13
]
.

For Yamabe solitons, that is, the special solutions of the Yamabe flow, there are many classification results under some assumptions of the curvatures (cf.
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
8
]
).
Recently, the third author classified nontrivial 3-dimensional complete gradient Yamabe solitons
[
11
]
.
To understand the Yamabe soliton, many generalizations of it have been introduced.
For example, almost Yamabe solitons
[
1
]
(see also
[
14
]
), gradient
k
-Yamabe solitons
[
5
]
,
h
-almost gradient Yamabe solitons
[
19
]
have been introduced.
The most general notion of Yamabe solitons might be conformal solitons
[
9
]
(see also
[
16
]
,
[
15
]
):

Definition 1.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

A Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is called a
conformal soliton
if there exists a complete vector field
v
such that

(1)

\varphi g=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{v}g,

where
\varphi:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function and
\mathcal{L}_{v}g

is the Lie derivative of the metric tensor
g
with respect to
v
.
If
v\equiv 0
, then the conformal soliton is called trivial.

J. Cheeger and T. H. Colding studied conformal gradient solitons and gave a characterization theorem
[
6
]
.
Recently, the second and the third authors classified conformal solitons on hypersurfaces in Euclidean spaces under some assumptions
[
9
]
.

Although the notion of conformal solitons was given and studied in a Riemannian manifold, the conformal soliton equation
(
1
) can be defined in a pseudo–Riemannian manifold as follows.

Definition 1.2
.

A pseudo–Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
is called a
conformal soliton
if there exists a complete vector field
v
such that

(2)

\varphi g=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{v}g,

where
\varphi:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function and
\mathcal{L}_{v}g

is the Lie derivative of the metric tensor
g
with respect to
v
.
We denote the conformal soliton by
(M,g,v,\varphi)
.
If
v\equiv 0
, then
(M,g,v,\varphi)
is called trivial.
If
v
is the gradient of some smooth function
f
on
M
, then
(M,g,f)
is called a conformal gradient soliton.
For smooth functions
f
and
\varphi
, we have
\varphi g=\nabla\nabla f.

Remark 1.3
.

Conformal solitons include Yamabe solitons, almost Yamabe solitons, gradient
k
-Yamabe solitons,
h
-almost Yamabe solitons and conformal gradient solitons. Therefore,
all the results in this paper can be applied to all these solitons.

In
[
23
]
, M. Brozos-Vázquez et. al. studied three dimensional Lorentzian homogeneous Ricci
soliton and the first author
[
24
]
also classified the Ricci soliton on a pseudo–Riemannian
hypersurface of a Minkowski space
\mathbb{E}^{4}_{1}
whose the potential vector field
is the tangential part of the position vector.

The position vector
V
of a pseudo–Riemannian submanifold in the pseudo–Euclidean space
is the most elementary and geometric object, see
[
20
,
21
]
.
A. Fialkow
[
22
]
introduced the notion of concircular vector field
v
on a Riemannian
manifold
M
as vector field which satisfy

(3)

\nabla_{X}v=\mu X,\;\;X\in TM

where
\nabla
denotes the Levi–Civita connection of
M
,

TM
is the tangent bundle of
M

and
\mu
is a nontrivial function on
M
.
As a particular case for the function
\mu=1
, a concircular vector
field
v
is called a concurrent vector field. The notion of
concircular vector fields on Riemannian manifolds can be extended naturally to concircular vector fields
on pseudo–Riemannian manifolds.
There are several studies of concurrent vector fields (see for example
[
3
]
,
[
17
]
and
[
18
]
).
The position vector field
V
on a pseudo-Euclidean space
\mathbb{E}_{s}^{m}
satisfies
\nabla_{X}V=X
, for any vector field
X
. Therefore, the position vector
V
is the best known example as concurrent vector field.

In this paper, we completely classify conformal solitons on a pseudo–Riemannian hypersurface in a pseudo-Euclidean space

\mathbb{E}^{n+1}_{s}
arisen from the position vector field
V
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Any conformal soliton
(M,g,V^{T},\varphi)
on a pseudo–Riemannian hypersurface
M

in a pseudo-Euclidean space

\mathbb{E}^{n+1}_{s}
is contained in a hyperplane, a conic hypersurface, a pseudo hyperbolic space, or a pseudo sphere.

As a corollary, we completely classify Yamabe solitons on a pseudo–Riemannian hypersurface in a pseudo-Euclidean space arisen from the position vector field.

Corollary 1.5
.

Any Yamabe soliton
(M,g,V^{T},\varphi)
on a pseudo–Riemannian hypersurface
M

in a pseudo-Euclidean space

\mathbb{E}^{n+1}_{s}
is contained in a hyperplane, a conic hypersurface, a pseudo hyperbolic space, or a pseudo sphere.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
(N,\tilde{g})
be an
m
-dimensional pseudo-Riemannian manifold and
(M,g)
be an
n
-dimensional submanifold in
(N,\tilde{g})
.
All manifolds in this paper are assumed to be smooth, orientable and connected.
We denote Levi-Civita connections on
(M,g)
and
(N,\tilde{g})
by
\nabla
and
\tilde{\nabla}
, respectively.
The Lie derivative of
g
is defined by

\left(\mathcal{L}_{X}g\right)(Y,Z)=X(g(Y,Z))-g([X,Y],Z)-g(Y,[X,Z]),

for any vector fields
X,Y,Z
on
M
.

For any vector fields
X,Y
tangent to
M
and
\eta
normal to
M
, the formula of Gauss is given by

{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{X}Y={\nabla}_{X}Y+h(X,Y),

where
{\nabla}_{X}Y
and
h(X,Y)
are the tangential and the normal components of
{\tilde{\nabla}}_{X}Y
.
The formula of Weingarten is given by

{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{X}\eta=-A_{\eta}(X)+D_{X}\eta,

where
-A_{\eta}(X)
and
D_{X}\eta
are the tangential and the normal components of
{\widetilde{\nabla}}_{X}\eta
.

A_{\eta}(X)
and
h(X,Y)
are related by

g(A_{\eta}(X),Y)=\tilde{g}(h(X,Y),\eta).

The mean curvature vector
H
of
M
in
N
is given by

\displaystyle H(p)=\frac{1}{n}~\sum_{i=1}^{n}\epsilon_{i}h(e_{i},e_{i}),

where
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is any orthonormal frame on
M
at
p
, and
\epsilon_{i}=g(e_{i},e_{i}).
For any vector fields
X,Y,Z,W
tangent to
M
, the equation of Gauss is given by

\tilde{g}(\widetilde{Rm}(X,Y)Z,W)=

g(Rm(X,Y)Z,W)

+\tilde{g}(h(X,Z),h(Y,W))

-\tilde{g}(h(X,W),h(Y,Z)),

where
Rm
and
\widetilde{Rm}
are Riemannian curvature tensors of
M
and
N
, respectively.
The equation of Codazzi is given by

(\widetilde{Rm}(X,Y)Z)^{\perp}=({\bar{\nabla}}_{X}h)(Y,Z)-({\bar{\nabla}}_{Y}h)(X,Z),

where
(\widetilde{Rm}(X,Y)Z)^{\perp}
is the normal component of
\widetilde{Rm}(X,Y)Z
and
{\bar{\nabla}}_{X}h
is defined by

({\bar{\nabla}}_{X}h)(Y,Z)=D_{X}h(Y,Z)-h({\nabla}_{X}Y,Z)-h(Y,{\nabla}_{X}Z).

If
N
is a space of constant curvature, then the equation of Codazzi reduces to

0=({\bar{\nabla}}_{X}h)(Y,Z)-({\bar{\nabla}}_{Y}h)(X,Z).

Let
\mathbb{E}^{n+1}_{s}
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional pseudo–Euclidean space with the flat metric
\tilde{g}

given as follows

\tilde{g}=-dx_{1}^{2}-\cdots-dx_{s}^{2}+dx_{s+1}^{2}+\cdots+dx_{n+1}^{2},

where
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{s},x_{s+1},\cdots,x_{n+1})
denotes the usual coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
The pseudo–Riemannian space forms in
\mathbb{E}^{n+1}_{s}
are defined by

(4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{n}_{s}(c^{2})

\displaystyle=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{E}^{n+1}_{s}\;|\;\tilde{g}({\bf x},{\bf x})=c^{-2}\}

(5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{n}_{s-1}(-c^{2})

\displaystyle=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{E}^{n+1}_{s}\;|\;\tilde{g}({\bf x},{\bf x})=-c^{-2}\}.

These spaces are complete and of constant curvature
c^{2}
and
-c^{2}
, respectively.

\mathbb{S}^{n}_{s}(c^{2})
and
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{s-1}(-c^{2})
are called a pseudo–sphere and a pseudo–hyperbolic
space, respectively.

3.
Conformal solitons with a concurrent vector field

In this section, we consider a conformal soliton with a concurrent vector field.

Proposition 3.1
.

Any conformal soliton
(M,g,v,\varphi)
which has a concurrent vector field
v
is a conformal gradient soliton with

\varphi=1
.

Proof.

Since
v
is a concurrent vector field, we have

(6)

g(v,X)=g(v,{\nabla}_{X}v)=X(\frac{1}{2}g(v,v)),

and

(7)

\left(\mathcal{L}_{v}g\right)(X,Y)

=
vg(X,Y)-g([v,X],Y)-g(X,[v,Y])

=vg(X,Y)-vg(X,Y)+g({\nabla}_{X}v,Y)+g(X,{\nabla}_{Y}v)

=2g(X,Y),

for any vector fields
X
,
Y
on
M
.
By putting
f=\frac{1}{2}g(v,v)
on the equation
(\ref{c.0})
, we obtain
v=\nabla f
.
Substituting
(\ref{c.1})
into
(\ref{CS})
, we have

\varphi=1.

∎

4.
Conformal solitons on pseudo–Riemannian submanifolds

In this section, we assume that
(N,\tilde{g})
is a pseudo–Riemannian manifold endowed with a concurrent vector field
V
and
(M,g)
is a pseudo–Riemannian submanifold in
(N,\tilde{g})
.

V^{T}
and
V^{\perp}
denote the tangential and the normal components of
V
, respectively.

Firstly, we show the following lemma which will be used later for the purpose of classification of the conformal solitons.

Lemma 4.1
.

Any conformal soliton
(M,g,V^{T},\varphi)
on a pseudo–Riemannian submanifold
M
in
N
satisfies

(8)

(\varphi-1)g(X,Y)=g(A_{V^{\perp}}(X),Y),

for any vector fields
X,Y
on
M
.

Proof.

From the definition of the Lie derivative, we have

(9)

(\mathcal{L}_{V^{T}}g)(X,Y)

=
V^{T}g(X,Y)-g(\nabla_{V^{T}}X-\nabla_{X}{V^{T}},Y)-g(X,\nabla_{V^{T}}Y-\nabla_{Y}{V^{T}})

=g(\nabla_{X}{V^{T}},Y)+g(X,\nabla_{Y}{V^{T}})

=\tilde{g}(\tilde{\nabla}_{X}{V}-\tilde{\nabla}_{X}{V^{\perp}},Y)+\tilde{g}(X,\tilde{\nabla}_{Y}{V}-\tilde{\nabla}_{Y}{V^{\perp}})

=2g(X,Y)+2g(A_{V^{\perp}}(X),Y)
,

for any vector fields
X,Y
on
M
.
Combining
(\ref{s.3})
with
(\ref{CSn})
, we obtain
(\ref{ENSAYS})
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Any conformal soliton
(M,g,V^{T},\varphi)
on a pseudo–Riemannian submanifold
M
in
N

is a conformal gradient soliton.

Proof.

Set

\displaystyle f=\frac{1}{2}~\tilde{g}(V,V).

For any vector field
X
on
M
, we obtain

g(V^{T},X)=\tilde{g}(V,X)=\tilde{g}(V,\tilde{\nabla}_{X}V)=X(\frac{1}{2}\tilde{g}(V,V))=Xf=g(\nabla f,X).

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

If a conformal soliton
(M,g,V^{T},\varphi)
on a pseudo–Riemannian submanifold
M
in
N
is minimal,
then
\varphi=1
.

Proof.

Let
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
be an orthonormal frame on
M
. By Lemma
4.1
, we have

(\varphi-1)g_{ij}=g(A_{V^{\perp}}(e_{i}),e_{j})=\tilde{g}(h(e_{i},e_{j}),V^{\perp}).

Since
M
is minimal and taking the trace, we obtain

n(\varphi-1)=n\tilde{g}(H,V^{\perp})=0.

Therefore, we conclude that

\varphi=1.

∎

To show Theorem

B. Y. Chen and Y. M. Oh

(

We can relax the assumption, and get the following.

Proposition 5.2

Let

Then,

For the position vector field

So we conclude

Therefore, by the same argument as in the proof of Proposition
5.1
, one can get the proposition.
∎

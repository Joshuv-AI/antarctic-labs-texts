1
Introduction

Definition 1.1

Consider a random variable
X
with density
f
, the differential entropy of
X
is

h(X)=h(f):=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\ln f(x)dx.

Definition 1.2

Set
X
is a random variable with absolutely continuous density
f
and its Radon-Nikodym derivative
f^{\prime}
, call

J(X)=J(f):=\int_{\{f(x)>0\}}\frac{f^{\prime}(x)^{2}}{f(x)}dx

the Fisher information of
X
.

If a density
\hat{f}
satisfies
\hat{f}(x)=f(x),a.s.
then we put

J(\hat{f})=J(f).

In other cases, we set
J(X)=\infty.

There is a inequility focused by some studies, the Entropy Power Inequility(EPI)

e^{2h(X+Y)}\geq e^{2h(X)}+e^{2h(Y)},

or its corollary, the Entropy Jump Inequility(EJI)

h(\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}})\geq\dfrac{h(X)+h(Y)}{2},

(1.1)

for any independent random variables
X,Y
with densities. Carlen(1991)
[
3
]
used the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Semigroup to prove the existance of the non-zero gap
\delta
in EJI and the entropic central limit theorem can be a directly corollary.

Johnson(2000)
[
5
]
extended Carlen’s work to the independent case with some special conditions(not just i.i.d.), and Ball etc.(2003)
[
2
]
, Johnson etc.(2004)
[
7
]
gave a feasible expression of the gap
\delta
for i.i.d. case respectively by different methods. Particularly, the paper
[
7
]
showed the speed of convergence in standard Fisher information sense is
O(\frac{1}{n}).
Ball ect.(2004)
[
1
]
also gave the speed and considered the weighted sum case. We analogize the porjection method in Johnson(2004)
[
7
]
to the indepenednt case(not i.i.d.), and obtain that for two nonsingular random variables
X,Y
with absolutely continuous densities and their Poincar
\acute{\mbox{e}}
constant satisfies
R^{*}_{X},R^{*}_{Y}<\infty
, then
\exists a=a(\sigma^{2}_{x},\sigma^{2}_{Y}),R=R(R_{X}^{*},R_{Y}^{*})>0
,

h(\dfrac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}})-\dfrac{h(X)+h(Y)}{2}\geq\dfrac{\min\{a,1\}}{\min\{a,1\}+2R}\left[\dfrac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi e\dfrac{\sigma_{X}^{2}+\sigma_{Y}^{2}}{2})-\dfrac{h(X)+h(Y)}{2}\right].

Recently, Johnson(2020)
[
6
]
relaxed the finite Poincar
\acute{\mbox{e}}
constant condition for entropy gap theory used some functional analysis tools.

Definition 2.1

Give a random variable
X

R_{X}^{*}=\sup_{g\in H_{1}^{*}(X)}\dfrac{\mathbb{E}[g(X)^{2}]}{\mathbb{E}[g^{\prime}(X)^{2}]},

H_{1}^{*}(X):=\{f|f~is~absolutely~continuous,\text{Var}[f(X)]>0,\mathbb{E}[f(X)]=0,\mathbb{E}[f(X)^{2}]<\infty,\mathbb{E}[f^{\prime}(X)]=0\}.

R^{*}_{X}
is called the Poincar
\acute{\mbox{e}}
constant of
X
.

By the definition,
R^{*}_{aX}=a^{2}R^{*}_{X},\;\forall a\neq 0.
Suppose finite Poincar
\acute{\mbox{e}}
constant, then Johnson(2004)
[
7
]
obtained:

Proposition 2.1

(
[

Consider
X_{1},X_{2}
IID with absolutely continuous densities, variance

and restricted Poincar
\acute{\mbox{e}}
constant

J(\dfrac{X_{1}+X_{2}}{\sqrt{2}})\leq\dfrac{2R^{*}}{\sigma^{2}+2R^{*}}J(X_{1}).

\displaystyle h(\dfrac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}})-\dfrac{h(X)+h(Y)}{2}

Central Limit Theorem for Independent Sum

Theorem 4.1

Consider an independent nonsingular random variable sequence
X_{1},X_{2},\cdots
with absolutely continuous densities, and denote the sum under the power of 2 as

\hat{S}_{n}=\dfrac{X_{1}+X_{2}+\cdots+X_{2^{n}}}{2^{\frac{n}{2}}}.

If the sequence
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
satisfies the following conditions:

1.

Uniformly finite Poincar
\acute{\mbox{e}}
condition
R:=\sup_{n}R^{*}_{X_{i}}<\infty,\;\;\forall i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
;

2.

Stable entropy condition

h(\dfrac{X_{1}+X_{2}\cdots+X_{2^{m}}}{2^{m/2}})=h(\dfrac{X_{2^{m}+1}+X_{2^{m}+2}\cdots+X_{2^{m+1}}}{2^{m/2}}),\;\;\forall m\in\mathbb{N};

3.

Convergent variance condition
\text{Var}(\hat{S}_{n})\to\sigma^{2}>0,\;n\to\infty
, for a real number
\sigma
;

4.

Zero mean condition
\mathbb{E}(X_{i})=0,\;\;\forall i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

Then

\|p_{\hat{S}_{n}}-\phi_{\sigma^{2}}\|_{L^{1}}\to 0,\;\;n\to\infty,

(4.1)

where
p_{\hat{S}_{n}}
is the density of
\hat{S}_{n}
,
\phi_{\sigma^{2}}
is the density of
\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
.

Proof:
For any given
m
, by condtion 1 and (
3.1
). we can use induction to show that

R^{*}_{\frac{X_{1}+X_{2}\cdots+X_{2^{m}}}{2^{m/2}}}\leq R,R^{*}_{\frac{X_{2^{m}+1}+X_{2^{m}+2}\cdots+X_{2^{m+1}}}{2^{m/2}}}\leq R,\;\;\forall m\in\mathbb{N}.

We denote
h_{m}:=h(\hat{S}_{m})
, then due to Theorem
3.4
and condition 2, we obtain

h_{m+1}-h_{m}\geq\dfrac{\min\{b,1\}}{\min\{b,1\}+2R}\left(\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi e\text{Var}(\hat{S}_{m+1}))-h_{m}\right),\;\;\forall m\in\mathbb{N},

(4.2)

where
b:=\inf_{n}\text{Var}(\hat{S}_{n})>0
since the condition 3 and the nonsingular condition hold.

On the other hand, according to EJI (
1.1
), sequence
\{h_{n},n\geq 1\}
is increasing with upper bound
\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi e[\sup_{n}\text{Var}(\hat{S}_{n})])<\infty
since the condition 3, thus the sequence
\{h_{n},n\geq 1\}
is convergent. This implies

h_{m+1}-h_{m}\to 0,\;\;m\to\infty.

Recall that
\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi e\text{Var}(\hat{S}_{m+1}))-h_{m}\geq h_{m+1}-h_{m}\geq 0
, from (
4.2
) we obatin

\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi e\text{Var}(\hat{S}_{m+1}))-h_{m}\to 0,\;\;m\to\infty,

therefore by condition 3,

\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi e\text{Var}(\hat{S}_{m}))-h_{m}\to 0,\;\;m\to\infty,

i.e.

\text{KL}(\hat{S}_{m}\|\mathcal{N}(0,\text{Var}(\hat{S}_{m})))\to 0,\;\;m\to\infty,

since condition 4 holds. By Pinsker inequility,

\|p_{\hat{S}_{n}}-\phi_{\text{Var}(\hat{S}_{m})}\|_{L^{1}}\to 0,\;\;n\to\infty.

Then the proof is completed since

\|p_{\hat{S}_{n}}-\phi_{\sigma^{2}}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|p_{\hat{S}_{n}}-\phi_{\text{Var}(\hat{S}_{n})}\|_{L^{1}}+\|\phi_{\sigma^{2}}-\phi_{\text{Var}(\hat{S}_{n})}\|_{L^{1}},\;\;\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},

and condition 3.

\blacksquare

Corollary 4.1

If the condition 3 in Theorem
4.1
is replaced by

\text{Var}(X_{i})=\sigma^{2},\;\;\forall i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},

then

\text{KL}(\hat{S}_{n}\|\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}))=O\left((\dfrac{2R}{\min\{\sigma^{2},1\}+2R})^{n}\right),\;\;n\to\infty.

(4.3)

Proof:
By (
4.2
) and equal variance condition,

h-h_{m+1}\leq(1-c)(h-h_{m}),\;\;\forall m\in\mathbb{N},

where
h=\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi e\sigma^{2}),c=\dfrac{\min\{\sigma^{2},1\}}{\min\{\sigma^{2},1\}+2R}.
Thus

\text{KL}(\hat{S}_{n}\|\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}))=h-h_{n}\leq(1-c)^{n}(h-h_{0}),

where
h_{0}=h(X_{1})
. Using Pinsker inequility again we complete the proof.

\blacksquare

For normal sum
S_{n}:=1/\sqrt{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}

4.1
.

Corollary 4.2

Consider an independent nonsingular random variable sequence
X_{1},X_{2},\cdots
with absolutely continuous densities, and denote the sum

S_{n}:=\dfrac{X_{1}+X_{2}+\cdots+X_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}.

If the sequence
\{X_{n},n\geq 1\}
satisfies the following conditions:

1.

Uniformly finite Poincar
\acute{\mbox{e}}
condition
R:=\sup_{n}R^{*}_{X_{i}}<\infty,\;\;\forall i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
;

2.

Increasing adding entropy condition

h(\dfrac{X_{1}+X_{2}\cdots+X_{2^{m}}}{2^{m/2}})\leq h(\dfrac{X_{2^{m}+1}+X_{2^{m}+2}\cdots+X_{2^{m+1}}}{2^{m/2}}),\;\;\forall m\in\mathbb{N};

3.

Mean and variance condition
\mathbb{E}(X_{i})=0,\text{Var}(X_{i})=\sigma^{2},\;\;\forall i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.

Then the entropy sequence
\{Ent_{n}:=h(S_{n}),n\geq 1\}
is convergent if and only if

\text{KL}(S_{n}\|\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}))\to 0,\;\;n\to\infty.

Proof:
Since
\text{KL}(S_{n}\|\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}))=\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi e\sigma^{2})-Ent_{n}
, the one part of proof is finished.

Next we suppose
\{Ent_{n},n\geq 1\}
is convergent, thus its subsequence
\{h_{n},n\geq 1\}
defined in Theorem
4.1
is also convergent. then consider entropy jump

h_{n+1}-\dfrac{h_{n}+\bar{h}_{n}}{2},

where

\bar{h}_{n}=h(\dfrac{X_{2^{n}+1}+X_{2^{n}+2}\cdots+X_{2^{n+1}}}{2^{n/2}}).

For one hand, by Theorem
3.4
,

h_{n+1}-\dfrac{h_{n}+\bar{h}_{n}}{2}\geq 0,

thus

\liminf_{n\to\infty}h_{n+1}-\dfrac{h_{n}+\bar{h}_{n}}{2}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{h_{n}-\bar{h}_{n}}{2}\geq 0.

(4.4)

One the other hand, by condition 2,

h_{n}-\bar{h}_{n}\leq 0,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.

(4.5)

Combining (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{n}-\bar{h}_{n}=0,

which implies

\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{n+1}-\dfrac{h_{n}+\bar{h}_{n}}{2}=0.

Use Theorem
3.4
again we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi e\sigma^{2})-h_{n}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi e\sigma^{2})-\dfrac{h_{n}+\bar{h}_{n}}{2}=0,

i.e. a subsequence of
\{Ent_{n},n\geq 1\}
converge to
\dfrac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi e\sigma^{2})

\{Ent_{n},n\geq 1\}
.

When

\mathbb{E}[\frac{g_{1}(X)+g_{2}(Y)}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}}]^{2}\geq\mathbb{E}[K+\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}}]^{2},

(5.1)

since the hypotenuse of a triangle is the longest.

Note that
K

\lambda[\frac{\rho_{X}(X)+\rho_{Y}(Y)}{\sqrt{2}}]-(1-\lambda)[\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}}],
thus

\lambda=\arg\min_{a\in[0,1]}\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}}}(\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}})-\left(a[\frac{\rho_{X}(X)+\rho_{Y}(Y)}{\sqrt{2}}]-(1-a)[\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}}]\right)\right]^{2}.

According to

\arg\min_{a}\mathbb{E}[U-aV]^{2}=\dfrac{\mathbb{E}(UV)}{\mathbb{E}(V^{2})},

and Lemma
5.4
,

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\mathbb{E}\left([\rho_{\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}}}(\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}})+\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}}][\frac{\rho_{X}(X)+\rho_{Y}(Y)}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}}]\right)}{\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{\rho_{X}(X)+\rho_{Y}(Y)}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{2}}

(5.2)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{J(\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}})-2+(\sigma_{X}^{2}+\sigma_{Y}^{2})/2}{1/2J(X)+1/2J(Y)-2+(\sigma_{X}^{2}+\sigma_{Y}^{2})/2}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\overset{\Delta}{=}\dfrac{A^{\prime}}{A}.

(5.4)

Put (
5.2
) in (
5.1
),

\mathbb{E}[\frac{g_{1}(X)+g_{2}(Y)}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}}]^{2}=\dfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[g_{1}(X)+X]^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}[g_{2}(Y)+Y]^{2}\geq\dfrac{A^{\prime 2}}{A}.

(5.5)

Next, define

h_{1}(x)=h_{2}(x)=-x.

By Lemma
5.1
and (

)

\mathbb{E}\left(\rho_{\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}}}(\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}})-\frac{g_{1}(X)+g_{2}(Y)}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^{2}\leq A^{\prime}-\dfrac{A^{\prime 2}}{A}.

So due to Lemma
5.2

\displaystyle A^{\prime}-\dfrac{A^{\prime 2}}{A}\geq\mathbb{E}[\rho_{\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}}}(\frac{X+Y}{\sqrt{2}})-\frac{g_{1}(X)+g_{2}(Y)}{\sqrt{2}}]^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1}{2\bar{J}}\left(\dfrac{\beta}{R_{X}^{*}}\mathbb{E}[g_{1}(X)-\mu X]^{2}+\dfrac{1-\beta}{R_{Y}^{*}}\mathbb{E}[g_{2}(Y)-\mu Y]^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\bar{J}}[\dfrac{\beta}{R_{X}^{*}}[\mathbb{E}(g_{1}(X)+X)^{2}-2(\mu+1)(\sigma_{X}^{2}-1)+\sigma_{X}^{2}(\mu+1)^{2}]

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;+\dfrac{1-\beta}{R_{Y}^{*}}[\mathbb{E}(g_{2}(Y)+Y)^{2}-2(\mu+1)(\sigma_{Y}^{2}-1)+\sigma_{Y}^{2}(\mu+1)^{2}]].

5.5

\displaystyle A^{\prime}-\dfrac{A^{\prime 2}}{A}

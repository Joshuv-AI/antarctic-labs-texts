Abstract
. A vortex pair solution of the incompressible 2 ​ d 2d Euler equation in vorticity form ω t + ∇ ⟂ Ψ ⋅ ∇ ω = 0 , Ψ = ( − Δ ) − 1 ω , in ℝ 2 × ( 0 , ∞ ) \omega_{t}+\nabla^{\perp}\Psi\cdot\nabla\omega=0,\quad\Psi=(-\Delta)^{-1}\omega,\quad\hbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,\infty) is a travelling wave solution of the form ω ⁡ ( x , t ) = W ⁡ ( x 1 − c ​ t , x 2 ) \omega(x,t)=W(x_{1}-ct,x_{2}) where W ⁡ ( x ) W(x) is compactly supported and odd in x 2 x_{2} . 1.
Introduction

This paper investigates the Euler equations for an incompressible, inviscid fluid in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In vorticity-stream formulation these equations take the form

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \omega_{t}+\nabla^{\perp}\Psi\cdot\nabla\omega&=0\ \ \ \ &&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,T),\\
\Psi=(-\Delta)^{-1}&\omega\ \ &&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}\times(0,T),\\
\omega(\cdot,0)=&\mathring{\omega}&&\text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.\end{aligned}\right.

The stream function
\Psi
is determined as the inverse Laplacian applied to
\omega
, yielding:

\Psi(x,t)=(-\Delta)^{-1}\omega(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\frac{1}{|x-y|}\omega(y,t)\,dy\ \

and the fluid velocity field
v=\nabla^{\perp}\Psi\coloneqq(\Psi_{x_{2}},-\Psi_{x_{1}})
follows the Biot-Savart Law:

v(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(y-x)^{\perp}}{|y-x|^{2}}\omega(y,t)\,dy\ .

The Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is well-posed in the weak sense in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
as deduced from the classical theories by Wolibner
[
16
]
and Yudovich
[
9
]
. Solutions are regular if the initial condition
\mathring{\omega}
is so. See for instance Chapter 8 in Majda-Bertozzi’s book
[
11
]
.

Of special interest are solutions with vorticity
concentrated
around a finite number of points
\xi_{1}(t),\ldots,\xi_{N}(t)
in the form

(1.2)

\omega_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{m_{j}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Big[W\left(\frac{x-\xi_{j}(t)}{\varepsilon}\right)+o(1)\Big],

where
W(y)
is a fixed radially symmetric profile with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}W(y)dy=1
,
o(1)\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
and
m_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
are the masses of the vortices. We have that

(1.3)

\omega_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\rightharpoonup\omega_{S}(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}{m_{j}}\delta_{0}({x-\xi_{j}(t)})\quad\hbox{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

where
\delta_{0}(y)
designates a Dirac mass at
0
.
Formally the trajectories
\xi_{j}(t)
satisfy the Kirchhoff-Routh vortex system

(1.4)

\dot{\xi}_{j}(t)=-\sum_{\ell\not=j}\frac{m_{\ell}}{2\pi}\frac{(\xi_{j}(t)-\xi_{\ell}(t))^{\perp}}{|\xi_{j}(t)-\xi_{\ell}(t)|^{2}},\quad j=1,\ldots,N.

The study of point vortex dynamics evolving due to (
1.4
) is itself a rich topic with many deep works, see
[
2
]
and references within.

Finding regular solutions of (
1.1
) of the form (
1.2
) that approximate a superposition of point vortices satisfying (
1.4
) in the sense of (
1.3
) is a version of the classical
vortex desingularization problem
, which has been the subject of many works, see for example
[
12
,
8
,
5
,
14
]
and further references inside.

A key tool in finding solutions to the desingularization problem is to build them from the class of
steady solutions
to (
1.1
), namely solutions whose shape does not depend on time. Examples include radially symmetric decaying functions
W(y)
, which correspond to stationary solutions. Other examples are traveling and rotating waves. See for instance
[
14
,
5
]
.

One can find such solutions by reducing to semilinear elliptic problems, and a common method of doing so is the stream function method, which starts from the observation that if a
\Psi(x)
satisfies the equation

(1.5)

\displaystyle\Delta\Psi+f\left(\Psi\right)=0,

for
f
a
C^{1}
function, then the stream-function/vorticity pair
\left(\Psi,f\left(\Psi\right)\right)
is a stationary solution to (
1.1
). From here, the family of rescaled solutions
\left(\Psi(x/\varepsilon),\varepsilon^{-2}f\left(\Psi(x/\varepsilon)\right)\right)
is an example of a desingularized vortex solution for a single stationary point vortex.

We are most in interested travelling wave solutions which can be constructed in a similar way; if
\omega(x,t)=\omega_{0}(x-cte_{1})
for some constant
c
, then the transport equation in (
1.1
) is reduced to

\displaystyle\nabla^{\perp}\left(\Psi-cx_{2}\right)\cdot\grad\omega_{0}=0

in moving coordinate frame
x^{\prime}=x-cte_{1}
. Thus if we can find a
C^{1}
functions
f
and a solution
\Psi
to

\displaystyle\Delta\Psi+f\left(\Psi-cx_{2}\right)=0,

we have a travelling wave solution to (
1.1
).

The aim of this paper is to construct and give certain fine information for a desingularization of the simplest travelling wave solution to the Kirkhhoff-Routh system (
1.4
), a vortex pair. Let
q>0
,
M>0
. Then a solution to (
1.4
) for
N=2
is given by

(1.6)

\displaystyle\xi_{1}(t)=qe_{2}+cte_{1},\quad\xi_{2}(t)=-qe_{2}+cte_{1},\quad c=\frac{M}{4\pi q},

which clearly describes two point vortices a distance
2q
apart in the
e_{2}
direction with opposite masses travelling uniformly in the
e_{1}
direction at speed
c
.

Desingularizing vortex pairs has been a topic of great interest, yielding numerous results in various contexts. Our paper focuses on constructing a desingularized vortex pair solution to (
1.1
). This solution has previously been established by Smets and Van Schaftingen, in
[
14
]
. In their work, they treated the problem using variational techniques, obtaining least energy solutions by minimizing the associated energy functional.
Cao, Liu, and Wei, in
[
5
]
, addressed the case of bounded domains through a reduction method. Further results in this direction can be found in
[
4
,
13
]
, and earlier constructions are explored in
[
3
,
15
]
by Burton and Turkington.

Vortex pairs have also been constructed and studied for related systems, see
[
1
]
for a vortex pair solution to the surface quasi-geostrophic equation, and
[
10
,
6
]
for travelling wave solutions to the Gross-Pitaevskii equation.

Contrasting with
[
14
]
, in this paper we construct the solution instead using Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction, give precise estimates in terms of
\varepsilon
on lower order terms, and show differentiability of the solution with respect to the parameter

.

We now describe the desingularized vortex pair

\displaystyle-\Delta\Psi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-2}f_{\varepsilon}(\Psi_{\varepsilon}(x)-c_{q,\varepsilon}x_{2}).

in the upper half plane

, and is even in
x_{1}
and odd in
x_{2}
. Our stream function

\displaystyle\Psi_{\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle\Delta\Gamma+\Gamma^{\gamma}_{+}=0\ \text{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},\quad\{\Gamma>0\}=B_{1},

Suppressing the dependency of

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section

be the Banach space defined as follows

(2.13)

\displaystyle X\coloneqq\left\{f\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)|f\ \text{satisfies}\ \eqref{solution-symmetry-assumptions}\ \text{and}\ \eqref{solution-Z2-orthogonality-condition}\right\},

equipped with the standard
L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
norm. Then the problem we are trying to solve can be precisely stated as

\displaystyle L[\psi_{q^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=E-N[\psi_{q^{\prime}}]\ \text{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},

\displaystyle\psi_{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\in X.

2.2.
Projected Linear Problem

It will be useful to construct a slightly more general linear theory than is required given the discussed in the previous section. Thus we consider the function space consisting of
\psi\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
that satisfy

(2.14)

\displaystyle\psi(y_{1},-y_{2})=-\psi(y_{1},y_{2}),

satisfying the orthogonality conditions

(2.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}VZ_{i}\psi=0,\ i=1,2.

To that end, define
Y
to be the function space

(2.16)

\displaystyle Y\coloneqq\left\{f\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)|f\ \text{satisfies}\ \eqref{generalised-symmetry-assumptions}\ \text{and}\ \eqref{generalised-orthogonality-conditions}\right\}

equipped with the standard
L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)
norm. Note that
X\subset Y
, with
X
defined in (
2.13
).

With these definitions in hand, we first consider the projected linear problem

\displaystyle L[\psi]

\displaystyle=h+aVZ_{1}+bVZ_{2}\ \text{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},

(2.17)

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle\in Y.

Here
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, and
h
is a function satisfying

\displaystyle\left\|h\right\|_{**}\coloneqq\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}W(y)^{-\left(2+\sigma\right)}\left|h(y)\right|<\infty,

with
\sigma\in(0,1)
, and

(2.18)

\displaystyle W(y)=\frac{1}{1+\left|y-q^{\prime}e_{2}\right|}+\frac{1}{1+\left|y+q^{\prime}e_{2}\right|}.

We first have a priori estimates on solutions to (
2.17
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(q,c,\varepsilon)
be chosen so that (
1.13
)–(
1.14
) hold. Suppose we have a solution to (
2.17
), with
h
satisfying
\left\|h\right\|_{**}<\infty
and the symmetry assumption (
2.14
). Then

\displaystyle\left(\left|a\right|+\left|b\right|\right)\leq C\left(\varepsilon\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}+\left\|h\right\|_{**}\right),

some constant
C>0
.

Proof.

See
[
1
]
.
∎

Next is an a priori estimate for the solution of (
2.17
) in terms of
\left\|h\right\|_{**}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(q,c,\varepsilon)
be chosen so that (
1.13
)–(
1.14
) hold.. Suppose we have a solution to (
2.17
), with
h
satisfying
\left\|h\right\|_{**}<\infty
and the symmetry assumption (
2.14
). Then

\displaystyle\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq C\left\|h\right\|_{**},

some constant
C>0
.

Proof.

This argument is again very similar to the corresponding a priori estimate in
[
1
]
. We proceed by contradiction and suppose there is a sequence of
\varepsilon_{n}
such that
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Suppose further that for this sequence, we have a sequence of solutions
\psi_{n}
to (
2.17
) such that

(2.19)

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=1\ \text{for all}\ n,

(2.20)

\displaystyle\left\|h_{n}\right\|_{**}

\displaystyle\to 0\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty.

Let
q_{n}=\varepsilon_{n}q^{\prime}_{n}
for some sequence
q_{n}\in(Q_{0}^{-1},Q_{0})
for all
n
. Then as in
[
1
]
, (
2.19
)–(
2.20
) implies

(2.21)

\displaystyle\left(\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{R}(q^{\prime}_{n}e_{2})\right)}+\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{R}(-q^{\prime}_{n}e_{2})\right)}\right)\to 0\ \text{as}\ n\to\infty,

for any fixed radius
R>0
, due to the orthogonality conditions (
2.15
) and the non-degeneracy argument for the linearized operator around the power nonlinearity vortex (
1.9
) given in
[
7
]
. In particular it is true for
R=2\rho
, where the support of
V_{n}
in contained in
B_{\rho}\left(q^{\prime}_{n}e_{2}\right)
for all
n
.

Next, we obtain bounds for
\psi_{n}(y)
on the region
|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}|\geq 3q_{n}^{\prime}
. Using the Green’s function to write,

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\log{\left(\frac{\left|z-\bar{y}\right|}{\left|z-y\right|}\right)}\left(h_{n}(z)-V_{n}\psi_{n}+a_{n}V_{n}Z_{1}^{(n)}+b_{n}V_{n}Z_{2}^{(n)}\right)\ dz,

we have

(2.22)

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\{|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}|\geq 3q_{n}^{\prime}\}\right)}\leq C\left(\left\|h_{n}\right\|_{**}+\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\rho}(q^{\prime}_{n}e_{2})\right)}+\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\right).

Now let
G_{n}
be the annulus defined by
|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}|\in[2\rho,3q_{n}^{\prime}]
. We have shown that

(2.23)

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\partial G_{n}\right)}\leq CK_{n},

where

(2.24)

\displaystyle K_{n}=\max\left\{\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\partial B_{2\rho}(q^{\prime}_{n}e_{2})\right)},\left\|h_{n}\right\|_{**}+\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\rho}(q^{\prime}_{n}e_{2})\right)}+\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\right\}.

On the annulus
G_{n}
,

(2.25)

\displaystyle-\Delta\psi_{n}=h_{n}.

Define
\beta_{n}
by

\displaystyle\beta_{n}(y)=c_{1}K_{n}\left(1-\left|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|^{-\sigma}\right),

for
c_{1}
chosen appropriately so that

\displaystyle\beta_{n}(y)\geq\psi_{n}(y)

on
\partial G_{n}
. Such a choice is possible on this region as

\displaystyle 1-\left(2\rho\right)^{-\sigma}\leq 1-\left|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|^{-\sigma}\leq 1-\left(3q_{n}^{\prime}\right)^{-\sigma}.

Note that
-\Delta\beta_{n}=c_{1}\sigma^{2}\left|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|^{-(2+\sigma)}K_{n}
. We know from (
2.24
) that

\displaystyle K_{n}\geq\left\|h_{n}\right\|_{**}+\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\rho}(q^{\prime}_{n}e_{2})\right)}+\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\geq\left\|h_{n}\right\|_{**}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\left|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|^{-(2+\sigma)}K_{n}\geq\left|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|^{-(2+\sigma)}\left\|h_{n}\right\|_{**}

\displaystyle=\left|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|^{-(2+\sigma)}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}W(z)^{-\left(2+\sigma\right)}\left|h_{n}(z)\right|,

where we can take the supremum over
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
due to the symmetry assumption (
2.14
), and the definition of
W
in (
2.18
). Now, since
W(z)^{-\left(2+\sigma\right)}\to\infty
as
\left|z\right|\to\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\left|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|^{-(2+\sigma)}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}W(z)^{-\left(2+\sigma\right)}\left|h(z)\right|

(2.26)

\displaystyle\geq C_{0}\left|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|^{-(2+\sigma)}\left(\frac{\left(1+\left|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|\right)\left(1+\left|y+q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|\right)}{2+\left|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|+\left|y+q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|}\right)^{2+\sigma}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\left|h(z)\right|,

some constant
C_{0}
. Now as
y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
,
\left|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|\leq\left|y+q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|
, and thus (
2.26
) becomes

\displaystyle\left|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|^{-(2+\sigma)}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}W(z)^{-\left(2+\sigma\right)}\left|h(z)\right|

\displaystyle\geq C_{0}\left(1+\frac{1}{\left|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|}\right)^{2+\sigma}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2+\sigma}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\left|h(z)\right|

\displaystyle\geq C_{1}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\left|h(z)\right|,

as
\left|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|\leq 3q_{n}^{\prime}
. Thus we can say

\displaystyle-\Delta\beta_{n}\geq C_{2}\left\|h_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\right)}\geq-\Delta\psi_{n},

for some constant
C_{2}
large enough, obtained by enlarging
c_{1}
if necessary. We can also use (
2.23
) and (
2.25
) in the other direction to obtain

\displaystyle\beta_{n}(y)

\displaystyle\geq-\psi_{n}(y),\quad y\in\partial G_{n}

\displaystyle-\Delta\beta_{n}

\displaystyle\geq\Delta\psi_{n},\quad y\in G_{n}.

Thus by comparison with
\beta_{n}
on
G_{n}
, we have

(2.27)

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(G_{n}\right)}\leq CK_{n}.

Combining (
2.27
) with (
2.22
) gives us

(2.28)

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\{\left|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|\geq 2\rho\}\right)}\leq CK_{n}.

Combining (
2.21
) and (
2.28
) gives, as
n\to\infty
, that

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{2\rho}(q^{\prime}_{n}e_{2})\right)},\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\{\left|y-q_{n}^{\prime}e_{2}\right|\geq 2\rho\}\right)}\right\}

(2.29)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{2\rho}(q^{\prime}_{n}e_{2})\right)},C\left(\left\|h_{n}\right\|_{**}+\left\|\psi_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\rho}(q^{\prime}_{n}e_{2})\right)}+\left|a_{n}\right|+\left|b_{n}\right|\right)\right\}\to 0,

by the assumptions on
h_{n}
, Lemma
2.1
, and (
2.21
). We have that (
2.29
) gives us a contradiction on the
\psi_{n}
, as desired.
∎

We combine Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
to obtain the main a priori estimate:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(q,c,\varepsilon)
be chosen so that (
1.13
)–(
1.14
) hold.. Suppose we have a solution to (
2.17
), with
h
satisfying
\left\|h\right\|_{**}<\infty
and the symmetry assumption (
2.14
). Then

\displaystyle\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}+\left(\left|a\right|+\left|b\right|\right)\leq C\left\|h\right\|_{**},

some constant
C>0
.

We finish Section
2.2
by showing existence of solutions to the projected linear problem.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(q,c,\varepsilon)
be chosen so that (
1.13
)–(
1.14
) hold.. Then there is a linear operator
T_{q}
from the space of
h
satisfying (
2.14
) and
\left\|h\right\|_{**}<\infty
to
Y
such that

\psi\coloneqq T_{q}(h)
is the unique solution to (
2.17
) associated to that
h
. Moreover, we have the bound

(2.30)

\displaystyle\left\|T_{q}(h)\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}+\left(\left|a\right|+\left|b\right|\right)\leq C\left\|h\right\|_{**}.

for some
C
independent of
\varepsilon
.

Proof.

We can reformulate the problem (
2.17
) as

\displaystyle\psi=K\psi+\tilde{h},

for an
\tilde{h}
depending linearly on
h
, and
K
is a linear operator acting on
Y
defined by

\displaystyle(K\psi)(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\log{\left(\frac{\left|z-\bar{y}\right|}{\left|z-y\right|}\right)}V(z)\psi(z)\ dz,

and extending by symmetry to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Using methods analogous to those in the proof of Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|K\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left|\log{\varepsilon}\right|\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\rho}\left(q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla K\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\rho}\left(q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)\right)}.

These bounds show compactness of
K
for fixed
\varepsilon
, and by the Fredholm alternative, solutions to (
2.17
) are unique for a particular
h
as long as
\psi=K\psi
only has the zero solution in
Y
, guaranteed by the a priori estimate stated in Lemma
2.3
. This lemma also gives the bound (
2.30
).
∎

2.3.
Projected Nonlinear Problem

We now use the linear theory developed in Section
2.2
to solve a projection of the full nonlinear problem. That is, we seek to construct a solution to the problem

\displaystyle L[\psi]

\displaystyle=E-N[\psi]+aVZ_{1}+bVZ_{2}\ \text{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},

(2.31)

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle\in Y,

where we recall the definitions of
Y
from (
2.16
), and of
E
and
N[\psi]
from (
2.8
)–(
2.9
).

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
(q,c,\varepsilon)
be chosen so that (
1.13
)–(
1.14
) hold.. Then for
\varepsilon>0
small enough, there is a unique solution
\psi_{q^{\prime}}
to (
2.31
) in the ball

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\left\{\psi\in Y:\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq\varepsilon\left|\log{\varepsilon}\right|\right\}.

This solution has the bound

(2.32)

\displaystyle\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq C\varepsilon,

where
C>0
is a constant, and moreover the solution is continuous with respect to
q
.

Proof.

This is a fixed point argument. Indeed, we can define a map
\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:Y

\displaystyle\rightarrow Y,

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle\mapsto T_{q}\left(-E+N[\psi]\right).

Then solving (
2.31
) is equivalent to solving

\displaystyle\psi=\mathcal{A}(\psi).

We run the fixed point procedure in the closed ball
\mathcal{B}\subset Y
defined in Theorem
2.5
.

From (
1.15
), (
2.4
), (
2.5
), and (
2.8
), we can see that

\displaystyle\left\|E\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq C_{1}\varepsilon.

Moreover, for small enough
\varepsilon>0
, by (
2.6
), (
2.7
), and (
2.9
), we have that
N[\psi]
is supported in
B_{\rho}(q^{\prime}e_{2})\cup B_{\rho}(-q^{\prime}e_{2})
for
\psi\in\mathcal{B}
, some
\rho>0
independent of
\varepsilon
. This means the support of
N[\psi]
can be contained inside a compact set uniformly for elements in this closed ball. Given the definition of
N[\psi]
in (
2.9
), we have the estimate

\displaystyle\left\|N[\psi]\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq C_{2}\left\|\psi\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}^{2}.

Then we can show that if
\varepsilon
is small enough,
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
is a contraction, and admits a unique fixed point. Moreover, we can see that for this fixed point
\psi_{q^{\prime}}

(2.34)

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{q^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq C\left\|E\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq C\varepsilon,

giving the bound (
2.32
). Finally, the fixed point characterisation of the solution
\psi_{q^{\prime}}
gives continuity with respect to
q
.
∎

2.4.
Full Solution

We wish to solve

\displaystyle L[\psi_{q^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=E-N[\psi_{q^{\prime}}]\ \text{on}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},

(2.35)

\displaystyle\psi_{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\in X.

Note that solving (
2.31
) with the condition that
\psi\in X\subset Y
immediately forces
a=0
. So we are reduced to the problem of finding a relationship between
c
and
q
so that

\displaystyle L[\psi_{q^{\prime}}]

\displaystyle=E-N[\psi_{q^{\prime}}]+bVZ_{2},

(2.36)

\displaystyle\psi_{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\in X,

has
b=0
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
(q,c,\varepsilon)
be chosen so that (
1.13
)–(
1.14
) hold.. Then for
\varepsilon>0
small enough, there is a unique
c=c(q)
such that the corresponding solution of (
2.36
) has
b=0
. This
c
is of the form

(2.37)

\displaystyle c=\frac{m}{2q}+g(q^{\prime}),

where
g(q^{\prime})
is continuous in
q^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We know that our solution to (
2.36
)
\psi_{q^{\prime}}
solves

\displaystyle L\left[\psi_{q^{\prime}}\right]=E-N\left[\psi_{q^{\prime}}\right]+bVZ_{2}.

Integrating against
Z_{2}
gives

(2.38)

\displaystyle b\underbrace{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}VZ_{2}^{2}\right)}_{\geq c_{0}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}Z_{2}\left(L\left[\psi_{q^{\prime}}\right]-E+N\left[\psi_{q^{\prime}}\right]\right),

where
c_{0}
is some absolute positive constant. Thus,
b=0
is equivalent to the right hand of (
2.38
) being
0
. We know from the proof of Lemma
2.1
, and the bound on
\psi_{q^{\prime}}
, (
2.34
), that

(2.39)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}Z_{2}N\left[\psi_{q^{\prime}}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\psi_{q^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}^{2}\leq C\varepsilon^{2},

(2.40)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}Z_{2}L\left[\psi_{q^{\prime}}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\left\|\psi_{q^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

We know from the definition of the error
E
given in (
2.4
)–(
2.5
), and the definition of
Z_{2}
given in (
2.11
), we have,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}EZ_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{\rho}\left(q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)}E_{1}Z_{2,1}+\int_{B_{\rho}\left(q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)}E_{1}Z_{2,2}-\int_{B_{\rho}\left(-q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)}E_{2}Z_{2,1}-\int_{B_{\rho}\left(-q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)}E_{2}Z_{2,2}

\displaystyle=2\left(\int_{B_{\rho}\left(q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)}E_{1}Z_{2,1}+\int_{B_{\rho}\left(q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)}E_{1}Z_{2,2}\right),

with

(2.41)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{B_{\rho}\left(q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)}E_{1}Z_{2,2}\right|\leq C\varepsilon^{2},

and,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}\left(q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)}E_{1}Z_{2,1}=-\int_{B_{\rho}\left(q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)}\partial_{2}\left(\left(\Gamma(y-q^{\prime}e_{2})\right)^{\gamma}_{+}\right)\left(\Gamma\left(2q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)-\Gamma\left(y+q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)+c\varepsilon\left(q^{\prime}-y_{2}\right)\right)dy

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}{\left(\left|\Gamma\left(2q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)-\Gamma\left(y+q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)+c\varepsilon\left(q^{\prime}-y_{2}\right)\right|^{2}\chi_{B_{\rho}\left(q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)}\right)}

(2.42)

\displaystyle=-\int_{B_{\rho}\left(q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)}\partial_{2}\left(\left(\Gamma(y-q^{\prime}e_{2})\right)^{\gamma}_{+}\right)\left(\Gamma\left(2q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)-\Gamma\left(y+q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)+c\varepsilon\left(q^{\prime}-y_{2}\right)\right)dy+\mathcal{O}{\left(\varepsilon^{2}\right)}.

Integrating by parts we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}\left(q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)}E_{1}Z_{2,1}=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}\left(q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)}\left(\Gamma(y-q^{\prime}e_{2})\right)^{\gamma}_{+}\left(-\partial_{2}\Gamma\left(y+q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)-c\varepsilon\right)dy+\mathcal{O}{\left(\varepsilon^{2}\right)}.

Now we concentrate on calculating
-\partial_{2}\Gamma\left(y+q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)-c\varepsilon
:

(2.43)

\displaystyle-\partial_{2}\Gamma\left(y+q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)-c\varepsilon=\frac{m\left(y_{2}+q^{\prime}\right)}{\left|y+q^{\prime}e_{2}\right|^{2}}-c\varepsilon=\varepsilon\left(\frac{m}{2q}-c\right)-\frac{m\varepsilon^{2}}{4q^{2}}\left(y_{2}-q^{\prime}\right)+\mathcal{O}{\left(\varepsilon^{3}\right)},

where we have used the fact that we are on
B_{\rho}\left(q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)
. We recall that

(2.44)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}\left(q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)}\left(\Gamma(y-q^{\prime}e_{2})\right)^{\gamma}_{+}=\int_{B_{1}}\nu^{\gamma}_{+}=M,

and using (
2.39
)–(
2.44
), we have

(2.45)

\displaystyle b\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}VZ_{2}^{2}\right)=2M\varepsilon\left(c-\frac{m}{2q}+\mathcal{O}{\left(\varepsilon\right)}\right),

where
M
is an absolute constant defined in (
1.10
), and the remainder in the parentheses on the right hand side of order
\mathcal{O}{\left(\varepsilon\right)}
is continuous in
q^{\prime}
. Thus, there is a solution to
b=0
of the form

(2.46)

\displaystyle c=\frac{m}{2q}+g(q^{\prime}),

as required.
∎

We note that for
g
defined in (
2.37
), (
2.45
) and (
2.46
) imply that
g
has the bound

(2.47)

\displaystyle\left|g(q^{\prime})\right|\leq C\varepsilon,

for some absolute constant
C>0
. We can then use Theorem
2.6
to improve the bounds on
E
, and therefore
\psi_{q^{\prime}}
, and
g
itself.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\left(q,c(q),\varepsilon\right)
be as in Theorem
2.6
. Then the corresponding solution to (
2.35
),
\psi_{q^{\prime}}
has the bound

(2.48)

\displaystyle\left\|\psi_{q^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2},

(2.49)

\displaystyle\left|g(q^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

Proof.

As in (

) gives us

\displaystyle\partial_{q^{\prime}}c=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{m}{2\left(q^{\prime}\right)^{2}}+\partial_{q^{\prime}}g(q^{\prime}),

we have

(3.44)

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{q^{\prime}}c\right|\leq C\varepsilon,\ \ \left|\partial_{q^{\prime}}g\right|\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

Step IV: Repeating Steps II and III
.

\displaystyle\gamma\left(\Gamma\left(y-q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)-\Gamma\left(y+q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)-c\varepsilon y_{2}-\left|\log{\varepsilon}\right|\Omega\right)^{\gamma-1}_{+}\chi_{B_{\frac{s}{\varepsilon}}\left(q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{q^{\prime}}\left(E_{1}Z_{2,1}\right)=-

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\Gamma(y-q^{\prime}e_{2})\right)^{\gamma}_{+}\left(2\partial_{2}^{2}\Gamma\left(y+q^{\prime}e_{2}\right)+\partial_{q^{\prime}}\left(c\right)\varepsilon\right)\ dy+\mathcal{O}{\left(\varepsilon^{5}\right)}.

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{q^{\prime}}g\right|\leq C\varepsilon^{3},

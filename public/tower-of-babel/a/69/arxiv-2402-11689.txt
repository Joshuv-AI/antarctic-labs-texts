Regularized double shuffle relations of μ \mu -multiple Hurwitz zeta values
Jia Li, Jianqiang zhao

Abstract
. In this paper we consider a family of multiple Hurwitz zeta values with bi-indices parameterized by μ \mu with {Re} ⁡ ( μ ) > 0 \Ree(\mu)>0 . These values are equipped with both the μ \mu -stuffle product from their series definition and the shuffle product from their integral expressions. We will give a detailed analysis of the two different products and discuss their regularization when the bi-indices are non-admissible. Our main goal is to prove the comparison theorem relating the two ways of regularization, which is an analog of Ihara, Kaneko, and Zagier’s celebrated result concerning the regularization of multiple zeta values. We will see that the comparison formula is μ \mu -invariant, that is, it is independent of the parameter μ \mu . As applications, we will first provide a few interesting closed formulas for some multi-fold infinite sums that are closely related to the multiple zeta values of level N N studied by Yuan and the second author. We will also derive two sum formulas for μ \mu -double Hurwitz zeta values which generalize the sum formulas for double zeta values and double zeta star values, respectively, and a weighted sum formula which generalizes the weighted sum formulas for both double zeta values and double T T -values simultaneously. At the end of the paper, we will propose a problem extending that for multiple zeta values first conjectured by Ihara et al.



Keywords
: multiple zeta values; Hurwitz multiple zeta values; stuffle algebra; shuffle algebra; double shuffle relations; regularization.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Multiple zeta values and some variants

Let
\mathbb{Z}
denote the integer set,
\mathbb{N}
the positive integer set,
\mathbb{Q}
the rational number set, and
\mathbb{R}
the real
number set. For any
r\in\mathbb{N}
and any index
{\boldsymbol{k}}=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, the multiple zeta values (MZVs)
and the multiple zeta star values (MZSVs) are defined by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\zeta({\boldsymbol{k}}):=\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}},\quad\zeta^{\star}({\boldsymbol{k}}):=\sum_{1\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}},

respectively. These series converge if and only if
k_{r}>1
when we say
{\boldsymbol{k}}
is admissible.
It is easy to convert these values from one type to the other and both of them have
played important and sometimes unexpected roles in the studies of many subjects in mathematics
and physics alike, such as the knot theory, motives, modular forms, and the Feynman integrals,
to name just a few. See, for example, the two books
[
3
,
17
]
and the references therein.

Recently, by restricting the summation indices
n_{j}
’s in (
1.1
) to even or odd numbers,
Hoffman
[
8
]
, Kaneko and Tsumura
[
10
]
, and Xu and the second author
[
14
]

have defined a few different variations of MZVs. For example, Hoffman’s multiple
t
-values and
multiple
t
-star values (MtSVs) are defined as follows:

\displaystyle t({\boldsymbol{k}}):=\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r},\,2\nmid n_{i}}\frac{2^{r}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}},\quad t^{\star}({\boldsymbol{k}}):=\sum_{1\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r},\,2\nmid n_{i}}\frac{2^{r}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}},

where
{\boldsymbol{k}}=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
k_{r}>1
. Note that there exist different conventions to define such variations
(e.g., see
[
10
,
18
]
) where the 2-power might be omitted. In these previous works, many
interesting results and insightful relations have been discovered and some conjectures have been proposed.
It is not hard to see that all these values can be expressed as
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combinations of Euler sums
defined as follows. For
s_{1},\dots,s_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{r}=\pm 1
, we define the
Euler sums

(1.2)

\zeta\binom{k_{1},\cdots,k_{r}}{\varepsilon_{1},\cdots,\varepsilon_{r}}:=\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}}\;\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{\varepsilon_{j}^{n_{j}}}{n_{j}^{s_{j}}}.

To save space, if
\varepsilon_{j}=-1
then
\overline{s}_{j}
will be used and if a substring
S
repeats
n

times in the list then
\{S\}_{n}
will be used. For example,
\zeta(\{3\}_{n})=8^{n}\zeta(\{1,\bar{2}\}_{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

(see
[
16
]
).

1.2.
\mu
-multiple Hurwitz zeta values

In this article, we will consider a further generalization of MZVs as follows.
Fix a parameter
\mu
with
\Ree(\mu)>0
. For any admissible
{\boldsymbol{k}}=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
{\boldsymbol{m}}=(m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})\in(\mathbb{Q}_{>0})^{r}
,
we define the
\mu
-multiple Hurwitz zeta value
(
\mu
-MHZV) by

\zeta^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}}):=\sum_{0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}}\frac{\mu^{r}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}}.

It is easy to see that for all admissible
{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}

\displaystyle\zeta({\boldsymbol{k}})=

\displaystyle\,\zeta^{\mu=1}({\boldsymbol{k}};1,2,\cdots,r),\quad

\displaystyle\zeta^{\star}({\boldsymbol{k}})=

\displaystyle\,\zeta^{\mu=1}({\boldsymbol{k}};1,1,\cdots,1),

\displaystyle t({\boldsymbol{k}})=

\displaystyle\,\zeta^{\mu=2}({\boldsymbol{k}};1,3,\cdots,2r-1),\quad

\displaystyle t^{\star}({\boldsymbol{k}})=

\displaystyle\,\zeta^{\mu=2}({\boldsymbol{k}};1,1,\cdots,1).

Then we have a canonical embedding from MZSVs (resp. MtSVs) to 1-MHZVs (resp. 2-MHZVs) which agrees with the algebraic
structure, respectively. We use the name
\mu
-MHZVs because they can be regarded as special cases of the more general
multiple Hurwitz zeta values defined by

\zeta^{H}(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};a_{1},\cdots,a_{r}):=\sum_{0<n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots<n_{r}}\frac{1}{(n_{1}+a_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}+a_{r})^{k_{r}}},

where
k_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Ree(a_{j})>0
for all
j
. Many analytic properties and some arithmetic
properties of multiple Hurwitz zeta values have been investigated, see

[
12
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
.
However, very little has been done on their algebraic structures generalizing that for the MZVs.
Our primary goal of
this paper is to initiate such a study, at least when they have the special form of
\mu
-MHZVs.

Definition 1.1
.

A
bi-index

({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}\times\mathbb{Q}^{r}

is called
positive
if
m_{1},\cdots,m_{r}>0
, and is called
admissible
if it is positive and, in addition,
satisfies
k_{r}>1
. We sometimes also say
{\boldsymbol{k}}
is
admissible
if
k_{r}>1
and
{\boldsymbol{m}}
is positive.
The number
r
is called the
length
of
{\boldsymbol{k}}
, denoted by
\ell({\boldsymbol{k}})=r.

Furthermore, the sum
|{\boldsymbol{k}}|:=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}
is called the
weight
of
{\boldsymbol{k}}
.
By convention, the empty bi-index (when
r=0
) will also be considered admissible and we set
\zeta^{\mu}(\emptyset):=1
.

By the above notation, the
\mu
-MHZV
\zeta^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})
converges
if
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})
is admissible. Furthermore, let

\displaystyle({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})

\displaystyle\,=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r-1},1;m_{1},\cdots,m_{r-1},m_{r}),

\displaystyle({\boldsymbol{k}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})

\displaystyle\,=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r-1},1;m_{1},\cdots,m_{r-1},{\widetilde{m}}_{r})

be a pair of non-admissible positive bi-indices with
m_{r}\neq{\widetilde{m}}_{r}
, in which case
we call
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})
a
special triple
. Then the value

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})

\displaystyle:=({\widetilde{m}}_{r}-m_{r})\sum_{0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}}\frac{\mu^{r}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r-1}\mu+m_{r-1})^{k_{r-1}}(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})(n_{r}\mu+{\widetilde{m}}_{r})}

is called a
quasi-
\mu
-multiple Hurwitz zeta value
(quasi-
\mu
-MHZV),
which clearly always converges. Let
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu}
denote the
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]
-span of all (quasi-)
\mu
-MHZVs, that is,

{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu}:=\left\langle\left.\begin{aligned} &1,\zeta^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}}),\\
&{\mathcal{D}}^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}}_{1};{\boldsymbol{m}}_{1};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}_{1})\end{aligned}\right|\begin{aligned} &({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}}):\text{ admissible},\\
&({\boldsymbol{k}}_{1};{\boldsymbol{m}}_{1};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}_{1}):\text{ special triple}\end{aligned}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}[\mu]}.

One of our main goals is to explore a couple of useful algebraic structures on
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu}
.

1.3.
\mu
-stuffle and shuffle products and regularization

It is well-known that MZVs are equipped with a stuffle product due to their series representations. It is
natural to expect that this product structure can be extended to the
\mu
-stuffle product on
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu}
–
the algebra of
\mu
-MHZVs – using the series definition of
\mu
-MHZVs, see Theorem
2.3
for the details.

Further, for every classical MZV, we have an integral representation (first discovered by Kontsevich) as follows:

\zeta({\boldsymbol{k}})=\zeta(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r})=\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}}\omega_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{1}-1}\frac{dt}{1-t}\cdots\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r}-1}\frac{dt}{1-t}.

See
[
3
, Ch. 1]
for the simplex notation and
[
17
, Ch. 2]
for the iterated integral notation
(see §
2.2
for more details).
Using this representation, we can obtain another product on
{\mathcal{Z}}
– the algebra of MZVs – called the shuffle product. For the
\mu
-MHZVs, we have similar representations given below.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Integral representation)
Let
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})
be an admissible bi-index. Then we have the following integral representation
for
\mu
-MHZVs:

\zeta^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}}\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}.

For quasi-
\mu
-MHZVs, we have for any special triple
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})

{\mathcal{D}}^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})=\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}}\left(\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}-\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})}\right).

We know that the multiplication rules on the MZV space
obtained by the stuffle product and the shuffle product are generally different, which yield the
famous double shuffle relations which in turn provide one of the main tools to generate linear dependencies among MZVs.
In fact, to conjecturally generate all possible
\mathbb{Q}
-linear relations, it is necessary to
regularize these values in two different ways according to the product structures to use,
as done by Ihara, Kaneko and Zagier. Moreover, they proved a comparison theorem relating
these two ways of regularization
[
9
, Theorem 2]
. We now extend this to the
\mu
-MHZVs.

Theorem 1.3
.

(The
\mu
-stuffle regularization homomorphism)

Let
Y=\{y_{k,m}|k\in\mathbb{N},m\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\}
be an infinite alphabet and
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle
the non-commutative polynomial
algebra on words over
Y
. For any given
m\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, there exists a unique
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]
-linear map

\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}:(\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle,{\overset{\mu}{*}})\longrightarrow({\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu},\cdot)[T]

satisfying

\begin{cases}\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}(w)=\zeta_{*}^{\mu}(w)\qquad&\forall w\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle^{0};\\
\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}(y_{1,m})=T;\\
\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}(v\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{*}}w)=\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}(v)\cdot\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}(w)&\forall v,w\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle,\end{cases}

where
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle^{0}
is the sub-algebra of
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle
generated by (quasi-)admissible words
(see Prop.
3.10
).

Similarly, we also have

Theorem 1.4
.

(The shuffle regularization homomorphism)

Let
X^{\mu}=\{x,y^{\mu}_{m}|m\in\mathbb{Q}\}
be an infinite alphabet and
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle X^{\mu}\rangle
the non-commutative
polynomial
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]
-algebra on words over
X^{\mu}
.
For any
m\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, there exists a unique
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]
-linear map

\zeta_{\shuffle,m}^{\mu,T}:(\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle X^{\mu}\rangle^{1},\shuffle)\longrightarrow({\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu},\cdot)[T]

satisfying

\begin{cases}\zeta_{\shuffle,m}^{\mu,T}(w)=\zeta_{\shuffle}^{\mu}(w)\qquad&\forall w\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle X^{\mu}\rangle^{0};\\
\zeta_{\shuffle,m}^{\mu,T}(y^{\mu}_{m})=T;\\
\zeta_{\shuffle,m}^{\mu,T}(v\shuffle w)=\zeta_{\shuffle,m}^{\mu,T}(v)\cdot\zeta_{\shuffle,m}^{\mu,T}(w)&\forall v,w\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle X^{\mu}\rangle^{1},\end{cases}

where
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle X\rangle^{0}
is the sub-algebra of
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle X\rangle
generated by (quasi-)admissible words
(see Prop.
3.19
).

1.4.
Comparison theorems

We have seen by Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
that there are two ways to regularize divergent
\mu
-MHZVs. However, there are close relations between them as stated by the following comparison theorem
which plays the key role in this paper.

Theorem 1.5
.

(The Comparison Theorem)
Fix
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Ree(\mu)>0
. Let
H(x):=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-t^{x}}{1-t}dt
.
Then the following three statements hold.

(1).
For any given
m,m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, their exists a unique
\mathbb{R}
-linear map

\displaystyle{\tau}^{\mu,*}_{m,m^{\prime}}:\mathbb{R}[T]

\displaystyle\,\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}[T],

\displaystyle T^{s}

\displaystyle\,\longmapsto(T+H(m^{\prime}/\mu-1)-H(m/\mu-1))^{s}\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0},

called the
\mu
-stuffle comparison map
, that satisfies

\zeta_{*,m^{\prime}}^{\mu,T}(w)={\tau}^{\mu,*}_{m,m^{\prime}}(\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}(w))\qquad\forall w\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle.

That is, the following diagram commutes:
(\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle,{\overset{\mu}{*}})
({\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu},\cdot)[T]
({\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu},\cdot)[T]
\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}\ \
\ \ \zeta_{*,m^{\prime}}^{\mu,T}
{\tau}^{\mu,*}_{m,m^{\prime}}

(2).
For any
m,m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, their exists a unique
\mathbb{R}
-linear map

\displaystyle{\tau}^{\mu,\shuffle}_{m,m^{\prime}}:\mathbb{R}[T]

\displaystyle\,\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}[T],

\displaystyle T^{s}

\displaystyle\,\longmapsto(T+H(m^{\prime}/\mu-1)-H(m/\mu-1))^{s}\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0},

called the
shuffle comparison map
, that satisfies

\zeta_{\shuffle,m^{\prime}}^{\mu,T}(w)={\tau}^{\mu,\shuffle}_{m,m^{\prime}}(\zeta_{\shuffle,m}^{\mu,T}(w))\qquad\forall w\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle X\rangle^{1}.

That is, the following diagram commutes:
(\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle X^{\mu}\rangle^{1},\shuffle)
({\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu},\cdot)[T]
({\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu},\cdot)[T]
\zeta_{\shuffle,m}^{\mu,T}\ \

{\tau}^{\mu,\shuffle}_{m,m^{\prime}}

For any

\mathbb{R}

\zeta_{\shuffle,m^{\prime}}^{\mu,T}(\varphi_{\mu}(w))=\rho_{m,m^{\prime}}^{\mu}(\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}(w))\qquad\forall w\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle

2.
Algebraic structures on
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu}

In this section, using their series definition, we will prove that the product of two
\mu
-MHZVs is a

\mathbb{Q}[\mu]
-linear combination of
\mu
-MHZVs. We call this the
\mu
-stuffle product. Then we will describe
the integral representation of
\mu
-MHZVs similar to Kontsevich’s integral representation for MZVs. These two
kinds of product of
\mu
-MHZVs play the key roles in our theory, leading to many
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]
-linear
relations in a systematic way.

2.1.
The
\mu
-stuffle product on
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu}

We first introduce some preliminary results.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
m,n\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
,
m\neq n
, and
j,k\in\mathbb{N}
we have the partial fraction decomposition

\frac{1}{(x+m)^{j}(x+n)^{k}}=\frac{(-1)^{j-1}(j+k-2)!}{(j-1)!(k-1)!(n-m)^{j+k-1}}\left(\frac{1}{x+m}-\frac{1}{x+n}\right)\\
+\sum_{i=0}^{k-2}\binom{i+j-1}{i}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{(n-m)^{i+j}}\frac{1}{(x+n)^{k-i}}+\sum_{i=0}^{j-2}\binom{i+k-1}{i}\frac{(-1)^{i}}{(n-m)^{i+k}}\frac{1}{(x+m)^{j-i}}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\,\frac{(-1)^{j-1}(j-1)!}{(x+m)^{j}(x+n)}=\frac{\partial^{j-1}}{\partial m^{j-1}}\frac{1}{(x+m)(x+n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\partial^{j-1}}{\partial m^{j-1}}\left[\frac{1}{n-m}\left(\frac{1}{x+m}-\frac{1}{x+n}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{(j-1)!}{(n-m)^{j}}\left(\frac{1}{x+m}-\frac{1}{x+n}\right)+\sum_{i=0}^{j-2}\binom{j-1}{i}\frac{i!}{(n-m)^{i+1}}\frac{(-1)^{j-i-1}(j-i-1)!}{(x+m)^{j-i}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\,\frac{(-1)^{j-1}(j-1)!(-1)^{k-1}(k-1)!}{(x+m)^{j}(x+n)^{k}}=\frac{\partial^{k-1}}{\partial n^{k-1}}\frac{\partial^{j-1}}{\partial m^{j-1}}\frac{1}{(x+m)(x+n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{(-1)^{k-1}(j+k-2)!}{(n-m)^{j+k-1}}\left(\frac{1}{x+m}-\frac{1}{x+n}\right)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\,\sum_{i=0}^{k-2}\binom{k-1}{i}\frac{(-1)^{i}(i+j-1)!}{(n-m)^{i+j}}\frac{(-1)^{k-i-1}(k-i-1)!}{(x+n)^{k-i}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\sum_{i=0}^{j-2}\binom{j-1}{i}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}(i+k-1)!}{(n-m)^{i+k}}\frac{(-1)^{j-i-1}(j-i-1)!}{(x+m)^{j-i}}.

The lemma follows immediately.
∎

Let
M\in\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
, and
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})
be a positive bi-index. Define

\zeta_{M}^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}}):=\sum_{0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}\leqslant M}\frac{\mu^{r}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}}.

Set
\zeta^{\mu}(\emptyset):=1
by convention.
If
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})
is a non-empty admissible bi-index, then we have

\zeta^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=\lim_{M\to+\infty}\zeta^{\mu}_{M}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}}).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

Proof.

By the definition,

\displaystyle\zeta^{\mu}_{M}(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leqslant n_{r}\leqslant M}\frac{\mu}{(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}}\sum_{0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r-1}\leqslant n_{r}}\frac{\mu^{r-1}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r-1}\mu+m_{r-1})^{k_{r-1}}}

2.2.
Integral representation and shuffle product on
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu}

For the classical MZVs, Kontsevich and Drinfel’d discovered the following integral representation.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{k}}=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r})
be an admissible bi-index (
k_{r}>1
). Then we have

\zeta({\boldsymbol{k}})=\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}}\omega_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{1}-1}\frac{dt}{1-t}\cdots\left(\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{k_{r}-1}\frac{dt}{1-t}.

Proof.

See
[
3
, Chapter 1]
and
[
17
, Chapter 2]
.
∎

For the
\mu
-MHZV
\zeta^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})
, we have a similar result. To state it, we need some additional definitions
including the “
\mu
-multiple polylogarithm function”.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
. A permutation of the set

\{1,2,\cdots,r+s\}
is called a shuffle of type
(r,s)
if the following two conditions
are satisfied:

\sigma(1)<\sigma(2)<\cdots<\sigma(r),\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma(r+1)<\sigma(r+2)<\cdots<\sigma(r+s).

We denote the set of all shuffles of type
(r,s)
by
\shuffle(r,s)
. That is

\shuffle(r,s):=\left\{\sigma\in S_{r+s}\Bigg|\ \begin{aligned} &\sigma(1)<\sigma(2)<\cdots<\sigma(r),\\
&\sigma(r+1)<\sigma(r+2)<\cdots<\sigma(r+s)\end{aligned}\right\}.

Notice that the subset
\shuffle(r,s)
is not a subgroup of the permutation group
S_{r+s}
.

Example 2.9
.

If
(r,s)=(2,2)
, then

\shuffle(2,2)=\{(1),(23),(243),(123),(1243),(13)(24)\}\subset S_{4}.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})
be a positive bi-index. We define

\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};t):=\sum_{0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}}\frac{\mu^{r}t^{n_{r}\mu}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}},\qquad 0\leqslant t<1,

and call it the
\mu
-multiple polylogarithm function
.

It is easy to see that the ordinary multiple polylogarithm of single variable
[
17
, p. 78, (3.20)]

(2.10)

\Li_{\boldsymbol{k}}(t):=\sum_{0<n_{1}<\dots<n_{r}}\frac{t^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}=\Li^{\mu=1}({\boldsymbol{k}};1,2,\cdots,r;t).

Obviously, if
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})
is an admissible bi-index, then

\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};t)=\zeta^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}}).

An important property of the classical multiple polylogarithms is that they satisfy many functional equations.
But more useful to us is that they possess an iterative structure (from which its name comes), which we now extend. Recall that
{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}

is obtained from
{\boldsymbol{k}}
by removing its last component.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})
be a positive bi-index. For all
0\leqslant t<1
, we have

\displaystyle t^{m_{r}}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime},k_{r}+1;{\boldsymbol{m}};t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};t_{1})t_{1}^{m_{r}}\frac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}},

\displaystyle t^{m_{r+1}}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}},1;{\boldsymbol{m}},m_{r+1};t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};t_{1})t_{1}^{m_{r+1}}\frac{\mu dt_{1}}{t_{1}(1-t_{1}^{\mu})}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};t_{1})t_{1}^{m_{r}}\frac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}}\frac{\mu^{r}t_{1}^{n_{r}\mu}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}}\right)t_{1}^{m_{r}}\frac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mu^{r}t_{1}^{n_{r}\mu+m_{r}-1}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}}dt_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}}\frac{\mu^{r}t_{1}^{n_{r}\mu+m_{r}}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}+1}}

\displaystyle=t^{m_{r}}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime},k_{r}+1;m_{1},\cdots,m_{r};t)

which proves the first identity. For the second identity, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};t_{1})t_{1}^{m_{r+1}}\frac{\mu dt_{1}}{t_{1}(1-t_{1}^{\mu})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}}\frac{\mu^{r}t_{1}^{n_{r}\mu}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}}\right)t_{1}^{m_{r+1}}\frac{\mu dt_{1}}{t_{1}(1-t_{1}^{\mu})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}}\frac{\mu^{r}t_{1}^{n_{r}\mu}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}}\cdot\frac{\mu t_{1}^{m_{r+1}}}{t_{1}(1-t_{1}^{\mu})}\right)dt_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}}\frac{\mu^{r+1}t_{1}^{n_{r}\mu}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}}\cdot\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}t_{1}^{m\mu+m_{r+1}-1}\right)dt_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\sum_{0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}\leqslant n_{r+1}}\frac{\mu^{r+1}t_{1}^{n_{r+1}\mu+m_{r+1}-1}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}}\right)dt_{1}\qquad(n_{r+1}=n_{r}+m)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}\leqslant n_{r+1}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mu^{r+1}t_{1}^{n_{r+1}\mu+m_{r+1}-1}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}}dt_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}\leqslant n_{r+1}}\frac{\mu^{r+1}t^{n_{r+1}\mu+m_{r+1}}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}(n_{r+1}\mu+m_{r+1})}

\displaystyle=t^{m_{r+1}}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}},1;{\boldsymbol{m}},m_{r+1};t),

as desired.
∎

Another notation is needed to describe
the general integral representation of
\mu
-MHZVs.

Definition 2.12
.

Given a real number
0<t\leqslant 1
, we define

\Delta^{r}(t):=\left\{(t_{1},\cdots,t_{r})\in\mathbb{R}^{r}\big|0<t_{r}<\cdots<t_{1}<t\right\},\qquad 0<t\leqslant 1.

When
t=1
, we will simply write
\Delta^{r}:=\Delta^{r}(1)
. Furthermore, consider the 1-forms on the open interval
(0,1)

\omega_{0}(t):=\frac{dt}{t},\qquad\omega_{1,\mu}^{(m)}(t):=\frac{\mu t^{m}dt}{t(1-t^{\mu})},

where
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}\times\mathbb{N}^{r}
is a positive bi-index.
Put
s_{i}=k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i}(i=1,\cdots,r)
. For convenience, we write
s_{0}=0,m_{0}=0
. Let
\omega_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}

be the measure on the interior of the simplex
\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}
given by

\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\omega_{1,\mu}^{(m_{i}-m_{i-1})}(t_{s_{r+1-i}})\omega_{0}(t_{s_{r+1-i}-1})\cdots\omega_{0}(t_{s_{r-i}+1}).

For example, one has:

\displaystyle\omega^{\mu}_{(2;m_{1})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu t_{2}^{m_{1}}dt_{2}}{t_{2}(1-t_{2}^{\mu})}\ \frac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}}

\displaystyle=\omega_{1,\mu}^{(m_{1})}(t_{2})\omega_{0}(t_{1}),

\displaystyle\omega^{\mu}_{(2,2;m_{1},m_{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu t_{4}^{m_{1}}dt_{4}}{t_{4}(1-t_{4}^{\mu})}\ \frac{dt_{3}}{t_{3}}\ \frac{\mu t_{2}^{m_{2}-m_{1}}dt_{2}}{t_{2}(1-t_{2}^{\mu})}\ \frac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}}

\displaystyle=\omega_{1,\mu}^{(m_{1})}(t_{4})\omega_{0}(t_{3})\omega_{1,\mu}^{(m_{2}-m_{1})}(t_{2})\omega_{0}(t_{1}),

\displaystyle\omega^{\mu}_{(1,3;m_{1},m_{2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu t_{4}^{m_{1}}dt_{4}}{t_{4}(1-t_{4}^{\mu})}\ \frac{\mu t_{3}^{m_{2}-m_{1}}dt_{3}}{t_{3}(1-t_{3}^{\mu})}\ \frac{dt_{2}}{t_{2}}\ \frac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}}

\displaystyle=\omega_{1,\mu}^{(m_{1})}(t_{4})\omega_{1,\mu}^{(m_{2}-m_{1})}(t_{3})\omega_{0}(t_{2})\omega_{0}(t_{1}),

\displaystyle\omega^{\mu}_{(2,1,2;m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu t_{5}^{m_{1}}dt_{5}}{t_{5}(1-t_{5}^{\mu})}\ \frac{dt_{4}}{t_{4}}\ \frac{\mu t_{3}^{m_{2}-m_{1}}dt_{3}}{t_{3}(1-t_{3}^{\mu})}\ \frac{\mu t_{2}^{m_{3}-m_{2}}dt_{2}}{t_{2}(1-t_{2}^{\mu})}\ \frac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}}

\displaystyle=\omega_{1,\mu}^{(m_{1})}(t_{5})\omega_{0}(t_{4})\omega_{1,\mu}^{(m_{2}-m_{1})}(t_{3})\omega_{1,\mu}^{(m_{3}-m_{2})}(t_{2})\omega_{0}(t_{1}).

Now the integral representation is a particular case of the next result.

Theorem 2.13
.

If
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})
is a positive bi-index and
0<t<1
, then the following identity holds:

t^{m_{r}}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};t)=\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}(t)}\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on the weight
|{\boldsymbol{k}}|
. If
|{\boldsymbol{k}}|=1
, then
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(1;m_{1})
and the statement is just the second identity in Lemma
2.11
. The inductive step follows from
the iterative procedure given by Lemma
2.11
. Indeed, let
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})
be a
positive bi-index and assume that the result is true for all bi-indices of lower
weights. If
k_{r}>1
, then by writing

({\boldsymbol{k}}_{-};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r}-1;m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})

and applying the first identity in Lemma
2.11
, we have, by the
induction assumption,

t^{m_{r}}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};t)=\int_{0}^{t}t_{1}^{m_{r}}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}}_{-};{\boldsymbol{m}};t_{1})\frac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}_{-}|}(t_{1})}\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}}_{-};{\boldsymbol{m}})}\frac{dt_{1}}{t_{1}}=\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}(t)}\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}.

If
k_{r}=1
then by the second identity in Lemma
2.11
and the
induction assumption

\displaystyle t^{m_{r}}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}t_{1}^{m_{r-1}}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}}_{-};{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\prime};t_{1})\frac{\mu t_{1}^{m_{r}-m_{r-1}}dt_{1}}{t_{1}(1-t_{1}^{\mu})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}_{-}|}(t_{1})}\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime};{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\prime})}\right)\frac{\mu t_{1}^{m_{r}-m_{r-1}}dt_{1}}{t_{1}(1-t_{1}^{\mu})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}(t)}\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}.

We have now finished the proof of the theorem.
∎

Corollary 2.14
.

If
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})
is an admissible bi-index, then the following identity holds:

\zeta^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}}\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}.

For any special triple
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})
, we have

{\mathcal{D}}^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})=\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}}(\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}-\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})}).

Proof.

By assumption, if
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})
is an admissible bi-index then

\zeta^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}t^{m_{r}}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};t)=\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}(t)}\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}=\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}}\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}.

If
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})
is a special triple, then

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}(t^{m_{r}}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};t)-t^{{\widetilde{m}}_{r}}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}};t))

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}(t)}(\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}-\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})}),

as desired.
∎

.

2.3.
Bi-indices and binary sequences

To exploit the preceding results to derive relations among polylogarithms, and in particular among
\mu
-MHZVs,
we need a new notation. This will enable us to go from bi-indices to some binary sequences
and vice versa.

Definition 2.16
.

To each positive bi-index
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})
we attach the
positive binary sequence

\bs({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(1,0_{k_{1}-1},1,0_{k_{2}-1},\cdots,1,0_{k_{r}-1};m_{1},0_{k_{1}-1},m_{2}-m_{1},0_{k_{2}-1},\cdots,m_{r}-m_{r-1},0_{k_{r}-1})

where
0_{k}
means that the entry zero is repeated
k
times.

It clearly has an inverse map

\text{bs}^{-1}(1,0_{k_{1}},1,0_{k_{2}},\cdots,1,0_{k_{r}};m_{1},0_{k_{1}},m_{2},0_{k_{2}},\cdots,m_{r},0_{k_{r}})=(k_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,k_{r}^{\prime};m_{1}^{\prime},\cdots,m_{r}^{\prime})

where
k_{i}^{\prime}=k_{i}+1,m_{i}^{\prime}=m_{1}+\cdots+m_{i},i=1,\cdots,r
.

.

\displaystyle\bs(2,1,2;m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})

\displaystyle\int_{\Delta^{r}(t)}\phi_{1}(t_{1})\cdots\phi_{r}(t_{r})\cdot\int_{\Delta^{s}(t)}\phi_{r+1}(t_{r+1})\cdots\phi_{r+s}(t_{r+s})=\sum_{\sigma\in\shuffle(r,s)}\int_{\Delta^{r+s}(t)}\phi_{\sigma^{-1}}(t_{1})\cdots\phi_{\sigma^{-1}(r+s)}(t_{r+s})

Let

is the

We say that a word
w=y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{r},m_{r}}
has
length

\ell(w)=r
. We put 1 as the empty word and set
\ell(1)=0
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]Y
as above. We define a
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]
-bilinear map

-\odot-:\mathbb{Q}[\mu]Y\times\mathbb{Q}[\mu]Y\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}[\mu]Y

as follows:

y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\odot y_{k_{2},m_{2}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k_{1}}a_{i}\,y_{i,m_{1}}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k_{2}}b_{j}y_{j,m_{2}},&\hbox{if $m_{1}\neq m_{2}$;}\\
y_{k_{1}+k_{2},m},&\hbox{if $m_{1}=m_{2}=m$,}\end{array}\right.

where the coefficients
a_{1},\cdots,a_{k_{1}},b_{1},\cdots,b_{k_{2}}\in\mathbb{Q}
are determined by the following decomposition

\frac{1}{(x+m_{1})^{k_{1}}(x+m_{2})^{k_{2}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}}\frac{a_{i}}{(x+m_{1})^{i}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{2}}\frac{b_{j}}{(x+m_{2})^{j}},

according to Lemma
2.1

Lemma 3.2
.

The
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]
-bilinear map
-\odot-:\mathbb{Q}[\mu]Y\times\mathbb{Q}[\mu]Y\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}[\mu]Y
is a commutative and associative product.

Proof.

The commutativity is clear so that we only need to prove the associativity.
Further, by commutativity, we only need to consider the following three cases:

(i). If
m_{1}=m_{2}=m_{3}=m
, then we have

\displaystyle(y_{k_{1},m}\odot y_{k_{2},m})\odot y_{k_{3},m}

\displaystyle=y_{k_{1}+k_{2},m}\odot y_{k_{3},m}

\displaystyle=y_{k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3},m}

\displaystyle=y_{k_{1},m}\odot y_{k_{2}+k_{3},m}

\displaystyle=y_{k_{1},m}\odot(y_{k_{2},m}\odot y_{k_{3},m}).

(ii). If
m_{1}=m_{2}=m\neq m_{3}
, then we have two different decomposition

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(x+m)^{k_{1}+k_{2}}(x+m_{3})^{k_{3}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}\frac{a_{i}}{(x+m)^{i}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{3}}\frac{b_{j}}{(x+m_{3})^{j}},

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(x+m)^{k_{1}+k_{2}}(x+m_{3})^{k_{3}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(x+m)^{k_{1}}}\left(\frac{1}{(x+m)^{k_{2}}(x+m_{3})^{k_{3}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(x+m)^{k_{1}}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}\frac{a_{k_{1}+i}^{\prime}}{(x+m)^{i}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{3}}\frac{b_{j}^{\prime}}{(x+m_{3})^{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}\frac{a_{k_{1}+i}^{\prime}}{(x+m)^{k_{1}+i}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{3}}\frac{b_{j}^{\prime}}{(x+m)^{k_{1}}(x+m_{3})^{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}\frac{a_{k_{1}+i}^{\prime}}{(x+m)^{k_{1}+i}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{3}}b_{j}^{\prime}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{k_{1}}\frac{c_{t}}{(x+m)^{t}}+\sum_{s=1}^{j}\frac{d_{s}}{(x+m_{3})^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}\frac{a_{k_{1}+i}^{\prime}}{(x+m)^{k_{1}+i}}+\sum_{t=1}^{k_{1}}\left(c_{t}\sum_{j=1}^{k_{3}}b_{j}^{\prime}\right)\frac{1}{(x+m)^{t}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{3}}\left(d_{j}\sum_{s=j}^{k_{3}}b_{s}^{\prime}\right)\frac{1}{(x+m_{3})^{j}}.

We conclude that

a_{i}=\begin{cases}c_{i}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k_{3}}b_{j}^{\prime},\qquad&\text{if }1\leqslant i\leqslant k_{1};\\
a_{k_{1}+i}^{\prime},&\text{if }k_{1}+1\leqslant i\leqslant k_{2},\end{cases}

and
b_{j}=d_{j}\sum\limits_{s=j}^{k_{3}}b_{s}^{\prime}
for all
1\leqslant j\leqslant k_{3}
. Hence,

\displaystyle(y_{k_{1},m}\odot y_{k_{2},m})\odot y_{k_{3},m_{3}}=y_{k_{1}+k_{2},m}\odot y_{k_{3},m_{3}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}a_{i}\,y_{i,m}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{3}}b_{j}y_{j,m_{3}},

and

\displaystyle y_{k_{1},m}\odot\left(y_{k_{2},m}\odot y_{k_{3},m_{3}}\right)

\displaystyle=y_{k_{1},m}\odot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}a_{k_{2}+i}^{\prime}y_{i,m}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{3}}b_{j}^{\prime}y_{j,m_{3}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}a_{k_{2}+i}^{\prime}y_{k_{1}+i,m}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{3}}b_{j}^{\prime}y_{k_{1},m}\odot y_{j,m_{3}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}a_{k_{2}+i}^{\prime}y_{k_{1}+i,m}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{3}}b_{j}^{\prime}\left(\sum_{t=1}^{k_{1}}c_{t}y_{t,m}+\sum_{s=1}^{j}d_{s}y_{s,m_{3}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}a_{k_{1}+i}^{\prime}y_{k_{1}+i,m}+\sum_{t=1}^{k_{1}}\left(c_{t}\sum_{j=1}^{k_{3}}b_{j}^{\prime}\right)y_{t,m}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{3}}\left(d_{j}\sum_{s=j}^{k_{3}}b_{s}^{\prime}\right)y_{j,m_{3}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=k_{1}+1}^{k_{2}}a_{i}\,y_{i,m}+\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}}a_{i}\,y_{i,m}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{3}}b_{j}y_{j,m_{3}}

\displaystyle=(y_{k_{1},m}\odot y_{k_{2},m})\odot y_{k_{3},m_{3}}.

(iii). If
m_{i}\neq m_{j},i\neq j,1\leqslant i,j\leqslant 3
, then we can apply the same technique to get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(x+m_{1})^{k_{1}}(x+m_{2})^{k_{2}}(x+m_{3})^{k_{3}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}}\frac{a_{i}}{(x+m_{1})^{i}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{2}}\frac{b_{j}}{(x+m_{2})^{j}}\right)\frac{1}{(x+m_{3})^{k_{3}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}}a_{i}\left(\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{i}\frac{c_{i_{1}}}{(x+m_{1})^{i_{1}}}+\sum_{i_{2}=1}^{k_{3}}\frac{d_{i_{2}}}{(x+m_{3})^{i_{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{2}}b_{j}\left(\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{j}\frac{e_{j_{1}}}{(x+m_{2})^{j_{1}}}+\sum_{j_{2}=1}^{k_{3}}\frac{f_{j_{2}}}{(x+m_{3})^{j_{2}}}\right).

Thus,

\displaystyle(y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\odot y_{k_{2},m_{2}})\odot y_{k_{3},m_{3}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{1}}a_{i}\left(\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{i}c_{i_{1}}y_{i_{1},m_{1}}+\sum_{i_{2}=1}^{k_{2}}d_{i_{2}}y_{i_{2},m_{3}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{2}}b_{j}\left(\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{j}e_{j_{1}}y_{j_{1},m_{2}}+\sum_{j_{2}=1}^{k_{3}}f_{j_{2}}y_{j_{2},m_{3}}\right).

On the other hand,,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(x+m_{1})^{k_{1}}(x+m_{2})^{k_{2}}(x+m_{3})^{k_{3}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(x+m_{1})^{k_{1}}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}\frac{a_{i}^{\prime}}{(x+m_{2})^{i}}+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{3}}\frac{b_{j}^{\prime}}{(x+m_{3})^{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}a_{i}^{\prime}\left(\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{k_{1}}\frac{c_{i_{1}}^{\prime}}{(x+m_{1})^{i_{1}}}+\sum_{i_{2}=1}^{i}\frac{d^{\prime}_{i_{2}}}{(x+m_{2})^{i_{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{3}}b_{j}^{\prime}\left(\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{k_{1}}\frac{e^{\prime}_{j_{1}}}{(x+m_{1})^{j_{1}}}+\sum_{j_{2}=1}^{j}\frac{f^{\prime}_{j_{2}}}{(x+m_{3})^{j_{2}}}\right).

Hence

\displaystyle y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\odot(y_{k_{2},m_{2}}\odot y_{k_{3},m_{3}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k_{2}}a_{i}^{\prime}\left(\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{k_{1}}c_{i_{1}}^{\prime}y_{i_{1},m_{1}}+\sum_{i_{2}=1}^{i}d^{\prime}_{i_{2}}y_{i_{2},m_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{k_{3}}b_{j}^{\prime}\left(\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{k_{1}}e^{\prime}_{j_{1}}y_{j_{1},m_{1}}+\sum_{j_{2}=1}^{j}f^{\prime}_{j_{2}}y_{j_{2},m_{3}}\right).

By comparing the coefficients, we arrive at the following conclusion

(y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\odot y_{k_{2},m_{2}})\odot y_{k_{3},m_{3}}=y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\odot(y_{k_{2},m_{2}}\odot y_{k_{3},m_{3}}).

∎

First, we check the commutativity

(iii). If

\displaystyle\,y_{1,m_{r}}\odot y_{1,m_{s}^{\prime\prime}}-y_{1,m_{r}}\odot y_{1,m_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}}

y_{2,m},&\text{if }m=m^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Hence
a{\overset{\mu}{*}}b\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle^{0}

\zeta^{\mu}_{*}
on non-admissible words. First we observe that
there is a bijection between the set of positive bi-indices and the set of words over
Y
:

({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})\leftrightarrow y_{{\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}}}.

We will use this bijection to identify both sets. The next lemma will be useful when we estimate
partial sums of
\mu
-MHZVs.

Lemma 4.1
.

Put
n_{0}=0
. Let

U_{r,i}(M):=\left\{(n_{1},\cdots,n_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}\big|0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{i}\leqslant M\leqslant n_{i+1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}\right\}.

Let
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})
be a positive bi-index. Then

(4.1)

\sum_{U_{r,r}(M)}\frac{\mu^{r}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}}=O(\ln^{r}(M))\quad\text{as}\quad M\to+\infty.

If
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})
is admissible and
0\leqslant i\leqslant r-1
, then

(4.2)

\sum_{U_{r,i}(M)}\frac{\mu^{r}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}}=O\left(\frac{\ln^{i}(M)}{M}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad M\to+\infty.

In both (
4.1
) and (
4.1
) the big-
O
constants only depend on
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})
and
\mu
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume
\mu>0
and by scaling we can even assume
m_{j}
’s are positive integers.
We prove (
4.1
) by induction on
r
. When
r=1
this is obvious. For
r>1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{U_{r,r}(M)}\frac{\mu^{r}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{U_{r,r}(M)}\frac{\mu^{r}}{(n_{1}\mu+1)\cdots(n_{r}\mu+1)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{0\leqslant n_{2}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}\leqslant M}\frac{\mu^{r}}{(n_{2}\mu+1)\cdots(n_{r}\mu+1)}+\sum_{1\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}\leqslant M}\frac{1}{n_{1}\cdots n_{r}}

\displaystyle\leqslant O(\ln^{r-1}(M))+\sum_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{r}=1}^{\lfloor M\rfloor}\frac{1}{n_{1}\cdots n_{r}}\quad(\text{by induction})

\displaystyle=O(\ln^{r}(M)).

To prove (
4.2
) we notice that

\displaystyle\sum_{U_{r,i}(M)}\frac{\mu^{r}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{U_{i,i}(M)}\frac{\mu^{i}}{(n_{1}\mu+1)\cdots(n_{i}\mu+1)}\sum_{M\leqslant n_{i+1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}}\frac{\mu^{r-i}}{(n_{i+1}\mu+1)\cdots(n_{r-1}\mu+1)(n_{r}\mu+1)^{2}}.

By (
4.1
) we only need to show that for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
, as
M\to\infty

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{M\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}}\frac{1}{n_{1}\cdots n_{r-1}n_{r}^{2}}=O(1/M).

This is clear if
r=1
. For general
r>1
, we have, for all
M>2

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{r-1}\leqslant n_{r}}\frac{1}{n_{r}^{2}}<\sum_{n_{r-1}\leqslant n_{r}}\frac{2}{n_{r}(n_{r}+1)}=\frac{2}{n_{r-1}}

by telescoping series. Thus (
4.3
) follows immediately by induction.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
M
be a positive real number. Then

(1).
If
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})
is an admissible bi-index (i.e.,
k_{r}>1
), then

\zeta_{M}^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=\zeta^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})+O\left(\frac{\ln^{r-1}(M)}{M}\right)\qquad M\to+\infty.

(2).
If
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})
is a special triple then

{\mathcal{D}}^{\mu}_{M}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})={\mathcal{D}}^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})+O\left(\frac{\ln^{r-1}(M)}{M}\right).

In (1) (resp. (2)) the big-
O
constant only depends on
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})
(resp.
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})
) and
\mu
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume
\mu>0
and
m_{j}
’s are positive integers.

(1). If
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})
is an admissible bi-index, then

\displaystyle|\zeta_{M}^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})-\zeta^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\sum_{U_{r,i}(M)}\frac{\mu^{r}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\sum_{U_{r,i}(M)}\frac{\mu^{r}}{(n_{1}\mu+1)\cdots(n_{r-1}\mu+1)(n_{r}\mu+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{equ:Uri}}{=}\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}O\left(\frac{\ln^{i}(M)}{M}\right)=O\left(\frac{\ln^{r-1}(M)}{M}\right).

(2). Notice that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}^{\mu}_{M}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})=\zeta^{\mu}_{M}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})-\zeta^{\mu}_{M}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})

\displaystyle=({\widetilde{m}}_{r}-m_{r})\sum_{0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}\leqslant M}\frac{\mu^{r}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r-1}\mu+m_{r-1})^{k_{r-1}}(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})(n_{r}\mu+{\widetilde{m}}_{r})}.

Hence,

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{D}}^{\mu}_{M}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})-{\mathcal{D}}^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}|{\widetilde{m}}_{r}-m_{r}|\sum_{U_{r,i}(M)}\frac{\mu^{r}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r-1}\mu+m_{r-1})^{k_{r-1}}(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})(n_{r}\mu+{\widetilde{m}}_{r})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}|{\widetilde{m}}_{r}-m_{r}|\sum_{U_{r,i}(M)}\frac{\mu^{r}}{(n_{1}\mu+1)\cdots(n_{r-1}\mu+1)(n_{r}\mu+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{equ:Uri}}{=}\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}|{\widetilde{m}}_{r}-m_{r}|O\left(\frac{\ln^{i}(M)}{M}\right)=O\left(\frac{\ln^{r-1}(M)}{M}\right).

∎

Next, we will prove a crucial theorem which allows us to consider non-admissible words and then non-admissible
\mu
-MHZVs.

Theorem 4.3
.

The map

\displaystyle\psi_{*,m}:(\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle^{0},{\overset{\mu}{*}})[T]

\displaystyle\longrightarrow(\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle,{\overset{\mu}{*}}),

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle\longmapsto y_{1,m}

is an isomorphism of
(\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle^{0},{\overset{\mu}{*}})
-algebra.

Proof.

We will prove this result in two steps.

Step 1. We first show that the map
\psi_{*,m}
is surjective, which amounts to saying that any element
w\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle
can be written as a polynomial in
y_{1,m}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle^{0}
.
To this end, we observe that there exists a filtration on
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle
induced by the length
\ell
as follows

0\subset F_{0}\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle\subset F_{1}\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle\subset F_{2}\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle\subset\cdots

where

F_{\ell}\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle:=\Big\langle y_{{\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}}}|({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}}):\text{ positive bi-index},\,\ell({\boldsymbol{k}})\leqslant\ell\Big\rangle_{\mathbb{Q}[\mu]}.

To show the surjectivity by induction on
\ell
, we only need to show that, for a fixed length
\ell
and a word

w\in F_{\ell}\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle
, there are elements
v_{1}\in F_{\ell}\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle^{0}
and

v_{2},v_{3}\in F_{\ell-1}\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle
such that

w=v_{1}+v_{2}{\overset{\mu}{*}}y_{1,m}+v_{3}.

Let
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r},1,\cdots,1;m_{1},\cdots,m_{r},m_{r+1},\cdots,m_{r+s})
,
k_{r}>1
and

y_{{\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}}}=y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{r},m_{r}}y_{1,m_{r+1}}\cdots y_{1,m_{r+s}}.

Then,

\displaystyle y_{{\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}}}

\displaystyle=y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{r},m_{r}}y_{1,m_{r+1}}\cdots y_{1,m_{r+s}}

\displaystyle=y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{r},m_{r}}y_{1,m}^{s}+y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{r},m_{r}}(y_{1,m_{r+1}}-y_{1,m})y_{1,m}^{s-1}

\displaystyle+y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{r},m_{r}}y_{1,m_{r+1}}(y_{1,m_{r+2}}-y_{1,m})y_{1,m}^{s-2}+\cdots

\displaystyle+y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{r},m_{r}}y_{1,m_{r+1}}\cdots y_{1,m_{r+s-1}}(y_{1,m_{r+s}}-y_{1,m}).

We now apply induction of the power of
y_{1,m}
to
treat all the terms on the right-hand side by breaking them into two cases.

(i). For the word
y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{r},m_{r}}y_{1,m}^{s}
, by recursive calculation, we have

\displaystyle y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{r},m_{r}}y_{1,m}^{s-1}{\overset{\mu}{*}}y_{1,m}

\displaystyle=s\cdot y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{r},m_{r}}y_{1,m}^{s}+y_{1,m}y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{r},m_{r}}y_{1,m}^{s-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{i},m_{i}}y_{1,m}y_{k_{i+1},m_{i+1}}\cdots y_{k_{r},m_{r}}y_{1,m}^{s-1}+v_{3},

with
v_{3}\in F_{r+s-1}\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle
. Applying the induction hypothesis with respect to
s
we obtain the desired conclusion.

(ii). For the word
y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{r},m_{r}}y_{1,m_{r+1}}\cdots y_{1,m_{r+i-1}}(y_{1,m_{r+i}}-y_{1,m})y_{1,m}^{s-i},i=1,\cdots s
, by recursive calculation, we also have

\displaystyle y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{r},m_{r}}y_{1,m_{r+1}}\cdots y_{1,m_{r+i-1}}(y_{1,m_{r+i}}-y_{1,m})y_{1,m}^{s-i-1}{\overset{\mu}{*}}y_{1,m}

\displaystyle=(s-i)\cdot y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{r},m_{r}}y_{1,m_{r+1}}\cdots y_{1,m_{r+i-1}}(y_{1,m_{r+i}}-y_{1,m})y_{1,m}^{s-i}

\displaystyle\quad+y_{1,m}y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{r},m_{r}}y_{1,m_{r+1}}\cdots y_{1,m_{r+i-1}}(y_{1,m_{r+i}}-y_{1,m})y_{1,m}^{s-i-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\cdots

\displaystyle\quad+y_{k_{1},m_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{r},m_{r}}y_{1,m_{r+1}}\cdots y_{1,m_{r+i-1}}y_{1,m}(y_{1,m_{r+i}}-y_{1,m})y_{1,m}^{s-i-1}+v_{3}

with
v_{3}\in F_{r+s-1}\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle
. Applying the induction hypothesis with respect to
s-i
we obtain the desired conclusion.

Moreover, by keeping track of the lengths of all the words involved we conclude that

y_{{\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}}}=w_{s}{\overset{\mu}{*}}\overbrace{y_{1,m}{\overset{\mu}{*}}\cdots{\overset{\mu}{*}}y_{1,m}}^{s}+w_{s-1}{\overset{\mu}{*}}\overbrace{y_{1,m}{\overset{\mu}{*}}\cdots{\overset{\mu}{*}}y_{1,m}}^{s-1}+\cdots+w_{0}

where
w_{s},\cdots,w_{0}\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle^{0}
such that
\ell(w_{j})=r+s-j
for all
r\leqslant j\leqslant r+s
. It follows that
\psi_{*,m}
is surjective.

Step 2. To prove the injectivity of
\psi_{*,m}
, we write each non-zero

P\in(\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle^{0},{\overset{\mu}{*}})[T]
as

P=w_{1}T^{s}+w_{2}

where
0\neq w_{1}\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle^{0}
and
w_{2}
has
T
-degree less than
s
. Then

\psi_{*,m}(P)=s!w_{1}y_{1,m}^{s}+v_{2}

where all the words in
v_{2}
have less than
s
copies of
y_{1,m}
at the end. Thus,
\psi_{*,m}(P)\neq 0
and
\psi_{*,m}
is injective.

∎

Using the previous results, we can now prove one of the most important theorems in the paper.

Theorem-Definition 4.4
.

Let
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r},1_{s};m_{1},\cdots,m_{r+s})\in\mathbb{N}^{r+s}\times\mathbb{Q}^{r+s}
with
k_{r}>1
.
For any fixed
m\in\mathbb{Q}
we have

\zeta_{M}^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=a_{s}(\zeta_{M}^{\mu}(1;m))^{s}+a_{s-1}(\zeta_{M}^{\mu}(1;m))^{s-1}+\cdots+a_{0}+O\left(\frac{\ln^{r+s-1}(M)}{M}\right),

where
a_{0},\cdots,a_{s}\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu}
. We define the
\mu
-stuffle regularization homomorphism
as follows

\displaystyle\zeta^{\mu,T}_{*,m}:(\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle,{\overset{\mu}{*}})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow({\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu},\cdot)[T],

\displaystyle y_{{\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}}}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{i=0}^{s}a_{i}T^{i}.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.3
,

y_{{\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}}}=w_{s}{\overset{\mu}{*}}\overbrace{y_{1,m}{\overset{\mu}{*}}\cdots{\overset{\mu}{*}}y_{1,m}}^{s}+w_{s-1}{\overset{\mu}{*}}\overbrace{y_{1,m}{\overset{\mu}{*}}\cdots{\overset{\mu}{*}}y_{1,m}}^{s-1}+\cdots+w_{0}

where
w_{s},\cdots,w_{0}\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle^{0}
such that
\ell(w_{j})=r+s-j
for all
r\leqslant j\leqslant r+s
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\zeta^{\mu}_{M}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})

\displaystyle=\zeta^{\mu}_{*,M}(y_{{\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\zeta^{\mu}_{*,M}(w_{i}{\overset{\mu}{*}}\overbrace{y_{1,m}{\overset{\mu}{*}}\cdots{\overset{\mu}{*}}y_{1,m}}^{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\zeta^{\mu}_{*,M}(w_{i})\cdot(\zeta^{\mu}_{*,M}(y_{1,m}))^{i}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{equ:Urr}}{=}\sum_{i=0}^{s}\left(a_{i}+O\left(\frac{\ln^{r+s-i-1}(M)}{M}\right)\right)\cdot(\zeta^{\mu}_{M}(1;m))^{i}\quad(\text{since }\ell(w_{i})=r+s-i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{s}a_{i}\cdot(\zeta^{\mu}_{M}(1;m))^{i}+O\left(\frac{\ln^{r+s-1}(M)}{M}\right)

since
\zeta^{\mu}_{M}(1;m)=O(\ln(M))
by (
4.1
), where
a_{i}=\zeta^{\mu}_{*}(w_{i})\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu}
for all
0\leqslant i\leqslant s
.
∎

Example 4.5
.

Let
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(1,1;1,2)
be a positive bi-index. Then we have

\displaystyle y_{1,1}y_{1,2}

\displaystyle=y_{1,1}(y_{1,2}-y_{1,1})+y_{1,1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{1,1}{\overset{\mu}{*}}y_{1,1}+\mu y_{2,1}}{2}+y_{1,1}(y_{1,2}-y_{1,2}).

Hence

\displaystyle\zeta_{M}^{\mu}(1,1;1,2)

\displaystyle=\zeta_{*,M}^{\mu}(y_{1,1}y_{1,2})

\displaystyle=\frac{(\zeta^{\mu}_{*,M}(y_{1,1}))^{2}}{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\zeta^{\mu}_{*,M}(y_{2,1})+\zeta^{\mu}_{*,M}(y_{1,1}(y_{1,2}-y_{1,1}))

\displaystyle=\frac{(\zeta^{\mu}_{*,M}(y_{1,1}))^{2}}{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\zeta^{\mu}_{*}(y_{2,1})+\zeta^{\mu}_{*}(y_{1,1}(y_{1,2}-y_{1,1}))+O\left(\frac{\ln^{2}(M)}{M}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(\zeta^{\mu}_{*,M}(y_{1,1}))^{2}}{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\zeta^{\mu}(2;1)+{\mathcal{D}}^{\mu}(1,1;1,2;1,1)+O\left(\frac{\ln^{2}(M)}{M}\right).

Theorem 4.6
.

The map
\zeta^{\mu,T}_{*,m}:(\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle,{\overset{\mu}{*}})\longrightarrow({\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu},\cdot)[T]
is a
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]
-algebra homomorphism.

Proof.

By the definition of
\zeta^{\mu}_{*,M}
, we know that

\zeta^{\mu}_{*,M}(w{\overset{\mu}{*}}v)=\zeta^{\mu}_{*,M}(w)\cdot\zeta^{\mu}_{*,M}(v)\qquad\forall w,v\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle.

By the definition of
\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}
, we have

\zeta^{\mu}_{*,M}(w)=\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}(w)+O\left(\frac{\ln^{r}(M)}{M}\right).

Since
\zeta^{\mu}_{M}(1;m)=O(\ln(M))
by (
4.1
) we get

\zeta^{\mu,T}_{*,m}(w{\overset{\mu}{*}}v)=\zeta^{\mu,T}_{*,m}(w)\cdot\zeta^{\mu,T}_{*,m}(v)\qquad\forall w,v\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle,

as desired.
∎

Furthermore, the map
\zeta^{\mu,T}_{*,m}
is characterized by the following conditions.

Theorem 4.7
.

For any given
m\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, there exists a unique
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]
-linear map

\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}:(\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle,{\overset{\mu}{*}})\longrightarrow({\mathcal{Z}}^{\mu},\cdot)[T]

that satisfies

\begin{cases}\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}(w)=\zeta_{*}^{\mu}(w)\qquad&\forall w\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle^{0};\\
\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}(y_{1,m})=T;\\
\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}(v{\overset{\mu}{*}}w)=\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}(v)\cdot\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}(w)&\forall v,w\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle.\end{cases}

Proof.

The existence of
\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}
is guaranteed by Theorem
4.6
.
We only need to prove the uniqueness. By Theorem
4.3
, we have

w=w_{s}{\overset{\mu}{*}}\overbrace{y_{1,m}{\overset{\mu}{*}}\cdots{\overset{\mu}{*}}y_{1,m}}^{s}+w_{s-1}{\overset{\mu}{*}}\overbrace{y_{1,m}{\overset{\mu}{*}}\cdots{\overset{\mu}{*}}y_{1,m}}^{s-1}+\cdots+w_{0}

where
w_{0},\cdots,w_{s}\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle Y\rangle^{0}
. If
f
is another map satisfying the above properties, then we have

f(w)=\sum_{i=0}^{s}f(w_{i})\cdot(f(y_{1,m}))^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{s}f(w_{i})\cdot T^{i}.

But
w_{i}
is the
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]
-linear combination of (quasi-)admissible words and therefore

f(w)=\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}(w).

This proves the uniqueness
\zeta_{*,m}^{\mu,T}
.
∎

4.2.
Regularization homomorphisms for shuffle product

In this section, we will consider the regularization of
\zeta_{\shuffle}^{\mu}
on non-admissible words.
Recall that each bi-index
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})
defines a word
(y,x)^{\mu}_{{\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}}}.

In fact, there is a bijection between the set of positive bi-indices and the set of words in
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle X^{\mu}\rangle^{1}
.

For
0<t<1
, we defines the
\mathbb{Q}[\mu]
-linear map

\displaystyle\zeta^{\mu}_{\shuffle,t}:(\mathbb{Q}[\mu]\langle X^{\mu}\rangle^{1},\shuffle)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(0,1),

\displaystyle(y,x)^{\mu}_{{\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}}}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}(t)}\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}.

Lemma 4.9
.

For
0<t<1
, we have

(1)
. If
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})
is a positive bi-index, then

\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}(t)}\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}=O\Big(\ln^{r}\Big(\frac{1}{1-t}\Big)\Big)\quad\text{as}\quad t\to 1^{-}.

(2)
. If
({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})=(k_{1},\cdots,k_{r};m_{1},\cdots,m_{r})
is an admissible bi-index, then

\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}(t)}\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}=\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}}\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}+O\Big((1-t)\ln^{r}\Big(\frac{1}{1-t}\Big)\Big)\quad\text{as}\quad t\to 1^{-}.

Proof.

(1). For
0<t<1
we have

\frac{\mu}{1-t^{\mu}}\leqslant\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{1-t},&\hbox{if $\mu\geqslant 1$;}\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-t},&\hbox{if $0<\mu\leqslant 1$.}\end{array}\right.\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{\mu}{1-t^{\mu}}\leqslant\frac{c}{1-t}\quad\forall t\in(0,1),

where
c:=\max\{1,\mu\}
. By Theorem
2.13
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}(t)}\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}=t^{m_{r}}\Li^{\mu}({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}};t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}}\frac{\mu^{r}t^{n_{r}\mu+m_{r}}}{(n_{1}\mu+m_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}\mu+m_{r})^{k_{r}}}.

It is clear that for
t
sufficiently close to 1 we always have
t^{m_{r}}<2t
. Further,
by scaling we now may assume all
m_{j}
’s are positive integers. Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{\Delta^{|{\boldsymbol{k}}|}(t)}\omega^{\mu}_{({\boldsymbol{k}};{\boldsymbol{m}})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{0\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant\cdots\leqslant n_{r}}\frac{2\mu^{r}t^{n_{r}\mu+1}}{(n_{1}\mu+1)\cdots(n_{r}\mu+1)}

\displaystyle=2\int\limits_{0<t_{r}<\cdots<t_{1}<t}\frac{\mu dt_{r}}{1-t_{r}^{\mu}}\frac{\mu dt_{r-1}}{t_{r-1}(1-t_{r-1}^{\mu})}\frac{\mu dt_{r-2}}{t_{r-2}(1-t_{r-2}^{\mu})}\cdots\frac{\mu dt_{1}}{t_{1}(1-t_{1}^{\mu})}

Lemma 4.24
.

As
t\to 1^{-}
, we have

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mu}{1-\tau^{\mu}}d\tau=\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-t}\right)+c_{0}(\mu)+c_{1}(\mu)(1-t)+c_{2}(\mu)(1-t)^{2}+O((1-t)^{3}),

where

\displaystyle c_{0}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\ln\left(\frac{1}{\mu}\right)-H\left(\frac{1}{\mu}-1\right),\ c_{1}(\mu)=\frac{1-\mu}{2},\ c_{2}(\mu)=\frac{1-\mu^{2}}{24},\ c_{3}(\mu)=\frac{1-\mu^{2}}{72},

\displaystyle c_{4}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-\mu^{2})(19-\mu^{2})}{2880},\ c_{5}(\mu)=\frac{(1-\mu^{2})(9-\mu^{2})}{2400},\ldots

Generally, we have
c_{n}(\mu)\in\mathbb{Q}[\mu]
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

To compute
c_{0}(\mu)
we make the change of variable
\tau\to\tau^{1/\mu}
on the left and then combine it with

\ln(1-t)=-\int_{0}^{t}1/(1-\tau)d\tau
. The other coefficients can be computed by repeatedly applying
L’Hôpital’s rule. For example, setting

\psi_{\mu}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\mu}{1-\tau^{\mu}}d\tau-\ln\left(\frac{1}{1-t}\right)-c_{0}(\mu),\qquad 0<t<1,

we then get

\lim_{t\to 1^{-}}\frac{\psi_{\mu}(t)}{1-t}=\frac{1-\mu}{2}

by L’Hôpital’s rule. We leave the details to the interested reader.

Lemma 4.25
.

If
0<t<1
, then we have

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\ln^{j}(m+1)t^{m}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\ln^{j}(x+1)t^{x}dx+O(1)

and

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln^{j}(m+1)}{m+1}t^{m}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln^{j}(x+1)}{x+1}t^{x}dx+O(1).

Proof.

Taking
f(x)=\ln^{j}(x+1)t^{x}
in the Abel-Plana formula (
4.6
), we have

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\ln^{j}(m+1)t^{m}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\ln^{j}(x+1)t^{x}dx+i\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln^{j}(1+ix)t^{ix}-\ln^{j}(1-ix)t^{-ix}}{e^{2\pi x}-1}dx.

For the second term above, we have

\displaystyle\left|i\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln^{j}(1+ix)t^{ix}-\ln^{j}(1-ix)t^{-ix}}{e^{2\pi x}-1}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|\ln(1+ix)|^{j}+|\ln(1-ix)|^{j}}{e^{2\pi x}-1}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\ln(1+ix)|^{j}+|\ln(1-ix)|^{j}}{e^{2\pi x}-1}dx+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{|\ln(1+ix)|^{j}+|\ln(1-ix)|^{j}}{e^{2\pi x}-1}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\ln(1+ix)|^{j}+|\ln(1-ix)|^{j}}{e^{2\pi x}-1}dx+2\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{\ln(1+x^{2})}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{j}{2}}\frac{dx}{e^{2\pi x}-1}

\displaystyle<+\infty.

Moreover, if we let
f(x)=\frac{\ln^{j}(x+1)}{x+1}t^{x}
, then we may use the same procedure as above to get

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln^{j}(m+1)}{m+1}t^{m}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln^{j}(x+1)}{x+1}t^{x}dx+O(1).

This completes the proof of the lemma.

Lemma 4.26
.

Let
j
be a positive integer. Then we have

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma^{(j)}(m+1)}{\Gamma(m+1)}t^{m}=\frac{1}{1-t}O\Big(\ln^{j}\Big(\frac{1}{1-t}\Big)\Big)\quad\text{as}\quad t\to 1^{-}.

Proof.

For all
j\geqslant 0
, we have the estimate

\frac{\Gamma^{(j)}(m+1)}{\Gamma(m+1)}=\ln^{j}(m+1)+O\left(\frac{\ln^{j-1}(m+1)}{m+1}\right)\quad\text{as}\quad m\to\infty.

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma^{(j)}(m+1)}{\Gamma(m+1)}t^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\ln^{j}(m+1)t^{m}+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}O\left(\frac{\ln^{j-1}(m+1)}{m+1}\right)t^{m}.

By Lemma
4.20
we have

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}O\left(\frac{\ln^{j-1}(m+1)}{m+1}\right)t^{m}=O\Big(\ln^{j}\Big(\frac{1}{1-t}\Big)\Big)\quad\text{as}\quad t\to 1^{-}.

By Lemma
4.25
we get

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\ln^{j}(m+1)t^{m}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\ln^{j}(x+1)t^{x}dx+O(1)\quad\text{as}\quad t\to 1^{-}.

Finally,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\ln^{j}(x+1)t^{x}dx

\displaystyle=\ln^{j}(x+1)\frac{t^{x}}{\ln(t)}\bigg|_{0}^{\infty}-\frac{1}{\ln(t)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln^{j-1}(x+1)}{x+1}t^{x}dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\ln(t)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln^{j-1}(x+1)}{x+1}t^{x}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln^{j-1}(x+1)}{x+1}t^{x}dx

\displaystyle(\text{by\ lemme\ \ref{oonb}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-t}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\ln^{j-1}(m+1)}{m+1}t^{m}+O(1)\right)

\displaystyle(\text{by\ lemme\ \ref{oijx}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-t}O\Big(\ln^{j}\Big(\frac{1}{1-t}\Big)\Big)

as desired.

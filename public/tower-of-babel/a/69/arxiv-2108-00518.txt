Equivariant Burnside groups and representation theory
Andrew Kresch, Yuri Tschinkel

Abstract
. We apply the equivariant Burnside group formalism to distinguish linear actions of finite groups, up to equivariant birationality. Our approach is based on De Concini-Procesi models of subspace arrangements.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group, and
k
a field of characteristic zero containing all
roots of unity of order dividing
|G|
.
In this paper, we continue the study of the
equivariant Burnside group

\Burn_{n}(G),

introduced in
[
16
]
, building on
[
15
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
17
]
, and

[
13
]
. This group receives
G
-equivariant birational equivalence classes

[X\righttoleftarrow G]

of smooth projective varieties
X
with generically free
G
-action.

Prior to the introduction of this invariant, the main tools to distinguish
G
-actions, up to equivariant birationality, were

•

existence of fixed points upon restriction to abelian subgroups,

•

group cohomology
{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(G^{\prime},\Pic(X))
, for subgroups
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
,

•

the equivariant Minimal Model Program (MMP)
and equivariant birational rigidity.

The new invariant allows to
distinguish actions for which the classical approaches fail.
In
[
13
]
, we presented several geometric applications, distinguishing nonlinear actions from linear actions, e.g., we produced a rational cubic fourfold with
G={\mathbb{Z}}/6
-action, not
G
-birational to a linear action on
{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
.

Motivated by these developments, in
[
18
]
we considered various functorial properties of
\Burn_{n}(G)
,
such as restriction, fibrations, and products.
In this paper, we develop techniques that allow to distinguish linear
G
-actions on projective space
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
, modulo conjugation in the Cremona group, i.e., modulo
G
-equivariant birationality.
The corresponding classification is an open problem already for
n=2
(see
[
8
]
).

Linear
G
-actions on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
arise in different ways.
We may consider:

•

the natural compactification a linear action of
G
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
,

•

the action induced by a linear action of
G
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n+1}
,

•

a
projective linear representation of
G
.

Standard examples are actions on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
of a cyclic group, of the symmetric group
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
, and of the Klein
4
-group
{\mathfrak{K}}_{4}={\mathbb{Z}}/2\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}/2
, respectively.

One of our main results is an explicit combinatorial algorithm to compute the class

[{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\righttoleftarrow G]\in\Burn_{n}(G)

(1.1)

for each of the cases above (Theorem
8.5
).
Our principal tool is the formalism of De Concini-Procesi
compactifications of subspace arrangements, adopted to the equivariant context.
The De Concini-Procesi model is well-suited
for recursive computations, allowing us to extract the class (
1.1
) from classes of strata together with the corresponding normal bundle data.

We then apply this formalism in
representative examples. In particular, we are able to address the long-standing problem of birational classification of
G
-actions on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.
We also present new classes of examples of nonbirational linear actions on
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
.

In Section
2
we give a new definition of
\Burn_{n}(G)
, rewriting generators and relations in terms of centralizers rather than normalizers as in
[
16
]
; this equivalent presentation is better suited for applications.
In Section
3
we recall some properties of
\Burn_{n}(G)
and introduce a new operation, induction, that plays a dual role to the restriction operation of
[
18
]
. We discuss simplifications of the equivariant Burnside group based on the combinatorics of subgroups and properties of
G
-actions in smaller dimensions.
In Section
4
we consider
smooth projective
X
with a simple normal crossing divisor
D=\bigcup_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}D_{i}
and explain how to compute the class
[X\righttoleftarrow G]
in terms of
classes of strata
D_{I}:=\bigcap_{i\in I}D_{i}
for
I\subseteq{\mathcal{I}}
and the corresponding normal bundle data.
In Section
5
we provide the framework for this computation, when the normal bundles are replaced by an arbitrary sequence of line bundles.
In Section
6
we study De Concini-Procesi models for linear representations; these depend on the lattice of stabilizer subgroups of
G
.
In Section
7
we develop the analogous theory for projective linear actions and consider the corresponding De Concini-Procesi models.
In Section
8
we compute the classes of linear and projective linear actions in the corresponding Burnside groups.
In Section
9
we specialize to permutation actions, illustrating the
general theory in examples. In Sections
10
and
11
we
consider actions in dimension 2 and 3.

2.
Generalities

In this paper, we work with smooth quasi-projective
G
-varieties
X
over
k
.
By this, we mean smooth quasi-projective schemes
X
with an action of
G
that is transitive on the set of irreducible components.
The last requirement is made, in order to be able to speak of the generic behavior of a
G
-variety.
For instance,
G
acts
generically freely
on
X
if
G
acts freely on some nonempty, hence dense, invariant open subvariety.

In
[
16
]
and
[
18
]
we introduced and studied
the
equivariant Burnside group

\Burn_{n}(G)=\Burn_{n,k}(G).

The group-theoretic framework of the formalism in
[
16
]
was based on (conjugacy classes of)
abelian
subgroups
H\subseteq G
and their
normalizers

N_{G}(H)
; here we present an equivalent version based on
centralizers

Z_{G}(H)
instead. The advantage of the centralizer language is that the equivariant Burnside group itself is easier to describe; this comes at the cost of additional complexity in the expression for the class
[X\righttoleftarrow G]
of a smooth projective
G
-variety
X
with generically free action (Definition
2.3
, below).

The basic ingredients of the construction are:

•

abelian subgroups
H\subseteq G
, the quotients
Z:=Z_{G}(H)/H
, and the character groups

H^{\vee}:=\Hom(H,k^{\times}),

•

the sets
\Bir_{d}(k)
of isomorphism classes of function fields of algebraic varieties of dimension
d
over
k
; we identify a field with its isomorphism class,

•

the sets
\Alg_{Z}(K_{0})
of isomorphism classes of
Z
-Galois algebras
K/K_{0}
, with
K_{0}\in\Bir_{d}(k)
,
satisfying

Assumption 1
: The homomorphism

{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(Z_{G}(H),K^{\times})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(H,K^{\times})^{Z}\cong H^{\vee}

(2.1)

is surjective.

The
equivariant Burnside group

\Burn_{n}(G)=\Burn_{n,k}(G)

is the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module, generated by symbols

\mathfrak{s}:=(H,Z\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta),

where
H
and
Z
are as above and:

•

K\in\Alg_{Z}(K_{0})
, with
K_{0}\in\Bir_{d}(k)
, and
d\leq n
,

•

\beta=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n-d})
, a sequence of nonzero elements generating
H^{\vee}
.

We call
d
the dimension of
\mathfrak{s}
.
We permit ourselves to write a symbol in the form

(H,Y\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta)

with a subgroup
Y\subset Z
and
K\in\Alg_{Y}(K_{0})
, the set of isomorphism classes of

Y
-Galois algebras over
K_{0}
satisfying
the analogous condition to Assumption 1:

(H,Y\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta):=(H,Z\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}\Ind_{Y}^{Z}(K),\beta).

(2.2)

These generators are subject to relations:

(O):

(H,Z\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta)=(H,Z\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta^{\prime})
if

\beta^{\prime}
is a reordering of
\beta
.

(C):

(H,Z\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta)=(H^{\prime},Z^{\prime}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta^{\prime})
,
when
H^{\prime}=gHg^{-1}
and
Z^{\prime}=Z_{G}(H^{\prime})/H^{\prime}
, with
g\in G
, and
\beta
and
\beta^{\prime}
are related by conjugation by
g
.

(B1):

(H,Z\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta)=0
when
b_{1}+b_{2}=0
.

(B2):

(H,Z\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta)=\Theta_{1}+\Theta_{2}
,
where

\Theta_{1}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $b_{1}=b_{2}$},\\
(H,Z\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta_{1})+(H,Z\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta_{2}),&\text{if $b_{1}\neq b_{2}$},\end{cases}

with

\beta_{1}:=(b_{1},b_{2}-b_{1},b_{3},\ldots,b_{n-d}),\quad\beta_{2}:=(b_{2},b_{1}-b_{2},b_{3},\ldots,b_{n-d}),

and

\Theta_{2}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $b_{i}\in\langle b\rangle$ for some $i$, $b:=b_{1}-b_{2}$},\\
(\overline{H},\overline{Z}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}\overline{K},\bar{\beta}),&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

with

\displaystyle\overline{H}:=\Ker(b),\quad\overline{Z}:=Z_{G}(H)/\overline{H},\quad\overline{K}:=K(t),

\displaystyle\bar{\beta}:=(\bar{b}_{2},\bar{b}_{3},\dots,\bar{b}_{n-d}),\quad\bar{b}_{i}\in\overline{H}^{\vee},

and
\overline{K}
carries the action described in Construction
(A)

in
[
16
, Section 2]
, applied to the character
b=b_{1}-b_{2}\in H^{\vee}
:
the
Z_{G}(H)
-action on
K(t)
arises by lifting
b

via (
2.1
) and is trivial on
\overline{H}
.

Remark 2.1
.

We see that the above description reproduces the equivariant Burnside group defined in
[
16
]
by appealing to
[
18
, Lemma 2.1]
, which is the observation that a symbol involving an algebra in
\Alg_{N}(K_{0})
,
N:=N_{G}(H)/H
, is equivalent, in a sense analogous to (
2.2
), to one with an algebra in
\Alg_{Z}(K_{0})
.

Assumption 2
: All stabilizers
for the
G
-action on
X
are abelian,
and for every abelian subgroup
H
and
component of
X^{H}
with generic stabilizer
H
, the

Z_{G}(H)
-orbit
F
has the property that
the composite homomorphism

\Pic^{G}(X)\to{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(Z_{G}(H),k(F)^{\times})\to H^{\vee}

is surjective.
Here, the first map is given by restriction, and the second is the
map from Assumption 1 with
K=k(F)
.

Note that Assumption 2 implies, for every

H
and
F
, that
Z_{G}(H)
is the maximal subgroup of
G

for which
F
is invariant, and

Z\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}k(F)
satisfies
Assumption 1
(see
[
16
, Remark 3.2(i)]
and
[
18
, Lemma 2.1]
).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
H
be an abelian subgroup of
G
, and
\Gamma
an abelian group. A
\Gamma
-pairing on
H
is a bilinear map

\Gamma\times H\to k^{\times},

i.e., a homomorphism

\Gamma\to H^{\vee}.

The group
G
acts naturally by conjugation on abelian subgroups with
\Gamma
-pairing.

Reformulating
[
16
, Definition 4.4]
, we express
the class

[X\righttoleftarrow G]\in\Burn_{n}(G)

in the language of centralizers, as follows:

Definition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective
G
-variety with a generically free
G
-action satisfying Assumption 2.
Put

[X\righttoleftarrow G]:=\!\!\sum_{\text{$H$, $\Pic^{G}(X)$-pairing}}\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{$F\subset X$ with generic}\\
\text{stabilizer $H$ and $\Pic^{G}(X)$-pairing}\end{subarray}}\!\!(H,Z\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}k(F),\beta_{F}(X)),

where

•

the outer sum is over conjugacy class representatives of abelian subgroups with
\Pic^{G}(X)
-pairing;

•

for each conjugacy class representative

\Pic^{G}(X)\to H^{\vee}

(2.3)

we consider
the components of the fixed locus
X^{H}
that have generic stabilizer
H
and, by the map in Assumption 2,
\Pic^{G}(X)
-pairing (
2.3
);

•

the inner sum is over
Z_{G}(H)
-orbits of components
F\subset X
;

•

the generic normal bundle representation along
F
is recorded as
\beta_{F}(X)
.

We recall the key fact
[
16
, Theorem 5.1]
:

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety with a generically free
G
-action.
The class

[X\righttoleftarrow G]\in\Burn_{n}(G)

is a
G
-equivariant birational invariant.

3.
Properties of equivariant Burnside groups

In this section, we record several basic constructions concerning equivariant Burnside groups. Let
G
be a finite group and
G^{\prime}\subset G
a subgroup.

Burnside groups of stacks

In
[
17
]
we defined

\overline{\Burn}_{n},

the Burnside group of orbifolds, receiving
birational equivalence classes of algebraic orbifolds; we write

[\mathcal{X}]\in\overline{\Burn}_{n}

for the class of the quotient stack

\mathcal{X}:=[X/G]
.
In
[
16
, Section 7]
we
defined a homomorphism

\kappa^{G}:\Burn_{n}(G)\to\overline{\Burn}_{n},

(3.1)

satisfying

\kappa^{G}([X\righttoleftarrow G])=[\mathcal{X}]\in\overline{\Burn}_{n},

see
[
16
, Proposition 7.9]
.

Restriction

A
G
-action on a quasi-projective variety
X
induces an action of
G^{\prime}
on
X
. In
[
18
, Theorem 7.2]
we defined a homomorphism

\mathrm{res}_{G^{\prime}}^{G}:\Burn_{n}(G)\to\Burn_{n}(G^{\prime}),

via an explicit formula on generators

(H,Z\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta).

With this definition, we have

\mathrm{res}_{G^{\prime}}^{G}([X\righttoleftarrow G])=[X\righttoleftarrow G^{\prime}]\in\Burn_{n}(G^{\prime}).

Induction

A
G^{\prime}
-action on a quasi-projective variety
X
induces an action of
G
on
X\times^{G^{\prime}}G
, the quotient of
X\times G
by a diagonal action of
G^{\prime}
.
Then we obtain

[X\righttoleftarrow G^{\prime}]\mapsto[X\times^{G^{\prime}}G\righttoleftarrow G]

(3.2)

from a natural homomorphism of equivariant Burnside groups:

Definition 3.1
.

The
induction homomorphism

\mathrm{ind}_{G^{\prime}}^{G}\colon\Burn_{n}(G^{\prime})\to\Burn_{n}(G)

is given by

(H^{\prime},Z^{\prime}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta)\mapsto(H^{\prime},Z^{\prime}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta).

Here,
Z^{\prime}
denotes
Z_{G^{\prime}}(H^{\prime})/H^{\prime}
, and on the right we follow the notational convention (
2.2
), for the subgroup
Z

of
Z_{G}(H^{\prime})/H^{\prime}
:

(H^{\prime},Z^{\prime}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta)=(H^{\prime},Z_{G}(H^{\prime})/H^{\prime}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}\Ind_{Z^{\prime}}^{Z_{G}(H^{\prime})/H^{\prime}}(K),\beta).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
, and
let
X
be a quasi-projective variety with generically free
G^{\prime}
-action.
Then
\mathrm{ind}_{G^{\prime}}^{G}
obeys (
3.2
), and additionally,

\kappa^{G}\circ\mathrm{ind}_{G^{\prime}}^{G}=\kappa^{G^{\prime}},

where

\kappa^{G}
is the homomorphism (
3.1
) to
\overline{\Burn}_{n}
.

Proof.

The first assertion is clear.
For the second, we combine

[
16
, Prop. 7.9]
with the observation, stated as

[
16
, Rmk. 5.16]
, that the classes
[X\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}G^{\prime}]
generate
\Burn_{n}(G^{\prime})
, and the fact that the quotient stacks, associated with the left- and right-hand sides of (
3.2
), are isomorphic.
∎

Products

There is a product map

\Burn_{n^{\prime}}(G^{\prime})\times\Burn_{n^{\prime\prime}}(G^{\prime\prime})\to\Burn_{n^{\prime}+n^{\prime\prime}}(G^{\prime}\times G^{\prime\prime}),

which satisfies

([X^{\prime}\righttoleftarrow G^{\prime}],[X^{\prime\prime}\righttoleftarrow G^{\prime\prime}])\mapsto[X^{\prime}\times X^{\prime\prime}\righttoleftarrow G^{\prime}\times G^{\prime\prime}],

see
[
18
, Section 6]
.

Filtrations

The equivariant Burnside group admits various projections

\Burn_{n}(G)\to\Burn_{n}^{\mathbf{H}}(G).

(3.3)

These are based on the combinatorics of
G
and its subgroups.
Let

\mathbf{H}:=\{(H,Y)\},

be a set of pairs, with
H\subseteq G
abelian and
Y\subseteq Z_{G}(H)/H
, such that

(gHg^{-1},gYg^{-1})\in\mathbf{H},\quad\text{ for all }(H,Y)\in\mathbf{H},g\in G.

The projection (
3.3
) is obtained by annihilating all symbols

(H,Y\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta),\quad\text{ with }K\text{ a field and }(H,Y)\notin\mathbf{H}.

Upon such projections one can reduce the set of generators and relations, provided
\mathbf{H}
is such
that for all
(H,Y)\in\mathbf{H}
and
all
g\in Z_{G}(H)
with
\bar{g}\in Y

we have

(\langle H,g\rangle,Y/\langle\bar{g}\rangle)\in\mathbf{H}.

Then
\Burn_{n}^{\mathbf{H}}(G)
is generated by symbols

(H,Y\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta),\quad\text{ with }(H,Y)\in\mathbf{H},

(with
K
a field), and relations applied
only
to these triples
[
18
, Section 3]
.
Here are some basic examples of such
\mathbf{H}
:

•

With
\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{triv}}:=\{(\mathrm{triv},Y)\,|\,Y\subseteq G\}
, the projection to
\Burn^{\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{triv}}}_{n}(G)
extracts just the
n
-dimensional symbols.

•

When
G
is abelian, we can take
\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{max}}:=\{(G,\mathrm{triv})\}
, and

\Burn_{n}^{\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{max}}}(G)=\Burn^{G}_{n}(G),

the group introduced in
[
16
, Section 8]
.
For general
G

we take
\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{max}}
to consist of all
(H,\mathrm{triv})
with
H
a maximal abelian subgroup of
G
.

Incompressibles

Other simplifications arise when we focus on geometric properties of the the function fields of strata, i.e., the middle terms in the symbols.
The examination of defining relations of
\Burn_{n}(G)
reveals that it contains a distinguished subgroup

\Burn_{n}^{\mathrm{inc}}(G)\subset\Burn_{n}(G),

generated by

incompressible
divisor symbols, where by a divisor symbol we mean a symbol of dimension
d=n-1
, i.e.,

\mathfrak{s}=(H,Z\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta),\quad K\in\Alg_{Z}(K_{0}),\quad K_{0}\in\Bir_{n-1}(k),

therefore
H
a nontrivial cyclic group and
\beta=(b)
,
a single character, generating
H^{\vee}
.

Definition 3.3
.

A divisor symbol
\mathfrak{s}
is called
incompressible
if
it cannot arise from
the term

\Theta_{2}
in relation
\mathbf{(B2)}
.
Otherwise,
\mathfrak{s}
is called
compressible
.

The following is immediate:

Proposition 3.4
.

The subgroup

\Burn_{n}^{\mathrm{inc}}(G)\subseteq\Burn_{n}(G),

is a direct summand, freely generated by incompressible divisor symbols, modulo Conjugation relation
\mathbf{(C)}
.

Proof.

Indeed, these symbols are not involved in any relations
(O)
,
(B1)
, or
(B2)
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

When
n=1
, every divisor symbol in incompressible, hence

\Burn_{1}(G)=\Burn_{1}^{\mathrm{inc}}(G)\oplus\Burn_{1}^{\mathbf{H}_{\mathrm{triv}}}(G).

Let
n\geq 2
and let

\mathfrak{s}:=(H,Z\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta)

be an incompressible divisor symbol. Geometrically, this symbol stands for the contribution of (an orbit of) an irreducible divisor
F
, which does not arise as the exceptional divisor
of an equivariant blow-up, i.e., via projectivization of the normal bundle to a codimension
\geq 2
stratum
with nontrivial stabilizer.
This is a stronger requirement, than just the failure to be
Z
-equivariantly birational to
F^{\prime}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
for some
F^{\prime}
of codimension
2
, where the action of
Z
on the second factor
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
is trivial.

A version of incompressibility appeared in the definition of

{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(G,k)
in
[
14
, Remark 5]
, see also
[
13
, Section 5.1]
, in the context of abelian groups and maximal stabilizers.
In practice, we will be able to check this condition in small dimensions, where we have explicit control over possible groups and over equivariant birationality.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
n=2
, let
G
be a finite group, and suppose that the base field
k
is algebraically closed.
Then a divisor symbol
(H,Y\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta)
,
\beta=(b)
,
K
a field,
is compressible if and only if
Y
is cyclic and
K
is a rational function field.

Proof.

Since
k
is algebraically closed, a symbol of dimension
0
must arise as in (
2.2
) from the trivial group acting on
k
, and Construction
(A)
yields term
\Theta_{2}
, when nontrivial, with a cyclic group acting on
k(t)
.
Conversely, suppose we have a divisor symbol with
Y
cyclic, and let us write
Y
as
H_{1}/H
.
The condition
H_{1}\subset Z_{G}(H)

(cf. Remark
2.1
) implies that
H_{1}
is abelian.
Since
k
is algebraically closed, there must be an isomorphism
K\cong k(t)
such that
Y
acts is by roots of unity on
t
.
Compressibility is evident, via a symbol
(H_{1},\mathrm{triv}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}k,(a,a^{\prime}))
with
a
a lift of
b
to
H_{1}^{\vee}
and
a^{\prime}-a
a generator of
(H_{1}/H)^{\vee}
.
∎

4.
Stratifications

In this section,
X
is a smooth projective variety with generically free
G
-action, and

D=\bigcup_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}D_{i}\subset X,\quad{\mathcal{I}}=\{1,\dots,\ell\},

is a simple normal crossing divisor, where each
D_{i}
is invariant under
G
. We assume that

X
satisfies Assumption 2.

Our goal in this section is to compute the class

[X\righttoleftarrow G]\in\Burn_{n}(G)
in terms of
D_{i}
, their intersections, and the corresponding normal bundle data.

We start by rephrasing the definition of the equivariant indexed Burnside group

\Burn_{n,I}(G),

in the setting of centralizers, rather than normalizers as in
[
18
, Section 4]
.
Here, and throughout,

I\subset{\mathbb{N}}

is a finite index set.
The indexed equivariant Burnside group is generated by symbols

(H\subseteq H^{\prime},Z^{\prime}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}K,\beta,\gamma)

where

•

H\subseteq H^{\prime}
are abelian subgroups of
G
,

•

Z^{\prime}:=Z_{G}(H^{\prime})/H^{\prime}
,

•

K\in\Alg_{Z^{\prime}}(K_{0})
,
K_{0}\in\Bir_{d}(k)
,
d\leq n-|I|
,

•

\beta=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n-d-|I|})
, a sequence of nonzero characters of
H^{\prime}
restricting trivially to
H
and generating
(H^{\prime}/H)^{\vee}
,

•

\gamma=(c_{i})_{i\in I}
, a sequence of characters of
H^{\prime}
whose restrictions generate
H^{\vee}
.

The relations are:

(O):
reordering of
\beta
,

(C):
conjugation,

(B1):
vanishing, when
b_{1}+b_{2}=0
,

(B2):
blowup relation, similar to that in Section
2
.

See
[
18
, Section 4]
for precise definitions.

Example 4.1
.

We may view
\gamma
as a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{I}
-pairing of
H^{\prime}
(Definition
2.2
),
where

{\mathbb{Z}}^{I}:=\bigoplus_{i\in I}{\mathbb{Z}}.

An automorphism

6.
De Concini-Procesi models for linear actions

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional vector space over
k
.
In this section, we lay the groundwork for computing the class

[V\righttoleftarrow G]^{\mathrm{naive}}\in\Burn_{n}(G),

of a given linear action of
G
on
V
,
using as our main tool the
wonderful compactifications
of De Concini and Procesi
[
7
]
.

Here,
V
has the right action of
G
determined by a faithful linear representation

G\to{\mathrm{GL}}(V^{\vee}).

We follow the
presentation in
[
11
]
.

The space
V
contains an arrangement of linear subspaces,
called the
stabilizer stratification
in
[
1
]
,
indexed by the stabilizer groups
\Gamma\subseteq G
of vectors in
V
.
We denote by
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(V)
the set of these stabilizer groups, partially ordered by inclusion.
Associated with
\Gamma\in\mathcal{L}
is the linear subspace

V_{\Gamma}:=\{v\in V\,|\,v\cdot g=v\text{ for all $g\in\Gamma$}\}.

Remark 6.1
.

The poset
{\mathcal{L}}
is not, generally, a sublattice of the lattice of all subgroups of
G
.
For instance, for the tensor product of the standard and sign representations of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{4}
we have

\langle(1,2)\rangle
,
\langle(1,2,3)\rangle\in{\mathcal{L}}
but

{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}\notin{\mathcal{L}}
.
Generally, however,
{\mathcal{L}}
is a lattice, with

\Gamma\wedge\Gamma^{\prime}=\Gamma\cap\Gamma^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{L}}
for

\Gamma
,
\Gamma^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{L}}
, but
more intricate
join operation:
\Gamma\vee\Gamma^{\prime}
is characterized as the generic stabilizer of

V_{\Gamma}\cap V_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
.

The De Concini-Procesi model

V_{\mathcal{L}}
is the closure of the image of

V^{\circ}\to V\times\prod_{\{1\}\neq\Gamma\in\mathcal{L}}{\mathbb{P}}(V/V_{\Gamma}),

where

V^{\circ}:=V\setminus\bigcup_{\{1\}\neq\Gamma\in\mathcal{L}}V_{\Gamma}.

There is a natural projection

V_{\mathcal{L}}\to V

which is an isomorphism on
V^{\circ}
.
The complement in
V_{\mathcal{L}}
is
a normal crossing divisor

10.
Projective linear actions on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

In this section we address an open problem, stated in
[
8
, Section 9]
:

Problem 10.1
.

Find the conjugacy classes in the Cremona group
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
of subgroups of
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{3}
.

In fact, the authors wrote:

We do not know whether any
two isomorphic non-conjugate subgroups of
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{3}

are conjugate in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}
.

Here, we identify all finite groups
G
that admit
embeddings into
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{3}
giving nonbirational actions on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, up to a small list of potential exceptions.

We follow
[
8
, Section 4.2]
to organize the classification.
Recall the basic terminology: a (faithful) representation
G\to{\mathrm{GL}}(V^{\vee})
is called:

•

intransitive
: if it is reducible,
transitive
if it is irreducible;

•

imprimitive
if it is transitive but contains an intransitive
normal
subgroup
G^{\prime}
; in this case
G/G^{\prime}
permutes the
G^{\prime}
representations;

•

primitive
if it is neither intransitive, nor imprimitive.

The same terminology is used for subgroups in
{\mathrm{PGL}}(V^{\vee})
, if
one of their liftings is as above.
The classification of finite subgroups

G\subset{\mathrm{PGL}}_{3}
takes the form:

(1)

If
G
is intransitive then

G=C_{n}\times G^{\prime}\subset k^{\times}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(k),\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}},

and
G^{\prime}
is
a finite subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
, a binary extensions of a subgroup
\bar{G}^{\prime}\subset{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
, which in turn is:

•

cyclic, dihedral,
{\mathfrak{A}}_{4}
,
{\mathfrak{S}}_{4}
, or
{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
.

The classification of all possibilities for
G^{\prime}
is in
[
8
, Corollary 4.6]
.

(2)

If
G
is transitive but imprimitive then
it is one of the four types
(see
[
8
, Theorem 4.7]
):

(2.1)

extension of
C_{3}
by
({\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}})^{2}
, with the action

(\zeta_{n}x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}),\quad(x_{0},\zeta_{n}x_{1},x_{2}),\quad(x_{2},x_{0},x_{1}),

(2.2)

extension of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
by
({\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}})^{2}
,
here the
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
acts permutation of the coordinates
(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})
, and the abelian subgroup acts as above,

(2.3)

extension of
C_{3}
by
({\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}})\oplus({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})
,
m=n/r
, with
r>1
,
r\mid n
,

s^{2}-s+1=0\pmod{r}
, and
with the action

(\zeta_{m}x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}),\quad(\zeta_{n}^{s}x_{0},\zeta_{n}x_{1},x_{2}),\quad(x_{2},x_{0},x_{1}),

(2.4)

extension of
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
by
({\mathbb{Z}}/n{\mathbb{Z}})\oplus({\mathbb{Z}}/m{\mathbb{Z}})
,
m=n/3
,
3\mid n
,
here the
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
acts permuation of the coordinates
(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})
, and the abelian subgroup by

(\zeta_{m}x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}),\quad(\zeta_{n}^{2},\zeta_{n}x_{1},x_{2}).

In each of these actions, there are
3
distinguished points on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, fixed by the abelian subgroup, and permuted by
C_{3}
, respectively
{\mathfrak{S}}_{3}
. This means that
G
can be realized on a del Pezzo surface of degree
6
, with the abelian subgroup acting freely on the torus.

(3)

If
G
is primitive then it is one of the groups:

•

{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
,
{\mathfrak{A}}_{6}
,
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{7})
, the
Hessian group
3^{2}:\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
,
and two of its subgroups.

As mentioned in the Introduction, the standard approaches to distinguishing
G
-actions, up to birationality, rely on group cohomology
{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(G,\Pic(X))
, or birational rigidity.
The first is not applicable
since
\Pic({\mathbb{P}}^{n})={\mathbb{Z}}
, which implies vanishing of cohomology.

Primitive actions

These actions are now understood, via birational rigidity techniques:

Theorem 10.2
.

[
20
]

Let
G\subset{\mathrm{PGL}}_{3}
be a finite group. Then
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
is
G
-birationally rigid if and only if
G
is transitive and not isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{A}}_{4}
or
{\mathfrak{S}}_{4}
.

Recall that
G
-birational rigidity in our context means that there are no
G
-equivariant birational maps
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}\dashrightarrow X
, where

X
is a
G
-Mori fiber space (i.e., conic bundle or a del Pezzo surface) not isomorphic to
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. Birational super-ridigity means that every equivariantly birational model has the
same
action on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, up to automorphisms.

In fact,
[
20
]
identifies rigid and super-rigid actions.
For example, there are
two
actions of
{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, coming from the two nonisomorphic 3-dimensional represesentations of
{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
; they are not conjugated in
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{3}
but are conjugated in the Cremona group
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}

[
4
, Remark 6.3.9]
.
On the other hand,
{\mathfrak{A}}_{6}
and
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{7})
also admit two actions on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, but these are not conjugated in
\mathrm{Cr}_{2}

[
2
, Theorems B.8 and A.20]
.

Transitive imprimitive actions

In this case,
G
contains an abelian subgroup
H
of rank
2
.
The classification of
G
-actions can be
approached via the Reichstein-Youssin invariant
[
19
]
: the equivariant birational type upon restriction to
H
is governed by the
determinant, up to
\pm 1
.
This gives examples of nonbirational actions of
G
.
The argument is similar to
[
19
, Theorem 7.2]
.

Intransitive actions

From the perspective of birational rigidity,
this class is more difficult to analyze:
the existence of
G
-fixed points allows for different
G
-birational models. However, it is well-suited for applications of the Burnside group formalism.

We can assume that

G=C_{n}\times G^{\prime},\quad n\geq 2,

where
G^{\prime}\subset{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
is a lift of
\bar{G}^{\prime}\subset{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
.

•

G^{\prime}=C_{m}
: Actions of cyclic groups on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
lift to
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
. Thus we have an action of
G:=C_{n}\times C_{m}
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. By
[
19
]
, such actions are birational if and only if
the determinants differ by
\pm 1
.

•

\bar{G}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{D}_{m}
,
{\mathfrak{A}}_{4}
,
{\mathfrak{S}}_{4}
, or
{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
.
Let
n
be such that
\varphi(n)\geq 3
.
Then
G
admits nonbirational
actions on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.

Indeed, let
\epsilon
be a primitive character of
C_{n}
.
Let
V
be a faithful 2-dimensional representation of
G^{\prime}

lifting
\bar{G}^{\prime}\subset{\mathrm{PGL}}_{2}
, and
V_{\epsilon}:=V\otimes\epsilon
.
This gives generically a free action of
G
on

{\mathbb{P}}(1\oplus V_{\epsilon}).

To compute the class of this action in
\Burn_{2}(G)
, we may apply Theorem
8.5
. We may also turn to geometry and observe that there are two distinguished loci in
{\mathbb{P}}(1\oplus V_{\epsilon})
: the fixed point, and the line
{\mathbb{P}}(V)
. To obtain a standard model, we need to blow up the fixed point. On the blow-up, there will be two divisors with stabilizer
C_{n}
, the corresponding contribution to

[{\mathbb{P}}(1\oplus V_{\epsilon})\righttoleftarrow G]

is given by

(C_{n},\bar{G}^{\prime}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}k({\mathbb{P}}(V)),\epsilon)+(C_{n},\bar{G}^{\prime}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}k({\mathbb{P}}(V)),-\epsilon).

These symbols are incompressible, by Proposition
3.6
.

Let
\epsilon^{\prime}
be another primitive character, and
V_{\epsilon^{\prime}}:=V\otimes\epsilon^{\prime}
the corresponding twist. Applying Proposition
3.4
, we obtain

[{\mathbb{P}}(1\oplus V_{\epsilon})\righttoleftarrow G]\neq[{\mathbb{P}}(1\oplus V_{\epsilon^{\prime}})\righttoleftarrow G]\in\Burn_{2}(G),

provided
\epsilon\neq\pm\epsilon^{\prime}
.

This construction generalizes
[
18
, Example 5.3]
.

11.
Projective linear actions on
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}

As in the case of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, primitive actions on
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
are
amenable to birational rigidity techniques.
An analog of Theorem
10.2
in dimension 3 is:

Theorem 11.1
.

[
5
]

Let
G\subset{\mathrm{PGL}}_{4}
be a finite subgroup. Then
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
is
G
-birationally rigid if and only if
G
is primitive and not isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
or
{\mathfrak{S}}_{5}
.

The classification goes further, by identifying
G
-super-rigid actions, e.g.,
two embeddings of
G=\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{7})
into
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{4}
are conjugated in
\mathrm{Cr}_{3}
if and only if they are conjugated in
{\mathrm{PGL}}_{4}

[
3
, Corollary 1.11]
; this yields
examples of nonbirational actions by
considering

{\mathbb{P}}(1\oplus V_{3})
, where
V_{3}
is a 3-dimensional representation of
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{7})
, and

{\mathbb{P}}(V_{4})
, where
V_{4}
is
a 4-dimensional representation of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{7})
.

Much harder to handle, from the perspective of rigidity, are intransitive actions, in particular, those with a
G
-fixed point.

Theorem 11.2
.

Consider

G=C_{n}\times G^{\prime},

where
G^{\prime}\subset{\mathrm{GL}}_{3}
is a lift of

\bar{G}^{\prime}\subset{\mathrm{PGL}}_{3}
.
Let
\bar{G}^{\prime}
be one of the following groups

{\mathfrak{S}}_{4},{\mathfrak{A}}_{5},\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{7}),{\mathfrak{A}}_{6}\subset{\mathrm{PGL}}_{3}.

Assume that
\varphi(n)\geq 3
.
Then
G
admits nonbirational actions on

{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
.

Proof.

Let
V
be a faithful 3-dimensional representation of
G^{\prime}
.
We consider generically free actions on
{\mathbb{P}}(1\oplus V_{\epsilon})
,
where
V_{\epsilon}:=V\otimes\epsilon
, and
\epsilon

is a nontrivial character of
C_{n}
.
Applying Theorem
8.5
, and
the formalism of Section
3
(or
[
18
, Section 5]
), we
extract symbols with
C_{n}
-stabilizers which appear in the computation of the class

[{\mathbb{P}}(1\oplus V_{\epsilon})\righttoleftarrow G]\in\Burn_{3}(G),

namely,

(C_{n},\bar{G}^{\prime}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}k({\mathbb{P}}(V)),\epsilon)+(C_{n},\bar{G}^{\prime}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}k({\mathbb{P}}(V)),-\epsilon).

(11.1)

The main point is that
these symbols are incompressible (see Section
3
).
Indeed, the
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}({\mathbb{F}}_{7})
and
{\mathfrak{A}}_{6}
-actions on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
cannot be obtained by blowing up an isolated point on a threefold (it has an abelian stabilizer), or a line
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, since these groups do not act (generically freely) on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.
The
{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
, respectively
{\mathfrak{S}}_{4}
-action, on

{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, with trivial action
on the second factor, is not equivariantly birational to the linear action of the corresponding group on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. Indeed,
the restriction of the action to a Klein subgroup
\mathfrak{K}_{4}\subset G
, for
G={\mathfrak{S}}_{4}
or
{\mathfrak{A}}_{5}
, has no fixed points on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, but does have fixed points on
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.

By Proposition
3.4
, the symbols

(C_{n},\bar{G}^{\prime}\mathrel{\reflectbox{$\righttoleftarrow$}}k({\mathbb{P}}(V)),\epsilon)

are linearly independent in
\Burn_{3}^{\mathrm{inc}}(G)
, a direct summand of
\Burn_{3}(G)
.
Therefore,

[{\mathbb{P}}(1\oplus V_{\epsilon})\righttoleftarrow G]\neq[{\mathbb{P}}(1\oplus V_{\epsilon^{\prime}})\righttoleftarrow G]\in\Burn_{3}(G),

provided

\epsilon\neq\pm\epsilon^{\prime},

in which case the actions are not equivariantly birational.
∎

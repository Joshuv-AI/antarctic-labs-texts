A Minkowski inequality for Horowitz-Myers geon Thanks: A. Alaee acknowledges the support of the AMS-Simons travel grant.
Aghil Alaee, Pei-Ken Hung

Abstract
. We prove a sharp inequality for toroidal hypersurfaces in three and four dimensional Horowitz-Myers geon. This extend previous results on Minkowski inequality in the static spacetime to toroidal surfaces in asymptotically hyperbolic manifold with flat toroidal conformal infinity.



1.
Introduction

The classical Minkowski inequality for a closed convex hypersurface
\Sigma
with induced metric
\gamma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
reads

(1.1)

\int_{\Sigma}H\,\text{dvol}_{\gamma}\geq|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|^{\frac{1}{n-1}}|\Sigma|^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}.

Moreover, the rigidity holds if and only if
\Sigma
is isometric to the
(n-1)
-sphere. The inequality (
1.1
) has been improved to include star-shaped surfaces
[
6
,
7
]
. One can replace the star-shaped condition by an outward minimizing condition using the weak inverse mean curvature flow of Huisken and Ilmanen
[
10
]
. It is an open problem whether (
1.1
) holds for general mean convex
hypersurfaces. This inequality has been generalized to different settings. In
[
4
]
, Brendle, Wang and the second author extended this inequality to convex, star-shaped hypersurface
\Sigma
with induced metric
\gamma
in the Anti-de-Sitter Schwarzschild space with horizon
\Sigma_{H}
and static potential
\phi

(1.2)

\int_{\Sigma}\phi H\,\text{dvol}_{\gamma}-n(n-1)\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\text{dvol}_{g}\geq(n-1)|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\left(|\Sigma|^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}-|\Sigma_{H}|^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}\right),

where
\Omega
is a bounded region with boundary
\partial\Omega=\Sigma\cup\Sigma_{H}
. Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
\Sigma
is a coordinate sphere. For bounded region with outward minimizing boundary

\Sigma
in the Schwarzschild space with mass
m
, Wei
[
11
]
proved that

(1.3)

\frac{1}{(n-1)|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}\int_{\Sigma}\phi H\,\text{dvol}_{\gamma}\geq\left(\frac{|\Sigma|}{|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}-2m.

with rigidity for coordinate sphere. Recently, McCormick
[
9
]
showed that the inequality (
1.3
) holds for asymptotically flat static spacetime of dimension
3\leq n\leq 7
. Note that all of these inequalities are for static manifolds with spherical infinity.

In 1999, Horowitz and Myers
[
8
]
discovered a complete static spacetime
(N^{n+1},-\phi^{2}dt^{2}+g)
with negative cosmological constant
-n
, that is a solution of the following static equation

(1.4)

g\Delta_{g}\phi+\phi\text{Ric}_{g}-\text{Hess}_{g}\phi=0,\qquad R_{g}=-n(n-1)

where
\text{Ric}_{g}
,
R_{g}
,
\Delta_{g}
, and
\text{Hess}_{g}
are Ricci curvature, scalar curvature, Laplace-Beltrami operator, and the hessian with respect to metric
g
, respectively. The time constant slice of this spacetime is an asymptotically hyperbolic manifold with flat
toroidal conformal infinity and it is called
the Horowitz-Myers geon

(M,g,\phi)

[
12
]
. The metric takes the form

(1.5)

g=ds^{2}+\left(\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{2}d\xi^{2}+\phi^{2}\sum\limits_{i=3}^{n}(d\theta^{i})^{2},\qquad\phi(s):=\cosh^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\frac{ns}{2}\right),

where
s\in[0,\infty)
is the geodesic coordinate from central torus,
\xi\in[0,4\pi/n]
, and
\theta^{i}\in[0,a_{i}]
for
0<a_{3}\leq\dots\leq a_{n}
. The period of
\xi
is chosen such that, after a coordinate change, the metric is smooth up to the central torus
\{s=0\}
. The function
\phi(s)
satisfies the equation

(1.6)

\frac{d\phi}{ds}=\phi(1-\phi^{-n})^{1/2}.

The total mass of the Horowitz-Myers geon is

(1.7)

m=-\frac{4\pi}{n}\prod_{i=3}^{n}a_{i}\,.

They postulated that this may be the ground state for a conjectural nonsupersymmetric AdS/CFT correspondence
[
8
]
. More precisely, let
(M,g)
be an asymptotically hyperbolic manifold with flat toroidal conformal infinity and scalar curvature
R(g)\geq-n(n-1)
. Then the total mass is at least equal to the mass of the Horowitz-Myers geon. Moreover, the rigidity holds if and only if
(M,g)
is isometric to the Horowitz-Myers geon. The Horowitz-Myers conjecture follows from Conjecture 3 of
[
8
]
. Progress on the Horowitz-Myers conjecture has been very limited. For small perturbations of Horowitz-Myers the conjecture is true by Constable and Myers
[
5
]
and Woolgar proved the rigidity of this conjecture in 3 dimensions
[
12
]
. Recently, in
[
1
]
, there are more supporting evidence in the validity of this conjecture.

In this paper, we investigate a Minkowski type inequality for toroidal hypersurfaces
\Sigma
with induced metric
\gamma
in the Horowitz-Myers geon
(M,g,\phi)
. We consider a Minkowski-type quantity

(1.8)

\displaystyle Q(\Sigma):=n(n-1)\int_{\Omega}\phi\,\text{dvol}_{g}-\int_{\Sigma}\phi H\,\text{dvol}_{\gamma}.

Here
H
is the mean curvature of
\Sigma
in
M
and
\Omega
is the bonded region enclosed by
\Sigma
. Applying the Divergence Theorem and the static equation (
1.4
),
Q(\Sigma)
can be rewritten as

(1.9)

\displaystyle Q(\Sigma)=\int_{\Sigma}(n-1)g(\nabla\phi,\nu)-\phi H\,\text{dvol}_{\gamma}.

We aim to prove that under certain convexity assumption,

(1.10)

Q(\Sigma)\leq-2^{-1}nm,

where
m
is the mass defined in (
1.7
). The main results are

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n=3
and
\Sigma\subset M
be a graph over
T^{2}
. Suppose that the second fundamental form
\tilde{h}_{ab}
of
\Sigma
that corresponds to the conformal metric
\tilde{g}=\phi^{2}g
is non-negative definite and that

(1.11)

\min_{\Sigma}\phi>1.

Then (
1.10
) holds and the equality is achieved if and only if
\Sigma
is given by a coordinate torus
s\equiv\textup{constant}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n=4
and
\Sigma\subset M
be a graph over
T^{3}
. Assume
\Sigma
is symmetric along the
\xi
direction. Suppose that the second fundamental form
\bar{h}_{ab}
of
\Sigma
that corresponds to the conformal metric
\bar{g}=\left(\phi\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{2}g
is non-negative definite and that

(1.12)

\min_{\Sigma}\phi^{4}\geq 1+\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}.

Then (
1.10
) holds and the equality is achieved if and only if
\Sigma
is given by a coordinate torus
s\equiv\textup{constant}
.

Remark 1.3
.

The assumption (
1.11
) in Theorem
1.1
is to avoid the coordinate singularity at
\{s\equiv 0\}.
The assumption (
1.12
) in Theorem
1.2
is to ensure the flow (
4.1
) admits a long-time solution. As long as the solution of (
4.1
) exists for all time, the result of Theorem
1.2
holds. See Lemma
4.1
for more detail.

The structure of this article is as follows. In Section
2
we discuss the geometry of graphs and prove the monotinicity of the
Q
in (
1.8
) under a weighted normal flow. In Section
3
, we prove the global existence of the flow in dimensions 3 and prove Theorem
1.1
. Finally, in Section
4
, we prove the global existence of the flow in dimensions 4 for axially symmetric graphs and prove Theorem
1.2
.

2.
geometry of graphs

In this section, we investigate the geometry of graph
\Sigma
in
(M,g,\phi)
. First, we define the function

(2.1)

\displaystyle q(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{ds^{\prime}}{\phi(s^{\prime})}.

Using
q
as a coordinate, the conformal metric
g^{\prime}:=\phi^{-2}g
is asymptotic to a flat metric on
\mathbb{R}\times T^{n-1}

(2.2)

\displaystyle g^{\prime}=dq^{2}+\left(\phi^{-1}\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{2}d\xi^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=3}^{n}(d\theta^{i})^{2}.

We will further consider other conformal metrics

(2.3)

\tilde{g}=\phi^{2}g,\ \bar{g}=\left(\phi\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{2}g.

The flows we consider, (
3.1
) and (
4.1
), are unit normal flows in
\tilde{g}
and
\bar{g}
respectively.

We use
\nabla
and
R_{\alpha\beta\lambda\mu}
to denote the Levi-Civita connection and the Riemannian curvature tensor of
g
respectively. For a hypersurface
\Sigma
, we use
\{x^{a}\},a=1,2,\dots,n-1
as a local coordinate. The induced metric and the second fundamental form are denoted by
\gamma_{ab}
and
h_{ab}
respectively. We write
D
for the Levi-Civita connection of
\gamma
. We use
d\textup{vol}_{g}
and
d\textup{vol}_{\gamma}
to denote the volume form of
g
and
\gamma
respectively. The tensors and connections induced by the conformal metrics
g^{\prime},\tilde{g}
or
\bar{g}
will carry the corresponding accents. For instance, the second fundamental form with respect to
g^{\prime}
is written as
h^{\prime}_{ab}
. Also, the index in Weingarten tensor is raised accordingly. For example,
\tilde{h}_{a}^{b}=\tilde{\gamma}^{bc}\tilde{h}_{ca}
.

In the Appendix
A
, we calculate the curvatures and the second fundamental forms with respect to the metric

dq^{2}+\Psi(q)^{2}d\xi^{2}+\sum_{i=3}^{n}(d\theta^{i})^{2},

for a general positive function
\Psi(q)
. The metric
g^{\prime}
corresponds to
\Psi=\phi^{-1}\frac{d\phi}{ds}
, which is the only case we will use. We further record the curvatures and the second fundamental forms under a conformal transformation
\check{g}=e^{2\psi}g^{\prime}
. The metrics
g
,
\tilde{g}
and
\bar{g}
correspond to
\psi=\log\phi
,
2\log\phi
and
2\log\phi+\log\frac{d\phi}{ds}
respectively.

Let
\Sigma
be a graph given by
s=v(\xi,\theta^{i})
for some smooth function
v
defined on
T^{n-1}
. Define the height function in the
q
coordinate as

u(\xi,\theta^{i})=q(v(\xi,\theta^{i})).

Define the slope of
\Sigma
by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\rho^{2}=1+\left(\phi\bigg/\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial\xi}\right)^{2}+\delta^{ij}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta^{i}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta^{j}}.

Let
H
be the mean curvature of
\Sigma
with respect to
g
. A direct computation using (
A.11
) with
\Psi=\phi^{-1}\frac{d\phi}{ds}
,
\psi=\log\phi
and (
1.6
) shows

\displaystyle H=-\rho\phi^{-1}\Delta^{\prime}u+(2^{-1}n)\rho^{-1}\phi^{1-n}\left(\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{-1}\delta^{ij}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta^{i}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta^{j}}+(n-1)\rho^{-1}\phi^{-1}\frac{d\phi}{ds}+(2^{-1}n)\rho^{-1}\phi^{1-n}\left(\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{-1}.

Here
\Delta^{\prime}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator of
\gamma^{\prime}
. Together with

d\textup{vol}_{\gamma}=\rho\phi^{n-2}\frac{d\phi}{ds}\,d\xi\wedge d\theta^{3}\wedge\dots\wedge d\theta^{n},

we derive from (
1.9
) that

(2.5)

\begin{split}&Q(\Sigma)+2^{-1}nm=\int_{T^{n-1}}\rho^{2}\phi^{n-2}\frac{d\phi}{ds}\Delta^{\prime}u-(2^{-1}n)\delta^{ij}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta^{i}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\theta^{j}}\,d\xi\wedge d\theta^{3}\wedge\dots\wedge d\theta^{n}.\end{split}

Let
p
be a function defined on
M
to be determined. Let
F:[0,T_{0})\times T^{n-1}\to M
be a family of embeddings that satisfies

(2.6)

\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}=p\nu,

where
\nu
is the unit outward normal. Denote by
\Sigma_{t}
the image of
F(t,\cdot)
and by
\gamma_{t}
the induced metric.

Lemma 2.1
.

•

For
n=3
, by taking
p=\phi^{-1}
,
Q(\Sigma_{t})
is monotone non-decreasing along the flow (
2.6
).

•

For
n=4
, assume
\Sigma_{0}
is symmetric along the
\xi
direction. Then by taking
p=(\phi\frac{d\phi}{ds})^{-1}
,
Q(\Sigma_{t})
is monotone non-decreasing along the flow (
2.6
).

Furthermore, in the two cases above,
Q(\Sigma_{t})
is strictly increasing unless
\Sigma_{0}
is a coordinate torus.

Proof.

We compute

(2.7)

\frac{d}{dt}Q(\Sigma_{t})=n(n-1)\int_{\Sigma_{t}}p\phi\,d\textup{vol}_{\gamma_{t}}-\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}H+p\phi H^{2}+\phi\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}\right)\,d\textup{vol}_{\gamma_{t}}.

Using the first variation of area and the Gauss equation,

(2.8)

\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}=-\Delta_{\gamma_{t}}p-p|h|^{2}-p\text{Ric}(\nu,\nu),

(2.9)

n(n-1)-H^{2}+|h|^{2}=-R_{\gamma_{t}}-2\text{Ric}(\nu,\nu).

Hence

(2.10)

\frac{d}{dt}Q(\Sigma_{t})=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}p\phi\left[-R_{\gamma_{t}}-2\text{Ric}(\nu,\nu)\right]-pHg(\nabla\phi,\nu)+p\phi\text{Ric}(\nu,\nu)+\phi\Delta_{\gamma_{t}}p\,d\textup{vol}_{\gamma_{t}}.

Using the static equation (
1.4
),

(2.11)

\Delta_{\gamma_{t}}\phi=\Delta_{g}\phi-\nabla^{2}\phi(\nu,\nu)-Hg(\nabla\phi,\nu)=-\phi\text{Ric}(\nu,\nu)-Hg(\nabla\phi,\nu),

we obtain

(2.12)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}Q(\Sigma_{t})&=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}-p\phi R_{\gamma_{t}}+p\Delta_{\gamma_{t}}\phi+\phi\Delta_{\gamma_{t}}p\,d\textup{vol}_{\gamma_{t}}\\
&=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}-p\phi R_{\gamma_{t}}-2\gamma_{t}(D\phi,Dp)\,d\textup{vol}_{\gamma_{t}}.\end{split}

For
n=3
, by choosing
p=\phi^{-1}
, we obtain from the Gauss-Bonnet Theorem that

(2.13)

\frac{d}{dt}Q(\Sigma_{t})=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}-R_{\gamma_{t}}+2\phi^{-2}\gamma_{t}(D\phi,D\phi)\,d\textup{vol}_{\gamma_{t}}\geq 0.

Moreover,
\frac{d}{dt}Q(\Sigma_{t})=0
if and only if
\phi
is a constant on
\Sigma_{t}
. This implies
\Sigma_{t}
is a coordinate torus. By reversing the flow (
2.6
),
\Sigma_{0}
is also a coordinate torus.

For
n=4
, we assume that
\Sigma_{0}
is symmetric along the
\xi
direction. Hence
\Sigma_{t}
preserves the same symmetry. Denote by
\mathring{\gamma}_{t}
the induced metric on
\mathring{\Sigma}_{t}=\Sigma_{t}\cap\{\xi\equiv\textup{constant}\}
. Then

\gamma_{t}=\mathring{\gamma}_{t}+\left(\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{2}d\xi^{2}.

The scalar curvature of
\gamma_{t}
and
\mathring{\gamma}_{t}
are related through

R_{\gamma_{t}}=R_{\mathring{\gamma}_{t}}-2\left(\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{-1}\Delta_{\mathring{\gamma}_{t}}\left(\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right).

Assuming
p
is also symmetric along the
\xi
direction, we get

\frac{d}{dt}Q(\Sigma_{t})=\frac{4\pi}{n}\int_{\mathring{\Sigma}_{t}}-p\phi\frac{d\phi}{ds}R_{\mathring{\gamma}_{t}}+2p\phi\Delta_{\mathring{\gamma}_{t}}\left(\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)-2\frac{d\phi}{ds}\mathring{\gamma}_{t}(D\phi,Dp)\,d\textup{vol}_{\mathring{\gamma}_{t}}.

Taking
p=\left(\phi\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{-1}
, we deduce from the Gauss-Bonnet Theorem that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}Q(\Sigma_{t})=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi}{n}\int_{\mathring{\Sigma}_{t}}-R_{\mathring{\gamma}_{t}}+2\left(\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{-1}\Delta_{\mathring{\gamma}_{t}}\left(\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)-2\frac{d\phi}{ds}\mathring{\gamma}_{t}\left(D\phi,D\left(\phi\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{-1}\right)\,d\textup{vol}_{\mathring{\gamma}_{t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\pi}{n}\int_{\mathring{\Sigma}_{t}}\left(-2\frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{-1}\frac{d^{2}\phi}{ds^{2}}-2\left(\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{2}\frac{d}{ds}\left(\phi\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{-1}\right)\cdot\mathring{\gamma}_{t}(Dv_{t},Dv_{t})\,d\textup{vol}_{\mathring{\gamma}_{t}}.

From (
1.6
) and
n=4
, we derive

\displaystyle-2\frac{d}{ds}\left(\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{-1}\frac{d^{2}\phi}{ds^{2}}-2\left(\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{2}\frac{d}{ds}\left(\phi\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{-1}=(1-\phi^{-4})^{-1}(6+2\phi^{-8})\geq 0.

Thus
Q(\Sigma_{t})
is monotone non-decreasing along the flow. Using the same argument for the
3
dimensional case,
\frac{d}{dt}Q(\Sigma_{t})=0
if and only if
\Sigma_{0}
is a coordinate sphere.
∎

3.
Three Dimensional Case

In this section, we fix
n=3
and prove Theorem
1.1
. Recall that
F:[0,T_{0})\times T^{n-1}\to M
solves

(3.1)

\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}=\phi^{-1}\nu.

Here we take
T_{0}\in(0,\infty]
to be the largest number such that (
3.1
) has a smooth graphical solution in
t\in[0,T_{0}).

Equation (
3.1
) can be viewed as the unit normal flow with respect to the conformal metric
\tilde{g}=\phi^{2}g
. Recall that
\tilde{h}_{ab}
is the the second fundamental form with respect to
\tilde{g}
. Our main assumption is

(3.2)

\displaystyle\tilde{h}_{ab}\ \textup{is non-negative definite for }\Sigma_{0}\ \textup{and}\ \min_{\Sigma_{0}}\phi>1.

We list two lemmas which allow us to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
T_{0}=\infty
. Then

(3.3)

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}Q(\Sigma_{t})\leq-\frac{3m}{2}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Under the assumption (
3.2
), we have
T_{0}=\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\Sigma_{t}
be the solution of (
3.1
) starting from
\Sigma_{0}=\Sigma
. Combining Lemmas
2.1
,
3.2
and
3.1
,

\displaystyle Q(\Sigma)\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}Q(\Sigma_{t})\leq-\frac{3m}{2}.

Furthermore, the equality implies
Q(\Sigma_{t})
is a constant along the flow. And Lemma
2.1
implies
\Sigma
is a coordinate torus.
∎

We prove Lemmas
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
in the rest of this section. We adapt the convention that
C
denotes a large constant depending on
\Sigma_{0}
. The value of
C
may change from line to line.

Denote by
v_{t}(\xi,\theta)
the height function of
\Sigma_{t}
and
u_{t}=q(v_{t})
. Then
v_{t}
solves the equation

(3.4)

\frac{\partial v_{t}}{\partial t}=\rho\phi(v_{t})^{-1}.

Here
\rho
is the slope of
\Sigma_{t}
defined in (
2.4
). We start with the
C^{0}
and
C^{1}
estimates for
v_{t}
and
u_{t}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists a constant
C
depending on
\Sigma_{0}
such that for all
t\in[0,T_{0})
,

(3.5)

\begin{split}|\phi(v_{t})-t|\leq&C,\ \phi(v_{t})\geq 1+C^{-1}(t+1).\end{split}

Proof.

At the minimum point of
v_{t}
, we have
\rho=1
. Together with (
1.6
),

\frac{d}{dt}\min_{T^{2}}\phi(v_{t})\geq\left(1-(\min_{T^{2}}\phi(v_{t}))^{-3}\right)^{1/2}.

Let
\Phi(t,a)
be the solution to the ODE

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}=(1-\Phi^{-3})^{1/2},\ \Phi(0,a)=a.

Through the ODE comparison,

\displaystyle\min_{T^{2}}\phi(v_{t})\geq\Phi(t,\min_{T^{2}}\phi(v_{0})).

From now on, we take
a=\displaystyle\min_{T^{2}}\phi(v_{0})
. By the assumption (
3.2
),
a>1
. Because
\Phi(t,a)
is increasing in
t
, we have
\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}\geq(1-a^{-3})^{1/2}
. Through integration,
\Phi(t,a)\geq(1-a^{-3})^{1/2}t+a
. This implies
\phi(v_{t})\geq 1+C^{-1}(t+1)
for
C
large enough. Furthermore, using the equation of
\Phi
again,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}-1\right|\leq\frac{C}{(1+t)^{3}}.

Thus
|\Phi(t,a)-t|\leq C
and
\displaystyle\min_{T^{2}}\phi(v_{t})\geq t-C
. With a similar argument, we can also derive

\displaystyle\max_{T^{2}}\phi(v_{t})\leq t+C.

The proof is finished.
∎

For the notation simplicity, we later denote
\phi(v_{t})
and
\frac{d\phi}{ds}(v_{t})
by
\phi
and
\frac{d\phi}{ds}
respectively. Recall that

\displaystyle\rho^{2}=1+\left(\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial\theta}\right)^{2}+\left(\phi\bigg/\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial\xi}\right)^{2}.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists a constant
C
depending on
\Sigma_{0}
such that for
t\in[0,T_{0})
,

(3.6)

\max_{\Sigma_{t}}(\rho^{2}-1)\leq(C^{-1}t+1)^{-4}\max_{\Sigma_{0}}(\rho^{2}-1).

Proof.

Using (
1.6
) and (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}u_{t}}{\partial x^{a}\partial t}=

\displaystyle\phi^{-2}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial x^{a}}-2\rho\phi^{-1}(1-\phi^{-3})^{1/2}\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial x^{a}}

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\phi\bigg/\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle-3\rho\phi^{-4}(1-\phi^{-3})^{-3/2}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\rho^{2}-1)=

\displaystyle 2\phi^{-2}\left(\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial\theta}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\theta}+\left(\phi\bigg/\frac{d\phi}{ds}\right)^{2}\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial\xi}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\xi}\right)

\displaystyle-3\rho\phi^{-4}(1-\phi^{-3})^{3/2}\left(\frac{\partial u_{t}}{\partial\xi}\right)^{2}-4\rho\phi^{-1}(1-\phi^{-3})^{1/2}(\rho^{2}-1).

At the maximum point of
\rho^{2}
,
d\rho=0
and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\rho^{2}-1)\leq

\displaystyle-4\rho\phi^{-1}(1-\phi^{-3})^{1/2}(\rho^{2}-1).

Using (
3.5
), there exists a constant
C
such that for all
t\in[0,T_{0})
,

\displaystyle 4\rho\phi^{-1}(1-\phi^{-3})^{1/2}\geq 4\phi^{-1}(1-\phi^{-3})^{1/2}\geq 4(t+C)^{-1}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\max_{\Sigma_{t}}(\rho^{2}-1)\leq

\displaystyle-4(t+C)^{-1}\max_{\Sigma_{t}}(\rho^{2}-1).

By the ODE comparison,

\displaystyle\max_{\Sigma_{t}}(\rho^{2}-1)\leq(C^{-1}t+1)^{-4}\max_{\Sigma_{0}}(\rho^{2}-1).

The proof is finished.
∎

Recall that
g^{\prime}=\phi^{-2}g
is a conformal metric which asymptotic to the flat metric on
\mathbb{R}\times T^{2}
. Let
\gamma^{\prime}_{t}
be the induced metric of
g^{\prime}
on
\Sigma_{t}
and
D^{\prime}
be the Levi-Civita connection of
\gamma^{\prime}_{t}
. Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
imply, provided
T_{0}=\infty
,
\gamma^{\prime}_{t}
converges the the flat metric
d\xi^{2}+d\theta^{2}
in
C^{0}
. Next, we use the second fundamental form
h^{\prime}_{ab}
to bound the hessian of
u_{t}
from below.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose
T_{0}=\infty.
Then there exists a constant
C
depending on

, we derive that non-zero components of

\displaystyle-3\phi^{8}(1-\phi^{-4})^{3},

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{qiqi}=

\displaystyle-\phi^{8}(3-6\phi^{-4}-\phi^{-8}),

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{\xi i\xi i}=

\displaystyle-\phi^{8}(9-\phi^{-8})(1-\phi^{-4}),

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{ijij}=

\displaystyle-\phi^{8}(1-\phi^{-4})^{2},\ i\neq j.

Besides

4.2

along the flow. Hence the term

is non-negative definite and
\bar{h}_{b}^{a}
also remains non-negative definite . On the other hand, from (
4.5

for some function

∎

.

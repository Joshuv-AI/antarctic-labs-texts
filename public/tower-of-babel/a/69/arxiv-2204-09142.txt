Bi-Colored Expansions of Geometric Theories
S. Jalili, M. Pourmahdian, M. Khani

Abstract
This paper concerns the study of Bi-colored expansions of geometric theories in the light of the Fraïssé-Hrushovski construction method. Substructures of models of a geometric theory T T are expanded by a color predicate p p , and the dimension function associated with the pre-geometry of the T T -algebraic closure operator together with a real number 0 < α ⩽ 1 0<\alpha\leqslant 1 is used to define a pre-dimension function δ α \delta_{\alpha} . The pair ( 𝒦 α + , ⩽ α ) (\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{+},\leqslant_{\alpha}) consisting of all such expansions with a hereditary positive pre-dimension along with the notion of substructure ⩽ α \leqslant_{\alpha} associated to δ α \delta_{\alpha} is then used as a natural setting for the study of generic bi-colored expansions in the style of Fraïssé-Hrushovski construction. Imposing certain natural conditions on T T , enables us to introduce a complete axiomatization 𝕋 α \mathbb{T}_{\alpha} for the class of rich structures in this class. We will show that if T T is a dependent theory (NIP) then so is 𝕋 α \mathbb{T}_{\alpha} . We further prove that whenever α \alpha is rational the strong dependence transfers to 𝕋 α \mathbb{T}_{\alpha} . We conclude by showing that if T T defines a linear order and α \alpha is irrational then 𝕋 α \mathbb{T}_{\alpha} is not strongly dependent. Keywords: Geometric Theories; Fraïssé-Hrushovski construction; Bi-Colored Expansions; Rich Structures; Dependence (NIP); Strong Dependence. AMS Subject Classification 2020 03C45.

Introduction

Extending methods of stability and applying them to a larger class of theories is a dominating theme in current research of pure model theory.
In addition to showing the prevalence of these methods, this line of research also presents potential applications beyond model theory. To instantiate general concepts in stability-hierarchy and perhaps examine some related open questions/conjectures one would need to look for some new examples, conceivably through adapting known model-theoretic methods.
Our main aim in this paper
is to study the Fraïssé-Hrushovski method beyond the realm of stability/simplicity. We aim to take the first step of the more general plan of modifying the Fraïssé-Hrushovski construction to study Bi-colored expansions of geometric theories without the independence property (NIP).

There are two major (independent) research themes with which our proposed plan is naturally connected, as explained below.

Our presentation somehow shows that both subjects could be put in a common framework where the Fraïssé-Hrushovski construction is seen as a sort of generalization of the theme of the generic expansions.

Fact 2.1

Examples of indecomposable geometric theories with free-amalgamation include
strongly minimal expansions of divisible abelian groups,
o-minimal expansions of ordered divisible abelian groups (ODAG),
any completion of the theory of algebraically closed valued fields, and

p

The indecomposability of the mentioned theories indeed stems in the fact that no vector space over an infinite field can be written as a finite union of its proper subvector spaces.

Definition 2.11

3
Rich Structures and their Axiomatization

3.1
Rich Structures

We have already defined what we mean by
T

having
the free-amalgamation property (see Definition
2.7
).
Along the same lines,
we say that

(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{+},\leqslant)
has the free-amalgamation property (defined more precisely after the following definition)
if
the conditions in Definition

2.7
hold
with
M_{i},M
structures in the class

\mathcal{K}^{+}_{\alpha}
,
and

f_{i},g_{i}
strong embeddings in the sense of the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

By
a
strong embedding

f:M\to N

we mean an

\mathcal{L}_{p}
-embedding

f

such that

f(M)\leqslant N
.

So by
the class
(\mathcal{K}^{+}_{\alpha},\leqslant)
having the amalgamation property we mean that
for each

M_{0},M_{1},M_{2}

and each pair of strong
embeddings

f_{1}:M_{0}\to M_{1}
,

f_{2}:M_{0}\to M_{2}
, there are

M

and
strong
embeddings

g_{1}:M_{1}\to M
,

g_{2}:M_{2}\to M

such that

g_{1}\circ f_{1}=g_{2}\circ f_{2}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The class
(\mathcal{K}^{+}_{\alpha},\leqslant)
has the amalgamation property. Moreover, if both
f_{1},f_{2}
are algebraically-closed then we can choose
M\in\mathcal{K}^{+}_{\alpha}
such that

g_{1}(M_{1})
and
g_{2}(M_{2})
are free over
g_{1}\circ f_{1}(M_{0})
in
M
.

Proof.

Assume that

\mathcal{L}_{p}
-structures

M_{0},M_{1},M_{2}
in
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{+}

and the strong
embeddings

f_{1}
,

f_{2}

are as above.

We can assume, without loss of generality that

M_{1},M_{2}
are models of
T
.
This can be obtained
by
\mathcal{L}
-embedding of each
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
into a model of
T
and a suitable extension of the coloring.

We may also assume that
f_{1}
,

f_{2}
are algebraically-closed strong embeddings. This is because

\acl_{M_{1}}(M_{0})
and
\acl_{M_{2}}(M_{0})

are

\mathcal{L}_{p}
-isomorphic over
M_{0}
,
as
both
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are strong (see the explanation after Definition
2.12
).

Now since

T

has the free-amalgamation property, there are a
model

M

and
\mathcal{L}
-embeddings

g_{i}:M_{i}\to M
,
such that
g_{1}\circ f_{1}=g_{2}\circ f_{2}
.
Defining

p(M)=g_{1}(p(M_{1}))\cup g_{2}(p(M_{2}))
, it is clear that

M\in\mathcal{K}^{+}_{\alpha}
.

It remains only to prove that

g_{1},g_{2}

are strong
embeddings.
For notational simplicity
we denote again by
M_{1},M_{2}
and
M_{0}

their respective images in
M
, that is

g_{1}(M_{1})
,
g_{2}(M_{2})

and

g_{1}\circ f_{1}(M_{0})
.
In the following we show that
M_{1}\leqslant M
(and
M_{2}\leqslant M

follows similarly).

Let

A\subseteq M\setminus M_{1}
be arbitrary. Writing

A=BC

where

B\subseteq{M}_{2}
,

C\subseteq M\setminus{M}_{2}

we have

\displaystyle\delta(A/{M}_{1})=\delta(C/{M}_{1})+\delta(B/{M}_{1}C).

Now
\delta(C/{M}_{1})\geqslant 0
because
all elements of
C
are non-colored.
Also
\delta(B/{M}_{1}C)=\delta(B/{M_{0}}C)\geqslant 0
, because

{M}_{1}\mathrel{\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$|$\cr\raise-3.87495pt\hbox{$\smile$}\crcr}}}}^{\dim}_{{M}_{0}}{M}_{2}
.

∎

As we have let

\emptyset
in
\mathcal{K}^{+}_{\alpha}
,
the amalgamation property implies
the joint embedding property. It is also clear that
if
M
is in
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{+}

then so are all its substructures.
In this sense,
we call

(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{+},\leqslant)

a
Fraïssé class
, and
it is natural to look for its Fraïssé limit, or the
rich

structures in the sense below.

Definition 3.3
.

An

\mathcal{L}_{p}
-structure

M

in

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{+}

is called

\lambda
-rich,
for
\lambda\geqslant\aleph_{0}
a cardinal,
if

1.

M\models T
,

2.

If

M_{1}\leqslant M_{2}

are
generated by a set of cardinality

<\lambda
,
then each strong embedding

f:M_{1}\to M

extends to
a strong embedding

g:M_{2}\to M
.

The Amalgamation and Joint Embedding properties together with the fact
that
\mathcal{K}^{+}_{\alpha}
is closed
under the
union of
\leqslant
-chains, imply
that the
\lambda
-rich structures exist.
Indeed a union of
\leqslant
-chains of models of
T
, and
the
quantifier-elimination (for this union to be a model) give such a structure.
We prefer to state this also as the following fact.

Fact 3.4
.

(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{+},\leqslant)
contains

\lambda
-rich structures,
for all

\lambda\geqslant\aleph_{0}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Each
\lambda
-rich structure is

\lambda
-saturated as a model of
T
.

Proof.

Let

M

be

\lambda
-rich.
Assume that

\Sigma(x)
is a partial
1
-type over a set

X\subseteq{M}
,
where
|X|<\lambda
and
without loss of generality assume that

X
is closed in

{M}
.
Let

be a solution of

\Sigma(x)
in
an
\mathcal{L}
-elementary extension

{N}
of

{M}
.
Extend the coloring of
{M}
to
{N}

by letting
\neg p(x)
for each

, so that now
{N}\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{+}
.
Observe that

\cl_{M}(X)\leqslant{M},{N}
and hence we keep the notation

\cl(X)
.
It is clear that

\langle\cl(X)d\rangle\in\mathcal{K}^{+}_{\alpha}
,

\langle\cl(X)\rangle\leqslant\langle\cl(X)d\rangle

and
\langle\cl(X)\rangle\leqslant{M}
.
Now since

M
is
\lambda
-rich, there is a strong embedding

, and hence

is the solution of

\Sigma(x)
in

{M}
.
∎

It is worth noting that
in the literature,

.

Definition 3.6

Let

1.

2.

\mathbb{M}\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{+}

3.

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}\models

\displaystyle\forall\bar{y}\hskip 8.53581pt\Big[D_{\varphi,k}(\bar{y})\wedge d_{\varphi,k}(\bar{y})\to\exists\bar{z}\hskip 5.69054pt\big(\varphi(\bar{z},\bar{y})\wedge\bigwedge_{p(d_{i})}p(z_{i})\wedge\bigwedge_{\neg p(d_{i})}\neg p(z_{i})\hskip 5.69054pt\big)\Big]\hskip 5.69054pt

The items above can be expressed

In the rest of this section we aim to establish the following theorem.

Theorem

is a complete theory that axiomatizes

-rich structures of

.

Towards a proof of the theorem above, what we can prove in the rest of this subsection with

Proving that models of

are rich and therefore giving
the full proof of the theorem will be

Lemma 3.7
.

Any

\omega_{1}
-rich structure

\mathbb{M}

is a model of

\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}
(and hence

\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}
is consistent).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.5
,

\mathbb{M}
is
\omega_{1}
-saturated as
a model of
T
.
Now we only need to prove that
item 3 in Definition

holds.
Let
\bar{a},\bar{d}
and

\varphi
be as in there,
and
\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}}
in
\mathbb{M}
be such that
\mathbb{M}\models D_{\varphi,k}(\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})\wedge d_{\varphi,k}(\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})
.
So there is

\bar{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{M}^{|\bar{d}|}

such that

\mathbb{M}\models\varphi(\bar{d}^{{}^{\prime}},\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})
,
\bar{d}^{{}^{\prime}}\cap\acl(\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})=\emptyset
,
and

\dim(\bar{d}^{{}^{\prime}}/\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})=k
. Since
\langle\cl(\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})\rangle
is countable, by

\omega_{1}
-saturation of

\mathbb{M}
and Observation

2.8

we may assume that

\langle\cl(\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})\rangle\cap\bar{d}^{{}^{\prime}}=\emptyset

and,
\bar{d}^{\prime}
and
\langle\cl(\bar{a}^{\prime})\rangle

are free over
\bar{a}^{\prime}
.
Consider the

\mathcal{L}
-structure generated by

\cl(\bar{a}^{\prime})\bar{d}^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{M}
and make it into
the
\mathcal{L}_{p}
-structure

by coloring

with the same colors as
\cl(\bar{a})\bar{d}

and leaving the rest of the elements non-colored.
It is clear that
\langle\cl(\bar{a}^{\prime})\rangle\leqslant\mathbb{M}
.
We claim that also
\langle\cl(\bar{a}^{\prime})\rangle\leqslant{N}
.
If this claim is proved then since

\mathbb{M}
is
\omega_{1}
-rich, there is a strong embedding

which fixes

\langle\cl(\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})\rangle
pointwise. If we put

,

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}\models\big(\varphi(\bar{e},\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})\wedge\bigwedge_{p(d_{i})}p(e_{i})\wedge\bigwedge_{\neg p(d_{i})}\neg p(e_{i})\big).

\bar{d}^{\prime}_{1}\subseteq\bar{d}^{\prime}
and

\bar{e}\subseteq N\setminus\cl(\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})\bar{d}^{\prime}
. Notice that

\dim(\bar{d}^{\prime}_{1}/\langle\cl(\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})\rangle)=\dim(\bar{d}^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}/\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})

\bar{d}\mathrel{\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$|$\cr\raise-3.87495pt\hbox{$\smile$}\crcr}}}}^{\dim}_{\cl(\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})}\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}}

\dim(\bar{d}^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}/\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})\geqslant\dim(\bar{d}_{1}/\bar{a})
. Therefore

\displaystyle\delta(\bar{d}^{\prime}_{1}\bar{e}/\langle\cl(\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})\rangle)

\displaystyle=\dim(\bar{d}^{\prime}_{1}\bar{e}/\langle\cl(\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})\rangle)-\alpha|p(\bar{d}^{\prime}_{1}\bar{e})|

\displaystyle=\dim(\bar{d}^{\prime}_{1}/\langle\cl(\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})\rangle)-\alpha|p(\bar{d}^{\prime}_{1})|

\displaystyle=\dim(\bar{d}^{{}^{\prime}}_{1}/\bar{a}^{{}^{\prime}})-\alpha|p(\bar{d}^{\prime}_{1})|

\displaystyle\geqslant\dim(\bar{d}_{1}/\bar{a})-\alpha|p(\bar{d}_{1})|\geqslant 0.

∎

It follows from the proof above that

But this gives infinitely many elements satisfying

which contradicts the algebraicity of
\psi(x,b_{0})
.

∎

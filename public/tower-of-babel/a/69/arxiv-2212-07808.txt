Abstract




Factorization of Characteristic Functions of Iterated Liftings

Abstract:
We obtain a factorization of the characteristic function of a contractive two-step iterated lifting in terms of the characteristic functions of constituent liftings of the iterated lifting and the Julia-Halmos matrix. We also give an expression for the characteristic function of the minimal part of a contractive two-step iterated lifting as a restriction of the product of the characteristic functions of constituent liftings of the iterated lifting.

47A20, 47A13, 47A15, 47A68

keywords:
row contractions, contractive lifting, Fock space, characteristic functions, multi-analytic operators, completely non-coisometric

1.
Introduction

The aim of this article is to study lifting of row contractions on a Hilbert space. The foundation for the study of contractions on Hilbert spaces using dilation theory was laid down by Sz-Nagy, C. Foias, Sarason, etc. (
[
11
]
). Let
\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
be a set of bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Then
\underline{S}=(S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{d})
is called a
row contraction
, if
\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\sum}}S_{i}S_{i}^{*}\leq I_{\mathcal{H}}
. The work by Sz-Nagy and C. Foias
[
11
]
is extended to different setups, for example, to row contractions by G. Popescu
[
12
,
13
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
, to commutative row contraction by W. Arveson
[
1
]
and to a pair of commuting contractions by Ando
[
2
]
.

To every contraction on a Hilbert space, Sz-Nagy and C. Foias
[
11
]
adjoined an operator valued analytic function, called a characteristic function, which is a completely invariant upto unitary equivalence for the contractions. G. Popescu
[
12
]
introduced characteristic function for (non commutative) row contractions and extended Sz-Nagy-Foias theory of characteristic function. Characteristic functions of commuting row contractions are studied in
[
3
,
4
]
and we refer to references therein for more details. Later, S. Dey and R. Gohm (
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
) introduced characteristic functions for contractive liftings and established that it is a unitary invariant for a large class of liftings. Popescu’s theory of characteristic function of row contractions was shown as a special case of the theory of characteristic function of lifting in
[
7
]
. In this article, we study factorizations of the characteristic function of contractive liftings defined in
[
6
]
.

In this article, one of the key element is Julia-Halmos matrix. Julia-Halmos matrix corresponding to a bounded linear operator
L
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is a unitary matrix of the form

J_{L}=\begin{pmatrix}D_{*,L}&L\\
-L^{*}&D_{L}\end{pmatrix}

where
D_{L}
and
D_{*,L}
denote the defect operators of
L.
Julia-Halmos matrix plays a significant role in dilation theory and we refer to
[
18
]
for more details about the matrix.

There are many factorizations of characteristic function available in the literature. Among them the most significant one is inner-outer factorization
[
11
]
. A considerable amount of research work has been published on inner-outer factorization and some of the references for this are
[
11
,
12
]
. A more general factorization of a contractive analytic function is the regular factorization (
[
11
, Page 297]
). The inner-outer factorization is also a regular factorization. Moreover, regular factorization is connected to another very interesting area in operator theory, that is, invariant subspaces of the contraction. Corresponding to every non-trivial invariant subspace of a contraction, there is a non-trivial regular factorization of the characteristic function. On other hand, if a given non-trivial factorization of the characteristic function is regular, then there exist a non-trivial invariant subspace for the contraction. G. Popescu
[
13
]
defined regular factorization for row contraction and established a one-one correspondence between joint invariant subspace and regular factorization.

Besides regular factorization, Sz-Nagy and C. Foias
[
10
]
also demonstrated a factorization technique using Julia-Halmos matrix, where regularity condition of the characteristic function is not required. As in regular factorization, the factorization in
[
10
]
for the characteristic function of the contraction

T=\begin{pmatrix}T_{1}&X\\
0&T_{2}\end{pmatrix}

is achieved in terms of the characteristic function of operators
T_{1}
and
T_{2}.
However the proof is more direct and does not use functional model. K. J. Haria et. al.
[
9
]
extended this notion to characteristic functions of non commutative row contractions. In this article, we prove a similar factorization for characteristic functions of contractive two-step iterated liftings.

In section
2
of this article, we introduce notations and recall results related to row contractions which are used in this article. In section
3
, we give a Nagy-Foias type matrix factorization of characteristic functions for minimal contractive iterated liftings based on Julia-Halmos matrix. Let
\underline{E^{\prime}}
be a contractive iterated lifting consisting of two minimal liftings and let
\underline{\tilde{E}}
be the minimal part of the iterated lifting. In section
4
, we prove that the characteristic function of the minimal part
\underline{\tilde{E}}
of the iterated lifting
\underline{E^{\prime}}
is the restriction to the defect space of
\underline{\tilde{E}}
of product of characteristic functions of these minimal liftings.

2.
Preliminaries

For
d\geq 2
, the full Fock space over
\mathbb{C}^{d}
is defined by

\Gamma(\mathbb{C}^{d}):=\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{d}\oplus(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes 2}\oplus\cdots(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes m}\cdots.

In this article, we denote
\Gamma(\mathbb{C}^{d})
by
\Gamma
. The standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{C}^{d}
is denoted by
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{d}\}
and
e_{0}:=1\oplus 0\oplus\cdots
is the vacuum vector. Let
\Lambda
be the set
\{1,2,\ldots,d\}
and
\tilde{\Lambda}=\underset{n=0}{\overset{\infty}{\bigcup}}\Lambda^{n}
, where
\Lambda^{0}:=\{0\}
. For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\Lambda^{n}
, we define
|\alpha|:=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
and
e_{\alpha}:=e_{\alpha_{1}}\otimes e_{\alpha_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{\alpha_{n}}.
Note that the set
\{e_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\tilde{\Lambda}\}
forms an orthonormal basis for the full Fock space
\Gamma
. The operators
L_{i}
on
\Gamma,
defined by
L_{i}h=e_{i}\otimes h
for
i=1,\ldots,d
and
h\in\Gamma,
are called the (left) creation operators.

Let
\underline{S}=(S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{d})
be a row contraction on
\mathcal{H}_{S}
. Then the
defect operators
and
defect spaces
are defined by

\displaystyle D_{S}=\left(\delta_{ij}I-S_{i}^{*}S_{j}\right)^{1/2},\,\,\,\,

\displaystyle D_{*,S}=\left(I-\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\sum}}S_{i}S_{i}^{*}\right)^{1/2}\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{S}=\overline{\text{range }D_{S}},\,\,\,\,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{*,S}=\overline{\text{range }D_{*,S}},

respectively. For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\Lambda^{n}
, we set
S_{\alpha}:=S_{\alpha_{1}}S_{\alpha_{2}}\ldots S_{\alpha_{n}}
.
For a row contraction
\underline{S}
on
\mathcal{H}_{S}
, Popescu, in
[
14
]
, obtained a realization of the minimal isometric dilation (mid in short)
\underline{V}^{S}
of
\underline{S}
on
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{S}=\mathcal{H}_{S}\oplus(\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{S})
as

V_{j}^{S}\left(h\oplus\underset{\alpha\in\tilde{\Lambda}}{\sum}e_{\alpha}\otimes d_{\alpha}\right)=S_{j}h\oplus\left(e_{0}\otimes(D_{S})_{j}h\oplus e_{j}\otimes\underset{\alpha\in\tilde{\Lambda}}{\sum}e_{\alpha}\otimes d_{\alpha}\right),

for
h\in\mathcal{H}_{S},\,d_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{D}_{S}
, where
(\mathcal{D}_{S})_{j}h=\mathcal{D}_{S}(0,\ldots,h,\ldots,0).

Let
\underline{C}=(C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{d})
be a row contraction on
\mathcal{H}_{C}
. Then
\underline{E}
is called a
lifting
of
\underline{C}
by
\underline{A}
on
\mathcal{H}_{E}=\mathcal{H}_{C}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{A}
, if

E_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}C_{i}&0\\
B_{i}&A_{i}\end{bmatrix}\text{ for }i=1,2,\ldots,d.

Throughout this article, we work with contractive liftings which are characterized by the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
6
, Proposition 3.1]

Let
\underline{E}=(E_{1},E_{2},\ldots,E_{d})
, where
E_{i}
be a bounded linear operator on
\mathcal{H}_{E}=\mathcal{H}_{C}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{A}
with block matrix representation

E_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}C_{i}&0\\
B_{i}&A_{i}\end{bmatrix}\text{ for }i=1,2,\ldots,d.

Then
\underline{E}
is a row contraction if and only if
\underline{C}
and
\underline{A}
are row contractions and there exists a contraction
\gamma:\mathcal{D}_{*,A}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{C}
such that
\underline{B}^{*}=D_{C}\gamma D_{*,A}.

The contraction
\gamma:\mathcal{D}_{*,A}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{C}
in Proposition
2.1
is called
resolving

[
6
, Definition 3.2]
, if for all
h\in\mathcal{H}_{A}
we have the following:

(\gamma D_{*,A}A_{\alpha}^{*}h=0\mbox{~for all~}\alpha\in\tilde{\Lambda})\implies(D_{*,A}A_{\alpha}^{*}h=0\mbox{~for all~}\alpha\in{\tilde{\Lambda}}).

A row contraction
\underline{T}=(T_{1},\ldots,d)
on a Hilbert space
H
is said to be
completely non-coisometric
(cf.
[
12
]
) if

\{h\in H:\sum_{|\alpha|=n}\|T^{*}_{\alpha}h\|^{2}=\|h\|^{2}\mbox{~for all~}n\in\mathbb{N}\}=\{0\}

Definition 2.2
.

[
6
, Definition 3.4]

A lifting
\underline{E}
of
\underline{C}
by
\underline{A}
is called
reduced
, if
\underline{A}
is completely non-coisometric and
\gamma
is resolving.

Definition 2.3
.

A contractive lifting
\underline{E}
of
\underline{C}
is called minimal, if

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{E}=\overline{span}\{E_{\alpha}x:x\in\mathcal{H}_{C}\;\;\text{for all }\,\alpha\in\tilde{\Lambda}\}.

As noted in the remark that follows
[
7
, Proposition 3.8]
(also see
[
8
]
), reduced liftings are same as the
minimal contractive liftings
. For more detail, we refer to
[
7
]
.
Let
\underline{C}
be a row contraction and
\underline{E}
be a minimal lifting of
\underline{C}
by
\underline{A}
on
\mathcal{H}_{E}=\mathcal{H}_{C}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{A}
. Then the minimal isometric dilation
\underline{V}^{E}
of
E
is also an isometric dilation of
\underline{C}
, thus
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{C}
can be embedded in
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{E}
as a reducing subspace of
\underline{V}^{E}.
We denote the orthogonal complement of the image of
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{C}
in
\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{E}
by
\mathcal{K}
and denote the restriction of
{\underline{V}}^{E}
on
\mathcal{K}
by
\underline{Y}
. Then there exist a unitary operator
W:\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{E}\rightarrow\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{C}\oplus\mathcal{K}
satisfying

(2.1)

W|_{{\mathcal{H}_{C}}}=I_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}\text{ and }W{V_{i}}^{E}=(V^{C}\oplus Y)_{i}W\;\text{for all}\;i=1,2,..,d.

S. Dey and R. Gohm
[
6
]
used the operator
W
to define a characteristic function for minimal contractive lifting of a row contraction as follows.

Definition 2.4
.

The characteristic function of the minimal contractive lifting
\underline{E}
of
\underline{C}
is the operator
M_{C,E}:\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{E}\rightarrow\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{C}
defined by

M_{C,E}=P_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{C}}W|_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{E}},

where
P_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{C}}
is the orthogonal projection onto
\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{C}
.

The characteristic function
M_{C,E}
is a multi-analytic operator (cf.
[
15
]
), i.e.,
M_{C,E}(L_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}_{E}})=(L_{i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{D}_{C}})M_{C,E}
for
i=1,\ldots,d
and therefore it is determined by its symbol
\theta_{C,E}:\mathcal{D}_{E}\rightarrow\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{C}
, where
\theta_{C,E}:=W|_{e_{0}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{E}}
. We refer to
[
6
,
7
]
for detailed discussion about characteristic function of liftings. We frequently use the following representation of
\theta_{C,E}
, which is given in section
4
of the article
[
6
]
.

For
h\in\mathcal{H}_{C}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\theta_{C,E}(D_{E})_{i}h=e_{0}\otimes[(D_{C})_{i}h-\gamma D_{*,A}B_{i}h]-\underset{|\alpha|\geq 1}{\sum}e_{\alpha}\otimes\gamma D_{*,A}A_{\alpha}^{*}B_{i}h,

and for
h\in\mathcal{H}_{A}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\theta_{C,E}(D_{E})_{i}h=

\displaystyle-e_{0}\otimes\gamma\underline{A}(D_{A})_{i}h+\underset{j=1}{\overset{d}{\sum}}e_{j}\otimes\underset{\alpha}{\sum}e_{\alpha}\otimes\gamma D_{*,A}A_{\alpha}^{*}P_{j}D_{A}(D_{A})_{i}h,

where
\gamma:\mathcal{D}_{*,A}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{C}
is the contraction from Proposition
2.1
.

The unique formal Fourier expansion of
M_{C,E}
is given by,

\displaystyle M_{C,E}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{\alpha\in\tilde{\Lambda}}R_{\alpha}\otimes(\theta_{C,E})_{(\alpha)}

where
R_{i}
is the right creation operator defined on the Fock space and the “coefficients”
(\theta_{C,E})_{(\alpha)}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{E},\mathcal{D}_{C})
are given by

\langle(\theta_{C,E})_{(\alpha)}x,y\rangle=\langle\theta_{C,E}x,e_{\tilde{\alpha}}y\rangle

for
x\in\mathcal{D}_{E}
and for
y\in\mathcal{D}_{C}
. The symbol
\tilde{\alpha}
is the reverse of
\alpha,
i.e., if
\alpha=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\ldots\alpha_{n}
then
\tilde{\alpha}=\alpha_{n}\alpha_{(n-1)}\ldots\alpha_{1}.

Let
x=D_{E}h
for
h\in\mathcal{H}_{C}
. Then

(2.4)

\displaystyle(\theta_{C,E})_{(\alpha)}x=\begin{cases}D_{C}h-\gamma D_{*A}\underline{B}h&\text{if }\alpha=0\\
-\gamma D_{*A}A_{\tilde{\alpha}}^{*}\underline{B}h&\text{if }\alpha\neq 0\end{cases}

Let
x=D_{E}h
for
h\in\mathcal{H}_{A}
. Then
\theta_{C,E}x=(I\otimes\gamma)\theta_{A}x
. Hence,

(2.5)

\displaystyle(\theta_{C,E})_{(\alpha)}x=\gamma{\theta_{A}}_{(\alpha)}x

By von Neumann inequality
[
13
]
for non commutative operators, we have

\displaystyle{M_{C,E}}

\displaystyle=\text{SOT}-\underset{r\rightarrow 1}{\lim}M_{C,E}(rR)\text{ for }r\in[0,1)

\displaystyle M_{C,E}

\displaystyle(rR)=\sum_{\alpha\in\tilde{\Lambda}}r^{|\alpha|}R_{\alpha}\otimes\theta_{\alpha}

By Equations (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we obtain

(2.6)

\displaystyle{M_{C,E}(rR)(I_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{E})|_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}}=

\displaystyle I_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{C}-(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma D_{*,A})\left(I_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{H}_{E}}-r\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\sum}}R_{i}\otimes A_{i}^{*}\right)^{-1}(I_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{*,A}\gamma^{*}D_{C})

(2.7)

\displaystyle{M_{C,E}(rR)(I_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{E})|_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A}}}=

\displaystyle-I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma\underline{A}D_{A}+(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma D_{*,A})\left(I_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{H}_{E}}-r\underset{i=1}{\overset{d}{\sum}}R_{i}\otimes A_{i}^{*}\right)^{-1}r(\underline{R}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}_{E}})(I_{\Gamma}\otimes D_{A}^{2})

For a row contraction
\underline{T}=(T_{1},T_{2},\ldots,T_{n})
, we denote
(I_{\Gamma}\otimes T_{1},\ldots,I_{\Gamma}\otimes T_{n})
by
\underline{T}_{\Gamma}.
Also
I_{\Gamma}\otimes\gamma
and
(R_{1}\otimes I_{H},\ldots,R_{n}\otimes I_{H})
are denoted by
\gamma_{\Gamma}
and
\underline{R}_{H}.
Then

(2.8)

\displaystyle M_{C,E}(rR)D_{E_{\Gamma}}|_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}}=

\displaystyle D_{C_{\Gamma}}-\gamma_{\Gamma}D_{*,A_{\Gamma}}\left(I-r\underline{R}_{H}\underline{A}^{*}_{\Gamma}\right)^{-1}D_{*,A_{\Gamma}}\gamma^{*}_{\Gamma}D_{C_{\Gamma}},

(2.9)

\displaystyle{M_{C,E}(rR)D_{E_{\Gamma}}|_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{H}_{A}}}=

\displaystyle\left(-\gamma_{\Gamma}\underline{A}_{\Gamma}+\gamma_{\Gamma}D_{*,A_{\Gamma}}(I-r\underline{R}_{H}\underline{A}_{\Gamma}^{*})^{-1}r\underline{R}_{H}D_{A_{\Gamma}}\right)D_{A_{\Gamma}}.

For a row contraction
\underline{A}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, we denote the characteristic function of
\underline{A}
(cf.
[
12
]
) by
M_{A}
. In
[
13
]
, it is shown that
M_{A}=\text{SOT}-\underset{r\rightarrow 1}{\lim}M_{A}(rR)
where

M_{A}(rR)=-\underline{A}_{\Gamma}+D_{*,A_{\Gamma}}(I-r\underline{R}_{H}\underline{A}_{\Gamma}^{*})^{-1}r\underline{R}_{H}D_{A_{\Gamma}},

and its symbol
\theta_{A}:\mathcal{D}_{A}\rightarrow\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{*,A}
is defined by
\theta_{A}=M_{A}|_{e_{0}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{A}}.
Forthcoming result is used very frequently in the subsequent sections.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
9
, Lemma 2.1]

For a row contraction
\underline{A}
on
\mathcal{H}
and for
r\in[0,1)
, we have

D_{*,A_{\Gamma}}(I-r\underline{R}_{H}\underline{A}_{\Gamma}^{*})^{-1}D_{*,A_{\Gamma}}=I_{\mathcal{D}_{*,A_{\Gamma}}}+M_{A}(rR)\underline{A}_{\Gamma}^{*}.

3.
Factorization of an iterated lifting

In this section, our aim is to factorize the characteristic function of an iterative lifting. If we start with a row contraction
\underline{C}
and consider a contractive lifting
\underline{E}
of
\underline{C}
by
\underline{A}
and
\underline{E}^{\prime}
is a contractive lifting of
\underline{E}
by
\underline{A}^{\prime}
, then we want to factorize
M_{C,E^{\prime}}
in terms of
M_{A}
and
M_{A^{\prime}}
.

We start with a result, which gives a relation between the defect spaces of contractions appearing in a lifting.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\underline{C}=[C_{1},\ldots,C_{d}]
and
\underline{A}=[A_{1},\ldots,A_{d}]
be two row contractions on Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{C}
and
\mathcal{H}_{A}
, respectively. Suppose
\delta\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{*,A},\mathcal{D}_{C})
is a contraction and
\underline{E}
is a contractive lifting of
\underline{C}
by
\underline{A}
on
\mathcal{H}_{E}=\mathcal{H}_{C}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{A}
such that

E_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}C_{i}&0\\
(D_{*,A}\delta^{*}D_{C})_{i}&A_{i}\end{bmatrix}\text{ for }i=1,2,\ldots d.

Then there exist a unitary
\sigma_{E}:\mathcal{D}_{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{*,\delta}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{A}
such that

\sigma_{E}D_{E}=\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\delta}D_{C}&0\\
-A^{*}\delta^{*}D_{C}&D_{A}\end{bmatrix}.

Proof.

Let
h=h_{C}\oplus h_{A}\in\mathcal{H}_{C}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{A}
. Then

\displaystyle\|D_{E}h\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\|h\|^{2}-\|\underline{E}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|h_{C}\|^{2}+\|h_{A}\|^{2}-\|\underline{C}h_{C}\|^{2}-\|D_{*,A}\delta^{*}D_{C}h_{C}+\underline{A}h_{A}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|D_{C}h_{C}\|^{2}+\|h_{A}\|^{2}-\|D_{*,A}\delta^{*}D_{C}h_{C}\|^{2}-\|\underline{A}h_{A}\|^{2}-\langle D_{*,A}\delta^{*}D_{C}h_{C},\underline{A}h_{A}\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\underline{A}h_{A},D_{*,A}\delta^{*}D_{C}h_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|D_{C}h_{C}\|^{2}+\|D_{A}h_{A}\|^{2}-\|\delta^{*}D_{C}h_{C}\|^{2}+\|\underline{A}^{*}\delta^{*}D_{C}h_{C}\|^{2}-\langle\delta^{*}D_{C}h_{C},D_{*,A}\underline{A}h_{A}\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle D_{*,A}\underline{A}h_{A},\delta^{*}D_{C}h_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|D_{*,\delta}D_{C}h_{C}\|^{2}+\|D_{A}h_{A}\|^{2}+\|\underline{A}^{*}\delta^{*}D_{C}h_{C}\|^{2}-\langle\delta^{*}D_{C}h_{C},\underline{A}D_{A}h_{A}\rangle-\langle\underline{A}D_{A}h_{A},\delta^{*}D_{C}h_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|D_{*,\delta}D_{C}h_{C}\|^{2}+\|D_{A}h_{A}\|^{2}+\|\underline{A}^{*}\delta^{*}D_{C}h_{C}\|^{2}-\langle\underline{A}^{*}\delta^{*}D_{C}h_{C},D_{A}h_{A}\rangle-\langle D_{A}h_{A},\underline{A}^{*}\delta^{*}D_{C}h_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|D_{*,\delta}D_{C}h_{C}\|+\|D_{A}h_{A}-\underline{A}^{*}\delta^{*}D_{C}h_{C}\|^{2}.

Thus

\sigma_{E}D_{E}=\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,\delta}D_{C}&0\\
-\underline{A}^{*}\delta^{*}D_{C}&D_{A}\end{bmatrix}

is an isometry from
\mathcal{D}_{E}
onto
\mathcal{D}_{*,\delta}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{A}
.
∎

Similar to the previous result, the following lemma gives another relation between the defect spaces of contractions appearing in a lifting.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\underline{C},\,\underline{A},\,\delta
and
\underline{E}
be as defined in Lemma
3.1
. Then there exist a unitary
\sigma^{\prime}_{E}:\mathcal{D}_{*,E}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{*,C}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{\delta}
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}_{E}D_{*,E}=\begin{bmatrix}D_{*,C}&-\underline{C}\delta D_{*,A}\\
0&D_{\delta}D_{*,A}\end{bmatrix}.

Proof.

Let
h=h_{C}\oplus h_{A}\in\mathcal{H}_{C}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{A}
. Then

\displaystyle\|D_{*,E}h\|^{2}=

\displaystyle\|h\|^{2}-\|\underline{E}^{*}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|h_{C}\|^{2}+\|h_{A}\|^{2}-\|\underline{C}^{*}h_{C}+D_{C}\delta D_{*,A}h_{A}\|^{2}-\|\underline{A}^{*}h_{A}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|h_{C}\|^{2}+\|D_{*,A}h_{A}\|^{2}-\|D_{C}\delta D_{*,A}h_{A}\|^{2}-\|\underline{C}^{*}h_{C}\|^{2}-\langle D_{C}\delta D_{*,A}h_{A},\underline{C}^{*}h_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\underline{C}^{*}h_{C},D_{C}\delta D_{*,A}h_{A}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|D_{*,C}h_{C}\|^{2}+\|D_{*,A}h_{A}\|^{2}-\|\delta D_{*,A}h_{A}\|^{2}+\|\underline{C}\delta D_{*,A}h_{A}\|^{2}-\langle\delta D_{*,A}h_{A},D_{C}\underline{C}^{*}h_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle D_{C}\underline{C}^{*}h_{C},\delta D_{*,A}h_{A}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|D_{*,C}h_{C}\|^{2}+\|D_{\delta}D_{*,A}h_{A}\|^{2}+\|\underline{C}\delta D_{*,A}h_{A}\|^{2}-\langle\delta D_{*,A}h_{A},\underline{C}^{*}D_{*,C}h_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle\underline{C}^{*}D_{*,C}h_{C},\delta D_{*,A}h_{A}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|D_{*,C}h_{C}\|^{2}+\|D_{\delta}D_{*,A}h_{A}\|^{2}+\|\underline{C}\delta D_{*,A}h_{A}\|^{2}-\langle\underline{C}\delta D_{*,A}h_{A},D_{*,C}h_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle D_{*,C}h_{C},\underline{C}\delta D_{*,A}h_{A}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|D_{*,C}h_{C}-\underline{C}\delta D_{*,A}h_{A}\|^{2}+\|D_{\delta}D_{*,A}h_{A}\|^{2}.

This gives the required isometry from

\displaystyle{\tilde{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}{M_{A}}&0\\

If

is a purely contractive multi-analytic operator, then there exist a unitary operator
\phi:\mathcal{D}_{*,\hat{A}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{*,A}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{*}

{M_{C,E^{\prime}}}
of the lifting

.

Let
{U}^{*}:=\begin{bmatrix}P&Q\\

,

(3.9)

f^{\prime}_{*}={U}{U}^{*}f^{\prime}_{*}={Q}^{*}{Q}f^{\prime}_{*}.

If we denote

3.9

.

and

to
\mathcal{D}_{{R}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{*}
to
\mathcal{D}_{*,{R}}

\displaystyle{U}=\begin{bmatrix}P^{*}&{R}^{*}\\

\underline{E}^{\prime}
be a minimal contractive lifting of
\underline{E}
. Define

(4.1)

\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{E}}:=\overline{span}\{{E^{\prime}_{\alpha}}x:x\in\mathcal{H}_{C},\,\alpha\in\tilde{\Lambda}\}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\tilde{\underline{E}}={\underline{E}}^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{E}}}.

Consider
\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{A}}=\mathcal{H}_{E^{\prime}}\ominus\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{E}}
, and define

\underline{\tilde{X}}=P_{\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{E}}}\underline{E}^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{A}}}
and

\underline{\tilde{A}}=P_{\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{A}}}\underline{E}^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{A}}}
. Then

\underline{E}^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}\underline{\tilde{E}}&\underline{\tilde{X}}\\
\underline{0}&\underline{\tilde{A}}\end{bmatrix}.

As
\underline{E}^{\prime}
is a row contraction, we have

(4.2)

\tilde{\underline{X}}=D_{*,\tilde{E}}\gamma D_{\tilde{A}}

for some contraction
\gamma\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{A}},\mathcal{D}_{*,\tilde{E}})
.
By Nagy-Foias (see
[
10
]
) there exist a unitary

\sigma:\mathcal{D}_{E^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{E}}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}
, such that

(4.3)

\sigma D_{E^{\prime}}=\begin{bmatrix}D_{\tilde{E}}&-\tilde{E}^{*}\gamma D_{\tilde{A}}\\
0&D_{\gamma}D_{\tilde{A}}\end{bmatrix}.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

\underline{E}
be a minimal contractive lifting of a row contraction
\underline{C}
and

\underline{E}^{\prime}
be a minimal contractive lifting of
\underline{E}
. If
\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{E}}
and
\underline{\tilde{E}}
are as defined in Equation
4.1
,
then
\tilde{\underline{E}}
is a minimal contractive lifting of
\underline{C}
and
M_{C,\tilde{E}}=M_{C,E}M_{E,E^{\prime}}(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1}|_{\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{E}}})
.

Proof.

From the definition of
\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{E}}
, it is easy to see that
\mathcal{H}_{C}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{E}}
and
\underline{\tilde{E}}
is a lifting of
\underline{C}
. Also,

\overline{span}\{\tilde{E}_{\alpha}x:x\in\mathcal{H}_{C}\}=\overline{span}\{{E^{\prime}_{\alpha}}x:x\in\mathcal{H}_{C}\}=\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{E}}.

Thus
\tilde{\underline{E}}
is a minimal contractive lifting of
\underline{C}
.

By Definition
2.4
, we know that the characteristic functions
M_{C,E}
and
M_{E,E^{\prime}}
are given by

M_{C,E}=P_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{C}}W_{1}|_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{E}}\text{ and }M_{E,E^{\prime}}=P_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{E}}W_{2}|_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{E^{\prime}}},

where

W_{1}:\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{E}\rightarrow\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{C}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{1}
and
W_{2}:\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{E^{\prime}}\rightarrow\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{E}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{2}
are unitary operators, as in Equation (
2.1
), satisfying

(4.4)

\displaystyle W_{1}{V_{i}}^{E}=(V^{C}\oplus Y_{1})_{i}W_{1},\,W_{1}|_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}=I_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}\text{ and }W_{2}{V_{i}}^{E^{\prime}}=(V^{E}\oplus Y_{2})_{i}W_{2},\,W_{2}|_{\mathcal{H}_{E}}=I_{\mathcal{H}_{E}},

for
i=1,2,\ldots,d.

Now, we define
Z:=(W_{1}\oplus I_{\mathcal{K}_{2}})W_{2}:{\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{E^{\prime}}}\rightarrow\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{C}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{2}
. Note that
Z
is a unitary operator, as it is a composition of two unitary operators. By Equation (
4.4
), we have

Z|_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}=I_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}\text{ and }Z{V_{i}}^{E^{\prime}}=(V^{C}\oplus Y_{1}\oplus Y_{2})_{i}Z\;\;\text{for all}\;i=1,2,..,d.

It is easy to see that

(4.5)

M_{C,E}M_{E,E^{\prime}}=P_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{C}}Z|_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{E^{\prime}}}.

Let the unitary

\sigma:\mathcal{D}_{E^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{E}}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{\gamma}
be as defined in Equation (
4.3
).
Thus
Z(\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{E^{\prime}})=(W_{1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}_{2}})W_{2}(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1})((\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{E}})\oplus(\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{\gamma})).

We claim that
P_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{C}}Z(\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{E^{\prime}})=P_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{C}}Z(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1})(\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{E}})
. To verify the claim, it is enough to show that
Z(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1})(e_{0}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{\gamma})
is orthogonal to
e_{0}\otimes\mathcal{D}_{C}
. For
h_{A}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
h_{C}\in\mathcal{H}_{C}
,

\displaystyle\langle Z(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1})(e_{0}\otimes D_{\gamma}h_{A}),(e_{0}\otimes(D_{C})_{i}h_{C})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle W_{2}(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1})(e_{0}\otimes D_{\gamma}h_{A}),W_{1}^{*}(e_{0}\otimes(D_{C})_{i}h_{C})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle W_{2}(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1})(e_{0}\otimes D_{\gamma}h_{A}),W_{1}^{*}({V_{i}}^{C}h_{C}-C_{i}h_{C})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle W_{2}(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1})(e_{0}\otimes D_{\gamma}h_{A}),{V_{i}}^{E}W_{1}^{*}h_{C}-W_{1}^{*}C_{i}h_{C})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle W_{2}(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1})(e_{0}\otimes D_{\gamma}h_{A}),{V_{i}}^{E}h_{C}-C_{i}h_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1})(e_{0}\otimes D_{\gamma}h_{A}),W_{2}^{*}({V_{i}}^{E}h_{C}-C_{i}h_{C})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1})(e_{0}\otimes D_{\gamma}h_{A}),{V_{i}}^{E^{\prime}}W_{2}^{*}h_{C}-W_{2}^{*}C_{i}h_{C})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1})(e_{0}\otimes D_{\gamma}h_{A}),{V_{i}}^{E^{\prime}}h_{C}-{C_{i}}h_{C})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle e_{0}\otimes\sigma^{-1}D_{\gamma}h_{A},{E_{i}}^{\prime}h_{C}-C_{i}h_{C}+e_{0}\otimes(D_{E^{\prime}})_{i}h_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle D_{\gamma}h_{A},\sigma(D_{E^{\prime}})_{i}h_{C}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle D_{\gamma}h_{A},(D_{\tilde{E}})_{i}h_{C}\rangle=0.

Consider the unitary operator
Z_{1}:\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{\tilde{E}}\rightarrow\hat{\mathcal{H}}_{C}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{2}
defined by

Z_{1}:=Z(I_{\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{E}}}\oplus(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1}|_{\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{E}}})).

For
h_{\tilde{E}}\oplus\sum_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}\otimes D_{\tilde{E}}h_{\alpha}\in\hat{H}_{\tilde{E}}
, we have

\displaystyle Z_{1}{V_{i}}^{\tilde{E}}\left(h_{\tilde{E}}\oplus\sum_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}\otimes D_{\tilde{E}}h_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=Z_{1}\left(\tilde{E}_{i}h_{\tilde{E}}\oplus[e_{0}\otimes D_{\tilde{E}i}h_{\tilde{E}}+e_{i}\otimes\sum_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}\otimes D_{\tilde{E}}h_{\alpha}]\right)

\displaystyle=Z(I_{\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{E}}}\oplus(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1}))\left(\tilde{E}_{i}h_{\tilde{E}}\oplus[e_{0}\otimes D_{\tilde{E}i}h_{\tilde{E}}+e_{i}\otimes\sum_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}\otimes D_{\tilde{E}}h_{\alpha}]\right)

\displaystyle=Z\left(E^{\prime}_{i}h_{\tilde{E}}\oplus[e_{0}\otimes D_{E^{\prime}i}h_{\tilde{E}}+e_{i}\otimes\sum_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}\otimes D_{E^{\prime}}h_{\alpha}]\right)

\displaystyle=Z{V_{i}}^{E^{\prime}}\left(h_{\tilde{E}}\oplus\sum_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}\otimes D_{E^{\prime}}h_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=(V^{C}\oplus Y_{1}\oplus Y_{2})_{i}Z\left(h_{\tilde{E}}\oplus\sum_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}\otimes D_{E^{\prime}}h_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=(V^{C}\oplus Y_{1}\oplus Y_{2})_{i}Z\left(I_{\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{E}}}\oplus\left(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1}\right)\right)\left(h_{\tilde{E}}\oplus\sum_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}\otimes D_{\tilde{E}}h_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=(V^{C}\oplus Y_{1}\oplus Y_{2})_{i}Z_{1}\left(h_{\tilde{E}}\oplus\sum_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}\otimes D_{\tilde{E}}h_{\alpha}\right).

Thus we obtained

Z_{1}{V_{i}}^{\tilde{E}}=(V^{C}\oplus Y_{1}\oplus Y_{2})_{i}Z_{1}\;\;\text{for all}\;i=1,2,...,d

Also, we have
Z_{1}|_{\mathcal{H}_{C}}=I
. By definition
2.4
, the characteristic function for the minimal lifting
\underline{\tilde{E}}
of
\underline{C}
is

M_{C,\tilde{E}}=P_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{C}}Z_{1}|_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{E}}}.

From Equation (
4.5
), we have

\displaystyle M_{C,\tilde{E}}

\displaystyle=P_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{C}}Z(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1}|_{\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{E}}})

\displaystyle=P_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{C}}Z|_{\Gamma\otimes\mathcal{D}_{E^{\prime}}}(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1}|_{\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{E}}})

\displaystyle=M_{C,E}M_{E,E^{\prime}}(I_{\Gamma}\otimes\sigma^{-1}|_{\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{E}}}).

This completes the proof.
∎

The following example validates the Theorem
4.1
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
C,\,A,\,A^{\prime}=0
be zero operators on
\mathcal{H}_{C},\,\mathcal{H}_{A},\,\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime}}=\mathbb{C}
, respectively. Consider the lifting
E
of
C
and
E^{\prime}
of
E
defined by

E=\begin{bmatrix}C&0\\
B&A\end{bmatrix}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}\text{ and }E^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}E&0\\
B^{\prime}&A^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0\\
1&0&0\\
1&0&0\end{bmatrix}

on
\mathcal{H}_{E}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
and
\mathcal{H}_{E^{\prime}}=\mathbb{C}^{3}
, respectively.
Then
E^{\prime}
is also a lifting for
C
and

E^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}C&0\\
B^{\prime}_{C}&A^{\prime}_{C}\end{bmatrix},\text{
where }B^{\prime}_{C}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\end{bmatrix}\text{ and }A^{\prime}_{C}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}.

It is easy to see that
E
is a minimal contractive lifting of
C
and
E^{\prime}
is a minimal contractive lifting of
E
. We know

\overline{\text{span}}\{{E^{\prime}}^{n}x:x\in\mathcal{H}_{C}\;\text{and}\;n\geq 0\}=\text{span}\left\{(1,0,0),\left(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right\}\neq\mathcal{H}_{E^{\prime}}.

Thus
E^{\prime}
is not a minimal contractive lifting of
C
.

Also note that
D_{C}=I_{\mathbb{C}}
,
D_{*,A}=I_{\mathbb{C}}
,
D_{*,A^{\prime}}=I_{\mathbb{C}}
and

{D_{E}}=\sqrt{I_{\mathcal{H}_{E}}-\underline{E}^{\ast}\underline{E}}\\
=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix},\,D_{E^{\prime}}=\sqrt{I_{\mathcal{H}_{E^{\prime}}}-E^{\prime\ast}E^{\prime}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}.

We have
B^{*}={D_{C}}\gamma D_{*,A}
and
{B^{\prime}}^{\ast}={D_{E}}\gamma^{\prime}D_{*,A^{\prime}}
, which implies
\gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
and
\gamma^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\end{bmatrix}
.

By Equations (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we have the following for
h_{C}\in\mathcal{H}_{C}
and
h_{A}\in\mathcal{H}_{A}
:

\displaystyle\theta_{C,E}(D_{E})h_{C}=e_{0}\otimes\frac{h_{C}}{2}\text{ and }\theta_{C,E}(D_{E})h_{A}=e_{1}\otimes\frac{h_{A}}{\sqrt{2}}.

Similarly for
h_{E}=\begin{bmatrix}h_{C}\\
h_{A}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{H}_{E}
and
h_{A^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime}}
, we have

\displaystyle\theta_{E,E^{\prime}}(D_{E^{\prime}})h_{E}=e_{0}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}0\\
h_{A}\end{bmatrix}\text{ and }\theta_{E,E^{\prime}}(D_{E^{\prime}})h_{A^{\prime}}=e_{1}\otimes\begin{bmatrix}h_{A^{\prime}}\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

As we have already mentioned,
E^{\prime}
is a contractive lifting for
C
, thus

({B^{\prime}_{C}})^{*}=D_{C}\gamma^{\prime}_{C}D_{*,A_{C}}
. This implies
\gamma^{\prime}_{C}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\end{bmatrix}
and

\displaystyle\theta_{C,E^{\prime}}(D_{E^{\prime}})h_{C}

\displaystyle=0,\text{ for }h_{C}\in\mathcal{H}_{C},

\displaystyle\theta_{C,E^{\prime}}(D_{E^{\prime}})h_{{A^{\prime}_{C}}}

\displaystyle=e_{1}\otimes\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(h_{A}+h_{A^{\prime}})\text{ for }h_{{A^{\prime}_{C}}}=\begin{bmatrix}h_{A}\\
h_{A^{\prime}}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathcal{H}_{A^{\prime}_{C}}.

We can also represent
\theta_{C,E}
,
\theta_{E,E^{\prime}}
and
\theta_{C,E^{\prime}}
in the following matrix form:

\theta_{C,E}(z)=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&\frac{z}{\sqrt{2}}\end{bmatrix},\,\theta_{E,E^{\prime}}(z)=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&z\\
0&1&0\end{bmatrix},\,\theta_{C,E}\theta_{E,E^{\prime}}(z)=\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac{z}{\sqrt{2}}&\frac{z}{\sqrt{2}}\end{bmatrix}.

Now consider,

\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{E}}=\overline{\text{span}}\{{E^{\prime}}^{n}x:x\in\mathcal{H}_{C}\;\text{and}\;n\geq 0\}=\text{span}\left\{(1,0,0),\left(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right\}.

Then
\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{E}}=\mathcal{H}_{C}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{A}}
where
\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{A}}=\text{span }\left\{(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})\right\}.
To write
E^{\prime}
in terms of the basis

\{(1,0,0)(0,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})(0,-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})\},

consider the unitary operator

P=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\end{bmatrix},

and obtain

P^{-1}E^{\prime}P=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix}

Since
\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{E}}
is a reducing subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{E^{\prime}}
w.r.t.
\underline{E^{\prime}},
we identify

\displaystyle\tilde{E}=E^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{E}}}=\begin{bmatrix}C&0\\
\tilde{B}&\tilde{A}\end{bmatrix}

with

\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}.

We have
D_{*,\tilde{A}}=I_{\mathbb{C}},

D_{\tilde{E}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\mbox{~and~}\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{E}}=\{(0,k):k\in\mathbb{C}\}.

Since
\tilde{B}^{*}=D_{C}\tilde{\gamma}D_{*,\tilde{A}}
, that implies
\tilde{\gamma}=1
. Again using Equations (
2.2
) and (
2.9
), we get

\displaystyle\theta_{C,\tilde{E}}(D_{\tilde{E}})h_{C}

\displaystyle=0,\text{ and }\theta_{C,\tilde{E}}(D_{\tilde{E}})h_{\tilde{A}}=e_{1}\otimes h_{\tilde{A}}\text{ for }h_{C}\in\mathcal{H}_{C},\,h_{\tilde{A}}\in\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{A}}.

In other words,

\displaystyle\theta_{C,\tilde{E}}(z)

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}0&z\end{bmatrix}.

We also have,

\displaystyle\theta_{C,E}\theta_{E,E^{\prime}}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\end{bmatrix}|_{\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{E}}}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}0&z\end{bmatrix}.

Thus

\theta_{C,\tilde{E}}=\theta_{C,E}\theta_{E,E^{\prime}}\sigma^{-1}|_{\mathcal{D}_{\tilde{E}}},

where
\sigma^{-1}=P
.

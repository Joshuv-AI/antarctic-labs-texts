Minimal-norm solution to the Fredholm integral equations of the first kind via the H - H K H_{K} formulation Journal: Journal of L a T e X Templates
Renjun Qiu, Ming Xu, Wei Qu

Abstract
The Fredholm integral equations of the first kind is a typical ill-posed problem, so that it is usually difficult to obtain its analytical minimal-norm solution. This paper gives a closed-form minimal-norm solution for the degenerate kernel equations based on the H - H K H_{K} formulation. Furthermore, it has been shown that the structure of solutions to degenerate kernel equations and matrix equations are consistent. Subsequently, the obtained results are extended to non-degenerate integral equations. Finally, the validity and applicability of the proposed method are demonstrated by some examples.



1
Introduction

Consider the Fredholm integral equations of the first kind

\int_{E}k(x,t)u(t)dt=f(x),~x\in D,

(1.1)

where the function
u(t)
is unknown and to be determined,
k(x,t)
and
f(x)
are given. Usually in the sense of
L^{2}-
norm, Eq. (
1.1
) is ill-posed, in other words, it does not satisfy one of the Hadamard conditions at least

1.

Eq. (
1.1
) has a solution
u(t)
,

2.

Solution
u(t)
is unique, and

3.

Continuous dependence of
u(t)
on
f(x)
.

Therefore it is difficult to find feasible numerical solutions, let alone feasible analytical solutions. To address this problem, from another perspective, literatures
golbabai2008modified
,
Wazwaz2011regularization
,
golbabai2009solution
,
alturk2019use
,
alturk2016regularization
,
yuldashev2023nonlinear
have given the analytical solutions for a special type of the integral kernel, i.e., degenerate kernel

k(x,t)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}(x)h_{i}(t),n\geq 1.

(1.2)

In reality, as long as one obtains analytical solutions of the degenerate kernel Fredholm integral equation (DKFIE), then solutions of the non-degenerate kernel Fredholm integral equation (NDKFIE) can be approximated well
qiu2022solving
,
qiu2022best
. On account of this reason, exploring analytical solutions of the DKFIE (
2.1
) is very necessary to a NDKFIE (
1.1
), in particular, the analytical minimal-norm solution.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\{g_{i}(x)\}_{i=1}^{n}
and
\{h_{i}(t)\}_{i=1}^{n}
are linearly independent functions in this paper. In despite of literatures have appled the modified homotopy (perturbation) methods
golbabai2008modified
,
alturk2016regularization
,
golbabai2009solution
or regularization methods
Wazwaz2011regularization
,
alturk2019use
,
yuldashev2023nonlinear
to obtain many solutions of the DKFIE, yet not the minimal-norm solution. The motivation of this paper is to excavate a closed-form representation of the minimal-norm solution, furthermore, to explore the structure of solutions to a DKFIE, imitating a linear matrix equation.

To achieve this target, based on the
H
-
H_{k}
formulation given in
qian2020reproducing
, the minimal-norm solution of the DKFIE has been obtained by us, which is a crucial part of the structure of solutions. Next, we give the structure of solutions for a DKFIE. Similar to a linear matrix equation, any solution in which can be decomposed into two parts, i.e., one is in the null space and the other is in the null complement space. Finally, the obtained results are extended form DKFIE to NDKFIE.

2
Preliminaries

For the convenience of writing, Eq. (
1.1
) can be abbreviated as

L(u)(x):=\langle k_{x},u\rangle_{L^{2}(E)}=f(x),

(2.1)

where
k_{x}(t):=k(x,t)=G^{T}(x)H(t)
,
G^{T}(x)=(g_{1}(x),\cdots,g_{n}(x))
,
H^{T}(t)=(h_{1}(t),\cdots,h_{n}(t))
, i.e., Eq. (
2.1
) is a DKFIE.

2.1
Minimal-norm solution

Let
N(L)
and
R(L)
be denoted the null space and range space of
L
,
N(L)^{\perp}
be the null complementary space of
N(L)
, and
P_{N(L)^{\perp}}
be the orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(E)
onto
N(L)^{\perp}
. Let
f(x)\in L^{2}(D)
, a function
u\in L^{2}(E)
of DKFIE (
2.1
) is called a
least-squares solution

pes2023projection
, if

\|Lu-f\|_{L^{2}(D)}=\inf\left\{\|Lv-f\|_{L^{2}(D)}:v\in L^{2}(E)\right\}.

(2.2)

An element
u^{\dagger}\in S
is called the
minimal-norm solution
of the DKFIE (
2.1
), if
\|u^{\dagger}\|_{L^{2}(E)}=\inf\limits_{u\in S}\|u\|_{L^{2}(E)},

here
S
is the set composed of least-squares solutions. Meanwhile,
L^{\dagger}
is referred to as the
Moore-Penrose inverse operator
, if
L^{\dagger}f:=u^{\dagger}
.

For the minimal-norm solution of the NDKFIE (
1.1
), some interesting discussions and remarks may be found in
bechouat2023collocation
,
bechouat2023numerical
.

2.2
H
-
H_{K}
formulation

In this subsection, the
H
-
H_{K}
formulation originated by
qian2020reproducing
is introduced to study a DKFIE (
2.1
), which can establish an isometric isomorphism between
N(L)^{\perp}
and
R(L)
.

Firstly,
N(L)^{\perp}
can be described accurately, which determines the representations of all solutions of the DKFIE (
2.1
).

Lemma 1
.

N(L)^{\perp}
can be represented as

N(L)^{\perp}=span\left\{h_{1}(t),\cdots,h_{n}(t),~t\in E\right\}.

(2.3)

Proof.
Let
u\in N(L)
, since
\{g_{i}(x)\}_{i=1}^{n}
are linearly independent functions in DKFIE (
2.1
), then

\int_{E}h_{i}(t)u(t)dt=0,~i=1,\cdots,n.

(2.4)

That is,
h_{i}(t)\in N(L)^{\perp}
, thereby
span\left\{h_{1}(t),\cdots,h_{n}(t),~t\in E\right\}\subseteq N(L)^{\perp}.

Let
u\in span\left\{h_{1}(t),\cdots,h_{n}(t),~t\in E\right\}^{\perp}
, then Eq. (
2.4
) holds. Consequently, we have

L(u(t))(x)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}(x)\int_{E}h_{i}(t)u(t)dt=0,

that is,
u\in N(L)
,
span\left\{h_{1}(t),\cdots,h_{n}(t),~t\in E\right\}^{\perp}\subseteq N(L)
. Hence
N(L)^{\perp}\subseteq span\left\{h_{1}(t),\cdots,h_{n}(t),~t\in E\right\}
.

\square

Secondly, the range space
R(L)
designated a specific norm becomes a RKHS.

For any given
f_{1},f_{2}\in R(L)
, an inner product can be seen in
qiu2022solving
,
qiu2022best
,
qian2020reproducing
defined by

\left\langle f_{1},f_{2}\right\rangle_{R(L)}\!:=\left\langle L^{\dagger}f_{1},L^{\dagger}f_{2}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(E)}.

(2.5)

Lemma 2
.

Under the inner product (
2.5
),
R(L)
is a RKHS with reproducing kernel defined by

K(x,x^{\prime})=G^{T}(x)HG(x^{\prime}),~x,x^{\prime}\in D.

(2.6)

where
\displaystyle{H:=H_{n\times n}=[h_{ij}]=[\int_{E}h_{i}(t)h_{j}(t)dt]}
. Moreover, there exists
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in D
such that

R(L)=span\left\{K(x,x_{1}),\cdots,K(x,x_{n}),~x\in D\right\}.

(2.7)

Proof.
For given
x^{\prime}\in D
, we have

L(G^{T}(x^{\prime})H(t))(x)=\int_{E}G^{T}(x^{\prime})H(t)H^{T}(t)G(x)dt=G^{T}(x)HG(x^{\prime})=K(x,x^{\prime}).

Note that
K_{x^{\prime}}(x):=K(x,x^{\prime})
, then
L(G^{T}(x^{\prime})H(t))=K_{x^{\prime}}
. By lemma
1
, we get

G^{T}(x^{\prime})H(t)\in N(L)^{{}^{\perp}}.

For any
f(x)\in R(L)
, there exists
C\in R^{n}
such that
L(C^{T}H(t))(x)=f(x)
, i.e.,

G^{T}(x)HC=f(x),

then we have

\left\langle f,K_{x}\right\rangle_{R(L)}=\left\langle C^{T}H(t),G^{T}(x)H(t)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(E)}=G^{T}(x)HC=f(x).

That is to say,
K
defined by Eq. (
2.6
) is a reproducing kernel in
R(L)
, and
R(L)
is a RKHS.

Since

\{g_{i}(x)\}_{i=1}^{n}
are linearly independent, then there exists
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in D
such that

(g_{1}(x_{1}),\cdots,g_{n}(x_{n}))

becomes an invertible matrix, then
R(L)=span\left\{K(x,x_{1}),\cdots,K(x,x_{n}),~x\in D\right\}
by Eq. (
2.6
).
\square

3
Structure of solutions

Note that
\displaystyle{F:=F_{n\times 1}=[f_{k}]=[\int_{E}f(x)h_{k}(t)dt]}
,
\displaystyle{A:=A_{n\times n}=[a_{ij}]=[\int_{E}h_{i}(t)g_{j}(t)dt]}
. Based on these notations, the following analytic solution has been obtained for literature
alturk2019use
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
A
be invertible and
f(x)=(A^{-1}F)^{T}G(x)
, then DKFIE (
2.1
) has a solution

u(x)=((A^{-1})^{2}F)^{T}G(x),

(3.1)

\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=((A^{-1})^{2}F)^{T}G((A^{-1})^{2}F),

(3.2)

where
\displaystyle{G:=G_{n\times n}=[g_{ij}]=[\int_{E}g_{i}(x)g_{j}(x)dx]}
.

This analytical solution (
3.1
) is available, which is not our target. Our targets are twofold, one is to give the minimal-norm solution and structure of solutions of the DKFIE, the other is to extend the results obtained to a NDKFIE. To achieve these targets, we assume that functions
\left\{\varphi_{i}(t)\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
are a basis in
N(L)
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
A
be invertible and
f(x)=(A^{-1}F)^{T}G(x)
, then DKFIE (
2.1
) has the minimal-norm solution

u^{\dagger}(t)=(H^{-1}A^{-1}F)^{T}H(t),

(3.3)

\|u^{\dagger}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=(A^{-1}F)^{T}H^{-1}(A^{-1}F).

(3.4)

Moreover, the structure of solutions can be represented as

u(t)=u^{\dagger}(t)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}\varphi_{i}(t),

(3.5)

where the real sequences
\left\{c_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\in l^{2}
, i.e.,
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}c^{2}_{i}<+\infty
.

\mathbf{Proof.}
By
f(x)=(A^{-1}F)^{T}G(x)
, DKFIE (
2.1
) is solvable, i.e.,
f(x)\in R(L)
. By lemma
2
, there exists
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in D
, as well as matrix
U
such that

G(x)=UK_{X}(x),

where
K^{T}_{X}(x)=(K(x,x_{1}),\cdots,K(x,x_{n}))
. Based on Eq. (
2.6
), we get

K_{X}(x)=G_{X}^{T}HG(x),

where
G_{X}:=[g_{i}(x_{j})]
. Hence, we get
U=H^{-1}(G_{X}^{T})^{-1}
and

f(x)=F^{T}(A^{-1})^{T}H^{-1}(G_{X}^{T})^{-1}K_{X}(x).

Based on the
H
-
H_{K}
formulation, for given
x_{i}\in D,~1\leq i\leq n
, we have

L[G^{T}(x_{i})H(t)]=K(x,x_{i}),~L^{\dagger}K_{X}(x)=G_{X}^{T}H(t).

According to the
H
-
H_{K}
formulation again, we get

u^{\dagger}(t)=L^{\dagger}f(x)=F^{T}(A^{-1})^{T}H^{-1}(G_{X}^{T})^{-1}L^{\dagger}K_{X}(x)=(H^{-1}A^{-1}F)^{T}H(t).

\|u^{\dagger}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\int_{E}(H^{-1}A^{-1}F)^{T}H(t)H^{T}(t)H^{-1}A^{-1}Fdt=(A^{-1}F)^{T}H^{-1}(A^{-1}F).

Since
\left\{\varphi_{i}(t)\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a basis in
N(L)
, as well as
\left\{h_{i}(t)\right\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a basis in
N(L)^{\perp}
, then

\left\{h_{i}(t)\right\}_{i=1}^{n}\cup\left\{\varphi_{i}(t)\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}

(3.6)

is a complete basis in
L^{2}(E)
, then any solution
u(t)
can be decomposed as

u(t)=u^{\dagger}(t)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}\varphi_{i}(t),

where the real sequences
\left\{c_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\in l^{2}
.
\square

Actually, according to the process of proof,
A^{-1}F
can be substituted fully by a given vector
C\in R^{n}
, which can ensure the existence of a solution to DKFIE. Namely, we no longer need
A
invertible in the present paper, see example 3.

Corollary 1
.

Let
f(x)=C^{T}G(x)
for a given vector
C\in R^{n}
in DKFIE (
2.1
), then

u^{\dagger}(t)=C^{T}H^{-1}H(t),

(3.7)

\|u^{\dagger}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=C^{T}H^{-1}C.

(3.8)

Corollary 2
.

Let
C=A^{-1}F
in corollary
1
and
G(x)=KH(x)
for a given invertible matrix
K
, then (
3.1
) and (
3.3
), as well as (
3.2
) and (
3.4
) are consistent.

Finally, for a general NDKFIE, the minimal-norm solution can be obtained by discussing analogously under the
H
-
H_{K}
formulation. In fact, assuming that

k(x,t)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}g_{i}(x)h_{i}(t),

(3.9)

f(x)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}g_{i}(x).

(3.10)

Theorem 2
.

Under the assumptions of Eq. (
3.9
) and Eq. (
3.10
), then NDKFIE (
1.1
) has the minimal-norm solution

u^{\dagger}(t)=C^{T}H^{-1}H(t),~

(3.11)

\|u^{\dagger}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=C^{T}H^{-1}C,

(3.12)

where
C=[c_{i}]
and
H(t)
are infinite dimensional column vectors, and
H
is an infinite dimensional matrix.

This theorem extends corollary
1
from DKFIE to NDKFIE, which is the desired result, see example 5. The proof of this theorem can be modified directly on the basis of theorem
1
, so it will not be elaborated here.

Remark 1
.

For DKFIEs, literatures
golbabai2008modified
,
alturk2016regularization
,
golbabai2009solution
,

,

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we study the minimal-norm solution of the Fredholm integral equations of the first kind based on the
H
-
H_{K}
formulation. The basic idea of solving problems is to proceed from simple to difficult, specifically, from DKFIE to NDKFIE. For DKFIE, we obtain a closed-form representation of the minimal-norm solution using an operator method without conventional regularization methods or some other complicated methods. In addition, based on
H
-
H_{K}
formulation, we obtain the spatial structure of
N(L)^{\perp}
and
R(L)
. Fortunately, the results that have been obtained can be extended easily to NDKFIE. Finally, multiple examples show that our proposed methods are feasible and effective.

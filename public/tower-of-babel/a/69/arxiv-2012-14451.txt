Size quantization of an exciton: A toy model of the “dead layer”
M. E. Raikh

Abstract
Size-quantization levels of an exciton in large nanocrystals is studied theoretically. For the nanocrystal size, L L , much bigger than the Bohr radius, a B a_{B} , the level positions do not depend on a B a_{B} . The correction to the levels in a small parameter a B / L a_{B}/L depends on the reflection phase of the exciton from the boundary. Calculation of this phase constitutes a three-body problem: electron, hole, and the boundary. This calculation can be performed analytically in the limit when the hole is much heavier than the electron. Physically, a slow motion of the hole towards the boundary takes place in the effective potential created by the fast motion of the electron orbiting the hole and touching the boundary. As a result, the hole is reflected before reaching the boundary. The distance of the closest approach of the hole to the boundary (the dead layer) exceeds a B a_{B} parametrically .

Introduction

A concept of the exciton dead layer
pekar

in Ref.

In the absence of the exciton-photon coupling,

On the theoretical side, microscopic description of the exciton dead layer poses
a challenge, since it is a three-body problem: electron, hole and a boundary.
In other words, while the motion of electron and hole can be separated into
the motion of the center of mass and the relative motion, this separation
is inconsistent with the boundary conditions that the two-particle wave function
turns to zero when electron and hole touch the boundary
individually
.
These no-escape boundary conditions constitute a microscopic origin
of the dead layer.

Below we consider a toy model which allows to derive the dead layer analytically and confirm Eq. (
1

which allows us to capture the electron-hole correlation in the presence of the boundary, is that the hole mass,
m_{h}
, is much bigger than the electron mass,
m_{e}
. Under this assumption, to the first
order in
m_{e}/m_{h}
, the fast motion of electron takes place in the field of a “static” hole.
DelSole

Our main conclusion is that, in the formula Eq. (
1
), the thickness of the dead layer,

II
The model

To maximally simplify the calculations, we assume that the confinement is one-dimensional in the form of two hard walls at
x=\pm\frac{L}{2}
, see Fig.
1
. Moreover, we will replace the Coulomb attraction between the electron and hole by a short-range attraction. Under these assumptions, the Hamiltonian of the model reads

{\hat{H}}=\frac{{\hat{p}}_{e}^{2}}{2m_{e}}+\frac{{\hat{p}}_{h}^{2}}{2m_{h}}-v_{0}\delta(x_{e}-x_{h}),

(2)

where
x_{e}
and
x_{h}
are the coordinates of the electron and hole, respectively;
v_{0}
is the attraction strength.
The wave function
\Psi(x_{e},x_{h})
satisfies the boundary conditions

\Psi\left(\pm\frac{L}{2},x_{h}\right)=0,~~~~\Psi\left(x_{e},\pm\frac{L}{2}\right)=0.

(3)

If
m_{e}
and
m_{h}
were comparable, the conditions Eq. (
3
) would
strongly mix the motion of the center of mass and the relative motion near the boundaries. Thus, to
proceed further, we assume that the hole is much heavier than
the electron and exploit the smallness of the parameter
m_{e}/m_{h}
.

II.1
Lowest order in
m_{e}/m_{h}

In the limit of large
m_{h}
, we can neglect the kinetic energy of
the hole. After that
x_{h}
enters the Hamiltonian
Eq. (
2
) only as a parameter, which can
be viewed as fixed.
Then the general form of the eigenfunction,
\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})
, which turns to zero at
x_{e}=\pm\frac{L}{2}
is the following

\displaystyle\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})=\alpha\sinh\Big[\kappa_{h}\Big(\frac{L}{2}+x_{e}\Big)\Big],~~~-\frac{L}{2}<x_{e}<x_{h}

\displaystyle\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})=\beta\sinh\Big[\kappa_{h}\Big(x_{e}-\frac{L}{2}\Big)\Big],~~~x_{h}<x_{e}<\frac{L}{2}.

(4)

The corresponding energy is
E_{e}(\kappa_{h})=-\frac{\hbar^{2}\kappa_{h}^{2}}{2m_{e}}
.
The condition of continuity at
x_{e}=x_{h}
reads

\alpha\sinh\Big[\kappa_{h}\Big(\frac{L}{2}+x_{h}\Big)\Big]=\beta\sinh\Big[\kappa_{h}\Big(x_{h}-\frac{L}{2}\Big)\Big].

(5)

The discontinuity of the derivative at
x_{e}=x_{h}
yields the second condition

\displaystyle\kappa_{h}\alpha

\displaystyle\cosh\Big[\kappa_{h}\Big(\frac{L}{2}+x_{h}\Big)\Big]-\kappa_{h}\beta\cosh\Big[\kappa_{h}\Big(x_{h}-\frac{L}{2}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=-\frac{2m_{e}v_{0}}{\hbar^{2}}\alpha\sinh\Big[\kappa_{h}\Big(\frac{L}{2}-x_{h}\Big)\Big].

(6)

Combining Eqs. (
5
) and (
II.1
), we get
the equation for
\kappa_{h}

\sinh\kappa_{h}L=\frac{2m_{e}v_{0}}{\hbar^{2}\kappa_{h}}\sinh\Big[\kappa_{h}\Big(\frac{L}{2}-x_{h}\Big)\Big]\sinh\Big[\kappa_{h}\Big(\frac{L}{2}+x_{h}\Big)\Big].

(7)

This equation defines the dependence
\kappa_{h}(x_{h})
, i.e. the dependence of the electron energy on the position of the hole.

The concept of a dead layer is meaningful only when the “nanocrystal” is big, namely,
L
is much bigger than the size of the exciton.
In the limit
\kappa_{h}L\gg 1
and
\big(\frac{L}{2}-x_{h}\big)\ll L
, we can replace

\sinh\kappa_{h}L
by
\frac{1}{2}\exp\big(\kappa_{h}L\big)
and

\sinh\big[\kappa_{h}\big(\frac{L}{2}+x_{h}\big)\big]
by

\frac{1}{2}\exp\big[\kappa_{h}\big(\frac{L}{2}+x_{h}\big)\big]
. Then
Eq. (
7
) assumes the form

\kappa_{h}=\frac{m_{e}v_{0}}{\hbar^{2}}\bigg\{1-\exp\Big[-2\kappa_{h}\Big(\frac{L}{2}-x_{h}\Big)\Big]\bigg\},

(8)

where the exponent in the square brackets accounts for electron “hitting” the right wall and
does not “sense” the left wall.
When the exponent is small, Eq. (
8
) yields

\kappa_{h}=\frac{m_{e}v_{0}}{\hbar^{2}}=\frac{1}{a_{B}},

(9)

and, correspondingly,
E_{B}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{e}a_{B}^{2}}
for the binding energy.

As
x_{h}
approaches the right (or left) wall, the bound state disappears at critical

\frac{L}{2}-x_{h}=\frac{a_{B}}{2}
. Near the threshold, the solution of Eq. (
8
)
behaves linearly with
x_{h}

\kappa_{h}=\frac{\Big(\frac{L}{2}-x_{h}-\frac{a_{B}}{2}\Big)}{\left(\frac{L}{2}-x_{h}\right)^{2}}\approx\frac{4}{a_{B}^{2}}\left[\frac{L}{2}-x_{h}-\frac{a_{B}}{2}\right].

(10)

The corresponding normalized wave functions have the form

\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})=\frac{\left(2\kappa_{h}\right)^{1/2}}{a_{B}}\begin{cases}\bigl(L-2x_{e}\bigr),~x_{h}<x_{e}<\frac{L}{2},\\
a_{B}\exp\bigl[\kappa_{h}(x_{e}-x_{h})\bigl],~x_{e}<x_{h}.\end{cases}

(11)

With
E_{e}(\kappa_{h})
depending on
x_{h}
via Eq. (
8
), a
hole with a
finite
mass will move slowly in the effective potential created by a fast-moving electron. We will establish the form of this potential in the next Section.

III
Effective potential for a hole

To incorporate the hole motion, we search for the solution of the Scrödinger equation
{\hat{H}}(x_{e},x_{h})\Psi=E\Psi

in the form

\Psi(x_{e},x_{h})=\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})\Phi(x_{h}).

(12)

Substitution of this form
into the Schrödinger equation yields

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{h}}\left[\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial x_{h}^{2}}+2\frac{\partial\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})}{\partial x_{h}}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial x_{h}}+\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})}{\partial x_{h}^{2}}\Phi(x_{h})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[E-E_{e}(\kappa_{h})\Big]\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})\Phi(x_{h}).

(13)

In deriving Eq. (
III
) we took into
account that
\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})
satisfies the equation

\left[\frac{{\hat{p}}_{e}^{2}}{2m_{e}}-v_{0}\delta(x_{e}-x_{h})\right]\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})=E_{e}(\kappa_{h})\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e}).

(14)

We assume that the electron wave function
\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})
is normalized

\int dx_{e}\Big(\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})\Big)^{2}=1
. To obtain a closed equation for the
hole wave function,
\Phi(x_{h})
, we multiply Eq. (
III
) by
\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})

and integrate over
x_{e}
. This yields

-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{h}}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial x_{h}^{2}}+2I(x_{h})\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial x_{h}}+J(x_{h})\Phi\right]=\Big[E-E_{e}(\kappa_{h})\Big]\Phi,

(15)

where the functions
I(x_{h})
and
J(x_{h})
are defined as

\displaystyle I(x_{h})=

\displaystyle\int dx_{e}\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})\frac{\partial\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})}{\partial x_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{2\partial x_{h}}\int dx_{e}\Big(\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})\Big)^{2},

(16)

\displaystyle J(x_{h})=

\displaystyle\int dx_{e}\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})}{\partial x_{h}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{2\partial x_{h}^{2}}\int dx_{e}\Big(\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})\Big)^{2}-\int dx_{e}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{h}}\right)^{2}.

(17)

Normalization condition ensures that
I(x_{h})=0
and that the first term in the right-hand side in
the expression for
J(x_{h})
is zero.

The fact that the equation for
\Phi(x_{h})
is closed is a consequence
of our choice Eq. (
12
) of the wave function
\Psi(x_{e},x_{h})
in the form of the product.
By making this choice, we neglected the excited states of the electron wave function.
This choice is justified if the typical electron energy,
E_{e}(\kappa_{h})
, is much
bigger than the size-quantization energy,
\sim\hbar^{2}/m_{h}L^{2}
, of the hole. Upon setting
I(x_{h})=0
we cast Eq. (
18
) into the form of the
Schrödinger equation

-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{h}}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial x_{h}^{2}}+V(x_{h})\Phi=E\Phi,

(18)

with effective potential,
V(x_{h})
, representing the sum

V(x_{h})=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{h}}\int dx_{e}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})}{\partial x_{h}}\right)^{2}-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{e}}\Big(k_{h}(x_{h})\Big)^{2}.

(19)

IV
Comparing contributions to the effective potential

At distances much bigger than
a_{B}
from the boundary the second term in
V(x_{h})
dominates, we thus have
\kappa_{h}=\frac{1}{a_{B}}
,
so that
V(x_{h})=-E_{B}
. Upon approach to the right boundary
x_{h}=\frac{L}{2}
the term
\frac{\hbar^{2}\kappa_{h}^{2}}{2m_{e}}
falls off and finally vanishes at
x_{h}=\frac{L-a_{B}}{2}
.
Near this point the first term in
V(x_{h})
dominates the effective potential. To
trace the crossover between the first and second terms, it is sufficient to use the asymptotic expressions Eqs. (
10
) and (
11
).
Differentiating
Eq. (
11
) with respect to
x_{e}
we get

\frac{\partial\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})}{\partial x_{h}}=\bigl[1+2\kappa_{h}(x_{e}-x_{h})\bigr]\exp\bigl[\kappa_{h}(x_{e}-x_{h})\bigr]\frac{d\kappa_{h}}{dx_{h}}.

(20)

Expressing
\frac{d\kappa_{h}}{dx_{h}}
from Eq. (
10
) we have

\displaystyle\Biggl(\frac{\partial\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})}{\partial x_{h}}\Biggr)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{8}{\kappa_{h}a_{B}^{4}}\Bigl[1+2\kappa_{h}(x_{e}-x_{h})\Bigr]^{2}\exp\bigl[2\kappa_{h}(x_{e}-x_{h})\bigr].

(21)

Then the integration over
x_{e}
yields

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{h}}\int dx_{e}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi_{x_{h}}(x_{e})}{\partial x_{h}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\hbar^{2}}{m_{h}\kappa_{h}^{2}a_{B}^{4}}=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{8m_{h}\big[L-a_{B}-2x_{h}\big]^{2}}.

(22)

Thus, the first term in
V(x_{h})
falls off quadratically away from the threshold

x_{h}=\frac{1}{2}\left(L-a_{B}\right)
. It should be compared to
the second term,
\frac{\hbar^{2}\kappa_{h}^{2}}{2m_{e}}
. Using the threshold behavior
Eq. (
10
) of
\kappa_{h}
, we have

\frac{\hbar^{2}\kappa_{h}^{2}}{2m_{e}}=\frac{2\hbar^{2}}{m_{e}a_{B}^{4}}\bigg[L-a_{B}-2x_{h}\bigg]^{2}.

(23)

Comparing Eqs. (
IV
) and (
23
)
we conclude that the crossover from the first to the second term
in
V(x_{h})
takes place at

L-a_{B}-2x_{h}=\frac{a_{B}}{2}\left(\frac{m_{e}}{m_{h}}\right)^{1/4}.

(24)

We see that crossover takes place in the threshold region, i.e. at
\left(L-a_{B}-2x_{h}\right)\ll a_{B}
. This is ensured by the smallness of the ratio
m_{e}/m_{h}
. Note, that this smallness also justifies the above use
of the asymptotic expressions for
\varphi_{h}(x_{e})

and for
\kappa_{h}(x_{h})
.

V
Energy levels

Semiclassical quantization condition for the particle with mass,
m_{h}
, moving in the potential,
V(x_{h})
, see Fig.
1
, reads

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{2m_{h}}{\hbar^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\int\limits_{0}^{x_{t}}dx_{h}\Bigg[E_{N}+\frac{\hbar^{2}\bigl(\kappa_{h}(x_{h})\bigr)^{2}}{2m_{e}}\Bigg]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\pi\left(N+\frac{1}{2}\right).

(25)

where
x_{t}
is the turning point defined as

\hbar\kappa_{h}(x_{t})=\left(2m_{e}|E_{N}|\right)^{1/2}
.
Using Eq. (
8
) we get the following expression for
x_{t}

x_{t}=\frac{L}{2}+\frac{a_{B}}{2}\Biggl(\frac{E_{B}}{|E_{N}|}\Biggr)^{1/2}\ln\Biggl[1-\biggl(\frac{|E_{N}|}{E_{B}}\biggr)^{1/2}\Biggr].

(26)

Since
\kappa_{h}
changes only in the vicinity of the upper limit and is
equal to
\frac{1}{a_{B}}
otherwise, it is convenient to isolate the
constant part

\displaystyle\Bigg[E_{N}+\frac{\hbar^{2}\kappa_{h}^{2}}{2m_{e}}\Bigg]^{1/2}=\Bigg[E_{N}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{e}a_{B}^{2}}\Bigg]^{1/2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{e}}\bigl(\kappa_{h}^{2}-\frac{1}{a_{B}^{2}}\bigr)}{\biggl(E_{N}+\frac{\hbar^{2}\kappa_{h}^{2}}{2m_{e}}\biggr)^{1/2}+\biggl(E_{N}+\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{e}a_{B}^{2}}\biggr)^{1/2}}.

(27)

Integration of the first term is elementary. To perform integration
of the second term it is convenient to switch from the variable
x_{h}
to
the dimensionless variable
u=\kappa_{h}a
. To do so, we differentiate both sides of Eq. (
8
).
This yields

\frac{du}{2u^{2}}\bigg[\ln\left(1-u\right)+\frac{u}{1-u}\bigg]=-\frac{dx_{h}}{a_{B}}.

(28)

Then the condition Eq. (
V
) takes the form

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}\left(N+\frac{1}{2}\right)\Biggl(\frac{m_{e}}{m_{h}}\Biggr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(1-q_{N}\right)^{1/2}\Bigg[\frac{L}{2a_{B}}+\frac{\ln\left(1-q_{N}^{1/2}\right)}{2q_{N}^{1/2}}\Bigg]-I(q_{N}).

(29)

where the function
I(q_{N})
is defined as

\displaystyle I(q_{N})=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{q_{N}^{1/2}}^{1}\frac{du}{2u^{2}}\bigg[\ln\left(1-u\right)+\frac{u}{1-u}\bigg]\frac{1-u^{2}}{\left(u^{2}-q_{N}\right)^{1/2}+\left(1-q_{N}\right)^{1/2}}.

(30)

The parameter

q_{N}=\frac{|E_{N}|}{E_{B}}

(31)

is the dimensionless exciton energy, so that
1-q_{N}
has a meaning of the
dimensionless size-quantization energy. In the limit of large
L
the size-quantization energy is much smaller than
E_{B}
, so that
\left(1-q_{N}\right)\ll 1
. This, in turn, means that the lower limit in the integral Eq. (
V
)
is close to
1
. Then the integral
I(q_{N})
can be evaluated asymptotically
in small parameter
1-q_{N}
, which yields

I(q_{N})=\frac{\left(1-q_{N}\right)^{1/2}}{2}\ln 2.

(32)

Substituting this expression in the right-hand side of Eq. (
V
), we arrive at a closed equation for
q_{N}
, which can be also viewed as the equation for
the electron energies,
E_{N}
. Solving this equation in the limit of large
L
,
we get

E_{N}=-E_{B}+\frac{\hbar^{2}\pi^{2}n^{2}}{2m_{h}\bigl(L-l_{N}\bigr)^{2}},

(33)

where
l_{N}
is given by

\displaystyle l_{N}=a_{B}\ln\Biggl[\frac{E_{B}}{\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}}{2m_{h}L^{2}}\Bigl(N+\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)^{2}}\Biggr]

\displaystyle=2a_{B}\Biggl[\ln\frac{L}{\pi a_{B}\Bigl(N+\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)}+\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{m_{h}}{m_{e}}\Biggr].

(34)

We see that the result Eq. (
33
) essentially reproduces Eq. (
1
)
but with the width of the dead layer specified. We see that the width,

,
grows with

, which is somewhat non-trivial.

VI
Discussion

It is seen from Eq. (
V
) that the arguments of both logarithms in the right-hand side
are large. This suggests that the size of the dead layer exceeds the exciton radius
parametrically
.
On the other hand, it follows from Eq. (
IV
) that the
effective repulsive potential for a hole diverges at
x_{h}=\frac{1}{2}\left(L-a_{B}\right)
,
i.e. at a distance
\frac{a_{B}}{2}
from the “hard wall” at
x_{h}=\frac{L}{2}
.

The fact that the turning point,
x_{t}
, for the hole motion, given by Eq. (
26
),
is parametrically further away from the boundary than
a_{B}
suggests
the following picture of the exciton reflection.
As a hole slowly moves towards the boundary,
it is orbited by a fast-moving electron. The energy of the system is
-E_{B}
.
Upon the approach to the boundary, the electron “senses” the
boundary by virtue of the no-escape boundary condition. As a result,
the energy of the system electron+hole increases. This increase acts
as a barrier for the hole, from which the hole is reflected being accompanied by the electron.

Our conclusion that
l\gg a_{B}
suggests that the above assumption about the
short-range character of the electron-hole attraction can be relaxed. Moreover,
we can extend the above consideration to the 3D case. In the Appendix we derive
the form of potential barrier,
{\tilde{V}}(x_{h})
, which enters into the quantization
condition

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{2m_{h}}{\hbar^{2}}\right)^{1/2}\int\limits_{0}^{x_{t}}dx_{h}\Bigg[E_{N}+E_{B}-{\tilde{V}}(x_{h})\Bigg]^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\pi\left(N+\frac{1}{2}\right).

(35)

With the help of Eq. (
A
) we find the positions
of the turning points

x_{t}=\frac{L}{2}+\frac{a_{B}}{2}\ln\Biggl(1-\frac{|E_{N}|}{E_{B}}\Biggr),

(36)

which appears to be similar to the 1D expression Eq. (
26
). Repeating the steps in the previous section,
it can be readily shown that our main result Eq. (
V
) for the width of the dead layer remains valid in three
dimensions.

Appendix A
Effective potential in 3D

In this Appendix we calculate the correction to the ground state energy
of electron in the field of a hole due to the presence of a boundary at
x=\frac{L}{2}
.
We start by writing down the Schrödinger equations with and without the boundary

-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{e}}\Delta\psi_{0}-\frac{e^{2}}{|{\bf r}_{e}-{\bf r}_{h}|}\psi_{0}=-E_{B}\psi_{0},

(37)

-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{e}}\Delta\psi-\frac{e^{2}}{|{\bf r}_{e}-{\bf r}_{h}|}\psi+W\big(x_{e}-\frac{L}{2}\big)\psi=E\psi,

(38)

where the function
W\big(x_{e}-\frac{L}{2}\big)
describes a barrier which ensures that
electron does not penetrate into the region
x_{e}>\frac{L}{2}
.

Multiplying Eq. (
37
) by
\psi({\bf r})
and Eq. (
38
) by
\psi_{0}({\bf r})
,
subtracting the two, and integrating over the domain
x_{e}<\frac{L}{2}
,
we find

\displaystyle E(x_{h})+E_{B}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{e}}\int\limits_{x_{e}=\frac{L}{2}}dy_{e}dz_{e}\Bigg(\psi\frac{\partial\psi_{0}}{\partial x_{e}}-\psi_{0}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x_{e}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\hbar^{2}}{4m_{e}}\int\limits_{x_{e}=\frac{L}{2}}dy_{e}dz_{e}\frac{\partial\psi_{0}^{2}}{\partial x_{e}}.

(39)

Analysis and approximation to parabolic optimal control problems with measure-valued controls in time
Wei Gong, Dongdong Liang

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate an optimal control problem governed by parabolic equations with measure-valued controls over time. We establish the well-posedness of the optimal control problem and derive the first-order optimality condition using Clarke’s subgradients, revealing a sparsity structure in time for the optimal control. Consequently, these optimal control problems represent a generalization of impulse control for evolution equations. To discretize the optimal control problem, we employ the space-time finite element method. Here, the state equation is approximated using piecewise linear and continuous finite elements in space, alongside a Petrov-Galerkin method utilizing piecewise constant trial functions and piecewise linear and continuous test functions in time. The control variable is discretized using the variational discretization concept. For error estimation, we initially derive a priori error estimates and stabilities for the finite element discretizations of the state and adjoint equations. Subsequently, we establish weak-* convergence for the control under the norm ℳ ⁡ ( I ¯ c , L 2 ​ ( ω ) ) \mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega)) , with a convergence order of O ⁡ ( h 1 2 + τ 1 4 ) O(h^{\frac{1}{2}}+\tau^{\frac{1}{4}}) for the state. Keywords: Optimal control, parabolic equation, measure valued control, finite element, error estimate

The contributions of this article are threefold. Firstly, we investigate the well-posedness of both the state equation and the optimal control problem. Additionally, we derive the first-order optimality condition, revealing that the optimal control exhibits a sparsity structure independent of space. Secondly, for the state equation approximation, we utilize piecewise linear and continuous functions in space and a Petrov-Galerkin scheme from
[
20
]
in time. Specifically, we employ piecewise constant trial functions and piecewise linear and continuous test functions. We also employ a variational discretization concept for the control. Lastly, we provide an a priori error estimate for the finite element approximation of the control problem. Building upon the a priori error and stability estimates for finite element discretizations of the state and adjoint equations, we establish a convergence order of
O(h^{\frac{1}{2}}+\tau^{\frac{1}{4}})
for the approximation of the state. Furthermore, we demonstrate weak-* convergence for the control under the
\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))

O(h+\tau^{\frac{1}{2}})
for the discrete cost functional.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents some preliminary results, including the definition of very weak solutions to the state equation, as well as the global and local regularity and weak-* continuity of the state variable. In Section 3, we derive the first-order optimality system and investigate the sparse structure and regularity of the optimal control. Two discrete optimal control problems and their associated optimality systems are provided in Section 4. Section 5 primarily focuses on the error analysis for the optimal control problem.

3

With the above preparations, we are in the position to study the existence and uniqueness of solutions to the optimal control problem (

3.1
Well-posedness of the optimal control problem

Recall that
X:=L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))\times L^{2}(\Omega)
is the observation space, then we introduce the control-to-observation operator
S:\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))\to X
as

Sq:=(S_{1}q,S_{2}q),

where
S_{1}q:=u_{q}
and
S_{2}q:=u_{q}(T)
, and
u_{q}
solves equation (
2
) with the control variable
q
on the right-hand side. Theorem
3
and Proposition
4
imply that the operator
S
is well-defined, affine linear and bounded, and weak continuous under the weak-
∗
topology in
\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
. Obviously, the operator
S
is injective since
I_{c}\subseteq I
. With the help of the control-to-observation operator
S
the reduced cost functional of (
1
) can be defined as

(17)

j(q):=J_{1}(q)+J_{2}(q)\quad\forall q\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega)),

where

J_{1}(q):=\frac{1}{2}\|S_{1}q-u_{d}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\frac{\beta}{2}\|S_{2}q-u_{T}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad\ J_{2}(q):=\alpha\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}.

J_{1}(q)
is a quadratic functional of tracking type, which is continuous under the weak-
∗
topology in
\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
and strictly convex by the weak-
∗
continuity and injection of the operator
S
. On the other hand,
J_{2}(q)
is weakly-
∗
lower semicontinuous in space
\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
. Therefore, we conclude that the reduced functional
j
is also weakly-
∗
lower semicontinuous and strictly convex. With this observation we can provide the following result.

Theorem 5
.

The optimal control problem (
1
)-(
2
) admits a unique solution
(\bar{u},\bar{q})\in X\times\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
, where
\bar{q}
is an optimal control that minimizes the reduced cost functional
(
17
)
and
\bar{u}
is the optimal state that solves the state equation (
2
) associated with
\bar{q}
.

Proof.

According to Theorem
3
, the objective functional
j
is well defined on
\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
. For the existence of solutions, we follow the standard arguments. Since
j\geq 0
is bounded from below on
\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
, we can find a minimizing sequence
\{q_{n}\}
with

\lim_{n\to\infty}j(q_{n})=\mathop{\rm inf}_{q\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}j(q)=j^{*}\quad{\rm and}\quad\|q_{n}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}j(q_{n})\leq C.

On the other hand, the predual space
{C}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
is separable, then the bounded set in
\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
is weakly-
∗
compact by the Banach-Alaoglu theorem. Hence, we can extract a weakly-
∗
convergent subsequence, still denoted by
\{q_{n}\}
, such that
q_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\bar{q}
in
\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
for some
\bar{q}\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
. Let
u_{n}
and
\bar{u}
be the state corresponding to
q_{n}
and
\bar{q}
, respectively. Then
u_{n}\to\bar{u}
in
X
since the operator
S
is weakly-
∗
continuous. It is easy to check that
(\bar{u},\bar{q})
is an optimal pair. In fact,
j
is weakly-
∗
lower semicontinuous, then

j(\bar{q})\leq\mathop{\lim\inf}_{n\to\infty}\ j(q_{n})=j^{*},

which means that
\bar{q}
is optimal, i.e.,
(\bar{u},\bar{q})
is an optimal pair.

Furthermore, the control-to-observation mapping
S
is injective, thus the objective functional
j
is strictly convex. Therefore, the optimal pair
(\bar{u},\bar{q})
is unique.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

As pointed out in

, if the state observation is of the form

3.2

Below, we are in the position to derive the first order optimality condition.

Theorem 6
.

A control

(22)

\displaystyle\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{{C}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}\left\{\begin{aligned} &=\alpha&&\mbox{if}\,\,\bar{q}\neq 0,\\

) we have the identity:

(32)

\displaystyle(\bar{q}^{\prime}(t),\bar{\varphi})_{L^{2}(\omega)}=-\|\bar{q}^{\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=-\alpha,\quad\mbox{for}\ |\bar{q}|-\mbox{almost\ all}\ t\in\bar{I}_{c},

which is equivalent to

(33)

\displaystyle\|\bar{\varphi}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\alpha\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bar{\varphi}(t,x)=-\alpha\bar{q}^{\prime}(t,x)

for
|\bar{q}|
-almost all
t\in\bar{I}_{c}
and a.e.
x\in\omega
. Thus, we finish the proof of (
28
).

In view of (
3
), we have

\|\bar{\varphi}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\alpha\|\bar{q}^{\prime}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\alpha\quad\mbox{for}\ |\bar{q}|\ \mbox{-almost all}\ t\in\bar{I}_{c}.

Namely,

|\bar{q}|(\bar{I}_{c})=|\bar{q}|(\mbox{supp}\ |\bar{q}|)=|\bar{q}|(\{t\in\bar{I}_{c}:\|\bar{\varphi}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\alpha\}),

which means that
\mbox{supp}\ |\bar{q}|\subseteq\{t\in\bar{I}_{c}:\|\bar{\varphi}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\alpha\}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

In view of (
27
) in Theorem
7
, we find that the optimal control
\bar{q}\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
has sparsity pattern in time that is independent of the spatial domain. Moreover, if
\|\bar{\varphi}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\alpha
holds for a finite set of time instances, namely,
\{t\in\bar{I}_{c}:\|\varphi(t)\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\alpha\}=\{\tau_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, then
\bar{q}
has the representation
\bar{q}(t,x)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\chi_{\omega}\bar{q}_{i}(x)\delta_{\tau_{i}}
such that
\bar{q}_{i}\in L^{2}(\omega)
(cf.
[
35
]
). This is exactly the impulse control problem, studied extensively in the literature, e.g.,
[
21
,
22
,
46
,
47
,
52
]
, and the references therein.

Proposition 8
.

There exists
\alpha_{0}>0
such that the optimal control
\bar{q}=0
when
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
.

Proof.

The main idea follows from
[
5
, Corollary 3.5]
, see also
[
4
, Proposition 2.2]
, and we sketch it here. Note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{u}-u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\|\bar{u}(T)-u_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq J(\bar{q})\leq J(0),

where
J(0)
is independent of
\alpha
. Then we have obtained a uniform upper bound of
\|\bar{u}-u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}
and
\|\bar{u}(T)-u_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
with respect to
\alpha
. For any
u_{d}\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)),\ u_{T}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, let
\bar{\varphi}\in H^{1}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\hookrightarrow C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega))
be the optimal adjoint state defined by equation (
18
) with the following estimate:

\displaystyle\|\bar{\varphi}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|\bar{u}-u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\beta\|\bar{u}(T)-u_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\leq 2CJ(0),

where we have used Lemma
1
. Setting
\alpha_{0}=2CJ(0)
, it follows from (
27
) in Theorem
7
that
\bar{q}=0
for all
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

3.3
The regularity of solutions

In this subsection we prepare to state the regularity of solutions to the optimality system, which will be used in the finite element approximation to the optimal state and adjoint state.

Theorem 9
.

For any
u_{d},\,f\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)),\,u_{0},\,u_{T}\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, let
(\bar{q},\bar{u},\bar{\varphi})
be the optimal solution of the optimal control problem (
1
)-(
2
), where
\bar{q},\bar{u}
and
\bar{\varphi}
are the optimal control, optimal state and adjoint state, respectively. Then there hold

\displaystyle\bar{u}\in L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega)),\quad\bar{\varphi}\in H^{1}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)),\quad\bar{q}\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};H^{1}(\omega)),

\displaystyle\bar{u}|_{(\tilde{t},T)}\in L^{2}((\tilde{t},T);H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}((\tilde{t},T);L^{2}(\Omega))\hookrightarrow C([\tilde{t},T];H^{1}_{0}({\Omega})),

\displaystyle\bar{\varphi}\in L^{2}(I_{c};H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(I_{c};L^{2}(\Omega))\hookrightarrow C(\bar{I}_{c};H^{1}_{0}({\Omega})),

where
(\tilde{t},T)\cap I_{c}=\emptyset
, and there hold the following stability estimates

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}+\|\bar{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{H^{1}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))}+\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}+\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\bar{q}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};H^{1}(\omega))}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}\|_{H^{1}((\tilde{t},T);L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\bar{u}\|_{L^{2}((\tilde{t},T);H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}+\|\bar{u}\|_{C([\tilde{t},T];H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

Moreover, if
u_{T}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, then the adjoint state has the following improved regularity:

\bar{\varphi}\in H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))

with the estimate

\displaystyle\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}+\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{C(\bar{I};H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u_{T}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

Proof.

To begin with, we first consider the regularity of
\bar{u},\,\bar{\varphi}
and derive the associated estimates. By using Theorem
3
and Lemma
1
,
\bar{u}
and
\bar{\varphi}
have the above mentioned regularity, but the local regularity of
\bar{\varphi}
and the associated stability estimates depend on
\|\bar{q}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}
. Therefore, we first provide the estimate for
\|\bar{q}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}
.

Let
\tilde{u}_{0}
be the solution of equation (
2
) with
q=0
. Using the identity (
21
) there holds

\displaystyle\alpha\|\bar{q}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}\leq

\displaystyle J(\bar{u},\bar{q})\leq J(\tilde{u}_{0},0)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|u_{d}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+{\beta}\|u_{T}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}).

Below, we show that
\bar{\varphi}\in H^{1}(I_{c};L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(I_{c};H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\hookrightarrow C(\bar{I}_{c};H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
and
\bar{q}\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};H^{1}(\omega))
.
Since
I_{c}=(t_{1},t_{2})\subseteq I
is relatively compact, there exist
\tilde{t}_{1}
,
\tilde{t}_{2}
satisfying
\tilde{t}_{1}<t_{1}<t_{2}<\tilde{t}_{2}
, such that
I_{c}\subseteq(\tilde{t}_{1},\tilde{t}_{2})
. Let
\tilde{\omega}
be a smooth cut-off function satisfying

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}(t)\in[0,1]\quad\forall t\in[0,T];\quad\tilde{\omega}(t)=1\quad\forall t\in I_{c};\quad\tilde{\omega}(t)=0\quad\forall t\in\bar{I}\backslash(\tilde{t}_{1},\tilde{t}_{2}),

and
\tilde{\varphi}:=\bar{\varphi}\tilde{\omega}
, then
\tilde{\varphi}
satisfies the following equation:

(34)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} -\partial_{t}\tilde{\varphi}-\Delta\tilde{\varphi}&=-\partial_{t}\tilde{\omega}\bar{\varphi}+\tilde{\omega}(\bar{u}-u_{d})&&\mbox{in}\,\,\,\Omega\times(\tilde{t}_{1},\tilde{t}_{2}),\\
\tilde{\varphi}&=0&&\mbox{on}\,\,\Gamma\times(\tilde{t}_{1},\tilde{t}_{2}),\\
\tilde{\varphi}(\tilde{t}_{2})&=0&&\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Therefore, by using Lemma
1
we obtain
\tilde{\varphi}\in H^{1}((\tilde{t}_{1},\tilde{t}_{2});L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}((\tilde{t}_{1},\tilde{t}_{2});H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
, which implies that
\bar{\varphi}\in H^{1}(I_{c};L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(I_{c};H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\hookrightarrow C(\bar{I}_{c};H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
and the following estimate holds:

(35)

\displaystyle\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{H^{1}(I_{c};L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(I_{c};H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}+\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{C(\bar{I}_{c};H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\varphi}\|_{H^{1}((\tilde{t}_{1},\tilde{t}_{2});L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}((\tilde{t}_{1},\tilde{t}_{2});H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\tilde{\varphi}\|_{C([\tilde{t}_{1},\tilde{t}_{2}];H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\bar{u}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|f\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}).

Finally, we prove that
\bar{q}\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};H^{1}(\omega))
. In view of Theorem
7
, there holds the relation
d\bar{q}=\bar{q}^{\prime}d|\bar{q}|
, where
\bar{q}^{\prime}(t,x)=-\frac{1}{\alpha}\bar{\varphi}(t,x)
with
\bar{\varphi}\in C(\bar{I}_{c};H_{0}^{1}(\Omega))
. Therefore, for any
\psi\in C(\bar{I}_{c};(H^{1}(\omega))^{*})
, there holds

\displaystyle\langle\bar{q},\psi\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}=

\displaystyle\int_{I_{c}}\langle\psi,d\bar{q}\rangle=-\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{I_{c}}\langle\psi,\bar{\varphi}\rangle d|\bar{q}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\psi\|_{C(\bar{I}_{c};(H^{1}(\omega)^{*}))}\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{C(\bar{I}_{c};H^{1}(\omega))}\|\bar{q}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}.

Thus,
\|\bar{q}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};H^{1}(\omega))}\leq C\frac{1}{\alpha}\|\bar{\varphi}\|_{C(\bar{I}_{c};H^{1}(\Omega))}\|\bar{q}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}
(cf.
[
45
]
). This combining with the estimate (
35
) yields the conclusion. We thus finish the proof. ∎

4
Finite element approximations

In this section we consider the space-time finite element approximation for optimal control problems.

4.1
Notations for finite element methods

Let
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}_{h>0}
be a family of quasi-uniform and shape regular triangulations of
\Omega
in the sense of Ciarlet
[
16
]
, such that
\bar{\Omega}=\cup_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\bar{K}
, where
h
is the mesh parameter. Define the piecewise linear and continuous finite element space

V_{h}:=\left\{v_{h}\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega):\ v_{h}|_{K}\in P_{1}(K),\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\},

where
P_{1}(K)
denotes the space of linear functions in
K
.

For simplicity, we assume that
\omega\subseteq\Omega
is polygonal and the restriction of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
on
\omega
gives a partition of
\omega
. Thus, we define
U_{h}:=V_{h}|_{\omega}
consisting of piecewise linear and continuous functions in
\omega
.

Next, we divide
[0,T]
into a family of subintervals
I_{m}:=(t_{m-1},t_{m}],\,m=1,2,\cdots,M-1,\,I_{M}:=(t_{M-1},t_{M})
with step size
\tau_{m}=t_{m}-t_{m-1}
such that
[0,T]=\{0\}\cup\mathop{\cup}\limits_{i=1}^{M}I_{m}\cup\{T\}
, where
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{M}=T
. We assume that there exist
1<k_{1}<k_{2}<M
such that
\bar{I}_{c}=[t_{1},t_{2}]=\{t_{k_{1}-1}\}\cup\mathop{\cup}\limits_{m=k_{1}}^{k_{2}}I_{m}
, this can be achieved by setting
M
sufficiently large.
The maximal time step is defined by
\tau:=\mathop{\max}\limits_{1\leq m\leq M}{\tau_{m}}
.

Now, we are ready to define two time semi-discrete finite element spaces consisting of either piecewise constant or piecewise linear and continuous Ansatz. Define (cf.
[
20
]
)

\displaystyle P_{\tau}:=\{v\in C(\bar{I};H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)):\ v|_{I_{m}}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(I_{m};H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)),\ m=1,2,\dots,M\},

\displaystyle Y_{\tau}:=\{v\in L^{2}(I;H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)):\ v|_{I_{m}}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(I_{m};H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)),\ m=1,2,\dots,M,\ v(T)\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\},

where
\mathcal{P}_{i}(I_{m};H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
(
i=0,1
) denotes the set of polynomial functions of degree at most
i
on time interval
I_{m}
and valued in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and let

P_{\tau}^{0}:=\{v_{\tau}\in P_{\tau}:\ v_{\tau}(T)=0\}.

The notation
\sigma=(\tau,h)
denotes the vector of two discretization parameters
\tau
and
h
. In order to introduce the Petrov-Galerkin scheme for parabolic equations, we also need to define the following two time-space finite element spaces:

\displaystyle P_{\sigma}:=\left\{v\in P_{\tau}:\,v|_{I_{m}}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(I_{m};V_{h}),\ m=1,2,\dots,M\right\},

\displaystyle Y_{\sigma}:=\left\{v\in Y_{\tau}:\ v|_{I_{m}}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(I_{m};V_{h}),\ m=1,2,\dots,M,\ v(T)\in V_{h}\right\},

where the definition of
\mathcal{P}_{i}(I_{m};V_{h})
(
i=0,1
) is similar to
\mathcal{P}_{i}(I_{m};H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
(
i=0,1
). We set

P_{\sigma}^{0}:=\{v_{\sigma}\in P_{\sigma}:\ v_{\sigma}(T)=0\}.

It is clear that
P_{\sigma}\subset L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))
and
Y_{\sigma}\subseteq L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
.

Let
\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N_{h}}
be the interior nodes of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and
\{\psi_{x_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{N_{h}}
,
\{e_{t_{m}}\}_{m=0}^{M}
be the nodal basis functions of
\mathcal{P}_{1}
elements of
H^{1}(\Omega)
and
H^{1}(I)
, respectively. Obviously,
P_{\sigma}
and
Y_{\sigma}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle P_{\sigma}={\rm span}\{\psi_{x_{j}}\otimes e_{t_{m}}:1\leq j\leq N_{h},\ 0\leq m\leq M\},

\displaystyle Y_{\sigma}={\rm span}\{\psi_{x_{j}}\otimes\chi_{m}:1\leq j\leq N_{h},\ 1\leq m\leq M\},

where
\chi_{m}
denotes the characteristic function on
I_{m}
. We define the indices

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}=\Big\{(j,m):(x_{j},t_{m})\in\bar{\omega}\times\bar{I}_{c},1\leq j\leq N_{h},\ 0\leq m\leq M\Big\},\ \mathcal{I}_{\tau}=\big\{m:t_{m}\in\bar{I}_{c},0\leq m\leq M\big\},

and the discrete spaces

\displaystyle U_{\tau}={\rm span}\{\delta_{t_{m}}:m\in\mathcal{I}_{\tau}\}\subset\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c}),\quad V_{\tau}={\rm span}\{e_{t_{m}}|_{\bar{I}_{c}}:m\in\mathcal{I}_{\tau}\}\subset{C}(\bar{I}_{c}),

\displaystyle U_{\sigma}={\rm span}\{\psi_{x_{j}}|_{\bar{\omega}}\otimes\delta_{t_{m}}:(j,m)\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}\},\quad V_{\sigma}={\rm span}\{\psi_{x_{j}}\otimes e_{t_{m}}|_{\bar{\omega}\times\bar{I}_{c}}:(j,m)\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}\}.

For
q_{\tau}\in U_{\tau}
and
v_{\tau}\in V_{\tau}
we identify them with
\vec{q}_{\tau}=(q_{1},\dots,q_{M_{c}})
and
\vec{v}_{\tau}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{M_{c}})
where
M_{c}
is the cardinality of
\mathcal{I}_{\tau}
. The linear functions in
V_{\tau}
attain their maximum and minimum at the nodes. Therefore, for
v_{\tau}\in V_{\tau}
we define

\displaystyle\|v_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{c})}=\max\limits_{1\leq m\leq M_{c}}|v_{m}|.

Similarly, we have for
q_{\tau}\in U_{\tau}
that

\displaystyle\|q_{\tau}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c})}=\sup\limits_{v\in{C}(\bar{I}_{c}),\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{c})}\leq 1}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{M_{c}}q_{m}\langle\delta_{t_{m}},v\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}}=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{M_{c}}|q_{m}|.

Given two functions
u\in L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
with
u(T)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
v\in L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))
, we define a bilinear form
A(u,v)\to\mathbb{R}
as follows:

\displaystyle A(u,v):=-\langle u,\partial_{t}v\rangle_{L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0},H^{-1})}+\int_{I}(\nabla u(t),\nabla v(t))dt+(u(T),v(T)),

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0},H^{-1})}
denotes the duality pairing between
L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
and
L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))
.

If, in addition,
u_{\tau}\in Y_{\tau}
and
v_{\tau}\in P_{\tau}
, applying integration by parts to the bilinear form
A(u_{\tau},v_{\tau})
we can obtain the following dual representation:

(36)

A(u_{\tau},v_{\tau})=\sum_{m=1}^{M}([u_{\tau}]_{m},v_{\tau}(t_{m}))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(u_{\tau,0}^{+},v_{\tau}(0))_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\int_{I}(\nabla u_{\tau}(t),\nabla v_{\tau}(t))dt,

where

\displaystyle u_{\tau,m+1}=u^{+}_{\tau,m}:=\mathop{\rm lim}\limits_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}u_{\tau}(t_{m}+\epsilon),\quad u_{\tau,m}=u^{-}_{\tau,m}:=\mathop{\rm lim}\limits_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}u_{\tau}(t_{m}-\epsilon),

\displaystyle[u]_{m}:=u^{+}_{\tau,m}-u^{-}_{\tau,m},\ m=1,2,\cdots,M-1,\ [u_{\tau}]_{M}=u_{\tau}(T)-u^{-}_{\tau,M},

and
u_{\tau,m}:=u_{\tau}|_{I_{m}},\ m=1,2,\cdots,M
.

In view of the identity (
14
), the very weak solution to the state equation (
2
) reads: Find
u\in L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
such that

\displaystyle A(u,v)=\!\!\int_{I}(f,v)dt+\langle q,v\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}+(u_{0},v(x,0))\quad\forall v\in L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega)).

Similarly, the weak formulation of the backward equation (
5
) can be rewritten as: Find
z\in L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))
such that

(37)

\displaystyle A(v,z)=\int_{I}(g,v)dt+(v(T),z_{T})\quad\forall v\in L^{2}(I;H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega)).

4.2
Interpolation and projection operators

In the following we introduce some interpolation operators defined in
\bar{I}_{c}
and give their properties whose proofs are very similar to
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
, see also
[
8
, Proposition 4.1]
.

Lemma 10
.

Let the linear operators
\Lambda_{\tau}
and
\Pi_{\tau}
be defined as follows:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\tau}:\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c})\rightarrow U_{\tau}\subset\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c}),\quad\Lambda_{\tau}q:=\sum\limits_{m\in\mathcal{I}_{\tau}}\delta_{t_{m}}\int_{\bar{I}_{c}}e_{t_{m}}dq,

\displaystyle\Pi_{\tau}:{C}(\bar{I}_{c})\rightarrow V_{\tau}\subset{C}(\bar{I}_{c}),\quad\Pi_{\tau}v:=\sum\limits_{m\in\mathcal{I}_{\tau}}v(t_{m})e_{t_{m}}.

Then for every
q\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c})
,
v\in{C}(\bar{I}_{c})
and
v_{\tau}\in V_{\tau}
, there hold

(38)

\displaystyle\langle q,\Pi_{\tau}v\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}}=\langle\Lambda_{\tau}q,v\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}},

(39)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{\tau}q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c})}\leq\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c})},

(40)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\tau}q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}q\ \mbox{\rm in}\ \mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c})\quad\mbox{and}\ \|\Lambda_{\tau}q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c})}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c})},

(41)

\displaystyle\|q-\Lambda_{\tau}q\|_{(H^{1}(I_{c}))^{\prime}}\leq C\tau^{1\over 2}\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c})},\quad\|q-\Lambda_{\tau}q\|_{(W^{1,\infty}(I_{c}))^{\prime}}\leq C\tau\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c})}.

Proof.

The proofs of (
38
)-(
40
) are trivial, thus we only provide the proof for (
41
). For an arbitrary
v\in H^{1}(I_{c})
, using (
38
) and the standard Lagrange interpolation error estimate we have for
s>1
that

\displaystyle\langle q-\Lambda_{\tau}q,v\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}}=\langle q,v-\Pi_{\tau}v\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}}\leq\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c})}\|v-\Pi_{\tau}v\|_{{C}(\bar{I}_{c})}\leq C\tau^{1-{1\over s}}\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c})}\|v\|_{W^{1,s}(I_{c})}.

From the duality we can obtain two desired results by setting
s=2
and
s=\infty
.
∎

Let
\pi_{h}:\,L^{2}(\omega)\to V_{h}|_{\omega}
be the usual
L^{2}
-projection defined by

(v-\pi_{h}v,\varphi_{h})_{L^{2}(\omega)}=0\quad\forall\varphi_{h}\in V_{h}|_{\omega},

then there holds

\|v-\pi_{h}v\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}+h\|v-\pi_{h}v\|_{H^{1}(\omega)}\leq Ch^{m}\|v\|_{H^{m}(\omega)}\quad\forall v\in H^{m}(\omega),\ m=1,2,

where
C>0
is a constant independent of
h
and
v
. Note that the application of the operator
\pi_{h}
to time-dependent arguments has to be understood pointwisely in time. Below, we will extend
\pi_{h}
to a negative exponent Sobolev space that includes
L^{2}(\omega)
.

Let
V:=H^{1}(\omega)
(resp.
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
),
H:=L^{2}(\omega)
(resp.
L^{2}(\Omega)
), then the inclusion
V\subseteq H
is dense and continuous. Note that
V\hookrightarrow H=H^{*}\hookrightarrow V^{*}
is a Gelfand triple, where
H\hookrightarrow V^{*}
is given by
y\in H\to(y,\cdot)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\in H^{*}\subseteq V^{*}
. Therefore, we extend in the following definition the usual projection
\pi_{h}
from
H
to
V^{*}
.

Definition 11
.

Define the action of the
L^{2}
-projection
\pi_{h}
on
V^{*}
as

\displaystyle\pi_{h}:V^{*}\to V_{h}|_{\omega}\quad v\to\pi_{h}v,

where
\pi_{h}v\in V_{h}|_{\omega}
satisfies

(42)

(\pi_{h}v,\varphi_{h})_{H}=\langle v,\varphi_{h}\rangle_{V^{*},V}\quad\forall\varphi_{h}\in V_{h}|_{\omega}\subset V.

Furthermore, we also define the following two interpolation operators:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\sigma}:\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};H)\rightarrow U_{\sigma}\subset\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};H),\quad\Lambda_{\sigma}q:=\pi_{h}(\Lambda_{\tau}q)=\sum\limits_{(j,m)\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}q_{j,m}\delta_{t_{m}}\otimes\psi_{x_{j}}|_{\bar{\omega}},

\displaystyle\Pi_{\sigma}:{C}(\bar{I}_{c};V^{*})\rightarrow V_{\sigma}\subset{C}(\bar{I}_{c};V^{*}),\quad\Pi_{\sigma}v:=\pi_{h}(\Pi_{\tau}v)=\sum\limits_{(j,m)\in\mathcal{I}_{\sigma}}v_{j,m}e_{t_{m}}\otimes\psi_{x_{j}}|_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\bar{\omega}},

where
q_{j,m}:=\pi_{h}(\int_{\bar{I}_{c}}e_{t_{m}}dq)(x_{j})
and
v_{j,m}:=\pi_{h}(v(t_{m}))(x_{j})
.

It is easy to check that there exists a unique
\pi_{h}v\in V_{h}|_{\omega}
such that the identity (
42
) holds for any
v\in V^{*}
. Moreover,
\pi_{h}|_{H}:H\to V_{h}|_{\omega}
is consistent with the usual
L^{2}
-projection and
\pi_{h}
is stable, i.e.,

\displaystyle\|\pi_{h}v\|_{V^{*}}\leq\|v\|_{V^{*}},\quad\|\pi_{h}{v}\|_{H}\leq\|{v}\|_{H}

for
v\in V^{*}
and
{v}\in H
, respectively.
The definition of above two interpolation operators
\Lambda_{\sigma}
and
\Pi_{\sigma}
are very similar to
[
5
, Theorem 4.2]
, see also
[
8
, Proposition 4.2]
.

Lemma 12
.

For every
q_{\sigma}\in U_{\sigma}
and
v_{\sigma}\in V_{\sigma}
one have

(43)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\sigma}q_{\sigma}=q_{\sigma}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Pi_{\sigma}v_{\sigma}=v_{\sigma}.

Moreover, there hold

(44)

\displaystyle\langle q,\Pi_{\sigma}v\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}=\langle\Lambda_{\sigma}q,v\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}\quad\forall\,(q,v)\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};S)\times{C}(\bar{I}_{c};S^{*}),

(45)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{\sigma}q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};S)}\leq\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};S)},\quad\|\Pi_{\sigma}v\|_{{C}(\bar{I}_{c};S^{*})}\leq\|v\|_{{C}(\bar{I}_{c};S^{*})},

(46)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\sigma}q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}q\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};H),\quad\ \|\Lambda_{\sigma}q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};H)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle|\sigma|\to 0}}{{\longrightarrow}}\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};H)},

where
S=H
or
V
.

Proof.

A simple calculation gives

(47)

\displaystyle\langle q,\Pi_{\sigma}v\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle q,\pi_{h}(\Pi_{\tau}v)\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}=\big\langle q,\pi_{h}\big(\sum_{m}v(t_{m})e_{t_{m}}\big)\big\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle\sum_{m}\pi_{h}(v(t_{m})),\int_{I_{m}}e_{t_{m}}dq\Big\rangle_{S^{*},S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle\sum_{m}v(t_{m}),\pi_{h}(\int_{I_{m}}e_{t_{m}}dq)\Big\rangle_{S^{*},S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\langle v,\sum_{m}\pi_{h}(\int_{I_{m}}e_{t_{m}}dq)\otimes\delta_{t_{m}}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\Lambda_{\sigma}q,v\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}

for any
(q,v)\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};S)\times{C}(\bar{I}_{c};S^{*})
.
Moreover, by using (
43
) and (
44
) we have
\langle q,v_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}=\langle q,\Pi_{\sigma}v_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}=\langle\Lambda_{\sigma}q,v_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}
.

For the second inequality in (

), we use the stability of the projection
\pi_{h}
to conclude

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\sigma}v\|_{{C}(\bar{I}_{c};S^{*})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in\bar{I}_{c}}\|\pi_{h}(\Pi_{\tau}v)(t)\|_{S^{*}}\leq\max\limits_{1\leq m\leq M_{c}}\|\pi_{h}v(t_{m})\|_{S^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\limits_{1\leq m\leq M_{c}}\|v(t_{m})\|_{S^{*}}\leq\|v\|_{{C}(\bar{I}_{c};{S^{*}})}.

Next, we prove the first inequality in (

\displaystyle\|\Lambda_{\sigma}q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};S)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\|v\|_{{C}(\bar{I}_{c};S^{*})}\leq 1}\langle\Lambda_{\sigma}q,v\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}=\sup\limits_{\|v\|_{{C}(\bar{I}_{c};S^{*})}\leq 1}\langle q,\Pi_{\sigma}v\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\|v\|_{{C}(\bar{I}_{c};S^{*})}\leq 1}\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};S)}\|\Pi_{\sigma}v\|_{{C}(\bar{I}_{c};S^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};S)},

where we have used the second inequality in (

). This proves the first inequality in (

), while (

) is obvious. This finishes the proof.
∎

4.3
Discretization of the optimal control problem

With the above preparation the discrete optimal control problem reads:

(53)

\displaystyle\min_{q\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))\atop{u_{\sigma}\in Y_{\sigma}}}J_{\sigma}(u_{\sigma},q):=\frac{1}{2}\|u_{\sigma}-u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\|u_{\sigma}(T)-u_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\alpha\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))},

where
u_{\sigma}\in Y_{\sigma}
is the discrete state variable satisfying the following discrete state equation:

(54)

\displaystyle A(u_{\sigma},v_{\sigma})=\int_{I}(f,v_{\sigma})dt+\langle q,v_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}+(u_{0},v_{\sigma}(0))\quad\forall v_{\sigma}\in P_{\sigma}.

Note that in the above discrete optimal control problem (
53
) the control variable is not explicitly discretized, but in the following we will see that the discretization of the adjoint state indeed automatically yields the discretization of the control variable, which is the so-called variational discretization for optimal control problems proposed in
[
33
]
.

Similar to subsection
3.1
, we can easily check that the discrete optimal control problem (
53
) exists at least one optimal pair. However, the optimal control is not unique in general, since the discrete control-to-state operator is not injective. In fact, we have
\langle\Lambda_{\sigma}q,v_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}=\langle q,v_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}
for any
v_{\sigma}\in P_{\sigma}
, which implies that the cost functional
J_{\sigma}
is not strictly convex on
\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
. Fortunately, we find that the discrete optimal control problem (
53
) does indeed have a unique optimal control in the subspace
U_{\sigma}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
.

To derive the discrete first order optimality condition, we denote the solution of (
54
) by
u_{\sigma}(q)\in Y_{\sigma}
, then we can obtain the following reduced optimization problem:

(55)

\displaystyle\min_{q\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}j_{\sigma}(q):=j_{\sigma,1}(q)+j_{\sigma,2}(q),

where

\displaystyle j_{\sigma,1}(q):=\frac{1}{2}\|u_{\sigma}(q)-u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\|u_{\sigma}(q)(T)-u_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},\quad j_{\sigma,2}(q):=\alpha\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}.

Obviously,
j_{\sigma,1}:\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))\to\mathbb{R}
is a quadratic functional of tracking type that is differentiable, and
j_{\sigma,2}:\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))\to\mathbb{R}
is convex and subdifferentiable.

By straightforward calculations, we obtain for any
q,\,p\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
that

\displaystyle j^{\prime}_{\sigma,1}(q)p=\left(u_{\sigma}(q)-u_{d},\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(p)\right)_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\beta\left(u_{\sigma}(q)(T)-u_{T},\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(p)(T)\right),

where
\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(p)\in Y_{\sigma}
is the finite element approximation of the state equation (
2
) with
f=0,\,u_{0}=0,\,q=p
, i.e., satisfying equation (
54
) with the assumed data. On the other hand, we define a discrete adjoint variable as follows: Find
\varphi_{\sigma}\in P_{\sigma}
such that

(56)

\displaystyle A(w_{\sigma},\varphi_{\sigma})=\int_{I}(u_{\sigma}(q)-u_{d},w_{\sigma})dt+\beta(u_{\sigma}(q)(\cdot,T)-u_{T},w_{\sigma}(T))\quad\forall w_{\sigma}\in Y_{\sigma}.

Then, we obtain for any
q,\,p\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
that

(57)

\displaystyle j^{\prime}_{\sigma,1}(q)p

\displaystyle=\left(u_{\sigma}(q)-u_{d},\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(p)\right)_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\beta\left(u_{\sigma}(q)(T)-u_{T},\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(p)(T)\right)

\displaystyle=A(\tilde{u}_{\sigma}(p),\varphi_{\sigma})=\langle p,\varphi_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega},

which implies that
j^{\prime}_{\sigma,1}(q)=\varphi_{\sigma}|_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}
with
\varphi_{\sigma}
given by (
56
).

Now we are in the position to derive the first order optimality condition for the discrete optimization problem (
55
).

Theorem 14
.

Let
\hat{q}_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
be an optimal control and
\bar{u}_{\sigma}\in Y_{\sigma}
be the corresponding optimal state of the optimal control problem (
53
). Then there exists an adjoint state
\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma}\in P_{\sigma}
solving (
56
) with
u_{\sigma}(q)
replaced by
\bar{u}_{\sigma}
on the right-hand side, such that the following subgradient condition holds

(58)

\displaystyle 0\in\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma}|_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}+\alpha\mathscr{\partial}\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}(\hat{q}_{\sigma})\ \ \mbox{\rm in}\ \,(\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega)))^{*},

i.e.,

(59)

\displaystyle-\langle p-\hat{q}_{\sigma},\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}+\alpha\|\hat{q}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}\leq\alpha\|p\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}\quad\forall p\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega)),

where
\mathscr{\partial}\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}(\hat{q}_{\sigma})
denotes the set of subgradients for
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}
at
\hat{q}_{\sigma}
which is nonempty since
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}
is convex on
\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
, and
(\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega)))^{*}
denotes the topological dual of
\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
.

Furthermore, from the above condition (
59
) we can easily conclude the following relation between
\hat{q}_{\sigma}
and
\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma}
:

(60)

\displaystyle\alpha\|\hat{q}_{\sigma}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}+\langle\hat{q}_{\sigma},\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}=0,

(61)

\displaystyle\|\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma}\|_{{C}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}\left\{\begin{aligned} &=\alpha&&\mbox{if}\,\,\hat{q}_{\sigma}\neq 0,\\
&\leq\alpha&&\mbox{if}\,\,\hat{q}_{\sigma}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

In addition, since the discrete control-to-state mapping has an infinite-dimensional kernel, the discrete optimal control problem (
53
) admits more than one optimal control
\hat{q}_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
corresponding to the identical optimal state
\bar{u}_{\sigma}\in Y_{\sigma}
. Among these optimal controls there exists a unique one
\bar{q}_{\sigma}\in U_{\sigma}
, such that for any other optimal control
\hat{q}_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
there holds
\bar{q}_{\sigma}=\Lambda_{\sigma}\hat{q}_{\sigma}
, i.e.,

j_{\sigma}(\bar{q}_{\sigma})=j_{\sigma}(\hat{q}_{\sigma})=\min\limits_{q\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}j_{\sigma}(q).

In other words,
(\bar{q}_{\sigma},\bar{u}_{\sigma})\in U_{\sigma}\times Y_{\sigma}
is the unique optimal pair of the following fully discrete optimal control problem:

(62)

\displaystyle\min_{q_{\sigma}\in U_{\sigma}\atop{u_{\sigma}\in Y_{\sigma}}}J_{\sigma}(u_{\sigma},q_{\sigma}):=\frac{1}{2}\|u_{\sigma}-u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}\|u_{\sigma}(T)-u_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\alpha\|q_{\sigma}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))},

where
u_{\sigma}\in Y_{\sigma}
is the discrete state variable satisfying (
54
) with the discrete control
q_{\sigma}\in U_{\sigma}
, which can be computed in practice.

Proof.

Similar to Theorem
6
, we can easily obtain (
58
)-(
61
) by recalling that

j^{\prime}_{\sigma,1}(\bar{q}_{\sigma})=\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma}|_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}
in (
57
).

Next, we state the non-uniqueness of optimal controls and the unique solvability of problem (
62
). Let
\hat{q}_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
be any optimal control of problem (
53
). Setting
\bar{q}_{\sigma}:=\Lambda_{\sigma}\hat{q}_{\sigma}
, it follows from (
44
) and (
45
) that
\langle\bar{q}_{\sigma},v_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}=\langle\hat{q}_{\sigma},v_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}
for any
v_{\sigma}\in P_{\sigma}
and

\displaystyle J_{\sigma}(\bar{q}_{\sigma})\leq J_{\sigma}(\hat{q}_{\sigma}).

This means
\bar{q}_{\sigma}\in U_{\sigma}
is also optimal, and
\bar{q}_{\sigma}\neq\hat{q}_{\sigma}
unless
\hat{q}_{\sigma}\in U_{\sigma}
. On the other hand, the functional
j_{\sigma}
is not strictly convex on
\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
. Therefore, the discrete optimization problem (
53
) admits more than one solution in
\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
.

Obviously, the control-to-state mapping is injective on
U_{\sigma}
, and thus the discrete cost functional
J_{\sigma}
is strictly convex on
U_{\sigma}
. Therefore, the optimization problem (
62
) admits a unique optimal pair
(\bar{q}_{\sigma},\bar{u}_{\sigma})\in U_{\sigma}\times Y_{\sigma}
. The uniqueness of optimal controls for the cost functional
J_{\sigma}
in
U_{\sigma}
ensures that any other optimal control
\hat{q}_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
satisfies
\bar{q}_{\sigma}=\Lambda_{\sigma}\hat{q}_{\sigma}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

5.1
Stability estimates for the discrete state and adjoint state

We first give a stability result for the semi-discrete approximation of the backward parabolic equation (

Lemma 15
.

Let
z_{\tau}\in P_{\tau}
solve (
64
) for given

and

, then there exists a constant

, independent of
\tau
, such that

\displaystyle\|z_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\sqrt{T}\|z_{\tau}\|_{C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega))},

\displaystyle\|z_{\tau}\|_{C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}z_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))}+\|z_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

In addition, if
g\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))
and
z_{T}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, then there hold

\displaystyle\|\nabla z_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\sqrt{T}\|\nabla z_{\tau}\|_{C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega))},

(66)

\displaystyle\|\nabla z_{\tau}\|_{C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\partial_{t}z_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\nabla z_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

For any fixed
m_{0}\in\{1,2,\dots,M\}
, setting
v_{\tau}|_{I_{m}}=0
for
m=1,\cdots,m_{0}-1
,
v_{\tau}|_{I_{m}}=-\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{-1}z_{\tau}|_{I_{m}}
for
m=m_{0},\cdots,M
and
v_{\tau}(\cdot,T)=z_{\tau}(T)=z_{T}
in (
64
), it is clear that such
v_{\tau}\in Y_{\tau}
and we have

\displaystyle A(v_{\tau},z_{\tau})

\displaystyle=\|\nabla\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{-1}z_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I^{\prime};L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+{1\over 2}(\|z_{\tau}(t_{m_{0}-1})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|z_{\tau}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2})

\displaystyle=-\int^{T}_{t_{m_{0}-1}}\langle g,\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{-1}z_{\tau}\rangle dt+\|z_{T}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I^{\prime};H^{-1}(\Omega))}+{1\over 2}\|\nabla\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{-1}z_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I^{\prime};L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|z_{T}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where

, this implies that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\partial_{t}(-\Delta)^{-1}z_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I^{\prime};L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|z_{\tau}(t_{m_{0}-1})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))}+\|z_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

That is, for any
t_{m}

(m=0,\cdots,M-1)
we have the stability

\displaystyle\|z_{\tau}(t_{m})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))}+\|z_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

i.e.,
\|z_{\tau}\|_{L^{\infty}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{-1}(\Omega))}+\|z_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. This finishes the proof of (
65
).

Next, we prove the estimate (
66
). Similar to the above procedure, for any fixed
m_{0}\in\{1,2,\cdots,M\}
, setting
v_{\tau}|_{I_{m}}=0
for
m=1,\cdots,m_{0}-1
,
v_{\tau}|_{I_{m}}=-\partial_{t}z_{\tau}|_{I_{m}}
for
m=m_{0},\cdots,M
and
v_{\tau}(\cdot,T)=z_{\tau}(T)=z_{T}
in (
64
), it is clear that
v_{\tau}\in Y_{\tau}
and we have

\displaystyle A(v_{\tau},z_{\tau})

\displaystyle=\|\partial_{t}z_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I^{\prime};L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+{1\over 2}(\|\nabla z_{\tau}(t_{m_{0}-1})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\|\nabla z_{\tau}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2})+\|z_{\tau}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int^{T}_{t_{m_{0}-1}}(g,\partial_{t}z_{\tau})dt+\|z_{\tau}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I^{\prime};L^{2}(\Omega))}+{1\over 2}\|\partial_{t}z_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I^{\prime};L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|z_{\tau}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}z_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I^{\prime};L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\|\nabla z_{\tau}(t_{m_{0}-1})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I^{\prime};L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\nabla z_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

then the estimate (

5.2
A priori error estimates for the state and adjoint equations

In this subsection we are now able to give a priori error estimates for the finite element solutions to the state and adjoint equations.

Theorem 19
.

For arbitrary
f\in L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))
,
q\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};H^{1}(\omega))
and
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, let
u\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))
be the solution to problem (
2
) and
u_{\sigma}\in Y_{\sigma}
be its discretization defined in (
54
). Then there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
\sigma
, such that

(75)

\displaystyle\|u-u_{\sigma}(q)\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|(u-u_{\sigma}(q))(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(h+\tau^{1\over 2})(\|f\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))}+\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};H^{1}(\omega))}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}).

Proof.

We split the fully discrete error estimate into the temporal and spatial parts. To begin with, let
u_{\tau}\in Y_{\tau}
be the semi-discrete solution to problem (
63
). Then we estimate respectively
\|u-u_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u(T)-u_{\tau}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
and
\|u_{\tau}-u_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u_{\tau}(T)-u_{\sigma}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. To prove the two estimates we use the duality argument (cf.
[
5
,
26
]
).

We first prove the estimate for
\|u-u_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u(T)-u_{\tau}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
. Let
z\in H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
be the solution to the backward parabolic equation (
5
) with
g:=u-u_{\tau},\ z_{T}:=u(T)-u_{\tau}(T)
. Setting
\tilde{z}_{\tau}:={\Pi}_{P_{\tau}}z\in P_{\tau}
, then there holds
(z-\tilde{z}_{\tau})(T)=0
and

(v_{\tau},\partial_{t}(z-\tilde{z}_{\tau}))_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}=0\quad\forall v_{\tau}\in Y_{\tau}.

From (
37
) and (
63
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{I}(g,u-u_{\tau})dt+(z_{T},u(T)-u_{\tau}(T))=A(u,z)-A(u_{\tau},z_{\tau})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(u,z)-A(u_{\tau},z)-\int_{I}(f,\tilde{z}_{\tau})dt-\langle q,\tilde{z}_{\tau}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}-(u_{0},\tilde{z}_{\tau}(0))+A(u_{\tau},\tilde{z}_{\tau})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{I}(f,z-\tilde{z}_{\tau})dt+\langle q,z-\tilde{z}_{\tau}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}+(u_{0},z(0)-\tilde{z}_{\tau}(0))-A(u_{\tau},z-\tilde{z}_{\tau})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{I}(f,z-\tilde{z}_{\tau})dt+\langle q,z-\tilde{z}_{\tau}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}-\int_{I}(\nabla u_{\tau},\nabla(z-\tilde{z}_{\tau}))dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\Delta u_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\right)\|z-\tilde{z}_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}\|z-\tilde{z}_{\tau}\|_{C(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\tau\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\Delta u_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\right)\|z\|_{H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+C\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}\|z\|_{H^{1}({I}_{c};L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\tau\left(\|f\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\Delta u_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\right)\left(\|g\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|z_{T}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle+C\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}\left(\|g\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|z_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\tau H\left(\|g\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+H\right)+C\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}\left(\|g\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|z_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\tau(1+\tau)H^{2}+C\tau\|q\|^{2}_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}+{1\over 2}(\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|z_{T}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}),

where we have used Lemma
16
, Theorem
3
and

H:=\|f\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))}+\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};H^{1}(\omega))}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
. Therefore, we have obtained

(76)

\displaystyle\|u-u_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u(T)-u_{\tau}(T)\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|f\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))}+\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};H^{1}(\omega))}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}).

Next, we estimate
\|u_{\tau}-u_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u_{\tau}(T)-u_{\sigma}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.
Note that there exists the following splitting:

\displaystyle u_{\tau}-u_{\sigma}=u_{\tau}-\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{\tau}+\mathcal{R}_{h}u_{\tau}-u_{\sigma}:=\eta_{\sigma}+\xi_{\sigma},

is the standard spatial Ritz projection (cf.
[

]

Let
z_{\sigma}\in P_{\sigma}
be the fully discrete solution to problem (
72
) with
g:=\xi_{\sigma}
and
z_{T}:=\xi_{\sigma}(T)
. Taking

in the scheme (

\displaystyle\int_{I}(g,\xi_{\sigma})dt+(z_{T},\xi_{\sigma}(T))=A(\xi_{\sigma},z_{\sigma})=-A(\eta_{\sigma},z_{\sigma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{I}(\eta_{\sigma},\partial_{t}z_{\sigma})-(\nabla\eta_{\sigma},\nabla z_{\sigma})dt-(\eta_{\sigma}(T),z_{\sigma}(T))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{I}(\eta_{\sigma},\partial_{t}z_{\sigma})dt-(\eta_{\sigma}(T),z_{\sigma}(T))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla\eta_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}\|\nabla\partial_{t}(-\Delta_{h})^{-1}z_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\eta_{\sigma}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|z_{\sigma}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|\nabla\eta_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\eta_{\sigma}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})(\|g\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|z_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}),

where we have used Lemma

(77)

\displaystyle\|u_{\tau}-u_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u_{\tau}(T)-u_{\sigma}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|\nabla\eta_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\eta_{\sigma}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch(\|\Delta u_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\nabla u_{\tau}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch(\|f\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{1}(\Omega))}+\|q\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};H^{1}(\omega))}+\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}),

where we have used Lemma
16
. Combining the above two estimates we finish the proof.

Theorem 20
.

For any
z_{T}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
g\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))
, let
z_{\sigma}\in P_{\sigma}
be the solution of the discrete scheme (

), and

be the solution of equation (

). Then there exists a positive constant
C>0
, independent of
\sigma
, such that

(78)

\|z-z_{\sigma}\|_{C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C(h+\tau^{\frac{1}{2}})(\|g\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|z_{T}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}).

Proof.

Let
e_{\sigma}:=z-z_{\sigma}=(z-\pi_{h}\Pi_{P_{\tau}}z)+(\pi_{h}\Pi_{P_{\tau}}z-z_{\sigma})=:\eta_{\sigma}+\zeta_{\sigma}
, then by the Galerkin orthogonality there holds for any

that

(79)

\displaystyle A(v_{\sigma},\zeta_{\sigma})=-A(v_{\sigma},\eta_{\sigma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{I}(v_{\sigma},\partial_{t}\eta_{\sigma})-(\nabla v_{\sigma},\nabla\eta_{\sigma})dt-(v_{\sigma}(T),\eta_{\sigma}(T))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{M}\Big(v_{\sigma}|_{I_{m}},(z-\pi_{h}\Pi_{P_{\tau}}z)(t_{m})-(z-\pi_{h}\Pi_{P_{\tau}}z)(t_{m-1})\Big)

\displaystyle-(v_{\sigma}(T),\eta_{\sigma}(T))-\int_{I}(\nabla v_{\sigma},\nabla\eta_{\sigma})dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{I}(\nabla v_{\sigma},\nabla\eta_{\sigma})dt,

i.e.,
\zeta_{\sigma}
satisfies the following variational problem: Find
\zeta_{\sigma}\in P^{0}_{\sigma}
such that

(80)

\displaystyle\int_{I}-(v_{\sigma},\partial_{t}\zeta_{\sigma})+(\nabla v_{\sigma},\nabla\zeta_{\sigma})dt=-\int_{I}(\nabla v_{\sigma},\nabla\eta_{\sigma})dt\quad\ \forall v_{\sigma}\in Y_{\sigma}.

For arbitrary
1\leq m_{0}\leq M
, taking the test function
v_{\sigma}
satisfying
v_{\sigma}|_{I_{m}}=-\partial_{t}(-\Delta_{h})^{-1}\zeta_{\sigma}|_{I_{m}}
,
m=m_{0},m_{0}+1,\cdots,M
and
v_{\sigma}|_{I_{m}}=0
,
m=1,2,\cdots,m_{0}-1
,
v_{\sigma}(T)=0
in the above identity (
80
), then we have

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\partial_{t}(-\Delta_{h})^{-1}\zeta_{\sigma}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I^{\prime};L^{2}(\Omega))}+\frac{1}{2}\|\zeta_{\sigma}(t_{m_{0}-1})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{m_{0}-1}}^{T}(\nabla\partial_{t}(-\Delta_{h})^{-1}\zeta_{\sigma},\nabla\eta_{\sigma})dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\partial_{t}(-\Delta_{h})^{-1}\zeta_{\sigma}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({I^{\prime}};L^{2}(\Omega))}+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\eta_{\sigma}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({I^{\prime}};L^{2}(\Omega))}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\zeta_{\sigma}(t_{m_{0}-1})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\nabla\eta_{\sigma}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({I^{\prime}};L^{2}(\Omega))}

for arbitrary
1\leq m_{0}\leq M
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\|\zeta_{\sigma}\|_{C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega))}=\max_{1\leq m\leq M-1}\|\zeta_{\sigma}(t_{m})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|\nabla\eta_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}.

Combining with the expression
e_{\sigma}=\eta_{\sigma}+\zeta_{\sigma}
one deduces

(81)

\displaystyle\|e_{\sigma}\|_{C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega))}\leq\|\eta_{\sigma}\|_{C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\nabla\eta_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}.

Then it suffices to bound the two terms on the right-hand side.

The first term on the right-hand side of (
81
) can be bounded by

(82)

\displaystyle\|\eta_{\sigma}\|_{C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|z-\pi_{h}\Pi_{P_{\tau}}z\|_{C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|z-\pi_{h}z\|_{C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\pi_{h}(z-\Pi_{P_{\tau}}z)\|_{C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|z-\pi_{h}z\|_{C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega))}+C\|z-\Pi_{P_{\tau}}z\|_{C(\bar{I};L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch\|z\|_{C(\bar{I};H^{1}(\Omega))}+C\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}\|z\|_{H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(h+\tau^{\frac{1}{2}})(\|z\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|z\|_{H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(h+\tau^{\frac{1}{2}})(\|g\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|z_{T}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}),

where we have used the stability of the
L^{2}
-projection
\pi_{h}
. On the other hand,
\|\nabla\eta_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}
can be estimated by

(83)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\eta_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\nabla(z-\pi_{h}\Pi_{P_{\tau}}z)\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla(z-\pi_{h}z)\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\nabla\pi_{h}(z-\Pi_{P_{\tau}}z)\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla(z-\pi_{h}z)\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\nabla(z-\Pi_{P_{\tau}}z)\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch\|z\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))}+C\tau^{\frac{1}{2}}(\|z\|_{L^{2}(I;H^{2}(\Omega))}+\|z\|_{H^{1}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(h+\tau^{\frac{1}{2}})(\|g\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|z_{T}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}),

where we have used the
H^{1}
-stability of the
L^{2}
-projection
\pi_{h}
and the estimate (
52
) in Lemma
13
. Combining the two estimates (
82
) and (
83
) we finish the proof.
∎

5.3
Error estimates for the optimal control problem

At first we prove a plain convergence for the solution of problem (
53
) to that of problem (
1
) as
|\sigma|:=\tau+h\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

Theorem 21
.

Let
\{\hat{q}_{\sigma}\}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
be the set of optimal controls for the discrete optimal control problem (
53
), and
\bar{u}_{\sigma}\in Y_{\sigma}
be the unique discrete optimal state associated to
\{\hat{q}_{\sigma}\}
. Let
(\bar{q},\bar{u})\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))\times X
be the unique optimal pair of the continuous problem (
1
), where
\bar{q}
is the optimal control and
\bar{u}
is the optimal state. Then we obtain

(84)

\displaystyle{q}_{\sigma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\bar{q}\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))\quad\ \forall q_{\sigma}\in\{\hat{q}_{\sigma}\},

(85)

\displaystyle\|q_{\sigma}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}\rightarrow\|\bar{q}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}\quad\ \forall q_{\sigma}\in\{\hat{q}_{\sigma}\},

(86)

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}_{\sigma}-\bar{u}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|(\bar{u}_{\sigma}-\bar{u})(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0,\quad

(87)

\displaystyle J_{\sigma}(q_{\sigma})\rightarrow J(\bar{q})\quad\ \forall q_{\sigma}\in\{\hat{q}_{\sigma}\},

when
|\sigma|\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

Proof.

The main ideas follow from
[
5
, Theorem 4.9]
and
[
31
, Theorem 1.2]
, see also
[
4
, Theorem 3.5]
. Similar to Theorem
9
, since
q_{\sigma}
is optimal, we can easily show that the sequence
\{q_{\sigma}\}
is uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
with respect to
\sigma
. Then there exists a subsequence, still denoted by
\{q_{\sigma}\}
, such that
q_{\sigma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\tilde{q}
in
\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
for some
\tilde{q}
when
\sigma\rightarrow 0^{+}
. Below, we show that
\tilde{q}=\bar{q}
.

Let
u_{\tilde{q}}
be the solution of equation (
2
) with
q
replaced by
\tilde{q}
. We first show that

(88)

\displaystyle\|u_{\tilde{q}}-\bar{u}_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|(u_{\tilde{q}}-\bar{u}_{\sigma})(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{as}\ |\sigma|\rightarrow 0.

In fact, from the triangle inequality we have

(89)

\displaystyle\|u_{\tilde{q}}-\bar{u}_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|(u_{\tilde{q}}-\bar{u}_{\sigma})(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{\tilde{q}}-u_{q_{\sigma}}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|(u_{\tilde{q}}-u_{q_{\sigma}})(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|u_{q_{\sigma}}-\bar{u}_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|(u_{q_{\sigma}}-\bar{u}_{\sigma})(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Applying Proposition
4
to the first two terms and Theorem
19
to the latter two terms on the right-hand side of the above estimate yields the result.

Next, there holds

(90)

\displaystyle j(\tilde{q})\leq\liminf\limits_{|\sigma|\rightarrow 0}j_{\sigma}(q_{\sigma})\leq\limsup\limits_{|\sigma|\rightarrow 0}j_{\sigma}(q_{\sigma})\leq\limsup\limits_{|\sigma|\rightarrow 0}j_{\sigma}(\Lambda_{\sigma}\bar{q})=j(\bar{q}),

where in the first inequality we have used the weakly-
∗
lower semicontinuity of the cost functional
j
,
in the third inequality we used the optimality of
q_{\sigma}
, while in the last equality we have used (
46
) and (
88
).
Therefore,
\tilde{q}
is also optimal, so
\tilde{q}=\bar{q}
since
\bar{q}
is unique, i.e.,
q_{\sigma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\bar{q}\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
, then
\bar{u}=u_{\tilde{q}}
by the unique solvability of the state equation, which proves (
84
) and (
86
). Obviously, (
87
) can be concluded from (
90
). Lastly, (
85
) follows from (
86
) and (
87
).
∎

A second convergence result concerns the convergence order of the objective functional.

Theorem 22
.

Let
q_{\sigma}\in\{\hat{q}_{\sigma}\}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
be any optimal control to the discrete problem (
53
) and
\bar{q}\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
be the unique optimal control for the continuous problem (
1
). Then there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
\sigma
, such that

(91)

\displaystyle|j(\bar{q})-j_{\sigma}(q_{\sigma})|\leq C(h+\tau^{1\over 2}).

Proof.

The proof uses the approach of
[
5
, Theorem 5.1]
, see also
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
. It follows from the optimality of
\bar{q}
and
q_{\sigma}
that

\displaystyle j(\bar{q})-j_{\sigma}(\bar{q})\leq j(\bar{q})-j_{\sigma}(q_{\sigma})\leq j(q_{\sigma})-j_{\sigma}(q_{\sigma}),

which means that

(92)

\displaystyle|j(\bar{q})-j_{\sigma}(q_{\sigma})|\leq\max\{|j(\bar{q})-j_{\sigma}(\bar{q})|,|j(q_{\sigma})-j_{\sigma}(q_{\sigma})|\}.

Now it remains to estimate the two terms on the right-hand side.

For arbitrary
\tilde{q}\in\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))
, we denote by
u_{\tilde{q}}
and
u_{\sigma}(\tilde{q})
the unique solutions to problems (
2
) and (
54
), respectively. Then we obtain from Theorem
19
that

\displaystyle\|u_{\tilde{q}}-u_{\sigma}(\tilde{q})\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|(u_{\tilde{q}}-u_{\sigma}(\tilde{q}))(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C(h+\tau^{1\over 2}).

It is straightforward to show that

\displaystyle|j(\tilde{q})-j_{\sigma}(\tilde{q})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{1\over 2}\Big|\|u_{\tilde{q}}-u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}-\|u_{\sigma}(\tilde{q})-u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}\Big|

\displaystyle+{\beta\over 2}\Big|\|u_{\tilde{q}}(T)-u_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\|u_{\sigma}(\tilde{q})(T)-u_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|u_{\tilde{q}}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u_{\sigma}(\tilde{q})\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|u_{d}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))})\|u_{\tilde{q}}-u_{\sigma}(\tilde{q})\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle+C(\|u_{\tilde{q}}(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|u_{\sigma}(\tilde{q})(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|u_{T}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\|(u_{\tilde{q}}-u_{\sigma}(\tilde{q}))(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(h+\tau^{1\over 2}),

where we have used Lemma
18
and Theorem
3
. By setting
\tilde{q}=q
and
\tilde{q}=q_{\sigma}
in the above error estimate we finish the proof by considering (
92
).
∎

The last convergence result is about the approximation of the state equation.

Theorem 23
.

Let
\bar{u}\in L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))
be the optimal state of the continuous optimal control problem (
1
) and
\bar{u}_{\sigma}\in Y_{\sigma}
be the discrete optimal state of the discrete optimization problem (
53
). Then there exists a constant
C
, independent of
\sigma
, such that

(93)

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\beta\|(\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{\sigma})(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C(h+\tau^{1\over 2}).

Proof.

In order to obtain the above estimate (
93
), we first introduce two auxiliary variables. The first one is the finite element approximation to the state equation (
2
) with the optimal control
\bar{q}
: Find
\hat{u}_{\sigma}\in Y_{\sigma}
such that

A(\hat{u}_{\sigma},v_{\sigma})=\int_{I}(f,v_{\sigma})dt+\langle\bar{q},v_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}+(u_{0},v_{\sigma}(0))\quad\ \forall v_{\sigma}\in P_{\sigma},

while the second is the finite element approximation to the adjoint equation (
18
) with the optimal state
\bar{u}
: Find
\hat{\varphi}\in P_{\sigma}
such that

A(\omega_{\sigma},\hat{\varphi}_{\sigma})=\int_{I}(\bar{u}-u_{d},\omega_{\sigma})dt+\beta(\bar{u}(T)-u_{T},\omega_{\sigma}(T))\quad\ \forall\omega_{\sigma}\in Y_{\sigma}.

Taking
p=\hat{q}_{\sigma}
in the continuous optimality condition (
20
) and
p=\bar{q}
in the discrete optimality condition (
59
), where
\hat{q}_{\sigma}
is any optimal control for the discrete optimization problem (
55
), then adding them up we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\langle\hat{q}_{\sigma}-\bar{q},\bar{\varphi}-\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\hat{q}_{\sigma}-\bar{q},\bar{\varphi}-\hat{\varphi}_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}+\langle\hat{q}_{\sigma}-\bar{q},\hat{\varphi}_{\sigma}-\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\hat{q}_{\sigma}-\bar{q},\bar{\varphi}-\hat{\varphi}_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}+A(\bar{u}_{\sigma}-\hat{u}_{\sigma},\hat{\varphi}_{\sigma}-\bar{\varphi}_{\sigma})

\displaystyle\langle\hat{q}_{\sigma}-\bar{q},\bar{\varphi}-\hat{\varphi}_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}+(\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{\sigma},\bar{u}_{\sigma}-\hat{u}_{\sigma})+\beta((\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{\sigma})(T),(\bar{u}_{\sigma}-\hat{u}_{\sigma})(T))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\hat{q}_{\sigma}-\bar{q},\bar{\varphi}-\hat{\varphi}_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}+(\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{\sigma},\bar{u}_{\sigma}-\bar{u})+\beta((\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{\sigma})(T),(\bar{u}_{\sigma}-\bar{u})(T))

\displaystyle+(\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{\sigma},\bar{u}-\hat{u}_{\sigma})+\beta((\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{\sigma})(T),(\bar{u}-\hat{u}_{\sigma})(T)).

Therefore, there holds

\displaystyle\|\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{\sigma}\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}^{2}+\beta\|(\bar{u}-\bar{u}_{\sigma})(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\langle\hat{q}_{\sigma}-\bar{q},\bar{\varphi}-\hat{\varphi}_{\sigma}\rangle_{\bar{I}_{c}\times\omega}+\|\bar{u}-\hat{u}_{\sigma}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\beta\|(\bar{u}-\hat{u}_{\sigma})(T)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle 2\|\hat{q}_{\sigma}-\bar{q}\|_{\mathcal{M}(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\omega))}\|\bar{\varphi}-\hat{\varphi}_{\sigma}\|_{C(\bar{I}_{c};L^{2}(\Omega))}+\|\bar{u}-\hat{u}_{\sigma}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I;L^{2}(\Omega))}+\beta\|(\bar{u}-\hat{u}_{\sigma})(T)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle C(h+\tau^{1\over 2}),

where we have used Theorems
19
and
20
.
This finishes the proof.
∎

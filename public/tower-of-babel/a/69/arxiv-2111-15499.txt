3 3 -Manifolds with Constant Ricci Eigenvalues ( λ , λ , 0 ) (\lambda,\lambda,0)
Thomas G. Brooks

Abstract
. We consider complete Riemannian 3 3 -manifolds whose Ricci tensors have constant eigenvalues ( λ , λ , 0 ) (\lambda,\lambda,0) . When π 1 \pi_{1} is finitely generated, we classify the topology of such manifolds by showing that they have a free fundamental group if non-trivial and that every free group is obtained. We give a description up to isometry, when the metric is locally irreducible or when it is analytic.



A manifold

of their tangent spaces such that the curvature tensor

The study of curvature homogeneous manifolds originates with
[

]
where the following question was posed.

Question

.

Are all curvature homogeneous manifolds locally homogeneous?

This was answered in the negative in
[

]
, see (

The following open conjecture provides a related motivation
[

]
.

Conjecture

Fix a compact manifold

Then the space of curvature homogeneous metrics on

(up to isometry) which have curvature tensor

is finite dimensional.

The Sekigawa examples give two infinite dimensional moduli spaces of complete curvature homogeneous metrics, but these are not compact.

Since, in dimension 3, the Ricci tensor determines the curvature tensor, curvature homogeneous manifolds are those manifolds whose Ricci eigenvalues are constant.
Such manifolds are well studied locally
[

,
BKV96
,

]
.
In this paper, we study the global behavior of the special case where the Ricci eigenvalues are
(\lambda,\lambda,0)
.
This is the only case where

can be isometric to a product metric locally.
Since some regions may be locally irreducible while others are locally a product metric, this condition allows for a large class of examples differing in geometry as well as topology.

By scaling, we will assume from now on that
\lambda=-1,0,
or
1

If
\lambda=0,1

or
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}

Hence we will assume that

Apart from quotients of

[

We classify the fundamental group, under the assumption that it is finitely generated, which classifies their topology since they are Hadamard manifolds .

1.
Preliminaries

In the remainder of the paper, we will assume that
M
is complete and that the eigenvalues of the Ricci tensor are constants
(\lambda,\lambda,0)
with
\lambda=\pm 1
unless otherwise stated.
Since the eigenvalues of the Ricci tensor determine the curvature tensor
R
at any point up to an orthogonal transformation, we know that
R
must be pointwise the curvature tensor of
\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}
for
\Sigma
either the round sphere or the hyperbolic plane.

Observe that this implies that at a point
p\in M
, there is a unit eigenvector
T
of the zero eigenvalue of the Ricci tensor such that
\sec(T,X)=0
for all
X
and
\sec(X,Y)=\lambda
when
\{X,Y\}
form a basis of
T^{\perp}\subset T_{p}M
.
Defining
\ker R_{p}:=\{X\in T_{p}M|R(X,\cdot)\cdot=0\}
we get that
T
spans
\ker R_{p}
.
We may define
T
globally on
M
by passing to a double cover if necessary.

It is well known that for any complete manifold, the distribution
\ker R
has complete, totally geodesic leaves on the open subset where
\dim\ker R
is minimal, see
[
Mal72
]
.
Hence the integral curves of
T
are complete geodesics.
We call these the
nullity geodesics
of
M
.
Thus we have that
T^{\perp}
is a parallel distribution along each nullity geodesic.

Define
C:T^{\perp}\rightarrow T^{\perp}
to be the splitting tensor of
T
, i.e.
C(X)=-\nabla_{X}T
.
Note that if
C
vanishes in an open set, then by the de Rham splitting theorem, that set is locally isometric to a product
V\times\mathbb{R}
with
V
a surface of constant curvature
\lambda
.

Along a nullity geodesic
\gamma(t)
, we can choose a parallel basis
\left\{e_{1},e_{2}\right\}
of
\ker R^{\perp}
.
Then
C
written in this basis is a matrix
C(t)
along
\gamma(t)
satisfying

(6)

C^{\prime}(t)=C^{2}

since

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=R(X,T)T=\nabla_{T}(C(X))+C(\nabla_{X}T)=(\nabla_{T}C)(X)-C(C(X)).

Note that (
6
) has solutions
C(t)=C_{0}(I-tC_{0})^{-1}
for some matrix
C_{0}=C(0)
.
Hence any real eigenvalue of
C
must be zero.
Since
C
is a
2\times 2
matrix, it either is nilpotent or has two non-zero complex eigenvalues.

Along a nullity geodesic,

\Scal^{\prime}=-2\trace C\cdot\Scal.

Indeed, from the second Bianchi identity
and the fact that in our case
\Scal=2\left\langle R(X,Y)Y,X\right\rangle
for some orthonormal basis
\{X,Y\}
of
T^{\perp}
,

\displaystyle\Scal^{\prime}

\displaystyle=2\left\langle(\nabla_{T}R)(X,Y)Y,X\right\rangle=2\left\langle R(Y,\nabla_{X}T)Y,X\right\rangle+2\left\langle R(\nabla_{Y}T,X)Y,X\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\Scal\cdot\left\langle C(X),X\right\rangle-2\Scal\cdot\left\langle C(Y),Y\right\rangle=-2\trace C\cdot\Scal

Since
M
has constant scalar curvature, it follows that
\trace C
is zero.
Note that (
6
) also implies that
(\trace C)^{\prime}=\trace(C^{2})=(\trace C)^{2}-2\det C
along a nullity geodesic.
Hence
\det C=0
as well.
Since the only real eigenvalues of
C
are zero, it follows that
C
is nilpotent.

Define
M_{C}
to be the subset of

on which

is non-zero.
Note that
M_{irred}
is the closure of
M_{C}
and that
M_{split}
is the complement of
M_{irred}
, i.e.
M_{split}
is the set of points

where

in a neighborhood of

.
Note that (
6
) implies that if a nullity geodesic intersects

(
M_{C}
respectively) then it is contained in
M_{split}
(
M_{C}
respectively).
By
[

]
Proposition 2.1, the universal cover of any connected component of

is an isometric product

where
\Sigma
is a surface of constant curvature
\lambda
.

By going to a cover if necessary, we can define an orthonormal basis

at any point in
M_{C}
by
T\in\ker R
and
e_{1}\in\ker C
.
Since
C^{\prime}=0
,
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are parallel along nullity geodesics.
There exists a smooth function

on
M_{C}
so that

.
Hence

\nabla_{T}e_{1}=\nabla_{T}e_{2}=\nabla_{T}T=0,\quad\nabla_{e_{1}}T=0,\quad\nabla_{e_{2}}T=-ae_{1}

\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{1}=\alpha e_{2},\quad\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{2}=\beta e_{1},\quad\nabla_{e_{1}}e_{2}=-\alpha e_{1},\quad\nabla_{e_{2}}e_{1}=aT-\beta e_{2}

for some smooth functions

.
Thus for the curvature tensor we have

\displaystyle R(e_{2},e_{1})e_{1}

\displaystyle=(e_{1}(\beta)+e_{2}(\alpha)-\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2})e_{2}+(a\beta-e_{1}(a))T

\displaystyle R(e_{1},e_{2})e_{2}

\displaystyle=(e_{1}(\beta)+e_{2}(\alpha)-\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2})e_{2}+\alpha aT.

Since

, this implies that

\alpha=0,\quad\Scal_{M}=e_{1}(\beta)-\beta^{2},\quad\mbox{ and }\quad e_{1}(a)=a\beta.

Again, since

, i.e.

and

We let

Note that (

We can scale the metric so that

4.

We now consider a lower-dimensional analog of

This will be used in Theorem

.
Again, these foliations are Lipschitz by

]
and have

We study some basic properties of these curves.

Let
H
be the turning angle of
\gamma
, i.e. the angle between
\gamma^{\prime}(x)
and
V
where
V
is a parallel translation of
\gamma^{\prime}(0)
along
\gamma
.
The following lemma about turning angles is no doubt well-known, but we provide a proof for completeness since we were unable to find a reference for it.

Lemma 17
.

Suppose that
H:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is locally Lipschitz and
\Sigma
is a complete surface.
For any starting point
p_{0}
and initial unit vector
v_{0}
, there exists a unique arc-length parametrized
C^{1,1}
curve
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\Sigma
whose turning angle at
\gamma(t)
is
H(t)
.

Proof.

Choose local coordinates
\mathbf{x}
of a neighborhood
U\subset\Sigma
of
p_{0}
.
We proceed by modifying the standard argument for the existence of the geodesic flow on
TU
.
Choose coordinates
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
on
TU
such that
\mathbf{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}y_{i}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
.
For two vectors
\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z}
in
T_{\mathbf{x}}U
, define
\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z})=-\sum_{i,j}\Gamma^{k}_{ij}y_{i}z_{j}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y_{k}}
where
\Gamma^{k}_{ij}
are the Christoffel symbols at
\mathbf{x}
.

Define
\Theta_{\mathbf{x},r}:T_{\mathbf{x}}U\rightarrow T_{\mathbf{x}}U
to be the rotation of of
T_{\mathbf{x}}U
by angle
r
at each
\mathbf{x}
and
W
, a time-dependent vector field on
TU
given by

W(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},t)=(\Theta_{\mathbf{x},H(t)}(\mathbf{y}),\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{y},\Theta_{H(t)}(\mathbf{y})).

Then the ODE defined by
\frac{d}{dt}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=W(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y},t)
is continuous in
t
and smooth in
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
.
By the standard Picard-Lindelöf theorem, there exists a unique solution
(\gamma(t),Y(t))
where
\gamma
is a
C^{1,1}
curve and
Y
a vector field along that curve.
We choose initial conditions so that
\gamma(0)=p_{0}
and
Y(0)=\gamma^{\prime}(0)=v_{0}
.

Now we compute
\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}Y
.
Writing
Y=(y_{1},y_{2})
and
\gamma^{\prime}=(z_{1},z_{2})
we get that

\displaystyle\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}Y

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\left(\sum_{ij}\Gamma^{k}_{ij}y_{i}z_{j}+\gamma^{\prime}(y_{k})\right)\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{x}}(Y,\gamma^{\prime}(t))+\sum_{k}\frac{d}{dt}(y_{k})\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}

\displaystyle=-\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{x}}(\gamma^{\prime}(t),Y)+\mathbf{v}_{\mathbf{x}}(\gamma^{\prime}(t),Y)=0

so

is parallel along
\gamma(t)
and
\left\|\gamma^{\prime}\right\|=1
.
By the ODE,
\gamma^{\prime}=\Theta_{H(t)}(Y)
, and
\gamma
has turning angle

.

Lastly, note that Picard-Lindelöf gives existence of
\gamma
and

for at least time

is the maximum derivative of components of

on
U
.
For

, there exists such a

centered at
\gamma(0)
.
Therefore we may repeatedly extend the existence of
\gamma
until either it exists for at least time

or it has left the ball

.
Since
\gamma
has unit speed, the second case cannot occur without also existing until at least time

.
Taking

,

exists for all time.
∎

Suppose that
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2}

Let

be a unit vector field along

that is perpendicular to

everywhere.
Define

\exp^{\perp}(s,t)=\exp_{\gamma(s)}(tX).

Proposition 18
.

Fix a point

and a vector
V\in T_{p}\mathbb{H}^{2}
.
There is a bijection between Lipschitz functions
H:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with Lipschitz constant

and arc-length parameterized curves
\gamma
such that

(a)

\gamma(0)=p
,

(b)

\gamma^{\prime}(0)=V
, and

(c)

\mathbb{H}^{2}
.

In particular,

is the turning angle of the corresponding

Proof.

Lemma
17

is determined uniquely by its starting conditions and turning angle

.

Next, we assume that
\gamma
satisfies (a) and (b) and has turning angle

.
To see that (c) holds, it suffices to show that each point

has a unique closest point

\gamma
.
Then

\mathbb{H}^{2}
.

Let
\delta(q)=d(p,q)
be the distance function to

.
By standard hyperbolic trigonometry,

\nabla_{X}\grad\delta=\coth(\delta)\left\langle X,\grad\delta^{\perp}\right\rangle\grad\delta^{\perp}

\grad\delta^{\perp}
is a unit vector orthogonal to

Note that almost everywhere

and

The case where

Since

So

\displaystyle(L\circ\gamma)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\grad L,\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle=\sinh(\delta)\left\langle\grad\delta,\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle,

\displaystyle(L\circ\gamma)^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\cosh(\delta)\left\langle\grad\delta,\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle^{2}+\sinh(\delta)\left[\coth(\delta)\left\langle\grad\delta^{\perp},\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle^{2}+\left\langle\grad\delta,h(t)(\gamma^{\prime})^{\perp}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\cosh(\delta)+h(t)\sinh(\delta)\left\langle\grad\delta,(\gamma^{\prime})^{\perp}\right\rangle.

Since the Lipschitz constant of

, and therefore

For the converse, we assume that

In
[

it is shown that any co-dimension one geodesic foliation of a smooth manifold is locally Lipschitz.
So

is differentiable a.e.
If

does not have Lipschitz constant 1, then there is a point

\left|h(x)\right|>1
, where

.
Then it is possible to choose

.
Note that the Jacobi field of geodesics orthogonal to

(where

Then

and so the orthogonal geodesics do not foliate

.
∎

Recall that the geodesic curvature of a curve is the derivative of its turning angle

.

If the orthogonal geodesics of

Lemma 37
.

Suppose that
U
is a split region of
\widetilde{M}
with at least one boundary component.
If
G(U)\subset U
, then
G
is a free group.

Proof.

Since

on

,

is isometric to
\Sigma\times\mathbb{R}
with
\Sigma
a subset of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
with complete geodesics for its boundary components.
Suppose for contradiction that there is a non-trivial

fixes a point

Then let

where
\left\{\gamma_{j}\right\}

Then

must act on the set

of those boundary components.

is invariant under

.
But then the boundary

of

is invariant under

By the previous lemma, we know the only such isometries are trivial.
Then

has order at most

cannot have torsion.
So

must act fixed-point freely on
\Sigma

Similarly, we can see that

acts properly discontinuously on
\Sigma
.
Suppose that
p_{0}\in U
and there is a sequence of distinct points

,

with

.
Then let

be any geodesic of minimal distance to

For
\epsilon>0
, choose

for

Each

Hence there exists

of distance at most

from

Lemma 38

Proof.

We again use Lemma

Specifically, pick a leaf

of

of

Next, since

\displaystyle A(p_{0},q_{1})-A(p_{0},q_{2})

\displaystyle=(A(p_{0},p_{0}^{\prime})+A(p_{0}^{\prime},q_{1}))-(A(p_{0},p_{0}^{\prime})+A(p_{0}^{\prime},q_{1}))

\displaystyle=A(p_{0}^{\prime},q_{1})-A(p_{0}^{\prime},q_{2}).

\displaystyle=A(L_{0},g(L_{p}))-A(L_{0},g(L_{q}))

\displaystyle=A(g(L_{0}),g(L_{p}))-A(g(L_{0}),g(L_{q}))

Then

We may now assume without loss of generality that

By assumption, there are

Proof.

Note that for any

implies that the leaf

must have
p_{0}
and
hp_{0}
on the same side.
Thefore there must be a split region

such that

has

and
hp_{0}
on three separate connected components.
To see this, take

to be the split region with the following three leaves of

•

L_{p_{0}}

to

and

(which is therefore also the last leaf from

to

Series representations for the characteristic function of the multidimensional Markov random flight


Abstract
Two series representations of the characteristic function of the multidimensional symmetric Markov random flight with respect to Bessel functions and with respect to time variable, are given. Asymptotic formula for the second mixed moment function of the three-dimensional random flight is presented.

1
Introduction

Finite-velocity random motions in the Euclidean spaces of different dimensions whose evolution is driven by a homogeneous Poisson process, also called the Markov random flights, have become a very interesting and attractive model in recent decades. Historically, this field of stochastic analysis has started from the pioneering works by S. Goldstein
[
11
]
and M. Kac
[
13
]
, where a random motion of a particle moving with finite speed on the real line
\mathbb{R}^{1}
and alternating its direction at Poissonian random time instants, was examined. The main result obtained in these works states that the transition density of the motion is the fundamental solution (the Green’s function) to the hyperbolic telegraph equation (also called the damped wave equation), and can be found by solving it with respective initial conditions. Such one-dimensional stochastic motion, which is now referred to as the Goldstein-Kac telegraph process, as well as its numerous generalizations, have become the subject of a lot of researches, both theoretical and applied. The most important feature of the Goldstein-Kac telegraph process is that it can serve as an appropriate model of the one-dimensional transport that generates a diffusion with finite speed of propagation. For this reason, a generalized telegraph process has become a basis for describing many important physical properties of hyperbolic finite-velocity transport

[
1
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
. Relativistic aspects of the diffusion generated by finite-velocity random motions on the line, as well as their connections with some other physical processes, were given in
[
4
,
5
]
. An approach to modeling the finite-velocity radioactive transfer based on some aspects of random flights theory was developed in
[
6
]
.
A model of cosmic microwave background (CMB) radiation described by a telegraph equation with random initial conditions on the surface of the unit sphere in the three-dimensional space, as well as a number of its astrophysical interpretations, was presented in recent works
[
2
,
3
]
. At present, the number of works devoted to telegraph processes and their applications in various fields of science, technology and engineering is really huge. The reader interested in the modern theory of telegraph processes and their applications in financial modeling may address to recent monograph
[
20
]
.

The multidimensional counterpart of the Goldstein-Kac telegraph process is one of the most important
generalizations performed by a particle moving at constant finite speed in the multidimensional Euclidean
space
\mathbb{R}^{m},\;m\geq 2
, and changing its direction at random Poissonian time instants by choosing the initial and new random directions on the surface of the unit
(m-1)
-dimensional sphere according to some probability law. Such multidimensional stochastic motions are now referred to as the Markov random flights.
The main difference between the one-dimensional Goldstein-Kac telegraph process and its multidimensional counterparts steams from the fact that, while there are only two possible directions of motion on the real line
\mathbb{R}^{1}
, the multidimensional motion in the space
\mathbb{R}^{m},\;m\geq 2
, has a continuum number of directions. Moreover, it turned out that the difficulty of analysis of the multidimensional Markov random flights fundamentally depends on the dimension of the phase space. While in the even-dimensional spaces

\mathbb{R}^{2},\;\mathbb{R}^{4}
and
\mathbb{R}^{6}
one managed to obtain the transition densities of the motions in explicit forms (see
[
22
,
25
,
19
,
18
,
15
]
), in other dimensions, including the most interesting and important case of Markov random flight in the three-dimensional space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, such analysis is turned out to be extremely difficult. For more details on the modern theory of multidimensional Markov random flights, see recent monograph
[
14
]
.

To overcome these difficulties, a method of integral transforms for describing the behaviour of multidimensional Markov random flights, was developed (see
[
16
]
or
[
14
, Chapter 4]
). In the framework of this method, the basic characteristics of the Markov random flights in arbitrary dimensions
in terms of Fourier-Laplace transforms, were given. One of the most important results is an explicit formula for the Fourier-Laplace transform of the transition density of the
m
-dimensional symmetric Markov random flight expressed in terms of Gauss hypergeometric function (see formula (
10
) below). In order to get the most desirable goal, namely, the transition probability density, one needs to evaluate the double inverse Fourier and Laplace transforms of general relation (
10
). However, this is an impracticable, at least directly, problem due to its very complicated form. That is why we make the first step in solving this problem and obtain two series representations of the characteristic function of the
m
-dimensional symmetric Markov random flight by evaluating the inverse Laplace transform of general relation (
10
).

The article is organized as follows. In Section 2 we describe the
m
-dimensional symmetric Markov random flight and the structure of its distribution. We recall the general formula (
10
) for the Fourier-Laplace transform of the transition density of the process. We also give a simple, but useful, auxiliary lemma concerning series decompositions of two special functions related to the Gauss hypergeometric function. This lemma will be applied in the next Sections 3 and 4 where two series representations of the characteristic function of the process with respect to Bessel functions and with respect to time variable

t
, will be obtained. The coefficients of these series representations are given by recurrent relations,
as well as in the form of special determinants. Using these results in the final Section 5, we study the mixed moment function of the symmetric Markov random flight in the three-dimensional Euclidean space

\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We show that, for arbitrary time
t>0
, the first mixed moment is zero. For the second mixed moment function, we give an asymptotic relation with respect to the powers of time variable
t
. The error in this asymptotic relation has the order
o(t^{7})
, that provides a very good approximation of the second mixed moment function for not too big values of time variable
t
.

2
Preliminaries

In this auxiliary section we give some basic notions and known results on the multidimensional
symmetric Markov random flights which our further analysis will be relying on.

Consider a particle that, at the initial time instant
t=0
, starts from the
origin
\mathbb{0}=(0,\dots,0)
of the
m
-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{m},\;m\geq 2
.
The particle moves with a constant finite speed
c
(note that
c
is treated as the
constant norm of the velocity). The initial direction is a random
m
-dimensional vector
uniformly distributed (the Lebesgue probability measure) on the
unit sphere

S_{1}^{m}=\left\{\mathbb{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\;\|\mathbb{x}\|^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{m}^{2}=1\right\}.

(1)

We emphasize that here and thereafter the upper index
m
means the dimension of the space,
which the sphere
S_{1}^{m}
is considered in, not its own dimension which, clearly, is
m-1
.
The particle changes its direction at random time instants that form a homogeneous Poisson
flow of rate
\lambda>0
. In each of such Poissonian moments the particle instantly takes on a new random direction uniformly distributed on
S_{1}^{m}
independently of its previous direction.
Each sample path of this motion represents a broken line of total length
ct
composed of
the segments of
\lambda
-exponentially distributed random lengths and uniformly oriented
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The trajectories of the process are continuous and differentiable almost everywhere.

Let
\mathbb{X}(t)=(X_{1}(t),\dots,X_{m}(t))
be the particle’s position at
arbitrary time instant
t>0
. The stochastic process
\mathbb{X}(t)
is referred to as the

Markov random flight
. Let
N(t)
denote the number of Poisson events that
have occurred in the time interval
(0,t)
and let
d\mathbb{x}
be an infinitesimal
element in the space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with Lebesgue measure
\mu(d\mathbb{x})=dx_{1}\dots dx_{m}
.

At arbitrary time moment
t>0
the particle, with probability 1, is located in the

m
-dimensional ball of radius
ct
:

\mathbb{B}_{ct}^{m}=\left\{\mathbb{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\;\|\mathbb{x}\|^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{m}^{2}\leq c^{2}t^{2}\right\}.

(2)

The distribution

\text{Pr}\left\{\mathbb{X}(t)\in d\mathbb{x}\right\}=\text{Pr}\left\{X_{1}(t)\in dx_{1},\dots,X_{m}(t)\in dx_{m}\right\},\quad\mathbb{x}\in\mathbb{B}_{ct}^{m},\quad t>0,

(3)

consists of two components. The singular component of distribution (
3
)
is related to the case when no Poisson events occur in the time interval
(0,t)

and, therefore, the particle does not change its initial direction. The singular component
is concentrated on the sphere of radius
ct
:

S_{ct}^{m}=\partial\mathbb{B}_{ct}^{m}=\left\{\mathbb{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\;\|\mathbb{x}\|^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{m}^{2}=c^{2}t^{2}\right\}.

(4)

The probability of being on
S_{ct}^{m}
at time moment
t
is the probability
that no Poisson events occur until time
t
and it is equal to

\text{Pr}\left\{\mathbb{X}(t)\in S_{ct}^{m}\right\}=e^{-\lambda t}.

From this fact it follows that the density (in the sense of generalized functions)
of the singular part of distribution (
3
) has the form:

p^{(s)}(\mathbb{x},t)=\frac{e^{-\lambda t}}{\text{mes}(S_{ct}^{m})}\;\delta(c^{2}t^{2}-\|\mathbb{x}\|^{2})=\frac{e^{-\lambda t}\;\Gamma\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)}{2\pi^{m/2}(ct)^{m-1}}\;\delta(c^{2}t^{2}-\|\mathbb{x}\|^{2}),\qquad m\geq 2,

(5)

where
\delta(x)
is the Dirac delta-function and
\text{mes}(S_{ct}^{m})

is the surface measure of sphere
S_{ct}^{m}
.

If at least one Poisson event occurs in the time interval
(0,t)
and, therefore,
the particle at least once changes its direction, then, at time
t
, it is located
strictly inside the ball
\mathbb{B}_{ct}^{m}
and the probability of this event
is equal to

\text{Pr}\left\{\mathbb{X}(t)\in\text{int}\;\mathbb{B}_{ct}^{m}\right\}=1-e^{-\lambda t}.

(6)

The part of distribution (
3
) corresponding to this case is concentrated
in the interior of the ball
\mathbb{B}_{ct}^{m}

\text{int}\;\mathbb{B}_{ct}^{m}=\left\{\mathbb{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:\;\|\mathbb{x}\|^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{m}^{2}<c^{2}t^{2}\right\}

(7)

and forms its absolutely continuous component. Therefore, there exists the density of the absolutely continuous component of the distribution (
3
)

p^{(ac)}(\mathbb{x},t)=f(\mathbb{x},t)\Theta(ct-\|\mathbb{x}\|),\qquad\mathbb{x}\in\text{int}\;\mathbb{B}_{ct}^{m},\quad t>0,

(8)

where
f(\mathbb{x},t)
is some positive function absolutely continuous in
\text{int}\;\mathbb{B}_{ct}^{m}

and
\Theta(x)
is the Heaviside unit-step function. The existence of
density (
8
) follows from the fact that, since the sample paths of the process
\mathbb{X}(t)

are continuous and differentiable almost everywhere, the distribution (
3
) must contain an absolutely continuous component and this justifies the existence of density (
8
).
The existence of this density follows also from the fact that it can be represented as a Poissonian
sum of convolutions.

Hence, the density of distribution (
3
) has the structure

p(\mathbb{x},t)=p^{(s)}(\mathbb{x},t)+p^{(ac)}(\mathbb{x},t),\qquad\mathbb{x}\in\mathbb{B}_{ct}^{m},\quad t>0,

where
p^{(s)}(\mathbb{x},t)
and
p^{(ac)}(\mathbb{x},t)
are the densities (in the sense
of generalized functions) of the singular and absolutely continuous components
of distribution (
3
) given by (
5
) and (
8
), respectively.

Consider the characteristic function of the Markov random flight
\mathbb{X}(t)
defined by

H(\boldsymbol{\alpha},t)=\mathbb{E}\left\{e^{i\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha},\mathbb{X}(t)\rangle}\right\},

(9)

where
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the real-valued

m
-dimensional vector of inversion parameters and
\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha},\mathbb{X}(t)\rangle

is the inner product of the vectors
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
and
\mathbb{X}(t)
.

It is known (see
[
14
, Theorem 4.6.1]
or
[
16
, Formula (4.8)]
) that the Laplace transform
of the characteristic function (
9
) of the
m
-dimensional symmetric Markov random flight

\mathbb{X}(t)
is given by the formula:

\mathcal{L}\left[H(\boldsymbol{\alpha},t)\right](s)=\frac{F\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{m-2}{2};\frac{m}{2};\frac{(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}}{(s+\lambda)^{2}+(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}}\right)}{\sqrt{(s+\lambda)^{2}+(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}}-\lambda\;F\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{m-2}{2};\frac{m}{2};\frac{(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}}{(s+\lambda)^{2}+(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}}\right)},\quad m\geq 2,

(10)

where

F(\xi,\eta;\zeta;z)\equiv\;_{2}F_{1}(\xi,\eta;\zeta;z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{(\xi)_{n}(\eta)_{n}}{(\zeta)_{n}}\dfrac{z^{n}}{n!}

(11)

is the Gauss hypergeometric function and

(a)_{n}=a(a+1)\dots(a+n-1)=\frac{\Gamma(a+n)}{\Gamma(a)}

is the Pochhammer symbol.

Function (
10
) is holomorphic (analytical)
and single-valued in the right half-plane of the complex plane, that is, for
\text{Re}\;s>0
.
It contains, in a hidden form, all the information concerning the distribution
of
\mathbb{X}(t)
. In order to obtain the distribution, one needs to evaluate the inverse Laplace (with respect to complex parameter
s
) and Fourier (with respect to
m
-dimensional parameter

\boldsymbol{\alpha}
) transforms of function (
10
). However, since it has a very complicated form, such double inversion seems to be an impracticable problem.

In this article we make the first step in this direction and derive two series representations of the characteristic function of the process
\mathbb{X}(t)
by evaluating the inverse Laplace transform of
function (
10
). The first representation is a functional series with respect to Bessel functions
with variable indices. The second one is a series with respect to time variable
t
.

Thus, in view of (
10
) and the well-known properties of Laplace transform,
we can write down the general formula for the characteristic function
H(t)
as follows:

H(\boldsymbol{\alpha},t)=e^{-\lambda t}\;\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\left[\frac{F\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{m-2}{2};\frac{m}{2};\frac{(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}}{s^{2}+(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}}\right)}{\sqrt{s^{2}+(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}}-\lambda\;F\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{m-2}{2};\frac{m}{2};\frac{(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}}{s^{2}+(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}}\right)}\right](t),\qquad m\geq 2,

(12)

where
\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}
means the inverse Laplace transformation with respect to inversion parameter
s
.
Our main efforts will be focused on evaluating the inverse Laplace transform in (
12
).

The forthcoming analysis is substantially based on the following simple auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 1.

For arbitrary real constant
b
and arbitrary positive constant
\lambda>0
, the following
series decompositions hold:

zF(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;bz^{2})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\xi_{n}\;z^{n},

(13)

1-\lambda zF(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;bz^{2})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\eta_{n}\;z^{n},

(14)

where the coefficients
\xi_{n},\;\eta_{n}
are given by the formulas:

\xi_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 0,\qquad&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r,\\
\frac{(\alpha)_{r}\;(\beta)_{r}}{(\gamma)_{r}\;\;r!}\;b^{r},&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r+1,\end{aligned}\right.\qquad r=0,1,2,\dots

(15)

\eta_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 1,\qquad\qquad&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=0,\\
0,\qquad\qquad&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r,\\
-\lambda\;\frac{(\alpha)_{r-1}\;(\beta)_{r-1}}{(\gamma)_{r-1}\;\;(r-1)!}\;b^{r-1},&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r-1,\end{aligned}\right.\qquad r=1,2,3,\dots

(16)

Proof.

Using series representation of the Gauss hypergeometric function, we have:

\displaystyle zF(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;bz^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha)_{l}\;(\beta)_{l}\;b^{l}}{(\gamma)_{l}\;\;l!}\;z^{2l+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\;\text{is odd}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha)_{(k-1)/2}\;(\beta)_{(k-1)/2}\;\;b^{(k-1)/2}}{(\gamma)_{(k-1)/2}\;\;\left(\frac{k-1}{2}\right)!}\;z^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\xi_{n}\;z^{n},

where the coefficients
\xi_{n}
are given by (
15
).
Series representation (
14
) immediately follows from (
13
).
The lemma is proved.
∎

In particular, formula (
15
) yields:

\xi_{0}=\xi_{2}=\xi_{4}=0,\qquad\xi_{1}=1,\quad\xi_{3}=b\;\frac{\alpha\beta}{\gamma},\quad\xi_{5}=\frac{b^{2}}{2}\;\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)\beta(\beta+1)}{\gamma(\gamma+1)}.

Similarly, from (
16
) we get:

\eta_{0}=1,\quad\eta_{1}=-\lambda,\quad\eta_{2}=\eta_{4}=0,\quad\eta_{3}=-\lambda b\;\frac{\alpha\beta}{\gamma},\quad\eta_{5}=-\frac{\lambda b^{2}}{2}\;\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)\beta(\beta+1)}{\gamma(\gamma+1)}.

We see that the coefficients
\xi_{n}
and
\eta_{n}
are different for the index
n=0
, that is,
\xi_{0}=0
,
while
\eta_{0}=1
. For all other indices, these coefficients are connected with each other by the relation

\eta_{n}=-\lambda\xi_{n},\qquad n\geq 1.

(17)

Note also that both these coefficients are equal to zero for even indices, that is,

\xi_{2k}=\eta_{2k}=0,\;k\geq 1
.

3
Series representation with respect to Bessel functions

In this section we derive a series representation of the characteristic function
H(\boldsymbol{\alpha},t)

of the
m
-dimensional symmetric Markov random flight
\mathbb{X}(t)
with respect to Besel functions. Since the planar case (
m=2
) is well studied and its characteristic function is already known in the integral and series forms (see, for instance,
[
14
, Theorem 5.3.1]
), we omit this planar case and consider only
the higher dimensions
m\geq 3
. The main result of this section is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 1.

For arbitrary dimension
m\geq 3
, the characteristic function
H(\boldsymbol{\alpha},t)
of the

m
-dimensional symmetric Markov random flight
\mathbb{X}(t)
has the following series representation:

H(\boldsymbol{\alpha},t)=e^{-\lambda t}\;\sqrt{\pi}\;\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}\;\left(\frac{t}{2c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|}\right)^{(n-1)/2}J_{(n-1)/2}(ct\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|),

(18)

where
J_{\nu}(z)
are Bessel functions and the coefficients
\zeta_{n}=\zeta_{n}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})

are given by the recurrent relation:

\zeta_{1}=1,\qquad\zeta_{n}=\xi_{n}+\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\zeta_{n-k}\;\xi_{k},\qquad n\geq 2,

(19)

with

\xi_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 0,\hskip 85.35826pt&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r,\\
\frac{(2r-1)!!}{(2r)!!}\;\frac{m-2}{2r+m-2}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2r},&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r+1,\end{aligned}\right.\qquad r=0,1,2,\dots

(20)

where

(2r)!!=2\cdot 4\cdot 6\cdot\;\dots\;\cdot(2r),\;\;(2r-1)!!=1\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdot\;\dots\;\cdot(2r-1),\;r\geq 0,\;\;0!!=(-1)!!\overset{\text{def}}{=}1
.

Proof.

Consider separately the fraction in square brackets in (
12
).
Dividing the numerator and denominator of this fraction by

\sqrt{s^{2}+(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}}
and introducing the variable

z=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{2}+(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}}}

(21)

we can represent it as follows:

A(z)=\frac{z\;F\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{m-2}{2};\frac{m}{2};c^{2}\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|^{2}z^{2}\right)}{1-\lambda z\;F\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{m-2}{2};\frac{m}{2};c^{2}\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|^{2}z^{2}\right)}

(22)

Applying Lemma 1 to this fraction, we get:

A(z)=\frac{\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\xi_{n}\;z^{n}}{\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\eta_{n}\;z^{n}},

(23)

where, according to (
15
) and (
16
), the coefficients
\xi_{n},\;\eta_{n}
are given by

\xi_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 0,\qquad&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r,\\
\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}\;\left(\frac{m-2}{2}\right)_{r}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2r}}{\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)_{r}\;\;r!},&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r+1,\end{aligned}\right.\qquad r=0,1,2,\dots

(24)

\eta_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 1,\qquad\qquad&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=0,\\
0,\qquad\qquad&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r,\\
-\lambda\;\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r-1}\;\left(\frac{m-2}{2}\right)_{r-1}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2(r-1)}}{\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)_{r-1}\;\;(r-1)!},&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r-1,\end{aligned}\right.\qquad r=1,2,3,\dots

(25)

Coefficients (
24
) and (
25
) depend on the vector variable
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
, but, for the sake of brevity, we omit it thereafter, as well as in the coefficients

\zeta_{n}=\zeta_{n}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
(see recurrent relation (
30
) below), bearing in mind, however, that all these coefficients are, in fact, functions of the vector variable
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
(more precisely, of its norm
\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|
).

Using the definition of Pochhammer symbol and the relation

\frac{(a)_{k}}{(a+1)_{k}}=\frac{a}{a+k},

as well as the well-known relations for Euler gamma-function:

\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=\sqrt{\pi},\qquad\Gamma\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{k}}(2k-1)!!,\qquad\Gamma(x+1)=x\Gamma(x),\qquad k\in\mathbb{N},

(26)

one can easily show that

\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}=2^{-2r}\;\frac{(2r)!}{r!},\qquad\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r-1}=2^{-2(r-1)}\;\frac{(2r-2)!}{(r-1)!},

\frac{\left(\frac{m-2}{2}\right)_{r}}{\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)_{r}}=\frac{m-2}{2r+m-2},\qquad\frac{\left(\frac{m-2}{2}\right)_{r-1}}{\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)_{r-1}}=\frac{m-2}{2r+m-4},

and, therefore, coefficients (
24
) and (
25
) take the form:

\xi_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 0,\hskip 85.35826pt&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r,\\
\frac{(2r-1)!!}{(2r)!!}\;\frac{m-2}{2r+m-2}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2r},&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r+1,\end{aligned}\right.\qquad r=0,1,2,\dots

(27)

\eta_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 1,\hskip 85.35826pt&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=0,\\
0,\hskip 85.35826pt&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r,\\
-\lambda\;\frac{(2r-3)!!}{(2r-2)!!}\;\frac{m-2}{2r+m-4}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2r-2},&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r-1,\end{aligned}\right.\qquad r=1,2,3,\dots

(28)

where

(2r)!!=2\cdot 4\cdot 6\cdot\;\dots\;\cdot(2r),\;\;(2r-1)!!=1\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdot\;\dots\;\cdot(2r-1),\;r\geq 0,\;\;0!!=(-1)!!\overset{\text{def}}{=}1
.

Applying now a formula for the quotient of two power series (see, for instance,
[
12
, Formula 0.313]

or
[
23
, page 754, item 4]
) to fraction (
23
) and taking into account (
21
), we get:

A(s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n}}{(s^{2}+(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2})^{n/2}},

(29)

where the coefficients
\zeta_{n},\;n\geq 0,
are given by the recurrent relation:

\zeta_{0}=0,\qquad\zeta_{1}=1,\qquad\zeta_{n}=\xi_{n}-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\zeta_{n-k}\;\eta_{k},\qquad n\geq 2.

(30)

In particular, in view of (
27
) and (
28
), we have:

\displaystyle\zeta_{0}

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\zeta_{1}=1,\quad\zeta_{2}=\lambda,\qquad\zeta_{3}=\lambda^{2}+\frac{m-2}{2m}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2},

(31)

\displaystyle\zeta_{4}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{3}+\lambda\;\frac{m-2}{m}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}\qquad\zeta_{5}=\lambda^{4}+\frac{3}{2}\;\lambda^{2}\;\frac{m-2}{m}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}+\frac{3}{8}\;\frac{m-2}{m+2}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{4}.

Series (
29
) is uniformly convergent in the right half-plane of the complex plane. Applying
the inverse Laplace transformation
\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}
to this series (
29
) and taking into account
(see, for instance
[
21
, Table 8.4-1, Formula 57]
) that

\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{(s^{2}+a^{2})^{k}}\right](t)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(k)}\;\left(\frac{t}{2a}\right)^{k-1/2}J_{k-1/2}(at),\qquad k>0,

where
J_{\nu}(z)
is Bessel function, we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}[A(s)](t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{n}\;\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{(s^{2}+(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2})^{n/2}}\right](t)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\pi}\;\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{n}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)}\;\left(\frac{t}{2c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|}\right)^{(n-1)/2}J_{(n-1)/2}(ct\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|).

Returning to (
12
), we arrive at the statement of the theorem.
∎

4
Series representation with respect to time variable

Theorem 1 yields a series representation of the characteristic function
H(\boldsymbol{\alpha},t)
with respect to Bessel functions with variable indices. However, one can also obtain a sereis representation
of
H(\boldsymbol{\alpha},t)
with respect to powers of time variable
t
. This result is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 2.

For arbitrary dimension
m\geq 3
, the characteristic function
H(\boldsymbol{\alpha},t)
of the
m
-dimensional symmetric Markov random flight
\mathbb{X}(t)
has the following series representation:

H(\boldsymbol{\alpha},t)=e^{-\lambda t}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma_{n+1}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})}{n!}\;t^{n},

(35)

where the coefficients
\gamma_{n}=\gamma_{n}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
are given by the recurrent relation:

\gamma_{1}=1,\qquad\gamma_{n}=\theta_{n}+\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\gamma_{n-k}\;\theta_{k},\qquad n\geq 2,

(36)

and

\theta_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 0,\qquad&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r,\\
(-1)^{r}\;\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}{\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)_{r}}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2r},&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r+1,\end{aligned}\right.\qquad r=0,1,2,\dots

(37)

Proof.

According to
[
12
, Formula 9.131(1)]
,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{2}+(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}}}\;F\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{m-2}{2};\frac{m}{2};\frac{(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}}{s^{2}+(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{s}\;F\left(\frac{1}{2},1;\frac{m}{2};-\frac{(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}}{s^{2}}\right),

and, therefore, by introducing the variable

z=\frac{1}{s}

(38)

we can rewrite formula (
22
) as follows:

A(z)=\frac{z\;F\left(\frac{1}{2},1;\frac{m}{2};-c^{2}\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|^{2}z^{2}\right)}{1-\lambda z\;F\left(\frac{1}{2},1;\frac{m}{2};-c^{2}\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|^{2}z^{2}\right)}.

(39)

Applying Lemma 1 to this fraction, we get:

A(z)=\frac{\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\theta_{n}\;z^{n}}{\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\sigma_{n}\;z^{n}},

(40)

where the coefficients
\theta_{n},\;\sigma_{n}
are given by

\theta_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 0,\qquad&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r,\\
(-1)^{r}\;\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}\;(1)_{r}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2r}}{\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)_{r}\;\;r!},&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r+1,\end{aligned}\right.\qquad r=0,1,2,\dots

(41)

\sigma_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 1,\qquad\qquad&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=0,\\
0,\qquad\qquad&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r,\\
(-1)^{r}\;\lambda\;\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r-1}\;(1)_{r-1}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2(r-1)}}{\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)_{r-1}\;\;(r-1)!},&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r-1,\end{aligned}\right.\qquad r=1,2,3,\dots

(42)

Coefficients (
41
) and (
42
) depend on the vector variable
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
, but, for the sake of brevity, we omit it thereafter, as well as in the coefficients

\gamma_{n}=\gamma_{n}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
(see recurrent relation (
47
) below), bearing in mind, however, that all these coefficients are, in fact, functions of the vector variable
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
.

Since
(1)_{r}=r!,\;(1)_{r-1}=(r-1)!
, the coefficients (
41
) and (
42
) reduce to

\theta_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 0,\qquad&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r,\\
(-1)^{r}\;\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r}}{\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)_{r}}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2r},&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r+1,\end{aligned}\right.\qquad r=0,1,2,\dots

(43)

\sigma_{n}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 1,\qquad\qquad&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=0,\\
0,\qquad\qquad&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r,\\
(-1)^{r}\;\lambda\;\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{r-1}}{\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)_{r-1}}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2(r-1)},&\quad\text{if}\;\;n=2r-1,\end{aligned}\right.\qquad r=1,2,3,\dots

(44)

In particular,

\displaystyle\theta_{0}=\theta_{2}=\theta_{4}=\theta_{6}=0,\qquad\theta_{1}=1,\quad\theta_{3}=-\frac{1}{m}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2},

(45)

\displaystyle\theta_{5}=\frac{3!!}{m(m+2)}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{4},\quad\theta_{7}=-\frac{5!!}{m(m+2)(m+4)}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{6},

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}=1,\quad\sigma_{2}=\sigma_{4}=0,\quad\sigma_{1}=-\lambda,\quad\sigma_{3}=\lambda\;\frac{1}{m}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2},\quad\sigma_{5}=-\lambda\;\frac{3}{m(m+2)}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{4}.

Applying now a formula for the quotient of two power series (see, for instance,
[
12
, Formula 0.313]

or
[
23
, page 754, item 4]
) to fraction (
40
) and taking into account (
38
), we get:

A(s)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma_{n}}{s^{n}},

(46)

where the coefficients
\gamma_{n},\;n\geq 0,
are given by the recurrent relation:

\gamma_{0}=0,\qquad\gamma_{1}=1,\qquad\gamma_{n}=\theta_{n}-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\gamma_{n-k}\;\sigma_{k},\qquad n\geq 2.

(47)

Taking onto account that
\sigma_{n}=-\lambda\theta_{n},\;n\geq 1,
we can rewrite recurrent relation
(
47
) as follows:

\gamma_{0}=0,\qquad\gamma_{1}=1,\qquad\gamma_{n}=\theta_{n}+\lambda\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\gamma_{n-k}\;\theta_{k},\qquad n\geq 2.

(48)

In particular, some simple calculations yield:

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}=0,\qquad\gamma_{1}=1,\qquad\gamma_{2}=\lambda,\qquad\gamma_{3}=\lambda^{2}-\frac{1}{m}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2},

(49)

\displaystyle\gamma_{4}=\lambda^{3}-\lambda\;\frac{2}{m}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2},\qquad\gamma_{5}=\lambda^{4}-\lambda^{2}\;\frac{3}{m}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}+\frac{3}{m(m+2)}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{4}

\displaystyle\gamma_{6}=\lambda^{5}-\lambda^{3}\;\frac{4}{m}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}+\lambda\;\frac{7m+2}{m^{2}(m+2)}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{4}

\displaystyle\gamma_{7}=\lambda^{6}-\lambda^{4}\;\frac{5}{m}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}+\lambda^{2}\;\frac{12m+6}{m^{2}(m+2)}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{4}-\frac{15}{m(m+2)(m+4)}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{6}

\displaystyle\gamma_{8}=\lambda^{7}-\lambda^{5}\;\frac{6}{m}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}+\lambda^{3}\;\frac{18m+12}{m^{2}(m+2)}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{4}-\lambda\;\frac{36m+24}{m^{2}(m+2)(m+4)}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{6}

Function
A(s)
given by series (
46
) is holomorphic (analytical) everywhere in the right half-plane
of the complex plane, that is, for
\text{Re}\;s>0
. Therefore, by applying the inverse Laplace transformation
\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}
to series (
46
) and taking
into account (see, for instance
[
21
, Table 8.4-1, Formula 3]
) that

\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{s^{n}}\right](t)=\frac{t^{n-1}}{(n-1)!},\qquad n=1,2,\dots,

and that
\gamma_{0}=0
, we obtain:

\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}[A(s)](t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}\;\frac{t^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma_{n+1}}{n!}\;t^{n}.

Multiplying this by
e^{-\lambda t}
, we arrive at the statement of the theorem.
∎

(54)

5
Mixed moments

Series representations of the characteristic function
H(\boldsymbol{\alpha},t)
given above by Theorems 1
and 2 enable us to calculate the mixed moments. Let
\mathbb{q}=(q_{1},\dots,q_{m})
be a multi-index. Since at arbitrary fixed time
t>0
the Markov random flight
\mathbb{X}(t)=(X_{1}(t),\dots,X_{m}(t))
is concentrated
in the ball
\mathbb{B}_{ct}^{m}
, this process, as well as its coordinates
X_{j}(t),\;j=1,\dots,m
,
are bounded and, therefore, for arbitrary positive integer
k
, the condition
\mathbb{E}|X_{j}(t)|^{k}<\infty

fulfills for all
j=1,\dots,m
. Then, for arbitrary fixed
t>0
, there exist the mixed moments

\mu_{\mathbb{q}}(t)=\mu_{(q_{1},\dots,q_{m})}(t)=\mathbb{E}X_{1}^{q_{1}}(t)\dots X_{m}^{q_{m}}(t),\qquad q_{j}\geq 0,\quad q_{1}+\dots+q_{m}\leq k,

of the
m
-dimensional symmetric Markov random flight
\mathbb{X}(t)=(X_{1}(t),\dots,X_{m}(t))
given by the formula:

\mu_{\mathbb{q}}(t)=\mu_{(q_{1},\dots,q_{m})}(t)=(-i)^{q_{1}+\dots+q_{m}}\;\frac{\partial^{q_{1}+\dots+q_{m}}}{\partial\alpha_{1}^{q_{1}}\dots\partial\alpha_{m}^{q_{m}}}\;H(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{m};t)\biggr|_{\alpha_{1}=\dots=\alpha_{m}=0}.

(55)

Note, that if the characteristic function depends only on the square of the norm

\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|^{2}=\alpha_{1}^{2}+\dots+\alpha_{m}^{2}
of inversion parameter
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
, then, for arbitrary dimension
m\geq 2
and arbitrary positive integer
n\geq 1
, the following useful relation holds:

\frac{\partial^{2m}}{\partial\alpha_{1}^{2}\dots\partial\alpha_{m}^{2}}\;(\alpha_{1}^{2}+\dots+\alpha_{m}^{2})^{n}\;\biggr|_{\alpha_{1}=\dots=\alpha_{m}=0}=\left\{\begin{aligned} (2m)!!,\qquad&\text{if}\;\;n=m,\\
0,\qquad&\text{if}\;\;n\neq m,\end{aligned}\right.\qquad m\geq 2,\quad n\geq 1.

(56)

Since the moment functions of the
2D
and
4D
Markov random flights are known (see
[
17
]
),
we will focus on evaluating the first and second mixed moments of the three-dimensional symmetric
Markov random flight
\mathbb{X}(t)=(X_{1}(t),X_{2}(t),X_{3}(t)),\;t>0
. In this case
m=3
and
formula (
56
) becomes:

\frac{\partial^{6}}{\partial\alpha_{1}^{2}\;\partial\alpha_{2}^{2}\;\partial\alpha_{3}^{2}}\;(\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}+\alpha_{3}^{2})^{n}\;\biggr|_{\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}=0}=\left\{\begin{aligned} 6!!,\qquad&\text{if}\;\;n=3,\\
0,\qquad&\text{if}\;\;n\neq 3,\end{aligned}\right.\qquad n\geq 1.

(57)

To find these mixed moments, it is convenient to use the series representation (
35
) of
the characteristic function
H(t)
rather than more complicated one (
18
). Since each
coefficient
\gamma_{n}=\gamma_{n}(\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|),\;n\geq 1,
is some polynomial with respect
to even powers of the variable
\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|
, then, taking into account that

\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial\alpha_{1}\;\partial\alpha_{2}\;\partial\alpha_{3}}\;\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|^{2k}\;\biggr|_{\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}=0}=\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial\alpha_{1}\;\partial\alpha_{2}\;\partial\alpha_{3}}\;(\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}+\alpha_{3}^{2})^{k}\;\biggr|_{\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}=0}=0,\qquad k\geq 1,

and

\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial\alpha_{1}\;\partial\alpha_{2}\;\partial\alpha_{3}}\;\gamma_{n}(\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)\;\biggr|_{\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}=0}=0,\qquad n\geq 1,

we see that

\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial\alpha_{1}\;\partial\alpha_{2}\;\partial\alpha_{3}}\;H(\boldsymbol{\alpha},t)\;\biggr|_{\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}=0}=e^{-\lambda t}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial^{3}}{\partial\alpha_{1}\;\partial\alpha_{2}\;\partial\alpha_{3}}\;\gamma_{n+1}(\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)\;\biggr|_{\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}=0}\;\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=0,

and, therefore, for arbitrary fixed
t>0
, the first mixed moment corresponding to the multi-index

\mathbb{q}_{1}=(1,1,1)
is zero, that is,

\mu_{\mathbb{q}_{1}}(t)=\mu_{(1,1,1)}(t)=0.

(58)

Let us now concentrate on evaluating the second mixed moment function

\mu_{\mathbb{q}_{2}}(t)=\mu_{(2,2,2)}(t)
corresponding to the multi-index
\mathbb{q}_{2}=(2,2,2)
.
For the sake of brevity, let us denote the operator:

\mathcal{D}=\frac{\partial^{6}\;\cdot}{\partial\alpha_{1}^{2}\;\partial\alpha_{2}^{2}\;\partial\alpha_{3}^{2}}\;\biggr|_{\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}=0}

(59)

In order to evaluate the second mixed moment, one needs to calculate the action of this operator on the coefficients
\gamma_{n}(\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)
, that is,

\mathcal{D}\;\gamma_{n}(\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)=\frac{\partial^{6}\;}{\partial\alpha_{1}^{2}\;\partial\alpha_{2}^{2}\;\partial\alpha_{3}^{2}}\;\gamma_{n}(\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)\;\biggr|_{\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}=0},\qquad n\geq 2.

However, this is a very difficult analytical problem because we need an explicit form of the coefficients

\gamma_{n}(\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|),\;n\geq 2
, while they are given only in the determinant form
(
51
). Application of operator (
59
) to such a determinant leads to an extremely complicated and cumbersome expression containing
n^{6}
sub-determinants that, obviously, makes the problem impracticable (at least, analytically).

That is why we confine ourselves to the case of several terms of series (
35
). This will give us not exact, but asymptotic relation for the second mixed moment function
\mu_{(2,2,2)}(t)
with respect to time variable. According to (
35
), we have:

\displaystyle H(\boldsymbol{\alpha},t)=e^{-\lambda t}

\displaystyle\biggl[\gamma_{1}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})+\gamma_{2}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\;\frac{t}{1!}+\gamma_{3}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\;\frac{t^{2}}{2!}+\gamma_{4}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\;\frac{t^{3}}{3!}+\gamma_{5}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\;\frac{t^{4}}{4!}

(60)

\displaystyle+\gamma_{6}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\;\frac{t^{5}}{5!}+\gamma_{7}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\;\frac{t^{6}}{6!}+\gamma_{8}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})\;\frac{t^{7}}{7!}+o(t^{7})\biggr].

From (
49
), for
m=3
, we get:

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=1,\qquad\gamma_{2}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\lambda,\qquad\gamma_{3}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\lambda^{2}-\frac{1}{3}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2},

(61)

\displaystyle\gamma_{4}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\lambda^{3}-\lambda\;\frac{2}{3}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2},\qquad\gamma_{5}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\lambda^{4}-\lambda^{2}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}+\frac{1}{5}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{4}

\displaystyle\gamma_{6}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\lambda^{5}-\lambda^{3}\;\frac{4}{3}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}+\lambda\;\frac{23}{45}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{4}

\displaystyle\gamma_{7}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\lambda^{6}-\lambda^{4}\;\frac{5}{3}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}+\lambda^{2}\;\frac{14}{15}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{4}-\frac{1}{7}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{6}

\displaystyle\gamma_{8}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\lambda^{7}-2\lambda^{5}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{2}+\lambda^{3}\;\frac{22}{15}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{4}-\lambda\;\frac{44}{105}\;(c\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|)^{6}

In view of (
57
), we have:

\mathcal{D}\;\gamma_{1}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\mathcal{D}\;\gamma_{2}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\mathcal{D}\;\gamma_{3}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\mathcal{D}\;\gamma_{4}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\mathcal{D}\;\gamma_{5}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\mathcal{D}\;\gamma_{6}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=0,

while

\mathcal{D}\;\gamma_{7}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=-\frac{48}{7}\;c^{6},\qquad\mathcal{D}\;\gamma_{8}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=-\lambda\;\frac{704}{35}\;c^{6}.

Therefore, applying operator
\mathcal{D}
to (
60
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\;H(\boldsymbol{\alpha},t)

\displaystyle=-e^{-\lambda t}\biggl[\frac{48}{7}\;c^{6}\;\frac{t^{6}}{6!}+\lambda\;\frac{704}{35}\;c^{6}\;\frac{t^{7}}{7!}+o(t^{7})\biggr]

\displaystyle=-e^{-\lambda t}\biggl[\frac{1}{105}\;(ct)^{6}+\lambda\;\frac{44}{11025}\;c^{6}t^{7}+o(t^{7})\biggr].

Then, according to (
55
), and taking into account that
i^{6}=-1
, we finally get:

\mu_{\mathbb{q}_{2}}(t)=\mu_{(2,2,2)}(t)=e^{-\lambda t}\biggl[\frac{1}{105}\;(ct)^{6}+\lambda\;\frac{44}{11025}\;c^{6}t^{7}+o(t^{7})\biggr].

(62)

Thus, we can summarize the above results (
58
) and (
62
) in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.

For arbitrary
t>0
, the first mixed moment of the three-dimensional symmetric
Markov random flight
\mathbb{X}(t)=(X_{1}(t),X_{2}(t),X_{3}(t))
is identically equal to zero, that is,

\mu_{\mathbb{q}_{1}}(t)=\mu_{(1,1,1)}(t)=0.

(63)

For the second mixed moment function of
\mathbb{X}(t)
, the following asymptotic relation holds:

\mu_{\mathbb{q}_{2}}(t)=\mu_{(2,2,2)}(t)=e^{-\lambda t}\biggl[\frac{1}{105}\;(ct)^{6}+\lambda\;\frac{44}{11025}\;c^{6}t^{7}+o(t^{7})\biggr].

(64)

Remark 7.
Asymptotic formula (
64
) provides a very good approximation of the second mixed
moment, especially for small values of time
t
. For example, for the time value
t=0.3
, relation
(
64
) yields (for
c=3,\;\lambda=5
) the estimate:
\mu_{(2,2,2)}(0.3)\approx 0.00183921
and
the error of this estimate does not exceed the value 0.00001464 multiplied by some constant.

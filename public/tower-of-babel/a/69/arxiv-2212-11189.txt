A note on the homogenization of incommensurate thin films
Irene Anello, Andrea Braides, Fabrizio Caragiulo

Abstract
Dimension-reduction homogenization results for thin films have been obtained under hypotheses of periodicity or almost-periodicity of the energies in the directions of the mid-plane of the film. In this note we consider thin films, obtained as sections of a periodic medium with a mid-plane that may be incommensurate; that is, not containing periods other than 0 0 . A geometric almost-periodicity argument similar to the cut-and-project argument used for quasicrystals allows to prove a general homogenization result.

Introduction

The energy of heterogeneous thin films of a hyperelastic material in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
can be described by integral functionals of the form

\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\int_{\omega\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}f_{\varepsilon}(x,\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}x,

(1.1)

where
f_{\varepsilon}
are hyperelastic energy densities satisfying suitable growth conditions,
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is the middle section of the thin film,
2\varepsilon
is its thickness, and
u:\omega\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is the displacement (in the physical case,
d=2
and
m=3
). We use
\mathrm{d}x
to denote integration with respect to the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
both for
k=d
and
k={d+1}
, which one being clear from the context.
In the seminal paper by Le Dret and Raoult
[
9
]
, in the case
f_{\varepsilon}=f
independent of
\varepsilon
and homogeneous (i.e.,
x
-independent), the behaviour of such energies as
\varepsilon\to 0
has been proven to be described by a dimensionally reduced hyperelastic energy of the form

\int_{\omega}f^{\rm LDR}(\nabla v)\,\mathrm{d}y,

(1.2)

where now
v:\omega\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
has a domain identified as the mid-section of the film, and
f^{\rm LDR}
is a quasiconvex function explicitly described from
f
.
For general energies (
1.1
) a compactness theorem
[
5
]
ensures that, up to subsequences, their behaviour can be described by a possibly heterogeneous dimensionally reduced energy

\int_{\omega}f_{0}(y,\nabla v)\,\mathrm{d}y,

(1.3)

with
f_{0}
possibly depending on the subsequence.
In the case of a periodic integrand we can suppose, as this is the relevant case, that the oscillation be at the same scale of the thickness, so that we assume
f_{\varepsilon}(x,A)=f\bigl({x\over\varepsilon},A\bigr)
with
f(\cdot,A)
periodic in the coordinate directions. In this case we have
homogenization

[
5
]
; that is, the limit
f_{0}
is homogeneous and independent of the subsequence of
\varepsilon
, and described by a suitable asymptotic formula. In order to prove this result, a key argument is the invariance property of the energies by translations of the form
\varepsilon e_{j}
for
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, where
\{e_{i}:i\in\{1,\ldots,d+1\}\}
is the canonical orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, entailing the translation invariance of
f_{0}
. This argument is made possible by the assumption that the middle plane of the thin film contains a
d
-dimensional lattice of periods for
f
; that is, it is ‘commensurate’ with
\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
.

In this paper we consider the general case of a periodic energy density
f
, when the middle plane of the thin film contains a
n
-dimensional lattice of periods for
f
with
n\leq d
. ‘Incommensurate’ thin films are those with
n=0
; that is, when the middle plane of the thin film does not contain any period for
f
except
0
.

In order to treat general, possibly incommensurate, thin films, we introduce some notation, slightly different from the one used for commensurate thin films, due to the necessity to distinguish between the periods of the energy density and the directions of the thin film. We choose to maintain
\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
as set of periods, considering a Carathéodory function

\widetilde{f}:\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{M}^{m\times{(d+1)}}\to\left[0,+\infty\right)
satisfying the
standard p-growth conditions

\alpha|A|^{p}\leq\widetilde{f}(x,A)\leq\beta(1+|A|^{p})

(1.4)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and
A\in\mathbb{M}^{m\times{d+1}}
and some
\alpha,\beta>0
,
which is
1
-periodic in all coordinate directions; i.e.,

\widetilde{f}(x+{e}_{i},A)=\widetilde{f}(x,A)

(1.5)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, all matrices
A\in\mathbb{M}^{m\times{(d+1)}}
and all vectors
{e}_{i}
of the standard orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
. We use the notation
\mathbb{M}^{m\times k}
for the space of
{m\times k}
matrices with real entries.

We fix a unit vector
\nu
and consider the hyperplane
\Pi=\{x:\langle x,\nu\rangle=0\}
.
The interesting case, to keep in mind as the most relevant one, is when
\Pi
is an
irrational
hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
; that is, such that

\Pi\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}=\{0\}.

(1.6)

We fix
\widetilde{\omega}
a bounded subset of
\Pi
, open in the relative topology,
h>0
, and for each
\varepsilon>0
consider the functional

\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{2h\varepsilon}\int_{\widetilde{\omega}+(-h\varepsilon,h\varepsilon)\nu}\widetilde{f}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla{u}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x,

(1.7)

with domain
\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(\widetilde{\omega}+(-h\varepsilon,h\varepsilon)\nu,\mathbb{R}^{m})
,
which ideally represents the elastic free energy of a thin film of size
2h\varepsilon>0
around
\widetilde{\omega}
.
The introduction of a constant
h>0
amounts to assuming that thickness and periods are comparable, so that
h
represents their ratio, which we highlight for possible future reference.
We will prove that there exists a function
\widetilde{f}_{\text{hom}}\colon\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}\to[0,+\infty)

satisfying an asymptotic formula, such that

\Gamma{\text{-}}\mkern-1.0mu\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{\widetilde{\omega}}\widetilde{f}_{\text{hom}}\big(\nabla u(y)\big)\,\mathrm{d}y.

The
\Gamma
-limit is performed in a dimension-reduction fashion, using a convergence of
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(\widetilde{\omega}+(-h\varepsilon,h\varepsilon)\nu,\mathbb{R}^{m})
to
u\in\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(\widetilde{\omega},\mathbb{R}^{m})
, where
\widetilde{\omega}
is identified with a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in order for the integration to be well defined.

The result is obtained by first resorting to the theory of
[
5
]
. To that end we rewrite the functionals in the form (
1.1
), with
f_{\varepsilon}(x,A)=f\smash{\bigl({x\over\varepsilon},A\bigr)}
, and
f
obtained from
\smash{\widetilde{f}}
by a linear change of variables.
Using the dimension-reduction convergence of functions as in
[
9
]
, we can then apply the compactness theorem in
[
5
]
to obtain a limit of the form (
1.3
). Since
f
may not be periodic in the coordinate directions we cannot immediately conclude that homogenization takes place, since an invariance-by-translation argument does not apply. However, we can use a sort of
geometric almost periodicity
property: the set of periods for
\widetilde{f}
which are close to
\Pi
(closeness suitably quantified by a small parameter) is projected to a uniformly dense set in
\Pi
. We remark that the former set corresponds also to the set of periods for
f
which are close to the hyperplane identified with
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, This argument reminds the
cut-and-project
arguments typical of quasicrystalline structures (see
[
7
,
2
,
8
,
10
,
6
,
3
]
).
The existence of such geometric quasi-periods is not sufficient to prove the necessary translation-invariance properties for
f_{0}
and the homogenization asymptotic formula. To that end it is necessary to construct test functions by using translation arguments, which are not directly at hand since translation by a quasi-period may exit the thin film domain. The key technical point of the paper is a novel lemma, which ensures that in the constructions of test functions it is sufficient to modify functions defined in a smaller thin film, which is then mapped inside the original thin film by any of the above-mentioned translations (scaled by
\varepsilon
).

1.1
Statement of the results

We now formalize what we anticipated in the Introduction, rewriting the energies
\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon}
in (
1.7
) in order to apply the results in
[
5
]
with more ease. To that end, we make a change to coordinates more suitable to the problem: we let
\phi
be the linear isometry in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
sending
{e}_{1},\ldots,{e}_{d}
to an orthonormal basis
{\pi}_{1},\ldots,{\pi}_{d}
of
\Pi
and
{e}_{d+1}
to
\nu
. Let
\omega
be the open set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\phi^{-1}(\widetilde{\omega})=\omega\times\{0\}
, so that

\phi^{-1}\big(\widetilde{\omega}+(-h\varepsilon,h\varepsilon)\nu\big)=\omega\times(-h\varepsilon,h\varepsilon)\eqqcolon\Omega_{\varepsilon}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}

After this change of variables, setting

{I}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{2h\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}f\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\nabla{u(x)}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x,

(1.8)

where
u\in\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{R}^{m})
and, denoted by
R
the constant matrix equal to
\nabla\phi
, having set

f(x,A)=\widetilde{f}(\phi(x),AR),

(1.9)

we have

{I}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon}(\widetilde{u}),\quad\hbox{ where }\quad\widetilde{u}(\widetilde{x})=u(\phi^{-1}(\widetilde{x})).

(1.10)

Hence, the functionals
{I}_{\varepsilon}
and
\widetilde{I}_{\varepsilon}
are equivalent, up to a linear change of variables.
In order to simplify the statement of the convergence, as is customary we scale all functionals to a common domain,
obtaining

\begin{split}F_{\varepsilon}(u)&\coloneqq\frac{1}{2h}\int_{\omega\times(-h,h)}f\Big({x\over\varepsilon},y,\Big(\nabla_{x}u(x,y)\Big|{1\over\varepsilon}{\partial_{y}u(x,y)}\Big)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\end{split}

(1.11)

for
u\in W^{1,p}(\omega\times(-h,h),\mathbb{R}^{m})
, so that
F_{\varepsilon}(\overline{u})=I_{\varepsilon}(u)
, where
\overline{u}(x,y)=u(x,\varepsilon y)
.
Here, we have rewritten, with a little abuse of notation, a variable in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
as a pair
(x,y)
with
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
and identified a matrix
A\in\mathbb{M}^{m\times{d+1}}
with a pair which we denote
(A^{\prime}|\xi)
, where
A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a (column) vector. The notation
\nabla_{x}u
denotes the gradient with respect to the coordinates of
x
. We will keep the standard notation
\nabla u
when the gradient is performed with respect to all the variables in the domain, be it in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.

We will use the following notion of convergence.

Definition 1.1

(convergence to dimensionally reduced parameters)
.

A sequence of functions
u_{\varepsilon}
with
u_{\varepsilon}\in{W}^{1,p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon},\mathbb{R}^{m})

converges to

u\in{W}^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
if the corresponding functions
\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}
defined by
\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=u_{\varepsilon}(x,\varepsilon y)
converge to some
\overline{u}
weakly in
W^{1,p}(\omega\times(-h,h),\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
\overline{u}(x,y)=u(x)
.

We recall that
I_{\varepsilon}
are equicoercive with respect to this convergence, in the sense that if
I_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
is equibounded and
\overline{u}_{\varepsilon}
are bounded in
L^{p}(\Omega\times(-h,h),\mathbb{R}^{m})
then
u_{\varepsilon}
converge to some
u
, up to subsequences
[
5
]
.

Definition 1.2

(convergence to dimensionally reduced energies)
.

We say that
I_{\varepsilon}

\Gamma
-
converges to

F_{0}
with respect to the convergence of
u_{\varepsilon}
above if the corresponding
F_{\varepsilon}

\Gamma
-converge to the functional
\overline{F}_{0}
on functions independent of
y
, defined by
F_{0}(\overline{u})=\overline{F}_{0}(u)
if
\overline{u}(x,y)=u(x)
, with respect to the weak convergence in
W^{1,p}
.

We can then state the homogenization result as follows.

Theorem 1.3

(homogenization theorem)
.

Let
\nu
be a unit vector and
\Pi=\{x:\langle x,\nu\rangle=0\}
be a hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.
Let
\smash{\widetilde{f}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{M}^{m\times(d+1)}\to\mathbb{R}}
be a Carathéodory function satisfying the
p
-growth conditions (
1.4
) for
p>1
and periodic in the coordinate direction as in (
1.5
). Let
\phi
be the linear isometry defined above and let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{M}^{m\times(d+1)}\to\mathbb{R}
be the Carathéodory integrand defined by (
1.9
).
Let
h>0
, let
\omega
be an open and bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
I_{\varepsilon}
be defined by (
1.8
), where
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\omega\times(-h\varepsilon,h\varepsilon)
. Then
I_{\varepsilon}

\Gamma
-converges to
F_{\rm hom}
with respect to the convergence in Definition
1.1
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, where

F_{\rm hom}(u)=\int_{\omega}f_{\rm hom}(\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}x

(1.12)

for
u\in W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and
f_{\rm hom}\colon\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a quasiconvex function satisfying a

-growth conditions and the asymptotic homogenization formula

\begin{split}f_{\mathrm{hom}}(A)=\lim_{T\to+\infty}\frac{1}{T^{d}}\inf_{u\in\mathcal{W}_{T}}\bigg\{\frac{1}{2h}\int_{(0,T)^{d}\times(-h,h)}&f\big(x,y,\big(A+\nabla_{x}u\big|{\partial_{y}u}\big)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\Bigg\}\end{split}

(1.13)

where

\mathcal{W}_{T}\coloneqq\Big\{u\in\mathrm{W}^{1,p}\big((0,T)^{d}\times(-h,h),\,\mathbb{R}^{m}\big)\colon u=0\text{ on }\partial\big((0,T)^{d}\big)\times(-h,h)\Big\}.

(homogenization on

)
.

By using (

(with underlying measure the

Remark 1.5

(connections with almost periodicity)
.

The theorem above has been proved in
[
5
]
if
f
is periodic in the first variable, which is the case if the lattice
\Pi\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
has dimension
d
(or, equivalently, if it spans
\Pi
).
In the case at hand we will use a sort of
geometric quasi-periodicity
. Namely, we will use the fact that, for fixed
\eta>0
the set
\mathcal{T}_{\eta}
of
\tau\in\Pi
such that dist
(\tau,\mathbb{Z}^{d+1})<\eta
is uniformly dense in
\Pi
; that is, there exists an inclusion length
L_{\eta}>0
such that
\mathcal{T}_{\eta}+[0,L_{\eta}]^{d+1}\supset\Pi
. This is an immediate consequence of the periodicity of the function
\psi(x)={\rm dist}(x,\mathbb{Z}^{d+1})
on
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and the consequent quasi-periodicity of its restriction to
\Pi
. This property in turn implies its uniform almost periodicity (see
[
1
]
Definition 1.7), which exactly states that for all
\eta>0
there exist a uniformly dense set of
\eta
-
almost periods
; i.e.,
\tau
such that
|\psi(x+\tau)-\psi(x)|<\eta
for all
x\in\Pi
. In particular, taking
x=0
, we have that
\psi(\tau)\leq\eta
, which proves the claim. Note that this property is most relevant if
\Pi
is irrational, and is trivial if
\Pi\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
has dimension
d+1
. In Fig.
1
, in a two-dimensional setting, we picture an element of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\eta}
on
\Pi
(black dot) and its closest element in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
(grey dot).

This quasi-periodicity argument also shows that if
\widetilde{f}
is continuous in the first variable uniformly with respect to the second one, then
f
is almost periodic in the
x
-directions uniformly with respect to the second variable, and we can apply the results of
[
4
]
Chapter 24. This observation suggests that we can then generalize Theorem
1.3
by supposing that for all
\eta>0
there exists a uniformly dense set
\mathcal{T}_{\eta}
in
\Pi
such that for all
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\eta}
there exists
z_{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
such that
\|\tau-z_{\tau}\|<\eta
and
|\widetilde{f}(x+z_{\tau},A)-\widetilde{f}(x,A)|\leq\eta(1+|A|^{p})
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and
A
. This trivially holds if
\widetilde{f}
is periodic as above taking
z_{\tau}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
.

The geometric quasi-periodicity property highlighted above must be complemented by a lemma, which will be used to cope with the fact that periods may not belong to
\Pi
, so that translation arguments within the thin film cannot be directly applied.

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
h>0
and let
g:[0,h]\to[0,+\infty)
be an integrable function and define

C\coloneqq\int_{0}^{h}g(y)\,\mathrm{d}y<+\infty.

Then, for every
\delta>\eta>0
, the set

E_{\eta}\coloneqq\bigg\{y\in[h-\delta,h]\,:\,(h+\eta-y)g(y)\leq\frac{C}{\log\big(\delta/\eta\big)}\bigg\}

(1.14)

has positive measure.

Proof.

Suppose, by contradiction, that for some
\delta
there exists a
\eta
such that
E_{\eta}
has null measure, then

(h+\eta-y)g(y)>\frac{C}{\log\big(\delta/\eta\big)},\quad\text{ for almost all }y\in[h-\delta,h].

Thus the strict inequality would persist under integration, and

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{[h-\delta,h]}g(y)\,\mathrm{d}y>\frac{C}{\log\big(\delta/\eta\big)}\int_{[h-\delta,h]}\frac{1}{h+\eta-y}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\log\big(\delta/\eta\big)}\big(\log(\delta+\eta)-\log(\eta)\big)=C\frac{\log{\big(\delta/\eta+1\big)}}{\log{\big(\delta/\eta\big)}}>C

providing a contradiction.
∎

2
Proof of the homogenization theorem

We directly prove the theorem under the more general assumptions in Remark
1.5
, which can be stated as follows for the function
f
: for all
\eta>0
there exists
L_{\eta}>0
and
\mathcal{T}_{\eta}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\mathcal{T}_{\eta}+[0,L_{\eta}]^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d},

and for all
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\eta}
there exists
z_{\tau}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
|z_{\tau}|<\eta
and

|f(x+(\tau,z_{\tau}),A)-f(x,A)|\leq\eta(1+|A|^{p})

(2.1)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and
A
.

We first note that, by the compactness theorem for thin structures (
[
5
]
and
[
4
]
Theorem 24.20) for every sequence
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}_{k}
of positive real numbers,
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
, there exist a subsequence (still denoted by
\varepsilon_{k}
) and a Carathéodory function
f_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}\to[0,+\infty)
satisfying

0\leq f_{0}(x,A)\leq\beta(1+|A|^{p}),

for all
x\in\omega
and
A\in\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}
such that

\Gamma\hbox{-}\mkern-10.0mu\lim_{k\to+\infty}I_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u)=\int_{\omega}f_{0}(x,\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}x

(2.2)

for all
u\in W^{1,p}(\omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})
with respect to the convergence in Definition
1.1
.
We then have to prove that
f_{0}
does not depend on the space variable and is given by formula (
1.13
), which also implies that it does not depend on the subsequence
\varepsilon_{k}
.
To that end, we will use localization arguments and results ensuring the possibility of fixing boundary values taken from
[
5
]
and Lemma
1.6
.

In the following result we prove that
f_{0}
depends only on the gradient,
f_{0}(x,A)=f_{0}(A)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any sequence
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}_{k}
such that the
\Gamma
-limit in (
2.2
) exists,
f_{0}
depends only on the gradient; that is,
f_{0}(x,A)=f_{0}(A)
.

Proof.

For any open subset
U\subset\omega
we consider the
localized
functionals

\begin{split}F_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u,U)&\coloneqq\frac{1}{2h}\int_{U\times(-h,h)}f\big({\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}}x,y,\big(\nabla_{x}u\big|{\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}}{\partial_{y}u}\big)\big)\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
F_{0}(u,U)&\coloneqq{1\over 2h}\int_{U\times(-h,h)}f_{0}(x,\nabla_{x}u)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

Let
B^{d}_{\rho}(x)
denote the ball of radius
\rho
and center
x
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
It suffices to prove that given any
x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime}\in\omega
and
\rho>0
such that

B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\Subset\omega
and
B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime\prime})\Subset\omega
, and any
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
, we have

F_{0}\big(Ax,B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\big)=F_{0}\big(Ax,B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime\prime})\big).

(2.3)

Up to fixing lateral boundary values, we find a sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k}\in\mathrm{W}^{1,p}\big(B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\times(-h,h),\mathbb{R}^{m}\big)
with
u_{k}=0\text{ on }\big(\partial B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\big)\times(-h,h)
and such that
u_{k}\to 0
in
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{1},\mathbb{R}^{m})
and

\lim_{k\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{k}}\big(Ax+u_{k},B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\big)=F_{0}\big(Ax,B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\big)

(2.4)

(see
[
5
]
).

Let
\delta>0
be fixed, and let
\eta\in(0,\delta)
. From now on we use the shorthand
\partial_{y}
for the partial derivative with respect to
y
. Applying Lemma
1.6
to the integrable functions

g(t)=\int_{B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})}\big|\big(A+\nabla_{x}u_{k}(x,t)\big|\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}\,\partial_{y}u_{k}(x,t)\big)\big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

for
t\in[0,h]
, we get the existence of
y_{\eta}^{+}\in(h-\delta,h)
such that

\displaystyle{\int_{y_{\eta}^{+}}^{h+\eta}}\int_{B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})}\big|\big(A+\nabla_{x}u_{k}(x,y_{\eta}^{+})\big|\,\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}\partial_{y}u_{k}(x,y_{\eta}^{+})\big)\big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\,{\mathrm{d}y}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\int_{B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\times(0,h)}\big|\big(A+\nabla_{x}u_{k}(x,y_{\eta}^{+})\big|\,\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}\partial_{y}u_{k}(x,y_{\eta}^{+})\big)\big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{k}}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|},

where
C_{k}\coloneqq F_{\varepsilon_{k}}(Ax+u_{k},B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime}))
.
Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt{\int_{y_{\eta}^{+}}^{h+\eta}}\int_{B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})}f\Big(\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}x,y,\big(A+\nabla_{x}u_{k}(x,y_{\eta}^{+})\big|\,\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}\partial_{y}u_{k}(x,y_{\eta}^{+})\big)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,{\mathrm{d}y}

(2.5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta\left(|B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})|{(\delta+\eta)}+\frac{C_{k}}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\right)\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\left(c\rho^{d}{(\delta+\eta)}+\frac{C_{k}}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\right)\,,

(2.6)

being
c\coloneqq|B^{1}|
. Similarly, we obtain the existence of
y_{\eta}^{-}\in(-h,-h+\delta)
such that

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt{\int_{-h-\eta}^{y_{\eta}^{-}}}\int_{B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})}f\Big(\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}x,y,\big(A+\nabla_{x}u_{k}(x,y_{\eta}^{-})\big|\,\varepsilon_{k}^{-1}\partial_{y}u_{k}(x,y_{\eta}^{-})\big)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,{\mathrm{d}y}

(2.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta\left(c\rho^{d}{(\delta+\eta)}+\frac{C_{k}}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\right).

Define
\widetilde{u}_{k}:B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
as

\widetilde{u}_{k}(x,y)\coloneqq\begin{cases}u_{k}(x,y_{\eta}^{+})&\text{ if }y\geq y^{+}_{\eta}\\
u_{k}(x,y)&\text{ if }y_{\eta}^{-}\leq y\leq y_{\eta}^{+}\\
u_{k}(x,y_{\eta}^{-})&\text{ if }y\leq y^{-}_{\eta}.\end{cases}

Note that
\widetilde{u}_{k}(x,y)=0
if
x\in\partial B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})
.

Let
\{\tau_{k}\}_{k}
be a sequence in
\varepsilon_{k}\mathcal{T}_{\eta}
(the set of scaled almost-periods) such that

\tau_{k}\to x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime}
. Such a sequence exists by the uniform density of
\mathcal{T}_{\eta}
. Let
z_{k}=z_{\tau_{k}}
be such that (
2.1
) holds,
and define
v_{k}(x,y)=\widetilde{u}_{k}(x-\tau_{k},y-z_{k})
. Noting that
v_{k}
can be extended by
0
outside
\big(\tau_{k}+B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\big)\times(-h,h)
we get from almost-periodicity, (
2.5
) and (
2.7
):

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon_{k}}\big(Ax+v_{k},\,\tau_{k}+B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2h}\int_{\big(\tau_{k}+B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\big)\times(-h,h)}f\bigg(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},y,\Big(A+\nabla_{x}v_{k}\Big|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}}\partial_{y}v_{k}\Big)\bigg)\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2h}\int_{B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\times(-h-z_{k},h+z_{k})}f\bigg(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}}+\frac{\tau_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k}},y+z_{k},\Big(A+\nabla_{x}\widetilde{u}_{k}\Big|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}}\partial_{y}\widetilde{u}_{k}\Big)\bigg)\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2h}\int_{B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\times(-h-\eta,h+\eta)}f\bigg(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}}+\frac{\tau_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k}},y+z_{k},\Big(A+\nabla_{x}\widetilde{u}_{k}\Big|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}}\partial_{y}\widetilde{u}_{k}\Big)\bigg)\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\beta c\rho^{d}(\eta+\delta)}{h}

\displaystyle\mkern 50.0mu+\frac{1}{2h}\Big(1+\frac{2\beta}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\Big)\int_{B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\times(-h,h)}f\bigg(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}}+\frac{\tau_{k}}{\varepsilon_{k}},y+z_{k},\Big(A+\nabla_{x}{u}_{k}\Big|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}}\partial_{y}{u}_{k}\Big)\bigg)\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\beta c\rho^{d}}{h}+\frac{1}{2h}\Big(1+\frac{2\beta}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\Big)\int_{B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\times(-h,h)}f\bigg(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},y,\Big(A+\nabla_{x}u_{k}\Big|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}}\partial_{y}u_{k}\Big)\bigg)\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\mkern 50.0mu+\frac{\eta}{2h}\Big(1+\frac{2\beta}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\Big)\int_{B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\times(-h,h)}\Bigl(1+\Big|\Big(A+\nabla_{x}u_{k}\Big|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}}\partial_{y}u_{k}\Big)\Big|^{p}\Bigr)\mathrm{d}x)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\beta c\rho^{d}(\eta+\delta)}{h}+\Big(1+\frac{2\beta}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\Big)F_{\varepsilon_{k}}\big(Ax+u_{k},\,B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle\mkern 50.0mu+\frac{\eta}{2h}\Big(1+\frac{2\beta}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\Big)\Big(2{h}c\rho^{d}+\frac{1}{\alpha}F_{\varepsilon_{k}}\big(Ax+u_{k},\,B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\big)\Big).

Fix now
r>1
, and note that for
k
large enough we have
\tau_{k}+B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\subset B^{d}_{r\rho}(x^{\prime\prime})\Subset\omega
.
Since
v_{k}\to 0
in
L^{p}\big(B^{d}_{r\rho}(x^{\prime})\times(-h,h),\mathbb{R}^{m}\big)
:

\displaystyle F_{0}\big(Ax,B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime\prime})\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,F_{0}\big(Ax,B^{d}_{r\rho}(x^{\prime\prime})\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{k}}\big(Ax+v_{k},B^{d}_{r\rho}(x^{\prime\prime})\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\Big(1+\frac{\eta}{\alpha}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{2\beta}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\Big)\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{k}}\big(Ax+u_{k},B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle\mkern 40.0mu+\frac{c\rho^{d}\eta}{h}\Big(1+\frac{2\beta}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\Big)+\frac{\beta c\rho^{d}(\eta+\delta)}{h}

\displaystyle\mkern 40.0mu+\frac{\beta}{h}\big(1+|A|^{p}\big)\big|B^{d}_{r\rho}(x^{\prime\prime})\setminus B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime\prime})\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\Big(1+\frac{\eta}{\alpha}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{2\beta}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\Big)F_{0}\big(u_{k},B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle\mkern 40.0mu+\frac{c\rho^{d}\eta}{h}\Big(1+\frac{2\beta}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\Big)+\frac{\beta c\rho^{d}}{h}(\eta+\delta)

\displaystyle\mkern 40.0mu+\frac{\beta}{h}\big(1+|A|^{p}\big)\big|B^{d}_{r\rho}(x^{\prime\prime})\setminus B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime\prime})\big|.

Letting first
r\to 1
, then
\eta\to 0
and eventually
\delta\to 0
, we finally obtain the inequality

F_{0}\big(Ax,B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime\prime})\big)\leq F_{0}\big(Ax,B^{d}_{\rho}(x^{\prime})\big).

By symmetry we then obtain also equality and the claim.
∎

We can then proceed in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Take
\omega=(0,1)^{d}
and let
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}
be a subsequence given by (
2.2
). The
\Gamma
-limit of the family
F_{\varepsilon_{k}}
exists and, for Proposition
2.1
, is equal to

F_{0}(u)=\int_{(0,1)^{d}}f_{0}(\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}x.

This functional is sequentially weakly lower semicontinuous on
\mathrm{W}^{1,p}(\Omega_{1},\mathbb{R}^{m})
so the function
f_{0}
is
\mathrm{W}^{1,p}
-quasiconvex. This means we can write

f_{0}(A)=\min\biggl\{\int_{(0,1)^{d}}f_{0}(A+\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}x:u=0\hbox{ on }\partial(0,1)^{d}\biggr\}

(2.8)

for all
A\in\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}
.
From the property of convergence of minima for
\Gamma
-convergence and a change of variable in the right-hand side we obtain

\displaystyle f_{0}(A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\bigg\{\int_{(0,1)^{d}}f_{0}(A+\nabla u)\,\mathrm{d}x:u=0\hbox{ on }\partial(0,1)^{d}\bigg\}

(2.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{2h}\int_{(0,1)^{d}\times(-h,h)}f_{0}(A+\nabla_{x}u)\,\mathrm{d}x:u=0\hbox{ on }\partial\big((0,1)^{d}\big)\times(-h,h)\bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\inf\biggl\{\frac{1}{2h}\int_{(0,1)^{d}\times(-h,h)}f\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon_{k}},y,\Big(A+\nabla_{x}u\Big|\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{k}}\partial_{y}u\Big)\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y:{u\in\mathcal{W}_{1}}\biggr\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\inf\biggl\{\frac{1}{2hT_{k}^{d}}\int_{(0,T_{k})^{d}\times(-h,h)}f\big(x,y,(A+\nabla_{x}u|\partial_{y}u)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y:{u\in\mathcal{W}_{T_{k}}}\biggr\},

where
T_{k}=1/\varepsilon_{k}
.

The following proposition states the existence of the limit in (
1.13
), giving in particular the independence of
f_{0}
from the subsequence.

Proposition 2.2
.

For every
A\in\mathbb{M}^{m\times d}
the following limit exists

f_{\hom}(A)=\lim_{T\to+\infty}\inf_{u\in\mathcal{W}_{T}}\biggl\{\frac{1}{2hT^{d}}\int_{(0,T)^{d}\times(-h,h)}f\big(x,y,(A+\nabla_{x}u|\partial_{y}u)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\biggr\}.

(2.10)

Proof.

For every
T>0
let

g_{A}(T)\coloneqq\inf_{u\in\mathcal{W}_{T}}\biggl\{\frac{1}{2hT^{d}}\int_{(0,T)^{d}\times(-h,h)}f\big(x,y,(A+\nabla_{x}u|\partial_{y}u)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\biggr\}.

Fix
\delta>0
and let
\eta
be such that
\delta>\eta>0
. Now fix
T>0
and let
u_{T}\in\mathcal{W}_{T}
be such that

\frac{1}{2hT^{d}}\underbrace{\int_{(0,T)^{d}\times(-h,h)}f\big(x,y,(A+\nabla_{x}u_{T}|\partial_{y}u_{T})\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y}_{\eqqcolon C_{T}}\leq g_{A}(T)+\frac{1}{T}.

We want to estimate
g_{A}(S)
in terms of
g_{A}(T)
when
S\gg T
. For this purpose, we construct test functions
u_{S}\in\mathcal{W}_{S}
by a patchwork procedure, and we then exploit the almost-periodicity of the energy density
f
, extending
u_{T}
to a function which has an energy contribution of
\mathrm{o}(\frac{1}{\eta})C_{T}
.
More precisely, we apply Lemma
1.6
to the integrable functions defined for
t\in[0,h]
by

g(t)\coloneqq\int_{(0,T)^{d}}|A+\nabla_{x}u_{T}(x,\pm{t}),\partial_{y}u_{T}(x,\pm{t})|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

We then obtain two values
y_{\eta}^{+}\in(h-\delta,h)
and
y_{\eta}^{-}\in(h,-h+\delta)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,T]^{d}\times{(y_{\eta}^{+},h+\eta)}}f\big(x,y,\big(A+\nabla_{x}u_{T}(x,y_{\eta}^{+})\big|\,\partial_{y}u_{T}(x,y_{\eta}^{+})\big)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

(2.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta\int_{[0,T]^{d}\times{(y_{\eta}^{+},h+\eta)}}\Bigl(1+\big|\big(A+\nabla_{x}u_{T}(x,y_{\eta}^{+})\big|\,\partial_{y}u_{T}(x,y_{\eta}^{+})\big)\big|^{p}\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta T^{d}{|h+\eta-y_{\eta}^{+}|}+\frac{\beta}{|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\int_{[0,T]^{d}\times(-h,h)}\big|\big(A+\nabla_{x}u_{T}(x,y_{\eta}^{+})\big|\,\partial_{y}u_{T}(x,y_{\eta}^{+})\big)\big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta\left(T^{d}(\eta+\delta)+\frac{C_{T}}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\right)

and

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\int_{[0,T]^{d}\times{(-h-\eta,y_{\eta}^{-})}}f\Big(x,y,\big(A+\nabla_{x}u_{T}(x,y_{\eta}^{+})\big|\,\partial_{y}u_{T}(x,y_{\eta}^{+})\big)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\beta\left(T^{d}(\eta+\delta)+\frac{C_{T}}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\right).

(2.12)

Thus, we can modify
u_{T}
setting

\widetilde{u}_{T}(x,y)\coloneqq\begin{cases}u_{T}(x,y_{\eta}^{+})&\text{ if }y\geq y^{+}_{\eta}\\
u_{T}(x,y)&\text{ if }y_{\eta}^{-}\leq y\leq y_{\eta}^{+}\\
u_{T}(x,y_{\eta}^{-})&\text{ if }y\leq y^{-}_{\eta}\end{cases}

Let
L_{\eta}
be the inclusion length related to
\mathcal{T}_{\eta}
and let
S>T+L_{\eta}
. Define

\mathcal{I}_{S}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap\Big[0,\Big\lfloor\frac{S}{T+L_{\eta}}\Big\rfloor-1\Big)^{d}

and, for every
\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{S}
, choose

\tau_{\ell}\in\big((T+L_{\eta})\ell+[0,L_{\eta}]^{d})\big)\cap\mathcal{T}_{\eta},

and the related
z_{\ell}
.
We then define a new test function by

u_{S}(x,y)=\begin{cases}\widetilde{u}_{T}(x-\tau_{\ell},y-z_{\ell})&(x,y)\in\big(\tau_{\ell}+(0,T)^{d}\big)\times(-h,h)\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

for every
(x,y)\in(0,S)^{d}\times(-h,h)
.

Note that
u_{S}
is equal to zero on the set

Q_{S}\coloneqq\Big((0,S)^{d}\setminus\bigcup_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{S}}\tau_{\ell}+(0,T)^{d}\Big)\times(-h,h).

We have

|Q_{S}|\leq 2hS^{d}\bigg(1-\Big(\frac{T}{T+L_{\eta}}-\frac{T}{S}\Big)^{d}\bigg).

We can now estimate
g_{A}(S)
using (
2.11
) and (
2
)

\begin{split}2S^{d}g_{A}(S)&\leq\int_{(0,S)^{d}\times(-h,h)}f\big(x,y,A+\nabla_{x}u_{S}(x,y),\partial_{y}u_{S}(x,y)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{S}}\int_{(\tau_{\ell}+(0,T)^{d})\times(-h,h)}f\big(x,y,A+\nabla_{x}\widetilde{u}_{T}(x-\tau_{\ell},y-z_{\ell}),\partial_{y}\widetilde{u}_{T}(x-\tau_{\ell},y-z_{\ell})\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\mkern 90.0mu+\int_{Q_{S}}f(x,A)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\leq\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{I}_{S}}\int_{(0,T)^{d}\times(-h,h)}f\big(\tau_{\ell}+x,z_{\ell}+y,A+\nabla_{x}{u}_{T}(x,y),\partial_{y}{u}_{T}(x,y)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\mkern 90.0mu+\Big\lfloor\frac{S}{T+L_{\eta}}\Big\rfloor^{d}\frac{2\beta C_{T}}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}+\Big\lfloor\frac{S}{T+L_{\eta}}\Big\rfloor^{d}2T^{d}\beta(\delta+\eta)+\int_{Q_{S}}f(x,A)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\end{split}

Now, exploiting the almost-periodicity of
f
we get

\begin{split}2S^{d}g_{A}(S)&\leq\Big\lfloor\frac{S}{T+L_{\eta}}\Big\rfloor^{d}\bigg(\int_{(0,T)^{d}\times(-h,h)}f\Big(x,y,\big(A+\nabla_{x}{u}_{T}(x,y)\big|\partial_{y}{u}_{T}(x,y)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\mkern 90.0mu+\eta\int_{(0,T)^{d}\times(-h,h)}1+\Big|\big(A+\nabla_{x}u_{T}(x,y)\big|\partial_{y}u_{T}(x,y)\big)\Big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg)\\
&\mkern 90.0mu+\Big\lfloor\frac{S}{T+L_{\eta}}\Big\rfloor^{d}\frac{2\beta C_{T}}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}+\Big\lfloor\frac{S}{T+L_{\eta}}\Big\rfloor^{d}2T^{d}\beta(\delta+\eta)+\int_{Q_{S}}f(x,A)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\end{split}

Using the p-growth conditions

\begin{split}2S^{d}g_{A}(S)&\leq\Big(1+\frac{\eta}{\alpha}\Big)\Big\lfloor\frac{S}{T+L_{\eta}}\Big\rfloor^{d}{\int_{(0,T)^{d}\times(-h,h)}f\Big(x,y,\big(A+\nabla_{x}{u}_{T}(x,y)\big|\partial_{y}{u}_{T}(x,y)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y}\\
&+2\eta hT^{d}\Big\lfloor\frac{S}{T+L_{\eta}}\Big\rfloor^{d}+\Big\lfloor\frac{S}{T+L_{\eta}}\Big\rfloor^{d}\frac{2\beta C_{T}}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}+\Big\lfloor\frac{S}{T+L_{\eta}}\Big\rfloor^{d}2T^{d}\beta(\delta+\eta)\\
&\mkern 90.0mu+2\beta hS^{d}\bigg(1-\Big(\frac{T}{T+L_{\eta}}-\frac{T}{S}\Big)^{d}\bigg)\big(1+|A|\big)^{p}\end{split}

We now exploit
C_{T}/(2T^{d})\leq g_{A}(T)+1/T

\begin{split}g_{A}(S)&\leq\Big(\frac{T}{T+L_{\eta}}\Big)^{d}\Big(1+\frac{\eta}{\alpha}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{2\beta}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\Big)\Big(g_{A}(T)+\frac{1}{T}\Big)\\
&+\Big(\eta h+\beta(\delta+\eta)\Big)\Big(\frac{T}{T+L_{\eta}}\Big)^{d}+\beta h\bigg(1-\Big(\frac{T}{T+L_{\eta}}-\frac{T}{S}\Big)^{d}\bigg)\big(1+|A|\big)^{p}\end{split}

Taking the limit, first as
S\to\infty
and then as
T\to\infty

\limsup_{S\to+\infty}g_{A}(S)\leq\Big(1+\frac{\eta}{\alpha}\Big)\Big(1+\frac{2\beta}{\alpha|\log(\delta/\eta)|}\Big)\liminf_{T\to+\infty}g_{A}(T)+\eta h+\beta(\delta+\eta)

We first send
\eta\to 0
and obtain

\limsup_{S\to+\infty}g_{A}(S)\leq\liminf_{T\to+\infty}g_{A}(T)+\beta\delta,

and then conclude by the arbitrariness of
\delta
.
∎

Eventually, it suffices to remark that Proposition
2.2
implies that
f_{0}(A)
is independent on the subsequence
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}
, so that we simultaneously have the existence of the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
and the representation (
1.12
), which concludes the proof of the theorem.

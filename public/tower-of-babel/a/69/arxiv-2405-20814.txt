Abstract
. In this paper, we study hyperrigidity for singly generated commutative unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebras. We look at hyperrigidity from four different points of view. The first is via operator-moment characterizations of spectral measures, the subject intensively exploited in quantum theory. The second is based on dilation theory and the Stone-von Neumann calculus for normal operators. The third, inspired by a recent result by L. G. Brown, concerns the weak and strong convergence of sequences of subnormal (or normal) operators. Finally, the fourth deals with the multiplicativity of unital completely positive maps on C ∗ C^{*} -subalgebras generated by single normal elements. The latter is inspired by Petz’s theorem and its generalizations established first by Arveson in the finite-dimensional case and then by L. G. Brown in general. The above machinery, together with sophisticated operator inequalities (Kadison’s, Hansen’s and Lieb-Ruskai’s), makes it possible to identify certain subsets G G of the set { t ∗ m t n : m , n = 0 , 1 , 2 , … } \{t^{*m}t^{n}\colon m,n=0,1,2,\ldots\} that are hyperrigid in C ∗ ​ ( t ) C^{*}(t) , the unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebra generated by a single normal element t t . Examples based on the Choquet boundary are included to confirm the optimality of our results.

1.
Introduction

Denote by
\mathbb{N}
the set of all positive integers.
Henceforth,
\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
stands for the
C^{*}
-algebra
of all bounded linear operators on a (complex) Hilbert
space
\mathcal{H}
and
I
denotes the identity operator on

\mathcal{H}
. We always assume that completely positive maps
are linear and representations of unital

C^{*}
-algebras preserve units and involutions.

The classical approximation theorem due to P. P.
Korovkin
[
54
]
states that for any sequence of
positive linear maps
\varPhi_{k}\colon C[0,1]\to C[0,1]
(
k\in\mathbb{N}
),

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\varPhi_{k}(x^{j})-x^{j}\|=0\;\;\forall j\in\{0,1,2\}\implies\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\varPhi_{k}(f)-f\|=0\;\;\forall f\in C[0,1].

In other words, the asymptotic behaviour of the
sequence
\{\varPhi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
on the

C^{*}
-algebra
C[0,1]
is uniquely determined by the
set
G=\{1,x,x^{2}\}
. Sets with this property are
called Korovkin sets. Korovkin’s theorem unified many
existing approximation processes such as the Bernstein
polynomial approximation of continuous functions and
the Fejér trigonometric polynomial approximation
of continuous functions on the unit circle. Another
major achievement was the discovery of geometric
theory of Korovkin sets by Y. A. Šaškin

[
74
]
. Namely, Šaškin observed that the
key property of
G
is that the Choquet boundary of
the vector space spanned by
G
coincides with
[0,1]
(see Section
3
for the definition).
Detailed surveys of most of these developments can be
found in
[
13
,
2
,
3
]
. Recently, the
Šaškin theorem has been extended by Davidson
and Kennedy to the case of (not necessarily
metrizable) compact Hausdorff spaces (see

[
32
, Theorem 5.3]
). Motivated both by the
fundamental role of the Choquet boundary in classical
approximation theory, and by the importance of
approximation in the contemporary theory of operator
algebras, Arveson introduced hyperrigidity as a form
of ’noncommutative’ approximation that captures many
important operator-algebraic phenomena.

For the purposes of this paper, we use the concept of
hyperrigidity in a more general context.

Definition 1.1

(Hyperrigidity)
.

A nonempty subset
G
of a unital
C^{*}
-algebra

\mathscr{A}
is said to be
hyperrigid
(
relative to

\mathscr{A}
) if for any faithful
representation
\pi\colon\mathscr{A}\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
on a
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and for any sequence

\varPhi_{k}\colon\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
(
k\in\mathbb{N}
) of unital completely positive (UCP) maps,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\varPhi_{k}(\pi(g))-\pi(g)\|=0\;\forall g\in G\implies\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\varPhi_{k}(\pi(a))-\pi(a)\|=0\;\forall a\in\mathscr{A}.

Unlike Arveson’s original definition of hyperrigidity
(cf.
[
7
, Definition 1.1]
), we do not
require the set
G
to be at most countable or
generate
\mathscr{A}
. To find out more about this, we
refer the reader to Appendix
B
. Let us also
note that even if
\mathscr{A}
is commutative, the concept
of hyperrigidity is
a priori
stronger than the
concept of Korovkin’s set, because in the former case
each map
\varPhi_{k}
(
k\in\mathbb{N}
) can take
noncommutative values.

Arveson gave several examples of hyperrigid sets. In
particular, he proved that if
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a
selfadjoint operator and
\mathscr{A}
is the unital

C^{*}
-algebra generated by
T
, then the set
G=\{I,T,T^{2}\}
is hyperrigid in
\mathscr{A}
(this is not the
case for normal operators, see Example
8.9
).
Moreover, if
V_{1},\dots,V_{n}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a
finite system of isometries that generates the unital

C^{*}
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
, then the set
G=\{V_{1},\dots,V_{n},V_{1}V_{1}^{*}+\dots+V_{n}V_{n}^{*}\}
is hyperrigid in

\mathscr{A}
. Arveson asked in
[
7
, Remark 9.5]

whether the set
\{I,T,f(T)\}
is hyperrigid in the
unital
C^{*}
-algebra generated by a selfadjoint
operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
, where
f
is a strictly
convex continuous function on the closed subinterval
of the real line
\mathbb{R}
containing the spectrum of

T
. A positive answer was given by L. G. Brown in

[
19
]
, by using the following theorem, which
inspired us to find a completely new characterization
of hyperrigidity for singly generated commutative
unital
C^{*}
algebras (see statements (v) and (vi) of
Theorem
2.2
). Below, the spectrum of an
operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
(resp., an element

of a
unital
C^{*}
-algebra

(resp.,
\sigma(t)
).

Theorem 1.2

(
[
19
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let

be a strictly convex
continuous function on an interval

,
let

be a Hilbert space, and let

In accordance with Šaškin’s insightful

Dor-On in
[
16
]
(see also
[
15
]
).
However, Arveson’s hyperrigidity conjecture is still
open for commutative C*-algebras (even the singly

In recent years, this issue has attracted considerable
interest in various parts of operator algebras and
operator theory

,

,

,

,

In particular, it is related to the Arveson-Douglas

In this paper, we show that the concept of
hyperrigidity is closely related to the problem of
characterizing the spectrality of semispectral
measures by their operator-moments (see
Section
3
for the definition of a
semispectral measure). The classical von Neumann
approach to quantum mechanics via Borel spectral
measures on
\mathbb{R}
(read: selfadjoint operators) is
insufficient in describing many natural properties of
measurement, such as measurement inaccuracy and
measurement statistics. Therefore, in standard modern
quantum theory, the generalization to semispectral
measures is widely used (see, e.g.,

[

,

,

,

,

,

,

The main purpose of the quantization proposed by J.
Kiukas, P. Lahti and K. Ylinen in

[

52
]
was to construct observables
that are not spectral measures and this was done by
applying the technique of operator moments. They
needed a criterion to determine via moments whether a
given observable is a spectral measure or not (see

[
52
, Section VI]
). They asked the question
of a general nature:
When is a positive
operator measure projection valued?
(see

[
52
]
). One of the first results on this
topic states that if

is a Borel semispectral
measure on

with compact support, then

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}xF(\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}x)\Big)^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}F(\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}x)\;\;\iff\;\;\textit{$F$ is spectral.}

(1.1)

(See
[
55

,

[

, Theorem 5]
; see also

[
67
, Remark 5.3]

Theorem 1.3

(
[

, Theorem 4.2]

[

, Theorem 1.7]

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and

(i)

, where

(ii)

is selfadjoint,

and

If

-valued Borel semispectral

\displaystyle T^{k}=\int_{X}x^{k}F(\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}x)\;\;\forall k\in\varOmega\;\;\iff\;\;\text{$F$ is the
spectral measure of $T$}.

Characterizations of this type make it possible to
solve problems in pure operator theory. Namely,
Problem 1.1 posted in
[

[

applying Theorem
1.3
(i).

2.
Main results

In this section we will formulate the main results of
this paper. We begin by showing that there is a close
connection between the concept of hyperrigidity and
the characterizations of spectral measures when the

C^{*}
-algebras under consideration are commutative. To
provide
C^{*}
-algebra generalization of
Theorem
1.3
, it seems natural to replace the
set
\{x^{k}\colon k\in\varOmega\}
by a subset
G
of
a commutative unital
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
satisfying
the following condition:

for every representation
\pi\colon\mathscr{A}\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and every regular

\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
-valued Borel semispectral measure

on

\pi(a)=\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}}}\widehat{a}\,\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}F\;\;\forall a\in G\implies\textit{$F$ is the spectral measure of
$\pi$,}

(2.1)

where

and

are the maximal ideal

\mathscr{A}
and the Gelfand transform of

means

-algebras, the hyperrigidity of

is completely characterized by (

following theorem is a direct consequence of Theorems

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

be a nonempty subset of a commutative unital

-algebra
\mathscr{A}
. Then

is hyperrigid if and
only if

satisfies (

). Moreover, if

\mathscr{A}
is separable, these two conditions are still
equivalent regardless of whether the Hilbert spaces
considered in either of them are separable or not.

Let us now focus on the case of a commutative unital

C^{*}
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
generated by a single element

8.

In this section, we establish examples illustrating

2.2

Lemma 8.1

Theorem A.1

distinct points
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
such that

f(x_{1})=f(x_{2})
for all
f\in G
. Since evaluations
are characters,
f(x_{1})=f(x_{2})
for all
f\in C^{*}(G)
.
As compact Hausdorff spaces are normal, Urysohn’s
lemma implies that there exists
f_{0}\in C(X)
such
that
f_{0}(x_{1})\neq f_{0}(x_{2})
, so
f_{0}\in C(X)\setminus C^{*}(G)
, a contradiction.
∎

The following observation plays an important role in
this paper.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a separable commutative unital

C^{*}
-algebra. Then
C(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}})
is
separable
(
relative to the supremum norm
)
,

\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}}
is a separable metrizable and
compact Hausdorff space, and any Borel complex measure
on
\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}}
is regular.

Proof.

Since, by the Gelfand-Naimark theorem, the Gelfand
transform
\mathscr{A}\ni a\mapsto\widehat{a}\in C(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}})
is an isometric
*
-algebra
isomorphism, we see that
C(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}})
is
separable. By
[
36
, Theorem 2.7]
,

\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}}
is a metrizable compact
Hausdorff space. As a consequence, any nonempty open
subset of
\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}}
is
\sigma
-compact.
Hence, by
[
72
, Theorem 2.18]
, finite positive
Borel measures on
\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}}
are
automatically regular. Using the Jordan decomposition
of real measures (see
[
72
, Section 6.6]
), we
conclude that Borel complex measures on

\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}}
are regular. The metric space

\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}}
, being compact is automatically
separable (see
[
56
, Theorem IV.§41.VI.1]
).
∎

Next we state a version of Bochner’s theorem for
operator-valued maps on commutative
C^{*}
-algebras,
together with the spectral theorem for representations
(for the case of
C(X)
with compact
X\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, we refer the reader to

[
64
, Proposition 4.5]
).

Theorem A.3
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a commutative unital
C^{*}
-algebra,

\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
\pi,\varPhi\colon\mathscr{A}\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
be arbitrary maps. Then the following
statements are valid:

(i)

\pi
is a
representation of
\mathscr{A}
if and only if
there exists a
(
unique
)
regular spectral
measure
E\colon\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}})\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})

such that

\displaystyle\pi(a)=\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}}}\widehat{a}\,\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}E,\quad a\in\mathscr{A},

(A.1)

(ii)

\varPhi
is a
UCP map if and only if there exists a

(
unique
)
regular semispectral measure

F\colon\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}})\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that

\displaystyle\varPhi(a)=\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}}}\widehat{a}\,\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}F,\quad a\in\mathscr{A}.

Moreover, if
E\colon\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}})\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a regular semispectral measure
satisfying (
A.1
), where
\pi
is a
representation of
\mathscr{A}
, then
E
is spectral.

The (unique) regular spectral measure
E

appearing in statement (i) of
Theorem
A.3
will be called the
spectral measure
of the representation

\pi
.

Proof of Theorem
A.3
.

(i) Suppose
\pi
is a representation of
\mathscr{A}
. By

[
61
, Theorem 3.1.6]
,
\mathscr{B}:=\pi(\mathscr{A})
is a
commutative unital
C^{*}
-algebra, so by

[
71
, Theorem 12.22]
, there exists a regular
spectral measure
G\colon\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{B}})\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that
b=\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{B}}}\widehat{b}\,\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}G
for all

b\in\mathscr{B}
. Hence

\pi(a)=\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{B}}}\widehat{\pi(a)}\,\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}G
for all
a\in\mathscr{A}
. Define the continuous map

\hat{\pi}\colon\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{B}}\to\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}}
by
\hat{\pi}(\chi)=\chi\circ\pi
for
\chi\in\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{B}}
. Applying

[
17
, Theorem 5.4.10]
yields

\displaystyle\pi(a)=\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{B}}}\widehat{\pi(a)}\,\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}G=\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{B}}}\widehat{a}\circ\hat{\pi}\,\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}G=\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}}}\widehat{a}\,\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}G_{\pi},\quad a\in\mathscr{A},

(A.2)

where
G_{\pi}\colon\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}})\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is the spectral measure given by

G_{\pi}(\varDelta)=G(\hat{\pi}^{-1}(\varDelta))
for

\varDelta\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}})
. By the
continuity of
\hat{\pi}
,
G_{\pi}
is regular.
According to (
A.2
),
E:=G_{\pi}
satisfies
(
A.1
). Since
\{\hat{a}\colon a\in\mathscr{A}\}=C(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}})
(see

[
71
, Theorem 11.18]
), the uniqueness of
E
in
(
A.1
) follows from
[
72
, Theorem 6.19]
.
This proves necessity. The sufficiency is well known.

(ii) Suppose
\varPhi
is a UCP map. By the
Stinespring dilation theorem (see

[
80
, Theorem 1]
), there exists a
Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
containing
\mathcal{H}
and a
representation
\rho\colon\mathscr{A}\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K})
such that
\varPhi(a)=P\rho(a)|_{\mathcal{H}}
for
a\in\mathscr{A}
, where

P\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K})
is the orthogonal
projection of
\mathcal{K}
onto
\mathcal{H}
. By (i),
there exists a regular spectral measure

E\colon\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}})\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K})
such that
\rho(a)=\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}}}\widehat{a}\,\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}E
for
a\in\mathscr{A}
. These two identities
yield
\varPhi(a)=\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}}}\widehat{a}\,\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}F
for
a\in\mathscr{A}
, where
F\colon\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}})\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})

is the regular semispectral measure given by

F(\varDelta)=PE(\varDelta)|_{\mathcal{H}}
for

\varDelta\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}})
.
A similar argument as in (i) shows the
uniqueness of
F
, proving necessity.
Sufficiency follows from the observation
that if
F
is semispectral, then the map

\mathscr{A}\ni a\mapsto\int_{\mathfrak{M}_{\mathscr{A}}}\widehat{a}\,\mathrm{d\hskip 0.43057pt}F\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is linear and positive,
so by
[
80
, Theorem 4]
, it is
completely positive.

The “moreover” part is a consequence of
(i), the uniqueness part of (ii) and the
fact that representations are UCP.
∎

Appendix B
Hyperrigidity revisited

In this appendix, we will revise Arveson’s and
Kleski’s characterizations of the hyperrigidity of
operator systems. Our goal is to show that these
characterizations are true if we remove two
assumptions from
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
and

[
53
, Proposition 2.2]
: first, that the
operator system is separable and generates a given

C^{*}
-algebra, and second, that the Hilbert spaces
appearing explicitly in the characterizations are
separable. The only relevant assumption guaranteeing
this is that the algebra under consideration is
separable (see Theorem
B.2
). Recall that
Arveson gave in
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
a
characterization of hyperrigidity that replaces the
limit process by the so-called
unique extension
property
(see also
[
6
, Proposition 2.4]
).
The following definition is essentially in this
spirit.

Definition B.1
.

Let
G
be a nonempty subset of a unital

C^{*}
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
and
\pi\colon\mathscr{A}\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a representation of
\mathscr{A}
on a Hilbert
space
\mathcal{H}
. We say that
\pi|_{G}
has the
unique extension property
if the only UCP map

\varPhi\colon\mathscr{A}\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
that satisfies

\pi|_{G}=\varPhi|_{G}
is
\varPhi=\pi
itself.

Theorem
B.2
shows that the
characterizations of hyperrigidity given by Arveson
(the first four) and Kleski (the last) are equivalent
in a more general context.

Theorem B.2

(
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
and

[
53
, Proposition 2.2]
)
.

Suppose that
G
is a nonempty subset of a unital

C^{*}
-algebra
\mathscr{A}
. Consider the following
conditions:

(i)

G
is hyperrigid,

(ii)

for every Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, every
representation
\pi\colon\mathscr{A}\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
every sequence
\varPhi_{n}\colon\mathscr{A}\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})

(
n\in\mathbb{N}
)
of UCP maps,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\varPhi_{n}(g)-\pi(g)\|=0\;\;\forall g\in G\implies\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\varPhi_{n}(a)-\pi(a)\|=0\;\;\forall a\in\mathscr{A},

(iii)

for every Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and every
representation
\pi\colon\mathscr{A}\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
,

\pi|_{G}
has the unique extension property,

(iv)

for every unital
C^{*}
-algebra

\mathscr{B}
, every unital
*
-homomorphism of

C^{*}
-algebras
\theta\colon\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{B}
and
every UCP map
\varPhi\colon\mathscr{B}\to\mathscr{B}
,

\displaystyle\varPhi(x)=x\;\forall x\in\theta(G)\implies\varPhi(x)=x\;\forall x\in\theta(\mathscr{A}),

(v)

for every Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
,
every representation
\pi\colon\mathscr{A}\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})

and every sequence
\varPhi_{n}\colon\mathscr{A}\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})

(
n\in\mathbb{N}
)
of UCP maps,

\displaystyle\mathop{{\sf{w}\textrm{-}}lim}\displaylimits_{n\to\infty}\varPhi_{n}(g)=\pi(g)\;\forall g\in G\implies\mathop{{\sf{s}\textrm{-}}lim}\displaylimits_{n\to\infty}\varPhi_{n}(a)=\pi(a)\;\forall a\in\mathscr{A}.

Then conditions (i)-(iv) are equivalent.
Moreover, if
\mathscr{A}
is separable, then conditions
(i)-(v) are equivalent, and what is more
they are still equivalent regardless of whether the
Hilbert spaces considered in either of them are
separable or not.

Proof.

A careful analysis of the proof of

[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
shows that conditions
(i)-(iv) are equivalent without the assumption that
the Hilbert spaces appearing in them are separable,
and if
\mathscr{A}
is separable, they are still equivalent
if the Hilbert spaces appearing in at least one of
them are separable (this also applies to
Definition
1.1
). Our next goal is to show that
if
\mathscr{A}
is separable, then conditions (iii) and (v)
are equivalent. Since (v) implies (iii) (consider
constant sequence), it suffices to prove the converse
implication.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(v) provided
\mathscr{A}
is separable.
Let
\pi\colon\mathscr{A}\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a
representation and
\varPhi_{n}\colon\mathscr{A}\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})

(
n\in\mathbb{N}
)
be a sequence of UCP
maps such that

\displaystyle\mathop{{\sf{w}\textrm{-}}lim}\displaylimits_{n\to\infty}\varPhi_{n}(g)=\pi(g),\quad g\in G.

(B.1)

Set
\mathcal{H}_{h}=\overline{\{\pi(a)h\colon a\in\mathscr{A}\}}
for
h\in\mathcal{H}
. Since
\pi
is a
representation (which is automatically continuous, see

[
5
, Theorem 1.3.2]
), we deduce that
\mathcal{H}_{h}

reduces
\pi
. As
\mathscr{A}
is separable, each
\mathcal{H}_{h}

is separable. Let
\pi_{h}\colon\mathscr{A}\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}_{h})

be the representation defined by
\pi_{h}(a)=\pi(a)|_{\mathcal{H}_{h}}
for
a\in\mathscr{A}
and
h\in\mathcal{H}
.
Define also the maps
\varPhi_{n,h}\colon\mathscr{A}\to\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}_{h})
by
\varPhi_{n,h}(a)=P_{h}\varPhi_{n}(a)|_{\mathcal{H}_{h}}
for
a\in\mathscr{A}
,
h\in\mathcal{H}

and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
P_{h}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is the
orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{H}
onto
\mathcal{H}_{h}
. Clearly,
each
\varPhi_{n,h}
is a UCP map as the composition
of two UCP maps
\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})\ni T\mapsto P_{h}T|_{\mathcal{H}_{h}}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}_{h})
and
\varPhi_{n}
. In view of
(
B.1
), we have
\mathop{{\sf{w}\textrm{-}}lim}\displaylimits_{n\to\infty}\varPhi_{n,h}(g)=\pi_{h}(g)
for all
g\in G
and

h\in\mathcal{H}
. Applying
[
53
, Proposition 2.2]
to

A=\mathscr{A}
,
H=\mathcal{H}_{h}
and
S=\lin(\{e\}\cup G\cup G^{*})
, we see that

\mathop{{\sf{w}\textrm{-}}lim}\displaylimits_{n\to\infty}\varPhi_{n,h}(a)=\pi_{h}(a)
for
all
a\in\mathscr{A}
and
h\in\mathcal{H}
. Since
h\in\mathcal{H}_{h}
for
every
h\in\mathcal{H}
, we conclude that

\mathop{{\sf{w}\textrm{-}}lim}\displaylimits_{n\to\infty}\varPhi_{n}(a)=\pi(a)
for every
a\in\mathscr{A}
. Combined with
[
53
, Lemma 2.1]
, this
shows that (v) holds. That (iii) and (v) are still
equivalent if the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
appearing in (v)
is separable follows from the above discussion.
∎

A direct consequence of equivalence

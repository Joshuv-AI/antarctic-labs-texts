K K -polynomials of multiplicity-free varieties
Federico Castillo, Yairon Cid-Ruiz, Fatemeh Mohammadi, Jonathan Montaño

Abstract
. We describe the twisted K K -polynomial of multiplicity-free varieties in a multiprojective setting. More precisely, for multiplicity-free varieties, we show that the support of the twisted K K -polynomial is a generalized polymatroid. As applications, we show that the support of the Möbius function of a linear polymatroid is a generalized polymatroid and we settle a conjecture of Monical, Tokcan and Yong regarding Grothendieck polynomials for the case of zero-one Schubert polynomials.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field,
{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
be a multiprojective space, and
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}
be a subvariety.
Following Brion
[
9
]
, we say that
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}
is
multiplicity-free
if the corresponding class
[X]
in the Chow ring
A^{*}({\mathbb{P}})\cong\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]/\big(t_{1}^{m_{1}+1},\ldots,t_{p}^{m_{p}+1}\big)
is a linear combination only admitting coefficients
0
or
1
, of the classes of
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{a_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{a_{p}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}
with
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{p}=\dim(X)
.
As proved by Brion
[
9
]
, these varieties have a number of surprising and desirable properties (e.g., they are arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay and normal, and they admit a flat degeneration to a reduced union of products of projective spaces).
The family of multiplicity-free varieties is large enough that it has become of remarkable importance in applications in algebraic geometry, commutative algebra, representation theory, and combinatorics (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
10
,
51
,
40
,
9
,
58
,
25
,
39
,
52
,
44
,
15
,
22
,
20
,
21
,
19
,
14
]
).

The main goal of this paper is to provide a precise description of the class
[X]
of a multiplicity-free variety
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}
in the Grothendieck ring
K({\mathbb{P}})\cong\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]/\big((t_{1}-1)^{m_{1}+1},\ldots,(t_{p}-1)^{m_{p}+1}\big)
of coherent sheaves on
{\mathbb{P}}
.
We write the Hilbert series of
X
as
{\text{Hilb}}_{X}({\mathbf{t}})=\mathcal{K}(X;{\mathbf{t}})/(1-t_{1})^{m_{1}+1}\cdots(1-t_{p})^{m_{p}+1}
.
Following
[
38
]
, we say that
\mathcal{K}(X;{\mathbf{t}})
is the
K
-polynomial of
X
and we call
\mathcal{K}(X;{\mathbf{z}})=\mathcal{K}(X;1-z_{1},\ldots,1-z_{p})
the
twisted
K
-polynomial of
X
.
The coefficients of
{\mathcal{K}}(X;{\mathbf{z}})
correspond to the expression of
[X]\in K({\mathbb{P}})
as a linear combination of the classes of
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{a_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{a_{p}}}
with
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{a_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{a_{p}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}
.
Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem A

(
Theorem 7.9
)
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
be a multiplicity-free variety.
Then the support of
\mathcal{K}(X;{\mathbf{z}})
is a generalized polymatroid.
Furthermore, we have the specific description that
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in{\text{supp}}\left(\mathcal{K}(X;{\mathbf{z}})\right)
if and only if the following inequality holds

{\text{codim}}_{\mathfrak{J}}\left(X,{\mathbb{P}}\right)\;\leq\;\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}n_{j}\;\leq\;b_{\mathfrak{J}}(X,{\mathbb{P}})

for any nonempty subset
{\mathfrak{J}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,p\}
.

We now explain the notation in
Theorem A
.
For a given subset
{\mathfrak{J}}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subseteq[p]=\{1,\ldots,p\}
, we introduce the following notation.
We consider the natural projection

\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{j_{1}}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{j_{k}}}.

The codimension of
\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}(X)
in
\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}({\mathbb{P}})
is denoted as

{\text{codim}}_{\mathfrak{J}}\left(X,{\mathbb{P}}\right)={\text{codim}}\left(\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}(X),\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}({\mathbb{P}})\right).

We also define a certain bounding number
b_{\mathfrak{J}}(X,{\mathbb{P}})
; see
§7.1
for the precise definition.
When
{\mathfrak{J}}=[p]
, we have the expression
b_{[p]}(X,{\mathbb{P}})={\text{codim}}(X,{\mathbb{P}})+\text{reg}\left(\mathcal{S}(X)\right)
, where the second summand denotes the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity of the graded section ring

\mathcal{S}(X)\;=\;\bigoplus_{u\in\mathbb{N}}\left[\mathcal{S}(X)\right]_{u}\quad\text{ where }\quad\left[\mathcal{S}(X)\right]_{u}\;=\;\bigoplus_{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\;\,\sum_{j\in[p]}v_{j}=u}\text{H}^{0}\left(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})\right).

If we write
{\mathcal{K}}(X;{\mathbf{z}})=\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}\,c_{\mathbf{n}}(X){\mathbf{z}}^{\mathbf{n}}
,
then
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}
is multiplicity-free when
c_{\mathbf{n}}(X)\in\{0,1\}
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|={\text{codim}}(X,{\mathbb{P}})
.
The support of
\mathcal{K}(X;{\mathbf{z}})
is given by
{\text{supp}}\left(\mathcal{K}(X;{\mathbf{z}})\right)=\{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\mid c_{\mathbf{n}}(X)\neq 0\}
.

The problem of studying the support of the class of
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}
in
A^{*}({\mathbb{P}})

is relatively well-understood.
In the projective case (i.e.,
p=1
), this problem is vacuous as there is only one positive coefficient to consider (i.e., the degree of
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m}
).
In the biprojective case (i.e.,
p=2
) we have the works of Trung
[
59
]
and Huh
[
34
]
.
In
[
16
]
, we have explicitly described which multidegrees of
X
are positive in terms of the dimensions of the natural projections of
X
, and we have shown that they form a polymatroid.
Moreover, due to the work of Brändén and Huh
[
7
]
, we have that the volume polynomial of
X
(a polynomial encoding the class
[X]\subset A^{*}({\mathbb{P}})
) is Lorentzian.

On the other hand, much less is known regarding the support of the class of
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}
in the Grothendieck ring
K({\mathbb{P}})
.
For the projective case (i.e.,
p=1
), in
§3.1
, we give a simple extension of Marley’s work
[
45
]
on the coefficients of the Hilbert-Samuel polynomial of a zero-dimensional ideal in a Noetherian local ring.
As a consequence, in
§3.1
, we obtain that the class
[X]\in K({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m})
of an arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay subvariety
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m}
can be written as an alternating sign linear combination of the classes of
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{a}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m}
and that the corresponding support forms a segment containing all the intermediate points (i.e., it is a generalized polymatroid).
An extension of Marley’s work is not possible in general for the case
p\geq 2
(see
§3.1
).
For the entire multiprojective case (i.e, any
p\geq 1
), under the assumption that
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}
has rational singularities, another work of Brion
[
8
]
provides the natural extension of the aforementioned sign alternation statement (see also the works of Buch
[
12
]
and Anderson, Griffeth and Miller
[
2
]
).
In
Section 4
, we settle this sign alternation result for varieties with a square-free Gröbner degeneration whose corresponding simplicial complex is shellable.
We point out that multiplicity-free varieties have rational singularities and their multigraded generic initial ideals yield shellable simplicial complexes.
To the best of our knowledge,
Theorem A
is the first result that addresses the following question:

Given a subvariety
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}
, when do we have that
{\text{supp}}\left({\mathcal{K}}(X;{\mathbf{z}})\right)
is a generalized polymatroid?

In
Section 6
, we present additional results for multiplicity-free varieties.
There, we slightly change the notation as it is most convenient to work in multiprojective geometric terms.
For each
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, we denote by
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)
the corresponding
Hilbert coefficient
(see
§2.1
), when
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}=\dim(X)
we have that
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)
coincides with the usual notion of
multidegree
.
For a multiplicity-free variety
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}
, we prove the following results:

(i)

In
Section 6
, we show that the Hilbert polynomial equals the Hilbert function everywhere in
\mathbb{N}^{p}
.

(ii)

In
Section 6
, we prove that any lexicographical ordering of the points in
\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)=\{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\mid|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(X)\text{ and }\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)\neq 0\}
induces a shelling on the simplicial complex corresponding to the multigraded generic initial ideal (which is square-free for multiplicity-free varieties, see
Theorem 6.5
).
This is an important technical result for us.

(iii)

In
Theorem 6.9
, we show that the whole Hilbert polynomial of
X
is determined by
\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)
, and we provide an algorithmic process to compute it.

(iv)

Section 6
contains several technical results.
For instance, part (iv) gives an alternative proof of a result of Knutson
[
39
]
yielding a Möbius-like formula: for any
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
such that there exists
\mathbf{w}\in\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)
with
\mathbf{w}\geq{\mathbf{n}}
, we obtain the equality

\sum_{\mathbf{w}\geq{\mathbf{n}}}\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{w}}(X)\;=\;1.

(v)

In
Theorem 6.13
, we show that
\text{HSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)=\{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\mid\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)\neq 0\}
is a generalized polymatroid.

Our results in
Section 6
rely on the ones obtained in
Section 4
and
Section 5
.

Section 4
contains several results regarding the Hilbert polynomial of a square-free monomial ideal such that the corresponding simplicial complex is shellable (see
Section 4
,
Section 4
,
Section 4
and
Section 4
).

Section 5
provides a gluing result for generalized polymatroids that allows us to conclude that
\text{HSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)
is a generalized polymatroid (see
Theorem 5.17
); this is the most involved combinatorial part of our work and for better exposition we introduce certain combinatorial terms: the
stalactites
and the
caves
.

We now present some applications of our main result:
Theorem A
.

Schubert
and
Grothendieck polynomials
were introduced by Lascoux and Schützenberger
[
42
,
43
]
(for precise definitions, see
§7.3
).
These polynomials have played a central role in algebraic combinatorics (see, e.g.,
[
27
,
48
,
4
,
5
,
37
,
38
]
).
In
[
48
]
, Monical, Tokcan and Yong made conjectures on the Newton polytopes of several combinatorially defined polynomials.
In particular, they conjectured that Schubert and Grothendieck polynomials have the saturated Newton polytope property (see
[
48
, §5]
).
These conjectures have been settled in the positive for Schubert polynomials:
in
[
27
]
, it was shown that the support of an ordinary Schubert polynomial is a polymatroid;
in
[
17
]
, it was shown that the support of a double Schubert polynomial is also a polymatroid.
More recently, Huh–Matherne–Mészáros–St. Dizier
[
33
]
and Mészáros–Setiabrata–St. Dizier
[
46
]
conjectured that the support of a Grothendieck polynomial is a generalized polymatroid, and they proved it for a special class of permutations called Grassmannian.
Here we settle another important case.

Theorem B

(
Theorem 7.13
)
.

Let
w\in S_{p}
be a permutation such that
\mathfrak{S}_{w}
is a zero-one Schubert polynomial, then the support
{\text{supp}}(\mathfrak{G}_{w})
of the Grothendieck polynomial
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
is a generalized polymatroid.

Our proof of
Theorem B
follows by combining
Theorem A
and the work of Knutson and Miller
[
38
]
.
By
[
38
, Theorem A]
, the Schubert polynomial
\mathfrak{S}_{w}
equals the multidegree polynomial
\mathcal{C}(\overline{X}_{w};{\mathbf{z}})
and the Grothendieck polynomial
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
coincides with the twisted
K
-polynomial
\mathcal{K}(\overline{X}_{w};{\mathbf{z}})
.
Here
\overline{X}_{w}
denotes the
matrix Schubert variety
of
w
which can be seen as a subvariety of the product of projective spaces
\big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{p-1}\big)^{p}
.
If
\mathfrak{S}_{w}
is a zero-one Schubert polynomial (see
[
28
]
), then
\overline{X}_{w}
is a multiplicity-free variety.
Notice that
Theorem A
also provides defining inequalities for the Newton polytope of the Grothendieck polynomial
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
.
The description of these inequalities is in terms of algebraic invariants of the corresponding matrix Schubert variety
\overline{X}_{w}
.
It would be interesting to find combinatorial analogues of these inequalities.
In particular, the global upper bound (i.e., when
{\mathfrak{J}}=[p]
) involves the regularity of the matrix Schubert variety
\overline{X}_{w}
which has been recently studied in
[
50
]
from a combinatorial perspective.

We point out that our general approach under shellability assumptions (as in
Section 4
) could be used to study arbitrary Grothendieck polynomials.
For instance, since the initial ideal of the
Schubert determinantal ideal

I_{w}
with respect to the antidiagonal order is square-free and the corresponding simplicial complex is shellable (see
[
47
, §16.5]
),
Section 4
gives yet another proof of the alternation of sign of the coefficients in Grothendieck polynomials (also, see
Section 4
and
Section 4
for further possible consequences).
We restrict ourselves to the case of multiplicity-free varieties because the corresponding multigraded generic initial ideals are square-free and their associated primes are in bijection with the positive multidegrees (see
Section 6
).
This latter fact is a stepping stone for our combinatorial considerations in
Section 5
.

Polymatroids
are fundamental objects in the areas of combinatorics and optimization (see
[
32
, Chapter 12]
and
[
54
, Part IV]
).
They are a
natural extension of
matroids
.
Polymatroids
have also been studied under the names of
M-convex sets

[
49
]
and
generalized permutohedron

[
53
]
.
There are
two distinguished classes of polymatroids,
linear
and
algebraic polymatroids
, whose main properties are inherited by their respective representations.
By utilizing
Theorem A
, we obtain interesting consequences for linear polymatroids.
The general idea is that we can use multiplicity-free varieties as a model to study linear polymatroids.
Indeed, from the work of Li
[
44
]
, for a given linear polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
on
[p]
we can find a
multiview variety

X\subset{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
(a special type of multiplicity-free variety) that satisfies
\mathscr{P}=\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)
(see
§7.4
).
When
\mathscr{P}
is a matroid on
[p]
, we can assume that
X
is embedded in the product
\big({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{1}\big)^{p}
of projective lines (see
[
3
]
).
Our main result in this direction is the following theorem regarding the Möbius function of the partially ordered set associated to a polymatroid.

Theorem C

(
Theorem 7.17
)
.

Let
\mathscr{P}
be a linear polymatroid.
Consider the set

\mathscr{P}_{\leq}\;=\;\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}~\mid~\mathbf{u}\leqslant\mathbf{v}\text{ for some }\mathbf{v}\in\mathscr{P}\}

and the partially ordered set
\Gamma
on it given by componentwise ordering, with a maximum
\hat{1}
added.
Let
\mu_{\Gamma}
be the Möbius function of
\Gamma
.
Then the set

\mu\text{\rm-supp}(\mathscr{P})\;=\;\left\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathscr{P}_{\leq}~\mid~\mu_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{u},\hat{1})\neq 0\right\}

is a generalized polymatroid.

We expect the result of
Theorem C
to hold for arbitrary polymatroids (see
§7.4
).

2.
Preliminaries

Notation.
We first fix the notation that is used throughout the article.
We let
[p]:=\{1,\ldots,p\}
for
p\in\mathbb{N}
and we denote the standard basis vectors of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{p}
.
Given any vector
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, we denote
[\mathbf{a}]_{i}
for its
i^{\rm th}
coordinate and set
{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{a}):=\{i\mid[\mathbf{a}]_{i}\neq 0\}
.
We also denote
|\mathbf{a}|
for the
1
-norm of
\mathbf{a}
, i.e.,
\sum_{i=1}^{p}[\mathbf{a}]_{i}
.
For a pair of vectors
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
we write
\mathbf{a}\leq\mathbf{b}
if
[\mathbf{a}]_{i}\leq[\mathbf{b}]_{i}
for all
i
.
We denote by
\mathbf{0}
the vector
(0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.

Following
[
48
]
, we say that a polynomial
f=\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}}c_{{\mathbf{n}}}{\mathbf{z}}^{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{p}]
has the
Saturated Newton Polytope property
(SNP property for short) if the support
{\text{supp}}(f)=\{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\mid c_{\mathbf{n}}\neq 0\}
of
f
is equal to
\text{Newt}(f)\cap\mathbb{N}^{p}
, where
\text{Newt}(f):=\text{ConvexHull}\{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\mid c_{\mathbf{n}}\neq 0\}
denotes the
Newton polytope
of
f
; in other words, if the support of
f
consists of the integer points of a polytope.

2.1.
A short recap on multidegrees

Here we briefly recall the notion of multidegrees and some of its basic properties (for more details the reader is referred to
[
47
,
18
]
).

Let
p>0
be a positive integer,
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a standard graded
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring (i.e.,
\deg(x_{i})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
and
|\deg(x_{i})|=1
for all
i\in[p]
).
Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded
S
-module and
F_{\bullet}
be a
\mathbb{Z}^{p}
-graded free
S
-resolution

F_{\bullet}:\;\cdots\rightarrow F_{i}\rightarrow F_{i-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}

of
M
.
Let
t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}
be variables over
\mathbb{Z}
and consider the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{t}}]=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
, where the variable
t_{i}
corresponds with the
i
-th elementary vector
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
.
If we write
F_{i}=\bigoplus_{j}S(-\mathbf{b}_{i,j})
with
\mathbf{b}_{i,j}=(\mathbf{b}_{i,j,1},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{i,j,p})\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
, then we define the Laurent polynomial

\left[F_{i}\right]_{\mathbf{t}}\,:=\,\sum_{j}{\mathbf{t}}^{\mathbf{b}_{i,j}}=\sum_{j}t_{1}^{\mathbf{b}_{i,j,1}}\cdots t_{p}^{\mathbf{b}_{i,j,p}}.

The
Hilbert function
of
M
is denoted by
h_{M}:\mathbb{Z}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
with
h_{M}(\mathbf{v}):=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[M\right]_{\mathbf{v}}\right)
for any
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{p}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Under the above notation, we have the following notions:

(i)

The
K-polynomial
of
M
is given by

\mathcal{K}(M;{\mathbf{t}})\,:=\,\sum_{i}{(-1)}^{i}\left[F_{i}\right]_{\mathbf{t}}.

(ii)

The
twisted
K
-polynomial
of
M
is defined as
{\mathcal{K}}(M;{\mathbf{z}})={\mathcal{K}}(M;1-z_{1},\ldots,1-z_{p})
, and we write
{\mathcal{K}}(M;{\mathbf{z}})=\sum_{\mathbf{n}}c_{\mathbf{n}}(M)\,{\mathbf{z}}^{\mathbf{n}}
.

(iii)

The
multidegree polynomial
of
M
is the homogeneous polynomial
\mathcal{C}(M;{\mathbf{z}})\in\mathbb{Z}[{\mathbf{z}}]
given as the sum of all terms in

\mathcal{K}(M;{\mathbf{z}})

of smallest degree, which is equal to
{\text{codim}}(M)=n-\dim(M)
.

Another approach to study multidegrees is via the use of multigraded Hilbert polynomials.
For each
i\in[p]
, suppose that
S
has
m_{i}+1
variables with degree equal to
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
.
Thus we have
\text{MultiProj}(S)={\mathbb{P}}:={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.
We denote the (multigraded)
Hilbert polynomial
of
M
by
P_{M}({\mathbf{t}})=P_{M}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\in\mathbb{Q}[{\mathbf{t}}]=\mathbb{Q}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
.
Then, the degree of
P_{M}
equals the dimension
\dim(\widetilde{M})
of the correspondent coherent sheaf
\widetilde{M}
on
{\mathbb{P}}
and it satisfies

P_{M}(\mathbf{v})=h_{M}(\mathbf{v})

for all
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
such that
\mathbf{v}\gg\mathbf{0}
.
Furthermore, if we write

P_{M}({\mathbf{t}})\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}e_{\mathbf{n}}(M)\binom{t_{1}+n_{1}}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{t_{p}+n_{p}}{n_{p}},

then
e_{\mathbf{n}}(M)\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
{\mathbf{n}}
and
e_{\mathbf{n}}(M)\geq 0
when
|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim\big(\widetilde{M}\big)
.

Definition 2.2
.

For any
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, we say that
e_{\mathbf{n}}(M)
is the
Hilbert coefficient of
M
of type
{\mathbf{n}}
.
In particular, when
|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(\widetilde{M})
, we say that
e_{\mathbf{n}}(M)
is the
mixed multiplicity of
M
of type
{\mathbf{n}}
.

Let
J\subset S
be an
S
-homogeneous ideal that is saturated with respect to the multigraded irrelevant ideal of
S
, and consider the standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
R=S/J
.
Let

X=\text{MultiProj}(R)\subset{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}=\text{MultiProj}(S)

be the corresponding closed subscheme.
The
K
-polynomial
and the
multidegree polynomial
of
X
are given by
\mathcal{K}(X;{\mathbf{t}}):=\mathcal{K}(R;{\mathbf{t}})
and
\mathcal{C}(X;{\mathbf{t}}):=\mathcal{C}(R;{\mathbf{t}})
, respectively.
Similarly, the
Hilbert polynomial
of
X
is given by
P_{X}({\mathbf{t}}):=P_{R}({\mathbf{t}})
.

Definition 2.3
.

Under the above notation, we have the following notions:

(i)

For any
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
, we say that
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X):=e_{\mathbf{n}}(R)
is the
Hilbert coefficient of
X
of type
{\mathbf{n}}
.
In particular, when
|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(X)
, we say that
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)
is the
multidegree of
X
of type
{\mathbf{n}}
.

(ii)

We say that the
Hilbert support of
X
is given by the set

\text{HSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)\;:=\;\big\{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\mid\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)\neq 0\big\}.

(iii)

We say that the
multidegree support of
X
is given by the set

\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)\;:=\;\big\{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\mid|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(X)\text{ and }\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)\neq 0\big\}.

Under the above notation, we have the equality

\mathcal{C}(X;{\mathbf{z}})\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(X)\end{subarray}}\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)\,z_{1}^{m_{1}-n_{1}}\cdots z_{p}^{m_{p}-n_{p}};

see
[
16
, Remark 2.9]
.

In
§7.1
, we shall prove further connections between Hilbert coefficients and the
K
-polynomial.

Following standard notations, we say that
X
is a
variety
over
\mathbb{k}
if
X
is a reduced and irreducible separated scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{k}
(see, e.g.,
[
55
,
Tag 020C
]
).
Since multidegrees are non-negative integers, it becomes natural to address the positivity of these numbers.
We now recall a complete characterization for the positivity of multidegrees from
[
16
]
, which is an important tool in our work.
For each subset
\mathfrak{J}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subseteq[p]
, consider the natural projection
\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{j_{1}}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{j_{k}}}
and denote by
R_{({\mathfrak{J}})}
the
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra given by

R_{({\mathfrak{J}})}:=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\geq 0,\ldots,i_{p}\geq 0\\
i_{j}=0\text{ if }j\not\in{\mathfrak{J}}\end{subarray}}{\left[R\right]}_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{p})}.

The following theorem characterizes the positivity of multidegrees.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
16
, Theorem A, Proposition 5.1]
)
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
be a variety.
Then, for any
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(X)
, we have that
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)>0
if and only if
\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}n_{j}\;\leq\;\dim\left(\Pi_{\mathfrak{J}}(X)\right)
for all
{\mathfrak{J}}\subseteq[p]
.
Furthermore,
\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)
is an algebraic polymatroid.

3.
Motivating results in the literature

3.1.
Marley’s result for Cohen-Macaulay modules in a single graded setting

In this subsection, we extend Marley’s work
[
45
]
on the coefficients of the Hilbert-Samuel polynomial of a zero-dimensional ideal in a Noetherian local ring.
Here we prove the equivalent of Marley’s result for any module over a standard graded algebra.

Setup 3.1
.

Let
(A,{\mathfrak{n}})
be an Artinian local ring,
R
be a standard
\mathbb{N}
-graded finitely generated
A
-algebra, and
{\mathfrak{m}}=[R]_{+}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}[R]_{n}
be the graded irrelevant ideal of
R
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
R
-module.
We denote the
Hilbert function of
M
as

h_{M}:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z},\quad h_{M}(n):=\text{length}_{A}\left([M]_{n}\right).

Let
d=\dim(M)
be the dimension of
M
.
The
Hilbert polynomial of
M
can be written as

P_{M}(t)\;=\;\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}e_{i}(M)\binom{t+i}{i}\;\in\;\mathbb{Q}[t],

and it satisfies
e_{i}(M)\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
i\geq 0
,
e_{d-1}(M)>0
, and
P_{M}(n)=\text{length}_{A}\left([M]_{n}\right)
for
n\gg 0
(see, e.g.,
[
11
, §4.1]
).
The integers
e_{0}(M),\ldots,e_{d-1}(M)
are called the
Hilbert coefficients of
M
.
In particular,
e(M):=e_{d-1}(M)
is called the
multiplicity of
M
.

Given a function
f:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
, we define its first difference function as

\Delta^{1}(f):\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z},\quad\Delta^{1}(f)(n):=f(n+1)-f(n).

Inductively, we set
\Delta^{i}(f):=\Delta^{1}\left(\Delta^{i-1}(f)\right)
.
Also, we take the convention
\Delta^{0}(f):=f
.
The main result of this subsection is the following extension of
[
45
, Theorem 1]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume
§3.1
.
Let
M
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
R
-module of dimension
d=\dim(M)
.
Suppose that
M
is a Cohen-Macaulay module.
Then the following inequality

{(-1)}^{d-i}\Delta^{i}\left(h_{M}-P_{M}\right)(n)\;\geq\;0

holds for all
0\leq i\leq d
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

By utilizing the (standard) faithfully flat extension
A\rightarrow A[x]_{{\mathfrak{n}}A[x]}
, we may assume that the residue field of
A
is infinite.
Set
f:=h_{M}-P_{M}
.

First, we prove that
\Delta^{d}(f)(n)\geq 0
.
To that end, we proceed by induction on
d=\dim(M)
.
If
d=0
, then
P_{M}(t)=0
, and so the inequality
\Delta^{0}(f)(n)=h_{M}(n)-P_{M}(n)\geq 0
holds for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Suppose that
d\geq 1
.
Since the residue field of
A
is infinite and
M
is Cohen-Macaulay, we may find a general element
\ell\in[R]_{1}
which is a nonzerodivisor over
M
.
It follows that
M/\ell M
is a Cohen-Macaulay module of dimension
d-1
and that the equalities
\Delta^{1}(h_{M})(n)=h_{M/\ell M}(n+1)
and
\Delta^{1}(P_{M})(n)=P_{M/\ell M}(n+1)
hold.
Therefore, the induction hypothesis yields that

\Delta^{d}(f)(n)=\Delta^{d-1}\left(\Delta^{1}(f)\right)(n)=\Delta^{d-1}(h_{M/\ell M}-P_{M/\ell M})(n+1)\geq 0

for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Hence we have settled the case
i=d
.

Let
0<i\leq d
.
Suppose that we have showed
{(-1)}^{d-i}\Delta^{i}(f)(n)\geq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
On the other hand, we know that
f(n)=0
for all
n\gg 0
.
Thus, we have

{(-1)}^{d-i}\Delta^{i-1}(f)(n+1)\,-\,{(-1)}^{d-i}\Delta^{i-1}(f)(n)\,\geq\,0\quad\text{for all $n\in\mathbb{Z}$}

and
\Delta^{i-1}(f)(n)=0
for all
n\gg 0
.
This implies that
{(-1)}^{d-(i-1)}\Delta^{i-1}(f)(n)\geq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, finishing the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
M
be a Cohen-Macaulay finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
R
-module of positive dimension.
If
h_{M}(k)=P_{M}(k)
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
h_{M}(n)=P_{M}(n)
for all
n\geq k
.

Proof.

Let
d=\dim(M)\geq 1
.
By
Theorem 3.2
, we have
{(-1)}^{d-1}\Delta^{1}(h_{M}-P_{M})(n)\geq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and thus we obtain

0\;\geq\;{(-1)}^{d-1}(h_{M}-P_{M})(n)\;\;\text{ for all $n\in\mathbb{Z}$}

due to the fact that
h_{M}(n)=P_{M}(n)
for all
n\gg 0
.
Therefore

0\;\geq\;{(-1)}^{d-1}(h_{M}-P_{M})(n)\;\geq\;{(-1)}^{d-1}(h_{M}-P_{M})(k)\;=\;0

for all
n\geq k
, and the result follows.
∎

Finally, the following corollary provides positivity and rigidity results for the Hilbert coefficients of a Cohen-Macaulay module.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
M
be a Cohen-Macaulay finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
R
-module of dimension
d=\dim(M)
.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

{(-1)}^{d-1-j}e_{j}(M)\geq 0
for all
0\leq j\leq d-1
.

(ii)

For
0\leq j<i<d-1
, if
e_{i}(M)=0
then
e_{j}(M)=0
.

Proof.

As in
Theorem 3.2
, we may assume that the residue field of
A
is infinite, and thus may find a regular sequence
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{j}\in[R]_{1}
over
M
.
Then
M^{\prime}=M/(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{j})M
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
d-j
and we have the equality
e_{k}(M)=e_{k-j}(M^{\prime})
for all
k\geq j
.
Hence we may assume that
j=0
to prove both parts of the corollary.

(i) From
Theorem 3.2
, we get the inequality

0\;\leq\;{(-1)}^{d}\left(h_{M}(-1)-P_{M}(-1)\right)\;=\;{(-1)}^{d}\left(0-e_{0}(M)\right)\;=\;{(-1)}^{d-1}e_{0}(M)

that completes the proof of this part.

(ii) It suffices to show that: if
e_{1}(M)=0
then
e_{0}(M)=0
.
Thus assume that
e_{1}(M)=0
.
Choose a nonzerodivisor
\ell\in[R]_{1}
over
M
.
Then
e_{0}(M/\ell M)=e_{1}(M)=0
,
\Delta^{1}(h_{M})(n)=h_{M/\ell M}(n+1)
, and
\Delta^{1}(P_{M})(n)=P_{M/\ell M}(n+1)
.
Consequently,
P_{M/\ell M}(-1)=e_{0}(M/\ell M)=0
, and
§3.1
implies that
h_{M/\ell M}(n)=P_{M/\ell M}(n)
for all
n\geq-1
.
Inductively, since
h_{M}(n)=P_{M}(n)
for
n\gg 0
, we obtain

h_{M}(n)\;=\;P_{M}(n)\;\;\text{for all $n\geq-2$}.

Therefore,
P_{M}(-1)=0
and this implies that
e_{0}(M)=0
, finishing the proof.
∎

Example 3.5
.

Here we discuss a basic reason why an extension of Marley’s work
[
45
]
is not possible for the case
p\geq 2
.
Let
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
and
I=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{s})\subset S
be an ideal generated by homogeneous polynomials of the same positive degree.
The Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I)=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}I^{n}t^{n}
can be written as a quotient of the standard bigraded polynomial ring
T=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r},y_{0},\ldots,y_{s}]
.
It gives the bihomogeneous coordinate ring of the graph
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s}
of the rational map
\mathcal{F}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s}
with representative
(f_{0}:\cdots:f_{s})
.
Let
{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})\mathcal{R}(I)
,
{\mathfrak{m}}_{2}=(y_{0},\ldots,y_{s})\mathcal{R}(I)
, and
Y\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s}
be the image of
\mathcal{F}
.
To emulate the approach in the proof of
Theorem 3.2
for the bigraded algebra
\mathcal{R}(I)
, we would need to successively cut by nonzerodivisors that are either in
{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}
or
{\mathfrak{m}}_{2}
.
However, we have very few such elements in this case.
Indeed, we have that
{\text{codim}}({\mathfrak{m}}_{2})=1
and
{\text{codim}}({\mathfrak{m}}_{1})=(r+1)-\dim(Y)
(see, e.g.,
[
57
, Chapter 5]
); in particular, we also obtain that
{\text{codim}}({\mathfrak{m}}_{1})=1
when
\mathcal{F}
is generically finite.

3.2.
Brion’s positivity result for varieties with rational singularities

In this short subsection, we quickly present Brion’s positivity result
[
8
]
for varieties with rational singularities (also, see
[
10
]
), and we restrict ourselves to a multiprojective setting.
Since this result has motivated our positivity result for ideals with a shellable initial ideal, we provide a short account.

We recall the definition of rational singularities (for more details, see
[
41
, §5.1]
).
Let
X
be a variety over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic zero, and
f:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
be a resolution of singularities.
We say that
f:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
is a
rational resolution
if

(a)

f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
(equivalently,
X
is normal), and

(b)

R^{i}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}=0
for
i>0
.

We say that
X
has
rational singularities
if every resolution
f:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
is rational.

We present the following result of Brion that partly motivated our positivity results.

Theorem 3.6

(Brion
[
8
]
)
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
be variety with rational singularities.
Then the Hilbert coefficients of
X
alternate in sign.
More precisely, we have the inequality

(-1)^{\dim(X)-|{\mathbf{n}}|}\,\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)\,\geq\,0

for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.

Proof.

Let
f:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
be a rational resolution of
X
.
Consider the ample line bundle
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(1,\ldots,1)
.
From the rational singularities assumption and the projection formula (see, e.g.,
[
31
, Exercise III. 8.3]
), it follows that the Leray spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}\;=\;\text{H}^{p}\big(X,\,R^{q}f_{*}\left(f^{*}\mathcal{L}^{-1}\right)\big)\;\cong\;\text{H}^{p}\big(X,\,R^{q}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{L}^{-1}\big)\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{H}^{p+q}\big(\widetilde{X},f^{*}\mathcal{L}^{-1}\big)

degenerates, and so we get the isomorphism
\text{H}^{i}(X,\mathcal{L}^{-1})\cong\text{H}^{i}(\widetilde{X},f^{*}\mathcal{L}^{-1})
for all
i\geq 0
.
The Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing theorem (see, e.g.,
[
26
, §5]
) implies that
\text{H}^{i}(X,\mathcal{L}^{-1})\cong\text{H}^{i}(\widetilde{X},f^{*}\mathcal{L}^{-1})=0
for all
i<r:=\dim(X)
.
By utilizing this vanishing and the expression of the (multigraded) Hilbert polynomial in terms of Euler characteristic (see, e.g.,
[
36
, Lemma 4.3]
)

P_{X}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\;=\;\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{i}\big(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\big)\right),

we obtain the equalities

\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{0}}(X)\;=\;P_{X}(-1,\ldots,-1)=(-1)^{r}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{r}\big(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1,\ldots,-1)\big)\right).

Therefore the inequality
(-1)^{r}\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{0}}(X)\geq 0
follows, and we can conclude the proof by induction on
r
.
Indeed, for any
1\leq i\leq p
and any general hyperplane
H\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{i}}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
, we have that
Y=X\cap H
also has rational singularities (see the corresponding adaptation of Bertini’s theorem in
[
29
, Remark 3.4.11(3)]
) and that
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{{\mathbf{n}}-{\mathbf{e}}_{i}}(Y)=\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{{\mathbf{n}}}(X)
for all
{\mathbf{n}} \in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
{\mathbf{n}}\geq{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
.
∎

4.
Shellability and Hilbert polynomials

In this section, we deal with the Hilbert polynomial of a square-free monomial ideal whose corresponding simplicial complex is shellable.
For this family of ideals, we recover and strengthen the previous motivating results from
Section 3
(see
§3.1
and
Theorem 3.6
).

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\Delta
be a simplicial complex with facets
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}
.
We say that
\Delta
is
shellable with order
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}
if
\Delta
is of pure dimension and
\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{i-1}\rangle\cap\langle F_{i}\rangle
is pure of dimension
\dim(F_{i})-1
for all
2\leq i\leq k
, where
\langle F_{1},\ldots,F_{i-1}\rangle
denotes the simplicial complex generated by
F_{1},\ldots,F_{i-1}
.

Setup 4.2
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring.
Let
{\mathbb{P}}=\text{MultiProj}(S)
be the corresponding multiprojective space.
For a square-free monomial ideal
J\subset S
, we denote by
\Delta(J)
its Stanley-Reisner simplicial complex.

First, we have a general formula for the Hilbert polynomial of a square-free monomial ideal; the following Inclusion-Exclusion polynomial is useful to provide a simplified version of this formula.

Notation 4.3
.

Given a sequence of prime ideals
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\in\text{Spec}(S)
and a subset
\mathfrak{J}\subseteq[k]
, we define the ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathfrak{J}}:=\sum_{i\in\mathfrak{J}}{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
.
If
\mathfrak{J}=\emptyset
, we set
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathfrak{J}}=S
.
We further specify

\text{IE}(\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\})({\mathbf{t}}):=\sum_{\mathfrak{J}\subseteq[k]}(-1)^{|\mathfrak{J}|-1}P_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathfrak{J}}}({\mathbf{t}}).

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
J\subset S
be a square-free monomial ideal, and consider the corresponding minimal primary decomposition
J={\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}
.
Then we have the equality

P_{S/J}({\mathbf{t}})\;=\;{\rm IE}(\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\})({\mathbf{t}}).

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
k
.
The case
k=1
is clear.
We have the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow S/J\rightarrow S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{k-1}\oplus S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\rightarrow S/({\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{k-1})+{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\rightarrow 0.

The additivity of Hilbert polynomials gives the equality

P_{S/J}({\mathbf{t}})=P_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{k-1}}({\mathbf{t}})+P_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}({\mathbf{t}})-P_{S/({\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{k-1})+{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}({\mathbf{t}}).

Since each
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
is generated by a collection of variables, we have the distributive equality

({\mathfrak{p}}_{1} \cap\cdots\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{k-1})+{\mathfrak{p}}_{k} \;=\;({\mathfrak{p}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{k})\cap\cdots\cap({\mathfrak{p}}_{k-1}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}).

After applying the inductive hypothesis to the square-free monomial ideals
J_{1}={\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{k-1}
and
J_{2}=({\mathfrak{p}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{k})\cap\cdots\cap({\mathfrak{p}}_{k-1}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{k})
, we obtain

P_{S/J}({\mathbf{t}})\;=\;\sum_{\mathfrak{J}\subseteq[k-1]}(-1)^{|\mathfrak{J}|-1}P_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathfrak{J}}}({\mathbf{t}})+P_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}({\mathbf{t}})+\sum_{\{k\}\subsetneq\mathfrak{J}\subseteq[k]}(-1)^{|\mathfrak{J}|-1}P_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathfrak{J}}}({\mathbf{t}}),

whence the result follows.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

If
{\mathfrak{p}}\subset S
is a monomial prime ideal, then the corresponding Hilbert polynomial is given by

P_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}}({\mathbf{t}})\;=\;\binom{t_{1}+n_{1}({\mathfrak{p}})-1}{n_{1}({\mathfrak{p}})-1}\cdots\binom{t_{p}+n_{p}({\mathfrak{p}})-1}{n_{p}({\mathfrak{p}})-1}

where
n_{i}({\mathfrak{p}})
equals the number of variables of degree
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
not in
{\mathfrak{p}}
.
In particular,
P_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}}({\mathbf{t}})
has only one nonzero Hilbert coefficient.

Under a shellable assumption, we have a significant improvement of the formula given in
Section 4
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
J\subset S
be a square-free monomial ideal, and consider the corresponding minimal primary decomposition
J={\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}
.
Suppose that
\Delta(J)
is shellable, and that the shelling order of the facets is given by
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}

where
F_{i}
corresponds to
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
.
Let
c
be the common codimension of the primes
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
.
Then, depending on this shelling order, we have the equality

(1)

P_{S/J}({\mathbf{t}})\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left(P_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}}({\mathbf{t}})-D_{j}({\mathbf{t}})\right),

where
D_{j}({\mathbf{t}})={\rm IE}(\mathfrak{P}_{j})({\mathbf{t}})
and
\mathfrak{P}_{j}
is the following sequence
(
without repetitions
)
of prime ideals

\mathfrak{P}_{j}\,:=\,\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{\ell}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}\mid\ 1\leq\ell\leq j-1\text{ and }{\text{codim}}({\mathfrak{p}}_{\ell}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{j})=c+1\}.

Furthermore, for each
1\leq j\leq k
, there exists a set of distinct variables
\{x_{\ell_{j,1}},\ldots,x_{\ell_{j,b_{j}}}\}\subset{\mathfrak{p}}_{[j-1]}\setminus{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}
such that
\mathfrak{P}_{j}=\{({\mathfrak{p}}_{j},x_{\ell_{j,1}}),\ldots,({\mathfrak{p}}_{j},x_{\ell_{j,b_{j}}})\}
.

Proof.

Again, the proof follows by induction on
k
, and the base case
k=1
is clear.
The shellability assumption yields the equalities

J^{\prime}:=({\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{k-1})+{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}=({\mathfrak{p}}_{1}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{k})\cap\cdots\cap({\mathfrak{p}}_{k-1}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{k})\\
=\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\mathfrak{P}_{k}}{\mathfrak{p}}.

Moreover, if
{\text{codim}}({\mathfrak{p}}_{j}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{k})=c+1
, then the ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}
equals the sum of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}
and a variable not in
{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}
.
The short exact sequence
0\rightarrow S/J\rightarrow S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\cap\cdots\cap{\mathfrak{p}}_{k-1}\oplus S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\rightarrow S/J^{\prime}\rightarrow 0
and the induction hypothesis give the equality

P_{S/J}({\mathbf{t}})\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\left(P_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}}({\mathbf{t}})-D_{j}({\mathbf{t}})\right)+P_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}({\mathbf{t}})-D_{k}({\mathbf{t}}),

and so the result follows.
∎

We discuss several important consequences of the formula given in
Section 4
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Adopt the same assumptions and notation of
Section 4
.
Consider the corresponding closed subscheme
Z=\text{MultiProj}(S/J)\subset{\mathbb{P}}
.
Then, for every
1\leq j\leq k
and
{\mathfrak{J}}\subset[b_{j}]
, we have
{\text{codim}}({\mathfrak{p}}_{j,{\mathfrak{J}}})={\text{codim}}(J)+|{\mathfrak{J}}|
where
{\mathfrak{p}}_{j,{\mathfrak{J}}}:=\sum_{i\in\mathfrak{J}}({\mathfrak{p}}_{j},x_{\ell_{j,i}})
.
Thus,
(1)
provides a cancellation-free expression for
P_{S/J}({\mathbf{t}})
as a sum of the polynomials
P_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}}({\mathbf{t}})
and
(-1)^{|{\mathfrak{J}}|}P_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{j,{\mathfrak{J}}}}({\mathbf{t}})

(
see
Section 4
)
.
In particular,
(-1)^{\dim(Z)-|{\mathbf{n}}|}\deg^{\mathbf{n}}(Z)\geq 0
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.

Proof.

Only the last statement requires a proof.
For that, we note that for each
{\mathfrak{J}}\subset[b_{j}]
, we obtain

P_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{j,{\mathfrak{J}}}}({\mathbf{t}})\;=\;\binom{t_{1}+n_{1}({\mathfrak{p}}_{j,{\mathfrak{J}}})-1}{n_{1}({\mathfrak{p}}_{j,{\mathfrak{J}}})-1}\cdots\binom{t_{p}+n_{p}({\mathfrak{p}}_{j,{\mathfrak{J}}})-1}{n_{p}({\mathfrak{p}}_{j,{\mathfrak{J}}})-1},\quad\text{and}

|{\mathfrak{J}}|={\text{codim}}({\mathfrak{p}}_{j,{\mathfrak{J}}})-{\text{codim}}(J)=\left(\dim(S/J)-p\right)-\left(\dim(S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{j,{\mathfrak{J}}})-p\right)=\dim(Z)-\sum_{i=1}^{p}(n_{i}({\mathfrak{p}}_{j,{\mathfrak{J}}})-1)
.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
J\subset S
be a square-free monomial ideal such that
\Delta(J)
is shellable.
Consider the corresponding closed subscheme
Z=\text{MultiProj}(S/J)\subset{\mathbb{P}}
.
Then we have the equality

\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(Z)=1.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
P_{S/J}(\mathbf{0})=1
.
As in
Section 4
, suppose
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}
is an ordering of the minimal primes such that the corresponding facets with this order give a shelling of
\Delta(J)
.
We have that
P_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}(\mathbf{0})=1
.
On the other hand, for all
j\geq 2
, we have that

P_{S/{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}}(\mathbf{0})-D_{j}(\mathbf{0})\;=\;(1-1)^{b_{j}}\;=\;0;

this follows directly from the binomial formula.
Thus the claimed equality follows from
(1)
.
∎

Definition 4.9
.

We say that a sequence of lattice points

\mathbf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{a}_{u}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
is an
increasing lattice path
if for every
2\leq j\leq u
we have
\mathbf{a}_{j+1}-\mathbf{a}_{j}=\mathbf{e}_{i}
for some
i\in[p]
.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
J\subset S
be a square-free monomial ideal such that
\Delta(J)
is shellable.
Consider the corresponding closed subscheme
Z=\text{MultiProj}(S/J)\subset{\mathbb{P}}
.
Then for every
{\mathbf{n}}\in\text{HSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(Z)
there is an increasing lattice path from
{\mathbf{n}}
to a vector
\mathbf{a}\in\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(Z)
.

Proof.

This is a consequence of
(1)
and
Section 4
.
Indeed, if
{\mathbf{n}}\in\text{HSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(Z)\setminus\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(Z)
, then the corresponding term
\binom{t_{1}+n_{1}}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{t_{p}+n_{p}}{n_{p}}
is induced by a sum
D_{j}({\mathbf{t}})
(which is an inclusion-exclusion sum of ideals given as the sum of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}
and a variable not in
{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}
).
Furthermore, in
Section 4
we pointed out that the expression of
(1)
is cancellation-free.
∎

5.
Stalactites, caves, and polymatroids

This section includes the definitions and important properties of our main combinatorial constructions:
stalactites
and
caves
(see
Section 5
and
Section 5
).
In the main theorem of this section, we show that every cave is a generalized polymatroid
(see
Theorem 5.17
). We begin with some general considerations about finite sets of lattice points.

Notation 5.1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
be a finite subset.
We say that
\mathscr{A}
is
homogeneous
if
|\mathbf{a}|=|\mathbf{b}|
for every
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathscr{A}
.
For an arbitrary
\mathscr{A}
, we define
\max(\mathscr{A}):=\max\{|\mathbf{a}|\mid\mathbf{a}\in\mathscr{A}\}
and the
homogenization
of
\mathscr{A}
by

\mathscr{A}^{\text{hom}}\;:=\;\left\{(\mathbf{a},\,\max(\mathcal{A})-|\mathbf{a}|)\in\mathbb{N}^{p+1}~\mid~\mathbf{a}\in\mathscr{A}\right\}.

The
top
of the set
\mathscr{A}
is defined as

\mathscr{A}^{\text{top}}\;:=\;\{\mathbf{a}\in\mathscr{A}~\mid~|\mathbf{a}|=\max(\mathscr{A})\}.

For each
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we define the
\mathbf{b}
-truncation
of
\mathscr{A}
by

\mathscr{A}_{\mathbf{b}}:=\{\mathbf{a}\in\mathscr{A}~\mid~\mathbf{a}\geqslant\mathbf{b}\}
.

.

Here is an example of a
\mathbf{b}
-truncation with
\mathbf{b}=(2,0,0)
.

, we depict a set where we have marked the points in the top.
On the right hand side, we truncated by removing the points whose first coordinate are less than or equal to 1 (cf.

).

We continue by recalling the definition of (discrete) base and generalized polymatroids.

Definition 5.3
.

A
base polymatroid
(
polymatroid
) is a finite homogeneous subset
\mathscr{P}\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
satisfying the following property:

For every
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathscr{P}
and index
i\in[p]
with
[\mathbf{u}]_{i}>[\mathbf{v}]_{i}
there exists
j\in[p]
with
[\mathbf{u}]_{j}<[\mathbf{v}]_{j}
such that
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathscr{P}
.

The following result by Herzog and Hibi provides a
useful property satisfied by base polymatroids.

Theorem 5.4

(Symmetric Exchange,
[
32
, Theorem 12.4.1]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{P}
be a base polymatroid.
Then
\mathscr{P}
satisfies the following property:

For every
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathscr{P}
and index
i\in[p]
with
[\mathbf{u}]_{i}>[\mathbf{v}]_{i}
there exists
j\in[p]
with
[\mathbf{u}]_{j}<[\mathbf{v}]_{j}
such that
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathscr{P}
and
\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathscr{P}
.

Definition 5.5
.

A
generalized polymatroid
(
g-polymatroid
) is a subset
\mathscr{G}\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
such that
\mathscr{G}^{\text{hom}}
is a polymatroid.

The defining symmetric exchange property of polymatroids translates to the following two properties of g-polymatroids:

Exchange
:

For every pair
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\in\mathscr{G}\times\mathscr{G}
and index
i\in[p]
such that
[\mathbf{u}]_{i}>[\mathbf{v}]_{i}
at least one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

There exists
j\in[p]
with
[\mathbf{u}]_{j}<[\mathbf{v}]_{j}
such that
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}
and

\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{i}
are both in
\mathscr{G}
.

(2)

|\mathbf{u}|>|\mathbf{v}|
, and
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{e}_{i}
and
\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{e}_{i}
are both in
\mathscr{G}
.

Expansion
:

For every pair
(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\in\mathscr{G}\times\mathscr{G}
with
|\mathbf{u}|<|\mathbf{v}|
, there exists an index
j\in[p]
such that
[\mathbf{u}]_{j}<[\mathbf{v}]_{j}
, and
\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{e}_{j}
and
\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{e}_{j}
are both in
\mathscr{G}
.

Remark 5.6
.

We note that, by
Section 5
, in order to show a set is a g-polymatroid, it is enough to verify the Exchange and Expansion conditions for
\mathbf{u}
.

Definition 5.7
.

For a given polymatroid
\mathscr{P}\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
and
\mathbf{u}\in\mathscr{P}
, we define the following related objects:

(i)

We say that
\mathbf{v}\in\mathscr{P}
is the
neighbor
of
\mathbf{u}
in direction
(-\ell,j)
if
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{e}_{\ell}+\mathbf{e}_{j}
for some
\ell,j\in[p]
.

(ii)

Let
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m}
be distinct elements of
{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{u})
, we define the
stalactite

\text{St}(\mathbf{u};\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m})
to be the following set

\text{St}(\mathbf{u};\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{m}):=\left\{\mathbf{u}-\sum_{i\in\mathfrak{J}}\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{i}}\mid\mathfrak{J}\subseteq[m]\right\}.

Here,
\sum_{i\in\mathfrak{J}}\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{i}}=0
if
\mathfrak{J}=\emptyset
.

(iii)

Given
\mathscr{V}\subset\mathscr{P}
, we consider the set
\mathfrak{I}
of all
\ell\in[p]
such that there is a neighbor of
\mathbf{u}
in
\mathscr{V}
in direction
(-\ell,j)
for some
j\in[p]
. We define
\text{St}(\mathbf{u};\mathscr{V}):=\text{St}(\mathbf{u};\mathfrak{I}).

We are now ready to define the concept of caves.

Definition 5.8
.

A finite set
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
is a
cave
if for every truncation
\mathscr{A}:=\mathscr{C}_{\mathbf{b}}
with
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
the following conditions hold:

(a)

\mathscr{A}^{\text{top}}
is a polymatroid.

(b)

For a given order of the elements in
[p]
, we let
\prec
be the induced lexicographical (lex) order on
\mathbb{N}^{p}
, and we sort the elements of
\mathscr{A}^{\text{top}}
as:

\mathbf{a}_{1}\prec\mathbf{a}_{2}\prec\cdots\prec\mathbf{a}_{|\mathscr{A}^{\text{top}}|}.

Then, for every possible order on
[p]
, we have

(2)

\mathscr{A}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{|\mathscr{A}^{\text{top}}|}\text{St}(\mathbf{a}_{i};\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{a}_{i-1}\}).

(c)

If
\mathbf{b}\neq\mathbf{0}
, then
\mathscr{A}
is a g-polymatroid.

.

Let

Fact 5.11
.

For any
\mathbf{a}\in\mathscr{C}
there exist
u:=\max(\mathscr{C})-|\mathbf{a}|
and indices
\ell_{1}<\cdots<\ell_{u}
in
[p]
such that

\mathbf{a}^{\text{top}}:=\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{1}}+\dots+\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{u}}\in\mathscr{C}^{\text{top}}
and
\mathbf{a}\in\text{St}(\mathbf{a}^{\text{top}};\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{u})\subset\mathscr{C}
.
Moreover, there exist
j_{1},\ldots,j_{u}\in[p]
with
\ell_{i}<j_{i}\leqslant p
such that for each
i\in[u]
, the neighbor
\mathbf{b}_{i}:=\mathbf{a}^{\text{top}}-\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{i}}+\mathbf{e}_{j_{i}}
of
\mathbf{a}^{\text{top}}
belongs to
\mathscr{C}^{\text{top}}
.

Fact 5.12
.

Continuing with the notation in
Section 5
, assume further that
p\in{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{a})
. If
\mathbf{a}^{\text{top}}
has a neighbor
\mathbf{b}:=\mathbf{a}^{\text{top}}-\mathbf{e}_{p}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathscr{C}^{\text{top}}
, then
\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{e}_{p}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathscr{C}
.
To see this, we apply
Exchange
on
(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{b}_{i})
at index
\ell_{i}
to obtain a neighbor
\mathbf{c}_{i}:=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{i}}+\mathbf{e}_{\alpha_{i}}
for
\alpha_{i}\in\{j_{i},p\}
and we notice that
\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{e}_{p}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\text{St}(\mathbf{b};\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{u})=\text{St}(\mathbf{b};\{\mathbf{c}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{c}_{u}\})\subset\mathscr{C}
.

Fact 5.13
.

Exchange
and
Expansion
hold for any
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathscr{C}
with
{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{a})\cap{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{b})\neq\emptyset
. Indeed, this follows as
\mathscr{C}_{\mathbf{e}_{k}}
is a g-polymatroid for any
k\in{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{a})\cap{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{b})
.

In the following lemmas we prove some technical results about caves which are important for the proof of the main result of this section.

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
be a cave.
Then for every
\mathbf{\mathbf{u}}\in\mathscr{C}
we have
\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathscr{C}
for every
i\in[p]
such that
\mathscr{C}_{\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

By passing to the truncation
\mathscr{C}_{\mathbf{u}}
it is enough to consider the case
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0}
.
Set
r=\max(\mathscr{C})
. Let
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r}
and
j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}
be the indices obtained by applying
Section 5
to
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{0}
.
In particular, we get that
r\leq p
,
{\mathbf{n}}:=\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{1}}+\dots+\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{r}}\in\mathscr{C}^{\text{top}}
, and
\text{St}({\mathbf{n}};\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r})\subset\mathscr{C}
. It follows that
\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{i}}\in\mathscr{C}
for every
i\in[r]
.
Now, for each
k\in[r]
, we reorder
[p]
as
(1,\ldots,\ell_{k}-1,\ell_{k}+1,\ldots,p,\ell_{k})
and use
Section 5
with
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{k}}
,

\mathbf{a}^{\text{top}}={\mathbf{n}}
, and
\mathbf{b}={\mathbf{n}}-\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{k}}+\mathbf{e}_{j_{k}}
to show
\mathbf{e}_{j_{k}}\in\mathscr{C}
for each
k
.

To complete the proof, consider an index
i\in[p]\setminus\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r},j_{1},\dots,j_{r}\}

such that
\mathscr{C}_{\mathbf{e}_{i}}\neq\emptyset
.
Then, by
Section 5
there exists

\mathbf{u}^{\text{top}}\in\mathscr{C}^{\text{top}}

with
i\in{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{u})
.
From
Exchange
on the pair (
\mathbf{u}^{\text{top}}
,
\mathbf{n}
) at index
i
, we obtain a point
\mathbf{n}^{\prime}=\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{m}}+\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathscr{C}^{\text{top}}

for some
\ell_{m}
. If we reorder
[p]
as
(1,\ldots,i-1,i+1,\ldots,p,i)

we obtain that
\mathbf{n}^{\prime}
is smaller than
\mathbf{n}
in the lex order.
Thus, if we replace
j_{m}
by
i
at the start of the proof we obtain that
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathscr{C}
, finishing the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.15
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
be a cave such that
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathscr{C}
for some
i\in[p]
.

(i)

Assume that there exists
\mathbf{u}\in\mathscr{C}
such that
i\notin{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{u})
, then
\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathscr{C}
for some
j\in{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{u})
.

(ii)

Let
j\in[p]
with
j\neq i
be such that
\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{k}\in\mathscr{C}
for some
k\neq j
, then either
\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathscr{C}
or
\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathscr{C}
.

Proof.

Set
r=\max(\mathscr{C})
. After reordering the elements in
[p]
, we can assume
i=p
.

We begin with (i). Let
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r-1}
and
j_{1},\ldots,j_{r-1}
be the indices obtained by applying
Section 5
to
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{e}_{p}
. Then, we obtain
{\mathbf{n}}:=\mathbf{e}_{p}+\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{1}}+\dots+\mathbf{e}_{\ell_{r-1}}\in\mathscr{C}^{\text{top}}
.
Set
u=r-|\mathbf{u}|
.
Similarly, there exist
h_{1}<\cdots<h_{u}<p
such that

\mathbf{u}^{\text{top}}:=\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{e}_{h_{1}}+\cdots+\mathbf{e}_{h_{u}}\in\mathscr{C}^{\text{top}}
.
As
p\not\in{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{u}^{\text{top}})
, from
Exchange
on (
\mathbf{n}
,
\mathbf{u}^{\text{top}}
) at index
p
, there exists
j\in{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{u}^{\text{top}})

such that
{\mathbf{n}}-\mathbf{e}_{p}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathscr{C}^{\text{top}}
. By
Section 5
with
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{e}_{p}
,
\mathbf{a}^{\text{top}}={\mathbf{n}}
, and
\mathbf{b}={\mathbf{n}}-\mathbf{e}_{p}+\mathbf{e}_{j}
, it follows that
\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathscr{C}
.

We continue with (ii) following the same notations introduced above. In particular, we still use
i=p
. We can assume
k\neq p
.
If
j\in\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r-1}\}
, then from

\mathscr{C}_{\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{p}}\neq\emptyset
it follows that
\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{p}\in\mathscr{C}
by
Section 5
.
Otherwise, let
\mathbf{v}:=\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{k}
and
\mathbf{v}^{\text{top}}\in\mathscr{C}^{\text{top}}
be such that
\mathbf{v}\leqslant\mathbf{v}^{\text{top}}
(see
Section 5
). From
Exchange
on
(\mathbf{v}^{\text{top}},\,{\mathbf{n}})
at index
j
, we obtain

{\mathbf{n}}-\mathbf{e}_{\alpha}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathscr{C}^{\text{top}}\;\text{ where }\alpha\in\{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{r-1},p\}.

If

, then
\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{p}\in\mathscr{C}
by
Section 5
. Otherwise
{\mathbf{n}}-\mathbf{e}_{p}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathscr{C}^{\text{top}}
and as in (i) we conclude
\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathscr{C}
, finishing the proof.
∎

We need one last lemma before the main theorem of this section.

Lemma 5.16
.

Let
\mathscr{C}\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
be a cave.
Let
(\mathbf{u},\,\mathbf{v})\in\mathscr{C}\times\mathscr{C}
be such that
{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{u})\cap{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{v})=\emptyset
and let
i\in{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{v})
.
If
|\mathbf{u}|>1
, then either
\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathscr{C}
for some
j\in{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{u})
or
\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathscr{C}
.

Proof.

By
Section 5
it suffices to show that there exists
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}^{\text{top}}
such that
i\in{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})
and
{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{u})\cap{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{v}^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
. By
Section 5
there exists
\mathbf{u}^{\text{top}}\in\mathscr{C}^{\text{top}}
such that
\mathbf{u}\leqslant\mathbf{u}^{\text{top}}
. If
i\in{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{u}^{\text{top}})
, we can take
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}=\mathbf{u}^{\text{top}}
.
Otherwise, consider
\mathbf{v}^{\text{top}}\in\mathscr{C}^{\text{top}}
such that
\mathbf{v}\leqslant\mathbf{v}^{\text{top}}
.
By
Exchange
on
(\mathbf{v}^{\text{top}},\,\mathbf{u}^{\text{top}})
at index
i
, we obtain
\mathbf{u}^{\text{top}}-\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{i}\in\mathscr{C}^{\text{top}}
for some
j\in{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{u}^{\text{top}})
.
Moreover,
{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{u})\cap{\text{supp}}(\mathbf{u}^{\text{top}}-\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{i})\neq\emptyset
as
|\mathbf{u}|>1
.
Therefore, we can take
\mathbf{v}^{\prime}=\mathbf{u}^{\text{top}}-\mathbf{e}_{j}+\mathbf{e}_{i}

6.
Results on multiplicity-free varieties

In this section, we prove several results on multiplicity-free varieties.
These results form the core of our applications. Throughout this section, we utilize the following setup.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
be a variety over an arbitrary field
\mathbb{k}
.
We say that
X
is
multiplicity-free
if all its multidegrees are at most one, i.e.,
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)\in\{0,1\}
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(X)
. Let
{\mathbf{m}}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{p})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{p}
and

S=\mathbb{k}\left[x_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq p,0\leq j\leq m_{i}\right]

be the standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring with
\deg(x_{i,j})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
for every
i,j
.
We note that

\text{MultiProj}(S)={\mathbb{P}}:={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}.

Our approach on multiplicity-free varieties comes from the general fact that we can degenerate these varieties to a reduced union of certain multiprojective spaces (and these multiprojective spaces are determined by the multidegrees).
In algebraic terms, this says that the multigraded generic initial ideals are square-free.
This follows from an important theorem of Brion
[
9
]
.

We now introduce some notation regarding multigraded generic initial ideals.

Setup 6.2
.

Let
B_{m_{i}+1}(\mathbb{k})
be the subgroup of
\text{GL}_{m_{i}+1}(\mathbb{k})
of upper triangular invertible matrices.
The
Borel subgroup of

G:=\text{GL}_{m_{1}+1}(\mathbb{k})\times\cdots\times\text{GL}_{m_{p}+1}(\mathbb{k})
is the one given by

B:=B_{m_{1}+1}(\mathbb{k})\times\cdots\times B_{m_{p}+1}(\mathbb{k})\subset G.

We consider the natural action of
G
on
S
. Let
>
be a monomial order on
S
satisfying
x_{i,0}>x_{i,1}>\cdots>x_{i,m_{i}}
for all
i\in[p]
.

We now recall the definition of multigraded generic initial ideals; see
[
24
, §15.9]
for the single graded case.

Definition 6.3
.

Let
J\subset S
be an
S
-homogeneous ideal. If
\mathbb{k}
is infinite, we define the

multigraded generic initial ideal

{\text{gin}}_{>}(J)
of
J
with respect to
>
as the ideal
{\text{in}}_{>}(g(J))
, where
g
belongs to a Zariski dense open subset
\mathcal{U}\subset G
.

An
S
-homogeneous ideal
J\subset S
is said to be
Borel-fixed
if
g(J)=J
for all
g\in B
.
As in the single graded setting, we have that
{\text{gin}}_{>}(J)
is Borel-fixed (see
[
24
, Theorem 15.20]
).

Remark 6.4
.

The properties of Borel-fixed ideals (see
[
24
, Chapter 15]
) extend to the multigraded setting:

(i)

A Borel-fixed prime ideal in
S
is of the form

{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathbf{a}}\,:=\,\left(x_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq p\text{ and }0\leq j<a_{i}\right)\subset S

for some
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
(see
[
19
, Lemma 3.1]
).

(ii)

An
S
-homogeneous Borel-fixed ideal
J\subset S
is monomial and all of its associated primes are also Borel-fixed (see
[
19
, Lemma 3.2]
).

In the following theorem we gather some important properties of multiplicity-free varieties.

Theorem 6.5

(

Assume

The following lemma shows that for multiplicity-free varieties, Hilbert functions and polynomials agree everywhere in
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
be a multiplicity-free variety and
{\mathfrak{P}}\subset S
be the corresponding
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded prime ideal.
Then we have the equality

P_{X}(\mathbf{v})\;=\;\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[S/{\mathfrak{P}}\right]_{\mathbf{v}}\right)

for all
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.

Proof.

After possibly extending the field, we can assume
\mathbb{k}
is infinite. Choose a monomial order
>
and the corresponding generic initial ideal
J={\text{gin}}_{>}({\mathfrak{P}})\subset S
.
We have that
P_{X}({\mathbf{t}})=P_{S/J}({\mathbf{t}})
(see, e.g.,
[
24
, Theorem 15.3]
).
By
Theorem 6.5
, we know that
J
is a square-free monomial ideal.
From
Section 4
, we can compute the Hilbert polynomial of
S/J
in terms of the Hilbert polynomials of sums
of the minimal primes of
J
.
From
Section 6
(i), we know that the minimal primes of
J
are of the form
{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathbf{a}_{1}},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathbf{a}_{k}}
.
Since the sum of minimal primes

{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathbf{a}_{i_{1}}}+\cdots+{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathbf{a}_{i_{\ell}}}\;=\;{\mathfrak{p}}_{\max\{\mathbf{a}_{i_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{a}_{i_{\ell}}\}}

gives a relevant monomial prime ideal (see
Section 6
), it follows that

P_{S/({\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathbf{a}_{i_{1}}}+\cdots+{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathbf{a}_{i_{\ell}}})}(\mathbf{v})=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left({\big[S/({\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathbf{a}_{i_{1}}}+\cdots+{\mathfrak{p}}_{\mathbf{a}_{i_{\ell}}})\big]}_{\mathbf{v}}\right)

for all
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

The following lemma shows that for a multiplicity-free variety, the order of the facets of
\Delta\left({\text{gin}}_{>}({\mathfrak{P}})\right)
induced by the lex order on the multidegrees is a shelling. We note that, when considering the lex order, we can do so with respect to any ordering of the elements in
[p]
.

Lemma 6.8
.

(
\mathbb{k}
infinite
)
Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
be a multiplicity-free variety and
{\mathfrak{P}}\subset S
be the corresponding
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded prime ideal.
Let
J={\text{gin}}_{>}({\mathfrak{P}})\subset S
, and for each
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\text{MSupp}}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)
, let
F_{\mathbf{n}}
be the corresponding facet of
\Delta(J)
, i.e.,

F_{\mathbf{n}}:=\{x_{1,m_{1}-n_{1}},\ldots,x_{1,m_{1}}\}\cup\cdots\cup\{x_{p,m_{p}-n_{p}},\ldots,x_{p,m_{p}}\}.

Then, the lex order on
\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
induces a shelling on
\Delta(J)
.

Proof.

The set
\mathscr{P}:={\text{MSupp}}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
is a
polymatroid by
Theorem 2.4

(see
Section 5
).
Denote by
\prec
the lexicographical order on
\mathbb{N}^{p}
.
Fix a point
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathscr{P}
, by induction, it suffices to show that for any
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{p})\in\mathscr{P}
with
\mathbf{s}\prec\mathbf{n}
and
|F_{\mathbf{n}}\cap F_{\mathbf{s}}|<|F_{\mathbf{n}}|-1
there exists
\mathbf{v}\in\mathscr{P}
such that
\mathbf{v}\prec\mathbf{n}
and
F_{\mathbf{s}}\cap F_{\mathbf{n}}\subsetneq F_{\mathbf{v}}\cap F_{\mathbf{n}}
.
We note that,
F_{\mathbf{s}}\cap F_{\mathbf{n}}=F_{\min\{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{n}\}}
where
\min\{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{n}\}:=(\min\{s_{1},n_{1}\},\ldots,\min\{s_{p},n_{p}\})
.

Let
i\in[p]
be the smallest index such that
s_{i}\neq n_{i}
, since
\mathbf{s}\prec\mathbf{n}
we have by definition that
s_{i}<n_{i}
.
Thus,
there exists
j\in[p]
such that
i<j
,
n_{j}<s_{j}
, and
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}:=\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\mathscr{P}
.
Since
\min\{\mathbf{s}^{\prime},\mathbf{n}\}=\min\{\mathbf{s},\mathbf{n}\}+\mathbf{e}_{i}
, it follows that
F_{\mathbf{s}}\cap F_{\mathbf{n}}\subsetneq F_{\mathbf{s}^{\prime}}\cap F_{\mathbf{n}}
.
If
\mathbf{s}^{\prime}\prec\mathbf{n}

If
s_{i^{\prime}}<n_{i^{\prime}}
for some other

, then there exists

such that

and

and

,
and again the proof is finished.
Finally, if no such

exists, we must have

and

for every

It would follow that

, contradicting the hypothesis

.
∎

In the following theorem we show that if one knows the multidegrees of a multiplicity-free variety, then one can determine the whole Hilbert polynomial. Moreover, we provide an algorithmic way to do so.

\mathbb{k}
is infinite.

{\mathfrak{P}}\subset S

The next proposition includes further important properties of multiplicity-free varieties.

Proposition 6.12
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}} \times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}

[Behavior under a projection].

Lemma 7.2

Let

-graded

{\mathcal{K}}(M;{\mathbf{z}})=\sum_{\mathbf{n}}c_{\mathbf{n}}(M)\,{\mathbf{z}}^{\mathbf{n}}
of

\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial extension

of

Proof.

The
K

M

coincide because their multigraded Betti numbers are the same. We have the following equation

{\text{Hilb}}_{M^{\prime}}({\mathbf{t}})\;=\;\frac{{\mathcal{K}}(M;{\mathbf{t}})}{(1-t_{1})^{m_{1}^{\prime}+1}\cdots(1-t_{p})^{m_{p}^{\prime}+1}}.

Notice that
{\mathcal{K}}(M;{\mathbf{t}})=\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})}c_{{\mathbf{n}}}(M)(1-t_{1})^{n_{1}}\cdots(1-t_{p})^{n_{p}}

{\text{Hilb}}_{M^{\prime}}({\mathbf{t}})\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}\frac{c_{\mathbf{n}}(M)}{(1-t_{1})^{m_{1}^{\prime}+1-n_{1}}\cdots(1-t_{p})^{m_{p}^{\prime}+1-n_{p}}}.

The number of

that are nonzero is finite, and so we may choose

for all

{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}

Hence we may expand the above Hilbert series and obtain the identity below

{\text{Hilb}}_{M^{\prime}}({\mathbf{t}})\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}}c_{\mathbf{n}}(M)\left(\sum_{v_{1},\ldots,v_{p}\geq 0}\binom{v_{1}+m_{1}^{\prime}-n_{1}}{m_{1}^{\prime}-n_{1}}\cdots\binom{v_{p}+m_{p}-n_{p}}{m_{p}^{\prime}-n_{p}}t_{1}^{v_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{v_{p}}\right).

The claimed formula for

follows from this.

.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
be a multiplicity-free variety.
Consider the twisted
K
-polynomial
{\mathcal{K}}(X;{\mathbf{z}})=\sum_{\mathbf{n}}c_{\mathbf{n}}(X)\,{\mathbf{z}}^{\mathbf{n}}
of
X
.
Let
{\mathfrak{J}}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subset[p]
be a subset.
Then we have the equality

\deg_{z_{j_{1}},\ldots,z_{j_{k}}}\left({\mathcal{K}}(X;{\mathbf{z}})\right)\;=\;b_{\mathfrak{J}}(X,{\mathbb{P}}),

where
\deg_{z_{j_{1}},\ldots,z_{j_{k}}}\left({\mathcal{K}}(X;{\mathbf{z}})\right)
denotes the degree of
{\mathcal{K}}(X;{\mathbf{z}})
in terms of the variables
z_{j_{1}},\ldots,z_{j_{k}}
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathfrak{P}}\subset S
be the
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded prime ideal corresponding to
X
, and set
R=S/{\mathfrak{P}}
. By
§7.1
and
Theorem 6.5
, we have the equalities

\left[\mathcal{S}(X)\right]_{u}\;=\;\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
\sum_{j\in[p]}v_{j}=u\end{subarray}}R_{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})}\quad\text{ and }\quad\left[\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{J}}(X)\right]_{(u,v)}\;=\;\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}v_{j}=u,\;\;\sum_{j\notin{\mathfrak{J}}}v_{j}=v\end{subarray}}R_{(v_{1},\ldots,v_{p})}

for any
\emptyset\subsetneq{\mathfrak{J}}\subsetneq[p]
.

We first assume
{\mathfrak{J}}=[p]
.
Since
{\text{Hilb}}_{\mathcal{S}(X)}(t)={\text{Hilb}}_{R}(t,\ldots,t)
, we have an equality
{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{S}(X);z)={\mathcal{K}}(X;z,\ldots,z)
in terms of twisted
K
-polynomials.
From
Section 4
and
Theorem 6.5
, we know that the sign of
c_{\mathbf{n}}(R)
changes depending on the parity of
|{\mathbf{n}}|
.
The above substitution is degree preserving, and so we must have
\deg\left({\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{S}(X);z)\right)=\deg\left({\mathcal{K}}(X;{\mathbf{z}})\right)
.
Since
\mathcal{S}(X)
is Cohen-Macaulay (see
Theorem 6.5
),
[
11
, Theorem 4.4.3]
yields the necessary equality

\deg\left({\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{S}(X);z)\right)={\text{codim}}(P)+\text{reg}(\mathcal{S}(X))=b_{[p]}(X,{\mathbb{P}}).

So the case
{\mathfrak{J}}=[p]
is complete.

We now consider a nonempty subset
{\mathfrak{J}}=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}\subsetneq[p]
.
Without any loss of generality, we may assume that
{\mathfrak{J}}=\{1,\ldots,k\}
.
To simplify notation, let

T=\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{J}}(X),\quad M_{1}=-1+\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}(m_{j}+1)\quad\text{and}\quad M_{2}=-1+\sum_{j\notin{\mathfrak{J}}}(m_{j}+1).

Again, after making substitutions
t_{1}=t^{\prime},\ldots,t_{k}=t^{\prime}
and
t_{k+1}=t^{\prime\prime},\ldots,t_{p}=t^{\prime\prime}
, we get

{\text{Hilb}}_{T}(t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime})={\text{Hilb}}_{R}(t^{\prime},\ldots,t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime},\ldots,t^{\prime\prime})
, and so we obtain the following equality in terms of twisted
K
-polynomials

{\mathcal{K}}\left(T;z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime}\right)\;=\;{\mathcal{K}}\left(X;z^{\prime},\ldots,z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime},\ldots,z^{\prime\prime}\right).

As the sign of
c_{\mathbf{n}}(X)
alternates depending on
|{\mathbf{n}}|
(see
Section 4
and
Theorem 6.5
) and the above substitution is degree preserving, it follows that
\deg_{z^{\prime}}\left({\mathcal{K}}(T;z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime})\right)=\deg_{z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}}\left({\mathcal{K}}(X;{\mathbf{z}})\right)
.
Due to
§7.1
, we also have that
c_{(n_{1},n_{2})}(T)=0
whenever
n_{1}\geq M_{1}+1
or
n_{2}\geq M_{2}+1
.
After the above reductions, we need to compute
\deg_{z^{\prime}}\left({\mathcal{K}}(T;z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime})\right)
.
We also know that the Hilbert polynomial of
T
is given by

P_{T}(t_{1},t_{2})\;=\;\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{N}}c_{(n_{1},n_{2})}(T)\binom{t_{1}+M_{1}-n_{1}}{M_{1}-n_{1}}\binom{t_{2}+M_{2}-n_{2}}{M_{2}-n_{2}}

(the proof of this fact follows verbatim the proof of
§7.1
).
From this we can conclude that

-\left(M_{1}+1-\deg_{z^{\prime}}\left({\mathcal{K}}(T;z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime})\right)\right)\;=\;\max\big\{u\in\mathbb{Z}\mid P_{T}(u,v)\neq h_{T}(u,v)\;\text{ for }v\gg 0\big\},

and so the proof is complete.
∎

Finally, we are ready to state and prove the main result of this paper.

Theorem 7.9
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
be a multiplicity-free variety.
Then
{\text{supp}}\left(\mathcal{K}(X;{\mathbf{z}})\right)
is a g-polymatroid.
Furthermore, we have the specific description that
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in{\text{supp}}(\mathcal{K}(X;{\mathbf{z}}))
if and only if the following inequality holds

{\text{codim}}_{\mathfrak{J}}\left(X,{\mathbb{P}}\right)\;\leq\;\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}n_{j}\;\leq\;b_{\mathfrak{J}}\left(X,{\mathbb{P}}\right)

for any nonempty subset
{\mathfrak{J}}\subseteq[p]
.

Proof.

Translations and reflections of g-polymatroids are g-polymatroids
[
30
, Theorem 14.2.1]
, thus the first statement follows from
Theorem 6.13
and
§7.1
.
Therefore, by
[
30
, Theorem 14.2.8]
, we have that
{\text{supp}}\left(\mathcal{K}(X;{\mathbf{z}})\right)
equals to the set of the integer points of the polytope

\mathscr{G}:=\left\{\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{p})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}~\mid~c({\mathfrak{J}})\leq\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}y_{j}\leq b({\mathfrak{J}}),\;\text{ for all }{\mathfrak{J}}\subseteq[p]\right\},

where
c:2^{[p]}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z},\,c({\mathfrak{J}}):=\min\{\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}y_{j}\mid\mathbf{y}\in{\text{supp}}\left(\mathcal{K}(X;{\mathbf{z}})\right)\}
and
b:2^{[p]}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z},\,b({\mathfrak{J}}):=\max\{\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}y_{j}\mid\mathbf{y}\in{\text{supp}}\left(\mathcal{K}(X;{\mathbf{z}})\right)\}.

By
[
16
, Proposition 3.1]
and
§7.1
, we have
c({\mathfrak{J}})={\text{codim}}_{\mathfrak{J}}\left(X,{\mathbb{P}}\right)
.
Finally, the maximum
b({\mathfrak{J}})
is by definition the degree of the twisted
K
-polynomial in the variables
\{z_{j}\mid j\in{\mathfrak{J}}\}
. Then, by
§7.1
, the proof is complete.
∎

Möbius function

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
be a multiplicity-free variety and set
\mathscr{P}:=\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)
which, by
Theorem 2.4
, is a polymatroid.
Consider

\mathscr{P}_{\leq}:=\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}~\mid~\mathbf{u}\leqslant\mathbf{v}\text{ for some }\mathbf{v}\in\mathscr{P}\},

and the poset

\Gamma:=\mathscr{P}_{\leqslant}\cup\{\hat{1}\}
given by the componentwise order
\leq

and such that
\hat{1}
is larger than any element in
\mathscr{P}_{\leqslant}
.
The minimum element of this set is
\mathbf{0}
. The
meet
and
join
of
\Gamma
are defined as

\mathbf{u}\wedge\mathbf{v}:=\min\{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\},\quad\text{and,}\quad\mathbf{u}\vee\mathbf{v}:=\begin{cases}\max\{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\}&\text{if }\max\{\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\}\in\mathscr{P}_{\leqslant}\\
\hat{1}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The
Möbius function
of
\Gamma
is the function
\mu_{\Gamma}:\Gamma\times\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}
uniquely determined by the conditions
\mu_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})=0
if
\mathbf{u}\not\leq\mathbf{v}
,
\mu_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u})=1
for every
\mathbf{u}\in\Gamma
, and

\sum_{\mathbf{u}\leq\mathbf{w}\leq\mathbf{v}}\mu_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{w})=0,\text{ for every pair }\mathbf{u}<\mathbf{v}.

We now reframe
Section 6
(iii) in the context of posets.

Theorem 7.11
.

For any
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathscr{P}_{\leqslant}
, we have the equality
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)=-\mu_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{n},\hat{1}).

Proof.

By
Section 6
(iii), we have that
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)
is determined by the same recurrence relation as the Möbius function.
We correct the formula with a sign since we are assuming
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)=1
for every
{\mathbf{n}}\in\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)
, whereas
\mu({\mathbf{n}},\hat{1})=-1
by definition.
∎

Notice that
\Gamma
is a
semimodular lattice

[
56
, §3.3]
.
As an application of
Theorem 7.11
we obtain another proof of the sign alternation in
Section 4
:
by
[
56
, Proposition 3.10.1]
the Möbius function of a semimodular lattice, in particular of
\Gamma
, alternates in sign.

Let
\mathscr{V}:=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{p})-\mathscr{P}_{\leqslant}
and consider the poset
\Psi=\mathscr{V}\cup\{\hat{0}\}
with componentwise order and
\hat{0}

As an application of

[

in the case of multiprojective varieties.

7.3.
Grothendieck polynomials

Given an integer
p\geq 1
and
j\in[p-1]
, consider the following operators: for any polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Z}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{p}]
, we set

\partial_{j}(f):=\frac{f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{p})-f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{j-1},z_{j+1},z_{j},z_{j+2},\ldots,z_{p})}{z_{j}-z_{j+1}},

and

\overline{\partial}_{j}(f):=\partial_{j}((1-z_{j+1})f).

The
Grothendieck polynomial

\mathfrak{G}_{w}\in\mathbb{Z}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{p}]
of a permutation
w\in S_{p}
was originally defined by Lascoux and Schützenberger in

[
43
]
.
These polynomials can be computed inductively as: if
w_{0}=(p,p-1,\ldots,1)\in S_{p}
is the longest permutation, then

\mathfrak{G}_{w_{0}}=z_{1}^{p-1}z_{2}^{p-2}\cdots z_{p-1}
.
For any
w\neq w_{0}
and
j\in[p-1]
with
w(j)<w(j+1)
, the polynomial
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
is defined as
\overline{\partial}_{j}\mathfrak{G}_{s_{j}w}
,
where
s_{j}=(j,j+1)
is the transposition of the positions
j
-th and
(j+1)
-th.
The sum of the terms of lowest degree of
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
is the
Schubert polynomial
of
w
, and it is denoted by
\mathfrak{S}_{w}\in\mathbb{Z}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{p}]
.

In
[
38
]
, Knutson and Miller showed that
\mathfrak{G}_{w}
is equal to
\mathcal{K}(\overline{X}_{w};{\mathbf{z}})
, where
\overline{X}_{w}
is the
matrix Schubert variety
of
w\in S_{p}
.
If
\overline{X}_{w}
is multiplicity-free, the corresponding
\mathfrak{S}_{w}
is said to be a
zero-one Schubert polynomial

[
28
]
.
Thus,
Theorem 7.9
implies the following theorem.

Theorem 7.13
.

Let
w\in S_{p}
be such that
\mathfrak{S}_{w}
is a zero-one Schubert polynomial, then
{\text{supp}}(\mathfrak{G}_{w})
is a g-polymatroid.

Theorem 7.13
settles a special case of
[
48
, Conjecture 5.5]
that every Grothendieck polynomial has SNP.

[

7.4.
Linear polymatroids and beyond

Definition 7.14
.

Let
V_{1},\dots,V_{p}
be
\mathbb{k}
-subspaces of a finite dimensional
\mathbb{k}
-vector space
V
.
We define a polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
as

\mathbb{N}^{p}\cap\left\{(y_{1},\dots,y_{p})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\;\,\mid\;\,\sum_{j\in{\mathfrak{J}}}y_{j}\leq\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathfrak{J}}}V_{j}\right)\text{ for all ${\mathfrak{J}}\subset[p]$},\;\,\sum_{i=1}^{p}y_{i}=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{p}V_{i}\right)\right\}.

A polymatroid that arises in this way is called
linear
.

The following proposition is an extension of
[
16
, Proposition 5.4]
. Here,
we show that for a given linear polymatroid, there exists a multiplicity-free variety that realizes it as the multidegree support.

Proposition 7.15
.

Given a linear
polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
on
[p]
, there is a multiplicity-free variety
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
such that
\mathscr{P}=\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)
.

Proof.

The proof follows similarly to
[
16
, Proposition 5.4]
.
Choose a representation of
\mathscr{P}
given by a
\mathbb{k}
-vector space
V
and
\mathbb{k}
-vector subspaces
V_{1},\ldots,V_{p}
.
Let
S
be the polynomial ring
S=\text{Sym}(V)[x_{0}]=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{q}]
, where
q=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}(V)
.
By using the isomorphism
[S]_{1}\cong V\oplus\mathbb{k}
, we identify each
V_{i}
with a
\mathbb{k}
-subspace
U_{i}
of
[S]_{1}
.
For each
1\leq i\leq p
, let
\{x_{i,1},x_{i,2},\ldots,x_{i,r_{i}}\}\subset[S]_{1}
be a basis of the
\mathbb{k}
-vector space
U_{i}
.
Let
T
be the
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring

T:=\mathbb{k}\left[y_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq p,\,0\leq j\leq r_{i},\,\deg(y_{i,j})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}\right].

Induce an
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-grading on
S[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
given by
\deg(t_{i})={\mathbf{e}}_{i}
and
\deg(x_{j})=\mathbf{0}
.
Consider the
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra homomorphism

\varphi=T\rightarrow S[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}],\qquad\begin{array}[]{ll}y_{i,0}\mapsto x_{0}t_{i}&\text{ for }\;1\leq i\leq p\\
y_{i,j}\mapsto x_{i,j}t_{i}&\text{ for }\;1\leq i\leq p,\,1\leq j\leq r_{i}.\end{array}

Note that
{\mathfrak{P}}:=\text{Ker}(\varphi)\subset T
is an
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded prime ideal.
Set
R:=T/{\mathfrak{P}}
and
X:=\text{MultiProj}(R)
.
Notice that
\varphi
yields a rational map

\Phi:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{q}\,\dashrightarrow\,{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r_{p}},

and that
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r_{p}}
equals the closure of the image of
\Phi
.
By either
[
44
, Theorem 1.1]
or
[
23
, Theorem 3.9]
, we obtain that
X
is a multiplicity-free variety.

It remains to show the equality
\mathscr{P}=\text{MSupp}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)
.
For that we utilize the description given in
Theorem 2.4
.
By construction, for each
{\mathfrak{J}}\subseteq[p]
, we obtain the isomorphism

R_{({\mathfrak{J}})}\;\cong\;\mathbb{k}\left[x_{i,j}t_{i}\mid i\in{\mathfrak{J}},\,1\leq j\leq r_{i}\right]\left[x_{0}t_{i}\mid i\in{\mathfrak{J}}\right]\;\subset\;S[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}];

thus, it is clear that

\displaystyle\dim\left(R_{({\mathfrak{J}})}\right)

\displaystyle={\rm trdeg}_{\mathbb{k}}\big(\mathbb{k}\left[x_{i,j}t_{i}\mid i\in{\mathfrak{J}},\,1\leq j\leq r_{i}\right]\left[x_{0}t_{i}\mid i\in{\mathfrak{J}}\right]\big)

\displaystyle={\rm trdeg}_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\mathbb{k}\left[\frac{x_{i,j}}{x_{0}}\mid i\in{\mathfrak{J}},\,1\leq j\leq r_{i}\right]\Big[t_{i}\mid i\in{\mathfrak{J}}\Big]\right)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathfrak{J}}}U_{i}\right)+\lvert{\mathfrak{J}}\rvert=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathfrak{J}}}V_{i}\right)+\lvert{\mathfrak{J}}\rvert.

Therefore, we have that
\dim\left(\Pi_{{\mathfrak{J}}}(X)\right)=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\sum_{i\in{\mathfrak{J}}}V_{i}\right)
, and so the result follows from
Theorem 2.4
.
∎

From our methods we obtain the following surprising result for linear polymatroids.

Setup 7.16
.

Given a polymatroid
\mathscr{P}\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
, we define the set
\mathscr{P}_{\leq}\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
and the poset
\Gamma:=\Gamma(\mathscr{P})
following the same strategy in
§7.2
. As before,
\hat{1}
denotes the largest element in
\Gamma
. Moreover, we denote by
\hat{0}
the smallest element of
\Gamma
and by

\mu\text{-supp}(\mathscr{P}):=\left\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathscr{P}_{\leq}~\mid~\mu_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{u},\hat{1})\neq 0\right\}
its
Möbius support
.

Theorem 7.17
.

Assume
§7.4
.
If
\mathscr{P}
is linear, then the Möbius support

\mu\text{\rm-supp}(\mathscr{P})

is a g-polymatroid.

Proof.

By
§7.4
, there exists a multiplicity-free variety
X
such that
{\text{MSupp}}_{\mathbb{P}}(X)=\mathscr{P}
.
Therefore, the result follows by
Theorem 7.11
and
Theorem 6.13
.
∎

We propose the following conjecture whose validity would extend
Theorem 7.17
to all polymatroids.

Conjecture 7.18
.

Assume
§7.4
. For any
(
base
)
polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
, the Möbius support
\mu\text{\rm-supp}(\mathscr{P})
is a g-polymatroid.

We now show that
§7.4
is also true for all matroids. First, we briefly review some notions.
A
matroid

\mathscr{M}
is a polymatroid such that
[\mathbf{u}]_{i}\in\{0,1\}
for every
\mathbf{u}\in\mathscr{M}
and
i\in[p]
.
If we interpret the elements of a matroid
\mathscr{M}
as subsets of
[p]
, then
\mathscr{M}_{\leq}
can be seen as a simplicial complex
\Delta(\mathscr{M})
with vertex set
[p]
.
The poset
\Gamma(\mathscr{M})
is the face poset of the complex (with a greatest element added).
An element
i\in[p]
is called a
coloop
if it is contained in every facet of
\Delta(\mathscr{M})
.
Given any
\mathfrak{I}\in\mathscr{M}_{\leq}
, we have that
\text{link}_{\mathfrak{I}}(\Delta(\mathscr{M}))
induces a matroid
\mathscr{M}/\mathfrak{I}
on the set
[p]\setminus\mathfrak{I}
, called the
contraction
of
\mathfrak{I}
.

Theorem 7.19
.

§7.4
holds for matroids.

Proof.

By
[
56
, Proposition 3.8.8]
, the value of
\mu_{\Gamma}(\hat{0},\hat{1})
equals the (reduced) Euler characteristic of the complex
\Delta(\mathscr{M})
.
It is known that the Euler characteristic of the independence complex of a matroid is zero if and only if it has a coloop:
this follows from
[
6
, Corollary 7.2.4]
and the determination of the restrictions sets in
[
6
, Equation 7.9]
.
More specifically, if
\mathscr{M}
is coloopless, then in the last lexicographic basis every element can be exchanged (necessarily by a smaller index), hence they are all internally passive.

Next, we prove that if
\mathscr{M}
is coloopless, then so are all of its contractions
\mathscr{M}/\mathfrak{I}
.
Assume by contradiction that there exists an element
i\in[p]\setminus\mathfrak{I}
such that it is contained in every basis of
\mathscr{M}
containing
\mathfrak{I}
.
Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a basis of
\mathscr{M}
containing
\mathfrak{I}
and
i
.
Since
\mathscr{M}
is coloopless, there exists a basis
\mathfrak{B}^{\prime}
not including
i
. Now, performing exchange between
\mathfrak{B}
and
\mathfrak{B^{\prime}}
at index
i
, we can find a basis
\mathfrak{B}^{\prime\prime}
containing
\mathfrak{I}
but not
i
, which is a contradiction. Set
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{I}}:=\Gamma\left(\mathscr{M}/\mathfrak{I}\right)
.
Since
\mu_{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{I}}}(\hat{0},\hat{1})=\mu_{\Gamma}(\mathfrak{I},\hat{1})
, we conclude that if
\mathscr{M}
is coloopless then
\mu\text{-supp}(\mathscr{M})=\mathscr{M}_{\leq}

and so, in particular, it is a g-polymatroid.

More generally, if there is a nonempty subset
\mathfrak{A}\subset[p]
of coloops, then by the same reasoning we have that
\mu(\mathfrak{I},\hat{1})=0
for every
\mathfrak{I}\in\mathscr{M}_{\leq}
that does not contain
\mathfrak{A}
.
We obtain that
\mu_{\Gamma}(\mathfrak{I},\hat{1})\neq 0
if and only if
\mathfrak{I}
is the union of an independent set of the contraction
\mathscr{M}/\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{A}
.
Hence, as a point configuration the set
\mu\text{-supp}(\mathscr{M})
is equal to the set of indicator vectors of the independence sets of
\mathscr{M}/\mathfrak{A}
each translated by the vector
\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{A}}=\sum_{i\in\mathfrak{A}}\mathbf{e}_{i}
.
∎

Remark 7.20
.

Theorem 7.19
shows that the Möbius support of matroids always has exactly one minimum element.
This does not necessarily hold for an arbitrary polymatroid as seen in our
running example
§2.2
.

In
[
33
, Conjecture 22]
, it was conjectured that the normalization of the homogeneous Grothendieck polynomial is Lorentzian.
Motivated by this, we wonder about the Lorentzian property of the following polynomial: for any polymatroid
\mathscr{P}
, we consider the polynomial

g_{\mathscr{P}}(w_{0},w_{1},\ldots,w_{p})\;:=\;\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathscr{P}_{\leq}}-\mu_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{n},\hat{1})(-w_{0})^{r(\mathscr{P})-|\mathbf{n}|}{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}[w_{0},\ldots,w_{p}].

The sign in
-w_{0}
is there to ensure that the coefficients are always nonnegative, since
\mu_{\Gamma}
alternates in sign.
Via a similar construction, the proof of Mason’s conjecture in
[
7
, Theorem 4.14]
proceeds by associating to each matroid
\mathscr{M}
the polynomial

f_{\mathscr{M}}(w_{0},w_{1},\ldots,w_{p})=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathscr{M}_{\leq}}w_{0}^{r(\mathscr{M})-|\mathbf{n}|}{\mathbf{w}}^{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{Z}[w_{0},\ldots,w_{p}]
and proving that
f_{\mathscr{M}}
is Lorentzian.
We consider the normalization operator
N:\mathbb{Z}[w_{0},\ldots,w_{p}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[w_{0},\ldots,w_{p}]
given by
N(w_{0}^{a_{0}}\cdots w_{p}^{a_{p}})=\frac{w_{0}^{a_{0}}\cdots w_{p}^{a_{p}}}{a_{0}!\cdots a_{p}!}
.
Since the support of a Lorentzian polynomial is a polymatroid, in light of
§7.4
, it is natural to ask the following:

Question 7.21
.

Is
N(g_{\mathscr{P}})
Lorentzian?

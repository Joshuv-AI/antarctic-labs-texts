LLT Polynomials and Hecke Algebra Traces
Alejandro H. Morales, Mark A. Skandera Jiayuan Wang

Abstract
We show that coefficients in unicellular LLT polynomials are evaluations of Hecke algebra traces at Kazhdan–Lusztig basis elements. We express these in terms of traditional trace bases, induction, and Kazhdan–Lusztig R R -polynomials.



1
Introduction

The study of proper colorings of
a graph
G
is a fundamental topic in discrete mathematics. Stanley
[
10
]
defined the chromatic symmetric function
X_{G,q}
which is a symmetric function generalization of the chromatic polynomial. This function was generalized by Shareshian and Wachs
[
9
]
: for a graph
G=(V,E)
let

X_{G,q}:=\sum_{\kappa}q^{\mathrm{asc}(\kappa)}x_{\kappa(1)}x_{\kappa(2)}\cdots,

where the sum is over all proper
colorings
\kappa:V(G)\to\mathbb{N}
of
G
and
\mathrm{asc}(\kappa)
denotes the number of ascents of
\kappa
, pairs
(i,j)
with
i<j
such that
\kappa(i)<\kappa(j)
.
When
G=\mathrm{inc}(P)
is an incomparability graph of a unit interval order
P
,
X_{G,q}
is a symmetric function. There are important positivity conjectures about
X_{\mathrm{inc}(P),q}
like the
e
-positivity conjecture of Stanley–Stembridge–Shareshian–Wachs.

In another context, the functions
X_{\mathrm{inc}(P),q}
appeared in the
study of the space of
diagonal harmonics
. Let
\mathrm{LLT}_{\mathrm{inc}(P),q}:=\sum_{\kappa}q^{\textup{asc}(\kappa)}x_{\kappa(1)}x_{\kappa(2)}\cdots
, where the sum is over arbitrary vertex
colorings of
\mathrm{inc}(P)
. This is also a symmetric function called a

unicellular LLT polynomial
, a special case of a family of symmetric functions introduced by
Lascoux–Leclerc–Thibon in 1997 in a different context. These functions

\mathrm{LLT}_{\mathrm{inc}(P),q}
appear in the
Shuffle conjecture
of diagonal
harmonics
[
7
]
proved by
Carlsson–Mellit
[
3
]
. In their proof of the shuffle conjecture, they show that both these symmetric functions are related by a
plethystic substitution
:

X_{\mathrm{inc}(P),q}[X]=(q-1)^{-n}\mathrm{LLT}_{\mathrm{inc}(P),q}[(q-1)X],

where
n
is the size of
P
. From work of Grojnowski and Haiman
[
6
]
,
\mathrm{LLT}_{\mathrm{inc}(P)}
are Schur positive and it is an open question to find a combinatorial interpretation for this expansion.

An important basis of the Hecke algebra
H_{n}(q)
is the (modified, signless)
Kazhdan–Lusztig basis
defined by

\widetilde{C}_{w}(q):=q^{\frac{\ell(w)}{2}}C^{\prime}_{w}(q)=\sum_{u\leq v}P_{u,w}(q)T_{w},

where
\{T_{w}\mid w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\}
is the natural basis of
H_{n}(q)
,
P_{v,w}(q)
are the
Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials
and
\leq
denotes the Bruhat order of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
It is known
[
4
]
that the various expansions of the chromatic quasi-symmetric function
X_{\mathrm{inc}(P),q}
can be viewed as evaluations of traces at
\{\widetilde{C}_{w}(q)\mid w\text{ avoiding }312\}
,
when
P=P(w)
is a unit interval order corresponding to
w
,

X_{\mathrm{inc}(P(w)),q}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\epsilon_{q}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{C}_{w}(q))m_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\eta_{q}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{C}_{w}(q))f_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\chi_{q}^{\lambda^{\negthickspace\top\negthickspace}}(\widetilde{C}_{w}(q))s_{\lambda}=\cdots,

where
\epsilon_{q}^{\lambda}
,
\eta_{q}^{\lambda}
,
\chi_{q}^{\lambda^{\negthickspace\top\negthickspace}}
are induced sign, induced trivial, and irreducible characters of
H_{n}(q)
. In this context, the
e
-positivity conjecture of
X_{\mathrm{inc}(P),q}
is part of a more general conjecture of Haiman
[
8
]
for symmetric functions associated to
\widetilde{C}_{w}(q)
for any
w
in the context of immanants. In
[
2
, §11.3]
, Abreu and Nigro used the plethystic relation to define analogs of unicellular LLT polynomials for all permutations.

The coefficients of various expansions of LLT polynomials can also be viewed as evaluations of traces at
\{\widetilde{C}_{w}(q)\mid w\text{ avoiding }312\}
.

\mathrm{LLT}_{\mathrm{inc}(P),q}=\sum_{\lambda}\epsilon^{\lambda}_{q,LLT}(\widetilde{C}_{w}(q))m_{\lambda},

where
\epsilon^{\lambda}_{q,LLT}(\widetilde{C}_{w}(q))
is a certain LLT-analog of the trace
\epsilon^{\lambda}_{q}
.

We describe similar analogs of induced trivial characters
\eta^{\lambda}_{q,\mathrm{LLT}}

and power sum traces
\psi^{\lambda}_{q,\mathrm{LLT}}
. The evaluations at
\widetilde{C}_{w}(q)
were known as expansions of the LLT but now we obtain evaluations at the natural basis
T_{w}
, which were not known before.

We also give change of basis equations between
\psi^{n}_{q,\mathrm{LLT}}
,
\epsilon^{n}_{q,\mathrm{LLT}}
, and
\eta^{n}_{q,\mathrm{LLT}}
and known traces that resemble the Cauchy identity of symmetric functions after a principal specialization.

Background

Symmetric functions.
We mostly use the notation from
[
11
, Ch. 7]
. We denote by
\Lambda_{n}
the ring of symmetric functions of degree
n
and
m_{\lambda}
,
e_{\lambda}
,
h_{\lambda}
,
p_{\lambda}
,
s_{\lambda}
,
f_{\lambda}
denote the
monomial
,
elementary
,
complete
,
power sum
,
Schur
, and
forgotten
symmetric functions. Also
\omega
denotes the standard involution in
\Lambda_{n}
.

Hecke algebra and traces.
The Hecke algebra
H_{n}(q)
is a noncommutative

\mathbb{Z}[q^{\frac{1}{2}},q^{\negthinspace\bar{\,}\frac{1}{2}}]
-algebra
generated by
natural generators

\{T_{s_{i}}\,|\,1\leq i\leq n-1\}

subject to the relations

\displaystyle T_{s_{i}}^{2}

\displaystyle=(q-1)T_{s_{i}}+q,

\displaystyle\text{for }i=1,\dotsc,n-1,

\displaystyle T_{s_{i}}T_{s_{j}}T_{s_{i}}

\displaystyle=T_{s_{j}}T_{s_{i}}T_{s_{j}},

\displaystyle\text{if }|i-j|=1,

\displaystyle T_{s_{i}}T_{s_{j}}

\displaystyle=T_{s_{j}}T_{s_{i}},

\displaystyle\text{if }|i-j|\geq 2.

Specializing
H_{n}(q)
at
q^{\frac{1}{2}}=1
, we obtain the classical group algebra
\mathbb{Z}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
of the symmetric group.

Let
\mathcal{T}(H_{n}(q))
be the
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\frac{1}{2}},q^{\negthinspace\bar{\,}\frac{1}{2}}]
-module of

H_{n}(q)
-
traces
, linear functionals

\theta_{q}:H_{n}(q)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[q^{\frac{1}{2}},q^{\negthinspace\bar{\,}\frac{1}{2}}]
satisfying

\theta_{q}(DD^{\prime})=\theta_{q}(D^{\prime}D)
for all
D,D^{\prime}\in H_{n}(q)
.
For any trace
\theta_{q}:T_{w}\mapsto a(q)
in
\mathcal{T}(H_{n}(q))
,
the
q^{\frac{1}{2}}=1
specialization
\theta:w\mapsto a(1)
belongs to the space
\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{S}_{n}):=\mathcal{T}(H_{n}(1))
of
\mathbb{Z}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
-traces
from
\mathbb{Z}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
(
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-class functions). Like the
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\Lambda_{n}
of homogeneous degree-
n
symmetric functions, the trace spaces
\mathcal{T}(H_{n}(q))
and
\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{S}_{n})
have
dimension equal to the number of
integer partitions
of
n
,
the weakly decreasing positive integer sequences

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{r})
satisfying

\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{r}=n
.

It can be useful to record trace evaluations
in a symmetric generating function. In particular, for
D\in\mathbb{Q}(q)\otimes H_{n}(q)
,
we record induced sign character evaluations by defining

Y_{q}(D):=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\epsilon_{q}^{\lambda}(D)m_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{Q}(q)\otimes\Lambda_{n}.

(2.1)

This symmetric generating function in fact gives us all of the standard trace evaluations.

Proposition 2.1
.

The symmetric function
Y_{q}(D)
is equal to

\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\eta_{q}^{\lambda}(D)f_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\frac{sgn(\lambda)\psi_{q}^{\lambda}(D)}{z_{\lambda}}p_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\chi_{q}^{\lambda^{\negthickspace\top\negthickspace}}(D)s_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\phi_{q}^{\lambda}(D)e_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\gamma_{q}^{\lambda}(D)h_{\lambda},

where
sgn(\lambda):=(-1)^{n-\ell(\lambda)}
; equivalently,
\omega Y_{q}(D)
is equal to

\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\epsilon_{q}^{\lambda}(D)f_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\eta_{q}^{\lambda}(D)m_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\frac{\psi_{q}^{\lambda}(D)}{z_{\lambda}}p_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\chi_{q}^{\lambda}(D)s_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\phi_{q}^{\lambda}(D)h_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\negthinspace\gamma_{q}^{\lambda}(D)e_{\lambda}.\negthickspace\negthickspace

Quantum matrix bialgebra and immanants.

An important computational tool in the evaluation of
H_{n}(q)
-traces is the
quantum matrix bialgebra

\mathcal{A}_{n}(q)
, the noncommutative
ring generated as a
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\frac{1}{2}},q^{\negthinspace\bar{\,}\frac{1}{2}}]
-algebra by the
n^{2}
variables

t=(t_{i,j})_{i,j\in[n]}
subject to relations

\displaystyle t_{i,\ell}t_{i,k}

\displaystyle=\smash{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}t_{i,k}t_{i,\ell},

\displaystyle\qquad t_{j,k}t_{i,\ell}

\displaystyle=t_{i,\ell}t_{j,k}

(2.2)

\displaystyle t_{j,k}t_{i,k}

\displaystyle=\smash{q^{\frac{1}{2}}}t_{i,k}t_{j,k}

\displaystyle\qquad t_{j,\ell}t_{i,k}

\displaystyle=t_{i,k}t_{j,\ell}+\smash{(q^{\frac{1}{2}}-q^{\negthinspace\bar{\,}\frac{1}{2}})}t_{i,\ell}t_{j,k},

for all indices
1\leq i<j\leq n
and
1\leq k<\ell\leq n
.
As a
\smash{\mathbb{Z}[q^{\frac{1}{2}},q^{\negthinspace\bar{\,}\frac{1}{2}}]}
-module,
\mathcal{A}_{n}(q)
has a natural basis of monomials

t_{\ell_{1},m_{1}}\cdots t_{\ell_{r},m_{r}}
in which index pairs
appear in lexicographic order. The relations (
2.2
)
allow one to express other monomials in terms of this natural basis.

To state immanant generating functions for

H_{n}(q)
-traces,
it will be convenient to express monomials in
\mathcal{A}_{n}(q)
as follows.
Given
u=u_{1}\cdots u_{n}
,
v=v_{1}\cdots v_{n}\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, define

t^{u,v}:=t_{u_{1},v_{1}}\cdots t_{u_{n},v_{n}}.

For any linear function
\theta_{q}:H_{n}(q)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}[q^{\frac{1}{2}},q^{\negthinspace\bar{\,}\frac{1}{2}}]
,
define the
\theta_{q}
-immanant in
\mathcal{A}_{n}(q)
to be

\mathrm{Imm}_{\theta_{q}}(t)=\sum_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}q^{\negthinspace\bar{\,}\frac{\ell(w)}{2}}\theta_{q}(T_{w})t^{e,w}.

Proposition 2.2
.

Given Hecke algebra traces

\theta_{1}\in\mathcal{T}(H_{k}(q)),\quad\theta_{2}\in\mathcal{T}(H_{n-k}(q)),\quad\theta=(\theta_{1}\otimes\theta_{2})\big\uparrow\negthickspace\negthickspace\phantom{\uparrow}_{H_{k}(q)\times H_{n-k}(q)}^{H_{n}(q)}\in\mathcal{T}(H_{n}(q)),

we have

\mathrm{Imm}_{\theta}(t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\text{ where }|I|=k\end{subarray}}\,\mathrm{Imm}_{\theta_{1}}(t_{I,I})\mathrm{Imm}_{\theta_{2}}(t_{\overline{I},\overline{I}}).

Since Hecke algebra traces are determined by the values on minimum length representatives (see
[
5
, Cor. 8.2.6]
), then the following result will be useful.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
be of minimum length in its conjugacy class, then

w
avoids the patterns
3412
and
4231
.
Furthermore, each

v\leq w
also avoids the patterns
3412
and
4231
,
and also is of minimum length in its conjugacy class.

Plethystically defined characters

Suppose that a certain plethystic substitution transforms
symmetric functions written
\{Y_{q}(D)\,|\,D\in H_{n}(q)\}

into symmetric functions
\{Z_{q}(D)\,|\,D\in H_{n}(q)\}
, i.e.

Z_{q}(D):=r(q)Y_{q}(D)[s(q)X]

(3.1)

for some rational functions
r(q)
and
s(q)
.
This substitution yields a transformation of
H_{n}(q)
traces

\theta_{q}\mapsto\theta_{q,Z}

as well. Namely, we define
\epsilon_{q,Z}^{\lambda}

to be the
H_{n}(q)
-trace that maps
D
to the coefficient of
m_{\lambda}

in the monomial expansion of
Z_{q}(D)
,

Z_{q}(D)=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\epsilon_{q,Z}^{\lambda}(D)m_{\lambda}.

(3.2)

Then we extend linearly over
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\frac{1}{2}},q^{\negthinspace\bar{\,}\frac{1}{2}}]
, mapping

\theta_{q}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}b_{\lambda}\epsilon_{q}^{\lambda}\quad\mapsto\quad\theta_{q,Z}:=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}b_{\lambda}\epsilon_{q,Z}^{\lambda}.

(3.3)

Observation 3.1
.

The symmetric function
Z_{q}(D)
is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\eta_{q,Z}^{\lambda}(D)f_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\frac{sgn(\lambda)\psi_{q,Z}^{\lambda}(D)}{z_{\lambda}}p_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\chi_{q,Z}^{\lambda^{\negthickspace\top\negthickspace}}(D)s_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\phi_{q,Z}^{\lambda}(D)e_{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\gamma_{q,Z}^{\lambda}(D)h_{\lambda}.

Proposition 3.2
.

For any plethystically defined map
Y\mapsto Z

(
3.1
)
of symmetric functions,
if
\theta_{q}
is a trace function, then so is
\theta_{q,Z}
.

Furthermore by (
3.3
),
the change of basis matrix which relates two symmetric function bases (and necessarily the traces which correspond by the Frobenius map), also relates the
Z
-analogs of those traces.

For example when
Z=\mathrm{LLT}_{\mathrm{inc}(P),q}
and

avoiding the patterns

\epsilon_{q,\mathrm{LLT}}^{n}(\widetilde{C}_{w}(q))=1.

(3.4)

It is possible to

LLT analogs of power sum traces
The LLT analogs of the power sum trace can be expressed simply in terms of the ordinary power sum trace.

Proposition 3.3
.

We have

\psi_{q,\mathrm{LLT}}^{\lambda}=(q-1)^{n}\prod_{i}\frac{1}{q^{\lambda_{i}}-1}\cdot\psi_{q}^{\lambda}.

Proof.

Omitted.

LLT analogs of induced sign characters and induced trivial characters

The evaluations of
\epsilon_{q,\mathrm{LLT}}^{\lambda}
and
\eta_{q,\mathrm{LLT}}^{\lambda}

\{\widetilde{C}_{w}(q)\mid w\text{ avoiding }312\}

Proposition 3.4
.

Fix
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
avoiding the pattern
312

P=P(w)

\lambda\vdash n

\epsilon_{q,\mathrm{LLT}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{C}_{w}(q))=\sum_{U}q^{\textsc{inv}_{\negthinspace P}(U)},

U

\lambda^{\negthickspace\top\negthickspace}

\eta_{q,\mathrm{LLT}}^{\lambda}(\widetilde{C}_{w}(q))=\sum_{U}q^{\textsc{inv}_{\negthinspace P}((U_{1}\circ\cdots\circ U_{r})^{R})},

where the sum is over all row-strict Young tableaux
U

of shape
\lambda
and
(U_{1}\circ\cdots\circ U_{r})^{R}
is the reversal of the concatenation of rows in
U
.

Proof.

Omitted.

We can express
\epsilon_{q,\mathrm{LLT}}^{n}
and
\eta_{q,\mathrm{LLT}}^{n}
in terms of ordinary
H_{n}(q)
-characters and principal specialization of symmetric functions.

Corollary 3.7
.

\frac{\epsilon_{q,\mathrm{LLT}}^{n}}{(1-q)^{n}}\,=\,\sum_{\lambda}\frac{1}{z_{\lambda}}\prod_{i}\frac{1}{1-q^{\lambda_{i}}}\psi_{q}^{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{q^{b(\lambda)}}{\prod_{u\in\lambda}(1-q^{h(u)})}\chi_{q}^{\lambda}\\
\,=\,\sum_{\lambda}\prod_{i}\frac{1}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\cdots(1-q^{\lambda_{i}})}\phi_{q}^{\lambda}=\sum_{\lambda}\prod_{i}\frac{q^{\binom{\lambda_{i}}{2}}}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\cdots(1-q^{\lambda_{i}})}\gamma_{q}^{\lambda},

and

\frac{\eta_{q,\mathrm{LLT}}^{n}}{(1-q)^{n}}\,=\,\sum_{\lambda}\frac{\mathrm{sgn}(\lambda)}{z_{\lambda}}\prod_{i}\frac{1}{1-q^{\lambda_{i}}}\psi_{q}^{\lambda}\,=\,\sum_{\lambda}\frac{q^{b(\lambda^{\prime})}}{\prod_{u\in\lambda^{\prime}}(1-q^{h(u)})}\chi_{q}^{\lambda},\\
\,=\,\sum_{\lambda}\prod_{i}\frac{q^{\binom{\lambda_{i}}{2}}}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\cdots(1-q^{\lambda_{i}})}\phi_{q}^{\lambda}\,=\,\sum_{\lambda}\prod_{i}\frac{1}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\cdots(1-q^{\lambda_{i}})}\gamma_{q}^{\lambda},

where
h(u)
is the hook-length of
u
and
b(\lambda)=\sum_{i}(i-1)\cdot\lambda_{i}=\sum_{i}\binom{\lambda^{\prime}_{i}}{2}
.

Proof.

Omitted.
∎

A remark on cohomology of nilpotent Lie algebras
Grigory Papayanov

Abstract




A remark on cohomology of nilpotent Lie algebras

Grigory Papayanov

Abstract

We prove that the cohomology algebra of a
conilpotent Lie coalgebra is generated in
degree 1 as an
A_{\infty}
-algebra. By dualizing,
the same is true about cohomology of finite dimensional nilpotent
Lie algebras. In the process, we provide a proof of the conilpotent
version of the dual Poincare-Birkhoff-Witt theorem.

1
Introduction

This note grew out of desire to calculate the cohomology
of free nilpotent Lie algebras
L^{\leq n}(V):=L(V)/L^{>n}(V)
.
Here
V
is a vector space,
L(V)
is the free Lie algebra
generated by
V
, naturally graded by the commutator length:

L(V)=\oplus_{n\geq 1}L^{n}(V)
, and
L^{\leq n}(V)
and
L^{>n}(V)

are, respectively, the quotient algebras and the
ideals associated to this grading.
Since a free nilpotent Lie algebra is a very natural object, it
seems that its cohomology should also be a very natural object,
and hence any understanding of its structure might be desirable.
Indeed, even in the simplest nontrivial case
n=2
the calculation
of the
GL({\mathbb{R}}^{m})
-character of the representation
H(L^{\leq 2}({\mathbb{R}}^{m}))

performed by Józefiak-Weyman (
[
6
]
) and by
Sigg (
[
18
]
) gives a categorification of the well-known
Littlewood identity:

\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-x_{i})\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq m}(1-x_{i}x_{j})=\sum_{\lambda}(-1)^{\frac{1}{2}(|\lambda|+r(\lambda))}s_{\lambda}(x).

Here the sum in the right-hand part is the sum of the Schur polynomials
corresponding to all self-conjugate Young diagrams. By
|\lambda|

is denoted the number of all squares in the diagram and by
r(\lambda)
the
number of diagonal squares.

So it is plausible that knowledge of cohomology of free nilpotent Lie
algebras of higher nilpotency degree may bring some insights
on other mathematical questions — at the very least, it will be
a source of new identities on symmetric functions. However, for now
only partial results are available,
mostly obtained by direct
calculations, see, e.g.,
[
20
]
.

It is often the case that the existence of extra structures
helps calculations by limiting their scope. In the case of
cohomology calculations one such extra structure is the multiplication.
Moreover, the multiplication on cohomology
is part of a richer structure — the
C_{\infty}
structure (
[
8
]
),
which consists
of a collection of of polylinear operations
that refine Massey products in a certain way.
For semisimple
{\mathfrak{g}}
, the algebra
H({\mathfrak{g}})

knows little information about
{\mathfrak{g}}
itself —
it is just the exterior algebra on some generators
readable from the Dynkin diagram.
There is no nontrivial
C_{\infty}
-structure in this case.
Nilpotent algebras are the opposite: knowing the
C_{\infty}
-structure, one
can reconstruct from it the original Lie algebra up to isomorphism.
Using the calculations of Sigg, Dubois-Violette and Popov in
[
4
]

were able to prove that as a
C_{\infty}
-algebra, the cohomology algebra

H(L^{\leq 2}(V))
is generated by
H^{1}
with only
m_{2}
and
m_{3}
operations.

Since free nilpotent Lie algebras admit such a simple
(four words!) description, and since their cohomology algebras
are tied very closely to them, one is lead to think that perhaps

C_{\infty}
-algebra

H(L^{\leq n}(V))
also admits a short description. From the results
of this paper it follows that as a
C_{\infty}
-algebra it is generated by its
component of degree 1. However, not much is clear about the relations.
It is not hard to calculate
H^{2}
— indeed, the exact sequence

0{\>\longrightarrow\>}L^{n+1}(V){\>\longrightarrow\>}L^{\leq n+1}(V){\>\longrightarrow\>}L^{\leq n}(V){\>\longrightarrow\>}0

is the universal central extension of
L^{\leq n}(V)
, hence

H^{2}(L^{\leq n}(V))=(L^{n+1}(V))^{\vee}
. The
C_{\infty}
-operations

m_{i}:H^{1}=L^{i}(V^{\vee}){\>\longrightarrow\>}(L^{n+1}(V))^{\vee}
are either zero
when
i\neq n+1
or natural isomorphisms when
i=n+1
. However,
the behaviour in higher degrees remains elusive. To have
an example of complications arising when trying to generate infinity-algebras
by generators and relations, the reader may consult
[
9
]
.

On the positive side, the result about 1-generatedness
is not particular to the case of free nilpotent Lie algebras:
the results of this paper show that the cohomology algebra
H({\mathfrak{g}})

is
C_{\infty}
-generated by
H^{1}({\mathfrak{g}})
for
any nilpotent
finite dimensional
Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
. It also follows that for inverse limits
of finite dimensional nilpotent Lie algebras the
C_{\infty}
-algebra
of
continious cohomology

H_{c}({\mathfrak{g}})
is generated by
H^{1}
. This result
is probably known to some experts in the field
(especially in the case where there exists a positive grading on
{\mathfrak{g}}
—
the general case of nilpotent
{\mathfrak{g}}
seems to be a more obscure knowledge).
However, since from time to time papers with partial results appear
(see, for example,
[
12
]
dealing with the case of the Witt algebra),
there might be a demand for a paper where 1-generatedness result
is written.

We prove the result for conilpotent Lie coalgebras rather than nilpotent
Lie algebras, because the proof is more natural
and we feel that it is more easily digestible that way. First, this step
from algebras to coalgebras removes the necessity to have a positive
grading. Second, in this way we don’t have to mention the
cobar construction at all, and it seems
that mathematicians are in general more acquainted
with the bar construction. Even though our proof is just an
application of the general cobar-bar duality (and we formulate
a theorem for a general pair of Koszul operads), we wanted to make
the paper largely self-contained —
the inclusion of the discussion of Koszul duality
for operads would probably double its length.
This is also the reason we’re working
with
A_{\infty}
-algebras and not
C_{\infty}
-algebras: the former
type algebras are familiar to more mathematicians, and the latter
is sometimes perceived as exotic. As a side consequence of our choice
of approach, we obtain a proof of the analogue of the Poincare-Birkhoff-Witt
theorem for the conilpotent coenveloping coalgebra of a conilpotent Lie algebra.
We claim no originality for our proof, since it is just the dualization
of the Positselski’s proof of the PBW theorem (
[
14
]
). However, since
Positselski wrote down his proof in great generality — for
nonhomogeneous quadratic deformations of Koszul algebras — it might be
useful to someone to have it in the traditional formulation.

Our proof works only over the field of characteristic zero.
The only time the characteristic comes up is during the
calculation of
\operatorname{Ext}_{\Lambda(V)}({\Bbbk},{\Bbbk})
, but
this calculation is crucial to the proof.
Reader, interested in the cohomology of
free nilpotent Lie algebras over
{\mathbb{Z}}
, may find

[
17
]
useful.

2
Lie coalgebras

Definition 2.1:
A
Lie coalgebra
over a field
{\Bbbk}
is a vector
space
L
together with the comultiplication operation

\Delta:L{\>\longrightarrow\>}L\otimes L
satisfying the skew-cocommutativity and
coJacobi relations. The skew-cocommutativity relation
means that the image of
\Delta
lies in the subspace

\Lambda^{2}L\subset L\otimes L
of skew-symmetric tensors. The coJacobi
relations could be phrased as follows: let
\xi
be an
operation of cyclic permutation on
L\otimes L\otimes L
.
Then the coJacobi relation tells that the following map

L{\>\longrightarrow\>}L\otimes L\otimes L

(\operatorname{Id}+\xi+\xi^{2})\circ(\operatorname{Id}\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta

(1)

must vanish.

Remark 2.2:
The dual space
L^{\vee}
to a Lie coalgebra
L
is a Lie algebra,
the converse, verbatim, is not true, but it becomes true if one
considers the space
L^{\vee}
as a
topological
vector
space with its natural Tychonoff topology. Topological vector spaces
which are dual to discrete vector spaces are precisely

linearly compact
spaces (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
),
and the space
W^{\vee}
of continious linear functionals on a linearly
compact vector space
W
is precisely the vector space of which

W
is the dual. As a result, the categories of Lie coalgebras
and of linearly compact Lie algebras are (anti-)equivalent.

Definition 2.3:
A
Chevalley-Eilenberg
algebra
C(L)
of a Lie coalgebra
L
is
the exterior algebra
\Lambda L
with the grading

C^{k}(L):=\Lambda^{k}L
and
the differential
\delta
which on the generator
space
L
is equal to
\Delta
. It is naturally augmented
by the projecton on the degree zero component.

Remark 2.4:
To obtain
C(L)
from
L^{\vee}
one has to take the
space of continious polylinear functionals on
L^{\vee}
.

The calculation which can be found in, e.g.,
[
11
]
,
shows that
\delta^{2}
is zero and hence
C(L)
is a dg-algebra.

Definition 2.5:

A coalgebra
L
is called
filtered
or
conilpotent

if its dual topological Lie algebra is a projective limit of finite
dimensional nilpotent
Lie algebras. Alternativelty, it means
that there exists an increasing ascending exhaustive filtration
by subspaces
F_{*}L

such that
F_{0}L=0
and such that
\Delta(F_{i})
lies
inside
\sum_{p+q=i}F_{p}\otimes F_{q}\subset L\otimes L
.

Remark 2.6:
The previous definition could be applied to other kinds of coalgebras,
not necessarily Lie ones. Beware, though, that in the coaugmented
case one may require
F_{0}L
to be equal to the image of coaugmentation.
In this article all coalgebras are not supposed to have neither a counit
nor a coaugmentation.

The tensor powers of conilpotent Lie coalgebra
L

inherit the filtration and hence its Chevalley-Eilenberg
algebra
C=C(L)
is then also endowed with an ascending
exhaustive filtration such that
F_{0}C={\Bbbk}=C^{0}
. The
Chevalley-Eilenberg differential preserves this filtration.

For a linearly compact Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
we will denote by
U{\mathfrak{g}}
its
topological universal enveloping associative algebra.
It is a quotient of the completed (both in the topology
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and in tensor degree topology) free algebra generated by
{\mathfrak{g}}

by the closure of the ideal generated by elements
of the form
xy-yx=[x,y]
. This algebra is naturally
augmented. We will denote its augmentation ideal by
I
.

Definition 2.7:
Let
L
be a Lie coalgebra, and let
{\mathfrak{g}}:=L^{\vee}

be the corresponding dual linearly compact Lie algebra.
Consider the coassociative coalgebra

UL:=\operatorname{colim}_{k}(U{\mathfrak{g}}/I^{k})^{\vee}.

(2)

This coalgebra is called a
conilpotent coenveloping coalgebra
of
L
.

In contrast to the
algebra case, there exist Lie coalgebras for which
conilpotent coenveloping coalgebras
are trivial. One can take, for example,
the Lie coalgebra dual to any semisimple Lie algebra.
However, for conilpotent Lie
coalgebras an exact analogue of the Poincare-Birkhoff-Witt theorem holds.
One proof of this theorem is available in the appendix
of
[
2
]
. We will provide another one, which
basically coincides with the proof of the usual PBW theorem
due to Positselski (
[
14
]
).

3
A-infinity algebras

Definition 3.1:
Let
A
be an associative dg-algebra, not necessarily unital.
Its
bar-construction

is a coassociative dg-coalgebra
\mathrm{B}A
which, as a coalgebra,
is the tensor coalgebra generated by
A[1]
, and whose codifferential
is the unique codderivation of the tensor coalgebra
whose corestriction to
A[1]
is given by the differential and multiplication
in
A
. Modulo signs, we have

d_{\mathrm{B}}(a_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes a_{n})=\sum\pm a_{1}\otimes\dots da_{k}\dots\otimes a_{n}+\sum\pm a_{1}\otimes\dots a_{i}a_{i+1}\dots\otimes a_{n}.

(3)

The condition that
d_{\mathrm{B}}=0
is precisely the defining
equations for an associative dg-algebra: the multiplication
is associative, the differential is square-zero and satisfies
the Leibniz rule. The bar-construction is naturally
(moreover, uniquely) coaugmented
by the inclusion of tensors of degree zero.
A morphism
f:A{\>\longrightarrow\>}B
induces
a map of dg-coalgebras, which we will denote by the
same letter
f:\mathrm{B}A{\>\longrightarrow\>}\mathrm{B}B
. This map preserves the coaugmentations
of bar-constructions.

Definition 3.2:
An
A-infinity
algebra
A
is a graded vector space
endowed with the square-zero coderivation
d_{\mathrm{B}}A

of the tensor coalgebra generated by
A[1]
preserving
its unique coaugmentation. We will denote the
resulting dg-coalgebra by
\mathrm{B}A
also. A
morphism

between two
A_{\infty}
-algebras
A
and
B
is, by definition,
a morphism of dg-coalgebras
\mathrm{B}A{\>\longrightarrow\>}\mathrm{B}B
. It
preserves the coaugmentations automatically.

From the universal property of the tensor coalgebra
it follows that the codifferential
d_{\mathrm{B}}
is determined
by its corestriction to
A[1]
, that is, by a collection of maps

m_{i}:A[1]^{\otimes i}{\>\longrightarrow\>}A[2],

(4)

satisfying a collection of quadratic equations corresponding
to
d_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}=0

and an
A_{\infty}
-morphism
f:\mathrm{B}A{\>\longrightarrow\>}\mathrm{B}B

is determined by a collection of maps

f_{i}:A[1]^{\otimes i}{\>\longrightarrow\>}B[1],

(5)

satisfying a collection of quadratic equations corresponding
to
d_{\mathrm{B}B}f=fd_{\mathrm{B}A}.
It is clear that dg-algebras are
precisely those
A_{\infty}
-algebras for which
m_{i}=0
for
i\neq 1,2
.
However, a notion of an
A_{\infty}
-morphism is more general than the notion
of a morphism of dg-algebras, except for the case
when both algebras are concentrated in a single degree
\neq 1
. We will
call the collections
\{m_{i}\}
and
\{f_{i}\}
the

Taylor components
of the entities defined by them.

Note that for a dg-algebra
A
the bar-construction
\mathrm{B}A
is a
filtered coalgebra. The filtration is given by

F_{i}\mathrm{B}A:=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{i}(A[1])^{\otimes k}.

(6)

If an
A_{\infty}
-algebra
A
is determined by Taylor components

(m_{1},m_{2},\dots)
, then
\operatorname{gr}_{F}\mathrm{B}A
is
\oplus_{i}A[1]^{\otimes i}

with the differential
m_{1}
. In particular,
m_{1}^{2}=0
, and it defines
a structure of a complex on
A
.

Note that if
f:\mathrm{B}A{\>\longrightarrow\>}\mathrm{B}B
is an
A_{\infty}
-morphism,
then it automatically preserves this filtration. If the Taylor components
of
f
are
(f_{1},f_{2},\dots)
, then
\operatorname{gr}_{F}f=f_{1}
. In particular,

f_{1}m_{A,1}=m_{B,1}f_{1}
.

Definition 3.3:
An
A_{\infty}
-morphism
f:\mathrm{B}A{\>\longrightarrow\>}\mathrm{B}B
is called a quasiisomorphism
if it is a filtered quasiisomorphism of dg-coalgebras, that is,
if
\operatorname{gr}_{F}f
is a quasi-isomorphism. Equivalently, since
tensor products are exact over a field,
f_{1}
is a quasiisomorphism.

Remark 3.4:

It follows then that
f
is a quasi-isomorphism of dg-coalgebras,
if
f
is an
A_{\infty}
-quasiisomorphism. Indeed,
from 5-lemma it follows that

f|_{F_{k}}
is a quasi-isomorphism for any
k
. Since

\mathrm{B}=\operatorname{colim}_{k}F_{k}\mathrm{B}
the result follows from the exactness
of the direct limit.

The usefulness of
A_{\infty}
-algebras is illustrated by the following
theorem, which is usually attributed to Kadeishvili:

Definition 3.5:
An
A_{\infty}
-algebra
A
is called
minimal

if
\operatorname{gr}_{F}\mathrm{B}A
has zero differential.

Theorem 3.6:
Let
(A,m_{1},m_{2},\dots)
be an
A_{\infty}
algebra. Let
H
be the cohomology
of the differential
m_{1}
. Then
H
admits a structure of a minimal

A_{\infty}
-algebra and there is an
A_{\infty}
quasi-isomorphism
between this structure and
A
. Moreover, the structure on
H
is unique
up to an
A_{\infty}
-
isomophism
.

Remark 3.7:
The theorems of this kind were considered by rational homotopists
before Kadeishvili: see, for example,
[
19
, Theorem 2.2]
.
However, the actual Kadeishvili’s result (
[
7
]
)
is more general than the theorem above: for example,
he doesn’t require the base ring to be a field,
but only requires
H
to be a projective module. See
[
13
]

for discussion.

4
1-generation

Let
(A,m_{2},m_{3},\dots)
be a minimal
A_{\infty}
-algebra.
We are interested in the cohomology of its bar-complex
H(\mathrm{B}A)
.
As
\mathrm{B}A
is a filtered (by the tensor filtration) complex,
its cohomology
H(\mathrm{B}A)
inherit the filtration.
That is,
F^{k}H(\mathrm{B}A)
are classes that could be represented by
cocycles that lie in
F^{k}\mathrm{B}A
.

Definition 4.1:
A minimal
A_{\infty}
-algebra
A
is called

1-generated
if it is positively graded and

F^{1}H^{j}(\mathrm{B}A)=0
for
j\geq 1
.

Remark 4.2:
Note that this notion is well-defined in the sense
that it is invariant under
A_{\infty}
-automorphisms.
Indeed, an
A_{\infty}
-automorphism of
A

defines a filtered automorphism
of the dg-coalgebra
\mathrm{B}A
.

The following lemma is a generalization to the

A_{\infty}
case of a well-known criterion of 1-generatedness
of graded algebras, see e.g.
[
15
]

Lemma 4.3:
A positively graded
A_{\infty}
algebra
A
is 1-generated if and only if
any element
x\in A^{k}
can be expressed as
a linear combination of iterated
compositions of maps of the form

\operatorname{Id}^{\otimes i}\otimes m_{j}\otimes\operatorname{Id}^{\otimes k}

applied to elements in
A^{\otimes n}
.

Remark 4.4:
This is equivalent to the fact that the map

FREE(A^{1}){\>\longrightarrow\>}A
, where
FREE
is the functor
of the free minimal
A_{\infty}
-algebra
(which exists since minimal
A_{\infty}
-algebras
are algebras over a certain operad, see e.g.
[
10
]
), is surjective.
The fact that this is invariant under
A_{\infty}
-isomorphisms
is less obvious then the previous definition of 1-generatedness.

Proof:

First remember that the space
F^{1}H^{k}(\mathrm{B}A)
is the subspace
of
H^{k}(\mathrm{B}A)
that could be represented by classes
in
F^{1}(\mathrm{B}A)=A[1]\subset\mathrm{B}(A)
. Since
A
is minimal,
the subspace
A[1]
consists of closed elements, hence

F^{1}H^{k}(\mathrm{B}A)
is the quotient of
A^{k-1}
by the image of

d_{\mathrm{B}A}
.
Now we can prove the equivalence of two definitions by induction.
Note that
\operatorname{Im}d_{\mathrm{B}A}\cap A^{2}
is spanned by images of arrows

(A^{1})^{\otimes i}{\>\longrightarrow\>}A^{2}
which up to sign coincide with the
operations
m_{i}
. Hence
A^{2}
is generated from
m_{i}
’s by
A^{1}

if and only if
F^{1}H^{1}(\mathrm{B}A)=0
. Next, suppose we proved the equivalence
for all
A^{k}
, where
k<n
for some
n
. Then space

\operatorname{Im}d_{\mathrm{B}A}\cap A^{n}
is spanned by images of
m_{i}
’s applied to
the subspaces
\otimes_{j=2}^{i}A^{p_{j}}
, where

\sum p_{j}=n-i+2
. Again, by degree reasons all
p_{j}
’s are less then
n
,
and the vanishing of
A^{n}/\operatorname{Im}d_{\mathrm{B}A}
is equivalent to the fact
that the elements of
A^{n}
are expressable via
the operations
m_{i}
through the elements of smaller degree, hence, by the
base of induction, they are expressable through elements of
A^{1}
.

5
Conilpotent PBW theorem

The PBW theorem will follow from the following computation
of the bar-construction of the Chevalley-Eilenberg algebra
of a conilpotent Lie coalgebra
L
. Denote by
C^{>0}(L)

the quotient of
C(L)
by the coaugmentation map
{\Bbbk}{\>\longrightarrow\>}C(L)
.

Lemma 5.1:

For a conilpotent Lie coalgebra
L
, the
cohomology of
\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L)
vanish in positive degrees.
The coalgebra
H^{0}(\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L))
is isomorphic to
U(L)
,
the conilpotent coenveloping coalgebra of
L
.

Proof:
We have two naturally defined filtrations
on
\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L)
. The ”stupid” filtration on
C^{>0}(L)

given by
G^{k}=\oplus_{i\geq k}C^{i}(L)

extends to a filtration on
\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L)
, which we will denote by

G
. The filtration induced
from the conilpotent filtration on
L
will be denoted by
N
.
The filtration
G
is descending and non-complete,
the filtration
N
is ascending and exhaustive.

Consider the complex
\operatorname{gr}_{N}\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L)
. The filtration
G

induces a filtration on it, which we will also denote by
G
. An important
fact is that
G
is finite on each
N_{i}/N_{i-1}
. Consider now the
complex
\operatorname{gr}_{G}\operatorname{gr}_{N}\mathrm{B}C_{>0}(L)
. We want to show that its higher cohomology
vanish. From finiteness of
F
and exhaustiveness of
N

it will follow that higher cohomology of
\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L)
would
vanish as well.

Note that the double associated graded

\operatorname{gr}_{G}\operatorname{gr}_{N}\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L)
does not depend on the cobracket
on
L
and only depends on the underlying vector space.
Indeed,
\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L)
is naturally a bicomplex, namely,

\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L)=\oplus_{i,j\geq 0}B^{i,j}
, where

B^{i,j}=\bigoplus_{n_{1}+\dots+n_{j}=i,n_{k}>0}C^{n_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes C^{n_{j}}.

(7)

So, one grading, let us call it the horizontal one, is just
counting the number of tensor factors, and the other one,
let us call it vertical,
comes from the grading on
C^{>0}(L)
.
The total grading is their
difference
:

(\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L))^{n}=\oplus_{i-j=n}B^{i,j}
.
The differential in (
3
) is decomposed into the
sum of the vertical differential and the horizontal differential.
The vertical differential depends on the cobracket in
L
,
the horizontal one only depends on the multiplication
in the exterior algebra.

Now note that the operation of transition fron
\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L)

to
\operatorname{gr}_{N}\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L)
does not change the horizontal
differential and only changes vertical. The operation
of then taking
\operatorname{gr}_{G}
doesn’t change the horizontal differential either,
and erases the vertical one.
Moreover, the
complex
\operatorname{gr}_{G}\operatorname{gr}_{N}\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L)
could be identified
with
\operatorname{gr}_{G}\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L)
, which is just the bar construction
for an exterior coalgebra. Its cohomology
are
\operatorname{Tor}_{C(L)}({\Bbbk},{\Bbbk})
, which could be then
calculated by the Koszul resolution

[
16
]
. This calculation shows that its cohomology lie in total degree zero
and are isomorphic to the symmetric coalgebra of
L
.

The zeroth cohomology
H^{0}(\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L))
could be calculated
directly. The space of zero-chains of
\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L)

is the tensor coalgebra generated by
C_{1}(L)=L
,
that is, the direct sum
\oplus_{i\geq 0}L^{\otimes i}
.
The space
of one-chains is

\oplus_{i,j\geq 0}L^{\otimes i}\otimes\Lambda^{2}L\otimes L^{\otimes j}
.
The differential, from the universal property of tensor coalgebra,
is completely defined by its corestriction to

\Lambda^{2}L\subset(\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L))^{1}
. This is the difference of two maps

d_{1}:L{\>\longrightarrow\>}\Lambda^{2}L
and
d_{2}:L\otimes L{\>\longrightarrow\>}\Lambda^{2}L
,
the first of which is the cobracket in
L
and the second is
the alternation map.

To make everything more familiar, it is better to take the dual spaces,
especially since we defined the conilpotent coenveloping coalgebra
as the coalgebra topologically dual to the completion of the usual
enveloping algebra in the augmentation ideal. If
{\mathfrak{g}}:=L^{\vee}
then
the coalgebra

H^{0}(\mathrm{B}C^{>0}(L))
is dual to the algebra
\prod_{i}{\mathfrak{g}}^{\otimes i}

factored by the topological ideal generated by elements
of the form
x\otimes y-y\otimes x-[x,y]
, that is, the usual
(completed) universal enveloping algebra. The theorem is proved.

Remark 5.2:
Positselski’s proof of usual PBW theorem uses the
Chevalley-Eilenberg
coalgebra

C_{*}({\mathfrak{g}})

of a Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
. We include it here
for the convenience of the reader, since
the proof in
[
14
]
is about more general situation.
It goes as follows. First,
consider (the coaugmented variant of)
the cobar construction
\Omega C_{*}({\mathfrak{g}})
.
It is a dg-algebra, whose zero cohomology, as we’ve just seen,
are isomorphic to the usual (uncompleted) universal enveloping algebra

U{\mathfrak{g}}
. Consider the filtration on
\Omega C_{*}({\mathfrak{g}})
induced from
the ”stupid” filtration on
C_{*}({\mathfrak{g}})
given by

G_{i}C_{*}({\mathfrak{g}}):=\oplus_{k=-i}^{0}C_{k}({\mathfrak{g}})
. It is positive ascending
exhaustive filtration on
\Omega C_{*}({\mathfrak{g}})
, whose
associated graded could be identified with
\Omega C_{*}(V)
,
where
V
is an abelian Lie algebra which is isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{g}}

as a vector space. In this case
C_{*}(V)
is just
an exterior coalgebra without any differential. As the cobar construction
for a coalgebra
C
without differential calculates

\operatorname{Ext}_{C}({\Bbbk},{\Bbbk})
, the Ext algebra from the trivial
C
-comodule
to itself, the cohomology algebra
H(\Omega C_{*}(V))

is the symmetric algebra

\operatorname{Sym}(V)
in degree zero and zero in higher degrees, as could be seen
from the same Koszul resolution. Since the filtration
G

is exhaustive, the associated spectral sequence converges and,
moreover, degenerates at the first page by the dimension reasons.
It follows that

\operatorname{gr}_{F}H^{0}(\Omega C_{*}({\mathfrak{g}}))=\operatorname{gr}_{F}U{\mathfrak{g}}=H^{0}(\operatorname{gr}_{F}\Omega C_{*}({\mathfrak{g}}))=\operatorname{Sym}{\mathfrak{g}}.

(8)

This is the statement of the classical PBW theorem. The difference
between the Lie algebras and Lie coalgebras is that the filtration
on the cobar construction induced from the ”stupid” filtration
on the Chevalley-Eilenberg coalgebra
is ascending and exhaustive (in other
words, cocomplete), but the analogous filtration on the bar construction
of the Chevalley-Eilenberg algebra
is descending and not complete. To safely transit from the associated graded
complex to the original one,
in the Lie coalgebra case one needs an extra filtration, which
is provided by the conilpotency condition.

6
C-infinity algebras

If
A
is a commutative dg-algebra,
then
\mathrm{B}A
is not only a dg-coalgebra, but also
a dg-bialgebra: the bar-differential
derives the shuffle multiplication

\bullet:\mathrm{B}A\otimes\mathrm{B}A{\>\longrightarrow\>}\mathrm{B}A
.
If
I
is the augmentation ideal of
\mathrm{B}A
, then
the
complex of indecomposables

I/I\bullet I

obtains the structure of a dg-Lie coalgebra.
This dg-Lie coalgebra is called the
Harrison complex

of
A
and will be denoted by

\mathrm{T}A
. By the dual Milnor-Moore theorem
(see
[
1
]
),
the dg-bialgebra
\mathrm{B}A
is the conilpotent
coenveloping coalgebra of
\mathrm{T}A
.

Remark 6.1:
An important consequence of the dual Milnor-Moore theorem
is that in characteristic zero

\mathrm{T}A
is the
direct summand
of
\mathrm{B}A
.
In particular,
the embedding
\mathrm{T}A{\>\longrightarrow\>}\mathrm{B}A
induces an injection on cohomology.

Definition 6.2:
An
A_{\infty}
-algebra
A
is called a
C-infinity

algebra, if the coderivation
\delta
of
\mathrm{B}A

derives the shuffle product
\bullet
. Alternatively,
a

-algebra is a graded vector space
A

endowed with a square-zero coderivation of the
free Lie coalgebra generated by
A[1]

A
C_{\infty}
-morphism between two
A_{\infty}
-algebras
is an
A_{\infty}
-morphism that additionally preserve
shuffle multiplication. Alternatively,
this is a morphism of the corresponding
Harrison dg-Lie coalgebras.

A
C_{\infty}
-algebra is minimal if it is minimal as an
A_{\infty}
-algebra.
The minimal model of an
A_{\infty}
-algebra
admits a
C_{\infty}
-structure, and an
A_{\infty}
-quasiisomorphism
between a
C_{\infty}
-algebra and its minimal model could be chosen
to be a
C_{\infty}
-quasiisomorphism. See, e.g.
[
5
]
.

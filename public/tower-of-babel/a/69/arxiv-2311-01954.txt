Regular bi-interpretability and finite axiomatizability of Chevalley groups Thanks: The paper is written as part of the second author’s post-doctoral fellowship at Bar-Ilan University, Department of Mathematics; and is supported by ISF grant 1994/20.
Elena Bunina and Pavel Gvozdevsky

Abstract
. In this paper we consider Chevalley groups over commutative rings with 1 1 , constructed by irreducible root systems of rank > 1 >1 . We always suppose that for the systems A 2 , B ℓ , C ℓ , F 4 , G 2 A_{2},B_{\ell},C_{\ell},F_{4},G_{2} our rings contain 1 / 2 1/2 and for the system G 2 G_{2} also 1 / 3 1/3 . Under these assumptions we prove that the central quotients of Chevalley groups are regularly bi-interpretable with the corresponding rings, the class of all central quotients of Chevalley groups of a given type is elementarily definable and even finitely axiomatizable (see Definition 2.2 ). The same holds for adjoint Chevalley groups and for bondedly generated Chevalley groups. We also give an example of Chevalley group with infinite center, which is not bi-interpretable with the corresponding ring and is elementarily equivalent to a group that is not a Chevalley group itself.

1.
Introduction. Historical overview

1.1.
Elementary equivalence of groups, Maltsev-type theorems

Elementary (first-order) classification of algebraic structures goes back to the works of Tarski and Maltsev. In general, the task is to characterize, in somewhat algebraic terms, all algebraic structures elementarily equivalent to a given one. Recall, that two algebraic structures
\mathfrak{A}
and
\mathfrak{B}
in a language
\mathcal{L}
are
elementarily equivalent
(
\mathfrak{A}\equiv\mathfrak{B}
) if they satisfy precisely the same
first-order sentences in
\mathcal{L}
.

Classification of objects up to an equivalence relation is one of the principal problems in mathematics.
The equivalence relation on a structure is determined by the type of property one would like to be preserved,
namely in model theory one classifies modulo elementary theory; in algebra, modulo isomorphism;
in geometric group theory, modulo quasi-isometries, etc.
To determine which groups (or other structures) are elementarily equivalent to a given one is usually a very hard problem. The first powerful result for groups is the classification of groups elementarily equivalent
to an Abelian group. It was obtained by W. Szmielew
[
64
]
(two Abelian groups are elementarily equivalent if and only if they have the same special ‘‘characteristic numbers’’ like
\,\mathrm{Exp}\,A
,
\dim p^{n}A[p]
, etc.).
Nowadays the detailed descriptions of the groups elementary equivalent to a given one are obtained for wide list of groups, see for example the survey in
[
22
]
.

When we restrict ourselves to the class of finitely generated groups elementarily equivalent to the given one, the problem becomes especially challenging.

An outstanding result was the answer to the old problem raised by A. Tarski around 1945:
for non-Abelian finitely generated free groups the elementary theory doesn’t distinguish these groups (see for details the series of works of Kharlampovich–Myasnikov and Z. Sela, e. g.
[
37
]
,
[
58
]
). The similar situation takes place for the torsion free hyperbolic groups (see Sela,
[
57
]
).

On the other hand, a theorem of F. Oger
[
52
]
states that two finitely generated
nilpotent groups
G
and
H
are elementarily equivalent if and only if
G\times\mathbb{Z}\cong H\times\mathbb{Z}
. In the paper
[
49
]
, A. Myasnikov
gave a description of groups elementarily equivalent to a given finitely generated nilpotent
K
-group,
where
K
is a field of characteristic zero. Recently, A. Myasnikov and M. Sohrabi classified
groups elementarily equivalent to a free nilpotent
R
-group of finite rank, where
R
is a domain of
characteristic zero, see
[
45
]
. M. Casals-Ruiz and I. Kazachkov in
[
22
]
proved that all finitely generated groups elementarily equivalent to
a solvable Baumslag–Solitar group
BS(m,1)
are isomorphic to it.

Certainly, any new such complete classification result is a rather rare mathematical phenomenon.

Another way of studying elementary equivalence of algebraic models is to establish a connection between derivative models (linear groups over rings and fields, automorphism groups and endomorphism rigs of different structures, etc.) and initial models (and ‘‘parameters’’) used for the construction. In his classical paper
[
43
]
A.I. Maltsev studied elementary equivalence of matrix groups
\mathcal{G}_{n}(F)
where
\mathcal{G}_{n}=\,\mathrm{GL}\,_{n},\,\mathrm{SL}\,_{n},\,\mathrm{PGL}\,_{n},\,\mathrm{PSL}\,_{n}
,
n\geqslant 3
, and
F
is a field. Namely, he showed that
\mathcal{G}_{n}(F)\equiv\mathcal{G}_{m}(L)
if and only if
n=m
and
F\equiv L
. His proof was based on two principal results. The first one states that for any integer
k\geqslant 3
and
\mathcal{G}_{n}
as above there is a group sentence
\Phi_{k,\mathcal{G}}
such that for any
n
, and a field
F
,
\Phi_{k,\mathcal{G}}
holds in
\mathcal{G}_{n}(F)
if and only if
k=n
. The second one is that
F
and
\mathcal{G}_{n}(F)
are mutually interpretable in each other. More precisely,
\mathcal{G}_{n}(F)
is absolutely interpretable in
F
(i. e., no use of parameters), while
F
is interpretable in
\mathcal{G}_{n}(F)
uniformly with respect to some definable subset of tuples of parameters (so-called
regular
interpretability). This implies that the theories
Th(F)
and
Th(\mathcal{G}_{n}(F))
are reducible to each other in polynomial time, hence
Th(\mathcal{G}_{n}(F))
is decidable if and only if
Th(F)
is decidable. Later Beidar and Mikhalev introduced another general approach to elementary equivalence of classical matrix groups
[
7
]
. Their proof was based on Keisler–Shelah theorem (
two structures are elementarily equivalent if and only if their ultrapowers over non-principal ultraflters are isomorphic
, see
[
35
]
,
[
59
]
) and the description of the abstract isomorphisms of the groups of the type
\mathcal{G}_{n}(F)
. Then these results were extended to unitary linear and Chevalley groups (see
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
16
]
). Note that in all the results above the first-order theories include only the standard constants from the languages of groups and rings. The model theory of the group
UT_{n}(R)
, where
n\geqslant 3
, and
R
is an arbitrary unitary associative ring, was studied in details by O. Belegradek
[
8
]
. He used heavily that the ring
R
is interpretable
(with parameters) in
UT_{n}(R)
. A. Myasnikov and M. Sohrabi studied model theory of groups
\,\mathrm{SL}\,_{n}(\mathcal{O})
,
\,\mathrm{GL}\,_{n}(\mathcal{O})
, and
T_{n}(\mathcal{O})
over fields and rings of algebraic integers in
[
46
]
and
[
47
]
.
Their method exploits the mutual interpretability (and also bi-interpretability)
of the group and the ring. In a similar manner N. Avni, A. Lubotsky, and C. Meiri in
[
5
]

proved the first order rigidity of non-uniform higher rank arithmetic groups, and then Avni and Meiri in
[
6
]
did the same for centerless irreducible higher rank arithmetic lattices in characteristic zero.

D. Segal and K. Tent (see
[
56
]
) showed that for Chevalley groups
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
of rank

>1
over an integral domain

if

has finite elementary width or is adjoint, then
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
and

are bi-interpretable. In
[

it was proved that over local rings Chevalley groups

of rank

are regularly bi-interpretable with the corresponding rings.

1.2.
Chevalley groups, their automorphisms, isomorphisms and elementary equivalence.

In the 1950’s Chevalley, Steinberg and others introduced the concept of Chevalley groups
over commutative rings, which includes classical linear groups (special linear
\,\mathrm{SL}\,_{n}
, special orthogonal
\,\mathrm{SO}\,_{n}
, symplectic
\,\mathrm{Sp}\,_{2n}
,
spinor
\,\mathrm{Spin}\,_{n}

Isomorphisms and automorphisms of Chevalley groups were also studied intensively.
The standard description of isomorphisms of Chevalley groups over fields was obtained by
R. Steinberg
[
61

for the finite case and by J. Humphreys
[
33
]
for the infinite one. Many papers were devoted
to description of automorphisms of Chevalley groups over different

[

]

[

, E. Abe
[

[

Then automorphisms of arbitrary Chevalley groups over local rings were described (these rings supposed to contain
1/2
for the root systems
A_{2},B_{\ell},C_{\ell}
and
F_{4}
and
1/3
for the root system
G_{2}
) and then in 2012 (see
[

) all automorphisms of adjoint Chevalley groups over arbitrary commutative rings were described. Recently (see
[
14

) the complete desription of automorphisms and isomorphisms of Chevalley groups over arbitrary commutative rings subject to natural invertability restrictions was obtained:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G=G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
be a Chevalley group (or
G=(E_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)

be its elementary subgroup) with an irreducible root system
\Phi

>1

be a commutative ring with

. Suppose that for
\Phi=A_{2},B_{\ell},C_{\ell}

is standard (the composition of ring, central, diagram and inner automorphisms). If

Description of automorphisms and isomorphisms of Chevalley groups over rings is a powerful instrument needed for solutions of diefferent model-theoretic problems of Chevalley groups in general (including Maltsev-type theorems, bi-interpretability, first order rigidity, elementary definability etc.).
Talking about Maltsev-type theorems, the following result (see
[

]
and
[

) almost

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

and
G^{\prime}=G_{\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}(\Phi^{\prime},R^{\prime})

(

and

be two

(for the cases of the roots systems

Then the groups

are elementarily equivalent if and only if the root systems

1.3.
First order rigidity and QFA property, bi-interpretability of linear groups and their elementary definability.

Recent years have been characterized by a real breakthrough in model theory of linear groups. Within quite a few years the papers of A. Nies (see
[
51
]
,
[
50
]
, etc.), N. Avni, A. Lubotzky, C. Meiri (see
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
, also see
[
34
]
), A. Myasnikov, O. Kharlampovich and M. Sohrabi (see
[
36
]
,
[
47
]
,
[
48
]
, etc.),
D. Segal and K. Tent (see
[
56
]
), B. Kunyavskii, E. Plotkin, N. Vavilov (see
[
41
]
) and others appeared (see
[
54
]
for a survey of results).

Since according to Löwenheim–Skolem theorem
for any infinite structure
\mathcal{A}
there exists a structure
\mathcal{B}
such that
\mathcal{A}\equiv\mathcal{B}
and they
have different cardinalities, the classification up to elementary equivalence is always strictly wider than classification up to isomorphism. But for finitely generated case there exists a chance that elementary equivalence could imply isomorphism.
For example if any finitely generated ring
A
is elementarily equivalent to
\mathbb{Z}
, then it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
. This phenomenon is not true for infinitely generated rings.

A question dominating research in this area has been if and when elementary
equivalence between finitely generated groups (rings) implies isomorphism. Recently,
Avni–Lubotzky–Meiri
[
5
]
found the term
first-order rigidity
.
Namely, a finitely generated
group (ring or other structure)
\mathcal{A}
is
first-order rigid
if any other finitely generated group (ring or other structure)
elementarily equivalent to
\mathcal{A}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{A}
.

In many cases a more strong QFA property holds. It says that
a single first-order axiom is sufficient to distinguish a group among
all the finitely generated groups. The corresponding concept was introduced in
[
50
]
for
groups only, and then in
[
51
]
for arbitrary structures.

For a fixed finite signature an infinite finitely generated structure
\mathcal{A}
is
quasi finitely
axiomatizable
(
QFA
) if there is a first order sentence
\varphi
such that

(1)
\mathcal{A}\vDash\varphi
;

(2) if
H
is a finitely generated structure in the same signature such that
H\vDash\varphi
, then

H\cong\mathcal{A}
.

The most important example of QFA rings is the ring of integers
(\mathbb{Z},+,\cdot)
, see
[
53
]
.

New horizons in this area were opened towards application of the bi-interpretability property and finding new classes of QFA rings and groups.

Definition 1.3
.

Suppose structures
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
in finite signatures are given, as well as
interpretations of
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathcal{B}
, and vice versa. Then an isomorphic copy
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
of
\mathcal{A}
can be defined in
\mathcal{A}
, by ‘‘decoding’’
\mathcal{A}
from the copy of
\mathcal{B}
defined in
\mathcal{A}
.
Similarly, an isomorphic copy
\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
of
\mathcal{B}
can be defined in
\mathcal{B}
. An isomorphism
\Phi:{\mathcal{A}}\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
can be viewed as a relation on
\mathcal{A}
, and similarly for an isomorphism
\mathcal{B}\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}
.
We say that
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are
bi-interpretable
(with parameters) if there
are such isomorphisms that are first-order definable (see
[
30
]
, Ch. 5).

The case of special interest is when one can prove that a given structure is bi-interpretable with the ring
\mathbb{Z}
of integers.
A. Khelif (see
[
38
]
, see also
[
36
]
) realized that one can use bi-interpretability of a finitely generated structure
\mathcal{A}
with
(\mathbb{Z},+\times)
as a general method
to prove that
\mathcal{A}
is QFA.
Somewhat later, P. Dittmann and F. Pop (see
[
26
]
) used this method to show that each finitely generated field is QFA.

Anyway, suppose the structure
\mathcal{A}
in a finite signature is bi-interpretable with the ring
\mathbb{Z}
. Then
\mathcal{A}
is QFA.

Along with Maltsev-type theorems, the problems of the
elementary definability
of classes of groups are quite important. These problems are formulated as follows:

Suppose that we have a class
\mathcal{G}
of groups
(
ring, etc.
)
and an arbitrary group
(
rings, etc.
)

H
which is elementarily equivalent to some
G\in\mathcal{G}
. Is it true that
H\in\mathcal{G}
?

For example the class of all linear groups over fields (commutative rings) is elementarily definable (A.I. Maltsev,
[
42
]
),
but on the other hand for general linear groups even over fields there is no elementary definability: there exists a group
H\equiv\,\mathrm{GL}\,_{n}(F)
which is not of type
\,\mathrm{GL}\,
itself.

In 1984 B.I. Zilber (see
[
69
]
) proved that for algebraic groups over algebraically closed fields the problem of elementary definability has the positive answer:

the class of all simple algebraic groups over algebraically closed fields is elementarily definable.

To extend this Zilber’s result for the case of rings it is crucial to introduce a notion of regular bi-interpretability.
If
\mathcal{A}
is interpreted in
\mathcal{B}
uniformly with respect
to a
\emptyset
-definable subset
D\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{k}
then we say that
\mathcal{A}
is
regularly interpretable
in
\mathcal{B}
and write in this case
\mathcal{A}\cong\Gamma(\mathcal{B},\varphi)
, provided
D
is defined by
\varphi
in
\mathcal{B}
(see
[
36
]
).

Definition 1.4
.

Two algebraic structures
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are called
regularly bi-interpretable

in each other if the following conditions hold:

(1)
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are regularly interpretable in each other, so
\mathcal{A}\cong\Gamma(\mathcal{B},\varphi)
and
\mathcal{B}\cong\Delta(\mathcal{A},\psi)
for some interpretations (also called
codes
)
\Gamma
and
\Delta
and the corresponding formulas
\varphi,\psi

(without parameters). By transitivity
\mathcal{A}
, as well as
\mathcal{B}
, is regularly interpretable in itself, so we can write

\mathcal{A}\cong(\Gamma\circ\Delta)(\mathcal{A},\varphi^{*})\text{ and }\mathcal{B}\cong(\Delta\circ\Gamma)(\mathcal{B},\psi^{*}),

where
\circ
denotes composition of interpretations and
\varphi^{*},\psi^{*}
are the corresponding formulas.

(2) There is a formula
\theta(\overline{y},x,\overline{z})
in the language of
\mathcal{A}

such that for every tuple
p^{*}
satisfying
\varphi^{*}(\overline{z})
in
\mathcal{A}
the formula
\theta(\overline{y},x,p^{*})
defines in
\mathcal{A}
the isomorphism
\overline{\mu}_{\Gamma\circ\Delta}:(\Gamma\circ\Delta)(\mathcal{A},p^{*})\to\mathcal{A}
and there is a formula
\sigma(\overline{u},x,\overline{t})
in the
language of
\mathcal{B}
such that for every tuple
q^{*}
satisfying
\psi^{*}(\overline{v})
in
\mathcal{B}
the formula

\sigma(\overline{v},x,q^{*})
defines in
\mathcal{B}
the isomorphism
\overline{\mu}_{\Delta\circ\Gamma}:(\Delta\circ\Gamma)(\mathcal{B},q^{*})\to\mathcal{B}
.

The existence of regular bi-interpretation plays the decisive role for establishing elementary definability of given classes of groups.

If linear/Chevalley groups (or another derivative structures) of any concrete type over some classes of fields/rings are regularly bi-interpretable with the corresponding rings, then this class of groups (structures) is elementarily definable
(see
[
15
]
).

A. Myasnikov and M. Sohrabi proved that for all linear groups considered in the paper
[
47
]
the bi-interpretation with the corresponding rings is indeed a regular bi-interpretation,
hence the elementary definability problem has positive solution. One can show that this is also the case for the results of Segal-Tent (see
[
56
]
) mentioned above.

2.
Main results

All mentioned recent results illustrate that along with bi-interpretability with parameters it is of unprecedented importance to state
regular
bi-interpretability of linear groups and the corresponding rings.

In
[
15
]
the following theorem was proved:

Theorem 2.1
.

If
G(R)=G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
is a Chevalley group
(
or
G(R)=E_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
is its elementary subgroup
)
of rank
>1
,
R
is a local ring
(
with
\frac{1}{2}
for the root systems
{B}_{\ell},{C}_{\ell},{F}_{4},{G}_{2}
and with
\frac{1}{3}
for
{G}_{2})
, then the group
G(R)
is regularly bi-interpretable with
R
. Therefore the class of Chevalley groups over local rings
(
with mentioned restrictions
)
is elementarily definable.

This paper is devoted to obtaining the most general answer to the following fundamental question:

Problem
(*)
.

For which rings and under which conditions the class of all Chevalley groups is elementarily definable?

Also, we will consider other model-theoretic questions of Chevalley groups in maximal generality.

As it was observed in Section 1, Problem
(*)
has a positive solution for the Chevalley groups
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
, when
R
is an algebraically closed field
[
69
]
, when
R
is a Dedekind ring of the arithmetic type (can be deduced from
[
56
]
), and when
R
is a local ring
[
15
]
. In the later two cases some mild invertability conditions on
R
are assumed.

The best situation would be, if for all commutative rings with
1
the corresponding Chevalley groups are regularly bi-interpretable with the rings, which implies the fact that Chevalley groups constitute an elementary class of groups. Unfortunately this is not the case. We will see in Section 6 that if a Chevalley group has an infinite center, then in certain cases it is possible to prove that this group is not bi-interpretable with the corresponding ring and not elementarily definable in the class of all groups. To the contrary, if the center is trivial (it holds for all adjoint Chevalley groups over all rings), then regular bi-interpretability and elementary definability take place for ‘‘almost all’’ commutative rings, i. e., for all commutative rings with
\frac{1}{2}
for the root systems
{B}_{\ell},{C}_{\ell},{F}_{4},{G}_{2}
and with
\frac{1}{3}
for
{G}_{2}
.

For the case of non-trivial finite center the answer for the
Chevalley groups over arbitrary rings is not known.
However, we prove that in case of boundedly generated Chevalley groups the answer is positive (Theorem
2.8
). The same is true for the arbitrary central quotients of Chevalley groups (Theorem
2.7
).

Moreover, in all positive cases we show that the classes of the corresponding Chevalley groups (of a given type and with a given weight lattices) are finitely axiomatizable in the sense of the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
S
be a class of structures in some fixed signature. We say that
S
is finitely axiomatizable if there is a proposition
\varphi
in that signature such that for any structure
\mathcal{A}
in that signature we have
\mathcal{A}\vDash\varphi
if and only if
\mathcal{A}\in S
.

To formulate short informal and detailed formal versions of two main theorems and further conjectures we need some auxiliary notations/definitions.

Definition 2.3
.

For any group
G
we will denote by
PG

PG:=G/Z(G).

In particular, we will use the notation
PG_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
and will call this group a

Over an algebraically closed ring

G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\Phi,-)
is a sheafification of

in the fppf-topology. Thus
PG_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
is a normal subgroup of
G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\Phi,R)
and the quotient group can be embedded into the cohomology group
H^{1}_{\mathrm{fppf}}(R,\mu)
, where
\mu=\mathrm{Ker}(G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,-)\to G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,-))
, see, for example,
[
40

.

.

A commutative ring

;

— when we deal with the root system

and

.

For the cases
\Phi=A_{\ell},D_{\ell},E_{\ell}
,

, there are no restrictions on

.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
G
be a group,
X\subseteq G
be a subset, and

be a non-negative integer. We say that

is

boundedly generated

, if every element of

.

Definition 2.6
.

For a group
G
, elements
g_{1}
,
\ldots
,

X^{(m)}_{g_{1},\ldots,g_{l}}=\{[a,b]\mid a,b\in G\}\cup\{a^{m}\mid a\in G\}\cup\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{l}\}.

Now we are in a position to give a brief account of our results.

Let us always assume that

— a root system
\Phi
is irreducible and it’s rank is
>1
;

— a ring

.

Also let us call results about bi-interpretability and elementary definability
positive
, if:

— the Chevalley group
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
(or

) is regularly bi-interpretable with

— any group elementarily equivalent to
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
(or

) has the form
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,S)
(resp.
PG_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,S)
), where the ring
S
is elementarily equivalent to
R
;

— the class of all Chevalley groups
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,-)
(or
PG_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,-)
) over all good rings is finitely axiomatizable.

So, shortly speaking, in this paper we obtain
positive results
for the following groups and rings:

1.
All central quotients
PG_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
of Chevalley groups over good rings;

2.
In particular, all adjoint Chevalley groups
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)

over good rings;

3.
All Chevalley groups
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
over good rings that are
N
-boundedly generated by some set
X^{(m)}_{g_{1},\ldots,g_{l}}
for some natural

and some special

.

Also we show the
negative
result (by negative we mean that all
1
–

do not hold) for some

in the case of infinite center.

Our negative conjectures for today are the following:

1.
If the center of
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
is infinite, usually the results are negative.

2.
Even if the center of
G=G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
is finite and

is not

and any

, then there exist negative examples.

Below are the precise formulations of two main theorems of the paper. The first one is about central quotients of Chevalley groups:

>1

Remark 2.9
.

The statement of Theorem
2.8
makes it natural to ask for what rings the group
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
is boundedly generated by the set
X^{(m)}_{g_{1},\ldots,g_{l}}
as above. Let us list what we know.

\bullet
If
R
is a Dedekind domain of arithmetic type then
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
s boundedly generated by elementary unipotents, and hence by commutators, see for example
[
20
]
,
[
44
]
,
[
41
]
.

\bullet
Assume that
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)=G_{\mathrm{sc}}(\Phi,R)
is simply connected, and the ring
R
is finitely generated, then by
[
29
]
the group
K_{1}(\Phi,R)=G_{\mathrm{sc}}(\Phi,R)/E_{\mathrm{sc}}(\Phi,R)
is nilpotent. Further assume that
K_{1}(\Phi,R)
is finitely generated (the question whether this groups is always finitely generated for a finitely generated ring
R
is an old open problem). Then
K_{1}(\Phi,R)
is polycyclic, see
[
55
, Chapter 4A, Exercise 1.2]
. It then follows that
K_{1}(\Phi,R)
is boundedly generated by
m
-th powers and the set of its generators, which is finite. Indeed, the group
\mathbb{Z}
is boundedly generated by
m
-th powers and a generator, because
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
is finite; now for a polycyclic group it follows by induction. Thus we reduce the question to an elementary group. Similarly, we can do this for a non simply connected group
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
, if we assume additionally that its quotient by the image of
G_{\mathrm{sc}}(\Phi,R)
is finitely generated; this quotient is a subgroup in the cohomology group
H^{1}_{\mathrm{fppf}}(R,\mu)
, where
\mu=\mathrm{Ker}(G_{\mathrm{sc}}(\Phi,-)\to G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,-))
, see, for example,
[
40
, Chapter III, Lemma 2.6.1]
.

\bullet
In papers
[
66
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
it is proved that an element from the Chevalley group of type
\Phi
, where
\mathrm{rk}\,\Phi\geqslant 2
and
\Phi\neq F_{4},G_{2},E_{8}
, over a polynomial ring with coefficients in a small-dimensional ring can be reduced to an element of certain proper subsystem subgroup by a bounded number of elementary root elements (the bound depends on the number of variables). By induction, for the ring
\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
we can reduce such an element to a subsystem subgroup of type
A_{1}
. Since every finitely generated ring is a quotient of
\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, and the map on corresponding elementary subgroups is surjective, we conclude that for a finitely generated ring any element from the elementary subgroup can be reduced to a subsystem subgroup of type
A_{1}
. Note that the systems
\Phi\neq F_{4},G_{2},E_{8}
are inessential for us here, because for them the all Chevalley groups are adjoint; hence we can apply Theorem
2.7
to them, so we don’t need bounded generation.

\bullet
The question whether the group
G(\Phi,\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}])
is boundedly generated by elementary root elements is an open problem. The educated guesses of the specialists are that the answer is negative. Now it is natural to formulate a weaker version of that problem:
is the group
G(\Phi,\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}])
boundedly generated by commutators,
m
-th powers, and a finite set, where
m
is as above?

3.
Basic notations and useful facts about Chevalley groups

We refer for the facts related to root systems and semisimple Lie algebras to
[
10
]
and
[
32
]
.
More detailed information about elementary Chevalley groups is
contained in the books
[
62
]
, and about the Chevalley
groups (also over rings) in
[
67
]
,
[
68
]
,
[
25
]
(see also
later references in these papers).

We always fix some arbitrary irreducible root system
\Phi
of the
rank
\ell>1
.

We consider an arbitrary Chevalley group
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
,
constructed by the root system
\Phi
, a ring
R
and a lattice
\mathcal{P}
of a
representation
\pi
of the corresponding Lie algebra. It is known,
that Chevalley group is defined by these three parameters. If we consider an elementary
Chevalley group, we denote it by
E_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
.

Relations between Chevalley groups and the corresponding elementary
subgroups is an important problem in the theory of Chevalley
groups over rings. For elementary Chevalley groups there exists a
convenient system of generators
x_{\alpha}(\xi)
,
\alpha\in\Phi
,

\xi\in R
, and all relations between these generators are well-known.
For general Chevalley groups it is not always true.

If
R
is an algebraically closed field, then

G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)=E_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)

for any weight lattice
\mathcal{P}
. This equality is not true even for the
case of fields, which are not algebraically closed.

If
\Phi
is an irreducible root system of rank
\ell>1
, then
E_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
is always normal in
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
(see
[
65
]
,
[
29
]
) and even is characteristic in it.

We will use the following result about normal subgroups of elementary Chevalley groups (see
[
3
]
).

Note that for every ideal
I
of
R
the natural mapping
R\to R/I
induces a homomorphism

\lambda_{I}:G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)\to G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R/I).

If
I
is a proper ideal of
R
, then the kernel of
\lambda_{I}
is a non-central normal subgroup of
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
.

Definition 3.1
.

We denote the inverse image of the center of
G_{\mathcal{P}}(R/I)
under
\lambda_{I}
by
Z_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R,I)
.

By
E_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R,I)
we denote the minimal normal subgroup of
E_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
which contains all

x_{\alpha}(t)
,
\alpha\in\Phi
,
t\in I
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let the rank of an irreducible root system
\Phi
is greater than
1
. If a
subgroup
H
of
E_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
is normal in
E_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
, then

E_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R,I)\leqslant H\leqslant Z_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R,I)\cap E_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)

for some uniquely defined ideal
I
of the ring
R
.

For adjoint Chevalley groups (which always have trivial center) the group
Z_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R,I)
coincides with the group
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R,I)
consisting of all matrices
A
from
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
such that
A-E
have coefficients from
I
.

To prove our main theorem, we will need to study isomorphisms between central quotients of Chevalley groups. This result is of interest by itself.

Theorem 3.3
.

If
\Phi
,
\Psi
are irreducible root systems of ranks
>1
,
\mathcal{P}_{1}
,
\mathcal{P}_{2}
are any corresponding lattices,
R
and
S
are good commutative rings with
1
, then if the groups

PG_{\mathcal{P}_{1}}(\Phi,R)=G_{\mathcal{P}_{1}}(\Phi,R)/Z(G_{\mathcal{P}_{1}}(\Phi,R))\text{ and }PG_{\mathcal{P}_{2}}(\Psi,S)=G_{\mathcal{P}_{2}}(\Psi,S)/Z(G_{\mathcal{P}_{2}}(\Psi,S))

are isomorphic, then the rings
R
and
S
are isomorphic and the systems
\Phi
and
\Psi
coincide.

Proof.

Since the (elementary Chevalley) subgroups
E_{\mathcal{P}_{1}}(\Phi,R)
and
E_{\mathcal{P}_{2}}(\Psi,S)
are normal in the corresponding Chevalley groups
G_{\mathcal{P}_{1}}(\Phi,R)
and
G_{\mathcal{P}_{2}}(\Psi,S)
, then their quotients by their centers are normal subgroups in
PG_{\mathcal{P}_{1}}(\Phi,R)
and
PG_{\mathcal{P}_{2}}(\Psi,S)
. Since for any elementary Chevalley group
E_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
the quotient group by its center is elementary adjoint Chevalley group
E_{ad}(\Phi,R)
, then we can assume that

E_{ad}(\Phi,R)\vartriangleleft PG_{\mathcal{P}_{1}}(\Phi,R)\text{ and }E_{ad}(\Psi,S)\vartriangleleft PG_{\mathcal{P}_{2}}(\Psi,S).

Since the groups
PG_{\mathcal{P}_{1}}(\Phi,R)
and
PG_{\mathcal{P}_{2}}(\Psi,S)
are isomorphic (let us denote an isomorphism by
f
), then we can suppose that the group
G=PG_{\mathcal{P}_{2}}(\Psi,S)
contains two normal subgroups:
G_{1}=E_{ad}(\Psi,S)
and
G_{2}=f(E_{ad}(\Phi,R))
.

The subgroups
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
satisfy the property
(*)
:

C_{G}(G_{i})=e,\quad i=1,2,

or
the centralizers of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
in
G
are trivial.

Since both
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are normal, if their intersection is trivial, then any
g_{1}\in G_{1}
commutes with any
g_{2}\in G_{2}
, which is impossible by this property
(*)
. Therefore
H:=G_{1}\cap G_{2}
is a nontrivial normal subgroup of
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
and
G
. According to Theorem
3.2

E_{ad}(\Psi,S,I)\leqslant H\leqslant G_{\mathcal{P}_{2}}(\Psi,S,I)\cap E_{\mathcal{P}_{2}}(\Psi,S)

and

f(E_{ad}(\Phi,R,J))\leqslant H\leqslant f(G_{\mathcal{P}_{1}}(\Phi,R,J)\cap E_{\mathcal{P}_{1}}(\Phi,R)),

where
I
is an ideal of
S
and
J
is an ideal of
R
.

Let us factorize the group
G_{\mathcal{P}_{2}}(\Psi,S)
by the ideal
I
: every matrix from
G_{\mathcal{P}_{2}}(\Psi,S)
will be taken modulo
I
. Clearly the groups
PG_{\mathcal{P}_{2}}(\Psi,S)
and
G_{1}=E_{ad}(\Psi,S)
also will be factorized and will be mapped to
PG_{\mathcal{P}_{2}}(\Psi,S/I)
and
G_{1}^{I}=E_{ad}(\Phi,S/I)
, respectively. The normal subgroup
G_{2}
also will be mapped to the normal subgroup
G_{2}^{I}
of
PG_{\mathcal{P}_{2}}(\Psi,S/I)
under this factorization, and the subgroup
H
will be mapped to
\{e\}
.

Therefore we have the following situation:
the group
PG_{\mathcal{P}_{2}}(\Psi,S/I)
contains a normal subgroup
G_{2}^{I}
intersecting with
E_{ad}(\Psi,S/I)
by
\{e\}
. By property
(*)

G_{2}^{I}
is trivial, which means
G_{2}=H
and
f(E_{ad}(\Phi,R))\subseteq E_{ad}(\Psi,S)
in the initial group. From symmetry
f(E_{ad}(\Phi,R))=E_{ad}(\Psi,S)
and therefore
R\cong S
and
\Phi=\Psi
.

∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
2.7

First of all we will give the main scheme of the proof in simple words, and after that the detailed proof will follow.

We need in general to describe all central quotients of Chevalley groups of some given type
PG_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,-)
over all good rings by one sentence.

Let us suppose that the set of parameters
M=\{\overline{x_{\alpha}}(1)\mid\alpha\in\Phi\}
is fixed. Then (see Lemma
4.1
) for any
\alpha\in\Phi
the subgroup
X_{\alpha}:=\{\overline{x_{\alpha}}(t)\mid t\in R\}
is definable with the parameters from
M
(and the defining formula is the same for all rings). According to Lemma
4.2
on each this set we can (by the same formulas for all rings
R
) define operations of addition and multiplication so that
X_{\alpha}
with these operations becomes isomorphic to the corresponding ring
R
.

We deal with some algebraic group scheme and therefore for our type
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,-)
we can fix the dimension
n
of the corresponding
\,\mathrm{GL}\,_{n}(-)
and the set of the corresponding algebraic equations.

Therefore the elements of
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
can be defined as
n\times n
-matrices (
n^{2}
-tuples) of elements of
R
satisfying given equations.

These arguments give us mutual interpretability of
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
or
PG_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
and
R
(given by same code for all rings).
To show bi-interpretability between
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
and
R
we need two more things:

(1) Present a formula stating an isomorphism between
R
and the matrices representing
R
in
PG_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
, i. e., the matrices
\overline{x_{\alpha}}(t)
for some fixed
\alpha
. It is easy, since we know the form of
x_{\alpha}(t)
in
\,\mathrm{GL}\,_{n}(R)
. The similar formula is of course possible for the Chevalley group
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
itself, not only for its central quotient.

(2) Present a formula stating an isomorphism between the group
PG_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
(as an abstract group) and the matrices from
\,\mathrm{GL}\,_{n}(R)
satisfying the corresponding relations (see Lemma
4.4
). To do this we will use
[
63
, Corollary 5.2]
, where it was proved that there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any commutative ring
R
, any
g\in G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
, and any
\beta\in\Phi
the element
x_{\beta}(1)^{g}
is a product of at most
N
elementary root elements. Therefore for any
g\in G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
we can describe its action on the elements of the set
M
by a formula and show that two actions coincide if and only if these are the same elements in
PG_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
.

Now we just need to prove that our parameters from
M
are regularly definable. The idea here is that we take some parameters
\{g_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi\}
and postulate that by above methods we define for any
\alpha
subgroups
Y_{\alpha}
with ring structures (and they are all isomorphic to some ring
S
), then with the help of this
S
we describe (by the scheme with matrices and equations, by a single formula) the group
PG_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,S)
and postulate that this is our initial group itself and the elements
g_{\alpha}
correspond to
\overline{x_{\alpha}}(1)
. According to the description of isomorphisms of central quotients of Chevalley groups the rings
R
and
S
are isomorphic. The proof yields, additionally, that the groups
PG_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,-)
are defined by one formula and that our parameters are defined up to automorphisms.

Now we will show the detailed proof.

The first lemma essentially says that the root subgroups are definable.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\beta\in\Phi
, then there is a
(|\Phi|+1)
-ary formula
\IsElUnip_{\beta}(g,g_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi)
such that for any commutative ring
R
and any
g\in PG_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
we have

\left[PG_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)\models\IsElUnip_{\beta}(g,\overline{x}_{\alpha}(1)\mid\alpha\in\Phi)\right]\Longleftrightarrow\left[\exists\xi\in R\mid g=\overline{x}_{\beta}(\xi)\right].

Proof.

Follows from the proofs of
[
17
, Propositions 3 and 4]
in case of adjoint group. Note that the difference between
G_{\,\mathrm{ad}\,}(\Phi,R)
and
PG_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
does not affect these proofs.
∎

The next lemma allows to realize ring operations in terms of elementary unipotents.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\beta\in\Phi
, then there are
(|\Phi|+3)
-ary formulas
\Add_{\beta}(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi)
and
\Mult_{\beta}(g_{1},g_{2},g_{3},g_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi)
such that for any commutative ring
R
and any
\xi_{1}
,
\xi_{2}
,
\xi_{3}\in R
we have

\left[PG_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)\models\Add_{\beta}(\overline{x_{\beta}}(\xi_{1}),\overline{x_{\beta}}(\xi_{2}),\overline{x_{\beta}}(\xi_{3}),\overline{x_{\alpha}}(1)\mid\alpha\in\Phi)\right]\Longleftrightarrow\left[\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}=\xi_{3}\right]\text{,}

and

\left[PG_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)\models\Mult_{\beta}(\overline{x_{\beta}}(\xi_{1}),\overline{x_{\beta}}(\xi_{2}),\overline{x_{\beta}}(\xi_{3}),\overline{x_{\alpha}}(1)\mid\alpha\in\Phi)\right]\Longleftrightarrow\left[\xi_{1}\xi_{2}=\xi_{3}\right]\text{.}

Proof.

Follows from the proof of
[
17
, Theorem 4]
.
∎

As we mentioned above, let us fix some faithful representation of
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,-)
, i.e a closed embedding of algebraic group schemes
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,-)\to\,\mathrm{GL}\,(n,-)
for some
n
(see
[
25
]
); and let us fix some root
\alpha_{0}\in\Phi
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
N
be a positive integer, and let
\beta,\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{N}\in\Phi
. Then there exists an
N+n^{2}+|\Phi|
-ary formula
\DecompConj_{\beta,\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{N}}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{N},g_{i,j}\mid 1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n,g_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi)
such that for any commutative ring

.

Firstly, the implication from the right to the left in item (1) holds true by construction of
\varphi
.

Secondly, item (3) also holds by construction.

Now let us prove the implication from the left to the right in item (1). Assume that for some
g_{\alpha},g_{k},g_{ij}^{(k)}\in G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
we have

G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)\models\varphi(g_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi;\;g_{k}\mid 1\leqslant k\leqslant l;\;g_{ij}^{(k)}\mid 1\leqslant k\leqslant l,\;1\leqslant i,j\leqslant n)\text{.}

By item (3), there exists an isomorphism
f\colon G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,S)\to G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
for some ring
S
such that

\bullet

f(g_{\alpha})=x_{\alpha}(1)
for all
\alpha\in\Phi
;

\bullet

f(g_{ij}^{(k)})=x_{\alpha_{0}}(\xi_{ij}^{(k)})
for all
1\leqslant k\leqslant l
for some
\xi_{ij}^{(k)}\in R
;

\bullet

\varpi(f(g_{k}))
is the matrix
(\xi_{ij}^{(k)})
for all
1\leqslant k\leqslant l
.

By Theorem
3.3
the rings
R
and
S
are isomorphic (here we use that they are good). Now composing
f
with the ring isomorphism from
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,S)
to
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
, we obtain the required automorphism.

It remains to prove the item (2). By item (1) it is enough to prove that there exists a bi-interpretation of
R
and
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
such that the interpretation of
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
in
R
is absolute and the parameters for interpretation of
R
in
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
are
x_{\alpha}(1)
,
g_{k}
, and
x_{\alpha_{0}}(\xi_{ij}^{(k)})
, where
\varpi(g_{k})=(\xi_{ij}^{(k)})
.

We interpret
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
in
R
by an obvious way: elements of
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
are matrices over
R
that satisfy certain equations and the multiplication is the multiplication of matrices.

Further we interpret
R
in
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
using Lemmas
5.1
and
5.2
.

It is easy to see that the set of pairs

such that

is a set of points of a closed subscheme in
\mathbb{A}_{1}\times G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,-)
; hence the isomorphism between

and its interpretation inside
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
is defined by polynomial formulas.

The isomorphism between
G_{\mathcal{P}}(\Phi,R)
and its interpretation inside

can be defined using Lemma

∎

6.
A counterexample for the infinite center

In this section we will give the example of a Chevalley group with infinite center, which is not bi-interpretable with the corresponding ring and which is elementary equivalent to a non-Chevalley group.

Consider the Chevalley group
G_{\mathcal{P}}(A_{3},R)
, where
\mathcal{P}
is the only suitable lattice that is neither root lattice nor weight lattice (note that such lattices are possible only for systems of types
A
and
D
). That is we take the special orthogonal group
\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,R)
, i.e. the group of
6\times 6
matrices that preserves the quadratic form
x_{1}x_{6}+x_{2}x_{5}+x_{3}x_{4}
, and have the trivial Dickson invariant. The corresponding elementary subgroup will be denoted by
EO(6,R)
.

The torus
TO(6,R)\leqslant\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,R)
consists of diagonal matrices that have shape
{\rm diag}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{3}^{-1},\xi_{2}^{-1},\xi_{1}^{-1})
. where
\xi_{i}\in R^{*}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

The element
{\rm diag}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{3}^{-1},\xi_{2}^{-1},\xi_{1}^{-1})
belong to
EO(6,R)
if and only if
\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\xi_{3}\in(R^{*})^{2}
.

Proof.

The simply connected Chevalley group
G_{\mathrm{sc}}(A_{3},R)
is the group
\,\mathrm{SL}\,(4,R)
. The element from
TO(6,R)
belong to
EO(6,R)
if and only if it belong to the image of the toric subgroup of
\,\mathrm{SL}\,(4,R)
under the natural map
{\,\mathrm{SL}\,(4,R)}\to{\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,R)}
. Indeed, the toric elements of
\,\mathrm{SL}\,(4,R)
are elementary; hence so are their images. Conversely, every elementary element belongs to the image of
\,\mathrm{SL}\,(4,R)
and the preimage of
TO(6,R)
is the torus.

Now the map on tori is given by

{\rm diag}(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\zeta_{3},\zeta_{4})\mapsto{\rm diag}(\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{1}\zeta_{3},\zeta_{1}\zeta_{4},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4},\zeta_{2}\zeta_{4},\zeta_{2}\zeta_{3})\text{.}

Since
\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}=1
, it follows that the product of the first three diagonal entries of the element from the image is equal to
\zeta_{1}^{2}\in(R^{*})^{2}
. Conversely, suppose we have
\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\xi_{3}=\zeta_{1}^{2}
for some
\zeta_{1}\in R^{*}
, Then we set
\zeta_{2}=\xi_{1}\zeta_{1}^{-1}
,
\zeta_{3}=\xi_{2}\zeta_{1}^{-1}
,
\zeta_{4}=\xi_{3}\zeta_{1}^{-1}
; hence we have
\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4}=1
. and

{\rm diag}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{3}^{-1},\xi_{2}^{-1},\xi_{1}^{-1})={\rm diag}(\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2},\zeta_{1}\zeta_{3},\zeta_{1}\zeta_{4},\zeta_{3}\zeta_{4},\zeta_{2}\zeta_{4},\zeta_{2}\zeta_{3})\text{.}

∎

Lemma 6.2
.

We have

\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,\mathbb{F}_{2}[t])=EO(6,\mathbb{F}_{2}[t])

Proof.

Follows from
[
60
]
.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

Set
R=\mathbb{F}_{2}[t,\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^{2})
. Then

\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,R)=EO(6,R)\times(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\aleph_{0}}\text{.}

Proof.

The center of
\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,R)
consists of scalar matrices with the scalar being the square root of one. Thus the center is isomorphic to
\mu_{2}(R)=(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{\aleph_{0}}
. Clearly, the center commutes with
EO(6,R)
. Therefore, it remains to show that
EO(6,R)\cap Z(\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,R))=e
, and that
\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,R)
is generated by
EO(6,R)
and
Z(\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,R))
.

Let us prove the first claim. Let
g\in EO(6,R)\cap Z(\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,R))
. Since
g\in Z(\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,R))
, we have
g={\rm diag}(\xi,\ldots,\xi)
, with
\xi^{2}=1
. By Lemma
6.1
we have
\xi^{3}\in(R^{*})^{2}=\{1\}
; hence
\xi=\xi^{3}=1
.

Let us prove the second claim. Clearly,
\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,R)
is generated by
\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,\mathbb{F}_{2}[t])
and the congruence subgroup
\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,\varepsilon R,R)
. By Lemma
6.2
the group
\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,\mathbb{F}_{2}[t])
is contained in the elementary subgroup. Since
\varepsilon
is nilpotent, it follows that the congruence subgroup
\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,\varepsilon R,R)
is contained in the big Gauss cell; hence it is contained in
TO(6,R)EO(6,R)
. Therefore, it remains to show that any element of
TO(6,R)
is a product of an elementary element and a central element. Note that all the elements of
R^{*}
are square roots of one. So given an element
{\rm diag}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{3},\xi_{2},\xi_{1})\in TO(6,R)
we can multiply it by the central element
{\rm diag}(\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\xi_{3},\ldots\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\xi_{3})
, and by Lemma
6.1
the result will be in the elementary subgroup.
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

For
R=\mathbb{F}_{2}[t,\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^{2})
the group
\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,R)
is not bi-interpretable with
R
.

Proof.

Assume the converse. Then it is easy to see that the subgroup of
\,\mathrm{Aut}\,(\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,R))
that stabilises the corresponding parameters must be isomorphic to a certain subgroup of
\,\mathrm{Aut}\,(R)
. However, the group
\,\mathrm{Aut}\,(R)
is countable (because
R
is countable and the automorphism is uniquely defined by the images of
t
and
\varepsilon
); and it follows from Proposition
6.3
that for any finite subset of
\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,R)
the subgroup of
\,\mathrm{Aut}\,(\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,R))
that stabilises the elements of this set is uncountable. This is a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 6.5
.

For
R=\mathbb{F}_{2}[t,\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^{2})
there is the group
G
that is elementary equivalent to
\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,R)
, but not isomorphic to any Chevalley group over any ring.

Proof.

Set
G=EO(6,R)\times(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{I}
, where
I=[0,1]
. It follows from Proposition
6.3
that
G
is elementary equivalent to
\,\mathrm{SO}\,(6,R)
.

It is easy to see that an infinite Chevalley group over a ring must have the same cardinality as its derived subgroup. Therefore,
G
is not a Chevalley group.
∎

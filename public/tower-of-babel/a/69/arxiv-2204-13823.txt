Partial regularity of suitable weak solutions of the model arising in amorphous molecular beam epitaxy
Yanqing Wang, Yike Huang, Gang Wu and Daoguo Zhou

Abstract
In this paper, we are concerned with the precise relationship between the Hausdorff dimension of possible singular point set 𝒮 \mathcal{S} of suitable weak solutions and the parameter α \alpha in the nonlinear term in the following parabolic equation h t + h x ​ x ​ x ​ x + ∂ x ​ x | h x | α = f . h_{t}+h_{xxxx}+\partial_{xx}|h_{x}|^{\alpha}=f. It is shown that when 5 / 3 ≤ α < 7 / 3 5/3\leq\alpha<7/3 , the 3 ​ α − 5 α − 1 \frac{3\alpha-5}{\alpha-1} -dimensional parabolic Hausdorff measure of 𝒮 \mathcal{S} is zero, which generalizes the recent corresponding work of Ozánski and Robinson in [ 31 , SIAM J. Math. Anal. 51: 228–255, 2019] for α = 2 \alpha=2 and f = 0 f=0 . The same result is valid for a 3D modified Navier-Stokes system.

Introduction

The fourth-order parabolic equation describing dynamic crystal growth in materials science is given by

h_{t}+h_{xxxx}+\partial_{xx}|h_{x}|^{\alpha}=f,\quad\alpha>1.

(1.1)

Here,
h
represent the height of a crystalline layer.
The surface growth model (
1.1
) plays an important role in molecular-beam-epitaxy (MBE) process and its physical background can be found in
[
23
,
36
,
2
,
18
,
35
]
.

Considering the
diffusion term
h_{xx}
due to evaporation-condensation on the left hand side of (
1.1

[
35
]
constructed the global mild solution with
1<\alpha\leq 5/3
and established the global weak solutions with
5/3<\alpha<10/3
.
As a special case of equation (
1.1
) with
\alpha=2
and
f=0
,

h_{t}+h_{xxxx}+\partial_{xx}|h_{x}|^{2}=0,

(1.2)

this equation is known as
the conserved Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (Kuramoto-Sivashinsky) equation.
Recently,
starting from the work of Blömer and Romito
[
6
]
,
the mathematical study of equation (
1.2
)
attracts a lot of attention (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
9
,
30
,
31
,
12
]
and references therein), since its shares similar features to
the 3D Navier-Stokes equations.
A celebrated result of the 3D Navier-Stokes system
is that 1-dimensional Hausdorff measure of
singular set of its suitable weak solutions is
zero. This is so-called Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg theorem
[
10
]
. Notice that most recent generalized Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg theorem
[
38
,
32
,
13
,
11
]
mainly interpret how the
fractional dissipation

(-\Delta)^{\alpha}
affects
the regularity of suitable
weak solutions in the 3D Navier-Stokes equations. It seems that there are few works involving
how the nonlinear terms affects
the regularity of suitable weak solutions in the Navier-Stokes equations.

We switch our attention to the surface growth model.
In
[
31
]
, Ozánski and Robinson first studied the
partial regularity of suitable weak solution and successfully
extended Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg theorem to equation (
1.2
) via the following
\varepsilon
-regularity criterion involving dimensionless quantity

\limsup_{\varrho\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{\varrho}\int_{t-\varrho^{4}}^{t+\varrho^{4}}\int^{x+\varrho}_{x-\varrho}|h_{yy}|^{2}dyd\tau\leq\varepsilon,

(1.3)

which means that
h
is Hölder continuous at point
(x_{0},t_{0})
.
Based on this, it is shown that 1-dimensional Hausdorff measure of the set of potential singular points
is zero. Here, a point is said to be a regular point to (
1.2

h
is
Hölder continuous in some neighborhood
of this point. The rest points will be called singular points and denotes by
\mathcal{S}
.
To this end, they applied the blow-up technology developed by Lin
[
29
]
and Ladyzenskaja and Seregin
[
27
]
to the suitable
weak solutions of equation (

\varepsilon
-regularity criterion below

\frac{1}{\varrho}\int_{t-\varrho^{4}}^{t+\varrho^{4}}\int^{x+\varrho}_{x-\varrho}|h_{y}|^{3}dyd\tau\leq\varepsilon.

(1.4)

Subsequently, the higher regularity of suitable weak solutions under
the
\varepsilon
-regularity criterion (
1.4

[
9
]
. The generalization of

-regularity criteria (

]
.

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{u_{i}}_{t}-\Delta u_{i}+u\cdot\nabla u^{\alpha-1}_{i}+\partial_{i}\Pi=0,i=1,2,3,\alpha>1,\\

\displaystyle E_{\alpha+1}(u;\,2r)\leq C\left(\dfrac{\rho}{r}\right)^{\frac{4\alpha-6}{\alpha-1}}E^{\frac{5-\alpha}{4}}(u;\,\rho)E^{\frac{3\alpha-3}{4}}_{\ast}(u;\,\rho)+C\left(\dfrac{r}{\rho}\right)^{\frac{3-\alpha}{\alpha-1}}E_{\alpha+1}(u;\,\rho),

(4.9)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{1/4}P_{{}^{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}}(2r)\leq C\varepsilon^{1/4}\left(\dfrac{\rho}{r}\right)^{\frac{4\alpha-6}{\alpha-1}}E_{{}^{{\alpha+1}}}(u;\,\rho)+C\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{\frac{3\alpha-1}{\alpha(\alpha-1)}}\varepsilon^{1/4}P_{{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}}(\rho).

Before going further, we write

G(r):=E_{\alpha+1}(u;\,r)+\varepsilon^{3/16}E(u;\,r)+\varepsilon^{3/16}E_{\ast}(u;\,r)+\varepsilon^{1/4}P_{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}(r).

(4.10)

Plugging (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) into (
4.10
), we arrive at

\displaystyle G(r)\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{\frac{3(\alpha+1)}{16(\alpha-1)}}+CE_{\alpha+1}(u;\,2r)+C\varepsilon^{\frac{3(\alpha+1)}{16\alpha}}P_{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}(2r)+C\varepsilon^{1/4}P_{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}(r)

(4.11)

\displaystyle{\leq}

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{\frac{3(\alpha+1)}{16(\alpha-1)}}+C\left(\dfrac{\rho}{r}\right)^{\frac{4\alpha-6}{\alpha-1}}E^{\frac{5-\alpha}{4}}(u;\,\rho)E^{\frac{3\alpha-3}{4}}_{\ast}(u;\,\rho)+C\left(\dfrac{r}{\rho}\right)^{\frac{3-\alpha}{\alpha-1}}E_{\alpha+1}(u;\,\rho)

\displaystyle+C\left(\dfrac{\rho}{r}\right)^{\frac{4\alpha-6}{\alpha-1}}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}E_{{}^{{\alpha+1}}}(u;\,\rho)+C\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{\frac{3\alpha-1}{\alpha(\alpha-1)}}\varepsilon^{1/4}P_{{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}}(\rho).

Since
\frac{5-\alpha}{4}<1
, one can apply the iteration method as
[
10
]
to show that there exist a positive constant
r_{1}
, such that

E_{\alpha+1}(u;\,r)\leq\varepsilon
for any
r\leq r_{1}.

Using the local energy inequality (
4.7
) and the decay estimates (
4.9
), we get

\displaystyle

\displaystyle E(u;\,r)+E_{\ast}(u;\,r)+P_{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}(r)

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(E_{\alpha+1}(u;\,2r))^{\frac{2}{\alpha+1}}+CE_{\alpha+1}(u;\,2r)+CP_{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}(2r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C[\left(\dfrac{\rho}{r}\right)^{\frac{4\alpha-6}{\alpha-1}}E_{\alpha+1}(u;\,\rho)]^{\frac{2}{\alpha+1}}+C\left(\dfrac{\rho}{r}\right)^{\frac{4\alpha-6}{\alpha-1}}E_{\alpha+1}(u;\,\rho)

\displaystyle+C\left(\dfrac{\rho}{r}\right)^{\frac{4\alpha-6}{\alpha-1}}E_{{}^{{\alpha+1}}}(u;\,\rho)+C\left(\frac{r}{\rho}\right)^{\frac{3\alpha-1}{\alpha(\alpha-1)}}P_{{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}}(\rho).

Now, we can apply the iteration argument as above once again to show that, there exists a positive constant

such that
E(u;\,r)+E_{\ast}(u;\,r)+P_{\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}}(r)\leq\varepsilon_{0}
for any

∎

4.3
Proof of Theorem
1.7

The key estimate for the proof of Theorem
1.7
is to show

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\Omega}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\leq C\|u\|^{\frac{2q(\alpha-1)}{q-3\alpha+3}}_{L^{q}({\Omega})}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\Omega})},q>3\alpha-3.

(4.13)

Taking
q=6
in the latter inequality and using the Sobolev inequality, we get

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\Omega}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\leq C\|\nabla u\|^{\frac{2+2\alpha}{3-\alpha}}_{L^{2}({\Omega})},

which leads to the local well-posedness of initial data in
H^{1}(\Omega)
. In addition, the Gronwall inequality and estimate (
4.13
) mean the rest result of this theorem.

Proof of estimate (
4.13
).

Multiplying the modified Navier-Stokes system (
1.6
) by
\partial_{k}\partial_{k}u
, integrating over
\Omega
, using div
u
=0, and integrating by
parts, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla^{2}u|dx

\displaystyle=\sum^{3}_{i=1}\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\nabla u_{i}^{\alpha-1}\partial_{k}\partial_{k}{u_{i}dx}.

(4.14)

From Hölder’s inequality, we have

{I=}\sum^{3}_{i=1}\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\nabla u_{i}^{\alpha-1}\partial_{k}\partial_{k}{u_{i}dx}\leq C\||u|^{\alpha-1}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{\alpha-1}}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2\alpha+2}}(\Omega)}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

It follows from the interpolation inequality and Sobolev’s embedding that

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{q-2\alpha+2}}(\Omega)}\leq\|\nabla u\|^{\frac{3\alpha-3}{q}}_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u\|^{\frac{q+3-3\alpha}{q}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|\nabla^{2}u\|^{\frac{3\alpha-3}{q}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u\|^{\frac{q+3-3\alpha}{q}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(4.15)

We derive from Young’s inequality and the latter inequality that

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq{C\|u\|}^{\alpha-1}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\|\|\nabla^{2}u\|^{1+\frac{3\alpha-3}{q}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u\|^{\frac{q+3-3\alpha}{q}}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(4.16)

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|^{\frac{2q(\alpha-1)}{q-3\alpha+3}}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{32}\|\nabla^{2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

where the fact that
q>3\alpha-3
were used.

Collecting above estimates and absorbing the terms containing
\|\nabla^{2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
by the left-hand
side in (
4.14
), we deduce that

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\leq C\|u\|^{\frac{2q(\alpha-1)}{q-3\alpha+3}}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(4.17)

The desired estimate is derived.
∎

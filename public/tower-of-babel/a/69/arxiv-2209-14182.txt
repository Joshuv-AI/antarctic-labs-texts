Abstract
. This paper incorporates the theory of Hochschild homology into our program on log motives. We discuss a geometric definition of logarithmic Hochschild homology of animated pre-log rings and construct an André-Quillen type spectral sequence. The latter degenerates for derived log smooth maps between discrete pre-log rings. We employ this to show a logarithmic version of the Hochschild-Kostant-Rosenberg theorem and that logarithmic Hochschild homology is representable in the category of log motives. Among the applications, we deduce a generalized residue sequence involving blow-ups of log schemes.

1.3.
The logarithmic André–Quillen spectral sequence

One way to prove the HKR-theorem for ordinary Hochschild homology is to exhibit
\HH_{*}(A/R)
as the abutment of an
André–Quillen spectral sequence
. Indeed, one readily shows that the HKR-theorem holds for
A=R[x]
, from which it follows that it holds for any free commutative
R
-algebra. Using this, results of Quillen
[
45
, Theorem 8.1]
yield a strongly convergent spectral sequence

(1.4)

E^{2}_{p,q}=\pi_{p}(\bigwedge\nolimits_{A}^{q}{\mathbb{L}}_{A/R})\implies\pi_{p+q}(A\otimes_{A\otimes_{R}^{\mathbb{L}}A}^{\mathbb{L}}A)

relating the derived wedge powers of
{\mathbb{L}}_{A/R}
and the derived self-intersections of the (derived)
“diagonal embedding”
A\otimes_{R}^{\mathbb{L}}A\to A
.
If
R\to A
is smooth,
then
{\mathbb{L}}_{A/R}\simeq\Omega^{1}_{A/R}[0]
.
Since the derived tensor product in (
1.4
) computes
\HH_{*}(A/R)
,
this finishes the proof.

To apply this proof strategy in the context of animated log rings,
we define log Hochschild homology
{\rm logHH}((A,M)/(R,P))
to be the derived self-intersections of a
derived version of Kato–Saito’s log diagonal embedding
[
28
, §4]
.
We refer to Definition
5.3
for details of this construction.
The fact that our geometric description agrees with the direct generalization to animated log rings of
Rognes’ notion of log
\HH
for discrete log rings
[
49
, Definition 3.21]
is the content of the
following result.

Proposition 1.4

(
Proposition

5.6
)
.

There is a natural equivalence between Rognes’ log Hochschild homology and

{\rm logHH}((A,M)/(R,P))
.

Our definition of log
\HH
is inspired by the observation that in the underived context,
the conormal bundle of Kato–Saito’s log diagonal coincides with the module of log Kähler differentials

\Omega^{1}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}

[
28
, Corollary 4.2.8(ii)]
.
Passing to the derived context begets Gabber’s log cotangent complex
{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
essentially
by definition,
see Proposition
3.17
.
Combining this observation with
Proposition

1.4
, we can apply
Quillen’s construction once again to obtain a strongly convergent spectral sequence

(1.5)

E^{2}_{p,q}=\pi_{p}(\bigwedge\nolimits_{A}^{q}{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)})\implies\pi_{p+q}\logHH((A,M)/(R,P))

relating the derived wedge powers of Gabber’s log cotangent complex with log Hochschild homology.
This is the point of the proof where the adjective “derived” in the statement of Theorem
1.1

comes into play: In general,
Gabber’s log cotangent complex is
not
the log differentials concentrated in degree zero for log smooth maps, see Example
4.9
. Thus the log André–Quillen spectral sequence generally fails to collapse for log smooth maps. For this reason, it is necessary to impose additional flatness assumptions on the log smooth map
(R,P)\to(A,M)
: It suffices to require that the map
{\mathbb{Z}}[P]\to{\mathbb{Z}}[M]
is flat,
a condition that holds for any integral log smooth map.

1.5.
Étale base change for log Hochschild homology

Using the André-Quillen spectral sequence,
Weibel–Geller
[
62
]
proved the canonical equivalence

B\otimes_{A}\HH(A/R)\xrightarrow{\simeq}\HH(B/R)

for
étale maps
A\to B
of commutative
R
-algebras.
Their result follows from the transitivity sequence for the cotangent complex combined with the vanishing
of the relative cotangent complex for étale morphisms.
As Gabber’s log cotangent complex enjoys a similar transitivity property,
the spectral sequence (
1.5
) yields:

Theorem 1.6

(Theorem
5.22
)
.

Let
(R,P)
be an animated pre-log ring. A map
(A,M)\to(B,N)
of
(R,P)
-algebras is derived log étale if and only if the canonical map

B\otimes_{A}\logHH((A,M)/(R,P))\to\logHH((B,N)/(R,P))

is an equivalence.

Theorem
1.6
applies for example to tamely ramified extensions of discrete valuation rings (for which the classical result doesn’t hold).
The adjective “derived” is necessary here for the same reason as in Theorem
1.1
:
There exist
log étale maps for which Gabber’s log cotangent complex does not vanish, see again Example
4.9
.

Remark 1.7
.

In this work we use Gabber’s cotangent complex for log schemes.
Olsson has proposed another definition
[
43
]
.
Each has its own advantages and disadvantages,
and there is no choice of log cotangent complex satisfying all desirable properties
[
43
, §7]
.
The main reasons for our choice are the following:
1) Gabber’s cotangent complex enjoys the same kind of transitivity property that the classical cotangent complex has,
and 2) Gabber’s cotangent complex admits a straightforward adaptation to animated log rings.

1.8.
Extension to fs log schemes

Hochschild homology of rings extends to schemes owing to the étale base change theorem
[
62
]
.
Likewise,
log Hochschild homology of log rings extends to log schemes by appeal to Theorem
1.6
.

Several Grothendieck topologies are available in the category of log schemes. We can broadly divide them into two classes: the
strict
topologies, where the coverings are defined on the underlying schemes, and the log structures are simply pulled back; and the
log
topologies, where a non-trivial modification of the log structure happens along the covering. Among these, the
dividing Nisnevich
topology,
which we review in Section
6
,
plays a crucial role for the logarithmic motivic homotopy theory in
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
.

While log Hochschild homology satisfies strict étale descent (in fact, it satisfies
integral
étale descent) thanks to

Theorem

1.6
, it does not satisfy dividing Nisnevich descent;
see Example
7.28
.
To remedy this,
we need to consider the dividing Nisnevich localization of log Hochschild homology, which we now briefly discuss.
In this process,
we fix a base fs log scheme
S
and define
\logHH(X/S)
for every morphism of fs log schemes
X\to S
.
The precise construction carried out in
Sections

6
and
7
involves arrow categories, and produces two sheaves of complexes,
\logHH(-/-)
in
\mathrm{Shv}_{sNis}(\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lAff}))
and
\logHH^{-}_{dNis}(-)
in
\mathrm{Shv}_{dNis}(\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lAff}))
. The dividing Nisnevich sheafification process is well-controlled:
in fact, if
X\to S
is integral log smooth,
Proposition
8.8
shows that the localization map

\logHH(X/S)\rightarrow\logHH_{dNis}(X/S)
is an equivalence. Along the way, we obtain similar results for the dividing Nisnevich localized log cotangent complex
\mathbb{L}^{dNis}
.

1.9.
Representability of log Hochschild homology

We refer to
[
9
]
for an overview of log motives explaining our choice
\overline{\Box}=(\mathbb{P}^{1},\infty)

of the unit interval.
For an fs log scheme
S
,
the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}^{\mathrm{eff}}(S)
of effective motives without transfers is defined as the
\overline{\Box}
-localization
of the
\infty
-category of dividing Nisnevich sheaves of chain complexes of abelian groups on
\mathrm{lSm}/S
,
see also
[
8
]
.
Here
\mathrm{lSm}/S
denotes the category of fs log schemes log smooth over
S
.
We claim that the dividing Nisnevich sheaf
\logHH_{dNis}(-/S)
is
\overline{\Box}
-invariant and hence representable in
\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}^{\mathrm{eff}}(S)
.
Owing to the logarithmic André–Quillen spectral sequence (
1.5
),
it suffices to show that there is a canonical equivalence

\mathbb{L}^{dNis}(X\times\overline{\Box}\to S)\simeq\mathbb{L}^{dNis}(X\to S)

for all
X\in\mathrm{lSm}/S
.
Proposition
7.27
yields a canonical equivalence

\mathbb{L}^{dNis}(X\to S)\simeq R\Gamma_{Nis}(\underline{X},\Omega_{X/S}^{1}),

where
\underline{X}
denotes the underlying scheme of
X
.
Finally,
by an explicit calculation,
we have

R\Gamma_{Nis}(\underline{X},\Omega_{X/S}^{1})\simeq R\Gamma_{Nis}(\underline{X\times\overline{\Box}},\Omega_{X\times\overline{\Box}/S}^{1}).

This establishes a basic result relating logarithmic Hochschild homology and log motives.
We write
\mathrm{lSch}
for the category of noetherian fs log schemes of finite Krull dimensions and finite type morphisms.

Theorem 1.10

(Theorem
8.10
)
.

Suppose that
S
is a noetherian fs log scheme of finite Krull dimension.
Then we have

\logHH_{dNis}(-/S)\in\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}^{\mathrm{eff}}(S).

In particular, for every
X\in\mathrm{lSm}/S
, we have

\Map_{\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}^{\mathrm{eff}}(S)}(M_{S}(X),\logHH_{dNis}(-/S))=\logHH_{dNis}(X/S).

Note that if
S
has a valuative log structure (for example,
S
is the spectrum of a DVR with canonical log structure, or
S
has trivial log structure), then every morphism
f\colon X\to S
is integral, and thus the dividing Nisnevich sheafification is redundant. See Section
8
for more details.

1.11.
Residue sequences in log Hochschild homology

Cofiber sequences for log Hochschild homology as in (
1.3
) have to this point only been available for log structures generated by a single element. Theorem
1.10
allows for the results of
[
10
]
to be applied in the context of log Hochschild homology.
As we discuss in Section
9
, this perspective shows that the cofiber sequences in
log
\HH
should be thought of as generalizations of residue sequences in log geometry, as opposed to approximations of localization sequences in algebraic
K
-theory. For this reason, it seems the term “
residue sequence
” is more appropriate than
“
localization sequence
” in the context of cofiber sequences in log (topological) Hochschild homology of
[
51
]
.

Moreover, the representability result Theorem
1.10
allows us to apply the results of
[
10
]
to generalize the residue sequence in log Hochschild homology in the following way:

Theorem 1.12

(Theorem
9.5
)
.

Fix a noetherian base
S
of finite Krull dimension. If
X
is a smooth
S
-scheme and
Z\subset X
is a smooth closed subscheme, then there is a cofiber sequence

\HH(X/S)\to\logHH((\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X,E)/S)\to\HH(Z/S)[1].

In fact, a more general result holds, when a non-trivial log structure on both
X
and
Z
is allowed. See Theorem
9.5
for the exact statement.
As we explain in Remark
9.7
, one special case of Theorem
1.12
is the cofiber sequence

{\rm HH}(A/R)\to{\rm logHH}(({\rm Bl}_{(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})}{\rm Spec}(A),E)/R)\to{\rm HH}((A/(f_{1},\dots,f_{r}))/R)[1]

for a regular sequence
(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})
cutting a smooth subscheme in a smooth commutative
R
-algebra

, where
R
is a commutative ring of finite Krull dimension. This recovers the discrete version of the Rognes–Sagave–Schlichtkrull sequence for
r=1
. In other words, in order to have cofiber sequences in log Hochschild homology for log structures generated by several elements, we must leave the affine context behind and invoke blow-ups.

In Remark
9.8
we discuss the existence of residue sequences in (log) topological Hochschild homology

(1.6)

{\rm THH}(BP\langle n\rangle)\to{\rm logTHH}(BP\langle n\rangle)\to{\rm THH}(BP\langle n-1\rangle)[1]

involving the truncated Brown–Peterson spectrum

for which there is provably no analogous localization sequence in algebraic
K
-theory.
In a slightly different setup,
a version of this sequence will appear in the forthcoming work of Ausoni–Bayındır–Moulinos.
Using an appropriate notion of blow-ups in spectral algebraic geometry,
we suspect that Theorem
1.12
admits a generalization to log topological Hochschild homology,
and that this will give rise to the generalization of the sequence (

).
We
refer to Remark
9.8
for further discussion in this direction.

2.
Affine derived logarithmic geometry

The definition of Hochschild homology, even in the classical case, is inherently derived and better understood in the setting of derived algebraic geometry. In order to pursue this point of view in the logarithmic context,
let us recall from
[
55
, §2, 3]
the notion of animated (therein modelled by simplicial) (pre-)log rings.

2.1.
Logarithmic morphisms

We begin with the following basic definition.

Definition 2.2
.

We denote by
\mathrm{sCMon}
the category of simplicial commutative monoids.
By elementary model category techniques (see e.g.
[
55
, Proposition 2.1]
), this admits a proper simplicial combinatorial model structure in which a map is a fibration or a weak equivalence precisely when the underlying map of simplicial sets is so in the (standard) Kan–Quillen model structure. This is referred to as the
standard
model structure on
\mathrm{sCMon}
.
We write
{\rm Ani(CMon)}
for the associated
\infty
-category of simplicial commutative monoids,
which we shall refer to as the
\infty
-category of
animated (commutative) monoids
. We remark that
{\rm Ani(CMon)}
is presentable and in particular cocomplete
[
37
, Proposition A.3.7.6]
.
We let
\oplus
denote the coproduct in
{\rm Ani(CMon)}
.

We now proceed to define and study derived analogues of logarithmic morphisms. See

[
41
, Chapter I.4.1]
for the context of discrete commutative monoids.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
M
be an animated monoid.
Its
space of units
is the grouplike animated monoid
\mathrm{GL}_{1}(M)
of path components representing units in
\pi_{0}(M)
.

This definition allows for the following generalization of
[
41
, Definition I.4.1.1(3)]
:

Definition 2.4
.

A map
f\colon P\to M
of animated monoids is
logarithmic
if the projection

f^{-1}\mathrm{GL}_{1}(M):=P\times_{M}\mathrm{GL}_{1}(M)\to\mathrm{GL}_{1}(M)

is an equivalence. If
f\colon P\to M
is an arbitrary map of simplicial commutative monoids, we define its
logification

f^{a}\colon P^{a}\to M
by the universal property of the cocartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{-1}{\rm GL}_{1}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\rm GL}_{1}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{a}.}

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
f\colon P\to M
be a map of animated monoids.
Then the logification
f^{a}\colon P^{a}\to M
is the initial logarithmic map under
f
.

Proof.

By construction,
the composite map
(f^{a})^{-1}{\rm GL}_{1}(M)\xrightarrow{}P^{a}\xrightarrow{f^{a}}M
is equivalent to the inclusion

{\rm GL}_{1}(M)\to M
, so that
f^{a}
is logarithmic.
The universal property of a pushout verifies the remaining claim.
∎

2.6.
Animated pre-log rings

Let
{\rm Ani(CRing)}

Definition 2.7
.

A

is a simplicial object in the category of pre-log rings. That is, a simplicial commutative ring

By adjunction,

Following

3.1.
The log differentials

Let
(f_{0},f_{0}^{\flat})\colon(R_{0},P_{0},\beta_{0})\to(A_{0},M_{0},\alpha_{0})
be a map of discrete pre-log rings. The log Kähler differentials
\Omega^{1}_{(A_{0},M_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0})}
are defined as the

-module

(\Omega^{1}_{A_{0}/R_{0}}\oplus(A_{0}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}M_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}))/\!\!\sim.

The equivalence relation is

-linearly generated by
(d\alpha_{0}(m),0)\sim(0,\alpha_{0}(m)\otimes\gamma_{0}(m))
and
0\sim(0,1\otimes\gamma(f_{0}^{\flat}(n)))
, where
\gamma_{0}\colon M_{0}\to M_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}
is the canonical map from
M_{0}
to its group completion. It is common to write
d\hskip 0.70007pt\mathrm{log}(m)
for the element
(0,1\otimes\gamma_{0}(m))
, so that the first relation reads
d\alpha_{0}(m)=\alpha_{0}(m)d\hskip 0.70007pt\mathrm{log}(m)
and the second relation reads
0=d\hskip 0.70007pt\mathrm{log}(f_{0}^{\flat}(n))
.
By definition, the log Kähler differentials of the canonical map
(R_{0},P_{0})\to F_{(R_{0},P_{0})}(X,Y)
are given by

(3.1)

\Omega^{1}_{F_{(R_{0},P_{0})}(X,Y)/(R_{0},P_{0})}=R_{0}\langle X\sqcup Y\rangle\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(\bigoplus_{x\in X}{\mathbb{Z}}\{d\hskip 0.70007pt\mathrm{log}(x)\}\oplus\bigoplus_{y\in Y}{\mathbb{Z}}\{{\rm d}y\}).

Let
\mathrm{PreLog}
denote the category of ordinary (i.e., discrete) pre-log rings.
The module of log Kähler differentials corepresent logarithmic derivations as in
[
41
, IV.1.1.1]

{\rm Der}_{(R_{0},P_{0})}((A_{0},M_{0}),J_{0}):={\rm Hom}_{\mathrm{PreLog}_{(R_{0},P_{0})//(A_{0},M_{0})}}((A_{0},M_{0}),(A_{0}\oplus J_{0},M_{0}\oplus J_{0}))

with values in some
A_{0}
-module

. Here the pre-log structure

on the trivial square-zero extension

is defined by

. This is the coproduct of the natural map

and the inclusion of the units

in

.

3.2.
The log differentials via the log diagonal

Recall that the module of relative differentials
\Omega^{1}_{A_{0}/R_{0}}
arises as the indecomposables of
A_{0}\otimes_{R_{0}}A_{0}\to A_{0}
, that is, the conormal bundle of the diagonal map. If
(R_{0},P_{0})\to(A_{0},M_{0})
is a map of pre-log rings, this naturally leads to the question of how the multiplication map

(A_{0}\otimes_{R_{0}}A_{0},M_{0}\oplus_{P_{0}}M_{0})\to(A_{0},M_{0})

relates to the log differentials. It is not clear what should be meant by the module of indecomposables of this map; forming the indecomposables of the underlying map of commutative rings merely recovers the ordinary module of differentials
\Omega^{1}_{A_{0}/R_{0}}
.

However, after replacing the multiplication map with its repletion, we obtain a map

((A_{0}\otimes_{R_{0}}A_{0})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[M_{0}\oplus_{P_{0}}M_{0}]}\mathbb{Z}[(M_{0}\oplus_{P_{0}}M_{0})^{\rm rep}],(M_{0}\oplus_{P_{0}}M_{0})^{\rm rep})\to(A_{0},M_{0})

of pre-log rings. Moreover, this map is strict by Corollary
2.23
. Hence it is determined by the underlying map of commutative rings, and it makes sense to define its module of indecomposables as that of the underlying map

(3.2)

(A_{0}\otimes_{R_{0}}A_{0})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[M_{0}\oplus_{P_{0}}M_{0}]}{\mathbb{Z}}[(M_{0}\oplus_{P_{0}}M_{0})^{\rm rep}]\to A_{0}

of commutative rings. As a formal consequence of
[
28
, Corollary 4.2.8(ii)]
(see also
[
41
, Remark IV.1.1.8]
), the module of indecomposables of (

) is precisely the module of log differentials
\Omega^{1}_{(A_{0},M_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0})}
. Indeed, the map (

) is a special case of Kato–Saito’s
log diagonal
.

This implies that the log derivations
{\rm Der}_{(R_{0},P_{0})}((A_{0},M_{0}),J_{0})
is naturally isomorphic to the set of augmented
A_{0}
-algebra maps

{\rm Hom}_{{\rm CRing}_{A_{0}//A_{0}}}((A_{0}\otimes_{R_{0}}A_{0})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[M_{0}\oplus_{P_{0}}M_{0}]}{\mathbb{Z}}[(M_{0}\oplus_{P_{0}}M_{0})^{\mathrm{rep}}],A_{0}\oplus J_{0}).

This works as expected because log Kähler differentials are corepresented by an
A_{0}
-module. This observation and its derived analogue serve as a starting point for our approach to logarithmic Hochschild homology.

It admits a canonical section
(A,M)\to(A\oplus J,M\oplus J)
.
We define the space
\mathrm{Der}_{(R,P)}((A,M),J)
of
(R,P)
-linear derivations of
(A,M)
with values in

as the fiber of the map

(3.3)

\Map_{{\rm Ani(PreLog)}_{(R,P)/}}((A,M),(A\oplus J,M\oplus J))\to\Map_{{\rm Ani(PreLog)}_{(R,P)/}}((A,M),(A,M))

induced by
(A\oplus J,M\oplus J)\to(A,M)
at the point
\mathrm{id}_{(A,M)}
.
As in
derived algebraic geometry
[

, 1.4.1.14]
,
we say that
(R,P)\to(A,M)
has a
cotangent complex
(or that
(A,M)
admits a

(R,P)
)
if there exists a connective
A
-module co-representing the functor

\Mod_{A}^{\rm cn}\to\mathcal{S},\quad J\mapsto\mathrm{Der}_{(R,P)}((A,M),J).

We note that such a log cotangent complex is unique up to equivalence in the
\infty
-category
\Mod_{A}^{\rm cn}
of
connective
A
-modules.

Let
(R,P)
be a simplicial pre-log ring.
By extending the definition of the log Kähler differentials
degreewise,
we obtain a functor

\Omega^{1}_{(-,-)/(R,P)}\colon{\rm sPreLog}_{(R,P)/}\to{\rm sMod}_{R}

from the category of pre-log simplicial
(R,P)
-algebras to simplicial
R
-modules.
As discussed in
[
55
, §4.2]
,
this is a left Quillen functor whose right adjoint is given by the levelwise square-zero construction

J\mapsto(A\oplus J,M\oplus J)
.
This gives rise to an adjunction on the underlying
\infty
-categories;
we denote the left adjoint by
{\mathbb{L}}
.

The following is the definition of the log cotangent complex pursued in
[
55
]
:

Definition 3.5
.

Let
(R,P)\to(A,M)
be a map of animated pre-log rings. The
log cotangent complex

{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
is the
R
-module

{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}:={\mathbb{L}}(A,M).

We note that
{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
has a natural
A
-module structure.
Following Illusie and Quillen,
we write
\bigwedge\nolimits^{i}_{A}{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
for the
i
-th derived exterior power of
{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
.

It is clear by construction that
{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
co-represents the functor of log-derivations.
In particular, every morphism of animated pre-log rings
(R,P)\to(A,M)
admits a log cotangent complex. Well-known properties of the log cotangent complex (as recorded in e.g.
[
35
, Lemma 2.2.2.5]
) that we will freely use include

(1)

strict invariance: the canonical map
{\mathbb{L}}_{A/R}\to{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
is an equivalence for
(R,P)\to(A,M)
strict;

(2)

base-change: if
(C,K)
is the pushout of the diagram
(B,N)\xleftarrow{}(R,P)\xrightarrow{}(A,M)
, then the canonical map
C\otimes_{B}{\mathbb{L}}_{(B,N)/(R,P)}\to{\mathbb{L}}_{(C,K)/(A,M)}
is an equivalence; and

(3)

transitivity: for a composite
(R,P)\to(A,M)\to(B,N)
, the sequence

(3.4)

B\otimes_{A}{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}\to{\mathbb{L}}_{(B,N)/(R,P)}\to{\mathbb{L}}_{(B,N)/(A,M)}

is a cofiber sequence of
B
-modules.

Remark 3.6
.

If
X\to S
is a map of affine pre-log derived schemes, we write
{\mathbb{L}}_{X/S}
for the log cotangent complex.

Remark 3.7
.

The cotangent complex defined above is a model for Gabber’s cotangent complex,
defined as the
A
-module

{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}:=A\otimes_{A_{\bullet}}\Omega^{1}_{(A_{\bullet},M_{\bullet})/(R,P)}

for a free
(R,P)
-algebra resolution
(A_{\bullet},M_{\bullet})\xrightarrow{\simeq}(A,M)
.
This should not be confused with Olsson’s log cotangent complex in
[
43
, Definition 3.2]
.
Note that the derived exterior powers can be modeled as

\bigwedge\nolimits^{q}_{A}{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}=A\otimes_{A_{\bullet}}\Omega^{q}_{(A_{\bullet},M_{\bullet})/(R,P)}
.

We now aim to describe some elementary properties of the log cotangent complex.
Using the definition of log derivations in (

of

The following describes the log cotangent complex in a way that is reminiscent of the

Proposition 3.8
.

There is a canonical equivalence

In Proposition

Proof of
Proposition

3.8
.

We show that there is a natural equivalence

(3.6)

{\rm Map}_{{\rm Mod}_{{\mathbb{Z}}[M]}}({\mathbb{L}}_{({\mathbb{Z}}[M],M)/({\mathbb{Z}}[P],P)},J)\simeq{\rm Map}_{{\rm Mod}_{{\mathbb{Z}}[M]}}({\mathbb{Z}}[M]\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(M^{\rm gp}/P^{\rm gp}),J)

of mapping spaces for any
{\mathbb{Z}}[M]
-module

. To see this, we first use that the log cotangent complex
{\mathbb{L}}_{({\mathbb{Z}}[M],M)/({\mathbb{Z}}[P],P)}
corepresents log derivations

{\rm Map}_{{\rm Ani(PreLog)}_{({\mathbb{Z}}[P],P)//({\mathbb{Z}}[M],M)}}(({\mathbb{Z}}[M],M),({\mathbb{Z}}[M]\oplus J,M\oplus J)).

Moreover, by the description of mapping spaces in (

), this can be simplified to

{\rm Map}_{{\rm Ani(CMon)}_{P//M}}(M,M\oplus J)\simeq{\rm Map}_{{\rm Ani(CMon)}_{M//M}}(M\oplus_{P}M,M\oplus J).

Since the map

{\rm Map}_{{\rm Ani(CMon)}_{M//M}}(M\oplus_{P}M,M\oplus J)\simeq{\rm Map}_{{\rm Ani(CMon)}_{M//M}}((M\oplus_{P}M)^{\rm rep},M\oplus J).

We now apply

(3.7)

By restriction of scalars and pullback along

{\rm Map}_{{\rm Ani(CMon)}_{M//M}}(M\oplus(M^{\mathrm{gp}}/P^{\mathrm{gp}}),M\oplus J)\simeq{\rm Map}_{\rm Ani(Ab)}(M^{\rm gp}/P^{\rm gp},J).

By extension of scalars along

∎

Derived indecomposables

Let

3.14.
The replete diagonal map

In what follows,

Suppose

The

\Delta^{\mathrm{rep}}\colon X\to X\times_{S}^{\mathrm{rep}}X

is the repletion of the diagonal map
\Delta\colon X\to X\times_{S}X
.

Remark 3.16
.

If

is a map of ordinary affine log schemes,
the replete diagonal map defined above is a derived analogue of Kato-Saito’s log diagonal map

X\to X\times_{S,[P]}^{\log}X

[

.
See also
[

for an equivalent construction.

The following gives a description of the log cotangent complex analogous to that of the log differentials in Section
3.2
:

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
X\to S
be a map of affine pre-log derived schemes. The cotangent complex
{\mathbb{L}}_{X/S}
is equivalent to the conormal of
\underline{\Delta^{\mathrm{rep}}}
, the map of derived schemes underlying the replete diagonal
\Delta^{\mathrm{rep}}\colon X\to X\times_{S}^{\mathrm{rep}}X
.

For a morphism of affine derived schemes arising from a map of animated commutative rings admitting a section,
we define its indecomposables as the module obtained by applying the indecomposable functor levelwise.
It may be helpful to observe that the ordinary cotangent complex
{\mathbb{L}}_{A/R}
arises as the indecomposables of

: Indeed, the derived tensor product

can be computed via a polynomial

, and the indecomposables of

Proof of Proposition
3.17
.

The replete diagonal corresponds to an augmentation

((A\otimes_{R}A)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[M\oplus_{P}M]}{\mathbb{Z}}[(M\oplus_{P}M)^{\mathrm{rep}}],(M\oplus_{P}M)^{\mathrm{rep}})\to(A,M)

of animated pre-log rings.
This evidently admits a section,
so that Corollary
2.23
implies the map is strict.
The module in question is therefore the levelwise indecomposables of

Since the ordinary cotangent complex

is the indecomposables of

, and similarly for

, the indecomposables of (

is the indecomposables functor. By Proposition

4.
Étale and smooth morphisms in derived log geometry

We shall use the following notion of formal étaleness in the context of derived log geometry from
[
55
, Definition 5.3]
:

Definition 4.1
.

A map
(f,f^{\flat})\colon(R,P)\to(A,M)
of animated pre-log rings is
derived formally log étale
if the log cotangent complex
{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
vanishes.

Following
[
55
]
, we say that a map
(f,f^{\flat})
is derived log étale if it is formally derived log étale and if
f
is homotopically finitely presented in the sense of
[
57
, Definition 1.2.3.1]
. By
[
55
, Theorem 5.6]
, a derived log étale map
(f,f^{\flat})
such that
\pi_{0}(f^{\flat})
is finitely presented induces a log étale map
(\pi_{0}(f),\pi_{0}(f^{\flat}))
of discrete log rings in the sense that
(\pi_{0}(f),\pi_{0}(f^{\flat}))
lifts uniquely against log square-zero extensions
[
27
]
.
In particular,
[
27
, Theorem 3.5]
shows that, locally on charts,

(\pi_{0}(f),\pi_{0}(f^{\flat}))
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\pi_{0}(R)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[\pi_{0}(P)]}{\mathbb{Z}}[\pi_{0}(M)]\to\pi_{0}(A)
is étale;

(2)

\pi_{0}(M)^{\mathrm{gp}}/\pi_{0}(P)^{\mathrm{gp}}
is finite of order invertible in
\pi_{0}(A)
.

In future work, we intend to develop the deformation theory of derived log schemes.
We prove a derived analogue of
[
27
, Theorem 3.5]
,
obtaining several equivalent notions of log étaleness in the context of derived log geometry.

Definition 4.2
.

A map
(f,f^{\flat})\colon(R,P)\to(A,M)
of animated pre-log rings is
derived formally log smooth
if the log cotangent complex
{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
is a projective
A
-module, i.e., a direct summand of a free
A
-module.

Our definition of derived formal log smoothness is equivalent to
[
55
, Definition 6.3]
by

[
37
, Propositions 7.2.2.6, 7.2.2.7]
,
[
57
, 1.2.8.3]
.
One can define a map of animated pre-log rings to be
derived log smooth
if the underlying map of
animated commutative rings is homotopically finitely presented.
By
[
55
, Theorem 6.4]
, this yields a log smooth map of log rings on
\pi_{0}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

The classes of derived formally log étale morphisms and derived formally log smooth morphisms of animated pre-log rings are closed under compositions and pushouts.

Proof.

Let
(R,P)\xrightarrow{(f,f^{\flat})}(A,M)\xrightarrow{(g,g^{\flat})}(B,N)
be maps of animated pre-log rings, and consider the transitivity sequence (
3.4
).
If
(f,f^{\flat})
and
(g,g^{\flat})
are derived formally log smooth,
then
B\otimes_{A}\mathbb{L}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}
and
\mathbb{L}_{(B,N)/(A,M)}
are projective
B
-modules.
Hence
\mathbb{L}_{(B,N)/(R,P)}
is a projective
B
-module by
[
37
, Proposition 7.2.2.6]
.
It follows that
(gf,g^{\flat}f^{\flat})
is derived formally log smooth.
Let

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(R,P)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(A,M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(R^{\prime},P^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(A^{\prime},M^{\prime})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(f,f^{\flat})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(f^{\prime},f^{\prime\flat})}

be a pushout square of animated pre-log rings.
By
[

, Proposition 4.12(ii)]
,
we have

The assumption on

implies that

Therefore,

\mathbb{L}_{(A^{\prime},M^{\prime})/(R^{\prime},P^{\prime})}
is a projective

-module,
i.e.,

(f^{\prime},f^{\prime\flat})

The claim for derived formally log étale maps is shown similarly.
∎

4.4.
Derived formally log étale maps of discrete log rings

Example
4.9
below shows that log étale maps of ordinary log rings need not give rise to derived formally log étale maps of affine pre-log derived schemes. This is in contrast to ordinary derived algebraic geometry and closely related to the fact that the log cotangent complex may fail to be contractible for a given log étale map. However, a derived formally log étale map has a vanishing cotangent complex by definition. Derived formally log smooth maps enjoy the property of being concentrated as the log differentials in degree zero:

Proposition 4.5
.

If
(R_{0},P_{0})\xrightarrow{(f_{0},f^{\flat}_{0})}(A_{0},M_{0})
is a derived formally log smooth map of discrete pre-log rings,
there is a canonical equivalence

{\mathbb{L}}_{(A_{0},M_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0})}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\pi_{0}({\mathbb{L}}_{(A_{0},M_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0})})\cong\Omega^{1}_{(A_{0},M_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0})}.

Proof.

Since
{\mathbb{L}}_{(A_{0},M_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0})}
is a direct summand of a free
A_{0}
-module and
A_{0}
is discrete,
{\mathbb{L}}_{(A_{0},M_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0})}
is concentrated in degree
0
.
This implies the claim.
∎

As suggested by the application of base change in the proof of Corollary
3.9
and the resulting integrality hypothesis in Corollary
3.10
, flatness hypotheses are necessary when passing from ordinary to derived log geometry. Corollary
3.10
is particularly convenient when using the characterization of log étale and log smooth morphisms in terms of charts, for which we need to impose an additional finiteness hypothesis:

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
(R_{0},P_{0})\xrightarrow{(f_{0},f^{\flat}_{0})}(A_{0},M_{0})
be an integral map of discrete fine pre-log rings.

(1)

If
(f_{0},f_{0}^{\flat})
is log smooth, then it is derived log smooth.

(2)

If
(f_{0},f_{0}^{\flat})
is log étale, then it is derived log étale.

Proof.

We give a proof of (1), since (2) is similar (in fact, easier). Using the base change property for strict étale maps (
3.9
), one readily verifies that the claims can be checked strict étale locally on
(A_{0},M_{0})
. Indeed, suppose
(A_{0},M_{0})\to(B_{0},N_{0})
is a strict étale cover such that
(R_{0},P_{0})\to(B_{0},N_{0})
is derived log smooth.
Then
B_{0}\otimes_{A_{0}}\mathbb{L}_{(A_{0},M_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0})}\simeq\mathbb{L}_{(B_{0},N_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0})}
is a finitely generated projective
B_{i}
-module.
By fppf descent
[
56
, 03DX]
, we deduce that
\mathbb{L}_{(A_{0},M_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0})}
is a finitely generated projective
A_{0}
-module as well.

Since the log cotangent complex is invariant under logification
[
43
, Theorem 8.16]
,
it suffices to check the statement on charts.
Since the monoids are assumed to be fine,
by appealing to
[
27
, Theorem 3.5]
or
[
41
, Theorem IV.3.3.1]
we may assume

(1)

R_{0}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[P_{0}]}{\mathbb{Z}}[M_{0}]\to A_{0}
is smooth, and

(2)

the induced map
P_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}\to M_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}
is injective,
and the torsion part of
M_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}/P_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}
is of finite order invertible in
A_{0}
.

After a further localization,

[
41
, Theorem IV.3.3.1]
allows us to assume that
P_{0}\to M_{0}
remains integral.
Hence Corollary
3.10
yields a cofiber sequence

A_{0}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\mathbb{L}}(M_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}/P_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}})\to{\mathbb{L}}_{(A_{0},M_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0})}\to{\mathbb{L}}_{A_{0}/R_{0}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[P_{0}]}{\mathbb{Z}}[M_{0}]}.

The right-hand module is concentrated in degree zero as the differentials by (1),
while the left-hand module is concentrated in degree zero as the free
A_{0}
-module of rank that of the
free part of
M_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}/P_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}
by (2).
Hence the middle term is concentrated in degree zero as the log differentials.
∎

In summary, there are implications for morphisms of discrete fine pre-log rings:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\text{(integral log smooth)}\Rightarrow\text{(derived log smooth)}\Rightarrow\text{(log smooth),}

(4.2)

\displaystyle\text{(integral log \'{e}tale)}\Rightarrow\text{(derived log \'{e}tale)}\Rightarrow\text{(log \'{e}tale).}

Here the first implications in both (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) follow from
Proposition

4.6
, while second implications follow from
[
55
, Theorem 5.6 and Theorem 6.4]
.

5.
Logarithmic Hochschild homology of animated pre-log rings

After a brief review of Hochschild homology,
we define log Hochschild homology of animated pre-log rings and
note that it recovers Rognes’ definition for discrete pre-log rings.
Throughout,
all tensor products are implicitly derived unless explicitly stated otherwise.

5.1.
Hochschild homology

Let
R\to A
be a map of animated commutative rings. The
Hochschild homology

\HH(A/R)
of
A
relative to
R
is the colimit
S^{1}\otimes_{R}A
of the constant diagram
A
from
S^{1}
to animated commutative
R
-algebras. The maps
*\to S^{1}\to*
exhibit
A\to{\rm HH}(A/R)\to A
as an animated augmented commutative
A
-algebra, while the equivalence
S^{1}\simeq*\sqcup_{*\sqcup*}*
gives an equivalence
\HH(A/R)\simeq A\otimes_{A\otimes_{R}A}A
. If
\underline{X}\to\underline{S}
denotes the corresponding map of affine derived schemes, we see that

\HH({\underline{X}}/{\underline{S}})\simeq{\underline{X}}\times_{\underline{X}\times_{\underline{S}}\underline{X}}\underline{X},

the derived self-intersection of the derived diagonal
\Delta\colon{\underline{X}}\to{\underline{X}}\times_{\underline{S}}\underline{X}
.

If
R_{0}\to A_{0}
is a map of discrete commutative rings,
we note there are isomorphisms (see e.g.,
[
33
, Corollary 1.18, Proposition 1.1.10]
)

(5.1)

\pi_{0}\HH(A_{0}/R_{0})\cong A_{0},\qquad\pi_{1}\HH(A_{0}/R_{0})\cong\Omega^{1}_{A_{0}/R_{0}}.

5.2.
Log Hochschild homology

Inspired by the description of Hochschild homology as the derived self-intersections of the diagonal
and the relationship between the replete diagonal and the log cotangent complex described in
Proposition
3.17
,
we propose the following definition
(the reader is invited to compare with
[
49
, §13]
):

Definition 5.3
.

Let
(R,P)\to(A,M)
be a map of animated pre-log rings. The
log Hochschild homology

{\rm logHH}((A,M)/(R,P))
is the animated commutative ring

A\otimes_{(A\otimes_{R}A)_{M\oplus_{P}M}^{\mathrm{rep}}}A=A\otimes_{(A\otimes_{R}A)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[M\oplus_{P}M]}{\mathbb{Z}}[(M\oplus_{P}M)^{\mathrm{rep}}]}A.

Let
X\to S
be the map of affine pre-log derived schemes corresponding to
(R,P)\to(A,M)
.
Then log Hochschild homology

{\rm logHH}(X/S)
is by definition the affine derived scheme

\underline{X\times_{X\times_{S}X}^{\mathrm{rep}}X}

underlying the derived self-intersections
X\times_{X\times_{S}X}^{\mathrm{rep}}X
of the replete diagonal
X\to X\times_{S}^{\mathrm{rep}}X
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
(R,P)\to(A,M)\to(B,N)
be maps of animated pre-log rings. There is an equivalence of animated commutative rings

A\otimes_{\logHH((A,M)/(R,P))}\logHH((B,N)/(R,P))\simeq\logHH((B,N)/(A,M)).

Proof.

It suffices to prove that

(5.2)

A\otimes_{(A\otimes_{R}A)^{\mathrm{rep}}_{M\oplus_{P}M}}(B\otimes_{R}B)^{\mathrm{rep}}_{N\oplus_{P}N}\simeq(B\otimes_{A}B)^{\mathrm{rep}}_{N\oplus_{M}N}.

Since
A\otimes_{A\otimes_{R}A}(B\otimes_{R}B)\simeq B\otimes_{A}B
and
{\mathbb{Z}}[M]\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[M\oplus_{P}M]}{\mathbb{Z}}[N\oplus_{P}N]\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}[N\oplus_{M}N]
, (
5.2
) follows from the equivalences

\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}}[M]\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[(M\oplus_{P}M)^{\mathrm{rep}}]}{\mathbb{Z}}[(N\oplus_{P}N)^{\mathrm{rep}}]

\displaystyle\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}[M]\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[M\oplus(M^{\mathrm{gp}}/P^{\mathrm{gp}})]}{\mathbb{Z}}[N\oplus(N^{\mathrm{gp}}/P^{\mathrm{gp}})]

\displaystyle\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}[N\oplus(N^{\mathrm{gp}}/M^{\mathrm{gp}})]

\displaystyle\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}[(N\oplus_{M}N)^{\mathrm{rep}}]

provided by Proposition
2.21
.
∎

5.5.
Comparison with Rognes’ definition of log
\HH

In
[
49
, Definition 3.21]
,
Rognes introduced absolute log Hochschild homology of discrete log rings. A direct generalization goes as follows: for a map
(R,P)\to(A,M)
of animated pre-log rings, let
S^{1}\oplus_{P}M
denote the tensor with
S^{1}
in the
\infty
-category of animated commutative monoids under
P
. Let
(S^{1}\oplus_{P}M)^{\mathrm{rep}}
denote the repletion of the augmentation
S^{1}\oplus_{P}M\to M
, and consider the pushout of the diagram

(5.3)

S^{1}\otimes_{R}A\xleftarrow{}S^{1}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[P]}{\mathbb{Z}}[M]\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}[S^{1}\oplus_{P}M]\xrightarrow{}{\mathbb{Z}}[(S^{1}\oplus_{P}M)^{\mathrm{rep}}]

in the
\infty
-category of animated commutative rings.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
(R,P)\to(A,M)
be a map of animated
pre-log rings. The pushout of the diagram (
5.3
) is naturally equivalent to
{\rm logHH}((A,M)/(R,P))
.

Proof.

There are natural equivalences

S^{1}\otimes_{R}A\simeq A\otimes_{A\otimes_{R}A}A\quad\text{and}\quad S^{1}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[P]}{\mathbb{Z}}[M]\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}[M]\oplus_{{\mathbb{Z}}[M\oplus_{P}M]}{\mathbb{Z}}[M].

Moreover, we see that

{\mathbb{Z}}[(S^{1}\oplus_{P}M)^{\mathrm{rep}}]\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}[M]\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[(M\oplus_{P}M)^{\mathrm{rep}}]}{\mathbb{Z}}[M]

by applying Proposition
2.21
twice and the equivalence
B^{\rm cy}_{P^{\mathrm{gp}}}(M^{\mathrm{gp}})/M^{\mathrm{gp}}\simeq B(M^{\mathrm{gp}}/P^{\mathrm{gp}})
: Indeed, there are equivalences of animated monoids

B^{\rm cy}_{P}(M)^{\mathrm{rep}}\simeq M\oplus B(M^{\mathrm{gp}}/P^{\mathrm{gp}})\simeq M\oplus_{M\oplus(M^{\mathrm{gp}}/P^{\mathrm{gp}})}M\simeq M\oplus_{(M\oplus_{P}M)^{\mathrm{rep}}}M.

For the second equivalence we use that
B(-)
is the suspension functor on the category of animated monoids. Hence the result follows from commuting colimits in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\otimes_{R}A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Z}}[M]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Z}}[M\oplus_{P}M]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Z}}[M]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Z}}[M]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Z}}[(M\oplus_{P}M)^{\mathrm{rep}}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Z}}[M]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces=}

of animated commutative rings:
Forming the horizontal

while forming the vertical pushouts first gives our formulation.
∎

5.7.
Log Hochschild homology of free pre-log algebras

Let
(R_{0},P_{0})
be a discrete pre-log ring and let
(A_{0},M_{0})
be a free pre-log algebra over
(R_{0},P_{0})
. Since
(A_{0},M_{0})
is cofibrant over
(R_{0},P_{0})
, no replacement is necessary when defining log Hochschild homology
{\rm logHH}((A_{0},M_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0}))
. In particular, by virtue of
Proposition

5.6
, we have that
{\rm logHH}((A_{0},M_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0}))
is naturally equivalent to the point-set pushout of the diagram

(5.4)

{\rm HH}(A_{0}/R_{0})\xleftarrow{}{\mathbb{Z}}[B^{\rm cy}_{P_{0}}(M_{0})]\xrightarrow{}{\mathbb{Z}}[B^{\mathrm{rep}}_{P_{0}}(M_{0})]

of simplicial commutative rings.
Here
B^{\rm cy}_{P_{0}}(M_{0})
is the (underived) cyclic bar construction of
M_{0}
relative to
P_{0}
and
B^{\mathrm{rep}}_{P_{0}}(M_{0})

is the (discrete) pullback of
M_{0}\xrightarrow{}M_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}\xleftarrow{}B^{\rm cy}_{P_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}}(M_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}})
.

We will consider these (underived) constructions for any map of discrete commutative monoids
P_{0}\to M_{0}

and apply the following results when
M_{0}
is free over
P_{0}
:

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
P_{0}\to M_{0}
be a map of discrete commutative monoids.
There is an isomorphism of animated commutative monoids

M_{0}\oplus B(M_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}/P_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}})\xrightarrow{\cong}B^{\mathrm{rep}}_{P_{0}}(M_{0}).

In simplicial degree
q
,
it is given by

(m,[g_{1}],\dots,[g_{q}])\mapsto(m,(\gamma_{0}(m)(g_{1}\cdots g_{q})^{-1},g_{1},\dots,g_{q})
.

Proof.

The proposed map is well-defined, as for different representatives of the elements in
M_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}/P_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}
, the inversion used in the map will cancel their difference. An inverse sends a
q
-simplex
(m,(g_{0},\dots,g_{q}))
to the element
(m,[g_{1}],\dots,[g_{q}])
. It is straight-forward to check that this map respects the simplicial structure maps.∎

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
P_{0}\to M_{0}
be a map of discrete commutative monoids. There are isomorphisms of
{\mathbb{Z}}[M_{0}]
-modules

\pi_{0}{\mathbb{Z}}[B^{\mathrm{rep}}_{P_{0}}(M_{0})]\cong{\mathbb{Z}}[M_{0}]\quad\text{ and }\quad\pi_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}[B^{\mathrm{rep}}_{P_{0}}(M_{0})]\cong{\mathbb{Z}}[M_{0}]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}(M_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}/P_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}).

Proof.

By Lemma
5.8
, the replete bar construction
B^{\mathrm{rep}}_{P_{0}}(M_{0})
is levelwise isomorphic to
M_{0}\oplus B(M_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}/P_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}})
. Hence the Moore complex associated to
{\mathbb{Z}}[B^{\mathrm{rep}}_{P_{0}}(M_{0})]
takes the form

\cdots\xrightarrow{}{\mathbb{Z}}[M_{0}]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Z}}[M_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}/P_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}]^{\otimes 2}\xrightarrow{\partial_{2}}{\mathbb{Z}}[M_{0}]\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{Z}}[M_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}/P_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}]\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}=0}{\mathbb{Z}}[M_{0}]\to 0.

This immediately gives the desired identification in degree
0
. In degree
1
, we notice that the map

({\mathbb{Z}}[M_{0}]\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{{\mathbb{Z}}[M_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}/P_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}]})/\!\!\sim\xrightarrow{}{\mathbb{Z}}[M_{0}]\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(M_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}}/P_{0}^{\mathrm{gp}})

sending
[m\otimes g]
to
m\otimes g
is a well-defined isomorphism, where
\sim
is the equivalence relation
{\mathbb{Z}}[M_{0}]
-linearly generated by
m\otimes g_{2}-m\otimes g_{1}g_{2}+m\otimes g_{1}
. The source of this map is by definition
H_{1}
of the Moore complex of the replete bar construction.
∎

The following description of the log Hochschild homology of a free
(R_{0},P_{0})
-algebra, which follows from Proposition
5.4
, will prove useful:

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
(R_{0},P_{0})
be a discrete pre-log ring.
There is a natural equivalence of animated commutative rings

{\rm logHH}((F_{(R_{0},P_{0})}(X,Y))/(R_{0},P_{0}))\simeq R_{0}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\HH({\mathbb{Z}}\langle Y\rangle)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\rm logHH}(({\mathbb{Z}}\langle X\rangle,\langle X\rangle)/({\mathbb{Z}},\{1\})).

5.11.
Recovering the log Kähler differentials from log Hochschild homology

Let
(f_{0},f_{0}^{\flat})\colon(R_{0},P_{0},\beta_{0})\to(A_{0},M_{0},\alpha_{0})
be a map of discrete pre-log rings.
Recall the definition of the log Kähler differentials from Section

.
Lemma

gives the following:

Corollary 5.12
.

Let

There is an isomorphism of

-modules

\Phi_{1}\colon\Omega^{1}_{F_{(R_{0},P_{0})}(X,Y)/(R_{0},P_{0})}\to\pi_{1}{\rm logHH}((F_{(R_{0},P_{0})}(X,Y))/(R_{0},P_{0})).

Proof.

Since

is free, we can model log Hochschild homology by the pushout of (

). Apply Lemma

and consider the convergent

-spectral sequence

From this,

Proposition 5.13
.

Let
(R_{0},P_{0})\to(A_{0},M_{0})
be a map of discrete pre-log rings.
There is an isomorphism of

-modules

\Phi_{1}\colon\Omega^{1}_{(A_{0},M_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0})}\to\pi_{1}\logHH((A_{0},M_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0})).

Proof.

In view of

5.6
,

, where

As the resulting object

is the diagonal of a bisimplicial commutative ring, there is a convergent spectral sequence

Keeping track of the maps involved in constructing the map

The map
\Phi_{1}
in Proposition

of graded commutative rings

(5.5)

\Phi_{*}\colon\Omega^{*}_{(A_{0},M_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0})}\to\pi_{*}\logHH((A_{0},M_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0})).

5.14.
The log
{\rm HKR}
-filtration

We are ready to construct a logarithmic version of the
{\rm HKR}
-filtration
[
40
, Proposition IV.4.1]
:

Theorem 5.15
.

Let
(R,P)\to(A,M)
be a map of animated pre-log rings. Then the relative log Hochschild homology
\logHH((A,M)/(R,P))
admits a descending separated filtration with graded pieces of the form
(\bigwedge\nolimits_{A}^{n}{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)})[n]
. In particular, there is a strongly convergent spectral sequence

E^{2}_{p,q}:=\pi_{p}(\bigwedge\nolimits\nolimits_{A}^{q}{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)})\implies\pi_{p+q}\logHH((A,M)/(R,P))

relating the derived wedge powers of the log cotangent complex and log Hochschild homology.

Theorem
5.15
is proven at the end of this section.
As a corollary, we obtain a version of the
{\rm HKR}
-theorem for log smooth log rings:

Corollary 5.16
.

Let
(R_{0},P_{0})\to(A_{0},M_{0})
be a derived formally log smooth map of discrete pre-log rings. Then the map defined in (
5.5
) is an isomorphism of graded commutative rings.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.5
, the spectral sequence of Theorem
5.15
collapses to give the desired isomorphism.
∎

We first establish the
{\rm HKR}
-theorem for free pre-log algebras and use this to construct the logarithmic
{\rm HKR}
-filtration.

5.17.
The log
{\rm HKR}
-theorem for free pre-log algebras

The general log
{\rm HKR}
-theorem for free pre-log algebras will reduce to the following:

Lemma 5.18
.

The map

\Omega^{*}_{F_{(R_{0},P_{0})}(\{x\},\emptyset)/(R_{0},P_{0})}\xrightarrow{}\pi_{*}{\rm logHH}(F_{(R_{0},P_{0})}(\{x\},\emptyset)/(R_{0},P_{0}))

defined in (
5.5
) is an isomorphism of graded rings.

Stated differently, the log
{\rm HKR}
-theorem holds for free pre-log algebras
(R_{0}\langle x\rangle,\langle x\rangle)
on a single generator.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.8
and Lemma
5.10
, there is an equivalence

{\rm logHH}(F_{(R_{0},P_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0})}(\{x\},\emptyset))\simeq R_{0}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\rm logHH}({\mathbb{Z}}[x],\langle x\rangle)\cong R_{0}[x]\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Z}}[B\langle x\rangle^{\mathrm{gp}}].

The homotopy groups of
{\mathbb{Z}}[B\langle x\rangle^{\mathrm{gp}}]
are the
{\rm Tor}
-groups
{\rm Tor}^{{\mathbb{Z}}[x,x^{-1}]}_{*}({\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Indeed, observe that
B\langle x\rangle^{\mathrm{gp}}
is the suspension of
\langle x\rangle^{\mathrm{gp}}
in the category of animated abelian groups. Hence, since
{\mathbb{Z}}[-]
commutes with homotopy pushouts, the animated commutative ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[B\langle x\rangle^{\mathrm{gp}}]
is equivalent to the derived tensor product
{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[\langle x\rangle^{\mathrm{gp}}]}^{\mathbb{L}}{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Using the free resolution

0\to{\mathbb{Z}}[x,x^{-1}]\xrightarrow{\cdot(x-1)}{\mathbb{Z}}[x,x^{-1}]\xrightarrow{}{\mathbb{Z}}\to 0,

we find the
{\rm Tor}
-groups to be
copies of
\mathbb{Z}
concentrated in degrees 0 and 1

so that the graded ring

\pi_{*}{\rm logHH}(F_{(R_{0},P_{0})}(\{x\},\emptyset)/(R_{0},P_{0}))
is concentrated as
R_{0}[x]
in degrees
0
and
1
. By Proposition
5.13
, there is a preferred isomorphism

\Phi_{1}\colon\Omega^{1}_{F_{(R_{0},P_{0})}(\langle x\rangle,\emptyset)/(R_{0},P_{0})}\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_{1}{\rm logHH}(F_{(R_{0},P_{0})}(\langle x\rangle,\emptyset)/(R_{0},P_{0})).

Since
\pi_{*}\logHH(F_{(R_{0},P_{0})}(\langle x\rangle,\emptyset)/(R_{0},P_{0}))
is concentrated in degrees
0
and
1
, this suffices to conclude that the map is an isomorphism.
∎

By commuting colimits, Lemma
5.10
implies the log
{\rm HKR}
-theorem for any free pre-log ring

F_{(R,P)}(X,\emptyset)
. Combining this with the ordinary
{\rm HKR}
-theorem, we obtain:

Corollary 5.19
.

Let
F_{(R_{0},P_{0})}(X,Y)
be a free
(R_{0},P_{0})
-algebra. Then the map (
5.5
) is an isomorphism of strictly commutative graded rings

\Phi_{*}\colon\Omega^{*}_{F_{(R_{0},P_{0})}(X,Y)/(R_{0},P_{0})}\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_{*}{\rm logHH}(F_{(R_{0},P_{0})}(X,Y)/(R_{0},P_{0})).

We can now deduce the log André–Quillen spectral sequence:

Proof of Theorem
5.15
.

On free pre-log algebras, it is clear by Corollary
5.19
that the Whitehead filtration of
{\rm logHH}((A,M)/(R,P))
gives the result. Since both the log cotangent complex and log Hochschild homology are in general left Kan extended from free pre-log algebras, this also gives the filtration in general. Hence we obtain a conditionally convergent spectral sequence

E^{2}_{p,q}:=\pi_{p}(\bigwedge\nolimits\nolimits_{A}^{q}{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)})\implies\pi_{p+q}\logHH((A,M)/(R,P))

concentrated in the first quadrant. Therefore it is strongly convergent, see e.g.
[
15
, Theorem 7.1]
and the subsequent remark. ∎

Remark 5.20
.

From a more classical perspective,

Corollary

5.19
allows us to use
the results of Quillen
[
45
]
to obtain the log HKR-theorem. One key point is that Corollary
5.19
implies the simplicial augmentation ideal of

(A\otimes_{R}A)_{(M\oplus_{P}M)}^{\mathrm{rep}}\to A

is levelwise
quasi-regular
in the sense of
[
45
, Definition 6.10]
. The definition of
\logHH
as derived self-intersections makes
[
45
, Theorem 6.3]
applicable and the resulting spectral sequence converges. We also observe that the spectral sequence can be obtained from the totalization of a double complex, see e.g.,
[
45
, §8]
.

5.21.
Base change for log Hochschild homology

The following is a generalization of Weibel–Geller’s
[
62
]
étale base change formula for Hochschild homology:

Theorem 5.22
.

Let
(R,P)
be an animated pre-log ring.
A map
(A,M)\to(B,N)
of pre-log
(R,P)
-algebras is derived formally log étale
if and only if the canonical map

B\otimes_{A}\logHH((A,M)/(R,P))\xrightarrow{}\logHH((B,N)/(R,P))

is an equivalence.

Remark 5.23
.

If
(R_{0},P_{0})\to(A_{0},M_{0})
is log étale and integral,
then
\mathbb{L}_{(A_{0},M_{0})/(R_{0},P_{0})}
is contractible by Proposition
4.6
(2).
On the other hand,

Example

4.9
shows the base change formula does not hold for a general log étale map of discrete commutative rings.

The next result makes use of the indecomposables functor discussed in Section
3.13
.

Lemma 5.24
.

Let
(R,P)\to(A,M)
be a map of animated pre-log rings.
There is a natural equivalence of
A
-modules

Q_{A}(\logHH((A,M)/(R,P)))\simeq{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}[1].

Proof.

Recall
\logHH((A,M)/(R,P))
is the suspension of the augmented simplicial commutative
A
-algebra
(A\otimes_{R}A)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[M\oplus_{P}M]}{\mathbb{Z}}[(M\oplus_{P}M)^{\mathrm{rep}}]
. Since
Q_{A}
is left Quillen, we obtain

Q_{A}(\logHH((A,M)/(R,P)))\simeq Q_{A}((A\otimes_{R}A)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[M\oplus_{P}M]}{\mathbb{Z}}[(M\oplus_{P}M)^{\mathrm{rep}}])[1].

The result now follows from Proposition
3.17
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.22
.

If the base change map is an equivalence,
then the induced map of indecomposables is also an equivalence.
By
Lemma

5.24
,
this holds if and only if there is an equivalence

(5.6)

B\otimes_{A}{\mathbb{L}}_{(A,M)/(R,P)}[1]\to{\mathbb{L}}_{(B,N)/(R,P)}[1],

implying that the cofiber
{\mathbb{L}}_{(B,N)/(A,M)}[1]
is contractible.
Conversely, if
{\mathbb{L}}_{(B,N)/(A,M)}
is contractible, the map (
5.6
) is an equivalence, and so we obtain the base change formula for log Hochschild homology by comparing the Quillen spectral sequences converging to
B\otimes_{R}{\rm logHH}((A,M)/(R,P))
and
{\rm logHH}((B,N)/(R,P))
.
∎

Corollary 5.25
.

Let
(R,P)\to(A,M)
be a derived formally log étale map of derived pre-log rings. Then the unit map
A\to\logHH((A,M)/(R,P))
is an equivalence.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
5.4
and Theorem
5.22
.
∎

Proposition 5.26
.

Let
R\to A
be a tamely ramified finite extension of discrete valuation rings with perfect residue fields, and let
(f,f^{\flat})\colon(R,R-\{0\})\to(A,A-\{0\})
be the induced map of log rings.
Then there is a canonical equivalence

A\otimes_{R}\logHH(R,R-\{0\})\to\logHH(A,A-\{0\}).

Proof.

Owing to
[
41
, Proposition IV.3.1.19]
,
(f,f^{\flat})
is log étale.
Furthermore,

(f,f^{\flat})
is integral since
R-\{0\}
is a valuative monoid
[
41
, Proposition I.4.6.3(5)]
.
Proposition
4.6
(2) shows that
(f,f^{\flat})
is derived log étale,
and we conclude by Theorem
5.22
.
∎

6.11.

Proposition 6.12

satisfies

Lemma 6.13
.

Suppose
\mathcal{V}
is an
\infty
-category admitting colimits and
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{P}\mathrm{sh}(\mathrm{lSch}/S,\mathcal{V})
, where
S\in\mathrm{lSch}
.
Then
\mathcal{F}
satisfies dividing descent if and only if
\mathcal{F}(X)\to\mathcal{F}(Y)
is an equivalence for every dividing cover
Y\to X
in
\mathrm{lSch}/S
.
The same result holds for
\mathrm{lFan}/S
.

Proof.

We focus on
\mathrm{lSch}/S
since the proofs are similar.
Suppose
\mathcal{F}
satisfies dividing descent.
For a dividing cover
f\colon Y\to X
in
\mathrm{lSch}/S
, the Čech nerve associated with
f
is isomorphic to the constant simplicial scheme
Y
.
The above shows that
\mathcal{F}(X)\to\mathcal{F}(Y)
is an equivalence.
Conversely, suppose
\mathcal{F}(X)\to\mathcal{F}(Y)
is an equivalence for every dividing cover
Y\to X
in
\mathrm{lSch}/S
.
If
\mathscr{X}\to X
is a dividing hypercover, then
\mathscr{X}_{i}\to X
is a dividing cover for every integer

.
Hence
\mathcal{F}(X)\to\lvert\mathcal{F}(\mathscr{X})\rvert
is an equivalence, i.e.,
\mathcal{F}
satisfies dividing descent.
∎

.

6.15.
Dividing invariance of Hodge cohomology

Owing to
[
10
, (9.1.1)]
,
for all

, quasi-coherent sheaf

on

, and integer

(6.2)

H_{Zar}^{i}(X,\mathcal{F})\cong H_{sNis}^{i}(X,\mathcal{F})\cong H_{s{\acute{e}t}}^{i}(X,\mathcal{F}).

If

,

,
we have a canonical isomorphism

H_{t}^{i}(\underline{X},\mathcal{F})\cong H_{t}^{i}(X,\mathcal{F}).

In fact,

Theorem 6.16
.

Let

be a dividing cover in
\mathrm{lSch}
.
Then the functor

\underline{f}^{*}\colon\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(\underline{X}))\to\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(\underline{Y}))

of the bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves naturally induced by the underlying morphism

is fully faithful.
In particular, the naturally induced homomorphism

(6.3)

\mathbf{H}_{Zar}^{i}(\underline{X},\mathcal{F})\to\mathbf{H}_{Zar}^{i}(\underline{Y},f^{*}\mathcal{F})

is an isomorphism for all
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Coh}(\underline{X}))
and integers
i
.

We prove Theorem
6.16
at the end of this section.
It improves on
[
10
, Proposition 9.2.4]
,
where we assumed that
X
and
Y
are log smooth over a field.

Corollary 6.17
.

Suppose

.
Then

R\Gamma_{sNis}(-,\Omega_{-/S}^{d})\in\mathcal{S}\mathrm{hv}_{sNis}(\mathrm{lSch}/S,\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab}))

satisfies dividing Nisnevich descent.

Proof.

Owing to (
6.2
), it suffices to show that the naturally induced homomorphism

is an isomorphism for all dividing covers

in
\mathrm{lSch}/S
and integers

.

.
Corollary IV.3.2.4(1) in

.

If

H_{Z_{m}}^{i}(\lvert\Sigma\rvert,A)\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}A&\text{if }i=0\text{ and }m\in P,\\

,

\lvert\Sigma\rvert
.
For all integers

the intersection of

What we have established above for the case of cones produces an isomorphism of Čech complexes

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

7.
Sheaves on arrow categories

7.1.
Topologies on arrow categories

One aspect of the dividing Nisnevich topology is that it involves non-affine schemes.
Hence to “sheafify”
\logHH
with respect to the dividing Nisnevich topology,
we need to define
\logHH(X/S)
for arbitrary morphism of fs log schemes
X\to S
.
For this purpose,
we first view
\logHH
as a presheaf on an arrow category.
In this setup,
we can sheafify with respect to the dividing Nisnevich topology.

Definition 7.2
.

The
arrow
\infty
-category of an
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
is defined to be
\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathcal{C})
.
Informally speaking,
the objects are the morphisms
(X\to S)
in
\mathcal{C}
,
and the morphisms
(X^{\prime}\to S^{\prime})\to(X\to S)
are the commutative diagrams

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S.}

If fiber products are representable in
\mathcal{C}
, so are in
\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathcal{C})
.
Indeed, if
(X^{\prime}\to S^{\prime})\to(X\to S)
and
(X^{\prime\prime}\to S^{\prime\prime})\to(X\to S)
are two morphisms in
\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathcal{C})
, then the fiber product is
(X^{\prime}\times_{X}X^{\prime\prime}\to S^{\prime}\times_{S}S^{\prime\prime})
.

Definition 7.3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}
is an
\infty
-category with a topology
t
.
A family of morphisms
\{(X_{i}\to S_{i})\to(X\to S)\}_{i\in I}
is called a
t
-covering sieve
if both families

\{X_{i}\to X\}_{i\in I}\text{ and }\{S_{i}\to S\}_{i\in I}

are
t
-covering sieve.
One can readily check that the set of
t
-covering sieves on
\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathcal{C})
is a topology too.

7.4.
\logHH
as a presheaf on an arrow
\infty
-category

Definition 7.5
.

An
fs semi-log ring
is a pre-log ring
(A,M)
such that
\overline{M}:=M/M^{*}
is fine and saturated and the naturally induced map

M\to\Gamma(\operatorname{Spec}(A,M),\mathcal{M}_{\operatorname{Spec}(A,M)})

is an isomorphism, where
\operatorname{Spec}(A,M)
is the spectrum of
(A,M)

[
41
, Definition III.1.2.3]
.
Let
\mathrm{SemiLog}
denote the full category of pre-log rings consisting of fs semi-log rings
(A,M)
such that
A
is noetherian and has a finite Krull dimension.

Remark 7.6
.

There are at least three possible variants of affine log schemes.
First,
one may define an affine log scheme to be a log scheme
X=(\underline{X},\mathcal{M}_{X})
such that

\underline{X}=\operatorname{Spec}(R)
is an affine scheme.
Second,
following
[
4
, 1.1]
,
one can add the condition that the sheaf
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{X}
is generated by global sections,
i.e.,

\Gamma({X},\mathcal{M}_{X})\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{X,x}
is surjective for every point
x
.
Lastly,
following
[
29
]
,
one can ask that the identity map on
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{M}_{X})
is a chart for
\mathcal{M}_{X}
.
The third one is taken as our definition of an affine log scheme.

Proposition 7.7
.

The functor

\mathrm{Spec}\colon\mathrm{SemiLog}^{op}\to\mathrm{lSch}

is fully faithful.

Proof.

Let
(A,M)
and
(R,P)
be fs semi-log rings.
We have an isomorphism

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{SemiLog}}((R,P),(A,M))\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{CRing}}(R,A)\times_{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{CMon}}(P,A)}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{CMon}}(P,M),

where
\mathrm{CMon}
(resp.
\mathrm{CRing}
) denotes the category of commutative monoids (resp. commutative rings).

We set
X:=\operatorname{Spec}(A,M)
and
S:=\operatorname{Spec}(R,P)
for simplicity of notation.
We have isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{lSch}}(X,S)\cong

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{lSch}}(X,\underline{S}\times_{\underline{\mathbb{A}_{P}}}\mathbb{A}_{P})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{lSch}}(X,\underline{S})\times_{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{lSch}}(X,\underline{\mathbb{A}_{P}})}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{lSch}}(X,\underline{\mathbb{A}_{P}})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Sch}}(\underline{X},\underline{S})\times_{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{CMon}}(P,\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}))}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{CMon}}(P,\Gamma(X,\mathcal{M}_{X})),

where we use
[
41
, Proposition III.1.2.4]
for the last isomorphism.
Together with the isomorphisms
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\cong A
and
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{M}_{X})\cong M
, we finish the proof.
∎

Definition 7.8
.

Let
\mathrm{lAff}
be the full subcategory of
\mathrm{lSch}
consisting of the spectra of fs semi-log rings.
An important fact about
\mathrm{lAff}
that we will frequently use is that every
X\in\mathrm{lSch}
admits a Zariski covering
\{U_{i}\to X\}_{i\in I}
such that
U_{i}\in\mathrm{lAff}
for all
i\in I
, see
[
59
, Lemmas 1.3.2, 1.3.3]
.

The assignment

(\operatorname{Spec}(A,M)\to\operatorname{Spec}(R,P))\mapsto\logHH((A,M)/(R,P))

gives a presheaf
\logHH\in\mathcal{P}\mathrm{sh}(\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lAff}),\mathcal{S}\mathrm{CRing})
.
By composing with the canonical functor
\mathcal{S}\mathrm{CRing}\to\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab})
, we also view
\logHH
as an object of
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{sh}(\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lAff}),\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab}))
.

Lemma 7.9
.

Let
(R,P)\xrightarrow{f}(A,M)\xrightarrow{g}(B,N)
be homomorphisms of fs semi-log rings.
If
f
is integral log étale, then there is an equivalence

\logHH((B,N)/(R,P))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\logHH((B,N)/(A,M)).

Proof.

According to Proposition
5.4
, we have equivalences

\logHH((B,N)/(A,M))\simeq A\otimes_{\logHH(A,M)}\logHH(B,N),

\logHH((B,N)/(R,P))\simeq R\otimes_{\logHH(R,P)}\logHH(B,N).

Together with Corollary
5.25
, we finish the proof.
∎

Lemma 7.10
.

Let
(R,P)\xrightarrow{f}(A,M)\xrightarrow{g}(B,N)
be homomorphisms of fs semi-log rings.
If
g
is integral log étale, then there is an equivalence

B\otimes_{A}\logHH((A,M)/(R,P))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\logHH((B,N)/(R,P)).

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
5.22
.
∎

A morphism
f\colon X\to S
is called an
integral log étale cover
if
f
is integral, log étale,
and universally surjective in the category
\mathrm{lSch}
.
This defines an
integral log étale topology
.
Every integral log étale morphism is Kummer by
[
41
, Corollary I.4.3.10, Proposition IV.3.4.1]

(see also
[
41
, p. 344]
).
Hence the integral log étale topology is coarser than the Kummer log étale topology.

For a morphism of affine fs log schemes
X:=\operatorname{Spec}(A,M)\to S:=\operatorname{Spec}(R,P)
, we set

\logHH(X/S):=\logHH((A,M)/(R,P)).

Proposition 7.11
.

The presheaf
\logHH\in\mathcal{P}\mathrm{sh}(\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lAff}),\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab}))
satisfies integral log étale descent.

Proof.

Suppose
(X^{\prime}\to S^{\prime})\to(X\to S)
is an integral log étale cover in
\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lAff})
.
By Lemma
7.9
, we have an equivalence

(7.1)

\logHH(X^{\prime}/S^{\prime})\simeq\logHH(X^{\prime}/S).

If
(\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{S})\to(X\to S)
is an integral log étale hypercover in
\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lAff})
,
we need to show that the canonical map

\logHH(X/S)\to\lim_{i\in\Delta}\logHH(\mathscr{X}_{i}/{\mathscr{S}_{i}})

is an equivalence.
Thanks to (
7.1
),
we are reduced to considering the canonical map

\logHH(X/S)\to\lim_{i\in\Delta}\logHH(\mathscr{X}_{i}/S).

Suppose
S
(resp.
X
,
\mathscr{X}_{i}
) is given by
\operatorname{Spec}(A,M)
(resp.
\operatorname{Spec}(B,N)
,
\operatorname{Spec}(B_{i},N_{i})
).
The underlying morphism of schemes of an integral log étale morphism is flat by
[
41
, Theorem IV.4.3.5]
.
Hence,
by faithfully flat descent,
we obtain the exact sequence of
A
-modules

0\to B\to B_{0}\to B_{1}\to\cdots.

To conclude we apply
(-)\otimes_{A}\logHH(X/S)
and appeal to Lemma
7.10
.
∎

Remark 7.12
.

An anonymous referee suggested to consider a topology on the
\infty
-category
{\rm Ani(PreLog)}^{\rm op}
such that
\logHH
is a hypersheaf with this topology.
This can be achieved as follows.

Consider the topology on
{\rm Ani(PreLog)}^{\rm op}
generated by the family of derived formally log étale maps
\{f_{i}\colon(A,M)\to(B_{i},N_{i})\}_{i\in I}
with finite
I
such that
A\to\prod_{i\in I}B_{i}
is faithfully flat in the sense of
[
38
, Definition B.6.1.1]
.
The faithfully flat descent theorem holds for simplicial commutative rings, see
[
38
, Corollary D.6.3.4]
.
Hence using Proposition
5.22
,
we can argue as in the proof of Proposition
7.11
to show that the presheaf
\logHH\in\mathcal{P}\mathrm{sh}(\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},{\rm Ani(PreLog)}^{\rm op}),\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab}))
is a hypersheaf for the induced topology on
\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},{\rm Ani(PreLog)}^{\rm op})
. Note that the condition that the underlying map of rings
A\to\prod_{i\in I}B_{i}
is faithfully flat is fairly strong: for example, log blow-ups are formally log étale but clearly the underlying map of (classical) schemes is not faithfully flat.

This is the class of morphism we would be interested in considering for the dividing topology. It is unfortunately unclear how to generalize the dividing Nisnevich topology to derived setting of
{\rm Ani(PreLog)}^{\rm op}
.
The main technical advantage of the dividing topology is that we can invert log blow-ups by imposing dividing descent since a log blow-up is a monomorphism in the category of
saturated
log schemes.
In the category of log schemes,
a nontrivial log blow-up is no longer a monomorphism.
Hence working within the category of saturated log schemes is essential to take advantage of the dividing topology.
However, it is not clear what is the useful definition of a
saturated animated
monoid.

This is the reason why we have to stick to discrete fs log schemes when we need to use the dividing Nisnevich topology later.

Proposition 7.13
.

There is an equivalence

\mathrm{Shv}_{sNis}(\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lAff}))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Shv}_{sNis}(\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lSch})).

Similar equivalences hold for
\mathcal{S}\mathrm{hv}(-,\mathcal{S}\mathrm{pc})
and
\mathcal{S}\mathrm{hv}(-,\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab}))
.

Proof.

Suppose
(X\to S)
is an object of
\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lSch})
.
Choose a strict Nisnevich cover
S^{\prime}\to S
such that
S^{\prime}\in\mathrm{lAff}
, and choose a strict Nisnevich cover
X^{\prime}\to X\times_{S}S^{\prime}
such that
X^{\prime}\in\mathrm{lAff}
.
Then the morphism
(X^{\prime}\to S^{\prime})\to(X\to S)
is a strict Nisnevich cover.
Hence every object of
\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lSch})
has a strict Nisnevich cover in
\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lSch})
.
Proposition
6.8
finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 7.29

(Comparison to Olsson’s cotangent complex)
.

As explained in Section
3
,
we use Gabber’s log cotangent complex.
It is a more or less direct extension to the context of pre-log (animated) rings of the classical construction due to
Illusie and Quillen.
There is another approach to the deformation theory of log schemes:
In
[
43
, Definition 3.2]
,
Olsson defines,
for every morphism
f\colon X\to S
of fine log schemes,
a projective system
\mathbb{L}^{O}_{X/S}=(\mathbb{L}^{\geq-n}_{X/S})_{n}

of essentially constant ind-objects in
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}})
.
As such,
a direct comparison with
\mathbb{L}_{X/S}
considered in this paper (and in
[
43
, §8]
) is not immediate.
We will make a condition comparison, in the following sense.

Assume the existence of an object
\mathbb{L}^{O}\in\mathcal{P}\mathrm{sh}(\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lSch}),\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab}))
with the following two formal properties:

(i)

The projective system

(\cdots\to\tau_{\geq-n-1}\mathbb{L}^{O}(X\to S)\to\tau_{\geq-n}\mathbb{L}^{O}(X\to S)\to\cdots\to\tau_{\geq 0}\mathbb{L}^{O}(X\to S))

is canonically equivalent to
\mathbb{L}_{X/S}^{O}
.

(ii)

There exists a map
\mathbb{L}\to\mathbb{L}^{O}
such that for all integers
n\geq 0
, the canonical map

\tau_{\geq-n}\mathbb{L}(X\to S)\to\tau_{\geq-n}\mathbb{L}^{O}(X\to S)

is naturally induced by the map constructed in
[
43
, §8.31]
.

In other words,
\mathbb{L}^{O}(-)
is a presheaf on the arrow
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lSch})
whose Postnikov tower is
precisely the projective system considered by Olsson.

We claim that under the above assumptions,
the map
\mathbb{L}\to\mathbb{L}^{O}
becomes an equivalence after dividing Nisnevich sheafification.
Indeed,
let
\mathrm{iAr}
be the full subcategory of
\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lSch})
consisting of the integral morphisms in
\mathrm{lSch}
.
If
(X\to S)
is an object of
\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lSch})
,
then there exists a dividing cover
(X^{\prime}\to S^{\prime})
of
(X\to S)
with
(X^{\prime}\to S^{\prime})\in\mathrm{iAr}
by

[
26
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Proposition
6.8
gives an equivalence

\mathrm{Shv}_{dNis}(\mathrm{iAr},\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab}))\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathrm{Shv}_{dNis}(\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lSch}),\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab})).

To conclude,
we observe that the restriction of
\mathbb{L}\to\mathbb{L}^{O}
to
\mathrm{iAr}
is an equivalence owing to
[
43
, Corollary 8.34]
.

8.2.

This subsection aims to show that hypercohomology with coefficients in

Proposition 8.3
.

Suppose

is a log smooth morphism in
\mathrm{lSch}
.
There is a canonical quasi-isomorphism

(8.1)

\Omega_{X/S}^{d}\simeq R_{Zar}p_{*}\Omega_{X\times\overline{\Box}/S}^{d}

for every integer
d\geq 0
, where
p\colon\underline{X}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\underline{X}
denotes the projection.

Proof.

By
[
41
, Proposition IV.1.2.15]
, there is a canonical isomorphism

\Omega_{X\times\overline{\Box}/S}^{1}\cong p^{*}\Omega_{X/S}^{1}\oplus\Omega_{X\times\overline{\Box}/X}^{1}.

By taking exterior powers we obtain an isomorphism

\Omega_{X\times\overline{\Box}/S}^{d}\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{d}p^{*}\Omega_{X/S}^{j}\otimes\Omega_{X\times\overline{\Box}/X}^{d-j}.

Since
\Omega_{X/S}^{j}
is locally free by
[
41
, Proposition IV.3.2.1]
,
the projection formula gives a quasi-isomorphism

R_{Zar}p_{*}\Omega_{X\times\overline{\Box}/S}^{d}\simeq\bigoplus_{j=0}^{d}\Omega_{X/S}^{j}\otimes R_{Zar}p_{*}\Omega_{X\times\overline{\Box}/X}^{d-j}.

It remains to check that the naturally induced morphism

(8.2)

\Omega_{X/X}^{d}\to R_{Zar}p_{*}\Omega_{X\times\overline{\Box}/X}^{d}

is a quasi-isomorphism.
By
[
41
, Proposition IV.1.2.15]
,
there is a canonical isomorphism

\Omega_{X\times\overline{\Box}/X}^{1}\cong\Omega_{\underline{X}\times\overline{\Box}/\underline{X}}^{1}.

Hence we reduce to the case when
X=\underline{X}
.
We may also assume that
X
is the spectrum of a local ring
A
.
Elementary computations show that there are isomorphisms

\displaystyle\Omega_{X\times\overline{\Box}/X}^{1}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{1})\cong A[t]dt,\quad\Omega_{X\times\overline{\Box}/X}^{1}(X\times(\mathbb{P}^{1}-0))\cong A[1/t]dt/t,

\displaystyle\Omega_{X\times\overline{\Box}/X}^{1}(X\times\mathbb{G}_{m})\cong A[t,1/t]dt.

By computing the Čech cohomology of the Zariski cover
\{X\times\mathbb{A}^{1},X\times(\mathbb{P}^{1}-0)\to X\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\}
,
we see that (
8.2
) is a quasi-isomorphism for
d=1
.
Moreover,
\Omega_{X\times\overline{\Box}/X}^{d}=0
if
d>1
, so (
8.2
) is a quasi-isomorphism for
d>1
.
If
d=0
, then (
8.2
) becomes
\mathcal{O}_{X}\simeq R_{Zar}p_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X\times\mathbb{P}^{1}}
.
∎

0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

The convergent spectral sequence

E_{2}^{pq}:=H_{Zar}^{p}(\mathbb{P}_{S}^{n},\Omega_{\mathbb{P}_{S}^{n}}^{-q})\Rightarrow\pi_{-p-q}\logHH(\mathbb{P}_{S}^{n}/S)

obtained from Proposition
8.13
finishes the proof.
∎

For topological Hochschild homology of schemes,
the following result is a special case of the projective bundle formula
[
14
, Theorem 1.5]
.

Proposition 8.15
.

Suppose
S\in\mathrm{lSch}
and
X\in\mathrm{lSch}/S
.
There is a canonical equivalence in
\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab})

\logHH(X\times\mathbb{P}^{n}/S)\simeq\logHH(X/S)^{\oplus n+1}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be the full subcategory of
\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lSch})
consisting of morphisms whose targets are in
\mathrm{lAff}
.
As in Proposition
7.13
, there is an equivalence

\mathrm{Shv}_{sNis}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab}))\simeq\mathrm{Shv}_{sNis}(\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lSch}),\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab})).

Hence it suffices to give a natural equivalence

((X\to S)\in\mathcal{C}\mapsto\logHH(X\times\mathbb{P}^{n}/S))\xrightarrow{\simeq}((X\to S)\in\mathcal{C}\mapsto\logHH(X/S)^{\oplus n+1}).

Due to Proposition
8.12
, we reduce to constructing a natural equivalence

(S\in\mathrm{lAff}\mapsto\logHH(S\times\mathbb{P}^{n}/S))\xrightarrow{\simeq}(S\in\mathrm{lAff}\mapsto\logHH(S/S)^{\oplus n+1}).

This follows from Proposition
8.14
.
∎

Hence for
S\in\mathrm{Sch}
, we obtain an equivalence in
\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)

(8.13)

\logHH(-/S)\simeq\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\logHH(-/S),

where
\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}:=\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}(M(\mathbb{P}^{1}/\mathrm{pt}),-)
, and
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}
denotes the internal Hom functor.

Definition 8.16
.

The logarithmic Hochschild homology of
S\in\mathrm{Sch}
is given by

\mathbf{logHH}(-/S):=(\logHH(-/S),\logHH(-/S),\ldots)\in\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S),

where the bonding maps are given by (
8.13
).

For every map
(R,P)\to(A,P)
of log rings,
\logHH((A,M)/(R,P))
is an animated commutative ring.
Hence
\logHH
is an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring in
\mathrm{Psh}(\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lAff}),\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab}))
. The localization functor is symmetric monoidal, so
\logHH
is an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring in

\mathrm{Shv}_{sNis}(\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lAff}),\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab}))\simeq\mathrm{Shv}_{sNis}(\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lSch}),\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab})).

The restriction and localization functors

\mathrm{Shv}_{sNis}(\mathrm{Fun}(\Delta^{1},\mathrm{lSch}),\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab}))\to\mathrm{Shv}_{sNis}(\mathrm{lSm}/S,\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab}))\to\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}^{\mathrm{eff}}(S)

are symmetric monoidal too, so
\logHH
is an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring in
\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}^{\mathrm{eff}}(S)
.

By

[
8
, Construction 6.2.3]
,
one can show that
\mathbf{logHH}(-/S)
is a homotopy commutative monoid in
\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)
,
i.e., there are multiplication and unit maps

\mathbf{logHH}(-/S)\wedge\mathbf{logHH}(-/S)\to\mathbf{logHH}(-/S)\text{ and }\mathbbm{1}\to\mathbf{logHH}(-/S)

satisfying the associative, commutative, and unital axioms in the homotopy category of
\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)
.
For all
X\in\mathrm{lSm}/S
,
there is a canonical equivalence

(8.14)

\mathrm{map}_{\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)}(M(X),\mathbf{logHH}(-/S))\simeq\logHH(X/S),

where
\mathrm{map}_{\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)}
denotes the mapping spectrum in
\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)
,
and we regard the right hand side as a spectrum using the Dold-Kan correspondence in
[
37
, §1.2.4]
.
Moreover,
for all
X\in\mathrm{lSm}/S
,
there are canonical equivalences

(8.15)

\begin{split}\mathbf{logHH}(-/S)=(\logHH(-/S),\logHH(-/S),\ldots)\simeq&(\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\logHH(-/S),\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\logHH(-/S),\ldots)\\
\simeq&\mathbf{logHH}(-/S)(-1)[-2].\end{split}

We note that (
8.15
) is analogous to the Bott periodicity witnessed in the algebraic and complex
K
-theory spectra

[
60
]
.

The next notion is a direct adaptation of

[
8
, Definition 7.1.1]

to
\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)
.

Definition 8.17
.

Suppose
S\in\mathrm{Sch}
.
A homotopy commutative monoid
\mathbb{E}
in
\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)
is called
oriented
if there is a class

c_{\infty}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)}(M(\mathbb{P}_{S}^{\infty}/\mathrm{pt}),\mathbb{E}(1)[2])

whose restriction to
M(\mathbb{P}_{S}^{1}/\mathrm{pt})
is given by

M(\mathbb{P}_{S}^{1}/\mathrm{pt})\otimes\mathbbm{1}\colon M(\mathbb{P}_{S}^{1}/\mathrm{pt})\to M(\mathbb{P}_{S}^{1}/\mathrm{pt})\otimes\mathbb{E}\simeq\mathbb{E}(1)[2],

where
\mathbbm{1}
denotes the unit of
\mathbb{E}
, and
M(\mathbb{P}_{S}^{\infty}/\mathrm{pt}):=\mathop{\mathrm{colim}}_{n\to\infty}M(\mathbb{P}_{S}^{n}/\mathrm{pt})
.

Theorem 8.18
.

The homotopy commutative monoid
\mathbf{logHH}(-/S)\in\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)
is oriented for all
S\in\mathrm{Sch}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
8.15
, there are isomorphisms

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)}(M(\mathbb{P}_{S}^{\infty}/\mathrm{pt}),\mathbf{logHH}(-/S)(1)[2])\cong\lim_{n\to\infty}{\pi_{0}}\logHH(\mathbb{P}_{S}^{n}/S)\cong\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\Gamma(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}).

The element
(1,0,\ldots)
of the above infinite product gives an orientation class.
∎

9.
Generalized residue sequences for
\logHH

Let
S=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
be an affine scheme. In Section
8
we have seen how

\logHH(-/S)\in\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}(\mathrm{Shv}_{dNis}(\mathrm{SmAff}_{S},\mathcal{D}(\mathrm{Ab})))

gives rise to a homotopy commutative monoid object
\mathbf{logHH}(-/S)
in
\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)
.
Combined with the formal properties of the motivic category
\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)
we will deduce sequences relating logarithmic and ordinary
Hochschild homology. The fundamental ingredient is the Gysin, or residue, sequence in
\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)
.
Let us recall the statement from
[
10
]
.

Definition 9.1

(
[
10
, Definition 7.4.3]
)
.

Suppose
S\in\mathrm{Sch}
,
X\in\mathrm{lSm}/S
, and
\mathcal{E}\to X
is a vector bundle (i.e.,
\xi\colon\underline{\mathcal{E}}\to\underline{X}
is a vector bundle on the underlying scheme
\underline{X}
, and the log structure on
\mathcal{E}
is the pullback of the log structure on
X
via
\xi
).
The
Thom motive of
\mathcal{E}
over
X
is defined to be

MTh(\mathcal{E}):=M(\mathcal{E}/(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}\mathcal{E},E))\in\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S),

where
Z
is the zero section of
\mathcal{E}
, and
E
is the exceptional divisor.

Definition 9.2
.

Suppose
S\in\mathrm{Sch}
,
X\in\mathrm{Sm}/S
,
Z_{1}+\cdots+Z_{r}
is a strict normal crossing divisor on
X
, and
Z
is a smooth closed subscheme of
X
that is strict normal crossing with
Z_{1}+\cdots+Z_{r}
in the sense of
[
10
, Definition 7.2.1]
.
Let
p\colon N_{Z}X\to Z
be the projection, where
N_{Z}X
denotes the normal bundle of
Z
in
X
.
We set

N_{Z}(X,Z_{1}+\cdots+Z_{r}):=N_{Z}X\times_{Z}(Z,Z\cap Z_{1}+\cdots+Z\cap Z_{r}).

Observe that
N_{Z}(X,Z_{1}+\cdots+Z_{r})
is a vector bundle over
(Z,Z\cap Z_{1}+\cdots+Z\cap Z_{r})
.

Theorem 9.3
.

There exists a canonical cofiber sequence

M(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X,\widetilde{Z}_{1}+\cdots+\widetilde{Z}_{r}+E)\xrightarrow{M(u)}M(X,Z_{1}+\cdots+Z_{r})\to MTh(N_{Z}(X,Z_{1}+\cdots+Z_{r}))

in
\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)
, where
\widetilde{Z}_{i}
is the strict transform of
Z_{i}
in
\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X
for
i=1,\ldots,r
,
E
is the exceptional divisor on
\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X
, and
u\colon(\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X,\widetilde{Z}_{1}+\cdots+\widetilde{Z}_{r}+E)\to(X,Z_{1}+\cdots+Z_{r})
is the obvious morphism.

Proof.

We refer to
[
10
, Theorem 7.5.4]
.
∎

Theorem 9.4
.

Suppose
S\in\mathrm{Sch}
and
\mathbb{E}
is an oriented homotopy commutative monoid in
\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)
.
For
X\in\mathrm{lSm}/S
, a rank
d
vector bundle
\mathcal{E}\to X
, and integers
p
and
q
, there is a canonical equivalence

(9.1)

\mathrm{map}_{\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)}(M(X),\mathbb{E}(q)[p])\simeq\mathrm{map}_{\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)}(MTh(\mathcal{E}),\mathbb{E}(q+d)[p+2d]).

Proof.

When
S
is the spectrum of a perfect field
k
with resolution of singularities,
and
\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)
is replaced by the version with transfers
\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DM}(k)
,
this is a consequence of
[
10
, Theorem 8.3.7]
.
Indeed,
there is an isomorphism
MTh(\mathcal{E})\simeq M(X)(d)[2d]
,
and an orientation on
\mathbb{E}
is not needed.
The equivalence (
9.1
) holds in greater generality:
our proof of

[
8
, Proposition 7.3.9]

adapts to
\mathrm{log}\mathcal{DA}(S)
under the assumption that
\mathbb{E}
is oriented.
∎

Theorem 9.5

(Generalized residue sequence)
.

With the notations of Definition
9.2
and Theorem
9.3
, there exists a canonical cofiber sequence

\displaystyle\logHH((Z,Z\cap Z_{1}+\cdots+Z\cap Z_{r})/S)\to

\displaystyle\logHH((X,Z_{1}+\cdots+Z_{r})/S)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{u^{*}}

\displaystyle\logHH((\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X,\widetilde{Z}_{1}+\cdots+\widetilde{Z}_{r}+E)/S).

The above cofiber sequence is called the
Gysin (or residue) sequence for
\logHH
.

As a particular case, we obtain a canonical cofiber sequence

(9.2)

\HH(Z/S)\to\HH(X/S)\to\logHH((\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X,E)/S).

Proof.

Apply Theorem
9.4
to the oriented homotopy commutative monoid
\mathbf{logHH}(-/S)
and use (
8.14
),
(
8.15
), and Theorem
9.3
.
∎

The interested reader can note that (
9.2
) is similar to the generalized Gysin sequence available
for the cohomology of reciprocity sheaves in
[
11
, Theorem 7.16]
.

9.6.
Comparison of cofiber sequences

Let
A
be a discrete commutative ring and let
a\in A
be a non-zero divisor. By
[
51
, Theorem 5.5, Example 5.7]
, there is a cofiber sequence

\THH(A)\to\THH(A,\langle a\rangle)\to\THH(A/(a))[1],

where
\langle a\rangle\to(A,\cdot)
is the pre-log structure generated by
a
. Here
\THH(A,\langle a\rangle)
denotes log topological Hochschild homology in the sense of
[
51
]
. By cobase change along the augmentation
\THH({\mathbb{Z}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}
,

Proposition

5.6
and
[
34
, Lemma 5.4]
yield the cofiber sequence

(9.3)

{\rm HH}(A)\to{\rm logHH}(A,\langle a\rangle)\to{\rm HH}(A/(a))[1].

We point out that the cofiber sequence (
9.3
) is a special case of Theorem
9.5
. Indeed, set
S={\rm Spec}({\mathbb{Z}})
(with trivial log structure),
X={\rm Spec}({\mathbb{Z}}[x])
and
Z={\rm Spec}({\mathbb{Z}})
in (
9.2
), so that the resulting cofiber sequence reads

{\rm HH}({\mathbb{Z}}[x])\to{\rm logHH}({\mathbb{Z}}[x],\langle x\rangle\times{\rm GL}_{1}({\mathbb{Z}}[x]))\to{\rm HH}({\mathbb{Z}})[1].

Now we cobase-change along
{\rm HH}({\mathbb{Z}}[x])\to{\rm HH}(A)
and apply logification invariance of
{\rm logHH}
to obtain a cofiber sequence of the form (
9.3
). Logification invariance of
{\rm logHH}
can be seen using cobase change along
\THH({\mathbb{Z}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}
and a similar equivalence in log
\THH
, see
[
51
, Theorem 4.24]
, or by using our Quillen spectral sequence and the analogous statement for the
log cotangent complex in
[
43
, Theorem 8.16]
.

When
a\in A
is a non-zero divisor, the subscheme
Z=\operatorname{Spec}(A/(a))
is an effective Cartier divisor in
X
, and clearly
\mathrm{Bl}_{Z}X=X
, i.e., the blow-up is irrelevant. It is then clear by construction that the sequences (
9.2
) and (
9.3
) agree.

Remark 9.7
.

Theorem
9.5
extends the results in
[
51
]
in two directions.
First,
it works for log schemes (and not just for log rings),
and the log structure is allowed to be non-trivial in all the terms of the sequence.
Second,
it allows for a higher-dimensional generalization of the residue sequence.
Allowing for non-affine log schemes is essential to that end.

To explain it better, let us consider the following situation. Let
R
be a noetherian commutative ring of finite Krull dimension, and let
A
be a smooth
R
-algebra. Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}
be a regular sequence in
A
, such that the quotient
A/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})
is a smooth complete intersection
R
-algebra. Then, since the normal bundle of
Z:=\operatorname{Spec}(A/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}))
in
X:=\operatorname{Spec}(A)
is trivial,
(
9.2
) reads as

\HH(A/R)\to\logHH((\mathrm{Bl}_{(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})}\operatorname{Spec}(A),E)/R)\to\HH((A/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}))/R)[1],

where
E\cong\mathbb{P}(N_{Z}X)\cong\mathbb{P}_{Z}^{r-1}
.
For
r=1
,
using the HKR Theorem,
we obtain the classical residue sequence (see e.g.,
[
20
, 2.3.a]
)

0\to\Omega^{n}_{A/R}\to\Omega^{n}_{A/R}(\log f)\xrightarrow{Res}\Omega^{n-1}_{(A/f)/R}\to 0.

Note that
the map
\pi_{n}\HH((A/f_{1})/R)\to\pi_{n}\HH(A/R)
is trivial for all
n\geq 0
in this case since
the differentials inject into the log differentials.

We end with a discussion about residue sequences in log
\THH
,
and how this could potentially relate to the results of this paper:

Remark 9.8
.

Quillen’s localization theorem
[
46
, §5]
produces a cofiber sequence

K(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to K(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\to K(\mathbb{F}_{p})[1]

in algebraic
K
-theory, while Blumberg–Mandell

[
13
, Localization Theorem]
establish a cofiber sequence

K({\rm ku}_{p})\to K({\rm KU}_{p})\to K(\mathbb{Z}_{p})[1].

Here
{\rm ku}_{p}
and
{\rm KU}_{p}
are the connective and periodic complex (
p
-complete)
K
-theory spectra.
These examples answer the following question in the affirmative for
n=0,1
: Is there a cofiber sequence

(9.4)

K(BP\langle n\rangle)\to K(BP\langle n\rangle[v_{n}^{-1}])\to K(BP\langle n-1\rangle)[1]

for the truncated Brown–Peterson spectrum
BP\langle n\rangle
? For
n\geq 2
,
such cofiber sequences were proven to
not
exist in
[
1
]
.

The search for cofiber sequences in log
\THH
is often motivated by the problem of finding close approximations
of cofiber sequences in algebraic
K
-theory that do not exist in ordinary Hochschild homology:
Such a perspective is appealing for the examples of
[
51
]
.

We sketch a possible construction of
a cofiber sequence

(9.5)

{\rm THH}(BP\langle n\rangle)\to{\rm logTHH}(BP\langle n\rangle,\langle v_{n}\rangle_{*})\to{\rm THH}(BP\langle n-1\rangle)[1].

This is a cofiber sequence in log
{\rm THH}
involving
BP\langle n\rangle
for which there is no corresponding
K
-theory sequence (
9.4
) when
n\geq 2
. The truncated Brown–Peterson spectra
BP\langle n\rangle
do in general not admit the structure of
{\mathbb{E}}_{\infty}
-rings
[
31
, Theorem 1.1.2]
, and hence they do not fall under the framework of logarithmic
{\rm THH}
as developed by Rognes–Sagave–Schlichtkrull
[
51
]
,
[
52
]
. We shall combine groundbreaking work of Hahn–Wilson
[
23
]
with a construction of Sagave–Schlichtkrull
[
54
]
to give an
ad hoc
definition of
{\rm logTHH}(BP\langle n\rangle,\langle v_{n}\rangle_{*})
participating in cofiber sequences (
9.5
).

We start with the cofiber
sequence

{\rm THH}({\rm MUP}_{\geq 0})\to{\rm logTHH}({\rm MUP}_{\geq 0},V^{\cal U}_{\geq 0})\to{\rm THH}({\rm MU})[1]

of Sagave–Schlichtkrull
[
54
, Example 8.6]
. Here
{\rm MUP}_{\geq 0}=\oplus_{i\geq 0}{\rm MU}[2i]
is the positively weighted part of the periodic complex cobordism spectrum
{\rm MUP}
, equipped with a log structure
V^{\cal U}_{\geq 0}
localizing to
{\rm MUP}
. Subobjects of
{\rm MUP}_{\geq 0}

whose weights are multiples of
p^{n}-1
are denoted by
{\rm MU}[y]
in Hahn–Wilson
[
23
, §2.1]
. We think of
{\rm MU}[y]
as a polynomial ring over
{\rm MU}
, and Sagave–Schlichtkrull’s argument still applies to produce a cofiber sequence

(9.6)

{\rm THH}({\rm MU}[y])\to{\rm logTHH}({\rm MU}[y],\langle y\rangle_{*})\to{\rm THH}({\rm MU})[1].

As part of their construction of
\mathbb{E}_{3}
-
{\rm MU}
-algebra structures on
BP\langle n\rangle
, Hahn–Wilson
[
23
]
construct
\mathbb{E}_{3}
-
{\rm MU}[y]
-algebra structures on
BP\langle n\rangle
, where
y
acts by
v_{n}
. Applying a cobase change of (
9.6
) along the resulting map
{\rm THH}({\rm MU}[y])\to{\rm THH}(BP\langle n\rangle)

gives (
9.5
), where
{\rm logTHH}(BP\langle n\rangle,\langle v_{n}\rangle_{*})
is defined as the
cobase change of
{\rm logTHH}({\rm MU}[y],\langle y\rangle_{*})
along
{\rm THH}({\rm MU}[y])\to{\rm THH}(BP\langle n\rangle)
. A version of this sequence will appear in the forthcoming work of Ausoni–Bayındır–Moulinos.
This further accentuates the difference between the behavior of cofiber sequences in log
{\rm THH}
and localization sequences in algebraic
K
-theory.

One may attempt to define log structures on
BP\langle n\rangle
generated by all the
v_{i}
’s in the hope of obtaining
a sequence in
{\rm logTHH}
analogous to the
K
-theory sequence of Barwick–Lawson
[
3
, Corollary 5.2]
.
By
[
23
, Remark 2.1.2]
, the approach sketched here does not apply to produce such cofiber sequences in
{\rm logTHH}
. However, Theorem
9.5
suggests one way to approach this problem: Replace
BP\langle n\rangle
with an appropriate blow-up in the context of spectral algebraic geometry and prove that it participates in
a
{\rm logTHH}
residue sequence. In upcoming work, we hope to address this question, as well as how the resulting sequence relates to variants of algebraic
K
-theory.

Regularity of the distance function from arbitrary closed sets
Sławomir Kolasiński, Mario Santilli

Abstract
We investigate the distance function 𝜹 K ϕ \bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi} from an arbitrary closed subset K K of a finite-dimensional Banach space ( 𝐑 n , ϕ ) (\mathbf{R}^{n},\phi) , equipped with a uniformly convex 𝒞 2 \mathscr{C}^{2} -norm ϕ \phi . These spaces are known as Minkowski spaces and they are one of the fundamental spaces of Finslerian geometry (see [ MSW01 ] ). We prove that the gradient of 𝜹 K ϕ \bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi} satisfies a Lipschitz property on the complement of the ϕ \phi -cut-locus of K K (a.k.a. the medial axis of 𝐑 n ​ ∼ ⁡ K \mathbf{R}^{n}\without K ) and we prove a structural result for the set of points outside K K where 𝜹 K ϕ \bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi} is pointwise twice differentiable, providing an answer to a question raised by Hiriart-Urruty in [ HU82 ] . Our results give sharp generalisations of some classical results in the theory of distance functions and they are motivated by critical low-regularity examples for which the available results gives no meaningful or very restricted informations. The results of this paper find natural applications in the theory of partial differential equations and in convex geometry.



1
Introduction

For the basic notation we refer the reader to section
2.1
.

Suppose
K\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n}
is a closed set and
\phi
is a uniformly
convex norm on
\mathbf{R}^{n}
; cf.
2.8
. Our central
object of study is the
\phi
-distance function

(1)

\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(x)=\inf\bigl\{\phi(y-x):y\in K\bigr\}\quad\text{for $x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}$}\,.

We investigate in detail the set of points where
\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
is not
differentiable and then also the set of points where it is not pointwise twice
differentiable. Define

(2)

\Sigma^{\phi}(K)=(\mathbf{R}^{n}\without K)\cap\bigl\{x:\textrm{$\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}$ is not differentiable at $x$}\bigr\}\,.

A basic and fundamental result in the theory of distance functions asserts what follows.

1.1 Theorem

(
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
-regularity)
.

If
K\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n}
is an arbitrary closed set, then

\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
is
\mathcal{C}^{1}
with a locally Lipschitz gradient on
the open subset

U:=\mathbf{R}^{n}\without\big(K\cup\mathop{\mathrm{Clos}}\Sigma^{\phi}(K)\big)
.

This result can be deduced employing general results from the theory
of Hamilton-Jacobi equations (see
[
Lio82
, Theorem 15.1]

or
[
Fat03
]
). Indeed, for a general closed set
K
it is well known
that
\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
is a locally semiconcave function on
\mathbf{R}^{n}\without K
and it satisfies, in a viscosity sense, the Eikonal equation
\phi^{\ast}(\grad u)=1
on
\mathbf{R}^{n}\without K
(where
\phi^{\ast}
is the
dual norm of
\phi
as defined in
2.7
); see
[
Asp73
]
,

[
Lio82
]
,
[
Zaj83a
]
. For the Euclidean norm Theorem
1.1
can
also be obtained using a purely geometric argument (see

[
Fed59
, 4.8]
).

Of course, the conclusion of the theorem can be improved if we know that
K

is at least a
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-submanifold. In fact, in this case

\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
is at least of class
\mathscr{C}^{2}
on the open subset
U

and
\mathop{\mathrm{Clos}}\Sigma^{\phi}(K)
is a set of
\mathscr{L}^{n}
-measure zero; if
K
is
a
\mathcal{C}^{2,1}
-submanifold, then
\mathop{\mathrm{Clos}}\Sigma^{\phi}(K)
is a set of
locally finite
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
-measure; see
[
IT01
]
,
[
MM03
]
,

[
LN05a
]
,
[
LN05b
]
, and
[
CM07
]
. A sufficient condition
that guarantees
\mathscr{L}^{n}(\mathop{\mathrm{Clos}}\Sigma(K))=0
for closed

\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
-hypersurfaces
K
in terms of the inner radius of
curvature is given in
[
Miu16
, Theorem 4.1]
. Moreover, if
K
is a
closed
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-hypersurface, then
[
Miu16
, Theorem 1.3]

provides a necessary and sufficient condition for a point

x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\without K
to lie in
\mathbf{R}^{n}\without\mathop{\mathrm{Clos}}(\Sigma(K))
.

On the other hand, it turns out that the
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-regularity is a
critical hypothesis; indeed the second named author has shown,
in
[
San21
]
, that for a convex open subset
\Omega
with
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
-boundary the set
\mathop{\mathrm{Clos}}\Sigma^{\phi}(\mathbf{R}^{n}\without\Omega)
might have non empty interior in
\Omega
; moreover, for a typical
(in the sense of Baire Category) convex open subset
\Omega
with
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-boundary we have that
\Sigma^{\phi}(\mathbf{R}^{n}\without\Omega)
is dense in
\Omega
. There exist even closed
\mathcal{C}^{1,\alpha}
-hypersurfaces
K
such that
\Sigma^{\phi}(K)
is dense in all of
\mathbf{R}^{n}
; see
[
San21
, Corollary 2.9]
. In all these examples one can
choose
\phi
to be the Euclidean norm. Therefore, the set
U
defined
in
1.1
might easily be empty even if
K=\mathbf{R}^{n}\without\Omega
and
\Omega
is a convex open subset with
\mathcal{C}^{1}
boundary, or
might reduce to a small tubular neighbourhood around
K
if
\Omega
has a
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
boundary. Consequently Theorem
1.1
provides no (or
very limited) information in these situations. On the other hand it is well
known that the gradient of
\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
is a continuous map on its domain
\mathbf{R}^{n}\without(K\cup\Sigma^{\phi}(K))
. Therefore, it is a natural to
ask for a characterisation of the largest set on which the gradient of
\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
satisfies a Lipschitz condition. We identify that set in
Theorem
1.4
, providing an effective sharp generalization of
Theorem
1.1
that is applicable in the aforementioned critical
low-regularity cases.

Besides its central role in Theorem
1.1
, the set
\Sigma^{\phi}(K)

has been extensively studied in the last decades. Indeed, if we define the

\phi
-nearest point projection

\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}
to be the multivalued
function (see
2.11
and
2.28
) mapping a point
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
into the set

(3)

\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}(x)=K\cap\bigl\{a:\phi(x-a)=\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(x)\bigr\},\,

then it is well known that
\Sigma^{\phi}(K)
is precisely the set of points
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\sim K
where
\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}(x)
is not a singleton. It
is remarkable that
\Sigma^{\phi}(K)
can be always covered by countably many

\mathcal{C}^{2}
-hypersurfaces (see
[
Zaj79
]
and
[
Haj22
]
);
moreover upper bounds on its Hausdorff measure are known (see

[
AAC92
]
). Lower bounds and results on the propagation of the
non-differentiability points can be obtained from
[
AC99
]
,

[
AC02
]
and
[
CY09
]
. The topological properties of the set
\Sigma^{\phi}(K)
in a Euclidean or Riemannian setting are studied
in
[
CP01
]
,
[
Lie04
]
,
[
ACNS13
]
.

Since
\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
is locally semiconcave outside
K
, it is a natural
question to investigate the set of points
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\sim K
where
\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
is pointwise twice differentiable, which means the set of points
where the function admits a second-order Taylor polynomial;
see
2.22
. Thus, we consider the set

(4)

\Sigma^{\phi}_{2}(K)=(\mathbf{R}^{n}\without K)\cap\bigl\{x:\textrm{$\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}$ is not pointwise twice differentiable at $x$}\bigr\}\,.

A classical theorem on the twice differentiability of convex functions of
Alexandrov (see
[
Ale39
]
) readily implies the following result.

1.2 Theorem
.

If
K\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n}
is an arbitrary closed set, then
\mathscr{L}^{n}(\Sigma^{\phi}_{2}(K))=0
.

The example in
4.19
shows that the dimension of the
set
\Sigma^{\phi}_{2}(K)
might be exactly
n
even if
K
is a closed
convex body with
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
-boundary. On the other hand, it is
natural to ask about the structure of
\Sigma^{\phi}_{2}(K)
for a general
closed set
K
; however, nothing is known in the literature.
The problem, in the Euclidean setting, goes back at least to
[
HU82
]
(see
last paragraph on page 458). We remark that the set of
twice-differentiability points of the
\phi
-distance function
\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}

corresponds to the set of differentiability points of the
\phi
-nearest point
projection
\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}
; see

2.41
(e)
. Only
if
K
is convex sharp results are
available, that describe the structure of
\Sigma^{\phi}_{2}(K)
in terms of the
unit
\phi
-normal bundle
of
K
. This is
defined for an arbitrary closed set
K\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n}
as

(5)

N^{\phi}(K)=\bigl\{(a,\eta):a\in K,\;\eta\in\mathbf{R}^{n},\;\phi(\eta)=1,\;\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(a+s\eta)=s\;\textrm{for some $s>0$}\bigr\}\,.

We recall that
N^{\phi}(K)
is a Borel and countably
(n-1)
-rectifiable
subset of
\mathbf{R}^{2n}
; cf.
[
DRKS20
, Lemma 5.2]
, see

[
Fed69
, 3.2.14(2)]
for the notion of rectifiability.

1.3 Theorem
.

Suppose
K\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n}
is
convex. Then there exists
Z\subseteq N^{\phi}(K)
with
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(Z)=0
such that

\Sigma^{\phi}_{2}(K)=\{a+r\eta:0<r<\infty,\;(a,\eta)\in Z\}.

In particular, for
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
almost all
(a,\eta)\in N^{\phi}(K)

the distance function
\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
is pointwise twice differentiable at
all points of the ray
\{a+r\eta:0<r<+\infty\}
.

The exceptional set
Z
cannot be excluded. In fact,
even if
\phi
is the Euclidean norm, there exist convex bodies
K
with

\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
boundaries such that the set
Z
is dense in

N^{\phi}(K)
with Hausdorff dimension
n-1
; see

4.19
. Indeed, the construction of the

\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
-convex hypersurface in Theorem
4.19
shows
that one can choose
Z
to be somewhat arbitrarily complicated. In the Euclidean setting
Theorem
1.3
is a classical fact in convex
geometry; see
[
Sch14
]
. The general anisotropic version in Theorem
1.3
can be proved employing Theorem
1.1
and following a similar argument. We also remark that for
n=2
Theorem 1.3 can be deduced from a more general statement in
[
BHS16
]
. See also
[
Hug99
]
for related results.

Our Theorem
1.5
extends Theorem
1.3
to arbitrary closed sets and it gives the first answer to the question
of Hiriart-Urruty, providing a new insight into the structure of
\Sigma^{\phi}_{2}(K)
.

1.1
The main results of the present paper

In addition to the notions already introduced in the previous section, we
introduce here a few additional definitions and facts. Here
K\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n}
is always an arbitrary closed set. The
\phi
-reach of
K

is the function
\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}:N^{\phi}(K)\to(0,+\infty]
given by

(6)

\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)=\sup\bigl\{s>0:\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(a+s\eta)=s\bigr\}\qquad\text{for $(a,\eta)\in N^{\phi}(K)$}\,.

Simple arguments show that
\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}
is upper semicontinuous;
see
2.35
. Moreover, we define
\Cut^{\phi}(K)
, the

\phi
-cut locus of
K
, by

(7)

\Cut^{\phi}(K)=\bigl\{a+\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)\eta:(a,\eta)\in N^{\phi}(K)\bigr\}\,.

In view of this last definition, the number
\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)
can be seen
as
the
\phi
-distance of
a
from the cut locus of
K
in
direction
\eta
; indeed, this function plays a central role in the seminal
work
[
IT01
]
, where it is proved to be Lipschitz continuous provided
K
is a smooth submanifold, and in other papers on the subject; see for
instance
[
LN05a
]
and
[
CM07
]
). Note that (see
Remark
4.1
)

\Sigma^{\phi}(K)\subseteq\Cut^{\phi}(K)\subseteq\mathop{\mathrm{Clos}}\Sigma^{\phi}(K)\,.

We notice also that
\mathscr{L}^{n}(\Cut^{\phi}(K))=0
; see
Remark
4.4
. Since
\Sigma^{\phi}(K)
might not be nowhere dense,
the same is true for
\Cut^{\phi}(K)
. Observe that
\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}
induces a
natural fibration

\mathbf{R}^{n}\without(\Cut^{\phi}(K)\cup K)=\bigl\{a+\rho\eta:(a,\eta)\in N^{\phi}(K)\,,0<\rho<\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)\bigr\}\,.

Our goal is to study regularity properties of
\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
on
\mathbf{R}^{n}\without(\Cut^{\phi}(K)\cup K)
. To this end we look at the sets of points of

\mathbf{R}^{n}\without(\Cut^{\phi}(K)\cup K)
with a
uniform
positive
relative
\phi
-distance to the cut-locus; in other words, we consider the
sets

(8)

K_{\sigma}=\bigl\{a+\rho\eta:(a,\eta)\in N^{\phi}(K),\,0<\sigma\rho\leq\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)\bigr\}\qquad\text{for $\sigma\geq 1$}\,.

Notice that
\mathbf{R}^{n}\without(\Cut^{\phi}(K)\cup K)=\bigcup_{\sigma>1}K_{\sigma}
but
K_{\sigma}
might have empty interior for every
\sigma>1
.

Our first result asserts that
\grad\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
is locally Lipschitz
continuous on the sets
K_{\sigma}
, which is a sharp generalisation of
Theorem
1.1
. More precisely we prove the following result.

1.4 Theorem

(Lipschitz estimates for the gradient)
.

Suppose
\phi:\mathbf{R}^{n}\to\mathbf{R}
is a uniformly convex norm of
class
\mathscr{C}^{2}
away from the origin,
K\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n}
is closed,

1<\sigma<\infty
,
0<s<t<\infty
, and

K_{\sigma,s,t}=\bigl\{a+\rho\eta:(a,\eta)\in N^{\phi}(K)\,,s\leq\rho\leq t\,,\sigma\rho\leq\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)\bigr\}\,.

Then
\grad\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}|K_{\sigma,s,t}
is Lipschitz continuous.

The restriction “
\sigma\rho\leq\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)
” cannot be
avoided since the Lipschitz constant of
\grad\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
may explode near
points of
\Cut^{\phi}(K)
; cf.
4.2
. Observe that if
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\without\bigl(\mathop{\mathrm{Clos}}\Sigma^{\phi}(K)\cup K\bigr)
, then
x

has positive distance from
\Cut^{\phi}(K)
; hence,
Theorem
1.4
includes Theorem
1.1
as a special case;
moreover, it is sharp in terms of specifying the set of points where a Lipschitz
condition for
\grad\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
holds. There are two main difficulties in
proving
1.4
. The first one arises from the fact that
\Cut^{\phi}(K)
might be dense in
\mathbf{R}^{n}\without K
and consequently
it does not seem to be possible to rely on general results for Hamilton-Jacobi
equations as for Theorem
1.1
. The second difficulty comes from working
with a possibly non-Euclidean norm. In fact, if
\phi
is the Euclidean norm
then the proof of Theorem
1.4
follows rather directly from the
geometric argument originally found by Federer for sets of positive reach in

[
Fed59
]
, see
[
San20
, 3.10(1)]
. However, this argument is not
applicable if
\phi
is not the Euclidean norm, in which case one needs a
considerably more sophisticated approach, based on a careful analysis of the
geometric properties of the
\phi
-balls (which occupies the entire section

3
). In fact this analysis allows to show that the
\phi
-nearest point projection
\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}
onto
K
satisfies the asserted
Lipschitz property. Recalling the well known relation between
\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}

and
\grad\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
(see
Lemma
2.41
(c)
)

\grad\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(x)=\grad\phi(x-\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}(x))\quad\textrm{for every $x\in\dmn\grad\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}$,}

we get the conclusion of
1.4
. Notice that
uniform
convexity and regularity
of the norm for
n\geq 3
are crucial to
obtain the Lipschitz property in Theorem
1.4
; see the last
section in
[
BHS16
]
.

The second goal of this paper is to extend Theorem
1.3
to arbitrary closed sets. In case of convex sets the
reach function satisfies
\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)=+\infty
for every
(a,\eta)\in N^{\phi}(K)
and consequently
\Cut^{\phi}(K)=\varnothing
. This is a
very special situation given by the assumption of convexity; indeed, even if we
consider
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
convex hypersurfaces the reach function might be
discontinuous on a dense set and the cut-locus might not be nowhere dense;
see
2.37
. This suggests that a generalization of Theorem

1.3
to non-convex sets requires a careful analysis of the behaviour of
\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}
and the connection with the points of differentiability of
\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}
. This can be done considering the new reach-type function (recall (
8
))

(9)

\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}(a,\eta)=\sup\bigl\{\sigma r:0<r<\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta),\,\sigma>1,\,\bm{\Theta}^{n}(\mathscr{L}^{n}\mathop{\rule[1.0pt]{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\rule[1.0pt]{4.0pt}{0.5pt}}\nolimits K_{\sigma},a+r\eta)=1\bigr\}\cup\{0\}

for
(a,\eta)\in N^{\phi}(K)
. It holds that
0\leq\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}(a,\eta)\leq\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)
for every
(a,\eta)\in N^{\phi}(K)
; see Remark
4.10
. However simple examples
show that there exist closed sets
K
for which
\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}(a,\eta)<\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)
for some
(a,\eta)\in N^{\phi}(K)
;
cf.
4.17
.
If
K
is a closed set such that the
\phi
-tubular neighbourhood
\{x:\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(x)<\rho\}
of radius
\rho>0
does not intersect
\Sigma^{\phi}(K)
, then
\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)\geq\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}(a,\eta)\geq\rho
for every
(a,\eta)\in N^{\phi}(K)
;
cf. Lemma
4.16
.

Employing the reach function
\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}
we obtain the following result
on the structure of the
\Sigma^{\phi}_{2}(K)
for an arbitrary closed set
K
.

1.5 Theorem
.

Suppose
K\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n}
is closed. Then

(a)

\Cut^{\phi}(K)\subseteq\Sigma^{\phi}_{2}(K)
.

(b)

If
(a,\eta)\in N^{\phi}(K)
and there exists
0<r<\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}(a,\eta)
such that
a+r\eta\notin\Sigma^{\phi}_{2}(K)
, then
a+s\eta\notin\Sigma^{\phi}_{2}(K)
for every
0<s<\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}(a,\eta)
.

(c)

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\bigl(\{(a,\eta):\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}(a,\eta)<\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)\}\bigr)=0
.

(d)

there exist
Z\subseteq N^{\phi}(K)
with
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(Z)=0
and a residual set

R\subseteq\bigl\{a+r\eta:\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}(a,\eta)\leq r<\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)\bigr\}

such that

\Sigma^{\phi}_{2}(K)\setminus\Cut^{\phi}(K)=\bigl\{a+r\eta:0<r<\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}(a,\eta),\;(a,\eta)\in Z\bigr\}\cup R.

In particular, for
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}
almost all
(a,\eta)\in N^{\phi}(K)
the
distance function
\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
is pointwise twice differentiable at all
points of the line segment
\{a+r\eta:0<r<\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)\}
.

We do not know whether
R=\{a+r\eta:\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}(a,\eta)\leq r<\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)\}
; this is left as an open problem. In
Remark
4.18
we show, however, that

\mathscr{L}^{n}(\{a+r\eta:\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}(a,\eta)\leq r<\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)\})=0\,.

The proof of Theorem
1.5
is based on the Lipschitz
property proved in Theorem
1.4
and on some general estimates
for the pointwise principal curvatures of level sets of
\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
(these
level sets might not even be topological manifolds but they admit a natural
notion of pointwise curvature; see
2.44
). Moreover, results on the
preservation of the density points under bilipschitz transformations
(see
[
Buc92
]
) and on the approximate differentiability of multivalued
functions (see
2.38
) are used in a crucial way.

1.2
Applications

Here we briefly mention a couple of different applications of the results of the present paper.

Pointwise regularity and gradient Lipschitz estimates for solutions
of the Eikonal equation.

Suppose
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n}
is an
arbitrary open set,
\phi
is a uniformly convex
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-norm and

\phi^{\ast}(u)=\sup\{u\bullet v:\phi(v)=1\}
. It is well known that

\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
is the unique viscosity solution of the following Eikonal
equation on
\Omega

\begin{cases}\phi^{\ast}(\grad u)=1&\textrm{on $\Omega$}\\
u=0&\textrm{on $\partial\Omega$}\end{cases}

If
\partial\Omega
is hypersurface of class at least
\mathcal{C}^{2}
, then
the local structure of this solution has been extensively studied and it is by
now very well understood (see the references cited at the beginning of this
introduction). On the other hand, as already explained, if
\partial\Omega
is
not
\mathcal{C}^{2}
then such a solution can have a very complicated (in
particular dense!) singular set (see
[
San21
]
) and many classical
results in the theory do not give an insight about its local structure. In this
direction our results in Theorems
1.4

and
1.5
provide a new and rather sharp description of
the structure of the solutions of the Eikonal equation for
arbitrary

domains.

Steiner formula and curvature measures in uniformly convex finite
dimensional Banach spaces

One of the original motivation of the second author
for the present work is to provide results that can be used to advance
the theory of convex and integral geometry in Minkowski spaces; see
[
Hug99
]
.
In
[
HS22
]
the second author in collaboration with Daniel Hug
employs Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
to
prove the Steiner formula for arbitrary closed sets in a uniformly convex Banach
space (Minkowski space); thus, extending the same formula previously obtained in

[
HLW04
]
in the Euclidean space. The Steiner formula is then used as a
starting point to develop the theory of curvature measures for sets of positive
reach in a Minkowski space.

Preliminaries

2.1
Notation

We follow traditional well established and widely accepted conventions and
notations typical for geometric measure theory. For convenience of the reader
we briefly describe them here. We use the following symbols

\mathbf{R}

set of real numbers;

\overline{\mathbf{R}}=\mathbf{R}\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}

extended reals;

\mathbf{Z}_{+}

set of positive integers;

\varnothing

the empty set;

\mathscr{H}^{d}

the
d
-dimensional Hausdorff measure;

\mathscr{L}^{n}

the Lebesgue measure over
\mathbf{R}^{n}
;

\bm{\alpha}(k)

Lebesgue measure of the unit ball in
\mathbf{R}^{k}
;

\mathbf{S}^{n-1}

the unit Euclidean sphere in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
;

x\bullet y

the inner product of two vectors
x
and
y
in a Euclidean space;

|x|

the norm of a vector
x
in a normed vectorspace;

A\without B

set-theoretic difference of two sets
A
and
B
;

\mathop{\mathrm{Clos}}A

closure of a subset
A
of a topological space;

\Int{A}

interior of a subset

2.33 Lemma

For every closed set

the function

\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x)=t\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(a+t(x-a))\qquad\textrm{for $x\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}$, $a\in\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}(x)$ and $0<t\leq\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x)$}\,.

Moreover,
\Cut^{\phi}(K)=\mathbf{R}^{n}\cap\{x:\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x)=1\}
.

Proof.

Let
x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\ldots\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
and
\beta\in\mathbf{R}
be such that

\lim_{i\to\infty}x_{i}=x_{0}
,
\phi(x_{i}-x_{0})<1
for

, and

\lim_{i\to\infty}\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x_{i})>\beta
. Since
\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
is
continuous we have

\lim_{i\to\infty}\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(x_{i})=\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(x_{0})
and we may
assume
\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(x_{i})<\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(x_{0})+1
for
i\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}
.
Choose
a_{i}\in\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}(x_{i})
for
i\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}
. Since

\{a_{i}:i\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}\}\subseteq\mathbf{B}^{\phi}(x_{0},\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(x_{0})+2)

we may, possibly choosing a subsequence, assume that

\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}=a_{0}
and then
a_{0}\in\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}(x_{0})
by
continuity of both
\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
and
\phi
. Assume further that

\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x_{i})\geq\beta
for
i\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}
. Recalling the definition
of
\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}
we obtain

\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(a_{0}+\beta(x_{0}-a_{0}))=\lim_{i\to\infty}\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(a_{i}+\beta(x_{i}-a_{i}))=\lim_{i\to\infty}\beta\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(x_{i})=\beta\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(x_{0})\,;

hence,
\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x_{0})\geq\beta
. Since this holds for any
\beta\in\mathbf{R}
satisfying
\lim_{i\to\infty}\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x_{i})>\beta

that
\lim_{i\to\infty}\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x_{i})\leq\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x_{0})
and
we conclude that
\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}
is upper semicontinuous.

Suppose
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n+1}
,
a\in\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}(x)
and

and we prove that
\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x)=t\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(a+t(x-a))
. Evidently, if
\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x)=\infty
,
then
\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(a+t(x-a))=\infty
for all

and the
assertion is true. Therefore, we assume

and define

. Notice that
\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(y)=t\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(x)
and
a\in\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}(y)
. Since

(12)

\displaystyle a+\frac{\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x)}{t}(y-a)=a+\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x)(x-a)

(13)

\displaystyle\text{we have}\quad\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}\big(a+\tfrac{\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x)}{t}(y-a)\big)=\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x)\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(x)=\tfrac{\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x)}{t}\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(y)\,;

(14)

\displaystyle\text{hence,}\quad t\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(y)\geq\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x)\,.

Noting that
x=a+\frac{1}{t}(y-a)
,
\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(y)\geq\frac{1}{t}
, and

, we can apply the inequality in
(

and

with

respectively, to obtain the reverse inequality; hence, equality.

Finally the assertion about the cut locus follows directly from the

\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}

3.2 Lemma
.

Assume

(29)

\displaystyle\text{$\phi$ is strictly convex}\,,\quad K\subseteq X\text{ is
closed}\,,\quad 1<\lambda<\infty\,,

(30)

\displaystyle K_{\lambda}=(X\without K)\cap\{x:\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}(x)\geq\lambda\}\,.

Then there exists

such that

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\omega_{\lambda}(t)=0

\phi(a-b)\leq\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(x)\,\omega_{\lambda}\bigl(\phi(x-y)/\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(x)\bigr)\quad\text{for $x\in K_{\lambda}$, $y\in X$, $a\in\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}(x)$, $b\in\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}(y)$}\,.

Proof.

For
0\leq t<\infty
define

(31)

\displaystyle K_{\lambda}(t)=X\times X\cap\bigl\{(a,b):\phi(a)=\lambda\,,\phi(b)\geq\lambda\,,\phi\bigl((1-1/\lambda)a-b\bigr)\leq 1+2t\bigr\}\,,

(32)

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda}(t)=\sup\bigl\{\phi(a-b):(a,b)\in K_{\lambda}(t))\bigr\}\,.

Observe that strict convexity of
\phi
yields

{\textstyle\bigcap}\bigl\{K_{\lambda}(t):0<t<\infty\bigr\}=X\times X\cap\bigl\{(a,a):\phi(a)=\lambda\bigr\}\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\omega_{\lambda}(t)=0\,.

Indeed, assume
\limsup_{t\downarrow 0}\omega_{\lambda}(t)=\delta
. Find sequences
X\cap\{a_{j}:j\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}\}
and
X\cap\{b_{j}:j\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}\}
such that
(a_{j},b_{j})\in K_{\lambda}(1/j)
,
\phi(a_{j}-b_{j})\geq\delta-1/j
,
\lim_{j\to\infty}a_{j}=a_{0}
and
\lim_{j\to\infty}b_{j}=b_{0}
with
\phi(a_{0})=\lambda
,
\phi(b_{0})\geq\lambda
,

\phi(b_{0}-a_{a})\geq\delta
,
\phi(z_{0}-(1-1/\lambda)a_{0})\leq 1
. Then

\lambda\leq\phi(b_{0})\leq\phi(b_{0}-(1-1/\lambda)a_{0})+\phi((1-1/\lambda)a_{0})\leq 1+\lambda-1=\lambda

which implies that
a_{0}=b_{0}
and
\delta=0
by
2.5
.

Let
x\in K_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathrm{Unp}^{\phi}(K)
,
y\in X
. Choose

(33)

\displaystyle\bar{a}\in\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}(x)\,,\quad\bar{b}\in\bm{\xi}_{K}^{\phi}(y)\,,\quad c=\bar{a}+\lambda(x-\bar{a})\,,

(34)

\displaystyle r=\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}(x)\,,\quad a=(\bar{a}-c)/r\,,\quad b=(\bar{b}-c)/r\,,\quad t=\phi(x-y)/r\,.

Clearly we have

(35)

\displaystyle\phi(\bar{b}-x)

\displaystyle\leq\phi(\bar{b}-y)+\phi(y-x)

(36)

\displaystyle\leq\phi(\bar{a}-y)+\phi(y-x)\leq\phi(\bar{a}-x)+2\phi(x-y)=r(1+2t)\,.

Since

we obtain

r\phi((1-1/\lambda)a-b)=\phi((x-c)-(\bar{b}-c))=\phi(x-\bar{b})\leq r(1+2t)\,.

Because

we know also that
\phi(\bar{a}-c)<\phi(\bar{b}-c)
; hence,

r\phi(b)=\phi(\bar{b}-c)>\phi(\bar{a}-c)=r\phi(a)=\lambda\phi(x-\bar{a})=\lambda r\,.

This shows that
(a,b)\in K_{\lambda}(t)
so

and
\phi(\bar{a}-\bar{b})\leq r\omega_{\lambda}(t)

∎

0<s<\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}(a,\eta)
such that
\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
is pointwise twice
differentiable at
a+s\eta
. Since
\underline{\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}}(a+s\eta)>1
and

\underline{\bm{\rho}^{\phi}_{K}}(a+s\eta)=s^{-1}\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}(a,\eta)>1
by Lemma

4.14
, we infer
from
4.12
(f)

that
\bm{\delta}_{K}^{\phi}
is pointwise twice differentiable at
a+t\eta

for all
0<t<\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}(a,\eta)
.

Consequently, it remains to show that
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(Z)=0
. This can be
done with an argument similar as in the proof
of
(c)
. Since
\mathscr{L}^{n}(\Sigma^{\phi}_{2}(K))=0
it follows from coarea formula
[
Fed69
, 3.2.11]
that there is

I\subseteq\mathbf{R}_{+}
such that
\mathscr{L}^{1}(\mathbf{R}_{+}\without I)=0
and

(102)

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(\Sigma^{\phi}_{2}(K)\cap S^{\phi}(K,t))=0\qquad\textrm{for $t\in I$}\,.

We define
Z_{t}=Z\cap\{(a,\eta):t<\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}(a,\eta)\}
for

t\in\mathbf{R}_{+}
. Noting that

f_{t}[Z_{t}]\subseteq\Sigma^{\phi}_{2}(K)\cap S^{\phi}(K,t)
for
t>0
,
we infer that

(103)

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(f_{t}[Z_{t}])=0\qquad\textrm{for $t\in I$}\,.

Since

g[K_{\lambda}\cap f_{t}[Z_{t}]]=g[K_{\lambda}\cap S^{\phi}(K,t)]\cap Z_{t}
for
\lambda>1
and
t>0
, we conclude that

\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(g[K_{\lambda}\cap S^{\phi}(K,t)]\cap Z_{t})=0\qquad\textrm{for $\lambda>1$ and $t\in I$}\,.

It follows that
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(Z_{t})=0
for
\mathcal{L}^{1}
almost all

t>0
. Noting that
Z=\bigcup_{t>0}Z_{t}
and
Z_{t}\subseteq Z_{s}

for
s\leq t
, we see that
\mathscr{H}^{n-1}(Z)=0
.
∎

4.18 Remark
.

Recalling the set
B
from the proof above and noting that

\{a+r\eta:(a,\eta)\in N^{\phi}(K),\;\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}(a,\eta)\leq r<\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)\}\subseteq B,

we conclude that
\mathscr{L}^{n}(\{a+r\eta:\underline{\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}}(a,\eta)\leq r<\bm{r}_{K}^{\phi}(a,\eta)\})=0
.

The following theorem shows that even in the case of convex bodies with
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
-boundaries, the dimension of the set
Z
in the statement
(d)
can be precisely
n-1
. See also
[
Zaj83a
, Theorem 1]
for a similar construction.

4.19 Theorem
.

There exists a convex set
K\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{2}
with

\mathscr{C}^{1,1}
boundary and a dense set
S\subseteq\partial{K}
such that

(a)

\mathscr{H}^{1}(S)=0
and
\mathscr{H}^{s}(S)=+\infty
for all
s<1
,

(b)

\mathscr{H}^{1}(N(K)|S)=0
and
\mathscr{H}^{s}(N(K)|S)=+\infty
for all

s<1
,

(c)

\bm{\xi}_{K}|S^{\phi}(K,r)
is not differentiable at
a+ru
for all

r>0
and
(a,u)\in N(K)|S
.

In particular, both
\Sigma_{2}(K)
and the set of points where
\bm{\delta}_{K}
is
not directionally differentiable are dense in
\mathbf{R}^{2}\without K
with
Hausdorff dimension
2
.

Proof.

We choose a dense
G_{\delta}
-set
G\subseteq\mathbf{R}
with

\mathscr{L}^{1}(G)=0
and
\mathscr{H}^{s}(G)=+\infty
for all
s<1
. Then
Zahorski theorem (see
[
Zah46
]
or
[
FP09
]
) ensures the
existence of non-decreasing Lipschitz function
f:\mathbf{R}\to\mathbf{R}

such that
f
is not differentiable at each point of
G
and it is
differentiable at each point of
\mathbf{R}\without G
. Let
g
be a primitive
of
f
and notice that
g
is a
\mathscr{C}^{1,1}
convex function. Let

K=(\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R})\cap\{(x,y):g(x)\leq y\}\,,\qquad S=(G\times\mathbf{R})\cap\{(x,y):g(x)=y\}\,.

Evidently
K
is a convex set with
\mathscr{C}^{1,1}
boundary,

\mathscr{H}^{1}(S)=0
and
\mathscr{H}^{s}(S)=+\infty
for every
s<1
.
If
\eta:\partial{K}\to\mathbf{S}^{1}
is the exterior unit normal
of
K
then

\eta(x,f(x))=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+f(x)^{2}}}\bigl(f(x),-1\bigr)\qquad\textrm{for $x\in\mathbf{R}$}\,,

whence we infer that
\eta
is not differentiable at each point of
S

and it is differentiable at all points of
\partial{K}\without S
. Moreover,
we notice that the map
\phi:\partial{K}\to N(K)
, defined by

\phi(z)=(z,\eta(z))
for
x\in\partial{K}
, is a bilipschitz
homeomorphism and
N(K)|S=\phi(S)
. Therefore, (
(b)
)
holds.

To check
(c)
we assume that
\bm{\xi}_{K}|S^{\phi}(K,r)
is
differentiable at
a+ru
for some
r>0
and
(a,u)\in N(K)|S
.
We notice that
\bm{\xi}_{K}|S^{\phi}(K,r)
is a bilipschitz homeomorphism onto

\partial{K}
with

(\bm{\xi}_{K}|S^{\phi}(K,r))^{-1}(b)=b+r\eta(b)\quad\text{for $b\in\partial{K}$.}

Therefore,

\mathrm{D}(\bm{\xi}_{K}|S^{\phi}(K,r))(a+ru):\Tan(S^{\phi}(K,r),a+ru)\to\Tan(\partial{K},a)
is a linear homeomorphism and
(\bm{\xi}_{K}|S^{\phi}(K,r))^{-1}
is differentiable
at
a
. This contradicts the fact that
\eta
is not differentiable
at
a
.
∎

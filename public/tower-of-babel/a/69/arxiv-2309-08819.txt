The Cohen-Macaulay Type of Edge-Weighted r r -path Ideals
Shuai Wei

Abstract
. We describe combinatorially the Cohen-Macaulay type of edge-weighted r r -path suspensions of edge-weighted graphs for an arbitrary positive integer r r . The computation of the Cohen-Macaulay type of edge-weighted suspensions of edge-weighted graphs becomes a special case of r = 1 r=1 .



Introduction

Assumption
.

Throughout, let
G
be a (finite simple) graph with vertex set
V=V(G)=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\}
of cardinality
d\geq 1
and edge set
E=E(G)
. An edge between vertices
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
is denoted
v_{i}v_{j}
. Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field and set
R=\mathbb{K}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}]
. Set
\mathfrak{m}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{d})R
. Fix an integer
r\in\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\dots\}
and set
R^{\prime}=\mathbb{K}[\{X_{i,j}\mid i=1,\ldots,d,j=0,\ldots,r\}]
. An
edge-weighting
on
G
is a function
\omega:E\to\mathbb{N}
, and
G_{\omega}
denotes a graph
G
equipped with an edge-weighting
\omega
.

Combinatorial commutative algebra uses combinatorics and graph theory to understand certain algebraic constructions; it also uses algebra to understand certain objects in combinatorics and graph theory. In this paper, we explore aspects of this area via edge ideals and path ideals of edge-weighted graphs.

The

of

of

I(G)=(X_{i}X_{j}\mid v_{i}v_{j}\in E)R.

[

]
characterizes the trees

for which

U
by adding an edge

to each vertex

of

It is straightforward to show that the elements
v_{i}-v_{i1}
form a maximal regular sequence on
R^{\prime}/I(T)
such that the ensuing quotient is
R/(I(U)+\langle x_{1}^{2},\dots,x_{d}^{2}\rangle)
. From this, one readily computes the Cohen-Macaulay type of
R^{\prime}/I(T)
as the number of ideals in an irredundant irreducible decomposition of
I(U)
, in other words, the number of minimal vertex covers of
U
. For instance, in the displayed example, the type of
R^{\prime}/I(T)
is 2, either by the decomposition
I(U)=\langle v_{1}v_{2},v_{2}v_{3}\rangle=\langle v_{1},v_{3}\rangle\cap\langle v_{2}\rangle
or by the minimal vertex covers
\{v_{1},v_{3}\}
and
\{v_{2}\}
. The goal of this paper is to extend this computation to the following more general constructions.

Paulsen and Sather-Wagstaff
[
6
]
generalized Villarreal’s construction in on direction with the edge ideal of an edge-weighted graph
G_{\omega}
: the ideal
I(G_{\omega})
of
R
which is “generated by all weighted-edges of
G_{\omega}
”:

I(G_{\omega})=\left(X_{i}^{\omega(v_{i}v_{j})}X_{j}^{\omega(v_{i}v_{j})}\mathrel{\big|}v_{i}v_{j}\in E\right)R.

In particular, if
\omega
is the constant function defined by
\omega(v_{i}v_{j})=1
for
v_{i}v_{j}\in E
, then
I(G_{\omega})=I(G)
.

Building from Villarreal’s work in another direction, Conca and De Negri
[
2
]
defined the
r
-path ideal
associated to
G
to be the ideal
I_{r}(G)
of
R
that is “generated by the paths in
G
of length
r
”:

I_{r}(G)=(X_{i_{1}}\cdots X_{i_{r+1}}\mid v_{i_{1}}\cdots v_{i_{r+1}}\text{ is a path of length $r$ in }G)R.

In particular, if
r
= 1, then
I_{1}(G)=I(G)
.

Kubik and Sather-Wagstaff
[
4
]
gave a construction that subsumes each of these: the
edge-weighted
r
-path ideal

I_{r}(G_{\omega})
of an edge-weighted graph

; see Definition

(a)
. When
G
is a tree, a theorem of Kubik and Sather-Wagstaff
[
4
]
gives a graph-theoretic characterization when

is Cohen-Macaulay. The main result of this paper computes the type for these ideals when they are Cohen-Macaulay. As with the above computation our result is purely graph-theoretical. These results are in Theorem
2.26
and Corollary
2.28
. They form the bulk of Section
2
. Necessary background information is collected in Section

Definition 1.1
.

Let

r_{R}(R/I)=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{d}(\mathbb{K},R/I).

An

-path vertex cover

.

(a)

The

I_{r}(G_{\omega})=\mathrel{\bBigg@}{4.8}(X_{i_{1}}^{e_{i_{1}}}\cdots X_{i_{r+1}}^{e_{i_{r+1}}}\mathrel{\bBigg@{4.8}|}\begin{array}[]{l}v_{i_{1}}\cdots v_{i_{r+1}}\text{ is a path in }G\text{ with }e_{i_{1}}=\omega(v_{i_{1}}v_{i_{2}}),\\

\text{and }e_{i_{r+1}}=\omega(v_{i_{r}}v_{i_{r+1}})\end{array}\mathrel{\bBigg@}{4.8})R.

(b)

Let
(V^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})
be a pair such that
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
and
\delta^{\prime}:V^{\prime}\to\mathbb{N}
. We define the cardinality of
(V^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})
, denoted

. Set

to be the ideal “generated by the elements of

P(V^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})=\Bigl(X_{i}^{\delta^{\prime}(v_{i})}\mathrel{\big|}v_{i}\in V^{\prime}\Bigr)R.

Since
\mathbb{K}
is a field, the ideals

are irreducible.

.

Let
(\Sigma_{r}G)_{\lambda}
be an edge-weighted

-path suspension of

-path ideal

Convention 1.10
.

Let
(\Sigma_{r}G)_{\lambda}
be an edge-weighted
r
-path suspension of

\Sigma_{r}G
and variables of

for

for

Definition 1.12

to

, where

in

. Let

This section is devoted to proving the main result of this paper, namely Theorem

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(\Sigma_{r-1}G)_{\lambda}
be an edge-weighted
(r-1)
-path suspension of
G_{\omega}
. Let
\mathfrak{P}:=(V^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime})
be such that
V^{\prime\prime}\subseteq V((\Sigma_{r-1}G)_{\lambda})
and
\delta^{\prime\prime}:V^{\prime\prime}\to\mathbb{N}
. If
W_{i}(\mathfrak{P})\neq\emptyset
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, then for any
v_{i}\in q(V^{\prime\prime})
,

\gamma_{(V^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime})}(v_{i})=\delta^{\prime\prime}(v_{i,j_{0}})+\sum_{k=0}^{j_{0}-1}h_{i,k},\text{ where }j_{0}:=\min\{j\mid v_{i,j}\in W_{i}(\mathfrak{P})\}.

Proof.

Suppose that
\delta^{\prime\prime}(v_{i,j})+\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}h_{i,k}<\delta^{\prime\prime}(v_{i,j_{0}})+\sum_{k=0}^{j_{0}-1}h_{i,k}
for some
v_{i,j}\in W_{i}(\mathfrak{P})
. Then by the definition of
j_{0}
, we have that
j>j_{0}\geq 0
and so
\delta^{\prime\prime}(v_{i,j})+\sum_{k=j_{0}}^{j-1}h_{i,k}<\delta^{\prime\prime}(v_{i,j_{0}})
. Thus,

h_{i,j_{0}}<\delta^{\prime\prime}(v_{i,j})+h_{i,j_{0}}\leq\delta^{\prime\prime}(v_{i,j})+\sum_{k=j_{0}}^{j-1}h_{i,k}<\delta^{\prime\prime}(v_{i,j_{0}}),\text{ i.e., }h_{i,j_{0}}<\delta^{\prime\prime}(v_{i,j_{0}}),

contradicting
v_{i,j_{0}}\in W_{i}(\mathfrak{P})
.
∎

The following theorem is a key for decomposing
I_{r}((\Sigma_{r-1}G)_{\lambda^{\prime}})R
with
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda|_{\Sigma_{r-1}G}
and hence
I_{r}((\Sigma_{r}G)_{\lambda})R
. The reader may wish to follow the argument with Example
1.18
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(\Sigma_{r-1}G)_{\lambda}
be an edge-weighted
(r-1)
-path suspension of
G_{\omega}
such that
\lambda(v_{i}v_{j})\leq\lambda(v_{i},v_{i,1})
and
\lambda(v_{i}v_{j})\leq\lambda(v_{j}v_{j,1})
for all edges
v_{i}v_{j}\in E
. Let
\mathfrak{P}:=(V^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime})
be such that
V^{\prime\prime}\subseteq V((\Sigma_{r-1}G)_{\lambda})
and
\delta^{\prime\prime}:V^{\prime\prime}\to\mathbb{N}

I_{r}((\Sigma_{r-1}G)_{\lambda})R\subseteq P(q(V^{\prime\prime}),\gamma_{(V^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime})})
if and only if
(V^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime})
is an edge-weighted
r
-path vertex cover of

.

Proof.

\Longrightarrow
Assume that

. Let

be an

-path in

. Set

\max(\lambda(v_{p_{k-1},q_{k-1}}v_{p_{k},q_{k}}),\lambda(v_{p_{k},q_{k}}v_{p_{k+1},q_{k+1}}))&\text{if }k=2,\dots,r,\\
\lambda(v_{p_{r},q_{r}}v_{p_{r+1},q_{r+1}})&\text{if }k=r+1.\end{array}\right.

Then
X_{p_{1}}^{e_{p_{1},q_{1}}}\cdots X_{p_{r+1}}^{e_{p_{r+1},q_{r+1}}}\in\llbracket I_{r}((\Sigma_{r-1}G)_{\lambda})R\rrbracket\subseteq\llbracket P(q(V^{\prime\prime}),\gamma_{(V^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime})})\rrbracket
. Hence

X_{i_{0}}^{\gamma_{(V^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime})}(v_{i_{0}})}\mathrel{\big|}X_{p_{1}}^{e_{p_{1},q_{1}}}\cdots X_{p_{r+1}}^{e_{p_{r+1},q_{r+1}}}\text{ for some }v_{i_{0}}\in q(V^{\prime\prime}).

Then
v_{i_{0}}=v_{p_{l}}
for some

,

and so

where

Hence

. Since

P(q(V^{\prime\prime}),\gamma_{(V^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime})})
. Let

be a generator corresponding to the
r
-path

in

. We need to show that
\underline{X}^{\underline{b}}\in P(q(V^{\prime\prime}),\gamma_{(V^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime})})
. Note that
P_{r}
in
\Sigma_{r-1}G
is of the following form.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v_{i_{1},0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v_{i_{1},1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v_{i_{1},j_{1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\vdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v_{i_{r+1},0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v_{i_{r+1},1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v_{i_{r+1},j_{r+1}}}

where
j_{1}
or
j_{r+1}
may be 0. Since
P_{r}
is an
r
-path in
(\Sigma_{r-1}G)_{\lambda}
and
(V^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime})
is an edge-weighted
r
-path vertex cover of
(\Sigma_{r-1}G)_{\lambda}
, we have that there exists some
l\in\{1,\ldots,r+1\}
such that
v_{i_{l},j_{l}}\in V^{\prime\prime}
and
\delta^{\prime\prime}(v_{i_{l},j_{l}})\leq e_{i_{l},j_{l}}
. Hence
v_{i_{l},j_{l}}\in W_{i_{l}}(\mathfrak{P})
and then

\gamma_{(V^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime})}(v_{i_{l}})=\min\biggl\{\delta^{\prime\prime}(v_{i_{l},t})+\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}h_{i_{l},k}\mathrel{\Big|}v_{i_{l},t}\in W_{i_{l}}(\mathfrak{P})\biggr\}\leq\delta^{\prime\prime}(v_{i_{l},j_{l}})+\sum_{k=0}^{j_{l}-1}h_{i_{l},k}.

. Then

. Therefore,

\delta^{\prime\prime}(v_{i_{l},j_{l}})+\sum_{k=0}^{j_{l}-1}e_{i_{l},k}\leq e_{i_{l},j_{l}}+\sum_{k=0}^{j_{l}-1}e_{i_{l},k}=\sum_{k=0}^{j_{l}}e_{i_{l},k}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{M_{0}}e_{i_{l}.k},\text{ i.e., }\delta^{\prime\prime}(v_{i_{l},j_{l}})+\sum_{k=0}^{j_{l}-1}e_{i_{l},k}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{M_{0}}e_{i_{l},k}.

(a)

Assume that
j_{l}=0
. Then

\gamma_{(V^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime})}(v_{i_{l}})\leq\delta^{\prime\prime}(v_{i_{l},j_{l}})+\sum_{k=0}^{j_{l}-1}h_{i_{l},k}=\delta^{\prime\prime}(v_{i_{l},j_{l}})+\sum_{k=0}^{j_{l}-1}e_{i_{l},k}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{M_{0}}e_{i_{l},k}=\sum_{k=0}^{r+1}\mathbbm{1}_{l,k}\cdot e_{i_{k},j_{k}}=b_{i_{l}},

\mathbbm{1}_{l,k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{if }j_{k}=j_{l},\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.\forall\mkern 2.0muk=1,\ldots,r+1
.

. Then

. Since

Proposition 2.3
.

Let

be an edge-weighted
(r-1)
-path suspension of

and

.

Proof.

[
5
, Theorem 7.5.1]
gives a decomposition of

. Let

\beta_{b_{k}}
we can find at least one
r
-path
P_{r}
in

such that the weights on

are related to it. Let
k\in\{1,\dots,s\}
. Then by

5
, Theorem 7.5.1]

p(X_{i_{1},j_{1}}^{e_{i_{1},j_{1}}}\cdots X_{i_{r+1},j_{r+1}}^{e_{i_{r+1},j_{r+1}}})
of

an

\beta_{b_{k}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}e_{b_{k},0}&\text{if }M_{k}=0,\\

Repeat the process for each

and set

. Then

. Define

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime\prime}:V^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle v_{b_{k},M_{k}}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda(v_{b_{k},M_{k}}v_{b_{k},M_{k}-1})&\text{if }M_{k}\geq 1,\\
\beta_{b_{k}}(=e_{b_{k},0})&\text{if }M_{k}=0,\end{array}\right.\forall\mkern 2.0muk=1,\dots,s.

(X_{b_{1}}^{\beta_{b_{1}}},\dots,X_{b_{s}}^{\beta_{b_{s}}})R=P(q(V^{\prime\prime}),\gamma_{(V^{\prime\prime},\delta^{\prime\prime})})
. It suffices to show that

for

. Let

. By definition, we have that

for

.

(a)

. Then

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}:=(V_{1}^{\prime\prime},\delta_{1}^{\prime\prime}),\mathfrak{P}:=(V_{2}^{\prime\prime},\delta_{2}^{\prime\prime})
be such that

, then

Proof.

Let
X_{i}^{\gamma_{(V_{1}^{\prime\prime},\delta_{1}^{\prime\prime})}(v_{i})}
be a nonzero generator of

, we have that

Theorem 2.26
.

be an edge-weighted
r
-path suspension of

\displaystyle r_{R^{\prime}}\biggl(\frac{R^{\prime}}{I_{r}((\Sigma_{r}G)_{\lambda})}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\sharp\mkern 2.0mu\{\text{ideals in an irredundant irreducible decomposition of }I_{r}((\Sigma_{r}G)_{\lambda})R\}

Abstract

We describe and analyse a quasi-Trefftz discontinuous Galerkin method for solving boundary value problems for the homogeneous diffusion-advection-reaction equation with piecewise-smooth coefficients.

The discontinuous Galerkin (DG) weak formulation is derived including an interior penalty parameter and using the classical upwind numerical fluxes. We compare three different formulations which arise from different choices of the symmetrization parameter, including the Symmetric Interior Penalty Galerkin (SIPG) one. DG methods may require a higher number of degrees of freedom compared to continuous ones, especially in the case of high-order schemes; combining them with quasi-Trefftz functions allows for a reduction in the number of degrees of freedom.

Trefftz schemes are high-order Galerkin methods whose discrete functions satisfy exactly the underlying partial differential equation (PDE) in each mesh element (for example, harmonic polynomials for the Laplace equation).
Since a family of local exact solutions is needed,
Trefftz basis functions can be easily computed for many PDEs that are linear, homogeneous and with piecewise-constant coefficients. However, if the equation has varying coefficients, in general, exact solutions are unavailable, hence the construction of discrete Trefftz spaces is not possible.

The quasi-Trefftz methods have been introduced to overcome this limitation. They rely on discrete spaces of functions that are not exact solutions but elementwise “approximate solutions” of the PDE in the sense of Taylor polynomials: the Taylor polynomial of a given order of their image under the partial differential operator vanishes at the barycentre of a mesh element.

A space-time quasi-Trefftz DG method has recently been studied in
[
23
]
, extending
to the case of smoothly varying coefficients the space-time Trefftz DG scheme for the acoustic wave equation of
[
28
]
.
Since it has shown excellent results, we propose a related method that can be applied to the second-order diffusion-advection-reaction elliptic equation.

We choose polynomial quasi-Trefftz basis functions, whose coefficients can be computed with a simple algorithm, which is initialized assigning a sort of Cauchy conditions and is based on the Taylor expansion of the coefficients of the PDE.
The main advantage of Trefftz and quasi-Trefftz schemes over more classical ones is the higher accuracy for comparable numbers of degrees of freedom.
We prove that the dimension of the quasi-Trefftz space is smaller than the dimension of the polynomial space of the same degree and that yields the same optimal convergence rates as the full polynomial space.
The quasi-Trefftz DG method is well-posed, consistent and stable and we prove its high-order convergence.
We present some numerical experiments in two dimensions that show excellent properties in terms of approximation and convergence rate.

Introduction

Trefftz methods are numerical schemes for approximating solutions of boundary value problems.
While Standard Finite Element methods seek to approximate a solution of a partial differential equation (PDE) by piecewise polynomials, Trefftz methods rely on problem-dependent bases, incorporating some properties of the PDE into the test and trial spaces, hence also into the discrete solution.
Trefftz schemes were proposed in 1926 by Erich Trefftz
[
32
]
and are high-order Galerkin methods whose trial and test functions are exact solutions of the governing PDE in each mesh element. For example, if we consider the Laplace equation, instead of piecewise polynomials, a natural choice of approximating functions is given by piecewise harmonic polynomials.

Since local exact solutions are required, in general the PDEs considered are linear, homogeneous, i.e., with no source term, and with piecewise-constant coefficients, for which Trefftz basis functions can be easily computed.
However, if the equation has varying coefficients, in general, exact solutions are unavailable, making the construction of discrete Trefftz spaces impossible.

Quasi-Trefftz methods have been introduced to solve this problem: they use discrete spaces of functions that are not exact solutions but elementwise “approximate solutions” of the PDE. More precisely, quasi-Trefftz functions satisfy the following property: the Taylor polynomial of a given order of their image under the partial differential operator vanishes at the barycentre of a mesh element.

Trefftz methods proved successful especially for time-harmonic wave propagation (Helmholtz equation) using plane waves as basis functions
[
19
]
and have been extended in
[
22
]
to the case of smoothly varying coefficients using Generalized Plane Waves (GPWs), which are approximate solutions of the Helmholtz equation in the sense of Taylor polynomials.

An extension of a space-time Trefftz DG scheme for the acoustic wave equation of
[
28
]
to the case of smoothly varying coefficients has recently been studied in
[
23
]
using the quasi-Trefftz method:
since it has shown excellent results, we propose a related method that can be applied to the second-order elliptic equation.
In this thesis we describe and analyse a quasi-Trefftz discontinuous Galerkin method for solving boundary value problems for the homogeneous diffusion-advection-reaction equation with piecewise-smooth coefficients.

We choose polynomial quasi-Trefftz basis functions, whose coefficients can be computed with a simple algorithm, which is initialized assigning a sort of Cauchy conditions and is based on the Taylor expansion of the coefficients of the PDE.

The main advantage of Trefftz and quasi-Trefftz schemes over more classical ones is the higher accuracy for comparable numbers of degrees of freedom.
The quasi-Trefftz space, compared to the full polynomial space of the same degree, provides the same convergence rates with respect to the mesh size and has a much smaller dimension, especially for large polynomial degrees.

Trefftz and quasi-Trefftz methods are often formulated in a discontinuous Galerkin (DG) framework.
DG methods are based on discontinuous piecewise polynomial functions and they can be easily combined with Trefftz and quasi-Trefftz functions due to the decoupling of the basis construction of elements.
DG schemes often require a higher number of degrees of freedom compared to continuous ones, especially in the case of high-order schemes and combining them with quasi-Trefftz functions allows for a reduction in the number of degrees of freedom.

For PDEs with diffusion, DG methods originated from the use of an interior penalty to weakly enforce continuity conditions as in
[
2
]
. See
[
3
]
for a unified analysis of DG methods for the Laplace equation.
For advection–reaction problems, it is common to use upwind numerical fluxes in the DG formulation
[
8
]
.
DG scheme for diffusion-advection-reaction problems that combine these two approaches have been studied in
[
20
,
4
,
11
]
.

1.1
Abstract setting

In this section we introduce the abstract framework for the analysis of the discontinuous Galerkin methods.
We start with the variational setting, then we consider the Galerkin method and non-conforming methods and then we recall some classical function spaces that we will use in the analysis.

1.1.1
Variational problems and Galerkin

Let
V
be a real Hilbert space equipped with the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{V}
, let
{\mathcal{A}}:V\times V\to\mathbb{R}
be a bilinear form on
V
and
{\mathcal{F}}:V\to\mathbb{R}
be a linear functional on
V
.
Consider the following abstract variational problem:

\text{Find }u\in V\text{ such that }{\mathcal{A}}(u,v)={\mathcal{F}}(v)\text{ for all }v\in V.

(1.1)

We recall the definitions of coercivity and continuity of a bilinear form
{\mathcal{A}}
and of continuity of a linear form
{\mathcal{F}}
.

Definition 1.1.1

(Coercivity)
.

A bilinear form
{\mathcal{A}}
defined on
\left(V,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{V}\right)
is
coercive
if there exists
\alpha>0
such that

{\mathcal{A}}(v,v)\geq\alpha\left\|v\right\|_{V}^{2}\quad\forall v\in V.

Definition 1.1.2

(Continuity)
.

A bilinear form
{\mathcal{A}}
defined on
\left(V,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{V}\right)
is
continuous
if there exists
M>0
such that

\left|{\mathcal{A}}(v,w)\right|\leq M\left\|v\right\|_{V}\left\|w\right\|_{V}\quad\forall v,w\in V.

Definition 1.1.3

(Continuity)
.

A linear form
{\mathcal{F}}
defined on
\left(V,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{V}\right)
is
continuous
if there exists
M>0
such that

\left|{\mathcal{F}}(v)\right|\leq M\left\|v\right\|_{V}\quad\forall v\in V.

Sufficient conditions for the well-posedness of the problem (
1.1
) are given by the Lax–Milgram Theorem:

Theorem 1.1.1

(Lax–Milgram)
.

Given the problem (
1.1
), assume that the bilinear form
{\mathcal{A}}
is continuous and coercive on
V
and that the linear functional
{\mathcal{F}}
is continuous on
V
.
Then, there exists a unique solution
u\in V
of the variational problem (
1.1
).

In order to approximate a variational problem one can use the Galerkin method, which consists in restricting the variational problem (
1.1
) to a finite-dimensional subspace of
V
. Let
V_{h}\subseteq V
be a finite-dimensional subspace of
V
, called
discrete space
, the Galerkin method has the following form:

\text{Find }u_{h}\in V_{h}\text{ such that }{\mathcal{A}}(u_{h},v_{h})={\mathcal{F}}(v_{h})\text{ for all }v_{h}\in V_{h}.

(1.2)

Note that the equation is the same, the only differences are that the discrete solution
u_{h}
is searched only in
V_{h}
and that the variational equation is satisfied only for test functions
v_{h}\in V_{h}
.
Observe that, if the bilinear form
{\mathcal{A}}
is coercive and continuous on
V
and the linear functional
{\mathcal{F}}
is continuous on
V
, then they satisfy these properties also on
V_{h}
, since
V_{h}\subseteq V
.
Hence, if the continuous problem (
1.1
) is well-posed due to the Lax–Milgram Theorem, then also the discrete one admits a unique solution.
We can now study the error between the exact solution and the approximate one.
An error estimate is provided by the Céa’s Lemma:

Lemma 1.1.2

(Céa)
.

Under the assumption of the Lax–Milgram Theorem, let
u
be the solution of (
1.1
) and
u_{h}
be the solution of (
1.2
). Then, the following error bound holds

\left\|u-u_{h}\right\|_{V}\leq\frac{M}{\alpha}\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\left\|u-v_{h}\right\|_{V}.

This inequality is known as the
quasi-optimality inequality
.

1.1.2
Non-conforming methods

Another way of approximating the problem (
1.1
) is with non-conforming methods. We will consider discontinuous Galerkin approximations that belong to this class of schemes.
For this part we rely on the abstract non-conforming error analysis presented in
[
11
, Section 1.3]
.
In the case of non-conforming methods the discrete space
V_{h}
is a finite-dimensional space that is not contained in
V
.
The discrete problem has this form:

\text{Find }u_{h}\in V_{h}\text{ such that }{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u_{h},v_{h})={\mathcal{F}}_{h}(v_{h})\text{ for all }v_{h}\in V_{h},

(1.3)

with
V_{h}\not\subseteq V
, a discrete bilinear form
{\mathcal{A}}_{h}:V_{h}\times V_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
and a discrete linear form
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}:V_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Observe that a sufficient condition for the well-posedness of the discrete problem (
1.3
) is the coercivity of the bilinear form
{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
on
V_{h}
.

Definition 1.1.4

(Discrete coercivity)
.

Let
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
be a norm defined on
V_{h}
.
The discrete bilinear form
{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
enjoys
discrete coercivity
if it is coercive on
V_{h}
, i.e., if there exists
\alpha>0
such that

{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(v_{h},v_{h})\geq\alpha{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\quad\forall v\in V_{h}.

In order to have an error estimate analogous to the Céa’s Lemma we will need three properties satisfied: consistency, discrete coercivity and boundedness.

Consistency means that the exact solution
u
of (
1.1
) satisfies the discrete variational equation (
1.3
).
To check this property we need to evaluate
{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
on the exact solution
u
but the discrete bilinear form is defined only on
V_{h}\times V_{h}
.
For this reason we assume that exists a subspace
V_{*}\subseteq V
such that
u\in V_{*}
and such that we can extend the bilinear form
{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
to
V_{*}\times V_{h}
.

Definition 1.1.5

(Consistency)
.

The discrete problem (
1.3
) is
consistent
if the exact solution
u\in V_{*}
satisfies

{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u,v_{h})={\mathcal{F}}_{h}(v_{h})\text{ for all }v_{h}\in V_{h}.

We note that consistency is equivalent to the
Galerkin orthogonality property
:

{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(u-u_{h},v_{h})=0\text{ for all }v_{h}\in V_{h}.

The error
u-u_{h}
belongs to the space
V_{*h}:=V_{*}+V_{h}
.
We assume that the norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
can be extended to
V_{*h}
, since we want to measure the error in the same norm in which the discrete coercivity holds.
We also need the boundedness in
V_{*h}\times V_{h}
, but, in general, is not achievable only with the norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
and a stronger norm
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}
on
V_{*h}
is needed.

Definition 1.1.6

(Boundedness)
.

Let
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}
be two norms defined on
V_{*h}
such that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}
for all
v\in V_{*h}
.
The discrete bilinear form
{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
is
bounded
in
V_{*h}\times V_{h}
if there exists
M>0
such that

\left|{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(v,w_{h})\right|\leq M{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\quad\forall(v,w_{h})\in V_{*h}\times V_{h}.

Theorem 1.1.3

(Abstract error estimate)
.

Let
u
solve (
1.1
) and let
u_{h}
solve (
1.3
).
Assume that
u\in V_{*}\subseteq V
and that the discrete bilinear form
{\mathcal{A}}_{h}
can be extended to
V_{*h}\times V_{h}
, where
V_{*h}=V_{*}+V_{h}
. Let
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}
and
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}
be two norms defined on
V_{*h}
such that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}
for all
v\in V_{*h}
.
Assume consistency, discrete coercivity and boundedness. Then, the following error estimate holds true:

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq\left(1+\frac{M}{\alpha}\right)\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}.

Proof.

Let
v_{h}\in V_{h}
. Owing to the triangle inequality, we get

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}.

For the first term we simply have

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}.

For the second term,

\alpha{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}\leq{\mathcal{A}}_{h}(v_{h}-u_{h},v_{h}-u_{h})={\mathcal{A}}_{h}(v_{h}-u,v_{h}-u_{h}),

and the boundedness yields

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq\frac{M}{\alpha}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}-u\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}.

Combining the previous bounds we arrive at

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}\leq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*}+\frac{M}{\alpha}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}-u\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{*},

and we conclude the proof by taking the infimum over

.

is a real Hilbert space equipped with the scalar product

(w,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}:=\int_{\Omega}wv.

We now recall two useful inequalities: the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and the Young’s inequality.

Proposition 1.1.4

(Cauchy–Schwarz inequality)
.

For all
w,v\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, then
wv\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and

\left|(w,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right|\leq\left\|w\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\forall w,v\in L^{2}(\Omega).

Proposition 1.1.5

(Young’s inequality)
.

For all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}

ab\leq\frac{a^{2}}{2}+\frac{b^{2}}{2}.

Definition 1.1.8

(Sobolev space)
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and let
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
We define the
Sobolev space

W^{m,p}(\Omega):=\left\{v\in L^{p}(\Omega)\mid\begin{aligned} &\forall{\bm{i}},\left|{\bm{i}}\right|\leq m,\exists g_{{\bm{i}}}\in L^{p}(\Omega)\text{ such that }\\
&\int_{\Omega}uD^{{\bm{i}}}\phi=(-1)^{{\bm{i}}}\int_{\Omega}g_{{\bm{i}}}\phi\quad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)\end{aligned}\right\},

where we use the notation
{\bm{i}}=(i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
for multi-indices with length
\left|{\bm{i}}\right|=\sum_{j=1}^{d}i_{j}
and the notation for partial derivatives

D^{{\bm{i}}}=\frac{\partial^{\left|{\bm{i}}\right|}\phi}{\partial x_{1}^{i_{1}}\dots\partial x_{d}^{i_{d}}}.

For a function
v\in W^{m,p}(\Omega)
, the functions
g_{{\bm{i}}}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
are its distributional derivatives and we denote them with
D^{{\bm{i}}}v
.
With the norm

\left\|v\right\|_{W^{m,p}(\Omega)}:=\sum_{0\leq\left|{\bm{i}}\right|\leq m}\left\|D^{{\bm{i}}}v\right\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},

the space
W^{m,p}(\Omega)
is a Banach space.
If
p=2
, the space
W^{m,2}(\Omega)
is denoted by
H^{m}(\Omega)
and is a Hilbert space with the scalar product

(w,v)_{H^{m}(\Omega)}:=\sum_{0\leq\left|{\bm{i}}\right|\leq m}(D^{{\bm{i}}}w,D^{{\bm{i}}}v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

In particular, we will focus on the space

H^{1}(\Omega):=\left\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\mid\nabla v\in[L^{p}(\Omega)]^{d}\right\},

with the norm

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}=\left\|\cdot\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left|\cdot\right|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2},

where
\left|\cdot\right|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
denotes the seminorm
\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.
For simplicity of notation, we have written the norm of
L^{2}(\Omega)
instead of
[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}
, this is done for all vector quantities.

A fundamental difference between
L^{p}(\Omega)
and
W^{m,p}(\Omega)
is that the functions in
L^{p}(\Omega)
do not have a trace on the boundary
\partial\Omega
.
Instead, the trace of a function
v
of
W^{m,p}(\Omega)
on
\partial\Omega
is well-defined and we denote it with the restriction
v_{|_{\partial\Omega}}
.
We recall the kernel of the trace operator in the case
p=2
:

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega):=\left\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega)\mid v_{|_{\partial\Omega}}=0\right\}.

For a sufficiently smooth subset
\Gamma
of
\partial\Omega
, we define

H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega):=\left\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega)\mid v_{|_{\Gamma}}=0\right\}.

In these spaces an important inequality is valid:

Proposition 1.1.6

(Poincaré’s inequality)
.

There is a constant
C_{p}>0
such that

\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{p}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\quad\forall v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(1.4)

This inequality holds true also for all
v\in H^{1}_{\Gamma}(\Omega)
.

We denote the gradient of a function
v\in W^{m,p}(\Omega)
, with
m>0
, and the divergence of
{\bm{w}}\in[W^{m,p}(\Omega)]^{d}
, with
m>0
, as

\nabla v:=\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{d}}\right),\qquad\text{div}({\bm{w}}):=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\frac{\partial{\bm{w}}_{i}}{\partial x_{i}},

respectively.
We define the space
H(\text{div};\Omega)
, which is relevant in the analysis:

Definition 1.1.9
.

H(\text{div};\Omega):=\{\bm{w}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}\mid\text{div}(\bm{w})\in L^{2}(\Omega)\}

(1.5)

Moreover, we recall the divergence theorem:

Theorem 1.1.7

(Divergence theorem)
.

For all
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, for all
\bm{w}\in[H^{1}(\Omega)]^{d}

\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}\left(\bm{w}\right)v+\int_{\Omega}\bm{w}\cdot\nabla v=\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\bm{w}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}\right)v

where
{{\mathbf{n}}}
is the outward unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
.

1.2
Diffusion-advection-reaction problem

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
d\geq 1
be the space dimension and let
\Omega
be an open, bounded, Lipschitz subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Denote by
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
the boundary of the domain
\Omega
.

We define the diffusion-advection-reaction operator
{\mathcal{L}}
applied to a function
v:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
as

\mathcal{L}v:=\text{div}\left(-\bm{K}\nabla v+\bm{\beta}v\right)+\sigma v,

with coefficients
{\bm{K}}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sigma:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
.

{\mathcal{L}}
is a linear partial differential operator of the second order in which we can distinguish three terms:
\text{div}\left(-\bm{K}\nabla v\right)
is the diffusion term,
\text{div}\left(\bm{\beta}v\right)
is the advection term and
\sigma v
is the reaction term.

Let
\Gamma_{D}
and
\Gamma_{N}
be sufficiently regular subsets of the boundary such that
\Gamma_{D}\neq\emptyset
,
\Gamma=\Gamma_{D}\cup\Gamma_{N}
and
\Gamma_{D}\cap\Gamma_{N}=\emptyset
. Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions are imposed on
\Gamma_{D}
and
\Gamma_{N}
, respectively.
Let
{{\mathbf{n}}}({{\mathbf{x}}})
be the outward unit normal vector to the boundary at
{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\Gamma
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
,
g_{D}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{D})
and
g_{N}\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})
,
we consider the following boundary value problem for the diffusion-advection-reaction equation:

\displaystyle\text{div}(-\bm{K}\nabla u+\bm{\beta}u)+\sigma u

\displaystyle=f

in
\Omega
,

(1.6)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=g_{D}

on
\Gamma_{D}
,

(1.7)

\displaystyle-\bm{K}\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle=g_{N}

on
\Gamma_{N}
.

(1.8)

We make the following assumptions on the data:

{\bm{K}}\in\left[L^{\infty}(\Omega)\right]^{d\times d},\quad{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\in\left[W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\right]^{d},\quad\sigma\in L^{\infty}(\Omega).

(1.9)

In particular, this implies the regularity of
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
:

{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\in\left[H^{1}(\Omega)\right]^{d}
and
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\in H(\mathrm{div};\Omega).

We also assume that the following condition of ellipticity is satisfied.

Definition 1.2.1

(Ellipticity condition)
.

The
ellipticity condition
yields if there exists a constant
k_{min}>0
such that

\bm{\xi}^{T}{\bm{K}}({{\mathbf{x}}})\bm{\xi}\geq k_{min}\left\|\bm{\xi}\right\|^{2}\quad\forall\bm{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\forall{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\Omega,

(1.10)

where
\left\|\cdot\right\|
denotes the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Choosing
\bm{\xi}=(1,0,\dots,0)
, we deduce from the ellipticity condition (
1.10
)
that

{\bm{K}}_{11}({{\mathbf{x}}})\geq k_{min}>0\quad\text{for all ${{\mathbf{x}}}\in\Omega$}.

(1.11)

We observe that, if the diffusion term vanishes, then the ellipticity condition is not satisfied, so this case is not included in our analysis.
Moreover, we make the following assumption: if at least one between
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
and
\sigma
is not null, then there exists a constant
\sigma_{0}>0
such that

\sigma({{\mathbf{x}}})+\frac{1}{2}\text{div}\left({\boldsymbol{\beta}}({{\mathbf{x}}})\right)\geq\sigma_{0}\quad\text{ a.e. }{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\Omega.

(1.12)

Under these hypothesis is always present the diffusion term while the advection and reaction terms can both vanish,
but if we have at least one of them, they need to satisfy the assumption (
1.12
).

We also distinguish between the inflow and outflow parts of the boundary
\Gamma
defined as

\Gamma_{-}:=\{{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\Gamma\mid\bm{\beta}({{\mathbf{x}}})\cdot\mathbf{n}({{\mathbf{x}}})<0\},\quad\Gamma_{+}:=\{{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\Gamma\mid\bm{\beta}({{\mathbf{x}}})\cdot\mathbf{n}({{\mathbf{x}}})\geq 0\},

(1.13)

respectively.
We assume that,
when the advection term
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
is present,
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}\geq 0
on
\Gamma_{N}
and that
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}<0
on
\Gamma_{D}
.
We are assuming

\Gamma_{D}=\Gamma_{-},\qquad\Gamma_{N}=\Gamma_{+}.

(1.14)

This is done for simplicity but it it also physically reasonable, for example, if we want to model the movement of a substance knowing its concentration at the entrance but not at the exit.

We now consider the classical weak variational formulation of the model problem.
Assume that for all
g_{D}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{D})

there exists an extension
\bar{g}_{D}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)
such that
\bar{g_{D}}_{|_{\Gamma_{D}}}=g_{D}
and that there is a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\left\|\bar{g}_{D}\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)}\leq C_{1}\left\|g_{D}\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{D})}
.
Then, we use the lifting of
\bar{g}_{D}
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
, i.e., a function
u_{g_{D}}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
such that
u_{g_{D}}=\bar{g}_{D}
on
\partial\Omega
and that

\left\|u_{g_{D}}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{2}\left\|\bar{g}_{D}\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)}
for some constant
C_{2}>0
.
Hence, we have a function
u_{g_{D}}
in
H^{1}(\Omega)
such that
u_{g_{D}}=g_{D}
on
\Gamma_{D}
and that

\left\|u_{g_{D}}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\left\|g_{D}\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma_{D})}
for some constant
C>0
.

The classical weak variational formulation of the problem (
1.6
)-(
1.8
) is:

\begin{cases}\text{Find }u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\text{ such that}\\
u=u_{g_{D}}+u_{0},\quad u_{0}\in H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega),\\
a(u_{0},v)=L(v)-a(u_{g_{D}},v)\text{ for all }v\in H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega),\end{cases}

(1.15)

with the bilinear form

a(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}\bm{K}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+\int_{\Omega}\left({\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot\nabla u\right)v+\int_{\Omega}\left(\sigma+\text{div}(\bm{\beta})\right)uv

and the linear functional

L(v):=\int_{\Omega}fv-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}g_{N}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}v.

We prove the equivalence between the model problem (
1.6
)–(
1.8
) and the variational formulation (
1.15
) in the following proposition.

Proposition 1.2.1
.

If
u
is a weak solution of the problem (
1.6
)–(
1.8
), then
u
satisfies the variational problem (
1.15
). Conversely, if
u
satisfies (
1.15
), then
u
solves the problem (
1.6
)–(
1.8
).

Proof.

First, we prove that if
u
is solution of (
1.6
)–(
1.8
), then it also solves (
1.15
).
Multiply (
1.6
) by a test function
v\in H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)
and integrate over the domain
\Omega
:

\int_{\Omega}\text{div}(-\bm{K}\nabla u)v+\int_{\Omega}\left({\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot\nabla u\right)v+\int_{\Omega}\left(\sigma+\text{div}(\bm{\beta})\right)uv=\int_{\Omega}fv.

Integrating by parts the first term, we get

\int_{\Omega}\bm{K}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v-\int_{\partial\Omega}\bm{K}\nabla u\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}v+\int_{\Omega}\left({\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot\nabla u\right)v+\int_{\Omega}\left(\sigma+\text{div}(\bm{\beta})\right)uv=\int_{\Omega}fv.

Owing to the fact that
v=0
on
\Gamma_{D}
and to the Neumann boundary condition (
1.8
) we arrive at

\int_{\Omega}\bm{K}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+\int_{\Omega}\left({\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot\nabla u\right)v+\int_{\Omega}\left(\sigma+\text{div}(\bm{\beta})\right)uv=\int_{\Omega}fv-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}g_{N}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}v.

We have reached
a(u,v)=L(v)
and we obtain the thesis separating
u_{0}
and
u_{g_{D}}
.

Conversely, let
u
be a solution to (
1.15
). Consider
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
as a test function, we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\left(\text{div}(-\bm{K}\nabla u+\bm{\beta}u)+\sigma u\right)\phi=\int_{\Omega}f\phi.

This implies that
{\mathcal{L}}u=f
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, since
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
is dense in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Hence, the equation (
1.6
) holds a.e. in
\Omega
.
The Dirichlet boundary condition (
1.7
) follows from the fact that
u_{0}=0
on
\Gamma_{D}
since
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)
and
u_{g_{D}}=g_{D}
on
\Gamma_{D}
for the lifting.
Moreover, let
\phi\in H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)
, now that we have
{\mathcal{L}}u=f
a.e. in
\Omega
, we obtain

-\int_{\partial\Omega}{\bm{K}}\nabla u\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}\phi=-\int_{\Gamma_{N}}g_{N}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}\phi.

From this equality we recover the Neumann boundary condition (
1.8
).
∎

We prove that the bilinear form
a
is coercive and continuous on
V=H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)
with respect to the
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
-norm and that the linear functional
L
is continuous on
V=H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)
with respect to the
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}
-norm.
Hence, for the Lax–Milgram Theorem the problem (
1.15
) is well-posed.

For all
w,v\in H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)
, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields

\displaystyle\left|a(w,v)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla w\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left\|\nabla w\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\left(\left\|\sigma\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left\|\text{div}({\boldsymbol{\beta}})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\left\|w\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left\|\sigma\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left\|\text{div}({\boldsymbol{\beta}})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\left\|w\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

The bilinear form
a
is continuous with the constant
M=\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left\|\sigma\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left\|\text{div}({\boldsymbol{\beta}})\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
.

For the coercivity, we have to consider the quantity
a(v,v)=\int_{\Omega}\bm{K}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v+\int_{\Omega}\left({\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot\nabla v\right)v+\int_{\Omega}\left(\sigma+\text{div}(\bm{\beta})\right)v^{2}
.
For the first term
\int_{\Omega}\bm{K}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v
,
owing to the ellipticity condition (
1.10
) and to the Poincaré’s inequality (
1.4
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\bm{K}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v

\displaystyle\geq k_{min}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\frac{k_{min}}{2}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{k_{min}}{2}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k_{min}}{2}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{k_{min}}{2C_{p}^{2}}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k_{min}}{2}\min{\biggl\{1,\frac{1}{C_{p}^{2}}\biggl\}}\left\|v\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}.

The second term can be rewritten using integration by parts as

\int_{\Omega}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot\nabla v)v=\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot\left(\nabla\frac{v^{2}}{2}\right)=-\int_{\Omega}\text{div}({\boldsymbol{\beta}})\frac{v^{2}}{2}+\int_{\partial\Omega}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}\frac{v^{2}}{2}.

Using the hypothesis (
1.12
), the fact that
v=0
on
\Gamma_{D}
and that
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}\geq 0
on
\Gamma_{N}
(
1.14
):

\int_{\partial\Omega}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}\frac{v^{2}}{2}+\int_{\Omega}\left(\sigma+\frac{\text{div}(\bm{\beta})}{2}\right)v^{2}\geq 0+\sigma_{0}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq 0.

The bilinear form
a
is coercive in
H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)
with the constant
\alpha=\frac{k_{min}}{2}\min{\big\{1,\frac{1}{C_{p}^{2}}\big\}}
.

Owing to Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and to the continuity of the Neumann trace we obtain the continuity of the linear functional
L
:

\left|L(v)\right|\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\|g_{N}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}\leq\left(\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\left\|g_{N}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{N})}\right)\left\|v\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

As a consequence of the inequalities that we have shown and Lax–Milgram Theorem, the variational problem (
1.15
) admits a unique solution.

Chapter 2
The discrete setting

In this chapter we introduce the discrete setting for the discontinuous Galerkin methods.
In Section
2.1
we fix the notation, give some definitions and list the assumptions on the mesh of the domain
\Omega
.
In Section
2.2
we define the typical tools of DG methods such as jumps, averages and broken functional spaces together with important properties that will be used later in the analysis.
We follow
[
11
, Chapter 1]
.

2.1
Domain and mesh sequences

We assume that the domain
\Omega
is an open, bounded, Lipschitz polytope in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
d\geq 1
.

In order to define the polytopes, which are a generalization of 2D polygons and 3D polyhedrons, we proceed by induction on the dimension
d
.
In one dimension a polytope is an open, bounded segment. In two dimensions is an open, bounded, connected and Lipschitz subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that its boundary is a finite union of closures of one dimensional polytopes.
In three dimensions a polytope is an open, bounded, connected and Lipschitz subset of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that its boundary is a finite union of closures of two dimensional polytopes.

Definition 2.1.1

(Polytope, facet)
.

A
0
-dimensional polytope is a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
containing a single point.
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq n\leq d
.
A
n
-dimensional polytope
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a relatively open, bounded, connected and Lipschitz subset of a
n
-dimensional affine subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that its relative boundary is a finite union of
(n-1)
-facets
, i.e., closures of
(n-1)
-dimensional polytopes.

We discretize the domain
\Omega
using a polytopic mesh.

Definition 2.1.2

(Polytopic mesh)
.

A
polytopic mesh

{\mathcal{T}}
of the domain
\Omega
is a finite family of disjoint
d
-dimensional polytopes
{\mathcal{T}}=\{T\}_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}}
such that

\bar{\Omega}=\bigcup_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}}\bar{T}.

Each
T\in{\mathcal{T}}
is called a
mesh element
.

The advantage of assuming that
\Omega
is a polytope is that it can be exactly covered by a mesh consisting of polytopic elements.
We work with a conforming mesh.

Definition 2.1.3

(Conforming mesh)
.

{\mathcal{T}}
is a
conforming mesh
if, for all
T,T^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{T}}
,
T\neq T^{\prime}
, the intersection
\partial T\cap\partial T^{\prime}
is either empty or a common
n
-dimensional facet with
n\leq d-1
.

We now fix the notation and give some definitions about the mesh.

Definition 2.1.4

(Element diameter, meshsize)
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a mesh of the domain
\Omega
. For all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}
, the
diameter
of the element
T
is defined by

h_{T}:=\sup_{{{\mathbf{x}}},{{\mathbf{y}}}\in T}\left|{{\mathbf{x}}}-{{\mathbf{y}}}\right|,

and the
meshsize

h
is defined as

h:=\sup_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}}h_{T}.

In order to analyse the convergence of the discontinuous Galerkin method as the meshsize
h
goes to zero, we will denote by
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
a mesh with meshsize
h
and we will consider a mesh sequence
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{H}}:=\{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\}_{h\in\mathcal{H}}
where
\mathcal{H}
is a countable subset of
\{h\in\mathbb{R}\mid h>0\}
having
0
as only accumulation point.

Definition 2.1.5

(Radius, barycentre, measure)
.

For any element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, we denote by
\rho_{T}
the
radius
of the largest ball inscribed in
T
, by
{{\mathbf{x}}}_{T}
the
barycentre
of the element
T
and by
\left|T\right|
its
d
-dimensional
measure
.

\partial T
denotes the boundary of
T
which is the union of
(d-1)
-dimensional facets
of
T
and
\left|\partial T\right|
denotes its
(d-1)
-dimensional measure.

Definition 2.1.6

(Element outward normal)
.

Let
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, we define
{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T}
on
\partial T
as the unit
outward normal
vector to the element
T
.

We now fix the notation about the mesh facets of a subdivision
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
.

Definition 2.1.7

(Mesh facets)
.

A
mesh facet
is a facet of a polytopic mesh element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, i.e., the closure of a
(d-1)
-dimensional polytope that form the boundary
\partial T
.
We denote by
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
the set of all facets of
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and by
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
a mesh facet.

We distinguish between
interior facets
for which there exist two elements
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
such that
e=\partial T_{1}\cap\partial T_{2}
and
boundary facets
for which there exists an element
T
such that
e\subseteq\partial T\cap\partial\Omega
.
The sets of interior and boundary facets are denoted by
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{B}
, respectively.
We assume that it is possible to collect the boundary facets where Dirichlet conditions are assigned in a set, denoted
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{D}
, and the boundary facets where Neumann conditions are assigned in a set, denoted
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{N}
.

Definition 2.1.8

(Facets diameter, measure, unit normal)
.

For all
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
, we denote by
h_{e}
the
diameter
of the facet
e
and by
\left|e\right|
its
(d-1)
-dimensional
measure
.
We associate a
unit normal
vector
{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}
to each facet
e
. If
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{B}
then
{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}
coincides with the unit outward vector
{{\mathbf{n}}}
normal to
\partial\Omega
.

The following analysis does not depend on the choice of
{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}.

Definition 2.1.9
.

For each element
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
we define the set of all its facets as

{\mathcal{F}}_{T}:=\{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}\mid e\subseteq\partial T\}.

The maximum number of mesh facets composing the boundary of mesh elements is indicated by

N_{\partial}:=\max_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}card({\mathcal{F}}_{T}),

where
card
denotes the cardinality of the set.

We now define three properties that a mesh sequence
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{H}}
can have.

Definition 2.1.10

(Shape regularity)
.

The mesh sequence
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{H}}
is
shape-regular
if, for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists
C_{sr}>0
, independent of
h
, such that, for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,

h_{T}\leq C_{sr}\rho_{T}.

(2.1)

Definition 2.1.11

(Graded mesh)
.

The mesh sequence
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{H}}
is
graded
if, for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists
C_{g}>0
, independent of
h
, such that, for all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and for all
e\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}
,

C_{g}h_{e}\geq h_{T}.

(2.2)

Definition 2.1.12

(Star-shaped property)
.

The
star-shaped property
holds for a mesh sequence
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{H}}
if, for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists
0<r\leq\frac{1}{2}
, independent of
h
, such that, each
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
is star-shaped with respect to a ball centred at some
{{\mathbf{x}}}\in T
and with radius
rh_{T}
.

We assume to work with mesh sequences that are shape-regular, graded and with the star-shaped property.
We will use these assumptions later in the analysis, together with the following one, which is a condition on the “chunkiness” of the mesh, similar to
[
23
, eq. 26]
.

We assume that, for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists a constant
\eta>0
, independent of
h
, such that, for all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
,

h_{T}\left|\partial T\right|\leq\eta\left|T\right|.

(2.3)

Remark 2.1.1
.

We prove the above inequality in the case
d=2
for shape-regular meshes with triangular elements.
Let
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, we observe that
\left|\partial T\right|h_{T}\leq 3h_{T}^{2}
, since
h_{T}
is the diameter of
T
and
\left|e\right|\leq h_{T}
for all
e\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}
.
Moreover, for the shape-regularity assumption (
2.1
) there exists
C_{sr}>0
, independent of
h
, such that, for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,

h_{T}\leq C_{sr}\rho_{T},

implying that
\left|\partial T\right|h_{T}\leq 3h_{T}^{2}\leq 3C_{sr}^{2}\rho_{T}^{2}
.
Finally, we note that
\pi\rho_{T}^{2}\leq\left|T\right|
, since the area of the triangle
T
is greater than the area of the largest circle inscribed, so we obtain
\left|\partial T\right|h_{T}\leq\eta\left|T\right|
with

\eta=\frac{3}{\pi}C_{sr}^{2}
.

We also observe that the inequality (
2.3
) holds for shape-regular meshes with convex elements. This is a consequence of the fact that the measure of the boundary of a convex element is smaller than the measure of the boundary of any ball circumscribed, due to convexity.

2.2
Discontinuous Galerkin tools

We now introduce the typical tools of a discontinuous Galerkin approximation such as jumps and averages of scalar and vector-valued functions across the facets of a mesh
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
.

Given
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, for all
\varphi\in H^{1}(T)
the trace of
\varphi
along the boundary
\partial T
is well-defined and we denote it by
\varphi_{|_{T}}
.
Let
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}
be an interior facet shared by the elements
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
.
If we consider a function
\varphi
in
H^{1}(T_{1}\cup T_{2})
, then along
e
there are two traces of
\varphi
:
\varphi_{|_{T_{1}}}
and
\varphi_{|_{T_{2}}}
. We can add or subtract those values and we obtain an average and a jump for
\varphi
.
Recall that the unit vector
{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T_{1}}
normal to
e
points exterior to
T_{1}
and that the unit vector
{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T_{2}}
normal to
e
points exterior to
T_{2}
, so
{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T_{1}}=-{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T_{2}}
.

Definition 2.2.1

(Averages and jumps of a scalar function)
.

For all
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}
with
e=\partial T_{1}\cap\partial T_{2}
and for all
\varphi\in H^{1}(T_{1}\cup T_{2})
, we define the
average
of
\varphi
on
e
as

\{\!\!\{\varphi\}\!\!\}:=\frac{\varphi_{|_{T_{1}}}+\varphi_{|_{T_{2}}}}{2},

and the
jump
of
\varphi
on
e
as

[\![\varphi]\!]:=\varphi_{|_{T_{1}}}{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T_{1}}+\varphi_{|_{T_{2}}}{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T_{2}}.

For all
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{B}
with
e\subseteq\partial T\cap\partial\Omega
and for all
\varphi\in H^{1}(T)
, we define the
average
and the
jump
of
\varphi
on
e
as

\{\!\!\{\varphi\}\!\!\}:=\varphi_{|_{T}},\quad[\![\varphi]\!]:=\varphi_{|_{T}}{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T}.

Definition 2.2.2

(Averages and jumps of a vector-valued function)
.

For all
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}
with
e=\partial T_{1}\cap\partial T_{2}
and for all
{\bm{w}}\in[H^{1}(T_{1}\cup T_{2})]^{d}
, we define the
average
of
{\bm{w}}
on
e
as

\{\!\!\{{\bm{w}}\}\!\!\}:=\frac{{\bm{w}}_{|_{T_{1}}}+{\bm{w}}_{|_{T_{2}}}}{2},

and the
jump
of
{\bm{w}}
on
e
as

[\![{\bm{w}}]\!]:=\bm{w}_{|_{T_{1}}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T_{1}}+{\bm{w}}_{|_{T_{2}}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T_{2}}.

For all
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{B}
with
e\subseteq\partial T\cap\partial\Omega
and for all
{\bm{w}}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{d}
, we define the
average
and the
jump
of
\bm{w}
on
e
as

\{\!\!\{{\bm{w}}\}\!\!\}:={\bm{w}}_{|_{T}},\quad[\![{\bm{w}}]\!]:={\bm{w}}_{|_{T}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T}.

Notice that the jump
[\![\varphi]\!]
of a scalar function
\varphi
is a vector while the jump
[\![{\bm{w}}]\!]
of a vector-valued function
{\bm{w}}
is a scalar. The advantage of these definitions is that they do not depend on the ordering assigned to the elements
T_{i}
.

For simplicity, on an interior facet
e=\partial T_{1}\cap\partial T_{2}
we use the notation
\varphi_{i}=\varphi_{|_{T_{i}}}
,
\bm{w}_{i}={\bm{w}}_{|_{T_{i}}}
and
{{\mathbf{n}}}_{i}={{\mathbf{n}}}_{T_{i}}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
and on a boundary facet
e=\partial T\cap\partial\Omega
we use
\varphi=\varphi_{|_{T}}
,
\bm{w}={\bm{w}}_{|_{T}}
and
{{\mathbf{n}}}={{\mathbf{n}}}_{T}={{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}
.

A classical tool in discontinuous Galerkin methods to deal with the advection term is the use of upwind numerical fluxes. We introduce them and some of their useful properties.

For each element
T
we define the inflow and outflow parts of
\partial T
as

\partial T_{-}:=\{{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\partial T\mid\bm{\beta}({{\mathbf{x}}})\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T}({{\mathbf{x}}})<0\},\quad\partial T_{+}:=\{{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\partial T\mid\bm{\beta}({{\mathbf{x}}})\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T}({{\mathbf{x}}})>0\},

respectively,
and the neutral part as

\partial T_{0}:=\{{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\partial T\mid\bm{\beta}({{\mathbf{x}}})\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T}({{\mathbf{x}}})=0\}.

For each
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
, we make the following assumption:

{\boldsymbol{\beta}}({{\mathbf{x}}})\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}({{\mathbf{x}}})
has the same sign for all
{{\mathbf{x}}}\in e
. Hence, for all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and for all
e\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}
we have that
e\subseteq\partial T_{-}
or
e\subseteq\partial T_{+}
or
e\subseteq\partial T_{0}
.
For all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, instead of considering the arithmetic average, we can consider the upwind idea.
This means that for all function
\bm{w}\in[H^{1}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})]^{d}
on
\partial T_{-}
we consider only the trace from outside, on
\partial T_{+}
the trace from inside and on
\partial T_{0}
we can choose any value directed as
\bm{w}
.

Definition 2.2.3

(Upwind numerical flux)
.

For all
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}
with
e=\partial T_{1}\cap\partial T_{2}
and for all
{\bm{w}}\in[H^{1}(T_{1}\cup T_{2})]^{d}
, we define the

upwind numerical flux
of
{\bm{w}}
on
e
as

\{\!\!\{{\bm{w}}\}\!\!\}_{\text{upw}}:=\begin{cases}{\bm{w}}_{2}&\text{if ${\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{1}<0$,}\\
{\bm{w}}_{1}&\text{if ${\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{1}>0$,}\\
\{\!\!\{{\bm{w}}\}\!\!\}&\text{if ${\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{1}=0$.}\end{cases}

(2.4)

For all
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{B}
with
e\subseteq\partial T\cap\partial\Omega
and for all
{\bm{w}}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{d}
, we define the
upwind numerical flux
of
\bm{w}
on
e
as

\{\!\!\{{\bm{w}}\}\!\!\}_{\text{upw}}={\bm{w}}_{|_{T}}.

We can define a more general average:

Definition 2.2.4

(Weighted averages)
.

For all
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}
with
e=\partial T_{1}\cap\partial T_{2}
and for all
\bm{w}\in[H^{1}(T_{1}\cup T_{2})]^{d}
, we define the
weighted average
of
\bm{w}
on
e
as

\{\!\!\{\bm{w}\}\!\!\}_{\alpha}:=\alpha_{1}\bm{w}_{|_{T_{1}}}+\alpha_{2}\bm{w}_{|_{T_{2}}},

with
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=1
,
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\geq 0
.

For all
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{B}
with
e\subseteq\partial T\cap\partial\Omega
and for all
\bm{w}\in[H^{1}(T)]^{d}
, we define the
weighted average
of
\bm{w}
on
e
as

\{\!\!\{\bm{w}\}\!\!\}_{\alpha}:=\bm{w}_{|_{T}}.

We note that the arithmetic average is obtained for
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=\frac{1}{2}
while the upwind flux is obtained when
\alpha_{i}=\frac{(sign({\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{i})+1)}{2}
for
i=1,2
.

Proposition 2.2.1
.

For all
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}
and for all
\varphi\in H^{1}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
,

\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\varphi\}\!\!\}_{\mathrm{upw}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}=\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\varphi\}\!\!\}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}+\frac{1}{2}\left|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right|[\![\varphi]\!]\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}.

(2.5)

Proof.

Using the definitions of jumps and averages and the continuity of
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
(
1.9
), the right-hand side is equal to

\frac{1}{2}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}){\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}+\frac{1}{2}\left|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right|\left(\varphi_{1}{{\mathbf{n}}}_{1}+\varphi_{2}{{\mathbf{n}}}_{2}\right)\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}.

If
{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}={{\mathbf{n}}}_{1}
we obtain

\frac{1}{2}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}){\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}+\frac{1}{2}\left|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right|\left(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}\right).

and coincides with
\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\varphi\}\!\!\}_{\text{upw}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}
, using the definition of the upwind flux.
∎

Proposition 2.2.2
.

For all
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}
and for all
\varphi\in H^{1}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
,

\frac{1}{2}\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![\varphi^{2}]\!]=\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\varphi\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![\varphi]\!].

(2.6)

Proof.

Using the definitions of jumps and averages and the continuity of
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
(
1.9
) we can rewrite the assertion as

\frac{1}{2}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot(\varphi_{1}^{2}{{\mathbf{n}}}_{1}+\varphi_{2}^{2}{{\mathbf{n}}}_{2})=\frac{1}{2}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}\varphi_{1}+{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\varphi_{2})\cdot(\varphi_{1}{{\mathbf{n}}}_{1}+\varphi_{2}{{\mathbf{n}}}_{2}).

Since
{{\mathbf{n}}}_{1}=-{{\mathbf{n}}}_{2}
the two quantities coincide.
∎

We now prove the “DG magic formula”, which is fundamental for deriving and analysing the discontinuous Galerkin formulation.

Proposition 2.2.5

(DG magic formula)
.

For all
\varphi\in H^{1}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
and for all
{\bm{w}}\in\left[H^{1}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})\right]^{d}
,

\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}{\bm{w}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T}\varphi=\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\left(\{\!\!\{{\bm{w}}\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![\varphi]\!]+[\![{\bm{w}}]\!]\{\!\!\{\varphi\}\!\!\}\right)+\int_{\partial\Omega}{\bm{w}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}\varphi.

(2.7)

Proof.

Let
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}
with
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
the elements sharing the facet
e
.
Using the definitions of jumps and averages and the fact that for each internal facet
{{\mathbf{n}}}_{1}=-{{\mathbf{n}}}_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{{\bm{w}}\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![\varphi]\!]+[\![{\bm{w}}]\!]\{\!\!\{\varphi\}\!\!\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left({\bm{w}}_{1}+{\bm{w}}_{2}\right)\cdot\left(\varphi_{1}{{\mathbf{n}}}_{1}+\varphi_{2}{{\mathbf{n}}}_{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left({\bm{w}}_{1}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{1}+{\bm{w}}_{2}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{2}\right)\left(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}\right)

\displaystyle={\bm{w}}_{1}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{1}\varphi_{1}+{\bm{w}}_{2}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{2}\varphi_{2}.

The assertion follows considering also the boundary facets.
∎

We recall the discrete inverse trace inequality that is a useful tool to analyse DG methods.

Lemma 2.2.6

(Discrete trace inequality)
.

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{\mathcal{H}}
be a shape-regular mesh sequence with the star-shaped property. Then, exists
C_{tr}>0
such that

\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial T)}\leq C_{tr}h_{T}^{-1}\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(T)},

(2.8)

for all

, all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, all
v\in\mathbb{P}^{p}_{d}(T)
.

Proof.

The assertion follows combining two results that hold under the assumptions.
The estimate of
[
28
, Lemma 2]
: for all

\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial T)}\leq\frac{d+c}{rh_{T}}\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(T)}+\frac{h_{T}}{rc}\left\|\nabla v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(T)},

where

is defined in
2.1.12
and
the bound of
[
5

exists

∎

We observe that the inequality above and the value of

are relative to polynomial spaces.
Since we will use as discrete space in the DG scheme the quasi-Trefftz space, which is a subspace of the full polynomial space as we will see in Chapter
5
, this inequality can be applied.
We observe that we can have inverse trace inequalities also for different spaces, since the only property of the polynomial space that is used is its finite dimension.

Chapter 3
The DG variational formulation

We study the discontinuous Galerkin method for approximating the diffusion-advection-reaction problem (
1.15
).
In this chapter, we derive the weak discontinuous Galerkin variational formulation of the model problem.
We proceed considering two cases separately and then we combine them together to deal with the general case.
First, we consider only the diffusion problem in Section
3.1
. We derive a formulation that depends on two parameters: the penalty parameter
\gamma>0
and the symmetrization parameter
\epsilon\in\{-1,0,1\}
.
The choice of
\epsilon
leads to three different formulations including the Symmetric Interior Penalty Galerkin (SIPG) one.
Then, we deal with the advection-reaction problem in Section
3.2
using the classical upwind numerical fluxes and, finally, in Section
3.3
we consider the diffusion-advection-reaction problem combining the previous two.
We remark that these two cases are considered separately for simplicity of the construction and of the following discrete coercivity and boundedness analysis.
Notice that, under the assumptions made in Section
1.2
, the diffusion equation is a particular case of the equation (
1.6
), since
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
and
\sigma
could vanish. However, this is not true for the problem with only advection and reaction terms, since a direct consequence of the ellipticity condition is that the diffusion matrix
{\bm{K}}
cannot be null.
We mostly follow the analysis of
[
11
, Chapter 4]
, which considers the SIPG method to handle the diffusion term and the upwind DG method to handle the advection-reaction terms, and combine it with the analysis of
[
31
, Chapter 2]
, which considers the three different formulations that arise from the choice of the symmetrization parameter
\epsilon
for diffusion problems.

Let
u
be the weak solution of problem (
1.6
)–(
1.8
) and recall that the trial and test space is
V=H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)
.
We want to approximate the problem (
1.15
) using DG methods.
Consider as discrete space
V_{h}
a finite-dimensional subspace of the broken Sobolev space
H^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
.
Hence
V_{h}\subseteq H^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})\not\subseteq V
.
In the spirit of Theorem
1.1.3
,
we assume that

u\in V_{*}:=H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})

and we define
V_{*h}:=V_{*}+V_{h}
.
From now on we will use this notation for the above spaces unless otherwise specified.

3.1
Diffusion

In this section we consider the following boundary value problem for the diffusion equation:

\displaystyle\text{div}(-\bm{K}\nabla u)

\displaystyle=f

in
\Omega
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=g_{D}

on
\Gamma_{D}
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle-\bm{K}\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle=g_{N}

on
\Gamma_{N}
.

(3.3)

This is a particular case of the model problem (

)-(

) with
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=\bm{0}
and

. We work under the same assumptions made in the previous chapters.
We will derive the following discrete bilinear form
a_{h}^{d}:V_{*h}\times V_{h}\to\mathbb{R}:

\displaystyle a_{h}^{d}(w,v_{h}):=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\bm{K}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v_{h}-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla w\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\epsilon\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}[\![w]\!]\cdot\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\}\!\!\}

\displaystyle-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}\bm{K}\nabla w\cdot\mathbf{n}v_{h}+\epsilon\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}w\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\frac{\gamma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}[\![w]\!]\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\frac{\gamma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}wv_{h}.

We construct it in the spirit of Theorem (
1.1.3
), so we want to achieve consistency, discrete coercivity and boundedness.

Let
u
be the exact solution of the diffusion problem (
3.1
)-(
3.3
).
Let

, we multiply (

) by the test function

and integrate on one element

\int_{T}\text{div}(-\bm{K}\nabla u)v_{h}=\int_{T}fv_{h}.

Integrating by parts
and summing over all elements, we obtain

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\bm{K}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v_{h}-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}\bm{K}\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}_{T}v_{h}=\int_{\Omega}fv_{h}.

Owing to the DG magic formula (
2.7
) with
{\bm{w}}={\bm{K}}\nabla u
and
\varphi=v_{h}
the second term can be rewritten as a sum over mesh facets in this way:

\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}\bm{K}\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}_{T}v_{h}=\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\left(\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla u\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+[\![\bm{K}\nabla u]\!]\{\!\!\{v_{h}\}\!\!\}\right)+\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{B}}\int_{e}\bm{K}\nabla u\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}v_{h}.

Note that
\bm{K}\nabla u\in H(\text{div};\Omega)
since
u
satisfies (
3.1
) and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Hence, for Proposition
2.2.4
we know that the jump
[\![\bm{K}\nabla u]\!]
is zero on each interior facet, implying that
\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}[\![\bm{K}\nabla u]\!]\{\!\!\{v_{h}\}\!\!\}=0
.
Using the Neumann boundary condition (
3.3
), we get

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\bm{K}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v_{h}-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla u\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}\bm{K}\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}v_{h}=\int_{\Omega}fv_{h}-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{N}}\int_{e}g_{N}v_{h}.

(3.4)

If the model problem is symmetric, like in this case, is good, in general, to preserve the symmetry property at the discrete level.
We can think of making the discrete bilinear form symmetric, this will lead to the Symmetric Interior Penalty Galerkin (SIPG) method.
Focusing on the left-hand side of (
3.4
), we note that the first term is already symmetric, so in order to achieve the symmetry we need to add two quantities:

-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}[\![u]\!]\cdot\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\}\!\!\}
and

-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}u\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}
.
We will consider not only the symmetric formulation but also two nonsymmetric ones.
For this reason, we introduce a parameter
\epsilon
that can assume value
-1
,
0
or
1
and generalize the previous step, adding to the left-hand side of (
3.4
) the two following terms

+\epsilon\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}[\![u]\!]\cdot\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\}\!\!\},\quad+\epsilon\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}u\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}.

The choice of
\epsilon
leads to three different bilinear forms and, hence, different variational formulations.
For
\epsilon=-1
the resulting method is called the Symmetric Interior Penalty Galerkin (SIPG) method, for
\epsilon=0
we have the Incomplete Interior Penalty Galerkin (IIPG) method and for
\epsilon=1
the Nonsymmetric Interior Penalty Galerkin (NIPG) method.
We observe that the quantity
+\epsilon\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}[\![u]\!]\cdot\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\}\!\!\}
can be added to the left-hand side of (
3.4
) without loosing the consistency property.
This is due to the fact that, since
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, from Proposition
2.2.3
we have
[\![u]\!]=0
on each interior facet.
The term
+\epsilon\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}u\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}
, instead, is not consistent, so the same quantity is added to the right-hand side of (
3.4
) to restore the consistency and using the Dirichlet boundary condition (
3.2
) on this term, we arrive at:

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\bm{K}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v_{h}-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla u\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\epsilon\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}[\![u]\!]\cdot\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\}\!\!\}

\displaystyle-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}\bm{K}\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}v_{h}+\epsilon\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}u\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}fv-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{N}}\int_{e}g_{N}v_{h}+\epsilon\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}g_{D}\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}.

To achieve the discrete coercivity, we need to add a stabilization term which penalizes interior facets and boundary jumps.
Let
\gamma>0
be the penalty parameter, we add on the left-hand side the two following terms:

+\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}}\frac{\gamma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}[\![u]\!]\cdot[\![v]\!],\quad+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\frac{\gamma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}uv.

(3.5)

As before, we note that the first term of (
3.5
) is consistent, since
[\![u]\!]=0
on interior facets, while the other term is not. Hence, we add the second term of (
3.5
) also to the right-hand side of the previous equation in order to maintain the consistency and then use Dirichlet condition (
3.2
).
We obtain the following formulation:

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\bm{K}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v_{h}-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla u\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\epsilon\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}[\![u]\!]\cdot\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\}\!\!\}

\displaystyle-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}\bm{K}\nabla u\cdot\mathbf{n}v_{h}+\epsilon\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}u\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle+\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}}\frac{\gamma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}[\![u]\!]\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\frac{\gamma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}uv_{h}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}fv_{h}-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{N}}\int_{e}g_{N}v_{h}+\epsilon\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}g_{D}\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\frac{\gamma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}g_{D}v_{h}.

3.2
Advection-reaction

In this section we consider the following boundary value problem for the advection-reaction equation:

\displaystyle\text{div}(\bm{\beta}u)+\sigma u

\displaystyle=f

in
\Omega
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=g_{D}

on
\Gamma_{D}
.

(3.7)

All the data satisfy the same assumptions we made for the general case, even though this is not a special case of our model problem, since the diffusion matrix is null.
In this case we will arrive at the following discrete bilinear form
a_{h}^{ar}:V_{*h}\times V_{h}\to\mathbb{R}:

\displaystyle a_{h}^{ar}(w,v_{h}):=

\displaystyle-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}(\bm{\beta}w)\cdot\nabla v_{h}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\sigma wv_{h}

\displaystyle+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{\beta}w\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}|\bm{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{e}|[\![w]\!]\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{N}}\int_{e}(\bm{\beta}w)\cdot\mathbf{n}v_{h}.

As before, we derive it in the spirit of Theorem
1.1.3
, so we want to achieve consistency, discrete coercivity and boundedness.

Let
u
be the exact solution of the problem (
3.6
)-(
3.7
).
Let

, we multiply (
3.6
) by the test function

and integrate on one element

\int_{T}\text{div}(\bm{\beta}u)v_{h}+\int_{T}\sigma uv_{h}=\int_{T}fv_{h}.

Integrating by parts the first term
and summing over all elements, we obtain

-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\left({\boldsymbol{\beta}}u\right)\cdot\nabla v_{h}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}\left({\boldsymbol{\beta}}u\right)\cdot\mathbf{n}_{T}v_{h}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\sigma uv_{h}=\int_{\Omega}fv_{h}.

From the DG magic formula (
2.7
) with
{\bm{w}}={\boldsymbol{\beta}}u
and
\varphi=v_{h}
we deduce that

\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}(\bm{\beta}u)\cdot\mathbf{n}_{T}v_{h}=\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\left(\{\!\!\{\bm{\beta}u\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+[\![\bm{\beta}u]\!]\{\!\!\{v_{h}\}\!\!\}\right)+\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{B}}\int_{e}(\bm{\beta}u)\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}v_{h}.

Note that
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}u\in H(\text{div};\Omega)
since
u
satisfies (
3.6
) and
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\sigma\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
From Proposition
2.2.4
we know that the jump
[\![{\boldsymbol{\beta}}u]\!]
is zero on each interior facet, implying that
\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}[\![{\boldsymbol{\beta}}u]\!]\{\!\!\{v_{h}\}\!\!\}=0
.
Using the Dirichlet boundary condition (
3.7
), we get

\displaystyle-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}(\bm{\beta}u)\cdot\nabla v_{h}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{\beta}u\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{N}}\int_{e}(\bm{\beta}u)\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}v_{h}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\sigma uv_{h}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}fv_{h}-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}(\bm{\beta}g_{D})\cdot\mathbf{n}v_{h}.

On every interior facet
e
we substitute the average
\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}u\}\!\!\}
by the classical upwind numerical flux
\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}u\}\!\!\}_{\text{upw}}
defined in (
2.4
). This is done in order to arrive at a formulation that is stable in a stronger norm than the

-norm, leading to a quasi-optimal error estimates.
We observe that we can do this without loosing the consistency.
We notice that the average
\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}u\}\!\!\}
appears on the second term multiplied by the jump
[\![v_{h}]\!]
, hence only the normal component of
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}u
is relevant.
For Proposition

, since

for the regularity of
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
(

), the jump
[\![{\boldsymbol{\beta}}u]\!]=0
.
This means that on each interior facet the two traces of

coincide, implying that
\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}u\}\!\!\}=\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}u\}\!\!\}_{\text{upw}}
.
By doing this substitution and by using the identity (
2.5
) valid on the interior facets, the second term becomes

\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{\beta}u\}\!\!\}_{\text{upw}}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{\beta}u\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\left|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right|[\![u]\!]\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!].

With this formula, we can also see the substitution of

with

like the addition of a stabilization term containing
[\![u]\!]

.
This leads to:

\displaystyle-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}(\bm{\beta}u)\cdot\nabla v_{h}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{\beta}u\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\left|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right|[\![u]\!]\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{N}}\int_{e}(\bm{\beta}u)\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}v_{h}

\displaystyle+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\sigma uv_{h}=\int_{\Omega}fv_{h}-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}(\bm{\beta}g_{D})\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}v_{h}.

3.3
Diffusion-advection-reaction

In this section we consider the diffusion-advection-reaction problem (
1.6
)-(
1.8
).
The DG method for the general problem combines the
a_{h}^{d}
bilinear form defined in Section
3.1
for the diffusion part and the
a_{h}^{ar}
bilinear form defined in Section
3.2
for the advection-reaction part.

The discontinuous Galerkin variational formulation of the problem (
1.6
)–(
1.8
) is as follows:

\text{Find }u_{h}\in V_{h}\text{ such that }a_{h}^{\text{dar}}(u_{h},v_{h})=L_{h}(v_{h})\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},

(3.8)

with the DG bilinear form

a_{h}^{\text{dar}}:V_{*h}\times V_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle a_{h}^{\text{dar}}(w,v_{h}):=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{T}{\bm{K}}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v_{h}-\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla w\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\epsilon\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}[\![w]\!]\cdot\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\}\!\!\}

\displaystyle-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}\bm{K}\nabla w\cdot\mathbf{n}v_{h}+\epsilon\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}w\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle+\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}}\frac{\gamma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}[\![w]\!]\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\frac{\gamma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}wv_{h}

\displaystyle-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}w)\cdot\nabla v_{h}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\sigma wv_{h}

\displaystyle+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}w\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}|\bm{\beta}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}|[\![w]\!]\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{N}}\int_{e}(\bm{\beta}w)\cdot\mathbf{n}v_{h},

and the linear form

L_{h}(v_{h}):=\int_{\Omega}fv_{h}-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{N}}\int_{e}g_{N}v_{h}+\epsilon\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}g_{D}\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\frac{\gamma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}g_{D}v_{h}-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}(\bm{\beta}g_{D})\cdot\mathbf{n}v_{h}.

The variational problem (
3.8
) is derived collecting together the steps of the two previous sections, indeed

a_{h}^{dar}(w,v_{h})=a_{h}^{d}(w,v_{h})+a_{h}^{ar}(w,v_{h})\quad\forall(w,v_{h})\in V_{*h}\times V_{h}.

The bilinear form

contains the parameter
\epsilon
that may take the value
-1
,
0
or

and the penalty parameter

that penalizes the jump of the function values.
Note that the problem (
3.8
) is independent of the choice of the normal
{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}
on the internal facets, since its only occurrence in

is inside the absolute value.

For the well-posedness of the problem (
3.8
) and for the convergence analysis in the spirit of Theorem
1.1.3
we need to prove: discrete coercivity, consistency, and boundedness for the bilinear form
a_{h}^{dar}
.
The consistency is established in the following section, while the discrete coercivity and the boundedness in the next chapter.

\displaystyle+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{\beta}u\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v]\!]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\left|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right|[\![u]\!]\cdot[\![v]\!]+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{N}}\int_{e}(\bm{\beta}u)\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}v+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\sigma uv

Conversely, let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
be solution of (

) and
take

T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, for the density of
C^{\infty}_{c}(T)
in
L^{2}(T)
, we have

\text{div}(-\bm{K}\nabla u+\bm{\beta}u)+\sigma u=f

in

and, hence,
a.e in

.

Let

with

[\![u]\!]=0

From the two previous equations, that we have derived, it follows that

A consequence is that
[\![-\bm{K}\nabla u+{\boldsymbol{\beta}}u]\!]=0

.

\text{div}(-{\bm{K}}\nabla u+{\boldsymbol{\beta}}u)+\sigma u=f\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega.

(3.11)

From (

Comparing the two formulas above we arrive at

on

4.1
Bilinear forms, norms and preliminary estimates

Recall that the trial and test space is
V=H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)
, that the exact solution

u\in V_{*}:=H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
and
V_{*h}:=V_{*}+V_{h}
.
In Chapter

we have considered as discrete space

a finite-dimensional subspace of the broken Sobolev space

. Now we consider

p

2.2.8

2.8
) for polynomial spaces.

We recall the
a_{h}^{dar}
bilinear form: for all
(v,w_{h})\in V_{*h}\times V_{h}
, we have

a_{h}^{dar}(w,v_{h})=a_{h}^{d}(w,v_{h})+a_{h}^{ar}(w,v_{h}),

where

\displaystyle a_{h}^{d}(w,v_{h})=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{T}{\bm{K}}\nabla w\cdot\nabla v_{h}-\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla w\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\epsilon\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}[\![w]\!]\cdot\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\}\!\!\}

\displaystyle-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}\bm{K}\nabla w\cdot\mathbf{n}v_{h}+\epsilon\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}w\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\cdot\mathbf{n}

\displaystyle+\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}}\frac{\gamma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}[\![w]\!]\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\frac{\gamma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}wv_{h},

\displaystyle a_{h}^{ar}(w,v_{h})=

\displaystyle-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}w)\cdot\nabla v_{h}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\sigma wv_{h}

\displaystyle+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}w\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}|\bm{\beta}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}|[\![w]\!]\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{N}}\int_{e}(\bm{\beta}w)\cdot\mathbf{n}v_{h}.

For the convergence analysis, in the spirit of Theorem
1.1.3
, we need to define some norms on
V_{*h}
.
For all
v\in V_{*h}
we define the following norms.
For the diffusion term we consider the energy norm

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{d}:=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{T}{\bm{K}}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v+\left|v\right|^{2}_{J},

with the jump seminorm

\left|v\right|^{2}_{J}:=\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}}\frac{\gamma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}[\![v]\!]^{2}+\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{D}}\frac{\gamma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}v^{2},

and the following stronger norm for the boundedness

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{d,*}:={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{d}+\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}\left\|{\bm{K}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla v\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial T)}.

For handling the advection-reaction term, we use

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{ar}:=\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}\int_{e}\left|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right|[\![v]\!]^{2},

and the stronger norm for the boundedness

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{ar,*}:={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{ar}+\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial T)}.

Combining the previous norms we define two norms, one stronger than the other, for the error analysis of the DG method for the diffusion-advection-reaction problem:

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{dar}:=

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{d}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{ar},

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{dar,*}:=

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{d,*}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{ar,*}.

In order to see that all of them are norms the only non trivial property to check is that for all
v\in V_{*h}
, if
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}=0
then
v=0

v=0

In the next two lemmas, we estimate the quantity

\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}\cup{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla v\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![w_{h}]\!]
with two different bounds as we will require both of them in following analysis.

Lemma 4.1.1
.

For all
(v,w_{h})\in V_{*h}\times V_{h}

\left|\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}\cup{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla v\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![w_{h}]\!]\right|\leq\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}\frac{h_{e}}{\gamma}\left\|({\bm{K}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla v)_{|_{T}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|w_{h}\right|_{J}.

(4.1)

Proof.

Let
(v,w_{h})\in V_{*h}\times V_{h}
and let

.
Using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we get

\left|\mathfrak{T}\right|\leq\left(\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}\cup{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{D}}\frac{h_{e}}{\gamma}\int_{e}\left(\{\!\!\{{\bm{K}}\nabla v\}\!\!\}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right)^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}\cup{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{D}}\frac{\gamma}{h_{e}}\left\|[\![w_{h}]\!]\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(4.2)

where the last term is the jump seminorm
\left|w_{h}\right|_{J}

In the other term we distinguish between interior facets and boundary ones:
for all

with

\displaystyle\int_{e}\left(\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla v\}\!\!\}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{e}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left({\bm{K}}_{|_{T_{1}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}({\bm{K}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla v)_{|_{T_{1}}}+{\bm{K}}_{|_{T_{2}}}^{\frac{1}{2}}({\bm{K}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla v)_{|_{T_{2}}}\right)\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\left\|({\bm{K}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla v)_{|_{T_{1}}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e)}+\left\|({\bm{K}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla v)_{|_{T_{2}}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e)}\right),

\displaystyle\left|\mathfrak{T}\right|\leq

\displaystyle\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}}\frac{h_{e}}{\gamma}\frac{1}{2}\left(\left\|({\bm{K}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla v)_{|_{T^{e}_{2}}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e)}+\left\|({\bm{K}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla v)_{|_{T^{e}_{2}}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e)}\right)\right.

Lemma 4.2.1

(Discrete coercivity: diffusion)

(4.5)

Proof.

Let

\begin{split}a_{h}^{d}(v_{h},v_{h})=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{T}{\bm{K}}\nabla v_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}+(\epsilon-1)\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}\cup{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}\cup{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{D}}\frac{\gamma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}[\![v_{h}]\!]^{2}.\end{split}

For
\epsilon=1
we have
a_{h}^{d}(v_{h},v_{h})={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{d}
for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}
. Hence, in this case the coercivity property is satisfied for all
\gamma>0
with
\alpha_{d}=1
.

Consider now the case where
\epsilon=0
or
\epsilon=-1
.
Owing to the fact that
h_{e}\leq h_{T}
for all
e\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}
and for all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and to the discrete trace inequality (
2.8
), we deduce from the bound (
4.3
) that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}\cup{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}\frac{h_{T}}{\gamma}\left\|\nabla v_{{h}_{|_{T}}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|v_{h}\right|_{J}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}\frac{h_{T}}{\gamma}C_{tr}h_{T}^{-1}\left\|\nabla v_{{h}_{|_{T}}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T}\right\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|v_{h}\right|_{J}.

Recalling that
N_{\partial}
is the maximum number of facets composing the boundary of a mesh element, using the ellipticity condition (
1.10
) and then the Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}\cup{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla v_{h}\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{C_{tr}N_{\partial}}{\gamma}\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left\|\nabla v_{h_{|_{T}}}\right\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|v_{h}\right|_{J}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{N_{\partial}C_{tr}}{\gamma k_{min}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left\|({\bm{K}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla v_{h})_{|_{T}}\right\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|v_{h}\right|_{J}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{N_{\partial}C_{tr}}{\gamma k_{min}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left\|({\bm{K}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla v_{h})_{|_{T}}\right\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left|v_{h}\right|_{J}^{2}\right).

Using this bound we achieve:

\displaystyle a_{h}^{d}(v_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle\geq{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{d}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon-1)\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{N_{\partial}C_{tr}}{\gamma k_{min}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left\|({\bm{K}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla v_{h})_{|_{T}}\right\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}+\left|v_{h}\right|_{J}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon-1)\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{N_{\partial}C_{tr}}{\gamma k_{min}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right){\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{d}^{2}.

Choosing
\gamma
large enough,
\gamma>\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\frac{N_{\partial}C_{tr}}{4k_{min}}
if
\epsilon=0
and
\gamma>\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\frac{N_{\partial}C_{tr}}{k_{min}}
if
\epsilon=-1
, we obtain the coercivity property with
\alpha_{d}=1+\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon-1)\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{N_{\partial}C_{tr}}{\gamma k_{min}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}>0
.
∎

We notice that in this proof we could have used the bound (
4.1
) where
{\bm{K}}
is already inside the
L^{2}
-norm, instead of using (
4.3
) and then pay
\frac{1}{k_{min}}
. However, this is done to maintain the same constant
C_{tr}
when applying the discrete trace inequality (
2.8
), as
{\bm{K}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla v_{h}
is not a polynomial.

The next lemma establishes the coercivity property of the advection-reaction bilinear form
a_{h}^{ar}
on
V_{h}
with respect to the
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{ar}
-norm.

Lemma 4.2.2

(Discrete coercivity: advection-reaction)
.

There exists
\alpha_{ar}>0
, independent of
h
, such that

a_{h}^{\text{ar}}(v_{h},v_{h})\geq\alpha_{ar}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{ar}\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},

where
\alpha_{ar}=\min\{1,\sigma_{0}\}
, with
\sigma_{0}
defined in (
1.12
).

Proof.

Let
v_{h}\in V_{h}
,

\displaystyle a_{h}^{ar}(v_{h},v_{h})=

\displaystyle-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}v_{h})\cdot\nabla v_{h}+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\sigma v_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}v_{h}\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!]+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}|\bm{\beta}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}|[\![v_{h}]\!]^{2}+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{N}}\int_{e}(\bm{\beta}v_{h})\cdot\mathbf{n}_{e}v_{h}.

Integration by parts on the first term yields

\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{T}({\boldsymbol{\beta}}v_{h})\cdot\nabla v_{h}=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{v_{h}^{2}}{2}\right)=-\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{T}\text{div}({\boldsymbol{\beta}})\frac{v_{h}^{2}}{2}+\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T}\frac{v_{h}^{2}}{2}.

Applying the DG magic formula (
2.7
) on the last term with
{\bm{w}}={\boldsymbol{\beta}}
and
\varphi=v_{h}^{2}
, we get

\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T}\frac{v_{h}^{2}}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}(\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}^{2}]\!]+[\![{\boldsymbol{\beta}}]\!]\{\!\!\{v_{h}^{2}\}\!\!\})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{B}}\int_{e}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}v_{h}^{2}.

We now use the formula (
2.6
):

\frac{1}{2}\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}^{2}]\!]=\{\!\!\{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}v_{h}\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![v_{h}]\!],

which holds true on each interior facet and observe that
[\![{\boldsymbol{\beta}}]\!]=0
on
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}
for the regularity assumption (
1.9
).
Combining the previous steps,
the bilinear form can be rewritten as follows:

\displaystyle a_{h}^{ar}(v_{h},v_{h})=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\bigg(\sigma+\frac{\text{div}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}{2}\bigg)v_{h}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}v_{h}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{N}}\int_{e}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}v_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}|\bm{\beta}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}|[\![v_{h}]\!]^{2}.

Recalling that

\Gamma_{D}=\Gamma_{-}
and
\Gamma_{N}=\Gamma_{+}
for (
1.14
) and using the assumption (
1.12
), we deduce that

a_{h}^{\text{ar}}(v_{h},v_{h})\geq\sigma_{0}\left\|v_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{e}|\bm{\beta}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}|[\![v_{h}]\!]^{2}\geq\min\{1,\sigma_{0}\}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{ar}^{2}.

The coercivity constant is
\alpha_{ar}=\min\{1,\sigma_{0}\}
.
∎

By combining the two previous lower bounds we obtain that the diffusion-advection-reaction bilinear form
a_{h}^{dar}
is coercive on
V_{h}
with respect to the
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{dar}
-norm.

Lemma 4.2.3

(Discrete coercivity)
.

Let
\gamma_{\epsilon}
be as in (
4.4
).
For all
\gamma>\gamma_{\epsilon}
, there exists
\alpha>0
, independent of
h
, such that

a^{\text{dar}}(v_{h},v_{h})\geq\alpha{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{dar}\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},

with
\alpha=\min\{1,\sigma_{0},\alpha_{d}\}
, where
\sigma_{0}
is defined in (
1.12
) and
\alpha_{d}
in (
4.5
).

Proof.

Let
v_{h}\in V_{h}
, from Lemma
4.2.1
we have

a_{h}^{d}(v_{h},v_{h})\geq\alpha_{d}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{d},

and from Lemma
4.2.2
we get

a_{h}^{ar}(v_{h},v_{h})\geq\min\{1,\sigma_{0}\}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{ar}.

Since
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{dar}={\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{d}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{ar}
, we infer the thesis

Lemma 4.3.1

(Boundedness: diffusion)
.

Proof.

\displaystyle a_{h}^{d}(v,w_{h})=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\bm{K}\nabla v\cdot\nabla w_{h}-\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}^{D}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{\bm{K}\nabla v\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![w_{h}]\!]

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{1}+\mathfrak{T}_{2}+\mathfrak{T}_{3}+\mathfrak{T}_{4}.

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\bm{K}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\bm{K}\nabla w_{h}\cdot\nabla w_{h}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left|v\right|_{J}\left|w_{h}\right|_{J}

\displaystyle\leq 2{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{d}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{d}.

Since

for all

and for all

, from to the bound (
4.1
) we get

\displaystyle\left|\mathfrak{T}_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}}\frac{h_{T}}{\gamma}\left\|({\bm{K}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla v)_{|_{T}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right\|_{L^{2}(e)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|w_{h}\right|_{J}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{d,*}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{d}.

Finally, we proceed as in the proof of Lemma
4.2.1
applying the discrete trace inequality

and the ellipticity condition (
1.10
) to the bound (
4.3
), achieving:

\displaystyle\left|\mathfrak{T}_{3}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{N_{\partial}C_{tr}}{\gamma k_{min}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left\|({\bm{K}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla w_{h})_{|_{T}}\right\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|v\right|_{J}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{N_{\partial}C_{tr}}{\gamma k_{min}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{d}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{d}.

By combining all these bounds we infer the assertion with
M_{d}=2+\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{N_{\partial}C_{tr}}{\gamma k_{min}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
∎

(Boundedness: advection-reaction)
.

, independent of

, such that

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}k_{min}^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{ar}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{d}+\left\|\sigma\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{ar}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{ar}.

\displaystyle\left|\mathfrak{T}_{4}+\mathfrak{T}_{5}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}\cup{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{N}}\int_{e}\left|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right|[\![v]\!]^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ \left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}\cup{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{N}}\int_{e}\left|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right|[\![w_{h}]\!]^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\left|\mathfrak{T}_{3}\right|=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{I}}\int_{e}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\{\!\!\{v\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![w_{h}]\!]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial T)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\ {\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{ar}

.

Lemma 4.3.3

(Boundedness)
.

There exists

, independent of

, such that

a_{h}^{\text{dar}}(v,w_{h})\leq M{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{dar,*}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|w_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{dar}\quad\forall(v,w_{h})\in V_{*h}\times V_{h},

Proof.

, Lemma

5.1
Definitions and notation

We call multi-indices the vectors
{\boldsymbol{i}}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}

We define their length

and we establish a partial order

if

for all

The factorial and the binomial coefficient are defined, respectively, as

{\boldsymbol{i}}!:=i_{1}!\cdots i_{d}!\quad\text{and}\quad\binom{{\boldsymbol{i}}}{{\boldsymbol{j}}}:=\frac{{\boldsymbol{i}}!}{{\boldsymbol{j}}!({\boldsymbol{i}}-{\boldsymbol{j}})!}=\binom{i_{1}}{j_{1}}\cdots\binom{i_{d}}{j_{d}}.

We use standard multi-index notation for partial derivatives and monomials:

D^{\boldsymbol{i}}f:=\partial_{x_{1}}^{i_{1}}\cdots\partial_{x_{d}}^{i_{d}}f,\quad\text{and}\quad{{\mathbf{x}}}^{\boldsymbol{i}}=x_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{i_{d}}.

We recall the following formula

\sum_{{\bm{i}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\left|{\bm{i}}\right|=k}\frac{1}{{\bm{i}}!}=\frac{d^{k}}{k!}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N},

(5.1)

obtained from the multinomial theorem

(x_{1}+\dots+x_{d})^{k}=\sum_{{\bm{i}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\left|{\bm{i}}\right|=k}\frac{k!}{{\bm{i}}!}{{\mathbf{x}}}^{{\bm{i}}}\quad\forall{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},k\in\mathbb{N},

by choosing

{{\mathbf{x}}}=(1,\dots,1)

[
27
, p. 198]
.
We use the canonical basis of

, namely
\{{{\mathbf{e}}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},1\leq k\leq d,({{\mathbf{e}}}_{k})_{l}=\delta_{kl}\}

We recall the Leibniz product rule for multi-indices:

D^{\boldsymbol{i}}(fg)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\boldsymbol{j}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\;{\boldsymbol{j}}\leq{\boldsymbol{i}}\end{subarray}}\binom{{\boldsymbol{i}}}{{\boldsymbol{j}}}D^{\boldsymbol{j}}fD^{{\boldsymbol{i}}-{\boldsymbol{j}}}g.

(5.2)

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\Upsilon\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open, bounded, Lipschitz set and let
{{\mathbf{z}}}\in\Upsilon
.

Definition 5.1.1

(Taylor polynomial)
.

Given

, the

of order

of

, centred at

{\mathsf{T}}^{p+1}_{{{\mathbf{z}}}}[v]({{\mathbf{x}}}):=\sum_{|{\boldsymbol{j}}|\leq p}\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{j}}!}D^{\boldsymbol{j}}v({{\mathbf{z}}})({{\mathbf{x}}}-{{\mathbf{z}}})^{{\boldsymbol{j}}}.

Notice that
{\mathsf{T}}^{p+1}_{{{\mathbf{z}}}}[v]
is a polynomial of degree at most
p

We highlight the following property: for every multi-index
{\boldsymbol{i}}

\begin{split}D^{\boldsymbol{i}}{\mathsf{T}}^{p+1}_{{{\mathbf{z}}}}[v]({{\mathbf{x}}})&=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left|{\bm{j}}\right|\leq p\\
{\bm{j}}\geq{\bm{i}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{j}}!}D^{{\boldsymbol{j}}}v({{\mathbf{z}}})\frac{{\boldsymbol{j}}!}{({\boldsymbol{j}}-{\boldsymbol{i}})!}({{\mathbf{x}}}-{{\mathbf{z}}})^{{\boldsymbol{j}}-{\boldsymbol{i}}}\\
&=\sum_{\left|\bm{\gamma}\right|\leq p-\left|{\bm{i}}\right|}\frac{1}{\bm{\gamma}!}D^{\bm{\gamma}+{\bm{i}}}v({{\mathbf{z}}})({{\mathbf{x}}}-{{\mathbf{z}}})^{\bm{\gamma}}={\mathsf{T}}^{p+1-\left|{\bm{i}}\right|}_{{{\mathbf{z}}}}[D^{{\bm{i}}}v]({{\mathbf{x}}}).\end{split}

(5.3)

From the first step, evaluated at
{{\mathbf{z}}}
, it follows that

D^{\boldsymbol{i}}{\mathsf{T}}^{p+1}_{{{\mathbf{z}}}}[v]({{\mathbf{z}}})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}D^{\boldsymbol{i}}v({{\mathbf{z}}})&\text{ if }|{\boldsymbol{i}}|\leq p,\\
0&\text{ if }|{\boldsymbol{i}}|>p.\end{array}\right.

(5.4)

We also recall the Lagrange’s form of the Taylor remainder
[

, Cor. 3.19]

and the segment

{{\mathbf{z}}}
and

v({{\mathbf{x}}})-{\mathsf{T}}^{p+1}_{{{\mathbf{z}}}}[v]({{\mathbf{x}}})=\sum_{|{\boldsymbol{j}}|=p+1}\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{j}}!}D^{\boldsymbol{j}}v({{\mathbf{x}}}_{*})({{\mathbf{x}}}-{{\mathbf{z}}})^{{\boldsymbol{j}}}.

(5.5)

If
\Upsilon
is a mesh element

, which is the case of our interest, we denote the centre of the Taylor expansion as
{{\mathbf{x}}}_{T}\in T
and the Taylor polynomial as
{\mathsf{T}}^{p+1}_{T}[v]({{\mathbf{x}}})={\mathsf{T}}^{p+1}_{{{\mathbf{x}}}_{T}}[v]({{\mathbf{x}}})
.

For each
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, for

we use the standard

\left\|v\right\|_{C^{0}(T)}:=\sup_{{{\mathbf{x}}}\in T}|v({{\mathbf{x}}})|,\qquad\left|v\right|_{C^{m}(T)}:=\max_{|{\boldsymbol{i}}|=m}\left\|D^{\boldsymbol{i}}v\right\|_{C^{0}(T)}.

We define the global space

C^{m}({\mathcal{T}}_{h}):=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega)\mid v_{|_{T}}\in C^{m}(T)\quad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}

\left|v\right|_{C^{m}{({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}}:=\max_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left|v_{|_{T}}\right|_{C^{m}(T)}.

We now define the quasi-Trefftz space and prove its optimal approximation properties for a general linear PDE with varying coefficients. This is possible since no special property of the diffusion-advection-reaction equation will be used in the proof of Theorem
5.2.1

Then, we will focus on the homogeneous diffusion-advection-reaction equation of our model problem.

We consider a linear partial differential operator of order

denoted as

{\mathcal{M}}:=\sum_{|\bm{j}|\leq m}\alpha_{\bm{j}}D^{\bm{j}},

(5.6)

with coefficients

for

We introduce local Trefftz and quasi-Trefftz spaces.

Definition 5.1.2

(Local Trefftz space)
.

Let

We define the

\mathbb{T}^{p}(T):=\{v\in\mathbb{P}_{d}^{p}(T)\mid{\mathcal{M}}v=0\text{ in }T\}.

This is the space of degree-

{\mathcal{M}}u=0
in

.
We observe that, by definition,
\mathbb{T}^{p}(T)
is a subspace of the full polynomial space
\mathbb{P}_{d}^{p}(T)
.

Remark 5.1.1
.

When all the terms in the differential operator
{\mathcal{M}}
are derivatives of the same order, such as, for example, in the Laplace’s equation and in the wave equation, the polynomial Trefftz space
\mathbb{T}^{p}(T)
offers the same orders of

-convergence
as the full polynomial space
\mathbb{P}^{p}_{d}(T)

[
28
, Lemma 1]
.
On the other hand, if the differential operator
{\mathcal{M}}
includes derivatives of different orders, in general,
the convergence rates for the Trefftz subspace of degree-
p
polynomials are not the same as those for full polynomial space of the same degree
[
15
]

For example, in
[
15

for the linear time-dependent Schrödinger equation the same accuracy as for full polynomials of degree at most

.
Moreover, when the derivatives are of different orders, the polynomial Trefftz space defined above can also be trivial, such as in the case of the Helmholtz’s equation, which does not admit polynomial solutions.
The quasi-Trefftz space, instead, is always rich enough to give the same approximation bounds as the full polynomial space of the same degree, as we will see below in Theorem
5.2.1
.
This suggests that also for problems with piecewise-constant coefficients could be a good idea to use quasi-Trefftz methods.

Definition 5.1.3

(Local quasi-Trefftz space)

Let

5.2

The following theorem states the approximation properties of the local quasi-Trefftz spaces.
We prove that Taylor polynomials of smooth solutions of the homogeneous linear PDE considered are quasi-Trefftz functions. This implies that we can approximate smooth solutions in

Theorem 5.2.1

(5.10)

Proof.

First we prove that
\mathsf{T}^{p+1}_{T}[u]\in{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)
.
By definition, we already know that
\mathsf{T}^{p+1}_{T}[u]\in\mathbb{P}^{p}_{d}(T)
.
For all
|{\boldsymbol{i}}|\leq p-m
, we have

\displaystyle D^{\boldsymbol{i}}{\mathcal{M}}\mathsf{T}^{p+1}_{T}[u]({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left|{\bm{j}}\right|\leq m}\sum_{{\bm{r}}\leq{\bm{i}}}\binom{{\bm{i}}}{{\bm{r}}}D^{\bm{r}}\alpha_{{\bm{j}}}({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})D^{{\bm{i}}-{\bm{r}}+{\bm{j}}}\mathsf{T}^{p+1}_{T}[u]({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left|{\bm{j}}\right|\leq m}\sum_{{\bm{r}}\leq{\bm{i}}}\binom{{\bm{i}}}{{\bm{r}}}D^{\bm{r}}\alpha_{{\bm{j}}}({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})D^{{\bm{i}}-{\bm{r}}+{\bm{j}}}u({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})

\displaystyle=D^{\boldsymbol{i}}{\mathcal{M}}u({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T}).

The first step is the identity (
5.8
) with
v=\mathsf{T}^{p+1}_{T}[u]\in C^{\left|{\bm{i}}\right|+m}(T)
, while the second step follows from the property (
5.4
) with partial derivatives of order at most equal to
\left|{\bm{i}}\right|+m\leq p
, and the third one is (
5.8
) again with
v=u\in C^{\left|{\bm{i}}\right|+m}(T)
.
Since
u
is solution of
{\mathcal{M}}u=0
in
T
, the last term
D^{\boldsymbol{i}}{\mathcal{M}}u({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})
vanishes, implying that
D^{\boldsymbol{i}}{\mathcal{M}}\mathsf{T}^{p+1}_{T}[u](\mathbf{x}_{T})=0
for all
|{\boldsymbol{i}}|\leq p-m
.
This shows that the Taylor polynomial
\mathsf{T}^{p+1}_{T}[u]
belongs to the quasi-Trefftz space
{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)
defined in (
5.7
).

In order to prove the inequality (
5.10
), fix
q\in\mathbb{N}
such that
q\leq p
.
Using the fact that
\mathsf{T}^{p+1}_{T}[u]\in{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)
, the best approximation error is estimated by the error of the Taylor polynomial

\inf_{v\in{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)}\left|u-v\right|_{C^{q}(T)}\leq\left|u-\mathsf{T}^{p+1}_{T}[u]\right|_{C^{q}(T)}.

Using the
\left|\cdot\right|_{C^{q}}
-norm definition, the linearity of the derivatives and the identity
D^{\boldsymbol{i}}{\mathsf{T}}^{p+1}_{T}[u]={\mathsf{T}}^{p+1-|{\boldsymbol{i}}|}_{T}[D^{\boldsymbol{i}}u]

for
|{\boldsymbol{i}}|=q\leq p
from (
5.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|u-\mathsf{T}^{p+1}_{T}[u]\right|_{C^{q}(T)}

\displaystyle=\max_{{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\;|{\boldsymbol{i}}|=q}\left\|D^{\boldsymbol{i}}(u-\mathsf{T}^{p+1}_{T}[u])\right\|_{C^{0}(T)}

\displaystyle=\max_{{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\;|{\boldsymbol{i}}|=q}\left\|D^{\boldsymbol{i}}u-\mathsf{T}^{p+1-q}_{T}[D^{\boldsymbol{i}}u]\right\|_{C^{0}(T)}.

Since
u\in C^{p+1}(T)
and
T
is star-shaped with respect to
\mathbf{x}_{T}
, we can consider the Lagrange’s form of the Taylor remainder (
5.5
) and estimate the last term as follows:

\displaystyle\max_{{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\;|{\boldsymbol{i}}|=q}\left\|D^{\boldsymbol{i}}u-\mathsf{T}^{p+1-q}_{T}[D^{\boldsymbol{i}}u]\right\|_{C^{0}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\;|{\boldsymbol{i}}|=q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|{\boldsymbol{j}}|=p+1-q\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{j}}!}\sup_{{{\mathbf{x}}}\in T}\left|D^{{\boldsymbol{i}}+{\boldsymbol{j}}}u({{\mathbf{x}}}_{*})({{\mathbf{x}}}-{{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})^{\boldsymbol{j}}\right|.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{d^{p+1-q}}{(p+1-q)!}h_{T}^{p+1-q}\left|u\right|_{C^{p+1}(T)},

where we use the formula

\sum_{|{\boldsymbol{j}}|=p+1-q}\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{j}}!}=\frac{d^{p+1-q}}{(p+1-q)!}
, obtained from (
5.1
) with
k=p+1-q
.
From the chain of inequalities, the thesis follows.
∎

5.3
Convergence analysis of the quasi-Trefftz DG method

5.3.1
Quasi-Trefftz space for the homogenous diffusion-advection-reaction equation

We now focus on the diffusion-advection-reaction operator
{\mathcal{L}}
with variable coefficients
{\bm{K}}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sigma:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
:

\mathcal{L}v=\text{div}\left(-\bm{K}\nabla v+\bm{\beta}v\right)+\sigma v.

Since
{\mathcal{L}}
is a linear partial differential operator of the second order, the quasi-Trefftz space (
5.7
) for the homogeneous diffusion-advection-reaction equation on a mesh element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
is

{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)=\{v\in\mathbb{P}^{p}_{d}(T)\mid D^{\bm{i}}(\mathcal{L}v)(\mathbf{x}_{T})=0,\forall\bm{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{d},|\bm{i}|\leq p-2\},\quad p\in\mathbb{N}.

(5.11)

As seen before, this space is well-defined if the PDE coefficients are sufficiently smooth.
The operator
{\mathcal{L}}
can be written in the form (
5.6
) with
m=2
, for which we have shown that the coefficients
\alpha_{\bm{j}}
need to belong to
C^{\max\{p-2,0\}}(T)
.
In order to deduce the regularity assumptions of
{\bm{K}}
,
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
and
\sigma
, under which the quasi-Trefftz space is well-defined, we expand the operator
{\mathcal{L}}
, using linearity and Leibniz formula (
5.2
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}v

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}D^{\bm{e}_{j}}(-\bm{K}\nabla v+\bm{\beta}v)_{j}+\sigma v

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\biggl(-\sum_{m=1}^{d}D^{\bm{e}_{j}}\big(\bm{K}_{jm}D^{\bm{e}_{m}}v\big)+D^{\bm{e}_{j}}(\bm{\beta}_{j}v)\biggl)+\sigma v

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\;\bm{\ell}\leq\bm{e}_{j}\end{subarray}}\binom{\bm{e}_{j}}{\bm{\ell}}\biggl(-\sum_{m=1}^{d}D^{\bm{\ell}}\bm{K}_{jm}D^{\bm{e}_{j}-\bm{\ell}+\bm{e}_{m}}v+D^{\bm{\ell}}\bm{\beta}_{j}D^{\bm{e}_{j}-\bm{\ell}}v\biggl)+\sigma v

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\biggl[-\sum_{m=1}^{d}\bigg(\bm{K}_{jm}D^{\bm{e}_{j}+\bm{e}_{m}}v+D^{\bm{e}_{j}}\bm{K}_{jm}D^{\bm{e}_{m}}v\bigg)+\bm{\beta}_{j}D^{\bm{e}_{j}}v+D^{\bm{e}_{j}}{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{j}v\biggl]+\sigma v.

It follows that
D^{\bm{\ell}}\bm{K}_{jm}
,
D^{\bm{\ell}}\bm{\beta}_{j}
and
\sigma
need to belong to
C^{\max\{p-2,0\}}(T)
for all
\bm{\ell}\leq\bm{e}_{j}
and for all
j,m=1,\dots,d
.
Hence, the quasi-Trefftz space
{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)
for the diffusion-advection-reaction equation is well-defined if

{\bm{K}}_{jm}\in C^{\max\{p-1,1\}}(T)
,
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{j}\in C^{\max\{p-1,1\}}(T)
and
\sigma\in C^{\max\{p-2,0\}}(T)
for all
j,m=1,\dots,d
.

Using linearity and Leibniz formula (
5.2
), we also derive the following expression of the partial derivatives of
{\mathcal{L}}v
which will be useful for the construction of the quasi-Trefftz basis functions:

\displaystyle D^{\bm{i}}\mathcal{L}v=

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{m=1}^{d}D^{\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}}(\bm{K}_{jm}D^{\bm{e}_{m}}v)+\sum_{j=1}^{d}D^{{\bm{i}}+\bm{e}_{j}}(\bm{\beta}_{j}v)+D^{{\bm{i}}}\sigma v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{m=1}^{d}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\leq\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}}\binom{\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}}{\bm{\ell}}D^{\bm{\ell}}\bm{K}_{jm}D^{\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}-\bm{\ell}+\bm{e}_{m}}v

(5.12)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\leq\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}}\binom{\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}}{\bm{\ell}}D^{\bm{\ell}}\bm{\beta}_{j}D^{\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}-\bm{\ell}}v+\sum_{\bm{\ell}\leq\bm{i}}\binom{\bm{i}}{\bm{\ell}}D^{\bm{\ell}}\sigma D^{\bm{i}-\bm{\ell}}v.

5.3.2
h
-convergence of the quasi-Trefftz DG method

We consider only homogenous diffusion-advection-reaction equation, i.e, with no source term
f
, since the quasi-Trefftz space defined in (
5.11
) is suited for this kind of problems.
Choosing the global quasi-Trefftz space of degree
p
defined in (
5.9
) as discrete space
V_{h}
in the DG scheme (
3.8
) with
f=0
, we obtain the quasi-Trefftz DG method.

The next theorem establishes the convergence rate in
h
for the error
u-u_{h}
in the
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{dar}
-norm.
From the quasi-optimality inequality (
4.6
) we deduce the convergence rate for the quasi-Trefftz DG method, using the the error bound (
5.10
).
We recall that

V_{*}:=H^{1}_{\Gamma_{D}}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
.

Theorem 5.3.1

(Quasi-Trefftz DG convergence rate)
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
and let
u\in V_{*}\cap C^{p+1}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
solve (
1.6
)-(
1.8
) with

{\bm{K}}_{jm}\in C^{\max\{p-1,1\}}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
,
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{j}\in C^{\max\{p-1,1\}}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
and
\sigma\in C^{\max\{p-2,0\}}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
for all
j,m=1,\dots,d
and
f=0
.
Let
u_{h}
solve (
3.8
) with discrete space
V_{h}={\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
and the penalty parameter
\gamma
as in Lemma
4.2.1
.
Assume that each mesh element
T
is star-shaped with respect to
\mathbf{x}_{T}
. Then, the following convergence rate holds

\begin{split}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-u_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{dar}\leq&\left(1+\frac{M}{\alpha}\right)\frac{d^{p}}{p!}\left[\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left|T\right|\biggl(\left(1+\eta\right)\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}\right.\\
&\left.+\frac{d^{2}}{(p+1)^{2}}\left(2C_{g}\gamma\eta+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}\eta h_{T}+h_{T}^{2}+\eta h_{T}\right)\biggr)h_{T}^{2p}\left|u\right|^{2}_{C^{p+1}(T)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

(5.13)

with
\alpha
as in Lemma
4.2.3
and
M
as in Lemma
4.3.3
.

Proof.

Since we have the quasi-optimality inequality (
4.6
), we now focus on estimating the quantity

\inf_{v_{h}\in{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{dar,*}
.
We recall, for
v\in V_{*h}=V_{*}+{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
, the definition of the
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{dar,*}
-norm defined in Section
4.1

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{dar,*}=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{T}{\bm{K}}\nabla v\cdot\nabla v+\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{I}\cup{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{D}}\frac{\gamma}{h_{e}}\int_{e}[\![v]\!]^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}\int_{e}\left|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{e}\right|[\![v]\!]^{2}

\displaystyle+\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}\left\|{\bm{K}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla v\cdot{{\mathbf{n}}}_{T}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial T)}+\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial T)}.

We recall the assumption (
2.2
): the mesh
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
is graded, i.e., there exists a constant
C_{g}>0
such that, for all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and all
e\in{\mathcal{F}}_{T}
,

C_{g}h_{e}\geq h_{T}.

Observe that, from the Young’s inequality,
[\![v]\!]^{2}=(v_{1}-v_{2})^{2}\leq 2(v_{1}^{2}+v_{2}^{2})
on an internal facet
e=\partial T_{1}\cap\partial T_{2}

and
[\![v]\!]^{2}=v^{2}
on a boundary facet
e
.
Using these two facts in the second and third terms of the norm, we rearrange the sums over parts of the mesh skeleton as sums over elements and obtain the bound:

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{dar,*}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\biggl(\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}\left\|\nabla v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(T)}+2C_{g}\frac{\gamma}{h_{T}}\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial T)}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial T)}

\displaystyle+\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(T)}+\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}h_{T}\left\|\nabla v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial T)}+\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial T)}\biggr).

Next, we use the definition of the
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{C^{m}}
-norms and obtain

\displaystyle{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|v\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{dar,*}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\biggl(\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}\left|T\right|\left\|\nabla v\right\|^{2}_{C^{0}(T)}+2C_{g}\frac{\gamma}{h_{T}}\left|\partial T\right|\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{C^{0}(T)}

\displaystyle+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}\left|\partial T\right|\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{C^{0}(T)}+\left|T\right|\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{C^{0}(T)}

\displaystyle+\left|\partial T\right|\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{C^{0}(T)}+\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}\left|\partial T\right|h_{T}\left\|\nabla v\right\|^{2}_{C^{0}(T)}\biggr).

Considering the quantity of our interest we have

\displaystyle\inf_{v_{h}\in{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{dar,*}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\inf_{v_{h}\in{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)}\biggl[\left(\left|T\right|+\left|\partial T\right|h_{T}\right)\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}\left\|\nabla(u-v_{h})\right\|^{2}_{C^{0}(T)}

\displaystyle+\left(2C_{g}\frac{\gamma}{h_{T}}\left|\partial T\right|+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}\left|\partial T\right|+\left|T\right|+\left|\partial T\right|\right)\left\|u-v_{h}\right\|^{2}_{C^{0}(T)}\biggr].

Using the Quasi-Trefftz approximation properties (
5.10
) we get

\displaystyle\inf_{v_{h}\in{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{dar,*}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\left[\left(\left|T\right|+\left|\partial T\right|h_{T}\right)\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}\frac{d^{2p}}{(p!)^{2}}h_{T}^{2p}\left|u\right|^{2}_{C^{p+1}(T)}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left(2C_{g}\frac{\gamma}{h_{T}}\left|\partial T\right|+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}\left|\partial T\right|+\left|T\right|+\left|\partial T\right|\right)\times\right.

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d^{2(p+1)}}{((p+1)!)^{2}}h_{T}^{2(p+1)}\left|u\right|^{2}_{C^{p+1}(T)}\right].

We now use the assumption (
2.3
): exists
\eta>0
such that

h_{T}\left|\partial T\right|\leq\eta\left|T\right|
for all
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
,
and we infer that

\displaystyle\inf_{v_{h}\in{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|u-v_{h}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}^{2}_{dar,*}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2p}}{(p!)^{2}}\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\biggl[\left(1+\eta\right)\left|T\right|\left\|{\bm{K}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}+\frac{d^{2}}{(p+1)^{2}}\times

\displaystyle\left(2C_{g}\frac{\gamma}{h_{T}}\eta h_{T}^{-1}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(T)}\eta h_{T}^{-1}+1+\eta h_{T}^{-1}\right)\left|T\right|h_{T}^{2}\biggr]h_{T}^{2p}\left|u\right|^{2}_{C^{p+1}(T)}.

Combining this bound with the inequality (
4.6
) yields the assertion.
∎

We remark that we use the
C^{m}
-norms in the convergence analysis of the quasi-Trefftz DG method, instead of the classical Sobolev norms
H^{m}
. We observe that in Theorem
5.2.1
and in Theorem
5.3.1
the solution
u
needs to belong to
C^{p+1}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
, which is a stronger regularity assumption than the one required in Theorem
4.4.3
, i.e.,
u\in H^{p+1}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
.
For the Trefftz space the analysis has been extended to the case of less regular solutions using the fact that “averaged Taylor polynomials”
[
28
, p. 421]
of solutions are Trefftz functions. However, we cannot use this argument since, in general, they are not quasi-Trefftz functions and to our knowledge a study using Sobolev norms for the quasi-Trefftz space is still missing
[
23
, Remark 4.7]
.
Apart from this difference, we have optimal convergence rates in the
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{dar}
-norm.

5.4
Basis functions

In this section, we define, for any
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, a family of bases for the quasi-Trefftz space
{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)
for the homogeneous diffusion-advection-reaction equation and derive an explicit formula to compute them.

Since each function
v\in{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)
is a degree-
p
polynomial,
v
can be expressed as a linear combination of scaled monomials centred in
{{\mathbf{x}}}_{T}\in T
:

v({{\mathbf{x}}})=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d},|{\boldsymbol{k}}|\leq p}a_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{T}}{h_{T}}\right)^{{\boldsymbol{k}}},

(5.14)

where the coefficients
\{a_{\boldsymbol{k}}\}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d},|{\boldsymbol{k}}|\leq p}
are such that

a_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\frac{h_{T}^{\left|\bm{k}\right|}}{{\boldsymbol{k}}!}D^{\boldsymbol{k}}v({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T}).

This is obtained by considering the derivatives of
v

D^{{\bm{i}}}v({{\mathbf{x}}})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\left|{\boldsymbol{k}}\right|\leq p\\
{\boldsymbol{k}}\geq{\bm{i}}\end{subarray}}a_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{T}}{h_{T}}\right)^{{\boldsymbol{k}}-{\bm{i}}}\frac{{\boldsymbol{k}}!}{({\boldsymbol{k}}-{\bm{i}})!}\frac{1}{h_{T}^{\left|{\bm{i}}\right|}},

and evaluating them at
{{\mathbf{x}}}_{T}
.
The construction of
v\in{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)
as in (
5.14
) is equivalent to finding the set of coefficients
\{a_{\boldsymbol{k}}\}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d},|{\boldsymbol{k}}|\leq p}
such that
v
satisfies
D^{\bm{i}}\mathcal{L}v({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})=0
for all
\left|{\bm{i}}\right|\leq p-2
.
Evaluating (
5.3.1
) in
{{\mathbf{x}}}_{T}
and using the relation
D^{\bm{k}}v({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})=\frac{a_{\bm{k}}\bm{k}!}{h_{T}^{\left|\bm{k}\right|}}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle D^{\bm{i}}\mathcal{L}v({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})=

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{m=1}^{d}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\leq\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}}\binom{\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}}{\bm{\ell}}D^{\bm{\ell}}\bm{K}_{jm}({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})\frac{a_{\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}-\bm{\ell}+\bm{e}_{m}}(\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}-\bm{\ell}+\bm{e}_{m})!}{h_{T}^{\left|{\bm{i}}-\bm{\ell}\right|+2}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\leq\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}}\binom{\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}}{\bm{\ell}}D^{\bm{\ell}}\bm{\beta}_{j}({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})\frac{a_{\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}-\bm{\ell}}(\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}-\bm{\ell})!}{h_{T}^{\left|{\bm{i}}-\bm{\ell}\right|+1}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\bm{\ell}\leq\bm{i}}\binom{\bm{i}}{\bm{\ell}}D^{\bm{\ell}}\sigma({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})\frac{a_{\bm{i}-\bm{\ell}}(\bm{i}-\bm{\ell})!}{h_{T}^{\left|{\bm{i}}-\bm{\ell}\right|}}.

Using the definition of the binomial coefficients, this can be rewritten as

\displaystyle D^{\bm{i}}\mathcal{L}v({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})=

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{m=1}^{d}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\leq\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}}\frac{(\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j})!}{\bm{\ell}!}D^{\bm{\ell}}\bm{K}_{jm}({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})\frac{a_{\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}-\bm{\ell}+\bm{e}_{m}}(i_{m}+(\bm{e}_{j})_{m}-\ell_{m}+1)}{h_{T}^{\left|{\bm{i}}-\bm{\ell}\right|+2}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{\bm{\ell}\leq\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}}\frac{(\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j})!}{\bm{\ell}!}D^{\bm{\ell}}\bm{\beta}_{j}({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})\frac{a_{\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}-\bm{\ell}}}{h_{T}^{\left|{\bm{i}}-\bm{\ell}\right|+1}}

(5.15)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\bm{\ell}\leq\bm{i}}\frac{{\bm{i}}!}{\bm{\ell}!}D^{\bm{\ell}}\sigma({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})\frac{a_{\bm{i}-\bm{\ell}}}{h_{T}^{\left|{\bm{i}}-\bm{\ell}\right|}}.

We recall the assumption (
1.11
):

{\bm{K}}_{11}({{\mathbf{x}}})\geq k_{min}>0\quad\text{for all}\quad{{\mathbf{x}}}\in\Omega.

From the conditions
D^{\bm{i}}\mathcal{L}v({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})=0
for all
\left|{\bm{i}}\right|\leq p-2
,
we can compute the element with
\bm{\ell}=\bm{0}
,
j=1
and
m=1
in the first sum of (
5.4
), dividing by its non-zero coefficient
{\bm{K}}_{11}({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})>0
:

\displaystyle a_{{\bm{i}}+2{{\mathbf{e}}}_{1}}=

\displaystyle\frac{{h_{T}^{\left|{\bm{i}}\right|+2}}}{{\bm{K}}_{11}({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})({\bm{i}}+2{{\mathbf{e}}}_{1})!}\times

\displaystyle\Biggl(-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,m=1,\dots,d\\
\bm{\ell}\leq\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}\\
(j,m,\bm{\ell})\neq(1,1,\bm{0})\end{subarray}}\frac{(\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j})!}{\bm{\ell}!}D^{\bm{\ell}}\bm{K}_{jm}({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})\frac{a_{\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}-\bm{\ell}+\bm{e}_{m}}(i_{m}+(\bm{e}_{j})_{m}-\ell_{m}+1)}{h_{T}^{\left|{\bm{i}}-\bm{\ell}\right|+2}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1,\dots,d\\
\bm{\ell}\leq\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{(\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j})!}{\bm{\ell}!}D^{\bm{\ell}}\bm{\beta}_{j}({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})\frac{a_{\bm{i}+\bm{e}_{j}-\bm{\ell}}}{h_{T}^{\left|{\bm{i}}-\bm{\ell}\right|+1}}

(5.16)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\bm{\ell}\leq\bm{i}}\frac{{\bm{i}}!}{\bm{\ell}!}D^{\bm{\ell}}\sigma({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})\frac{a_{\bm{i}-\bm{\ell}}}{h_{T}^{\left|{\bm{i}}-\bm{\ell}\right|}}\Biggr).

We have found this recurrence relation (
5.4
) between the coefficients
a_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}
, which will be used to compute the
coefficients iteratively.
We will describe an algorithm in Section
5.5
that at every steps relies only on values already computed and is initialized by the coefficients
a_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}
with
k_{1}\in\{0,1\}
.
It is clear from (
5.4
) that the recursive formula is based only on the partial derivatives at
{{\mathbf{x}}}_{T}
of the PDE coefficients
{\bm{K}}
,
{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
and
\sigma
.
We note that the equations (
5.4
) could also be written as a square linear system with
a_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}
with
k_{1}\neq 0,1
as unknowns and
a_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}
with
k_{1}\in\{0,1\}
as right-hand side. However, we will not follow this path but a recursive implementation.

We use the relation (
5.4
) to construct a basis for the local quasi-Trefftz space.
We recall the dimension of the space of polynomials of maximum degree
p
in
d
variables defined in
2.2.7
:

S_{d,p}=\text{dim}(\mathbb{P}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))=\binom{p+d}{d}.

To initialize the recursion, we choose two
2
polynomial bases:

\left\{\widehat{f}_{s}\right\}_{s=1,\dots,S_{d-1,p}}\text{ basis for }\mathbb{P}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\{\widetilde{f}_{s}\right\}_{s=1,\dots,S_{d-1,p-1}}\text{ basis for }\mathbb{P}^{p-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1}).

Their total cardinality is

N_{d,p}:=S_{d-1,p}+S_{d-1,p-1}=\binom{p+d-1}{d-1}+\binom{p+d-2}{d-1}=\frac{(p+d-2)!(2p+d-1)}{(d-1)!p!}.

We define the following set of
N_{d,p}
elements of
{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{T}^{p}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}b_{J}\in{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T):\\
b_{J}(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot)=\widehat{f}_{J}\;\text{ and }\;\partial_{x_{1}}b_{J}(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot)=0\text{ for }J\leq S_{d-1,p},\\
b_{J}(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot)=0\;\text{ and }\;\partial_{x_{1}}b_{J}(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot)=\widetilde{f}_{J-S_{d-1,p}}\text{ for }J\geq S_{d-1,p}+1.\end{array}\right\}_{J=1,\dots,N_{p,d},}

where
b_{J}(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot)
denotes the restriction of
b_{J}
to the hyperplane
\{x_{1}=({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1}\}
.
Equivalently,
the elements
b_{J}
are degree-
p
polynomials that satisfy the following system of equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}D^{\boldsymbol{i}}{\mathcal{L}}b_{J}({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})=0&\quad J=1,\ldots,N_{d,p},\;{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\;|{\boldsymbol{i}}|\leq p-m,\\
b_{J}(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot)=\widehat{f}_{J}\text{ and }\partial_{x_{1}}b_{J}(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot)=0&\quad J=1,\ldots S_{d-1,p},\\
b_{J}(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot)=0\text{ and }\partial_{x_{1}}b_{J}(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot)=\widetilde{f}_{J-S_{d-1,p}}&\quad J=S_{d-1,p}+1,\ldots,N_{d,p}.\end{cases}

(5.17)

We observe that the second and third set of conditions in (
5.17
) determine the coefficients
a_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}
with
k_{1}\in\{0,1\}
of the monomial expansion (
5.14
) of an element
b_{J}
.
In the next proposition we show that the elements
b_{J}
are well-defined and that they constitute a basis of
{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)
.

Proposition 5.4.1
.

For any
p\in\mathbb{N}
and
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, the set
\mathcal{B}^{p}_{T}
constitutes a basis for the space
{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)
.

Proof.

We need to verify that the elements
b_{J}
are well defined and that
\mathcal{B}_{T}^{p}
is a set of linearly independent generators.
The algorithms described in Section
5.5
show that the coefficients
a_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}
of an element
b_{J}
are uniquely determined once the coefficients
a_{{\boldsymbol{k}}}
with
k_{1}\in\{0,1\}
are assigned.
This implies that the
N_{d,p}
polynomials
b_{J}
are well and uniquely defined, since the second and third set of conditions in (
5.17
) fix the coefficients
a_{0,k_{2},\dots,k_{d}}
and
a_{1,k_{2},\dots,k_{d}}
.
We also notice that, since (
5.4
) holds for any
v\in{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)
, we can apply the algorithms described in Section
5.5
to construct
v
.
From this we deduce that each
v\in{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)
is uniquely determined by the coefficients
a_{0,k_{2},\dots,k_{d}}
and
a_{1,k_{2},\dots,k_{d}}
and hence by its restriction
v(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot)
and the restriction of its derivative
\partial_{x_{1}}v(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot)
.

For any
v\in{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)
, since its restriction
v\left(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot\right)
is a polynomial of degree
p
and the restriction of its derivative
\partial_{x_{1}}v(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot)
is a polynomial of degree
p-1
, there exist some coefficients
\{\lambda_{J}\}_{J=1}^{N_{p,d}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle v(\left({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{J=1}^{S_{d-1,p}}\lambda_{J}\widehat{f}_{J}=\sum_{J=1}^{S_{d-1,p}}\lambda_{J}b_{J}(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot),

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{1}}v(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot)

\displaystyle=\sum_{J=S_{d-1,p}+1}^{N_{d,p}}\lambda_{J}\widetilde{f}_{J-S_{d-1,p}}=\sum_{J=S_{d-1,p}+1}^{N_{d,p}}\lambda_{J}\partial_{x_{1}}b_{J}(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot).

Hence,
v=\sum_{J=1}^{N_{d,p}}\lambda_{J}b_{J}
is a linear combination of
b_{J}
.

The fact that the polynomials
\{b_{J}\}_{J=1,\dots,N_{d,p}}
are linearly independent is due to their restrictions to
x_{1}=({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1}
. Assume that
\sum_{J=1}^{N_{d,p}}c_{J}b_{J}=0
for some coefficients
\{c_{J}\}_{J=1}^{N_{d,p}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
.
Then, restricting to
x_{1}=({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{J=1}^{S_{d-1,p}}c_{J}b_{J}(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot)=

\displaystyle\sum_{J=1}^{S_{d-1,p}}c_{J}\widehat{f}_{J}=0,

\displaystyle\sum_{J=S_{d-1,p}+1}^{N_{d,p}}c_{J}\partial_{x_{1}}b_{J}(({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{1},\cdot)=

\displaystyle\sum_{J=S_{d-1,p}+1}^{N_{d,p}}c_{J}\widetilde{f}_{J-S_{d-1,p}}=0.

This implies that
c_{J}=0
for
J=1,\dots,N_{d,p}
, since
\left\{\widehat{f}_{s}\right\}_{s=1,\dots,S_{d-1,p}}
and
\left\{\widetilde{f}_{s}\right\}_{s=1,\dots,S_{d-1,p-1}}
are linearly independent.
∎

A consequence of Proposition
5.4.1
is that the conditions in the definition of
{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)
are linearly independent:

\dim\big(\mathbb{P}^{p}_{d}(T)\big)-\#\{{\boldsymbol{i}}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\mid|{\boldsymbol{i}}|\leq p-2\}=\begin{pmatrix}p+d\\
d\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}p+d-2\\
d\end{pmatrix}=N_{d,p}=\dim\big({\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)\big).

In particular, we have

\dim\big({\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)\big)=N_{d,p}=\begin{cases}2&d=1\\
2p+1&d=2\\
(p+1)^{2}&d=3\end{cases}\quad={\mathcal{O}}_{p\to\infty}(p^{d-1}).

Notice that, in the one dimensional case, if we vary the polynomial degree
p
, then the dimension of the quasi-Trefftz space remains the same but the space changes.

By comparing the dimension of
{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)
with the dimension of
\mathbb{P}^{p}_{d}(T)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\dim\big({\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)\big)=

\displaystyle\frac{(p+d-2)!(2p+d-1)}{(d-1)!p!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{p\to\infty}(p^{d-1})\ll\dim(\mathbb{P}^{p}(T))=\binom{p+d}{d}={\mathcal{O}}_{p\to\infty}(p^{d}).

We highlight that for large polynomial degrees
p
, the dimension of the quasi-Trefftz space is much smaller than the dimension of the full polynomial space of the same degree.

We have shown in Theorem
5.2.1
that smooth solutions of the diffusion-advection-reaction equation with variable coefficients are approximated in
{\mathbb{Q\!T}}^{p}(T)
and in
\mathbb{P}_{d}^{p}(T)
with the same convergence rates with respect to the meshsize
h
.
This is the main advantage of Trefftz and quasi-Trefftz schemes: offering the same level of accuracy for much fewer degrees of freedom.

In this chapter we present some numerical tests in two dimensions.
We begin with a simple example with polynomial solution in Section
6.1

we consider two test cases with smooth solutions in order to validate the

Moreover, in Section

Finally, in Section

we consider advection-dominated and reaction-dominated examples to show the behaviour of the quasi-Trefftz DG method also in these cases.

The DG scheme (
3.8
) with as discrete space the global quasi-Trefftz space (
5.9
) for the diffusion-advection-reaction equation has been implemented in MATLAB.
We remark that we consider the homogeneous diffusion-advection-reaction equation, i.e., with
f=0
.
In all the experiments we use the SIPG method, i.e., the symmetrization parameter
\epsilon=-1
, except in the example of Section

where the three formulations are compared.

All the experiments are performed on the unit square
\Omega=(0,1)^{2}
, which is partitioned using conforming meshes of triangular elements generated by
DistMesh, see
[
29
]
, and satisfying the assumptions of Section
2.1
.
For all the experiments we will say, with an abuse of notation, the meshsize
h
used, though this is just the input of the mesh generator and usually the meshsize as defined in
2.1.4
is a little larger than this input.
We calculate the “correct”
h
and we use this value in the plots of the
h
-convergence in Section
6.1
and in Section
6.2
.

We compute all the volume integrals in the assembly of the matrix and of the right-hand side using the following quadrature rule.
First, we use the Gauss–Legendre quadrature rule on the interval
[0,1]
, then we consider the tensor product of the nodes in
[0,1]^{2}
and then with the Duffy transformation
[
1
, p. 21]
we map the points into the reference triangle, whose vertices are
(0,0)
,
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
.
Finally, the quadrature points are mapped onto the generic triangle
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
using the classical affine map, the reference element onto
T
.
For computing the integrals over edges we simply use the Gauss–Legendre quadrature rule combined with this affine map.
Since the Gauss–Legendre quadrature rule with
n

equal to
2n-1
, we use
p+1
points in
[0,1]
, hence
(p+1)^{2}
on each triangles, where
p
is the polynomial degree used.
The linear system is solved using the backslash command.

To construct the quasi-Trefftz basis functions, we use the symbolic computations only once to calculate the derivative of the PDE coefficients that we need for Algorithm
2
and to evaluate them in the barycentre of each element. Their values on the barycentres are stored and passed in input to the quasi-Trefftz algorithm.
To initialize Algorithm
2
, we choose scaled monomials as polynomial bases:

\displaystyle\left\{\widehat{f}_{s}(x_{2})=\bigg(\frac{x_{2}-({{\mathbf{x}}}_{T})_{2}}{h_{T}}\bigg)^{s-1}\right\}_{s=1,\dots,p+1}

6.4
Advection-dominated and reaction-dominated problems

In this section we consider advection-dominated and reaction-dominated problems in order to illustrate the approximate solutions obtained with the quasi-Trefftz DG method in these particular cases.
We recall that the exact solution of this kind of problems can present boundary and internal layers, which are narrow regions where the solution and its gradient change rapidly.
For this reason the numerical treatment of such problems can be difficult. It is known that, if we use the standard Finite Element Method, the approximate solution, in general, presents spurious oscillations and can be completely different from the exact one.

First, we consider the problem (
1.6
)-(
1.8
) with
f=0

Chapter 7
Conclusions and future developments

We have introduced a quasi-Trefftz discontinuous Galerkin method for approximating the homogeneous diffusion-advection-reaction equation with varying coefficients.
The DG variational formulation, its well-posedness and the quasi-optimality in a mesh-dependent norm have been analysed.
We have presented the polynomial quasi-Trefftz space for a general homogeneous linear partial differential equation with sufficiently smooth coefficients and proved that it reaches the same approximation orders with respect to the mesh size
as the full polynomial space but with fewer degrees of freedom.
For the homogeneous diffusion-advection-reaction equation, we have proved optimal
h
-convergence of the quasi-Trefftz DG scheme
and described an algorithm for the construction of the quasi-Trefftz basis functions in dimension
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
d\geq 1
.
We have presented some numerical experiments in two dimensions that validate the accuracy of the method.

We now consider some possible improvements of the method and future research directions.

To better assess the computational performance of the method, the method should be implemented
in the three-dimensional case and in a high-performance open-source software such as NGSolve, where the Trefftz method and quasi-Trefftz method for the wave equation have already been implemented.

The algorithm for the construction of the quasi-Trefftz basis functions is initialized assigning two polynomial bases. Those conditions could be optimized, through a more accurate study of the properties of the basis functions, in order to improve conditioning, computing time and accuracy.

The construction of non-polynomials quasi-Trefftz functions might also be analysed; they could be useful, for example, to efficiently approximate solutions that present boundary layers, such as in the case of advection-dominated or reaction-dominated problems.

We have proved optimal convergence rate with respect to the mesh size (
h
-convergence) and a challenging extension is the analysis of the approximation properties for increasing polynomial degrees (
p
-convergence).
This is a difficult task since in the context of Trefftz schemes has been achieved for time-harmonic equations (Helmholtz
[
17
]
and Maxwell
[
18
]
) but not yet for the wave equation and neither in the context of the quasi-Trefftz methods.

The analysis of the DG scheme considered includes the source term while the quasi-Trefftz space is designed for the homogenous equation.
A significant improvement of the method is the extension to the inhomogeneous advection-diffusion-reaction equation.
We need to construct a particular approximate local solution, considering a variant of the algorithm presented, and then apply the quasi-Trefftz DG method in an adequate way to solve for the difference.

Another interesting extension is the combination of the method presented and the quasi-Trefftz DG method already implemented for the wave equation with piecewise-smooth coefficients
[
23
]
, in order to approximate PDEs whose nature changes in the domain, such as the Euler-Tricomi equation (
\partial^{2}_{x}u+x\partial^{2}_{y}u=0
), useful in the study of transonic flow.
Other similar equations are used in plasma physics, for example,
-\Delta u+\sigma u=0
, with
\sigma
that changes sign,
which is a Helmholtz equation in a part of the domain and a diffusion-reaction equation in the other
[
25
]
.

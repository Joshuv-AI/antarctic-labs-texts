Laws of the iterated and single logarithm for sums of independent indicators, with applications to the Ginibre point process and Karlin’s occupancy scheme
Dariusz Buraczewski Alexander Iksanov, Valeriya Kotelnikova

Abstract
We prove a law of the iterated logarithm (LIL) for an infinite sum of independent indicators parameterized by t t and monotone in t t as t → ∞ t\to\infty . It is shown that if the expectation b b and the variance a a of the sum are comparable, then the normalization in the LIL includes the iterated logarithm of a a . If the expectation grows faster than the variance, while the ratio log ⁡ b / log ⁡ a \log b/\log a remains bounded, then the normalization in the LIL includes the single logarithm of a a (so that the LIL becomes a law of the single logarithm). Applications of our result are given to the number of points of the infinite Ginibre point process in a disk and the number of occupied boxes and related quantities in Karlin’s occupancy scheme.



Key words: Ginibre point process; independent indicators; infinite occupancy; law of the iterated logarithm

The main purpose of the article is to prove a law of the iterated logarithm (LIL) for an infinite sum of independent indicators. Although this problem is interesting on its own, the present work has originally been motivated by an application of the aforementioned LIL to the number of points of the infinite Ginibre point process in a disk and the number of occupied boxes and related objects in Karlin’s occupancy scheme.

Let

and

See
[
2
]
for detailed information on determinantal point processes and particularly Sections 4.3.7 and 4.7 for a discussion of the Ginibre point processes.

An application to Karlin’s occupancy scheme

Let
(p_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a discrete probability distribution with
p_{k}>0
for infinitely many
k
. We are interested in an infinite balls-in-boxes scheme which is defined as follows. There are infinitely many balls and an infinite array of boxes
1
,
2,\ldots
. The balls are allocated, independently of each other, over the boxes with probability
p_{k}
of hitting box
k
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
. The scheme is commonly referred to as Karlin’s occupancy scheme due to Karlin’s article
[
17
]
, which offered the first systematic investigation of the scheme. A survey of many results available for the scheme up to 2007 can be found in
[
13
]
.

In a
deterministic version
of Karlin’s occupancy scheme the
n
th ball is thrown at time
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For
j,n\in\mathbb{N}
, denote by
\mathcal{K}_{j}(n)
and
\mathcal{K}_{j}^{\ast}(n)
the number of boxes containing at time
n
at least
j
balls and exactly
j
balls, respectively. In particular,
\mathcal{K}_{1}(n)
denotes the number of occupied boxes at time
n
.

Denote by
(S_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
a standard random walk with independent increments having an exponential distribution of unit mean. Put
\pi(t):=\#\{k\in\mathbb{N}:S_{k}\leq t\}
for
t\geq 0
, so that
\pi:=(\pi(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a Poisson process on
[0,\infty)
of unit intensity. It is assumed that
\pi
is independent of random variables which represent the indices of boxes hit by the balls
1
,
2,\ldots

In a
Poissonized version
of Karlin’s occupancy scheme the
n
th ball is thrown at time
S_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, so that there are
\pi(t)
balls at time
t\geq 0
. For
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\geq 0
, denote by
K_{j}(t)
and
K_{j}^{*}(t)
the number of boxes at time
t
in the Poissonized scheme containing at least
j
balls and exactly
j
balls, respectively.
In particular,
K_{1}(t)
denotes the number of occupied boxes at time
t
. For
i\in\mathbb{N}
, denote by
Y_{i}
the index of a box that the
i
th ball falls into. Also, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\geq 0
, denote by
\pi_{k}(t)
the number of balls which fall into the box
k
in the Poissonized scheme at time
t
. Then
\pi_{k}(t)=\#\{i\leq\pi(t):Y_{i}=k\}
and
\pi(t)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\pi_{k}(t)
for all
t\geq 0
. The reason behind introducing the
Poissonized version
is the thinning property of Poisson processes. Since the variables
Y_{1}
,
Y_{2},\ldots
are independent, and also independent of
\pi
, the property ensures that the processes
(\pi_{1}(t))_{t\geq 0}
,
(\pi_{2}(t))_{t\geq 0},\ldots
are independent, and
(\pi_{k}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a Poisson process of intensity
p_{k}
. Thus, the numbers of balls which hit distinct boxes are independent, and
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, the variable
K_{j}(t)
is an infinite sum of independent indicators. This is in contrast with the deterministic version, in which the aforementioned independence is absent. This justifies a common approach which is exploited when analyzing the deterministic version. First, the scheme is Poissonized. Then the result in focus, or rather its counterpart, is proved for the Poissonized scheme. Finally, a transition, called de-Poissonization, is made from the Poissonized to the deterministic scheme. As far as the quantities introduced above are concerned, a relative simplicity of the approach is secured by the equalities
K_{j}(t)=\mathcal{K}_{j}(\pi(t))
for
t\geq 0
a.s. and
K_{j}(S_{n})=\mathcal{K}_{j}(n)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
a.s. The latter equality is not as useful as the former, for the process
(K_{j}(t))_{t\geq 0}
and the variable
S_{n}
are dependent.

Put

\rho(t):=\#\{k\in\mathbb{N}:1/p_{k}\leq t\},\quad t>0,

so that
\rho
is the counting function of the sequence
(1/p_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. Observe that
\rho(t)=0
for
t\in(0,1]
. Typically, assumptions on the distribution
(p_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are formulated in terms of
\rho
rather than the distribution itself. Regular variation of
\rho
at
\infty

To be more precise, Theorem

]

[

t^{-1}\int_{0}^{1}y^{-2}{\rm e}^{-1/y}\rho(ty){\rm d}y\leq t^{-1}\rho(t)\int_{0}^{1}y^{-2}{\rm e}^{-1/y}{\rm d}y~\sim~\int_{0}^{1}y^{-2}{\rm e}^{-1/y}{\rm d}y\,L(t)=o(\hat{L}(t)).

Here, the first inequality is implied by monotonicity of
\rho
and the last asymptotic relation is secured by (
63
). The latter is justified as follows

t^{-1}\int_{1}^{\infty}y^{-2}(1-{\rm e}^{-1/y})\rho(ty){\rm d}y\leq t^{-1}\int_{1}^{\infty}y^{-3}\rho(ty){\rm d}y=t\int_{t}^{\infty}y^{-3}\rho(y){\rm d}y\sim L(t)=o(\hat{L}(t)).

The asymptotic equivalence is ensured by Proposition 1.5.10 in
[
4
]
and formula (
63
) entails the last asymptotic relation.
∎

Lemma 7.4
.

For any function
\ell
slowly varying at
\infty
there exists a positive function
c
satisfying

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ell(c(t))}{\ell(t)}=1\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{c(t)}{t}=\infty.

Proof.

According to the representation theorem for slowly varying function (Theorem 1.3.1 in
[
4
]
),

\ell(t)=A(t)\exp\Big(\int_{a}^{t}u^{-1}\varepsilon(u){\rm d}u\Big),

where
\lim_{t\to\infty}A(t)=A\in(0,\infty)
,
a>0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}\varepsilon(t)=0
. Therefore,
the first limit relation in the statement of the lemma is equivalent to

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{t}^{c(t)}u^{-1}\varepsilon(u){\rm d}u=0.

In view of

\Big|\int_{t}^{c(t)}u^{-1}\varepsilon(u){\rm d}u\Big|\leq\int_{t}^{c(t)}u^{-1}|\varepsilon(u)|{\rm d}u\leq\sup_{u\geq t}|\varepsilon(u)|\log(t^{-1}c(t)),

one may take
c(t)=t\exp\big((\sup_{u\geq t}|\varepsilon(u)|)^{-1/2}\big)
. Since
\lim_{t\to\infty}\sup_{u\geq t}|\varepsilon(u)|=0
, both claimed limit relations hold true.
∎

Recall that we denoted by
\Pi_{\ell,\,\infty}
the subclass of the de Haan class
\Pi
with the auxiliary function
\ell
satisfying
\lim_{t\to\infty}\ell(t)=\infty
. Also, we recall that
K_{j}(t)
and
K_{j}^{\ast}(t)
denote the number of boxes at time
t
in the Poissonized Karlin scheme containing at least
j
balls and exactly
j
balls, respectively. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\geq 0
, denote by
\pi_{k}(t)
the number of balls in box
k
at time
t
. By the thinning property of Poisson processes, the processes
(\pi_{1}(t))_{t\geq 0}
,
(\pi_{2}(t))_{t\geq 0},\ldots
are independent and, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
(\pi_{k}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a Poisson process with intensity
p_{k}
. Now we are ready to exhibit representations of
K_{j}(t)
and
K_{j}^{\ast}(t)
as the infinite sums of independent indicators

K_{j}(t)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\1_{\{\pi_{k}(t)\geq j\}}\quad\text{and}\quad K^{\ast}_{j}(t)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\1_{\{\pi_{k}(t)=j\}},\quad j\geq 1,~t\geq 0.

(64)

7.2
Asymptotic behavior of
\mathbb{E}K_{j}(t)
and
{\rm Var}\,K_{j}(t)

Passing to the proofs of Theorems
4.1
,
4.3
and
4.4
we first show that the asymptotics of
\mathbb{E}K_{j}(t)
and
{\rm Var}\,K_{j}(t)
are as stated in these theorems. In addition, we find the asymptotics of
\mathbb{E}K_{j}^{\ast}(t)
.

Lemma 7.5
.

Assume that
\rho\in\Pi_{\ell,\,\infty}
. Then, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{E}K_{j}(t)~\sim~\rho(t),\quad t\to\infty,

(65)

{\rm Var}\,K_{j}(t)~\sim~\Big(\log 2-\sum_{k=1}^{j-1}\frac{(2k-1)!}{(k!)^{2}2^{2k}}\Big)\ell(t),\quad t\to\infty

(66)

and

\mathbb{E}K^{\ast}_{j}(t)~\sim~\frac{\ell(t)}{j},\quad t\to\infty.

(67)

Assume that
\rho(t)\sim t^{\alpha}L(t)
as
t\to\infty
for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
and some
L
slowly varying at
\infty
. If
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
or
\alpha=1
and
j\geq 2
, then, as
t\to\infty
,

\mathbb{E}K_{j}(t)~\sim~\frac{\Gamma(j-\alpha)}{(j-1)!}\rho(t),

(68)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{{\rm Var}\,K_{j}(t)}{\rho(t)}=\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{\Gamma(i+j-\alpha)}{i!(j-1)!2^{i+j-1-\alpha}}-\frac{\Gamma(j-\alpha)}{(j-1)!}\Big)>0

(69)

and

\mathbb{E}K_{j}^{\ast}(t)~\sim~\frac{\alpha\Gamma(j-\alpha)}{j!}\rho(t),\quad t\to\infty.

(70)

If
\alpha=1
, then

{\rm Var}\,K_{1}(t)~\sim~\mathbb{E}K_{1}(t)~\sim~\mathbb{E}K^{*}_{1}(t)~\sim~t\hat{L}(t),\quad t\to\infty.

(71)

Proof.

Assume that
\rho\in\Pi_{\ell,\,\infty}
. By Theorem 1 in
[
17
]
,
\mathbb{E}K_{1}(t)\sim\rho(t)
as
t\to\infty
. By Proposition 2.5 in
[
16
]
, (
67
) holds. Relation (
11
) entails
\lim_{t\to\infty}(\rho(t)/\ell(t))=\infty
. Hence, for
j\geq 2
,
\mathbb{E}K_{j}(t)=\mathbb{E}K_{1}(t)-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\mathbb{E}K_{i}^{\ast}(t)\sim\rho(t)
as
t\to\infty
, that is, (
65
) holds true. Formula (
66
) can be found in Corollary 2.8 of
[
16
]
.

By formula (23) in
[
17
]
, (
70
) holds whenever
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
or
\alpha=1
and
j\geq 2
. Assume that
\alpha\in(0,1)
. By Theorem 1 in
[
17
]
,
\mathbb{E}K_{1}(t)\sim\Gamma(1-\alpha)t^{\alpha}L(t)
as
t\to\infty
. Noting that
\mathbb{E}K_{j+1}(t)=\mathbb{E}K_{j}(t)-\mathbb{E}K_{j}^{\ast}(t)
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\geq 0
, relation (
68
) follows with the help of mathematical induction.

Assume that
\alpha=1
and
j\geq 2
. The previous argument does not apply and we have to resort to direct calculations. Write with the help of the first equality in (
64
), for
t\geq 0
,

\mathbb{E}K_{j}(t)=\mathbb{E}\sum_{k\geq 1}\1_{\{\pi_{k}(t)\geq j\}}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\Big(1-{\rm e}^{-p_{k}t}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{(p_{k}t)^{i}}{i!}\Big)=\int_{(1,\,\infty)}\Big(1-{\rm e}^{-t/x}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{(t/x)^{i}}{i!}\Big){\rm d}\rho(x).

Using the inequality

1-{\rm e}^{-x}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{x^{i}}{i!}\leq\frac{x^{j}}{j!},\quad x\geq 0

(72)

and (
62
) we infer

\limsup_{x\to\infty}\Big(1-{\rm e}^{-t/x}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{(t/x)^{i}}{i!}\Big)\rho(x)\leq\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{(t/x)^{j}}{j!}\rho(x)=0.

(73)

Hence, integrating by parts and changing the variable
t/x=y
we obtain

\mathbb{E}K_{j}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{-y}\frac{y^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}\rho(t/y){\rm d}y,\quad t\geq 0.

(74)

According to Potter’s bound (Theorem 1.5.6 in
[
4
]
), given
\delta\in(0,1)
there exists
x_{0}>1
such that, for
y\leq t/x_{0}
and
t\geq x_{0}
,

\frac{\rho(t/y)}{\rho(t)}\leq 2\max(y^{-1-\delta},\,y^{-1+\delta}).

With this at hand, write, for
t\geq x_{0}
,

\frac{\mathbb{E}K_{j}(t)}{\rho(t)}=\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{-y}\frac{y^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}\frac{\rho(t/y)}{\rho(t)}{\rm d}y=\int_{0}^{t/x_{0}}\ldots+\int_{t/x_{0}}^{t}\ldots=:A(t)+B(t).

By monotonicity of
\rho
,

B(t)\leq\frac{\rho(x_{0})}{\rho(t)}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-y}\frac{y^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}{\rm d}y=\frac{\rho(x_{0})}{\rho(t)}~\to~0,\quad t\to\infty.

Observe that

\1_{[0,\,t/x_{0}]}(y){\rm e}^{-y}y^{j-1}\frac{\rho(t/y)}{\rho(t)}\leq{\rm e}^{-y}y^{j-1}\max(y^{-1-\delta},\,y^{-1+\delta}),\quad y>0

and that the function on the right-hand side is integrable on
(0,\infty)
. Hence, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem,

\lim_{t\to\infty}A(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lim_{t\to\infty}\1_{[0,\,t/x_{0}]}(y){\rm e}^{-y}\frac{y^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}\frac{\rho(t/y)}{\rho(t)}{\rm d}y=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-y}\frac{y^{j-2}}{(j-1)!}{\rm d}y=\frac{1}{j-1}.

This proves (
68
) in the case
\alpha=1
and
j\geq 2
.

Assume that
\alpha=1
. By Theorem 1 and formulae (23) and (26) in
[
17
]
,

{\rm Var}\,K_{1}(t)~\sim~\mathbb{E}K_{1}(t)~\sim~\mathbb{E}K_{1}^{*}(t)~\sim~\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{-2}{\rm e}^{-1/y}\rho(ty){\rm d}y,\quad t\to\infty.

This in combination with Lemma
7.3
entails (
71
).

Assume that
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
or
\alpha=1
and
j\geq 2
. Arguing along the lines of the first part of the proof one can show that

{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(t)={\rm Var}\,\sum_{k\geq 1}\1_{\{\pi_{k}(t)\geq j\}}=\sum_{k\geq 1}{\rm e}^{-p_{k}t}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{(p_{k}t)^{i}}{i!}\Big(1-{\rm e}^{-p_{k}t}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{(p_{k}t)^{i}}{i!}\Big)\\
=2\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{-2y}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{y^{i+j-1}}{i!(j-1)!}\rho(t/y){\rm d}y-\int_{0}^{t}{\rm e}^{-y}\frac{y^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}\rho(t/y){\rm d}y.

(75)

Changing in the first integral the variable
z=2y
and invoking equality (
74
) we obtain

{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\mathbb{E}K_{i+j}(2t)\frac{\binom{i+j-1}{i}}{2^{i+j-1}}-\mathbb{E}K_{j}(t).

This in combination with the already proved relation (
68
) and the fact that
\rho
is regularly varying of index
\alpha
yields

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(t)}{\rho(t)}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\Big(\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\mathbb{E}K_{i+j}(2t)\frac{\binom{i+j-1}{i}}{2^{i+j-1}}}{\rho(2t)}\frac{\rho(2t)}{\rho(t)}-\frac{\mathbb{E}K_{j}(t)}{\rho(t)}\Big)\\
=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{\Gamma(i+j-\alpha)}{i!(j-1)!2^{i+j-1-\alpha}}-\frac{\Gamma(j-\alpha)}{(j-1)!}:=c_{j}.

(76)

Plainly, the constants
c_{j}
are nonnegative. Now we intend to show that
c_{j}>0
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
if
\alpha\in(0,1)
and for each
j\geq 2
if
\alpha=1
. In the latter case the constants
c_{j}
take a simpler form

c_{j}=\frac{(2j-3)!}{((j-1)!)^{2}2^{2j-3}},\quad j\geq 2,

which immediately proves positivity. Indeed, according to Lemma 2.3 in
[
16
]
, with
a=b=1
and
l=j-1
,

\sum_{i=0}^{j-2}\frac{\binom{i+j-2}{j-2}}{2^{i+j-2}}=1,\quad j\geq 2,

whence

\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{\Gamma(i+j-1)}{i!(j-1)!2^{i+j-2}}-\frac{\Gamma(j-1)}{(j-1)!}=\frac{1}{j-1}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{j-2}\frac{\binom{i+j-2}{j-2}}{2^{i+j-2}}+\frac{\binom{2j-3}{j-2}}{2^{2j-3}}-1\Big)=\frac{\binom{2j-3}{j-2}}{(j-1)2^{2j-3}}.

Assume now that
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Recalling a series representation of
{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(t)
(see (
75
)) and using

{\rm e}^{-x}\frac{x^{j}}{j!}\leq 1-{\rm e}^{-x}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{x^{i}}{i!},\quad x\geq 0,

we infer

{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(t)\geq\sum_{k\geq 1}{\rm e}^{-2p_{k}t}\frac{(p_{k}t)^{j}}{j!}=2^{-j}\mathbb{E}K^{\ast}_{j}(2t),\quad t\geq 0,\,j\in\mathbb{N}.

Here, the equality follows from the second equality in (
64
).
With this at hand, invoking now (
70
) and (
76
) we obtain

c_{j}\geq 2^{\alpha-j}\frac{\alpha\Gamma(j-\alpha)}{j!}>0,\quad j\in\mathbb{N}.

∎

(77)

(78)

Remark 7.7
.

If
\varsigma_{1}=1
, the existence of
c

. If
\varsigma_{1}<1

Proof.

Assume first that
\rho
is regularly varying of index
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
or
\alpha=1
and
j\geq 2
. In view of (
69
) it is enough to show that

{\rm Var}\,\Big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\1_{\{1/p_{k}>d(t)\}}\1_{\{\pi_{k}(t)\geq j\}}\Big)=o(\rho(t)),\quad t\to\infty

(79)

and

{\rm Var}\,\Big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\1_{\{1/p_{k}\leq c(t)\}}\1_{\{\pi_{k}(t)\geq j\}}\Big)=o(\rho(t)),\quad t\to\infty.

(80)

Recalling the first equality in (
64
) we start by writing

{\rm Var}\,\Big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\1_{\{1/p_{k}>d(t)\}}\1_{\{\pi_{k}(t)\geq j\}}\Big)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\1_{\{1/p_{k}>d(t)\}}{\rm e}^{-p_{k}t}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{(p_{k}t)^{i}}{i!}\Big(1-{\rm e}^{-p_{k}t}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{(p_{k}t)^{i}}{i!}\Big)\\

{\rm Var}\,\Big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\1_{\{1/p_{k}>d(t)\}}\1_{\{\pi_{k}(t)\geq j\}}\Big)=-{\rm e}^{-t/d(t)}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{(t/d(t))^{i}}{i!}\Big(1-{\rm e}^{-t/d(t)}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{(t/d(t))^{i}}{i!}\Big)\rho(d(t))\\
+\int_{0}^{t/d(t)}\rho(t/y){\rm e}^{-y}\frac{y^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}\Big(2{\rm e}^{-y}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{y^{i}}{i!}-1\Big){\rm d}y=:-A_{1}(t)+A_{2}(t).

(81)

In view of (
72
),

0\leq\frac{A_{1}(t)}{\rho(t)}\leq\Big(\frac{t}{d(t)}\Big)^{j}\frac{\rho(d(t))}{\rho(t)}~\sim~\Big(\frac{L(d(t))}{(d(t))^{j-\alpha}}\Big)/\Big(\frac{L(t)}{t^{j-\alpha}}\Big),\quad t\to\infty.

By Lemma
7.1
(a), the right-hand side converges to

as

because
j-\alpha>0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-1}d(t)=\infty
.

Now we intend to show that
\lim_{t\to\infty}(A_{2}(t)/\rho(t))=0
. As a preparation, observe that, for each
y>0
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\1_{(0,\,t/d(t))}(y)\frac{\rho(t/y)}{\rho(t)}{\rm e}^{-y}\frac{y^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}\Big(2{\rm e}^{-y}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{y^{i}}{i!}-1\Big)=0.

It remains to find an integrable majorant for the function under the limit. Let
y^{*}
be the unique root of the equation
2{\rm e}^{-y}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{y^{i}}{i!}=1
. According to Potter’s bound (Theorem 1.5.6 in
[
4
]
), given
\delta>0

x_{0}>1

) we obtain

{\rm Var\,}\sum_{k\geq 1}\1_{\{1/p_{k}\leq c(t)\}}\1_{\{\pi_{k}(t)\geq j\}}\\
={\rm e}^{-t/c(t)}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{(t/c(t))^{i}}{i!}\Big(1-{\rm e}^{-t/c(t)}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{(t/c(t))^{i}}{i!}\Big)\rho(c(t))\\

whence

as

\rho
once again yields, for large

0\leq B_{2}(t)\leq\rho(c(t))\int_{t/c(t)}^{t}{\rm e}^{-y}\frac{y^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}\Big(1-2{\rm e}^{-y}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{y^{i}}{i!}\Big){\rm d}y=o(\rho(c(t)))=o(\rho(t)),\quad t\to\infty,

and (
80
) follows.

Consider now the case
\alpha=1
and

. Since

and particularly

, a contradiction. The claim about

.

{\rm Var}\,\Big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\1_{\{1/p_{k}>c(t)\}}\1_{\{\pi_{k}(t)\geq 1\}}\Big)~\sim~\varsigma_{1}t\hat{L}(t),\quad t\to\infty.

We shall prove that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathbb{P}(A_{k}(j,n))(1-\mathbb{P}(A_{k}(j,n)))-{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)}{{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)}=0

(95)

and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{i\neq k}(\mathbb{P}(A_{i}(j,n)\cap A_{k}(j,n))-\mathbb{P}(A_{i}(j,n))\mathbb{P}(A_{k}(j,n)))}{{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)}=0.

(96)

Proof of (
95
)
. Note that

\mathbb{P}(A_{k}(j,n))=1-\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\binom{n}{i}p_{k}^{i}(1-p_{k})^{n-i}

and

{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)=\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}{\rm e}^{-p_{k}n}\frac{(p_{k}n)^{i}}{i!}\Big(1-\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}{\rm e}^{-p_{k}n}\frac{(p_{k}n)^{i}}{i!}\Big).

Hence, the numerator in (
95
) is equal to

\sum_{k\geq 1}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\Big(\binom{n}{i}p_{k}^{i}(1-p_{k})^{n-i}-{\rm e}^{-p_{k}n}\frac{(p_{k}n)^{i}}{i!}\Big)\\
-\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\sum_{m=0}^{j-1}\Big(\binom{n}{i}\binom{n}{m}p_{k}^{i+m}(1-p_{k})^{2n-i-m}-{\rm e}^{-2p_{k}n}\frac{(p_{k}n)^{i+m}}{i!m!}\Big)\Big).

The penultimate inequality in the proof of Lemma 2.13 in
[
16
]
states that, for large enough
n
and all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
i\leq n
,

-B_{i}p_{k}\leq\binom{n}{i}p_{k}^{i}(1-p_{k})^{n-i}-{\rm e}^{-p_{k}n}\frac{(p_{k}n)^{i}}{i!}\leq A_{i}p_{k}

(97)

for some positive constants
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
. Therefore,

\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\Big|\binom{n}{i}p_{k}^{i}(1-p_{k})^{n-i}-{\rm e}^{-p_{k}n}\frac{(p_{k}n)^{i}}{i!}\Big|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\max(A_{i},B_{i})=o({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)),\quad n\to\infty

because
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)=\infty
. Further, write

{\rm e}^{-2p_{k}n}\frac{(p_{k}n)^{i+m}}{i!m!}-\binom{n}{i}\binom{n}{m}p_{k}^{i+m}(1-p_{k})^{2n-i-m}\\
=\binom{i+m}{i}2^{-(i+m)}\Big({\rm e}^{-2p_{k}n}\frac{(2p_{k}n)^{i+m}}{(i+m)!}-2^{i+m}\frac{(n!)^{2}}{(n-i)!(n-m)!(i+m)!}p_{k}^{i+m}(1-p_{k})^{2n-i-m}\Big)\\
=\binom{i+m}{i}2^{-(i+m)}\Big({\rm e}^{-2p_{k}n}\frac{(2p_{k}n)^{i+m}}{(i+m)!}-\binom{2n}{i+m}p_{k}^{i+m}(1-p_{k})^{2n-i-m}\Big)\\
+\binom{i+m}{i}2^{-(i+m)}\Big(\binom{2n}{i+m}-2^{i+m}\frac{(n!)^{2}}{(n-i)!(n-m)!(i+m)!}\Big)p_{k}^{i+m}(1-p_{k})^{2n-i-m}\\
=:D_{i,m,k}(n)+E_{i,m,k}(n).

By (
97
),

\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\sum_{m=0}^{j-1}|D_{i,m,k}(n)|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\sum_{m=0}^{j-1}\binom{i+m}{i}2^{-(i+m)}\max(A_{i+m},B_{i+m})=o({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)),\quad n\to\infty.

Observe now that
E_{0,0,k}(n)=E_{0,1,k}(n)=E_{1,0,k}(n)=0
. For
i
and
m
such that
i+m\geq 2
,
\binom{2n}{i+m}-2^{i+m}\frac{(n!)^{2}}{(n-i)!(n-m)!(i+m)!}
is a polynomial of order at most
i+m
. Since the coefficient in front of
n^{i+m}
is
0
, we infer

\Big|\binom{2n}{i+m}-2^{i+m}\frac{(n!)^{2}}{(n-i)!(n-m)!(i+m)!}\Big|\leq C_{i,m}n^{i+m-1}

for a positive constant
C_{i,m}
. This in combination with Lemma
7.9
(put there
l=i+m
and replace
n
by
2n-i-m
) and (
94
) yields, for
i,m\leq j-1
such that
i+m\geq 2
,

\sum_{k\geq 1}|E_{i,m,k}(n)|\leq C_{i,m}\binom{i+m}{i}2^{-(i+m)}\sum_{k\geq 1}n^{i+m-1}p_{k}^{i+m}(1-p_{k})^{2n-i-m}~\sim~{\rm const}\frac{{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)}{n}\to 0

as
n\to\infty
. Hence,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\sum_{m=0}^{j-1}|E_{i,m,k}(n)|=0
. The proof of (
95
) is complete.

Proof of (
96
)
. We start by writing

\mathbb{P}(A_{i}(j,n)\cap A_{k}(j,n))-\mathbb{P}(A_{i}(j,n))\mathbb{P}(A_{k}(j,n))\\
=\mathbb{P}((A_{i}(j,n))^{c}\cap(A_{k}(j,n))^{c})-\mathbb{P}((A_{i}(j,n))^{c})\mathbb{P}((A_{k}(j,n))^{c})\\
=\sum_{a=0}^{j-1}\sum_{b=0}^{j-1}\Big(\binom{n}{a}\binom{n-a}{b}p_{i}^{a}p_{k}^{b}(1-p_{i}-p_{k})^{n-a-b}-\binom{n}{a}\binom{n}{b}p_{i}^{a}p_{k}^{b}(1-p_{i})^{n-a}(1-p_{k})^{n-b}\Big)\\
=:\sum_{a=0}^{j-1}\sum_{b=0}^{j-1}C_{a,b}(i,k,n),

where
X^{c}
denotes the complement of a set
X
. Further, we obtain

C_{a,b}(i,k,n)=\binom{n}{a}\binom{n-a}{b}p_{i}^{a}p_{k}^{b}\Big((1-p_{i}-p_{k})^{n-a-b}-(1-p_{i})^{n-a}(1-p_{k})^{n-b}\Big)\\
-\binom{n}{a}\Big(\binom{n}{b}-\binom{n-a}{b}\Big)p_{i}^{a}p_{k}^{b}(1-p_{i})^{n-a}(1-p_{k})^{n-b}=:C^{(1)}_{a,b}(i,k,n)+C^{(2)}_{a,b}(i,k,n).

To analyze
C^{(1)}_{a,b}
we argue as on p. 152 in
[
13
]
. Using an expansion

(x-y)^{m}=x^{m}-mx^{m-1}y+O(m^{2}x^{m-2}y^{2}),\quad m\to\infty,

which holds for positive
x
and
y
,
x>y
, with
x=(1-p_{i})(1-p_{k})
,
y=p_{i}p_{k}
and
m=n-a-b
for
0\leq a,b\leq j-1
yields

(1-p_{i}-p_{k})^{n-a-b}=(1-p_{i})^{n-a-b}(1-p_{k})^{n-a-b}-(n-a-b)p_{i}p_{k}(1-p_{i})^{n-a-b-1}(1-p_{k})^{n-a-b-1}\\
+O(n^{2}p_{i}^{2}p_{k}^{2}(1-p_{i})^{n-a-b-2}(1-p_{k})^{n-a-b-2}),\quad n\to\infty.

Hence,

C^{(1)}_{a,b}(i,k,n)=\binom{n}{a}\binom{n-a}{b}p_{i}^{a}p_{k}^{b}\Big((1-p_{i})^{n-a-b}(1-p_{k})^{n-a-b}\Big(1-(1-p_{i})^{b}(1-p_{k})^{a}\Big)\\
-(n-a-b)p_{i}p_{k}(1-p_{i})^{n-a-b-1}(1-p_{k})^{n-a-b-1}+O(n^{2}p_{i}^{2}p_{k}^{2}(1-p_{i})^{n-a-b-2}(1-p_{k})^{n-a-b-2})\Big)\\
=:F_{a,b}(i,k,n)+G_{a,b}(i,k,n)+H_{a,b}(i,k,n).

Now we investigate the contributions of the summands to
\sum_{i\neq k}\sum_{a=0}^{j-1}\sum_{b=0}^{j-1}C_{a,b}(i,k,n)
.

Analysis of
H_{a,b}
:

\sum_{i\neq k}\binom{n}{a}\binom{n-a}{b}n^{2}p_{i}^{a+2}p_{k}^{b+2}(1-p_{i})^{n-a-b-2}(1-p_{k})^{n-a-b-2}\\
\leq\sum_{i\geq 1}n\binom{n}{a}p_{i}^{a+2}(1-p_{i})^{n-a-b-2}\sum_{k\geq 1}n\binom{n-a}{b}p_{k}^{b+2}(1-p_{k})^{n-a-b-2}\\
~\sim~{\rm const}\,\frac{\big({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)\big)^{2}}{n^{2}}~\to~0,\quad n\to\infty.

Here,
{\rm const}
denotes a positive constant, the asymptotic equivalence follows from Lemma
7.9
, and the last limit relation is justified by (
94
).

Analysis of
F_{a,b}
. Assume first that
a\geq 2
and
b\geq 2
. An application of Bernoulli’s inequality
1-(1-x)^{m}\leq mx
, which holds for
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in[0,1]
, yields

F_{a,b}(i,k,n):=\binom{n}{a}\binom{n-a}{b}p_{i}^{a}p_{k}^{b}(1-p_{i})^{n-a-b}(1-p_{k})^{n-a-b}\Big(1-(1-p_{i})^{b}(1-p_{k})^{a}\Big)\\
\leq b\binom{n}{a}\binom{n-a}{b}p_{i}^{a+1}p_{k}^{b}(1-p_{i})^{n-a-b}(1-p_{k})^{n-a-b}+a\binom{n}{a}\binom{n-a}{b}p_{i}^{a}p_{k}^{b+1}(1-p_{i})^{n-a-b}(1-p_{k})^{n-a-b}\\
=:F^{(1)}_{a,b}(i,k,n)+F^{(2)}_{a,b}(i,k,n).

By Lemma
7.9
and (
94
),

0\leq\sum_{i\neq k}F^{(1)}_{a,b}(i,k,n)\leq b\sum_{i\geq 1}\binom{n}{a}p_{i}^{a+1}(1-p_{i})^{n-a-b}\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{n-a}{b}p_{k}^{b}(1-p_{k})^{n-a-b}\\
~\sim~\text{\rm const}\,\frac{{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)}{n}{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)=o({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)),\quad n\to\infty.

The argument for
F^{(2)}_{a,b}(i,k,n)
is analogous.

Consider now
F_{a,b}(i,k,n)
with
a\leq 1
or
b\leq 1
. Notice that
F_{0,0}(i,k,n)=0
. Further,
F_{0,1}(i,k,n)=F_{1,0}(i,k,n)=np_{i}p_{k}(1-p_{i})^{n-1}(1-p_{k})^{n-1}
, whence

and
F_{0,1}(i,k,n)+C^{(2)}_{1,1}(i,k,n)=0
.

For
b\geq 2
, using Pascal’s rule we obtain

F_{0,b}(i,k,n)+C^{(2)}_{1,b}(i,k,n)\\
=\binom{n}{b}p_{k}^{b}(1-p_{i})^{n-b}(1-p_{k})^{n-b}\Big(1-(1-p_{i})^{b}\Big)-n\Big(\binom{n}{b}-\binom{n-1}{b}\Big)p_{i}p_{k}^{b}(1-p_{i})^{n-1}(1-p_{k})^{n-b}\\
=\binom{n}{b}p_{k}^{b}(1-p_{i})^{n-b}(1-p_{k})^{n-b}\Big(1-(1-p_{i})^{b}-bp_{i}(1-p_{i})^{b-1}\Big).

Since, for
x\in[0,1]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
(1-x)^{n}+nx(1-x)^{n-1}\leq 1
, we conclude that the last expression is nonnegative. Invoking Bernoulli’s inequality twice and then Lemma
7.9
and (
94

0\leq\sum_{i\neq k}(F_{0,b}(i,k,n)+C^{(2)}_{1,b}(i,k,n))\leq\sum_{i\neq k}b\binom{n}{b}p_{i}p_{k}^{b}(1-p_{i})^{n-b}(1-p_{k})^{n-b}\Big(1-(1-p_{i})^{b-1}\Big)\\
\leq\sum_{i\neq k}b(b-1)\binom{n}{b}p_{i}^{2}p_{k}^{b}(1-p_{i})^{n-b}(1-p_{k})^{n-b}\leq b(b-1)\sum_{i\geq 1}p_{i}^{2}(1-p_{i})^{n-b}\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{n}{b}p_{k}^{b}(1-p_{k})^{n-b}\\
~\sim~{\rm const}\,\frac{({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n))^{2}}{n^{2}}\to 0,\quad n\to\infty.

Analogously, for
a\geq 2
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i\neq k}(F_{a,0}(i,k,n)+C^{(2)}_{a,1}(i,k,n))=0.

Consider now
F_{1,1}(i,k,n)=n(n-1)p_{i}p_{k}(1-p_{i})^{n-2}(1-p_{k})^{n-2}(p_{i}+p_{k}-p_{i}p_{k})
. The first summand of
F_{1,1}
satisfies

and (

\sum_{i\neq k}n(n-1)p_{i}^{2}p_{k}^{2}(1-p_{i})^{n-2}(1-p_{k})^{n-2}\leq\sum_{i\geq 1}np_{i}^{2}(1-p_{i})^{n-2}\sum_{k\geq 1}(n-1)p_{k}^{2}(1-p_{k})^{n-2}\\
~\sim~{\rm const}\,\frac{({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n))^{2}}{n^{2}}\to 0,\quad n\to\infty.

For

,

F_{1,b}(i,k,n)+G_{0,b}(i,k,n)=n\binom{n-1}{b}p_{i}p_{k}^{b}(1-p_{i})^{n-1-b}(1-p_{k})^{n-1-b}\Big(1-(1-p_{i})^{b}(1-p_{k})\Big)\\
-(n-b)\binom{n}{b}p_{i}p_{k}^{b+1}(1-p_{i})^{n-1-b}(1-p_{k})^{n-1-b}\\
=n\binom{n-1}{b}p_{i}p_{k}^{b}(1-p_{i})^{n-1-b}(1-p_{k})^{n-1-b}\Big(1-(1-p_{i})^{b}(1-p_{k})-p_{k}\Big).

0\leq\sum_{i\neq k}(F_{1,b}(i,k,n)+G_{0,b}(i,k,n))\leq\sum_{i\neq k}bn\binom{n-1}{b}p_{i}^{2}p_{k}^{b}(1-p_{i})^{n-1-b}(1-p_{k})^{n-1-b}\\
\leq b\sum_{i\geq 1}np_{i}^{2}(1-p_{i})^{n-1-b}\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{n-1}{b}p_{k}^{b}(1-p_{k})^{n-1-b}\\
~\sim~{\rm const}\,\frac{{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)}{n}{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)=o({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)),\quad n\to\infty.

Analogously, for
a\geq 2
,

\sum_{i\neq k}(F_{a,1}(i,k,n)+G_{a,0}(i,k,n))=o({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)),\quad n\to\infty.

Analysis of

(cont)
. We have not investigated

\leq\sum_{i\geq 1}(n-a-b)\binom{n}{a}p_{i}^{a+1}(1-p_{i})^{n-a-b-1}\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{n-a}{b}p_{k}^{b+1}(1-p_{k})^{n-a-b-1}\\
~\sim~{\rm const}\,{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)\frac{{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)}{n}=o({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)),\quad n\to\infty.

Analysis of

(cont)
. Observe that
C^{(2)}_{a,b}(i,k,n)=0
whenever

or

. Thus, we are left with analyzing

\binom{n}{b}-\binom{n-a}{b}=O(n^{b-1})

. We have used Lemma

Combining all the fragments together we arrive at (

Proof of Theorem
4.7
.

We divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1
. Under the present assumptions, for
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
|\mathbb{E}\mathcal{K}_{j}(n)-\mathbb{E}K_{j}(n)|\to 0
and
{\rm Var\,}\mathcal{K}_{j}(n)\sim{\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)
as

by (
19
) and formula (
20
) of Proposition
4.6
.

Step 2
. Recall that, for
j,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{K}_{j}(n)=K_{j}(S_{n})
. We are going to prove that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{K_{j}(S_{n})-K_{j}(n)}{({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)f({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)))^{1/2}}=0\quad\text{a.s.},

where
f(t)=\log t
under (
11
) and (
12
) and
f(t)=\log\log t
under the other assumptions of Theorem
4.7
. This is equivalent to showing that, for all
\varepsilon>0
and large enough
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}\1_{A_{n}(\varepsilon)}<\infty
a.s., where

A_{n}=A_{n}(\varepsilon):=\Big\{\frac{|K_{j}(S_{n})-K_{j}(n)|}{({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)f({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)))^{1/2}}>\varepsilon\Big\}

(the value of
n_{0}
is chosen large enough to ensure that
n_{0}\geq 3

f({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n))>0

n\geq n_{0}
). For any
\delta>0
and integer
n\geq 3

B_{n}=B_{n}(\delta):=\Big\{\frac{|S_{n}-n|}{(2n\log\log n)^{1/2}}\leq 1+\delta\Big\}.

Write

\1_{A_{n}}=\1_{A_{n}\cap B_{n}}+\1_{A_{n}\cap B_{n}^{c}}\leq\1_{A_{n}\cap B_{n}}+\1_{B_{n}^{c}}\quad\text{a.s.},

where, as usual,
B_{n}^{c}
is the complement of
B_{n}
.
According to the LIL for the standard random walk
(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
,

\sum_{n\geq 3}\1_{B_{n}^{c}}<\infty
a.s. Put
d_{n}:=(1+\delta)\sqrt{2n\log\log n}
for
n\geq 3
. We are left with proving that
\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}\1_{A_{n}\cap B_{n}}<\infty
a.s.
To this end, it is enough to check that
\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}\mathbb{P}(A_{n}\cap B_{n})<\infty

\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}\mathbb{P}(A_{n}\cap B_{n})\leq\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}\mathbb{P}\Big\{\frac{K_{j}(n+d_{n})-K_{j}(n)}{({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)f({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)))^{1/2}}>\varepsilon\Big\}\\
+\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}\mathbb{P}\Big\{\frac{K_{j}(n)-K_{j}(n-d_{n})}{({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)f({\rm Var\,}K_{j}(n)))^{1/2}}>\varepsilon\Big\}=:I(\varepsilon)+J(\varepsilon).

We shall only show that
I(\varepsilon)<\infty
, the proof for
J(\varepsilon)
being analogous. For
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\geq 0
, put

b_{j}(t):=\mathbb{E}K_{j}(t)
and
a_{j}(t):={\rm Var\,}K_{j}(t)
. Since, for each
t\geq 0

\sum_{k\geq 1}{\rm e}^{-p_{k}t}\frac{(p_{k}t)^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}p_{k}\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}p_{k}=1,

the series
\sum_{k\geq 1}{\rm e}^{-p_{k}t}\frac{(p_{k}t)^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}p_{k}
converges uniformly in

. This fact enables us to differentiate the series termwise:

(b_{j}(t))^{\prime}=\sum_{k\geq 1}\Big(1-{\rm e}^{-p_{k}t}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\frac{(p_{k}t)^{i}}{i!}\Big)^{\prime}=\sum_{k\geq 1}{\rm e}^{-p_{k}t}\frac{(p_{k}t)^{j-1}}{(j-1)!}p_{k}=\frac{j\mathbb{E}K^{*}_{j}(t)}{t}.

By the mean value theorem for differentiable functions, for
n\geq 3
and some

b_{j}(n+d_{n})-b_{j}(n)=d_{n}b_{j}^{\prime}(\theta_{n}).

Under the present assumptions, the function

, hence also
b_{j}^{\prime}
, is regularly varying at
\infty

67
), (
70
) and (
71
). Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}(\theta_{n}/n)=1
we conclude that
b_{j}^{\prime}(\theta_{n})\sim b_{j}^{\prime}(n)
as

by the uniform convergence theorem for regularly varying functions (Theorem 1.5.2 in
[
4
]
) and thereupon

b_{j}(n+d_{n})-b_{j}(n)\sim d_{n}\frac{j\mathbb{E}K^{*}_{j}(n)}{n},\quad n\to\infty.

(98)

Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
, we infer

\frac{d_{n}}{(a_{j}(n)f(a_{j}(n)))^{1/2}}\frac{j\mathbb{E}K^{*}_{j}(n)}{n}~\sim~{\rm const}\,\Big(\frac{\ell(n)}{f(\ell(n))}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\frac{\log\log n}{n}\Big)^{1/2}~\to~0,\quad n\to\infty

having utilized (
66
) and (
67
). Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.3
, according to (
69
) and (
70
),

\frac{d_{n}}{(a_{j}(n)f(a_{j}(n)))^{1/2}}\frac{j\mathbb{E}K^{*}_{j}(n)}{n}=\frac{d_{n}}{(a_{j}(n)\log\log a_{j}(n))^{1/2}}\frac{j\mathbb{E}K^{*}_{j}(n)}{n}\\
~\sim~{\rm const}\,n^{(\alpha-1)/2}(L(n))^{1/2}~\to~0,\quad n\to\infty

(99)

because
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.4
, relation (

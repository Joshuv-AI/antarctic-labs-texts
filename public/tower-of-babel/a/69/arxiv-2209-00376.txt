Minimally tough chordal graphs with toughness at most 1 / 2 1/2
Humara Khan, Gyula Y. Katona, Humara Khan

Abstract
Let t t be a positive real number. A graph is called t t -tough if the removal of any vertex set S S that disconnects the graph leaves at most | S | / t |S|/t components. The toughness of a graph is the largest t t for which the graph is t t -tough. A graph is minimally t t -tough if the toughness of the graph is t t and the deletion of any edge from the graph decreases the toughness. A graph is chordal if it does not contain an induced cycle of length at least 4 4 . We characterize the minimally t t -tough, chordal graphs for all t ≤ 1 / 2 t\leq 1/2 . As a corollary, a characterization of minimally t t -tough, interval graphs is obtained for t ≤ 1 / 2 t\leq 1/2 .

Introduction

All graphs considered in this paper are finite, simple and
undirected. Let
\omega(G)

\alpha(G)
denote the independence number and
\kappa(G)
denote the
connectivity number of the graph

. (Using
\omega(G)
to denote the
number of components may be confusing, however, most of the literature
on toughness uses this notation.) For a connected graph

The notion of toughness was introduced by Chvátal in

Definition 1
.

Let
t
be a real number. A graph

is called
t

|S|\geq t\cdot\omega(G-S)
for any cutset

. The toughness of

for which G is
t
-tough, taking
\tau(K_{n})=\infty

We say that a cutset

is a tough set if
\omega(G-S)=|S|/\tau(G)

Note that a graph is disconnected if and only if its toughness is

Definition 2
.

A graph

\tau(G)=t

\tau(G-e)<t

It follows directly from the definition that every

-tough noncomplete graph is

for noncomplete graphs. Therefore, the minimum degree of any

The following conjecture is motivated by a theorem of Mader

Conjecture 3

(Kriesell

Every minimally

This conjecture can be naturally generalized.

Conjecture 4

(Generalized Kriesell’s Conjecture)

Every minimally

-tough graph has a vertex of degree

Definition 5

A graph is chordal if it does not contain an induced cycle of length at least

A vertex

Our goal is to characterize minimally tough, chordal graphs with toughness at most

Let

in the tree

It is also proved in

Theorem 7

(
[
7
]
)
.

•

A connected graph

•

A connected graph

is chordal if and only if there exists a tree

for which the induced-subtree property holds.

In other words, a connected graph

Definition 8
.

We call a graph a

Now remove all vertices of

Lemma 11
.

If
G
is a chordal graph such that in every clique tree, all the edges have weight
1
, then every vertex of

is either a simplicial vertex or a cut-vertex.

Proof.

Notice that the clique intersection property implies that if there are two maximal cliques with intersection of size at least
2
, then the clique tree will have an edge of weight at least
2
even if the corresponding vertices are not adjacent in the clique tree. This would contradict the assumption of the lemma.

Clearly, every vertex of the graph must be contained in a maximal clique. If a vertex
v
is contained in only one, then it is a simplicial vertex. So assume that
v
is contained in at least two maximal cliques, say
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
. The assumption implies that
|Q_{1}\cap Q_{2}|=1
. Now we show that

is a cut-vertex.

Suppose to the contrary that it is not, then for any vertex
x\in Q_{1}-Q_{2}
and any vertex

there is a path
P
connecting
x
and
y
and avoiding
v
, choose a shortest such path. If
xy\in E(G)
, then the intersection of the maximal clique containing the triangle
vxy
and

has size at least
2
, a contradiction. If

, then the path

with the path

forms a cycle of length at least

. Since

is chordal, this cycle must be triangulated. Moreover, since we chose a shortest path,

must be connected to all the other vertices on the path

. Now if

is the first edge of the path leaving
Q_{1}
, then the intersection of the maximal clique containing the triangle
vx^{\prime}y^{\prime}
and

has size at least
2
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 12
.

If
G
is a chordal graph with
\tau(G)=t\leq 1/2
which has a clique tree containing an edge of weight at least

, then

is not minimally

Since the toughness of

All vertices of the two cliques

Notice that there must also be a vertex also in

To show that condition

We claim that

The above claim implies that either all neighbors of

Let

In the introduction, the modified degree was defined for TT-graphs. However, it can be easily extended for any graph in which each vertex is either simplicial vertex or a cut-vertex:

Definition 15
.

If
G
is graph in which each vertex is either simplicial vertex or a cut-vertex, then the modified degree of a vertex
v
, denoted by
md(v)
, is the number of components in
G-v
. Let
\mu(G)
denote the maximum modified degree in
G
.

For these graphs it is easy to determine the toughness.

Lemma 16
.

If
G
is a connected, noncomplete graph in which each vertex is either a simplicial vertex or a cut-vertex, then
\tau(G)=1/\mu(G)
.

Proof.

If
md(v)=\mu(G)
, then clearly the number of components of
G-v
is
\mu(G)
, so
\tau(G)\leq 1/\mu(G)
.

Let
S
be a tough set. We claim that
|S|=1
. If
|S|>1
, then let
x\in S
be a vertex having the smallest modified degree, and let
S^{\prime}=S-x
. If
x
is a simplicial vertex, then
\omega(G-S^{\prime})=\omega(G-S)
, which contradicts the assumption that
S
is a tough set. Thus
x
is a cut-vertex. Consider the components of
G-S^{\prime}
. The vertex
x
belongs to one of these components. Since
x
is a cut-vertex, we have
\omega(G-S)\leq\omega(G-S^{\prime})+\mu(G)-1
, because deleting
x
besides
S^{\prime}
would give new

components instead of this old component. Now

\tau(G)\omega(G-S^{\prime})\geq\tau(G)\omega(G-S)-\tau(G)(\mu(G)-1)=\\
|S|-\mu(G)\tau(G)+\tau(G)>|S|-1=|S^{\prime}|,

since
\mu(G)\tau(G)<1
by our first claim, and
\tau(G)>0
. On the other hand, since
\tau(G)
is the toughness, we have
\omega(G-S^{\prime})\tau(G)\leq|S^{\prime}|
, a contradiction.

Hence, it is enough to consider the cutsets of size one to determine the toughness, and it is clear that the most number of components is obtained by deleting a vertex with maximum modified degree.
∎

Let
G
be a connected, noncomplete chordal graph so that in every clique tree all the edges have weight

and all cliques have size at most
3
in

is minimally

-tough, then it is a TT-graph and

.

Proof.

In Claim
11

is either a simplicial vertex or a cut-vertex, so we can use Definition
15

We show that if
G
is minimally

-tough and

is contained in a triangle

, then
md(v)=\mu(G)
. Suppose on the contrary that

. Let
e=uw
be the edge of

that is not incident to

. Consider now

. If

or

was a cut-vertex in

. If either one of them was a simplicial vertex in

are still either simplicial or cut-vertices since for other vertices nothing will change.

Notice that

. Also,

When

Proof of Theorem
10
.

Let
G
be a minimally
t
-tough, chordal graph with

. Since

is chordal and connected, it has a set of clique trees. By Lemma

, none of these clique trees contain an edge of weight at least

To complete the proof, we show that any TT-graph

is minimally

-tough. It is clear that in a TT-graph every vertex is either a leaf, and therefore simplicial, or a cut-vertex. Thus Lemma

can be applied, implying that
\tau(G)=1/\mu(G)
. It is also clear that TT-graphs have two kinds of edges: every edge is either a bridge or it is contained in a triangle.

If an edge

is a bridge, then
G-e
is disconnected, thus its toughness is

. So the toughness of the graph clearly decreases by removing such an edge.

Now let
e
be an edge of a triangle
Q
. By the definition of TT-graphs, we know that all vertices of
Q
have maximum modified degree
\mu(G)
. This implies that in
G-e
, the vertex of

has modified degree
\mu(G)+1
. Since every vertex of
G-e
is still either simplicial or a cut-vertex, Lemma
16
shows that the toughness of

is

. So the toughness of the graph decreases by removing this kind of edges, too.

Implication for interval graphs

Definition 18
.

A graph is called an interval graph if it is obtained from intervals on the real line in such a way that each interval represents a vertex and the intersection of any two intervals represent an edge. Such graphs are also known as the intersection graph of intervals.

An independent triple of vertices
x,y,z
is called an
asteroidal triple
(AT, for short) if between any pair in the triple there exists a path that avoids the neighborhood of the third vertex.
Caterpillars
are those trees for which removing the leaves produces a path.

Theorem 19

(
[
5
]
)
.

A graph is an interval graph if and only if it is chordal and asteroidal triple-free.

Corollary 20
.

If
G
is a minimally
t
-tough, interval graph with
t\leq 1/2
, then
G
is a caterpillar graph.

Proof.

Since every interval graph is chordal, by Theorem
10
,
G
must be a TT-graph. Now we show that
G

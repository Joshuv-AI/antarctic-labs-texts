Very well–covered graphs via the Rees algebra
Marilena Crupi, Antonino Ficarra

Abstract
. A very well–covered graph is a well–covered graph without isolated vertices such that the size of its minimal vertex covers is half of the number of vertices. If G G is a Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graph, we deeply investigate some algebraic properties of the cover ideal of G G via the Rees algebra associated to the ideal, and especially when G G is a whisker graph.

1.
Introduction

In this article, a
graph
will always mean a finite undirected graph without loops or multiple edges. Let
G
be a graph with the
vertex set

V(G)=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
and the
edge set

E(G)
. Let
K
be a field and let
R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring with coefficients in
K
. If
x_{i}\in V(G)
, the
neighborhood
of
x_{i}
is the set

N(x_{i})=\{x_{j}\in V(G):x_{i}x_{j}\in E(G)\}.

By abuse of notation, we use an edge
e=\{x_{i},x_{j}\}
interchangeably with the monomial
x_{i}x_{j}\in R
.

Let
W\subseteq V(G)
.
W
is called a
vertex cover
if
e\cap W\neq\varnothing
for all
e\in E(G)
, and it is called a
minimal vertex cover
if no proper subset of
W
is a vertex cover of
G
. The set of all minimal vertex covers of
G
is denoted by
\mathcal{C}(G)
. Attached to
G

[
36
,
37
]
there are the
edge ideal
of
G
, defined as

I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}\ :\ x_{i}x_{j}\in E(G)),

and the
cover ideal
of
G
, defined as

J(G)=(x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{s}}\ :\ \{x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{s}}\}\in\mathcal{C}(G)).

The cover ideal
J(G)
is the
Alexander dual
of the edge ideal
I(G)
and conversely. Indeed, the minimal primes of the edge ideals, correspond to the minimal vertex covers of their underlying graph.

Hereafter, for an integer
n\geq 1
, we set
[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}
.

A graph
G
is
well–covered
or
unmixed
if all its minimal vertex covers have the same cardinality. In particular, all the associated primes of
I(G)
have the same height
[
37
]
. By
[
16
, Corollary 3.4]
, for a well–covered graph
G
without isolated vertices we have

2\cdot\min\{|C|:C\in\mathcal{C}(G)\}\ \geq\ |V(G)|.

If equality holds, the graph
G
is called
very well–covered
.
Note that, if
G
is a very well–covered graph, then its number of vertices is even.

Finally, a graph
G
is called
Cohen–Macaulay
over the field
K
if
R/I(G)
is a Cohen–Macaulay ring,
R=K[x_{i}:x_{i}\in V(G)]
. It is well–known that a Cohen–Macaulay graph is well–covered
[
37
, Proposition 7.2.9]
. The fundamental Eagon–Reiner theorem
[
19
, Theorem 8.1.9]
says that
R/I(G)
is a Cohen–Macaulay ring if and only if
J(G)
has a linear resolution.

Very well–covered graphs have been studied from view points of both Commutative Algebra and Combinatorics. See, for instance,
[
7
,
11
,
16
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
31
,
35
]
.
In
[
5
]
we deeply studied very well–covered graphs by means of
Betti splittings

[
15
]
. Recently, many authors have managed the Betti splitting technique for studying algebraic and combinatorial properties of classes of monomial ideals (see, for instance,
[
4
,
6
,
14
]
and references therein).

In the present article, we continue the algebraic study of the class of Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graphs started in
[
5
]
. If
G
is a graph in such a class, our main tool will be the Rees algebra of the cover ideal
J(G)
. We state that if
G
is a Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graph, then the Rees algebra of
J(G)
is a normal Cohen–Macaulay domain and as a consequence we obtain some relevant properties on the behavior of the powers of
J(G)
, when
G
is a
whisker graph
. Adding a
whisker
to a graph
G
at a vertex

means adding a new vertex

and an edge
vw
to the set
E(G)
. If a whisker is added to every vertex of
G
, then the resulting graph, denoted by
G^{\ast}
, is called the
whisker graph
or
suspension
of
G
. It is important to point out that the whisker graph

of a graph

with

2n
vertices (see, for instance,
[
28
]
and references therein). In
[

, Question 6.6]
it is asked in which way attaching whiskers to a graph

gives rise to a graph

such that

has linear powers. In Corollary
4.7
we partially answer this question. See also
[
30

and
[

.

Here are the outline of the article. In Section

). This result is borrowed from
[

2.
A normality criterion for monomial ideals

Let
I
be an ideal of a domain
R
. An element
f\in R
is
integral over
I
if it satisfies an equation of the type

f^{k}+a_{1}f^{k-1}+\dots+a_{k-1}f+a_{k}=0,\ \ \ a_{i}\in I^{i}.

The set of all these elements, denoted by
\overline{I}
, is an ideal containing
I
and called the
integral closure
of
I
. We say that
I
is
integrally closed
if
\overline{I}=I
, and we say that
I
is
normal
if all its powers
I^{k}
,
k\geq 1
, are integrally closed.

Let
I
be an ideal of a commutative ring
R
generated by
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}
. The
Rees algebra
of
I
, denoted by
\mathcal{R}(I)
or
R[It]
, is the subring of
R[t]
, defined as follows

\mathcal{R}(I)=R[It]=R[u_{1}t,\ldots,u_{m}t]=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}I^{k}t^{k}\subset\ R[t],

where
t
is a new variable. We quote the next fundamental result from
[
24
]
(see, also,
[
37
, Theorem 4.3.17]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
I
be an ideal of a normal domain
R
. Then the
following are equivalent:

(a)

I
is a normal ideal;

(b)

the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I)
is normal.

This property will be crucial in the sequel.

Now, let
R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be the standard graded polynomial ring with coefficients in a field
K
and let
I
be a monomial ideal of
R
. As usual we denote by
G(I)=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\}
the unique minimal set of monomial generators of
I
. Then the Rees algebra of
I
is the following
K
-algebra

\mathcal{R}(I)\ =\ K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},u_{1}t,\dots,u_{m}t]\ \subset\ R[t].

The next criterion quickly follows from
[
33
, Theorem 3.1]
(see, also,
[
34
, Theorem 3.1]
).

Criterion 2.2
.

Let
I_{1},I_{2}\subset K[x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]
be two squarefree monomial ideals. If
I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}
are normal ideals, then
I=I_{1}+x_{1}I_{2}\subset R
is a normal squarefree monomial ideal.

Indeed, by
[
33
, Theorem 3.1]
, it is enough to check that
I_{1}+I_{2}
is normal and that
\textup{gcd}(x_{1},u)=1
for all
u\in G(I_{1})\cup G(I_{2})
. Since
I_{1}\subseteq I_{2}
, the first assertion follows because
I_{1}+I_{2}=I_{2}
is normal by hypothesis. The second assertion follows because the generators of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are monomials of
K[x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]
.

3.
The Rees algebra

In this section we study the Rees algebra of the vertex cover ideal of a Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graph.

The main result in this section is the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
G
be a Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graph. Then the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(J(G))
is a normal Cohen–Macaulay domain.

In order to prove it, we recall the following fundamental algebraic characterization of Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graphs.

Characterization 3.2
.

(
[
7
]
,
[
31
, Lemma 3.1]
).
Let
G
be a very well–covered graph with
2n
vertices. Then, the following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

G
is Cohen–Macaulay.

(b)

There exists a labeling of
V(G)=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
such that

(i)

X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
is a minimal vertex cover of
G
and
Y=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
is a maximal independent set of
G
,

(ii)

x_{i}y_{i}\in E(G)
for all
i\in[n]
,

(iii)

if
x_{i}y_{j}\in E(G)
then
i\leq j
,

(iv)

if
x_{i}y_{j}\in E(G)
then
x_{i}x_{j}\notin E(G)
,

(v)

if
z_{i}x_{j},y_{j}x_{k}\in E(G)
then
z_{i}x_{k}\in E(G)
for any distinct
i,j,k
and
z_{i}\in\{x_{i},y_{i}\}
.

From now on, if
G
is a Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graph with
2n
vertices, we tacitly assume that its set of vertices
V(G)=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
satisfies the conditions (i)-(v) of Characterization
3.2
, without having to relabel it. See
[
7
,
5
]
for more details on this topic.

Hereafter, denote by
S
the polynomial ring
K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]
in the
2n
variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
with coefficients in the field
K
.

Let
F\subseteq[n]
be a non empty set. We set
{\bf x}_{F}=\prod_{i\in F}x_{i}
,
{\bf y}_{F}=\prod_{i\in F}y_{i}
. Otherwise, we set
{\bf x}_{\varnothing}={\bf y}_{\varnothing}=1
. The
support
of a monomial
u\in S
is the set

\operatorname{supp}(u)=\{x_{i}:x_{i}\ \textit{divides}\ u\}\cup\{y_{j}:y_{j}\ \textit{divides}\ u\}.

If
W\subseteq V(G)
, we denote by
G\setminus W
the subgraph of
G
with the vertices of
W
and their incident edges deleted.

The following results were proved in
[
5
, Lemma 2.2, Proposition 2.1]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graph with
2n
vertices.

(a)

For each
u\in G(J(G))
there exists a unique subset
F
of
[n]
such that
u={\bf x}_{F}{\bf y}_{[n]\setminus F}
.

(b)

For any
A\subseteq[n]
,
G\setminus\{x_{i},y_{i}:i\in A\}
is a Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graph.

For a subset
C
of
X\cup Y=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
, we define

{\bf z}_{C}={\bf x}_{C_{x}}{\bf y}_{C_{y}},

where
C_{x}=\{i:x_{i}\in C\}
and
C_{y}=\{j:y_{j}\in C\}
. The next result holds true.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G
be a Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graph with
2n
vertices. Then

J(G)\ =\ {\bf z}_{N(x_{1})}J(G_{1})+x_{1}J(G\setminus\{x_{1},y_{1}\}),

(1)

where
G_{1}=G\setminus\{x_{i},y_{i}:i\in N(x_{1})_{x}\cup N(x_{1})_{y}\}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
[
5
, Proposition 2.3]
. We include it for completeness. Let
u\in G(J(G))
. By Lemma
3.3
(a), either
x_{1}
divides
u
or
y_{1}
divides
u
.

Case 1.
Suppose
x_{1}
divides
u
. Note that
N(y_{1})=\{x_{1}\}
. Indeed, by Characterization
3.2
(i),
N(y_{1})
is a subset of
X
, since
Y
is a maximal independent set. Moreover, by (iii) if
x_{i}y_{1}\in E(G)
then
i\leq 1
. Hence,
N(y_{1})=\{x_{1}\}
. Consequently
{\bf z}_{N(y_{1})}=x_{1}
and the support
C^{\prime}
of
u/{\bf z}_{N(y_{1})}=u/x_{1}
is a vertex cover of
G\setminus\{x_{1},y_{1}\}
. But
C^{\prime}
is a minimal vertex cover, for
u/x_{1}
has degree
n-1
and
G\setminus\{x_{1},y_{1}\}
is a Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graph with
2(n-1)
vertices (Lemma
3.3
(b)). Thus
u/x_{1}\in G(J(G\setminus\{x_{1},y_{1}\}))
and so
u\in G(x_{1}J(G\setminus\{x_{1},y_{1}\}))
.

Case 2.
Suppose
y_{1}
divides
u
. Since the support
C
of
u
is a minimal vertex cover of
G
and
x_{1}\notin C
, then
z_{i}\in C
for all
z_{i}\in N(x_{1})
. Consequently, the support
C_{1}
of
u/{\bf z}_{N(x_{1})}
is a vertex cover of
G_{1}
. But
C_{1}
is a minimal vertex cover of
G_{1}
, for
|C_{1}|=n-|N(x_{1})|
and
G_{1}
is a Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graph with
2(n-|N(x_{1})|)
vertices (Lemma
3.3
(b)). Hence
u\in G({\bf z}_{N(x_{1})}J(G_{1}))
.

These two cases show the inclusion “
\subseteq
” in equation (
1
). The other inclusion is acquired as in the last part of the proof of
[
5
, Proposition 2.3]
.

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
G
be a Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graph with
2n
vertices. Then
J(G)
is a normal ideal.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.4
, equation (
1
) holds. Set
J=J(G)
,
J_{1}={\bf z}_{N(x_{1})\setminus y_{1}}J(G_{1})
and
J_{2}=J(G\setminus\{x_{1},y_{1}\})
. Thus

J\ =\ y_{1}J_{1}+x_{1}J_{2}.

Since
y_{1}J_{1},J_{2}\subset K[x_{2},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{n}]
, it is enough to show that
J_{1}\subseteq J_{2}
. Then
y_{1}J_{1}\subset J_{2}
and the result follows from Criterion
2.2
and induction on
n
.

Let
u\in G(J_{1})
. We must prove that
u\in G(J_{2})
, too. That is, we must show that
C=\operatorname{supp}(u)
is a minimal vertex cover of
G\setminus\{x_{1},y_{1}\}
. It is enough to prove
C
is a vertex cover of
G\setminus\{x_{1},y_{1}\}
. Minimality follows because
|C|=n-1
. Hence, we must prove that
e\cap C\neq\varnothing
for all edges
e\in E(G\setminus\{x_{1},y_{1}\})
. Let
e\in E(G\setminus\{x_{1},y_{1}\})
. Since
y_{1}u\in G(J)
, it follows that
C\cup y_{1}
is a minimal vertex cover of
G
. Hence
e\cap(C\cup y_{1})\neq\varnothing
. Therefore
e\cap C\neq\varnothing
because
y_{1}\notin e
. Our assertion follows.

∎

Finally, we are in the position to prove the main result in the section.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

By Corollary
3.5
,
J(G)
is a normal ideal. Hence, the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(J(G))
is normal (Theorem
2.1
). Next, by a theorem of Hochster
[
25
]
, since
\mathcal{R}(J(G))
is a normal affine semigroup ring, it follows that
\mathcal{R}(J(G))
is Cohen–Macaulay.

∎

The
toric ring
of
G(J(G))
is the
K
-algebra
K[J(G)]=K[u:u\in G(J(G))]\subset S
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
G
be a Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graph. Then the toric ring
K[J(G)]
is a normal Cohen–Macaulay domain.

Proof.

Since
J(G)
is generated in one degree, the statement follows from Theorem
3.1
together with
[
37
, Proposition 4.3.42]
.

∎

Now let
I
be an ideal of a noetherian ring
R
. As usual, denote by
V(I)
the set of prime ideals containing
I
and by
\textup{Ass}(I)
the set of associated prime ideals of
R/I
. For all
P\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, we denote by
\mathfrak{m}_{P}
the maximal ideal of the local ring
R_{P}
. Recall that
I
satisfies the
persistence property
(with respect to associated ideals) if

\textup{Ass}(I)\subseteq\textup{Ass}(I^{2})\subseteq\textup{Ass}(I^{3})\subseteq\cdots.

In
[
23
]
, Herzog and Qureshi introduced the notion of
strong persistence property
. More in detail, let
P\in V(I)
. We say that
I
satisfies the
strong persistence property with respect to
P
if for all
k
and all
f\in(I^{k}_{P}:\mathfrak{m}_{P})\setminus I^{k}_{P}
there exists
g\in I_{P}
such that
fg\notin I^{k+1}_{P}
. The ideal
I
is said to satisfy the
strong persistence property
if it satisfies the strong persistence property for all
P\in V(I)
. One can verify that the strong persistence property implies the persistence property
[
23
]
(see, also,
[
33
, Proposition 2.1]
).

Theorem
3.1
yields the next result.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
G
be a Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graph. Then
J(G)
satisfies the strong persistence property, and in particular, the persistence property.

Proof.

The assertion follows from Theorem
3.1
and
[
23
, Corollary 1.6]
.

∎

4.
Whisker graphs

In this section we study some algebraic properties of the powers of the cover ideals of a special class of Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graphs. Our main tool is the so called
\ell
-exchange property introduced in
[
20
]
.

Let
I\subset R=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
be a monomial ideal generated in one degree, and let
K[I]=K[u:u\in G(I)]
be the toric ring of
G(I)
. Then
K[I]
has the presentation
\psi:T=K[t_{u}:u\in G(I)]\rightarrow K[I]
defined by
\psi(t_{u})=u
for all
u\in G(I)
. The kernel
\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)=J
is called the
toric ideal
of
K[I]
.

Fix a monomial order
>
on
T
. We say that the monomial
t_{u_{1}}\cdots t_{u_{N}}\in T
is
standard with respect to
>
, if
t_{u_{1}}\cdots t_{u_{N}}
does not belong to the initial ideal,
\text{in}_{<}(J)
, of the toric ideal
J
of
K[I]
.

Let
u\in S
be a monomial. The
x_{i}
-degree
and the
y_{i}
-degree
of
u
are the integers
\deg_{x_{i}}(u)=\max\{j:x_{i}^{j}\ \textit{divides}\ u\}
,
\deg_{y_{i}}(u)=\max\{j:y_{i}^{j}\ \textit{divides}\ u\}
, respectively.

Definition 4.1
.

(
[
8
, Definition 3.3]
)
.
The equigenerated monomial ideal
I\subset R
satisfies the
\ell
-exchange property with respect to
>
, if the following condition is satisfied: for all standard monomials
t_{u_{1}}\cdots t_{u_{N}}
,
t_{v_{1}}\cdots t_{v_{N}}\in T
of degree
N
such that

(i)

\deg_{x_{i}}(u_{1}\cdots u_{N})=\deg_{x_{i}}(v_{1}\cdots v_{N})
, for all
1\leq i\leq j-1
with
j\leq n-1
,

(ii)

\deg_{x_{j}}(u_{1}\cdots u_{N})<\deg_{x_{j}}(v_{1}\cdots v_{N})
,

there exist
h
and
k
with
j<h\leq n
and
1\leq k\leq N
, such that
x_{j}(u_{k}/x_{h})\in G(I)
.

The following lemmata will be needed later.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G
be a Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graph with
2n
vertices. Let
C\in\mathcal{C}(G)
such that
C_{y}\neq\varnothing
and let
i=\min C_{y}
. Then
(C\setminus y_{i})\cup x_{i}\in\mathcal{C}(G)
.

Proof.

Firstly we prove that
C^{\prime}=(C\setminus y_{i})\cup x_{i}
is a vertex cover of
G
. Let
e\in E(G)
, we must show that
e\cap C^{\prime}
is non empty. Since
C
is a vertex cover of
G
, then
e\cap C\neq\varnothing
. If
\{x_{i},y_{i}\}\cap e=\varnothing
, then
e\cap C^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
, too. If
x_{i}\in e
then
e\cap C^{\prime}
contains
x_{i}
and therefore the intersection is non empty. Finally, suppose
y_{i}\in e
but
x_{i}\notin e
. Since
Y
is a maximal independent set, it follows that
N(y_{i})\subseteq X
. Hence,
e=x_{j}y_{i}
for some
j
. By Characterization
3.2
(iii) we have
j\leq i
. Thus
j<i
, because
x_{i}\notin e
. Since
i=\min C_{y}
and
j<i
, it follows from Lemma
3.3
(a) that
x_{j}\in C
. Hence

The fact that

Lemma 4.3
.

Let

Theorem 4.5
.

Let

(*)
For all monomials

,
t_{v_{1}}\cdots t_{v_{N}}
of

Recall that an ideal

if

has linear resolution, for all

. Moreover, a monomial ideal

of

if for some order

of its minimal generating set

, all colon ideals
(u_{1},\dots,u_{\ell-1}):u_{\ell}
,
\ell=2,\dots,m
, are generated by a subset of the set of variables
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
.

As a first consequence of Theorem
4.5
we prove that the cover ideal of a whisker graph

has linear powers. Such a result has been recently obtained in
[

, Corollary 4.5]
(see, also,
[

, Theorem 2.3]
) by showing that the ordinary powers of

are weakly polymatroidal, which implies having linear powers.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
G
be a whisker graph with
2n
vertices. Then,

(a)

for all
k\geq 1
,

has linear quotients with respect to the lexicographic order
>_{\operatorname{lex}}
induced by
x_{1}>y_{1}>x_{2}>y_{2}>\cdots>x_{n}>y_{n}

(b)

has linear powers. In particular, the depth function

is a non-increasing function of
k
, that is,
\operatorname{depth}S/J(G)^{k}\geq\operatorname{depth}S/J(G)^{k+1}
for all

Proof.

(a) Since
J(G)
is generated in a single degree, each minimal monomial generator of

is a product
u_{1}\cdots u_{N}
of
N
arbitrary, non necessarily distinct, monomials
u_{i}\in G(J(G))
. Let
u=u_{1}\cdots u_{N}\in G(J(G)^{N})
, where each
u_{i}\in G(J(G))
. Setting
P=(v_{1}\cdots v_{N}:v_{i}\in G(J(G)),v_{1}\cdots v_{N}>_{\operatorname{lex}}u_{1}\cdots u_{N})
, we must prove that the ideal
P:u
is generated by variables.

Let
v=v_{1}\cdots v_{N}\in G(P)
. Using the labeling
z_{i}
on the variables, given in the proof of Theorem
4.5
, by the definition of
>_{\operatorname{lex}}
, for some
i
and
j
we have

(i)

\deg_{z_{i}}(v_{1}\cdots v_{N})=\deg_{z_{i}}(u_{1}\cdots u_{N})
, for all
1\leq i\leq j-1
with
j\leq 2n-1
,

(ii)

\deg_{z_{j}}(v_{1}\cdots v_{N})>\deg_{z_{j}}(u_{1}\cdots u_{N})
.

Hence, by the property
(*)
proved in Theorem
4.5
, there exist integers

and

such that
z_{j}=x_{h}
and
x_{h}(u_{k}/y_{h})\in G(J(G))

and

divides the monomial

(
[

(b) That

has linear powers follows from (a) and the fact that all powers

are monomial ideals generated in a single degree. The claim about the non-increasingness of the function

Theorem 4.8

Let

(a),

\textup{set}(u)\ =\ \{i\ :\ z_{i}\in\{x_{i},y_{i}\},z_{i}\in(v\in G(J(G)^{\ell}):v>u):(u)\}.

The definition of the order

is a subset of

Thus, by

we have

\textup{HS}_{k}(J(G)^{\ell})\ =\ \big({\bf x}_{F}u\ :\ u\in G(J(G)^{\ell}),\ F\subseteq\textup{set}(u),\ |F|=k\big).

Let
{\bf x}_{D}v\in G(\textup{HS}_{k}(J(G)^{\ell}))
,

,

and consider the colon ideal

We must prove that

is generated by variables.

Let
{\bf x}_{F}u\in G(\textup{HS}_{k}(J(G)^{\ell}))
,

be the labeling on the variables such that

,
z_{2}=x_{2}
,
\dots

(4)

Now, we distinguish two cases.

Indeed, by Lemma

. But this would imply that

By Lemma
4.3
and equations (

Another relevant consequence of Theorem
4.5
concerns the
limit depth
of
J(G)
. The role of the Rees algebra of the cover ideal
J(G)
will be crucial to calculating it.

Let
I
be a graded ideal of a polynomial ring
R
with
n
variables. By a theorem of Brodmann
[
3
]
,
\operatorname{depth}R/I^{k}
is constant for
k
large enough. This eventually constant value is called the
limit depth
of
I
, and it is denoted by
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{depth}R/I^{k}
.

The
depth stability
of
I
, denoted by
\textup{dstab}(I)
, is the least integer
k_{0}
such that
\operatorname{depth}R/I^{k}=\operatorname{depth}R/I^{k_{0}}
for all
k\geq k_{0}
. Brodmann proved that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{depth}R/I^{k}\leq n-\ell(I),

where
\ell(I)
is the
analytic spread
of
I
, that is, the Krull dimension of the fiber ring
\mathcal{R}(I)/\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{R}(I)
, where
\mathfrak{m}
is the maximal graded ideal of
R
.

If the Rees algebra of
I
is Cohen–Macaulay, then by
[
19
, Proposition 10.3.2]
(see, also,
[
9
]
combined with
[
26
, Proposition 1.1]
), we have

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{depth}R/I^{k}=n-\ell(I).

(6)

Hence, we have

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
G
be a whisker graph with
2n
vertices. Then

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{depth}S/J(G)^{k}\ =\ n-1.

Moreover,
\textup{dstab}(J(G))\leq n
and
\ell(J(G))=n+1
.

For the proof of this result, we need the next more general lemma. Let
u\in S
be a monomial. Using the notation in Section
3
, we have
\operatorname{supp}(u)_{x}=\{i:x_{i}\ \textit{divides}\ u\}
and
\operatorname{supp}(u)_{y}=\{i:y_{i}\ \textit{divides}\ u\}
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
G
be a Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graph with
2n
vertices. Then, for all

such that
\min\operatorname{supp}(u)_{y}=i
.

We proceed by induction on

. For the base case,

and the statement holds. Now, let

. Then, by Lemma

we have
J(G)={\bf z}_{N(x_{1})}J(G_{1})+x_{1}J(G\setminus\{x_{1},y_{1}\})
. If

, then
\min\operatorname{supp}(u)_{y}=1
. Let

with

is a Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graph (Lemma

(b)), by induction there exists

and Lemma
4.3
imply that the set of variables generating the ideal
P:u_{1}\cdots u_{N}
is a subset of
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
. Pick
n
monomials
u_{i}={\bf x}_{F_{i}}{\bf y}_{[n]\setminus F_{i}}
, where
\min([n]\setminus F_{i})=i
, for
i=1,\dots,n
. The existence of these monomials follows from Lemma
4.10
. By Lemma
4.2
,
x_{i}(u_{i}/y_{i})\in G(J(G))
for
i=1,\dots,n
. Hence,
s(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})=n
. This shows that
\operatorname{depth}S/J(G)^{n}=n-1
. We claim that
\operatorname{depth}S/J(G)^{N}=n-1
for all
N\geq n
. It is enough to consider
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}
and
N-n
arbitrary monomials
v_{n+1},\dots,v_{N}\in G(J(G))
. Then
s(u_{1},\dots,u_{n},v_{n+1},\dots,v_{n})=n
and
\operatorname{depth}S/J(G)^{N}=n-1
. Hence,
\textup{dstab}(J(G))\leq n
. Moreover, from Theorem
3.1
and equation (
6
), since
S
is a polynomial ring in
2n
variables,
\ell(J(G))=2n-\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\operatorname{depth}S/J(G)^{k}=n+1
.

∎

We close the section with some remarks on the reduced Gröbner basis of the presentation ideal
J
of
\mathcal{R}(J(G))
.

Hereafter, we follow closely
[
10
, Section 6.4.1]
.

Let
G
be a Cohen–Macaulay very well–covered graph with
2n
vertices
X\cup Y=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\}
. Let
G(J(G))=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\}
and let
\mathcal{R}(J(G))
be the Rees algebra of

. Let
{\bf x}=x_{1},\dots,x_{n}

{\bf y}=y_{1},\dots,y_{n}

\mathcal{R}(J(G))

of

>^{\prime}
be an arbitrary monomial order on

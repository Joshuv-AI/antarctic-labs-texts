Hermitian Preconditioning for a class of Non-Hermitian Linear Systems
Nicole Spillane

Abstract
This work considers the convergence of GMRES for non-singular problems. GMRES is interpreted as the GCR method which allows for simple proofs of the convergence estimates. Preconditioning and weighted norms within GMRES are considered. The objective is to provide a way of choosing the preconditioner and GMRES norm that ensure fast convergence. The main focus of the article is on Hermitian preconditioning (even for non-Hermitian problems). It is proposed to choose a Hermitian preconditioner 𝐇 \mathbf{H} and to apply GMRES in the inner product induced by 𝐇 \mathbf{H} . If moreover, the problem matrix 𝐀 \mathbf{A} is positive definite, then a new convergence bound is proved that depends only on how well 𝐇 \mathbf{H} preconditions the Hermitian part of 𝐀 \mathbf{A} , and on how non-Hermitian 𝐀 \mathbf{A} is. In particular, if a scalable preconditioner is known for the Hermitian part of 𝐀 \mathbf{A} , then the proposed method is also scalable. This result is illustrated numerically.

GMRES, preconditioning, convergence, Krylov subspace method, GCR, Minimal residual iteration

The idea of separating the Hermitian and skew-Hermitian part of a matrix in order to approximate the solution of a linear system has been exploited in the Hermitian and skew-Hermitian splitting methods introduced in

2
Problem posed, notation and main results

Let
\mathbf{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
be the field over which the linear system is considered. Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
be a non-singular matrix. Given any
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
, the problem at hand is to find
{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
such that:

\mathbf{A}{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{b}.

The chosen methodology is to apply weighted and preconditioned (WP-) GMRES. Let
\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
denote the preconditioner and
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
denote the weight matrix. It is assumed that
\mathbf{H}
is non-singular and that
\mathbf{W}
is Hermitian positive definite (hpd). The inner product and norm induced by
\mathbf{W}
are denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbf{W}}
, respectively:

\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}=\langle\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{W}\mathbf{y}\rangle=\mathbf{y}^{*}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x}\text{ and }{\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbf{w}}={\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}}^{1/2}},\,\forall\,\mathbf{x},\,\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}.

Any matrix
\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
can be split into the sum of its Hermitian part and its skew-Hermitian part. The notation used is

\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{B})=\frac{\mathbf{B}+\mathbf{B}^{*}}{2}\text{ (Hermitian part) and }\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{B})=\frac{\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{B}^{*}}{2}\text{(skew-Hermitian part)}.

(2)

An overview of some results in the article is given in the following theorem.

Theorem 1

(Summary of main results)
.

Assume that the operator
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
and preconditioner
\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
are non-singular, and that the weight matrix
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
is hpd. The
i
-th iterate of weighted and preconditioned GMRES (WP-GMRES) with right preconditioning satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}}{\|\mathbf{r}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{W}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left[1-\inf\limits_{\mathbf{y}\neq 0}\frac{|\langle{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{y}},\mathbf{y}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}|^{2}}{\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{y}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}\|\mathbf{y}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}}\right]^{i/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left[1-\inf\limits_{\mathbf{y}\neq 0}\frac{|\langle\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A}^{-1})\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle|}{\langle\mathbf{H}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle}\times\inf\limits_{\mathbf{y}\neq 0}\frac{|\langle\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle|}{\langle\mathbf{H}^{-1}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle}\right]^{i/2}\text{ if $\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{W}$ is hpd}

\displaystyle\leq\left[1-\frac{1/\kappa(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A}))}{1+\rho(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A}))^{2}}\right]^{i/2}\text{if $\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{W}$ is hpd and $\mathbf{A}$ positive definite},

where
\kappa(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A}))
is the condition number of
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})
and
\rho(\cdot)
denotes the spectral radius of a matrix. For the last two estimates, it has been assumed that the preconditioner
\mathbf{H}
is hpd and that
\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{H}
. In this case, the algorithm will be referred to as WHP-GMRES for Weighted with a Hermitian Preconditioner.

Proof.

The proof proceeds as follows. First, in Theorem
2
, the equivalence between weighted and preconditioned GCR, or WP-GCR, (Algorithm
1
) and WP-GMRES is established as long as the origin is not in the field of values of the preconditioned operator. In the opposite case, the bounds above simplify to
\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}\leq\|\mathbf{r}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{W}}
, a trivial result. The first estimate in the theorem is proved for WP-GCR in Theorem
3
. The second estimate in the theorem is proved for WHP-GCR (where again HP stands for Hermitian preconditioning) in Theorem
5
. It is a direct consequence of the first when
\mathbf{H}
is spd and
\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{H}
. The last estimate in the theorem is proved in Corollary
2
.
∎

Field of value estimates have previously been considered.
The first result in the theorem generalizes

3
Convergence of WP-GMRES viewed as WP-GCR

GCR
[
13
]
, also known as Orthomin
[
45
]
is equivalent to GMRES in the sense that it generates the same approximate solutions at each iteration. GMRES is usually preferred as it is slightly less computationally expensive and more stable. However, GCR has the advantage of a simpler presentation and the proofs in this article all come from the GCR formulation of GMRES.

3.1
WP-GCR with right preconditioning

Weighted and preconditioned GCR (WP-GCR) with right preconditioning is presented in Algorithm
1
. The initial guess
\mathbf{x}_{0}
is assumed to be any vector in
\mathbb{K}^{n}

Algorithm 1

WP-GCR with right preconditioning

0:

\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\mathbf{r}_{0}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{0}

\mathbf{z}_{0}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{r}_{0}

\mathbf{p}_{0}=\mathbf{z}_{0}

\mathbf{q}_{0}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}_{0}

for

i=0,\,1,\,\dots,\;
convergence
do

\delta_{i}=\langle\mathbf{q}_{i},\mathbf{q}_{i}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}
;
\gamma_{i}=\langle{\mathbf{q}_{i}},\mathbf{r}_{i}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}
;
\alpha_{i}=\gamma_{i}/\delta_{i}

\mathbf{x}_{i+1}=\mathbf{x}_{i}+\alpha_{i}\mathbf{p}_{i}

\mathbf{r}_{i+1}=\mathbf{r}_{i}-\alpha_{i}\mathbf{q}_{i}

{\mathbf{z}_{i+1}}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{r}_{i+1}

for

j=0,\,1,\,\dots,i

do

\Phi_{i,j}=\langle\mathbf{q}_{j},\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}_{i+1}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}
;
\beta_{i,j}=\Phi_{i,j}/\delta_{j}

end

for

\mathbf{p}_{i+1}=\mathbf{z}_{i+1}-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i}\beta_{i,j}\mathbf{p}_{j}

\mathbf{q}_{i+1}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}_{i+1}-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i}\beta_{i,j}\mathbf{q}_{j}

end

for

return
Return
\mathbf{x}_{i+1}

In the following theorem, it is proved that WP-GCR is indeed equivalent to WP-GMRES unless WP-GCR has an unlucky breakdown. This can only happen if
0
is in the
\mathbf{W}
-field of values of
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H}

W_{\mathbf{W}}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H})=\left\{\frac{\langle{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{u}},\mathbf{u}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}}{\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{u}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}};\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\right\}.

Although the proof is not new it has been included. Indeed, some intermediary results in the proof are used in subsequent proofs.

Theorem 2
.

Assume that the operator
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
and preconditioner
\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
are non-singular, and that the weight matrix
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
is hpd. The
i
-th residual of Algorithm
1
satisfies

\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}=\operatorname{min}\left\{\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbf{W}};\,{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{x}_{0}+\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{p}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{i-1}\}}\right\}.

(4)

Moreover, if
0\not\in W_{\mathbf{W}}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H})
, then
\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{p}_{0},\dots,\mathbf{p}_{i-1}\}=\mathcal{K}_{i}
where

\mathcal{K}_{i}:=\{\mathbf{H}\mathbf{r}_{0},\mathbf{H}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{r}_{0},\dots,(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{A})^{i-1}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{r}_{0}\}

is the Krylov subspace, i.e., WP-GCR returns the same approximate solutions as WP-GMRES.

Proof.

The vectors
\mathbf{q}_{i}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}_{i}
are pairwise orthogonal,
i.e.
,

\langle\mathbf{q}_{j},\mathbf{q}_{i}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}=0\text{ if }i\neq j.

(5)

Indeed, by symmetry, it suffices to prove by recursion over
i\geq 0
that:
\langle\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}_{i},\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}_{j}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}=0
for every
j<i
.
This is easy by recalling the definitions of
\mathbf{p}_{i}
and
\beta_{i,j}
.

Next, the minimization property (
4
) is proved. By an immediate recursion, it holds that
\mathbf{r}_{i}=\mathbf{r}_{0}-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i-1}\gamma_{j}/\delta_{j}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}_{j}
. We notice that

\gamma_{i}=\langle\mathbf{q}_{i},\mathbf{r}_{i}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}=\langle\mathbf{q}_{i},\mathbf{r}_{0}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i-1}\gamma_{j}/\delta_{j}\langle\mathbf{q}_{i},\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}_{j}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{q}_{i},\mathbf{r}_{0}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}

by (
5
). The
i
-th residual can now be rewritten as

\mathbf{r}_{0}-\mathbf{r}_{i}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i-1}\frac{\langle\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}_{j},\mathbf{r}_{0}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}}{\langle\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}_{j},\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}_{j}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}_{j}.

This means that
\mathbf{r}_{0}-\mathbf{r}_{i}
is the
\mathbf{W}
-orthogonal projection of
\mathbf{r}_{0}
onto
\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{q}_{j};\,j<i\}
. Two other equivalent characterizations of the orthogonal projection are

\mathbf{r}_{i}\in\mathbf{r}_{0}+\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{q}_{j};\,j<i\}\text{ with }\mathbf{r}_{i}=(\mathbf{r}_{0}-\mathbf{r}_{i})-\mathbf{r}_{0}\perp^{\mathbf{W}}\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{q}_{j};\,j<i\},

(6)

and
\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}=\operatorname{min}\left\{\|\mathbf{r}\|_{\mathbf{W}};\,{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbf{r}_{0}+\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{q}_{j};\,j<i\}}\right\}
. Then (
4
) follows by the change of variables
\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{r}_{0}=\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}_{0}
.

It remains to justify that
\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{p}_{j};\,j<i\}=\mathcal{K}_{i}
. It is obvious that
\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{p}_{j};\,j<i\}\subset\mathcal{K}_{i}
. The reverse inclusion is true unless
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}_{i-1}\in\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{q}_{j};\,j<i-1\}
. Then, by (
6
),
\mathbf{r}_{i-1}\perp^{\mathbf{W}}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}_{i-1}
,
i.e.,

\langle\mathbf{r}_{i-1},\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{r}_{i-1}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}=0=\gamma_{i-1}
. If
0\not\in W_{\mathbf{W}}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H})
, this implies
\mathbf{r}_{i-1}=\mathbf{0}
which is a lucky breakdown of both GCR and GMRES.
The proof ends by recalling the characterization of the
i
-th iterate of preconditioned GMRES in
e.g.
,
[
33
]
[Section 9]. Following the idea in

The following observations can be made:

•

Preconditioning modifies the Krylov subspace.

•

Weighting modifies which norm of the residual is minimized.

•

Weighting does not modify the Krylov subspace.

The speed of convergence of the algorithm is addressed next by comparing two subsequent residuals.

Theorem 3

(Convergence of WP-GCR)
.

Assume that the operator
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
and preconditioner
\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
are non-singular, and that the weight matrix
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
is hpd. The
i
-th iterate of Algorithm
1
satisfies

\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}}{\|\mathbf{r}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{W}}}\leq\left(1-\inf\limits_{\mathbf{y}\neq\mathbf{0}}\frac{|\langle{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{y}},\mathbf{y}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}|^{2}}{\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{y}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}\|\mathbf{y}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}}\right)^{i/2}.

(7)

Proof.

From the residual update formula we get
\mathbf{r}_{i}=\mathbf{r}_{i+1}+\alpha_{i}\mathbf{q}_{i}
. By the choice of
\alpha_{i}
,
\mathbf{r}_{i+1}\perp^{\mathbf{W}}\mathbf{q}_{i}
so

\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}=\|\mathbf{r}_{i+1}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}+|\alpha_{i}|^{2}\|\mathbf{q}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}=\|\mathbf{r}_{i+1}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}+\frac{|\langle{\mathbf{q}_{i}},\mathbf{r}_{i}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}|}{\|\mathbf{q}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{4}}\|\mathbf{q}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}.

The relative decrease in residual between two subsequent iterations is

\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{i+1}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}}{\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}}=1-\frac{|\langle{\mathbf{q}_{i}},\mathbf{r}_{i}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}|^{2}}{\|\mathbf{q}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}}.

(8)

Taking the
\mathbf{W}
-inner product of
\mathbf{q}_{i}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}_{i}-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i-1}\mathbf{q}_{j}\beta_{i,j}
by
\mathbf{r}_{i}
leads to

\langle\mathbf{q}_{i},\mathbf{r}_{i}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}=\langle\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}_{i},\mathbf{r}_{i}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i-1}\beta_{i,j}\langle\mathbf{q}_{j},\mathbf{r}_{i}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}=\langle\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}_{i},\mathbf{r}_{i}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}},

and

\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{i+1}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}}{\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}}=1-\frac{|\langle{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}_{i}},\mathbf{r}_{i}\rangle|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}}{\|\mathbf{q}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}}=1-\frac{|\langle{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{r}_{i}},\mathbf{r}_{i}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}|^{2}}{\|\mathbf{q}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}}.

(9)

Next, from the orthogonalisation formula and (
5
), it is deduced that

\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}=\|\mathbf{q}_{i}+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i-1}\beta_{i,j}\mathbf{q}_{j}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}=\|\mathbf{q}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i-1}|\beta_{i,j}|^{2}\|\mathbf{q}_{j}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}\geq\|\mathbf{q}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}.

(10)

Finally, the decrease in residual between two subsequent iterations of Algorithm
1
is bounded by

\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{i+1}\|_{\mathbf{W}}}{\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}}\leq\left[1-\frac{|\langle{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}_{i}},\mathbf{r}_{i}\rangle|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}}{\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}}\right]^{1/2}=\left[1-\frac{|\langle{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{r}_{i}},\mathbf{r}_{i}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}|^{2}}{\|\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}^{2}}\right]^{1/2},

Remark 1

(Breakdown and equivalence with WP-GMRES)
.

If

\mathbf{W}
-field of values of
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{A}
, the right hand side of the Elman estimate is
1
rendering it useless. In fact, the proof in
[

makes the assumption that the

is positive-definite so the case where

If
0

\mathbf{W}
-field of values of

is non-singular (a sufficient but not necessary condition). If an unlucky breakdown occurs in GCR, the algorithm can be restarted by computing the next few search directions as in the weighted and preconditioned Orthodir algorithm.

A very simple way of understanding that WP-GCR can breakdown is to consider taking
\mathbf{r}_{0}
such that
\langle\mathbf{r}_{0},\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{r}_{0}\rangle_{\mathbf{W}}=0
.

3.2
Restarted and Truncated versions

Within WP-GCR as well as WP-GMRES, the new search directions are orthogonalized against all previous ones. The cost of this procedure in terms of computation and storage can become prohibitive if the algorithm takes many iterations to converge. Well established variants of the algorithms have been proposed as early as in
[
13
,
45
]
where either the orthogonalization is truncated (weighted and preconditioned Orthomin(
k
), or WP-Orthomin(
k
)) or the algorithm is restarted every
k
iterations (WP-GMRES(
k
), WP-GCR(
k

, no orthogonalization is performed at all and the algorithm is called the weighted and preconditioned Minimal Residual iteration (WP-MR). A fact that is not so frequently known is that the convergence result given in Theorem

holds for all restarted and truncated versions of the algorithms. This was already fully understood by
[

]
.

Theorem 4

(Convergence of truncated and restarted versions)
.

The result in Theorem

Proof.

The numbering convention from
[

]
is followed.

k
) or WP-GMRES(

During the first cycle (
i.e.
, up to the computation of
\mathbf{r}_{k+1}
included), Theorem
3
applies. The second cycle consists in

. For

and this argument generalizes to any number of restarts.

WP-MR and WP-Orthomin(

)

These algorithms are defined by replacing the formulae for the search directions in Algorithm

by

for WP-MR and

∎

Equations (
9
) and (
10
) explain how orthogonalization helps: decreasing
\|\mathbf{q}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{W}}
leads to a decrease in the residual and orthogonalization does just that. Full orthogonalization provides the best choice of
\mathbf{q}_{i}
in
\mathbf{A}\mathcal{K}_{i}
. A very important realization is that the field of value convergence bound, as well as all the convergence bounds in this article do not account for the advantages provided by orthogonalization. For this reason they are expected to be over-pessimistic when applied to WP-GMRES and WP-GCR.

Two conclusions can be drawn from this. Either the decrease in residual predicted by the convergence bound is satisfactory, then WP-MR, or another truncated or restarted algorithm can be applied. Or, the decrease guaranteed by the theory is not satisfactory, then full WP-GCR/WP-GMRES can be applied with the hope that the bound is over-pessimistic. In practice this is very likely to be the case as WP-GCR/WP-GMRES often exhibits a superlinear convergence behaviour. The convergence bounds of the form presented in this article allow to check that the algorithm cannot stagnate (or near-stagnate), and the superlinear convergence behaviour should kick in.

All subsequent convergence proofs follow from manipulating the minimized quantity in Theorems
3
and
4
so they hold also for truncated and restarted versions of the algorithms.

4.1
Hermitian positive definite preconditioning

Two strong assumptions are made:

1.

2.

\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{H}

WP-GCR with right hpd preconditioning and

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbf{H}}

In application of Theorem
2
, WHP-GCR (Algorithm
3

\mathbf{q}_{i}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{p}_{i}
are pairwise orthogonal in the
\mathbf{H}
-inner product. Second, the residuals satisfy the minimization property:

\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{H}}=\operatorname{min}\left\{\|\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbf{H}};\,{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbf{x}_{0}+\mathcal{K}_{i}}\right\}\text{ if $0\not\in W_{\mathbf{H}}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{H})$}

\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}

(13)

\displaystyle\times\inf\limits_{\mathbf{y}\neq 0}\frac{|\langle\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle|}{\langle\mathbf{H}^{-1}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle}

\displaystyle\geq\inf\limits_{\mathbf{y}\neq 0}\frac{\langle\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A}^{-1})\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle}{\langle\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle}

\displaystyle\times\inf\limits_{\mathbf{y}\neq 0}\frac{\langle\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle}{\langle\mathbf{H}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle}

\displaystyle\times\inf\limits_{\mathbf{y}\neq 0}\frac{\langle\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle}{\langle\mathbf{H}^{-1}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle},

where division by
\langle\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle
is not by zero and removing the absolute values does not change the result. Indeed, by
[

[Property (1.6) on page 10],

is well defined and that

Let
\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A}))
and
\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A}))
denote the smallest and largest eigenvalues of
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})
. The eigenvalues of
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})
, are also the eigenvalues of the generalized eigenvalue problems
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{y}=\lambda\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{y}
and
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{H}\mathbf{y}=\lambda\mathbf{H}\mathbf{y}

\inf\limits_{\mathbf{y}\neq 0}\frac{\langle\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle}{\langle\mathbf{H}^{-1}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle}=\inf\limits_{\mathbf{y}\neq 0}\frac{\langle\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{H}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle}{\langle\mathbf{H}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle}=\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})).

and

\inf\limits_{\mathbf{y}\neq 0}\frac{\langle\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle}{\langle\mathbf{H}\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle}=\left(\sup\limits_{\mathbf{y}\neq 0}\frac{\langle\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle}{\langle\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}\rangle}\right)^{-1}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A}))},

For the remaining term, recalling from (
2

Lemma 1

Let

)
.

Assume that the operator
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
is positive definite. Assume also that the preconditioner
\mathbf{H}\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}
is hpd and that right preconditioned GCR is applied in the inner product induced by
\mathbf{H}
.
The
i
-th iterate of Algorithm
3
satisfies

\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}{\|\mathbf{r}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}\leq\left[1-\frac{\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A}))}{\lambda_{\max}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A}))}\times\frac{1}{1+\rho(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A}))^{2}}\right]^{i/2}.

(15)

The same result holds for all truncated and restarted versions of WHP-GCR and WHP-GMRES, including WHP-MR.

The estimate in the theorem has split the residual bound into two components:

•

the condition number of the Hermitian part of
\mathbf{A}
preconditioned by
\mathbf{H}
,

•

a measure of the strength of non-Hermitianness of
\mathbf{A}
that is independent of
\mathbf{H}
.

Corollary 2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
6
, if
\mathbf{H}
is a preconditioner for
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})
such that the condition number of the preconditioned operator is
\kappa(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A}))
then

\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}{\|\mathbf{r}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}\leq\left[1-\frac{\kappa(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A}))^{-1}}{1+\rho(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A}))^{2}}\right]^{i/2}.

(16)

The same result holds for all truncated and restarted versions of WHP-GCR and WHP-GMRES, including WHP-MR.

In particular, if
\mathbf{H}
is a domain decomposition preconditioner such that
\kappa(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A}))
can be bounded independently of the number of subdomains, then the convergence bound above does not depend on the number of subdomains either. In other words, if a scalable domain decomposition method is known for the Hermitian part of
\mathbf{A}
, the proposed algorithm for the non-Hermitian problem is also scalable. For many problems arising from the discretization of PDEs,
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})
is derived from a differential operator of lower order than that producing
\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A})
so
\rho(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A}))
is bounded independently of the discretization step (an observation already made in a different context in
[
46
]
and
[
14
]
[Section 10]). If
\mathbf{H}
can also be chosen so that the preconditioned Hermitian part is well conditioned independently of
h
then the residual bound is
h
-independent.

Next, it is illustrated that WHP-GCR is efficient for mildly non-Hermitian problems. If the preconditioner is well chosen, the algorithm is scalable and optimal (in the sense that convergence does not depend on the discretization step).

Illustration: Convection-Diffusion-Reaction

In this section, the problem considered is the convection-diffusion-reaction problem posed in
\Omega=[0,1]^{2}
. It is a real-valued problem (
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
), so Hermitian means symmetric. The presentation, notation and test case are inspired by
[
3
,
4
]
. The strong formulation of the problem is:

\displaystyle c_{0}u+\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{a}u)-\operatorname{div}(\nu\nabla u)

\displaystyle=f\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

The variational formulation is:
Find
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\underbrace{\int_{\Omega}\left(\left(c_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{div}\mathbf{a}\right)uv+\nu\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\right)}_{\text{symmetric part}}+\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}\cdot\nabla uv-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}\cdot\nabla vu\right)=\int_{\Omega}fv,

for all
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

The right hand side and the convection field are chosen as

f(x,y)=\operatorname{exp}(-10((x-0.5)^{2}+(y-0.1)^{2}))\text{ and }\mathbf{a}(x,y)=2\pi[-(y-0.1),x-0.5].

The reaction coefficient
c_{0}>0
and viscosity
\nu>0
are chosen to be constant over
\Omega
. Together with
\operatorname{div}\mathbf{a}=0
, positivity of
c_{0}
and
\nu
ensures that
\mathbf{A}
is positive-definite. Varying
c_{0}
and
\nu
inside
\Omega
would not cause any additional difficulty since the proposed preconditioner handles heterogeneous
c_{0}
and
\nu
(in the Hermitian part). The problem is discretized by Lagrange
\mathbb{P}_{1}
finite elements on a regular triangular mesh of characteristic length
h
. The WHP-GCR algorithm is implemented in FreeFem++
[
20
]
with the ffddm library
[
44
]
. Except in one case (where it is specified otherwise), all iteration counts for WHP-GCR correspond to the number of iterations needed to reach
\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{H}}<10^{-6}\|\mathbf{b}\|_{\mathbf{H}}
starting from a zero initial guess. The Dirichlet boundary condition has been enforced by penalization. The solution computed by a direct solve on a
501\times 501
degree of freedom (dof) mesh has been plotted in Figure
1
(left) for different values of
c_{0}=\nu=0.1
.

Figure 1
:
Left: Solution for
c_{0}=\nu=0.1

subdomains computed by Metis.

Let
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})
be the matrix corresponding to the discretization of the symmetric part of the problem.
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})
is preconditioned by the Additive Schwarz domain decomposition method with the GenEO coarse space
[
38
,
39
]
. The partition of
\Omega
into
N
subdomains
\Omega^{s}
is computed automatically by Metis. One layer of overlap is added to each
\Omega^{s}
. Letting
{\mathbf{R}^{s}}^{\top}
(
s=1,\dots,N
) denote the prolongation by zero of local finite element functions (in
\Omega^{s}
) to the whole of
\Omega
, the preconditioner can be written as:

\mathbf{H}=\boldsymbol{\Pi}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{N}{\mathbf{R}^{s}}^{\top}\underbrace{(\mathbf{R}^{s}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A}){\mathbf{R}^{s}}^{\top})^{-1}}_{\text{local solves}}\mathbf{R}^{s}\boldsymbol{\Pi}^{\top}+{\mathbf{R}^{0}}^{\top}\underbrace{(\mathbf{R}^{0}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A}){\mathbf{R}^{0}}^{\top})^{-1}}_{\text{coarse solve}}\mathbf{R}^{0},

where
\boldsymbol{\Pi}=\mathbf{{I}}-{\mathbf{R}^{0}}^{\top}(\mathbf{R}^{0}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A}){\mathbf{R}^{0}}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{R}^{0}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})
is the coarse projector (also known as a deflation operator) and the vectors in
\mathbf{R}_{0}^{\top}
span the coarse space (or deflation space). The particularity of GenEO is that the coarse vectors are constructed by solving the low frequency eigenmodes for a generalized eigenvalue problem in each subdomain. The user provides a threshold
\tau>0
,
e.g.
,
\tau=0.15
. The corresponding ffddm options are

-ffddm_schwarz_method asm

-ffddm_geneo_threshold 0.15

-ffddm_schwarz_coarse_correction BNN
.

The condition number of the resulting preconditioned operator is bounded by

\kappa(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A}))\leq k_{0}\left(1+\frac{k_{0}}{\tau}\right),

where
k_{0}
denotes the maximal number of subdomains that each mesh element belongs to
[
36
]
[Theorem 4.40]. This constant does not depend on the total number
N
of subdomains. In all examples
\tau
has been set to
0.15
. As an illustration, the partition into
N=16
subdomains provided by Metis is shown in Figure
1
(right). For this case, it holds that
k_{0}=3
. Consequently, the condition number of the preconditioned symmetric part of the problem is bounded from above by
\kappa(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A}))\leq 3(1+3/0.15)=63
.
Injecting this into the bound from Theorem
6
, it is obtained that the residuals produced by WHP-GCR or WHP-GMRES satisfy

\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}{\|\mathbf{r}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}\leq\left[1-\frac{0.016}{1+\rho(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A}))^{2}}\right]^{i/2}.

If, for example
\rho(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A}))\leq 1
, the bound gives

\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{i}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}{\|\mathbf{r}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}\leq{\sqrt{\left[1-\frac{1}{126}\right]}}\,^{i}=0.996^{i};\,\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{500}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}{\|\mathbf{r}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}\leq 0.14\text{ and }\frac{\|\mathbf{r}_{3468}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}{\|\mathbf{r}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{H}}}<1.0\cdot 10^{-6}.

The residual is guaranteed to decrease only by
0.4\%
at each iteration. As was previously explained, the bound is pessimistic for fully orthogonalized WP-GCR and WP-GMRES and we expect in practice to observe much faster decrease in residual.

Value of
\rho(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A}))

It remains to consider the value of
\rho(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A}))
. In the proof of
[
3
]
[Lemma 4.5], by Cauchy Schwarz and some arithmetic identities, it is proved that

\langle\mathbf{y},\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{x}\rangle\leq\alpha\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})}\|\mathbf{y}\|_{\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})},\quad\forall\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

with
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\|\mathbf{a}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{\sqrt{\inf(\nu)\inf(c_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{a}))}}
. Consequently, for
\mathbf{x}\neq\mathbf{0}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{x}\rangle^{1/2}\langle\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{x},\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{x}\rangle^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\alpha\|\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})}\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{x}\|_{\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})}.

The spectral radius of a matrix is bounded from above by any of its natural norms, and in particular by the norm induced by
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})
, from which it immediately follows that

\rho(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A}))\leq\|\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A})\|_{\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\|\mathbf{a}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{\sqrt{\inf(\nu)\inf(c_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{div}(\mathbf{a}))}}.

(17)

Bound (
17

\rho(\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A}))
does not depend on the discretization step
h
so neither does the overall convergence bound.

6
Conclusion

In this article, the convergence of GMRES, GCR and their truncated and restarted versions has been studied. The influence of the preconditioner and the inner product have been made explicit. It has been proposed, even for non-Hermitian problems to apply a hpd preconditioner
\mathbf{H}
. Then, GMRES or GCR can be applied in the
\mathbf{H}
inner product. This is referred to as WHP-GMRES. A new convergence result is proved for cases where
\mathbf{A}
is positive definite. The two terms in the convergence bound are the condition number of the Hermitian part of
\mathbf{A}
once preconditioned by
\mathbf{H}
and the spectral radius of
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{N}(\mathbf{A})
. This last term can be seen as a measure of the strength of non-Hermitianness. It does not depend on the choice of the preconditioner. A particular application is the case where
\mathbf{H}
is a domain decomposition preconditioner. If the preconditioner applied to the Hermitian part of
\mathbf{A}
leads to a scalable method, then WHP-GCR will be scalable too. For the Convection-Diffusion-Reaction problem, it has also been proved that convergence will not depend on the mesh size
h
as long as the condition number of the preconditioned symmetric part does not depend on
h
. Numerical results have confirmed these findings. It remains to improve the algorithm in cases where the problem is strongly non-Hermitian or indefinite.

Self-dual twisted generalized Reed-Solomon codes
Canze Zhu 1, Qunying Liao 2

Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper, by using some properties for linear algebra methods, the parity-check matrices for twisted generalized Reed-Solomon codes with any given hook
h
and twist
t
are presented, and then a sufficient and necessary condition for the twisted generalized Reed-Solomon code with
h\geq t
to be self-dual is given. Furthermore, several classes of self-dual codes with small Singleton defect are constructed based on twisted generalized Reed-Solomon codes, especially some of these self-dual codes are MDS or NMDS.

Keywords.

Twisted generalized Reed-Solomon codes; Self-dual codes; Small Singleton defect.

1
Introduction

In this paper, let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements and
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}=\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\{0\}
, where
q
is a prime power. An
[n,k,d]
linear code
\mathcal{C}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with minimum (Hamming) distance
d
and length
n
. For any
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
,
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, the inner product is defined as
\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\rangle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{i}
, and
the dual code of
\mathcal{C}
is defined as

\mathcal{C}^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{c}^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}~|~\langle\mathbf{c}^{{}^{\prime}},\mathbf{c}\rangle=0,\text{for any}~\mathbf{c}\in\mathcal{C}\}.

\mathcal{C}
is called self-dual code if
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
.
In recent years, study of self-dual maximum distance separable (in short, MDS) codes have attracted a lot of attention
[
1
,
4
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
18
,
19
,
28
,
29
,
31
]
. On the one hand, due to their nice structures, self-dual codes have attracted attention by coding theorists, cryptographers and mathematicians, or rather, self-dual codes have
various applications in cryptography (in particular secret sharing)
[
5
,
7
,
25
]
and combinatorics
[
16
,
24
]
. On the other hand, the MDS code not only
can correct maximal number of errors for a given code rate, but also are closely connected to combinatorial designs and finite geometry
[
16
,
24
]
. Thus, it is natural to consider the intersection of these two classes of codes, namely, self-dual MDS codes. Especially, generalized Reed-Solomon (in short, GRS) codes are a class of MDS codes and a lot of self-dual MDS codes from GRS codes are constructed in
[
8
,
17
,
21
,
32
,
33
]
.

For a non-negative integer
m
, the
m
-MDS code is introduced independently in
[
23
,
34
]
. A linear code
\mathcal{C}
is called an
m
-MDS code if both
\mathcal{C}
and its dual code have
m
Singleton defect from being an MDS code. Especially, for
m=0
or
1
,
\mathcal{C}
is called MDS or Near MDS (NMDS)
[
6
]
, respectively. It is easy to see that a self-dual code is an
m
-MDS code. For small
m
,
m
-MDS codes approximate maximal minimum Hamming distance for a given code rate. Furthermore,
m
-MDS codes also have been applied in secret sharing scheme
[
30
]
, the index coding problem
[
35
]
and the informed source coding problem
[
36
]
. From both theoretical and practical points of view, to study self-dual
m
-MDS codes with small
m
is interesting.

In 2017, inspired by twisted Gabidulin codes
[
27
]
, twisted Reed-Solomon (in short, TRS) codes were firstly introduced by Beelen et al. as a generalization for Reed-Solomon codes, they also showed that TRS codes could be well decoded. Furthermore, different from GRS codes, the twisted generalized Reed-Solomon (in short, TGRS) code is not necessarily MDS
[
2
]
. The authors also showed that most of TGRS MDS codes are not GRS codes
[
3
,
22
]
. Furthermore, basing on TGRS codes, Lavauzelle et al. presented an efficient key-recovery attack used in the McEliece cryptosystem
[
20
]
. TGRS codes are also used to construct linear complementary dual MDS codes by their applications in cryptography
[
14
,
22
]
. Recently, Huang et, al. gave the parity-check matrix for a TGRS code with hook
k-1
and twist
1
, and then constructed several classes of self-dual MDS or NMDS codes from these TGRS codes
[
15
]
.

In this paper, we extend the main results in
[
15
]
. In Section 2, some basic notations and results about TGRS codes are given. In Section 3, the parity-check matrix for the TGRS code with any given hook
h
and twist
t
is presented. In Section 4, a sufficient and necessary condition for the TGRS code with
h\geq t
to be self-dual is given, and then several classes of self-dual
m
-MDS codes with
m\leq t
are constructed. In Section 5, we conclude the whole paper.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we mainly give some notations and lemmas about
m
-MDS codes and TGRS codes.

For an
[n,k,d]
linear code
\mathcal{C}
, the singleton bound implies that
n+1-k-d\geq 0
, inspired by which, for a non-negative integer
m
, the
m
-MDS code is defined as follows.

Definition 2.1
(
[
6
,
23
,
33
]
)

For a linear code
\mathcal{C}
with parameters
[n,k,d]
, the Singleton defect is defined as
S(\mathcal{C})=n+1-k-d
.
\mathcal{C}
is called an
m
-MDS code if
S(\mathcal{C})=S(\mathcal{C^{\perp}})=m
. Especially,
\mathcal{C}
is called an MDS or NMDS code if
m=0
or
1
, respectively.

Some related definitions of TGRS codes are given in the following.

Definition 2.2
(
[
2
,
3
,
20
]
)

Let
t
,
h
and
k
be integers with
0\leq h<k\leq q
and
\eta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. Define the set of
(k,t,h,\eta)
-twisted polynomials as

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}=\Big\{f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}a_{i}x^{i}+\eta a_{h}x^{k-1+t}~|~a_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}~(i=0,\ldots,k-1)\Big\},

which is a
k
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear subspace. We call
h
the hook and
t
the twist.

Definition 2.3
(
[
2
,
3
,
20
]
)

Let
t
,
h
,
k
and
n
be positive integers and
\eta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. Let
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}

(i\neq j)
and

\bm{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*})^{n}
. The TGRS code with length
n
and dimension
k
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},t,h,\eta)=\{(v_{1}f({\alpha_{1}}),\ldots,v_{n}f(\alpha_{n}))~|~f(x)\in\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}\}.

Especially, for
\bm{1}=(1,\ldots,1)
,
\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1},t,h,\eta)
is called a TRS code.

By Definition
2.2
, it is easy to see that the generator matrix of
\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},t,h,\eta)
is

\displaystyle G_{k}(\bm{v})=\left(\begin{matrix}&v_{1}~&v_{2}~&\ldots~&v_{n}\\
&v_{1}\alpha_{1}~&v_{2}\alpha_{2}~&\ldots~&v_{n}\alpha_{n}\\
&\vdots~&\vdots&~&\vdots\\
&v_{1}\big(\alpha_{1}^{h}+\eta\alpha_{1}^{k-1+t}\big)~&v_{2}\big(\alpha_{2}^{h}+\eta\alpha_{2}^{k-1+t}\big)~&\ldots~&v_{n}\big(\alpha_{n}^{h}+\eta\alpha_{n}^{k-1+t}\big)\\
&\vdots~&\vdots&~&\vdots\\
&v_{1}\alpha_{1}^{k-1}~&v_{2}\alpha_{2}^{k-1}~&\cdots~&v_{n}\alpha_{n}^{k-1}\end{matrix}\right).

The following lemma gives a sufficient condition for a TGRS code to be MDS.

Lemma 2.1
(Theorem 17, in
[
2
]
)

Let
\mathbb{F}_{s}
be a proper subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{F}_{s}^{n}
. If
\eta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{s}
, then
\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},t,h,\eta)
is MDS.

The following lemma gives the bound for the minimum distance of
\mathcal{C}_{k,n}^{\perp}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},t,h,\eta)
.

Lemma 2.2

\mathcal{C}_{k,n}^{\perp}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},t,h,\eta)
is an
[n,n-k,d]
linear code with
h+1\leq d\leq k+1
.

Proof
. If
d\leq h
, then there are
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{d}\leq n
, such that

\displaystyle D(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})=\left(\begin{matrix}&1~&1~&\ldots~&1\\
&\vdots~&\vdots&~&\vdots\\
&\alpha_{i_{1}}^{h}+\eta\alpha_{i_{1}}^{k-1+t}~&\alpha_{i_{2}}^{h}+\eta\alpha_{i_{2}}^{k-1+t}~&\ldots~&\alpha_{i_{d}}^{h}+\eta\alpha_{i_{d}}^{k-1+t}\\
&\vdots~&\vdots&~&\vdots\\
&\alpha_{i_{1}}^{k-1}~&\alpha_{i_{2}}^{k-1}~&\cdots~&\alpha_{i_{d}}^{k-1}\end{matrix}\right),

with
d
linearly dependent columns, thus
\mathrm{Rank}\big(D(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})\big)<d
, which leads
\mathbf{1}
,
\tilde{\bm{\alpha}}
,
\ldots
,
\tilde{\bm{\alpha}}^{d-1}
are linearly dependent, where

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\alpha}}^{j}=(\alpha_{i_{1}}^{j},\alpha_{i_{2}}^{j},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{d}}^{j})\quad(j=1,\ldots,d-1).

Thus there exist not all zero elements
b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{d-1}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, such that
\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{d}}
are roots of the polynomial
f(x)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{d-1}b_{i}x^{i}
, it contradicts with
\deg f(x)\leq d-1
. Thus
d\geq h+1
. Furthermore, the upper bound of
d
follows from the Singleton bound directly.

\hfill\Box

It is easy to see that a self-dual code is always an
m
-MDS code, the following lemma gives the upper bound for the Singleton defect of the self-dual TGRS code.

Lemma 2.3

If
\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},t,h,\eta)
is self-dual, then it is an
m
-MDS code with

m\leq\min\{t,k-h\}.

Proof
. By Lemma
2.2
, we have

S(\mathcal{C}_{k,n}^{\perp}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},t,h,\eta))\leq k+1-(h+1)=k-h.

Furthermore, by the definition of the TGRS code, one has

S(\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},t,h,\eta))\leq t.

Thus if
\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},t,h,\eta)
is self-dual, then

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad S(\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},t,h,\eta))=S(\mathcal{C}_{k,n}^{\perp}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},t,h,\eta))\leq\min\{t,k-h\}.\qquad\qquad\hfill\Box

The following lemma gives a sufficient and necessary condition for
\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},1,k-1,\eta)
to be MDS or NMDS, respectively.

Lemma 2.4
(Lemma 2.6 in
[
15
]
)

For positive integers
k
and
n
with
k<n
and
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}

(i\neq j)
, denote

S_{k}(1,\bm{\alpha})=\Big\{\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}~\bigg|~\forall I\subsetneq\{1,\ldots,n\}\text{~with~}|I|=k\Big\},

then we have

(1)

\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},1,k-1,\eta)
is an MDS code if and only if
\eta^{-1}\notin S_{k}(1,\bm{\alpha})
;

(2)

\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},1,k-1,\eta)
is an NMDS code if and only if
\eta^{-1}\in S_{k}(1,\bm{\alpha})
.

In the following lemma, a sufficient and necessary condition for the Singleton defect of
\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},2,k-2,\eta)
equal to
0,1
or
2
is given, respectively.

Lemma 2.5

For positive integers
k
and
n
with
k<n
and
\bm{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}

(i\neq j)
, denote

\displaystyle S_{k}(2,\bm{\alpha})=\left\{\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{i,j\in I}\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}-\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{i,j,l\in I}\\
{(i-j)(i-l)(j-l)\neq 0}\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{l}~\bigg|~\forall I\subsetneq\{1,\ldots,n\}\text{~with~}|I|=k\right\}

and

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{k}(2,\bm{\alpha})=\left\{-\!\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{i,j,l\in\tilde{I}}\\
{(i-j)(i-l)(j-l)\neq 0}\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{l}~\bigg|~\forall\tilde{I}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\text{~with~}|\tilde{I}|=k+1~\text{and}~\sum\limits_{i\in\tilde{I}}\alpha_{i}=0\right\},

then we have

(1)

\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},2,k-2,\eta)
is an MDS code if and only if
\eta^{-1}\notin S_{k}(2,\bm{\alpha})
;

(2)

S\big(\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},2,k-2,\eta)\big)=1
if and only if
\eta^{-1}\in S_{k}(2,\bm{\alpha})\backslash\tilde{S}_{k}(2,\bm{\alpha})
;

(3)

S\big(\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},2,k-2,\eta)\big)=2
if and only if
\eta^{-1}\in S_{k}(2,\bm{\alpha})\cap\tilde{S}_{k}(2,\bm{\alpha})
.

The proof for Lemma
2.5
. By the definition of the TGRS code, it is easy to see that
S\big(\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},2,k-2,\eta)\big)\leq 2
.
In the following, we firstly give the proof for
(1)
, and then show that
(3)
holds, finally, by
(1)
and
(3)
, we can get
(2)
directly.

For the convenience, we denote
w_{H}(\mathbf{c}_{f})
to be the Hamming weight for a codeword
\mathbf{c}_{f}=\big(f(\alpha_{1}),\ldots,f(\alpha_{n})\big)\in\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},2,k-2,\eta)
.

The proof for
(1)
. It is enough to prove that the converse-negative proposition for
(1)
holds. In fact, if
S\big(\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},2,k-2,\eta)\big)\neq 0
, then there exists a
I\subsetneq\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
|I|=k
and
f(x)=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{k-1}x^{k-1}+\eta a_{k-2}x^{k+1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]\backslash\{0\}
, such that
f(\alpha_{i})=0~(\forall i\in I)
,
which implies
a_{k-2}\neq 0
. Thus there exists some
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that

\displaystyle f(x)=\eta a_{k-2}(x-b)\prod_{i\in I}(x-\alpha_{i}).

It leads that

\displaystyle-b-\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}=0\quad\text{and}\quad-\eta\left(b\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{i,j\in I}\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{i,j,l\in I}\\
{(i-j)(i-l)(j-l)\neq 0}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{l}\right)=1,

and then
\eta^{-1}\in S_{k}(2,\bm{\alpha})
.

Conversely, for
\eta^{-1}\in S_{k}(2,\bm{\alpha})
, there exists some
I\subsetneq\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
|I|=k
, such that

\displaystyle\eta=\left(\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{i,j\in I}\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{i,j,l\in I}\\
{(i-j)(i-l)(j-l)\neq 0}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{l}\right)^{-1},

thus
f(x)=\eta(x+\sum\limits_{i\in I}\alpha_{i})\prod\limits_{i\in I}(x-\alpha_{i})\in\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}
and

w_{H}(\bm{c}_{f})\leq n-k
, which means that

S\big(\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},2,k-2,\eta)\big)\neq 0.

By the above discussions,
S\big(\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},2,k-2,\eta)\big)=0
if and only if
\eta^{-1}\notin S_{k}(2,\bm{\alpha})
.

The proof for
(3)
. If
S(\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},2,k-2,\eta))=2
, then there exist some
\tilde{I}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
|\tilde{I}|=k+1
and
f(x)=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{k-1}x^{k-1}+\eta a_{k-2}x^{k+1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]\backslash\{0\}
, such that
f(\alpha_{i})=0~(\forall i\in\tilde{I})
,
which implies
a_{k-2}\neq 0
, thus

\displaystyle f(x)=\eta a_{k-2}\prod_{i\in\tilde{I}}(x-\alpha_{i}),

and then

\displaystyle\eta^{-1}=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{i,j,l\in\tilde{I}}\\
{(i-j)(i-l)(j-l)\neq 0}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{l}.

Conversely, for
\eta^{-1}\in\tilde{S}_{k}(2,\bm{\alpha})
, there exist some
\tilde{I}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
|\tilde{I}|=k+1
, such that

\displaystyle\eta=\left(-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{i,j,l\in\tilde{I}}\\
{(i-j)(i-l)(j-l)\neq 0}\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}\alpha_{l}\right)^{-1},

thus
f(x)=\eta\prod\limits_{i\in\tilde{I}}(x-\alpha_{i})\in\mathcal{V}_{k,t,h,\eta}
and

w_{H}(\bm{c}_{f})\leq n-k-1
, which leads

S\big(\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},2,k-2,\eta)\big)=2.

So far, we know that
S\big(\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},2,k-2,\eta)\big)=2
if and only if

\eta^{-1}\in S_{k}(2,\bm{\alpha})\cap\tilde{S}_{k}(2,\bm{\alpha}).

The proof for
(2)
. By
(1)
and
(3)
, we get
(2)
directly.
\hfill\Box

3
The parity-check matrix for the TGRS code

By the theory of Vandermonde matrices
[
26
]
, one has the following lemma, which is necessary to calculate the parity-check matrix for the code
\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},t,h,\eta)
.

Lemma 3.1

Denote

\displaystyle G=\left(\begin{matrix}&1~&1~&\ldots~&1\\
&\alpha_{1}~&\alpha_{2}~&\ldots~&\alpha_{n}\\
&\vdots~&\vdots&~&\vdots\\
&\alpha_{1}^{n-1}~&\alpha_{2}^{n-1}~&\cdots~&\alpha_{n}^{n-1}\end{matrix}\right),

then for the system of equations over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

\displaystyle G(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n})^{T}=(0,0,\ldots,1)^{T},

(3.1)

there is an unique solution
(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n})^{T}
, where
u_{i}=\prod\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})^{-1}

(1\leq i\leq n)
.

The parity-check matrix for
\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},t,h,\eta)
is given in Theorem
3.1
. In order to give the proof, we need the following nonations.

For
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
1\leq i_{1},i_{2}\leq n
and
n\times n
matrix
M
, let
P\big(i_{1},i_{2}(r)\big)
,
Q\big(i_{1},i_{2}(r)\big)
and
T\big(i_{1},i_{2}\big)
be
n\times n
elementary matrices satisfying the following conditions, respectively.

\bullet
the
i_{1}
th row of
P\big(i_{1},i_{2}(r)\big)M
is replaced by the sum of
r
times the
i_{2}
th row and the
i_{1}
th row of
M
;

\bullet
the
i_{1}
th column of
MQ\big(i_{1},i_{2}(r)\big)
is replaced by the sum of
r
times the
i_{2}
th column and the
i_{1}
th column of
M
;

\bullet

MT(i_{1},i_{2})
that exchanges the
i_{1}
th column and the
i_{2}
th column of
M
.

Theorem 3.1

For any integer
m
, let
L_{m}=\sum\limits_{l=1}^{n}u_{l}\alpha_{l}^{n-1+m}
and
u_{i}=\prod\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})^{-1}

(i=1,\ldots,n)
, then
\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},t,h,\eta)
has the parity-check matrix

H_{n-k}(\bm{v})=\left(\begin{matrix}\bm{\beta}_{1},\bm{\beta}_{2},\ldots,\bm{\beta}_{n}\end{matrix}\right),

where

\displaystyle\bm{\beta}_{j}=\left(\begin{matrix}&\frac{u_{j}}{v_{j}}\\
&\frac{u_{j}}{v_{j}}\alpha_{j}\\
&\vdots\\
&\frac{u_{j}}{v_{j}}\alpha_{j}^{n-(k+t+1)}\\
&\frac{u_{j}}{v_{j}}\Big(\alpha_{j}^{n-(h+1)}-\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k-h-1}L_{m}\alpha_{j}^{n-(h+1)-m}+\tilde{L}\alpha_{j}^{n-(k+t)}\Big)\\
&\frac{u_{j}}{v_{j}}\big(\alpha_{j}^{n-(k+t-1)}-L_{1}\alpha_{j}^{n-(k+t)}\big)\\
&\vdots\\
&\frac{u_{j}}{v_{j}}\big(\alpha_{j}^{n-(k+1)}-L_{t-1}\alpha_{j}^{n-(k+t)}\big)\\
\end{matrix}\right)_{(n-k)\times 1}

and

\displaystyle\tilde{L}=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k-h-1}L_{m}L_{k+t-h-1-m}-\eta^{-1}(1+\eta L_{k+t-h-1}).

Proof
. It is easy to see that

\displaystyle H_{n-k}(\bm{v})=\left(\begin{matrix}&v_{1}^{-1}~&0~&\ldots~&0\\
&0~&v_{2}^{-1}~&\ldots~&0\\
&\vdots~&\vdots~&~&\vdots\\
&0~&0~&\ldots~&v_{n}^{-1}\\
\end{matrix}\right)H_{n-k}(\mathbf{1}).

(3.2)

Thus it is enough to investigate the parity-check matrix
H_{n-k}(\mathbf{1})
for
\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{1},t,h,\eta)
.
Let

\displaystyle G=\left(\begin{matrix}&1~&1~&\ldots~&1\\
&\alpha_{1}~&\alpha_{2}~&\ldots~&\alpha_{n}\\
&\vdots~&\vdots&~&\vdots\\
&\alpha_{1}^{n-1}~&\alpha_{2}^{n-1}~&\cdots~&\alpha_{n}^{n-1}\end{matrix}\right)

and

\displaystyle H=\left(\begin{matrix}&u_{1}\alpha_{1}^{n-1}~&\ldots~&u_{1}\alpha_{1}^{n-k-1}~&\ldots~&u_{1}\\
&u_{2}\alpha_{2}^{n-1}~&\ldots~&u_{2}\alpha_{2}^{n-k-1}~&\ldots~&u_{2}\\

4

4.1
A sufficient and necessary condition for the TGRS code to be self-dual

Before giving a sufficient and necessary condition for
\mathcal{C}_{k,n}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{v},t,h,\eta)
(h\geq t)
to be self-dual, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1

For any positive integers
m
and
s
with
s\geq 2
, let
u_{i}=\prod\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})^{-1}
,
L_{m}=\sum\limits_{l=1}^{n}u_{l}\alpha_{l}^{n-1+m}
and

\sigma_{i}=(-1)^{n-i}\sum\limits_{1\leq l_{1}<\cdots<l_{n-i}\leq n}\alpha_{l_{1}}\cdots\alpha_{l_{n-i}}~(0\leq i\leq n-1).
Then the following two assertions hold.

(1)

L_{1}=\cdots=L_{s-1}=0
if and only if
\sigma_{n-1}=\cdots=\sigma_{n-(s-1)}=0
.

(2)
If
L_{1}=\cdots=L_{s-1}=0
, then
L_{2s-1}=-\sigma_{n-(2s-1)}
.

Proof
.
(1)
Let
P(x)=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}(x-\alpha_{i})=x^{n}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}\sigma_{i}x^{i}
, note that

P(\alpha_{i})=0~(i=1,\ldots,n)
, thua we have
\alpha_{i}^{n}=-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}\sigma_{j}\alpha_{i}^{j}
. Now by Lemma
3.1
, for any
1\leq m\leq n
, it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} L_{m}&=-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\sigma_{j}\sum_{l=1}^{n}u_{l}\alpha_{l}^{j+m-1}=-\sum_{j=n-m}^{n-1}\sigma_{j}\sum_{l=1}^{n}u_{l}\alpha_{l}^{j+m-1}=-\sigma_{n-m}-\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\sigma_{n-m+j}L_{j}.\end{aligned}

(4.1)

If
L_{1}=\cdots=L_{s-1}=0
, then
(\ref{lm})
yields that

\displaystyle L_{i}=-\sigma_{n-i}-\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\sigma_{n-i+j}L_{j}=-\sigma_{n-i}~(1\leq i\leq s-1),

thus
\sigma_{n-1}=\cdots=\sigma_{n-(s-1)}=0
.
Conversely, if
a_{n-1}=\cdots=a_{n-(s-1)}=0
, then
(\ref{lm})
yields
L_{1}=\cdots=L_{s-1}=0
directly.

(2)
If
L_{1}=\cdots=L_{s-1}=0
, by
(1)
, we can get

\displaystyle L_{2s-1}

\displaystyle=-\sigma_{n-(2s-1)}-\sum_{j=1}^{2s-2}\sigma_{n-(2s-1)+j}L_{j}

\displaystyle=-\sigma_{n-(2s-1)}-\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}\sigma_{n-(2s-1)+j}L_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}\sigma_{n-s+j}L_{s-1+j}

\displaystyle=-\sigma_{n-(2s-1)}.

\hfill\Box

Conclusions

In

]
, the authors gave the parity-check matrix for

In our works, we give the following main results for

15
]

(1)
The parity-check matrix for

A sufficient and necessary condition for

(3)
Construction for several classes of self-dual
m

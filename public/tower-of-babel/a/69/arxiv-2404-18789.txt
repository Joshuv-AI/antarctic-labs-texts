Abstract
A recent combination of measurements performed by the LHCb collaboration determined that γ = ( 63.8 − 3.7 + 3.5 ) ∘ \gamma=(63.8^{+3.5}_{-3.7})^{\circ} . The fit combined the results of γ \gamma and charm measurements, which resulted in precision improvements for the strong-phase difference between D 0 → K − ​ π + {{\mathrm{D}}^{0}}\to{{\mathrm{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}} and D 0 → K + ​ π − {{\mathrm{D}}^{0}}\to{{\mathrm{K}}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}} decays, δ K ​ π D \delta_{{{\mathrm{K}}}\pi}^{\mathrm{D}} , and the D 0 {\mathrm{D}}^{0} – D ¯ 0 {\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt\mathrm{D}}}{}^{0} mixing parameter, y y . In addition, new LHCb measurements of the CKM angle γ \gamma using B 0 → DK ∗ 0 {{\mathrm{B}}^{0}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{*0}} and B − → D ∗ ​ K − {{{\mathrm{B}}^{-}}}\to{{\mathrm{D}}^{*}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{-}} decays with the D → K S 0 ​ h + ​ h − {\mathrm{D}}\to{{\mathrm{K}}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{\mathrm{h}}^{+}{\mathrm{h}}^{-} final state (where h = π , K {\mathrm{h}}=\pi,{{\mathrm{K}}} ) are presented.

A key goal of flavour physics is to test the unitarity of the CKM matrix by overconstraining the Unitarity Triangle (UT). The angle
\gamma
is particularly interesting because it can be measured with a negligible theoretical uncertainty using tree-level decays
[
1
]
. The direct measurements of
\gamma
are compared to indirect determinations from global fits to parameters related to the other angles and sides of the UT
[
2
,
3
]
that are measured in decays with additional loop diagrams. Discrepancies between the direct and indirect measurements of
\gamma
would be a sign of beyond Standard Model effects.

The angle
\gamma
is most precisely measured using
{{{\mathrm{B}}^{-}}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{-}}
decays, where
\mathrm{D}
is a superposition of
{\mathrm{D}}^{0}
and
{\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt\mathrm{D}}}{}^{0}
mesons. The ratio of amplitudes for the two decay paths is

r_{\mathrm{B}}e^{i(\delta_{\mathrm{B}}-\gamma)}=\frac{\rm{A}({{{\mathrm{B}}^{-}}}\to{{\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt\mathrm{D}}}{}^{0}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{-}})}{\rm{A}({{{\mathrm{B}}^{-}}}\to{{\mathrm{D}}^{0}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{-}})},

(1.1)

where
r_{\mathrm{B}}
and
\delta_{\mathrm{B}}
are the amplitude ratio and strong-phase difference between the decays. The angle
\gamma
can be determined by exploiting interference between
{{{\mathrm{B}}^{-}}}\to{{\mathrm{D}}^{0}}(\to\mathrm{f}){{\mathrm{K}}^{-}}
and
{{{\mathrm{B}}^{-}}}\to{{\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt\mathrm{D}}}{}^{0}}(\to\mathrm{f}){{\mathrm{K}}^{-}}
decays, where
\mathrm{f}
is a common final state to both
{\mathrm{D}}^{0}
and
{\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt\mathrm{D}}}{}^{0}
mesons. The same equations above apply to
{{{\mathrm{B}}^{+}}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{+}}
decays under the exchange
\gamma\to-\gamma
. The procedure can be generalised to
{\mathrm{B}}^{0}
and
{\mathrm{B}}^{0}_{\rm{s}}
decays, because the sensitivity to
\gamma
originates from the interference between
{\rm{b}}\to{\rm{u}}
and
{\rm{b}}\to{\rm{c}}
quark transitions that is shared by all
\mathrm{B}
-meson decays into a mixture of
{\mathrm{D}}^{0}
and
{\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt\mathrm{D}}}{}^{0}
decays.

The squared amplitude for the
{{{\mathrm{B}}^{-}}}\to{\mathrm{D}}(\to\mathrm{f}){{\mathrm{K}}^{-}}
decay is

|\rm{A}({{{\mathrm{B}}^{-}}})|^{2}\propto\rm{A}_{\mathrm{D}}^{2}+\rm{r}_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}\rm{A}_{{\kern 0.79727pt\overline{\kern-0.79727pt\mathrm{D}}}}^{2}+2\rm{A}_{\mathrm{D}}\rm{A}_{{\kern 0.79727pt\overline{\kern-0.79727pt\mathrm{D}}}}\rm{r}_{\mathrm{B}}\cos(\delta_{\mathrm{B}}+\delta_{\mathrm{D}}-\gamma),

(1.2)

where
\rm{A}_{\mathrm{D}}
and
\rm{A}_{{\kern 0.79727pt\overline{\kern-0.79727pt\mathrm{D}}}}
are the magnitude of the amplitudes for the
{{\mathrm{D}}^{0}}\to\mathrm{f}
and
{{\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt\mathrm{D}}}{}^{0}}\to\mathrm{f}
decays, respectively, and
\delta_{\mathrm{D}}
is the strong-phase difference between them. Therefore, external knowledge of the
\mathrm{D}
decay amplitudes is used to extract
\gamma
. The specific inputs depend on the final state of the
\mathrm{D}
decay, and are most often expressed in terms of the amplitude ratio,
r_{\mathrm{D}}
, and strong-phase difference,
\delta_{\mathrm{D}}
, between the
{{\mathrm{D}}^{0}}\to\mathrm{f}
and
{{\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt\mathrm{D}}}{}^{0}}\to\mathrm{f}
decays. The reverse scenario is also possible, such that the results of the
\gamma
analyses can be used to constrain the values of
\delta_{\mathrm{D}}
and
r_{\mathrm{D}}
. This is particularly useful for the
{{\mathrm{D}}^{0}}\to{{\mathrm{K}}^{-}}{{\pi}^{+}}
final state, because the determination of the
{\mathrm{D}}^{0}
–
{\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt\mathrm{D}}}{}^{0}
mixing parameters,
x
and
y
, in this decay channel require knowledge of the strong-phase difference
\delta_{{{\mathrm{K}}}\pi}^{\mathrm{D}}

[
4
]
. In 2021, the
LHCb
collaboration combined the results of the
\gamma
measurements with those in the charm quark sector, which determined a noticeable improvement in the precision of
\delta_{{{\mathrm{K}}}\pi}^{\mathrm{D}}
and
y
, as is displayed in Fig.
1

[
5
]
.

2
Measuring
\gamma
with
{\mathrm{D}}\to{{\mathrm{K}}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{\mathrm{h}}^{+}{\mathrm{h}}^{-}
final states

The amplitude for the
{\mathrm{D}}\to{{\mathrm{K}}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{\mathrm{h}}^{+}{\mathrm{h}}^{-}
decay is phase-space dependent due to intermediate resonances. It is found that the sensitivity to
\gamma
is optimised by studying regions of this phase space, which is categorised by the squared invariant masses of the
{{\mathrm{K}}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{\mathrm{h}}^{+}
and
{{\mathrm{K}}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{\mathrm{h}}^{-}
pairs, labelled
m_{+}^{2}
and
m_{-}^{2}
, respectively
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
. The phase space is divided into
2\mathcal{N}
regions labelled from
i=-\mathcal{N}
to
i=\mathcal{N}
(excluding 0), and is symmetrical about the line
m_{-}^{2}=m_{+}^{2}
, where a region with
m_{-}^{2}>m_{+}^{2}
(
m_{-}^{2}<m_{+}^{2}
) is the
i^{th}
(
-i^{th}
) bin. For the
{\mathrm{D}}\to{{\mathrm{K}}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\pi}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
(
{\mathrm{D}}\to{{\mathrm{K}}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{+}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{-}}
) decay a scheme with
\mathcal{N}=8
(
\mathcal{N}=2
) regions is used
[
15
]
.

The signal yields in a phase-space region,
i
, are given by

\displaystyle\rm{N}_{i}({{{\mathrm{B}}^{-}}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{-}})=h^{{{{\mathrm{B}}^{-}}}}\left[\rm{F}_{i}+(x_{-}^{2}+y_{-}^{2})\rm{F}_{-i}+2\sqrt{\rm{F}_{i}\rm{F}_{-i}}\left(\rm{c}_{i}x_{-}+\rm{s}_{i}y_{-}\right)\right],

(2.1)

\displaystyle\rm{N}_{i}({{{\mathrm{B}}^{+}}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{+}})=h^{{{{\mathrm{B}}^{+}}}}\left[\rm{F}_{-i}+(x_{+}^{2}+y_{+}^{2})\rm{F}_{i}+2\sqrt{\rm{F}_{i}\rm{F}_{-i}}\left(\rm{c}_{i}x_{+}-\rm{s}_{i}y_{+}\right)\right],

(2.2)

where
x_{\pm}
and
y_{\pm}
are the
C\!P
violation observables defined as
x_{\pm}=r_{\mathrm{B}}\cos(\delta_{\mathrm{B}}\pm\gamma)
and
y_{\pm}=r_{\mathrm{B}}\sin(\delta_{\mathrm{B}}\pm\gamma)
. Furthermore, the
h^{{{\mathrm{B}}^{\pm}}}
are normalisation factors which absorb the detector effects and production asymmetries, the
\rm{F}_{i}
parameters are the efficiency corrected probabilities for a
{{\mathrm{D}}^{0}}\to{{\mathrm{K}}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{\mathrm{h}}^{+}{\mathrm{h}}^{-}
decay in a phase-space region,
i
, and the parameters
\rm{c}_{i}
and
\rm{s}_{i}
are given by integrals over the
\mathrm{D}
decay amplitude averaged cosines and sines of the strong-phase difference,
\delta_{\mathrm{D}}
,

\displaystyle\rm{K}_{i}

\displaystyle=\int_{i}\mathrm{d}m_{-}^{2}\mathrm{d}m_{+}^{2}\,|\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{D}}(m_{-}^{2},m_{+}^{2})|^{2},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\rm{c}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{K_{i}K_{-i}}}\int_{i}\mathrm{d}m_{-}^{2}\mathrm{d}m_{+}^{2}\,|\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{D}}(m_{-}^{2},m_{+}^{2})||\mathrm{A}_{{\kern 0.81949pt\overline{\kern-0.81949pt\mathrm{D}}}}(m_{-}^{2},m_{+}^{2})|\cos\delta_{\mathrm{D}}(m_{-}^{2},m_{+}^{2}),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\rm{s}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rm{K}_{i}\rm{K}_{-i}}}\int_{i}\mathrm{d}m_{-}^{2}\mathrm{d}m_{+}^{2}\,|\mathrm{A}_{\mathrm{D}}(m_{-}^{2},m_{+}^{2})||\mathrm{A}_{{\kern 0.81949pt\overline{\kern-0.81949pt\mathrm{D}}}}(m_{-}^{2},m_{+}^{2})|\sin\delta_{\mathrm{D}}(m_{-}^{2},m_{+}^{2}).

(2.5)

The
\gamma
measurements presented in these proceedings do not rely on amplitude models for the
\rm{c}_{i}
and
\rm{s}_{i}
inputs. Instead, they are determined using quantum-correlated
{{\mathrm{D}}^{0}}{\kern-0.70007pt{{\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt\mathrm{D}}}{}^{0}}}
pairs produced at charm factories
[
16
,
17
]
. In each measurement, fits to the
\mathrm{B}
and
{\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt\mathrm{B}}}
meson invariant mass distributions in each phase-space region are performed with the signal yields parameterised by equations Eqs.
2.1
and
2.2
to extract values for the
C\!P
violation observables and thus
\gamma
,
r_{\mathrm{B}}
and
\delta_{\mathrm{B}}
.

3
Measuring
\gamma
in
{{\mathrm{B}}^{0}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{*0}}(892)
decays

The measurement of
\gamma
using
{{\mathrm{B}}^{0}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{*0}}(892)
decays
[
7
]
is performed without accounting for time dependence because the flavour of the
{\mathrm{B}}^{0}
meson at decay is unambiguously tagged by the charge on the kaon from the
{{\mathrm{K}}^{*0}}\to{{\mathrm{K}}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
decay. Both paths to the
{{\mathrm{B}}^{0}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{*0}}
decay final state are colour suppressed, whereas only one is in
{{{\mathrm{B}}^{-}}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{-}}
decays. Therefore, the interference in
{{\mathrm{B}}^{0}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{*0}}
decays is expected to be around 3 times larger and thus each event has a higher sensitivity to
\gamma
compared to
{{{\mathrm{B}}^{-}}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{-}}
.

, compared to that of
{{\mathrm{B}}^{0}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{*0}}

. The asymmetry is highlighted on the plot.

Criteria are imposed to select the
{{\mathrm{B}}^{0}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{*0}}
resonance in the
{{\mathrm{B}}^{0}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{+}}{{\pi}^{-}}
phase space. However, non-signal decays still remain in this region which need to be accounted for. This is achieved by introducing a coherence factor,
\kappa=0.958^{+0.005}_{-0.046}

[
18
]
, which dilutes the interference terms in Eqs.
2.1
and
2.2
. In the fit to data, the
\rm{F}_{i}
values are fixed to those determined in
{{{\mathrm{B}}^{-}}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\pi}^{-}}
decays determined in Ref.
[
19
]
. The relative efficiency differences across the phase space between
{{\mathrm{B}}^{0}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{*0}}
and
{{{\mathrm{B}}^{-}}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\pi}^{-}}
decays are examined in simulation and found to be small. Figure
3
compares the fit to
{{\mathrm{B}}^{0}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{*0}}
candidates in the phase-space region
i=1
, with that of
{{\kern 1.00006pt\overline{\kern-1.00006pt\mathrm{B}}}{}^{0}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\overline{{\mathrm{K}}}}^{*0}}
candidates in the phase-space region
i=-1
, to provide an example of the
C\!P
asymmetry. The analysis determines that

\displaystyle\gamma=(49^{+22}_{-19})^{\circ},

\displaystyle r_{{\mathrm{B}}^{0}}=0.271^{+0.065}_{-0.066},

\displaystyle\delta_{{\mathrm{B}}^{0}}=(236^{+19}_{-21})^{\circ},

where the uncertainties are statistically dominated. The values presented supersede those determined using a smaller dataset in Ref.
[
20
]
.
The measurement is required to break the four fold degeneracy of
\gamma
values from measurements of
{{\mathrm{B}}^{0}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{*0}}
decays with
\mathrm{D}
decays to 2- and 4-body final states presented in Ref.
[
8
]
. The combined precision is around
7^{\circ}
, with a best-fit value that will reduce the tensions with measurements performed using
{{\mathrm{B}}^{\pm}}
decays.

Measuring
\gamma
using
{{\mathrm{B}}^{\pm}}\to{{\mathrm{D}}^{*}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{\pm}}
decays

Two new measurements of
\gamma
using
{{{\mathrm{B}}^{-}}}\to{{\mathrm{D}}^{*}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{-}}
decays have recently been performed by the
LHCb
collaboration. The study presented in Ref.
[
10
]
reconstructs the full decay chain, whilst that described in Ref.
[
9
]
does not reconstruct the soft neutral particle from the
{{\mathrm{D}}^{*}}\to{\mathrm{D}}X

X={{\pi}^{0}},\gamma

LHCb

In each measurement,
{{{\mathrm{B}}^{-}}}\to{{\mathrm{D}}^{*}}{{\pi}^{-}}

\gamma
. Instead, they are used to determine the
\rm{F}_{i}
values which are simultaneously fitted alongside the
C\!P
violation observables.

Summary

The
LHCb
collaboration continues to drive precision on the CKM angle
\gamma
, with a current average value, determined in combination with charm measurements, of
\gamma=(63.8^{+3.5}_{-3.7})^{\circ}

[
5
]
. New
LHCb
measurements will be included in the next combination, and those that use the self-conjugate
{\mathrm{D}}\to{{\mathrm{K}}^{0}_{\mathrm{S}}}{\mathrm{h}}^{+}{\mathrm{h}}^{-}
, where
{\mathrm{h}}=\pi,{{\mathrm{K}}}
, final state are presented in these proceedings. An analysis of
{{\mathrm{B}}^{0}}\to{\mathrm{D}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{*0}}
decays determined that
\gamma=(49^{+22}_{-19})^{\circ}

[
7
]
and will reduce tensions between measurements made using
{{\mathrm{B}}^{\pm}}
and
{{\mathrm{B}}^{0}}
decays in the next combination. Finally, two new
LHCb
measurements performed using
{{\mathrm{B}}^{\pm}}\to{{\mathrm{D}}^{*}}{{\mathrm{K}}^{\pm}}
decays
[
9
,
10
]
for the first time provide additional sensitivity to
\gamma
, as is necessary for a more precise test of CKM unitarity.

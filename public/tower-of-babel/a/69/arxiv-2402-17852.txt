Descending finite projective modules from a Novikov ring
Dongryul Kim

Abstract
. We prove a descent result for finite projective modules, motivated by a question in perfectoid geometry. Given a commutative ring A A , we formulate a descent problem for descending a finite projective module over the Novikov ring with coefficients in A A to a finite projective module over A A . The main theorem of this paper is that all such descent data are effective. As an application, we prove for every perfect 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} -algebra A A , a vector bundle on Spd ⁡ A \operatorname{Spd}A always descends to a vector bundle on Spec ⁡ A \operatorname{Spec}A .



1.
Introduction

The goal of this paper is to prove the following descent result. We refer the
reader to Section
2
for the definition of the rings and maps
involved.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
4.9
)
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring and let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an
additive submonoid. Then the category of descent data of finite projective
modules along the semi-cosimplicial diagram

A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)\rightrightarrows A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma})\!)\rightrightarrows\hskip-10.00002pt\to A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma},v^{\Gamma})\!)

is equivalent to the category of finite projective
A
-modules.

Remark 1.2
.

In general, the ring
A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma})\!)
is much larger than the tensor product

A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)\otimes_{A}A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)
, which is usually where the isomorphism of
the descent datum is defined. As a consequence, standard descent results
for finite projective modules are inapplicable to this setting.

The theorem is motivated by question in perfectoid geometry, namely that of
descending a vector bundle from
\Spd A
to a vector bundle on
\Spec A
, where

A
is a discrete perfect ring in characteristic
p
. Using
Theorem
4.9
with
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}[p^{-1}]
, we deduce the
following result as an immediate consequence.

Theorem 1.3

(Corollary
5.2
,
5.4
)
.

Let
A
be a perfect
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra. Then the natural functor

\{\text{vector bundles on }\Spec A\}\to\{\text{vector
bundles on }\Spd A\}

is an exact tensor equivalence. Similarly, the natural functor

\{\text{schemes finite \'{e}tale over }\Spec A\}\to\{\text{v-sheaves finite \'{e}tale over }\Spd A\}

is an equivalence.

Remark 1.4
.

It is not true in general that a v-sheaf étale over
\Spd A
descends to a
scheme étale over
\Spec A
. For example, consider the perfect

\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
A=\mathbb{F}_{p}[t^{1/p^{\infty}}]
. The associated
v-sheaf is
(\Spd A)(R,R^{+})=R^{+}
, and admits a v-subsheaf
X(R,R^{+})=R^{\circ\circ}
. Then
X\to\Spd A
is an open embedding, hence étale,
but does not correspond to an étale
A
-scheme. Indeed, for every closed
point
x\in\Spec A
, the fiber
\Spd k(x)\times_{\Spd A}X
is empty when

x\neq 0
and is
\Spd\mathbb{F}_{p}
when
x=0
.

Remark 1.5
.

The first part of the theorem is false when we replace
\Spec A
with an
affinoid rigid analytic variety over a perfectoid field of characteristic
zero, by the main result of
[
Heu22
]
. On the other hand, the second part
of the theorem remains true by
[
Sch22
, Lemma 15.6]
.

The second part of the theorem, Corollary
5.4
, will be
used in a future work
[
DKvHZ24
]
to construct integral models of Hodge type
Shimura varieties at parahoric level. Specifically, it allows one to construct a
finite étale cover of the special fiber a Shimura variety from the finite
étale cover of the associated v-sheaf.

Remark 1.6
.

Ian Gleason announced via private communication an alternative proof of
Corollary
5.4
, which works by comparing the category
of étale
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-local systems on
\Spd A
and
\Spec A
.

1.1.
Outline of proof

We sketch the main ideas that go into the proof of Theorem
4.9
.
In Section
3
, we introduce the notion of a Novikov
isocrystal, which somewhat easier to control than a descent datum as it involves
only one variable. In Subsection
4.1
, we construct a functor
from the category of Novikov descent data for
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}
to the
category of Novikov isocrystals, and prove that this functor is always fully
faithful. This allows us to reduce the effectivity of a Novikov descent datum
into a similar statement about the associated Novikov isocrystal.

In Subsection
4.2
, we prove effectivity of Novikov descent data
when
A
is reduced and
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}
. We first take care the case when

A
is an algebraically closed field by using the structure theory of Novikov
isocrystals developed in Section
3
. Next, we check that when

A
is an arbitrary product of algebraically closed fields, effectivity over
A

follows from that over each field. This allows to deduce effectivity for a
general reduced ring
A
by embedding
A
into the product of the algebraic
closures of its residue fields.

In Subsection
4.3
, we remove the hypothesis that
A
is
reduced. We first check that effectivity of a Novikov descent datum can be
extended along a finite-order nilpotent thickening. On the other hand, it turns
out that we can reduce an arbitrary nilpotent thickening to a finite-order
thickening by studying the associated Novikov isocrystal. Combining the two
allows us to prove effectivity in the case of a general ring
A
with
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}
. Finally, in Subsection
4.4
, we remove the
hypothesis that
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}
and prove Theorem
4.9
in
its full generality.

2.
Novikov rings

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an additive submonoid, and let
A
be a
commutative ring. We define the
Novikov ring
with exponents in

\Gamma
and coefficients in
A
to be the
A
-algebra

A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)=\biggl\{\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}t^{d_{i}}:a_{i}\in A,d_{i}\in\Gamma,\{i\in I:d_{i}<C\}\text{ is finite for all }C\in\mathbb{R}\biggr\}.

For
M
an
A
-module, we similarly define the
A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)
-module

M(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)=\biggl\{\sum_{i\in I}m_{i}t^{d_{i}}:m_{i}\in M,d_{i}\in\Gamma,\{i\in I:d_{i}<C\}\text{ is finite for all }C\in\mathbb{R}\biggr\}.

Remark 2.2
.

Such rings naturally appear as coefficient rings in Lagrangian Floer theory

[
FOOO09
]
, and are named after Novikov for his use of a formal variable in
the coefficient ring of a homology theory to keep track of topological
invariants
[
Nov81
]
.

We note that the functor
M\mapsto M(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)
sending
A
-modules to

A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)
-modules commutes with limits and finite colimits. In particular,
when
M
is a finitely presented
A
-module, the natural map

M\otimes_{A}A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)\to M(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)

is an isomorphism. As a consequence, for any other
A
-module
N
there is a
canonical isomorphism

\Hom_{A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)}(M(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!),N(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!))\cong\Hom_{A}(M,N)(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)

of
A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)
-modules.

To formulate our descent problem, we define a version of the Novikov ring with
multiple variables.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an additive submonoid, let
A
be a
commutative ring, and let
M
be an
A
-module. We define the

generalized Novikov ring
with
n
variables

A(\!(t_{1}^{\Gamma},\dotsc,t_{n}^{\Gamma})\!)=\left\{\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}t_{1}^{d_{i,1}}\dotsm t_{n}^{d_{i,n}}:\begin{matrix}a_{i}\in A,d_{i,j}\in\Gamma,\{i\in I:\sum_{j}s_{j}d_{i,j}<C\}\text{ is}\\
\text{finite for all }C\in\mathbb{R}\text{ and }s_{1},\dotsc,s_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\end{matrix}\right\}

and the
A(\!(t_{1}^{\Gamma},\dotsc,t_{n}^{\Gamma})\!)
-module

M(\!(t_{1}^{\Gamma},\dotsc,t_{n}^{\Gamma})\!)=\left\{\sum_{i\in I}m_{i}t_{1}^{d_{i,1}}\dotsm t_{n}^{d_{i,n}}:\begin{matrix}m_{i}\in M,d_{i,j}\in\Gamma,\{i\in I:\sum_{j}s_{j}d_{i,j}<C\}\text{ is}\\
\text{finite for all }C\in\mathbb{R}\text{ and }s_{1},\dotsc,s_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\end{matrix}\right\},

where we note that the definitions of
A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)
and
M(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)
agree
with that of Definition
2.1
.

We define
A
-algebra homomorphisms

\displaystyle\pi_{1}^{\ast}

\displaystyle\colon A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)\to A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma})\!);

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto t,

\displaystyle\pi_{2}^{\ast}

\displaystyle\colon A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)\to A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma})\!);

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto u,

\displaystyle\pi_{12}^{\ast}

\displaystyle\colon A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma})\!)\to A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma},v^{\Gamma})\!);

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto t,u\mapsto u,

\displaystyle\pi_{13}^{\ast}

\displaystyle\colon A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma})\!)\to A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma},v^{\Gamma})\!);

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto t,u\mapsto v,

\displaystyle\pi_{23}^{\ast}

\displaystyle\colon A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma})\!)\to A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma},v^{\Gamma})\!);

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\mapsto u,u\mapsto v.

They form an augmented semi-cosimplicial diagram of
A
-algebras

A\to A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)\rightrightarrows A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma})\!)\rightrightarrows\hskip-10.00002pt\to A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma},v^{\Gamma})\!).

We will also use the notation
\rho_{1}^{\ast}=\pi_{12}^{\ast}\circ\pi_{1}^{\ast}=\pi_{13}^{\ast}\circ\pi_{1}^{\ast}
and similarly
\rho_{2}^{\ast}=\pi_{12}^{\ast}\circ\pi_{2}^{\ast}=\pi_{23}^{\ast}\circ\pi_{1}^{\ast}
and
\rho_{3}^{\ast}=\pi_{13}^{\ast}\circ\pi_{2}^{\ast}=\pi_{23}^{\ast}\circ\pi_{2}^{\ast}
. For a ring homomorphism

f^{\ast}\colon A\to B
and an
A
-module
M
, we will also denote
f^{\ast}M=M\otimes_{A,f^{\ast}}B
, and abuse notation to write
f^{\ast}\colon M\to f^{\ast}M
for the map
m\mapsto m\otimes 1
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
Novikov descent datum
for a ring
A
is a pair
(M,\varphi)

where
M
is a finite projective module over
A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)
and

\varphi\colon\pi_{1}^{\ast}M\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_{2}^{\ast}M

is a
A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma})\!)
-linear automorphism satisfying the
cocycle condition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{1}^{\ast}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{3}^{\ast}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{2}^{\ast}M.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{13}^{\ast}\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{12}^{\ast}\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{23}^{\ast}\varphi}

We denote by
\mathsf{NovDD}(A,\Gamma)
the category of Novikov descent data.

Denote by
\mathsf{Vect}(A)
the category of finite projective
A
-modules. There is a
natural functor

\mathsf{Vect}(A)\to\mathsf{NovDD}(A,\Gamma);\quad M_{0}\mapsto(M_{0}\otimes_{A}A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!),\id_{M_{0}\otimes_{A}A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma})\!)}),

which will turn out to be an equivalence by Theorem
4.9
.

Definition 2.5
.

For an element
x\in A(\!(t_{1}^{\Gamma},\dotsc,t_{n}^{\Gamma})\!)
expressed as

x=\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}t_{1}^{d_{i,1}}\dotsm t_{n}^{d_{i,n}}

where
a_{i}\neq 0
for all
i\in I
and
(d_{i,1},\dotsc,d_{i,n})\in\Gamma^{n}
are all distinct, we define the
support
of
x
as

\supp(x)=\{(d_{i,1},\dotsc,d_{i,n}):i\in I\}\subseteq\Gamma^{n}.

We similarly define the support of an element of
M(\!(t_{1}^{\Gamma},\dotsc,t_{n}^{\Gamma})\!)
for
M
an

A
-module.

The support of
0\in A(\!(t_{1}^{\Gamma},\dotsc,t_{n}^{\Gamma})\!)
is empty. We always have
\supp(x+y)\subseteq\supp(x)\cup\supp(y)
and
\supp(xy)\subseteq\supp(x)+\supp(y)
,
where
+
is the Minkowski sum defined as
X+Y=\{x+y:x\in X,y\in Y\}
. By definition, for every element
x\in A(\!(t_{1}^{\Gamma},\dotsc,t_{n}^{\Gamma})\!)
or
x\in M(\!(t_{1}^{\Gamma},\dotsc,t_{n}^{\Gamma})\!)
, the intersection of
\supp(x)
with the half-space

\{(d_{1},\dotsc,d_{n}):s_{1}d_{1}+\dotsb+s_{n}d_{n}<C\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

is finite whenever
s_{1},\dotsc,s_{n}>0
.

Proposition 2.6
.

The functor
\mathsf{Vect}(A)\to\mathsf{NovDD}(A,\Gamma)
is fully faithful.

Proof.

Given finite projective
A
-modules
M_{0}
and
N_{0}
, let

f\colon M_{0}(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)\to N_{0}(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)

be a
A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)
-linear map compatible with the descent data on both sides.
Viewing
f
as an element of
\Hom_{A}(M_{0},N_{0})(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)
, we see that
compatibility with descent data translates to

\pi_{1}^{\ast}f=\pi_{2}^{\ast}f\in\Hom_{A}(M_{0},N_{0})(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma})\!).

We see that
\supp(\pi_{1}^{\ast}f)=\supp(f)\times\{0\}
while

\supp(\pi_{2}^{\ast}f)=\{0\}\times\supp(f)
. This immediately
shows that
\supp(f)\subseteq\{0\}
and hence
f\in\Hom_{A}(M_{0},N_{0})
.
∎

2.1.
Topological rings and modules

We start by reviewing general facts about finite projective modules over
topological rings, which we do not assume are Hausdorff. (In practice, we will
apply the results to
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
, which is always Hausdorff.) A

topological ring
is a ring
R
together with a topology such that the
addition and multiplication maps
R\times R\to R
are continuous, where
R\times R
is given the product topology. A
topological module
over a
topological ring
R
is an
R
-module
M
together with a topology such that the
addition map
M\times M\to M
and the scalar multiplication map
R\times M\to M
are continuous.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
R
be a topological ring, and let
M
be a finite projective
R
-module.
The
canonical topology
on
M
is defined as the coarsest topology
under which all
R
-linear homomorphisms
M\to R
are continuous.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
R
be a topological ring, and let
M,N
be finite projective

R
-modules.

(1)

The canonical topology on
R
as an
R
-module agrees with the
topology on
R
.

(2)

The canonical topology on
M
makes
M
into a topological
R
-module.

(3)

The canonical topology on
M\oplus N
is the product topology of the
canonical topologies on
M
and
N
.

(4)

Every
R
-linear map
M\to N
is continuous with respect to the
canonical topologies on
M
and
N
.

Proof.

The statement (4) follows from unraveling the definition of the coarsest
topology; the canonical topology on
N
is generated by preimages of open
sets
U\subseteq R
along
R
-linear maps
N\to R
, and its preimage in
M

is the preimage of
U
under the composition
M\to N\to R
, hence open. For
(1), we note that the canonical topology is at least as fine as the ring
topology, since
\id\colon R\to R
is linear. On the other hand, it is fine
enough because every scaling
r\colon R\to R
for
r\in R
is continuous.
For (3), we similarly note that the canonical topology is at least as fine as
a product topology, because the projection maps
M\oplus N\to M
and
M\oplus N\to N
are continuous. It is fine enough, because any
M\oplus N\to R
can be written as a composition

M\times N\xrightarrow{f\times g}R\times R\xrightarrow{+}R

where
f\colon M\to R
and
g\colon N\to R
are
R
-linear, hence
continuous. Finally, for (2), we choose a splitting
M\oplus M^{\prime}\cong R^{\oplus n}
of
R
-modules. By (4), both the inclusion
M\hookrightarrow R^{\oplus n}
and the projection
R^{\oplus n}\twoheadrightarrow M
are
continuous. The addition and scalar multiplication maps on
M
can be
written as the composition

M\times M\hookrightarrow R^{\oplus n}\times R^{\oplus n}\xrightarrow{+}R^{\oplus n}\twoheadrightarrow M,\quad R\times M\hookrightarrow R\times R^{\oplus n}\xrightarrow{\times}R^{\oplus n}\twoheadrightarrow M

of continuous maps.
∎

We focus on the case when there is one variable and the exponent group is

\Gamma=\mathbb{R}
. For convenience, for
A
a commutative ring and
M
an

A
-module, we denote

A[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]=A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}})\!),\quad M[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]=M(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}})\!).

Definition 2.9
.

We define the
t
-adic topology
on
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
to be the topology
generated by
x+t^{d}A[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]
for varying
x\in A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
and
d\in\mathbb{R}
. We similarly define the
t
-adic topology
on

M(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
to be the topology generated by
x+t^{d}M[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]
for
varying
x\in M(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
and
d\in\mathbb{R}
.

It can be readily verified that
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
is a topological ring. Indeed,
it is a complete Tate ring in the sense of
[
Hub93
]
, where
A[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]
is
a ring of definition. It can also be checked that
M(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
is a complete
topological
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
-module.

If
M
is a finite projective
A
-module, then
M(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
is a finite
projective
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
-module, and moreover the
t
-adic topology on

M(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
agrees with the canonical topology. We now a prove a converse
statement.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring, and let
M
be an
A
-module. Assume that

M(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
is a finite projective
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
-module, and moreover the

t
-adic topology on
M(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
agrees with the canonical topology. Then

M
is a finite projective
A
-module.

Proof.

We first show that
M
is finitely generated as an
A
-module. We choose a
finite collection of generators
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\in M(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
as an

A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
-module. Since
M(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
is projective, the natural
map

A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)^{\oplus n}\xrightarrow{(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})}M(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)

splits and hence is open by Lemma
2.8
. This implies
that there exists a large enough
N
for which

t^{N}M(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\subseteq\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}A[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]].

In particular, for every
m\in M
, the element
t^{N}m\in M(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
can be
written as
A[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]
-linear combination of
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}
. Therefore
once we write
x_{i}=\sum_{j}m_{ij}t^{d_{ij}}
, the collection of
m_{ij}

with
d_{ij}\leq N
generate
M
, and so
M
is finitely generated.

We now choose an
A
-linear surjection

0\to K\to A^{\oplus m}\to M\to 0.

It follows that

0\to K(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\to A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)^{\oplus m}\to M(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\to 0

is short exact, and hence split. This implies that
K(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
is finite
projective over
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
. Moreover the canonical topology of

K(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
is the subspace topology induced from
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)^{\oplus m}
,
again by Lemma
2.8
, and hence agrees with the
t
-adic
topology. By applying the same argument, we conclude that
K
is also finitely
generated, and therefore
M
is finitely presented.

This shows that the natural map

M\otimes_{A}A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\to M(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)

is an isomorphism. Since
A\hookrightarrow A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
splits as a map of

A
-modules, it is universally injective. Then

[
Sta24
, Theorem 08XD]
applies to show that
M
is a finite
projective
A
-module.
∎

3.
Novikov isocrystals

We fix a real number
\lambda>1
. For each commutative ring
A
, we define
the
A
-algebra automorphism

F\colon A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\to A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!);\quad\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}t^{d_{i}}\mapsto\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}t^{\lambda d_{i}}.

Definition 3.1
.

A
Novikov isocrystal
over a ring
A
is a pair
(M,F_{M})
, where
M

is a finite projective
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
-module and
F_{M}\colon M\to M
is a

F
-semilinear bijection. A morphism
f\colon(M,F_{M})\to(N,F_{N})
between
two Novikov isocrystals is a
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
-linear map
f\colon M\to N

satisfying
f\circ F_{M}=F_{N}\circ f
. We denote by
\mathsf{NovIsoc}(A)
the
category of Novikov isocrystals over
A
.

Remark 3.2
.

The name “Novikov isocrystal” comes from the analogy with the notion of a
F-isocrystal
[
Man63
]
. For
k
a perfect field in characteristic
p>0
,
a
F-isocrystal
over
k
is a
W(k)[p^{-1}]
-vector space
M

together with a
F
-semilinear bijection
F_{M}\colon M\to M
, where
W(k)
is
the ring of
p
-typical Witt vectors on
k
and
F\colon W(k)[p^{-1}]\to W(k)[p^{-1}]
is the canonical lift of the absolute Frobenius.

Note that a ring homomorphism
A\to B
induces a
F
-equivariant ring
homomorphism
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\to B(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
, and hence base changing along this
map defines a functor
\mathsf{NovIsoc}(A)\to\mathsf{NovIsoc}(B)
. There is also a natural
functor

\mathsf{Vect}(A)\to\mathsf{NovIsoc}(A);\quad M_{0}\mapsto(M_{0}\otimes_{A}A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!),\id_{M_{0}}\otimes F).

Proposition 3.3
.

For every ring
A
, the functor
\mathsf{Vect}(A)\to\mathsf{NovIsoc}(A)
is fully
faithful.

Proof.

Let
M_{0}
and
N_{0}
be two finite projective
A
-modules, and let
f\colon M_{0}\otimes_{A}A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\to N_{0}\otimes_{A}A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
be a

A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
-linear map satisfying
f\circ(\id_{M_{0}}\otimes F)=(\id_{N_{0}}\otimes F)\circ f
. Regarding
f
as an
element of

\Hom_{A}(M_{0},N_{0})\otimes_{A}A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)=\Hom_{A}(M_{0},N_{0})(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!),

we see that
f
must be fixed under the automorphism
\id_{\Hom_{A}(M_{0},N_{0})}\otimes F
, and hence
\supp(f)=\lambda\supp(f)
. On the other hand, the
intersection
[-1,1]\cap\supp(f)
is finite, and therefore
\supp(f)\subseteq\{0\}
. This shows that
f\in\Hom_{A}(M_{0},N_{0})
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The functor
\mathsf{Vect}(A)\to\mathsf{NovIsoc}(A)
is far from being essentially
surjective. If we denote by
\mathsf{VectAut}(A)
the category of pairs
(M_{0},F_{M_{0}})
where
M_{0}
is a finite projective
A
-module and
F_{M_{0}}\colon M_{0}\to M_{0}
is an
A
-linear automorphism, there is a fully faithful functor

\mathsf{VectAut}(A)\to\mathsf{NovIsoc}(A)
sending
(M_{0},F_{M_{0}})
to
(M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!),F_{M_{0}}\otimes F)
. Even then, the functor
\mathsf{VectAut}(A)\to\mathsf{NovIsoc}(A)
is
not necessarily essentially surjective. Already for

a field, the Novikov
isocrystal

is not contained in the essential image of

In this subsection, we provide some results that allow us to control the

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field. Then objects of the form

(k(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!),cF)
for some
c\in k^{\times}
generate
\mathsf{NovIsoc}(k)
under
extensions.

Proof.

We imitate the argument in the proof of the Dieudonné–Manin
classification
[
Man63
]
. Because
k(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
is a field, it suffices
to prove that for every nonzero Novikov isocrystal
(M,F_{M})
, there exists a
nonzero element
m\in M
and
c\in k^{\times}
satisfying
F_{M}(m)=cm
. Fix
an arbitrary nonzero
m\in M
and find
n\geq 1
such that
m,F_{M}(m),\dotsc,F_{M}^{n-1}(m)
are linearly independent while

F_{M}^{n}(m)=a_{0}m+a_{1}F_{M}(m)+\dotsb+a_{n-1}F_{M}^{n-1}(m)

for
a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}\in k(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
. Replacing
m
with
t^{r}m
for a real
number
r\in\mathbb{R}
has the effect of replacing
a_{i}
with
t^{r(\lambda^{n}-\lambda^{i})}
. Thus by choosing
r
appropriately, we may further
assume that
a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}\in k[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]
with
a_{i}\in k[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]^{\times}
for some
0\leq i\leq n-1
.

Our goal is to find elements
b_{0},\dotsc,b_{n-1}\in k(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
, not all zero, and a scalar
c\in k^{\times}

satisfying the equation

F_{M}(b_{0}m+b_{1}F_{M}(m)+\dotsb+b_{n-1}F_{M}^{n-1}(m))=cb_{0}m+cb_{1}F_{M}(m)+\dotsb+cb_{n-1}F_{M}^{n-1}(m).

This amounts to the system of equations

F(b_{n-1})a_{0}=cb_{0},\;F(b_{0})+F(b_{n-1})a_{1}=cb_{1},\;\dotsc,\;F(b_{n-2})+F(b_{n-1})a_{n-1}=cb_{n-1},

which can be rewritten as a single equation

F^{n}(b)F^{n-1}(a_{0})+cF^{n-1}(b)F^{n-2}(a_{1})+\dotsb+c^{n-1}F(b)a_{n-1}=c^{n}b

in terms of
b=b_{n-1}
.

We claim that this equation has a nonzero solution in
k[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]
. Let

\bar{a}_{i}\in k
be the constant terms of
a_{i}\in k[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]
. Since
we are assuming that
\bar{a}_{i}\neq 0
for some
1\leq i\leq n-1
and that

k
is algebraically closed, there exists a
c\in k^{\times}
for which

\bar{a}_{0}+c\bar{a}_{1}+\dotsb+c^{n-1}\bar{a}_{n-1}=c^{n}.

Write

g(b)=c^{-n}F^{n}(b)F^{n-1}(a_{0})+c^{-n+1}F^{n-1}(b)F^{n-2}(a_{1})+\dotsb+c^{-1}F(b)

so that we need to find
b\in k[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]
with
g(b)=b
. Set

b^{(0)}=1
and
b^{(j+1)}=g(b^{(j)})
. By construction of
c
, the
constant term of
b^{(1)}=g(b^{(0)})
is
1
, and hence there exists a
positive real number
\epsilon>0
for which
b^{(1)}-b^{(0)}\in t^{\epsilon}k[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]
. On the other hand, we observe that
x-y\in t^{d}k[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]
implies
g(x)-g(y)\in t^{\lambda d}k[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]
.
This shows that
b^{(j+1)}-b^{(j)}\in t^{\lambda^{i}\epsilon}k[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]

and therefore

b=\lim_{i\to\infty}b^{(j)}\in 1+t^{\epsilon}k[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]

exists and satisfies
g(b)=b
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring, and let
M_{0}
be an
A
-module. Consider the
topological
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
-module
M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
, where
M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
has
the
t
-adic topology. If
S\subseteq M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
is a closed

A
-submodule that is stable under the map

F\colon M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\to M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!);\quad\sum_{i}m_{i}t^{d_{i}}\mapsto\sum_{i}m_{i}t^{\lambda d_{i}},

and also stable under multiplication by
t^{d}
for every
d\in\mathbb{R}
,
then there exists an
A
-submodule
S_{0}\subseteq M_{0}
for which
S=S_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
.

Proof.

Choose any element
s\in S
and write

s=\sum_{i\geq 1}m_{i}t^{d_{i}}\in M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!),\quad m_{i}\in M_{0},\quad d_{1}<d_{2}<\dotsb.

Since
S
is closed and stable under
F
, we have

m_{1}=\lim_{k\to\infty}F^{k}(t^{-d_{1}}s)\in S.

Then
\sum_{i\geq 2}m_{i}t^{d_{i}}=s-m_{1}t^{d_{1}}\in S
and similarly
m_{2}\in S
. Iterating the process shows that
m_{i}\in S
for all
i
, and
therefore all coefficients that appear in the power series expansion of
elements of
S
are in
S
. Let
S_{0}\subseteq M_{0}
be the subset of all such
coefficients that appear in elements of
S
, so that
S\subseteq S_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
. The above analysis shows that
S_{0}\subseteq S
,
and by writing each element of
S_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
as a convergent sum,
we moreover obtain
S=S_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Let

be an injective ring homomorphism, and let

be a Novikov isocrystal with the property that its

lies in the essential image of
\mathsf{Vect}(B)\to\mathsf{NovIsoc}(B)

Then

lies in the essential image of
\mathsf{Vect}(A)\to\mathsf{NovIsoc}(A)
.

Proof.

By assumption, there exists a finite projective

-module
N_{0}
together with
a
B(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
-linear isomorphism

M\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}B(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\cong N_{0}\otimes_{B}B(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)

such that the action of
F_{M}\otimes F
on the left hand side agrees with the
action of
\id_{N_{0}}\otimes F
on the right hand side.

Since
M
is flat over
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
and
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\to B(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
is injective, we
may use the composition

M\hookrightarrow M\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}B(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\cong N_{0}\otimes_{B}B(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)=N_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)

to regard
M
as a
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
-submodule of
N_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
that is stable
under the action of
\id_{N_{0}}\otimes F
. Note that
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\hookrightarrow B(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
is a closed subring with the subspace topology.
By writing
M
as a direct summand of a finite free module, we see that
M
is
also closed in

, and moreover the canonical topology on
M

agrees with the subspace topology from

.

Using Lemma
3.6
, we see that there exists an

A
-submodule
M_{0}\subseteq N_{0}
for which

. On the other
hand, we observe from above that the canonical topology on

agrees with the

-adic topology. It therefore follows from

2.10
that

is a finite projective

-module.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring, and let
(M,F_{M})\in\mathsf{NovIsoc}(A)
be a Novikov
isocrystal. Consider a finitely generated
A[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]
-submodule
M^{\circ}\subseteq M
with the property that
M^{\circ}[t^{-1}]=M
. Assume that for
each
m^{\circ}\in M^{\circ}
there exists a real number
\epsilon>0
for which

F_{M}(m^{\circ})-m^{\circ}\in t^{\epsilon}M^{\circ}.

Then
(M,F_{M})
is in the essential image of
\mathsf{Vect}(A)\to\mathsf{NovIsoc}(A)
.

Proof.

Consider the canonical topology on
M
. We first claim that
t^{d}M^{\circ}
for

d\in\mathbb{R}
form a neighborhood basis of
0\in M
. To see this, we
choose the finite set of generators of
M^{\circ}
so that

f\colon A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)^{\oplus n}\twoheadrightarrow M

is surjective with the image of
A[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]^{\oplus n}
being
M^{\circ}
.
Since
M
is projective, it split and hence
f
is open by
Lemma
2.8
. This immediately implies that

M^{\circ}
is open, and hence
t^{d}M^{\circ}
are also open. On the other hand,
for every open neighborhood
0\in U
in
M
, its preimage
f^{-1}(U)\subseteq A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)^{\oplus n}
is open and hence contains some
t^{d}A[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]^{\oplus n}
. It follows that
t^{d}M^{\circ}
is contained in
U
.

Note that there is an ideal
(t^{>0})\subseteq A[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]
generated by the
elements
t^{\epsilon}
for all real numbers
\epsilon>0
. The quotient ring

A[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]/(t^{>0})
is naturally identified with
A
. Consider the

A
-submodule

M_{0}=\{m^{\circ}\in M^{\circ}:F_{M}(m^{\circ})=m^{\circ}\},

of
M^{\circ}
. We now show that the composition

M_{0}\hookrightarrow M^{\circ}\twoheadrightarrow M^{\circ}/(t^{>0})M^{\circ}

is an isomorphism of
A
-modules. For
m^{\circ},n^{\circ}\in M^{\circ}
, if

m^{\circ}-n^{\circ}\in t^{d}M^{\circ}
then
F_{M}(m^{\circ})-F_{M}(n^{\circ})\in t^{\lambda d}M^{\circ}
, because
M^{\circ}
is stable under
F_{M}
. This implies
that for every
m^{\circ}\in M^{\circ}
, the limit

\tilde{m}^{\circ}=\lim_{k\to\infty}F_{M}^{k}(m^{\circ})

exists, because if
F_{M}(m^{\circ})-m^{\circ}\in t^{\epsilon}M^{\circ}
then

F_{M}^{k+1}(m^{\circ})-F_{M}^{k}(m^{\circ})\in t^{\lambda^{k}\epsilon}M^{\circ}
.
Since
\tilde{m}^{\circ}\in M_{0}
and
\tilde{m}^{\circ}-m^{\circ}\in(t^{>0})M^{\circ}
, we conclude that the composition
M_{0}\to M^{\circ}/(t^{>0})M^{\circ}
is surjective. Similarly, if

satisfy

, then by iteratively applying
F_{M}
we see that
n_{0}-m_{0}\in t^{d}M^{\circ}
for all
d\in\mathbb{R}
, and hence
n_{0}=m_{0}
.

The isomorphism
M_{0}\cong M^{\circ}/(t^{>0})M^{\circ}
in particular implies
that
M_{0}
is finitely generated as an
A
-module. Choose a finite set of
generators
m_{1},\dotsc,m_{n}\in M_{0}
. Because any element in
1+(t^{>0})\subseteq A[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]
is invertible, Nakayama’s lemma implies that
m_{1},\dotsc,m_{n}
generate
M^{\circ}
as an
A[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]
-module. It follows that

A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)^{\oplus n}\xrightarrow{(m_{1},\dotsc,m_{n})}M

is surjective. We can further promote it to a surjective map of Novikov
isocrystals

(A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)^{\oplus n},F^{\oplus n})\xrightarrow{(m_{1},\dotsc,m_{n})}(M,F_{M}).

We now consider the kernel

K=\ker(A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)^{\oplus n}\to M).

This is a closed
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
-submodule of
(A^{\oplus n})(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
that is
stable under
F^{\oplus n}
. Lemma
3.6
implies that there
exists a
A
-submodule
K_{0}\subseteq A^{\oplus n}
for which
K=K_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
. On the other hand,
K
has the subspace topology from

A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)^{\oplus n}
, and therefore the
t
-adic topology on

K_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
agrees with the canonical topology. Hence
Lemma
2.10
shows that
K_{0}
is a finite projective

A
-module. At this point, we have

\displaystyle(M,F_{M})

\displaystyle\cong(A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)^{\oplus n},F^{\oplus n})/(K_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)^{\oplus n},\id_{K_{0}}\otimes F)

\displaystyle=((A^{\oplus n}/K_{0})(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!),\id_{A^{\oplus n}/K_{0}}\otimes F).

Finally, as in the proof of Lemma
2.10
,

being finitely presented over

with

being finite projective over
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
implies that
A^{\oplus n}/K_{0}
is
finite projective over
A
, by
[
Sta24
, Theorem 08XD]
.
∎

\pi_{1}^{\ast}\colon A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\to A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
for
the
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
-module structure. Next, from the commutativity of
the top face we see that the bottom face commutes when we replace

with

\pi_{2}^{\ast}g
. By uniqueness of the morphisms, this implies that
h=\pi_{2}^{\ast}g
. On the other hand, by comparing the left face with the top
face we obtain
h=\pi_{2}^{\ast}f
, as both

and
\pi_{2}^{\ast}f
are

A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
-linear while the image of

The case of a reduced ring

We now prove Theorem

when

Proposition 4.3
.

Let

be an algebraically closed field. Then the functor
\mathsf{Vect}(k)\to\mathsf{NovDD}(k,\mathbb{R})
is an equivalence of categories.

Proof.

In view of Proposition
2.6
, it suffices to show
that the functor is essentially surjective. Then by
Proposition
4.2
, it is enough to check that the
image of
\mathsf{NovDD}(k,\mathbb{R})\to\mathsf{NovIsoc}(k)
is contained in the essential image of

\mathsf{Vect}(k)\to\mathsf{NovIsoc}(k)
.

Assume that
(M,\varphi)\in\mathsf{NovDD}(k,\mathbb{R})
induces a Novikov isocrystal
(M,F_{M})\in\mathsf{NovIsoc}(k)
. By Proposition
3.5
, there exists a
filtration

0=M_{0}\hookrightarrow M_{1}\hookrightarrow\dotsb\hookrightarrow M_{n}=M

with the proper that each associated graded
M_{i}/M_{i-1}
is isomorphic to

(k(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!),c_{i}F)
for some

We inductively prove that each
(M_{i},F_{M}|_{M_{i}})\in\mathsf{NovIsoc}(k)

the essential image of
\mathsf{Vect}(k)\to\mathsf{NovIsoc}(k)

(M_{i-1},F_{M}|_{M_{i-1}})\cong(k(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!),F)^{\oplus(i-1)}.

Fixing a splitting of the short exact sequence
0\to M_{i-1}\to M_{i}\to M_{i}/M_{i-1}\to 0
, just as
k(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
-vector spaces, we find an
isomorphism
M_{i}\cong k(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)^{\oplus i}
under which
F_{M}|_{M_{i}}

corresponds to

F_{M}|_{M_{i}}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{i-1}&C\\
0&c_{i}\end{pmatrix}F,

where
I_{i-1}
denotes the identity matrix and
C=(c_{1},\dotsc,c_{i-1})^{T}\in k(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)^{\oplus(i-1)}
is a column vector.

On the other hand, by Lemma
4.1
, the map

\id-F_{M}|_{M_{i}}\otimes F_{2}\colon\pi_{2}^{\ast}M_{i}\to\pi_{2}^{\ast}M_{i}

has an

\pi_{2}^{\ast}M_{i-1}
is
(i-1)
-dimensional, there exist

Because
c_{i}\in k^{\times}
and
x_{i}\neq 0
, it follows from the last equation
that
x_{i}\in k(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)^{\times}\subseteq k(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
and

. Then
setting
y_{j}=x_{j}/x_{i}

y_{1}=F_{2}(y_{1})+\pi_{2}^{\ast}c_{1},\quad\dotsb,\quad y_{i-1}=F_{2}(y_{i-1})+\pi_{2}^{\ast}c_{i-1}.

For each

Then we have

Finally, we can write

F_{M}|_{M_{i}}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{i-1}&C\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}F=\begin{pmatrix}I_{i-1}&Z\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}F\begin{pmatrix}I_{i-1}&Z\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}^{-1},

where
Z=(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{i-1})^{T}\in k(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)^{\oplus(i-1)}
. This shows
that under this change of basis, we have
(M_{i},F_{M}|_{M_{i}})\cong(k(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!),F)^{\oplus i}
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let

be a product of algebraically closed fields.
Then the functor
\mathsf{Vect}(A)\to\mathsf{NovDD}(A,\mathbb{R})

Proof.

By Proposition
2.6
, it remains to check that
the functor is essentially surjective. Let
(M,\varphi)\in\mathsf{NovDD}(A,\mathbb{R})
be
a descent datum. By Proposition

, for each

-vector space

that

to show that

Taking the product of

, we obtain an isomorphism

f=\prod_{i\in I}f_{i}\colon M\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}\prod_{i\in I}k_{i}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\cong M_{0}\otimes_{A}\prod_{i\in I}k_{i}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!).

Compatibility of each

\pi_{2}^{\ast}f_{i}\circ(\varphi\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}k_{i}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!))=\pi_{1}^{\ast}f_{i},

and taking the product over all

gives a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}^{\ast}M\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}\prod_{i\in I}k_{i}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}

-submodule of

of

\displaystyle\pi_{1}^{\ast}M

\displaystyle=M\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!),\pi_{1}^{\ast}}A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\hookrightarrow M_{0}\otimes_{A}\prod_{i\in I}k_{i}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!),

\displaystyle\pi_{2}^{\ast}M

\displaystyle=M\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!),\pi_{2}^{\ast}}A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\hookrightarrow M_{0}\otimes_{A}\prod_{i\in I}k_{i}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!),

agree.

For every element
\alpha=(\alpha_{i})\in M_{0}\otimes_{A}\prod_{i\in I}k_{i}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
, define the support of
\alpha
as
\supp(\alpha)=\bigcup_{i\in I}\supp(\alpha_{i})\subseteq\mathbb{R}
, where the support of

We now claim that for each

is

a finite collection of generators as an

(

∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
A
be a reduced ring. Then the functor
\mathsf{Vect}(A)\to\mathsf{NovDD}(A,\mathbb{R})
is an
equivalence.

Proof.

In view of Proposition
2.6
, it suffices to show
that the functor is essentially surjective. Then by
Proposition
4.2
, it is enough to check that the

\mathsf{NovDD}(A,\mathbb{R})\to\mathsf{NovIsoc}(A)

4.3.
The case of a general ring

We now prove Theorem
4.9
when
A
is an arbitrary commutative
ring and
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}
. We begin by studying the square-zero deformation
theory of Novikov descent data.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring, and let
I\subseteq A
be an ideal satisfying

I^{2}=0
.

(1)

Let
M,N
be two finite projective
A
-modules together with an
isomorphism
\bar{f}\colon M/IM\cong N/IN
of
A/I
-modules. Then

\bar{f}
lifts to an isomorphism
f\colon M\cong N
of
A
-modules.

(2)

Let
\bar{M}
be a finite projective
A/I
-module. Then there exists a
finite projective
A
-module
M
together with an isomorphism
M/IM\cong\bar{M}
.

Proof.

(1) Letting
P=\Hom_{A}(M,N)
, we observe that
P
is again a finite
projective
A
-module with
\bar{f}\in P/IP
. Lift
\bar{f}
to any element

f\in P
. We now check that
f
is indeed an isomorphism
f\colon M\to N

because we can work locally so that both
M
and
N
are free, and then the
determinant being a unit can be checked modulo
I
.

(2) Note that
\Spec A
is homeomorphic to
\Spec A/I
. There exists a cover

\{U_{i}\}
of
\Spec A\cong\Spec A/I
consisting of affine opens
over which
\bar{M}
is free. We lift
\bar{M}|_{U_{i}}
to
M_{i}
on each

U_{i}
separately, and use (1) to choose isomorphisms
M_{i}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}\cong M_{j}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}
lifting the identity map on
\bar{M}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}
. These isomorphisms need not satisfy the cocycle
condition, but the induced
2
-cocycle takes values in

\ker(\mathrm{GL}_{A}(M_{i}|_{U_{i}})\to\mathrm{GL}_{A/I}(\bar{M}|_{U_{i}}))\cong\Hom_{A/I}(\bar{M},I\otimes_{A/I}\bar{M})|_{U_{i}}.

Since the Čech
H^{2}
of quasi-coherent sheaves with respect to
\{U_{i}\}
vanish on
\Spec A/I
as each
U_{i}
is affine, this is a
coboundary, and hence we may modify the isomorphisms
M_{i}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}\cong M_{j}|_{U_{i}\cap U_{j}}
so that they do satisfy the cocycle condition.
∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring, and let
I\subseteq A
be an ideal satisfying

I^{2}=0
. Let
(M,\varphi)\in\mathsf{NovDD}(A,\mathbb{R})
be a descent datum satisfying the
property that its base change to

lies in the essential image of

\mathsf{Vect}(A/I)\to\mathsf{NovDD}(A/I,\mathbb{R})
. Then
(M,\varphi)
is in the essential image of

\mathsf{Vect}(A)\to\mathsf{NovDD}(A,\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

By assumption, there exists a finite projective
A/I
-module
\bar{M}_{0}

together with an isomorphism

(M/I(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)M,\bar{\varphi})\cong(\bar{M}_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!),\id)

in
\mathsf{NovDD}(A/I,\mathbb{R})
. Using Lemma
4.6
, we choose a
finite projective
A
-module
M_{0}
with
M_{0}/IM_{0}\cong\bar{M}_{0}
. As

A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\twoheadrightarrow(A/I)(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
is also a square-zero
thickening, we may use Lemma
4.6
again to choose an
isomorphism

M\cong M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)

lifting the isomorphism
M/I(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)M\cong\bar{M}_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\cong M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)/(IM_{0})(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
. Under this isomorphism, we obtain a new
cocycle

\psi\colon M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\xrightarrow{\cong}M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)

that reduces to the identity map on
(M_{0}/IM_{0})(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
.

It now suffices to show that
\psi
is a coboundary, i.e., there exists an

A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
-linear automorphism
\xi\colon M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\cong M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
for which
\psi=\pi_{2}^{\ast}\xi^{-1}\circ\pi_{1}^{\ast}\xi
. We
first write

\psi=\id+\psi_{+},\quad\psi\colon M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\to(IM_{0})(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!).

Since

squares to zero, the cocycle condition on

\pi_{13}^{\ast}\psi_{+}=\pi_{23}^{\ast}\psi_{+}+\pi_{12}^{\ast}\psi_{+},

as

(IM_{0})(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}},v^{\mathbb{R}})\!)=I(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}},v^{\mathbb{R}})\!)M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}},v^{\mathbb{R}})\!)

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{A}(M_{0},IM_{0})(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!),

\displaystyle\Hom_{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}},v^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}(M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}},v^{\mathbb{R}})\!),(IM_{0})(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}},v^{\mathbb{R}})\!))

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{A}(M_{0},IM_{0})(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}},v^{\mathbb{R}})\!).

Using this isomorphism, we identify
\psi_{+}
as an element of
\Hom_{A}(M_{0},IM_{0})(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
, and similarly the additive cocycle condition as an
equation valued in the group
\Hom_{A}(M_{0},IM_{0})(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}},v^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
. By
considering the coefficients in the cocycle condition, we readily see that

is supported on

agrees with the coefficient
of

for each

\psi_{+}=\pi_{1}^{\ast}\xi_{+}-\pi_{2}^{\ast}\xi_{+},\quad\xi_{+}\in\Hom_{A}(M_{0},IM_{0})(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!).

Regarding

as a

-linear map

. It follows that

\psi=\id+\pi_{1}^{\ast}\xi_{+}-\pi_{2}^{\ast}\xi_{+}=(\id-\pi_{2}^{\ast}\xi_{+})\circ(\id+\pi_{1}^{\ast}\xi_{+})=\pi_{2}^{\ast}\xi^{-1}\circ\pi_{1}^{\ast}\xi.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Let

\mathsf{Vect}(A)\to\mathsf{NovDD}(A,\mathbb{R})

Proof.

By Proposition

, it suffices to show that

(M,F_{M})

Proposition
4.5
, there exists a finite projective

B
-module
N_{0}
for which

(M\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}B(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!),F_{M}\otimes F)\cong(N_{0}\otimes_{B}B(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!),\id_{N_{0}}\otimes F)

as Novikov isocrystals over
B
.

Choose a finite set of generators
n_{1},\dotsc,n_{k}\in N_{0}
as
B
-modules.
Because
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\to B(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
is surjective, there is also a
surjection

M\to M\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}B(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)\cong N_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!).

Lift the elements
n_{1},\dotsc,n_{k}\in N_{0}\subseteq N_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
to
elements
m_{1},\dotsc,m_{k}\in M
. Since elements of
1+I(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
are
invertible in
A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
, the elements
m_{1},\dotsc,m_{k}
generate
M
as a

A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
-module by Nakayama’s lemma. This implies that if we set

M^{\circ}=A[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]m_{1}+\dotsb+A[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]m_{k}

then
M^{\circ}[t^{-1}]=M
.

For each
1\leq i\leq k
, we have

F_{M}(m_{i})-m_{i}\in\ker(M\to M\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}B(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!))=M\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}I(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!).

That is, we have

F_{M}(m_{i})\in m_{i}+I(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)m_{1}+I(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)m_{2}+\dotsb+I(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)m_{k}.

Separating out the monomials with non-positive degree, we can further write

F_{M}(m_{i})-m_{i}-\sum_{j\in I_{i}}a_{i,j}t^{d_{i,j}}m_{l_{i,j}}\in\sum_{i=1}^{k}(t^{>0})I[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]m_{i}\subseteq(t^{>0})M^{\circ},

where
I_{i}
is finite and
a_{i,j}\in I
. That is, if we consider the
finitely generated ideal
J\subseteq I
generated by
a_{i,j}
over all
1\leq i\leq k
and
j\in I_{i}
, we obtain

F_{M}(m_{i})-m_{i}\in(t^{>0})M^{\circ}

modulo
J(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
. Defining
C=A/J
, so that
A\twoheadrightarrow C\twoheadrightarrow B
, we conclude that the Novikov isocrystal

(M\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}C(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!),F_{M}\otimes F)

has the property that the
C[[t^{\mathbb{R}}]]
-submodule of
M\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}C(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)
generated by the images of
m_{1},\dotsc,m_{k}
satisfies the
hypotheses of Proposition
3.8
. This shows that the
base change of
(M,F_{M})
to
C
lies in the essential image of
\mathsf{Vect}(C)\to\mathsf{NovIsoc}(C)
, and by Proposition
4.2
, it also
follows that the base change of
(M,\varphi)
to
C
lies in the essential
image of
\mathsf{Vect}(C)\to\mathsf{NovDD}(C,\mathbb{R})
.

On the other hand, because all elements of
J
are nilpotent and
J
is
finitely generated, some finite power of
J
is the zero ideal. This allows us
to iteratively apply Lemma
4.7
to conclude that

(M,\varphi)
lies in the essential image over
\mathsf{Vect}(A)\to\mathsf{NovDD}(A,\mathbb{R})
.
∎

4.4.
Restricting the exponents

We finally remove the hypothesis that
\Gamma=\mathbb{R}
. Note there is a
natural functor
\mathsf{NovDD}(A,\Gamma)\to{\mathsf{NovDD}(A,\mathbb{R})}
given by tensoring along the
ring homomorphism
A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)\to{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}
.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
A
be a commutative ring, and let
\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an
additive submonoid. Then the functor
\mathsf{Vect}(A)\to\mathsf{NovDD}(A,\Gamma)
is an
equivalence of categories.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.6
, it is enough to prove
essential surjectivity. Let
(M,\varphi)\in\mathsf{NovDD}(A,\Gamma)
be a Novikov descent
datum. By Proposition
4.8
, there exists a finite projective

A
-module
M_{0}
together with an isomorphism

f\colon(M\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)}{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)},\varphi\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma})\!)}{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)})\xrightarrow{\cong}({M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)},\id_{M_{0}}\otimes{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)})

in
{\mathsf{NovDD}(A,\mathbb{R})}
. This in particular allows us to regard
M
as an

A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)
-submodule of
{M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}
with the property that the
images of

M\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!),\pi_{1}^{\ast}}A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma})\!)\hookrightarrow{M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)},\quad M\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!),\pi_{2}^{\ast}}A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma})\!)\hookrightarrow{M_{0}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}},u^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}

agree.

Consider any element
x
in the common image. Since it can be written as a
finite sum

x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\pi_{1}^{\ast}m_{i})a_{i},\quad m_{i}\in M,\quad a_{i}\in A(\!(t^{\Gamma},u^{\Gamma})\!),

we see that
\supp(x)
must be contained in
\mathbb{R}\times\Gamma
. For
every element
m\in M
, we in particular have
\supp(\pi_{2}^{\ast}m)\subseteq\mathbb{R}\times\Gamma
, and hence
\supp(m)\subseteq\Gamma
. This shows
that

M\subseteq M_{0}(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!),

and it now suffices to prove that this inclusion is an equality.

We achieve this by running the same argument for the dual descent datum. Write

g\colon M\hookrightarrow M_{0}(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)
for the inclusion above. If we
consider the linear dual of
f
, we obtain an isomorphism

(f^{\vee})^{-1}\colon M^{\vee}\otimes_{A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)}{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}\xrightarrow{\cong}{M_{0}^{\vee}(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}

compatible with descent data. By the argument above, there exists an inclusion

h\colon M^{\vee}\hookrightarrow M_{0}^{\vee}(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)
that recovers

(f^{\vee})^{-1}
upon base changing along
A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)\to{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}
.
This shows that the composition

M_{0}(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)\xrightarrow{h^{\vee}}M\xrightarrow{g}M_{0}(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)

is the identity map upon base changing along
A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)\to{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}
. Because both sides are finite projective and
A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)\hookrightarrow{A(\!(t^{\mathbb{R}})\!)}
is injective, the composition
g\circ h^{\vee}
is the identity map. In particular,
g
is surjective, and therefore a
bijection.
∎

5.
Application to perfectoid geometry

We now record some consequences of our descent result. We will use
Theorem
4.9
with the exponent submonoid
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}[p^{-1}]
. It is customary to write

{A(\!(t^{\Gamma})\!)}=A(\!(t^{{1/p^{\infty}}})\!)

in the setting of perfectoid geometry.

Let
\mathsf{Perf}
be the category of affinoid perfectoid spaces in
characteristic
p
, equipped with the v-topology. For a perfect

\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra
A
, we consider the v-sheaf

\Spd A\colon\mathsf{Perf}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{Set};\quad\Spa(R,R^{+})\mapsto\Hom(A,R^{+}).

Similarly for
B
a perfect (hence perfectoid) complete Tate

\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra, we define the v-sheaf

\Spd B=\Spd(B,B^{\circ})\colon\mathsf{Perf}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{Set};\quad\Spa(R,R^{+})\mapsto\Hom_{\mathrm{cts}}((B,B^{\circ}),(R,R^{+})).

We refer the reader to
[
SW20
]
for the detailed
definitions of the objects appearing in this section.

Definition 5.1
.

For a v-sheaf
X
, we define a
vector bundle
on
X
to be an
association to each
(R,R^{+})
-point
\xi\in X(R,R^{+})
a locally finite free
sheaf
\mathscr{V}_{\xi}
on
\Spa(R,R^{+})
, together with the data of, for each
map
f\colon\Spa(S,S^{+})\to\Spa(R,R^{+})
and
\xi\in X(R,R^{+})
, an
isomorphism
f^{\ast}\mathscr{V}_{\xi}\cong\mathscr{V}_{f^{\ast}\xi}
,
satisfying the condition that for every

\Spa(T,T^{+})\xrightarrow{g}\Spa(S,S^{+})\xrightarrow{f}\Spa(R,R^{+})

and
\xi\in X(R,R^{+})
, the three identifications between
g^{\ast}f^{\ast}\mathscr{V}_{\xi}
,
g^{\ast}\mathscr{V}_{f^{\ast}\xi}
, and
\mathscr{V}_{g^{\ast}f^{\ast}\xi}
commute.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
A
be a perfect
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra. Then the natural functor

\{\text{vector bundles on }\Spec A\}\to\{\text{vector
bundles on }\Spd A\}

is an exact tensor equivalence.

Proof.

As the natural map

X=\Spd(A(\!(t^{{1/p^{\infty}}})\!),A[[t^{{1/p^{\infty}}}]])\to\Spd A=Y

is a v-cover, by
[
SW20
, Lemma 17.1.8]
the category of
vector bundles on
\Spd A
can be identified with the category of pairs

(\mathscr{V},\varphi)
, where
\mathscr{V}
is a vector bundle on
X
and

\varphi
is an isomorphism of vector bundles over
X\times_{Y}X
satisfying a
cocycle condition over
X\times_{Y}X\times_{Y}X
.
By
[
KL15
, Theorem 2.7.7]
and again

[
SW20
, Lemma 17.1.8]
, the vector bundle
\mathscr{V}

corresponds to a finite projective module
M
over
A(\!(t^{{1/p^{\infty}}})\!)
.

Next, we identify

X\times_{Y}X=\bigcup_{n=2}^{\infty}\Spd A[[t^{{1/p^{\infty}}},u^{{1/p^{\infty}}}]]\Bigl\langle\frac{t^{n}}{u},\frac{u^{n}}{t}\Bigr\rangle[1/tu].

If we denote by
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\colon X\times_{Y}X\to X
the two projection
maps, then
\varphi
is an isomorphism of vector bundles

\varphi\colon\pi_{1}^{\ast}\mathscr{V}\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_{2}^{\ast}\mathscr{V}

over
X\times_{Y}X
. Because

H^{0}(X\times_{Y}X,\mathscr{O}_{X\times_{Y}X})=\bigcap_{n=2}^{\infty}A[[t^{{1/p^{\infty}}},u^{{1/p^{\infty}}}]]\Bigl\langle\frac{t^{n}}{u},\frac{u^{n}}{t}\Bigr\rangle[1/tu]=A(\!(t^{{1/p^{\infty}}},u^{{1/p^{\infty}}})\!),

we see that
\varphi
corresponds to an isomorphism

\varphi\colon M\otimes_{A(\!(t^{{1/p^{\infty}}})\!),\pi_{1}^{\ast}}A(\!(t^{{1/p^{\infty}}},u^{{1/p^{\infty}}})\!)\xrightarrow{\cong}M\otimes_{A(\!(t^{{1/p^{\infty}}})\!),\pi_{2}^{\ast}}A(\!(t^{{1/p^{\infty}}},u^{{1/p^{\infty}}})\!).

Finally the cocycle condition is defined on

X\times_{Y}X\times_{Y}X=\bigcup_{n=3}^{\infty}\Spd A[[t^{{1/p^{\infty}}},u^{{1/p^{\infty}}},v^{{1/p^{\infty}}}]]\Bigl\langle\frac{t^{n}}{uv},\frac{u^{n}}{tv},\frac{v^{n}}{tu}\Bigr\rangle[1/tuv],

and hence be checked after tensoring to

H^{0}(X\times_{Y}X\times_{Y}X,\mathscr{O}_{X\times_{Y}X\times_{Y}X})=A(\!(t^{{1/p^{\infty}}},u^{{1/p^{\infty}}},v^{{1/p^{\infty}}})\!).

This shows that a vector bundle on
\Spd A
corresponds to a Novikov descent
datum for
A
. Applying Theorem
4.9
now gives the desired
result.
∎

We now compare the finite étale sites of
\Spec A
and
\Spd A
.

Definition 5.3

(
[
Sch22
, Definition 10.1]
)
.

We say that a map
f\colon X\to Y
of v-sheaves is
finite
étale
when for every map
\Spa(R,R^{+})\to Y
, the fiber product
\Spa(R,R^{+})\times_{Y}X
is representable by an affinoid perfectoid
\Spa(S,S^{+})

where
R\to S
is finite étale and
S^{+}
is the integral closure of the
image of
R^{+}
.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
A
be a perfect
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra. Then the natural functor

\{\text{schemes finite \'{e}tale over }\Spec A\}\to\{\text{v-sheaves finite \'{e}tale over }\Spd A\}

is an equivalence.

Proof.

We note that a finite étale
R
-algebra is necessarily a finite projective

R
-module. By Corollary
5.2
, we have an equivalence
between the category of vector bundles on
\Spec A
with an algebra structure
and the category of vector bundles on
\Spd A
with an algebra structure. It
remains to check that the condition of being étale agrees on both sides.

If the finite
A
-algebra
B
is étale, it is clear that the corresponding

R
-algebra
R\otimes_{A}B
is also étale over
R
for every
A\to R^{+}
.
In the other direction we note that if
A(\!(t^{{1/p^{\infty}}})\!)\to B\otimes_{A}A(\!(t^{{1/p^{\infty}}})\!)
is finite étale, then so is
A\to B
by

[
Sta24
, Theorem 08XE]
, as the inclusion
A\to A(\!(t^{{1/p^{\infty}}})\!)

splits as a map of
A
-modules.
∎

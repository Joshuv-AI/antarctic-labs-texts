Abstract
. The paper studies existence of ground states for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation (0.1) − ( ∇ + i ​ A ​ ( x ) ) 2 ​ u + V ⁡ ( x ) ​ u = | u | p − 1 ​ u , 2 < p < 2 ∗ , -(\nabla+iA(x))^{2}u+V(x)u=|u|^{p-1}u,\quad 2<p<2^{*}, with a general external magnetic field. In particular, no lattice periodicity or symmetry of the magnetic field, or presence of external electric field is required.

1.
Introduction

We study the existence of solutions for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with bounded external magnetic field
B
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. External magnetic field enters Schrödinger equation, as well as other equations of quantum mechanics, by addition of a real-valued covector field
A
, called the magnetic potential, to the momentum operator
\frac{\hbar}{i}d
. Magnetic field, which is a measurable quantity, is a differential 2-form
B=dA
, while the magnetic potential is defined up to an arbitrary additive term
d\varphi
, where
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is an arbitrary scalar function. Magnetic momentum

\frac{\hbar}{i}d+A
has the following gauge invariance property:

(\frac{\hbar}{i}d+A)(e^{\mathbf{i}\frac{\varphi}{\hbar}}u)=e^{\mathbf{i}\frac{\varphi}{\hbar}}(\frac{\hbar}{i}d+A+d\varphi)u.

In this paper we follow the convention that normalizes the mass of the particle and sets the Planck constant
\hbar
to be equal to
1
(which is always possible by rescaling the time and space variables).

To our best knowledge, the earliest existence result for nonlinear magnetic Schrödinger equation
is the paper by Esteban and Lions
[
14
]
where the magnetic field is assumed to be constant. Their approach was generalized to periodic magnetic field by Arioli and Szulkin
[
2
]
(see also
[
16
]
,
[
4
]
). In addition to that, there is a number of important existence results for quasiclassical solutions, that is, solutions of the nonlinear magnetic Schrödinger equation that exist if
\hbar
is sufficiently small, see
[
13
,
7
,
8
]
and references therein, as well as existence for specific magnetic fields such as the Aharonov-Bohm field,
[
9
]
, and studies of properties of solutions to the magnetic Schrödinger equation, such as
[
5
]
.

A major technical difficulty in proving existence results for the magnetic Schrödinger equation is the lack of compactness of Sobolev embeddings in the whole
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and it is overcome by the use of a concentration-compactness argument. For instance, in
[
2
]
, which generalizes
[
14
]
, one controls the loss of compactness in problems with a periodic magnetic field by means of energy-preserving operators

(1.1)

g_{y}:=\;u\mapsto e^{\mathbf{i}\varphi_{y}(\cdot)}u(\cdot-y),\quad y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},

(where
\varphi_{y}
is a suitable re-phasing function (see (
2.8
) below)),
known as
magnetic shifts
with their inverse

g^{-1}_{y}:=\;v\mapsto e^{-\mathbf{i}\varphi_{y}(\cdot+y)}v(\cdot+y),\quad y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}\,.

Another concentration mechanism applies to quasiclassical asymptotics and is not considered here.

The concern of this paper is in finding sufficient existence conditions for solutions to (
0.1
) that are not restricted to the periodic magnetic field and, furthermore, do not use strong electric field at infinity to dominate both electric and magnetic field in the whole space, as in the existence condition of
[
16
]

(1.2)

|A(x)|^{2}+V(x)<V_{\infty}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x).

For the case of periodic magnetic field, a sufficient condition for existence of a ground state, provided by Arioli and Szulkin (generalizing the case of constant magnetic field of
[
14
]
), is
V<V_{\infty}
, which can be understood as energy penalty by electric field alone when the magnetic field does not change.
A different condition for existence of a solution (which is not a ground state, and the electric potential is constant) is offered by
[
10
]
:

(1.3)

B_{\infty}=0,\|B\|_{\infty}<b_{0},

where
b_{0}
is a specified positive constant. There is no assumption of periodicity of the magnetic field in
[
10
]
. The lack of periodicity there is handled by a refined concentration-compactness analysis (see Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.3
below) that uses a suitable non-isometric counterpart of magnetic shifts. While the declared objective of that paper is to describe the concentration mechanism in the general non-periodic case and give a sample existence result. The present paper uses the concentration-compactness approach of
[
10
]
in order to refine the existence condition of (
1.2
). Its existence condition, that uses limits at infinity evaluated via lattice shifts,

(1.4)

|A(x)|^{2}+V(x)<|A_{\infty}(x)|^{2}+V_{\infty}(x),

does not require the magnetic field to be dominated by the electric field at infinity. Remarkably, however, this condition is also accompanied with a requirement of sufficiently small
B
like in
[
10
]
although this time it arises to assure that the problem at infinity has a nonvanishing ground state.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we recall basic properties of magnetic Sobolev spaces associated with respective energy functionals, define (non-isometric) magnetic shift operators as well as asymptotic values of magnetic field and energy at infinity. In Section 3 we quote the structural result from
[
10
]
about asymptotic behavior of sequences in magnetic Sobolev spaces. This result, usually called profile decomposition, was first proved, for general sequences in Sobolev spaces, by Solimini
[
19
]
, and then extended, to Hilbert spaces and to Banach spaces in
[
17
]
and in
[
20
]
(with further details in
[
11
]
), respectively. The profile decomposition of
[
10
]
that we use here is set in a Frechet space and does not follow from prior functional-analytic results, it is, however, close in spirit to profile decomposition for Sobolev spaces on manifolds,
[
18
]
.

The main results of this paper are Theorem
4.5
, Theorem
4.2
and Theorem
5.3
.

Two first two theorems state existence of minimizers in the constraint problem (
4.3
) under a penalty condition. The third one deals with a model minimization problem (
5.2
) involving the Aharonov-Bohm magnetic potential, a singular electric potential, and critical Sobolev nonlinearity.

The minimizers, also called ground states, satisfy the Euler-Lagrange equation

-(\nabla+iA(x))^{2}u+V(x)u=\lambda|u|^{p-1}u

with some
\lambda>0
. Since the right and the left hand sides of the equation have different homogeneity, a suitable scalar multiple of
u
satisfies (
0.1
).

In Appendix we present profile decompositions for sequences in Sobolev spaces in relation to critical Sobolev embeddings.

2.
Preliminaries: magnetic Sobolev space, magnetic shifts, energy at infinity

Throughout the paper the set
\mathbb{N}
of natural numbers will be understood to include zero.
To an extent that it causes no ambiguity, we will treat the magnetic potential
A
either as a linear form on
R^{N}
or as its coordinate representation as a vector field on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We define

(2.1)

\nabla_{A}(u)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}(\nabla+\mathbf{i}A)u,

and the scalar product

\langle u,v\rangle_{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\nabla_{A}u(x)\overline{\nabla_{A}v(x)}\mathrm{d}{x}\,,

(the symbol of the scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
will be omitted throughout the paper).
Then, we introduce the (Dirichlet-type) energy functional

(2.2)

E_{A}(u)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla_{A}u(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}{x}.

Elementary calculations using the Cauchy inequality yield the following well known pointwise estimates:

(2.3)

|\nabla_{A}u(x)|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}-8|A(x)|^{2}\,|u|^{2}\,,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\

or, conversely,

(2.4)

|\nabla_{A}u(x)|^{2}\leq 2|\nabla u(x)|^{2}+16|A(x)|^{2}\,|u|^{2}\,,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.\\

In this section we assume

(2.5)

\begin{cases}A\in L^{N}_{\mathrm{l}oc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})&N\geq 3,\\
A\in L^{2+\epsilon}_{\mathrm{l}oc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})&N=2,\epsilon>0,\end{cases}

or that
A
is the Aharonov-Bohm potential
A(x)=\lambda\frac{(x_{1},-x_{2},0,\dots,0)}{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}}

Further on,
A
will be restricted to a smaller class.
When dealing with a magnetic potential
A
we shall define
\dot{H}_{A}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}
with respect to the norm
\|u\|_{A}=(E_{A}(u))^{\frac{1}{2}}

(note that
\dot{H}^{1,2}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a space of measurable functions whenever
N>2
or
N=2
and
dA\not\equiv 0
, see e.g.
[
12
]
), and the space

H_{A}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as the intersection
\dot{H}_{A}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
(equipped with the standard intersection norm). From (
2.5
) it easily follows that
H_{A}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

is continuously embedded in
H^{1,2}_{\mathrm{l}oc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and that Frechet spaces
H_{A\,\mathrm{l}oc}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
qnd
H^{1,2}_{\mathrm{l}oc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
coincide (for details see, for example,
[
10
]
).
Note that
|u|^{2}
in this model equals the probability density of a particle in space which doesn’t change if
u
is multiplied by
e^{\mathbf{i}\varphi}
(on the other hand the magnetic potential
A
and
A+\nabla\varphi
give rise to the same magnetic field
B=dA
).

Note also that, when
A\equiv 0
, we obtain the corresponding usual Sobolev spaces
\dot{H}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
H^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
respectively.
Setting, as usual,
2^{*}=\begin{cases}\frac{2N}{N-2},&N>2\\
\infty,&N=2\end{cases}

the critical Sobolev exponent, we have the following continuous embeddings

H^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for every
p\in[2,2^{*})
,
and
H^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
when
N>2
. Furthermore, from the well-known
diamagnetic inequality

(2.6)

|\nabla_{A}u(x)|\geq\left|\nabla|u(x)|\right|,

we deduce that

E_{A}(u)\geq E_{0}(|u|)\geq C\|u\|_{2^{*}}^{2},\quad\mbox{ if }N>2,

and, as a consequence, the continuous embeddings

\dot{H}_{A}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad H_{A}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\;\forall p\in[2,2^{*}),

(the latter one extends by analogous argument to the case
N=2
).

Let
N\geq 2
and let
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
denote the linear spaces, respectively, of 1-forms on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and of antisymmetric 2-forms on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Note that when
B
is a constant magnetic field, its magnetic potential
A
equals, up to a gradient of an arbitrary function,
\frac{1}{2}Bx
. Its linear growth suggests that, generally, one shouold not try to define, for a given function
u\in H^{1,2}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the limit of the magnetic energy functional (
2.2
) when the function
u
is shifted at infinity like in the non-magnetic case (e.g.
[
15
]
), by taking
\lim_{|y|\to\infty}E_{0}(u(\cdot+y))
. The appropriate construction takes advantage, instead, of the gauge invariance of the magnetic energy.
In the case of lattice-periodic
B
, Arioli and Szulkin
[
2
]
derived energy functionals at infinity using ”magnetic shifts”

(2.7)

g_{y}:=u\mapsto e^{\mathbf{i}\varphi_{y}(\cdot)}u(\cdot-y)\quad\forall u\in C_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\;y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N},

where the function
\varphi_{y}
is defined by the property

(2.8)

A(\cdot-y)=A(\cdot)+\nabla\varphi_{y}(\cdot).

(Indeed, this relation is equivalent to lattice-periodicity of the field
dA(\cdot-y)=dA
.)

In
[
10
]
analogous magnetic shifts were introduced without assumption of perioidicity of the magnetic field (the case
N=2
was not included, but the original proof remains valid in this casse as well):

Lemma 2.1

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let

be a bounded magnetic field with

, such that
\varphi_{0}=0

|A+\nabla\varphi_{y}|\leq\|B\|_{\infty}\,|x-y|,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where

(2.10)

\|B\|_{\infty}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{m<n}\|\partial_{n}A_{m}-\partial_{m}A_{n}\|_{\infty}^{2},

The function

[
10
]

Defining the corrected potential

by

A_{y}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}A+\nabla\varphi_{y},

note that

) the value of

at

is
is zero.

Definition 2.2

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
B=dA
be a magnetic field with
A\in C^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\Lambda_{1})
.
Any map

(2.12)

g_{y}:=u\mapsto e^{\mathbf{i}\varphi_{y}(\cdot)}u(\cdot-y),\quad u\in C_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\;y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

with the
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\varphi_{y}
as in Lemma

a magnetic shift
(relative to the magnetic field

) determined by the vector
y
.

Elementary calculations using
(
2.11
) (which hold for any continuous real-valued function in the place of

) show that

(2.13)

\nabla_{A}(g_{y}u)=g_{y}(\nabla_{A_{y}(\cdot+y)}u)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad(g_{y})^{-1}(\nabla_{A}u)=\nabla_{A_{y}(\cdot+y)}((g_{y})^{-1}u).

Furthermore, we get

\langle g_{y}u,g_{y}v\rangle_{A}=\langle u,v\rangle_{A_{y}(\cdot+y)}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\langle(g_{y})^{-1}u,(g_{y})^{-1}v\rangle_{A_{y}(\cdot+y)}=\langle u,v\rangle_{A},

and, in particular, that

(2.14)

E_{A}(g_{y}u)=E_{A_{y}(\cdot+y)}(u)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad E_{A}(u)=E_{A_{y}(\cdot+y)}((g_{y})^{-1}u).\\

In what follows
\dot{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the space of functions with uniformly bounded continuous derivatives and
\dot{C}^{0,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the space of functions satisfying
|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C|x-y|
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. In particular,
\dot{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
contains all linear functions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
Y=(y_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a divergent sequence in
R^{N}
. If
A\in\dot{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\Lambda_{1})
, then applying Arzelà-Ascoli theorem
to the sequence
(A_{y_{k}}(\cdot+y_{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
,
we get a renamed subsequence such that

(A_{y_{k}}(\cdot+y_{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges, uniformly on bounded sets to some Lipschitz function
A^{(Y)}_{\infty}\in\dot{C}^{0,1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\Lambda_{1})
, and and
(dA_{y_{k}}(\cdot+y_{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges almost everywhere with a constand bound to a bounded function
B^{(Y)}_{\infty}=dA^{(Y)}_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\Lambda_{2})
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
Y=(y_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a divergent sequence in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
and consider the renamed subsequence
(y_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and the associated magnetic potential

described in Remark

. Then, the corresponding functional

(see (

the energy at infinity relative to the renamed subsequence

, corresponding to the
magnetic field at infinity

B^{(Y)}_{\infty}=dA^{(Y)}_{\infty}
.

3.
Profile decomposition

In this section we quote from
[
10
]
the results on structure of bounded sequences in
H_{A}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. While the original result is stated for
N\geq 3
, the original argument extends to the case
N=2
with no modifications.

Definition 3.1
.

A set
\Xi\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is called a discretization of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
if
0\in\Xi
,

\inf_{x,y\in\Xi,x\neq y}|x-y|>0,

and if, for some
\rho>0
,
\{B_{\rho}(x)\}_{x\in\Xi}
is a covering of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
of uniformly finite multiplicity.

Note that if
\Xi
is a discretization of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
then, for any
R>\rho
, the covering
\{B_{R}(x)\}_{x\in\Xi}
is still of uniformly finite multiplicity. A trivial example for a discretization of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is
\mathbb{Z}^{N}
.

When we shall deal with sequences

Y^{(n)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}(y_{k}^{(n)})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
depending on a parameter
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we will abbreviate the notation for the magnetic potentials at infinity
A^{(Y^{(n)})}_{\infty}
as
A^{(n)}_{\infty}
(and the corresponding magnetic field as
B^{(n)}_{\infty}
).
Moreover, for any
n
and for any
k
,
g_{y^{(n)}_{k}}
will represent the magnetic shift defined by (
2.12
) with
y=y^{(n)}_{k}
, and we will use the abbreviated notation

(3.1)

g^{(n)}_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}g_{y^{(n)}_{k}}\quad\mbox{ as well as }\quad A^{(n)}_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}A_{y^{(n)}_{k}}(\cdot+y^{(n)}_{k})\,.

In what follows we use weak convergence in
H^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which is a weak convergence in a Fréchet space. In particular weakly converging sequences will converge in the sense of distributions, strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega)
whenever
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded measurable set and
p\in(2,2^{*})
, as well as almost everywhere.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Xi\ni 0
be a discretization of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Let
A\in\dot{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\Lambda_{1})
,
N\geq 2
,
A(0)=0
,
and let
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a bounded sequence in

.
Then, there
exist
v^{(n)}\in H^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
(with
v^{(0)}=\mathop{w-\rm{lim}}_{k\to\infty}u_{k}\in H_{A}^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
),
Y^{(n)}:=(y_{k}^{(n)})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\Xi
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
with
y_{k}^{(0)}=0
,
such that, on a renamed subsequence,

(3.2)

\displaystyle(g^{(n)}_{k})^{-1}u_{k}\rightharpoonup v^{(n)}\quad\mbox{ in }H^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(3.3)

\displaystyle|y_{k}^{(n)}-y_{k}^{(m)}|\to\infty\mbox{ for }n\neq m,

(3.4)

\displaystyle r_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}u_{k}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g^{(n)}_{k}v^{(n)}\to 0\,\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\,\forall p\in(2,2^{*}),

and the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|v^{(n)}(\cdot-y_{k}^{(n)})|
and the series in (

) converge

Moreover, for any

g^{-1}_{y_{k}}u_{k}\rightharpoonup 0\mbox{ in }H^{1,2}_{\mathrm{{}_{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad\mbox{ for all }\;(y_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\mbox{ s.t. }|y_{k}-y^{(n)}_{k}|\to+\infty.

.

Assume conditions of Theorem
3.2

4.
Applications

In this section make the following assumption:

(4.1)

A\in\dot{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\Lambda_{1}),\;V\in C(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)>0,

and consider

J_{A,V}(u)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(|(\nabla_{A}u(x)|^{2}+V(x)|u(x)|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}{x}.

For a fixed

we consider a minimization problem

(4.3)

\kappa_{A,V}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\inf_{u\in H^{1,2}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}{x}=1}J_{A,V}(u).

.
Given a sequence

, let

be as in Remark

, let

) with

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume (
4.1
). Let

be a discretization of

. If for every sequence

(4.5)

\kappa_{\infty}^{(Y)}>\kappa_{A,V},

then the minimum in (

Proof.

Let
(u_{k})
be a minimization sequence for (

3.2
, and using the notation

for
V_{\infty}^{Y^{(}n)}
(here and further on in the similar context),
we easily get

\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V|u_{k}|^{2}\mathrm{d}{x}\geq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V_{\infty}^{(n)}|v^{(n)}|^{2}\mathrm{d}{x},

which, combined with (

), yields

(4.7)

\kappa_{A,V}=\liminf_{k\to\infty}J_{A,V}(u_{k})\geq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}J_{\infty}^{(n)}(v^{(n)}).

At the same time, setting

, we have from (

Then from (

which in view of (

and for all

. This impies that

and therefore
u_{k}\to v^{(0)}
in

and
v^{(0)}
is a minimizer.
∎

This lemma allows us to reduce the proof of existence of ground state below to verification of (

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that

and let

(5.1)

\displaystyle J(u)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\bigl|\nabla_{A}u\bigr|^{2}-\frac{\mu}{x_{1}^{2}}\bigl|u\bigr|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}{x}

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
\lambda^{2}\leq\mu<\frac{1}{4}
. Then the minimum in the problem

(5.2)

\kappa\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\inf_{u\in\dot{H}^{1,2}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x)|^{p^{*}}\mathrm{d}{x}=1}J(u)

By the diamagnetic and the Hardy inequalities

(u_{k})

Consider the action of scaling on the electric potential V(x)=-

(5.3)

or, respectively

possibly up to a fixed scaling, which can be eliminated by redefining the respective concentration profile

Let us write (

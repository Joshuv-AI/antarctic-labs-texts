Introduction

Modal and intuitionistic propositional logics are often poly-time
embeddable into their own fragments with a few variables (typically,
zero, one, or two), and similar embeddings are sometimes constructed
of fragments of logics with special properties into finite-variable
fragments of those logics. The literature on the topic is quite

As a result, the validity problem for such fragments is as
computationally hard as the validity problem for the full logic. (In
general, modal and superintuitionistic propositional logics, even
linearly approximable ones, may have arbitrarily hard fragments with a
few variables since, for every set

, one can

2
Preliminaries

The intuitionistic modal language contains a countable set

\mathcal{P}
of propositional variables, the constant
\bot
, binary
connectives
\wedge
,
\vee
, and
\to
, and unary modal connectives

\Diamond
and
\Box
. Formulas are defined in the usual way. A
formula is
positive
if it does not contain occurrences of

\bot
. The set of propositional variables of a formula
\varphi
is
denoted by
\mathop{\mathit{var}}\varphi
. The result of substituting a
formula
\psi
for a variable
p
into a formula
\varphi
is denoted by

[\psi/p]\varphi
. The modal depth of a formula
\varphi
, denoted by

\mathop{\mathit{md}}\varphi
, is the maximal number of nested modal connectives in

\varphi
. The length of a formula
\varphi
, defined as the number of symbols
in
\varphi
(with the binary encoding of variables), is denoted by

|\varphi|
.

We define the logics
\mathbf{FS}
and
\mathbf{MIPC}
semantically.
A
Kripke frame
is a pair

\mathfrak{F}=\langle W,R\rangle
where
W
is a non-empty set of

worlds
and
R
is a partial order on
W
. An

\mathbf{FS}
-frame
is a triple

\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{F}}=\langle W,R,\delta\rangle
, where

\langle W,R\rangle
is a Kripke frame and
\delta
is a map
associating with each
w\in W
a structure

\langle\Delta_{w},S_{w}\rangle
, with
\Delta_{w}
being a non-empty set
of
points
and
S_{w}
a binary relation on
\Delta_{w}
such
that, for every
w,v\in W
,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}v\in R(w)\penalty\ \Rightarrow\penalty\ \Delta_{w}\subseteq\Delta_{v}&\mbox{ and }&\mbox{$S_{w}\subseteq S_{v}$}\end{array}

An

\mathbf{FS}
-frame

\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{F}}=\langle W,R,\delta\rangle

is an
\mathbf{MIPC}
-frame
if

S_{w}=\Delta_{w}\times\Delta_{w}
, for every
w\in W
. A

valuation
on an
\mathbf{FS}
-frame

\langle W,R,\delta\rangle
is a map associating with each

w\in W
and each
p\in\mathcal{P}
a subset
V(w,p)
of

\Delta_{w}
in such a way that

v\in R(w)\penalty\ \Rightarrow\penalty\ V(w,p)\subseteq V(v,p).

The pair
\mathfrak{M}=\langle\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{F}},V\rangle
, where

\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{F}}
is an
\mathbf{FS}
-frame and
V
a valuation on

\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{F}}
, is called an
\mathbf{FS}
-model
. An

\mathbf{MIPC}
-model
is an
\mathbf{FS}
-model over an

\mathbf{MIPC}
-frame. The
truth-relation
\models
is
defined by recursion (here,
\mathfrak{M}
is a model,
w\in W
,

x\in\Delta_{w}
, and
\varphi
is a formula):

•

\mathfrak{M},w,x\models p

\leftrightharpoons

x\in V(w,p)
if
p\in\mathcal{P}
;

•

\mathfrak{M},w,x\not\models\bot
;

•

\mathfrak{M},w,x\models\varphi_{1}\wedge\varphi_{2}

\leftrightharpoons

\mathfrak{M},w,x\models\varphi_{1}

and
\mathfrak{M},w,x\models\varphi_{2}
;

•

\mathfrak{M},w,x\models\varphi_{1}\vee\varphi_{2}

\leftrightharpoons

\mathfrak{M},w,x\models\varphi_{1}

or
\mathfrak{M},w,x\models\varphi_{2}
;

•

\mathfrak{M},w,x\models\varphi_{1}\to\varphi_{2}

\leftrightharpoons

\mathfrak{M},v,x\not\models\varphi_{1}

or
\mathfrak{M},v,x\models\varphi_{2}

whenever
v\in R(w)
;

•

\mathfrak{M},w,x\models\Diamond\varphi_{1}

\leftrightharpoons

\mathfrak{M},w,y\models\varphi_{1}
,
for some
y\in S_{w}(x)
;

•

\mathfrak{M},w,x\models\Box\varphi_{1}

\leftrightharpoons

\mathfrak{M},v,y\models\varphi_{1}

whenever
v\in R(w)
and
y\in S_{v}(x)
.

A formula
\varphi
is
true
in a model
\mathfrak{M}
(notation:

\mathfrak{M}\models\varphi
) if
\mathfrak{M},w,x\models\varphi
, for
every world
w
of
\mathfrak{M}
and every point
x
of
w
. A
formula
\varphi
is
valid
an
\mathbf{FS}
-frame
\mathfrak{F}

if
\varphi
is true in every model over
\mathfrak{F}
. Logics

\mathbf{FS}
and
\mathbf{MIPC}
are defined as sets of formulas
valid on, respectively, every
\mathbf{FS}
-frame and every

\mathbf{MIPC}
-frame.

3
Main results

In this section, we prove that logics
\mathbf{FS}
and

\mathbf{MIPC}
are polynomial-time embeddable into their own
fragments with a single propositional variable. We first poly-time
embed these logics into their own positive fragments. Let
\varphi
be a
formula and
f\in\mathcal{P}\setminus\mathop{\mathit{var}}\varphi
.
Define

\begin{array}[]{llll}\varphi^{f}=[f/\bot]\varphi;&F_{1}=\Diamond^{\leqslant\mathop{\mathit{md}}\varphi}f\to f;&F_{2}=f\to\Box^{\leqslant\mathop{\mathit{md}}\varphi}f;&\displaystyle F_{3}=\bigwedge\limits_{\mathclap{p\in\mathop{\mathit{var}}\varphi}}\Box^{\leqslant\mathop{\mathit{md}}\varphi}(f\to p),\end{array}

and put
F=F_{1}\wedge F_{2}\wedge F_{3}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\varphi
be a formula,

f\in\mathcal{P}\setminus\mathop{\mathit{var}}\varphi
, and

L\in\{\mathbf{FS},\mathbf{MIPC}\}
. Then,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\varphi\in L&\iff&F\to\varphi^{f}\in L.\end{array}

Since
\varphi^{f}
and
F
are both positive, the map

e\colon\varphi\mapsto(F\to\varphi^{f})
embeds
\mathbf{FS}
and

\mathbf{MIPC}
into their own positive fragments.

We next define a polytime computable function
\cdot^{\ast}
from the
set of positive formulas to the set of one-variable positive formulas
and show that, for
L\in\{\mathbf{FS},\mathbf{MIPC}\}
and every
positive
\varphi
,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\varphi^{\ast}\in L&\iff&\varphi\in L.\end{array}

Hence, for every
\varphi
,

\begin{array}[]{lclcl}\varphi\in L&\iff&e(\varphi)\in L&\iff&e(\varphi)^{\ast}\in L.\end{array}

The formula
\varphi^{\ast}
shall be obtain from
\varphi
using a
substitution. We next define the formulas that shall be substituted
for propositional variables of
\varphi
. These formulas, except
G_{1}
,

G_{2}
, and
G_{3}
, are divided into ‘levels’, indexed by elements
of
\mathds{N}
; formulas of level
0
are denoted
A^{0}_{i}
or
B^{0}_{i}
,
those of level
1
, by
A^{1}_{i}
and
B^{1}_{i}
, etc. We begin with
G_{1}
,

G_{2}
, and
G_{3}
, as well as formulas of levels
0
and
1
:

\begin{array}[]{lclclcl}G_{1}&=&\Diamond p;&A_{1}^{1}&=&A_{1}^{0}\wedge A_{2}^{0}\to B_{1}^{0}\vee B_{2}^{0};\\
G_{2}&=&\Diamond p\to p;&A_{2}^{1}&=&A_{1}^{0}\wedge B_{1}^{0}\to A_{2}^{0}\vee B_{2}^{0};\\
G_{3}&=&p\to\Box p;&A_{3}^{1}&=&A_{1}^{0}\wedge B_{2}^{0}\to A_{2}^{0}\vee B_{1}^{0};\\
A_{1}^{0}&=&G_{2}\to G_{1}\vee G_{3};&B_{1}^{1}&=&A_{2}^{0}\wedge B_{1}^{0}\to A_{1}^{0}\vee B_{2}^{0};\\
A_{2}^{0}&=&G_{3}\to G_{1}\vee G_{2};&B_{2}^{1}&=&A_{2}^{0}\wedge B_{2}^{0}\to A_{1}^{0}\vee B_{1}^{0};\\
B_{1}^{0}&=&G_{1}\to G_{2}\vee G_{3};&B_{3}^{1}&=&B_{1}^{0}\wedge B_{2}^{0}\to A_{1}^{0}\vee A_{2}^{0}.\\
B_{2}^{0}&=&A_{1}^{0}\wedge A_{2}^{0}\wedge B_{1}^{0}\to G_{1}\vee G_{2}\vee G_{3};\\
\end{array}

We proceed by recursion. Let
k\geqslant 1
. Suppose the formulas

A^{k}_{1},\ldots,A^{k}_{n_{k}}
and
B^{k}_{1},\ldots,B^{k}_{n_{k}}
have been
defined, with
n_{k}
being the number of formulas of the form
A^{k}_{i}

and, also, the number of formulas of the form
B^{k}_{i}
(e.g., if

k=1
, then
n_{k}=3
; the recursive definition for the cases where

k\geqslant 2
is to be given). Define a linear order
\prec
on the
set
(\mathds{N}\setminus\{0,1\})\times(\mathds{N}\setminus\{0,1\})
as in
the following picture, so that

\langle i,j\rangle\prec\langle i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\rangle
if, and only if,
there exists a path along one or more arrows from

\langle i,j\rangle
to
\langle i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\rangle
:

1
2
3
4
5
6
7
8
9
1
2
3
4
5
6
7
8
\vdots

We can then define an enumeration
g
of the pairs

\langle i,j\rangle\in(\mathds{N}\setminus\{0,1\})\times(\mathds{N}\setminus\{0,1\})
according to
\prec
, i.e., so that

g(2,2)=1
,
g(3,2)=2
,
g(3,3)=3
,
g(2,3)=4
, etc. Now,
for every
i,j\in\{2,\ldots,n_{k}\}
, define

\begin{array}[]{lclclcl}A_{g(i,j)}^{k+1}&=&A_{1}^{k}\to B_{1}^{k}\vee A_{i}^{k}\vee B_{j}^{k};&&B_{g(i,j)}^{k+1}&=&B_{1}^{k}\to A_{1}^{k}\vee A_{i}^{k}\vee B_{j}^{k},\end{array}

and let
n_{k+1}
be the number of the formulas of the form

A_{i}^{k+1}
(which is the same as the number of formulas of the form

B_{i}^{k+1}
) so defined; notice that
n_{k+1}=(n_{k}-1)^{2}
. This
completes the recursive definition of
A^{k}_{i}
and
B^{k}_{i}
.

Next, put

\begin{array}[]{lcl}l_{0}&=&|A^{0}_{1}|+|B^{0}_{1}|+|A^{0}_{2}|+|B^{0}_{2}|.\end{array}

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists
k_{0}\in\mathds{N}
such that
n_{k}>l_{0}\cdot 5^{k}

whenever
k\geqslant k_{0}
.

Now, let
\varphi
be a positive formula with

\mathop{\mathit{var}}\varphi=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{s}\}
. Let
{k_{\varphi}}
be
the least integer
k
such that
|\varphi|<l_{0}\cdot 5^{k}
. By
Lemma
3.2
,

n_{{k_{\varphi}}+k_{0}}>l_{0}\cdot 5^{{k_{\varphi}}+k_{0}}
; hence,

n_{{k_{\varphi}}+k_{0}}>l_{0}\cdot 5^{{k_{\varphi}}+k_{0}}>5^{k_{0}}\cdot|\varphi|>|\varphi|\geqslant s.

Lastly, define
\varphi^{\ast}
to be the result of substituting into
\varphi
,
for every
r\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
, the formula

A_{r}^{{k_{\varphi}}+k_{0}}\vee B_{r}^{{k_{\varphi}}+k_{0}}
for the variable
p_{r}

(this substitution is well defined since

n_{{k_{\varphi}}+k_{0}}>s
).

We next show that
\varphi^{\ast}
is poly-time computable from
\varphi
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For every
k\geqslant 0
and every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n_{k}\}
,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}|A^{k}_{i}|<l_{0}\cdot 5^{k}&\mbox{and}&|B^{k}_{i}|<l_{0}\cdot 5^{k}.\end{array}

Lemma 3.4
.

The formula
\varphi^{\ast}
is computable in time polynomial
in
|\varphi|
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
|\varphi^{\ast}|
is polynomial in
|\varphi|
.
Since
{k_{\varphi}}
is the least integer
k
such that

|\varphi|<l_{0}\cdot 5^{k}
, surely

l_{0}\cdot 5^{k_{\varphi}-1}\leqslant|\varphi|
, and so

l_{0}\cdot 5^{k_{\varphi}+k_{0}}\leqslant 5^{k_{0}+1}|\varphi|.

By Lemma
3.3
, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n_{k_{\varphi}+k_{0}}\}
,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}|A^{k_{\varphi}+k_{0}}_{i}|<l_{0}\cdot 5^{k_{\varphi}+k_{0}}\leqslant 5^{k_{0}+1}|\varphi|&\mbox{and}&|B^{k_{\varphi}+k_{0}}_{i}|<l_{0}\cdot 5^{k_{\varphi}+k_{0}}\leqslant 5^{k_{0}+1}|\varphi|.\end{array}

Hence,
|\varphi^{\ast}|<2\cdot 5^{k_{0}+1}|\varphi|^{2}
.
∎

To obtain the desired result, it remains to show the following:

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
L\in\{\mathbf{FS},\mathbf{MIPC}\}
. Then, for every
positive formula
\varphi
,

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\varphi\in L&\iff&\varphi^{\ast}\in L.\end{array}

From Lemmas
3.1
,
3.4
,
and
3.5
, we immediately obtain the following:

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
L\in\{\mathbf{FS},\mathbf{MIPC}\}
. Then, there exists a
polynomial-time computable function embedding
L
into its own
positive one-variable fragment.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
L\in\{\mathbf{FS},\mathbf{MIPC}\}
. Then, the positive
one-variable fragment of
L
is polytime-equivalent to
L
.

The results presented here are not immediately applicable to obtaining
the computational complexity of finite-variable fragments of
intuitionistic modal logics since the complexity of full logics
remains unknown (we are only aware of decidability
results
[

,

,

,
1
,

for modal intuitionistic logics).

Abstract
We study charged massless fermionic perturbations in the background of 4 4 -dimensional linear dilaton black holes in Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theory with double Liouville-type potentials. We present the analytical fermionic quasinormal modes, whose Dirac equations are solved in terms of hypergeometric functions. We also discuss the stability of these black holes under the charged fermionic perturbations.

Solution of charged Dirac equation in LDBH geometry

After substituting the spin coefficients (
3.4

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f_{2}}\widetilde{Z}f_{1}-\frac{\left(LA_{3}\right)}{A_{1}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f_{1}}\overline{\widetilde{Z}}f_{2}+\frac{\left(L^{{\dagger}}A_{1}\right)}{A_{3}}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{g_{1}}\widetilde{Z}g_{2}-\frac{\left(L^{{\dagger}}A_{2}\right)}{A_{4}}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{g_{2}}\overline{\widetilde{Z}}g_{1}+\frac{\left(LA_{4}\right)}{A_{2}}

\displaystyle=0.

The radial operators appear in the above simplified Dirac equations are

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}

\displaystyle=A\sqrt{\Lambda}\partial_{r}+H+\frac{iAkr}{\sqrt{\Lambda}},

\displaystyle\overline{\widetilde{Z}}

The angular operators are

\displaystyle=\partial_{\theta}+\frac{m}{\sin\theta}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-p\right)\cot\theta,

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}g_{2}

\displaystyle=-\lambda g_{1},

\displaystyle L^{{\dagger}}A_{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda A_{4},

\displaystyle LA_{4}

\Lambda G_{j}^{\prime\prime}-\frac{b(r_{2}+r_{1}-2r)}{2}G_{j}^{\prime}+\left\{(-1)^{j}ik\left[\frac{b(r_{2}r_{1}-r^{2})}{\Lambda}-\frac{b(r_{2}+r_{1}-2r)}{2\Lambda}\right]+\frac{k^{2}r^{2}}{\Lambda}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{A^{2}}\right\}G_{j}=0.

(4.33)

As
r\rightarrow\infty
, Eq. (
4.33
) takes the following form

r^{2}G_{j}^{\prime\prime}+rG_{j}^{\prime}+(\frac{k^{2}A^{2}-\lambda^{2}b}{A^{2}b^{2}}=\widetilde{\alpha}^{2})G_{j}=0.

(4.34)

whose solutions are found to be

G_{j}=\widetilde{D}_{j}r^{i\widetilde{\alpha}}+\widehat{C}_{j}r^{-i\widetilde{\alpha}}.

(4.35)

Since
e^{i\eta r_{\ast}}=r^{i\widetilde{\alpha}}
and the wave should be
purely outgoing at the spatial infinity, we set
\widehat{C}_{j}=0
and thus find

G_{j}=\widetilde{D}_{j}r^{i\widetilde{\alpha}}
which is nothing but the same
result that was obtained in Eq. (
4.32
).

5
Exact QNM frequencies

To find the analytical solutions of
G_{j}
, we first introduce

G_{j}=H_{j}e^{(-1)^{j+1}ikr_{\ast}},

(5.1)

and change the variable to

z=-x=-\frac{r-r_{2}}{r_{2}-r_{1}}.

(5.2)

After inserting Eq. (
5.2
) in Eq. (
4.33
), we obtain

z(1-z)H_{j}^{\prime\prime}+\left[\boldsymbol{c}-(1+\boldsymbol{a}+\boldsymbol{b})z\right]H_{j}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{b}H_{j}=0,

(5.3)

which is the Euler’s hypergeometric differential equation
[
56
]
with

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{a}

\displaystyle=i\left[(-1)^{j+1}\frac{k}{b}+\widetilde{\alpha}\right],

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{b}

\displaystyle=i\left[(-1)^{j+1}\frac{k}{b}-\widetilde{\alpha}\right],

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}+2ik(-1)^{j+1}\frac{r_{2}}{b(r_{2}-r_{1})}.

(5.4)

The general solution of Eq. (
5.3
) is given by
[
56
]

H_{j}=C_{2}F(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b};\boldsymbol{c};z)+C_{1}z^{1-\boldsymbol{c}}F(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{a}},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{b}};\widetilde{\boldsymbol{c}};z),

(5.5)

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are the integration constants and
F(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b};\boldsymbol{c};z)
and
F(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{a}},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{b}};\widetilde{\boldsymbol{c}};z)
are the
hypergeometric (or Gaussian) functions with

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{a}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{c}+1=\frac{1}{2}-i\left[k(-1)^{j+1}\frac{r_{2}+r_{1}}{b(r_{2}-r_{1})}+\widetilde{\alpha}\right],

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{b}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{b}-\boldsymbol{c}+1=\frac{1}{2}-i\left[k(-1)^{j+1}\frac{r_{2}+r_{1}}{b(r_{2}-r_{1})}-\widetilde{\alpha}\right],

\displaystyle\widetilde{\boldsymbol{c}}

\displaystyle=2-\boldsymbol{c}=\frac{3}{2}-2ik(-1)^{j+1}\frac{r_{2}}{b(r_{2}-r_{1})}.

(5.6)

In the near-horizon region:
r\rightarrow r_{2},

r_{\ast}\rightarrow-\infty

\Rightarrow

z\approx e^{r_{\ast}}\rightarrow 0
. By recalling that

F(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b};\boldsymbol{c};0)=1

[
56
]
, the
physical solutions (
5.1
), which fulfill the QNM condition that only

\displaystyle G_{2}

\displaystyle=H_{2}e^{-ikr_{\ast}}=C_{2}e^{-ikr_{\ast}}F(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b};\boldsymbol{c};z),

\displaystyle G_{1}

\displaystyle=H_{1}e^{ikr_{\ast}}=C_{1}z^{1-\boldsymbol{c}}e^{+ikr_{\ast}}F(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{a}},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{b}};\widetilde{\boldsymbol{c}};z).

(5.7)

By using one of the special features of the hypergeometric functions

[
56
]

\displaystyle F(\boldsymbol{a},\mathbf{b};\boldsymbol{c};y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\boldsymbol{c})\Gamma(\boldsymbol{b}-\boldsymbol{a})}{\Gamma(\boldsymbol{b})\Gamma(\boldsymbol{c}-\boldsymbol{a})}(-y)^{-\boldsymbol{a}}F(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{a}+1-\boldsymbol{c};\boldsymbol{a}+1-\boldsymbol{b};1/y)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Gamma(\boldsymbol{c})\Gamma(\boldsymbol{a}-\boldsymbol{b})}{\Gamma(\boldsymbol{a})\Gamma(\boldsymbol{c}-\boldsymbol{b})}(-y)^{-\boldsymbol{b}}F(\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{b}+1-\boldsymbol{c};\boldsymbol{b}+1-\boldsymbol{a};1/y),

(5.8)

one can extend the solutions (

\displaystyle=C_{1}z^{1-\boldsymbol{c}}e^{ikr_{\ast}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\bigg[}\frac{\Gamma(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{c}})\Gamma(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{b}}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{a}})}{\Gamma(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{b}})\Gamma(\boldsymbol{c}-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{a}})}(x)^{-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{a}}}F(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{a}},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{a}}+1-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{c}};\widetilde{\boldsymbol{a}}+1-\widetilde{\boldsymbol{b}};1/z)

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have analytically computed the QNMs of charged fermionic
perturbations for the
4
-dimensional LDBHs which are the solutions to the EMD
theory with the double Lioville-type potentials. The fermionic fields are the solutions of the Dirac equation. For the LDBH geometry, we have found that the massless but charged fermionic fields are governed by the Gaussian hypergeometric functions. We matched the exact solutions obtained with the solutions found for near-horizon and asymptotic regions. Thus, we have shown that there are two sets of frequencies for each component (
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
) of the fermionic fields. As being summarized in Tables
1
and
2
, in each set the frequencies obtained can belong to either the stable modes (QNMs) or the unstable modes depending on the relations between the parameters of
A,\lambda,b,
and
n
. Meanwhile, the reader may question what the advantage/disadvantage of perturbing the LDBH with fermionic fields instead of the bosonic fields is. If precise measurements of QNM frequencies become possible in the future and if the fermionic QNM data obtained for spin-up and spin-down fields (i.e.,
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
) coincide with (a kind of double check) the analytical expressions that we have derived, those results will likely reveal the presence of LDBHs more accurately than the bosonic QNM ones.

As is known, rotating BH solutions are more considerable for testing relevant theories with the astrophysical observations. For this reason, in the near future, we plan to extend our study to the rotating LDBHs. To this end, we shall apply the Newman–Janis algorithm
[
57
]
to the metric (
2.9
) and perturb the obtained rotating LDBH to reveal the effect of rotation on their QNMs.

Flavour anti- k T k_{\text{T}} algorithm applied to W ⁡ b ​ b ¯ \operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b} production at the LHC Preprint: CAVENDISH-HEP-22/07
Heribertus Bayu Hartanto, Rene Poncelet, Andrei Popescu, Simone Zoia

Abstract
We apply the recently proposed flavoured anti- k T k_{\text{T}} jet algorithm to W ⁡ b ​ b ¯ \operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b} production at the Large Hadron Collider at s = 8 \sqrt{s}=8 TeV. We present results for the total cross section and differential distributions at the next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO) in QCD. We discuss the effects of the remaining parametric freedom in the flavoured anti- k T k_{\text{T}} prescription, and compare it against the standard flavour- k T k_{\text{T}} algorithm. We compare the total cross section results against the CMS data, finding good agreement. The NNLO QCD corrections are significant, and their inclusion substantially improves the agreement with the data.



1
Introduction

The Large Hadron Collider (LHC) has recently started its Run 3 phase, following the successful Run 1 and 2 campaigns.
The LHC dataset collected from the previous runs, combined with the current Run 3 and the upcoming
high-luminosity LHC (HL-LHC) phase, will provide opportunities to stress test the Standard Model of Particle Physics (SM) and constrain new physics scenarios.
To keep pace with the increasingly accurate experimental measurements, theory predictions must be provided with the highest possible precision.
One of the current precision frontiers at the LHC is the next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO) in QCD for
2\to 3
scattering processes.
The last few years have seen a major breakthrough in the massless
2\to 3
computations, where NNLO
QCD predictions for
pp\to\gamma\gamma\gamma

Kallweit:2020gcp
;
Chawdhry:2019bji
,
pp\to\gamma\gamma+
jet
Chawdhry:2021hkp
;
Badger:2021ohm
and, most notably,
pp\to
3-jet production
Czakon:2021mjy
;
Chen:2022ktf
have become available.
The first NNLO QCD calculation for
2\to 3
process involving one external mass has recently been
completed for the production of W-boson in association with a
b\bar{b}
pair, where the W-boson decays leptonically and the bottom quark is treated as a massless particle
Hartanto:2022qhh
.
We call this process ‘
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
production’ hereafter.

\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
production has been studied quite extensively, particularly at next-to-leading order (NLO) in QCD
Ellis:1998fv
;
FebresCordero:2006nvf
;
FebresCordero:2009xzo
;
Badger:2010mg
;
Oleari:2011ey
;
Frederix:2011qg
;
Luisoni:2015mpa
;
Anger:2017glm
.
The studies carried out at NLO QCD demonstrated that, for inclusive production (W in association with at least two
b
-jets in the final state), the higher-order corrections are significant and the theoretical uncertainty at NLO is larger than that of the LO prediction.
This pathological behaviour is attributed to the contribution of
qg
-initiated subprocesses, which start contributing at NLO.
The inclusion of the NNLO QCD corrections improves the perturbative convergence: the corrections become moderate, compared to NLO QCD, and the theoretical uncertainties appear to be under control.
Experimental measurements for
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
production have been undertaken at both the Tevatron
D0:2004prj
and the LHC
CMS:2013xis
;
CMS:2016eha
.

This interest in
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
production stems from multiple reasons.
First of all, the
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0pt+2b
-jets signature is also produced by other SM scattering processes, such as
the
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0pt+
Higgs-strahlung process, where the Higgs boson decays to a bottom-quark pair (i.e.
pp\to\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0pt(\operatorname{H}\kern-1.0pt\to b\bar{b})
), and single top production (i.e.
pp\to(t\to\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb)\bar{b}
).
The analysis of these important processes therefore requires good control of the irreducible background, represented by
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
production.
We note that the theoretical predictions for both the
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0pt\operatorname{H}\kern-1.0pt\to b\bar{b})
and single top productions are known through
NNLO in QCD
Ferrera:2013yga
;
Campbell:2016jau
;
Ferrera:2017zex
;
Caola:2017xuq
;
Gauld:2019yng
;
Behring:2020uzq
;
Astill:2016hpa
;
Zanoli:2021iyp
.
Processes involving new physics may lead to the very same experimental signature as well.

\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
production is also a perfect testing ground to study the modelling of flavoured jets from both theoretical and experimental points of view.
An exclusive final state, where additional jets are vetoed,
is often required in the experimental analyses to suppress the contributions from certain background processes.
The exclusive setup requires a more careful treatment of the theoretical uncertainties, as we already showed in Ref.
Hartanto:2022qhh
, where we compared the standard and the more conservative prescription of Ref.
Stewart:2011cf
.

The presence of
b
-quark-flavoured jets in the final state requires a flavour tagging procedure.
Experimentally,
b
-jets are tagged by identifying jets that contain a B-hadron by exploiting its long lifetime, looking for secondary vertices and tracks with a large impact parameter.
In theoretical simulations, the procedure depends on the type of calculation employed.
If a parton shower is involved, one can basically apply the experimental prescription, i.e. sequentially hadronise the showered event and put ‘
b
-jet’ labels on the clustered jets which contain a B-hadron.
In a fixed-order calculation, the final state partons are assigned to jets by means of a clustering algorithm.

If the bottom quark is treated as a massless particle in a fixed-order calculation, care must be taken to ensure the infrared (IR) safety.
A problem might in fact arise from the assignment of flavoured particles which make up a soft pair to different clusters, thus spoiling the IR safety of the jet algorithm for the soft gluon emission.
This issue indeed sets in for the standard (flavour-blind) clustering algorithms, starting at NNLO.
We note that, if the bottom quark mass is instead taken into account, these IR divergences in the fixed-order calculation are regulated.

This problem can be remedied by employing a flavour-sensitive jet algorithm. The flavour-
k_{\text{T}}
jet algorithm of Ref.
Banfi:2006hf
was proposed for this reason, and has been applied to several scattering processes at the LHC which involve final state bottom- or charm-quark jet(s)
Caola:2017xuq
;
Ferrera:2017zex
;
Gauld:2019yng
;
Gauld:2020ced
;
Gauld:2020deh
;
Czakon:2020coa
;
Hartanto:2022qhh
.
The major drawback in using the flavour-
k_{\text{T}}
algorithm is that the experimental analyses at the LHC widely employ the standard anti-
k_{\text{T}}
prescription
Cacciari:2008gp
.
A comparison of the flavour-
k_{\text{T}}
-based theoretical predictions to the experiments requires the experimental data to be unfolded from the standard anti-
k_{\text{T}}
to the flavour-
k_{\text{T}}
clustering, through the use of an NLO calculation matched to a parton shower (NLO+PS)
Gauld:2020deh
.
The unfolding procedure represents an additional source of uncertainty, which becomes sizeable when the clustering behaviours are significantly different.
In order to minimise the effect of unfolding, a flavour-sensitive anti-
k_{\text{T}}
jet algorithm
Czakon:2022wam
has been recently introduced.
It matches more closely the standard anti-
k_{\text{T}}
prescription, and thus enables a more direct comparison with the experimental measurements at the LHC.
Recently, a new approach to dress anti-
k_{\text{T}}
jets with flavour has been proposed in Ref.
Gauld:2022lem
.

In this work we apply the newly proposed flavoured anti-
k_{\text{T}}
jet algorithm to
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
production at NNLO QCD, compare with the results obtained using the flavour-
k_{\text{T}}
algorithm, and compare the resulting cross sections to the measurements by the CMS collaboration
CMS:2016eha
.
This paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we briefly review the ingredients of the calculation.
Phenomenological results employing both variants of flavour sensitive jet algorithm are discussed in Section
3
.
The genuine impact of NNLO QCD corrections is further studied in Section
4
by comparing the NNLO calculation against an improved NLO prediction. We finally present our conclusions in Section
5
.

2
Calculation and setup

We present the calculation of the
pp\to\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0pt(\to\ell\nu)b\bar{b}
process at NNLO in QCD.
The computation has been performed within the
Stripper
framework, a
C++
implementation of the four-dimensional formulation of the sector-improved residue subtraction scheme
Czakon:2010td
;
Czakon:2014oma
;
Czakon:2019tmo
.
The tree-level matrix elements are supplied by the
AvH
library
Bury:2015dla
, while the one-loop matrix elements are provided by the
OpenLoops
package
Buccioni:2017yxi
;
Buccioni:2019sur
.
We computed the two-loop virtual amplitudes analytically in the leading colour approximation as described in Ref.
Hartanto:2022qhh
, following the strategy of Ref.
Badger:2021nhg
.
The resulting analytic expressions were implemented in
C++
for a fast numerical evaluation with the
PentagonFunctions++
library
Chicherin:2021dyp
as a backend for evaluating the special functions.

We work in the five-flavour scheme (5FS), where the bottom quark is treated as massless and is present in the initial state.
We present numerical results for the LHC center-of-mass energy
\sqrt{s}=8
TeV.
We take the Cabbibo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) matrix to be diagonal and use the following input parameters:

\displaystyle M_{\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0pt}=80.351972\;\mathrm{GeV},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0pt}=2.0842989\;\mathrm{GeV},

\displaystyle M_{\operatorname{Z}\kern-1.0pt}=91.153481\;\mathrm{GeV},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\operatorname{Z}\kern-1.0pt}=2.4942665\;\mathrm{GeV},

\displaystyle G_{F}=1.16638\cdot 10^{-5}\;\mathrm{GeV}^{-2}.

The electromagnetic coupling
\alpha
is determined within the
G_{\mu}
scheme using the following relation:

\alpha=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\pi}G_{F}M_{\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0pt}^{2}\;\bigg(1-\frac{M_{\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0pt}^{2}}{M_{\operatorname{Z}\kern-1.0pt}^{2}}\bigg).

(1)

We use the NNPDF3.1 PDF sets
NNPDF:2017mvq
via the LHAPDF interface
Buckley:2014ana
.
Specifically, we employ
NNPDF31_lo_as_0118
,
NNPDF31_nlo_as_0118
,
NNPDF31_nnlo_as_0118

for the leading order (LO), NLO and NNLO QCD calculations, respectively.

As we mentioned in Section
1
, we employ both the flavour-sensitive
k_{\text{T}}
and anti-
k_{\text{T}}
jet algorithms, with the separation parameter
R=0.5
.
To define the fiducial region, we adopt the following selection cuts for
the jets and charged leptons
CMS:2016eha
;
Hartanto:2022qhh
:

\displaystyle p_{\text{T},\ell}>30\;\mathrm{GeV},\qquad\qquad|\eta_{\ell}|<2.1,

\displaystyle p_{\text{T},j}>25\;\mathrm{GeV},\qquad\qquad|\eta_{j}|<2.4,

(2)

\displaystyle p_{\text{T},b}>25\;\mathrm{GeV},\qquad\qquad|\eta_{b}|<2.4.

In addition, we consider two different final state signatures:

•

inclusive
: at least two
b
-jets are required in the final state;

•

exclusive
: exactly two
b
-jets and no other jets are required in the final state.

The jet veto parameter required to define the exclusive final state follows from Eq. (
2
).

To obtain our predictions, we use a kinematic-dependent quantity for the renormalisation (
\mu_{R})
and factorisation (
\mu_{F}
) scales.
In particular, we utilise

H_{\text{T}}=E_{\text{T}}(\ell\nu)+p_{\text{T}}(b_{1})+p_{\text{T}}(b_{2})\,,

(3)

where
E_{\text{T}}(\ell\nu)=\sqrt{M^{2}(\ell\nu)+p^{2}_{\text{T}}(\ell\nu)}
is
the transverse energy of the lepton-neutrino system (i.e. the off-shell
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0pt
boson),
M(\ell\nu)
and
p_{\text{T}}(\ell\nu)
are the
corresponding invariant mass and transverse momentum, and
p_{\text{T}}(b_{1})
(
p_{\text{T}}(b_{2})
) is the hardest (second hardest) bottom-quark-flavoured jet. We set
\mu_{R}=\mu_{F}=H_{\text{T}}
for the central scale.

Before presenting our phenomenological results, let us briefly review the flavoured anti-
k_{\text{T}}
jet algorithm proposed in Ref.
Czakon:2022wam
.
The standard anti-
k_{\text{T}}
distance is multiplied by the following damping function
\mathcal{S}_{ij}
if both pseudo-jets
i
and
j
have the same non-zero flavour of opposite sign:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{ij}=1-\theta(1-x)\,\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}x\right)\leq 1\,,

(4)

where
\theta
is the Heaviside step function, and

\displaystyle x\equiv\frac{1}{a}\frac{k^{2}_{\text{T},i}+k^{2}_{\text{T},j}}{2k^{2}_{\text{T},\max}}\,,

(5)

k_{\text{T},\max}
being the transverse momentum of the hardest pseudo-jet at each clustering step.
The parameter
a
in eq. (
5
) is arbitrary and regulates the turn-on of the damping function.
Small non-zero values of
a
are favourable, as the resulting clustering is closer to that of the standard anti-
k_{\text{T}}
algorithm.
Large logarithms of
a
may however spoil the perturbative convergence if
a
is chosen small.
The value of
a
should therefore be tuned, possibly process-wise, to minimise unfolding effects and perturbative corrections at the same time.
Unfolding effects can be estimated by studying partonic NLO+PS predictions.
This however is beyond the scope of this study.
As suggested in Ref.
Czakon:2022wam
, we investigate the values of
a=0.05,0.1,0.2
.
Furthermore, there is some additional degree of freedom in the definition of
k_{\text{T},\max}
.
For the
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
process, we decided to include the transverse momentum of the final state leptons, i.e.
k_{\text{T},\max}
is the lepton transverse momentum if the latter is the largest transverse momentum at a given clustering step.

It is instructive to apply the standard
k_{\text{T}}
and anti-
k_{\text{T}}
jet algorithms and their flavour-sensitive versions at NLO, where they are all IR-safe and can be compared to each other.
In particular, let us consider the distribution of the distance between the
b
-flavoured jets, as displayed in Figure
1
, for
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0pt^{+}b\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
production at NLO QCD.
One can see that the distribution, obtained with the flavoured
k_{\text{T}}
algorithm, is significantly suppressed with respect to all the others in the region of small distances
\Delta R_{b\bar{b}}<1.5
.
We also note that the flavoured
k_{\text{T}}
algorithm deviates substantially even from the standard
k_{\text{T}}
prescription, which is instead aligned with all the other anti-
k_{\text{T}}
-based algorithms.
The suppression for small
\Delta R_{b\bar{b}}
can be attributed to the modification of the beam distance in the case of the flavour
k_{\text{T}}
algorithm
Banfi:2006hf
.
The reason is that, in the flavour
k_{\text{T}}
algorithm, the flavoured pseudo-jets have a larger beam distance than in the standard prescription, which favours the recombination of the
b
and
\bar{b}
quarks when they are close in angular separation.
We will encounter this sharp difference in the low
\Delta R_{b\bar{b}}
region again in the next section, where we will extend the analysis to NNLO, using exclusively the flavour-sensitive jet algorithms.

3
Phenomenology

In this section we discuss the results obtained with both the flavoured
k_{\text{T}}
and flavoured anti-
k_{\text{T}}
jet algorithms for
\mathrm{W^{+}}(\to\ell\nu)b\bar{b}
production, focussing on the
\mathrm{W^{+}}
signature.
The previous study of the flavoured version of the anti-
k_{\text{T}}
algorithm
Czakon:2022wam
investigated examples of
\operatorname{Z}\kern-1.0pt+b
and
t\bar{t}
processes.
By comparing NLO+PS and NNLO QCD corrections, it was concluded that an optimal value for the jet-algorithm parameter
a
is around
a=0.1
.
This choice minimises the estimated unfolding corrections and, at the same time, leads to perturbatively stable predictions.

We extend the previous studies of this algorithm to a high multiplicity process:
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
production.
We structure our presentation to highlight two important aspects in particular:

•

the difference between the flavoured
k_{\text{T}}
and flavoured anti-
k_{\text{T}}
algorithms;

•

the impact of the jet-algorithm parameter
a
.

We quantify the latter as the difference between the results for different values of
a
, which we choose as
a=0.05,0.1,0.2
following Ref.
Czakon:2022wam
.
We therefore present differential distributions for which the difference between
k_{\text{T}}
and anti-
k_{\text{T}}
is particularly strong, to highlight its origins.
Similarly, we present differential distributions which are particularly dependent on the value of
a
, and identify the corresponding sensitive regions.

In addition, the usage of the flavoured anti-
k_{\text{T}}
algorithm puts us in a good position to compare our theoretical predictions against the experimental analyses, which are typically done using the anti-
k_{\text{T}}
algorithm.
To this end, we compare our theoretical predictions for the total cross section to the measurement of Ref.
CMS:2016eha
, showing good agreement and corroborating the need for NNLO QCD corrections in this process.

We present results for the total cross sections in Section
3.1
, and for a number of differential distributions in Section
3.2
.
Unless explicitly indicated by ‘standard’, we will be referring to the flavoured versions of the jet algorithms when mentioning the
k_{\text{T}}
and anti-
k_{\text{T}}
algorithms.

To obtain the uncorrelated theoretical uncertainty, we write the exclusive
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
cross section at NLO (NNLO) as

\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{(N)}\mathrm{NLO},\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b},\mathrm{exc}}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\text{(N)}\mathrm{NLO},\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b},\mathrm{inc}}-\sigma_{\text{(N)}\mathrm{LO},\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}j,\mathrm{inc}}\,.

(8)

The first term on the right-hand side is the original inclusive
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
production, while the second term is the inclusive
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
production in association with a hard jet.
The theoretical uncertainty of the exclusive
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
cross section is then taken to be

\displaystyle\Delta\sigma_{\text{(N)}\mathrm{NLO},\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b},\mathrm{exc}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left(\Delta\sigma_{\text{(N)}\mathrm{NLO},\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b},\mathrm{inc}}\right)^{2}+\left(\Delta\sigma_{\text{(N)}\mathrm{LO},\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}j,\mathrm{inc}}\right)^{2}},

(9)

where
\Delta\sigma_{\text{(N)}\mathrm{NLO},\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b},\mathrm{inc}}
and
\Delta\sigma_{\text{(N)}\mathrm{LO},\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}j,\mathrm{inc}}
are the theoretical uncertainties of the
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
and
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}j
inclusive cross sections respectively, as obtained from direct 7-point scale variation.
We observe that the corrections, as well as the standard and uncorrelated scale-variation uncertainties, behave similarly to the inclusive case.

In Figure
2
we show the comparison of the CMS measurement against our theoretical predictions, obtained with the flavoured anti-
k_{\text{T}}
algorithm.
We note that our theoretical predictions include both the multiplicative hadronisation correction factor (
0.81\pm 0.07
pb) and the additive double parton interaction (DPI) correction (
0.06\pm 0.06
pb), as specified in Ref.
CMS:2016eha
.
Since uncertainties on these factors do not originate from fixed order predictions, we separate them from the estimate of missing higher orders.
After being added in quadrature to the uncorrelated theoretical uncertainties,
they contribute to the full theoretical uncertainty, shown by dotted error bars in Figure
2
.
We checked that our NLO results are compatible with the NLO predictions shown in Figure 3 of Ref.
CMS:2016eha
.
The NNLO QCD corrections appear to be significant, and their inclusion in the theoretical prediction improves substantially the agreement with the experimental data.
Moreover, we find consistent agreement for all the considered values of
a
.

4
NNLO versus NLO-merged calculations

The large corrections and strong dependence on the renormalisation and factorisation scales in the NLO QCD calculation of the inclusive
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
production originate from the tree-level
qg(\bar{q}g)\to\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}q(\bar{q})
subprocess, which opens up only at
this order
Ellis:1998fv
;
FebresCordero:2006nvf
;
FebresCordero:2009xzo
;
Badger:2010mg
.
Efforts have been made to evaluate such subprocesses at higher accuracy by computing the
pp\to\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}j
process at
NLO QCD
Reina:2011mb
;
Luisoni:2015mpa
.

pp\to\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}j
production at NLO QCD constitutes one of the ingredients of NNLO QCD calculation of
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
production.
The NLO QCD prediction for
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
production can then be improved by taking into account the contribution from
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}j
production computed also at NLO QCD accuracy, achieved through a particular merging scheme.
Such an NLO-merged prediction contains some of the NNLO QCD corrections.
The aim of this section is to compare the NNLO prediction for inclusive
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
production
against the NLO-merged calculation and assess the genuine impact of the full NNLO QCD corrections.

We employ only fixed order calculations to carry out the comparisons and focus on the
\operatorname{W}^{+}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
signature.
To this end, we make use of the
exclusive sums
method to merge fixed order
NLO QCD calculations with different jet multiplicities
SM:2012sed
;
Anger:2017glm
.
The resulting NLO-merged prediction will further be denoted as ‘NLO+’.
The inclusive NLO+ prediction for
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
production is derived by summing up the exclusive NLO QCD
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}

and inclusive NLO QCD
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}j
contributions

\sigma_{\mathrm{NLO+},\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b},\mathrm{inc}}=\sigma_{\mathrm{NLO},\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b},\mathrm{exc}}+\sigma_{\mathrm{NLO},\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}j,\mathrm{inc}}.

(10)

Note that, if the last term of Eq. (
10
) were evaluated at leading order, we would recover the NLO inclusive cross section for
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
production.

5
Conclusions

We considered the production and leptonic decay of a
\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0pt
-boson in association with a bottom-quark pair at the LHC, at NNLO QCD accuracy.
Such a final state requires the use of a flavour sensitive jet algorithm to define an IR-safe fixed order prediction beyond NLO QCD.
We computed cross sections and differential distributions using the flavoured modification of the anti-
k_{\text{T}}
jet algorithm proposed in Ref.
Czakon:2022wam
.
We compared its output for three different values of the tuneable parameter
a
against predictions by the flavour-
k_{\text{T}}
jet algorithm
Banfi:2006hf
, where we observed a 50% difference for the integrated cross section, coming from the small
b\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
-distance region.
The scale band is increased accordingly, and can be understood as a consequence of sensitivity to gluon to
b
-quark pair splittings.

We compared our theoretical predictions for the exclusive cross section, where we require exactly two
b
-jets and no other jets in the final state, obtained with the flavoured anti-
k_{\text{T}}
jet algorithm, against the measurement by the CMS collaboration
CMS:2016eha
.
We found good agreement for all values of the considered values for the
a
-parameter.
The NNLO QCD corrections are significant, and their inclusion substantially improves the agreement with the data.

We showed differential observables that are particularly sensitive to the choice of the jet algorithm.
The dominant differences between the flavoured
k_{\text{T}}
and anti-
k_{\text{T}}
algorithm can be attributed to the region of small angular separation between the
b\bar{b}
-pair.
This region shows also an enhanced sensitivity to the
a
parameter of the flavoured anti-
k_{\text{T}}
algorithm.
The determination of an optimal value for
a
for this process is left for future work.

Finally, we studied the genuine impact of the full NNLO QCD corrections by comparing the NNLO calculation against an improved NLO prediction, that is obtained by merging the
pp\to\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}
and
pp\to\operatorname{W}\kern-1.0ptb\kern 1.0pt\bar{b}j
NLO QCD calculations, using the exclusive sums technique.
We found that the NLO-merged prediction captures a significant amount of the NNLO QCD corrections, and at the same time shows improvements on the scale dependence, both at the level of integrated cross sections and differential distributions.
Still, the NLO-merged predictions do not capture entirely the NNLO effects and, therefore, the complete NNLO QCD corrections are indispensable.

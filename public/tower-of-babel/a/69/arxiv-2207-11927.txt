1.
introduction

In this paper, we study the entire solutions of the following Ginzburg-Landau system in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, for complex vector-valued functions
w=(w^{+},w^{-}):\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}w^{+}+\Big[A_{+}\big(|w^{+}|^{2}-{t^{+}}^{2}\big)+B\big(|w^{-}|^{2}-{t^{-}}^{2}\big)\Big]w^{+}=0,\\[8.53581pt]
-\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}w^{-}+\Big[A_{-}\big(|w^{-}|^{2}-{t^{-}}^{2}\big)+B\big(|w^{+}|^{2}-{t^{+}}^{2}\big)\Big]w^{-}=0,\end{cases}

(1.1)

here
A_{\pm}>0
,
t^{\pm}
,
B
and
\epsilon>0
are parameters, the notation
\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}
is the Laplacian operator in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Throughout the paper we make
the following assumptions concerning the constants appearing in (
1.1
):

\displaystyle A_{+},A_{-}>0,\ B^{2}<A_{+}A_{-},

(H1)

\displaystyle\ t^{\pm}>0,\ \text{and}~{t^{+}}^{2}+{t^{-}}^{2}=1.

(1.2)

The corresponding energy functional to system (
1.1
) is

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}(w)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla w|^{2}\ +\

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big[\,A_{+}(|w^{+}|^{2}-{t^{+}}^{2})^{2}+A_{-}(|w^{-}|^{2}-{t^{-}}^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+2B(|w^{+}|^{2}-{t^{+}}^{2})(|w^{-}|^{2}-{t^{-}}^{2})\,\Big].

The hypothesis (
H1
) ensures that the potential term in the energy is positive definite and attains its minimum when
|w^{\pm}|=t^{\pm}
. From the previous work about the classical Ginzburg-Landau equation in
[
9
]
, we would look for solutions which satisfy

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Big[\,A_{+}(|w^{+}|^{2}-{t^{+}}^{2})^{2}+A_{-}(|w^{-}|^{2}-{t^{-}}^{2})^{2}+2B(|w^{+}|^{2}-{t^{+}}^{2})(|w^{-}|^{2}-{t^{-}}^{2})\,\Big]<+\infty.

Therefore the function
w=(w^{+},w^{-})
satisfies the superconducting boundary condition

\displaystyle|w|^{2}=|w^{+}|^{2}+|w^{-}|^{2}\rightarrow{t^{+}}^{2}+{t^{-}}^{2}=1,\quad\text{when}\quad|x|\rightarrow+\infty.

(1.3)

When
N=2,3
in (
1.1
), Ginzburg-Landau systems of this type have been introduced in physical models of
p
-wave superconductors
[
26
]
and
two-component Bose-Einstein condensates (BEC)
[
3
,
25
]
.
When the coupled term
B
vanishes, system (
1.1
) is decoupled and is precisely the classical (single-component) Ginzburg-Landau equation

\displaystyle\epsilon^{2}\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u+(1-|u|^{2})u=0,\quad u\in\mathbb{C},

(1.4)

which arises in the theory of superconductivity and superfluids
[
20
]
without external applied magnetic field. From the theory of superconductivity,

|u|^{2}
is proportional to the density of superconducting electrons, i.e,
|u|^{2}\approx 1
corresponds to the superconducting state and

|u|^{2}\approx 0
corresponds to the normal state.
The zero set of
u
, which the superconductivity is not present, is said to be vortex set or vorticity.
The solutions with vortex lines or vortex filaments to the system (
1.1
) are the main objects in this paper.

1.1.
The case
B=0

For the classical Ginzburg-Landau equation in a smooth bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\epsilon^{2}\Delta u_{\epsilon}+(1-|u_{\epsilon}|^{2})u_{\epsilon}=0\quad\text{in}~\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad u_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{C},\\[8.53581pt]
u_{\epsilon}=f\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.5)

the solutions with vortices have been widely studied in the past years.

For
N=2
, F. Bethuel, H. Brezis, and F. Hélein
[
7
]
considered the asymptotic behavior of
u_{\epsilon}
with a boundary condition
f:\partial\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{1}
of degree
k\geq 1
. They showed that there exists a subsequence
\{\epsilon_{j}\}
and exactly
k
points
b_{1},\cdots b_{k}
in
\Omega
such that

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon_{j}}(x)\rightarrow{\bf u}=e^{i\varphi(x)}\prod_{l=1}^{k}\frac{x-b_{l}}{|x-b_{l}|}\quad\text{in}~C_{loc}(\Omega\setminus\{b_{1},\cdots b_{k}\}),\quad\text{as $\epsilon_{j}\rightarrow 0$},

(1.6)

where
\varphi
is a harmonic function satisfying
\varphi|_{\partial\Omega}=f.

Furthermore, they also showed that the
k
-tuple
(b_{1},\cdots b_{k})
globally minimizes the
renormalized energy functional

\mathcal{W}(b_{1},\cdots b_{k})
which is characterised by

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(b_{1},\cdots b_{k})=:\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\int_{\Omega\setminus\cup_{l=1}^{k}B_{r}(b_{l})}\Big[|\nabla{\bf u}|^{2}-k\pi|\ln r|\Big].

(1.7)

And in
[
7
]
, an implicit expression of
\mathcal{W}(b_{1},\cdots b_{k})
in terms of Green’s function was given, see also
[
15
,
36
]
for similar results.
One better approximation than
{\bf u}
in (
1.6
) is

e^{i\varphi(x)}\prod_{l=1}^{k}\omega\Big(\frac{x-b_{l}}{\epsilon}\Big),

(1.8)

where
\omega

is the
standard degree
+1
vortex solution
of the following Ginzburg-Landau equation in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

\Delta u+(1-|u|^{2})u=0,\quad u\in\mathbb{C}.

(1.9)

In other words, equation (
1.9
) has a unique solution
of the form

\omega(\tilde{z})=e^{i\theta}U(r),\quad\tilde{z}=re^{i\theta},\quad U\in\mathbb{R},

(1.10)

where
U>0
is a solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &U^{\prime\prime}+\frac{U^{\prime}}{r}-\frac{U}{r^{2}}+(1-U^{2})U=0{\quad\hbox{in }}(0,\infty),\\
&U(0^{+})=0,\quad U(+\infty)=1,\end{aligned}\right.

see
[
11
,
21
]
.
On the other hand, a natural question is to find solutions of (
1.5
) with vortices at other critical points of the renormalized energy functional
\mathcal{W}(b_{1},\cdots b_{k})
(see
[
6
,
17
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
33
]
and the references therein for researchs on this problem).
In
[
33
]
, F. Pacard and T. Rivière constructed solutions of (
1.5
) with vortices of combined degree
\pm 1
and showed that the vortex points converge to non-degenerate critical point of corresponding renormalized energy.
In
[
17
]
, M. del Pino, M. Kowalczyk and M. Musso considered the Ginzburg-Landau equation (
1.5
) with added zero Neumann boundary condition and constructed solutions by using Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction without non-degenerate assumption on the critical point of renormalized energy.

In the high dimension
N\geq 3
, the locations of vortices to the classical Ginzburg-Landau with suitable boundary conditions and energy levels do not occur at points, but along a generalized minimal sub-manifold structure with co-dimension
2
, which is naturally interpreted as “vortex sub-manifold”.
In
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the structure of vortices should typically be the form of curves which are called vortex filaments. In the work
[
16
]
, M. del Pino and M. Kowalczyk studied (
1.5
) in a cylinder
\Omega=B_{R}(0)\times(0,2\pi)
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and formally gave an approximation of solution to (
1.5
) with helical vortex filaments structure. Moreover, they conjectured in
[
16
]
that (
1.5
) has a solution
u_{\epsilon{\bf g}}
of the form

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon{\bf g}}\approx e^{i\varphi(\tilde{z},t)}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\omega\Big(\frac{\tilde{z}-g_{j}(t)}{\epsilon}\Big),\quad(\tilde{z},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1.11)

where
\varphi
is a harmonic function satisfying the boundary condition and
{\bf g}=(g_{1},\cdots,g_{k})
represents
k
curves :
t\rightarrow\big(g_{j}(t),t\big),j\leq k
. For simplicity, the authors assumed that the
k
curves are
2\pi
-periodic in
t
.
The asymptotic expansion of energy functional
E_{0\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon{\bf g}})
of (
1.5
) is

I_{\epsilon}({\bf g}):=E_{0\epsilon}(u_{\epsilon{\bf g}})\approx 2\pi\times k\pi|\log\epsilon|+\mathcal{I}_{\epsilon}({\bf g}),

where

E_{0\epsilon}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|{\nabla}u|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\epsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega}(1-|u|^{2})^{2},

and

\mathcal{I}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{g}):=\pi\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big(\,|\ln\epsilon|\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{k}|g^{\prime}_{l}(t)|^{2}-\sum_{j\neq l}\log|g_{j}(t)-g_{l}(t)|\,\Big)\,{\mathrm{d}}t.

Therefore, the asymptotic expressions
for equilibrium location in (
1.11
) are

\displaystyle g_{j}(t)\approx\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\ln\epsilon|}}\sqrt{k-1}e^{it}e^{2i(j-1)\pi/k},\ \ j=1,\cdots,k,

(1.12)

and they can be obtained by solving the following Euler-Lagrange equations of

\mathcal{I}_{\epsilon}(\mathbf{g})

{\bf g}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\ln\epsilon|}}\tilde{\bf g}(t),\quad\tilde{\bf g}=(\tilde{g}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{g}_{k}),\quad-\tilde{g}_{l}^{\prime\prime}(t)=2\sum_{i\neq l}\frac{\tilde{g}_{l}(t)-\tilde{g}_{i}(t)}{|\tilde{g}_{l}(t)-\tilde{g}_{i}(t)|^{2}}.

Recently, J. Dávila, M. del Pino, M. Medina and R. Rodiac in
[
12
]
have rigorously proved this conjecture in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
by constructing a family of entire solutions
u_{\epsilon}(\tilde{z},t)
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
with the form

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}(\tilde{z},t)\approx\prod_{l=1}^{k}\omega\Big(\frac{\tilde{z}-g_{j}(t)}{\epsilon}\Big),\quad(\tilde{z},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Now we recall the main results provided in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
12
]
).
For every integer
k\geq 2
and
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small, there exists a solution
u_{\epsilon}(\tilde{z},t)\in\mathbb{C}
of (
1.4
) with
N=3
,
2\pi
-periodic in the

t
-variable, with the following asymptotic profile:

u_{\epsilon}(\tilde{z},t)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\omega\left(\frac{\tilde{z}-{g}^{\epsilon}_{j}(t)}{\epsilon}\right)+\varphi_{\epsilon}(\tilde{z},t),

where
{g}_{j}^{\epsilon}(t)
is
2\pi
-periodic with the asymptotic behavior (
1.12
) and

|\varphi_{\epsilon}(\tilde{z},t)|\,\leq\,\frac{C}{|\ln\epsilon|}.

∎

It is worth pointing out that the solutions
u_{\epsilon}(\tilde{z},t)
constructed in
[
12
]
satisfy

\lim_{|\tilde{z}|\rightarrow+\infty}|u_{\epsilon}(\tilde{z},t)|=1\quad\text{uniformly in}~t,

(1.13)

and still depend on
t
. As we know that H. Brezis has postulated the following Gibbons conjecture:
whether a solution
u_{\epsilon}(\tilde{z},t)\in\mathbb{C}
of classical Ginzburg-Landau with the uniform convergence condition (
1.13
) must necessarily be a function in
\tilde{z}
.

However, the results obtained in
[
12
]
actually negatively answered the conjecture proposed by H. Brezis when
N=3
. The reader can refer to
[
10
]
,
[
19
]
for more information about the Gibbons conjecture.

1.2.
The case
B\neq 0

When
N=2
, with space rescaling, we can reduce the system (
1.1
) into the following one:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta w^{+}+\Big[A_{+}\big(|w^{+}|^{2}-{t^{+}}^{2}\big)+B\big(|w^{-}|^{2}-{t^{-}}^{2}\big)\Big]w^{+}=0,\\[8.53581pt]
-\Delta w^{-}+\Big[A_{-}\big(|w^{-}|^{2}-{t^{-}}^{2}\big)+B\big(|w^{+}|^{2}-{t^{+}}^{2}\big)\Big]w^{-}=0,\end{cases}\quad\text{in}~\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(1.14)

The two-component model (
1.14
) was studied by
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
in the “balanced” case,
A_{+}=A_{-}
and
t^{+}=t^{-}=1/\sqrt{2}
.
S. Alama, L. Bronsard and P. Mironescu in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
proved the existence, uniqueness, monotonicity and stability of radial
solutions with symmetric vortices.
Furthermore, under the assumption (
H1
), S. Alama and Q. Gao obtained the existence, uniqueness, asymptotic behaviors and quantization results of symmetric equivariant vortex solution with degree pair
(n^{+},n^{-})
in the form

\displaystyle w_{n}^{+}(\ell,\theta)=W_{n}^{+}(\ell)e^{in^{+}\theta},\qquad w_{n}^{-}(\ell,\theta)=W_{n}^{-}(\ell)e^{in^{-}\theta},

to the system (
1.14
), where
(\ell,\theta)
are the polar coordinates (see
[
3
]
).

In order to give our results in a more precise way, we use the notation in the rest of this paper to denote the radially symmetric solution with vortices of degree pair
(n_{+},n_{-})=(+1,+1)
as below:

\displaystyle w(\tilde{z})=\big(w^{+}(\tilde{z}),w^{-}(\tilde{z})\big)\quad\mbox{where}\quad w^{\pm}(\tilde{z})=W^{\pm}(\ell)e^{i\theta},\quad~\tilde{z}=\ell e^{i\theta}.

Then we get the corresponding ODE system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-{W^{+}}^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{\ell}{W^{+}}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}{W^{+}}+\Big[\,A_{+}({W^{+}}^{2}-{t^{+}}^{2})+B({W^{-}}^{2}-{t^{-}}^{2})\,\Big]W^{+}=0,\vskip 8.53581pt\\
-{W^{-}}^{\prime\prime}-\frac{1}{\ell}{W^{-}}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}{W^{-}}+\Big[\,A_{-}({W^{-}}^{2}-{t^{-}}^{2})+B({W^{+}}^{2}-{t^{+}}^{2})\,\Big]W^{-}=0,\end{cases}

(1.15)

where
\ell\in[0,+\infty)
.
The facts below about the properties of
W=(W^{+},W^{-})
are given in
[
3
]
.

Lemma 1.2
.

(
[
3
]
).
Suppose
W=(W^{+},W^{-})
is a solution of the ODE system (
1.15
).Then

W^{+}
and
W^{-}
satisfy the following:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&W^{\pm}\in C^{\infty}(0,\infty),\qquad 0<W^{\pm}<t^{\pm}~\text{for~all }~\ell>0,\\[5.69054pt]
&W^{\pm}\sim\ell~\text{as }~\ell\rightarrow 0,\qquad W^{\pm}\sim t^{\pm}-\frac{c_{\pm}}{2\ell^{2}}~\text{as }~\ell\rightarrow\infty,\\[5.69054pt]
&{W^{\pm}}^{\prime}>0~\text{when}~B<0,\qquad{W^{\pm}}^{\prime}\sim\frac{c_{\pm}}{\ell^{3}}~\text{as }~\ell\rightarrow\infty,\end{cases}

(1.16)

where

c_{\pm}=\frac{A_{\mp}-B}{(A_{+}A_{-}-B^{2})t^{\pm}}.

Moreover,
(W^{+},W^{-})
is the unique solution of (
1.15
).
∎

The linear Ginzburg-Landau operator of (
1.14
) around the standard vortex

is given by

L_{0}(\phi):=\big(L_{0}^{+},L_{0}^{-}\big)(\phi),

with

\displaystyle L_{0}^{\pm}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\Delta\phi^{\pm}+\Big[A_{\pm}\big({t^{\pm}}^{2}-{W^{\pm}}^{2}\big)+B\big({t^{\mp}}^{2}-{W^{\mp}}^{2}\big)\Big]\phi^{\pm}

\displaystyle\quad-2A_{\pm}{\rm{Re}}\Big(w^{\pm}\overline{\phi^{\pm}}\Big)w^{\pm}\,-\,2B{\rm{Re}}\Big(w^{\mp}\overline{\phi^{\mp}}\Big)w^{\pm}.

(1.17)

S. Alama and Q. Gao in
[
4
]
also studied the stability of

in the sense of spectrum of the linear operator

in a disk of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Furthermore, in
[

, L. Duan and J. Yang studied the linearized operator

vortex solution in a natural Hilbert space

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{\mathscr{H}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(|{\nabla}\phi^{+}|^{2}+|{\nabla}\phi^{-}|^{2})+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big[A_{+}\big({t^{+}}^{2}-{W^{+}}^{2}\big)-B\big({t^{-}}^{2}-{W^{-}}^{2}\big)\Big]|\phi^{+}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big[A_{+}\big({t^{-}}^{2}-{W^{-}}^{2}\big)-B\big({t^{+}}^{2}-{W^{+}}^{2}\big)\Big]|\phi^{-}|^{2}.

(1.18)

(

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that (
H1
) and
B<0
hold.
For any integer
k\geq 2
and
\epsilon
small enough, there exists a solution

w_{\epsilon}(\tilde{z},t)=\big(w_{\epsilon}^{+}(\tilde{z},t),w_{\epsilon}^{-}(\tilde{z},t)\big)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}

\displaystyle w_{\epsilon}(\tilde{z},t)=\Bigg((t^{+})^{1-k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}w^{+}\Big(\frac{\tilde{z}-g^{+}_{\epsilon,j}(t)}{\epsilon}\Big),\,(t^{-})^{1-k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}w^{-}\Big(\frac{\tilde{z}-g^{-}_{\epsilon,j}(t)}{\epsilon}\Big)\Bigg)+\phi_{\epsilon}(\tilde{z},t),

where
\phi_{\epsilon}(\tilde{z},t)=\big(\phi_{\epsilon}^{+}(\tilde{z},t),\phi_{\epsilon}^{-}(\tilde{z},t)\big)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}

and all

\displaystyle g^{\pm}_{\epsilon,j}(t)\approx\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\ln\epsilon|}}\sqrt{k-1}e^{it}e^{i\frac{2(j-1)\pi}{k}},\quad\text{for}~j=1,\cdots k.

(1.20)

Furthermore, we also have

\displaystyle|\phi_{\epsilon}^{\pm}|\leq\frac{D}{|\ln\epsilon|},\quad\text{for some constant $D>0$},

(1.21)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{|\tilde{z}|\rightarrow+\infty}|w_{\epsilon}(\tilde{z},t)|=\lim_{|\tilde{z}|\rightarrow+\infty}\sqrt{|w^{+}_{\epsilon}(\tilde{z},t)|^{2}+|w^{-}_{\epsilon}(\tilde{z},t)|^{2}}=1,\quad\text{uniformly in}~t.

(1.22)

∎

Note that, due to (

with

Definition 1.6
.

We say that a function

has screw-symmetry if

u(r,\theta+h,t+h)=u(r,\theta,t),\quad\text{i.e.,}\quad u^{\pm}(r,\theta+h,t+h)=u^{\pm}(r,\theta,t),

(1.25)

for any

.

2.
formulation of the problem

2.1.
Reduction to a 2-dimensional problem by using screw-symmetry

By using the screw-symmetry, we will transform the problem (
1.1
), which is of
3

2

For simplicity, we only treat the case
k=2
in Theorem
1.4
and the arguments for general

can be easily adapted.

Notice that the condition (
1.25
) in Definition
1.6
is equivalent to

u(r,\theta,t+h)=u(r,\theta-h,t)\mbox{ for any }h\in\mathbb{R}.

Then a function with screw-symmetry can be expressed as a function of two variables, i.e. for any
(r,\theta,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
,

u(r,\theta,t)=u(r,\theta-t,0)=:{\tilde{u}}(r,\theta-t).

We will apply these facts to make a setting to reduce the dimension.

Here is the observation in
[
12
]
.
We can write the standard degree pair
(+1,+1)
vortex solution for (
1.14
) in polar coordinates
(\ell,\theta)
, i.e.,

w(\tilde{z})=\big(w^{+}(\tilde{z}),w^{-}(\tilde{z})\big)=\Big(W^{+}(r)e^{i\theta},W^{-}(r)e^{i\theta}\Big),

and consider a function
w_{d}
in the form

\displaystyle w_{d}(\tilde{z},t)

\displaystyle=\Big(w_{d}^{+}(\tilde{z},t),w_{d}^{-}(\tilde{z},t)\Big),

(2.1)

where

\displaystyle w_{d}^{\pm}(\tilde{z},t)=({t^{\pm}})^{-1}\prod_{j=1}^{2}w^{\pm}\left(\frac{{{\rm{Re}}(\tilde{z})}-d\cos\big(t+(j-1)\pi\big)}{\epsilon},\,\frac{{{\rm{Im}}(\tilde{z})}-d\sin\big(t+(j-1)\pi\big)}{\epsilon}\right).

(2.2)

It can be checked that

w_{d}(r,\theta,t+h)=\Big(e^{2ih}w^{+}_{d}(r,\theta-h,t),e^{2ih}w^{-}_{d}(r,\theta-h,t)\Big)=e^{2ih}w_{d}(r,\theta-h,t),

for any

in
\mathbb{R}
.
That is,

does not have screw-symmetry but

The above arguments suggest that we should look for a solution

of (
1.1
) that can be written as

\displaystyle w_{\epsilon}(r,\theta,t)=\Big(w_{\epsilon}^{+}(r,\theta,t),w_{\epsilon}^{-}(r,\theta,t)\Big)=\Big(e^{2it}\tilde{w}^{+}(r,\theta,t),e^{2it}\tilde{w}^{-}(r,\theta,t)\Big)

(2.3)

with

being screw-symmetric.
Thus

\displaystyle\tilde{w}^{\pm}(r,\theta,t)=u^{\pm}(r,\theta-t),

(2.4)

where

is
2\pi
-periodic in the second variable.
Denoting

u^{\pm}(r,s)=u^{\pm}(r,\theta-t)\quad\mbox{with }s:=\theta-t,

we can see that

\partial_{r}w_{1}^{\pm}=e^{2it}\partial_{r}u^{\pm}(r,s),\quad\partial^{2}_{{r}{r}}w_{1}^{\pm}=e^{2it}\partial^{2}_{rr}u^{\pm}(r,s),\,\quad\partial_{\theta}w_{1}^{\pm}=e^{2it}\partial_{s}u^{\pm}(r,s),\quad\partial^{2}_{\theta\theta}w_{1}^{\pm}=e^{2it}\partial^{2}_{ss}u^{\pm}(r,s),

\partial_{t}w_{1}^{\pm}=[2iu^{\pm}-\partial_{s}u^{\pm}]e^{2it},\ \ \ \partial^{2}_{tt}w_{1}^{\pm}=[\partial^{2}_{ss}u^{\pm}-4i\partial_{s}u^{\pm}-4u^{\pm}]e^{2it}.

\partial^{2}_{rr}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{{r}^{2}}\partial^{2}_{\theta\theta}+\partial^{2}_{tt},

we conclude that

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{{r}^{2}}\partial^{2}_{ss}u^{\pm}+\partial^{2}_{ss}u^{\pm}-4i\partial_{s}u^{\pm}-4u^{\pm}\Big)

(2.5)

\displaystyle+\Big[A_{\pm}\big({t}^{\pm}-|u^{\pm}|^{2}\big)+B\big({t}^{\mp}-|u^{\mp}|^{2}\big)\Big]u^{\pm}=0\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{*}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}.

(2.6)

\displaystyle v^{\pm}(r,s):=u^{\pm}(\epsilon r,s),

(2.7)

and search for a solution to the system of equations

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{rr}v^{\pm}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}v^{\pm}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\partial^{2}_{ss}v^{\pm}

2.2.
Additive-multiplicative perturbation

Let

S(v):=\Big(S^{+}(v),S^{-}(v)\Big),\quad\text{for}~v=(v^{+},v^{-}),

with

S^{\pm}(v)=\Delta v^{\pm}+\epsilon^{2}(\partial^{2}_{ss}v^{\pm}-4i\partial_{s}v^{\pm}-4v^{\pm})+\Big[A_{\pm}\big({t}^{\pm}-|v^{\pm}|^{2}\big)+B\big({t}^{\mp}-|v^{\mp}|^{2}\big)\Big]v^{\pm}.

The operator

\displaystyle S=S_{0}+S_{1}\ ,

\displaystyle S_{0}(v)=\Big(S_{0}^{+}(v),S_{0}^{-}(v)\Big),\quad S_{1}(v)=\Big(S_{1}^{+}(v),S_{1}^{-}(v)\Big),

\displaystyle S_{0}^{\pm}(v)=\Delta v^{\pm}+\Big[A_{\pm}\big({t}^{\pm}-|v^{\pm}|^{2}\big)+B\big({t}^{\mp}-|v^{\mp}|^{2}\big)\Big]v^{\pm},

\displaystyle S_{1}^{\pm}(v)

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{2}(\partial^{2}_{ss}v^{\pm}-4i\partial_{s}v^{\pm}-4v^{\pm}).

Therefore, the equation (
2.1

\displaystyle S(v)=0.

(2.14)

We first try to search for a solution of (
2.14
) with the form

v=v_{d}+\phi,\quad\text{for some$~\phi=(\phi^{+},\phi^{-})$ small}.

We see that

\displaystyle S_{0}(v_{d}+\phi)

\displaystyle=S_{0}(v_{d})+{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(\phi)+{\mathcal{N}}_{0}(\phi),

\displaystyle S_{1}(v_{d}+\phi)

\displaystyle=S_{1}(v_{d})+S_{1}(\phi).

In the above formulae, we have denoted the linear operator in the form

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{0}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\Big({\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{+},{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{-}\Big)(\phi),

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{0}^{\pm}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\Delta\phi^{\pm}+\Big[A_{\pm}\big({t^{\pm}}^{2}-|v_{d}^{\pm}|^{2}\big)+B\big({t^{\mp}}^{2}-|v_{d}^{\mp}|^{2}\big)\Big]\phi^{\pm}

\displaystyle\quad\,-\,2A_{\pm}{\rm{Re}}\Big(v_{d}^{\pm}\overline{\phi^{\pm}}\Big)v_{d}^{\pm}\,-\,2B{\rm{Re}}\Big(v_{d}^{\mp}\overline{\phi^{\mp}}\Big)v_{d}^{\pm},

and the nonlinear operator as the following

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}_{0}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\Big({\mathcal{N}}_{0}^{+},{\mathcal{N}}_{0}^{-}\Big)(\phi),

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}_{0}^{\pm}(\phi)

\displaystyle=-2A_{\pm}{\rm{Re}}(v_{d}^{\pm}\overline{\phi^{\mp}})\phi^{\pm}-A_{\pm}|\phi^{\pm}|^{2}(v_{d}^{\pm}+\phi^{\pm})

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\,2B{\rm{Re}}\Big(v_{d}^{\mp}\overline{\phi^{\mp}}\Big)\phi^{\pm}-B|\phi^{\mp}|^{2}(v_{d}^{\pm}+\phi^{\pm}).

We note that, due to the strong coupling effect in the following terms

-\,2A_{\pm}{\rm{Re}}\Big(v_{d}^{\pm}\overline{\phi^{\pm}}\Big)v_{d}^{\pm}\,-\,2B{\rm{Re}}\Big(v_{d}^{\mp}\overline{\phi^{\mp}}\Big)v_{d}^{\pm},

it is very complicated to handle the linear operator

.

[

to look for a solution of (

) in the following form

\displaystyle v=(v^{+},v^{-}),\qquad v^{\pm}=\eta v_{d}^{\pm}(1+i\psi^{\pm})+(1-\eta)v_{d}^{\pm}e^{i\psi^{\pm}},

(2.15)

where

2.9

and

Rewrite

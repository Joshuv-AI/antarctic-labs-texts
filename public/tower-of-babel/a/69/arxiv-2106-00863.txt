Bose-Einstein Condensation for Lattice Bosons
Tohru Koma

Abstract




Abstract:
We present a class of models of interacting lattice bosons which show complete Bose-Einstein
condensation for the ground state.

1
Introduction

As is well known, it is very difficult to prove the existence of Bose-Einstein condensation for interacting bosons.
Actually, the mathematically rigorous results are still rare. (See, e.g.,
[
1
,
2
]
and references therein.)
In this paper, we present a class of interacting boson models on the
d
-dimensional hypercubic lattice for
d\geq 1
.
We prove that the models show complete Bose-Einstein condensation for the ground state.
Although the interactions in the Hamiltonians are not necessarily standard, each of them is finite range or
rapidly decays with the diameter of the support.

The present paper is organized as follows: In the next Sec.
2
, we give the precise
definition of the simplest model in our class and the corresponding theorem with the proof. In Sec.
3
,
general models in our class are presented, and we also give an argument about the absence of Bose-Einstein condensation
[
3
]

for certain models at zero temperature. However, the argument is not necessarily mathematically rigorous.

2
Model and Result

We begin with the simplest model in our class.
The extension to general models will be given in the next Sec.
3
.

We consider a Bose gas on a
d
-dimensional hypercubic lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with the dimension
d\geq 1
.
The Hamiltonian with nearest neighbor hopping and nearest neighbor interactions is given by

H_{g}^{(N)}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda:|x-y|=1}(a_{x}^{\dagger}-a_{y}^{\dagger})(a_{x}-a_{y})+g\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda:|x-y|=1}[n_{x}(n_{x}-1)+n_{x}n_{y}-a_{x}^{\dagger}n_{x}a_{y}-a_{y}^{\dagger}n_{x}a_{x}],

(2.1)

where
a_{x}^{\dagger}
and
a_{x}
are, respectively, the creation and annihilation operators at
the site
x=(x^{(1)},x^{(2)},\ldots,x^{(d)})
in the finite lattice
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
;

n_{x}:=a_{x}^{\dagger}a_{x}
is the number operator at the site
x
, and
g\geq 0
is the coupling constant.
As usual, the operators,
a_{x}^{\dagger}
and
a_{x}
, obey the commutation relations,

[a_{x},a_{y}^{\dagger}]=\delta_{x,y},\quad[a_{x},a_{y}]=0,\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ [a_{x}^{\dagger},a_{y}^{\dagger}]=0.

We fix the total number of the bosons to
\sum_{x}n_{x}=N
, and we impose the periodic boundary condition.
Then, the Fourier transform of the operator
a_{x}
is given by

\hat{a}_{k}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\Lambda|}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}e^{-ikx}a_{x},

where
k:=(k^{(1)},k^{(2)},\ldots,k^{(d)})
is the momentum, and
kx=\sum_{i=1}^{d}k^{(i)}x^{(i)}
;

|\Lambda|
denotes the number of the sites in the finite lattice
\Lambda
. The inverse is

a_{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\Lambda|}}\sum_{k}e^{ikx}\hat{a}_{k}.

(2.2)

By using this expression, the kinetic term of the Hamiltonian
H_{g}^{(N)}
is written

H_{0}^{(N)}=\sum_{k}\mathcal{E}_{k}\hat{a}_{k}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{k}

(2.3)

with the standard dispersion relation,

\mathcal{E}_{k}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}(1-\cos k^{(i)}).

(2.4)

The complete Bose-Einstein condensation state is given by

\Phi_{\rm BEC}^{(N)}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N!}}(\hat{a}_{0}^{\dagger})^{N}|0\rangle,

(2.5)

where
|0\rangle
is the vacuum state, i.e.,
\hat{a}_{k}|0\rangle=0
for all the momenta
k
.

Our result is:

Theorem 2.1

The complete Bose-Einstein condensation state
\Phi_{\rm BEC}^{(N)}
is the unique ground state
of the Hamiltonian
H_{g}^{(N)}
of (
2.1
) for any coupling constant
g\geq 0
in any dimensions
d\geq 1
.

Proof:

The summand of the interaction term of the Hamiltonian
H_{g}^{(N)}
of (
2.1
) is written

n_{x}(n_{x}-1)+n_{x}n_{y}-a_{x}^{\dagger}n_{x}a_{y}-a_{y}^{\dagger}n_{x}a_{x}=(a_{x}^{\dagger}-a_{y}^{\dagger})a_{x}^{\dagger}a_{x}(a_{x}-a_{y}).

(2.6)

One notices that this right-hand side is positive.
Using the expression (
2.2
) of the operator
a_{x}
in terms of
\hat{a}_{k}
, one has

a_{x}-a_{y}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\Lambda|}}\sum_{k}(e^{ikx}-e^{iky})\hat{a}_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\Lambda|}}\sum_{k\neq 0}(e^{ikx}-e^{iky})\hat{a}_{k}.

(2.7)

Namely, the two contributions of the zero momentum mode
\hat{a}_{0}
cancel with each other in the sum.
Therefore, one has

(a_{x}-a_{y})\Phi_{\rm BEC}^{(N)}=0.

(2.8)

Combining this with (
2.6
), the summand of the interaction term also annihilates
the state
\Phi_{\rm BEC}^{(N)}
, i.e.,

[n_{x}(n_{x}-1)+n_{x}n_{y}-a_{x}^{\dagger}n_{x}a_{y}-a_{y}^{\dagger}n_{x}a_{x}]\Phi_{\rm BEC}^{(N)}=0.

(2.9)

Immediately,

\langle\Phi_{\rm BEC}^{(N)},H_{\rm int}^{(N)}\Phi_{\rm BEC}^{(N)}\rangle=0,

(2.10)

where we have written
H_{\rm int}^{(N)}:=H_{g}^{(N)}-H_{0}^{(N)}
for the interaction term of the Hamiltonian
H_{g}^{(N)}
.
This yields

\langle\Phi_{\rm BEC}^{(N)},H_{g}^{(N)}\Phi_{\rm BEC}^{(N)}\rangle=0

(2.11)

for the total Hamiltonian
H_{g}^{(N)}
as well.
We write
\Phi_{\rm GS}^{(N)}
for the ground state of
H_{g}^{(N)}
. Then, from this result (
2.11
), we have

0=\langle\Phi_{\rm BEC}^{(N)},H_{g}^{(N)}\Phi_{\rm BEC}^{(N)}\rangle\geq\langle\Phi_{\rm GS}^{(N)},H_{g}^{(N)}\Phi_{\rm GS}^{(N)}\rangle\geq\sum_{k}\mathcal{E}_{k}\langle\Phi_{\rm GS}^{(N)},\hat{a}_{k}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{k}\Phi_{\rm GS}^{(N)}\rangle\geq 0,

(2.12)

where we have used the positivity
H_{\rm int}^{(N)}\geq 0
which is obtained from the positivity (
2.6
).
This implies

\langle\Phi_{\rm GS}^{(N)},\hat{a}_{k}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{k}\Phi_{\rm GS}^{(N)}\rangle=0

for all
k\neq 0
. Thus, we obtain
\Phi_{\rm GS}^{(N)}=\Phi_{\rm BEC}^{(N)}
, and the ground state is unique.

Remark:
The method in the above proof is similar to that in
[
4
]
.
Namely, both of kinetic and interaction terms in a Hamiltonian annihilate a state.

3
Extension to a Class of Models and Discussion

In this section, we extend the model of (
2.1
) and Theorem
2.1
to general models.
We also discuss certain interactions for which the absence of Bose-Einstein condensation can be expected
at zero temperature.

A straightforward extension of (
2.6
) is as follows: Consider an interaction Hamiltonian,

H_{\rm int}^{(N)}=g\sum_{x\in\Lambda}C_{x}^{\dagger}A_{x}C_{x},

(3.1)

where
A_{x}
is a positive local operator and
C_{x}
is a local operator which is written in the form,

C_{x}=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}a_{x_{j}},

(3.2)

with coefficients
\alpha_{j}
which satisfy
\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}=0
.

We can also choose
C_{x}
to be

C_{x}=\sum_{y\in\Lambda}K(x-y)a_{y},

(3.3)

where the function
K(x-y)
is given by the Fourier transform of
\hat{K}(k)
, i.e.,

K(x-y)=\frac{1}{|\Lambda|}\sum_{k}\hat{K}(k)e^{ik(x-y)}.

(3.4)

When the function
\hat{K}(k)
is sufficiently many times differentiable and satisfies
\hat{K}(0)=0
,
the operator
C_{x}
is local and annihilates the state
\Phi_{\rm BEC}^{(N)}
. Actually, one has

C_{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\Lambda|}}\sum_{k}\hat{K}(k)\hat{a}_{k}e^{ikx}.

(3.5)

Conversely, when the function
\hat{K}(k)
has a narrow distribution with a high peak at
k=0
,
we can expect the absence of Bose-Einstein condensation for the zero momentum mode.
In fact, one has

C_{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\Lambda|}}\hat{K}(0)\hat{a}_{0}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\Lambda|}}\sum_{k\neq 0}\hat{K}(k)\hat{a}_{k}e^{ikx}.

(3.6)

If we choose
A_{x}=a_{x}^{\dagger}a_{x}
for the operator
A_{x}
in (
3.1
),
then the contribution of the zero momentum mode in (
3.1
) is written

\sum_{x}\frac{1}{|\Lambda|}(\hat{K}(0))^{2}\hat{a}_{0}^{\dagger}A_{x}\hat{a}_{0}=\sum_{x}\frac{1}{|\Lambda|}(\hat{K}(0))^{2}\hat{a}_{0}^{\dagger}a_{x}^{\dagger}a_{x}\hat{a}_{0}=\frac{N}{|\Lambda|}(\hat{K}(0))^{2}\hat{a}_{0}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{0},

(3.7)

where we have used
\sum_{x}a_{x}^{\dagger}a_{x}=N
. This right-hand side raises the energy by order
of the volume
|\Lambda|
with the large amplitude
(\hat{K}(0))^{2}

for a fixed particle density
N/|\Lambda|
when the zero momentum mode exhibits a long-range order.
Therefore, the long-range order of the zero momentum mode is expected to be suppressed for a large
\hat{K}(0)>0
.

When
\hat{K}(k)=1
for all
k
, one has
K(x-y)=\delta_{x,y}
from (
3.4
). Then, we have

H_{\rm int}^{(N)}=g\sum_{x\in\Lambda}n_{x}(n_{x}-1)

(3.8)

for
A_{x}=a_{x}^{\dagger}a_{x}
, from (
3.1
) and (
3.3
). This is one of the standard interactions
[
5
]
.
However, it seems to be very difficult to prove the existence of Bose-Einstein condensation in this case
because the function
\hat{K}(k)=1
is an intermediate function in the above class.

Thus, in general, whether Bose-Einstein condensation occurs or not is expected to depend on interactions of models.
In particular, it may depend on the dimension
[
3
]
of the lattice.

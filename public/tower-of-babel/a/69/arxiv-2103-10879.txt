Asymptotic Syzygies of Secant Varieties of Curves
Gregory Taylor

Abstract
. We prove that the minimal free resolution of the secant variety of a curve is asymptotically pure. As a corollary, we show that the Betti numbers of converge to a normal distribution.



1.
Introduction

Let
C
be a smooth curve of genus
g\geq 1
over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{k}
of characteristic zero. Consider the embedding of
C
in
\mathbb{P}H^{0}(C,L_{d})=\mathbb{P}^{r_{d}}
by a complete linear system of degree
d
. Since Green’s fundamental work on Koszul cohomology
[
Gre84
]
, it is well understood that the geometry of
C
is reflected in the minimal free resolution of the homogeneous coordinate ring of
C
as an
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\dots,x_{r_{d}}]
-module. One result along these lines concerns Boij-Söderberg decompositions. Boij-Söderberg theory
[
ES09
]
shows that every Betti table is a nonnegative linear combination of pure diagrams, the simplest diagrams of
S
-modules. In
[
Erm15
]
, Erman shows that as
d
grows, the Boij-Söderberg decomposition is dominated by a single pure diagram which only depends on the genus of
C
.

In
[
SV09b
]
, Sidman and Vermeire proved that Green’s celebrated “
2g+p+1
” Theorem
[
Gre84
]
extends to the secant variety of lines in a natural way, and they conjectured an extension to the secant variety of
k
-planes, which we denote by
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
. Recently Ein, Niu, and Park proved these conjectures
[
ENP20
]
(see Section
2
for precise statements). Not much is known about syzygies of higher dimensional varieties, due in no small part to a dearth of examples
[
EL12
]
. Secant varieties of curves provide valuable examples of higher dimensional varieties whose syzygies we can understand.

Our main result is an analogue of Erman’s theorem for secant varieties of curves. That is, the Boij-Söderberg decomposition of the Betti table of the homogeneous coordinate ring of
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
is increasingly dominated by a single pure diagram as
d
grows. Moreover, this diagram only depends on
k
and the genus of
C
. See Section
2
for a brief introduction to Boij-Söderberg decompositions and Section 3 for an explanation of the (nonstandard) notation for the pure diagrams
\pi_{k}(\mathbf{i};d)
.

Theorem 1.1

(Asymptotic Purity)
.

The normalized Betti table
\overline{\beta}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))
converges to
\pi_{k}((g,\dots,g);d)
as
d\to\infty
. That is, given a Boij-Söderberg decomposition
\overline{\beta}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))=\sum_{\mathbf{i}}c_{\mathbf{i};d}\pi_{k}(\mathbf{i};d)
for each
d
, we have

\lim_{d\to\infty}c_{\mathbf{i};d}=\begin{cases}1&\mathbf{i}=(g,\dots,g)\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

As pure diagrams are the building blocks of all Betti tables, Theorem 1.1 states that as the degree of the embedding grows, the Betti diagram of
\Sigma_{k}(C,L)
becomes as simple as possible. Being a direct generalization of Erman’s theorem
[
Erm15
]
for curves, this result further suggests that we should expect theorems about syzygies of curves to admit generalizations to secant varieties.

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we determine the asymptotic distribution of the Betti numbers of
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
. In particular, they are essentially normally distributed, as one might expect given the work of Ein, Erman, Lazarsfeld
[
EEL21
]
. In the statement below
\kappa_{p,q}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))
denotes the dimension of the Koszul cohomology group of degree
p
and weight
q
(see Section
2
for more details).

Theorem 1.2

(Normal Distribution)
.

Let
L_{d}
be a line bundle of degree
d
on
C
. Fix a sequence
\{p_{d}\}
of integers such that
p_{d}\to\frac{r_{d}}{2}+\frac{a\sqrt{r_{d}}}{2}
for some real number
a
. Then as
d\to\infty

F_{g,k}(d)\kappa_{p_{d},k+1}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))\to e^{-a^{2}}

where
F_{g,k}(d)=\frac{(k+1)!}{2^{r_{d}-2k}r_{d}^{k}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{r_{d}}}
.

Note that the function
F_{g,k}
controlling the rate of convergence depends only on
k
and
g
. For
k=0
(i.e. for high degree embeddings of curves), Theorem
1.2
was observed by Ein, Erman, and Lazarsfeld in
[
EEL21
, Proposition A]
using an explicit formula for the Betti numbers. They conjectured that a similar statement should hold for higher dimensional varieties. As heuristic evidence for the conjecture, they demonstrate that the rows of a sequence of “randomly chosen” Betti tables become normally distributed as
d\to\infty
. Some other special cases of normally distributed Betti numbers appear in
[
BEGY20
,
Bru
,
EY18
]
.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we use Erman’s technique from
[
Erm15
]
, leveraging the powerful results from
[
ENP20
]
. In particular, we study the limiting behavior of the Hilbert numerator of the homogeneous coordinate ring of
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
. For Theorem
1.2
, we leverage the fact that the Betti diagram is asymptotically pure. Since the Betti numbers of the pure diagrams have similar asymptotic behavior, we need only study the asymptotics of the pure diagram which dominates the decomposition, for which there are explicit formulas.

2.
Background

2.1.
Koszul Cohomology and Secant Varieties

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,L)=\mathbb{P}^{r}
be a projective variety embedded by a line bundle
L
, and let
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\dots,x_{r}]
. Let
R(X,L)
be the homogeneous coordinate ring of
X
, which is a graded
S
-module. Hence,
R(X,L)
admits a minimal free resolution

0\leftarrow R(X,L)\leftarrow F_{0}\leftarrow F_{1}\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow F_{n}\leftarrow 0

where each
F_{p}
is a free module. We write
F_{p}=\bigoplus_{q}K_{p,q}(X,L)\otimes_{k}S(-p-q)
where
K_{p,q}(X,L)
is a
\mathbb{k}
-vector space whose dimension counts the independent
syzygies of degree
p
and weight
q
. The spaces
K_{p,q}(X,L)
are the
Koszul cohomology groups

[
AN10
]
. Note that
K_{p,q}(X,L)\cong\Tor_{p}^{S}(R(X,L),\mathbb{k})_{p+q}
. The numbers
\kappa_{p,q}(X,L)=\dim K_{p,q}(X,L)
are called
Betti numbers
1
1

1

Some authors use the convention
\beta_{i,j}(X,L)=\kappa_{i,j-i}(X,L)
. We suppress the linear system in the notation when no confusion can arise. We express the multiplicity of the graded pieces in the
Betti table

\begin{array}[]{c|cccc}&0&1&2&\cdots\\
\hline\cr 0&\kappa_{0,0}&\kappa_{1,0}&\kappa_{2,0}&\cdots\\
1&\kappa_{0,1}&\kappa_{1,1}&\kappa_{2,1}&\cdots\\
2&\kappa_{0,2}&\kappa_{1,2}&\kappa_{2,2}&\cdots\\
\vdots&&\vdots&&\ddots\end{array}

which we denote by
\beta(X,L)
. The number of non-zero rows in the Betti diagram is the
Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
of
X
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
. Note that
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
is projectively normal if and only if
F_{0}=S
if and only if

\kappa_{0,q}(X,L)=\begin{cases}1&q=0\\
0&q>0\end{cases}.

In this case,
\kappa_{1,q}(X,L)
is equal to the number of minimal generators of the ideal
I_{X/\mathbb{P}^{r}}
of degree
q+1
. Following
[
ENP20
]
, we say that
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}
satisfies condition
N_{k+2,p}
if
K_{i,j}(X,L)=0
for
i\leq p
and
j\geq k+2
. When
X
is projectively normal and
k=0
, this coincides with Green’s condition
N_{p}

[
Gre84
]
which occurs when the homogeneous ideal
I_{X/\mathbb{P}^{r}}
is generated by quadrics and the resolution is linear up to the
p
th step.

From now on,
C
denotes a smooth curve of genus
g\geq 1
, and
L_{d}
is a line bundle of degree
d
on
C
. In his landmark paper on Koszul cohomology
[
Gre84
]
, Green showed that when
d\geq 2g+p+1
, the embedding
C\subseteq\mathbb{P}H^{0}(C,L)
satisfies
N_{2,p}
. Recently, Ein, Niu, and Park showed that Green’s theorem is a special case of a more general statement about the syzygies of the secant varieties to
C
. The
secant variety of
k
-planes
to
C
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
is the closure of the points contained in the
k
-planes generated by
k+1
points of
C
. The Ein-Niu-Park paper completes earlier work of Vermeire
[
Ver01
,
Ver02
,
Ver08
,
Ver12
]
and Sidman-Vermeire
[
SV09a
,
SV09b
]
on the case
k=1
. We summarize the results from
[
ENP20
]
that we will use in the sequel.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
ENP20
]
)
.

Let
C
be a curve of genus
g\geq 1
, and let
L_{d}
be a line bundle of degree
d=2g+2k+p+1
. Let
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
be the secant variety of
k
-planes to
C\subseteq\mathbb{P}H^{0}(C,L_{d})=\mathbb{P}^{r_{d}}
. Then

(1)

\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
is arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay (i.e.
R(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))
has projective dimension
r_{d}-2k-1
),

(2)

If
g\geq 1
, the ideal sheaf of
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
has Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
2k+3
. Otherwise, it has regularity
k+2
,

(3)

\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
is projectively normal and satisfies
N_{k+2,p}
,

(4)

The bottom-right Betti number is given by

\kappa_{r_{d}-2k-1,2k+2}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))={g+k\choose k+1}.

(5)

The degree of
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
is given by

\deg(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))=\sum_{i=0}^{\min\{k+1,g\}}{d-g-k-i\choose k+1-i}{g\choose i}.

Proof.

Parts (
1
)-(
4
) are Theorem 5.8, Corollary 5.9(3), Theorem 5.2(4), and Corollary 5.9(1), respectively in
[
ENP20
]
. Part (
5
) is proven in
[
Sou04
, Theorem 1]
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

We ignore the case
C=\mathbb{P}^{1}
because
\Sigma_{k}(\mathbb{P}^{1},L_{d})
is known to have a pure Betti diagram. Indeed, the resolution is an Eagon-Northcott complex associated to the
(k+1)\times(k+1)
minors of a 1-generic matrix. This is proved in
[
Eis88
]
, where the result is attributed to unpublished work of R. K. Wakerling.

Remark 2.3
.

Parts (
1
)-(
4
) were conjectured in
[
SV09b
]
, where they proved the conjectures for
k=1
. For elliptic curves, they were proven for all
k
in
[
GvBH04
]
.

It is well known that
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
does not lie on any hypersurface of degree less than
k+2

[
CJ08
]
. Thus for
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
, the condition
N_{k+2,p}
means that its ideal is generated in degree
k+2
and the resolution is linear to the
p
th step. This fact along with parts (
1
) and (
2
) of Theorem
2.1
imply that
\beta(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))
is of the form

(1)

\begin{array}[]{c|cccccccc}&0&1&2&\cdots&r_{d}-g-2k-1&r_{d}-g-2k&\cdots&r_{d}-2k-1\\
\hline\cr 0&1&-&-&\cdots&-&-&\cdots&-\\
1&-&-&-&\cdots&-&-&\cdots&-\\
\vdots&&\vdots&&&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
k&-&-&-&\cdots&-&-&\cdots&-\\
k+1&-&*&*&\cdots&*&*&\cdots&*\\
k+2&-&-&-&\cdots&-&*&\cdots&*\\
\vdots&&\vdots&&&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
2k+2&-&-&-&\cdots&-&*&\cdots&*\end{array}

where, a “
-
” denotes a zero entry and a “
*
” denotes a (possibly) non-zero entry.

Remark 2.4
.

We should point out that although
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
is a higher dimensional variety, we are not in the setting of
[
EL12
]
. In that paper, they fix a higher dimensional
X
and study the syzygies of large degree embeddings of
X
. Here, we consider large degree embeddings of a curve
C
and study the resulting higher dimensional variety
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
. In particular, the secant varieties
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
are not isomorphic for different values of
d
.

2.2.
Boij-Söderberg Decompositions

Boij-Söderberg theory provides a novel approach to the study of minimal free resolutions. We will not need the deepest aspects of the theory, so we only describe the bare minimum. We direct the interested reader to
[
ES09
]
for proofs of the core results and
[
EE17
]
for some of the deeper investigations of this phenomenon. We also recommend the excellent survey article
[
Flø12
]
.

As above, let
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\dots,x_{r}]
. For any finitely generated, Cohen-Macaulay graded
S
-module
M
of dimension 0, we have an associated Betti diagram
\beta(M)
. We consider the data of
\beta(M)
as an element in the infinite dimensional vector space
\mathbf{V}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n}\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. An element of
\mathbf{V}
is called a
formal diagram
.

The Betti diagrams of such modules generate a convex cone in
\mathbf{V}
. The fundamental results of Boij-Söderberg identify this cone as the cone spanned by the
pure diagrams
. A pure diagram has a unique non-zero entry in each column. Each pure diagram is characterized (up to scalar multiple of the entire diagram) by its
degree sequence

\mathbf{e}=(e_{0},\dots,e_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus n+1}
with
e_{0}<e_{1}<\dots<e_{n}
. Indeed, this follows from the Herzog-Kühl equations
[
Flø12
, Section 1.3]
. For this paper, we normalize he Betti numbers of the pure diagram
\beta(\mathbf{e})
associated to
\mathbf{e}
as

(2)

\kappa_{p,q}(\beta(\mathbf{e}))=\begin{cases}n!\prod_{i\not=e_{p}}\frac{1}{|e_{i}-e_{p}|}&p+q=e_{p}\\
0&otherwise\end{cases}.

The product in the denominator arises from the Herzog-Kühl equations, and the numerator is chosen so that this diagram has multiplicity 1. This follows from the computation of the multiplicity of an algebra with pure resolution
[
HM85
]
and
[
Erm15
, Lemma 1.2]
.

More explicitly, for any finitely generated, Cohen-Macaulay, graded
S
module
M
, we may write
\beta(M)
as a linear combination
\sum_{\mathbf{e}}c_{\mathbf{e}}\beta(\mathbf{e})
where
c_{\mathbf{e}}\geq 0
. This decomposition is not unique. However, there is a fan structure on the cone of Betti diagrams, and by fixing a simplex in the fan, one obtains a unique linear combination of pure diagrams corresponding to the extremal points of the simplex. As we shall see in the proof of the main theorem, we need not take this deeper structure into account.

2.3.
Hilbert Numerator

Let
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\dots,x_{r}]
. For a finitely generated graded
S
-module
M
, the Hilbert series
\mathrm{HS}_{M}(t):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\dim_{k}(M_{j})t^{j}
can be uniquely expressed as

\mathrm{HS}_{M}(t)=\frac{\HN_{M}(t)}{(1-t)^{\dim(M)}}

where
\HN_{M}(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
is a polynomial called the
Hilbert numerator
. The
multiplicity
of
M
is
\HN_{M}(1)
. Note that when
\dim{M}=0
, the Hilbert series and Hilbert numerator are equal.

The dimensions of the graded pieces of
M
(and thus the Hilbert numerator) are determined by the Betti diagram
[
Eis05
, Chapter 1]
. Thus, we can and do extend the notion of Hilbert numerator and multiplicity to formal diagrams.

3.
Asymptotic Purity

In this section, we study the behavior of Boij-Söderberg decompositions of the Betti diagram of
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
as
d\to\infty
. That is, we consider
C\subseteq\mathbb{P}H^{0}(C,L_{d})
embedded by the complete linear system and let
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
be the secant variety of
k
-planes. We always assume that
d\geq 2g+2k+1
so that Theorem
2.1
applies.

Recall that the Betti table of
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
has the form given in (
1
). Apart from the row of weight
(k+1)
, the possible non-zero entries are confined to a
(k+1)\times g
box. In particular, the number of possible pure diagrams in any Boij-Söderberg decomposition of the Betti table is a bounded function of
d
. Let us fix notation for the possible pure diagrams that can appear in the Boij-Söderberg decomposition of
\beta(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))
. We denote the set
\{0,\dots,n\}
by
[n]
. Fix a genus
g\geq 1
, so that
r_{d}=d-g
. Let
\mathbf{i}=(i_{0},\dots,i_{k})
be a tuple of integers with
0\leq i_{0}\leq i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{k}
. We define
\pi_{k}(\mathbf{i};d)
to be the pure diagram of formal multiplicity 1 associated to the degree sequence

\mathbf{e}_{k}(\mathbf{i};d):=[r_{d}+1]\setminus\{1,2,\dots,k+1,r_{d}+1-(i_{k}+k),\dots,r_{d}+1-(i_{1}+1),r_{d}+1-i_{0}\}.

Example 3.1
.

Let
k=1
,
g=3
, and
d=11
. So
r_{11}=8
. Then,

\mathbf{e}_{1}((1,3);11)=[9]\setminus\{1,2,5,8\}=\{0,3,4,6,7,9\},

and
\pi_{1}((1,3);11)
is of the form

\begin{array}[]{c|cccccc}&0&1&2&3&4&5\\
\hline\cr 0&*&-&-&-&-&-\\
1&-&-&-&-&-&-\\
2&-&*&*&-&-&-\\
3&-&-&-&*&*&-\\
4&-&-&-&-&-&*\end{array}.

Let
k=2
,
g=4
, and
d=15
. So
r_{d}=11
. Then

\mathbf{e}_{2}((1,3,3);15)=[12]\setminus\{1,2,3,7,8,11\}=\{0,4,5,6,8,10,12\},

and
\pi_{2}((1,3,3);15)
is of the form

\begin{array}[]{c|ccccccc}&0&1&2&3&4&5&6\\
\hline\cr 0&*&-&-&-&-&-&-\\
1&-&-&-&-&-&-&-\\
2&-&-&-&-&-&-&-\\
3&-&*&*&*&-&-&-\\
4&-&-&-&-&-&-&-\\
5&-&-&-&-&*&*&-\\
6&-&-&-&-&-&-&*\\
\end{array}.

To motivate this choice of notation, note that a pure diagram in a Boij-Söderberg decomposition of
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
has a nontrivial row in degree
k+1
and then “jumps” down at most
k+1
times. The indices
i_{j}
indicate the columns where the jumps occur (counting from the last column of the diagram). The number of jumps is the number of positive entries. So for instance, the diagram
\pi_{1}((1,3),11)
“jumps” down twice, once at the last column and once at the third to last column.
Zeros in the vector
\mathbf{e}_{k}(\mathbf{i};d)
indicate that a jump does not occur. Indeed, the diagram
\pi_{k}((0,\dots,0);d)
is the pure diagram with a single non-zero row of weight
k+1
(other than the
\kappa_{0,0}
entry which is always non-zero). That is, this diagram does not “jump down” at all.
∎

In order to compare the Betti diagrams for
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
as
d\to\infty
, it is natural to define

\overline{\beta}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})):=\frac{1}{\deg(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))}\beta(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})))

so that
\overline{\beta}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))
has multiplicity 1 for any
d
. As a consequence, we have the following boundedness property of the Boij-Söderberg coefficients.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
L_{d}
be a line bundle on
C
of degree
d\geq 2g+2k+1
. For any Boij-Söderberg decomposition

\overline{\beta}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))=\sum_{0\leq i_{0}\leq\cdots\leq i_{k}\leq g}c_{(i_{0},\dots,i_{k});d}\pi_{k}((i_{0},\dots,i_{k});d),

we have
\sum_{\mathbf{i}}c_{\mathbf{i};d}=1
and
0\leq c_{\mathbf{i};d}\leq 1
.

Proof.

The fact that we need only consider diagrams
\mathbf{i}=(i_{0},\dots,i_{k})
with each
i_{j}\leq g
follows from the discussion above. The nonnegativity of the coefficients
c_{\mathbf{i};d}
is a feature of the Boij-Söderberg decomposition. Finally, since multiplicity is linear, we see that

\sum_{\mathbf{i}}c_{\mathbf{i};d}=\sum_{\mathbf{i}}c_{\mathbf{i};d}\mult(\pi_{k}(\mathbf{i};d))=\mult(\overline{\beta}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})))=1.

Since each
c_{\mathbf{i};d}\geq 0
, the expression above implies
c_{\mathbf{i};d}\leq 1
.
∎

To prove the main theorem, we mimic the strategy of
[
Erm15
]
. In particular, we compare the coefficient of the highest degree term of the Hilbert numerator of
\overline{\beta}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))
and of its Boij-Söderberg decomposition.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
L_{d}
be a line bundle on
C
of degree
d\geq 2g+2k+1
. The Hilbert numerator of
\overline{\beta}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))
satisfies

\HN_{\overline{\beta}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))}(t)=\left(\frac{{g+k\choose k+1}}{\deg(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))}\right)t^{2k+2}+(\text{lower order terms in }t).

If
\mathbf{i}=(i_{0},\dots,i_{k})
with
1\leq i_{0}\leq\cdots\leq i_{k}\leq g
, then the Hilbert numerator of
\pi_{k}(\mathbf{i};d)
satisfies

\HN_{\pi_{k}(\mathbf{i};d)}(t)=\left(\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{k}(i_{j}+j)}{(r_{d}+1)\prod_{i=k+1}^{2k}(r_{d}-i)}\right)t^{2k+2}+(\text{lower order terms in }t).

Proof.

In general, the Hilbert numerator of a module
M
is a polynomial of degree
\reg(M)-1
. By taking general hyperplane sections, we may assume
M
is a module of finite length. Then the leading coefficient of the Hilbert series is the bottom-right entry of the Betti table.

Now, we address the specific cases at hand. Recall from Theorem
2.1
(
2
) that
\reg(\Sigma_{k}(L_{d}))=2k+3
, so the Hilbert numerator of
\beta(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))
has degree
2k+2
. The bottom-right Betti number in the minimal free-resolution of
\Sigma_{k}(L_{d})
is
{g+k\choose k+1}
by Theorem
2.1
(
4
). Since the Hilbert numerator is linear with respect to scaling of Betti tables, we see that the bottom right Betti number of
\overline{\beta}(\Sigma_{k}(L_{d}))
is
{g+k\choose k+1}/\deg(\Sigma_{k}(L_{d}))
as required.

Since
i_{j}>0
for all
j
, we see that
\pi_{k}(\mathbf{i};d)
has Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity
2k+3
. As above, it suffices to compute the bottom right Betti number
\kappa_{r_{d}-2k-1,2k+2}
which is given by the formula (
2
). Since each
i_{j}\geq 1
, the largest degree in the degree sequence is
r_{d}+1
. Thus, the bottom-right Betti number is given by

\displaystyle\kappa_{r_{d}-2k-1,2k+2}

\displaystyle=(r_{d}-2k-1)!\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\ell\leq r_{d}\\
\ell\notin\{1,2,\dots,k+1,r_{d}+1-(i_{k}+k),\dots,r_{d}+1-i_{0}\}\end{subarray}}(r_{d}+1-\ell)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{k}(i_{j}+j)}{(r_{d}+1)\prod_{\ell=k+2}^{2k+1}(r_{d}+1-\ell)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{k}(i_{j}+j)}{(r_{d}+1)\prod_{i=k+1}^{2k}(r_{d}-i)}

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Theorem 3.5
.

The normalized Betti table
\overline{\beta}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))
converges to
\pi_{k}((g,\dots,g);d)
as
d\to\infty
. That is, given a Boij-Söderberg decomposition
\overline{\beta}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))=\sum_{\mathbf{i}}c_{\mathbf{i};d}\pi_{k}(\mathbf{i};d)
for each
d
, we have

\lim_{d\to\infty}c_{\mathbf{i};d}=\begin{cases}1&\mathbf{i}=(g,\dots,g)\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}.

Proof.

By Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
, we see that the coefficient of
t^{2k+2}
in the Hilbert numerator of
\overline{\beta}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))
is

\displaystyle\frac{{g+k\choose k+1}}{\deg(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))}

\displaystyle={g+k\choose k+1}\cdot\frac{(k+1)!}{r_{d}^{k+1}}+\delta_{d}=\frac{\prod_{\ell=0}^{k}(g+\ell)}{r_{d}^{k+1}}+\delta_{d}

where
r_{d}^{k+1}\delta_{d}\to 0
as
d\to\infty
. Similarly, we see that the growth rate of the
t^{2k+2}
coefficient of the Hilbert numerator of
\pi_{k}(\mathbf{i};d)
is

\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{k}(i_{j}+j)}{r_{d}^{k+1}}+\varepsilon_{\mathbf{i};d}

where
r^{k+1}_{d}\varepsilon_{\mathbf{i};d}\to 0
as
d\to\infty
. The Hilbert numerator is an additive invariant. Applying the decomposition of Lemma
3.2
, we obtain
\HN_{\overline{\beta}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))}(t)=\sum c_{\mathbf{i};d}\HN_{\pi_{k}(\mathbf{i};d)}(t)
. Comparing the
t^{2k+2}
coefficients of each side of this equality yields

\frac{\prod_{\ell=0}^{k}(g+\ell)}{r_{d}^{k+1}}+\delta_{d}=\sum_{1\leq i_{0}\leq\cdots\leq i_{k}\leq g}\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{k}(i_{j}+j)}{r_{d}^{k+1}}+\varepsilon_{\mathbf{i};d}.

Multiplying by
r_{d}^{k+1}
and taking
d\to\infty
gives

\prod_{\ell=0}^{k}(g+\ell)=\lim_{d\to\infty}\sum_{1\leq i_{0}\leq\cdots\leq i_{k}\leq g}c_{\mathbf{i};d}\prod_{j=0}^{k}(i_{j}+j).

Since the diagrams
\overline{\beta}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))
and have multiplicity 1, we see that
\sum c_{\mathbf{i};d}=1
and
0\leq c_{\mathbf{i};d}\leq 1
. On the other hand, note that the product
\prod_{j=0}^{k}(i_{j}+j)
is maximized when
i_{0}=\cdots=i_{k}=g
, and in this case
\prod_{j=0}^{k}(i_{j}+j)=\prod_{\ell=0}^{l}(g+k)
. So
c_{(g,\dots,g);d}\to 1
while
c_{\mathbf{i};d}\to 0
for
\mathbf{i}\not=(g,\dots,g)
, as required.
∎

Note that Theorem
3.5
demonstrates that every entry in the bottom row that is not guaranteed to vanish by
[
ENP20
]
is nonzero for large
d
.

Corollary 3.6
.

For
d\gg 0
,
\kappa_{p,2k+2}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))\not=0
if and only if
r_{d}-2k-g\leq p\leq r_{d}-2k-1
.

Proof.

The coefficients in a Boij-Söderberg decomposition are non-negative. So if a pure diagram appears in the decomposition of a free resolution, any nonzero entries in the pure diagram are nonzero in the free resolution. For
d\gg 0
, the main theorem implies that the diagram
\pi_{k}((g,\dots,g);d)
appears in a Boij-Söderberg decomposition of
\overline{\beta}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))
with nonzero coefficient. Since the
(p,2k+2)
Betti number of this pure diagram is nonzero for
p
in the indicated range, the result follows.
∎

4.
Normal Distribution of Betti Numbers

In this section, we study the distribution of the Betti numbers in the minimal free resolution of
\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})
. By Theorem
2.1
, we need only consider the growth of the groups
K_{p,k+1}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d}))
. Using Theorem
3.5
, we observe that asymptotically, one only needs to analyze the Betti numbers of the pure diagram
\pi_{k}((g,\dots,g);d)
for which there is an explicit formula.

Our analysis relies on Stirling’s approximation
[
Dur01
, §3.1]
. In particular, for a fixed sequence
p_{d}
of integers such that
p_{d}\to\frac{r_{d}}{2}+\frac{a\sqrt{r_{d}}}{2}
(for some fixed
a\in\mathbb{R}
)
2
2

2

By
p_{d}\to\frac{r_{d}}{2}+\frac{a\sqrt{r_{d}}}{2}
, we mean that
\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{2p_{d}-r_{2}}{\sqrt{r_{d}}}=a
, we have

(3)

\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{\sqrt{2\pi r_{d}}}{2^{r_{d}+1}}{r_{d}\choose p_{d}}=e^{-a^{2}}.

We refer to this equality as Stirling’s formula. In making asymptotic comparisons, we write
f(d)\sim g(d)
if
\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{f(d)}{g(d)}=1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
1\leq p\leq r_{d}-g-2k-1
, we have

\displaystyle\kappa_{p,k+1}(\pi_{k}((g,\dots,g);d))

\displaystyle={r_{d}-2k\choose p}\cdot\frac{p}{p+k+1}\cdot\frac{\prod_{i=k}^{2k}(r_{d}-p-g-i)}{(r_{d}-2k)\prod_{i=k}^{2k-1}(r_{d}-p-i)}

Proof.

The degree sequence of
\pi_{k}((g,\dots,g);d)
is

\mathbf{e}_{k}((g,\dots,g);d)=(0,k+2,k+3,\dots,p+k+1,\dots,r_{d}-k-g,r_{d}-g+2,\dots,r+1).

So by definition,

(4)

\kappa_{p,k+1}((g,\dots,g);d)=\frac{(r_{d}-2k-1)!}{(p+k+1)(p-1)!(r_{d}-2k-g-p-1)!\prod_{i=0}^{g-1}(r_{d}-p-k-i)}.

Then since

\displaystyle(r_{d}-2k-g-p-1)!\prod_{i=0}^{g-1}(r_{d}-p-k-i)

\displaystyle=\frac{(r_{d}-p-k)!}{\prod_{i=k}^{2k}(r_{d}-g-p-i)}

we get

\displaystyle\kappa_{p,k+1}(\pi_{k}((g,\dots,g);d))

\displaystyle=\frac{(r_{d}-2k-1)!}{(p-1)!(r_{d}-p-k)!}\cdot\frac{\prod_{i=k}^{2k}(r_{d}-g-p-i)}{p+k+1}.

To conclude, we notice that

\displaystyle\frac{(r_{d}-2k-1)!}{(p-1)!(r_{d}-p-k)!}

\displaystyle={r_{d}-2k-1\choose p-1}\cdot\prod_{i=k}^{2k-1}\frac{1}{(r_{d}-p-i)}

\displaystyle={r_{d}-2k\choose p}\frac{p}{(r_{d}-2k)}\prod_{i=k}^{2k-1}\frac{1}{(r_{d}-p-i)}.

The second equality above follows from the relation
{n\choose k}=\frac{n}{k}{n-1\choose k-1}
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

For
1\leq p_{d}\leq r_{d}-g-2k-1
, we have

\kappa_{p_{d},k+1}(\pi_{k}((g,\dots,g);d))\sim\frac{1}{2^{2k+1}}{r_{d}\choose p_{d}}

as
p_{d}\to\frac{r_{d}}{2}+\frac{a\sqrt{r_{d}}}{2}
.

Proof.

We analyze the terms appearing in the statement of Lemma
4.1
. Notice that for
1\leq i\leq k
, we have

\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{r_{d}-p_{d}-g-i}{r_{d}-p_{d}-i}=1~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{p_{d}}{p_{d}+k+1}=1.

Furthermore,

\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{r_{d}-p_{d}-g-2k}{r_{d}-2k}=\frac{1}{2}.

Finally, the equality

\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{{r_{d}-2k\choose p_{d}}}{{r_{d}\choose p_{d}}}=\frac{1}{2^{2k}}

completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
C

Proof.

Consider a Boij-Söderberg decomposition of the normalized Betti table of

Notice that that

grows at least as fast each of the

and

3
3

3

Indeed, analyzing the proof of Lemma

. So as

\kappa_{p_{d},k+1}(\overline{\beta}(\Sigma_{k}(C,L_{d})))\sim\kappa_{p_{d},k+1}(\pi_{d}((g,\dots,g);d))

since

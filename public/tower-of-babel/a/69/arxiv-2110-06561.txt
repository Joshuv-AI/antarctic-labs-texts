An algebra isomorphism on U ⁡ ( 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n ) U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
Yang Li and Genqiang Liu

Abstract
. For each positive integer n n , let 𝔰 n = 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n ⋉ ℂ n \mathfrak{s}_{n}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\ltimes\mathbb{C}^{n} . We show that U ​ ( 𝔰 n ) X n ≅ 𝒟 n ⊗ U ⁡ ( 𝔰 n − 1 ) U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})_{X_{n}}\cong\mathcal{D}_{n}\otimes U(\mathfrak{s}_{n-1}) for any n ∈ ℤ ≥ 2 n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2} , where U ​ ( 𝔰 n ) X n U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})_{X_{n}} is the localization of U ⁡ ( 𝔰 n ) U(\mathfrak{s}_{n}) with respect to the subset X n := { e 1 i 1 ⋯ e n i n ∣ i 1 , … , i n ∈ ℤ + } X_{n}:=\{e_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots e_{n}^{i_{n}}\mid i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\} , and 𝒟 n \mathcal{D}_{n} is the Weyl algebra ℂ ⁡ [ x 1 ± 1 , ⋯ , x n ± 1 , ∂ ∂ x 1 , ⋯ , ∂ ∂ x n ] \mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\cdots,x_{n}^{\pm 1},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}] . As an application, we give a new proof of the Gelfand-Kirillov conjecture for 𝔰 n \mathfrak{s}_{n} and 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n \mathfrak{gl}_{n} . Moreover we show that the category of Harish-Chandra U ​ ( 𝔰 n ) X n U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})_{X_{n}} -modules with a fixed weight support is equivalent to the category of finite dimensional 𝔰 n − 1 \mathfrak{s}_{n-1} -modules whose representation type is wild, for any n ∈ ℤ ≥ 2 n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2} .



Keywords:
Weyl algebra, localization, Gelfand-Kirillov conjecture,

1.
introduction

Weyl type algebras or differential operator algebras play an important role in the structure theory and representation theory of Lie algebras.
The famous Gelfand-Kirillov conjecture (see
[
GK
]
) states that the skew fraction field of the universal enveloping algebra of an algebraic Lie algebra over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
is isomorphic to the skew field of fractions of some Weyl algebra over a purely transcendental extension of
\mathbb{K}
. The conjecture was settled by Gelfand and Kirillov
[
GK
]
for nilpotent Lie algebras, and for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
. For recent results on
Gelfand-Kirillov conjecture, we can see
[
P
]
. In the representation theory, an important application of Weyl algebras is the Beilinson-Bernstein localization Theorem, see
[
BB
]
.

Many important finite dimensional Lie algebras in mathematical physics are not semi-simple. Unlike complex semi-simple Lie algebras, the theory of non semi-simple Lie algebras is still not well developed. In the last decade, there were several important progresses on the representation of non semi-simple Lie algebras, see

[
BL
,
Du
,
DLMZ
,
LMZ
,
MM
]
and references therein.

For each positive integer
n
, let
\mathfrak{s}_{n}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\ltimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
which is isomorphic to the maximal parabolic subalgebra

\mathfrak{p}_{n}=\{(a_{ij})\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}\mid a_{n+1,k}=0,1\leq k\leq n\}
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
. The algebra
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is important for constructing
cuspidal
\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
-modules, see
[
GS
]
. On the other hand, the skew fraction field of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n+1})
is generated by the skew fraction field of
U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})
and its
n+1
central elements. These facts motivate us to study

U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})
and its representations.

We use the Weyl algebra
\mathcal{D}_{n}
to study
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
. We show that there is an isomorphism between
U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})_{X_{n}}
and
\mathcal{D}_{n}\otimes U(\mathfrak{s}_{n-1})
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
, see Theorem
3
.
This isomorphism makes it possible to study
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
by induction on
n
.
As an application, we give a new proof of the Gelfand-Kirillov conjecture for
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, see Theorem
6
and Theorem

8
. We can also reduce some infinite dimensional
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
-modules to finite dimensional
\mathfrak{s}_{n-1}
-modules, see Proposition
11
.

In this paper, we denote by
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\mathbb{C}
the sets of integers, positive integers, nonnegative
integers and complex numbers, respectively. All vector spaces and algebras are over
\mathbb{C}
. For a Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}
we denote by
U(\mathfrak{g})
its universal enveloping algebra. We write
\otimes
for

\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}
.

2.
Preliminaries

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
be the general linear Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
is consisting of
n\times n
-complex matrices.
Let
e_{ij}
denote the
n\times n
-matrix unit whose
(i,j)
-entry is
1
and
0
elsewhere,
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Then
\{e_{ij}\mid 1\leq i,j\leq n\}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.

Let
A_{n}=\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
be the Laurent polynomial algebra in
n
variables.
Then the subalgebra of
\text{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(A_{n})
generated by
\{x_{i},x_{i}^{-1},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
is called the Weyl algebra
\mathcal{D}_{n}
over
A_{n}
.
It is clear that
\mathcal{D}_{n}\cong\mathcal{D}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathcal{D}_{1}
. It is well known that there is an algebra homomorphism
\phi
from
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
to
\mathcal{D}_{n}
such that
\phi(e_{ij})=x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}
,
1\leq i,j\leq n
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}_{n}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}\ltimes\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the semidirect product of the general linear Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
and its natural representation
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. When
n=1
,
\mathfrak{s}_{1}
is the two dimensional Lie algebra
\mathbb{C}d_{0}\oplus\mathbb{C}d_{1}
such that
[d_{0},d_{1}]=d_{1}
. Let
\{e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}_{n}=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}e_{ii}
which is a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
. An
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
-module
M
is called a
weight module
if
\mathfrak{h}_{n}
acts diagonally on
M
, i.e.

M=\oplus_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{*}}M_{\lambda},

where
M_{\lambda}:=\{v\in M\mid hv=\lambda(h)v,\ \forall\ h\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}\}.
Denote

\mathrm{Supp}(M):=\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{*}\mid M_{\lambda}\neq 0\}.

For a weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}_{n}^{*}
, we identify
\lambda
with the

n
-tuple
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
, where
\lambda_{i}=\lambda(e_{ii})
. Conversely any vector
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
can be viewed as a
weight such that
\mu(e_{ii})=\mu_{i}
, for any
i
.
A nonzero element
v\in M_{\lambda}
is called a weight vector.
A weight
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
-module
M
is called a Harish-Chandra module if all its weight spaces are finite dimensional.

3.
The tensor product decomposition of
U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})_{X_{n}}

In this section, we will study the structure of
U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})
using the Weyl algebra
\mathcal{D}_{n}
and the localization technique.

3.1.
The isomorphism theorem

For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
, let
\mathfrak{a}_{n-1}
be the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
spanned by

e_{ij}-e_{jj},\ \ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

Lemma 1
.

For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
, the linear map
\xi:\mathfrak{s}_{n-1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}_{n-1}
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle e_{ij}\mapsto\tilde{e}_{ij}:=e_{ij}-e_{nj},

\displaystyle e_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto\tilde{e}_{i}:=\sum_{l=1}^{n}(e_{il}-e_{nl}),\ 1\leq i,j\leq n-1,

is a Lie algebra isomorphism.

Proof.

Since
\dim\mathfrak{s}_{n-1}=\dim\mathfrak{a}_{n-1}
and
\xi
is surjective,
\xi
is bijective.
For any
i,j,k,l\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
, we have

\displaystyle\ [\tilde{e}_{ij},\tilde{e}_{kl}]

\displaystyle=[e_{ij}-e_{nj},e_{kl}-e_{nl}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}(e_{il}-e_{n,l})-\delta_{li}(e_{kj}-e_{nj})

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}\tilde{e}_{il}-\delta_{li}\tilde{e}_{kj},

\displaystyle\ [\tilde{e}_{i},\tilde{e}_{j}]

\displaystyle=[\sum_{l=1}^{n}(e_{il}-e_{nl}),\sum_{k=1}^{n}(e_{jk}-e_{nk})]

\displaystyle=0,

and

\displaystyle\ [\tilde{e}_{ij},\tilde{e}_{k}]

\displaystyle=[e_{ij}-e_{nj},\sum_{l=1}^{n}(e_{kl}-e_{nl})]

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}\sum_{l=1}^{n}(e_{il}-e_{nl})

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}\tilde{e}_{i}.

Therefore
\xi
is a Lie algebra isomorphism.
∎

In
U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})
, every
e_{k}
is a locally
ad
-nilpotent element. So the subset
X_{n}:=\{e_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots e_{n}^{i_{n}}\mid i_{1},\dots,i_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}
is an ore subset of
U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})
, see Lemma 4.2 in
[
M
]
. We use
U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})_{X_{n}}
to denote the localization of
U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})
with respect to the subset
X_{n}
.

Proposition 2
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
, the linear map
\psi
defined by

(3.2)

\displaystyle U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})_{X_{n}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{n}\otimes U(\mathfrak{a}_{n-1}),

\displaystyle e_{ij}

\displaystyle\mapsto x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\otimes 1+x_{i}x_{j}^{-1}\otimes(e_{ij}-e_{jj}),

\displaystyle e_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto x_{i}\otimes 1,

\displaystyle e_{i}^{-1}

\displaystyle\mapsto x^{-1}_{i}\otimes 1,\ \ 1\leq i\leq n,

is an associative algebra isomorphism.

Proof.

For any
u\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
and
v\in U(\mathfrak{a}_{n-1})
, we also use
u
and
v
to denote the elements
u\otimes 1
and
1\otimes v
in
\mathcal{D}_{n}\otimes U(\mathfrak{a}_{n-1})
respectively. From

\displaystyle\psi([e_{ij},e_{kl}])=\psi(\delta_{jk}e_{il}-\delta_{li}e_{kj})

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{l}}\otimes 1+\delta_{jk}x_{i}x_{l}^{-1}\otimes(e_{il}-e_{ll})

\displaystyle-\delta_{li}x_{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\otimes 1-\delta_{li}x_{k}x_{j}^{-1}\otimes(e_{kj}-e_{jj})

\displaystyle=[\psi(e_{ij}),\psi(e_{kl})]

and
\psi([e_{ij},e_{k}])=[\psi(e_{ij}),\psi(e_{k})]
,
we can see the
\psi
preserves the Lie bracket relations of
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
. So
\psi
is an algebra homomorphism.
It can be also checked directly that the algebra homomorphism

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}:\mathcal{D}_{n}\otimes U(\mathfrak{a}_{n-1})

\displaystyle\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})_{X_{n}},

\displaystyle e_{ij}-e_{jj}

\displaystyle\mapsto e_{j}e_{i}^{-1}e_{ij}-e_{jj},

\displaystyle x_{i}

\displaystyle\mapsto e_{i},

\displaystyle x_{i}^{-1}

\displaystyle\mapsto e^{-1}_{i},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n,

is the inverse of
\psi
.
Therefore
\psi
is an isomorphism.
∎

Combining Lemma
1
with Proposition
2
, we have the following tensor product decomposition of
U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})_{X_{n}}
.

Theorem 3
.

We have the associative algebra isomorphism

U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})_{X_{n}}\cong\mathcal{D}_{n}\otimes U(\mathfrak{s}_{n-1}),

for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
.

Remark 4
.

This isomorphism in Theorem
3
is motivated by the tensor field modules over the Witt algebra, see
[
Sh
]
.

3.2.
The Gelfand-Kirillov conjecture for
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}

For any
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, define
\mathcal{D}_{r,s}=\mathcal{D}_{r}\otimes\mathbb{C}[y_{1},\dots,y_{s}]
which is a Noetherian domain. Denote its skew field of fractions by
\mathbf{F}_{r,s}
.
If
s=0
, then we denote
\mathbf{F}_{r,0}
by
\mathbf{F}_{r}
.

In 1966, in the famous paper
[
GK
]
, Gelfand and Kirillov put forward the following conjecture.

Conjecture 5
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is the Lie algebra of a linear algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
, then the skew fraction field
\mathbf{F}(\mathfrak{g})
of
U(\mathfrak{g})
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{F}_{r,s}
for some

r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
depending
\mathfrak{g}
.

The conjecture was settled for nilpotent Lie algebras,
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}

by Gelfand and Kirillov in the same paper
[
GK
]
.

In 1979, Nghiêm Xuân Hai showed that the Gelfand-Kirillov conjecture is true for the semi-direct products
\mathfrak{sl}(n),\mathfrak{sp}(2n)
and
\mathfrak{so}(n)
with their standard representation. Next using Theorem
3
, we give another proof for the Gelfand-Kirillov conjecture of
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
.

Theorem 6
.

The skew fraction field
\mathbf{F}(\mathfrak{s}_{n})
of
U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{F}_{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}
, for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
. If
n=1
, then
\mathfrak{s}_{1}
is the two dimensional non-abelian Lie algebra. Recall that
\mathfrak{s}_{1}=\mathbb{C}d_{0}\oplus\mathbb{C}d_{1}
such that
[d_{0},d_{1}]=d_{1}
. Let

U(\mathfrak{s}_{1})_{d_{1}}
denote the localization of
U(\mathfrak{s}_{1})
respect to the subset
\{d_{1}^{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\}
. Then the map

U(\mathfrak{s}_{1})_{d_{1}}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{1}:d_{1}^{-1}d_{0}\mapsto\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},d_{1}\mapsto x_{1}

can give an isomorphism between
\mathbf{F}(\mathfrak{s}_{1})
and the
the skew fraction field of
\mathcal{D}_{1}
. For arbitrary positive integer
n
, by the isomorphism in Theorem
3
,

\mathbf{F}(\mathfrak{s}_{n})\cong\mathbf{F}_{n}\otimes\mathbf{F}(\mathfrak{s}_{n-1})
. Then by the inductive hypothesis,
\mathbf{F}(\mathfrak{s}_{n-1})\cong\mathbf{F}_{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}
.
Consequently
\mathbf{F}(\mathfrak{s}_{n})\cong\mathbf{F}_{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}
.
∎

Corollary 7
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, the center of
U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})
is
\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

By Proposition 5.12 in Chapter
1
of
[
D
]
, the center of
\mathcal{D}_{n}
is
\mathbb{C}
. Then the center of the Weyl field
\mathbf{F}_{n}
is also
\mathbb{C}
. Hence so is the center of
U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})
by Theorem
6
.
∎

Using Theorem
6
and the generators of the center of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
,
we give a proof of the Gelfand-Kirillov conjecture for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
.

Theorem 8

(Gelfand and Kirillov, 1966)
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
, the skew fraction field
\mathbf{F}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{F}_{\frac{n(n-1)}{2},n}
.

Proof.

For every
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, let

c_{k}\ =\ \displaystyle{\sum_{(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\in\{1,\ldots,n\}^{k}}}E_{i_{1}i_{2}}E_{i_{2}i_{3}}\ldots E_{i_{k}i_{1}}.

It is well known that the center
Z_{n}
of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})

is generated by
c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
, see
[
GT
]
.
By the result in
[
Di
]
,
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\subset\mathbf{F}(\mathfrak{p}_{n-1})Z_{n}
, where
\mathfrak{p}_{n-1}
is the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
spanned by
e_{i,j},1\leq i\leq n-1,1\leq j\leq n
. Note that
\mathfrak{p}_{n-1}\cong\mathfrak{s}_{n-1}
.
Then by Theorem
6
, we have that
\mathbf{F}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})\cong\mathbf{F}_{\frac{n(n-1)}{2},n}
.
∎

Remark 9
.

Although Gelfand and Kirillov used the induction method in
[
GK
]
,
but they did not give the isomorphism in Theorem
3
.
In 2010, Premet showed that the Gelfand-Kirillov conjecture does not hold for
simple Lie algebras of types
B_{n},n\geq 3,D_{n},n\geq 4,E_{6},E_{7},E_{8}
and
F_{4}
, see
[
P
]
. The Gelfand-Kirillov conjecture remains open for simple Lie algebras of types
C_{n},G_{2}
.
We expect that the tensor product decomposition method for
U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})
may be useful to the study of
U(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n})
.

3.3.
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
-modules

Using the isomorphism in Theorem
3
, we can study weight
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
-modules.
For any
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and any
\mathfrak{s}_{n-1}
-module
V
, the tensor product
x^{\lambda}A_{n}\otimes V
can be defined to be an
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
-module denoted by
T(\lambda,V)
via the isomorphism in Theorem
3
, where
x^{\lambda}=x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}
.

Lemma 10
.

If
M=\oplus_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}M_{\lambda+\alpha}
is a Harish-Chandra
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
-modules with each
e_{i},1\leq i\leq n
acting on it bijectively, then
M\cong T(\lambda,V)
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and some
\mathfrak{s}_{n-1}
-module
V
.

Proof.

Using the isomorphism in Theorem
3
, we view
M
as
a module over
\mathcal{D}_{n}\otimes U(\mathfrak{s}_{n-1})
. Moreover
M
is a weight
\mathcal{D}_{n}
-module with respect to the action of
\{x_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,x_{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\}
. Set
V=M_{\lambda}
which is a
U(\mathfrak{s}_{n-1})
-modules, since
[\mathcal{D}_{n},U(\mathfrak{s}_{n-1})]=0
. As a vector space

M=\oplus_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}x^{\alpha}\otimes V=x^{\lambda}A_{n}\otimes V.

For any
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\cdot x^{\alpha}\otimes v=x_{i}^{-1}\cdot(x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}})\cdot x^{\alpha}\otimes v=(\lambda_{i}+\alpha_{i})x^{\alpha-\epsilon_{i}}\otimes v.

So as a
\mathcal{D}_{n}\otimes U(\mathfrak{s}_{n-1})
-module,

M\cong x^{\lambda}A_{n}\otimes V
. Consequently,
M\cong T(\lambda,V)
as an
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
-module.
∎

Proposition 11
.

For a fixed
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda,n}

be the category of Harish-Chandra
U(\mathfrak{s}_{n})_{X_{n}}
-modules
M
such that
\text{Supp}(M)=\lambda+\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda,n}
is
equivalent to the category
\mathcal{A}_{n-1}
of finite dimensional
\mathfrak{s}_{n-1}
-modules, where
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
.

Proof.

We will show that the functor

\displaystyle T(\lambda,-):\ \mathcal{A}_{n-1}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{\lambda,n},\ \ V

\displaystyle\mapsto T(\lambda,V),

is an equivalence of the two categories.
By Schur’s Lemma, we have
\text{End}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}(x^{\lambda}A_{n})=\mathbb{C}
.
So for any
V,W\in\mathcal{A}_{n-1}
,

\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}\otimes U(\mathfrak{s}_{n-1})}(T(\lambda,V),T(\lambda,W))\cong\text{Hom}_{\mathfrak{s}_{n-1}}(V,W).

So the functor
T(\lambda,-)
is fully faithful. By Lemma
10
,
T(\lambda,-)
is an equivalence.
∎

Let
\mathbb{C}\langle x,y\rangle
be the free associative algebra over
\mathbb{C}
generated by two variables
x,y
. Recall that an abelian category
\mathcal{C}
is wild if there exists an exact functor from the category of finite dimensional
\mathbb{C}\langle x,y\rangle
-modules to
\mathcal{C}
, preserving indecomposability and mapping non-isomorphic modules to
non-isomorphic objects.

Corollary 12
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the category
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda,n}
is wild.

Proof.

By the results in
[
Mak
]
, the category
\mathcal{A}_{n-1}
is wild. So does
\mathcal{C}_{\lambda,n}
.
∎

Remark 13
.

In
[
Di
]
, it was shown that there is an element
d\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})

such that the localization of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})_{d}
is isomorphic to

U(\mathfrak{s}_{n-1})_{d}\otimes Z(U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n}))
. So the study of
\mathfrak{s}_{n-1}
-modules may be
meaningful for constructing
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
-modules.

Abstract
. In this work, we investigate the global existence and asymptotic behavior of a mathematical model of nonlinear ultrasonic heating based on a coupled system of the Westervelt equation and the hyperbolic Pennes bioheat equation (Westervelt–Pennes–Cattaneo model). First, we prove that the solution exists globally in time, provided that the lower-order Sobolev norms of the initial data are considered to be small, while the higher-order norms can be arbitrarily large. This is done using a continuity argument together with some interpolation inequalities. Second, we prove an exponential decay of the solution under the same smallness assumptions on the initial data.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The model

This paper is concerned with the global existence and asymptotic behavior of solutions to the Westervelt–Pennes–Cattaneo system subject to Dirichlet boundary conditions for both temperature and pressure. More precisely, we consider the system

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &p_{tt}-c^{2}(\bar{\Theta})\Delta p-b\Delta p_{t}=K(\bar{\Theta})\left(p^{2}\right)_{tt},\qquad&\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T),\\
&\rho_{\textup{a}}C_{\textup{a}}\bar{\Theta}_{t}+\nabla\cdot q+\rho_{\textup{b}}C_{\textup{b}}W(\bar{\Theta}-\Theta_{\textup{a}})=\mathcal{Q}(p_{t}),\qquad&\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T),\\
&\tau q_{t}+q+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\nabla\bar{\Theta}=0,\qquad&\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T).\end{aligned}\right.

Here
p
and
\bar{\Theta}
denotes respectively, the acoustic pressure and the temperature fluctuations. The thermal parameters
\rho_{\textup{a}},C_{\textup{a}}
and
\kappa_{\textup{a}}
are, respectively, the ambient density, the ambient heat capacity and thermal conductivity of the tissue. The additional term
\rho_{\textup{b}}C_{\textup{b}}W(\bar{\Theta}-\Theta_{\textup{a}})
represents the heat loss caused by blood circulation, with
\rho_{\textup{b}},C_{\textup{b}}
being the density and specific heat capacity of the blood, and
W
expressing the tissue’s volumetric perfusion rate measured in milliliters of blood per milliliter of tissue per second. We denote by
\Theta_{\textup{a}}
the ambient temperature, that is typically taken in the human body to be
37^{\circ}C
; see
[
8
]
. The diffusivity of sound is
b
and
c
is the speed of sound, which we assume to depend on the temperature
\bar{\Theta}
. The function
K
is given by
K(\bar{\Theta})=\beta/(\rho c^{2}(\bar{\Theta}))
,
where
\rho
is the mass density and
\beta
is the parameter of nonlinearity.

We complement (
1.1
) with the initial conditions

(1.2)

p|_{t=0}=p_{0},\quad p_{t}|_{t=0}=p_{1},\quad\bar{\Theta}|_{t=0}=\bar{\Theta}_{0},\quad q|_{t=0}=q_{0}

and Dirichlet-Dirichlet boundary conditions

(1.3)

p|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\qquad\bar{\Theta}|_{\partial\Omega}=\Theta_{\textup{a}}.

When
c
and
K
are constant, the first equation in (
1.1
) reduces to the well-known Westervelt equation, which is a classical model in nonlinear acoustics and was originally derived in
[
33
]
. It is frequently used for describing the propagation of high-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) in thermo-viscous media
[
32
,
8
,
9
]
.

The second and third equations in (
1.1
) describe the evolution of the temperature
\bar{\Theta}
where the heating source is the acoustic energy absorbed by the tissue represented here by the function
\mathcal{Q}
, which we assume, to have the form

(1.4)

\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})=\frac{2b}{\rho_{\textup{a}}c_{a}^{4}}(p_{t})^{2}

where
c_{a}
is the ambient speed
[
8
]
. Indeed, together these two equations constitute the hyperbolic version of the Pennes’ equation
[
26
]

(see also
[
12
, Eq. 3]
and
[
35
, Eq. 7]
)

(1.5)

\displaystyle\tau\rho_{\textup{a}}C_{\textup{a}}\bar{\Theta}_{tt}+(\rho_{\textup{a}}C_{\textup{a}}+\tau\rho_{\textup{b}}C_{\textup{b}}W)\bar{\Theta}_{t}+\rho_{\textup{b}}C_{\textup{b}}W(\bar{\Theta}-\Theta_{\textup{a}})-\kappa_{\textup{a}}\Delta\bar{\Theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})+\tau\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t}),

which we employ here to model heat transfer in biological systems. Its hyperbolic nature comes from adopting the Cattaneo law of heat conduction
[
5
]
:

(1.6)

\tau q_{t}+q+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\nabla\bar{\Theta}=0

which differs from the classical Fourier law:

(1.7)

q+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\nabla\bar{\Theta}=0

by the presence of the relaxation term
\tau q_{t}
, where
\tau
is the relaxation time parameter. The Cattaneo law was introduced to overcome the paradox of the infinite speed of propagation of thermal signals in the Fourier law; namely equation (
1.7
) implies that any temperature disturbance causes an instantaneous perturbation in the temperature at each point in the medium, which does not accurately represent the physical reality, for instance in situations that involve short heating periods; see
[
35
]
,
[
12
]
.

An important feature of the current model (
1.1
) is the dependence of the speed of sound
c
on the temperature
\bar{\Theta}

[
8
,
11
]
.
This connection holds practical significance in various applications. Experimental studies on HIFU therapy have indicated
that the temperature elevation in the tissue, induced by the ultrasonic energy, will lead to changes in the physical properties of the tissue, which in turn affect the acoustic and the temperature fields
[
6
]
.

1.2.
Related literature

The Westervelt equation has received considerable attention in recent years and significant progress has been made recently toward the understanding of its solutions and their behavior; see
[
23
,
7
,
13
,
22
,
17
,
30
,
14
]
and the references therein.
In
[
14
]
, the authors proved, under homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, that a unique solution exists globally in time and decays exponentially fast. This was achieved by exploiting the strong damping expressed by the term
-b\Delta p_{t},\,b>0
. In addition, the initial data were assumed to be small, a constraint that is typically required for the solutions to nonlinear acoustics equations, even for local well-posedness, to avoid potential degeneracy. Similar well-posedness results for the Westervelt equation subject to other types of boundary conditions, such as Neumann and absorbing boundary conditions, were provided in the papers
[
18
,
15
,
30
,
23
]
. We mention also the work
[
16
]
where the authors addressed the behavior of the solutions of the Westervelt equation when the sound diffusivity
b\rightarrow 0^{+}
. They proved that the solution converges at a linear rate, as
b
goes to zero, to the solution of the wave equation, corresponding to
b=0
.

For
\tau=0
, system (
1.1
) reduces to the Westervelt–Pennes–Fourier system:

(1.8)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &p_{tt}-c^{2}(\bar{\Theta})\Delta p-b\Delta p_{t}=K(\bar{\Theta})\left(p^{2}\right)_{tt},\\
&\rho_{\textup{a}}C_{\textup{a}}\bar{\Theta}_{t}-\kappa_{\textup{a}}\Delta\bar{\Theta}+\rho_{\textup{b}}C_{\textup{b}}W(\bar{\Theta}-\Theta_{\textup{a}})=\mathcal{Q}(p_{t}),\end{aligned}\right.

where the second equation is the
the parabolic Pennes equation, that is widely used for studying heat transfer in biological systems as it accounts for both conduction-based heat transfer in tissues and convective heat transfer due to blood perfusion
[
26
]
.

To the best of the authors’ knowledge, it seems that the first mathematical study of (
1.8
) is the one presented in
[
24
]
where Nikolić and Said-Houari considered (
1.8
) with Dirichlet–Dirichlet boundary conditions, and proved local well-posedness using the energy method together with a fixed point argument. The work in
[
24
]
was followed by
[
25
]
, where under a smallness assumption on the initial data, the authors established the existence of a global-in-time solution of (
1.8
) and proved an exponential decay of the solution. Using the maximal regularity estimate for parabolic systems, Wilke in
[
34
]
improved slightly the regularity assumptions in
[
24
]
.

Recently in
[
2
]
we investigated (
1.1
) and by employing the energy method together with a fixed point argument, we established the local well-posedness of (
1.1
). In addition, we showed that (
1.1
) does not degenerate under a smallness assumption on the pressure data in the Westervelt equation. Furthermore, we performed a singular limit analysis and proved that the Westervelt–Pennes–Fourier model (
1.8
) can
be seen as an approximation of the Westervelt–Pennes–Cattaneo model (
1.1
), when the relaxation parameter
\tau
tends to zero.

1.3.
Main contributions

The main goal of this work is to study the global existence and asymptotic behavior of the solution to the Westervelt–Pennes–Cattaneo model (
1.1
). First, using the energy method together with the bootstrap argument, we show that the solution is global in time, provided that the initial data are small enough. We emphasize here that we assume smallness only on the lower-order Sobolev norms of the initial data, while the higher-order norms can be arbitrarily large. Second, under the same smallness assumption on the initial data, and by using a Gronwall’s type inequality, we prove that the solution decays exponentially fast to the steady state.

1.4.
Outline of the presentation

The rest of this paper is structured as follows: In Section
2
, we reformulate our problem into a new system that is more convenient for the energy analysis. We also introduce the necessary theoretical preliminaries and the main assumptions used in the proofs. In Section
3
, we state the main results and give some comments about them.
Section
4
is dedicated to the energy analysis. Achieving the control of the different energies involves delicate estimates, particularly as we aim to assume smallness solely on the lower-order energy. To this end, we apply some interpolation inequalities to facilitate the extraction of these lower-order norms in the nonlinear estimates. In Section
5
, we carry out the proofs of the global wellposedness and decay rate of solutions to (
1.1
) by putting together the energy estimates established in Section
4
.

2.
Statement of the problem and main results

To state and prove our result and to lighten the notation, we put

(2.1)

m=\rho_{\textup{a}}C_{\textup{a}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ell=\rho_{\textup{b}}C_{\textup{b}}W.

We combine the second and third equations in (
1.1
) to get the equation (
1.5
), then we make the change of variables

\Theta=\bar{\Theta}-\Theta_{a}

in the temperature so that now the bioheat equation becomes

(2.2)

\tau m\Theta_{tt}+(m+\tau\ell)\Theta_{t}+\ell\Theta-\kappa_{\textup{a}}\Delta\Theta=\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})+\tau\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t}),\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T),

and denoting by

(2.3)

k(\Theta)=K(\Theta+\Theta_{\textup{a}})=\frac{\beta}{\rho c^{2}(\Theta+\Theta_{\textup{a}})}\qquad\text{and}\qquad h(\Theta)=c^{2}(\Theta+\Theta_{\textup{a}}),

we recast the pressure equation in (
1.1
) as

(2.4)

(1-2k(\Theta)p)p_{tt}-h(0)\Delta p-b\Delta p_{t}=2k(\Theta)(p_{t})^{2}+\tilde{h}(\Theta)\Delta p,\qquad\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T)

where we have decomposed
h(\Theta)
as follows

(2.5)

h(\Theta)=h(0)+\tilde{h}(\Theta)\quad\text{with}\quad\tilde{h}(\Theta)=\int_{0}^{\Theta}h^{\prime}(s)\,\textup{d}s,\quad h(0)=c^{2}(\Theta_{\textup{a}}).

So henceforth, we consider system (
1.1
) in the following form

(2.6a)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(1-2k(\Theta)p)p_{tt}-h(0)\Delta p-b\Delta p_{t}=2k(\Theta)(p_{t})^{2}+\tilde{h}(\Theta)\Delta p,\quad&\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T),\\
&\tau m\Theta_{tt}+(m+\tau\ell)\Theta_{t}+\ell\Theta-\kappa_{\textup{a}}\Delta\Theta=\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})+\tau\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t}),\quad&\text{in}\ \Omega\times(0,T).\end{aligned}\right.

Note that the purpose of the shift in the temperature variable is that now we have homogeneous boundary conditions for both the pressure and the temperature

(2.6b)

\displaystyle p|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\qquad\Theta|_{\partial\Omega}=0

and the initial conditions are given by

(2.6c)

\displaystyle p|_{t=0}=p_{0},\quad p_{t}|_{t=0}=p_{1},\quad\Theta|_{t=0}=\Theta_{0}:=\bar{\Theta}_{0}-\Theta_{\textup{a}},\quad\Theta_{t}|_{t=0}=\Theta_{1}.

The function

is given by (

); however our proofs also work for quite general

satisfying Assumption 2 in
[

. Further, the dependence of the acoustic parameters

and

[

]

3.1.
Discussion of the main result

Before moving onto the proof, we briefly discuss the statements made above in Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
.

1.

Similarly to the result in
[
4
]
(see also
[
28
]
), we only assume the lower-order Sobolev norms of the initial data to be small, while the higher-order norms can be arbitrarily large. To do this, we do not rely directly on the embeddings
H^{2}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty},H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{4}
, but we instead use the interpolation inequalities (
3.8
), (
3.9
), which give the flexibility to impose smallness on lower-order norms
when we estimate the nonlinear terms.

2.

Among the difficulties encountered when trying to prove our result stems from the fact that the two equations in (
2.6a
) are coupled via the temperature in the sound coefficients of the Westervelt equation and via the source term in the hyperbolic Pennes’ equation. This differs from the thermoelastic systems
[
27
]
, in which the coupling is within the linearized system, which generates dissipation for both components of the solution (even when the elastic component is not directly damped). Hence, the presence of the term
-b\Delta p_{t},\,b>0
is crucial in our analysis. It is an interesting open problem to show a global existence in the case
b=0
.

3.

The hyperbolic nature of the Pennes’ equation (
1.5
) introduces a higher degree of complexity in the analysis compared to the parabolic Pennes’ equation
[
25
]
; first, it does not yield any smoothing properties of the equation of the temperature and second it produces the source term
\tau\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})
, which requires more regularity assumption on the pressure component to control it.

3.2.
Some useful inequalities and embedding results

In preparation for the upcoming analysis, we invoke some theoretical results that we shall frequently use.

Sobolev embeddings

Among the main helpful tools are Sobolev embeddings, especially the continuous embeddings
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
and
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. In particular, using Poincaré’s inequality we obtain for
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
(see
[
29
, Theorem 7.18]
)

(3.5)

\displaystyle\text{if}\ d>2,\quad\|v\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq p\leq\frac{2d}{d-2},

\displaystyle\text{if}\ d=2,\quad\|v\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}\quad\text{for}\quad 2\leq p<\infty.

Moreover, taking into account the boundedness of the operator
(-\Delta)^{-1}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we find the inequality

\qquad\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{1}\|v\|_{H^{2}}\leq C_{2}\|\Delta v\|_{L^{2}}.

Young’s inequality

We recall Young’s
\varepsilon
-inequality

(3.6)

xy\leq\varepsilon x^{n}+C(\varepsilon)y^{m},\quad\text{where}\quad\ x,y>0,\quad 1<m,n<\infty,\quad\frac{1}{m}+\frac{1}{n}=1,

and
C(\varepsilon)=(\varepsilon n)^{-m/n}m^{-1}
. In particular, we will make repeated use of the inequality

(3.7)

xy\leq\varepsilon x^{2}+\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}y^{2},\qquad\varepsilon>0.

Interpolation inequalities

Further, we will make use of Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality for
u\in H^{1}(\Omega)

(3.8)

\|u\|_{L^{4}}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{1-d/4}\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{d/4},\qquad 1\leq d\leq 4,

and of Agmon’s interpolation inequality
[
1
, Ch. 13]
for functions in
H^{2}(\Omega)
:

(3.9)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\textup{A}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{1-d/4}\|u\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}^{d/4},\qquad d\leq 4.

We state a version of Gronwall’s inequality from which the exponential decay of solutions to (
2.6
) will follow. For the proof, the reader is referred to
[
19
, Appendix A]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
y\in L^{1}_{\textup{loc}}[0,\infty)
be a nonnegative function. Assume that there exists
\nu>0
such that for almost every
s\geq 0
and for every
t\geq s
, it holds that

(3.10)

y(t)+\nu\int_{s}^{t}y(r)\text{d}r\leq y(s).

Further, assume that (
3.10
) holds for
s=0
. Then, we have for all
t\geq 0

(3.11)

y(t)\leq y(0)e^{-\nu t}.

4.
Energy analysis

The main goal of this section is to derive uniform with respect to time energy estimates for the solution
(p,\Theta)
of (
2.6
). These estimates will play a crucial role in the subsequent proof of global well-posedness and the asymptotic behavior of the solution. First, we establish the estimate of the total energy related to the equation of
\Theta
in (
2.6a
) and then we estimate the total energy associated with the equation of
p
in (
2.6a
). From the technical point of view, seeking only to assume smallness on a lower topology makes the proof more involved since some extra estimates of lower-order energies are needed. These estimates should be properly factored out in the nonlinear estimates using the interpolation inequalities (
3.8
) and (
3.9
).

4.1.
Energy functionals

We begin by introducing the energy functionals and the associated dissipation rates that will be used in the proofs.

4.1.1.
Bioheat energies

We define the energies for the bioheat equation in (
2.6a
):

(4.1a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{k}[\Theta](t):=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(m+\ell+\tau\ell)\|\partial_{t}^{k}\Theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\tau m}{2}\|\partial_{t}^{k}\Theta_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\kappa_{\textup{a}}}{2}\|\nabla\partial_{t}^{k}\Theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\qquad k=0,1.

The corresponding dissipation rates are given by

(4.1b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{k}[\Theta](t):=

\displaystyle\ell\|\partial_{t}^{k}\Theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(m+\tau\ell)\|\partial_{t}^{k}\Theta_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\nabla\partial_{t}^{k}\Theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\qquad k=0,1.

Thus, the total energy for the temperature equation and the total dissipation rate are given, respectively, as:

(4.2a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}[\Theta](t)=\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta](t)+\mathcal{E}_{1}[\Theta](t)+\tau m\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\Delta\Theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and

(4.2b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}[\Theta](t)=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{0}[\Theta](t)+\mathcal{D}_{1}[\Theta](t)+(m+\tau\ell)\|\nabla\Theta_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\Delta\Theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

4.1.2.
Acoustic energies

The acoustic energies are defined as

(4.3)

\displaystyle E_{1}[p,\Theta](t)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\big(\|\sqrt{1-2k(\Theta)p}p_{tt}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+(1+h(0))\|\nabla p_{t}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+(b+h(0))\|\Delta p(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big),

\displaystyle E_{2}[p](t)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\big((1+b)\|\nabla p_{tt}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+b\|\nabla\Delta p(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+h(0)\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big).

We denote by
D_{i}[p],i=1,2
the corresponding dissipation rates given by

(4.4)

\displaystyle D_{1}[p](t)

\displaystyle:=b\|\nabla p_{tt}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{h(0)}\Delta p(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+b\|\Delta p_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle D_{2}[p](t)

\displaystyle:=\|\sqrt{h(0)}\nabla\Delta p(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+b\|\Delta p_{tt}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|p_{ttt}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

As we will be applying a continuity argument to obtain global a priori bounds for the local solution
(p,\Theta)\in X_{p}\times X_{\Theta}
, we make the following a priori assumption
[
31
]

(4.5)

2\|k(\Theta)p\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq\mathsf{m}<1,

with
\mathsf{m}>0
independent of
t
. This then yields that there exist positive constants
0<\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}
independent of time such that

(4.6)

\alpha_{1}\leq 1-2k(\Theta)p\leq\alpha_{2}.

This is necessary to avoid degeneracy of the pressure wave equation and persists for all times as long as we impose a smallness condition on a lower-order norm of the initial data, see
[
14
,
20
]
and Section
5.1
below.

4.2.
The bioheat equation

In what follows, we will show that, for the local-in-time solution to the temperature equation in (
2.6
)
\Theta\in X_{\Theta}
, the energies
\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta],\mathcal{E}_{1}[\Theta]
and
\mathcal{E}[\Theta]
satisfy a priori estimates that are uniform with respect to
t
. In fact, we have a global bound for the total energy
\mathcal{E}[\Theta]
, which is stated in the proposition below.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\tau>0
. For all
t\geq 0
, the solution
(\Theta,p)\in X_{p}\times X_{\Theta}
satisfies

(4.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}[\Theta](t)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}[\Theta](s)\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}[\Theta](0)+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(E_{1}[p]+(E_{1}[p])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(E_{2}[p])^{\frac{d}{4}}\Big)\big(D_{1}[p]+D_{2}[p]\big)\,\textup{d}s

where the hidden constant does not depend on
t
.

The proof of Proposition
4.1
will be given later, and it is the result of Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\tau>0
. It holds that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta](t)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}_{0}[\Theta](s)\,\textup{d}s\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta](0)+\int_{0}^{t}E_{1}[p](s)D_{1}[p](s)\,\textup{d}s

for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

We multiply the bioheat equation (
2.2
) by
\Theta_{t}
and integrate over space, using integration by parts, we obtain the identity

(4.9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\big(\tau m\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\ell\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+(m+\tau\ell)\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})+\tau\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t}))\Theta_{t}\,\textup{d}x.

Applying Hölder and Young inequalities, we get

(4.10)

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\big(\tau m\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\ell\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+(m+\tau\ell)\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m}\big(\|\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\tau^{2}\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+\frac{m}{2}\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The last term on the right-hand side can be absorbed by the dissipative term on the left. Thus, integrating with respect to time, it follows that

(4.11)

\displaystyle\tau m\|\Theta_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\ell\|\Theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\nabla\Theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(\frac{m}{2}+\tau\ell)\|\Theta_{t}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta](0)+\frac{1}{2m}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\tau^{2}\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\,\textup{d}s.

Next, we test the equation (
2.2
) by
\Theta
and integrate with respect to
x
, using integrating by parts, we find

(4.12)

\displaystyle\frac{m+\tau\ell}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\ell\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tau m\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\left(\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{t}\Theta\,\textup{d}x\right)+\tau m\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})+\tau\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t}))\Theta\,\textup{d}x

where we have relied on the relation

(4.13)

\tau m\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\left(\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{t}\Theta\,\textup{d}x\right)=\tau m\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{tt}\Theta\,\textup{d}x+\tau m\int_{\Omega}|\Theta_{t}|^{2}\,\textup{d}x.

Then, we can estimate the right-hand side of (
4.12
) as follows

(4.14)

\displaystyle\frac{m+\tau\ell}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\ell\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\tau m\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\left(\int_{\Omega}\Theta_{t}\Theta\,\textup{d}x\right)+\tau m\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\ell}\big(\|\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\tau^{2}\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+\frac{\ell}{2}\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Observe that the last term on the right can be absorbed by the dissipation on the left of (
4.14
). Further, integrating from
0
to
t
, we obtain

(4.15)

\displaystyle\frac{m+\tau\ell}{2}\|\Theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\frac{\ell}{2}\|\Theta(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\nabla\Theta(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{m+\tau\ell}{2}\|\Theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\tau m\int_{\Omega}|\Theta_{t}(t)\Theta(t)|\,\textup{d}x+\tau m\int_{\Omega}|\Theta_{1}\Theta_{0}|\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\tau m\|\Theta_{t}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s+\frac{1}{2\ell}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\tau^{2}\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\,\textup{d}s.

Now, using Young’s inequality gives the bound

(4.16)

\displaystyle\frac{m+\tau\ell}{2}\|\Theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\frac{\ell}{2}\|\Theta(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\nabla\Theta(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{m+\tau\ell}{2}\|\Theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\tau m^{2}}{\ell}\|\Theta_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\tau\ell}{4}\|\Theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\tau m}{2}\big(\|\Theta_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\tau m\|\Theta_{t}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s+\frac{1}{2\ell}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\tau^{2}\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\,\textup{d}s.

Clearly, the third term on the right of the above estimate can be absorbed by the first term on the left, so that we obtain

(4.17)

\displaystyle\big(\frac{m}{2}+\frac{\tau\ell}{4}\big)\|\Theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\frac{\ell}{2}\|\Theta(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\nabla\Theta(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{m+\tau(m+\ell)}{2}\big(\|\Theta_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Theta_{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+\frac{\tau m^{2}}{\ell}\|\Theta_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\tau m\|\Theta_{t}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2\ell}\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\tau^{2}\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\,\textup{d}s.

In order to absorb the second and third term on the right-hand side of (
4.17
), we sum up the estimates (
4.11
) and
\frac{\ell}{2m}\times
(
4.17
), then we have for all
t\geq 0

(4.18)

\displaystyle\frac{\ell}{2m}\big(\frac{m}{2}+\frac{\tau\ell}{4}\big)\|\Theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\ell\|\Theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\tau m}{2}\|\Theta_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\nabla\Theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\ell}{2m}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\frac{\ell}{2}\|\Theta(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\nabla\Theta(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)\,\textup{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}(\frac{m}{2}+\frac{\tau\ell}{2})\|\Theta_{t}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta](0)+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\,\textup{d}s.

Recalling the formula for
\mathcal{Q}
in (
1.4
), we have

(4.19)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|(p_{t})^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|p_{t}p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|p_{t}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|p_{t}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\|p_{t}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

where we have used the embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
and the inequalities

(4.20)

\|p_{t}\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\|p_{t}\|_{H^{2}}\lesssim\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}.

Integrating over
(0,t)
, we obtain

(4.21)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\,\textup{d}s\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}E_{1}[p](s)D_{1}[p](s)\,\textup{d}s.

Thus, combining (
4.18
) and (
4.21
), we end up with estimate (
4.8
), thereby concluding the proof of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Next, we obtain some extra estimates for higher-order norms of
\Theta
that will be useful in the sequel.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\tau>0
. Then for all
t\geq 0
, it holds that

(4.22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}[\Theta](t)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{D}_{1}[\Theta](s)\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}[\Theta](0)+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(E_{1}[p]+(E_{1}[p])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(E_{2}[p])^{\frac{d}{4}}\Big)\big(D_{1}[p]+D_{2}[p]\big)\,\textup{d}s.

The hidden constant in (
4.22
) does not depend on
t
.

Proof.

By taking the time derivative of the equation (
2.2
), we can see that the function
\tilde{\Theta}:=\Theta_{t}
satisfies in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))
the same wave equation as
\Theta
with a different source term

(4.23)

\tau m\tilde{\Theta}_{tt}+(m+\tau\ell)\tilde{\Theta}_{t}+\ell\tilde{\Theta}-\kappa_{\textup{a}}\Delta\tilde{\Theta}=\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})+\tau\partial_{t}^{2}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t}).

Thus to establish the bound (
4.22
), we test the equation (
4.23
) by
\tilde{\Theta}_{t},\tilde{\Theta}
and go through exactly the same steps as in the proof of Lemma
4.1
, only the source term changes. This will lead to a similar estimate to (
4.18
) for the function
\tilde{\Theta}

(4.24)

\displaystyle\frac{\ell}{2m}\big(\frac{m}{2}+\frac{\tau\ell}{4}\big)\|\tilde{\Theta}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\ell\|\tilde{\Theta}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\tau m}{2}\|\tilde{\Theta}_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\nabla\tilde{\Theta}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{\ell}{2m}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\frac{\ell}{2}\|\tilde{\Theta}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\nabla\tilde{\Theta}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)\,\textup{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\frac{m}{2}+\frac{\tau\ell}{2}\big)\|\tilde{\Theta}_{t}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}[\tilde{\Theta}](0)+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\,\textup{d}s.

Further, we have by using the definition of
\mathcal{Q}
in (
1.4
)

(4.25)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|p_{t}p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(p_{tt})^{2}+p_{t}p_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|p_{t}p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(p_{tt})^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|p_{t}p_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|p_{t}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\|p_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|p_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

From here, using the interpolation inequalities (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) together with the embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
, elliptic regularity, Poincaré’s inequality and (
4.6
), we get the following bound

(4.26)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}\|p_{tt}\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{d}{2}}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}\|p_{t}\|_{H^{2}}^{\frac{d}{2}}\|p_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(D_{1}[p]+D_{2}[p]\big)\Big(E_{1}[p]+(E_{1}[p])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(E_{2}[p])^{\frac{d}{4}}\Big).

Inserting this bound into (
4.24
) and recalling that
\tilde{\Theta}=\Theta_{t}
which means
\mathcal{E}_{0}[\tilde{\Theta}]=\mathcal{E}_{1}[\Theta]
, we reach (
4.22
); thus completing the proof of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

At this point, we are ready to proceed with the proof of Proposition
4.1
. We remark though, that with the smoothness displayed by the solution
\Theta
, we can only perform the computations below in a formal manner. To get around this, first we deal with more regular solutions, then by a density argument we acquire the same estimates for the solution
\Theta\in X_{\Theta}
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

We test the equation (
2.2
) by
-\Delta\Theta
and we integrate over
\Omega
to find

(4.27)

\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\Delta\Theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\int_{\Omega}(-\tau m\Theta_{tt}-(m+\tau\ell)\Theta_{t}-\ell\Theta+\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})+\tau\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t}))(-\Delta\Theta)\,\textup{d}x.

The right-hand side can be estimated by using Young’s inequality as follows:

(4.28)

\displaystyle\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\Delta\Theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\kappa_{\textup{a}}}\Big(\ell^{2}\|\Theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+(m+\tau\ell)^{2}\|\Theta_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\tau^{2}m^{2}\|\Theta_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\tau^{2}\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\frac{\kappa_{\textup{a}}}{2}\|\Delta\Theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

which together with (
4.8
), (
4.22
) and (
4.21
), implies

(4.29)

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_{\textup{a}}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|\Delta\Theta(s)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\big(\mathcal{D}_{0}[\Theta](s)+\mathcal{D}_{1}[\Theta](s)\big)\,\textup{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta](0)+\mathcal{E}_{1}[\Theta](0)+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(E_{1}[p]+(E_{1}[p])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(E_{2}[p])^{\frac{d}{4}}\Big)\big(D_{1}[p]+D_{2}[p]\big)\,\textup{d}s.

Next, we multiply the bioheat equation (
2.2
) by
-\Delta\Theta_{t}
and integrate in space, using integration by parts yields the identity

(4.30)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\big(\tau m\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+(m+\tau\ell)\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\ell\int_{\Omega}\nabla\Theta\cdot\nabla\Theta_{t}\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla(\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})+\tau\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t}))\cdot\nabla\Theta_{t}.

We note that the available regularity for the local-in-time solution
(p,\Theta)\in X_{p}\times X_{\Theta}
makes the computations leading to the identity (
4.30
) and the estimates below, only formal. However, this could be justified by first establishing the bounds for smoother solutions to the equation (
2.2
), whose existence is provided in
[
10
, Theorem 6, p. 391]
. Hence, by an approximation argument like the one presented in
[
21
, Proposition 2.1]
, we can recover the same estimate (
4.34
) below for the solution
\Theta\in X_{\Theta}
.

Applying Young’s inequality to the right-hand side of (
4.30
), we obtain

(4.31)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\big(\tau m\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+(m+\tau\ell)\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\ell^{2}}{m}\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{m}\|\nabla(\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})+\tau\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t}))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{m}{2}\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The second term on the right-hand side can be bounded as follows

(4.32)

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})+\tau\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t}))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\mathcal{Q}(p_{t}))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla(\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t}))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|2p_{t}\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|2p_{tt}\nabla p_{t}+2p_{t}\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|p_{t}\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|p_{tt}\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|p_{t}\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|p_{t}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\|p_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By using the interpolation inequalities (
3.8
), (
3.9
), the embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
, elliptic regularity and (
4.6
), we can further estimate the right-hand side of the above inequality

(4.33)

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\mathcal{Q}(p_{t})+\tau\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(p_{t}))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{d}{2}}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{d}{2}}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big(E_{1}[p]+E_{1}[p]^{1-\frac{d}{4}}E_{2}[p]^{\frac{d}{4}}\Big)D_{1}[p].

Plugging (
4.33
) into (
4.31
) and integrating in time, we find

(4.34)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big(\tau m\|\nabla\Theta_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\textup{a}}\|\Delta\Theta(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+(\frac{m}{2}+\tau\ell)\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\Theta_{t}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}[\Theta](0)+\frac{\ell^{2}}{m}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\Theta(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\textup{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(E_{1}[p]+(E_{1}[p])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(E_{2}[p])^{\frac{d}{4}}\Big)D_{1}[p]\,\textup{d}s.

Now we add
\lambda\times
(
4.34
) to (
4.8
) where we suitably select
\lambda>0
in order to absorb the second term on the right of (
4.34
) by the left-hand side of (
4.8
). Then we sum up the resulting estimate, (
4.22
) and (
4.29
). This leads to the estimate (
4.7
) for the total energy
\mathcal{E}[\Theta]
, which ends the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
∎

4.3.
The acoustic pressure equation

Our focus in this section is to prove some uniform in time energy estimates for the acoustic pressure. Relying on the smoothness exhibited by the local-in-time solution
(p,\Theta)\in X_{p}\times X_{\Theta}
and assuming (
4.6
), we are able to show the estimate stated in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.2
.

The local-in-time solution to (
2.6
)
(p,\Theta)
satisfies the estimate

(4.35)

\displaystyle E_{1}[p](t)+E_{2}[p](t)+\int_{0}^{t}(D_{1}[p](s)+D_{2}[p](s))\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E_{1}[p](0)+E_{2}[p](0)+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{2+\gamma_{2}}+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\big(E_{1}[p]+(E_{1}[p])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(E_{2}[p])^{\frac{d}{4}}+\big(\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta]\big)^{1-\frac{d}{4}}\big(\mathcal{E}[\Theta]\big)^{\frac{d}{4}}\big)\big(D_{1}[p]+D_{2}[p]+\mathcal{D}[\Theta]\big)\,\textup{d}s

independently of

.

The assertion in Proposition
4.2
will follow by putting together the results provided in the two upcoming lemmas, where we prove a priori bounds for
E_{1}[p]
and
E_{2}[p]
separately. The first of which is attained by by using the multipliers
p_{tt},-\Delta p
and
-\Delta p_{t}
. More precisely, we have the following estimate.

Lemma 4.3
.

For all
t\geq 0
, it holds that

(4.36)

\displaystyle E_{1}[p](t)+\int_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle D_{1}[p](s)\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim E_{1}[p](0)+\int_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle\Big(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{2+\gamma_{2}}+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\Big(E_{1}[p](s)+\big(\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta]\big)^{1-\frac{d}{4}}\big(\mathcal{E}[\Theta]\big)^{\frac{d}{4}}\Big)D_{1}[p](s)\,\textup{d}s

where the hidden constant above does not depend on
t
.

Proof.

We differentiate with respect to time the equation (
2.4
) to get

(4.37)

\displaystyle(1-2k(\Theta)p)p_{ttt}-h(0)\Delta p_{t}-b\Delta p_{tt}=

\displaystyle 2k^{\prime}(\Theta)\Theta_{t}((p_{t})^{2}+pp_{tt})+6k(\Theta)p_{t}p_{tt}

\displaystyle+\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)\Theta_{t}\Delta p+\tilde{h}(\Theta)\Delta p_{t}.

We multiply (
4.37
) by
p_{tt}
and integrate over
\Omega
, using integration by parts leads to the identity

(4.38)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\|\sqrt{1-2k(\Theta)p}p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{h(0)}\nabla p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\Big)+b\|\nabla p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(2k^{\prime}(\Theta)\Theta_{t}(p_{t})^{2}p_{tt}+k^{\prime}(\Theta)\Theta_{t}p(p_{tt})^{2}+5k(\Theta)p_{t}(p_{tt})^{2}\Big)\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\Big(\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)\Theta_{t}\Delta pp_{tt}\,\textup{d}x-\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)\nabla\Theta\cdot\nabla p_{t}p_{tt}-\tilde{h}(\Theta)\nabla p_{t}\cdot\nabla p_{tt}\Big)\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle R_{1}+R_{2}.

First we derive a bound for
R_{1}

(4.39)

R_{1}=\int_{\Omega}\big(2k^{\prime}(\Theta)\Theta_{t}(p_{t})^{2}p_{tt}+k^{\prime}(\Theta)\Theta_{t}p(p_{tt})^{2}+5k(\Theta)p_{t}(p_{tt})^{2}\big)\,\textup{d}x.

For this, we apply Hölder’s inequality to find

(4.40)

\displaystyle|R_{1}|\leq

\displaystyle 2\|k^{\prime}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{4}}\|p_{t}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{4}}+\|k^{\prime}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{4}}\|p\|_{L^{\infty}}\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{4}}

\displaystyle+5\|k(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|p_{t}\|_{L^{4}}\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{4}}.

Using the embeddings
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega),H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, Young and Poincaré inequalities, elliptic regularity and (
K2
), it follows that

(4.41)

\displaystyle|R_{1}|\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)\Big[(1+\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\gamma_{2}})^{2}\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big(\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+k_{1}\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big]+\varepsilon\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{2+\gamma_{2}}\Big)E_{1}[p]D_{1}[p]+\varepsilon\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Similarly, we can get an estimate for
R_{2}

(4.42)

R_{2}=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)\Theta_{t}\Delta pp_{tt}\,\textup{d}x-\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)\nabla\Theta\cdot\nabla p_{t}p_{tt}-\tilde{h}(\Theta)\nabla p_{t}\cdot\nabla p_{tt}\Big)\,\textup{d}x.

Applying Hölder’s inequality yields

(4.43)

\displaystyle|R_{2}|\leq

\displaystyle\|\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{4}}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{4}}+\|\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{4}}

\displaystyle+\|\tilde{h}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}.

Recall that

(4.44)

\tilde{h}(\Theta)=\int_{0}^{\Theta}h^{\prime}(s)\,\textup{d}s,

then we have the bound

(4.45)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{h}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|h^{\prime}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}}(1+\|\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1+\gamma_{1}})

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}(1+\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\gamma_{1}})

which results from (
H3
) and elliptic regularity. Furthermore, since
h^{\prime}(\Theta)=\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)
, the inequality (
H3
) gives

(4.46)

\|\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 1+\|\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1+\gamma_{1}}.

Hence, the above estimates along with Young’s inequality yield

(4.47)

\displaystyle|R_{2}|\leq C(\varepsilon)(1+\|\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1+\gamma_{1}})^{2}\Big(

\displaystyle\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\varepsilon\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Moreover, the interpolation inequalities (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) together with the embedding
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and elliptic regularity leads to

(4.48)

\displaystyle|R_{2}|\lesssim(1+\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2+2\gamma_{1}})\Big(

\displaystyle\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{d}{2}}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{d}{2}}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{d}{2}}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\varepsilon\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Observe that we have also used the following inequality to reach the estimate above

(4.49)

\|\nabla\Theta\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|\Theta\|_{H^{2}}\lesssim\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}.

Then, it follows that

(4.50)

\displaystyle|R_{2}|\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{1}})\big(\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta]\big)^{1-\frac{d}{4}}\big(\mathcal{E}[\Theta]\big)^{\frac{d}{4}}D_{1}[p]+\varepsilon\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By putting together the bounds for
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
in (
4.41
) and (
4.50
) respectively, then selecting
\varepsilon
suitably small, we conclude

(4.51)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\|\sqrt{1-2k(\Theta)p}p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{h(0)}\nabla p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\Big)+\frac{b}{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{2+\gamma_{2}}+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{1}}\Big)\Big(E_{1}[p]+\big(\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta]\big)^{1-\frac{d}{4}}\big(\mathcal{E}[\Theta]\big)^{\frac{d}{4}}\Big)D_{1}[p].

Next, we test the equation (
2.4
) by
-\Delta p
and we integrate in space; thus we get the identity

(4.52)

\displaystyle\frac{b}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+h(0)\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big(p_{tt}-2k(\Theta)pp_{tt}-2k(\Theta)(p_{t})^{2}-\tilde{h}(\Theta)\Delta p\big)\Delta p\,\textup{d}x.

We estimate the right-hand side of the identity above using Hölder’s inequality to obtain

(4.53)

\displaystyle\frac{b}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+h(0)\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}+2\|k(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|p\|_{L^{4}}\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{4}}+2\|k(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|p_{t}\|_{L^{4}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\tilde{h}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}.

Thanks to the bound (
4.45
), the embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
, Poincaré and Young inequalities and (
K2
), we obtain

(4.54)

\displaystyle\frac{b}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}

\displaystyle\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+h(0)\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon)\Big(

\displaystyle\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+k_{1}^{2}\|\nabla p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+k_{1}^{2}\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}

\displaystyle+(1+\|\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1+\gamma_{1}})^{2}\|\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\varepsilon\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

From here, by appealing to the interpolation inequality (
3.9
) and elliptic regularity, and selecting
\varepsilon
small enough, it results

(4.55)

\displaystyle\frac{b}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{h(0)}{2}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+(1+\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2+2\gamma_{1}})\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{d}{2}}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{1}})\Big(E_{1}[p]+\big(\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta]\big)^{1-\frac{d}{4}}\big(\mathcal{E}[\Theta]\big)^{\frac{d}{4}}\Big)D_{1}[p].

Now, we add up
\lambda\times
(
4.55
) to (
4.51
), where
\lambda
is suitably chosen to absorb the first term on the right-hand side of (
4.55
) by the left-hand side of (
4.51
), we obtain the bound

(4.56)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\|\sqrt{1-2k(\Theta)p}p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{h(0)}\nabla p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+b\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{b}{4}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{h(0)}{2}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{2+\gamma_{2}}+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{1}}\Big)\Big(E_{1}[p]+\big(\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta]\big)^{1-\frac{d}{4}}\big(\mathcal{E}[\Theta]\big)^{\frac{d}{4}}\Big)D_{1}[p].

In the last step of the proof, we multiply the equation (
2.4
) by
-\Delta p_{t}
and we integrate over
\Omega
, to find

(4.57)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+h(0)\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+b\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big(-2k(\Theta)pp_{tt}-2k(\Theta)(p_{t})^{2}-\tilde{h}(\Theta)\Delta p\big)\Delta p_{t}\,\textup{d}x.

Then, we have by using Hölder’s inequality together with Young’s inequality

(4.58)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+h(0)\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+b\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(2\|k(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|p\|_{L^{4}}\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{4}}+2\|k(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{4}}+\|\tilde{h}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(k_{1}^{2}\|\nabla p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+k_{1}^{2}\|\nabla p_{t}\|^{4}_{L^{2}}+\|\tilde{h}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\frac{b}{2}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where we have made use of (
K2
) and the embedding
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
. Moreover, recalling (
4.45
) and using Ladyzhenskaya’s inequality (
3.8
), we obtain

(4.59)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+h(0)\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\frac{b}{2}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\|\Delta p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+(1+\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2+2\gamma_{1}})\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{d}{2}}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{1}}\Big)\Big(E_{1}[p]+\big(\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta]\big)^{1-\frac{d}{4}}\big(\mathcal{E}[\Theta]\big)^{\frac{d}{4}}\Big)D_{1}[p].

Finally, adding up the bounds (
4.59
) and (
4.56
), then integrating in time the resulting estimate, (
4.36
) folllows. This completes the proof of Lemma
4.3
.

By inspecting the already-established estimates, it becomes clear that the higher-order energy
E_{2}[p]
is needed to bound some nonlinear terms. Consequently, in the subsequent lemma, our focus will be on deriving an estimate for
E_{2}[p]
.
For this, we will differentiate the pressure equation in space as well as in time and use suitable multipliers.

Lemma 4.4
.

For all
t\geq 0
, we have

(4.60)

\displaystyle E_{2}[p](t)+\int_{0}^{t}D_{2}[p](s)\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E_{2}[p](0)+\int_{0}^{t}D_{1}[p](s)\,\textup{d}s

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\big(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{2+\gamma_{2}}+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{1}}\big)

\displaystyle\times\big(E_{1}[p]+(E_{1}[p])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(E_{2}[p])^{\frac{d}{4}}+(\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(\mathcal{E}[\Theta])^{\frac{d}{4}}\big)\big(D_{1}[p]+D_{2}[p]+\mathcal{D}[\Theta]\big)\,\textup{d}s,

where the hidden constant is independent of
t
.

Proof.

First, we apply the gradient to the pressure equation (
2.4
),

(4.61)

\displaystyle\nabla p_{tt}-h(0)\nabla\Delta p-\tilde{h}(\Theta)\nabla\Delta p-b\nabla\Delta p_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2k(\Theta)(p\nabla p_{tt}+p_{tt}\nabla p+2p_{t}\nabla p_{t})+2k^{\prime}(\Theta)\nabla\Theta(pp_{tt}+(p_{t})^{2})+\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)\nabla\Theta\Delta p,

multiply by
-\nabla\Delta p
and integrate over
\Omega
to obtain

(4.62)

\displaystyle\frac{b}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\|\nabla\Delta p\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\sqrt{h(0)}\nabla\Delta p\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\big\|\sqrt{\tilde{h}(\Theta)}\nabla\Delta p\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla p_{tt}\cdot\nabla\Delta p\,\textup{d}x-2\int_{\Omega}k(\Theta)(p\nabla p_{tt}+p_{tt}\nabla p+2p_{t}\nabla p_{t})\cdot\nabla\Delta p\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Omega}k^{\prime}(\Theta)(pp_{tt}+(p_{t})^{2})\nabla\Theta\cdot\nabla\Delta p\,\textup{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)\Delta p\nabla\Theta\cdot\nabla\Delta p\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla p_{tt}\cdot\nabla\Delta p\,\textup{d}x+R_{11}+R_{12}+R_{13}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{5}{4h(0)}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{5}\|\sqrt{h(0)}\nabla\Delta p\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|R_{11}|+|R_{12}|+|R_{13}|.

Our next goal is to estimate the terms
R_{1j},\,j=1,2,3
on the right-hand side of (
4.62
).

The integral
R_{11}
can be estimated as follows

(4.63)

\displaystyle|R_{11}|\leq 2\|k(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big(\|p\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}+\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla p\|_{L^{4}}+2\|p_{t}\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{4}}\Big)\|\nabla\Delta p\|_{L^{2}},

which becomes after using Young’s inequality, the embeddings
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
,
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, elliptic regularity and (
K2
)

(4.64)

\displaystyle|R_{11}|\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)\big(\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+\varepsilon\|\nabla\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle E_{1}[p]D_{1}[p]+\varepsilon\|\nabla\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Likewise, we can treat the integral
R_{12}
as

(4.65)

\displaystyle|R_{12}|\leq 2\|k^{\prime}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\big(\|p\|_{L^{\infty}}\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{4}}+\|p_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|p_{t}\|_{L^{4}})\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}.

Once again, thanks to the embeddings
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
,
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and (
K2
), we infer that

(4.66)

\displaystyle|R_{12}|\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)(1+\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\gamma_{2}})^{2}\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big(\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\|\nabla\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{2}}\big)\mathcal{E}[\Theta]E_{1}[p]D_{1}[p]+\varepsilon\|\nabla\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The last integral
R_{13}
in (
4.62
) can be handled in a similar way. We have

(4.67)

\displaystyle|R_{13}|\leq

\displaystyle\|\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)\|\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\nabla\Theta\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\nabla\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\gamma_{1}})^{2}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}\|\Delta p\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{d}{2}}\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|\nabla\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

where we have exploited the same ideas as above along with the fact that
h^{\prime}(\Theta)=\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)
, (
H3
) and the interpolation inequality (
3.8
). Further, thanks to the inequality

(4.68)

(a+b)^{\nu}\leq\max\{1,2^{\nu}\}(a^{\nu}+b^{\nu}),\quad a,b\geq 0,\ \nu>0

we have

(4.69)

\displaystyle\|\Delta p\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{d}{2}}=

\displaystyle(\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\Delta p\|_{L^{2}})^{\frac{d}{2}}\lesssim\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{d}{2}}+\|\nabla\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{d}{2}}

and by plugging (
4.69
) into (

), we deduce

(4.70)

\displaystyle|R_{13}|\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{1}}\big)\big(E_{1}[p]+(E_{1}[p])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(E_{2}[p])^{\frac{d}{4}}\big)\mathcal{D}[\Theta]+\varepsilon\|\nabla\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Incorporating the bounds (
4.64
), (
4.66
) and (
4.70
) into (
4.62
) and taking
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, it follows that

(4.71)

\displaystyle\frac{b}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\|\nabla\Delta p\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{h(0)}\nabla\Delta p\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\big\|\sqrt{\tilde{h}(\Theta)}\nabla\Delta p\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\big(

\displaystyle 1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{2+\gamma_{2}}+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{1}}\big)

\displaystyle\times\big(E_{1}[p]+(E_{1}[p])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(E_{2}[p])^{\frac{d}{4}}\big)\big(D_{1}[p]+\mathcal{D}[\Theta]\big).

In the next step, we test the equation (
4.37
) by
p_{ttt}
and we integrate in space, we arrive at the identity

(4.72)

\displaystyle\frac{b}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|p_{ttt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}h(0)\Delta p_{t}p_{ttt}\,\textup{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\Big(6k(\Theta)p_{t}p_{tt}+2k^{\prime}(\Theta)\Theta_{t}((p_{t})^{2}+pp_{tt})+2k(\Theta)pp_{ttt}\Big)p_{ttt}\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\Big(\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)\Theta_{t}\Delta p+\tilde{h}(\Theta)\Delta p_{t}\Big)p_{ttt}\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}h(0)\Delta p_{t}p_{ttt}\,\textup{d}x+R_{21}+R_{22}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)(h(0))^{2}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|p_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|R_{21}|+|R_{22}|.

We need upper bounds for the integrals
R_{21},R_{22}
. For an estimate of
R_{21}
, we begin by applying Hölder’s inequality to get

(4.73)

\displaystyle|R_{21}|\leq

\displaystyle\Big(6\|k(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|p_{t}\|_{L^{4}}\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{4}}+2\|k(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|p\|_{L^{\infty}}\|p_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+2\|k^{\prime}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{4}}\big(\|p_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|p_{t}\|_{L^{4}}+\|p\|_{L^{\infty}}\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{4}}\big)\Big)\|p_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}}.

By taking advantage of the properties of the function
k

H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
,
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we find

(4.74)

\displaystyle|R_{21}|\lesssim

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)\Big(\,k_{1}^{2}\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+k_{1}^{2}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|p_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+(1+\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\gamma_{2}})^{2}\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big(\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\Big)+\varepsilon\|p_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

This clearly implies

(4.75)

\displaystyle|R_{21}|\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{2+\gamma_{2}}\big)E_{1}[p]\big(D_{1}[p]+D_{2}[p]\big)+\varepsilon\|p_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We derive a bound for
R_{22}
by relying on the same tools. Indeed, we have

(4.76)

\displaystyle|R_{22}|\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\|\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{4}}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{4}}+\|\tilde{h}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\|p_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}},

which combined with (
4.45
), the fact that
h^{\prime}(\Theta)=\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)
and (
H3
) yields

(4.77)

\displaystyle|R_{22}|\lesssim

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)(1+\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\gamma_{1}})^{2}\Big(\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{4}}^{2}+\|\Theta\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\varepsilon\|p_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Moreover, an application of the interpolation inequalities (
3.8
), (
3.9
) gives

(4.78)

\displaystyle|R_{22}|\lesssim

\displaystyle C(\varepsilon)(1+\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2+2\gamma_{1}})\Big(\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}\|\Delta p\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{d}{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2}}\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{d}{2}}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\varepsilon\|p_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Exploiting the estimate (
4.69
), it results

(4.79)

\displaystyle|R_{22}|\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{1}}\big)\big(E_{1}[p]+(E_{1}[p])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(E_{2}[p])^{\frac{d}{4}}+(\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(\mathcal{E}[\Theta])^{\frac{d}{4}}\big)

\displaystyle\times\big(D_{1}[p]+\mathcal{D}[\Theta]\big)+\varepsilon\|p_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Inserting the estimates (
4.75
), (
4.79
) into (
4.72
), keeping in mind (
4.6
), for small enough
\varepsilon
, we get

(4.80)

\displaystyle\frac{b}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\|p_{ttt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Big(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{1}}+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{2+\gamma_{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\Big(E_{1}[p]+(E_{1}[p])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(E_{2}[p])^{\frac{d}{4}}+(\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(\mathcal{E}[\Theta])^{\frac{d}{4}}\Big)\big(D_{1}[p]+D_{2}[p]+\mathcal{D}[\Theta]\big).

In order to obtain an estimate for the energy
E_{3}[p]
, we next multiply the times-differentiated equation (
4.37
) by
-\Delta p_{tt}
, integrate in space and use integration by parts to get

(4.81)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+h(0)\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+b\|\Delta p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\big(2k(\Theta)pp_{ttt}+2k^{\prime}(\Theta)\Theta_{t}((p_{t})^{2}+pp_{tt})+6k(\Theta)p_{t}p_{tt}\big)\Delta p_{tt}\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\big(\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)\Theta_{t}\Delta p+\tilde{h}(\Theta)\Delta p_{t}\big)\Delta p_{tt}\,\textup{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{31}+R_{32}.

First, we estimate
R_{31}
as follows:

(4.82)

\displaystyle|R_{31}|\leq

\displaystyle\|k(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big(2\|p\|_{L^{\infty}}\|p_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}}+6\|p_{t}\|_{L^{4}}\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{4}}\Big)\|\Delta p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+2\|k^{\prime}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{4}}\Big(\|p_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|p_{t}\|_{L^{4}}+\|p\|_{L^{\infty}}\|p_{tt}\|_{L^{4}}\Big)\|\Delta p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}.

Further, appealing to the embeddings
H^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\Omega)
,
H^{2}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and (
K2
), we find

(4.83)

\displaystyle|R_{31}|\lesssim

\displaystyle k_{1}\Big(\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}\|p_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\|\Delta p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+(1+\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\gamma_{2}})\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\Big(\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\|\Delta p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}.

Applying Young’s inequality then yields

(4.84)

\displaystyle|R_{31}|\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|p_{ttt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+(1+\|\Delta\Theta\|_{L^{2}}^{1+\gamma_{2}})^{2}\|\nabla\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big(\|\nabla p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta p\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\|\Delta p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{2+\gamma_{2}}\big)E_{1}[p]\big(D_{1}[p]+D_{2}[p]\big)+\varepsilon\|\Delta p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

As for the integral
R_{32}
, we have

(4.85)

\displaystyle|R_{32}|\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\|\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Theta_{t}\|_{L^{4}}\|\Delta p\|_{L^{4}}+\|\tilde{h}(\Theta)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\|\Delta p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}.

Obviously estimating
R_{32}
does not differ much from estimating
R_{22}
, which means that we can follow the same strategy to reach the bound

(4.86)

\displaystyle|R_{32}|\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{1}}\big)\big(E_{1}[p]+(E_{1}[p])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(E_{2}[p])^{\frac{d}{4}}+(\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(\mathcal{E}[\Theta])^{\frac{d}{4}}\big)

\displaystyle\times\big(D_{1}[p]+\mathcal{D}[\Theta]\big)+\varepsilon\|\Delta p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Plugging (
4.84
), (
4.86
) into (
4.81
) and choosing
\varepsilon
small enough, we conclude

(4.87)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}\Big(\|\nabla p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+h(0)\|\Delta p_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\frac{b}{2}\|\Delta p_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{2+\gamma_{2}}+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{1}}\big)

\displaystyle\times\big(E_{1}[p]+(E_{1}[p])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(E_{2}[p])^{\frac{d}{4}}+(\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(\mathcal{E}[\Theta])^{\frac{d}{4}}\big)\big(D_{1}[p]+D_{2}[p]+\mathcal{D}[\Theta]\big).

Lastly, collecting the estimates (
4.71
), (
4.80
) and (
4.87
), we obtain

(4.88)

\displaystyle\frac{\textup{d}}{\textup{d}t}E_{2}[p](t)+D_{2}[p]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle D_{1}[p]+\,\big(1+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{2+\gamma_{2}}+\mathcal{E}[\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{1}}\big)

\displaystyle\times\big(E_{1}[p]+(E_{1}[p])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(E_{2}[p])^{\frac{d}{4}}+(\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(\mathcal{E}[\Theta])^{\frac{d}{4}}\big)\big(D_{1}[p]+D_{2}[p]+\mathcal{D}[\Theta]\big),

which after integration in time results in the bound (
4.60
). This ends the proof of Lemma
4.4
.
∎

5.
Global well-posedness and exponential decay

The main goal in this section is to prove Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
.
To show global well-posedness for small initial data with smallness imposed only on a lower-order norm, see e.g.,
[
20
,
4
]
, we define the combined lower-order energy, which is the energy norm that we assume to be small:

(5.1a)

\mathsf{E_{low}}[p,\Theta](t):=E_{1}[p](t)+\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta](t),

and the higher energy norm, which indicates the regularity needed to prove the main result

(5.1b)

\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta](t):=E_{1}[p](t)+E_{2}[p](t)+\mathcal{E}[\Theta](t).

The associated dissipation rates are given respectively as

(5.1c)

\displaystyle\mathsf{D_{low}}[p,\Theta](t)

\displaystyle:=D_{1}[p](t)+\mathcal{D}_{0}[\Theta](t),\vskip 5.69046pt

\displaystyle\mathsf{D_{high}}[p,\Theta](t)

\displaystyle:=D_{1}[p](t)+D_{2}[p](t)+\mathcal{D}[\Theta](t).

5.1.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

The proof follows from the local existence, uniform-in-time a priori estimates, and a continuity argument. We define

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{low}}(t)=

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\mathsf{E_{low}}[p,\Theta](s),\qquad\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{high}}(t)=\,\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta](s),

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{low}}(t)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\mathsf{D_{low}}[p,\Theta](s)\,\textup{d}s,\qquad\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{high}}(t)=\,\int_{0}^{t}\mathsf{D_{high}}[p,\Theta](s)\,\textup{d}s,\qquad t\geq 0.

According to what we have proven in the previous section, we can assemble the estimates on
E_{1}[p]
and
\mathcal{E}_{0}[\Theta]
to yield a uniform bound for
\mathsf{E_{low}}[p,\Theta]
for all
0\leq s\leq t

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathsf{E_{low}}[p,\Theta](s)+\int_{0}^{s}

\displaystyle\mathsf{D_{low}}[p,\Theta](r)\,\textup{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\Big\{\mathsf{E_{low}}[p,\Theta](0)+\int_{0}^{s}

\displaystyle\Big(1+\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta]+\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta]^{2+\gamma_{2}}+\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\Big(\mathsf{E_{low}}[p,\Theta]+(\mathsf{E_{low}}[p,\Theta])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta])^{\frac{d}{4}}\Big)\mathsf{D_{low}}[p,\Theta](r)\,\textup{d}r\Big\},

which in turn implies

(5.4)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{low}}(t)+\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{low}}(t)\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\mathsf{E_{low}}[p,\Theta](0)

\displaystyle+C_{1}\Big(1+\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{high}}(t)+(\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{high}}(t))^{2+\gamma_{2}}+(\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{high}}(t))^{1+\gamma_{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\,\Big(\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{low}}(t)+(\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{low}}(t))^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{high}}(t))^{\frac{d}{4}}\Big)\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{low}}(t).

In addition, by adding (
4.7
) and (
4.35
), we get the following estimate for the higher-order energy
\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta]
for
0\leq s\leq t

(5.5)

\displaystyle\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta](s)+\int_{0}^{s}

\displaystyle\mathsf{D_{high}}[p,\Theta](r)\,\textup{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\Big\{\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta](0)+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\Big(1+\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta]+\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta]^{2+\gamma_{2}}+\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta]^{1+\gamma_{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\Big(\mathsf{E_{low}}[p,\Theta]+(\mathsf{E_{low}}[p,\Theta])^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta])^{\frac{d}{4}}\Big)\mathsf{D_{high}}[p,\Theta](r)\,\textup{d}r\Big\}.

This also means the following bound for
\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{high}}(t)

(5.6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{high}}(t)+\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{high}}(t)\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta](0)

\displaystyle+C_{2}\Big(1+\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{high}}(t)+(\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{high}}(t))^{2+\gamma_{2}}+(\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{high}}(t))^{1+\gamma_{1}}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\,\Big(\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{low}}(t)+(\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{low}}(t))^{1-\frac{d}{4}}(\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{high}}(t))^{\frac{d}{4}}\Big)\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{high}}(t).

Let us make the following a priori assumption:

(5.7)

\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{low}}(t)\leq\eta,\qquad\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{high}}(t)\leq M,\qquad 2\|k(\Theta)p\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq\mathsf{m}<1

for constants
\eta,\,M,\mathsf{m}>0
with
\eta
and
\mathsf{m}
small enough. Then, estimate (
5.4
) implies

(5.8)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{low}}(t)+\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{low}}(t)\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\mathsf{E_{low}}[p,\Theta](0)

\displaystyle+C_{1}(1+M+M^{2+\gamma_{2}}+M^{1+\gamma_{1}})(\eta+\eta^{1-\frac{d}{4}}M^{\frac{d}{4}})\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{low}}(t).

Similarly, from (
5.6
), we have

(5.9)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{high}}(t)+\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{high}}(t)\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta](0)

\displaystyle+C_{2}(1+M+M^{2+\gamma_{2}}+M^{1+\gamma_{1}})(\eta+\eta^{1-\frac{d}{4}}M^{\frac{d}{4}})\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{high}}(t),

which gives if
\eta
and
\mathsf{m}
are small enough

(5.10)

\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{low}}(t)+\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{low}}(t)\leq\,\tilde{C}_{1}\mathsf{E_{low}}[p,\Theta](0),

and

(5.11)

\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{high}}(t)+\mathbf{D}_{\mathsf{high}}(t)\leq\tilde{C}_{2}\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta](0).

Furthermore, we have by the Sobolev embedding

(5.12)

\|k(\Theta)p\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq k_{1}\|p\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\lesssim\mathbf{E}_{\mathsf{low}}(t)\lesssim\mathsf{E_{low}}[p,\Theta](0).

Therefore, in the standard way, see e.g.
[
31
]
and
[
20
, Section 4]
, as long as
\mathsf{E_{low}}[p,\Theta](0)
is sufficiently small and
\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta](0)
is bounded, say; by
M_{0}
with
M_{0}\leq\frac{M}{2\tilde{C}_{2}}
, then the above uniform a priori estimates obtained under the assumption (
5.7
) imply the global existence of the solution. This completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

5.2.
Proof of Theorem
3.2

Let
(p,\Theta)
be the global solution whose existence is provided by Theorem
3.1
. Going back to (
5.5
) and integrating from
s
to
t
in all the estimates leading to (
5.5
), we have, by taking account of (
5.7
), the bound

(5.13)

\displaystyle\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta](t)+\int_{s}^{t}

\displaystyle\mathsf{D_{high}}[p,\Theta](r)\,\textup{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta](s)+

\displaystyle C_{2}(1+M+M^{2+\gamma_{2}}+M^{1+\gamma_{1}})(\eta+\eta^{1-\frac{d}{4}}M^{\frac{d}{4}})\int_{s}^{t}\mathsf{D_{high}}[p,\Theta](r)\,\textup{d}r.

We can take
\eta
sufficiently small so that

(5.14)

2C_{2}(1+M+M^{2+\gamma_{2}}+M^{1+\gamma_{1}})(\eta+\eta^{1-\frac{d}{4}}M^{\frac{d}{4}})\leq 1

is satisfied. Thus, the estimate (
5.13
) becomes

(5.15)

\displaystyle\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta](t)+\int_{s}^{t}

\displaystyle\mathsf{D_{high}}[p,\Theta](r)\,\textup{d}r\lesssim\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta](s).

On the other hand, it is clear from the definition of
\mathcal{E}[\Theta]
and
\mathcal{D}[\Theta]
that (cf. (
4.1a
), (
4.1b
) and (
4.2
))

(5.16)

\mathcal{E}[\Theta](t)\lesssim\mathcal{D}[\Theta](t),\qquad t\geq 0.

Moreover, we have by using Poincaré’s inequality and elliptic regularity

(5.17)

\|p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\nabla p_{tt}\|^{2}_{L^{2}},\qquad\quad\|\nabla p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\Delta p_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

then these inequalities together with the last bound in (
5.7
) yield

(5.18)

E_{1}[p](t)+E_{2}[p](t)\lesssim D_{1}[p](t)+D_{2}[p](t),\qquad t\geq 0.

Combining (
5.16
) and (
5.18
) and recalling (
5.1b
) and (
5.1c
), we obtain

(5.19)

\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta](t)\lesssim\mathsf{D_{high}}[p,\Theta](t).

Inserting the above inequality in (
5.15
), it follows

(5.20)

\displaystyle\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta](t)+\int_{s}^{t}\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta](s)\,\textup{d}r\lesssim\mathsf{E_{high}}[p,\Theta](s),\quad 0\leq s<t,

thereby we deduce that the energy decays exponentially fast by applying Lemma
3.1
.

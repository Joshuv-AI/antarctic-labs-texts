Global well-posedness of a two dimensional wave-Klein-Gordon system with small non-compactly supported data
Xinyu Cheng

Abstract
. In this paper we are interested in the coupled wave and Klein-Gordon equations in ℝ + × ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2} . We want to establish the global well-posedness of such system by showing the uniform boundedness of the energy for the global solution without any compactness assumptions on the initial data. In addition, we also demonstrate the pointwise asymptotic behavior of the solution pair. In order to achieve that we apply a modified Alinhac’s ghost weight method together with a newly developed normal-form framework to remedy the lack of the space-time scaling vector field. Finally we show the global solutions scatter linearly strongly for the Klein-Gordon field ϕ \phi and weakly for the wave field n n as t → + ∞ t\to+\infty . To our best knowledge such scattering phenomenon is novel in the literature.



1.
introduction

1.1.
Introduction to the model and main results

In this work we are interested in certain coupled wave-Klein-Gordon system (wKG) in
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
that results from the study of differential geometry and plasma physics. More specifically speaking, we first focus on the following two model systems:

(1.1a)

\begin{cases}&\Box n=A^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}(|\phi|^{2}),\\
&\Box\phi+\phi=B^{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\alpha}n;\end{cases}

(1.1b)

\begin{cases}&\Box n=A^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha\beta}(|\phi|^{2}),\\
&\Box\phi+\phi=Bn\phi.\end{cases}

Here the initial data is given by
(\phi,\partial_{t}\phi,n,\partial_{t}n)|_{t=0}=(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1},N_{0},N_{1}),

\square=\partial_{tt}-\Delta
is the standard d’Alembertian and
\Delta
is the usual Laplacian. We denote
\partial=(\partial_{t},\partial_{1},\partial_{2})
, see Section
2.1
for more information on the notation. The Einstein convention is adopted with the Greek letters running from 0 to 2 and Latin letters running from 1 to 2;
A^{\alpha},B^{\alpha},A^{\alpha\beta},B
are given constants. We emphasize here that the constants
A^{\alpha},B^{\alpha},A^{\alpha\beta}
need not obey any null conditions. The unknowns
\phi,n
take values in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathbb{R}
respectively.

The impetus behind this coupled wKG system (
1.1
) stems from the integration of two research areas, differential geometry and plasma physics, each offering unique perspectives and insights from different vantage points. The geometric problem concerns the stability of a totally geodesic wave map (cf.
[
1
,
13
]
) from Minkowski space
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
to a
n-
dimensional manifold
\mathcal{M}^{n}
. To be more explicit, in
[
1
]
the following system was formulated:

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Box\phi^{1}=-2\sum_{k=2}^{n}\phi^{k}\partial_{\alpha}\phi^{k}+\text{h.o.t}\\
&-\Box\phi^{k}-\phi^{k}=2\phi^{k}\partial_{\alpha}\phi^{1}+\text{h.o.t.},\quad k=2,\cdots,n\end{aligned}\right.

where
\phi^{1}
and
\phi^{k}
are scalar functions defined in
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Based on this formulation, global stability results on the wave maps in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and higher dimensions have been established in
[
1
]
(
d\geq 3
), while the 2 dimensional case
(d=2)
was suggested to be open due to its rather slow decay-in-time. Later
[
13
]
gave a preliminary answer to the 2 dimensional case, where a vector field method on hyperboloids
dating back to Klainerman and Hörmander was adopted to show the global well-posedness of (
1.1
) with small compactly supported initial data. In this article, one of our modest goals is to adopt the usual vector field bootstrap method (armed with the newly-developed normal form strategy for nonlinear wave systems in
[
28
]
) and eventually remove the compact support requirement. For more discussions on wave maps, we refer the readers to
[
39
,
23
,
45
]
.

The significance of such wave map models (
1.2
) extends beyond its immediate context of differential geometry, finding relevance in both the fields of general relativity and plasma physics (the nonlinear
\sigma
-model including the Skyrme model
[
42
,
29
]
for example), where it describes the interaction between Langmuir waves and ion acoustic
waves in plasma via ion density fluctuation and the electric field (cf.
[
7
]
). To be clearer, the global well-posedness of (
1.2
) is profoundly related to the global stability problem of the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov system:

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Box E^{a}+E^{a}=-nE^{a},\quad a=1,2,\\
&\Box n=\Delta\big(|E^{1}|^{2}+|E^{2}|^{2}\big),\end{aligned}\right.

where the ion density
n
is a scalar function and the electronic field
E=(E^{1},E^{2})
take values in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. (
1.3
) can be dated back to the original Zakharov equation that was firstly introduced in
[
49
]
to describe certain type of oscillation of a plasma. To be accurate, it is known from the physical point of view that for strong Langmuir turbulence, the Langmuir phase velocity in the
Klein-Gordon equation in (
1.3
) is different (usually about one thousand times as large, cf.
[
35
,
7
]
) from the
ion acoustic phase velocity in the wave equation. Such phenomenon results from the classic two-fluid, two-timescale theory, taking the huge difference between the mass of an electron and that of an ion into account. In fact there is no scaling transformation that can make these two speeds
equal (unlike the original Zakharov system as in
[
49
]
). Despite all this, such Klein-Gordon-Zakharov system is still regarded as an incredibly interesting model from the mathematical perspective due to its complexity. To be more specific such Klein-Gordon-Zakharov model (
1.3
) can be viewed as a nonlinear wave-Klein-Gordon coupled system, therefore numerous techniques have been attempted to tackle the complicated nonlinear coupling structure through the development of wave-related methods, which we put the literature review on hold for the time being (see Section
1.2
below). It is worthwhile pointing out here that the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov system (
1.3
) is a special case of our model problem (
1.1b
) by choosing
A^{00}=A^{\alpha\beta}=0
for
\alpha\neq\beta
and
A^{11}=A^{22}=-B^{\alpha}=1
for
\alpha=0,1,2
.

In this paper, we are interested in the small data global well-posedness (GWP) of the model problem (
1.1
) and the long-time energy/pointwise asymptotic behavior of the global solutions. Furthermore, we carefully explore the scattering phenomenon of (
1.1
). Our first main result states below:

Theorem 1.1
.

Consider the coupled wave-Klein-Gordon system as in (
1.1a
) and let
K
be an integer no less than
12
. For any fixed small parameter
0<\delta<\frac{1}{2}
, there exists small
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and all initial data
(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1},N_{0},N_{1})
satisfying the smallness condition below:

(1.4)

\displaystyle\|(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1})\|_{\text{ID1}}+\|(N_{0},N_{1})\|_{\text{ID2}}<\varepsilon,

where the initial-data-norms of
(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1})
and
(N_{0},N_{1})
are given by

(1.5)

\begin{split}\|(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1})\|_{\text{ID1}}=&\|\langle x\rangle^{K}\langle\nabla\rangle^{K+1}\Phi_{0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{K}\langle\nabla\rangle^{K}\Phi_{1}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{K+1}\log(1+\langle x\rangle)\langle\nabla\rangle^{K}\Phi_{0}\|_{2}\\
&+\|\langle x\rangle^{K+1}\log(1+\langle x\rangle)\langle\nabla\rangle^{K-1}\Phi_{1}\|_{2},\\
\|(N_{0},N_{1})\|_{\text{ID2}}=&\sum_{j=0}^{K-5}\Big(\|\langle x\rangle^{j+\frac{5}{2}}\nabla^{j+3}(\nabla N_{0},N_{1})\|_{1}+\|\langle x\rangle^{j+\frac{5}{2}}\nabla^{j}(\nabla N_{0},N_{1})\|_{1}\Big)\\
&+\sum_{j=0}^{K}\|\langle x\rangle^{j+1}\nabla^{j}(\nabla N_{0},N_{1})\|_{2}.\end{split}

We thus can conclude the following:

(i).
The Cauchy problem for the (
1.1a
) system admits a couple of global solutions
(\phi,n)
in time with the following energy of polynomial growth-in-time:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}+\|\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}+\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}n\|_{2}+\|\Gamma^{\leq K}n\|_{2}\leq C\varepsilon\langle t\rangle^{\delta}

for some constant
C>0
and
\Gamma
are the usual vector fields excluding the space-time scaling operator
L_{0}
(see Section
2.1
for definition). Moreover, we have the following uniform boundedness of the lower-order energy for some constant
C>0
:

(1.7)

\displaystyle\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K-1}\phi\|_{2}+\|\Gamma^{\leq K-1}\phi\|_{2}+\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K-2}n\|_{2}\leq C\varepsilon.

(ii).
Such global solutions satisfy the following asymptotic pointwise decay estimate:

(1.8)

\displaystyle|\phi(t,x)|\leq C_{1}\varepsilon\langle t\rangle^{-1},\quad|\partial n(t,x)|\leq C_{2}\varepsilon\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}},

for some positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
. The notation
\langle t\rangle
denotes the usual Japanese bracket that we refer to Section
2.1
for more details of the notation.

Remark 1.2
.

There exist a few global well-posedeness results for different wave-Klein-Gordon coupled system (including the totally geodesic wave map model) with
small initial data (cf.
[
1
,
12
,
22
,
34
,
8
,
11
,
13
]
) in different dimensions. For clarification our main contribution to this problem is to prove the global stability of system (
1.1
) by obtaining the uniform-in-time
boundedness of the energy with small non-compactly supported initial data in
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In order to overcome the difficulties including the slow decay-in-time resulting from the low dimension and the compact support requirement, our approach is mainly based on a newly developed normal-type estimate
where we manage to handle the lack of the space-time scaling operator
L_{0}
using vector field method on the usual flat space-time. We also provide a proof of a strong linear scattering (see Theorem
1.7
below) for the Klein-Gordon field
\phi
and a weak linear scattering for the wave field
n
as
t\to+\infty
. Another novelty of our work is in giving a clear description of the smallness conditions of non-compactly supported initial data (see Remark
1.6
).

Remark 1.3
.

As will be clear later, our approach to prove the global well-posedness of (
1.1a
) is to separate
n=n_{0}+A^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{n}
, where

(1.9)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Box n_{0}=0,\\
(n_{0},\partial_{t}n_{0})|_{t=0}=(N_{0},N_{1}),\end{cases}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ \begin{cases}\Box\tilde{n}=|\phi|^{2},\\
(\tilde{n},\partial_{t}\tilde{n})|_{t=0}=(0,0).\end{cases}

In effect the global well-posedness of (
1.1b
) can be dealt with via the same strategy: we decompose
n=n_{0}+A^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha\beta}N
where

(1.10)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Box n_{0}=0,\\
(n_{0},\partial_{t}n_{0})|_{t=0}=(N_{0},N_{1}),\end{cases}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ \begin{cases}\Box N=|\phi|^{2},\\
(N,\partial_{t}N)|_{t=0}=(0,0).\end{cases}

Our energy bootstrap framework holds in such similar situation while the initial condition is slightly different and we will discuss more about the (
1.1b
) case in Section
5
.

Remark 1.4
.

It is worth mentioning here that usually the best pointwise decay-in-time of
(\phi,n)
one can expect (ignoring the nonlinear structure) is

(1.11)

\displaystyle|\phi(t,x)|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1},\quad|\partial n(t,x)|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}},

which is obviously weaker than (
1.8
). To see (
1.11
) is the best one can expect, we consider the homogeneous linear wave and Klein-Gordon equation in
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
:

(1.12)

\displaystyle\Box u=0,\quad\Box v+v=0,

with sufficiently nice initial data. Then it follows from the usual Klainerman-Sobolev inequality (cf. Lemma
2.2
) and the
L^{\infty}
estimate (cf. Lemma
2.9
). We refer the readers to
[
20
]
for the wave equation and
[
15
]
for the Klein-Gordon equation. Such pointwise decay-in-time (
1.11
) brings major difficulties in showing the global well-posedness (and linear scattering for the wave component), which will be explained in more details in Section
1.3
. Fortunately such situation can be improved due to the special nonlinear structure, we pause our discussion in this direction for now and will continue in Section
1.4
.

Remark 1.5
.

We remark here that the requirement (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) on the initial condition is of slightly high regularity (around 12 times of differentiablity in the Sobolev sense). In fact in the existing literature there are many applicable techniques such as multilinear space-time estimates and frequency
decomposition that have been used intensively to reduce
regularity assumptions (cf.
[
16
,
17
]
); however we do not dig deeper in this direction. Our main contribution in this work is to prove the global stability with non-compactly supported initial data via our flat space-time normal form strategy.

Remark 1.6
.

Another point we would like to emphasize is that the initial assumption (
1.4
) is essentially different from the ones in nonlinear waves (cf.
[
6
]
). More specifically speaking, one can observe from (
1.4
) that
\|\langle x\rangle^{K}\langle\nabla\rangle^{K+1}\Phi_{0}\|_{2}<\varepsilon
is yet strong since even
\Phi_{0}
has to obey certain spatial decay properties:
\|\langle x\rangle^{K}\Phi_{0}\|_{2}<\varepsilon
. Unfortunately such assumption is necessary. This can be explained through a crucial observation that when performing energy estimates with vector field
\Omega_{i0}
, one may run into the quantity
\|\Omega_{i0}^{4}\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}
. With no loss it suffices to consider
\|\langle x\rangle^{4}\partial_{t}^{4}\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}
. Note that by applying the Klein-Gordon equation twice, one arrive that

(1.13)

\displaystyle\|\langle x\rangle^{4}\partial_{t}^{4}\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\langle x\rangle^{4}\partial_{t}^{2}\Delta\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{4}\partial_{t}^{2}\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{4}\partial_{t}^{2}(\partial\phi n)|_{t=0}\|_{2}

(1.14)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\langle x\rangle^{4}\Delta^{2}\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{4}\Delta\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{4}\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}+\text{nonlinear terms}.

As shown above, one already needs to control terms such as
\|\langle x\rangle^{4}\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}=\|\langle x\rangle^{4}\Phi_{0}\|_{2}
. For more discussion on this we refer the readers to
[
6
]
and Proposition
C.1
later.

Our next result states that the couple of solutions
(\phi,n)
scatters linearly:

Theorem 1.7
.

Assume the same setting as in Theorem
1.1
is adopted and in addition we denote the energy space
\mathcal{X}_{K}=H^{K}\times H^{K-1}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{K}=\dot{H}^{K}\times\dot{H}^{K-1}\cap\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
where
H^{m},\dot{H}^{m}
are standard Sobolev spaces in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
that are defined in (
2.10
). We also denote

D=(D_{i})_{i=1}^{2}
to be the usual differential operator with
D_{1}=\partial_{1},D_{2}=\partial_{2}
. Then the following statements hold:

(i)
. The solution
\phi
scatters to a free solution in
\mathcal{X}_{K}
as
t\to+\infty
, namely, there exists
(\Phi_{l_{0}},\Phi_{l_{1}})\in\mathcal{X}_{K}
such that

(1.15)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\|(\phi,\partial_{t}\phi)-(\phi_{l},\partial_{t}\phi_{l})\|_{\mathcal{X}_{K}}=0,

where
\phi_{l}
is the linear homogeneous solution to the Klein-Gordon equation with initial data
(\Phi_{l_{0}},\Phi_{l_{1}})
.

(ii)
. The solution
n
scatters to a free solution weakly in
\mathcal{Y}_{K}
as
t\to+\infty
, i.e. there exists
(N_{l_{0}},N_{l_{1}})\in\mathcal{Y}_{K}
such that for any
(F_{0},F_{1})\in\mathcal{Y}_{K}
we have

(1.16)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla D^{K-1}(n-n_{l})\cdot\nabla D^{K-1}f+\partial_{t}\nabla D^{K-1}(n-n_{l})\cdot\partial_{t}\nabla D^{K-1}f=0,

(1.17)

and

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla(n-n_{l})\cdot\nabla f+\partial_{t}(n-n_{l})\partial_{t}f=0,

where
n_{l}
is the linear homogeneous solution to the wave equation with initial data
(N_{l_{0}},N_{l_{1}})
and
f
is the linear homogeneous solution to the wave equation with initial data
(F_{0},F_{1})
.

Remark 1.8
.

We want to emphasize that the scattering result in Theorem
1.7
is valid under high regularity assumptions on the initial data; we refer the readers to other scattering results in
[
8
,
34
]
for the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov system with initial data of much higher regularity. Difficulties arise when appealing to initial data of low regularity data (cf.
[
16
]
). Nevertheless, the strong scattering of the wave field
n
cannot be shown through our argument due to the critical decay-in-time as

(1.18)

\displaystyle\|\partial(|\phi|^{2})\|_{H^{m}}(s)\lesssim\langle s\rangle^{-1}\implies\int_{1}^{t}\|\partial(|\phi|^{2})\|_{H^{m}}\ ds\sim\log(t).

In fact whether the wave
field
n
scatters strongly (linearly or nonlinearly) is unknown yet and we leave it open. However, a weak scattering can be shown from the extra dual structure under the crucial observation
\left|\frac{\phi}{\langle r-t\rangle}\right|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-2}
, see (
3.62
) later; we also prove that energy of wave field
n
is uniformly bounded (
1.7
). Indeed both results are necessary to the strong linear scattering. Moreover the weak linear scattering leads to the uniform boundedness of the usual energy (without any weight) of
n
applying the classic Uniform Boundedness Principle (see Remark
4.2
for more information).

1.2.
Historical review

As mentioned earlier the model we are interested is of a nonlinear wave and Klein-Gordon coupling system, therefore it is of great significance to go through some related results. To start with, nonlinear wave equations have been widely studied: consider the following second order quasilinear wave equation in
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

(d\geq 2)
:

(1.19)

\Box u=g^{kij}\partial_{k}u\partial_{ij}u,

where
g^{kij}
are constants and assume the initial data behave sufficiently well. For the
d\geq 4
case, small data global solutions of (
1.19
) were known to exist (cf.
[
20
]
). When
d=3
, GWP of (
1.19
) was obtained in the pioneering work by Klainerman
[
26
]
and Christodoulou
[
5
]
under the null condition (
g^{kij}\omega_{k}\omega_{i}\omega_{j}=0,
for
\omega_{0}=-1
and
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
). On the other hand, the solutions admit finite time blow-up behaviors without the null conditions (cf.
[
21
]
). When it comes to the
d=2
case, the relatively slow decay-in-time causes major difficulties. To overcome such challenge Alinhac in the seminal work
[
2
,
3
]
shows that (
1.19
) admits a small data global smooth solution under the null conditions by introducing the well-known “ghost weight” technique and on the contrary the solution blows up in finite time if the null conditions are not satisfied. In addition in
[
2
]
Alinhac showed that the highest norm of the solution grows at most polynomially in time. Recently many work have successfully improved the previous results and we list several of relation here. In
[
28
]
the author proved the uniform boundedness of the highest norm of the solution by developing a new normal-form type strategy; in
[
10
]
similar results were obtained by applying vector field method on hyperboloids dating back to Klainerman and Hörmander. Beyond these, there are studies focusing on dealing with models where the Lorentz invariance is not available. Such systems include non-relativistic wave systems with multiple wave speeds (cf.
[
41
]
) and exterior domains (cf.
[
32
]
). It is also worth pointing out that in the work
[
28
]
, the author developed a new systematic normal-form framework and proved GWP of 2D quasilinear wave equations without the Lorentz boost
\Omega_{i0}
; in fact this strategy inspired us to handle models where the space-time scaling operator
L_{0}
is not applicable.

The nonlinear Klein-Gordon equations have been roundly studied as well. In the pioneering work by Klainerman
[
24
]
and Shatah
[
38
]
, the Klein-Gordon equations with quadratic nonlinearities were shown to possess small global solutions. Motivated by the large amount of inventive work on nonlinear wave equation and Klein-Gordon equation mentioned above, the coupled wave and Klein-Gordon systems have arouse a great deal of interest for decades. Among which, to our best knowledge, one of the very first result belonging to this area was derived by Bachelot
[
4
]
on
the Dirac-Klein-Gordon equations. Later in
[
14
]
Georgiev proved GWP with strong null nonlinearities. Much more physical models described by the coupled wave and Klein-Gordon systems
have been studied since then. We list a few models of interest here: the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov equations
[
8
,
12
,
22
,
34
]
, the
Maxwell-Klein-Gordon equations
[
25
,
36
,
37
]
, the Einstein-Klein-Gordon equations
[
27
,
47
]
.

We also review here some results concerning the wave-Klein-Gordon type coupled system such as the Zakharov system arising in plasma physics. We refer the readers to its physical background in
[
7
,
49
]
and briefly review some related models. We recall that the Euler-Maxwell equations are of fundamental importance in the area of plasma physics. In fact one can derive the Zakharov equations via the Euler equation for the electrons and ions coupled with the Maxwell equation for the electric field (cf.
[
44
]
) and in particular the Euler-Poisson system in

. There are many other interesting results concerning different aspects of other closely related models of the wave-Klein-Gordon system. We hereby list a few of them: GWP of the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov equations with multiple propagation speeds
[

, the convergence of the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov equations to the Schrödinger

(cf.

), the finite time blow-up for Klein-Gordon-Zakharov with rough data

, the long-term pointwise decay of the small global solution (cf.

The presenting paper was mainly motivated by several papers
[

,

,

. In the work
[

]
, the authors studied the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov equations on

[

[
11

studied the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov equations on
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
and showed such system admits global small solutions with non-compactly supported initial data. However, they assumed the wave field

is of the form
\Delta n^{\Delta}
for some function

; this in return required the initial data

to be in
H^{-2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Actually such transformation makes partial sense due to a key energy conservation law; we refer the readers to
[

]
and the references therein, where the energy was defined as

(1.20)

\displaystyle E=\int|\phi|^{2}+|\partial\phi|^{2}+\frac{|D^{-1}\partial_{t}n|^{2}+|n|^{2}}{2}-n|\phi|^{2}\ dx.

Last but not least, the work
[
13
]
studied the exact wave-Klein-Gordon system as in (
1.1
). The authors obtained global small solutions with compactly supported solutions where a vector field on hyperboloids strategy dating back to Klainerman and Hörmander was adopted. Motivated by the existing results on coupled wave and Klein-Gordon systems, one of our modest
goals is to show the global existence result without any compactness assumptions on the initial data through a new energy bootstrap based on the normal-form strategy. In addition we want to give a clear description of the pointwise asymptotic behavior together with showing the linear scattering of these global solutions.

1.3.
Main Difficulties

Before we sketch the strategy of our proof, we briefly demonstrate the main difficulties of this model problem here. To begin with, as we mentioned earlier in Remark
1.4
, the best pointwise decay of
(\phi,n)
one can expect (ignoring the constant) is (
1.11
) (instead of (
1.8
)):

(1.21)

\displaystyle|\phi(t,x)|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1},\quad|\partial n(t,x)|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Recall that the Klein-Gordon component

(1.22)

\displaystyle\Box\phi+\phi=B^{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\alpha}n.

The usual energy estimate (by taking
L^{2}
inner product with
\partial_{t}\phi
on (
1.22
)) yields

(1.23)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(\|\partial\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\|\phi\|_{2}^{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}B^{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\alpha}n\cdot\partial_{t}\phi\ dx,

where
\partial=(\partial_{t},\nabla)
(see Section 2 for the notation). It is then very natural to integrate in time and estimate the following quantity:

(1.24)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi\partial n\|_{2}(s)\ ds\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|\partial n\|_{2}\ ds+\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial n\|_{\infty}\|\phi\|_{2}\ ds.

Taking (
1.21
) into consideration and naively assuming the
L^{2}
norms are uniformly bounded in time we arrive at:

(1.25)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi\partial n\|_{2}\ ds\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}(\langle s\rangle^{-1}+\langle s\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}})\ ds\lesssim\log(1+t)+\langle t\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}},

which even implies a blow-up of the energy as
t
goes to
+\infty
! We believe there are two reasons causing such terrible blow-up and holding us back.

•

First we observe that the decay
\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}
has not been made fully use of while taking supremum in space. To our understanding, one of the main advantages of the aforementioned hyperbolic change of variable (vector field method on hyperboloids) is that one can gain better control of the so-called “conformal energy” thanks to the extra integrability in the hyperbolic time
s=\sqrt{t^{2}-r^{2}}
(cf.
[
9
]
).

•

Second is that we did not pay proper attention to the crucial machinery of the Klein-Gordon field
\phi
that extra decay in
\langle t+r\rangle
can be gained with a trade-off losing
\langle t-r\rangle
. For a more detailed discussion, we refer the readers to Remark
2.12
or Lemma
3.5
and Lemma
3.7
alternatively.

To handle the above challenges, we follow the similar idea as in
[
6
]
. Briefly speaking, we are about to apply the well-adapted Alinhac’s ghost weight method to absorb extra
\langle t-r\rangle
weight and thereby generate better decay-in-time. Afterwards we will take good care of the nonlinear structure together with the key machinery of the Klein-Gordon field
\phi
mentioned earlier to derive even better decay-in-time. Another main difficulty is that the space-time scaling is not invariant in the KGZ system therefore the
L_{0}
vector field is not applicable. In the presenting paper, we develop a new normal-form strategy that is motivated by the work
[
28
]
where the Lorentz transformation is not invariant. Lastly, another question is whether one can prove the scattering (linearly or nonlinearly) of the wave field
n
. Unlike the Klein-Gordon field
\phi
is known to scatter linearly, scattering of the wave field
n
is still an open question in
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
in the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov context. Such problem results from the critical decay-in-time due to the low dimension as explained earlier in Remark
1.8
. In other words, when approaching to show the linear scattering of
n
in the energy space we usually argue by semi-group techniques. As a result, it seems inevitable to estimate terms such as
\|\partial(|\phi|^{2})\|_{H^{m}}
, which decays as
\langle t\rangle^{-1}
at best. Therefore the space-time integral is not uniformly bounded (
1.18
):

\int_{1}^{t}\|\partial(|\phi|^{2})\|_{H^{m}}\ ds\sim\log(t).

Although it seems unlikely to show the wave field
n
scatters linearly, we manage to prove a weak scattering result from the extra dual structure under the crucial machinery mentioned earlier
\left|\frac{\phi}{\langle r-t\rangle}\right|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-2}
, see Lemma
3.7
later. In addition we also prove that energy of wave field
n
is uniformly bounded (
1.7
). It is worth emphasizing that both the uniform boundedness of the energy and the weak linear scattering are necessary to the strong linear scattering.

1.4.
Main steps of the proof.

We hereby outline the key steps of our proof. To illustrate the idea clearly, we fix any multi-index
\alpha
and denote
\Phi=\Gamma^{\alpha}\phi
and
V=\partial\Gamma^{\alpha}n
with
|\alpha|=K
(we suppress the dependence on
\alpha
to ease the notation). We point out here
\Gamma
cannot be
L_{0}
due to the lack of space-time scaling invariance; for the exact definition we refer to (
2.1
)–(
2.2
).

Step 1.
Energy estimate. To start with, we make the following
a\ priori
hypothesis:

(1.26)

\displaystyle|\Gamma^{\leq k}n|\leq C\varepsilon\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle r-t\rangle^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}},\quad|\Gamma^{\leq k}\phi|\leq C\varepsilon\langle t+r\rangle^{-1},

where positive constant
C
is chosen later and
k
is around half size of
K
. We first apply Alinhac’s ghost weight method by choosing
p(r,t)=q(r-t)
with
q^{\prime}(s)
scaling as
\langle s\rangle^{-1-\delta}
. In light of such choice of
q
, we remark here that it is already the best as far as we know; in fact it seems totally non-trivial to find a “ghost weight” with
q(s)
scaling exactly as
\langle s\rangle^{-1}
. As a matter of fact, the
\langle s\rangle^{-1-\delta}
scale brings technical difficulties to both the energy estimate and the pointwise asymptotic analysis. To see this, we briefly discuss about performing energy estimats below. Let us focus on the Klein-Gordon equation after applying
\Gamma^{\alpha}
:

(1.27)

\displaystyle\Box\Phi+\Phi=\Gamma^{\alpha}(B^{\beta}\phi\partial_{\beta}n).

Then by taking the
L^{2}
inner product on (
1.27
) with
\partial_{t}\Phi\cdot\exp(p)(r,t)
we can obtain that

(1.28)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}(\|e^{\frac{p}{2}}\partial\Phi\|_{2}^{2}+\|e^{\frac{p}{2}}\Phi\|_{2}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\int e^{p}q^{\prime}(|T\Phi|^{2}+|\Phi|^{2})=\sum_{I_{1}+I_{2}=I}C^{\alpha}_{I;I_{1},I_{2}}\int e^{p}\partial_{\alpha}\Gamma^{I_{1}}n\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\partial_{t}\Phi

for some constant
C^{\alpha}_{I;I_{1},I_{2}}
. For the sake of simplicity, we focus on the terms with the highest index, i.e.
\int e^{p}(\Phi\partial n+V\phi)\partial_{t}\Phi\ dx
. At first glance, clearly we can control

(1.29)

\displaystyle\left|\int V\phi e^{p}\partial_{t}\Phi\ dx\right|\lesssim\|V\|_{2}\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{2}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1}\|V\|_{2}\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{2}.

The other term is handled by applying the usual Cauchy-Schwartz inequality as below:

(1.30)

\left|\int\Phi\partial ne^{p}\partial_{t}\Phi\ dx\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}\int e^{p}q^{\prime}\cdot|\Phi|^{2}\ dx+\mbox{constant}\cdot\int|\partial\Phi|^{2}\cdot\frac{|n|^{2}}{q^{\prime}}e^{p}\ dx,

which leads to the energy estimate of the Klein-Gordon field (here
\frac{|n|^{2}}{q^{\prime}}\sim t^{-1}
by (
1.26
) ). Similar energy estimates on the wave component yields the same result and both lead to a polynomially growth of the energy, namely,
\|\partial\Phi\|_{2}+\|\Phi\|_{2}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{\delta_{1}}
for arbitrarily small
\delta_{1}>0
. In order to close the bootstrap scheme by showing the pointwise asymptotic decay-in-time, it is necessary to prove the lower-order energy is uniformly bounded for both wave field
n
and Klein-Gordon field
\phi
. To proceed, we notice from the previous work
[
6
]
that the wave field
\partial n
indeed is able to absorb extra
\langle r-t\rangle
while in return the crucial machinery of the Klein-Gordon
\frac{\phi}{\langle r-t\rangle}
can gain better decay
\langle t\rangle^{-2}
. More specifically, our approach is to decompose
n
into two parts:
n=n_{0}+A^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{n}
, where

(1.31)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Box n_{0}=0,\\
(n_{0},\partial_{t}n_{0})|_{t=0}=(N_{0},N_{1}),\end{cases}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\ \begin{cases}\Box\tilde{n}=|\phi|^{2},\\
(\tilde{n},\partial_{t}\tilde{n})|_{t=0}=(0,0).\end{cases}

This kind of reformulation is dated back to
[
22
]
, where the author considered nonlinear wave equations with
nonlinearities of divergence form. One way to understand this decomposition is to treat
\tilde{n}
as a small perturbation of order
O(\varepsilon^{2})
(the “leading” term
n_{0}
is of order
O(\varepsilon)
). We refer the readers to Remark
3.3
. For the details of the proof of the uniform energy boundedness, we refer the readers to Section
3
.

Step 2.
Pointwise decay-in-time and scattering. We then close the energy bootstrap by showing the asymptotic pointwise decay (
1.8
). The decay of the Klein-Gordon component
\phi
follows from a result by Georgiev in
[
15
]
(cf. Lemma
2.9
). In short words, it suffices for us to bound

(1.32)

\displaystyle\|\langle t+|\cdot|\rangle\Gamma^{\leq k+4}(\phi\partial n)\|_{2}.

We notice that Lemma
2.11
plays an important role in dealing with terms such as

(1.33)

\displaystyle\|\langle t+|\cdot|\rangle(\Gamma^{\leq k+4}\phi)\partial n\|_{2}+\|\langle t+|\cdot|\rangle(\Gamma^{\leq k+4}\partial n)\phi\|_{2}.

As explained earlier we indeed view Lemma
2.11
as an iteration process: the Klein-Gordon component
\phi
can gain better
\langle t+r\rangle
decay as long as it can absorb more
\langle t-r\rangle
weight. For the wave component
n=n_{0}+A^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{n}
.
n_{0}
is a free wave solution therefore we handle it by developing a
L^{\infty}-L^{1}
estimate (see Lemma
2.7
and Lemma
2.8
). On the other hand, to estimate the
\tilde{n}
part and remedy the lack of
L_{0}
vector fields, we employ
L^{\infty}
and
L^{2}
estimates involving the weight-factor
\langle r-t\rangle
. The key observation (see Lemma
2.5
) is that one can control
\langle t-r\rangle\partial^{2}u
. At the expense of the smallness of the energy, we obtain the desired asymptotic decay-in-time. These in turn lead to the closure of the energy bootstrap with a careful choice of the constants. In the end, we show the Klein-Gordon field
\phi
scatters linearly as
t\to+\infty
. In fact this results from a semi-group method and the uniform boundedness of the energy in time. Although it seems unlikely to show the wave field
n
scatters linearly by our method, we still manage to prove a weak scattering result from the extra dual structure (see Theorem
1.7
). In addition we also prove that energy of wave field
n
is uniformly bounded (
1.7
). We highlight here that both the uniform boundedness of the energy and the weak linear scattering are necessary to the strong linear scattering of the wave field
n
.

(2.36)

\displaystyle\implies|(t-r)\partial_{ij}u|=

\displaystyle\frac{(t-r)}{t}(\partial_{j}\Omega_{i0}u-\delta_{ij}\partial_{t}u-x_{i}\partial_{t}\partial_{j}u)\lesssim|\partial\Gamma^{\leq 1}u|+|(t-r)\partial_{t}\partial_{j}u|

(2.37)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\partial\Gamma^{\leq 1}u|+(t+r)|\Box u|\qquad(\text{by }\eqref{2.350}).

Case 2.
r\geq\frac{t}{2}
. Recall that
\Omega=t\partial_{r}+r\partial_{t}
. Therefore we have

(2.38)

\displaystyle\Omega\partial_{t}u=t\partial_{r}\partial_{t}u+r\partial_{tt}u,\qquad\Omega\partial_{r}u=t\partial_{rr}u+r\partial_{t}\partial_{r}u.

Then we have

(2.39)

\displaystyle(t^{2}-r^{2})\partial_{t}\partial_{r}u=

\displaystyle\ t\Omega\partial_{t}u-r\Omega\partial_{r}u-tr(\partial_{tt}u-\partial_{rr}u)

(2.40)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ t\Omega\partial_{t}u-r\Omega\partial_{r}u-tr(\Box u+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}u+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}u),

(2.41)

\displaystyle\implies|(t-r)\partial_{t}\partial_{r}u|\lesssim

\displaystyle|\partial\Gamma^{\leq 1}u|+(t+r)|\Box u|,

recalling
\Delta=\partial_{rr}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}}{r^{2}}
. Here
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\partial_{\theta\theta}
.
Therefore we get
|(t-r)\partial_{t}\nabla u|\lesssim|\partial\Gamma^{\leq 1}u|+(t+r)|\Box u|
.
On the other hand, we have

(2.42)

\displaystyle t^{2}\partial_{rr}u-r^{2}\partial_{tt}u=t\Omega\partial_{r}u-r\Omega\partial_{t}u,

(2.43)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle(t^{2}-r^{2})\partial_{rr}u=2r\partial_{r}u+\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}u+r^{2}\Box u+t\Omega\partial_{r}u-r\Omega\partial_{t}u,

(2.44)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle|(t-r)\partial_{rr}u|\lesssim|\partial\Gamma^{\leq 1}u|+(t+r)|\Box u|.

This implies that
\langle t-r\rangle|\Delta u|
and
\langle t-r\rangle|\nabla^{2}u|
can be controlled by RHS of (
2.44
). Furthermore we use the identity
\partial_{tt}u=\Delta u+\Box u
and deduce that
\langle t-r\rangle|\partial_{tt}u|\lesssim|\partial\Gamma^{\leq 1}u|+(t+r)|\Box u|
. Thus we complete the proof.

∎

2.3.
Pointwise estimates for the wave component

We here collect some pointwise estimates for the wave components.

Let
v(t,x)
be a solution of the following system:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Box v=F(t,x);\\
(v,\partial_{t}v)|_{t=0}=(v_{0},v_{1}).\end{cases}

It is known that the solution to such system can be written as the following mild form:

\displaystyle v(t,x)=

\displaystyle(\cos t|\nabla|\ v_{0})(x)+(\frac{\sin t|\nabla|}{|\nabla|}v_{1})(x)+\int_{0}^{t}(\frac{\sin(t-s)|\nabla|}{|\nabla|}F)(s,x)ds

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle v_{\text{hom}}+v_{\text{inh}},

where
|\nabla|
is the differential operator that is corresponding to the Fourier multiplier
|\xi|
; moreover
v_{\text{hom}}=\cos t|\nabla|v_{0}+\frac{\sin t|\nabla|}{|\nabla|}v_{1}
and
v_{\text{inh}}=\int_{0}^{t}(\frac{\sin(t-s)|\nabla|}{|\nabla|}F)(s,x)ds
. It is well known that
v_{\text{hom}}
is the solution to the following homogeneous wave system

(2.46)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Box v_{\text{hom}}=0,\\
(v_{\text{hom}},\partial_{t}v_{\text{hom}})|_{t=0}=(v_{0},v_{1})\end{cases}

and in particular

(2.47)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&(\cos t|\nabla|v_{0})(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\partial_{t}\int_{|x-y|<t}\frac{v_{0}(y)dy}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x-y|^{2}}}\\
&(\frac{\sin t|\nabla|}{|\nabla|}v_{1})(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|x-y|<t}\frac{v_{1}(y)dy}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x-y|^{2}}}.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.7

(Homogeneous estimate)
.

Assume
v_{\text{hom}}
is defined above in (
2.46
) and assume
v_{0},v_{1}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, then the following estimate holds: for any
t\geq 2
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

(2.48)

\displaystyle|v_{\text{hom}}(t,x)|\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-{\frac{1}{2}}}(\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\nabla\rangle v_{1}\|_{L^{1}}+\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\nabla\rangle^{2}v_{0}\|_{L^{1}}).

Proof.

We postpone the proof to the Appendix (see Lemma
B.1
).
∎

Lemma 2.8

(Extra homogeneous estimate.)
.

Assume
v_{\text{hom}}
is defined as in (
2.46
) above and assume
v_{0},v_{1}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Then for any
t\geq 2
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, there exists some constant
C>0
such that the following holds: for
r=|x|
we have

(2.49)

\displaystyle|\partial v_{\text{hom}}(t,x)|\leq C\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}}(\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{\frac{3}{2}}\langle\nabla\rangle^{3}v_{0}\|_{L^{1}})+\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{\frac{3}{2}}\langle\nabla\rangle^{2}v_{1}\|_{L^{1}}).

Proof.

We first note that by a simple observation:

(2.50)

\displaystyle(t^{2}-r^{2})(\partial_{t},\partial_{1},\partial_{2})=(tL_{0}-x_{i}\Omega_{i0},-x_{1}L_{0}+t\Omega_{10}+x_{2}\partial_{\theta},-x_{2}L_{0}+t\Omega_{20}-x_{1}\partial_{\theta}).

It then follows from (
2.50
) that for
t>0

(2.51)

\displaystyle\langle t-|x|\rangle|\partial v_{\text{hom}}|\lesssim|\widetilde{\Gamma}v_{\text{hom}}(t,x)|,

where
\widetilde{\Gamma}
is defined as in (
2.3
). Note that

(2.52)

\displaystyle[\Gamma,\Box]=0\ ,\quad[L_{0},\Box]=-2\Box,

where
[\cdot,\cdot]
is the usual commutator, then in effect
\widetilde{\Gamma}v_{\text{hom}}
is again a solution to the system (
2.46
) with certain initial data. Then combining (
2.48
), (
2.51
) and the initial condition estimate (cf.
[
6
]
). We thus conclude (
2.49
).
∎

Lemma 2.9

(Georgiev)
.

Let
w
be the solution of the Klein-Gordon equation

(2.55)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Box w+w=f,\\
(w,\partial_{t}w)|_{t=0}=(w_{0},w_{1}),\end{cases}

with

a sufficiently nice function. Then for all
t\geq 0
, it holds

(2.56)

\displaystyle\langle t+|x|\rangle|w(t,x)|\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}(\sup_{0<s\leq t}p_{j}(s)\|\langle s+|\cdot|\rangle\Gamma^{\leq 4}f(s,\cdot)\|_{2}+\|\langle|\cdot|\rangle p_{j}(|\cdot|)\Gamma^{\leq 5}w(0,\cdot)\|_{2}).

In this article we shall use the following variation as in
[
8
,
11
]
.

Lemma 2.10

(Dong et al.)
.

With the same settings as in Lemma
2.9
, letting
\delta^{\prime}>0
and assuming

(2.57)

\displaystyle\sum_{|I|\leq 4}\|\langle s+|\cdot|\rangle\Gamma^{I}f(s,\cdot)\|_{2}\leq C_{f}\langle s\rangle^{-\delta^{\prime}},

then for all

we have

(2.58)

\displaystyle\langle t+|x|\rangle|w(t,x)|\lesssim\frac{C_{f}}{1-2^{-\delta^{\prime}}}+\sum_{|I|\leq 5}\|\langle|\cdot|\rangle\log\langle\cdot\rangle\Gamma^{I}w(0,\cdot)\|_{2}.

Lemma 2.11
.

Suppose
u=u(t,x)
is a smooth solution to
\Box u+u=F
. Then for
t>0
we have

(2.59)

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle}u\Big|\lesssim

\displaystyle|\partial\Gamma^{\leq 1}u|+\Big|\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle}F\Big|.

Proof.

If
r<\frac{t}{2}
or
r>2t
, we have
\langle t+r\rangle\sim\langle t-r\rangle
. This implies that

\Big|\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle}u\Big|\lesssim|u|\lesssim|\Box u|+|F|\lesssim|\partial\Gamma^{\leq 1}u|+|F|.

If
\frac{t}{2}\leq r\leq 2t
, we recall that (
2.30
) gives that

(2.60)

\displaystyle(t^{2}-r^{2})\Delta u=

\displaystyle r^{2}\Box u+r\partial_{r}u-2t\partial_{t}u-x_{i}\partial_{t}\Omega_{i0}u+t\partial_{i}\Omega_{i0}u

(2.61)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-r^{2}u+r^{2}F+r\partial_{r}u-2t\partial_{t}u-x_{i}\partial_{t}\Omega_{i0}u+t\partial_{i}\Omega_{i0}u\qquad(\text{by }\Box u+u=F)

(2.62)

\displaystyle\implies u=

\displaystyle-\frac{t^{2}-r^{2}}{r^{2}}\Delta u+F+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}u-\frac{2t}{r^{2}}\partial_{t}u-\frac{1}{r^{2}}(x_{i}\partial_{t}\Omega_{i0}u-t\partial_{i}\Omega_{i0}u).

This implies (
2.59
) holds for
\frac{t}{2}\leq r\leq 2t
.
∎

Remark 2.12
.

Note that one can iterate (
2.59
) as long as the nonlinear term
F
is sufficiently nice. A typical example is
F=u^{2}
then
F
can easily absorb the weight
\frac{\langle t+r\rangle}{\langle t-r\rangle}
and eventually we can derive the decay of
|u|\lesssim\langle t+r\rangle^{-M}\langle t-r\rangle^{M}
for arbitrarily large
M
. Such property of
u
can be considered as a machinery that one can gain any decay in
\langle t+r\rangle
with a trade-off of losing
\langle t-r\rangle
decay.

Lemma 2.13
.

Suppose
u=u(t,x)
is a smooth solution to
\Box u+u=F
. Then for
t\geq 2
and
r\leq 3t
we have

(2.63)

\displaystyle|u|\lesssim\frac{\langle r-t\rangle}{t}|\partial^{2}u|+\frac{1}{t}|\partial\Gamma^{\leq 1}u|+|F|.

Proof.

The proof is very similar to Lemma
2.5
. Recall (
2.30
) that

(2.64)

\displaystyle(t^{2}-r^{2})\Delta u=r^{2}\Box u+r\partial_{r}u-2t\partial_{t}u-x_{i}\partial_{t}\Omega_{i0}u+t\partial_{i}\Omega_{i0}u

(2.65)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle(t^{2}-r^{2})\partial_{tt}u+(r^{2}-t^{2})\Box u=r^{2}\Box u+r\partial_{r}u-2t\partial_{t}u-x_{i}\partial_{t}\Omega_{i0}u+t\partial_{i}\Omega_{i0}u

(2.66)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle t^{2}u=(r^{2}-t^{2})\partial_{tt}u+t^{2}F+r^{2}\Box u+r\partial_{r}u-2t\partial_{t}u-x_{i}\partial_{t}\Omega_{i0}u+t\partial_{i}\Omega_{i0}u.

We thus conclude (
2.63
) from here.

∎

3.
Estimates of the energy

To prove Theorem
1.1
,
we first make an
a priori
hypothesis: for any
K\geq 12
we assume

(3.1)

\displaystyle|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K-5}n_{0}|+|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq K-5}\tilde{n}|\leq C_{1}\varepsilon{\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\langle r-t\rangle^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}},

(3.2)

\displaystyle|\Gamma^{\leq K-5}\phi|\leq C_{2}\varepsilon\langle t+r\rangle^{-1},

where the positive constants
C_{1},\ C_{2}
will be chosen later and
\delta>0
is a small fixed parameter as in Theorem
1.1
.

Remark 3.1
.

It is worth mentioning that here our
a priori
assumption on wave field
n
is only of the form
\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle r-t\rangle^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}
yet in fact we will show such decay can be the usual
\langle t+r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle r-t\rangle^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}
to close the bootstrap scheme (see Section
4
).

As mentioned earlier, we consider the initial conditions on
\phi
and
n
:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\phi,\partial_{t}\phi)|_{t=0}=(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1}),\\
(n,\partial_{t}n)|_{t=0}=(N_{0},N_{1}).\end{cases}

Recall that we separate
n=n_{0}+A^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{n}
, where

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\Box n_{0}=0,\\
&(n_{0},\partial_{t}n_{0})|_{t=0}=(N_{0},N_{1}),\end{cases}

and

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\Box\tilde{n}=|\phi|^{2},\\
&(\tilde{n},\partial_{t}\tilde{n})|_{t=0}=(0,0).\end{cases}

To begin with, we first show the top-order energy is “almost” bounded that is it grows polynomially as
\langle t\rangle^{\delta_{1}}
where the power
\delta_{1}
can be made arbitrarily small.

Lemma 3.2

(Polynomial growth of the top-order energy)
.

Let
(\phi,n)
be a couple of solutions to (
1.1
). Assume (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) hold. Then we have for some constant
C>0
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}+\|\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}+\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}n\|_{2}\leq C\varepsilon t^{\delta_{1}}

where
\delta_{1}
satisfying
0<\delta_{1}<\frac{\delta}{4}
is an arbitrarily small constant. In particular, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}\tilde{n}\|_{2}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\langle t\rangle^{\delta_{1}}.

Proof.

To begin with we first consider the wave component
\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}n\|_{2}
.

Step 1, the wave component
n
.

Note that since
n=n_{0}+A^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{n}
, we have for any multi-index
I
,

(3.8)

\displaystyle|\Gamma^{I}n|\lesssim|\Gamma^{I}n_{0}|+|\Gamma^{I}\partial\tilde{n}|\lesssim|\Gamma^{I}n_{0}|+|\partial\Gamma^{\leq|I|}\tilde{n}|.

As a result for
|I|\leq K
,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}n\|_{2}\lesssim\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}n_{0}\|_{2}+\|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq K}\tilde{n}\|_{2}.

Note that by a simple energy estimate

(3.10)

\displaystyle\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}n_{0}\|_{2}(t)=\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}n_{0}\|_{2}(0)\leq C_{i}\varepsilon,

where
C_{i}>0
is some fixed constant resulting from the initial condition. Therefore it suffices to estimate
\|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq K}\tilde{n}\|_{2}
. Let
|I|=m
and
m\leq K
is a running index, we have from (
3.5
) and (
3.4
) that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\Box\Gamma^{I}\tilde{n}=\sum_{I_{1}+I_{2}=I}C_{I;I_{1},I_{2}}\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi.

Moreover, we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\Box\partial\Gamma^{I}\tilde{n}=\sum_{I_{1}+I_{2}=I}\tilde{C}_{I;I_{1},I_{2}}\partial\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi.

Multiplying both sides of (
3.12
) by
\partial_{t}\partial\Gamma^{I}\tilde{n}
, we obtain

(3.13)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{I}\tilde{n}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int|\partial\Gamma^{I_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\partial_{t}\partial\Gamma^{I}\tilde{n}|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K-1}{2}]}\phi\|_{\infty}\|\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}+\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}\|\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K}{2}]}\phi\|_{\infty})\|\partial_{t}\partial\Gamma^{I}\tilde{n}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K-1}{2}]+1}\phi\|_{\infty}(\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}+\|\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2})\|\partial_{t}\partial\Gamma^{I}\tilde{n}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon t^{-1}(\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}+\|\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2})\|\partial_{t}\partial\Gamma^{I}\tilde{n}\|_{2}\qquad\qquad(\text{by \eqref{A3}}).

Note that here we indeed have
[\frac{K-1}{2}]+1\leq K-5
since
K\geq 12
. This implies that

(3.14)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{I}\tilde{n}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\ C\varepsilon t^{-1}(\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq K}\tilde{n}\|_{2}^{2}).

Step 2, we estimate the Klein-Gordon component
\|\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2},\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}
.

Let
I
be a multi-index and
|I|\leq K
.
Write
\Phi=\Gamma^{I}\phi
. We have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\square\Phi+\Phi=\sum_{I_{1}+I_{2}=I}C^{\alpha}_{I;I_{1},I_{2}}\partial_{\alpha}\Gamma^{I_{1}}n\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi.

Let
p(t,r)=q(r-t)
, where

(3.16)

\displaystyle q(s)=\int_{-\infty}^{s}\langle\tau\rangle^{-1}\bigl(\log(2+\tau^{2})\bigr)^{-2}d\tau.

Clearly

(3.17)

\displaystyle-\partial_{t}p=\partial_{r}p=q^{\prime}(r-t)=\langle r-t\rangle^{-1}\bigl(\log(2+(r-t)^{2})\bigr)^{-2}.

Multiplying both sides of (
3.15
) by
e^{p}\partial_{t}\Phi
, we obtain

(3.18)

LHS

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}(\|e^{\frac{p}{2}}\partial\Phi\|_{2}^{2}+\|e^{\frac{p}{2}}\Phi\|_{2}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\int e^{p}q^{\prime}(|T\Phi|^{2}+|\Phi|^{2}),

where
|\partial\Phi|^{2}=\sum_{i=0}^{2}|\partial_{i}\Phi|^{2}
and
|T\Phi|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}|T_{i}\Phi|^{2}
. We split the RHS into two cases:

(3.19)

RHS

\displaystyle=\sum_{I_{1}+I_{2}=I}C^{\alpha}_{I;I_{1},I_{2}}\int e^{p}\partial_{\alpha}\Gamma^{I_{1}}n\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi\partial_{t}\Phi.

Case 1:

|I_{1}|<|I_{2}|\leq|I|
.

(3.20)

\displaystyle\text{RHS}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{10}\int e^{p}q^{\prime}|\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi|^{2}+C\int\frac{e^{p}}{q^{\prime}}|\partial\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{|I|}{2}]}n|^{2}|\partial_{t}\Phi|^{2}

(3.21)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{10}\int e^{p}q^{\prime}|\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi|^{2}+C\|\langle r-t\rangle^{\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\partial\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{|I|}{2}]}n\|_{\infty}^{2}\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{2}^{2}

(3.22)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{10}\int e^{p}q^{\prime}|\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi|^{2}+(C\varepsilon)^{2}t^{-1}\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{2}^{2}\qquad(\text{by \eqref{A2}}).

Here the positive constant
C
may vary from line to line. We also notice that after summing the index
I
the term
\frac{1}{10}\int e^{p}q^{\prime}|\Gamma^{I_{2}}\phi|^{2}
can be absorbed by the LHS (by choosing
K\geq 12
we indeed have
[\frac{K}{2}]+1\leq K-5
).

Case 2:

|I|\geq|I_{1}|\geq|I_{2}|
.

(3.23)

\displaystyle\text{RHS}\leq

\displaystyle C\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq|I|}n\|_{2}\|\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{|I|}{2}]}\Phi\|_{\infty}\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{2}

(3.24)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon t^{-1}(\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq|I|}\partial\tilde{n}\|_{2}+\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq|I|}n_{0}\|_{2})\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{2}\qquad(\text{by \eqref{A3} and $n=n_{0}+A^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}\tilde{n}$})

(3.25)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon t^{-1}\|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq|I|}\tilde{n}\|_{2}\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{2}+C\varepsilon^{2}t^{-1}\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{2}.

Then we arrive at

(3.26)

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}(\|\partial\Phi\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Phi\|_{2}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\int e^{p}q^{\prime}(|T\Phi|^{2}+|\Phi|^{2})\\
\leq&\ (C\varepsilon)^{2}t^{-1}(\|\partial\Phi\|_{2}^{2}+\|\partial\Phi\|_{2})+C\varepsilon t^{-1}\|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq|I|}\tilde{n}\|_{2}\|\partial_{t}\Phi\|_{2}.\end{split}

Combining (
3.14
) and (
3.26
), it follows that

(3.27)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq K}\tilde{n}\|_{2}^{2})

(3.28)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ (C\varepsilon)^{2}t^{-1}(\|\partial\Phi\|_{2}^{2}+\|\partial\Phi\|_{2})+\ C\varepsilon t^{-1}(\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\|\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}^{2}+\|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq K}\tilde{n}\|_{2}^{2}).

This immediately implies that

(3.29)

\displaystyle\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}+\|\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}+\|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq K}\tilde{n}\|_{2}\leq C\varepsilon t^{\delta_{1}}.

For the term
\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}\tilde{n}\|_{2}
:
let
J
be a multi-index and
|J|=m\leq K
. Then we have

(3.30)

\displaystyle\Box\Gamma^{J}\tilde{n}=\sum_{J_{1}+J_{2}=J}C_{J;J_{1},J_{2}}\Gamma^{J_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{J_{2}}\phi.

Multiplying both sides of (
3.30
) by
\partial_{t}\Gamma^{J}\tilde{n}
, we obtain

(3.31)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial\Gamma^{J}\tilde{n}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{J_{1}+J_{2}=J}\int|\Gamma^{J_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{J_{2}}\phi\partial_{t}\Gamma^{J}\tilde{n}|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K}{2}]}\phi\|_{\infty}\|\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}\|\partial_{t}\Gamma^{J}\tilde{n}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2}\langle t\rangle^{-1+\delta_{1}}\|\partial\Gamma^{J}\tilde{n}\|_{2}

\displaystyle(\text{by \eqref{A3} and \eqref{3.32}}),

which shows

(3.32)

\displaystyle\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K}\tilde{n}\|_{2}\leq C\varepsilon\langle t\rangle^{\delta_{1}}

by taking the initial data estimate into consideration. Here we need the following smallness assumption (also see (
C.1
),(
C.2
) and (
C.3

\displaystyle|\partial\Gamma^{\alpha}n_{0}|\leq C_{i}\varepsilon\langle r-t\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}}\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{i}\varepsilon\langle t-r\rangle^{-1}\langle t+r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}},

where the constant
C_{i}>0
results from the initial condition and should be clear in the context. Then by recalling (
3.8
) it suffices for us to show that

(4.3)

\displaystyle|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq|\alpha|}\tilde{n}|\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\langle r-t\rangle^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\langle t+r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

For this purpose, we assume that
t>2
with no loss. Recall that (
2.28
):
|\langle r-t\rangle\partial^{2}u|\leq|\partial\Gamma^{\leq 1}u|+(t+r)|\Box u|
for all
r>0
. We then consider two cases.

Case 1:
r\leq\frac{t}{2}
or
r>2t
.
Noting that
\Box\tilde{n}=|\phi|^{2}
, we arrive at

(4.4)

\displaystyle|\langle r-t\rangle\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha}\tilde{n}|\lesssim

\displaystyle|\partial\Gamma^{\leq|\alpha|+1}\tilde{n}|+(t+r)|\Box\Gamma^{\alpha}\tilde{n}|

(4.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \langle t+r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}{\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq|\alpha|+4}\tilde{n}\|_{2}}+\|\langle t+|\cdot|\rangle\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{|\alpha|}{2}]}\phi\|_{\infty}\|\Gamma^{\leq|\alpha|}\phi\|_{\infty}\qquad(\text{by }\eqref{t12},\eqref{t12b})

(4.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ \varepsilon^{2}\langle t+r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}t^{\delta_{1}}+\varepsilon^{2}\langle t+r\rangle^{-1}\qquad(\text{by }\eqref{A3}).

Then by (
3.7
), we obtain by taking
0<\delta<\frac{1}{2}
and
\delta_{1}<\frac{\delta}{4}
that

(4.7)

\displaystyle|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha}\tilde{n}|\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1-\delta}{2}}\langle t+r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle t\rangle^{\delta_{1}}\quad(\mbox{by }\eqref{3.4})

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\langle r-t\rangle^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\langle r+t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Case 2:
\frac{t}{2}\leq r\leq 2t
. If
|t-r|\leq 1
, it follows from (
2.15
) that

(4.8)

\displaystyle\langle t-r\rangle^{1+\delta}t|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha}\tilde{n}|^{2}\lesssim t|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha}\tilde{n}|^{2}\lesssim\|\Gamma^{\leq 1}\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha}\tilde{n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq 1}\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha}\tilde{n}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim\|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq K-3}\tilde{n}\|_{2}^{2}.

Then by (
3.69
) we can conclude

(4.9)

\displaystyle|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha}\tilde{n}|\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\langle r-t\rangle^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\langle r+t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

It then remains to show (
4.3
) for the case when
|t-r|\geq 1
and
\frac{t}{2}\leq r\leq 2t
. To begin with, it follows by (
2.20
) that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\langle r-t\rangle^{\frac{1+\delta}{2}}t^{\frac{1}{2}}|\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha}\tilde{n}|

(4.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\langle t-|\cdot|\rangle^{\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\partial_{r}^{\leq 1}\partial_{\theta}^{\leq 1}\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\alpha}\tilde{n}\|_{2}

(4.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\chi^{\frac{1}{2}}(|\cdot|-t)\langle t-|\cdot|\rangle^{\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq|\alpha|+2}\tilde{n}\|_{2}+\|(1-\chi^{\frac{1}{2}}(|\cdot|-t))\langle t-|\cdot|\rangle^{\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq|\alpha|+2}\tilde{n}\|_{2}

(4.13)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle B_{1}+B_{2}.

Note that we can estimate
B_{1}
as follows:

(4.14)

\displaystyle B_{1}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\chi^{\frac{1}{2}}(|\cdot|-t)\langle t-|\cdot|\rangle\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq K-3}\tilde{n}\|_{2}

(4.15)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\chi^{\frac{1}{2}}(|\cdot|-t)\partial\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\tilde{n}\|_{2}+\|\chi^{\frac{1}{2}}(|\cdot|-t)(t+|\cdot|)\Box\Gamma^{\leq K-3}\tilde{n}\|_{2}\leq C\varepsilon^{2},\quad\text{ by \eqref{3.34b} and \eqref{3.52b}}.

To control
B_{2}
, we notice that

(4.16)

\displaystyle(1-\chi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho-t))^{2}\lesssim 1-\chi(\rho-t)\lesssim\chi(t-\rho)+\eta(\rho,t),

where the cut-off function
\eta(\rho,t)
is chosen to be supported in
\{|\rho-t|\leq 2\}
and its value is 1 in the region
\{|\rho-t|\leq 1\}
. As a result, it follows that

(4.17)

\displaystyle B_{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|(1-\chi(\rho-t))^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\rho-t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}}\langle\rho-t\rangle\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq K-3}\tilde{n}\|_{2}

(4.18)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\chi^{\frac{1}{2}}(t-\rho)\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}}\langle t-\rho\rangle\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq K-3}\tilde{n}\|_{2}+\|\eta(\rho,t)\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq K-3}\tilde{n}\|_{2}

(4.19)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}+\|\chi^{\frac{1}{2}}(t-\rho)\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}}\langle t-\rho\rangle\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq K-3}\tilde{n}\|_{2},\qquad\text{ by \eqref{3.34}}

(4.20)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}+\|\chi^{\frac{1}{2}}(t-\rho)\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}}\partial\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\tilde{n}\|_{2}+\|\chi^{\frac{1}{2}}(t-r)\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}}\Box\Gamma^{\leq K-3}\tilde{n}\|_{2},\qquad\text{ by \eqref{2.260}}

(4.21)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}+\|\chi^{\frac{1}{2}}(t-\rho)\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}}\partial\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\tilde{n}\|_{2}+\|\chi^{\frac{1}{2}}(t-\rho)\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}}\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K-3}{2}]}\phi\|_{\infty}\|\Gamma^{\leq K-3}\phi\|_{2}

(4.22)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}+\|\chi^{\frac{1}{2}}(t-\rho)\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}}\partial\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\tilde{n}\|_{2},\qquad\text{ by \eqref{A3} and \eqref{3.36},}

where the requirement
[\frac{K-3}{2}]\leq K-5
is guaranteed by our choice of
K
. It then suffices to control
\|\chi^{\frac{1}{2}}(t-|\cdot|)\langle t-|\cdot|\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}}\partial\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\tilde{n}\|_{2}
. Letting
\beta
be a multi-index and
|\beta|\leq K-2
, we get

(4.23)

\displaystyle\Box\Gamma^{\beta}\tilde{n}=\sum_{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\beta}C_{\beta;\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}\Gamma^{\beta_{1}}\phi\Gamma^{\beta_{2}}\phi.

Taking the test function
\left[\chi(t-\rho)\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-1+\delta}+1-\chi(t-\rho)\right]\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\beta}\tilde{n}
, it leads to that

(4.24)

\displaystyle\text{LHS}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\chi(t-\rho))\langle t-r\rangle^{-1+\delta}+1-\chi(t-\rho)\right]|\partial\Gamma^{\beta}\tilde{n}|^{2}

(4.25)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\chi^{\prime}(t-\rho)-\chi^{\prime}(t-\rho)\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-1+\delta}+(1-\delta)\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-2+\delta}(t-\rho)\chi(t-\rho)\right)|T\Gamma^{\beta}\tilde{n}|^{2};

(4.26)

\displaystyle\text{RHS}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\chi(t-\rho)\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-1+\delta}+1-\chi(t-\rho)\right]|\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\phi\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K-2}{2}]}\phi\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\tilde{n}|.

To proceed now we split RHS into 3 parts as follows:

(4.27)

\displaystyle\chi(t-\rho)\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-1+\delta}+1-\chi(t-\rho)\leq\chi(t-\rho)\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-1+\delta}+\eta(t,\rho)+\chi(\rho-t).

It then follows that
\text{RHS}\lesssim D_{1}+D_{2}+D_{3}
, where

(4.28)

\displaystyle D_{1}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi(t-\rho)\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-1+\delta}|\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\phi\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K-2}{2}]}\phi\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\tilde{n}|

(4.29)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\langle t-\rho\rangle^{\delta}\frac{\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K-2}{2}]}\phi}{\langle t-\rho\rangle}\right\|_{\infty}\|\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\phi\|_{2}\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\tilde{n}\|_{2}

(4.30)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2}\langle t\rangle^{-2+\delta+2\delta_{1}},\qquad\text{ by \eqref{3.52}, \eqref{3.32} and \eqref{3.44e}}.

Similarly, we have

(4.31)

\displaystyle D_{2}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\eta(t,\rho)|\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\phi\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K-2}{2}]}\phi\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\tilde{n}|

(4.32)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K-2}{2}]}\phi}{\langle t-\rho\rangle}\right\|_{\infty}\|\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\phi\|_{2}\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\tilde{n}\|_{2}

(4.33)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2}\langle t\rangle^{-2+2\delta_{1}},\qquad\text{ by \eqref{3.52}, \eqref{3.32} and \eqref{3.44e}};

and we have

(4.34)

\displaystyle D_{3}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi(\rho-t)|\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\phi\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K-2}{2}]}\phi\partial_{t}\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\tilde{n}|

(4.35)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\chi^{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho-t)\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K-2}{2}]}\phi\|_{\infty}\|\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\phi\|_{2}\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\tilde{n}\|_{2}

(4.36)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2}\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{5}{4}+\frac{\delta_{1}}{2}+\delta_{1}},\qquad\text{ by \eqref{3.52b},\eqref{3.32} and \eqref{3.44e}}.

Here our requirements
[\frac{K-2}{2}]\leq K-7
is guaranteed by choosing
K\geq 12
.
Observing that

(4.37)

\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}(t-\rho)-\chi^{\prime}(t-\rho)\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-1+\delta}+(1-\delta)\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-2+\delta}(t-\rho)\chi(t-\rho)\geq 0

and the assumptions on the initial data

(4.38)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\chi(t-\rho))\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-1+\delta}+1-\chi(t-\rho)\right]|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\tilde{n}|^{2}\ dx\Big|_{t=0}\leq C_{i}\varepsilon^{2},

we get the desired estimate
B_{2}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}
by noting

(4.39)

\displaystyle\|\chi^{\frac{1}{2}}(t-\rho)\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}}\partial\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\tilde{n}\|^{2}_{2}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left[\chi(t-\rho))\langle t-\rho\rangle^{-1+\delta}+1-\chi(t-\rho)\right]|\partial\Gamma^{\leq K-2}\tilde{n}|^{2}.

Thus we have proved (
4.3
) and (
4.1
). Then it suffices to choose
C_{1}>C_{i}+\widetilde{C}_{n}\varepsilon
in (
3.1
), where
C_{i}
is the exact known constant from the initial condition (cf. (
C.1
)-(
C.6
)).

4.2.
Assumption 2: Klein-Gordon component (
3.2
)

Let
\beta
be a multi-index and
|\beta|\leq K-5
. Then
\Gamma^{\beta}\phi
satisfies

(4.40)

\begin{cases}\Box\Gamma^{\beta}\phi+\Gamma^{\beta}\phi=-\sum_{\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}=\beta}C_{\beta;\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}\partial\Gamma^{\beta_{1}}n\Gamma^{\beta_{2}}\phi=:\widetilde{F},\\
(\phi,\partial_{t}\phi)|_{t=0}=(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1}).\end{cases}

By (
2.58
), we have

(4.41)

\displaystyle\langle t+|x|\rangle|\Gamma^{\beta}\phi(t,x)|\lesssim{\sup_{0<s\leq t}}\langle s\rangle^{\delta^{\prime}}\|\langle s+|\cdot|\rangle\Gamma^{\leq 4}\widetilde{F}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}+\|\langle|\cdot|\rangle\log\langle|\cdot|\rangle\Gamma^{\leq 5}\Gamma^{\beta}\phi(0,\cdot)\|_{2},

for some
0<\delta^{\prime}\ll 1
.
For any
s\in(0,t)
we have

(4.42)

\displaystyle\|\langle s+|\cdot|\rangle\Gamma^{\leq 4}\widetilde{F}(s,\cdot)\|_{2}

(4.43)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\langle s-|\cdot|\rangle\partial\Gamma^{\leq|\beta|+4}n\|_{2}\left\|\frac{\langle s+|\cdot|\rangle}{\langle s-|\cdot|\rangle}\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{|\beta|+4}{2}]}\phi\right\|_{\infty}+\|\langle s-|\cdot|\rangle\partial\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{|\beta|+4}{2}]}n\|_{\infty}\left\|\frac{\langle s+|\cdot|\rangle}{\langle s-|\cdot|\rangle}\Gamma^{\leq|\beta|+4}\phi\right\|_{2}

(4.44)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle G_{1}+G_{2}.

Note that we have

(4.45)

\displaystyle G_{1}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}\langle s\rangle^{-1+\delta_{1}}\qquad\text{(by \eqref{3.52} and \eqref{3.59a})};

on the other hand,

\displaystyle G_{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\langle s\rangle^{\delta_{1}}\|\langle s-|\cdot|\rangle\partial\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{|\beta|+4}{2}]}n\|_{\infty},\quad\text{ by \eqref{3.322}},

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\langle s\rangle^{\delta_{1}}(\|\langle s-|\cdot|\rangle\partial\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K-1}{2}]}n_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|\langle s-|\cdot|\rangle\partial^{2}\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K-1}{2}]}\tilde{n}\|_{\infty}),

\displaystyle C_{i}\varepsilon\langle s\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta_{1}}+C\langle s\rangle^{\delta_{1}}\left(\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K-1}{2}]+1}\tilde{n}\|_{\infty}+\|\langle s+|\cdot|\rangle\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K-1}{2}]}\phi\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K-1}{4}]+1}\phi\|_{\infty}\right),

\displaystyle C_{i}\varepsilon\langle s\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta_{1}}+C\varepsilon^{2}\langle s\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\delta_{1}}+C\varepsilon^{2}\langle s\rangle^{-1+\delta_{1}},\quad\text{ by \eqref{t12},\eqref{t12b}, \eqref{3.44e} and \eqref{A3}.}

Here the constant

may vary from line to line and we need the index

,which is true since

. It then follows by taking

and by (

) that

(4.46)

\displaystyle\langle t+r\rangle^{-1}|\Gamma^{\beta}\phi(t,x)|\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2}+\|\langle|\cdot|\rangle\log\langle|\cdot|\rangle\Gamma^{\leq 5}\Gamma^{\beta}\phi(0,\cdot)\|_{2}

(4.48)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{i}\varepsilon+\widetilde{C}_{\phi}\varepsilon^{2},\qquad\text{(by \eqref{ID2} and \eqref{B3}).}

Then it suffices to choose
C_{2}>{C_{i}}+\widetilde{C}_{\phi}\varepsilon
in (
3.2
),
where

is the known constant from the initial condition (cf. (

)-(

.

4.3.
Scattering

In this subsection, we briefly discuss about the scattering of the wave-Klein-Gordon system (
1.1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We show that the Klein-Gordon field
\phi
scatters to a linear Klein-Gordon
equation in its energy space as
t\to+\infty
and the wave field
n
scatters “weakly”. We now proceed to show the scattering of
\phi
in the energy space
\mathcal{X}_{K}=H^{K}\times H^{K-1}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{K}=\dot{H}^{K}\times\dot{H}^{K-1}\cap\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
, namely there exists a couple of solutions
(\phi_{l},n_{l})
to the linear system such that (
1.15
), (
1.16
) and (
1.17
) hold:

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|(\phi,\partial_{t}\phi)-(\phi_{l},\partial_{t}\phi_{l})\|_{\mathcal{X}_{K}}=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla D^{K-1}(n-n_{l})\cdot\nabla D^{K-1}f+\partial_{t}D^{K-1}(n-n_{l})\cdot\partial_{t}D^{K-1}f=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla(n-n_{l})\cdot\nabla f+\partial_{t}(n-n_{l})\partial_{t}f=0,

where
(\phi,\partial_{t}\phi,n,\partial_{t}n)|_{t=0}=(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1},N_{0},N_{1})
and
(\phi_{l},\partial_{t}\phi_{l},n_{l},\partial_{t}n_{l})|_{t=0}=(\Phi_{l_{0}},\Phi_{l_{1}},N_{l_{0}},N_{l_{1}})
for some
(\Phi_{l_{0}},\Phi_{l_{1}},N_{l_{0}},N_{l_{1}})\in\mathcal{X}_{K}\times\mathcal{Y}_{K}
. Assume that
f
is the linear homogeneous solution to the wave equation with arbitrary initial data
(F_{0},F_{1})\in\mathcal{Y}_{K}
; here we denote

D=(D_{i})_{i=1}^{2}
to be the usual differential operator with
D_{1}=\partial_{1},D_{2}=\partial_{2}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

We show the linear scattering of
\phi
and
n
separately. To start with, we consider the Klein-Gordon field
\phi
.

Linear scattering of the Klein-Gordon component.

Recall that for the Cauchy problem for the inhomogeneous 2d Klein-Gordon equation:

(4.49)

\displaystyle\Box\phi+\phi=H\,,\ \mbox{with }(\phi,\partial_{t}\phi)|_{t=0}=(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1}),

the solution
\vec{\phi}=(\phi,\partial_{t}\phi)
is given by

(4.50)

\displaystyle\vec{\phi}(t,x)=N(t)\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{0}\\
\Phi_{1}\end{pmatrix}+\int_{0}^{t}N(t-s)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
H(s)\end{pmatrix}\ ds,

where

(4.51)

\displaystyle N(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(t\langle\nabla\rangle)&\langle\nabla\rangle^{-1}\sin(t\langle\nabla\rangle)\\
-\langle\nabla\rangle\sin(t\langle\nabla\rangle)&\cos(t\langle\nabla\rangle)\end{pmatrix}.

More specifically
H(n,\phi)=\phi B^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}n
in our setting.
By a standard semi-group argument, it is known that the Klein-Gordon flow
N(t)
is a unitary semi-group on
H^{J}\times H^{J-1}
for
J\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
. More precisely one can verify on the Fourier side that for any
J\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
one has

(4.52)

\displaystyle\|N(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{J}\to\mathcal{H}^{J}}=1,

where
\mathcal{H}^{J}=H^{J}\times H^{J-1}
. Moreover, one recalls that

(4.53)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{l}\\
\partial_{t}\phi_{l}\end{pmatrix}=N(t)\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{l_{0}}\\
\Phi_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}.

Note that by (
3.6
)-(
3.69
), we have

(4.54)

\displaystyle\|H(\phi,n)\|_{H^{K-1}}\lesssim\|\phi\partial n\|_{H^{K-1}}

(4.55)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\langle t-\rho\rangle\partial\Gamma^{\leq K-1}n\|_{2}\left\|\frac{\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K-1}{2}]}\phi}{\langle t-\rho\rangle}\right\|_{\infty}+\langle t\rangle^{-1}\|\langle t-\rho\rangle\partial\Gamma^{\leq[\frac{K-1}{2}]}n\|_{\infty}\left\|\frac{\langle t+\rho\rangle}{\langle t-\rho\rangle}\Gamma^{\leq K-1}\phi\right\|_{2}

(4.56)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{2}(\langle t\rangle^{-2+\delta_{1}}+\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}+2\delta_{1}}),\qquad\text{ by \eqref{3.59a}, \eqref{3.52}, \eqref{t12}-\eqref{t12b} and \eqref{3.322}}.

Note that we have [
\frac{K-1}{2}]\leq K-7
. Therefore it follows immediately that

(4.57)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\|H(\phi,n)\|_{H^{K-1}}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

In particular, we set

(4.58)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{l_{0}}\\
\Phi_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{0}\\
\Phi_{1}\end{pmatrix}+\int_{0}^{\infty}N(-s)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
H(\phi,n)\end{pmatrix}\ ds,

then since
N(t)
is unitary, we get

(4.59)

\displaystyle\left\|\begin{pmatrix}\phi\\
\partial_{t}\phi\end{pmatrix}-N(t)\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{l_{0}}\\
\Phi_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{K}}\leq\int_{t}^{\infty}\left\|H(\phi,n)\right\|_{H^{K-1}}\ ds.

Therefore we have shown that
\phi
scatters to
\phi_{l}
as
t\to+\infty
:

(4.60)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\|(\phi,\partial_{t}\phi)-(\phi_{l},\partial_{t}\phi_{l})\|_{\mathcal{X}_{K}}=0.

Weak linear scattering of the Klein-Gordon component.
We show (
1.16
) and then (
1.17
) follows as a corollary. Recall that we have

(4.61)

\displaystyle\Box n=A^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}(|\phi|^{2}),

(4.62)

\displaystyle\Box n_{l}=0.

Then we consider the inner product of
\vec{n}=(D^{K-1}n,\partial_{t}D^{K-1}n)
and
\vec{n_{l}}=(D^{K-1}n_{l},\partial_{t}D^{K-1}n_{l})
on
\mathcal{Z}\coloneqq\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
. Here

D=(D_{i})_{i=1}^{2}
is the usual differential operator with
D_{1}=\partial_{1},D_{2}=\partial_{2}
.
We denote the inner product to be
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathcal{Z}}
. Then we differentiate it in time to get

(4.63)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla D^{K-1}n\cdot\nabla D^{K-1}n_{l}+\partial_{t}D^{K-1}n\cdot\partial_{t}D^{K-1}n_{l}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{t}D^{K-1}n_{l}(A^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}D^{K-1}|\phi|^{2}).

Integrating in the time slot
(s,t)
we thus arrive at

(4.64)

\displaystyle\big(\vec{n}(t),\vec{n_{l}}(t)\big)_{\mathcal{Z}}-\big(\vec{n}(s),\vec{n_{l}}(s)\big)_{\mathcal{Z}}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{t}D^{K-1}n_{l}(A^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}D^{K-1}|\phi|^{2})\ dxd\tau.

Recall that the Cauchy problem for the following homogeneous (inhomogeneous) 2d wave system:

(4.65)

\displaystyle\Box D^{K-1}n=G\,,\ \mbox{with }(D^{K-1}n,\partial_{t}D^{K-1}n)|_{t=0}=(D^{K-1}N_{0},D^{K-1}N_{1}),

the solution
\vec{n}=(D^{K-1}n,\partial_{t}D^{K-1}n)
is given by

(4.66)

\displaystyle\vec{n}(t,x)=M(t)\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}N_{0}\\
D^{K-1}N_{1}\end{pmatrix}+\int_{0}^{t}M(t-\tau)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
G(\tau)\end{pmatrix}\ d\tau,

where the wave flow
M(t)
is given by

(4.67)

\displaystyle M(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(t|\nabla|)&|\nabla|^{-1}\sin(t|\nabla|)\\
-|\nabla|\sin(t|\nabla|)&\cos(t|\nabla|)\end{pmatrix}.

Similarly we have

(4.68)

\displaystyle\vec{n_{l}}(t,x)=M(t)\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}N_{l_{0}}\\
D^{K-1}N_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}.

It is known that
M(t)
defines a unitary semi-group on
\mathcal{Y}_{k}
. Accordingly we can rewrite (
4.64
) as

(4.69)

\displaystyle\left(M(t)\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}N_{0}\\
D^{K-1}N_{1}\end{pmatrix}+\int_{0}^{t}M(t-\tau)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
G(\tau)\end{pmatrix},M(t)\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}N_{l_{0}}\\
D^{K-1}N_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\mathcal{Z}}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left(M(s)\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}N_{0}\\
D^{K-1}N_{1}\end{pmatrix}+\int_{0}^{s}M(s-\tau)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
G(\tau)\end{pmatrix},M(s)\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}N_{l_{0}}\\
D^{K-1}N_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\mathcal{Z}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{t}n_{l}(A^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}D^{K-1}|\phi|^{2})\ dxd\tau.

Noticing
M(t)
and
M(s)
are unitary in
\mathcal{Z}
, (
4.69
) is then equivalent to

(4.70)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{s}^{t}M(-\tau)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
G(\tau)\end{pmatrix}\ d\tau,\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}N_{l_{0}}\\
D^{K-1}N_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\mathcal{Z}}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\partial_{t}D^{K-1}n_{l}(A^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}D^{K-1}|\phi|^{2})\ dxd\tau.

We observe that the right hand side of (
4.70
) can be estimated as follows:

(4.71)

\displaystyle\text{RHS}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(|D^{\leq[\frac{K-1}{2}]}\phi\partial D^{\leq K-1}\phi|+|\partial D^{\leq[\frac{K-1}{2}]}\phi D^{\leq K-1}\phi|)|\partial_{t}D^{K-1}n_{l}|\ dxd\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\left(\left\|\frac{D^{\leq[\frac{K-1}{2}]}\phi}{\langle\rho-\tau\rangle}\right\|_{\infty}\|D^{\leq K}\phi\|_{2}+\langle\tau\rangle^{-1}\left\|\frac{\langle\rho+\tau\rangle}{\langle\rho-\tau\rangle}D^{\leq K-1}\phi\right\|_{2}\|\partial D^{\leq[\frac{K-1}{2}]}\phi\|_{\infty}\right)\|\langle\rho-\tau\rangle\partial_{t}D^{K-1}n_{l}\|_{2}\ d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\langle\tau\rangle^{-2+2\delta_{1}}(\|\langle x\rangle\nabla D^{K-1}N_{l_{0}}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle D^{K-1}N_{l_{1}}\|_{2}),\quad\text{ by \eqref{3.52}, \eqref{3.34}, \eqref{DE-1} and \eqref{S4-4}}.

Note that here we have indeed
[\frac{K-1}{2}]\leq K-7
by the choice
K\geq 12
. On top of this we observe that the right hand side of (
4.70
) goes to 0 as
s,t\to+\infty
so long as
\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}N_{l_{0}}\\
D^{K-1}N_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}
lies in a suitable space; one can take it to be
\text{ID}2
, namely the initial condition space for the wave component. It then follows from (
4.71
) that

(4.72)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{t}^{+\infty}M(-\tau)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
G(\tau)\end{pmatrix}\ d\tau,\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}N_{l_{0}}\\
D^{K-1}N_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\mathcal{Z}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1+2\delta_{1}}\left\|\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}N_{l_{0}}\\
D^{K-1}N_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{\mathcal{W}}\xrightarrow{t\to+\infty}0,

where

(4.73)

\displaystyle\left\|\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}N_{l_{0}}\\
D^{K-1}N_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{\mathcal{W}}=\|\langle x\rangle\nabla D^{K-1}N_{l_{0}}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle D^{K-1}N_{l_{1}}\|_{2}.

Therefore we obtain the convergence
\int_{t}^{+\infty}M(-\tau)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
G(\tau)\end{pmatrix}\ d\tau
to 0 in the dual space
\mathcal{W^{\prime}}
:

(4.74)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\left\|\int_{t}^{+\infty}M(-\tau)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
G(\tau)\end{pmatrix}\ d\tau\right\|_{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}}=0,\quad\text{with a rate of }\langle t\rangle^{-1+2\delta_{1}}.

On the other hand notice that
\mathcal{W}
is dense in
\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
, one has the weak convergence:

(4.75)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\left(\int_{t}^{+\infty}M(-\tau)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
G(\tau)\end{pmatrix}\ d\tau,\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}F_{l_{0}}\\
D^{K-1}F_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\mathcal{Z}}=0,

for any
(D^{K-1}F_{l_{0}},D^{K-1}F_{l_{1}})\in\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
. In particular, we set

(4.76)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}N_{l_{0}}\\
D^{K-1}N_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}N_{0}\\
D^{K-1}N_{1}\end{pmatrix}+\int_{0}^{+\infty}M(-\tau)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
G(\phi)\end{pmatrix}\ d\tau.

Rearranging (
4.75
) and (
4.76
) we arrive at

(4.77)

\displaystyle\left(\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}N_{l_{0}}\\
D^{K-1}N_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}N_{0}\\
D^{K-1}N_{1}\end{pmatrix}-\int_{0}^{t}M(-\tau)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
G(\phi)\end{pmatrix}\ d\tau,\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}F_{l_{0}}\\
D^{K-1}F_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\mathcal{Z}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{t}^{+\infty}M(-\tau)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
G(\tau)\end{pmatrix}\ d\tau,\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}F_{l_{0}}\\
D^{K-1}F_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\mathcal{Z}}\xrightarrow{t\to+\infty}0.

Noting that
M(t)
is unitary in
\mathcal{Z}
, we have actually

(4.78)

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\left(M(t)\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}N_{l_{0}}\\
D^{K-1}N_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}-M(t)\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}N_{0}\\
D^{K-1}N_{1}\end{pmatrix}-\int_{0}^{t}M(t-\tau)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
G(\phi)\end{pmatrix}\ d\tau,M(t)\begin{pmatrix}D^{K-1}F_{l_{0}}\\
D^{K-1}F_{l_{1}}\end{pmatrix}\right)_{\mathcal{Z}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla D^{K-1}(n-n_{l})\cdot\nabla D^{K-1}f+\partial_{t}D^{K-1}(n-n_{l})\cdot\partial_{t}D^{K-1}f\ dx,

where
f
,
n_{l}
are the free waves with initial data
(F_{l_{0}},F_{l_{1}})
and
(N_{l_{0}},N_{l_{1}})
respectively. This proves (
1.16
) and (
1.17
) follows afterwards.

∎

Remark 4.1
.

The scattering results for the Klein-Gordon-Zakharov equations have been obtained in
[
8
,
34
]
, with high regular initial data. It is worth pointing out here that there are different
difficulties arising in seeking scattering results for initial data of low regularity data (cf.
[
16
]
on Klein-Gordon-Zakharov equations). Nevertheless, the strong scattering of the wave field
n
cannot be shown via our method due to the critical decay-in-time as

\|\partial(|\phi|^{2})\|_{H^{m}}(s)\lesssim\langle s\rangle^{-1}\implies\int_{1}^{t}\|\partial(|\phi|^{2})\|_{H^{m}}\ ds\sim\log(t).

In fact, whether the wave
field
n
scatters strongly (linearly or nonlinearly) is unknown. As a consequence we leave it open here.

Remark 4.2
.

We note that the weak linear scattering we proved above is necessary to the strong linear scattering. Nevertheless, it is also worth pointing out here that the weak convergence immediately leads to a uniform boundedness in the energy class by the classic Uniform Boundedness Principle. More specifically speaking, one has

(4.79)

\displaystyle\|(n,\partial_{t}n)\|_{\mathcal{Y}_{K}}<+\infty,

which is also necessary to the strong linear scattering.

\begin{split}\|(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1})\|_{\text{ID1}}=&\|\langle x\rangle^{K}\langle\nabla\rangle^{K+1}\Phi_{0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{K}\langle\nabla\rangle^{K}\Phi_{1}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{K+1}\log(1+\langle x\rangle)\langle\nabla\rangle^{K}\Phi_{0}\|_{2}\\

Estimate of

Lemma A.1
.

Assume
\widetilde{\Gamma}\Gamma^{\beta}n_{0}
is defined as in (

(A.2)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\Gamma}\Gamma^{\beta}n_{0}\|_{2}\lesssim\|\langle x\rangle\nabla v_{0}\|_{2}+\log(t+2)\|\langle x\rangle v_{1}\|_{2}.

Before we give a proof to it, it is convenient to recall a useful lemma as below.

Lemma A.2

(Log-type Hardy)
.

Let
B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
h\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B(0,1))
. Then

(A.3)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{h}{|x|\log|x|}\right\|_{L^{2}(B(0,1))}\lesssim\|\nabla h\|_{L^{2}(B(0,1))}.

Proof.

The proof is standard. First we choose

\varphi(r)=-\frac{0.1}{r\log r}
. Then

\displaystyle|h\varphi+\partial_{r}h|^{2}

\displaystyle=h^{2}\varphi^{2}+\varphi\partial_{r}(h^{2})+(\partial_{r}h)^{2}\geq 0,

\displaystyle\implies\ \int_{B(0,1)}|\partial_{r}h|^{2}\ dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B(0,1)}(-\varphi^{2}+\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(r\varphi))h^{2}\ dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,1)}(-\frac{0.01}{r^{2}(\log r)^{2}}+\frac{0.1}{r^{2}(\log r)^{2}})h^{2}\ dx\gtrsim\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{h^{2}}{r^{2}(\log r)^{2}}\ dx.

This implies (
A.3
).
∎

Proof of Lemma

Similarly, we have

(B.17)

\displaystyle t^{-1}\int_{|x-y|<t}\frac{v_{0}(y)}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x-y|^{2}}}dy\lesssim\ t^{-\frac{3}{2}}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\langle y\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla^{\leq 1}v_{0}\|_{L^{1}};

(B.18)

\displaystyle\int_{|x-y|<t-1}\frac{\nabla v_{0}(y)\cdot\frac{y-x}{t}}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x-y|^{2}}}dy\lesssim\int_{|x-y|<t-1}\frac{|\nabla v_{0}(y)|}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x-y|^{2}}}dy\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\langle y\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\nabla\rangle^{2}v_{0}\|_{L^{1}}.

The remaining terms can be estimated as follows:

(B.19)

\displaystyle\int_{t-1<|x-y|<t}\frac{\nabla v_{0}(y)\cdot\frac{y-x}{t}}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x-y|^{2}}}dy

(B.20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|\omega|=1}\int_{t-1}^{t}\frac{\nabla v_{0}(x+\rho\omega)\cdot\frac{\rho\omega}{t}}{\sqrt{t^{2}-\rho^{2}}}\rho d\rho d\omega=-\int_{|\omega|=1}\int_{t-1}^{t}\nabla v_{0}(x+\rho\omega)\cdot\frac{\rho\omega}{t}\partial_{\rho}(\sqrt{t^{2}-\rho^{2}})d\rho d\omega

(B.21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|\omega|=1}\nabla v_{0}(x+(t-1)\omega)\cdot\frac{(t-1)\omega}{t}\sqrt{2t-1}d\omega

(B.22)

\displaystyle+\int_{t-1}^{t}\int_{|\omega|=1}\left[((\omega\cdot\nabla)^{2}v)(x+\rho\omega)+\frac{1}{\rho}(\nabla v_{0})(x+\rho\omega)\cdot\omega\right]\frac{\sqrt{t^{2}-\rho^{2}}}{t}\rho d\rho d\omega

(B.23)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle J_{3}+J_{4}.

Here we can estimate
J_{3}
as below:

(B.24)

\displaystyle J_{3}\lesssim

\displaystyle\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{|\omega|=1}|\langle x+(t-1)\omega\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}(\omega\cdot\nabla)v_{0}(x+(t-1)\omega)\sqrt{2t-1}|d\omega

(B.25)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{|\omega|=1}|\int_{t-1}^{\infty}\frac{d}{d\rho}\left(\langle x+\rho\omega\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}(\omega\cdot\nabla)v_{0}(x+\rho\omega)\sqrt{2\rho+1}\right)d\rho|d\omega

(B.26)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{|\omega|=1}|\int_{t-1}^{\infty}\langle x+\rho\omega\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}|(\omega\cdot\nabla)v_{0}(x+\rho\omega)|\frac{\sqrt{2\rho+1}}{\rho}\rho d\rho|d\omega

(B.27)

\displaystyle+\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{|\omega|=1}|\int_{t-1}^{\infty}\langle x+\rho\omega\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\left((\omega\cdot\nabla)^{2}v_{0}(x+\rho\omega)\sqrt{2\rho+1}+(\omega\cdot\nabla)v_{0}(x+\rho\omega)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\rho+1}}\right)d\rho|d\omega

(B.28)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\ t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla v_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\langle y\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla^{2}v_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+t^{-\frac{3}{2}}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\langle y\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla v_{0}\|_{L^{1}}.

J_{4}
can be handled similarly:

(B.29)

\displaystyle J_{4}\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\langle t-r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\langle y\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\nabla\rangle^{2}v_{0}\|_{L^{1}}.

Therefore we conclude (
B.1
)
∎

Appendix C
Verification of the smallness conditions of the Initial data

In this section we verify the smallness condition (
1.4
) is consistent with the vector fields:

Proposition C.1
.

Assume
(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{1},N_{0},N_{1})
satisfy the condition (
1.4
), then for any
J\leq K
we have

(C.1)

\displaystyle\|\Gamma^{\leq J}\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}\lesssim\big(\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\langle\nabla\rangle^{J}\Phi_{0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\langle\nabla\rangle^{J-1}\Phi_{1}\|_{2}\big)\big(1+\sum_{j=0}^{J-2}\|\langle x\rangle^{j+1}\nabla^{j}(\nabla N_{0},N_{1})\|_{2}\big),

(C.2)

\displaystyle\|\Gamma^{\leq J}n|_{t=0}\|_{2}\lesssim\big(\|\langle x\rangle^{J-1}\langle\nabla\rangle^{J-1}\Phi_{0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{J-1}\langle\nabla\rangle^{J-2}\Phi_{1}\|_{2}\big)\big(1+\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\|\langle x\rangle^{j+1}\nabla^{j}(\nabla N_{0},N_{1})\|_{2}\big).

In particular, the following also holds:

(C.3)

\displaystyle\|\partial\Gamma^{\leq J}\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}\lesssim\big(\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\langle\nabla\rangle^{J+1}\Phi_{0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\langle\nabla\rangle^{J}\Phi_{1}\|_{2}\big)\big(1+\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\|\langle x\rangle^{j+1}\nabla^{j}(\nabla N_{0},N_{1})\|_{2}\big),

(C.4)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\Gamma}\Gamma^{\leq J-1}n|_{t=0}\|_{2}\lesssim\big(\|\langle x\rangle^{J-1}\langle\nabla\rangle^{J-1}\Phi_{0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{J-1}\langle\nabla\rangle^{J-2}\Phi_{1}\|_{2}\big)\big(1+\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\|\langle x\rangle^{j+1}\nabla^{j}(\nabla N_{0},N_{1})\|_{2}\big).

Similarly, we also have

(C.5)

\displaystyle\|\langle x\rangle\log(\langle x\rangle)\Gamma^{\leq K}\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\big(\|\langle x\rangle^{K+1}\log(\langle x\rangle)\langle\nabla\rangle^{K}\Phi_{0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{K+1}\log(\langle x\rangle)\langle\nabla\rangle^{K-1}\Phi_{1}\|_{2}\big)\big(1+\sum_{j=0}^{K-2}\|\langle x\rangle^{j+1}\nabla^{j}(\nabla N_{0},N_{1})\|_{2}\big),

and

(C.6)

\displaystyle\|(\langle x\rangle^{\frac{3}{2}}\langle\nabla\rangle^{3}\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\leq K-4}n_{0})|_{t=0}\|_{1}+\|(\langle x\rangle^{\frac{3}{2}}\langle\nabla\rangle^{2}\partial_{t}\widetilde{\Gamma}^{\leq K-4}n_{0})|_{t=0}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{K-5}(\|\langle x\rangle^{j+\frac{5}{2}}\nabla^{j+3}(\nabla N_{0},N_{1})\|_{1}+\|\langle x\rangle^{j+\frac{5}{2}}\nabla^{j}(\nabla N_{0},N_{1})\|_{1}).

All the constants in the inequalities are exact and computable; for simplicity we identify all quantities related to the initial conditions by
C_{i}\varepsilon
, where
C_{i}
is a fixed exact positive constant that can be computed directly.

Proof.

The proof relies on an induction that is very similar to
[
6
]
, therefore we only sketch the idea. In fact the wave filed
n
is simpler to deal with because of the decomposition
n=n_{0}+A^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}n
so that the initial conditions
(N_{0},N_{1})
can be taken care of essentially through the homogeneous equation up to some higher order perturbation. For the Klein-Gordon field
\phi
, with no loss, we only consider the “worst” scenario as follows:

(C.7)

\displaystyle\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\partial_{t}^{J}\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}\lesssim\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\partial_{t}^{J-2}\Delta\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\partial_{t}^{J-2}\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\partial_{t}^{J-2}(\phi\partial n)|_{t=0}\|_{2}.

Note that by induction assumption we shall have

(C.8)

\displaystyle\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\partial_{t}^{J-2}\Delta\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\partial_{t}^{J-2}\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}\lesssim\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\langle\nabla\rangle^{J}\Phi_{0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\langle\nabla\rangle^{J-1}\Phi_{1}\|_{2}.

For the nonlinear part, we also only present the worst case:

(C.9)

\displaystyle\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\partial_{t}^{J-2}(\phi\partial n)|_{t=0}\|_{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\langle x\rangle^{J}(\partial_{t}^{\leq J-2}\phi)(\partial_{t}^{\leq\frac{J}{2}}n)|_{t=0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{J}(\partial_{t}^{\leq J-1}n)(\partial_{t}^{\leq\frac{J-2}{2}}\phi)|_{t=0}\|_{2}

(C.10)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\partial_{t}^{\leq J-2}\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}\|\partial_{t}^{\leq\frac{J}{2}}n|_{t=0}\|_{\infty}+\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\partial_{t}^{\leq\frac{J-2}{2}}\phi|_{t=0}\|_{\infty}\|\partial_{t}^{\leq{J-1}}n|_{t=0}\|_{2}.

Then by induction and the standard
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
embedding we have

(C.11)

\displaystyle\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\partial_{t}^{\leq J-2}\phi|_{t=0}\|_{2}\|\partial_{t}^{\leq\frac{J}{2}}n|_{t=0}\|_{\infty}

(C.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\langle\nabla\rangle^{J-2}\Phi_{0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\langle\nabla\rangle^{J-3}\Phi_{1}\|_{2})\sum_{j=0}^{\frac{J}{2}+1}\|\langle x\rangle^{j+1}\nabla^{j}(\nabla N_{0},N_{1})\|_{2}

and

(C.13)

\displaystyle\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\partial_{t}^{\leq\frac{J-2}{2}}\phi|_{t=0}\|_{\infty}\|\partial_{t}^{\leq{J-1}}n|_{t=0}\|_{2}

(C.14)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\langle\nabla\rangle^{\frac{J+2}{2}}\Phi_{0}\|_{2}+\|\langle x\rangle^{J}\langle\nabla\rangle^{\frac{J}{2}}\Phi_{1}\|_{2})\sum_{j=0}^{J-2}\|\langle x\rangle^{j+1}\nabla^{j}(\nabla N_{0},N_{1})\|_{2}.

The other cases follow from similar arguments and we omit the details.

∎

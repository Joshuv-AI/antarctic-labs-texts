2.1.
Modular Forms

Let
\mathbb{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \text{Im}(z)>0\}
,
be the upper half-plane
and let
\Gamma=G=\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})=\{\,\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\,\mid\,a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R}\text{ and }ad-bc=1\,\}

be the group of
2\times 2
-matrices. Let

\displaystyle\Gamma(N)

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\penalty\ \left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\penalty\ |\penalty\ \left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\equiv\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\

and is called the

level N
. A subgroup
H
of

is called a

if it contains
\Gamma(N)
.

For our purposes the following congruence subgroup is relevant:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}(N)

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\penalty\ \left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\penalty\ |\penalty\ c\equiv 0\pmod{N}\penalty\ \bigr\}.

Let
k,N\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma
be a congruence
subgroup of level
N
.
Let
k\in\mathbb{Z},\gamma\in\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and
f:\mathbb{H}\cup\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
.
We denote by

f^{[\gamma]_{k}}
the function whose value at

is
(cz+d)^{-k}f(\gamma(z))
, i.e.,
f^{[\gamma]_{k}}(z)=(cz+d)^{-k}f(\gamma(z))
.
We use the definition of modular forms according to
N. Koblitz

[
6
, p. 108]
.

Let us denote by
\mbox{$M$}_{2k}(\Gamma^{\prime})
the set of modular forms of weight
2k

for
\Gamma^{\prime}
, by
\mbox{$S$}_{2k}(\Gamma^{\prime})
the set of cusp forms of
weight
2k
for
\Gamma^{\prime}
and by
\mbox{$E$}_{2k}(\Gamma^{\prime})
the set of Eisenstein forms.
The sets
\mbox{$M$}_{2k}(\Gamma^{\prime}),\,\mbox{$S$}_{2k}(\Gamma^{\prime})
and
\mbox{$E$}_{2k}(\Gamma^{\prime})

are vector spaces over
\mathbb{C}
.
Therefore,
\mbox{$M$}_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
is the space of

2k
for

\Gamma_{0}(N)
,
\mbox{$S$}_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
is the space of
cusp forms of weight
2k
for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
,
and
\mbox{$E$}_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
is the space of Eisenstein forms.
The decomposition of the space of modular forms as a direct sum
of the space generated by the Eisenstein series and the space of cusp
forms, i.e.,

\mbox{$M$}_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))=\mbox{$E$}_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))\oplus\mbox{$S$}_{2k}(\Gamma_{0}(N))

is well-known; see for example

W. A. Stein

[

and

and

,
respectively.
W. A. Stein

[

2.2.
Eta Quotients

On the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}
, the Dedekind
\eta
-function,
\eta(z)
,
is defined by

\eta(z)=e^{\frac{2\pi iz}{24}}\overset{\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\prod}}(1-e^{2\pi inz})
.
Let us set

. Then it follows that

\eta(z)=q^{\frac{1}{24}}\overset{\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\prod}}(1-q^{n})=q^{\frac{1}{24}}F(q),\quad\text{where }F(q)=\overset{\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\prod}}(1-q^{n}).

Let

and

7

an

or

\underset{j|N}{\overset{}{\prod}}\eta(jz)^{e_{j}}
,
wherein
N
is the
level
of the eta product.
If
2k=\frac{1}{2}\underset{j=1}{\overset{\kappa}{\sum}}e_{j}
,
then an eta quotient

behaves like a modular form of weight

2k
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
with some multiplier system.

Note that eta function, eta quotient and eta product will use
interchangeably as synonyms.

[

[

]
.
This theorem will be effectively used

-quotients,

Theorem 2.1

(
M. Newman
and
G. Ligozat
)
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
D(N)
be the set of all positive divisors of

N
,
\delta\in D(N)
and
r_{\delta}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let furthermore

f(z)=\overset{}{\underset{\delta\in D(N)}{\prod}}\eta^{r_{\delta}}(\delta z)

be an eta quotient. If the following four conditions are satisfied

\displaystyle\text{{(i)}}\>\overset{}{\underset{\delta\in D(N)}{\sum}}\delta\,r_{\delta}\,\equiv 0\pmod{24},\qquad\text{{(ii)}}\>\overset{}{\underset{\delta\in D(N)}{\sum}}\frac{N}{\delta}\,r_{\delta}\,\equiv 0\pmod{24},

\displaystyle\text{{(iii)}}\>\overset{}{\underset{\delta\in D(N)}{\prod}}\delta^{r_{\delta}}\>\text{ is a square in }\mathbb{Q},\qquad\text{{(iv)}}\>\>\forall d\in D(N)\>\text{ it holds }\overset{}{\underset{\delta\in D(N)}{\sum}}\frac{\gcd{(\delta,d)}^{2}}{\delta}\,r_{\delta}\geq 0,

then

, where

.

Moreover, the
\eta
-quotient

is an element of

if
(iv)
is replaced by

(iv’)

\forall d\in D(N)

it holds

\mathbf{W_{(\boldsymbol{\upalpha,\upbeta})}^{2i-1,2j-1}(n)}

Given

Suppose that

\begin{split}W_{(\alpha,\beta)}^{2i-1,2j-1}(n)=&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{(k,l)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}}\\

{\delta\,\alpha_{1}\,k+\delta\,\beta_{1}\,l=n}\end{subarray}}\sigma_{2i-1}(k)\sigma_{2j-1}(l)\\
=&\,W_{(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})}^{2i-1,2j-1}(\frac{n}{\delta}).\end{split}

Therefore, we may simply assume that
\gcd{(\alpha,\beta)}=1

As an immediate consequence of the definition, we note that

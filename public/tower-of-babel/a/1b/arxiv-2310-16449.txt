Abstract
In this note we show that β ∗ ′ \beta^{\prime}_{*} , the β \beta -function slopes in the electric and magnetic theories are equal at the corresponding infrared fixed points. This follows from the scaling of the correlators of the trace of the energy momentum tensors. The slopes β ∗ ′ \beta^{\prime}_{*} determine the scaling dimensions. Our paper can be considered as a commentary to Anselmi et al. [ 1 ] – it proposes an improved derivation not based on a rather contrived construction by Kutasov et al. [ 2 ] . As a byproduct we note that γ Q ∗ ′ \gamma^{\prime}_{Q^{*}} – the slopes of the matter superfield anomalous dimension – vanish at both edges of the conformal window where one of the dual theories is strongly coupled. Finally, we determine the two-coupling magnetic fixed point at weak coupling correcting the result of [ 3 ] .

Introduction

Yang-Mills theories with
{\cal N}=1
supersymmetry

[
4

,
[
5

[

[

N_{f}

N_{f}

N_{f}^{2}
color-singlet mesons.
As is usual with dualities, when the original (electric) theory is weakly
coupled then the dual theory, referred to as magnetic, is strongly coupled, and vice versa.

In the conformal window (CW) which lies in the interval

\frac{3}{2}N_{c}\leq N_{f}\leq 3N_{c}\;,

(1)

both theories are asymptotically free and conformal in the IR due to an IR fixed point. We will limit ourselves to the window
(
1

N_{f,c}\gg 1

N_{f}/N_{c}
fixed.
The lower and upper boundaries are referred to as the CW edges.
Above the upper edge the electric theory becomes free while at
N_{f}<\tfrac{3}{2}N_{c}
the same transition happens in the magnetic theory. The edges can be obtained either
from the NSVZ beta functions
[
8
,
9
]
or from the unitarity bound
[
10
]
.
Recently an alternative interpretation has been given in terms of a smooth matching
to the chirally broken phase with pion physics
[
11
]
.

In this paper we will focus on the slopes of the
\beta

∗

\beta_{*}^{\prime}=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\beta\right|_{*}\,.

(2)

Since the above slope is related to the scaling dimension of a physically observable operator, the trace of the energy-momentum tensor (TEMT),
which has a geometrical meaning, the slopes in the electric and magnetic theories must coincide,

\left.\beta_{*}^{\prime}\right|_{\mathrm{e}l}=\left.\beta_{*}^{\prime}\right|_{\mathrm{m}ag}\,,

(3)

N_{f}
from the CW. For a

For a generic local operator
O(x)

the two-point function
\langle O(x)O(0)\rangle
in the conformal limit takes the form
(x^{2})^{-\Delta_{O}}

where
\Delta_{O}
is the sum of the normal (engineering) and anomalous dimensions of the operator
O
,

\Delta_{O}=d_{O}+\gamma_{O}\;.

(13)

The engineering dimension of TEMT obviously is
d_{G^{2}}=4
. We will focus on the anomalous dimension.

The energy-momentum tensor
\theta_{\alpha\beta}

is symmetric and conserved and has a geometric nature.
This tensor itself does not change under the variation of
\mu
, as we descend from the UV to the IR.
Hence, its trace is not renormalized either. The particular expression for
\theta^{\rho}_{\rho}
depends on how we normalize
the gluon field stress tensor, but the final result for the
x
scaling dependence near the conformal point
is unabiguously determined by the theory and is physical,

\langle{\mathcal{O}}_{G}(x){\mathcal{O}}_{G}(0)\rangle\propto\frac{1}{(x^{2})^{\Delta_{G^{2}}}}\,,\qquad\Delta_{G^{2}}=d_{G^{2}}+\gamma_{G^{2}}\,,

(14)

where
d_{G^{2}}
is the engineering dimension of the operator
{\mathcal{O}}_{G}
and
\gamma_{G^{2}}
is the anomalous dimension of
{\mathcal{O}}_{G}
.

Without loss of generality we can choose
{\mathcal{O}}_{G}
normalized in accordance with Eq. (
4
). The TEMT is given in (
10
)-(
12
)
where the
\beta
function in the electric theory is presented in (
8
). Differentiating both sides over
L=\log\mu
we arrive at

0={\mathcal{O}}_{G}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial L}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha^{2}}\right)+\frac{\beta}{\alpha^{2}}\frac{\partial{\mathcal{O}}_{G}}{\partial L}\,,

(15)

which implies, in turn,

\beta(\alpha){\mathcal{O}}_{G}\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\left(\frac{\beta}{\alpha^{2}}\right)-\gamma_{G^{2}}\,{\mathcal{O}}_{G}\frac{\beta}{\alpha^{2}}=0\,.

(16)

Taking into account that
\beta(\alpha_{*})=0
while
\alpha_{*}\neq 0
we find the well-known result

\left(\gamma_{G^{2}}\right)_{*}=\left.\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial\alpha}\right|_{*}\equiv\beta^{\prime}_{*}\,.

(17)

Finally we can present the two-point function of the TEMTs at the points
x
and
0
. Note that the
x
dependence is fully determined by the

14

\displaystyle\langle\theta^{\rho}_{\rho}(x)\theta^{\rho}_{\rho}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\left[\left.\left(16\pi\,\alpha^{2}\right)^{-1}\beta(\alpha)\right|_{\mu}\right]^{2}\langle{\mathcal{O}}_{G}(x)\,{\mathcal{O}}_{G}(0)\rangle_{\mu}

(18)

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle\left[\left.\left(16\pi\,\alpha^{2}\right)^{-1}\beta(\alpha)\right|_{\mu}\right]^{2}\,\frac{1}{(x^{2})^{4}}\,\frac{1}{(x^{2}\mu^{2})^{\beta^{\prime}_{*}}}\;.

The
\mu

\mu^{-2\beta_{*}^{\prime}}

At the conformal point
\mu\to 0

P=\left.\left(16\pi\,\alpha^{2}\right)^{-1}\beta(\alpha)\right|_{\mu}\,.

5
Relation between
\gamma^{\prime}_{Q^{*}}
and
\beta^{\prime}_{*}

The
\beta
function of the electric theory (
8
) is essentially a relation between the
matter-field anomalous dimension
\gamma_{Q}
and the
\beta
function itself. Since we have gained information
on
\beta^{\prime}_{*}
we may exploit this fact to deduce information on
(\gamma_{Q}^{\prime})_{*}
by directly differentiating at the IR fixed point

\beta^{\prime}_{*}=-\frac{\alpha^{2}_{*}}{2\pi}\,\,\frac{N_{f}}{1-\frac{\alpha_{*}}{2\pi}N_{c}}\,\left(\gamma^{\prime}_{Q}\right)_{*}\;.

(44)

Hence,
\beta^{\prime}_{*}
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{Q^{*}}
are proportional to each other throughout the CW with a coefficient depending on the unknown critical coupling
\alpha_{*}
.
We note that the relation (
44
) is generally scheme-dependent and so is the fixed point coupling
\alpha_{*}
but
\beta^{\prime}_{*}
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{Q^{*}}
are scheme-independent under analytic redefinitions of the coupling, cf. footnote
3
.
The fact that both
\beta^{\prime}_{*}
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{Q^{*}}
are zero simultaneously might not be completely accidental.
They both describe the perturbation around the fixed point for a gauge theory with massive mater, e.g.
[
16
]
.
We remind the reader that
\gamma_{Q}
equals minus the anomalous dimension of the mass
to all orders in perturbation theory in
{\cal N}=1
supersymmetry.

With (
3
) one obtains a strong coupling relation but unfortunately the right hand side
contains the two unknowns
\alpha_{*}
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{Q^{*}}
for which we cannot solve simultaneously.
Nevertheless, one can deduce interesting information.
Since,
\beta^{\prime}_{*}
as a function of
N_{f}
starts at zero for
N_{f}=3N_{c}
and then raises and lowers towards
zero again at
N_{f}=\frac{3}{2}N_{c}
, there must be at least two number of flavours,

N_{f}^{(w)}>N_{f}^{(s)}
, for which

\beta^{\prime}_{*}|_{N_{f}^{(w)}}=\beta^{\prime}_{*}|_{N_{f}^{(s)}}\;,

(45)

holds (the superscripts
w
and
s
stand for weak and strongly coupled with regards to the electric coupling).
As we expect the electric coupling to become continuously

Conclusions

In this paper we have shown that the slopes of the
\beta
function at the IR fixed point,
are equal to each other (
3
) in the electric and the magnetic theories of the Seiberg duality.
This result was derived some time ago using the Konishi currents and the Kutasov construction
[
1
]
.
We found a simpler way to obtain this result by matching the two-point function
of the trace of the energy momentum tensor in the electric and magnetic theory. By the very assumption of
the Seiberg duality such a geometric quantity has to match and since its scaling is governed by

\beta^{\prime}_{*}
the result follows.

In passing we obtained
a new relation between between
\gamma^{\prime}_{Q^{*}}
and
\beta^{\prime}_{*}
given in Eq. (
44
).
The RG flow near the edge of the conformal window previously discussed is
[
3
]
is corrected.
We obtain in addition the corresponding eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the flow in the magnetic theory.
These results might be useful in that
\beta^{\prime}_{*}
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{Q^{*}}
are the quantities that
describe perturbations around a fixed point in a gauge theory with matter.

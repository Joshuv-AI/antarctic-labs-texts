Lemma 4
.

r_{0}
is strictly decreasing.

Proof.

Suppose

\mu_{t_{2}}(r_{0}(t_{1}))=\left\|\left(\frac{1-r_{0}(t_{1})}{a_{1}},\frac{r_{0}(t_{1})}{a_{2}}\right)\right\|_{t_{2}}<\left\|\left(\frac{1-r_{0}(t_{1})}{a_{1}},\frac{r_{0}(t_{1})}{a_{2}}\right)\right\|_{t_{1}}=\mu_{t_{1}}(r_{0}(t_{1}))=\frac{1}{a_{2}}=\mu_{t_{2}}(r_{0}(t_{2}))

Therefore, by lemma

.

Lemma 5
.

\text{range}(r_{0})=[r_{0}(\infty),r_{0}(1)]=[\frac{a_{2}-a_{1}}{a_{2}},1]

Proof.

Fix

. So

Proof.

We have that

\mu_{\infty}\left(\frac{a_{2}-a_{1}}{a_{2}}\right)=\left\|\left(\frac{1-\frac{a_{2}-a_{1}}{a_{2}}}{a_{1}},\frac{a_{2}-a_{1}}{a_{2}^{2}}\right)\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\left(\frac{1}{a_{2}},\frac{a_{2}-a_{1}}{a_{2}^{2}}\right)\right\|_{\infty}

Since
\frac{a_{2}-a_{1}}{a_{2}^{2}}<\frac{a_{2}}{a_{2}^{2}}=\frac{1}{a_{2}}
, we have
\left\|\left(\frac{1}{a_{2}},\frac{a_{2}-a_{1}}{a_{2}^{2}}\right)\right\|_{\infty}=\frac{1}{a_{2}}
. So
r_{0}(\infty)=\frac{a_{2}-a_{1}}{a_{2}}
.

Now, because
(a_{2}-a_{1})(a_{2}+a_{1})=a_{2}^{2}-a_{1}^{2}<a_{2}^{2}
,
r_{0}(\infty)=\frac{a_{2}-a_{1}}{a_{2}}<\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}+a_{2}}
. So by lemma
4
and part 2 of corollary
1
, there exists
T_{1}
such that if
t\geq T_{1}
,
r_{0}(t)<\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}+a_{2}}
. Let
r:=r_{0}(T_{1})
. By corollary
2
, there exists
T_{2}
such that if
t\geq T_{2}
,
\mu_{t}^{\prime}(r)<-\frac{1}{a_{2}}
.

Suppose
t>\max(T_{1},T_{2})
. By lemma
4
,
r_{0}
is decreasing in
t
, so
r_{0}(t)<r_{0}(T_{1})=r
. By lemma
1
,
\mu_{t}^{\prime}
is increasing in
r
, so
P(t)<\mu_{t}^{\prime}(r)
. As
t>T_{2}
,
\mu_{t}^{\prime}(r)<-\frac{1}{a_{2}}
. Therefore, if
t\in(T,\infty)
, then
|P(t)|>\frac{1}{a_{2}}
. By Corollary
1
,
r_{0}^{-1}(\mathbb{Q})
is dense in
(T,\infty)
, so
\mathcal{T}
is dense in
(T,\infty)
.
∎

7
Conclusion

We conclude with the main theorem and some closing remarks on open work.

Theorem 1
.

For all
S:=\langle a_{1},a_{2}\rangle
,
\mathcal{P}\subseteq r_{0}^{-1}(\mathbb{Q})
. It is countable, but dense in some ray
(T,\infty)
.

Proof.

By propositions
1
and
2
, if
r_{0}(t)\not\in\mathbb{Q}
, then
\Delta_{t}(S)
is dense in
(0,|a_{1}a_{2}P(t)|)
and
(|a_{1}a_{2}P(t)|,a_{2})
, meaning
\Delta_{t}(S)
is dense in
[0,a_{2}]
. So
\mathcal{P}\subseteq r_{0}^{-1}(\mathbb{Q})
. By corollary
1
,
r_{0}^{-1}(\mathbb{Q})
is countable, so
\mathcal{P}
is countable.

Furthermore, by proposition
3
,

\mathcal{T}:=\left\{t:r_{0}(t)\in\mathbb{Q};~|P(t)|\geq\frac{1}{a_{2}}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{P}

. By proposition
4
,
\mathcal{T}
is dense in some ray
(T,\infty)
. So
\mathcal{P}
is dense in some ray
(T,\infty)
.
∎

This theorem handles some notable special cases.

Corollary 3
.

If
t\in(2,\infty)\cap\mathbb{Q}
, then
t
is dull.

Proof.

An elementary extension of Fermat’s Last Theorem
[
5
]
shows that there are no rational solutions to
x^{t}+y^{y}=z^{t}
with
x,y,z
rational and
t\in(2,\infty)\cap\mathbb{Q}
. Therefore, if
t\in(2,\infty)\cap\mathbb{Q}
, then
\left(\frac{1-r}{a_{1}}\right)^{t}+\left(\frac{r}{a_{2}}\right)^{t}=\left(\frac{1}{a_{2}}\right)^{t}
only if
\frac{1-r}{a_{1}}
or
\frac{r}{a_{2}}
equal
0
. This means
r=1
or
0
; by inspection,
r=1
is the only value that works. But by lemma
3
,
r_{0}(t)<r_{0}(1)=1
, so
r_{0}(t)
cannot be rational.
∎

It is, however, possible to engineer some irrational perspicacious examples. We will compute the gaps in the
t
-delta set induced by such an example.

Example 1
.

We first show that in a general numerical semigroup
\langle a_{1},a_{2}\rangle
,
r_{0}(log_{\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}+a_{2}}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=\left(\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}+a_{2}}\right)
. Let
t_{0}:=log_{\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}+a_{2}}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)
. Then

\mu_{t_{0}}\left(\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}+a_{2}}\right)=\left(\frac{1}{a_{2}}\right)\cdot\left(\left(\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}}\left(1-\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}+a_{2}}\right)\right)^{t_{0}}+\left(\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}+a_{2}}\right)^{t_{0}}\right)^{1/t_{0}}=\left(\frac{1}{a_{2}}\right)\left(2\left(\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}+a_{2}}\right)^{t_{0}}\right)^{1/t_{0}}

=\left(\frac{1}{a_{2}}\right)\left(\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}+a_{2}}\right)\left(2\right)^{1/t_{0}}=\left(\frac{1}{a_{2}}\right)\left(\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}+a_{2}}\right)\left(\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}+a_{2}}\right)^{-1}=\frac{1}{a_{2}}

as needed.

In particular, setting
S:=\langle 2,7\rangle
and
t=log_{\frac{7}{9}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)
gives
r_{0}(t)=\frac{7}{9}
. By inspection,
P(t)\approx-0.2298<-\frac{1}{7}
, so
a_{1}a_{2}\left|P(t)\right|>a_{1}
. As
r_{0}(t)
is rational as well, by proposition
3
,
t
is perspicacious.

By proposition
3
, if
d\in(2,|14P(t)|)-\Lambda
, where
\Lambda:=\left\{\frac{\lambda_{1}\mu_{t}^{\prime}(r_{0})}{9}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{7}:\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
, then
d
is not a limit point of
\Delta_{t}(S)
. (Note that
14P(t)\approx 3.2172
, so this set is nonempty.) A relatively large gap in
\Lambda\cap(2,|14P(t)|)
occurs between
\frac{58P(t)}{9}+\frac{4}{7}
and
\frac{19P(t)}{9}+\frac{11}{7}
; accordingly, let

d:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(\frac{58P(t)}{9}+\frac{4}{7}\right)+\left(\frac{19P(t)}{9}+\frac{11}{7}\right)\right)\approx 2.05446

Following the proof of proposition
3
, let
\epsilon
be one fourth of the distance from
d
to the closest of
2,|14P(t)|,\Lambda
, which, in this case, is

\epsilon:=\frac{1}{4}\left(d-\left(\frac{58P(t)}{9}+\frac{4}{7}\right)\right)\approx 0.0005250

. According to the proof of proposition
3
, if
x
is big enough that
\frac{k_{t}(2\cdot 7)^{2}}{x}<\epsilon
(i.e.,
\frac{196k_{t}}{\epsilon}<x
), then
\Delta_{t}(x)
is disjoint to
(d-\epsilon,d+\epsilon)
. By
[
7
]
,
k_{t}\leq\left|\frac{\sup\{\mu^{\prime\prime}_{t}([0,1])\}}{2}\right|
. Numerical approximations bound this expression above by
1.5
, meaning if
x>\frac{196\cdot 1.5}{\epsilon}
(i.e.,
x>560001
), then
\Delta_{t}(x)
is disjoint to
(d-\epsilon,d+\epsilon)
. Brute force computation of
\Delta_{t}(x)
for
x\in[0,560001]
shows that
\Delta_{t}(S)
is completely disjoint to
(d-\epsilon,d+\epsilon)
, an interval with width approximately
0.00105
.

Remark 1
.

We have shown that there is
T
where
r_{0}^{-1}(\mathbb{Q})\cap(T,\infty)\subseteq\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{P}\subseteq r_{0}^{-1}(\mathbb{Q})\cap(1,\infty)
, but have not computed
T
or shown whether any of these containments are strict. We also have no knowledge of
r_{0}^{-1}(\mathbb{Q})
beyond its density in
(1,\infty)
, as we have no closed form for
r_{0}
or
r_{0}^{-1}
. As such, though we know that almost all
t\in(1,\infty)
are dull, (including the special in corollary
3
), we cannot, in general, determine whether any particular
t
is dull or perspicacious.

Notably, some algebraic manipulation reveals that for all
S
,
r_{0}(2)=\frac{a_{2}^{2}-a_{1}^{2}}{a_{2}^{2}+a_{1}^{2}}\in\mathbb{Q}
, and that
|\mu_{2}^{\prime}(r_{0}(2))|=\frac{1}{a_{2}}
exactly. Therefore,
2\not\in\mathcal{T}
, but
2\in r_{0}^{-1}(\mathbb{Q})
, so our results cannot determine the perspicacity of
2
.

We also remark that experimental evidence suggests that for all
S
,
P(t)
is increasing in
t
on all of
(1,\infty)
. This would imply that
|P(t)|>\frac{1}{a_{2}}
iff
t>2
, and thus that
\mathcal{T}=r_{0}^{-1}(\mathbb{Q})\cap(2,\infty)
.

Furthermore, we suspect that
\mathcal{P}=r_{0}^{-1}(\mathbb{Q})
, i.e., that
t
is perspicacious if
r_{0}(t)\in\mathbb{Q}
, no matter whether
|a_{1}a_{2}P(t)|>a_{1}
(whether
t\in\mathcal{T}
).

Finally, aside from some limited preliminary results by Cyrusian et. al.,
[
3
]
the question of the limit points of
\Delta_{t}(S)
for
t\in(1,\infty)
and
S
with higher embedding dimension remain unknown.

From the Berest conjecture to the Dixmier conjecture

In this section we’ll prove our first result:

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume there exists a non-zero polynomial
f(X,Y)\in K[X,Y]
, which has a non-trivial commuting solution pair
(P,Q)\in A_{1}^{2}
, and the number of orbits under the group action of
Aut(A_{1})
on solutions of
f
in
A_{1}^{2}
is finite. Then the Dixmier conjecture holds, i.e
\forall\varphi\in End(A_{1})-\{0\}
,
\varphi
is an automorphism.

The proof will follow from some preliminary lemmas. First recall one definition, cf.
[
5

Definition 3.1
.

The Newton Polygon
N
is called of subrectangular type, if
N
is contained in the rectangle constructed by
\{(0,0),(0,k),(l,0),(l,k)\}
, and
(l,k)
is the vertex of
N
with
l,k\geq 1
.

Let
\varphi
be an endomorphism of
A_{1}
. If the Newton Polygons of
\varphi(\partial),\varphi(x)
are of subrectangular type, then we call such
\varphi
of subrectangular type (a specially condition of monomial type).

It is known (see
[
5
, Prop. 6.9]
) that for given
\varphi\in\mathop{\rm End}(A_{1})\backslash\mathop{\rm Aut}(A_{1})
there is an automorphism
\psi
such that
\psi\circ\varphi
is of subrectangular type.
So, from now on, we can always assume that
\varphi\in\mathop{\rm End}(A_{1})
is of the subrectangular type. It’s easy to see that for any positive pair
(\sigma,\rho)

f_{\sigma,\rho}(\varphi(\partial))
is a monomial. In contrast, the Newton polygon of an automorphism
\Phi
has the following properties.

Lemma 3.1
.

Any automorphism of
A_{1}
can’t be of subrectangular type. More precisely: if
\Phi\in Aut(A_{1})
, with
\Phi(\partial)=\sum a_{ij}x^{i}\partial^{j}
, and
n=sup\{i|\quad\exists j,a_{ij}\neq 0\}
,
m=sup\{j|\quad\exists i,a_{ij}\neq 0\}
, then

1.

(0,n),(m,0)\in E(\Phi(\partial))
;

2.

\forall i,j>0,(i,n),(m,j)\notin E(\Phi(\partial))
; in particular,
f_{n,m}(\Phi(\partial),\Phi(x))
are not monomials;

3.

one of the rate
\frac{m}{n}
or
\frac{n}{m}
must be an integer.

The proof is contained e.g. in
[
4
, L. 8.7]
and
[
5
, Prop. 6.3]

Definition 3.2
.

Suppose
\varphi
is an endomorphism of subrectangular type. Let
(\sigma,\rho)
be any two positive integer numbers.

Assume

are non-zero integers,

is proportional with

. Define
\epsilon(\varphi)\in\mathbf{Q}

(l^{\prime},k^{\prime})=\epsilon(\varphi)(l,k)

We’ll call this
\epsilon(\varphi)

\varphi(\partial)

\varphi(x)

\epsilon(\varphi)

(\sigma,\rho)

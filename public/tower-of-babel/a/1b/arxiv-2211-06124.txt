Optimal regularity and fine asymptotics for the porous medium equation in bounded domains
Tianling Jin, Xavier Ros-Oton, Jingang Xiong

Abstract
We prove the optimal global regularity of nonnegative solutions to the porous medium equation in smooth bounded domains with the zero Dirichlet boundary condition after certain waiting time T ∗ T^{*} . More precisely, we show that solutions are C 2 , α ​ ( Ω ¯ ) C^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}) in space, with α = 1 m \alpha=\frac{1}{m} , and C ∞ C^{\infty} in time (uniformly in x ∈ Ω ¯ x\in\overline{\Omega} ), for t > T ∗ t>T^{*} . Furthermore, this allows us to refine the asymptotics of solutions for large times, improving the best known results so far in two ways: we establish a faster rate of convergence O ⁡ ( t − 1 − γ ) O(t^{-1-\gamma}) , and we prove that the convergence holds in the C 1 , α ​ ( Ω ¯ ) C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}) topology. Keywords : Porous medium equations, regularity, asymptotics. MSC (2010) : Primary 35B65; Secondary 35K59, 35K67.

Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 1
and let
m>1
. Consider the porous medium equation (PME):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}\partial_{t}u-\Delta(u^{m})&=&0\qquad\mbox{ in }\ \Omega\times(0,\infty)\\
u&=&0\qquad\mbox{ on }\ \partial\Omega\times(0,\infty)\\
u(x,0)&=&u_{0}(x)\geq 0.\end{array}\right.

(1)

The equation (
1
) is a slow diffusion equation, which means that if
u_{0}
is compactly supported in
\Omega
, then the solution
u(\cdot,t)
with such initial data will still be compactly supported in
\Omega
at least for a short time, and thus,
\partial\{u>0\}
is a free boundary.
Due to the degeneracy of the equation near
\{u=0\}
, the initial boundary value problem (
1
) does not possess in general a classical solution (see, e.g., Oleinik-Kalashnikov-Chzou
[
51
]
). Thus, it is necessary to work with a suitable class of weak solutions. We say that
u
is a nonnegative weak solution of (
1
) if
u\in C([0,+\infty):L^{1}(\Omega))
such that
u^{m}\in L^{2}_{loc}([0,+\infty):H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
and
u
satisfies (
1
) in the sense of distribution. The initial boundary value problem (
1
) is then well-posed for such weak solutions.
Hölder continuity of the weak solutions and their free boundaries were proved by Caffarelli-Friedman
[
19
]
. Their higher regularities were proved, for example, by Caffarelli-Vázquez-Wolanski
[
20
]
, Caffarelli-Wolanski
[
21
]
, Daskalopoulos-Hamilton
[
25
]
, Koch
[
43
]
, Daskalopoulos-Hamilton-Lee
[

]
and Kim-Lee
[
42
]
under a non-degeneracy condition of the initial data. Kienzler-Koch-Vázquez
[
40
]
proved the smoothness of the weak solution and the free boundary for all large times.
The one spatial dimension case has been studied earlier in, e.g., Aronson
[
4
,
5
]
, Knerr
[
44
]
, Aronson-Caffarelli-Vázquez
[
7
]
, Aronson-Vázquez
[

]

[

]
, and Angenent
[
2
]
, from which we know that the free boundary interface is eventually analytic for the equation (

with compactly supported initial data.
Several universal estimates for the porous medium equation have also been obtained by Aronson-Bénilan
[
6
]
and Dahlberg-Kenig
[
24
]
.
We refer to Daskalopoulos-Kenig
[

An explicit solution to the PME which often plays an important role is the so-called

, given by

U(x,t):=t^{-\frac{1}{m-1}}S(x),

(2)

where

is the unique nontrivial nonnegative solution of

-\Delta(S^{m})={\textstyle\frac{1}{m-1}}S\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in }\Omega\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002ptS=0\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{on }\partial\Omega.

(3)

The existence and uniqueness of this

[

]

(4)

See also Proposition 1.3 of Vázquez

1.1
Optimal regularity of solutions

Aronson-Peletier
[
8
]
proved that there exists some waiting time
T^{*}
such that any nontrivial solution
u
of (
1
) will be positive in
\Omega
for all time afterwards, and moreover

u\asymp d^{1/m}\asymp S\hskip 10.00002pt\textrm{for}\hskip 10.00002ptt>T^{*}.

(5)

A uniform estimate of
T^{*}
is given by Bonforte-Vázquez
[
18
]
. See also Bonforte-Figalli-Vázquez
[
15
]
.
This, combined with interior regularity estimates, implies that
u(\cdot,t)\in C^{1/m}(\overline{\Omega})
for all
t>T^{*}
(see
[
14
]
) and that

u^{m}(\cdot,t)\in{\rm Lip}(\overline{\Omega})\hskip 10.00002pt\textrm{for all}\ t>T^{*}.

(6)

A long standing open question in this context is the following:

Are solutions to the PME (
1
) classical up to the boundary for
t>T^{*}
?

In other words, is
u^{m}(\cdot,t)\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
for all
t>T^{*}
?

The goal of this paper is to answer this question, and to obtain the optimal regularity near
\partial\Omega
for solutions to (
1
).
Furthermore, as explained below, this will allow us to obtain finer asymptotics for large times
t\to\infty
.

Our first result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be any bounded smooth domain,

u_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega)

u_{0}\not\equiv 0

be the first time for which (

Then,

u^{m}(\cdot,t)\in C^{2+\frac{1}{m}}(\overline{\Omega})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all }t>T^{*},

(7)

and

u^{m}(x,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}((T^{*},\infty))\hskip 10.00002pt\textrm{uniformly in}\ x\in\overline{\Omega}.

Moreover, (

2+\frac{1}{m}+\varepsilon
for any
\varepsilon>0

Furthermore, we also have

\partial_{t}^{k}(u^{m})(\cdot,t)\in C^{2+\frac{1}{m}}(\overline{\Omega})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all }t>T^{*}

for all

Notice that, for any

.
This is actually false even for the friendly giant solution

Since

1.2
Long time behavior

As said before, Aronson-Peletier
[
8
]
proved that there exists a waiting time
T^{*}\geq 0
for which (
5
) holds.
Concerning the large time behavior
t\to\infty
, they showed that there exists
\tau>0
such that

u(x,t)\geq(\tau+t)^{-\frac{1}{m-1}}S(x)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in }\overline{\Omega}\times(T^{*},+\infty),

(9)

where
S
is given by (
3
). Consequently, they proved the stability of the friendly giant solution (
2
) in the sense that

\left\|\frac{u(\cdot,t)}{U(\cdot,t)}-1\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C}{t}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for }t>T^{*},

(10)

where
C>0
depends only on
n,m,u_{0}
and
\Omega
.
The decay rate in (
10
) is optimal by considering the particular solution
u_{s}(x,t)=(s+t)^{\frac{1}{1-m}}S(x)
for arbitrary
s>0
.
See Theorem 1.1 in Vázquez
[
54
]
for another similar stability result of the separable solution to the porous medium equation (
1
) with general initial data and general domains, Theorem 5.8 in Bonforte-Grillo-Vázquez
[
16
]
for another proof using a new entropy method, and also Theorems 2.5 and 2.6 in Bonforte-Sire-Vázquez
[
17
]
for more general porous medium type equations.

Here, thanks to our fine boundary estimates from Theorem
1.1
, we can establish finer estimates for the long time behavior of solutions to the PME.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be any bounded smooth domain,
m>1
, and
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\Omega)
with
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
.
Let
u
be the nonnegative weak solution of (
1
), and
T^{*}
be the first time for which (
5
) holds. Let
\delta>0
.

Then, there exist constants
A_{1}\geq 0
,
\tau^{*}\geq 0
,
C_{1}>0
and
\gamma>0
such that

\left\|t^{\frac{m}{m-1}}u^{m}(\cdot,t)-S^{m}+\frac{A_{1}}{t}S^{m}\right\|_{C^{2+\frac{1}{m}}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{t^{1+\gamma}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all }t>T^{*}+\delta

(11)

and

\left\|\frac{u(\cdot,t)}{U(\cdot,\tau^{*}+t)}-1\right\|_{C^{1+\frac{1}{m}}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq\frac{C_{1}}{t^{1+\gamma}}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all }t>T^{*}+\delta,

(12)

where
S
and
U
are given by (
3
) and (
2
), respectively,
C_{1}
depends only on
n,m,\delta,\Omega
and
u_{0}
,
A_{1}
and
\tau^{*}
depend only on
n,m,\Omega
and
u_{0}
, while
\gamma
depends only on
n
,
m
, and
\Omega
.
In particular,

\textrm{in the dimension}\hskip 10.00002ptn=1\hskip 10.00002pt\textrm{we have}\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma=1.

Furthermore, for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
C_{2}>0
depending only on
n,m,\delta,\Omega,\ell
and
u_{0}
such that

\|\partial_{t}^{\ell}(u^{m})(\cdot,t)\|_{C^{2+\frac{1}{m}}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C_{2}t^{-\frac{m}{m-1}-\ell}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all }t>T^{*}+\delta.

(13)

This result improves substantially the best known results so far for the long time behavior of solutions to the PME.
Indeed, it not only improves Aronson-Peletier’s stability result (
10
) from the
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
topology to the
C^{1+\frac{1}{m}}(\overline{\Omega})
topology, but also gives a faster rate of convergence than (
10
), which we expect to be optimal.

Indeed, on the one hand, recall that the
C^{1+\frac{1}{m}}(\overline{\Omega})
regularity of the relative error
u/U
is optimal; see Corollary
1.2
.

On the other hand, as shown in the proof of Theorem
1.3
, the constant
\gamma
is determined by the second eigenvalue of the linearized operador of the equation
-\Delta\Theta=\frac{1}{m-1}\Theta^{1/m}
in
\Omega
, with the zero Dirichlet condition on
\partial\Omega
.
In particular,
\gamma>0
depends strongly on the domain
\Omega
, and in general we do not expect any lower bound on
\gamma>0
like the one we prove for the case of dimension
n=1
.

Finally, it is interesting to notice that, as explained in Remark
4.2
, by adding extra terms (involving higher eigenfunctions of such linearized operator) in the expansion (
11
), one could expand to arbitrary orders.

1.3
Strategy of the proof

As in many nonlinear PDE problems, in order to establish higher regularity of solutions one would like to use Schauder-type estimates and a bootstrap argument but, for this, some initial regularity is needed.

In our context, the a priori Schauder-type estimates we need were established by Kim-Lee
[
42
]
—for compatible initial data satisfying some nondegeneracy conditions—, using the methods of Daskalopoulos-Hamilton
[
25
]
.

The initial regularity we need in order to use the Schauder-type estimates, however, is

\mbox{the H\"{o}lder continuity of }\frac{u^{m}}{d}\mbox{ on }\overline{\Omega}\times(T^{*},+\infty).

(14)

This does not follow from previously known results, which give at best that such quotient is bounded — recall (
6
).

We prove (
14
) by using the ideas of a recent work of the first and last authors
[
39
]
on the fast diffusion equation (corresponding to
m\in(0,1)
in (
1
)), which solved a problem raised by Berryman and Holland
[
12
]
in 1980.
The rough idea is that we need to develop a De Giorgi iteration for a singular and degenerate nonlinear parabolic equation.
Moreover, while the equation in
[
39
]
corresponds to the case
m\in(0,1)
, here we need to treat the case
m>1
.

After proving (
14
), we establish the all time regularity of solutions with compatible initial data — by using a boostrap argument and the Schauder estimates from
[
42
]
—, and finally we need an appropriate approximation argument to establish the eventual regularity for solutions with general initial data.

Finally, to prove Theorem
1.3
, we find an equation for
t^{\frac{m}{m-1}}(u^{m}-U^{m})
(after a change of the time variable of the form
\tau:=\log t
), and prove that, up to errors that decay faster as
\tau\to\infty
, the solution is well approximated by an expansion involving the eigenfunctions of the linearized operator of the equation
-\Delta\Theta=\frac{1}{m-1}\Theta^{1/m}
in
\Omega
, with the zero Dirichlet condition on
\partial\Omega
.
Since the first eigenfunction turns out to be
\Theta
itself (this gives the constants
A_{1}
or
\tau^{*}
), then we get a rate of convergence which is dictated by the second eigenvalue of such operator, and this gives the constant
\gamma>0
.

Theorem 3.5

4

Theorem 4.1

Let
u_{0}\in C^{2}(\Omega)

Let

,

For every sufficiently small
\delta>0

\eta_{\delta}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

3

|\nabla v_{0,\delta}|\leq C_{0}.

Note that
v_{0,\delta}=S^{m}
near
\partial\Omega
. Hence,
v_{0,\delta}
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
3.7
. Therefore, there exists a unique positive function
v_{\delta}\in\mathscr{C}^{2+\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,+\infty))
satisfying that

pv^{p-1}_{\delta}\partial_{t}v_{\delta}=\Delta v_{\delta}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in }\Omega\times[0,+\infty),

v_{\delta}(\cdot,0)=v_{0,\delta},\hskip 10.00002ptv_{\delta}=0\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{on }\partial\Omega\times[0,+\infty),

and (
38
) holds. Consequently, by Theorem (
3.8
), for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
C_{1}>0
depending only on
n,\Omega,m,\varepsilon
and
c_{0}
but independent of
\delta
such that

\|v_{\delta}\|_{\mathscr{C}^{2+\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}\times[\varepsilon,+\infty))}\leq C_{1}.

Since

|v_{0,\delta}-v_{0}|\leq C(1-\phi_{\delta})d(x)\leq C\delta,

we have
v_{0,\delta}
converges to
v_{0}
uniformly on
\overline{\Omega}
as
\delta\to 0
. Hence, by the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem and the uniqueness of the weak solutions,
v_{\delta}\to v
locally uniformly on
\overline{\Omega}\times(0,+\infty)
. Hence,
v\in\mathscr{C}^{2+\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}\times[t_{0},+\infty))
for every
t_{0}>0
. The higher regularity then follows from Theorem
3.7
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the main results of this paper.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Theorem
4.1
, we have that
u^{m}(x,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}((T^{*},+\infty))
for every
x\in\overline{\Omega}
, and

\partial_{t}^{\ell}u^{m}(\cdot,t)\in C^{2+\frac{1}{m}}(\overline{\Omega})\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all }t>T^{*}\mbox{ and }\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\cup\{0\}.

In particular, we can take
\ell=0
to deduce (
7
).

The optimality of the exponent follows by recalling that the friendly giant solution (
2
) does not belong to
C^{2+\frac{1}{m}+\varepsilon}(\overline{\Omega})
for any
\varepsilon>0
.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Fix
t>T^{*}
.
Since both
u^{m}(\cdot,t)
and
S^{m}(\cdot,t)
are
C^{2+\frac{1}{m}}(\overline{\Omega})
and vanish linearly on the boundary
\partial\Omega
, it then follows that
u^{m}/S^{m}\in C^{1+\frac{1}{m}}(\overline{\Omega})
, and that
u^{m}/S^{m}\asymp 1
in
\Omega
.
Thus, the regularity for
u/S
follows by raising
u^{m}/S^{m}
to the power
\frac{1}{m}
.

Finally, the optimality of the exponent follows from Example
4.3
below.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Using Theorem
4.1
, the estimate (
13
) follows from (
4
), (
9
) and Theorem
3.8
with
v=u^{m}
and
p=\frac{1}{m}
. In the following, we will prove (
11
) and (
12
).

Let

\displaystyle\theta(x,\tau)

\displaystyle=t^{\frac{m}{m-1}}u^{m}(x,t)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with }t=e^{\tau}.

Then

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\theta^{p}

\displaystyle=\Delta\theta+\frac{p}{1-p}\theta^{p}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in }\Omega\times(0,\infty).

Consequently, the asymptotic expansion in (
10
) becomes

\left\|\frac{\theta(\cdot,\tau)}{\Theta(\cdot)}-1\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq Ce^{-\tau}\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all }\tau>1,

(41)

where

\Theta=S^{m}

(42)

satisfying

-\Delta\Theta-\frac{p}{1-p}\Theta^{p}=0\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in }\Omega\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\Theta=0\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{on }\partial\Omega.

(43)

Let

h=\theta-\Theta.

Then

\displaystyle p\theta^{p-1}h_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\Delta h+c(x,\tau)d(x)^{p-1}h\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in }\Omega\times(\log(T^{*}+1),\infty),

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=0\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{on }\partial\Omega\times(\log(T^{*}+1),\infty),

where

c=\int_{0}^{1}\left[\frac{s\theta+(1-s)\Theta}{d(x)}\right]^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Note that the function
c
shares the same regularity and the same estimates as those for
\theta
.
By Theorem
3.2
and elliptic Schauder estimates on each time slice, there exists
C>0
depending only on
n,\Omega,p
and
u_{0}
such that for all
T\geq\log T^{*}+2
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\tau\in[T-1,T]}\|h(\cdot,\tau)\|_{C^{2+p}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}\times[T-2,T])}\leq Ce^{-T},

where we used (
41
) in the last inequality. In particular,

\displaystyle\|\theta(\cdot,T)-\Theta\|_{C^{2+p}(\overline{\Omega})}=\|h(\cdot,T)\|_{C^{2+p}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq Ce^{-T}.

(44)

Since
\theta(\cdot,\tau)=\Theta(\cdot)=0
on
\partial\Omega\times[T^{*},\infty)
, we have for all
\tau\geq\log T^{*}+2
that

\left\|\frac{\theta(\cdot,\tau)-\Theta}{\Theta}\right\|_{C^{1+p}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\|\theta(\cdot,\tau)-\Theta\|_{C^{2+p}(\overline{\Omega})}=C\|h(\cdot,\tau)\|_{C^{2+p}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq Ce^{-\tau}.

(45)

Now let us obtain a higher order asymptotic expansion. This requires some spectrum analysis on the linearized equation of (
43
) in a similar way to those in Bonforte-Figalli
[
13
]
and Choi-McCann-Seis
[
23
]
for the fast diffusion equation. Let

L^{2}(\Omega;\Theta^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x)=\left\{f\in L^{2}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}f^{2}(x)\Theta^{p-1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x<+\infty\right\}.

For
f,g\in L^{2}(\Omega;\Theta^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x)
, we denote

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\Omega}f(x)g(x)\Theta^{p-1}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\|f\|=\sqrt{\langle f,f\rangle}.

Let

\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}=-\Delta-\frac{p^{2}}{1-p}\Theta^{p-1}

be the linearized operator of (
43
). Since
\Theta(x)/d(x)
is uniformly bounded from above and below by two positive constants and
p>0
, then the embedding
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega;\Theta^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x)
is compact, and thus,
[\Theta^{1-p}(-\Delta)]^{-1}
is a compact operator from
L^{2}(\Omega;\Theta^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x)
to itself. Therefore, the weighted eigenvalue problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}(\psi)&=&\mu\Theta^{p-1}\psi&\mbox{on }\Omega,\\
\Theta&=&0&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

(46)

admits eigenpairs
\{(\mu_{j},\psi_{j})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
such that

•

the eigenvalues with multiplicities can be listed as
\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}\leq\mu_{3}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{j}\to+\infty
as
j\to+\infty
,

•

the eigenfunctions
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
form a complete orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega;\Theta^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x)
, that is,
\langle\psi_{i},\psi_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
for
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover,
\psi_{1}
does not change signs, and thus, we assume that
\psi_{1}\geq 0
.

Since
\Theta
satisfies (
43
), then

\mu_{1}=p\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\psi_{1}=\frac{\Theta}{\|\Theta\|}=\frac{\Theta}{\|\Theta\|_{L^{p+1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{p+1}{2}}}.

(47)

The equation of
h
can be rewritten as

\begin{split}p\Theta^{p-1}h_{\tau}&=-\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}h+N(h)\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\times[T_{0},\infty),\\
h&=0\quad\mbox{on }\partial\Omega\times[T_{0},\infty),\end{split}

(48)

where

N(h)=\frac{p}{1-p}\Theta^{p}\left[\left(\frac{h}{\Theta}+1\right)^{p}-1-\frac{ph}{\Theta}\right]+p\Theta^{p-1}\left[1-\left(\frac{h}{\Theta}+1\right)^{p-1}\right]h_{\tau}.

It follows from (
40
) and (
45
) that for all
\tau\geq\tau_{0}
, where
\tau_{0}
is sufficiently large, we have

|N(h)|\leq C\Theta^{p-2}(h^{2}+hh_{\tau})\leq C\Theta^{p}e^{-2\tau}.

(49)

Therefore, for every
f\in L^{2}(\Omega;\Theta^{p-1}\,\mathrm{d}x)
, we have

\int_{\Omega}|N(h(x,\tau))||f(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq Ce^{-2\tau}\|f\|.

Since
\mu_{1}=p
, we can define
J
to be the largest positive integer such that
\mu_{J}<2p\leq\mu_{J+1}
. Multiplying
\psi_{j}
,
j=1,\cdots,J
, to (
48
) and integrating by parts, we obtain

\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\langle h,\psi_{j}\rangle+\frac{\mu_{j}}{p}\langle h,\psi_{j}\rangle\right|\leq Ce^{-2\tau}.

That is,

\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\left(e^{\frac{\mu_{j}}{p}\tau}\langle h,\psi_{j}\rangle\right)\right|\leq Ce^{-(2-\frac{\mu_{j}}{p})\tau}.

Hence, for any
\tau_{2}>\tau_{1}\geq\tau_{0}
, we have

\left|e^{\frac{\mu_{j}}{p}\tau_{2}}\langle h(\cdot,\tau_{2}),\psi_{j}\rangle-e^{\frac{\mu_{j}}{p}\tau_{1}}\langle h(\cdot,\tau_{1}),\psi_{j}\rangle\right|\leq Ce^{-(2-\frac{\mu_{j}}{p})\tau_{1}}.

Therefore, the limit
\lim_{\tau\to\infty}e^{\frac{\mu_{j}}{p}\tau}\langle h(\cdot,\tau),\psi_{j}\rangle
exists, and we denote it as
c_{j}
. Hence,

\langle h(\cdot,\tau),\psi_{j}\rangle=c_{j}e^{-\frac{\mu_{j}}{p}\tau}+O(e^{-2\tau}).

Let
z(\tau)=\|h-\sum_{j=1}^{J}\langle h(\cdot,\tau),\psi_{j}\rangle\psi_{j}\|
. Then we obtain a similar but just one side inequality from (
48
):

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}z(\tau)+\frac{\mu_{J+1}}{p}z(\tau)\leq Ce^{-2\tau}.

That is

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}\left(e^{\frac{\mu_{J+1}}{p}}z(\tau)\right)\leq Ce^{-(2-\frac{\mu_{J+1}}{p})\tau}.

Hence, for all
\tau\geq\tau_{0}
, we have

z(\tau)\leq\begin{cases}Ce^{-2\tau}&\text{ if }\mu_{J+1}>2p,\\
C\tau e^{-2\tau}&\text{ if }\mu_{J+1}=2p.\end{cases}

Therefore, we have

\left\|h(\cdot,\tau)-\sum_{j=1}^{J}c_{j}e^{-\frac{\mu_{j}}{p}\tau}\psi_{j}\right\|\leq\begin{cases}e^{-2\tau}&\text{ if }\mu_{J+1}>2p,\\
\tau e^{-2\tau}&\text{ if }\mu_{J+1}=2p.\end{cases}

(50)

In particular,

\left\|h(\cdot,\tau)-c_{1}e^{-\frac{\mu_{1}}{p}\tau}\psi_{1}\right\|\leq\begin{cases}Ce^{-\frac{\mu_{2}}{p}\tau}+Ce^{-2\tau}&\text{ if }\mu_{J+1}>2p,\\
Ce^{-\frac{\mu_{2}}{p}\tau}+C\tau e^{-2\tau}&\text{ if }\mu_{J+1}=2p.\end{cases}

(51)

By using (
47
), we know that there exists
\gamma>0
such that

\left\|\theta(\cdot,\tau)-\Theta+A_{1}e^{-\tau}\Theta\right\|\leq Ce^{-(1+\gamma)\tau},

where

A_{1}=-c_{1}\|\Theta\|^{-1}=-c_{1}\|\Theta\|_{L^{p+1}(\Omega)}^{-\frac{p+1}{2}}=-c_{1}\|S\|_{L^{m+1}(\Omega)}^{-\frac{m+1}{2}}.

By (
4
), we know that
h\leq 0
. Hence,
c_{1}\leq 0
, and thus,
A_{1}\geq 0
.
If we let

\widetilde{h}=\theta(\cdot,\tau)-\Theta+A_{1}e^{-\tau}\Theta,

then

\begin{split}p\Theta^{p-1}\widetilde{h}_{\tau}&=-\mathcal{L}_{\Theta}\widetilde{h}+N(h)\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\times[T_{0},\infty),\\
\widetilde{h}&=0\quad\mbox{on }\partial\Omega\times[T_{0},\infty).\end{split}

(52)

Since

\|\partial_{\tau}N(h(\cdot,\tau))\|_{C^{p}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq Ce^{-2\tau},

we obtain from Theorem
3.2
and Hölder’s inequality that

\|\widetilde{h}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{C^{2+p}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\|\widetilde{h}(\cdot,\tau)\|+Ce^{-2\tau}\leq Ce^{-(1+\gamma)\tau}.

Then (
11
) follows by changing the variables back to
u
and
t
, and (
12
) follows from (
11
).

Finally, in the dimension
n=1
case, as Berryman
[
11
]
observed,
\mu_{2}=\frac{3p}{1-p}
with the eigenfunction
\Theta\Theta^{\prime}
. Hence,
J=1
. Therefore, it follows from (
51
) that
\gamma=1
.

This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

In fact, we can keep expanding the solution up to an arbitrary order in the same way as that Han-Li-Li
[
34
]
did for the singular Yamabe equation. If
J\geq 2
, then
N(h)=C_{1}e^{-2\tau}\Theta^{p}+O(e^{-\frac{(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})\tau}{p}})\Theta^{p}=:N_{1}(h)+N_{2}(h)
, where
C_{1}
is a constant. Then one can solve the linear equation (
48
) with the forcing term
N(h)
replaced by
N_{1}(h)
and
N_{2}(h)
, respectively. The equation with
N_{1}(h)
has an explicit solution. Since
N_{2}(h)=O(e^{-\frac{(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})\tau}{p}})\Theta^{p}
, we can expand its solution up to the largest
K
such that
\mu_{K}<\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}
. If
J=1
, then
N(h)=N_{1}(h)+N_{2}(h)
with the same
N_{1}(h)
, and
N_{2}(h)=O(e^{-3\tau})\Theta^{p}
or
O(\tau e^{-3\tau})\Theta^{p}
. In each case,
N_{2}(h)
has strictly better decay than
N(h)
, and one can expand the solutions up to the largest
K
such that
\mu_{K}<3
. One can keep expanding
N(h)
and iterating this process to reach any desired order, which is ensured by the regularity of the solution
\theta
. In the final expansion, the exponential exponents are not only the
\{\mu_{j}/p\}
but also some of their linear combinations. In 1-D, an arbitrarily high order expansion for a special class of solutions under self-similar type coordinates has been obtained by Angenent
[
3
]
.

In the next example in one spatial dimension, we will show that the
C^{1+\frac{1}{m}}(\overline{\Omega})
regularity of the relative error
\frac{u^{m}(\cdot,t)}{S^{m}}
cannot be improved in a short time after
T^{*}
.

Example 4.3
.

Consider the equations (
1
) and (
3
) for
\Omega=(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}
. By the regularity of
S
, we have the expansion of
S^{m}
near
x=0
:

S^{m}=ax-\frac{a^{\frac{1}{m}}m^{2}}{(m-1)(m+1)(2m+1)}x^{2+\frac{1}{m}}+\mbox{higher order terms}

for some constant
a>0
.

Let
v_{0}
be a function such that

v_{0}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}&ax+\frac{a^{\frac{1}{m}}m^{2}}{(m-1)(m+1)(2m+1)}x^{2+\frac{1}{m}}&\quad\mbox{for }x\in[0,1/4],\\
&a(1-x)+\frac{a^{\frac{1}{m}}m^{2}}{(m-1)(m+1)(2m+1)}(1-x)^{2+\frac{1}{m}}&\quad\mbox{for }x\in[3/4,1],\end{array}\right.

and
v_{0}
is smooth and positive in
(1/8,7/8)
. Let
d(x)=\min(x,1-x)
for
x\in[0,1/4]\cup[3/4,1]
, and
d(x)
be smooth and positive in
(1/8,7/8)
. Then both
d^{1-p}(v_{0})^{\prime\prime}
and
d^{1-p}[d^{1-p}(v_{0})^{\prime\prime}]^{\prime\prime}
are Hölder continuous on
[0,1]
. Then by the Schauder estimate in Theorem
3.1
and the implicit function theorem, one can show that there exists
T>0
and a unique positive function
v\in\mathscr{C}^{2+\alpha}([0,1]\times[0,T])
satisfying that
v_{t}\in\mathscr{C}^{2+\alpha}([0,1]\times[0,T])
and

pv^{p-1}\partial_{t}v=\Delta v\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{in }[0,1]\times[0,T],

v(\cdot,0)=v_{0},\hskip 10.00002ptv=0\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{on }\{0,1\}\times[0,T].

This solution
v
is more regular in the time variable than the one obtained by Theorem
3.3
. This is achievable because the initial condition
v_{0}
is more regular. In fact, such a solution
v
can be obtained by a second approximation (in the time variable) in a similar way to that in Theorem 3.2 of
[
37
]
.

Let
u=v^{1/m}
. Then by the regularity of
u^{m}
and
(u^{m})_{t}
, we have near
x=0
that

\displaystyle u^{m}

\displaystyle=A(t)x+O(x^{1+\alpha}),

(53)

\displaystyle(u^{m})_{t}

\displaystyle=B(t)x+O(x^{1+\alpha})

for some positive continuous functions
A(t)
and
B(t)
on
[0,1]
with
A(0)=a
and
B(0)=\frac{am}{m-1}
, and some
\alpha>0
, where
O(x^{1+\alpha})
is uniform on
[0,T]
.
Then

\displaystyle u_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}u^{1-m}(u^{m})_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\left(A(t)x+O(x^{1+\alpha})\right)^{\frac{1-m}{m}}(B(t)x+O(x^{1+\alpha}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}A(t)^{\frac{1-m}{m}}B(t)x^{\frac{1}{m}}(1+O(x^{\alpha}))^{\frac{1-m}{m}}(1+O(x^{\alpha}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}A(t)^{\frac{1-m}{m}}B(t)x^{\frac{1}{m}}(1+O(x^{\alpha})).

Since
(u^{m})_{xx}=u_{t}
, then integrating in
x
and using (
53
), we have that

(u^{m})_{x}(x,t)-(u^{m})_{x}(0,t)=\frac{1}{m}A(t)^{\frac{1-m}{m}}B(t)x^{1+\frac{1}{m}}(1+O(x^{\alpha})).

That is,

(u^{m})_{x}(x,t)=A(t)+\frac{1}{m+1}A(t)^{\frac{1-m}{m}}B(t)x^{1+\frac{1}{m}}(1+O(x^{\alpha})).

Integrating in
x
again, we have

(u^{m})(x,t)=A(t)x+\frac{m}{(m+1)(2m+1)}A(t)^{\frac{1-m}{m}}B(t)x^{2+\frac{1}{m}}(1+O(x^{\alpha})).

Hence, near
x=0
, we have

\frac{u^{m}}{S^{m}}=\frac{A(t)}{a}\left(1+f(t)x^{1+\frac{1}{m}}+\mbox{higher order terms}\right),

where

f(t)=\frac{m}{(m+1)(2m+1)}A(t)^{\frac{1-2m}{m}}B(t)+\frac{a^{\frac{1-m}{m}}m^{2}}{(m-1)(m+1)(2m+1)}.

Since
f(t)
is continuous on
[0,T]
and

f(0)=\frac{2a^{\frac{1-m}{m}}m^{2}}{(m-1)(m+1)(2m+1)}>0,

we have
f(t)>0
on
[0,\widetilde{T}]
for some small
\widetilde{T}>0
. Hence,
\frac{u^{m}(\cdot,t)}{S^{m}}
is precisely
C^{1+\frac{1}{m}}([0,1])
for
t\in[0,\widetilde{T}]
.

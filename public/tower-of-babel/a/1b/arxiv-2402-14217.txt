The diagonal derivative of a skew Schur polynomial
Darij Grinberg, Nazar Korniichuk, Kostiantyn Molokanov, and Severyn Khomych

Abstract
Abstract. We prove a formula for the image of a skew Schur polynomial s λ / μ ​ ( x 1 , x 2 , … , x N ) s_{\lambda/\mu}\left(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N}\right) under the differential operator ∇ := ∂ ∂ x 1 + ∂ ∂ x 2 + ⋯ + ∂ ∂ x N \nabla:=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}+\cdots+\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}} . This generalizes a formula of Weigandt for ∇ ( s λ ) \nabla\left(s_{\lambda}\right) .

2
The results

The main result of this note is the following formula, which generalizes
Weigandt’s recent result
[
Weigan23
, The Symmetric Derivative Rule]
:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
a
and
b
be two integers such that
a+b=N-1
. Let

\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
. Define two further

N
-tuples
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
by setting

\ell_{i}:=\lambda_{i}-i\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ m_{i}:=\mu_{i}-i\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{for each }i\in\left[N\right].

Then,

\nabla\left(s_{\lambda/\mu}\right)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\lambda-e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\end{subarray}}\left(\ell_{i}+a\right)s_{\left(\lambda-e_{i}\right)/\mu}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\mu+e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\end{subarray}}\left(b-m_{i}\right)s_{\lambda/\left(\mu+e_{i}\right)}.

Example 2.2
.

Let
N=3
and
\lambda=\left(3,2,1\right)
and
\mu=\left(1,1,0\right)
. Then, the
N
-tuples
\ell
and
m
defined in Theorem
2.1
are

\ell=\left(2,0,-2\right)
and
m=\left(0,-1,-3\right)
. Let
a
and

b
be two integers such that
a+b=N-1=2
. Thus, Theorem
2.1
says that

\displaystyle\nabla\left(s_{\left(3,2,1\right)/\left(1,1,0\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i\in\left\{1,2,3\right\}}\left(\ell_{i}+a\right)s_{\left(\left(3,2,1\right)-e_{i}\right)/\left(1,1,0\right)}+\sum\limits_{i\in\left\{1,3\right\}}\left(b-m_{i}\right)s_{\left(3,2,1\right)/\left(\left(1,1,0\right)+e_{i}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(2+a\right)s_{\left(2,1,1\right)/\left(1,1,0\right)}+\left(0+a\right)s_{\left(3,1,1\right)/\left(1,1,0\right)}+\left(-2+a\right)s_{\left(3,2,0\right)/\left(1,1,0\right)}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\left(b-0\right)s_{\left(3,2,1\right)/\left(2,1,0\right)}+\left(b-\left(-3\right)\right)s_{\left(3,2,1\right)/\left(1,1,1\right)}.

Note that the second sum has no
i=2
addend, since
\mu+e_{2}=\left(1,2,0\right)\notin\mathcal{P}_{N}
.

Remark 2.3
.

Let us comment on the combinatorial meaning of Theorem
2.1
. A pair

\left(\mu,\lambda\right)
of
N
-tuples
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{N}

is called a
skew partition
if
\mu\subseteq\lambda
(that is,
\mu_{i}\leq\lambda_{i}
for each
i\in\left[N\right]
). Such a skew partition

\left(\mu,\lambda\right)
has a
skew Young diagram
assigned to it,
which is defined as the set difference of the Young diagrams of
\lambda
and

\mu
. This diagram is denoted by
\lambda/\mu
.

Let
\lambda/\mu
be a skew partition with
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
.
The sum
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\lambda-e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\end{subarray}}\left(\ell_{i}+a\right)s_{\left(\lambda-e_{i}\right)/\mu}
in Theorem
2.1
can be rewritten as
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\lambda-e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N};\\
\mu\subseteq\lambda-e_{i}\end{subarray}}\left(\ell_{i}+a\right)s_{\left(\lambda-e_{i}\right)/\mu}
, because
any addend in which
\mu\subseteq\lambda-e_{i}
does not hold is
0
by
(
3
). This is a sum over all skew partitions obtained from

\lambda/\mu
by removing an outer corner (i.e., a removable box on the
southeastern boundary of
\lambda/\mu
). Moreover, the number
\ell_{i}=\lambda_{i}-i
tells us which diagonal this corner belongs to.

Likewise, the sum
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\mu+e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\end{subarray}}\left(b-m_{i}\right)s_{\lambda/\left(\mu+e_{i}\right)}
can be rewritten as
\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\mu+e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N};\\
\mu+e_{i}\subseteq\lambda\end{subarray}}\left(b-m_{i}\right)s_{\lambda/\left(\mu+e_{i}\right)}
, which is a sum over
all skew partitions obtained from
\lambda/\mu
by removing an inner corner
(i.e., a removable box on the northwestern boundary of
\lambda/\mu
).
Moreover, the number
m_{i}=\mu_{i}-i
tells us which diagonal this corner
belongs to.

Remark 2.4
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
. Let
\ell_{i}:=\lambda_{i}-i
for each

i\in\left[N\right]
. Set
\mu:=\left(0,0,\ldots,0\right)\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
. Then, the skew Schur polynomial
s_{\lambda/\mu}
is
usually called
s_{\lambda}
. The only

satisfying

\mu+e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
is

. Hence, Theorem
2.1
(applied to

and

) yields

\displaystyle\nabla\left(s_{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\lambda-e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\end{subarray}}\left(\ell_{i}+N\right)s_{\lambda-e_{i}}+\underbrace{\left(1-0+\left(-1\right)\right)}_{=0}s_{\lambda/\left(\mu+e_{1}\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\lambda-e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\end{subarray}}\left(\ell_{i}+N\right)s_{\lambda-e_{i}},

which recovers
[
Weigan23
, The Symmetric Derivative Rule]
.

Once Theorem
2.1
is proved, we will derive the following curious corollary:

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
.
Then,

\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\lambda-e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\end{subarray}}s_{\left(\lambda-e_{i}\right)/\mu}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\mu+e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\end{subarray}}s_{\lambda/\left(\mu+e_{i}\right)}.

We note that Corollary
2.5
follows easily from the theory of skewing
operators (see
[
GriRei20
, §2.8]
) and the Pieri rule.

In a
nutshell: Corollary

s_{\mu}^{\perp}\left(s_{1}^{\perp}s_{\lambda}\right)=\left(s_{1}s_{\mu}\right)^{\perp}s_{\lambda}

3
A lemma on
\nabla\left(h_{n}\right)

First we need a formula for the image of the complete homogeneous symmetric
polynomial
h_{n}
under the operator
\nabla
:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n
be a integer. Then,
\nabla\left(h_{n}\right)=\left(n+N-1\right)h_{n-1}
.

Proof 3.2

(Proof of Lemma
3.1
.)
.

For each
k\in\left[N\right]
, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}h_{n}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{N}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{N};\\
i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{N}=n\end{subarray}}x_{1}^{i_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots x_{N}^{i_{N}}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \left(\text{by \eqref{eq.def.hn}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{N}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{N};\\
i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{N}=n\end{subarray}}\underbrace{\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\left(x_{1}^{i_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots x_{N}^{i_{N}}\right)}_{\begin{subarray}{c}=\begin{cases}i_{k}x_{1}^{i_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots x_{k}^{i_{k}-1}\cdots x_{N}^{i_{N}},&\text{if }i_{k}>0;\\
0,&\text{if }i_{k}=0\end{cases}\\
\text{(where }x_{1}^{i_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots x_{k}^{i_{k}-1}\cdots x_{N}^{i_{N}}\text{ means the monomial }x_{1}^{i_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots x_{N}^{i_{N}}\\
\text{with the exponent on }x_{k}\text{ decremented by
}1\text{)}\end{subarray}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{N}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{N};\\
i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{N}=n;\\
i_{k}>0\end{subarray}}i_{k}x_{1}^{i_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots x_{k}^{i_{k}-1}\cdots x_{N}^{i_{N}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{N}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{N};\\
i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+\left(i_{k}+1\right)+\cdots+i_{N}=n\end{subarray}}}_{=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{N}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{N};\\
i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{N}=n-1\end{subarray}}}\left(i_{k}+1\right)\underbrace{x_{1}^{i_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots x_{k}^{\left(i_{k}+1\right)-1}\cdots x_{N}^{i_{N}}}_{=x_{1}^{i_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots x_{N}^{i_{N}}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \left(\text{here, we substituted
}i_{k}+1\text{ for }i_{k}\text{ in the sum}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{N}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{N};\\
i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{N}=n-1\end{subarray}}\left(i_{k}+1\right)x_{1}^{i_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots x_{N}^{i_{N}}.

Summing this equality over all
k\in\left[N\right]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k\in\left[N\right]}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}h_{n}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k\in\left[N\right]}\ \ \sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{N}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{N};\\
i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{N}=n-1\end{subarray}}\left(i_{k}+1\right)x_{1}^{i_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots x_{N}^{i_{N}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{N}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{N};\\
i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{N}=n-1\end{subarray}}\underbrace{\left(\sum\limits_{k\in\left[N\right]}\left(i_{k}+1\right)\right)}_{\begin{subarray}{c}=\left(i_{1}+1\right)+\left(i_{2}+1\right)+\cdots+\left(i_{N}+1\right)\\
=\left(i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{N}\right)+N\\
=n-1+N\\
\text{(since }i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{N}=n-1\text{)}\end{subarray}}x_{1}^{i_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots x_{N}^{i_{N}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left(n-1+N\right)}_{=n+N-1}\underbrace{\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{N}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{N};\\
i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{N}=n-1\end{subarray}}x_{1}^{i_{1}}x_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots x_{N}^{i_{N}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}=h_{n-1}\\
\text{(by the definition of }h_{n-1}\text{)}\end{subarray}}=\left(n+N-1\right)h_{n-1}.

This can be rewritten as
\nabla\left(h_{n}\right)=\left(n+N-1\right)h_{n-1}
(since
\nabla=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}+\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{2}}+\cdots+\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{N}}=\sum\limits_{k\in\left[N\right]}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}
). Thus, Lemma
3.1

is proved.

4
Lemmas on determinants

We will next need a few simple lemmas about determinants:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
. Define
\ell,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
as in Theorem
2.1
. Then,

\det\left(h_{\ell_{i}-m_{j}}\right)_{i,j\in\left[N\right]}=s_{\lambda/\mu}.

Proof 4.2

(Proof of Lemma
4.1
.)
.

For every
i,j\in\left[N\right]
, we have

\ell_{i}=\lambda_{i}-i
(by the definition of
\ell
) and
m_{j}=\mu_{j}-j

(similarly). Subtracting these two equalities from each other, we obtain that

\ell_{i}-m_{j}=\lambda_{i}-i-\left(\mu_{j}-j\right)=\lambda_{i}-\mu_{j}-i+j\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{for every }i,j\in\left[N\right].

Hence, we can rewrite (
2
) as
s_{\lambda/\mu}=\det\left(h_{\ell_{i}-m_{j}}\right)_{i,j\in\left[N\right]}
. This proves Lemma

4.1
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
and
k\in\left[N\right]
be such that
\lambda-e_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
. Define
\ell,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
as in Theorem
2.1
. Then,

\det\left(h_{\left(\ell-e_{k}\right)_{i}-m_{j}}\right)_{i,j\in\left[N\right]}=s_{\left(\lambda-e_{k}\right)/\mu}.

Proof 4.4

(Proof of Lemma
4.3
.)
.

Recall that the
N
-tuple
\ell
is
defined from the
N
-tuple
\lambda
by subtracting
1
from its
1
-st entry,
subtracting
2
from its
2
-nd entry, subtracting
3
from its
3
-rd entry,
etc.. Thus, the
N
-tuple
\ell-e_{k}
is obtained from
\lambda-e_{k}
in the
same way. Hence, Lemma
4.1
(applied to
\lambda-e_{k}
and

\ell-e_{k}
instead of
\lambda
and
\ell
) yields

\det\left(h_{\left(\ell-e_{k}\right)_{i}-m_{j}}\right)_{i,j\in\left[N\right]}=s_{\left(\lambda-e_{k}\right)/\mu}.

This proves Lemma
4.3
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
and

be such that
\mu+e_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
. Define
\ell,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}

. Then,

\det\left(h_{\ell_{i}-\left(m+e_{k}\right)_{j}}\right)_{i,j\in\left[N\right]}=s_{\lambda/\left(\mu+e_{k}\right)}.

Proof 4.6

(Proof of Lemma
4.5
.)
.

Similarly to Lemma
4.3
,
we can show this by applying Lemma
4.1
to
\mu+e_{k}
and

m+e_{k}
instead of
\mu
and
m
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{N}
and
k\in\left[N\right]
be such that
\lambda-e_{k}\notin\mathcal{P}_{N}
. Define

\ell,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
as in Theorem

\mu+e_{k}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\end{subarray}}\left(b-m_{k}\right)s_{\lambda/\left(\mu+e_{k}\right)}=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\mu+e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\end{subarray}}\left(b-m_{i}\right)s_{\lambda/\left(\mu+e_{i}\right)},

this can be rewritten as

\nabla\left(s_{\lambda/\mu}\right)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\lambda-e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\end{subarray}}\left(\ell_{i}+a\right)s_{\left(\lambda-e_{i}\right)/\mu}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\mu+e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\end{subarray}}\left(b-m_{i}\right)s_{\lambda/\left(\mu+e_{i}\right)}.

Thus, Theorem
2.1
is proved.

Proof 6.2

(Proof of Corollary
2.5
.)
.

Define
\ell,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
as in
Theorem
2.1
. Theorem
2.1
(applied to
a=N-1
and
b=0
) yields

\nabla\left(s_{\lambda/\mu}\right)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\lambda-e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\end{subarray}}\left(\ell_{i}+N-1\right)s_{\left(\lambda-e_{i}\right)/\mu}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\mu+e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\end{subarray}}\left(0-m_{i}\right)s_{\lambda/\left(\mu+e_{i}\right)}.

Theorem
2.1
(applied to
a=N
and
b=-1
) yields

\nabla\left(s_{\lambda/\mu}\right)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\lambda-e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\end{subarray}}\left(\ell_{i}+N\right)s_{\left(\lambda-e_{i}\right)/\mu}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\mu+e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\end{subarray}}\left(-1-m_{i}\right)s_{\lambda/\left(\mu+e_{i}\right)}.

Subtracting the latter equality from the former, we find

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\
\lambda-e_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\end{subarray}}\left(-1\right)s_{\left(\lambda-e_{i}\right)/\mu}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\

\displaystyle=-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in\left[N\right];\\

7
Final remarks

1.
Clearly, Theorem
2.1
can be generalized by replacing

\mathbb{Z}
with any commutative ring
\mathbf{k}
. In this generality,
a

and
b
can be any two elements of
\mathbf{k}
(rather than just integers)
satisfying
a+b=\left(N-1\right)\cdot 1_{\mathbf{k}}
. However, not much
generality is gained in this way, since Corollary
2.5
easily shows
that all choices of
a
and
b
lead to the same sum.

2.
Theorem
2.1
can also be lifted to the “infinite setting”, i.e., to the ring of symmetric functions
in infinitely many variables (see, e.g.,
[
Stanle24
, Chapter 7]
or

[
GriRei20
, Chapter 2]
for introductions to this ring). This is not
completely straightforward, since the diagonal derivative
\nabla
is defined
only for finitely many indeterminates and depends on their number
N
(for
instance,
\nabla\left(s_{\left(1\right)}\right)=N
). In the infinite
setting, it has to be replaced by a derivation
\nabla_{q}
depending on a
scalar
q
:

Let
\mathbf{k}
be a commutative ring, and let
q\in\mathbf{k}
be an
element. (For example, we can have
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{Z}\left[q\right]

and
q=q
.) Let
\Lambda
be the ring of symmetric functions in infinitely
many indeterminates
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots
over
\mathbf{k}
. For each

n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we let
h_{n}
denote the
n
-th complete homogeneous
symmetric function in
\Lambda
(so that
h_{0}=1
and
h_{i}=0
for all

i<0
). Let
\nabla_{q}:\Lambda\rightarrow\Lambda
be the unique derivation
that satisfies

\nabla_{q}\left(h_{n}\right)=\left(n+q-1\right)h_{n-1}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{for each }n>0.

5
5

5

This condition uniquely determines a derivation of
\Lambda
, since
the elements
h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},\ldots
freely generate
\Lambda
as a
commutative
\mathbf{k}
-algebra. It is easy to see that this derivation

\nabla_{q}
satisfies the equality
\nabla_{q}\left(h_{n}\right)=\left(n+q-1\right)h_{n-1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

For
q=N\in\mathbb{N}
, this
derivation is a lift of the directional derivative operator
\nabla:R\rightarrow R
to
\Lambda
(meaning that
\nabla\circ\pi=\pi\circ\nabla_{N}
, where
\pi:\Lambda\rightarrow R
is the evaluation homomorphism at

x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N},0,0,0,\ldots
). With these definitions, we can
extend Theorem
2.1
to a general property of
\nabla_{q}
:

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
a
and
b
be two elements of
\mathbf{k}
such that

a+b=q-1
. Let
\lambda=\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots\right)
and
\mu=\left(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\ldots,\right)

be two partitions. Set

\ell_{i}:=\lambda_{i}-i\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ m_{i}:=\mu_{i}-i\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{for each }i\geq 1.

Then,

\nabla_{q}\left(s_{\lambda/\mu}\right)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq 1;\\
\lambda-e_{i}\text{ is a partition}\end{subarray}}\left(\ell_{i}+a\right)s_{\left(\lambda-e_{i}\right)/\mu}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq 1;\\
\mu+e_{i}\text{ is a
partition}\end{subarray}}\left(b-m_{i}\right)s_{\lambda/\left(\mu+e_{i}\right)}.

Here,
e_{i}
means the infinite sequence
\left(0,0,\ldots,0,1,0,0,0,\ldots\right)
with the
1
in its
i
-th position.

The proof of this theorem is similar to our above proof of Theorem
2.1

(with the minor complication that we have to fix an
N\in\mathbb{N}
that is
strictly larger than the lengths of
\lambda
and
\mu
, in order to apply the
Jacobi–Trudi formula), and is left to the reader.

3.
It is natural to attempt generalizing Theorem
2.1
to
higher-order differential operators, such as
\nabla^{\prime}:=\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{1}^{2}}+\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{2}^{2}}+\cdots+\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{N}^{2}}
. However, finding similar

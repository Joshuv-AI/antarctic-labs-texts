Whittaker modules and hyperbolic Toda lattices
Limeng Xia

Abstract
. Let 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} be a complex finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra and let 𝔤 l \mathfrak{g}_{l} be the corresponding generalized Takiff algebra. This paper studies the affine variety 𝐟 + 𝔟 l {\bf f}+\mathfrak{b}_{l} where 𝐟 {\bf f} is similar to a principal nilpotent element of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} and 𝔟 l \mathfrak{b}_{l} is a subalgebra corresponding to the Borel subalgebra 𝔟 \mathfrak{b} of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . Inspired by Kostant’s work then we deal with two questions. One of them is to construct the Whittaker model for the G l G_{l} -invariants of symmetric algebra S ⁡ ( 𝔤 l ) S(\mathfrak{g}_{l}) where G l G_{l} is the adjoint group of 𝔤 l \mathfrak{g}_{l} and G l G_{l} acts on S ⁡ ( 𝔤 l ) S(\mathfrak{g}_{l}) by coadjoint action, and then to classify all nonsingular Whittaker modules over 𝔤 l \mathfrak{g}_{l} . Another one is to describe the symplectic structure of the manifold Z ⊆ 𝐟 + 𝔟 l Z\subseteq{\bf f}+\mathfrak{b}_{l} of normalized Jacobi elements. Then the Hamiltonian corresponding to a fundamental invariant provides a class of hyperbolic Toda lattices. In particular, a simplest example describes the state of a dynamical system consisting of a positive mass particle and a negative mass particle.



Keywords:
Takiff algebras; Whittaker modules; simple modules; Hyperbolic Toda lattices; negative mass

1.
Introduction

This paper deals with a representation question as to which irreducible representations of a Takiff algebra have a Whittaker model, and with a question involving some Toda lattices which describes the dynamical systems consisting of both positive mass articles and negative mass particles. These Toda lattices attached to Takiff algebras were also studied by M. Lau in the very recent publication
[
14
]
.

1.1.

In his original paper Kostant suggested the so-called
Whittaker model
of the center
Z(\mathfrak{g})
of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
of a finite-dimensional complex semi-simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
in
[
11
]
in 1978. He studied the variety
\mathfrak{f}+\mathfrak{b}
where
\mathfrak{f}
is a principal nilpotent element and
\mathfrak{b}
is the Borel subalgebra. This variety induces a translated adjoint action of
N
on
\mathfrak{b}
and hence a translated coadjoint action on
\mathfrak{b}_{l}
, where
N
is the Lie subgroup corresponding to the nilradical
\mathfrak{n}
of
\mathfrak{b}
. This translated coadjoint action determines an isomorphism between the
G
-invariants
S(\mathfrak{g})^{G}
and the
N
-invariants
S(\overline{\mathfrak{b}})^{N}
. Then one constructs the Whittaker model. As a main result in the representation theory then Kostant classified all nonsingular Whittaker modules over
\mathfrak{g}
.

A more general definition of a Whittaker module for an algebra structure
\mathcal{A}
with a triangular decomposition
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{-}\mathcal{A}^{0}\mathcal{A}^{+}
is then an
\mathcal{A}
-module induced from a one-dimensional
\mathcal{A}^{+}
-module which is determined by some character
\mathcal{A}^{+}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
.

In the recent decades many authors have focused on the Whittaker models and Whittaker modules over various algebras including Lie algebras, Lie super algebras, vertex operator algebras and quantum groups. For example, Adamović-Lü-Zhao studied Whittaker modules for the affine Lie algebra of type
A^{(1)}
in
[
1
]
. Christodoulopoulou studied the so-called imaginary Whittaker modules for affine Lie algebras induced by the Whittaker modules for infinite-dimensional Heisenberg algebras in
[
5
]
. Hartwig-Yu studied Whittaker modules over free bosonic orbifold vertex operator algebras in
[
8
]
. Liu-Pei-Xia studied Whittaker modules for the super-Virasoro algebras in
[
16
]
. Sevostyanov studied Whittaker modules over topological quantum groups in
[
23
]
and Xia-Zhao studied twisted Whittaker modules over rational quantum groups of type
A
in
[
27
]
. Romanov developed a geometric algorithm for computing the composition multiplicities of stantard Whittaker modules in
[
22
]
.

1.2.

In 1979 by considering the coadjoint action of
\overline{B}
on the variety
\mathfrak{f}+\mathfrak{b}
Kostant studied the symplectic structure of the manifold
Z\subseteq\mathfrak{f}+\mathfrak{b}
of normalized Jacobi elements in
[
13
]
(also see
[
12
]
), where
\overline{B}
is the Lie subgroup corresponding to the opposite Borel subalgebra
\overline{\mathfrak{b}}
. Let
\kappa
be a positive multiple of the Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
. The invariant
I=\frac{1}{2}\kappa
then produces a generalized Toda lattice which is related to the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}
. Of course the Toda lattices relative to Lie algebras have attracted many attentions. For example, one is referred to
[
15
]
and
[
19
]
.

In this paper, the Toda lattice means a finite non-periodic Toda lattice. A Toda lattice is a Hamiltonian system describing particles on a line which was first introduced by Toda in
[
25
]
. The Hamiltonian of a system of
n
particles moving on a line so that the particles have masses
{\bf m}_{i}>0,i=1,\cdots,n
usually is written as

\displaystyle H=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{p_{i}^{2}}{2{\bf m}_{i}}+U,

where
U
is the potential function, then the state of this system can be determined by a Hamilton’s equation. Usually this problem was studied by a symplectic structure and the Hamiltonian defines a bilinear form on certain real space.

In theoretical physics, the possibility of having negative-mass was already present from Dirac established his famous equation in
[
6
]
. Of course many physicists have been concerned with problems involving negative-mass matters, for example see
[
20
]
.

Considering a simplest Hamiltonian system which consists of a positive mass particle and a negative mass particle, then one can assume

(1.1)

\displaystyle{H}=\frac{(p_{+})^{2}}{2}-\frac{(p_{-})^{2}}{2}+U(q_{+},q_{-})

so that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{H}}{\partial p_{\pm}}=\frac{dq_{\pm}}{dt},\;\frac{\partial{H}}{\partial q_{\pm}}=-\frac{dp_{\pm}}{dt}.

If we assume
p=(p_{+}+p_{-})/2,\overline{p}=(p_{+}-p_{-})/2,q=q_{+}+q_{-},\overline{q}=q_{+}-q_{-}
, then one has

(1.3)

\displaystyle{H}=2p\overline{p}+U,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{H}}{\partial p}=\frac{d{\overline{q}}}{dt},\;\frac{\partial{H}}{\partial\overline{p}}=\frac{d{q}}{dt},\;\frac{\partial{H}}{\partial q}=-\frac{d{p}}{dt},\;\frac{\partial{H}}{\partial\overline{q}}=-\frac{d\overline{p}}{dt}.

Clearly the corresponding symplectic structure induces a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on
{\mathbb{R}}q_{+}+{\mathbb{R}}q_{-}
determined by

\displaystyle(q_{\pm},q_{\pm})=\pm 1,(q_{\pm},q_{\mp})=0.

Then one has

\displaystyle(q,q)=(\overline{q},\overline{q})=0,(q,\overline{q})=2.

In general, if a system contains both positive mass particles and negative mass particles, it is impossible to determine the corresponding bilinear form on the position-phase space
\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{R}}q_{i}
by a multiple (even negative multiple) of the Killing form of some semi-simple Lie algebra.

1.3.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is a finite-dimensional complex simple Lie algebra of rank
n
and
\mathfrak{g}_{l}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[t]/(t^{l+1})
is the Takiff algebra. The Takiff algebra is an important non-semi-simple object of finite-dimensional Lie algebras. Of course lots of articles provides many results in its representation theory. For more details one is referred to
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
17
]
and
[
26
]
and references therein.

Let
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
be the center of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
of
\mathfrak{g}_{l}
. For any
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
-module
V
let
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{V}
denote the annihilator of
V
in
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
and put
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{V}=Z(\mathfrak{g}_{l})\cap U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{V}
. One says that
V
admits an infinitesimal character if
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{V}
is a maximal ideal of
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
. Clearly
V
admits an infinitesimal character if and only if there exists a character
\xi:Z(\mathfrak{g}_{l})\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
such that
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{V}={\rm Ker}\xi
.

For any subspace
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
let
\mathfrak{a}_{l}
denote the subspace
\mathfrak{a}\otimes{\mathbb{C}}[t]/(t^{l+1})\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{l}
. Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
and let
\mathfrak{b},\overline{\mathfrak{b}}
respectively be the positive Borel subalgebra and the opposite Borel subalgebra. Then both
\mathfrak{n}=[\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{b}]
and
\overline{\mathfrak{n}}=[\overline{\mathfrak{b}},\overline{\mathfrak{b}}]
are maximal nilpotent subalgebras. In particular one has a triangular decomposition

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{l}=\overline{\mathfrak{n}}_{l}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{l}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{l}.

If
0\not=v\in V
and
\eta:\mathfrak{n}_{l}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is a character such that

\displaystyle xv=\eta(x)v,\;\forall x\in\mathfrak{n}_{l},

the element
v
is called a Whittaker vector (of type
\eta
). If the module
V
is cyclically generated by a Whittaker vector, it is called a Whittaker module.

Let
{\mathbb{C}}_{\eta}
be the one-dimensional
U(\mathfrak{n}_{l})
-module defined by the character
\eta
of
\mathfrak{n}_{l}
and let
{\mathbb{C}}_{\eta,\xi}
be the one-dimensional
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{l})\otimes U(\mathfrak{n}_{l})
-module defined by the respective characters
\xi
and
\eta
of
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
and
\mathfrak{n}_{l}
. Considering the induced modules

\displaystyle W_{\eta}=U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{n}_{l})}{\mathbb{C}}_{\eta},

\displaystyle Y_{\eta,\xi}=U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})\otimes_{Z(\mathfrak{g}_{l})\otimes U(\mathfrak{n}_{l})}{\mathbb{C}}_{\eta,\xi}.

We prove that there exists a one-one correspondence between the set of all submodules of
W_{\eta}
and the set of all ideals of the center
Z(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
. In particular, the modules
Y_{\eta,\xi}
(
\eta
nonsingular) classify all simple Whittaker modules up to equivalence. These results generalize Kostant’s corresponding results from
\mathfrak{g}_{l}
to Takiff algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{l}
.

1.4.

It is known that
\mathfrak{g}_{l}
has a non-degenerate invariant symmetric bilinear form
Q
induced by the Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
. Due to this bilinear form, the symmetric algebra
S(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
of
\mathfrak{g}_{l}
can be regarded as the algebra of polynomial functions on
\mathfrak{g}_{l}
and then
S(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
has a Poisson structure. Moreover the restriction of this structure to
S(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
coincides with the Poisson structure of
S(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
as the algebra of polynomial functions on
\mathfrak{b}_{l}
. Let
G_{l}
be the adjoint group of
\mathfrak{g}_{l}
and let
\overline{B}_{l}
be the Lie subgroup corresponding to
\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l}
then
\overline{B}_{l}
acts on
\mathfrak{b}_{l}
by the coadjoint action. Considering the manifold
Z\subseteq{\bf f}+\mathfrak{b}_{l}
of normalized Jacobi elements, then there exists a modified version of the coadjoint action of
\overline{B}_{l}
on
Z
so that
Z
is a
\overline{B}_{l}
-orbit in
{\bf f}+\mathfrak{b}_{l}
, and hence
Z
has a coadjoint-symplectic theory of
\overline{B}_{l}
carried from
\mathfrak{b}_{l}
.

In particular one determines the symplectic structure of
Z
by its a global ordinate system. Let
I=\frac{1}{2}Q
, one obtains a Hamiltonian corresponding to
I
, which is of hyperbolic type. In the case
l=1
we give a simple expression for the
2
-form
\omega_{Z}
of
Z
. The Hamiltonian corresponding to
I
then is clearly of hyperbolic type. The associative systems are completely integrable and referred to the hyperbolic Toda lattices.

Finally, we give an example in the case
n=1,l=1
. In this case one obtains a hyperbolic differential operator on
{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
and an explicit example for (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) from the Hamiltonian corresponding to
I=\frac{1}{2}Q
. In particular, one shows that the dynamical system determined by the Hamiltonian in this example has a unique global smooth solution for any initial values under some bounded condition.

{\mathbb{C}},{\mathbb{C}}^{*},{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}}^{*},{\mathbb{Z}},\mathbb{N}

In Sections 2-5 and Subsection 6.3 Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
and

are defined over ground field

. In Sections 6-7 (except Subsection 6.3) the same Lie algebras are regarded as Lie algebras over ground field

.

2.1.

For any finite-dimensional simple complex Lie algebra

Associate a root vector

\displaystyle\mathfrak{n}=[\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{b}],\;\overline{\mathfrak{n}}=[\overline{\mathfrak{b}},\overline{\mathfrak{b}}].

Let

3.
A Decomposition result of
S(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})

3.1.

For any
0\leq j\leq l
fixing a principal nilpotent element
\mathfrak{f}_{j}\in\mathfrak{g}_{[-1]}
. We define elements

(3.1)

\displaystyle{\bf f}=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\mathfrak{f}_{j}(j),\;{\bf x}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\omega_{i}(0).

Let
\mathfrak{s}\subseteq\mathfrak{b}
be an
{{\rm ad}}x_{0}
-stable subspace such that
\mathfrak{b}=[\mathfrak{f}_{0},\mathfrak{n}]\oplus\mathfrak{s}
is a direct sum.

For each
j
, obviously the subspace
{\mathfrak{g}_{[j]}}_{l}
is the eigenspace for
{{\rm ad}}{\bf x}
corresponding to the eigenvalue
j
and one has

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{l}=\bigoplus_{j}{\mathfrak{g}_{[j]}}_{l}.

In particular one has
{\bf f}\in{\mathfrak{g}_{[-1]}}_{l}
,
\mathfrak{s}_{l}
is
{{\rm ad}}{\bf x}
-stable.

Lemma 3.1
.

One has

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{l}=[{\bf f},\mathfrak{n}_{l}]+\mathfrak{s}_{l}

and the sum is direct.

Proof.

Clearly one has

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}[l]=\mathfrak{b}(l)=[\mathfrak{f}_{0},\mathfrak{n}](l)+\mathfrak{s}(l)=[{\bf f},\mathfrak{n}(l)]+\mathfrak{s}(l),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}[l-1]=[\mathfrak{f}_{0},\mathfrak{n}](l-1)+\mathfrak{s}(l-1)+\mathfrak{b}(l)=[{\bf f},\mathfrak{n}(l-1)]+\mathfrak{s}(l-1)+\mathfrak{b}[l],

and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}[k]=\mathfrak{b}(k)+\mathfrak{b}[k+1]=[\mathfrak{f}_{0},\mathfrak{n}](k)+\mathfrak{s}(k)+\mathfrak{b}[k+1]=[{\bf f},\mathfrak{n}(k)]+\mathfrak{s}(k)+\mathfrak{b}[k+1],

for all
k\geq 0
. Then this lemma follows from the direct sum

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{l}=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\mathfrak{b}(j)

since

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}_{l}=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\mathfrak{b}(j)=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\left([{\bf f},\mathfrak{n}(j)]+\mathfrak{s}(j)\right)=[{\bf f},\mathfrak{n}_{l}]+\mathfrak{s}_{l}.

∎

Let
N_{l}\subseteq G_{l}
be the (unipotent) Lie subgroup corresponding to
\mathfrak{n}_{l}
. Regarding
N_{l},{\bf f}+\mathfrak{s}_{l}
and
{\bf f}+\mathfrak{b}_{l}
as affine varieties then one has

Theorem 3.2
.

The map

(3.4)

\displaystyle N_{l}\times({\bf f}+\mathfrak{s}_{l})\rightarrow{\bf f}+\mathfrak{b}_{l}

given by
(a,x)\mapsto ax
is an isomorphism of affine varieties.

Proof.

First we show that
a({\bf f}+x)={\bf f}+x
for
x\in\mathfrak{b}_{l}
and
a\in\mathfrak{n}_{l}
implies that
a
is the identity.

We may write
a=\exp(z)
for some
z=\sum_{j\geq 0}z_{j}(j)
and
x=\sum_{j}x_{j}(j)
where
z_{j}\in\mathfrak{n},x_{j}\in\mathfrak{b}
. Then one has

\displaystyle{\bf f}+x=a({\bf f}+x)\in\exp(z_{0}(0))(\mathfrak{f}(0)+x_{0}(0))+\sum_{j>0}\mathfrak{g}(j)

and hence
z_{0}=0
by Theorem
2.1
. Assume that
z=\sum_{j\geq 1}z_{j}(j)
. Then one has

\displaystyle{\bf f}+x=a({\bf f}+x)\in f_{0}(0)+x_{0}(0)+f_{1}(1)+x_{1}(1)+[z_{1}(1),\mathfrak{f}_{0}(0)+x_{0}(0)]+\sum_{j>1}\mathfrak{g}(j)

and hence
z_{1}=0
. By induction in the similar way one has
z=0
and
a
is the identity.

Next we show that
a({\bf f}+x)=({\bf f}+x^{\prime})
for some
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{s}_{l}
and
a\in\mathfrak{n}_{l}
implies that
x=x^{\prime}
and
a
is the identity. We write

Theorem 4.8
.

U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
is a free right module over

and the multiplication induces an isomorphism

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{p+q=k}\widetilde{A_{(p)}}\otimes W_{(q)}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})\rightarrow U_{(k)}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})

Proof.

Clearly one has
{\mathbb{C}}1=U_{(0)}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})=\widetilde{A}_{(0)}W_{(0)}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
. Then inductively we can assume that (

) holds with replacing
k
by any

. Let

) is surjective.

The proof for injectivity is similar. For any nontrivial relation

4.5.

Let
{\mathbb{C}}_{\eta}
be the one-dimensional
\mathfrak{n}_{l}
-module defined by the character
\eta
and let

denote the induced module

\displaystyle W_{\eta}=U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak{n}_{l})}{\mathbb{C}}_{\eta},

which is referred to the universal Whittaker module over
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
corresponding to
\eta
. Clearly it is the left quotient
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})/U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})U_{\eta}(\mathfrak{n}_{l})
and hence
one has an epimorphism

\displaystyle U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})\rightarrow W_{\eta}.

Let
1_{\eta}
denote the image of
1
. For any
u\in U(\mathfrak{g}_{l}),y\in W_{\eta}
the action of
u
on
y
is denoted by
uy
. Clearly, one has

(4.31)

\displaystyle u1_{\eta}=u^{\eta}1_{\eta}.

In particular,
W_{\eta}
is a free
U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
-module of rank one and this induces a
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
-module structure on
U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
which is defined by

\displaystyle u\circ w^{\eta}=(uw)^{\eta}.

Now one obtains an isomorphism

\displaystyle U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})\rightarrow W_{\eta}

of
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
-modules.

Next we consider the
\eta
-reduced action of
\mathfrak{n}_{l}
on
U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
:

\displaystyle x\cdot v=x\circ v-\eta(x)v.

Lemma 4.9
.

For any
u\in U(\mathfrak{g}_{l}),x\in\mathfrak{n}_{l}
one has

\displaystyle x\cdot u^{\eta}=[x,u]^{\eta}.

Proof.

First one has

\displaystyle xu1_{\eta}=[x,u]1_{\eta}+ux1_{\eta}=[x,u]1_{\eta}+\eta(x)u1_{\eta}

and

\displaystyle[x,u]1_{\eta}=xu1_{\eta}-\eta(x)u1_{\eta}.

Therefore it holds

\displaystyle(x\cdot u^{\eta})1_{\eta}=(x\circ u^{\eta}-\eta(x)u^{\eta})1_{\eta}=(xu-\eta(x)u)^{\eta}1_{\eta}=[x,u]^{\eta}1_{\eta}.

Then one has
x\cdot u^{\eta}=[x,u]^{\eta}
by the uniqueness of
u^{\eta}
in (
4.31
).
∎

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
p,q\in\mathbb{N},p>0,x\in{\mathfrak{g}_{[p]}}_{l}\subseteq\mathfrak{n}_{l}
and
u\in U_{(q)}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
. Then
x\cdot u\in U_{(q-p)}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
so that the
\eta
-reduced action of
\mathfrak{n}_{l}
induces a graded action of
\mathfrak{n}_{l}
on
{\rm Gr}U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
. Moreover one has

(4.32)

\displaystyle\tau_{q-p}(x\cdot u)=x\cdot\tau_{(q)}u

and hence the map

(4.33)

\displaystyle\tau_{\bf x}:{\rm Gr}U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})\rightarrow S(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l}),

is an isomorphism of graded
\mathfrak{n}_{l}
-modules.

Proof.

It is sufficient to assume
x=e_{\alpha_{i}}(j)
for some
1\leq i\leq n,0\leq j\leq l
since
\mathfrak{n}_{l}
is generated by the elements
e_{\alpha_{i}}(j)
.

Because
[e_{\alpha_{i}}(j),U(\overline{\mathfrak{n}}_{l})]\subseteq U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
and
[e_{\alpha_{i}}(j),U(\mathfrak{h}_{l})]\subseteq\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j}U(\mathfrak{h}_{l})e_{\alpha_{i}}(j^{\prime})
, then there are unique elements
u^{\prime},u_{j^{\prime}}\in U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
with
j^{\prime}\geq j
for any
u
such that

(4.34)

\displaystyle[e_{\alpha_{i}}(j),u]=u^{\prime}+\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j}u_{j^{\prime}}e_{\alpha_{i}}(j).

Since
{{\rm ad}}e_{\alpha_{i}}(j)
is a derivation of
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
this establishes (
4.34
). The uniqueness follows from the isomorphism
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})\cong U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})\otimes U(\mathfrak{n}_{l})
.

Now taking
u\in(U_{k}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l}))_{r}
with
k+r=q
. Then
u\in U_{k}(\mathfrak{g}_{l}),[{\bf x},e_{\alpha_{i}}(j)]=e_{\alpha_{i}}(j)
and
[{\bf x},u]=-ru
, this follows

[{\bf x},[e_{\alpha_{i}}(j),u]]=(1-r)[e_{\alpha_{i}}(j),u]
. By the uniqueness of (
4.34
) and

U_{k}(\mathfrak{g}_{l})=\sum_{t+s=k}U_{t}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})\otimes U_{s}(\mathfrak{n}_{l}),

it follows that
u^{\prime}\in(U_{k}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l}))_{r-1}
and
u_{j^{\prime}}\in(U_{k-1}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l}))_{r}
. So
u^{\prime},u_{j^{\prime}}\in U_{(q-1)}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
. Since
{u^{\prime}}^{\eta}=u^{\prime}
and

\displaystyle(u_{j^{\prime}}e_{\alpha_{i}}(j^{\prime}))^{\eta}=\eta(e_{\alpha_{i}}(j^{\prime}))u_{j^{\prime}}=Q(e_{\alpha_{i}}(j^{\prime}),e_{-\alpha_{i}}(l-j^{\prime}))u_{j^{\prime}},

one has

(4.35)

\displaystyle e_{\alpha_{i}}(j)\cdot u=u^{\prime}+\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j}Q(e_{\alpha_{i}}(j^{\prime}),e_{-\alpha_{i}}(l-j^{\prime}))u_{j^{\prime}}

and hence
e_{\alpha_{i}}(j)\cdot u\in U_{(q-1)}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
. The first statement holds.

In order to prove (
4.32
) again it suffices to assume
x=e_{\alpha_{i}}(j)
with the same reason. Assume
u\in(U_{k}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l}))_{r}
with
q=k+r
. By the definition of
\tau_{(q-1)}
and (
4.35
) one has

\displaystyle\tau_{(q-1)}(e_{\alpha_{i}}(j)\cdot u)=\tau_{k}(u^{\prime})+\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j}Q(e_{\alpha_{i}}(j^{\prime}),e_{-\alpha_{i}}(l-j^{\prime}))\tau_{k-1}(u_{j^{\prime}}).

Since
\tau_{k}
commutes with the adjoint action, by (
4.34
) and Proposition
3.14
then one has

\displaystyle e_{\alpha_{i}}(j)\cdot(\tau_{q}(u))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[e_{\alpha_{i}}(j),\tau_{q}(u)]^{\eta}=\big(\tau_{q-1}([e_{\alpha_{i}}(j),\tau_{q}(u)]\big)^{\eta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\tau_{(q-1)}(u^{\prime}+\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j}u_{j^{\prime}}e_{\alpha_{i}}(j))\right)^{\eta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\tau_{(q-1)}(u^{\prime})+\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j}\tau_{(q)}(u_{j^{\prime}})e_{\alpha_{i}}(j^{\prime})\right)^{\eta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{k}(u^{\prime})+\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j}Q(e_{\alpha_{i}}(j^{\prime}),e_{-\alpha_{i}}(l-j^{\prime}))\tau_{k-1}(u_{j^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau_{(q-1)}(e_{\alpha_{i}}(j)\cdot u).

This finishes the proof.∎

Lemma 4.11
.

For any
u\in U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l}),v\in W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
and
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{l}
one has

(4.36)

\displaystyle x\cdot(uv)=(x\cdot u)v

and hence

(4.37)

\displaystyle x\cdot v=0.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.6
there exists a unique
w\in Z(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
such that

. Since
u\in U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
one has
u=u^{\eta}
and
uw-u^{\eta}w^{\eta}=u(w-w^{\eta})\in U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})U_{\eta}(\mathfrak{n}_{l})
. Then
uv=(uw)^{\eta}
and then

by Lemma
4.9
. However
[x,uw]=[x,u]w
since

is central. Similar to

one has

. Thus

. However

by Lemma

, this proves (
4.36
).

In particular by takeing

, this proves (
4.37
).
∎

5.
Whittaker modules

In this section we classify all nonsingular Whittaker modules over Takiff algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{l}
. In Subsection 5.1, the proof has a little difference from that of
[
11
]
. In Subsection 5.2, the proof for all main results is just the duplication of those in
[
11
]
. So we write detailed proof in Subsection 5.1 and we only list main results without proof in Subsection 5.2.

5.1.

For any
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
-module
V
we let
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{V}
be the annihilator of
V
. Then
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{V}
defines an ideal
T_{V}=T\cap U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{V}
for any subalgebra
T\subseteq U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
.

Now let
V
be any
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
-module. For any
u\in U(\mathfrak{g}_{l}),v\in V
the action is denoted by
uv\in V
. A vector
w\in V
is called a Whittaker vector of type
\eta
if

\displaystyle xw=\eta(x)w,\;\forall x\in\mathfrak{n}_{l}.

A
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
-module
V
is called a Whittaker module if it is cyclically generated by a Whittaker vector.

Now we assume that
V
is a Whittaker module generated by a Whittaker vector
w
. Let
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{w}
be the annihilator of
w
. Then
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{w}
is a left ideal,
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{V}
is a two-sided ideal and
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{V}\subseteq U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{w}
. Clearly one has
V\cong U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})/U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{w}
as
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
-modules.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X=\{v\in U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})|(x\cdot v)w=0\;for\;all\;x\in\mathfrak{n}_{l}\}
,
W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})_{V}=(Z(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{V})^{\eta}
and
U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})_{w}=U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})\cap U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{w}
. Then

\displaystyle X=(\widetilde{A}\otimes W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})_{V})+W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})

Moreover, one has

\displaystyle U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})_{w}=\widetilde{A}\otimes W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})_{V}\subseteq X.

Proof.

For any
x\in\mathfrak{n}
and
v\in W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}})_{l}
, one has by (
4.37
) that

\displaystyle(x\cdot v)w=((x\cdot 1)v)w=((x^{\eta}-\eta(x))v)w=0.

Thus
W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})\subseteq X
.

For any
x\in\mathfrak{n}_{l},u\in Z(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{V}
and
v\in U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}})
one has

\displaystyle x\cdot(vu^{\eta})=(x\cdot v)u^{\eta}

by Lemma
4.11
. Clearly
u
and hence
u^{\eta}
is in
U_{w}
. Thus
vu^{\eta}\in X
and therefore

\widetilde{A}\otimes W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}})_{V}\subseteq X
and
hence

\displaystyle(\widetilde{A}\otimes W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})_{V})+W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})\subseteq X.

Let
\overline{W}
be any fixed complement subspace of
W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})_{V}
in
W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}})
. Denoted by

\displaystyle M_{r}=\widetilde{A}_{(r)}\otimes\overline{W},

then one has

\displaystyle M=\bigoplus_{r=1}^{\infty}M_{r},

and

\displaystyle U(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})=M\oplus(\widetilde{A}\otimes W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})_{V}+W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})).

Let
M_{[k]}=\sum_{r=1}^{k}M_{r}
so that the spaces
\{M_{[k]}|k\geq 1\}
are a filtration of
M
. Assume
X\cap M\not=0
and let
k
be the minimal such that there exists
0\not=y\in X\cap M_{[k]}
. Then
y
can be uniquely written as

\displaystyle y=\sum_{r=1}^{k}y_{r}

such that
y_{r}\in\widetilde{A}_{(r)}\otimes W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}})
. Of course one has
y_{k}\not=0
.

Noting that
\widetilde{A}
is an
\mathfrak{n}_{l}
-module with respect to
x\star v
. For each
1\leq i\leq n,0\leq j\leq l
, let
C_{i,j}=\{v\in\widetilde{A}_{(k)}|e_{\beta_{i}}(j)*v=0\}
. One has

\displaystyle\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\bigcap_{j=0}^{l}C_{i,j}=0

by Lemma
4.14
. Hence there exists
(i,j)
such that
y_{k}\not\in C_{i,j}\otimes\overline{W}
.

For any
q\in\mathbb{N}
let

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{[q]}=\sum_{p\leq q}\widetilde{A}_{(p)}.

By Lemma
4.15
one has
e_{\beta_{i}}(j)\cdot y_{r}\in\widetilde{A}_{[k-2]}\otimes W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}})
for
1\leq r\leq k-1
.

If
D_{i,j}
is the linear complement to
C_{i,j}
in
\widetilde{A}_{(k)}
we may write
y_{k}=u_{k}+v_{k}
such that

\displaystyle u_{k}\in C_{i,j}\otimes\overline{W},v_{k}\in D_{i,j}\otimes\overline{W}

and
v_{k}\not=0
. But now
e_{\beta_{i}}(j)\cdot u_{k}\in\widetilde{A}_{[k-2]}\otimes W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}})
by Lemma
4.15
. On the other hand the map

D_{i,j}\rightarrow\widetilde{A}_{(k-1)}
given by
u\mapsto e_{\beta_{i}}(j)*u
is injective. Thus one has
e_{\beta_{i}}(j)\cdot v_{k}=z_{k-1}+s^{\prime}
such that
s^{\prime}\in\widetilde{A}_{[k-2]}\otimes W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}})
and

\displaystyle 0\not=z_{k-1}\in\widetilde{A}_{(k-1)}\otimes\overline{W}=M_{k-1}.

So we can assume

(5.1)

\displaystyle e_{\beta_{i}}(j)\cdot y=z_{k-1}+s

for some
s\in\widetilde{A}_{[k-2]}\otimes W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}})
.

It is clear that
W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})\cap U_{w}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})\supseteq W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})_{V}
. If
v\in W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})\cap U_{w}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
then it can be uniquely written as
v=u^{\eta}
for some
u\in Z(\mathfrak{g}_{l})
. But
vw=0
implies
uw=0
and hence
u\in Z(\mathfrak{g}_{l})\cap U_{w}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
. Since
V
is cyclically generated by
w
, this implies
v=u^{\eta}\in W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})_{V}
. Thus one has

\displaystyle W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})\cap U_{w}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})=W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})_{V}.

By the definition of
X
one has
e_{\beta_{i}}(j)\cdot y\in U_{w}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
. Return to (
5.1
).

If
k=1
, then
s=0
and
0\not=z_{0}=e_{\beta_{i}}(j)\cdot y\in\overline{W}\cap U_{w}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}})
but which is zero. One obtains a contradiction. Then
k>1
and

e_{\beta_{i}}(j)\cdot y\in X
. Write
W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}})=\overline{W}\oplus W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}})_{V}
we may write
s=s_{1}+s_{2}
where

and
s_{2}\in(\widetilde{A}\otimes W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})_{V})+W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
. Put
M_{[0]}=0
. Thus
e_{\beta_{i}}(j)\cdot y=t_{2}+s_{2}

. However,

. Thus

. Again one obtains a contradiction. Hence

.

Applying
\rho^{\eta}
to
U(\mathfrak{g}_{l})Z(\mathfrak{g}_{l})_{V}\subseteq U_{w}

\widetilde{A}\otimes W(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})_{V}\subseteq U_{w}(\overline{\mathfrak{b}}_{l})
. By

7.1.

The Hamiltonian system which is a dynamical system and completely described by a function

stands for the state of the dynamical system, where

Applying to a system of particles moving on a line with respect to some potential, we envision

(7.1)

\displaystyle H=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{p_{i}^{2}}{2m_{i}}+U,

which describes the evolution in time of a mechanical system with total energy

is the state of the system at time

. Then

(7.2)

\displaystyle\frac{dp_{j}(x(t))}{dt}=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial q_{j}}(x(t)),

\displaystyle\frac{dq_{j}(x(t))}{dt}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_{j}}(x(t)).

In particular, when

and

U=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}e^{q_{i}-q_{i+1}}

the system is called the Toda lattice.

As usual one deals with the equation (
7.2
) by introducing a symplectic structure determined by a closed non-singular
2
-form

(7.3)

\displaystyle\omega=\sum_{i=1}^{n}dp_{i}\wedge dq_{i}.

This
2
-form defines a Poisson bracket
[,]
which makes
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
to be a Lie algebra. In particular one has
[p_{i},q_{j}]=\delta_{i,j}
. Regarding
p_{i}
as the partial derivative
\frac{\partial}{\partial q_{i}}
then
H
becomes an elliptic differential operator on functions in the variables
q_{i}
since all
m_{i}
are positive.

Furthermore there exists a symmetric bilinear form
B_{H}
such that

(7.4)

\displaystyle B_{H}(\varphi,\psi)1=[\varphi,[\psi,H]],\;\forall\varphi,\psi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n}).

In particular one has

(7.5)

\displaystyle B_{H}(q_{i},q_{j})=\frac{\delta_{i,j}}{m_{i}},

which defines a positive definite symmetric bilinear form on the space
\mathcal{Q}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{R}}q_{i}
.

Now considering
\mathfrak{g}
as a simple Lie algebra of rank
l
with simple roots
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{l}
(corresponding to a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
). Assume that

\displaystyle U=\sum_{i=1}^{l}r_{i}e^{\psi_{i}}

for some positive constants
r_{i}
and functions
\psi_{i}\in\mathcal{Q}
. We say that the system is associative to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
if

(7.6)

\displaystyle B_{H}(\psi_{i},\psi_{j})=\kappa(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})

for all
i,j=1,\cdots,l
, where
\kappa
is a positive multiple of the Killing form. In this case the Hamiltonian system sometimes is called the Bogoyavlensky-Toda lattice which was introduced by Bogoyavlensky in
[
2
]
.

7.2.

Return to the system of particles. Now we only focus on the fundamental invariant
I=\frac{1}{2}Q
of
S(\mathfrak{g}_{l})^{G_{l}}
. Explicitly one has

(7.7)

\displaystyle I=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{l}h_{i}(j)\omega_{i}(l-j)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{l}e_{-\alpha}(j)e_{\alpha}(l-j).

Thus for any

\displaystyle y={\bf f}+\sum_{1\leq i\leq n\atop 0\leq j\leq l}\rho_{i,j}h_{i}(j)+\sum_{1\leq i\leq n\atop 0\leq j\leq l}\gamma_{i,j}e_{\alpha_{i}}(j),

one has

\displaystyle I(y)=\frac{1}{2}Q(y,y)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,r=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{l}c_{i,r}\rho_{i,j}\rho_{r,l-j}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{l}\gamma_{i,j},

where
(c_{i,r})
is the Cartan matrix..

Next one considers the case
l=1
. Thus

\displaystyle I(y)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,r=1}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{l}c_{i,r}\rho_{i,0}\rho_{r,1}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\gamma_{i,0}+\gamma_{i,1}).

The matrix
[t^{\prime}_{i;j,s}]
is

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\gamma_{i,0}\\
-\gamma_{i,0}&-\gamma_{i,1}\end{array}\right]

and hence

\displaystyle T_{i}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{-\gamma_{i,1}}/{\gamma_{i,0}}^{2}&{\gamma_{i,0}}^{-1}\\
{\gamma_{i,0}}^{-1}&0\end{array}\right].

The symplectic structure of manifold
Z
then is given by

\displaystyle\omega_{Z}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-\frac{\gamma_{i,1}}{{\gamma_{i,0}}^{2}}d\rho_{i,0}\wedge d\gamma_{i,0}+\frac{1}{{\gamma_{i,0}}}d\rho_{i,0}\wedge d\gamma_{i,1}+\frac{1}{{\gamma_{i,0}}}d\rho_{i,1}\wedge d\gamma_{i,0}).

Let
\gamma_{i,0}=\exp(\phi_{i,0}),\gamma_{i,1}/\gamma_{i,0}=\phi_{i,1}
for some smooth functions
\phi_{i,0},\phi_{i,1}
where
\phi_{i,1}>0
. Then one has

(7.10)

\displaystyle\omega_{Z}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d\rho_{i,0}\wedge d\phi_{i,1}+d\rho_{i,1}\wedge d\phi_{i,0}).

The corresponding Hamiltonian is

(7.11)

\displaystyle H=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}c_{i,r}\rho_{i,0}\rho_{j,1}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(1+\phi_{i,1})e^{\phi_{i,0}}.

Theorem 7.1
.

The system described by (
7.11
) is completely integrable in the Liouville sense.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
6.3
.
∎

Example 1:
(1) Let
n=1
and denoting

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x},\frac{\partial}{\partial y},x,y\right)=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi_{1,0}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi_{1,1}},\phi_{1,0},\phi_{1,1}\right),

then one has

\displaystyle H=2\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+(1+y)e^{x},\;(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+},

which is a hyperbolic differential operator over the upper half plane.

(2) Let
n=1
and denoting

\displaystyle\left(p,\overline{p},q,\overline{q}\right)=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi_{1,0}},\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi_{1,1}},\phi_{1,0},\phi_{1,1}\right),

then one obtains

\displaystyle H=2p\overline{p}+(1+\overline{q})e^{q},

which is an explicit example of (
1.3
) with
U=(1+\overline{q})e^{q}
. Because
\overline{q}
denotes the difference of the positions of two particles, here the condition
\overline{q}>0
can be understood as that the paired particles can’t exchange their positions on the line.

The differential operator corresponding to (
7.11
) is of hyperbolic type which acts on functions with domain
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{+})^{n}
.

In general, one considers the following system:

(7.12)

\displaystyle H=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i,j}p_{i}\overline{p_{j}}+U,

(7.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_{i}}=\frac{d{\overline{q}_{i}}}{dt},\;\frac{\partial H}{\partial\overline{p}_{i}}=\frac{d{q}_{i}}{dt},\;\frac{\partial H}{\partial q_{i}}=-\frac{d{p}_{i}}{dt},\;\frac{\partial H}{\partial\overline{q}_{i}}=-\frac{d\overline{p}_{i}}{dt},

where
\overline{q}_{i}>0
,
(c_{i,j})
is the Cartan matrix of
\mathfrak{g}
, the smooth functions
{p}_{i},\overline{p}_{i},{q}_{i},\overline{q}_{i}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{4n})
are a linear canonical coordinate system of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{+})^{n})
and the potential function
U
is only dependent on the
q_{i},\overline{q}_{i}
.

We refer this system to a hyperbolic Toda lattice corresponding to the Takiff algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
. Of course, the invariant
I=\frac{1}{2}Q
of generalized Takiff algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{l}
suggests a more generalized Hamiltonian system.

7.3.

Now considering the hyperbolic Toda lattice in the above example, then one has

\displaystyle\frac{d\overline{q}}{dt}=2\overline{p},

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}\overline{q}}{dt^{2}}=\frac{2d\overline{p}}{dt}=2e^{q},

\displaystyle\frac{d^{3}\overline{q}}{dt^{3}}=\frac{2de^{q}}{dt}=4pe^{q},

\displaystyle\frac{d^{4}\overline{q}}{dt^{4}}=\frac{4pe^{q}}{dt}=4e^{q}\left(2p^{2}+\frac{dp}{dt}\right)=4e^{q}\left(2p^{2}+(1+\overline{q})e^{q}\right),

and hence

\displaystyle\frac{d^{4}\overline{q}}{dt^{4}}\frac{d^{2}\overline{q}}{dt^{2}}=\left(\frac{d^{3}\overline{q}}{dt^{3}}\right)^{2}+(1+\overline{q})\left(\frac{d^{2}\overline{q}}{dt^{2}}\right)^{3}.

Furthermore, it is obvious that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left({\frac{d^{3}\overline{q}}{dt^{3}}}\Big/{\frac{d^{2}\overline{q}}{dt^{2}}}\right)=\frac{d}{dt}\left(\overline{q}+\frac{\overline{q}^{2}}{2}\right).

Thus in order to solving the Hamiltonian equation, by letting
\overline{q}=2\Psi(t)
it suffices to solving the ordinary differential equation

(7.14)

\displaystyle\Psi^{{}^{\prime\prime\prime}}(t)=C_{0}\Psi^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(t)+\Psi(t)^{2}+\Psi(t)

for any constant
C_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. It is known that equation (
7.14
) is always (at least locally) solvable for any initial values

\displaystyle\Psi(t_{0})=C_{1},\Psi^{{}^{\prime}}(t_{0})=C_{2},\Psi^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(t_{0})=C_{3}.

Example 2:
Now we assume that
|C_{0}|<1
,
t_{0}=0
and

\displaystyle\Psi(t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}t^{n}

solves (
7.14
). Then one has

\displaystyle a_{n+3}=\frac{1}{(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}\left((n+1)(n+2)C_{0}a_{n+2}+a_{n}+\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}a_{n-i}\right),\;\forall n\geq 0.

If
a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}\in(-1,1)
, then

\displaystyle|a_{3}|=\frac{1}{6}\left|2C_{0}a_{2}+a_{0}+2a_{1}a_{2}\right|<1<\frac{2}{1^{2}},

\displaystyle|a_{4}|=\frac{1}{24}\left|6C_{0}a_{3}+a_{1}+2a_{1}a_{3}+a_{2}^{2}\right|<\frac{10}{24}<\frac{2}{2^{2}}

\displaystyle|a_{5}|=\frac{1}{60}\left|12C_{0}a_{4}+a_{2}+2a_{1}a_{4}+2a_{2}a_{3}\right|<\frac{1}{6}<\frac{2}{3^{2}}.

By induction we assume
|a_{k+2}|<\frac{2}{k^{2}}
for
1\leq k\leq n
where
n\geq 3
. Then one has

\displaystyle|a_{n+3}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left|(n+1)(n+2)C_{0}a_{n+2}+a_{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}a_{n-i}\right|}{(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{n+6}{(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}<\frac{2}{(n+1)^{2}}.

Hence for any
C_{0}\in(-1,1)
, the series function
\Psi(t)
in variable
t
absolutely converges to a smooth function on
{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\displaystyle C_{1}=\Psi(0)=a_{0},C_{2}=\Psi^{\prime}(0)=a_{1},C_{3}=\Psi^{\prime\prime}(0)=2a_{2}.

Thus the initial value problem of (
7.14
) is global solvable for bounded initial values.

Moreover, if
C_{0},C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
it follows
\Psi(t)>0
(or equivalently
\overline{q}>0
) at any time
t>0
, this agrees with the condition of Example 1 (2). Immediately one has the following

Theorem 7.2
.

Considering the initial value problem of the dynamical system

(7.15)

\displaystyle\frac{d{\overline{q}}}{dt}=2\overline{p},\;\frac{d{q}}{dt}=2p,\;\frac{d{p}}{dt}=-(1+\overline{q})e^{q},\;\frac{d\overline{p}}{dt}=-e^{q},\quad\overline{q}>0,

with initial values
c_{0}=\overline{q}(0),c_{1}=\overline{q}^{\prime}(0),c_{2}=\overline{q}^{\prime\prime}(0),c_{3}=\overline{q}^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)
. If the initial values satisfy

\displaystyle c_{0},c_{1},\frac{c_{2}}{2},\frac{2(c_{3}-c_{1}^{2}-c_{1})}{c_{2}}\in(0,1),

then (
7.15
) has a unique global smooth solution on
[0,\infty)
.

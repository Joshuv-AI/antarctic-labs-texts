Abstract
. In this paper, we establish some Stein-Weiss type inequalities with general kernels on the upper half space and study the existence of extremal functions for this inequality with the optimal constant. Furthermore, we also investigate the regularity, asymptotic estimates, symmetry and non-existence results of the positive solutions of the corresponding Euler-Lagrange integral system. As an application, we finally derive some Liouville type results for the Hartree type equations in the half space.

Introduction and main results

The research of inequality problems have received a great deal of attention in recent years. The inequalities such as Sobolev inequality, Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality (HLS for short) and Stein-Weiss type inequality play an essential role in the theory of partial differential equations and geometric analysis. For instance, the HLS inequality is widely used to investigate the qualitative properties and classification of solutions. Moreover, the HLS inequality means that sharp Sobolev inequality, as well as Gross’s logarithmic Sobolev inequality, which are key ingredients in the study of Yamabe problem and Ricci flow problem
[
31
]
. Let us first review the classical version of Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, which is found by Hardy, Littlewood and Sobolev in
[
33
,
48
]
.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
1<p,\penalty\ q^{\prime}<\infty
,
0<\mu<n
,
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
g\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then there exists a sharp constant
C\left(p,q^{\prime},\mu,n\right)
such that

(1.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(y)g(x)}{|x-y|^{\mu}}dxdy\leq C(p,q^{\prime},n,\mu)\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{q^{\prime}},

where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{\mu}{n}=2
and
C(p,q^{\prime},n,\mu)
is independent of
f
and
g
.
Moreover, the optimal constant satisfy

C\left(p,q^{\prime},\mu,n\right)\leq\frac{n}{(n-\mu)}\left(|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|/n\right)^{\frac{\mu}{n}}\frac{1}{pq^{\prime}}\left(\left(\frac{\mu/n}{1-1/p}\right)^{\mu/n}+\left(\frac{\mu/n}{1-q^{\prime}}\right)^{\mu/n}\right),

where we use
q^{\prime}
to stand for the dual index of
q
.

If one of
p
or
q^{\prime}
equals
2
or
p=q^{\prime}
, the existence of extremals to the HLS inequality with the optimal constant was discussed by Lieb
[
40
]

1.1
). However, for

, either the sharp constant or the extremals is not known. Later, with the aid of the reflection positivity of inversions in spheres, Frank and Lieb
[
28
]
explored the best constant and extremals to this inequality in the special case
p=q^{\prime}=\frac{2n}{2n-\mu}
. It was also studied by Carlen and Loss
[
6

via the competing symmetry argument. For the case
\mu=n-2
, Carlen et al.
[
7

simplified the proof and obtained the sharp version of inequality (
1.1
) with
n\geq 3
. More generally, Frank and Lieb
[
30

considered the sharp constant of inequality (
1.1
) under the more general parameters by using the rearrangement argument. The HLS inequality on the Heisenberg groups was firstly established in
[
27

by Folland and Stein, and the authors in
[
29

[

\hat{o}

Stein and Weiss in

(1.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(y)g(x)}{|x|^{\alpha}|x-y|^{\mu}|y|^{\beta}}dxdy\leq C(p,q^{\prime},n,\mu,\alpha,\beta)\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{q^{\prime}},

where
p,q^{\prime}>1
,
0<\mu<n
,
\alpha+\beta\geq 0
and the double weights also satisfies
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{\alpha+\beta+\mu}{n}=2
and
1-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\mu}{n}<\frac{\alpha}{n}<1-\frac{1}{p}
. It is well known that, for the case
p<q

[
40

proved that the extremals for this inequality can be obtained with the restriction

1.2

p=q

37

[

[
10

In fact, many people are also interested in the extremal functions and sharp constant of the integral inequalities on upper half space. In
[
34
]
, Hang, Wang and Yan firstly established the following integral inequality with harmonic kernel,

(1.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{x_{n}f(y)g(x)}{\left(|x^{\prime}-y|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}}dxdy\leq C(n)\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}\|g\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)},\penalty\ \penalty\ x=\left(x^{\prime},\penalty\ x_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\penalty\ y\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

where
1<p,q^{\prime}<\infty
, satisfying
\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
. Actually, inequality (
1.3
) can be
regarded as Carleman’s inequality in the higher dimension, and its implies the sharp isoperimetric inequality
[
35
]
. The authors considered the existence of extremal function for inequality (
1.3
) through the method of symmetrization and the concentration compactness principle, and they also discussed a sequence of qualitative properties of extremal functions including regularity and symmetry. Later, Chen
[
13

generalized the above inequality to the more general inequality (
1.3

\|P_{\mu}f\|_{L^{\frac{np}{n-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C(n,\mu,p)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})},

for all
1<p\leq\infty
and
n\geq 2
, where

P_{\mu}(f)(x):=\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{x_{n}^{\mu+1-n}}{\left(|x^{\prime}-y|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\mu}{2}}}f(y)dy

is the poly-harmonic extension operator. Moreover, the author classified the positive extremals via the rearrangement method in the case
p=\frac{2(n-1)}{2n-2-\mu}

[
24

explored the sharp HLS inequality on the half space. With the help of Riesz’s rearrangement technique, they proved the existence of extremals and computed explicitly the sharp constant. Recently, Gluck
[
32

obtained the following sharp inequalities with the a family of kernels on the upper half space

(1.4)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}K\left(x^{\prime}-y,x_{n}\right)f(y)g(x)dxdy\right|\leq C(n,\mu,\lambda,p)\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}\|g\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)},

where
K
is a family of kernels

K(x)=K_{\mu,\lambda}(x)=\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}}{\left(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\mu}{2}}}.

It’s worth showing that, this kernel

includes the Riesz kernel and the classical Poisson kernel. By a subcritical method, Gulck computed the best constant for a family of HLS type inequalities (
1.4

[
44

generalized the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality with
general kernel in the conformal invariant case for all critical indices. The reverse inequalities on the upper half spaces are also widely studied. In
[
20

K
.
For the doubly weighted case, the Stein-Weiss type inequality on the half space was established by Dou
[
21

1.3

(1.5)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P(x,y,\mu)f(y)g(x)|x|^{-\beta}dxdy\leq C(n,p,q^{\prime},\alpha,\beta,\mu)\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}\|g\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)},

where
1<p,q^{\prime}<\infty
,
2\leq\mu<n
satisfying

\displaystyle\alpha<\frac{n-1}{p^{\prime}},\beta<\frac{n+q}{q},\alpha+\beta\geq 0,

\displaystyle\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{\alpha+\beta+2-\mu}{n}=1,

and

. By applying the rearrangement approach and Lorentz interpolation inequality, they studied the existence of extremals of the sharp constant for inequality (
1.5

\check{z}

In this paper, we are devoted to study the existence of extremal functions of the Stein-Weiss type inequality with the optimal constant with a general kernel in the half space
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}

[
32

First of all, we will investigate the Stein-Weiss type inequality with a general kernel. The first main result of this paper is the following integral inequality.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
1<p,q^{\prime}<\infty
,
\lambda\geq 0
,
\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}\geq 1
,
0<\mu<n-2\lambda
and
\frac{\mu-2\lambda}{2n}+\frac{\mu}{2(n-1)}<1
satisfies

(1.6)

\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{\alpha+\beta+\mu-\lambda}{n}=\frac{2n-1}{n}

with
\alpha<\frac{n-1}{p^{\prime}}
,
\beta<\frac{n+q}{q}
and
\alpha+\beta\geq 0
. Then there exists some positive constant
C(n,p,q^{\prime},\alpha,\beta,\lambda,\mu)
such that for any functions
f\in L^{p}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
and
g\in L^{q^{\prime}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
, it holds that

(1.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)f(y)g(x)|x|^{-\beta}dxdy\leq C(n,p,q^{\prime},\alpha,\beta,\lambda,\mu)\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}\|g\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)},

where
P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)=\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}}{\left(|x^{\prime}-y|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\mu}{2}}}

with
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

In fact, by defining the following integral operator with double weights

V(f)(x):=\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)f(y)|x|^{-\beta}dy,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

and

W(g)(y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)g(x)|x|^{-\beta}dx,\ \ y\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

inequality (
1.7
) is equivalent to the following weighted inequality via a dual argument

(1.8)

\|V(f)\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}\leq C(n,p,q^{\prime},\alpha,\beta,\lambda,\mu)\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)},

and

(1.9)

\|W(g)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}\leq C(n,p,q^{\prime},\alpha,\beta,\lambda,\mu)\|g\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}

where
\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\alpha+\beta+\mu-\lambda-n+1}{n}=\frac{1}{q}
and
\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=\frac{n}{n-1}\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{\alpha+\beta+\mu-\lambda-n}{n-1}
.

Next, based on symmetric rearrangement inequality, we shall prove that the existence of extremal functions to inequality (
1.7
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
1<p,q^{\prime}<\infty
,
\lambda\geq 0
,
\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}\geq 1
,
0<\mu<n-2\lambda
and
\frac{\mu-2\lambda}{2n}+\frac{\mu}{2(n-1)}<1
satisfying

(1.10)

\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{\alpha+\beta+\mu-\lambda}{n}=\frac{2n-1}{n}

with
\alpha<\frac{n-1}{p^{\prime}}
,
\beta<\frac{n+q}{q}
and
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
. Then there exists some positive functions
f\in L^{p}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
satisfying
\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}=1
and
\|V(f)\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}=C(n,p,q^{\prime},\alpha,\beta,\lambda,\mu)
. Moreover, if
\left(f(y),g(x)\right)
is a pair of maximizer of inequality (
1.7
), then
f(y)
is radially symmetric and monotone decreasing about the origin, and there exists some positive constant
c_{0}
such that
g(x)=c_{0}V(f)(x)
.

Furthermore, we will study the properties of these extremal functions. For this goal, we may maximize the following functional

(1.11)

J(f,g)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)f(y)g(x)|x|^{-\beta}dxdy,

under the assumption
\|f\|_{L^{p}({\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})}=\|g\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})}=1
. According to the Euler-Lagrange multiplier theorem, we can deduce the following integral system with double weights

(1.12)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &J(f,g)g(x)^{q^{\prime}-1}=\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)f(y)|x|^{-\beta}dy,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
&J(f,g)f(y)^{p-1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)g(x)|x|^{-\beta}dx,&y\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.\end{aligned}\right.

In particular, we assume that
u=c_{1}f^{p-1}
,
v=c_{2}g^{q^{\prime}-1}
,
q_{0}=\frac{1}{q^{\prime}-1}
and
p_{0}=\frac{1}{p-1}
with two suitable constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
in (
1.12
), the system can be rewritten as

(1.13)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u(y)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|x|^{-\beta}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v^{q_{0}}(x)|y|^{-\alpha}dx,&y\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
v(x)&=\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)u^{p_{0}}(y)|x|^{-\beta}dy,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.\end{aligned}\right.

where
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
,
0<\mu<n-2\lambda
and
p_{0},q_{0}
satisfies the identity
\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p_{0}+1}+\frac{1}{q_{0}+1}=\frac{\alpha+\beta+\mu-\lambda}{n}
.

By applying the regularity lifting arguments, we can derive the regularity results of the positive solutions for system (
1.13
) under the integral assumption.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that
0<\alpha,\beta<n
,
0<\mu<n-2\lambda
,
0<p_{0},q_{0}<\infty
.
Let
\left(u,v\right)\in L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{q_{0}+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
be a pair of positive solutions of the integral system (
1.13
). Then
\left(u,v\right)\in L^{r}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
with

\frac{1}{r}\in\left(\frac{\alpha}{n-1},\frac{\alpha+\mu-\lambda+1}{n-1}\right)\cap\left(\frac{1}{p_{0}+1}-\frac{n}{n-1}\frac{1}{q_{0}+1}+\frac{\beta-1}{n-1},\frac{1}{p_{0}+1}-\frac{n}{n-1}\frac{1}{q_{0}+1}+\frac{\beta+\mu-\lambda}{n}\right)

and

\frac{1}{s}\in\left(\frac{\beta-1}{n},\frac{\beta+\mu-\lambda}{n}\right)\cap\left(\frac{1}{q_{0}+1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p_{0}+1}+\frac{\alpha}{n},\frac{1}{q_{0}+1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p_{0}+1}+\frac{\alpha+\mu-\lambda+1}{n-1}\right).

Next, we are going to explore the asymptotic behavior of the positive solutions for system (
1.13
). In light of the regularity theorem, we derive the following result.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose that
0<\alpha,\beta<n
,
0<\mu<n-2\lambda
and
p_{0},q_{0}>1
.
Let
\left(u,v\right)\in L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{q_{0}+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
be a pair of positive solutions of the integral system (
1.13

(1)

If
\frac{1}{q_{0}}-\frac{\mu+\beta-\lambda}{q_{0}n}>\frac{\beta-1}{n}

\lim\limits_{|y|\rightarrow 0}u(y)|y|^{\alpha}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}v^{q_{0}}(x)}{|x|^{\mu+\beta}}dx.

(2)

If
\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{\mu+\alpha-\lambda+1}{p_{0}(n-1)}>\frac{\alpha}{n-1}
, then

\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow 0}\frac{v(x)|x|^{\beta}}{x_{n}^{\lambda}}=\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{u^{p_{0}}(y)}{|y|^{\alpha+\mu}}dy.

In the spirit of inequality (
1.7
) and the integral assumption, we will apply the method of moving plane in integral form in

,
18
]
by Chen et al. to study the symmetry property of the solutions.

Theorem 1.6
.

Assume that
0<\alpha,\beta<n
,
0<\mu<n-2\lambda
,
0<p_{0},q_{0}<\infty
. If

\left(u,v\right)\in L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{q_{0}+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
is pair of positive solution of integral system (

and

satisfying

Finally, as a special case, our results can be applied to the study of the following integral system with single weight

(1.14)

\left\{\begin{aligned} u(y)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|x|^{-\beta}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v^{q_{0}}(x)dx,&y\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
v(x)&=\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)u^{p_{0}}(y)dy,&x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.\end{aligned}\right.

By the Poho
\check{z}
aev identity, we study the necessary condition for the existence of non-trivial solutions for the single weighted system (
1.14
).

Theorem 1.7
.

Assume that
0<p_{0}<\infty
,
0<q_{0}<\infty
and
0<\mu<n-2\lambda
. Let
\left(u,v\right)\in C^{1}(\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})\times C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
be a pair of non-negative solutions of the integral system (
1.14

\left(u,v\right)\in L^{p_{0}+1}\left(|y|^{-\alpha}dy,\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{q_{0}+1}\left(|x|^{-\beta}dx,\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right).

Then it must hold that

\frac{n-1-\alpha}{p_{0}+1}+\frac{n-\beta}{q_{0}+1}=\mu-\lambda.

Clearly, according to the above theorem, we have the following Liouville type result for nonnegative solutions of the single weighted integral system (
1.14
).

Corollary 1.8
.

For
0<\mu<n-2\lambda
, assume that

\frac{n-1-\alpha}{p_{0}+1}+\frac{n-\beta}{q_{0}+1}\not=\mu-\lambda,

then there are no non-trivial solutions
\left(u,v\right)\in L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{q_{0}+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
of the integral system (
1.14
).

The second part of this paper is devoted to the study of the Hartree type elliptic equations on half space, we consider

(1.15)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u(x)&=\left(\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{F\left(u(y)\right)}{|x|^{\beta}|x-y|^{\mu}|y|^{\alpha}}dy\right)x_{n}^{\lambda}g\left(u(x)\right),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\upsilon}(y)&=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{G\left(u(x)\right)x_{n}^{\lambda}}{|x|^{\beta}|x-y|^{\mu}|y|^{\alpha}}dx\right)f\left(u(y)\right),\ \ y\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.\end{aligned}\right.

\lambda,\alpha,\mu,\beta
and the functions

will be given specific conditions later. It’s well-known that, Hartree type equation is an essential equation in mathematics and physics for the study of the Hartree-Fock model. It is also widely used in Bose–Einstein condensates theory to study how to avoid collapse phenomena. Moreover, the boson star is used to describe the source of dark matter in classical quantum mechanics. Then problem (
1.15
) plays an important role in development of Black Hole theory. For convenience, the reader may see
[
38
,
41
]
and the references therein for more backgrounds about the Hartree type equations.

), we are ready to study the monotonicity and non-existence results of positive solution for problem (
1.15
) via moving plane argument. In order to present our main result precisely, we firstly give the definition of weak solution to the Hartree type equations (
1.15
).

Definition 1.9
.

We call that
u\in W^{1,2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\cap C^{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})
is a weak solution of Hartree type elliptic equations (
1.15
) if it satisfies for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\nabla u(x)\nabla\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{F(u(y))x_{n}^{\lambda}g(u(x))\varphi(x)}{|x|^{\beta}|x-y|^{\mu}|y|^{\alpha}}dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{G(u(x))x_{n}^{\lambda}f(u(y))\varphi(y)}{|x|^{\beta}|x-y|^{\mu}|y|^{\alpha}}dxdy

on the upper half space.

Now we are in a position to present our main results.

Theorem 1.10
.

Assume that
0<\alpha,\beta<n
,
0<\lambda<n
and
0<\mu<n-2\lambda
. Let
u\in W^{1,2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\cap C^{0}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})
be a positive solution of the system (
1.15
). Suppose further the functions
f(t),\penalty\ g(t),\penalty\ F(t),\penalty\ G(t):\left[0,\infty\right)\rightarrow\left[0,\infty\right)
are continuous in
\left[0,\infty\right)
satisfying the following conditions,

(1)

f(t)
,
g(t)
,
F(t)
and
G(t)
are increasing in
\left(0,+\infty\right)
,

(2)

H(t)=\frac{F(t)}{t^{\frac{2(n-1)-(2\alpha+\mu)}{n-2}}}
,
K(t)=\frac{G(t)}{t^{\frac{2n+2\lambda-(2\beta+\mu)}{n-2}}}
,
k(t)=\frac{g(t)}{t^{\frac{n+2\lambda+2-(2\beta+\mu)}{n-2}}}
and

h(t)=\frac{f(t)}{t^{\frac{n-(2\alpha+\mu)}{n-2}}}
are non-increasing in
\left(0,+\infty\right)
.

Then
u
depend only on
x_{n}
.

It is worth observing that Theorem
1.10
provide a useful method to discuss the non-existence of positive solutions for Hartree type equations (
1.15
) with the special case
\alpha=\beta=0
. The main content is the next result.

Corollary 1.11
.

Under the assumption of Theorem
1.10
, Suppose that at least one of the functions
h
,
k
,
H
and
K
is not a constant in
\Big(0,\sup\limits_{y\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}u(y)\Big)
or
\Big(0,\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}u(x)\Big)
. Then

with
F(\tilde{c})=G(\tilde{c})=0
.

Then we apply the Riesz’s rearrangement inequality and Lorentz norm to obtain the existence of extremals for this inequality. In Section 3, by applying the regularity lifting argument and the method of moving plane in integral form, we obtain the qualitative properities of the non-negative solutions to the integral system with the double weights. In Section 4, by using the Poho

2.1.
Stein-Weiss type inequality

In this subsection, we will use the following integral estimates to prove Theorem
1.2
. The method of these integral estimates on the upper half space was established in
[

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
W(x)
and
U(y)
are two positive locally integrable functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
respectively, for
1<p\leq q<\infty
and
f
is non-negative on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, then

(2.1)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}W(x)\left(\int_{B^{n-1}_{|x|}}f(y)dy\right)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C(p,q)\left(\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f^{p}(y)U(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

holds if and only if

(2.2)

A_{0}=\sup\limits_{R>0}\left\{\left(\int_{|x|\geq R}W(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{|y|\leq R}U^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\right\}<\infty.

While,

(2.3)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}W(x)\left(\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash B^{n-1}_{|x|}dy}f(y)\right)^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C(p,q)\left(\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f^{p}(y)U(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

holds if and only if

(2.4)

A_{1}=\sup\limits_{R>0}\left\{\left(\int_{|x|\leq R}W(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{|y|\geq R}U^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\right\}<\infty.

By applying the above Lemma, we are ready to complete the proof of Theorem
1.2

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.
Without loss of generality, we may suppose that function
f
is positive. In addition, we consider the double weighted integral operator related to a general kernel as follows

P_{\lambda}(f)(x)=\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)f(y)dy.

Obviously, we note that inequality (
1.7
) is equivalent to the following inequality,

\|P_{\lambda}(f)|x|^{-\beta}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C(n,\alpha,\beta,\lambda,\mu,p,q^{\prime})\|f|y|^{\alpha}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

Since
q>1
, we may split the integral items into the following three parts, that is

\|P_{\lambda}(f)|x|^{-\beta}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\lesssim P_{\lambda,1}+P_{\lambda,2}+P_{\lambda,3},

where

\displaystyle P_{\lambda,1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\left(|x|^{-\beta}\int_{B^{n-1}_{\frac{|x|}{2}}}\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}f(y)}{\left(|x^{\prime}-y|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\mu}{2}}}dy\right)^{q}dx,

\displaystyle P_{\lambda,2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\left(|x|^{-\beta}\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash B^{n-1}_{2|x|}}\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}f(y)}{\left(|x^{\prime}-y|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\mu}{2}}}dy\right)^{q}dx,

and

P_{\lambda,3}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\left(|x|^{-\beta}\int_{B^{n-1}_{2|x|}\backslash B^{n-1}_{\frac{|x|}{2}}}\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}f(y)}{\left(|x^{\prime}-y|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\mu}{2}}}dy\right)^{q}dx.

Based on the above analysis, we only need to prove

P_{\lambda,i}\leq C(n,\alpha,\beta,\lambda,\mu,p,q^{\prime})\|f|y|^{\alpha}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}^{q},\quad i=1,2,3.

Firstly, we estimate
P_{\lambda,1}
. From the definition of
P_{\lambda,1}

(2.5)

\displaystyle P_{\lambda,1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\left(|x|^{-\beta}\int_{B^{n-1}_{\frac{|x|}{2}}}\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}f(y)}{\left(|x^{\prime}-y|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\mu}{2}}}dy\right)^{q}dx

Set

and
U(y)=|y|^{\alpha p}
, according to Lemma
2.1
, to verify

P_{\lambda,1}\leq C(n,\alpha,\beta,\lambda,\mu,p,q^{\prime})\|f|y|^{\alpha}\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})},

one only need to show that
W(x)
and
U(y)
satisfies (
2.2

\alpha<\frac{n-1}{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq R}W(x)dx

\displaystyle=C(n,\beta,\lambda,\mu,q)R^{n-\beta q-(\mu-\lambda)q},

and

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\leq R}U^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{|y|\leq R}\left(|y|^{\alpha p}\right)^{1-p^{\prime}}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{s^{n-2}}d\nu\int_{0}^{R}r^{\alpha p(1-p^{\prime})}dr

\displaystyle=C(n,\alpha,p)R^{\alpha p(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}.

It follows from (

\displaystyle\left(\int_{|x|\geq R}W(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{|y|\leq R}U^{1-p^{\prime}}(y)dy\right)^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle<C(n,\alpha,\beta,\lambda,\mu,p,q^{\prime})R^{-\beta-(\mu-\lambda)+\frac{n}{q}+\frac{\alpha p(1-p^{\prime})+n-1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=C(n,\alpha,\beta,\lambda,\mu,p,q^{\prime}).

Next, we consider

|y|\geq 2x

P_{\lambda,2}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|x|^{(-\beta+\lambda)q}\left(\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash B^{n-1}_{2|x|}}f(y)|y|^{-\mu}dy\right)^{q}dx\leq C(n,\alpha,\beta,\lambda,\mu,p,q^{\prime})\|f|y|^{\alpha}\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

We only need to check that

and

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq R}W(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{|x|\geq R}|x|^{(-\beta+\lambda)q}dx=\int_{\partial B_{1}^{+}}d\nu\int_{R}^{\infty}r^{(-\beta+\lambda)q}dr

\displaystyle=C(n,\beta,\lambda,q)R^{(-\beta+\lambda)q+n},

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\leq R}U^{1-{p^{\prime}}}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{|y|\leq R}|y|^{\left((\mu+\alpha)p\right)1-{p^{\prime}}}dy=\int_{s^{n-2}}d\nu\int_{0}^{R}r^{\left((\mu+\alpha)p\right)1-{p^{\prime}}}dr

\displaystyle=C(n,\alpha\mu,p)R^{(\mu+\alpha)p(1-p^{\prime}+n-1)}.

Combining the above estimates, it’s easy to find the condition (

Finally, we estimate

Furthermore, we get

\displaystyle P_{\lambda,3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\left(|x|^{-\beta}\int_{B^{n-1}_{2|x|}\backslash B^{n-1}_{\frac{|x|}{2}}}\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}f(y)}{\left(|x^{\prime}-y|^{2}+x_{n}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\mu}{2}}}dy\right)^{q}dx

Under the assumptions of Theorem

P_{\lambda,3}\leq C(n,\alpha,\beta,\lambda,\mu,p,q^{\prime})\|f|y|^{\alpha}\|^{q}_{L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

Therefore, the proof is completed.

The extremal functions for inequality (

In this subsection, we will prove the existence of extremal functions for inequality (

(2.8)

C(\alpha,\beta,\mu,\lambda,p,q^{\prime},n):=\sup\left\{\|V(f)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}:f\geq 0,\ \ \|f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\right\}.

We shall exploit that the existence of maximiziers to the above supreme problem via the Riesz rearrangement and Lorentz norm, which implies that the extremal functions of inequality (

f
on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, and we introduce the Lorentz norm with
0<r,s<+\infty

\|f\|_{L^{r,s}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{t}^{\frac{1}{r}}f^{*}(t)\right)^{s}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}},&\mbox{if}\ \ s<\infty,\\
&\ \ \sup_{t>0}t^{\frac{1}{p}}f^{*}(t),&\mbox{if}\ \ s=\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

where
f^{*}(t)
denote the decreasing and radially symmetric rearrangement function to
f

Clearly, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
, given some positive functions
f
,
g
and
h
, then the following Riesz rearrangement inequality holds (See
[
39
,
2

(2.9)

I(f,g,h)\leq I(f^{*},g^{*},h^{*}),

with
I(f,g,h):=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\int_{\partial R^{n}_{+}}f(x)g(x-y)h(y)dxdy
.

In the following, we are ready to investigate the existence of the extremal functions.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Suppose that
\left\{f_{j}\right\}_{j}
is a maximizing sequence of the maximizing problem (
2.8
), i.e.

\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\|V(f_{j})\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=C(\alpha,\beta,\mu,\lambda,p,q^{\prime},n).

It follows from (

\|f^{*}_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=1,\ \ \mbox{and}\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\|V(f_{j})\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\|V(f^{*}_{j})\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})},

since
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
. As a consequence, we know that
\left\{f_{j}\right\}_{j}
is a positive radially non-increasing sequence. Next we take any

and set
f^{\kappa}_{j}=\kappa^{-\frac{n-1}{p}}f(\frac{y}{\kappa})
with
\kappa>0

\|f^{\kappa}_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}=\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\|V(f_{j}^{\kappa})\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\|V(f^{\kappa}_{j})\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})},

which implies that
\left\{f_{j}^{\kappa}\right\}_{j}
is still a maximizing sequence to problem (
2.8
). Furthermore, we write

e_{1}:=\left(1,0,...,0\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\ \ A_{j}:=\sup_{\kappa>0}f_{j}^{\kappa}(e_{1})=\sup_{\kappa>0}\kappa^{-\frac{n-1}{p}}f_{j}\left(\frac{e_{1}}{\kappa}\right).

By direct calculation, we get

(2.10)

0\leq f_{j}(y)\leq A_{j}|y|^{-\frac{n-1}{p}}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \|f_{j}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq w_{n-2}^{\frac{1}{p}}A_{j}.

Moreover, according to the Marcinkiewicz interpolation
[
45
,
46
]
with (
1.8

\|V(f)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\leq C(\alpha,\beta,\mu,\lambda,p,q^{\prime},n)\|f\|_{L^{p,q}}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}).

Thus, by means of the above inequality, we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|V(f_{j})\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha,\beta,\mu,\lambda,p,q^{\prime},n)\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p,q}}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha,\beta,\mu,\lambda,p,q^{\prime},n)\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p,\infty}}^{1-\frac{p}{q}}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha,\beta,\mu,\lambda,p,q^{\prime},n)A_{j}^{1-\frac{p}{q}}

A_{j}\geq c_{0}
for some positive constant

\kappa_{j}>0
satisfies

with
\left\{f_{j}^{\kappa_{j}}\right\}_{j}

\displaystyle\omega_{n-1}f_{j}^{p}(R)R^{n-1}

for some constant

V(f_{j})(x)\leq C|x|^{-\beta}\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}}{|y|^{\alpha}\left(|x^{\prime}-y|+x_{n}\right)^{\frac{\mu}{2}}}\frac{1}{|y|^{-\frac{n-1}{p}}}dy.

3.
Qualitative analysis of the positive solutions

In this section, we study the qualitative properties of positive solutions to the integral equations (
1.13
). More precisely, we are ready to obtain the regularity, asymptotic behaviors and symmetry.
First of all, we introduce some basic definitions. Suppose that
V
is a topological vector space, and we define two fundamental norms
\|\cdotp\|_{X}
and
\|\cdotp\|_{Y}
on
V
,

\|\cdotp\|_{X},\|\cdotp\|_{Y}:V\rightarrow[0,\infty].

Let

X:=\{v\in V:\|v\|_{X}<\infty\},Y:=\{v\in V:\|v\|_{Y}<\infty\}.

We recall that the operator
T:X\rightarrow Y

•

is named to be contracting if for any
f,h\in X
, there exists some constant
\varrho\in(0,1)
such that

\|T(f)-T(h)\|_{X}\leq\varrho\|f-h\|_{Y}.

•

is named to be shrinking if for any
h\in X
, there exists some constant
\delta\in(0,1)
such that

\|T(h)\|_{X}\leq\delta\|h\|_{Y}.

Clearly, it is not difficult to show that, a linear shrinking operator must be contracting.

Next, we recall the classical regularity lifting lemma (See
[
15
]
), which will plays an essential role in our discussion.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
15
]

Let
T
be a contraction map from
X\rightarrow X
and
Y\rightarrow Y
,
f\in X
and there exists a function
g\in X\cap Y
such that
f=Tf+g
in
X
. Then
f\in X\cap Y
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.
For any constant
A>0
, we define

\displaystyle u_{A}(y)=\begin{cases}u(y),\ \ |u(y)|>A\penalty\ \mbox{or}\penalty\ |y|>A,\\
0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle v_{A}(x)=\begin{cases}v(x),\ \ |v(x)|>A\penalty\ \mbox{or}\penalty\ |x|>A,\\
0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

u_{B}(y)=u(y)-u_{A}(y)
and
v_{B}(x)=v(x)-v_{A}(x)
. Define the linear operator
T_{1}
as

T_{1}(h)(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|x|^{-\beta}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v_{A}^{q_{0}-1}(x)h(x)|y|^{-\alpha}dx,\ \ y\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},

and

T_{2}(h)(x)=\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)u_{A}^{p_{0}-1}h(x)|x|^{-\beta}dy,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

Noticing that
\left(u,v\right)\in L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{q_{0}+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
is a pair of non-negative solutions of the integral system (
1.13
), we have

\displaystyle u(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\alpha}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)\left(v_{A}(x)+v_{B}(x)\right)^{q_{0}-1}v(x)|x|^{-\alpha}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v_{A}^{q_{0}-1}(x)v(x)|x|^{-\alpha}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v_{B}^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\alpha}dx

\displaystyle:=T_{1}(v)(y)+F(y).

Similarly,

\displaystyle v(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)u^{p_{0}}(y)|x|^{-\beta}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)\left(u_{A}(y)+u_{B}(y)\right)^{p_{0}-1}u(y)|x|^{-\beta}dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)u_{A}^{p_{0}-1}(y)u(y)|x|^{-\beta}dy+\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)u_{A}(y)+u_{B}^{p_{0}}(y)|x|^{-\beta}dy

\displaystyle:=T_{2}(u)(x)+G(x),

where

F(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v_{B}^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\alpha}dx,\ \ G(x)=\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)u_{A}(y)+u_{B}^{p_{0}}(y)|x|^{-\beta}dy.

Furthermore, we may define the operator
T:L^{r}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})\times L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
,

T(h_{1},h_{2})=\left(T_{1}(h_{2}),T_{2}(h_{1})\right)

with the norm
\|\left(h_{1},h_{2}\right)\|_{r,s}=\|h_{1}\|_{L^{r}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}+\|h_{2}\|_{L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}
. Obviously, it holds that

\left(u,v\right)=T(u,v)+(F,G).

To take full advantage of the regularity lifting argument via contracting map, we set the parameters
r
and
s
satisfies

\frac{1}{s}+\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p_{0}+1}=\frac{1}{q_{0}+1}+\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{r}.

Noticing that under the hypothesis of Theorem
1.4
, the existence of parameters
r
and
s
can be ensure. With the purpose of obtain the desired result that
\left(u,v\right)\in L^{r}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
, we only require to prove that, for
A
sufficiently large, the following three cases hold.

(1)

T
is shrinking from
L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{q_{0}+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
to
L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{q_{0}+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
.

(2)

T
is shrinking from
L^{r}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
to
L^{r}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
.

(3)

\left(F,G\right)\in L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{q_{0}+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\cap L^{r}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
.

Based on the above preparation, we now begin the proof of the case (1). In fact, by applying the weighted integral inequality (
1.8
) and the Hölder inequality, for
(h_{1},h_{2})\in L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{q_{0}+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
, we know

\displaystyle\|T_{1}(h_{2})\|_{L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|v_{A}^{q_{0}-1}\|_{L^{\frac{q_{0}+1}{q_{0}-1}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}\|h_{2}\|_{L^{q_{0}+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|v_{A}\|^{q_{0}-1}_{L^{q_{0}+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}\|h_{2}\|_{L^{q_{0}+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)},

and

\displaystyle\|T_{2}(h_{1})\|_{L^{q_{0}+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\|u_{A}^{p_{0}-1}\|_{L^{\frac{p_{0}+1}{p_{0}-1}}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}\|h_{1}\|_{L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\|u_{A}\|^{p_{0}-1}_{L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}\|h_{1}\|_{L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}

where constant
C_{1},C_{2}>0
.

Notice that since the integrability
L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{q_{0}+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
, for
A
sufficiently large, we deduce

\|T\left(h_{1},h_{2}\right)\|_{p_{0}+1,q_{0}+1}=\|T_{1}(h_{2})\|_{p_{0}+1}+\|T_{2}(h_{1})\|_{q_{0}+1}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\left(h_{1},h_{2}\right)\|_{p_{0}+1,q_{0}+1}

which immediately implies that
T
is a shrinking operator from
L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{q_{0}+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
to itself.

Next, we will make full use of Stein-Weiss type inequality (
1.7
) with a general kernel to prove the case (2), which is similar to what we used in the case (1). For convenience, we only verify that
\|T_{2}(h_{1})\|_{L^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|h_{1}\|_{L^{r}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}
, since the other situation
\|T_{1}(h_{2})\|_{L^{r}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|h_{2}\|_{L^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}
can be proved in the same way.

Actually, there exists positive constant
C
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|T_{2}(h_{1})\|_{L^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{A}^{p_{0}-1}h_{1}\|_{L^{z}(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{A}\|^{p_{0}-1}_{L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}\|h_{1}\|_{L^{r}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}

In view of
u\in L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
, by selecting
A
is sufficiently large in (
3.1
), then we have

\|T_{2}(h_{1})\|_{L^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|h_{1}\|_{L^{r}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)},\quad\forall h_{1}\in L^{r}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right).

In conclusion, we require the parameters
r
,
s
and
z
satisfies

\frac{1}{z}-\frac{1}{r}=\frac{p_{0}-1}{p_{0}+1},

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha+\beta+\mu-\lambda-n+1}{n}+\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha+\beta+\mu-\lambda-n+1}{n}+\frac{n-1}{n}\left(\frac{p_{0}-1}{p_{0}+1}+\frac{1}{r}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p_{0}+1}+\frac{1}{q_{0}+1}-\frac{n-1}{n}+\frac{n-1}{n}\left(\frac{p_{0}-1}{p_{0}+1}+\frac{1}{r}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{q_{0}+1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p_{0}+1},

where we have used the equation’s condition

\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p_{0}+1}+\frac{1}{q_{0}+1}=\frac{\alpha+\beta+\mu-\lambda}{n}.

Moreover, it is suffices to show that
\alpha\leq\frac{n-1}{z^{\prime}}
and
\beta\leq\frac{n+s}{s}
.

Based on the above analysis, we conclude that
\|T(h_{1},h_{2})\|_{r,s}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|h_{1},h_{2}\|_{r,s}
, which indicates that
T
is a shrinking operator from
L^{r}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
to itself.

Finally, we prove the case (3), that is
\left(F,G\right)\in L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{q_{0}+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\cap L^{r}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
. It should be noted that,
u_{B}
and
v_{B}
are uniformly bounded by
A
. then we know that this case holds. Thus the proof of Theorem
1.4
is completed by using the regularity lifting Lemma
3.1
.

3.1.
Asymptotic Estimates

In this subsection, we are going to explore the asymptotic behaviors of the non-negative solutions of the integral system (
1.13
).

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.
In order to prove that

(3.2)

\lim\limits_{|y|\rightarrow 0}u(y)|y|^{\alpha}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}v^{q_{0}}(x)}{|x|^{\mu+\beta}}dx.

First, we verify that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{v^{q_{0}}(x)}{|x|^{\mu+\beta-\lambda}}dx<+\infty
.
In fact, for any
R>0
, we observe that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}v^{q_{0}}(x)}{|x|^{\mu+\beta}}dx=\int_{B^{+}_{R}}\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}v^{q_{0}}(x)}{|x|^{\mu+\beta}}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash B^{+}_{R}}\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}v^{q_{0}}(x)}{|x|^{\mu+\beta}}dx.

On one hand, since
u\in L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
, then there exists
y_{0}\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
satisfies
|y_{0}|<\frac{R}{2}
such that
u(y_{0})<+\infty
. From the system (
1.13
),

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash B^{+}_{R}}\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}v^{q_{0}}(x)}{|x|^{\mu+\beta}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash B^{+}_{R}}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}dx+\int_{B^{+}_{R}}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}dx=|y_{0}|^{\alpha}u(y_{0})<+\infty,

since
|x-y|<|x|
.

On the other hand, by using the Hölder inequality, we get

\int_{B^{+}_{R}}\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}v^{q_{0}}(x)}{|x|^{\mu+\beta}}dx\leq C\left(\int_{B^{+}_{R}}\left(\frac{1}{|x|^{\mu+\beta-\lambda}}\right)^{t^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{t^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{B^{+}_{R}}v^{q_{0}t}(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{t}}.

With the aim of
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{v^{q_{0}}(x)}{|x|^{\mu+\beta-\lambda}}dx<+\infty
, now we require that
\left(\mu+\beta-\lambda\right)t^{\prime}<n
and

\frac{1}{q_{0}t}\in\left(\frac{\beta-1}{n},\frac{\beta+\mu-\lambda}{n}\right)\cap\left(\frac{1}{q_{0}+1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p_{0}+1}+\frac{\alpha}{n},\frac{1}{q_{0}+1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p_{0}+1}+\frac{\alpha+\mu-\lambda+1}{n-1}\right).

Noting that
\left(\mu+\beta-\lambda\right)t^{\prime}<n
, we have

\frac{\mu+\beta-\lambda}{q_{0}n}<\frac{1}{q_{0}}-\frac{1}{q_{0}t}.

Therefore, since
q_{0}>1
and
\frac{1}{p_{0}+1}>\frac{\alpha}{n-1}
, it is easy to find that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{0}}-\frac{\mu+\beta-\lambda}{q_{0}n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q_{0}}-\frac{1}{q_{0}}\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p_{0}+1}+\frac{1}{q_{0}+1}-\frac{\alpha}{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q_{0}+1}-\frac{1}{q_{0}}\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p_{0}+1}-\frac{\alpha}{n}\right)

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{q_{0}+1}-\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p_{0}+1}+\frac{\alpha}{n},

here we applied the condition

\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{1}{p_{0}+1}+\frac{1}{q_{0}+1}=\frac{\alpha+\beta+\mu-\lambda}{n}.

With the above analysis, we can select a suitable parameter
t
such that
\left(\mu+\beta-\lambda\right)t^{\prime}<n
and
\|v\|_{L^{q_{0}t}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}<+\infty
, which immediately means that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{v^{q_{0}}(x)}{|x|^{\mu+\beta-\lambda}}dx<+\infty
.

Next, we prove (
3.2
). Direct calculation yields that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}v^{q_{0}}(x)}{|x|^{\mu+\beta}}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{B^{+}_{\rho}}\left(P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}-\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}v^{q_{0}}(x)}{|x|^{\mu+\beta}}\right)dx\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash B^{+}_{\rho}}\left(P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}-\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}v^{q_{0}}(x)}{|x|^{\mu+\beta}}\right)dx\right|

\displaystyle:=A_{1}+A_{2}.

On one hand, for
A_{1}
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{B^{+}_{\rho}}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B^{+}_{\rho}(y)}\frac{v^{q_{0}}(x)}{|x-y|^{\mu+\beta-\lambda}}dx+\int_{B^{+}_{\rho}}\frac{v^{q_{0}}(x)}{|x-y|^{\mu+\beta-\lambda}}dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\|v^{q_{0}}\|_{L^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}\|\frac{1}{|x|^{\mu+\beta-\lambda}}\|_{L^{t^{\prime}}(B^{+}_{\rho})}.

Taking the limit in (
3.4
), which leads to
\lim\limits_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\lim\limits_{|y|\rightarrow 0}A_{1}=0
.

On the other hand, according to the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem for
A_{2}
, we get

(3.5)

\lim\limits_{|y|\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\backslash B^{+}_{\rho}}\left(P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}-\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}v^{q_{0}}(x)}{|x|^{\mu+\beta}}\right)dx=0.

With the help of (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we derive

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{|y|\rightarrow 0}

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\left(\frac{x_{n}^{\lambda}v^{q_{0}}(x)}{|x|^{\mu+\beta}}\right)dx\right|

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\lim\limits_{|y|\rightarrow 0}A_{1}+\lim\limits_{\rho\rightarrow 0}\lim\limits_{|y|\rightarrow 0}A_{2}=0.

For the other case, if
\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{\mu+\alpha-\lambda+1}{p_{0}(n-1)}>\frac{\alpha}{n-1}
, then

\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow 0}\frac{v(x)|x|^{\beta}}{x_{n}^{\lambda}}=\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{u^{p_{0}}(y)}{|y|^{\alpha+\mu}}dy.

Similarly, we can prove the second conclusion. In conclusion, the proof of Theorem
1.5
is completed.

3.2.
Symmetry via the moving plane argument

In this subsection, we will prove the symmetry of positive solutions under the integral conditions. In order to apply the moving plane argument, we give some basic notations. For any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, one write

y^{\tau}=\left(2\tau-y_{1},...,y_{n-1}\right),\ \ x^{\tau}=\left(2\tau-x_{1},...,x_{n}\right),\ \ u(y^{\tau})=u_{\tau}(y),\ \ v(x^{\tau})=v_{\tau}(x),

and

T_{\tau}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{1}=\tau\right\},\ \ \Sigma_{y,\tau}=\left\{y\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}:y_{1}<\tau\right\},\ \ \ \ \Sigma_{x,\tau}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}:x_{1}<\tau\right\}.

Next, we will show the following equalities, which are useful in the method of moving plane.

Lemma 3.2
.

Supposed that
\left(u,v\right)
be a pair of non-negative solution of the integral system (
1.13
), for any
y\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, it holds that

(3.6)

\displaystyle u(y)-u_{\tau}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{x,\tau}}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)\left(|y|^{-\alpha}v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}-|y^{\tau}|^{-\alpha}v_{\tau}^{q_{0}}(x)|x^{\tau}|^{-\beta}\right)dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma_{x,\tau}}P_{\lambda}(x^{\tau},y,\mu)\left(|y|^{-\alpha}v_{\tau}^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}-|y^{\tau}|^{-\alpha}v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}\right)dx,

and

(3.7)

\displaystyle v(x)-v_{\tau}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{y,\tau}}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)\left(|y|^{-\alpha}u^{p_{0}}(y)|x|^{-\beta}-|y^{\tau}|^{-\alpha}u_{\tau}^{p_{0}}(y)|x^{\tau}|^{-\beta}\right)dy

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma_{y,\tau}}P_{\lambda}(x^{\tau},y,\mu)\left(|y|^{-\alpha}u_{\tau}^{p_{0}}(y)|x|^{-\beta}-|y^{\tau}|^{-\alpha}u^{p_{0}}(y)|x|^{-\beta}\right)dy,

Proof.

By direct computation, we know

\displaystyle u(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{x,\tau}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}dx+\int_{\Sigma_{x,\tau}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x^{\tau},y,\mu)v_{\tau}^{q_{0}}(x)|x^{\tau}|^{-\beta}dx,

and

\displaystyle u_{\tau}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y^{\tau}|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y^{\tau},\mu)v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{x,\tau}}|y^{\tau}|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y^{\tau},\mu)v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}dx+\int_{\Sigma_{x,\tau}}|y^{\tau}|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x^{\tau},y^{\tau},\mu)v_{\tau}^{q_{0}}(x)|x^{\tau}|^{-\beta}dx.

Since
P_{\lambda}(x^{\tau},y^{\tau},\mu)=P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)
and
P_{\lambda}(x,y^{\tau},\mu)=P_{\lambda}(x^{\tau},y,\mu)
, it’s not difficult to find that

\displaystyle u(y)-u_{\tau}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{x,\tau}}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)\left(|y|^{-\alpha}v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}-|y^{\tau}|^{-\alpha}v_{\tau}^{q_{0}}(x)|x^{\tau}|^{-\beta}\right)dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma_{x,\tau}}P_{\lambda}(x^{\tau},y,\mu)\left(|y|^{-\alpha}v_{\tau}^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}-|y^{\tau}|^{-\alpha}v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}\right)dx.

Clearly, in the same ways, we get

\displaystyle v(x)-v_{\tau}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{y,\tau}}\left(|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)|x|^{-\beta}-|y^{\tau}|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y^{\tau},\mu)|x|^{-\beta}\right)u^{p_{0}}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma_{y,\tau}}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)\left(|y|^{-\alpha}u^{p_{0}}(y)|x|^{-\beta}-|y^{\tau}|^{-\alpha}u_{\tau}^{p_{0}}(y)|x^{\tau}|^{-\beta}\right)dy

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma_{y,\tau}}P_{\lambda}(x^{\tau},y,\mu)\left(|y|^{-\alpha}u_{\tau}^{p_{0}}(y)|x|^{-\beta}-|y^{\tau}|^{-\alpha}u^{p_{0}}(y)|x|^{-\beta}\right)dy.

This finishes the proof.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.
We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1.
We claim that for
\tau
sufficiently negative, there holds

(3.8)

u_{\tau}(y)\geq u(y),\ \ v_{\tau}(x)\geq v(x),\ \ \forall x\in\Sigma_{x,\tau},\ \ y\in\Sigma_{y,\tau}.

Let

\Sigma_{y,\tau}^{u}=\left\{y\in\Sigma_{y,\tau}|u(y)>u_{\tau}(y)\right\},\ \ \Sigma_{x,\tau}^{v}=\left\{x\in\Sigma_{x,\tau}|v(x)>v_{\tau}(x)\right\}.

Thus, we will show that for
\tau
sufficiently negative, the sets
\Sigma_{y,\tau}^{v}
and
\Sigma_{x,\tau}^{u}
must have measure zero.

For any
x\in\Sigma_{x,\tau}^{v}
and
y\in\Sigma_{y,\tau}^{u}
, since
|x^{\tau}|<|x|
, we know from (
3.6
)

\displaystyle u(y)-u_{\tau}(y)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma_{x,\tau}}|y|^{-\alpha}\left(P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)-P_{\lambda}(x^{\tau},y,\mu)\right)\left(v^{q_{0}}(x)|x|^{-\beta}-v_{\tau}^{q_{0}}(x)|x^{\tau}|^{-\beta}\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Sigma_{x,\tau}^{v}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}\left(v^{q_{0}}(x)-v_{\tau}^{q_{0}}(x)\right)dx.

Applying Mean Value Theorem, we get

u(y)-u_{\tau}(y)\leq q_{0}\int_{\Sigma_{x,\tau}^{v}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v^{q_{0}-1}(x)\left(v(x)-v_{\tau}(x)\right)dx.

Similarly,

v(x)-v_{\tau}(x)\leq p_{0}\int_{\Sigma_{y,\tau}^{u}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)u^{p_{0}-1}(y)\left(u(y)-u_{\tau}(y)\right)dy.

Using the Hölder inequality and Theorem
1.2
in the above inequalities, we obtain

(3.9)

\|u-u_{\tau}\|_{L^{p_{0}+1}(\Sigma_{y,\tau}^{u})}\leq C\|v\|^{q_{0}-1}_{L^{q_{0}+1}(\Sigma_{x,\tau})}\|v-v_{\tau}\|_{L^{q_{0}+1}(\Sigma_{x,\tau}^{v})},

and

(3.10)

\|v-v_{\tau}\|_{L^{q_{0}+1}(\Sigma_{x,\tau}^{v})}\leq C\|u\|^{p_{0}-1}_{L^{p_{0}+1}(\Sigma_{y,\tau})}\|u-u_{\tau}\|_{L^{p_{0}+1}(\Sigma_{y,\tau}^{u})}.

In view of
\left(u,v\right)\in L^{p_{0}+1}\left(\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{q_{0}+1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\right)
, for
\tau
sufficiently negative, it holds that

\|v\|_{L^{q_{0}+1}(\Sigma_{x,\tau})}\leq\frac{1}{2},\ \ \|u\|_{L^{p_{0}+1}(\Sigma_{y,\tau})}\leq\frac{1}{2},

which indicates that

(3.11)

\|u-u_{\tau}\|_{L^{p_{0}+1}(\Sigma_{y,\tau}^{u})}=0,\ \ \|v-v_{\tau}\|_{L^{q_{0}+1}(\Sigma_{x,\tau}^{v})}=0.

Therefore, we deduce that
\Sigma_{y,\tau}^{u}
and
\Sigma_{x,\tau}^{v}
must be empty set. This implies that (
3.8
).

Step 2.
Inequality (

4.
The single weighted integral system

In this section, we will consider the necessary condition for the existence of non-negative solutions of the integral system (
1.14
) with single weight.

\left\{\begin{aligned} u(y)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|x|^{-\beta}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v^{q_{0}}(x)dx,&y\in\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+},\\

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.
Firstly, according to the integration by parts, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B^{n-1}_{R}\backslash B^{n-1}_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle|y|^{-\alpha}u^{p_{0}}(y)\left(y\cdot\nabla u(y)\right)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p_{0}+1}\int_{B^{n-1}_{R}\backslash B^{n-1}_{\varepsilon}}|y|^{-\alpha}y\cdot\nabla\left(u^{p_{0}+1}(y)\right)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{1-\alpha}}{p_{0}+1}\int_{\partial B^{n-1}_{R}}u^{p_{0}+1}(y)d\tau+\frac{\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}}{p_{0}+1}\int_{\partial B^{n-1}_{\varepsilon}}u^{p_{0}+1}(y)d\tau

\displaystyle-\frac{n-1-\alpha}{p_{0}+1}\int_{B^{n-1}_{R}\backslash B^{n-1}_{\varepsilon}}u^{p_{0}+1}(y)|y|^{-\alpha}dy.

In the same way, we know

\displaystyle\int_{B^{+}_{R}\backslash B^{+}_{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle|x|^{-\beta}v^{q_{0}}(x)\left(x\cdot\nabla v(x)\right)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q_{0}+1}\int_{B^{+}_{R}\backslash B^{+}_{\varepsilon}}|x|^{-\beta}x\cdot\nabla\left(v^{q_{0}+1}(x)\right)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{R^{1-\beta}}{q_{0}+1}\int_{\partial B^{+}_{R}}v^{q_{0}+1}(y)d\tau+\frac{\varepsilon^{1-\beta}}{q_{0}+1}\int_{\partial B^{+}_{\varepsilon}}v^{q_{0}+1}(x)d\tau

\displaystyle-\frac{n-\beta}{q_{0}+1}\int_{B^{+}_{R}\backslash B^{+}_{\varepsilon}}v^{q_{0}+1}(x)|x|^{-\beta}dx.

In particular, under the assumptions of Theorem
1.7
, we know
\left(u,v\right)\in L^{p_{0}+1}\left(|y|^{-\alpha}dy,\partial R^{n}_{+}\right)\times L^{q_{0}+1}\left(|x|^{-\beta}dx,R^{n}_{+}\right)
, then there exists
R_{i}\rightarrow+\infty
and
\varepsilon_{i}\rightarrow 0
such that

R^{1-\alpha}_{i}\int_{\partial B^{n-1}_{R_{i}}}u^{p_{0}+1}(y)d\tau\rightarrow 0,\quad R^{1-\beta}_{i}\int_{\partial B^{+}_{R_{i}}}v^{q_{0}+1}(x)d\tau\rightarrow 0.

Similarly, we obtain

\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}_{i}\int_{\partial B^{n-1}_{\varepsilon_{i}}}u^{p_{0}+1}(y)d\tau\rightarrow 0,\quad\varepsilon^{1-\beta}_{i}\int_{\partial B^{+}_{\varepsilon_{i}}}v^{q_{0}+1}(x)d\tau\rightarrow 0.

Based on the above analysis, we further get

(4.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}

\displaystyle|y|^{-\alpha}u^{p_{0}}(y)\left(y\cdot\nabla u(y)\right)dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|x|^{-\beta}v^{q_{0}}(x)\left(x\cdot\nabla v(x)\right)dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{n-1-\alpha}{p_{0}+1}\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}u^{p_{0}+1}(y)dy-\frac{n-\beta}{q_{0}+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|x|^{-\beta}v^{q_{0}+1}(x)dx.

Noticing that the weighted integral equations (
1.14
), and by a direct calculation, we obtain

(4.2)

\displaystyle\nabla u(y)\cdot y

\displaystyle=\frac{du(\rho y)}{d\rho}|_{\rho=1}

\displaystyle=-\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)|x-y|^{-2}(y-x)\cdot y|x|^{-\beta}v^{q_{0}}(x)dx,

and

(4.3)

\displaystyle\nabla v(x)\cdot x

\displaystyle=\frac{dv(\rho x)}{d\rho}|_{\rho=1}

\displaystyle=-\mu\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)|x-y|^{-2}(x-y)\cdot x|y|^{-\alpha}u^{p_{0}}(y)dy

\displaystyle+\lambda\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)|y|^{-\alpha}u^{p_{0}}(y)dy.

It follows from (
4.2

4.3

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}

\displaystyle|y|^{-\alpha}u^{p_{0}}(y)\left(y\cdot\nabla u(y)\right)dy+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|x|^{-\beta}v^{q_{0}}(x)\left(x\cdot\nabla v(x)\right)dx

\displaystyle=-\left(\mu-\lambda\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\int_{\partial R^{n}_{+}}|y|^{-\alpha}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)u^{p_{0}+1}(y)v^{q_{0}+1}(x)|x|^{-\beta}dydx

\displaystyle=-\left(\mu-\lambda\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)v^{q_{0}+1}(x)|x|^{-\beta}dx

\displaystyle=-\left(\mu-\lambda\right)\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}P_{\lambda}(x,y,\mu)u^{p_{0}+1}(y)|y|^{-\alpha}dy.

Combining (

\frac{n-1-\alpha}{p_{0}+1}+\frac{n-\beta}{q_{0}+1}=\mu-\lambda

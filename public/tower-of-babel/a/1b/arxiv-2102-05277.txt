Approximation of weak geodesics and subharmonicity of Mabuchi energy, II: ε \varepsilon -geodesics
Long Li

Abstract
. The purpose of this article is to study the strict convexity of the Mabuchi functional along a 𝒞 1 , 1 ¯ {\mathcal{C}}^{1,\bar{1}} -geodesic, with the aid of the ε \varepsilon -geodesics. We proved the L 2 L^{2} -convergence of the fiberwise volume element of the ε \varepsilon -geodesic. Moreover, the geodesic is proved to be uniformly fiberwise non-degenerate if the Mabuchi functional is ε \varepsilon -affine.



1.
Introduction

In order to study the uniqueness and existence problems of the Kähler-Einstein metrics
on a Fano manifold
X
, Mabuchi (
[
15
]
,
[
1
]
) introduced a useful energy functional
{\mathcal{M}}
,
called the
K
-energy or Mabuchi functional, on the space
{\mathcal{H}}_{[\omega]}
.
This is the space of all smooth Kähler potentials
in a given cohomology class
[\omega]
on
X
,
and it can be written as

{\mathcal{H}}_{[\omega]}:=\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X);\ \ \omega+dd^{c}\varphi>0\}.

Through out this paper, we always normalize the Kähler class as

\int_{X}\omega^{n}=1
.

It is also observed by Mabuchi (
[
15
]
) that this functional
{\mathcal{M}}
is convex along a smooth geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
,
connecting with arbitrary two points in
{\mathcal{H}}_{[\omega]}
.
Moreover, if the Mabuchi functional is affine along a smooth geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
,
then
{\mathcal{G}}
must be generated by a holomorphic vector field.
In this case, we say that the Mabuchi functional is strictly convex along the geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
.

It turns out that the Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{M}}
has also played an important role in the study of the
constant scalar curvature Kähler(cscK) metrics.
First, the convexity of
{\mathcal{M}}
is crucial in the proof of the uniqueness of the cscK metrics (
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
).
Second, on a Kähler manifold, the asymptotic behavior of this convex function along a geodesic ray
{\mathcal{G}}
is an invariant
\Upsilon
(
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
).
It is proved that this manifold admits a cscK metric if and only if it is geodesic-stable (Theorem (1.2),
[
11
]
).
That is to say, either we have
\Upsilon>0
, or
\Upsilon=0
and the ray
{\mathcal{G}}
is parallel to another geodesic ray generated by a holomorphic vector field.

However, one can not expect that there always exists a smooth geodesic connecting
two points in
{\mathcal{H}}_{[\omega]}
, due to the example in Darvas-Lempert (
[
12
]
).
In realty, we can only rely on the so called
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\bar{1}}
-geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
.
As the solution of the homogenous complex Monge-Ampère equation (see equation (
2.2
)),
it always exists (
[
9
]
) between arbitrary two points in
{\mathcal{H}}_{[\omega]}
.
However, the difficulties to deal with a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\bar{1}}
-geodesic
are the lack of the regularities and the possible degeneracy of
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Nevertheless, the Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{M}}
was proved to be
convex and continuous along a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\bar{1}}
-geodesic,
by the work of Berman-Berndtsson (
[
4
]
) and also Chen-Li-Păun (
[
6
]
).
Then people try to ask the question if the Mabuchi functional is also strictly convex
along such a geodesic.

If the boundary of a geodesic segment
{\mathcal{G}}
has merely
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\bar{1}}
-regularities,
then the answer is negative due to the example of Berman (
[
5
]
).
However, the situation becomes very different if we require a stronger condition
on the boundary regularities of
{\mathcal{G}}
.
The answer is affirmative (
[
14
]
), when

{\mathcal{G}}
is connecting with two non-degenerate energy minimizers of
{\mathcal{M}}
.
According to the work of He-Zeng (
[
13
]
),
the boundary of
{\mathcal{G}}
is actually smooth in this case.
Another example is on a toric Kähler manifold.
Then it is well known that a geodesic segment must be smooth and non-degenerate
if it is boundary is.
In these two examples,
the strict convexity of
{\mathcal{M}}
follows from the smoothness of the boundary of the geodesic.
For this reason, we always assume that the boundary of a geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}

belongs to the space
{\mathcal{H}}_{[\omega]}
through out this paper.

In order to circumvent the difficulties arising from a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\bar{1}}
-geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
,
we are appealing to using the so called
\varepsilon
-geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
(
[
9
]
).
The
\varepsilon
-geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
is a sequence of smooth approximation of
{\mathcal{G}}
,
satisfying a non-homogenous complex Monge-Ampère equation (see equation (
3.1
)).
In fact, we have proved in (
[
6
]
) that the Mabuchi functional is
almost convex

along the
\varepsilon
-geodesic.

However, the difficulty is that the convergence
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow{\mathcal{G}}

is merely weakly
L^{p}
for all
p>1
.
In other words, if we denote

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)
as the fiberwise
\varepsilon
-geodesic potential of
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}|_{X_{t}}
, and
\varphi(t,\cdot)
the fiberwise geodesic potential of
{\mathcal{G}}|_{X_{t}}
,
then it is not clear to us whether the
energy
{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})

converges to the energy
{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi)
or not.
Therefore, we can not directly conclude the convexity of
{\mathcal{M}}

by using the
\varepsilon
-geodesic in
[
6
]
.

The new observation is that the convergence
{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\rightarrow{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi)

is true, provided that the Mabuchi function
{\mathcal{M}}
is affine along
{\mathcal{G}}
(see Theorem (
3.4
) and Corollary (
3.7
)).
This first leads us to the following
L^{2}
-convergence of the fiberwise volume element of
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
.
Write
\omega_{\varepsilon}:={\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}|_{X_{t}}
and
\omega_{\varphi}:={\mathcal{G}}|_{X_{t}}
on a fiber
X_{t}:=\{t\}\times X

for any
t\in[0,1]
.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem (
3.6
))
.

Suppose the Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{M}}
is affine along a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\bar{1}}
-geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Then the fiber-wise volume element of the
\varepsilon
-geodesic converges to the
volume element of the geodesic in the strong
L^{2}
sense.
In other words, we have on each fiber
X_{t}

(1.1)

\frac{\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}}{\omega^{n}}\rightarrow\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}{\omega^{n}},\ \ \ \varepsilon\rightarrow 0,

under the
L^{2}
-norm, possibly after passing to a subsequence.

We emphasis that the
L^{2}
-convergence of the volume element (equation (
1.1
)) may not be true in general.
Next, a slightly stronger condition than the affine Mabuchi functional will be introduced,
aiming to resolve the possible degeneracy on the geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Write the restriction of the Mabuchi functional along a
\varepsilon
-geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
as

{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}(t):={\mathcal{M}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t))

for all
t\in[0,1]
.
Then we say that the Mabuchi functional is
\varepsilon
-affine
along the geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
if it satisfies

(1.2)

\frac{d{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}}{dt}|_{t=1}-\frac{d{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}}{dt}|_{t=0}=O(\varepsilon),

for all
\varepsilon>0
small (see Definition (
4.4
)).
We note that
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
are uniquely determined if the boundary of
{\mathcal{G}}
is given.
In fact,
the Mabuchi functional must be
affine along the
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\bar{1}}
-geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
if it is
\varepsilon
-affine along each
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
(see Lemma (
4.5
)).
Moreover, we proved the following result.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
4.6
)
.

Suppose the Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{M}}
is
\varepsilon
-affine along a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\bar{1}}
-geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Then
{\mathcal{G}}
is uniformly fiberwise non-degenerate,
namely, there exists a uniform constant
{\kappa}_{1}>0
such that

{\mathcal{G}}|_{X_{t}}>\kappa_{1}\omega,

for almost everywhere
t\in[0,1]
.

There are three main
steps
of the proof for the above Theorem.

Step (1)
is to figure out the so called
gap phenomenon
of the geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
,
which is first observed in our previous work (
[
14
]
).

Step (2)
is to establish a kind of
W^{1,2}
-estimate for the volume element
\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}
,
which is provided from the
\varepsilon
-affine condition.

Step (3)
(see Proposition (
5.1
)) is to prove
that a non-negative function must have a positive lower bound
if it has the gap phenomenon and satisfies a certain partial
W^{1,2}
-estimate.

As an application of our Theorem (
1.1
) and (
1.2
),
we can further estimate (see Theorem (
6.2
)) the limit of the complex hessian
of
{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})
, if the Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi)
is
\varepsilon
-affine.
More precisely, we have

(1.3)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime\prime}_{\varepsilon}(t)\geq 0,

for almost everywhere
t\in[0,1]
.

Another application is that we can recover the strict convexity result
in (
[
14
]
), as stated before.
Moreover, if the manifold
X
satisfies
c_{1}(X)=0

or
c_{1}(X)<0
, then we can utilize Chen’s argument (
[
9
]
) to conclude the strict convexity of the Mabuchi functional,
provided the
\varepsilon
-affine condition (see Theorem (
6.4
)).

Therefore, we conjecture that the Mabuchi functional is strictly convex
along a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\bar{1}}
-geodesic, if it is
\varepsilon
-affine.
In fact, the convergence (equation (
1.3
)) further implies
an
L^{2}
-estimate on
\bar{\partial}v_{\varepsilon}
for a sequence of smooth vector fields
v_{\varepsilon}
(see Theorem (
6.2
)).
Unfortunately, there is still some difficulties to conclude the holomorphicity of
v_{\infty}
as the limit of this sequence
v_{\varepsilon}
.

Finally, it might be worthy to point out that
a geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
possibly possess more regularities than
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\bar{1}}
,
when the Mabuchi functional is
\varepsilon
-affine along it.
Hopefully, we will see more examples about this fact, and
the regularity problem will be considered in our following projects.

2.
Preliminary

Denote
\Gamma
by the following strip domain in
{\mathbb{C}}

\Gamma:=\{\tau\in{\mathbb{C}};\ \ 0\leq\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\tau\leq 1\},

and we write the complex coordinate
{\tau}
as
t+\sqrt{-1}s
.
Consider the product manifold
Y:=\Gamma\times X
,
and then
Y
is a complex Kähler manifold with boundary.
Assume that
\pi

is the holomorphic projection from the product space
Y
to
X
.
Let
\Phi
be a quasi-plurisubharmonic function on
Y
continuous up to the boundary.
Denote
{\mathcal{G}}
by the following closed positive
(1,1)
current

(2.1)

\pi^{*}\omega+dd^{c}\Phi\geq 0

on
Y
.
We say that
{\mathcal{G}}
is a geodesic in the space of Kähler potential,
if the function
\Phi
is independent of
s=\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\tau
, and satisfies the following

Homogeneous complex Monge-Ampère
(HCMA) equation on
Y

(2.2)

{\mathcal{G}}^{n+1}=(\pi^{*}\omega+dd^{c}\Phi)^{n+1}=0.

This wedge product performs in the sense of Bedford and Talyor (
[
3
]
).

The boundary value of
\Phi
is required to be in the space
{\mathcal{H}}_{[\omega]}
,
and we say that
{\mathcal{G}}
is a geodesic connecting two points
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{[\omega]}

if

\Phi|_{X\times\{0\}}=\varphi_{0};\ \ \ \Phi|_{X\times\{1\}}=\varphi_{1}.

It is proved by Chen (
[
9
]
) that such a geodesic always exists,
and is unique with fixed boundary value.
It also has the so called
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\bar{1}}
-regularities on
Y
,
namely, writing
{\mathcal{G}}
locally as

g_{{\tau}\bar{\tau}}d{\tau}\wedge d\bar{\tau}+\sum_{{\alpha},\beta=1}^{n}(g_{{\tau}\bar{\beta}}d{\tau}\wedge d\bar{z}^{\beta}+g_{{\alpha}\bar{\tau}}dz^{{\alpha}}\wedge d\bar{\tau}+g_{{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}dz^{{\alpha}}\wedge d\bar{z}^{\beta}),

we have

||g_{{\tau}\bar{\tau}}||_{L^{\infty}}+\sum_{{\alpha},\beta=1}^{n}(||g_{{\tau}\bar{\beta}}||_{L^{\infty}}+||g_{{\alpha}\bar{\tau}}||_{L^{\infty}}+||g_{{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}||_{L^{\infty}})<+\infty.

In other words, there exist a uniform constant
C>0
such that we have

0\leq{\mathcal{G}}\leq C(\pi^{*}\omega+id{\tau}\wedge d\bar{\tau})

on
Y
.
Thanks to the Sobolev embedding theorem, this quasi-plurisubharmonic function
\Phi
is of class
C^{1,{\alpha}}
for any
{\alpha}\in(0,1)
.

Remark 2.1
.

We emphasis that a geodesic potential
\Phi
and an
\varepsilon
-geodesic potential
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
(see equation (
3.1
))
are always independent of the imaginary part of
\tau
.
For this reason, we can think of that
\Phi

and
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
are periodic functions with period
2\pi
in the direction
s
.
Therefore, they are actually
S^{1}
-invariant
functions defined on
{\mathcal{R}}\times X
,
where
{\mathcal{R}}:=[0,1]\times S^{1}
is a cylinder
(
[
9
]
)
.
In other words, the defining domain of
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
can be taken
as a compact complex Kähler manifold with boundary.

For the same reason, we abuse the complex variable
\tau
and its real part
t

from now on, and hope that this will be clear to readers from the context.

2.1.
The Mabuchi functional

On the space
{\mathcal{H}}_{[\omega]}
, Mabuchi (
[
1
]
) introduced the following energy functional

{\mathcal{M}}:=\underline{R}{\mathcal{E}}-{\mathcal{E}}^{Ric\omega}+H,

where the constant
\underline{R}
is the average of the scalar curvature

\underline{R}=\frac{nc_{1}(X)\cdot[\omega]^{n-1}}{[\omega]^{n}}.

The energy functional
{\mathcal{E}}
is defined for any
\varphi\in{\mathcal{H}}_{[\omega]}
as

{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi):=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\int_{X}\varphi\omega^{i}\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{n-i}.

The twisted energy functional
{\mathcal{E}}^{{\alpha}}
(by a closed smooth
(1,1)
form
{\alpha}
)
is defined as

{\mathcal{E}}^{{\alpha}}(\varphi):=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\int_{X}\varphi\omega^{i}\wedge\omega_{\varphi}^{n-i-1}\wedge{\alpha}.

Finally, the entropy functional is

H(\varphi):=\int_{X}\left(\log\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}{\omega^{n}}\right)\omega_{\varphi}^{n}.

Consider the pull back
\pi^{*}\omega
of the Kähler form on
X
, and then it is a smooth positive
(1,1)
-current on
Y
.
Suppose
\Phi
is a
\pi^{*}\omega
-plurisubharmonic function on
Y
(see equation (
2.1
)), which corresponds to a geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Then its restriction

\varphi_{{\tau}}:=\Phi|_{X_{\tau}}

on the fiber
X_{{\tau}}
is actually a
\omega
-plurisubharmonic function and
has the
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\bar{1}}
-regularities. It is observed (
[
4
]
) that

{\mathcal{M}}
can be defined on such functions.
Therefore, we can write the Mabuchi functional along the geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
as

{\mathcal{K}}({\tau}):={\mathcal{M}}(\varphi_{\tau}),\ \ {\tau}\in\Gamma.

Next, we introduce the following modified versions of the Mabuchi functional
due to Berman and Berndtsson (
[
4
]
) .
For any large constant
A>0
,
we define the
A-truncated Mabuchi functional
along the geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
as

(2.3)

{\mathcal{K}}^{\Psi_{A}}({\tau}):=\underline{R}{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi_{\tau})-{\mathcal{E}}^{Ric\omega}(\varphi_{\tau})+\int_{X}\log\left(\max\left\{\frac{\omega^{n}_{\varphi_{\tau}}}{\omega^{n}},\frac{h_{A}}{\omega^{n}}\right\}\right)\omega_{\varphi_{\tau}}^{n}.

Here
h_{A}
is a continuous metric on the relative canonical line bundle
K_{Y/\Gamma}
,
and it is constructed as follows.

Let
\chi_{0}
be a smooth metric on the line bundle
K_{X}
,
and
k_{0}
be a positive number such that
dd^{c}\chi_{0}+k_{0}\omega>0
.
If set
\chi:=\pi^{*}\chi_{0}-k_{0}\Phi
, then we have

dd^{c}\chi=\pi^{*}\chi_{0}+k_{0}\pi^{*}\omega-k_{0}(\pi^{*}\omega+dd^{c}\Phi)\geq-k_{0}{\mathcal{G}}.

Taking
h_{A}:=e^{\chi-A}
,
we obtain on
Y

dd^{c}\log h_{A}\geq-k_{0}(\pi^{*}\omega+dd^{c}\Phi),

for some positive integer
k_{0}
.

As explained in Remark (
2.1
),
The values of these energy functions
{\mathcal{K}},{\mathcal{K}}^{\Psi_{A}}

do not depend on
\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits\tau
.
Hence they can be thought of defining
on the unit interval
[0,1]
.
Moreover, it is proved (see Theorem (3.4),
[
4
]
) that
{\mathcal{K}}^{\Psi_{A}}
is a convex function on
[0,1]
,
and it converges to
{\mathcal{K}}
as
A\rightarrow\infty
by the dominated convergence theorem.
Eventually, we conclude the following convexity result (
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
).

Theorem 2.2
.

The Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{M}}
is convex and continuous along a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\bar{1}}
-geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
.

3.
Energies on the
\varepsilon
-geodesics

Suppose we have two points
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{[\omega]}
.
For each
\varepsilon>0
small enough,
there exists a smooth
S^{1}
-invariant strictly
\pi^{*}\omega
-plurisubharmonic function
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
on
\Gamma\times X
satisfying

(3.1)

{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n+1}=(\pi^{*}\omega+dd^{c}\Phi_{\varepsilon})^{n+1}=\varepsilon\sqrt{-1}dt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge\omega^{n},

with boundary conditions

\Phi_{\varepsilon}(0,\cdot)=\varphi_{0}(\cdot);\ \ \ \Phi_{\varepsilon}(1,\cdot)=\varphi_{1}(\cdot).

Then we say that
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}:=\pi^{*}\omega+dd^{c}\Phi_{\varepsilon}
is the
\varepsilon
-geodesic
connecting with
\varphi_{0}
and
\varphi_{1}
.
From the uniform ellipticity of equation (
3.1
), this
\varepsilon
-geodesic is uniquely determined, when its boundary values are fixed.

It is proved in (
[
9
]
) that
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}

is uniformly bounded in the
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,\bar{1}}
-norm.
Moreover, we have known that
\Phi_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\Phi
in
C^{1,{\alpha}}
-norm for any
{\alpha}\in(0,1)
,
and also in weakly
W^{2,p}
-norm for all
1<p<\infty
.

In general, we write the restriction of the geodesic potential on each fiber
X_{t}:=\{t\}\times X
as

\varphi(t,\cdot):=\Phi|_{X_{t}};\ \ \ \omega_{\varphi}:={\mathcal{G}}|_{X_{t}}=\omega+dd_{X}^{c}\varphi.

Similarly, we have for the
\varepsilon
-geodesic potential

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot):=\Phi_{\varepsilon}|_{X_{t}};\ \ \ \omega_{\varepsilon}:={\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}|_{X_{t}}=\omega+dd_{X}^{c}\varphi_{\varepsilon}.

Define an operator
\rho
on the space of all Kähler metrics on
Y
as

\rho(g):=g_{t\bar{t}}-g^{\bar{\beta}{\alpha}}g_{{\alpha}\bar{t}}g_{t\bar{\beta}}.

Writing
\rho_{\varepsilon}:=\rho({\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon})
,
a standard computation shows the following equation

(3.2)

(\pi^{*}\omega+dd^{c}\Phi_{\varepsilon})^{n+1}=\rho_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{-1}dt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}.

Hence the
\varepsilon
-geodesic equation (
3.1
) can be re-written as

(3.3)

\rho_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\frac{\omega^{n}}{\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}}.

Remark 3.1
.

It is a well known fact that the fiberwise volume element

\omega^{n}_{\varepsilon}/\omega^{n}
converges to
\omega_{\varphi}^{n}/\omega^{n}

weakly in
L^{p}
, for all
p>1
, possibly passing to a subsequence.
In fact, the
C^{1,{\alpha}}
convergence of
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
to
\varphi
implies
the convergence of the Monge-Ampère measure
\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}
to
\omega^{n}_{\varphi}
in the sense of currents.
With the uniform upper bound of the volume elements, this further implies that
\omega^{n}_{\varepsilon}

converges to
\omega_{\varphi}^{n}
in the weak sense of measures (Proposition (2.2),
[
2
]
).

On the other hand, we can infer from the uniform
L^{\infty}
-norm of the sequence
\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}/\omega^{n}

that the following holds. There exists a non-negative
L^{\infty}
function
f_{\infty}
satisfying

\frac{\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}}{\omega^{n}}\rightarrow f_{\infty},

in the weakly
L^{p}
-norm for all
p>1
, possibly after passing to subsequence.
Therefore, the two measure
\omega_{\varphi}^{n}
and
f_{\infty}\omega^{n}

must coincide with each other, and our claim follows.

3.1.
Energy and entropy

Denote
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}:\Gamma\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by the restriction of the Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{M}}
to the
\varepsilon
-geodesic as

{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}(t):=\underline{R}{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})-{\mathcal{E}}^{Ric\omega}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})+\int_{X}\log\frac{\omega^{n}_{\varepsilon}}{\omega^{n}}\omega^{n}_{\varepsilon}.

Then the
A
-truncated Mabuchi functional along the
\varepsilon
-geodesic can also be introduced as

{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}(t):=\underline{R}{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})-{\mathcal{E}}^{Ric\omega}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})+\int_{X}\log\max\left\{\frac{\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}}{\omega^{n}},\frac{h_{A}}{\omega^{n}}\right\}\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n},

where
h_{A}:=h_{\varepsilon,A}=e^{\chi_{\varepsilon}-A}
is a smooth volume whose associated curvature is greater than
-C{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
for some fixed positive constant
C
.
More precisely, we construct this auxiliary element as before.

Let
\chi_{0}
be a smooth metric on the line bundle
K_{X}
,
and
k_{0}
be a positive number such that
dd^{c}\chi_{0}+k_{0}\omega>0
.
Then we set
\chi_{\varepsilon}:=\pi^{*}\chi_{0}-k_{0}\Phi_{\varepsilon}
, and hence

dd^{c}\chi_{\varepsilon}=\pi^{*}\chi_{0}+k_{0}\pi^{*}\omega-k_{0}(\pi^{*}\omega+dd^{c}\Phi_{\varepsilon})\geq-k_{0}{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}.

We note that the sub-index
\varepsilon
in the notation
h_{A}
is omitted, since

\chi_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded in its
C^{1,\bar{1}}
-norm, and converges uniformly to

\chi:=\pi^{*}\chi_{0}-k_{0}\Phi

in
C^{1,{\alpha}}
-norm for any
{\alpha}\in(0,1)
.
Therefore, this omission will be harmless for our later argument.

This new energy function
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}
is continuous in
t
, since the metric
\omega_{\varepsilon}

and the volume form
h_{A}
both are continuous on
Y
, and moreover the maximum

\max\{\omega^{n}_{\varepsilon},h_{A}\}
is uniformly bounded below away from zero.

We can further compute the complex Hessian of this energy function in the sense of local currents,
and our almost convexity result reads as follows.

Theorem 3.2

(Chen-Li-Păun,
[
6
]
)
.

For each positive number
A
, there is a uniform constant
C_{A}>0
such that the function

(3.4)

\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}_{\varepsilon,A}:={\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}(t)-\varepsilon C_{A}t(1-t)

is convex and continuous on
[0,1]
for each
\varepsilon>0
small.

For the convenience of readers,
we recall the proof of the above Theorem briefly.
A similar idea of this proof will be used in Section (4) and (6).

Sketch of the proof.

Suppose
v
is a locally compact supported smooth test function on
\Gamma
, and then we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\langle dd^{c}{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A},v\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underline{R}\int_{\Gamma\times X}v(\pi^{*}\omega+dd^{c}\Phi_{\varepsilon})^{n+1}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma\times X}v(\pi^{*}\omega+dd^{c}\Phi_{\varepsilon})^{n}\wedge\pi^{*}Ric(\omega)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma\times X}vdd^{c}\left(\max\left\{\log\frac{\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}}{\omega^{n}},\log\frac{h_{A}}{\omega^{n}}\right\}\right)\wedge(\pi^{*}\omega+dd^{c}\Phi_{\varepsilon})^{n}.

Taking the fiberwise integral
\int_{X_{t}}
as the push forward operator acting on the currents from
\Gamma\times X
to
\Gamma
,
we obtain the following equation.

(3.6)

\displaystyle dd^{c}{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\underline{R}}{n+1}\int_{X_{t}}{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n+1}-\int_{X_{t}}Ric(\omega)\wedge{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{X_{t}}dd^{c}\left(\max\left\{\log\frac{\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}}{\omega^{n}},\log\frac{h_{A}}{\omega^{n}}\right\}\right)\wedge{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n}.

On the one hand, we have seen locally

(3.7)

\frac{1}{n+1}\left(\Delta_{{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}}\log{h_{A}}\right){\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n+1}=dd^{c}\log{h_{A}}\wedge{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n}\geq-k_{0}{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n+1}.

On the other hand,
we denote the function
f_{\varepsilon}:\Gamma\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by the equality

\left.\frac{\omega^{n}_{\varepsilon}}{\omega^{n}}\right|_{X_{t}}=e^{f_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)},

and introduce the following open sub-level set

(3.8)

P_{\varepsilon,A}:=\left\{(t,z)\in\Gamma\times X;\ \ \ f_{\varepsilon}(t,z)>\log\frac{h_{A}}{\omega^{n}}(t,z)\right\}.

It is proved in (
[
6
]
) that there exists a positive number
c_{A}
such that the following inequality is satisfied in
P_{\varepsilon,A}

(3.9)

\left(dd^{c}f_{\varepsilon}-Ric(\omega)\right)\wedge{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n}\geq-c_{A}{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n+1}.

We emphasis that
c_{A}
only depends on the constant
A
, the background metric
\omega
and the uniform upper bound of
\omega_{\varepsilon}
,
and it can be assumed to be increasing in
A
.
Hence we have the following estimate locally in
P_{\varepsilon,A+1}

(3.10)

\left(\Delta_{{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}}\log\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}\right){\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n+1}\geq-c_{A+1}{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n+1}.

Now we can utilize a theorem by Greene-Wu (Lemma (5.2),
[
6
]
),
and establish the following inequality in an open neighbourhood of each point on
\Gamma\times X
.

(3.11)

\left(\Delta_{{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}}\max\{\log\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n},\log h_{A}\}\right){\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n+1}\geq-c^{\prime}_{A}{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n+1},

where
c^{\prime}_{A}:=\max\{c_{A+1},(n+1)k_{0}\}
.
Thus we infer the following inequality holds

(3.12)

\left(dd^{c}\max\left\{\log\frac{\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}}{\omega^{n}},\log\frac{h_{A}}{\omega^{n}}\right\}-Ric\omega\right)\wedge{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n}\geq-c^{\prime}_{A}{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n+1},

globally on
\Gamma\times X
.
Combing this estimate with equation (
3.6
), our result follows.

∎

One may expect that
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}
converges to the energy
{\mathcal{K}}
.
If so, then we can directly conclude the convexity of
{\mathcal{M}}
by Theorem (
3.2
).

The convergence of the energy parts of
{\mathcal{M}}
, i.e.
{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi)
and
{\mathcal{E}}^{{\alpha}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}^{{\alpha}}(\varphi)

as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, is indeed true (
[
4
]
,
[
14
]
).
However, there is a rather severe difficulty:
the convergence of the fiber-wise volume element

\frac{\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}}{\omega^{n}}\rightharpoonup\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}{\omega^{n}}

is only known to be in the weakly
L^{p}
sense for any
1<p<\infty
.
Unfortunately, this is not enough to conclude the convergence of the entropy functional
H(\varphi_{\varepsilon})
.

Nevertheless, we have the following estimate on the entropy functionals.
Recall that the entropy functional along a geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
is defined as

H(\varphi):=\int_{X}{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi)\log{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi)\cdot\omega^{n},

where
{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi):=\omega_{\varphi}^{n}/\omega^{n}
,
and its truncated version is

H_{A}(\varphi):=\int_{X}{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi)\log{\mathfrak{f}}_{A}(\varphi)\cdot\omega^{n},

where

{\mathfrak{f}}_{A}(\varphi):=\max\left\{\frac{\omega_{\varphi}^{n}}{\omega^{n}},\frac{h_{A}}{\omega^{n}}\right\}.

As before, we omit the sub-index
\varepsilon
in the definition of
{\mathfrak{f}}_{A}
as in
h_{A}
’s.
Then the energy function
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}
can be re-written as

(3.13)

{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}(t)=\underline{R}{\mathcal{E}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})-{\mathcal{E}}^{Ric\omega}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})+H_{A}(\varphi_{\varepsilon}).

Finally we state the following lower semi-continuity type property (Lemma (4.8),
[
6
]
) for the truncated entropy functionals.

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists a function
\eta:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}

with
\eta(x)\rightarrow 0
as
x\rightarrow+\infty
such that we have

(3.14)

\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}H_{A}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\geq H_{A}(\varphi)-\eta(A),

for all
A
large enough.

We emphasis that
\eta(A)
is independent of
\varepsilon
and
t
.

3.2.
The affine energy

From now on, we assume that the Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{M}}
is affine along
{\mathcal{G}}
, namely,
the energy function
{\mathcal{K}}(t)
is linear on
[0,1]
.
Denote
{\mathcal{K}}_{A}
by the following limit for each
t\in[0,1]
and
A
large

{\mathcal{K}}_{A}(t):=\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}(t)=\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}_{\varepsilon,A}(t),

where
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}_{\varepsilon,A}
is defined in equation (
3.4
).
Then
{\mathcal{K}}_{A}
is a convex function on the unit interval
[0,1]
,
since the
\limsup
of a sequence of convex functions which are locally bounded above is still convex.

Eventually, we will see that this convex function
{\mathcal{K}}_{A}
obtained from taking the limit of
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}

is exactly equal to
{\mathcal{K}}^{\Psi_{A}}
(see Corollary (
3.5
)).
First, we note that
{\mathcal{K}}_{A}
is actually a decreasing sequence in
A
.
This is because
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}
is a decreasing sequence in
A
for each
\varepsilon
fixed.
In fact, we have

H_{A^{\prime}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\leq H_{A}(\varphi_{\varepsilon}),

for each
t\in[0,1]
and any
A^{\prime}\geq A
, since
{\mathfrak{f}}_{A^{\prime}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\leq{\mathfrak{f}}_{A}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})
and
{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi)\geq 0
in this case.
Bearing this in mind, we conclude the following result.

Theorem 3.4
.

Suppose the Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{M}}
is affine along a geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Then there is a positive number
A_{0}
such that we have

{\mathcal{K}}_{A}(t)={\mathcal{K}}(t),

for all
t\in[0,1]
and
A\geq A_{0}
.

Proof.

Up to a linear function on
[0,1]
, we can assume that the Mabuchi functional

{\mathcal{M}}
is identically zero along
{\mathcal{G}}
, namely we have on
[0,1]

{\mathcal{K}}(t)\equiv 0.

The first observation is that we have

(3.15)

{\mathcal{K}}_{A}(0)=0;\ \ \ {\mathcal{K}}_{A}(1)=0,

since the
\varepsilon
-geodesic potential
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
coincides with the geodesic potential
\Phi
for each
\varepsilon
on the boundary of
{\mathcal{R}}\times X
.
Then it is easy to see that
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}(0)={\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}(1)=0
for all
A
large enough.

As a convex function on
[0,1]
,
{\mathcal{K}}_{A}
is upper semi-continuous near the boundaries, and then we have

\limsup_{t\rightarrow 0,1}{\mathcal{K}}_{A}(t)\leq 0.

Thanks to the convexity again,
{\mathcal{K}}_{A}
must be below the line segment joining its two boundaries.
Therefore, it is non-positive under our assumption and we have

(3.16)

{\mathcal{K}}_{A}(t)\leq 0,

for all
t\in[0,1]
.
On the other hand ,
we have for each
t\in(0,1)

(3.17)

{\mathcal{K}}_{A}(t)\geq{\mathcal{K}}(t)-\eta(A),

This directly follows from Lemma (
3.3
), where we have proved

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}H_{A}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\geq H_{A}(\varphi)-\eta(A).

Next define a new function on
[0,1]
as

\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}(t):=\limsup_{A\rightarrow+\infty}{\mathcal{K}}_{A}(t).

For the same reason,
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}
is a convex function which verifies the boundary condition

\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}(0)=\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}(1)=0
by equation (
3.15
).
Moreover, inequality (
3.17
) implies that

\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}(t)\geq 0
for each
t\in(0,1)
,
since
\eta(A)\rightarrow 0
as
A\rightarrow+\infty
by Lemma (
3.3
).
Therefore, we conclude that
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}
must be identically equal to zero for all
t\in[0,1]
.

However, as mentioned before,
{\mathcal{K}}_{A}
is actually a decreasing sequence in
A
,
namely, we have
{\mathcal{K}}_{A}(t)\searrow\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}(t)
for each
t\in[0,1]
.
Therefore, the following inequality follows

{\mathcal{K}}_{A}(t)\geq 0,

for each
t\in[0,1]
.
Combining this with equation (
3.16
), we have for all
A
large enough

{\mathcal{K}}_{A}(t)\equiv 0

on
[0,1]
, and our result follows.

∎

In other words, the limit of the
A
-truncated Mabuchi functional on the
\varepsilon
-geodesics
will coincide with
{\mathcal{M}}
for all
A
large enough, provided the linearity of the Mabuchi functional along
{\mathcal{G}}
.

3.3.
Gap phenomenon

When the Mabuchi functional is affine along
{\mathcal{G}}
, we can prove (Lemma (3.1),
[
14
]
) that the
A
-truncated Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{K}}^{\Psi_{A}}
(equation (
2.3
))
also coincide with
{\mathcal{K}}
for all
A
large.
This implies

(3.18)

H_{A}(\varphi)=H(\varphi),

on each fiber
X_{t}
, for all
A
large enough.
Furthermore, we can infer the so called
gap phenomenon
(Proposition (3.2),
[
14
]
)
for the fiber-wise volume element of the geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
from the above equation.
Denote
P
by the following measurable subset

P:=\left\{(t,z)\in\Gamma\times X;\ \ \ {\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi)>0\right\},

and
P_{A}
by the following sub-level set

P_{A}:=\left\{(t,z)\in\Gamma\times X;\ \ \ {\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi)>\frac{e^{\chi-A}}{\omega^{n}}\right\}.

For each
t\in[0,1]
, we introduce
their fiber-wise restrictions as

P_{t}:=P\bigcap X_{t};\ \ \ P_{A,t}:=P_{A}\bigcap X_{t}.

The gap phenomenon says that
there is a large constant
A_{0}
such that for all
A\geq A_{0}
, and each
t\in[0,1]
,
the following equality holds

(3.19)

X_{t}=P_{A,t}\bigcup P_{t}^{c}

up to a set of measure zero.
In other words,
there exists a uniform constant
{\kappa}_{0}>0
such that either we have
on each fiber
X_{t}

(3.20)

{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi)>{\kappa}_{0}\ \ \emph{or}\ \ \ \ {\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi)=0

almost everywhere.
We emphasis that
{\kappa}_{0}
does not depend on
t
or
x
.

Thanks to this gap phenomenon, we can prove that the limit of the energy function
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}

exists as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
on each fiber
X_{t}
, and it actually coincides with
{\mathcal{K}}

for all
A
large.

Corollary 3.5
.

Suppose the Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{M}}
is affine along a geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Then for all
A
large enough, the following limit exists for each
t\in[0,1]
and satisfies

(3.21)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}(t)={\mathcal{K}}^{\Psi_{A}}(t)={\mathcal{K}}(t).

Proof.

For each
t\in[0,1]
fixed, we define

\widetilde{H}_{A}(t):=\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}H_{A}(\varphi_{\varepsilon}).

Thanks to Lemma (
3.3
), we have

(3.22)

\lim_{A\rightarrow+\infty}\widetilde{H}_{A}(t)\geq H(\varphi),

by equation (
3.18
).
However,
\widetilde{H}_{A}
is actually a decreasing sequence in
A
.
This follows from its construction and the fact that
H_{A}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})
is decreasing for each
\varepsilon
fixed.
Hence, we conclude

(3.23)

\widetilde{H}_{A}(t)\geq H(\varphi).

On the other hand,
Theorem (
3.4
) implies for all
A
large

(3.24)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}H_{A}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})=H(\varphi)=H_{A}(\varphi).

Combined the two inequalities above together, we have

(3.25)

H(\varphi)=\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}H_{A}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}H_{A}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\geq H(\varphi),

at each
t\in[0,1]
, and then our result follows.

∎

3.4.
L^{2}
-Convergence

Recall that the
\varepsilon
-geodesic potential
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
converges to the geodesic potential
\Phi

in the weak
C^{1,\bar{1}}
-norm.
Fixing a fiber
X_{\tau}
,
we can pick up a convergent subsequence of the volume elements on this fiber

(3.26)

f_{\ell}(x):={\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon_{{\ell}}})(x);\ \ \ f(x):={\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi)(x),

satisfying

f_{\ell}\rightarrow f

weakly in
L^{p}
for all
p\geq 1
on
X_{\tau}
.
It is interesting to know whether we have strong convergence in
L^{p}
for some
p>1
for this sequence or not,
and it turns out that this is indeed the case if the Mabuchi functional is affine.

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose the Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{M}}
is affine along a geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Then the fiber-wise volume element of the
\varepsilon
-geodesic converges to the
volume element of the geodesic in the strong
L^{2}
sense as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
,
possibly after passing to a subsequence.
More precisely, we have

\lim_{{\ell}\rightarrow+\infty}||f_{\ell}-f||_{L^{2}}=0,

on each fiber
X_{\tau}
.

Proof.

According to Chen-Tian (Theorem (7.1.1),
[
8
]
),
the
L^{2}
-convergence of the difference
(f_{\ell}-f)

follows from the convergence of the entropy as

(3.27)

\lim_{{\ell}\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon_{\ell}})\log{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon_{\ell}})=\int_{X}{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi)\log{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi),

and this is proved in the Corollary (
3.7
) below.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

Suppose the Mabuchi functional is affine along a geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Then on each fiber
X_{{\tau}},{\tau}\in[0,1]
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}H(\varphi_{\varepsilon})=H(\varphi).

Proof.

In the proof of Corollary (
3.5
),
we have seen that the
A
-truncated entropy
H_{A}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})
is decreasing in
A
for each
\varepsilon
fixed.
Moreover, we proved

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}H_{A}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})=H_{A}(\varphi)=H(\varphi),

for all
A
large enough.
Therefore, we conclude
the following inequality

(3.28)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}H(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}H_{A}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})=H(\varphi).

On the other hand, by the lower semi-continuity property of the entropy functional, we have

(3.29)

\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}H(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\geq H(\varphi),

and then our result follows.

∎

Remark 3.8
.

Under our assumptions, this Theorem says that for each subsequence
f_{{\ell}}

converges to
f
in the weakly
L^{2}
-norm actually converges in the strong
L^{2}
-norm.
Moreover, the Corollary (
3.7
) implies the convergence of the Mabuchi functional along the
\varepsilon
-geodesic,
namely, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}(t)={\mathcal{K}}(t),

for each
t\in[0,1]
.

Remark 3.9
.

As a simple application of Theorem (
3.6
),
the fiber-wise volume element
f_{\ell}
of the
\varepsilon
-geodesic converges to
the volume element
f
of the geodesic in measure.
Moreover, thanks to the Riesz-Lebesgue Theorem, we have

f_{\ell}\rightarrow f

almost everywhere on each fiber, possibly after passing to a subsequence.

4.
The
\varepsilon
-affine energy and non-degneracy

Suppose the Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{M}}
is affine along a geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Due to the convergence
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}_{\varepsilon,A}\rightarrow{\mathcal{K}}_{A}
of a sequence of convex functions, the first derivative
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,A}(t)

also converges uniformly to the slope
k:={\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{A}(t)
on the closed interval
[\delta,1-\delta]
for any
\delta>0
small.
However, the boundary behaviour of
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,A}(t)
is unclear in general.
Therefore, we impose the following condition on the boundary of
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Definition 4.1
.

The Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{M}}
is essentially affine along a geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
, if

{\mathcal{K}}(t)
is a linear function on
[0,1]
with slope
k
and we have

(4.1)

-\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{t}(R_{\varphi_{t}}-\underline{R})\omega_{\varphi_{t}}^{n}=k,

at
t=0,1
.

It is proved in Berman-Berndtsson (
[
4
]
) that the one side inequality of equation (
4.1
)
always holds at the two boundaries.

(4.2)

-\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{0}(R_{\varphi_{0}}-\underline{R})\omega_{\varphi_{0}}^{n}\leq\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0^{+}}{\mathcal{K}}(t)=k,

and

(4.3)

-\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{1}(R_{\varphi_{1}}-\underline{R})\omega_{\varphi_{1}}^{n}\geq\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=1^{-}}{\mathcal{K}}(t)=k.

4.1.
A stronger condition

As we have seen before, the potential
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)
of the
\varepsilon
-geodesic converges to
the potential
\varphi(t,\cdot)
of the geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
in
C^{1,{\alpha}}
-norm for each
{\alpha}\in(0,1)
on
\Gamma\times X
.
Therefore, we have

(4.4)

-\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(t)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(R_{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}-\underline{R})\omega_{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}^{n}=\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}(R_{\varphi}-\underline{R})\omega_{\varphi}^{n},

at the boundaries
t=0,1
, since the metric
\omega_{\varepsilon}
coincides with
\omega_{\varphi}
at these two boundaries.
Hence we can compute the first derivative of the energy
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}
near the boundary as follows.

Lemma 4.2
.

We have

(4.5)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,A}(t)=-\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}(R_{\varphi}-\underline{R})\omega_{\varphi}^{n},

at
t=0,1
.

Proof.

We first note that the volume element
\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}
varies smoothly near the boundaries.
Therefore, there exist a small number
\delta>0
,
possibly depends on
\varepsilon
and
A
, such that
the truncated volume element
{\mathfrak{f}}_{A}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})
coincides with
{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})
for all
A
large enough
and all
t\in[0,\delta)\bigcup(1-\delta,1]
.

This implies that the two entropies
H_{A}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})
and
H(\varphi_{\varepsilon})
also coincides in this small interval.
Therefore,
the
A
-truncated Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}
along
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}

is equal to the Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}
along
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}

in a small neighbourhood of the two boundaries, namely,
we have

(4.6)

{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}(t)={\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}(t),

for all
t\in[0,\delta)\bigcup(1-\delta,1]
.
Therefore, we have for all
t
in this small interval

(4.7)

{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,A}(t)={\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(t)=-\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{\varepsilon}(R_{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}-\underline{R})\omega_{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}^{n},

and then our result follows from equation (
4.4
).

∎

In particular, we conclude the following result.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose the Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{M}}
is essentially affine along a geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
with slope
k
.
Then
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,A}
converges uniformly to
k
on
[0,1]
for all
A
large enough.

Proof.

Combing with equation (
4.4
) and (
4.7
), we have for each
A
large

(4.8)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,A}(t)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,A}(t)=k,

at
t=0,1
.
Moreover, the function
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}
is convex on the interval
[0,1]
,
and then we have the control

\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}^{\prime}(0)\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,A}(t)\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,A}(1),

for all
t\in(0,1)
.
Therefore,
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,A}(t)\rightarrow k

uniformly as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, and then our result follows from equation (
3.4
).

∎

Next we will introduce an even stronger condition based on the
\varepsilon
-geodesic.

Definition 4.4
.

The Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{M}}
is
\varepsilon
-affine along a geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
,
if there exists a uniform constant
C>0
such that we have

(4.9)

{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(1)-{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0)\leq C\varepsilon,

for all
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon}
with
\varepsilon>0
small enough.

The geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
and its
\varepsilon
-geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
are uniquely determined, when the boundary values
\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1}
are fixed.
Therefore, our definition of
\varepsilon
-affine energy is well posed.
In fact, this is a stronger condition as follows.

Lemma 4.5
.

If the Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{M}}
is
\varepsilon
-affine along a geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
,
then it is essentially affine along
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Proof.

Thanks to Berman-Berndtsson’s estimates (equation (
4.2
), (
4.3
))
and the fact that the energy
{\mathcal{K}}
is a convex function, it is enough to prove at the boundary of
{\mathcal{G}}
we have

\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{0}(R_{\varphi_{0}}-\underline{R})\omega_{\varphi_{0}}^{n}=\int_{X}\dot{\varphi}_{0}(R_{\varphi_{1}}-\underline{R})\omega_{\varphi_{1}}^{n}.

Due to equation (
4.4
), this reduces to prove

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(0)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(1).

One side inequality, namely,

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(1)\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(0)

is directly implied by our condition (equation (
4.9
)).
The other side follows from the Lemma (
4.2
), and the fact that the energy
\widetilde{{\mathcal{K}}}_{\varepsilon,A}

is a convex function.
∎

In other words, the first derivative
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,A}(t)
converges to the slope
k
in a uniform speed controlled by
C\varepsilon
for all
t\in[0,1]
,
if
{\mathcal{M}}
is
\varepsilon
-affine along
{\mathcal{G}}
. This can be inferred from a similar argument as in Lemma (
4.3
).
Based on this stronger condition, we state our main theorem on the uniformly non-degenerate of
{\mathcal{G}}
as follows.

Theorem 4.6
.

Suppose the Mabuchi functional
{\mathcal{M}}
is
\varepsilon
-affine along a geodesic
{\mathcal{G}}
.
Then
{\mathcal{G}}
is uniformly fiberwise non-degenerate,
namely, there exists a uniform constant
{\kappa}_{1}>0
such that

{\mathcal{G}}|_{X_{t}}>{\kappa}_{1}\omega,

for almost everywhere
t\in[0,1]
.

We emphasis that the constant
{\kappa}_{1}
does not depend on
t
.
Before moving on, we need to recall and improve some computations in
[
6
]
.

4.2.
Estimates

We are going to find a lower bound of the complex Hessian of the energy
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}
(equation (
3.1
)).
As explained in the proof of Theorem (
3.2
),
this boils down to evaluate the lower bound of the following
(n+1,n+1)
form on
P_{\varepsilon,A}

(dd^{c}f_{\varepsilon}-Ric(\omega))\wedge{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n},

Locally near a point
p\in\Gamma\times X
, we write the
\varepsilon
-geodesic as follows

{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}:=g_{t\bar{t}}dt\wedge d\bar{t}+\sum_{{\alpha},\beta=1}^{n}\left(g_{t\bar{\beta}}dt\wedge d\bar{z}^{\beta}+g_{{\alpha}\bar{t}}dz^{{\alpha}}\wedge d\bar{t}+g_{{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}dz^{{\alpha}}\wedge d\bar{z}^{\beta}\right).

This is a Kähler metric on
\Gamma\times X
,
and its restriction on the fiber
X_{t}:=\{t\}\times X
can be written as

\omega_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot):={\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}|_{X_{t}}=\sum_{{\alpha},\beta=1}^{n}g_{{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}dz^{{\alpha}}\wedge d\bar{z}^{\beta}.

Up to a change of holomorphic coordinates on
X_{t}
, we can assume

(4.10)

g_{{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}=\delta_{{\alpha}\beta};\ \ \ dg=0

at this particular point
p
.
Near this point, the
\varepsilon
-geodesic equation can be re-written as

(4.11)

\rho_{\varepsilon}:=g_{t\bar{t}}-g^{\bar{\beta}{\alpha}}g_{{\alpha}\bar{t}}g_{t\bar{\beta}}=\varepsilon e^{-f_{\varepsilon}}.

Then we introduce another
(1,1)
form
\chi_{\varepsilon}
defined by the following equation

\chi_{\varepsilon}:={\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}-\rho_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{-1}dt\wedge d\bar{t}.

This
(1,1)
-form may not be closed anymore, but it is still positive definite on each fiber
X_{t}
,
and satisfies

\chi_{\varepsilon}^{n+1}=0.

Hence we have

(4.12)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{\varepsilon}^{n}+n\rho_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{-1}dt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge\chi_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{\varepsilon}^{n}+n\rho_{\varepsilon}\sqrt{-1}dt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1},

and then we estimate the first factor as follows.

Lemma 4.7
.

There exists a constant
c_{A}>0
, possibly depending on
A
, such that we have

(4.13)

\displaystyle n\rho_{\varepsilon}(dd^{c}f_{\varepsilon}-Ric(\omega))\wedge\sqrt{-1}dt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left\{(\Delta_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon})-c_{A}\right\}{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n+1},

in the open set
P_{\varepsilon,A}
.

Proof.

The L.H.S. of equation (
4.13
) can be computed as follows.

(4.14)

n\rho_{\varepsilon}dd^{c}f_{\varepsilon}\wedge\sqrt{-1}dt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}=\varepsilon(\Delta_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon})\sqrt{-1}dt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge\omega^{n},

and

(4.15)

-n\rho_{\varepsilon}Ric(\omega)\wedge\sqrt{-1}dt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}=-\varepsilon(\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits_{\omega_{\varepsilon}}Ric\omega)\sqrt{-1}dt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge\omega^{n}.

We note that
there exist a constant
c_{A}
satisfying

(4.16)

\mathop{\rm tr}\nolimits_{\omega_{\varepsilon}}Ric\omega<c_{A},

on
P_{\varepsilon,A}
, since the eigenvalues of
\omega_{\varepsilon}
are bounded from below (and above) by a uniform constant on this set.
Therefore, we conclude the inequality

(4.17)

\displaystyle n\rho_{\varepsilon}(dd^{c}f_{\varepsilon}-Ric(\omega))\wedge\sqrt{-1}dt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}){\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n+1}-c_{A}{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n+1}.

∎

Next, we compute the second factor as follows.
Introduce the following vector field on
\Gamma\times X
as

v:=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-g^{\bar{\beta}{\alpha}}g_{t\bar{\beta}}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{{\alpha}}}.

Then one observes that this vector field
v
generates
the kernel of the
(1,1)
-form
\chi_{\varepsilon}
.
Hence we have

(4.18)

(dd^{c}f_{\varepsilon}-Ric(\omega))\wedge\chi_{\varepsilon}^{n}=(dd^{c}f_{\varepsilon}-Ric(\omega))(v,\bar{v})\sqrt{-1}dt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge\chi_{\varepsilon}^{n}

Therefore, the goal is to compute the lower bound of the following term

\partial\bar{\partial}\log\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits(g_{{\alpha}\bar{\beta}})(v,\bar{v}),

and then we have the following computation.

Lemma 4.8
.

We have

(4.19)

\displaystyle\partial\bar{\partial}\log\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits(g_{{\alpha}\bar{\beta}})(v,\bar{v})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||\bar{\partial}_{X}v||^{2}_{\omega_{\varepsilon}}+\varepsilon e^{-f_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-\varepsilon e^{-f_{\varepsilon}}(\Delta_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||\bar{\partial}v||^{2}_{{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}}-\varepsilon e^{-f_{\varepsilon}}(\Delta_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}).

Proof.

At the chosen point
p
,
a standard computation shows the following equation (here we are using the Einstein summation convention)

(4.20)

\displaystyle\partial\bar{\partial}\log\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits(g_{{\alpha}\bar{\beta}})(v,\bar{v})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{t\bar{t},{\alpha}\bar{\alpha}}-\sum_{{\alpha},\beta}|g_{t\bar{\beta},{\alpha}}|^{2}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle g_{t\bar{\gamma}}g_{\gamma\bar{t},{\alpha}\bar{\alpha}}-g_{\gamma\bar{t}}g_{t\bar{\gamma},{\alpha}\bar{\alpha}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle R_{{\alpha}\bar{\gamma}}g_{t\bar{\alpha}}g_{\gamma\bar{t}}

Taking the Laplacian
\Delta_{\varepsilon}
with respect to the metric
\omega_{\varepsilon}
on the both sides of equation (
4.11
), we have

(4.21)

\displaystyle g_{t\bar{t},{\alpha}\bar{\alpha}}-\varepsilon\Delta_{\varepsilon}(e^{-f_{\varepsilon}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\alpha},p}|g_{p\bar{t},\bar{\alpha}}|^{2}+\sum_{\beta,q}|g_{t\bar{q},\bar{\beta}}|^{2}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle R_{q\bar{p}}g_{p\bar{t}}g_{t\bar{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle g_{t\bar{p}}g_{p\bar{t},{\alpha}\bar{\alpha}}+g_{p\bar{t}}g_{t\bar{p},{\alpha}\bar{\alpha}}.

Combing with the two equations above, it turns out that we have

(4.22)

\partial\bar{\partial}\log\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits(g_{{\alpha}\bar{\beta}})(v,\bar{v})=||\bar{\partial}_{X}v||^{2}_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon e^{-f_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-\varepsilon e^{-f_{\varepsilon}}(\Delta_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}).

Furthermore, we can improve the above equality as follows.
First, we observe

\bar{\partial}_{X}v=-g^{\bar{\beta}{\alpha}}g_{t\bar{\beta},\bar{\lambda}}d\bar{z}^{\lambda}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{{\alpha}}}.

In other words, it can be written in tensors as

(4.23)

\bar{\partial}_{\lambda}v^{{\alpha}}=-g_{t\bar{\alpha},\bar{\lambda}};\ \ \ \bar{\partial}_{\lambda}v^{t}=0,

at the point
p
.
Moreover, we have in the time direction

(4.24)

\bar{\partial}_{t}v^{\gamma}=-g_{t\bar{t},\bar{\gamma}}+g_{t\bar{\beta}}g_{\beta\bar{\gamma},\bar{t}};\ \ \ \bar{\partial}_{t}v^{t}=0.

By differentiating equation (
4.11
) once we get

(4.25)

\varepsilon e^{-f_{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{{\alpha}}f_{\varepsilon})=g_{t\bar{t},{\alpha}}-g_{t\bar{p}}g_{p\bar{t},{\alpha}}-g_{p\bar{t}}g_{t\bar{p},{\alpha}},

and similarly

(4.26)

\varepsilon e^{-f_{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{\bar{\beta}}f_{\varepsilon})=g_{t\bar{t},\bar{\beta}}-g_{t\bar{q}}g_{q\bar{t},\bar{\beta}}-g_{q\bar{t}}g_{t\bar{q},\bar{\beta}}.

Hence we have

(4.27)

\displaystyle(\varepsilon e^{-f_{\varepsilon}})^{2}|\nabla_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\bar{\beta}{\alpha}}\left\{(g_{t\bar{t},{\alpha}}-g_{p\bar{t}}g_{{\alpha}\bar{p},t})-g_{t\bar{p}}g_{p\bar{t},{\alpha}}\right\}

\displaystyle\cdot\left\{(g_{t\bar{t},\bar{\beta}}-g_{t\bar{q}}g_{q\bar{\beta},\bar{t}})-g_{q\bar{t}}g_{t\bar{q},\bar{\beta}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{\bar{\beta}{\alpha}}(\partial_{t}v^{\bar{\alpha}}-g_{t\bar{p}}\partial_{p}v^{\bar{\alpha}})(\bar{\partial}_{t}v^{\beta}-g_{q\bar{t}}\bar{\partial}_{q}v^{\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta}|\bar{\partial}_{t}v^{\beta}|^{2}+g_{t\bar{\mu}}g_{\lambda\bar{t}}\partial_{\mu}v^{\bar{\beta}}\bar{\partial}_{\lambda}v^{\beta}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle g_{\mu\bar{t}}\partial_{t}v^{\bar{\beta}}\bar{\partial}_{\mu}v^{\beta}-g_{t\bar{\lambda}}\bar{\partial}_{t}v^{\beta}\partial_{\lambda}v^{\bar{\beta}}.

On the other side, we can compute the inverse matrix of
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
at the point
p
.
First notice that

\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}(p)=\varepsilon e^{-f_{\varepsilon}(p)},

since
g_{{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}=\delta_{{\alpha}\beta}
at this point.
Then a standard calculation shows the following equations.

{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{\bar{t}t}=(\varepsilon e^{-f_{\varepsilon}})^{-1};\ \ \ {\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{\bar{t}p}=-(\varepsilon e^{-f_{\varepsilon}})^{-1}g_{t\bar{p}},

and also

(4.28)

{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{\bar{p}q}=\delta_{pq}+(\varepsilon e^{-f_{\varepsilon}})^{-1}g_{p\bar{t}}g_{t\bar{q}}

Therefore, the four terms on the RHS of equation (
4.27
) can be re-written as

(4.29)

(\varepsilon e^{-f_{\varepsilon}})^{-1}\sum_{\beta}|\bar{\partial}_{t}v^{\beta}|^{2}={\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{\bar{t}t}g_{{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\bar{\partial}_{t}v^{{\alpha}}\partial_{t}v^{\bar{\beta}};

(4.30)

-(\varepsilon e^{-f_{\varepsilon}})^{-1}g_{\mu\bar{t}}\partial_{t}v^{\bar{\beta}}\bar{\partial}_{\mu}v^{\beta}={\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{\bar{\mu}t}g_{{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\bar{\partial}_{\mu}v^{{\alpha}}\partial_{t}v^{\bar{\beta}};

(4.31)

(\varepsilon e^{-f_{\varepsilon}})^{-1}g_{t\bar{\mu}}g_{\lambda\bar{t}}\partial_{\mu}v^{\bar{\beta}}\bar{\partial}_{\lambda}v^{\beta}={\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{\bar{\lambda}\mu}g_{{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\bar{\partial}_{\lambda}v^{{\alpha}}\partial_{\mu}v^{\bar{\beta}}-g_{{\alpha}\bar{\beta}}\bar{\partial}_{\lambda}v^{{\alpha}}\partial_{\lambda}v^{\bar{\beta}}.

Combining with equations (
4.27
) - (
4.31
) above, our equation (
4.19
) follows.

∎

Remark 4.9
.

This new equality (the second line of equation (
4.19
)) is an improvement of what we have obtained in (
[
6
]
).
It will not be used in the proof of our main theorem.
However, it reveals the following important fact: the vanishing of the complex hessian
\partial\bar{\partial}\log\mathop{\rm det}\nolimits(g_{{\alpha}\bar{\beta}})(v,\bar{v})

implies the holomorphicity of the vector field
v
, both in
t
and
X
directions as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, provided with enough regularities of
{\mathcal{G}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
.

Proposition 4.10
.

We have the following integral estimate:

(4.32)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,A+1}(1)-\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,A+1}(0)\geq\int_{P_{\varepsilon,A}}||\bar{\partial}v||^{2}_{{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}}idt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{P_{\varepsilon,A}}||\bar{\partial}_{X}v||^{2}_{\omega_{\varepsilon}}idt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}+\varepsilon\int_{P_{\varepsilon,A}}|\nabla_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}|^{2}idt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge\omega^{n}.

Proof.

Combining with Lemma (
4.7
) and Lemma (
4.8
),
we can estimate the lower bound of the complex hessian of the energy
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}
as follows.

(4.33)

\displaystyle\left(dd^{c}\max\left\{\log\frac{\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}}{\omega^{n}},\log\frac{h_{A}}{\omega^{n}}\right\}-Ric\omega\right)\wedge{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n}+c^{\prime}_{A}{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}^{n+1}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\chi_{P_{\varepsilon,A-1}}\left(||\bar{\partial}_{X}v||^{2}_{\omega_{\varepsilon}}+\varepsilon e^{-f_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\right)idt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n},

where
\chi_{P_{\varepsilon,A-1}}
is the characteristic function of the set
P_{\varepsilon,A-1}
.
In fact, the above inequality (equation
4.33
) is purely local.
For points in the open set
P_{\varepsilon,A}
, it directly follows from our previous computations.
For points in the interior of the complement
(P_{\varepsilon,A})^{c}
,
it follows from the equation (
3.7
).
Finally, for points near the boundary
\partial P_{\varepsilon,A}
, the R.H.S of equation (
4.33
)
reduces to zero by our choice of
\chi_{\varepsilon,A-1}
, and then the inequality is implied by Greene-Wu’s theorem again.

According to equation (
3.6
),
we conclude the following integral estimate

(4.34)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,A+1}(1)-{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,A+1}(0)+\varepsilon C_{A+1}\geq\int_{P_{\varepsilon,A}}||\bar{\partial}v||^{2}_{{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}}idt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{P_{\varepsilon,A}}||\bar{\partial}_{X}v||^{2}_{\omega_{\varepsilon}}idt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge\omega_{\varepsilon}^{n}+\varepsilon\int_{P_{\varepsilon,A}}|\nabla_{\varepsilon}f_{\varepsilon}|^{2}idt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge\omega^{n}.

Replacing the energy
{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}
by its modified version
\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}_{\varepsilon,A}

as in Theorem (
3.2
),
our result follows.
∎

Based on this integral estimate, we further obtain the following partial
L^{2}
control
on the gradient of the fiberwise volume element.

Lemma 4.11
.

Suppose the Mabuchi functional is
\varepsilon
-affine along the geodesic.
Then there exists a constant
C
(possibly depending on
t
and
A
),
and a subsequence of volume elements
{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon_{\ell}})

satisfying

(4.35)

\int_{P_{\varepsilon_{\ell},A}\bigcap X_{t}}|\nabla{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon_{\ell}})|^{2}\omega^{n}\leq C,

for almost everywhere
t\in[0,1]
and any
{\ell}
large enough.

Proof.

Suppose the Mabuchi functional is
\varepsilon
-affine along the geodesic,
and then equation (
4.6
) implies

(4.36)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,A+1}(1)-\widetilde{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon,A+1}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(1)-{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(0)+2\varepsilon C_{A+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon C^{\prime}_{A},

for some constant
C^{\prime}_{A}
.
Therefore, we conclude the following estimate from equations (
4.34
) and (
4.36
).

(4.37)

\displaystyle\int_{P_{\varepsilon,A}}|\nabla{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})|^{2}idt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{2}\int_{P_{\varepsilon,A}}\frac{|\nabla{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})|^{2}}{{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})}idt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{2}\int_{P_{\varepsilon,A}}|\nabla\log{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})|^{2}idt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{\prime}\int_{P_{\varepsilon,A}}|\nabla_{\varepsilon}\log{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})|^{2}idt\wedge d\bar{t}\wedge\omega^{n}\leq C^{\prime\prime}_{A},

where we used the fact
f_{\varepsilon}=\log{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})
, and
\omega_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded from the above.
Moreover, if we take its fiberwise integral as

F_{\varepsilon,A}(t):=\int_{P_{\varepsilon,A}\bigcap X_{t}}|\nabla{\mathfrak{f}}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})|^{2}\omega^{n},

then Fatou’s lemma implies

(4.38)

\int_{0}^{1}\liminf_{\varepsilon}F_{\varepsilon,A}(t)dt\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1}F_{\varepsilon,A}(t)dt\leq C^{\prime\prime}_{A}.

Therefore, for almost everywhere
t\in[0,1]
, there exist a constant
C_{t}

and a subsequence
F_{\varepsilon_{\ell},A}
such that

\lim_{{\ell}\rightarrow+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{\ell},A}(t)\leq C_{t}.

Hence the result follows.
∎

.

\varphi_{\varepsilon_{\ell}}

).

-convergence of

.

5.
The uniform Positive lower bound

To deal with the non-degeneracy of the fiber-wise volume element of
{\mathcal{G}}
,
we would like to utilise
the partial
W^{1,2}
-estimate obtained in equation (
4.35
) and the
L^{2}
-convergence of the volume elements.
However, the difficulty is that the integral on the LHS of this equation is not taken on the whole manifold
X
,
and the integration domain varies with respect to
\varepsilon
and
A
.
In order to overcome this difficulty, we first investigate a local model as follows.

Suppose
f_{{\ell}}
is a sequence of positive smooth functions on the domain
D:=[0,1]^{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}

with uniformly bounded
L^{\infty}
-norm,
and
f
is an
L^{\infty}
non-negative function on
D
such that

f_{\ell}
converges to
f
in
L^{2}
-norm.
We further assume that
the function
f
satisfies the gap phenomenon, namely,
there exists a constant
{\kappa}_{0}>0
such that we have

\{f>{\kappa}_{0}\}\bigcup\{f=0\}=D,

up to a set of measure zero.
In the following, we set

P:=\{f>{\kappa}_{0}\};\ \ \ P^{c}:=\{f=0\}.

Let
{\kappa}
denote a positive continuous function on
D
and we set

P_{{\ell},{\kappa}}:=\{x\in D;\ \ \ f_{\ell}(x)>{\kappa}\}.

Then the following result is crucial.

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose we have
\mu(P)>0
and
\max{\kappa}<{\kappa}_{0}/4
on
D
,
and assume that there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
, such that the following estimate holds

(5.1)

\int_{P_{{\ell},{\kappa}}}|\nabla f_{\ell}|^{2}<C_{1},

for a fixed
{\kappa}
and all
{\ell}
large enough.
Then
f>{\kappa}_{0}
almost everywhere on
D
.

First we will prove that Proposition (
5.1
)
holds in
\mathbb{R}
, namely, we first assume
D=[0,1]
,
and then the following fact is clear by Hölder’s inequality.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose
u
is a smooth positive function on
[0,1]
, and we assume
that
u(a)>2k
and
u(b)<k
for some
0\leq a<b\leq 1
and a constant
k>0
.
Then we have

|b-a|>\frac{k^{2}}{\int_{a}^{b}(u^{\prime}(t))^{2}dt}.

Suppose
E_{1},E_{2}
are two subsets of
D
,
and we denote
d(E_{1},E_{2})
by the distance between them

d(E_{1},E_{2}):=\inf_{x_{1}\in E_{1},x_{2}\in E_{2}}d(x_{1},x_{2}).

Then we have the following observation.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose
P,Q
are two non-empty disjoint subsets of the interval
[0,1]
.
Assume that the union of
P,Q
is the whole interval
[0,1]
up to a set with measure zero.
Then we have
d(P,Q)=0
.

Proof.

We will prove by contradiction.
Suppose the distance between the two sets is positive as

d(P,Q)>\delta>0.

Take a large number
m\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfying
\frac{1}{m}<\frac{\delta}{100}
.
For each point
p\in P
, we define the following open interval as

\mathcal{I}_{p,m}:=(p-\frac{1}{m},p+\frac{1}{m}),

and similarly for each point
q\in Q

\mathcal{I}_{q,m}:=(q-\frac{1}{m},q+\frac{1}{m}).

We note that
\mathcal{I}_{p,m}
is disjoint from the set
Q
for each
p\in P
,
and
\mathcal{I}_{q,m}
is disjoint from the set
P
for each
q\in Q
from our constructions.
In fact, we have

\mathcal{I}_{p,m}\bigcap\mathcal{I}_{q,m}=\emptyset,

for all
p\in P,q\in Q
.
Therefore, the following two unions

\mathcal{U}:=\bigcup_{p\in P}\mathcal{I}_{p,m},\ \ \ \mathcal{V}:=\bigcup_{q\in Q}\mathcal{I}_{q,m}

are mutually disjoint open subsets of the interval
[0,1]
.

Moreover, there is a subset
E\subset[0,1]
with measure zero satisfying

P\bigcup Q\bigcup E=[0,1].

We claim that
E
is contained in the union
\mathcal{U}\bigcup\mathcal{V}
.
Otherwise, there is a point
a\in E
such that we have

d(\{a\},P)>\frac{1}{2m},\ \ \ d(\{a\},Q)>\frac{1}{2m}.

Then the open interval
(a-\frac{1}{2m},a+\frac{1}{2m})
must be contained in
E
,
but this is impossible since
E
has measure zero.
Therefore, we conclude that the union
\mathcal{U}\bigcup\mathcal{V}
is exactly the interval
[0,1]
,
which is impossible since
[0,1]
is a connected set.

∎

After passing to a subsequence, we can further assume that
f_{\ell}
converges to
f
almost everywhere
on
[0,1]
due to the
L^{2}
convergence (see Remark (
3.9
)).
Therefore, there is a subset
E\subset[0,1]
with measure zero such that

f_{\ell}\rightarrow f
in the poinwise sense outside of
E
.
Then we are ready to prove the
1
-dimensional case as follows.

Lemma 5.4
.

If we have
n=1
, then Proposition (
5.1
) holds.

Proof.

We will prove by contradiction. Suppose that
P^{c}
has a positive measure.
Thanks to Lemma (
5.3
), we observe that the following two non-empty sets are not separable
in distance, namely, we have

d(P-E,P^{c}-E)=0.

Then there exists a sequence of pairs
(a_{j},b_{j})\in(P-E)\times(P^{c}-E)

such that
|a_{j}-b_{j}|\rightarrow 0
as
j\rightarrow\infty
.
Without loss of generality, we assume
a_{j}<b_{j}
in the following.
Now fix an arbitrary pair
(a_{j},b_{j})
.
Due to the pointwise convergence of
f_{{\ell}}
to

and

\lim_{{\ell}\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X}(f_{\ell}-f)^{2}\omega^{n}\leq\left(2+\frac{8C}{{\kappa}_{0}}\right)\max_{X_{{\tau}}}{\kappa},

7.1.
The maximum function

In order to prove this lemma,
the first step is to investigate the following maximum function.
Define a function
F:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as

F(x):=x\log x,

and
F(0)=0
. Then
F
is a convex continuous function on its domain.
In fact, it is smooth in
\mathbb{R}^{+}
, and
its first and second derivatives are

F^{\prime}(x)=\log x+1;\ \ \ F^{\prime\prime}(x)=x^{-1}

Moreover, we can truncate
F
by a small number
{\kappa}>0
and introduce the following maximum function

h_{{\kappa}}(x):=\max\{x\log x,x\log{\kappa}\}.

This is also a convex and continuous function on
[0,+\infty)
,
and it is piecewise smooth in this domain.
Its first derivative exists everywhere on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
except at the point
x={\kappa}
, and we have

(7.3)

h^{\prime}_{{\kappa}}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\log{\kappa},&\mbox{for}&x<{\kappa}\\
1+\log x,&\mbox{for}&x>{\kappa}.\\
\end{array}\right.

Furthermore, we can also compute its second derivative on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
as

(7.4)

h^{\prime\prime}_{{\kappa}}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}0,&\mbox{for}&x<{\kappa}\\
\delta({\kappa}),&\mbox{for}&x={\kappa}\\
\frac{1}{x},&\mbox{for}&x>{\kappa},\\
\end{array}\right.

where
\delta({\kappa})
is the
Dirac-delta
function at the point
x={\kappa}
.
We note that the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus is still satisfied for
h_{{\kappa}},h^{\prime}_{{\kappa}},h^{\prime\prime}_{{\kappa}}

on the interval
[0,1]
, since we can take the differentiation in the sense of the generalized derivatives.

Fixing a point
x
on the fiber, we introduce another variable
t\in[0,1]
and take

u_{t}:=tf_{\ell}+(1-t)f=at+b,

where
a:=(f_{\ell}-f)(x)
and
b:=f(x)
.
We note that
u_{t}
is non-negative and has a uniform upper bound for all
t
,
{\ell}
and
x
,
and it is strictly positive for all
t>0
, or
t=0
and
f(x)>0
.
Define a new composition function as

F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(t):=h_{{\kappa}}(u_{t}).

Observe that this function
F_{{\ell}.{\kappa}}
is actually linear in
t
for all
u_{t}
small.
Moreover, its derivatives can be written as

(7.5)

F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}a\log{\kappa},&\mbox{for}&at+b<{\kappa}\\
a\log(at+b)+a,&\mbox{for}&at+b>{\kappa};\\
\end{array}\right.

(7.6)

F^{\prime\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}0,&\mbox{for}&at+b<{\kappa}\\
a\delta(t_{0}),&\mbox{for}&at+b={\kappa}\\
\frac{a^{2}}{at+b},&\mbox{for}&at+b>{\kappa},\\
\end{array}\right.

where
t_{0}
is determined by the equation

at_{0}+b={\kappa}.

In particular, we have
F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)=a\log{\kappa}
if
f(x)<{\kappa}
,
and
F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)=a\log b+a
if
f(x)>{\kappa}
.
Then the following convergence holds.

Lemma 7.2
.

For all
A
large enough, we have

\lim_{{\ell}\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)\omega^{n}=0.

Proof.

When the constant
A
is large enough, we can assume
{\kappa}<{\kappa}_{0}/2
on the fiber
X_{{\tau}}
,
where
{\kappa}_{0}
is the gap of the fiber-wise volume element of
{\mathcal{G}}
defined in equation (
3.20
).
Then the fiber can be completely decomposed into two parts as in equation (
3.19
)

X_{{\tau}}=P_{A,{\tau}}\bigcup P_{{\tau}}^{c},

up to a set of measure zero.
Recall that the two sets can be re-written as follows

P_{A,{\tau}}=\{x\in X_{{\tau}};\ \ \ f(x)>{\kappa}(x)\};\ \ \ P^{c}_{{\tau}}:=\{x\in X_{\tau};\ \ \ f(x)=0\}.

Then we have

(7.7)

\displaystyle\int_{X}F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P_{A,{\tau}}}F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)+\int_{P_{{\tau}}^{c}}F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P_{A,{\tau}}}(f_{{\ell}}-f)\log f+\int_{P_{A,{\tau}}}(f_{{\ell}}-f)+\int_{P_{\tau}^{c}}f_{{\ell}}\log{\kappa}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{P_{A,{\tau}}}(f_{{\ell}}-f)\log f+\int_{P_{{\tau}}^{c}}(f_{\ell}-f)\log{\kappa}+\int_{P_{{\tau}}^{c}}f\log{\kappa}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{X}(f_{\ell}-f)-\int_{P^{c}_{\tau}}(f_{\ell}-f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}(f_{\ell}-f)\log\max\{f,{\kappa}\}+\int_{X}(f_{\ell}-f)-\int_{P^{c}_{{\tau}}}f_{\ell}.

The three terms on the RHS of equation (
7.7
) will all converge to zero as
{\ell}\rightarrow+\infty
,
since
||f||_{L^{\infty}},||f_{\ell}||_{L^{\infty}}
are uniformly bounded and
f_{\ell}\rightarrow f
weakly in
L^{p}
for any
p\geq 1
,
and then our result follows.

∎

7.2.
The four cases

Next we will apply the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus on the function
F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(t)
and its first derivative,
namely, we have

(7.8)

\displaystyle F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(1)-F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{{\kappa}}(f_{\ell})-h_{{\kappa}}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{0}}F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(t)dt+\int_{t_{0}}^{1}F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(t)dt.

A first observation is that the point
t_{0}
may not be in the integration domain above.
Suppose the point
x
is in the subset
P_{{\tau}}^{c}
,
and then we have
b=f(x)=0
,
a=f_{{\ell}}(x)>0
.
Therefore, the point
t_{0}
belongs to the interval
(0,1)

if and only if
0<{\kappa}<a
.

Otherwise, we have
t_{0}\geq 1
if
f_{\ell}(x)\leq{\kappa}
, but
t_{0}\leq 0
is not possible in this case
since it means
{\kappa}\leq 0
.

On the other hand, suppose the point
x
is in the subset
P_{A,{\tau}}
.
Then we have
b=f(x)>{\kappa}
and
a=f_{\ell}(x)-f(x)
.
Hence
t_{0}\in(0,1)
if and only if

a<0
and
b>{\kappa}>a+b=f_{{\ell}}(x)
.

Otherwise, when
a\geq 0
, we have
f_{l}(x)\geq f(x)>{\kappa}
for
t_{0}\leq 0
;
or when
a<0
, we have
{\kappa}\leq f_{\ell}(x)
for
t_{0}\geq 1
.

In conclusion, we distinguish all situations into the following four cases:

(i)

x\in P_{A,{\tau}}
,
f_{\ell}(x)\geq f(x)>{\kappa}
or
f(x)>f_{\ell}(x)\geq{\kappa}
;

(ii)

x\in P^{c}_{{\tau}}
,
f(x)=0
and
f_{\ell}(x)\leq{\kappa}
;

(iii)

x\in P^{c}_{{\tau}}
,
f(x)=0
and
f_{\ell}(x)>{\kappa}
;

(iv)

x\in P_{A,{\tau}}
,
f_{\ell}(x)<{\kappa}<f(x)
.

We note that these four cases are disjoint from each other.
Then we will discuss case by case.
For
Case (i)
, we note that

u_{t}>{\kappa}
and then
h_{{\kappa}}(u_{t})=u_{t}\log u_{t}
for all
t\in[0,1]
.
Therefore, we can further compute as follows.

(7.9)

\displaystyle F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(1)-F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{t}F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}^{\prime\prime}(s)dsdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{a^{2}ds}{as+b}dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)+\frac{a^{2}}{2C},

where the constant
C
is the uniform upper bound of
u_{t}
.

For
\mathbf{Case(ii)}
, we note
u_{t}\leq{\kappa}
and then
h_{{\kappa}}(u_{t})=u_{t}\log{\kappa}
.
Hence we have

(7.10)

\displaystyle F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(1)-F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{t}F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}^{\prime\prime}(s)dsdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0).

The two cases above are the easy ones.
For the remaining cases, we will utilise equations (
7.5
) and (
7.6
) in the computation.
In
\mathbf{Case(iii)}
, we note that
h_{{\kappa}}(u_{t})=u_{t}\log{\kappa}
for
t\leq t_{0}
and
h_{{\kappa}}(u_{t})=u_{t}\log u_{t}
for
t>t_{0}
.
Recall that
t_{0}={\kappa}/a
in this case, and hence the computation follows.

(7.11)

\displaystyle F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(1)-F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{{\kappa}/a}F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(t)dt+\int_{{\kappa}/a}^{1}F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(t)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\kappa}}{a}F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)+\int_{0}^{{\kappa}/a}\int_{0}^{t}F^{\prime\prime}_{{\ell},{\tau}}(s)dsdt+\int_{{\kappa}/a}^{1}F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}^{\prime}(t)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)+\int_{{\kappa}/a}^{1}\left\{a+\int_{{\kappa}/a}^{t}F^{\prime\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(s)ds\right\}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)+(a-{\kappa})+\int_{{\kappa}/a}^{1}\int_{{\kappa}/a}^{t}\frac{a^{2}ds}{as+b}dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)+(a-{\kappa})+\frac{a^{2}}{2C}(1-{\kappa}/a)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)+\frac{a^{2}}{2C}+a(1-{\kappa}/C)-{\kappa}.

Recall that
a>0
in this case, and then we have

(7.12)

F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(1)-F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)\geq F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)+\frac{a^{2}}{2C}-{\kappa},

for all
{\kappa}
small enough.
Finally, the most difficult one is
\mathbf{Case(iv)}
.
As before, we first note that
h_{{\kappa}}(u_{t})=u_{t}\log u_{t}
for

t\leq t_{0}
and
h_{{\kappa}}(u_{t})
and
h_{{\kappa}}(u_{t})=u_{t}\log{\kappa}

for
t>t_{0}
. Then we compute in a similar way.

(7.13)

\displaystyle F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(1)-F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{{\kappa}-b}{a}}F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(t)dt+\int_{\frac{{\kappa}-b}{a}}^{1}F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(t)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\kappa}-b}{a}F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)+\int_{0}^{\frac{{\kappa}-b}{a}}\int_{0}^{t}F^{\prime\prime}_{{\ell},{\tau}}(s)dsdt+\int_{\frac{{\kappa}-b}{a}}^{1}F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}^{\prime}(t)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)+\int_{0}^{\frac{{\kappa}-b}{a}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{a^{2}ds}{as+b}dt+\int^{1}_{\frac{{\kappa}-b}{a}}\left\{\int_{0}^{\frac{{\kappa}-b}{a}}F^{\prime\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(s)ds+a\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)+a+b+\frac{({\kappa}-b)^{2}}{2C}+\frac{({\kappa}-b)(a+b-{\kappa})}{C}-{\kappa}.

Recall that we have
a+b=f_{\ell}(x)>0
,
{\kappa}-b={\kappa}-f(x)<0
and
a+b-{\kappa}=f_{\ell}(x)-{\kappa}<0
.
Hence the following estimate holds.

(7.14)

\displaystyle F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(1)-F_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)+\frac{({\kappa}-b)^{2}}{2C}-{\kappa}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)+\frac{{\kappa}_{0}^{2}}{8C}-{\kappa}.

The last inequality in equation (
7.14
) holds is because that
we have picked up
{\kappa}<{\kappa}_{0}/2
, and
P_{A,{\tau}}
is actually the set where
f(x)>0
on the fiber which is equal to

\{x\in X_{{\tau}};\ \ \ f(x)>{\kappa}_{0}\}

up to a set of measure zero by the gap phenomenon.

Combining with equations (
7.9
) - (
7.14
) above, we conclude the following inequality after taking the integral on
X_{{\tau}}
.

(7.15)

\displaystyle\int_{X}(h_{{\kappa}}(f_{\ell})-h_{{\kappa}}(f))\omega^{n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{X}F^{\prime}_{{\ell},{\kappa}}(0)\omega^{n}-\max_{X_{{\tau}}}{\kappa}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2C}\int_{\left(P^{c}_{{\tau}}\bigcap\{f_{\ell}\leq{\kappa}\}\right)^{c}\bigcap\left(P_{A,{\tau}}\bigcap\{f_{l}<{\kappa}<f\}\right)^{c}}(f_{\ell}-f)^{2}\omega^{n}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8C}{\kappa}_{0}^{2}\mu\left(P_{A,{\tau}}\bigcap\{f_{l}<{\kappa}<f\}\right).

Then we are ready to prove the main theorem in this section.

Proof of Lemma (
7.1
).

By our choice on the function
{\kappa}
and the maximum function
h_{{\kappa}}
,
it follows

(7.16)

H_{A}(\varphi_{\varepsilon_{\ell}})-H_{A}(\varphi)=\int_{X}(h_{{\kappa}}(f_{\ell})-h_{{\kappa}}(f))\omega^{n}.

Thanks to Corollary (
3.5
), the LHS of equation (
7.15
) converges to zero as
{\ell}\rightarrow+\infty
.
Meanwhile, our Lemma (
7.2
) implies the first term on the RHS of equation (
7.15
) also converges to zero.
Therefore, it implies

(7.17)

\frac{8C}{{\kappa}_{0}^{2}}(\max_{X_{\tau}}{\kappa})\geq\lim_{{\ell}\rightarrow+\infty}\mu\left(P_{A,{\tau}}\bigcap\{f_{l}<{\kappa}<f\}\right).

Furthermore, we note that the two subsets
P_{A,{\tau}}\bigcap\{f_{l}<{\kappa}<f\}
and
P^{c}_{{\tau}}\bigcap\{f_{\ell}\leq{\kappa}\}
are mutually disjoint.
Then the third term on the RHS of equation (
7.15
) can be decomposed into the following three parts.

\int_{X}(f_{\ell}-f)^{2}-\int_{P^{c}_{{\tau}}\bigcap\{f_{\ell}\leq{\kappa}\}}(f_{\ell}-f)^{2}-\int_{P_{A,{\tau}}\bigcap\{f_{l}<{\kappa}<f\}}(f_{\ell}-f)^{2}.

The first negative term in the equation above can be estimated as

(7.18)

\int_{P^{c}_{{\tau}}\bigcap\{f_{\ell}\leq{\kappa}\}}(f_{\ell}-f)^{2}\leq\max_{X_{\tau}}{\kappa}^{2},

and the second negative term can be estimated by equation (
7.17
) as

(7.19)

\int_{P_{A,{\tau}}\bigcap\{f_{l}<{\kappa}<f\}}(f_{\ell}-f)^{2}\leq C^{2}\mu\left(P_{A,{\tau}}\bigcap\{f_{l}<{\kappa}<f\}\right).

Combing with equations (
7.16
) - (
7.20
) and take the limit in
{\ell}
, we eventually conclude the following estimate

(7.20)

\left(2+\frac{8C^{3}}{{\kappa}_{0}}\right)\max_{X_{\tau}}{\kappa}\geq\lim_{{\ell}\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}(f_{\ell}-f)^{2}\omega^{n},

and then our result follows.

∎

Abstract
We present GFlaT, a new algorithm that uses a graph-neural-network to determine the flavor of neutral B \mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}} mesons produced in Υ ( 4 S ) \mathit{{\Upsilon}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}{(4S)} decays. It improves previous algorithms by using the information from all charged final-state particles and the relations between them. We evaluate its performance using B \mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}} decays to flavor-specific hadronic final states reconstructed in a 362 fb − 1 362\text{\,}\mathrm{f}\mathrm{b}^{-1} sample of electron-positron collisions collected at the Υ ( 4 S ) \mathit{{\Upsilon}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}{(4S)} resonance with the Belle II detector at the SuperKEKB collider. We achieve an effective tagging efficiency of 37.40 ± ± 0.43 % 37.40\pm\pm 0.43\text{\,}\mathrm{\%} , where the first uncertainty is statistical and the second systematic, which is 18% better than the previous Belle II algorithm. III
GFlaT

GFlaT is designed to run after
\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{\text{sig}}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
is reconstructed and uses information from the tracks and energy deposits in the ECL (clusters) not associated with
\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{\text{sig}}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
, in the same manner as the category-based flavor tagger
Abudinén et al. 2022
.
We refer to these tracks and clusters as the rest of the event (ROE), which mostly originates from
\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{\text{tag}}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
.
Tracks from the ROE must be within the CDC and have points of closest approach (POCAs) to the

\mathit{{e}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{e}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}
interaction region (IR) that are less than
3\text{\,}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{m}
from the IR in the
z
direction and less than
1\text{\,}\mathrm{c}\mathrm{m}
from it in the transverse plane.
The shape and location of the IR are determined from
\mathit{{e}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{e}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}\to\mathit{{\mu}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{+}}}\mathit{{\mu}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{-}}}
events in 30-minute intervals.
We retain only the first 16 charged particles in the ROE, ordered by decreasing momentum in the lab frame.
According to simulation,
the average number of charged particles in the ROE is 4.8, and less than 0.001% of events have more than 16 charged particles.

GFlaT uses 25 input variables for each ROE charged particle:
the lab-frame Cartesian components of its momentum and the displacement of its POCA from the IR;
particle-identification likelihoods for each of the six possible charged final-state particles,
\mathit{{e}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
,
\mathit{{\mu}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
,
\mathit{{\pi}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
,
\mathit{{K}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}
,
proton, and deuteron;
and the products of the charge of the particle and the output of the category-based flavor tagger for each of its 13 categories.
3
3

3

corresponding to
q_{\rm cand}y_{\rm cat}
defined in Ref.
Abudinén et al. 2022
.

The input variables have the same distributions for
\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{$0$}}}
and
\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{B}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{$0$}}}
except for
differences in the detection and reconstruction efficiency for negative and positive charged particles.

GFlaT uses a dynamic-graph-convolutional-neural-network that has been used for jet tagging at LHC experiments
Qu and Gouskos 2020
.
GFlaT first processes the input variables using the EdgeConv algorithm
Wang et al. 2019
, which consists of three neural networks:
edge and node networks run in parallel, and a weight network runs on their output.
In the context of graph-neural-networks, the set of ROE charged particles is a graph with each particle a node and each pair an edge.
The node network processes the variables of each particle to update them.
The edge network processes the variables of each pair of particles to update the variables of each particle.
To reduce computational resources, with no impact on performance, the edge network processes information from pairs formed from only the five nearest neighbors to each particle.
The weight network processes the outputs of the edge and node networks with a squeeze-and-excitation algorithm that calculates weights based on variable importance
Hu et al. 2017
.
The output of the EdgeConv consists of the updated variables for each particle that are improved to more accurately reflect the characteristics of each particle.

GFlaT runs EdgeConv twice.
The first run processes the measured particle variables, with its edge network finding nearest neighbors based on POCAs.
The second run processes the output of the first run, with its edge network finding nearest neighbors based on particle similarity using the updated particle variables.
To keep output reasonably symmetric between
\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{$0$}}}
and
\mathit{{\mkern 4.0mu\overline{\mkern-4.0mu{B}}}{}_{\mspace{-2mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{0mu}\scriptstyle{$0$}}}
,
the output variables of each particle from the second EdgeConv are multiplied by its charge.
The averages, maxima, and minima of the outputs are processed with a final network, the event network, which outputs one variable,
qr_{\text{\!GFlaT }}
, which is in
[-1,1]
, with
q_{\text{tag}}=\sign(qr_{\text{\!GFlaT }})
and
r=\absolutevalue*{\qrgflat}
.

\mathit{{B}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{\text{tag}}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}

The training data set consists of
5\text{\times}{10}^{6}

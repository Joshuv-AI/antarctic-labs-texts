1.
Introduction

The concept of Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity or simply regularity was first presented in the early 1980s by Eisenbud and Goto
[
8
]
, and Ooishi
[
23
]
as an algebraic equivalent to the concept of regularity for coherent sheaves on projective spaces, which was formerly explored by Mumford
[
22
]
. It is a kind of universal bound for important invariants of graded algebras such as the maximum degree of the syzygies and the maximum non-vanishing degree of the local cohomology modules.

The concept of regularity can also be defined for graded structures over a commutative ring
like the Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I)=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I^{n}t^{n}\subseteq R[t]
, where
t
is an indeterminate over
R
and the associated graded ring
G(I)=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}I^{n}/I^{n+1}
of
R
with respect to
I
.
The relation between
\operatorname{reg}\mathcal{R}(I)
and
\operatorname{reg}G(I)
was well studied by numerous authors in the past (see
[
24
,
41
]
).

Efforts are frequently made to determine its upper bounds using simpler invariants which are computationally easier to determine. The most fundamental invariants that reflect the complexity of a graded algebra are its dimension and multiplicity. Nevertheless, it is not easy to establish a simpler upper bound only based on the multiplicity and dimension. Previous results of Srinivas and Trivedi
[
34
,
35
]
, and Trivedi
[
38
,
39
]
gave bounds on regularity in terms of dimension and multiplicity for Cohen-Macaulay rings and generalized Cohen-Macaulay modules. A celebrated result by Rossi, Trung and Valla
[
31
, Theorem 3.3]
, gave a bound on the regularity of the associated graded ring with respect to the the maximal ideal in a local ring involving extended degree, dimension and multiplicity.
For Cohen-Macaulay local rings
[
31
, Corollary 3.4]
, authors proved that

\operatorname{reg}G(\mathfrak{m})\leq\begin{cases}e(\mathfrak{m})-1,&\text{if }d=1\\
e(\mathfrak{m})^{2((d-1)!)-1}[e(\mathfrak{m})-1]^{(d-1)!},&\text{if }d\geq 2.\end{cases}

Linh
[
15
, Theorem 4.4]
further extended these bounds to the associated graded module of an arbitrary finitely generated graded
R
-module with respect to an arbitrary
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. There are various bounds on
\operatorname{reg}G(I)
in terms of other invariants of
I
(see
[
6
,
7
,
36
,
43
]
).

Recall that in a Noetherian ring
R,
the ascending chain of ideals
I\subseteq(I^{2}:I)\subseteq(I^{3}:I^{2})\subseteq\ldots\subseteq(I^{n+1}:I^{n})\subseteq\ldots

eventually stabilizes to an ideal
\widetilde{I}=\displaystyle\bigcup_{n\geq 1}(I^{n+1}:I^{n})
. This ideal
\widetilde{I}
is known as the
Ratliff-Rush closure
of
I
or the
Ratliff-Rush ideal
associated with
I.
An ideal
I
is termed
Ratliff-Rush closed
if
\widetilde{I}=I.
The
Ratliff-Rush filtration
with respect to
I
, is the filtration
\mathcal{F}=\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}_{n\geq 0},
and if
\operatorname{grade}(I)>0
, then
\widetilde{I^{n}}=I^{n}
for sufficiently large
n.
For an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
I,
we define

\rho(I)=\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\widetilde{I^{k}}=I^{k}\text{ for all }k\geq n\}.

This numerical invariant is known as the
Stability index
of the Ratliff-Rush filtration or the
Ratliff-Rush Index.

The principal goal of this paper is to introduce upper bounds on the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity using higher Hilbert coefficients (see Definition
2.1
). The starting point of our approach is the description of the regularity by Rossi, Trung and Trung
[
30
, Theorem 2.4]
. The authors proved that for a two-dimensional
Buchsbaum ring
R
with
0ptR>0
,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal, and
J
a minimal reduction of
I
,

\operatorname{reg}G(I)=\operatorname{reg}\mathcal{R}(I)=\max\{r_{J}(I),\rho(I)\}.

To establish an upper bound on the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity in dimension two, it is crucial to determine effective bounds on both
r_{J}(I)
and
\rho(I)
. A remarkable result of Rossi
[
28
, Corollary 1.5]
, gave bound on
r_{J}(I)
in Cohen-Macaulay local rings of dimension at most two.
Consequently, if
\operatorname{reg}G(I)=\rho(I)
, then it is essential to give a bound on
\rho(I).
The computational task of determining
\rho(I)
poses significant challenges even in dimension two, just because
\widetilde{I^{n}}=I^{n}
does not imply
\widetilde{I^{n+1}}=I^{n+1}.
Thus, our aim is to establish bounds on
\rho(I)
for Cohen-Macaulay local rings of dimension
d
.

Huneke
[
14
, Proposition 4.3]
established a significant relationship between
\rho(I)
and
r_{J}(I)
in dimension two. He proved that for a Cohen-Macaulay local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
of dimension
d=2,

I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
J\subseteq I
a minimal reduction, if
\widetilde{I^{m}}=I^{m}
for some
m\geq r_{J}(I)
, then
\widetilde{I^{n}}=I^{n}
for all
n\geq m.
Building on this foundation, we first extend this result to

d
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local rings (see Proposition
3.1
). As a direct consequence of this generalization, we establish the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 2
,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
J\subseteq I
be a minimal reduction of
I
.
If
\widetilde{I^{r_{J}(I)}}\neq I^{r_{J}(I)},
then

\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+(-1)^{d+1}(e_{d+1}(I)-\widetilde{e}_{d+1}(I)).

We observe that under the assumption that the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
behaves well mod a superficial sequence
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-2}\in I
(see Definition
2.7
) in Theorem
1.1
, if the dimension of the ring is odd then
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+e_{d+1}(I),
and if the dimension of the ring is even then
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1-e_{d+1}(I)+\left(\frac{r_{J}(I)-1}{d}\right)(e_{d}(I)-e_{d-1}(I)+\cdots+e(I)-\lambda(R/I)+\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I)).

For two-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local rings, we prove the following bound on
\rho(I):

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. For a minimal reduction
J
of
I
, if
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I),
then

(1)

\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I)-e_{3}(I).

Though the bound in (
1
) is quadratic in
e_{2}(I),
we illustrate various examples where it is tighter than the earlier known bounds.

One of the generalizations of a Cohen-Macaulay ring is a Buchsbaum ring. In such rings, the condition of a system of parameters being a regular sequence is relaxed to being a weak sequence. Recall that a Noetherian local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is called a Buchsbaum ring if every system of parameter
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
of
R
is a weak sequence, i.e.,

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}):x_{i}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}):\mathfrak{m}\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,r.

Therefore, it is worth asking if we can find a similar bound on
\rho(I)
for Buchsbaum rings of positive depth. Thus, we prove the following result:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Buchsbaum ring with
0ptR>0
and
\mathbb{S}
be the
S_{2}
-fication of
R
. Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal with minimal reduction
J
and assume that
I=I\mathbb{S}
. If
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I),
then
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(J)-1)(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(J))-e_{3}(I)
.

As an application, the bounds we establish on
\rho(I)
enables us to derive corresponding bounds on
\operatorname{reg}G(I)
in two-dimensional Cohen Macaulay local rings, when
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I)
(see Proposition
5.5
and Proposition
5.6
).

This paper is organized into five sections. Definitions, introductory ideas and notations are all covered in Section 2. In Section 3, we establish bounds on
\rho(I)
and prove Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 1.2. Further in this section, we gather various bounds on
\rho(I)
only in terms of
r_{J}(I).
In Section 4, we prove Theorem 1.3 and establish bounds on
\rho(I)
in two-dimensional Buchsbaum rings with positive depth.
Section 5 is an application of the results of Sections 3 and 4, where we establish bounds on the regularity. We provide examples to support our claims.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we provide an overview of the definitions and relevant facts that will be used throughout the paper, as well as establish some conventions and notations. Throughout,
(R,\mathfrak{m})
will be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring (
unless stated otherwise
) with an infinite residue field and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of
R
. The Krull dimension of the ring
R
will be denoted by
d
and we will always assume that
d\geq 2
. For undefined terms, we redirect readers to
[
4
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A sequence of ideals
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called an
I
-
admissible filtration
, if for all
n,m\in\mathbb{Z},

(i)

I_{n+1}\subseteq I_{n},

(ii)

I_{m}I_{n}\subseteq I_{m+n}
and
(iii)

I^{n}\subseteq I_{n}\subseteq I^{n-k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}.
The
first Hilbert function
or the
Hilbert-Samuel function
of
\mathcal{I}
,
H^{1}_{\mathcal{I}}(n):=\lambda(R/I_{n+1})
(where
\lambda()
denotes the length) agrees with a polynomial
P^{1}_{\mathcal{I}}(n)
for sufficiently large
n
. This polynomial, termed the
first Hilbert polynomial
or the
Hilbert-Samuel polynomial
of
\mathcal{I}
, assumes only numerical values for all integers
n
. For simplicity we write
H^{1}_{\mathcal{I}}(n)
as
H_{\mathcal{I}}(n)
and
P^{1}_{\mathcal{I}}(n)
as
P_{\mathcal{I}}(n)
. By the theory on numerical polynomials we can write

P_{\mathcal{I}}(n)=e_{0}(\mathcal{I})\dbinom{n+d}{d}-e_{1}(\mathcal{I})\dbinom{n+d-1}{d-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{d}e_{d}(\mathcal{I}).

The unique integers
e_{0}(\mathcal{I}),e_{1}(\mathcal{I}),\ldots,e_{d}(\mathcal{I})
are referred to as the
Hilbert coefficients.

When
\mathcal{I}
is the
I
-adic filtration
\{I^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, we write
H_{I}(n)
and
P_{I}(n)
in the place of
H_{\mathcal{I}}(n)
and
P_{\mathcal{I}}(n)
respectively.
The leading coefficient
e_{0}(I)
of the polynomial
P_{I}(n)
is called the
multiplicity
of the ideal
I
, and is denoted by
e(I).
We write
\widetilde{e}_{i}(I)
for the coefficients
e_{i}(\mathcal{I})
, when
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{F}=\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}_{n\geq 0}.

Definition 2.2
.

The
Hilbert series
of
\mathcal{I}
, is the formal power series
\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\lambda(I_{n}/I_{n+1})z^{n}
.
By Hilbert-Serre theorem, we write

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\lambda(I_{n}/I_{n+1})z^{n}=\frac{h_{\mathcal{I}}(z)}{(1-z)^{d}},

where
h_{\mathcal{I}}(z)\in\mathbb{Z}[z]
is the unique polynomial with
h_{\mathcal{I}}(1)\neq 0
, known as the
h-polynomial
of
\mathcal{I}.
The power series,
\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}H_{\mathcal{I}}(n)z^{n}
is called the
Hilbert-Samuel series
of
\mathcal{I}.
Note that
\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}H_{\mathcal{I}}(n)z^{n}=\frac{h_{\mathcal{I}}(z)}{(1-z)^{d+1}},

and an easy computation shows that for all
i\geq 0

e_{i}(\mathcal{I})=\frac{h^{(i)}_{\mathcal{I}}(1)}{i!},

where
h^{(i)}_{\mathcal{I}}(1)
is the
i^{th}
-formal derivative of
h_{\mathcal{I}}(z)
at
z=1.

The conventional literature on local rings concentrates only on the integers
e_{i}(\mathcal{I})
with
0\leq i\leq d.
However, more apparent results can be achieved by including the integers
e_{i}(\mathcal{I})
with
i>d.

Definition 2.3
.

The
second Hilbert function
of
\mathcal{I}
, defined as
H^{2}_{\mathcal{I}}(n):=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\lambda(R/I_{j+1})
coincides with a polynomial
P^{2}_{\mathcal{I}}(n)
known as the
second Hilbert polynomial
for all sufficiently large values of
n
. This is a polynomial of degree
d+1,
and can be formulated as

P^{2}_{\mathcal{I}}(n)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{d+1}(-1)^{j}e_{j}(\mathcal{I})\binom{n+d-j+1}{d-j+1}.

Definition 2.4
.

An ideal
J\subseteq I
is a reduction of
I
if
JI^{n}=I^{n+1}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N.}

If
J
is a reduction of
I
, the
reduction number
of
I
with respect to
J,
denoted by
r_{J}(I),
is the smallest
n
such that
JI^{n}=I^{n+1}.

A reduction is
minimal
if it is minimal with respect to inclusion among all reductions. The
reduction number
of
I
is defined as

r(I):=\min\{r_{J}(I):J\text{ is a minimal reduction of }I\}.

The concept of reduction and minimal reduction was first introduced by Northcott and Rees.
Reduction number is useful in studying the Cohen-Macaulayness of the associated graded ring
G(I)
.

Definition 2.5
.

An element
x\in I\backslash I^{2}
is called a
superficial element
for
I,
if there exist a positive integer
c
such that
(I^{n+1}:x)\cap I^{c}=I^{n}
for all
n\geq c.
A sequence
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{j}
is a
superficial sequence
for
I,
if
x_{1}
is a superficial element for
I
, and
x_{i}
is a superficial element for
I/(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{i-1})
for all
i=2,\ldots,j.

If
x
is a superficial element for
I
, then
(I^{n+1}:x)=I^{n}
for sufficiently large
n.
Let
x
be any superficial element for
I,
then the
stability index
for
x
is defined as

\rho_{x}(I):=\min\{k\in\mathbb{N}:(I^{n+1}:x)=I^{n}\text{ for all }n\geq k\}.

In
[
25
, Corollary 2.7]
, Puthenpurakal proved that
\rho(I)=\rho_{x}(I)
, and thus
\rho_{x}(I)
is independent of the choice of the superficial element.

3.
Bounds on the Stability Index for Cohen-Macaulay Local Rings

In this section, we give upper bounds on
\rho(I)
in terms of reduction number with respect to a minimal reduction and higher Hilbert coefficients.
We begin this section with the following proposition, which extends a key result
[
14
, Proposition 4.3]
, by Huneke to
d
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local rings.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 2,

I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
J\subseteq I
be a minimal reduction. If
\widetilde{I^{m}}=I^{m}
for some
m\geq r_{J}(I)
, then
\widetilde{I^{n}}=I^{n}
for all
n\geq m.
In particular,
\rho(I)\leq m.

Proof.

Using
[
30
, Lemma 1.2]
, any minimal reduction
J
of
I
can be generated by

x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d},
which forms a superficial sequence for
I.
Fix
m\geq r_{J}(I)
such that
\widetilde{I^{m}}=I^{m}.
We have
I^{m}\subseteq(I^{m+1}:x_{1})\subseteq(\widetilde{I^{m+1}}:x_{1})
. By
[
32
, Lemma 3.1(5)]
, we have
(\widetilde{I^{m+1}}:x_{1})=\widetilde{I^{m}},
therefore,
(I^{m+1}:x_{1})=I^{m}
as
\widetilde{I^{m}}=I^{m}
.
Since for every
n
,
I^{n+1}\cap(x_{1})=(x_{1})I^{n}
if and only if
(I^{n+1}:x_{1})=I^{n},
thus, we get

(6)

I^{m+1}\cap(x_{1})=(x_{1})I^{m}.

Now, we will show that
I^{n+1}\cap[(x_{1}):x_{2}]=I^{n+1}\cap(x_{1})
for all
n.
Let
a\in I^{n+1}\cap[(x_{1}):x_{2}],
then
ax_{2}\in(x_{1}).
This implies that there exists
r\in R
such that
ax_{2}+rx_{1}=0.
Since
x_{1},x_{2}
is a regular sequence in
R
, therefore,
a\in(x_{1}).
As
a\in I^{n+1},
we get
a\in I^{n+1}\cap(x_{1}).
So we can conclude that
I^{n+1}\cap[(x_{1}):x_{2}]\subseteq I^{n+1}\cap(x_{1}).
Since the other inclusion is obvious, therefore,

(7)

I^{n+1}\cap[(x_{1}):x_{2}]=I^{n+1}\cap(x_{1})\text{ for all }n.

From equations (
6
) and (
7
), we have
I^{m+1}\cap[(x_{1}):x_{2}]=(x_{1})I^{m}.
Then from Proposition
2.13
, we get
I^{n+1}\cap[(x_{1}):x_{2}]=(x_{1})I^{n}
for all
n\geq m.
This implies
I^{n+1}\cap(x_{1})=(x_{1})I^{n}
for all
n\geq m,
therefore,
(I^{n+1}:x_{1})=I^{n}
for all
n\geq m.
This implies
\rho_{x_{1}}(I)\leq m.
Now from Definition
2.5
, we have
\rho(I)\leq m.

∎

Remark 3.2
.

In general, establishing a straightforward relationship between
r_{J}(I)
and
\rho(I)
is difficult to achieve. When
0ptG(I)\geq 1,
then
\widetilde{I^{n}}=I^{n}
for all
n\geq 1,
therefore,
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)
for every minimal reduction
J
of
I.
However, there are examples which suggest that when
0ptG(I)=0,
both inequalities are possible. Therefore, from Proposition
3.1
, we get that if
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is Cohen-Macaulay of dimension
\geq 2
and
\widetilde{I^{r_{J}(I)}}\neq I^{r_{J}(I)},
then
\widetilde{I^{n}}\neq I^{n}
for all
r_{J}(I)\leq n<\rho(I).
In particular,
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I).

This also provides a lower bound on
\rho(I).
Define
b:=\max\{r_{J}(I):J\text{ is a minimal reduction of }I\}.
If
\widetilde{I^{b}}\neq I^{b},
then
b<\rho(I).

In the next theorem, we give an upper bound on
\rho(I)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 2
,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
J\subseteq I
be a minimal reduction of
I
.
If
\widetilde{I^{r_{J}(I)}}\neq I^{r_{J}(I)},
then

\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+(-1)^{d+1}(e_{d+1}(I)-\widetilde{e}_{d+1}(I)).

Proof.

Since
\widetilde{I^{r_{J}(I)}}\neq I^{r_{J}(I)},
from Remark
3.2
, we have
\widetilde{I^{n}}\neq I^{n}
for all
r_{J}(I)\leq n<\rho(I).
Thus, we get

\begin{split}\rho(I)&\leq r_{J}(I)-1+\sum_{n=r_{J}(I)-1}^{\rho(I)-2}\lambda(\widetilde{I^{n+1}}/I^{n+1})\\
&\leq r_{J}(I)-1+\sum_{n=0}^{m}\lambda(\widetilde{I^{n+1}}/I^{n+1})\text{ (where $m\gg 0$ is an integer)}\\
&\text{$=r_{J}(I)-1+\sum_{n=0}^{m}\lambda(R/I^{n+1})-\sum_{n=0}^{m}\lambda(R/\widetilde{I^{n+1}})$}\\
&=r_{J}(I)-1+H_{I}^{2}(m)-H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(m)\text{ (where $\mathcal{F}:=\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}_{n\geq 0}$)}\\
&=\text{$r_{J}(I)-1+P_{I}^{2}(m)-P_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(m)$ (since $m\gg 0$)}\\
&=r_{J}(I)-1+(-1)^{d+1}(e_{d+1}(I)-\widetilde{e}_{d+1}(I)).\end{split}

Note that the last equality holds since
e_{i}(I)=\widetilde{e}_{i}(I)
for all
0\leq i\leq d.
∎

As an easy consequence, we have the following corollary under the hypothesis that the Ratliff-Rush filtration behaves well mod a superficial sequence.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of odd dimension
d\geq 3
,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
J\subseteq I
be a minimal reduction of
I
. Suppose the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
behaves well mod a superficial sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2}\in I.
If
\widetilde{I^{r_{J}(I)}}\neq I^{r_{J}(I)},
then

\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+e_{d+1}(I).

Proof.

Since the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
behaves well mod a superficial sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2}\in I,
from Remark
2.9

(i)
, we have
0pt\widetilde{G}(I)\geq d-1.
Thus, from
[
32
, Theorem 2.5]
, we have
\widetilde{e}_{d+1}(I)=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq d}\binom{n}{d}\lambda(\widetilde{I^{n+1}}/J\widetilde{I^{n}})\geq 0.
Therefore,
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+e_{d+1}(I).

∎

Note that the bound in Corollary
3.4
gives a linear bound on
\rho(I).
In the next corollary, we give an upper bound on
\rho(I)
for Cohen-Macaulay local rings of even dimension. Before we proceed, we need the following lemma. We remark that the technique used in the proof of the following lemma is inspired from
[
33
, Proposition 3.5]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of
even dimension
d\geq 2
,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
J\subseteq I
be a minimal reduction of
I.
Suppose that the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
behaves well mod a superficial sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2}\in I
, then

\widetilde{e}_{d+1}(I)\leq\left(\frac{r_{J}(I)-1}{d}\right)(e_{d}(I)-e_{d-1}(I)+\cdots+e(I)-\lambda(R/I)+\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I)).

Proof.

Set
\widetilde{r}_{J}(I)=\widetilde{r}
and
v(n)=\lambda(\widetilde{I^{n+1}}/J\widetilde{I^{n}}).
Note that
v(n)=0
for all
n\geq\widetilde{r}.
Since the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
behaves well mod a superficial sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2}\in I,
therefore, from Remark
2.9

(i)
, we have
0pt\widetilde{G}(I)\geq d-1.
Thus, from
[
32
, Theorem 2.5]
, we have
\widetilde{e}_{i}(I)=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq i-1}\binom{n}{i-1}v(n),
for all
i\geq 1.
Consider

\widetilde{e}_{d+1}(I)-\left(\frac{\widetilde{r}-1}{d}\right)(e_{d}(I)-e_{d-1}(I)+\cdots+e(I)-\lambda(R/I))

\begin{split}&=\displaystyle\sum_{n=d}^{\widetilde{r}-1}\binom{n}{d}v(n)-\left(\frac{\widetilde{r}-1}{d}\right)\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n=d-1}^{\widetilde{r}-1}\binom{n}{d-1}v(n)-\displaystyle\sum_{n=d-2}^{\widetilde{r}-1}\binom{n}{d-2}v(n)+\cdots+\lambda(R/J)-\lambda(R/I)\right)\\
&=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\widetilde{r}-1}\binom{n}{d}v(n)-\left(\frac{\widetilde{r}-1}{d}\right)\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\widetilde{r}-1}\binom{n}{d-1}v(n)-\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\widetilde{r}-1}\binom{n}{d-2}v(n)+\cdots+\lambda(R/J)-\lambda(R/I)\right)\\
&=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\widetilde{r}-1}\binom{n}{d}v(n)-\left(\frac{\widetilde{r}-1}{d}\right)\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\widetilde{r}-1}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}(-1)^{d-1-j}\binom{n}{j}\right)v(n)-\lambda(\widetilde{I}/J)+\lambda(R/J)-\lambda(R/I)\right)\\
&=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\widetilde{r}-1}\binom{n}{d}v(n)-\left(\frac{\widetilde{r}-1}{d}\right)\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\widetilde{r}-1}\binom{n-1}{d-1}v(n)-\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I)\right)\text{ (since $\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}(-1)^{d-1-j}\binom{n}{j}=\binom{n-1}{d-1}$ for every $d\geq 1$)}\\
&=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\widetilde{r}-1}\left(\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)\ldots(n-d+1)}{d!}-\left(\frac{\widetilde{r}-1}{d}\right)\frac{(n-1)(n-2)\ldots(n-d+1)}{(d-1)!}\right)v(n)+\left(\frac{\widetilde{r}-1}{d}\right)\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I)\\
&=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\widetilde{r}-1}\left(\frac{(n-1)(n-2)\ldots(n-d+1)}{d!}(n-\widetilde{r}+1)\right)v(n)+\left(\frac{\widetilde{r}-1}{d}\right)\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I)\\
&\leq\left(\frac{\widetilde{r}-1}{d}\right)\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I),\end{split}

since
(n-\widetilde{r}+1)\leq 0
for all
1\leq n\leq\widetilde{r}-1.
Therefore,
\widetilde{e}_{d+1}(I)\leq\left(\frac{\widetilde{r}_{J}(I)-1}{d}\right)(e_{d}(I)-e_{d-1}(I)+\cdots+e_{0}(I)-\lambda(R/I)+\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I)).
From Remark
2.9

(iii)
, we have
\widetilde{e}_{d+1}(I)\leq\left(\frac{r_{J}(I)-1}{d}\right)(e_{d}(I)-e_{d-1}(I)+\cdots+e(I)-\lambda(R/I)+\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I))
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay ring of even dimension
d\geq 2
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Suppose the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
behaves well mod a superficial sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2}\in I
. For a minimal reduction
J
of
I
, if
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I),
then

\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1-e_{d+1}(I)+\left(\frac{r_{J}(I)-1}{d}\right)(e_{d}(I)-e_{d-1}(I)+\cdots+e(I)-\lambda(R/I)+\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I)).

Proof.

From Theorem
3.3
, we have
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+\widetilde{e}_{d+1}(I)-e_{d+1}(I).
Again, from Lemma
3.5
, we have
\widetilde{e}_{d+1}(I)\leq\left(\frac{r_{J}(I)-1}{d}\right)(e_{d}(I)-e_{d-1}(I)+\cdots+e_{0}(I)-\lambda(R/I)+\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I))
. Therefore,
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1-e_{d+1}(I)+\left(\frac{r_{J}(I)-1}{d}\right)(e_{d}(I)-e_{d-1}(I)+\cdots+e(I)-\lambda(R/I)+\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I)).

∎

We now establish bounds on the stability index of the Ratliff-Rush filtration, with a particular focus on dimension two.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
J\subseteq I
be a minimal reduction of
I.
If
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I),
then

\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1-e_{3}(I)+\left(\frac{r_{J}(I)-1}{2}\right)(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e(I)-\lambda(R/I)+\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I)).

Proof.

From Corollary
3.6
, for
d=2
, we get
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1-e_{3}(I)+\left(\frac{r_{J}(I)-1}{2}\right)(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e(I)-\lambda(R/I)+\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I)).

∎

In the next proposition, we give another bound on
\rho(I)
. Note that the bound given in Proposition
3.7
depends on the Ratliff-Rush closure of
I
, which can be challenging to compute. In contrast, the following bound is computationally more accessible as it relies solely on the higher Hilbert coefficients. Before proceeding further, it is necessary to introduce the following lemma. We note that the proof follows a similar approach to
[
18
, Theorem 4.1]
, but we include the steps for completeness.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Then
\widetilde{e}_{3}(I)\leq(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}:=\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
H_{\mathcal{F}}(n):=\lambda(R/\widetilde{I^{n}})
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. By the difference formula in
[
2
, Proposition 4.4]
, we have for all
n\geq-1

(8)

P_{\mathcal{F}}(n)-H_{\mathcal{F}}(n)=\lambda(\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{R_{+}}}(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{F}))_{n+1}).

Now taking sum for large
m
on both sides of the equation, we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{m}\lambda(\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{R_{+}}}(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{F}))_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{m}P_{\mathcal{F}}(n)-\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{m}H_{\mathcal{F}}(n)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{m}P_{\mathcal{F}}(n)-H_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(m)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{e}_{0}(I)\binom{m+3}{3}-\widetilde{e}_{1}(I)\binom{m+2}{2}+\widetilde{e}_{2}(I)\binom{m+1}{1}-P_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(m)

(9)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{e}_{3}(I).

Note that
R
is a two-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay ring, by
[
2
, Lemma 4.7]
, we have

\lambda(\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{R_{+}}}(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{F}))_{n})\leq\lambda(\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{R_{+}}}(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{F}))_{n-1})\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Now in equation (
8
), we substitute
n=-1
to get

(10)

\lambda(\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{R_{+}}}(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{F}))_{0})=\widetilde{e}_{2}(I)=e_{2}(I).

Therefore, combining equations (
9
) and (
10
), we get

(11)

\widetilde{e}_{3}(I)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{m}\lambda(\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{R_{+}}}(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{F}))_{n+1})\leq\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{a_{2}(\mathcal{R(\mathcal{F})})-1}\lambda(\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathcal{R_{+}}}(\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{F}))_{0})=a_{2}(\mathcal{R(\mathcal{F})})e_{2}(I),

where
a_{2}(\mathcal{R(\mathcal{F})})\leq a_{2}(\widetilde{G}(I))
from the proof method of
[
41
, Theorem 3.1]
for the filtration

\mathcal{F}:=\{\widetilde{I_{{}^{n}}}\}_{n\geq 0}.
Furthermore, by
[
19
, Corollary 5.7(2)]
, we have
a_{2}(\widetilde{G}(I))=\widetilde{r}(I)-2
and by
[
18
, Theorem 2.2]

\widetilde{r}(I)-1\leq e_{2}(I).
Therefore, by equation (
11
), we have
\widetilde{e}_{3}(I)\leq(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I).

∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. For a minimal reduction
J
of
I
, if
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I),
then

\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I)-e_{3}(I).

Proof.

From Theorem
3.3
, we have
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+\widetilde{e}_{3}(I)-e_{3}(I).
Now, from Lemma
3.8
, we have
\widetilde{e}_{3}(I)\leq(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I).
Therefore,
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I)-e_{3}(I).

∎

Note that from the proof of Theorem
3.3
, we have
e_{3}(I)=\widetilde{e}_{3}(I)-\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\lambda\left(\widetilde{I^{n+1}}/I^{n+1}\right)\leq\widetilde{e}_{3}(I)

and from Lemma
3.8
, we have
\widetilde{e}_{3}(I)\leq(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I).
Therefore,
e_{3}(I)\leq(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I).
This implies that if
e_{2}(I)\leq 1
, then
e_{3}(I)\leq 0,
which is similar to a relation between
e_{2}(I)
and
e_{3}(I)
that was proved in
[
26
, Proposition 6.4]
and
[
16
, Proposition 2.6]
, in a three-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay ring.
In the next corollary, we observe that if
e_{3}(I)
attains its upper bound, then
\rho(I)
is always less than
r_{J}(I)
for every minimal reduction
J
of
I.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. If
e_{3}(I)=(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I),
then
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)
for every minimal reduction
J
of
I.

Proof.

Suppose for a minimal reduction
J
of
I
,
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I),
then by Proposition
3.9
, we have
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1,
a contradiction. Therefore, if
e_{3}(I)=(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I),
then
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)
for every minimal reduction
J
of
I.

∎

The following example illustrates the above propositions.

Example 3.11
.

[
30
, Example 4.6]

Let
R=\mathbb{Q}[[x,y]]
and
I=(x^{7},x^{6}y,x^{2}y^{5},y^{7})
. By CoCoA, the Hilbert series is

h_{I}(t)=\frac{35+6t+7t^{2}+2t^{3}-t^{5}}{(1-t)^{2}}.

Then
e(I)=49
,
e_{1}(I)=21
,
e_{2}(I)=3
and
e_{3}(I)=-8
. By
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
, we have
\widetilde{I}=(x^{7},x^{6}y,x^{4}y^{3},x^{2}y^{5},y^{7})
and
\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I)=4.
Here
J=(x^{7},x^{6}y+y^{7})
is a minimal reduction of
I
with
r_{J}(I)=3
. Using Macaulay 2, we get
x^{17}y^{4}\in(I^{4}:I)\subseteq\widetilde{I^{3}}
but
x^{17}y^{4}\notin I^{3}
, therefore,
\widetilde{I^{3}}\neq I^{3}
. Thus, from Proposition
3.7
, we get
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1-e_{3}(I)+\left(\frac{r_{J}(I)-1}{2}\right)(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e(I)-\lambda(R/I)+\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I))=10
. Further, from Proposition
3.9
, we get
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I)-e_{3}(I)=16
.

In the next corollary, we establish a bound on
\rho(I)
in terms of Hilbert coefficients using Rossi’s bound on the reduction number.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. For a minimal reduction
J
of
I
, if
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I),
then

\rho(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I)-e_{3}(I).

Proof.

From
[
28
, Corollary 1.5]
we have,
r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1.
Thus, the conclusion follows from Proposition
3.9
.
∎

In
[
21
, Question 3.14]
, Miranda-Neto and Queiroz proposed the following question: In a two-dimensional Buchsbaum ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
with positive depth, is it true that
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)+1,
for an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
I
with minimal reduction
J?

In the next proposition, we give a class of
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals for which
\rho(I)
is at most
r_{J}(I)+1.
This gives a partial answer to the above question for Cohen-Macaulay local rings.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal such that
e_{2}(I)=0
or
1
and
e_{3}(I)=-1.
Then for any minimal reduction
J\subseteq I
,

\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)+1.

Proof.

Let
e_{2}(I)=0
or
1.
From
[
32
, Theorem 2.5]
, we have
\widetilde{e_{2}}(I)=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}n\lambda\left(\widetilde{I^{n+1}}/J\widetilde{I^{n}}\right).
Also,
\widetilde{e_{2}}(I)=e_{2}(I)=0
or
1
, therefore,
\widetilde{I^{n+1}}=J\widetilde{I^{n}}
for all
n\geq 2.
Again from
[
32
, Theorem 2.5]
, we have
\widetilde{e_{3}}(I)=\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 2}\binom{n}{2}\lambda\left(\widetilde{I^{n+1}}/J\widetilde{I^{n}}\right),
thus
\widetilde{e_{3}}(I)=0,
this implies
e_{3}(I)=-\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda(\widetilde{I^{n}}/I^{n}).
Since
e_{3}(I)=-1
, therefore,
\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda(\widetilde{I^{n}}/I^{n})=1.
Note that
\widetilde{I^{n}}=I^{n}
for all
n\geq\rho(I)
and
\widetilde{I^{\rho(I)-1}}\neq I^{\rho(I)-1}
. Thus,
\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{n=\rho(I)-1}\lambda(\widetilde{I^{n}}/I^{n})=1,
this implies
\widetilde{I^{m}}=I^{m}
for all
1\leq m\leq\rho(I)-2.
Suppose
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I)-1,
then
\widetilde{I^{r_{J}(I)}}=I^{r_{J}(I)},
thus by
[
14
, Proposition 4.3]

\widetilde{I^{n}}=I^{n}
for all
n\geq r_{J}(I)
, which implies
\widetilde{I^{\rho(I)-1}}=I^{\rho(I)-1},
a contradiction. Therefore,
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)+1.

∎

The following example illustrates the above proposition.

Example 3.14
.

[
32
, Example 3.3]

Let
R=\mathbb{Q}[[x,y]]
and
I=(x^{4},x^{3}y,xy^{3},y^{4}).
By CoCoA, the Hilbert series is

h_{I}(t)=\frac{11+3t+3t^{2}-t^{3}}{(1-t)^{2}}.

Then
e_{2}(I)=0
and
e_{3}(I)=-1
. Note that
J=(x^{4},y^{4})
is a minimal reduction of
I
and
r_{J}(I)=2
. Therefore, from
[
14
, Proposition 4.3]
, we have
\widetilde{I^{2}}=I^{2}
, hence,
\rho(I)=2.
Thus, from Proposition
3.13
, we have
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)+1=3.

In the next proposition, we give another bound on
\rho(I)
without any assumptions on the Hilbert coefficients.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring, and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal with
0ptG(I^{t})>0
for some
t>1.
Let
J
be a minimal reduction of
I
such that
\widetilde{I^{r_{J}(I)}}\neq I^{r_{J}(I)}
. If
\rho(I)\equiv 0\mod t
then

\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+t
.
Furthermore, if
\rho(I)\equiv k\mod t
for
1\leq k\leq t-1,
then

\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)+k
.

Proof.

Suppose
\rho(I)=mt
for some
m>1.
Then by Remark
3.2
,
(m-1)t<r_{J}(I)<mt,
this implies
mt-t<r_{J}(I).
Thus,
\rho(I)<r_{J}(I)+t.
Therefore,
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+t
.

Now, suppose
\rho(I)=mt+k
for some
m\geq 1.
We claim that
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)+k.
Suppose
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I)-k=mt
, then
r_{J}(I)<mt<\rho(I)
. Therefore, by Remark
3.2
,
\widetilde{I^{mt}}\neq I^{mt},
but
0ptG(I^{t})>0
which implies
\widetilde{I^{mt}}=I^{mt}
for all
m
. This is a contradiction. Therefore,

\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)+k
.
∎

As a immediate corollary we get

Corollary 3.16
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring, and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Let
J
be a minimal reduction of
I.
Suppose
0ptG(I^{2})>0
and
r_{J}(I)
is odd. If
\widetilde{I^{r_{J}(I)}}\neq I^{r_{J}(I)}
then

\rho(I)=r_{J}(I)+1.

Proof.

Note that by Remark
3.2
,
\rho(I)=2m
for some
m>1.
By Proposition
3.15
, for
t=2,

\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)+1.
Since
\widetilde{I^{r_{J}(I)}}\neq I^{r_{J}(I)},
therefore,
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)+1,
which implies
\rho(I)=r_{J}(I)+1.

∎

In
[
27
]
, the authors proved that if

is Cohen-Macaulay with

, then
\operatorname{type}(R)\geq e(\mathfrak{m})-\mu(\mathfrak{m})+d-1
where
\operatorname{type}(R)=\dim_{k}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{d}(k,R)
and

Further, if

and

, then

is Cohen-Macaulay. In the next corollary, we consider the next boundary case i.e.,

Corollary 3.17
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring with
e_{2}(\mathfrak{m})=e_{1}(\mathfrak{m})-e(\mathfrak{m})+1\neq 0
and
\operatorname{type}R=e(\mathfrak{m})-\mu(\mathfrak{m})+2.
Suppose
J
is a minimal reduction of
\mathfrak{m}
such that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}^{r_{J}(I)}}\neq\mathfrak{m}^{r_{J}(I)},
then
\rho(\mathfrak{m})\leq r_{J}(\mathfrak{m})+3.

Proof.

If
0ptG(\mathfrak{m})\geq 1,
then
\rho(\mathfrak{m})=1\leq r_{J}(\mathfrak{m})+3.
Suppose
0ptG(\mathfrak{m})=0.
By
[
27
, Theorem 4.1]
,
\widetilde{\mathfrak{m}^{i}}=\mathfrak{m}^{i}
for all
i\geq 3,
which implies
0ptG(\mathfrak{m}^{3})>0.
Therefore, by Proposition
3.15
, if
\rho(I)\equiv 0\mod 3
then

\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)+2<r_{J}(I)+3
and
if
\rho(I)\equiv k\mod t
for

then

\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)+3
.
∎

We conclude this section with the following proposition, in which we examine the relationship between
r_{J}(I)
and
\rho(I)
, focusing specifically on the cases where
\rho(I)
takes small values. This proposition highlights the condition under which
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I).

Proposition 3.18
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring,
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal with minimal reduction
J
. Then the following holds:

(i)

If
\rho(I)=2,
then
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I).

(ii)

If
\rho(I)=3
and
I
is Ratliff-Rush closed, then
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I).

Proof.

(i)

Suppose
\rho(I)=2,
then from
[
29
, Remark 1.6]
, we have
0ptG(I)=0
. Let
x\in J
be a superficial element for
I.
If
\rho(I/(x))=1,
then again by
[
29
, Remark 1.6]
,
0ptG(I/(x))>0
. Hence, by Sally’s Descent,
0ptG(I)>1
, which is a contradiction. Therefore,
\rho(I)\leq\rho(I/(x))\leq r_{J/(x)}(I/(x))\leq r_{J}(I),
where the second inequality follows from
[
29
, Proposition 4.2
(i)
]
.

(ii)

Suppose
\rho(I)=3
. Let
x\in J
be a superficial element for
I,
then from the similar arguments as above, we have
\rho(I/(x))>1.
Note that
\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}\subseteq\frac{\widetilde{I^{n}}+(x)}{(x)}\subseteq\widetilde{\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}}
for all
n\geq 1.
If possible let
\rho(I/(x))=2,
then
\widetilde{\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}}=\frac{I^{n}+(x)}{(x)}=\frac{\widetilde{I^{n}}+(x)}{(x)}
for all
n\geq 2.
At
n=2,

\widetilde{I^{n}}\neq I^{n}
. Therefore, there exist
y\in\widetilde{I^{2}}\backslash I^{2},
such that
\bar{y}\in\frac{\widetilde{I^{2}}+(x)}{(x)}=\frac{I^{2}+(x)}{(x)}.
This implies
y\in I^{2}+(x),
thus there exists
z\in I^{2}
such that
y=z+rx
for some
r\in R.
Note that
y-z=rx\in\widetilde{I^{2}},
this implies
r\in(\widetilde{I^{2}}:x)=\widetilde{I}
. Since
I
is Ratliff-Rush closed, therefore
r\in I
which implies
rx\in I^{2}.
Thus,
y\in I^{2},
which is a contradiction. Therefore,
\rho(I/(x))>2.
Thus,
\rho(I)\leq\rho(I/(x))\leq r_{J/(x)}(I/(x))\leq r_{J}(I),
where the second inequality follows from
[
29
, Proposition 4.2
(i)
]
. ∎

4.
Bounds on the Stability Index for Buchsbaum Rings

We begin this section by extending
[
14
, Proposition 4.3]
to two-dimensional Buchsbaum rings.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
R
be a two dimensional Buchsbaum ring with
0ptR>0
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Let
x,y
be a superficial sequence for
I
such that
J=(x,y)
is a minimal reduction of
I.
Set
r_{J}(I)=r.
If
\widetilde{I^{m}}=I^{m}
for some
m\geq r,
then
\widetilde{I^{n}}=I^{n}
for all
n\geq m.
In particular,
\rho(I)\leq m.

Proof.

We will first show that
(I^{n+1}:x)=I^{n}+y(I^{n}:x)
for all
n\geq r+1.
Let
f
be an arbitrary element in
(I^{n+1}:x)
for any
n\geq r+1,
this implies that
xf\in I^{n+1}.
Since
I^{n+1}=(x,y)I^{n},
thus there are elements
p,q\in I^{n}
such that
xf=xp+yq,
this implies
q\in[(x):y].
From this, it follows that
q\in I^{n}\cap[(x):y].
Now from the proof of
[
30
, Theorem 2.4]
, we have

I^{n}\cap[(x):y]=I^{n}\cap(x)=x(I^{n}:x).

Hence,
q=xq^{\prime}
for some
q^{\prime}\in(I^{n}:x).
Thus,
xf=xp+xyq^{\prime}
. Since
x
is a non-zero divisor, we have
f=p+yq^{\prime}\in I^{n}+y(I^{n}:x).
Therefore,
(I^{n}:x)\subseteq I^{n}+y(I^{n}:x).
The reverse inclusion is obvious, thus we have
(I^{n+1}:x)=I^{n}+y(I^{n}:x)
for all
n\geq r+1.

Now it is enough to prove that if
\widetilde{I^{n}}=I^{n}
for
n=m+1
, then the conclusion follows.
By
[
32
, Lemma 3.1 (5)]
, we get the first equality in the chain

I^{m}\subseteq(I^{m+1}:x)\subseteq(\widetilde{I^{m+1}}:x)=\widetilde{I^{m}}=I^{m}.

The last equality follows from the hypothesis. Hence equalities hold from left to right in the last chain; in particular,
(I^{m+1}:x)=I^{m}.
Next as
m+1\geq r+1,
by the first paragraph of the proof

(I^{m+2}:x)=I^{m+1}+y(I^{m+1}:x)=I^{m+1}+yI^{m}=I^{m+1}.

Arguing similarly, we have
(I^{n+1}:x)=I^{n}
for all
n\geq m.
Thanks to Definition
2.5
, we deduce
\rho(I)=\rho_{x}(I)\leq m,
as desired.
∎

The formula in Proposition
3.9
,
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I)-e_{3}(I)
raises an intriguing question that if
R
is not Cohen-Macaulay but still has dimension two, what possible bounds can be established? In this section, we explore this question in the context of Buchsbaum rings. Note that in Theorem
3.3
, for two-dimensional Buchsbaum rings with positive depth, the same arguments follow from Lemma
4.1
, which gives
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1-e_{3}(I)+\widetilde{e}_{3}(I).
But the bound on
\widetilde{e}_{3}(I)
in Lemma
3.8
does not hold for the non Cohen-Macaulay rings. Therefore, we need a change of rings to understand whether a similar bound on
\widetilde{e}_{3}(I)
exists or not.

For a Buchsbaum ring
R,
we have a finite integral extension into a ring
\mathbb{S}
, called the
S_{2}
-fication of
R,
We briefly recall the
S_{2}
-fication of generalized Cohen-Macaulay rings from the literature, for further reading and for the elementary properties of the
S_{2}
-fication, we redirect the readers to
[
1
, Theorem 3.2]
and
[
10
, Proposition 4.1, Theorem 4.2]
.

S_{2}
-fication of generalized Cohen-Macaulay rings:
[
10
, Section 4]
If
R
is a Noetherian ring with the total ring of fractions
\mathbb{K}
, the smallest finite extension
\phi:R\longrightarrow\mathbb{S}\subseteq\mathbb{K}
with the
S_{2}
property is called the
S_{2}
-fication of
R
. The map
\phi
or
\mathbb{S}
is referred as the
S_{2}
-fication of
R.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two-dimensional Buchsbaum ring with
0ptR>0
and
\mathbb{S}
be the
S_{2}
-fication of
R
. We may assume
R
to be complete (see
[
37
, Chapter 1, Lemma 1.13]
). From
[
10
, Proposition 4.1(2)]
, we get that
\mathbb{S}
is a semi-local ring, therefore, let
\{\mathfrak{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{M}_{l}\}
be the maximal ideals of
\mathbb{S},
and let
\mathbb{S}_{1},\ldots,\mathbb{S}_{l}
denote the corresponding localizations. For each
i=1,2,\ldots,l,
let
f_{i}
be the relative degree
[\mathbb{S}/\mathfrak{M}_{i}:R/\mathfrak{m}].
If
W
is an
R
-module of finite length which is also an
\mathbb{S}
-module, then from the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 4.3]
, we have that

(12)

\lambda_{R}(W)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}\lambda_{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(W\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}.

The following lemma gives the relationship between the Hilbert coefficients of the ideal
I\mathbb{S}
and the ideals
I\mathbb{S}_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,l.
While the proof of this lemma is analogous to the arguments in
[
10
, Theorem 4.3]
, we include the steps for completeness.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two-dimensional Buchsbaum ring with
0ptR>0
and
\mathbb{S}
be the
S_{2}
-fication of
R
. Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Then

(i)

e^{R}(I\mathbb{S})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}e^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}

(ii)

e_{1}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}e_{1}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}

(iii)

e_{2}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}

(iv)

e_{3}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}e_{3}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}.

Proof.

Applying formula (
12
) to the modules
I^{n}\mathbb{S}/I^{n+1}\mathbb{S}
and
\mathbb{S}/I^{n+1}\mathbb{S}
, we get
\lambda_{R}\left(I^{n}\mathbb{S}/I^{n+1}\mathbb{S}\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}\lambda_{\mathbb{S}_{i}}\left(I^{n}\mathbb{S}_{i}/I^{n+1}\mathbb{S}_{i}\right)f_{i}
and
\lambda_{R}\left(\mathbb{S}/I^{n+1}\mathbb{S}\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}\lambda_{\mathbb{S}_{i}}\left(\mathbb{S}_{i}/I^{n+1}\mathbb{S}_{i}\right)f_{i}
. Then for sufficiently large
n
, we have the following equations respectively:

(13)

e^{R}(I\mathbb{S})(n+1)-e_{1}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}e^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}(n+1)-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}e_{1}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i},

(14)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{2}(-1)^{j}e_{j}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})\binom{n+2-j}{2-j}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{2}(-1)^{j}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}e_{j}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}\right)\binom{n+2-j}{2-j}.

On comparing the coefficient of
n
in equation (
13
), we get
e^{R}(I\mathbb{S})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}e^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}
and

e_{1}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}e_{1}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}.

For
n=0
in equation (
14
), we get
e^{R}(I\mathbb{S})-e_{1}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})+e_{2}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}e^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}e_{1}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}
. Therefore, we have
e_{2}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}.

Now consider
\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\lambda_{R}\left(\mathbb{S}/I^{j+1}\mathbb{S}\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}\lambda_{\mathbb{S}_{i}}\left(\mathbb{S}_{i}/I^{j+1}\mathbb{S}_{i}\right)f_{i},
then for sufficiently large
n
, we get that

(15)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{3}(-1)^{j}e_{j}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})\binom{n+3-j}{3-j}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{3}(-1)^{j}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}e_{j}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}\right)\binom{n+3-j}{3-j}.

For
n=0
in equation (
15
) and substituting values of
e^{R}(I\mathbb{S})
,
e_{1}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})
, and
e_{2}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})
from above, we get
e_{3}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}e_{3}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}.

∎

Remark 4.3
.

(i)

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two-dimensional Buchsbaum ring with
0ptR>0
and
\mathbb{S}
is the
S_{2}
-fication of
R
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal with minimal reduction

. Then
from the proof of

, we have

r_{J\mathbb{S}}(I\mathbb{S})=\max\{r_{J\mathbb{S}_{1}}(I\mathbb{S}_{1}),\ldots,r_{J\mathbb{S}_{l}}(I\mathbb{S}_{l})\}.

(ii)

Note that
\rho(I\mathbb{S})=\max\{\rho(I\mathbb{S}_{i}),\ldots,\rho(I\mathbb{S}_{l})\}.
To see this, consider the localization maps
\phi_{i}:\mathbb{S}\longrightarrow\mathbb{S}_{i},
for every
i=1,2,\ldots,l.
Then clearly
\rho(I\mathbb{S}_{i})\leq\rho(I\mathbb{S})
for all

this implies that
\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq l}(\rho(I\mathbb{S}_{i}))\leq\rho(I\mathbb{S}).
Now let
m=\max\{\rho(I\mathbb{S}_{i}),\ldots,\rho(I\mathbb{S}_{l})\},
and let
x\mathbb{S}_{i}
be the image of
x
is
\mathbb{S}_{i}
for every

where

is a superficial element for

,
(I^{n+1}\mathbb{S}_{i}:x\mathbb{S}_{i})=I^{n}\mathbb{S}_{i}
for all

and for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{M}_{i}.
Therefore, from the local properties of localization, we have
(I^{n+1}\mathbb{S}:x\mathbb{S})=I^{n}\mathbb{S}
for every

this implies
\rho(I\mathbb{S})\leq m.
Thus,
\rho(I\mathbb{S})=\max\{\rho(I\mathbb{S}_{i}),\ldots,\rho(I\mathbb{S}_{l})\}
.

In the following lemma, we establish the relationships between
e_{i}(I)
and
e_{i}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})
for

under the assumption that

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Buchsbaum ring with
0ptR>0
and
\mathbb{S}
be the
S_{2}
-fication of
R.
Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal with minimal reduction
J
and assume that
I=I\mathbb{S}
. Then we have the following:

(i)

e_{i}(I)=e_{i}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})
for
0\leq i\leq 1,

(ii)

e_{2}(I)=e_{2}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})+e_{1}(J),

(iii)

e_{3}(I)=e_{3}^{R}(I\mathbb{S}).

Proof.

From the proof of
[
10
, Proposition 4.1]
, consider the short exact sequence

(16)

0\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow\mathbb{S}\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow 0.

Note that
D\cong\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
is a module of finite length. Tensoring (
16
) with

we get

0\longrightarrow\frac{I^{n+1}\mathbb{S}\cap R}{I^{n+1}}\longrightarrow R/I^{n+1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{S}/I^{n+1}\mathbb{S}\longrightarrow D/I^{n+1}D\longrightarrow 0.

Since
I\mathbb{S}=I
, we have
I^{n}\mathbb{S}=\underbrace{(I\mathbb{S})\cdots(I\mathbb{S})}_{n\text{-times}}=\underbrace{I\cdots I}_{n\text{-times}}=I^{n}
for all
n.
Therefore,
\dfrac{I^{n+1}\mathbb{S}\cap R}{I^{n+1}}=0
for all
n.
Also, from
[
37
, Chapter 2, Proposition 2.1
(iii)
]
,
\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=0,
thus,
I^{n+1}\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=0
for all
n,
which implies
I^{n+1}D=0
for all
n.
Therefore, we get the following short exact sequence:

(17)

0\longrightarrow R/I^{n+1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{S}/I^{n+1}\mathbb{S}\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow 0.

From the exact sequence (
17
), we have
\lambda(R/I^{n+1})+\lambda_{R}(D)=\lambda_{R}(\mathbb{S}/I^{n+1}\mathbb{S}).
Then for sufficiently large
n,
we have the following equation:

(18)

e_{0}(I)\binom{n+2}{2}-e_{1}(I)(n+1)+e_{2}(I)+\lambda_{R}(D)=e_{0}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})\binom{n+2}{2}-e_{1}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})(n+1)+e_{2}^{R}(I\mathbb{S}).

On comparing the coefficients in (
18
), we get
e_{0}(I)=e_{0}^{R}(I\mathbb{S}),

e_{1}(I)=e_{1}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})
and

e_{2}(I)+\lambda_{R}(D)=e_{2}^{R}(I\mathbb{S}).
Furthermore,
\lambda_{R}(D)=\lambda\left(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\right)=-e_{1}(J)
because
R
is Buchsbaum
[
37
,
Chapter 1, Propositions 2.6 and 2.7]
. Thus,
e_{2}(I)=e_{2}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})+e_{1}(J).

Now consider
\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\left[\lambda(R/I^{j+1})+\lambda_{R}(D)\right]=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\lambda_{R}\left(\mathbb{S}/I^{j+1}\mathbb{S}\right),
then for sufficiently large
n
, we get that

(19)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{3}(-1)^{j}e_{j}(I)\binom{n+3-j}{3-j}+(n+1)\lambda_{R}(D)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{3}(-1)^{j}e_{j}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})\binom{n+3-j}{3-j}.

Substituting values of
e_{0}(I)
,
e_{1}(I)
and
e_{2}(I)
from
(\ref{eo})
and
(\ref{e1})
, we get
e_{3}(I)=e_{3}^{R}(I\mathbb{S}).

∎

In the following theorem, we give a bound on
\rho(I)
for two-dimensional Buchsbaum rings.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Buchsbaum ring with
0ptR>0
and
\mathbb{S}
be the
S_{2}
-fication of
R
. Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal with minimal reduction
J
and assume that
I=I\mathbb{S}
. If
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I),
then
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(J)-1)(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(J))-e_{3}(I)
.

Proof.

We first note that for any
n\geq\rho(I\mathbb{S}),
we have
\widetilde{I^{n}}\subseteq\widetilde{I^{n}}\mathbb{S}\cap R\subseteq\widetilde{I^{n}\mathbb{S}}\cap R=I^{n}\mathbb{S}\cap R=I^{n}.
This implies
\widetilde{I^{n}}=I^{n}
for all
n\geq\rho(I\mathbb{S}),
and therefore,
\rho(I)\leq\rho(I\mathbb{S}).
Without the loss of generality, let
\rho(I\mathbb{S})=\rho(I\mathbb{S}_{1}).
It is not hard to see that
r_{J\mathbb{S}_{1}}(I\mathbb{S}_{1})\leq r_{J\mathbb{S}}(I\mathbb{S})
and
r_{J\mathbb{S}}(I\mathbb{S})\leq r_{J}(I)
. Thus, we get
r_{J\mathbb{S}_{1}}(I\mathbb{S}_{1})<\rho(I\mathbb{S}_{1}).
Therefore, from Proposition
3.9
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(I\mathbb{S}_{1})

\displaystyle\leq r_{J\mathbb{S}_{1}}(I\mathbb{S}_{1})-1+(e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{1}}(I\mathbb{S}_{1})-1)e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{1}}(I\mathbb{S}_{1})-e_{3}^{\mathbb{S}_{1}}(I\mathbb{S}_{1})

(20)

\displaystyle\leq r_{J\mathbb{S}_{1}}(I\mathbb{S}_{1})-1+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left[(e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})-1)e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})-e_{3}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})\right]

\displaystyle\leq r_{J\mathbb{S}_{1}}(I\mathbb{S}_{1})-1+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left[\left((e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})-1)e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})-e_{3}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})\right)f_{i}\right]

\displaystyle=r_{J\mathbb{S}_{1}}(I\mathbb{S}_{1})-1+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left[\left(e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})\right)^{2}f_{i}-e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}-e_{3}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}\right]

\displaystyle\leq r_{J\mathbb{S}_{1}}(I\mathbb{S}_{1})-1+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left[(e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i})^{2}-e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}-e_{3}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}\right]

(21)

\displaystyle\leq r_{J\mathbb{S}_{1}}(I\mathbb{S}_{1})-1+\displaystyle\left[\sum_{i=1}^{l}(e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i})\right]^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left[e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}+e_{3}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})f_{i}\right]

\displaystyle\leq r_{J\mathbb{S}}(I\mathbb{S})-1+(e_{2}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})-1)e_{2}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})-e_{3}^{R}(I\mathbb{S})\quad\text{ (from Lemma \ref{bintere} and Remark \ref{remark1} (i))}

\displaystyle\leq r_{J}(I)-1+(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(J)-1)(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(J))-e_{3}(I)\quad\text{ (from Lemma \ref{coefflemma}}).

Note that the inequality in (
20
) holds by the proof of Theorem
3.3
and Lemma
3.8
,
e_{3}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})\leq\widetilde{e}_{3}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})\leq e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})(e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})-1)
for all
1\leq i\leq l.
Further, since
\mathbb{S}_{i}
is Cohen- Macaulay of dimension 2 for all
1\leq i\leq l,
thus the inequality in (
21
) holds since
e_{2}^{\mathbb{S}_{i}}(I\mathbb{S}_{i})\geq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq l
from
[
32
, Proposition 3.1]
.
∎

As an immediate consequence, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Buchsbaum ring with positive depth. For any minimal reduction
J
of
\mathfrak{m}
, if
r_{J}(\mathfrak{m})<\rho(\mathfrak{m}),
then

\rho(\mathfrak{m})\leq r_{J}(\mathfrak{m})-1+(e_{2}(\mathfrak{m})-e_{1}(J)-1)(e_{2}(\mathfrak{m})-e_{1}(J))-e_{3}(\mathfrak{m}).

Proof.

Using
[
10
, Proposition 4.1]
and from
[
37
, Chapter 2, Proposition 2.1
(iii)
]
,
\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{H}^{1}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=0
, we have that
\mathfrak{m}\mathbb{S}=\mathfrak{m}.
Therefore, the conclusion follows directly from Theorem
4.5
.
∎

.

[

5.
Bounds on the Castelnuovo-Mumford Regularity

In this section, we give applications of the theorems of the last sections by giving bounds on the Castelnuovo-Mumford regularity. In
[
30
]
, Rossi, Trung and Trung established a relationship between the regularity of the associated graded ring of
I
and the seemingly unrelated Ratliff-Rush closure of an ideal. They characterized
\operatorname{reg}\mathcal{R}(I)
in terms of
r_{J}(I)
and
\rho(I)
for an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
I
in a two-dimensional Buchsbaum ring
[
30
, Theorem 2.4]
. Note that Ooishi
[
24
, Lemma 4.8]
showed that there is always an equality

\operatorname{reg}G(I)=\mathrm{reg}\,\mathcal{R}(I)
;
see also
[
41
, Corollary 3.3]
.
In the following proposition, drawing inspirations from their result, we characterize
\operatorname{reg}G(I)
in terms of
\rho(I),\ldots,\rho\left(I/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2})\right),
and
r_{J}(I)
, where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2}\in I
is a superficial sequence for
I.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 2,
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal with minimal reduction
J=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}),
where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
is a superficial sequence for
I,
then

\operatorname{reg}G(I)=\max\{\rho(I),\rho(I/(x_{1})),\ldots,\rho(I/(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-2})),r_{J}(I)\}.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
d.
For
d=2,
the conclusion follows from
[
30
, Theorem 2.4]
, as
\operatorname{reg}G(I)=\max\{r_{J}(I),\rho(I)\}.
We assume the proposition for
d-1
and prove for
d.
Set
\bar{R}=R/(x_{1})
and
\bar{I}=I/(x_{1})
, then
\bar{R}
is a
d-1
dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring. By the induction hypothesis, we have

\operatorname{reg}G(\bar{I})=\max\{r_{\bar{J}}(\bar{I}),\rho(\bar{I}),\rho\left(\bar{I}/(x_{2})\right),\ldots,\rho\left(\bar{I}/(x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-2})\right)\}=\max\{r_{\bar{J}}(\bar{I}),\rho(I/(x_{1})),\rho\left(I/(x_{1},x_{2})\right),\ldots,\rho\left(I/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2})\right)\}
. This implies

a_{i}(G(\bar{I}))+i\leq\max\{r_{\bar{J}}(\bar{I}),\rho(I/(x_{1})),\ldots,\rho\left(I/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2})\right)\}\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,d-1.

We claim that
a_{i+1}(G(I))+(i+1)\leq a_{i}(G(\bar{I}))+i
for all
i=1,\ldots,d-1.

Note that

\frac{\bar{I}^{n}}{\bar{I}^{n+1}}=\frac{I^{n}+(x_{1})}{I^{n+1}+(x_{1})}\cong\frac{I^{n}}{I^{n}\cap(I^{n+1}+(x_{1}))}=\frac{I^{n}}{((x_{1})\cap I^{n})+I^{n+1}}.

So letting
W=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x_{1}(I^{n}:x_{1})+I^{n+1}}{x_{1}I^{n-1}+I^{n+1}}
, there is an exact sequence

(22)

0\longrightarrow W\longrightarrow\frac{G(I)}{x_{1}^{*}G(I)}=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{I^{n}}{x_{1}I^{n-1}+I^{n+1}}\longrightarrow G(\bar{I})\longrightarrow 0.

Since
x_{1}
is superficial for
I
, we get
W_{n}=0
for sufficiently large
n.
As
I
is
\mathfrak{m}
-primary, we deduce that
\lambda(W)<\infty.
Moreover,
x_{1}^{*}=x_{1}+I^{2}
is
G(I)
-filter-regular. Taking the long exact sequence of cohomology associated to (
22
), we deduce

\mathcal{H}^{i}(G(\bar{I}))\cong\mathcal{H}^{i}(G(I)/x_{1}^{*}G(I))\text{ for all }i\geq 1.

Invoking the proof of
[
40
, Lemma 2.3]
, and the fact that
x_{1}^{*}
is
G(I)
-filter-regular, we deduce

a_{i+1}(G(I))\leq a_{i}(G(I)/x_{1}^{*}G(I))-1=a_{i}(G(\bar{I}))-1\text{ for all i }\geq 1,

as claimed.
Therefore, for all
i=1,\ldots,d-1,
we get

\displaystyle a_{i+1}(G(I))+(i+1)

\displaystyle\leq a_{i}(G(\bar{I}))+i

\displaystyle\leq\max\{r_{\bar{J}}(\bar{I}),\rho(I/(x_{1})),\rho\left(I/(x_{1},x_{2})\right),\ldots,\rho\left(I/(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-2})\right)\}

(23)

\displaystyle\leq\max\{r_{J}(I),\rho(I/(x_{1})),\rho\left(I/(x_{1},x_{2})\right),\ldots,\rho\left(I/(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-2})\right)\}.

From
[
25
, Proposition 4.7(2)]
, we have
\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(L^{\bar{I}}(\bar{R})\right)=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\widetilde{\overline{I^{n+1}}}/\overline{I^{n+1}}
. Thus,
\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(L^{\bar{I}}(\bar{R})\right)_{n}=0
for all
n\geq\rho\left(I/(x_{1})\right)-1,
and from
[
25
, Theorem 6.4(1)]
, we have
\mathcal{H}^{1}\left(L^{I}(R)\right)_{n}=0
for sufficiently large
n.
Therefore, from the long exact sequence (
5
) of local cohomology modules:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(x,R)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(L^{I}(R)\right)(-1)\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(L^{I}(R)\right)\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(L^{\bar{I}}(\bar{R})\right)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{1}\left(L^{I}(R)\right)(-1)\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{1}\left(L^{I}(R)\right)\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{1}\left(L^{\bar{I}}(\bar{R})\right)\ldots

the map
f_{n}:\mathcal{H}^{1}\left(L^{I}(R)\right)_{n-1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{1}\left(L^{I}(R)\right)_{n}
is injective for all
n\geq\rho\left(I/(x_{1})\right)-2.
Thus,
\mathcal{H}^{1}\left(L^{I}(R)\right)_{n}=0
for all
n\geq\rho\left(I/(x_{1})\right)-2.
From
[
25
, Proposition 4.7(2)]
, we have
\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(L^{I}(R)\right)=\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\widetilde{I^{n+1}}/I^{n+1}
, thus
\mathcal{H}^{0}\left(L^{I}(R)\right)_{n}=0
for all
n\geq\rho(I)-1.
Therefore, from the long exact sequence (
3
), we have
\mathcal{H}^{1}\left(G(I)\right)_{n}=0
for all
n\geq\max\{\rho(I),\rho\left(I/(x_{1})\right)-2\},
This implies

(24)

a_{1}(G(I))+1\leq\max\{\rho(I),\rho\left(I/(x_{1})\right)\}.

From inequalities (
23
) and (
24
), we get

a_{i}(G(I))+i\leq\max\{r_{J}(I),\rho(I),\rho(I/(x_{1})),\ldots,\rho\left(I/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2})\right)\}\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,d.

Therefore,

\operatorname{reg}G(I)\leq\max\{r_{J}(I),\rho(I),\ldots,\rho\left(I/(x_{1}\ldots,x_{d-2})\right)\}.

Set
R_{i}=R/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})
for
i=0,\ldots,d-2,
so that
R_{0}=R.
From
[
30
, Lemma 1.2 and Theorem 1.3]
, we have
r_{J}(I)\leq\operatorname{reg}G(I)
, and from
[
30
, Proposition 2.1(ii)]
,
\rho(I/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}))\leq\operatorname{reg}G(I/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}))
for all
i=0,\ldots,d-2.

Using the exact sequence (
22
) from above, the fact that
\lambda(W)<\infty,

x_{1}^{*}
is
G(I)
-filter-regular, we deduce that

\operatorname{reg}G(\bar{I})=\operatorname{reg}G(I/(x_{1}))\leq\operatorname{reg}\frac{G(I)}{x_{1}^{*}G(I)}\leq\operatorname{reg}G(I).

By induction, we get
\operatorname{reg}G(I/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2}))\leq\cdots\leq\operatorname{reg}G(I),
hence
\operatorname{reg}G(I)\geq\rho(I/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}))
for all
i=0,\ldots,d-2.

Thus,

\operatorname{reg}G(I)\geq\max\{r_{J}(I),\rho(I),\ldots,\rho\left(I/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2})\right)\}

Therefore, we get
\operatorname{reg}G(I)=\max\{\rho(I),\rho(I/(x_{1})),\ldots,\rho(I/(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d-2})),r_{J}(I)\}.

∎

The formula for
\operatorname{reg}G(I)
in Theorem
5.1
, provides an effective tool for the computation of
\operatorname{reg}G(I)
especially under the assumption that the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well mod a superficial sequence. As an immediate consequence, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 2,
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal with minimal reduction
J=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}),
where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
is a superficial sequence for
I.
Suppose the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
behaves well mod a superficial sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2},
then

\operatorname{reg}G(I)=\max\{r_{J}(I),\rho(I)\}.

Further, if
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I),
then
\operatorname{reg}G(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+(-1)^{d+1}(e_{d+1}(I)-\widetilde{e}_{d+1}(I)).

Proof.

Since the Ratliff-Rush filtration behaves well mod a superficial sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2}
for
I
, thus, from
[
17
, Lemma 4.9]
, we have
\rho(I)\geq\rho\left(I/(x_{1})\right)\geq\ldots\geq\rho\left(I/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2})\right).
Therefore, from Theorem
5.1
, we get that
\operatorname{reg}G(I)=\max\{r_{J}(I),\rho(I)\}.

Next, let
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I),
then from Theorem
3.3
, we have
\operatorname{reg}G(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+(-1)^{d+1}(e_{d+1}(I)-\widetilde{e}_{d+1}(I)).

∎

Miranda-Neto and Queiroz proposed
[
21
, Question 4.4]
that in a Cohen-Macaulay ring of dimension
d\geq 2,
for an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
I
with minimal reduction
J\subseteq I
, if
\widetilde{I^{r_{J}(I)}}=I^{r_{J}(I)}
, then is
\operatorname{reg}G(I)=r_{J}(I)?
The following corollary gives partial answer to this question under the hypothesis that the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
behaves well mod a superficial sequence.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 2,

I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
J=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}),
where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
is a superficial sequence for
I
. Suppose that the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
behaves well mod a superficial sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2}
and
\widetilde{I^{r_{J}(I)}}=I^{r_{J}(I)}
. Then
\operatorname{reg}G(I)=r_{J}(I).

Proof.

As the Ratliff-Rush filtration behaves well mod a superficial sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2}
for
I
, thus, from Corollary
5.2
, we have
\operatorname{reg}G(I)=\max\{r_{J}(I),\rho(I)\}.

Since
\widetilde{I^{r_{J}(I)}}=I^{r_{J}(I)}
, therefore, from Proposition
3.1
, we have
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I),
which implies
\operatorname{reg}G(I)=r_{J}(I).

∎

The following example illustrates the above corollary.

Example 5.4
.

[
32
, Example 3.1]

Let
R=\mathbb{Q}[[x,y,z]]
and
I=(x^{2}-y^{2},y^{2}-z^{2},xy,yz,zx).
By CoCoA, the Hilbert series is

h_{I}(t)=\frac{5+6t^{2}-4t^{3}+t^{4}}{(1-t)^{3}}.

Then
e_{2}(I)=e_{3}(I)=0.
By
[
26
, Theorems 4.5 and 6.2]
, the Ratliff-Rush filtration with respect to
I
behaves well mod a superficial element. Note that
J=(x^{2}-y^{2},x^{2}-z^{2},xy+yz+zx)
is a minimal reduction of
I
with
r_{J}(I)=2
such that
\widetilde{I^{2}}=I^{2}.
Therefore, by Corollary
5.3
,
\operatorname{reg}G(I)=r_{J}(I)=2.

We now give upper bounds on the regularity of the associated graded ring in terms of higher Hilbert coefficients.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring, and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Let
J\subseteq I
be a minimal reduction of
I.
Then

\operatorname{reg}G(I)\leq\begin{cases}e_{1}(I)-e(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1&,\text{if }\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I)\\
r_{J}(I)-1-e_{3}(I)+\left(\frac{r_{J}(I)-1}{2}\right)(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e(I)-\lambda(R/I)+\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I))&,\text{if }r_{J}(I)<\rho(I).\end{cases}

Proof.

From
[
30
, Theorem 2.4]
,
\operatorname{reg}G(I)=\max\{r_{J}(I),\rho(I)\}.
If
\rho(I)\leq r_{J}(I),
then by
[
28
, Corollary 1.5]
, we get
\operatorname{reg}G(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1.
Further, if
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I),
then by Proposition
3.7
, we have
\operatorname{reg}G(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1-e_{3}(I)+\left(\frac{r_{J}(I)-1}{2}\right)(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e(I)-\lambda(R/I)+\lambda(\widetilde{I}/I)).

∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring, and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. For any minimal reduction
J\subseteq I
, if
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I)
then

\operatorname{reg}G(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I)-e_{3}(I).

Proof.

Since
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I),
therefore, from
[
30
, Theorem 2.4]
,
\operatorname{reg}G(I)=\rho(I).
Again from Proposition
3.9
, we have
\operatorname{reg}G(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1+(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I)-e_{3}(I).

∎

From
[
28
, Corollary 1.5]
we have,
r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1.
Thus, if
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I),
then
\operatorname{reg}G(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+(e_{2}(I)-1)e_{2}(I)-e_{3}(I).
As an immediate corollary, we show that in dimension two, for certain values of
e_{2}(I)
, we get linear bounds on
\operatorname{reg}G(I)
in terms of higher Hilbert coefficients.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a two dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring, and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. For any minimal reduction
J\subseteq I
, if
r_{J}(I)<\rho(I)
the following statements hold.

(i)

If
e_{2}(I)=0
or
1
, then
\operatorname{reg}G(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)-e_{3}(I).

(ii)

If
e_{2}(I)=2
, then
\operatorname{reg}G(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)-e_{3}(I)+2.

Proof.

(i)

If
e_{2}(I)=0
or
1
, then from Proposition
5.6
, we have
\operatorname{reg}G(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-1-e_{3}(I).
Further, from
[
28
, Corollary 1.5]
we have,
r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1.

Therefore,
\operatorname{reg}G(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)-e_{3}(I).

(ii)

If
e_{2}(I)=2
, then
\operatorname{reg}G(I)\leq r_{J}(I)-e_{3}(I)+1
from Proposition
5.6
. Further, from
[
28
, Corollary 1.5]
we have,
r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)+1.

Therefore,
\operatorname{reg}G(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\lambda(R/I)-e_{3}(I)+2.
∎

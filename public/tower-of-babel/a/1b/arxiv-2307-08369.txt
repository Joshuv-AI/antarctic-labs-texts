1.
Introduction

Singular elliptic and parabolic equations are widely recognised as
one of the important branches of modern analysis from the viewpoint of the physical significance of the problems at hand and the
novel analytical methods that they generate (see, for example,
[
Du
,
V1
,
V2
]
and the references therein).
Singularities can develop or manifest in many ways for elliptic PDEs, such as the blow-up of a solution at an isolated point (see, for instance,
[
CD
]
) or at the whole boundary (see, for example,
[
CD1
,
ZD
]
). Singularities can also arise through the coefficients in an equation, usually in the form of singular potentials (see, e.g.,
[
WD
]
). Here, we tackle singularities that appear in anisotropic elliptic PDEs via the nonlinear lower order term going to infinity when a positive solution tends to 0 on the boundary.

The current demand to predict with a higher and higher degree of accuracy the behaviour in systems arising from the physical and biological sciences means
that more variables need to be taken into account in the modelling of the natural phenomena, generating
nonlinear PDEs with nonstandard growth and featuring singularities
[
AS
]
. Anisotropic elliptic and parabolic equations represent a very active research area in the last decades. The increasing interest in nonlinear anisotropic problems is justified by their
applications in many areas from image recovery and the mathematical modelling of non-Newtonian fluids to biology, where they serve as models for the propagation of epidemic diseases in heterogeneous domains (see, for example,
[
ADS
]
).

Important tools available for the isotropic case cannot be extended to the anisotropic setting (such as the strong maximum principle, see
[
V
]
).
Despite
important strides on existence, uniqueness, and regularity of weak solutions of anisotropic equations (see, for instance,
[
ACCZ
,
AC
,
CV
,
dBFZ
,
DiCastro
,
FVV
,
MT
]
), the influence of anisotropy in many problems remains as yet elusive in many essential aspects. This note is a continuation of our study initiated in
[
baci
,
BC
]
to prove existence and boundedness of solutions for general singular anisotropic elliptic equations.

Throughout this paper, we assume that
\Omega
is a bounded, open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 2
). We impose no smoothness assumption on the boundary of
\Omega
.
We always assume that

(1.1)

p_{j}\in(1,\infty)\ \mbox{for every }1\leq j\leq N\quad\text{and}\quad p<N,

where
p:=N/\sum_{j=1}^{N}(1/p_{j})
is the harmonic mean of
p_{1},\ldots,p_{N}
.
For any
r>1
, let
r^{\prime}=r/(r-1)
be the conjugate exponent of
r
.
We set
\overrightarrow{p}=\left(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{N}\right)
and

\overrightarrow{p}^{\prime}=(p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,p_{N}^{\prime})
. We define by
W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)

the closure of

C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, the set of smooth functions with compact supports in
\Omega
,
with respect to the norm

\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\partial_{j}u\|_{L^{p_{j}}(\Omega)}.

As usual,
\nabla u=(\partial_{1}u,\ldots,\partial_{N}u)
is the gradient of
u
.
The assumption
p<N
gives that the embedding
W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{s}(\Omega)
is continuous
for every

s\in[1,p^{\ast}]
and compact for every
s\in[1,p^{\ast})
,
where
p^{\ast}:=Np/(N-p)
stands for the anisotropic Sobolev exponent.
The anisotropic
\overrightarrow{p}
-Laplacian operator given by

(1.2)

-\Delta_{\overrightarrow{p}}u=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{j}(|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u)

is a bounded, coercive and pseudo-monotone operator of Leray–Lions type from

W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
into its dual
W^{-1,\overrightarrow{p}^{\prime}}(\Omega)
.
For every
(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we define

(1.3)

\Phi_{0}(t,\xi)=\left(\mathfrak{a}_{0}+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathfrak{a}_{j}|\xi_{j}|^{p_{j}}\right)|t|^{m-2}t,

where

m>1
,
\mathfrak{a}_{0}>0
,
\mathfrak{a}_{j}\geq 0
for
1\leq j\leq N
, whereas

(1.4)

\Psi(t,\xi)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}|t|^{\theta_{j}-2}t\,|\xi_{j}|^{q_{j}}

with
\theta_{j}>0
and

0\leq q_{j}<p_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq N
.

Let
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
. In our recent paper
[
BC
]
, we give sufficient conditions for the existence of solutions for general singular anisotropic elliptic equations,
including for our model problem

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta_{\overrightarrow{p}}u+\Phi_{0}(u,\nabla u)=\Psi(u,\nabla u)+f\quad\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
&u\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega),\quad\Phi_{0}(u,\nabla u)\in L^{1}(\Omega).\end{aligned}\right.

We distinguish the following cases according to the values of the
\theta_{j}
’s.

Case 1:
(
Non-singular
) For every
1\leq j\leq N
, we have
\theta_{j}>1
.

Case 2:
(
Mildly singular
) There exists
1\leq j\leq N
such that
\theta_{j}\leq 1
.
In this situation, we will consider non-negative solutions of (
1.5
) and assume, in addition, that
f\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
.

As in
[
BC
]
, by a solution of (
1.5
), we understand the following.

Definition
1.1
.

A function
u\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
, which is non-negative in Case 2, is said to be a solution of (
1.5
) if

\Phi_{0}(u,\nabla u)\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and for every
v\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we have

(1.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle I_{u}(v):=\int_{\{|u|>0\}}\Psi(u,\nabla u)\,v\,dx\quad\mbox{is finite}

and

(1.7)

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}-2}\partial_{j}u\,\partial_{j}v\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{0}(u,\nabla u)\,v\,dx=I_{u}(v)+\int_{\Omega}f\,v\,dx.

Under suitable conditions on
m
, we proved in
[
BC
]
that (
1.5
) has a solution in the above sense. Before recalling the relevant result, we
introduce the following sets:

(1.8)

\displaystyle N_{\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{a}}}:=\left\{1\leq j\leq N:\ \mathfrak{a}_{j}q_{j}=0,\ \ \frac{\theta_{j}p_{j}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}\geq p\right\},

\displaystyle P_{\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{a}}}:=\left\{1\leq j\leq N:\ \mathfrak{a}_{j}q_{j}>0,\ \mathfrak{m}_{j}>1\right\},\quad\mbox{where }\mathfrak{m}_{j}:=\frac{p_{j}-q_{j}}{q_{j}}\left(\frac{\theta_{j}p_{j}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}-p\right).

Suppose that
m
satisfies

(1.9)

m>\max_{j\in N_{\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{a}}}}\frac{\theta_{j}p_{j}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}\quad\text{and}\quad m>\min\left\{\theta_{j},\mathfrak{m}_{j}\right\}\ \ \text{for every }j\in P_{\overrightarrow{\mathfrak{a}}}.

As a consequence of the main results in
[
BC
]
, we obtain the following existence result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) and (
1.9
) hold.
Assume Case 1 or Case 2.
If
f\in L^{(p^{\ast})^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, then (
1.5
) has a solution
u\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
, which satisfies that

(1.10)

\Phi_{0}(u,\nabla u)\,u,\ \Psi(u,\nabla u)\,u\in L^{1}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\eqref{ff1}\ \mbox{holds for }v=u.

We point out that, if
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and

\min_{1\leq j\leq N}\mathfrak{a}_{j}>0
, then in
[
BC
]
we also prove that (
1.5
) has a solution
u\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
.

1.1.
Main result

In this note, in the framework of Theorem
1.2
, we prove that, if
f\in L^{r}(\Omega)
with
r>N/p
,
then every solution of (
1.5
) satisfying (
1.10
) is bounded. Moreover, every bounded solution satisfies a uniform bound. More precisely, we prove the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) and (
1.9
) hold.
Assume Case 1 or Case 2. If
f\in L^{r}(\Omega)
with
r>N/p
,
then for every solution

u\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
of (
1.5
) satisfying (
1.10
), we have
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C
, depending only on
\Omega,N,m,\overrightarrow{p},\overrightarrow{q},r,\|f\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)},\{\mathfrak{a}_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq N}
and
\{\theta_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq N}
, such that

(1.11)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C.

We remark that our assumption (
1.9
) is equivalent to requiring that,
for every
1\leq j\leq N
, we have one of the following:

(1.12)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{j}>0,\>q_{j}\geq 0,\>0<\theta_{j}<\max\{m,p\}\frac{p_{j}-q_{j}}{p_{j}}+\frac{mq_{j}}{p_{j}},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{j}=0,\>q_{j}\geq 0,\>0<\theta_{j}<\max\{m,p\}\frac{p_{j}-q_{j}}{p_{j}}.

1.2.
Strategy of the proof

The crucial ingredient in the proof of our Theorem
1.3
is the celebrated Stampacchia’s method (see Lemma 4.1 in
[
S
]
, which is recalled in Section 2).
Even if we know that Stampacchia’s procedure is nowadays a typical technique to prove that a solution of an elliptic equation is bounded, its application in this context is far from being trivial. For the reader’s convenience, we provide all the details, and we split the proof into several propositions of increasing generality until we reach the full range in (
1.12
).

1.3.
Further comments

We conclude our introduction by comparing our results with the ones known in the isotropic case, for which the model problem is

(1.13)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta_{p}u+\lambda|u|^{m-2}u=g(u)|\nabla u|^{q}+f&\mbox{in }\Omega,\\
\\
u=0&\mbox{on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Assume that
\lambda=0
. The exhaustive analysis of the necessary growth condition on
g
to obtain a solution for every datum
f\in L^{r}(\Omega)
, where
r>1
, was made by Porretta and Segura de León in
[
PS
]
. When
f\in L^{\frac{N}{p}}(\Omega)
and
\lim_{|s|\to+\infty}g(s)=0
, they prove the existence of a bounded weak solution
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Under the same assumptions, in
[
LMS
]
the authors investigate the case
p=2
,
1<q<2
and prove an analogous existence result of a bounded solution of (
1.13
).

When
g(u)
is singular in the
u
-variable at
u=0
, that is
g(u)=|u|^{-\nu}
with
\nu>0
, and we take
q=p
, the existence of a bounded weak solution is proved in
[
GM
,
GPS
]
when
f\in L^{r}(\Omega)
with
r>N/p
.
In
[
GM
]
the presence of an absorption term, with exponent
m=2
, is used to prove the existence of a bounded solution in
H^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(\Omega)
when
p=q=2
and
f
is a bounded, non-negative function. In
[
GPS1
]
the authors prove existence results when
p=q=2
,
\nu\in(0,1)
and
f\in L^{r}(\Omega)
with
r\geq N/2
, allowing the presence of a sign-changing datum
f
. They also discuss related questions such as the existence of solutions when the datum
f
is less
regular, or the boundedness of the solutions when
r>N/2
.

2.
Notation and auxiliary results

For
k>0
, we let
T_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
stand for the truncation at height
k
, that is,

(2.1)

T_{k}(s)=s\quad\mbox{if }|s|\leq k,\quad T_{k}(s)=k\,\frac{s}{|s|}\quad\mbox{if }|s|>k.

Moreover, we define
G_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(2.2)

G_{k}(s)=s-T_{k}(s)\quad\mbox{for every }s\in\mathbb{R},

so that
G_{k}=0
on
[-k,k]
.

In order to prove the boundedness of a solution to our problem, we will make use of the following result, contained in the celebrated paper
[
S
]
by G. Stampacchia.

Lemma 2.1

(See Lemma 4.1 in
[
S
]
)
.

Let
k_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varphi:[k_{0},+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be a non-increasing function. Assume that, for some positive constants
a
,
b
and
C
, we have

\varphi(h)\leq\frac{C}{(h-k)^{a}}[\varphi(k)]^{b}\quad\mbox{for all }h>k\geq k_{0}.

Then:

i)

if
b>1
, it holds

\varphi(k_{0}+d)=0

where

d=C^{\frac{1}{a}}[\varphi(k_{0})]^{\frac{b-1}{a}}2^{\frac{b}{b-1}};

ii)

if
b=1
, it holds

\varphi(h)\leq e\,\exp\left(-\zeta(h-k_{0})\right)\varphi(k_{0})

where

\zeta=\left(eC\right)^{-\frac{1}{a}};

iii)

if
b<1
and
k_{0}>0
, it holds

\varphi(h)\leq 2^{\frac{s}{1-b}}\left(C^{\frac{1}{1-b}}+(2k_{0})^{s}\varphi(k_{0})\right)h^{-s}

where

s=\frac{a}{1-b}.

We next recall the anisotropic Sobolev inequality in
[
T
]
*Theorem 1.2.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
N\geq 2
be an integer. If
1<p_{j}<\infty
for every
1\leq j\leq N
and
p<N
,
then there exists a
positive constant
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}(N,\overrightarrow{p})
, such that

(2.3)

\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\mathcal{S}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\|\partial_{j}u\|_{L^{p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{1/N}\quad\mbox{for all }u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where, as usual,
p^{\ast}:=Np/(N-p)
.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded, open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 2
. If
1<p_{j}<\infty
for every
1\leq j\leq N
and
p<N
, then by a density argument,
(
2.3
) extends to all
u\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
so that the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality yields

(2.4)

\|u\|_{L^{p^{\ast}}(\Omega)}\leq\mathcal{S}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\|\partial_{j}u\|_{L^{p_{j}}(\Omega)}^{1/N}\leq\frac{\mathcal{S}}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\partial_{j}u\|_{L^{p_{j}}(\Omega)}=\frac{\mathcal{S}}{N}\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)}

for all
u\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
. Moreover,
using Hölder’s inequality, the embedding
W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{s}(\Omega)
is continuous
for every

s\in[1,p^{\ast}]
and compact for every
s\in[1,p^{\ast})
.

In the sequel, we need the following version of Young’s inequality.

Lemma 2.4

(Young’s inequality)
.

Let
N\geq 2
be an integer. Assume that
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{N}
are positive numbers
and
1<R_{k}<\infty
for each
1\leq k\leq N-1
. If
\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(1/R_{k})<1
, then for every
\delta>0
, there exists
a positive constant
C_{\delta}
(depending on
\delta
) such that

\prod_{k=1}^{N}\beta_{k}\leq\delta\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\beta_{k}^{R_{k}}+C_{\delta}\,\beta_{N}^{R_{N}},

where we define
R_{N}=\left[1-\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(1/R_{k})\right]^{-1}
.

3.
Boundedness of solutions

We first assume that

(3.1)

\theta_{j}<m\quad\mbox{and}\quad\quad q_{j}\geq 0,\quad\mbox{ for every }1\leq j\leq N.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) hold and
f\in L^{r}(\Omega)
with
r>N/p
.
Assume Case 1 or Case 2.
If
\min_{1\leq j\leq N}\mathfrak{a}_{j}>0
and (
3.1
) holds,
then for every solution

u\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
of (
1.5
) satisfying (
1.10
), we have
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Moreover, there exists a positive constant
C
, which depends only on
\Omega,N,m,\overrightarrow{p},\overrightarrow{q},r,\|f\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)},\{\mathfrak{a}_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq N}
and
\{\theta_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq N}
, such that

(3.2)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C.

Proof.

Let
u\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
be a solution of (
1.5
) satisfying (
1.10
).
In what follows, we take
k\geq k_{0}>1
as large as needed and define

(3.3)

A(k):=\{x\in\Omega:\ |u(x)|\geq k\},\quad\mu_{u}(k):=\mbox{meas}\,(A(k)).

Note that
G_{k}(u)=u-T_{k}(u)\in W_{0}^{1,\overrightarrow{p}}(\Omega)
and
\partial_{j}G_{k}(u)=\partial_{j}u
on
A(k)
for all
1\leq j\leq N
.
The property (
1.10
) enables us to take
v=G_{k}(u)
as a test function in (
1.7
). Hence, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{A(k)}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}}\,dx+\mathfrak{a}_{0}\int_{A(k)}|u|^{m-1}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathfrak{a}_{j}\int_{A(k)}|u|^{m-1}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{A(k)}|u|^{\theta_{j}-1}|\partial_{j}u|^{q_{j}}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx+\int_{A(k)}|f|\,|G_{k}(u)|\,dx.

In what follows, we let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
be small such that

(3.5)

3N\varepsilon<\mathfrak{a}_{0},\quad N\varepsilon<\min_{j=1,\ldots,N}\mathfrak{a}_{j}

and we let
k>k_{0}
with
k_{0}>1
large satisfying

(3.6)

k_{0}\geq(1/\varepsilon)^{1/(m-\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\theta_{i})}.

Let
1\leq j\leq N
be fixed.

(a) Assume first that
q_{j}=0
. By our hypotheses, we have
\theta_{j}<m
so that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\int_{A(k)}|u|^{\theta_{j}-1}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq k^{\theta_{j}-m}\int_{A(k)}|u|^{m-1}\,|G_{k}(u)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{A(k)}|u|^{m-1}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx.

(b) Assume that
q_{j}>0
. We define

(3.8)

\eta_{j}:=\theta_{j}-1-\frac{\left(m-1\right)q_{j}}{p_{j}}.

We distinguish the following sub-cases:

(b
1
) Let
\eta_{j}\leq 0
. For simplicity of notation, we define

(3.9)

R_{k}(u):=\int_{A(k)}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx.

By Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
, depending on
\varepsilon
, but independent of

, such that

(3.10)

\displaystyle\int_{A(k)}|u|^{\theta_{j}-1}|\partial_{j}u|^{q_{j}}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq k^{\eta_{j}}\left(\int_{A(k)}|u|^{m-1}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx\right)^{\frac{q_{j}}{p_{j}}}\left[R_{k}(u)\right]^{\frac{p_{j}-q_{j}}{p_{j}}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{A(k)}|u|^{m-1}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx+C_{\varepsilon}\,R_{k}(u).

(b

) Let
\eta_{j}>0
, where

is defined in (

). Remark that

1-\frac{q_{j}}{p_{j}}-\frac{\eta_{j}}{m-1}=\frac{m-\theta_{j}}{m-1}\in(0,1).

Thus, using Hölder’s inequality and (

(3.11)

\displaystyle\int_{A(k)}|u|^{\theta_{j}-1}|\partial_{j}u|^{q_{j}}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{A(k)}|u|^{m-1}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx\right)^{\frac{q_{j}}{p_{j}}}\left(\int_{A(k)}|u|^{m-1}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx\right)^{\frac{\eta_{j}}{m-1}}[R_{k}(u)]^{\frac{m-\theta_{j}}{m-1}}.

By Young’s inequality in Lemma

, for every

, there exists a constant

such that
the right-hand side of the inequality in (

(3.12)

\varepsilon\int_{A(k)}|u|^{m-1}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx+\varepsilon\int_{A(k)}|u|^{m-1}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx+C_{\varepsilon}\,R_{k}(u).

Letting

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{A(k)}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}}\,dx+\frac{\mathfrak{a}_{0}}{3}\int_{A(k)}|u|^{m-1}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx\leq\int_{A(k)}|f|\,|G_{k}(u)|\,dx.

Now we focus our attention on the left-hand side of (

, there exists a positive constant

C
, which depends only on
\Omega,N,m,\overrightarrow{p},\overrightarrow{q},r,\|f\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)},\{\mathfrak{a}_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq N}
and
\{\theta_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq N}
, such that

(3.36)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C.

Proof.

If
m\geq p
or (
3.1
) holds, then by Proposition
3.1
, we have
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. On the other hand, if
m<p
and
(
3.26
) holds, then by Proposition
3.3
, we get the claim. So, it remains to assume that
m<p
and
there exists
1\leq j_{1}\leq N
such that

q_{j_{1}}>0\quad\mbox{and}\quad m\leq\theta_{j_{1}}<p\left(1-\frac{q_{j_{1}}}{p_{j_{1}}}\right)+\frac{mq_{j_{1}}}{p_{j_{1}}}.

As in the proof of Proposition
3.1
,
we let
k>1
and take
v=G_{k}(u)
in (
1.7
) to recover (
3.4
).

Assume that
u\in L^{\gamma}(\Omega)
for some
\gamma\geq p^{\ast}
. We define
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}
and
I_{4}
as in the proof of Proposition
3.3
.
By assumption, we have
I_{5}\not=\emptyset
, where
I_{5}
is given by

I_{5}:=\left\{1\leq j\leq N:\ q_{j}>0,\ m\leq\theta_{j}<p\left(1-\frac{q_{j}}{p_{j}}\right)+\frac{mq_{j}}{p_{j}}\right\}.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
small as needed and
k>k_{0}
with
k_{0}>1
sufficiently large.

If
j\in I_{1}
or
j\in I_{2}\cup I_{3}\cup I_{4}
, we regain (
3.28
) or
(
3.29
), respectively.

If
j\in I_{5}
, then by Hölder’s inequality, we obtain that

(3.37)

\displaystyle\int_{A(k)}|u|^{\theta_{j}-1}\,|\partial_{j}u|^{q_{j}}\,|G_{k}(u)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{A(k)}|G_{k}(u)|^{p^{\ast}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p_{j}-q_{j}}{p_{j}p^{\ast}}}\left(\int_{A(k)}|u|^{m-1}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx\right)^{\frac{q_{j}}{p_{j}}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{A(k)}|u|^{\gamma}\,dx\right)^{\frac{\eta_{j}}{\gamma}}\left(\mu_{u}(k)\right)^{\frac{p_{j}-q_{j}}{p_{j}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\right)-\frac{\eta_{j}}{\gamma}},

where
\eta_{j}
is given by (
3.8
).

Using (
3.15
) in the right-hand side of (
3.37
), then Young’s inequality, we arrive at

(3.38)

\displaystyle\int_{A(k)}|u|^{\theta_{j}-1}\,|\partial_{j}u|^{q_{j}}\,|G_{k}(u)|\,dx\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\int_{A(k)}|\partial_{\ell}G_{k}(u)|^{p_{\ell}}\,dx

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\int_{A(k)}|u|^{m-1}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx

\displaystyle+C_{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{\frac{p_{j}\eta_{j}p^{\prime}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}}\left(\mu_{u}(k)\right)^{p^{\prime}\left(1-\frac{p_{j}\eta_{j}}{(p_{j}-q_{j})\gamma}-\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\right)}

for some large positive constant
C_{\varepsilon}
.

From (
3.4
), (
3.21
), (
3.28
), (
3.29
) and (
3.38
), choosing
\varepsilon\in(0,1/(2N))
small and
k_{0}>1
large, we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\int_{A(k)}|\partial_{\ell}u|^{p_{\ell}}\,dx\leq

\displaystyle 2C_{\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{j\in I_{1}}\|u\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{p^{\prime}(\theta_{j}-1)}\left(\mu_{u}(k)\right)^{p^{\prime}\left(1-\frac{\theta_{j}-1}{\gamma}-\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle+2C_{\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{j\in I_{5}}\|u\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{\frac{p_{j}\eta_{j}p^{\prime}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}}\left(\mu_{u}(k)\right)^{p^{\prime}\left(1-\frac{p_{j}\eta_{j}}{(p_{j}-q_{j})\gamma}-\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle+2\|u\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\|f\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\left(\mu_{u}(k)\right)^{1-\frac{1}{\gamma}-\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\ \leq

\displaystyle M\,\mu_{u}(k)^{\min\left\{p^{\prime}\left(1-\frac{\theta-1}{\gamma}-\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\right),\,p^{\prime}\left(1-\frac{\eta-1}{\gamma}-\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\right),\,1-\frac{1}{\gamma}-\frac{1}{r}\right\}},

where

\theta:=\max_{j\in I_{1}}\theta_{j},\quad\eta:=\max_{j\in I_{5}}\left\{\theta_{j}+\frac{q_{j}(\theta_{j}-m)}{p_{j}-q_{j}}\right\}=\max_{j\in I_{5}}\frac{p_{j}\eta_{j}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}+1>1,

C_{\Omega}
is a positive constant depending on
|\Omega|
,
p
,
N
, and
r
, and

(3.39)

M:=C_{\Omega}\,\left(C_{\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{j\in I_{1}}\|u\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{p^{\prime}(\theta_{j}-1)}\right)+C_{\varepsilon}\left(\sum_{j\in I_{5}}\|u\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{\frac{p_{j}\eta_{j}p^{\prime}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}}\right)+\|u\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}\|f\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\right).

Taking
h>k\geq k_{0}
and using (
3.15
) and (
3.16
), we obtain that

(3.40)

\mu_{u}(h)\leq\frac{\mathcal{S}^{p^{\ast}}M^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{p}}}{(h-k)^{p^{\ast}}}\,\mu_{u}(k)^{\min\left\{\widehat{\beta}(\gamma),\widetilde{\beta}(\gamma),\beta(\gamma)\right\}},

where we define
\widehat{\beta}(\gamma)
and
\beta(\gamma)
as in (
3.31
), and

(3.41)

\widetilde{\beta}(\gamma):=\left(1-\frac{\eta-1}{\gamma}-\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}\right)\frac{p^{\ast}}{p-1}.

If
\theta\geq\eta
, then
\min\{\widetilde{\beta}(\gamma),\widehat{\beta}(\gamma)\}=\widehat{\beta}(\gamma)
. Then, as in the proof of Proposition
3.3
, we get
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. If
\theta<\eta
, then
\min\{\widetilde{\beta}(\gamma),\widehat{\beta}(\gamma)\}=\widetilde{\beta}(\gamma)

and, reasoning as in the proof of Proposition
3.3
with
\eta
instead of

, we conclude that
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Indeed, in order to get a contradiction we need that
\eta<p
, which is true according to the definition of

. This finishes the proof of Proposition
3.5
.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.3
concluded

Assume that
u\in L^{\gamma}(\Omega)
for some
\gamma\geq p^{\ast}
. In view of Proposition
3.5
, Remarks
3.2
and
3.4
, in order to conclude the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we have to consider the remaining possibility that
I_{0}\neq\emptyset
, where

I_{0}:=\left\{1\leq j\leq N:\mathfrak{a}_{j}=0,\,q_{j}>0,\,0<\theta_{j}<\max\{m,p\}\frac{(p_{j}-q_{j})}{p_{j}}\right\}.

Let
j\in I_{0}
be arbitrary. Fix
\varepsilon>0
small as needed and
k>k_{0}
with
k_{0}>1
sufficiently large.

(
\bullet
) We first assume that
m\geq p
, which means that

(4.1)

\frac{\theta_{j}p_{j}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}<m.

By Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities, there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{A(k)}|u|^{\theta_{j}-1}|\partial_{j}u|^{q_{j}}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{A(k)}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q_{j}}{p_{j}}}\left(\int_{A(k)}|u|^{\frac{(\theta_{j}-1)p_{j}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}}|G_{k}(u)|^{\frac{p_{j}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p_{j}-q_{j}}{p_{j}}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{A(k)}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}}\,dx+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{A(k)}|u|^{\frac{\theta_{j}p_{j}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}-1}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{A(k)}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}}\,dx+C_{\varepsilon}k^{\frac{\theta_{j}p_{j}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}-m}\int_{A(k)}|u|^{m-1}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx.

Finally, since (
4.1
) holds, the arguments in Proposition
3.5
apply.

(
\bullet\bullet
) Let
m<p
. If
\theta_{j}<m(p_{j}-q_{j})/p_{j}
, we conclude the proof with the same arguments as above. It remains to consider the case
j\in J_{0}
, where

J_{0}:=\left\{1\leq j\leq N:\mathfrak{a}_{j}=0,\,q_{j}>0,\,\frac{m(p_{j}-q_{j})}{p_{j}}\leq\theta_{j}<\frac{p(p_{j}-q_{j})}{p_{j}}\right\}.

By Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities, there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{A(k)}|u|^{\theta_{j}-1}|\partial_{j}u|^{q_{j}}|G_{k}(u)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{A(k)}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{q_{j}}{p_{j}}}\left(\int_{A(k)}|u|^{\frac{\theta_{j}p_{j}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p_{j}-q_{j}}{p_{j}}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{A(k)}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}}\,dx+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{A(k)}|u|^{\frac{\theta_{j}p_{j}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{A(k)}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}}\,dx+C_{\varepsilon}k^{\frac{\theta_{j}p_{j}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}-p}\int_{A(k)}|u|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\int_{A(k)}|\partial_{j}u|^{p_{j}}\,dx+C_{\varepsilon}k^{\frac{\theta_{j}p_{j}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}-p}\left(\int_{A(k)}|u|^{\gamma}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p}{\gamma}}\left(\mu_{u}(k)\right)^{1-\frac{p}{\gamma}}.

Let
\widehat{\beta}(\gamma)
and
\beta(\gamma)
as in (
3.31
),
\widetilde{\beta}(\gamma)
as in (
3.41
), and
M
as in (
3.39
), respectively. Taking
h>k\geq k_{0}
and similar to the proof of (
3.40
), we obtain that

(4.2)

\mu_{u}(h)\leq\frac{\mathcal{S}^{p^{\ast}}\overline{M}^{\frac{p^{\ast}}{p}}}{(h-k)^{p^{\ast}}}\,\mu_{u}(k)^{\min\left\{\widehat{\beta}(\gamma),\widetilde{\beta}(\gamma),\beta(\gamma),\overline{\beta}(\gamma)\right\}},

where

\overline{\beta}(\gamma)=\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{\gamma}\right)p^{\ast},

and

(4.3)

\overline{M}=M+C_{\Omega}\left(C_{\varepsilon}k^{\max_{j\in J_{0}}\left\{\frac{\theta_{j}p_{j}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}\right\}-p}\|u\|_{L^{\gamma}(\Omega)}^{p}\right).

It is easy to check that

(4.4)

\widehat{\beta}(\gamma)-\overline{\beta}(\gamma)>0,\quad\widetilde{\beta}(\gamma)-\overline{\beta}(\gamma)>0.

Moreover,
\overline{\beta}(\gamma)-\beta(\gamma)
is increasing in
\gamma
. Hence, we can apply an iteration scheme analogous to the one in Case
(E_{2})
of Proposition
3.1
. Note that, for every
\gamma>p
, if
\overline{\beta}(\gamma)<1
, then
\gamma<p^{\ast}/(1-\overline{\beta}(\gamma))
. On the other hand, once the iteration procedure starts, it has to end in a finite number of steps. Indeed, if this were not the case, we would be able to construct two increasing sequences
\{\gamma_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
in
(p,+\infty)
and
\{\overline{\beta}(\gamma_{i})\}_{i\geq 1}
in
(0,1)
, with
\overline{\beta}(\gamma_{i})\to L\in(0,1]
satisfying

L=\frac{p^{\ast}}{p}-1+L,

which is a contradiction.
As a consequence we get that

\mbox{there exists }\gamma=\gamma(N,p,r)\geq p^{\ast}\mbox{ such that }\min\{\overline{\beta}(\gamma),\beta(\gamma)\}>1.

Hence, from Lemma
2.1

i)
, we deduce that
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
Moreover, using (
4.3
) and that
\max_{j\in J_{0}}\left\{\frac{\theta_{j}p_{j}}{p_{j}-q_{j}}\right\}-p<0
, we get (
1.11
).
This concludes the proof.

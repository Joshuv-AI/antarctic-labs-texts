A Criterion for the Normality of Polynomials over Finite Fields Based on Their Coefficients
Xiang-dong Hou

Abstract
. An irreducible polynomial over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} is said to be normal over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} if its roots are linearly independent over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} . We show that there is a polynomial h n ​ ( X 1 , … , X n ) ∈ ℤ ⁡ [ X 1 , … , X n ] h_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}] , independent of q q , such that if an irreducible polynomial f = X n + a 1 ​ X n − 1 + ⋯ + a n ∈ 𝔽 q ​ [ X ] f=X^{n}+a_{1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X] is such that h n ​ ( a 1 , … , a n ) ≠ 0 h_{n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\neq 0 , then f f is normal over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} . The polynomial h n ​ ( X 1 , … , X n ) h_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n}) is computed explicitly for n ≤ 5 n\leq 5 and partially for n = 6 n=6 . When char ​ 𝔽 q = p \text{char}\,\mathbb{F}_{q}=p , we also show that there is a polynomial h p , n ​ ( X 1 , … , X n ) ∈ 𝔽 p ​ [ X 1 , … , X n ] h_{p,n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}] , depending on p p , which is simpler than h n h_{n} but has the same property. These results remain valid for monic separable irreducible polynomials over an arbitrary field with a cyclic Galois group.

1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a field and
K
be a finite Galois extension over
K
. If
a\in K
is such that
\{\sigma(a):\sigma\in\text{Aut}(K/F)\}
forms a basis of
K/F
,
a
is called a
normal element
of
K
over
F
and
\{\sigma(a):\sigma\in\text{Aut}(K/F)\}
is called a
normal basis
of
K
over
F
. The existence of normal bases was proved a long time ago by Deuring
[
4
]
. (For a more accessible reference, see Lang
[
9
, §VI.13]
.) Normal bases have applications in many areas, especially in the theory and applications of finite fields.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
denote the finite field with
q
elements. An irreducible polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
of degree
n
is said to be
normal
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if its roots are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, i.e., the roots form a normal basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Many theoretic results and computational procedures concerning finite fields utilize normal bases. There is an extensive literature on normal bases of finite fields; see
[
3
]
,
[
11
, §§5.2 – 5.4]
, and the references therein.

In this paper, we are interested in finding sufficient conditions for an irreducible polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
to be normal. More precisely, we show that there is a polynomial
h_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
, independent of
q
, such that if
f=X^{n}+a_{1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
is irreducible and
h_{n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\neq 0
, then
f
is normal over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The polynomial
h_{n}
is computed explicitly for
n\leq 5
and partially for
n=6
. There is also a characteristic specific version of the polynomial
h_{n}
which is simpler but has the same property. Let
p=\text{char}\,\mathbb{F}_{q}
. There is a polynomial
h_{p,n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
, depending on
p
, such that if
f=X^{n}+a_{1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
is irreducible and
h_{p,n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\neq 0
, then
f
is normal. We remind the reader that
h_{p,n}
is not necessarily the reduction of
h_{n}
modulo
p
.

The basic idea of our approach is the notion of symmetrization of polynomials.
Let
F
be a field,
n\geq 2
be an integer and
f(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1})\in F[X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}]
. We are interested in finding a symmetric polynomial
g(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1})\in F[X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}]
such that
f\mid g
; we refer to such a
g
as a
symmetrization
of
f
. Naturally, we require the degree of
g
to be as low as possible. The relevance of this question will become clear in the next section.

Let the symmetric group
S_{n}
act on
F[X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}]
by permuting
X_{0}\cdots,X_{n-1}
. Let
\text{Stab}(f)=\{\sigma\in S_{n}:\sigma(f)=f\}
be the stabilizer of
f
in
S_{n}
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a system of representatives of the left cosets of
\text{Stab}(f)
in
S_{n}
. Then
g=\prod_{\sigma\in\mathcal{C}}\sigma(f)
is a symmetrization of
f
. (Note that
g
is independent of the choice of
\mathcal{C}
.) To reduced the degree of the symmetrization of
f
, it is sometimes helpful to factor
f
first and then determine the symmetrization fo each factor.

2.
Symmetrization of
\Delta_{n}

Define

(2.1)

\Delta_{n}(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1})=\det\left[\begin{matrix}X_{0}&X_{1}&\cdots&X_{n-1}\cr X_{n-1}&X_{0}&\cdots&X_{n-2}\cr\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\cr X_{1}&X_{2}&\cdots&X_{0}\end{matrix}\right]\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{0}\dots,X_{n-1}].

Let
f=X^{n}+a_{1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
be irreducible, and let
r_{0}=r,r_{1}=r^{q},\dots,r_{n-1}=r^{q^{n-1}}
be the roots of
f
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. It is well known that
r_{0},\dots,r_{n-1}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
\Delta_{n}(r_{0},\dots,r_{n-1})\neq 0
; see
[
10
, Lemma 3.51]
.
Note that
\Delta_{n}(r_{0},\dots,r_{n-1})
cannot be expressed in terms of the coefficients
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
. However, let
\Sigma_{n}
denote a symmetrization of
\Delta_{n}
. Then
\Sigma_{n}(r_{0},\dots,r_{n-1})
is a polynomial in
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
, and moreover,
\Sigma_{n}(r_{0},\dots,r_{n-1})\neq 0
implies
\Delta_{n}(r_{0},\dots,r_{n-1})\neq 0
. The purpose of this section is to determine
\Sigma_{n}
.

We can write

(2.2)

\Delta_{n}=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}\epsilon_{n}^{ij}X_{j}\Bigr),

where
\epsilon_{n}=e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/n}
. Let

\Psi_{n}(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1})=\prod_{i\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}\epsilon_{n}^{ij}X_{j}\Bigr).

Clearly,
\Psi_{n}
is invariant under the action of the Galois group
\text{Aut}(\mathbb{Q}(\epsilon_{n})/\mathbb{Q})
. Hence

. Since the coefficients of
\Psi_{n}
are integral over
\mathbb{Z}
, we have
\Psi_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}]
. For the formulas of
\Psi_{n}
with
n\leq 6
, see the appendix A1. From (
2.2
) we have

(2.4)

\Delta_{n}=\prod_{m\mid n}\Psi_{m}\Bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{n/m-1}X_{mj},\sum_{j=0}^{n/m-1}X_{1+mj},\dots,\sum_{j=0}^{n/m-1}X_{m-1+mj}\Bigr).

We treat the symmetric group
S_{n}
as the permutation group of
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}=\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}
and
S_{n-1}<S_{n}
as the permutation group of
\{1,2,\dots,n-1\}
.
For
a\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
and

, let
\alpha_{a,b}
be the permutation of
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
defined by the affine map

. Let
G_{n}=\{\alpha_{a,b}:a\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times},b\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\}=\text{AGL}(1,\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})<S_{n}

Lemma 2.1
.

We have

\text{\rm Stab}(\Psi_{n})=\begin{cases}\{\text{\rm id}\}&\text{if}\ n=1,2,\cr G_{n}&\text{if}\ n>2.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
\Psi_{2}(X_{0},X_{1})=X_{0}-X_{1}
, its stabilizer in
S_{2}
is the trivial subgroup
\{\text{id}\}
.

Now assume that

\displaystyle\alpha_{a,0}(\Psi_{n})\,

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}\epsilon_{n}^{ij}X_{aj}\Bigr)=\prod_{i\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}\epsilon_{n}^{ia^{-1}j}X_{j}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}\epsilon_{n}^{ij}X_{j}\Bigr)=\Psi_{n}.

For

\displaystyle\alpha_{1,1}(\Psi_{n})\,

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}\epsilon_{n}^{ij}X_{j+1}\Bigr)=\prod_{i\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}\epsilon_{n}^{i(j-1)}X_{j}\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\epsilon_{n}^{-i}\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}\epsilon_{n}^{ij}X_{j}\Bigr)=\epsilon_{n}^{\sum_{i\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}i}\prod_{i\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}\epsilon_{n}^{ij}X_{aj}\Bigr)

In the above, since

\sum_{i\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}i=0.

Hence
\alpha_{1,1}(\Psi_{n})=\Psi_{n}
. Since

On the other hand, assume that

It follows from here that

and

.
∎

On the other hand, assume that
\alpha\in\text{Stab}(\Psi_{m}(Y_{0},\dots,Y_{m-1}))

3.
A Sufficient Condition for Normal Polynomials

Let
s_{i}
denote the
i
-th elementary symmetric polynomial in
X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}
. In (
2.9
), since
\Theta_{n,m}\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1}]
is a symmetric polynomial, there exists
\theta_{n,m}\in\mathbb{Z}[s_{1},\dots,s_{n}]
such that

(3.1)

\Theta_{n,m}(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1})=\theta_{n,m}(s_{1},\dots,s_{n}).

For the formulas of
\theta_{n,m}
with
1\leq n\leq 6
,
m\mid n
,
(n,m)\neq(6,6)
, see the appendix A4. (For the entirety of
\theta_{5,5}
,
\theta_{6,2}
and
\theta_{6,3}
, see
[
7
]
.)
\theta_{6,6}
is a polynomial of degree 120 in
s_{1},\dots,s_{6}
with at most 436140 terms, where 436140 is the number of nonnegative integer solutions of
x_{1}+2x_{2}+\cdots+6x_{6}=120
. The computation of
\theta_{6,6}
is not impossible but will require enormous computing power.

Let

(3.2)

h_{n}=\prod_{m\mid n}\theta_{n,m}.

Then

(3.3)

\Sigma_{n}=h_{n}(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{n}).

Our main result is the following criterion for the normality of an irreducible polynomial in terms of its coefficients.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
f=X^{n}+a_{1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
be irreducible. If
h_{n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\neq 0
, where
h_{n}
is defined in (
3.2
), then
f
is normal over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Proof.

Let
r\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
be a root of
f
, and let
r_{0}=r,r_{1}=r^{q},\dots,r_{n-1}=r^{q^{n-1}}
, which are all the roots of
f
. By (
3.3
),

\Sigma_{n}(-r_{0},\dots,-r_{n-1})=h_{n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\neq 0.

Since
\Delta_{n}\mid\Sigma_{n}
, we have
\Delta_{n}(-r_{0},\dots,-r_{n-1})\neq 0
. Thus
f(-X)
is a normal polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and so is
f(X)
.
∎

The above result holds in a more general setting. Let
F
be any field. Let
f\in F[x]
be a separable irreducible polynomial of degree
n
and let
K
be its splitting field over
F
. Such an
f
is called a
normal polynomial
over
F
if its roots form a basis (hence a normal basis) of the
K
over
F
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K/F
be a finite Galois extension of degree
n
with Galois group
\text{\rm Aut}(K/F)=\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{n}\}
. Then
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in K
are linearly independent over
F
if and only if

\det\left[\begin{matrix}\sigma_{1}(x_{1})&\sigma_{1}(x_{2})&\cdots&\sigma_{1}(x_{n})\cr\sigma_{2}(x_{1})&\sigma_{2}(x_{2})&\cdots&\sigma_{2}(x_{n})\cr\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\cr\sigma_{n}(x_{1})&\sigma_{n}(x_{2})&\cdots&\sigma_{n}(x_{n})\end{matrix}\right]\neq 0.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
)
Assume that
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in K^{n}
is such that

(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\left[\begin{matrix}\sigma_{1}(x_{1})&\sigma_{1}(x_{2})&\cdots&\sigma_{1}(x_{n})\cr\sigma_{2}(x_{1})&\sigma_{2}(x_{2})&\cdots&\sigma_{2}(x_{n})\cr\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\cr\sigma_{n}(x_{1})&\sigma_{n}(x_{2})&\cdots&\sigma_{n}(x_{n})\end{matrix}\right]=0.

This means that
a_{1}\sigma_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}\sigma_{n}=0
as a function from

to
K
since

form a basis of

over

. By the linear independence of characters

, we have

.

(
\Leftarrow
) Assume to the contrary that there exists

, not all

. Then

\left[\begin{matrix}\sigma_{1}(x_{1})&\sigma_{1}(x_{2})&\cdots&\sigma_{1}(x_{n})\cr\sigma_{2}(x_{1})&\sigma_{2}(x_{2})&\cdots&\sigma_{2}(x_{n})\cr\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\cr\sigma_{n}(x_{1})&\sigma_{n}(x_{2})&\cdots&\sigma_{n}(x_{n})\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}b_{1}\cr\vdots\cr b_{n}\end{matrix}\right]=0,

A

Let

Write
n=p^{e}m
, where
p\nmid m
and let
\varepsilon_{m}
be a primitive
m

\mathbb{F}_{p}
). The polynomial
\Delta_{n}
defined in (

) is now treated as a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The
p
-ary version of
\Psi_{m}
in (

) is

(4.1)

\Psi_{p,m}=\prod_{i\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\varepsilon_{m}^{ij}X_{j}\Bigr).

(Note that
\Psi_{m}
is defined only when
p\nmid m
.) In fact,
\Psi_{p,m}
is the reduction of
\Psi_{m}
modulo
p
. To see this claim, let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
\mathbb{Z}[\epsilon_{m}]
(the ring of integers of
\mathbb{Q}(\epsilon_{m})
) lying above
p
and treat
\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varepsilon_{m})
as
\mathbb{Z}[\epsilon_{m}]/\mathfrak{p}
.

We have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}(X_{0},\dots,X_{n})\,

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\Bigl(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\varepsilon_{m}^{ij}(X_{j}+X_{j+m}+\cdots+X_{j+(p^{e}-1)m})\Bigr)^{p^{e}}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{m}(Y_{0},\dots,Y_{m-1})^{p^{e}},

where
Y_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{p^{e}-1}X_{j+km}
. Moreover,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\Delta_{m}(Y_{0},\dots,Y_{m-1})\,

\displaystyle=\prod_{l\mid m}\Psi_{p,l}\Bigl(\;\sum_{j\equiv 0\,\text{(mod\, $l$)}}Y_{j},\;\sum_{j\equiv 1\,\text{(mod\, $l$)}}Y_{j},\;\dots,\;\sum_{j\equiv l-1\,\text{(mod\, $l$)}}Y_{j}\Bigr),

\displaystyle=\prod_{l\mid m}\Psi_{p,l}(Z_{0},\dots,Z_{l-1}),

where

Z_{k}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq m-1\cr j\equiv k\,\text{(mod\, $l$)}\end{subarray}}Y_{j}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq n-1\cr i\equiv k\,\text{(mod\, $l$)}\end{subarray}}X_{i}.

The computations in Section 3 carry through almost entirely in the case of characteristic
p
. For
l\mid m
, let
\Theta_{p,n,l}
and
\theta_{p,n,l}
be the reductions of
\Theta_{n,l}
and
\theta_{n,l}
modulo
p
, respectively. Then
\Theta_{p,n,l}
is a symmetrization of
\Psi_{p,l}(Z_{0},\dots,Z_{l-1})
and
\prod_{l\mid m}\Theta_{p,n,l}

is a symmetrization of
\Delta_{m}(Y_{0},\dots,Y_{m-1})
. Moreover

\Theta_{p,n,l}(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1})=\theta_{p,n,l}(s_{1},\dots,s_{n}),

Let

(4.4)

h_{p,n}=\prod_{l\mid m}\theta_{p,n,l}.

Then we have the following analogue of Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f=X^{n}+a_{1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
be irreducible, where
\text{\rm char}\,\mathbb{F}_{q}=p
. If
h_{p,n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\neq n
, then
f
is normal over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Remark 4.2
.

(i) For
l\mid m
and
0\leq t\leq e
, the mod
p
reduction of
\Theta_{n,p^{t}l}
is a power of
\Theta_{p,n,l}
, hence the mod
p
reduction of
\theta_{n,p^{t}l}
is a power of
\theta_{p,n,l}
. Therefore, for
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
h_{n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\neq 0
if and only if
h_{p,n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\neq 0
.

(ii) Let
f=X^{n}+a_{1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
be irreducible. A necessary condition for
f
to be normal over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is that the sum of its roots is nonzero, i.e.,
a_{1}\neq 0
. When
n=p^{e}
, i.e.,
m=1
, we have
h_{p,n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=a_{1}
. Thus in this case,
f
is normal if and only if
a_{1}\neq 0
. This fact was first proved by Perlis
[
13
]
.

(iii) Assume that
n
is a prime different from
\text{char}\,\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then (
4.3
) becomes

\Delta_{n}(X_{0},\dots,X_{n-1})=(X_{0}+\cdots+X_{n-1})\Psi_{p,n}(X_{0},\cdots,X_{n-1}).

Further assume that
q
is a generator of
(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We claim that

\Psi_{p,n}(\alpha,\alpha^{q},\dots,\alpha^{q^{n-1}})\neq 0.

Assume the contrary. Then
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}\varepsilon_{n}^{ij}\alpha^{q^{j}}=0
for some
i\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Raising this equation to the power
q^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, gives

0=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}\varepsilon_{n}^{q^{k}ij}\alpha^{q^{j+k}}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}\varepsilon_{n}^{q^{k}i(j-k)}\alpha^{q^{j}}=\varepsilon_{n}^{-ikq^{k}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}\varepsilon_{n}^{q^{k}ij}\alpha^{q^{j}},

i.e.,

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}\varepsilon_{n}^{q^{k}ij}\alpha^{q^{j}}=0
. Since
q
generates
(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, it follows that that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}}\varepsilon_{n}^{ij}\alpha^{q^{j}}=0

for
all

i\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Consequently,
\alpha=\alpha^{q}=\cdots=\alpha^{q^{n-1}}
, which is a contradiction. Now,
\Delta_{n}(\alpha,\alpha^{q},\dots,\alpha^{q^{n-1}})\neq 0
if and only if
\alpha+\alpha^{q}+\dots+\alpha^{q^{n-1}}\neq 0
.
Therefore, in this case,
a_{1}\neq 0
is also a necessary and sufficient condition for
f
(irreducible) to be normal over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. This result was proved first proved by Pei et. al.
[
12
]
for
q=2
.

(iv) Chang et. al.
[
2
]
proved the converse of (ii) and (iii) in the following sense: If every degree
n
irreducible polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with nonzero trace is normal over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then
n
is either a power of
p\,(\,=\text{char}\,\mathbb{F}_{q})
or a prime different from
p
such that
q
is a generator of
(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.

5.
Number of Normal Polynomials

Let
N(q,n)
denote the number of monic normal polynomials of degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. This number is known; see Proposition
5.1
below. There are at least two proofs, given in
[
1
]
and
[
10
, Theorem 3.73]
, respectively. We include a short proof which only uses linear algebra. Let
p=\text{char}\,\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
n=p^{e}t
, where
e\geq 0
and
t>0
are integers such that
p\nmid t
. Let
X^{t}-1=Q_{1}\cdots Q_{k}
be the factorization of
X^{t}-1
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
and let
d_{i}=\deg Q_{i}
. The integers
d_{1},\dots,d_{k}
are the sizes of the orbits in
\mathbb{Z}/t\mathbb{Z}
under the multiplication by powers of
q
, i.e., the sizes of the
q
-cyclotomic cosets modulo
t
. For
d\mid t
, let
o_{d}(q)
denote the order of
q
in
(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. Then the multiset
\{d_{1},\dots,d_{k}\}
consists of
o_{d}(q)
with multiplicity
\phi(d)/o_{d}(q)
for all
d\mid t
.

Proposition 5.1
.

In the above notation, we have

N(q,n)=\frac{1}{n}q^{(p^{e}-1)t}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(q^{d_{i}}-1)=\frac{1}{n}q^{(p^{e}-1)t}\prod_{d\mid t}(q^{o_{d}(q)}-1)^{\phi(d)/o_{d}(q)}.

Proof.

Let
\rho
be the Frobenius of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
, that is,
\rho(x)=x^{q}
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Then
\rho
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear map from
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
to itself. The minimal polynomial of
\rho
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is
X^{n}-1=\prod_{i=1}^{k}Q_{i}^{p^{e}}
, so the elementary divisors of
\rho
are
Q_{i}^{p^{e}}
,
1\leq i\leq k
. Therefore, the matrix of
\rho
is similar to

M:=\left[\begin{matrix}M_{1}\cr&\ddots\cr&&M_{k}\end{matrix}\right],

where
M_{i}
is the companion matrix of
Q_{i}^{p^{e}}
. We know that
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
is a root of a normal polynomial of degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
g(\rho)(x)\neq 0
for all
g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
with
g\mid X^{n}-1
and
g\neq X^{n}-1
. Identify
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
with
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
which is compatible with the above matrix
M
. Then
N(q,n)=|\mathcal{X}|/n
, where

\mathcal{X}=\{x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}:g(M)x\neq 0\ \text{for all $g\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]$ with $g\mid X^{n}-1$ and $g\neq X^{n}-1$}\}.

For

x=\left[\begin{matrix}x_{1}\cr\vdots\cr x_{k}\end{matrix}\right]\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},

where
x_{i}
is of length
p^{e}d_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle g(M)x\neq 0\ \text{for all $g\mid X^{n}-1$ with $g\neq X^{n}-1$}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\

\displaystyle\Bigl(\frac{X^{n}-1}{Q_{i}}\Bigr)(M)x\neq 0\ \text{for all $1\leq i\leq k$}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\

\displaystyle\Bigl(\frac{X^{n}-1}{Q_{i}}\Bigr)(M_{i})x_{i}\neq 0\ \text{for all $1\leq i\leq k$}.

By
[
6
, Lemma 6.11]
,

\text{nullity}\,\Bigl(\Bigl(\frac{X^{n}-1}{Q_{i}}\Bigr)(M_{i})\Bigr)=\text{nullity}\,(Q_{i}^{p^{e}-1}(M_{i}))=\deg Q_{i}^{p^{e}-1}=(p^{e}-1)d_{i}.

Hence

|\mathcal{X}|=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(q^{p^{e}d_{i}}-q^{(p^{e}-1)d_{i}})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}q^{(p^{e}-1)d_{i}}(q^{d_{i}}-1)=q^{(p^{e}-1)t}\prod_{i=1}^{k}(q^{d_{i}}-1).

∎

6.
Conclusions and Final Remarks

Theoretically, for each
n\geq 1
, there exists a polynomial
h_{n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
such that a separable irreducible polynomial
f(X)=X^{n}+a_{1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n}
over any field
F
with a cyclic Galois group is normal over
F
if
h_{n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\neq 0
. When
\text{char}\,F=p>0
, there is also a
p
-ary version
h_{p,n}(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
with the same property. The polynomials
h_{n}
and
h_{p,n}
are difficult to compute in general. Explicit computation of
h_{n}
and
h_{p,n}
can be achieved only for small
n
. Once
h_{n}
(
h_{p,n}
) is determined, it can be applied to separable irreducible polynomials of degree
n
over any field (any field of characteristic
p
) with a cyclic Galois group. Experiments indicate that the sufficient condition for the normality of polynomials over finite fields provided by
h_{p,n}
are close to being necessary.

The following two questions arise naturally from the approach of the paper:

1. If we are interested in normal polynomials over finite fields with few terms, then the corresponding question is to compute
h_{p,n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
, where most of the coefficients
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
are zero. It turns out that there is an indirect way to compute such
h_{p,n}(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
for slightly larger
n
.

2. The polynomial
h_{n}
applies to separable irreducible polynomials of degree
n
over any field with a cyclic Galois group. More generally, we may consider separable irreducible polynomials of degree
n
with an arbitrary Galois group
G
of order
n
. Then the analogy of the polynomial
\Delta_{n}
is the
group determinant

\Theta_{G}
of
G
considered by Frobenius; see
[
5
,
8
]
. The factors of
\Theta_{G}
correspond to the irreducible characters of
G
, and the symmetrizations of the factors of
\Theta_{G}
can be determined at least in theory.

We plan to discuss the above questions in detail in a separate paper.

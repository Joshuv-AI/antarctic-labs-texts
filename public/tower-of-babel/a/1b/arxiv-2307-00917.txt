A graph for which the second largest distance eigenvalue is less than − 3 + 5 2 \frac{-3+\sqrt{5}}{2} is chordal
Haiyan Guo, Bo Zhou

Abstract
Let G G be a connected graph with vertex set V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) . The distance, d G ​ ( u , v ) d_{G}(u,v) , between vertices u u and v v in G G is defined as the length of a shortest path between u u and v v in G G . The distance matrix of G G is the matrix D ⁡ ( G ) = ( d G ​ ( u , v ) ) u , v ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) D(G)=(d_{G}(u,v))_{u,v\in V(G)} . The second largest distance eigenvalue of G G is the second largest one in the spectrum of D ⁡ ( G ) D(G) . We show that any connected graph with the second largest distance eigenvalue less than − 3 + 5 2 \frac{-3+\sqrt{5}}{2} is chordal, and characterize those bicyclic graphs and split graphs with the second largest distance eigenvalue less than − 1 2 -\frac{1}{2} . The distance eigenvalues of a connected graph are the eigenvalues of its distance matrix.
The distance eigenvalues of graphs were first studied by Graham and Pollak

As far as we know, the only studies on the second largest distance eigenvalue of graphs are as follows. Lin
[

showed that the second largest distance eigenvalue of a graph

when the independent number is less than or equal to two, confirming a conjecture in
[

[

characterized all connected graphs with the second largest distance eigenvalue less than

In

A graph is chordal if every cycle of length at least four has a chord, where
a chord is an edge joining two non-adjacent vertices of the cycle.
A connected graph on
n
vertices with
n+1
edges is called a bicyclic graph. A graph

G
and
\overline{G}
are chordal.
In this paper, we show that any connected graphs whose second largest distance eigenvalue is less than
-\frac{1}{2}
must be chordal and characterize all bicyclic graphs and split graphs with the second largest distance eigenvalue less than
-\frac{1}{2}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
G
is a graph with
\lambda_{2}(G)<-\frac{1}{2}
, then
G
is chordal.

Proof.

We prove the theorem by contradiction. Suppose that it is not true. Then there exists a graph
G
with
\lambda_{2}(G)<-\frac{1}{2}
but
G
is not chordal. So there is an induced cycle in
G
with length at least four. We choose such an induced cycle
C_{k}
of
G
so that its length
k
is minimum. For odd
k\geq 5
,
-\frac{1}{4}\sec^{2}\frac{\pi}{k}
is strictly increasing. By Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
,
C_{k}
is not a distance-preserving subgraph of
G
, and in particular,
k\geq 6
.
So there are two non-adjacent vertices, say
u
and
v
, on
C_{k}
such that
d_{C_{k}}(u,v)>d_{G}(u,v)
. Let

\mathcal{C}=\{\{u,v\}:d_{C_{k}}(u,v)>\max\{d_{G}(u,v),1\},u,v\in V(C_{k})\}.

Assume that
\{x,y\}\in\mathcal{C}
so that
d_{G}(x,y)
is minimum.
Let
s=d_{G}(x,y)
and
t=d_{C_{k}}(x,y)
.
Since
C_{k}
is an induced cycle of
G
, we have
2\leq s<t\leq\frac{k}{2}
.

Let
P=x_{0}x_{1}\dots x_{s}
be a shortest path between
x
and
y
in
G
, where
x=x_{0}
and
y=x_{s}
.
Let
Q=y_{0}y_{1}\dots y_{t}
be the path on
C_{k}
from
x
to
y
with length
t
, where
x=y_{0}
and
y=y_{t}
.
Let
G^{\prime}=G[V(P)\cup V(Q)]
. Note that
G^{\prime}
contains a Hamiltonian cycle
C=x_{0}x_{1}\dots x_{s}y_{t-1}\dots y_{1}x_{0}
.

Claim 1.

G^{\prime}
is chordal.

Note that
|V(G^{\prime})|=s+t<2t\leq k
. Thus, if
G^{\prime}
contains an induced cycle, then its length is less than
k
, so its length must be three by the choice of
C_{k}
. Thus
G^{\prime}
is chordal. Claim 1 follows.

By the choice of
C_{k}
and
P
,
any chord of
C
joins a vertex from
V(Q)\setminus\{x,y\}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{t-1}\}
and a vertex from
V(P)\setminus\{x,y\}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{s-1}\}
.

Claim 2.

x_{1}y_{1}\in E(G^{\prime})
.

If
x_{1}y_{1}\notin E(G^{\prime})
, then there is an induced cycle of length at least four (and less than
k
) containing
x
in
G^{\prime}
, contradicting Claim 1. This proves Claim 2.

By Claim 2,
G^{\prime}-x_{0}
contains a Hamiltonian cycle
C^{\prime}=x_{1}\dots x_{s}y_{t-1}\dots y_{1}x_{1}
.
By Claims 1 and 2,
G^{\prime}-x_{0}
is chordal.

Case 1.

C^{\prime}
has a chord incident with
x_{1}
.

Since
G^{\prime}-x_{0}
is chordal, it has no induced cycle of length at least four, so
x_{1}y_{2}\in E(G^{\prime})
. If
x_{1}y_{3}\in E(G^{\prime})
, then

G^{\prime}[\{x_{0},x_{1},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\}]\cong F_{11}
, which is a forbidden subgraph, a contradiction. This shows that
t\geq 4
and
x_{1}y_{3}\notin E(G^{\prime})
.
Then, step by step, we have
x_{1}y_{i}\notin E(G^{\prime})
for
i=4,\dots,t-1
, as, otherwise, there is an induced cycle of length at least four in
G^{\prime}
, contradicting Claim 1. If
s=2
, then
y_{2}y_{3}\dots y_{t}x_{1}y_{2}
is an induced cycle of length at least four in
G^{\prime}
, contradicting Claim 1. So
s\geq 3
.

Case 1.1

x_{2}y_{1}\in E(G^{\prime})
.

If
x_{2}y_{2}\notin E(G^{\prime})
, then
G^{\prime}[\{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\}]\cong F_{12}
, which is a forbidden subgraph, a contradiction. So
x_{2}y_{2}\in E(G^{\prime})
.

If
x_{2}y_{3}\in E(G^{\prime})
, then
G^{\prime}[\{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}\}]\cong F_{13}
, which is a forbidden subgraph, also a contradiction. So
x_{2}y_{3}\notin E(G^{\prime})
. Step by step, we have
x_{2}y_{i}\notin E(G^{\prime})
for
i=4,\dots,t-1
if
t\geq 5
. So
x_{2}y_{i}\notin E(G^{\prime})
for
3\leq i\leq t-1
.

Next, we claim that
x_{3}y_{i}\notin E(G^{\prime})
for
1\leq i\leq t-1
if
s\geq 4
. Otherwise,
the subgraph of
G^{\prime}[\{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},y_{1}\}]\cong F_{11}
if
x_{3}y_{1}\in E(G^{\prime})
,
the subgraph of
G^{\prime}[\{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},y_{1},y_{2}\}]\cong F_{13}
if
x_{3}y_{2}\in E(G^{\prime})
, and there is an induced cycle of length at least four in
G^{\prime}
if
x_{3}y_{i}\in E(G^{\prime})
for
3\leq i\leq t-1
, so we have a contradiction in any case.
Step by step, we have
x_{i}y_{j}\notin E(G^{\prime})
for
4\leq i\leq s-1
and
1\leq j\leq t-1
. So
G^{\prime}[V(G^{\prime})\setminus\{x_{0},x_{1},y_{1}\}]
is an induced cycle of
G^{\prime}
with length at least four, a contradiction.

Case 1.2

x_{2}y_{1}\notin E(G^{\prime})
.

In this case,
x_{2}y_{2}\notin E(G^{\prime})
. Otherwise,
G^{\prime}[\{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\}]\cong F_{11}
, which is a forbidden subgraph, a contradiction. Step by step, we have
x_{2}y_{i}\notin E(G^{\prime})
for
3\leq i\leq t-1
, and
x_{i}y_{j}\notin E(G^{\prime})
for
3\leq i\leq s-1
and
1\leq j\leq t-1
if
s\geq 4
. So
G^{\prime}[V(G^{\prime})\setminus\{x_{0},y_{1}\}]
is an induced cycle of
G^{\prime}
with length at least four, a contradiction.

Case 2.

C^{\prime}
has no chord incident with
x_{1}
.

Suppose that
t=3
. Then
s=2
. In the case of
t=3
or
s=2
,
G^{\prime}[V(G^{\prime})\setminus\{x_{0}\}]
is an induced cycle with length at least four, a contradiction. So
s\geq 3
and

.

Since
G^{\prime}-x_{0}
is chordal,
x_{2}y_{1}\in E(G^{\prime})
.

We claim that
x_{2}y_{i}\notin E(G^{\prime})
for
2\leq i\leq t-1
. Otherwise,
G^{\prime}[\{x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\}]\cong F_{11}
if
x_{2}y_{2}\in E(G^{\prime})
, and there is an induced cycle of length at least four if

for
3\leq i\leq t-1
, so we have a contradiction in either case. Step by step, we have
x_{i}y_{j}\notin E(G^{\prime})
for
3\leq i\leq s-1
and

. So

Combing case 1 and 2, we complete the proof.
∎

s\geq 7
,
f(-\infty)<0
. It follows that the second largest root
\lambda^{(2)}
of
f(\lambda)=0
satisfies
-\frac{s+1}{2s}<\lambda^{(2)}<-\frac{1}{2}
for large enough
s
. Thus,
\lambda^{(2)}
is the second largest distance eigenvalue of
G
, i.e.,
\lambda_{2}(G)<-\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

For a graph
G
and its subgraph
H
and a vertex
v
of
G
outside
H
, let
N_{H}(v)=N_{G}(v)\cap V(H)
and
d_{H}(v)=|N_{H}(v)|
.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
5.1
.

Proof of Theorem

.

Let
G
be a split graph of order
n=s+t
. Let

be the maximum clique and
I
be the independent set in
G
of size

. Then
s\geq 2
.

If
I=\emptyset
, then
G\cong K_{s}=K_{s}(0)
and
\lambda_{2}(G)=-1<-\frac{1}{2}
.

Suppose that
I\neq\emptyset
.

Suppose first that
s=2
. Let
V(K_{2})=\{u,v\}
. Assume that
d_{G}(u)\geq d_{G}(v)
. Since

F_{1}
being forbidden. Thus

or
K_{2}(2,1)
. If
d_{G}(v)=1

By a direct calculation, we have
\lambda_{2}(K_{2}(2,1))\approx-0.5120<-\frac{1}{2}
. From Lemma
2.1
, we have
\lambda_{2}(K_{2}(1,1))<-\frac{1}{2}
and
\lambda_{2}(K_{2}(t))<-\frac{1}{2}
.

Suppose next that
s\geq 3
. Let
ID_{G}=\{z\in I:d_{K_{s}}(z)\geq 2\}
.

Claim.

|ID_{G}|=0,1
.

Otherwise, there exist

and
z_{2}
in

Suppose that

\{x_{1},x_{2}\}=\{y_{1},y_{2}\}
.
Note that
s\geq 3
. Since
K_{s}
is a maximum clique, there exist
x_{3},x_{4}\in V(K_{s})
such that
x_{3}z_{1}\notin E(G)
,
x_{4}z_{2}\notin E(G)
. Suppose that
x_{3}\neq x_{4}
. Then

. Otherwise,
G[\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},z_{2}\}]\cong K_{5}-e
, and
\lambda_{2}(K_{5}-e)\approx-0.4495>-\frac{1}{2}
, a contradiction. So either
x_{3}\neq x_{4}
and
x_{3}z_{2}\notin E(G)

x_{3}=x_{4}
. In either case,

Suppose that
|\{x_{1},x_{2}\}\cap\{y_{1},y_{2}\}|=1

By the above claim,
|ID_{G}|=0,1
.

Case 1.

|ID_{G}|=1
.

Let
ID_{G}=\{z\}
.

If
s=3
, then

is a

-bicyclic graph, so by Lemma
4.2
, we have

Assume that
s\geq 4
. Then
d_{K_{s}}(z)=2
. Otherwise, there is a distance-preserving subgraph isomorphic to

\lambda_{2}(K_{5}-e)\approx-0.4495>-\frac{1}{2}
, a contradiction.
Suppose that
s\geq 5
. Then there is a distance-preserving subgraph, say
H
, induced by
V(K_{5})\cup\{z\}
. By a direct calculation, we have
\lambda_{2}(H)\approx-0.4913>-\frac{1}{2}
, a contradiction.
It thus follows that
s=4
. Let
N_{K_{s}}(z)=\{u,v\}
with
d_{G}(u)\geq d_{G}(v)
. Let

. Since
F_{9}
is a forbidden graph,
d_{G}(w)=3

F_{2}

d_{G}(v)=5

6
Conclusions

In this article, we consider graphs for which the second largest distance eigenvalue is less than

-\frac{1}{2}
. We show that any such graph must be chordal and characterize all bicyclic graphs and split graphs with the desired property. Characterizing graphs with the desired property among graphs with forbidden subgraphs or minors would be a way to have a fuller understanding the behavior of the second largest distance eigenvalue.

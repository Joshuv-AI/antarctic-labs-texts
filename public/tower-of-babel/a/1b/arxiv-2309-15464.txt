On staticity of bifurcate Killing horizons Thanks: Vienna preprint UWThPh 2023-12 Thanks: Email marc@usal.es
Piotr T. Chruściel

Abstract
We show that bifurcate Killing horizons with closed torsion form, in spacetimes of arbitrary dimension satisfying a Ricci-structure condition, arise from static Killing vectors. The result applies in particular to Λ \Lambda -vacuum spacetimes.



1
Introduction

Stationary spacetimes are of particular physical importance as they describe equilibrium configurations of the gravitational field. The static subcase, where the spacetime is not only time independent but also invariant under time reversal, plays a particularly important role. It is therefore of interest to identify conditions that guarantee that a stationary spacetime is static. Recall that a stationary spacetime with a Killing vector field
\xi
is
static
if the twist form

\omega:=\frac{1}{2}\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\wedge d\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\,,

(1.1)

where
\xi
is the
1
-form
g(\xi,\cdot)
,
vanishes; such Killing vectors will also be called
static
. In situations where the spacetime is globally hyperbolic and the Killing vector
\xi
is timelike everywhere it is immediate to show that the spacetime is static if and only if the twist form vanishes on a Cauchy surface. This is a consequence of the fact that
\omega
is always Lie constant along the Killing field. However, in black hole configurations the Killing field is not timelike everywhere and finding necessary and sufficient conditions for staticity is more delicate.

One of the central notions in the study of stationary black hole spacetimes is that of Killing horizons
[
5
]
. Large families of examples have been constructed in
[
11
,
10
,
15
,
16
]
; see also
[
8
]
. This raises the question of staticity in terms of the properties of Killing horizons. In fact, the vanishing – or not – of the twist form plays an important role in the classification of stationary vacuum black holes, cf. e.g.
[
6
,
7
]
and references therein.

The aim of this work is to give a characterisation of static spacetimes containing a bifurcate Killing horizon in terms of the data on the horizon.
We note that
\omega
always vanishes on a Killing horizon
\mycal H
, so a criterion on a Killing horizon must involve at least one transverse derivatives on
\mycal H
.
Further, both
\omega
and its first derivatives vanish at the bifurcation surface of a bifurcate Killing horizon, so a criterion there must involve at least two transverse derivatives of
\omega
. The relevant fields are a priori not clear.

We aim at finding such a characterization independently both of the matter fields involved and of the field equations of the theory, i.e. a purely geometry characterization valid in full generality. Given that no field equations are imposed, it is clear that such a characterization will necessarily involve two types of conditions, namely a bulk condition at the level of curvature that holds everywhere and a boundary condition that holds on the horizon. In this paper we deal with bifurcate Killing horizons, so it is plausible to expect that the boundary conditions can be formulated directly on the bifurcation surface. We find necessary and sufficient conditions for staticity both for the bulk and for the boundary conditions. The bulk condition involves a one-form constructed from the Ricci tensor and the Killing field. Specifically, it demands that, on all points where the Killing vector is not null, the one-form, denoted by

g(\xi,\xi)r
, and
defined as the tangential-normal components of the Ricci tensor along the vector
\xi
, is closed. The boundary condition at the bifurcation surface is that the
torsion one-form
is closed.

It turns out that the analysis in the region to the causal future of the bifurcation surface of the bifurcate Killing horizon, and in its complement, is essentially different. This is related to the different causal character of the Killing orbits in these regions. The timelike-orbits region is analysed in Section
4.1
, while the space-like-orbits one in Section
4.2
.
The precise statements of our staticity results appear in Theorems
4.2
and
4.4
below. In addition to being independent of any field equations, our results hold in any spacetime dimension and for any topology of the bifurcation surface.

An interesting by-product of our just-mentioned staticity theorems
is that whenever the torsion one-form of the bifurcation surface is closed, the fact that the bulk one-form
g(\xi,\xi)r
is closed implies that
\xi
is an eigenvector of the Ricci tensor (equivalently, that the one-form

g(\xi,\xi)r
being closed implies that it vanishes everywhere, provided the torsion form of the bifurcations surface is closed).

Of course, of particular importance is the case where the spacetime is vacuum. In this case, the characteristic initial data for the spacetime on the bifurcate Killing horizon consists of the metric induced on
the bifurcation surface
S
together with the torsion
1
-form of
S
.
A special case of our results is the following theorem, where a non-vanishing cosmological constant is allowed,
with
\mycal H^{-}
denoting the part of
\mycal H
lying to the past of the bifurcation surface:

Theorem
1.1
.

Consider a

(n+1)
-dimensional vacuum spacetime
(M,g)
,
n\geq 2
,
containing a bifurcate Killing horizon
\mycal H
. Let
{\mycal D}^{-}(\mycal H^{-})
be the past domain of dependence of
\mycal H^{-}
in
M

and let
\mathring{M}

denote that connected component of the set

{\mycal D}^{-}(\mycal H^{-})\cup\{g(\xi,\xi)<0\}

(1.2)

which contains
{\mycal D}^{-}(\mycal H^{-})
.
Then
g
is static on
\mathring{M}
if and only if the torsion
1
-form of the bifurcation surface is closed.

Remark
1.2
.

As already said, we show in fact that a necessary and sufficient condition for staticity is closedness of the torsion together with

d\big(g(\xi,\xi)\hat{r}\big)=0\,,

(1.3)

where
\hat{r}
is the quotient-space equivalent of the covector field
r_{\alpha}dx^{\alpha}
appearing in the decomposition (
2.11
) of the Ricci tensor.
This condition will of course hold in vacuum, but it will also hold for, e.g., a perfect fluid supported in the region exterior to a black hole and comoving with the Killing flow.

\Box

Remark
1.3
.

Our analysis below likewise shows that a vacuum spacetime metric will be static if and only if (
1.3
) holds and the first derivatives of
\omega
vanish on one of the branches of a bifurcate horizon
\mycal H
. This statement, however, does not seem to have an obvious formulation in terms of characteristic Cauchy data on
\mycal H
.

\Box

At the heart of the proof of Theorem
1.1
lies the derivation of an equation for a relevant field, to which the unique continuation results of Mazzeo
[
14
]
and the uniqueness results of Fuchsian symmetric hyperbolic equations apply.
Another ingredient is a calculation tying the twist of the Killing vector to the torsion
1
-form of the horizon.
In order to finish the proof we need to treat separately the region
{\mycal D}(S)
, where the Killing vector is spacelike, and
its complement in
\mathring{M}
.
Our claim on
{\mycal D}(\mycal H)
is obtained from uniqueness of solutions of Fuchsian hyperbolic equations; this is the vacuum case of Theorem
4.4
below.
The result in the complement of
{\mycal D}(\mycal H)
in
\mathring{M}
is the vacuum case of Theorem
4.2
below; it is based on the observation, which might have some interest of its own (cf. Proposition
3.1
below), that the bifurcation surface of a Killing horizon can be viewed as the conformal boundary at infinity of a natural rescaling of the quotient space-metric.

We note that in real-analytic spacetimes the result can be established by Taylor expansion at the horizon; this requires a similar computational effort. We further note that while a vacuum spacetime metric will be real analytic near a Killing horizon in many situations of interest
[
3
,
4
]
,
Theorems
4.2
and
4.4
neither assume vacuum nor analyticity.

\displaystyle\Omega=-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{2}d\left(\lambda^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right),\qquad\mbox{on }\quad{M^{*}}

This is equation (B.10) in Appendix B of

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\nabla_{\beta}\xi_{\gamma}=\xi_{\sigma}R^{\sigma}{}_{\alpha\beta\gamma}.

and

The operation

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}T_{\beta_{1}\cdots\beta_{p}}:=h^{\gamma}{}_{\alpha}h^{\delta_{1}}{}_{\beta_{1}}\cdots h^{\delta_{p}}{}_{\beta_{p}}\nabla_{\gamma}T_{\delta_{1}\cdots\delta_{p}}

We already noted that

\xi

Exterior differentiation commutes with the operation of descending to the quotient for forms that descend (this is clearly seen in local coordinates adapted to the isometries),

\displaystyle d\left(\lambda^{-2}\widehat{\Omega}\right)=0.

2

3
Rácz-Wald coordinates

We wish to tie the quantities introduced above to objects defined on the bifurcation surface of a bifurcate Killing horizon. For this a convenient
set of coordinates is the one due to Rácz and Wald
[
17
,
18
]
. Such coordinates can be introduced in either of the following circumstances:

H1.

The spacetime contains a bifurcate Killing horizon.

H2.

The spacetime has a null boundary
{\mycal N}
with a causal Killing vector
\xi
which is tangent to the generators of
{\mycal N}
, with non-zero surface gravity. The orbits of
\xi
are complete and there exists a hypersurface
\mycal S
to which
\xi
is transverse and such that every orbits of
\xi
intersects
\mycal S
precisely once.

In the latter case, which is typical for well-behaved domains of outer communications, one constructs
[
17
]
an auxiliary spacetime which contains a bifurcate Killing horizon. So, in this sense, it suffices to consider spacetimes
({M},g)
which contain a bifurcate Killing horizon, i.e. two smooth null connected hypersurfaces

\mathcal{H}^{+}
and
\mathcal{H}^{-}
intersecting on a spacelike codimension-two surface

S=\mathcal{H}^{+}\cap\mathcal{H}^{-}
and such that
({M},g)
admits a non-trivial
Killing vector
\xi
which vanishes on
S
. It follows automatically that

\mathcal{H}^{\pm}\setminus S
are Killing horizons of
\xi
with two connected components each.

A spacetime neighbourhood
\mycal O

of
S
can be coordinatised with an atlas of

Rácz-Wald coordinates

\{U,V,x_{\mathfrak{i}}^{a}\}
, where
\{x^{a}_{\mathfrak{i}},O_{\mathfrak{i}}\}
is an atlas of
S
. In such coordinates, the metric
g
takes the form

\displaystyle g=GdU\left(dV+Vq_{a}dx^{a}\right)+\gamma_{ab}dx^{a}dx^{b}\,,

(3.1)

where all metric coefficients
G,q_{a},\gamma_{ab}
depend only upon
\{UV,x^{a}\}
,
and where
G
is a positive function. The bifurcation surface
S
is the set

\{U=V=0\}
.
We choose the time orientation so that
\partial_{U}
is future pointing and
\partial_{V}
is past pointing. The construction of
[
17
]
guarantees that there exists a neighborhood of the bifurcate Killing horizon on which
G
is strictly positive.

Recall that the torsion
1
-form of a spacelike surface of codimension equal to two is defined as

\displaystyle\zeta(X)=g(k,\nabla_{X}\ell)

where
X
is a vector field in
S
and
\{k,\ell\}
are a pair of null normals to
S
satisfying the normalization condition

. The null normals

can be boosted or interchanged. The boost transforms the torsion by adding the differential of a function,
while the interchange

. Note that the property of
\zeta
being closed if not affected by these changes.

In what follows we shall need the explicit form of

\displaystyle k=-\sqrt{\frac{2}{G}}\partial_{V}\,,\qquad\ell=\sqrt{\frac{2}{G}}\partial_{U}\,,

and after a simple calculation based on the metric (

\displaystyle\zeta=\frac{1}{2}q_{a}|_{\{UV=0\}}dx^{a}\,.

(3.2)

In Rácz-Wald coordinates

\displaystyle\xi=U\partial_{U}-V\partial_{V}.

(3.3)

and therefore

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}:=g(\xi,\cdot)=\frac{1}{2}G\left(UdV-VdU+UVq_{a}dx^{a}\right)\,.

(3.4)

We shall also need the explicit form of the twist
3

\xi

\omega:=\frac{1}{2}\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\wedge d\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\,.

For this it is simplest to work away from the bifurcate Killing horizon and write

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}=\frac{1}{2}GUV\left(d\ln\left|\frac{V}{U}\right|-q_{a}dx^{a}\right)=:\frac{1}{2}GUV\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}.

Since
q_{a}=q_{a}(UV,x^{b})
, a direct calculation gives, where a prime denotes a derivative with respect to

\displaystyle\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}\wedge d\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}=

\displaystyle 2q^{\prime}_{c}dU\wedge dV\wedge dx^{c}+q^{\prime}_{a}q_{c}\left(UdV+VdU\right)\wedge dx^{a}\wedge dx^{c}

\displaystyle-\left(\partial_{a}q_{b}-\partial_{b}q_{a}\right)\left(\frac{dV}{V}-\frac{dU}{U}\right)\wedge dx^{a}\wedge dx^{b}+q_{c}(\partial_{a}q_{b}-\partial_{b}q_{a})dx^{c}\wedge dx^{a}\wedge dx^{b}

\displaystyle\omega=\,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}G^{2}U^{2}V^{2}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}\wedge d\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8}G^{2}\left(\partial_{a}q_{b}-\partial_{b}q_{a}\right)\left(U^{2}VdV-V^{2}UdU\right)\wedge dx^{a}\wedge dx^{b}+U^{2}V^{2}\widetilde{\omega}\,,

where

is a smooth

The transverse derivative

\displaystyle\pounds_{\partial_{V}}\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}|_{\{V=0\}}=-\frac{1}{8}G^{2}\left(\partial_{a}q_{b}-\partial_{b}q_{a}\right)|_{UV=0}U^{2}dV\wedge dx^{a}\wedge dx^{b}.

This

-form

\{U=0\}
.

Recall that

denotes the Lorentzian norm-squared of the Killing vector

Since

a straightforward computation gives

\displaystyle h=\frac{G}{4UV}\left((UdV+VdU)^{2}+U^{2}V^{2}(q_{a}dx^{a})^{2}+2UV(UdV+VdU)q_{a}dx^{a}\right)+\gamma_{ab}dx^{a}dx^{b}.

We introduce the scalar

descend to local coordinates on the quotient space (both are Lie-constant along
\xi

\displaystyle h=\frac{G}{4s}\left(ds+sq_{a}dx^{a}\right)^{2}+\gamma_{ab}dx^{a}dx^{b},\qquad\mbox{and}\ \lambda=sG

where

are functions of
\{s,x^{a}\}
. Let us perform the coordinate change

and note that

where the Killing is timelike, while

corresponds to the domain

In the coordinates

, the quotient metric is

\displaystyle h=\epsilon G\big(dz+\frac{1}{2}zq_{a}dx^{a}\big)^{2}+\gamma_{ab}dx^{a}dx^{b},\qquad\lambda=\epsilon z^{2}G

where
G,q_{a},\gamma_{ab}
are functions of
\{\epsilon z^{2},x^{a}\}
.
This defines a metric smooth-up-to the boundary

positive

\mycal O
.
Clearly

is Riemannian when

, i.e. in the region where the Killing vector field

is timelike, and Lorentzian when

Let

be defined by

\lambda=\epsilon u^{2}\,,\quad\epsilon=\pm 1\,.

(3.6)

The function

, so it is smooth-up-to-and-including the boundary, where it has a zero of first order. Thus

The metric

becomes the conformal boundary at timelike infinity for the metric
\bar{h}
. This property will be exploited in what follows.

Perhaps unexpectedly,

is constant on
\partial\Sigma
.
To see this it is convenient to introduce

so that

, and raise indices
a,b

defined to be the inverse of

. The inverse metric

is given by

\displaystyle h^{\sharp}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{\epsilon}{P^{2}}+\frac{1}{4}z^{2}q^{a}q_{a}&-\frac{1}{2}zq^{a}\\
-\frac{1}{2}zq^{a}&\gamma^{ab}\\
\end{array}\right).

Thus

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z}\mathrm{grad}_{h}z=\frac{\epsilon}{zP^{2}}\partial_{z}+O(1),\qquad\frac{1}{u}\mathrm{grad}_{h}u=\frac{1}{z}\mathrm{grad}_{h}z+\frac{1}{P}\mathrm{grad}_{h}P=\frac{\epsilon}{zP^{2}}\partial_{z}+O(1),

where

Using the fact that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{z}\left<\mathrm{grad}_{h}z,\mathrm{grad}_{h}P\right>=O(1)\,,

where

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\mathrm{grad}_{h}u,\mathrm{grad}_{h}u\rangle}{u^{2}}=\frac{\langle\mathrm{grad}_{h}z,\mathrm{grad}_{h}z\rangle}{z^{2}}+O(1)=\frac{\epsilon}{z^{2}P^{2}}+O(1)=\frac{\epsilon}{u^{2}}+O(1)

(3.7)

This has the well known
geometric interpretation that

Proposition

.

In the region
\epsilon>0
the metric

is asymptotically locally hyperbolic, i.e., it is smoothly conformally compactifiable, with sectional curvatures tending to a negative constant when the conformal boundary at infinity

\Box

Hence

and, in the coordinates

\widehat{\Xi}_{ij}=-\frac{1}{4}\left(\partial_{i}q_{j}-\partial_{j}q_{i}\right)\,,\qquad q_{i}:=\delta^{a}{}_{i}q_{a}\,,

i.e.

Since

, all odd

\Box

4
Staticity

We are ready now to provide necessary and sufficient conditions for staticity of the spacetime metric. It turns out that the sides of the Killing horizons where the Killing vector is timelike and these where the Killing vector is spacelike require separate treatment.

4.1
The timelike region

We start by considering the region where the Killing vector is timelike, which corresponds to
\epsilon=1
. However, all local calculations in this section apply as long as the Killing vector is not null, and will also be relevant for Section
4.2
.

Using the notation
of
[
12
]
,
it follows that
(\Sigma,\bar{h})
has a compact conformal boundary at infinity and that
\Delta_{\bar{h}}
is a uniformly degenerate elliptic operator.
We also use the notion of indicial map
I_{\sigma}
as defined in

[
12
]
. Namely, given a uniformly degenerate elliptic operator

\displaystyle L:\Gamma(E)\longrightarrow\Gamma(E)

where
\Gamma(E)
is the set of sections of a tensor bundle
E
over
\Sigma
,
and using our function
u
as the defining function for the boundary
(which applies near horizons with non-zero surface gravity, but does not when the surface gravity vanishes),
for
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}

the indicial map is the map

\displaystyle I_{\sigma}:\Gamma(E|_{\partial\Sigma}\otimes\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Gamma(E|_{\partial\Sigma}\otimes\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\widehat{\Psi}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow I_{\sigma}(\widehat{\Psi}):=\lim_{u\to 0}\frac{L(u^{\sigma}\Psi)}{u^{\sigma}}\,,

where
\Gamma(E|_{\partial\Sigma})

is the set of sections over

\partial\Sigma
of the bundle
E
,
E|_{\partial\Sigma}\otimes\mathbb{C}
is its complexification and

\Psi\in\Gamma(E\otimes\mathbb{C})
is any smooth extension
of
\widehat{\Psi}
. The indicial map is a well-defined (i.e. independent of the choice of extension
\Psi
),
and is also independent of the choice of the defining function.
The indicial exponents of
L
are the
values
\sigma
for with there exists a point
p\in\partial\Sigma

and a non-zero
\widehat{\Psi}

such that
I_{\sigma}(\widehat{\Psi})|_{p}=0
.

We wish to prove a vanishing theorem for
\widehat{\Upsilon}^{(a)}
, for this we need to determine the indicial exponents for the operator (
2.25
).

Lemma
4.1
.

In the setup where
({M},g)
admits a bifurcate Killing horizon, consider the
quotient
\Sigma
in the region where
\xi
is timelike, i.e.

\epsilon=1
.
Define
L:=L_{a}
for
a=(n-1)/2
, cf. (
2.20
).
Then, the indicial
exponents of
L
are

\displaystyle\sigma\in\left\{a-4,a-3,a-1,a\right\}=\left\{\frac{n-1}{2}-4,\frac{n-1}{2}-3,\frac{n-1}{2}-1,\frac{n-1}{2}\right\}\,.

Proof:

Define (cf. Corollary
A.3
, Appendix
A
)

\displaystyle\bar{S}_{ij}{}^{kl}:=2u^{2}(R^{h})^{[k}{}_{i}{}^{l]}{}_{j}-\delta^{[k}{}_{j}B^{l]}{}_{i}-B^{[k}{}_{j}\delta^{l]}{}_{i}

so that
L=\Delta_{\bar{h}}+S
.
We apply item (ii) in Lemma
A.2
. Let

V^{i}:=D^{i}u|_{\partial\Sigma}

be the restriction of the gradient of
u
to
\partial\Sigma
.
Given that

\displaystyle B^{k}{}_{i}|_{\partial\Sigma}=\big((n-1)V^{k}V_{i}+\frac{n^{2}-2n-7}{8}|V|^{2}\big)\delta^{k}_{i}\big|_{\partial_{\Sigma}}

we have

\displaystyle\bar{S}_{ij}{}^{kl}|_{\partial\Sigma}\widehat{\Psi}_{kl}=(n-1)\left(V^{k}V_{i}\widehat{\Psi}_{kj}-V^{k}V_{j}\widehat{\Psi}_{ki}\right)+\frac{n^{2}-2n-7}{4}\widehat{\Psi}_{ij}

and
the indicial map is

\displaystyle I_{\sigma}(\widehat{\Psi})_{ij}=c_{1}|V|^{2}\widehat{\Psi}_{ij}+3\left(V^{k}V_{i}\widehat{\Psi}_{kj}-V_{k}V_{j}\widehat{\Psi}_{ki}\right),\quad c_{1}

\displaystyle:=\left(\sigma-\frac{n-1}{2}\right)\left(\sigma-\frac{n-9}{2}\right).

Given
p\in\partial\Sigma
we want to find the values
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}
and tensors

\widehat{\Psi}_{ij}
at
p
such that
I_{\sigma}(\widehat{\Psi})|_{p}
is zero.
Assuming that
I_{\sigma}(\widehat{\Psi})|_{p}
vanishes, a contraction of

I_{\sigma}(\widehat{\Psi})_{ij}
with
V^{i}
gives

\displaystyle(c_{1}+3)|V|^{2}_{h}V^{i}\widehat{\Psi}_{ij}=0.

If
c_{1}+3\neq 0
(i.e.
\sigma\not\in\{\frac{n-3}{2},\frac{n-7}{2}\}
)
then necessarily
V^{i}\widehat{\Psi}_{ij}=0
and the condition

I_{\sigma}(\widehat{\Psi})=0
reduces to
c_{1}\widehat{\Psi}_{ij}=0
. If in addition
c_{1}\neq 0
, the kernel is trivial. If
c_{1}=0
, i.e.
\sigma=\frac{n-1}{2}
or

\sigma=\frac{n-9}{2}
the kernel is non-trivial and consists of tensor fields taking the form
\widehat{\Psi}_{ij}=X_{i}Y_{j}-X_{j}Y_{i}
with covectors
X,Y
annihilating
V
; equivalently, the associated vectors are tangent to
\partial\Sigma
.

If
c_{1}+3=0
then

\widehat{\Psi}_{ij}:=X_{i}V_{j}-X_{j}V_{i}
(with
X
as before) solves

I_{\sigma}(\widehat{\Psi}_{ij})=0
:

\displaystyle I_{\sigma}(\widehat{\Psi}_{ij})

\displaystyle=I_{\sigma}(X_{i}V_{j}-X_{j}V_{i})

\displaystyle=c_{1}|V|^{2}_{h}(X_{i}V_{j}-X_{j}V_{i})+3|V|^{2}_{h}X_{i}V_{j}-3|V|^{2}_{h}X_{j}V_{i}

\displaystyle=(c_{1}+3)|V|^{2}_{h}\left(X_{i}V_{j}-X_{j}V_{i}\right)=0\,,

which concludes the proof.

\hfill\Box

This leads to:

Theorem
4.2
(Staticity extension, timelike region).

Assume that
({M},g)
contains a bifurcate Killing horizon
\mycal H
and consider the
quotient manifold
\Sigma
in the region where
\xi
is timelike. Assume that the Ricci tensor of
({M},g)

satisfies

(in particular this is true for

\Lambda
-vacuum spacetimes),
where the

\widehat{\Xi}_{ij}
vanishes on
\partial\Sigma

\mycal H

, where
\zeta
is the torsion
1
-form of
S
.

Remark
4.3
.

It further follows from (
3.5
) that, for bifurcate horizons, this is equivalent to the vanishing of the transverse derivative of
\omega
at any of the branches of the horizon.

\Box

Proof:

Clearly
\widehat{\Xi}
vanishes everywhere when the metric is static
(cf. (
2.3
), (

) and (

)), in particular on
\partial\Sigma
. Thus the condition is necessary.

To prove the converse implication, we start by noting that
for any

the vanishing of
\widehat{\Xi}
at

\partial\Sigma
is equivalent to
\widehat{\Upsilon}^{(a)}=o(u^{a})
at
\partial\Sigma
.

As already hinted-to, we choose

.
The operator

is elliptic, uniformly degenerate and self-adjoint with respect
to the

norm of the metric

. For such an operator

[
1

[

[
1

shows that

Matching coefficients in the polyhomogeneous expansion shows that

The vanishing of

The relation to the vanishing of

\Box

4.2
The spacelike region

We continue our analysis in the region where
\epsilon=-1
, thus

is a time function there. The aim is to find necessary and sufficient conditions for the twist to vanish in that region.

We consider a configuration where the fields are smooth to the causal past of the bifurcation surface, or sufficiently differentiable as needed in the argument below, where finite differentiability suffices. Such metrics can be constructed, for example, by solving the characteristic Cauchy problem with smooth KID data on a bifurcate null hypersurface, as described e.g. in
[

We formulate the staticity theorem in the past of a bifurcation surface, an identical result holds of course to the future:

Theorem

(Staticity extension, spacelike region).

Let

(n-1)

(n+1)
-dimensional spacetime

({M},g)

the Killing vector is static on the past domain of dependence

if and only if the tensor field

\widehat{\Xi}_{ij}

\partial\Sigma
, if and only if the torsion covector of

Proof:

The local calculations at
\partial\Sigma
implicit in the proof of Theorem

vanishes to all orders at

In the smooth case one can instead rewrite the Fuchsian wave equation
(

Appendix A
Appendix: Conformal behavior of the Laplacian on two-forms

Consider an
n
-dimensional pseudo-Riemannian
manifold
(\Sigma,h)
. Let
u
be a smooth positive function on
\Sigma

and consider the metric

\bar{h}:=u^{-2}h\,.

Objects defined with
\bar{h}
carry an overline. Indices on such objects are moved with
\bar{h}
while objects without an overline are manipulated using

h
.

The following lemma relates
\Delta_{h}
and

\Delta_{\bar{h}}
on two-forms.

Lemma
A.1
.

Let
\Psi
be a smooth two-form on
\Sigma
. Then

\displaystyle\Delta_{\bar{h}}\Psi_{ij}

\displaystyle=u^{2}\Delta_{h}\Psi_{ij}+2\left(u\Delta_{h}u+(2-n)|du|^{2}_{h}\right)\Psi_{ij}

\displaystyle+(4-n)\left(uD^{k}u\,D_{k}\Psi_{ij}+D^{k}uD_{i}u\,\Psi_{kj}-D^{k}uD_{j}u\,\Psi_{ki}\right)

\displaystyle-2u\left(D_{i}u(\delta\Psi)_{j}-D_{j}u(\delta\Psi)_{i}\right)+2uD^{k}u(d\Psi)_{kij}.

(A.1)

Before going into the proof we need some preliminaries. We write

Q
for the difference tensor
Q(X,Y):=\bar{D}_{X}Y-D_{X}Y
. Its explicit form is

\displaystyle Q^{k}{}_{il}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{u}\left(-\delta^{k}{}_{i}D_{l}u-\delta^{k}{}_{l}D_{i}u+D^{k}u\,h_{il}\right).

(A.2)

Computing
\Delta_{\bar{h}}
directly in terms of

\Delta_{h}
and the tensor
Q
leads to long computations. We take a shortcut
via the Weizenböck identity. It is convenient to
introduce the tensor

\displaystyle H^{k}{}_{ilj}=-2(R^{h})^{k}{}_{ilj}+\left(\delta^{k}{}_{l}(R^{h})_{ij}-\delta^{k}{}_{j}(R^{h})_{il}+(R^{h})^{k}{}_{l}h_{ij}-(R^{h})^{k}{}_{j}h_{il}\right)

(A.3)

and the corresponding tensor
\bar{H}^{k}{}_{ilj}
. The Weizenböck
identity (
2.18
) reads

\displaystyle\Delta_{h}\Psi_{ij}=-\Delta_{\mbox{\tiny dR}}\Psi_{ij}+H^{k}{}_{i}{}^{l}{}_{j}\Psi_{kl}

so we can relate
\Delta_{\bar{h}}
and
\Delta_{h}
by

\displaystyle\Delta_{\bar{h}}\Psi_{ij}=u^{2}\Delta_{h}\Psi_{ij}+\left(\bar{H}^{k}{}_{i}{}^{l}{}_{j}-u^{2}H^{k}{}_{i}{}^{l}{}_{j}\right)\Psi_{kl}-\left(\bar{\Delta}_{\mbox{\tiny dR}}\Psi_{ij}-u^{2}\Delta_{\mbox{\tiny dR}}\Psi_{ij}\right).

(A.4)

Let us momentarily assume
n\geq 3
, we will consider the case
n=2
shortly.
In terms of the Schouten tensor
L
,

\displaystyle(R^{h})_{ij}:=(n-2)L_{ij}+Lh_{jl},\quad L:=h^{ij}L_{ij}\,,

and letting
(C^{h})^{i}{}_{ijk}
be the Weyl tensor of
h
, we have the decomposition

\displaystyle H^{k}{}_{ilj}=-2(C^{h})^{k}{}_{ilj}-2(4-n)\left(\delta^{k}{}_{l}L_{ij}-\delta^{k}{}_{j}L_{il}+L^{k}{}_{l}h_{ij}-Lh_{il}\right)+2L\left(\delta^{k}{}_{l}h_{ij}-\delta^{k}{}_{j}h_{il}\right).

Under the conformal rescaling
\bar{h}=u^{-2}h
, the Schouten tensor transforms
as

\displaystyle\bar{L}_{ij}=L_{ij}+\frac{1}{u}D_{i}D_{j}u-\frac{|du|^{2}_{h}}{2u^{2}}h_{ij}.

This, combined with conformal invariance of the Weyl tensor leads to

\displaystyle\bar{H}^{k}{}_{i}{}^{l}{}_{j}-u^{2}H^{k}{}_{i}{}^{l}{}_{j}=h^{kl}W_{ij}-\delta^{k}{}_{j}W^{l}{}_{i}+W^{kl}h_{ij}-W^{k}{}_{j}\delta^{l}{}_{i}\,,

(A.5)

\displaystyle W_{ij}:=(n-4)uD_{i}D_{j}u+\left(u\Delta_{h}u+(2-n)|du|^{2}_{h}\right)h_{ij}\,.

(A.6)

Let us check that these equations also hold when
n=2
. Since the Riemann and Ricci tensors in two dimensions can be expressed in terms of the Gauss curvature and the metric, the tensor
H^{k}{}_{ilj}
defined in (
A.3
) is identically zero, and so is the difference left-hand side of (
A.5
). Next, in dimension
n=2
the tensor
W_{ij}
defined in (
A.6
) is trace-free. A tensor with the symmetries of a Riemann tensor in two-dimensions vanishes if and only if its double trace vanishes. The trace in

\{ij\}
and then
\{kl\}
of the right-hand side of (
A.5
) is

2\mbox{\rm tr}\,_{h}W
, hence zero. So, the right-hand side of (
A.5
) is also zero and the validity of the expression in dimension
n=2
is established.

In order to determine the conformal change of the de Rahm Laplacian we need to
relate the codifferentials
\bar{\delta}
and
\delta
. Using the expression

(\delta T)_{i_{2}\cdots i_{p}}=-D^{i_{1}}T_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}}

on any
p
-form, an easy calculation leads to

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}T=u^{2}(\delta T)+(n-2p)i_{u\mathrm{grad}_{h}u}T.

So, for a two-form
\Psi
we have

\displaystyle(d\circ\bar{\delta})\Psi

\displaystyle=d(u^{2}(\delta\Psi)+(n-4)i_{u\mathrm{grad}_{h}u}\Psi)=2udu\wedge(\delta\Psi)+u^{2}(d\circ\delta)\Psi+(n-4)d\circ i_{u\mathrm{grad}_{h}u}\Psi,

\displaystyle(\bar{\delta}\circ d)\Psi

\displaystyle=u^{2}(\delta\circ d)\Psi+(n-6)i_{u\mathrm{grad}_{h}u}\circ d\Psi\,,

and hence

\displaystyle\bar{\Delta}_{\mbox{\tiny dR}}\Psi-u^{2}\Delta_{\mbox{\tiny dR}}\Psi=2du\wedge(\delta\Psi)+(n-4)\pounds_{u\mathrm{grad}_{h}u}\Psi-2i_{u\mathrm{grad}_{h}u}\circ d\Psi\,,

(A.7)

after using Cartan’s identity
\pounds_{V}=d\circ i_{V}+i_{V}\circ d
.
With these identities at hand we may proceed with the proof of
Lemma
A.1
.

Proof:

Insert (
A.5
) and (
A.7
) into (
A.4
) to get

\displaystyle\Delta_{\bar{h}}\Psi_{ij}=

\displaystyle u^{2}\Delta_{h}\Psi_{ij}+W^{k}{}_{i}\Psi_{kj}-W^{k}{}_{j}\Psi_{ki}-(n-4)\pounds_{u\mathrm{grad}_{h}u}\Psi_{ij}

\displaystyle-2u\left(D_{i}u(\delta\Psi)_{j}-D_{j}u(\delta\Psi)_{i}\right)+2uD^{k}u(d\Psi)_{kij}.

Expanding the Lie derivative and using the definition (
A.6
)
of
W
yields (
A.1
).

\Box

To continue, we write down two simple identities.
The first one is the
well-known conformal behaviour of the Laplacian on functions

\displaystyle\Delta_{\bar{h}}f=u^{2}\Delta_{h}f+(2-n)uD^{i}uD_{i}f\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Delta_{\bar{h}}u=u^{2}\Delta_{h}u+(2-n)u|du|^{2}_{h}.

(A.8)

The second is an expression for
\bar{D}^{l}u\bar{D}_{l}\Psi_{ij}
. Using

D^{l}u\,Q^{k}{}_{il}=-u^{-1}|du|^{2}_{h}\delta^{k}{}_{i}

(which follows directly from (
A.2
)) we get

\displaystyle\bar{D}^{l}u\bar{D}_{l}\Psi_{ij}

\displaystyle=u^{2}D^{l}u\left(D_{l}\Psi_{ij}-Q^{k}{}_{il}\Psi_{kj}-Q^{k}{}_{jl}\Psi_{ik}\right)

\displaystyle=u^{2}D^{l}uD_{l}\Psi_{ij}+2u|du|^{2}_{h}\Psi_{ij}.

(A.9)

The next lemma analyses self-adjointness of certain second order operators and
their indicial maps. For this result we assume
that
\Sigma
is a manifold with boundary, that

h
is positive definite, and that
u
is a defining
function for the boundary, i.e.
u=0

and
|du|^{2}_{h}>0
on
\partial\Sigma
.

Lemma
A.2
.

With these assumptions, let
L(\Psi)_{ij}:=\Delta_{\bar{h}}\Psi_{ij}+\bar{S}_{ij}{}^{kl}\Psi_{kl}
, with
\bar{S}_{ij}{}^{kl}
smooth up to and including the boundary. Suppose that

\bar{S}_{ij}{}^{kl}=\bar{S}_{[ij]}{}^{kl}=\bar{S}_{ij}{}^{[kl]}
.
Then

(i)

L
is formally self-adjoint (with respect to the
L^{2}
-scalar product
with volume form defined by
\bar{h}
) if and only if

\displaystyle\bar{S}\,^{kl}{}_{ij}=\bar{S}_{ij}{}^{kl}.

(A.10)

(ii)

For
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}
, the indicial map
I_{\sigma}:\Lambda^{2}(\Sigma)|_{\partial\Sigma}\otimes\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\Lambda^{2}(\Sigma)|_{\partial\Sigma}\otimes\mathbb{C}
is

\displaystyle I_{\sigma}(\widehat{\Psi})_{ij}=

\displaystyle\left(2(2-n)+\sigma(5+\sigma-n)\right)|V|^{2}_{h}\widehat{\Psi}_{ij}

\displaystyle+(4-n)\left(V^{k}V_{i}\widehat{\Psi}_{kj}-V^{k}V_{j}\widehat{\Psi}_{ki}\right)+\bar{S}_{ij}{}^{kl}|_{\partial\Sigma}\widehat{\Psi}_{kl}

(A.11)

where
V^{i}:=D^{i}u|_{\partial\Sigma}
.

Proof:

Recall that the formal adjoint
L^{\dagger}
is the (unique) operator that
for any pair of two-forms
\bar{\Psi}_{ij}
,
\bar{\Phi}_{ij}
satisfies
(recall also that indices of objects with an overline are moved with
\bar{h}
)

\displaystyle\bar{\Psi}^{ij}L^{\dagger}(\bar{\Phi})_{ij}-\bar{\Phi}^{ij}L(\bar{\Psi})_{ij}=\bar{D}_{k}(\bar{F}^{k}(\bar{\Psi},\bar{\Phi},\bar{D}\bar{\Psi},\bar{D}\Phi))

for some vector
\bar{F}^{l}
. It is immediate that

\displaystyle L^{\dagger}(\bar{\Phi})_{ij}=\Delta_{\bar{h}}\bar{\Phi}_{ij}+\bar{S}^{kl}{}_{ij}\bar{\Phi}_{kl}

because

\displaystyle\bar{\Psi}^{ij}L^{\dagger}(\bar{\Phi})_{ij}-\bar{\Phi}^{ij}L(\bar{\Psi})_{ij}=\bar{D}_{k}\left(\bar{\Psi}^{ij}\bar{D}^{k}\bar{\Phi}_{ij}-\bar{\Psi}^{ij}\bar{D}^{k}\bar{\Phi}_{ij}\right)\,.

Consequently
L
is formally self-adjoint (
L^{\dagger}=L
) if and only if (
A.10
) holds. For item (ii) we compute
u^{-\sigma}\Delta_{\bar{h}}(u^{\sigma}\Psi)
and get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{u^{\sigma}}\Delta_{\bar{h}}(u^{\sigma}\Psi)_{ij}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\sigma(\sigma-1)}{u^{2}}\bar{D}^{k}u\bar{D}_{k}u+\frac{\sigma}{u}\Delta_{\bar{h}}u\right)\Psi_{ij}+\frac{2\sigma}{u}\bar{D}^{k}u\,\bar{D}_{k}\Psi_{ij}+\Delta_{\bar{h}}\Psi_{ij}

\displaystyle=\left(\sigma u\Delta_{h}u+\sigma(5+\sigma-n)|du|^{2}_{h}\right)\Psi_{ij}+2\sigma uD^{k}u\,D_{k}\Psi_{ij}+\Delta_{\bar{h}}\Psi_{ij}\,,

where in the second equality we inserted (
A.8
), (
A.9
) and
\bar{D}^{l}u\bar{D}_{l}u=u^{2}|du|^{2}_{h}
.
By Lemma
A.1
we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\bar{h}}\Psi_{ij}|_{u=0}=\left.2(2-n)|du|^{2}_{h}\Psi_{ij}+(4-n)\left(D^{k}uD_{i}u\,\Psi_{kj}-D^{k}uD_{j}u\,\Psi_{ki}\right)\right|_{u=0}.

The definition of indicial map gives (
A.11
).

\hfill\Box

The following corollary is used in the main text.

Corollary
A.3
.

Let
B_{ij}
be a symmetric tensor and
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, the operator

\displaystyle L(\Psi)_{ij}=\Delta_{\bar{h}}\Psi_{ij}+c(R^{h})^{k}{}_{i}{}^{l}{}_{j}\Psi_{kl}+B^{k}{}_{i}\Psi_{kj}-B^{k}{}_{j}\Psi_{ki}

is formally self-adjoint with respect to the
L^{2}
norm
of
\bar{h}
.

Proof:

One easily checks that the tensor

\displaystyle S_{ij}{}^{kl}:=c(R^{h})^{[k}{}_{i}{}^{l]}{}_{j}-\delta^{[k}{}_{j}B^{l]}{}_{i}-B^{[k}{}_{j}\delta^{l]}{}_{i}

is antisymmetric in
ij
(the antisymmetry in
kl
is obvious). In terms
of
S
we can rewrite

L(\Psi)_{ij}=\Delta_{\bar{h}}\Psi_{ij}+S_{ij}{}^{kl}\Psi_{kl}

The symmetry condition (
A.10
) can be checked with either

\bar{h}
or with
h
. We use
h
and find

\displaystyle S^{ij}{}_{kl}=c(R^{h})_{[k}{}^{i}{}_{l]}{}^{j}-\delta_{[k}{}^{j}B_{l]}{}^{i}-B_{[k}{}^{j}\delta_{l]}{}^{i}=c(R^{h})^{[i}{}_{k}{}^{j]}{}_{l}-\delta^{[i}{}_{l}B^{j]}{}_{k}-B^{[i}{}_{l}\delta^{j]}{}_{k}=S_{kl}{}^{ij}

after using the symmetries of the Riemann tensor and of
B
.

\hfill\Box

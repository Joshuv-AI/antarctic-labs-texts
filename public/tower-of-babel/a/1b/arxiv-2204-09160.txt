Statistical moments and integrability properties of monatomic gas mixtures with long range interactions
Ricardo Alonso, Hajer Orf

Abstract
. This document presents a priori estimates related to statistical moments and integrability properties for solutions of systems of monatomic gas mixtures modelled with the homogeneous Boltzmann equation with long range interactions for hard potentials. We detail the conditions for the generation and propagation of polynomial and exponential moments, and the integrability in Lebesgue spaces.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we study
a priori
estimates for the homogeneous Boltzmann system describing multi-component monatomic gas mixtures for binary interactions in three dimension. We focus in long range and hard interactions using techniques that have been developed recently for the scalar equation combining recent result and notation presented in the works
[
18
,
11
]
for the case of cutoff angular kernels. The Cauchy problem will be addressed elsewhere.

The method of proof follows a classical approach where the generation and propagation of polynomial and exponential moments use, at its core, a Povzner inequality associated to the binary interaction of different monatomic species. The Povzner inequality for monatomic species is similar to the scalar version, yet less symmetric. This loss of symmetry poses important difficulties that modify the classical mathematical treatment. The mathematical analysis at the level of moments, polynomial and exponential, follows ideas from refined versions for the scalar case given in
[
18
,
22
,
15
]
, however, it is worthwhile to mention that the moment analysis requires considering the system as a whole and not component by component independently. In other words, a global moment inequality has to be made for the system involving all species at once. This is particularly challenging for the case of exponential moment analysis which turns out to be subtle.

In a second stage, once the statistical moments are understood, a theory of generation and propagation of higher integrability norms is performed. We implement an approach given in
[
3
]
for the treatment of
L^{p}
-norm generation in the range
p\in(1,\infty]
. Interestingly, for higher integrability generation and propagation, each species of the gas mixture can be considered independently by interpreting the rest of the components as given. This is related to the fact that the type of scattering considered in this document is only forward; in such a case only statistical moments are needed to prove higher Lebesgue integrability generation and propagation.

2.
Preliminaries and Main Results

2.1.
Preliminaries

Let us consider a gas mixture of
I
species where each component of the mixture, namely
A_{i}
with
1\leq i\leq I
, is described by its distribution density function denoted with
f_{i}:=f_{i}(t,v)\geq 0
depending on time
t>0
and particle velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The evolution of the gas mixture
\mathbb{F}=[f_{i}]_{1\leq i\leq I}
satisfies the Boltzmann system

(2.1)

\partial_{t}\mathbb{F}(t,v)=\mathbb{Q}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F}),

where
\mathbb{Q}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F})
is the vector of collision operator defined for each component as

(2.2)

[\mathbb{Q}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F})]_{i}:=\sum_{j=1}^{I}Q_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j})\,.

Here the collision Boltzmann operators
Q_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j})
measure the influence that the species
f_{j}
exerts on the species
f_{i}
. Consequently, for any
i
fixed the distribution function
f_{i}
solves a Boltzmann equation where the collision operator takes into account the influence of all the species
A_{i}
as

(2.3)

\partial_{t}f_{i}(t,v)=[\mathbb{Q}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F})]_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{I}Q_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j})\,,\qquad 1\leq i\leq I\,.

2.1.1.
Collision process

Consider two interacting particles from the species
A_{i}
with associated particle mass
m_{i}>0
and
A_{j}
with associated particle mass
m_{j}>0
. The shorthand
(^{\prime}\!v_{ij},\,^{\prime}\!v_{*,ij})
denotes the particles velocities before collision. The dependence of such pre-collisional velocities with respect to the post-collisional velocities
(v,v_{*})
is given by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{}^{\prime}\!v_{ij}&=\frac{m_{i}v+m_{j}v_{*}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}+\frac{m_{j}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}|v-v_{*}|\sigma=v-\frac{2\,m_{j}\,u^{-}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}\\
{}^{\prime}\!v_{*,ij}&=\frac{m_{i}v+m_{j}v_{*}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}-\frac{m_{i}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}|v-v_{*}|\sigma=v_{*}+\frac{2\,m_{i}\,u^{-}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}\,.\end{split}

Here
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}^{2}
is the scattering direction and
u^{\pm}=\frac{u\pm|u|\sigma}{2}
. After collision, the species remain in the same species with the same associated mass. The collision laws (
2.4
) conserve momentum and kinetic energy, that is, the interactions are elastic. In this way one has that pre and post collisional velocities are interchangeable
(^{\prime}\!v_{ij},\,^{\prime}\!v_{*,ij})=(v^{\prime}_{ij},\,v^{\prime}_{*,ij})
and the conservation laws

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}m_{i}v^{\prime}_{ij}+m_{j}v^{\prime}_{*,ij}&=m_{i}v+m_{j}v_{*}\qquad\text{conservation of momentum}\,,\\
m_{i}|v^{\prime}_{ij}|^{2}+m_{j}|v^{\prime}_{*,ij}|^{2}&=m_{i}|v|^{2}+m_{j}|v_{*}|^{2}\qquad\text{conservation of energy}\,,\end{split}

are valid. Now, note that in the collision laws (
2.4
) (or in the conservation laws (
2.5
)) one can divide the laws by
\sum_{j=1}^{I}m_{j}
giving normalised component masses
\tilde{m}_{i}=\frac{m_{i}}{\sum_{j=1}^{I}m_{j}}\in(0,1)
, for each species
1\leq i\leq I
. For such reason, one can assume without loss of generality that the component masses add up to unity
\sum_{j=1}^{I}m_{j}=1.

Remark 2.1
.

To simplify notation we eliminate the subindices from the shorthand
(v^{\prime}_{ij},v^{\prime}_{*,ij})
and simply write
(v^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{*})
. Just keep in mind that the post and pre collisional relations will differ for each collision operators
Q_{ij}
.

2.1.2.
Collision operators

The collision operator
Q_{ij}
describing the binary interaction between the particles of species
A_{i}
, denoted by
f
, and the particles of species
A_{j}
, denoted by
g
, is explicitly defined by

(2.6)

Q_{ij}(f,g)(v)=\int\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}\big(f(v^{\prime})g(v^{\prime}_{*})-f(v)g(v_{*})\big)B_{ij}(|u|,\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)d\sigma dv,

where
u=v-v_{*}
is the relative velocity between interacting particles,
\widehat{u}=\frac{u}{|u|}
its direction,
\sigma=\frac{u^{\prime}}{|u^{\prime}|}
the scattering direction, and
B_{ij}
the collisional cross section. The
B_{ij}
are positive functions satisfying the
symmetric condition

B_{ij}=B_{ji}
and the following micro-reversibility assumption

(2.7)

B_{ij}(v,v_{*},\sigma)=B_{ij}(v^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{*},\sigma^{\prime})=B_{ij}(v_{*},v,-\sigma),

with
\sigma^{\prime}=\frac{u}{|u|}
.

In this document we concentrate our efforts in hard potential interactions without angular cutoff. More precisely, the collision cross sections
B_{ij}
, for
1\leq i,j\leq I
, are assumed to satisfy the explicit expression

(2.8)

B_{ij}(|u|,\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)=|u|^{\lambda_{ij}}b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma),\qquad\lambda_{ij}\in(0,2]\,,

where
b_{ij}
is the angular scattering kernel. Writing
\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma=\cos\theta
with
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, the angular scattering satisfies the condition

(2.9)

\exists\,\kappa^{1}_{ij},\kappa^{2}_{ij}\in(0,\infty)\qquad\kappa^{1}_{ij}\,\theta^{-s_{ij}-1}\leq\beta_{ij}(\theta)=\sin\theta\,b_{ij}(\cos\theta)\leq\kappa^{2}_{ij}\,\theta^{-s_{ij}-1},\quad s_{ij}\in(0,2).

The condition on the support of
b
implies that the regime we treat in this document is the so-called peaked forward scattering. Note that the symmetric condition implies that

\lambda_{ij}=\lambda_{ji}\qquad\text{and}\qquad b_{ij}=b_{ji}\quad(\text{that is}\;\,s_{ij}=s_{ji})\,.

The following parameters, related to the minimum and the maximum value of the potential rates
\lambda_{ij}
, will be important when considering the different properties of solutions,

\overline{\lambda}_{i}:=\min_{1\leq j\leq I}\lambda_{ij},\qquad\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}:=\max_{1\leq j\leq I}\lambda_{ij},

\overline{\lambda}:=\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}\lambda_{ij},\qquad\overline{\overline{\lambda}}:=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq I}\lambda_{ij},

and

\lambda^{\natural}:=\min_{1\leq i\leq I}\max_{1\leq j\leq I}\lambda_{ij}\,.

Additionally, we will consider the following parameters related to the angular singularities,

\overline{s}_{i}:=\min_{1\leq j\leq I}s_{ij},\qquad\overline{\overline{s}}_{i}:=\max_{1\leq j\leq I}s_{ij},

\overline{s}:=\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}s_{ij},\qquad\overline{\overline{s}}:=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq I}s_{ij}\,,

and

s^{\natural}:=\min_{1\leq i\leq I}\max_{1\leq j\leq I}s_{ij}.

Before continuing, let us mention that condition (
2.9
) is only needed in a vicinity of
\theta=0
. Away from this point
b
can be assumed just integrable. Additionally, let us remark that for the scalar Boltzmann equation the support of the angular scattering
b(\cos(\theta))
can be assumed in the upper scattering sphere
\theta\in(0,\pi/2]
with no loss of generality since
v^{\prime}\leftrightarrow v^{\prime}_{*}
under the change
\sigma\leftrightarrow-\sigma
. This is not the case for monatomic gas mixtures due to lack of symmetry in the collision laws. We point out, however, that the arguments and results of Section 3 are all valid, without modification, with a kernel
b(\cos(\theta))
defined in
(0,\pi]
. Morevover, from a physical perspective, the most generic assumption for the backscattering is
b(\widehat{e}\cdot\sigma)1_{\{\widehat{e}\cdot\sigma\leq 0\}}\in L^{1}_{\sigma}(\mathcal{S}^{2})
. In other words, terms associated to the backscattering are of lower order in comparison to the forward and can be included in the analysis of Section 4 at a technical price, which may slightly impact some of the arguments in this section. For the interested reader, we refer to
[
11
]
for the nuances of the cutoff scattering analysis.

2.1.3.
Functional spaces

First, we recall some notations and definitions that will be important along the manuscript.

\cdot

The mixture’s bracket form is defined as

\langle v\rangle_{i}:=\sqrt{1+\frac{m_{i}}{\sum_{j=1}^{I}m_{j}}|v|^{2}}=\sqrt{1+m_{i}\,|v|^{2}},\qquad\qquad v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,,\quad 1\leq i\leq I\,.

\cdot

The scalar
k^{th}
-polynomial moment, with
k\geq 0
, is defined for any
0\leq f\in L^{1}_{k}
as

{\bf m}_{k,i}[f](t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(t,v)\langle v\rangle_{i}^{k}dv\,,\qquad 1\leq i\leq I\,.

For the gas mixture
\mathbb{F}=[f_{i}]_{1\leq i\leq I}
we use the shorthand

{\bf m}_{k,i}(t)={\bf m}_{k,i}[f_{i}](t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}(t,v)\langle v\rangle_{i}^{k}dv\,,

that is, the subindex
i
in the shorthand
{\bf m}_{k,i}(t)
prescribes the bracket and the associate component in
\mathbb{F}
. The cumulative
k^{th}
moment of the gas mixture is defined as

{\bf m}_{k}[\mathbb{F}](t):=\sum_{i=1}^{I}{\bf m}_{k,i}[f_{i}](t)=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}(t,v)\langle v\rangle_{i}^{k}dv.

The Banach
L^{p}_{n}
spaces associated to the mixture are defined as follow, refer to
[
11
]
,

L^{p}_{n}=\Big\{\mathbb{F}=[f_{i}]_{1\leq i\leq I}\,\Big|\,\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(\langle v\rangle_{i}^{n}|f_{i}(v)|\Big)^{p}dv<\infty,\;n\geq 0,\;1\leq p<\infty\Big\}

with associated norm

\|\mathbb{F}\|_{L^{p}_{n}}:=\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(\langle v\rangle_{i}^{n}|f_{i}(v)|\Big)^{p}dv\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

For the special case
p=+\infty
, the space is defined as

L^{\infty}_{n}=\Big\{\mathbb{F}=[f_{i}]_{1\leq i\leq I}\,\Big|\,\sum_{i=1}^{I}{\rm ess}\,\sup\langle v\rangle_{i}^{n}|f_{i}(v)|<\infty,\;n\geq 0\Big\}

with associated norm

\|\mathbb{F}\|_{L^{\infty}_{n}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{I}{\rm ess}\sup\,\langle v\rangle_{i}^{n}|f_{i}(v)|\,.

The
L\log L
space is defined as

L\log L=\Big\{\mathbb{F}=[f_{i}]_{1\leq i\leq I}\,\Big|\,\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f_{i}(v)|\log\big(1+|f_{i}(v)|\big)dv<\infty\Big\}

with associated norm

\|\mathbb{F}\|_{L\log L}:=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f_{i}(v)|\log\big(1+|f_{i}(v)|\big)dv.

We also work with the Sobolev spaces

H^{k}:=\Big\{\mathbb{F}=[f_{i}]_{1\leq i\leq I}\,\Big|\,\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big|\big(1+(-\Delta)\big)^{\frac{k}{2}}f_{i}(v)\Big|^{2}dv<\infty,\;k\geq 0\Big\}\,,

endowed with the norm

\|\mathbb{F}\|_{H^{k}}=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big|\big(1+(-\Delta)\big)^{\frac{k}{2}}f_{i}(v)\Big|^{2}dv\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big(1+|\xi|^{2}\big)^{k}|\mathcal{F}(f_{i})(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

2.1.4.
Weak form of the Boltzmann collision operator

Testing the collision operator against a suitable test vector function
[\psi_{i}]_{1\leq i\leq I}
it holds that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j})(v)\psi_{i}(v)dv=\int\int\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}f_{i}(v)f_{j}(v_{*})\big(\psi_{i}(v^{\prime})-\psi_{i}(v)\big)B_{ij}(|u|,\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)d\sigma dv_{*}dv\,,

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q_{ji}(f_{j},f_{i})(v)\psi_{j}(v)dv=\int\int\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}f_{j}(v)f_{i}(v_{*})\big(\psi_{j}(v^{\prime})-\psi_{j}(v)\big)B_{ji}(|u|,\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)d\sigma dv_{*}dv.

Thus, splitting the sum in the sets
\{i\geq j\}
and
\{i<j\}
we can interchange indexes
i\leftrightarrow j
to obtain that

\displaystyle 2\sum^{I}_{i=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big[\mathbb{Q}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F})\big]_{i}\psi_{i}(v)\,dv

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j})(v)\psi_{i}(v)dv

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(Q_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j})(v)\psi_{i}(v)+Q_{ji}(f_{j},f_{i})\psi_{j}(v)\Big)dv\,.

Recalling that
B_{ji}=B_{ij}
and the micro reversibility condition (
2.7
), we can interchange variables
(v,v_{*},\sigma)\rightarrow(v_{*},v,-\sigma)
in the term associated to
Q_{ji}(f_{j},f_{i})
(it holds that
v^{\prime}_{ji}=v^{\prime}_{*,ij}
) to obtain that

(2.10)

\begin{split}2\sum^{I}_{i=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big[\mathbb{Q}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F})\big]_{i}\psi_{i}(v)\,&dv=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int\int\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}f_{i}(v)f_{j}(v_{*})\\
&\times\big(\psi_{i}(v^{\prime})+\psi_{j}(v^{\prime}_{*})-\psi_{i}(v)-\psi_{j}(v_{*})\big)B_{ij}(|u|,\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)d\sigma dv_{*}dv\,.\end{split}

The weak form of Boltzmann collision operator imply the conservation properties of the system. In particular,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j})(v)dv=0\,,\qquad 1\leq i,\,j\leq I\,,

which leads to conservation of mass for each single component and for the system as a whole

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big[\mathbb{Q}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F})\big]_{i}dv=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big[\mathbb{Q}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F})\big]_{i}dv=0\,.

Moreover, when we choose the test functions
\psi_{i}=m_{i}\,v
and
\psi_{i}=m_{i}\,|v|^{2}
in the weak form (
2.10
) we obtain the conservation of momentum and energy for the system as a whole (but not for each single species)

\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big[\mathbb{Q}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F})\big]_{i}\,m_{i}\,v\,dv=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big[\mathbb{Q}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F})\big]_{i}\,m_{i}\,|v|^{2}\,dv=0\,.

More precisely, if
\mathbb{F}
is a solution of Boltzmann gas mixture system then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}(v)dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{0,i}(v)dv\,,\qquad 1\leq i\leq I\,,\qquad\text{conservation of mass per species}\,,

and

\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m_{i}\,v\,f_{i}(v)dv=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m_{i}\,v\,f_{0,i}(v)dv\,,\qquad\text{conservation of total momentum}\,,

and

\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m_{i}|v|^{2}f_{i}(v)dv=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m_{i}|v|^{2}f_{0,i}(v)dv\,,\qquad\text{conservation of total energy}\,.

This latter yields

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m_{i}|v|^{2}f_{i}(v)dv\leq\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m_{i}|v|^{2}f_{0,i}(v)dv\,,\qquad 1\leq i\leq I\,.

Also, we recall the gas mixture entropy

\mathcal{H}[\mathbb{F}]:=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}(v)\log(f_{i})(v)dv\,,

and the gas mixture entropy production, given by

D[\mathbb{F}]:=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big[\mathbb{Q}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F})\big]_{i}(v)\log(f_{i})(v)dv.

It is possible to deduce from the weak form (
2.10
) that
D[\mathbb{F}]\leq 0
, refer to
[
13
]
, which yields that

(2.11)

\mathcal{H}[\mathbb{F}](t)\leq\mathcal{H}[\mathbb{F}](0)=\mathcal{H}_{0},\qquad\forall\,t>0.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{F}\|_{L\log L}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}(v)\log(1+f_{i}(v))dv=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\{f_{i}\leq 1\}}f_{i}(v)\log(1+f_{i}(v))dv

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt+\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\{f_{i}\geq 1\}}f_{i}(v)\log(1+f_{i}(v))dv

\displaystyle\leq\log(2)\sum^{I}_{i=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}(v)dv+\sum^{I}_{i=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}(v)\log(f_{i}(v))dv-\sum^{I}_{i=1}\int_{\{f_{i}\leq 1\}}f_{i}(v)\log(f_{i}(v))dv\,.

Note that

-\sum^{I}_{i=1}\int_{\{f_{i}\leq 1\}}f_{i}(v)\log(f_{i}(v))dv\leq\tfrac{3}{2}\sum^{I}_{i=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|f_{i}(v)|^{\frac{3}{4}}dv\leq C\bigg(\sum^{I}_{i=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}(v)\langle v\rangle^{\frac{4}{3}}_{i}dv\bigg)^{\frac{3}{4}}

for
C=\frac{3}{2}\big(\sum^{I}_{i=1}\int\langle v\rangle^{-4}_{i}\big)^{\frac{1}{4}}
. Therefore, by conservation of total mass, energy, and dissipation of entropy, it holds that

(2.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\mathbb{F}\|_{L\log L}\leq\log(2)\sum^{I}_{i=1}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{0,i}(v)dv\\
&+\sum^{I}_{i=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{0,i}(v)\log(f_{0,i}(v))dv+C\bigg(\sum^{I}_{i=1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{0,i}\langle v\rangle^{2}_{i}\,dv\bigg)^{\frac{3}{4}}\,.\end{split}

Thus, we consider initial data satisfying that

(2.13)

\begin{split}{\bf m}_{0}&=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{0,i}(v)dv<\infty\,,\qquad{\bf m}_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}m_{i}\,v\,f_{0,i}(v)dv=0\,,\\
{\bf m}_{2}&=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{0,i}(v)\langle v\rangle^{2}_{i}dv<\infty\,,\qquad\mathcal{H}_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{0,i}(v)\,\log(f_{0,i})(v)dv<\infty\,,\end{split}

so that
\|\mathbb{F}\|_{L\log L}\leq\log(2){\bf m}_{0}+\mathcal{H}_{0}+C\,{\bf m}^{\frac{3}{4}}_{2}<\infty
as argued in (
2.12
). In this way, solutions to the gas mixture will formally lie in the space

U(D_{0},E_{0})=\big\{\mathbb{G}\in(L^{1}_{2}\cap L\log L)\,,\,\mathbb{G}\geq 0\,\big|\,\|g_{i}\|_{L^{1}}\geq D_{0},\;\|\mathbb{G}\|_{L^{1}_{2}}+\|\mathbb{G}\|_{L\log L}\leq E_{0}\big\},

where
D_{0},\,E_{0}>0
. In particular, we take initial data
\mathbb{F}_{0}\in U(D_{0},E_{0})
.

2.2.
Main Results

Recall that we address
a priori
estimates related to statistical moments and higher integrability for a system of spatially homogeneous Boltzmann equations describing a gas mixture of monatomic components
A_{1},...,A_{I}
, where the component
A_{i}
has associated particle mass
m_{i}>0

(2.14)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\mathbb{F}(t,v)=\mathbb{Q}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F})(t,v),\qquad t>0,\;\;v\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
\mathbb{F}(0,v)=\mathbb{F}_{0}(v).\end{cases}

The
i^{th}
entry of the vector
\mathbb{F}:=\mathbb{F}(t,v)=[f_{i}(t,v)]_{1\leq i\leq I}
is the velocity distribution function
f_{i}:=f_{i}(t,v)\geq 0
associated to each component
A_{i}
of the mixture. Recall that

\mathbb{Q}(\mathbb{F},\mathbb{F})=\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{I}Q_{ij}\big(f_{i},f_{j}\big)\Big]_{1\leq i\leq I}

was defined in (
2.2
). Also recall in the following that
\lambda_{ij}\in(0,2]
and
s_{ij}\in(0,2)
with
1\leq i,j\leq I
. Keep in mind that the following are
a priori
estimates, so we consider solutions
\mathbb{F}(t)
to (
4.12
) with sufficient regularity so that all computations performed are allowed. Finally, in the results is explicit that constants depend of the parameters of the model, that is, on the
\lambda_{ij}
,
s_{ij}
,
\kappa^{1}_{ij}
,
\kappa^{2}_{ij}
,
m_{i}
, and
\|\theta^{2}b_{ij}\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{S}^{2})}
. Such constant will depend on the mass, enegy and entropy
D_{0}
and
E_{0}
of the gas mixture initial configuration as well.

Proposition 2.2
.

Take
\lambda^{\natural}>0
and
\mathbb{F}_{0}\in U(D_{0},E_{0})
. For any integer
n\geq 2,

is decreasing. Hence,

\displaystyle{\bf B}_{n}^{ij}(t)

\displaystyle\leq 8(m_{i}m_{j})^{\frac{\lambda_{ij}}{2}}\lambda^{2}_{ij}\sum^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}_{a=1}\Big(\begin{matrix}n\\
a\end{matrix}\Big)\frac{n^{s_{ij}/2}}{a^{s_{ij}/2+1}}\Big({\bf m}_{2a+\lambda_{ij},i}(t){\bf m}_{2(n-a),j}(t)

\displaystyle\qquad+{\bf m}_{2a,j}(t){\bf m}_{2(n-a)+\lambda_{ij},i}(t)+{\bf m}_{2a+\lambda_{ij},j}(t){\bf m}_{2(n-a),i}(t)+{\bf m}_{2a,i}(t){\bf m}_{2(n-a)+\lambda_{ij},j}\Big)\,.

Now, since
n-a\geq a
for
1\leq a\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor
we can invoke the Lemma
5.1
with
b=2(n-a)
and
\beta=\lambda_{ij}
, which is a generalisation of the classical lemma
[

, to conclude that

{\bf m}_{2a+\lambda_{ij},i}(t){\bf m}_{2(n-a),j}(t)\leq\theta_{ij}{\bf m}_{2(n-a)+\lambda_{ij},i}(t){\bf m}_{2a,j}(t)+(1-\theta_{ij}){\bf m}_{2(n-a)+\lambda_{ij},j}(t){\bf m}_{2a,i}(t)\,,

resulting in the estimation

\displaystyle{\bf B}_{n}^{ij}(t)

\displaystyle\leq 16(m_{i}m_{j})^{\frac{\lambda_{ij}}{2}}\lambda^{2}_{ij}\sum^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}_{a=1}\Big(\begin{matrix}n\\
a\end{matrix}\Big)\frac{n^{s_{ij}/2}}{a^{s_{ij}/2+1}}\Big({\bf m}_{2a,j}(t){\bf m}_{2(n-a)+\lambda_{ij},i}(t)+{\bf m}_{2a,i}(t){\bf m}_{2(n-a)+\lambda_{ij},j}\Big)\,.

Consequently,

(3.5)

\partial_{t}{\bf m}^{ij}_{2n}(t)\leq-C^{1}_{ij}n^{\frac{s_{ij}}{2}}\big({\bf m}_{2n+\lambda_{ij},i}(t)+{\bf m}_{2n+\lambda_{ij},j}(t)\big)+C_{ij}^{2}\,S^{ij}_{n}(t)\,.

where the constants can be taken as

C^{1}_{ij}=2\,\lambda^{1}_{ij}\,c\qquad\text{and}\qquad C^{2}_{ij}=16(m_{i}m_{j})^{\frac{\lambda_{ij}}{2}}\lambda^{2}_{ij}\,.

This is precisely statement (i).

Now, statement (ii) follow from (i) after some observations. First, for the coercive term it readily follows that

\displaystyle\sum^{I}_{i=1}\sum^{I}_{j=1}C^{1}_{ij}n^{s_{ij}/2}\big({\bf m}_{2n+\lambda_{ij},i}(t)

\displaystyle+{\bf m}_{2n+\lambda_{ij},j}(t)\big)\geq 2\min_{ij}\{C^{1}_{ij}n^{s_{ij}/2}\}\sum^{I}_{i=1}\sum^{I}_{j=1}{\bf m}_{2n+\lambda_{ij},i}(t)

\displaystyle\geq 2\,C_{1}\,n^{\frac{\overline{s}}{2}}\sum^{I}_{i=1}{\bf m}_{2n+\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i},i}(t)\geq 2\,C_{1}\,n^{\frac{\overline{s}}{2}}\,{\bf m}_{2n+\lambda^{\natural}}(t)\,.

The upper estimate related to
S^{ij}_{n}
follows after some moment interpolations. Note that (since
n/a\geq 1
)

\displaystyle\sum^{I}_{i=1}\sum^{I}_{j=1}C^{2}_{ij}

\displaystyle\,S^{ij}_{n}(t)\leq 2\max_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C^{2}_{ij}\sum^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}_{a=1}\Big(\begin{matrix}n\\
a\end{matrix}\Big)\frac{n^{\overline{\overline{s}}/2}}{a^{\overline{\overline{s}}/2+1}}{\bf m}_{2a}(t){\bf m}_{2(n-a)+\overline{\overline{\lambda}}}(t)\,,

Furthermore,
2<2(n-a)+\overline{\overline{\lambda}}<2n
(recalling that
n\geq 2
), therefore, the following interpolations hold

\displaystyle{\bf m}_{2(n-a)+\overline{\overline{\lambda}}}(t)

\displaystyle\leq{\bf m}_{2}^{1-\theta_{1}}\,{\bf m}^{\theta_{1}}_{2n+\lambda^{\natural}}(t)\,,\qquad\theta_{1}=\frac{2(n-a)+\overline{\overline{\lambda}}-2}{2n+\lambda^{\natural}-2}\,,

\displaystyle{\bf m}_{2a}(t)

\displaystyle\leq{\bf m}_{2}^{1-\theta_{2}}\,{\bf m}^{\theta_{2}}_{2n+\lambda^{\natural}}(t)\,,\qquad\theta_{2}=\frac{2a-2}{2n+\lambda^{\natural}-2}\,,

which yields that (since
\overline{\overline{\lambda}}-\lambda^{\natural}<2
)

{\bf m}_{2a+\overline{\overline{\lambda}}}(t)\,{\bf m}_{2(n-a)}(t)\leq{\bf m}^{2-\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}}_{2}{\bf m}^{\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}}_{2n+\lambda^{\natural}}\,,\qquad\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}=\frac{2n+\overline{\overline{\lambda}}-4}{2n+\lambda^{\natural}-2}<1\,.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\sum^{I}_{i=1}\sum^{I}_{j=1}C^{2}_{ij}\,S^{ij}_{n}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C({\bf m}_{2})\,2^{\frac{n}{2}}\,n^{\frac{\overline{\overline{s}}}{2}}\,{\bf m}^{\theta}_{2n+\lambda^{\natural}}(t)\,,\qquad\theta=\frac{2n+\overline{\overline{\lambda}}-4}{2n+\lambda^{\natural}-2}<1\,,

\displaystyle\leq\Big(C\,2^{\frac{n}{2}}\,n^{\frac{\overline{\overline{s}}}{2}-\frac{\theta\overline{s}}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{1-\theta}}+C_{1}\;n^{\frac{\overline{s}}{2}}\;{\bf m}_{2n+{\lambda}^{\natural}}(t)\,,

where in the latter we used Young’s inequality. Consequently,

\sum^{I}_{i=1}\sum^{I}_{j=1}\partial_{t}{\bf m}^{ij}_{2n}(t)\leq-C_{1}\;n^{\frac{\overline{s}}{2}}\;{\bf m}_{2n+{\lambda}^{\natural}}(t)+\Big(C\,2^{\frac{n}{2}}\,n^{\frac{\overline{\overline{s}}}{2}-\frac{\theta\overline{s}}{2}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{1-\theta}}\,,

which proves item (ii) of the proposition.
∎

.

Use the interpolation

in Proposition

to conclude that

\displaystyle{\bf m}^{\prime}_{2n}(t)

\displaystyle=:-A_{n}\,{\bf m}^{1+\frac{\lambda^{\natural}}{2n-2}}_{2n}(t)+B_{n}\,,\qquad c_{n}=\frac{\lambda^{\natural}}{2n-2}\,.

Thus, a comparison principle for order, see

, gives that the super solution

3.3.
Exponential moments

Let us now focus in the generation and propagation of exponential moments result of Theorem
2.4
. Our goal is to show that the partial sums

\sum_{n=0}^{p}\frac{(\sigma t)^{\rho n}{\bf m}_{2n}[\mathbb{F}](t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}}

are bounded uniformly in time
t
and summation truncation
p
for some explicit
\rho>0
and
\alpha>0
. To this end we use a methodology reminiscent of
[
15
,
5
]
. The proof relies on generation and propagation of polynomial moments stated in Theorem
2.3
and the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2

(Equivalence property)
.

Let
\mathbb{G}=[g_{i}]_{1\leq i\leq I}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then we have the following equivalence:

(i)

(System) For
\sigma_{0}\in(0,\infty),\;\alpha\in[1,\infty)
and
K\in[1,\infty)
, we have

\sup_{n\geq 0}\frac{\sigma_{0}^{n}\,{\bf m}_{2n}[\mathbb{G}]}{(n!)^{\alpha}}\leq K\;\Longrightarrow\;\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\exp\Big(\frac{\sigma_{0}^{1/\alpha}\langle v\rangle_{i}^{2/\alpha}}{2}\Big)g_{i}(v)dv\leq 2\,{\bf m}_{0}^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}K^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

(ii)

(Component) Let
\rho\in(0,2]
,
\sigma_{0}\in(0,1]
and
K\geq 1
, there exists
\sigma_{1}:=\sigma_{1}(\rho,\sigma_{0},K)
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\exp\big(\sigma_{0}\langle v\rangle_{i}^{\rho}\big)\,g_{i}(v)\,dv\leq\frac{K}{I}\;\Longrightarrow\;\sup_{n\geq 0}\frac{\sigma_{1}^{n}\,{\bf m}_{2n,i}[g_{i}]}{(n!)^{2/\rho}}\leq\frac{1}{I}\,.

Proof.

We start with
(i)
noticing that by Holder’s inequality (recall that
\alpha\geq 1
),

{\bf m}_{\frac{2n}{\alpha}}[\mathbb{G}]\leq{\bf m}_{2n}[\mathbb{G}]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}{\bf m}_{0}^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\leq{\bf m}_{0}^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}K^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\sigma_{0}^{-\frac{n}{\alpha}}\,n!\,.

Then,

\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\exp\Big(\frac{\sigma_{0}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\langle v\rangle_{i}^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}}{2}\Big)g_{i}(v)dv=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\sigma_{0}^{\frac{n}{\alpha}}{\bf m}_{\frac{2n}{\alpha}}[\mathbb{G}]}{2^{n}n!}\leq{\bf m}_{0}^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}K^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\sum_{n\geq 0}2^{-n}=2\,{\bf m}_{0}^{1-\frac{1}{\alpha}}K^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\,.

Regarding to
(ii)
, note that

\sup_{n\geq 0}\frac{\sigma_{0}^{n}{\bf m}_{\rho n,i}[g_{i}]}{n!}\leq\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\sigma_{0}^{n}{\bf m}_{\rho n,i}[g_{i}]}{n!}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\exp\big(\sigma_{0}\langle v\rangle_{i}^{\rho}\big)g_{i}(v)dv\leq\frac{K}{I}\,.

For
n\geq 1
we set
k_{n}=\lceil\frac{2n}{\rho}\rceil\in[\frac{2n}{\rho},\frac{2n}{\rho}+1)
. Using that
\rho\,k_{n}\geq 2n
we obtain that

{\bf m}_{2n,i}[g_{i}]\leq{\bf m}_{\rho k_{n},i}[g_{i}]\leq\frac{Kk_{n}!}{I\sigma_{0}^{k_{n}}}\,,

and consequently,

\frac{\sigma_{1}^{n}{\bf m}_{2n,i}[g_{i}]}{(n!)^{2/\rho}}\leq\frac{\sigma_{1}^{n}Kk_{n}!}{I\sigma_{0}^{k_{n}}(n!)^{2/\rho}}.

Invoking Stirling‘s formula
n!\sim\sqrt{2\pi n}(n/e)^{n}
and since
k_{n}=\lceil\frac{2n}{\rho}\rceil
we conclude that for some constant
A\in(0,\infty)
depending only on
\rho
it holds that

\frac{k_{n}!}{(n!)^{2/\rho}}\leq A\bigg(\frac{4}{\rho}\bigg)^{\frac{2n}{\rho}},\qquad n\geq 1\,.

We refer to
[
15
]
for additional details. Moreover, since
\sigma_{0}\in(0,1]
then
\sigma_{0}^{k_{n}}\geq\sigma_{0}^{\frac{2n}{\rho}+1}
, and consequently

\frac{\sigma_{1}^{n}{\bf m}_{2n,i}[g_{i}]}{(n!)^{2/\rho}}\leq\frac{K\,A\,\sigma_{1}^{n}}{I\sigma_{0}^{\frac{2n}{\rho}+1}}\bigg(\frac{4}{\rho}\bigg)^{\frac{2n}{\rho}}<1,\qquad n\geq\frac{1}{I}\,,

after choosing, for example,

\sigma_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}\inf_{n\geq 1}\frac{I\sigma^{\frac{2}{\rho}+1}_{0}}{(K\,A)^{\frac{1}{n}}}\Big(\frac{\rho}{4}\Big)^{\frac{2}{\rho}}\,.

This concludes the proof.
∎

3.3.1.
Generation of exponential moments.

First we introduce for
\sigma\in(0,1]
and
\alpha>0
, to be chosen later sufficiently small and large respectively,
p\geq 2
and
t>0

E_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t):=\sum_{n=0}^{p}\frac{(\sigma t)^{2n/{\lambda^{\natural}}}{\bf m}_{2n}[\mathbb{F}](t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}}.

Additionally, we define the objects, for
1\leq i,j\leq I
,

E_{p}^{i}[f_{i}](t):=\sum_{n=0}^{p}\frac{(\sigma t)^{2n/{\lambda^{\natural}}}{\bf m}_{2n,i}[f_{i}](t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}},\qquad F_{p}^{ij}[f_{i}](t):=\sum_{n=2}^{p}\frac{n^{s_{ij}/2}(\sigma t)^{2n/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2n+\lambda_{ij},i}[f_{i}](t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}},

G_{p}^{ij}[f_{i},f_{j}](t):=\sum_{n=2}^{p}\frac{(\sigma t)^{2n/\lambda^{\natural}}\mathcal{S}_{n}^{ij}(t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}},\qquad H_{p}^{i}[f_{i}](t):=\sum_{n=0}^{p}\frac{n(\sigma t)^{2n/{\lambda^{\natural}-1}}{\bf m}_{2n,i}[f_{i}](t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}}.

We start by time differentiation of
E_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)
and then using Proposition
2.2
item (i). Since
{\bf m}_{0}^{\prime}={\bf m}_{2}^{\prime}=0
, we deduce that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}E_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)&=\sum_{n=0}^{p}\bigg[\frac{(\sigma t)^{2n/{\lambda^{\natural}}}{\bf m}^{\prime}_{2n}[\mathbb{F}](t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}}+\frac{2\sigma}{\lambda^{\natural}}\frac{n(\sigma t)^{2n/{\lambda^{\natural}}-1}{\bf m}_{2n}[\mathbb{F}](t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}}\bigg]\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{p}\bigg[\frac{(\sigma t)^{2n/{\lambda^{\natural}}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\partial_{t}{\bf m}^{ij}_{2n}(t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}}+\frac{2\sigma}{\lambda^{\natural}}\frac{n(\sigma t)^{2n/{\lambda^{\natural}-1}}\sum^{I}_{i=1}{\bf m}_{2n,i}[f_{i}](t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}}\bigg]\\
&\leq\sum^{I}_{i,j=1}\bigg[-C^{1}_{ij}\Big(F_{p}^{ij}[f_{i}](t)+F_{p}^{ji}[f_{j}](t)\Big)+C^{2}_{ij}G_{p}^{ij}[f_{i},f_{j}](t)\bigg]+\frac{2\sigma}{\lambda^{\natural}}\sum^{I}_{i=1}H_{p}^{i}[f_{i}](t).\end{split}

The following lemma is crucial as it expresses the convolution structure of the moments in the summation. This is where the nonlinear part is estimated.

Lemma 3.3

(Moment Convolution)
.

Let
1\leq i,j\leq I
,
\lambda^{\natural}>0
,
s_{ij}\in(0,2)
, and
\mathbb{F}_{0}\in U(D_{0},E_{0})
. Given
\epsilon>0
there are explicit constants
B_{\epsilon}
and
D_{\epsilon}
, depending on the parameters of the model,
E_{0}
, and
D_{0}
, such that for
\sigma\in(0,1]
,
\alpha\geq 1
and
p\geq 2
, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle G^{ij}_{p}[f_{i},f_{j}](t)\leq

\displaystyle B_{\epsilon}\,\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\,\Big[F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big]

\displaystyle+\epsilon\,\Big[F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)E^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)E^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)\Big]+\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\,D_{\epsilon},\qquad t\in(0,1]\,.

Proof.

Using the definition of
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{ij}(t)
it follows that

\displaystyle G_{p}^{ij}[f_{i},f_{j}](t)=\sum_{n=2}^{p}(\sigma t)^{2n/\lambda^{\natural}}\sum^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}_{a=1}\Big(\begin{matrix}n\\
a\end{matrix}\Big)\frac{n^{s_{ij}/2}}{a^{s_{ij}/2+1}(n!)^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\Big({\bf m}_{2a,i}(t){\bf m}_{2(n-a)+\lambda_{ij},j}(t)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+{\bf m}_{2a,j}(t){\bf m}_{2(n-a)+\lambda_{ij},i}(t)\Big).

For
1\leq a\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor
one has that
n^{s_{ij}/2}\leq 2(n-a)^{s_{ij}/2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle G^{ij}_{p}[f_{i},f_{j}](t)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{n=2}^{p}(\sigma t)^{2n/\lambda^{\natural}}\sum^{n-1}_{a=1}\Big(\begin{matrix}n\\
a\end{matrix}\Big)^{\alpha}\frac{(n-a)^{s_{ij}/2}}{a^{s_{ij}/2+1}(n!)^{\alpha}}\Big({\bf m}_{2a,i}(t){\bf m}_{2(n-a)+\lambda_{ij},j}(t)

\displaystyle\hskip 199.16928pt+{\bf m}_{2a,j}(t){\bf m}_{2(n-a)+\lambda_{ij},i}(t)\Big)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{n=2}^{p}\sum^{n-1}_{a=1}\frac{(\sigma t)^{2a/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2a,i}(t)}{(a!)^{\alpha}}\frac{(n-a)^{s_{ij}/2}(\sigma t)^{2(n-a)/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2(n-a)+\lambda_{ij},j}(t)}{a^{s_{ij}/2+1}((n-a)!)^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{(n-a)^{s_{ij}/2}(\sigma t)^{2(n-a)/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2(n-a)+\lambda_{ij},i}(t)}{((n-a)!)^{\alpha}}\frac{(\sigma t)^{2(n-a)/\lambda^{\natural})}{\bf m}_{2a,j}(t)}{a^{s_{ij}/2+1}(a!)^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{G}_{p,1}^{ij}[f_{i},f_{j}](t)+\mathcal{G}_{p,2}^{ij}[f_{i},f_{j}](t),

with obvious definitions for each term. Now, note that interchanging the summation it hols that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{p,1}^{ij}[f_{i},f_{j}](t)

\displaystyle=2\sum^{p-1}_{a=1}\sum_{l=a}^{p-a}\frac{l^{s_{ij}/2}(\sigma t)^{2l/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2l+\lambda_{ij},j}(t)}{(l!)^{\alpha}}\frac{(\sigma t)^{2a/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2a,i}(t)}{a^{s_{ij}/2+1}(a!)^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\bigg(\sum_{l=1}^{p}\frac{l^{s_{ij}/2}(\sigma t)^{2l/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2l+\lambda_{ij},j}(t)}{(l!)^{\alpha}}\bigg)\bigg(\sum_{a=1}^{p}\frac{(\sigma t)^{2a/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2a,i}(t)}{a^{s_{ij}/2+1}(a!)^{\alpha}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\Big(F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)+(\sigma t)^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2+\lambda_{ij},j}(t)\Big)I^{ij}_{p,1}[f_{i}](t),

where

I^{ij}_{p,1}[f_{i}](t):=2\sum_{a=1}^{p}\frac{(\sigma t)^{2a/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2a,i}(t)}{a^{s_{ij}/2+1}(a!)^{\alpha}}.

Setting
N_{\epsilon}=\lceil 2\,{\epsilon}^{-1}\rceil
, it holds that for
a\geq N_{{\epsilon}}
implies
2\,a^{-1}\leq\epsilon
. Hence,

I^{ij}_{p,1}[f_{j}](t)\leq\epsilon\,E^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+J_{\epsilon}^{i}[f_{i}](t)\quad{\rm where}\quad J_{\epsilon}^{i}[f_{i}](t)=2\sum_{a=1}^{N_{\epsilon}}\frac{(\sigma t)^{2a/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2a,i}(t)}{(a!)^{\alpha}}.

Using the first result of Theorem
2.3
, we deduce that there are explicit constants
C
and
C_{\epsilon}
such that

(\sigma t)^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2+\lambda_{ij},j}(t)\leq C(\sigma t)^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}(1+t^{-\frac{\lambda_{ij}}{\lambda^{\natural}}})\leq 2\,C\,\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}},\qquad\sigma\in(0,1],\quad t\in(0,1]\,,

\displaystyle J_{\epsilon}^{i}[f_{i}](t)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\epsilon}\sum_{a=1}^{N_{\epsilon}}\frac{(\sigma t)^{2a/\lambda^{\natural}}(1+t^{-\frac{2a-2}{\lambda^{\natural}}})}{(a!)^{\alpha}}\leq C_{\epsilon}\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{(a!)^{\alpha}}=:A_{\epsilon}\,\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\,.

(3.7)

(3.8)

In this way, adding (
3.7
) and (
3.8
), it holds that

\displaystyle G_{p}^{ij}[f_{i},f_{j}](t)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{G}_{p,1}^{ij}[f_{i},f_{j}](t)+\mathcal{G}_{p,2}^{ij}[f_{i},f_{j}](t)

\displaystyle\qquad+{\epsilon}\Big[F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)E^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)E^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)\Big]+4\,\sigma^{4/\lambda^{\natural}}A_{\epsilon}\,C\,.

Furthermore, for

\displaystyle E^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+E^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)

\displaystyle\leq{\bf m}_{0}(t)+\sigma\,{\bf m}_{2}(t)+F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\,.

Consequently, we are led to

\displaystyle G^{ij}_{p}[f_{i},f_{j}](t)

\displaystyle\leq B_{\epsilon}\,\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\Big[F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad+\epsilon\,\Big[F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)E^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)E^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)\Big]+\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\,D_{\epsilon},\qquad t\in(0,1]\,,

where

This concludes the proof.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
1\leq i,j\leq I
,
\lambda^{\natural}>0
,
s^{\natural}>0
,
\alpha=\max\big\{1,\frac{2-s^{\natural}}{\lambda^{\natural}}\big\}
, and
\mathbb{F}_{0}\in U(D_{0},E_{0})
. There are explicit constants
K
and
L
, depending on the parameters of the model,
E_{0}
, and
D_{0}
, such that for any

and

H^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)\leq K\,F^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+L,\qquad t\in(0,1]\,,

where
F^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t):=\sum_{n=2}^{p}\frac{n^{\overline{\overline{s}}_{i}/2}(\sigma t)^{2n/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2n+\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i},i}(t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}}.

Proof.

First observe that for
\kappa\geq 1

\cdot

\frac{n\,{\bf m}_{2n,i}(t)}{\sigma t}\leq\frac{n}{\sigma t}\Big(\frac{n^{1-\frac{\overline{\overline{s}}_{i}}{2}}}{\kappa\sigma t}\Big)^{\frac{2n-2}{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}}
.

\cdot

If
{\bf m}_{2n,i}(t)\geq\Big(\frac{n^{1-\frac{\overline{\overline{s}}_{i}}{2}}}{\kappa\sigma t}\Big)^{\frac{2n-2}{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}}

\frac{n\,{\bf m}_{2n,i}(t)}{\sigma t}\leq\kappa n^{\frac{\overline{\overline{s}}_{i}}{2}}{\bf m}_{2n,i}(t)^{1+\frac{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}{2n-2}}
.

Thus, overall we get that

\frac{n\,{\bf m}_{2n,i}(t)}{\sigma t}\leq\kappa\,n^{\overline{\overline{s}}_{i}/2}{\bf m}_{2n,i}(t)^{1+\frac{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}{2n-2}}+\frac{n}{\sigma t}\Big(\frac{n^{1-\overline{\overline{s}}_{i}/2}}{\kappa\sigma t}\Big)^{\frac{2n-2}{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}}.

Interpolation gives that

{\bf m}_{2n,i}(t)^{1+\frac{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}{2n-2}}\leq{\bf m}_{2n+\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i},i}(t)\,{\bf m}_{2,i}^{\frac{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}{2n-2}}(t)\leq{\bf m}_{2n+\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i},i}(t)\,\max\{1,{\bf m}_{2}\}\,,

and consequently,

\displaystyle H^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)\leq\kappa\,{\bf m}_{2,i}(t)(\sigma t)^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}+\kappa\max\{1,{\bf m}_{2}\}\,F^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+(\sigma t)^{2/\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}-1}\sum_{n=1}^{p}\frac{n^{\frac{(2-\overline{\overline{s}}_{i})n}{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}-\frac{(2-\overline{\overline{s}}_{i})}{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}+1}}{(n!)^{\alpha}\kappa^{(2n-2)/\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}}\,.

Conservation of mass and energy implies that

{\bf m}_{2,i}(t)(\sigma t)^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\leq{\bf m}_{2}\,\sigma^{2/{\lambda^{\natural}}},\qquad t\in(0,1]\,.

Moreover, recall that
\alpha=\max\{1,\frac{2-s^{\natural}}{\lambda^{\natural}}\}\geq\frac{2-\overline{\overline{s}}_{i}}{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}
and
\kappa\geq 1
, then

(\sigma t)^{2/\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}-1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{\frac{(2-\overline{\overline{s}}_{i})n}{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}-\frac{2-\overline{\overline{s}}_{i}}{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}+1}}{(n!)^{\alpha}\kappa^{(2n-2)/\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}}\leq\kappa^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{\alpha n+1}}{(n!)^{\alpha}\kappa^{n}}=:S\,,

where the series
S
is convergent provided
\kappa>2e^{\alpha}

(
invoking the Stirling’s formula
n!\sim\sqrt{2\pi n}(n/e)^{n}
)
. Therefore, for all
1\leq i\leq I
,

H^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)\leq 3e^{\alpha}\max\{1,{\bf m}_{2}\}\,F^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+3e^{\alpha}{\bf m}_{2}+S,\qquad\sigma\in(0,1]\,,\quad t\in(0,1]\,,

which is the statement of the lemma for
K=3e^{\alpha}\max\{1,{\bf m}_{2}\}
and
L=3e^{\alpha}{\bf m}_{2}+S
.
∎

We can finally proceed with the proof of exponential moment generation.

Proof of the generation result of Theorem
2.4
.

We devise the proof in two steps.

Step 1.
We show first that for
\lambda^{\natural}>0
,
s^{\natural}>0
and
\alpha=\max\big\{1,\frac{2-{s}^{\natural}}{\lambda^{\natural}}\big\}
, there exist
\sigma\in(0,1]
and
T\in(0,1]
, depending only on the parameters of the model such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\sigma t)^{2n/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2n}(t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}}\leq 2\,{\bf m}_{0}\,.

Invoking Theorem
2.3
it holds that for some constant
C_{p}\in(0,\infty)
,

{\bf m}_{0}\leq E_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)\leq{\bf m}_{0}+C_{p}\,t^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\,,

then
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}E_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)={\bf m}_{0}
.

Now, using identity (
3.6
) and lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
, we get that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}E_{p}

\displaystyle[\mathbb{F}](t)\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big[-C_{ij}^{1}\Big(F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big)+C_{ij}^{2}G^{ij}_{p}[f_{i},f_{j}](t)\Big]+\frac{2\sigma}{\lambda^{\natural}}\sum_{i=1}^{I}H^{i}_{p}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big[-C_{ij}^{1}\Big(F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big)+C_{ij}^{2}\Big(B_{\epsilon}\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\big[F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\big]

\displaystyle\qquad+{\epsilon}\,\big[F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)E^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)E^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)\big]+D_{\epsilon}\Big)\Big]+\frac{2\sigma}{\lambda^{\natural}}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\Big(KF^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+L\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big[-C_{ij}^{1}\big[F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}(t)[f_{j}](t)\big]+R^{ij}_{{\epsilon}}\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\big[F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\big]

\displaystyle\qquad+\epsilon\,C^{2}_{ij}\Big(F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big)\Big(E^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+E^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big)+\frac{2\sigma}{\lambda^{\natural}}K\,\sum_{i=1}^{I}F^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+Q_{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=I^{1}+I^{2}+Q_{\epsilon}\,,

where
R^{ij}_{\epsilon}=C^{2}_{ij}\,B_{\epsilon}
,
Q_{\epsilon}=D_{\epsilon}\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}C^{2}_{ij}+\frac{2\sigma}{\lambda^{\natural}}IL
, and

\displaystyle I^{1}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big[-C_{ij}^{1}\big[F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}(t)[f_{j}](t)\big]+R^{ij}_{{\epsilon}}\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\big[F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 128.0374pt+\epsilon\,C^{2}_{ij}\,\Big(F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big)\Big(E^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+E^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big)\Big]\,,

\displaystyle I^{2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{2\sigma}{\lambda^{\natural}}K\sum_{i=1}^{I}F^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)\,.

Choosing

\epsilon:=\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}\frac{C^{1}_{ij}}{8IC_{ij}^{2}{\bf m}_{0}}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{0}:=\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}\Big(\frac{C^{1}_{ij}}{4R^{ij}_{\epsilon}}\Big)^{\lambda^{\natural}/2}\,,

we get for any
\sigma\leq\min\{1,\sigma_{0}\}

\displaystyle I^{1}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{3\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C_{ij}^{1}}{4}\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big(F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C_{ij}^{1}}{8I{\bf m}_{0}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big(F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big)\Big(E^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+E^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big).

Similar, for
\sigma\leq\sigma_{1}:=\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}\frac{C_{ij}^{1}\lambda^{\natural}}{8K}
, we obtain that

I^{2}\leq\frac{\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C^{1}_{ij}}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{I}F^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)\leq\frac{\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C^{1}_{ij}}{4}\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)\,,

where the latter inequality is a direct consequence of the definitions of
F^{i}_{p}[f_{i}]
and
F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}]
. Thus, overall we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle E_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)\leq I^{1}+I^{2}+Q\leq-\frac{\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C_{ij}^{1}}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big(F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C_{ij}^{1}}{8I{\bf m}_{0}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big(F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big)\Big(E^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+E^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big)+Q.

Now, let us introduce the time

T_{p}:=\sup\big\{0<t\leq 1\,\big|\,E_{p}[\mathbb{F}](s)\leq 2\,{\bf m}_{0},\,s\in(0,t]\big\}>0\,.

This time is indeed positive by continuity of the polynomial moments (we pointed out previously that
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}E_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)={\bf m}_{0}
). Thus, for all
t\in(0,T_{p}]
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}E_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C_{ij}^{1}}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big(F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt+\frac{\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C_{ij}^{1}}{8I{\bf m}_{0}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big(F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big)\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big(E^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+E^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big)+Q

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C_{ij}^{1}}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big(F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C_{ij}^{1}}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big(F^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+F^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big)+Q.

Then, we comclude that for all
t\in(0,T_{p}]
it holds that

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}E_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)\leq Q\,,\qquad\text{and, thus}\qquad E_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)\leq E_{p}[\mathbb{F}](0)+Q\,t.

Recalling that
E_{p}[\mathbb{F}](0)={\bf m}_{0},
we deduce directly from the definition of
T_{p}
that

T_{p}\geq\min\{1,\frac{{\bf m}_{0}}{Q}\}=:T\,,\quad\text{independent of}\;\;p\,.

Thus, for
\sigma\leq\min\{1,\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1}\}
,

E[\mathbb{F}](t)=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}E_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)\leq 2\,{\bf m}_{0},\qquad t\in(0,T]\,.

Step 2.
Let us proceed with the concluding argument. To this end, fix the rate

\rho=\min\Big\{\frac{2\lambda^{\natural}}{2-{s}^{\natural}},2\Big\}\,,

and observe that
\alpha=\max\big\{1,\frac{2-{s}^{\natural}}{\lambda^{\natural}}\big\}=\frac{2}{\rho}
.
Using the result of Step 1, we obtain that

\frac{(\sigma t)^{2n/\lambda^{\natural}}\,{\bf m}_{2n}(\mathbb{F})(t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}}\leq E[\mathbb{F}](t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\sigma t)^{2n/\lambda^{\natural}}\,{\bf m}_{2n}(\mathbb{F})(t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}}\leq 2{\bf m}_{0}\,,\quad t\in(0,T]\,.

Then, Lemma
3.2
item (i) with
\sigma_{0}=(\sigma t)^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}
and
K=2{\bf m}_{0}
implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}

\displaystyle\exp\Big[\frac{(\sigma t)^{\rho/\lambda^{\natural}}\langle v\rangle_{i}^{\rho}}{2}\Big]f_{i}(v)dv

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\exp\Big[\frac{(\sigma t)^{2/{(\lambda^{\natural}\alpha)}}\langle v\rangle_{i}^{2/\alpha}}{2}\Big]f_{i}(v)dv\leq 2^{1+1/\alpha}{\bf m}_{0}\leq 4{\bf m}_{0},\qquad t\in(0,T]\,,

which is precisely the statement of the exponential moment generation.
∎

3.3.2.
Propagation of exponential moment.

The argument to propagate exponential moments follows a similar path to that of propagation; we present the main steps for completeness. Let us introduce for
\sigma\in(0,1]
, to be chosen sufficiently small, and
p\geq 2
,

\mathcal{E}_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t):=\sum_{n=0}^{p}\frac{\sigma^{2n/{\lambda^{\natural}}}{\bf m}_{2n}[\mathbb{F}](t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}},\qquad t\geq 0\,.

Analogously, for all
1\leq i,j\leq I
, we denote

\mathcal{E}_{p}^{i}[f_{i}](t):=\sum_{n=0}^{p}\frac{\sigma^{2n/{\lambda^{\natural}}}{\bf m}_{2n,i}[f_{i}](t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}},\qquad\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ij}[f_{i}](t):=\sum_{n=2}^{p}\frac{n^{s_{ij}/2}\sigma^{2n/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2n+\lambda_{ij},i}[f_{i}]}{(n!)^{\alpha}},

\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{G}_{p}^{ij}[f_{i},f_{j}](t):=\sum_{n=2}^{p}\frac{\sigma^{2n/\lambda^{\natural}}\mathcal{S}_{n}^{ij}(t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}},\qquad t\geq 0\,.

Note that the analogous to (
3.6
) in this context is given by

(3.9)

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{E}_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big[-C^{1}_{ij}\Big(\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ij}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ji}[f_{j}](t)\Big)+C^{2}_{ij}\mathcal{G}_{p}^{ij}[f_{i},f_{j}](t)\Big].

As before, we have the key estimate on the convolution structure of moments.

Lemma 3.5

(Moment Convolution)
.

Let
1\leq i,j\leq I
,
\lambda^{\natural}>0
,
s_{ij}\in(0,2)
,
\mathbb{F}_{0}\in U(D_{0},E_{0})
, and

\tilde{\sigma}_{0}=\sup_{n\geq 0}\frac{\sigma_{0}^{n}\,{\bf m}_{2n}(0)}{(n!)^{\alpha}}\leq 1,\qquad\text{for some}\;\;\sigma_{0}>0\,.

Given
{\epsilon}>0
there are explicit constants
\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}
, depending on the parameters of the model,
E_{0}
,
D_{0}
, and
\tilde{\sigma}_{0}
, such that for any
\sigma\in(0,1]
,
p\geq 2
, and
\alpha\geq 1
, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{ij}_{p}[f_{i},f_{j}](t)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}\,\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\Big[\mathcal{P}^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{P}^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+\epsilon\,\Big[\mathcal{P}^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)\mathcal{E}^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)+\mathcal{P}^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\mathcal{E}^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)\Big]+\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon},\qquad t\geq 0\,.

Proof.

Using a copycat argument in the proof of Lemma
3.3
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{p}^{ij}[f_{i},f_{j}](t)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\mathcal{P}^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)+\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2+\lambda_{ij},j}(t)\Big)\Big(\epsilon\,\mathcal{E}^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}^{i}[f_{i}](t)\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\mathcal{P}^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2+\lambda_{ij},i}(t)\Big)\Big(\epsilon\,\mathcal{E}^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)+\mathcal{J}_{{\epsilon}}^{j}[f_{j}](t)\Big),

where,
setting
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}=\lceil 2\,{\epsilon}^{-1}\rceil,

\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}^{i}[f_{i}](t):=2\sum_{a=1}^{\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}}\frac{\sigma^{2a/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2a,i}[f_{i}](t)}{(a!)^{\alpha}}\leq\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}\,\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\,.

For the later inequality in the right we have used the propagation of polynomial moments given in Theorem
2.3
, which applies since the initial datum satisfies
{\bf m}_{2n}(0)\leq\frac{(n!)^{\alpha}}{\sigma^{n}_{0}}
, for
n\geq 1
. Then, we can simply take the constant

\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}:=\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}(\tilde{\sigma}_{0})=2\,\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma^{2a/\lambda^{\natural}}}{(a!)^{\alpha}}\,\sup_{t\geq 0}{\bf m}_{2\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}}[\mathbb{F}](t)<\infty\,.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{p}^{ij}[f_{i},f_{j}](t)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\mathcal{P}^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)+2\,C\,\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\Big)\Big(\epsilon\,\mathcal{E}_{p}^{i}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}\,\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\mathcal{P}^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+2\,C\,\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\Big)\Big(\epsilon\,\mathcal{E}^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)+\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}\,\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\Big)\,.

And, since

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{E}^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)

\displaystyle\leq{\bf m}_{0}+\sigma{\bf m}_{2}+\mathcal{P}^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{P}^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\,,

we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{ij}_{p}[f_{i},f_{j}](t)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\Big[\mathcal{P}^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{P}^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad+\epsilon\Big[\mathcal{P}^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)\mathcal{E}^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)+\mathcal{P}^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\mathcal{E}^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)\Big]+\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon},

where

\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}=2\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}+2\,\epsilon\,C\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}=4\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\mathcal{A}_{\epsilon}C+2\,\epsilon\,C\,\big({\bf m}_{0}+\sigma{\bf m}_{2}\big)\,,

which is the statement of the result.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
1\leq i,j\leq I
,
\lambda^{\natural}>0
,
s_{ij}\in(0,2)
and
p>2
. For any
\sigma\in(0,1]
,
\alpha\geq 1
it holds that

\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ij}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ji}[f_{j}](t)\geq\frac{1}{\sigma^{\lambda^{\natural}/2}}\Big[\mathcal{E}^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{E}^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)-2\,e\max\big\{{\bf m}_{0}[f_{i}],{\bf m}_{0}[f_{j}]\big\}\Big],\qquad t\geq 0\,.

Proof.

Using that
x^{b}\geq x^{a}-1
for any
b\geq a\geq 0
we can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ij}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ji}[f_{j}](t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2}^{p}\frac{n^{s_{ij}/2}\sigma^{n}}{(n!)^{\alpha}}\Big({\bf m}_{2n+\lambda_{ij},i}[f_{i}](t)+{\bf m}_{2n+\lambda_{ij},j}[f_{j}](t)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sigma^{\lambda^{\natural}/2}}\sum_{n=2}^{p}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{(\sigma\langle v\rangle_{i}^{2})^{n+{\lambda}^{\natural}/2}f_{i}(t,v)dv}{(n!)^{\alpha}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{(\sigma\langle v\rangle_{j}^{2})^{n+{\lambda}^{\natural}/2}f_{j}(t,v)dv}{(n!)^{\alpha}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sigma^{{\lambda}^{\natural}/2}}\sum_{n=2}^{p}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\big[(\sigma\langle v\rangle_{i}^{2})^{n}-1\big]f_{i}(t,v)dv}{(n!)^{\alpha}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\big[(\sigma\langle v\rangle_{j}^{2})^{n}-1\big]f_{j}(t,v)dv}{(n!)^{\alpha}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sigma^{{\lambda}^{\natural}/2}}\sum_{n=2}^{p}\frac{\sigma^{n}}{(n!)^{\alpha}}\Big({\bf m}_{2n}[f_{i}](t)-{\bf m}_{0}[f_{i}]+{\bf m}_{2n}[f_{j}](t)-{\bf m}_{0}[f_{j}]\Big)\,.

Then, since
\sigma\in(0,1]
and
{\alpha}\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ij}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ji}[f_{j}](t)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sigma^{\lambda^{\natural}/2}}\bigg[\mathcal{E}^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{E}_{p}^{j}[f_{j}](t)-{\bf m}_{0}[f_{i}](t)-\sigma{\bf m}_{2}[f_{i}](t)

\displaystyle\quad-{\bf m}_{0}[f_{j}](t)-\sigma{\bf m}_{2}[f_{j}](t)-{\bf m}_{0}[f_{i}](t)\sum_{n=2}^{p}\frac{1}{(n!)^{\alpha}}-{\bf m}_{0}[f_{j}](t)\sum_{n=2}^{p}\frac{1}{(n!)^{\alpha}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sigma^{\lambda^{\natural}/2}}\Big[\mathcal{E}^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{E}^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)-2\,e\max\big\{{\bf m}_{0}[f_{i}],{\bf m}_{0}[f_{j}]\big\}\Big].

∎

Let us proceed with the proof the propagation of exponential moments.

Proof of the propagation result of Theorem
2.4
.

As before, we present two steps.

Step 1.
First we prove that for any
\sigma_{0}>0
and
\alpha\geq 1
there exists
\sigma>0
(sufficiently small) depending only on the parameters of the model,

\alpha\geq 1
,
\sigma_{0}>0
, and assume that
\sup_{n\geq 0}\frac{\sigma_{0}^{n}\,{\bf m}_{2n}(0)}{(n!)^{\alpha}}\leq 1

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{E}_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}

\displaystyle\bigg[-C_{ij}^{1}\Big(\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ij}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ji}[f_{j}](t)\Big)+C^{2}_{ij}\,\mathcal{B}_{\epsilon}\,\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}\,\Big[\mathcal{P}^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{P}^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+\epsilon\,C^{2}_{ij}\,\Big[\mathcal{E}^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)\mathcal{P}^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)+\mathcal{E}^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)\mathcal{P}^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)\Big]+\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}\bigg]\,,\qquad t>0\,.

we conclude that for any choice of

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{E}_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C_{ij}^{1}}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big(\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ij}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ji}[f_{j}](t)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C_{ij}^{1}}{12\,I\,e\,({\bf m}_{0}+1)}\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big(\mathcal{P}^{ji}_{p}[f_{j}](t)+\mathcal{P}^{ij}_{p}[f_{i}](t)\Big)\mathcal{E}_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)+I^{2}\mathcal{D},\qquad t>0\,.

Recall that for
\sigma\in(0,\sigma_{0}/2]

is positive. Thus, invoking Lemma
3.6
, and provided
\sigma\leq\min\{\frac{\sigma_{0}}{2},\,\sigma_{1}\}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}{\mathcal{E}}_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)\leq-\frac{\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C_{ij}^{1}}{4}\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big(\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ij}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{P}_{p}^{ji}[f_{j}](t)\Big)+I^{2}\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C_{ij}^{1}}{4\sigma^{\lambda^{\natural}/2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{I}\Big[\mathcal{E}^{i}_{p}[f_{i}](t)+\mathcal{E}^{j}_{p}[f_{j}](t)-2\,e\,\max\big\{{\bf m}_{0}[f_{i}],{\bf m}_{0}[f_{j}]\big\}\Big]+I^{2}\mathcal{D}\,,\quad t\in(0,T_{p}]\,,

and consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{E}_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)\leq-\frac{\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C_{ij}^{1}}{2\sigma^{\lambda^{\natural}/2}}\Big[\mathcal{E}_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)-2\,I\,e\,{\bf m}_{0}\Big]+I^{2}\mathcal{D}\,,\qquad t\in(0,T_{p}].

Then,

\mathcal{E}_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)\leq 2\,I\,e\,{\bf m}_{0}+\frac{2\sigma^{\lambda^{\natural}/2}\,I^{2}\,\mathcal{D}}{\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C^{1}_{ij}}\,,\qquad t\in[0,T_{p}]\,.

Choosing
\sigma\leq\min\{\frac{\sigma_{0}}{2},\,\sigma_{1}\}
sufficiently small so that

\frac{2\sigma^{\lambda^{\natural}/2}\,I^{2}\mathcal{D}}{\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C^{1}_{ij}}\leq I\,e\,{\bf m}_{0}\,,\qquad\text{that is}\qquad\sigma\leq\bigg(\frac{e\,{\bf m}_{0}\,\min_{1\leq i,j\leq I}C^{1}_{ij}}{2\,I\,\mathcal{D}}\bigg)^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}=:\sigma_{2}\,,

we conclude that, for all
p\geq 2
, it holds that

\mathcal{E}_{p}[\mathbb{F}](t)\leq 3\,I\,e\,{\bf m}_{0}\,\qquad t\in[0,T_{p}]\,.

Then, by continuity of the polynomial moments and the definition of
T_{p}
, we must have
T_{p}=\infty
for all
p\geq 2
. We conclude sending
p\rightarrow\infty
that, for
\sigma\leq\min\{1,\sigma_{0}/2,\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\}
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma^{2n/{\lambda^{\natural}}}{\bf m}_{2n}[\mathbb{F}](t)}{(n!)^{\alpha}}\leq 3\,I\,e\,\big({\bf m}_{0}+1\big)\,,\qquad t\geq 0\,.

Step 2.
Let us conclude. Fix
\rho\in(0,2]
,
\sigma_{0}\in(0,1]
, and
A>1
. Assume that

\sum^{I}_{i=1}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\exp\big(\sigma_{0}\langle v\rangle_{i}^{\rho}\big)\,f_{i}(0,v)dv\leq A\,,

and set
\alpha=\frac{2}{\rho}\geq 1
. Using Lemma
3.2
item (ii) it follows that for some
\tilde{\sigma}:=\tilde{\sigma}(\rho,\sigma_{0},A)

\sup_{n\geq 0}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}^{n}{\bf m}_{2n,i}(0)}{(n!)^{2/\rho}}\leq\frac{1}{I}\,,\qquad\text{and then},\qquad\sup_{n\geq 0}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}^{n}{\bf m}_{2n}(0)}{(n!)^{2/\rho}}\leq 1\,.

Thus, we apply Step 1 to conclude that there exists
\sigma\in(0,\tilde{\sigma}/2]
(computed is Step 1) such that

\sup_{n\geq 0}\frac{\sigma^{2n/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2n}(t)}{(n!)^{2/\rho}}\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\sigma^{2n/\lambda^{\natural}}{\bf m}_{2n}(t)}{(n!)^{2/\rho}}\leq 3\,I\,e\,\big({\bf m}_{0}+1\big)\,,\qquad t\geq 0\,.

We deduce from Lemma
3.2
item (i) (applied with
\sigma_{0}=\sigma^{2/\lambda^{\natural}}
) that, for
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\exp\Big(\frac{\sigma^{\rho/\lambda^{\natural}}\langle v\rangle_{i}^{\rho}}{2}\Big)f_{i}(t,v)dv

\displaystyle\leq 2\,{\bf m}_{0}^{1-\rho/2}\Big(3\,I\,e\,({\bf m}_{0}+1)\Big)^{\rho/2}\leq 6\,I\,e\,({\bf m}_{0}+1)\,,

which is the statement of the theorem.
∎

Proposition 4.1

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}}g_{j}(v_{*})f(v)\big(f(v^{\prime})-f(v)\big)B_{ij}(v,v_{*},\sigma)d\sigma dv_{*}dv

Corollary 4.2
.

Assume that
\mathbb{G}=[g_{j}]_{1\leq j\leq I}\in U(D_{0},E_{0})
,

and

\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q_{ij}(f,g_{j})(v)f(v)^{p-1}dv\leq-c_{i}\big\|\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\overline{\lambda}_{i}}{2}}_{i}f^{\frac{p}{2}}\big\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{\overline{\overline{s}}_{i}}{2}}}+C_{i}\big\|\langle\cdot\rangle_{i}^{\frac{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}{p}}f\big\|^{p}_{L^{p}}

for positive constants
c_{i}
and
C_{i}
depending on the parameters of the model,
D_{0}
,
E_{0}
, and

.

Proof.

This arguments is based on
[
3
, Lemma 1]
. Using the weak formulation, it holds for any fixed

that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}

\displaystyle Q_{ij}(f,g_{j})(v)f(v)^{p-1}dv

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}g_{j}(v_{*})f(v)\big(f(v^{\prime})^{p-1}-f(v)^{p-1}\big)|v-v_{*}|^{\lambda_{ij}}b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)d\sigma dv_{*}dv\,.

Using that, with notation
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
,

\displaystyle f(v)\big[f(v^{\prime})^{p-1}-f(v)^{p-1}\big]

\displaystyle=f(v)^{p}\Big[\Big(\frac{f(v^{\prime})^{\frac{p}{2}}}{f(v)^{\frac{p}{2}}}\Big)^{\frac{2}{p^{\prime}}}-1\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}f(v)^{p}\Big[\frac{f(v^{\prime})^{p}}{f(v)^{p}}-1\Big]-\frac{1}{\max\{p,p^{\prime}\}}f(v)^{p}\Big[\frac{f(v^{\prime})^{\frac{p}{2}}}{f(v)^{\frac{p}{2}}}-1\Big]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\Big[f(v^{\prime})^{p}-f(v)^{p}\Big]-\frac{1}{\max\{p,p^{\prime}\}}\Big[f(v^{\prime})^{\frac{p}{2}}-f(v)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]^{2}\,,

we conclude that

(4.6)

\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q_{ij}(f,g_{j})(v)f(v)^{p-1}dv\leq J_{1,i}(g_{j},f)-J_{2,i}(g_{j},f)\,,

where

J_{1,i}(g_{j},f):=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}g_{j}(v_{*})\Big[f(v^{\prime})^{p}-f(v)^{p}\Big]|v-v_{*}|^{\lambda_{ij}}b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)d\sigma dv_{*}dv,

and

J_{2,i}(g_{j},f):=\frac{1}{\max\{p,p^{\prime}\}}\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}g_{j}(v_{*})\Big[f(v^{\prime})^{\frac{p}{2}}-f(v)^{\frac{p}{2}}\Big]^{2}|v-v_{*}|^{\lambda_{ij}}b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)d\sigma dv_{*}dv.

For the term
J_{1}(g_{j},f)
we use the Cancellation Lemma
5.1
to obtain that

\displaystyle p^{\prime}\,J_{1,i}(g_{j},f)

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{S}|\,\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}|v-v_{*}|^{\lambda_{ij}}g_{j}(v_{*})f(v)^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Big[\frac{1}{\beta(\cos\theta)^{3+\lambda_{ij}}}-1\Big]\sin\theta\,b_{ij}(\cos\theta)d\theta dv_{*}dv

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}|v-v_{*}|^{\lambda_{ij}}g_{j}(v_{*})f(v)^{p}\,b_{i}(\cos\theta)d\theta dv_{*}dv\,,

where recall that the scattering
b_{i}
was defined as

b_{i}(\cos\theta)=|\mathcal{S}|\,\sin\theta\,\Big[\frac{1}{\beta(\cos\theta)^{3+\lambda_{ij}}}-1\Big]b_{ij}(\cos\theta)\in L^{1}\big([0,\tfrac{\pi}{2}]\big)\,.

Consequently,

(4.7)

p^{\prime}\,J_{1,i}(g_{j},f)\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq I}(m_{i}m_{j})^{-\frac{\lambda_{ij}}{2}}\|\overline{b}_{i}\|_{L^{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{I}\|\langle\cdot\rangle_{j}^{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}g_{j}\|_{L^{1}}\|\langle\cdot\rangle_{i}^{\frac{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}{p}}f\|^{p}_{L^{p}}\leq C^{\prime}_{i}\,\|\langle\cdot\rangle_{i}^{\frac{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}{p}}f\|^{p}_{L^{p}}\,.

Given that
\lambda_{ij}\leq 2
, we can take

C^{\prime}_{i}=\max_{1\leq j\leq I}(m_{i}m_{j})^{-\frac{\lambda_{ij}}{2}}\|b_{i}\|_{L^{1}}E_{0}\,.

Focusing in the term
J_{2,i}(g_{j},f)
now, we use the coercive estimate (
4.1
)

(4.8)

\max\big\{p,p^{\prime}\big\}J_{2,i}(g_{j},f)\geq\tilde{c}_{i}\big\|\langle v\rangle^{\overline{\lambda}_{i}/2}_{i}f^{\frac{p}{2}}\big\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{\overline{\overline{s}}_{i}}{2}}}-\tilde{C}_{i}\|\langle\cdot\rangle_{i}^{\frac{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}{p}}f\|^{p}_{L^{p}}\,.

The result follows from (
4.6
), (
4.7
), (
4.8
) with
c_{i}=\frac{\tilde{c}_{i}}{\max\{p,p^{\prime}\}}
and
C_{i}=\frac{C^{\prime}_{i}}{p^{\prime}}+\frac{\tilde{C}_{i}}{\max\{p,p^{\prime}\}}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Take

and multiplying the

row of the Boltzmann equation (

) by
p\,f_{i}
. Integrating on

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|f_{i}\|^{p}_{L^{p}}&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q_{ij}(f_{i},f_{j})(v)f_{i}(v)^{p-1}dv\leq-c_{i}\big\|\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\overline{\lambda}_{i}}{2}}_{i}f^{\frac{p}{2}}_{i}\big\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{\overline{\overline{s}}_{i}}{2}}}+C_{i}\|\langle\cdot\rangle_{i}^{\frac{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}{p}}f_{i}\|^{p}_{L^{p}}\end{split}

with the corresponding constants
c_{i}
and

\displaystyle\sqrt{c_{s}}\,\big\|\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\overline{\lambda}_{i}}{p}}_{i}f_{i}\big\|^{\frac{p}{2}}_{L^{\frac{p\,q_{s}}{2}}}=\sqrt{c_{s}}\,\big\|\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\overline{\lambda}_{i}}{2}}_{i}f^{\frac{p}{2}}_{i}\big\|_{L^{q_{s}}}\leq\big\|\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\overline{\lambda}_{i}}{2}}_{i}f^{\frac{p}{2}}_{i}\big\|_{H^{\frac{\overline{\overline{s}}_{i}}{2}}}\,,\qquad q_{s}:=\frac{6}{3-\overline{\overline{s}}_{i}}>2\,.

\begin{split}\big\|\langle\cdot\rangle_{i}^{\frac{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}{p}}f_{i}\big\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|\langle\cdot\rangle_{i}^{\frac{\alpha_{i}}{p}}f_{i}\|^{1-\theta_{s}}_{L^{1}}\big\|\langle v\rangle^{\frac{\overline{\lambda}_{i}}{p}}_{i}f_{i}\big\|^{\theta_{s}}_{L^{\frac{p\,q_{s}}{2}}}\end{split}

[
6

in the interval

This proves the estimate (
2.15
) when particularised to

with
C_{t_{0}}=K^{\frac{1}{p}}_{t_{0},t_{0}/2}
.

Next, take an integer

. Consequently, estimate (

This proves estimate (

∎

4.3.
L^{\infty}
Theory

Let us study in this section the particular case of
p=\infty
. We start with a coercive estimate for the levels of levels of each component. We set in the sequel

f^{+}_{K}(v):=\big(f(v)-K\big)1_{\{f_{K}\geq 0\}}\,.

Lemma 4.3
.

Take
\mathbb{G}\in U(D_{0},E_{0})
,
f
sufficiently smooth,
\lambda_{ij}\in(0,2]
and
s_{ij}\in(0,2)
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q_{ij}

\displaystyle(f,g_{j})(v)f^{+}_{K}(v)dv

\displaystyle\leq-c_{i}\|\langle v\rangle_{i}^{\frac{\overline{\lambda}_{i}}{2}}f^{+}_{K}\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{\overline{\overline{s}}_{i}}{2}}}+\tilde{C}_{i}\Big(\|\langle\cdot\rangle_{i}^{\frac{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}{2}}f^{+}_{K}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+K\,\|\langle\cdot\rangle_{i}^{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}f^{+}_{K}\|_{L^{1}}\Big)\,,\qquad K\geq 0\,,

for positive constant

\tilde{C}_{i}\lesssim C_{i}+\max_{1\leq j\leq I}\Big[\big(m_{i}m_{j}\big)^{-\frac{\lambda_{ij}}{2}}\Big]\|b_{i}\|_{L^{1}}E_{0}\,,

where the constants
c_{i}
and
C_{i}
are defined in Proposition
4.1
.

Proof.

Set
K\geq 0
, define
f_{K}(v):=f(v)-K
, and recall that
f^{+}_{k}(v)=f_{K}(v)1_{\{f_{K}\geq 0\}}
. Note that

\displaystyle f(v)\big[f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})-f_{K}^{+}(v)\big]

\displaystyle=f_{K}(v)\big[f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})-f^{+}_{K}(v)\big]+K\big[f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})-f^{+}_{K}(v)\big]

\displaystyle=f_{K}(v)\big(1_{\{f_{K}\geq 0\}}+1_{\{f_{K}<0\}}\big)\big[f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})-f^{+}_{K}(v)\big]+K\big[f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})-f^{+}_{K}(v)\big]

\displaystyle\leq f^{+}_{K}(v)\big[f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})-f^{+}_{K}(v)\big]+K\big[f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})-f^{+}_{K}(v)\big]\,,

where we used in the last step that

f_{K}(v)1_{\{f_{K}<0\}}\big[f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})-f^{+}_{K}(v)\big]=f_{K}(v)1_{\{f_{K}<0\}}f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})\leq 0\,.

Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle f(v)\big[f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})-f_{K}^{+}(v)\big]

\displaystyle=f^{+}_{K}(v)\big[f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})-f^{+}_{K}(v)\big]+K\big[f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})-f^{+}_{K}(v)\big]

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\big[f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})-f^{+}_{K}(v)\big]^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\big[\big(f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})\big)^{2}-\big(f^{+}_{K}(v)\big)^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+K\big[f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})-f^{+}_{K}(v)\big]\,.

And consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q_{ij}(f,g_{j})(v)f^{+}_{K}(v)dv

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}g_{j}(v_{*})f(v)\big[f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})-f^{+}_{K}(v)\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times|v-v_{*}|^{\lambda_{ij}}b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)d\sigma dv_{*}dv

\displaystyle\leq J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3},

where

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}g_{j}(v_{*})\big[f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})-f^{+}_{K}(v)\big]^{2}|v-v_{*}|^{\lambda_{ij}}b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)d\sigma dv_{*}dv

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}g_{j}(v_{*})\big[\big(f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})\big)^{2}-\big(f^{+}_{K}(v)\big)^{2}\big]|v-v_{*}|^{\lambda_{ij}}b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)d\sigma dv_{*}dv

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle=K\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}g_{j}(v_{*})\big[f^{+}_{K}(v^{\prime})-f^{+}_{K}(v)\big]|v-v_{*}|^{\lambda_{ij}}b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)d\sigma dv_{*}dv\,.

Using the coercive estimate given in the proof of Proposition
4.1
one deduces that

J_{1}\leq-c_{i}\big\|\langle v\rangle_{i}^{\frac{\overline{\lambda}_{i}}{2}}f^{+}_{K}\big\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{\overline{\overline{s}}_{i}}{2}}}+C_{i}\|\langle\cdot\rangle_{i}^{\frac{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}{2}}f^{+}_{K}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,.

Furthermore, using the Cancellation Lemma
5.1
for the terms
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
, it follows that

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max_{1\leq j\leq I}\Big[\big(m_{i}m_{j}\big)^{-\frac{\lambda_{ij}}{2}}\Big]\|b_{i}\|_{L^{1}}E_{0}\|\langle\cdot\rangle_{i}^{\frac{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}{2}}f^{+}_{K}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim K\max_{1\leq j\leq I}\Big[\big(m_{i}m_{j}\big)^{-\frac{\lambda_{ij}}{2}}\Big]\|b_{i}\|_{L^{1}}E_{0}\|\langle\cdot\rangle_{i}^{\overline{\overline{\lambda}}_{i}}f^{+}_{K}\|_{L^{1}}.

The result follows adding the estimates.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Fix dimension
d\geq 2
,
s\in(0,2)
and
1-\frac{s}{d}\leq\alpha\leq 1
. Then,

\|\varphi\|^{q\alpha}_{L^{q}}\leq\|\varphi\|^{2(\frac{1}{\theta}-1)}_{L^{2}}\|\varphi\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{s}{2}}}\,,

where

q:=q(s,\alpha)=2\Big(1+\frac{s}{d\alpha}\Big)>2\,,\qquad\theta:=\theta(s,\alpha)=\frac{d}{s+d\alpha}\in(0,1]\,.

Proof.

Note that for
2\leq q\leq p_{*}=\frac{2}{1-s/d}
Lebesgue’s interpolation and Sobolev inequality gives that

\|\varphi\|^{q\alpha}_{L^{q}}\leq\|\varphi\|^{(1-\theta)q\alpha}_{2}\|\varphi\|^{\theta q\alpha}_{L^{p_{*}}}\leq\|\varphi\|^{(1-\theta)q\alpha}_{2}\|\varphi\|^{\theta q\alpha}_{H^{\frac{s}{2}}}\,,\qquad\theta=\frac{d}{s}\Big(1-\frac{2}{q}\Big)\,.

Choosing
\theta q\alpha=2
we arrive to

q=2\Big(1+\frac{s}{d\alpha}\Big)\in(2,p_{*}]\,,\quad\text{and consequently}\quad\theta=\frac{d}{s+d\alpha}>0\,.

The proof follows by writing
(1-\theta)q\alpha=2(\frac{1}{\theta}-1)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.7
.

We follow the spirit of the argument used in the proof of

. For solution of the Boltzmann system we apply Lemma

5.1.
Interpolation Lemma

Lemma 5.1

(Mixed interpolation)
.

For any
b\geq a\geq 0
and
\beta>0

{\bf m}_{a+\beta,i}[f]\,{\bf m}_{b,j}[g]\leq\theta\,{\bf m}_{b+\beta,i}[f]\,{\bf m}_{a,j}[g]+(1-\theta)\,{\bf m}_{b+\beta,j}[g]\,m_{a,i}[f]\,,

with
\theta=\frac{\beta}{b+\beta-a}\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

Standard interpolation gives that

\displaystyle{\bf m}_{a+\beta,i}[f]

\displaystyle\leq{\bf m}^{\theta}_{b+\beta,i}[f]\,{\bf m}^{1-\theta}_{a,i}[f]\,,\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle{\bf m}_{b,j}[g]

\displaystyle\leq{\bf m}^{1-\theta}_{b+\beta,j}[g]\,{\bf m}^{\theta}_{a,j}[g]\,,\qquad\theta=\frac{\beta}{b+\beta-a}\,.

The result follows from here associating the common powers and applying Young’s inequality.
∎

5.2.
Cancellation Lemma

We show in this section the cancellation Lemma of Boltzmann equation for monatomic gas mixtures. All results are proven assuming a integrable approximation of the scattering kernel, so that the collision operator can be separated, and then arguing by density. For the classical Boltzmann equation the reader can consult
[
1
]
where technical details are filled.

In all the results below we assume the scattering is forward, that is,
B_{ij}
has support in the set
\{\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma\geq 0\}
.

Lemma 5.2

(Cancellation lemma)
.

For a.e.
v_{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
1\leq i,j\leq I
we have that

(5.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}B_{ij}(u,\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)\big(f(v^{\prime})-f(v)\big)dvd\sigma=(f*S_{ij})(v_{*}),\qquad u=v-v_{*}\,,

where

S_{ij}(u)=|\mathcal{S}|\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\bigg[\frac{1}{\beta(\cos\theta)^{3}}B_{ij}\Big(\frac{|u|}{\beta(\cos\theta)},\cos(\theta)\Big)-B_{ij}\big(|u|,\cos(\theta)\big)\bigg]\sin\theta\,d\theta.

Here
\beta(x)=\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}+2\alpha(1-\alpha)x}\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

For each
\sigma
and
v_{*}
fixed we preform the change of variables
v\rightarrow v^{\prime}_{ij}
. Recall that

v^{\prime}=v_{*}+\frac{m_{i}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}(v-v_{*})+\frac{m_{j}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}|v-v_{*}|\sigma\,.

This change of variables is well defined on the set
\{\cos\theta\geq 0\}
. Indeed, it follows by a direct calculation that the Jacobian is given by

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{dv^{\prime}}{dv}\Big|

\displaystyle=\alpha^{2}\big(\alpha+(1-\alpha)\,\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma\big)\,,\qquad\alpha=\frac{m_{i}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}\,,

where
\widehat{u}=\frac{v-v_{*}}{|v-v_{*}|}
. One can also find the relations between magnitude and scattering angles

\displaystyle\frac{|v^{\prime}-v_{*}|}{|v-v_{*}|}=\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}+2\alpha(1-\alpha)\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma}=:\beta(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)\in(0,1]\,,

\displaystyle\qquad\text{and}\qquad\widehat{u^{\prime}}\cdot\sigma=\frac{\alpha\,\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma+1-\alpha}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}+2\alpha(1-\alpha)\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma}}=:\phi(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)\,,

where
\widehat{u^{\prime}}=\frac{v^{\prime}-v_{*}}{|v^{\prime}-v_{*}|}
. Since
\Big|\frac{dv^{\prime}}{dv}\Big|\geq\alpha^{3}>0
in the set
\{\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma=\cos\theta\geq 0\}
, the inverse transformation
v^{\prime}\rightarrow\psi_{\sigma}(v^{\prime})=v
is, then, well defined. Similarly, note that
\phi
is also invertible and its derivative is given by

\phi^{\prime}(x)=\frac{\alpha^{2}\big(\alpha+(1-\alpha)x\big)}{\big(\alpha^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}+2\alpha(1-\alpha)x\big)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\geq\alpha^{3}>0\,,\qquad x\in[0,1]\,.

Applying this change of variable to the term
\displaystyle\int B_{ij}\,f^{\prime}
in the left-hand of (
5.1
) we find that

(5.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}B_{ij}(v-v_{*},\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)f(v^{\prime})dvd\sigma=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}B_{ij}\big(\psi_{\sigma}(v^{\prime})-v_{*},\phi^{-1}(\widehat{u^{\prime}}\cdot\sigma)\big)f(v^{\prime})\Big|\frac{dv}{dv^{\prime}}\Big|dv^{\prime}d\sigma\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(v)\int_{\phi^{-1}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)\geq 0}\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}\big(\alpha+(1-\alpha)\,\phi^{-1}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)\big)}B_{ij}\Big(\frac{|u|}{\beta(\phi^{-1}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma))},\phi^{-1}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)\Big)d\sigma dv\,.\end{split}

We renamed
v^{\prime}
to
v
before interchanging integrals in the last step. The inner integral is further expanded with polar coordinates performing the change of variables
x=\phi^{-1}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)
. In this way,

\displaystyle\int_{\phi^{-1}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)\geq 0}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}\big(\alpha+(1-\alpha)\,\phi^{-1}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)\big)}B_{ij}\Big(\frac{|u|}{\beta(\phi^{-1}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma))},\phi^{-1}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)\Big)d\sigma

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{S}|\int_{x\geq 0}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(x)}{\alpha^{2}\big(\alpha+(1-\alpha)x\big)}B_{ij}\Big(\frac{|u|}{\beta(x)},x\Big)dx

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{S}|\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{\sin\theta}{\beta(\cos\theta)^{3}}B_{ij}\Big(\frac{|u|}{\beta(\cos\theta)},\cos(\theta)\Big)d\theta\,.

In the last step we used that

\frac{\phi^{\prime}(x)}{\alpha^{2}\big(\alpha+(1-\alpha)x\big)}=\frac{1}{\beta(x)^{3}}\,.

Therefore, estimate (
5.1
) follows with

\displaystyle S_{ij}(u)=|\mathcal{S}|\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\bigg[\frac{1}{\beta(\cos\theta)^{3}}B_{ij}\big(\frac{|u|}{\beta(\cos\theta)},\cos(\theta)\big)-B_{ij}\big(|u|,\cos(\theta)\big)\bigg]\sin\theta\,d\theta.

∎

Remark 5.3
.

Note that for cross sections of the form
B_{ij}(|u|,\cos\theta)=|u|^{\lambda_{ij}}\cos\theta\,,

one has the explicit form

S_{ij}(u)=|\mathcal{S}|\,|u|^{\lambda_{ij}}\,\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\Big[\frac{1}{\beta(\cos\theta)^{3+\lambda_{ij}}}-1\Big]\sin\theta\,b_{ij}(\cos\theta)\,d\theta\geq 0\,.

5.3.
Coercive estimate of the Dirichlet form

In this subsection we explore a coercive estimate for Dirichlet form of the collision operator for monatomic gas mixtures. The argument is inspired in the classical case developed in
[
1
]
and is based in a series of lemmata.

Recall the weak formulation for a suitable functions
f
,
g
, and
\varphi(v)
,

(5.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q_{ij}^{+}(f,g)\varphi(v)dv=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}B_{ij}(v-v_{*},\sigma)g(v_{*})f(v)\varphi(v^{\prime}_{ij})dvdv_{*}d\sigma.

Lemma 5.4

(Bobylev identity)
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. For

B_{ij}(v-v_{*},\sigma)=b_{ij}\Big(\frac{v-v_{*}}{|v-v_{*}|}\cdot\sigma\Big)\,,

it holds that

\mathcal{F}\big(Q^{+}_{ij}(f,g)\big)(\xi)=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}\mathcal{F}(g)(\xi^{-}_{ij})\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi^{+}_{ij})b_{ij}\Big(\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot\sigma\Big)d\sigma\,,\qquad 1\leq i,j\leq I\,,

where
\xi=\xi^{+}_{ij}+\xi^{-}_{ij}
. More explicitly,

\xi^{+}_{ij}:=\frac{m_{i}\,\xi}{m_{i}+m_{i}}+\frac{m_{j}\,|\xi|\,\sigma}{m_{i}+m_{j}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\xi^{-}_{ij}:=\frac{m_{j}\,\xi}{m_{i}+m_{i}}-\frac{m_{j}\,|\xi|\,\sigma}{m_{i}+m_{j}}\,.

Proof.

Plugging
\varphi(v)=e^{-iv\cdot\xi}
in the weak formulation (
5.3
), we get that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\big(Q_{ij}^{+}(f,g)\big)(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times S^{2}}g(v_{*})f(v)B_{ij}(v-v_{*},\sigma)e^{-iv^{\prime}_{ij}\cdot\xi}dvdv_{*}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times S^{2}}g(v_{*})f(v)B_{ij}(v-v_{*},\sigma)e^{-i\frac{m_{i}v+m_{j}v_{*}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}\cdot\xi}e^{-\frac{m_{j}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}|v-v_{*}|\sigma\cdot\xi}dvdv_{*}d\sigma\,.

Note that, a key remark by Bobylev,

\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}\Big(\frac{v-v_{*}}{|v-v_{*}|}\cdot\sigma\Big)e^{-i\frac{m_{j}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}|v-v_{*}|\sigma\cdot\xi}d\sigma=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}\Big(\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot\sigma\Big)e^{-i\frac{m_{j}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}|\xi|\sigma\cdot(v-v_{*})}d\sigma\,.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle\big(Q_{ij}^{+}(f,g)\big)(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}g(v_{*})f(v)b_{ij}\Big(\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot\sigma\Big)e^{-i\xi\cdot\frac{m_{i}v+m_{j}v_{*}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}}e^{-i\frac{m_{j}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}|\xi|\sigma\cdot(v-v_{*})}dvdv_{*}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(v)e^{-iv\cdot\xi^{+}_{ij}}dv\bigg)\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g(v_{*})e^{-iv_{*}\cdot\xi^{-}_{ij}}dv_{*}\bigg)b_{ij}\Big(\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot\sigma\Big)d\sigma\,.

The result follows from here.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)g(v_{*})\big(f(v^{\prime}_{ij})-f(v)\big)^{2}dvdv_{*}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}\Big(\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot\sigma\Big)\Big[\mathcal{F}(g)(0)\,|\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)|^{2}+\mathcal{F}(g)(0)\,|\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi^{+}_{ij})|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\mathcal{F}(g)(\xi^{-}_{ij})\,\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi^{+}_{ij})\,\overline{\mathcal{F}(f)}(\xi)-\overline{\mathcal{F}(g)}(\xi^{-}_{ij})\,\overline{\mathcal{F}(f)}(\xi^{+}_{ij})\,\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)\Big]d\xi d\sigma,

with the same definitions of
\xi^{\pm}_{ij}
of Lemma
5.4
.

Proof.

Expanding the quadratic term gives three terms, namely,

\big(f(v^{\prime}_{ij})-f(v)\big)^{2}=f(v^{\prime}_{ij})^{2}+f(v)^{2}-2f(v^{\prime}_{ij})f(v).

We begin with the middle term. By the pre-post collisional change of variables and Parseval’s identity,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)g(v_{*})f(v)f(v^{\prime}_{ij})dvdv_{*}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q^{+}_{ij}(f,g)\,f\,dv

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{F}\big[Q^{+}_{ij}(f,g)\big]\overline{\mathcal{F}(f)}d\xi.

Furthermore, using Bobylev’s identity Lemma
5.4
in the right-side it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)g(v_{*})

\displaystyle f(v)f(v^{\prime}_{ij})dvdv_{*}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}\big(\widehat{\xi}\cdot\sigma\big)\mathcal{F}(g)(\xi^{-}_{ij})\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi^{+}_{ij})\overline{\mathcal{F}(f)}(\xi)d\xi d\sigma.

The right-side is equal to its complex conjugate since the left-side is real valued. Now, we note that
\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)d\sigma
does not depend on the unit vector
\widehat{u}\in\mathcal{S}^{2}
, consequently it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)g(v_{*})f(v)^{2}dvdv_{*}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)d\sigma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g(v_{*})dv_{*}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(v)^{2}dv

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}\big(\widehat{\xi}\cdot\sigma\big)d\sigma\mathcal{F}(g)(0)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)|^{2}d\xi,

where we have applied the usual Plancherel identity. Finally, for the term involving
f(v^{\prime})^{2}
we first make the change of variables
(v,v_{*})\rightarrow(v-v_{*},v_{*})
, and then
v\rightarrow v^{+}_{ij}
(and rename as
v
) as in Cancellation lemma to obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}

\displaystyle b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)g(v_{*})f(v^{\prime}_{ij})^{2}\,d\sigma\,dv\,dv_{*}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}b_{ij}(\widehat{v}\cdot\sigma)g(v_{*})\big|f(v^{+}_{ij}+v_{*})\big|^{2}dvd\sigma dv_{*}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g(v_{*})\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{b_{ij}\big(\phi^{-1}(\widehat{v}\cdot\sigma)\big)}{\alpha^{2}\big(\alpha+(1-\alpha)\,\phi^{-1}(\widehat{v}\cdot\sigma)\big)}\big|\tau_{-v_{*}}f(v)\big|^{2}dvd\sigma dv_{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g(v_{*})dv_{*}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}\frac{b_{ij}\big(\phi^{-1}(\widehat{\xi}\cdot\sigma)\big)}{\alpha^{2}\big(\alpha+(1-\alpha)\,\phi^{-1}(\widehat{\xi}\cdot\sigma)\big)}|\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)|^{2}d\xi d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\mathcal{F}(g)(0)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}(\widehat{\xi}\cdot\sigma)\big|\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi^{+}_{ij})\big|^{2}d\xi d\sigma,

which achieve the proof of Lemma
5.5
after adding the three respective terms.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
g\geq 0
. Then,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{S^{2}}b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)g(v_{*})\big(f(v^{\prime}_{ij})-f(v)\big)^{2}dvdv_{*}d\sigma

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)|^{2}\,\int_{S^{2}}b_{ij}\Big(\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot\sigma\Big)\Big[\mathcal{F}(g)(0)-\big|\mathcal{F}(g)(\xi^{-}_{ij})\big|\Big]d\sigma d\xi\,.

Proof.

In Lemma
5.5
use Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to control the latter two terms as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(g)(\xi^{-}_{ij})\,\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi^{+}_{ij})\,\overline{\mathcal{F}(f)}(\xi)+\overline{\mathcal{F}(g)}(\xi^{-}_{ij})\,\overline{\mathcal{F}(f)}(\xi^{+}_{ij})\,\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\big|\mathcal{F}(g)(\xi^{-}_{ij})\big|\Big(\big|\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)\big|^{2}+\big|\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi^{+}_{ij})\big|^{2}\Big)\,.

The result follows after observing that for
g\geq 0
one has that
\mathcal{F}(g)(0)-\big|\mathcal{F}(g)(\xi^{-}_{ij})\big|\geq 0
.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Suppose that
b_{ij}
satisfies assumption (
2.8
) and (
2.9
). Then, there exists a constant
K^{ij}>0
such that

\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}\Big(\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot\sigma\Big)\Big[\mathcal{F}(g)(0)-|\mathcal{F}(g)(\xi^{-}_{ij})|\Big]d\sigma\geq K^{ij}\big(|\xi|^{2}\wedge|\xi|^{s_{ij}}\big)\,.

The constant can be taken as
K^{ij}:=K^{ij}(g)=\Big(\frac{m_{j}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}\Big)^{2}\frac{1}{2-s_{ij}}\,\kappa^{1}_{ij}\,C_{g}\,|\mathcal{S}|
with the constant
C_{g}>0
depending only on
\|g\|_{L^{1}_{1}}
and
\|g\|_{L\log L}
.

Proof.

Using
[
1
, Lemma 3]
, there exists a constant
C_{g}
depending on
\|g\|_{L^{1}_{1}}
and
\|g\|_{L\log L}
such that

\mathcal{F}(g)(0)-|\mathcal{F}(g)(\xi^{-}_{ij})|\geq C_{g}\,\big(|\xi^{-}_{ij}|^{2}\wedge 1\big)\geq 0\,.

Then,

\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}\Big(\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot\sigma\Big)\big(\mathcal{F}(g_{j})(0)-|\mathcal{F}(g_{j})(\xi^{-}_{ij})|\big)d\sigma\geq C_{g}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}\Big(\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot\sigma\Big)\big(|\xi^{-}_{ij}|^{2}\wedge 1\big)d\sigma\,.

We note that

|\xi^{-}_{ij}|^{2}=2\Big(\frac{m_{j}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}\Big)^{2}|\xi|^{2}\Big(1-\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot\sigma\Big)\,.

Thus, using spherical coordinates and recalling assumption (
2.9
) it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}\Big(\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot\sigma\Big)\big(|\xi^{-}_{ij}|^{2}\wedge 1\big)d\sigma

\displaystyle=\Big(\frac{m_{j}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}\Big)^{2}|\mathcal{S}|\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin\theta\,b_{ij}(\cos\theta)\,\big[|\xi|^{2}(1-\cos\theta)\wedge 1\big]d\theta

\displaystyle\geq\Big(\frac{m_{j}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}\Big)^{2}\kappa^{1}_{ij}|\mathcal{S}|\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\big(|\xi|^{2}\theta^{2}\wedge 1\big)\frac{d\theta}{\theta^{1+s_{ij}}}\,.

Using the change of variables
\tilde{\theta}=|\xi|\theta
, this latter integral can be estimated as

|\xi|^{s_{ij}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}|\xi|}\big(\tilde{\theta}^{2}\wedge 1\big)\frac{d\tilde{\theta}}{\tilde{\theta}^{1+s_{ij}}}\geq|\xi|^{s_{ij}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\big(\tilde{\theta}^{2}\wedge 1\big)\frac{d\tilde{\theta}}{\tilde{\theta}^{1+s_{ij}}}\geq\frac{|\xi|^{s_{ij}}}{2-s_{ij}}\,,\quad\text{for}\quad|\xi|\geq 1\,.

Meanwhile, when
|\xi|\leq 1
the integral is estimated as

|\xi|^{s_{ij}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}|\xi|}\big(\tilde{\theta}^{2}\wedge 1\big)\frac{d\tilde{\theta}}{\tilde{\theta}^{1+s_{ij}}}\geq|\xi|^{s_{ij}}\int_{0}^{|\xi|}\big(\tilde{\theta}^{2}\wedge 1\big)\frac{d\tilde{\theta}}{\tilde{\theta}^{1+s_{ij}}}=\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{2-s_{ij}}\,.

This concludes the proof with the constant

K_{ij}=\Big(\frac{m_{j}}{m_{i}+m_{j}}\Big)^{2}\frac{\kappa^{1}_{ij}}{2-s_{ij}}\,C_{g}\,|\mathcal{S}|\,.

∎

Corollary
5.6
and Lemma
5.7
readily prove the main coercive estimate.

Proposition 5.8

(Coercivity estimate)
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
g\in(L^{1}_{1}\cap L\log L)(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
g\geq 0
. Then,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)g(v_{*})\big(f(v^{\prime}_{ij})-f(v)\big)^{2}dvdv_{*}d\sigma\geq\frac{K_{ij}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)|^{2}\,\big(|\xi|^{2}\wedge|\xi|^{s_{ij}}\big)\,d\sigma d\xi\,,

with constant
K_{ij}
given in Lemma
5.7
. And, as a consequence, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}b_{ij}(\widehat{u}\cdot\sigma)g(v_{*})\big(f(v^{\prime}_{ij})-f(v)\big)^{2}dvdv_{*}d\sigma\geq\frac{K_{ij}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\Big(2^{-\frac{s_{ij}}{2}}\|f\|_{H^{\frac{s_{ij}}{2}}}-\|f\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\,.

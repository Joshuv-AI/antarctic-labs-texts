Scott Complexity of Reduced Abelian p p -Groups
Rachael Alvir, Barbara F. Csima, Luke MacLean

Abstract
Given a reduced abelian p p -group, we give an upper bound on the Scott complexity of the group in terms of its Ulm invariants. For limit ordinals, we show that this upper bound is tight. This gives an explicit sequence of such groups with arbitrarily high Scott complexity below ω 1 \omega_{1} . Along the way, we give a largely algebraic characterization of the back-and-forth relations on reduced abelian p p -groups, making progress on an open problem of Ash and Knight’s from [ 4 ] .

Giving a characterization of the back-and-forth relations within a class of structures is an interesting result in its own right with a wide array of possible uses. For example, in addition to results about Scott complexity, they are often used to apply general results on intrinsically

There is a connection between Scott sentences and descriptive set theory. For example, a result of Lopez-Escobar with several strengthenings due to Vaught and D. Miller states that a structure

There are several common classes of groups for which is it known that the isomorphism relation is not a Borel equivalence relation; however, it is not usually known exactly which structures of that class achieve arbitrarily high Scott complexity and why, which is one thing our paper accomplishes. In Friedman and Stanley

Our first theorem gives a Scott sentence for an arbitrary reduced abelian
p
-group, depending on the length of the group and whether certain Ulm invariants are infinite or finite. This yields an upper bound on the Scott complexity of the group.

Using the characterization of the back-and-forth relations, we can show that the Scott sentences we give in Theorem
3.1
are optimal for arbitrarily high
\alpha

Theorem 4.5
.

Suppose that
{\mathcal{A}}
is a structure with
\lambda_{\mathcal{A}}=\omega\cdot\alpha
where
\alpha
is a limit ordinal. Then
{\mathcal{A}}
has Scott complexity
\Pi_{2\alpha+1}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
,
{\mathcal{A}}
has a Scott sentence of complexity
\Pi_{2\alpha+1}
. It remains to show that
{\mathcal{A}}
has no lower-complexity Scott sentence. To this end, it is enough to exhibit
{\mathcal{A}}\not\cong{\mathcal{B}}
such that
{\mathcal{A}}\equiv_{2\alpha}{\mathcal{B}}
. If so, then it is impossible for
{\mathcal{A}}
to have a
d
-
\Sigma_{2\alpha}
Scott sentence.

Consider
{\mathcal{B}}
of strictly longer length than
{\mathcal{A}}
, such that the Ulm invariants of both structures agree below
\omega\cdot\alpha
. It is known that any Ulm sequence with infinitely many nonzero Ulm invariants “between” each relevant pair of limit ordinals is realizable as the Ulm sequence of a reduced abelian
p
-group (see e.g.
[
10
]
) so such a group exists. Then
{\mathcal{A}}\equiv_{2\alpha}{\mathcal{B}}
by
Theorem

4.4
, part (3).
∎

5
Open Questions and Problems

We were able to prove that when
G
is finite-length and has at least two infinite Ulm invariants that our upper bounds on Scott sentences were best possible. One can ask the same question about
G
of arbitrary length:

5.1
.

Are the Scott sentences given in
Theorem

3.1
optimal in every case where there are at least two infinite Ulm invariants?

It is often of interest whether certain kinds of Scott complexities are realizable in a class of structures. In particular, it difficult to construct an example of structure of Scott complexity
\Sigma_{\lambda+1}
where
\lambda
is a limit ordinal. Our examples only show that the Scott complexity
\Pi_{\alpha}
is realizable for various
\alpha
.

5.2
.

Are the Scott complexities
\Sigma_{\alpha}
or
d
-
\Sigma_{\alpha}
realizable within the class of abelian
p
-groups? If so, for which
\alpha
?

Finally, it should be noted that the best characterization of the back-and-forth relations, an open problem from
[
4
]
, should be an entirely algebraic characterization.

5.3
.

Can one give a completely algebraic characterization of the back-and-forth relations on abelian
p
-groups?

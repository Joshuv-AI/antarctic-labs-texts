Relations Between Discrete Maximal Operators in Harmonic Analysis


Abstract
In this paper, we define several types of maximal operators on sequence spaces occuring in Harmonic analysis and present various connections between them.

Introduction

In this paper, we define several types of maximal operators on real valued sequence spaces and study relationships between them. Several such operators are defined in continuous case and studied in standard literature in harmonic analysis. We present some of them namely, Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator (both centered and non-centered), dyadic maximal operator and sharp maximal opeator. A good-
\lambda
inequality is presented which relates dyadic maximal operator and sharp maximal operator. For details of these maximal operators on real line, refer to
[
1
]
.

The method of Calderon-Zygmund decomposition on sequence spaces plays an important role in studying the relationship between these operators
[
4
]
. In the discrete case, Calderon-Zygmund decomposition uses dyadic intervals. When we study the relation between the maximal operators, we are required to double the intervals that destroys the dyadic nature of the intervals. This challenge is not there in the case of real line
[
1
]
.

Preliminaries and Notation

Throughout this paper,
\mathcal{Z}
denotes set of all integers and
\mathcal{Z}_{+}
denotes set of all positive integers. For a given interval
I
in
\mathcal{Z}
,
\lvert I\rvert
always denotes the cardinality of
I
.
For each positive integer N, consider the collection of disjoint intervals of cardinality
2^{N}
,

\left\{I_{N,j}\right\}_{j\in\mathcal{Z}}=\left\{[(j-1)2^{N}+1,\dots,j2^{N}]\right\}_{j\in\mathcal{Z}}

The set of all intervals which are of the form
I_{N,j}
where
N\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
and
j\in\mathcal{Z}
are
called dyadic intervals. For fixed
N
, we denote the set of all intervals
\left\{I_{N,j}\right\}_{j\in\mathcal{Z}}
as
\mathcal{I}_{N}
. For fixed N, the intervals in
\mathcal{I}_{N}
are disjoint.
For a dyadic interval I, we define

\displaystyle 2LI

\displaystyle=[(j-2)2^{N}+1,\dots,j2^{N}]

\displaystyle 2RI

\displaystyle=[(j-1)2^{N}+1,\dots,(j+1)2^{N}]

\displaystyle 3I

\displaystyle=[(j-2)2^{N}+1,\dots,(j+1)2^{N}]

Note
\lvert 3I\rvert=3.2^{N}
. Note that
2LI,2RI
are dyadic intervals each of length
2^{N+1}
but
3I
is not an dyadic interval.

The Calderon-Zygmund decomposition theorem for sequences
[
4
]
is as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and a
\in\ell^{p}(\mathcal{Z})
. For every
t>0
, and
0\leq\alpha<1
, there exists a sequence of disjoint dyadic intervals
\left\{I_{j}^{t}\right\}
such that

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\quad t<\frac{1}{\lvert I_{j}^{t}\rvert^{1-\alpha}}\sum_{k\in I_{j}^{t}}\lvert a(k)\rvert\leq 2t,\forall j\in\mathcal{Z}

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\quad\forall n\not\in\cup_{j}I_{j}^{t},\quad\lvert a(n\rvert\leq t

\displaystyle(iii)

\displaystyle\quad\text{If}\quad t_{1}>t_{2},\quad\textbf{then each}\quad I_{j}^{t_{1}}\quad\textbf{is subinterval of some}\quad I_{m}^{t_{2}},\quad\forall j,m\in\mathcal{Z}

An operator
T
is bounded on
\ell^{p}(\mathcal{Z})
if
\forall a\in\ell^{p}(\mathcal{Z})

\left\lVert Ta\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}(\mathcal{Z})}\leq C_{p}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}(\mathcal{Z})}

An operator
T
is of weak type (1,1) on
\ell^{p}(\mathcal{Z})
if for each
a\in\ell_{1}(\mathcal{Z})

\lvert\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:\lvert Ta(m)\rvert>\lambda\right\}\rvert\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{1}

For
\left\{a(n):n\in\mathcal{Z}\right\}\in\ell^{p}(\mathcal{Z})
, norm in
\ell^{p}(\mathcal{Z})
(refer to
[
1
]
) is given by

\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}(\mathcal{Z})}=\int_{0}^{\infty}p{\lambda}^{p-1}\lvert\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:\lvert a(m)\rvert>\lambda\right\}\rvert d\lambda

Definitions

3.1
Maximal Operators

Let
\left\{a(n):n\in\mathcal{Z}\right\}
be a sequence. We define the following three types of Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators as follows:

Definition 3.1
.

If
I_{r}
is the interval
\left\{-r,-r+1,\dots,0,1,2,\dots,r-1,r\right\}
, define centered Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator

M^{\prime}a(m)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{(2r)}\sum_{n\in I_{r}}\lvert a(m-n)\rvert

We define Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator as follows

Ma(m)=\sup_{m\in I}\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{n\in I}\lvert a(n)\rvert

where the supremum is taken over all intervals containing
m
.

Definition 3.2
.

We define dyadic Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator as follows:

M_{d}a(m)=\sup_{m\in I}\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{k\in I}\lvert a(k)\rvert

where supremum is taken over all dyadic intervals containing
m
.

Given a sequence
\left\{a(n):n\in\mathcal{Z}\right\}
and an interval
I
, let
a_{I}
denote average of
\left\{a(n):n\in\mathcal{Z}\right\}
on
I
, i.e
a_{I}=\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{m\in I}a(m)
. Define the sharp maximal operator
M^{\#}
as follows

M^{\#}a(m)=\sup_{m\in I}\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{n\in I}\lvert a(n)-a_{I}\rvert

where the supremum is taken over all intervals
I
containing
m
. We say that sequence
\left\{a(n):n\in\mathcal{Z}\right\}
has bounded mean oscillation if the
sequence
M^{\#}a
is bounded. The space of sequences with this property is called sequences of bounded mean oscillation and is denoted by BMO(
\mathcal{Z}
).
We define a norm in BMO(
\mathcal{Z}
) by
\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\star}=\left\lVert M^{\#}a\right\rVert_{\infty}
. The space BMO(
\mathcal{Z}
) is studied in
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
.

4
Relation between Maximal operators

Theorem 4.1
.

Given a sequence
\left\{a(m):m\in\mathcal{Z}\right\}
, the following relation holds:

M^{\prime}a(m)\leq Ma(m)\leq 3M^{\prime}a(m)

Proof.

First inequality is obvious as
M^{\prime}a
considers supremum over centered intervals, while
M
considers supremum over all intervals.
For second inequality,
let
I=[m-r_{1},m-r_{1}+1,\dots,m+r_{2}-1,m+r_{2}]
containing
m
. Let
r=max\left\{r_{1},r_{2}\right\}
. Consider
I_{1}=[m-r,m-r+1,\dots,m+r-1,m+r]
containing
m
. Note that
\lvert I_{1}\rvert=2r+1,\lvert I\rvert=r_{1}+r_{2}+1
.
Then

\lvert I\rvert=r_{2}+r_{1}+1\geq r=\frac{1}{3}3r\geq\frac{1}{3}(2r+1)=\frac{1}{3}\lvert I_{1}\rvert

This gives

\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{k\in I}\lvert a(k)\rvert\leq\frac{3}{\lvert I_{1}\rvert}\sum_{k\in I_{1}}a(k)\leq 3M^{\prime}a(m)

∎

Theorem 4.2
.

If
\textbf{a}=\left\{a(k):k\in\mathcal{Z}\right\}
is a sequence with
\textbf{a}\in\ell_{1}
, then

\lvert\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:M^{\prime}a(m)>4\lambda\right\}\rvert\leq 3\lvert\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:M_{d}a(m)>\lambda\right\}\rvert

Proof.

Using Calderon Zygmund decomposition at height
\lambda
, we obtain a collection of disjoint dyadic intervals
\left\{I_{j}:j\in\mathcal{Z}^{+}\right\}
such that

\lambda<\frac{1}{\lvert I_{j}\rvert}\sum_{k\in I_{j}}\lvert a(k)\rvert\leq 2\lambda

Then

\cup_{j}I_{j}\subseteq\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:M_{d}a(m)>\lambda\right\}

It suffices to show that

\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:M^{\prime}a(m)>4\lambda\right\}\subset\cup_{j}3I_{j}

Let
m\notin\cup_{j}3I_{j}
. We shall prove
m\notin\left\{k\in\mathcal{Z}:M^{\prime}a(k)>4\lambda\right\}
.
Let
I
be any interval centered at
m
. Choose
N\in\mathcal{Z}_{+}
such that
2^{N-1}\leq\lvert I\rvert<2^{N}
. Then
I
intersects exactly 2 dyadic intervals in
\mathcal{I}_{N}
say
R_{1},R_{2}
. Assume
R_{1}
intersects
I
on the left and
R_{2}
intersects
I
on the right. Since
m\notin\cup_{j=1}^{\infty}3I_{j}
,
m\notin 2RI_{j},j=1\dots
and
m\notin 2LI_{j},j=1,\dots
. But
m\in 2RR_{1}
and
m\in 2LR_{2}
.

Therefore, both
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
cannot be any one of
I_{j}
.

Hence, the average of
\left\{a(n):n\in\mathcal{Z}\right\}
on each
R_{i},i=1,2
is at most
\lambda
. Further note that
\frac{\lvert R_{1}\rvert}{\lvert I\rvert}\leq 2,\frac{\lvert R_{2}\rvert}{\lvert I\rvert}\leq 2
. So

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{m\in I}\lvert a(m)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\biggl(\sum_{k\in R_{1}}\lvert a(k)\rvert+\sum_{k\in R_{2}}\lvert a(k)\rvert\biggr)

\displaystyle=\biggl(\frac{1}{\lvert R_{1}\rvert}\frac{\lvert R_{1}\rvert}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{k\in R_{1}}\lvert a(k)\rvert+\frac{1}{\lvert R_{2}\rvert}\frac{\lvert R_{2}\rvert}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{k\in R_{2}}\lvert a(k)\rvert\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq 2\biggl(\frac{1}{\lvert R_{1}\rvert}\sum_{k\in R_{1}}\lvert a(k)\rvert+\frac{1}{\lvert R_{2}\rvert}\sum_{k\in R_{2}}\lvert a(k)\rvert\biggr)=2(\lambda+\lambda)=4\lambda

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

For a sequence
\left\{a(n):n\in\mathcal{Z}\right\}
, if
M_{d}a\in\ell^{p}(\mathcal{Z}),1<p<\infty
, then

\left\lVert M^{\prime}a\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}(\mathcal{Z})}\leq C\left\lVert M_{d}a\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}(\mathcal{Z})}

Proof.

\displaystyle\left\lVert M^{\prime}a\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}(\mathcal{Z})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}p\lambda^{p-1}\lvert\left\{m:M^{\prime}a(m)>\lambda\right\}\rvert d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq 3(4)^{p-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}p({\frac{\lambda}{4}})^{p-1}\lvert\left\{m:M_{d}a(m)>\frac{\lambda}{4}\right\}\rvert d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq 3(4)^{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}pu^{p-1}\lvert\left\{m:M_{d}a(m)>u\right\}\rvert du

\displaystyle\leq 3(4)^{p}\left\lVert M_{d}a\right\rVert_{\ell^{p}(\mathcal{Z})}

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Consider a non-negative sequence
\textbf{a}=\left\{a(k):k\in\mathcal{Z}\right\}
. Then the following are valid.

\displaystyle 1.

\displaystyle\quad\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\star}\leq\sup_{m\in I}\inf_{b\in{\mathcal{R}}}\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\lvert a(m)-b\rvert\leq\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\star}

\displaystyle 2.

\displaystyle\quad M^{\#}(\lvert a\rvert)(i)\leq M^{\#}a(i),i\in\mathcal{Z}

Proof.

For first inequality, note for all
b\in{\mathcal{R}}
,

\sum_{m\in I}\lvert a(m)-a_{I}\rvert\leq\sum_{m\in I}\lvert a(m)-b\rvert+\sum_{m\in I}\lvert b-a_{I}\rvert=A+B(say)

Now

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\lvert I\rvert\lvert b-a_{I}\rvert=\lvert I\rvert\lvert b-\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\sum_{k\in I}a(k)\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert I\rvert\lvert\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\biggl(\sum_{k\in I}(b-a(k))\biggr)\rvert\leq\sum_{k\in I}\lvert b-a(k)\rvert

So,

\sum_{m\in I}\lvert a(m)-a_{I}\rvert\leq\sum_{m\in I}\lvert a(m)-b\rvert+\sum_{m\in I}\lvert b-a_{I}\rvert\leq 2\sum_{m\in I}\lvert a(m)-b\rvert

Now, divide both sides by
\lvert I\rvert
, and take infimum over all
b
followed by, supremum over all
I
. This proves

\quad\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\star}\leq\sup_{m\in I}\inf_{b\in{\mathcal{R}}}\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\lvert a(m)-b\rvert

The proof for second inequality

\sup_{m\in I}\inf_{b\in{\mathcal{R}}}\frac{1}{\lvert I\rvert}\lvert a(m)-b\rvert\leq\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{\star}

is obvious.

The proof of (2) follows from the fact that
\lvert\lvert a\rvert-\lvert b\rvert\rvert\leq\lvert a\rvert-\lvert b\rvert
for any
a,b\in{\mathcal{R}}
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

If
a\in\ell^{p_{0}}(\mathcal{Z})
for some
p_{0}
,
1\leq p_{0}<\infty
, then for all
\gamma>0
and
\lambda>0

\lvert\left\{n\in\mathcal{Z}:M_{d}a(n)>2\lambda,M^{\#}a(n)\leq\gamma\lambda\right\}\rvert\leq 2\gamma\lvert\left\{n\in\mathcal{Z}:M_{d}a(n)>\lambda\right\}\rvert

Proof.

Perform Calderon-Zygmumd decomposition for the sequence
\left\{a(n):n\in\mathcal{Z}\right\}
at height
\lambda
, which gives collection of intervals
\left\{I_{j}\right\}
such that for each
j
,

\lambda\leq\frac{1}{\lvert I_{j}\rvert}\sum_{k\in I_{j}}\lvert a(k)\rvert\leq 2\lambda

Let
I
be one of the interval in the collection
\left\{I_{j}\right\}
. In Calderon-Zygmund decomposition, there exists interval
\tilde{I}
such that
\tilde{I}
is either
2RI
or
2LI
and

\frac{1}{\lvert\tilde{I}\rvert}\sum_{k\in\tilde{I}}\lvert a(k)\rvert\leq\lambda

For details, refer to section on Preliminaries and Notation.

It is easy to observe that
\forall m\in I
,
M_{d}a(m)>2\lambda
implies
M_{d}(a\chi_{I})(m)>2\lambda
.
Let,

\displaystyle a_{1}

\displaystyle=(a-a_{\tilde{I}})\chi_{I}

\displaystyle a_{2}

\displaystyle=a_{\tilde{I}}\chi_{I}

Then, since
M_{d}
is sublinear

\displaystyle a_{1}+a_{2}

\displaystyle=(a-a_{\tilde{I}})\chi_{I}+a_{\tilde{I}}\chi_{I}

\displaystyle M_{d}(a_{1}+a_{2})

\displaystyle\leq M_{d}((a-a_{\tilde{I}})\chi_{I})+M_{d}(a_{\tilde{I}}\chi_{I})

\displaystyle\leq M_{d}((a-a_{\tilde{I}})\chi_{I})+(a_{\tilde{I}})

Since
M_{d}(a_{\tilde{I}}\chi_{I})(k)\leq a_{\tilde{I}}\quad\forall k
, it follows that

M_{d}(a_{1}+a_{2})=M_{d}(a\chi_{I})\leq M_{d}((a-a_{\tilde{I}})\chi_{I})+(a_{\tilde{I}})

Hence for every,
k\in I
, it follows that

M_{d}((a-a_{\tilde{I}})\chi_{I})(k)\geq M_{d}(a\chi_{I})(k)-a_{\tilde{I}}

So, for those
k^{\prime}s
,

M_{d}((a-a_{\tilde{I}})\chi_{I})(k)\geq M_{d}(a\chi_{I})(k)-a_{\tilde{I}}>\lambda

By remark [
4.4
], using
weak(1,1)
inequality for
M_{d}

\displaystyle\left\{k\in\mathcal{Z}:M_{d}((a-a_{\tilde{I}})\chi_{I}))(k)>\lambda\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\sum_{I}\lvert a(k)-a_{\tilde{I}}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{{\lambda}}\lvert I\rvert\frac{C}{\lvert\tilde{I}\rvert}\sum_{\tilde{I}}\lvert a(k)-a_{\tilde{I}}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C}{{\lambda}}\lvert I\rvert\inf_{m\in I}M^{\#}a(m)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C}{{\lambda}}\gamma\lambda\lvert I\rvert=2C\gamma\lvert I\rvert

∎

As a consequence of good-
\lambda
inequality, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\left\{a(n):n\in\mathcal{Z}\right\}
be a nonnegative sequence in
\ell^{p}(\mathcal{Z}),1<p<\infty
.Then

\sum_{m\in\mathcal{Z}}\lvert M_{d}a(m)\rvert^{p}\leq C\sum_{m\in\mathcal{Z}}\lvert M^{\#}a(m)\rvert^{p}

where
M_{d}
is the dyadic maximal operator and
M^{\#}
is the sharp maximal operator, whenever, the left hand side is finite.

Proof.

For a positive integer
N>0
, let

I_{N}=\int_{0}^{N}p\lambda^{p-1}\lvert\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:M_{d}a(m)>\lambda\right\}\rvert d\lambda

I_{N}
is finite, since
a\in\ell^{p}(\mathcal{Z})
implies
M_{d}a\in\ell^{p}(\mathcal{Z})

\displaystyle I_{N}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{N}p\lambda^{p-1}\lvert\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:M_{d}a(m)>\lambda\right\}\rvert d\lambda

\displaystyle=2^{p}\int_{0}^{\frac{N}{2}}p\lambda^{p-1}\lvert\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:M_{d}a(m)>2\lambda\right\}\rvert d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}\int_{0}^{\frac{N}{2}}p\lambda^{p-1}\lvert\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:M_{d}a(m)>2\lambda,M^{\#}a(m)\leq\gamma\lambda\right\}\rvert d\lambda+

\displaystyle 2^{p}\int_{0}^{\frac{N}{2}}p\lambda^{p-1}\lvert\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:M_{d}a(m)>2\lambda,M^{\#}a(m)>\gamma\lambda\right\}\rvert d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}\int_{0}^{\frac{N}{2}}p\lambda^{p-1}C{\gamma}\lvert\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:M_{d}a(m)>\lambda\right\}\rvert d\lambda+

\displaystyle 2^{p}\int_{0}^{\frac{N}{2}}p\lambda^{p-1}\lvert\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:M^{\#}a(m)>\gamma\lambda\right\}\rvert d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p}C{\gamma}\int_{0}^{N}p\lambda^{p-1}\lvert\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:M_{d}a(m)>\lambda\right\}\rvert d\lambda\quad+

\displaystyle 2^{p}\int_{0}^{\frac{N}{2}}p\lambda^{p-1}\lvert\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:M^{\#}a(m)>\gamma\lambda\right\}\rvert d\lambda

It follows that

(1-2^{p}C{\gamma})I_{N}\leq 2^{p}\int_{0}^{\frac{N}{2}}p\lambda^{p-1}\lvert\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:M^{\#}a(m)>\gamma\lambda\right\}\rvert d\lambda

Now choose
\gamma=\frac{1}{C2^{p+1}}
such that
(1-2^{p}C{\gamma})=\frac{1}{2}
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}I_{N}\leq 2^{p}\int_{0}^{\frac{N}{2}}p\lambda^{p-1}\lvert\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:M^{\#}a(m)>\gamma\lambda\right\}\rvert d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{p}}{\gamma^{p}}\int_{0}^{\frac{N}{2}}p\lambda^{p-1}\lvert\left\{m\in\mathcal{Z}:M^{\#}a(m)>\lambda\right\}\rvert d\lambda

Now, take
N\to\infty
, we get

\sum_{m\in\mathcal{Z}}M_{d}{a(m)}^{p}\leq C\sum_{m\in\mathcal{Z}}{M^{\#}a(m)}^{p}

∎

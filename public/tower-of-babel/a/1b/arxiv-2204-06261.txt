Abstract

When the underlying form is self-dual, we show that there are ≫ ε X 5 / 6 − ε \gg_{\varepsilon}X^{5/6-\varepsilon} sign changes among the coefficients { A ⁡ ( m , 1 ) } m ≤ X \{A(m,1)\}_{m\leq X} and that there is a positive proportion of sign changes for many self-dual forms. 1  Introduction

Signs of Fourier coefficients of automorphic forms have been connected to classical problems in number theory and their study has attracted a lot of interest recently. For example, in
[
17
]
the authors related the question of finding the first sign change among the Fourier coefficients of a classical modular form to the question of finding the least quadratic non-residue modulo
d
, which has historically been a central problem in analytic number theory. More precisely, the connection is the following. Automorphic forms can be thought of as higher rank analogues of Dirichlet characters and it is natural to study whether properties of lower rank objects generalise to the higher rank setting. The problem of finding the least quadratic non-residue modulo
d
concerns finding the least natural number
m
so that
\chi_{d}(m)=-1
, where
\chi_{d}
is the real Dirichlet character modulo
d
. It is not obvious how to formulate an analogue for this in the higher rank setting. Namely, the real Dirichlet character
\chi_{d}
attains only two non-zero values (
+1
and
-1
) and these (non-zero) values are distributed similarly as are independent random variables taking values
\pm 1
with equal probability. Contrast this to Hecke eigenvalues (which match to certain Fourier coefficients when the underlying form is properly normalised) of Maass cusp forms for the group
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, which take more than two non-zero values and are (conjecturally in the higher rank setting) distributed according to the (generalised) Sato-Tate measure. Despite these differences, the authors were able to retain the analogy when considering signs of the Hecke eigenvalues instead of their values. In particular, the analogue for finding the least quadratic non-residue is to find the first negative Hecke eigenvalue.

In addition to the work discussed above, the signs have been studied from many different points of view for both classical modular forms and their generalisations (see e.g.
[
16
,
31
,
24
,
22
,
26
]
). Besides finding the first negative Fourier coefficient, one can estimate the number of forms (in some family) for which the signs of the Fourier coefficients at primes lying in certain region are specified. Yet another interesting question is whether a primitive cusp form is determined uniquely by the sequence of signs of its Fourier coefficients at primes. Finally, one may ask how many sign changes does the sequence of Fourier coefficients have up to some point? For more complete survey on these topics and related results, see
[
19
]
. The last question is the one we study in the present article. We do not consider the situation where for instance
\lambda(m)>0
and
\lambda(m+1)=0
to be a sign change, because the sign of zero is undefined. Thus, by number of sign changes of a possibly vanishing sequence
\{\lambda(m)\}_{m}
we mean the number of sign changes of
\lambda(m)
on the subset of
m
for which
\lambda(m)\neq 0
.

For classical Hecke cusp forms (rank two case) Matomäki and Radziwiłł
[
32
]
have shown that there is a positive proportion of sign changes among the Fourier coefficients. Related to this, our aim is to establish a non-trivial lower bound for the number of sign changes for higher rank cusp forms. Specifically we will consider Hecke-Maass cusp forms for the group
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
(rank three case). Such forms admit a Fourier expansion
[
6
, Equation 6.2.1]
with Fourier coefficients
A(m,n)
indexed by pairs of integers. The most interesting among these coefficients are those of the form
A(m,1)
as these are the Hecke eigenvalues when the underlying form is normalised so that
A(1,1)=1
. There is a subtle point that the coefficients
A(m,n)
are not generally real-valued. Fortunately they are so in special instances, and thus the sign change problem is well-defined when this happens. What is relevant for the present paper is that the coefficients
A(m,1)
are real-valued when the underlying form is self-dual and that the coefficients
A(m,m)
are real-valued for any Hecke-Maass cusp form.

The proof of Theorem

[

[
9

[
32
]

\displaystyle S_{1}(x;H):=\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq mk\leq x+H\\
m\sim M\\
(k,m)=1\end{subarray}}A(mk,1)\right|\quad\text{and}\quad S_{2}(x;H):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\leq mk\leq x+H\\
m\sim M\\
(k,m)=1\end{subarray}}|A(mk,1)|,

where
M<H
is a small power of

[x,x+H]
. Our aim is now to show that for some small
H
we have

for
\gg_{\varepsilon}X^{1-\varepsilon}
of

and so there is a sign change in the interval
[x,x+H]
for such
x\sim X
. This clearly implies that there are at least
\gg_{\varepsilon}X^{1-\varepsilon}H^{-1}
sign changes for any
\varepsilon>0
. Unconditionally the smallest value of
H
we can take turns out to be
\approx X^{1/6}
(more generally, for
\theta>1/2

and for

H
to be an arbitrarily small power of
X

and
S_{2}(x;H)
leads to an improved exponent. Indeed, unconditionally our argument would only yield

sign changes for the Hecke eigenvalues when comparing the quantities

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{x\leq m\leq x+H}A(m,1)\right|\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{x\leq m\leq x+H}\left|A(m,1)\right|.

This matches the amount of sign changes which follows from the convexity bound of

.

There are some differences compared to the treatment in
[
32
]

\text{diag}(2/3,-1/3,-1/3)
,
\text{diag}(1/3,1/3,-2/3)
of
\mathrm{SL}_{3}
. The coefficients of
\ell=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
with respect to this basis can be obtained by evaluating
\ell
at the two co-roots
\text{diag}(1,-1,0)
,
\text{diag}(0,1,-1)\in\mathfrak{a}
and they are given by
3\nu_{1},3\nu_{2}
. In this case we say that
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
is the type of the form or that
\nu_{1}
and
\nu_{2}
are the spectral parameters of the form. We then have the following relations between the Langlands parameters and the spectral parameters:
\alpha_{1}=2\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}
,
\alpha_{2}=-\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}
,
\alpha_{3}=-\nu_{1}-2\nu_{2}
. It is also convenient to define
\nu_{3}:=-\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}
. For any finite prime
p
, the local representation
\pi_{p}
has a
\mathrm{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
-fixed vector and the space of such vectors is one-dimensional. The local Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{p}
acts on this space of invariant vectors. The Satake isomorphism tells that the algebra
\mathcal{H}_{p}
has three generators and the eigenvalues of these generators when acting on the space of
\mathrm{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
-fixed vectors are called the Satake parameters of
\phi
at
p
. They will be denoted by
\alpha_{1,p}(\phi)
,
\alpha_{2,p}(\phi)
and
\alpha_{3,p}(\phi)
. Sometimes we will write
\alpha_{\phi}(p):=(\alpha_{1,p}(\phi),\alpha_{2,p}(\phi),\alpha_{3,p}(\phi))\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
.

With the above normalisation,
\phi
is an eigenform of the Laplacian with eigenvalue

\displaystyle 1-3\nu_{1}^{2}-3\nu_{1}\nu_{2}-3\nu_{2}^{2}=1-\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}+\alpha_{3}^{2}).

The Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture predicts that the Langlands parameters
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}
of Maass cusp forms are purely imaginary (equivalently, the spectral parameters
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
are purely imaginary). A Maass cusp form is called exceptional if it violates the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture. An equivalent formulation of the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture in terms of the Fourier coefficients is that
|A(p,1)|\leq 3
for any prime
p
(similarly, in the general
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
-setting the conjecture states
|A(p,1,...,1)|\leq n
for all primes
p
). We shall write bounds towards the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture as
|A(p,1)|\leq 3p^{\vartheta}
for some
\vartheta\geq 0
. Currently it is only known that
\vartheta\leq 5/14
unconditionally
[
15
]
, but the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture of course predicts that
\vartheta=0
is admissible (for classical Maass cusp forms we currently know that
|\lambda(p)|\leq 2p^{7/64}
unconditionally). However, for any fixed prime
p
, the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture in
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
is known to hold for almost all forms meaning that the density of the set of exceptional forms is zero as was recently shown by Lau, Ng, and Wang
[
21
]
. We formulate a version of their result, which is suitable for our needs. To state this we need some notation. Let
\Omega\subset i\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
be a compact Weyl-group invariant subset disjoint from the Weyl chamber walls
\{\mu\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}:w.\mu=\mu\,\text{for some }w\in W,w\neq 1\}
and
T>1
be a large parameter. Fix some
\nu_{0}\in\Omega
. For
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
we set
\psi(\nu):=\exp(3(\nu_{1}^{2}+\nu_{2}^{2}+\nu_{3}^{2}))
and

\displaystyle P(\nu):=\prod_{0\leq n\leq A}\prod_{j=1}^{3}\frac{\nu_{j}^{2}-\frac{1}{9}(1+2n)^{2}}{T^{2}}

for some large, fixed
A
to compensate poles of the spectral measure in a large tube. Now we define the weight function

(1)

\displaystyle h_{T}(\nu):=P(\nu)^{2}\left(\sum_{w\in W}\psi\left(\frac{w.\nu-T\nu_{0}}{T^{1-\eta}}\right)\right)^{2}

for some very small
\eta>0
. Note that
h_{T}
is non-negative and
h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})\asymp 1
for
\phi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{T}
. We write
X_{p}
for the set of Hecke-Maass cusp forms for the group
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
not satisfying the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture at
p
. Then it follows from
[
21
]
that

(2)

\displaystyle\sum_{\phi\in X_{p}}h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})\ll\left(\sum_{\phi}h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})\right)\left(\frac{\log p}{\log T}\right)^{3}.

Let
\phi
be a Maass cusp form of type
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
for the group
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
. By analogue to the classical situation, it follows that for every integer
m\geq 1
, there is a Hecke operator given by

\displaystyle T_{m}\phi(z):=\frac{1}{m^{5/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\prod_{\ell=1}^{3}c_{\ell}=m\\
0\leq c_{i,\ell}<c_{\ell}\,\,(1\leq i<\ell\leq 3)\end{subarray}}\phi\left(\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}&c_{1,2}&c_{1,3}\\
&c_{2}&c_{2,3}\\
&&c_{3}\end{pmatrix}\cdot z\right)

acting on the space
L^{2}(\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}_{3})
of square-integrable automorphic functions. Unlike in the classical situation, these operators are not self-adjoint, but they are normal. If a Maass cusp form
\phi
is an eigenfunction of every Hecke operator, it is called a Hecke-Maass cusp form. We remark that if the Fourier coefficient
A(1,1)
is zero, then the form vanishes identically. For more about the theory of Hecke operators for the group
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
, see
[
6
, Section 9.3.]
.

Fourier coefficients and Satake parameters of a given Hecke-Maass cusp form are closely related by the work of Shintani
[
42
]
together with results of Casselman and Shalika
[
5
]
. They showed that for any prime number
p
and
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\cup\{0\}
one has

(3)

\displaystyle A_{\phi}(p^{\beta_{1}},p^{\beta_{2}})=S_{\beta_{2},\beta_{1}}(\alpha_{1,p}(\phi),\alpha_{2,p}(\phi),\alpha_{3,p}(\phi)),

where

(4)

\displaystyle S_{\beta_{2},\beta_{1}}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}):=\frac{1}{V(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})}\det\left[\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}^{2+\beta_{2}+\beta_{1}}&x_{2}^{2+\beta_{2}+\beta_{1}}&x_{3}^{2+\beta_{2}+\beta_{1}}\\
x_{1}^{1+\beta_{2}}&x_{2}^{1+\beta_{2}}&x_{3}^{1+\beta_{2}}\\
1&1&1\end{pmatrix}\right]

is a Schur polynomial, and
V(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
is the Vandermonde determinant given by

\displaystyle V(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}):=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}(x_{i}-x_{j}).

Next, we define an important notion of a dual Maass cusp form. Let

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}(z):=\phi(w\cdot\,^{t}(z^{-1})\cdot w),\quad\text{where}\qquad w:=\begin{pmatrix}&&-1\\
&1&\\
1&&\end{pmatrix}.

Then
\widetilde{\phi}
is a Maass cusp form of type
(\nu_{2},\nu_{1})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
for the group
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
and it is called the dual Maass cusp form of
\phi
. We say that Hecke-Maass form
\phi
is self-dual if
\phi=\widetilde{\phi}
. It turns out that

(5)

\displaystyle A_{\phi}(m_{1},m_{2})=A_{\widetilde{\phi}}(m_{2},m_{1})

for every
m_{1},m_{2}\geq 1
.

The Fourier coefficients of a Hecke-Maass cusp form satisfy the multiplicativity relation

(6)

\displaystyle A(m,1)A(m_{1},m_{2})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\prod_{\ell=1}^{3}c_{\ell}=m\\
c_{j}|m_{j}\,\,\text{for }1\leq j\leq 2\end{subarray}}A\left(\frac{m_{1}c_{3}}{c_{1}},\frac{m_{2}c_{1}}{c_{2}}\right),

which, together with Möbius inversion, gives

(7)

\displaystyle A(m_{1},m_{2})=\sum_{d|(m_{1},m_{2})}\mu(d)A\left(\frac{m_{1}}{d},1\right)A\left(1,\frac{m_{2}}{d}\right)

for positive integers
m,m_{1}
, and a non-negative integer
m_{2}
. Furthermore, the relation

\displaystyle A(m_{1},m_{2})A(m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}^{\prime})=A(m_{1}m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}m_{2}^{\prime})

holds if
(m_{1}m_{2},m_{1}^{\prime}m_{2}^{\prime})=1
. In particular, the coefficients
A(m,1)
and
A(m,m)
are multiplicative. For the proofs of these facts, see
[
6
, Theorem 9.3.11.]

For a Hecke eigenfunction, one can use Möbius inversion to show that the relation

\displaystyle A(m_{1},m_{2})=\overline{A(m_{2},m_{1})}

holds
[
6
, Theorem 9.3.6, Theorem 9.3.11, Addendum]
. In particular, together with the relation (
5
) this yields that

\displaystyle\overline{A_{\phi}(m,1)}=A_{\widetilde{\phi}}(m,1),

and consequently the Hecke eigenvalues
A(m,1)
are real-valued for self-dual forms. The fact that
A(m,m)
is always real-valued follows from (
7
).

Associated to the form
\phi
is the
L
-series given by

\displaystyle L(s,\phi):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{A_{\phi}(m,1)}{m^{s}},

which converges for
\sigma>1
. This has an entire continuation to the whole complex
plane via the functional equation

(8)

\displaystyle L(s,\phi)=\pi^{3s-3/2}\frac{G(1-s,\widetilde{\phi})}{G(s,\phi)}L(1-s,\widetilde{\phi}),

where

\displaystyle G(s,\phi):=\prod_{j=1}^{3}\Gamma\left(\frac{s-\alpha_{j}}{2}\right)\quad\text{and so }\quad G(s,\widetilde{\phi})=\prod_{j=1}^{3}\Gamma\left(\frac{s-\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}}{2}\right).

Recall that here
\alpha_{j}
and
\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}
are the Langlands parameters of
\phi
and
\widetilde{\phi}
, respectively. This
defines an
L
-function attached to the form
\phi
called the Godement-Jacquet
L
-function. For more information, see
[
6
, Chapter 6.5.]
. The generalised Lindelöf hypothesis predicts that

\displaystyle L\left(\frac{1}{2}+it,\phi\right)\ll_{\varepsilon}(3+|t|)^{\varepsilon}

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Currently we know that

\displaystyle L\left(\frac{1}{2}+it,\phi\right)\ll_{\varepsilon}(3+|t|)^{3/5+\varepsilon}

for self-dual
\phi

[
27
]
.

In what follows we will make use of the following result of Jiang and Lü
[
12
]
.

Lemma 7
.

For a self-dual Hecke eigenform we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m\leq x}|A(m,1)|\gg_{\varepsilon}x^{1-\varepsilon}

for any
\varepsilon>0
.

Remark 8
.

This improves the bound
\gg_{\varepsilon}x^{1-\vartheta-\varepsilon}
, which follows from the Rankin-Selberg theory and the pointwise bound towards the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture.

The proof of Theorem
2
is based on a reduction to the lower rank setting. This is achieved by the following result of Ramakrishnan
[
38
]
.

Lemma 9
.

There is a bijection between the set of non-dihedral Maass cusp forms for the group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and the set of self-dual Maass cusp forms for the group
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}
) given by the symmetric square lift.

Remark 10
.

This follows from Ramakrishnan’s main result as cusp forms we consider are assumed to have a trivial central character in which case the adjoint square lift is identical to the symmetric square lift.

Moreover, if a self-dual Hecke-Maass cusp form
\phi
on
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
is a symmetric square lift of a Hecke-Maass cusp form

on
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
, then their Fourier coefficients at primes are related by the relation
A_{\phi}(p,1)=\lambda_{g}(p^{2})=\lambda_{g}(p)^{2}-1
.

Let us then discuss the measures involved in this article following
[
4
]
. Let
\Phi

\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
and
\Phi^{+}

for the half-sum of positive roots. Let
\mathcal{P}^{+}
be the positive Weyl chamber of dominant weights. Finally, let

be a prime number and let
\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle T_{0}:=\left\{(e^{i\theta_{1}},e^{i\theta_{2}},e^{i\theta_{3}}):\,e^{i(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2}+\theta_{3})}=1\right\}\subset\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C}).

Then the (generalised) Sato-Tate measure on

is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mu_{\infty}:=\frac{1}{|W|}\prod_{\beta\in\Phi}\left(1-e^{\beta}(s)\right)\mathrm{d}s.

The
p
-adic Plancherel measure on
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
, which is supported on

(9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}:=\frac{W(p^{-1})}{\prod_{\beta\in\Phi}\left(1-p^{-1}e^{\beta}(s)\right)}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\infty}

with

\displaystyle W(q):=\sum_{w\in W}q^{\text{length}(w)},

where the length of

is the smallest

such that

\ell

Explicitly, integration with respect to these measures is given by

\displaystyle\int_{T_{0}/W}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\infty}=\frac{1}{24\pi^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}f(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\prod_{1\leq\ell<j\leq 3}\left|e^{i\theta_{\ell}}-e^{i\theta_{j}}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}\mathrm{d}\theta_{2}

and

(10)

\displaystyle\int_{T_{0}/W}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}=\frac{(1-p^{-2})(1-p^{-3})}{6(1-p^{-1})^{2}}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}f(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\prod_{1\leq\ell<j\leq 3}\left|\frac{e^{i\theta_{\ell}}-p^{-1}e^{i\theta_{j}}}{e^{i\theta_{\ell}}-e^{i\theta_{j}}}\right|^{-2}\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}\mathrm{d}\theta_{2}

for any continuous function
f:T_{0}/W\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, respectively. Here we have identified
T_{0}/W
with a subset of

Next we need some facts about Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials. For a symbol

\displaystyle P_{q}(\beta):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta=\sum n(\alpha)\alpha\\

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\lambda}^{\beta}(q):=\sum_{w\in W}(-1)^{\text{length}(w)}P_{q}(w.(\lambda+\rho)-(\beta+\rho))

for any root

.

Let

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\aleph(\ell_{2},\ell_{1})}^{0}(p^{-1})=\int_{T_{0}/W}S_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}(\sigma),

which can be found in the work of Kato

]
.

We recall the following result due to Buttcane and Zhou

For integers

It is known that

S_{1,1}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})=(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})(\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3})(\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{1})
for
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})\in T_{0}
and that
A_{\phi}(p,p)=(\alpha_{1,p}(\phi)+\alpha_{2,p}(\phi))(\alpha_{2,p}(\phi)+\alpha_{3,p}(\phi))(\alpha_{3,p}(\phi)+\alpha_{1,p}(\phi))
.

Proof.

Let
0<\delta<(\beta-\alpha)/2
be a small parameter optimised later. Let
w_{1}
be a non-negative weight function, which is supported in the interval
[(\alpha-\delta+1)/9,(\beta+\delta+1)/9]
, identically one in
[(\alpha+1)/9,(\beta+1)/9]
, and satisfies
\|w_{1}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\ll 1/\delta
. It is well-known that such smooth function can be approximated by the means of Bernstein polynomials
[
1
]
. Indeed, we have

(18)

\displaystyle w_{1}\left(x\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}w_{1}\left(\frac{j}{n}\right)\binom{n}{j}x^{j}(1-x)^{n-j}+O\left(\frac{\|w_{1}\|_{\infty}^{1/3}}{n^{1/3}\delta^{2/3}}\right)

for any
x\in[0,1]
, where
n
is a natural number specified later.

For simplicity let us set

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{\phi}(p,p):=\frac{A_{\phi}(p,p)+1}{9}.

Observe that under the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture we have
\widetilde{A}_{\phi}(p,p)\in[0,1]
.

Note also that

(19)

\displaystyle 1_{[\alpha,\beta]}(A_{\phi}(p,p))\leq w_{1}\left(\widetilde{A}_{\phi}(p,p)\right).

It follows from the Hecke relations that

\displaystyle\left(\widetilde{A}_{\phi}(p,p)\right)^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{9^{\ell}}|A_{\phi}(p,1)|^{2\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\leq 2\ell}\alpha_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell}A_{\phi}(p^{\ell_{1}},p^{\ell_{2}})

for some real numbers
\alpha_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell}
. By induction we have

(20)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\leq 2\ell}|\alpha_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell}|\leq 1,

see
[
3
, Estimate (14)]
.

Hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\int_{\Re\nu=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\mathrm{d}\nu}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\\
A_{\phi}(p,p)\in[\alpha,\beta]\end{subarray}}h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\int_{\Re\nu=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\mathrm{d}\nu}\sum_{\phi}w_{1}\left(\widetilde{A}_{\phi}(p,p)\right)h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\int_{\Re\nu=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\mathrm{d}\nu}\sum_{\phi}\sum_{j=0}^{n}w_{1}\left(\frac{j}{n}\right)\binom{n}{j}\widetilde{A}_{\phi}(p,p)^{j}(1-\widetilde{A}_{\phi}(p,p))^{n-j}h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+O(n^{-1/3}\delta^{-2/3})+O\left(\left(\frac{\log p}{\log T}\right)^{3/2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\int_{\Re\nu=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\mathrm{d}\nu}\sum_{j=0}^{n}w_{1}\left(\frac{j}{n}\right)\binom{n}{j}\sum_{\phi}\sum_{k=0}^{n-j}\binom{n-j}{k}(-1)^{k}\widetilde{A}_{\phi}(p,p)^{n-k}h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+O(n^{-1/3}\delta^{-2/3})+O\left(\left(\frac{\log p}{\log T}\right)^{3/2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\int_{\Re\nu=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\mathrm{d}\nu}\sum_{j=0}^{n}w_{1}\left(\frac{j}{n}\right)\binom{n}{j}\sum_{k=0}^{n-j}\binom{n-j}{k}(-1)^{k}

(21)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\sum_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\leq 2(n-k)}\alpha_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},n-k}\sum_{\phi}A_{\phi}\left(p^{\ell_{1}},p^{\ell_{2}}\right)h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})+O(n^{-1/3}\delta^{-2/3})+O\left(\left(\frac{\log p}{\log T}\right)^{3/2}\right).

In the second step we use (
18
) for forms satisfying the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture at prime
p
, but the treatment of the rest of the forms requires additional explanation. Let us define the polynomial
f:\mathbb{C}^{3}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}
by

\displaystyle f(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle\quad:=\sum_{j=0}^{n}w_{1}\left(\frac{j}{n}\right)\binom{n}{j}((x_{1}+x_{2})(x_{2}+x_{3})(x_{3}+x_{1}))^{j}(1-(x_{1}+x_{2})(x_{2}+x_{3})(x_{3}+x_{1}))^{n-j}.

Clearly
f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm},x_{2}^{\pm},x_{3}^{\pm}]^{W}
. Note that this is well-defined regardless whether or not
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in S^{1}
.

Recall that
X_{p}
is the set of Hecke-Maass cusp forms for the group
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z})
not satisfying the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture at
p
. We have to estimate the sums

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\int_{\Re\nu=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\mathrm{d}\nu}\sum_{\phi\in X_{p}}h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{\int_{\Re\nu=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\mathrm{d}\nu}\sum_{\phi\in X_{p}}h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})|f(\alpha_{\phi}(p))|.

The first sum is
\ll(\log p/\log T)^{3}
by (
2
). By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality the second sum is

\displaystyle\ll\left(\frac{1}{\int_{\Re\nu=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\mathrm{d}\nu}\sum_{\phi\in X_{p}}h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{1}{\int_{\Re\nu=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\mathrm{d}\nu}\sum_{\phi}h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})|f(\alpha_{\phi}(p))|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

The second factor on the right-hand side is

\displaystyle\ll\left(\int_{T_{0}/W}|f(\sigma)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}(\sigma)+O\left(T^{-1/2}\left(4p^{A}\right)^{2n}\right)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\ll 1+T^{-1/4}p^{An}

\displaystyle\ll 1

using Theorem
15
and the choice of
n
we make below, justifying the second step in (
6
).

Returning to (
6
), by Lemma
11
the innermost sum is

(22)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{\aleph(\ell_{2},\ell_{1})}^{0}(p^{-1})\left(\int_{\Re(\nu)=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\,\mathrm{d}\nu\right)+O_{\varepsilon}\left(T^{14/3+\varepsilon}p^{(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})/2+\varepsilon}\right).

Let us first concentrate on the contribution of the main term. The latter factor in that term is cancelled by the denominator in (
6
). By using Kato’s formula (
11
) the contribution from the main term

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}w_{1}\left(\frac{j}{n}\right)\binom{n}{j}\sum_{k=0}^{n-j}\binom{n-j}{k}(-1)^{k}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\sum_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\leq 2(n-k)}\alpha_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},n-k}\int_{T_{0}/W}S_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}(\sigma)+O(n^{-1/3}\delta^{-2/3})+O\left(\left(\frac{\log p}{\log T}\right)^{3/2}\right).

By the relation
A_{\phi}(p^{\ell_{1}},p^{\ell_{2}})=S_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}(\alpha_{1,p}(\phi),\alpha_{2,p}(\phi),\alpha_{3,p}(\phi))
it follows that

\displaystyle\left(\widetilde{S}_{1,1}(\sigma)\right)^{\ell}=\sum_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\leq 2\ell}\alpha_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell}S_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}(\sigma)

for any
\sigma\in T_{0}
, where we set

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{1,1}(\sigma):=\frac{S_{1,1}(\sigma)+1}{9}.

Note that
\widetilde{S}_{1,1}(\sigma)\in[0,1]
for
\sigma\in T_{0}
.

Therefore the main term above is

\displaystyle\int_{T_{0}/W}\sum_{j=0}^{n}w_{1}\left(\frac{j}{n}\right)\binom{n}{j}\widetilde{S}_{1,1}(\sigma)^{j}(1-\widetilde{S}_{1,1}(\sigma))^{n-j}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\int_{T_{0}/W}w_{1}\left(\widetilde{S}_{1,1}(\sigma)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}(\sigma)+O\left(n^{-1/3}\delta^{-2/3}\right)+O\left(\left(\frac{\log p}{\log T}\right)^{3/2}\right).

The contribution of the error term in (
22
) is

\displaystyle\ll\frac{1}{\int_{\Re\nu=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\mathrm{d}\nu}\sum_{j=0}^{n}w_{1}\left(\frac{j}{n}\right)\binom{n}{j}\sum_{k=0}^{n-j}(-1)^{n-j-k}\binom{n-j}{k}\sum_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\leq 2(n-k)}\alpha_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},n-k}T^{14/3+\varepsilon}p^{(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2})/2+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{T^{14/3+\varepsilon}}{\int_{\Re\nu=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\mathrm{d}\nu}\sum_{j=0}^{n}w_{1}\left(\frac{j}{n}\right)\binom{n}{j}\sum_{k=0}^{n-j}\binom{n-j}{k}p^{(n-k)}\sum_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\leq 2(n-k)}|\alpha_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},n-k}|

\displaystyle\ll\frac{T^{14/3+\varepsilon}}{\int_{\Re\nu=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\mathrm{d}\nu}\sum_{j=0}^{n}w_{1}\left(\frac{j}{n}\right)\binom{n}{j}p^{j}(1+p)^{n-j}\sum_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\leq 2(n-k)}|\alpha_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},n-k}|

\displaystyle\ll\frac{T^{14/3+\varepsilon}}{\int_{\Re\nu=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\mathrm{d}\nu}(2p)^{n}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{(2p)^{n}}{T^{1/3-\eta^{\prime}}}

for some very small
\eta^{\prime}>0
, where in the penultimate step we have used the estimate (
20
).

Thus we have showed that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\int_{\Re\nu=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\mathrm{d}\nu}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\\
A_{\phi}(p,p)\in[\alpha,\beta]\end{subarray}}h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{T_{0}/W}w_{1}\left(\widetilde{S}_{1,1}(\sigma)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}(\sigma)+O\left(n^{-1/3}\delta^{-2/3}+\frac{(2p)^{n}}{T^{1/3-\eta^{\prime}}}+\left(\frac{\log p}{\log T}\right)^{3/2}\right).

Similar computation shows that

\displaystyle\int_{T_{0}/W}w_{2}\left(\widetilde{S}_{1,1}(\sigma)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}(\sigma)+O\left(n^{-1/3}\delta^{-2/3}+\frac{(2p)^{n}}{T^{1/3-\eta^{\prime}}}+\left(\frac{\log p}{\log T}\right)^{3/2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\frac{1}{\int_{\Re\nu=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\mathrm{d}\nu}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\\
A_{\phi}(p,p)\in[\alpha,\beta]\end{subarray}}h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})

for a non-negative weight function
w_{2}
, which is supported in the interval
[(\alpha+1)/9,(\beta+1)/9]
, identically one in
[(\alpha+\delta+1)/9,(\beta-\delta+1)/9]
, and satisfies
\|w_{2}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\ll 1/\delta
.

Now it is enough to observe that by (
10
),

\displaystyle\int_{T_{0}/W}\left(1_{[\alpha,\beta]}\left(S_{1,1}(\sigma)\right)-w_{i}\left(\widetilde{S}_{1,1}(\sigma)\right)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}(\sigma)\ll\delta

uniformly on
p
for
i\in\{1,2\}
, and to optimise the choice of
\delta
and
n
. Choose
\delta\asymp n^{-1/5}
and
n=\large\lfloor\frac{W\left(5T^{5/3-5\eta^{\prime}}\log(2p)\right)}{8A\log(2p)}\large\rfloor
, where
W
is the Lambert
W
-function. These choices, using the estimate
W(x)\asymp\log x
, yield

\displaystyle\delta\asymp\frac{1}{n^{1/3}\delta^{2/3}}\asymp\frac{(2p)^{n}}{T^{1/3-\eta^{\prime}}}\asymp\left(\frac{\log p}{\log T}\right)^{1/5}

and so we have shown that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\int_{\Re\nu=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\mathrm{d}\nu}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\\
A_{\phi}(p,p)\in[\alpha,\beta]\end{subarray}}h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})=\int_{T_{0}/W}1_{[\alpha,\beta]}(S_{1,1}(\sigma))\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p}(\sigma)+O\left(\left(\frac{\log p}{\log T}\right)^{1/5}\right),

as desired.
∎

Remark 25
.

The exponent
1/5
in the error term is probably not optimal. Conjecturally it should be one (compare to the theorems of Lau-Wang and Murty-Sinha). We have not tried to optimise the exponent and used direct methods (i.e. Bernstein polynomials) in order to get a result, which is sufficient for the applications given below.

Remark 26
.

Actually just an upper bound for the sum

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\int_{\Re\nu=0}h_{T}(\nu)\text{spec}(\nu)\mathrm{d}\nu}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\\
A_{\phi}(p,p)\in[\alpha,\beta]\end{subarray}}h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})

would suffice for our purposes. We have written down an asymptotic formula as it might have applications in other situations.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
3
. It follows from
[
44
, Corollary 2]
that for almost all forms
\phi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{T}
there exists a natural number
m
so that
A_{\phi}(m,m)<0
and so by Lemma
17
it suffices to show that for all but
\varepsilon\#\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{T}
forms
\phi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{T}
we have that
A_{\phi}(m,m)\neq 0
for a positive proportion of integers
m
. By
[
41
, Théorème 14]
it is enough to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\\
A_{\phi}(p,p)=0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}<\infty

for all but
\varepsilon\#\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{T}

\phi
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{T}
. We will do this by employing our version of effective Sato-Tate theorem. It is just enough to note that by Theorem
23
we have, for any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{T}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
A_{\phi}(p,p)=0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi\\
|A_{\phi}(p,p)|\leq p^{-\delta}\end{subarray}}h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})

\displaystyle\ll\left(\sum_{\phi}h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})\right)\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{1}{p^{\delta}}+\left(\frac{\log p}{\log T}\right)^{1/5}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\#\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{T}

for any
X>0
such that, say,
\log X\ll(\log T)^{1/5}
provided that
T>0
is sufficiently large. Here the first step follows from the fact that
h_{T}(\nu_{\phi})\gg 1
for
\phi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{T}
. This implies that there is an absolute constant
C>0
such that, for all but at most
\varepsilon\#\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{T}
forms in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{T}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
A_{\phi}(p,p)=0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\leq\frac{C}{2\varepsilon}.

Letting
T\longrightarrow\infty
gives the desired result if
X
is chosen so that
X\longrightarrow\infty
along with
T
. ∎

7  Proofs of Theorems
4
and
6

It follows from the multiplicativity of Hecke eigenvalues that

(23)

\displaystyle\#\{m\leq X\,:\,A(m,1)\neq 0\}\asymp X\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
A(p,1)\neq 0\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)

assuming that
A(p,1)<0
for a positive proportion of primes
p

[
32
, Lemma 2.4.]
and so we shall show the latter statement for almost all forms. For this we will follow the approach in
[
25
]
.

In the self-dual case note that as we assume the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture for
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
Maass cusp forms, it follows that
A(p,1)\in[-1,3]
. Hence, the Hecke relations imply

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
A(p,1)<0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{A(p,1)^{2}-3A(p,1)}{4p}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\log X\leq p\leq X^{1/10000}}\frac{1+A(p,p)-3A(p,1)}{4p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+o(1)}{4}\log\log X+\sum_{\log X\leq p\leq X^{1/10000}}\frac{A(p,p)-3A(p,1)}{4p}.

Dividing the sum on the right-hand side into dyadic intervals it follows immediately from Lemma
19
that the sum is
o(\log\log X)
for almost all self-dual forms if
X\longrightarrow\infty
as
T\longrightarrow\infty
, concluding the proof in this case.

For the coefficients
A(p,p)
we note that
A(p,p)\in[-1,8]
. This follows from the identity
A(p,p)=|A(p,1)|^{2}-1
at primes
p
and the fact that we assume the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture for
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
Maass cusp forms. Using the Hecke relations we have

\displaystyle A(p,p)^{2}-8A(p,p)=1-6A(p,p)+A(p^{2},p^{2})+A(p^{3},1)+A(1,p^{3}).

This yields

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
A(p,p)<0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{p\leq X}\frac{A(p,p)^{2}-8A(p,p)}{9p}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\log X\leq p\leq X^{1/10000}}\frac{1-6A(p,p)+A(p^{2},p^{2})+A(p^{3},1)+A(1,p^{3})}{9p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+o(1)}{9}\log\log X+\sum_{\log X\leq p\leq X^{1/10000}}\frac{-6A(p,p)+A(p^{2},p^{2})+A(p^{3},1)+A(1,p^{3})}{9p}.

Again, by dividing the sum on the right-hand side into dyadic intervals and using Lemma
18
we see that it is
o(\log\log X)
for almost all forms as
X\longrightarrow\infty
as
T\longrightarrow\infty
. This completes the proof of Theorem
4
. ∎

Finally, we prove Theorem
6
. It follows from
[
32
, Lemma 2.4.]
that asymptotically half of the non-zero coefficients
A(m,1)
are positive (and half are negative) if there exists functions
K,L:\mathbb{R}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
so that
K(X)\longrightarrow 0
and
L(X)\longrightarrow\infty
as
X\longrightarrow\infty
, and for which

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\geq X\\
A(p,1)=0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\leq K(X)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
A(p,1)<0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\geq L(X)

for every
X\geq 2
.

Above we have shown that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\\
A(p,1)=0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}<\infty

for all but
\varepsilon\#\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{T}
forms in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{T}
as
T\longrightarrow\infty
and that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
A(p,1)<0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\geq\frac{1+o(1)}{4}\log\log X+o(\log\log X)

for almost all forms in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{T}
when
X\longrightarrow\infty
along with
T
assuming the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture for classical Maass cusp forms. Therefore the desired conclusion follows immediately. The second part for the coefficient
A(m,m)
follows analogously (assuming the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture for
\mathrm{GL}_{3}
Maass cusp forms) from the corresponding results established above. This concludes the proof. ∎

Isoperimetric estimates for solutions to the p-Laplacian with variable Robin boundary conditions
Vincenzo Amato, Francesco Chiacchio, Andrea Gentile

Abstract
In this paper we study the p p -Poisson equation with Robin boundary conditions, where the Robin parameter is a function. By means of some weighted isoperimetric inequalities, we provide various sharp bounds for the solutions to the problems under consideration. We also derive a Faber-Krahn-type inequality. MSC 2020: 35J92, 35P15, 35B45 Keywords: Weighted symmetrization, p p -Laplacian, variable Robin parameter, comparison results.

Introduction

Symmetrization methods have been revealed to be a very effective and
flexible tool in the study of partial differential equations (in the sequel
just PDEs). The related bibliography is very wide and one of the
cornerstone of this theory is the Talenti’s Theorem (see
[
Tal76
]
), which makes use
of the Schwarz symmetrization. The technique he introduced has been refined
through the years and it has been adapted to treat a large class of linear and
nonlinear elliptic and parabolic PDEs (see, e.g., the survey papers
[
Tal16
]
,
[
Tro00
]
and the monographs
[
Kaw85
]
,
[
Kes06
]
and
[
Bae19
]
). However, in most of the subsequent works, the authors, in order to
follow Talenti’s original idea, consider problems whose solutions have level
sets that do not intersect the boundary of the domain where the problem is
defined.

Recently it has been discovered a method to adapt symmetrization techniques
to a class of problems that does not exhibit this feature. More precisely, in

[
ANT
]
, the authors were able to obtain a Talenti-type comparison result for the
Laplacian with Robin boundary conditions. For further developments in
this direction see, e.g.,
[
AGM22
,
CGNT
]
, where the
p
-Laplace and Hermite operator are studied, respectively,
while in
[
ACNT21
]
and
[
ACNT
]
the authors investigate linear
problems where the Robin parameter is allowed to be a function.

Here we generalize the above results by considering the following problem

\begin{cases}-\text{div}\bigl({\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}\nabla u\bigr)=f(x){\left|x\right|}^{\ell}&\text{in }\Omega\\[5.16663pt]
\displaystyle{{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}\,\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\beta(x){\left|u\right|}^{p-2}u=0}&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where, here and throughout,
n\geq 2
,

\nu
denotes the outer unit normal to
\partial\Omega
and
\Omega
is a bounded and Lipschitz domain of
\R^{n}
such that
0\notin\partial\Omega
.

Furthermore we will assume that

p\geq n,

(
H_{1}
)

-n<\ell<0,

(
H_{2}
)

m=\inf_{\partial\Omega}\beta(x)>0\text{ \ and \ }M=\sup_{\partial\Omega}\beta(x)<+\infty,

(
H_{3}
)

and, finally,

0\leq f(x)\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega,{\left|x\right|}^{\ell}\,dx),

(
H_{4}
)

where
p^{\prime}=\displaystyle\frac{p}{p-1}
.

Hypotheses (

H
1

) and (

2

) ensure the validity of certain isoperimetric inequalities, where two different weights (which are suitable powers of the distance from the origin) appear in the perimeter and in the area element, respectively (see, e.g.,
[

,

,
BBMP99
,
Csa15
,
ABCMP17
]
and the references therein). Indeed we need to use, in place of the more common Schwarz symmetrization, some weighted rearrangement, based on these “double density” isoperimetric inequalities.

A weak solution to problem (
1.1

u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that

\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}\nabla u\nabla\varphi\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}\beta(x)\left|u\right|^{p-2}u\varphi d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)=\int_{\Omega}f\left|x\right|^{\ell}\varphi\,dx

(1.2)

for any

Now we need to construct the so-called symmetrized problem. That is a radial problem, of the same type of (
1.1
), whose solution will estimate the one to problem (
1.1
).

To this aim we introduce the set
\Omega^{\sharp}
which is the ball centered at the origin (in the sequel just centered ball) of
radius

, with

such that

\lvert\Omega^{\sharp}\rvert_{\ell}=\int_{\Omega^{\sharp}}\left|x\right|^{\ell}dx=\int_{\Omega}\left|x\right|^{\ell}dx=\left|\Omega\right|_{\ell},

and the function

defined by the following relation

\lvert\Set{x\in\Omega:{\left|f(x)\right|}>t}\rvert_{\ell}=\lvert\{x\in\Omega:f^{\sharp}(x)>t\}\rvert_{\ell}\text{ \ for a.e. }t\geq 0.

Our symmetrized problem is the following

\begin{cases}-\text{div}\bigl({\left|\nabla v\right|}^{p-2}\nabla v\bigr)=f^{\sharp}(x){\left|x\right|}^{\ell}&\text{in }\Omega^{\sharp}\\[5.16663pt]
\displaystyle{{\left|\nabla v\right|}^{p-2}\,\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}+\tilde{\beta}(r^{\sharp})^{\frac{\ell}{p^{\prime}}}{\left|v\right|}^{p-2}v=0}&\text{on }\partial\Omega^{\sharp}\end{cases}

(1.3)

where

\widetilde{\beta}=\inf_{\partial\Omega}\beta(x){\left|x\right|}^{-\frac{\ell}{p^{\prime}}}>0,

(1.4)

since

From the (

Lemma 2.5

(Gronwall)
.

Let
\xi(t):[\tau_{0},+\infty[\,\to\R
be a continuous and differentiable function satisfying, for some non negative constant

\tau\xi^{\prime}(\tau)\leq(p-1)\xi(\tau)+C\quad\forall\tau\geq\tau_{0}>0.

Then we have

\displaystyle{\xi(\tau)\leq\left(\xi(\tau_{0})+\frac{C}{p-1}\right)\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)^{p-1}-\frac{C}{p-1}\quad\forall\tau\geq\tau_{0}}
;

(ii)

\displaystyle{\xi^{\prime}(\tau)\leq\left(\frac{(p-1)\xi(\tau_{0})+C}{\tau_{0}}\right)\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)^{p-2}\quad\forall\tau\geq\tau_{0}}
.

Proof.

Dividing both sides of the differential inequality (

, we get

\frac{\xi^{\prime}(\tau)}{\tau^{p-1}}-(p-1)\frac{\xi(\tau)}{\tau^{p}}=\left(\frac{\xi(\tau)}{\tau^{p-1}}\right)^{\prime}\leq\frac{C}{\tau^{p}}.

Integrating the last inequality on

we obtain

\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\left(\frac{\xi(\tau)}{\tau^{p-1}}\right)^{\prime}\,d\tau\leq\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}\frac{C}{\tau^{p}}\,d\tau,

and therefore

\xi(\tau)\leq\left(\xi(\tau_{0})+\frac{C}{p-1}\right)\left(\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)^{p-1}-\frac{C}{p-1},

which gives
(i)
.

The second statement of the Lemma, claim

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla\psi\right|}^{n}\,dx+\frac{1}{n}\int_{\partial\Omega}\beta(x){\left|\psi\right|}^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)-\int_{\Omega}f\psi{\left|x\right|}^{\ell}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla\psi\right|}^{n}\,dx+C\int_{\partial\Omega}{\left|\psi\right|}^{n}-\int_{\Omega}f\psi{\left|x\right|}^{\ell}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq C{\left\|\psi\right\|}_{W^{1,n}(\Omega)}^{n}-\int_{\Omega}f\psi{\left|x\right|}^{\ell}\,dx,

where in the last inequality we have used the well-known trace embedding Theorem.
Using Hölder inequality and Proposition
2.3
, we have

G(\psi)\geq C{\left\|\psi\right\|}_{W^{1,n}(\Omega)}^{n}-{\left\|\psi\right\|}_{W^{1,n}(\Omega)}{\left\|f\right\|}_{L^{n^{\prime}}(\Omega,{\left|x\right|}^{\ell}\,dx)}

By Young parametric inequality we finally get

\displaystyle G(\psi)\geq\left(C-\frac{\varepsilon^{n}}{n}\right){\left\|\psi\right\|}_{W^{1,n}(\Omega)}^{n}-\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{n^{\prime}}n^{\prime}}{\left\|f\right\|}_{L^{n^{\prime}}(\Omega,{\left|x\right|}^{\ell}\,dx)}

(5.2)

For
\varepsilon
small enough we have

G(\psi)\geq-\frac{1}{\varepsilon n^{\prime}}\,{\left\|f\right\|}_{L^{n^{\prime}}(\Omega,{\left|x\right|}^{\ell}\,dx)}>-\infty.

Now let
\Set{u_{k}}_{k}
a minimizing sequence for
G
, we can assume, without loss of generality, that
G(u_{k})\leq m_{G}+1
where

m_{G}=\inf_{u\in W^{1,n}(\Omega)}G(u).

So, by (
5.2
) for a fixed
\varepsilon
small, we have

m_{G}+1+\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{n^{\prime}}n^{\prime}}{\left\|f\right\|}_{L^{n^{\prime}}(\Omega,{\left|x\right|}^{\ell}\,dx)}\geq\left(C-\frac{\varepsilon^{n}}{n}\right){\left\|u_{k}\right\|}_{W^{1,n}(\Omega)}^{n}.

So the sequence
\Set{u_{k}}_{k}
is bounded in
W^{1,n}(\Omega)
hence, up to a subsequence, it converges weakly in
W^{1,n}(\Omega)
and strongly in
L^{n}(\Omega,{\left|x\right|}^{\ell}\,dx)
and in
L^{n}(\partial\Omega)
to a function
u
.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{k}\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla u_{k}\right|}^{n}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{n}\,dx

\displaystyle\liminf_{k}\int_{\Omega}\beta(x)u_{k}^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\Omega}\beta(x)u^{n}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)

\displaystyle\liminf_{k}\int_{\Omega}u_{k}f{\left|x\right|}^{\ell}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}uf{\left|x\right|}^{\ell}\,dx

and finally

m_{G}=\liminf_{k}G(u_{k})\geq G(u)=m_{G}.\qed

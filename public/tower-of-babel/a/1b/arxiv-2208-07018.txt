On the intersection form of fillings
Zhengyi Zhou

Abstract
. We prove, by an ad hoc method, that exact fillings with vanishing rational first Chern class of flexibly fillable contact manifolds have unique integral intersection forms. We appeal to the special Reeb dynamics (stronger than ADC in [ 26 ] ) on the contact boundary, while a more systematic approach working for general ADC manifolds is developed independently by Eliashberg, Ganatra and Lazarev [ 19 ] . We also discuss cases where the vanishing rational first Chern class assumption can be removed. We derive the uniqueness of diffeomorphism types of exact fillings of certain flexibly fillable contact manifolds and obstructions to contact embeddings, which are not necessarily exact. MSC 2020: 53D10, 53D42.



1.
Introduction

In his seminal work of introducing pseudo-holomorphic curves into symplectic geometry, Gromov
[
23
]
proved the uniqueness of exact fillings of
(S^{3},\xi_{\rm std})
. In higher dimensions, the celebrated Eliashberg-Floer-McDuff theorem
[
29
]
asserts the uniqueness of diffeomorphism type for exact fillings of
(S^{2n-1},\xi_{\rm std})
whenever
n\geq 3
. Starting from those monumental results in the late 1980s - early 1990s, understanding the uniqueness of exact fillings of some contact manifolds has been a fundamental and influential question. In dimension
3
, the intersection theory of holomorphic curves can be used to construct foliations of symplectic fillings. A landmark result is Wendl’s theorem on planar contact
3
-folds
[
42
]
, which translates the classification of symplectic fillings into factorizations in mapping class groups. In higher dimensions, only “homological” foliations by holomorphic curves can be obtained, just like the Eliashberg-Floer-McDuff theorem compared to Gromov’s theorem. Based on “homological” foliations, various generalizations of the Eliashberg-Floer-McDuff theorem were obtained, e.g. Oancea-Viterbo
[
36
]
and Barth-Geiges-Zehmisch’s
[
3
]
works on topological uniqueness of exact fillings of subcritically fillable contact manifolds, Bowden-Gironella-Moreno
[
9
]
and Geiges-Kwon-Zehmisch’s
[
20
]
works on topological uniqueness of exact fillings of the cosphere bundle
S^{*}T^{n}
. On the other hand, we studied the filling question from the perspective of Floer theories and obtained various uniqueness results including uniqueness on cohomology groups
[
43
,
44
]
or rings
[
47
]
, diffeomorphism types
[
46
,
48
]
as well as certain properties of the symplectic cohomology
[
44
,
45
]
. Unlike the cohomology ring, which is a homotopy invariant, the intersection form
H_{p}(W^{2n};{\mathbb{Z}})\otimes H_{2n-p}(W^{2n};{\mathbb{Z}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is more sensitive to the manifold structure. In this note, we show the uniqueness of the integral intersection form for exact fillings of some flexibly fillable contact manifolds, which shall yield the uniqueness of diffeomorphism types in some cases.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
(Y^{2n-1},\xi)
has a flexible filling
W^{flex}
with vanishing rational first Chern class
c^{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{1}(\xi)
, then the integral intersection form of any exact filling
W
with
c^{\mathbb{Q}}_{1}(W)=0
is isomorphic to the integral intersection form of the flexible filling
W^{flex}
, that is there is an isomorphism between
H_{n}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})
and
H_{n}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Z}})
, such that the intersection form on them are identified.

Remark 1.2
.

By
Proposition

1.3
below, we have
H^{*}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Z}})=H^{*}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})
, which then implies that
H_{*}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Z}})=H_{*}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})
by the universal coefficient theorem. Therefore the only non-trivial information about the intersection form is contained in the middle dimension
H_{n}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})\otimes H_{n}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})\to{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Theorem

1.4
below lists some cases where the

The idea of the proof is as follows: take two cycles

A\cdot B=\#(A\pitchfork B)
, where the latter is a finite set of oriented points. By
[
44
]
, we have
SH^{*}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})=0
for any topologically simple (i.e.
c^{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{1}(W)=0
and
H_{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Q}})\to H_{1}(W;{\mathbb{Q}})
is injective, the comparison with topologically simple fillings used in
[
43
,
44
]
is in §
3
.) exact filling. Geometrically, it means that there is one curve counted algebraically passing through a fixed point. If we choose the point constraint to be
A\pitchfork B
, which might consist of several points, this allows us to view

as the counting of one boundary component of a
1
-dimensional moduli space with one positive puncture asymptotic to a non-constant Hamiltonian orbit and two negative punctures asymptotic to points on
A
and
B
as removable singularities. Then we can establish the independence result by looking at the other boundaries and applying neck-stretching. It is important that
Y
is flexibly fillable rather than just being asymptotically dynamically convex (ADC)
[
26

, as we need to use the special Reeb dynamics on the boundary of a flexibly fillable contact manifold constructed by Lazarev

Our approach is ad hoc in the sense that we present the intersection number as a counting of holomorphic curves contained in the cylindrical end of the filling. It is the combination of such curves that carries the information, while some moduli spaces that appear in the process do not bear meanings as structural maps in symplectic cohomology or symplectic field theory. 1.1.
Dropping the topologically simple assumption

The topologically simple condition in
[
43
,
44
]
was used to make sure the grading of symplectic cohomology of a filling is consistent with the grading induced from the contact boundary so that we can do effective dimension computation after neck-stretching. We first note that the
H_{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Q}})\to H_{1}(W;{\mathbb{Q}})
injective condition can be dropped for flexibly fillable contact manifolds (but not for general ADC manifolds).

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
(Y^{2n-1},\xi)
be a contact manifold filled by a flexible Weinstein domain
W^{flex}
with
c^{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{1}(\xi)=0
. For any exact filling
W
of
Y
with
c_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}}(W)=0
, we have that
H^{*}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{*}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
is isomorphic to
H^{*}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{*}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Following a different perspective which was used in
[
48
]
, by exploiting the degeneracy of a spectral sequence from the “boundary” grading, we can drop the
c_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}}(W)=0
condition in some cases.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(Y^{2n-1},\xi)
be a contact manifold filled by a flexible Weinstein domain
W^{flex}
with
c^{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{1}(\xi)=0
.

(1)

If
H^{n}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{n}(Y;{\mathbb{Q}})
is injective for the flexible filling
W^{flex}
, then for any exact filling
W
of
Y
, we have
H^{*}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{*}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
is isomorphic to
H^{*}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{*}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
. In particular, we have
c_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}}(W)=0
, as
H^{2}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{2}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
is injective and
c_{1}^{{\mathbb{Q}}}(\xi)=0\in H^{2}(Y;{\mathbb{Q}})
.

(2)

If
n
is even, then for any exact filling
W
of
Y
, we have
SH^{*}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})=0
and
\dim\oplus_{*=0}^{2n}H^{*}(W;{\mathbb{Q}})\leq\dim\oplus_{*=0}^{2n}H^{*}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Q}})
. Moreover,

(a)

if
H^{1}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Q}})=0
, then
\dim\oplus_{*=0}^{2n}H^{*}(W;{\mathbb{Q}})=\dim\oplus_{*=0}^{2n}H^{*}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Q}})
;

(b)

if moreover
\dim\ker(H^{n}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{n}(Y;{\mathbb{Q}}))=1
, then
H^{*}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{*}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
is isomorphic to
H^{*}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{*}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
. In particular, we have
c_{1}^{\mathbb{Q}}(W)=0
as before.

For example, the flexible version
\mathrm{Flex}(T^{*}S^{n})
of
T^{*}S^{n}
for odd
n
satisfies the condition (
1
), and it satisfies condition (
2b
) when
n
is even. Therefore any exact filling of
\partial\mathrm{Flex}(T^{*}S^{n})
is topologically simple using
Proposition

1.3
and
Theorem

1.4
. However, in the
H^{n}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{n}(Y;{\mathbb{Q}})
injective case, the intersection form of
W^{flex}
is necessarily trivial. To see this, since the intersection form
H_{n}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Z}})\otimes H_{n}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{0}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Z}})={\mathbb{Z}}
is isomorphic to the cup product
H^{n}(W^{flex},Y;{\mathbb{Z}})\otimes H^{n}(W^{flex},Y;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{2n}(W^{flex},Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
using the Lefschetz duality. If
H^{n}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{n}(Y;{\mathbb{Q}})
injective, then
H^{n}(W^{flex},Y;{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{n}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Q}})
is trivial, hence
H^{n}(W^{flex},Y;{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes H^{n}(W^{flex},Y;{\mathbb{Q}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cup}}{{\to}}H^{n}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes H^{n}(W^{flex},Y;{\mathbb{Q}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cup}}{{\to}}H^{2n}(W^{flex},Y;{\mathbb{Q}})={\mathbb{Q}}
is trivial, which implies the intersection form is trivial. By rank of the intersection form, we mean the rank of the bilinear form on
H_{n}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Q}})
. Since
H^{n}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Q}})\otimes H^{n}(W^{flex},Y;{\mathbb{Q}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cup}}{{\to}}H^{2n}(W^{flex},Y;{\mathbb{Q}})={\mathbb{Q}}
is non-degenerate, the rank is the dimension of the image of
H^{n}(W^{flex},Y;{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{n}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Q}})
.

1.2.
Uniqueness of diffeomorphism types

Using

1.4
, we can get the following uniqueness result of diffeomorphism types for symplectic fillings following the same topological argument in
[

,

]
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let

\pi_{1}(Q)
is abelian and
\chi(Q)=0

\mathrm{Flex}(T^{*}Q)
(the flexible version of

For highly connected manifolds, the diffeomorphism type is restricted to a large extent by its intersection form, c.f.

.

If

2n

only has flexible

is diffeomorphic to
W^{flex}
. If moreover the intersection form
W^{flex}
is of rank

, then any exact filling
W
of
\partial W^{flex}
is diffeomorphic to
W^{flex}
.

It is worth noting that the uniqueness of diffeomorphism type above follows from completely different topological argument compared to results in
[

,
9
,
20

46
]

1.3.
Obstructions to contact embeddings

Let
V
be a symplectic manifold, we call a closed hypersurface
Y\subset V
a contact hypersurface if there exists a local Liouville vector field near
Y
that is transverse to
Y
. When
V
is exact, and the Liouville vector field can be defined globally, we say
Y
is an exact contact hypersurface. When
Y
is separating, which is automatic if
H_{2n-1}(V;{\mathbb{Z}})=0
, and is an exact contact hypersurface, the Liouville vector field must point out along
Y
w.r.t. the compact domain (inside the completion of
V
) bounded by
Y
. Note that if we drop the exactness assumption, the local Liouville vector field can either point out or point in along
Y
w.r.t. the compact domain it bounds. Understanding whether a contact manifold can be embedded in a symplectic manifold, e.g.
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, is a fundamental question. In dimension
4
, it is closely related to the Gompf conjecture
[
22
]
, see
[
28
]
for recent advances on this conjecture. Obstructions to exact contact hypersurfaces were studied by Cieliebak and Frauenfelder
[
15
]
using the Rabinowitz-Floer homology. For example, they showed that there is no exact embedding of
S^{*}S^{n}
into
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Using
Theorem

1.1
, we have the following obstructions to contact embeddings (not necessarily exact), where the obstructions from the Rabinowitz-Floer homology typically vanish.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
V
be a
2n
-dimensional exact domain with
c^{\mathbb{Q}}_{1}(V)=0
and
W
a flexible Weinstein domain. Suppose the rank of the intersection form on
H_{n}(V;{\mathbb{Q}})
is smaller than that of
W
. Then
\partial W
cannot be embedded into
V
as a separating contact hypersurface with the local Liouville vector field points out w.r.t. the bounded domain. If we assume moreover that
V
is
P\times{\mathbb{D}}
for an exact domain
P
,
\partial W
can not be embedded into
V
as a contact hypersurface.

For example, we can consider the flexible version
W^{flex}
of a Brieskorn variety
\{x_{0}^{a_{0}}+\ldots+x_{n}^{a_{n}}=1\}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
for
a_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{+}
. When
a_{i}\geq 2
for all
i
, the intersection form of the Brieskorn variety/
W^{flex}
has a positive rank if one of
a_{i}\geq 3
or
n
is even. Then by
Theorem

1.7
, there is no contact embedding of the contact boundary
\partial W^{flex}
into
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Note that for suitable choices of
a_{i}
, the contact boundary
\partial W^{flex}
has the same almost contact structure as
(S^{2n-1},\xi_{\rm std})

[
25
]
, in particular, such embedding is not obstructed topologically.

2.
Preliminaries on flexibly fillable manifolds and symplectic cohomology

2.1.
Preliminaries on flexibly fillable contact manifolds

We assume throughout this note that
(Y,\xi)
is a contact manifold of dimension
2n-1\geq 5
, such that
\xi
is co-oriented and its first rational Chern class
c^{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{1}(\xi)

Definition 2.1
.

We first recall definitions of symplectic fillings of various flavors:

•

(W,\lambda)
is a Liouville filling of
(Y,\xi)
iff
{\rm d}\lambda
is a symplectic form on

.

•

(W,\lambda,\phi)
is a Weinstein filling of
(Y,\xi)
iff
(W,\lambda)
is a Liouville filling,
\phi:W\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Morse function with maximal level set
\partial W
, and
X_{\lambda}
is a gradient-like vector field for
\phi
.

•

(W,\lambda,\phi)
is a flexible Weinstein filling of
(Y,\xi)
iff
(W,\lambda,\phi)
is a Weinstein filling, and there exist regular values

of

[c_{k},\max\phi]
and each

whose attaching sphere
\Lambda_{p}
is either subcritical or a loose Legendrian in
(\phi^{-1}(c_{i}),\lambda|_{\phi^{-1}(c_{i})})
, see
[
14
, Definition 11.29]
and
[
26
, Definition 2.4]
.

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a contact manifold such that the rational first Chern class
c_{1}^{{\mathbb{Q}}}(\xi)\in H^{2}(Y;{\mathbb{Q}})
is zero. Therefore there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}_{+}
, such that
Nc_{1}(\xi)=0\in H^{2}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
. Since
c_{1}(\det_{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus^{N}\xi)=Nc_{1}(\xi)=0
,
\det_{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus^{N}\xi
can be trivialized, where we view
\xi
as a complex bundle using a complex structure compatible with
{\rm d}\alpha|_{\xi}
. Then we can assign a rational Conley-Zehnder index
\mu_{\rm CZ}
to each non-degenerate Reeb orbit
\gamma
, i.e.

\oplus^{N}\Phi(t)

\det_{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus^{N}\xi

\Phi(t)
is the linearization of the Reeb flow along

\xi
, see
[
21

31
]
for details. For those orbits with torsion homology classes, the Conley-Zehnder index is independent of

[
21

. For contractible Reeb orbits, it is the usual integer-valued Conley-Zehnder index using any induced trivialization from a bounding disk.

In
[
26
]
, Lazarev introduced the concept of asymptotically dynamically convex (ADC) manifolds, and in the process of proving flexible fillable contact manifolds are ADC, he proved the following property:

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(Y^{2n-1},\xi)
be a flexibly fillable contact manifold with
c^{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{1}(\xi)=0
and a fixed contact form
\alpha_{0}
. Then for any
D\gg 0
, there exists a contact form
\alpha_{D}<\alpha_{0}
such that Reeb orbits of

(for any fixed trivialization of

This follows from the proof of

, if we consider Reeb orbits of period smaller than

.

2.2.
Symplectic cohomology

We will only recall the basics of symplectic cohomology to set up notations and relevant structures for our main results. We refer readers to
[
16
,
37
,
38
]
for a more complete treatment of the subject.

By a strict contact manifold, we mean a contact manifold
Y
equipped with a contact form
\alpha
and an exact filling
(W,\lambda)
is a strict exact filling
(Y,\alpha)
if
\alpha=\lambda|_{Y}
. Given an exact filling
(W,\lambda)
, we consider the completion
(\widehat{W},\widehat{\lambda})
, where
\widehat{W}=W\cup(1,\infty)_{r}\times\partial W
,
\widehat{\lambda}
is
\lambda
on
W
and
r(\lambda|_{\partial W})
on
(1,\infty)_{r}\times\partial W
. Roughly speaking, symplectic cohomology is the “Morse cohomology” of the free loop space w.r.t. the symplectic action functional

(2.1)

{\mathcal{A}}_{H}(x):=-\int x^{*}\widehat{\lambda}+\int_{S^{1}}(x^{*}{H}){\rm d}t

where
H:S^{1}\times\widehat{W}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Hamiltonian on the completion such that “the slope
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}H
” goes to infinity as
r
goes to infinity. Let
R
be any commutative ring, the cochain complex
CF^{*}(H)
is a free
R
-module generated by
1
-periodic orbits of
X_{H}
when
H
is non-degenerate.
Let

be two generators in
CF^{*}(H)
, we use
{\mathcal{M}}^{x}_{y}
to denote the compactified moduli space of Floer cylinders from
x
to

:

{\mathcal{M}}^{x}_{y}=\overline{\left\{u:\mathbb{R}_{s}\times S^{1}_{t}\to\widehat{W}\left|\partial_{s}u+J(\partial_{t}u-X_{H})=0,\displaystyle\lim_{s\to\infty}u=x,\lim_{s\to-\infty}u=y\right.\right\}/\mathbb{R}}.

We say an almost complex structure
J
on
\widehat{W}
is admissible, if
J
is compatible with
{\rm d}\widehat{\lambda}
and
J
is cylindrically convex for

so that the integrated maximum principle
[

16

\xi

and

(\partial W,\lambda|_{\partial W})
. In both non-degenerate and Morse-Bott cases, for a generic
S^{1}
-dependent admissible complex structure

,
{\mathcal{M}}^{x}_{y}
is cut out transversely as a manifold with boundary for those with expected dimension

\delta(x)=\sum_{y,\virdim{\mathcal{M}}^{x}_{y}=0}(\#{\mathcal{M}}^{x}_{y})y

The symplectic cochain complex is graded by

\mu_{\rm CZ}
is only well-defined in

, symplectic cohomology always has a

, which is independent of the trivialization for any periodic orbit with finite order homology class. Moreover, if

\mu_{\rm CZ}
is well-defined in
{\mathbb{Q}}
(as in §

\det_{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus^{N}TW
and symplectic cohomology can be graded by
{\mathbb{Q}}
. We say an exact filling
W
of
Y
is

if
H_{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Q}})\to H_{1}(W;{\mathbb{Q}})
is injective and
c_{1}^{{\mathbb{Q}}}(W)=0
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume
W
is topologically simple, then there exists
N,M\in{\mathbb{N}}_{+}
, such that
\det_{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus^{N}\xi
can be trivialized, and the induced trivialization on
\det_{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus^{NM}\xi
from any trivialization of
\det_{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus^{N}\xi
can be extended to a trivialization of
\det_{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus^{NM}TW
.

Proof.

Since
c_{1}^{{\mathbb{Q}}}(W)=0
, we may assume
Nc_{1}(W)=0\in H^{2}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})
for
N\in{\mathbb{N}}_{+}
. Therefore we have a trivialization of
\det_{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus^{N}TW
, which induces an trivialization of
\det_{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus^{N}\xi
. Now, different trivializations of
\det_{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus^{N}\xi
are parameterized the gauge group
[Y:S^{1}]=H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
. Since
H_{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Q}})\to H_{1}(W;{\mathbb{Q}})
is injective, we have
H^{1}(W;{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Q}})
is surjective and hence
H^{1}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
is surjective onto
M\cdot H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})\subset H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
for some

. As the difference between two induced trivializations on
\det_{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus^{NM}\xi
from two trivializations of
\det_{{\mathbb{C}}}\oplus^{N}\xi
is given by en element in
M\cdot H^{1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})
, the claim follows.
∎

Exploiting the independence of augmentations using grading constraints was initiated by Bourgeois and Oancea

Definition 3.1

Remark 3.4

(Clarification on asymptotically dynamical convexity and dynamical convexity)
.

Dynamical convexity was introduced by Hofer, Wysocki, and Zehnder
[
24
]
as a dynamical consequence of convexity, namely a contact form
\alpha
on
(S^{2n-1},\xi_{std})
is dynamical convex if all Reeb orbits have Conley-Zehnder indices at least
n+1
. It was shown recently by Chaidez and Edtmair
[
12
,
13
]
that dynamical convexity does not imply convexity. In terms of cylindrical contact homology, dynamical convexity means that there are no Reeb orbits of small Conley-Zehnder indices that are not visible in the cylindrical contact homology. This is the perspective used by Abreu and Macarini in
[
2
]
for contact manifolds other than spheres. On the other hand, Bourgeois, Cieliebak, Oancea, and Uebele
[
6
,
16
,
40
]
introduced index positivity of a contact form, namely if the SFT degree
\mu_{\rm CZ}+n-3
of any contractible Reeb orbit is positive. For
(S^{2n-1},\xi_{std})
, this means that the Conley-Zehnder indices are at least
2-n\neq n+1
. From this perspective, the ADC condition is “asymptotical” index positivity by considering a sequence of contact forms instead of just one.

To get results with even weaker topological assumptions, we need properties on Conley-Zehnder indices for general Reeb orbits. In particular, we have the following:

.

(Y,\xi)
be a contact manifold such that

. Let

. We say

is
\pi_{1}
-sensitive ADC
if there exist contact forms
\alpha_{1}>\alpha_{2}>\ldots
, positive real numbers
D_{1}<D_{2}<\ldots
converging to infinity, such that
all

Proof.

The proof is similar to Proposition
4.6
by fully stretching the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}^{\check{\gamma}_{j}}_{B}
. By a similar dimension argument, the moduli space must break into
{\mathcal{N}}^{\check{\gamma}_{j}}_{\gamma_{i}}\times{\mathcal{N}}^{\gamma_{i}}_{B}
. Therefore it suffices to prove
i\leq j
. When
H
is autonomous and only depends on
r
,
X_{H}
is parallel to the Reeb vector. Therefore for any solution
u\in{\mathcal{N}}^{\check{\gamma}_{j}}_{\gamma_{i}}

\alpha
-energy

(as in (

)), which implies that the period of
\gamma_{j}
must be greater than
\gamma_{i}

\gamma_{i}=\gamma_{j}
. Then for

sufficient close to the autonomous one, we have
{\mathcal{N}}_{\check{\gamma}_{j},\gamma_{i}}\neq\emptyset
implies that
i\leq j
. Moreover, for the autonomous Hamiltonian, any curve
u
in

have trivial
\alpha

is contained in

. In this case, the equation becomes

\partial_{s}u+J(\partial_{t}u-h^{\prime}(r)\partial_{\theta})=0,\quad u:\mathbb{R}_{s}\times S^{1}_{t}\to\mathbb{R}_{\rho}\times([0,T]_{\theta}/{0\simeq T}),

where

is period of
\gamma_{j}
so that
\theta
is the coordinate on
\Ima(\gamma_{j})
,
\rho=\log(r)
on
\widehat{Y}=(0,\infty)_{r}\times Y
,
J(\partial_{\rho})=\partial_{\theta}
. It has a solution

u(s,t)=(f(s),Tt+\theta_{0})

where

such that

for

. Different solutions are related by translations on

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In view of
Proposition

1.3
,
c^{\mathbb{Q}}_{1}(W)=0
implies that
W
is topologically simple. If we are given
\langle\,\delta([\check{\gamma}_{j}]),B\,\rangle
and
\#{\mathcal{N}}^{\check{\gamma}_{i}}_{\gamma_{j}}
, we can solve uniquely
\#{\mathcal{N}}^{\gamma_{i}}_{B}
by Proposition
4.7
, since the coefficient matrix is triangular with ones on the diagonal. Then by
Propositions

4.4
,
4.5
and
4.6
, we can represent the intersection
A\cdot B
by
{\mathcal{N}}^{\overline{\alpha}}_{\check{\gamma}_{i},\gamma_{j}}
,
{\mathcal{N}}^{\overline{\alpha}}_{\gamma_{i},\check{\gamma}_{j}}
,
{\mathcal{N}}^{\check{\gamma}_{i}}_{\gamma_{j}}
,
\langle\,\delta([\check{\gamma}_{j}]),A\,\rangle
and
\langle\,\delta([\check{\gamma}_{j}]),B\,\rangle
. The first three moduli spaces are independent of the filling, as they are contained in the symplectization. Note that
H^{n}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})
is independent of filling, and a basis can be represented by combinations of
[\check{\gamma}_{i}]
, and the same combinations work for
W^{flex}
by
Proposition

4.1
. By the universal coefficient theorem,
H_{n}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})
is isomorphic to the free part of
H^{n}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})
since
H^{*}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})
is supported in degree
\leq n
. Fixing a basis of a fixed free part of
H^{n}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})
induces a dual basis on
H_{n}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})
. We use this dual basis to identify the homology of two fillings, that is we have an isomorphism
H_{n}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\to}}H_{n}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Z}})
, such that

(4.7)

\langle\,[\check{\gamma}_{i}],A\,\rangle=\langle\,[\check{\gamma}_{i}],\psi(A)\,\rangle,A\in H_{n}(W;{\mathbb{Z}}),

where we view
[\check{\gamma}_{i}]
both in
H^{n}(W;{\mathbb{Z}})
and
H^{n}(W^{flex};{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Since
{\mathcal{N}}^{\check{\gamma}_{i}}_{\gamma_{j}}
is contained in the symplectization and is the same for
W
and
W^{flex}
, by
Proposition

4.7
we have

(4.8)

\#{\mathcal{N}}^{\gamma_{i}}_{B}=\#{\mathcal{N}}^{\gamma_{i}}_{\psi(B)}.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle A\cdot B

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Prop }\ref{prop:int}}}{{=\joinrel=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\#\left({\mathcal{M}}^{\overline{\alpha}}_{\check{\gamma}_{i},B}\times{\mathcal{M}}^{\check{\gamma}_{i}}_{A}\right)+\#\left({\mathcal{M}}^{\overline{\alpha}}_{A,\check{\gamma}_{i}}\times{\mathcal{M}}^{\check{\gamma}_{i}}_{B}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Prop }\ref{prop:SFT1}}}{{=\joinrel=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\#\left({\mathcal{N}}^{\overline{\alpha}}_{\check{\gamma}_{i},\gamma_{j}}\times{\mathcal{N}}^{\gamma_{j}}_{B}\times{\mathcal{M}}^{\check{\gamma}_{i}}_{A}\right)+\#\left({\mathcal{N}}^{\overline{\alpha}}_{\gamma_{j},\check{\gamma}_{i}}\times\times{\mathcal{N}}^{\gamma_{j}}_{A}\times{\mathcal{M}}^{\check{\gamma}_{i}}_{B}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Prop }\ref{prop:pair}}}{{=\joinrel=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\#\left({\mathcal{N}}^{\overline{\alpha}}_{\check{\gamma}_{i},\gamma_{j}}\times{\mathcal{N}}^{\gamma_{j}}_{B}\right)\times\langle\,[\check{\gamma}_{i}],A\,\rangle+\#\left({\mathcal{N}}^{\overline{\alpha}}_{\gamma_{j},\check{\gamma}_{i}}\times\times{\mathcal{N}}^{\gamma_{j}}_{A}\right)\times\langle\,[\check{\gamma}_{i}],B\,\rangle\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eqn:iso},\eqref{eqn:iso'}}}{{=\joinrel=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\#\left({\mathcal{N}}^{\overline{\alpha}}_{\check{\gamma}_{i},\gamma_{j}}\times{\mathcal{N}}^{\gamma_{j}}_{\psi(B)}\right)\times\langle\,[\check{\gamma}_{i}],\psi(A)\,\rangle+\#\left({\mathcal{N}}^{\overline{\alpha}}_{\gamma_{j},\check{\gamma}_{i}}\times\times{\mathcal{N}}^{\gamma_{j}}_{\psi(A)}\right)\times\langle\,[\check{\gamma}_{i}],\psi(B)\,\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\joinrel=

\displaystyle\psi(A)\cdot\psi(B).

That is
\psi
identifies the intersection form.

Proof of

1.7
.

In the first case, if
\partial W
embeds into
V
as a separating contact hypersurface with the local Liouville vector field pointing out w.r.t. the bounded domain

. Then

becomes a Liouville filling of

with a closed
1
-form on

1.3
and

imply that the rank of the intersection form on

In the second case, if

as a contact surface, as

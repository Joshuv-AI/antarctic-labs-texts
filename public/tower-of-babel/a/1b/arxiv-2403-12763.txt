A smoothing effect for the fractional Schrödinger equations on the circle and observability
Paul Alphonse, Nikolay Tzvetkov

Abstract
. We show that, after a renormalisation, one can define the square of the modulus of the solution of the fractional Schrödinger equations on the circle with data in Sobolev spaces of arbitrary negative index. As an application, we obtain observability estimates with rough controls.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35Q93, 35J10, 35B65, 47D06

1.
Introduction and main results

We consider the fractional Schrödinger equation on the circle

(
S_{\alpha}
)

i\partial_{t}u+|D_{x}|^{\alpha}u=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T},

where
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
and
\alpha>1
is a positive real number.
In this equation, the operator
|D_{x}|^{\alpha}
is the Fourier multiplier defined on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})=(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}))^{\prime}
by

\widehat{|D_{x}|^{\alpha}f}(n)=|n|^{\alpha}\widehat{f}(n),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},\,f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}),

where
\widehat{\cdot}
denotes the Fourier transform on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
, given by

\widehat{f}(n)=(2\pi)^{-1}\langle f,e^{-inx}\rangle_{\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}),C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

When
\alpha
is an even integer
|D_{x}|^{\alpha}
is simply the differential operator

(-1)^{\alpha/2}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}.

For every
u_{0}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
, the unique solution in
C(\mathbb{R},\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}))
of the equation (

S
α

)
with initial datum

u|_{t=0}=u_{0},

naturally denoted
e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0}
, is given by the exponential sum

(1.1)

(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0})(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{u_{0}}(n)\,e^{it|n|^{\alpha}}e^{inx}.

Our goal in this work is to describe a remarkable smoothing property enjoyed by (
1.1
) and its application to observability estimates.

1.1.
Smoothing effect

For every
u_{0}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
and every
N\geq 0
, we denote by
\Pi_{N}u_{0}
the truncation of
u_{0}
at the energy level
N
, that is,

(1.2)

(\Pi_{N}u_{0})(x)=\sum_{|n|\leq N}\widehat{u_{0}}(n)\,e^{inx}.

Of course
\Pi_{N}u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
For
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the operator
\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}
on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
as

\widehat{\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}f}(n)=\langle n\rangle^{\sigma}\widehat{f}(n),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},\,f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T}),

where
\langle n\rangle=(1+n^{2})^{1/2}
. We can therefore define the Sobolev spaces
H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
via the norm

\|f\|_{H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}=\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\,.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. Then, ignoring the time oscillations, it follows directly from (
1.1
) that

\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0})\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T})),

and that

(1.3)

\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0})\,=\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}\Pi_{N}u_{0})\quad\text{in $L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T}))$}.

Suppose that
\sigma>0
.
Then, the convergence (
1.3
) is too weak to imply a convergence of nonlinear expressions of

\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}\Pi_{N}u_{0}).

For instance, there is no hope to make converge the sequence of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
functions

(1.4)

\big|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}\Pi_{N}u_{0})\big|^{2},

simply because their zero Fourier coefficients (in
x
) equal the numbers

\|\Pi_{N}u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})},

which is a sequence of positive numbers which diverges as soon as
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
is such that
u_{0}\notin H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
.
Remarkably, this is the only obstruction to the convergence of (
1.4
), as shown by the next statement which is the first main result in this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\alpha>1
be a positive real number and
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. For every
\sigma>0
, the following limit exists in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})

(1.5)

:\hskip-3.0pt\big|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0})\big|^{2}\hskip-3.0pt:\,=\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\Big(\big|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}\Pi_{N}u_{0})\big|^{2}-\|\Pi_{N}u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}\Big).

Moreover,

:\hskip-3.0pt\big|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0})\big|^{2}\hskip-3.0pt:\,\in W^{-s_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})),

with

s_{1}=\frac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1}\quad\text{and}\quad s_{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}2\sigma&\text{when $\sigma>\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4\alpha}$,}\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1}\bigg)_{+}&\text{when $\sigma\leq\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4\alpha}$,}\end{array}\right.

where
(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1})_{+}
stands for any positive number
\lambda>0
such that
\lambda>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1}
, and the above convergence actually holds in the space
W^{-s_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))
.

We refer to Remark
2.2
where the functional space
W^{-s_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))
is defined.
Moreover, let us add the fact that in the following three situations

s_{1}<\dfrac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1}\quad\&\quad s_{2}\in\mathbb{R},\quad s_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\quad\&\quad s_{2}<2\sigma,\quad s_{1}=\dfrac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1}\quad\&\quad s_{2}\leq\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1},

we construct a function
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
such that the convergence (
1.5
)
does not hold in the space
W^{-s_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))
. This implies in particular that when
\sigma>\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4\alpha}
, then the exponents
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
in Theorem
1.1
are optimal. However, the situation remains partially unclear when
\sigma\leq\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4\alpha}
, since we are not able to perform such a construction when
s_{1}>\frac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1}
and
2\sigma\leq s_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1}
. In this case, we can only conclude to a partial optimality of the exponents
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
.

The result of Theorem
1.1
says that the sequence (
1.4
) converges after a renormalization.
Let us mention that a renormalization of similar spirit is used in the study of nonlinear PDE in the presence of singular randomness (see
[
9
]
) or in the classical work in quantum field theory (see
[
6
]
).
The key difference between Theorem
1.1
and
[
6
,
9
]
is that in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we pass to the limit in a singular nonlinear expression thanks to time oscillation effects while in
[
6
,
9
]
one passes to the limit in a singular nonlinear expression thanks to random oscillation effects. Interestingly, for quadratic expressions in both cases (random or time oscillations), the only obstruction comes from the zero Fourier coefficient.

The result of Theorem
1.1
also says that for every
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the sequence

(\partial_{x}|(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}\Pi_{N}u_{0})|^{2})_{N}

converges in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
. In other words, for
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we may give a sense of the distribution

\partial_{x}|(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0})|^{2}.

In Theorem
1.1
we only deal with a quadratic expression of (
1.1
). It would be interesting to understand how the result extends to higher degree polynomials.
In the case of random oscillations, such an analysis is performed in
[
6
]
.

1.2.
Observability

The second main result in this paper is the following one, dealing with the observability properties of the equation (

S
α

).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\alpha>1
,
T>0
be a positive time and
b\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})\setminus\{0\}
be a non-negative function.
There exists a positive constant
C_{b,T}>0
such that for every
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
,

(1.6)

\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C_{b,T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}b(x)\big|(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

The observability properties of type (
1.6
) of Schrödinger equations on tori
\mathbb{T}^{d}
have been widely studied, mostly in the non-fractional case
\alpha=2
when
b=\mathbbm{1}_{\omega}
and
\omega\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
is an open set. Among all this literature, the two articles
[
2
,
4
]
are exceptions, since they consider more general controls
b\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
as in the present paper. Theorem
1.2
is in particular a generalization of
[
2
, Lemma 2.4]
to the fractional case
\alpha>1
. On the other hand, the paper
[
2
]
allows to treat the two-dimensional case. Our proof basically follows the strategy of
[
2
]
. However, the techniques used in order to prove Lemma
3.2
are different from
[
2
]
. Indeed, the article
[
2
]
relies on an approach based on results of propagation of singularities
via
the notion of semiclassical defect measure. The present paper, as for it, combines the smoothing effect stated in Theorem
1.1
(not in its full strength) with an ergodic argument, which is simplified to a projection argument when
b\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
(including the interesting case where
b=\mathbbm{1}_{\omega}
and
\omega\subset\mathbb{T}
is a measurable set with positive measure). Besides, we hope that our approach will allow us to deal with dispersive equations on higher dimensional tori
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

It should be mentioned that Theorem
1.2
improves

[
5
, Theorem 1]
when
d=1
, which states an observability estimate for the equation (

S
α

) in any positive time
T>0
with localized functions of the form
b=\mathbbm{1}_{\omega}
with
\omega\subset\mathbb{T}
an open set. On the other hand,
[
5
, Theorem 1]
also treats the higher-dimensional case.

In addition, let us mention that the assumption
\alpha>1
in Theorem
1.2
cannot be relaxed. Indeed, we know from
[
5
, Theorem 1]
that on the one hand, in the situation where
\alpha=1
, the observability properties of the equation (
S_{1}
) are linked to the so-called Geometric Control Condition on
\omega\subset\mathbb{T}
. On the other hand, in the setting
0<\alpha<1
, the very same result
[
5
, Theorem 1]
states that the set
\omega\subset\mathbb{T}
has to be dense so that an observability estimate holds.

2.
Smoothing estimates

In this section, we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.1.
Prolegomena

We therefore consider
\alpha>1
a positive real number,
\sigma>0
and
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. Recall that the projectors
\Pi_{N}
are defined in (
1.2
). First notice that the conservation of mass property of the equation (

S
α

) implies

\Pi_{0}\big(\big|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}\Pi_{N}u_{0})\big|^{2}\big)=\big\|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}\Pi_{N}u_{0})\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}=\|\Pi_{N}u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}.

As a consequence, the elements of the sequence

(2.1)

\Big(\big|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}\Pi_{N}u_{0})\big|^{2}-\|\Pi_{N}u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}\Big)_{N\geq 0},

can be written in the following way

\big|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}\Pi_{N}u_{0})\big|^{2}-\|\Pi_{N}u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}=\Pi^{\perp}_{0}\big(\big|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}\Pi_{N}u_{0})\big|^{2}\big).

We therefore deduce that for every test function
\varphi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi(t,x)\Big(\big|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}\Pi_{N}u_{0})(x)\big|^{2}-\|\Pi_{N}u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi(t,x)\,\Pi^{\perp}_{0}\big(\big|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}\Pi_{N}u_{0})\big|^{2}\big)(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\Pi^{\perp}_{0}\varphi)(t,x)\big|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}\Pi_{N}u_{0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

The convergence of the sequence (
2.1
) in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
and the regularity of the limit
:\penalty\ \hskip-7.0pt|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0})|^{2}\hskip-3.0pt:
will therefore be a consequence of the following bilinear smoothing estimates.

Proposition 2.1
.

For every
\sigma>0
, there exists a positive constant
c_{\sigma}>0
such that for every test function
\varphi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
and for every initial data
u_{0},v_{0}\in H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T})
,

\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\Pi^{\perp}_{0}\varphi)(t,x)(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0})(x)\overline{(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}v_{0})(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|\\
\leq c_{\sigma}\|\varphi\|_{W^{s_{1},1}(\mathbb{R},H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}\|v_{0}\|_{H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T})},

with

s_{1}=\frac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1}\quad\text{and}\quad s_{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}2\sigma&\text{when $\sigma>\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4\alpha}$,}\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1}\bigg)_{+}&\text{when $\sigma\leq\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4\alpha}$,}\end{array}\right.

where
(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1})_{+}
stands for any positive number
\lambda>0
such that
\lambda>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1}
.

Remark 2.2
.

The functional space
W^{s_{1},1}(\mathbb{R},H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))
appearing in the above statement is defined by

W^{s_{1},1}(\mathbb{R},H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))=\big\{\varphi\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}):\langle D_{t}\rangle^{s_{1}}\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R},H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))\big\},

and is endowed with the following norm

\|\varphi\|_{W^{s_{1},1}(\mathbb{R},H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{s_{1}}\varphi(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})}\,\mathrm{d}t.

For every
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the operator
\langle D_{t}\rangle^{s}
is defined on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
by

\mathscr{F}_{t}(\langle D_{t}\rangle^{s}f)(\tau)=\langle\tau\rangle^{s}\mathscr{F}_{t}(f)(\tau),\quad\tau\in\mathbb{R},\,f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}),

where
\mathscr{F}_{t}
denotes the Fourier transform with respect to the time variable
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover, let us introduce the following space

W^{-s_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))=\big\{\varphi\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}):\langle D_{t}\rangle^{-s_{1}}\varphi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))\big\},

equipped with the norm

\|\varphi\|_{W^{-s_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))}=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{-s_{1}}\varphi(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})}.

By classical arguments, we have the following duality in norm

(2.2)

\|\varphi\|_{W^{-s_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))}=\sup_{\|\psi\|_{W^{s_{1},1}(\mathbb{R},H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))}\leq 1}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}}\varphi(t,x)\psi(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|.

Before proving Proposition
2.1
, let us check that Theorem
1.1
is a consequence of this result. Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
and

\varphi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
. In order to simplify the writing, we set

u_{N}(t,x)=\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}\Pi_{N}u_{0})(x),\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T},

and also

\displaystyle c_{N}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\Pi^{\perp}_{0}\varphi)(t,x)|u_{N}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Recall that we first aim at proving that the sequence
(c_{N}(u_{0}))_{N}
converges.
By combining the following decomposition

|z_{1}|^{2}-|z_{2}|^{2}=z_{1}\overline{z_{1}}-z_{2}\overline{z_{2}}=z_{1}(\overline{z_{1}}-\overline{z_{2}})+\overline{z_{2}}(z_{1}-z_{2}),\quad z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C},

and the bilinear estimate given by Proposition
2.1
, we get that for every
N_{1},N_{2}\geq 0
,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\ |c_{N_{1}}(u_{0})-c_{N_{2}}(u_{0})|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\ \|\varphi\|_{W^{s_{1},1}(\mathbb{R},H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))}(\|u_{N_{1}}\|_{H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}+\|u_{N_{2}}\|_{H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T})})\|u_{N_{1}}-u_{N_{2}}\|_{H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\sigma}

\displaystyle\ \|\varphi\|_{W^{s_{1},1}(\mathbb{R},H^{s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\|\Pi_{N_{1}}u_{0}-\Pi_{N_{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\underset{N_{1},N_{2}\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

This proves that the sequence
(c_{N}(u_{0}))_{N}
is a numerical Cauchy sequence, which therefore converges. As a consequence, the sequence (
2.1
) converges in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
. Now, let us denote by
f_{N}
the elements of the sequence (
2.1
). It follows from the duality relation (
2.2
) and the inequality (
2.3
) that

\|f_{N_{1}}-f_{N_{2}}\|_{W^{-s_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))}\lesssim_{\sigma}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\|\Pi_{N_{1}}u_{0}-\Pi_{N_{2}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\underset{N_{1},N_{2}\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

This estimate proves that the sequence (
2.1
) is a Cauchy sequence in the Banach space
W^{-s_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))
, and therefore converges in this space. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is now ended.

2.2.
Bilinear estimates

We now give the proof of Proposition
2.1
. Let us consider
\sigma>0
,
\varphi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})
,
u_{0},v_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
be fixed functions, and write

u_{0}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{n}e^{inx},\quad v_{0}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}b_{n}e^{inx},\quad x\in\mathbb{T},

and

(\Pi^{\perp}_{0}\varphi)(t,x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}c_{n}(t)e^{inx},\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}.

A straightforward integration in space first shows that

\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\Pi_{0}^{\perp}\varphi)(t,x)(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0})(x)\overline{(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}v_{0})(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n+n_{1}-n_{2}=0,\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}c_{n}(t)a_{n_{1}}\overline{b_{n_{2}}}e^{it(|n_{1}|^{\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{\alpha})},

where
\mathrm{d}x
is properly normalized. The integration in time then makes the Fourier transform of the coefficients
c_{n}
appear as follows

\int_{\mathbb{R}}c_{n}(t)e^{it(|n_{1}|^{\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{\alpha})}\,\mathrm{d}t=\widehat{c_{n}}(|n_{2}|^{\alpha}-|n_{1}|^{\alpha}).

Thus, the term we aim at estimating is given by

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\Pi_{0}^{\perp}\varphi)(t,x)(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0})(x)\overline{(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}v_{0})(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n+n_{1}-n_{2}=0,\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}\widehat{c_{n}}(|n_{2}|^{\alpha}-|n_{1}|^{\alpha})a_{n_{1}}\overline{b_{n_{2}}}.

Notice that since
n\neq 0
in the above sum, we have
n_{1}\neq n_{2}
. There are then two cases to consider for the integers
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
.

\triangleright
Resonant case.

In the case where
n_{1}=-n_{2}
, we get
n=-2n_{1}
and Cauchy-Schwarz’ inequality gives the following bound

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n+n_{1}-n_{2}=0,\\
n\neq 0,\,n_{1}=-n_{2}\end{subarray}}|\widehat{c_{n}}(|n_{2}|^{\alpha}-|n_{1}|^{\alpha})||a_{n_{1}}||b_{n_{2}}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{c_{-2n_{1}}}(0)||a_{n_{1}}||b_{-n_{1}}|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle n_{1}\rangle^{2\sigma}|\widehat{c_{n_{1}}}(0)|\sum_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{|a_{n_{1}}||b_{-n_{1}}|}{\langle 2n_{1}\rangle^{2\sigma}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle n_{1}\rangle^{2\sigma}|\widehat{c_{n_{1}}}(0)|\|u_{0}\|_{H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}\|v_{0}\|_{H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}.

Moreover, the above supremum can be estimated by noticing that for every
n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\langle n_{1}\rangle^{2\sigma}|\widehat{c_{n_{1}}}(0)|\leq\langle n_{1}\rangle^{2\sigma}\sup_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}}\,\langle\tau\rangle^{s}|\widehat{c_{n_{1}}}(\tau)|\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle n_{1}\rangle^{2\sigma}|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{s}c_{n}(t)|\,\mathrm{d}t\\
\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{s}\varphi(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{2\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}\,\mathrm{d}t=\|\varphi\|_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R},H^{2\sigma}(\mathbb{T}))},

where we set
s=2\sigma/(\alpha-1)>0
. This value for
s>0
will be justified later, while studying the non-resonant case. We therefore deduce that

(2.4)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n+n_{1}-n_{2}=0,\\
n\neq 0,\,n_{1}=-n_{2}\end{subarray}}|\widehat{c_{n}}(|n_{2}|^{\alpha}-|n_{1}|^{\alpha})||a_{n_{1}}||b_{n_{2}}|\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R},H^{2\sigma}(\mathbb{T}))}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}\|v_{0}\|_{H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}.

\triangleright
Non-resonant case.

In the other situation where
n_{1}\neq-n_{2}
, we write

(2.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n+n_{1}-n_{2}=0,\\
n\neq 0,\,n_{1}\neq-n_{2}\end{subarray}}|\widehat{c_{n}}(|n_{2}|^{\alpha}-|n_{1}|^{\alpha})||a_{n_{1}}||b_{n_{2}}|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n+n_{1}-n_{2}=0,\\
n\neq 0,\,n_{1}\neq-n_{2}\end{subarray}}\Gamma_{n}\,\frac{|a_{n_{1}}||b_{n_{2}}|}{\langle|n_{1}|^{\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{\alpha}\rangle^{s}},

where
s=2\sigma/(\alpha-1)>0
is the same as before, and where we set

(2.6)

\Gamma_{n}=\|\langle\tau\rangle^{s}\widehat{c_{n}}(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

In order to treat the sum in the right-hand side of the estimate (
2.5
), we need to establish the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

For every real numbers
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

||x|^{\alpha}-|y|^{\alpha}|\geq\frac{1}{2^{\alpha-1}}(|x|+|y|)^{\alpha-1}||x|-|y||.

Proof.

It sufficient to consider the case where
x,y\geq 0
. Since
\alpha>1
, we can write

(2.7)

x^{\alpha}-y^{\alpha}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(tx+(1-t)y)^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{1}\alpha(tx+(1-t)y)^{\alpha-1}(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}t.

We therefore get that

\displaystyle|x^{\alpha}-y^{\alpha}|

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\alpha(tx+(1-t)y)^{\alpha-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)|x-y|

\displaystyle\geq\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\alpha\min(t,1-t)^{\alpha-1}\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)(x+y)^{\alpha-1}|x-y|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{\alpha-1}}(x+y)^{\alpha-1}|x-y|.

This is the expected estimate.
∎

We then deduce from Lemma
2.3
that when
n_{1}\neq n_{2}
, we have

||n_{1}|^{\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{\alpha}|\gtrsim(|n_{1}|+|n_{2}|)^{\alpha-1}||n_{1}|-|n_{2}||,

and since
||n_{1}|-|n_{2}||\geq 1
, we get that

\displaystyle\langle|n_{1}|^{\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{\alpha}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim||n_{1}|^{\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{\alpha}|^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim(|n_{1}|+|n_{2}|)^{2(\alpha-1)}||n_{1}|-|n_{2}||^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim(1+|n_{1}|^{2}+|n_{2}|^{2})^{\alpha-1}\langle|n_{1}|-|n_{2}|\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\langle n_{1}\rangle^{\alpha-1}\langle n_{2}\rangle^{\alpha-1}\langle|n_{1}|-|n_{2}|\rangle^{2}.

The parameter
s>0
is chosen in order to satisfy
s(\alpha-1)/2=\sigma
.
We therefore deduce that the sum we are studying is bounded in the following way

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n+n_{1}-n_{2}=0,\\
n\neq 0,\,n_{1}\neq-n_{2}\end{subarray}}|\widehat{c_{n}}(|n_{2}|^{\alpha}-|n_{1}|^{\alpha})||a_{n_{1}}||b_{n_{2}}|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{1}\neq\pm n_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Gamma_{n_{1}-n_{2}}}{\langle|n_{1}|-|n_{2}|\rangle^{s}}\,\frac{|a_{n_{1}}|}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{\sigma}}\frac{|b_{n_{2}}|}{\langle n_{2}\rangle^{\sigma}}.

In order to alleviate the writing, let us set

K_{n_{1},n_{2}}=\frac{\Gamma_{n_{1}-n_{2}}}{\langle|n_{1}|-|n_{2}|\rangle^{s}},\quad\tilde{a}_{n_{1}}=\frac{|a_{n_{1}}|}{\langle n_{1}\rangle^{\sigma}}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{b}_{n_{2}}=\frac{|b_{n_{2}}|}{\langle n_{2}\rangle^{\sigma}}.

We deduce from Cauchy-Schwarz’ inequality that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{1}\neq\pm n_{2}\end{subarray}}K_{n_{1},n_{2}}\tilde{a}_{n_{1}}\tilde{b}_{n_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{1}\neq\pm n_{2}\end{subarray}}K_{n_{1},n_{2}}\tilde{a}_{n_{1}}\bigg)\tilde{b}_{n_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum_{n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{1}\neq\pm n_{2}\end{subarray}}K_{n_{1},n_{2}}\tilde{a}_{n_{1}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\sum_{n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{b}_{n_{2}}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{1}\neq\pm n_{2}\end{subarray}}K_{n_{1},n_{2}}\tilde{a}_{n_{1}}\bigg)^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}\|v_{0}\|_{H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}.

We need to introduce two new notations still in order to alleviate the writting

A_{1}=\sup_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{2}\neq\pm n_{1}\end{subarray}}K_{n_{1},n_{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad A_{2}=\sup_{n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{1}\neq\pm n_{2}\end{subarray}}K_{n_{1},n_{2}}.

The above double sum is also controlled with Cauchy-Schwarz’ inequality as follows

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{1}\neq\pm n_{2}\end{subarray}}K_{n_{1},n_{2}}\tilde{a}_{n_{1}}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{1}\neq\pm n_{2}\end{subarray}}K_{n_{1},n_{2}}\bigg)\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{1}\neq\pm n_{2}\end{subarray}}K_{n_{1},n_{2}}\tilde{a}_{n_{1}}^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq A_{2}\sum_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{a}_{n_{1}}^{2}\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{2}\neq\pm n_{1}\end{subarray}}K_{n_{1},n_{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq A_{1}A_{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}.

It remains to study
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
. Since these two terms are similar, we only focus on
A_{1}
. To that end, we consider a non-negative number
\lambda\geq 0
such that
\lambda+s>1/2
. Let
n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
be fixed. We deduce once more from Cauchy-Schwarz’ inequality that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{2}\neq\pm n_{1}\end{subarray}}K_{n_{1},n_{2}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{2}\neq\pm n_{1}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Gamma_{n_{1}-n_{2}}}{\langle|n_{1}|-|n_{2}|\rangle^{s}}\leq\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{2}\neq\pm n_{1}\end{subarray}}\langle n_{1}-n_{2}\rangle^{2\lambda}\Gamma^{2}_{n_{1}-n_{2}}\bigg)^{1/2}\\
\times\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{2}\neq\pm n_{1}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\langle|n_{1}|-|n_{2}|\rangle^{2s}\langle n_{1}-n_{2}\rangle^{2\lambda}}\bigg)^{1/2}.

There are now two terms to treat. On the one hand, it follows from the definition (
2.6
) of the
\Gamma_{n}
and Minkowski’s integral inequality that

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle n\rangle^{2\lambda}\Gamma^{2}_{n}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle n\rangle^{2\lambda}\|\langle\tau\rangle^{s}\widehat{c_{n}}(\tau)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\langle n\rangle^{2\lambda}\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{s}c_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\bigg)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\|\langle n\rangle^{2\lambda}\langle D_{t}\rangle^{s}c_{n}\|_{l^{2}_{n}L^{1}_{t}}\leq\|\langle n\rangle^{2\lambda}\langle D_{t}\rangle^{s}c_{n}\|_{L^{1}_{t}l^{2}_{n}}=\|\varphi\|_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R},H^{\lambda}(\mathbb{T}))}.

On the other hand, the second term can be bounded by noticing that
||n_{1}|-|n_{2}||\leq|n_{1}-n_{2}|
and writing

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{2}\neq\pm n_{1}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\langle|n_{1}|-|n_{2}|\rangle^{2s}\langle n_{1}-n_{2}\rangle^{2\lambda}}\leq\sum_{n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\langle|n_{1}|-|n_{2}|\rangle^{2(s+\lambda)}}\lesssim\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{\langle l\rangle^{2(\lambda+s)}}.

Since
\lambda+s>1/2
, the above sum is finite. We therefore deduce that the term
A_{1}
is bounded as follows

A_{1}\lesssim_{\lambda,s}\|\varphi\|_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R},H^{\lambda}(\mathbb{T}))}.

A similar estimate holds for the other term
A_{2}
, with a similar proof. In a nutshell, we get that

(2.8)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n+n_{1}-n_{2}=0,\\
n\neq 0,\,n_{1}\neq-n_{2}\end{subarray}}|\widehat{c_{n}}(|n_{2}|^{\alpha}-|n_{1}|^{\alpha})||a_{n_{1}}||b_{n_{2}}|\lesssim_{s,\lambda}\|\varphi\|_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R},H^{\lambda}(\mathbb{T}))}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}\|v_{0}\|_{H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}.

\triangleright
Conclusion.
Gathering the estimates (
2.4
) and (
2.8
), we get that

\displaystyle\ \bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\Pi_{0}^{\perp}\varphi)(t,x)(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0})(x)\overline{(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}v_{0})(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{s,\lambda}

\displaystyle\ (\|\varphi\|_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R},H^{2\sigma}(\mathbb{T}))}+\|\varphi\|_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R},H^{\lambda}(\mathbb{T}))})\|u_{0}\|_{H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}\|v_{0}\|_{H^{-\sigma}(\mathbb{T})}.

Recall that
\lambda\geq 0
stands for any non-negative real number satisfying
\lambda+s>1/2
. Notice that in the case where
\lambda>2\sigma
, which occurs when

\frac{1}{2}-s\geq 2\sigma\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \sigma\leq\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{4\alpha},

we have

\|\varphi\|_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R},H^{2\sigma}(\mathbb{T}))}\leq\|\varphi\|_{W^{s,1}(\mathbb{R},H^{\lambda}(\mathbb{T}))}.

Moreover, this inequality reverses in the situation where
\lambda\leq 2\sigma
. The proof of Proposition
2.1
is now ended.

2.3.
Optimality

To end this section, let us discuss the optimality of the exponents
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
appearing in Theorem
1.1
. Precisely, in the following three situations

s_{1}<\dfrac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1}\quad\&\quad s_{2}\in\mathbb{R},\quad s_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\quad\&\quad s_{2}<2\sigma,\quad s_{1}=\dfrac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1}\quad\&\quad s_{2}\leq\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1},

we will construct a function
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
such that the associated sequence (
2.1
) does not converge in the space
W^{-s_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))
. Such functions
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
will be chosen in order to ensure that

\|f_{N}\|_{W^{-s_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))}\geq\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{-s_{1}}f_{N}(0)\|_{H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})}\underset{N\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}+\infty,

where
f_{N}
denotes the elements of the sequence (
2.1
). Notice that each function
f_{N}
is continuous with respect to the time variable
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Evaluating at
t=0
therefore makes sense.

Let us make some preliminary computations, in order to derive a manageable formula for the norm
\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{-s_{1}}f_{N}(0)\|_{H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})}
, given a function
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. In order to simplify the writing, the Fourier coefficients of the function
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
will be denoted by

a_{n}=\widehat{u_{0}}(n),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Recall from (
2.1
) that the functions
f_{N}
are given by

f_{N}(t,x)=\big|\langle D_{x}\rangle^{\sigma}(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}\Pi_{N}u_{0})\big|^{2}-\|\Pi_{N}u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{\sigma}(\mathbb{T})},\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}.

By first developing the squares, and then rearranging the terms while denoting
n=n_{1}-n_{2}
and
m=n_{2}
, we get the following expression for the functions
f_{N}

\displaystyle f_{N}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|n_{1}|,|n_{2}|\leq N\\
n_{1}\neq n_{2}\end{subarray}}\langle n_{1}\rangle^{\sigma}\langle n_{2}\rangle^{\sigma}a_{n_{1}}\overline{a_{n_{2}}}e^{it(|n_{1}|^{\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{\alpha})}e^{i(n_{1}-n_{2})x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<|n|\leq 2N}e^{inx}\bigg(\sum_{|m|,|n+m|\leq N}\langle n+m\rangle^{\sigma}\langle m\rangle^{\sigma}a_{n+m}\overline{a_{m}}\,e^{it(|n+m|^{\alpha}-|m|^{\alpha})}\bigg).

We therefore deduce that

(2.9)

\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{-s_{1}}f_{N}(t)\|_{H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\\
=\sum_{0<|n|\leq 2N}\frac{1}{\langle n\rangle^{2s_{2}}}\bigg|\sum_{|m|,|n+m|\leq N}\frac{\langle n+m\rangle^{\sigma}\langle m\rangle^{\sigma}}{\langle|n+m|^{\alpha}-|m|^{\alpha}\rangle^{s_{1}}}a_{n+m}\overline{a_{m}}\,e^{it(|n+m|^{\alpha}-|m|^{\alpha})}\bigg|^{2},

and the following formula at time
t=0

(2.10)

\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{-s_{1}}f_{N}(0)\|_{H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\\
=\sum_{0<|n|\leq 2N}\frac{1}{\langle n\rangle^{2s_{2}}}\bigg|\sum_{|m|,|n+m|\leq N}\frac{\langle n+m\rangle^{\sigma}\langle m\rangle^{\sigma}}{\langle|n+m|^{\alpha}-|m|^{\alpha}\rangle^{s_{1}}}a_{n+m}\overline{a_{m}}\bigg|^{2}.

\triangleright
Regularity in time.
Let us now focus on the optimality of the time-regularity, that is, on the optimality of the exponent
s_{1}
. To that end, let us consider
\varepsilon>0
and make the following choice for the Fourier coefficients
a_{n}

a_{n}=\frac{1}{\langle n\rangle^{1/2+\varepsilon}},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

By keeping only the term
n=1
in the first sum in (
2.10
), and restricting the second one to positive integers
m\geq 1
, we deduce that

\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{-s_{1}}f_{N}(0)\|_{H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\gtrsim_{s_{2}}\bigg|\sum_{1\leq m\leq N-1}\frac{\langle 1+m\rangle^{\sigma}\langle m\rangle^{\sigma}}{\langle|1+m|^{\alpha}-|m|^{\alpha}\rangle^{s_{1}}}\frac{1}{\langle 1+m\rangle^{1/2+\varepsilon}}\frac{1}{\langle m\rangle^{1/2+\varepsilon}}\bigg|^{2}.

We have to control the different terms appearing in the above sum. First of all, notice that the following estimates hold

\langle 1+m\rangle\lesssim\langle m\rangle\leq\langle 1+m\rangle.

Moreover, by using again the formula (
2.7
), we get that

0\leq|1+m|^{\alpha}-|m|^{\alpha}\lesssim_{\alpha}(1+m)^{\alpha-1},

which leads to the following estimate

\langle|1+m|^{\alpha}-|m|^{\alpha}\rangle\lesssim_{\alpha}\langle 1+m\rangle^{\alpha-1}.

We therefore deduce that

\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{-s_{1}}f_{N}(0)\|_{H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\gtrsim_{\alpha,s_{1},s_{2}}\bigg|\sum_{1\leq m\leq N-1}\frac{1}{\langle 1+m\rangle^{s_{1}(\alpha-1)+1+2\varepsilon-2\sigma}}\bigg|^{2}.

This proves that under the condition

s_{1}(\alpha-1)+1+2\varepsilon-2\sigma\leq 1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad s_{1}\leq\frac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1}-\frac{2\varepsilon}{\alpha-1},

we have

\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{-s_{1}}f_{N}(0)\|_{H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}=+\infty,\quad\text{whatever $s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}$},

and the sequence (
2.1
) therefore does not converge in the space
W^{-s_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))
.

\triangleright
Regularity in space.
Let us now focus on the optimality of the exponent
s_{2}
. Assuming first that
s_{2}<2\sigma
, we make the following choice for the Fourier coefficients
a_{n}

a_{n}=a_{-n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\dfrac{1}{n^{(2\sigma-s_{2})/2}}&\text{when $n=2^{k}$ for some $k\in\mathbb{N}$,}\\[10.0pt]
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Notice that the sequence
(a_{n})_{n}
indeed belongs to
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, due to the fact that since
2\sigma-s_{2}>0
, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}|a_{2^{k}}|^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{2^{2\sigma-s_{2}}}\bigg)^{k}<+\infty.

Let us come back to the expression (
2.10
), and keep only the even terms in the first sum in order to get

\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{-s_{1}}f_{N}(0)\|_{H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\\
\geq\sum_{0<|n|\leq N}\frac{1}{\langle 2n\rangle^{2s_{2}}}\bigg|\sum_{|m|,|2n+m|\leq N}\frac{\langle 2n+m\rangle^{\sigma}\langle m\rangle^{\sigma}}{\langle|2n+m|^{\alpha}-|m|^{\alpha}\rangle^{s_{1}}}a_{2n+m}a_{m}\bigg|^{2}.

The strategy is then to keep only the term
m=-n
in the second sum

\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{-s_{1}}f_{N}(0)\|_{H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\geq\sum_{0<|n|\leq N}\frac{\langle n\rangle^{4\sigma}}{\langle 2n\rangle^{2s_{2}}}|a_{n}a_{-n}|^{2}\gtrsim_{s_{2}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}|n|^{4\sigma-2s_{2}}|a_{n}a_{-n}|^{2}.

The sequence
(a_{n})_{n}
has been constructed in order to satisfy that

\sum_{n=1}^{2^{N}}|n|^{4\sigma-2s_{2}}|a_{n}a_{-n}|^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{2^{N}}|n|^{4\sigma-2s_{2}}|a_{n}|^{4}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\frac{|2^{k}|^{4\sigma-2s_{2}}}{|2^{k}|^{4\sigma-2s_{2}}}=1+N\underset{N\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}+\infty.

As a consequence, the sequence (
2.1
) cannot converge in the space
W^{-s_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))
.

Finally, let us assume that
s_{1}=\frac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1}
and
2(s_{1}+s_{2})=1
. Let us also choose the Fourier coefficients
a_{n}
being equal to
0
when
n\leq 0
,
a_{1}=1
and otherwise

a_{n}=\frac{1}{n^{1/2}(\log n)^{3/4}},\quad n\geq 2.

By using again the expression (
2.10
) for
2N
, while restricting the first sum to the indices
-N\leq n\leq-1
and restricting the second one to
-2n\leq m\leq 2N
, we get that

\displaystyle\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{-s_{1}}f_{2N}(0)\|_{H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n=-N}^{-1}\frac{1}{\langle n\rangle^{2s_{2}}}\bigg|\sum_{m=-2n}^{2N}\frac{\langle n+m\rangle^{\sigma}\langle m\rangle^{\sigma}}{\langle|n+m|^{\alpha}-|m|^{\alpha}\rangle^{s_{1}}}a_{n+m}a_{m}\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\langle n\rangle^{2s_{2}}}\bigg|\sum_{m=2n}^{2N}\frac{\langle m-n\rangle^{\sigma}\langle m\rangle^{\sigma}}{\langle|m-n|^{\alpha}-|m|^{\alpha}\rangle^{s_{1}}}a_{m-n}a_{m}\bigg|^{2}.

We now have to control the terms in the above sum. On the one hand, since
0\leq m-n\leq m
and that the sequence
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is decreasing, we get that

\langle m-n\rangle^{\sigma}\langle m\rangle^{\sigma}\geq\langle m-n\rangle^{2\sigma}\quad\text{and}\quad a_{m-n}a_{m}\geq a_{m}^{2}.

On the other hand, since
m\geq n
, it follows from the formula (
2.7
) that

|(m-n)^{\alpha}-m^{\alpha}|\lesssim_{\alpha}(m-n+m)^{\alpha-1}|m-n-m|=(2m-n)^{\alpha-1}n.

By using the fact that
n\geq 1
, we therefore deduce that

\displaystyle\langle|m-n|^{\alpha}-|m|^{\alpha}\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}1+(2m-n)^{2(\alpha-1)}n^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}(1+(2m-n)^{2(\alpha-1)})n^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}\langle 2m-n\rangle^{2(\alpha-1)}\langle n\rangle^{2}.

The restriction
m\geq 2n
allows to get the following bound
2m-n\leq 3(m-n)
, and as a consequence,

\langle|m-n|^{\alpha}-|m|^{\alpha}\rangle^{s_{1}}\lesssim_{\alpha,s_{1}}\langle m-n\rangle^{s_{1}(\alpha-1)}\langle n\rangle^{s_{1}}.

Gathering all these estimates, and recalling that
s_{1}=\frac{2\sigma}{\alpha-1}
and
2(s_{1}+s_{2})=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{-s_{1}}f_{2N}(0)\|_{H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim_{\alpha,s_{1}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\langle n\rangle^{2(s_{1}+s_{2})}}\bigg|\sum_{m=2n}^{2N}\frac{\langle m-n\rangle^{2\sigma}}{\langle m-n\rangle^{s_{1}(\alpha-1)}}a_{m}^{2}\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n}\bigg|\sum_{m=2n}^{2N}a_{m}^{2}\bigg|^{2}.

The above second sum will be controlled by using a comparison with an integral, the latter being computed with the change of variable
u=\log x
,

\sum_{m=2n}^{2N}a_{m}^{2}=\sum_{m=2n}^{2N}\frac{1}{m(\log m)^{3/2}}\geq\int_{2n}^{2N}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x(\log x)^{3/2}}=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log(2n)}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log(2N)}}\bigg).

As a consequence,

\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{-s_{1}}f_{2N}(0)\|_{H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}\gtrsim_{\alpha,s_{1}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n}\bigg|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log(2n)}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log(2N)}}\bigg|^{2}.

We now have three numerical series to study, coming from an expansion of the above square. First of all, we classically have

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{\log(2n)}\underset{N\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}+\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{\log(2N)}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n}\underset{N\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}1.

The last one is also compared to an integral as follows

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log(2N)}}\sum_{n=2}^{N}\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log(2n)}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log(2N)}}\int_{1}^{N}\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x\sqrt{\log(2x)}}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{\log(2N)}}{\sqrt{\log(2N)}}=1.

We therefore deduce that

\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\|\langle D_{t}\rangle^{-s_{1}}f_{2N}(0)\|_{H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}=+\infty,

and the sequence (
2.1
) cannot converge in the space
W^{-s_{1},\infty}(\mathbb{R},H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}))
. Notice that as a consequence, the very same holds when
2(s_{1}+s_{2})<1
since we have
H^{-s_{2}}(\mathbb{T})\hookrightarrow H^{-1/2+s_{1}}(\mathbb{T})
in this situation.

The discussion of the optimality of the exponents
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
in Theorem
1.1
is now ended. Let us finally mention that by using the formula (
2.9
), one could actually give a direct proof of Theorem
1.1
. However, it would not allow to derive the bilinear estimates stated in Proposition
2.1
, which is a stronger result being moreover key in the proof of the observability result stated in Theorem
1.2
.

3.
Observability estimates

The aim of this section is to prove Theorem
1.2
. Let
\alpha>1
be a positive real number and
b\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})\setminus\{0\}
be a non-negative function.
The strategy consists in four steps:

1.

Proof of the following
L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{t}
Strichartz estimate

\big\|e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T},L^{2}(0,T))}\leq C_{T}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}.

2.

Proof of the following weak observability estimate

\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C_{b,T}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}b(x)\big|(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\bigg).

3.

Proof of the following unique continuation result

\text{\big($\sqrt{b}e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0}=0$ on $(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}$}\big)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{$u_{0}\equiv 0$ on $\mathbb{T}$}.

4.

Removing the
H^{-\alpha}
-norm in the above weak observability estimate.

Steps 2,3 and 4 are now very classical and had been used to study the observability properties of Schrödinger-type equations on tori, see
[
3
]
for a review.
Steps 3 and 4 are known as the Bardos–Lebeau–Rauch uniqueness-compactness argument. The
L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{t}
Strichartz estimate presented in Step 1, crucial for dealing with the case
b\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, has been first introduced in the paper
[
1
]
in the non-fractional case
\alpha=2
.

3.1.
Step 1: Strichartz type estimate

As a preliminary, let us first prove a
L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{t}
estimate for the solutions of the equation (

S
α

), which is an adaptation of
[
1
, Proposition 2.1]
to the fractional case. A key feature of this estimate is that it does not encounter derivative losses as does e.g. the
L^{4}_{t,x}
Strichartz estimates for

e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}
obtained in
[
8
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every positive time
T>0
, there exists a positive constant
C_{T}>0
such that for every initial datum
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
,

\big\|e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0}\big\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T},L^{2}(0,T))}\leq C_{T}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}.

Proof.

Setting
c_{n}=\widehat{u_{0}}(n)
, we begin by developing the
L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{t}
norm we aim at estimating

\displaystyle\big\|e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0}\big\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T},L^{2}(0,T))}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{T}\bigg|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}c_{n}e^{it|n|^{\alpha}}e^{inx}\bigg|^{2}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}e^{it(|n_{1}|^{\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{\alpha})}\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)e^{i(n_{1}-n_{2})x}c_{n_{1}}\overline{c_{n_{2}}}.

There are then two cases to consider for the integers
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
in the above sum.

\triangleright
Resonant case.
In the situation where
n_{1}=\pm n_{2}
, we directly get that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{1}=\pm n_{2}\end{subarray}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}e^{it(|n_{1}|^{\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{\alpha})}\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)e^{i(n_{1}-n_{2})x}c_{n_{1}}\overline{c_{n_{2}}}\lesssim_{T}\sum_{n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}}|c_{n_{1}}|^{2}=\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}.

\triangleright
Non-resonant case.
In the case where
n_{1}\neq\pm n_{2}
, we begin by writing that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{1}\neq\pm n_{2}\end{subarray}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}e^{it(|n_{1}|^{\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{\alpha})}\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)e^{i(n_{1}-n_{2})x}c_{n_{1}}\overline{c_{n_{2}}}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{1}\neq\pm n_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{|c_{n_{1}}||c_{n_{2}}|}{||n_{1}|^{\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{\alpha}|}.

By using anew Lemma
2.3
, we obtain the following bound for the above denominator

||n_{1}|^{\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{\alpha}|\gtrsim(|n_{1}|+|n_{2}|)^{\alpha-1}||n_{1}|-|n_{2}||\geq||n_{1}|-|n_{2}||^{\alpha}.

Cauchy-Schwarz’ inequality then implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{1}\neq\pm n_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{|c_{n_{1}}||c_{n_{2}}|}{||n_{1}|^{\alpha}-|n_{2}|^{\alpha}|}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n_{1}\neq\pm n_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{|c_{n_{1}}||c_{n_{2}}|}{||n_{1}|-|n_{2}||^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\geq 0\\
n_{1}\neq n_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{|c_{n_{1}}||c_{n_{2}}|+|c_{-n_{1}}||c_{n_{2}}|+|c_{n_{1}}||c_{-n_{2}}|+|c_{-n_{1}}||c_{-n_{2}}|}{|n_{1}-n_{2}|^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\sum_{l\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{l^{\alpha}}\bigg)\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}.

Notice that the above sum is finite since
\alpha>1
. This ends the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

3.2.
Step 2: estimate with error term

We now derive the following weak observability estimates from the smoothing estimates stated in Proposition
2.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every positive time
T>0
, there exists a positive constant
C_{b,T}>0
such that for every
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
,

\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C_{b,T}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}b(x)\big|(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\bigg).

Proof.

Arguing by contradiction, we consider a sequence
(u_{n,0})_{n}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
such that
\|u_{n,0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}=1
and satisfying

(3.1)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}b(x)\big|(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{n,0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\|u_{n,0}\|^{2}_{H^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\frac{1}{n}.

Setting
u_{n}(t)=e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{n,0}
,
we aim at proving that the sequence
(u_{n})_{n}

satisfies that for all
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(0,T)

(3.2)

\int_{0}^{T}\psi(t)\|u_{n}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\,\mathrm{d}t\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

This would end the proof of Lemma
3.2
. Indeed, the conservation of mass of the equation (

S
α

) would then imply that

\int_{0}^{T}\psi(t)\|u_{n}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\psi(t)\|u_{n,0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\psi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

This convergence of course cannot hold for all functions
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(0,T)
.

\triangleright
Step 2.1.
First of all, let us prove that for every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{T}
,

(3.3)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi(t)(\tau_{x_{0}}b)(x)|u_{n}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0,

where the translation operator
\tau_{x_{0}}
is defined for every function in
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
by

(\tau_{x_{0}}f)(x)=f(x-x_{0}),\quad x\in\mathbb{T}.

To that end, we will widely use the fact that for every smooth function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, we get from the smoothing estimates stated in Proposition
2.1
applied with
\sigma=\alpha
,
u_{0}=v_{0}=u_{n,0}
and the tensorized test function
\psi\otimes\varphi
that

(3.4)

\bigg|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi(t)(\Pi_{0}^{\perp}\varphi)(x)|u_{n}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|\lesssim_{\psi,\varphi}\|u_{n,0}\|^{2}_{H^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\quad\text{by \eqref{18022024E1}}.

Let us consider a sequence
(b_{j})_{j}
in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
that converges to the function
b
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, and write

\bigg|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi(t)(b-\tau_{x_{0}}b)(x)|u_{n}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|\leq\bigg|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi(t)(b_{j}-\tau_{x_{0}}b_{j})(x)|u_{n}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|\\
+\bigg|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi(t)(b-b_{j}-\tau_{x_{0}}(b-b_{j}))(x)|u_{n}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|.

There are now two terms to treat. On the one hand, we deduce from the convergence (
3.4
) with the functions
\varphi=b_{j}-\tau_{x_{0}}b_{j}
, which satisfy
\Pi^{\perp}_{0}\varphi=\varphi
, that the following convergence holds for every integer
j\geq 0

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi(t)(b_{j}-\tau_{x_{0}}b_{j})(x)|u_{n}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

On the other hand, we deduce from the
L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{t}
Strichartz estimate stated in Lemma
3.1
that

\bigg|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi(t)(b-b_{j}-\tau_{x_{0}}(b-b_{j}))(x)|u_{n}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|\lesssim_{\psi}\|b-b_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})},

since the functions
u_{n,0}
are normalized in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. We therefore deduce that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\bigg|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi(t)(b-\tau_{x_{0}}b)(x)|u_{n}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|\lesssim\|b-b_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\underset{j\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Recalling from (
3.1
) that we also have

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi(t)b(x)|u_{n}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0,

this proves that the convergence (
3.3
) actually holds. Notice that in the very particular case where
b=\mathbbm{1}_{\omega}
and
\omega\subset\mathbb{T}
is an open set, we directly conclude to the convergence (
3.2
) by covering the circle
\mathbb{T}
with a finite number of open sets of the form
\tau_{x_{0}}\omega
with
x_{0}\in\mathbb{T}
. The
L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{t}
Strichartz estimate is in fact not necessary in this case, since it suffices to apply the convergence (
3.4
) with the functions
\varphi=f-\tau_{x_{0}}f
, where
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
is a smooth function such that
\supp f\subset\omega
.

\triangleright
Step 2.2.
In order to derive the convergence (
3.2
) from (
3.3
) when
b\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, we need to establish the following approximation result.

Lemma 3.3
.

For every function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, we have

1\in\overline{\vect\{\tau_{x_{0}}f:x_{0}\in\mathbb{T}\}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\widehat{f}(0)\neq 0,

the closure being taken with respect to the
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
topology.

Proof.

The necessary part of this statement follows from the fact that

\forall x_{0}\in\mathbb{T},\quad\widehat{\tau_{x_{0}}f}(0)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}(\tau_{x_{0}}f)(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\widehat{f}(0).

Concerning the sufficient part, we can assume without lost of generality that
\widehat{f}(0)=1
. Let us consider
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus 2\pi\mathbb{Q}
. In order to simplify the writing, we will use the following notation for every positive integer
n\geq 1
and
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})

S_{n}(g)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\tau_{k\alpha}g\in\vect\{\tau_{x_{0}}g:x_{0}\in\mathbb{T}\}.

We consider a sequence
(f_{j})_{j}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
that converges to the function
f
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, and we write

\displaystyle\|1-S_{n}(f)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\|1-S_{n}(f_{j})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}+\|S_{n}(f_{j})-S_{n}(f)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\|1-S_{n}(f_{j})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\|\tau_{k\alpha}f_{j}-\tau_{k\alpha}f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\leq|1-\widehat{f_{j}}(0)|+\|\widehat{f_{j}}(0)-S_{n}(f_{j})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}+\|f_{j}-f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}.

Moreover, a classical consequence of von Neumann’s ergodic theorem
[
7
, Theorem 5.1]
implies that for every integer
j\geq 0
,

\|\widehat{f_{j}}(0)-S_{n}(f_{j})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

This implies that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\|1-S_{n}(f)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\leq|1-\widehat{f_{j}}(0)|+\|f_{j}-f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\leq 2\|f_{j}-f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\underset{j\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

The proof of Lemma
3.3
is therefore ended. It should be noted, however, that in the particular case where
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, the above ergodic argument can be simplified to a projection argument. Indeed, let us introduce the following notation

F=\overline{\vect\{\tau_{x_{0}}f:x_{0}\in\mathbb{T}\}}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{T}).

Let
\varphi\in F
be the orthogonal projection of the constant function
1
onto the closed vector space
F
. Since
1-\varphi\in F^{\perp}
, we get in particular that

\forall x_{0}\in\mathbb{T},\quad 0=\langle 1-\varphi,\tau_{x_{0}}f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{1-\varphi}(n)\widehat{f}(n)e^{inx_{0}}.

This formula shows that the function
x_{0}\mapsto\langle 1-\varphi,\tau_{x_{0}}f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}
belongs to the space
C(\mathbb{T})
. We therefore deduce that

\forall n\in\mathbb{Z},\quad\widehat{1-\varphi}(n)\widehat{f}(n)=0.

Since
\widehat{f}(0)\neq 0
, we get that
\widehat{\varphi}(0)=1
. Moreover,
\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq 1
, and we conclude that
\varphi=1
, implying that
1\in F
as expected. We were not able then to pass directly to the case where
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
with an approximation argument. This is the reason why we made use of von Neumann’s ergodic theorem.
∎

We can now prove (
3.2
). Since the assumptions on the function
b
imply that
\widehat{b}(0)\neq 0
, we deduce from Lemma
3.3
that there exists a sequence
(b_{j})_{j}
with

b_{j}\in\vect\{\tau_{x_{0}}b:x_{0}\in\mathbb{T}\},

satisfying that

b_{j}\underset{j\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}1\quad\text{in $L^{1}(\mathbb{T})$}.

Let us now write

\bigg|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi(t)|u_{n}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|\leq\bigg|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi(t)(1-b_{j})(x)|u_{n}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|\\
+\bigg|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi(t)b_{j}(x)|u_{n}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|.

The first term can be treated as follows by using anew Lemma
3.1

\bigg|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi(t)(1-b_{j})(x)|u_{n}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|\lesssim_{\psi}\|1-b_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}.

Moreover, as a consequence of (
3.3
), we get that for every
j\geq 0
,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi(t)b_{j}(x)|u_{n}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

We therefore deduce that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\bigg|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\psi(t)|u_{n}(t,x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg|\lesssim\|1-b_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\underset{j\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

This ends the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

3.3.
Step 3: unique continuation

Let us now consider the following vector space

\mathcal{N}_{b,T}=\big\{u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}):\text{$\sqrt{b}e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0}=0$ on $(0,T)\times\mathbb{T}$}\big\}.

In this subsection, we prove the following unique continuation result.

Lemma 3.4
.

For every positive time
T>0
, we have

\mathcal{N}_{b,T}=\{0\}.

Proof.

Let us begin by checking that the vector space
\mathcal{N}_{b,T}
is invariant under the action of the operator
|D_{x}|^{\alpha}
, with moreover

(3.5)

\exists c>0,\forall u_{0}\in\mathcal{N}_{b,T},\quad\||D_{x}|^{\alpha}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq c\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}.

Let
u_{0}\in\mathcal{N}_{b,T}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,T)
. We also consider the function
u_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-1}(e^{i\varepsilon|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0}-u_{0})
. Since
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{N}_{b,T/2}
when
0<\varepsilon<T/2
, we deduce from Lemma
3.2
applied at time
T/2
that

(3.6)

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq C_{b,T/2}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}.

Moreover, Plancherel’s theorem and the dominated convergence theorem imply that

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}\rightarrow\||D_{x}|^{\alpha}\langle D_{x}\rangle^{-\alpha}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\quad\text{as $\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}$}.

Indeed, on the one hand, the following convergence holds for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}|\widehat{u_{\varepsilon}}(n)|=\bigg|\frac{e^{i\varepsilon|n|^{\alpha}}\widehat{u_{0}}(n)-\widehat{u_{0}}(n)}{\varepsilon\langle n\rangle^{\alpha}}\bigg|=\frac{|n|^{\alpha}}{\langle n\rangle^{\alpha}}\bigg|\frac{e^{i\varepsilon|n|^{\alpha}}-1}{\varepsilon|n|^{\alpha}}\bigg||\widehat{u_{0}}(n)|\underset{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\longrightarrow}\frac{|n|^{\alpha}}{\langle n\rangle^{\alpha}}|\widehat{u_{0}}(n)|.

On the other hand, we have the following domination from the mean value theorem for every
\varepsilon>0
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\langle n\rangle^{-\alpha}|\widehat{u_{\varepsilon}}(n)|\leq|\widehat{u_{0}}(n)|.

In particular, the sequence
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
H^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
. This fact combined with the estimate (
3.6
) implies that the sequence
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is also bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
and therefore weakly converges in this space up to extracting. Since the sequence
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
also converges to
i|D_{x}|^{\alpha}u_{0}
in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{T})
, the aforementionned weak limit is nothing but the function
i|D_{x}|^{\alpha}u_{0}
, which therefore belongs to
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. Plancherel’s theorem and the dominated convergence theorem then implies that

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\rightarrow\||D_{x}|^{\alpha}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}\quad\text{as $\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}$}.

As a consequence, the sequence
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
converges strongly to the function
i|D_{x}|^{\alpha}u_{0}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. The expected estimate (
3.5
) is then obtained by passing to the limit as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
in (
3.6
). Moreover, since
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{N}_{b,T-\delta}
when
0<\varepsilon<\delta
and that the strong convergence in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
implies almost everywhere pointwize convergence up to extraction, we deduce that
|D_{x}|^{\alpha}u_{0}\in\mathcal{N}_{b,T-\delta}
for every
\delta\in(0,T)
. This implies that the function
|D_{x}|^{\alpha}u_{0}
also belongs to
\mathcal{N}_{b,T}
. Hence, the vector space
\mathcal{N}_{b,T}
is invariant under the action of the linear operator
|D_{x}|^{\alpha}
.

Notice that the estimate (
3.5
) implies that the unit ball of
\mathcal{N}_{b,T}
is bounded in
H^{\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, and is therefore relatively compact by Rellich’s theorem. This implies that the vector space
\mathcal{N}_{b,T}
is finite-dimensional by Riesz’ theorem. As a consequence, since the linear operator
|D_{x}|^{\alpha}:\mathcal{N}_{b,T}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{b,T}
is bounded and in the situation where
\mathcal{N}_{b,T}\neq\{0\}
, there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
u\in\mathcal{N}_{b,T}\setminus\{0\}
such that
|D_{x}|^{\alpha}u=\lambda u
. The function
u
not being identically equal to zero on
\mathbb{T}
, we deduce by passing to the Fourier side that there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\lambda=|n|^{\alpha}
, and that the function
u
is actually given by

u(x)=c_{1}e^{inx}+c_{2}e^{-inx},\quad x\in\mathbb{T},

with
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
c_{1}c_{2}\neq 0
. Moreover, since
u
is an eigenfunction of the operator
|D_{x}|^{\alpha}
and
u\in\mathcal{N}_{b,T}
, we get that the function
u
vanishes on
\{b>0\}
, a measurable set with positive measure, which is absurd. As a consequence, we have
\mathcal{N}_{b,T}=\{0\}
as expected.
∎

3.4.
Step 4: removing the
H^{-\alpha}
-norm

We are now in position to end the proof of Theorem
1.2
. We argue once again by contradiction by considering a sequence
(u_{n,0})_{n}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
such that
\|u_{n,0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}=1
and satisfying

(3.7)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}b(x)\big|(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{n,0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{1}{n}.

The sequence
(u_{n,0})_{n}
being bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, there exists a function
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
such that, up to extracting, the sequence
(u_{n,0})_{n}
converges weakly to

u_{0}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
and strongly in
H^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, by Rellich’s theorem. Let us consider the sequence of non-negative functions
(b_{j})_{j}
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
whose elements are given by
b_{j}=b\mathbbm{1}_{\{b\leq j\}}
(recall that
b
is a non-negative function). This sequence
(b_{j})_{j}
converges to the function
b
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
by the dominated convergence theorem.
Notice that the weak convergence, which holds for every
j\geq 0
,

\forall t\in[0,T],\quad\sqrt{b_{j}}e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{n,0}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\rightharpoonup}\sqrt{b_{j}}e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0}\quad\text{in $L^{2}(\mathbb{T})$},

is a consequence of the following computations, where
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
,

\displaystyle\langle\sqrt{b_{j}}e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{n,0},\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle=\langle u_{n,0},e^{-it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}\sqrt{b_{j}}\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}\langle u_{0},e^{-it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}\sqrt{b_{j}}\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}=\langle\sqrt{b_{j}}e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0},\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}.

We therefore deduce from results on weak convergence and Fatou’s lemma that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}b_{j}(x)\big|(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}}b_{j}(x)\big|(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{n,0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}b_{j}(x)\big|(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{n,0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Let us split the integral in the right-hand side of the above inequality as follows

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}b_{j}(x)\big|(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{n,0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}b(x)\big|(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{n,0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}(b_{j}-b)(x)\big|(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{n,0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

These two terms can be bounded by using respectively the inequality (
3.7
) and the
L^{\infty}_{x}L^{2}_{t}
Strichartz estimate stated in Lemma
3.1
, which leads to the following estimate

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}b_{j}(x)\big|(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{n,0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\frac{1}{n}+\|b-b_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})},

since the functions
u_{n,0}
are normalized in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
. We therefore deduce from (
3.8
) that

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}b_{j}(x)\big|(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\|b-b_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}.

Coming back to the function
b
, this estimate and another use of Lemma
3.1
allow to derive that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}b(x)\big|(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\lesssim\|b-b_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{T})}\underset{j\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

Keeping the notations of Section
3.3
, this proves that
u_{0}\in\mathcal{N}_{b,T}
, and therefore
u_{0}\equiv 0
by Lemma
3.4
. We finally deduce from Lemma
3.2
that

\underbrace{\|u_{n,0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}}_{=\,1}\leq C_{b,T}\bigg(\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}b(x)\big|(e^{it|D_{x}|^{\alpha}}u_{n,0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t}_{\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0}+\underbrace{\|u_{n,0}\|^{2}_{H^{-\alpha}(\mathbb{T})}}_{\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0}\bigg).

This is of course absurd, and the proof of Theorem
1.2
is ended.

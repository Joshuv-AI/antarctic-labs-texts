Constrained Least Action and Quantum Mechanics


Abstract
The wave-particle duality and its probabilistic interpretation are at the heart of quantum mechanics. Here we show that, in some standard contexts like the double slit experiment, a deterministic interpretation can be provided. This interpretation arises from the fact that if one mininizes a deterministic action subject to spatial inequality contraints , in general the solution is not unique. Thus, the probabilistic setting is replaced in part by the non-uniqueness of solutions of certain constrained optimization problems, itself often a result of the non-Lipschitzness of the constrained dynamics. While the approach leaves the results of associated Feynman integrals unchanged, it may considerably simplify their computation as the problems grow more complex, since only specific deterministic paths computed from the constrained minimization need to be included in the integral.

Introduction

This papers derives simple results on optimization of Lagrangian dynamics subject to spatial inequality constraints, and applies them to elementary contexts of quantum mechanics. It shows that, in these contexts, quantum states computed by the usual Feynman integral along all possible paths
[
4
]
can be derived instead from a simpler minimization along the multiple
deterministic
paths of a Lagrangian dynamics with inequality constraints. In the double slit or
n
-slit experiments, for instance, the spatial inequality constraints simply represent the geometry of the slits. The results also suggest that some quantum systems may actually be simulated using purely
classical means.

Lagrangian dynamics within a constrained space
\mathbb{G}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, rather than the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, are first derived. At the border
\partial\mathbb{G}^{n}
of
\mathbb{G}^{n}
, a Dirac constraint force, corresponding to the derivative of a step function on each inequality constraint, ensures that the constraint is not violated.
This non-Lipschitz activation of the constraint force can lead to multiple, but deterministic, solutions of the constrained Lagrangian dynamics.

The resulting motion is a piece-wise Euler-Lagrange deterministic motion from an initial point, via some collision points, to the final point. At each constraint collision, multiple deterministic trajectories may arise, which are analogs to diffraction in optics or quantum physics. In the slit experiments of quantum mechanics, for instance, this yields a purely classical deterministic Lagrangian interpretation, with straight-line motions of free particles replaced by non-unique polygonal trajectories.

Theorem 1

The action

S=\int^{{\bf q}(t)}_{{\bf q}_{o}(t_{o})}Ldt

(13)

of a Lagrangian
L=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\bf q}^{T}{\bf M}({\bf q},t)\dot{\bf q}-V({\bf q},t)

constrained by
{\bf q}\in\mathbb{G}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

\mathbb{G}^{n}
is defined by the
j=1,...,J
inequality constraints

g_{j}({\bf q},t)\leq 0

(14)

is extremized between two points
{\bf q}_{o}(t_{o})
and
{\bf q}(t)
by a solution
\bar{\bf q}(t)={\bf q}(t)
of

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\bf q}}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial{\bf q}}=\sum_{all\ j\in\mathcal{A}}\frac{\partial g_{j}}{\partial{\bf q}}\lambda_{j}\dot{\theta}(g_{j})

(15)

where the Lagrange multipliers
\lambda_{j}
have to comply to Definition
2
. Multiple extremal solutions
\bar{\bf q}(t)
may occur at an active constraint, due to the non Lipschitz-continuous Dirac impulse
\dot{\theta}(g_{j})
(
2

For a quadratic Lagrangian (

\displaystyle\langle\psi({\bf q},t)|\psi({\bf q}_{o},t_{o})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}S(\bar{\bf q})}F(t,t_{o})

along each extremal solution of (

The left-hand side of (

Remark 2
In Theorem

•

First, solving the constrained Euler-Lagrange dynamics (

•

Next, applying the path integral (

If an analytic solution can be found, both methods yield identical results for quadratic Lagrangians. If an analytic solution of (

L=\frac{m}{2}\dot{\bf q}^{T}\dot{\bf q}

{\bf q}\in\mathbb{G}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle e^{\frac{im({\bf q}_{N}-{\bf q}_{N-1})^{T}({\bf q}_{N}-{\bf q}_{N-1})}{2\hbar(t_{N}-t_{N-1})}+...+\frac{im({\bf q}_{1}-{\bf q}_{o})^{T}({\bf q}_{1}-{\bf q}_{o})}{2\hbar(t_{1}-t_{0})}}

where the exponent simply corresponds to the particle travel time along the polygon. Note that

•

Since each corner of the polygon has normals in all directions, the trajectory can bend in any direction behind the constraint as long as the constraint is not violated. In quantum physics this effect is called diffraction.

•

Multiple solutions of the deterministic Lagrangian dynamics of Theorem
1
may exist because the Dirac collision force is not Lipschitz continuous. These multiple solutions of the deterministic Lagrangian of Theorem
1
correspond to the Huygens-Fresnel principle

7
]
of radial particle propagation in spherical waves.

•

If there are several polygons connecting
{\bf q}_{N}
and
{\bf q}_{o}
then the statisical distribution is given by the superposition of the quantum state of each Lagrangian polygon (
18

•

The Feynman path integral is only used to compute the stochastic

of the multiple solutions of the deterministic Lagrangian of Theorem

3
Summary

Theorem
1
extends the classical Lagrangian dynamics within an unconstrained space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to a constrained space
\mathbb{G}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. At the border
\partial\mathbb{G}^{n}
of
\mathbb{G}^{n}
, a Dirac Lagrangian constraint force ensures that the constraint is not violated. This non-Lipschitz activation of the constraint force can lead to multiple, but deterministic, collision solutions of the classical Lagrangian dynamics.

This result allows a classical Least Action interpretation of the single or double slit experiments of quantum mechanics. Example
2.1
and Example
2.2
show that in this context the only difference between quantum physics and the classical Least Action is the fact that the latter is defined in unconstrained Riemann spaces
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, leading to a single deterministic solution of the Lipschitz continuous Lagrangian dynamics. The non-Lipschitz Dirac collision force, which is introduced by inequality constraints
\mathbb{G}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, leads to a set of multiple solutions of a deterministic Least Action principle of Theorem
1
, which is intepreted as diffraction in quantum physics.
The shortest connecting path or Least Action path (
1
) of a particle
{\bf q}\in\mathbb{G}^{n}
with
L=\frac{m}{2}\ \dot{\bf q}^{T}\dot{\bf q}
is a polygon with corners at the active constraints, rather then a straight line. Each corner implies multiple deterministic solutions of the Least Action principle in Theorem
1
.

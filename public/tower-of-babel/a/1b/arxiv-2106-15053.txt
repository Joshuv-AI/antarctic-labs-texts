Magnetic moments and transition magnetic moments of P c P_{c} and P c ​ s P_{cs} states
Ming-Wei Li 1,2, Zhan-Wei Liu 1,2,4,5, Zhi-Feng Sun 1,4,5, Rui Chen 3

Abstract
We study the magnetic moments and transition magnetic moments of P c P_{c} and P c ​ s P_{cs} states in the molecular picture. We first revisit the magnetic moments of P c ​ ( 4312 ) P_{c}(4312) , P c ​ ( 4440 ) P_{c}(4440) , and P c ​ ( 4457 ) P_{c}(4457) as the S S wave molecular states without coupled channel effects. The coupled channel effects and the D D wave contributions are then investigated carefully. The coupled channel effects contribute to the change of 0.1 ∼ 0.4 0.1\sim 0.4 nuclear magneton μ N \mu_{N} for most cases while the D D wave only induces the variation of less than 0.03 ​ μ N 0.03~\mu_{N} . In addition, we obtain the transition magnetic moments between different P c P_{c} states and the related electromagnetic decay widths of P c ′ → P c ​ γ P_{c}^{\prime}\to P_{c}\gamma . The magnetic moments of P c ​ s ​ ( 4459 ) P_{cs}(4459) are much different for the assumption of spin being 1/2 or 3/2. The study of electromagnetic properties will help us disclose further the structure of these unconventional states.

Magnetic moments of
P_{c}

P_{cs}

In this section we provide the magnetic moments of
P_{c}
and
P_{cs}
states in the molecular picture. Before doing that, we first revisit how to obtain the magnetic moment for a conventional hadron within the quark model.

We use a baryon
|B\rangle
with
J=1/2
as an example. The magnetic moment is related to the matrix element of the electromagnetic current
J_{\mu}
, that is,
\langle B|J_{\mu}|B\rangle
. Usually the matrix element is constrained by the gauge invariance,
P
-parity conservation, Lorentz covariance, etc. Thus it can be parameterized in terms of few form factors. In the nonrelativistic limit

\langle B(p)|J_{\mu}|B(p^{\prime})\rangle=e\bar{u}_{B}\left\{v_{\mu}G_{E}(q^{2})-\frac{[S_{\mu},S_{\nu}]q^{\nu}}{M_{B}}G_{M}(q^{2})\right\}u_{B},

(1)

where the static velocity
v_{\nu}=(1,\vec{0})
, the spin matrix
S_{\mu}=\frac{i}{2}\gamma_{5}\sigma_{\mu\nu}v^{\nu}
, and the difference of baryon momenta
q=p^{\prime}-p
is carried by the electromagnetic current.

To see the connection between the magnetic moment and the form factor, we calculate the interacting energy among the baryon and the photon

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{\rm QED}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle B(p)\gamma(q)|\,J_{\mu}A^{\mu}\,|B(p^{\prime})\rangle

(2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\bar{u}_{B}\left\{v\cdot\epsilon_{\gamma}^{*}\,G_{E}(q^{2})-\frac{[S_{\mu},S_{\nu}]\,q^{\nu}\epsilon_{\gamma}^{*\mu}}{M_{B}}G_{M}(q^{2})\right\}u_{B}

\displaystyle\overset{\rm small~q}{\approx}

\displaystyle e\epsilon_{\gamma}^{*0}\,G_{E}(0)-\frac{e\,G_{M}(0)\,\vec{S}}{M_{B}}\cdot(i\vec{q}\times\vec{\epsilon}_{\gamma}^{*}).\qquad

The second term is related to the energy in magnetic field
\vec{\mathcal{B}}
,

\displaystyle\langle B\gamma|\,J_{\mu}A^{\mu}\,|B\rangle_{\vec{\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle-\frac{e\,G_{M}(0)\,\vec{S}}{M_{B}}\cdot(i\vec{q}\times\vec{\epsilon}_{\gamma}^{*})

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{e\,G_{M}(0)\,\vec{S}}{M_{B}}\cdot\vec{\mathcal{B}}.

Comparing it with the magnetic moment energy
-\vec{\mu}_{B}\cdot\vec{\mathcal{B}}
, one obtains

\vec{\mu}_{B}=\frac{e\,G_{M}(0)\,\vec{S}}{M_{B}}.

(4)

We can obtain the magnetic moment by directly calculating the matrix element at quark level within the constituent quark model. Take
\mu_{\Sigma_{c}^{++}}
as an example. One can give the wave function of
\Sigma_{c}^{++}
with
S_{z}=+\frac{1}{2}
in flavor-spin space where
z
axis is chosen along the
\vec{\mathcal{B}}
direction for convenience,

|\Sigma_{c}^{++}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}|u_{\uparrow}u_{\uparrow}c_{\downarrow}\rangle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}|u_{\uparrow}u_{\downarrow}c_{\uparrow}\rangle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}|u_{\downarrow}u_{\uparrow}c_{\uparrow}\rangle,

(5)

and the electromagnetic current becomes
J_{\mu}=Q_{u}\bar{u}\gamma_{\mu}u+Q_{d}\bar{d}\gamma_{\mu}d+...
explicitly. From the
\langle\Sigma_{c}^{++}\gamma|J_{\mu}A^{\mu}|\Sigma_{c}^{++}\rangle_{\vec{\mathcal{B}}}
at the quark level,

\displaystyle\langle\Sigma_{c}^{++}\gamma|J_{\mu}A^{\mu}|\Sigma_{c}^{++}\rangle_{\vec{\mathcal{B}}}

(6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3}\langle u_{\uparrow}u_{\uparrow}c_{\downarrow}\gamma|J_{\mu}A^{\mu}|u_{\uparrow}u_{\uparrow}c_{\downarrow}\rangle_{\vec{\mathcal{B}}}+\frac{1}{6}\langle u_{\uparrow}u_{\downarrow}c_{\uparrow}\gamma|J_{\mu}A^{\mu}|u_{\uparrow}u_{\downarrow}c_{\uparrow}\rangle_{\vec{\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{6}\langle u_{\downarrow}u_{\uparrow}c_{\uparrow}\gamma|J_{\mu}A^{\mu}|u_{\downarrow}u_{\uparrow}c_{\uparrow}\rangle_{\vec{\mathcal{B}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{3}[\vec{\mu}_{u\uparrow}+\vec{\mu}_{u\uparrow}+\vec{\mu}_{c\downarrow}]\cdot\vec{\mathcal{B}}-\frac{1}{6}[\vec{\mu}_{u\uparrow}+\vec{\mu}_{u\downarrow}+\vec{\mu}_{c\uparrow}]\cdot\vec{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{6}[\vec{\mu}_{u\downarrow}+\vec{\mu}_{u\uparrow}+\vec{\mu}_{c\uparrow}]\cdot\vec{\mathcal{B}}

we obtain

\mu_{\Sigma_{c}^{++}}=\frac{4}{3}\mu_{u}-\frac{1}{3}\mu_{c}.

(7)

Here, we neglect the anomaly magnetic moments of quarks,
\mu_{q}=-\mu_{\bar{q}}\approx\frac{Q_{q}}{2M_{q}}
.

II.3
Contributions from
D
waves

First we write the wave function of
P_{c}(4457)
with the contents of the
D
waves explicitly

\displaystyle|P_{c}(4457)\rangle

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle|\bar{D}^{*}\Sigma_{c},I=\frac{1}{2},J=\frac{3}{2}\rangle\,\otimes\,Y_{00}(\Omega)\,R_{S1}(r)

(13)

\displaystyle+|\bar{D}^{*}\Sigma_{c}^{*},I=\frac{1}{2},J=\frac{3}{2}\rangle\otimes Y_{00}(\Omega)\,R_{S2}(r)

\displaystyle+|\bar{D}^{*}\Sigma_{c},I=\frac{1}{2}\rangle\,\otimes|{}^{2}D_{3/2}\rangle\times R_{D1}(r)

\displaystyle+|\bar{D}^{*}\Sigma_{c}^{*},I=\frac{1}{2}\rangle\,\otimes|{}^{2}D^{\prime}_{3/2}\rangle\times R_{D2}(r)

\displaystyle+|\bar{D}^{*}\Sigma_{c},I=\frac{1}{2}\rangle\,\otimes|{}^{4}D_{3/2}\rangle\times R_{D3}(r)

\displaystyle+|\bar{D}^{*}\Sigma_{c}^{*},I=\frac{1}{2}\rangle\,\otimes|{}^{4}D^{\prime}_{3/2}\rangle\times R_{D4}(r)

\displaystyle+|\bar{D}^{*}\Sigma_{c}^{*},I=\frac{1}{2}\rangle\,\otimes|{}^{6}D^{\prime}_{3/2}\rangle\times R_{D5}(r),

where
|{}^{2S+1}L_{J}\rangle
can be constructed with the help of the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients, for example, for an upward
P_{c}(4457)
,

|{}^{2}D_{3/2}\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{4}{5}}|S_{\bar{D}^{*}\Sigma_{c}}\!\!=\!\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2}\rangle\times Y_{2,2}-\sqrt{\frac{1}{5}}|S_{\bar{D}^{*}\Sigma_{c}}\!\!=\!\frac{1}{2},+\frac{1}{2}\rangle\times Y_{2,1}.

(14)

Now the magnetic moment of
P_{c}(4457)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mu_{P_{c}(4457)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\mu_{Si}\int{\rm d}r\,r^{2}|R_{Si}|^{2}+\sum_{i\neq j}\mu_{Si\to Sj}\int{\rm d}r\,r^{2}R_{Si}R_{Sj}^{\dagger}

(15)

\displaystyle\hskip-40.00006pt+\sum_{i}\mu_{Di}\int{\rm d}r\,r^{2}|R_{Di}|^{2}+\sum_{i\neq j}\mu_{Di\to Dj}\int{\rm d}r\,r^{2}R_{Di}R_{Dj}^{\dagger}.

Please notice there is no transition magnetic moment between
S
and
D
waves since there is a selection rule
\Delta L=0
for the magnetic dipole transition due to the conservation of angular momentum and parity.

The magnetic moments
\mu_{Di}
and
\mu_{Di\to Dj}
from
D
-wave contributions contain two parts. One is from spin contributions, which can be obtained as in previous subsections. The other is contributed from the orbital angular momenta, and can be expressed as

\vec{\mu}^{L}_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}=\mu_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}^{\rm orbital}\vec{L},

(16)

where

\mu_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}^{\rm orbital}\equiv\frac{M_{\alpha_{1}}}{M_{\alpha_{1}}+M_{\alpha_{2}}}\frac{Q_{\alpha_{2}}}{2M_{\alpha_{2}}}+\frac{M_{\alpha_{2}}}{M_{\alpha_{1}}+M_{\alpha_{2}}}\frac{Q_{\alpha_{1}}}{2M_{\alpha_{1}}}.

(17)

Now we can obtain expressions for the magnetic moments
\mu_{Di}
and
\mu_{Di\to Dj}
from
D
-wave contributions. Take
\mu_{D1}
as an example.
\mu_{D1}
is contributed by the third line of Eq. (

\mu_{D1}=\frac{4}{5}(-\mu_{S^{\prime}}+2\mu_{\bar{D}^{*}\Sigma_{c}}^{\rm orbital})+\frac{1}{5}(\mu_{S^{\prime}}+\mu_{\bar{D}^{*}\Sigma_{c}}^{\rm orbital}),

(18)

where
\mu_{S^{\prime}}
is short for the magnetic moment of the
S
-wave state
|\bar{D}^{*}\Sigma_{c},I=\frac{1}{2},J=\frac{1}{2}\rangle
which only has the spin contribution.

P_{c}(4457)

\displaystyle\mu_{P_{c}(4457)^{+}}=1.153~\mu_{N},

\displaystyle\mu_{P_{c}(4457)^{0}}=0.093~\mu_{N}.

(19)

\mu_{P_{c}(4312)}
and

With the similar approach in Sec.

P_{c}

II
. We show the results of two different scenarios in Table

. One is without considering coupled channel effects and
D

. The other is with coupled channel effects and

. We show the results in Table

. The coupled channel effects make

change by about
0.9~\mu_{N}
.

Furthermore, we can study the decay width
P_{c}^{\prime}\to P_{c}\gamma
with the values of the transition magnetic moments. Of course,
P_{c}^{\prime}\to P_{c}\gamma
contains two kinds of contribution: magnetic dipole and electric quadrupole transitions. However, the quadrupole contribution would be suppressed by a factor
[(M_{P_{c}^{\prime}}-M_{P_{c}})/M_{P_{c}}]^{2}
compared with the magnetic dipole decay width. Therefore, we neglect the electric quardropole contributions.

The decay width is related to the transition magnetic moments as in Ref.
88 88

\Gamma_{P_{c}^{\prime}\to P_{c}\gamma}=\alpha_{EM}\frac{2}{2J+1}\frac{E_{\gamma}^{3}}{m_{N}^{2}}\left(\frac{\mu_{P_{c}^{\prime}\to P_{c}}}{\mu_{N}}\right)^{2}.

(22)

where
\alpha_{EM}
is electromagnetic fine-structure constant,
J
is initial baryon spin and
E_{\gamma}
is the energy of photon. The decay width is also listed in Table
5
.

From Table
5
,
\Gamma(P_{c}(4457)\to P_{c}(4440)\gamma)
is very small, which is because the the mass difference is tiny and thus the kinetic phase space is suppressed. The decay widths of the charged channels
P_{c}(4440)^{+}\to P_{c}(4312)^{+}\gamma)
and
P_{c}(4457)^{+}\to P_{c}(4312)^{+}\gamma)
are about 1 keV.

IV
Summary

Inspired by the recently observed
P_{c}(4312)^{+}
,
P_{c}(4440)^{+}
, and
P_{c}(4457)^{+}
as well as the
P_{cs}(4459)^{0}
, we calculate the magnetic moments, the transition magnetic moments, and the electromagnetic transition decay widths of these particles.
Firstly we consider these particles as pure molecular states without flavor mixing. Secondly we take into account the coupled channel effects and the
D
-wave contribution step by step.

The decay width of
P_{c}(4457)\to P_{c}(4312)
is also studied in the molecular picture in Ref.
29 29
. A hadronic triangle loop is introduced between the initial and final states. The results rely on the
P_{c}
-
\bar{D}^{(*)}
-
\Sigma^{(*)}
couplings which can be extracted from the binding energy of
P_{c}
states. They estimated the decay width of
P_{c}(4457)\to P_{c}(4312)\gamma
is around 1.5 keV, which is consistent with the results in this work.

The magnetic moments of
P_{c}
states are studied within the quark model in Ref.
76 76
. Without considering the coupled channel effects and
D
-wave contribution, the results for the negative-parity molecules are
\mu_{P_{c}(4312)^{+}}=1.760~\mu_{N}
,
\mu_{P_{c}(4440)^{+}}=-0.856~\mu_{N}
, and
\mu_{P_{c}(4457)^{+}}=1.357~\mu_{N}

76 76
, which are very close to ours.

With
P_{c}(4312)
as a
\bar{D}\Sigma_{c}
molecular state,
\mu_{P_{c}(4312)^{+}}=1.75_{-0.11}^{+0.15}~\mu_{N}
is given by QCD sum rules
80 80
. U. Özdem uses light-cone QCD sum rules and obtains
\mu_{P_{c}(4312)^{+}}=1.98\pm 0.75~\mu_{N}
with the molecule interpolating current while
\mu_{P_{c}(4312)^{+}}=0.40\pm 0.15~\mu_{N}
with the diquark-diquark-antiquark configuration
78 78
, which clearly shows the magnetic moment strongly depends on the structure of the hadron. It seems that the QCD sum rule results with the molecule assumptions are closer to that without considering the coupled channel effects and
D
-wave contribution in this work. We expect
\mu_{P_{c}(4312)^{+}}
might also decrease a little after studying the mixing effects of different meson-baryon interpolating currents within QCD sum rule.

The authors in Refs.
77 77
;
79 79
also obtain
\mu_{P_{c}(4380)^{+}}
,
\mu_{P_{c}(4457)^{+}}
,
\mu_{P_{c}(4440)^{+}}
, and
\mu_{P_{cs}(4459)^{0}}
with the molecule assumption within QCD sum rules. However, we cannot directly compare their results with ours because they construct
P_{c}
/
P_{cs}
molecules with different meson-baryon combinations from this work. In Ref.
81 81
, the magnetic moments of four pentaquark states with
J^{P}=\frac{3}{2}^{-}
are about 1.1
\sim
3.1
\mu_{N}
, but all masses of these four pentaquark states are lower than those of the observed
P_{c}
states.

We find that the coupled channel effects and the
D
-wave inclusions contribute to about 0.1
\sim
0.4
\mu_{N}
for most cases and less than 0.03
\mu_{N}
, respectively, which is not unexpected since these two elements are not dominant in forming the molecular structures of the corresponding states. The
D
-wave contributions of
P_{c}
states are very similar to that of the deuteron. Because the spin of
P_{cs}(4459)^{0}
has not been experimentally known yet due to lack of data, the magnetic moments of
P_{cs}(4459)^{0}
are obtained with the spin of
1/2
or
3/2
.

The
P_{c}
and
P_{cs}
electromagnetic properties are closely related to their structure and the
\bar{D}^{(*)}
-
\Sigma_{c}^{(*)}
/
\Xi_{c}^{(*)}
strong interactions, and thus the study will help us understand the nonperturbative behaviors of QCD from a different aspect. Hopefully, our investigation may attract the lattice QCD simulations and experiment plans in future.

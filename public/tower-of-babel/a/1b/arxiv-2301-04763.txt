Behaviors of pairs of dimensions and depths of edge ideals
Akihiro Higashitani, Akane Kanno, Ryota Ueji

Abstract
. Edge ideals of finite simple graphs G G on n n vertices are the ideals I ⁡ ( G ) I(G) of the polynomial ring S S in n n variables generated by the quadratic monomials associated with the edges of G G . In this paper, we consider the possible pairs of dimensions and depths of S / I ⁡ ( G ) S/I(G) for connected graphs with a fixed number of vertices. We discuss such pairs in the case where dimension is relatively large. As a corollary, we completely determine the pairs for connected graphs with small number of vertices. We also study the possible pairs for connected chordal graphs.

1.
Introduction

Monomial ideals are one of the most well-studied objects in the area of combinatorial commutative algebra.
Especially, edge ideals of graphs are of particular interest in this area.
On the other hand, dimensions and depths are fundamental invariants on graded (or local) rings.
The goal of this paper is the investigation of the behaviors of pairs of dimensions and depths for the quotient rings by edge ideals.

Throughout the present paper, we only treat finite simple graphs, so we omit “finite simple”.
Let
\operatorname{Graph}(n)
denote the set of all connected graphs on the vertex set
[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.
Let
S=\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
\Bbbk
.
The
edge ideal

I(G)
of
G\in\operatorname{Graph}(n)
is defined by
I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}:\{i,j\}\in E(G))\subset S
.
Invariants on edge ideals have been well studied in combinatorial commutative algebra.
Especially, the graded Betti numbers of the edge ideals of graphs have been considered in various viewpoints.
Since the (Castelnuovo–Mumford) regularity and the projective dimension of edge ideals can be obtained from the information on Betti numbers,
we can also obtain some characterizations or bounds on them. For example, see
[
3
,
4
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
.
Note that the depths of edge ideals and the projective dimensions are equivalent invariants in some sense (see Subsection
2.3
).
For more information on edge ideals, see, e.g.,
[
5
, Section 9]
.

In this paper, we focus on the relationship between dimensions and depths of edge ideals for connected graphs.
The study of their relationship is initiated in
[
6
, Section 2]
as far as the authors know.
Let us briefly recall what is obtained there. Given a positive integer
n
, let

\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n):=\left\{(\operatorname{depth}S/I(G),\dim S/I(G)):G\in\operatorname{Graph}(n)\right\},

where
\dim G
(resp.
\operatorname{depth}G
) denotes the dimension (resp. depth) of
S/I(G)
.
On the other hand, let

C^{*}(n):=\left\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:1\leq a\leq b\leq n-1,\;a\leq b+1-\left\lceil\frac{b}{n-b}\right\rceil\right\}.

Then the following is proved:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
6
, Theorem 2.8]
)
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Then we have
C^{*}(n)\subset\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)
.

By taking this theorem into account, the following question naturally arises:

Question 1.2
.

Does the equality
C^{*}(n)=\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)
hold?

In
[
6
, Example 2.2]
, it is mentioned that
\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)
is computed by using computer.
As a consequence of the computations, we can verify that
C^{*}(n)=\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)
holds when
n\leq 9
.

In this paper, we develop more theoretical considerations.
The first main result of this paper is the following:

Theorem 1.3

(See Remark
2.3
, Propositions
3.2
and
3.3
)
.

Let
(a,b)\in\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)
. If
b\geq n-3
, then
(a,b)\in C^{*}(n)
.

This theorem enables us to show the equality
C^{*}(n)=\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)
when
n
is relatively small as follows:

Corollary 1.4
.

The equality
C^{*}(n)=\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)
holds if
n\leq 12
.

For a graph
G
, we say that
G
is
chordal
if any cycle in
G
of length at least
4
has a chord.
Let

\displaystyle\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}^{\mathrm{chordal}}(n)=\left\{(\operatorname{depth}S/I(G),\dim S/I(G)):G\in\operatorname{Graph}(n),\;\text{$G$ is chordal}\right\}.

Namely, we restrict
\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)
into chordal graphs.

The second main result of this paper is the following:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Then we have
C^{*}(n)=\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}^{\mathrm{chordal}}(n)
.

Note that in
[
6
, Section 3]
, the possible pairs of dimensions and depths of the edge ideals of Cameron–Walker graphs are determined.
See
[
6
, Theorem 3.15]
. We can observe that such pairs for Cameron–Walker graphs are quite small compared with
C^{*}(n)
.

A brief structure of this paper is as follows.
In Section
2
, we recall some fundamental materials used for the proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
.
In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
1.4
.
In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.5
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we prepare some terminologies, notions and statements for the proofs of our main theorems.

2.1.
Terminologies on graphs

Let
G
be a graph on the vertex set
V(G)
with the edge set
E(G)
.

•

We call a subset
W
of
V(G)
an
independent set
(resp.
clique
) if
\{v,w\}\not\in E(G)
(resp.
\{v,w\}\in E(G)
) for any
v,w\in W
. Let

\displaystyle\alpha(G)=\max\{|W|:W\text{ is an independent set of }G\},

called the
independence number
of
G
.
It is well known that

\dim S/I(G)=\alpha(G).

•

Let
M\subset E(G)
be a subset of the edge set. We say that
M
is an
induced matching
of
G
if
M
satisfies that

–

e\cap e^{\prime}=\emptyset
for any
e,e^{\prime}\in M
with
e\neq e^{\prime}
; and

–

there is no
e^{\prime\prime}\in E(G)
such that
e\cap e^{\prime\prime}\neq\emptyset
and
e^{\prime}\cap e^{\prime\prime}\neq\emptyset
.

•

For a vertex
v
of
G
, let
N(v)=\{w\in V:\{v,w\}\in E(G)\}
, let
N[v]=N(v)\cup\{v\}
and let
\operatorname{deg}v=|N(v)|
.

•

For a vertex
v
of
G
, let
G\setminus v
denote the induced subgraph of
G
by
V(G)\setminus\{v\}
.
Similarly, for
U\subset V(G)
, let
G\setminus U
denote the induced subgraph of
G
by
V(G)\setminus U
.

•

We say that
C\subset V(G)
is a
vertex cover
if
C\cap e\neq\emptyset
for any
e\in E(G)
.
We call a vertex cover
C

minimal
if
C\setminus\{v\}
is not a vertex cover of
G
for any
v\in C
.

•

A
perfect elimination ordering
of
G
is an ordering
v_{n},\ldots,v_{1}
of the vertices of
G
such that

\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k-1}\}\cap N(v_{k})
forms a clique for each
k=2,\ldots,n
.
It is known that
G
is chordal if and only if
G
has a perfect elimination ordering (
[
2
]
).

In this paper, given a graph
G
, we use the notation
\dim G
,
\operatorname{depth}G
and
\operatorname{pdim}G

instead of
\dim S/I(G)
,
\operatorname{depth}S/I(G)
and
\operatorname{pdim}S/I(G)
for short, respectively, where
\operatorname{pdim}
denotes the projective dimension.

2.2.
Bouquets and projective dimensions of
S/I(G)

We recall the terminology and the notation used in
[
9
]
.
The graph
B
on the vertex set
V(B)=\{w,z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}\}
with the edge set
\{\{w,z_{i}\}:i=1,\ldots,d\}
is called a
bouquet
.
Namely, a bouquet is nothing but a star graph.
A vertex
w
is called a
root
, the vertices
z_{i}
are called
flowers
and the edges
\{w,z_{i}\}
are called
stems
of
B
.
These words were originally used in
[
10
, Definition 1.7]
.

Given a graph
G
, we call
B
a bouquet of
G
if
B
is a (not necessarily induced) subgraph of
G
.
Let
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{p}\}
be a set of bouquets of
G
. We define the following:

\displaystyle F(\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}\{z\in V(B_{i}):z\text{ is a flower of }B_{i}\};

\displaystyle R(\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}\{z\in V(B_{i}):z\text{ is a root of }B_{i}\};

\displaystyle S(\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}\{s\in E(B_{i}):s\text{ is a stem of }B_{i}\}.

Let
G
be a graph and let
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{p}\}
be a set of bouquets of
G
.
Assume that
V(B_{i})\cap V(B_{j})=\emptyset
holds for any
1\leq i<j\leq p
.

•

We say that
\mathcal{B}
is
strongly disjoint

if there are stems
s_{i}\in E(B_{i})
for each
i
such that
\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{p}\}
forms an induced matching of
G
.

•

We say that
\mathcal{B}
is
semi-strongly disjoint
if
R(\mathcal{B})
forms an independent set.

Note that if
\mathcal{B}
is strongly disjoint, since any stem in
B_{i}
is incident to the root of
B_{i}
,
we see that any two roots should not be adjacent. Namely,
\mathcal{B}
becomes semi-strongly disjoint.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
9
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Given any graph
G
, we have

\operatorname{pdim}G\geq\max\{|F(\mathcal{B})|:\mathcal{B}\text{ is a strongly disjoint set of bouquets of }G\}.

By Theorem
2.1
, we see that given a graph
G
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{deg}v\leq\operatorname{pdim}G\text{ for any }u\in V(G)

(2.1)

since
N(u)\cup\{u\}
forms a (set of a single) bouquet with a root
u
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
9
, Theorem 5.3, Corollary 5.6]
)
.

Let
G
be chordal. Then we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{pdim}G

\displaystyle=\max\{|F(\mathcal{B})|:\mathcal{B}\text{ is a semi-strongly disjoint set of bouquets of }G\}

\displaystyle=\max\{|C|:C\text{ is a minimal vertex cover of }G\}.

2.3.
Auslander–Buchsbaum formula

Let
G
be a graph on
[n]
. Then
S/I(G)
is an
S
-module. Since
S
is a polynomial ring,
\operatorname{pdim}G<\infty
holds.
Hence, by the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula (see, e.g.,
[
1
, Theorem 1.3.3]
), we have

\operatorname{pdim}G+\operatorname{depth}G=n.

We will often use this in the sequel.

2.4.
On the sets
C^{*}(n)
and
C^{-}(n)

Let us recall the set
C^{-}(n)
defined in
[
6
, Section 2]
. For
n\geq 2
, let

C^{-}(n):=\{(1,n-1)\}\cup\left\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:1\leq a\leq b,\;a\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor,\;b\leq n-2\right\}.

Then, by the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 2.9]
, we see that
C^{-}(n)\subset C^{*}(n)
.
The difference of
C^{-}(n)
and
C^{*}(n)
will be mentioned below. See Example
3.5
.

Remark 2.3

(cf.
[
6
, Proposition 1.3]
)
.

Let
G\in\operatorname{Graph}(n)
and assume that
\alpha(G)=n-1
.
Then
G
itself must be a bouquet. In particular,
G
is chordal. Thus, by Theorem
2.2
, we have
\operatorname{pdim}G=n-1
. Hence,
\operatorname{depth}G=1
.
These imply that if
(a,n-1)\in\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)
, then
a=1
. In particular,
(1,n-1)\in C^{-}(n)\subset C^{*}(n)
.

Remark 2.4
.

Given integers
a,b
with
1\leq a\leq b\leq n-1
, assume that
a+b\leq n
.
Then
(a,b)\in C^{-}
automatically holds. In fact, if
b=n-1
, then
a=1
; if
b\leq n-2
, since
2a\leq a+b\leq n
holds, we have
a\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor
.

3.
In the case of graphs with large independence number

In this section, we consider the inclusion
\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)\subset C^{*}(n)
for some special cases.
In particular, we discuss when
(a,b)\in\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)
belongs to
C^{*}(n)
.

First, we discuss the case where
a=b
, i.e., the corresponding graph is Cohen–Macaulay.

Proposition 3.1

(cf.
[
3
, Theorem 3.3]
)
.

Assume that
(b,b)\in\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)
. Then
(b,b)\in C^{*}(n)
.

Proof.

We see that
(b,b)\in C^{*}(n)
holds if and only if
\lceil b/(n-b)\rceil\leq 1
holds.
Here, it follows from
[
3
, Theorem 3.3]
that if
G
is Cohen–Macaulay, then
b\leq n/2
.
This implies that when
G
is Cohen–Macaulay with
b=\dim G
, we have
b\leq n/2
, i.e.,
(b,b)\in C^{*}(n)
.
∎

Next, we discuss the case where
b
is relatively large.
Note that we always have
1\leq b\leq n-1
and the case
b=n-1
has been already discussed in Remark
2.3
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
n\geq 4
. If
(a,n-2)\not\in C^{*}(n)
, then
(a,n-2)\not\in\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)
.

Proof.

Let

f(a,b):=b+1-\left\lceil\frac{b}{n-b}\right\rceil-a.

Note that for integers
a,b
with
1\leq a\leq b\leq n-1
,
f(a,b)\geq 0
holds if and only if
(a,b)\in C^{*}(n)
.

In what follows, we lead a contradiction if there exists a graph
G\in\operatorname{Graph}(n)
with
(\operatorname{depth}G,\dim G)=(a,n-2)
such that
f(a,n-2)<0
.

Since
f(a,n-2)=\lfloor n/2\rfloor-a
, we have
\lfloor n/2\rfloor<\operatorname{depth}G=n-\operatorname{pdim}G
, i.e.,
\operatorname{pdim}G<\lceil n/2\rceil
.
On the other hand, since
\dim G=n-2
, there is an independent set
W
of
G
with
|W|=n-2
.
Let
v_{1},v_{2}
be the remaining vertices, i.e.,
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}=V(G)\setminus W
.
Since
G
is connected and
W
is an independent set, we see that

\displaystyle n-2

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{w\in W}\operatorname{deg}w=|N(v_{1})\cap W|+|N(v_{2})\cap W|

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\operatorname{deg}v_{1}+\operatorname{deg}v_{2}-2,&\text{ if }\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\in E(G),\\
\operatorname{deg}v_{1}+\operatorname{deg}v_{2},&\text{ if }\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\not\in E(G).\end{cases}

On the other hand, since
\operatorname{pdim}G<\lceil n/2\rceil
, we see that
\operatorname{deg}v_{i}<\lceil n/2\rceil
for
i=1,2
by (
2.1
).
If
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\in E(G)
, then we see that

n-2\leq\operatorname{deg}v_{1}+\operatorname{deg}v_{2}-2\leq\frac{n-1}{2}+\frac{n-1}{2}-2=n-3,

a contradiction.
Hence,
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\not\in E(G)
.

Suppose that
n
is even. Then we see that

n-2\leq\operatorname{deg}v_{1}+\operatorname{deg}v_{2}\leq\frac{n-2}{2}+\frac{n-2}{2}=n-2,

i.e., we have
\operatorname{deg}w=1
for each
w\in W
. Since
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\not\in E(G)
, we see that there is no path in
G
connecting
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
,
a contradiction to the connectedness of
G
.
Hence,
n
should be odd. Moreover, there should be at least one vertex
w_{0}
in
W
with
\operatorname{deg}w_{0}\geq 2
.
Since
\sum_{w\in W}\operatorname{deg}w\geq n-1
and
\operatorname{deg}v_{i}\leq(n-1)/2
hold, we conclude that
G
should look like as follows:

Note that
\operatorname{deg}v_{1}=\operatorname{deg}v_{2}=(n-1)/2
.
In particular,

and
v_{2}
,
we see from Theorem

that

\left\lceil\frac{n}{2}\right\rceil>\operatorname{pdim}G\geq n-2,

a contradiction to
n\geq 4
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
n\geq 8
. If
(a,n-3)\not\in C^{*}(n)

(a,n-3)\not\in\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)

Let

Similarly, for any strongly disjoint set

such that
\{B_{j},B_{k}\}
is strongly disjoint.
In fact, if not, then either
F(B_{j})\subset F(B_{k})
or
F(B_{k})\subset F(B_{j})
holds for each
1\leq j<k\leq 3
.
This implies that we have
F(B_{1})\subset F(B_{2})\subset F(B_{3})
after properly changing the indices of
B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}
.
In particular,
F(B_{3})=W
. Thus,
n-3=\operatorname{deg}v_{3}\leq\lceil n/3\rceil\leq(n+2)/3
, i.e.,
n\leq 5
, a contradiction.

Let, say,
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},B_{2}\}
be a strongly disjoint set of bouquets of
G
.

•

Suppose that
|F(\mathcal{B})|\geq\lfloor 2n/3\rfloor-1
. Then we see that

\left\lceil\frac{n}{3}\right\rceil\geq|F(\mathcal{B})|\geq\left\lfloor\frac{2n}{3}\right\rfloor-1\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{n+2}{3}\geq\frac{2n-2}{3}-1\;\Longleftrightarrow\;7\geq n,

a contradiction.

•

Suppose that
|F(\mathcal{B})|\leq\lfloor 2n/3\rfloor-2
. Since
|W|=|F(\mathcal{B})|+|F(B_{3})|=n-3
and
|F(B_{3})|\leq\operatorname{deg}v_{3}-1

by the connectedness of
G
, we see that

\left\lceil\frac{n}{3}\right\rceil-1\geq\operatorname{deg}v_{3}-1\geq|F(B_{3})|\geq n-3-\left\lfloor\frac{2n}{3}\right\rfloor+2=\left\lceil\frac{n}{3}\right\rceil-1.

Hence, all the equalities of these inequalities are satisfied. In particular, we have
|F(B_{3})|=\operatorname{deg}v_{3}-1=\lceil n/3\rceil-1
.
Since
n-3>\lceil n/3\rceil
and
G
is connected, there is
w\in W\setminus N(v_{3})
adjacent to
v_{1}
or
v_{2}
, say,
v_{1}
.
Then we can construct a new strongly disjoint set of bouquets
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
consisting of bouquets
N(v_{3})\cup\{v_{3}\}
and
\{v_{1},w\}

such that
|F(\mathcal{B}^{\prime})|=\lceil n/3\rceil+1
, a contradiction to (
3.2
).

∎

By using Propositions
3.1
,
3.2
and
3.3
, we can prove Corollary
1.4
.

Proof of Corolalry
1.4
.

It is enough to show that
\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)\subset C^{*}(n)
for
n\leq 12
.

Take
(a,b)\in\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)
arbitrarily. Then the inequalities
1\leq a\leq b\leq n-1
automatically hold.

•

If
a=b
, then we see that
(a,b)\in C^{*}(n)
by Proposition
3.1
.

•

The case of
b=n-1
has been already discussed in Remark
2.3
.

Hence, in what follows, we assume that
1\leq a<b\leq n-2
.
Here, we consider the set

C^{\prime}(n):=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:1\leq a<b\leq n-2\}\setminus C^{*}(n).

Then the direct computations show the following:

\displaystyle C^{\prime}(n)=\emptyset\text{ for }2\leq n\leq 6,\;\;C^{\prime}(7)=\{(4,5)\},\;\;C^{\prime}(8)=\{(5,6)\},\;\;C^{\prime}(9)=\{(5,7),(6,7)\},

\displaystyle C^{\prime}(10)=\{(6,7),(6,8),(7,8)\},\;\;C^{\prime}(11)=\{(6,9),(7,8),(7,9),(8,9)\},

\displaystyle C^{\prime}(12)=\{(7,10),(8,9),(8,10),(9,10)\}.

Let
n\leq 12
.
Suppose that
(a,b)\not\in C^{*}(n)
. Then
(a,b)\in C^{\prime}(n)
.
By the above computations, we see that all elements of
\bigcup_{n=2}^{12}C^{\prime}(n)
satisfy
b=n-2
or
b=n-3
.
Hence, it follows from Propositions
3.2
and
3.3
that
(a,b)\not\in\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)
, a contradiction.

Therefore,
C^{*}(n)=\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)
holds if
n\leq 12
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

We see that

C^{\prime}(13)=\{(7,11),(8,9),(8,10),(8,11),(9,10),(9,11),(10,11)\}.

Since we cannot claim that
(8,9)\not\in\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(13)
only by Propositions
3.2
and
3.3
,
the above proof of Corollary
1.4
is not available if
n=13
.
Instead, we can claim that
\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(13)=C^{*}(13)
or
C^{*}(13)\cup\{(8,9)\}
.

Example 3.5
.

For small
n
’s, the direct computations show the following:

\displaystyle C^{*}(n)

\displaystyle=C^{-}(n)\text{ for }n=2,3,\ldots,8,10,

\displaystyle C^{*}(9)

\displaystyle=C^{-}(9)\cup\{(5,6)\},

\displaystyle C^{*}(11)

\displaystyle=C^{-}(11)\cup\{(6,6),(6,7),(6,8)\},

\displaystyle C^{*}(12)

\displaystyle=C^{-}(12)\cup\{(7,8),(7,9),(8,9)\}.

Note that
[
6
, Example 2.11]
also mentions the similar equalities in the case

.

4.
In the case of chordal graphs

In this section, we discuss the behavior of pairs of dimensions and depths of edge ideals of chordal graphs.

4.1.
The inclusion
C^{*}(n)\subset\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}^{\mathrm{chordal}}(n)

First, we discuss the inclusion
C^{*}(n)\subset\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}^{\mathrm{chordal}}(n)
.
Here, we remark that since we only know that
\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}^{\mathrm{chordal}}(n)\subset\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}(n)
by definition,
we cannot immediately claim
C^{*}(n)\subset\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}^{\mathrm{chordal}}(n)
from Theorem
1.1
.

Let
(a,b)\in C^{*}(n)
. We divide the discussions into two cases:

(i)

a+b>n
; or

(ii)

a+b\leq n
.

(i) In this case, we can apply the same proof as that of
[
6
, Theorem 2.8]
.
In that proof, the graphs
G(m;s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})
given in
[
6
, Construction 2.5]
play the essential role
and we see that
G(m;s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})
is chordal. Hence, we obtain the inclusion by the same discussion.

(ii) In this case, we cannot apply the same proof. In fact, Lemmas 2.3 and 2.4 are used for the proof in the case
a+b\leq n
.
Especially, Lemma 2.3 uses the technique of
S
-cone
(also known as
S
-suspension).
In general, the chordality is not preserved by taking
S
-cone. Hence, Lemma 2.3 is not immediately available for chordal graphs.

Instead, we directly prove the following:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
a,b,n
be integers satisfying
1\leq a\leq b\leq n-1
and
a+b\leq n
.
Then there exists a chordal graph
G\in\operatorname{Graph}(n)
such that
(\operatorname{depth}G,\dim G)=(a,b)
.
Namely, we have
(a,b)\in\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}^{\mathrm{chordal}}(n)
.

Note that the assumption
a+b\leq n
immediately implies
(a,b)\in C^{*}(n)
(see Remark
2.4
).

Proof.

Let
G
be the graph defined as follows:

\displaystyle V(G)

\displaystyle=U\sqcup W,\;\text{ where }\;U=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n-b}\}\text{ and }W=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{b}\};

\displaystyle E(G)

\displaystyle=\{\{u,u^{\prime}\}:u,u^{\prime}\in U\}\cup\{\{u_{i},w_{i}\}:1\leq i\leq a-1\}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\cup\{\{u_{j},w_{k}\}:a\leq j\leq n-b,\;a\leq k\leq b\}.

It is straightforward to check that the ordering
w_{1},\ldots,w_{b},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n-b}
becomes a perfect elimination ordering of
G
. Hence,
G
is chordal.
Moreover, we can easily see that
W
is an independent set with
|W|=\alpha(G)
. Hence,
\dim G=b
.

In what follows, we prove that
\operatorname{depth}G=a
, i.e.,
\operatorname{pdim}G=n-a
.

Regarding the minimal vertex covers of
G
, we observe that any minimal vertex cover contains at least
(n-b-1)
vertices of
U
since
U
forms a clique.
In particular, we see that
U
itself becomes a minimal vertex cover.
Fix
u_{j}\in U
and let
C
be a minimal vertex cover not containing
u_{j}
. Then
U\setminus\{u_{j}\}\subset C
.

•

If
1\leq j\leq a-1
, then
w_{j}\in C
, so
C=(U\setminus\{u_{j}\})\cup\{w_{j}\}
.

•

If
a\leq j\leq n-b
, then
w_{a},\ldots,w_{b}\in C
, so
C=(U\setminus\{u_{j}\})\cup\{w_{a},\ldots,w_{b}\}
.

Therefore, by Theorem
2.2
, we conclude that

\operatorname{pdim}G=\max\{|C|:C\text{ is a minimal vertex cover of }G\}=\max\{|U|,|U|+b-a\}=n-a.

∎

4.2.
The inclusion
\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}^{\mathrm{chordal}}(n)\subset C^{*}(n)

For the proof of this inclusion, we prepare the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

(1) For any vertex
v
of a graph
G
, we have

\dim G=\max\{\dim(G\setminus N[v])+1,\dim(G\setminus v)\}.

(2) Let
G
be a chordal graph and let
v_{n},\ldots,v_{1}
be a perfect elimination ordering of
G
. Then we have

\operatorname{depth}G=\min\{\operatorname{depth}(G\setminus N[v_{n}])+1,\operatorname{depth}(G\setminus v_{n})\}.

Proof.

(1) Let
W
be an independent set of
G
with
|W|=\alpha(G)
.
Then the required equality directly follows from the following observations:

•

If
v\in W
, then
W\setminus\{v\}\subset V(G\setminus N[v])
becomes an independent set of
G\setminus N[v]

with
|W\setminus\{v\}|=\alpha(G\setminus N[v])
.

•

If
v\not\in W
, then
W\subset V(G\setminus v)
becomes an independent set of
G\setminus v
with
|W|=\alpha(G\setminus v)
.

(2) We prove the equality by induction on
|V(G)|
.

Let
v=v_{n}
and
\operatorname{deg}v=m
. Let
C
be a minimal vertex cover of
G
with
|C|=\operatorname{pdim}G
(cf. Theorem
2.2
).

•

If
v\in C
, then
C^{\prime}=C\setminus\{v\}
becomes a minimal vertex cover of
G\setminus v
with
\operatorname{pdim}(G\setminus v)=|C^{\prime}|
.
Hence, by the hypothesis of induction, we see that

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}G

\displaystyle=|V(G)|-\operatorname{pdim}G=|V(G)|-(|C^{\prime}|+1)=|V(G\setminus v)|-|C^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=|V(G\setminus v)|-\operatorname{pdim}(G\setminus v)=\operatorname{depth}(G\setminus v).

•

If
v\not\in C
, since
N[v]
forms a clique by definition of perfect elimination ordering, we see that
N(v)\subset C
.
Thus,
C^{\prime\prime}=C\setminus N[v]
becomes a minimal vertex cover of
G\setminus N[v]
with
\operatorname{pdim}(G\setminus N[v])=|C^{\prime\prime}|
.
Hence, we see that

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}G

\displaystyle=|V(G)|-\operatorname{pdim}G=|V(G)|-(|C^{\prime\prime}|+m)=(|V(G\setminus N[v])|+1)-|C^{\prime\prime}|

\displaystyle=|V(G\setminus N[v])|-\operatorname{pdim}(G\setminus N[v])+1=\operatorname{depth}(G\setminus N[v])+1.

Here, Theorem
2.2
says that if
C_{0}
is a minimal vertex cover with
|C_{0}|\neq\operatorname{pdim}G
, then
|C_{0}|<\operatorname{pdim}G
.
Thus, the required equality follows.
∎

Now, we prove the inclusion
\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}^{\mathrm{chordal}}(n)\subset C^{*}(n)
. Take
(a,b)\in\operatorname{Graph}_{\operatorname{depth},\dim}^{\mathrm{chordal}}(n)
arbitrarily.
Then there is a chordal graph
G
such that
(\operatorname{depth}G,\dim G)=(a,b)
.
To prove
(a,b)\in C^{*}(n)
, it is enough to show that

\displaystyle b\leq(n-b)(b-a+1).

(4.1)

In fact, (
4.1
) is equivalent to the following:

\displaystyle\frac{b}{n-b}\leq b-a+1\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\left\lceil\frac{b}{n-b}\right\rceil\leq b-a+1\;\Longleftrightarrow\;a\leq b+1-\left\lceil\frac{b}{n-b}\right\rceil.

This means that
(a,b)\in C^{*}(n)
.

Let
v_{n},\ldots,v_{1}
be a perfect elimination ordering of
G
. We prove the inequality (
4.1
) by induction on
n
.

Let
v=v_{n}
and let

a^{\prime}=\operatorname{depth}(G\setminus v),\;\;b^{\prime}=\dim(G\setminus v),\;\;a^{\prime\prime}=\operatorname{depth}(G\setminus N[v])\text{ and }b^{\prime\prime}=\dim(G\setminus N[v]).

By Lemma
4.2
(1), either
b=b^{\prime}
or
b=b^{\prime\prime}+1
holds.

•

Assume that
b=b^{\prime}
. Since
a\leq a^{\prime}
holds by Lemma
4.2
(2), we see from the hypothesis of induction that

\displaystyle b=b^{\prime}\leq((n-1)-b^{\prime})(b^{\prime}-a^{\prime}+1)\leq(n-b)(b-a+1).

•

Assume that
b=b^{\prime\prime}+1
. Since
a\leq a^{\prime\prime}+1
, we see that

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=b^{\prime\prime}+1\leq((n-\operatorname{deg}v-1)-b^{\prime\prime})(b^{\prime\prime}-a^{\prime\prime}+1)+1

\displaystyle\leq(n-\operatorname{deg}v-b)(b-a+1)+1=(n-b)(b-a+1)-(\operatorname{deg}v(b-a+1)-1)

\displaystyle\leq(n-b)(b-a+1).

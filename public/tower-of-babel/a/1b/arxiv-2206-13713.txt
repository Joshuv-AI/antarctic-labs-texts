Strongly damped wave equations with mass-like terms of the logarithmic-Laplacian Thanks: Corresponding author: ikehatar@hiroshima-u.ac.jp
Alessandra Piske, Ruy Coimbra Charão

Abstract
We consider strongly damped wave equations with logarithmic mass-like terms with a parameter θ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \theta\in(0,1] . This research is a part of a series of wave equations that was initiated by Charão-Ikehata [ 6 ] , Charão-D’Abbicco-Ikehata considered in [ 5 ] depending on a parameter θ ∈ ( 1 / 2 , 1 ) \theta\in(1/2,1) and Piske-Charão-Ikehata [ 26 ] for small parameter θ ∈ ( 0 , 1 / 2 ) \theta\in(0,1/2) . We derive a leading term as t → ∞ t\to\infty of the solution, and by using it, a growth and a decay property of the solution itself can be precisely studied in terms of L 2 L^{2} -norm. An interesting aspect appears in the case of n = 1 n=1 , roughly speaking, a small θ \theta produces a diffusive property, and a large θ \theta gives a kind of singularity, expressed by growth rates.

\displaystyle u_{tt}-\Delta u+m^{2}L_{\theta}u-\Delta u_{t}=0,\quad t>0,\quad x\in{\bf R}^{n},

\displaystyle u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\quad x\in{\bf R}^{n},

(1.2)

[

[

We first define several function spaces related to the logarithmic Laplace operator

\displaystyle Y_{\theta}^{\delta}:=\{f\in L^{2}({\bf R}^{n}):\int_{{\bf R}_{\xi}^{n}}(1+\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta}))^{\delta}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi<\infty\}

Remark 1.1

Due to the fact that

The linear operator

L_{\theta}:D(L_{\theta})\subset L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})\to L^{2}({\bf R}^{n}),\quad\theta>0,

is defined as follows:

D(L_{\theta}):=\left\{f\in L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})\,\bigm|\,\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}(\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta}))^{2}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi<+\infty\right\}=Y_{\theta}^{2},

and for

(L_{\theta}f)(x):={\cal F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}\left(\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})\hat{f}(\xi)\right)(x).

Here, one has just denoted the Fourier transform

Since

Note that

We also note that

H^{s}({\bf R}^{n})\hookrightarrow Y_{\theta}^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})

for any

L_{\theta}=\log(I+(-\Delta)^{\theta}),

E_{u}(t)+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u_{s}(s,\cdot)\|^{2}ds=E_{u}(0),\quad t>0,

Under these previous developments it seems to be little investigations on the topic to observe the growth property (as

\log(I+(-\Delta)^{\theta})
on the decay structure of the equation. To catch information on the time behavior of the
L^{2}
-nom of the solution itself is very important to observe some singularity (near low frequency part) included in the solution itself to problem (1.1)-(1.2). Such a singularity reflects on the quantity of the function
t\mapsto\|u(t,\cdot)\|

Now, we introduce the asymptotic profile as
t\to\infty

\varphi(t,\xi)=P_{1}e^{-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{2}t}\frac{\sin\big(t\sqrt{|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}\big)}{\sqrt{|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}},

(1.9)

where the moment of the initial velocity
P_{1}\in{\bf R}
is defined by

P_{1}:=\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)dx.

Then, our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1

Let
n\geq 1
, and
0<\theta\leq 1
. Let
\varphi
be the function defined in (
1.9
). Choose
u_{0}=0
, and
u_{1}\in L^{1,\theta}({\bf R}^{n})\cap L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
. Then there exists a positive constant
C=C_{n,\delta_{0},\theta}
such that the mild solution
u\in C([0,\infty);H^{1}({\bf R}^{n}))\cap C^{1}([0,\infty);L^{2}({\bf R}^{n}))
to problem
(1.1)-(1.2)
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\hat{u}(t,\xi)-\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,\delta_{0},\theta}\Big(m^{-2}P^{2}_{1}t^{-\frac{n+8-6\theta}{2}}+m^{-2}P^{2}_{1}t^{-\frac{n+4-4\theta}{2}}+m^{-2}||u_{1}||^{2}_{1,\theta}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}+P_{1}^{2}e^{-\gamma t}

\displaystyle+||u_{1}||^{2}_{1}t^{2}e^{-\gamma t}+||u_{1}||^{2}_{1}t^{2}e^{-\alpha t}+||u_{1}||^{2}_{1}t^{2}e^{-\beta t}\Big),\quad t\geq 0,\quad

where
\alpha>0
,
\beta>0
and
\gamma>0
are generous constants.

As a reult of Theorem
1.1
, one can state the following optimal
L^{2}
-decay and/or growth estimates of the solution itself to problem (1.1)-(1.2)

Theorem 1.2

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
1.1
the following statements are true:

(i)

for
n=1
with
0<\theta<\frac{1}{2}
,
n=2
with
0<\theta<1
and
n\geq 3
with
0<\theta\leq 1
, there exist positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
depending on
n
,
\theta
and
m>0
such that

C_{1}|P_{1}|t^{-\frac{n-2\theta}{4}}\leq\|u(t,\cdot)\|\leq C_{2}\Big(\|u_{1}\|_{1}+\|u_{1}\|_{1,\theta}\Big)t^{-\frac{n-2\theta}{4}},\quad t\gg 1,

(ii)

for
n=1
and
\frac{1}{2}<\theta\leq 1
, there exist positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
such that

\frac{C_{1}}{(1+m^{2})^{\frac{1}{4\theta}}}|P_{1}|t^{\frac{2\theta-1}{2\theta}}\leq\|u(t,\cdot)\|\leq\frac{C_{2}}{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\theta-1}}\Big(\|u_{1}\|_{1}+\|u_{1}\|_{1,\theta}\Big)t^{\frac{2\theta-1}{2\theta}},\quad t\gg 1,

(iii)

for
n=1
and
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
or
n=2
and
\theta=1
, there exist positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
such that

\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{2+m^{2}}}|P_{1}|\sqrt{\log t}\leq\|u(t,\cdot)\|\leq\frac{C_{2}}{m}\Big(\|u_{1}\|_{1}+\|u_{1}\|_{1,\theta}\Big)\sqrt{\log t},\quad t\gg 1.

Let us reconsider the role of mass-like term from Theorem 1.2.
The result of (i) in Theorem 1.2 implies the decay estimate, and this comes from the stronger effect with decay order
t^{-\frac{n}{4}}
of the Gauss kernel
e^{-t|\xi|^{2}/2}
in the low frequency zone, and the oscillation effect comimg from the mass-like term
m^{2}\log(1+(-\Delta)^{\theta})u\sim m^{2}(-\Delta)^{\theta}u
gives a small role with growth order
t^{\frac{\theta}{2}}
. Totally, one can get the optimal decay rate
t^{-\frac{n}{4}+\frac{\theta}{2}}=t^{-\frac{n-2\theta}{4}}
. Recall that in case of
n\geq 3
the optimal decay order of the
L^{2}
-norm of the solution to (
1.6
) is
t^{-\frac{n-2}{2}}
(see
[
17
]
). In this case, one can see that
\frac{n-2}{2}<\frac{n-2\theta}{2}
for
\theta\in[0,1]
. In particular, in the case of
\theta=0
, the decay order coincides with that derived in
[
12
]
, and the result corresponds to the strongly damped Klein-Gordon equation (
1.7
). Thus, one can understand that the additional term
m^{2}\log(1+(-\Delta)^{\theta})u
plays a role to make the decay rate more fast. In this sense, the name of ”mass-like” term seems suitable.
While, (ii) of Theorem 1.2 reflects a stronger effect with growth order
t
of the oscillation factor in the low frequency part:

\frac{\sin\big(t\sqrt{|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}\big)}{\sqrt{|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}}\sim\frac{\sin(mtr^{\theta})}{mr^{\theta}}\quad(r\to+0),

and the diffusion part is no-effective. Roughly observing, for
n=1
one can see that

\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sin^{2}(mtr^{\theta})}{(mr^{\theta})^{2}}dr\right)^{1/2}\sim t\left(\int_{0}^{m^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}t^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}}dr\right)^{1/2}\sim t\cdot t^{-\frac{1}{2\theta}}\quad(t\to\infty),

since
|\displaystyle{\frac{\sin\eta}{\eta}}|\sim 1
when
\eta\to+0
. As a result one can get infinite time blow up rate
t\cdot t^{-\frac{1}{2\theta}}=t^{\frac{2\theta-1}{2\theta}}
when
t\to\infty
as shown in (ii). In some sense, in (ii) of Theorem 1.2 the dominant part in the low frequency zone as
t\to\infty
of the equation (1.1) will be

u_{tt}+m^{2}(-\Delta)^{\theta}u=0.

(iii) of Thorem 1.2 is just an intermediate case between (i) and (ii). These reflect a removable singularity in the low frequency zone included in the solution itself. This kind of growth property as shown in (ii) and (iii) has been newly discovered to the equation (1.1) itself.
In this connection, a regularity aspect which corresponds to high frequency asymptotics of the solution to (1.1) can be dominated by (
1.6
) even if
\theta>0
is large enough (cf.,
[
9
]
).

3
Existence and Uniqueness

The total energy associated to the equation (
1.1
) is defined by

\displaystyle E(t):=\frac{\|u_{t}\|^{2}+\|\nabla u\|^{2}+\|L_{\theta}^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|^{2}}{2},\quad t\geq 0,

and it satisfies the following energy identity

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E(t)+\|\nabla u_{t}\|^{2}=0,\quad t>0.

So it is natural to consider the following energy space

\displaystyle X=(H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\cap D(L_{\theta}^{1/2}))\times L^{2}.

(3.1)

Here, one can observe that
H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\subset D(L_{\theta}^{1/2}))
since
0\leq\theta\leq 1
, and due to the facts that
0\leq\log(1+r^{2\theta})\leq\log 2<1
for
0\leq r\leq 1
, and because of
\log(1+r^{2\theta})\leq r^{2}
for
r\geq 1
. Thus, in the case
0\leq\theta\leq 1
the energy space can be sufficiently chosen as

\displaystyle X=H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\times L^{2}({\bf R}^{n}).

(3.2)

We remember that the space
H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})
is a Hilbert space with the natural norm given by

\|f\|_{H^{1}}:=\left(\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}(1+|\xi|)^{2}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

but we may observe that this norm is equivalent to the following norm

\||f\||_{{H^{1}}}:=\left(\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}(1+m^{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})+|\xi|^{2})|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad f\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n}),

since
0\leq\theta\leq 1
. This second norm can be defined by the following inner product

((f,g))_{H^{1}}:=\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}(1+m^{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})+|\xi|^{2})\hat{f}(\xi)\overline{\hat{g}(\xi)}d\xi.

(3.3)

Therefore, in this section we consider the Hilbert space
H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})
with the inner product
((\cdot,\cdot))_{H^{1}}
just defined in (
3.3
).

In order to prove the existence and uniqueness, we reduce the original equation (
1.1
) to the first order system as usual.

We set
v=u_{t}
, and
U=\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}
and, we have (at least formally)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}U

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
u_{t}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{t}\\
\Delta u_{t}+\Delta u-m^{2}L_{\theta}u\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\Delta(v+u)-m^{2}L_{\theta}u\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\
\Delta-m^{2}L_{\theta}-I&\Delta\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u\\
v\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0\\
u\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=:BU+FU,

(3.4)

where

\displaystyle BU:=\begin{pmatrix}v\\
\Delta(u+v)-m^{2}L_{\theta}u-u\end{pmatrix}.

(3.5)

for
U:=(u,v)\in D(B)
,
and

\displaystyle FU=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
u\end{pmatrix}

(3.6)

for
U:=(u,v)\in X
.
In this case, we consider the domain
D(B)
of the linear operator
B
as

\displaystyle D(B)=\{(u,v)\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\times H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\;\;\mbox{such that }u+v\in H^{2}({\bf R}^{n})\}.

(3.7)

This idea is coming from
[
20
]
, and it is crucial to prove Lemma 3.2. The domain
D(B)
is dense in the energy space
X=H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\times L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
.

Let us see that the linear operator
B
is dissipative in
X
, and satisfies

.

Lemma 3.1

The linear operator
B
is dissipative in
X
.

Proof.
In the space
X=H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\times L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
, we consider the natural inner product

\left((u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})\right)_{X}=((u_{1},u_{2}))_{H^{1}}+(v_{1},v_{2})

for
(u_{j},v_{j})\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\times L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
, (
j=1,2
), where
((\cdot,\cdot))_{H^{1}}
is given by (
3.3
) and
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the usual inner product in
L^{2}
.

Let
(u,v)\in D(B)
. Then, the definition of the equivalent inner product (
3.3
) in
H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})
implies that

\displaystyle\left(B(u,v),(u,v)\right)_{X}

\displaystyle=\left((v,\Delta(u+v)-m^{2}L_{\theta}u-u),(u,v)\right)_{X}

\displaystyle=((v,u))_{H^{1}}+(\Delta(u+v)-m^{2}L_{\theta}u-u,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}(1+m^{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})+|\xi|^{2})\hat{v}\overline{\hat{u}}d\xi

\displaystyle-\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}(1+m^{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})+|\xi|^{2})\hat{u}\overline{\hat{v}}d\xi-\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2}|\hat{v}|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=2i\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}(1+m^{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})+|\xi|^{2})\text{Im}(\hat{v}\overline{\hat{u}})d\xi-\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2}|\hat{v}|^{2}d\xi.

Thus, one can get

\text{Re}\left(B(u,v),(u,v)\right)_{X}=-\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2}|\hat{v}|^{2}d\xi\leq 0,

and this concludes the desired statement.

\Box

Lemma 3.2

(I-B)(D(B))=X
.

Proof.
At first, the inclusion
(I-B)D(B)\subset X
is easily checked. So, it suffices to check

.
For this, let
(f,g)\in X=H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\times L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
. We need to show that there exists a pair
(u,v)\in D(B)
that satisfies the following two identities

\displaystyle\;u-v=f,

(3.8)

\displaystyle-\Delta(u+v)+m^{2}L_{\theta}u+u+v=g.

(3.9)

Since we need to find
u,v
such that

v=u-f
and
u
satisfying (

)
it is sufficient to obtain

such that

\displaystyle-2\Delta u+2u+m^{2}L_{\theta}u=g-\Delta f+f.

(3.10)

Note that

at this stage. Then we may apply the Lax-Milgram Theorem to obtain the existence of a unique function

satisfying (
3.10
) in weak sense. Since
v=u-f
and
f\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})
it follows that
(u,v)\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\times H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})
.

Now from (
3.9
) we have

-\Delta(u+v)+u+v=g-m^{2}L_{\theta}u\in L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})

due to the assumption on

and
u\in H^{1}({\bf R}^{n})
. Thus, the elliptic regularity theorem says that
u+v\in H^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
.
Therefore
(u,v)\in D(B)
and the operator
I-B
is surjective.

\Box

Cauchy problem in the Fourier space

The associated Cauchy problem in the Fourier space to problem (

1.2

\displaystyle\hat{u}_{tt}+|\xi|^{2}\hat{u}+m^{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})\hat{u}+|\xi|^{2}\hat{u}_{t}=0,\quad t>0,\quad\xi\in{\bf R}^{n}

\displaystyle\hat{u}(0,\xi)=\hat{u}_{0},\quad\hat{u}_{t}(0,\xi)=\hat{u}_{1},\quad\xi\in{\bf R}^{n}.

The characteristic equation associated to problem (

\lambda^{2}+|\xi|^{2}\lambda+(|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta}))=0,

and its characteristics roots are

\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{-|\xi|^{2}\pm\sqrt{|\xi|^{4}-4(|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta}))}}{2}.

We need to analyse the sign of the expression

|\xi|^{4}-4(|\xi|^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta}))
, for
\xi\in{\bf R}^{n}

to decide whether the characteristics roots are real or complex.

To do that we consider the function

f(r)=r^{4}-4r^{2}-4m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta}),\quad r\geq 0,

(4.3)

where
\theta\geq 0
. We set

g(r):=r^{2}(r^{2}-4),\quad h(r):=k\log(1+r^{2\theta}),

where
k:=4m^{2}>0
. Then, one has

f(r)=g(r)-h(r),

and
g(0)=g(2)=0
,
\displaystyle{\lim_{r\to\infty}}g(r)=\infty
,
h(0)=0
, and if
r\in[0,2]
, then
g(r)\leq 0
, and
g(r)>0
in case of
r>2
. Furthermore,
g(r)
is monotone increasing on
r\in(\sqrt{2},\infty)
, and is monotone decreasing on
[0,\sqrt{2}]
. Furthermore,
h(r)
is monotone (slowly) increasing on
r\in[0,\infty)
. By these observations on the drown graphs of such two functions
g(r)
and
h(r)
, the graphs on
r>0
of
g(r)
and
h(r)
have only one common point
\delta>2
, so that such only one point
\delta>2
plays a role to satisfy the following property:

\displaystyle f(r)=g(r)-h(r)=r^{4}-4r^{2}-4m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta}))<0,\quad\mbox{for}\;\;0<r<\delta,

\displaystyle f(r)=g(r)-h(r)=r^{4}-4r^{2}-4m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta}))\geq 0,\quad\mbox{for}\;\;r\geq\delta.

(4.4)

Note that thse arguments above hold good for each fixed
\theta\geq 0
, and the uniquely determined constant
\delta>2
depends on each fixed
\theta\geq 0
. Therefore, we conclude that the characteristics roots are complex on the zone
\{|\xi|<\delta\}
and are given by

\lambda_{\pm}=a(\xi)\pm ib(\xi),\quad i=\sqrt{-1},

where

\displaystyle a(\xi)=-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{2},\quad b(\xi)=\frac{\sqrt{4|\xi|^{2}+4m^{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})-|\xi|^{4}}}{2},

(4.5)

and they are real on the region
\{|\xi|\geq\delta\}
, and can be defined by

\lambda_{\pm}=a(\xi)\pm d(\xi),

\displaystyle d(\xi)=\frac{\sqrt{|\xi|^{4}-4|\xi|^{2}-4m^{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}}{2}.

(4.6)

0\leq\theta
by

\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{-|\xi|^{2}\pm\sqrt{|\xi|^{4}-4|\xi|^{2}-4m^{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}}{2},

and the solution formula in the high frequency region is

\displaystyle\hat{u}(t,\xi)=e^{-a(\xi)t}\frac{\sinh(b(\xi)t)}{b(\xi)}\hat{u}_{1},

(5.2)

where

\displaystyle a(\xi)=\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{2},\quad b(\xi)=\frac{\sqrt{|\xi|^{4}-4|\xi|^{2}-4m^{2}\log(1+|\xi|^{2\theta})}}{2}.

(5.3)

We recall that
\delta>0
is such that
f(\delta)=0
(
\delta>2
is the unique positive zero of
f(r)
) and
f(r)
is a increasing positive function for
r>\delta
defined by

f(r)=r^{4}-4r^{2}-4m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta}).

Then there exists
\delta_{1}>\delta
such that

\displaystyle 0<\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{\delta_{1}^{2}}-4\frac{m^{2}}{\delta_{1}^{4}}\log(1+\delta_{1}^{2\theta})}<1,

(5.4)

and, in particular,

\displaystyle\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{\delta_{1}^{2}}-4\frac{m^{2}}{\delta_{1}^{4}}\log(1+\delta_{1}^{2\theta})}\geq\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{r^{2}}-4\frac{m^{2}}{r^{4}}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}\geq 0

(5.5)

for
\delta\leq r\leq\delta_{1}.

Now we are able to estimate the
L^{2}
-norm of
\hat{u}
on the region of high frequency. We do that into two steps. The first step is to estimate on the middle frequency zone
\{\delta\leq|\xi|\leq\delta_{1}\}
:

\displaystyle\int_{\delta\leq|\xi|\leq\delta_{1}}|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\delta\leq|\xi|\leq\delta_{1}}|\hat{u}_{1}(t,\xi)|^{2}e^{-2ta(\xi)}\frac{\sinh^{2}(b(\xi)t)}{b^{2}(\xi)}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\int_{\delta\leq|\xi|\leq\delta_{1}}e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}\frac{\sinh^{2}(b(\xi)t)}{b^{2}(\xi)}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\int_{\delta\leq|\xi|\leq\delta_{1}}e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}t^{2}e^{2b(\xi)t}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\;t^{2}\int_{\delta}^{\delta_{1}}\omega_{n}r^{n-1}e^{-tr^{2}}e^{2b(r)t}dr

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{n}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\;t^{2}\int_{\delta}^{\delta_{1}}r^{n-1}e^{-tr^{2}}e^{\sqrt{r^{4}-4r^{2}-4m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}t}dr

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{n}\delta_{1}^{n-1}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\;t^{2}\int_{\delta}^{\delta_{1}}e^{-tr^{2}\big(1-{\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{r^{2}}-4\frac{m^{2}}{r^{4}}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}\big)}}dr,

which holds for any space dimensions
n\geq 1
and
t>0
. In the above estimates we have just used the elementary inequality Lemma
2.2
.

By using (
5.5
) in the last estimate above, one can get

\displaystyle\int_{\delta\leq|\xi\leq\delta_{1}}|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{n}\delta_{1}^{n-1}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\;t^{2}\int_{\delta}^{\delta_{1}}e^{-tr^{2}\big(1-{\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{\delta_{1}^{2}}-4\frac{m^{2}}{\delta_{1}^{4}}\log(1+\delta_{1}^{2\theta})}\big)}}dr

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{n}\delta_{1}^{n-1}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\;t^{2}(\delta_{1}-\delta)e^{-t\delta^{2}\big(1-{\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{\delta_{1}^{2}}-4\frac{m^{2}}{\delta_{1}^{4}}\log(1+\delta_{1}^{2\theta})}\big)}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\;t^{2}e^{-\alpha t},\quad t>0,

(5.6)

where
\alpha=\delta^{2}\big(1-{\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{\delta_{1}^{2}}-4\frac{m^{2}}{\delta_{1}^{4}}\log(1+\delta_{1}^{2\theta})}}\big)>0
(due to (
5.4
)), and
C_{n}
a positive constant depending only on
n
and
\theta
because
\delta
depends on
\theta
.

Now, to complete the estimate on the zone of high frequency we proceed with the second step, that is, we estimate
\hat{u}(t,\cdot)
on
|\xi|\geq\delta_{1}
. Then, because of Lemma
2.2
one can obtain

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi\geq\delta_{1}}

\displaystyle|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq\int_{|\xi|\geq\delta_{1}}|\hat{u}_{1}(t,\xi)|^{2}e^{-2ta(\xi)}\frac{\sinh^{2}(b(\xi)t)}{b^{2}(\xi)}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\int_{|\xi|\geq\delta_{1}}e^{-2t|\xi|^{2}}\;t^{2}e^{2b(\xi)t}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{n}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\;t^{2}\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\infty}r^{n-1}e^{-tr^{2}}e^{t\sqrt{r^{4}-4r^{2}-4m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}}dr

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{n}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\;t^{2}\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\infty}r^{n-1}e^{-tr^{2}\big(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{r^{2}}-\frac{4m^{2}}{r^{4}}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}\big)}dr

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{n}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\;t^{2}\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\infty}r^{n-1}e^{-\frac{tr^{2}}{2}\big(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{r^{2}}-\frac{4m^{2}}{r^{4}}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}\big)}e^{-\frac{tr^{2}}{2}\big(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{r^{2}}-\frac{4m^{2}}{r^{4}}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}\big)}dr

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{n}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\;t^{2}\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\infty}r^{n-1}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}\big(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{r^{2}}-\frac{4m^{2}}{r^{4}}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}\big)}e^{-\frac{{\bf t}\delta_{1}^{2}}{2}\big(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{\delta_{1}^{2}}-\frac{4m^{2}}{\delta_{1}^{4}}\log(1+\delta_{1}^{2\theta})}\big)}dr

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{n}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\;t^{2}\Big(\int_{\delta_{1}}^{\infty}r^{n-1}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}\big(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{{}^{2}}-\frac{4m^{2}}{r^{4}}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}}\big)dr\Big)e^{-\frac{{\bf t}\delta_{1}^{2}}{2}\big(1-\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{\delta_{1}^{2}}-\frac{4m^{2}}{\delta_{1}^{4}}\log(1+\delta_{1}^{2\theta})}\big)}

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{n}C_{n,\theta}\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\;t^{2}e^{-\beta t},

(5.7)

which holds for
t\geq 1
. The constant

\beta=\frac{\delta_{1}^{2}}{2}\big(1-{\sqrt{1-\frac{4}{\delta_{1}^{2}}-4\frac{m^{2}}{\delta_{1}^{4}}\log(1+\delta_{1}^{2\theta})}}\big)
is a positive constant (due to (
5.4
)) depending only on
n
and
\theta
because
\delta_{1}
depends on
\theta
.

The estimates (
5.1
) and (
5.1
) prove the exponential decay of
the
L^{2}
-norm of
\hat{u}
on the zone of high frequency.

6
Estimates to the asymptotic profile
\varphi(t,\xi)

In this section one shall study the
L^{2}
-estimates of the profile
\varphi(t,x)
itself in order to get sharp
L^{2}
-estimates of the solution itself. This is important to observe a singularity included in the solution
u(t,x)
. Such a singularity reflects on a “growth rate” as
t\to\infty
of the quantity
\|u(t,\cdot)\|
as stated in Theorem 1.2.

6.1
Decay estimates

In this subsection we present several estimates on the asymptotic profile of the solution to problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
).

To begin with, for low frequency parameter
|\xi|\leq 1
one has

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq\delta_{0}}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\delta_{0}}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}r^{n-1}dr

\displaystyle\leq P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{r^{2\theta}}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}r^{n-1-2\theta}dr,

where
\delta_{0}>0
is a number already defined in previous section 4 (see (
4.13
)). Since
0<\theta\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+0}\frac{r^{2\theta}}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}=\lim_{r\rightarrow+0}\frac{r^{2\theta}}{r^{2\theta}\left(r^{2-2\theta}+m^{2}\frac{\log(1+r^{2\theta})}{r^{2\theta}}\right)}=\frac{1}{m^{2}}

so that there is a constant
C>0
such that

\frac{r^{2\theta}}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}\leq\frac{C}{m^{2}},\quad 0<r\leq 1.

Thus, we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq\delta_{0}}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{m^{2}}P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-r^{2}t}r^{n-1-2\theta}dr.

Applying Lemma
2.1
the next statement holds.

Lemma 6.1

Let
0<\theta\leq 1
such that
n-2\theta>0
. Then, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq\delta_{0}}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq C_{n,\theta}\frac{P_{1}^{2}}{m^{2}}t^{-\frac{n-2\theta}{2}},\quad t\gg 1.

(6.1)

Remark 6.1

The decay estimate (
6.1
) holds in the following cases:

(i)

n=1
with
0<\theta<\frac{1}{2}
;

(ii)

n=2
with
0<\theta<1
;

(iii)

n\geq 3
and
0<\theta\leq 1
.

Note that the cases
n=1
with
\theta\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
and and
n=2
with
\theta=1
are not considered in the classification above. These missing cases are studied in the next Subsection 6.2.

The effect on the decay estimates of the function
\varphi(t,\xi)
in the zone of high frequency is exponentially small according to the next lemma.

Lemma 6.2

There exists a positive constant
C_{\delta_{0},n,\theta}
,
0\leq\theta\leq 1
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\geq\delta_{0}}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq C_{\delta_{0},n,\theta}P_{1}^{2}\;e^{-\frac{\delta_{0}^{2}}{2}\;t},\quad t\geq 1.

(6.2)

Proof.

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\geq\delta_{0}}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=\omega_{n}P^{2}_{1}\int_{\delta_{0}}^{\infty}e^{-r^{2}t}r^{n-1}\frac{\sin^{2}\big({t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}}\big)}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}dr

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{n}P^{2}_{1}\int_{\delta_{0}}^{\infty}e^{-r^{2}t}r^{n-1}\frac{1}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}dr

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{n}P^{2}_{1}\int_{\delta_{0}}^{\infty}e^{-r^{2}t}r^{n-1}\frac{1}{\delta_{0}^{2}}dr

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{n}\frac{P^{2}_{1}}{\delta_{0}^{2}}\;e^{-\frac{\delta_{0}^{2}}{2}t}\int_{\delta_{0}}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{n-1}dr,\quad t\geq 1.

This estimate proves the lemma because the last integral is finite.

\Box

Remark 6.2

The Lemmas
6.1
and
6.2
imply that

\displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq C_{n,\theta,\delta_{0}}\frac{P_{1}^{2}}{m^{2}}t^{-\frac{n-2\theta}{2}},\quad t>0,

(6.3)

with
C_{n,\theta,\delta_{0}}
a positive constant independent of
t
.

The estimate obtained in (
6.3
) may be optimal, but to check it, one needs more precise estimates from below. The lemma below is one of main difficulties to be proved in the paper.

Lemma 6.3

For
0<\theta\leq 1
with
n-2\theta>0
, there exist positive constants
C_{n,m,\theta}
and
t_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\geq C_{n,m,\theta}P_{1}^{2}t^{-\frac{n-2\theta}{{2}}},\quad t\geq t_{0}.

(6.4)

Proof.

In order to capture the precise estimate from below we need to estimate the
L^{2}
-norm of the profile only on the low frequency zone due to that on the high frequency the
L^{2}
-norm of the profile decays exponential, according to Lemma (
6.2
).

We first consider the change of variable
w=\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}
. Then

dw=\frac{r+\theta m^{2}\frac{r^{2\theta-1}}{1+r^{2\theta}}}{\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}}dr,

and

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}

\displaystyle|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi=\omega_{n}P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}r^{n-1}dr

\displaystyle=\omega_{n}P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-r^{2}t}r^{n-1}\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})})}{\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}\Big(r+\theta m^{2}\frac{r^{2\theta-1}}{1+r^{2\theta}}\Big)}\frac{r+\theta m^{2}\frac{r^{2\theta-1}}{1+r^{2\theta}}}{\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}}dr.

For
0\leq r\leq 1
and
0\leq\theta\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{m^{2}}{2}r^{2\theta}\leq w^{2}=r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})\leq(1+m^{2})r^{2\theta},

(6.5)

that implies the important equivalence
r\approx w^{\frac{1}{\theta}}
, for
0\leq r\leq 1
. We also notice that

r+\theta m^{2}\frac{r^{2\theta-1}}{1+r^{2\theta}}\approx r^{2\theta-1},\quad 0<r\leq 1,\quad 0\leq\theta\leq 1.

Thus, the above change of variable give us the estimate

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\approx\omega_{n}P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\eta}e^{-w^{\frac{2}{\theta}}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(tw)}{w}w^{\frac{n-2\theta}{\theta}}dw

\displaystyle=\omega_{n}P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\eta}e^{-w^{\frac{2}{\theta}}t}\sin^{2}(tw)w^{\frac{n-2\theta}{\theta}-1}dw

\displaystyle=\omega_{n}P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\eta}e^{-w^{\frac{2}{\theta}}t}\sin^{2}\Big(t^{1-\frac{\theta}{2}}t^{\frac{\theta}{2}}w\Big)w^{\frac{n-3\theta}{\theta}}dw,

where
\eta=\sqrt{1+m^{2}\log 2}
.

Now we set
y=t^{\frac{\theta}{2}}w
. Then for
t\gg 1

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\approx\omega_{n}P_{1}^{2}t^{-\frac{\theta}{2}}\int_{0}^{\eta}e^{-w^{\frac{2}{\theta}}t}\sin^{2}\Big(t^{1-\frac{\theta}{2}}t^{\frac{\theta}{2}}w\Big)w^{\frac{n-3\theta}{\theta}}t^{\frac{\theta}{2}}dw

\displaystyle=\omega_{n}P_{1}^{2}t^{-\frac{n-3\theta}{2}}t^{-\frac{\theta}{2}}\int_{0}^{\eta t^{\frac{\theta}{2}}}e^{-y^{\frac{2}{\theta}}}\sin^{2}\Big(t^{1-\frac{\theta}{2}}y\Big)y^{\frac{n-3\theta}{\theta}}dy

\displaystyle\geq\omega_{n}P_{1}^{2}t^{-\frac{n-2\theta}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-y^{\frac{2}{\theta}}}\sin^{2}\Big(t^{1-\frac{\theta}{2}}y\Big)y^{\frac{n-3\theta}{\theta}}dy

\displaystyle=\omega_{n}\frac{P_{1}^{2}}{2}t^{-\frac{n-2\theta}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}e^{-y^{\frac{2}{\theta}}}y^{\frac{n-3\theta}{\theta}}dy-\int_{0}^{1}e^{-y^{\frac{2}{\theta}}}y^{\frac{n-3\theta}{\theta}}\cos\Big(2t^{1-\frac{\theta}{2}}y\Big)dy\right)

\displaystyle=:\omega_{n}\frac{P_{1}^{2}}{2}t^{-\frac{n-2\theta}{2}}(A_{n}-F_{n}(t)),

where

\displaystyle A_{n}:=\int_{0}^{1}e^{-y^{\frac{2}{\theta}}}y^{\frac{n-3\theta}{\theta}}dy,

\displaystyle F_{n}(t):=\int_{0}^{1}e^{-y^{\frac{2}{\theta}}}y^{\frac{n-3\theta}{\theta}}\cos\Big(2t^{1-\frac{\theta}{2}}y\Big)dy.

For
0<\theta\leq 1
and
n-2\theta>0
the function
y\mapsto e^{-y^{\frac{2}{\theta}}}y^{\frac{n-3\theta}{\theta}}
is integrable on the interval
[0,1]
. Thus, it follows from the Riemann-Lebesgue theorem that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}F_{n}(t)=0,

due to
t^{1-\frac{\theta}{2}}\rightarrow\infty
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore, there exists
T>0
such that

-\frac{A_{n}}{2}\leq F_{n}(t)\leq\frac{A_{n}}{2},\quad t\geq T,

so that

A_{n}-F_{n}(t)\geq\frac{A_{n}}{2},\quad t\geq T.

Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\omega_{n}\frac{A_{n}P_{1}^{2}}{4}t^{-\frac{n-2\theta}{2}},\quad t\geq T,

which implies the desired statement.

\Box

6.2
Infinite time blow-up estimates

The omitted cases
n=1
with
\frac{1}{2}\leq\theta\leq 1
and
n=2
with
\theta=1
are more delicate and we discuss each of them in this subsection through some lemmas. We prove that the asymptotic profile of the solution blows up in infinite time. This is a quite novel property which reflects a singularity hidden in the solution itself.

(I) Estimates for
n=1
and
\theta\in(1/2,1]
.

To obtain sharp estimates to this cases, we first prepare the following fact.

Lemma 6.4

Let
c>0
and
\alpha>0
be constants. Then it holds that

\rm{erf}(ct^{-\alpha})\sim t^{-\alpha},\quad t\geq c^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},

where
\rm{erf}(\cdot)
is the Gauss error function defined by

{\rm erf}(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-w^{2}}dw.

Proof.
Using the change of variable
v=\frac{t^{\alpha}w}{c}
, we have

\displaystyle\text{erf}(ct^{-\alpha})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{ct^{-\alpha}}e^{-w^{2}}dw=\frac{2c}{\sqrt{\pi}t^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\frac{c^{2}v^{2}}{t^{2\alpha}}}dv.

(6.6)

Then, we can get the following upper estimate

\displaystyle\text{erf}(ct^{-\alpha})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2c}{\sqrt{\pi}t^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1}dv=\frac{2c}{\sqrt{\pi}}t^{-\alpha}

for
t>0
.
On the other hand, from (
6.6
) and assuming that
t\geq c^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
, we have the following lower estimate

\text{erf}(ct^{-\alpha})\geq\frac{2c}{\sqrt{\pi}t^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-v^{2}}dv=c\;{\rm erf}(1)t^{-\alpha}.

(6.7)

This completes the desired estimates.

\Box

In order to prove the following lemma we observe that for
n=1

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}dr.

(6.8)

In the proof of the following lemma, we use the fact that

\displaystyle\frac{m^{2}}{2}r^{2\theta}\leq r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})\leq(1+m^{2})r^{2\theta},

(6.9)

due to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}r^{2\theta}\leq\log(1+r^{2\theta})\leq r^{2\theta},

\displaystyle r^{2}\leq r^{2\theta}

for
0\leq r\leq 1
and
0\leq\theta\leq 1
.

Lemma 6.5

Let
n=1
and
\frac{1}{2}<\theta\leq 1
. There exist positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
such that

\frac{C_{1}}{(1+m^{2})^{\frac{1}{2\theta}}}P_{1}^{2}t^{\frac{2\theta-1}{\theta}}\leq\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq\frac{C_{2}}{m^{2}}\frac{1}{2\theta-1}t^{\frac{2\theta-1}{\theta}},\quad t\gg 1.

Proof.

First we get the upper estimate.

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{t^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}dr

\displaystyle+P_{1}^{2}\int_{t^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}}^{1}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}dr

\displaystyle=:P_{1}^{2}\Big(K_{1}(t)+K_{2}(t)\Big).

Set
L:=\displaystyle{\sup_{\nu\neq 0}|\frac{\sin\nu}{\nu}|}<+\infty
. Then, one can estimate as follows:

\displaystyle K_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq L^{2}t^{2}\int_{0}^{t^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}}e^{-r^{2}t}dr\leq L^{2}t^{2}\int_{0}^{t^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}}dr

\displaystyle\leq L^{2}t^{2}t^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}

\displaystyle=L^{2}t^{\frac{2\theta-1}{\theta}}.

(6.10)

Due to
|\sin a|\leq 1
, using (
6.9
) and integration by parts, assuming that
\theta\neq\frac{1}{2}
one can get an upper bound estimate to
K_{2}(t)
as follows:

\displaystyle K_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{t^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}}^{1}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}dr

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}}^{1}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{1}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}dr

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{m^{2}}\int_{t^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}}^{1}\frac{1}{r^{2\theta}}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{m^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2\theta-1}t^{\frac{2\theta-1}{\theta}}-\frac{1}{2\theta-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{m^{2}}\frac{1}{2\theta-1}t^{\frac{2\theta-1}{\theta}}.

(6.11)

From the estimates above one can observe that the Gauss kernel
e^{-tr^{2}}
is no effective on the growth rates.

From (
6.10
) and (
6.2
), we prove that if
\theta>\frac{1}{2}
there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq P_{1}^{2}\frac{C}{m^{2}}\frac{1}{2\theta-1}t^{\frac{2\theta-1}{\theta}},\quad t\gg 1.

In order to obtain the lower estimate we define

r_{0}(t):=\frac{2^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}{(1+m^{2})^{\frac{1}{2\theta}}}t^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}.

Then for
0\leq r\leq r_{0}(t)<1
we have

\displaystyle t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}\leq t\sqrt{1+m^{2}}r^{\theta}\leq 2,\quad t\gg 1,

(6.12)

due to (
6.9
). We observe that there exist a constant
C>0
such that

C\leq\displaystyle{\frac{\sin^{2}a}{a^{2}}}\leq L^{2}
for
0<|a|\leq 2
. Thus for
0\leq r\leq r_{0}(t)

\displaystyle C\leq\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})})}{t^{2}(r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta}))}\leq L^{2}.

(6.13)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}dr

\displaystyle\geq P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{r_{0}(t)}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}dr

\displaystyle\geq Ct^{2}P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{r_{0}(t)}e^{-r^{2}t}dr

\displaystyle=Ct^{\frac{3}{2}}P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{t}r_{0}(t)}e^{-w^{2}}dw

\displaystyle=CP_{1}^{2}t^{\frac{3}{2}}\text{erf}\left(\frac{2^{\frac{1}{\theta}}}{(1+m^{2})^{\frac{1}{2\theta}}}t^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq K\frac{P_{1}^{2}}{(1+m^{2})^{\frac{1}{2\theta}}}t^{\frac{2\theta-1}{\theta}},\quad t\gg 1,

(6.14)

with some generous constant
K>0
, where we just used the estimate (
6.7
). Note that (
6.2
) makes sense even if one chooses
m=0
.

\Box

(II) Estimate for
n=1
and
\theta=1/2
.

In this part we shall study the critical case of
\theta=1/2
just omitted in the statement of Lemma
6.5
. For this, we prepare the following lemma.

Lemma 6.6

There exists a positive constant
C
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}\sin^{2}(\sqrt{t}w)}{w}dw\geq C\log t,\quad t\gg 1.

(6.15)

Proof.
The whole idea comes from
[
18
]
.

Let

\nu_{j}=\left(\frac{1}{4}+j\right)\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{t}},\qquad\nu^{\prime}_{j}=\left(\frac{3}{4}+j\right)\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{t}},\quad j=0,1,2,3,\cdots

and observe that

\sin^{2}(\sqrt{t}w)\geq\frac{1}{2},\forall w\in[\nu_{j},\nu^{\prime}_{j}].

Then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}\sin^{2}(\sqrt{t}w)}{w}dw

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\nu_{j}}^{\nu^{\prime}_{j}}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}\sin^{2}(\sqrt{t}w)}{w}dw

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\nu_{j}}^{\nu^{\prime}_{j}}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}}{w}dw.

(6.16)

The function
(0,\infty)\ni w\mapsto\displaystyle{\frac{e^{-w^{2}}}{w}}
is decreasing and the intervals
[\nu_{j},\nu^{\prime}_{j}]
and
[\nu^{\prime}_{j},\nu_{j+1}]
have the same length, then

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\nu_{j}}^{\nu^{\prime}_{j}}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}}{w}dw\geq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\nu_{j}}^{\nu^{\prime}_{j}}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}}{w}dw+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\int_{\nu^{\prime}_{j}}^{\nu_{j+1}}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}}{w}dw=\int_{\frac{\pi}{4\sqrt{t}}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}}{w}dw.

(6.17)

From (
6.2
) and (
6.17
), for large
t>0
it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}\sin^{2}(\sqrt{t}w)}{w}dw

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{\frac{\pi}{4\sqrt{t}}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}}{w}dw

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\int_{\frac{\pi}{4\sqrt{t}}}^{1}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}}{w}dw

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}e^{-1}\int_{\frac{\pi}{4\sqrt{t}}}^{1}\frac{1}{w}dw

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}e^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\log t-\log\frac{\pi}{4}\right).

(6.18)

This implies the desired estimate.

\Box

The next lemma state the
\log
-growth rate of the critical case for
\theta=1/2
and
n=1
.

Lemma 6.7

Let
n=1
and
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
. There exist positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
such that

\frac{C_{1}}{m^{2}+2}P_{1}^{2}\log t\leq\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq\frac{C_{2}}{m^{2}}P_{1}^{2}\log t,\quad t\gg 1.

Proof.

For
n=1
and
\theta=1/2
and
t>1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2\theta})}dr

\displaystyle=P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{t^{2}}}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)}dr

\displaystyle=P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{t^{2}}}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)}dr

\displaystyle+P_{1}^{2}\int_{\frac{1}{t^{2}}}^{1}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)}dr

\displaystyle=:K_{1}(t)+K_{2}(t).

(6.19)

One first considers the upper bound estimates of (
6.19
).

Due to
\sin^{2}a\leq a^{2}
for
a\geq 0
, one has

\displaystyle K_{1}(t)=P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{t^{2}}}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)}dr\leq t^{2}P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{t^{2}}}dr=P_{1}^{2}.

(6.20)

In order to get an upper estimate to
K_{2}(t)
, we again remember that for
0\leq r\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\frac{m^{2}}{2}r\leq r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)\leq(1+m^{2})r,

(6.21)

as just observed in (
6.9
) with
2\theta=1
. Therefore

\displaystyle K_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=P_{1}^{2}\int_{\frac{1}{t^{2}}}^{1}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)}dr\leq P_{1}^{2}\int_{\frac{1}{t^{2}}}^{1}\frac{1}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)}dr

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{m^{2}}P_{1}^{2}\int_{\frac{1}{t^{2}}}^{1}\frac{1}{r}dr=\frac{2}{m^{2}}P_{1}^{2}(1-\log t^{-2})=\frac{2}{m^{2}}P_{1}^{2}(1+2\log t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{m^{2}}P_{1}^{2}\log t,\quad t\gg 1.

(6.22)

(
6.19
), (
6.20
) and (
6.22
) imply the desired statement

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq\frac{C}{m^{2}}P_{1}^{2}\log t,\quad t\gg 1

(6.23)

with some constant
C>0
.

One next derives the lower bound estimates of (
6.19
) in the case when
2\theta=1
and
n=1
.

One starts with the following inequality:

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}

\displaystyle|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi=P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)}dr

\displaystyle\geq P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-t(r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r))}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)}dr

\displaystyle=2P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-t(r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r))}\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)})}{\sqrt{t}\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)}\left(\frac{m^{2}}{r+1}+2r\right)}\frac{\sqrt{t}\left(\frac{m^{2}}{r+1}+2r\right)}{2\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)}}dr.

(6.24)

Set
\alpha=\sqrt{1+m^{2}\log 2}
. We apply the change of variable
w=\sqrt{t}\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r)}
in (
6.2
). Then, one has

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geq C^{*}P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\alpha\sqrt{t}}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}\sin^{2}(\sqrt{t}w)}{w}dw,

(6.25)

where one has used the estimate:

2\left(\frac{m^{2}}{r+1}+2r\right)^{-1}\geq 2(m^{2}+2)^{-1}=:C^{*}\quad(0\leq r\leq 1).

Now, for
t\geq 1
, we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\alpha\sqrt{t}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}\sin^{2}(\sqrt{t}w)}{w}dw

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\alpha\sqrt{t}}^{\infty}e^{-w^{2}}dw

\displaystyle\leq t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-w^{2}}dw

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(6.26)

Thus, applying Lemma
6.6
and the estimate just above, one obtains

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\alpha\sqrt{t}}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}\sin^{2}(\sqrt{t}w)}{w}dw

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}\sin^{2}(\sqrt{t}w)}{w}dw-\int_{\alpha\sqrt{t}}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}\sin^{2}(\sqrt{t}w)}{w}dw

\displaystyle\geq C\log t-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}t^{-\frac{1}{2}},

(6.27)

which implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\alpha\sqrt{t}}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}\sin^{2}(\sqrt{t}w)}{w}dw\geq\frac{C}{2}\log t,\quad t\gg 1.

(6.28)

(
6.25
) and (
6.28
) imply the desired estimate from below with a constant
C>0
:

\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\geq\frac{CC^{*}}{2}P_{1}^{2}\log t,\quad t\gg 1.

\Box

(III) Estimates for
n=2
and
\theta=1
.

Finally one treats the critical case for
n=2
and
\theta=1
to observe the growth property of the solution itself. In this case, one can get the following statement.

Lemma 6.8

Let
n=2
and
\theta=1
. There exist positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
such that

\frac{C_{1}}{2+m^{2}}P_{1}^{2}\log t\leq\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq\frac{C_{2}}{1+m^{2}}P_{1}^{2}\log t,\quad t\gg 1.

Proof.

For
t>0
, for
n=2
and
\theta=1
we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2})})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2})}rdr

(6.29)

\displaystyle=P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{t}}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2})})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2})}rdr

\displaystyle+P_{1}^{2}\int_{\frac{1}{t}}^{1}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2})})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2})}rdr

\displaystyle=:P_{1}^{2}(M_{1}(t)+M_{2}(t)).

(6.30)

First of all, one knows that
|\sin a|\leq a
for all
a\geq 0
. Thus

\displaystyle M_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{t}}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2})})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2})}rdr

\displaystyle\leq t^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{t}}e^{-r^{2}t}rdr\leq t^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{t}}rdr

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}.

(6.31)

In order to estimate
M_{2}(t)
, we observe that

\frac{r}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2})}\approx\frac{1}{1+m^{2}}\frac{1}{r},\quad 0<r\leq 1.

Then, the same steps to obtain estimates for
K_{2}(t)
in (
6.22
) can be applied to get

\displaystyle M_{2}(t)\leq\frac{C}{1+m^{2}}\log t,\quad t\gg 1.

Applying the estimates to
M_{1}(t)
and
M_{2}(t)
in (
6.30
), we can prove the upper estimate

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{1+m^{2}}P_{1}^{2}\log(t),\quad t\gg 1.

(6.32)

To obtain the lower bound estimate we set
\alpha=\sqrt{1+m^{2}\log 2}
. Then, similarly to (
6.25
) one can estimate as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}

\displaystyle|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi=P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-r^{2}t}\frac{\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2})})}{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2})}rdr

\displaystyle\geq P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-t\Big(r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2})\Big)}\sin^{2}(t\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2})})}{\sqrt{t}\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2})}\Big(2+\frac{m^{2}}{1+r^{2}}\Big)}\frac{\sqrt{t}r\Big(2+\frac{m^{2}}{1+r^{2}}\Big)}{\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2})}}dr

\displaystyle\geq C_{0}P_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\alpha\sqrt{t}}\frac{e^{-w^{2}}\sin(\sqrt{t}w)}{w}dw,

(6.33)

where we have just used the change of variable
w=\sqrt{t}\sqrt{r^{2}+m^{2}\log(1+r^{2})}
and the fact

\left(2+\frac{m^{2}}{1+r^{2}}\right)^{-1}\geq(2+m^{2})^{-1}=:C_{0}\quad(0\leq r\leq 1).

As we have already proved in (
6.28
), we have the desired estimate:

\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}|\varphi(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi\geq C_{0}P_{1}^{2}\log t,\quad t\gg 1.

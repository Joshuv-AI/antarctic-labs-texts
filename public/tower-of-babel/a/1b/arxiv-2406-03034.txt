Towards tree Yang-Mills and Yang-Mills-scalar amplitudes with higher-derivative interactions
Kang Zhou, Chang Hu

Abstract
In our recent works, a new approach for constructing tree amplitudes, based on exploiting soft behaviors, was proposed. In this paper, we extend this approach to effective theories for gluons which incorporate higher-derivative interactions. By applying our method, we construct tree Yang-Mills (YM) and Yang-Mills-scalar (YMS) amplitudes with the single insertion of F 3 F^{3} local operator, as well as the YM amplitudes those receive contributions from both F 3 F^{3} and F 4 F^{4} operators. All results are represented as universal expansions to appropriate basis. We also conjecture a compact general formula for tree YM amplitudes with higher mass dimension, which allows us to generate them from ordinary YM amplitudes, and discuss the consistent factorizations of the conjectured formula.

To effectively apply soft theorems to the new cases addressed in this paper, it is pertinent to discuss the universality of the aforementioned soft behaviors. It is a natural expectation that, within the same theory, the soft behavior remains universal for particles of the same type, irrespective of the number of external legs. For example the soft behavior of scalars in YMS theories. However, when comparing two different theories, it is generally unreasonable to assume the universality of soft behavior. For instance, one should allow for modifications to the soft theorem for YM amplitudes when introducing new types of interactions between gluons. We will now elucidate that the universality of soft behavior for scalars in BAS and YMS theories, as well as the universality of soft behavior for gluons in YMS and YM theories, are based on specific underlying reasons.

In this paper, we explore novel interactions between gluons with mass dimensions higher than those in YM theory. We affirm that the soft theorem for BAS scalars remains applicable when these scalars are coupled to gluons. The soft operator in (

However, when considering an external gluon as the soft particle, it’s logical to partition the full amplitude into two components: 1) The soft gluon attached to an ordinary vertex in YMS or YM theory follows the same soft theorem described in (

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{(1)_{i}}_{\rm YM^{+2}}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n})=S^{(1)_{i}}_{g}\,{\cal A}_{\rm YM^{+2}}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n-1})\,,~~

(26)

-point

\tau^{0}

On the other hand, the soft behavior of
\tau^{0}

The undetectable terms at the
\tau^{0}

i
is connected to
{\rm YM}^{+2}

\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{4}
. Suppose this characteristic is assumed at the beginning, then the cyclic symmetry among external legs, which fixes the amplitude completely, can be enlarged to the invariance under all permutations of external legs, as can be seen in the expansion (
35
). However, we did not use this assumption and restricted ourselves to the cyclic symmetry. It means in the story of this paper, the independence on
\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{4}
emerges, rather than assumed. From the CHY point of view, the independence on
\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{4}
implies the corresponding CHY integrand factorizes as
{\cal I}_{\rm CHY}={\cal I}_{L}{\cal I}_{R}
, where
{\cal I}_{R}
is the Parke-Taylor factor for the ordering
\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{4}
. Therefore, the emergence of independence on
\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{4}
is equivalent to the emergence of the double copy structure. Finally, the resulted amplitude in (
35
) is expressed as the expansion to YMS and BAS amplitudes which incorporate only trivalent interactions. Such expansion coincides with the statement at the beginning of the previous section
3.2
that all vertices under consideration in this paper can be decomposed into cubic ones.

3.4
General YM
+2
amplitudes

Repeating the same manipulation of section
3.3
recursively, one can obtain
the expansion formula for the general
{\rm YM}^{+2}
amplitude with arbitrary number of external legs

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{\rm YM^{+2}}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n})=\sum_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\,{\rm tr}(F_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}})\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}};\boldsymbol{g}_{n}\setminus\boldsymbol{\rho}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n})\,,~~~

(37)

where
\boldsymbol{g}_{n}
stands for the unordered
set of
n
external gluons. Again, the effective part of summation is for inequivalent
\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
which contain at least two elements.
At the end of this subsection, we will show the above expansion is coincide with that given in
[
42
]
, which is found
via the so called
covariant color-kinematic (CK) duality method based on the classical equation of motion.
The expansion for
4
-point amplitudes in (
35
) manifestly satisfies the
general formula (
37
). Now we show that (
37
) is correct for the
(m+1)
-point case if the
m
-point case is satisfied.
Some details and explanations will be omitted since they are paralleled to treatments for the
4
-point case in the previous subsection.

The subleading soft theorem for YM amplitudes indicates that

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{(1)_{i}}_{\rm YM^{+2}}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{(1)_{i}}_{g}\,{\cal A}_{\rm YM^{+2}}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1}\setminus i)\,.

(38)

One can figure out the subleading contribution
{\cal A}^{(1)_{i}}_{\rm YM^{+2}}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1})
as

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{(1)_{i}}_{\rm YM^{+2}}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{(1)_{i}}_{g}\,\Big(\sum_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\,{\rm tr}(F_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}})\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}};\boldsymbol{g}_{m+1}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{\rho}\cup i\}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1}\setminus i)\Big)

(39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\,{\rm tr}(F_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}})\,\Big(S^{(1)_{i}}_{g}\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}};\boldsymbol{g}_{m+1}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{\rho}\cup i\}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1}\setminus i)\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\,\Big(S^{(1)_{i}}_{g}\,{\rm tr}(F_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}})\Big)\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}};\boldsymbol{g}_{m+1}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{\rho}\cup i\}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1}\setminus i)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\,{\rm tr}(F_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}})\,{\cal A}^{(1)_{i}}_{\rm YMS}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}};\boldsymbol{g}_{m+1}\setminus\boldsymbol{\rho}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1})

\displaystyle+\tau\,\sum_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\,{\rm tr}(F_{\rho_{1},\vec{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\shuffle i})\,{\cal A}^{(0)_{i}}_{\rm YMS}(\rho_{1},\vec{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\shuffle i;\boldsymbol{g}_{m+1}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{\rho}\cup i\}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1})\,,

which indicates

\displaystyle{\cal P}^{i}_{\rm YM^{+2}}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\,{\rm tr}(F_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}})\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}};\boldsymbol{g}_{m+1}\setminus\boldsymbol{\rho}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1})

(40)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}}\,{\rm tr}(F_{\rho_{1},\vec{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\shuffle i})\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(\rho_{1},\vec{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\shuffle i;\boldsymbol{g}_{m+1}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{\rho}\cup i\}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1})\,.

Here
\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
is an ordered set which contains at least two elements in
\boldsymbol{g}_{m+1}\setminus i
,
and is labeled as
\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}=\{\rho_{1},\vec{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}\}
, where
\rho_{1}
is the first element in the ordering.
The shuffle
\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\shuffle\vec{\boldsymbol{{\beta}}}
means summing over all permutations such that the relative order in each of the ordered sets

\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}
and
\vec{\boldsymbol{{\beta}}}
is kept. For instance, suppose
\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}=\{1,2\}
,
\vec{\boldsymbol{{\beta}}}=\{3,4\}
, then

\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\shuffle\vec{\boldsymbol{{\beta}}}
is understood as

\displaystyle{\cal A}(\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}\shuffle\vec{\boldsymbol{{\beta}}})={\cal A}(1,2,3,4)+{\cal A}(1,3,2,4)+{\cal A}(1,3,4,2)\,.

(41)

It is straightforward to rewrite

{\cal P}^{i}_{\rm YM^{+2}}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1})
as

\displaystyle{\cal P}^{i}_{\rm YM^{+2}}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{\prime},|\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\prime}|=2\\
i\not{\in}\boldsymbol{\rho}\end{subarray}}\,{\rm tr}(F_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{\prime}})\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}};\boldsymbol{g}_{m+1}\setminus\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\prime}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1})

(42)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{\prime},|\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\prime}|\geq 3}\,{\rm tr}(F_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{\prime}})\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{\prime};\boldsymbol{g}_{m+1}\setminus\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\prime}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1})\,,~~

where
|\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\prime}|
denotes the number of elements in
\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\prime}
.
Requiring the invariance under cyclic permutation turns (
42
)
to

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{\rm YM^{+2}}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{\prime}}\,{\rm tr}(F_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{\prime}})\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{\prime};\boldsymbol{g}_{m+1}\setminus\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\prime}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{m+1})\,,

(43)

which satisfies the general formula (
37
).

The general expansion formula (
37
) manifests the permutation
symmetry among external gluons and the gauge invariance for each polarization, but is highly redundant. First, each pair of terms

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(F_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}})\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}};\boldsymbol{g}_{n}\setminus\boldsymbol{\rho}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n})~~{\rm and}~~{\rm tr}(F_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{T}})\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{T};\boldsymbol{g}_{n}\setminus\boldsymbol{\rho}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n})~~

(44)

are equivalent to each other, where the ordered set
\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{T}
is the reversing of
\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
.
This is because of the ordered reversed relations

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(F_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{T}})=(-)^{|\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}|}\,{\rm tr}(F_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}})\,,~~{\rm and}~~{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}^{T};\boldsymbol{g}_{n}\setminus\boldsymbol{\rho}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n})=(-)^{|\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}|}\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}};\boldsymbol{g}_{n}\setminus\boldsymbol{\rho}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n})\,,

(45)

where the first relation is based on the antisymmetry of
f_{i}^{\mu\nu}
, and the second one is a special case of the general relation

\displaystyle{\cal A}(1,\cdots,k)=(-)^{k}\,{\cal A}(k,\cdots,1)\,,~~

(46)

which is valid for any ordered amplitude. Secondly, various terms in (
37
) cancel each other. An example will be seen in sections
4.1
and
4.4
ahead.
As another example of cancelation, we now show that (
37
) can be reduced to

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{\rm YM^{+2}}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n})=\sum_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}},f\not\in\boldsymbol{\rho}}\,{\rm tr}(F_{\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}})\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}};\boldsymbol{g}_{n}\setminus\boldsymbol{\rho}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n})\,,~~

(47)

where all terms with
f\in\boldsymbol{\rho}
are eliminated, for arbitrary
f\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
. Let us collect all terms with
f
and another leg
i

are fixed at two ends of
\vec{\boldsymbol{\rho}}
, to get

\displaystyle G_{fi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(f_{i}\cdot f_{f})\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(f,i;\boldsymbol{g}_{n}\setminus\{f,i\}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n})

(48)

\displaystyle+2\,\sum_{\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\,{\rm tr}(f_{i}\cdot F_{\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}}}\cdot f_{f})\,{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}(f,\vec{\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}},i;\boldsymbol{g}_{n}\setminus\{\{f,i\}\cup\boldsymbol{{\alpha}}\}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n})\,.

Using the expansion of YM amplitudes with gauge invariant coefficients found in
[
26
]
,
we get
G_{fi}=-(k_{f}\cdot k_{i}){\cal A}_{\rm YM}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n})
. Thus, summing over all
G_{fi}
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq f}\,G_{fi}=-\sum_{i\neq f}\,(k_{f}\cdot k_{i})\,{\cal A}_{\rm YM}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n})=0\,.~~

(49)

Consequently, one can remove
\sum_{i\neq f}\,G_{fi}
in (
37
) to obtain (
47
). The simplified new version in(
47
)
is the expansion found in
[
42
]
,
it breaks the manifest permutation invariance due to the fiducial gluon
f
, but contains less terms.

6
Summary

In this paper, we extend the bottom-up approach developed in our recent works to effective field theories encompassing gluons and BAS scalars. By inverting the subleading soft theorem for YM amplitudes, we construct the tree
{\rm YM}^{+2}
amplitudes featuring a single insertion of the
{\rm YM}^{+2}
vertex. This construction benefits from the observation that the subleading soft theorem for YM amplitudes remains valid for
{\rm YM}^{+2}
amplitudes. However, the direct construction only captures a subset of the complete amplitude, with the remainder identified through the consideration of appropriate symmetries. Subsequently, we proceed to construct the tree
{\rm YMS}^{+2}
amplitudes by employing the inversion of the leading soft theorem for BAS amplitudes and the subleading soft theorem for YM amplitudes, guided by a similar rationale. Finally, we delve into the realm of tree
{\rm YM}^{+2h}
amplitudes with higher values of
h
. We derive the complete result for
h=2
and propose a conjecture for the general formula applicable to any value of
h
. We also discuss the consistent factorizations of the conjectured general formula. Importantly, all constructions in this paper are independent of the Lagrangian or Feynman rules, and the potential infinite tower of higher-derivative operators are automatically encompassed.

As a direct consequence of our method, all resulting amplitudes are expressed as universal expansions to YMS
{}^{+2h^{\prime}}
amplitudes with lower
h^{\prime}
or expansions to multiple-trace YMS amplitudes, maintaining manifest permutation and gauge invariance. Furthermore, in the expansion of
{\cal A}_{\rm YM}^{+2h}(\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n})
or
{\cal A}_{\rm YMS}^{+2h}(1,\cdots,n;\boldsymbol{g}_{m}|\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n+m})
, the coefficients are independent of the orderings
\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n}
or
\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{n+m}
. As noted at the end of section
3.3
, this feature is equivalent to the well-known double copy structure. Ultimately, these expansions naturally lead to the representations of these amplitudes as expansions to pure BAS ones, ensuring their adherence to the BCJ relations.

There are various aspects to further understand YM
+2h
amplitudes with
h\geq 3
. Firstly, as discussed at the end of section
3.4
, although the expansions exhibit gauge and permutation invariance, they are highly redundant. Therefore, it is important to discover alternative formulas that eliminate these redundancies. Secondly, it is intriguing to determine the Lagrangian corresponding to YM
+2h
amplitudes for each
h\geq 3
, as well as the correspondence between each term and Feynman diagrams. Thirdly, since the expansions identified in this paper display a manifest double-copy structure, another significant task is to search for the corresponding CHY formulas, serving as generalizations of the CHY formula for YM
+2
amplitudes found in
[
41
]
. Finally, assuming we can discover the correct formulas, the next essential question is their uniqueness.

We conclude with a brief discussion on potential generalizations of our results and method. Firstly, following the double copy structure, it is natural to anticipate that the expansions found in (
37
), (
81
), and (
103
) can be directly mapped to gravitational (GR) and Einstein-Yang-Mills (EYM) amplitudes with the same coefficients. The resulting GR and EYM amplitudes will naturally be interpreted as those with higher-derivative interactions, akin to Gauss-Bonnet gravity arising from the low-energy effective action of closed strings, among others. Secondly, since the soft limit can be taken for any massless particle, it is intriguing to investigate the applicability of our method in a broader scope, such as scalar effective field theories like the non-linear sigma model and beyond, as well as amplitudes of theories in which massless particles couple to fermions.

Global martingale and pathwise solutions and infinite regularity of invariant measures for a stochastic modified Swift-Hohenberg equation


Abstract
We consider a 2D stochastic modified Swift-Hohenberg equations with multiplicative noise and periodic boundary. First, we establish the existence of local and global martingale and pathwise solutions in the regular Sobolev space H 2 ​ m H^{2m} for each m ⩾ 1 m\geqslant 1 . Associated with the unique global pathwise solution, we obtain a Markovian transition semigroup. Then, we show the existence of invariant measures and ergodic invariant measures for this Markovian semigroup on H 2 ​ m H^{2m} . At last, we improve the regularity of the obtained invariant measures to H 2 ​ ( m + 1 ) H^{2(m+1)} . With appropriate conditions on the diffusion coefficient, we can deduce the infinite regularity of the invariant measures, which was conjectured by Glatt-Holtz et al. in their situation [ 19 ] . Keywords: Stochastic modified Swift-Hohenberg model; global martingale solution; global pathwise solution; ergodic invariant measure; infinite regularity. Introduction

In this paper, we investigate several stochastic problems,
including the local and global existence of martingale and pathwise solutions,
existence of ergodic invariant measures and infinite regularity of
the invariant measures,
for the following two-dimensional stochastic modified Swift-Hohenberg equation
(MSHE for short) with multiplicative noise and periodic boundary,

\displaystyle{\rm d}u+[\Delta^{2}u+2\Delta u+au+b|\nabla u|^{2}+u^{3}]{\rm d}t=\phi(u){\rm d}W,\;\;~t>0,

(1.1)

\displaystyle u(x,y,t)=u(x+2\pi,y,t)=u(x,y+2\pi,t),\;\;~t\geqslant 0,

(1.2)

\displaystyle u(x,y,0)=u_{0}(x,y),

(1.3)

where
u(x,y,t)
is the unknown amplitude function,
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\Delta=\partial_{xx}+\partial_{yy}
,
a,\,b\in\mathbb{R}

and
u_{0}
is
2\pi
-periodic with respect to
x
and
y
respectively.
The stochastic term
\phi(u){\rm d}W
in (
1.1
) will be specified
in Subsection
2.2
.

The Swift-Hohenberg equation was named after Swift and Hohenberg (see
[
43
]
),
and arose from convective phenomena in the research of geophysical fluid flows
in atmosphere, oceans and earth’s mantle.
It is closely contacted with nonlinear Navier-Stokes equations coupled
with the temperature equation.
The Swift-Hohenberg equation has played a significant role in different branches of physics,
ranging from hydrodynamics to nonlinear optics (see e.g.
[
25
,
32
,
33
,
38
]
).
For this modified case, the cubic term
u^{3}
in (
1.1
) is used as an approximation of
a nonlocal integral term (
[
45
,
53
]
).
The modified term
b|\nabla u|^{2}
in (
1.1
) comes from the various pattern formation phenomena
involving some kind of phase turbulence or phase transition (
[
40
]
),
which eliminates the symmetry
u\rightarrow-u
.

There have been lots of research on the subject of MSHEs.
Roughly speaking, these works mainly include three aspects: attractors
(

bifurcations of solutions (

What is more, for the nonautonomous MSHE,
Wang, Yang and Duan presented in

a lower number of recurrent solutions
by topological methods (see more in
[

46
,
47
,
48
]
);
Wang, Zhang and Zhao studied the existence of invariant measures and statistical solutions
in
[

.
Due to the delicate impact on the deterministic cases in real world,
randomness has captured more and more concern over the development of evolution systems
recently.
It becomes centrally significant to take stochastic effects into account for mathematical models
of complex phenomena in engineering and science.
However, the study of stochastic MSHEs is still inadequate in the literature up to now,
especially that of existence and regularity of the invariant measures.

In physics, invariant measures have performed a significant role in the research of
turbulence (
[
17
]
).
In the field of dynamical systems, invariant measures are to the measure space
for the phase space what invariant sets (even attractors) are to the phase space.
Essentially, invariant measures also illustrate the long-time dynamical behaviors for
a given dynamical system, but in view of measure.
Particularly, for stochastic systems, the solution process is random.
If we fix each random variable, the Wiener process
W
would become a deterministic function.
Then we can also study the attractor and its properties
for the corresponding deterministic system.
This treatment seems like straying from the essence of randomness.
According to this observation, we are encouraged to consider the existence and regularity
of invariant measures for the stochastic dynamical systems.

In recent decades, the research of (ergodic) invariant measures has witnessed
the development of (stochastic) dynamical systems.
References
[

,

,

,

,

studied the limit

as the noise vanishes or perturbs.
There have been more references concerning the existence of invariant measures,
such as
[

,

,
13
,

,

,

,

,

and their references.
For the existence of invariant measures for stochastic partial differential equation,
it often needs to assume that the drift terms to be dissipative.
If the drift terms contains nonlinear ones, the nonlinear terms are often hoped to be
globally Lipschitz or dissipative.
In the book
[
8
]
, Da Prato and Zabczyk provided basic theory on
the existence of invariant measures for stochastic partial differential equations
with globally Lipschitz or dissipative nonlinearity.
Relatively, regularity of invariant measures has attracted little attention until now.
Bogachev, Krylov and Röckner provided in
[
2
]
a method to
improve the regularity of an invariant measure.
By adopting this idea, Glatt-Holtz et al. in
[
20
]
presented
another result, showing higher regularity of the invariant measures
for the three dimensional stochastic primitive equations.
The authors of
[
20
]
even conjecture that “
the invariant measures for
the 3D primitive equation are in fact supported on
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}
function
”
with appropriate continuity and compatibility assumptions on the force.

Now, as presented in (
1.1
), the modified nonlinear terms lack
the global Lipschitz continuity or dissipation,
and the lack brings new challenges to our discussion.
Moreover, what we study in this paper is the stochastic MSHE
with a general multiplicative noise, which is allowed to be nonlinear.
In this situation, we will not consider the mild solutions
(studied in
[
36
,
45
,
54
]
),
but discuss the weak and strong solutions in the stochastic sense, saying,
the martingale and pathwise solutions, correspondingly, and establish their global existence.
With the unique global pathwise solution in hand,
we further explore the existence of ergodic invariant measures for the Markovian semigroup
associated with the solution in spaces of high regularity.
We also turn out that each invariant measure obtained is supported on
{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}\cap H

(defined in Subsection
2.1
),
which is said to be the infinite regularity of the invariant measure,
under some appropriate assumption on the diffusion coefficient
\phi
.
This means that we have actually proved the conjecture of Glatt-Holtz et al. in our situation.

Since the equation (
1.1
) contains the modified nonlinearity, the existence of martingale
and pathwise solutions is far from holding naturally without a detailed argument.
We initiate our topics by introducing cut-off functions to treat the nonlinear terms,
and then apply the Galerkin approximating methods to the cut-off system.
By this setting, we are prompted to use the unique global existence of solutions (
[
15
]
) for
finite-dimensional stochastic differential equation with globally Lipschitz nonlinearity
to start our work.

To show the local existence of martingale and pathwise solutions in
H^{2m}
(the Sobolev space
with periodic boundary given in Subsection
2.1
) for each
m\geqslant 1
,
we adopt the classical Galerkin method and Yamada-Wannabe theorem (see
[
9
,
11
,
20
]
)
for stochastic partial differential equations.
We actually conclude that the martingale solution (see Theorem
4.2
)
or pathwise solution (see Theorem
5.3
)
u(t)
satisfies

u(\cdot\wedge\tau)\in L^{2}(\Omega;{\mathcal{C}}([0,\infty);H^{2m})\cap L_{\rm loc}^{2}(0,\infty;H^{2(m+1)}))

for some stopping time
\tau\geqslant 0
or
\tau>0
,
where
\Omega
is a probability space that is described in Subsection 2.2.
Here it is only required that
\phi
is locally bounded (for martingale case)
or locally Lipschitz (for pathwise case).
As to the uniqueness of solutions, the usage of Skorohod embedding theorem
can not ensure the local uniqueness in the argument for existence of martingale solutions,
albeit with Lipschitz condition of
\phi
,
which, however, is sufficient to imply the local uniqueness in the pathwise case,
as the stochastic basis is chosen fixed in advance.
The uniqueness enables us to extend every local pathwise solution to a maximal one.
In particular, the maximal pathwise solution undergoes a blowup at the maximal living time.

For the global existence of martingale solutions, Dhariwal et al. in
[
10
]
considered
a stochastic population cross-diffusion system and gave
the existence of global martingale solutions, in the vension that the solution depends on
the time interval
[0,T]
, where
T
can be chosen arbitrarily.
In this paper, we consider a new version that the global martingale is defined on the entire
positive axis.
In order to obtain the existence of such global martingale solutions,
we need to assume
|b|<4
to constrain the modified term and
\phi
to be globally Lipschitz.
Although infeasible to ensure uniqueness for martingale solutions, the Lipschitz
condition makes the solutions coincide locally for the same finite-dimensional
approximating equation (
3.2
) with the same initial datum but different cut-off functions.
Based on this coincidence, we can then make good use of the diagonal method to pick
a subsequence of the approximating solutions, which converges all over
t\in[0,\infty)
.
Thus, we successfully obtain a global martingale solution on
[0,+\infty)

for (
1.1
) – (
1.3
).
This process can be well applied to many other stochastic partial differential equations
to construct a global martingale solution.

For the global existence of pathwise solutions, we need the same assumptions as the case for
global martingale solutions, i.e.,
|b|<4
and the global Lipschitz condition for
\phi
.
Here we prove a general and simple conclusion (Lemma
5.5
),
similar to the Dominated Convergence Theorem,
but the limit function takes values at the infinity almost surely.
The global unique existence follows immediately from this conclusion and a uniform estimate
for the maximal pathwise solution given above by means of reduction to absurdity.

The unique existence of the global pathwise solution draws forth a Markovian transition
semigroup
{\mathcal{P}}_{t}
on the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
{\mathcal{B}}(H^{2m})
of
H^{2m}
,
which is proved to be Feller by a process referring to
[
45
]

with an additional condition (
2.4
) on
\phi
.
The existence of the invariant measures is a direct consequence of tightness for
the measure family
\{\nu_{T}\}_{T>0}
with

\nu_{T}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(u,\cdot){\rm d}t,

by Krylov-Bogoliubov existence theorem and Prokhorov’s theorem (see
[
8
]
).
To guarantee the existence of ergodic measures, we utilize the Krein-Milman Theorem
(
[
7
]
) to find an extreme point of the set of invariant measures,
and this extreme point is exactly an ergodic measure.
Again we necessarily show the tightness of the set of all invariant measures,
which is assured in nature by the uniform integrability of
\|u\|_{2m}^{2}
,
the square of
H^{2m}
-norm of
u
, over
H^{2m}
with respect to each invariant measure.
This uniform integrability is not an easy deduction.
Thanks to Song, Zhang and Ma’s work
[
41
]
, in which they proved that the MSHE
possesses a global attractor in the Sobolev space
H^{k}
for each
k\geqslant 0
by using
iteration procedure, we can as well use iteration procedure or induction method
to raise the order
k\in[0,m]
one by one such that
\|u\|_{2k}^{2}
is uniformly
integrable over
H^{2m}
with respect to each fixed invariant measure.

For the rise of regularity of invariant measures, we consider to use the a posteriori method
by the idea propounded in
[
2
]
, and first show that
\|u\|^{2}_{2(m+1)}
is uniformly
integrable over
H^{2m}
with respect to each invariant measure under the same condition that
ensures the existence of invariant measures on
H^{2m}
.
This adds one to the regularity of invariant measures.
In this way, by assuming an appropriate stronger condition (
2.5
)
than (
2.3
) and (
2.4
),
we can eventually obtain the infinite regularity of the invariant measures.

2.4
On invariant measures

Let

For a set

.

on

{\mathcal{P}}^{*}_{t}\nu(\Gamma)=\int_{X}{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(x,\Gamma)\nu({\rm d}x),\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{for each }\Gamma\in{\mathcal{B}}(X).

(

) is said to be

if for arbitrary

and

,
which is a fixed point for

, that is to say,

\int_{X}{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(x,\Gamma)\nu({\rm d}x)=\nu(\Gamma),\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{for each }\Gamma\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)\mbox{ and }t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

Let

.
We say that

\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\varphi{\rm d}t=\int_{X}\varphi(x)\nu({\rm d}x)\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{for all }\varphi\in L^{2}(X,\mu).

,

4
Local and Global Existence of Martingale Solutions

4.1
Local existence of martingale solutions

Let
\mu_{0}
be a given initial distribution on
H^{2m}
.
We fix a stochastic basis
{\mathcal{S}}=(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},\{{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0},\mathbb{P},W)

upon which is defined an
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
measurable random element
u_{0}
with distribution
\mu_{0}
.
Consider the sequence of Galerkin approximations
\{u^{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
solving (
3.2
)
relative to this basis and initial condition.
We consider the phase spaces

{\mathcal{X}}_{u}=L^{2}(0,T;H^{2m})\cap{\mathcal{C}}([0,T];H^{2(m-2)}),\hskip 11.38092pt{\mathcal{X}}_{W}={\mathcal{C}}([0,T];\mathfrak{U})

(4.1)

and
{\mathcal{X}}={\mathcal{X}}_{u}\times{\mathcal{X}}_{W}
, with
{\mathcal{X}}_{u}
the space
where the solution
u^{n}
lives and
{\mathcal{X}}_{W}
the set where
the driving Brownian motions are defined.
We consider the probability measures

\mu_{u}^{n}(\cdot)=\mathbb{P}(u^{n}\in\cdot)\in{\rm Pr}({\mathcal{X}}_{u})\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{and}

(4.2)

\mu_{W}^{n}(\cdot)=\mu_{W}(\cdot)=\mathbb{P}(W\in\cdot)\in{\rm Pr}({\mathcal{X}}_{W}).

(4.3)

This defines a sequence of probability measures
\mu^{n}:=\mu^{n}_{u}\times\mu_{W}^{n}
on the phase space
{\mathcal{X}}
.
We now show that this sequence is tight as follows by using Lemma
3.2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
\phi
satisfies (
2.6
) and
\mu_{0}
satisfies (
2.9
).
Then the sequence
\{\mu^{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
is tight and weakly compact over the phase space
{\mathcal{X}}
.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
2.1
(i) by setting
X_{2}=H^{2(m-1)}
,
X_{0}=H^{2m}
,
X_{1}=H^{2(m+1)}
,

p=2
and
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, we have that

L^{2}(0,T;H^{2(m+1)})\cap W^{\alpha,2}(0,T;H^{2(m-1)})\subset\subset L^{2}(0,T;H^{2m}).

Given
R>1
, define a set

\displaystyle B_{R}^{1}=\{

\displaystyle u\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2(m+1)})\cap W^{\alpha,2}(0,T;H^{2(m-1)}):

\displaystyle\|u^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2(m+1)})}+\|u^{n}\|^{2}_{W^{\alpha,2}(0,T;H^{2(m-1)})}\leqslant R^{2}\}.

Then
B_{R}^{1}
is compact in
L^{2}(0,T;H^{2m})
.
The Chebyshev inequality applies and we have

\displaystyle\mu_{u}^{n}((B_{R}^{1})^{C})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\|u^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2(m+1)})}+\|u^{n}\|^{2}_{W^{\alpha,2}(0,T;H^{2(m-1)})}\geqslant R^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\|u^{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2(m+1)})}\geqslant\frac{R^{2}}{2}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\|u^{n}\|^{2}_{W^{\alpha,2}(0,T;H^{2(m-1)})}\geqslant\frac{R^{2}}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{2}{R^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|A^{m+1}u^{n}(s)\|^{2}{\rm d}s+\|u^{n}\|^{2}_{W^{\alpha,2}(0,T;H^{2(m-1)})}\right)\leqslant\frac{C^{\prime}_{5}}{R^{2}},

(4.4)

independent of
n
, where we have used (
3.3
) with
p=2
, (
3.6
),
(
3.5
) and (
2.2
).

Take
\alpha\in(\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{2})
with
\alpha q>1
.
By Lemma
2.1
(ii) with
Y_{0}=H^{2(m-2)}
and
Y=H^{2(m-1)}
, we have the compact embeddings

W^{1,2}(0,T;H^{2(m-1)})\subset\subset{\mathcal{C}}([0,T];H^{2(m-2)}),

W^{\alpha,q}(0,T;H^{2(m-1)})\subset\subset{\mathcal{C}}([0,T];H^{2(m-2)}).

Given
R>1
, let
B_{R}^{21}
and
B_{R}^{22}
be the closed balls of radius
R
in the spaces

W^{1,2}(0,T;H^{2(m-1)})
and
W^{\alpha,q}(0,T;H^{2(m-1)})
respectively.
It follows that for
R>1
,
B^{2}_{R}:=B_{R}^{21}+B_{R}^{22}
is compact in
{\mathcal{C}}([0,T];H^{2(m-2)})
.
Due to the inclusion

\{u^{n}\in B_{R}^{2}\}\supset\left\{u^{n}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}P_{n}\phi(u^{n}){\rm d}W\in B_{R}^{21}\right\}\cap\left\{\int_{0}^{t}P_{n}\phi(u^{n}){\rm d}W\in B_{R}^{22}\right\},

we obtain by Chebyshev inequality and (
3.6
), (
3.5
) that

\displaystyle\mu_{u}^{n}((B_{R}^{2})^{C})\leqslant

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|u^{n}(t)-\int_{0}^{t}P_{n}\phi(u^{n}){\rm d}W\right\|_{W^{1,2}(0,T;H^{2(m-1)})}\geqslant R^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\int_{0}^{t}P_{n}\phi(u^{n}){\rm d}W\right\|_{W^{\alpha,q}(0,T;H^{2(m-1)})}\geqslant R^{q}\right)\leqslant\frac{C^{\prime}_{6}}{R^{2}},

(4.5)

also independent of

.

It is trivial that

.

Then for every

, we are allowed to pick a set

(by increasing

for

)
such that

\mu_{u}^{n}({\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon})\geqslant 1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\hskip 22.76228pt\mbox{for all }n\geqslant 0.

(4.6)

Now we consider the sequence
\{\mu_{W}^{n}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
, which actually identically equals to
\mu_{W}
,
and is hence weakly compact.
By Prokhorov’s Theorem (
[
8
]
),
\{\mu_{W}^{n}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
is surely tight.
This helps find a compact set
{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}
in
{\mathcal{C}}([0,T];\mathfrak{U}_{0})
such that for all

,

\mu_{W}^{n}({\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon})\geqslant 1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

(4.7)

Combining (
4.6
) and (

\varepsilon>0

satisfies that for all

,

\mu^{n}({\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}\times{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon})\geqslant 1-\varepsilon,

and therefore
\{\mu^{n}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
is tight in
{\mathcal{X}}
and finally weakly compact.

Now we pick the sequence
\{(\hat{U}^{n_{k}^{k},k},\hat{W}^{n_{k}^{k},k})\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
.
Given each
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, let

\zeta_{N}=\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\zeta^{n_{k}^{k}}_{N}\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{and}\hskip 11.38092pt\zeta_{N}^{n_{k}^{k}}=\bigwedge_{j=N}^{\infty}\zeta_{N}^{n_{k}^{k},j}.

(4.58)

Since obviously
\zeta_{N}^{n_{k}^{k},j}\leqslant\zeta_{N+1}^{n_{k}^{k},j}

\zeta_{N}^{n_{k}^{k}}\leqslant\zeta_{N+1}^{n_{k}^{k}}
and hence
\zeta_{N}\leqslant\zeta_{N+1}
.
We will show that for each
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, as

,
the sequence
(\hat{U}^{n_{k}^{k},k},\hat{W}^{n_{k}^{k},k})
converges almost surely to the pair

(\hat{U}^{N},\hat{W}^{N})
on
[0,\zeta_{N}\wedge T]
,
and correspondingly, its law sequence

weakly converges to
\hat{\mu}^{N}
.

By Lemma
4.5
, Skorohod’s Theorem and (
4.56
),
we know whenever
1\leqslant N<N^{\prime}\leqslant k

for almost surely

, it holds that

(\hat{U}^{n_{k}^{k},N^{\prime}}(t),\hat{W}^{n_{k}^{k},N^{\prime}}(t))=(\hat{U}^{n_{k}^{k},N}(t),\hat{W}^{n_{k}^{k},N}(t)).

(4.59)

Since the right side of (
4.59
) is indeed a subsequence of

(\hat{U}^{n_{k}^{N},N}(t),\hat{W}^{n_{k}^{N},N}(t))
,
by the uniqueness of almost surely limit and picking all
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, we conclude that

(\hat{U}^{N^{\prime}}(t),\hat{W}^{N^{\prime}}(t))=(\hat{U}^{N}(t),\hat{W}^{N}(t))\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{for all }N^{\prime}>N

for almost every
t\in[0,\zeta_{N}\wedge T]
and
\omega\in\hat{\Omega}
and due to
the almost surely equality we also have that
\hat{\mu}^{N^{\prime}}=\hat{\mu}^{N}
for
N^{\prime}>N
.
These results have already implied the two convergences we desire.

We now claim that

\zeta_{N}\rightarrow\infty\hskip 22.76228pt\mbox{ almost surely as }N\rightarrow+\infty.

(4.60)

By assuming the claim, one can easily extend the pair
(\hat{U}^{N},\hat{W}^{N})
on
[0,\zeta_{N}\wedge T]

to
(\hat{U},\hat{W})
defined on
[0,T]
with
(\hat{U}(t),\hat{W}(t))=(\hat{U}^{N}(t),\hat{W}^{N}(t))
on

[0,\zeta_{N}\wedge T]
for each
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and
T>0
.
Correspondingly, for each
T>0
, the law
\hat{\mu}
of
(\hat{U},\hat{W})
is equal to
\hat{\mu}^{N}

for each
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, and the stochastic basis

\hat{\mathcal{S}}=(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\mathcal{F}},\{\hat{\mathcal{F}}\}_{t\geqslant 0},\hat{\mathbb{P}},\hat{W})
can be similarly obtained
by extending each
\hat{\mathcal{F}}^{N}
to
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
.
Since each
(\hat{\mathcal{S}}^{N},\hat{U}^{N},\hat{\rho}_{N})
is a local martingale solution of (

)
and
\hat{\rho}_{N}\leqslant\zeta_{N}
, then
(\hat{\mathcal{S}},\hat{U},\zeta_{N})=(\hat{\mathcal{S}}^{N},\hat{U}^{N},\zeta_{N})
is
also a local martingale solution.
Due to (
4.60
), we see that
(\hat{\mathcal{S}},\hat{U})
is a global martingale solution of (
2.13
).

Eventually, it is sufficient to show the claim (
4.60
).
We show it by contradiction and assume there is
\hat{\Omega}_{0}\subset\hat{\Omega}
with
\hat{\mathbb{P}}(\hat{\Omega}_{0})>0

such that
\zeta_{N}(\omega)
is bounded for all
\omega\in\hat{\Omega}_{0}
and

.
Observe that

\hat{\Omega}_{0}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}\hat{\Omega}_{k}\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{with }\hat{\Omega}_{k}:=\{\omega\in\hat{\Omega}_{0}:\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}\zeta_{N}(\omega)\leqslant k\}.

Surely, there exists

so that

\varepsilon_{0}:=\hat{\mathbb{P}}(\hat{\Omega}_{k_{0}})>0
.
Fix each
N\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
.
By the first definition in (
4.58
), we can pick a subsequence

of

so that

almost surely in
\hat{\Omega}_{k_{0}}
.
Then by the second definition in (

), we deduce that for some

,

\sup_{s\in[0,k_{0}+1]}\|\hat{U}^{n_{k_{i}}^{k_{i}},N_{k_{i}}}(s)\|_{2}\geqslant N\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{occurs almost surely on }\hat{\Omega}_{k_{0}}.

As a result, it follows from (
4.35
) and the embeddings that for
i=\mathbb{N}^{+}

and

,

\displaystyle N^{p}\varepsilon_{0}\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}_{k_{0}}}\sup_{s\in[0,k_{0}+1]}\|\hat{U}^{n_{k_{i}}^{k_{i}},N_{k_{i}}}(s)\|_{2}^{p}\hat{\mathbb{P}}(\omega)\leqslant\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in[0,k_{0}+1]}\|\hat{U}^{n_{k_{i}}^{k_{i}},N_{k_{i}}}(s)\|_{2}^{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle c_{m,p}\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in[0,k_{0}+1]}\|A^{m}\hat{U}^{n_{k_{i}}^{k_{i}},N_{k_{i}}}(s)\|^{p}\leqslant C^{\prime}_{9},

(4.61)

where

and

However, the

which contradicts the boundedness of the right hand.
The proof is finished.∎

Remark 4.7
.

The procedure we adopt in this subsection can be applied to much more examples of stochastic
partial differential equations with nonlinear forcing terms and
globally Lipschitz diffusion coefficients, to establish the global existence of martingale solutions.
The equation is required to be dissipative to some extent.
More importantly, we need not to be aware of the existence of global pathwise solutions here.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume that
\phi
satisfies (
2.7

Suppose
({\mathcal{S}},u^{(1)})
and
({\mathcal{S}},u^{(2)})

Define

\Omega_{0}=\{u^{(1)}(0)=u^{(2)}(0)\}.

(5.1)

Then
u^{(1)}
and
u^{(2)}
are indistinguishable on
\Omega_{0}
, i.e.,

\mathbb{P}\left((u^{(1)}(t)-u^{(2)}(t))\chi_{\Omega_{0}}=0;\,\mbox{for all }t\geqslant 0\right)=1.

(5.2)

Proof.

Define
V=u^{(1)}-u^{(2)}
and
\overline{V}=\chi_{\Omega_{0}}V
.
By Definition
2.2
, we know that

\overline{V}\in{\mathcal{C}}([0,\infty);H^{2m})\cap L_{\rm loc}^{2}(0,\infty;H^{2(m+1)})\mbox{ almost surely.}

Define a stopping time
\overline{\varrho}_{R}
such that

\overline{\varrho}_{R}=\inf\{t\geqslant 0:\|u^{(1)}(t)\|_{2m}+\|u^{(2)}(t)\|_{2m}>R\}.

Trivially, it can be seen that
\overline{\varrho}_{R}\rightarrow+\infty
, as
R\rightarrow+\infty
.
And the uniqueness follows immediately from the conclusion

\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in[0,T\wedge\overline{\varrho}_{R}]}\|\overline{V}(s)\|_{2m}^{p}=0.

(5.3)

Now we are devoted to proving (
5.3
).
Subtracting the equations (
3.1
) for
u^{(2)}
from that for
u^{(1)}
,
we get the following equation for
V
,

{\rm d}V+[A^{2}V+\Phi_{1}(u^{(1)},u^{(2)})]{\rm d}t=\Phi_{2}(u^{(1)},u^{(2)}){\rm d}W,

(5.4)

where

\Phi_{1}(u^{(1)},u^{(2)}):=\delta_{N}(\|u^{(1)}\|_{2})f(u^{(1)})-\delta_{N}(\|u^{(2)}\|_{2})f(u^{(2)}),

\Phi_{2}(u^{(1)},u^{(2)}):=\delta_{N}(\|u^{(1)}\|_{2})\phi(u^{(1)})-\delta_{N}(\|u^{(2)}\|_{2})\phi(u^{(2)}).

Apply
A^{m}
to (
5.4
).
It yields from Itô’s Formula for

that
for each fixed

and
\tau^{\prime}
,
\tau^{\prime\prime}
with

,
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\|A^{m}V(s)\|^{p}+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{s}\|A^{m}V\|^{p-2}\|A^{m+1}V\|^{2}{\rm d}s^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\|A^{m}V(\tau^{\prime})\|^{p}-\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{s}\|A^{m}V\|^{p-2}\left(A^{m}V,A^{m}\Phi_{1}(u^{(1)},u^{(2)})\right){\rm d}s^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\frac{p-1}{2}\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{s}\|A^{m}V\|^{p-2}\|A^{m}\Phi_{2}(u^{(1)},u^{(2)})\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathfrak{U},H)}{\rm d}s^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{s}\|A^{m}V\|^{p-2}\left(A^{m}V,A^{m}\Phi_{2}(u^{(1)},u^{(2)}){\rm d}W\right)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\|A^{m}V(\tau^{\prime})\|^{p}+M^{\prime}_{1}+M^{\prime}_{2}+M^{\prime}_{3}.

(5.5)

Noting that
\delta_{N}
is globally Lipschitz (depending on

) and similar to (
4.48
),
we deduce that

\displaystyle|M^{\prime}_{1}|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{s}\|A^{m}V\|^{p-2}\left|\left(A^{m+1}V,(\delta_{N}(\|u^{(1)}\|_{2})-\delta_{N}(\|u^{(2)}\|_{2}))A^{m-1}f(u^{(1)})\right)\right|{\rm d}s^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{s}\|A^{m}V\|^{p-2}\left|\left(A^{m+1}V,A^{m-1}(f(u^{(1)})-f(u^{(2)}))\right)\right|{\rm d}s^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{2p-1}{2p}\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{s}\|A^{m}V\|^{p-2}\|A^{m+1}V\|^{2}{\rm d}s^{\prime}+c_{m,p}\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{s}\|A^{m}V\|^{p}\|A^{m-1}f(u^{(1)})\|^{2}{\rm d}s^{\prime}

\displaystyle+c\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{s}\|A^{m}V\|^{p}{\rm d}s^{\prime}+c_{p}\!\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{s}\|A^{m}V\|^{p-2}\left\|A^{m-1}\!\left(b\nabla V\cdot\nabla(u^{(1)}+u^{(2)})\right)\right\|^{2}{\rm d}s^{\prime}

\displaystyle+c_{p}\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{s}\|A^{m}V\|^{p-2}\left\|A^{m-1}\left(V\left((u^{(1)})^{2}+u^{(1)}u^{(2)}+(u^{(2)})^{2}\right)\right)\right\|^{2}{\rm d}s^{\prime}.

For the norm

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{E}\|A^{m}\overline{V}(\tau^{\prime})\|^{p}+c_{m,p}\mathbb{E}\int_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\tau^{\prime\prime}}\|A^{m}\overline{V}\|^{p}\left(1+\|u^{(1)}\|_{2m}^{4}+\|u^{(2)}\|_{2m}^{4}\right){\rm d}s

By the claim we have

\displaystyle\|A^{m}u(t\wedge\tilde{\xi}_{r}^{m}(u_{0}))\|^{2}+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tilde{\xi}_{r}^{m}(u_{0})}{\rm e}^{\varsigma-s}\|A^{m+1}u\|^{2}{\rm d}\varsigma

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle c_{m}\left(\|A^{m}u_{0}\|^{2}+Q_{m}(u_{0})t\wedge\tilde{\xi}_{r}^{m}(u_{0})\right){\rm e}^{-t\wedge\tilde{\xi}_{r}^{m}(u_{0})}

\displaystyle+\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{m}+\Theta_{m}(t\wedge\tilde{\xi}_{r}^{m}(u_{0})).

(6.34)

Now let
R>0
and define

{\mathcal{D}}_{R}:=\{\omega\in\Omega:\|A^{m}u_{0}\|^{2}+Q_{m}(u_{0})\leqslant R\}.

Similarly multiply (
6.34
) with
\chi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{R}}
and take the expected value of
the obtained inequality.
One sees that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|A^{m}u(t\wedge\tilde{\xi}^{m}_{r}(u_{0}))\|^{2}\chi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{R}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle c_{m}\mathbb{E}\left(\|A^{m}u_{0}\|^{2}+Q_{m}(u_{0})t\wedge\tilde{\xi}_{r}^{m}(u_{0})\right){\rm e}^{-t\wedge\tilde{\xi}_{r}^{m}(u_{0})}\chi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{R}}+\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{m}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle c_{m}\mathbb{E}\left(\|A^{m}u_{0}\|^{2}+Q_{m}(u_{0})\right)\chi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{R}}+\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{m}.

(6.35)

Fix
R>0
, use the Dominated Convergence Theorem, and let
r
tend to the infinity.
We obtain that

\mathbb{E}\|A^{m}u(t)\|^{2}\chi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{R}}\leqslant c_{m}\mathbb{E}[\|A^{m}u_{0}\|^{2}+Q_{m}(u_{0})t]\chi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{R}}{\rm e}^{-t}+\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{m}

For each fixed
R>0
, we have a
t^{\prime}_{R}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\|A^{m}u(t)\|^{2}\chi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{R}}\leqslant 2\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{m}.

(6.36)

Again, using the property of invariant measures, we infer that as
R\rightarrow+\infty
,

\mathbb{E}\|A^{m}u(t)\|^{2}\chi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{R}}=\int_{H^{2m}}\|A^{m}u\|^{2}\chi_{u(t^{\prime}_{R};{\mathcal{D}}_{R})}\nu({\rm d}u)\rightarrow\int_{H^{2m}}\|A^{2m}u\|^{2}\nu({\rm d}u).

Since
\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{m}
is independent of
R
, we set
\tilde{\rho}_{m}=2\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{m}
and
R\rightarrow+\infty

in (
6.36
) and get (
6.28
).
∎

We now can present the tightness of
{\mathcal{I}}
as follows.

Lemma 6.10
.

The invariant measure set
{\mathcal{I}}
is tight.

Proof.

According to the definition, for every
\varepsilon>0
, we look for a compact subset

\overline{\bf B}_{2(m+1)}({\mathcal{R}})
with a sufficiently large
{\mathcal{R}}
such that

\nu(H^{2m}\setminus\overline{\bf B}_{2(m+1)}({\mathcal{R}}))<\varepsilon\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{for all }\nu\in{\mathcal{I}}.

(6.37)

Indeed, for some fixed
t>0
and by the invariance and Lemma
6.1
,
we have

\displaystyle\nu(H^{2m}\setminus\overline{\bf B}_{2(m+1)}({\mathcal{R}}))=\int_{H^{2m}}{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(\tilde{u},H^{2m}\setminus\overline{\bf B}_{2(m+1)}({\mathcal{R}}))\nu({\rm d}\tilde{u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{H^{2m}}\mathbb{P}(t\|A^{m+1}u(t,\tilde{u})\|^{2}>t{\mathcal{R}}^{2})\nu({\rm d}\tilde{u})\leqslant\int_{H^{2m}}\mathbb{P}\left(\eta_{t{\mathcal{R}}^{2}}(\tilde{u})<t\right)\nu({\rm d}\tilde{u})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{C_{5}}{t{\mathcal{R}}^{2}}\int_{H^{2m}}\left(\|\tilde{u}\|_{2m}^{2}+\|\tilde{u}\|^{2(2m+5)}+t\right)\nu({\rm d}\tilde{u}),

(6.38)

where
C_{5}=C_{5}(t)
is given by (
6.5
).
By Lemmas
6.8
and
6.9
, we know the integral with respect to
\nu
over
H^{2m}

in (
6.38
) is bounded only by a deterministic positive constant depending on
t
, i.e.,

\nu(H^{2m}\setminus\overline{\bf B}_{2(m+1)}({\mathcal{R}}))\leqslant\frac{C^{\prime}_{12}(t)}{t{\mathcal{R}}^{2}}.

Thus, for each
\varepsilon>0
, if we choose
{\mathcal{R}}
sufficiently large, we are able to ensure (
6.37
)
and complete the proof.
∎

Now we are prepared to verify the existence of ergodic invariant measures.

Theorem 6.11
.

Suppose that
|b|<4
and
\phi
satisfies (
2.3
) and (
2.4
).
Then there exists an ergodic invariant measure on
H^{2m}

for the Feller Markovian semigroup
{\mathcal{P}}_{t}
.

Proof.

We adopt a similar routine to
[
45
, Theorem IV.13]
to address this proof.
Since an invariant measure is ergodic if and only if it is an extreme point of
{\mathcal{I}}

(see
[
8
, Proposition 11.12]
),
we only need to show the existence of extreme points of
{\mathcal{I}}
,
for which, it suffices to show that
{\mathcal{I}}
is nonempty, compact and convex,
by the Krein-Milman Theorem (see
[
7
, Theorem 7.4, Chapter V]
).

The existence of invariant measures implies that
{\mathcal{I}}\neq\emptyset
.
The linearity of
{\mathcal{P}}^{*}_{t}
with respect to
\nu
implies that
{\mathcal{I}}
is convex.
For the compactness, we first show that
{\mathcal{I}}
is closed.

Let
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a given weakly convergent sequence in
{\mathcal{I}}

such that
\nu_{n}
weakly converges to
\nu
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
By the invariance, we know that for all
\Gamma\in{\mathcal{B}}(H^{2m})
,
when
n
tends to the infinity,

\displaystyle\nu(\Gamma)\leftarrow\nu_{n}(\Gamma)={\mathcal{P}}^{*}_{t}\nu_{n}(\Gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{H}{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(u,\Gamma)\nu_{n}({\rm d}u)\rightarrow\int_{H}{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(u,\Gamma)\nu({\rm d}u)={\mathcal{P}}^{*}_{t}\nu(\Gamma).

By the uniqueness of weak limit, we know that
\nu
is also an invariant measure.
Hence the closedness.

The closedness and Lemma
6.10
guarantee the compactness of
{\mathcal{I}}
,
which eventually proves this theorem.
∎

7
Infinite Regularity of Invariant Measures

In this section we are to give the infinite regularity of the invariant measures obtained
in Section
6
.
Let
m\geqslant 1
and
\nu
be the invariant measure for the Markovian semigroup
{\mathcal{P}}_{t}
on
H^{2m}

given in Theorem
6.7
.
First we increase the regularity of
\nu
to the smoother space
H^{2(m+1)}
.

For this aim, recall the discussion in Section
6
.
We note that if it could hold that (stated in Theorem
7.1
below)

\int_{H^{2m}}\|u\|_{2(m+1)}^{2}\nu({\rm d}u)<+\infty,

(7.1)

we can conclude
a posteriori
that the support of
\nu
is contained in
H^{2(m+1)}

(using the ideas in
[
2
,
19
]
).
Unfortunately, this can not come true in the way presented
in the proof of Lemma
6.9
,
since
A^{m+1}u(t)
makes no sense at
t=0
.
However, we can make it by reusing the method given in the proof of Lemma
6.1
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
|b|<4
and
\phi
satisfy (
2.3
) and (
2.4
).
Then (
7.1
) holds true.
Moreover, the support of
\nu
is contained in
H^{2(m+1)}
.

Proof.

We only show (
7.1
).
Similar to the estimation in the proof of Lemma
6.1
,
we apply Itô’s Formula to
\|A^{m+1}u\|^{2}
and deduce like
the argument in the proof of Lemma
6.8
that
for stopping times
0\leqslant\varsigma\leqslant s\leqslant t\wedge\tilde{\xi}_{r}^{m+1}(u_{0})
,
with
\tilde{\xi}_{r}^{m+1}(u_{0})
defined for each
r>0
as

\tilde{\xi}_{r}^{m+1}(u_{0}):=\inf\left\{t\geqslant 0:\inf_{0\leqslant k^{\prime}\leqslant m+1}\|A^{k^{\prime}}u(t)\|^{2}>r\right\}.

it holds that

\displaystyle\|A^{m+1}u(s)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\|A^{m+1}u(\varsigma)\|^{2}{\rm e}^{\varsigma-s}+c\int_{\varsigma}^{s}{\rm e}^{s^{\prime}-s}\left(\|A^{m+1}u\|^{2}+\|u\|^{2(2m+5)}+1\right){\rm d}s^{\prime}

\displaystyle+2\int_{\varsigma}^{s}{\rm e}^{s^{\prime}-s}(A^{m+1}u,A^{m+1}\phi(u){\rm d}W).

(7.2)

Integrating (
7.2
) over
\varsigma\in[0,s]
and recalling (
6.29
)
and the claim (
6.30
), we infer that

\displaystyle s\|A^{m+1}u(s)\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\|A^{m+1}u(\varsigma)\|^{2}{\rm e}^{\varsigma-s}{\rm d}\varsigma+cs\int_{0}^{s}{\rm e}^{s^{\prime}-s}\left(\|A^{m+1}u\|^{2}+\|u\|^{2(2m+5)}+1\right){\rm d}s^{\prime}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\varsigma}^{s}{\rm e}^{s^{\prime}-s}(A^{m+1}u,A^{m+1}\phi(u){\rm d}W(s^{\prime})){\rm d}\varsigma

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle(1+cs)\left(c_{m}(\|A^{m}u_{0}\|^{2}+Q_{m}(u_{0})s){\rm e}^{-s}+\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{m}+\Theta_{m}(s)\right)

\displaystyle+cs\int_{0}^{s}{\rm e}^{s^{\prime}-s}\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2(2m+5)}{\rm e}^{-s^{\prime}}+\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{0,2(2m+5)}+1\right){\rm d}s^{\prime}

\displaystyle+c_{m}s\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{s^{\prime}}{\rm e}^{\varsigma^{\prime}-s}\|u\|^{2(2m+4)}(u,\phi(u){\rm d}W(\varsigma^{\prime})){\rm d}s^{\prime}

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{s}s^{\prime}{\rm e}^{s^{\prime}-s}(A^{m+1}u,A^{m+1}\phi(u){\rm d}W(s^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle c_{m}(1+s)\left(\|A^{m}u_{0}\|^{2}+Q_{m+1}(u_{0})s\right){\rm e}^{-s}+c(1+s)\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{m+1}+\Theta_{m+1}(s),

(7.3)

where
Q_{m+1}(u_{0})
is given in (
6.31
),

\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{m+1}=\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{m}+\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{0,2(2m+5)}+1\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\Theta_{m+1}(s)=

\displaystyle\left(1+cs\right)\Theta_{m}(s)+c_{m}s\int_{0}^{s}(s-\varsigma^{\prime}){\rm e}^{\varsigma^{\prime}-s}\|u\|^{2(2m+4)}(u,\phi(u){\rm d}W(\varsigma^{\prime}))

\displaystyle+2\int_{0}^{s}s^{\prime}{\rm e}^{s^{\prime}-s}(A^{m+1}u,A^{m+1}\phi(u){\rm d}W(s^{\prime})).

Similar to the proof of Lemmas
6.8
and
6.9
, we again define

{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{R}}=\{\omega\in\Omega:\|A^{m}u_{0}\|^{2}+\|u_{0}\|^{2(2m+5)}\leqslant{\mathcal{R}}\}.

Picking
s=t\wedge\tilde{\xi}_{r}^{m+1}(u_{0})
in (
7.3
),
multiplying the obtained inequality by
\chi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{R}}}

and taking the expectation of what is just obtained,
we annihilate the stochastic term
\Theta_{m+1}(t\wedge\tilde{\xi}_{r}^{m+1}(u_{0}))
and easily obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(t\wedge\tilde{\xi}_{r}^{m+1}(u_{0})\right)\|A^{m+1}u(t\wedge\tilde{\xi}_{r}^{m+1}(u_{0}))\|^{2}\chi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{R}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle c_{m}\mathbb{E}(\|A^{m}u_{0}\|^{2}+Q_{m+1}(u_{0}))+c(1+t)\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{m+1},

(7.4)

by the boundedness of
s^{k}{\rm e}^{-s}
with
s\geqslant 0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
.
Fix
{\mathcal{R}}>0
. Using the Dominated Convergence Theorem to (
7.4
) and considering
r\rightarrow+\infty
,
we can obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|A^{m+1}u(t)\|^{2}\chi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{R}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle c_{m}\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right){\rm e}^{-t}\mathbb{E}\left(\|A^{m}u_{0}\|^{2}+Q_{m+1}(u_{0})t\right)\chi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{R}}}+c\left(1+\frac{1}{t}\right)\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{m+1}.

Given each fixed
{\mathcal{R}}>0
, it is easy to know that there exists
t_{{\mathcal{R}}}>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\|A^{m+1}u(t)\|^{2}\chi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{R}}}\leqslant 2c\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{m+1}.

(7.5)

Then as
{\mathcal{R}}\rightarrow+\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|A^{m+1}u(t)\|^{2}\chi_{{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{R}}}=

\displaystyle\int_{H^{2m}}\|A^{m+1}u\|^{2}\chi_{u(t_{\mathcal{R}};{\mathcal{D}}_{\mathcal{R}})}\nu({\rm d}u)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\int_{H^{2m}}\|A^{m+1}u\|^{2}\nu({\rm d}u).

Owing to the independence of
{\mathcal{R}}
for
\tilde{\rho}^{\prime}_{m+1}
, we immediately obtain (
7.1
)
by letting
{\mathcal{R}}\rightarrow+\infty
in (
7.5
). The proof is complete.
∎

8
Summary and Remarks

In this paper, we aim to obtain the existence and regularity of invariant measures for the stochastic
MSHE with multiplicative noise and periodic boundary.
To this end, we first confirm the unique existence of global pathwise solutions,
and by Yamada-Wannabe theorem, we are also required to provide the existence of martingale solutions
in advance.
With a further study, we also obtain the global existence of martingale solutions.

In our situation, the concept of global martingale solution is stronger a bit than that in a previous
work
[
37
]
, and we use a new method to obtain this kind of global martingale solutions.
Moreover, our new method can be applied extensively to other stochastic nonlinear partial differential
equations to get a global martingale solution, once the drift is dissipative somehow and the diffusion
coefficient is globally Lipschitz, or more weakly, the solutions coincide locally in time for Galerkin approximating
cut-off systems with different cut-off functions.
However, we are still unaware whether the global martingale solution is unique.

In the proof of existence of ergodic invariant measures in
H^{2m}
, we actually follow the estimation
methods used frequently in attractor theory to study the existence of the global attractor in spaces of
high regularity (see
[
41
,
53
,
50
]
).
Correspondingly, to improve the regularity of the invariant measures, we also use the
regularity estimation methods for deterministic systems.
All in all, we eventually obtain the infinite regularity of invariant measures for stochastic MSHE with
multiplicative noise and periodic boundary under appropriate conditions on the diffusion coefficient
\phi
,
which is just what Glatt-Holtz expected in
[
19
]
for the stochastic primitive equation.

Combining the research on invariant measures and attractors
in Chen et al.
[
3
,
4
]
and Huang et al.
[
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
,
we have some ideas about the relation between invariant measures for stochastic systems
and attractors for deterministic systems as follows.

Consider the following abstract deterministic differential equation

u_{t}=Au+f(u),\hskip 22.76228ptt>0,

(8.1)

and the corresponding stochastic differential equation with multiplicative noise

{\rm d}u=[Au+f(u)]{\rm d}t+\phi(u){\rm d}W,\hskip 22.76228ptt>0,

(8.2)

where
u:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow X
is the unknown function,
X
is a Hilbert (or Banach) space,

A:{\mathcal{D}}(A)\subset X\rightarrow X
is linear operator,
f
is the external force,
\phi(u){\rm d}W
is
the stochastic term and
W
is a Wiener process.

Firstly, we conjecture that,
if (
8.1
) possesses a unique global strong solution
in
X
for each initial datum, then (
8.2
) possesses a unique global pathwise solution
for a given stochastic basis under some appropriate global Lipschitz condition on
\phi
.

Secondly, we conjecture that,
if the dynamical system generated by (
8.1
) has
a global attractor
{\mathscr{A}}
in
X
, then the Markovian transition semigroup associated with
(
8.2
) has an invariant measure
\mu
on
X
under some appropriate global Lipschitz
condition on
\phi
.
Moreover,
if
{\mathscr{A}}
is contained in a subspace
Y
with higher regularity of
X
,
then
\mu
is supported on
Y
as well, under some appropriate global Lipschitz condition
on
\phi
.
Thirdly, if the conjectures mentioned above hold true, perhaps the converse of
these conclusions are also hopeful to be valid under some appropriate conditions.

The proof of these conjectures may need very elaborate investigations, but at least,
there should be many examples indicating these conclusions.
What is more, we are also interested in the existence of (ergodic) invariant measures
when the drift is not dissipative, for example, when
|b|\geqslant 4
for the stochastic
MSHE (
1.1
).

Following

Our main interest is

\leq 4

{\mathbb{P}}^{4}

It is a quintic hypersurface.

The universal deformation of a rank 3 quadric is given by

[formula]

Let

3
or
4

Note that

has codimension 5.

Let
U

(L^{2}\subset Q)
where
L^{2}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
is a 2-plane and
Q\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{4}
a quadric. Note that this forces
\operatorname{rank}Q\leq 4

{\mathbb{P}}^{8}

Let

These quadrics split into 2 hyperplanes, one of which must contain

So

\operatorname{Grass}(2,4)

Set

Since

In particular,

H^{0}(U^{\circ},{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}},\ H^{1}(U^{\circ},{\mathbb{Z}})\cong 0,\ H^{2}(U^{\circ},{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{2},\ H^{3}(U^{\circ},{\mathbb{Z}})\cong 0.

Now look at the morphism
p_{2}:U\to Z
with Stein factorization

U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau}}{{\longrightarrow}}Z.

In the coordinates (
1

Z\cong(z_{2}^{2}-4z_{1}z_{3}=0)\times{\mathbb{A}}^{11}

Set

w_{3}:=\sqrt{z_{3}}

[formula]

Thus

Claim 2
.

H^{3}(W^{\circ},{\mathbb{Z}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2

Proof. Let

We can think of

Thus the image of

(Note that this splitting of

In the Leray spectral sequence for

As we noted, the kernel is

and

Thus

H^{2}\bigl(W^{\circ},{\mathbb{Z}}\bigr)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}

Let

\pi:Y\to X

-bundle (étale locally trivial).
For a smooth curve

[formula]

\sigma_{Y}(C):=(C_{Y}\cdot K_{Y_{C}/C})\mod 2.

This is well defined as a function on

If

, then

Conversely, assume that we are over

In any case, we get the following.

Claim 9

If there is a numerically trivial 1-cycle

13
Another representation of

.

Let
P_{5}
be a general 5-dimensional linear system of quadrics on
P_{4}:={\mathbb{P}}^{4}

For

, we have the projections
\pi_{i}:P_{4}\times P_{5}\to P_{i}
. For brevity let us write
(a,b):=a\pi_{4}^{*}H_{4}+b\pi_{5}^{*}H_{5}
, where

Set

Y:=\bigl\{(p,Q):p\in P_{4},Q\in P_{5},p\in\operatorname{Sing}Q\bigr\}\subset P_{4}\times P_{5}.

The condition

is equivalent to the partial derivatives of the equation of

vanishing at
p
. Thus
Y\subset P_{4}\times P_{5}
is the complete intersection of 5 divisors of bidegree
(1,1)
. Write these as

[formula]

\textstyle{\sum}_{i=0}^{4}\textstyle{\sum}_{j=0}^{5}a^{\ell}_{ij}y_{i}x_{j}\quad\mbox{for}\quad\ell=1,\dots,5.

Over
P_{5}
, (
13
.1) is equivalent to a
5\times 5
symmetric matrix
whose entries are the linear forms
m_{i}^{\ell}=\textstyle{\sum}_{j=0}^{5}a^{\ell}_{ij}x_{j}
.
The condition
\det(m_{i}^{\ell})=0
defines
X_{Z}
as in Paragraph
3
.

Over
P_{4}
, (
13
.1) is equivalent to a
6\times 5
matrix
whose entries are the linear forms
n_{j}^{\ell}=\textstyle{\sum}_{i=0}^{4}a^{\ell}_{ij}y_{i}
.
Note that
\pi_{4}:Y\to P_{4}
is birational. Its inverse is the blow-up
of a surface

S\subset P_{4},\quad\mbox{defined by }\quad\operatorname{rank}(n_{j}^{\ell})\leq 4.

Let

denote the exceptional divisor.

Y
defines a rational map
P_{4}\dasharrow X_{Z}\subset P_{5}
, which is given by the

subdeterminants of

. Thus

.

The inverse rational map
X_{Z}\dasharrow P_{4}
is a bit harder to see.
It is given by a linear system of divisors as follows.
Let
H\in|H_{4}|
be a hyperplane and set

D_{H}:=\bigl\{Q\in X_{Z}:H\cap\operatorname{Sing}Q\neq\emptyset\bigr\}\subset X_{Z}.

Note that the condition
H\cap\operatorname{Sing}Q
is equivalent to

Q|_{H}
being singular. (Here we need that
Q
itself is singular.)
This gives us the equation
\det(Q|_{H})=0
for

. It has degree 4.

We claim that the intersection of
\left(\det(Q|_{H})=0\right)
with
X_{Z}
has multiplicity 2.
To see this, choose coordinates such that
H=(x_{0}=0)
and

Q=(x_{0}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}=0)
. For its deformations we can make linear coordinate changes to the

, but

can only be multiplied by a constant. Thus we get a miniversal deformation family

\bigl(x_{0}^{2}+t_{1}x_{0}x_{1}+t_{2}x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}=0\bigr)\subset P_{5}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{2}_{t_{1},t_{2}}.

For a given

4
iff
t_{1}^{2}-4t_{2}=0
, and the singular point is on
(x_{0}=0)
iff
t_{2}=0
. Their intersection is the length 2 scheme
(t_{1}^{2}=0)
.

Thus the

and

In particular, the divisor class

is 2-torsion in the class group

The corresponding double cover is our

The degree of

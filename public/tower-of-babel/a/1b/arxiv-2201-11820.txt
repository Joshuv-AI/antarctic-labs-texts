On the number of p p -hypergeometric solutions of KZ equations
Alexander Varchenko

Abstract
. It is known that solutions of the KZ equations can be written in the form of multidimensional hypergeometric integrals. In 2017 in a joint paper of the author with V. Schechtman the construction of hypergeometric solutions was modified, and solutions of the KZ equations modulo a prime number p p were constructed. These solutions modulo p p , called the p p -hypergeometric solutions, are polynomials with integer coefficients. A general problem is to determine the number of independent p p -hypergeometric solutions and understand the meaning of that number. In this paper we consider the KZ equations associated with the space of singular vectors of weight n − 2 ​ r n-2r in the tensor power W ⊗ n W^{\otimes n} of the vector representation of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 2 \mathfrak{sl}_{2} . In this case the hypergeometric solutions of the KZ equations are given by r r -dimensional hypergeometric integrals. We consider the module of the corresponding p p -hypergeometric solutions, determine its rank, and show that the rank equals the dimension of the space of suitable square integrable differential r r -forms.

1.
Introduction

The Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov (
KZ
)
equations is a system of differential equations for conformal blocks in conformal field theory, see
[
KZ
]
.
Versions of
KZ
equations appear in mathematical physics, representation theory, enumerative geometry, algebraic geometry,
theory of special functions, see for example
[
EFK
,
MO
,
B
]
.

It is known that solutions of the
KZ
equations can be written in the form of multidimensional hypergeometric
integrals, see
[
SV1
]
. Relatively recently, the construction of these
hypergeometric solutions was modified,
and solutions of the
KZ
equations modulo a prime number
p
were constructed in
[
SV2
]
.
These solutions modulo
p
, called the
p
-hypergeometric solutions, are vector-valued
polynomials with integer coefficients.
The general problem is
to understand how these polynomials reflect the remarkable properties of the
KZ
equations and their
hypergeometric solutions.

In this paper we address the particular problem to determine the number of independent
p
-hypergeometric solutions and understand the meaning of that number.
We consider the
KZ
equations associated with the space of singular
vectors of weight
n-2r
in the tensor power

W^{\otimes n}
of the vector representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. In this case the hypergeometric solutions of the
KZ
equations
are given by
r
-dimensional hypergeometric integrals. We consider the
module of the corresponding
p
-hypergeometric solutions, determine its rank,
and show that the rank equals the dimension of the space of suitable square integrable differential
r
-forms.

The KZ equations depend on a parameter
q\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
. In this paper we assume that

q
is a prime number less than
p
, and the pair
(p,q)
satisfies
certain conditions (the pair is of type 1).

On
p
-hypergeometric solutions and, more generally, on the solutions of the KZ equations modulo
p^{s}
see

[
SlV
,
V4
,
V5
,
V6
,
V7
,
V8
,
VZ1
,
VZ2
]
.

In Section
2
we define the KZ equations, recall the construction of solutions
in the form of hypergeometric integrals and the construction of
p
-hypergeometric solutions. We also
introduce the module of
p
-hypergeometric solutions.
The main result of the paper is Theorem
3.5
,
formulated in Section
3
and proved in Section
4
.
Theorem
3.5

determines the rank of the module of
p
-hypergeometric solutions.

In Section
5

we construct a suitable Cartier map, which relates
the hypergeometric solutions and
p
-hypergeometric solutions. As a result
of this construction,
we interpret the rank of the module of
p
-hypergeometric solutions as the dimension
of some vector space of square integrable differential
r
-forms on
\mathbb{P}^{r}
,
the
r
-th direct power of the complex projective line.
Previously this result was known for
r=1
, see
[
SlV
]
.

2.
{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}

KZ
equations

2.1.
Definition of equations

Consider the complex Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
with generators
e,f,h
and relations
[e,f]=h,\,[h,e]=2e,\,[h,f]=-2f
.
Consider the complex vector space
W
with basis
w_{1},w_{2}
and the
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
-action,

\displaystyle e=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0&\end{pmatrix},\qquad f=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0&\end{pmatrix},\qquad h=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1&\end{pmatrix}.

The
{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
-module
W^{\otimes n}
has a basis labeled by subsets
J\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
,

\displaystyle V_{J}=w_{j_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes w_{j_{n}},

where
j_{i}=1
if
j_{i}\notin J
and

j_{i}=2
if
j_{i}\in J
.

Consider the weight decomposition of
W^{\otimes n}

into eigenspaces of
h
,

W^{\otimes n}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
.
The vectors
V_{J}
with
|J|=r
form a basis of
W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
.
Denote
{\mathcal{J}_{r}}
the set of all
r
-element subsets of
\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Define the space of singular vectors of weight
n-2r
,

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Sing}\,}W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]=\{w\in W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]\mid ew=0\}.

This space is nonempty if and only if
n\geqslant 2r
. We assume that
n\geqslant 2r
.
Then

\displaystyle\dim{\operatorname{Sing}\,}W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]=\dim W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]-\dim W^{\otimes n}[n-2r+2]=\binom{n}{r}-\binom{n}{r-1}.

Let
w=\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{J}_{r}}}c_{J}V_{J}\in W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
. Then
w\in{\operatorname{Sing}\,}W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
,
if and only if its coefficients satisfy the system of linear equations labeled
by
r-1
-element subsets
K\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\notin K}c_{K\cup\{j\}}\,=\,0.

Define the Casimir element

\Omega=\frac{1}{2}h\otimes h+e\otimes f+f\otimes e\,\in\,{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}\otimes{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
,
and the linear operators on
W^{\otimes n}
depending on parameters
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
,

\displaystyle H_{m}(z)=\sum_{j=1\atop j\neq m}^{n}\frac{\Omega_{mj}}{z_{m}-z_{j}}\,,\qquad m=1,\dots,n,

where

\Omega_{mj}:W^{\otimes n}\to W^{\otimes n}
is the Casimir operator acting in the
m
th and
j
th tensor factors.
The operators
H_{m}(z)
are called the Gaudin Hamiltonians. Denote

\displaystyle\partial_{m}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{m}}\,,\qquad m=1,\dots,n.

For any nonzero number
q\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
and
1\leqslant m,l\leqslant n
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle[q\partial_{m}-H_{m}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}),q\partial_{l}-H_{l}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})]=0,

and for any
x\in{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}
and
m=1,\dots,n,
we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle[H_{m}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}),x\otimes 1\otimes\dots\otimes 1+\dots+1\otimes\dots\otimes 1\otimes x]=0.

The system of differential equations

(2.4)

\displaystyle(q\partial_{m}-H_{m}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}))\tilde{I}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}),\qquad m=1,\dots,n,

on a
W^{\otimes n}
-valued function

\tilde{I}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
is called the
KZ
equations, see
[
KZ
,
EFK
]
.

By property (
2.3
) the Gaudin Hamiltonians preserve every space

{\operatorname{Sing}\,}W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
. Hence the system of
KZ
equations can be considered with values in
any particular space

{\operatorname{Sing}\,}W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
.

2.2.
Gauge transformation

If
\tilde{I}(z)
satisfies the
KZ
equations (
2.4
), then the function
I(z)
, defined by

(2.5)

\displaystyle\tilde{I}(z)=I(z)\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(z_{i}-z_{j})^{1/2q}\,,

satisfies the equations

(2.6)

\displaystyle\Big(q\partial_{m}-\sum_{j=1\atop j\neq m}^{n}\frac{\Omega_{mj}-1/2}{z_{m}-z_{j}}\Big)I(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}),\qquad m=1,\dots,n,

which we also call the
KZ
equations.

The linear operator
\Omega-\frac{1}{2}:W^{\otimes 2}\to W^{\otimes 2}
acts as follows:

(2.7)

\displaystyle w_{1}\otimes w_{1}\mapsto 0,\qquad\phantom{aaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa}w_{2}\otimes w_{2}\mapsto 0,

\displaystyle w_{1}\otimes w_{2}\mapsto-w_{1}\otimes w_{2}+w_{2}\otimes w_{1},\qquad w_{2}\otimes w_{1}\mapsto w_{1}\otimes w_{2}-w_{2}\otimes w_{1}.

We shall consider the
KZ
equations (
2.6
) with values in
{\operatorname{Sing}\,}W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]

over the field of complex numbers, and we shall also consider
the
KZ
equations (
2.6
) modulo
p
, where
p
is a
prime number.

2.3.
Solutions of
KZ
equations over
{\mathbb{C}}

Denote
t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})
.
Define the master function

(2.8)

\displaystyle\Phi(t,z)=\prod_{1\leqslant i\leqslant j\leqslant r}(t_{i}-t_{j})^{2/q}\prod_{s=1}^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{r}(t_{i}-z_{s})^{-1/q}.

For any function
F(t_{1},\dots,t_{r})
, denote

\operatorname{Sym}_{t}[F(t_{1},\dots,t_{r})]=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{r}}\!F(t_{\sigma_{1}},\dots,t_{\sigma_{r}})
.
For
J=\{j_{1},\dots,j_{r}\}\in{\mathcal{J}_{r}}
define the weight function

\displaystyle W_{J}(t,z)=\operatorname{Sym}_{t}\Big[\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{t_{i}-z_{j_{i}}}\Big].

For example,

\displaystyle W_{\{3\}}=\frac{1}{t_{1}-z_{3}},\qquad W_{\{4,5\}}=\frac{1}{t_{1}-z_{4}}\frac{1}{t_{2}-z_{5}}+\frac{1}{t_{2}-z_{4}}\frac{1}{t_{1}-z_{5}}\,.

The function

(2.9)

\displaystyle W(t,z)=\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{J}_{r}}}W_{J}(t,z)V_{J}

is called the
W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
-weight vector-function.

Consider the
W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
-valued
function

(2.10)

\displaystyle I^{(\gamma)}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\int_{\gamma(z)}\Phi(t,z)\,W(t,z)\,dt_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dt_{r},

where
\gamma(z)
in
\{z\}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{r}_{t}
is a horizontal family of

r
-dimensional cycles of the twisted homology defined by the multivalued
function
\Phi(t,z)
, see
[
CF
,
SV1
,
DJMM
,
V1
,
V3
]
.
The cycles
\gamma(z)
are
r
-dimensional analogs of Pochhammer double loops.

Theorem 2.1
.

The function
I^{(\gamma)}(z)
takes values in

{\operatorname{Sing}\,}W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]

and satisfies the
KZ
equations (
2.6
).

This theorem and its generalizations can be found, for example, in
[
CF
,
DJMM
,
SV1
]
.

The solutions in Theorem
2.1
are called the
hypergeometric solutions of the
KZ
equations.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
V1
, Theorem 12.5.5]
)
.

If
q\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
is generic, then all solutions of the
KZ
equations (
2.6
) have this form.

For special values of the parameter
q
the space of
the hypergeometric solutions of the
KZ
equations may span only
a proper subspace of the space of all solutions,
see in
[
FSV1
,
FSV2
]
the discussion of
the relations of this subspace of solutions and conformal blocks in conformal field theory.

2.4.
p
-integrals

Let
p
be a positive integer. Let

f(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})=\sum_{d_{1},\dots,d_{k}}c_{d_{1},\dots,d_{k}}t_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots t_{k}^{d_{k}}

be a polynomial. Let
l=(l_{1},\dots,

l_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}^{r}
. The coefficient

c_{l_{1}p-1,\dots,l_{r}p-1}
will be called the
p
-integral of
f(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})

over the cycle
\{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}\}_{p}

and is denoted by

\displaystyle\int_{\{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}\}_{p}}f(t_{1},\dots,t_{k})\,dt_{1}\dots dt_{r}\,.

2.5.
KZ
equations modulo
p

Let
p
and
q
be prime numbers,
p>q
.
We consider the system of
KZ
equations (
2.6
) with parameter
q
modulo
p
.
Namely, we look for
W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
-valued polynomials in
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}

with integer coefficients, which satisfy system (
2.6
) modulo
p

and with values in the subspace
{\operatorname{Sing}\,}W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
modulo
p
.
More precisely, let
I(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{J}_{r}}}I_{J}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})V_{J}
with

I_{J}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z_{1},\dots,z_{n}]
. We request that

•

for any
m=1,\dots,n
, the rational function

(q\partial_{m}-H_{m}(z))I(z)
can be written as a ratio
of two polynomials with integer coefficients such that the denominator
is nonzero modulo
p
, while the numerator is zero modulo
p
;

•

the coefficients
I_{J}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
of the polynomial

I(z)
satisfy equations (
2.1
) modulo
p
.

A construction of such solutions was presented in
[
SV2
]
.

Let
M
,
c
be the least positive integers such that

(2.11)

\displaystyle M\equiv-\frac{1}{q},\qquad c\equiv\frac{2}{q}\ \ \pmod{p}.

Let
t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{r})
,
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
.
Define the master polynomial,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\Phi_{p}(t,z)=\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant r}(t_{i}-t_{j})^{c}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{s=1}^{n}(t_{i}-z_{s})^{M}.

Recall the weight vector-function
W(t,z)
in (
2.9
). The function

\Phi_{p}(t,z)W(t,z)
is a
W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
-valued polynomial in
t,z
.
Let
(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}^{r}
.
Denote

(2.13)

\displaystyle I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})}(z)=\int_{\{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}\}_{p}}\Phi_{p}(t,z)W(t,z)\,dt_{1}\dots dt_{r}\,.

This is a
W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
-valued polynomial in
z
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
SV2
]
)
.

For any positive integers
(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})
, the polynomial

I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})}(z)
is a solution of the
KZ
equations modulo
p
with values in

{\operatorname{Sing}\,}W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
modulo
p
.

We call the polynomials

I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})}(z)
the
p
-hypergeometric
solutions.

In this paper we determine the number of independent

p
-hypergeometric solutions under the assumption that
(p,q)
is a pair of type 1.

Remark
.

The symmetric group
S_{n}
acts on the polynomials in
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
with values in
W^{\otimes n}

by permuting the tensor factors and the variables simultaneously,

\displaystyle\sigma(p(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\,v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{n})=(p(z_{\sigma(1)},\dots,z_{\sigma(n)})\,v_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{\sigma^{-1}(n)},\qquad\sigma\in S_{n}\,.

It is clear that for any positive integers
(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})
, the polynomial

I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})}(z)
is symmetric with respect to the
S_{n}
-action.

2.6.
Module of
p
-hypergeometric solutions

Denote
{\mathbb{Z}}[z^{p}]={\mathbb{Z}}[z_{1}^{p},\dots,z_{n}^{p}]
.
Let

(2.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\,=\,\Big\{\sum_{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}f_{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}(z)I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})}(z)\mid f_{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}(z)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z^{p}]\Big\},

be the
{\mathbb{Z}}[z^{p}]
-module generated by the
p
-hypergeometric
solutions
I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})}(z)
of Theorem
2.3
.
Every element of
\mathcal{M}
is a solution of the
KZ
equations modulo
p
with values in

{\operatorname{Sing}\,}W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
modulo
p
. Indeed the
KZ
equations (
2.6
) are linear and

\frac{\partial z_{i}^{p}}{\partial z_{j}}\equiv 0
(mod
p
) for all
i,j
.

We say that two elements
I,I^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}
are equivalent and write
I\sim I^{\prime}
, if
I-I^{\prime}
is divisible by
p
.
Then the set
\mathcal{M}/(\sim)
of equivalence classes is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[z^{p}]
-module.

2.7.
More general choice of the numbers
M
and
c

For
s=1,\dots,n
fix a positive integer
M_{s}
such that

\displaystyle M_{s}\equiv-\frac{1}{q}\pmod{p}.

Fix a positive integer
c^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle c^{\prime}\equiv\frac{2}{q}\pmod{p}.

Define the master polynomial,

\displaystyle\Phi_{p}(t,z;\vec{M},c^{\prime})=\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant r}(t_{i}-t_{j})^{c^{\prime}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{s=1}^{n}(t_{i}-z_{s})^{M_{s}}.

Recall the weight vector-function
W(t,z)
in (
2.9
). The function

\Phi_{p}(t,z;\vec{M},c^{\prime})W(t,z)
is a
W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
-valued polynomial in
t,z
with integer coefficients.
Let
l=(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{>0}^{r}
.
Denote

\displaystyle I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})}(z;\vec{M},c^{\prime})=\int_{\{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}\}_{p}}\Phi_{p}(t,z;\vec{M},c^{\prime})W(t,z)\,dt_{1}\dots dt_{r}\,.

This is a
W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
-valued polynomial in
z
with integer coefficients.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
SV2
]
)
.

For any positive integers
(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})
, the polynomial

I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})}(z;\vec{M},c^{\prime})
is a solution of the
KZ
equations modulo
p
with values in

{\operatorname{Sing}\,}W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]
modulo
p
.

Theorem 2.5
.

For any positive integers
(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})
, the polynomial

I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})}(z;\vec{M},c^{\prime})
belongs to the module
\mathcal{M}
of
p
-hypergeometric solutions.

For
l=1
this statement follows from
[
SlV
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Proof.

Let
M,c
be defined in (
2.11
). Then
c^{\prime}=c+d_{0}p
,
M_{s}=M+d_{s}p
,
s=1,\dots,n
,
where
d_{0},\dots,d_{n}
are nonnegative integers. Then we have modulo
p
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{p}(t,z;\vec{M},c^{\prime})\equiv\Phi_{p}(t,z)\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant r}(t_{i}^{p}-t_{j}^{p})^{d_{0}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{s=1}^{n}(t_{i}^{p}-z_{s}^{p})^{d_{s}}\pmod{p}.

This formula implies the theorem.
∎

3.
Pairs
(p,q)
of type 1

3.1.
Numbers
M
and
c

Let
p,q
be prime numbers,
p>q
.
Let
k
be the minimal positive integer such that
q|(kp-1)
.
We have
1\leqslant k<q
.

We say that the pair
(p,q)
is of type 1 or type 2 if

(3.1)

\displaystyle 1\leqslant k\leqslant q/2\qquad\operatorname{or}\qquad q/2<k<q,

respectively.

The pairs
(p,2)
are all of type 1.

The pairs
(p,3)
are of type 1 if
p=3m+1
and
of type 2 if
p=3m+2
.

Let
p=mq+s
,
s\in\{1,\dots,q-1\}
. Then
the minimal positive integer
k
such that
q|(kp-1)

belongs to
\{1,\dots,q-1\}
and is determined by the property

ks\equiv 1\pmod{q}
. Hence
half of the values of
s
gives pairs

(p,q)
of type 1 and
half of the values of
s
gives pairs

(p,q)
of type 2.

In the rest of the paper
we always assume that
(p,q)
is of type 1.

Lemma 3.1
.

The integer
q-2k
is the least positive integer
m
such that
q|(mp+2)
.

Proof.

We have
q|(kp-1)
. Hence

q|(qp-2(kp-1))
, and
qp-2(kp-1)=(q-2k)p+2
. We also have
0\leqslant q-2k\leqslant q-2
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

The integers

(3.2)

\displaystyle M=\frac{kp-1}{q}\,,\qquad c=\frac{(q-2k)p+2}{q}

are the least positive integers satisfying the congruences (
2.11
).
The integer
c
is odd and

(3.3)

\displaystyle 2M+c=p.

∎

Example
.

If
q=2
, then
M=\frac{p-1}{2}
,
c=1
. The case
q=2
is the only case in which
c
does not depend on
p
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For positive integers
(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})

the polynomial
I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})}(z)
equals zero, if
l_{1},\dots,l_{r}
are not pairwise distinct.

Proof.

The integer
c
is odd. Hence the polynomial
\Phi_{p}(t,z)W(t,z)
is skew-symmetric with respect to permutations of

t_{1},\dots,t_{r}
.
∎

In what follows
we always assume

(3.4)

\displaystyle n=qg+2r-1

for some positive integer
g
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
q,k,g
be fixed. Let
p
be large enough so that

(3.5)

\displaystyle M-g=\frac{kp-1}{q}-g=\frac{kp-1-gq}{q}\geqslant 0\,.

Let
l_{1}>\cdots>l_{r}\geqslant 1
. Then the inequality

(3.6)

\displaystyle kg+r-1\geqslant l_{1}

is necessary for
I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})}(z)
to be nonzero.
Hence
there are at most
\binom{kg+r-1}{r}
tuples
(l_{1}>\dots>l_{r}\geqslant 1)
such that
I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})}(z)
is nonzero.

Proof.

For
i=1,\dots,r
we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\deg_{t_{i}}\Phi_{p}(t,z)W(t,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle nM+(r-1)c-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(gq+2r-1)\frac{kp-1}{q}+(r-1)\frac{(q-2k)p+2}{q}-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(kg+r-1)p-1+\frac{kp-1-gq}{q}\,.

This proves the lemma.
∎

3.2.
Main Theorem

For a polynomial
F
in some variables with integer coefficients, denote by

[F]_{p}
the polynomial
F
whose integer coefficients are projected to
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.

Let

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}a_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}

W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]

whose integer coefficients are projected to

Denote by

the vector space over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
of linear combinations of

with coefficients in

Theorem 3.5

4.
Leading term and leading index

4.1.
Leading term of a polynomial

Denote by
\succ
the lexicographical ordering of monomials

z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}
, where
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0}
.
Thus
z_{1}\succ z_{2}\succ\dots\succ z_{n-1}\succ z_{n}
and so on.
For example,
z_{1}^{2}z_{2}^{2}z_{3}^{2}\succ z_{1}^{2}z_{2}z_{4}^{5}
.

For a nonzero polynomial

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}a_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}\,

consider the summand

a_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}
corresponding to the
lexicographically largest
monomial
z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}
entering
f(z)
with a nonzero coefficient

a_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}
. We call this summand
the leading term of
f(z)
, the corresponding

a_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}
–  the leading coefficient, and

z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}
–  the leading monomial.

For example, let
I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})}(z)
be one of the

p
-hypergeometric solutions of Theorem
2.3
.
Then

\displaystyle[I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})}(z)]_{p}=\sum_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}[a_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}]_{p}\,z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}\,,

with
[a_{d_{1},\dots,d_{n}}]_{p}\in{\operatorname{Sing}\,}W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]_{p}
.
If
[I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})}(z)]_{p}
is nonzero, then it
has the leading term, monomial, and coefficient. The leading coefficient is a nonzero vector
of
{\operatorname{Sing}\,}W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]_{p}
.

4.2.
Leading index of a vector of
W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]_{p}

We order
\{V_{J}\,|\,J\in{\mathcal{J}_{r}}\}
, the basis vectors of
W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]_{p}
, lexicographically
with
the largest of them being

V_{\{1,2,\dots,r\}}=w_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes w_{2}\otimes w_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes w_{1}

and the smallest of them being

V_{\{n-r+1,n-r+2,\dots,n\}}=w_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes w_{1}\otimes w_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes w_{2}
.

Every nonzero vector
w\in W^{\otimes n}[n-2r]_{p}
is a linear combination of the basis vectors.
Let
V_{\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}}
be the largest of the basis vectors entering
w
with a nonzero coefficient.
We call the
index
\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}
the leading index of
w
.

4.3.
Index
\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}

Given integers
(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})
,
consider the system of inequalities for integers
m_{1},\dots,m_{r}
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle(m_{i}-1)M\ \leqslant\ nM+(r-i)c-l_{i}p\ <\ m_{i}M,\qquad i=1,\dots,r.

Clearly, these inequalities uniquely determine the integers
m_{1},\dots,m_{r}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let inequality (
3.5
) hold. Let
kg+r-1\geqslant l_{1}>\dots>l_{r}\geqslant 1
. Then

m_{i}+2\leqslant m_{i+1}
for

i=1,\dots,r-1
, and
1\leqslant m_{1}
,
m_{r}<n
.

Proof.

For
i=1,\dots,r-1
we have

(m_{i}-1)M\leqslant nM+(r-i)c-l_{i}p=nM+(r-i-1)c+c-l_{i+1}p+(l_{i+1}-l_{i})p\leqslant nM+(r-i-1)c+c-l_{i+1}p-p=nM+(r-i-1)c-l_{i+1}p-2M<(m_{i+1}-2)M
.
Hence
m_{i+1}>m_{i}+1
.
This implies that

m_{i}+2\leqslant m_{i+1}
.

The inequality
1\leqslant m_{1}
follows from the inequality

l_{1}p\leqslant nM+(r-1)c
, which is true since

\displaystyle l_{1}p\leqslant(kg+r-1)p\leqslant(kg+r-1)p+\frac{kp-1-gq}{q}=nM+(r-1)c,

see (
3.7
). We also have
M+2c=p\leqslant l_{r}p\leqslant(n-m_{r}+1)M
.
Hence
m_{r}<n
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})
and
(l_{1}^{\prime},\dots,l_{r}^{\prime})
be two distinct tuples of integers
such that
kg+r-1\geqslant l_{1}>\dots>l_{r}\geqslant 1

and
kg+r-1\geqslant l_{1}^{\prime}>\dots>l_{r}^{\prime}\geqslant 1
. Let

\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}
and
\{m_{1}^{\prime},\dots,m_{r}^{\prime}\}

be the corresponding sets defined by (
4.1
).
Then
\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}\neq\{m_{1}^{\prime},\dots,m_{r}^{\prime}\}
.

Proof.

Let
l_{i}>l_{i}^{\prime}
for some
i
. Then

(m_{i}-1)M\leqslant nM+(r-i)c-l_{i}p=nM+(r-i)c-l_{i}^{\prime}p+(l_{i}^{\prime}-l_{i})p\leqslant nM+(r-i)c-l_{i}^{\prime}p-p=nM+(r-i)c-l_{i}^{\prime}p-2M-c<nM+(r-i)c-l_{i}^{\prime}p-2M<(m_{i}^{\prime}-2)M
.
Hence
m_{i}^{\prime}>m_{i}+1
.
∎

4.4.
Special summand

We have

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant r}(t_{i}-t_{j})^{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant r}\Big(\sum_{a_{ij}+a_{ji}=c}\binom{c}{a_{ji}}(-1)^{a_{ji}}t_{i}^{a_{ij}}t_{j}^{a_{ji}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{(a_{ij})\in A}\,t_{1}^{\sum_{j\neq 1}a_{1j}}\cdots t_{r}^{\sum_{j\neq r}a_{rj}}\prod_{i<j}(-1)^{a_{ji}}\binom{c}{a_{ji}},

where
A=\{(a_{ij})_{1\leqslant i,j\leqslant r,\,i\neq j}\mid a_{ij}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geqslant 0},\,a_{ij}+a_{ji}=c\ \operatorname{for\,every\,}\,i\neq j\}
.
Hence

(4.2)

\displaystyle[I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})}(z)]_{p}=[\int_{\{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}\}_{p}}\Phi_{p}(t,z)W(t,z)dt_{1}\dots dt_{r}]_{p}\,=

\displaystyle=\sum_{(a_{ij})\in A}\,\Big(\prod_{i<j}(-1)^{a_{ji}}\binom{c}{a_{ji}}\Big)[\int_{\{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}\}_{p}}W(t,z)\prod_{i=1}^{r}t_{i}^{\sum_{j\neq i}a_{ij}}\prod_{s=1}^{n}(t_{i}-z_{s})^{M}dt_{1}\dots dt_{r}]_{p}.

This sum contains the special summand

\displaystyle S(z):=[\int_{\{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}\}_{p}}W(t,z)\prod_{i=1}^{r}t_{i}^{(r-i)c}\prod_{s=1}^{n}(t_{i}-z_{s})^{M}dt_{1}\dots dt_{r}]_{p}

corresponding to the collection
(a_{ij})
such that
a_{ij}=c
for
i<j
and
a_{ij}=0
for
i>j
.

For
1\leqslant u\leqslant nM
, denote

(4.3)

\displaystyle z(u):=z_{s}\,,\qquad\operatorname{if}\quad(s-1)M<u\leqslant sM.

Thus
z(1)=z(2)=\dots=z(M)=z_{1}
,
z(M+1)=z_{2}
and so on.

Lemma 4.3
.

The vector-polynomial
S(z)
is nonzero. The leading monomial of
S(z)
equals

(4.4)

\displaystyle z^{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}\,:=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{u=1}^{nM+(r-i)c-l_{i}p}z(u).

Denote by
C_{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}
the leading coefficient of
S(z)
. Then
the leading index of the vector
C_{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}
equals
\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}
determined by inequalities (
4.1
).
Moreover, the leading monomial of any other nonzero summand in (
4.2
) is lexicographically smaller than

z^{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}
.
Thus
C_{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}z^{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}
is the leading term of

[I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r})}(z)]_{p}
.

Proof.

We rewrite
z^{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}
using the integers
m_{1},\dots,m_{r}
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle z^{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}z_{1}^{M}\cdots z_{m_{i}-1}^{M}\,z_{m_{i}}^{(n-m_{i}+1)M+(r-i)c-l_{i}p}.

Let
z^{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}=z_{1}^{d_{1}}\dots z_{n}^{d_{n}}
for some
d_{1},\dots,d_{n}
. Then

(4.6)

\displaystyle d_{m_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-m_{i}+1)M+(r-i)(c+M)-l_{i}p,\qquad i=1,\dots,r;

\displaystyle d_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle rM,\qquad\qquad\ \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ j=1,\dots,m_{1}-1;

\displaystyle d_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(r-i)M,\quad j=m_{i}+1,\dots,m_{i+1}-1,\quad i=1,\dots,r-1;

\displaystyle d_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad\qquad\qquad\ \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ j=m_{r}+1,\dots,n.

It is clear that
z^{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}
is the lexicographically maximal monomial which
can be produced by
S(z)
. Let
C_{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}
be the coefficient
of
z^{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}
in
S(z)
. Then

C_{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}=\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{J}_{r}}}c_{J}V_{J}
with
c_{J}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
.
It is clear that
\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}
is the leading index of
C_{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}
,
if
c_{\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}}
is nonzero, but

(4.7)

\displaystyle c_{\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}(-1)^{(n-m_{i}+1)M+(r-i)c-l_{i}p}\binom{M-1}{(n-m_{i}+1)M+(r-i)c-l_{i}p}\,,

which is nonzero due to (
3.3
) and (
4.1
).
Thus
S(z)
is nonzero, its leading term equals

C_{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}z^{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}
, and the leading index of
C_{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}

is
\{m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}
.
It remains to prove that the leading monomial of any other
nonzero summand in (
4.2
) is lexicographically smaller than

z^{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}
.

Let
\tilde{S}(z)
be any other summand in (
4.2
),

\displaystyle\tilde{S}(z)\,:=\,\Big(\prod_{i<j}(-1)^{a_{ji}}\binom{c}{a_{ji}}\Big)[\int_{\{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}\}_{p}}W(t,z)\prod_{i=1}^{r}t_{i}^{\sum_{j\neq i}a_{ij}}\prod_{s=1}^{n}(t_{i}-z_{s})^{M}dt_{1}\dots dt_{r}]_{p}.

It is clear that the lexicographically maximal monomial which can be produced by
\tilde{S}(z)

equals

(4.8)

\displaystyle\tilde{z}^{\,l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}\,:=\,\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{u=1}^{nM-l_{i}p+\sum_{j\neq i}a_{ij}}z(u).

Assume that
\sum_{j\neq 1}a_{1j}<(r-1)c
. Let
s
be the least index such that the maximal power
of
z_{s}
dividing
\prod_{u=1}^{nM-l_{1}p+\sum_{j\neq 1}a_{1j}}z(u)
is strictly
smaller than the maximal power of
z_{s}
(which we denote by
b
) dividing
\prod_{u=1}^{nM+(r-1)c-l_{1}p}z(u)
.
Then the maximal power of
z_{s}
dividing
\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{u=1}^{nM-l_{i}p+\sum_{j\neq i}a_{ij}}z(u)

is strictly smaller than
b+(r-1)M
. This implies that
\tilde{z}^{\,l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}
is lexicographically smaller than
z^{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}
.

Thus, a summand
\tilde{S}(z)
must have
\sum_{j\neq 1}a_{ij}=(r-1)c
in order to have a monomial as large lexicographically as

z^{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}
. This means that
a_{1j}=c
and
a_{j1}=0
for
j\neq 1
.

Now take any summand
\tilde{S}(z)
in (
4.2
) with
\sum_{j\neq 1}a_{1j}=(r-1)c
. In a similar way we show that

\tilde{S}(z)
must have
\sum_{j\neq 2}a_{2j}=(r-2)c
in order to have a monomial as large lexicographically as

z^{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}
. Repeating this reasoning we conclude that the special summand
S(z)
is the only summand in (
4.2
)
which may have
the monomial
z^{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}
with a nonzero coefficient; and no summands in (
4.2
)
may have a monomial larger than
z^{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}}
.
Lemma
4.3
is proved.
∎

\displaystyle W(t,z)=\sum_{1\leqslant i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}\leqslant n}W_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}}(t,z)\,V_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}}\,,\quad W_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}}(t,z)=\operatorname{Sym}_{t_{1},\dots,t_{r}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{t_{j}-z_{i_{j}}}\,,

,

\displaystyle I^{(l_{1},\dots,l_{r}))}(z)=\int_{\{l_{1},\dots,l_{r}\}_{p}}\Phi_{p}(t,z)W(t,z)\,dt_{1}\dots dt_{r}\,,

\displaystyle\Phi_{p}(t,z)=\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant r}(t_{i}-t_{j})^{c}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{s=1}^{n}(t_{i}-z_{s})^{M},W(t,z)=\sum_{J\in{\mathcal{J}_{r}}}W_{J}(t,z)V_{J},

5.1.
Square integrability criterion

Let

be a complex manifold of complex dimension

. If

along

becomes univalued on a
finite (possibly ramified) cover of

. Then
f
has at a generic point of the
exceptional divisor a fractional order.

Let
\omega
be a multivalued meromorphic
r
-form on
M
with
only finitely many determinations and let
S
be an irreducible subvariety of

M
. Write
\omega
at some

of

as

where
\omega_{0}
is
d
-form on
M

along

Suppose that

We have the following trivial observation.

(

5.2.
Square integrable differential
r
-forms

Let
t=(t_{1},\dots,t_{r})
be coordinates on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}\subset(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{r}
and
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
distinct parameters.

Consider a differential
r
-form

(5.1)

\displaystyle\omega\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ P(t)\,\Phi(t,z)^{k}dt\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ P(t)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{s=1}^{n}(t_{i}-z_{s})^{-k/q}\cdot\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(t_{i}-t_{j})^{2k/q}\cdot dt_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dt_{r}

on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}\subset(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{r}
. Here
k
,
0<k<q
, is a positive integer;
dt=dt_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dt_{r}
; and
P(t)
is a polynomial
in
t
.

Let
n=qg+2r-1
where
g
is some positive integer, cf. (
3.4
).

Theorem 5.2
.

The form
\omega
is square integrable on
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{r}
if and only if

(5.2)

\displaystyle\deg_{t_{i}}P\leqslant kg-1\qquad\operatorname{for\,all}\qquad i=1,\dots,r.

Proof.

Denote by
D
the support of the divisor of
\omega
. The support
D
lies in the union
of hypersurfaces defined by
t_{i}=z_{s}
,
t_{i}=\infty
, and
t_{i}=t_{j}
for
i<j
. This union is
clearly arrangement-like. Its dense strata of codimension
l
are:

(i)

diagonals in
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{r}
defined by letting
l\geqslant 2
coordinates to coalesce;

(ii)

loci in
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{r}
defined by setting
l\geqslant 1
coordinates equal to
\infty
;

(iii)

loci in
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{r}
defined by setting
l\geqslant 1
coordinates equal to some
z_{s}
.

Let
S
be defined by the equations
t_{i_{1}}=\dots=t_{i_{l}}
for some
1\leqslant i_{1}<\dots<i_{l}\leqslant r
.
Then the logarithmic order of
\omega
along
S
is
\geqslant l-1+\binom{l}{2}\cdot 2k/q>0
.

Let
S
be defined by the equations
t_{i_{1}}=\dots=t_{i_{l}}=z_{s}
for some
1\leqslant i_{1}<\dots<i_{l}\leqslant r
.
Then the logarithmic order
\omega
along
S
is

\geqslant\ l-lk/q+\binom{l}{2}\cdot 2k/q=l(1-k/q)+\binom{l}{2}\cdot 2k/q>0
.

Let
S
be defined by the equation
t_{i_{1}}=\infty
for some
i_{1}
.
In the coordinates
u_{1},\dots,u_{r}
,
u_{i}=1/t_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,r
, we have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{r}\Big(P(1/u_{1},\dots,1/u_{r},z)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}u_{r}^{\deg_{t_{i}}P}\Big)\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{s=1}^{n}(1-z_{s}u_{i})^{-k/q}\cdot

\displaystyle\cdot

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(u_{j}-u_{i})^{2k/q}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{r}u_{r}^{-\deg_{t_{i}}P+nk/q-(r-1)2k/q-2}\cdot du_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge du_{r}

Hence the logarithmic order of
\omega
along
S
equals

\displaystyle 1+nk/q-(r-1)2k/q-2-\deg_{t_{i_{1}}}P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-2(r-1))k/q-1-\deg_{t_{i_{1}}}P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle kg-1-\deg_{t_{i_{1}}}P+k/q.

Hence the logarithmic order along
S
is positive
if and only if
\deg_{t_{i_{1}}}P\leqslant kg-1
.

Let
S
be defined by the equations
t_{i_{1}}=\dots=t_{i_{l}}=\infty
for some
1\leqslant i_{1}<\dots<i_{l}\leqslant r
.
It follows from (
5.3
) that
the logarithmic order along

is positive if the logarithmic order is positive along every hyperplpane
defined by the equation

. The theorem is proved.
∎

5.3.
Schur polynomials

A sequence of integers
a=(a_{1}\geqslant a_{2}\geqslant\dots\geqslant a_{r}\geqslant 0)

is called a partition. For a partition
a

the polynomial
m_{a}(t)=\operatorname{Sym}_{t}t_{1}^{a_{1}}\dots t_{r}^{a_{r}}

is called a symmetric monomial function.
The polynomial

\displaystyle s_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})}(t)=\frac{\det\,(t_{i}^{a_{j}+r-j})_{i,j=1,\dots,r}}{\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant r}(t_{i}-t_{j})}

is called a Schur polynomial. It is known that

(5.4)

\displaystyle s_{a}(t)\ =\ \sum_{b\leqslant a}\,K_{a,b}\,m_{b_{1},\dots,b_{r}}(t),

where
K_{a,b}
are nonnegative integers,
K_{a,a}=1
. The inequality

b\leqslant a
means
\sum_{j=1}^{i}b_{j}\leqslant\sum_{j=1}^{i}a_{j}
for all
i=1,\dots,r
.
The numbers

K_{a,b}
are called the Kostka numbers.

For a positive integer
d
, denote

\displaystyle A(d)=\{(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\mid d\geqslant a_{1}\geqslant a_{2}\geqslant\dots\geqslant a_{r}\geqslant 0,\,a_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}.

Let
\mathcal{V}(d)
be the free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module with basis

\{m_{a}(t)\,|\,a\in A(d)\}
.
The module
\mathcal{V}(d)
has rank
\binom{d+r}{r}
.
The set

\{s_{a}(t)\,|\,a\in A(d)\}

of Schur polynomials is a basis of
\mathcal{V}(d)
by formula (
5.4
).

Let us return to Theorem
5.2
.
Let

\mathcal{W}
be the vector space of all differential
r
-forms
\omega=P(t)\Phi(t,z)^{k}dt
such that
P(t)
is
a polynomial in
t_{1},\dots,t_{r}
symmetric with respect to
permutations of
t_{1},\dots,t_{r}
, and
\omega
is square integrable on
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{r}
.

Corollary 5.3
.

The set

\{s_{a}(t)\Phi(t,z)^{k}dt\,|\,a\in A(kg-1)\}

r
-forms on

\mathcal{W}

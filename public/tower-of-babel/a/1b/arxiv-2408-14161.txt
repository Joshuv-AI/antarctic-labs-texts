Radial 3D Focusing Energy Critical INLS equations with defocusing perturbation: Ground states, Scattering, and Blow-up
Tianxiang Gou, Mohamed Majdoub and Tarek Saanouni

Abstract
. We investigate the following inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation in the radial regime, featuring a focusing energy-critical nonlinearity and a defocusing perturbation: i ​ ∂ t u + Δ ​ u = | x | − a ​ | u | p − 2 ​ u − | x | − b ​ | u | 4 − 2 ​ b ​ u in ​ ℝ t × ℝ x 3 , \textnormal{i}\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=|x|^{-a}|u|^{p-2}u-|x|^{-b}|u|^{4-2b}u\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3}, where 0 < a 0<a , b < 2 b<2 and 2 + 4 − 2 ​ a 3 < p ≤ 6 − 2 ​ a {2+\frac{4-2a}{3}<p\leq 6-2a} . First, we establish the existence and nonexistence of ground states, along with their quantitative properties. Subsequently, we analyze the dichotomy between scattering and blow-up for solutions with energy below the ground-state energy threshold. An intriguing feature of this equation is the lack of scaling invariance, which arises from the competing effects of the inhomogeneous nonlinearities. Additionally, the presence of singular weights breaks translation invariance in the spatial variable, introducing further complexity to the analysis. To the best of our knowledge, this work represents the first comprehensive study of the inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation with a leading-order focusing energy-critical inhomogeneous nonlinearity and a defocusing perturbation. Our results provide new insights into the interplay between these competing nonlinearities and their influence on the dynamics of solutions.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following focusing energy critical inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation with perturbed defocusing inhomogeneous nonlinearity,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\textnormal{i}\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=|x|^{-a}|u|^{p-2}u-|x|^{-b}|u|^{4-2b}u\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3},

where
u(t,x)
is a complex-valued function defined on the spacetime
\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3}
,
0<a,b<2
, and
{2+\frac{4-2a}{3}<p\leq 6-2a}
. Equation (
1.1
) provides a versatile framework for describing a wide range of physical phenomena in plasma physics, nonlinear optics, and Bose-Einstein condensation. For deeper insights and further interpretations, we refer the readers to
[
5
,
44
]
and the works cited therein.

Given its Hamiltonian structure, the solutions to the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) exhibit the conservation of the energy defined by

(1.2)

E(u(t)):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-a}|u(t,x)|^{p}\,dx-\frac{1}{6-2b}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u(t,x)|^{6-2b}\,dx.

Furthermore, the solutions to the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) also preserve the mass defined by

(1.3)

M(u(t))=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx.

Equation (
1.1
) represents a specific case within a wider category of inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equations characterized by double inhomogeneous nonlinearities. These equations can be generally formulated as:

(1.4)

\textnormal{i}\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=\lambda\,|x|^{-a}|u|^{p-2}u+\mu\,|x|^{-b}|u|^{q-2}u\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{N},

where
a,b\geq 0
,
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, and
p,q>2
. The aforementioned equation generalized classical models like the nonlinear Schrödinger equations (NLS) and the inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equations (INLS). Such equations describe the propagation of laser beams in certain types of plasma media and nonlinear optics, see for example
[
47
,
58
]
and the references therein.

Specifically, when
a=b=\mu=0
, then (
1.4

(1.5)

\textnormal{i}\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=\lambda\,|u|^{p-2}u\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{N}.

The primary goal of this paper is to explore standing wave solutions to (
1.1
). A standing wave solution takes the form

u(t,x)=e^{\textnormal{i}\omega t}\phi(x),\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R},

where
\phi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfies the associated elliptic equation

(1.8)

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi+\omega\phi=|x|^{-b}|\phi|^{4-2b}\phi-|x|^{-a}|\phi|^{p-2}\phi\quad\mbox{in}\,\,\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Definition 1.1
.

(Ground state)
A solution
Q\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to (
1.8
) is called a ground state if it is non-trivial and minimizes the associated action functional

S_{\omega}(\phi):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx+\frac{\omega}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\phi|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-a}|\phi|^{p_{1}}\,dx-\frac{1}{6-2b}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|\phi|^{6-2b}\,dx,

over all non-trivial solutions to (
1.8
). That is,

S_{\omega}(Q)=\inf\Big\{S_{\omega}(\phi)\ :\ \phi\in H^{1}\backslash\{0\}\;\text{ solves }\eqref{equ1}\Big\}.

The first result of the paper concerning the existence and nonexistence of solutions to (
1.8
) reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<a,b<2
. Then the following assertions hold true.

(\textnormal{i})

If
2<p\leq 6-2a
and
\omega>0
, then there exists no solutions in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to (
1.8
).

(\textnormal{ii})

If
2<p<6-2a
and
\omega=0
, then there exists no solutions in
{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to (
1.8
).

(\textnormal{iii})

If
p=6-2a
,
b<a
and
\omega=0
, then there exist positive ground states in
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to (
1.8
).

(\textnormal{iv})

If
2<p\leq 6-2a
and
\omega<0
, then there exists no radially symmetric nonnegative solutions in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to (
1.8
).

To prove the nonexistence of solutions to (
1.8
) for
\omega\geq 0
, one needs to make use of Pohozaev identity satisfied by solutions to (
1.8
), see Lemma
2.5
. While, to prove the nonexistence of solutions to (
1.8
) for
\omega<0
, one needs to take advantage of
[
56
, Lemma 4.2]
. Finally, to establish the existence of solutions to (
1.8
) for
\omega=0
, we shall introduce the following minimization problem,

(1.9)

\displaystyle m_{0}:=\inf_{\phi\in P}E(\phi),

where

P:=\left\{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\}:K(\phi)=0\right\},

\displaystyle K(\phi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx+\frac{3(p-2)+2a}{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-a}|\phi|^{p}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|\phi|^{6-2b}\,dx.

Here
K(\phi)=0
is the so-called Pohozaev identity related to (
1.8
) and
P
is the Pohozaev manifold, which is indeed a natural constraint. It is straightforward to find that any minimizer to (
1.9
) is a ground state to (
1.8
). To detect the existence of minimizers, the essential difficulty is to check the compactness of sequences due to the presence of double energy critical exponents
p=6-2a
and
q=6-2b
. Our discussion is carried out in the spirit of the Lions concentration compactness principle.

Remark 1.1

We now briefly discuss some quantitative properties of the solutions to (

(\textnormal{i})

Using the polarization arguments developed in

(\textnormal{ii})

\frac{4-a}{8-2a}-\frac{2a+4(6-2a)}{(6-2b)(8-2a)}+\frac{b}{6-2b}=0.

As an application of

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0<b<a<2

2<p<6-2a

\displaystyle m:=\inf_{\phi\in P}E(\phi),\quad P:=\left\{\phi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\}:K(\phi)=0\right\}.

\displaystyle-\Delta Q=|x|^{-b}|Q|^{4-2b}Q,\quad Q(x)=\left(1+\frac{|x|^{4-2b}}{3-b}\right)^{\frac{1}{b-2}}.

The nonexistence of minimizers is a direct consequence of Theorem

Remark 1.2

The aforementioned ground state

\frac{1}{C_{*}}:=\inf_{u\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\|\nabla u\|}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{6-2b}}},

Proposition 1.1

Let

where the mass and the energy are defined by (

Furthermore, the solution mapping

is continuous from
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to

T_{\text{max}}<+\infty

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to T^{-}_{\text{max}}}\|\nabla u(t)\|=+\infty.

\eta_{R}(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 2R
and
0\leq\eta_{R}(x)\leq 1
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Therefore, by (
3.18
), we get that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla u_{n}\cdot\nabla(u_{n}\eta_{R})\,dx=\int_{|x|<R}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\,dx+o_{n}(1).

In addition, by (
3.14
) and (
3.15
), we know that

(3.19)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-a}|u_{n}|^{6-2a}\eta_{R}\,dx=\int_{|x|<R}|x|^{-a}|u_{n}|^{6-2a}\,dx+o_{n}(1),

(3.20)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u_{n}|^{6-2b}\eta_{R}\,dx=\int_{|x|<R}|x|^{-b}|u_{n}|^{6-2b}\,dx+o_{n}(1).

Since
\langle E^{\prime}(u_{n}),\eta_{R}u_{n}\rangle=o_{n}(1)
, by (
3.18
), (
3.19
) and (
3.20
), then

(3.21)

\displaystyle\int_{|x|<R}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\,dx+\int_{|x|<R}|x|^{-a}|u_{n}|^{6-2a}\,dx=\int_{|x|<R}|x|^{-b}|u_{n}|^{6-2b}\,dx+o_{n}(1).

Observe by Lemma
2.4
that

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|\eta_{R}u_{n}|^{6-2b}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{6-2b}}\leq S_{b}^{-1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla(\eta_{R}u_{n})|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Define

\mu_{R}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{|x|<R}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\,dx.

\nu_{b,R}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{|x|<R}|x|^{-b}|u_{n}|^{6-2b}\,dx,

Taking into account (
3.21
), we then obtain that

\nu_{b,R}^{\frac{1}{6-2b}}\leq S_{b}^{-1}\mu_{R}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq S_{b}^{-1}\nu_{b,R}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It clearly implies that
\nu_{b,R}=0
or
\nu_{b,R}\geq S_{b}^{{(6-2b)}/{(2-b)}}
.

Since
m_{0}>0
and
K(u_{n})=o_{n}(1)
, then

\nu_{b,\infty}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u_{n}|^{6-2b}\,dx>0.

It then shows that
\nu_{b,R}>0
for
R>1
large enough. Then we get that, for some
0<\nu_{0}<S_{b}^{{(6-2b)}/{(2-b)}}
, there exists
R_{n}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{|x|<R_{n}}|x|^{-b}|u_{n}|^{6-2b}\,dx=\nu_{0}.

Define

\widetilde{u}_{n}(x):=\left(\frac{R_{n}}{R}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{n}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{R}x\right),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

It is straightforward to compute that

(3.22)

\displaystyle\int_{|x|<R}|x|^{-b}|\widetilde{u}_{n}|^{6-2b}\,dx=\nu_{0}.

In addition, we find that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\widetilde{u}_{n}|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\,dx,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-a}|\widetilde{u}_{n}|^{6-2a}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-a}|u_{n}|^{6-2a}\,dx,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|\widetilde{u}_{n}|^{6-2b}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u_{n}|^{6-2b}\,dx.

Since
\{u_{n}\}\subset\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is a bounded Palais-Smale sequence for
E
at the level
m_{0}
, then
\{\widetilde{u}_{n}\}\subset\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is a also bounded Palais-Smale sequence for
E
at the level
m_{0}
. Replacing the role of
\{u_{n}\}
by
\{\widetilde{u}_{n}\}
, we can derive as previously that there exists a solution
\widetilde{u}\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
K(\widetilde{u})=0
and
m_{0}\leq E(\widetilde{u})
. Otherwise, there holds that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{|x|<R}|x|^{-b}|\widetilde{u}_{n}|^{6-2b}\,dx=0\,\,\mbox{or}\,\,\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{|x|<R}|x|^{-b}|\widetilde{u}_{n}|^{6-2b}\,dx\geq S_{b}^{{(6-2b)}/{(2-b)}}.

However, this is impossible by (
3.22
). Consequently, from the discussion above, we can conclude that there exists a nontrivial solution
u\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that
K(u)=0
and
m_{0}\leq E(u)
. If
E(u)=m_{0}
, then the proof is completed. If not, then we assume that
E(u)>m_{0}
. Observe that

\displaystyle E(v_{n})+o_{n}(1)

\displaystyle=E(v_{n})-\frac{1}{6-2b}\langle E^{\prime}(v_{n}),v_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{6-2b}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx+\left(\frac{1}{6-2a}-\frac{1}{6-2b}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-a}|v_{n}|^{6-2a}\,dx\geq 0.

This along with (
3.12
) implies that
E(u)\leq m_{0}
, which clearly contradicts with the assumption that
E(u)>m_{0}
. It then follows that
E(u)=m_{0}
.

Observe that if
K(u)=0
, then
K(|u|)\leq 0
. Hence, by Lemma
2.6
, there exists
0<\lambda_{|u|}<1
such that
K(u_{\lambda_{|u|}})=0
. In addition, we have that

\displaystyle E(|u|_{\lambda_{|u|}})

\displaystyle=E(|u|_{\lambda_{|u|}})-\frac{1}{6-2b}K(|u|_{\lambda_{|u|}})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{6-2b}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla(|u|_{\lambda_{|u|}})|^{2}\,dx+\left(\frac{1}{6-2a}-\frac{1}{6-2b}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-a}|u_{\lambda_{|u|}}|^{6-2a}\,dx

\displaystyle<\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{6-2b}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\left(\frac{1}{6-2a}-\frac{1}{6-2b}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-a}|u|^{6-2a}\,dx

\displaystyle=E(u)-\frac{1}{6-2b}K(u)=E(u).

Thereby, we can assume that
u
is nonnegative. From the maximum principle, we then know that
u
is positive. Thus, the proof is completed.
∎

4.
Global Theory

In this section, we establish the global existence of solutions to the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) in the energy space
C_{T}(H^{1})
. We shall take advantage of some ideas in
[
65
, Section 3]
. Let us start with the global existence result of the non-perturbed problem associated to (
1.1
). Here the underlying energy functional is given by

E^{c}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{6-2b}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
0<b<\frac{4}{3}
,
0<\varepsilon<1
and
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfy

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{0}\|<\|\nabla Q\|,\quad E^{c}(u_{0})<(1-\varepsilon)E^{c}(Q).

Then there is a unique global solution to the non-perturbed problem associated to (
1.1
) denoted by
v\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, namely

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\textnormal{i}\partial_{t}v+\Delta v=-|x|^{-b}|v|^{4-2b}v,\\
&v(0)=u_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover, there holds that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle v\|_{\Lambda(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim C(\varepsilon,\|u_{0}\|),

where

|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle v|:=|v|+|\nabla v|.

Proof.

By
[
14
, Theorem 1 and Proposition 1]
, then there exists a unique global solution
v\in C(\mathbb{R},\dot{H}^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
to (
4.2
) satisfying

\|\nabla v\|_{\Lambda(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim_{\varepsilon}1.

In addition, by the integral Duhamel formula, the Strichartz estimates in Lemma
2.7
, Hölder’s inequality and Hardy’s inequality as stated in Lemma
2.2
, one writes that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|v\|_{\Lambda(\mathbb{R})}&\lesssim\|u_{0}\|+\|(|x|^{-1}v)^{b}|v|^{4-3b}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R},L^{6/5})}\\
&\lesssim\|u_{0}\|+\||x|^{-1}v\|_{W_{b}(\mathbb{R})}^{b}\|v\|_{S(\mathbb{R})}^{4-3b}\|v\|_{W_{b}(\mathbb{R})}\\
&\lesssim\|u_{0}\|+\|\nabla v\|_{W_{b}(\mathbb{R})}^{b}\|v\|_{S(\mathbb{R})}^{4-3b}\|v\|_{\Lambda(\mathbb{R})},\end{split}

where we used Hölder’s inequality via the inequalities

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}=\frac{b}{\frac{10(1+b)}{1+3b}}+\frac{4-3b}{10}+\frac{1}{\frac{10(1+b)}{1+3b}},

\displaystyle\frac{5}{6}=\frac{b}{\frac{30(1+b)}{13+9b}}+\frac{4-3b}{10}+\frac{1}{\frac{30(1+b)}{13+9b}}.

Then the proof is completed by applying (
4.4
) and the property that
v\in S(\mathbb{R})
and
\nabla v\in W_{b}(\mathbb{R})
proved in
[
14
, Proposition 1.1]
.

∎

Now, we give a good local well-posedness of solutions to the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
). It plays an important role in the study of the global well-posedness and scattering of solutions.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
0<a<1
,
0<b<\frac{4}{3}
,
0<\varepsilon<1
, and
2<p<6-2a
. Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfy (
4.1
). Then there exist a constant
0<T<<1
and a unique solution
u\in C([0,T),H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
to (
1.1
) with
u(0)=u_{0}\in H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfying

\displaystyle\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle u\|_{\Lambda(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon,\|u_{0}\|).

Proof.

To prove the above lemma, we are going to look for a local solution to (
1.1
) of the form
u:=v+w
, where
v\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
is the solution to (
4.2
) given by Lemma
4.1
and
w\in C([0,T),H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
satisfies the equation

(4.5)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\textnormal{i}w_{t}+\Delta w=|x|^{-a}|v+w|^{p-2}(v+w)-|x|^{-b}\left(|v+w|^{4-2b}(v+w)-|v|^{4-2b}v\right),\\
&w(0)=0,\end{aligned}\right.

where
T>0
is a small constant to be specified later.
Let
0<\delta<<1
be a small constant. In view of (
4.3
), one then splits
[0,T]
for some
M:=M(\delta,\varepsilon,\|u_{0}\|)
as follows,

(4.6)

\displaystyle[0,T]:=\bigcup_{0\leq k\leq M}[t_{k},t_{k+1}]:=\bigcup_{0\leq k\leq M}I_{k},

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle v\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}<\delta,\quad\mbox{for all}\,\,\,0\leq k\leq M.

Let us resolve (
4.5
) by inductive arguments. Assume that there is a solution to (
4.5
) on
I_{k-1}
such that, for some constants
C,\nu>0
,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle w\|_{\Lambda(I_{k-1})}<(2C)^{k-1}T^{\nu}.

We are going to consider the problem on
I_{k}
. We now introduce the integral formula, a metric space and a complete norm as follows,

\displaystyle f(w)

\displaystyle:=e^{\textnormal{i}(t-t_{k})\Delta}w(t_{k})-\textnormal{i}\int_{t_{k}}^{t}e^{\textnormal{i}(\cdot-s)\Delta}\left(|x|^{-a}|v+w|^{p-2}(v+w)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.-|x|^{-b}\left(|v+w|^{4-2b}(v+w)-|v|^{4-2b}v\right)\right)\,ds,

X_{k}:=\left\{w\in C(I_{k},H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3})):\quad\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle w\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}<(2C)^{k}T^{\nu}\right\},

d(w,w^{\prime}):=\|w-w^{\prime}\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}.

Taking into account the Strichartz estimates in Lemma
2.7
, one writes that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle f(w)\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}&\lesssim\|w(t_{k})\|_{H^{1}}+\left\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle\left(|x|^{-a}|v+w|^{p-2}(v+w)\right)\right\|_{\Lambda^{\prime}(I_{k})}\\
&\quad+\left\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle\left(|x|^{-b}\left(|v+w|^{4-2b}(v+w)-|v|^{4-2b}v\right)\right)\right\|_{\Lambda^{\prime}(I_{k})}\\
&\lesssim\|w(t_{k})\|_{H^{1}}+\left\||x|^{-a}|v+w|^{p-2}\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle(v+w)\right\|_{\Lambda^{\prime}(I_{k})}\\
&\quad+\left\||x|^{-a-1}|v+w|^{p-2}(v+w)\right\|_{\Lambda^{\prime}(I_{k})}\\
&\quad+\left\||x|^{-b}\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle\left(|v+w|^{4-2b}(v+w)-|v|^{4-2b}v\right)\right\|_{\Lambda^{\prime}(I_{k})}\\
&\quad+\left\||x|^{-b-1}\left(|v+w|^{4-2b}(v+w)-|v|^{4-2b}v\right)\right\|_{\Lambda^{\prime}(I_{k})}.\end{split}

Making use of
[
37
, Lemma 3.4]
, one has that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\||x|^{-a}|v+w|^{p-2}\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle(v+w)\right\|_{\Lambda^{\prime}(I_{k})}+\left\||x|^{-a-1}|v+w|^{p-2}(v+w)\right\|_{\Lambda^{\prime}(I_{k})}\\
&\lesssim T^{\nu}\|\nabla(v+w)\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}^{p-2}\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle(v+w)\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}.\end{split}

Taking advantage of the spirit in the proof of
[
14
, Proposition 2.1]
, one gets that

\displaystyle\left\||x|^{-b}\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle\left(|v+w|^{4-2b}(v+w)-|v|^{4-2b}v\right)\right\|_{\Lambda^{\prime}(I_{k})}+\left\||x|^{-b-1}\left(|v+w|^{4-2b}(v+w)-|v|^{4-2b}v\right)\right\|_{\Lambda^{\prime}(I_{k})}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle v\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}^{4-2b}+\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle w\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}^{4-2b}+\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle v\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}^{3-2b}\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle w\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}\right)\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle w\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}.

Combining (
4.7
), (
4.8
), (
4.9
), (
4.11
) and (
4.10
), we then conclude that,

\displaystyle\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle f(w)\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w(t_{k})\|_{H^{1}}+T^{\nu}\left(\delta+\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle w\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}^{p-2}\right)\left(\delta+\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle w\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(\delta^{4-2b}+\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle w\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}^{4-2b}+\delta^{3-2b}\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle w\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}\right)\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle w\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}

\displaystyle\leq C(2C)^{k-1}T^{\nu}+CT^{\nu}\left(\delta+((2C)^{k}T^{\nu})^{p-2}\right)\left(\delta+(2C)^{k}T^{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+C\left(\delta^{4-2b}+((2C)^{k}T^{\nu})^{4-2b}+\delta^{3-2b}(2C)^{k}T^{\nu}\right)(2C)^{k}T^{\nu}

\displaystyle<(2C)^{k}T^{\nu},

where we picked
0<\delta,T<<1
in the last line. By the fact
M=M(\varepsilon,\delta,\|u_{0}\|)
, we can choose
T
uniformly of the process of the induction. Moreover, proceeding as the calculus in (
4.9
), we similarly have that, for any
w,w^{\prime}\in X_{k}
,

(4.12)

\displaystyle d(f(w),f(w^{\prime}))\leq\theta d(w,w^{\prime}),

where
0<\theta<1
. At this point, applying the Picard fixed point theorem, then there is a unique solution
w\in C(I_{k},H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
to (
4.5
) satisfying

\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle w\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}<(2C)^{k}T^{\nu}.

Therefore, there is a unique solution
w\in C([0,T),H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
to (
4.5
) such that

(4.13)

\displaystyle\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle w\|_{\Lambda(0,T)}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{M}\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle w\|_{\Lambda(I_{k})}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{M}(2C)^{k}T^{\nu}\leq M(2C)^{M}T^{\nu}\leq C(\varepsilon,\delta,\|u_{0}\|).

Then
u:=v+w\in C([0,T),H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
is the solution to the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
). By (
4.3
) and (
4.13
), one gets that

(4.14)

\displaystyle\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle u\|_{\Lambda(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle w\|_{\Lambda(0,T)}+\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle v\|_{\Lambda(0,T)}\leq C(\varepsilon,\|u_{0}\|).

This ends the proof.
∎

Next, we present a coercivity property of solutions to the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
0<a<1
,
0<b<\frac{4}{3}
,

) with

denoted by

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{{L^{\infty}}([0,T{{}_{max}),L^{2})}}\leq(1-\widetilde{\varepsilon})\|\nabla Q\|^{2}.

and for any admissible pair
(q,r)\in\Lambda
and any compact interval

\displaystyle\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle u\|_{L^{q}(I,L^{r})}\leq(1+|I|^{\frac{1}{q}})C(\varepsilon,\|u_{0}\|).

1.12
), (
4.15
) and the energy conservation, we write that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{3-b}{2-b}\left(y-\frac{1}{3-b}y^{3-b}\right)&=\frac{3-b}{2-b}\left(y-\frac{C_{*}^{6-2b}}{3-b}\|\nabla Q\|^{4-2b}y^{3-b}\right)\\
&=\frac{3-b}{2-b}\left(\frac{\|\nabla u\|^{2}}{\|\nabla Q\|^{2}}-\frac{C_{*}^{6-2b}}{3-b}\frac{\|\nabla u\|^{6-2p}}{\|\nabla Q\|^{2}}\right)\\
&<(1-\varepsilon).\end{split}

Therefore, (
4.16
) follows from continuity arguments by using (

) and the fact that

. Furthermore, we have that

by (

∎

In this section, we are going to discuss the energy scattering of solutions to (

. We shall assume that
0<a<1
,
0<b<\frac{4}{3}
and

. In the sprit of Lemma

, we denote by
u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))

) with

5.1.
Virial/Morawetz Estimate

Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function. Define the variance potential by

(5.2)

V_{\psi}[u]:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi(x)|u(\cdot,x)|^{2}\,dx.

Define the Morawetz action by

(5.3)

M_{\psi}[u]:=2\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\overline{u}(\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla u)\,dx.

Lemma 5.1
.

(
[
8
, Lemma 4.5]
)
Let
u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
be the global solution to the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
). Then the following identity holds true for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle V_{\psi}^{\prime\prime}[u]=M_{\psi}^{\prime}[u]

\displaystyle=4\Re\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{l}\partial_{k}\psi\partial_{k}u\partial_{l}\bar{u}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta^{2}\psi|u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2(p-2)}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta\psi|x|^{-a}|u|^{p}\,dx-\frac{4}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla(|x|^{-a})|u|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{4-2b}{3-b}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta\psi|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx+\frac{2}{3-b}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla(|x|^{-b})|u|^{6-2b}\,dx.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
be the global solution to the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) satisfying (
5.1
). Then there holds that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx\gtrsim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx.

Proof.

Taking into account (
1.13
), (
1.14
) and (
5.1
), we get that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\nabla u\|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx&\geq\left(1-C_{*}^{6-2b}\|\nabla u\|^{4-2b}\right)\|\nabla u\|^{2}\\
&\geq\left(1-C_{*}^{6-2b}(1-\widetilde{\varepsilon})^{2-b}\|\nabla Q\|^{4-2b}\right)\|\nabla u\|^{2}\\
&=\left(1-(1-\widetilde{\varepsilon})^{2-b}\right)\|\nabla u\|^{2}.\end{split}

It follows that

(5.5)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{(1-\widetilde{\varepsilon})^{2-b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx.

By (
5.4
) and (
5.5
), we then get that

(5.6)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx\geq\frac{1-(1-\widetilde{\varepsilon})^{2-b}}{(1-\widetilde{\varepsilon})^{2-b}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx.

This ends the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
be the global solution to the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) satisfying (
5.1
). Let
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
be a radial smooth function such that
\psi=1
on
B(1/4)
,
\psi=0
on
B^{c}(1/2)
and
0\leq\psi\leq 1
. Define
\psi_{R}:=\psi(\cdot/R)
. Then there holds that, for
R>>1
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\psi_{R}u)\|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|\psi_{R}u|^{6-2b}\,dx\gtrsim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|\psi_{R}u|^{6-2b}\,dx.

Proof.

Arguing as the proof of Lemma
5.2
, we only need to show that there exists
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
such that

(5.7)

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\psi_{R}u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},L^{2})}^{2}\leq(1-\varepsilon^{\prime})\|\nabla Q\|^{2}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\psi_{R}u)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\psi_{R}\nabla u\|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{R}\Delta\psi_{R}|u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla u\|^{2}+R^{-2}\|u\|^{2},

where we used integration by parts and the properties of the function
\psi_{R}
. This together with (
5.1
) gives rise to the desirable conclusion (
5.7
) and the proof is completed.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
be the global solution to the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) satisfying (
5.1
). Then there exist
R:=R(Q,\|u_{0}\|)>>1
and
\gamma>0
such that, for any
T>0
,

(5.8)

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B(R/4)}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx\,dt\lesssim\frac{R}{T}+R^{-\gamma}.

Moreover, there exist
\{t_{n}\},\{R_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
satisfying
t_{n},R_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
such that

(5.9)

\displaystyle\int_{B(R_{n})}|x|^{-b}|u(t_{n})|^{6-2b}\,dx\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\to\infty.

Proof.

For
R>>1
, we define a radial function
\zeta:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\zeta(x):=\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2}|x|^{2},&\quad\mbox{if}\quad|x|\leq R/2,\\
R|x|,&\quad\mbox{if}\quad|x|>R.\end{aligned}\right.

In addition, we assume that in the centered annulus
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:R/2<|x|<R\}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\zeta>0,\quad\partial^{2}_{r}\zeta\geq 0\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\partial^{\alpha}\zeta|\leq C_{\alpha}R|\cdot|^{1-{|\alpha|}},\quad\forall\,|\alpha|\geq 1.

Observe that on the centered ball of radius
R/2
there holds that

(5.10)

\displaystyle\partial_{j}\partial_{k}\zeta=\delta_{jk},\quad{\Delta\zeta=3}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Delta^{2}\zeta=0.

In view of the radial identity

(5.11)

\partial_{j}\partial_{k}=\left(\frac{\delta_{jk}}{r}-\frac{x_{j}x_{k}}{r^{3}}\right)\partial_{r}+\frac{x_{j}x_{k}}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}^{2},

we are able to compute that, for
|x|>R
,

\displaystyle{\partial_{j}\partial_{k}\zeta=\frac{R}{|x|}\left(\delta_{jk}-\frac{x_{j}x_{k}}{|x|^{2}}\right),\quad\Delta\zeta=\frac{2R}{|x|},\quad|\Delta^{2}\zeta|\lesssim\frac{R}{|x|^{3}}.}

Due to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the conservation of the mass, the fact that
|\nabla\zeta|\lesssim R
and (
5.1
), we have that

(5.12)

\displaystyle|M_{\zeta}[u]|=2\left|\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\overline{u}(\nabla\zeta\cdot\nabla u)\,dx\right|\lesssim R.

Furthermore, taking into account the identity (
5.11
), we obtain that

(5.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Re\sum_{l,k=1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{l}\partial_{k}\zeta\partial_{k}u\partial_{l}\overline{u}\,dx&=\Re\sum_{l.k=1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\left(\frac{\delta_{lk}}{r}-\frac{x_{l}x_{k}}{r^{3}}\right)\partial_{r}\zeta+\frac{x_{l}x_{k}}{r^{2}}\partial_{r}^{2}\zeta\right)\partial_{k}u\partial_{l}\overline{u}\,dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}-\frac{|x\cdot\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\right)\frac{\partial_{r}\zeta}{|x|}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|x\cdot\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\partial_{r}^{2}\zeta\,dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\not\nabla u|^{2}\frac{\partial_{r}\zeta}{|x|}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|x\cdot\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\partial_{r}^{2}\zeta\,dx,\end{split}

where the angular gradient is defined by

\not\nabla:=\nabla-\frac{x\cdot\nabla}{|x|^{2}}x.

Now, by Lemma
5.1
, (
5.10
) and (
5.13
), one writes that

\displaystyle M_{\zeta}^{\prime}[u]

\displaystyle=4\left(\int_{B(R/2)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{3(p-2)+2a}{2p}\int_{B(R/2)}|x|^{-a}|u|^{p}\,dx-\int_{B(R/2)}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\quad+4\int_{B^{c}(R/2)}|\not\nabla u|^{2}\frac{\partial_{r}\zeta}{|x|}\,dx+4\int_{B^{c}(R/2)}\frac{|x\cdot\nabla u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}\partial_{r}^{2}\zeta\,dx-\int_{B^{c}(R/2)}\Delta^{2}\zeta|u|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B^{c}(R/2)}\left(\frac{4a}{p}\frac{\nabla\zeta\cdot x}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{2(p-2)}{p}\Delta\zeta\right)|x|^{-a}|u|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{B^{c}(R/2)}\left(\frac{2b}{3-b}\frac{\nabla\zeta\cdot x}{|x|^{2}}+\frac{4-2}{3-b}\Delta\zeta\right)|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx.

The Sobolev embedding inequality (
2.2
), the properties of
\zeta
, the conservation of the mass and (
5.1
) then imply that

(5.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}M_{\zeta}^{\prime}[u]&\gtrsim 4\left(\int_{B(R/2)}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{3(p-2)+2a}{2p}\int_{B(R/2)}|x|^{-a}|u|^{p}\,dx-\int_{B(R/2)}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx\right)\\
&\quad-R^{-a}\int_{B^{c}(R/2)}|u|^{p}\,dx-R^{-b}\int_{B^{c}(R/2)}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx-R^{-2}\\
&\gtrsim\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B(R/2))}^{2}-\int_{B(R/2)}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx-R^{-(p-2)-a}-R^{-(4-b)}-R^{-2}.\end{split}

Taking advantage of the properties of
\psi
and integration by parts yield that

(5.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\nabla(\psi_{R}u)\|^{2}&=\|\psi_{R}\nabla u\|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{R}\Delta\psi_{R}|u|^{2}\,dx\\
&=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B(R/2))}^{2}-\int_{{R/4<|x|\leq R/2}}(1-\psi_{R}^{2})|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{R}\Delta\psi_{R}|u|^{2}\,dx,\end{split}

where
\psi_{R}
is the radial function defined in Lemma
5.3
. Therefore, by the properties of
\psi_{R}
and the conservation of the mass, (
5.15
) readily implies that

(5.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(B(R/2))}^{2}&=\|\nabla(\psi_{R}u)\|^{2}+\int_{{R/4<|x|\leq R/2}}(1-\psi_{R}^{2})|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{R}\Delta\psi_{R}|u|^{2}\,dx\\
&\gtrsim\|\nabla(\psi_{R}u)\|^{2}-R^{-2}.\end{split}

Similarly, by the Sobolev embedding inequality (
2.2
) and the conservation of the mass, we are able to derive that

(5.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\int_{B(R/2)}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|\psi_{R}u|^{6-2b}\,dx-\int_{{R/4<|x|\leq R/2}}(1-\psi_{R}^{6-2b})|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx\\
&\gtrsim-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|\psi_{R}u|^{6-2b}\,dx-R^{-(4-b)}.\\
\end{split}

At this point, using Lemma
5.3
, (
5.14
), (
5.16
) and (
5.17
), we then get that

(5.18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}M_{\zeta}^{\prime}[u]&\gtrsim\|\nabla(\psi_{R}u)\|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|\psi_{R}u|^{6-2b}\,dx-R^{-(p-2)-a}-R^{-(4-b)}-R^{-2}\\
&\gtrsim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|\psi_{R}u|^{6-2b}\,dx-R^{-(p-2)-a}-R^{-2}.\end{split}

Taking
\gamma:=\min\left\{2,p-2+a\right\}
, integrating (
5.18
) in time on
[0,T]
and applying (
5.12
), we then obtain that, for
R>>1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{B(R/4)}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|\psi_{R}u|^{6-2b}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|M_{\zeta}[u]\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}}{T}+R^{-\gamma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{R}{T}+R^{-\gamma}.

Then (
5.8
) follows immediately. Now, taking
R:=4T^{\frac{1}{1+\gamma}}
, one gets that

(5.19)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{|x|<T^{\frac{1}{1+\gamma}}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx\,dt\lesssim T^{-\frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma}}.

As a consequence, the mean value theorem and (
5.19
) indicate that there exist
\{t_{n}\},\{R_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
satisfying
t_{n},R_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
such that

\int_{B(R_{n})}|x|^{-b}|u(t_{n})|^{6-2b}\,dx\to 0\,\,\mbox{as}\,\,n\to\infty.

Then (
5.9
) holds true and this closes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Scattering Criteria

Now, we prove a scattering criteria in the spirit of
[
61
]
. For this, we need additionally assume that
p\geq 4
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{1}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
be a global solution to the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
). Assume that

(5.20)

0<\sup_{t\geq 0}\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}:=E<\infty.

There exist
R,\epsilon>0
depending on
E,a,p,b
such that if

(5.21)

{\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\int_{B(R)}|u(t,x)|^{6}\,dx<\epsilon^{6}},

then
u
scatters for positive time.

Proof.

To attain the desired conclusion, by the standard small data theory, it suffices to prove that
there exist
T,\alpha>0
such that

(5.22)

\displaystyle\|e^{\textnormal{i}(\cdot-T)\Delta}u(T)\|_{\Lambda^{1}(T,\infty)}\lesssim\epsilon^{\alpha}.

In what follows, the aim is to establish (
5.22
). Let
\alpha>0
and
0<\beta<<1
to be fixed later. Taking into account the Strichartz estimate in Lemma
2.7
, we know that there exist
\delta>0
and
T>\epsilon^{-\beta}
large enough such that

(5.23)

\|e^{\textnormal{i}\cdot\Delta}u_{0}\|_{S(T,\infty)}<\epsilon^{\delta}.

Let us now take the time slabs
J_{1}:=[0,T-\epsilon^{-\beta}]
and
J_{2}:=[T-\epsilon^{-\beta},T]
, where
T>>1
. The integral Duhamel formula gives that

(5.24)

\displaystyle\begin{split}e^{\textnormal{i}(t-T)\Delta}u(T)&=e^{\textnormal{i}(t-T)\Delta}\left(e^{\textnormal{i}T\Delta}u_{0}-\textnormal{i}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\textnormal{i}(T-s)\Delta}|x|^{-a}|u|^{p-2}u\,ds+\textnormal{i}\int_{0}^{T}e^{\textnormal{i}(T-s)\Delta}|x|^{-b}|u|^{4-2b}u\,ds\right)\\
&:=e^{\textnormal{i}t\Delta}u_{0}-\textnormal{i}\left(\int_{J_{1}}+\int_{J_{2}}\right)\left(e^{\textnormal{i}(t-s)\Delta}\left(\mathcal{N}_{a}(u)-\mathcal{N}_{b}(u)\right)\right)\,ds\\
&:=e^{\textnormal{i}t\Delta}u_{0}-\textnormal{i}\mathcal{F}_{1}-\textnormal{i}\mathcal{F}_{2}.\end{split}

First we estimate the term
\mathcal{F}_{1}
. By the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.1
), (
5.20
) and the dispersive estimate

(5.25)

\displaystyle\|e^{\textnormal{i}\cdot\Delta}u\|_{r}\lesssim|\cdot|^{-3(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}\|u\|_{r^{\prime}},\quad\forall\,\,r\geq 2,

we get that

(5.26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\mathcal{F}_{1}\|_{S(T,\infty)}&\lesssim\left\|\int_{J_{1}}|t-s|^{-3(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{10})}\|\mathcal{N}_{a}(u)-\mathcal{N}_{b}(u)\|_{\frac{10}{9}}\,ds\right\|_{L^{10}(T,\infty)}\\
&\lesssim\left\|\int_{J_{1}}|t-s|^{-\frac{6}{5}}\left(\||x|^{-a}|u|^{p-1}\|_{\frac{10}{9}}+\||x|^{-b}|u|^{5-2b}\|_{\frac{10}{9}}\right)\,ds\right\|_{L^{10}(T,\infty)}\\
&\lesssim\left\|\int_{J_{1}}|t-s|^{-\frac{6}{5}}\left(E^{p-1}+E^{5-2b}\right)\,ds\right\|_{L^{10}(T,\infty)}\\
&\lesssim\left\|(\cdot-T+\varepsilon^{-\beta})^{-\frac{1}{5}}\right\|_{L^{10}(T,\infty)}\\
&\lesssim\varepsilon^{\frac{\beta}{10}},\end{split}

where the conditions for the applications of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.1
) are satisfied, because we assumed that
0<a<1
and
0<b<\frac{4}{3}
.

Next we estimate the term
\mathcal{F}_{2}
. Let
\psi_{R}
be the radial function defined in Lemma
5.3
. By (
5.21
), then there is
T>>1
such that

(5.27)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{R}(x)|u(T,x)|^{6}\,dx<\epsilon^{6}.

Moreover, utilizing the identity
\partial_{t}|u|^{6}=-6\nabla\cdot(|u|^{4}\Im(\overline{u}\nabla u))
, integration by parts, the property of
\psi_{R}
, Hölder’s inequality and (
5.20
), we are able to infer that

(5.28)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{R}|u(t)|^{6}\,dx\right|\lesssim\left|\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(\nabla\psi_{R}\cdot\nabla u\right)|u|^{4}\bar{u}\,dx\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{R}\|u\|_{L^{6}}^{5}\|u\|_{W^{1,6}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{R}\|u\|_{W^{1,6}}.

Integrating in time on
[t,T]
for any
t\in J_{2}
, taking
R>\epsilon^{-(6-\beta)}
and invoking (
5.27
) and (
5.28
), we then get that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{R}|u(t)|^{6}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{R}|u(T)|^{6}\,dx+\left|\int_{t}^{T}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{R}|u(s)|^{6}\,dx\,ds\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{6}+\frac{\epsilon^{-\beta}}{R}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{6}.

It follows that

(5.29)

\displaystyle{\|\psi_{R}u\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{2},L^{6})}\lesssim\epsilon^{6}}.

Using the Strichartz estimates in Lemma
2.7
and the Sobolev embedding inequality in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we obtain that

(5.30)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\mathcal{F}_{2}\|_{S(T,\infty)}&\lesssim\||x|^{-a}|u|^{p-2}\nabla u\|_{L^{1}(J_{2},L^{2})}+\||x|^{-a-1}|u|^{p-2}u\|_{L^{1}(J_{2},L^{2})}\\
&\quad+\||x|^{-b}|u|^{4-2b}\nabla u\|_{L^{1}(J_{2},L^{2})}+\||x|^{-b-1}|u|^{4-2b}u\|_{L^{1}(J_{2},L^{2})}\\
&:=(I)+(II).\end{split}

In the following, we are going to estimate the terms
(I)
and
(II)
. Now, we claim that

(5.31)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{4}(J_{2},L^{\infty})}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\epsilon^{-\mu\beta}},\quad\mu>0.

We will defer the proof of (
5.31
) for now and proceed with the current proof. The term
(II)
can be estimated as follows, which is divided into two parts. As an application of Hölder’s inequality and Hardy’s inequality, we have that

(5.32)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(II)_{1}&:=\|(|x|^{-1}u)^{b}|u|^{4-3b}\nabla u\|_{L^{1}(J_{2},L^{2}(B({R/4})))}+\|(|x|^{-1}u)^{1+b}|u|^{4-3b}\|_{L^{1}(J_{2},L^{2}(B(({R/4})))}\\
&\leq{\||x|^{-1}u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{6})}^{b}}\|u\|_{L^{4}(J_{2},L^{\infty})}^{2(1-b)}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{2},L^{6}(B({R/4})))}^{2-b}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{6})}\\
&\quad+{\||x|^{-1}u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{6})}^{1+b}}\|u\|_{L^{4}(J_{2},L^{\infty})}^{2(1-b)}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{2},L^{6}(B({R/4})))}^{2-b}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{4}(J_{2},L^{\infty})}^{2(1-b)}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{2},L^{6}(B({R/4})))}^{2-b}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{6})}^{1+b}.\end{split}

Thus, for suitable choice of
0<\beta<<1
, (
4.17
), (
5.29
), (
5.31
) and (
5.32
) imply that there exists
\nu>0
such that

(5.33)

\displaystyle(II)_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\epsilon^{-2(1-b)\mu\beta+6(2-b)}\left(1+\epsilon^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)^{1+b}}\leq\epsilon^{\nu}.

On the other hand, by (
5.20
), (
5.31
), the Sobolev embedding inequality in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain that

(5.34)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(II)_{2}&:=\||x|^{-b}|u|^{4-2b}\nabla u\|_{L^{1}(J_{2},L^{2}(B^{c}({R/4})))}+\||x|^{-b-1}|u|^{4-2b}u\|_{L^{1}(J_{2},L^{2}(B^{c}({R/4})))}\\
&\leq R^{-b}\|u\|_{L^{4}({J_{2}},L^{\infty})}^{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}({J_{2}},L^{\sigma})}^{2(1-b)}\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{\sigma})}\\
&\lesssim R^{-b}{\epsilon^{-2\mu\beta}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{1})}^{2(1-b)}\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{\sigma})}\\
&\lesssim R^{-b}{\epsilon^{-2\mu\beta}}\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle u\|_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{\lambda}\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle u\|_{L^{2(1-\lambda)}(J_{2},L^{6})}^{1-\lambda}\\
&\lesssim R^{-b}{\epsilon^{-2\mu\beta}}\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{6})}^{1-\lambda}|J_{2}|^{\frac{\lambda}{2}}\\
&=R^{-b}{\epsilon^{-\left(2\mu+\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)\beta}\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{6})}^{1-\lambda}},\end{split}

where

2\leq\sigma=2(3-2b)<6,\quad 0<\lambda:=\frac{b}{3-2b}<1,\quad\frac{1}{\sigma}=\frac{\lambda}{2}+\frac{1-\lambda}{6}.

Therefore, for suitable choice of
R>>1
and
0<\beta<<1
, from (
5.34
), one similarly gets that

(5.35)

\displaystyle(II)_{2}\lesssim\epsilon^{\nu}.

Consequently, by (
5.33
) and (
5.35
), we arrive at

(5.36)

\displaystyle(II)\lesssim\epsilon^{\nu}.

Now, we estimate
(I)
as follows, which is also separated into two parts. In light of Hölder’s inequality and Hardy’s inequality, we have that

(5.37)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(I)_{1}&:=\|(|x|^{-1}u)^{a}|u|^{p-2-a}\nabla u\|_{L^{1}(J_{2},L^{2}(B({R/4})))}+\|(|x|^{-1}u)^{1+a}|u|^{p-2-a}\|_{L^{1}(J_{2},L^{2}(B({R/4})))}\\
&\leq\||x|^{-1}u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{6})}^{a}\|u\|_{L^{q}(J_{2},L^{\infty})}^{p-4}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{2},L^{6}(B({R/4})))}^{2-a}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{6})}\\
&\quad+\||x|^{-1}u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{6})}^{1+a}\|u\|_{L^{q}(J_{2},L^{\infty})}^{p-4}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{2},L^{6}(B({R/4})))}^{2-a}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{q}(J_{2},L^{\infty})}^{p-4}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{2},L^{6}(B({R/4})))}^{2-a}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{6})}^{1+a}\\
&{\leq|J_{2}|^{\frac{(4-q)(p-4)}{4q}}\|u\|_{L^{4}(J_{2},L^{\infty})}^{p-4}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{2},L^{6}(B(R/4)))}^{2-a}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{6})}^{1+a}}\\
&={\epsilon^{-\frac{\beta(4-q)(p-4)}{4q}}\|u\|_{L^{4}(J_{2},L^{\infty})}^{p-4}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{2},L^{6}(B(R/4)))}^{2-a}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{6})}^{1+a}},\end{split}

where
0\leq q=\frac{2(p-4)}{1-a}<4
.

Furthermore, arguing as the proof of (
5.34
), we are able to derive that

(5.38)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(I)_{2}&:=\||x|^{-a}|u|^{p-2}\nabla u\|_{L^{1}(J_{2},L^{2}(B^{c}(R/4)))}+\||x|^{-a-1}|u|^{p-2}u\|_{L^{1}(J_{2},L^{2}(B^{c}(R/4)))}\\
&\leq R^{-a}\|u\|_{L^{4}(J_{2},L^{\infty})}^{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(J_{2},L^{2(p-3)})}^{p-4}\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{2(p-3)})}\\
&\lesssim R^{-a}{\epsilon^{-2\mu\beta}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(H^{1})}^{p-4}\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{2(p-3)})}\\
&\lesssim R^{-a}{\epsilon^{-2\mu\beta}}\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle u\|_{L^{\infty}(L^{2})}^{\theta}\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle u\|_{L^{2(1-\theta)}(J_{2},L^{6})}^{1-\theta}\\
&\lesssim R^{-a}{\epsilon^{-2\mu\beta}}\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{6})}^{1-\theta}|J_{2}|^{\frac{\theta}{2}}\\
&=R^{-a}\epsilon^{-\left(2\mu+\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\beta}\|\left\langle\nabla\right\rangle u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{6})}^{1-\theta},\end{split}

where

0<\theta:=\frac{6-p}{2(p-3)}<1,\quad\frac{1}{2(p-3)}=\frac{\theta}{2}+\frac{1-\theta}{6}.

Therefore, for suitable choice of
R>>1
and
0<\beta<<1
, by (
5.37
) and (
5.38
), one analogously has that

(5.39)

\displaystyle(I)

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\nu}.

Hence, combining (
5.30
), (
5.36
) and (
5.39
), one gets that

(5.40)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}_{2}\|_{S(T,\infty)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{\nu}

At this stage, making use of (
5.23
), (
5.26
) and (
5.40
), we then conclude that (
5.22
) holds true.

Lastly, we move on to the proof of Claim (
5.31
). Utilizing the integral Duhamel formula, the Strichatz estimates in Lemma
2.7
, (
2.10
) and (
5.20
), we obtain that

(5.41)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u\|_{L^{4}(J_{2},L^{\infty})}&\lesssim E+\||x|^{-a}|u|^{p-2}\nabla u\|_{L^{1}(J_{2},L^{2})}+\||x|^{-a-1}|u|^{p-1}\|_{L^{1}(J_{2},L^{2})}\\
&\quad+\||x|^{-b}|u|^{4-2b}\nabla u\|_{L^{1}(J_{2},L^{2})}+\||x|^{-b-1}|u|^{5-2b}\|_{L^{1}(J_{2},L^{2})}\\
&:=(III)+(IV).\end{split}

Next we are going to estimate the term
(III)
and
(IV)
. Thanks to Hölder’s inequality and Hardy’s inequality, we infer that

\displaystyle\|(|x|^{-1}u)^{b}|u|^{4-3b}\nabla u\|+\|(|x|^{-1}u)^{1+b}|u|^{4-3b}\|

\displaystyle\leq\||x|^{-1}u\|_{6}^{b}\|u\|_{r}^{4-3b}\|\nabla u\|_{6}+\||x|^{-1}u\|_{6}^{1+b}\|u\|_{r}^{4-3b}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{r}^{4-3b}\|\nabla u\|_{6}^{1+b},

where

r:=\frac{6(4-3b)}{2-b}>0.

Furthermore, by the Sobolev embedding inequality, Hölder’s inequality and (
5.20
), we know that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{r}^{4-3b}\|\nabla u\|_{6}^{1+b}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla u\|_{\widetilde{r}}^{4-3b}\|\nabla u\|_{6}^{1+b}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|\nabla u\|^{\tau}\|\nabla u\|_{6}^{1-\tau}\right)^{4-3b}\|\nabla u\|_{6}^{1+b}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla u\|_{6}^{2},

where

\widetilde{r}:=\frac{6(4-3b)}{10-7b}>0,\quad 0<\tau:=\frac{3-2b}{4-3b}<1,\quad\frac{1}{\widetilde{r}}=\frac{\tau}{2}+\frac{1-\tau}{6}.

It then follows from (
4.17
) that

(5.42)

\displaystyle(IV)\lesssim\left(1+\epsilon^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)^{2}.

Similarly, employing Hölder’s inequality and Hardy’s inequality, one can write that

\displaystyle\|(|x|^{-1}u)^{a}|u|^{p-2-a}\nabla u\|+\|(|x|^{-1}u)^{1+a}|u|^{p-2-a}\|

\displaystyle\leq\||x|^{-1}u\|_{6}^{a}\|u\|_{\rho}^{p-2-a}\|\nabla u\|_{6}+\||x|^{-1}u\|_{6}^{1+a}\|u\|_{\rho}^{p-2-a}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{\rho}^{p-2-a}\|\nabla u\|_{6}^{1+a},

where

\rho:=\frac{6(p-2-a)}{2-a}>0,

Applying the Sobolev embedding inequality, Hölder’s inequality and (
5.20
), one then gets that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\rho}^{p-2-a}\|\nabla u\|_{6}^{1+a}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla u\|_{\widetilde{\rho}}^{p-2-a}\|\nabla u\|_{6}^{1+a}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|\nabla u\|^{\kappa}\|\nabla u\|_{6}^{1-\kappa}\right)^{p-2-a}\|\nabla u\|_{6}^{1+a}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla u\|_{6}^{\gamma},

where
1<\gamma:=\frac{p-4}{2}+1+a<2
and

\widetilde{\rho}:=\frac{6(p-2-a)}{2(p-2)+2-3a},\quad 0<\kappa:=\frac{p-2a}{2(p-2-a)}<1,\quad\frac{1}{\widetilde{\rho}}=\frac{\kappa}{2}+\frac{1-\kappa}{6}.

Thereby, using (
4.17
) and Hölder’s inequality, we derive that

(5.43)

\displaystyle(III)\lesssim\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(J_{2},L^{6})}^{\gamma}|J_{2}|^{\frac{2-\gamma}{2}}\lesssim\left(1+\epsilon^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)^{\gamma}\epsilon^{-\frac{\beta(2-\gamma)}{2}}.

As a consequence, from (
5.42
) and (
5.42
), we conclude that there exists
\mu>0
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{4}(J_{2},L^{\infty})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\epsilon^{-\mu\beta}.

The assertion (
5.31
) is now fully established and the proof is completed.
∎

2.9

5.9

R_{n}>R

6.
Blow-up

In this section, we will discuss the blow-up of solutions to the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) and provide the proof for Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
. Let

K^{c}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx,

\displaystyle I(u)=\frac{3(p-2)-2(2-a)}{6(p-2)+4a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{2(6-2b)-3(p-2)-2a}{(3(p-2)+2a)(6-2b)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
0<b<a<2
and
2+\frac{4-2a}{3}<p<6-2a
. Then there holds that

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{I(u):u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K^{c}(u)<0\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{I(u):u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K^{c}(u)\leq 0\right\}.

Proof.

Since
K^{c}(u)\leq K(u)
for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, by Lemma
2.8
, then

(6.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}m&=\inf\left\{I(u):u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K(u)<0\right\}\\
&\geq\inf\left\{I(u):u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K^{c}(u)<0\right\}.\end{split}

Let
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be such that
K^{c}(u)<0
. Define

u^{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}u(\lambda x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

It is not hard to calculate that
K^{c}(u^{\lambda})=K^{c}(u)
,
{I(u^{\lambda})=I(u)}
and

K(u^{\lambda})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx+\lambda^{\frac{p}{2}-3+a}\frac{3(p-2)+a}{2p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-a}|u|^{p}\,dx.

Then we are able to infer that
K(u^{\lambda})\to K^{c}(u)
as
\lambda\to+\infty
, because of
p<6-2a
. Therefore, there holds that

(6.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}m&=\inf\left\{I(u):u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K(u)<0\right\}\\
&\leq\inf\left\{I(u):u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K^{c}(u)<0\right\}.\end{split}

Consequently, combining (
6.1
) and (
6.2
) results in

(6.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}m&=\inf\left\{I(u):u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K(u)<0\right\}\\
&=\inf\left\{I(u):u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K^{c}(u)<0\right\}.\end{split}

Apparently, there holds that

(6.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\inf\left\{I(u):u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K^{c}(u)<0\right\}\\
&\geq\inf\left\{I(u):u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K^{c}(u)\leq 0\right\}.\end{split}

Let
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be such that
K^{c}(u)\leq 0
. Recall that

u_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}}u(\lambda x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Observe that

K^{c}(u_{\lambda})=\lambda^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\lambda^{6-2b}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx,

I(u_{\lambda})=\frac{3(p-2)-2(2-a)}{6(p-2)+4a}\lambda^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx+\frac{2(6-2b)-3(p-2)-2a}{(3(p-2)+2a)(6-2b)}\lambda^{6-2b}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx.

It then indicates that
K^{c}(u_{\lambda})<0
for any
\lambda>1
, due to
b<2
. In addition, one has that
I(u_{\lambda})\to I(u)
as
\lambda\to 1^{+}
. As a result, there holds that

\displaystyle\inf\left\{I(u):u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K^{c}(u)<0\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\inf\left\{I(u):u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K^{c}(u)\leq 0\right\}.

This together with (
6.4
) leads to

\displaystyle\inf\left\{I(u):u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K^{c}(u)<0\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{I(u):u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K^{c}(u)\leq 0\right\}.

Going back to (
6.3
), we then obtain the desired conclusion and the proof is completed.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

It follows from Theorem
1.1
that there exists no minimizers to (
1.10
), because any minimizer to (
1.10
) is a solution to (
1.8
) for
\omega=0
. Let us now prove that (
1.11
) holds true. Using Lemma
6.1
, we have that

(6.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}m&=\inf\left\{I(u):u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K^{c}(u)\leq 0\right\}\\
&\geq\inf\left\{I(u)+N(u):u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K^{c}(u)\leq 0\right\},\end{split}

where

\displaystyle N(u)

\displaystyle:=\frac{2(6-2b)-3(p-2)-2a}{(3(p-2)+2a)(6-2b)}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2(6-2b)-3(p-2)-2a}{(3(p-2)+2a)(6-2b)}K^{c}(u).

Clearly, the equality holds in (
6.5
) if and only if
N(u)=0
, i.e.

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx.

Observe that

\displaystyle\inf\left\{I(u)+N(u):u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K^{c}(u)\leq 0\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{3(p-2)(2-b)+2(2-b)a}{(6(p-2)+4a)(3-b)}\inf\left\{\|\nabla u\|^{2}:u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\},K^{c}(u)\leq 0\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{3(p-2)(2-b)+2(2-b)a}{(6(p-2)+4a)(3-b)}\inf\left\{\|\nabla u\|^{2}\left(\frac{\|\nabla u\|^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx}\right)^{1/(2-b)}:u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{3(p-2)(2-b)+2(2-b)a}{(6(p-2)+4a)(3-b)}\inf\left\{\left(\frac{\|\nabla u\|^{6-2b}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u|^{6-2b}\,dx}\right)^{1/(2-b)}:u\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\backslash\{0\}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{3(p-2)(2-b)+2(2-b)a}{(6(p-2)+4a)(3-b)}\frac{1}{{C_{*}}^{(6-2b)/(2-b)}}

(6.6)

\displaystyle=E^{c}(Q),

where
C_{*}>0
is the constant given by (
1.13
). This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
u\in C([0,T_{max}),H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
be the solution to the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) with initial datum
u_{0}\in\mathcal{K}^{-}
. First we verify that
u(t)\in\mathcal{K}^{-}
for any
t\in[0,T_{max})
. Suppose that there exists
t_{0}\in(0,T_{max})
such that
u(t_{0})\not\in\mathcal{K}^{-}
. By the conservation of laws, we know that
E(u(t))=E(u_{0})<m
for any
t\in[0,T_{max})
. Then there holds that
K(u(t_{0}))\geq 0
. Since
K(u_{0})<0
, then there exists
t_{1}\in(0,t_{0}]
such that
K(u(t_{1}))=0
, which indicates that
m\leq E(u(t_{1}))
. This is impossible by the conservation of the energy. Hence the desirable result follows.

Let
K(u)<0
, by Lemma
2.6
, then there exists
0<\lambda_{u}<1
such that
K(u_{\lambda_{u}})=0
and
E(u_{\lambda_{u}})\geq m
. In addition, the function
\lambda\mapsto E(u_{\lambda})
is concave on
[\lambda_{u},+\infty)
. It then follows that

E(u)-E(u_{\lambda_{u}})=\frac{d}{d\lambda}E(u_{\lambda})\mid_{\lambda=\xi}(1-\lambda_{u})\geq K(u)(1-\lambda_{u})\geq K(u),\quad\xi\in[\lambda_{u},1].

By the conservation of the energy and the fact that
E(u_{\lambda_{u}})\geq m
, then

K(u(t))\leq E(u_{0})-m<0,\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T_{max}).

To establish blow-up of the solution, we need to introduce the following localized virial quantity defined by

V_{R}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{R}(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}\,dx,\quad\psi_{R}(x):=R^{2}\psi\left(\frac{|x|}{R}\right),

where
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is a radial function such that
\psi(r)=r^{2}
for
0\leq r\leq 1
,
\psi(r)=0
for
r\geq 3
,
\partial_{r}\psi(r)\leq 2r
and
\partial_{r}^{2}\psi(r)\leq 2
for
r\geq 0
. It is easy to notice that
\Delta\psi_{R}(r)=6
for
0\leq r\leq R
and
\Delta^{2}\psi_{R}(r)=0
for
0\leq r\leq R
. In the spirit of Lemma
5.1
, then there holds that

\displaystyle V^{\prime\prime}_{R}(t)

\displaystyle=4\textnormal{Re}\sum_{j,k=1}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{r}^{2}\psi_{R}|\nabla u(t)|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Delta^{2}\psi_{R}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2(p-2)}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-a}|u(t)|^{p}\Delta\psi_{R}\,dx-\frac{4}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\left(|x|^{-a}\right)\cdot\nabla\psi_{R}|u(t)|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{4-2b}{3-b}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u(t)|^{6-2b}\Delta\psi_{R}\,dx+\frac{2}{3-b}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\left(|x|^{-b}\right)\cdot\nabla\psi_{R}|u(t)|^{6-2b}\,dx.

Further, we can compute that

\displaystyle V^{\prime\prime}_{R}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim 8K(u(t))+R^{-2}\int_{R\leq|x|\leq 3R}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+R^{-a}\int_{R\leq|x|\leq 3R}|u(t)|^{p}\,dx+R^{-b}\int_{R\leq|x|\leq 3R}|u(t)|^{6-2b}\,dx.

Applying Lemma
2.3
and the conservation of the mass, one gets that

R^{-a}\int_{R\leq|x|\leq 3R}|u(t)|^{p}\,dx\lesssim R^{-(p-2)-a}\|\nabla u(t)\|^{\frac{p-2}{2}},

R^{-b}\int_{R\leq|x|\leq 3R}|u(t)|^{6-2b}\,dx\lesssim R^{-(p-4)}\|\nabla u(t)\|^{2-b}.

Using Young’s inequality, then, for any
\epsilon>0
small, then there exists
R>1
large enough such that

(6.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}V^{\prime\prime}_{R}(t)&\lesssim 8K(u(t))+\epsilon\|\nabla u(t)\|^{2}+\epsilon\\
&=16(3-b)E(u(t))-(16-8b-\epsilon)\|\nabla u(t)\|^{2}\\
&\quad-\frac{16(3-b)-12(p-2)-8a}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-a}|u(t)|^{p}\,dx+\epsilon\\
&\leq 16(3-b)E(u(t))-(16-8b-\epsilon)\|\nabla u(t)\|^{2}+\epsilon,\end{split}

where we used fact that
16(3-b)-12(p-2)-8a>0
is due to
b<a
and
p<6-2a
.
Since
K(u(t))<0
for any
t\in[0,T_{max})
, by (
1.13
), Lemma
2.8
and (
6.6
), then we know that

\displaystyle m\leq I(u(t))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3(p-2)(2-b)+2(2-b)a}{(6(p-2)+4a)(3-b)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{-b}|u(t)|^{6-2b}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3(p-2)(2-b)+2(2-b)a}{(6(p-2)+4a)(3-b)}C_{*}^{6-2b}\|\nabla u(t)\|^{6-2b}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3(p-2)(2-b)+2(2-b)a}{(6(p-2)+4a)(3-b)}\right)^{3-b}m^{b-2}\|\nabla u(t)\|^{6-2b},

from which we obtain that

(6.8)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u(t)\|^{2}\geq\frac{(6(p-2)+4a)(3-b)}{3(p-2)(2-b)+2(2-b)a}m.

Since
E(u_{0})<m
, by the conservation of the energy, then there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that
E(u(t))\leq(1-\delta_{0})m
for any
t\in[0,T_{max})
. Taking
\epsilon>0
small enough and applying (
6.7
) and (
6.8
), we then derive that

\displaystyle V^{\prime\prime}_{R}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim 16(3-b)(1-\delta_{0})m-\frac{(6(p-2)+4a)(3-b)(16-8b-\epsilon)}{3(p-2)(2-b)+2(2-b)a}m+\epsilon

\displaystyle=16(3-b)(1-\delta_{0})m-16(3-b)m+\frac{(6(p-2)+4a)(3-b)\epsilon}{3(p-2)(2-b)+2(2-b)a}m+\epsilon

\displaystyle<-8(3-b)\delta_{0}m,

where we used the equality

\frac{(6(p-2)+4a)(16-8b)}{3(p-2)(2-b)+2(2-b)a}=16.

It obviously follows that
u
blows up in finite time and the proof is completed.
∎

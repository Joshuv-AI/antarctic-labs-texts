Electroweak evolution equations and isospin conservation
Paolo Ciafaloni a, Giampaolo Co’ a, Dimitri Colferai b, Denis Comelli c

Abstract
In processes taking place at energies much higher than the weak scale, electroweak corrections can be taken into account by using electroweak evolution equations, that are analogous to the DGLAP equations in QCD. We show that weak isospin conservation in these equations imposes to modify the expressions of the splitting functions commonly used in the literature. These modifications have a profound impact on the parton distribution functions.



I
Introduction

Electroweak evolution equations, which are analogous to the
Dokshitzer, Gribov, Lipatov, Altarelli, Parisi (DGLAP)
equations in Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD)
DGLAP
,
are of primary importance when the energies of the processes considered are much larger than those of the weak scale.
Indeed, at center-of-mass energies
Q
much higher than the electroweak
(EW) symmetry breaking scale
M\sim
100 GeV, radiative EW corrections grow like

\log^{2}(Q/M)

Ciafaloni:1998xg
;
verza
. One loop corrections reach the 30% level at the TeV scale, and, for this reason, keeping the perturbative series under control is challenging
NLL
, and will be particularly important for next generation of very high energy colliders
collider
. Moreover such EW corrections are present even for fully inclusive quantities
Ciafaloni:2000df
in contrast with QCD where large cancellations between real and virtual corrections take place, and are therefore ubiquitous whenever the initial state is charged under SU(2). With the purpose of addressing these issues, EW evolution equations have been developed in
Ciafaloni:2001mu
;
Ciafaloni:2005fm
.
These new equations
allow the resummation of all the terms
{\cal O}[\alpha\log^{2}(Q/M)]^{n}

of infrared/collinear origin, and of the
{\cal O}[\alpha\log(Q/M)]^{n}

terms of collinear
origin (actually, terms of order
\alpha^{n}\log^{k}(Q/M)
with
n\leq k\leq 2n
are resummed).
The EW evolution equations are integro-differential equations involving the so called
splitting functions, that are the kernels of the equations themselves.

The main point of the present work is to advocate the use of splitting functions that differ from those used until now in the literature since they include a cutoff near
z=0
,
z
being the momentum fraction. For instance, in the case of the kernel
P_{gf}^{R}
that describes the splitting of a fermion
f
into a gauge boson
g
and a final state fermion, we have that

\frac{1+(1-z)^{2}}{z}\to\frac{1+(1-z)^{2}}{z}\theta\left(z-\frac{\mu}{Q}\right)\;\;,

(1)

where in the left hand side there is the standard expression of the splitting function
and in the right hand side the one we propose here.
The variable
\mu
indicates
the soft sliding scale with respect to which the functions are evolved (see section
II
). As we explain in section
III
, the need to modify the splitting functions arises on one hand from the probabilistic interpretation of the Parton Distribution Functions (PDFs), and on the other hand from quantum numbers conservation (symmetries of the theory).

The introduction of the modified splitting functions produces sizeable effects on the PDFs, as we show in section
IV
. Indeed, the distribution functions we obtain differ significantly from the standard ones, not only in the region close to
z=0
but also for
z
of order 1. In section
V
we discuss a point which has been overlooked in the literature so far: the equivalence between the ultraviolet
(UV) evolution equations, that are evolved with respect to a hard scale
q
, and the
infrared (IR) evolution equations, where the running scale is a soft one
\mu
. We show that the two approaches are indeed equivalent, but they produce the same PDFs only with an appropriate choice of cutoffs.

II
The Electroweak Evolution Equations

Figure 1:
The overall cross section
\sigma(AB\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}+X)

In this work we consider a overall cross section
\sigma(AB\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}+X)

\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

We can write:

\sigma(AB\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}+X)=\sum_{i,j}\int dx_{i}dx_{j}\,f_{iA}(x_{i},{M})\;\sigma_{ij}^{H}(x_{j}x_{i}Q^{2})\;f_{jB}(x_{j},{M}),

\sigma^{H}
being the hard (partonic) cross serction. Note that X includes, besides the customary QED and QCD radiation, also EW gauge bosons with their decay products. ElectroWeak Evolution Equations (EWEEs) describe the scale dependence of the PDFs

i
inside parton
j
.
These functions depend on the momentum fraction
x

of the daughter particle and on
\varepsilon=\mu/Q
, where
\mu

is a running scale with the physical meaning of lower cutoff for
the daughter’s transverse momentum.
The PDFs
f_{ij}(x,\mu/Q)
are evaluated by solving the evolution equations
starting from the initial condition at
\mu=Q
and letting them evolve until

\mu
reaches the weak scale value
M
. At this point,
we obtain the final result
f_{ij}(x,M/Q)
.

In this work, we use a subset of the Standard Model (SM) equations,
specifically we consider only left leptons and we set the U(1) coupling constant

g^{\prime}
equal to zero. As a consequence, we have to deal
with only 7 different partons, that are gauge eigenstates:
the left fermions electron
e
and neutrino
\nu
,
the corresponding antifermions
\bar{e},\bar{\nu}

and the transverse gauge bosons
W_{-},W_{3},W_{+}
.
This limitation makes
our results not suitable to be directly compared with experimental data.
However, the purpose of our work consists in
clearly showing that the splitting functions in the EW evolution equations
need to be modified and that these modifications have a relevant effect on the PDFs.

We found convenient to use a basis of definite isospin and CP quantum numbers. We label
L_{T}^{CP}
the left fermion eigenstate with isospin
T

G_{T}^{CP}
.
We adopt the classification of states defined in
Ciafaloni:2005fm
, with respect to which we use a slightly different normalization:

f_{*\,L_{0}^{+}}=\frac{f_{*\,\nu}+f_{*\,e}+f_{*\,\bar{\nu}}+f_{*\,\bar{e}}}{2},\quad f_{*\,L_{0}^{-}}=\frac{f_{*\,\nu}+f_{*\,e}-f_{*\,\bar{\nu}}-f_{*\,\bar{e}}}{2}\;\;,

(2)

f_{*\,L_{1}^{+}}=\frac{f_{*\,\nu}-f_{*\,e}+f_{*\,\bar{\nu}}-f_{*\,\bar{e}}}{2},\quad f_{*\,L_{1}^{-}}=\frac{f_{*\,\nu}-f_{*\,e}-f_{*\,\bar{\nu}}+f_{*\,\bar{e}}}{2}\;\;,

(3)

f_{*\,G_{0}^{+}}=\frac{f_{*\,W_{+}}+f_{*\,W_{3}}+f_{*\,W_{-}}}{\sqrt{3}},\quad f_{*\,G_{1}^{-}}=\frac{f_{*\,W_{+}}-f_{*\,W_{-}}}{\sqrt{2}},\quad f_{*\,G_{2}^{+}}=\frac{f_{*\,W_{+}}-2f_{*\,W_{3}}+f_{*\,W_{-}}}{\sqrt{6}}\;\;,

T,CP

A=(L_{0}^{+},L_{0}^{-},L_{1}^{+},L_{1}^{-},G_{0}^{+},G_{1}^{-},G_{2}^{+})

The matrix

However, working in the total isospin

(6)

for a total of 11 independent PDFs that can be grouped in the following combinations:

\displaystyle(T=0,CP=+):\begin{array}[]{|cc|}f_{L_{0}^{+}\,L_{0}^{+}}&f_{L_{0}^{+}\,G_{0}^{+}}\\
f_{G_{0}^{+}\,L_{0}^{+}}&f_{G_{0}^{+}\,G_{0}^{+}}\end{array}\;,\quad(T=1,CP=-):\begin{array}[]{|cc|}f_{L_{1}^{-}\,L_{1}^{-}}&f_{L_{1}^{-}\,G_{1}^{-}}\\

\displaystyle(T=0,CP=-):f_{L_{0}^{-}\,L_{0}^{-}},\quad(T=1,CP=+):f_{L_{1}^{+}\,L_{1}^{+}}\;,\quad(T=2,CP=+):f_{G_{2}^{+}\,G_{2}^{+}}\;\;.

By considering the various reaction channels, for the IR evolution equations
we have the following kernels depending on the variable
\varepsilon
defined above:

P_{ff}^{V}=-\delta(1-z)\left(\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}-\frac{3}{2}\right);\quad P_{ff}^{R}=\frac{1+z^{2}}{1-z}\theta(1-\varepsilon-z)\;\;,

(12)

(13)

(14)

and produce the threshold effect proportional to
\theta(z-\varepsilon)
.
In the next section we will discuss the origin of these terms.

Schematically, the equations in the two different basis can be written as:

-\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\epsilon}f_{ij}(x,\varepsilon)=[f_{ik}\otimes P^{G}_{kj}](x,\varepsilon)\;\;,\qquad\qquad-\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\epsilon}f_{AB}(x,\varepsilon)=[f_{AC}\otimes P^{I}_{CB}](x,\varepsilon)\;\;,

(15)

where we have defined the convolution:

[f\otimes P](x,\varepsilon)=\int_{x}^{1}\frac{dz}{z}f(z,\varepsilon)P\left(\frac{x}{z},\varepsilon\right)\;.

(16)

In the above equations,
P^{G}
is a
7\times 7
matrix with all elements different from zero.
Also

is a
7\times 7
matrix, but it is block-diagonal: as can be seen from Eq. (

submatrix in the

The evolution equations have therefore a much simpler form in the isospin basis; we now write such equations.
We use the values of
P^{I}
taken from
Ciafaloni:2005fm
and obtain:

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\epsilon}f_{\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{-}\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{-}}(x,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}[f_{\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{-}\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{-}}\otimes(P_{ff}^{R}+P^{V}_{ff})](x,\varepsilon)\;\;,

(17)

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\epsilon}f_{\scriptscriptstyle L_{1}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{1}^{+}}(x,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle[f_{\scriptscriptstyle L_{1}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{1}^{+}}\otimes P^{V}_{ff}](x,\varepsilon)-\frac{1}{4}f_{\scriptscriptstyle L_{1}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{1}^{+}}\otimes(P_{ff}^{R}+P^{V}_{ff})\;\;,

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\epsilon}f_{\scriptscriptstyle G_{2}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle G_{2}^{+}}(x,\varepsilon)

\displaystyle 3[f_{\scriptscriptstyle G_{2}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle G_{2}^{+}}\otimes P^{V}_{gg}](x,\varepsilon)-[f_{\scriptscriptstyle G_{2}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle G_{2}^{+}}\otimes(P_{gg}^{R}+P^{V}_{gg})](x,\varepsilon)\;\;.

(19)

In the

(20)

The same happens in the
1^{-}
channel, with
A=L_{1}^{-}
and
A=G_{1}^{-}

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\epsilon}f_{\scriptscriptstyle A\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{1}^{-}}(x,\varepsilon)&=[f_{\scriptscriptstyle A\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{1}^{-}}\otimes P^{V}_{ff}](x,\varepsilon)-\frac{1}{4}[f_{\scriptscriptstyle A\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{1}^{-}}\otimes(P_{ff}^{R}+P^{V}_{ff})](x,\varepsilon)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[f_{\scriptscriptstyle A\,\scriptscriptstyle G_{1}^{-}}\otimes P^{R}_{gf}](x,\varepsilon)\;\;,\\

Overall we have

these initial conditions can be expressed as:

f_{AB}(x,\varepsilon=1)=\delta_{AB}\delta(1-x)\;\;.

(22)

III
Sum rules and cutoffs for the splitting functions

In this section we show that sum rules on the splitting functions
enforce the presence of precisely determined cutoffs near
z=0
.
Sum rules are requirements on the integrals over
z
of
P_{ij}(z)
and

zP_{ij}(z)
, obtained from the conservation of the total momentum and of
the quantum numbers.

In the literature, in all the works where IR corrections are resummed,
Fadin:1999bq
;
Melles:2001ye
;
Ciafaloni:1999ub
;
Ciafaloni:2001mu
,
the cutoffs near
z=1
have been considered.
In the present work, we introduce for the first time, to the best of our knowledge,
additional cutoffs near
z=0
.
We present in this Section the sources of these new cutoffs, while their
quantitative relevance on the values of the distribution functions
f_{ij}(z,\varepsilon)

will be discussed in Sect.
IV
.

Conserved quantities involve integrals of the distribution functions.
For instance, if the initial particle is a neutrino, then
the probability to become an electron is
\int dzf_{e\nu}(z,\varepsilon)
.
If we are interested in fermion number conservation, the initial fermion number, 1, must be conserved when we sum over all possible final states:

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dz(f_{e\nu}(z,\varepsilon)+f_{\nu\nu}(z,\varepsilon)-f_{\bar{\nu}\nu}(z,\varepsilon)-f_{\bar{e}\nu}(z,\varepsilon))

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{1}dzf_{\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{-}\,\scriptscriptstyle\nu}(z,\varepsilon)=\int_{0}^{1}dzf_{\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{-}\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{-}}(z,\varepsilon)\;\;,

where we used Eqs. (
2
-
4
) for both left and right indices, and
considered the conservation of the weak isospin. This condition must be satisfied for every value of
\varepsilon
.
This means that
the evolution equations must have kernels which allows the conservation of the
condition (
23
).

In order to explore the consequences of Eq. (
23
), it is convenient to write the evolution
in terms of the Mellin transform, i.e. by defining the
N
-th order moments:

\tilde{f}(N,\varepsilon)=\int_{0}^{1}dzf(z,\varepsilon)z^{N-1}\;\;,

(24)

such that Eq. (
15
) become factorized, with the ordinary product:

-\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\epsilon}\tilde{f}_{ij}(N,\varepsilon)=\tilde{f}_{ik}(N,\varepsilon)\,\tilde{P}^{G}_{kj}(N,\varepsilon)\qquad;\qquad-\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\epsilon}\tilde{f}_{AB}(N,\varepsilon)=\tilde{f}_{AC}(N,\varepsilon)\,\tilde{P}^{I}_{CB}(N,\varepsilon)\;\;.

(25)

Using these equations in the
N=1
case, and deriving Eq. (
23
) with respect
to
\varepsilon
we obtain:

0=-\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\epsilon}{\tilde{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{-}\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{-}}(1,\varepsilon)=\frac{3}{4}{\tilde{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{-}\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{-}}(1,\varepsilon)\,(\tilde{P}^{R}_{ff}(1,\varepsilon)+\tilde{P}^{V}_{ff}(1,\varepsilon))\;\;.

(26)

Since the value of
{\tilde{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{-}\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{-}}(1,\varepsilon)
depends on
\varepsilon
, therefore it
is arbitrary, the above equation is satisfied for every value of
\varepsilon
if

\tilde{P}^{R}_{ff}(1,\varepsilon)+\tilde{P}^{V}_{ff}(1,\varepsilon)=0\;\;.

We can now use Eq. (
12
) to obtain:

\tilde{P}^{R}_{ff}(1,\varepsilon)+\tilde{P}^{V}_{ff}(1,\varepsilon)=\int_{0}^{1}dz(P^{R}_{ff}(z,\varepsilon)+P^{V}_{ff}(z,\varepsilon))=2\varepsilon+{\cal O}(\varepsilon^{2})\to 0\;\;.

(27)

The sum rule is not exact, but leading terms in
\log\varepsilon
and subleading
constant terms cancel with each other,
while there is a remnant of (irrelevant) mass-suppressed terms.

We consider now the momentum conservation. Since
z
is the momentum fraction of the daughter (final) particle, considering a parent (initial) neutrino and
operating as we have done in the case of the fermion number we have:

1=\sum_{j}\int_{0}^{1}dz\,zf_{j\nu}(z,\varepsilon)=2{\tilde{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle\nu}(2,\varepsilon)+\sqrt{3}{\tilde{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle G_{0}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle\nu}(2,\varepsilon)={\tilde{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{+}}(2,\varepsilon)+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}{\tilde{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle G_{0}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{+}}(2,\varepsilon)\;\;.

(28)

Analogously, for a neutral gauge boson initial state we obtain:

1=\sum_{j}\int dzzf_{jW_{3}}(z,\varepsilon)=2{\tilde{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle W_{3}}(2,\varepsilon)+\sqrt{3}{\tilde{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle G_{0}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle W_{3}}(2,\varepsilon)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}{\tilde{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle G_{0}^{+}}(2,\varepsilon)+{\tilde{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle G_{0}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle G_{0}^{+}}(2,\varepsilon)\;\;.

(29)

We now use Eq. (
20
) in the
0^{+}
channel:

\displaystyle 0=\displaystyle{-\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\epsilon}}\left({\tilde{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{+}}(2,\varepsilon)+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}{\tilde{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle G_{0}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{+}}(2,\varepsilon)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}\left[\left({\tilde{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{+}}(2,\varepsilon)+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}{\tilde{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle G_{0}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{+}}(2,\varepsilon)\right)(\tilde{P}^{R}_{ff}+\tilde{P}^{V}_{ff})(2,\varepsilon)+\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}{\tilde{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle G_{0}^{+}}(2,\varepsilon)+{\tilde{f}}_{\scriptscriptstyle G_{0}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle G_{0}^{+}}(2,\varepsilon)\right)\tilde{P}_{gf}^{R}(2,\varepsilon)\right]\;\;,

and considering again Eqs. (
28
,
29
) we obtain the sum rule that can be seen to be obeyed by the splitting functions (
12
-
14
):

\tilde{P}^{R}_{ff}(2,\varepsilon)+\tilde{P}^{V}_{ff}(2,\varepsilon)+\tilde{P}^{R}_{gf}(2,\varepsilon)={\cal O}(\varepsilon)\rightarrow 0\;\;.

(30)

Up to this point we have considered sum rules that the electroweak evolution equations have in common with those of
the QCD. Indeed, in the case of strong interactions, due to confinement, initial states are color singlets, that
correspond to the
T=0
evolution equations. In the case of EW interactions instead, initial states have
isospin quantum numbers and, therefore, further equations in the channels
T=1
and
T=2
are present.
A consequence of this feature of the EW equations is that the double logs terms of IR origin
do not cancel. This effect has been called “Bloch-Nordsieck (BN) violation”
Ciafaloni:2000df
.

We now proceed to show that EW evolution equations lead
to sum rules that are absent in QCD, and are responsible ultimately for the necessity of precise cutoffs in the splitting functions near
z=0
.

The conservation of the third component of the weak isospin,
T_{3}
, is related to the
T=1,CP=-
structure function. Taking a neutrino and a neutral gauge boson as initial particles, we have:

\displaystyle t^{3}_{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}=\sum_{i}t^{3}_{i}\tilde{f}_{i\nu}(1,\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{f}_{L_{1}^{-}L_{1}^{-}}(1,\varepsilon)+\sqrt{2}\tilde{f}_{G_{1}^{-}L_{1}^{-}}(1,\varepsilon))\;\;,

(31)

\displaystyle t^{3}_{W^{+}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1=\sum_{i}t^{3}_{i}\tilde{f}_{iW^{+}}(1,\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\tilde{f}_{L_{1}^{-}G_{1}^{-}}(1,\varepsilon)+\sqrt{2}\tilde{f}_{G_{1}^{-}G_{1}^{-}}(1,\varepsilon))\;\;.

(32)

We use the evolution equation (
21
) and the relations (
31
,
32
)
and we operate as in the case of momentum conservation.
We finally obtain:

\displaystyle 3\tilde{P}^{V}_{ff}(1,\varepsilon)-\tilde{P}^{R}_{ff}(1,\varepsilon)+4\tilde{P}^{R}_{gf}(1,\varepsilon)=0\Rightarrow\tilde{P}^{R}_{ff}(1,\varepsilon)=\tilde{P}^{R}_{gf}(1,\varepsilon)\;\;,

(33)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\tilde{P}^{R}_{fg}(1,\varepsilon)+\tilde{P}^{R}_{gg}(1,\varepsilon)+2\tilde{P}^{V}_{gg}(1,\varepsilon)=0\;\;.

(34)

where the last step in Eq. (
33
) has been obtained by using Eq. (
27
).

Let us consider first the sum rule (
33
).
If we use the standard expression for
P^{R}_{gf}
, i.e.
that without the cut close to
z=0
, i.e.

P_{gf}^{R}(z)\to\frac{1+(1-z)^{2}}{z}\;\;,

(35)

Eq. (
33
) cannot be satisfied since
\tilde{P}^{R}_{ff}(1,\varepsilon)
is finite, while
\tilde{P}^{R}_{gf}(1,\varepsilon)
diverges due to the singularity for
z\to 0
proportional to
1/z
.
If instead we impose a cutoff and use the expression for
\tilde{P}^{R}_{gf}(1,\varepsilon)

of Eq. (
14
), then the sum rule is satisfied.

The following points are worth to be emphasized.

•

\tilde{P}^{R}_{ff}(1,\varepsilon)
is divergent for
z\to 1
while
\tilde{P}^{R}_{gf}(1,\varepsilon)
is divergent for
z\to 0
, therefore the sum rule connects the (known) cutoffs of IR origin close to 1 with the (new) cutoffs close to 0.

•

The value of the cutoff
z>\varepsilon
is precisely determined by the requirement of
Eq. (
33
). With a different value like
z>2\varepsilon
the sum rule would be violated.

•

The presence of the cutoff is justified from a probabilistic point of view.
We expect that the probability for a fermion to become a fermion of momentum fraction
z

has to be the same of the probability of becoming a gauge boson of momentum fraction

1-z
: both are related to the same tree level diagram (see diagram
(a)
in Fig.
3
).

Similar considerations hold for
P^{R}_{gg}
, whose integral in
z
must satisfy
Eq. (
34
). This is obtained by introducing
the cutoff near
z=0
which has been highlighted by the square brackets
in Eq. (
13
).

Finally, we point out
that the new splitting functions are
P_{gf}
and
P_{gg}
and they involve the splitting into a gauge boson. Indeed, the need for a cutoff close to 0 arises because of the

1/z
singularity related to the IR dynamics of a soft gauge boson.

IV
Quantitative results

In this section, we compare the PDFs obtained with the uncut splitting functions,
i.e. the standard ones without cutoff, with those obtained with our splitting
functions which include the cutoff.

In order to illustrate the different features characterizing the two types of PDFs,
we consider first the analytical expressions of the first order terms of a perturbative expansion
in powers of
\alpha
:

f_{AB}(x,\varepsilon)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\alpha^{i}f_{AB}^{(i)}(x,\varepsilon)\;\;,

(36)

where the zeroth order term is dictated by the initial conditions

f_{AB}^{(0)}(x,\varepsilon)=\delta_{AB}\delta(1-x)\;\;,

(37)

which indicate that, in the no-emission case, the probability
of finding a particle inside itself is equal to the unity.

Using the perturbative expansion in Eqs. (
17
-
21
),
we obtain analytical results for all first order terms of the PDFs.
For instance, in the case of the
(L_{0}^{+},G_{0}^{+})
channel we have

f_{L_{0}^{+},G_{0}^{+}}^{(1)}(x,\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}\frac{d\varepsilon^{\prime}}{\varepsilon^{\prime}}P^{R}_{fg}(x,\varepsilon^{\prime})\;\;,

(38)

and we obtain two different results for our, cut (c), and
standard, uncut (u), cases:

f_{\scriptscriptstyle G_{0}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{+}}^{(1)c}(x,\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\frac{1+(1-x)^{2}}{x}\theta(x-\varepsilon)\log\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\qquad;\qquad f_{\scriptscriptstyle G_{0}^{+}\,\scriptscriptstyle L_{0}^{+}}^{(1)u}(x,\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\frac{1+(1-x)^{2}}{x}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\;\;.

(39)

Already from these first order expressions we see that the differences between the PDFs obtained
with our and standard splitting functions are relevant. Indeed, in
the standard case the behaviour close to 0 is
\propto 1/x
and therefore divergent,
while in our case the PDF is
\propto(1/x)\log(x/\varepsilon)
therefore it is
continuous and, because of the step function, is 0 for
0\leq x\leq\varepsilon
.

V
IR and UV evolution equations

The EW evolution equations were originally derived as IR equations
Ciafaloni:2001mu
;
Ciafaloni:2005fm
, i.e. equations where the varying scale is an infrared parameter
\mu
having the meaning of lower bound on transverse momentum of the emitted particles. From an alternative point of view,
different groups
Bauer:2017isx
;
Garosi:2023bvq
used
UV evolution equations, where the varying scale is a parameter
q
having the meaning of upper bound on the transverse momentum of the emitted particles. It is not clear that the two approaches produce the same results.
In this section, we show that the solutions of the UV and IR evolution equations coincide,
when the same splitting functions, including the cutoffs, are consistently used in both approaches.

Figure 5:
IR (left) and UV (right) evolution equations.
The dashed lines represent particles belonging to the final state.

We consider splitting functions depending not only on the momentum fraction

z
but also on the transverse momentum
\mbox{$k$}_{\perp}
:
P_{AB}(z,\mbox{$k$}_{\perp})
. In the IR equation the contribution of the emitted particle with lower momentum is factorized (fig.
5
,left side); calling
\mu
the lower cutoff on this momentum we have:

\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}f^{IR}_{AB}(x;\mu,Q)=-f_{AC}^{IR}\otimes P_{CB}(\mu)\qquad;\qquad f^{IR}(x,\mu=Q)=f_{0}(x)\;\;.

(40)

In the case of UV equations instead, the contribution of the hardest emitted particle is factorized (fig.
5
,right side).
We call
q
the upper bound on the transverse momentum and obtain:

q\frac{\partial}{\partial q}f^{UV}_{AB}(x;M,q)=P_{AC}(q)\otimes f^{UV}_{CB}\qquad;\qquad f^{UV}(x,q=M)=f_{0}(x)\;\;.

(41)

The formal solution, in the IR case, is given by:

f^{IR}(\mu,Q)=f_{0}\otimes{\rm K}\{\exp[P]\}(\mu,Q)\;\;,

(42)

having defined:

\displaystyle{\rm K}\{\exp[P]\}_{ij}(\mu,q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta(1-z)\delta_{ij}+\int_{\mu}^{q}P_{ij}(k)\frac{dk}{k}

(43)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 2}\int\displaylimits_{\mu<k_{n}<k_{n-1}<\cdots<k_{1}<q}(\Pi_{i}\frac{dk_{i}}{k_{i}})P_{ii_{1}}(k_{1})\otimes P_{i_{1}i_{2}}(k_{2})\otimes\cdots\otimes P_{i_{n}j}(k_{n})\;\;.

Eq. (
42
) is indeed solution of Eq. (
40
), since
the only variable depending on
\mu
in the ordered product of Eq. (
43
) is
k_{n}
.
When we derive with respect to
\mu
we obtain (minus) the value of the integrand calculated for
k_{n}=\mu
and this gives the solution of (
40
). It is then sufficient to
implement the initial condition
f_{0}
.

A similar reasoning applies to the UV case.
In this case, only
k_{1}
depends on
q
and we have:

f^{UV}(M,q)={\rm K}\{\exp[P]\}(M,q)\otimes f_{0}\;\;.

(44)

Since we eventually set
\mu=M
in (
42
) and
q=Q
in (
44
), the IR case and UV case differ only because of a different placement of the
f_{0}
function. However, since we have

f_{0}=\delta(1-z)\delta_{AB}
and since
\delta(1-z)
is the identity for convolutions and
\delta_{AB}
is the identity in the
P
matrices space, the two solutions are equal:

f^{IR}(M,Q)=f^{UV}(M,Q)={\rm K}\{\exp[P]\}(M,Q)\;\;.

(45)

This establishes the equivalence between the IR and the UV approaches.

As a final comment, we remark
that Eqs. (
40
) and (
41
) are equivalent, i.e. they produce the same solutions for the PDFs, if, and only if, the same splitting functions are used, albeit with different arguments.
If, as in the case treated here,
there is a cutoff for
x<1-\mu/Q
in
P_{AB}(z,\mu)
for the IR equations,
then, in the ultraviolet equations a cutoff at
x<1-q/Q
must appear in
P_{AB}(z,q)
.
This might sound trivial, but the point is that in the literature different cutoffs appear.
For instance in
Bauer:2017isx

the cutoff is rather
z<1-M/q
, leading, in general, to PDFs that differ from
those we obtain.

VI
Summary, conclusions and perspectives

At c.m. energies much grater than the weak scale, energy growing Electroweak Radiative corrections can be taken into account by defining Parton Distribution Functions (PDFs) that obey Electroweak Evolution Equations (EWEE), in analogy with DGLAP in QCD.
In this work we propose to modify EWEEs with respect to what has been done until now in the literature and we analyze the impact on PDFs of these modifications.
In particular, electroweak interactions are characterized by isospin 1 evolution equations
that are absent in the corresponding isospin 0 QCD (DGLAP) and QED equations.
Isospin conservation, related to these isospin 1 equations, requires to modify the splitting functions (that are the kernels of EWEEs) by adding suitable cutoffs.
The solutions (PDFs) obtained with these new kernels differ significantly from the ones using the standard kernels used in the literature until now (see fig.
4
).

In this work we have also addressed the issue of comparing the results obtained by using a IR approach (ours) with previous results obtained using a UV approach, as is customary in the literature. We have shown that UV equations indeed produce the same PDFs as IR equations, but only if a careful choice of the cutoffs in the splitting functions is made. Finally, let us note that work has still to be done in order to provide theoretical results that can be compared with the experimental measurements. First, QCD and QED interactions have to be added and then the full particle spectrum of the Standard Model has to be considered, while we chose to consider only a subset. Moreover, for small momentum fraction
x
, additional terms proportional to
\log x
will possibly have to be added to the equations
Ciafaloni:2008cr
.

The modifications described here will be particularly relevant if
a 100 TeV hadronic collider and/or a TeV scale muon collider will see the light.

The Global well-posedness for Klein-Gordon-Hartree equation in modulation spaces
Divyang G. Bhimani

Abstract
. Modulation spaces have received considerable interest recently as it is the natural function spaces to consider low regularity Cauchy data for several nonlinear evolution equations. We establish global well-posedness for 3D Klein-Gordon-Hartree equation u t ​ t − Δ u + u + ( | ⋅ | − γ ∗ | u | 2 ) u = 0 u_{tt}-\Delta u+u+(|\cdot|^{-\gamma}\ast|u|^{2})u=0 with initial data in modulation spaces M 1 p , p ′ × M p , p M^{p,p^{\prime}}_{1}\times M^{p,p} for p ∈ ( 2 , 54 27 − 2 ​ γ ) , p\in\left(2,\frac{54}{27-2\gamma}\right), 2 < γ < 3 . 2<\gamma<3. We implement Bourgain’s high-low frequency decomposition method to establish global well-posedness, which was earlier used for classical Klein-Gordon equation. This is the first result on low regularity for Klein-Gordon-Hartree equation with large initial data in modulation spaces (which do not coincide with Sobolev spaces).

1.
Introduction

We study the Cauchy problem for Klein-Gordon-Hartree equation

(1.1)

\begin{cases}u_{tt}-\Delta u+u+(V\ast|u|^{2})u=0\\
u|_{t=0}=f,\quad\partial_{t}u|_{t=0}=g\end{cases}(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Here,
u(t,x)\in\mathbb{C}
and
V
is a Hartree kernel given by

V(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{\gamma}}\quad(0<\gamma<3).

The equation (
1.1
) has appeared in the context of various physical models, e.g. one-component plasma, relativistic particles, quantum semiconductor devices, etc.. See
[
25
]
and the references therein for a general review.
The case
\gamma=1
in
V
is known as Coulomb potential, which play an important role in quantum mechanics. Compared with the classical Klein-Gordon equation with local non-linearity
|u|^{p-1}u
, the nonlinearity

(V\ast|u|^{2})u
is non local.

Formally, the solution of (
1.1
) conserves the energy

\displaystyle E(u(t),\partial_{t}u(t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\partial_{t}u(t,x)|^{2}+|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}+|u(t,x)|^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(t,x)|^{2}|u(t,y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{\gamma}}dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(f,g)

and the momentum

(1.2)

\displaystyle P(u)(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u_{t}(t,x)\nabla u(t,x)dx=P(u)(0).

In the early 1980s, G. Perla and W. Strauss
[
19
]
have initiated
the study of (
1.1
). Later W. W. Strauss
[
27
, Theorem 19]
(cf
[
26
, Theorem 11]
) proved global well-posedness and scattering for (
1.1
) with
V\in L^{r}\ (1\leq r\leq 3/2)
in the energy space
H^{1}\times L^{2}.
Mochizuki
[
24
]

proved small data global well-posedness and scattering for (
1.1
) with
2\leq\gamma<3
in
H^{1}\times L^{2}.
Later Miao-Zheng
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
generalized this result for the general initial data in
H^{1}\times L^{2}
for subcritical non-linearity (i.e.
2<\gamma<3
). In
[
23
]
, Miao-Zheng established global well-posedness for defocusing (
1.1
) in higher dimensions in
H^{1}\times L^{2}.

While Miao and Zhang
[
21
, Theorem 1.1]
proved global well-posedness for (
1.1
) with low regularity Cauchy data, i.e. data in below energy space
H^{s}\times H^{s-1}\ (\gamma/4<s<1)
for
2<\gamma<3.
See also
[
22
]
. We note that these results did not cover Coulomb potential. We also remark that all previous authors have studied (
1.1
) on
L^{2}-
based Sobolev spaces. Mainly because Klein-Gordon semigroup
e^{it(I-\Delta)^{1/2}}
fails to be bounded on
L^{p}
if
p\neq 2.
Hence we cannot expect to solve equations (
1.1
) in
L^{p}-
spaces for
p\neq 2
. The question arises if it is possible to remove
L^{2}
constraint and consider (
1.1
) in function spaces which are not just
L^{2}
based.

In this paper we study (
1.1
) with Cauchy data in the modulation spaces
M^{p,q}_{s}-
which are based on mixed
\ell_{s}^{q}(L^{p})
norms and
contain rougher Cauchy data than in any given fractional Bessel potential space (see e.g.
[
3
, Chapter 6]
). In order to define these spaces, we briefly introduce some notations.
Let
\rho\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(Schwartz space),
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,1]
be a smooth function satisfying
\rho(\xi)=1\ \text{if}\ \ |\xi|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
\rho(\xi)=0\ \text{if}\ \ |\xi|_{\infty}\geq 1.
Here,
|\xi|_{\infty}=\max\{|\xi_{i}|:\xi=(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{d})\}.
Let
\rho_{k}
be a translation of
\rho,
that is,

\rho_{k}(\xi)=\rho(\xi-k)\ (k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}).

Denote

\sigma_{k}(\xi)=\frac{\rho_{k}(\xi)}{\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\rho_{l}(\xi)}\quad(k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}).

The frequency-uniform decomposition operators can be defined by

\square_{k}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\sigma_{k}\mathcal{F}

where
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
denote the Fourier and inverse Fourier transform respectively. The weighted modulation spaces
M^{p,q}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ (1\leq p,q\leq\infty,s\in\mathbb{R})
is defined as follows:

M^{p,q}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{s}}:\left\|\|\square_{k}f\|^{q}_{L^{p}_{x}}(1+|k|)^{s}\right\|_{\ell^{q}_{k}}<\infty\right\}.

See also Remark
1.4
. For
s=0,
we write
M^{p,q}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).
For
p\in[1,\infty]
, we denote
p^{\prime}
the Hilbert conjugate, i.e.
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1.

It is known that

M^{2,2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ \text{(Sobolev space)}\quad\text{and}\quad M^{p,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ \text{for}\ p\in[2,\infty].

In the last two decades
M^{p,q}_{s}-
spaces have been considered extensively as low regularity Cauchy data spaces for several nonlinear evolution equations, see
[
6
,
7
,
2
,
5
,
1
,
29
,
30
,
4
,

,
11
,

]
. We shall briefly mention some of them here.
In the seminal work of
J. Ginibre and G. Velo
[
16
, Proposition 3.2]
, they proved global well-posedness in
H^{1}\times L^{2}
for the classical Klein-Gordon (CKG) equation, i.e. (
1.1
) with local nonlinearity
|u|^{2}u
(in other words, (
1.1
) with
V
is the Dirac delta distribution). While in the seminal work of B. Wang and H. Hudzik
[
29
, Theorem 1.6]
, they established low regularity small data global well-posedness for the CKG equation in some modulation spaces
M^{2,1}_{s}\times M^{2,1}_{s-1}.
See also
[
32
]
. In
[
2
, Theorem 1.3]
, Bényi and Okoudjou proved local well-posedness for CKG
in
M^{p,1}_{s}.
See also
[
7
, Theorem 4.8]
. Recently L. Chaichenets and N. Pattakos
[
10
]
proved global well-posedness for CKG equation in
M_{1}^{p,p^{\prime}}\times M^{p,p}
for

sufficiently close to
2.

Now we state our main results.

Theorem 1.1

(Local well-posedness)
.

Let
(f,g)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})+M_{1}^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),
where
p_{\gamma}=\frac{18}{9-2\gamma}
and
0<\gamma<3.
Then, there exists
T=T(\|(f,g)\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}+M_{1}^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}\times M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}})>0
such that (
1.1
) has unique solution

u\in C([0,T],H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))+C([0,T],M_{1}^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))

with
\partial_{t}u\in u\in C([0,T],L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))+C([0,T],M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).
Moreover, if
T^{*}
is the maximum time of existence then the blowup alternative holds, i.e., if
T^{*}<\infty,
then

\limsup_{t\to T^{*}}\|\left(u(t,\cdot),\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right)\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}+M_{1}^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}\times M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}}=\infty.

For
\gamma\in(0,3)
we have
p_{\gamma}\in(2,6).

Notice that
M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{p_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is a sharp embedding and up to now we cannot get the local well-posedness of (
1.1
) in
L^{p_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
but in
M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Remark 1.1
.

We have several comments for Theorems
1.1
.

(1)

The proof of local well-posedness is based on Banach contraction principle. Specifically, we show that the integral version of (
1.1
), i.e. the operator
\Phi
in (
3.2
), is a contraction on some suitable ball.

(2)

In order to control the linear part in (
3.2
), we use the fact that Klein-Gordon semigroup is bounded on modulation spaces (see Proposition
2.4
below). While in order to control the nonlinear part in (
3.2
), we make use of Strichartz estimates, Hardy-Littlewood Sobolev inequality and Sobolev embedding.

(3)

We shall see that the life span of the local solution depends on the norm of the initial data, and so one can follow standard procedure to establish blowup alternative.

(4)

In order to treat Hartree non-linearity, we consider
p_{\gamma}=\frac{18}{9-2\gamma},
which turned out to be compatible with the several inequalities we used. See (
3.6
).

Theorem 1.2

(Global well-posedness)
.

Let real initial data
(f,g)\in M_{1}^{p,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times M^{p,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
where
p\in\left(2,\frac{54}{27-2\gamma}\right)
with
2<\gamma<3.

Then the local solution constructed in Theorem
1.1
of (
1.1
) extends globally and lies in

u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))+C(\mathbb{R},M_{1}^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))

with
\partial_{t}u\in C(\mathbb{R},L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))+C(\mathbb{R},M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

Theorem
1.2
is new and complement known results for (
1.1
) in
[
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
4
]
.
Exploiting the decay estimate (Proposition
2.2
), Bhimani
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]
proved small data global well-posedness for (
1.1
) for in some modulation space. While Theorem
1.2
establishes global well-posedness for any data (large) in some modulation spaces.

Remark 1.2
.

We briefly discuss proof strategy and imposed hypothesis of Theorem
1.2
.

(1)

The proof of global well-posedness is inspired from Bourgain’s high-low frequency decomposition method (see
[
8
, Section IV.2]
) and Chaichenets-Pattakos’s Theorem 1.2 in
[
10
]
)-where they considered CKG equation. See also
[
13
, Section 4.2]
and
[
28
, Section 3.9]
.

(2)

Using interpolation theory decompose initial data into two parts:

(f,g)=(f_{N},g_{N})+(f^{N},g^{N})\in X_{0}+Y_{0},

as in (
3.10
), say
(f_{N},g_{N})
good part (low frequencies) and
(f^{N},g^{N})
bad part (high frequency).

(3)

In view of blow-up alternative, it is sufficient to prove that

\|\left(u(t,\cdot),\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right)\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}+M_{1}^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}\times M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}}<\infty

on any bounded time interval
I=[0,T]
to ensure the global existence.

(4)

To this end, we convert (
1.1
) into Schrödinger equation

iv_{t}-Bv-B^{-1}\left([V\ast|\text{Re}\ v|^{2}]\text{Re}\ v\right)=0,\quad v(0)=F_{N}+F^{N}\in H^{1}+M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}

via change of variables
B=(Id-\Delta)^{1/2}.
See (
3.12
)- (
3.14
). Since the linear part is bounded on modulation space (Propositions
2.3
and
2.4
), we are left to control the energy space
H^{1}-
norm of
\tilde{v}(t)=(v(t)-e^{-itB}F^{N})
. This we shall do this using the Hamiltonian
H
. See (
3.15
).

(5)

Define
I(t)=H(\tilde{v}(t)).
We shall show that
I(0)\lesssim N^{\frac{4\theta}{1-\theta}}.
The restriction on
2<\gamma
comes at this stage. See (
3
). We push this forward to
[0,T]
by looking at
I(t)
.

(6)

We shall observe that
T\to\infty
as
N\to\infty;
which will yield global existence. This will impose conditions on
p.
In fact, the condition on
p
comes due to (
3.27
)- where we make use of the identity (
3.9
) in order to obtain the upper bound for
p
in terms of Hartree exponent
\gamma.

Remark 1.3
.

Our method of proof should be applied to treat higher dimension

(|\cdot|^{-\gamma}\ast|u|^{\alpha})|u|^{\alpha-2}u

Remark 1.4
.

In the early 1980s Feichtinger
[
14
]
introduced a class of Banach spaces, the so called modulation spaces, which allow a measurement of space variable and Fourier transform variable of a function or distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
simultaneously using the short-time Fourier transform (STFT). The STFT of a tempered distribution
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with respect to a window function
0\neq g\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(Schwartz space) is defined by

\displaystyle V_{g}f(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t)\overline{g(t-x)}e^{-2\pi iy\cdot t}dt,\ (x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}

whenever the integral exists. It is known
[
14
]
,
[
29
, Proposition 2.1]
that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{s}}\asymp\left\|\|V_{g}f(x,y)\|_{L^{p}_{x}}(1+|y|^{2})^{s/2}\right\|_{L_{y}^{q}}.

Preliminaries

Proposition 2.1

(
[
3
,
30
]
, see Theorem 9.9 in
[
9
]
)
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{R}.

(1)

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow M^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow S^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(2)

M^{p,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
p\in[2,\infty].

(3)

H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
p\in\left[2,\frac{2d}{d-2}\right]
(Sobolev embedding).

Recall that equation (
1.1
) have the following
equivalent form

u(t,x)=K^{\prime}(t)f(x)+K(t)g(x)-\mathcal{B}f(u),

where

K(t)=\frac{\sin t(I-\Delta)^{1/2}}{(I-\Delta)^{1/2}},\ \ K^{\prime}(t)=\cos t(I-\Delta)^{1/2},\ \ \mathcal{B}=\int_{0}^{t}K(t-\tau)\cdot d\tau.

Notice that the (semi-) group given by

\mathbb{K}(t)=\left(\begin{matrix}K^{\prime}(t)&K(t)\\
(\Delta-I)K(t)&K^{\prime}(t)\end{matrix}\right)\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}

is isometric on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with domain of its generator being
\mathcal{D}=H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proposition 2.2

(See Proposition 4.2 in
[
29
]
)
.

Let
G(t)=e^{it(I-\Delta)^{1/2}}\ (t\in\mathbb{R}).

(1)

Let
s\in\mathbb{R},2\leq p\leq\infty,

1\leq q<\infty,\theta\in[0,1],
and

2\sigma(p)=(d+2)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right).

Then we have

\|G(t)f\|_{M^{p,q}_{s}}\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-d\theta(1/2-1/p)}\|f\|_{M^{p^{\prime},q}_{s+\theta 2\sigma(p)}}.

(2)

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty.
Then we have

\|G(t)f\|_{M^{p,q}_{s}}\leq C(1+|t|)^{d\left|1/2-1/p\right|}\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{s}}.

Proposition 2.3

(See Theorem 6.6 in
[
14
]
, Proposition 2.4 in
[
29
]
)
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and

Then the map
(Id-\Delta)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}
is an isomorphism from
M^{p,q}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
onto
M^{p,q}_{s-\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proposition 2.4

(Uniform boundedness of Klein-Gordon propagator, See Corollary 3.2 in
[
12
]
)
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and

. Then for every
T>0

C_{T}=C_{T}(p,q,s)
such that

\|K(t)f\|_{M^{p,q}_{s+1}}+\|K^{\prime}(t)f\|_{M^{p,q}_{s}}\leq C_{T}\|f\|_{M^{p,q}_{s}}

for all

and all

Definition 2.1
.

We say that the pair
(q,r)\in[2,\infty]\times[2,\infty)
is (wave-)admissible if

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}+\frac{d-1}{2r}\leq\frac{d-1}{4}.

We will denote by
q_{a}(r)
the unique solution of the gap condition

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{d}{r}=\frac{d}{2}-1.

In Theorem
2.1
we recall well known Strichartz estimates (see

[
17
, Corollary 1.3]
,
[
31
, Estimate 1.1]
,
and
[
15
, Section 2.2]
for details). We use
L_{t}^{r}([0,T],X)
to denote the space time norm

\|u\|_{L^{r}_{T}X}=\|u\|_{L^{r}_{t}([0,T]X)}=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|u\|^{r}_{X}dt\right)^{1/r}.

where
X
is a Banach space.

Theorem 2.1

(Strichartz estimates)
.

If
u
is a solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
(f,g)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),
then we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}_{T}L^{r}}+\|u\|_{C_{T}H^{1}}+\|\partial_{t}u\|_{C_{T}L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{1}}+\|g\|_{L^{2}}+\|(V\ast|u|^{2})u\|_{L_{T}^{1}L^{2}}

for every admissible pair
(q,r)
that satisfies the gap condition in Definition
2.1
, i.e.

q=\left(d\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}\right)-1\right)^{-1},\quad r\in\left[\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{d-1}\right)^{-1},\frac{2(d+1)}{d-3}\right].

We briefly recall some useful fact in interpolation theory. We refer to
[
18
]
for a comprehensive introduction. We say that the Banach spaces
X,Y
form the interpolation couple
\{X,Y\}
if there is a topological Hausdroff space
\mathcal{V}
such that
X,Y\subset\mathcal{V}
and
X,Y\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}.
For an interpolation couple
\{X,Y\},t\in(0,\infty)
and
z\in X+Y,
we set (the
K-
functional)

K(t,z)=\inf_{{z=x+y}\atop{x\in X,y\in Y}}\left(\|x\|_{X}+t\|y\|_{Y}\right).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\{X,Y\}
be an interpolation couple and
\theta\in(0,1).
Put
\|z\|_{(X,Y)_{\theta,\infty}}=\sup_{t>0}\left[t^{-\theta}K(t,z)\right].
The real interpolation space is defined by

(X,Y)_{\theta,\infty}=\left\{z\in X+Y:\|z\|_{(X,Y)_{\theta,\infty}}<\infty\right\}.

Proposition 2.5

(See Lemma 16 in
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\{X,Y\}
be an interpolation couple,
\theta\in(0,1)
and
f\in[X,Y]_{\theta}.
Then for any
N\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
there is an
f_{N}\in X
and
f^{N}\in Y
such that

\|f_{N}\|_{X}\lesssim N^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}\|f\|_{[X,Y]_{\theta}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|f^{N}\|_{Y}\lesssim\frac{1}{N}\|f\|_{[X,Y]_{\theta}}.

Proposition 2.6

(Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality)
.

Assume that
0<\gamma<d
and
1<p<q<\infty
with

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\gamma}{d}-1=\frac{1}{q}.

Then

\||\cdot|^{-\gamma}\ast f\|_{L^{q}}\leq C_{d,\gamma,p}\|f\|_{L^{p}}.

Proposition 2.7

(Complex interpolation, see Theorem 6.1 D in
[
14
]
)
.

Let
p_{0},p_{1},q_{0},q_{1}\in[1,\infty]
such that
(q_{0},q_{1})\neq(\infty,\infty).
Furthermore, let
s_{0},s_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\theta\in(0,1).
Define
s=(1-\theta)s_{1}+\theta s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in[1,\infty]
via

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}.

Finally, define
q\in[1,\infty)
via

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}}.

Then

\left[M^{p_{0},q_{0}}_{s_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),M^{p_{1},q_{1}}_{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right]_{\theta}=M^{p,q}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

3.
Proof of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We may decompose initial data

(f,g)\in X:=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})+M_{1}^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

as follows

(3.1)

\displaystyle(f,g)=(f_{0},g_{0})+(\tilde{f_{0}},\tilde{g_{0}})=(f_{0}+\tilde{f_{0}},g_{0}+\tilde{g_{0}})

where

(f_{0},g_{0})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\quad\text{and}\quad(\tilde{f_{0}},\tilde{g_{0}})\in M_{1}^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

We present an argument for the first coordinate for the solution
(u,\partial_{t}u)
, i.e. for
u
only. As taking Theorem
2.1
into account, the argument for
\partial_{t}u
is similar.
Consider the Banach space

X(T)=X_{1}(T)+X_{2}(T)

where

X_{1}(T)=L^{\infty}([0,T],H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\quad\text{and}\quad X_{2}(T)=L^{\infty}([0,T],M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

The norm in
X(T)
is given by

\|u\|_{X(T)}=\inf_{{u=u_{1}+u_{2}}\atop{u_{1}\in X_{1}(T),u_{2}\in X_{2}(T)}}\left(\|u_{1}\|_{X_{1}(T)}+\|u_{2}\|_{X_{2}(T)}\right).

We write (
1.1
) as

(3.2)

\displaystyle u(t)=K^{\prime}(t)f+K(t)g-\int_{0}^{t}K(t-\tau)(V\ast|u|^{2})u(\tau)d\tau:=\Phi(u)(t).

We consider the ball
B(R,T)=\{u\in X(T):\|u\|_{X(T)}\leq R\}
for some
R
and
T>0
(to be chosen below).
We shall show that for the operator
\Phi
is a contraction on
B(R,T)
for some
R
and
T>0.
We shall first show that
\Phi
is a self map on
B(R,T).
We fix
u\in B(R,T)
and consider decomposition
u=v+w,

v\in X_{1}(T)
and
w\in X_{2}(T).

Without loss of generality we may assume that
T\leq 1.

By (
3.1
), Theorem
2.1
and Proposition
2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|K^{\prime}(t)f+K(t)g\|_{X(T)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|K^{\prime}(t)f_{0}+K(t)g_{0}+K^{\prime}(t)\tilde{f_{0}}+K(t)\tilde{g_{0}}\|_{X(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|K^{\prime}(t)f_{0}+K(t)g_{0}\|_{X_{1}(T)}+\|K^{\prime}(t)\tilde{f_{0}}+K(t)\tilde{g_{0}}\|_{X_{2}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+\|g_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+C(T)\left(\|\tilde{f_{0}}\|_{M_{1}^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}}+\|\tilde{g_{0}}\|_{M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|f_{0}\|_{H^{1}}+\|g_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\tilde{f_{0}}\|_{M_{1}^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}}+\|\tilde{g_{0}}\|_{M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}}.

Since the decomposition of a given data is arbitrary ((
3.1
)), we obtain

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|K^{\prime}(t)f+K(t)g\|_{X(T)}\lesssim\|(f,g)\|_{X}.

We now choose
R\approx 2\|(f,g)\|_{X}.

By Proposition
2.1
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle X(T)\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{p_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).

Notice that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{9}+\frac{9-2\gamma}{18}=\frac{1}{2},\quad\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3}=1\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{9-2\gamma}{9}+\frac{\gamma}{3}-1=\frac{\gamma}{9}.

By Theorem
2.1
, Hölder inequality, Proposition
2.6
(with
p=\frac{9}{9-2\gamma},q=\frac{9}{\gamma},d=3
) and (
3.4
), we obtain

(3.6)

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}K(t-\tau)(V\ast|u|^{2})u(\tau)d\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|(V\ast|u|^{2})u\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{2}}=\left\|\|(V\ast|u|^{2})u\|_{L^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\|(V\ast|u|^{2})\|_{L^{\frac{9}{\gamma}}}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{18}{9-2\gamma}}}\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|V\ast|u|^{2}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{T}L^{\frac{9}{\gamma}}}\|u\|_{L^{3}_{T}L^{\frac{18}{9-2\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\frac{18}{9-2\gamma}}}\right\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}_{T}}\|u\|_{L^{3}_{T}L^{\frac{18}{9-2\gamma}}}=\|u\|^{3}_{L^{3}_{T}L^{\frac{18}{9-2\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle T\|u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{\frac{18}{9-2\gamma}}}^{3}=T\|u\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}L^{p_{\gamma}}}^{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle T\|u\|_{X(T)}^{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle TR^{3}.

Using this together with (
3.3
) and taking
T=\frac{1}{2R^{2}}=\frac{1}{8\|(f,g))\|_{X}^{2}}
, we conclude that
\Phi
is self map on
B(R,T).
Next, we shall prove
\Phi
is a contraction on
B(R,T).

Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in X(T).
Recall the identity

(3.7)

\displaystyle(V\ast|u_{1}|^{2})u_{1}-(V\ast|u_{2}|^{2})u_{2}=(V\ast|u_{1}|^{2})(u_{1}-u_{2})+(V\ast(|u_{1}|^{2}-|u_{2}|^{2}))u_{2}.

Using (
3.7
) and performing similar arguments as above, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u_{1})-\Phi(u_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|(V\ast|u_{1}|^{2})(u_{1}-u_{2})\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{2}}+\|(V\ast(|u_{1}|^{2}-|u_{2}|^{2}))u_{2}\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle T\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{p_{\gamma}}}^{2}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{p_{\gamma}}}+\left\|\||u_{1}|^{2}-|u_{2}|^{2}\|_{L^{\frac{9}{9-2\gamma}}}\right\|_{L_{T}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\|u_{2}\|_{L^{3}_{T}L^{\frac{18}{9-2\gamma}}}.

By Cauchy–Schwartz and Hölder inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\||u_{1}|^{2}-|u_{2}|^{2}\|_{L^{\frac{9}{9-2\gamma}}}\right\|_{L_{T}^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\|(u_{1}-u_{2})\bar{u_{1}}\|_{L^{\frac{9}{9-2\gamma}}}+\|(\bar{u_{1}}-\bar{u_{2}})u_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{9}{9-2\gamma}}}\right\|_{L_{T}^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left\|\|\bar{u_{1}}\|_{L^{\frac{18}{9-2\gamma}}}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{18}{9-2\gamma}}}+\|(\bar{u_{1}}-\bar{u_{2}})\|_{L^{\frac{18}{9-2\gamma}}}\|u_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{18}{9-2\gamma}}}\right\|_{L_{T}^{\frac{3}{2}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\bar{u_{1}}\|_{L^{3}_{T}L^{\frac{18}{9-2\gamma}}}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{3}_{T}L^{\frac{18}{9-2\gamma}}}+\|\bar{u_{1}}-\bar{u_{2}}\|_{L^{3}_{T}L^{\frac{18}{9-2\gamma}}}\|u_{2}\|_{L^{3}_{T}L^{\frac{18}{9-2\gamma}}}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u_{1})-\Phi(u_{2})\|_{X_{1}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim T\left(\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{p_{\gamma}}}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{p_{\gamma}}}\|u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{1}}+\|u_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{p_{\gamma}}}\right)\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{p_{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim TR^{2}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{X(T)}

By choosing
T
further small enough (if necessary), it follows that
\Phi
is a contraction on
B(R,T)
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
(f,g)\in M_{1}^{p,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times M^{p,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).
By Proposition
2.7
, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle M^{p,p^{\prime}}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=\left[H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\right]_{\theta},\quad M^{p,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})=\left[L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\right]_{\theta}

where
\theta\in(0,1)
is fixed by

(3.9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{2}+\frac{\theta}{p_{\gamma}}.

By Proposition
2.5
, there is a decomposition

(3.10)

\displaystyle(f,g)=(f_{N},g_{N})+(f^{N},g^{N})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})+M_{1}^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

with

(3.11)

\displaystyle\|f_{N}\|_{H^{1}},\|g_{N}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim N^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}},\quad\|f^{N}\|_{M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}_{1}},\|g^{N}\|_{M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}}\lesssim\frac{1}{N}

for any
N>0
(to be fixed later, depending on time
T
).

Note that in view of Theorem
1.1
for real initial data
(f,g)
there is a real-valued local solution
u
to (
1.1
).
Now we shall see that the solution constructed before in Theorem
1.1
is global in time. In fact, in view of
blowup alternative, to prove Theorem
1.2
, it suffices to prove that the norm

\|\left(u(t,\cdot),\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right)\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}+M_{1}^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}\times M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}}

remain finite on
any
bounded time intervals
I=[0,T]
. We may now perform change of variable as in
[
8
, Equation IV. 2.4]
.
Specifically, we let

v=u+iB^{-1}u_{t}

where
B^{2}=Id-\Delta.
We can rewrite (
1.1
) in the form

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}iv_{t}-Bv-B^{-1}\left([V\ast|\text{Re}\ v|^{2}]\text{Re}\ v\right)=0\\
v(0)=u(0)+iB^{-1}\left(u_{t}(0)\right)=F_{N}+F^{N},\end{cases}

where

F_{N}:=f_{N}+iB^{-1}(g_{N})\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\quad\text{and}\quad F^{N}:=f^{N}+iB^{-1}(g^{N})\in M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

By (
3.11
) and Proposition
2.3
(with
\sigma=s=1
), we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\|F_{N}\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim N^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}},\quad\|F^{N}\|_{M_{1}^{p_{\gamma},p_{\gamma}^{\prime}}}\lesssim\frac{1}{N},

and
more generally,

(3.14)

\displaystyle\|\left(u(t,\cdot),\partial_{t}u(t,\cdot)\right)\|_{H^{1}\times L^{2}+M_{1}^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}\times M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}}\approx\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}+M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}_{1}}.

The Hamiltonian of (
3.12
) (cf.
[
8
, Equation IV.2.7]
) is formally given by the formula

(3.15)

\displaystyle H(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left[\frac{1}{2}|Bv|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(V\ast|\text{Re}\ v|^{2})|\text{Re}\ v|^{2}\right]dx.

However, we cannot use it to control the full solution
v,
since (
3.15
) does not make sense for general

By Proposition
2.4
the linear evolution

never blows up in modulation spaces on any bounded time interval

Specifically, by Proposition
2.4

\displaystyle\|e^{-itB}\|_{M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}_{1}\to M_{1}^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}}\lesssim 1\quad\text{for all}\ t\in[0,T].

Therefore, we are left to control

\displaystyle\tilde{v}:=v-e^{-itB}F^{N}

on

(see

8
, Equation IV 2.21]
) using the Hamiltonian.
By (

) and (

), we have
\tilde{v}(0)=v(0)-F^{N}=F_{N}.
Define

Given
\gamma\in(2,3),
we can choose

(3.17)

\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}=1,\quad\quad\frac{1}{r_{1}}+\frac{\gamma}{3}-1=\frac{1}{q_{1}}\quad\text{and}\quad r_{1}<q_{1}.

By (
3.17
) and Hölder inequality, Propositions
2.6
and
2.1
, and (

\displaystyle I(0)=H(F_{N})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left[\frac{1}{2}|BF_{N}|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(V\ast|\text{Re}F_{N}|^{2})|\text{Re}F_{N}|^{2}\right]dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\|F_{N}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\||F_{N}|^{2}\|_{L^{r_{1}}}\|F_{N}\|^{2}_{L^{2p_{1}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|F_{N}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\|F_{N}\|_{H^{1}}^{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{\frac{2\theta}{1-\theta}}+N^{\frac{4\theta}{1-\theta}}

(3.18)

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{\frac{4\theta}{1-\theta}}.

We aim to estimate the time
T
(as a function of
N
) that preserves (
3
), i.e.
T
such that for all
0\leq t\leq T
we have

(3.19)

\displaystyle I(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left[\frac{1}{2}|B\tilde{v}(t,x)|^{2}+\frac{1}{4}(V\ast|\text{Re}\ \tilde{v}(t,x)|^{2})|\text{Re}\ \tilde{v}(t,x)|^{2}\right]dx

\displaystyle\leq

Exploiting ideas from

and
[
8
, Equation IV 2.26]
, we may formally obtain

\displaystyle I^{\prime}(t)=\text{Im}\left\langle B\tilde{v},(V\ast|\text{Re}\ v|^{2})\text{Re}\ v-(V\ast|\text{Re}\ \tilde{v}|^{2})\text{Re}\ \tilde{v}\right\rangle.

By Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and (

) we have

(3.20)

\displaystyle|I^{\prime}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|B\tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}}\|(V\ast|\text{Re}\ v|^{2})\text{Re}\ v-(V\ast|\text{Re}\ \tilde{v}|^{2})\text{Re}\ \tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle I(t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(V\ast|\text{Re}\ v|^{2})\text{Re}\ v-(V\ast|\text{Re}\ \tilde{v}|^{2})\text{Re}\ \tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle N^{\frac{2\theta}{1-\theta}}\|(V\ast|\text{Re}\ v|^{2})\text{Re}\ v-(V\ast|\text{Re}\ \tilde{v}|^{2})\text{Re}\ \tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}}.

In view of (
3.7
) and (
3.16
), we have

(3.21)

\displaystyle\left|(V\ast|\text{Re}\ v|^{2})\text{Re}\ v-(V\ast|\text{Re}\ \tilde{v}|^{2})\text{Re}\ \tilde{v}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|(V\ast(|\text{Re}\ v|^{2})e^{-itB}F^{N}\right|+\left|(V\ast(|\text{Re}\ v|^{2}-|\text{Re}\ \tilde{v}\ |^{2}))\text{Re}\ \tilde{v}\right|.

In view of (
3.5
) and similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.1

2.3
(with

) and Proposition
2.1
(

), and (

), we obtain

(3.22)

\displaystyle\left\|(V\ast(|\text{Re}\ v|^{2})e^{-itB}F^{N}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|e^{-itB}F^{N}\|_{L^{\frac{18}{9-2\gamma}}}\|(V\ast|\text{Re}v|^{2})\|_{L^{\frac{9}{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|e^{-itB}F^{N}\|_{M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}}\|\text{Re}\ v\|^{2}_{L^{p_{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|F^{N}\|_{M_{1}^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}}\|e^{-itB}F^{N}\|_{L^{p_{\gamma}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|F^{N}\|_{M_{1}^{p_{\gamma},p_{\gamma}}}\|e^{-itB}F^{N}\|_{M^{p_{\gamma},p^{\prime}_{\gamma}}}^{2}

2.6

(

\displaystyle\|(V\ast(|\text{Re}\ v|^{2}-|\text{Re}\ \tilde{v}\ |^{2}))\text{Re}\ \tilde{v}\|_{L^{2}}

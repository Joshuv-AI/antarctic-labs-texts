1.
Introduction

Let
q
be an indeterminate and let
\mathfrak{G}
be an affine Kac-Moody Lie algebra.
The category
\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}
of finite-dimensional integrable modules over the quantum affine algebra
\mathcal{U}_{q}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{G})
takes a central and important part in the study of representations of quantum affine algebras (see
[
2
,
16
]
and references therein).
The
Hernandez-Leclerc category

\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0}
is a distinguished monoidal subcategory of
\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}
defined by certain fundamental modules (see
[
13
]
for precise definition). Hernandez-Leclerc showed a cluster-algebraic approach to the Grothendieck ring
K(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0})
of the Hernandez-Leclerc category
\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0}
, which led us to several remarkable research results (see
[
5
,
6
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
for examples).
The
quantum Grothendieck ring

\mathcal{K}_{t}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0})
is a
t
-deformation of the Grothendieck ring
K(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0})
inside a quantum torus, where
t
is an indeterminate (
[
12
,
33
,
35
]
).
It turns out that there exist several subalgebras of
\mathcal{K}_{t}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0})
having (quantum) cluster algebra structures of
skew-symmetric type

with initial clusters arising from the
(q,t)
-characters
of Kirillov–Reshetikhin modules (see
[
6
,
13
,
15
]
).
For the sake of simplicity, we assume that
\mathfrak{G}
is of simply-laced affine ADE type, and write
\mathbf{g}
for the finite ADE simple Lie algebra inside
\mathfrak{G}
with an index set
I
.
As an algebra, a presentation of
\mathcal{K}_{t}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0})
was studied in
[
14
]
. The algebra
\mathcal{K}_{t}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0})
has an infinitely many generators
f_{i,p}
(
i\in I
,
p\in\mathbb{Z}\mspace{1mu}
) and they satisfy the quantum Serre relations among
\{f_{i,p}\}_{i\in I}
and
t
-boson relations among
\{f_{i,p},f_{j,p+1}\}_{i,j\in I}
for each
p\in\mathbb{Z}\mspace{1mu}
.
Thus, for each
p\in\mathbb{Z}\mspace{1mu}
, the subalgebra of
\mathcal{K}_{t}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0})
generated by
f_{i,p}
(
i\in I
) is isomorphic to the quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}(\mathbf{g})
. This observation suggests that the algebra
\mathcal{K}_{t}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0})
can be regarded as an
affinization
of
\mathcal{U}_{t}^{-}(\mathbf{g})
.
We denote by
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbf{g}}}
the algebra defined formally by the generators
f_{i,p}
(
i\in I
,
p\in\mathbb{Z}\mspace{1mu}
) and the same defining relations as in
\mathcal{K}_{t}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0})
, which is called the
bosonic extension
of the quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{t}(\mathbf{g})
. By construction, there is an isomorphism
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbf{g}}}\simeq\mathcal{K}_{t}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0})
.
Despite the significance of the algebra
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbf{g}}}
, the algebra does not seem to have been intensively explored from the perspective of ring theory (we refer
[
14
, Introduction §1.3]
).
Our interest lies in the algebra structure of
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
for
any
simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

The bosonic extension
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
is defined in Definition
3.1
for a Cartan matrix
\mathsf{C}=(c_{i,j})_{i,j\in I}
of
arbitrary finite type
. In Definition
3.1
, we use
q
as an indeterminate to follow the usual notation in quantum groups. Remark
3.2
explains the relation between two indeterminates
q
and
t
.
Note that, for any quantum affine algebra
\mathcal{U}_{q}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{G})
of
arbitrary untwisted affine type
,
the quantum Grothendieck ring
\mathcal{K}_{t}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0})
is isomorphic to the bosonic extension
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbf{g}}}
associated with some
symmetric
Cartan matrix
\mathsf{C}_{\mathbf{g}}
(see Remark
3.2
). When Cartan matrix
\mathsf{C}
is of
non-symmetric type
, the bosonic extension
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
is isomorphic to the
quantum virtual Grothendieck ring
introduced in

[
25
,
17
,
18
]
. Similar to quantum Grothendieck ring, the quantum virtual Grothendieck ring is realized as a subalgebra inside a quantum torus and possesses a (quantum) cluster algebra structure.
However, the asymmetry of
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
aligns with that of
\mathfrak{g}
, whereas the asymmetry of
\mathcal{K}_{t}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0})
is consistently skew-symmetric (see
[
18
]
).
It was shown that
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
has an action of the (generalized) braid group
\mathtt{B}_{\mathsf{C}}
associated with the Cartan matrix
\mathsf{C}
(see
[
21
]
for symmetric types and
[
17
]
for general types). The braid group action
\mathcal{T}_{i}
(
i\in I
) on
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
is similar to Lusztig’s braid symmetry on
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
(
[
31
]
) in local parts and is understood as a ring-theoretic counterpart of the braid group action on the
extended crystal
(see
[
26
,
34
]
). It is conjectured in
[
21
]
that the braid group actions
\mathcal{T}_{i}
can be lifted to autofunctors on the category
\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0}
.

In this paper, we introduce a family of new subalgebras
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathtt{b})
of
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
for any elements
\mathtt{b}
in the positive submonoid
\mathtt{B}_{\mathsf{C}}^{+}
of the braid group
\mathtt{B}_{\mathsf{C}}
associated with
\mathsf{C}

and develop the
Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theory
(shortly PBW theory) for the subalgebra
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathtt{b})
using the braid group actions
\mathcal{T}_{i}
(
i\in I
).
Let
\mathsf{W}_{\mathsf{C}}
be the Weyl group associated with
\mathsf{C}
and
\pi:\mathtt{B}_{\mathsf{C}}^{+}\twoheadrightarrow\mathsf{W}_{\mathsf{C}}
the natural projection (see (
2.5
)).
When there is
w\in\mathsf{W}_{\mathsf{C}}
such that
w=\pi(\mathtt{b})
and
\ell(w)=\ell(\mathtt{b})
,
the subalgebra
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathtt{b})
coincides with the
quantum unipotent coordinate algebra

A_{q}(\mathfrak{n}(w))
.
Note that the algebra
A_{q}(\mathfrak{n}(w))
is defined by the (dual) PBW basis using Lusztig’s braid symmetries and
takes a crucial role in the intersection of quantum groups and cluster algebras (see
[
10
,
9
,
11
,
19
,
27
]
for examples).

Since the algebra
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
serves as an affinization of
\mathcal{U}_{q}^{-}(\mathfrak{g})
, one can regard the algebra
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{g}}}(\mathtt{b})
as a natural generalization of
A_{q}(\mathfrak{n}(w))
,
and the PBW theory developed in the paper is also understood as an extension of the PBW theory for
A_{q}(\mathfrak{n}(w))
through the braid group
\mathtt{B}
.

Let us explain our results more precisely. We write
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}
for
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{g}}}
for simplicity and, for an interval
\mathtt{J}
, define
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathtt{J}}}
to be the subalgebra of
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}
generated by
f_{i,p}
for
i\in I
and
p\in\mathtt{J}
. Note that
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{[k]}}\simeq\mathcal{U}_{q}^{-}(\mathfrak{g})
for any single interval
[k]:=\{k\}\subset\mathbb{Z}\mspace{1mu}
.
In Definition
4.5
, we define a bilinear form

(\hskip-3.01385pt(\ ,\ )\hskip-3.01385pt)
on
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{[a,b]}}
, which can be understood as a braid-analogue of the bilinear forms

(\cdot,\cdot)_{L}
of Lusztig and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{K}
of Kashiwara on
\mathcal{U}_{q}^{-}(\mathfrak{g})

in a simultaneously way, through the serial decomposition of
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{[a,b]}}
:

{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{[a,b]}}\simeq{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{[b]}}\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{[b-1]}}\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}\cdots\otimes_{\mathbf{k}}{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{[a]}}

(see Lemma
3.3
).
Lemma
4.6
asserts that the bilinear form
(\hskip-3.01385pt(\ ,\ )\hskip-3.01385pt)
is both symmetric and non-degenerate. Moreover, it exhibits compatibility with (anti-)automorphisms
\mathcal{D}
and
*
.
Similar to the quantum group case, it is revealed that the bilinear form
(\hskip-3.01385pt(\ ,\ )\hskip-3.01385pt)
remains invariant under the braid group action
\mathcal{T}_{i}
for
i\in I
(see Proposition
5.6
).

For a
PBW datum

\mathfrak{I}=(\textbf{{i}},\xi)
, which is a pair of
\textbf{{i}}=(i_{u},i_{u+1},\ldots,i_{v})\in I^{[u,v]}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}\mspace{1mu}
, we define the
PBW root vectors

\mathsf{F}^{\mathfrak{I}}_{k}\mathbin{:=}\mathcal{T}_{i_{u}}\mathcal{T}_{i_{u+1}}\cdots\mathcal{T}_{i_{k-1}}(f_{i_{k},{\xi}})\qquad\text{for any $k\in[u,v]$},

and the
PBW monomials

\mathsf{F}_{\mathfrak{I}}({\bm{d}})\mathbin{:=}\mathop{\prod}^{\longrightarrow}_{k\in[u,v]}\mathsf{F}^{d_{k}}_{k}=\mathsf{F}^{d_{v}}_{v}\mathsf{F}^{d_{v-1}}_{v-1}\cdots\mathsf{F}^{d_{u}}_{u}\qquad\text{for any ${\bm{d}}=(d_{u},d_{u+1},\ldots d_{v})\in\mathbb{Z}\mspace{1mu}_{\geqslant 0}^{\oplus[u,v]}$}.

The
*
-twisted PBW root vectors
\mathsf{G}^{\mathfrak{I}}_{k}
and PBW monomials
\mathsf{G}_{\mathfrak{I}}({\bm{d}})
are also defined (see (
5.1
) and (
5.2
)) and the relation between those two PBW root vectors is investigated in Lemma
5.7
.

We initially consider with the case when
\mathbf{i}\in I^{[1,N]}
is a
locally reduced sequence
(see (
5.3
)).
Here
N=\ell\times(b-a+1)
represents a multiple of the length
\ell=\ell(w_{\circ})
of the longest element
w_{\circ}\in\mathsf{W}_{\mathsf{C}}
. Lemma
5.5
asserts that the PBW root vectors
\mathsf{F}^{\mathfrak{I}}_{k}
satisfy an analogue of
Levendorskii-Soibelman formula
(
[
29
]
) (shortly LS-formula).
This formula is a convex property among
\mathsf{F}^{\mathfrak{I}}_{k}
.
We establish that the PBW monomials
\mathsf{F}_{\mathfrak{I}}({\bm{d}})
form an orthogonal basis for
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{[a,b]}}
(encompassing the entire algebra
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}
) with respect to the bilinear form
(\hskip-3.01385pt(\ ,\ )\hskip-3.01385pt)
. This is achieved by providing an explicit formula for
(\hskip-3.01385pt(\mathsf{F}_{\mathfrak{I}}({\bm{d}}),\mathsf{F}_{\mathfrak{I}}({\bm{d}}^{\prime}))\hskip-3.01385pt)

(see Theorem
5.4
and Corollary
5.8
). Proposition
4.3
tells us that the braid group action
\mathcal{T}_{i}
is locally equivalent to Lusztig’s braid symmetry
\mathrm{S}_{i}
(up to a non-zero scalar multiple). This equivalence plays a crucial role in the course of our proofs.
In the case of
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}\simeq\mathcal{K}_{t}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0})
, the fundamental modules and standard modules in the category
\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0}
give vectors and
a basis of
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}
via
(q,t)
-characters (
[
5
,
12
,
33
,
35
]
), which coincide with the PBW root vectors and PBW monomials associated with a
sequence
\mathbf{i}

adapted to a
\mathrm{Q}
-datum
Q
(see
[
7
,
5
]
). Since
Q
-adapted sequences are examples of locally reduced
sequences, our PBW vectors and PBW bases generalize the vectors and basis arising from fundamental modules and standard modules. Note that the PBW
theory for
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}
associated with locally reduced sequences can be understood as a ring-theoretic counterpart of the PBW theory for
\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0}

developed in
[
24
]
.

For the full generality, we consider
any
element
\mathtt{b}\in\mathtt{B}^{+}
.
Let
\mathfrak{I}=(\textbf{{i}},\xi)
be a PBW datum of
\mathtt{b}
, i.e,
\textbf{{i}}\in{\rm Seq}(\mathtt{b})
(see Definition
2.2
for
{\rm Seq}(\mathtt{b})
) and
\xi\in\mathbb{Z}\mspace{1mu}
, and let
L=\ell(\mathtt{b})
.
For any interval
\mathtt{J}\subset[1,L]
,
we denote by
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathtt{J},\mathfrak{I}}}
the subalgebra of
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}
generated by the PBW root vectors
\mathsf{F}^{\mathfrak{I}}_{k}
for all
k\in\mathtt{J}
.
In the case when
\xi=0
and
\mathtt{J}=[1,L]
, we simple write
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathtt{b})\mathbin{:=}{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{[1,N],\mathfrak{I}}}
.
Lemma
7.5
says that any subalgebra
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}^{\mathtt{J},\mathfrak{I}}}
can be reduced to the case of the form
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathtt{b}^{\prime})
for some
\mathtt{b}^{\prime}\in\mathtt{B}^{+}
from a ring theoretic point of view.

Utilizing the
Garside element

\Updelta
in
\mathtt{B}^{+}
and the PBW theory linked to locally reduced sequences, we establish an analogue of the LS-formula for PBW root vectors
\mathsf{F}^{\mathfrak{I}}_{k}
. Consequently, we demonstrate that the PBW monomials
\mathsf{F}_{\mathfrak{I}}({\bm{d}})
form an orthogonal basis for
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathtt{b})
with respect to the bilinear form
(\hskip-3.01385pt(\ ,\ )\hskip-3.01385pt)
(refer to Theorem
7.11
).
A crucial element in our proof involves the use of
adjoint operators

E^{\prime}_{i,p}
and
E^{\star}_{i,p}
introduced in Section
6
(see Definition
6.3
). These operators,
E^{\prime}_{i,p}
and
E^{\star}_{i,p}
, serve as adjoints to the left and right multiplication of
f_{i,p}
with respect to
(\hskip-3.01385pt(\ ,\ )\hskip-3.01385pt)
respectively.
We give an explicit formulas for applying
E^{\prime}_{i,p}
and
E^{\star}_{i,p}
to homogeneous elements (Theorem
6.4
) and show that
E^{\prime}_{i,p}
and
E^{\star}_{i,p}
are analogues of the
q
-derivations
e_{i}^{\prime}
and
e^{\star}_{i}
defined on the negative half
\mathcal{U}_{q}^{-}(\mathfrak{g})
of quantum group
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
(Corollary
6.8
).
We expect that
f_{i,p}
and
E^{\prime}_{i,p}
may give ring-theoretic
extended crystal operators
of the categorical crystal introduced in
[
26
]
.
Since
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathtt{b})
can be viewed as a generalization of the quantum unipotent coordinate ring
A_{q}(\mathfrak{n}(w))
, it is natural and interesting to ask whether
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathtt{b})
possesses a quantum cluster algebra structure associated with
\mathtt{b}
. We conjecture that
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathtt{b})
has a quantum cluster algebra structure whose compatible pairs appear in
[
6
, §2]
even if
i
is not locally reduced. We also conjecture that, in the case of
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}\simeq\mathcal{K}_{t}(\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0})
, there exists a subcategory
\mathscr{C}_{\mathtt{b}}
of the category
\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak{G}}^{0}
which provides a
monoidal categorification
of
{\widehat{\mathcal{A}}}(\mathtt{b})
. Note that, when
i
is a locally reduced sequence, this follows from the monoidal categorification in
[
22
]
.
It is also interesting to develop the
global basis
(or
canonical basis
) theory for
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(\mathtt{b})
.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section

, we give necessary backgrounds on quantum groups, braid groups, PBW bases and etc.
In Section

of a quantum group

and investigate its properties.
In Section

, we introduce and study the bilinear form
(\hskip-3.01385pt(\ ,\ )\hskip-3.01385pt)
on

.
In Section

In Section

and

.
In Section

2.
Preliminaries

We briefly recall some backgrounds on quantum groups
\mathcal{U}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
and
q
-boson algebras
\mathcal{B}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
(see
[
20
,
27
,
31
]
and references therein for details).

Let
\mathsf{C}=(c_{i,j})_{i,j\in I}
be the
finite type Cartan matrix
and let
\mathfrak{g}
be the finite dimensional simple Lie algebra associated with
\mathsf{C}
. We denote
by
\Pi=\{{\mspace{1mu}\alpha}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
the set of simple roots, by
\Pi^{\vee}=\{h_{i}\}_{i\in I}
the set of simple coroots.
We also denote by
\mathsf{P}
the weight lattice of
\mathfrak{g}
,
and by
\Phi
the set of roots of
\mathfrak{g}
. We write
\Phi_{\pm}
for the set of positive (resp. negative) roots. We call the quintuple
(\mathsf{C},\mathsf{P},\Pi,\mathsf{P}^{\vee},\Pi^{\vee})
the Cartan datum of
\mathfrak{g}
.

The free abelian group
\mathsf{Q}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}\mspace{1mu}{\mspace{1mu}\alpha}_{i}
is called the

and we set
\mathsf{Q}_{\pm}\mathbin{:=}\pm\sum_{i\in I}\mathbb{Z}\mspace{1mu}_{\geqslant 0}{\mspace{1mu}\alpha}_{i}
. For an element
{\mspace{1mu}\beta}=\sum_{i\in I}a_{i}{\mspace{1mu}\alpha}_{i}\in\mathsf{Q}_{\pm}
, we define

(2.1)

\displaystyle{\rm ht}({\mspace{1mu}\beta})=\sum_{i\in I}|a_{i}|\quad\text{ and }\quad\widetilde{{\mspace{1mu}\beta}}=\sum_{i\in I}|a_{i}|{\mspace{1mu}\alpha}_{i}\in\mathsf{Q}_{+}.

For
{\mspace{1mu}\alpha},{\mspace{1mu}\beta}\in\Phi
, we set

(2.2)

\displaystyle p_{{\mspace{1mu}\beta},{\mspace{1mu}\alpha}}\mathbin{:=}\max\{p\in\mathbb{Z}\mspace{1mu}\ |\ {\mspace{1mu}\beta}-p{\mspace{1mu}\alpha}\in\Phi\}.

There exists a diagonal matrix
\mathsf{D}=\mathrm{diag}(\mathsf{d}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\mspace{1mu}_{\geqslant 1})_{i\in I}
such that

\mathsf{D}\mathsf{C}
is symmetric. In this paper, we take such

satisfying

Words-to-Letters Valuations for Language Kleene Algebras with Variable Complements
Yoshiki Nakamura, Ryoma Sin’ya

Abstract
We investigate the equational theory of Kleene algebra terms with variable complements —(language) complement where it applies only to variables—w.r.t. languages. While the equational theory w.r.t. languages coincides with the language equivalence (under the standard language valuation) for Kleene algebra terms, this coincidence is broken if we extend the terms with complements. In this paper, we prove the decidability of some fragments of the equational theory: the universality problem is coNP-complete, and the inequational theory t ≤ s t\leq s is coNP-complete when t t does not contain Kleene-star. To this end, we introduce words-to-letters valuations ; they are sufficient valuations for the equational theory and ease us in investigating the equational theory w.r.t. languages. Additionally, we prove that for words with variable complements, the equational theory coincides with the word equivalence.

The identity inclusion problem

We first consider the

Given a term

This problem is relatively easily solvable.
Since
\mathsf{LANG}\models\mathsf{I}\leq t

\mathsf{I}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(t)

\mathfrak{v}
,
it suffices to consider the membership of the empty word

We use the following facts.

Proposition 3
.

For all languages

\displaystyle\mathsf{I}\in L\mathbin{\cup}K

\displaystyle\quad\iff\quad\mathsf{I}\in L\lor\mathsf{I}\in K

\displaystyle\mathsf{I}\in L\mathbin{\cdot}K

\displaystyle\quad\iff\quad\mathsf{I}\in L\land\mathsf{I}\in K

\displaystyle\mathsf{I}\in L^{*}

\displaystyle\quad\iff\quad\mathsf{True}.

Proof.

Clear, by definition.
∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mathfrak{v},\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}
be language valuations.
Assume that for all variables

\mathsf{I}\in\mathfrak{v}(x)
iff
\mathsf{I}\in\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}(x)

For all terms

\mathsf{I}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(t)\quad\iff\quad\mathsf{I}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{\prime}(t).

Proof.

By easy induction on
t

3
.
Case
t=x,x^{-}
:
Clear by the assumption.
Case

is a constant:
Trivial.
For inductive cases, e.g., Case

By using

, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{I}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(s\mathbin{\cup}u)\iff\mathsf{I}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(s)\lor\mathsf{I}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(u)

\displaystyle\iff\mathsf{I}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{\prime}(s)\lor\mathsf{I}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{\prime}(u)

(IH)

\displaystyle\iff\mathsf{I}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{\prime}(s\mathbin{\cup}u).

(Similarly for the other inductive cases.)
∎

By using

Proof.

(in coNP):

1.

Pick up a language valuation
\mathfrak{v}
over the empty set s.t. for all

\mathfrak{v}(x)\subseteq\{\mathsf{I}\}
, non-deterministically.

2.

If

, then return
\mathsf{True}
;
otherwise return
\mathsf{False}
.

Then
\mathsf{LANG}\not\models\mathsf{I}\leq t
if some execution returns
\mathsf{True}

\mathsf{LANG}\models\mathsf{I}\leq t

Hence, the identity inclusion problem is decidable in coNP (as its complemented problem is in NP).

(coNP-hard):

\mathsf{I}\in\mathfrak{v}(x)
and
\mathsf{False}
otherwise.
Thus by
Theorem

5
,
\mathsf{LANG}\models\mathsf{I}\leq t
iff
\varphi
is valid.
Hence, the identity inclusion problem is coNP-hard.
∎

Remark 7
.

Under the standard language valuation, the identity inclusion problem—given a term
t
, does
[\mathsf{I}]\subseteq[t]
? (i.e., does
\mathsf{I}\in[t]
?)—is decidable in P (because we can compute “
\mathsf{I}\in[t]
?” by induction on
t
, as
\mathsf{I}\not\in[x]
and
\mathsf{I}\in[x^{-}]
for every variable
x
).
Hence, for KA terms with variable complements, the identity inclusion problem w.r.t. languages is strictly harder than that under the standard language valuation unless P = NP.

4
The variable inclusion problem and the universality problem

Next, we consider the
variable inclusion problem
:

Given a variable
x
and a term
t
, does
\mathsf{LANG}\models x\leq t
?

In the identity inclusion problem, if
w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(\mathsf{I})\setminus\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(t)
, then
w=\mathsf{I}
should hold; so it suffices to consider the membership of the empty word
\mathsf{I}
.
However, in the variable inclusion problem, this situation changes: if
w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(x)\setminus\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(t)
, then
w
is possibly any word.

Nevertheless, we can overcome the problem above for KA terms with variable complements;
more precisely, from a language valuation
\mathfrak{v}
s.t.
w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(x)\setminus\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(t)
for some word
w
,
we can construct a language valuation
\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}
s.t.
\ell\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{\prime}(x)\setminus\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{\prime}(t)
for some
letter

\ell
.
If such
\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}
can be constructed from
\mathfrak{v}
,
then considering the membership of letters suffices because we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{LANG}\not\models x\leq t

\displaystyle\iff w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(x)\setminus\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(t)\mbox{ for some language valuation $\mathfrak{v}$ and word $w$}

(By definition)

\displaystyle\iff\ell\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{\prime}(x)\setminus\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{\prime}(t)\mbox{ for some language valuation $\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}$ and \emph{letter} $\ell$}

(
\Longrightarrow
: By the condition of
\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}
.
\Longleftarrow
: Trivial by letting
\mathfrak{v}=\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}
.)

Such a language valuation
\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}
can be defined as follows:

Definition 8
.

For a language valuation
\mathfrak{v}
over a set
X
and a word
w
over
X
,
the language valuation
\mathfrak{v}^{w}
over the set
\{\ell\}
(where
\ell
is a letter) is defined as follows:

\mathfrak{v}^{w}(x)\quad\mathrel{\hbox{\hskip 3.8889pt\hskip-3.8889pt\hbox{$=$}\hskip-3.8889pt\hskip-2.7431pt\raisebox{4.66875pt}{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\Delta$}}\hskip-2.7431pt\hskip 3.8889pt}}\quad\{\mathsf{I}\mid\mathsf{I}\in\mathfrak{v}(x)\}\mathbin{\cup}\{\ell\mid w\in\mathfrak{v}(x)\}.

In the following, we prove that
\mathfrak{v}^{w}
satisfies the condition of
\mathfrak{v}^{\prime}
above, that is, the following conditions:

•

w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(x)\Longrightarrow\ell\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(x)
;

•

w\not\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(t)\Longrightarrow\ell\not\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(t)
.

The first condition is clear by the definition of
\mathfrak{v}^{w}
.
We prove the second condition in
Lemma

10
.
We prepare the following fact:

Proposition 9

(cf.
Proposition

3
)
.

For all languages
L,K
and letters
a
, we have:

\displaystyle a\in L\mathbin{\cup}K

\displaystyle\quad\iff\quad a\in L\lor a\in K

\displaystyle a\in L\mathbin{\cdot}K

\displaystyle\quad\iff\quad(a\in L\land\mathsf{I}\in K)\lor(\mathsf{I}\in L\land a\in K)

\displaystyle a\in L^{*}

\displaystyle\quad\iff\quad a\in L.

Proof.

Clear, by definition.
∎

Lemma 10

(cf.
Lemma

4
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{v}
be a language valuation and
w
be a word.
For all terms
t
, we have:

\ell\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(t)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(t).

Proof.

By induction on
t
.

Case
t=x,x^{-}
:
By the construction of
\mathfrak{v}^{w}
,

\ell\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(x)
iff
w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(x)
.
(Hence, we also have
\ell\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(x^{-})
iff
w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(x^{-})
.)

Case
t=\bot,\mathsf{I}
:
By
\ell\not\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(t)
.

Case
t=s\mathbin{\cup}u
:
We have:

\displaystyle\ell\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(s)\mathbin{\cup}\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(u)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\ell\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(s)\lor\ell\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(u)

(
Proposition

9
)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(s)\lor w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(u)

(IH)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(s)\mathbin{\cup}\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(u).

Case
t=s\mathbin{\cdot}u
:
We have:

\displaystyle\ell\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(s)\mathbin{\cdot}\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(u)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow(\ell\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(s)\land\mathsf{I}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(u))\lor(\mathsf{I}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(s)\land\ell\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(u))

(
Proposition

9
)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow(w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(s)\land\mathsf{I}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(u))\lor(\mathsf{I}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(s)\land w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(u))

(IH with
Lemma

4
)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(s)\mathbin{\cdot}\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(u)

Case
t=s^{*}
:
We have:

\displaystyle\ell\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(s)^{*}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\ell\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(s)

(
Proposition

9
)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(s)

(IH)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(s)^{*}

∎

Thus we have obtained the expected condition for
\mathfrak{v}^{w}
as follows:

Corollary 11
.

Let
\mathfrak{v}
be a language valuation and
w
be a word.
For all variables
x
and terms
t
,

w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(x)\setminus\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(t)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\ell\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(x)\setminus\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(t).

Proof.

For
\ell\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(x)
:
By the construction of
\mathfrak{v}^{w}
,

\ell\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(x)
iff
w\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(x)
.
For
\ell\not\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}^{w}(t)
:
By
Lemma

10
.
∎

.

For all variables
x
and terms

, the following are equivalent:

\mathsf{LANG}\models x\leq t
;

2.

\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(x)\subseteq\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(t)

Corollary 13
.

The variable inclusion problem (given a variable
x
and a term
t
, does
\mathsf{LANG}\models x\leq t
?) is decidable and coNP-complete for KA terms with variable complements.

Proof.

(in coNP):
By the condition
2
of
Theorem

12
,
we can give an algorithm as with
Corollary

6
.
(coNP-hard):
We give a reduction from the validity problem of propositional formulas in disjunctive normal form, as with
Corollary

6
.
Given a propositional formula
\varphi
in disjunctive normal form,
let

be the term obtained by the translation in

4.2
The universality problem

The
universality problem
is the following problem:

Given a term
t
, does
\mathsf{LANG}\models\top\leq t
?

As a consequence of
Corollary

16
, the universality problem is also decidable and coNP-complete.

Corollary 18
.

The universality problem is decidable and coNP-complete for KA terms with variable complements.

Proof.

(in coNP):
We can apply
Corollary

16
because the term
x\mathbin{\cup}x^{-}
is composition-free and
\mathsf{LANG}\models\top=x\mathbin{\cup}x^{-}
holds.
(coNP-hard):
Similar to
Corollary

13
.
Given a propositional formula
\varphi
in disjunctive normal form,
let
t
be the term obtained by the translation in
Corollary

6
; so, we have that
\varphi
is valid iff
\mathsf{LANG}\models\mathsf{I}\leq t
.
Then we also have that
\mathsf{LANG}\models\mathsf{I}\leq t
iff
\mathsf{LANG}\models\top\leq\top\mathbin{\cdot}t
, which is proved as follows.

\Longrightarrow
:
By the congruence law.

\Longleftarrow
:
We prove the contraposition.
Assume
\mathsf{LANG}\not\models\mathsf{I}\leq t
; then
\mathsf{I}\not\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(t)
for some language valuation
\mathfrak{v}
.
Then
\mathsf{I}\not\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(\top\mathbin{\cdot}t)
holds; thus,
\mathsf{LANG}\not\models\top\leq\top\mathbin{\cdot}t
.
Hence, the universality problem is coNP-complete.
∎

Remark 19
.

In the standard language equivalence, because
[\mathbf{V}^{*}]=[\top]
(and the constant
\top
is usually not a primitive symbol of regular expressions),
the universality problem is always of the form:
[\mathbf{V}^{*}]=[t]
.
However,
\mathsf{LANG}\models\mathbf{V}^{*}\leq t
is different from
\mathsf{LANG}\models\top\leq t
, as
\mathsf{LANG}\not\models\mathbf{V}^{*}=\top
.

Remark 20
.

Under the standard language equivalence, the universality problem—given a term
t
, does
[\top]\subseteq[t]
? (i.e., does
[t]=\mathbf{V}^{*}
?)—is PSPACE-hard
[
9
,
13
,
6
]
.
Hence, for KA terms with variable complements, the universality problem w.r.t. languages is strictly easier (cf.
Remark

7
) than that under the standard language equivalence unless NP = PSPACE.

\mathbf{V}^{\prime}=\{x,x^{-}\mid x\in\mathbf{V}\}

9
)

(cf.

6
On the equational theory of words with variable complements

We prove that the equational theory of words over
\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
coincides with the word equivalence (
Theorem

32
).
We give language valuations for separating two distinct words based on words-to-letters valuations.

Lemma 31
.

Let
w=x_{0}\dots x_{n-1}
and
v=y_{0}\dots y_{m-1}
be words over
\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
, where
n\leq m
.
Let
\mathfrak{v}
be a language valuation over
\{\ell_{0},\dots,\ell_{n-1}\}
such that

•

for all
i<n
,
\ell_{i}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(x_{i})
;

•

for all
i<m
and
i\leq j\leq n
,

\ell_{i}\dots\ell_{j-1}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(y_{i})
iff
(y_{i}=x_{i}\land j=i+1)
.

If
w\neq v
, then
\ell_{0}\dots\ell_{n-1}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(w)\setminus\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(v)
.
Such a language valuation
\mathfrak{v}
always exists.

Proof.

Since
\ell_{i}\in\mathfrak{v}(x_{i})
for all
i
, we have
\ell_{0}\dots\ell_{n-1}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(w)
.
Assume that
\ell_{0}\dots\ell_{n-1}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(y_{0}\dots y_{m-1})
.
For
i=0
, by the condition of
\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(y_{i})
(i.e.,
\ell_{i}\dots\ell_{j-1}\not\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(y_{i})
unless
j=i+1
),
we should have
\ell_{i}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(y_{i})
,
\ell_{i+1}\dots\ell_{n-1}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(y_{i+1}\dots y_{m-1})
, and
y_{i}=x_{i}
.
By using the same argument iteratively, the condition above should hold for all
i<n
;
thus, we have
\mathsf{I}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(y_{n}\dots y_{m-1})
and
y_{0}\dots y_{n-1}=x_{0}\dots x_{m-1}
.
Since
\mathsf{I}\not\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(y_{n})
, we have
y_{n}\dots y_{m-1}=\mathsf{I}
; thus,
m=n
.
However this yields
w=x_{0}\dots x_{n-1}=y_{0}\dots y_{m-1}=v
, which contradicts the assumption.
Hence
\ell_{0}\dots\ell_{n-1}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(w)\setminus\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(v)
.
Additionally, such a language valuation
\mathfrak{v}
always exists as follows.
If some conditions conflict, then the first condition (
\ell_{i}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(x_{i})
) and the second condition when
j=i+1
(
\ell_{i}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(y_{i})
) are for some
i
.
If
y_{i}=x_{i}
, then
\ell_{i}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(y_{i})=\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(x_{i})
, so it does not conflict to the condition
\ell_{i}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(x_{i})
;
If
y_{i}=x_{i}^{-}
(or
y_{i}^{-}=x_{i}
), then
\ell_{i}\not\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(y_{i})
, so it does not conflict to the condition
\ell_{i}\in\hat{\mathfrak{v}}(x_{i})
.
Otherwise, they are not conflicted, as the variables occurring in
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
are different.
Thus, in either case, conditions are not conflicted.
Hence, this completes the proof.
∎

7
Conclusion and future work

We have introduced words-to-letters valuations.
By using them, we have shown the decidability and complexity of the identity/variable/word inclusion problems (
Corollaries

6
,
13
,
26
) and the universality problem (
Corollary

18
) of the equational theory of KA terms with variable complements w.r.t. languages; in particular, the inequational theory
t\leq s
is coNP-complete when
t
does not contain Kleene-star (
Corollary

29
).
Additionally, we have proved a completeness theorem for words with variable complements w.r.t. languages (
Theorem

32
);
moreover, for one-variable words, the equational theory over
\mathsf{LANG}
coincides with that over
\mathsf{LANG}_{2}
(
Theorem

36
).

A natural interest is to extend our decidability results, e.g., for full KA terms with variable complements.
As
Corollary

30
shows, even for full terms, words-to-letters valuations are sufficient valuations in investigating the equational theory.
The first author conjectures that the equational theory of KA terms with variable complements is decidable, possibly by combining the technique like
saturable paths

[
10
]
(which were introduced for the equational theory w.r.t. binary relations).
Additionally, we leave open the (finite) axiomatizability of the equational theory (including that over sets of bounded cardinality; cf.
Section

6.1
).

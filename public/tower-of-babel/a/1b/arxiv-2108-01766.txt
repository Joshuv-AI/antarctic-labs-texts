Abstract
We propose, analyze and test a new adaptive penalty scheme that picks the penalty parameter ϵ \epsilon element by element small where ∇ ⋅ u h \nabla\cdot u^{h} is large. We start by analyzing and testing the new scheme on the most simple but interesting setting, the Stokes problem. Finally, we extend and test the algorithm on the incompressible Navier Stokes equation on complex flow problems. Tests indicate that the new adaptive- ϵ \epsilon penalty method algorithm predicts flow behavior accurately. The scheme is developed in the penalty method but also can be used to pick a grad-div stabilization parameter.

Introduction

Consider the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations (NSE) with no-slip boundary condition:

\begin{split}&u_{t}+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p-\nu\Delta u=f,\ \text{and}\ \nabla\cdot u=0,\ \text{in}\ \Omega\times[0,T],\\
&u=0,\ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega\times[0,T],\ \text{and}\ u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\ \text{in}\ \Omega.\end{split}

(1)

Here
u
is the velocity,
f
is the known body force,
p
is the pressure, and
\nu
is the viscosity.

The penalty approximation to the Navier-Stokes equations replaces
\nabla\cdot u=0
by
\nabla\cdot u+\epsilon p=0
and eliminates the pressure. This uncouples velocity and pressure, and the resulting system is much easier to solve:

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon,t}+u_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon})u_{\epsilon}-\nu\Delta u_{\epsilon}-\nabla(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon})=f\ \text{in}\ \Omega\times[0,T],

(2)

\displaystyle u_{\epsilon}=0,\text{on}\ \partial\Omega\times[0,T],\ \text{and}\ u_{\epsilon}(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\ \text{in}\ \Omega.

Here
u_{\epsilon}\cdot\nabla u_{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon})u_{\epsilon}
is the modified bilinear term introduced by Temam
[
21
]
. This bilinear term ensures the dissipativity of the system (
2
).
Supposing the spacial discretization, a simple penalty method is given as follows. Given
u^{n}\approx u(x,t_{n}),k_{n}=t^{n+1}-t^{n}
the
n^{th}
time step

\begin{split}&\frac{u_{\epsilon}^{n+1}-u_{\epsilon}^{n}}{k_{n}}+u_{\epsilon}^{n}\cdot\nabla u_{\epsilon}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{n})u_{\epsilon}^{n+1}-\nu\Delta u_{\epsilon}^{n+1}-\nabla(\frac{1}{\epsilon_{n+1}}\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{n+1})=f(t^{n+1}),\ \text{in}\ \Omega,\\
&u_{\epsilon}^{n+1}=0,\ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega\ \text{and}\ u_{\epsilon}^{0}=u_{\epsilon}(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\ \text{in}\ \Omega.\end{split}

(3)

The term
-\nabla(\epsilon^{-1}\nabla\cdot u)
also arises in artificial compression method and with grad-div stabilization.
Penalty methods require less computing time and reduced storage but still face two unsolved problems:

1.

How to recover the pressure accurately, and

2.

How to pick an effective value of the grad-div coefficient
\epsilon
.

Herein we present a self-adaptive algorithm answering question 2.

There are many papers devoted to the parameter choice of grad-div term for both grad-div stabilization problem and penalty problem.
Jenkins, John, Linke, and Rebholz
[
14
]
found that the grad-div parameter for Stokes problem depends on the used norm, the solution, the finite element space and type of mesh used.
Ainsworth, Allendes, Barrenechea and Rankin
[
1
]
introduced an approach to select stabilization parameters for the Stokes problem.

The velocity error of penalty methods is also sensitive to the choice of
\epsilon
, see Bercovier and Engelman
[
3
]
.
Care must be taken when choosing
\epsilon
. If
\epsilon
is too large, it will poorly model incompressible flow.
Choosing
\epsilon
too small will cause numerical conditioning problems, see Hughes, Liu and Brooks
[
12
]
. In
[
12
]
, the authors introduced a theory for determining the penalty parameter, which depends on Reynolds number Re and viscosity
\mu
.
The optimal choice of the penalty parameter also varies according to the time discretization schemes and space discretization schemes used, see Shen
[
20
]
.
With so many dependencies, an automatic choice of
\epsilon
naturally becomes a problem to consider.

In Layton and McLaughlin
[
18
]
self-adaptive
\epsilon
selection in time (but not in space) algorithms were developed, analyzed and tested. The basic idea in
[
18
]
is to monitor
\|\nabla\cdot u^{n}\|
and pick
\epsilon=\epsilon(t_{n})
to make
\|\nabla\cdot u^{n}\|<
Tolerance in (
3
) in the computation of
u^{n+1}
.

The natural question we answer herein is:
can we let
\epsilon=\epsilon(x,t)
and pick
\epsilon(x,t_{n})
pointwise or element by element small where
\nabla\cdot u^{h}
is large to enforce in a realizable sense

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\cdot u^{h}|^{2}\ dx<\text{Tolerance}^{2}.

(4)

This means
\epsilon
is chosen small where
\nabla\cdot u^{h}
is large (and large where small). As a result, the term
(\epsilon^{-1}\nabla\cdot u^{h},\nabla\cdot v^{h})
becomes nonlinear. To our knowledge, this natural idea has not been considered.
Picking
\epsilon
pointwise and elementwise are two related ideas, but the resulting two algorithms are different; see (
9
) and (
11
) below.

The idea we use is the path of many adaptive methods: monitor the residual (the left-hand side of (
4
)), localize the global tolerance (
4
) and where the local residual
\int_{\Delta}|\nabla\cdot u^{h}|^{2}\ dx
is large, pick
\epsilon_{\Delta}
small (and visa versa).
Picking
\epsilon
locally in space leads to a nonlinear grad-div term in (
3
)
quite amenable to numerical analysis. In the next sections, we start the detailed analysis and test of this idea using the simplest setting, the Stokes problem.

1.2
Formulation

We begin the analysis and testing of this idea for the simplest interesting setting, the Stokes problem

-\nu\Delta u+\nabla p=f(x),\quad\quad\nabla\cdot u=0.

(5)

On a bounded, open polygonal domain
\Omega
subject to no-slip boundary conditions
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
. Let
d
denote the dimension of
\Omega
,
d=dim(\Omega)=2
or
3
.

The penalty method replaces
\nabla\cdot u=0
by
\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}+\epsilon p=0
and eliminate pressure using
p=-\epsilon^{-1}\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}
:

-\nu\Delta u_{\epsilon}-\nabla\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}\right)=f(x)\ \text{in}\ \Omega.

(6)

Let
X^{h}\subset X:=(H^{0,1}(\Omega))^{d},d=2
or 3 denote a finite element space for the fluid velocity.
(\cdot,\cdot)
is the
L^{2}
inner product with norm
\|\cdot\|
and
\Delta
denotes a mesh element (so that
\int_{\Omega}\phi\ dx=\sum_{\Delta}\int_{\Delta}\phi\ dx
). The area/volume of a region D is denoted
|D|
. The
L^{2}(\Delta)
norm on a mesh element
(\int_{\Delta}\phi^{2}\ dx)^{1/2}
is denoted as
\|\phi\|_{\Delta}
.

The penalty approximation we consider to (
5
) is: find
u^{h}\in X^{h}
such that

\nu(\nabla u_{\epsilon}^{h},\nabla v^{h})+\sum_{\Delta}\int_{\Delta}\epsilon_{\Delta}^{-1}\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\nabla\cdot v^{h}\ dx=(f,v^{h}),\quad\forall v^{h}\in V^{h}.

(7)

The idea is the same as behind most adaptive algorithms: Monitor the residual to control the error; localize a global residual tolerance; where the local residual
\|\nabla\cdot u^{h}\|_{\Delta}^{2}
is large pick
\epsilon_{\Delta}
small.

To develop this, we begin with the basic stability estimate. Setting
v^{h}=u^{h}
in (
7
) we find

\nu\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}^{h}\|^{2}+\sum_{\Delta}\int_{\Delta}\epsilon_{\Delta}^{-1}|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}|^{2}\ dx=(f,u_{\epsilon}^{h})=(f,u)+o(1),

\text{thus}\ \sum_{\Delta}\int_{\Delta}\epsilon_{\Delta}^{-1}|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}|^{2}\ dx=\mathcal{O}(1).

This sugggests that globally halving (doubling)
\epsilon
halves (doubles)
\|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\|^{2}
.

Next, we localize the global tolerance TOL for
\|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\|
as follows:

We seek
\|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\|^{2}\approx\frac{1}{2}
TOL
2
or

\displaystyle\|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\|^{2}=\sum_{\Delta}\int_{\Delta}|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}|^{2}\ dx

\displaystyle\approx\frac{1}{2}\text{TOL}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\Delta}\frac{TOL^{2}}{|\Omega|}|\Delta|.

Thus we define the local tolerance

\text{LocTol}_{\Delta}:=\frac{1}{2}\frac{TOL^{2}}{|\Omega|}|\Delta|,

and seek to enforce

\|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\|_{\Delta}^{2}\approx\text{LocTol}_{\Delta}.

If this local tolerance is satisfied, the global tolerance is satisfied:

\|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\|^{2}=\sum_{\Delta}\int_{\Delta}|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}|^{2}\ dx\approx\sum_{\Delta}LocTol_{\Delta}=\frac{1}{2}TOL^{2}.

The usual procedure would be to select (on each triangle
\Delta
)
\epsilon_{old}
, solve for
u_{\epsilon}^{h}
, compute the ratio

r=\frac{\text{LocTol}_{\Delta}}{\|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\|_{\Delta}^{2}},

then adjust
\epsilon
by
\epsilon_{new}=r\times\epsilon_{old}
and resolve.
The first step is therefore (starting with
\epsilon_{\Delta}\equiv 1
)

\epsilon_{\Delta}=\|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\|_{\Delta}^{-2}\times\text{LocTol}_{\Delta},

There are two options. Both result in a nonlinear discretization.

Option 1. Elementwise Penalty (EP)

\epsilon_{\Delta}:=\frac{LocTol_{\Delta}}{\|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}\|_{\Delta}^{2}},

so that

\displaystyle\sum_{\Delta}\int_{\Delta}\epsilon_{\Delta}^{-1}\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\nabla\cdot v^{h}\ dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{\Delta}LocTol_{\Delta}^{-1}\|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\|_{\Delta}^{2}\int_{\Delta}\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\nabla\cdot v^{h}\ dx.

(8)

Then (
7
) becomes: find
u^{h}_{\epsilon}\in X^{h}
such that

\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla u^{h}_{\epsilon}:\nabla v^{h}\ dx+\sum_{\Delta}\frac{1}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}\|^{2}_{\Delta}\int_{\Delta}\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}\nabla\cdot v^{h}\ dx=\int_{\Omega}f\cdot v^{h}\ dx.

(9)

Option 2. Pointwise Penalty (PP)

\epsilon_{\Delta}(x):=\frac{LocTol_{\Delta}}{|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}(x)|^{2}},

so that

\displaystyle\sum_{\Delta}\int_{\Delta}\epsilon_{\Delta}^{-1}\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\nabla\cdot v^{h}\ dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{\Delta}LocTol_{\Delta}^{-1}\int_{\Delta}|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}|^{2}\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\nabla\cdot v^{h}\ dx.

(10)

Then (
7
) becomes: find
u^{h}_{\epsilon}\in X^{h}
such that

\int_{\Omega}\nu\nabla u_{\epsilon}^{h}:\nabla v^{h}\ dx+\sum_{\Delta}\frac{1}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\int_{\Delta}|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}|^{2}\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\nabla\cdot v^{h}\ dx=\int_{\Omega}f\cdot v^{h}\ dx.

(11)

We focus herein on the analysis of option 2 (PP) and numerical result of option 1 (EP). In option 2 (PP), the resulting nonlinearity is both strongly monotone and locally Lipschitz continuous, sharing structures with the p-Laplacian. Then, there is a well-trodden analytical path to be adapted here. Before proceeding, we address two points:

1.

Imposing the global condition locally suggests but does not imply the local condition is satisfied. This will be tested in our experiments Section 6.1. We adapt based on the local condition but aim for global TOL to be satisfied.

2.

No analysis herein addresses how to pick TOL. TOL is user supplied.

Section 2 introduces some notation and preliminaries. Section 3 analyzes the stability and error for the Stokes problem of the new pointwise penalty (PP) method. In Section 4, algorithmic aspects are discussed for the Stokes problem and the Navier Stokes problem using the elementwise penalty (EP) method. Section 5, we present three numerical tests using the elementwise penalty (EP). The first two are for the Stokes problem and the third one is an extension to the Navier Stokes equations. Finally, in Section 6, we draw conclusions and point out future research directions.

3.1
Stability

First, we consider the elementwise penalty (EP) method (
9
). Recall that
LocTol_{\Delta}=\frac{1}{2}TOL^{2}\frac{|\Delta|}{|\Omega|}
.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{T}^{h}
be a mesh of
\Omega
and
\Delta
denote a mesh element in
\mathcal{T}^{h}
, the solution to (
9
) is stable, and the following stability bound holds

\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla u^{h}_{\epsilon}\|^{2}+\sum_{\Delta}\frac{1}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}\|_{\Delta}^{4}\leq\frac{1}{2\nu}\|f\|_{-1}^{2}.

Proof.

Take
v^{h}=u_{\epsilon}^{h}
in (
9
):

\displaystyle\nu\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}^{h}\|^{2}+\sum_{\Delta}\frac{1}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\|_{\Delta}^{2}\|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\|_{\Delta}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}f\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\ dx,

As
(f,u_{\epsilon}^{h})\leq\|f\|_{-1}\|\nabla u^{h}_{\epsilon}\|
and apply Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities (
13
):

\nu\|\nabla u^{h}_{\epsilon}\|^{2}+\sum_{\Delta}\frac{1}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}\|_{\Delta}^{4}\leq\frac{1}{2\nu}\|f\|_{-1}^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla u^{h}_{\epsilon}\|^{2}.

Combine similar terms and the claimed stability bound then follows.
∎

From Theorem 3.1, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 4
.

Let
N
denote the number of elements
\Delta
in mesh
\mathcal{T}^{h}
and TOL denote the global tolerance, then the solution
u_{\epsilon}^{h}
to (
9
) satisfy

\|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}\|^{4}\leq\left(\frac{N\cdot\max|\Delta|}{4\nu|\Omega|}\right)TOL^{2}\|f\|_{-1}^{2}.

Proof.

From Theorem 3.1, we have

\sum_{\Delta}\frac{1}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}\|_{\Delta}^{4}\leq\frac{1}{2\nu}\|f\|_{-1}^{2}.

Recall
LocTol_{\Delta}=\frac{1}{2}TOL^{2}\frac{|\Delta|}{|\Omega|}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\Delta}\frac{2}{TOL^{2}}\frac{|\Omega|}{|\Delta|}\|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}\|_{\Delta}^{4}\leq\frac{1}{2\nu}\|f\|_{-1}^{2},

\displaystyle\sum_{\Delta}\frac{1}{|\Delta|}\|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}\|_{\Delta}^{4}\leq\frac{TOL^{2}}{4\nu|\Omega|}\|f\|_{-1}^{2},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\max|\Delta|}\sum_{\Delta}\left(\int_{\Delta}|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}|^{2}\ dx\right)^{2}\leq\frac{TOL^{2}}{4\nu|\Omega|}\|f\|_{-1}^{2},

\displaystyle\sum_{\Delta}\left(\int_{\Delta}|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}|^{2}\ dx\right)^{2}\leq\frac{\max|\Delta|}{|\Omega|}\frac{TOL^{2}}{4\nu}\|f\|_{-1}^{2}.

Using the Cauchy Schwartz inequality:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{\Delta}\int_{\Delta}|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}|^{2}\ dx\right)^{2}\leq\sum_{\Delta}\left(\int_{\Delta}|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}|^{2}\ dx\right)^{2}\leq\frac{\max|\Delta|}{|\Omega|}\frac{TOL^{2}}{4\nu}\|f\|_{-1}^{2}.

Then the result follows.
∎

Remark 8

If

Proof.

As

a(u,u,v)
to the left-hand-side :

\nu(\nabla u,\nabla v)-(p,\nabla\cdot v)+a(u,u,v)=(f,v),\quad\forall\ v\in X.

Subtract (
11
) and let
v=v^{h}
:

\nu(\nabla(u-u_{\epsilon}^{h}),\nabla v^{h})+a(u,u,v^{h})-a(u_{\epsilon}^{h},u_{\epsilon}^{h},v^{h})=(p,\nabla\cdot v^{h}).

Denote
e=u-u^{h}_{\epsilon}
, let
\forall\ \tilde{u}\in X^{h},\eta=u-\tilde{u}
and
\phi^{h}=u^{h}_{\epsilon}-\tilde{u}
, then
e=\eta-\phi^{h}
, the error equation becomes:

\displaystyle\nu(\nabla\eta,\nabla v^{h})+a(u,u,v^{h})-a(\tilde{u},\tilde{u},v^{h})

\displaystyle=\nu(\nabla\phi^{h},\nabla v^{h})+a(u_{\epsilon}^{h},u_{\epsilon}^{h},v^{h})-a(\tilde{u},\tilde{u},v^{h})+(p,\nabla\cdot v^{h}),

Letting
v^{h}=\phi^{h}
, the error equation becomes:

\nu(\nabla\phi^{h},\nabla\phi^{h})+a(u^{h}_{\epsilon},u^{h}_{\epsilon},\phi^{h})-a(\tilde{u},\tilde{u},\phi^{h})=\nu(\nabla\eta,\nabla\phi^{h})+a(u,u,\phi^{h})-a(\tilde{u},\tilde{u},\phi^{h})-(p,\nabla\cdot\phi^{h}).

Apply Strong Monotonicity (
14
) to
a(u^{h}_{\epsilon},u^{h}_{\epsilon},\phi^{h})-a(\tilde{u},\tilde{u},\phi^{h})
:

\displaystyle a(u^{h}_{\epsilon},u^{h}_{\epsilon},\phi^{h})-a(\tilde{u},\tilde{u},\phi^{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\Delta}\frac{1}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\int_{\Delta}(|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}|^{2}\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}-|\nabla\cdot\tilde{u}|^{2}\nabla\cdot\tilde{u})\nabla\cdot(u^{h}_{\epsilon}-\tilde{u})\ dx

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\Delta}\frac{1}{LocTol_{\Delta}}C_{1}\int_{\Delta}|\nabla\cdot(u^{h}_{\epsilon}-\tilde{u})|^{4}\ dx.

Apply Local Lipschitz continuity (
15
) to
a(u,u,\phi^{h})-a(\tilde{u},\tilde{u},\phi^{h})
:

\displaystyle a(u,u,\phi^{h})-a(\tilde{u},\tilde{u},\phi^{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\Delta}\frac{1}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\int_{\Delta}(|\nabla\cdot u|^{2}\nabla\cdot u-|\nabla\cdot\tilde{u}|^{2}\nabla\cdot\tilde{u})\nabla\cdot\phi^{h}\ dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\Delta}\frac{1}{LocTol_{\Delta}}C_{2}r_{\Delta}^{2}\left(\int_{\Delta}|\nabla\cdot(u-\tilde{u})|^{4}\ dx\right)^{1/4}\left(\int_{\Delta}|\nabla\cdot\phi^{h}|^{4}\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\text{where}\ r_{\Delta}=\max\{\|\nabla\cdot u\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)},\|\nabla\cdot\tilde{u}\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}\}=\|\nabla\cdot\tilde{u}\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}.

Then the error equation becomes

\displaystyle\nu\|\nabla\phi^{h}\|^{2}+\sum_{\Delta}\frac{C_{1}}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot\phi^{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4}\leq\nu(\nabla\eta,\nabla\phi^{h})

\displaystyle+\sum_{\Delta}\frac{C_{2}r_{\Delta}^{2}}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot\eta\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}\|\nabla\cdot\phi^{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}-(p,\nabla\cdot\phi^{h}).

Apply Hölder’s and Young’s inequality (
13
) with
p=4,q=4/3
:

\displaystyle\nu\|\nabla\phi^{h}\|^{2}+\sum_{\Delta}\frac{C_{1}}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot\phi^{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla\eta\|^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla\phi^{h}\|^{2}+\sum_{\Delta}\left(\frac{C_{1}^{1/4}}{LocTol_{\Delta}^{1/4}}\|\nabla\cdot\phi^{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}\right)\left(\frac{C_{2}r_{\Delta}^{2}}{C_{1}^{1/4}LocTol_{\Delta}^{3/4}}\|\nabla\cdot\eta\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}\right)-(p,\nabla\cdot\phi^{h})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla\eta\|^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla\phi^{h}\|^{2}-(p,\nabla\cdot\phi^{h})

\displaystyle+\left(\sum_{\Delta}\frac{C_{1}}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot\phi^{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4}\right)^{1/4}\left(\sum_{\Delta}\frac{C_{2}^{4/3}r_{\Delta}^{8/3}}{C_{1}^{1/3}LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot\eta\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4/3}\right)^{3/4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla\eta\|^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla\phi^{h}\|^{2}-(p,\nabla\cdot\phi^{h})

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta}{4}\left(\sum_{\Delta}\frac{C_{1}}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot\phi^{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4}\right)^{1}+\frac{\delta^{-1/3}}{4/3}\left(\sum_{\Delta}\frac{C_{2}^{4/3}r_{\Delta}^{8/3}}{C_{1}^{1/3}LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot\eta\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4/3}\right)^{1}.

Letting
\delta=2
and combining similar terms gives

\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla\phi^{h}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\Delta}\frac{C_{1}}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot\phi^{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4}\leq\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla\eta\|^{2}+\frac{3}{4\sqrt[3]{2}}\sum_{\Delta}\frac{C_{2}^{4/3}r_{\Delta}^{8/3}}{C_{1}^{1/3}LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot\eta\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4/3}-(p,\nabla\cdot\phi^{h}).

Consider the last term of the error equation inspired by the proof of Falk
[
7
]
:

\displaystyle(p,\nabla\cdot\phi^{h})

\displaystyle=(p,\nabla\cdot(u^{h}_{\epsilon}-\tilde{u}))

\displaystyle=(p,\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon})+(p,\nabla\cdot(u-\tilde{u}))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\Delta}\int_{\Delta}p\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}\ dx+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|p\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2h^{2}}\|\nabla\cdot\eta\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\Delta}\int_{\Delta}\frac{1}{LocTol_{\Delta}^{1/4}}|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}|LocTol_{\Delta}^{1/4}|p|\ dx+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|p\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2h^{2}}\|\nabla\cdot\eta\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\Delta}\left(\int_{\Delta}\frac{1}{LocTol_{\Delta}}|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}|^{4}\ dx\right)^{1/4}\left(\int_{\Delta}LocTol_{\Delta}^{1/3}|p|^{4/3}\ dx\right)^{3/4}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|p\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2h^{2}}\|\nabla\cdot\eta\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\Delta}\int_{\Delta}\frac{1}{LocTol_{\Delta}}|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}|^{4}\ dx\right)^{1/4}\left(\sum_{\Delta}\int_{\Delta}LocTol_{\Delta}^{1/3}|p|^{4/3}\ dx\right)^{3/4}+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|p\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2h^{2}}\|\nabla\cdot\eta\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\Delta}\frac{1}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\Delta)}\right)^{1/4}\left(\sum_{\Delta}\left(\int_{\Delta}LocTol_{\Delta}^{1}\right)^{1/3}\left(\int_{\Delta}|p|^{2}\right)^{2/3}\right)^{3/4}

\displaystyle+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|p\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2h^{2}}\|\nabla\cdot\eta\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\Delta}\frac{1}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\Delta)}\right)^{1/4}\left(\left(\sum_{\Delta}\int_{\Delta}LocTol_{\Delta}\right)^{1/3}\left(\sum_{\Delta}\int_{\Delta}|p|^{2}\right)^{2/3}\right)^{3/4}

\displaystyle+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|p\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2h^{2}}\|\nabla\cdot\eta\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{\Delta}\frac{1}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4}\right)^{1/4}\left(\sum_{\Delta}|\Delta|LocTol_{\Delta}\right)^{1/4}\|p\|+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|p\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2h^{2}}\|\nabla\cdot\eta\|^{2}.

By the stability bound,

\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}^{h}\|^{2}+\sum_{\Delta}\frac{1}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4}\leq\frac{1}{2\nu}\|f\|_{-1}^{2}.

Thus,

(p,\nabla\cdot\phi^{h})\leq C\nu^{-1/4}\|f\|_{-1}^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\Delta}|\Delta|LocTol_{\Delta}\right)^{1/4}\|p\|+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|p\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2h^{2}}\|\nabla\cdot\eta\|^{2}.

Plug back to the error equation:

\displaystyle\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla\phi^{h}\|^{2}+\sum_{\Delta}\frac{C_{1}}{2LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot\phi^{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4}\leq\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla\eta\|^{2}+\sum_{\Delta}\frac{3C_{2}^{4/3}r_{\Delta}^{8/3}}{4\sqrt[3]{2}C_{1}^{1/3}LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot\eta\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4/3}

\displaystyle+C\nu^{-1/4}\|f\|_{-1}^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\Delta}|\Delta|LocTol_{\Delta}\right)^{1/4}\|p\|+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\|p\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2h^{2}}\|\nabla\cdot\eta\|^{2},

where

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\Delta}|\Delta|LocTol_{\Delta}\right)^{1/4}:=\left(\sum_{\Delta}|\Delta|\frac{1}{2}\frac{TOL^{2}}{|\Omega|}|\Delta|\right)^{1/4}=\left(\frac{TOL^{2}}{2|\Omega|}\sum_{\Delta}|\Delta|^{2}\right)^{1/4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{TOL^{1/2}}{2^{1/4}}\left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\max|\Delta|\sum_{\Delta}|\Delta|\right)^{1/4}=TOL^{1/2}\left(\frac{\max|\Delta|}{2}\right)^{1/4}.

Apply triangle inequality:
\|e\|\leq\|\eta\|+\|\phi^{h}\|

\displaystyle\nu\|\nabla e\|^{2}+\sum_{\Delta}\frac{C_{1}}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot e\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4}\leq\inf_{v^{h}\in X^{h}}\Big\{\nu\|\nabla(u-v^{h})\|^{2}+h^{-2}\|\nabla\cdot(u-v^{h})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+C(C_{1},C_{2})\sum_{\Delta}LocTol_{\Delta}^{-1}\left(r_{\Delta}^{8/3}\|\nabla\cdot(u-v^{h})\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4/3}+\|\nabla\cdot(u-v^{h})\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4}\right)\Big\}

\displaystyle+h^{2}\|p\|^{2}+C\nu^{-1/4}\|f\|^{1/2}_{-1}TOL^{1/2}(\max|\Delta|)^{1/4}\|p\|^{2},

where

\displaystyle\sum_{\Delta}LocTol_{\Delta}^{-1}r_{\Delta}^{8/3}\|\nabla\cdot(u-v^{h})\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4/3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\Delta}LocTol_{\Delta}^{-1}\|\nabla\cdot v^{h}\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{8/3}\|\nabla\cdot(u-v^{h})\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4/3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\Delta}LocTol_{\Delta}^{-1}\|\nabla\cdot(u-v^{h})\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4}.

The error satisfies

\displaystyle\nu\|\nabla e\|^{2}+\sum_{\Delta}\frac{C_{1}}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot e\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4}\leq\inf_{v^{h}\in X^{h}}\Big\{\nu\|\nabla(u-v^{h})\|^{2}+h^{-2}\|\nabla\cdot(u-v^{h})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+C(C_{1},C_{2})\sum_{\Delta}LocTol_{\Delta}^{-1}\|\nabla\cdot(u-v^{h})\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4}\Big\}+h^{2}\|p\|^{2}+C\nu^{-1/4}\|f\|^{1/2}_{-1}TOL^{1/2}(\max|\Delta|)^{1/4}\|p\|^{2}.

As
\|\nabla\cdot(u-v^{h})\|\leq\|\nabla(u-v^{h})\|

\displaystyle\nu\|\nabla e\|^{2}+\sum_{\Delta}\frac{C_{1}}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot e\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4}\leq\inf_{v^{h}\in X^{h}}\Big\{C(\nu)(1+h^{-2})\|\nabla(u-v^{h})\|^{2}

\displaystyle+C(C_{1},C_{2})\sum_{\Delta}LocTol_{\Delta}^{-1}\|\nabla\cdot(u-v^{h})\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4}\Big\}+h^{2}\|p\|^{2}+C\nu^{-1/4}\|f\|^{1/2}_{-1}TOL^{1/2}(\max|\Delta|)^{1/4}\|p\|^{2}.

Using the approximation properties (
12
) of the spaces
X^{h}

\displaystyle\nu\|\nabla e\|^{2}+\sum_{\Delta}\frac{C_{1}}{LocTol_{\Delta}}\|\nabla\cdot e\|_{L^{4}(\Delta)}^{4}\leq\inf_{v^{h}\in X^{h}}C(C_{1},C_{2})\sum_{\Delta}LocTol_{\Delta}^{-1}\|\nabla\cdot(u-v^{h})\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\Delta)}

\displaystyle+C(\nu)h^{2m-2}\|u\|_{H^{m+1}(\Omega)}^{2}+h^{2}\|p\|^{2}++C\nu^{-1/4}\|f\|^{1/2}_{-1}TOL^{1/2}(\max|\Delta|)^{1/4}\|p\|^{2}.

∎

Given tolerance TOL, epsilon lower bound LowerEps and mesh
\mathcal{T}
, MaxIter=10

Compute
on each element triangle LocTol
{}_{\Delta}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{TOL^{2}}{|\Omega|}|\Delta|

Set

\epsilon_{\Delta}=1

Solve for
u^{h}
using penalty method: find
u^{h}\in X^{h}
such that

\displaystyle\nu(\nabla u_{\epsilon}^{h},\nabla v^{h})+\sum_{\Delta}\int_{\Delta}\epsilon_{\Delta}^{-1}\nabla\cdot u_{\epsilon}^{h}\nabla\cdot v^{h}\ dx=(f,v^{h})\quad\forall v^{h}\in X^{h}

while

iteration
\leq
MaxIter and retry=true

do

Loop over all triangle elements

est_{\Delta}>LocTol_{\Delta}

retry=true;

REPEAT step;

end while

Recover pressure
p

\displaystyle p_{\Delta}=-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\Delta}}\nabla\cdot u^{h}_{\epsilon}

We also want to test the elementwise variable

for each time-step, repeat using

Since this is a new algorithm, we do not know which is better. We still need to do further research, and Algorithm 2 follows.

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we proposed a new variable
\epsilon
penalty method starting from the Stokes problem. We proved the stability and derived an error approximation of the new pointwise penalty (PP) (
11
) on the Stokes problem. And at the end, we test the algorithm on the Stokes problem and extend it to test the time-dependent nonlinear Navier Stokes problem using elementwise penalty (EP) (
9
). This is just a start of this new scheme, there are plenty of improvements possible. Picking the right global tolerance TOL and maximum iteration MaxIter is still a problem to consider. Algorithm 2 is new, we currently do not know if or not we need to repeat each time-step after setting the new
\epsilon
. We emphasize that our target is the 3d, time-dependent NSE problem for which the method is implemented as Algorithm 2,
without appreciable complexity increase over simple, linear constant
\epsilon
penalty methods.

In this paper, we focused on the velocity and did not pay attention to the accuracy of pressure. Pressure recovery is also a big problem to consider. In Kean and Schneier
[
16
]
, two different pressure recovery methods are introduced and analyzed. As for the time-dependent problem, only constant time-step schemes are considered in this paper. To further optimize the algorithm, adding a time filter Guzel and Layton
[
9
,
10
]
and adapt the time-step is also a good research direction in the future.
Both the stability and error analysis is given based on the assumption that the grad-div term can be replaced by the variational form (
10
). The numerical analysis based on assumption (
8
) (i.e. elementwise penalty) is also an interesting problem.

Massive Uncoordinated Access With Random User Activity
Khac-Hoang Ngo, Alejandro Lancho, Giuseppe Durisi, and Alexandre Graell i Amat

Abstract
We extend the seminal work by Polyanskiy (2017) on massive uncoordinated access to the case where the number of active users is random and unknown a priori . We define a random-access code accounting for both misdetection (MD) and false-alarm (FA), and derive a random-coding achievability bound for the Gaussian multiple access channel. Our bound captures the fundamental trade-off between MD and FA probabilities. It suggests that lack of knowledge of the number of active users entails a small penalty in power efficiency. For a typical scenario, to achieve both MD and FA probabilities below 0.1 0.1 , the required energy per bit predicted by our bound is 0.5 0.5 – 0.7 0.7 dB higher than that predicted by the bound in Polyanskiy (2017) for a known number of active users. Taking both MD and FA into account, we use our bound to benchmark the energy efficiency of some recently proposed massive random access schemes.

I
Introduction

Under the paradigm of the
Internet of Things (IoT)
, the number of connecting devices is increasing dramatically. IoT devices are mostly battery limited and transmit short packets in a sporadic and uncoordinated manner
[
1
,
2
]
. This calls for new theoretical frameworks that help to understand the fundamental limits of massive random access and provide guidelines for system design.
Polyanskiy
[
3
]
proposed a novel formulation for the massive uncoordinated access problem with three key assumptions: i) all users employ a common codebook and the decoder only aims to return a list of messages without recovering users’ identities; ii) the error event is defined per user and the error probability is averaged over the users; iii) each user sends a fixed amount of information bits within a finite frame length. Under this formulation, traditional as well as novel random access protocols
[
4
]
yield achievability bounds. In
[
3
]
, an achievability bound for the Gaussian
multiple access channel (MAC)
was derived and it was shown that modern random access schemes exhibit a large gap to this bound. This gap has been later reduced in, e.g.,
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
. Polyanskiy’s framework has been extended to the quasi-static fading channel
[
11
]
, multiple-antenna channel
[
12
]
, and a setup with common alarm messages
[
13
]
.

In Polyanskiy’s achievability bound, the number of active users is fixed and known to the receiver, an assumption that has practical shortcomings. Since
IoT
devices access the channel at random times and in a grant-free manner, the number of active users varies over time, and hence, it is typically unknown to the receiver. Therefore, the bound in
[
3
]
may be an overoptimistic benchmark for random-access schemes that are designed to work with unknown number of active users. Moreover, when the number of active users is unknown, the decoder needs to determine the list size. Choosing a list size smaller than the number of active users will result in
misdetections (MDs)
—i.e., transmitted messages that are not included in the decoded list—whereas choosing it larger than the number of active users will result in
false alarms (FAs)
—i.e., decoded messages that were not transmitted. Furthermore, additional
MDs
and
FAs
may occur in the decoding process. There is a trade-off between
MD
and
FA
probabilities. A decoder that always outputs the whole codebook will never misdetect, but has
FA
probability close to one; similarly, a decoder that always outputs an empty set will never raise a
FA
but always misdetects. Characterizing the
MD
–
FA
trade-off is a fundamental engineering challenge that was not addressed in
[
3
]
. An achievability bound for the Gaussian
MAC
with unknown number of active users was presented in
[
14
]
. However, the authors consider the joint-user error event instead of the per-user error event, and thus,
MD
and
FA
are not explicitly considered. In short, a random-coding bound accounting for both
MD
and
FA
, which can serve as a benchmark for common-codebook massive uncoordinated random access with random user activity, is still missing.

Most of the practical algorithms that have been proposed for common-codebook massive random access require knowledge of the number of active users. Advanced ALOHA schemes, such as irregular repetition slotted ALOHA (IRSA)
[
15
]
, can also operate when the number of active users is unknown. However, research on modern random access protocols
[
4
]
, such as IRSA, has traditionally focused on characterizing and minimizing the packet loss rate, which accounts only for
MD
. The scheme proposed in
[
6
]
also addressed
MD
only. Minimizing the
MD
probability alone can entail a high
FA
probability. In
[
16
]
, a tensor-based communication scheme was proposed, and both
MD
and
FA
probabilities are reported in the performance evaluation. Another scheme for which both
MD
and
FA
probabilities are reported was recently proposed in
[
17
]
for the quasi-static fading
MAC
and for the case in which the receiver has a large number of antennas.

In this work, we extend Polyanskiy’s bound to the case where the number of active users is
random
and
unknown
. To this end, we first extend the definition of a random-access code provided in
[
3
]
to account for both
MD
and
FA
probabilities. Then, we derive a random-coding bound for the Gaussian
MAC
. Unlike the scheme in
[
3
]
, our decoder does not assume knowledge of the number of active users, and thus cannot use it to set the decoded list size. Instead, we let our decoder decide the best list size within a predetermined interval around an estimated value of the number of active users. Our decoding metric is similar to that used in
[
13
]
. However, different from
[
13
]
, we limit the decoded list size to be in an interval to avoid overfitting.

Compared with the bound in
[
3
]
, our bound suggests that the lack of knowledge of the number of active users entails a small penalty in power efficiency. Furthermore, we apply our bound to characterize
MD
and
FA
in slotted ALOHA with multi-packet reception (SA-MPR). Using our bound, we benchmark the energy efficiency of SA-MPR and of the massive random access schemes proposed in
[
7
,
8
]
.
For instance, for a system with
300
active users in average, to achieve both
MD
and
FA
probabilities below
10^{-1}
, the required energy per bit predicted by our achievability bound is
0.65
dB higher than that predicted by the bound for a known number of active users
[
3
]
. In the same setting, the required energy per bit predicted by our bound is
9
dB,
4.1
dB, and
3.6
dB lower than that of the SA-MPR bound, the scheme based on sparse regression code (SPARC)
[
7
]
, and an enhancement of SPARC
[
8
]
, respectively.

II
Random-Access Channel

We consider a
MAC
in which a random set of
{\mathsf{K}}_{\rm a}
users transmit their messages to a receiver over
n
uses of a stationary memoryless channel. Let
{\mathsf{x}}_{k}\in{\mathcal{X}}
be the transmitted signal of user
k
in a channel use. Given
{\mathsf{K}}_{\rm a}=K_{\rm a}
, the channel law is given by
P_{{\mathsf{y}}\,|\,{\mathsf{x}}_{1},\dots,{\mathsf{x}}_{{K}_{\rm a}}}
. Thus this random-access channel is characterized by the
probability mass function (PMF)

P_{{\mathsf{K}}_{\rm a}}
of
{\mathsf{K}}_{\rm a}
and by the set of conditional probabilities
\{P_{{\mathsf{y}}\,|\,{\mathsf{x}}_{1},\dots,{\mathsf{x}}_{{K}_{\rm a}}}\colon{\mathcal{X}}^{K_{\rm a}}\to{\mathcal{Y}}\}_{K_{\rm a}\in\mathbb{N}}
.
As in
[
3
]
, we assume that the channel law is permutation invariant.
We further assume that the receiver does not know the realizations of
{\mathsf{K}}_{\rm a}
.

As in
[
3
]
, our model differs from the classical
MAC
in that the total number of users is not limited, all users employ the same codebook, and the receiver decodes up to a permutation of messages. However, as opposed to
[
3
]
, where the number of active users is assumed to be fixed and known, we assume that
{\mathsf{K}}_{\rm a}
is random and unknown. We therefore need to account for both
MD
and
FA
.
We next rigorously define the
MD
and
FA
probabilities, as well as the notion of a random-access code.

Definition 1
(Random-access code)

Consider a random-access channel characterized by
\big\{P_{{\mathsf{K}}_{\rm a}},\{P_{{\mathsf{y}}\,|\,{\mathsf{x}}_{1},\dots,{\mathsf{x}}_{{K}_{\rm a}}}\}_{K_{\rm a}\in\mathbb{N}}\big\}
. An
(M,n,\epsilon_{\rm MD},\epsilon_{\rm FA})
random-access code for this channel, where
M
and
n
are positive integers and
\epsilon_{\rm MD},\epsilon_{\rm FA}\in(0,1)
, consists of:

•

A random variable
{\mathsf{U}}
defined on a set
{\mathcal{U}}
that is revealed to both the transmitter and the receiver before the start of the transmission. This random variable acts as common randomness and allows for the use of randomized coding strategies.

•

An encoder mapping
f\colon{\mathcal{U}}\times[M]\to{\mathcal{X}}^{n}
defining the transmitted codeword
{\bm{\mathsf{x}}}_{i}=f({\mathsf{U}},{\mathsf{w}}_{i})
of user
i
for a given message
{\mathsf{w}}_{i}
, which is assumed to be uniformly distributed over
[M]
.

•

A decoding function
g\colon{\mathcal{U}}\times{\mathcal{Y}}^{n}\to\mathfrak{P}([M])
providing an estimate
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}=\{\hat{{\mathsf{w}}}_{1},\dots,\hat{{\mathsf{w}}}_{|\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}|}\}=g({\mathsf{U}},{\bm{\mathsf{y}}})
of the list of transmitted messages, where
{\bm{\mathsf{y}}}=[{\mathsf{y}}(1)\dots{\mathsf{y}}(n)]^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{T}}
denotes the channel output sequence.

Let
\widetilde{{\mathcal{W}}}=\{\widetilde{{\mathsf{w}}}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{{\mathsf{w}}}_{|\widetilde{{\mathcal{W}}}|}\}
denotes the set of distinct elements of
\{{{\mathsf{w}}}_{1},\dots,{{\mathsf{w}}}_{{\mathsf{K}}_{\rm a}}\}
. We assume that the decoding function satisfies the following constraints on the
MD
and
FA
probabilities:

\displaystyle\!\!\!P_{\rm MD}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[{{\mathbbm{1}{\{|\widetilde{{\mathcal{W}}}|\neq 0\}}}\cdot\frac{1}{|\widetilde{{\mathcal{W}}}|}\sum_{i=1}^{|\widetilde{{\mathcal{W}}}|}\mathbb{P}\left[{\widetilde{{\mathsf{w}}}_{i}\!\notin\!\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}}\right]}\!\Bigg]\!\leq\epsilon_{\rm MD},

(1)

\displaystyle\!\!\!P_{\rm FA}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Bigg[{{\mathbbm{1}{\{|\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}|\neq 0\}}}\cdot\frac{1}{|\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}|}\sum_{i=1}^{|\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}|}\mathbb{P}\left[{\hat{{\mathsf{w}}}_{i}\notin{{\mathcal{W}}}}\right]}\Bigg]\!\leq\epsilon_{\rm FA},

(2)

The expectations in (
1
) and (
2
) are with respect to the size of
{\mathcal{W}}
and
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}
, respectively.

In the random-access code defined in
[
3
]
, the decoder outputs a list of messages of size equal to the number of active users, which is assumed to be known. In such a setup, a
MD
implies a
FA
, and vice versa. Hence, the two types of errors become indistinguishable. In our setup, the decoded list size can be different from the number of transmitted messages. Hence, we introduce explicitly the
MD
and
FA
probabilities. This allows us to characterize the
MD
–
FA
trade-off.

Hereafter, we consider the Gaussian
MAC
with
\{P_{{\mathsf{y}}|{\mathsf{x}}_{1},\dots,{\mathsf{x}}_{{K}_{\rm a}}}\}
imposed by

{\bm{\mathsf{y}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{{\mathsf{K}}_{\rm a}}{\bm{\mathsf{x}}}_{i}+{\bm{\mathsf{z}}},

where
\{{\bm{\mathsf{x}}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{{\mathsf{K}}_{\rm a}}
are the transmitted signals over
n
channel uses and
{\bm{\mathsf{z}}}\sim{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{0},{\bm{I}}_{n})
is the Gaussian noise independent of
\{{\bm{\mathsf{x}}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{{\mathsf{K}}_{\rm a}}
.
We consider the power constraint
\|{\bm{\mathsf{x}}}_{i}\|^{2}\leq nP,\forall i\in[{\mathsf{K}}_{\rm a}]
.

III
Random-Coding Bound

The random-coding bound in
[
3
, Th. 1]
is derived by constructing a random-coding scheme as follows. Let
{\mathcal{W}}=\{{\mathsf{w}}_{1},\dots,{\mathsf{w}}_{K_{\rm a}}\}\subset[M]
be the set of transmitted messages. Each active user picks randomly a codeword
{\bm{c}}_{{\mathsf{w}}_{i}}
from a common codebook containing
M
codewords
{\bm{c}}_{1},\dots,{\bm{c}}_{M}
drawn independently from the distribution
{\mathcal{C}}{\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{0},P^{\prime}{\bm{I}}_{n})
for a fixed
P^{\prime}<P
. To convey message
{\mathsf{w}}_{i}
, user
i
transmits
{\bm{c}}_{{\mathsf{w}}_{i}}
provided that
\|{\bm{c}}_{{\mathsf{w}}_{i}}\|^{2}\leq nP
. Otherwise, it transmits the all-zero codeword.
The receiver employs a minimum distance decoder where the decoded list is
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}=\arg\min_{\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}\subset[M],|\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}|=K_{\rm a}}\|c(\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}})-{\bm{\mathsf{y}}}\|^{2}
, with
c({\mathcal{W}})=\sum_{i\in{\mathcal{W}}}{\bm{c}}_{i}
. The error analysis involves manipulations of unions of the pairwise error events via a change of measure and the application of the Chernoff bound combined with Gallager’s
\rho
-trick
[
18
, p. 136]
.
An alternative bound is also obtained by writing the pairwise error event as an inequality involving information densities, and by applying a property of the information density given in
[
19
, Cor. 17.1]
.

In the following, we derive a similar random-coding bound for the case in which
{\mathsf{K}}_{\rm a}
is random and unknown to the receiver. Specifically, we consider a random-coding scheme with the same encoder as in
[
3
]

{\mathsf{K}}_{\rm a}

\displaystyle K_{\rm a}^{\prime}=\arg\min_{K\in[K_{l}:K_{u}]}m({\bm{y}},K),

V
Conclusions

We proposed a formulation for massive uncoordinated access where both the identity and the number of active users are unknown. We derived a random-coding bound for the Gaussian multiple access channel that reveals a trade-off between misdetection and false alarm. Our bound provides an estimate of the penalty in terms of energy efficiency due to the lack of knowledge of the number of active users, and serves as a benchmark to assess the performance of practical schemes. Possible future works include extending our bound to the
MAC
with fading and multiple antennas.

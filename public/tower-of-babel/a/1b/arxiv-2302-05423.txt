1.
Motivation and the main theorem

For complex Hilbert spaces
\mathscr{H}
and
\mathscr{K},
let
\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H},\mathscr{K})
denote the Banach space of bounded linear transformations from
\mathscr{H}
into
\mathscr{K}.
We denote
\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H},\mathscr{H})
simply by
\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H}),
the
C^{*}
-algebra of bounded linear operators on
\mathscr{H}.
A closed subspace
\mathscr{M}
of
\mathscr{H}
is
invariant
for
T\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H})
if
T(\mathscr{M})\subseteq\mathscr{M}.
We say that
\mathscr{M}

reduces

T
if
\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{H}\ominus\mathscr{M}
are invariant for
T.
We say that
\mathscr{M}

essentially reduces

T
if
P_{\mathscr{M}}T|_{\mathscr{H}\ominus\mathscr{M}}\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H}\ominus\mathscr{M},\mathscr{M})
and
P_{\mathscr{H}\ominus\mathscr{M}}T|_{\mathscr{M}}\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{M},\mathscr{H}\ominus\mathscr{M})
are compact linear transformations, where
P_{\mathscr{K}}
denotes the orthogonal projection of
\mathscr{H}
onto a closed subspace
\mathscr{K}
of
\mathscr{H}.

The
hyper-range

\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(T)
of
T\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H})
is defined as the space
\cap_{n=0}^{\infty}T^{n}\mathscr{H}.

An operator
T\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H})
is said to be a
contraction
(resp. an
isometry
) if
T^{*}T\leqslant I
(resp.
T^{*}T=I
). An operator
T\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H})
is
completely nonunitary
if there is no nonzero closed subspace
L
of
\mathscr{H}
that reduces
T
to a unitary.

A pair
(S_{1},S_{2})
of operators
S_{1},S_{2}\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H})
is said to be
commuting
if
S_{1}S_{2}=S_{2}S_{1}.
A commuting pair
(S_{1},S_{2})
is
doubly commuting
if
S^{*}_{1}S_{2}=S_{2}S^{*}_{1}.
A commuting pair
(S_{1},S_{2})
of operators
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
in
\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H})
is called a
bi-contraction on
\mathscr{H}
if
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are contractions.
A commuting pair
(S_{1},S_{2})
of operators
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
in
\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H})
is a
bi-isometry on
\mathscr{H}
if
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are isometries.
If, in addition,
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are unitaries, then we refer to
(S_{1},S_{2})
as a
bi-unitary on
\mathscr{H}.
Needless to say, there is considerable literature on bi-contractions and related classes (see, for example,
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
7
,
8
,
10
,
11
,
14
]
).

Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the open unit disc centered at the origin in
\mathbb{C}.
For a complex Hilbert space
\mathscr{E},
let
\mathscr{H}^{2}_{\mathscr{E}}(\mathbb{D})
denote the Hardy space of
{\mathscr{E}}
-valued holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}.
For a complex Banach space
X,
let
\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{X}(\mathbb{D})
denote the Banach space of bounded
X
-valued holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}.
For
\phi\in\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{E}})}(\mathbb{D}),
let
M_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}
denote the operator of multiplication by
\phi
on
\mathscr{H}^{2}_{\mathscr{E}}(\mathbb{D}).

If
{\mathscr{E}}=\mathbb{C},
we denote
\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{E}})}(\mathbb{D})
(resp.
M_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}
) simply by
\mathscr{H}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
(resp.
M_{\phi}
). The
\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{E}})
-valued Schur class
of
\mathbb{D}
is the closed unit ball of
\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{E}})}(\mathbb{D}).

We say that
\phi\in\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{E}})}(\mathbb{D})
is an
inner function
if

\phi\in\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{E}})}(\mathbb{D})
and
\phi(\zeta)
is an isometry for almost every
\zeta
on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
(see
[
2
,
15
]
for the basic theory of the operator-valued Schur class).

The starting point of the present investigation is the following theorem that generalises the von Neumann-Wold decomposition (see
[
5
, Theorem I.3.6]
and
[
15
, Theorem I.3.2]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H})
be a contraction and let
\mathscr{E}:=\ker T^{*}.
Consider the subspaces
\mathscr{H}_{u}(T)
and
\mathscr{H}_{s}(T)
of
\mathscr{H}
given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{u}(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\big\{h\in\mathscr{H}:\|T^{n}h\|=\|h\|=\|T^{*n}h\|\big\},

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{s}(T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}\ominus\mathscr{H}_{u}(T).

Then
\mathscr{H}_{u}(T)
reduces
T
to the unitary
U=T|_{\mathscr{H}_{u}(T)},

S=T|_{\mathscr{H}_{s}(T)}
is completely non-unitary and the decomposition

T=U\oplus S~\mathrm{on}~\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}_{u}(T)\oplus\mathscr{H}_{s}(T)

is uniquely determined.

In particular, if
T
is an isometry, then

S
is unitarily equivalent to the operator
M_{z,\mathscr{E}}
of multiplication by
z
on
\mathscr{H}^{2}_{\mathscr{E}}(\mathbb{D}),

\mathscr{H}_{u}(T)=\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(T)
and

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{s}(T)=\bigvee_{w\in\mathbb{D}}\ker(S^{*}-w),\quad\mathscr{H}_{s}(T)=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}S^{n}(\ker S^{*}).

Remark 1.2
.

Note that
\mathscr{H}_{u}(T)\subseteq\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(T).
In general, this inclusion is strict (for example, consider a contraction having a non-unitary invertible orthogonal direct summand).

Let
T\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H})
be a contraction.
The parts
U=T|_{\mathscr{H}_{u}(T)}
and
S=T|_{\mathscr{H}_{s}(T)}
are called the
unitary part
and the
completely nonunitary
part
of
T
, respectively, and
T=U\oplus S
is called the
canonical decomposition
of
T.

We say that
T
has
Wold-type decomposition
if

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{u}(T)=\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(T)~\mbox{and}~\mathscr{H}_{s}(T)=\bigvee_{n=0}^{\infty}S^{n}(\ker S^{*})

(1.1)

(cf.
[
12
, Definition 1.1]
).

Remark 1.3
.

Let
T\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H})
be a left invertible operator. If
T
satisfies
TT^{*}+(T^{*}T)^{-1}\leqslant 2I,

then

T
has the Wold-type decomposition. This may be deduced from
[
12
, Theorem 3.6]
.

The present note is motivated by the following question concerning the structure of bi-isometries.

Question 1.4
.

Let
(S_{1},S_{2})
be a bi-isometry (resp. bi-contraction) on
\mathscr{H}.
When
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
reduces
S_{2}
to an isometry (resp. a contraction)?

In case
S_{2}
has finite dimensional cokernel, it has been shown in
[
4
, Theorem 2.3]
that
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
reduces
S_{2}
to an isometry. In general,
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
is not a reducing subspace for
S_{2}.

Example 1.5
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
belong to the Schur class of the open unit disc
\mathbb{D}.

Let
\mu
denote the weighted arc-length measure
w\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}
with weight function
w=1-|\varphi|^{2}.
Let
\mathscr{G}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{T},\mu)
and let
V:\mathscr{G}\rightarrow\mathscr{G}
be the operator of multiplication by the coordinate function
\zeta
in
\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{G}).
For an integer
n\geqslant 0,
let
e_{n}\in\mathscr{G}
be given by
e_{n}(\zeta)=\zeta^{n},

\zeta\in\mathbb{T}.

Define
B:\mathscr{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})\rightarrow\mathscr{H}^{2}_{\mathscr{G}}(\mathbb{D})
by

\displaystyle B(z^{n})=e_{n},\quad n\geqslant 0,

which is extended linearly and continuously as a bounded linear operator to the whole of
\mathscr{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
(indeed,
\|B\|\leqslant\mu(\mathbb{T})
).
Note that

M_{V}B(z^{n})=V(e_{n})=e_{n+1}=B(z^{n+1})=BM_{z}(z^{n}),\quad n\geqslant 0,

and hence we obtain

\displaystyle M_{V}B=BM_{z}.

(1.2)

Define bounded linear operators
S_{1},S_{2}
on
\mathscr{H}^{2}_{\mathscr{G}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathscr{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})
by

\displaystyle S_{1}=\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}M_{V}&0\\
0&M_{z}\end{smallmatrix}\Big],~S_{2}=\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}M_{z,{\mathscr{G}}}&B\\
0&M_{\varphi}\end{smallmatrix}\Big]~\mbox{on}~\mathscr{H}^{2}_{\mathscr{G}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathscr{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D}).

Clearly,
S_{1}
is an isometry. We check that
S_{2}
is also an isometry.
Since
M^{*}_{z,{\mathscr{G}}}B(z^{n})=M^{*}_{z,{\mathscr{G}}}(e_{n})=0
(as
e_{n}\in{\mathscr{G}}
), we have
M^{*}_{z,{\mathscr{G}}}B=0,
and hence

\displaystyle S^{*}_{2}S_{2}=\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}I&M^{*}_{z,{\mathscr{G}}}B\\
B^{*}M_{z,{\mathscr{G}}}&B^{*}B+M^{*}_{\varphi}M_{\varphi}\end{smallmatrix}\Big]=\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}I&0\\
0&B^{*}B+M^{*}_{\varphi}M_{\varphi}\end{smallmatrix}\Big].

(1.3)

Moreover, for any
f,g\in\mathscr{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D}),

\displaystyle\langle{(B^{*}B+M^{*}_{\varphi}M_{\varphi})f},\,{g}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{Bf},\,{Bg}\rangle+\langle{\varphi f},\,{\varphi g}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(\zeta)\overline{g(\zeta)}\,d\mu(\zeta)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\varphi(\zeta)|^{2}f(\zeta)\overline{g(\zeta)}\,d\zeta,

which is equal to
\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle
since
d\mu(\zeta)=(1-|\varphi(\zeta)|^{2})d\zeta.
It now follows from (
1.3
) that
S_{2}
is an isometry.
Moreover,

\displaystyle S_{1}S_{2}=\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}M_{V}M_{z,{\mathscr{G}}}&M_{V}B\\
0&M_{z}M_{\varphi}\end{smallmatrix}\Big],\quad S_{2}S_{1}=\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}M_{z,{\mathscr{G}}}M_{V}&BM_{z}\\
0&M_{\varphi}M_{z}\end{smallmatrix}\Big].

This, combined with (
1.2
), shows that

S_{1}
and
S_{2}
commute.
Thus
(S_{1},S_{2})
is a bi-isometry.
Note that

\displaystyle\mbox{$\varphi$ is an inner function}\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\mu=0\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\mu(\mathbb{T})=0\,\Longleftrightarrow\,{\mathscr{G}}=\{0\}.

Since
B(1)=\mu(\mathbb{T})
and
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})=\mathscr{H}^{2}_{\mathscr{G}}(\mathbb{D}),

\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
is a reducing subspace for
S_{2}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle B=0

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\varphi
is an inner function.

Assume that
\varphi
is not inner. Thus

\mathscr{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq{\mathscr{G}},
so that
{\mathscr{G}}
is infinite dimensional. Moreover, since
B(1)\neq 0,

\displaystyle S_{2}(\ker S^{*}_{1})=\{\big[\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha\\
\alpha\varphi\end{smallmatrix}\big]:\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\}.

In particular,
S_{2}(\ker S^{*}_{1})
and
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
are not orthogonal.

If
S_{1}
has the Wold-type decomposition, then it turns out that the orthogonality of
S_{2}(\ker S^{*}_{1})
and
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
is the
only
decisive factor in answer to Question
1.4
(see Remark
1.7
).

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(S_{1},S_{2})
be a bi-isometry on
\mathscr{H}
and let
{\mathscr{E}}:=\ker S^{*}_{1}.

The following statements are equivalent
:

\mathrm{(i)}

\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
reduces
S_{2}
to an isometry,

\mathrm{(ii)}

\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
reduces
(S_{1},S_{2})
to a doubly commuting bi-isometry,

\mathrm{(iii)}

S_{2}({\mathscr{E}})
and
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
are orthogonal subspaces of
\mathscr{H},

\mathrm{(iv)}

P_{\infty}\bigvee\{S^{*k}_{1}S_{2}(x):k\geqslant 1\}
is finite-dimensional for every
x\in{\mathscr{E}},
where
P_{\infty}
denotes the orthogonal projection of
\mathscr{H}
onto
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1}),

\mathrm{(v)}

\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
essentially reduces
S_{2}
to an isometry.

If
\mathrm{(i)}
holds, then there exist an inner function
\phi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{E}}),
a Hilbert space
{\mathscr{F}},
a constant unitary operator-valued function
\psi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{F}}),
a closed subspace
\mathscr{H}_{uu}
of
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
and
a bi-unitary
(V_{1},V_{2})
on
\mathscr{H}_{uu}

such that
(S_{1},S_{2})
is unitarily equivalent to

\displaystyle(V_{1},V_{2})\oplus(M_{\psi,{\mathscr{F}}},M_{z,{\mathscr{F}}})\oplus(M_{z,{\mathscr{E}}},M_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}})\,\mbox{on}\,\mathscr{H}_{uu}\oplus\mathscr{H}^{2}_{{\mathscr{F}}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathscr{H}^{2}_{{\mathscr{E}}}(\mathbb{D}).

(1.4)

Remark 1.7
.

An examination of the proof of Theorem
1.6
, as presented in Section 2, shows that if
(S_{1},S_{2})
be a bi-contraction on
\mathscr{H}
and
S_{1}
has the Wold-type decomposition, then
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
reduces
S_{2}

S_{2}({\mathscr{E}})
and
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

Let
(S_{1},S_{2})
be a bi-contraction on
\mathscr{H}.

Throughout this section, we use the following notations
:

\bullet

{\mathscr{E}}=\ker S^{*}_{1}

\bullet

P_{\infty}
the orthogonal projection of
\mathscr{H}
onto
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})

The proof of Theorem
1.6
, as presented below, requires several lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(S_{1},S_{2})
be a bi-contraction on
\mathscr{H}
.
Consider the closed subspace
\mathscr{M}=\bigvee_{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}}\ker(S_{1}-\lambda)
of
\mathscr{H}.

Then the following are valid
:

\mathrm{(i)}

for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{C},

\ker(S_{1}-\lambda)
reduces
(S_{1},S_{2})
and

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathbb{T}}\ker(S_{1}-\lambda).

(2.1)

\mathrm{(ii)}

\mathscr{M}
is a subspace of
\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})
such that

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})=\mathscr{M}\oplus\mathscr{H}_{u}({S_{1}}|_{\mathscr{H}\ominus\mathscr{M}}).

Proof.

(i)
Since
S_{1}|_{\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})}
is unitary (see Theorem
1.1
) and
\ker(S_{1}-\mu),

\mu\in\mathbb{T},
is a subspace of
\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1}),
the spaces
\ker(S_{1}-\lambda)
and
\ker(S_{1}-\mu)
are orthogonal for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
provided
\lambda\neq\mu.

Since the eigenvalues of any isometry on a Hilbert space lie on the unit circle, we obtain (
2.1
).
Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}.

Clearly,
\ker(S_{1}-\lambda)
is an invariant subspace of
S_{1}.

Also, for any
x\in\ker(S_{1}-\lambda),

S_{1}(S_{2}x)=\lambda S_{2}x
(since
S_{1}S_{2}=S_{2}S_{1}
), and hence

S_{2}x\in\ker(S_{1}-\lambda).
Thus

\ker(S_{1}-\lambda)
is invariant for
S_{2}.

To see that
\ker(S_{1}-\lambda)
reduces the pair
(S_{1},S_{2}),

let
x\in\ker(S_{1}-\lambda).
By

[
15
, Proposition 1.3.1]
,
S^{*}_{1}x=\overline{\lambda}x,
and hence
S^{*}_{1}x\in\ker(S_{1}-\lambda)
showing that
\ker(S_{1}-\lambda)
reduces
S_{1}.
This also shows that

S^{*}_{2}(S^{*}_{1}x-\overline{\lambda}x)=0
or equivalently,
S^{*}_{1}(S^{*}_{2}x)=\overline{\lambda}S^{*}_{2}x.
Another application of
[
15
, Proposition 1.3.1]
shows that
S_{1}(S^{*}_{2}x)=\lambda S^{*}_{2}x
or
S^{*}_{2}x\in\ker(S_{1}-\lambda)
completing the verification of (i).

(ii) Clearly,
\mathscr{M}
is a subspace of
\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})
such that
S_{1}\mathscr{M}=\mathscr{M}.
Thus
\mathscr{H}_{u}({S_{1}}|_{\mathscr{M}})=\mathscr{M},
and hence by (i),
\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})=\mathscr{M}\oplus\mathscr{H}_{u}({S_{1}}|_{\mathscr{H}\ominus\mathscr{M}}).

∎

A particular case of part (i) below (when
(S_{1},S_{2})
is a bi-isometry) has been obtained in
[
14
, Lemma 1]
by an entirely different method. Also, the space
\mathscr{N}_{x},
as given in (ii) below, appears in the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 2.3]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(S_{1},S_{2})
be a bi-contraction on
\mathscr{H}.
Assume that
S_{1}
is a left invertible operator that has the Wold-type decomposition
S_{1}=U\oplus S
on
\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})\oplus\mathscr{H}_{s}(S_{1}).

Then the following statements are valid
:

\mathrm{(i)}

\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})
is an invariant subspace for
S_{2},

\mathrm{(ii)}

if

\mathscr{N}_{x}:=P_{\infty}\bigvee\{S^{*k}_{1}S_{2}(x):k\geqslant 1\}
is finite-dimensional for every
x\in{\mathscr{E}},

then
\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})
is a reducing subspace for
S_{2},

\mathrm{(iii)}

if
\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})
reduces
S_{2},
then
({S_{1}}|_{\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})},{S_{2}}|_{\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})})
is a doubly commuting bi-contraction.

Proof.

Write
S_{2}=\big[\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{smallmatrix}\big]
relative to the orthogonal decomposition
\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})\oplus\mathscr{H}_{s}(S_{1}).

Since
S_{2}
commutes with
S_{1}=\big[\begin{smallmatrix}U&0\\
0&S\end{smallmatrix}\big],
a routine calculation shows that

\displaystyle UA=AU,~CU=SC,~UB=BS,~SD=DS.

(2.2)

(i)
We adopt an argument from
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
to the present situation.
It follows from (
2.2
) that for any
f\in\ker(S^{*}-w),

w\in\mathbb{D},

\displaystyle{w}C^{*}f=C^{*}S^{*}f=U^{*}C^{*}f,

and hence
(U^{*}-{w}I)C^{*}f=0.
Since
\sigma(U^{*})
is a subset of the unit circle,
C^{*}f=0
for any
f\in\ker(S^{*}-w)
and every
w\in\mathbb{D}.

Thus it suffices to check that

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}_{s}(S_{1})=\bigvee_{w\in\mathbb{D}}\ker(S^{*}-w).

(2.3)

Since
S=S_{1}|_{\mathscr{H}_{s}(S_{1})}
is an analytic left invertible operator, by the Shimorin’s analytic model (see
[
12
, p. 154]
),
S
is unitarily equivalent to the operator
M_{z}
of multiplication by
z
on a reproducing kernel Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
for some reproducing kernel
\kappa:\mathbb{D}_{r}\times\mathbb{D}_{r}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{E}),
where
\mathbb{D}_{r}
denote the disc centred at the origin and of radius
r>0.
In view of

\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}=\bigvee\{\kappa(\cdot,w):w\in\mathbb{D}_{r}\}=\bigvee_{w\in\mathbb{D}_{r}}\ker(M^{*}_{z}-w),

we obtain (
2.3
).

(ii)
Because of Lemma
2.1
, after replacing
\mathscr{H}
by
\mathscr{H}\ominus\mathscr{M},
if required, we may assume without loss of generality that

S_{1}
has no eigenvalues.

(2.4)

Assume that
\dim\mathscr{N}_{x}<\infty
for every
x\in{\mathscr{E}}.

We claim that
B=0.
Since
S_{1}
has Wold-type decomposition (see (
1.1
)),

\mathscr{H}_{s}(S_{1})=\bigvee_{n=0}^{\infty}S^{n}(\ker S^{*}).
Thus,
it suffices to check that
BS^{n}|_{\ker S^{*}}=0
for every integer
n\geqslant 0.
However, by (
2.2
),
BS^{n}=U^{n}B,
and hence it is enough to check that
B|_{\ker S^{*}}=0.
Let
x\in\ker S^{*}.
Since

\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})=\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1}),

U={S_{1}}|_{\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})}
commutes with
P_{\infty},
and hence

U^{*k}=P_{\infty}{S^{*k}_{1}}|_{\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})},

k\geqslant 1.
Since
S_{2}=\big[\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\
0&D\end{smallmatrix}\big],
it follows that

\displaystyle\mathscr{N}_{x}=\bigvee\{U^{*k}Bx:k\geqslant 1\}.

Since
\mathscr{N}_{x}
is finite dimensional,
U^{*}\mathscr{N}_{x}\subseteq\mathscr{N}_{x}
and
U
is unitary, the operators
U^{*}
and (equivalently)
U
have an eigenvalue provided
\mathscr{N}_{x}\neq\{0\}.
In view of (
2.4
), this is not possible forcing that
\mathscr{N}_{x}=\{0\}.
It follows that
Bx=0
completing the verification of (ii).

(iii) Since
{S_{2}}|_{\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})}
commutes with the unitary operator
{S_{1}}|_{\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})},
this is immediate.
∎

To prove Theorem
1.6
, we need another lemma, which describes all doubly commuting bi-isometries (see Corollary
2.4
below for a finer version).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(S_{1},S_{2})
be a bi-isometry on
\mathscr{H}.

If the pair
(S_{1},S_{2})
is doubly commuting, then there exist an isometry
V\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1}))
and a constant inner function
\phi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{E}})
such that

\displaystyle(S_{1},S_{2})\cong(U,V)\oplus(M_{z,{\mathscr{E}}},M_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}})~\mbox{on}~\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})\oplus\mathscr{H}^{2}_{{\mathscr{E}}}(\mathbb{D}),

(2.5)

where
U={S_{1}}|_{\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})}
and

\cong
denotes the unitary equivalence.
If, in addition,
S_{2}
is unitary, then
\phi
is a constant unitary operator-valued function.

Proof.

Note that
\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})=\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
(see Theorem
1.1
).
Assume that
(S_{1},S_{2})
is doubly commuting.
Thus,
S^{*k}_{1}S_{2}(x)=0
for every
x\in{\mathscr{E}},

and hence by Lemma
2.2
(ii),

\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
is a reducing subspace for
S_{2}
and
V:={S_{2}}|_{\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})}
is an isometry. By
[
15
, Lemma V.3.2]
and
[
15
, Proposition V.2.2]
, there exists an inner function
\phi\in H^{\infty}_{\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{E}})}(\mathbb{D})
such that
{S_{2}}|_{H\ominus\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})}\cong M_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}.

Thus (
2.5
) holds provided we check that
\phi
is constant.
Since
(S_{1},S_{2})
is doubly commuting,

\displaystyle M^{*}_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}M_{z,{\mathscr{E}}}=M_{z,{\mathscr{E}}}M^{*}_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}.

(2.6)

Let
P_{0}
denote the orthogonal projection of
\mathscr{H}^{2}_{{\mathscr{E}}}(\mathbb{D})
onto the space
{\mathscr{E}}
of constant polynomials.
Note that
M_{z,{\mathscr{E}}}M^{*}_{z,{\mathscr{E}}}=I-P_{0},
and hence

\displaystyle M^{*}_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}(I-P_{0})\overset{\eqref{m-phi-m-z}}{=}M_{z,{\mathscr{E}}}M^{*}_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}M^{*}_{z,{\mathscr{E}}}.

(2.7)

By
[
1
, Exercise 2.66]
,

\displaystyle M^{*}_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}\kappa(\cdot,w)f=\kappa(\cdot,w)\phi(w)^{*}f,\quad f\in{\mathscr{E}},~w\in\mathbb{D},

(2.8)

where
\kappa(z,w)=\frac{I_{{\mathscr{E}}}}{1-z\overline{w}},

z,w\in\mathbb{D}
with
I_{{\mathscr{E}}}
denoting the identity operator on
\mathscr{E}.

Thus for any
f,g\in{\mathscr{E}}
and
u,v\in\mathbb{D},

\displaystyle\langle{M^{*}_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}(I-P_{0})\kappa(\cdot,v)f},\,{\kappa(\cdot,u)g}\rangle

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{text-ex}}{=}

\displaystyle\langle{\kappa(\cdot,v)\phi(v)^{*}f},\,{\kappa(\cdot,u)g}\rangle-\langle{\phi(0)^{*}f},\,{\kappa(\cdot,u)g}\rangle,

and also

\displaystyle\langle{M_{z,{\mathscr{E}}}M^{*}_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}M^{*}_{z,{\mathscr{E}}}\kappa(\cdot,v)f},\,{\kappa(\cdot,u)g}\rangle\overset{\eqref{text-ex}}{=}u\overline{v}\langle{\kappa(\cdot,v)\phi(v)^{*}f},\,{\kappa(\cdot,u)g}\rangle.

This combined with the reproducing property

\langle{h},\,{\kappa(\cdot,u)f}\rangle=\langle{h(u)},\,{f}\rangle,\quad h\in\mathscr{H}^{2}_{{\mathscr{E}}}(\mathbb{D}),~f\in{\mathscr{E}},

and (
2.7
) yields
\phi(v)=\phi(0)
for every
v\in\mathbb{D}.
To see the remaining part, one may argue as above to show that

\displaystyle M_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}M^{*}_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}=I~\Rightarrow\phi(0)\phi(0)^{*}=I,

which completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

By Theorem
1.1
,
S_{1}
has the Wold-type decomposition
U\oplus S
on
\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})\oplus\mathscr{H}_{s}(S_{1}).

By Lemma
2.2
(i),

S_{2}
decomposes as
\big[\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\
0&D\end{smallmatrix}\big]
on
\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})\oplus\mathscr{H}_{s}(S_{1}).

In view of the proof of Lemma
2.2
, we may assume that (
2.2
) holds. Clearly, (i) holds if and only if
B=0.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) This follows from Lemma
2.2
(iii).

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii)
Since
B=P_{\infty}S_{2}|_{\mathscr{H}_{s}(S_{1})}
and
{\mathscr{E}}\subseteq\mathscr{H}_{s}(S_{1}),
(ii) implies that
P_{\infty}S_{2}({\mathscr{E}})=\{0\},
and hence (iii) holds.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv) By (iii),
P_{\infty}S_{2}({\mathscr{E}})=\{0\}.
Since
P_{\infty}
commutes with
S_{1},
for every
x\in{\mathscr{E}},

P_{\infty}\bigvee\{S^{*k}_{1}S_{2}(x):k\geqslant 1\}=\{0\}.

(iv)
\Rightarrow
(i) This follows from Lemma
2.2
(ii).

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(v) Clearly, it is enough to verify that (v)
\Rightarrow
(i). In turn, it suffices to check that if
B=P_{\infty}S_{2}|_{\mathscr{H}_{u}(S_{1})}
is compact, then
B=0.

By (
2.2
),
UB=BS,
and hence

\displaystyle(UB)^{*}UB=(BS)^{*}BS~\Rightarrow~B^{*}B=S^{*}B^{*}BS.

Thus
B^{*}B
is
S
-Toeplitz operator in the sense of
[
6
]
. Now, if
B
is compact, then
B^{*}B
is a compact
S
-Toeplitz operator, and since
S
does not have eigenvalues, by
[
6
, Theorem 3.3]
,
B^{*}B=0
or equivalently,
B=0.

Assume now that (i) holds. Then

\displaystyle S_{2}=A\oplus D~\mbox{on}~\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})\oplus{\mathscr{H}}_{s}(S_{1}),

(2.9)

where

A
and
D
are contractions such that

\displaystyle AU=UA,~DS=SD.

(2.10)

This yields

\displaystyle(S_{1},S_{2})=(U,A)\oplus(S,D)~\mbox{on}~\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})\oplus\mathscr{H}_{s}(S_{1}).

(2.11)

An application of
[
15
, Lemma V.3.2]
together with (
2.10
) shows that there exists
\phi\in\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{E}})}(\mathbb{D})
such that
D=M_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}.

Since
D
is an isometry, by
[
15
, Proposition V.2.2]
,
\phi
is an inner function.
It now follows from (
2.11
) and Theorem
1.1
that

\displaystyle(S_{1},S_{2})\cong(U,A)\oplus(M_{z},M_{\phi})~\mbox{on}~\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})\oplus\mathscr{H}^{2}_{{\mathscr{E}}}(\mathbb{D}).

Moreover, by Lemma
2.2
(iii),
(U,A)
is a doubly commuting bi-isometry. Since
U
is unitary, one may now apply Lemma
2.3
(applied to
S_{1}=A
and
S_{2}=U
) to obtain (
1.4
).
∎

We conclude this section with two consequences of Theorem
1.6
. The first one recovers

[
14
, Theorem 2]
.

Corollary 2.4

(M. Słociński)
.

Let
(S_{1},S_{2})
be a doubly commuting bi-isometry on
\mathscr{H}.

There exist Hilbert spaces
{\mathscr{E}_{j}},

j=1,\ldots,3,
constant unitary operator-valued functions
\phi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{E}_{1}})
and
\psi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{E}_{2}}),
a closed subspace
\mathscr{H}_{uu}
of
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
and a bi-unitary
(V_{1},V_{2})
on
\mathscr{H}_{uu}

such that

\displaystyle(S_{1},S_{2})\cong(V_{1},V_{2})\oplus(M_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}_{1}}},M_{z,{\mathscr{E}_{1}}})\oplus(M_{z,{\mathscr{E}_{2}}},M_{\psi,{\mathscr{E}_{2}}})\oplus(M_{z,{\mathscr{E}_{3}}},M_{z,{\mathscr{E}_{3}}})

\displaystyle~\mbox{on~}\mathscr{H}_{uu}\oplus\mathscr{H}^{2}_{{\mathscr{E}_{1}}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathscr{H}^{2}_{{\mathscr{E}_{2}}}(\mathbb{D})\oplus\mathscr{H}^{2}_{{\mathscr{E}_{3}}}(\mathbb{D}).

Proof.

Since
(S_{1},S_{2})
is a doubly commuting bi-isometry, condition (iv) of Theorem
1.6
is satisfied. Hence, by Theorem
1.6
,
there exist Hilbert space
{\mathscr{F}},
an inner function
\phi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{E}}),
a constant unitary operator-valued function
\psi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{F}}),
a closed subspace
\mathscr{H}_{uu}
of
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
and a bi-unitary
(V_{1},V_{2})
on
\mathscr{H}_{uu}

such that
(S_{1},S_{2})
admits the decomposition (
1.4
). Apply now Lemma
2.3

∎

The following result provides some finiteness conditions ensuring the orthogonal decomposition (
1.4
). In particular, it generalises
[
4
, Theorem 2.3]
.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
(S_{1},S_{2})
be a bi-isometry on
\mathscr{H}.

Assume that any one of the following conditions holds
:

\mathrm{(a)}

\dim\,\ker(P_{\infty}{S^{*}_{2}}|_{\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})})<\infty,

\mathrm{(b)}

\dim\,P_{\infty}\ker(S^{*}_{2})<\infty,

\mathrm{(c)}

\mathrm{card}\,\sigma({S_{1}}|_{\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})})<\infty,
where
\mathrm{card}
denotes the cardinality.

Then there exist a Hilbert space
{\mathscr{F}},
an inner function
\phi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{E}}),
a constant unitary operator-valued function
\psi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{F}}),
a closed subspace
\mathscr{H}_{u}
of
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
and a bi-unitary
(V_{1},V_{2})
on
\mathscr{H}_{u}

such that
(S_{1},S_{2})
admits the decomposition
(
1.4
).

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
(i),
S_{2}=\big[\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\
0&D\end{smallmatrix}\big],
where
A,B,D
satisfy (
2.2
). After replacing
\mathscr{H}
by
\mathscr{H}\ominus\mathscr{M},
if required, we may assume that (
2.4
) holds.

Assume that (a) holds.
A routine calculation using
S^{*}_{2}S_{2}=I
shows that

\displaystyle A^{*}A=I,\quad A^{*}B=0.

(2.12)

By (
2.2
),
UA=AU.
Since
U
is unitary,
UA^{*}=A^{*}U.
Thus,
\ker A^{*}
is a reducing subspace for
U.
If
\ker A^{*}\neq\{0\},
then
U
has an eigenvalue (since by (a),
\ker A^{*}
is finite dimensional), and hence
S_{1}
has eigenvalue, which is not possible in view of (
2.4
). Thus
\ker A^{*}=\{0\},
and hence by (
2.12
),
B=0.

To see the remaining assertions,
for
x\in\mathscr{H},
let

\displaystyle\mathscr{N}_{x}:=\bigvee\{U^{*k}P_{\infty}S_{2}(x):k\geqslant 1\}.

(2.13)

In view of Lemma
2.2
(ii), it suffices to check that

\mathscr{N}_{x}
is finite-dimensional for any
x\in{\mathscr{E}}
provided either of (b) and (c) holds.

First, assume that (b) holds.
Since
S_{1}S_{2}=S_{2}S_{1}
and
S^{*}_{2}S_{2}=I,
for any positive integer
k,

\displaystyle S^{*}_{2}\Big(S^{*k}_{1}S_{2}\big[\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
x\end{smallmatrix}\big]\Big)=S^{*k}_{1}\big[\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
x\end{smallmatrix}\big]=\big[\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
S^{*k}x\end{smallmatrix}\big]=0.

This combined with

\displaystyle S^{*k}_{1}S_{2}\big[\begin{smallmatrix}0\\
x\end{smallmatrix}\big]=\big[\begin{smallmatrix}U^{*k}&0\\
0&S^{*k}\end{smallmatrix}\big]\big[\begin{smallmatrix}Bx\\
Dx\end{smallmatrix}\big]=\big[\begin{smallmatrix}U^{*k}Bx\\
S^{*k}Dx\end{smallmatrix}\big]

shows that
\big[\begin{smallmatrix}U^{*k}Bx\\
S^{*k}Dx\end{smallmatrix}\big]\in\ker S^{*}_{2}
for every integer
k\geqslant 1.

Thus, by (
2.13
),
\mathscr{N}_{x}\subseteq P_{\infty}(\ker S^{*}_{2}),
and hence

\dim\mathscr{N}_{x}
is finite for every
x\in{\mathscr{E}}.

Next, assume that (c) holds. Thus there exists a polynomial
p
vanishing on
\sigma(U),
where
U:={S_{1}}|_{\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})}
is a unitary operator.
By the spectral mapping property,
\sigma(p(U))=\{0\}.
Since
p(U)
is a normal operator,
p(U)=0.
It is now easy to see that
\dim\mathscr{N}_{x}
is finite for every
x\in{\mathscr{E}}.

∎

It is worth noting that the conclusion of Corollary
2.5
extends naturally to commuting
n
-tuples
(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
of isometries for which the dimension of
\ker\prod_{j=1}^{n}S^{*}_{j}
is finite. The proof of this fact can be given along the lines of proof of
[

. We leave the details to the reader.

3.
Bi-isometries not reducing hyper-ranges of the coordinates

In this short section, we discuss a class of bi-isometries
(S_{1},S_{2})
on
\mathscr{H}
for which
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
is not a reducing subspace of
S_{2}.

We begin with the following consequence of the proof of Theorem
1.6
(see Lemma
2.2
(i), (
2.2
) and the discussion following (
2.9
)).

Proposition 3.1
.

If
(S_{1},S_{2})
is a bi-isometry on
\mathscr{H},
then there exist a unitary operator
U\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})),

\phi\in\mathscr{H}^{\infty}_{\mathscr{B}({\mathscr{E}})}(\mathbb{D})
with
\|\phi\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1,
an isometry
A\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1}))
and
B\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H}^{2}_{\mathscr{E}}(\mathbb{D}),\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1}))
such that

UA=AU,

UB=BM_{z,{\mathscr{E}}},

A^{*}B=0,

B^{*}B+M^{*}_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}M_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}=I
and

\displaystyle(S_{1},S_{2})\cong\Big(\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}U&0\\
0&M_{z,{\mathscr{E}}}\end{smallmatrix}\Big],\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}A&B\\
0&M_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}\end{smallmatrix}\Big]\Big)~\mbox{on}~\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})\oplus\mathscr{H}^{2}_{\mathscr{E}}(\mathbb{D}),

where
\cong
denotes the unitary equivalence.

The previous proposition raises the following question
:

Question 3.2
.

With the notations as in Proposition
3.1
, when does there exist a nonzero
B\in\mathscr{B}(\mathscr{H}^{2}_{\mathscr{E}}(\mathbb{D}),\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1}))
such that
UB=BM_{z,{\mathscr{E}}},

A^{*}B=0
and
B^{*}B+M^{*}_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}M_{\phi,{\mathscr{E}}}=I
?

Here is an answer to Question
3.2
in case of
{\mathscr{E}}=\mathbb{C}.

Proposition 3.3
.

Under the notations of Proposition
3.1
with
{\mathscr{E}}=\mathbb{C},

\displaystyle UB=BM_{z}~\Longleftrightarrow~B(p)=p(U)B(1)~\mbox{for every polynomial}~p.

(3.1)

If
UB=BM_{z},
then
the following statements are valid
:

\mathrm{(i)}

A^{*}B=0
if and only if
A^{*}B(1)=0,

\mathrm{(ii)}

if
\mu
denotes the spectral measure of the unitary operator
U

(
extended trivially to
\mathbb{T}),
then
B^{*}B+M^{*}_{\phi}M_{\phi}=I
if and only if

\displaystyle d\langle{\mu(\cdot)B(1)},\,{B(1)}\rangle=(1-|\phi|^{2})d\theta.

Proof.

Since the polynomials are dense in
\mathscr{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D}),

(
3.1
) is immediate.
To see (i) and (ii), assume that
UB=BM_{z}.

(i) Assume that
A^{*}B(1)=0.
By (
3.1
),
A^{*}B(p)=A^{*}p(U)B(1)
for every polynomial
p.
However, since
AU=UA,
by

A^{*}B(p)=p(U)A^{*}B(1)=0.
Since polynomials are dense in
\mathscr{H}^{2}(\mathbb{D}),

A^{*}B=0.

The converse is trivial.

(ii) Let

p,q
be polynomials in
z.
By (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\langle{B(p)},\,{B(q)}\rangle+\langle{M_{\phi}(p)},\,{M_{\phi}(q)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{p(U)B(1)},\,{q(U)B(1)}\rangle+\langle{\phi p},\,{\phi q}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}p(e^{i\theta})\overline{q(e^{i\theta})}d\langle{\mu(e^{i\theta})B(1)},\,{B(1)}\rangle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}p(e^{i\theta})\overline{q(e^{i\theta})}|\phi(e^{i\theta})|^{2}d\theta.

Thus
\langle{B(p)},\,{B(q)}\rangle+\langle{M_{\phi}(p)},\,{M_{\phi}(q)}\rangle=\langle{p},\,{q}\rangle
if and only if

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}p(e^{i\theta})\overline{q(e^{i\theta})}d\langle{\mu(e^{i\theta})B(1)},\,{B(1)}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}p(e^{i\theta})\overline{q(e^{i\theta})}(1-|\phi(e^{i\theta})|^{2})d\theta.

The desired conclusion in (ii) now follows from the uniqueness of the trigonometric moment problem (see

[
13
, Theorem 1.4]
).
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Under the notations of Proposition
3.1
with
{\mathscr{E}}=\mathbb{C},
if the arc-length measure of
\sigma(U)
is
0,
then
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1})
reduces
(S_{1},S_{2})
to a doubly commuting bi-isometry on
\mathscr{H}_{\infty}(S_{1}),

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
,
there exists
\phi\in\mathscr{H}^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
with
\|\phi\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1
such that
B^{*}B+M^{*}_{\phi}M_{\phi}=I.
It now follows from Proposition
3.3
(ii) and the assumption that the arc-length measure of
\sigma(U)
is
0
that
\phi
is an inner function. Thus
M_{\phi}
is an isometry or, equivalently,
B=0.
One may now apply Theorem
1.6
.
∎

It would be interesting to know if the conclusion of the previous corollary holds in case
{\mathscr{E}}
is of a dimension bigger than
1.

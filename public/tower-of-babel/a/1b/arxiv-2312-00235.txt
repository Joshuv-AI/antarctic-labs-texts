Cofiltrations of spanning trees in multiparameter persistent homology
Fritz Grimpen, Anastasios Stefanou

Abstract
. Given a multiparameter filtration of simplicial complexes, we consider the problem of explicitly constructing generators for the multipersistent homology groups with arbitrary PID coefficients. We propose the use of spanning trees as a tool to identify such generators by introducing a condition for persistent spanning trees, which is accompanied by an existence result for cofiltrations consisting of spanning trees. We also introduce a generalization of spanning trees, called spanning complexes, for dimensions higher than one, and we establish their existence as a first step towards this direction.

1.
Introduction

Persistent homology studies the evolution of topological features of a dataset (finite metric space) across a given filtration on the dataset
[
14
]
.
It is arguably the most heavily used tool in Topological Data Analysis (TDA)
[
4
,
13
]
.
One-parameter persistent homology has the algebraic structure of an
\mathbb{Z}
-graded module
[
7
]
.

1.1.
Multiparameter persistence

Persistent homology extends naturally from one-parameter filtrations
[
26
]
to multiparameter filtrations of datasets
[
6
]
. The corresponding algebraic structure of multiparameter homology is that of an
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-graded module, as shown in
[
6
]
.
In fact persistent homology can be defined for filtrations parameterized over any ordered set
Q

[
3
]
.
The extension from graded modules to multigraded modules is of particular importance to TDA, since there are situations where important information from data cannot be captured from persistent homology by merely considering one-parameter filtration, e.g. persistent homology is shown to be unstable to the presence of outliers
[
6
,
19
]
.
By introducing a density parameter as a second filtration parameter on datasets, outliers in datasets can then be regarded as noise, making two-parameter persistent homology robust to the presence of outliers in the data. Thus, additional information can be extracted from datasets by studying multiparameter persistent homology.
In multiparameter persistence, in general, there is no way to realize the indecomposable modules as interval modules
[
6
,
11
]
, as in the case of one-parameter persistence
[
2
,
10
]
.
Therefore, recent research works in multipersistence theory have been focusing on the study of multipersistent invariants
[
15
,
23
]
.

1.2.
Minimal presentations

The key techniques to better understand the structure of multiparameter persistence modules as well as to help towards an efficient computation of multipersistent invariants of modules, are the minimal free presentations, minimal free resolutions
[
9
]
, and the recently introduced minimal flat-injective presentations
[
21
]
of multigraded modules.
However, in practice, all of these techniques often pre-assume that the module is equipped with a set of generators to begin with, and that the multigraded module is a submodule of some given free module.
Gröbner basis methods can then be utilized to compute a minimal presentation and a minimal free resolution of the module
[
9
,
17
,
22
]
.
As of now, the problem of finding an efficient way to compute a minimal presentation of multiparameter persistence modules remains open.
Some works in TDA towards this direction include
[
5
,
8
,
18
]
.

1.3.
Related work

In
[
5
]
the authors show that if a multifiltration
X=(X_{a})_{a\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}
is one-critical, i.e. every simplex enters the multifiltration in at most one multidegree (thereby making each

a free module), then Schreyer’s algorithm
[

can be directly applied for computing a Gröbner basis for the

Z_{n}(X)=\Ker\partial_{n}
of the homology module
H_{n}(X)=\Ker\partial_{n}/\operatorname{Im}\partial_{n+1}
of a multifiltration

In
[

They developed a construction that realizes the

of a multifiltration

, as the homology
\Ker f/\operatorname{Im}g
of a certain chain complex
F_{1}\xrightarrow{f}F_{2}\xrightarrow{g}F_{3}
of free multigraded modules.
They also proved that the multigraded modules that can occur as
k
-spans of multifiltrations of sets are the direct sums of monomial ideals, where

is an arbitrary commutative, unitary ring.

In the case of a
2
-parameter filtration

, they construct a free presentation

F_{1}\to F_{0}\to H_{n}(X)\to 0

for the homology module
H_{n}(X)
, where
F_{1}
is explicitly defined and
F_{0}
is the kernel of a morphism between certain free modules.
In
[
18
]
the authors are divising a general algorithm for computing minimal presentation for
2
-parameter persistence modules (also called bigraded modules), whenever they are represented as
M\cong\Ker g/\operatorname{Im}g
(for instance via the combinatorial presentation of
[
8
]
), for some chain complex of free
2
-parameter persistence modules
F_{1}\xrightarrow{f}F_{2}\xrightarrow{g}F_{3}
.
In both works, for
d=2
, the authors are invoking the algebraic-geometric fact that over two variables (
2
-parameters), kernels of free bigraded modules are also free.
This is no longer true when we work on multigraded modules over
d\geq 3
parameters.

1.4.
Motivation

Our goal is to develop a method for computing (minimal) generators for the homology of a multifiltration.
Our work is motivated by the problem of computing generators of kernels of morphisms of
upper set decomposable
modules (direct sums of monomial ideals) as in
[
8
]
.
In this work our approach is to investigate a way to find (minimal) free generators for the
n
-cycle module
Z_{n}(X)
of a multifiltration
X
, since this would be enough for our goal.
Indeed, if we know the generators of
Z_{n}(X)
, because of the canonical epimorphism from
Z_{n}(X)
onto
H_{n}(X)
, we will also obtain free generators for
H_{n}(X)
.
This will then enable us, at least implicitly, to be able to apply the machinery of commutative algebra to compute a minimal free presentation and a minimal free resolution of the homology of a multifiltration.
In the static case of simplicial complexes, we can compute the generators of the first homology group
H_{1}(X)
of a given complex
X
by using spanning tree subcomplexes
T
of
X
.
We have the following theorem which is known from many sources in the literature
[
16
,
25
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
X
is a 1-dimensional simplicial complex and
T
a spanning tree of
X
, then
H_{1}(X)\cong C_{1}(X,T)
.

1.5.
Our contributions

The main contribution of this paper is a method to construct generators of the
1
-dimensional cycle module
Z_{1}(X;k)=\Ker\partial_{1}
for a given multifiltration
X=(X^{q})_{q\in Q}
of finite simplicial complexes over a finite ordered set
Q
and an arbitrary principal ideal domain
k
.
To the multifiltration
X
we associate a
cofiltration of spanning trees

T=(T^{q})_{q\in Q}
by endowing the simplicial complex
\bigcup_{q\in Q}X^{q}
with a simplicial order.
We show that these multifiltrations satisfy the following crucial property.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any ordered set
Q
and any
Q
-indexed filtration
X=(X^{q})_{q}
of simplicial complexes, there exists a
Q
-indexed filtration
T=(T^{q})_{q}
of spanning trees of
X
satisfying
X^{q}\setminus T^{q}\subseteq X^{q^{\prime}}\setminus T^{q^{\prime}}
for any pair
q\leq q^{\prime}
in
Q
.

This existence result for a cofiltration of spanning trees
T
associated to
X
allows us to associate to every edge
\sigma
contained in
X
but not in
T
an upper set decomposable module
M_{\sigma}
.
We show that the direct sum
\bigoplus_{\sigma}M_{\sigma}
, where
\sigma
ranges over any edge
\sigma
in
X
but not in
T
, admits a canonical epimorphism of persistence modules onto
Z_{1}(X)
, and subsequently onto
H_{1}(X)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
Q
be any finite ordered set and
X=(X^{q})_{q\in Q}
a
Q
-indexed filtration of finite simplicial complexes.
Then, given a choice of simplicial order on
X
there exists an epimorphism
\varphi_{1}\colon M\to Z_{1}(X)
, where
M
is an upper set decomposable module, constructed explicitly from the cofiltration of order-minimal spanning trees.

As a last step and in order to generalize that technique to higher homology groups
H_{n}(X)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we introduce a direct generalization of spanning trees to higher dimensions.
We call this generalized structure an
n
-spanning complex
and show the existence by an application of Zorn’s lemma.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any simplicial complex
X
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a subcomplex
A
such that
Z_{n}(A)=0
and
B_{n-1}(A)=B_{n-1}(X)
, i.e.
X
admits an
n
-spanning complex.

1.6.
Organization

In Section
2
, we recall the basic definitions of partially ordered sets, simplicial complexes, simplicial homology, persistence modules, and persistent simplicial homology.
In Section
3
, we define our notion of spanning trees in terms of the boundaries and chains of subcomplexes.
Subsequently, we state an edge-exchange lemma (Lemma
3.3
) and the existence of a classical isomorphism associated to spanning trees (Proposition
3.5
).

In Section
4
, we investigate the previously introduced notion of spanning trees on their behavior under persistence.
In order to describe this behavior, we introduce the notion of a cofiltration of spanning trees (Definition
4.5
) and prove that they always exist for finite ordered sets and (globally) finite filtrations (Theorem
4.8
).

The Section
5
is used to collect a variety of algebraic statements concerning so-called
k
-spans of set-valued diagrams over any small category.
These results are meant to be used in Section
6
, where we construct from a cofiltration of spanning trees an upper set decomposable persistence module that enjoys an epimorphism onto the first persistent homology group (Theorem
6.5
).

In the appendix, Section
7
, we state the existence of
n
-spanning complexes, which are the natural generalization of the concept of spanning trees to higher dimensions.

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, let
k
be a principal ideal domain.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
Q
be a non-empty set and
R\subseteq Q\times Q
.

(1)

The relation
R
is
reflexive
if
(q,q)\in R
for all
q\in Q
.

(2)

The relation

(q,q^{\prime}),(q^{\prime},q^{\prime\prime})\in R

.

(3)

The relation

(q,q^{\prime})\in R

for all

.

(4)

If

(partial) order
on

. The pair
(Q,R)
is called an

.

(5)

If

is an order and satisfies

(q^{\prime},q)\in R

, then

. The pair

.

(6)

If

.

2.1.
Simplicial complexes and simplicial homology

Definition 2.2
.

Let
V
be a non-empty set.

(1)

An
(abstract) simplicial complex
over
V
is a set
X
of finite subsets of
V
such that

\sigma\in X\text{ and }\tau\subseteq\sigma\implies\tau\in X

for all
\sigma,\tau\subset V
.

(2)

The elements of a simplicial complex are called
simplices
.
A simplex of cardinality
n+1
is called
n
-simplex
.

(3)

The
n
-skeleton
of a simplicial complex
X
is defined by
\skel_{n}X=\bigcup_{j\leq n}X_{j}
.

(4)

If
X
is a simplicial complex over
V
, the set
V
is denoted by
V(X)
and is called the
vertex set of
X
.

(5)

Let
X
and
Y
be simplicial complexes.
A map
f\colon V(X)\to V(Y)
is called
simplicial map
if
f(\sigma)\in Y
for all
\sigma\in X
.

Remark 2.3
.

Simplicial complexes with simplicial maps constitute the category
\operatorname{Simp}
of simplicial complexes.

For the purposes of (simplicial) homology, the vertex set
V(X)
of a simplicial complex
X
is endowed with a fixed total order
\preceq
.
We call such total order a
vertex order
.
We assume that every simplicial complex has a vertex order.

Given an
n
-simplex
\sigma=\{v_{0},\dotsc,v_{n}\}
, we write
[v_{0},\dotsc,v_{n}]
for the simplex if
v_{0}\preceq\dotsb\preceq v_{n}
.
Further, for
0\leq i\leq n
, let
[v_{0},\dotsc,\widehat{v_{i}},\dotsc,v_{n}]
denote the
(n-1)
-simplex that we obtain by removing the vertex
v_{i}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a simplicial complex with a vertex order.

(1)

The
chain module
is the free
k
-module
C_{n}(X)
generated by the
n
-simplices of
X
.

(2)

The
boundary homomorphism
of
X
is defined by

\displaystyle\partial_{n}\colon C_{n}(X)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow C_{n-1}(X)\text{,}

\displaystyle[v_{0},\dotsc,v_{n}]

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{n}[v_{0},\dotsc,\widehat{v_{i}},\dotsc,v_{n}]\text{.}

(3)

The
n
-cycle module
of
X
is defined by
Z_{n}(X)=\Ker\partial_{n}
, and the
n
-boundary module
of
X
is defined by
B_{n}(X)=\operatorname{Im}\partial_{n+1}
.

(4)

The
n
-th homology module
of
X
is the quotient
H_{n}(X)=Z_{n}(X)/B_{n}(X)
.

(5)

If
f\colon X\to Y
is a simplicial map, then the
induced homomorphism
of

is

H_{n}(f)=f_{\ast}\colon H_{n}(X)\to H_{n}(Y)\text{,}\quad\sigma+B_{n}(X)\mapsto f(\sigma)+B_{n}(Y)\text{.}

Remark 2.5
.

(1)

The
n
-th homology defines a functor from the category of simplicial complexes to the category of
k
-modules.

(2)

Because
k
is a principal ideal domain, the submodules
Z_{n}(X)
and
B_{n}(X)
are free.

2.2.
Ordered simplicial complexes

We extend the idea of endowing a vertex set with a total order to the whole simplicial complex.
Let
X
be a simplicial complex and
\preceq
be an order on the simplices of
X
.
The pair
(X,\mathord{\preceq})
is an
ordered simplicial complex
.

Note that an ordered simplicial complex
(X,\mathord{\preceq})
comes with a natural vertex order by the correspondence

V(X)\leftrightarrow\{\{v\}\mid v\in V\}\text{.}

Let
(X,\mathord{\preceq}_{X})
and
(Y,\mathord{\preceq}_{Y})
be ordered simplicial complexes.
A simplicial map
f\colon X\to Y
is said to be
ordered
or
order-preserving
if

\sigma\preceq_{X}\tau\implies f(\sigma)\preceq_{Y}f(\tau)

for all
\sigma,\tau\in X
.

Remark 2.6
.

Ordered simplicial complexes with order-preserving simplicial maps constitute the category of ordered simplicial complexes.
We denote this category by
\operatorname{Simp}_{\mathrm{ord}}
.

2.3.
Persistence modules and persistent homology

We give the definitions of persistence modules and of the persistent version of the simplicial homology theory of Definition
2.4
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(Q,\mathord{\leq})
be an ordered set.

(1)

A
persistence module

M
over
Q
consists of a family
(M^{q})_{q\in Q}
of
k
-modules and a family
(\varphi^{M}_{q^{\prime}q})_{q\leq q^{\prime}}
of
k
-homomorphisms
\varphi^{M}_{q^{\prime}q}\colon M^{q}\to M^{q^{\prime}}
such that

\varphi^{M}_{q^{\prime\prime}q}=\varphi^{M}_{q^{\prime\prime}q^{\prime}}\circ\varphi^{M}_{q^{\prime}q}

for all
q\leq q^{\prime}\leq q^{\prime\prime}
in
Q
.
The homomorphisms
\varphi^{M}_{q^{\prime}q}
are called
structure maps
of
M
.

(2)

A
persistent homomorphism

f\colon M\to N
is a family
(f^{q})_{q\in Q}
of
k
-homomorphisms
f^{q}\colon M^{q}\to N^{q}
such that

\varphi^{N}_{q^{\prime}q}\circ f^{q}=f^{q^{\prime}}\circ\varphi^{M}_{q^{\prime}q}

for all
q\leq q^{\prime}
in
Q
.

(3)

A family
(X^{q})_{q\in Q}
of simplicial complexes over a common vertex set is a
filtration
if
X^{q}\subseteq X^{q^{\prime}}
for all
q\leq q^{\prime}
in
Q
.

(4)

Let
X=(X^{q})_{q\in Q}
be a filtration.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
the
n
-dim. persistent homology module
of
X
is the persistence module
M
with

M^{q}=H_{n}(X^{q})

and structure maps defined by

\varphi^{M}_{q^{\prime}q}\colon H_{n}(X^{q})\longrightarrow H_{n}(X^{q^{\prime}})\text{,}\quad\sigma+B_{n}(X^{q})\longmapsto\sigma+B_{n}(X^{q^{\prime}})\text{,}

for
q\leq q^{\prime}\in Q
.
This persistence module is denoted by
H_{n}(X)
.

Furthermore, we call a set-valued functor on
Q
a
persistent set
and a corresponding natural transformation a
persistent map
.
The category of persistent sets is denoted by
\operatorname{Set}^{Q}
.

3.
Spanning trees of simplicial complexes

Spanning trees of graphs are subgraphs that reflect the connectivity properties of the original graph while preserving acyclicity and means to identify cyclic paths in a graph
[
1
,
16
,
20
]
.
As such, they are classically characterized by combinatiorial conditions.
We modify the standard definition of a spanning tree to facilitate the need of having an algebraic characterization in the following way:

•

We characterize spanning trees in terms of their cycle and boundary modules of simplicial homology, see Definition
3.1
below.

•

The zeroth homology module of a spanning tree does not need to trivialize, i.e. a spanning tree is not necessarily connected.

Throughout this section, let
X
be a simplicial complex with vertex set
V
.

3.1.
Homological spanning trees

Definition 3.1

(Homological spanning tree)
.

A
(homological) spanning tree
of
X
is a simplicial subcomplex
T
of
X
such that

(i)

X_{0}=T_{0}
,

(ii)

the boundary homomorphism
\partial_{1}\colon C_{1}(T)\to C_{0}(T)
is injective, and

(iii)

B_{0}(X)=B_{0}(T)
.

The simplicial complex
X
is said to be
spanned by
T
.

Condition
(i)
states the natural assumption that any spanning tree of
X
must have the same vertices (in the sense of 0-simplices) as
X
.
Further, condition
(ii)
requires that any spanning tree of
X
is a graph-theoretic tree, i.e. the 1-dimensional cycle module of any spanning tree is trivial.

Remark 3.2
.

Our main focus will be on condition
(iii)
, which states that a spanning tree spans the space
X
in the following sense:
Suppose that
T
is a spanning tree of
X
and let
u,v
be vertices of
X
.
Further, suppose that there exists a path in
X
from
u
to
v
, i.e.
u
and
v
lie in the same path component of
X
, or equivalent there is a 1-chain
c\in C_{1}(X)
such that
\partial_{1}c=u-v
.
Then, the third condition implies that there exists a 1-chain
c^{\prime}\in C_{1}(T)
such that
\partial_{1}c=\partial_{1}c^{\prime}\in B_{0}(T)
.
Thus, both vertices
u
and
v
also lie in the same path component of
T
.

More generally, if
c
is a 1-chain in
X
, then there always exists a 1-chain
c^{\prime}
in
T
such that
c-c^{\prime}
is a cycle.
It follows from Definition
3.1

(ii)
that the chain
c^{\prime}\in C_{1}(T)
is uniquely determined by
c\in C_{1}(X)
.

3.2.
Operations on spanning trees

Let
T
be a spanning tree of
X
.
We pick some edge (1-simplex)
e
in
X
that is not in
T
, and we ask whether there exists another spanning tree of
X
that actually contains
e
.
It is clear that
A=T\cup\{e\}
still satisfies
B_{0}(X)=B_{0}(A)
and
V(X)=V(A)
, and because of that the subcomplex
A
still satisfies two out of three conditions for a spanning tree, see Definition
3.1

(i)
and
(iii)
.
However, it is also easy to see that
Z_{1}(A)\neq 0
.
Thus,
A
does not satisfy the acyclicity condition of spanning trees.

Therefore, the actual question is whether we can find some edge
e^{\prime}
in
T
such that
T\cup\{e\}\setminus\{e^{\prime}\}
is again a spanning tree.
We call the following lemma the
edge-exchange lemma
.
A similar statement about spanning trees is known as the
tree exchange property
, see
[
1
, p. 113, Exercise 4.3.2]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
T
be a spanning tree of the simplicial complex
X
.
For every edge
\sigma\in X
that is not contained in
T
, there exists an edge
\tau\in T
such that
T\cup\{\sigma\}\setminus\{\tau\}
is a spanning tree of
X
.

Moreover, if
c
is a 1-chain in
T
such that
\partial_{1}c=\partial_{1}\sigma
, then every simplex in
c
is admissible as
\tau
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma
be an edge contained in
X
but not in
T
.
Since
T
is a spanning tree, we find a 1-chain
c
in
T
such that
\partial_{1}c=\partial_{1}\sigma
.
Moreover, we can choose
\lambda_{0},\dotsc,\lambda_{m}\in\{1,-1\}
and distinct edges
\tau_{0},\dotsc,\tau_{m}\in T
such that
c=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\lambda_{i}\tau_{i}
.
We set
\tau=\tau_{0}
, i.e.
c=\lambda_{0}\tau+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\tau_{i}
.

Let
b\in B_{0}(X)
.
Then, we can choosse a 1-chain
c^{\prime}=\mu_{0}\tau+\sum_{i=1}^{l}\mu_{i}\sigma_{i}
in
T
such that
\partial_{1}c^{\prime}=b
, where the simplices
\tau,\sigma_{1},\dotsc,\sigma_{m}
are distinct.
By replacing
\tau
in
c^{\prime}
with the 1-chain
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\tau_{i}-\sigma
, which is contained in
X\setminus T
, we obtain the chain

c^{\prime\prime}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{0}\lambda_{i}\tau_{i}-\mu_{0}\sigma+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mu_{i}\sigma_{i}\text{.}

Since the chains
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\tau_{i}
and
\sigma
have the same boundary, the boundary of
c^{\prime\prime}
coincides with the boundary of
c^{\prime}
.
Further, the chain
c^{\prime\prime}
is, by construction, contained in
T^{\prime}
.
Hence,
\partial_{1}c^{\prime\prime}=\partial_{1}c^{\prime}=b\in B_{0}(T^{\prime})
.
Since
T^{\prime}\subseteq X
, this proves in particular
B_{0}(X)=B_{0}(T^{\prime})
and
B_{0}(T)=B_{0}(T^{\prime})
.

Let
z\in Z_{1}(T+\sigma-\tau)
and choose
\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dotsc,\mu_{l}\in k
and edges
\tau_{1},\dotsc,\tau_{l}
distinct from
\tau
in
T
such that
z=\mu_{0}\sigma+\sum_{j=1}^{l}\mu_{j}\tau_{j}
.
Then, we have
\mu_{0}\neq 0
unless
z=0
.
Therefore, we assume that
z\neq 0
.

Then, we have

\mu_{0}(u-v)=\mu_{0}\partial_{1}\sigma=-\partial_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\mu_{j}\tau_{j}

for vertices distinct
u
and
v
in
X
.
Furthermore,
\mu_{0}\neq 0
is invertible since there exists some chain
c\in C_{1}(T)
satisfying
\partial_{1}c=u-v
and
\sum_{j=1}^{l}\mu_{j}\tau_{j}=\mu_{0}c
by the injectivity of
\partial_{1}
on
C_{1}(T)
.
Thus,
u-v=-\partial_{1}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\mu_{j}\mu_{0}^{-1}\tau_{j}
.

However, this means that some of the
\tau_{1},\dotsc,\tau_{j}
must coincide with
\tau
by construction.
This is a contradiction to the assumption that
\tau_{1},\dotsc,\tau_{j}
are distinct from
\tau
.
Hence,
Z_{1}(T+\sigma-\tau)=0
.
∎

Definition 3.4
.

Let
T
be a spanning tree of
X
.
A pair
(\sigma,\tau)
of edges
\sigma\in X\setminus T
and
\tau\in T
is called an
edge exchange pair
of
T
in
X
if
T\cup\{\sigma\}\setminus\{\tau\}
is a spanning tree.

If
(\sigma,\tau)
is an edge-exchange pair, then
T\cup\{\sigma\}\setminus\{\tau\}
is denoted by
T+\sigma-\tau
.

3.3.
Homology of spanning trees

Let
X
be a simplicial complex and
T
a spanning tree of
X
.
The relative homology of the pair
(X,T)
is intimately connected to the homology of
X
.
This is a standard result in combinatorial algebraic topology, and proofs of such results can be found in
[
12
, Theorem 1.9.4]
,
[
16
, Theorem 2.33]
, and
[
25
, Theorem 3, p. 140]
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
T
be a spanning tree of
X
.
The homomorphism

j\colon Z_{1}(X)\longrightarrow C_{1}(X,T)\text{,}\quad z\longmapsto z+C_{1}(T)\text{,}

is an isomorphism of
k
-modules.

Proof.

Let
z\in Z_{1}(X)
such that
z\in C_{1}(T)
.
Then,
z=0
by
Z_{1}(T)=0
.
Hence,
j
is a monomorphism.

In order to prove the surjectivity of
j
, let
c\in C_{1}(T)
.
By
B_{0}(T)=B_{0}(X)
, we find
c^{\prime}\in C_{1}(T)
such that
\partial_{1}c=\partial_{1}c^{\prime}
, that is
c-c^{\prime}\in Z_{1}(X)
.
Then, we have

j(c-c^{\prime})=c-c^{\prime}+C_{1}(T)=c+C_{1}(T)

since
c^{\prime}\in C_{1}(T)
.
Thus, the homomorphism
j
is surjective, and it follows
Z_{1}(X)\cong C_{1}(X,T)
.
∎

3.4.
Minimality of spanning trees

We assume that every ordered simplicial complex
(X,\mathord{\preceq})
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

The total order
\mathord{\preceq}
is a well-order, i.e. every subset
A
of
X
admits a minimal element.

(ii)

If
\sigma
is a simplex of
X
and
\tau
a face of
\sigma
, then
\tau\preceq\sigma
.

It turns out that every finite, ordered simplicial complex admits a special, distinguished spanning tree that is minimal in a certain sense w.r.t. the well-ordering
\preceq
.
We characterize these spanning trees using lexicographic orders.

Definition 3.6

(Lexicographic orders)
.

Let
(X,\mathord{\preceq})
be a well-ordered set, e.g.
(X,\mathord{\preceq})
is an ordered simplicial complex.
The
lexicographic order

\mathord{\preceq}_{\mathrm{lex}}
on
2^{X}
is given by

A\preceq_{\mathrm{lex}}B\iff A=B\text{ or }\min((A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A))\in A

for subsets
A,B\subseteq X
.

Remark 3.7
.

Let
A,B\subseteq X
, where
X
is an arbitrary well-ordered set
X
.
Then,
A\preceq_{\mathrm{lex}}B
if and only if there exists

such that for every
y\in(A\setminus B)\cup(B\setminus A)
it holds
x\preceq y
.

Lemma 3.8
.

The lexicographic order
\preceq_{\mathrm{lex}}
is a total order.

Remark 3.9
.

If
X
is a finite set, its power set
2^{X}
is also finite, and then the lexicographic order
\preceq_{\mathrm{lex}}
is a well-ordering of
2^{X}
.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let

be the order-minimal spanning tree of

.
For every edge exchange pair
(\sigma,\tau)

.

(\sigma,\tau)
,

\sigma\prec\tau

Thus, by assumption, the minimal element of

Hence,

Proposition 3.14
.

A spanning tree
T
of

is order-minimal w.r.t.

if and only if there is no edge-exchange pair
(\sigma,\tau)
such that
\sigma\preceq\tau
.

Proof.

The assertion follows from Lemma

together with Lemma
3.3
:
If

is order-minimal, every edge exchange pair

must satisfy
\sigma\preceq\tau

is not order-minimal, let

be an order-minimal spanning tree of

and pick an edge-exchange pair

where
\sigma
is an edge in

but not in

.
∎

Remark 3.15
.

The characterization of order-minimal spanning trees in terms of their edge-exchange pairs in conjunction with the edge-exchange lemma (Lemma

Let

such that

by

.

This method iteratively reduces the spanning tree

until

(Leading simplex)
.

.

4.
Filtrations and cofiltrations of spanning trees

Let
Q=(Q,\mathord{\leq})
be a finite ordered set and
X=(X^{q})_{q\in Q}
a
Q
-indexed filtration of finite simplicial complexes.
In this section, we answer the question of whether there exists a persistent analogue of spanning trees for filtrations of simplicial complexes.

4.1.
Filtrations

First, we assume that there exists a filtration
T=(T^{q})_{q\in Q}
such that
T^{q}
is a spanning tree of
X^{q}
for every
q\in Q
.
We say that
T
is a
filtration of spanning trees
of
X
.

We consider the persistence module
C_{1}(X,T)\colon Q\to\operatorname{Mod}{k}
with
C_{1}(X,T)(q)=C_{1}(X^{q},T^{q})
for
q\in Q
and structure maps

C_{1}(X^{q},T^{q})\to C_{1}(X^{q^{\prime}},T^{q^{\prime}})\text{,}\quad c+C_{1}(T^{q})\mapsto c+C_{1}(T^{q^{\prime}})\text{.}

Lemma 4.1
.

The structure maps of
C_{1}(X,T)
are well-defined module homomorphisms.

Proof.

Let
q\leq q^{\prime}\in Q
be a pair.
The well-definedness of the structure map from
C_{1}(X^{q},T^{q})
to
C_{1}(X^{q^{\prime}},T^{q^{\prime}})
follows immediately from
T^{q}\subseteq T^{q^{\prime}}
.

Let
c\in C_{1}(X^{q})
such that
c
is also a chain in
T^{q^{\prime}}
.
Then, we choose non-zero
\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{m}\in m
and distinct simplices
\sigma_{1},\dotsc,\sigma_{m}
in
T^{q^{\prime}}\cap X^{q}
such that
c=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\sigma_{i}
.
We suppose without loss of generality and to the contrary that
\sigma_{1}\notin T^{q}
.
Then,
T^{q}\cup\{\sigma_{1}\}
is a subcomplex of
X^{q}
with non-trivial cycle module.
However,
T^{q}\cup\{\sigma_{1}\}
is also a subcomplex of
T^{q^{\prime}}
since
\sigma_{1}\in T^{q^{\prime}}
.
Thus, we have a contradiction since
T^{q^{\prime}}
has trivial cycle module.
It follows that
\sigma_{1},\dotsc,\sigma_{m}
are contained in
T^{q}
.
Therefore,
c
is a 1-chain in
T^{q}
, and the kernel of the structure map from
C_{1}(X^{q},T^{q})
to
C_{1}(X^{q^{\prime}},T^{q^{\prime}})
is trivial.
∎

Now, we have for any
q\in Q
an
k
-module isomorphism
j^{q}\colon Z_{1}(X^{q})\to C_{1}(X^{q},T^{q})
.
These degreewise isomorphisms extend to a persistent isomorphism.

Proposition 4.2
.

Suppose there exists a filtration
T=(T^{q})_{q\in Q}
of simplicial complexes such that
T^{q}
is a spanning tree of
X^{q}
for each
q\in Q
.
The persistent homomorphism
j\colon Z_{1}(X)\to C_{1}(X,T)
that is defined at
q\in Q
by

j^{q}\colon Z_{1}(X^{q})\to C_{1}(X^{q},T^{q})\text{,}\qquad z\mapsto z+C_{1}(T^{q})\text{,}

is an isomorphism of persistence modules.

Proof.

The assertion is equivalent to the commutativity of the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{1}(X^{q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{1}(X^{q},T^{q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z_{1}(X^{q^{\prime}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C_{1}(X^{q^{\prime}},T^{q^{\prime}})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{q}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{q^{\prime}}}

where the structure map of
Z_{1}(X)
from
Z_{1}(X^{q})
to
Z_{1}(X^{q^{\prime}})
is the canonical inclusion map.
However, the commutativity of the diagram follows immediately.
∎

4.2.
Cofiltrations

Definition 4.5

(Cofiltrations of spanning trees)
.

Let
(T^{q})_{q\in Q}
be a family of simplicial complexes such that
T^{q}
is a spanning tree of
X^{q}
for any
q\in Q
.
The family
(T^{q})_{q\in Q}
is a
cofiltration of spanning trees
of
X
if
X^{q}\setminus T^{q}\subseteq X^{q^{\prime}}\setminus T^{q^{\prime}}
set-theoretically for any pair
q\leq q^{\prime}
in
Q
.

Remark 4.6
.

A similar but more general notion of cofiltrations was introduced in
[
24
]
:
Given a finite simplicial complex
X
, a
supcomplex

A
of
K
is a subset of
K
closed under taking cofaces.
Then, a
cofiltration
over
K
is a functor from
(Q,\mathord{\leq})
into the set of supcomplexes of
K
, ordered by inclusion.
In this sense, every cofiltration of spanning trees is a cofiltration in the sense of Patel and Rask, cf.
[
24
]
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(X,\mathord{\preceq})
be an ordered simplicial complex and
A
a subcomplex of
X
.
Interpret
A
as an ordered simplicial complex, with ordering given by the restriction of
\mathord{\preceq}
.
For order-minimal spanning trees
T_{A}
and
T_{X}
of
A
and
X
, respectively, it holds
A\setminus T_{A}\subseteq X\setminus T_{X}
set-theoretically.

Proof.

Let
e\in A\setminus T_{A}
.
Then, we find
c=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}e_{i}\in C_{1}(T_{A})
such that the chain
z=c-e
is a cycle in
A
.
By Lemma
3.17
, the leading simplex of
z
must be contained in
A\setminus T_{A}
, and the only possibility is
\LS(z)=e
.
Since
T_{X}
is an order-minimal spanning tree of
X
, it further follows that
\LS(z)\in X\setminus T_{X}
.
Hence, we have
e\in X\setminus T_{X}
.
∎

Our main result in this section is that we can use the above lemma to obtain, for every filtration of finite simplicial complexes over a finite ordered set, a corresponding cofiltration of spanning trees, cf. Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
X=(X^{q})_{q\in Q}
be a filtration of finite simplicial complexes.
There exists a cofiltration of spanning trees
T
of
X
, i.e. a family
T=(T^{q})_{q\in Q}
of simplicial complexes with
X^{q}\setminus T^{q}\subseteq X^{q^{\prime}}\setminus T^{q^{\prime}}
for any pair
q\leq q^{\prime}\in Q
.

Proof.

We choose for any
q\in Q
an order-minimal spanning tree
T^{q}
of
X^{q}
, which exists since
X^{q}
is finite.
Let
q\leq q^{\prime}\in Q
.
We know by assumption that
X^{q}
is a subcomplex of
X^{q^{\prime}}
.
Thus, by Lemma
4.7
, we have
X^{q}\setminus T^{q}\subseteq X^{q^{\prime}}\setminus T^{q^{\prime}}
.
Hence,
(T^{q})_{q\in Q}
is a cofiltration of spanning trees of
(X^{q})_{q\in Q}
.
∎

4.3.
Functoriality of order-minimal spanning trees

Lastly, we show that we can extend the above result in a functorial fashion for order- and dimension-preserving simplicial maps.
A morphism of simplicial complexes
f\colon X\to Y
is called
dimension-preserving
if
\dim\sigma=\dim f(\sigma)
.
Let
\operatorname{Simp}_{\mathrm{ord},\dim}
denote the subcategory of ordered simplicial complexes with dimension- and order-preserving simplicial maps as morphisms.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
A\subseteq X
be a pair of simplicial complexes and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The
n
-difference of
X
and
A
is the set

X\boxminus_{n}A=X_{0}\cup\dotsc\cup X_{n-1}\cup(X_{n}\setminus A_{n})\text{.}

It is immediately clear that
X\boxminus_{n}A
is again a simplicial complex contained in
X
.
Further, we note that the
(n-1)
-skeleton of
X\boxminus_{n}A
coincides with the
(n-1)
-skeleton of
X
.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
f\colon(X,\mathord{\leq}_{X})\to(Y,\mathord{\leq}_{Y})
be an order- and dimension-preserving simplicial map.
Suppose that
T_{X}
is an order-minimal spanning tree of
X
and
T_{Y}
is an order-minimal spanning tree of
Y
.
Then,
f(X\boxminus_{1}T_{X})\subseteq Y\boxminus_{1}T_{Y}
.

Hence,
f
restricts to a simplicial map
X\boxminus_{1}T_{X}\to Y\boxminus_{1}T_{Y}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{m}\neq 0
and
\sigma_{1},\dotsc,\sigma_{m}
be distinct
1
-simplices in
X
satisfying
\sigma_{1}\preceq_{X}\dotsb\preceq_{X}\sigma_{m}
.
That is, for the chain
c=\lambda_{1}\sigma_{1}+\dotsb+\lambda_{m}\sigma_{m}
we have
\LS(c)=\sigma_{m}
.

Since
f
is order-preserving, it follows
f(\sigma_{1})\preceq_{Y}\dotsb\preceq_{Y}f(\sigma_{m})
.
Moreover, since
f
is dimension-preserving, every simplex
f(\sigma_{i})
is an
1
-simplex for
i\in\{1,\dotsc,m\}
.

Let
f_{\sharp}\colon C_{1}(X)\to C_{1}(Y)
be the associated homomorphism of chain modules of
f
.
Therefore, we have
f_{\sharp}(c)=\lambda_{1}f(\sigma_{1})+\dotsb+\lambda_{m}f(\sigma_{m})
.
Hence,
\LS(f_{\sharp}(c))=f(\sigma_{m})=f(\LS(c))
.

Now, let
\sigma
be an
1
-simplex in
X
but not in
T_{X}
.
Then, we choose a chain
c
in
T_{X}
such that
c-\sigma\in Z_{1}(X)
.
Since the spanning tree
T_{X}
is order-minimal, we have
\LS(c-\sigma)\in X\setminus T_{X}
.
Thus, it holds
\LS(c-\sigma)=\sigma
.

By the considerations above, we have
\LS(f_{\sharp}(c-\sigma))=f(\LS(c-\sigma))=f(\sigma)
.
Since
T_{Y}
is also order-minimal and
f_{\sharp}(c-\sigma)
is by definition a non-zero
1
-cycle in
Y
, it follows
\LS(f_{\sharp}(c-\sigma))\in Y\setminus T_{Y}
.
Hence,
f(\sigma)\in Y\boxminus_{1}T_{Y}
, which proves the assertion.
∎

Remark 4.11
.

Every monomorphism of simplicial complexes is necessarily dimension-preserving.
Hence, if
f\colon(X,\mathord{\leq}_{X})\to(Y,\mathord{\leq}_{Y})
is a monomorphism, it follows
f(X\setminus T_{X})\subseteq Y\setminus T_{Y}
.

Example 4.12
.

Let
X
be a wedge of two triangles and
Y
a single triangle.
A simplicial map from
X
into

that sends each triangle in

onto the triangle

is dimension-preserving but not injective.

Corollary 4.13
.

There is an endofunctor

an ordered simplicial complex, let

be the unique order-minimal spanning tree of

.
Then, define

.

For a morphism

in
\operatorname{Simp}_{\mathrm{ord},\dim}
, let

be the order-minimal spanning tree of
Y
.
Define the morphism
\tau_{1}(f)\colon X\boxminus_{1}T_{X}\to Y\boxminus_{1}T_{Y}
to be the underlying simplicial map of
f
formally restricted to
X\boxminus_{1}T_{X}
, interpreted as a morphism in
\operatorname{Simp}_{\mathrm{ord},\dim}
.

In particular, this functor is faithful but not full in general.

Remark 4.14
.

Let
A\subseteq X
be a simplicial pair and let
i\colon A\to X
denote the canonical inclusion map.
Further, we assume that
X
is endowed with a simplicial order
\preceq_{X}
and we endow
A
with the restriction of
\preceq_{X}
as simplicial order.
Under this assumption, we see that
i
is an order-preserving simplicial map
(A,\preceq_{X})\to(X,\preceq_{X})
.

Moreover, it follows from Remark
4.11
that
i
is also dimension-preserving.
Thus, Corollary
4.13
implies that there is an order- and dimension-preserving simplicial map
\tau_{1}(i)\colon(A\boxminus_{1}T_{A},\mathord{\preceq}_{X})\to(X\boxminus_{1}T_{X},\mathord{\preceq}_{X})
, where
T_{A}
is the order-minimal spanning tree of
A
and
T_{X}
is the order-minimal spanning tree of
X
.

From this result, it directly follows that
A_{1}\setminus T_{A}
is a subset of
X_{1}\setminus T_{X}
.
Hence, Corollary
4.13
is a vast generalization of Lemma
4.7
and Theorem
4.8
.

5.
Upper-set decomposables as spans

Let
k
be a principal ideal domain and
Q=(Q,\mathord{\leq})
a ordered set.
Denote by
U_{0}
the forgetful functor
\operatorname{Mod}{k}\to\operatorname{Set}
sending a
k
-module to its underlying set.
The functor
U_{0}
is right-adjoint to the
k
-span functor
k\colon\operatorname{Set}\to\operatorname{Mod}{k}
, which assigns to a set
B
the free
k
-module
k(B)
with basis
B
.

Define the functors

\displaystyle U_{Q}\colon\operatorname{Mod}{kQ}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\operatorname{Set}^{Q}\text{,}

\displaystyle\qquad k_{Q}\colon\operatorname{Set}^{Q}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\operatorname{Mod}{kQ}\text{,}

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\longmapsto U_{0}\circ M\text{,}

\displaystyle\qquad B

\displaystyle\longmapsto k\circ B\text{.}

It follows from categorical standard arguments that
U_{Q}
and
k_{Q}
are a pair of adjoint functors.

Lemma 5.1
.

There is an adjunction
U_{Q}\dashv k_{Q}
.

Proof.

Let
\eta=(\eta_{MB}\colon\Hom_{\operatorname{Mod}{k}}(M,k(B))\to\Hom_{\operatorname{Set}}(U(M),B))_{M,B}
be the underlying natural isomorphism of the adjunction
U\dashv k
.
For
N\in\operatorname{Mod}{kQ}
and
C\in\operatorname{Set}^{Q}
, define
\eta_{\ast NC}\colon\Hom_{\operatorname{Mod}{kQ}}(N,k_{Q}(C))\to\Hom_{\operatorname{Set}^{Q}}(U_{Q}(N),C)
by
\eta_{\ast NC}(\varphi)_{q}=\eta_{N_{q},C_{q}}(\varphi_{q})
for all
\varphi=(\varphi_{q})_{q\in Q}\colon N\to k_{Q}(C)
and
q\in Q
.
It follows from a standard calculation that
\eta_{\ast}=(\eta_{\ast}NC)_{N\in\operatorname{Mod}{kQ},C\in\operatorname{Set}^{I}}
is a natural isomorphism from
\Hom_{\operatorname{Mod}{kQ}}(\mathord{-},k_{Q}(\mathord{-}))
in
\Hom_{\operatorname{Set}^{Q}}(U_{Q}(\mathord{-}),\mathord{-})
.
Thus,
U_{Q}
is left-adjoint to
k_{Q}
.
∎

In this sense, the functor
k_{Q}\colon\operatorname{Set}^{Q}\to\operatorname{Mod}{kQ}
can be seen as the persistent generalization of the free
k
-span functor
k\colon\operatorname{Set}\to\operatorname{Mod}{k}
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
Q
be an ordered set.

(1)

A subset
U\subseteq Q
is an
upper set
in
Q
if

\{q\in Q\mid q\geq a\}\subseteq U

for all
a\in U
.

(2)

For an upper set
U
in
Q
, define the persistent set
\mathrm{pt}[U]\colon Q\to\operatorname{Set}
by

\mathrm{pt}[U](q)=\begin{cases}\{q\}&if $q\in U$,\\
\emptyset&otherwise,\end{cases}

with the obvious inclusions.
The persistent set
\mathrm{pt}[U]
is called
upper set module
of
U
.

(3)

A persistence module
M\colon Q\to\operatorname{Mod}{k}
is said to be
upper set decomposable
if there exists a family
(U_{i})_{i\in I}
of upper sets in
Q
such that

M\cong\bigoplus_{i\in I}k_{Q}(\mathrm{pt}[U_{i}])\text{.}

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
B\colon Q\to\operatorname{Set}
a functor.
For
q\in Q
, denote by
\iota_{q}\colon B(q)\to\colim B
the colimit projection.
The following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

For every
q\in Q
, the map
\iota_{q}
is injective.

(ii)

The persistence module
\mathop{k_{Q}}B
is upper set decomposable.

(iii)

For every
q\in Q
, the colimit projection
\iota^{\prime}_{q}\colon\mathop{k_{Q}}B(q)\to\colim\mathop{k_{Q}}B
is a monomorphism.

Proof.

(i)
to
(ii)
:
We define the persistent set
B^{\prime}\colon Q\to\operatorname{Set}
as follows:
For
q\in Q
, let
B^{\prime}(q)=\iota_{q}(B(q))
.

For an ordered pair
q\leq q^{\prime}
in
Q
and every
x\in B^{\prime}(q)
we have
x\in B^{\prime}(q^{\prime})
because
\iota_{q}=\iota_{q^{\prime}}\circ B(q\leq q^{\prime})
.
Therefore, the map
B^{\prime}(q\leq q^{\prime})\colon\iota_{q}(B(q))\to\iota_{q^{\prime}}(B(q^{\prime}))
can be chosen as the inclusion.

Moreover,
B^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
B
by construction.
For every
q\in Q
, the map

B(q)\to B^{\prime}(q)\text{,}\qquad b\mapsto\iota_{q}(b)\text{,}

is bijective by the injectivity of
\iota_{q}
.
Thus,
B^{\prime}\cong B
.

Furthermore, the persistence module
B^{\prime}
can be decomposed into the coproduct

B^{\prime}=\coprod_{x\in\colim B}\mathrm{pt}[U_{x}]\text{,}

where
U_{x}=\{q\in Q\mid x\in B^{\prime}(q)\}
.
The set
U_{x}
is by construction an upper set.
Thus,
B^{\prime}
is upper set decomposable.

(ii)
to
(iii)
:
Let
(U_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of upper sets of
Q
such that
\mathop{k_{Q}}B=\bigoplus_{i\in I}k[U_{i}]
.
Since every upper set module
k[U_{i}]
admits injective colimit projections
k[U_{i}](q)\to\colim k[U_{i}]
for any
q\in Q
, it follows that
k_{Q}(B)
also admits injective colimit projections as claimed.

(iii)
to
(i)
:
Suppose that there exists some
q\in Q
such that
\iota_{q}
is not injective.
Then, there are
b,b^{\prime}\in B(q)
such that
b\neq b^{\prime}
and
\iota_{q}(b)=\iota_{q}(b^{\prime})
.
Thus,
b-b^{\prime}\in k_{Q}B(q)
is a non-zero element of the kernel of
\iota^{\prime}_{q}
since
k_{Q}
preserves colimits.
Hence,
\iota^{\prime}_{q}
is not injective and thus not a monomorphism.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

A similar result was established in
[
8
, Proposition 3.1]
.
A functor
B\colon Q\to\operatorname{Mod}{k}
is called
multifiltration
if the structure maps of
B
are monomorphisms.
For the ordered set
Q=\mathbb{N}^{d}
(or more generally for
Q
a lattice), the conditions of Proposition
5.3
are equivalent to the condition that the functor
B
is a multifiltration, cf.
[
8
, Proposition 3.3]
.
Moreover, any non-trivial multifiltration
B
is uniquely decomposable into indecomposables and the indecomposable multifiltrations over
\mathbb{N}^{d}
are precisely the
k
-linear generators of upper set modules
[
8
, Proposition 3.1, Corollary 3.2]
.
In this sense, Proposition
5.3
is a generalization of
[
8
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Example 5.5
.

Let
U=\{(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\mid a+b\geq 0\}
.
This is clearly an upper set of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.
However, the upper set module
k[U]
is not finitely generated, since we need a generator in each
(a,-a)
for
a\in\mathbb{Z}
to cover
U
by principal upper sets of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

6.
Upper set generators of 1-dimensional persistent homology

Let
X=(X^{q})_{q\in Q}
be a
Q
-filtration of simplicial complexes, and let
T=(T^{q})_{q\in Q}
be a subfiltration of spanning trees for
X
.
By Proposition
4.2
, the quotient persistence module
C_{1}(X,T)=C_{1}(X)/C_{1}(T)
is isomorphic to
Z_{1}(X)
.

Because
C_{1}(X,T)
has injective colimit projections and a persistent basis, Proposition
5.3
implies that
C_{1}(X,T)
is upper set decomposable.
Therefore, there is a decomposition
Z_{1}(X)\cong\bigoplus_{i\in I}k[U_{i}]
for some family
(U_{i})_{i\in I}
of upper sets in
Q
.

However, the existence of this decomposition depends on the requirement that
T
is a subfiltration of spanning trees.
Subfiltrations of spanning trees do not always exist, as demonstrated in Example
4.4
, if
Q
is an arbitrarily ordered set.
In fact, even for a
3\times 3
-sublattice of
\mathbb{N}^{2}
, the existence of filtrations of spanning trees fails.

As an alternative for the general case, we construct a covering of
Z_{1}(X)
by an upper set decomposable module, with a cofiltration of spanning trees of
X
as the main input.
Explicitly, we choose the cofiltration of order-minimal spanning trees, which admits the properties in Theorem
4.8
.

We assume that the ordered set
Q
and the simplicial complex
\bigcup_{q\in Q}X^{q}
are finite and endowed with a simplicial order
\preceq
.
Then, for every
q\in Q
we interpret the subcomplex
X^{q}
as the ordered simplicial complex
(X^{q},\mathord{\preceq})
, which naturally admits an injective order-preserving simplicial map into
(X,\mathord{\preceq})
.
We choose the uniquely determined order-minimal spanning tree
T^{q}
of
(X^{q},\mathord{\preceq})
, i.e.
T^{q}=\tau_{1}(X^{q},\mathord{\preceq})
.

It follows from Theorem
4.8
that
X^{q}\boxminus_{1}T^{q}
is a subcomplex of
X^{q^{\prime}}\boxminus_{1}T^{q^{\prime}}
for every ordered pair
q\leq q^{\prime}
in
Q
.
Thus,
T=(T^{q})_{q\in Q}=(\tau_{1}(X^{q}))_{q\in Q}
is a cofiltration of spanning trees of
X
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\sigma
be an edge in the total simplicial complex
\bigcup_{q\in Q}X^{q}\boxminus_{1}T^{q}
.
The
representative persistent set
of
\sigma
is

\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}^{q}=\{j^{-1}_{q^{\prime}}(\sigma)\mid q^{\prime}\in Q\text{,}\ q^{\prime}\leq q\text{,}\ \sigma\in X^{q^{\prime}}\boxminus_{1}T^{q^{\prime}}\}\text{,}

where
j_{q^{\prime}}\colon Z_{1}(X^{q^{\prime}})\to C_{1}(X^{q^{\prime}},T^{q^{\prime}})
is the isomorphism defined by
j_{q^{\prime}}(z)=z+C_{1}(T^{q^{\prime}})
.

Remark 6.2
.

Since
(T^{q})_{q\in Q}
is a cofiltration of
X
, it follows that the structure maps of the persistent set
\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}
are given by inclusions.

Definition 6.3
.

The
associated upper set precover
of
X=(X^{q})_{q\in Q}
with respect to the cofiltration of spanning trees
T
is defined by

\ug_{T}(X)=\bigoplus_{\sigma}k_{Q}\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}\text{,}

where
\sigma
goes over all edges in
\bigcup_{q\in Q}X^{q}\boxminus_{1}T^{q}
.

Remark 6.4
.

It follows from the adjunction
U_{Q}\vdash k_{Q}
, Lemma
5.1
, that
\ug_{T}(X)=k_{Q}(\coprod_{\sigma}B_{\sigma})
, where
\sigma
goes over all edges in
\bigcup_{q\in Q}X^{q}\boxminus_{1}T^{q}
.

Theorem 6.5
.

The associated upper set precover
\ug_{T}(X)
is upper set decomposable and admits an epimorphism
\ug_{T}(X)\to Z_{1}(X)
.

Proof.

The persistent set
\mathcal{B}=(\mathcal{B}^{q})_{q\in Q}=\coprod_{\sigma}\mathcal{B}_{\sigma}
is given by

\mathcal{B}^{q}=\{(\sigma,j^{-1}_{q^{\prime}}(\sigma))\mid q^{\prime}\in Q\text{,}\ q^{\prime}\leq q\text{,}\ \sigma\in(X^{q}\boxminus_{1}T^{q})\cap(X^{q^{\prime}}\boxminus_{1}T^{q^{\prime}})\}\text{.}

We define the persistent map
\varphi=(\varphi^{q})_{q\in Q}\colon\mathcal{B}\to\mathop{U_{Q}}Z_{1}(X)
as follows:
For
(\sigma,z)\in\mathcal{B}^{q}
,
z
is a cycle of
X^{q^{\prime}}
for some
q^{\prime}\in Q
satisfying
q^{\prime}\leq q
.
Then,
z
is a cycle in
X^{q}
because
X^{q^{\prime}}\subseteq X^{q}
, and we define
\varphi^{q}(\sigma,z)=z\in Z_{1}(X^{q})
.
This induces a uniquely determined homomorphism
\tilde{\varphi}^{q}\colon\ug_{T}(X)=k_{Q}(\mathcal{B})\to Z_{1}(X^{q})
.

Furthermore, for every
q\in Q
we have a basis

B_{q}=\{j_{q}^{-1}(\sigma+C_{1}(T^{q}))\mid\sigma\in(X^{q}\boxminus_{1}T^{q})_{1}\}

of
Z_{1}(X^{q})
, which is contained in
\mathcal{B}^{q}
.
Thus, the image of
\tilde{\varphi}^{q}
contains
B_{q}
, and
\tilde{\varphi}^{q}
is pointwise surjective.
Hence,
\tilde{\varphi}
is an epimorphism in the category of persistence modules.
∎

7.
Higher-dimensional spanning complexes

In the previous sections we have discussed spanning trees of simplicial complexes, which allow us to determine the
1
-dimensional cycle modules of simplicial complexes by inspecting
1
-dimensional relative chain modules.
An open question is whether there is a similar, easily computable type of subcomplex such that for a dimension
n\in\mathbb{N}
we can determine
n
-dimensional cycle modules in a similar manner by inspecting
n
-dimensional relative chain modules.

As a first step towards an answer of this question, we consider in this section a generalization of spanning trees to higher dimensions by merely generalizing Definition
3.1
.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
X
be a simplicial complex and
n>0
.
A subcomplex
A
of
X
is an
n
-spanning complex
of
X
if the following conditions hold:

(i)

\skel_{n-1}(A)=\skel_{n-1}(X)
.

(ii)

The boundary homomorphism
\partial_{n}\colon C_{n}(A)\to C_{n-1}(A)
is injective.

(iii)

B_{n-1}(A)=B_{n-1}(X)
.

This definition directly generalizes the notion of a spanning tree defined in Definition
3.1
by replacing the dimensions by an fixed but arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 7.2
.

For
n=1
, every spanning tree is an
n
-spanning complex.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
A
be an
n
-spanning complex of
X
.
The homomorphism

\displaystyle j\colon Z_{n}(A)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow C_{n}(X,A)\text{,}

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle\longmapsto z+C_{n}(A)\text{,}

is an isomorphism of
k
-modules.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to Proposition
3.5
.
The injectivity of
j
follows from
Z_{n}(A)=0
and the surjectivity of
j
follows from
B_{n-1}(X)=B_{n-1}(A)
together with
Z_{n}(A)=0
.
∎

Theorem 7.4

(Existence of
n
-spanning complexes)
.

For any simplicial complex
X
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a subcomplex
A
such that
Z_{n}(A)=0
and
B_{n-1}(A)=B_{n-1}(X)
, i.e.
X
admits an
n
-spanning complex.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{X}=\{A\subseteq X\mid\text{$A$ is a subcomplex s.t.\ $Z_{n}(A)=0$}\}
.
For every subset
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{X}
that is totally ordered by set inclusion, we consider the subcomplex
\bigcup\mathcal{A}=\bigcup_{A\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}A
of
X
.
We have
Z_{n}(\bigcup\mathcal{A}^{\prime})=0
:
Let
z
be an
n
-chain in
\bigcup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
such that
\partial_{n}z=0
.
Then, there are non-zero
\lambda_{1},\dotsc,\lambda_{m}\in k
and distinct
n
-simplices
\sigma_{1},\dotsc,\sigma_{m}
in
\bigcup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
such that
z=\lambda_{1}\sigma_{1}+\dotsb+\lambda_{m}\sigma_{m}
.
Then, we can choose
A_{1},\dotsc,A_{m}\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
such that
\sigma_{i}\in A_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\dotsc,m\}
.
Since
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is totally ordered by set inclusion there exists an
i_{0}\in\{1,\dotsc,m\}
such that
A_{i}\subseteq A_{i_{0}}
for all
i\in\{1,\dotsc,m\}
.
Thus,
z
is actually an
n
-chain in
B_{i_{0}}\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
.
Since
B_{i_{0}}
satisfies
Z_{1}(B_{i_{0}})=0
by assumption,
z=0
.

Therefore, every totally ordered subset of
\mathcal{A}_{X}
admits an upper bound in
\mathcal{A}_{X}
.
It follows from Zorn’s lemma that there exists
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
that is maximal w.r.t. set inclusion.

It remains to prove that such maximal
A
in
\mathcal{A}_{X}
satisfies
B_{n-1}(A)=B_{n-1}(X)
, i.e.
B_{n-1}(X)\subseteq B_{n-1}(A)
.
In particular, it suffices to prove that for every
n
-simplex
\sigma
in
X
there exists an
n
-chain
c
in
A
such that
\partial_{n}c=\partial_{n}\sigma
.
Let
\sigma
be an
n
-simplex in
X
.
If
\sigma\in A
, then trivially
\partial_{n}\sigma\in B_{n-1}(A)
.
Thus, we consider only the case that
\sigma
is not in
A
.
Then, we can consider the subcomplex
A^{\prime}=A\cup\{\sigma\}
of
X
.
Since
A
is maximal by construction in
\mathcal{A}_{X}
and
A\subsetneq A^{\prime}
, it follows that
A^{\prime}
cannot be contained in
\mathcal{A}_{X}
.
That is,
Z_{n}(A^{\prime})
cannot be trivial.

Therefore, there exists an
n
-cycle
z=\mu\sigma+c
in
A^{\prime}
with
\mu\in\{1,-1\}
and
c
an
n
-chain in
A
.
We can assume without loss of generality that
\mu=1
.
But then we have
\partial_{n}\sigma=\partial_{n}c\in B_{n-1}(A)
.
Hence,
B_{n-1}(X)\subseteq B_{n-1}(A)
.
∎

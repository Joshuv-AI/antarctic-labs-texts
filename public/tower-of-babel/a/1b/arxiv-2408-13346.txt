1.
Introduction

An integer partition
\lambda
of a positive integer
n
is a weakly decreasing sequence of positive integers
\lambda_{i}
whose sum is
n
. We use the notation
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
with
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\lambda_{\ell}>0
and
|\lambda|:=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lambda_{i}=n
. We refer to
|\lambda|
as the size of
\lambda
and the numbers
\lambda_{i}
as the parts of
\lambda
.
The length of
\lambda
is the number of parts of
\lambda
and is denoted by
\ell(\lambda)
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}(n)
the set of partitions of
n
and let
p(n):=|\mathcal{P}(n)|
. Since the empty partition is the only partition of
0
, we have
p(0)=1
. We use the convention that
p(x)=0
if
x\not\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
(and similarly for other partition statistics). For more on the theory of partitions, we refer the reader to
[
1
]
.

Different classes of symmetric polynomials defined in terms of partitions form bases for the graded algebra of symmetric functions defined over the rational numbers. For details, we refer the reader to
[
10
]
. Recently, the third author has derived several partition identities as specializations of the fundamental
relations between complete and elementary symmetric functions (see
[
5
]
).

In this article, we take a different approach and study properties of elementary symmetric polynomials evaluated at parts of partitions. Recall that the
j^{\textrm{th}}
elementary symmetric polynomial is defined by

e_{j}(X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n})=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{j}\leq n}X_{i_{1}}X_{i_{2}}\cdots X_{i_{j}}&\text{ if }j\leq n,\\
0&\text{ if }j>n.\end{cases}

Thus, given a partition
\lambda
, we have
e_{j}(\lambda)=0
if
\ell(\lambda)<j
and otherwise

e_{j}(\lambda)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{j}\leq\ell(\lambda)}\lambda_{i_{1}}\lambda_{i_{2}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{j}}.

In
[
8
]
, the authors study the case
e_{j}(\lambda)
where
\ell(\lambda)=j
and call this statistic the norm of
\lambda
.

Given a set
A
of positive integers, we denote by
\mathcal{P}(n|A)
the set of partitions of
n
with parts from
A
. We set
p(n|A):=|\mathcal{P}(n|A)|
. We introduce the function

e_{j}p_{A}(n):=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n|A)}e_{j}(\lambda)

and define the restricted divisor function

\sigma_{j,A}(n):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d|n\\
d\in A\end{subarray}}d^{j}.

Our first result establishes relations between the partition function
e_{j}p_{A}(n)
and the divisor function
\sigma_{j,A}(n)
when
j=2
or
3
.

Theorem 1
.

For
n>0
,

(1)

\displaystyle{e_{2}p_{A}(n)=\frac{1}{2}\left(n^{2}\,p(n|A)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sigma_{2,A}(k)\,p(n-k|A)\right)}
;

(2)

\displaystyle{e_{3}p_{A}(n)=\frac{1}{6}\left(n^{3}\,p(n|A)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big(3\,n\,\sigma_{2,A}(k)-2\,\sigma_{3,A}(k)\big)\,p(n-k|A)\right)}
.

Then we consider
\mathcal{P}(m,n)
, the set of partitions of
n
with length equal to
m
and set

p_{m}(n):=|\mathcal{P}(m,n)|
and

e_{j}p_{m}(n):=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(m,n)}e_{j}(\lambda).

In

Nijenhuis and Wilf establish the minimal periods for

2.
Proof of Theorem
1

First, we prove a useful lemma.
Given a function
f
defined on subset
A
of non-negative integers, let

S_{A,f}(n):=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n|A)}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell(\lambda)}f(\lambda_{i}).

Lemma 3
.

The generating function for
S_{A,f}(n)
is given by

(1)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{A,f}(n)\,q^{n}=\prod_{a\in A}\frac{1}{1-q^{a}}\sum_{a\in A}\frac{f(a)\,q^{a}}{1-q^{a}}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{A,f}(n)\,q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\Bigg|_{z=1}\prod_{a\in A}(1+z^{f(a)}\,q^{a}+z^{2f(a)}\,q^{a+a}+z^{3f(a)}\,q^{a+a+a}+\cdots)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\prod_{a\in A}\frac{1}{1-z^{f(a)\,q^{a}}}\Bigg|_{z=1}=\prod_{a\in A}\frac{1}{1-q^{a}}\sum_{a\in A}\frac{f(a)\,q^{a}}{1-q^{a}}.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

As given in
[
6
]
, the generating function for
\sigma_{j,A}(n)
is

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j,A}(n)\,q^{n}=\sum_{a\in A}\frac{a^{j}\,q^{a}}{1-q^{a}}.

For a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
and a positive integer
k
we define the power sum
P_{k}(\lambda)
as

P_{k}(\lambda):=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lambda_{i}^{k}.

From the relations

e_{2}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}\left(P_{1}(\lambda)^{2}-P_{2}(\lambda)\right)

and

e_{3}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{6}\left(P_{1}(\lambda)^{3}-3\,P_{1}(\lambda)\,P_{2}(\lambda)+2\,P_{3}(\lambda)\right),

we can write

\displaystyle e_{2}p_{A}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n|A)}e_{2}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n|A)}\left(P_{1}(\lambda)^{2}-P_{2}(\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(n^{2}\,p(n)-\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n|A)}P_{2}(\lambda)\right)

and

\displaystyle e_{3}p_{A}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n|A)}e_{3}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n|A)}\left(P_{1}(\lambda)^{3}-3\,P_{1}(\lambda)\,P_{2}(\lambda)+2\,P_{3}(\lambda)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\left(n^{3}\,p(n|A)-3\,n\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n|A)}P_{2}(\lambda)+2\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n|A)}P_{3}(\lambda)\right).

From (
1
) with
f(a)=a^{j}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j,A}(n)\,q^{n}=\sum_{a\in A}\frac{a^{j}\,q^{a}}{1-q^{a}}=\prod_{a\in A}(1-q^{a})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n|A)}P_{j}(\lambda)\right)q^{n}.

Thus we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n|A)}P_{j}(\lambda)\right)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{a\in A}\frac{1}{1-q^{a}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j,A}(n)\,q^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p(n|A)\,q^{n}\right)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j,A}(n)\,q^{n}\right).

Multiplying the two power series and comparing coefficients, we obtain

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(n|A)}P_{j}(\lambda)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sigma_{j,A}(k)\,p(n-k|A).

This concludes the proof.
∎

For the last equivalence we used Pascal’s formula again:

Proof of Theorem

Proof of
{e_{2}p_{4}(n)\equiv e_{2}p_{4}(n+48)\pmod{2}}
.

For two formal power series, we write

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n)q^{n}\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b(n)q^{n}\pmod{m}

to mean
a(n)\equiv b(n)\pmod{m}
for all
n\geq 0
.

By the definition of
e_{2}(\lambda)
, if
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(4,n)
, then
e_{2}(\lambda)
is odd if and only if
\lambda
has one or two even parts.
We first establish the generating function for the number of partitions of
n
into
4
parts with exactly two even parts.

Recall that the conjugate of the partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
is the partition
\lambda^{\prime}
with
\lambda^{\prime}_{j}=|\{i\mid\lambda_{i}\geq j\}|
. Considering conjugates of partitions, the generating function for the number of partitions of
n
into two even parts is given by

\frac{q^{4}}{1-q^{4}}\frac{1}{1-q^{2}}

and the generating function for the number of partitions of
n
into two odd parts is given by

\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{4}}\frac{1}{1-q^{2}}.

Thus, the generating function for the number of partitions of
n
into
4
parts with exactly two even parts is

\displaystyle\frac{q^{6}}{(1-q^{2})^{2}(1-q^{4})^{2}}.

Similarly, the generating function for the number of partitions of
n
into four parts with exactly one part even is given by

\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{2}}\cdot\frac{q^{3}}{(1-q^{2})(1-q^{4})(1-q^{6})}=\frac{q^{5}}{(1-q^{2})^{2}(1-q^{4})(1-q^{6})}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e_{2}p_{4}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{q^{6}}{(1-q^{2})^{2}(1-q^{4})^{2}}+\frac{q^{5}}{(1-q^{2})^{2}(1-q^{4})(1-q^{6})}\pmod{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\frac{q^{6}}{1-q^{4}}+\frac{q^{5}}{1-q^{6}}\right)\frac{1}{(1-q^{2})^{4}}\pmod{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\frac{q^{6}}{1-q^{4}}+\frac{q^{5}}{1-q^{6}}\right)\frac{1}{1-q^{8}}\pmod{2}.

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(e_{2}p_{4}(n)-e_{2}p_{4}(n-48))q^{n}

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\frac{q^{6}}{1-q^{4}}+\frac{q^{5}}{1-q^{6}}\right)\frac{1-q^{48}}{1-q^{8}}\pmod{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\frac{q^{6}}{1-q^{4}}+\frac{q^{5}}{1-q^{6}}\right)A(q)\pmod{2},

where
A(q):=1+q^{8}+q^{16}+q^{24}+q^{32}+q^{40}
.

Writing
A(q)=(1+q^{8})(1+q^{16}+q^{32})
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{q^{6}}{1-q^{4}}A(q)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{q^{6}}{1-q^{4}}(1-q^{8})(1+q^{16}+q^{32})\pmod{2}

\displaystyle=q^{6}(1+q^{4})(1+q^{16}+q^{32}).

Writing
A(q)=(1+q^{24})(1+q^{8}+q^{16})
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{q^{5}}{1-q^{6}}A(q)

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{q^{5}}{1-q^{6}}(1-q^{24})(1+q^{8}+q^{16})\pmod{2}

\displaystyle=q^{5}(1+q^{12})(1+q^{6})(1+q^{8}+q^{16}).

Hence, for
n\geq 48
,
e_{2}p_{4}(n)-e_{2}p_{4}(n-48)\equiv 0\pmod{2}
.

Proof of
{e_{2}p_{4}(n)\equiv e_{2}p_{4}(n+54)\pmod{3}}
.

By listing all combinations of residues modulo
3
for parts of
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}(4,n)
, we see that

(i)

e_{2}(\lambda)\equiv 1\pmod{3}
if and only if there are two different residues modulo
3
among the parts of
\lambda
and there are two parts congruent to each such residue;

(ii)

e_{2}(\lambda)\equiv 2\pmod{3}
if and only if there are three different residues modulo
3
among the parts of
\lambda
(thus, there are two parts congruent to each other modulo
3
and the other two parts have residues different from each other and from the pair).

(iii)

e_{2}(\lambda)\equiv 0\pmod{3}
in all other cases.

The generating function for the number of partitions of
n
in case (i) is given by

\displaystyle\frac{q^{6}}{1-q^{6}}\frac{1}{1-q^{3}}\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{6}}\frac{1}{1-q^{3}}

\displaystyle+\frac{q^{6}}{1-q^{6}}\frac{1}{1-q^{3}}\frac{q^{4}}{1-q^{6}}\frac{1}{1-q^{3}}

\displaystyle+\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{6}}\frac{1}{1-q^{3}}\frac{q^{4}}{1-q^{6}}\frac{1}{1-q^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{10}+q^{8}+q^{6}}{(1-q^{3})^{2}(1-q^{6})^{2}}.

The generating function for the number of partitions of
n
in case (ii) is given by

\displaystyle\frac{q^{6}}{(1-q^{6})(1-q^{3})}\frac{q}{1-q^{3}}\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{3}}

\displaystyle+\frac{q^{2}}{(1-q^{6})(1-q^{3})}\frac{q^{3}}{1-q^{3}}\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{3}}

\displaystyle+\frac{q^{4}}{(1-q^{6})(1-q^{3})}\frac{q^{3}}{1-q^{3}}\frac{q}{1-q^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{9}+q^{8}+q^{7}}{(1-q^{6})(1-q^{3})^{3}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e_{2}p_{4}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{q^{10}+q^{8}+q^{6}}{(1-q^{3})^{2}(1-q^{6})^{2}}+\frac{2(q^{9}+q^{8}+q^{7})}{(1-q^{6})(1-q^{3})^{3}}\pmod{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{6}+2q^{7}+3q^{8}+q^{9}+q^{10}-q^{11}-3q^{13}-2q^{14}-2q^{15}}{(1-q^{6})^{2}(1-q^{3})^{3}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{D(q)}{(1-q^{3})^{3}(1-q^{6})^{2}}\pmod{3},

where
D(q)=q^{6}+2q^{7}+q^{9}+q^{10}+2q^{11}+q^{14}+q^{15}
.

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(e_{2}p_{4}(n)-e_{2}p_{4}(n-54))q^{n}

\displaystyle\equiv D(q)\frac{1-q^{54}}{(1-q^{3})^{3}(1-q^{6})^{2}}\pmod{3}

\displaystyle\equiv D(q)\frac{(1-q^{3})^{9}(1+q^{3})^{9}}{(1-q^{3})^{3}(1-q^{6})^{2}}\pmod{3}

\displaystyle\equiv D(q)\frac{(1-q^{3})^{6}(1+q^{3})^{9}}{(1-q^{6})^{2}}\pmod{3}

\displaystyle\equiv D(q)(1-q^{3})^{4}(1+q^{3})^{7}\pmod{3}.

Hence, for
n\geq 54
,
e_{2}p_{4}(n)-e_{2}p_{4}(n-54)\equiv 0\pmod{3}
.

Proof of
{e_{2}p_{4}(n)\equiv e_{2}p_{4}(n+96)\pmod{4}}

Proposition 2

\textrm{pre}_{2}(\varphi(\lambda))=\textrm{pre}_{2}(\lambda\cup(1))
has
(m_{\lambda}(1)+1)m_{\lambda}(2)
parts equal to
2
. Hence,

(3)

b_{2,2}(2n+1)-b_{2,2}(2n)=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(2n)}m_{\lambda}(2).

The right hand side of (
3
) is the total number of parts equal to
2
in all partitions in
\mathcal{B}(2n)
.

Next, note that if
\lambda\in B(2n)
has no parts equal to
1
, then
\textrm{pre}_{2}(\lambda)
has no parts equal to
2
. Denote by
\mathcal{B}^{*}(2n)
the set of partitions in
\mathcal{B}(2n)
that have at least one part equal to
1
. Then, for
\lambda\in\mathcal{B}^{*}(2n)
,
m_{\lambda}(1)
is even.
Let
\psi:\mathcal{B}^{*}(2n)\to\mathcal{B}(2n-1)
be defined by
\psi(\lambda)=\lambda\setminus(1)
. Clearly,
\psi
is a bijection and
m_{\lambda}(2)=m_{\psi(\lambda)}(2)
for all
\lambda\in\mathcal{B}^{*}(2n)
. If
\lambda\in\mathcal{B}^{*}(2n)
, then
\textrm{pre}_{2}(\lambda)
has
m_{\lambda}(1)m_{\lambda}(2)
parts equal to
2
and, by construction,

\textrm{pre}_{2}(\psi(\lambda))=\textrm{pre}_{2}(\lambda\setminus(1))
has
(m_{\lambda}(1)-1)m_{\lambda}(2)>0
parts equal to
2
. Hence,

b_{2,2}(2n)-b_{2,2}(2n-1)=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{B}^{*}(2n)}m_{\lambda}(2)=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(2n-1)}m_{\lambda}(2),

which with (
3
) implies that for
n\geq 1
,

\Delta_{2,2}(n)=b_{2,2}(n+1)-b_{2,2}(n)=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{B}(n)}m_{\lambda}(2).

The generating function for the number of parts equal to
2
in all partitions in
\mathcal{B}(n)
is obtained by
taking the derivative of

\frac{1-q^{2}}{1-zq^{2}}\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{2^{i}}}

with respect to
z
and evaluating at
z=1
.

Thus

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{2,2}(n)q^{n}=\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{2}}\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{2^{i}}}.

Moreover,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\Delta_{2,2}(n)q^{n}=\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{2}}\frac{1}{1+q}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{2^{i}}}.

Since

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{2,2}(n)q^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{2,2}(2n)q^{2n}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{2,2}(2n+1)q^{2n+1}

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\Delta_{2,2}(n)q^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{2,2}(2n)q^{2n}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{2,2}(2n+1)q^{2n+1},

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{2,2}(2n)q^{2n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{q^{2}}{1-q^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{1-q}+\frac{1}{1+q}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{2^{i}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2}}{(1-q^{2})^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{2^{i}}}.

Replacing
q
by
q^{1/2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{2,2}(2n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{(1-q)^{2}}\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{2^{i}}}=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{1,2}(n)q^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{1,2}(n+1)q^{n}.

In the last equality we used
b_{1,2}(0)=0
.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{2,2}(2n+1)q^{2n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{3}}{(1-q^{2})^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{2^{i}}}.

Dividing by
q
and replacing
q
by
q^{1/2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Delta_{2,2}(2n+1)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{1,2}(n+1)q^{n}.

∎

5.
Conjectures on the log-concavity of
e_{j}(n)
and related functions

A sequence
\{a_{n}\}
is log-concave if

a_{n}^{2}\geq a_{n-1}a_{n+1}\text{ for all }n.

In
[
2
]
, DeSalvo and Pak proved the log-concavity of the partition function
p(n)
for
n>25
. Numerical evidence suggests that the function
e_{j}p(n)
is log-concave for
j>2
, and also for
j=1,2
if
n
is sufficiently large.

Conjecture 4
.

\displaystyle e_{1}p(n)^{2}\geq e_{1}p(n-1)\cdot e_{1}p(n+1),

\displaystyle\text{ \ for }n>23;

\displaystyle e_{2}p(n)^{2}\geq e_{2}p(n-1)\cdot e_{2}p(n+1),

\displaystyle\text{ \ for }n>17;

\displaystyle e_{j}p(n)^{2}\geq e_{j}p(n-1)\cdot e_{j}p(n+1),

\displaystyle\text{ \ for }j>2,n>0.

Moreover, numerical examples suggest that the functions
F(n):=e_{j}p(n)/p(n)
and
F(n)/n
are also log-concave.

Conjecture 5
.

For
j,n>0
,

(i)

\displaystyle{\left(\frac{e_{j}p(n)}{p(n)}\right)^{2}\geq\frac{e_{j}p(n-1)}{p(n-1)}\cdot\frac{e_{j}p(n+1)}{p(n+1)}}
,

(ii)

\displaystyle{\left(\frac{e_{j}p(n)}{n\,p(n)}\right)^{2}\geq\frac{e_{j}p(n-1)}{(n-1)\,p(n-1)}\cdot\frac{e_{j}p(n+1)}{(n+1)\,p(n+1)}}
.

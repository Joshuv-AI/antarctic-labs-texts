Automorphisms and twisted forms of rings of invariants
János Kollár

Abstract

Let
k
be a field and
G\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
a finite subgroup.
Then
G
also acts on the polynomial ring
k[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and on the power series ring
k[[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]]
, which we
abbreviate as
k[{\mathbf{x}}]
(resp.
k[[{\mathbf{x}}]]
). The ring of invariants is denoted by
k[{\mathbf{x}}]^{G}
(resp.
k[[{\mathbf{x}}]]^{G}
). Our aim is to describe the automorphism groups of these rings.

Let
N(G):=N(G,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))
denote the normalizer of
G
in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
.
Then the
N(G)
-action on
k[{\mathbf{x}}]
descends to an

N(G)/G
-action on
k[{\mathbf{x}}]^{G}
.
We prove that these automorphisms of
k[{\mathbf{x}}]^{G}
and
k[[{\mathbf{x}}]]^{G}
give the largest reductive quotient/subgroup of the automorphism group.

The normalizer always contains the scalars
k^{\times}
. They act
on homogeneous polynomials as
p({\mathbf{x}})\mapsto\lambda^{\deg p}p({\mathbf{x}})
, but we get a sometimes larger
k^{\times}
-action given as

p({\mathbf{x}})\mapsto\lambda^{(\deg p)/e}p({\mathbf{x}})
, where

e
is the
\gcd
of the degrees of all homogeneous
G
-invariants.

Other automorphisms are not always easy to write down, and it turns out that, depending on
G
,
\operatorname{Aut}\bigl(k[{\mathbf{x}}]^{G}\bigr)
is sometimes infinite dimensional, and sometimes 1 dimensional.
We give criteria for both extremes, but a complete answer remains conjectural.

More generally, we consider rings of invariants by finite, geometrically reduced subgroup schemes
G\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}
over
k
.

If
\operatorname{char}k
does not divide
|G|
, then, by the Chevalley-Shephard-Todd theorem
[
Che55
]
,
k[{\mathbf{x}}]^{G}\cong k[{\mathbf{y}}]^{H}
for a suitable
H
-action, where
H
is the quotient of
G
by the subgroup generated by all
pseudo-reflections, that is, elements that fix a hyperplane pointwise.
Thus we can harmlessly assume that
G
does not contain any
pseudo-reflections. The situation is less clear if

\operatorname{char}k
divides
|G|
.

Our first result computes
\operatorname{Aut}\bigl(k[{\mathbf{x}}]^{G}\bigr)
in terms of

G\subset\operatorname{Aut}\bigl(k[{\mathbf{x}}]\bigr)
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
k
be a field and
G\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}
a finite, geometrically reduced subgroup scheme without pseudo-reflections. Assume that

(1)

either
R=k[[{\mathbf{x}}]]
,

(2)

or
R=k[{\mathbf{x}}]
,
\operatorname{char}k\nmid|G|
, and
0\in k^{n}
is the only
G
-invariant vector.

Then there is a split exact sequence

[formula]

1\to C\bigl(G,\operatorname{Aut}^{\rm u}(R)\bigr)\to\operatorname{Aut}(R^{G})\to\bigl({\bf N}(G,\mathrm{GL}_{n})/G\bigr)(k)\to 1.

Here
\operatorname{Aut}^{\rm u}(R)\subset\operatorname{Aut}(R)
denotes the subgroup of those automorphisms that stabilize the maximal ideal
m=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
and act trivially on
m/m^{2}
. It is the group of
k
-points of an infinite dimensional ind-group

[
Sha66
,
Sha81
,
FK18
]
, but the
ind-group structure plays no role for us.

C(G,*)
denotes the centralizer, the subgroup of those elements of
*
that commute with
G
.
If the origin is the only
G
-fixed point, then the maximal ideal
m
is also fixed by the normalizer

N\bigl(G,\operatorname{Aut}(R)\bigr)
, so

N\bigl(G,\operatorname{Aut}(R)\bigr)=N\bigl(G,\operatorname{Aut}(R,m)\bigr)
.
Since
\operatorname{Aut}^{\rm u}(R)
is a normal subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(R,m)
, and its intersection with
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
is the identity, the normalizer

N\bigl(G,\operatorname{Aut}^{\rm u}(R)\bigr)
equals the centralizer

C\bigl(G,\operatorname{Aut}^{\rm u}(R)\bigr)
.

On the right in (
1
.3),
{\bf N}(G,\mathrm{GL}_{n})
denotes the normalizer of
G
in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, as an algebraic group over
k
. We take the quotient by
G
, as an algebraic group, and then
the
k
-points of the quotient.
It is usually larger than

N(G,\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k))/G(k)
, but the two agree if either
k
is separably closed, or if
G
acts irreducibly, and its center is trivial; see Examples
6
–
8
.
In any case, this part is quite well understood.

On the left in (
1
.3)

C\bigl(G,\operatorname{Aut}^{\rm u}(k[[{\mathbf{x}}]])\bigr)
is always infinite dimensional, see Example
11
.
By contrast,
C\bigl(G,\operatorname{Aut}^{\rm u}(k[{\mathbf{x}}])\bigr)

is sometimes trivial.
It is infinite dimensional, whenever
G
is diagonalizable over
k
, see Example
9
. The same holds for most reducible representations by Example
10
.
However, for irreducible representations, the following could be true.

Conjecture 2
.

Let
k
be a field and
G\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}
a finite, geometrically reduced subgroup scheme that acts irreducibly over
k
. Then

C\bigl(G,\operatorname{Aut}^{\rm u}(k[{\mathbf{x}}])\bigr)=\{1\}
.

We show this in dimension 2 for any
k
in Theorem
42
,
and in dimension 3 for
k={\mathbb{C}}
in Proposition
43
.
In higher dimensions, we develop a notion of

birationally irreducible
representations (see Definition
31
) and give a series of examples in (
35
–
40
).
(Very roughly speaking,
G
is birationally irreducible if the induced action
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}_{k}
is not birational to an action on a product.)
We prove the following special case of Conjecture
2
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
G\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}
be a geometrically reduced and birationally irreducible subgroup scheme.
Then the centralizer
C\bigl(G,\operatorname{Aut}^{\rm u}(k[{\mathbf{x}}])\bigr)
is trivial.

Combining the two theorems gives the following.

Corollary 4
.

Let
k
be a field and
G\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}
a finite, birationally irreducible subgroup scheme without pseudo-reflections. Assume that
\operatorname{char}k\nmid|G|
. Then

\operatorname{Aut}\bigl(k[{\mathbf{x}}]^{G}\bigr)=\bigl({\bf N}(G,\mathrm{GL}_{n})/G\bigr)(k).\hfill\qed

The sequence (
1
.3) also allows us to determine the
twisted forms of quotient singularities, where
a
twisted form
of a
k
-algebra
A
is a
k
-algebra
A^{\prime}
, such that

{\bar{k}}\otimes_{k}A\cong{\bar{k}}\otimes_{k}A^{\prime}
.
For illustrations see Examples
51
–
52
.

Corollary 5
.

Let
k
be a perfect field with algebraic closure
{\bar{k}}\supset k
and

G\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}
a finite, geometrically reduced
k
-subgroup scheme without pseudo-reflections.
Let
N\bigl(G,\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\bar{k}})\bigr)\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\bar{k}})
be the normalizer of
G
. Then there are natural bijections between the sets

(1)

twisted forms of
k[[{\mathbf{x}}]]^{G}
(up to isomorphisms),

(2)

grading preserving twisted forms of
k[{\mathbf{x}}]^{G}
(up to isomorphisms), and

(3)

H^{1}\bigl(\operatorname{Gal}({\bar{k}}/k),N\bigl(G,\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\bar{k}})\bigr)/G({\bar{k}})\bigr)
.

The proof of Theorem
1
builds on

. A key case is computed in

1.
Examples

We give a series of examples illustrating various possibilities for

\operatorname{Aut}\bigl(k[{\mathbf{x}}]^{G}\bigr)
.

Example 6
.

The automorphism groups of the
A_{n-1}
singularities are quite interesting.
Over a field
k
, write it as
(x^{2}-dy^{2}=z^{n})\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{3}_{k}
and assume that
\operatorname{char}k\nmid 2n
.

If
d
is a square, it is equivalent to
(xy=z^{n})
.
This is the invariant ring of the
\mu_{n}
-action given by

(u,v)\mapsto(\epsilon u,\epsilon^{-1}v)
, where
\mu_{n}\subset{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
is the group of
n
th roots of unity.
For any polynomial
p(x)
we get an automorphism

[formula]

(x,y,z)\mapsto\Bigl(x,y+\frac{(z+xp(x))^{n}-z^{n}}{x},z+xp(x)\Bigr),

hence
\operatorname{Aut}(xy=z^{n})
is infinite dimensional.

If
d
is not a square, then
(x^{2}-dy^{2}=z^{n})
is the invariant ring
k[u,v]^{G}
, where
G
is the

n
-torsion subgroup of
\mathrm{SO}(u^{2}-dv^{2})
. This is not diagonalizable over
k
. The connected component of its normalizer is

[formula]

\mathrm{GO}(u^{2}-dv^{2})=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}c&s\\
ds&c\end{pmatrix}\colon c^{2}-ds^{2}\neq 0\right\}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{2}.

Adding the reflection
(u,v)\mapsto(-u,v)
gives the whole normalizer.

Using Corollary
4
and Theorem
42
, we need to compute
\mathrm{GO}(u^{2}-dv^{2})/G
. We have 2 cases.

Case
6
.3.
If
n=2r+1
, then
the reflection descends to
(x,y)\mapsto(-x,y)
, and

\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(x^{2}-dy^{2}=z^{n})\cong\mathrm{GO}(u^{2}-dv^{2})/G\cong\mathrm{GO}(x^{2}-dy^{2}),

where the quotient map is given by

N\mapsto\det^{-r}N\cdot N^{2r+1}
, with

M\in\mathrm{GO}(x^{2}-dy^{2})
acting as

\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\\
z\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}{\det}^{r}M\cdot M\cdot\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}\\
\det M\cdot z\end{pmatrix}.

Case
6
.4.
If
n=2r
, then
the reflection descends to
(x,y)\mapsto(x,-y)
, and

\operatorname{Aut}^{\circ}(x^{2}-dy^{2}=z^{n+1})\cong\mathrm{GO}(u^{2}-dv^{2})/G\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\times\mathrm{O}(x^{2}-dy^{2}),

where the quotient map is given by

N\mapsto\bigl(\det N,\det^{-r}N\cdot N^{2r}\bigr)
, with
(\lambda,M)\in{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\times\mathrm{O}(x^{2}-dy^{2})
acting as

\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\\
z\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}{\lambda}^{r}\cdot M\cdot\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}\\
\lambda\cdot z\end{pmatrix}.

In particular, the ind-group
\operatorname{Aut}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(x^{2}+y^{2}=z^{n})
is
infinite dimensional, but its real form

\operatorname{Aut}_{{\mathbb{R}}}(x^{2}+y^{2}=z^{n})
is 2-dimensional.
Similar examples are in
[
Tot07
]
.
(Note that for a finite dimensional group, the dimension is the same over
{\mathbb{R}}
and
{\mathbb{C}}
.)

Example 7
.

Let
2\rm{I}\subset\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
be the binary icosahedral group.
Assume that
\operatorname{char}k\nmid|2\rm{I}|=120
.
The ring of invariants is

k[x,y,z]/(x^{2}+y^{3}+z^{5})
, where
the degrees of
x,y,z
are
30,20,12
.

Since
\rm{I}\subset\mathrm{PGL}_{2}
is a maximal finite subgroup, its normalizer is itself. So
{\bf N}(2\rm{I},\mathrm{GL}_{2})
is the product of
2\rm{I}
and of

{\bf C}(2\rm{I},\mathrm{GL}_{2})
; the latter is the group of scalars
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
.
The diagonal matrix

\operatorname{diag}(c,c)
acts on the invariants as

\tau_{c}:(x,y,z)\mapsto\bigl(c^{30}x,c^{20}y,c^{12}z\bigr).

However
\operatorname{diag}(-1,-1)\in 2\rm{I}
, so
the automorphism group is the
k
-points of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}/\{\pm 1\}
.
As in
[
AZ13
]
we get that

\operatorname{Aut}\bigl(k[x,y,z]/(x^{2}+y^{3}+z^{5})\bigr)=\bigl\{\tau^{\prime}_{c}:(x,y,z)\mapsto(c^{15}x,c^{10}y,c^{6}z)\bigr\}\cong k^{\times}.

Example 8
.

More generally,
let
G\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}
be a finite, geometrically irreducible subgroup
whose image in
\mathrm{PGL}_{n}
is maximal among finite subgroups.
Then

{\bf C}(G,\mathrm{GL}_{n})\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
surjects onto
{\bf N}(G,\mathrm{GL}_{n})/G
, and so

{\bf N}(G,\mathrm{GL}_{n})/G\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m}/(\mbox{center of $G$})\cong{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
.

If the assumptions of Corollary
4
also hold, then

\operatorname{Aut}\bigl(k[{\mathbf{x}}]^{G}\bigr)\cong k^{\times}
.

Example 9
.

Let
A
be a commutative group scheme with a faithful representation on
k^{n}
given by characters
\chi_{i}:A\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
.
If
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{n-1}
generate the whole character group of
A
, then there are infinitely many
(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
such that
\chi_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\chi_{n-1}^{m_{n-1}}=\chi_{n}
.
Then

(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},x_{n}+cx_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{n-1}^{m_{n-1}})

commutes with
A
, and descends to an automorphism of
k[{\mathbf{x}}]^{A}
. Thus

\operatorname{Aut}\bigl(k[{\mathbf{x}}]^{A}\bigr)
is infinite dimensional.

If
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{n-1}
do not generate the whole character group, then
there is an
a\in A
such that
\bigl(\chi_{1}(a),\dots,\chi_{n}(a)\bigr)

is a pseudo-reflection.

Example 10
.

Consider 2 representations
G\to\mathrm{GL}(U)
and
G\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
.
Assume that there is a
G
-equivariant linear map
\phi:\operatorname{Sym}^{m}(U)\to V
. Let
p
be a polynomial of degree
m
on
U
. Then

(u,v)\mapsto\bigl(u,v+\phi(p(u))\bigr)

is an automorphism of
U+V
that commutes with the diagonal
G
-action.

If
G\lhook\joinrel\to\mathrm{GL}(U)
, then there are nonzero
\phi:\operatorname{Sym}^{m}(U)\to V
by

[
KT23
]
.

Example 11
.

Consider a finite subgroup
G\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}
.
Assume that there is a
G
-equivariant linear map
\phi:\operatorname{Sym}^{m}(V)\to V
for some
m\geq 2
. Let
p
be a polynomial of degree
m
on
V
. Then

v\mapsto v+\phi(p(v))

is an automorphism of
k[[{\mathbf{x}}]]
that commutes with the
G
-action. (It is usually not an automorphism of
k[{\mathbf{x}}]
.)

For any
V
there are infinitely many
\phi:\operatorname{Sym}^{m}(V)\to V
by

[
KT23
]
.

Example 12
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic 2 and consider the
{\mathbb{Z}}/2
-action

(x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2})\mapsto(x_{1},y_{1}+x_{1},x_{2},y_{2}+x_{2}).

The invariants are

u_{i}:=x_{i},\quad v_{i}:=y_{i}(y_{i}+x_{i}),\quad\mbox{and}\quad w:=x_{1}y_{2}+x_{2}y_{1}.

They satisfy the equation

w^{2}+u_{1}u_{2}w+u_{1}^{2}v_{2}+u_{2}^{2}v_{1}=0.

Consider the automorphism
\phi_{m}
that fixes
u_{1},u_{2},w
and sends

(v_{1},v_{2})\mapsto(v_{1}+u_{1}^{m},v_{2}+u_{2}^{2}u_{1}^{m-2}).

Thus

\phi_{m}\bigl(y_{1}(y_{1}+x_{1})\bigr)=y_{1}^{2}+y_{1}x_{1}+x_{1}^{m}
, and
the right had side is irreducible for
m\geq 3
. So there is no automorphism of
k[x_{1},y_{2},x_{2},y_{2}]
that sends
y_{1}(y_{1}+x_{1})
to
y_{1}^{2}+y_{1}x_{1}+x_{1}^{m}
.

Thus the assumption
\operatorname{char}k\nmid|G|
is necessary in
Theorems
1
and
25
.
It is not clear what happens for irreducible or birationally irreducible actions.

Commuting with pseudo-reflections is a strong restriction.

Example 13

(Actions with many pseudo-reflections)
.

Any automorphism
\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{n})
that commutes with a pseudo-reflection fixes the invariant hyperplane. If there are
n
such linearly independent hyperplanes
(x_{i}=0)
, then
\phi
is an automorphism of

{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}\cong{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\setminus(x_{1}\cdots x_{n}=0)
. Note that the units on

{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}
are the monomials in the
x_{i}
, so
\operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n})
maps monomials to monomials.
The only ones that extend to
\operatorname{Aut}({\mathbb{A}}^{n})
are

(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto(c_{1}x_{1},\dots,c_{n}x_{n})
, where
c_{i}\in k^{\times}
.

If there are
n-1
linearly independent hyperplanes
(x_{i}=0)
for

i=1,\dots,n-1
, then
\phi
is an automorphism of

{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\cong{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\setminus(x_{1}\cdots x_{n-1}=0)
.
Since any polynomial map
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
is constant, we see that

\phi
is of the form

(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto\bigl(c_{1}x_{1},\dots,c_{n-1}x_{n-1},c_{n}x_{n}+p(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})\bigr),

where
c_{i}\in k^{\times}
and
p\in k[x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1}]
.

By contrast, many automorphisms are the identity on a codimension
\geq 2
subset.

Example 14
.

Let
Z\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
be a closed subset of dimension
\leq n-2
.
Let

\pi:(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})

be the projection and
W\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{n-1}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})
the closure of the image of
Z
. Let
p(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})
be a polynomial that vanishes on
W
. Then

\pi:(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto\bigl(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},x_{n}+p(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})\bigr)

is an automorphism of
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
that is the identity on
Z
. They generate an infinite dimensional subgroup of
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
that is transitive on
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\setminus Z
.

2.
Lifting of automorphisms

For the proof of Theorem
1
, it is better to use geometric language. Thus
let
\pi:Y\to X
be morphism of
k
-schemes. We aim to understand which
automorphisms of
X
lift to automorphisms of
Y
.

15
Basic set-up.

Let
\pi:Y\to X
be a finite, dominant morphism of geometrically irreducible and geometrically normal
k
-schemes.
Let
Y^{\circ}\subset Y
,
X^{\circ}\subset X
be nonsingular, dense, open subsets such that the restriction

\pi^{\circ}:Y^{\circ}\to X^{\circ}
is a finite and étale morphism.

We study situations when one of the following assumptions holds.

(1)

Y^{\circ}_{\bar{k}}
is simply connected.

(2)

Every étale cover of
Y^{\circ}_{\bar{k}}
of degree
\leq\deg\pi

is trivial.

(3)

Every Galois étale cover of
Y^{\circ}_{\bar{k}}
of degree dividing
\deg\pi

is trivial, and
\pi
is Galois.

Our main interest is in
Y={\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{k}:=\operatorname{Spec}_{k}k[{\mathbf{x}}]
or
Y=\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{k}:=\operatorname{Spec}_{k}k[[{\mathbf{x}}]]
, and (1–3) are dictated by what holds for
Y^{\circ}:=Y\setminus Z
, where
Z
is a closed subset of dimension
\leq n-2
. Then
Y^{\circ}
satisfies one of (1–3) in the following cases.

(4)

If
Y={\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{k}
and
\operatorname{char}k=0
, then (1) holds.

(5)

If
Y=\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{k}
and
\operatorname{char}k
arbitrary, then (1) holds.

(6)

If
\operatorname{char}k=p>0
and
\deg\pi<p
, then (2) holds.

(7)

\operatorname{char}k=p>0
,
\pi
is Galois and
p\nmid\deg\pi
, then (3) holds.

To see this, note that if
Y
is regular and

\dim Z\leq\dim Y-2
, then

\pi_{1}(Y\setminus Z)=\pi_{1}(Y)
by
Nagata’s purity theorem (cf.
[
Sta22
,
Tag 0BMB
]
).

Next
\pi_{1}(\hat{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{k})=\{1\}
over any separably closed field
(see, for example,
[
Sta22
,
Tag 04GE
]
).
The Hurwitz formula shows that

{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{\bar{k}}
has no étale covers that are tamely ramified at infinity.
Restricting a cover of
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}_{\bar{k}}
to a general line through the origin gives the rest our claims.

The following obvious lifting result applies
only to separably closed base fields.

Lemma 16
.

Using the notation of (
15
), assume that

Y^{\circ}
is simply connected. Then every automorphisms of
X
lifts to an automorphism of
Y
.

Proof. For
\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)
, let
\tilde{Y}
denote the normalization of the fiber product of

\pi:Y\to X
and
\phi\circ\pi:Y\to X
.
The first projection
\sigma:\tilde{Y}\to Y
is étale over
Y^{\circ}
, hence

\tilde{Y}
is a disjoint union of copies of
Y
since
Y^{\circ}
is simply connected. Each of these irreducible components gives a lifting. ∎

17
Multiplicity filtration.

Let
(A,m)
be a complete local
k
-algebra. The kernel of

\operatorname{Aut}_{k}(A)\to\mathrm{GL}(m/m^{2})
is unipotent.
Thus any reducive subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}_{k}(A)
is also a subgroup of

\mathrm{GL}(m/m^{2})
.

The problem is that in most cases the image of
\operatorname{Aut}_{k}(A)\to\mathrm{GL}(m/m^{2})
is much smaller than

\mathrm{GL}(m/m^{2})
, and is hard to determine.
For
A=k[[{\mathbf{x}}]]^{G}
,
the generators of the maximal ideal are the minimal generators of the ring of invariants. In most cases these are not known, we do not even have good bounds on the dimension of
m/m^{2}
.
We need to find a representation of
\operatorname{Aut}\bigl(k[[{\mathbf{x}}]]^{G}\bigr)

that relates more directly to
G\subset\mathrm{GL}_{n}
.

We understand much better
the
multiplicity filtration
of
k[[{\mathbf{x}}]]^{G}
. This is given as

[formula]

I_{r}:=k[[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]]^{G}\cap(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})^{r}.

That is,

Corollary 18

Over a number field, we give a 3-dimensional, irreducible example.

Example 44

Remark 53

Remark 58

Charmonium, B c B_{c} and X(3872) Transport at the LHC
\firstname Biaogang \lastname Wu \fnsep, \firstname Zhanduo \lastname Tang \fnsep, \firstname Min \lastname He \fnsep, \firstname Ralf \lastname Rapp \fnsep

Abstract
We deploy a kinetic-rate equation to evaluate the transport of J / ψ J/\psi , ψ ⁡ ( 2 ​ S ) \psi\left(2S\right) , B c B_{c} and X ⁡ ( 3872 ) X\left(3872\right) in ultrarelativistic heavy-ion collisions and compare their production yields to experimental data from the Large Hadron Collider. The rate equation has two main transport parameters, i.e. , the equilibrium limit and reaction rate for each state. The temperature-dependent equilibrium limits include charm- and bottom-quark fugacities based on their initial production. The reaction rates for charmonia, bottomonia and B c B_{c} rely on charm- and bottom-quark masses as well as binding energies from a thermodynamic T T -matrix approach. For the X ⁡ ( 3872 ) X\left(3872\right) particle, its internal structure information is encoded in reaction rates and initial conditions in the hadronic phase via two different scenarios: a loosely bound hadronic molecule vs. a compact diquark-antidiquark tetraquark.

Transport Approach in Thermal Medium

We focus on Pb-Pb collisions at 5 TeV, simulating the medium evolution via a cylindrically expanding fireball with a transverse flow of blastwave type
Grandchamp:2003uw
;
Zhao:2011cv
;
Du:2015wha
. In this background, we solve rate equations including dissociation and regeneration for each quarkonium state,
{\cal Q}
,

\frac{dN_{{\cal Q}}(\tau)}{d\tau}=-\Gamma(T(\tau))\left[N_{{\cal Q}}(\tau)-N_{{\cal Q}}^{\rm eq}(T(\tau))\right]\ .

(1)

This equation is governed by two transport parameters. The first one is the equilibrium limit,

N_{{\cal Q}}^{\rm eq}(T(\tau))=V_{\rm FB}d_{{\cal Q}}\gamma_{Q_{1}}\gamma_{Q_{2}}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\exp(-\sqrt{k^{2}+m_{{\cal Q}}^{2}}/T)\ ,

(2)

with the fireball volume,
V_{\rm FB}
, and the quarkonium degeneracy,
d_{{\cal Q}}
. The heavy-quark (HQ) fugacities,
\gamma_{Q_{i}}
(
Q_{i}
=
b,c
), are calculated assuming heavy-flavor conservation within the thermal statistical model,

N_{Q\bar{Q}}=\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{Q}n_{\rm{op}}V_{\rm{FB}}\frac{I_{1}(\gamma_{Q}n_{\rm{op}}V_{\rm{FB}})}{I_{0}(\gamma_{Q}n_{\rm{op}}V_{\rm{FB}})}+\gamma_{Q}^{2}n_{\rm{hid}}V_{\rm{FB}}\ ,

(3)

where
N_{Q\bar{Q}}
is the total number of charm-anticharm (or bottom-antibottom) pairs in the fireball, determined by hard production in primordial
NN
collisions at given collision centrality, and
I_{0}
and
I_{1}
are the modified Bessel functions of zeroth and first order.

The inelastic reaction rates,
\Gamma_{{\cal Q}}
, for charmonia, bottomonia and
B_{c}
are computed in quasi-free approximation

, with HQ masses,
m_{Q}
,
and binding energies taken from in-medium

\Gamma(T)=\Gamma_{0}\left(\frac{T}{T_{0}}\right)^{n}\ ,

(4)

akin to hadronic reaction rates for charmonia
Du:2015wha
and focusing on
n

Cleven:2019cre

\Gamma_{0}^{\rm mol}\simeq(400\pm 100)

4
Conclusions

We have conducted transport calculations of
J/\psi
,
\psi\left(2S\right)
,
B_{c}
and
X\left(3872\right)
production in URHICs within a well-tested kinetic rate-equation approach.
Our predictions for the
\psi\left(2S\right)
turn out to be in good agreement with recent ALICE data. For
B_{c}
production we find regeneration to dominate while pertinent
R_{\rm AA}
’s show a strong sensitivity to the (currently uncertain) input cross section from
pp
collisions.
For the
X\left(3872\right)
, we encoded different structure scenarios in a scale hierarchy of the reaction rates,
\Gamma^{\rm mol}\gtrsim T_{c}\geq\Gamma^{\rm tet}
. The resulting yields for the tetraquark scenario are 2-5 times higher than for the molecule scenario, which differs from most coalescence model predictions.

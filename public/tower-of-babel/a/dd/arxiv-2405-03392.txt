A note on adjoint reality in simple complex Lie algebras Thanks: ORCID ID: K. Gongopadhyay (0000-0003-4327-0660); C. Maity (0000-0003-0673-3953)
Krishnendu Gongopadhyay and Chandan Maity

Abstract
. Let G G be a Lie group with Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . In [ GM ] , an infinitesimal version of the notion of classical reality, namely adjoint reality, has been introduced. An element X ∈ 𝔤 X\in\mathfrak{g} is adjoint real if − X -X belongs to the adjoint orbit of X X in 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . In this paper, we investigate the adjoint real and the strongly adjoint real semisimple elements in complex simple classical Lie algebras. We also prove that every element in a complex symplectic Lie algebra is adjoint real.

1.
Introduction

In the group theoretical set-up an element
g
in a group
G
is called
real
or
reversible
if it is conjugate to
g^{-1}
in
G
. An element
g
is
strongly real
or
strongly reversible
in
G
if it is conjugate to
g^{-1}
by an involution. Classification of real and strongly real elements in a group is a problem of wide interest; see
[
OS
]
,
[
ST
]
.

Let
G
be a Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Consider the natural Ad
(G)
-representation of
G
on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

{\rm Ad}\colon G\longrightarrow{\rm GL}(\mathfrak{g})\,.

For
X\in\mathfrak{g}
,
the adjoint orbit
of
X
in
\mathfrak{g}
is defined as
\mathcal{O}_{X}:=\{{\rm Ad}(g)X\mid g\in G\}
.
If
X\in\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple, then
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is called
semisimple orbit
. Understanding the adjoint orbits in a semisimple Lie group has been an intense area of research, cf.
[
CM
]
,
[
Mc
]
. For various results related to semisimple orbits, see
[
CM
, Chapter 2]
.

In
[
GM
]
, the authors introduced the notion of
adjoint reality
which we recall now. Consider the Ad
(G)
-representation of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}
. For a linear Lie group
G
,
{\rm Ad}(g)X=gXg^{-1}
.

Definition 1.1

(
[
GM
, Definition 1.1]
)
.

An element
X\in\mathfrak{g}
is called Ad
G
-real
if
-X\in\mathcal{O}_{X}
. An Ad
G
-real element is called
strongly
Ad
G
-real
if
-X={\rm Ad}(\tau)X
for some
\tau\in G
so that
\tau^{2}={\rm Id}
.

This is an infinitesimal analogue of the reality in Lie groups.
It was shown in
[
GM
]
that the reality of the unipotent elements in a Lie group and the
{\rm Ad}_{G}
-reality of the nilpotent elements in the corresponding Lie algebras are equivalent via the exponential map. This correspondence was used to classify unipotent real elements in classical Lie groups in
[
GM
]
. However, this correspondence does not necessarily hold in general. Nevertheless, classifying the adjoint reality in Lie agebras is a problem of independent algebraic interest, and it also helps to understand the real and strongly real elements in the image of the exponential map, thus providing understanding of reality in the Lie group to a large extent. The above notion of adjoint reality has turned useful in classifying the strongly real elements in
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{H})
,
{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{D})\ltimes\mathbb{D}^{n}
for
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{R},~\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{H}
, respectively, cf.
[
GLM1
]
,
[
GLM2
]
.

With this motivation, it is a natural problem to investigate the adjoint reality for semisimple orbits. The aim of this note is to classify the adjoint real and strongly adjoint real semisimple elements in the complex simple classical Lie algebras; see Theorem
3.5
, Theorem
3.9
, Theorem
3.12
. The adjoint real nilpotent elements in these Lie algebras are classified in
[
GM
]
.

By the Jordan decomposition, every element in a semisimple Lie algebra decomposes as a unique sum of a semisimple and a unipotent element.
Thus, classifying an arbitrary adjoint real element in a Lie algebra is intimately related to such classification of semisimple and nilpotent elements. We demonstrate this for the symplectic Lie algebras, i.e. semisimple Lie algebras of type
C_{n}
. Recall that symplectic group plays an vital role in many branch of Mathematics. Thus the characterisation of adjoint real elements in the symplectic Lie algebra are fundamentally important which is done in Theorem
4.2
by using description of the centralizers.
We note here that classifying strongly real elements using this idea might require further technicalities as we have seen for the type
A_{n}
Lie algebras in
[
GLM3
]
. Other than Lie algebras of type
A_{n}
and
C_{n}
, adjoint reality for arbitrary elements in other semisimple Lie algebras are yet to be fully understood.

Given
X\in\mathfrak{g}
one defines the following subsets of
G
. The
centralizer
and the
reverser
of an element
X
in
G
are respectively defined as

{Z}_{G}(X):=\{s\in G\mid sXs^{-1}=X\},\hbox{ and \ }{R}_{G}(X):=\{r\in G\mid rXr^{-1}=-X\}.

Note that
{Z}_{G}(X)
is a subgroup but the set
{R}_{G}(X)
is a right coset of the centralizer
{Z}_{G}(X)
. Thus the reversing symmetry group or the extended centralizer
{E}_{G}(X):={Z}_{G}(X)\cup{R}_{G}(X)
is a subgroup of
G
in which
{Z}_{G}(X)
has index
1
or
2
.
The group
{E}_{G}(X)
is an extension of
{Z}_{G}(X)
of degree at most two.
In the group theoretical set-up we refer to
[
OS
, §2.1.4]
,
[
BR
]
on reversing symmetries for groups.

To find the reversing symmetric group
{E}_{G}(X)
, it is enough to construct one reversing element which is not in the centralizer.
We have explicitly constructed an element in
R_{G}(X)
for each adjoint real semisimple element. Recall that for a simply connected complex semisimple Lie group
G
, the centralizer
Z_{G}(X)
of a semisimple element
X
is connected; see
[
CM
, Theorem 2.3.3, p.28]
.
Thus the centralizer is determined by its Lie algebra which has a nice description in terms of Cartan subalgebra and certain root vectors; see
[
CM
, Lemma 2.1.2, p. 20]
.
Therefore, our construction also classifies the reverser of adjoint real semisimple elements in simple Lie groups.

3.
Adjoint reality for semisimple elements

Let
G
be a complex simple Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
H\in\mathfrak{g}
be a semisimple element, and
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra in
\mathfrak{g}
. Further we may assume
H\in\mathfrak{h}
as
{\rm Ad}(g)(H)\in\mathfrak{h}
for some
g\in G
.

3.1.
Semi-simple elements in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n}(\mathbb{C})

Let
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and
\mathfrak{h}
be the subalgebra consisting of all diagonal matrices in
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
\mathfrak{h}
is a Cartan subalgebra in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
H
be a semisimple element in
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Then
H
is
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}
-real in
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
if and only if whenever
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
H
,
-\lambda
is also an eigenvalue of
H
with the same multiplicity.

Remark 3.2
.

A semisimple element
H\in\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}
-real if and only if
H
is
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}
-real in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. The analogous statement is also true in the group theoretic sense; see
[
OS
, p. 77]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Every real semisimple element in
{\rm Lie(\,PSL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
is strongly
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm PSL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}
-real.

Proof.

Let
H\in{\rm Lie(\,PSL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
be a
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm PSL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}
-real semisimple element. We may assume
H={\rm diag}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{m},-h_{1},\ldots,-h_{m},0,\ldots,0)
. Then
\sigma:={\rm diag}({\rm J}_{m},\,\sqrt{-1}\,{\rm I}_{s})
will conjugate
H
and
-H
, where
2m+s=n
, and
{\rm J}_{n}
is as in (
2.1
).
∎

Example 3.4
.

Consider the semi-simple element
H={\rm diag}(x_{1},-x_{1})
. Let
g\in{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
so that
gH=-Hg
. Then
g
is of the form
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&b\\
c&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Hence
H
is a strongly
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
-real element in
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, and is an
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
-real element but not strongly
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})}
-real in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Note that
g\in{\rm Sp}(1,\mathbb{C})
if
bc=-1
. Similarly,
H
is a
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm Sp}(1,\mathbb{C})}
-real element but not strongly
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm Sp}(1,\mathbb{C})}
-real element in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1,\mathbb{C})
. ∎

The next result classifies strong
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}
-reality in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
; see
[
GLM3
, Proposition 2.5]
. Here we provide a detailed proof.

Theorem 3.5
.

An
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}
-real semisimple element in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is strongly
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}
-real if and only if either
0
is an eigenvalue or
n\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}
.

Proof.

Let
H\in\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}
-real semisimple element. If
0
is an eigenvalue of
H
, then using Example
3.4
it follows that
H
is strongly
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}
-real. Suppose
0
is not an eigenvalue of
H
, and
n\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}
, then
n=4m
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
and we can assume
H={\rm diag}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{2m},-h_{1},\ldots,-h_{2m})
. Then,
H
and
-H
will be conjugated by
g=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}&{\rm I}_{2m}\\
{\rm I}_{2m}&\\
\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

Next assume that
H
is strongly
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})}
-real and
0
is not an eigenvalue of
H
. We will show
n\in 4\mathbb{N}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume
H={\rm diag}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{m},-h_{1},\ldots,-h_{m})
and
gH=-Hg
for some involution. Let
e_{j}
be the standard column vector in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
1
in
j^{\rm th}
place and
0
elsewhere. For
1\leq j\leq m
,

Hge_{j}\,=\,-gHe_{j}\,=\,-gh_{j}e_{j}\,=\,-h_{j}ge_{j}.

Let
V_{j}:=\mathbb{C}e_{j}\oplus\mathbb{C}ge_{j}
and
\mathcal{C}_{j}:=\{e_{j},ge_{j}\}
. Since
g^{2}={\rm I}_{2}
,
g(V_{j})\subset V_{j}
. Then
\{e_{j},ge_{j}\mid 1\leq j\leq m\}

forms a basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2m}
.
Set
\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{C}_{1}\vee\cdots\vee\mathcal{C}_{m}
. Then the matrix
[g]_{\mathcal{C}}
is a
2\times 2
block-diagonal matrix and
\det\,[g]_{\mathcal{C}}=(-1)^{m}
. As
\det g=1
, it follows that
m\in 2\mathbb{N}
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Semi-simple elements in
\mathfrak{o}(n,\mathbb{C})
and
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}(n,\mathbb{C})

Up to conjugacy, any semisimple element in
\mathfrak{o}(n,\mathbb{C})
or
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}(n,\mathbb{C})
belongs to the following Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
, see
[
Kn
, p. 127]
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}:=\begin{cases}{\rm diag}(H_{1},\ldots,H_{m},0)&{\rm if}\,n=2m+1\\
\ {\rm diag}(H_{1},\ldots,H_{m})&{\rm if}\,n=2m\end{cases},{\rm\,where\,}H_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}0&x_{j}\\
-x_{j}&0\end{pmatrix},x_{j}\in\mathbb{C}.

(3.1)

Example 3.6
.

Consider
H=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&x\\
-x&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}(2,\mathbb{C})=\mathfrak{o}(2,\mathbb{C})
, where
x\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
g\in{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
so that
gHg^{-1}=-H
. Then
g
is of the form
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
b&-a\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.
Hence,
\det g=-a^{2}-b^{2}
, and
gg^{t}=g^{2}=(-\det g){\rm I}_{2}
. Thus, one can choose
g\in{\rm O}(2,\mathbb{C})
with
\det g=-1
. This shows that
H
is a
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm O}(n,\mathbb{C})}
-real, as well as strongly
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm O}(n,\mathbb{C})}
-real, element in
{\mathfrak{o}}(2,\mathbb{C})
but not
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm SO}(n,\mathbb{C})}
-real in
{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}}(2,\mathbb{C})
. ∎

The following result follows from the construction done in the above example.

Lemma 3.7
.

Every semisimple element in
\mathfrak{o}(n,\mathbb{C})
is
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm O}(n,\mathbb{C})}
-real.

Next we investigate strongly
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm O}(n,\mathbb{C})}
-real elements in
\mathfrak{o}(n,\mathbb{C})
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Every semisimple element in
\mathfrak{o}(n,\mathbb{C})
is strongly
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm O}(n,\mathbb{C})}
-real.

Proof.

Enough to consider the elements of
\mathfrak{h}
. For the elements in
\mathfrak{h}
one can easily construct the required involution using the Example
3.6
.
∎

Now we classify the strongly Ad
SO(n,C)
-real semisimple element in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}(n,\mathbb{C})
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
H\in\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}(n,\mathbb{C})
be a semisimple element. Then
H

is a strongly Ad
SO(n,C)
-real if and only if either
0
is an eigenvalue of
H
or
n\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}
.

Proof.

Since
H
is semisimple, there exists
\sigma\in{\rm SO}(n,\mathbb{C})
such that
{\rm Ad}(\sigma)H={\rm diag}(H_{1},\dots,H_{m},\underset{r\text{-many}}{\underbrace{0,\dots,0}})
,
r\geq 1
where
H_{j}
is as in (
3.1
).

First assume that
0
is an eigenvalue of
H
.
Let
g:={\rm diag}(\underset{m\text{-many}}{\underbrace{{\rm I}_{1,1},\dots,{\rm I}_{1,1}}},{\rm I}_{r})
, where
{\rm I}_{1,1}:={\rm diag}(1,-1)
.
Then
{\rm Ad}(\sigma^{-1}g\sigma)H=-H
. Note that
g^{2}={\rm I}_{n}
. If
\det g=1
, then we are done. Otherwise replace
g
by
{\rm diag}({\rm I}_{1,1},\dots,{\rm I}_{1,1},{\rm I}_{r-1},-1)
to get the required involution in
{\rm SO}(n,\mathbb{C})
. Next assume that
0
is not an eigenvalue, and
n\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}
. Thus
n\equiv 0\pmod{4}
, and hence up to conjugacy
H
is of the form
{\rm diag}(H_{1},\dots,H_{2k})
,
4k=n
. By choosing an involution
g
as above with
m=2k,r=0
, we have that
H
is strongly
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm SO}(n,\mathbb{C})}
-real.

Next suppose that
H
is strongly Ad
SO(n,C)
-real. Further assume that
0
is not an eigenvalue of
H
, and
H={\rm diag}(H_{1},\dots,H_{m})
where
H_{j}
is as in (
3.1
). Let
e_{j}
be the standard column vector in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then
e_{2j-1}+\sqrt{-1}e_{2j}
and
g(e_{2j-1}+\sqrt{-1}e_{2j})
are eigenvector of
H
corresponding to the eigenvalues
\sqrt{-1}x_{j}
and
-\sqrt{-1}x_{j}
, respectively.
Let
V_{j}:=\mathbb{C}(e_{2j-1}+\sqrt{-1}e_{2j})\oplus\mathbb{C}g(e_{2j-1}+\sqrt{-1}e_{2j})
, and
\mathcal{C}_{j}:=\{e_{2j-1}+\sqrt{-1}e_{2j},\,g(e_{2j-1}+\sqrt{-1}e_{2j})\}
. Since
g^{2}={\rm I_{2}}
,
g(V_{j})\subset V_{j}
. Then
\{e_{2j-1}+\sqrt{-1}e_{2j},\,g(e_{2j-1}+\sqrt{-1}e_{2j})\,\mid\,1\leq j\leq m\}
forms a basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Set
\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{C}_{1}\vee\dots\vee\mathcal{C}_{m}
. Then the matrix
[g]_{\mathcal{C}}
is a
2\times 2
block-diagonal matrix and
\det\,[g]_{\mathcal{C}}=(-1)^{m}
. As
\det g=1
, it follows that
m\in 2\mathbb{N}
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.3.
Semi-simple elements in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(n,\mathbb{C})

Recall that in Example
3.4

{\rm diag}(x,-x)
is not strongly Ad
Sp(1,C)
-real in but we can choose a conjugating involution

{\rm PSp}(1,\mathbb{C})

\mathfrak{h}:=\{{\rm diag}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{m},-h_{1},\ldots,-h_{m})\mid h_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\}

Theorem 3.10
.

Every semisimple element in
{\rm Lie\,(PSp}(n,\mathbb{C}))
is strongly
Ad

Let

Corollary 3.11

Every semisimple element in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(n,\mathbb{C})

Ad
Sp(n,C)
-real.

Theorem 3.12
.

A semisimple element in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(n,\mathbb{C})
is strongly
Ad
Sp(n,C)
-real if and only if the multiplicity of each non-zero eigenvalue is even.

Proof.

Let
H
be a strongly real semisimple element in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(n,\mathbb{C})
. We can assume
H={\rm diag}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n},-h_{1},\ldots,-h_{n})
, and
gH=-Hg
for some involution
g\in{\rm Sp}(n,\mathbb{C})
.
Suppose
h_{j}\neq 0
and multiplicity of
h_{j}
is
m
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{j}:=\{e_{j_{1}},\ldots,e_{j_{m}}\}
be an ordered basis of the eigenspace of
H
corresponding to the eigenvalue
h_{j}
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{n+j}:=\{e_{n+j_{1}},\ldots,e_{n+j_{m}}\}
is an ordered basis of the eigenspace corresponding to the eigenvalue
-h_{j}
. Let
V_{j}
be the
\mathbb{C}
-span of
\mathcal{C}_{j}\vee\mathcal{C}_{n+j}
. Then the involution
g
keeps
V_{j}
invariant and
(g|_{V_{j}})^{t}{\rm J}_{m}(g|_{V_{j}})\,={\rm J}_{m}
, where
{\rm J}_{m}
is as in (
2.1
). As
H(ge_{j_{l}})=-h_{j}ge_{j_{l}}
for
1\leq l\leq m
, we can write

\displaystyle[g]_{\mathcal{C}_{j}\vee\mathcal{C}_{n+j}}:=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}0&B\\
C&0\end{smallmatrix}\big)\,,\quad{\rm for\,some\,}B,C\in{\rm GL}_{m}(\mathbb{C})\,.

(3.2)

Since
g|_{V_{j}}
is an involution and
(g|_{V_{j}})^{t}{\rm J}_{m}(g|_{V_{j}})\,={\rm J}_{m}
, it follows that
C^{-1}=B=-B^{t}
. Hence,
m
(the multiplicity of
h_{j}
) has to be even.

Next assume that multiplicity of each non-zero eigenvalue is even. Can assume any semisimple element
H={\rm diag}(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n},-h_{1},\ldots,-h_{n})
, where
h_{2j-1}=h_{2j}
for
j=1,\ldots,n/2
. In this case to define a required involution
g
, set
B:=\begin{pmatrix}&&{\rm J}_{1}\\
&\reflectbox{$\ddots$}\\
{\rm J}_{1}\end{pmatrix}
in (
3.2
). This completes the proof.
∎

4.
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm Sp}(n,\mathbb{C})}
-reality in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(n,\mathbb{C})

The aim of this section is to prove that every element in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(n,\mathbb{C})
is adjoint real. For this, we need to recall some known construction and results, cf.
[
BCM1
]
. The structure of the centralizer of nilpotent elements
play an important role here.

Let
0\neq X\in\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(n,\mathbb{C})
be a nilpotent element. Let
\mathfrak{a}
be a
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2}
-triple in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(n,\mathbb{C})
containing
X
; see Theorem
2.1
.
Note that
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
is
\mathbb{C}
-module over
\mathfrak{a}
. By decomposing
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
as direct sum of irreducible
\mathfrak{a}
-module, let
\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{d}}:=\{d_{1},\dots,d_{s}\}
be the dimensions of irreducible
\mathfrak{a}
-modules. Let
M(d-1)
denote the sum of all
\mathbb{C}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
which are irreducible
\mathfrak{a}
-submodule of dimension
d
.
Then
M(d-1)
is the
isotypical component
of
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
containing all the irreducible submodules
of
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
with highest weight
d-1
. Let

L(d-1)\,:=\,\text{Span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{v\in M(d-1)\,\mid\,\,{\rm weight\,of}\,v\,{\rm is}\,1-d\,\}\,.

Then it follows that
M(d-1)\,=\,L(d-1)\oplus XL(d-1)\oplus\dots\oplus X^{d-1}L(d-1)
; see
[
BCM1
, Lemma A.1]
. Let
t_{d}\,:=\,\dim_{\mathbb{C}}L(d-1).
Then
\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{d}}}dt_{d}\,=\,2n
, and let
\{X^{l}v^{d}_{j}\,\mid\,0\leq l<d,\,1\leq j\leq t_{d},d\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{d}}\}
be a basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
as constructed in
[
BCM1
, Lemma A.6]
. We need to fix an ordering of the above basis.
Let
(v^{d}_{1},\,\dotsc,\,v^{d}_{t_{d}})

be an ordered
\mathbb{C}
-basis of
L(d-1)
for
d\,\in\,\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{d}}
. Then it follows that

\mathcal{B}^{l}(d)\,:=\,(X^{l}v^{d}_{1},\,\dotsc,\,X^{l}v^{d}_{t_{d}})

is an ordered
\mathbb{C}
-basis of
X^{l}L(d-1)
for
0\,\leq\,l\,\leq\,d-1
with
d\,\in\,\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{d}}
.
Define

\mathcal{B}(d)\,:=\,\mathcal{B}^{0}(d)\vee\cdots\vee\mathcal{B}^{d-1}(d)\ \forall\ d\,\in\,\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{d}}\,,\ \text{ and }\ \mathcal{B}\,:=\,\mathcal{B}(d_{1})\vee\cdots\vee\mathcal{B}(d_{s})\,.

Let
\langle\cdot,\,\cdot\rangle\,:\,\mathbb{C}^{2n}\times\mathbb{C}^{2n}\,\longrightarrow\,\mathbb{C}\
be the symplectic form given by
\langle x,\,y\rangle\,:=x^{t}{\rm J}_{n}y
.
Define another form on
L(d-1)
below, as in
[
CM
, p. 139]
,

(\cdot,\,\cdot)_{d}\,:\,L(d-1)\times L(d-1)\,\longrightarrow\,\mathbb{C}\quad;\ \ \ (v,\,u)_{d}\,:=\,\langle v\,,\,X^{d-1}u\rangle\,.

(4.1)

The following result is due to Springer-Steinberg, which describes the structure of the centralizer of the
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2}
-triple in
{\rm Sp}(n,\mathbb{C})
and
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(n,\mathbb{C})
.

Lemma 4.1

(cf.
[
CM
, Theorem 6.1.3]
,
[
BCM1
, Lemma 4.4]
)
.

The following isomorphisms hold:

(1)

If
\mathfrak{a}
is a
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2}
-triple in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(n,\mathbb{C})
, then

\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm Sp}(n,\mathbb{C})}(\mathfrak{a})=\left\{g\in{\rm SL}(\mathbb{C}^{2n})\middle|\begin{array}[]{cccc}g(X^{l}L(d-1))\,\subset\,X^{l}L(d-1),\\
\!\big[g|_{X^{l}L(d-1)}\big]_{{\mathcal{B}}^{l}(d)}=\big[g|_{L(d-1)}\big]_{{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(d)},(gx,gy)_{d}=(x,y)_{d}\\
\text{ for all }d\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{d}},~0\leq l\leq d-1,\text{ and }x,y\in L(d-1)\end{array}\right\};

here
(\cdot,\,\cdot)_{d}
is as in (
4.1
).

(2)

In particular,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{{\rm Sp}(n,\mathbb{C})}(\mathfrak{a})\,

\displaystyle\simeq\,\Big\{g\in\prod_{d\,\in\,\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{d}}}{\rm GL}(L(d-1))\,\bigm|\,\,(gx,gy)_{d}=(x,y)_{d},\,\mbox{\Large$\chi$}_{\mathbf{d}}(g)\,=\,1\Big\}

\displaystyle\simeq\,\Big\{\prod_{d\in\mathbb{O}_{\mathbf{d}}}{\rm Sp}(t_{\mathbf{d}}/2,\,\mathbb{C})_{\Delta}^{d}\,\times\,\prod_{d\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{d}}}{\rm O}(t_{\mathbf{d}},\,\mathbb{C})_{\Delta}^{d}\Big\}\,.

(4.2)

(3)

\mathfrak{z}_{{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}}(n,\mathbb{C})}(\mathfrak{a})=\left\{A\in{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}}(\mathbb{C}^{2n})\middle|\begin{array}[]{cccc}\!A(X^{l}L(d-1))\,\subset\,X^{l}L(d-1),\\
\!\!\big[A|_{X^{l}L(d-1)}\big]_{{\mathcal{B}}^{l}(d)}=\big[A|_{L(d-1)}\big]_{{\mathcal{B}}^{0}(d)},(Ax,y)_{d}+(x,Ay)_{d}=0\\
\text{ for all }d\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{d}},~0\leq l\leq d-1,\text{ and }x,y\in L(d-1)\end{array}\right\}\!.

(4)

In particular,

\mathfrak{z}_{{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}}(n,\mathbb{C})}(\mathfrak{a})\,\simeq\,\Big\{\Big(\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{O}_{\mathbf{d}}}{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}}(t_{\mathbf{d}}/2,\,\mathbb{C})_{\Delta}^{d}\Big)\,\,\,{{\bigoplus}}\,\,\Big(\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{d}}}{\mathfrak{o}}(t_{\mathbf{d}},\,\mathbb{C})_{\Delta}^{d}\Big)\Big\}\,.

Now we will characterize the
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm Sp}(n,\mathbb{C})}
-real elements in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(n,\mathbb{C})
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Every element of
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(n,\mathbb{C})
is
{\rm Ad}_{{\rm Sp}(n,\mathbb{C})}
-real.

Proof.

Let
X\in\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(n,\mathbb{C})
, and
X=X_{s}\,+\,X_{n}
be the Jordan decomposition of
X
where
X_{s}
and
X_{n}
are the semisimple and nilpotent part of
X
, respectively. In view of Corollary
3.11
and
[
GM
, Lemma 4.2]
, we may further assume that
X_{s}\neq 0,\,X_{n}\neq 0
.
We will construct below two elements
\sigma
and
\tau
in
{\rm Sp}(n,\mathbb{C})
so that

•

\sigma X_{n}\sigma^{-1}=-X_{n}
and
\sigma X_{s}\sigma^{-1}=X_{s}
,

•

\tau X_{s}\tau^{-1}=-X_{s}
and
\tau X_{n}\tau^{-1}=X_{n}
.

Then
\sigma\tau
will do the job, i.e.,
\sigma\tau X(\sigma\tau)^{-1}=-X
.

Construction of
\sigma
.
Define
\sigma\in{\rm GL}\,(\mathbb{C}^{2n})
below, as done in
[
GM
, (4.3)]
:

\displaystyle\sigma(X^{l}v^{d}_{j}):=\begin{cases}(-1)^{l}X^{l}v^{d}_{j}&\qquad\text{\ if\ }d\in\mathbb{O}_{\mathbf{d}}\,,\\
(-1)^{l}\sqrt{-1}X^{l}v^{d}_{j}&\qquad\text{\ if\ $d\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{d}}$}\,.\end{cases}

(4.3)

Note that
\sigma X_{n}=-X_{n}\sigma
, and
\langle\sigma x,\sigma y\rangle=\langle x,y\rangle
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{2n}
. This shows that
\sigma\in{\rm Sp}(n,\mathbb{C})
; cf.
[
GM
, Section 4.3]
.

The element
X_{s}
is a semisimple element and commutes with
X_{n}
, and
\mathfrak{z}_{{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}}(n,\mathbb{C})}(\mathfrak{a})
is reductive part (Levi part) of
\mathfrak{z}_{{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}}(n,\mathbb{C})}(X_{n})
; see
[
CM
, Lemma 3.7.3]
.
Thus,
X_{s}\in\mathfrak{z}_{{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}}(n,\mathbb{C})}(\mathfrak{a})
. Write
[X_{s}|_{L(d-1)}]_{\mathcal{B}^{0}_{d}}:=X_{sd}
. Then using Lemma
4.1
(
3
),
X_{s}=\oplus_{d\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{d}}}(X_{sd})_{\Delta}^{d}
.
Also
[\sigma]_{\mathcal{B}^{0}(d)}
is a scalar matrix; see
(
4.3
)
.
Thus from Lemma
4.1
(3), it follows that
\sigma X_{s}\sigma^{-1}=X_{s}
.

Construction of
\tau
.
Note that

X_{sd}\,\in\,\begin{cases}{\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}}(t_{d}/2,\,\mathbb{C})&\ {\rm for}\ \theta\in\mathbb{O}_{\mathbf{d}}\\
{\mathfrak{o}}(t_{d},\,\mathbb{C})&\ {\rm for}\ \theta\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{d}}\,.\end{cases}

In view of Corollary
3.11
and Proposition
3.8
, there exists

\tau_{d}\in\begin{cases}{\rm Sp}(t_{d}/2,\,\mathbb{C})&{\rm for}\ \theta\in\mathbb{O}_{\mathbf{d}}\\
{\rm O}(t_{d},\,\mathbb{C})&{\rm for}\ \theta\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{d}}\end{cases}

such that
\tau_{d}X_{sd}\tau_{d}^{-1}\,=\,-X_{sd}
for all
d\in\mathbb{N}_{\mathbf{d}}
. Finally set
\tau:=\mbox{\Large$\chi$}(\tau_{d})
, where
\chi
is an isomorphism in (
4.2
); see
[
BCM2
, Section 3.4]
for such isomorphism.
Then
\tau X_{s}\tau^{-1}=-X_{s}
and
\tau X_{n}\tau^{-1}=X_{n}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Arbitrary Lagrangian-Eulerian hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin methods for fluid-structure interaction Thanks: We acknowledge the partial support of this work from U.S. National Science Foundation through grant DMS-2012031.
Guosheng Fu

Abstract
. We present a novel (high-order) hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin (HDG) scheme for the fluid-structure interaction (FSI) problem. The (moving domain) incompressible Navier-Stokes equations are discretized using a divergence-free HDG scheme within the arbitrary Lagrangian-Euler (ALE) framework. The nonlinear elasticity equations are discretized using a novel HDG scheme with an H ⁡ ( curl ) H(\mathrm{curl}) -conforming velocity/displacement approximation. We further use a combination of the Nitsche’s method (for the tangential component) and the mortar method (for the normal component) to enforce the interface conditions on the fluid/structure interface. A second-order backward difference formula (BDF2) is use for the temporal discretization. Numerical results on the classical benchmark problem by Turek and Hron [ 27 ] show a good performance of our proposed method.

Introduction

Fluid-structure interaction (FSI) describes a multi-physics phenomenon that
involves the highly non-linear coupling between a deformable or moving structure
and a surrounding or internal fluid. There has been intensive interest in
numerically solving FSI problems due to its wide applications in biomedical, engineering and
architecture fields.

[
2
,
3
,
6
,
11
,
21
]
.

In this contribution, we present a novel hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin
(HDG) scheme to solve the
nonlinear FSI problem modeled by the
incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in the fluid domain and the equations for
nonlinear hyperelasticity in the structure domain.
The fluid problem is discretized on the moving domain with an arbitrary
Lagrangian-Eulerian divergence-free HDG scheme, which is similar to the
ALE
H(\mathrm{div})
-conforming HDG method
of Neunteufel and Schöberl
[
15
]
, and the structure problem is discretized using
a novel
H(\mathrm{curl})
-conforming HDG scheme, which is closely related to
the TDNNS method of Pechstein and Schöberl
[
17
]
.
The interface coupling terms are treated naturally via a combination of the
Nitsche’s technique in the tangential component and the mortar method (via
a Lagrange multiplier) in the normal component.
We then apply a second order backward difference formula (BDF2) for the temporal
discretization.
One salient feature of our method is that the fluid velocity approximation
maintains to be exactly
divergence-free throughout.

Let us make two comments on our choice of the HDG scheme for on the
first order system formulation, denoted as
L-HDG
since is it based on a
local discontinuous Galerkin formulation,
over another popular formulation for on the original second order equations,
denoted as
IP-HDG
since is is based on an interior penalty DG formulation.

(1)

L-HDG
has an advantage over
IP-HDG
in terms of the
choice of the stabilization parameter.

In the proposed first order system formulation,
the semidiscrete stability is ensured as long as the HDG stabilization parameters are
positive. We take the stabilization parameter
\alpha
to be of order
one
, in
particular, we use
\alpha=2\mu^{f}
in the fluid solver and the Nitsche coupling
term, and

\alpha=2\mu^{s}
in the structure solver, where
\mu^{f}
is the dynamic viscosity
and
\mu^{s}
is the Lamé’s second parameter.
Note that the original
H(\mathrm{div})
-conforming HDG method
[
13
]

and the Nitsche’s technique is based on a second order PDE and interior penalty, where one has to use
a
sufficiently large
stabilization/penalty parameter that depends on both the mesh size and the polynomial
degree. While it is easy to estimate the minimal stabilization parameter that
guarantees the convergence of the HDG scheme
[
13
]
for the

linear
Stokes operator, such an estimation is expected to be very hard for
the nonlinear elasticity operator, which depends on the maximum
eigenvalue of a nonlinear fourth order elasticity tensor.

(2)

For incompressible Navier-Stokes,

L-HDG
usually poses a superconvergence property (via postprocessing)
in the diffusion dominated regime, see, e.g.,
[
5
,
9
,
4
,
26
]
, and provide optimal convergence in the convection
dominated regime.
However, this superconvergence property is lost for the
original
IP-HDG
scheme.
Although the concept of
projected jumps

[
12
,
13
]
can be used for

IP-HDG
to restore
superconvergence for the Stokes problems, where polynomials of a lower degree is
used for the
hybrid
facet unknowns.
This technique might lead to accuracy loss for an (implicit) HDG discretization of the
Navier-Stokes problem in the convection-dominated regime
[
12
]
.

We note that superconvergent postprocessing is not exploited in this work.

In Section
2
, we introduce the ALE-divergence-free HDG scheme for
the moving domain incompressible Navier-Stokes equations.
In Section
3
, we introduce the
H(\mathrm{curl})
-conforming HDG scheme for
the equations for nonlinear elasticity.
We then introduce an HDG method to solve the FSI problem by combing the above
two HDG schemes in Section
4
.
Numerical results on a classical benchmark problem are presented in Section
5
.
We conclude in Section
6
.

The finite element spaces

\mathsf{tng}(\check{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f,up}):=\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{ll}$\mathsf{tng}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f})$&if $(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\hbox{\boldmath$w$}^{f})\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}>0$,\\[12.91663pt]
$\mathsf{tng}(\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f})$&if $(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\hbox{\boldmath$w$}^{f})\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}\leq 0$,\end{tabular}\right.

and the (positive) stabilization parameter
\alpha^{f}
is taken to be

\alpha^{f}=2\mu^{f}
.

The following result shows that the fluid velocity approximation is
globally divergence free.

Lemma 2.1
.

The semi-discrete scheme (
9
) produces a
globally divergence-free velocity approximation which has a
vanishing normal component on the domain boundary, i.e.,

\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\in H_{0}(\mathrm{div};\Omega_{t}^{f})
, and

\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\,\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}=0.

Proof.

To simplify notation, we suppress the superscript
t
in the following derivation.
Since functions in the finite element space
\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,f}
are transformed via the
Piola mapping, we have

\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}|_{K}=\frac{1}{J_{K}}F_{K}\left(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}\circ(\Phi_{K})^{-1}\right)

for some function
\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}\in[\mathcal{P}^{k}(\widehat{K})]^{d}
.
It is well-known
[
1
]
that
the following property holds for Poila transformations:

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f})\circ\Phi_{K}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{J_{K}}\mathrm{div}_{\widehat{\mathbf{x}}}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}),

which implies that

\displaystyle\int_{K}\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f})\,\widehat{q}\circ(\Phi_{K})^{-1}\mathrm{dx}=

\displaystyle\;\int_{\widehat{K}}\mathrm{div}_{\widehat{\mathbf{x}}}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}})\widehat{q}\;\mathrm{d\widehat{x}},

for any function
\widehat{q}
on the reference element
\widehat{K}
.
Since we have

\mathrm{div}_{\widehat{\mathbf{x}}}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}})\in\mathcal{P}^{k-1}(\widehat{K})
, by the definition of the scalar finite element space
W_{h}^{k-1,f}
,
we can take the test function
q_{h}^{f}\in W_{h}^{k-1,f}
in (
9c
) such that

q_{h}^{f}|_{K}=\mathrm{div}_{\widehat{\mathbf{x}}}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}})\circ(\Phi_{K})^{-1}
and

q_{h}^{f}=0
elsewhere, which leads to

0=\int_{K}\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f})\,\mathrm{div}_{\widehat{\mathbf{x}}}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}})\circ(\Phi_{K})^{-1}\,\mathrm{dx}=\int_{\widehat{K}}(\mathrm{div}_{\widehat{\mathbf{x}}}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}))^{2}\,\mathrm{d\widehat{x}}.

Hence,

(10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f})|_{K}=\frac{1}{J_{K}}\mathrm{div}_{\widehat{\mathbf{x}}}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}})\,\circ(\Phi_{K})^{-1}=0.

Next, let us prove normal continuity of
\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}
across
interior element boundaries.
Let
E_{ij}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{f,t}
be an interior facet which is the

l
-th facet,
E_{K_{i},l}
, of
element
K_{i}
, and the
m
-th facet,
E_{K_{j},m}
, of element
K_{j}
.
Restricting the equation (
9d
) to the facet

E_{ij}
, we have

(11)

\displaystyle\int_{E_{K_{i},l}}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}_{K_{i},l})\widehat{\tau}\circ(\Psi_{E_{ij}})^{-1}\mathrm{ds}+\int_{E_{K_{j},m}}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}_{K_{j},m})\widehat{\tau}\circ(\Psi_{E_{ij}})^{-1}\mathrm{ds}=0,\quad\forall\widehat{\tau}\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(\widehat{E}).

Let
\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{i}
and
\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{j}
be functions in

[\mathcal{P}^{k}(\widehat{K})]^{d}
such that

\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}|_{K_{i}}=\frac{1}{J_{K_{i}}}F_{K_{i}}\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{i},\quad\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}|_{K_{j}}=\frac{1}{J_{K_{j}}}F_{K_{j}}\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{j}.

A simple calculation yields

(12)

\displaystyle(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}_{K_{i},l})\circ\Phi_{K_{i}}=\;\frac{1}{J_{K_{i}}\|(F_{K_{i}})^{-T}\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}_{l}\|}\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{i}\cdot\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}_{l}\quad\text{ on }\widehat{E}_{l},

where
J_{K_{i}}\|(F_{K_{i}})^{-T}\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}_{l}\|=|E_{ij}|/|\widehat{E}_{l}|
is the ratio of facet measures.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle\int_{E_{K_{i},l}}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}_{K_{i},l})\widehat{\tau}\circ(\Psi_{E_{ij}})^{-1}\mathrm{ds}=

\displaystyle\;\int_{\widehat{E}_{l}}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{i}\cdot\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}_{l})\widehat{\tau}\circ(\widehat{\Psi}_{l})^{-1}\,\mathrm{d\widehat{s}}=\;\int_{\widehat{E}}\left(\widehat{J}_{l}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{i}\cdot\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}_{l})\circ\widehat{\Psi}_{l}\right)\widehat{\tau}\,\mathrm{d\widehat{s}}.

Substituting the above equation back to (
11
), we
get

\int_{\widehat{E}}\left(\widehat{J}_{l}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{i}\cdot\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}_{l})\circ\widehat{\Psi}_{l}+\widehat{J}_{m}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{j}\cdot\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}_{m})\circ\widehat{\Psi}_{m}\right)\widehat{\tau}\,\mathrm{d\widehat{s}}=0,\quad\forall\widehat{\tau}\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(\widehat{E}).

Since

\widehat{J}_{l}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{i}\cdot\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}_{l})\circ\widehat{\Psi}_{l}+\widehat{J}_{m}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{j}\cdot\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}_{m})\circ\widehat{\Psi}_{m}\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(\widehat{E})
, it must be
zero
.
Finally, by equation (
12
) and the fact that

\widehat{J}_{l}=|\widehat{E}_{l}|/|\widehat{E}|
, we have

(13)

\displaystyle\left((\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}_{K_{i},l})|_{K_{i}}+(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}_{K_{i},l})|_{K_{j}}\right)\circ\Psi_{E_{ij}}=\frac{|\widehat{E}|}{|\widehat{E}_{ij}|}(\widehat{J}_{l}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{i}\cdot\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}_{l})\circ\widehat{\Psi}_{l}+\widehat{J}_{m}(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{j}\cdot\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$n$}}_{m})\circ\widehat{\Psi}_{m})=0

Similarly, we can prove
\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}|_{\Gamma_{h}^{f}}=0
by
working on the equation (
9d
) on the domain boundary.
Combining the results (
10
) and (
13
), we readily get

\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\in H_{0}(\mathrm{div};\Omega)
and

\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}=0
.
This completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\;\hskip-44.85168pt+\left\langle\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$Flux$}}_{\mathrm{c}},\mathsf{tng}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f}-\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}^{f})\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}+\left\langle\rho^{f}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f})\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}),\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}},

(14c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}^{f}\left((p_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\sigma}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f}),(q_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$G$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f},\right.

\displaystyle\left.\widetilde{\tau}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}^{f})\right):=\;2\mu^{f}\left(\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f},\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}-\left(p_{h}^{f},\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}

\displaystyle\;\hskip-34.5021pt-\left\langle\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$Flux$}}_{\mathrm{v}},\;\mathsf{tng}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f}-\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}^{f})\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}-\left\langle\widetilde{\sigma}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}

\displaystyle\;\hskip-34.5021pt+2\mu^{f}\left(\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f}-\mathbf{D}_{\mathbf{x}}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}),\hbox{\boldmath$G$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}+2\mu^{f}\left\langle\mathsf{tng}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f}),\hbox{\boldmath$G$}_{h}^{f}\hbox{\boldmath$n$}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}

\displaystyle\;\hskip-34.5021pt+\left(\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},q_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}+\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$},\widetilde{\tau}_{h}^{f}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{h}^{f}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f}):=

\displaystyle\;\left(\rho^{f}\hbox{\boldmath$f$}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}.

Then the equations in (

(15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{h}^{f}(\left.\frac{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}}{\partial t}\right|_{\mathbf{x}_{0}},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f})+\mathcal{C}_{h}^{f}\left(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f};(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f}),(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}^{f})\right)\quad\quad

\displaystyle+\mathcal{A}_{h}^{f}\left((p_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\sigma}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f}),(q_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$G$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\tau}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}^{f})\right)

\displaystyle=\;\mathcal{F}_{h}^{f}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f}).

Energy stability of the semi-discrete scheme is given below.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

(p_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\sigma}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f})\in W_{h}^{k-1,f}\times\hbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}_{h}^{k,f}\times\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,f}\times\widetilde{W}_{h}^{k,f}\times\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h}^{k,f}
be the solution to the scheme (
9
).
The following energy identity holds:

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\rho^{f}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}+2\mu^{f}\left(\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}+\left\langle\gamma_{h}\left|\mathsf{tng}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f})\right|^{2},1\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}=\left(\rho^{f}\hbox{\boldmath$f$}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}},

where the parameter

\gamma_{h}:=\alpha^{f}+\rho^{f}|(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{h}^{f})\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}|

is positive.

Proof.

Taking test functions in equations (
9
)
as the trial functions, we get

(16)

\displaystyle T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}=\mathcal{F}_{h}^{f}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f}),

where

\displaystyle T_{1}=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{M}_{h}^{f}(\left.\frac{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}}{\partial t}\right|_{\mathbf{x}_{0}},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}),\quad\quad T_{2}=\;\mathcal{C}_{h}^{f}\left(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f};(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f}),(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f})\right),

\displaystyle T_{3}=

\displaystyle\;\mathcal{A}_{h}^{f}\left((p_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\sigma}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f}),(p_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\sigma}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f})\right).

Using the well-known identity

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}J_{0}^{t}=J_{0}^{t}\,\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f},

we get

\displaystyle T_{1}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(J_{0}^{t}\rho^{f}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,0}}-\frac{1}{2}\left(J_{0}^{t}(\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f})\rho^{f}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\rho^{f}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho^{f}(\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f})\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}},

Integration by parts
yields that

\displaystyle\left(\rho^{f}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f})\otimes\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho^{f}\mathrm{div}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f})\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\rho^{f}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f})\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}\;\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt=\;\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho^{f}(\mathrm{div}\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f})\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\rho^{f}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f})\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}\;\mathsf{tng}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}),\mathsf{tng}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f})\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}},

where we
used the fact that

\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\in H_{0}(\mathrm{div};\Omega_{f}^{t})
and

\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}=0

in the last step.
Combining the above identity with the definition of
T_{2}
,
and the definition
of the upwinding flux
\check{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f,up}

and simplifying the terms, we get

\displaystyle T_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho^{f}(\mathrm{div}\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f})\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\rho^{f}\left|(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f})\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}\right|\;\mathsf{tng}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f}),\mathsf{tng}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f})\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}

Simplifying the terms in
T_{3}
, we obtain

T_{3}=2\mu^{f}\left(\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}+\left\langle\alpha^{f}\,\mathsf{tng}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f}),\mathsf{tng}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f})\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}.

The equality in Theorem
2.1
is then obtained by simply
substituting the above three terms back to equation (
16
).
∎

2.4.
ALE-divergence-free HDG scheme: temporal discretization

In this subsection,
we consider the temporal discretization
for the semidiscrete scheme
(
9
).

We work on the time derivative term in (
9a
), restricting to
a single element
K^{t}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}
.
Let

\left\{\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{i}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})\right\}_{i=1}^{N}

be a set of basis functions for the space
[\mathcal{P}^{k}(\widehat{K})]^{d}
,
where

N=d{{n+d}\choose{k}}=\mathrm{dim}[\mathcal{P}^{k}(\widehat{K})]^{d}.

Then the function
\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}
, restricting to the element
K^{t}
,
can be expressed as

\left.\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\right|_{K^{t}}=\frac{1}{J_{K}^{t}}F_{K}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathsf{u}_{i}(t)\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{i}\circ(\Phi_{K}^{t})^{-1}=\frac{1}{J_{0}^{t}}F_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathsf{u}_{i}(t){\hbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{i}^{0}\circ(\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{t})^{-1},

where
\mathsf{u}_{i}:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

is the coefficient associated to the
i
-th basis, and

{\hbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{i}^{0}(\mathbf{x}_{0})=\frac{1}{J_{K}^{0}}F_{K}^{0}\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{i}\circ(\Phi_{K}^{0})^{-1}(\mathbf{x}_{0})

is the mapped (time-independent) basis on the initial element
K^{0}
.
Applying the chain rule, we get

(17)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}}{\partial t}\right|_{\mathbf{x}_{0}}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\frac{1}{J_{0}^{t}}F_{0}^{t})\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathsf{u}_{i}(t){\hbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{i}^{0}\circ(\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{t})^{-1}+(\frac{1}{J_{0}^{t}}F_{0}^{t})\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{d}{dt}\mathsf{u}_{i}(t){\hbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{i}^{0}\circ(\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{t})^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f}-(\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f})\mathbf{I}\right)\frac{1}{J_{0}^{t}}F_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathsf{u}_{i}(t){\hbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{i}^{0}\circ(\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{t})^{-1}+(\frac{1}{J_{0}^{t}}F_{0}^{t})\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{d}{dt}\mathsf{u}_{i}(t){\hbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{i}^{0}\circ(\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{t})^{-1}

where the first term in the above right hand side is due to the use
of the (time-dependent) Piola mapping; see
[
15
]
for a similar derivation.
Now time derivative only appears in the coefficients
\mathsf{u}_{i}
in the above
right hand side, which can be discretized using a standard ODE solver.
We use the second-order backward difference formula (BDF2) in this work.

To simplify notation, we denote

\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n}
as the solution
\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}
with
coefficients
\mathsf{u}_{i}
evaluated at time

t^{n}=n\Delta t
, where
\Delta t
is the time step size, i.e.,

\left.\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n}\right|_{K^{t}}=\frac{1}{J_{0}^{t}}F_{0}^{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathsf{u}_{i}(t^{n}){\hbox{\boldmath$\psi$}}_{i}^{0}\circ(\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{t})^{-1},\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

Then, the BDF2 time discretization of the term in (
17
) at time
t^{n}

is

(18)

\displaystyle D_{t}^{2}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n}:=\frac{1.5\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n}-2\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n-1}+0.5\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n-2}}{\Delta t}+\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f,n}-(\mathrm{div}_{\mathbf{x}}\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f,n})\mathbf{I}\right)\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n},

where
\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f,n}=\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f}(t^{n})
is the mesh velocity at time

t^{n}
.

The fully discrete ALE-divergence-free HDG scheme with BDF2 time stepping is
obtained from the semidiscrete scheme (
9
) by
replacing the term
\left.\frac{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}}{\partial t}(t^{n})\right|_{\mathbf{x}_{0}}
with
D_{t}^{2}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n}
in (
18
), and
evaluating all the other terms at
time level
t^{n}
:
Given
\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n-2},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n-1}\in\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,f}
at time
t^{n-2}
and

t^{n-1}
, find

(p_{h}^{f,n},\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f,n},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n},\widetilde{\sigma}_{h}^{f,n},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f,n})\in W_{h}^{k-1,f}\times\hbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}_{h}^{k,f}\times\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,f}\times\widetilde{W}_{h}^{k,f}\times\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h}^{k,f}

at time
t^{n}

with
\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f,n}|_{\Gamma_{h}^{f}}=0

such that

(19)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{h}^{f}\left(D_{t}^{2}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f}\right)+\mathcal{C}_{h}^{f}\left(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n}-\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f,n};(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f,n}),(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}^{f})\right)\quad\quad

\displaystyle+\mathcal{A}_{h}^{f}\left((p_{h}^{f,n},\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f,n},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n},\widetilde{\sigma}_{h}^{f,n},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f,n}),(q_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$G$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\tau}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}^{f})\right)

\displaystyle=\;\mathcal{F}_{h}^{f}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f}).

for all

(q_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$G$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\tau}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}^{f})\in W_{h}^{k-1,f}\times\hbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}_{h}^{k,f}\times\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,f}\times\widetilde{W}_{h}^{k,f}\times\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h}^{k,f}
with
\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}^{f}|_{\Gamma_{h}^{f}}=0
.

Remark 2.1

(Comment on fully discrete stability)
.

Unlike the static mesh case where a fully implicit BDF2 scheme leads to
unconditional energy stability, the ALE moving mesh scheme (
19
)
can be only shown to be
conditionally stable with maximum allowable time step size
\Delta t
depending
on the mesh velocity
\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f}
. We omit the proof of this result
and refer interested reader to the work
[
16
]
for a similar derivation.

Remark 2.2

(Semi-implicit convection treatment)
.

The scheme (
19
) leads to a nonlinear system due to
a fully implicit treatment of the nonlinear convection term.
A slightly cheaper method is to treat the convection term semi-implicitly
by replacing the convection velocity term
\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n}-\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f,n}

in equation (
19
)
with
the following second-order extrapolation

(2\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n-1}-\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n-2})-\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f,n}.

This leads to a linear scheme
with a similar stability property as the original nonlinear scheme (
19
).

3.
The
H(\mathrm{curl})
-conforming HDG scheme for nonlinear elasticity

In this section, we introduce the

H(\mathrm{curl})
-conforming HDG scheme
for nonlinear elasticity in the Lagrangian framework.

3.1.
The equations of elastodynamics

We consider the following nonlinear elasticity problem
with a hyperelastic material in a
Hu-Washizu formulation
[
28
]
on the fixed
reference domain
\Omega^{s}\in\mathbb{R}
:

(20a)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\hbox{\boldmath$d$}-\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{s}=

\displaystyle\;0,\quad

\displaystyle\text{in}\;\Omega^{s}\times[0,T]

(20b)

\displaystyle\rho^{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{s}-\mathrm{div}\hbox{\boldmath$P$}=

\displaystyle\;\rho^{s}\hbox{\boldmath$f$}^{s},\quad

\displaystyle\text{in}\;\Omega^{s}\times[0,T]

(20c)

\displaystyle\hbox{\boldmath$P$}-\frac{\partial\Psi(\hbox{\boldmath$F$})}{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$F$}}=

\displaystyle\;0,\quad

\displaystyle\text{in}\;\Omega^{s}\times[0,T]

(20d)

\displaystyle\hbox{\boldmath$F$}-\nabla\hbox{\boldmath$d$}-\mathbf{I}=

\displaystyle\;0,\quad

\displaystyle\text{in}\;\Omega^{s}\times[0,T]

where

d
is the structure displacement field,

\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{s}
is the structure velocity (on reference domain),

F
is the deformation gradient,

P
is
the first Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor, and

\Psi(\hbox{\boldmath$F$})
is the hyperelastic potential, where we use the following
Saint Venant-Kirchhoff model in this work

\Psi(\hbox{\boldmath$F$}):=\frac{\lambda^{s}}{2}\mathrm{tr}(\hbox{\boldmath$E$})^{2}+\mu^{s}\hbox{\boldmath$E$}:\hbox{\boldmath$E$},\quad\quad\hbox{\boldmath$E$}:=\frac{1}{2}(\hbox{\boldmath$F$}^{T}\hbox{\boldmath$F$}-\mathbf{I}),

with
\lambda^{s},\mu^{s}
being the two Lamé parameters.
In this case, we have

\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$F$}}=\hbox{\boldmath$F$}(\lambda^{s}\mathrm{tr}(\hbox{\boldmath$E$})\mathbf{I}+2\mu^{s}\hbox{\boldmath$E$}).

3.2.
Mesh and finite element spaces

3.2.1.
Mesh and mappings

We consider a similar mesh setting as that in Section
2
, except
that the structure domain and the associated mesh
do not move over time.
In particular,
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}:=\{K\}
is a conforming simplicial
triangulation of the structure
domain
\Omega^{s}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
where the element
K=\Phi_{K}(\widehat{K})
is a mapped
simplex from the reference simplex element

\widehat{K}
,

\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}:=\{\partial K:=\Phi_{K}(\partial\widehat{K})\}
is the collection of element
boundaries of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}
, and

\mathcal{E}_{h}^{s}:=\{E:=\Psi_{E}(\widehat{E})\}
is the mesh skeleton,
where
\Psi_{E}:\widehat{E}\rightarrow E

is the mapping between the reference surface element
\widehat{E}
and the
physical facet
E
.

3.2.2.
The finite element spaces

Again, we introduce both finite element spaces on the structure mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}
, and
the mesh skeleton
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{s}
:

(21a)

\displaystyle\hbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}_{h}^{k,s}:=

\displaystyle\;\{\hbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}\in[L^{2}(\Omega^{s})]^{d\times d}:\;\;\hbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}|_{K}=\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}}\circ(\Phi_{K})^{-1},\;\;\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$\sigma$}}\in[\mathcal{P}^{k}(\widehat{K})]^{d\times d},\;\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}\},

(21b)

\displaystyle\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,s}:=

\displaystyle\;\left\{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}\in H(\mathrm{curl};\Omega^{s}):\;\;\hbox{\boldmath$v$}|_{K}=F_{K}^{-T}\left(\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}\circ(\Phi_{K})^{-1}\right),\;\;\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}\in[\mathcal{P}^{k}(\widehat{K})]^{d},\;\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}\right\},

(21c)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h}^{k,s}:=

\displaystyle\;\{\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}\in[L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{h}^{s})]^{d}:\;\;\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}|_{E}=\mathrm{nrm}\left(\frac{1}{J_{E}}F_{E}\,\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}\circ(\Psi_{E})^{-1}\right),\;\;\widehat{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}\in[\mathcal{P}^{k}(\widehat{E})]^{d},\quad\forall E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{s}\},

where
\mathsf{nrm}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$})|_{E}:=(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}_{E})\hbox{\boldmath$n$}_{E}

denotes the normal component of the vector
v
on the facet
E
, whose
normal direction is
\hbox{\boldmath$n$}_{E}
.
Note that
the standard pull-back mapping is used to define the
the non-symmetric and discontinuous tensor space
\hbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}_{h}^{k,s}
,
which will be used to approximate the tensor fields
\hbox{\boldmath$F$}/\hbox{\boldmath$P$}
,
the covariant mapping is used to define the

H(\mathrm{curl})
-conforming
vector space
\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,s}
, which
will be used to approximate the structure displacement and velocity,
and the Piola mapping is used to define the hybrid space

\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h}^{k,s}
, which
will be used to approximate the normal component of
structure displacement and velocity on the mesh skeleton.

3.3.
The
H(\mathrm{curl})
-conforming HDG scheme: spatial discretization

In this subsection, we focus on the spatial discretization of the
equations (
20
) with
homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions

(22)

\displaystyle\hbox{\boldmath$d$}(\mathbf{x},t)=\hbox{\boldmath$u$}^{s}(\mathbf{x},t)=0,\quad\forall\mathbf{x}\in\partial\Omega^{s},\quad t\in[0,T].

We use polynomials of degree
k\geq 1
for all the variables.
The spatial HDG discretization reads as follows:
Find
(\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h},,\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{s})\in\hbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}_{h}^{k,s}\times\hbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}_{h}^{k,s}\times\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,s}\times\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,s}\times\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h}^{k,s}\times\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h}^{k,s}
with

\mathsf{tng}({\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h})|_{\partial\Omega^{s}}=\mathsf{nrm}(\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h})|_{\partial\Omega^{s}}=0,

such that

(23a)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h}}{\partial t}-\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{s}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}+\left\langle\frac{\partial\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h}}{\partial t}-\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{s},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}^{s}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}

\displaystyle=0,

(23b)

\displaystyle\left(\rho^{s}\frac{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s}}{\partial t},\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}+\left(\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h},\nabla\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}-\left\langle\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h}}\hbox{\boldmath$n$},\;\mathsf{nrm}(\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h})\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}

\displaystyle\;=\;\left(\rho^{s}\hbox{\boldmath$f$}^{s},\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}},

(23c)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\Psi(\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h})}{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$F$}}-\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$G$}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}

\displaystyle\;=\;0,

(23d)

\displaystyle\left(\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h}-\nabla\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h}-\mathbf{I},\hbox{\boldmath$Q$}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}+\left\langle\;\mathsf{nrm}(\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h}-\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h}),{\hbox{\boldmath$Q$}_{h}}\hbox{\boldmath$n$}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}

\displaystyle\;=\;0,

(23e)

\displaystyle\left\langle\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h}}\hbox{\boldmath$n$},\;\mathsf{nrm}(\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{h})\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}

\displaystyle\;=\;0,

for all

(\hbox{\boldmath$Q$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$G$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{s},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{h},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}^{s})\in\hbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}_{h}^{k,s}\times\hbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}_{h}^{k,s}\times\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,s}\times\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,s}\times\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h}^{k,s}\times\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h}^{k,s}
with

\mathsf{tng}({\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{h})|_{\partial\Omega^{s}}=\mathsf{nrm}(\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{h})|_{\partial\Omega^{s}}=0,

where
the numerical flux is

(23f)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h}}\hbox{\boldmath$n$}:=

\displaystyle\;\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h}\hbox{\boldmath$n$}-\alpha_{s}\,\mathsf{nrm}(\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h}-\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h}),

with the stabilization parameter taken to be
\alpha_{s}=2\mu^{s}
.

Note that equation (
23a
) implies that

(24)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h}}{\partial t}=\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s},\text{ and }\frac{\partial\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h}}{\partial t}=\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{s}.

To further simplify notation,
we introduce the following operators:

(25a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{h}^{s}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s},\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}):=

\displaystyle\;\left(\rho^{s}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s},\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}},

(25b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{h}^{s}\left((\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h}),(\hbox{\boldmath$Q$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$G$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{h})\right):=

\displaystyle\;\left(\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h},\nabla\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}-\left\langle\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h}}\hbox{\boldmath$n$},\;\mathsf{nrm}(\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}-\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{h})\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}

\displaystyle\hskip-86.25pt+\left(\frac{\partial\Psi(\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h})}{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$F$}}-\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$G$}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}+\left(\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h}-\nabla\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h}-\mathbf{I},\hbox{\boldmath$Q$}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}+\left\langle\;\mathsf{nrm}(\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h}-\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h}),{\hbox{\boldmath$Q$}_{h}}\hbox{\boldmath$n$}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}},

(25c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{h}^{s}(\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}):=

\displaystyle\;\left(\rho^{s}\hbox{\boldmath$f$}^{s},\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}.

Then the equations (
23b
)–(
23e
) can be expressed as the following
compact form:

(26)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{h}^{s}\left(\frac{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s}}{\partial t},\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}\right)+\mathcal{A}_{h}^{s}\left((\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h}),(\hbox{\boldmath$Q$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$G$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{h})\right)

\displaystyle=\;\mathcal{F}_{h}^{s}(\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}).

We have the following energy stability result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h},,\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{s})\in\hbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}_{h}^{k,s}\times\hbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}_{h}^{k,s}\times\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,s}\times\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,s}\times\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h}^{k,s}\times\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h}^{k,s}

be the solution to the scheme (
23
).
Then the following energy identity holds:

\frac{d}{dt}\mathsf{E}_{h}^{s}=\left(\rho^{f}\hbox{\boldmath$f$}^{s},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}},

where the elastic energy

(27)

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}_{h}^{s}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho^{s}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}+\left(\Psi(\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h}),1\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\alpha^{s}|\mathsf{nrm}(\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h}-\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h})|^{2},1\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}

Proof.

Taking the test function
\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}=\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s}
in (
23b
)
and
\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{h}=\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{s}
in (
23e
)
and adding, we get

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{M}_{h}^{s}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s})+\left(\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h},\nabla\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}-\left\langle\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h}}\hbox{\boldmath$n$},\;\mathsf{nrm}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s}-\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{s})\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}\;=\;\left(\rho^{s}\hbox{\boldmath$f$}^{s},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}.

Taking the test function
\hbox{\boldmath$G$}_{h}=\frac{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h}}{\partial t}
in
(
23c
), we get

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\Psi(\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h}),1\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}-\left(\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h},\frac{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h}}{\partial t}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}=0.

Taking time derivative of the equation (
23d
) and then taking
the test function
\hbox{\boldmath$Q$}_{h}=\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h}
and using
the relation (
24
), we get

\left(\frac{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h}}{\partial t}-\nabla\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s},\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}+\left\langle\;\mathsf{nrm}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s}-\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{s}),{\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h}}\hbox{\boldmath$n$}\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{s}}=0.

Adding the above equations and using the definition of the numerical flux
(
23f
), we get the
equality in Theorem
3.1
.
∎

Remark 3.1

(Relation with the TDNNS method)
.

We specifically mention that the use of an

H(\mathrm{curl})
-conforming
space
\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,s}
for
the displacement field
is inspired by the recent TDNNS method

[
17
]
, where good performance
for nonlinear elastostatics
was obersved in

[
14
]

for several numerical examples
including nearly incompressible materials and thick/thin structures.
A major difference
between the TDNNS method
[
14
]

and the current HDG method (
23
) is on the choice of
the finite element spaces for the tensor fields
\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h}
and
\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h}
.
We use a simple tensor space with standard pull-back mappings from the reference
element for both tensor fields, and use the (positive) HDG stabilization term in the
numerical flux to enforce the stability of the scheme.
On the other hand, the TDNNS method
[
14
]
use
a doublly Piola mapping for the symmetric part of the stress
\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h}
, and
a doublly covariant mapping for the symmetric part of the deformation gradient

\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h}
where the stabilization parameter can be set to be zero; see more details in
[
14
]
.

The TDNNS method was known to be particularly suitable for thin structures
where anisotripic elements are needed

[
18
,
19
]
.
We expect similar results for the current
HDG scheme (
23
)
due to the use of an
H(\mathrm{curl})
-conforming displacement approximation.

Semidiscrete scheme: the

We define the following quantities
on the interface
\Gamma_{0}

\overline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s}}:=\mathsf{tng}(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s})+\mathsf{nrm}(\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{s}),\quad\overline{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h}}:=\mathsf{tng}(\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h})+\mathsf{nrm}(\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h}),\quad\overline{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}}:=\mathsf{tng}(\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h})+\mathsf{nrm}(\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{h}).

We then define the following coupling term on the deformed interface
\Gamma_{t}

(33)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{h}\left((\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\sigma}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{s}),(\hbox{\boldmath$G$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\tau}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{h})\right):=

\displaystyle\;\left\langle(\overline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s}}\circ\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{t})\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}^{f},\widetilde{\tau}_{h}^{f}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{t}}-\left\langle\widetilde{\sigma}_{h}^{f},(\overline{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}^{s}}\circ\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{t})\cdot\hbox{\boldmath$n$}^{f}\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{t}}

\displaystyle\hskip-103.50105pt-2\mu^{f}\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f}\hbox{\boldmath$n$}^{f},\mathsf{tng}\left(\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}^{f}-(\overline{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}^{s}}\circ\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{t})\right)\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{t}}+2\mu^{f}\left\langle\hbox{\boldmath$G$}_{h}^{f}\hbox{\boldmath$n$}^{f},\mathsf{tng}\left(\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f}-(\overline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s}}\circ\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{t})\right)\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{t}}

\displaystyle\hskip-17.25105pt+\left\langle\alpha^{f}\mathsf{tng}\left(\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f}-(\overline{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s}}\circ\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{t})\right),\mathsf{tng}\left(\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}^{f}-(\overline{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}^{s}}\circ\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{t})\right)\right\rangle_{\Gamma_{t}}

Note that in the above term, the Nitsche’s technique is used to treat the coupling condition
in the tangential direction, and the mortar method (with
\widetilde{\tau}_{h}^{f}

as the Lagrange multiplier) in the normal direction.

The semidiscrete coupled HDG scheme
for the FSI model (
8
) and (
20
) with boundary and
interface conditions (
31
) reads as follows:
Find

(p_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\sigma}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f})\in W_{h}^{k-1,f}\times\hbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}_{h}^{k,f}\times\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,f}\times\widetilde{W}_{h}^{k,f}\times\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h}^{k,f}
with
\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f}|_{\partial\Omega_{t}^{f}\backslash\Gamma_{t}}=0
,
and

(\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{s})\in\hbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}_{h}^{k,s}\times\hbox{\boldmath$\Sigma$}_{h}^{k,s}\times\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,s}\times\hbox{\boldmath$V$}_{h}^{k,s}\times\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h}^{k,s}\times\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$V$}}_{h}^{k,s}

\mathsf{tng}({\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h})|_{\partial\Omega^{s}\backslash\Gamma_{0}}=\mathsf{nrm}(\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h})|_{\partial\Omega^{s}\backslash\Gamma_{0}}=0,

such that

(34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{h}^{f}(\left.\frac{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}}{\partial t}\right|_{\mathbf{x}_{0}},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f})+\mathcal{C}_{h}^{f}\left(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f};(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f}),(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}^{f})\right)\quad\quad

\displaystyle+\mathcal{A}_{h}^{f}\left((p_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\sigma}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f}),(q_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$G$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\tau}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}^{f})\right)

\displaystyle+\mathcal{I}_{h}\left((\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\sigma}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{s}),(\hbox{\boldmath$G$}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\tau}_{h}^{f},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$v$}}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{h})\right)

\displaystyle\;=\mathcal{F}_{h}^{f}(\hbox{\boldmath$v$}_{h}^{f})+\mathcal{F}_{h}^{s}(\hbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{h}),

for all

,
and

with

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\rho^{f}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}+\mathsf{E}_{h}^{s}\right)+2\mu^{f}\left(\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f},\hbox{\boldmath$\epsilon$}_{h}^{f}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}+\left\langle\gamma_{h}\left|\mathsf{tng}\left(\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}-\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{f}\right)\right|^{2},1\right\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}}

4.3.
Fully discrete scheme: the moving mesh algorithm

Following the previous two sections, we consider a BDF2 temporal discretization.
The fully discrete scheme is obtained from the equations (
34
)
by replacing the term
\left.\frac{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f}}{\partial t}\right|_{\mathbf{x}_{0}}
by
D_{t}^{2}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n}
in (
18
), and the term

\frac{\partial\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s}}{\partial t}

by
\mathsf{D}_{t}^{2}\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s,n}
in (
28a
),
and evaluating the other terms at time level
t^{n}
,
where the structure displacements are given explicitly by the formulas
(
30
). We skip the complete formulation.

The last ingredient for an implementable scheme is the construction
of the (unknown) ALE map (
1
), which shall satisfy the boundary
condition (
32
), and, ideally, can be used to handle large mesh deformations.
Various moving mesh algorithms exist in the literature
[
29
,
25
]
.
Here we adopt the nonlinear elasticity model
with a mesh-Jacobian-based stiffening proposed in
[
25
]
.
In particular, we compute the ALE map
\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{h}^{n}\in\hbox{\boldmath$S$}_{h}^{k}

at time
t^{n}

such that it satisfies the
boundary conditions

(36a)

\displaystyle\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{h}^{n}(\mathbf{x}_{0})=

\displaystyle\;\mathbf{x}_{0}+\overline{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h}^{n}}(\mathbf{x}_{0}),

\displaystyle\quad\forall\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\Gamma_{0},\quad\quad\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{h}^{n}(\mathbf{x}_{0})=\;\mathbf{x}_{0},\quad\forall\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\partial\Omega_{0}^{f}\backslash\Gamma_{0},

and

(36b)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{J_{K}^{0}}\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{a},\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_{0}}\hbox{\boldmath$\psi$}_{h}\right)_{\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,0}}=0,\quad\forall\hbox{\boldmath$\psi$}_{h}\in\hbox{\boldmath$S$}_{h}^{k}\text{ with }\hbox{\boldmath$\psi$}_{h}|_{\partial\Omega_{0}^{f}}=0,

where
J_{K}^{0}
is the Jacobian determinant for the initial configuration
given in (
6
), and
following
[
25
]
, we
use the
logarithmic variation of the neo-Hookean material law

\hbox{\boldmath$P$}_{a}:=\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{a}(\lambda_{a}\mathrm{ln}J_{a}\,\hbox{\boldmath$C$}_{a}^{-1}+\mu_{a}(\mathbf{I}-\hbox{\boldmath$C$}_{a}^{-1}))

where

\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{a}=\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_{0}}\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{h},\;\;J_{a}=\mathrm{det}(\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{a}),\;\;\hbox{\boldmath$C$}_{a}=\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{a}^{T}\hbox{\boldmath$F$}_{a}.

We take the artificial Lamé parameters
\mu_{a}=1
and
\lambda_{a}=1.5

so that the Poisson ration
\nu_{a}=\frac{\lambda_{a}}{2(\lambda_{a}+\mu_{a})}=0.3
.
After the discrete ALE maps
\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{h}^{n}
are computed, we again use BDF2 to
approximate the
the mesh velocity:

(37)

\displaystyle\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{f,n}_{h}:=\frac{1.5\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{h}^{n}-2\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{h}^{n-1}+0.5\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{h}^{n-2}}{\Delta t}.

To simplify the calculation, we further decouple the computation of the ALE map (
36
) with the
HDG solver (
34
) for the field unknowns
by extrapolating the boundary data (
36a
). Hence, the geometric nonlinearity
is treated explicitly.
Moreover,
following
[
25
]
,
we solve the nonlinear equations (
36
) approximately by
only solving a linearized problem of (
36
)
around the previous state

\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{h}^{n-1}
.
The algorithm to advance one time step is sketch below.

Algorithm 1:

Given the ALE map
\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{h}^{j}
and the quantities
\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,j},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s,j},\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h}^{j},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h}^{j}

for
j=n-2
and
n-1
,
find the solution
at time
t^{n}
by the following steps:

(1)

Replace the (implicit) boundary data

\overline{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h}^{n}}

in (
36a
) by
the second-order extrapolation
2\overline{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h}^{n-1}}-\overline{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h}^{n-2}}.

Then solve a linearized version of the
eqauations (
36
) to obtain
\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{h}^{n}
.
In particular,
we linearize the equations (
36
) around the previous
state
\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{h}^{n-1}
.

(2)

Deform the fluid mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,t}=\hbox{\boldmath$\phi$}_{h}^{n}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{f,0})
, and
set the mesh velocity
\hbox{\boldmath$\omega$}_{h}^{f,n}
using equation (
37
).
Solve for the velocities

\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{f,n},\hbox{\boldmath$u$}_{h}^{s,n},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$u$}}_{h}^{s,n}

using the HDG scheme (
34
) coupled with the BDF2 time stepping.
Then update displacements
\hbox{\boldmath$d$}_{h}^{n},\widetilde{\hbox{\boldmath$d$}}_{h}^{n}

using equations (
30
).

The major computational cost of the above algorithm lies in the HDG solver in
step 2, which takes more than 90% of the overall run time.

6.
Conclusion

We have presented a novel coupled HDG scheme for the FSI problem, where the
moving domain fluid Navier-Stokes equations are solved using an
ALE-divergence-free HDG scheme, and the equations for nonlinear elasticity is
solved in the Lagrangian framework using a novel
H(\mathrm{curl})
-conforming
HDG scheme.
Numerical results on the benchmark problem of Turek and Hron
[
27
]

showed good performance of our method.
Our ongoing work consists of
(1) the construction of good preconditioners for the linearized problems,
and (2) the construction of efficient, robust and accurate partitioned
algorithms for this HDG scheme.

Abstract




Abstract

A set of cycles is called independent if no two of them have a common vertex.
Let
S_{n,2k-1}
be the complete split graph, which is the join of a clique of size
2k-1
with an independent set of size
n-2k+1
.
In 1962, Erdős and Pósa established the following edge-extremal result: for every graph
G
of order
n
which contains no
k
independent cycles, where
k\geq 2
and
n\geq 24k
, we have
e(G)\leq(2k-1)(n-k),
with equality if and only if
G\cong S_{n,2k-1}.

In this paper, we prove a spectral version of Erdős-Pósa Theorem. Let
k\geq 1
and
n\geq\frac{16(2k-1)}{\lambda^{2}}
with
\lambda=\frac{1}{120k^{2}}
. If
G
is a graph of order
n
which contains no
k
independent cycles, then
\rho(G)\leq\rho(S_{n,2k-1}),
the equality holds if and only if
G\cong S_{n,2k-1}.

This presents a new example illustration for which edge-extremal problems have spectral analogues. Finally, a related problem is proposed for further research.

Keywords:
Spectral extrema, Independent cycles, Adjacency matrix, Spectral radius

05C50; 05C35

Introduction

Given a graph
H
, a graph is said to be
H
-free
,
if it does not contain a subgraph isomorphic to
H
.
The
Turán number
of
H
, denoted by
ex(n,H),

is the maximum number of edges in an
H
-free graph of order
n.

An
H
-free graph on
n
vertices with
ex(n,H)
edges is called
an
extremal graph
for
H.

A main purpose of studying Turán numbers is that they are very useful for Ramsey theory, where the original
statements can be seen in
[
13
]
.
Turán-type problem can be traced back to Mantel’s theorem
[
31
]
in 1907,
which states that
ex(n,C_{3})=\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor
.
In 1941, Turán
[
44
]
showed that
ex(n,K_{k+1})=e(T_{n,k})
for
n\geq k+1
,
and the
k
-partite Turán graph
T_{n,k}
is the unique extremal graph for
K_{k+1}
.
Since then, determining the Turán number of a given subgraph
H
has become a classic problem in extremal graph theory.
However, it is usually very difficult to determine the exact value of
ex(n,H).

A set of subgraphs is called independent if no two of them have a common vertex.
Particularly, we denote by
kH
the graph consisting of
k
independent copies of
H
.
In 1959, Erdős and Gallai
[
15
]
determined the maximum number of edges of a graph which contains no
k
independent edges.
Furthermore, in 1962, Erdős
[
14
]
proved the maximum number of edges of a graph
which contains no
k
independent triangles for
n>400(k-1)^{2}
.
Subsequently, Moon
[
32
]
showed that Erdős’s result was still valid whenever

n>\frac{9}{2}k-\frac{1}{2}.

Meanwhile, from the perspective of generalizing Turán’s theorem,
Simonovits
[
41
]
and Moon
[
32
]
determined the Turán number of
kK_{t}
for
t\geq 3
and sufficiently large
n
.
Very recently, Chen, Lu and Yuan
[
7
]
proved the Turán number of
2K_{t}
for
t\geq 3
and
n\geq 2t.

The Turán number
ex(n,H)
is also determined for some other independent subgraphs, see for example,
ex(n,kP_{3})

[
3
,
47
]
and
ex(n,kK_{1,k})

[
25
]
.
More extremal results on independent subgraphs can be seen in
[
27
,
22
,
23
]
.

On the other hand, from the perspective of cycle subgraphs,
Füredi and Gunderson
[
19
]
determined
ex(n,C_{2k+1})
for all
k
and
n
and characterized the corresponding extremal graphs.
More precisely,
ex(n,C_{2k+1})=\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\rfloor
for
n\geq 4k-2
.
However, the exact value of
ex(n,C_{2k})
is still
open even for
k=2.
One can refer to
[
20
]
for more results on even cycles.
A variation of Turán-type problem asks what is the maximum number of edges in a graph which contains no
k
independent cycles.
In 1962, Erdős and Pósa
[
16
]
investigated this extremal problem and characterized the unique extremal graph as follows.

For
n>2k-1
, let
S_{n,2k-1}
be the join of a clique on
2k-1
vertices with an independent set of
n-2k+1
vertices.
This kind of graphs is usually called
complete split graphs
,
or
threshold graphs
.

Theorem 1.1

(​
[
16
]
)
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
n\geq 24k
.
Suppose that
G
is a graph of order
n
which contains no
k
independent cycles. Then

\displaystyle e(G)\leq(2k-1)(n-k),

the equality holds if and only if
G\cong S_{n,2k-1}.

Let
A(G)
be the
adjacency matrix
of a graph
G
and
\rho(G)
be the
spectral radius
of
G
.
By Perron-Frobenius theorem, every connected graph
G

exists a positive unit eigenvector corresponding to
\rho(G)
,
which is called the
Perron vector
of
G
.
In 1986, Wilf
[
46
]
showed that
\rho(G)\leq n(1-\frac{1}{k})
for every
n
-vertex
K_{k+1}
-free graph
G
.
Wilf’s result was sharpened by Nikiforov
[
33
]
,
who proved a spectral version of Turán’s theorem, that is,

\rho(G)\leq\rho(T_{n,k})
for every
n
-vertex
K_{k+1}
-free graph
G
,
with equality if and only if
G\cong T_{n,k}
.
Babai and Guiduli
[
1
]
established
asymptotically a Kővári-Sós-Turán bound

\rho(G)\leq\big((s-1)^{\frac{1}{t}}+o(1)\big)n^{1-\frac{1}{t}}
for every
n
-vertex
K_{s,t}
-free graph
G
(where
s\geq t\geq 2
),
then Nikiforov
[
39
]
obtained an upper bound with main term
(s-t+1)^{\frac{1}{t}}n^{1-\frac{1}{t}}
.
In view of the inequality
\rho(G)\geq\frac{2e(G)}{n}
,
Nikiforov’s result is a slight improvement of a result of Füredi
[
18
]
.
In 2010, Nikiforov
[
38
]
formally posed a spectral version of Turán-type problem.

Problem 1.1
.

For a given graph
H
,
what is the maximum spectral radius of an
n
-vertex
H
-free graph?

In the past decades, much attention has been paid to Problem
3.1
for some specific graphs
H
, see for example,
C_{4}

[
33
,
48
]
,
C_{6}

[
49
]
, odd cycles
[
34
]
,
long cycles
[
21
]
, edge-disjoint cycles
[
30
]
, paths
[
38
]
,
linear forests
[
17
,
5
]
, star forests
[
6
]
, friendship graphs
[
8
]
,
odd wheels
[
9
]
, intersecting odd cycles
[
26
]
, intersecting cliques
[
12
]
,
and minors
[
24
,
40
,
28
,
42
,
43
,
50
]
.

To obtain these results, spectral techniques have been greatly developed.
For example, counting walks
[
35
]
, deleting vertices
[
38
]
,
triangle removal lemma
[
8
]
, majorization

Using the Perron eigenvector (leading eigenvector) to deduce structural properties of spectral extremal graphs (we call this “leading-eigenvector method”),
Tait and Tobin
[
42
]
successfully solved a spectral extremal conjecture on planar graphs, which was proposed by Boots and Royle
[
2
]
,
and independently by Cao and Vince
[
4
]
.
Very recently, leading-eigenvector method was excellently developed by
Cioabă, Desai and Tait
[

,
11
]
to solve the
spectral even cycle conjecture and the spectral Erdős-Sós conjecture,
which were proposed by Nikiforov
[

]
in 2010.

Since Problem
3.1
was proposed, the following problem almost
simultaneously abstracted wide interest of scholars.

Problem 1.2
.

When does an edge-extremal problem have its spectral analogue, or particularly,
when do they have the same extremal graphs?

For many Turán-type results, researchers managed to prove their spectral analogues, for example, spectral Turán’s theorem
[
33
]
, spectral Erdős-Stone-Bollobás theorem
[
36
]
, and
spectral chromatic critical edge theorem (see Theorem 2,
[
37
]
).
Moreover, Cioabă, Desai and Tait
[
9
]
proposed the following conjecture
which extends Nikiforov’s spectral chromatic critical edge theorem.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
H
be any graph such that its edge-extremal graphs are Turán graphs plus
O(1)
edges. Then its spectral extremal family is contained in its edge-extremal family for sufficiently large
n
.

Conjecture
1.1
has just been solved by Wang, Kang and Xue (see
[
45
]
).
Inspired by Problems
3.1
-
1.2
and leading-eigenvector method, we investigate a spectral extremal problem on graphs without
k
independent cycles and establish the following theorem.
Combining Theorem
1.1
, we obtain a new example illustration of Problem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
k\geq 1
and
n\geq\frac{16(2k-1)}{\lambda^{2}}
with
\lambda=\frac{1}{120k^{2}}
. Assume that
G
is a graph of order
n
which contains no
k
independent cycles.
Then

\displaystyle\rho(G)\leq\rho(S_{n,2k-1}),

the equality holds if and only if
G\cong S_{n,2k-1}.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
In Section
3
, a related problem is mentioned for further research.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.2

For
k=1,
every graph is a forest if it does not contain
k
independent cycles.
We know that the star
S_{n,1}
attains the maximum spectral radius over all forests of
order
n
. Hence, the theorem holds trivially for
k=1
.
Next we always assume that
k\geq 2.

Let
G^{*}
be an extremal graph
with maximum spectral radius over all graphs of order
n
which contain no
k
independent cycles, and
X
be the Perron vector of
G^{\ast}.

In the following, we denote by
\rho
the spectral radius of
G^{\ast}.

Moreover, let
u^{\ast}
be a vertex of
G^{\ast}
with maximum Perron component, that is,
x_{u^{\ast}}=\max_{u\in V(G)}x_{u}.

For any vertex
v\in V(G^{*}),
we denote by
N(v)
and
N^{2}(v)
the neighborhood of
v
and the set of vertices at distance two from
v
, respectively.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
k\geq 2
and
n\geq 2k+3
, then
\rho\geq\sqrt{(2k-1)n}.

Proof.

It is obvious that the complete split graph

S_{n,2k-1}
contains no
k
independent cycles.
Hence,
\rho=\rho(G^{\ast})\geq\rho(S_{n,2k-1}).

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\rho(S_{n,2k-1})=(k-1)+\sqrt{(k-1)^{2}+(2k-1)(n-2k+1)}.

We can check that
\rho(S_{n,2k-1})\geq\sqrt{(2k-1)n}
for
k\geq 2
and
n\geq 2k+3
.
It follows that
\rho\geq\sqrt{(2k-1)n}.

∎

In this section, our key goal is to determine the exact size of a vertex subset,
which consists of vertices with large Perron components in
G^{\ast}.

To do this, we need to introduce two larger vertex subsets
R
and
R^{\prime}
.
Let
\lambda=\frac{1}{120k^{2}}

and
R=\{v\in V(G^{\ast}):x_{v}>\lambda x_{u^{\ast}}\}
.
We will present an evaluation on the size of
R
.
For convenience, we define
|R|
and
e(R)
as the numbers of vertices
and edges within
R
, respectively. For convenience, we next define
f(n,k)=(2k-1)(n-k)
in Theorem
1.1
.

Before presenting our results, we first introduce the following three formulas which will be used in our arguments.
For any two vertex subsets
A
and
B
of
V(G^{*})
, we denote by
e(A,B)

the number of edges from
A
to
B
. Then we have

\displaystyle e(A,B)=e(A,B\setminus A)+e(A,A\cap B)=e(A,B\setminus A)+2e(A\cap B)+e(A\setminus B,A\cap B).

(1)

By (
1
), we can obtain that

\displaystyle e(A,B)\leq e(A\cup B)+e(A\cap B).

(2)

Moreover, it follows from (
1
) that

\displaystyle e(A,B)\leq|A|\cdot|B|.

(3)

Lemma 2.2
.

If
k\geq 2
and
n\geq\frac{16(2k-1)}{\lambda^{2}}
,
then
|R|\leq 2\sqrt{(2k-1)n}
.

Proof.

For each
v\in R
, it follows from Lemma
2.1
and the definition of
R
that

\displaystyle\lambda\sqrt{(2k-1)n}x_{u^{\ast}}\leq\rho x_{v}=\sum\limits_{u\in N(v)}x_{u}\leq\sum\limits_{u\in N(v)\cap R}x_{u^{\ast}}+\sum\limits_{u\in N(v)\setminus R}\lambda x_{u^{\ast}}.

(4)

Summing the inequality (
4
) for all
v\in R
, we have

\displaystyle|R|\lambda\sqrt{(2k-1)n}x_{u^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{v\in R}d_{R}(v)x_{u^{\ast}}+\lambda\sum\limits_{v\in R}d_{V(G^{\ast})\setminus R}(v)x_{u^{\ast}}

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(2e(R)+\lambda e(R,V(G^{\ast})\setminus R)\big)x_{u^{\ast}}.

If
|R|\leq 2\sqrt{(2k-1)n}
, then we are done.
Otherwise,
|R|>2\sqrt{(2k-1)n}
, then
|R|>24k

as
n\geq\frac{16(2k-1)}{\lambda^{2}}
and
\lambda=\frac{1}{120k^{2}}
.
Note that
G^{*}[R]
contains no
k
independent cycles.
Thus by Theorem
1.1
,
e(R)\leq f(|R|,k)\leq(2k-1)|R|
.
Moreover, we have

e(R,V(G^{\ast})\setminus R)\leq e(G^{\ast})\leq(2k-1)n
.
Hence, by (
5
) we obtain that

\displaystyle|R|\sqrt{(2k-1)n}\leq\frac{2}{\lambda}e(R)+e(G^{\ast})\leq\frac{2}{\lambda}\Big(2k-1\Big)|R|+(2k-1)n.

(6)

Since
n\geq\frac{16(2k-1)}{\lambda^{2}}
,
we have
\frac{2}{\lambda}(2k-1)\leq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(2k-1)n}.

Combining (
6
), we obtain that
\frac{1}{2}|R|\sqrt{(2k-1)n}\leq(2k-1)n
,
which contradicts the assumption
|R|>2\sqrt{(2k-1)n}
.
∎

Lemma
2.2
tells us
|R|=O\big(\!\sqrt{kn}\big)
.
We now need to find a vertex subset
R^{\prime}
,
which consists of vertices with larger Perron components.
Moreover, we hope that
|R^{\prime}|=O(k^{3})
.
To this end, we define
R^{\prime}=\{v\in V(G^{\ast}):x_{v}>4\lambda x_{u^{\ast}}\}
.
Clearly,
R^{\prime}\subseteq R.

We first give the following lemma,
and then give an evaluation on
|R^{\prime}|
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
k\geq 2
,
n\geq\frac{16(2k-1)}{\lambda^{2}}
and

v\in V(G^{\ast})
with
d(v)\leq\frac{1}{3}\lambda n
, then

\displaystyle e\big(N(v),N^{2}(v)\cap R\big)\leq\Big(\frac{5}{3}k-1\Big)\lambda n.

Proof.

Note that
N(v)\cup\big(N^{2}(v)\cap R\big)\subseteq N(v)\cup R
.
If
|N(v)\cup R|<24k
, then

e\big(N(v),N^{2}(v)\cap R\big)<(12k)^{2},

since
N(v)
and
N^{2}(v)\cap R
are disjoint and the sum of their numbers of vertices is less than
24k.

Hence
e\big(N(v),N^{2}(v)\cap R\big)<\big(\frac{5}{3}k-1\big)\lambda n
.
Now we assume that
|N(v)\cup R|\geq 24k
.
Note that
G^{\ast}[N(v)\cup\{v\}\cup R]
is a subgraph of
G^{\ast}.

By Theorem
1.1
, we have

e\big(N(v)\cup\{v\}\cup R\big)\leq f\big(|N(v)\cup R|+1,k\big)\leq(2k-1)|N(v)\cup R|.

Consequently,

\displaystyle e\big(N(v)\cup R\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2k-1)|N(v)\cup R|-d(v)\leq(2k-2)d(v)+(2k-1)|R|

(7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\big(2k-2\big)\lambda n+(2k-1)|R|.

By Lemma
2.2
, we have
|R|\leq 2\sqrt{(2k-1)n}
. Hence,

(2k-1)|R|\leq 2(2k-1)\sqrt{(2k-1)n}\leq\frac{1}{2}\big(2k-1\big)\lambda n,

where the last inequality follows from
n\geq\frac{16(2k-1)}{\lambda^{2}}
.
Now by (
7
), we have
e\big(N(v),N^{2}(v)\cap R\big)\leq e\big(N(v)\cup R\big)\leq\big(\frac{5}{3}k-1\big)\lambda n,
as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

If
k\geq 2
and
n\geq\frac{16(2k-1)}{\lambda^{2}}
,
then
|R^{\prime}|\leq\frac{6k}{\lambda},

and
d(v)>\frac{1}{3}\lambda n
for each
v\in R^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We first show that
d(v)>\frac{1}{3}\lambda n
for each
v\in R^{\prime}
.
Suppose to the contrary that
there exists a vertex
v\in R^{\prime}
such that
d(v)\leq\frac{1}{3}\lambda n
.
By Lemma
2.1
and the definition of
R^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\rho^{2}x_{v}\geq(2k-1)nx_{v}>4(2k-1)\lambda nx_{u^{\ast}}.

(8)

Note also that

\displaystyle\rho^{2}x_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(v)x_{v}+\sum\limits_{u\in N(v)}d_{N(v)}(u)x_{u}+\sum\limits_{u\in N^{2}(v)}d_{N(v)}(u)x_{u}

(9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d(v)x_{u^{\ast}}+2e\big(N(v)\big)x_{u^{\ast}}+\sum\limits_{u\in N^{2}(v)\cap R}d_{N(v)}(u)x_{u}+\sum\limits_{u\in N^{2}(v)\setminus R}d_{N(v)}(u)x_{u}.

Note that
x_{v}>4\lambda x_{u^{\ast}}
and
\rho\geq\sqrt{(2k-1)n}
.
Then
4\lambda\sqrt{(2k-1)n}x_{u^{\ast}}<\rho x_{v}=\sum_{u\in N(v)}x_{u}\leq d(v)x_{u^{\ast}}.
This implies that
d(v)>4\lambda\sqrt{(2k-1)n}\geq 24k
.
Since
G^{\ast}[N(v)\cup\{v\}]
contains no
k
independent cycles,
by Theorem
1.1
we have

e\big(N(v)\cup\{v\}\big)\leq f\big(d(v)+1,k\big)=(2k-1)\big(d(v)+1-k\big)\leq(2k-1)d(v),

and so
e\big(N(v)\big)\leq(2k-2)d(v)
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\Big(d(v)+2e\big(N(v)\big)\Big)x_{u^{\ast}}\leq(4k-3)d(v)x_{u^{\ast}}\leq\Big(\frac{4}{3}k-1\Big)\lambda nx_{u^{\ast}},

(10)

as
d(v)\leq\frac{1}{3}\lambda n
.
According to Lemma
2.3
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{u\in N^{2}(v)\cap R}d_{N(v)}(u)x_{u}\leq e\big(N(v),N^{2}(v)\cap R\big)x_{u^{\ast}}\leq\Big(\frac{5}{3}k-1\Big)\lambda nx_{u^{\ast}}.

(11)

Furthermore, by Theorem
1.1
we have
e\big(N(v),N^{2}(v)\setminus R\big)\leq e(G^{\ast})\leq f(n,k).

According to the definition of
R
, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{u\in N^{2}(v)\setminus R}d_{N(v)}(u)x_{u}\leq e\big(N(v),N^{2}(v)\setminus R\big)\lambda x_{u^{\ast}}\leq f(n,k)\lambda x_{u^{\ast}}\leq(2k-1)\lambda nx_{u^{\ast}}.

(12)

Combining the inequalities (
9
)-(
12
), we have

\displaystyle\rho^{2}x_{v}\leq\Big(\big(\frac{4}{3}k-1\big)\lambda n+\big(\frac{5}{3}k-1\big)\lambda n+(2k-1)\lambda n\Big)x_{u^{\ast}}=(5k-3)\lambda nx_{u^{\ast}}.

(13)

Comparing (
13
) with (
8
),
we obtain that
4(2k-1)<5k-3
,
a contradiction.
Therefore,
d(v)>\frac{1}{3}\lambda n
for each
v\in R^{\prime}
.

Next, we prove
|R^{\prime}|\leq\frac{6k}{\lambda}.

If
|R^{\prime}|\leq 24k
, then we are done as
\lambda=\frac{1}{120k^{2}}
.
Assume that
|R^{\prime}|\geq 24k
.
By Theorem
1.1
,

e(R^{\prime})\leq f(|R^{\prime}|,k)\leq(2k-1)|R^{\prime}|
. Moreover,

\displaystyle(2k-1)n\geq f(n,k)\geq e(G^{\ast})=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{v\in R^{\prime}}d(v)+\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{v\in V(G^{\ast})\setminus R^{\prime}}d(v),

where
\sum_{v\in R^{\prime}}d(v)\geq\frac{1}{3}\lambda n|R^{\prime}|
and

\sum\limits_{v\in V(G^{\ast})\setminus R^{\prime}}d(v)\geq e\big(R^{\prime},V(G^{\ast})\setminus R^{\prime}\big)=\sum\limits_{v\in R^{\prime}}d(v)-2e(R^{\prime})\geq\frac{1}{3}\lambda n|R^{\prime}|-2(2k-1)|R^{\prime}|.

Combining the above three inequalities, we obtain that

(2k-1)n\geq\frac{1}{3}\lambda n|R^{\prime}|-(2k-1)|R^{\prime}|.

It follows that

|R^{\prime}|\leq\frac{2kn-n}{\frac{1}{3}\lambda n-2k+1}\leq\frac{2kn}{\frac{1}{3}\lambda n}=\frac{6k}{\lambda},

where the second inequality follows from
\lambda=\frac{1}{120k^{2}}
and
n\geq\frac{16(2k-1)}{\lambda^{2}}
.

R=\{v\in V(G^{\ast}):x_{v}>\lambda x_{u^{\ast}}\}

and
R^{\prime}=\{v\in V(G^{\ast}):x_{v}>4\lambda x_{u^{\ast}}\}
,
where
\lambda=\frac{1}{120k^{2}}
.

Let
R^{\prime\prime}=\{v\in V(G^{\ast}):x_{v}\geq\frac{1}{4k}x_{u^{\ast}}\}

Clearly,

.
Before this, we need the following three lemmas.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
n\geq\frac{16(2k-1)}{\lambda^{2}}

with both endpoints
in
R^{\prime\prime\prime}
, which contradicts Claim 1.

Combining Claim 2 and Claim 3, we conclude that

R^{\prime\prime\prime}\cup R^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}
is an independent set.
Note that
G^{\ast}
is edge-maximal
(otherwise, adding edges will increase the spectral radius).
Therefore,
R^{\prime\prime}
must be a clique and

e(R^{\prime\prime},R^{\prime\prime\prime}\cup R^{\prime\prime\prime\prime})=|R^{\prime\prime}|\cdot|R^{\prime\prime\prime}\cup R^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}|.
This implies that
G^{\ast}\cong S_{n,2k-1}
.
This completes the proof.

\Box

Divergence Scaling for Distribution Matching
Gerhard Kramer

Abstract
Distribution matchers for finite alphabets are shown to have informational divergences that grow logarithmically with the block length, generalizing a basic result for binary strings.



I
Introduction

Distribution matchers (DMs) are random number generators with a one-to-one mapping
[
1
]
.
An important application of DMs is probabilistic amplitude shaping for energy-efficient communication
[
2
]
.
The objective of this paper is to prove that block (fixed-length) DMs cannot give low informational divergence
for finite alphabets, thereby generalizing a basic result of Schulte and Geiger
[
3
]
for binary strings.
The main tool we use is a large deviation theorem of Bahadur and Ranga Rao
[
4
]
that applies more generally than the binomial bounds and identities in
[
3
]
.

This paper is organized as follows.
Sec.
II
introduces notation and a few lemmas.
Sec.
III
gives bounds on sums and establishes two further results, namely Lemma
5
that bounds the size of optimal DM codebooks and Theorem
2
that bounds a divergence for average empirical distributions.
Sec.
IV
develops the desired scaling results.
Appendixes

Preliminiaries

II-A

Basic Notation

Sets are written with calligraphic letters
\mathcal{A}
and the empty set with
\emptyset
.
The cardinality of
\mathcal{A}
is
|\mathcal{A}|
and the
n
-fold Cartesian product of
\mathcal{A}
is
\mathcal{A}^{n}
.
For sequences
f(n)
and
g(n)
,
n=1,2,\dots
, the little-o notation
f(n)=o(g(n))
means that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f(n)/g(n)=0
, see
[
5
, p. 61]
.

Random variables (RVs) are written with uppercase letters such as
\mathsf{A}
and
their realizations with lowercase letters
a
.
A probability mass function (pmf) of
\mathsf{A}
is denoted by
P_{\mathsf{A}}\in\mathcal{P}
or
Q_{\mathsf{A}}\in\mathcal{P}
,
where
\mathcal{P}
is the set of pmfs for alphabet
\mathcal{A}
.
We often discard subscripts on pmfs.
Pmfs or functions are also written as vectors, e.g., we write pmf
Q
with
the
L
-letter alphabet
\mathcal{A}=\{1,\ldots,L\}
as
Q=[Q(1),\ldots,Q(L)]
.
The uniform pmf over a set of
K
elements is denoted by
U_{K}
.

A random string
\mathsf{A}^{n}=\mathsf{A}_{1}\mathsf{A}_{2}\ldots\mathsf{A}_{n}
has realizations
a^{n}=a_{1}a_{2}\ldots a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
.
The probability of a set
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{n}
of strings with respect to
P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}
is written as

P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}(\mathcal{S})=\sum_{a^{n}\in\mathcal{S}}P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}(a^{n}).

(1)

We write
Q_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}=Q_{\mathsf{A}}^{n}
for the pmf of a string of independent and
identically distributed (iid) RVs.

The
\ell_{1}
distance between two pmfs
P
and
Q
on
\mathcal{A}
is

\displaystyle d_{1}\left(P,Q\right)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\left|P(a)-Q(a)\right|

(2)

and we have
d_{1}\left(P,Q\right)\leq 2
with equality if and only if
\supp(P)\cap\supp(Q)=\emptyset
.
Of course, one may alternatively use the variational distance
[
6
, Ch. 11.6]
.

The expectation of a real-valued function
f
of a RV
\mathsf{A}
with respect to the pmf
P
is
denoted as

\displaystyle E_{f}(P)=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[f(\mathsf{A})\right]=\sum\limits_{a\in\supp(P)}P(a)f(a)

(3)

where
\supp(P)
is the support of
P
, i.e., the set of
a\in\mathcal{A}
with
P(a)>0
.
The notation
E_{f}(P)
is useful because we usually consider
P
as the variable while
f
is fixed.
Observe that
E_{f}(P)
is linear in
P
.

The variance of
f(A)
with respect to
P
is
V_{P}\left(f(A)\right)
and the entropy of
P
is
H\left(P\right)=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[-\log_{2}P(\mathsf{A})\right]
.
The cross entropy and divergence of pmfs
P
and
Q
with common alphabet
\mathcal{A}

are the respective

\displaystyle X\left(P\|Q\right)=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[-\log_{2}Q(\mathsf{A})\right]

(4)

\displaystyle D\left(P\|Q\right)=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\log_{2}\frac{P(\mathsf{A})}{Q(\mathsf{A})}\right]

(5)

and we have
X\left(P\|Q\right)=H\left(P\right)+D\left(P\|Q\right)
.

II-B

Empirical Probability

Let
\mathcal{P}_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{P}
be the set of pmfs that have rational values with
denominator
n
, also called the
n
-types. For a string
a^{n}
, let
n_{i}=n_{i}(a^{n})

be the number of occurrences of the letter
i
for
i\in\mathcal{A}
.
The empirical pmf, or type, of
a^{n}
is

\displaystyle\pi(a^{n})=\frac{1}{n}\left[n_{1},\dots,n_{L}\right]

(6)

and
\pi(a^{n})\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
. The number of
a^{n}
with the same empirical pmf
\pi(a^{n})

is given by the multinomial

\displaystyle\binom{n}{n_{1}\;\ldots\;n_{L}}=\frac{n!}{n_{1}!\dots n_{L}!}.

(7)

Let
\bar{n}_{i}=\sum_{a^{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}(a^{n})\,n_{i}(a^{n})

be the average number of occurrences of letter
i
for a specified
P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}
.
The average empirical pmf is

\displaystyle\bar{\pi}=\sum_{a^{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}}P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}(a^{n})\,\pi(a^{n})=\frac{1}{n}[\bar{n}_{1},\dots,\bar{n}_{L}]

(8)

and for any target pmf
Q_{\mathsf{T}}
we have

\displaystyle D\left(P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}\|Q_{\mathsf{T}}^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=n\,X\left(\bar{\pi}\|Q_{\mathsf{T}}\right)-H\left(P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}\right).

(9)

III
Bounds for Sample Means

Consider the sample mean

\displaystyle\mathsf{S}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(\mathsf{A}_{i}).

(14)

We are interested in characterizing
\Pr[\mathsf{S}_{n}\leq I]
.
Observe that
\mathsf{S}_{n}=E_{f}(\pi(\mathsf{A}^{n}))
so that

\mathsf{S}_{n}\leq I
is the same as requiring
\pi(A^{n})
to lie in
the half-space
\mathcal{E}=\{P:E_{f}(P)\leq I\}
.

III-A

Deviations of the Sample Mean

We distinguish between two classes of RVs:

1.

lattice: there are constants
c_{0}
and
d>0
such that
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\{c_{0}+jd:j\text{ an integer}\}
;

2.

non-lattice.

For example, every real-valued binary RV is lattice because one may choose
d
as the absolute difference between the two alphabet values. In general, one chooses
d
as the greatest common divisor
1
1

1

The greatest common divisor of a set of real numbers is the largest
d
such that all all numbers in the set are integer multiples of
d
, e.g., the set
\{1,5/3\}
has greatest common divisor
d=1/3
.
of the differences between consecutive possible values of
\mathcal{A}
, see
[
4
, Sec. 4]
.
The following large deviation theorem is developed in
[
4
, Thm. 1]
, see also
[
7
, Ch. 3.7]
.

Theorem 1

(Bahadur and Ranga Rao)
.

Consider the iid string
\mathsf{A}^{n}
with pmf
Q^{n}
and the real-valued
function
f
with domain
\mathcal{A}
.
Let
f_{\text{min}}=\min_{a\in\supp(Q)}f(a)
and consider an
I
satisfying

\displaystyle f_{\text{min}}<I<\mathbb{E}_{Q}[f(\mathsf{A})]=E_{f}(Q)

(15)

and also
\Pr[\mathsf{S}_{n}=I]>0
if
f(\mathsf{A})
is a lattice RV. We have

\displaystyle\Pr\left[\mathsf{S}_{n}\leq I\right]=\frac{2^{-nD\left(P^{*}\|Q\right)}}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}\,b\,(1+o(1))

(16)

where
P^{*}
is given by (
13
). The values

\displaystyle b=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{\alpha}\,\frac{\tau d}{1-e^{-\tau d}},&\text{if $f(\mathsf{A})$ is a lattice RV}\\
1/\alpha,&\text{else}\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\alpha=\sqrt{V_{P^{*}}\left(\ln\frac{P^{*}(\mathsf{A})}{Q(\mathsf{A})}\right)}

(19)

satisfy
0<b<\infty
and
0<\alpha<\infty
, and
\tau
is as in Lemma
4
.
Moreover, given
I
the
b
,
\alpha
, and
\tau
are independent of
n
.
However, if
f(\mathsf{A})
is lattice and
\Pr[\mathsf{S}_{n}=I]=0
then one must replace

b
with
b_{n}=be^{-\tau d\,\theta_{n}}
where
0\leq\theta_{n}<1
. Thus, the right-hand side
of (
16
) is an upper bound on
\Pr\left[\mathsf{S}_{n}\leq I\right]
.

Corollary 1
.

Choosing
Q=U_{L}
in Theorem
1
, (
16
) becomes

\displaystyle\Pr\left[\mathsf{S}_{n}\leq I\right]=\frac{L^{-n}\,2^{nH\left(P^{*}\right)}}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}\,b\,(1+o(1))

(20)

where the value
b
is as in (
1
) and

\displaystyle\alpha=\sqrt{V_{P^{*}}\left(\ln P^{*}(\mathsf{A})\right)}.

(21)

Furthermore,
P^{*}(a)>0
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
.

III-C

Convergence of the Average Empirical Distribution

We develop a scaling result for the conditional limit theorem
[
6
, Ch. 11.6]
.
Let
p_{\rm min}^{*}
and
p_{\rm max}^{*}
be the respective minimum and maximum probabilities of
P^{*}
.
Suppose that
q_{\rm min}>0
so that
p_{\rm min}^{*}>0
by Lemma
4
.
Define the function

\displaystyle\Delta(P)

\displaystyle=D\left(P\|Q\right)-D\left(P^{*}\|Q\right).

(31)

Observe that
\Delta(P)
is bounded and convex in
P
,
and non-negative for
P\in\mathcal{E}
. We have the following scaling result.

Theorem 2
.

Consider the iid string
\mathsf{A}^{n}
with pmf
Q^{n}
.
If
I
satisfies (
15
) then we have

\displaystyle D\left(\bar{\pi}\|P^{*}\right)\leq\Delta(\bar{\pi})

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{n}\,2^{\tilde{c}+1}\,(1+o(1))

(32)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{c}=(L-1)\log_{2}\frac{p_{\rm max}^{*}\,q_{\rm max}}{p_{\rm min}^{*}\,q_{\rm min}}.

(33)

Proof.

Observe that
E_{f}(\bar{\pi})\leq I
by the linearity of expectation.
The first inequality in (
32
) thus follows by a
“Pythagorean” inequality
[
6
, Ch. 11.6]
.
The second inequality is proved in Appendix
Divergence Scaling for Distribution Matching
.
∎

IV
Divergence for Distribution Matchers

The problem of DM, as described in
[
1
,
3
]
, is to
choose a codebook
\mathcal{S}
of strings
a^{n}
so that the divergence

D\left(P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}\|Q_{\mathsf{T}}^{n}\right)
is minimized where
P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}=U_{|\mathcal{S}|}
.
Without loss of essential generality, we assume
\supp(Q_{\mathsf{T}})=\mathcal{A}

and
Q_{\mathsf{T}}\neq U_{L}
. We have
H\left(P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}\right)=\log_{2}|\mathcal{S}|
,
and the rate in bits per symbol is
R_{\text{info}}=\frac{1}{n}\log_{2}|\mathcal{S}|
.

The paper
[
1
, Prop. 3]
shows that optimal
\mathcal{S}

have all strings
a^{n}
satisfying
X\left(\pi(a^{n})\|Q_{\mathsf{T}}\right)\leq I
for some
I
, see (
23
).
The paper
[
3
]
shows that for
L=2
the divergence
D\left(P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}\|Q_{\mathsf{T}}^{n}\right)
of
the best
\mathcal{S}
scales as
\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}n
with
n
, i.e., binary
DMs cannot provide low divergence.
We prove the same result for general discrete alphabets
\mathcal{A}
by using
Lemmas
1
-
5
and Theorem
2
.

IV-A

Small
I

The case
I\leq-\log_{2}t_{\rm max}
makes sense only if equality holds.

\mathcal{S}
is thus the set of
a^{n}
having only letters with maximal probability
Q_{\mathsf{T}}(a)=t_{\rm max}
.
The positive
P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}(a^{n})
then all have the same value
1/N_{\rm max}^{n}
where

N_{\rm max}
is the number of letters
a
with probability
Q_{\mathsf{T}}(a)=q_{\rm max}
.
Moreover, we compute

\displaystyle D\left(P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}\|Q_{\mathsf{T}}^{n}\right)=n\log_{2}\frac{1}{N_{\rm max}\,t_{\rm max}}

(34)

and the divergence grows linearly with
n
since
N_{\rm max}\,t_{\rm max}<1
by our assumption that
Q_{\mathsf{T}}
is not uniform.

IV-B

Large
I

For
I\geq X\left(U_{L}\|Q_{\mathsf{T}}\right)
, the following result is the analog of Lemma 17 in
[
1
, App. B]
.

Lemma 6
.

D\left(P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}\|Q_{\mathsf{T}}^{n}\right)
scales linearly with
n
for
I>\log_{2}L
.

Proof.

We have
\log_{2}L<X\left(U_{L}\|Q_{\mathsf{T}}\right)
because
Q_{\mathsf{T}}\neq U_{L}
.
Moreover, if
\log_{2}L<I<X\left(U_{L}\|Q_{\mathsf{T}}\right)
then
Theorem
2
gives
\bar{\pi}\rightarrow P^{*}
as
n\rightarrow\infty

and thus
X\left(\bar{\pi}\|Q_{\mathsf{T}}\right)\rightarrow X\left(P^{*}\|Q_{\mathsf{T}}\right)=I

since
X\left(P^{*}\|Q_{\mathsf{T}}\right)=I
by Lemma
4
.
But
H\left(P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}\right)\leq n\log_{2}L
and thus the divergence (
9
)
grows linearly with
n
. Finally, Lemma
3
states
that
X\left(\bar{\pi}\|Q_{\mathsf{T}}\right)
is non-decreasing in
I
.
∎

IV-C

Intermediate

For

\displaystyle H\left(P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}n+nH\left(P^{*}\right)+\log_{2}\frac{b}{\sqrt{2\pi}}+o(1).

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}n-n\left[H\left(P^{*}\right)-H\left(\bar{\pi}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+nD\left(\bar{\pi}\|Q_{\mathsf{T}}\right)-\log_{2}\frac{b}{\sqrt{2\pi}}+o(1)

\displaystyle 0\leq\Delta(\bar{\pi})=H\left(P^{*}\right)-H\left(\bar{\pi}\right)<\frac{1}{n}\,2^{\tilde{c}+1}\,(1+o(1)).

\displaystyle-2^{\tilde{c}+1}-\log_{2}\frac{b}{\sqrt{2\pi}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(D\left(P_{\mathsf{A}^{n}}\|Q_{\mathsf{T}}^{n}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\log_{2}n-nD\left(\bar{\pi}\|Q_{\mathsf{T}}\right)\right)

The divergence

The next result shows that one can achieve this growth.

Theorem 3
.

Finite-alphabet block DMs that minimize the divergence

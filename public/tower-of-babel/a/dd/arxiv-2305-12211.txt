Coordinate-Update Algorithms can Efficiently Detect Infeasible Optimization Problems Journal: Journal of Mathematical Analysis and Applications
Jinhee Paeng, Jisun Park, Ernest K. Ryu

Abstract
Coordinate update/descent algorithms are widely used in large-scale optimization due to their low per-iteration cost and scalability, but their behavior on infeasible or misspecified problems has not been much studied compared to the algorithms that use full updates. For coordinate-update methods to be as widely adopted to the extent so that they can be used as engines of general-purpose solvers, it is necessary to also understand their behavior under pathological problem instances. In this work, we show that the normalized iterates of randomized coordinate-update fixed-point iterations (RC-FPI) converge to the infimal displacement vector and use this result to design an efficient infeasibility detection method. We then extend the analysis to the setup where the coordinates are defined by non-orthonormal basis using the Friedrichs angle and then apply the machinery to decentralized optimization problems.



1
Introduction

Coordinate update/descent algorithms are widely used in large-scale optimization due to their low per-iteration cost and scalability. These algorithms update only a single block of coordinates of an optimization variable per iteration in contrast to full or stochastic gradient algorithms, which update all variables every iteration. The convergence of coordinate update algorithms has been analyzed extensively, and they have been shown to achieve strong practical and theoretical performance in many large-scale machine learning and optimization problems
[
1
]
for non-pathological problem instances.

However, the behavior of coordinate update algorithms on infeasible or misspecified problems has not been analyzed, which sharply contrasts with algorithms that use full (deterministic) updates. The recent interest in building general-purpose optimization solvers with first-order algorithms has led to much work analyzing the behavior of full-update first-order algorithms on pathological problem instances so that the solvers can robustly detect such instances. For coordinate-update methods to be as widely adopted, to the extent that they can be used as engines of general-purpose solvers, it is necessary to also understand their behavior under pathological problem instances.

1.1
Summary of results, contribution, and organization

In this work, we analyze the behavior of randomized coordinate-update fixed-point iterations (RC-FPI) applied to inconsistent problem instances. Analogous to the classical results of the full-update fixed-point iterations, we show that the normalized iterate
{x^{k}}/{k}
generated by RC-FPI converges toward the infimal displacement vector, which serves as a certificate of infeasibility, in the sense of both
L^{2}
and almost sure convergence. We then bound the asymptotic bias and variance of the estimator, thereby establishing an asymptotic convergence rate. Finally, we extend the analysis to the setup where the coordinates are defined by non-orthonormal basis using the Friedrichs angle and then apply the machinery to decentralized optimization problems.

2
Preliminaries and notations

In this section, we set up notations and review known results. First, let’s clarify the underlying space. Throughout this paper, a Hilbert space refers to a real Hilbert space. The underlying space is a real Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, which is consisted of
m
real Hilbert spaces.

{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{H}}_{1}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}_{2}\oplus\dots{\mathcal{H}}_{m}.

An element
u\in{\mathcal{H}}
can be decomposed into
m
blocks as

u=\left(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m}\right),\quad u_{i}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{i},

and
u_{i}
is called the
i
th block coordinates of
u
.

The Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
has its induced norm and inner product as

\|x\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\|x_{i}\|_{i}^{2},\qquad\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\langle x_{i},y_{i}\rangle_{i},

for all
x,y\in{\mathcal{H}}
, where
\|\cdot\|_{i}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{i}
are the norm and inner product of
{\mathcal{H}}_{i}
and
x_{i},y_{i}
are
i
th block coordinates of
x,y
, respectively.

Consider a linear, bounded, self-adjoint and positive definite operator
M:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
. The
M
-norm and
M
-inner product of
{\mathcal{H}}
are defined as

\|x\|_{M}=\sqrt{\langle x,Mx\rangle},\qquad\langle x,y\rangle_{M}=\langle x,My\rangle,

which can also be a pair of norm and inner product of the space
{\mathcal{H}}
.
\|\cdot\|
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
are simply the instances of
M
-norm and
M
-inner product with
M
as an identity map. For the remark, the map
M
can be expressed as a symmetric positive definite matrix if
{\mathcal{H}}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In this case,
M
-inner product and
M
-norm are

\|x\|_{M}=\sqrt{x^{T}Mx},\qquad\langle x,y\rangle_{M}=x^{T}My.

Define the
M
-variance of a random variable
X
with the domain
{\mathcal{H}}
as

\mathrm{Var}_{M}[X]=\mathbb{E}[\|X\|^{2}_{M}]-\|\mathbb{E}[X]\|^{2}_{M}.

We develop the theory of Sections
4
and
5
with the general
M
-norm so that the theory is applicable to the applications of Section
7
.

2.1
Operators

Denote
{\mathbb{I}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
as the identity operator.
For an operator
{\mathbb{T}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
, let
\mathrm{range}\,{\mathbb{T}}
be a range of
{\mathbb{T}}
.
If
x_{\star}\in{\mathcal{H}}
is a point such that
x_{\star}={\mathbb{T}}x_{\star}
, it is called a fixed point of
{\mathbb{T}}
.

We say an operator
{\mathbb{T}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
is non-expansive with respect to
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{M}
if

\left\lVert{\mathbb{T}}x-{\mathbb{T}}y\right\rVert_{M}\leq\left\lVert x-y\right\rVert_{M},\qquad\forall x,y\in{\mathcal{H}},

and is
\theta
-averaged for
\theta\in(0,1)
if
{\mathbb{T}}=(1-\theta){\mathbb{I}}+\theta{\mathbb{C}}
for some non-expansive operator
{\mathbb{C}}
.
For notational convenience, we will refer to non-expansive operators as
\theta
-averaged operators with
\theta=1
, even though, strictly speaking,
\theta=1
means the operator is not averaged.
An operator
{\mathbb{S}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
is
({1}/{2})
-cocoercive with respect to
{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{M}}
if

\left<{\mathbb{S}}x-{\mathbb{S}}y,\,x-y\right>_{M}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x-{\mathbb{S}}y\right\rVert_{M}^{2},\qquad\forall x,y\in{\mathcal{H}}.

{\mathbb{T}}
is non-expansive if and only if
{\mathbb{S}}={\mathbb{I}}-{\mathbb{T}}
is
({1}/{2})
-cocoercive.
Also,
{\mathbb{T}}
is
\theta
-averaged for some
\theta\in(0,1)
if and only if
{\mathbb{S}}=\theta^{-1}{({\mathbb{I}}-{\mathbb{T}})}
is
({1}/{2})
-cocoercive.

2.2
Inconsistent operators and infimal displacement vector

We say an operator
{\mathbb{T}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
is consistent if it has a fixed point, and inconsistent if it does not have a fixed point.

{\mathbb{T}}
is consistent if and only if
0\in\mathrm{range}\,({\mathbb{I}}-{\mathbb{T}})
.
When
{\mathbb{T}}
is non-expansive, the closure
\overline{\mathrm{range}\,\left({\mathbb{I}}-{\mathbb{T}}\right)}
is a nonempty closed convex set, so it has a unique minimum-norm element, which we denote by
\mathbf{v}

[
3
]
.

We call
\mathbf{v}
the
infimal displacement vector
of
{\mathbb{T}}

[
113
,
21
]
.
Alternatively,
\mathbf{v}
is the projection of
0
onto
\overline{\mathrm{range}\,\left({\mathbb{I}}-{\mathbb{T}}\right)}
. Equivalently,
\mathbf{v}\in\overline{\mathrm{range}\,\left({\mathbb{I}}-{\mathbb{T}}\right)}
is the infimal displacement vector of
{\mathbb{T}}
if and only if

\left<y-\mathbf{v},\,\mathbf{v}\right>_{M}\geq 0,\qquad\forall y\in\mathrm{range}\,({\mathbb{I}}-{\mathbb{T}}).

(1)

For a convex optimization problem, let
{\mathbb{T}}
be an operator corresponding to an iterative first-order method, such as the Douglas-Rachford splitting (DRS) operator
[
18
]
, and let
\mathbf{v}
be its infimal displacement vector. Loosely speaking, if the optimization problem is feasible and the problem is well-behaved, then
\mathbf{v}=0
. (However, it is possible for “weakly infeasible” problems to have
\mathbf{v}=0
, so
\mathbf{v}=0
does not guarantee feasibility.) On the other hand,
\mathbf{v}\neq 0
implies that the problem or its dual problem is infeasible, so
\mathbf{v}\neq 0
serves as a certificate of infeasibility
[
30
,
29
]
.

2.3
Fixed point iteration and normalized iterate

The fixed-point iteration (
FPI
) with respect to an operator
{\mathbb{T}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
is defined as

x^{k+1}={\mathbb{T}}x^{k},\quad k=0,1,2,\dots,

(FPI)

where
x^{0}\in{\mathcal{H}}
is a starting point.

Let
x^{0},x^{1},x^{2},\ldots
be the iterates of (
FPI
).
We call
{x^{k}}/{k}
the
k
th
normalized iterate
of (
FPI
) for
k=1,2,\dots
. The seminal work of
Pazy 1971
characterizes the dynamics of normalized iterates of (
FPI
).

Theorem 2.1

(
Pazy 1971
)
.

Let
{\mathbb{T}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
be non-expansive.
Let
x^{0},x^{1},x^{2},\ldots
be the iterates of (
FPI
).
Then, the normalized iterate
{x^{k}}/{k}
converges strongly,

\displaystyle\frac{x^{k}}{k}\rightarrow-\mathbf{v}

as
k\to\infty
,
where
\mathbf{v}
is the infimal displacement vector of
{\mathbb{T}}
.

Since the underlying space is a Hilbert space, we clarify that the convergence in the space
{\mathcal{H}}
throughout this paper refers to the strong convergence of Hilbert space.

Randomized-coordinate update setup

In this section, we focus on a variant of (

Consider a

Define

The randomized coordinate fixed-point iteration (

x^{k+1}={\mathbb{T}}_{\mathcal{I}^{k}}x^{k},\qquad k=0,1,2,\dots,

(RC-FPI)

Throughout this paper, we assume that

is randomly sampled from a distribution on

that satisfies the

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{I}}\left[\mathcal{I}\right]=\alpha\mathbf{1}

(2)

for some

, where

is the vector with all entries equal to

.
(Note,

The uniform expected step-size condition (
2

\bar{{\mathbb{T}}}x=\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{I}}\left[{\mathbb{T}}_{\mathcal{I}}x\right],\qquad\forall x\in{\mathcal{H}}.

Equivalently,

For any

u_{\mathcal{I}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\underbrace{\mathcal{I}_{i}}_{\in\mathbb{R}}\underbrace{(0,\dots,0,u_{i},0,\dots,0)}_{\in{\mathcal{H}}},

If

Let

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{I}}\left[\left\lVert u_{\mathcal{I}}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\right]\leq\beta\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{M}^{2},\qquad\forall\,u\in{\mathcal{H}}.

(3)

Lemma 3.1
.

Consider a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
with its norm

.
If

satisfies the uniform expected step-size condition (

Proof.

Since

∎

\begin{split}U^{k}=&-\alpha\left(\theta^{-1}-\alpha\right)\left\lVert\theta{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+\theta^{2}\left(\beta-\alpha^{2}\right)\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}.\end{split}

The key idea is to bound the

terms. This can be done by showing that
\mathbf{v}
and

are almost orthogonal. Such property is exhibited in the next two lemmas.

Lemma 5.2
.

Suppose
\theta
-averaged operator
{\mathbb{T}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
has an infimal displacement vector
\mathbf{v}
.
Consider a closed cone
C_{\delta}
in
{\mathcal{H}}
with
\delta\in\left(0,{\pi}/{2}\right)
,
which is a set of vectors whose angle between them and
\mathbf{v}
being less than
{\pi}/{2}-\delta
.

C_{\delta}=\left\{x:\left<\mathbf{v},x\right>_{M}\geq\sin\delta\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{M}\right\}.

When the points
y,z\in{\mathcal{H}}
satisfy that
{\mathbb{S}}y\in{\mathbb{S}}z+C_{\delta}
and
{\mathbb{S}}y\neq{\mathbb{S}}z
, then the following inequality holds.

\left<-\mathbf{v},y-z\right>_{M}\leq\cos\delta\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}\left\lVert y-z\right\rVert_{M}.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
{\mathbb{T}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
be a
\theta
-averaged operator with respect to
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{M}
. Let
\mathbf{v}
be the infimal displacement vector of
{\mathbb{T}}
. Let
{\mathbb{S}}={\theta^{-1}}\left({\mathbb{I}}-{\mathbb{T}}\right)
.
Consider a sequence
y^{0},y^{1},y^{2},\dots
in
{\mathcal{H}}
such that its normalized iterate converges strongly to
-\gamma\mathbf{v}
,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{y^{k}}{k}=-\gamma\mathbf{v},

for some
\gamma>0
.
Then, for any
\delta\in(0,\pi/2)
and
z\in{\mathcal{H}}
, there exists
N_{\delta,z}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
k>N_{\delta,z}
,

\left<\mathbf{v},{\mathbb{S}}y^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right>_{M}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}y^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}\sin\delta.

Returning to the proof outline of
Theorem

5.1
, by the Inequality
1
and
Lemma

5.3
,

0\leq\left<\mathbf{v},\theta{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-\mathbf{v}\right>_{M}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}\left\lVert\theta{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}\sin\delta\approx 0

for small
\delta
.
Therefore, for
k
large enough,

\begin{split}\left\lVert\theta{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\lesssim&\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+\left\lVert\theta{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}.\end{split}

Since
\theta{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}\rightarrow\mathbf{v}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\begin{split}&U^{k}\lesssim\left(\beta-\alpha^{2}\right)\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}.\end{split}

Finally, we conclude

\begin{split}&\limsup_{k\to\infty}\,\mathbb{E}\left[k\left\lVert\frac{x^{k}}{k}-\frac{z^{k}}{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\right]\lesssim\left(\beta-\alpha^{2}\right)\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}.\end{split}

Note that for (b),
k\mathrm{Var}_{M}\left({x^{k}}/{k}\right)
can be bounded by

k\mathrm{Var}_{M}\left(\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right)\leq\mathbb{E}\left[k\left\lVert\frac{x^{k}}{k}-\frac{z^{k}}{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\right],

and so is (a) with an extra
\mathcal{O}\left(1/{\sqrt{k}}\right)
term.

Proof.

When
x^{0},x^{1},x^{2},\dots
is a random sequence generated by RC-FPI of
{\mathbb{T}}
and
z^{0},z^{1},z^{2},\dots
is a sequence generated by FPI of
\bar{{\mathbb{T}}}
with
z^{0}=z
, from
Lemma

4.2
, it is already known that for all
k
,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{I}^{k}}\left[\left\lVert{\mathbb{T}}_{\mathcal{I}^{k}}x^{k}-\bar{{\mathbb{T}}}z^{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\right]\leq\left\lVert x^{k}-z^{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}-\alpha\theta\left(1-\alpha\theta\right)\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z^{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+\theta^{2}\left(\beta-\alpha^{2}\right)\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}.\\
\end{split}

For convenience, define

U^{k}=-\alpha\theta\left(1-\alpha\theta\right)\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z^{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+\theta^{2}\left(\beta-\alpha^{2}\right)\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}.

Consequently, by taking a full expectation,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left[k\left\lVert\frac{x^{k}}{k}-\frac{z^{k}}{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{1}{k}\left\lVert x^{0}-z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}U^{j}\right].\\
\end{split}

The key of this proof is to bound the term
\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}
in
U^{k}
using
Lemma

5.3
, since
{x^{k}}/{k}
is strongly convergent, i.e.,
\lim_{k\to\infty}{x^{k}}/{k}=-\alpha\mathbf{v}
almost surely.
Suppose the case where
\lim_{k\to\infty}{x^{k}}/{k}=-\alpha\mathbf{v}
holds, which actually does hold almost surely. For such
x^{k}
, for an arbitrary
\delta\in\left(0,{\pi}/{2}\right)
, there exists a
N_{\delta,z}
such that for all
k>N_{\delta,z}
,

\left<\mathbf{v},{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right>_{M}\leq\sin\delta\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}.

From the inequality above, for all sufficiently large
k>N_{\delta,z}
,

\begin{split}\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}&=\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+2\left<{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z,{\mathbb{S}}z\right>_{M}+\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\\
&=\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+2\left<{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z,{\mathbb{S}}z-\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbf{v}\right>_{M}+2\left<{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z,\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbf{v}\right>_{M}+\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\\
&\leq\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+2\left\{\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z-\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}+\sin\delta\left\lVert\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}\right\}\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}+\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}.\\
\end{split}

By the inequality above, the term
U^{k}
can be bounded as :

\begin{split}U^{k}&\leq-\left(\alpha\theta-\alpha^{2}\theta^{2}\right)\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z^{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\theta^{2}\left(\beta-\alpha^{2}\right)\left[\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+2\left\{\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z-\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}+\sin\delta\left\lVert\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}\right\}\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}+\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\right]\\
&=\theta^{2}\left(\beta-\alpha^{2}\right)\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}-\theta\left(\alpha-\beta\theta\right)\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+2\theta^{2}\left(\beta-\alpha^{2}\right)\left\{\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z-\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}+\sin\delta\left\lVert\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}\right\}\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\left(\alpha\theta-\alpha^{2}\theta^{2}\right)\left\{\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}-\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z^{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\right\}.\\
\end{split}

Here, the term
\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}-\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z^{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}
is bounded above by

\begin{split}&\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}-\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z^{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}-\left(\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z-{\mathbb{S}}z^{k}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+2\left<{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z,{\mathbb{S}}z-{\mathbb{S}}z^{k}\right>_{M}\right)\\

from the triangular inequality.

Thus, we can bound
U^{k}
as

\begin{split}U^{k}&\leq\theta^{2}\left(\beta-\alpha^{2}\right)\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}-\theta\left(\alpha-\beta\theta\right)\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+2\theta^{2}\left(\beta-\alpha^{2}\right)\left\{\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z-\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}+\sin\delta\left\lVert\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}\right\}\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}\\

&\hskip 14.22636pt-\theta\left(\alpha-\beta\theta\right)\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+2\theta\tau_{\delta,z,k}\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}\\

&\hskip 14.22636pt-\theta\left(\alpha-\beta\theta\right)\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+2\theta\tau_{\delta,z,k}\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}x^{k}-{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M},\\
\end{split}

where
\tau_{\delta,z,k}
is defined as

\tau_{\delta,z,k}=\left(\beta-\alpha^{2}\right)\left(\left\lVert\theta{\mathbb{S}}z-\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}+\sin\delta\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}\right)+\left(\alpha-\alpha^{2}\theta\right)\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z-{\mathbb{S}}z^{k}\right\rVert_{M}.

To make an upper bound of
\tau_{\delta,z,k}
regardless of
k

\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z-{\mathbb{S}}z^{k}\right\rVert_{M}
independent from
k

\mathbf{v}

\left<{\mathbb{S}}z^{k}-\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbf{v},\mathbf{v}\right>_{M}\geq 0,\quad\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}-\left\lVert\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\geq 0,

hold. Recall that

\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z^{k}\right\rVert_{M}\leq\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z^{0}\right\rVert_{M}=\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}

4.4

\begin{split}\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z-{\mathbb{S}}z^{k}\right\rVert_{M}&\leq\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z-\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}+\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z^{k}-\frac{1}{\theta}\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}\\

regardless of

. Since

By Fatou’s lemma, we also have

Thus,

&\leq\theta^{2}\left(\beta-\alpha^{2}\right)\left\lVert{\mathbb{S}}z\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+\frac{\theta}{\alpha-\beta\theta}\tilde{\tau}_{\delta,z}^{2}.\end{split}

\theta{\mathbb{S}}x_{\star}=\mathbf{v}

\delta\to 0

∎

6
Infeasibility detection

In this section, we present the infeasibility detection method for (
RC-FPI
) using the hypothesis testing.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
{\mathbb{T}}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
be
\theta
-averaged with respect to
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{M}
with
\theta\in(0,1]
.
Let
\mathbf{v}
be the infimal displacement vector of
{\mathbb{T}}
.
Assume
\mathcal{I}^{0},\mathcal{I}^{1},\ldots
is sampled IID from a distribution satisfying the uniform expected step-size condition (
2
) with
\alpha\in(0,1]
, and assume (
3
) holds with some
\beta>0
such that
\beta<\alpha/\theta
.
Let
x^{0},x^{1},x^{2},\ldots
be the iterates of (
RC-FPI
).
Then under the null hypothesis
\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}\leq\delta
with nonzero
\delta
, for
\epsilon
that satisfies
\alpha\delta<\epsilon
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lVert\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right\rVert_{M}\geq\varepsilon\right)\lesssim\frac{\left(\beta-\alpha^{2}\right)\delta^{2}}{k(\varepsilon-\alpha\delta)^{2}}

as
k\to\infty
, where
\mathbf{v}
is the infimal displacement vector of
{\mathbb{T}}
.

Therefore, for any statistical significance level
p\in(0,1)
, the test

\left\lVert\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right\rVert_{M}\geq\varepsilon

with

k\gtrsim\frac{\left(\beta-\alpha^{2}\right)\delta^{2}}{p\left(\varepsilon-\alpha\delta\right)^{2}}

can reject the null hypothesis and conclude that
{\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}>\delta}
, which implies that the problem is inconsistent.

We further discuss this iteration requirement.
Let’s rewrite the bound as

k\gtrsim\frac{(\beta-\alpha^{2})\delta^{2}}{p(\varepsilon-\alpha\delta)^{2}}=\frac{(\beta-\alpha^{2})}{p(\varepsilon/\delta-\alpha)^{2}}.

\alpha
and
\beta
are parameters satisfying
\beta\geq\alpha^{2}
, which comes from the distribution of
\mathcal{I}
.

\varepsilon
and
\delta
are chosen as certain small values such as the machine precision of the computation device, as our objective is to determine whether
v
is nonzero.
When the distribution of
\mathcal{I}
is fixed, the lower bound to
k
can be determined by balancing the accuracy of the hypothesis testing and the number of required iteration, regardless of the choice of nonexpansive operator
{\mathbb{T}}
or the optimization problem (RC-FPI) is solving.

For the proof of the
Theorem

6.1
, we begin with the simpler case where
\mathbf{v}\in\mathrm{range}\,\left({\mathbb{I}}-{\mathbb{T}}\right)
.
Let
{\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}\leq\delta}
be the null hypothesis with
\delta
satisfying
\alpha\delta<\epsilon
.
By the triangle inequality, Markov inequality, and
Theorem

5.1
, under the null hypothesis,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lVert\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right\rVert_{M}\geq\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lVert\frac{x^{k}}{k}+\alpha\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}\geq\varepsilon-\alpha\delta\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\varepsilon-\alpha\delta)^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\frac{x^{k}}{k}+\alpha\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\left(\beta-\alpha^{2}\right)\delta^{2}}{k(\varepsilon-\alpha\delta)^{2}}

as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

When
\mathbf{v}\notin\mathrm{range}\,({\mathbb{I}}-{\mathbb{T}})
, we can still obtain the same (asymptotic) statistical significance with the same test and the same iteration count
k\gtrsim\frac{\left(\beta-\alpha^{2}\right)\delta^{2}}{p\left(\varepsilon-\alpha\delta\right)^{2}}
. Below, we present the full proof of this general case.

Proof.

First by the triangle inequality and Markov inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lVert\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right\rVert_{M}\geq\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lVert\frac{x^{k}}{k}-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right]\right\rVert_{M}\geq\varepsilon-\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right]\right\rVert_{M}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\varepsilon-\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right]\right\rVert_{M}\right)^{-2}\mathrm{Var}_{M}\left(\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right).

Next, let’s bound the term
\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\left[{x^{k}}/{k}\right]\right\rVert_{M}
. Due to triangle inequality, Jensen’s inequality and
Proposition

4.6
with
z^{0}=x^{0}
, we have

\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right]\right\rVert_{M}\leq\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right]-\frac{z^{k}}{k}\right\rVert_{M}+\left\lVert\frac{z^{k}}{k}\right\rVert_{M}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\right)+\left\lVert\frac{z^{k}}{k}\right\rVert_{M}.

By
[
115
, Theorem 3]
, for any
\omega>0
, there exist
\omega
-dependent constant
C_{\omega}
such that

\left\lVert\frac{z^{k}}{k}\right\rVert_{M}\leq\alpha\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}+\frac{1}{k}C_{\omega}+\omega.

Thus,

\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right]\right\rVert_{M}\leq\alpha\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\right)+\frac{1}{k}C_{\omega}+\omega.

Substitute this inequality at
\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\left[{x^{k}}/{k}\right]\right\rVert_{M}
and obtain

\displaystyle k\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lVert\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right\rVert_{M}\geq\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\varepsilon-\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right]\right\rVert_{M}\right)^{-2}k\mathrm{Var}_{M}\left(\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\varepsilon-\alpha\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}-\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\right)-\frac{1}{k}C_{\omega}-\omega\right)^{-2}k\mathrm{Var}_{M}\left(\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right),

when choice of
\omega
is sufficiently small and that of
k
is sufficiently large to keep

\varepsilon-\alpha\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}-\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\right)-\frac{1}{k}C_{\omega}+\omega>0.

Take a limit supremum by
k\to\infty
. Then by
Theorem

5.1
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}k\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lVert\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right\rVert_{M}\geq\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\varepsilon-\alpha\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}-\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\right)-\frac{1}{k}C_{\omega}-\omega\right)^{-2}k\mathrm{Var}_{M}\left(\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\leq\frac{(\beta-\alpha^{2})\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}}{\left(\varepsilon-\alpha\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}-\omega\right)^{2}}

holds for all sufficiently small
\omega>0
. Thus, by
\omega\to 0
,

\limsup_{k\to\infty}k\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lVert\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right\rVert_{M}\geq\epsilon\right)\leq\frac{(\beta-\alpha^{2})\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}}{\left(\varepsilon-\alpha\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}\right)^{2}}

Now consider a null hypothesis of
{\left\lVert\mathbf{v}\right\rVert_{M}\leq\delta}
. Under the null hypothesis,

\limsup_{k\to\infty}k\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lVert\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right\rVert_{M}\geq\epsilon\right)\leq\frac{(\beta-\alpha^{2})\delta^{2}}{\left(\varepsilon-\alpha\delta\right)^{2}},

or in other word,

\mathbb{P}\left(\left\lVert\frac{x^{k}}{k}\right\rVert_{M}\geq\epsilon\right)\lesssim\frac{(\beta-\alpha^{2})\delta^{2}}{k\left(\varepsilon-\alpha\delta\right)^{2}},

as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

∎

7.1

Theorem 7.1
.

.
Let

(a)

, then
{x^{k}}/{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle L^{2}}}{{\rightarrow}}-\alpha\mathbf{v}
as

(b)

If

as

7.2
Proof of
Theorem

7.1

Proposition 7.2
.

Suppose the subspaces
U_{1},U_{2}
of
{\mathcal{H}}
with

U_{1}\cap U_{2}=\{0\}

satisfy the condition

\left|\left<u_{1},u_{2}\right>_{M}\right|\leq c_{F}\left\lVert u_{1}\right\rVert_{M}\left\lVert u_{2}\right\rVert_{M},\quad c_{F}\leq\sqrt{\frac{1-\theta}{1-\alpha\theta}}

for any
u_{1}\in U_{1},u_{2}\in U_{2}
. Assume that block components of
U_{2}
are orthogonal to each other.
Then, there exists
\beta\geq 0
such that
\beta\theta\leq\alpha
and

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{I}}\left[u_{\mathcal{I}}\right]=\alpha u,\quad\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{I}}\left[\left\lVert u_{\mathcal{I}}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\right]\leq\beta\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{M}^{2}

where
u_{\mathcal{I}}
and
u
are defined as

u_{\mathcal{I}}=\alpha g+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{I}_{i}(0,\dots,0,h_{i},0,\dots,0),\quad u=g+h,\quad g\in U_{1},\quad h\in U_{2}.

where
h_{i}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,m
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{h_{i}}\in U_{2}
as
\tilde{h_{i}}=(0,\dots,0,h_{i},0,\dots,0)
for
i=1,\dots,m
. First equation comes from,

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{I}}\left[u_{\mathcal{I}}\right]=\alpha g+\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{I}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{I}_{i}\tilde{h_{i}}\right]=\alpha g+\alpha h=\alpha u.

The expectation in the second equation is

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{I}}\left[\left\lVert u_{\mathcal{I}}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\right]&=\alpha^{2}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+2\mathbb{E}\left[\left<\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{I}_{i}\tilde{h_{i}},\alpha g\right>_{M}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{I}_{i}\tilde{h_{i}}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\right]\\
&=\alpha^{2}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+2\left<\alpha g,\alpha h\right>_{M}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{I}_{i}^{2}\right]\left\lVert\tilde{h_{i}}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\\
&\leq\alpha^{2}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+2\left<\alpha g,\alpha h\right>_{M}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{I}_{i}\right]\left\lVert\tilde{h_{i}}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\\
&=\alpha^{2}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+2\alpha^{2}\left<g,h\right>_{M}+\alpha\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\\
&=\alpha^{2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+(\alpha-\alpha^{2})\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{M}^{2}.\end{split}

In the second equality, the Pythagorean theorem is applied on
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{I}_{i}\tilde{h_{i}}
, since the block components of
U_{2}
are orthogonal to each other. Note that

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{M}^{2}=\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+2\left<h,g\right>_{M}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\geq\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{M}^{2}-2c_{F}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{M}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{M}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\geq\left(1-c_{F}^{2}\right)\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{M}^{2}.

Thus, set
\beta
as

\beta=\alpha^{2}+\frac{\alpha-\alpha^{2}}{1-c_{F}^{2}}.

Then,

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{I}}\left[\left\lVert u_{\mathcal{I}}\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\right]\leq\alpha^{2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{M}^{2}+(\alpha-\alpha^{2})\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{M}^{2}\leq\left(\alpha^{2}+\frac{\alpha-\alpha^{2}}{1-c_{F}^{2}}\right)\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{M}^{2}=\beta\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{M}^{2},

with

\theta\beta\leq\theta\left(\alpha+\frac{1-\alpha\theta}{\theta}\right)\alpha=\alpha.

∎

Consider the convex optimization problem

\begin{array}[]{ll}\underset{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\mbox{minimize}}&\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(x)},\end{array}

(8)

.
Consider the decentralized algorithm PG-EXTRA
[
117
]

\begin{split}x_{i}^{k+1}&=\mathrm{Prox}_{\tau f_{i}}\left({\scriptstyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}}W_{ij}x_{i}^{k}-w_{i}^{k}\right)\\
w_{i}^{k+1}&=w_{i}^{k}+\frac{1}{2}\left(x_{i}^{k}-{\scriptstyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}}W_{ij}x_{j}^{k}\right)\end{split}

(PG-EXTRA)

for
i=1,2,\dots,m
. In decentralized optimization, we use network of agents to compute the algorithm. If a pair of agents could communicate, we say that they are connected.
For each agents
i=1,2\dots,m
,

is a set of agents connected to agent

.
A matrix

is called a mixing matrix, and it is a symmetric

if

and

.
Furthermore,
1\geq|\lambda_{i}|
for each eigenvalue of
W
and
\ker(I-W)=\text{span}(\mathbf{1})
, which ensures consensus among agents at the solution.

A randomized coordinate-update version of PG-EXTRA randomly chooses
i
among
1,2,\dots,m
to update
x_{i}^{k}
, while every
w_{1},w_{2},\dots,w_{m}
gets updated at each iterations.

Algorithm 1

RC-PG-EXTRA

for

do

,
x_{i}=0
,
[Wx]_{i}=0

for

for

end

for

Not converged

\mathcal{I}

for

i

\Delta x_{i}=\mathrm{Prox}_{\tau f_{i}}\left([Wx]_{i}-w_{i}\right)-x_{i}

for

\Delta x_{i}
From
i
th agent to
j
th agent.

[Wx]_{j}=[Wx]_{j}+W_{ij}\Delta x_{i}

w_{j}=w_{j}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(x_{i}-[Wx]_{i}\right)

end

for

end

for

end

while

Note that

is the only quantity communicated across agents.

|N_{i}|

are stored in

th agent.

(
PG-EXTRA
) is a fixed-point iteration with an averaged operator with respect to

Corollary 7.3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{I}^{0},\mathcal{I}^{1},\ldots
is sampled IID from a distribution satisfying the uniform expected step-size condition (
2
) with
\alpha\in(0,1]
.
Consider

1
with

If the minimum eigenvalue of the symmetric mixing matrix

satisfies

\lambda_{\text{min}}(W)>-\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha},

the normalized iterate of
Algorithm

1
converges to
-\alpha\mathbf{v}
, where
\mathbf{v}
is the infimal displacement vector of (

and almost surely.

Proof.

In the proofs, we use a stack notation for convenience. With stack notation,
{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
refers

{\mathbf{x}}=\begin{bmatrix}\text{---}&x_{1}^{\intercal}&\text{---}\\
\text{---}&x_{2}^{\intercal}&\text{---}\\
&\vdots&\\
\text{---}&x_{m}^{\intercal}&\text{---}\end{bmatrix},\quad\left[W{\mathbf{x}}\right]_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}W_{ij}x_{j}.

(
PG-EXTRA
) originates from Condat-Vũ
[
118
,
119
]
with
{\mathbf{w}}^{k}=\tau U{\mathbf{u}}^{k}
, where Condat-Vũ is a method defined as

\begin{split}&{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}=\mathrm{Prox}_{\tau f}(W{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-\tau U{\mathbf{u}}^{k})\\
&{\mathbf{u}}^{k+1}={\mathbf{u}}^{k}+\frac{1}{\tau}U{\mathbf{x}}^{k},\end{split}

which is a fixed-point iteration with an operator that’s
({1}/{2})
-averaged in
M
-norm. Thus,
\theta
value in (
PG-EXTRA
) is
\theta={1}/{2}
.

The matrix
M
in (
PG-EXTRA
) is

\begin{split}M=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\tau}I&U\\
U&\tau I\end{bmatrix},\end{split}

where
U
is a positive semidefinite matrix such that
U^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(I-W\right)
. Note that the inner product in this case is

\left<\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{x}}\\
{\mathbf{u}}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{y}}\\
{\mathbf{v}}\end{bmatrix}\right>_{M}={\mathrm{tr}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{x}}\\
{\mathbf{u}}\end{bmatrix}^{T}M\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{y}}\\
{\mathbf{v}}\end{bmatrix}\right).

Due to the given inner product, two subspaces
V_{1}=\left(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\{0\}^{m}\right)^{d}
and
V_{2}=\left(\{0\}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)^{d}
are no longer orthogonal to each other. On the other hand,
m
subspaces of
V_{1}
,

\left(\{0\}^{i-1}\times\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}^{m-i}\times\{0\}^{m}\right)^{d},\quad i=1,2,\dots,m,

are orthogonal to each other. Inner product between
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
is constrained as

\left|\left<\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{x}}\\
\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}\\
{\mathbf{u}}\end{bmatrix}\right>_{M}\right|=\left|{\mathbf{x}}^{T}U{\mathbf{u}}\right|\leq\lambda_{\max}^{U}\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{x}}\\
\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}\\
{\mathbf{u}}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert=\lambda_{\max}^{U}\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{x}}\\
\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{M}\left\lVert\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}\\
{\mathbf{u}}\end{bmatrix}\right\rVert_{M}.

Since
\lambda_{\min}^{W}>-{\alpha}/({2-\alpha})
,

\lambda_{\max}^{U}=\sqrt{\frac{1-\lambda_{\min}^{W}}{2}}<\sqrt{\frac{1}{2-\alpha}}=\sqrt{\frac{1-\frac{1}{2}}{1-\frac{\alpha}{2}}},

and we may apply
Theorem

7.1
with

{\mathbf{u}}\in V_{2}=U_{1},\quad{\mathbf{x}}\in V_{1}=U_{2},\quad{\mathcal{H}}_{0}=\mathbb{R}^{m\times d},\quad{\mathcal{H}}_{1}={\mathcal{H}}_{2}=\dots={\mathcal{H}}_{m}=\mathbb{R}^{d}

and block coordinate update with each orthogonal blocks as

\left(\{0\}^{i-1}\times\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}^{m-i}\times\{0\}^{m}\right)^{d},\quad i=1,2,\dots,m,

to conclude
Corollary

7.3
.

∎

8
Conclusion

This work analyzes the asymptotic behavior of the (
RC-FPI
) and establishes convergence of the normalized iterates to the infimal displacement vector, and this allows us to use the normalized iterates to test for infeasibility. We also extend our analyses to the setup with non-orthogonal basis, thereby making our results applicable to the decentralized optimization algorithm (
PG-EXTRA
).

One possible direction of future work would be to use variance reduction techniques in the style of, say, SVRG
[
122
]
or
[
123
]
to improve the convergence rate. Such techniques allow stochastic-gradient-type methods to exhibit a rate faster than
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
, and may be applicable in to the coordinate-update setup accelerate the infeasibility detection.

Another possible direction is to generalize our (RC-FPI) framework to cover the coordinate-update primal-dual methods of Chambolle–Ehrhardt–Richtárik–Schönlieb
[
64
]
and Fercoq–Bianchi
[
65
]
, which show faster convergence speeds for feasible optimization problems. We believe that the practical usability of these methods can be enhanced with additional infeasibility detection mechanisms similar to those presented in this paper.

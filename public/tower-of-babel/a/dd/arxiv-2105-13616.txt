Positional information as a universal predictor of freezing
Tamoghna Das, Tsvi Tlusty

Abstract
Variation of positional information, measured by the two-body excess entropy 𝖲 2 \mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{2}} , is studied across the liquid-solid equilibrium transition in a simple two-dimensional system. Analysis reveals a master relation between 𝖲 2 \mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{2}} and the freezing temperature T f T_{\mathrm{f}} , from which a scaling law is extracted: − 𝖲 2 ∼ | T f − T | − 1 / 3 -\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{2}}\sim|T_{\mathrm{f}}-T|^{-1/3} . Theoretical and practical implications of the observed universality are discussed.



Introduction

Locating thermodynamic transition lines is an essential part of material characterisation. However, broad scanning of the phase diagram of a system for any sharp changes in its thermodynamic response, primarily, heat capacity, is an exhaustive computationally-heavy procedure. As freezing of a liquid is a global structural transition, we propose here that the positional information alone can be a quick and accurate indicator of this phase transition. Such positional information of particulate systems is often directly accessible via simple X-ray scattering measurements, rendering it a practical means to efficiently chart the phase diagram.

Liquids lack practically any
apriori
positional information, as all particles are arranged in a random configuration, with only occasional short range correlations. Thus, setting the totally random configuration as a reference state of
zero
configurational entropy,
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{c}}=0
, a liquid would have a very small negative
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{c}}
. During freezing, as spatial correlation increases, information is gained leading to further decrease in
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{c}}
. Finally, after freezing, all particles settle down in a specific crystalline symmetry described by a certain finite amount of information, corresponding to a large negative value of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{c}}
.

In general, a disordered yet correlated arrangement of
N
particles can be envisioned as a collection of
n
-tuples where each tuple is a possible representation of local
n
-body correlation occurring in the system with frequency
\rho^{n}
. The total configurational entropy
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{c}}
can then be computed as an integrated contribution of such correlations as
Green 1952
;
Nettleton and Green 1958
;
Wallace 1989

\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{c}}/N=\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{1}}+\sum_{n=2}^{N}\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{n}}
, where
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{1}}
is the contribution of a system of non-interacting particles, namely, a classical ideal gas (up to an additive constant) and

\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{n}}=-\frac{1}{n!}~\rho^{n-1}\int d\mathbf{r}^{2}g^{\mathrm{[n]}}\ln\left[\frac{g^{\mathrm{[n]}}}{\mathsf{H}}\right]~.

(1)

Here,
g^{\mathrm{[n]}}
is the
n
-body correlation and
\mathsf{H}
represents the combination of all possible
(n-1)
-tuples.

For a simple liquid, large
n
-tuples can be reasonably approximated as the product of smaller
n
-tuples due to the absence of any long-lived local structures.
Kirkwood 1934

Under such approximation,

\mathsf{H}\approx g^{\mathrm{[3]}}(1,2,3)=g^{\mathrm{[2]}}(1,2)g^{\mathrm{[2]}}(2,3)g^{\mathrm{[2]}}(3,1)

and
g^{\mathrm{[2]}}
becomes the sike relevant positional correlation. The configurational entropy
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{c}}
, expressed in terms of only two-body correlation, now reads as:
Baranyai and Evans 1989

\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{2}}={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\rho\int d{\mathbf{r}}~\left(g^{\mathrm{[2]}}-1\right)-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\rho\int d{\mathbf{r}}~\left(g^{\mathrm{[2]}}\ln g^{\mathrm{[2]}}\right)~.

(2)

The positional information
-\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{2}}
is the
excess
information over the random configuration due to pairwise interaction in the system. Note that the upper bound of
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{2}}
is
{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}
and not zero.

\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{c}}
, or its approximant
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{2}}
, appears to be a natural candidate to relate properties of a material to the statistical nature of its underlying internal structure. Such measures physically approximate the logarithm of the phase space volume available to the systems.
Chapman and Cowling 1990
As in a classical Enskog picture, one may relate this positional information with the timescale of a particle’s motion within the cage formed by its neighbours. Stemming from this idea, scaling laws

;
Rosenfeld 1999
;
Dzugutov 1996

;
Ma et al. 2013
;
Hansen et al. 2018
;
Galloway et al. 2020
and simulation studies.
Hoyt, Asta, and Sadigh 2000
;
Chakraborty and Chakravarty 2006
;
Mittal, Errington, and Truskett 2007
;
Agarwal et al. 2011
;
Joy 2017
;
Gao and Widom 2018
In the context of glassy systems,
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{c}}
is linked the non-Arhenius relaxation processes, as formalised in the Adam-Gibbs theory. Specifically, the relation between
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{2}}
and glassy relaxation times was examined.
Gallo and Rovere 2015
;
Banerjee et al. 2017
Recently,
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{2}}
has been recast to account for the angular correlation
Ingebrigtsen and Tanaka 2018

Piaggi and Parinello 2017
was formulated to study the microscopic features of materials’ structure.
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{2}}

Young et al. 2007

Rosenfeld 2000

In this study, we explore the possibility of using
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{2}}
as an indicator of freezing transition in a simple liquid. We recognise that the two integrands contributing to Eq.(
2
) originate from two independent physical processes.
Gao and Widom 2018
The first integrand originates from fluctuations in the number of particles in the grand-canonical ensemble. This positive-definite contribution,
\Delta\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{fluc}}
, termed as
number fluctuation entropy
, is related to the system’s isothermal compressibility
\kappa_{\mathrm{T}}
,

\Delta\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{fluc}}\equiv{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\rho\int d{\mathbf{r}}~\left(g^{\mathrm{[2]}}-1\right)={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\rho\mathsf{k}_{\mathrm{B}}\kappa_{\mathrm{T}}~,

(3)

where
\mathsf{k}_{\mathrm{B}}
is the Boltzmann constant. The second integrand is a negative-definite quantity termed
loss entropy
as it captures the entropy lost due to pair-wise correlation that is absent from the reference ideal gas,

\Delta\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{loss}}\equiv-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\rho\int d{\mathbf{r}}~\left(g^{\mathrm{[2]}}\ln g^{\mathrm{[2]}}\right)~.

(4)

These two entropy represent two opposing effects. On one hand, increasing spatial correlation would shrink the phase space volume resulting in more negative
\Delta\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{loss}}
. On the other hand, this would also make exchange of particles with the environment increasingly difficult,
i.e.
,
\Delta\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{fluc}}
becomes more positive. The two-body excess entropy
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{2}}
is governed by the interplay of these two opposing contributions.

To understand the temperature dependence of structural probe
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{2}}
, we examined an ensemble of simulated equilibrium configurations of a simple system of unit-mass point particles over a wide range of densities and temperatures. The particles in this model system interact pair-wise via a Lennard-Jones potential,

\phi(r)=4\epsilon\left[(\sigma/r)^{12}-(\sigma/r)^{6}\right]

The energy, length, and time scales are set by

\mathsf{2D}

Conclusion

The phenomenological scaling relation,
-\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{2}}\sim|T_{\mathrm{f}}-T|^{-1/3}
, proposes
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{2}}
as a reliable universal predictor of the freezing transition, at least in the case of simple liquids. Higher-order correlations would be necessary to construct similar scaling relations for more complex network forming systems.
Baranyai and Evans 1990
;
Molinero and Moore 2009
;
Cao et al. 2011
;
Dhabal et al. 2015
We expect that in systems such as carbon-based polymeric mixtures, nano-particle composites, aggregate forming colloidal and other bio-inspired materials,
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{2}}
-based relations would provide a primary guidance for transition lines.

The results of this study come with two implications. First, from a theoretical point of view, it suggests the
Corresponding States Principle

Pitzer 1939
;
Pitzer 1955
;
Pitzer et al. 1955
;
Noro and Frenkel 2000
— so far used to advocate the universality in liquids — might now be extended to the crystalline states of matter. Thus, a unified description of the states of matter might be drawn based on the system’s information content. Recalling the numerous efforts to relate
\mathsf{S}_{\mathrm{2}}
with the dynamical response in condensed materials,
Dyre 2018
one might also hope to gain insight regarding the links between the dynamics and thermodynamics of matter in terms of the information exchange within the system. Second, from a practical perspective, the scaling relations allow to predict the transition temperature from simple X-ray scattering measurements of positional correlation in the system at
any
density and temperature, at least for a simple liquid. Since identification of a phase transitions often requires an exhaustive scan of the parameter space, this scaling relation would effectively reduce that huge search space into a single measurement.

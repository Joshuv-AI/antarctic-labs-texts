Introduction

Let
G
be a finite, simple, and undirected graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. For any two vertices
x,y\in V(G)
, let
d_{G}(x,y)
denote the minimum number of edges on a path connecting
x
and
y
in
G
, should such a path exist; otherwise, we define
d_{G}(x,y)=\infty
. The
diameter
,
\textnormal{diam}(G)
, of
G
is
\max\{d_{G}(x,y):x,y\in V(G)\}
. The
degree
,
\deg_{G}(v)
, of a vertex
v\in V(G)
is the number of edges incident to
v
in
G
; an
end-vertex
is a vertex of degree one, a
support vertex
is a vertex that is adjacent to an end-vertex, and a
major vertex
is a vertex of degree at least three. The
complement
of
G
, denoted by
\overline{G}
, has vertex set
V(\overline{G})=V(G)
and edge set
E(\overline{G})
such that
xy\in E(\overline{G})
if and only if
xy\not\in E(G)
for any distinct
x,y\in V(G)
. Let
P_{n},C_{n}
and
K_{n}
, respectively, denote the path, the cycle and the complete graph on
n
vertices. For a positive integer
k
, let
[k]=\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
.

A vertex
z\in V(G)

resolves
a pair of distinct vertices
x
and
y
in
G
if
d_{G}(x,z)\neq d_{G}(y,z)
. For two distinct vertices
x,y\in V(G)
, let
R_{G}\{x,y\}=\{z\in V(G):d_{G}(x,z)\neq d_{G}(y,z)\}
. A set
S\subseteq V(G)
is a
resolving set
of
G
if
|S\cap R_{G}\{x,y\}|\geq 1
for every pair of distinct vertices
x,y\in V(G)
. The
metric dimension

\dim(G)
of
G
is the minimum cardinality among all resolving sets of
G
. Metric dimension, introduced by Slater
[
17
]
and by Harary and Melter
[
8
]
, has applications in robot navigation
[
13
]
, sonar
[
17
]
and combinational optimization
[
16
]
, to name a few. It was noted in
[
7
]
that determining the metric dimension of a general graph is an NP-hard problem.

Brigham and Dutton
[
4
]
studied domination parameter for a family of edge-disjoint graphs on a common vertex set; they focused on the domination number for a graph and its complement. Recently, Ramírez-Cruz et al.
[
14
]
introduced the notion of a simultaneous resolving set and the simultaneous (metric) dimension as follows: for a family
\mathcal{C}=\{H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{t}\}
of (not necessarily edge-disjoint) connected graphs
H_{i}=(V,E_{i})
with a common vertex set
V
(the union of whose edge sets is not necessarily the complete graph), a set
S\subseteq V
is a
simultaneous resolving set
of
\mathcal{C}
if
S
is a resolving set for every graph
H_{i}
, where
i\in[t]
, and the
simultaneous dimension
of
\mathcal{C}
, denoted by
\textnormal{Sd}(\mathcal{C})
or
\textnormal{Sd}(H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{t})
, is the minimum among the cardinalities of all simultaneous resolving sets of
\mathcal{C}
. It was shown in
[
14
]
that, for any family
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},\ldots,G_{k}\}
of connected graphs with a common vertex set
V
,
1\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\{\dim(G_{i})\}\leq\textnormal{Sd}(\mathcal{G})\leq\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dim(G_{i}),|V|-1\right\}\leq|V|-1
.

The fractionalization of various graph parameters has been studied extensively (see
[
15
]
). Currie and Oellermann
[
5
]
defined fractional (metric) dimension as the optimal solution to a linear programming problem by relaxing a condition of the integer programming problem for metric dimension. Arumugam and Mathew
[
1
]
officially studied the fractional dimension of graphs. For a function
g
defined on
V(G)
and for
U\subseteq V(G)
, let
g(U)=\sum_{s\in U}g(s)
. A real-valued function
g:V(G)\rightarrow[0,1]
is a
resolving function
of
G
if
g(R_{G}\{x,y\})\geq 1
for any pair of distinct
x,y\in V(G)
, and the
fractional dimension

\dim_{f}(G)
of
G
is
\min\{g(V(G)):g\mbox{ is a resolving function of }G\}
.

Analogous to the simultaneous dimension, we introduce the notion of simultaneous fractional (metric) dimension as follows. A real-valued function
g:V\rightarrow[0,1]
is a
simultaneous resolving function
for a family of graphs
\mathcal{C}=\{H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{t}\}
on a common vertex set
V
if
g
is a resolving function for every graph
H_{i}
,
i\in[t]
, and the
simultaneous fractional dimension
of
\mathcal{C}
, denoted by
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{C})
or
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{t})
, is
\min\{g(V):g\mbox{ is a simultaneous resolving function of }\mathcal{C}\}
. Note that
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{C})
reduces to
\textnormal{Sd}(\mathcal{C})
if the codomain of simultaneous resolving functions is restricted to
\{0,1\}
.

Next, we recall some known results on the fractional dimension of graphs. We recall some terminology. Fix a tree
T
. An end-vertex
\ell
is called a
terminal vertex
of a major vertex
v
in
T
if
d_{T}(\ell,v)<d_{T}(\ell,w)
for every other major vertex
w
in
T
. The terminal degree,
ter_{T}(v)
, of a major vertex
v
is the number of terminal vertices of
v
in
T
, and an
exterior major vertex
is a major vertex that has a positive terminal degree. Let
\sigma(T)
denote the number of end-vertices of
T
, and let
ex(T)
denote the number of exterior major vertices of
T
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(a)

[
12
]
For any connected graph
G
of order
n\geq 2
,
\dim_{f}(G)=1
if and only if
G=P_{n}
.

(b)

[
18
]
For any non-trivial tree
T
,
\dim_{f}(T)=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma(T)-ex_{1}(T))
, where
ex_{1}(T)
denotes the number of exterior major vertices
u
with
ter_{T}(u)=1
.

(c)

[
1
]
For
n\geq 3
,
\dim_{f}(C_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{n}{n-1}&\mbox{ if $n$ is odd},\\
\frac{n}{n-2}&\mbox{ if $n$ is even}.\end{array}\right.

(d)

[
1
]
For the Petersen graph
\mathcal{P}
,
\dim_{f}(\mathcal{P})=\frac{5}{3}
.

(e)

[
1
]
For the wheel graph
W_{n}=K_{1}+C_{n-1}
(the join of
K_{1}
and
C_{n-1}
) of order
n\geq 4
,

\dim_{f}(W_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2&\mbox{ if }n\in\{4,5\},\\
\frac{3}{2}&\mbox{ if }n=6,\\
\frac{n-1}{4}&\mbox{ if }n\geq 7.\end{array}\right.

(f)

[
12
]
If
B_{m}
is a bouquet of
m\geq 2
cycles with a cut-vertex (i.e., the vertex sum of
m
cycles at one common vertex), then
\dim_{f}(B_{m})=m
.

(g)

[
1
,
10
]
A connected graph
G
of order
n\geq 2
has
\dim_{f}(G)\leq\frac{n}{2}
; further,
\dim_{f}(G)=\frac{n}{2}
if and only if there is a fixed-point-free permutation
\phi
on
V(G)
such that
|R\{v,\phi(v)\}|=2
for each
v\in V(G)
.

In this paper, we obtain some general results on the simultaneous fractional dimension for a family of connected graphs on a common vertex set. We also obtain some results on the simultaneous fractional dimension for a graph and its complement. The paper is organized as follow. In Section
2
, we obtain some general results on the simultaneous fractional dimension of graph families. Let
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{k}\}
be a family of connected graphs on a common vertex set
V
, where
k\geq 2
and
|V|\geq 3
. We observe that
1\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\{\dim_{f}(G_{i})\}\leq\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})\leq\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dim_{f}(G_{i}),\frac{|V|}{2}\right\}\leq\frac{|V|}{2}
; we provide a family of graphs
\mathcal{G}
satisfying
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\{\dim_{f}(G_{i})\}
and
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dim_{f}(G_{i})=\frac{|V|}{2}
, respectively. We also show that
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})-\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\{\dim_{f}(G_{i})\}
and
\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dim_{f}(G_{i}),\frac{|V|}{2}\right\}-\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})
, respectively, can be arbitrarily large. We characterize
\mathcal{G}
satisfying
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=1
, and we examine
\mathcal{G}
satisfying
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\frac{|V|}{2}
. We also determine
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})
when
\mathcal{G}
is a family of vertex-transitive graphs. In Section
3
, we show that if
\{\textnormal{diam}(G),\textnormal{diam}(\overline{G})\}\neq\{3\}
and
\textnormal{diam}(G)\leq\textnormal{diam}(\overline{G})
, then
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=\dim_{f}(G)
. We also determine
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})
when
G
is a tree or a unicyclic graph.

General results on simultaneous fractional dimension

Throughout this section, let
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{k}\}
be a family of connected graphs with a common vertex set
V
, where
k\geq 2
and
|V|\geq 3
. In this section, we observe that
1\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\{\dim_{f}(G_{i})\}\leq\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})\leq\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dim_{f}(G_{i}),\frac{|V|}{2}\right\}\leq\frac{|V|}{2}
; we show the existence of
\mathcal{G}
satisfying
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\{\dim_{f}(G_{i})\}
and
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dim_{f}(G_{i}),\frac{|V|}{2}\right\}
, respectively. We characterize a family of graphs
\mathcal{G}
satisfying
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=1
, and we examine some families of graphs
\mathcal{G}
with
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\frac{|V|}{2}
. We also show the following: (i) if
\mathcal{G}
is a family of paths, then
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})\in\{1,\frac{|V|}{|V|-1}\}

\mathcal{G}

We begin with the following result that is useful in establishing the lower bound of
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})
. The
open neighborhood
of a vertex

is

are called

[
9

observed that the twin relation is an equivalence relation and that an equivalence class under it, called a

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
S
be a twin equivalence class of a graph
G
with
|S|=\alpha\geq 2
. Let
g:V(G)\rightarrow[0,1]
be a resolving function for either
G
or
\overline{G}
. Then
g(S)\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}
.

Proof.

Let
S=\{s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{\alpha}\}
be a twin equivalence class of
G
, where
\alpha\geq 2
. Let
g:V(G)\rightarrow[0,1]
be a resolving function for either
G
or
\overline{G}
. Note that, for any distinct
i,j\in[\alpha]

R_{G}\{s_{i},s_{j}\}=\{s_{i},s_{j}\}=R_{\overline{G}}\{s_{i},s_{j}\}
; thus,
g(s_{i})+g(s_{j})\geq 1
. By summing over the

(\alpha-1)g(S)\geq{\alpha\choose 2}
. Therefore,
g(S)\geq\frac{\alpha}{2}
. ∎

.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{k}\}

(

\dim_{f}(G_{i})=1

1.1
(a),

must be a path for each

\mathcal{G}

u_{i}\in V
, we can choose a graph
G_{j}\in\mathcal{G}
such that
\deg_{G_{j}}(u_{i})=2
, where
i\in[n]
and
j=j(i)\in[k]
. Let
h:V\rightarrow[0,1]
be any simultaneous resolving function of
\mathcal{G}
. Since
R_{G_{j}}(N(u_{i}))=V-\{u_{i}\}
, we have
h(V)-h(u_{i})\geq 1
for each
i\in[n]
. By summing over the
n
inequalities, we have
(n-1)h(V)\geq n
, i.e.,
h(V)\geq\frac{n}{n-1}
; thus,
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})\geq\frac{|V|}{|V|-1}>1
, contradicting the assumption that
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=1
. So,
\mathcal{G}
must be a collection of paths which share a common end-vertex. ∎

Based on Theorem
2.5
and its proof, together with the fact that
h:V\rightarrow[0,1]
defined by
h(u)=\frac{1}{|V|-1}
for each
u\in V
forms a simultaneous resolving function for a family of paths, we have the following.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a family of paths on a common vertex set
V
. Then

\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if all members of $\mathcal{G}$ share a common end-vertex},\\
\frac{|V|}{|V|-1}&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Next, we provide three families

Question 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a family of connected graphs on a common vertex set
V
. By Theorem
2.4
(b) and Lemma
2.1
, one can readily verify that
\textnormal{Sd}(\mathcal{G})=|V|-1
implies
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\frac{|V|}{2}
. But, as shown in Example
2.7
(b), there exists
\mathcal{G}
such that
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\frac{|V|}{2}
and
\textnormal{Sd}(\mathcal{G})<|V|-1
. Can we characterize

satisfying
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\frac{|V|}{2}

Let us call a twin equivalence class nontrivial if it contains two or more members. Given a graph family
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{k}\}
, let
E_{i}
denote the set of nontrivial twin equivalence classes of graph
G_{i}
. We will say a vertex

belongs to
E_{i}
if
u
belongs to a class in
E_{i}
. For each
u\in V
, define
m_{\mathcal{G}}(u)
to be the number of
E_{i}
’s to which

belongs.

Proposition 2.9
.

Given
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{k}\}
,
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\frac{|V|}{2}
if the function
m_{\mathcal{G}}
is a positive constant function on
V
.

Proof.

Let
m_{\mathcal{G}}=m\geq 1
and
h
be a minimum simultaneous resolving function of
\mathcal{G}
. Then,
h(E_{i})\geq\frac{|E_{i}|}{2}
by Lemma
2.1
. Summing the
k
inequalities (one for each
E_{i}
), while noting that each vertex of
V
appears
m
times, we get
mh(V)\geq\frac{m|V|}{2}
; thus,
h(V)\geq\frac{|V|}{2}
. Since
h(V)\leq\frac{|V|}{2}
by Observation
2.2
, we have
h(V)=\frac{|V|}{2}
and hence
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\frac{|V|}{2}
. ∎

Next, we determine
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})
when
\mathcal{G}
is a family of vertex-transitive graphs. Following
[
6
]
, let
r(G)=\min\{|R_{G}\{x,y\}|:x,y\in V(G)\mbox{ and }x\neq y\}
. We recall the following result on the fractional dimension of vertex-transitive graphs.

Theorem 2.10
.

[
6
]

If
G
is a vertex-transitive graph, then
\dim_{f}(G)=\frac{|V(G)|}{r(G)}
. Moreover,
g:V(G)\rightarrow[0,1]
defined by
g(u)=\frac{1}{r(G)}
, for each
u\in V(G)
, is a minimum resolving function of
G
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{k}\}
be a family of vertex-transitive graphs with a common vertex set
V
. Then
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\{\dim_{f}(G_{i})\}
.

Proof.

By relabeling the members of
\mathcal{G}
if necessary, let
\dim_{f}(G_{1})\geq\dim_{f}(G_{2})\geq\ldots\geq\dim_{f}(G_{k})
, and let
r=r(G_{1})=\min\{|R_{G_{1}}\{x,y\}|:x,y\in V\mbox{ and }x\neq y\}
. Then
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})\geq\dim_{f}(G_{1})
by Observation
2.2
. If
g:V\rightarrow[0,1]
is a function defined by
g(u)=\frac{1}{r}
for each
u\in V
, then
g
is a resolving function for
G_{1}
(and thus for
G_{i}
for each
i\in[k]
) with
g(V)=\frac{|V|}{r}
. So,
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})\leq\frac{|V|}{r}=\dim_{f}(G_{1})
by Theorem
2.10
. Therefore,
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\dim_{f}(G_{1})=\max_{1\leq i\leq k}\{\dim_{f}(G_{i})\}
. ∎

Proposition
2.11
, together with (c) and (d) of Theorem

Corollary 2.12
.

(a)

If
\mathcal{G}
is a family of odd cycles on a common vertex set
V

\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\frac{|V|}{|V|-1}
.

(b)

If
\mathcal{G}
is a family of even cycles on a common vertex set
V
, then
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\frac{|V|}{|V|-2}
.

(c)

If
\mathcal{G}
is a family of the Petersen graphs on a common vertex set
V
with

, then
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})=\frac{5}{3}
.

\textnormal{Sd}(\mathcal{G})\geq\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})

\textnormal{Sd}(\mathcal{G})-\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(\mathcal{G})

3
The simultaneous fractional dimension of a graph and its complement

In this section, we focus on the simultaneous fractional dimension of a graph and its complement. For any non-trivial graph
G
, notice that
1\leq\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})\leq\frac{|V(G)|}{2}
; we characterize graphs
G
satisfying
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=1
and
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=\frac{|V(G)|}{2}
, respectively. We show that if
\{\textnormal{diam}(G),\textnormal{diam}(\overline{G})\}\neq\{3\}
and
\textnormal{diam}(G)\leq\textnormal{diam}(\overline{G})
, then
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=\dim_{f}(G)
. We also determine
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})
when
G
is a tree or a unicyclic graph.

Based on Observation
2.2
, we have the following.

Corollary 3.1
.

For any graph
G
of order
n\geq 2
,

1\leq\max\{\dim_{f}(G),\dim_{f}(\overline{G})\}\leq\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})\leq\min\left\{\dim_{f}(G)+\dim_{f}(\overline{G}),\frac{n}{2}\right\}\leq\frac{n}{2}.

Now, for any graph
G
of order
n\geq 2
, we characterize
G
satisfying
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=1
and
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=\frac{n}{2}
, respectively. For an explicit characterization of connected graphs
G
with
\dim_{f}(G)=\frac{|V(G)|}{2}
, we refer to
[
2
]
. We recall the following results.

Observation 3.2
.

[
11
]

Graphs
G
and
\overline{G}
cannot both be disconnected.

Theorem 3.3
.

[
11
]

Let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq 2
. Then
\textnormal{Sd}(G,\overline{G})=1
if and only if
G\in\{P_{2},\overline{P}_{2},P_{3},\overline{P}_{3}\}
, and
\textnormal{Sd}(G,\overline{G})=n-1
if and only if
G\in\{K_{n},\overline{K}_{n}\}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq 2
. Then

(a)

\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=1
if and only if
G\in\{P_{2},\overline{P}_{2},P_{3},\overline{P}_{3}\}
;

(b)

\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=\frac{n}{2}
if and only if each vertex in
G
belongs to a twin equivalence class of cardinality at least two in
G
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq 2
.

(a) (
\Leftarrow
) Let
G\in\{P_{2},\overline{P}_{2},P_{3},\overline{P}_{3}\}
. Since
1\leq\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})\leq\textnormal{Sd}(G,\overline{G})\!=\!1
by Theorem
3.3
,
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})\!=\!1
.

(
\Rightarrow
) Let
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=1
; then
\dim_{f}(G)=\dim_{f}(\overline{G})=1
by Corollary
3.1
. We may, by Observation
3.2
, assume that
G
is connected; then
G=P_{n}
by Theorem
1.1
(a). If
n\geq 4
, then
\overline{G}
is connected but not a path that shares a common end-vertex with
G
; thus,
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})\neq 1
by Theorem
2.5
.

(b) (
\Leftarrow
) Let
Q_{1},Q_{2},\ldots,Q_{x}
be the twin equivalence classes of
G
such that
|Q_{i}|\geq 2
and each vertex in
G
belongs to some
Q_{i}
, where
i\in[x]
. Let
g:V(G)\rightarrow[0,1]
be any resolving function of
G
. Then, for each
i\in[x]
,
g(Q_{i})\geq\frac{|Q_{i}|}{2}
by Lemma
2.1
. By summing over the
x
inequalities, we have
g(V(G))=\sum_{i=1}^{x}g(Q_{i})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{x}\frac{|Q_{i}|}{2}=\frac{n}{2}
; thus,
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})\geq\dim_{f}(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}
by Corollary
3.1
. Since
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})\leq\frac{n}{2}
by Corollary
3.1
, we have
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=\frac{n}{2}
.

(
\Rightarrow
) Let
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=\frac{n}{2}
. Suppose
\{w\}
is a twin equivalence class of cardinality one in

. For
V=V(G)=V(\overline{G})
, let

be a function defined by

and
h(u)=\frac{1}{2}
for each
u\in V-\{w\}
; we show that
h
is a resolving function for both
G
and
\overline{G}
with
h(V)=\frac{n-1}{2}
. Let
x
and
y
be distinct vertices in
V
. First, let

. Then
R_{G}\{x,y\}\supseteq\{x,y\}
and
R_{\overline{G}}\{x,y\}\supseteq\{x,y\}
; thus,
h(R_{G}\{x,y\})\geq h(x)+h(y)=1
and
h(R_{\overline{G}}\{x,y\})\geq 1
. Second, let
w\in\{x,y\}
, say
x=w
without loss of generality. Then there exists
z\in V-\{x,y\}
such that
d_{G}(z,x)\neq d_{G}(z,y)
; notice that
d_{G}(z,x)=d_{G}(z,y)
, for every
z\in V-\{x,y\}
, means that
x=w
and

belong to the same twin equivalence class in

, which contradicts the assumption that
\{w\}
is a twin equivalence class of cardinality one in

. Similarly,
d_{\overline{G}}(z^{\prime},x)\neq d_{\overline{G}}(z^{\prime},y)
for some

. So,

and
R_{\overline{G}}\{x,y\}\supseteq\{x,y,z^{\prime}\}
; thus,
h(R_{G}\{x,y\})\geq h(x)+h(y)+h(z)=1
and
h(R_{\overline{G}}\{x,y\})\geq 1
. Thus,

is a resolving function for both
G
and
\overline{G}
, and hence
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})\leq h(V)=\frac{n-1}{2}
. Therefore, each vertex in

must belong to a twin equivalence class of cardinality at least two in

(and thus, in
\overline{G}
). ∎

Next, we show that if
\{\textnormal{diam}(G),\textnormal{diam}(\overline{G})\}\neq\{3\}
and
\textnormal{diam}(G)\leq\textnormal{diam}(\overline{G})
, then
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=\dim_{f}(G)=\max\{\dim_{f}(G),\dim_{f}(\overline{G})\}
. We recall the following useful result.

Proposition 3.5
.

[
3
]

If
G
is a graph with
\textnormal{diam}(G)\geq 4
, then
\textnormal{diam}(\overline{G})\leq 2
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
G
be a graph with
\textnormal{diam}(G)=2
. Let
V
be the common vertex set for
G
and
\overline{G}
, and let
g:V\rightarrow[0,1]
be a real-valued function. If
g
is a minimum resolving function of
G
, then
g
is a resolving function of
\overline{G}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph with
\textnormal{diam}(G)=2
, and let
V=V(G)=V(\overline{G})
. For any distinct vertices
x,y\in V(G)
, let
z\in R_{G}\{x,y\}
. Then
d_{G}(x,z)\neq d_{G}(y,z)
, say
0\leq d_{G}(x,z)<d_{G}(y,z)\leq 2
without loss of generality. If
d_{G}(x,z)=0
and
d_{G}(y,z)\in\{1,2\}
, then
d_{\overline{G}}(x,z)=0
and
d_{\overline{G}}(y,z)\geq 1
. If
d_{G}(x,z)=1
and
d_{G}(y,z)=2
, then
d_{\overline{G}}(x,z)\geq 2
and
d_{\overline{G}}(y,z)=1
. In each case,

. So,
R_{G}\{x,y\}\subseteq R_{\overline{G}}\{x,y\}
. If
g:V\rightarrow[0,1]
is a minimum resolving function of
G
, then
g(R_{G}\{x,y\})\geq 1
; thus,
g(R_{\overline{G}}\{x,y\})\geq 1
. So,
g
is a resolving function for
\overline{G}
. ∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
G
be a graph of order at least two. Suppose
\{\textnormal{diam}(G),\textnormal{diam}(\overline{G})\}\neq\{3\}
and
\textnormal{diam}(G)\leq\textnormal{diam}(\overline{G})

\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=\dim_{f}(G)
.

Proof.

Let

be a graph of order

. Then
\textnormal{diam}(G)\leq 2
by the hypotheses and Proposition

\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=\dim_{f}(G)=\max\{\dim_{f}(G),\dim_{f}(\overline{G})\}

3.1

h:V\rightarrow[0,1]

\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(C_{4},\overline{C}_{4})=2=\dim_{f}(\overline{C}_{4})=\dim_{f}(C_{4})
and
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(C_{5},\overline{C}_{5})=\frac{5}{4}=\dim_{f}(\overline{C}_{5})=\dim_{f}(C_{5})
. Now, suppose
G
is isomorphic to
H=K_{1,n-1}+e
with
V(H)=\{u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{n-1}\}
such that
\deg_{H}(u_{0})=n-1
and
e=u_{1}u_{2}\in E(H)
. Let
g:V\rightarrow[0,1]
be any resolving function for
H
and let
\tilde{g}:V\rightarrow[0,1]
be any resolving function for
\overline{H}
on the common vertex set
V=V(H)=V(\overline{H})
. If
n\geq 5
, then
g(u_{1})+g(u_{2})\geq 1
and
g(\cup_{i=3}^{n-1}\{u_{i}\})\geq\frac{n-3}{2}
by Lemma
2.1
; thus,
g(V)\geq\frac{n-1}{2}
, and hence
\dim_{f}(H)\geq\frac{n-1}{2}
. The same argument works to show that
\tilde{g}(V)\geq\frac{n-1}{2}
and
\dim_{f}(\overline{H})\geq\frac{n-1}{2}
. If
h:V\rightarrow[0,1]
is a function defined by
h(u_{0})=0
and
h(u_{i})=\frac{1}{2}
for each
i\in[n-1]
, then
h
forms a resolving function for both
H
and
\overline{H}
; thus,
\max\{\dim_{f}(H),\dim_{f}(\overline{H}),\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(H,\overline{H})\}\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
. By Corollary
3.1
,
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(H,\overline{H})=\dim_{f}(\overline{H})=\dim_{f}(H)=\frac{n-1}{2}
. So, suppose
n=4
; we show that
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(H,\overline{H})=\frac{3}{2}=\dim_{f}(H)=\frac{1}{2}+\dim_{f}(\overline{H})
. Since
\overline{H}
is isomorphic to the disjoint union of
K_{1}
and
P_{3}
,
\dim_{f}(\overline{H})=1
by Theorem
1.1
(a). Since
R_{G}\{u_{1},u_{2}\}=\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
,
R_{G}\{u_{0},u_{1}\}=\{u_{0},u_{1},u_{3}\}
and
R_{G}\{u_{0},u_{2}\}=\{u_{0},u_{2},u_{3}\}
, we have
g(u_{1})+g(u_{2})\geq 1
,
g(u_{0})+g(u_{1})+g(u_{3})\geq 1
and
g(u_{0})+g(u_{2})+g(u_{3})\geq 1
. By summing over the three inequalities, we have
2g(V)\geq 3
, and hence
\dim_{f}(H)\geq\frac{3}{2}
. If
h^{\prime}:V\rightarrow[0,1]
is a function defined by
h^{\prime}(u_{0})=0
and
h^{\prime}(u_{i})=\frac{1}{2}
for each
i\in[3]
, then
h^{\prime}
is a resolving function for both
H
and
\overline{H}
; thus,
\max\{\dim_{f}(H),\dim_{f}(\overline{H}),\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(H,\overline{H})\}\leq\frac{3}{2}
. By Corollary
3.1
,
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(H,\overline{H})=\dim_{f}(H)=\frac{3}{2}=\frac{1}{2}+\dim_{f}(\overline{H})
for
n=4
. ∎

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
G
be a unicyclic graph of order at least three. Let
H_{1}
be the graph obtained from
K_{1}\cup C_{3}
by joining an edge between the vertex in
K_{1}
and a vertex in
C_{3}
,
let
H_{2}
be the graph obtained from
K_{1}\cup C_{4}
by joining an edge between the vertex in
K_{1}
and a vertex in
C_{4}
, and let
H_{3}
be the graph obtained from
P_{6}
by joining an edge between the two support vertices of
P_{6}
. Then

\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dim_{f}(G)=\dim_{f}(\overline{G})+\frac{1}{2}&\mbox{ if }G\in\{H_{1},H_{2}\},\\
\dim_{f}(G)=\dim_{f}(\overline{G})+\frac{1}{3}&\mbox{ if }G=H_{3},\\
\dim_{f}(\overline{G})&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Let
G
be a unicyclic graph of order at least three. We consider the following three cases: (i)
\textnormal{diam}(G)=1
; (ii)
\textnormal{diam}(G)=2
or
\textnormal{diam}(\overline{G})=2
; (iii)
\textnormal{diam}(G)=3=\textnormal{diam}(\overline{G})
.

If
\textnormal{diam}(G)=1
, then
G=C_{3}
and
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(C_{3},\overline{C}_{3})=\dim_{f}(\overline{C}_{3})=\dim_{f}(C_{3})=\frac{3}{2}
by Proposition
3.12
. If
\textnormal{diam}(G)=2
, then, by Observation
3.15
,
G\in\{C_{4},C_{5}\}
or
G
is isomorphic to the graph
H_{n}^{*}
obtained from the star
K_{1,n-1}
by joining an edge between two end-vertices. By Lemma
3.16
, we have the following: (i) if
G
is isomorphic to
H_{4}^{*}=H_{1}
, then
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=\dim_{f}(G)=\dim_{f}(\overline{G})+\frac{1}{2}
; (ii) if
G\in\{C_{4},C_{5},H^{*}_{n}\}
for
n\geq 5
, then
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=\dim_{f}(\overline{G})
. If
\textnormal{diam}(\overline{G})=2
, then
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=\dim_{f}(\overline{G})
by Theorem
3.7
. If
\textnormal{diam}(G)=3=\textnormal{diam}(\overline{G})
, then
G
is isomorphic to one of the graphs in Figure
4
by Proposition
3.13
. By Proposition
3.14
, we have the following: (i) if
G=H_{2}
, then
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=\dim_{f}(G)=\dim_{f}(\overline{G})+\frac{1}{2}
; (ii) if
G=H_{3}
, then
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=\dim_{f}(G)=\dim_{f}(\overline{G})+\frac{1}{3}
; (iii) if
G\not\in\{H_{2},H_{3}\}
, then
\textnormal{Sd}_{f}(G,\overline{G})=\dim_{f}(\overline{G})
. ∎

There exists a graph

be the exterior major vertices of a tree
G
with

for each

\overline{G}

,
g(\{\ell_{i},\ell^{\prime}_{i},\ell^{\prime\prime}_{i}\})\geq\frac{3}{2}

i\in[k]
. Thus,
g(V)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{k}g(\{\ell_{i},\ell^{\prime}_{i},\ell^{\prime\prime}_{i}\})\geq\frac{3k}{2}

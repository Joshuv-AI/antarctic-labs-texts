Squared eigenfunction symmetry of the D Δ \Delta mKP hierarchy and its constraint
Kui Chen, Cheng Zhang, Da-jun Zhang

Abstract
In this paper squared eigenfunction symmetry of the differential-difference modified Kadomtsev-Petviashvili (D Δ \Delta mKP) hierarchy and its constraint are considered. Under the constraint, the Lax triplets of the D Δ \Delta mKP hierarchy, together with their adjoint forms, give rise to the positive relativistic Toda (R-Toda) hierarchy. An invertible transformation is given to connect the positive and negative R-Toda hierarchies. The positive R-Toda hierarchy is reduced to the differential-difference Burgers hierarchy. We also consider another D Δ \Delta mKP hierarchy and show that its squared eigenfunction symmetry constraint gives rise to the Volterra hierarchy. In addition, we revisit the Ragnisco-Tu hierarchy which is a squared eigenfunction symmetry constraint of the differential-difference Kadomtsev-Petviashvili (D Δ \Delta KP) system. It was thought the Ragnisco-Tu hierarchy does not exist one-field reduction, but here we find an one-field reduction to reduce the hierarchy to the Volterra hierarchy. Besides, the differential-difference Burgers hierarchy are also investigated in Appendix. A multi-dimensionally consistent 3-point discrete Burgers equation is given. PACS numbers: 02.30.Ik, 02.30.Ks, 05.45.Yv Keywords: D Δ \Delta mKP, squared eigenfunction symmetry constraint, relativistic Toda, one-field reduction, Burgers

6
Concluding remarks

We have mainly considered squared eigenfunction symmetry constraint of the D
\Delta
mKP system together with the D
\Delta
KP systems.
These two systems are entangled each other, as we can see in the paper.
As the new results, we have achieved the following.
The D
\Delta
KP gives rise to the Ragnisco-Tu hierarchy which is a discretization of the AKNS system
and is reduced to the Volterra hierarchy.
Note that it used to be thought that there does not exist one-field reduction for the Ragnisco-Tu hierarchy.
The D
\Delta
mKP gives rise to the R-Toda hierarchy which is reduced to the differential-difference Burgers hierarchy.
The D
\Delta
mKP(E) leads to the Volterra hierarchy as well, by either decomposition or reduction.
In some reductions, we have taken into account of nonzero asymptotic conditions of the wave functions
so that reductions can be implemented reasonably.

The D
\Delta

\Delta
mKP systems can be formally considered as subsystems of the 2DTL system,
since the 2DTL system employs both pseudo-difference operators
\bar{M}
and
\bar{L}^{*}

[
54
]
.
There exists a constraint imposed on

and

the time part in the Lax triplet of the 2DTL together with its adjoint form
gives rise to the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy (cf.
[

]

but that is different from the constraint of squared eigenfunction symmetry.
Besides, there exists a local transformation to bring the Ablowitz-Ladik hierarchy to the R-Toda hierarchy (

which is given by

]
, (also see §18.11 of

]
and its hierarchy
that gives rise to the Burgers hierarchy after a simple reduction
[
8
]
.
The result of the present paper implies possible connection
between the squared eigenfunction symmetry constraint of the D
\Delta
mKP
and the Kaup-Newell hierarchy, which is worthy to investigate elsewhere.
In addition, the differential-difference case exhibits richer results and reveals more links than the continuous case,
some of which already emerged in
[
29
,
45
]
,
but here have been much more elaborated.
Investigation of continuum limits of the D
\Delta
mKP hierarchies, their integrable characteristics, and
constrained systems will provide more insight to understand the D
\Delta
mKP system.
We believe that the results we obtained in the paper will provide a deeper understanding
for the connections of (1+1)-dimensional and (2+1)-dimensional differential-difference integrable systems.

In Appendix we will have a close look at the differential-difference Burgers hierarchy and a
fully discrete Burgers equation.
It turns out that the former act as auto Bäcklund transformations for the continuous Burgers hierarchy
and the nonlinear superposition formula of the Bäcklund transformation
gives rise to a fully discrete Burgers equation
that is defined on 3 points (not quadrilateral) but still consistent around cube.
Moreover, this discrete Burgers equation is linearisable (see (
A.28
)).

Appendix A
A close look at the Burgers hierarchies

Since the squared eigenfunction symmetry of the continuous mKP gives rise to the derivative Schrödinger hierarchy
hierarchy that is easily reduced to the Burgers hierarchy
[
8
]
,
it is not surprising the D
\Delta
mKP generates the differential-difference Burgers hierarchy.
However, the differential-difference Burgers equation can not only act as a Bäcklund transformation
for the whole continuous Burgers hierarchy,
but also generate a fully discrete Burgers equation from its nonlinear superposition formula.
Thus, we may have a full profile from the continuous to the discrete Burgers equations.

A.1
Connections with the KP and mKP system

Let us briefly recall the known connections between the Burgers hierarchy and the KP and mKP system formulated in

[
20
,
21
,
30
]
.
First, in Sato’s KP theory the pseudo-differential operator

L_{\mathrm{KP}}=\partial_{x}+u_{2}\partial^{-1}_{x}+u_{3}\partial^{-2}_{x}+\cdots

(A.1)

is introduced by
L_{\mathrm{KP}}=W\partial_{x}W^{-1}
where

W=1+w_{1}\partial^{-1}_{x}+w_{2}\partial^{-2}_{x}+\cdots.

(A.2)

The Burgers hierarchy (
4.61
) is equivalent to the Sato equation
[
20
]

W_{t_{s}}=B_{s}W-W\partial^{s}_{x},~~s=1,2,\cdots,

(A.3)

where
W
is truncated as

W=1+w_{1}\partial^{-1}_{x},~~w_{1}=-\gamma,

(A.4)

and
B_{s}=(W\partial_{x}^{s}W^{-1})_{\geq 0}
.
In this sense, the Burgers hierarchy is viewed as a sub-hierarchy of the KP equation.
Note that in terms of the
\tau
function in Sato’s KP theory (cf.
[
48
]
) there is

\gamma=-w_{1}=\tau_{x}/\tau,

(A.5)

which leads to the linearisation of the Burgers hierarchy.

Second, the pseudo-differential operator of the mKP hierarchy is

L_{\mathrm{mKP}}=\partial_{x}+v+v_{2}\partial^{-1}_{x}+v_{3}\partial^{-2}_{x}+\cdots.

(A.6)

The symmetry constraint

v=qq^{*}

(A.7)

gives rise to the Kaup-Newell spectral problem and its hierarchy
[
8
]
,
where
q
is the eigenfunction of the
L_{\mathrm{mKP}}
and
q^{*}
is its adjoint counterpart.
This constraint reduces
L_{\mathrm{mKP}}
to

L_{\mathrm{mKP}}=\partial_{x}+q\partial^{-1}_{x}q^{*}\partial_{x}

(A.8)

and the Burgers hierarchy is obtained from a single eigenfunction constraint

v=q
(i.e.
q^{*}=1
), which leads to

L=\partial_{x}+v,

(A.9)

and
Lq=\lambda q
is nothing but the spectral problem of the
Burgers hierarchy (cf. Sec.IV in
[
8
]
).
Note that the two operators
L_{\mathrm{KP}}
and
L_{\mathrm{mKP}}

are connected by gauge relation (cf.
[
30
]
)

L_{\mathrm{KP}}=f^{-1}L_{\mathrm{mKP}}f

where
v=f_{x}/f
, which coincides with (
A.5
)
and indicates
v
and
\gamma
in (
A.5
) can share same
\tau
function.

In the differential-difference case, the pseudo-difference operator
M
, (
2.5
),
is introduced by

where
[

(A.10)

The corresponding Sato equation reads

W_{t_{s}}=B_{s}W-W\Delta^{s},~~s=1,2,\cdots,

(A.11)

where

.
One can find that when

is truncated as

W=1+w_{1}\Delta^{-1}_{x},~~w_{1}=z_{n}+1,

(A.12)

the first two Sato equations are

(\ln z_{n})_{t_{1}}=Y_{1},~~(\ln z_{n})_{t_{2}}=-2Y_{1}+Y_{2},

where
Y_{j}
are the differential-difference Burgers flows given in (
4.57
).
More precise relation between the differential-difference Burgers hierarchy and the Sato equation (
A.11
)
with (
A.12
) will be investigated elsewhere.

Finally, back to the squared eigenfunction symmetry constraint (
4.5
)
of the D
\Delta
mKP hierarchy,
the differential-difference Burgers hierarchy is obtained when
r_{n}=-z_{n+1}
(see (

which holds when taking
b_{n}=1
in light of (

This leads to once again a single eigenfunction constraint

and the constrained operator
(

and the related spectral problem reads

(

It can be easily transformed to a trivial form
\beta_{n+1}=z_{n}\beta_{n}
by
\psi_{n}=(-\lambda)^{n}/\beta_{n}
,
which coincides with (

Note also that the gauge relation (

in (

(cf.

where

is an auxiliary independent variable and

A.2
Bäcklund and Galilean transformations of the Burgers hierarchy

Equation (
2.21b
) in the Lax triplet together with its adjoint form (
2.33b
)
leads to a differential-difference Burgers equation

\partial_{x}\ln z_{n}=z_{n-1}-z_{n}.

(A.13)

Let us look at the relation between (
A.13
) together with the differential-difference Burgers hierarchy

\partial_{t_{s+1}}\ln z_{n}=-\Delta E^{-1}z_{n}\Delta^{-1}\partial_{t_{s}}\ln z_{n},~~\partial_{t_{1}}\ln z_{n}=z_{n-1}-z_{n},~~s=1,2,\cdots,

(A.14)

and the Burgers hierarchy w.r.t.
z_{n}

\partial_{t_{s+1}}z_{n}=-\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}+z_{n})\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{t_{s}}z_{n},~~\partial_{t_{1}}z_{n}=z_{n,x}.

(A.15)

Lemma 1
.

Under (
A.13
) the differential-difference Burgers hierarchy (
A.14
) gives rise to the
Burgers hierarchy (
A.15
).

Proof.

As a single equation, (
A.14
) is an
(s+1)
-order difference equation.
In principle, one can repeatedly use (
A.13
) to express
z_{n-j}
in terms of
z_{n}
, e.g.

z_{n-1}=\frac{z_{n,x}+z_{n}^{2}}{z_{n}},~~z_{n-2}=\frac{z_{n}^{3}+3z_{n}z_{n,x}+z_{n,xx}}{z_{n}^{2}+z_{n,x}},~~\cdots,

to eliminate all
z_{n-j}
in (
A.14
).
In practice, rewriting (
A.14
) as

\partial_{t_{s+1}}E\Delta^{-1}\ln z_{n}=-z_{n}\partial_{t_{s}}\Delta^{-1}\ln z_{n},

(A.16)

and replacing the
\Delta^{-1}
term by using (
A.13
) which indicates
\Delta^{-1}\ln z_{n}=-\partial_{x}^{-1}z_{n-1}
,
from (
A.16
) we have

\partial_{t_{s+1}}z_{n}=-\partial_{x}z_{n}\partial_{t_{s}}\partial_{x}^{-1}z_{n-1}.

Then, replacing
z_{n-1}
by (
A.13
) we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{s+1}}z_{n}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}z_{n}\partial_{t_{s}}\partial_{x}^{-1}(z_{n}+\partial_{x}\ln z_{n})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{x}(z_{n}+\partial_{x})\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{t_{s}}z_{n},

which is the Burgers hierarchy (
A.15
).
∎

Lemma 2
.

If
z_{n}
satisfies (
A.15
),
then
z_{n-1}
defined by (
A.13
) gives rise to the Burgers hierarchy as well.

Proof.

First, making use of (
A.13
) and (
A.15
), one has

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{s+1}}z_{n-1}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t_{s+1}}(\partial_{x}\ln z_{n}+z_{n})

\displaystyle=(\partial_{x}\frac{1}{z_{n}}+1)\partial_{t_{s+1}}z_{n}

\displaystyle=-(\partial_{x}\frac{1}{z_{n}}+1)\partial_{x}(z_{n}+\partial_{x})\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{t_{s}}z_{n}.

Meanwhile,

\displaystyle\partial_{x}(z_{n-1}+\partial_{x})\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{t_{s}}z_{n-1}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}(\frac{z_{n,{x}}}{z_{n}}+z_{n})\partial_{x}^{-1}(\partial_{x}\frac{1}{z_{n}}+1)\partial_{t_{s}}z_{n}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}(\frac{z_{n,x}}{z_{n}}+z_{n})(\frac{1}{z_{n}}\partial_{x}+1)\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{t_{s}}z_{n}.

By direct calculation we can find the above two formulas are same and we then arrive at

\partial_{t_{s+1}}z_{n-1}=-\partial_{x}(z_{n-1}+\partial_{x})\partial_{x}^{-1}\partial_{t_{s}}z_{n-1},

(A.17)

which is the Burgers hierarchy for
z_{n-1}
.
∎

On the basis of the above two lemmas, we immediately find the following.

Theorem A.1
.

(
A.13
) and (
A.14
) define an auto Bäcklund transformation
for the Burgers hierarchy from (
A.17
) to (
A.15
).

Note that (
A.13
) and (
A.14
) are parameter free.
Let us introduce a parameter
\lambda
by replacing
z_{n}
with
z_{n}+\lambda
.
It follows from (
A.13
) and (
A.14
) that

\displaystyle z_{n,x}=(z_{n}+\lambda)(z_{n-1}-z_{n}),

(A.18a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{s+1}}\ln(z_{n}+\lambda)=(-1)^{s+1}\Delta[E^{-1}(z_{n}+\lambda)]^{s}z_{n-1},

(A.18b)

which, by repeating the proof of Lemma
1
and
2
,
compose a Bäcklund transformation with parameter
\lambda
for the Burgers hierarchy

\partial_{t_{s+1}}z_{n}=(-1)^{s}\partial_{x}(\partial_{x}+z_{n}+\lambda)^{s}z_{n,x}.

(A.19)

This is a linear combination of the original Burgers flows in (
A.15
),
but also as a whole it is a Galilean transformation of the
(s+1)
-order Burgers equation (see Theorem 1 in
[
58
]
).
Thus we can conclude that

Theorem A.2
.

(
A.18
) defines an auto Bäcklund transformation
for the Galilean transformed Burgers hierarchy (
A.19
).

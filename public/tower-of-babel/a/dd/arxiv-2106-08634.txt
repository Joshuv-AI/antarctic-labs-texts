MPC-based Reinforcement Learning for a Simplified Freight Mission of Autonomous Surface Vehicles
Wenqi Cai, Arash B. Kordabad, Hossein N. Esfahani, Anastasios M. Lekkas, Sébastien Gros

Abstract
In this work, we propose a Model Predictive Control (MPC)-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) method for Autonomous Surface Vehicles (ASVs). The objective is to find an optimal policy that minimizes the closed-loop performance of a simplified freight mission, including collision-free path following, autonomous docking, and a skillful transition between them. We use a parametrized MPC-scheme to approximate the optimal policy, which considers path-following/docking costs and states (position, velocity)/inputs (thruster force, angle) constraints. The Least Squares Temporal Difference (LSTD)-based Deterministic Policy Gradient (DPG) method is then applied to update the policy parameters. Our simulation results demonstrate that the proposed MPC-LSTD-based DPG method could improve the closed-loop performance during learning for the freight mission problem of ASV.



I
introduction

Autonomous Surface Vehicles (ASVs) are widely applied for many fields, such as freight transportation, military, search and rescue
[
1
]
, and therefore attract broad attention for scientific and industrial researches. Various methods have been proposed to solve the problem of operating and automating the ASV, including path following, collision avoidance, and autonomous docking
[
2
,
3
]
. However, designing a control strategy that could realize both collision-free path following and docking in a freight mission with time-varying disturbances is still a topic worth exploring. With the development of Machine Learning (ML), Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based control strategies are getting noticed by people, as they can exploit real data to reduce the impact of model uncertainties and disturbances.

Deterministic Policy Gradient (DPG), as the direct RL method, estimates the optimal policy by a parameterized function approximator, and optimizes the policy parameters directly via gradient descent steps of the performance
[
4
]
. Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) are very commonly used function approximators in RL
[
5
]
. However, DNN-based RL lacks the abilities concerning the closed-loop stability analysis, state/input constraints satisfaction, and meaningful weights initialization
[
6
]
. To address these problems, the perspective of using Model Predictive Control (MPC)-based RL has been proposed and justified in
[
7
]
, i.e. it suggests using MPC as the function approximation for the optimal policy in RL. Unlike DNNs, MPC-based policies satisfy the state/input constraints and safety requirements by construction, and its well-structured property enables the stability analysis of the system.

However, for computational reasons, simple models are usually preferred in the MPC-scheme. Hence, the MPC model often does not have the required structure to correctly capture the real system dynamics and stochasticity. As a result, MPC can deliver a reasonable approximation of the optimal policy, but it is usually suboptimal
[
8
]
. Besides, choosing the model parameters that best fit the MPC model to the real system does not necessarily yield a policy that achieves the best closed-loop performance
[
6
]
. Therefore, choosing appropriate MPC parameters to achieve the best closed-loop performance is extremely challenging. Nevertheless, according to
Theorem 1
and
Corollary 2
in
[
7
]
, it can conclude that by adjusting not only the MPC model parameters but also the parameters in the MPC cost and constraints, the MPC scheme can, theoretically, generate the optimal closed-loop policy even if the MPC model is inaccurate. It is also shown that RL is a suitable candidate to perform that adjustment. Recent researches focused on the MPC-based RL have further developed this approach
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
.

The contribution of this work is to provide a promising approach for a complete ASV freight mission problem. The problem is challenging since it needs to solve the obstacle avoidance, path following, and autonomous docking simultaneously, in a stochastic environment. We elaborate the proposed MPC-based RL method in the ASV problem framework, as well as formulate an algorithm for the MPC-LSTD-based DPG method.

ASV model

The 3-Degree of Freedom (3-DOF) position of the vessel can be represented by a pose vector
\boldsymbol{\eta}=[x,y,\psi]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

(1a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{M}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\dot{\nu}}}+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{D}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tau}}+\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tau}}_{a},

(1b)

and two azimuth thrusters
f_{2},f_{3}
. They are subjected to the bounds

\displaystyle{f_{p}}_{\mathrm{min}}\leq f_{p}\leq{f_{p}}_{\mathrm{max}},\quad p=1,2,3.

(2)

Matrix

presents the thruster configuration, written as

\displaystyle\small\boldsymbol{\mathrm{T}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\alpha}}\right)=\begin{bmatrix}0&\cos\left(\alpha_{2}\right)&\cos\left(\alpha_{3}\right)\\

(3)

where elements

. Constants

with

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\alpha}}=[\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the corresponding orientation vector. The angle
\alpha_{1}
is fixed (
\pi/2
), while
\alpha_{2}
and
\alpha_{3}
, associated to the two azimuth thrusters, are restricted in the range

\displaystyle\left|\alpha_{2}+\pi/2\right|\leq{\alpha}_{\mathrm{max}},\quad\left|\alpha_{3}-\pi/2\right|\leq{\alpha}_{\mathrm{max}}.

(4)

A maximum angle of
{\alpha}_{\mathrm{max}}
with a forbidden sector is considered in this work to avoid thrusters
2
and
3
directly work against each other, as shown in Fig.
1
. With a sampling time of
dt
, we discretize the ship system (
1
) as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}_{k+1}=F\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tau}}_{a}\right),

(5)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}_{k}=\left[\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}^{\top},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}_{k}^{\top}\right]^{\top}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k}=\left[\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}^{\top}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\alpha}}^{\top}_{k}\right]^{\top}
are system state and input vectors, respectively. Subscript
k
denotes the physical time and
F(\cdot)
is the discretized real system.

III
Simplified Freight Mission

In this work, we consider a simplified freight mission problem: the ASV starts from an origin
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{A}}
to the end
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{B}}
, which is supposed to follow a designed collision-free course and finally dock at the wharf autonomously. Note that the transition from path following to docking is a notable point of this problem.

III-A

Collision-Free Path Following

Given a reference path
P_{\mathrm{ref}}
. At time instance
k
,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{P}}^{\mathrm{ref}}_{k}=[x^{\mathrm{ref}}_{k},y^{\mathrm{ref}}_{k}]^{\top}
. Then path following could be thought as minimizing the error
l\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}\right)

\displaystyle l\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}\right)={\left\|{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}^{p}_{k}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{P}}^{\mathrm{ref}}_{k}\right\|}_{2}^{2}=(x_{k}-x^{\mathrm{ref}}_{k})^{2}+(y_{k}-y^{\mathrm{ref}}_{k})^{2},

(6)

where
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}^{p}_{k}=[x_{k},y_{k}]^{\top}
contains the first two elements of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}
. Besides, we assume obstacles of round shape. To avoid these obstacles, the following term
g_{n}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}\right)
, representing the position of the ship relative to the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
obstacle, should satisfy

\displaystyle\left(x_{k}-o_{x,n}\right)^{2}+\left(y_{k}-o_{y,n}\right)^{2}\geq\left(r_{n}+r_{o}\right)^{2},

(7)

i.e.,

\displaystyle\underbrace{1-\left({{{\left({{x_{k}}-{o_{x,n}}}\right)}^{2}}+{{\left({{y_{k}}-{o_{y,n}}}\right)}^{2}}}\right)\mathopen{\bBigg@}{1.6}/{{\left({{r_{n}}+{r_{o}}}\right)}^{2}}}_{g_{n}\left({{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}}}\right)}\leq 0,

(8)

where
(o_{x,n},o_{y,n})
and
r_{n}
are the center and radius of the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
circular obstacle (
n=1,\ldots,N_{o}
), respectively. Constant
r_{o}
is the radius of the vessel and
N_{o}
is the number of obstacles.

III-B

Autonomous Docking

Docking refers to stopping the vessel exactly at the endpoint
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{B}}
as well as avoiding collisions between any part of the vessel and the quay
[
3
]
. The “accurate stop” requires not only an accurate docking position but also zero-valued velocities and thruster forces at the final time, i.e., we ought to minimize

\displaystyle h\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}_{k}\right)=\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{d}\right\|^{2}_{2}+\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}_{k}\right\|^{2}_{2}+\left\|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}_{k}\right\|^{2}_{2},

(9)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{d}=(x_{d},y_{d},\psi_{d})
is the desired docking position. Successfully docking requires
h\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{K},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}_{K},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}_{K}\right)\approx 0
, where subscript
K
denotes the terminal time step of the freight mission. As for “collision avoidance”, we define a safety operation region
\mathbb{S}
as the spatial constraints for the vessel. The operation region is chosen as the largest convex region that encompasses the docking point but not intersecting with the land. Thus, as long as the vessel is within the region
\mathbb{S}
, no collision will occur during docking, i.e. the following condition should hold

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}^{p}_{k}\in\mathbb{S},\quad\mathbb{S}=\{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{A}}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{x}}<\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}\},

(10)

where
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}^{p}_{k}=[x_{k},y_{k}]^{\top}
describes the position of the vessel. The matrix
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{A}}
and the vector
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{b}}
are determined by the shape of the quay and together define the convex region
\mathbb{S}
.

III-C

Objective Function

In the context of RL, we seek a control policy
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}
that minimizes the following closed-loop performance
J

\displaystyle J(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}})=\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}\Bigg[\sum_{k=0}^{K}\gamma^{k}L\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k}\right)\Bigg|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}_{k})\Bigg],

(11)

where
\gamma\in(0,1]
is the discount factor. Expectation
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}
is taken over the distribution of the Markov chain in the closed-loop under policy
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}
. The RL-stage cost
L(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}_{k},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}_{k})
, in this problem, is defined as a piecewise function:

\displaystyle L=\left\{{\begin{array}[]{*{20}{l}}{l\left({{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}}}\right)+O\left({{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}}}\right)+\xi\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\alpha}}_{k}\right)}&{{{\left\|{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{d}}}\right\|}^{2}_{2}}>d}\\
{h\left({{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}},{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}_{k}},{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}_{k}}}\right)+\Gamma\left({{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}}}\right)+\xi\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\alpha}}_{k}\right)}&{{{\left\|{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{d}}}\right\|}^{2}_{2}}\leq d},\end{array}}\right.

where
O\left({{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}}}\right)
is the obstacle penalty for path following

\displaystyle O\left({{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}}}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{N_{o}}c_{n}\cdot\max(0,g_{n}\left({{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}}}\right)+d_{s}),

(14)

where
c_{n}>0
is the penalty weight, constant
d_{s}>0
is the desired safe distance between vessel and obstacles. Therefore, once the ship breaks the safe distance, i.e.
g_{n}\left({{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}}}\right)+d_{s}>0
, a positive penalty will be introduced to the objective function. Function
\Gamma\left({{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}}}\right)
is the collision penalty for docking

\displaystyle\Gamma\left({{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}}}\right)=\kappa\cdot(1-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{1}}_{\mathbb{S}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}^{p})),

(15)

where
\kappa>0
is the penalty weight and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{1}}_{\mathbb{S}}(\cdot)
is the indicator function. When the ship is out of the safe region, i.e.
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}^{p}_{k}\notin\mathbb{S}
, a positive penalty will be imposed in the objective function. Function
\xi\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\alpha}}_{k}\right)
is the singular configuration penalty, aiming to avoid the thruster configuration matrix
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{T}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\alpha}}_{k}\right)
in (
3
) being singular
[
16
]

\displaystyle\small\xi\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\alpha}}_{k}\right)=\frac{\rho}{{\varepsilon+\det\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{T}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\alpha}}_{k}\right)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{W}}^{-1}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{T}}^{\top}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\alpha}}_{k}\right)}\right)}},

(16)

where “
\det
” stands for the determinant of the matrix. Constant
\varepsilon>0
is a small number to avoid division by zero,
\rho>0
is the weighting of maneuverability, and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{W}}
is a diagonal weighting matrix. Constant
d>0
is designed to substitute the stage cost from path following to docking at
{{\left\|{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k}-{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{d}}}\right\|}^{2}_{2}}=d
, which means that our target transits from path-following to docking when the ship approaches the destination.

IV
MPC-Based Reinforcement Learning

The core idea of our proposed approach is to use a parameterized MPC-scheme as the policy approximation function, and apply the LSTD-based DPG method to update the parameters so as to improve the closed-loop performance.

IV-A

MPC-Based Policy Approximation

Consider the following MPC-scheme parameterized with
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}

\displaystyle\min_{\small{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\alpha}}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}}

\displaystyle\,\frac{\theta_{d}}{{{{\left\|{{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{N}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{d}}}\right\|}^{2}_{2}}+\delta}}\cdot\Big(h_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{N},\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}}_{N}\right)+\Gamma_{\theta}\left({\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{N}}\right)\Big)+

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\omega}}}_{f}^{\top}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}_{N}+\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\gamma^{i}\Big(l_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{i}\right)+\xi\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\alpha}}}_{i}\right)+{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\omega}}}^{\top}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}_{i}\Big)

(17a)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}\quad

\displaystyle\forall i=0,\ldots,N-1,\,\,n=1,\ldots,N_{o}

\displaystyle\left[\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}{{}^{\top}}_{i+1},\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}}{{}^{\top}}_{i+1}\right]{{}^{\top}}=F_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{i},\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}}_{i},\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}}_{i},\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\alpha}}}_{i},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}_{a})

(17b)

\displaystyle{f_{p}}_{\mathrm{min}}\leq\hat{f}_{p,i}\leq{f_{p}}_{\mathrm{max}},\quad p=1,2,3

(17c)

\displaystyle\left|\hat{\alpha}_{2,i}+\pi/2\right|\leq{\alpha}_{\mathrm{max}},\,\,\,\left|\hat{\alpha}_{3,i}-\pi/2\right|\leq{\alpha}_{\mathrm{max}},

(17d)

\displaystyle g_{n}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{i}\right)+\theta_{g}\leq\sigma_{n,i},\,\,\,g_{n}\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{N}\right)+\theta_{g}\leq\sigma_{n,N},

(17e)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}_{i}\geq 0,\,\,\,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}_{N}\geq 0,

(17f)

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{0}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{k},\,\,\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}}_{0}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}_{k},

(17g)

where
N
is the prediction horizon. Arguments
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}=\{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{0},\dots,\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{N}\}
,
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}}=\{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}}_{0},\dots,\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}}_{N}\}
,
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}}=\{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}}_{0},\dots,\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{f}}}_{N-1}\}
,
\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\alpha}}}=\{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\alpha}}}_{0},\dots,\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\alpha}}}_{N-1}\}
, and
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}}=\{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}_{0},\dots,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}_{N}\}
are the primal decision variables. The term
\frac{\theta_{d}}{{{{\left\|{{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{N}}-{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{d}}}\right\|}^{2}_{2}}+\delta}}\cdot\left(h_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\cdot\right)+\Gamma_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\cdot\right)\right)
introduces a gradually increasing terminal cost as the ship approaches the endpoint, where
\delta>0
is a small constant to avoid division by zero. The weighting parameter
\theta_{d}
, designed to balance the priority of path following and docking, is tuned by RL. Note that
\theta_{d}
is chosen to minimize the closed-loop performance that considering both path following and docking, although it may be suboptimal for either single problem. Parameter
\theta_{g}
is the tightening variable used to adjust the strength of the collision avoidance constraints. If the value of
\theta_{g}
(positive) is larger, it means that the constraints are tighter and the ship is supposed to be farther away from the obstacles. It is important to use RL to pick an appropriate
\theta_{g}
, since when
\theta_{g}
is too large, although we ensure that the ship safely avoids obstacles, the path following error is increased. Conversely, a smaller
\theta_{g}
reduces the following error, but we may gain more penalty when the vessel breaks the safe distance, as described in (
14
). Note that the obstacle penalties are considered directly as constraints (
17e
) in the MPC rather than as penalties in the MPC cost, because (
8
) is a conservative model of the obstacle penalty (
14
). Variables
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}_{i}

\big(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}_{i}=\{\sigma_{1,i},\dots,\sigma_{N_{0},i}\}\big)
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}_{N}

\big(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\sigma}}_{N}=\{\sigma_{1,N},\dots,\sigma_{N_{0},N}\}\big)
are slacks for the relaxation of the state constraints, weighted by the positive vectors
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\omega}}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\omega}}_{f}
. The relaxation prevents the infeasibility of the MPC in the presence of some hard constraints.

The parameterized stage cost

, terminal cost

, and docking collision penalty
\Gamma_{\theta}\left(\cdot\right)
in the MPC cost (
17a
) are designed as follows

\displaystyle l_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}

\displaystyle={\left\|{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}}^{p}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{P}}^{\mathrm{ref}}_{i}\right\|}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Theta}}_{l}}^{2}

(18a)

\displaystyle h_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}_{N}-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}_{d}\right\|_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Theta}}_{\eta}}^{2}+\left\|\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\nu}}}_{N}\right\|_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Theta}}_{\nu}}^{2}

(18b)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\theta}

\displaystyle=\theta_{\kappa}\cdot(1-\boldsymbol{\mathrm{1}}_{\mathbb{S}}({{\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\eta}}}}_{N}^{p}})),

(18c)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Theta}}_{l},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Theta}}_{\eta},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Theta}}_{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
are the weighing matrices that are symmetric semi-positive definite. They are expressed as
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Theta}}_{l}=(\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}_{l}))^{2}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Theta}}_{\eta}=(\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}_{\eta}))^{2}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Theta}}_{\nu}=(\mathrm{diag}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}_{\nu}))^{2}
. Operator “
\mathrm{diag}
” assigns the vector elements onto the diagonal elements of a square matrix. Parameter
\theta_{\kappa}
is treated as a degree of freedom for the docking collision penalty. The real model is (
5
) and we assume the disturbance
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\tau}}_{a}
follows a Gaussian distribution. To address the disturbance without using a complex stochastic model in the MPC scheme, one measure is to use a parameter vector
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}_{a}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
to parameterize the model as
F_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}}_{i},\hat{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}}_{i},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}_{a})
. As detailed in
[
7
]
, the full adaptation of the parametrized MPC scheme (model, costs, constraints) can compensate for that unmodelled disturbance. Overall, the adjustable parameters vector
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}
is consisted as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}=\{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}_{l},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}_{\eta},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}_{\nu},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}_{a},\theta_{\kappa},\theta_{d},\theta_{g}\}.

(19)

And
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}
will be adjusted by RL according to the principle of “improving the closed-loop performance”. Note that: 1. the span of the RL (
K\approx 550
) is much longer than the horizon of the MPC (
N=60
); 2. the RL cost (
III-C
) is a “switching” function, while the MPC cost (

) contains simultaneously the path following and docking cost to avoid the mixed-integer treatment of the problem; 3. the MPC model does not perfectly match the real system. For the above reasons, having different cost functions in the MPC scheme and RL is rational
[
7
]
. Therefore, in order to improve the closed-loop performance of the MPC scheme as assessed by the RL cost, it can be beneficial to parameterize the MPC cost functions, model, and constraints. RL then adjusts these parameters according to the principle of “improving the closed-loop performance”. From
Theorem 1
and
Corollary 2
in
[
7
]
, we know that, theoretically, under some assumptions, if the parametrization is rich enough, the MPC scheme is capable of capturing the optimal policy

in presence of model uncertainties and disturbances.

Importantly, the deterministic policy
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}})
can be obtained as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}})=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}^{\star}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}),

(20)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}_{0}^{\star}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}})
is the first element of
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{u}}}^{\star}
, which is the input solution of the MPC scheme (
17
).

IV-B

LSTD-Based DPG Method

The DPG method optimizes the policy parameters
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}
directly via gradient descent steps on the performance function

, defined in (
11
). The update rule is as follows

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}\leftarrow\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}-\alpha\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}J(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}),

(21)

where
\alpha>0
is the step size. Applying the DPG method developed by
[
17
]
, the gradient of

with respect to parameters
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}
is obtained as

\displaystyle\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}J(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}})=\mathbb{E}\left[{\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}){\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}}}{Q_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})|_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}\right],

(22)

where
Q_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
and its inner function
V_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}
are the action-value function and value function associated to the policy
{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}
, respectively, defined as follows

\displaystyle{Q_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}}\right)

\displaystyle=L\left({\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}}\right)+\gamma{\mathbb{E}}\left[{{V_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}\left({{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}^{+}}|(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})}\right)}\right]

(23a)

\displaystyle{V_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}})

\displaystyle={Q_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}\right)}\right),

(23b)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}^{+}
is the subsequent state of the state-input pair (
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}
). The calculations of
{\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}})
and
{\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}}}}{Q_{{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{a}})

IV-B
1
{\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\pi}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\theta}}}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}})

The primal-dual Karush Kuhn Tucker (KKT) conditions underlying the MPC scheme (

In practice, the assumption of a “rich enough” parametrization is typically not satisfied. Other practical issues can come in the way of optimality such as, e.g., the local convergence of the RL algorithm and of the solver treating the MPC scheme. Addressing these potential issues typically requires good initial guesses. Although these are often available in the MPC context, we can only claim that the final learned policy

obtained from the converged parameters

VI
Conclusion

This paper presents an MPC-based RL method for the ASV to accomplish a freight mission, which includes collision-free path following, autonomous docking, and an ingenious transition between them. We use a parameterized MPC-scheme as the policy approximation function, and adopt the LSTD-based DPG method to update the parameters such that the closed-loop performance gets improved with learning. For future works, we will further validate our proposed method by realizing the experimental implementations.

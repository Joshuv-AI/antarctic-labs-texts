Zeta functions of geometrically finite graphs of groups
Soonki Hong, Sanghoon Kwon

Abstract
In this paper, we explore the properties of zeta functions associated with infinite graphs of groups that arise as quotients of cuspidal tree-lattices, including all non-uniform arithmetic quotients of the tree of rank one Lie groups over local fields. Through various examples, we illustrate pairs of non-isomorphic cuspidal tree-lattices with the same Ihara zeta function. Additionally, we analyze the spectral behavior of a sequence of graphs of groups whose pole-free regions of zeta functions converge towards 0, which also presents an example of arbitrary small exponential error-term in counting geodesic formula.

Introduction

Given a cocompact subgroup
\Gamma
of
PSL(2,\mathbb{R})
, Selberg introduced a zeta function
Z_{\Gamma}(s)
and proved many important properties which resemble those of usual
L
-functions in number theory, such as Euler product, functional equation, and the analogue of Riemann hypothesis (
Se
). This function, now called with Selberg’s zeta function, is generalized to any discrete subgroup
\Gamma
of semi-simple Lie group of
\mathbb{R}
-rank one, even for non-uniform lattice
\Gamma
(
Gu
) or geometrically finite subgroups of
PSL(2,\mathbb{R})
with infinite area (
Bo
).

Meanwhile, inspired by Selberg’s zeta function, Ihara
Ih
initially introduced a zeta function that counts prime elements in discrete subgroups of rank one
p
-adic groups. It can be viewed as a geometric zeta function associated with the corresponding finite graph, which is a quotient of the Bruhat-Tits tree for the
p
-adic groups (
Ser
). Over time, it has been generalized by Sunada
Su
, Hashimoto
Ha
, and Bass
Ba 2
, revealing connections to number theory and representation theory. This zeta function is defined as the product

Z_{X}(u)=\prod_{p}\frac{1}{1-u^{\ell(p)}}

where
p
runs through the set of prime cycles in a finite graph
X
. This product, being infinite in general, converges to a rational function and satisfies the
Ihara determinant formula

Z_{X}(u)=\frac{(1-u^{2})^{\chi(X)}}{\det(1-uA+u^{2}Q)}

where
A
is the adjacency operator,
Q+1
is the valency operator, and
\chi(X)
is the Euler characteristic of the graph
X
. One of the most remarkable features of this formula is that if
X=\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{T}
for some Bruhat-Tits tree
\mathcal{T}
of a
p
-adic group
G
and a cocompact arithmetic subgroup
\Gamma
of
G
, then the right hand side of the formula equals the non-trivial part of the Hasse-Weil zeta function of the Shimura curve attached to
\Gamma
.

In recent years, there has been interest in generalizing these zeta functions to infinite graphs. For example,
CMS
and
Cl
utilizes a finite trace argument on a group von Neumann algebra and define the zeta function as a determinant. Another approach, described in
GZ
, involves approximating an infinite graph by finite ones and defining the zeta function as a suitable limit. In
CJK
, the Ihara zeta function is extended to infinite graphs by considering cycles that pass through a specific point using Heat kernel, rather than considering all cycles. Furthermore, in
LPS
, the zeta function is extended to the case of an infinite graph equipped with an invariant measure and a groupoid action, which is referred to as a measure graph. Meanwhile,
DK
take another approach by considering cycles obtained from geodesics in the universal covering tree of infinite weigted graphs. Especially, they defined the Bass-Ihara zeta function for a weighted graph
(X,w)
by

Z_{(X,w)}(u)=\prod_{[c]}\frac{1}{1-w(c)u^{\ell(c)}}.

Here,
c
runs through the set of prime cycles in a graph
X
. The precise definition of
w(c)
is given in Section
3
. It is also proved in
DK
that it converges to a rational function when
\Gamma
is a cuspidal tree lattice.

Our goal in this paper is to extend the idea of
DK
to develop a comprehensive definition of the zeta function for a graph of groups
(X,\mathcal{G})
. Graphs of groups naturally arise in the study of group actions on trees and their covering theory. The key distinction between the zeta function of weighted graphs and graphs of groups lies in the additional information regarding the centralizers of hyperbolic elements.

Of particular interest is the case where the graph of groups
(X,\mathcal{G})
is obtained from a geometrically finite (or equivalently, cuspidal) tree lattice. This encompasses every quotient of the tree of rank-one Lie groups over non-archimedean local fields by its discrete subgroup (as proved in
Lu
). Specifically, we define the zeta function
Z_{(X,\mathcal{G})}(u)
of
(q+1)
-regular graphs of groups
(X,\mathcal{G})
that resembles the Selberg zeta function, and investigate its relationship with the Bass-Ihara zeta function
Z_{(X,w)}(u)
for the weighted graph
(X,w)
associated with
(X,\mathcal{G})
under
centrally rigid
condition. (We refer to Definition
3.2
for precise details.) It is worth mentioning that the Selberg zeta function and its determinant expression for principal congruence subgroups of
PGL(2,\mathbb{F}_{q}[t])
have been also achieved in
Na
with algebraic approach. We build upon these algebraic achievements and provide a geometric perspective by examining zeta functions within the framework of graph of groups.

We define a Selberg zeta function of a graph of groups
(X,\mathcal{G})
by

Z_{(X,\mathcal{G})}(u)=\prod_{[\gamma]\in\textrm{P}(\pi_{1}(X,\mathcal{G}))}\frac{1}{1-u^{\ell(\gamma)}}

where
[\gamma]
runs over conjugacy classes of primitive hyperbolic elements

of
\pi_{1}(X,\mathcal{G})
. Precise definition for a graph of groups

and its fundamental group

If

is a subgroup of

\zeta_{\Gamma}{(s)}=\prod_{[\gamma]\in\textrm{P}(\Gamma)}\frac{1}{1-N(\gamma)^{-s}}

Then, it holds that

Meanwhile, if

is the weighted graph associated to

, then following the idea of

(X,w)
given by

Z_{(X,w)}(u)=\prod_{[c]}\frac{1}{1-w(c)u^{\ell(c)}}

where

is the natural weight counting the number of inequivalent pre-images of

of
(X,w)
.
The precise definition of

Let
\textrm{Aut}(\mathcal{T}_{q+1})

The following theorem describes the relation between two zeta functions for centrally rigid subgroups.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be a

In Section

For every

Additionally, we explore a family of graphs of groups introduced in

By investigating the poles of zeta functions, we establish the following theorem. The proof will be given in Section

.

There is a sequence of

We obtain from this Theorem that such a sequence of graphs of groups gives arbitrary small exponential error term when we count the number of geodesics. Given a graph of groups

and its fundamental group

\Gamma
and

is the primitive hyperbolic element underlying

Corollary 1.4

Given any

\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{N_{m}(\mathbf{X})}{q^{m}}=1\quad\textrm{ but }\quad|N_{m}(\mathbf{X})-q^{m}|>(q-\epsilon)^{m}

2
Graphs of groups and tree lattices

In this section, we review the basic setup about graphs of groups and associated weighted graphs. The Bass-Serre theory, being developed by Serre
Se
and the substantial contribution of the subsequent work of Bass
Ba
, deals with the theory of group actions on graphs and their covering theory via the notion of graphs of groups.

2.1
Graphs of groups

A graph
X
consists of a set of vertices
Ver(X)
and a set of oriented edges
Ed(X)
together with maps

\displaystyle Ed(X)\to Ver(X)\times Ver(X),\qquad e\mapsto(s(e),t(e))\,\,(\textrm{source and target})

and

\displaystyle Ed(X)\to Ed(X),\qquad e\mapsto\overline{e}\,\,(\textrm{inverse})

such that
\overline{\overline{e}}=e
,
\overline{e}\neq e
and

for

.

Definition 2.1
.

A graph of groups

is a pair

where

is a graph and

and

attached to each vertex and edge of

and monomorphisms
\phi_{e}\colon G_{e}\to G_{t(e)}
.

Upon choosing a maximal subtree

of

, we can define the fundamental group
\pi_{1}=\pi_{1}(\mathbf{X},T)
as follows. The group
\pi_{1}
is generated by
G_{x}

(x\in Ver(X))
and by elements
g_{e}

(e\in Ed(X))
, subject to the relations

g_{e}\phi_{e}(g)g_{e}^{-1}=\phi_{\overline{e}}(g),\qquad g_{\overline{e}}=g_{e}^{-1}

and
g_{e}=1
for
e\in Ed(T)
.

Given a group action on a graph, the quotient graph has a natural structure of graph of groups. Let
\Gamma
be a group acting on

without inversions of edges (that is,

for all

) and

be the quotient graph

of

and lift it to a subtree

of

. Let

be the representatives of the edges of

whose sources are in

be the vertex of

For each edge

\Gamma_{\gamma_{e}t(f_{e})}=\gamma_{e}\Gamma_{t(f_{e})}\gamma_{e}^{-1},

Theorem 2.1

(

The group

Thus, the pair

2.2
Cuspidal tree lattices

A
tree lattice
is a group
\Gamma
together with an action on a locally finite tree
\mathcal{T}
such that every stabilizer group
\Gamma_{x}
of each vertex
x\in Ver(\mathcal{T})
is finite and

\textrm{covol}(\Gamma\curvearrowright\mathcal{T})\overset{\textrm{def}}{=}\sum_{x\in X}\frac{1}{|\Gamma_{x}|}<\infty

X=\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{T}
.

A (finite or infinite) sequence

of edges in a graph

is called

if
t(e_{i})=s(e_{i+1})
. It is said to be without backtracking if
s(e_{i})\neq t(e_{i+1})
for all
i\geq 1
. A ray

in
\mathcal{T}
is an infinite path without backtracking such that
e_{i}\neq e_{j},\overline{e_{j}}
for all
i\leq j
. Two rays

and

are said to be
equivalent
if they join at some point, that is, if there exist
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
r_{j+k}=s_{j}
for all
j\geq N
. An equivalence class of rays is called an
end
of
\mathcal{T}
. The set
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{T}
of all ends is called the
visual boundary
of
\mathcal{T}
. For a given vertex
x_{0}
and any point
c\in\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{T}
, there exists a unique ray in

that starts at the point
x_{0}
.

Definition 2.2

(
Pa 2
, Theorem 1.1)
.

A tree lattice
\Gamma
acting on
\mathcal{T}
is called

(or

) if the quotient graph of groups
(X,\mathcal{G})
has the following form.

1.

There is a finite subset
C
of
X=\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{T}
such that
X-C
is a union of finitely many rays
r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{c}
.

2.

Each ray
r=\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots\}
in
X-C
satisfies the following: If
s(e_{1})
is adjacent to some vertex of
C
and
e_{i}
’s go towards at ends, then
\Gamma_{s(e_{i})}=\Gamma_{e_{i}}\subset\Gamma_{s(e_{i+1})}
for every

and all these groups
\Gamma_{s(e_{i})}
are finite subgroups of
\Gamma_{\omega}
where
\omega
denotes the end of
\mathcal{T}
represented by a lifting of

.

We recall that

gives the general structure theorem for lattices in

over a non-Archimedean local field

Theorem 2.2

(

.

Let

be a locally compact non-archimedean field,
G

3
Bass-Ihara zeta function and Selberg zeta function of tree lattices

In this section, we define Selberg zeta function of graphs of groups and compare with Bass-Ihara zeta function of weighted graphs. We also present a determinant formula for the zeta function in terms of the edge-adjacency operator.

3.1
Zeta function of tree lattices

Given a path
P=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
in a graph
X
, we call
n
the length of
P
and denote it by
\ell(P)
. A path
P=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
is
closed
if
t(e_{n})=s(e_{1})
. We say two closed paths
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
and
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
are
equivalent
if there is
k\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
such that
e_{i}=f_{i+k}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
. An equivalence class of closed path is called
cycle
. If a cycle
C
satisfies
C\neq D^{f}
for any
f>1
and a closed path
D
in
G
, then
C
is called a
primitive cycle
. Hence, for any cycle
C
, there is a unique primitive cycle
C_{0}
such that
C=C_{0}^{m}
for some
m\geq 1
. A cycle
C=[(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})]
is called
reduced
if it consists only of closed paths without backtracking, that is,
\overline{e_{i}}\neq e_{i+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.

We begin by reviewing the zeta function of finite graphs. For a finite graph
X
, the Ihara zeta function is defined at a complex number
u
, for which
\left|u\right|
is sufficiently small, by

Z_{X}(u)=\prod_{[C]}\frac{1}{1-u^{\ell(C)}}

where the product is taken over all reduced primitive cycles
\left[C\right]
in
X
. The product, being infinite in general, converges to a reciprocal of a polynomial. In fact, it satisfies Ihara determinant formula

Z_{X}(u)=\frac{1}{(1-u^{2})^{-\chi}\det(1-uA+u^{2}Q)},

where
A
is the adjacency operator of the graph,
Q+1
is the valency operator, and
\chi
is the Euler characteristic
|Ver(X)|-\frac{|Ed(X)|}{2}
of the graph
X
.

These zeta functions are generalized to certain infinite graphs. Indeed, the authors in
DK
extended the theory of Ihara zeta functions to cuspidal lattices, and derived a prime geodesic theorem for arithmetic lattices of rank one Lie groups over local fields.

Suppose now that
G
is a group acting on an infinite regular tree
\mathcal{T}
. We may classify elements in

that act without inversions on

, as follows:

(a)

identity

(b)

(c)

\mathcal{T}
(and hence have two fixed points on
\partial_{\infty}\mathcal{T}
)

\ell(g)=\min\{d(x,gx)\colon x\in Ver(\mathcal{T})\}.

Then the subset

and

on

. Here,

of

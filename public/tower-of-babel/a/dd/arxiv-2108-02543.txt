Boundary smoothness conditions for functions in R p ​ ( X ) R^{p}(X)
Stephen Deterding

Abstract
Let X X be a compact subset of the complex plane and let R p ​ ( X ) R^{p}(X) , 2 < p < ∞ 2<p<\infty , denote the closure of the rational functions with poles off X X in the L p L^{p} norm. In this paper we consider three conditions that show how the functions in R p ​ ( X ) R^{p}(X) can have a greater degree of smoothness at the boundary of X X than might otherwise be expected. We will show that two of the conditions are equivalent and imply the third but the third does not imply the other two.

Let

We say that

Let

\displaystyle\text{ for all }f\in R_{0}(X)\}

(B)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{2^{n(t+1)}\gamma(A_{n}(x)\setminus X)}{\phi(2^{-n})}

We will first show that (C

\phi

Theorem 1
.

Let
\phi(r)
be a positive non-decreasing function and let
X
be a compact subset of the plane and let
x\in X
. Let
2<p<\infty
and
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
, and suppose

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{nq}\phi(2^{-n})^{-q}\Gamma_{q}(A_{n}(x)\setminus X)<\infty.

Then there exists
g\in L^{q}(X)
which represents
x
on
R^{p}(X)
such that

\int\dfrac{|g(z)|^{q}dm}{\phi(|z-x|)^{q}}<\infty.

Proof.

We may assume that
X
is a subset of the unit disk and
x=0
without loss of generality. We abbreviate
A_{n}(x)
to
A_{n}
.

Let
T_{n}
be a linear functional defined by
T_{n}(f)=\int_{\partial A_{n}}\frac{f(z)}{z}dz
and let
f
be a rational function with poles off
X
. We can assume that
f
is modified off
X
so that it is continuous, but still analytic in a neighborhood of
X
. Then there exist closed sets
K_{n}\subseteq A_{n}\setminus X
with smooth boundaries such that
f
is analytic outside of
\cup K_{n}
and

\int_{A_{n}\setminus K_{n}}|f|^{p}dm\leq 2\int_{X\cap A_{n}}|f|^{p}dm.

We now bound
T_{n}(f)
. In
[
4
]
, Hedberg constructed smooth functions
P_{m}
such that each
P_{m}=1
on
K_{m}
, has support on
A_{m-1}\cup A_{m}\cup A_{m+1}
, and
\int|\nabla P_{m}|^{q}dm\leq C(\Gamma_{q}(A_{m}\setminus X)+4^{-m})
. (Note that in
[
4
]
these functions are called
\phi_{m}
.) By modifying this construction, we can make it so that
\int|\nabla P_{m}|^{q}dm\leq C(\Gamma_{q}(A_{m}\setminus X)+4^{-m}\phi(2^{-m})^{q})
. Let
P=\sup_{m}P_{m}
so that
P(z)=1
on
\cup K_{m}
. Then it follows from Green’s Theorem and the analyticity of
f
that

\displaystyle\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\int_{\partial A_{m}}\dfrac{f(z)}{z}dz

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\int_{\partial K_{m}}\dfrac{f(z)}{z}dz

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\int_{\partial K_{m}}\dfrac{f(z)P(z)}{z}dz

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\int_{A_{m}\setminus K_{m}}\dfrac{f(z)}{z}\dfrac{\partial P(z)}{\partial\overline{z}}dm,

and thus

T_{n}(f)=\sum_{m=n}^{\infty}\int_{\partial A_{m}}\dfrac{f(z)}{z}dz-\sum_{m=n+1}^{\infty}\int_{\partial A_{m}}\dfrac{f(z)}{z}dz=\int_{A_{n}\setminus K_{n}}\dfrac{f(z)}{z}\dfrac{\partial P(z)}{\partial\overline{z}}dm.

Hence it follows from Holder’s inequality that

\displaystyle|T_{n}(f)|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n+1}\left(\int_{A_{n}\setminus K_{n}}|f(z)|^{p}dm\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{A_{n}\setminus K_{n}}|\nabla P|^{q}dm\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{n}\|f\|_{L_{p}(X\cap A_{n})}\left[\Gamma_{q}(A_{n-1}\setminus X)+\Gamma_{q}(A_{n}\setminus X)+\Gamma_{q}(A_{n+1}\setminus X)+4^{-n}\phi(2^{-n})^{q}\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Thus
T_{n}(f)
is a bounded linear functional on
R^{p}(X\cap A_{n})
. Thus it follows from the Hahn-Banach theorem that there exists
g_{n}\in L^{q}(X)
with support on
A_{n}
such that
T_{n}(f)=\int fg_{n}dm
for all
f
in
R^{p}(X\cap A_{n})
and

\int|g_{n}|^{q}dm\leq C2^{nq}\left[\Gamma_{q}(A_{n-1}\setminus X)+\Gamma_{q}(A_{n}\setminus X)+\Gamma_{q}(A_{n+1}\setminus X)+4^{-n}\phi(2^{-n})^{q}\right].

Now let
\displaystyle g=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}
. Then by the Cauchy integral formula,

\displaystyle f(0)=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\partial A_{n}}\dfrac{f(z)}{z}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int f(z)g_{n}(z)dm

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int f(z)\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}(z)dm

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int fgdm.

Thus
g
represents
0
on
R^{p}(X)
. Finally, because
g_{n}
has support on
A_{n}
,

\displaystyle\int\dfrac{|g(z)|^{q}}{\phi(|z|)^{q}}dm

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\right)^{q}\int\phi(|z|)^{-q}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}(z)\right|^{q}dm

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\right)^{q}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{A_{m}}\phi(|z|)^{-q}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}(z)\right|^{q}dm

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{A_{m}}\phi(|z|)^{-q}|g_{m}(z)|^{q}dm

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{-m})^{-q}\int_{A_{m}}|g_{m}(z)|^{q}dm.

Hence

\displaystyle\int\dfrac{|g(z)|^{q}}{|\phi(z)|^{q}}dm

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{-m})^{-q}2^{mq}\left[\Gamma_{q}(A_{m-1}\setminus X)+\Gamma_{q}(A_{m}\setminus X)+\Gamma_{q}(A_{m+1}\setminus X)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+4^{-m}\phi(2^{-m})^{q}\right]<\infty.

∎

Theorem 2
.

Let
\phi(r)
be an admissible function with associated function
\psi(r)=\frac{r}{\phi(r)}
such that
\phi(0^{+})=0
, and let
2<p<\infty
and
q=\frac{p}{p-1}
. Then there is a compact set
X
containing
0
such that for each
\epsilon>0
the set
\{y\in X:|f(y)-f(0)|\leq\epsilon\phi(|y|)\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}\text{ for all }f\in R_{0}(X)\}
has full area density at
0
, but

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{-n})^{-q}2^{nq}\Gamma_{q}(A_{n}(0)\setminus X)=\infty.

Due to the length of the proof, it will be split up into four lemmas. We first describe the construction of the set
X
. Again we abbreviate
A_{n}(0)
to
A_{n}
. Let
a_{n}=\frac{3}{4}\cdot 2^{-n}
. We can choose a subsequence still denoted
a_{n}
such that
\phi(a_{n})<\frac{1}{2}\phi(a_{n-1})
and

\sum_{n=1}^{j-1}\psi(a_{n})^{-q}<\psi(a_{j})^{-q}.

Let
r_{n}=[n^{-1}a_{n}^{q}\phi(a_{n})^{q}]^{\frac{1}{2-q}}
. Then there exists
M>0
such that for
n\geq M
,
\frac{r_{n}}{a_{n}}<\frac{1}{5}n^{\frac{-1}{2q}}
. For such
n
let
D_{n}
denote the open disk centered at
a_{n}
with radius
r_{n}
; otherwise, let
D_{n}
be the empty set. Let
\Delta
denote the closed unit disk and let
X=\Delta\setminus\cup_{n}D_{n}
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
X
be the set constructed in the previous paragraph. Then

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{-n})^{-q}2^{nq}\Gamma_{q}(A_{n}\setminus X)=\infty.

Proof.

Since the
q
-capacity of a disk of radius
r
is
r^{2-q}
[
1
]
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{-n})^{-q}2^{nq}\Gamma_{q}(A_{n}\setminus X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=M}^{\infty}\phi(2^{-n})^{-q}2^{nq}\Gamma_{q}(A_{n}\setminus X)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=M}^{\infty}\phi(2^{-n})^{-q}2^{nq}n^{-1}a_{n}^{q}\phi(a_{n})^{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=M}^{\infty}\phi(2^{-n})^{-q}2^{nq}n^{-1}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{q}2^{-nq}\phi\left(\frac{3}{4}\cdot 2^{-n}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=M}^{\infty}\phi(2^{-n})^{-q}n^{-1}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{q}\phi\left(\frac{3}{4}\cdot 2^{-n}\right)^{q}.

Then since
\phi(r)=\dfrac{r}{\psi(r)}
and
\psi(r)
is non-decreasing

\displaystyle\sum_{n=M}^{\infty}\phi(2^{-n})^{-q}n^{-1}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{q}\phi\left(\frac{3}{4}\cdot 2^{-n}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=M}^{\infty}\left(\dfrac{2^{-n}}{\psi(2^{-n})}\right)^{-q}n^{-1}\left(\dfrac{3}{4}\right)^{q}\left(\dfrac{\frac{3}{4}\cdot 2^{-n}}{\psi(\frac{3}{4}\cdot 2^{-n})}\right)^{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=M}^{\infty}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{2q}\psi(2^{-n})^{q}n^{-1}\psi\left(\frac{3}{4}\cdot 2^{-n}\right)^{-q}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{2q}\sum_{n=M}^{\infty}n^{-1}=\infty.

Hence

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi(2^{-n})^{-q}2^{nq}\Gamma_{q}(A_{n}\setminus X)=\infty.

∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
d_{n}(z)
denote the distance from
z
to
D_{n}
and let
s_{n}=\frac{1}{5}n^{-\frac{1}{2q}}a_{n}
. Let
E=\bigcup_{n>M}\{z\in A_{n}:d_{n}(z)\geq r_{n}\}
. (See Figure 1). Then
E
has full area density at
0
.

Proof.

Since
\frac{r_{n}}{a_{n}}<\frac{1}{5}n^{\frac{-1}{2q}}
, it follows that
r_{n}<s_{n}
. Let
B(a,r)
denote the disk centered at
a
with radius
r
. Then it follows from the definition of
E
that
B(0,2^{-j})\setminus E
is the union of the disks
B(a_{n},r_{n}+s_{n})
, where the union is taken over
n\geq j
. Thus

\dfrac{m(B(0,2^{-j}))\setminus E)}{m(B(0,2^{-j}))}=\dfrac{\pi\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}(r_{n}+s_{n})^{2}}{2^{-2j}}=\dfrac{\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}(r_{n}+s_{n})^{2}}{\frac{16}{9}a_{j}^{2}}.

However, since
r_{n+1}<\frac{1}{2}r_{n}
and
s_{n+1}<\frac{1}{2}s_{n}
,

\displaystyle\dfrac{m(B(0,2^{-j})\setminus E)}{m(B(0,2^{-j}))}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{-2n}\right)(r_{j}+s_{j})^{2}}{\frac{16}{9}a_{j}^{2}}

\displaystyle<\frac{3}{4}\left(\dfrac{2s_{j}}{a_{j}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{3}{25}j^{-\frac{1}{q}}.

Thus
\dfrac{m(B(0,2^{-j})\setminus E)}{m(B(0,2^{-j}))}\to 0
as

and

has full area density at

.

Lemma 3
.

There exist smooth functions
P_{n}
such that
P_{n}(z)=1
on
D_{n}
,
\int|\nabla P_{n}|^{q}dm\leq C(\Gamma_{q}(D_{n})+16^{-n})
and
P_{n}
is supported on the set
F_{n}=\{z\in A_{n}:d_{n}(z)\leq\frac{s_{n}}{2}\}
.

Proof.

The proof is a modification of a construction of Hedberg
[
4
, pg. 277]
. It follows from the definition of
q
-capacity that there exists smooth functions
\omega_{n}
such that
\omega_{n}=1
on
D_{n}
and
\int|\nabla\omega_{n}|^{q}dm\leq\Gamma_{q}(D_{n})+16^{-n}
. Let
\xi_{n}(z)
be a piecewise linear continuous function of
|z|
such that
\xi_{n}=1
on
D_{n}
,
\xi_{n}=0
outside
F_{n}
and
|\nabla\xi_{n}(z)|\leq 2s_{n}^{-1}
, and let
P_{n}(z)=\xi_{n}(z)\omega_{n}(z)
. Then

\int|\nabla P_{n}(z)|^{q}dm\leq\left(\int\xi_{n}^{q}|\nabla\omega_{n}|^{q}dm+\int\omega_{n}^{q}|\nabla\xi_{n}|^{q}dm\right).

The first integral is bounded above by
\Gamma_{q}(D_{n})+16^{-n}
. To bound the second integral we first note that it follows from the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality (See
[
2
, pg.277]
) that
\left(\int\omega_{n}^{\frac{2q}{2-q}}dm\right)^{\frac{2-q}{2}}\leq C\int|\nabla\omega_{n}|^{q}dm
. Thus by Holder’s inequality

\displaystyle\int\omega_{n}^{q}|\nabla\xi_{n}|^{q}dm

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int\omega_{n}^{\frac{2q}{2-q}}dm\right)^{\frac{2-q}{2}}\left(\int_{F_{n}}|\nabla\xi_{n}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\int|\nabla\omega_{n}|^{q}dm\cdot m(F_{n})^{\frac{q}{2}}s_{n}^{-q}

\displaystyle\leq C(\Gamma_{q}(D_{n})+16^{-n})\cdot\left(r_{n}+\frac{s_{n}}{2}\right)^{q}s_{n}^{-q}

\displaystyle\leq C(\Gamma_{q}(D_{n})+16^{-n})\cdot\left(s_{n}+\frac{s_{n}}{2}\right)^{q}s_{n}^{-q}

\displaystyle=C(\Gamma_{q}(D_{n})+16^{-n})\cdot\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{q}.

Thus
\int|\nabla P_{n}|^{q}dm\leq C(\Gamma_{q}(D_{n})+16^{-n})
.

∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. Then for
N
sufficiently large,

|f(y)-f(0)|\leq\epsilon\phi(|y|)\|f\|_{p}

whenever
y\in A_{N}\cap X^{\circ}\cap E
and
f\in R_{0}(X)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in R_{0}(X)
and
y\in A_{N}\cap X^{\circ}\cap E
. Then it follows from the Cauchy integral formula that

\displaystyle|f(y)-f(0)|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\partial D_{n}}\dfrac{f(z)dz}{z-y}-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\partial D_{n}}\dfrac{f(z)dz}{z}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\partial D_{n}}\dfrac{yf(z)dz}{z(z-y)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|y|\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\partial D_{n}}\dfrac{f(z)dz}{z(z-y)}\right|.

Let
P_{n}
be the smooth functions constructed in Lemma
3
and let
P=\sup_{n}P_{n}
. Then
P(z)=1
on
\cup D_{n}
and hence by Green’s theorem,

\displaystyle|f(y)-f(0)|

\displaystyle\leq|y|\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\partial D_{n}}\dfrac{f(z)P(z)dz}{z(z-y)}\right|

\displaystyle=|y|\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{F_{n}\setminus D_{n}}\dfrac{f(z)\frac{\partial P}{\partial\overline{z}}dm}{z(z-y)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|y|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{F_{n}\setminus D_{n}}\left(a_{n}-r_{n}-\frac{s_{n}}{2}\right)^{-1}\delta_{n}(y)^{-1}|f(z)|(|\nabla P(z)|)dm.

where
\delta_{n}(y)
is the distance from
y
to
F_{n}
. Since
\frac{r_{n}}{a_{n}}<\frac{1}{5}n^{\frac{-1}{2q}}

\left(a_{n}-r_{n}-\frac{s_{n}}{2}\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{5}n^{\frac{-1}{2q}}-\frac{1}{10}n^{\frac{-1}{2q}}\right)a_{n}\geq\frac{7}{10}a_{n},

Next, it follows from Holder’s inequality that

\displaystyle|f(y)-f(0)|

\displaystyle\leq C|y|\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{F_{n}\setminus D_{n}}|f(z)|^{p}dm\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{-q}\delta_{n}(y)^{-q}\int_{F_{n}\setminus D_{n}}|\nabla P(z)|^{q}dm\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C|y|\cdot\|f\|_{p}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{-q}\delta_{n}(y)^{-q}(\Gamma_{q}(D_{n})+16^{-n})\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C|y|\cdot\|f\|_{p}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{-q}\delta_{n}(y)^{-q}(r_{n}^{(2-q)}+16^{-n})\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

We next obtain bounds for

. Recall that
\frac{r_{n}}{a_{n}}<\frac{1}{5}n^{\frac{-1}{2q}}
and

. If

\delta_{n}(y)\geq\left(a_{n}-r_{n}-\frac{s_{n}}{2}\right)-2^{-N}\geq\frac{7}{10}a_{n}-2^{-N}=\frac{21}{40}2^{-n}-2^{-N}\geq\frac{1}{40}2^{-n}=\frac{1}{30}a_{n}.

If

, then

\displaystyle\delta_{n}(y)

\displaystyle\geq 2^{-(N+1)}-\left(a_{n}+r_{n}+\frac{s_{n}}{2}\right)\geq 2^{-(N+1)}-\left(1+\frac{3}{10}n^{\frac{-1}{2q}}\right)a_{n}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{-(N+1)}-\frac{39}{40}2^{-n}\geq\frac{1}{40}2^{-(N+1)}=\frac{1}{60}a_{N}.

Lastly, if

, then
\delta_{N}(y)\geq\frac{s_{N}}{2}
. Now let

\displaystyle|f(y)-f(0)|

\displaystyle\leq C|y|\cdot\|f\|_{p}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{-q}\delta_{n}(y)^{-q}t_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C|y|\cdot\|f\|_{p}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}a_{n}^{-q}\delta_{n}(y)^{-q}t_{n}+a_{N}^{-q}\delta_{N}(y)^{-q}t_{N}+\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{-q}\delta_{n}(y)^{-q}t_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C|y|\cdot\|f\|_{p}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}a_{n}^{-2q}t_{n}+a_{N}^{-q}s_{N}^{-q}t_{N}+\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{-q}a_{N}^{-q}t_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C\phi(|y|)\cdot\|f\|_{p}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}a_{n}^{-2q}t_{n}\psi(a_{N})^{q}+\phi(a_{N})^{-q}s_{N}^{-q}t_{N}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{-q}\phi(a_{N})^{-q}t_{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

The last line follows because
|y|\leq\frac{4}{3}\phi(|y|)\psi(a_{N})
. To complete the proof, we must bound the 3 terms inside the parentheses. We begin by bounding the leftmost term

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}a_{n}^{-2q}t_{n}\psi(a_{N})^{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}a_{n}^{-2q}r_{n}^{(2-q)}\psi(a_{N})^{q}+\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}a_{n}^{-2q}16^{-n}\psi(a_{N})^{q}.

We will need to bound both sums. The first sum simplifies thus.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}a_{n}^{-2q}r_{n}^{(2-q)}\psi(a_{N})^{q}

\displaystyle=\psi(a_{N})^{q}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}a_{n}^{-q}n^{-1}\phi(a_{n})^{q}

\displaystyle=\psi(a_{N})^{q}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}n^{-1}\psi(a_{n})^{-q}.

Now we make use of the fact that
\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{j-1}\psi(a_{n})^{-q}<\psi(a_{j})^{-q}
and
\displaystyle\sum_{n=j}^{N-1}\psi(a_{n})^{-q}<\psi(a_{N})^{-q}
. Thus

\displaystyle\psi(a_{N})^{q}\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}n^{-1}\psi(a_{n})^{-q}

\displaystyle=\psi(a_{N})^{q}\sum_{n=1}^{j-1}n^{-1}\psi(a_{n})^{-q}+\psi(a_{N})^{q}\sum_{n=j}^{N-1}n^{-1}\psi(a_{n})^{-q}

\displaystyle\leq\psi(a_{N})^{q}\sum_{n=1}^{j-1}\psi(a_{n})^{-q}+j^{-1}\psi(a_{N})^{q}\sum_{n=j}^{N-1}\psi(a_{n})^{-q}

\displaystyle\leq\psi(a_{N})^{q}\psi(a_{j})^{-q}+j^{-1}.

To get a bound for the second sum, we note that
a_{n}^{-2q}=\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{-2q}2^{2qn}
. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}a_{n}^{-2q}16^{-n}\psi(a_{N})^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{-2q}\psi(a_{N})^{q}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{(2q-4)n}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{-2q}\psi(a_{N})^{q}\dfrac{2^{(2q-4)}}{1-2^{(2q-4)}}.

Thus we have the following bound for the leftmost term.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}a_{n}^{-2q}t_{n}\psi(a_{N})^{q}\leq\psi(a_{N})^{q}\psi(a_{j})^{-q}+j^{-1}+\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{-2q}\psi(a_{N})^{q}\dfrac{2^{(2q-4)}}{1-2^{(2q-4)}}.

Then by choosing
j
sufficiently large, it follows that

\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}a_{n}^{-2q}t_{n}^{(2-q)}\psi(a_{N})^{q}\to 0

as
N\to\infty
. We next bound the middle term.

\displaystyle\phi(a_{N})^{-q}s_{N}^{-q}t_{N}

\displaystyle=\phi(a_{N})^{-q}s_{N}^{-q}(r_{N}^{2-q}+16^{-N})

\displaystyle=\phi(a_{N})^{-q}5^{q}N^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{N}^{-q}(N^{-1}a_{N}^{q}\phi(a_{N})^{q}+16^{-N})

\displaystyle=5^{q}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+5^{q}\phi(a_{N})^{-q}N^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{N}^{-q}16^{-N}.

Since
\phi(r)=\dfrac{r}{\psi(r)}
,

\displaystyle 5^{q}\phi(a_{N})^{-q}N^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{N}^{-q}16^{-N}

\displaystyle=5^{q}a_{N}^{-2q}\psi(a_{N})^{q}N^{\frac{1}{2}}16^{-N}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3}{4\sqrt{5}}\right)^{-2q}\psi(a_{N})^{q}N^{\frac{1}{2}}2^{(2q-4)N}.

Thus

\phi(a_{N})^{-q}s_{N}^{-q}t_{N}^{2-q}=5^{q}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\frac{3}{4\sqrt{5}}\right)^{-2q}\psi(a_{N})^{q}N^{\frac{1}{2}}2^{(2q-4)N}\to 0

as
N\to\infty
. Finally, we bound the rightmost term.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{-q}\phi(a_{N})^{-q}t_{n}^{(2-q)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{-q}\phi(a_{N})^{-q}r_{n}^{(2-q)}+\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{-q}\phi(a_{N})^{-q}16^{-n}

We will need to bound both sums. To bound the first sum we make use of the property that
\phi(a_{n})<\frac{1}{2}\phi(a_{n-1})
.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{-q}\phi(a_{N})^{-q}r_{n}^{(2-q)}

\displaystyle=\phi(a_{N})^{-q}\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{-q}n^{-1}a_{n}^{q}\phi(a_{n})^{q}

\displaystyle<\phi(a_{N})^{-q}(N+1)^{-1}\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\phi(a_{n})^{q}

\displaystyle<\phi(a_{N})^{-q}(N+1)^{-1}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi(a_{N})^{q}2^{-nq}

\displaystyle=(N+1)^{-1}\dfrac{1}{2^{q}-1}.

The second sum of the rightmost term is bounded in a similar way to the second sum of the leftmost term.

\displaystyle\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{-q}\phi(a_{N})^{-q}16^{-n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{-q}a_{N}^{-q}\psi(a_{N})^{q}16^{-n}

\displaystyle\leq\psi(a_{N})^{q}\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{-2q}16^{-n}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{-2q}\psi(a_{N})^{q}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{(2q-4)n}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{-2q}\psi(a_{N})^{q}\dfrac{2^{(2q-4)}}{1-2^{(2q-4)}}.

Therefore,

\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{-q}\phi(a_{N})^{-q}t_{n}^{(2-q)}\leq(N+1)^{-1}\dfrac{1}{2^{q}-1}+\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^{-2q}\psi(a_{N})^{q}\dfrac{2^{(2q-4)}}{1-2^{(2q-4)}}

and hence

\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_{n}^{-q}\phi(a_{N})^{-q}t_{n}^{(2-q)}\to 0

as
N\to\infty
. Thus all three sums are bounded and tend to
0
as
N\to\infty
. Hence

|f(y)-f(0)|\leq\epsilon\phi(|y|)\|f\|_{p}

for
y\in A_{N}\cap X^{\circ}\cap E
, provided
N
is chosen sufficiently large.

∎

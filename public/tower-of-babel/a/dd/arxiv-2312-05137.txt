Abstract
. Additive perturbations, specifically, matrix Uvarov transformations for matrix orthogonal polynomials, are under consideration. Christoffel–Uvarov formulas are deduced for the perturbed biorthogonal families, along with their matrix norms. These formulations are expressed in terms of the spectral jets of the Christoffel–Darboux kernels.

The polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}[x]
serves as a free bimodule over the ring of matrices
\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
with the canonical basis
\{I_{p},I_{p}x,I_{p}x^{2},\dots\}
, where
I_{p}\in\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
is the identity matrix. Noteworthy free bisubmodules include the sets
\mathbb{C}_{m}^{p\times p}[x]
of matrix polynomials of degree less than or equal to
m
. A basis for
\mathbb{C}_{m}^{p\times p}[x]
, with a cardinality of
m+1
, is given by
\{I_{p},I_{p}x,\dots,I_{p}x^{m}\}
. Since
\mathbb{C}
possesses the invariant basis number (IBN) property, so does
\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
(see
[
rowen
]
). Consequently, as
\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
is an IBN ring, the rank of the free module
\mathbb{C}_{m}^{p\times p}[x]
is unique and equals
m+1.
In other words, any other basis has the same cardinality. The algebraic dual of
\big(\mathbb{C}_{m}^{p\times p}[x]\big)^{*}
consists of homomorphisms
\phi:\mathbb{C}_{m}^{p\times p}[x]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
that, for the right module, take the form

\displaystyle\langle\phi,P(x)\rangle

\displaystyle=\phi_{0}p_{0}+\dots+\phi_{m}p_{m},

\displaystyle P(x)

\displaystyle=p_{0}+\dots+p_{m}x^{m},

where
\phi_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
.
Hence, we can establish an identification between the dual of the right module and the corresponding left submodule. This dual module is a free module with a singular rank, precisely
m+1
, and it possesses a dual basis denoted by
\{(I_{p}x^{k})^{*}\}_{k=0}^{m}
. This basis satisfies

\langle(I_{p}x^{k})^{*},I_{p}x^{l}\rangle=\delta_{k,l}I_{p}.

Similar statements hold for the left module
\mathbb{C}_{m}^{p\times p}[x]
.

Definition 1.1

(Sesquilinear form)
.

A sesquilinear form
\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle
defined on the bimodule
\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}[x]
is a continuous mapping given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cccc}\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle:&\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}[x]\times\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}[x]&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{C}^{p\times p},\\
&(P(x),Q(x))&\mapsto&\left\langle{P(x),Q(y)}\right\rangle.\end{array}

Here,
\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle
has the following properties for any triple
P(x),Q(x),R(x)\in\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}[x]
, with
A,B\in\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}
.

i)

\left\langle{AP(x)+BQ(x),R(y)}\right\rangle=A\left\langle{P(x),R(y)}\right\rangle+B\left\langle{Q(x),R(y)}\right\rangle
,

ii)

\left\langle{P(x),AQ(y)+BR(y)}\right\rangle=\left\langle{P(x),Q(y)}\right\rangle A^{\top}+\left\langle{P(x),R(y)}\right\rangle B^{\top}
.

The reader may have observed that, despite dealing with complex polynomials in a real variable, we have chosen the transpose instead of the Hermitian conjugate. For any pair of matrix polynomials, the sesquilinear form is defined as follows

\displaystyle\left\langle{P(x),Q(y)}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1,\dots,\deg P\\
l=1,\dots,\deg Q\end{subarray}}p_{k}G_{k,l}(q_{l})^{\top},

\displaystyle P(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\deg P}p_{k}x^{k},

\displaystyle Q(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\deg Q}q_{l}x^{l},

where the coefficients are
G_{k,l}=\left\langle{I_{p}x^{k},I_{p}y^{l}}\right\rangle
.

Definition 1.2
.

A matrix of generalized kernels

\displaystyle u_{x,y}:=\begin{bmatrix}(u_{x,y})_{1,1}&\Cdots&(u_{x,y})_{1,p}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
(u_{x,y})_{p,1}&\Cdots&(u_{x,y})_{p,p}\end{bmatrix},

where
(u_{x,y})_{k,l}\in(\mathbb{C}[x,y])^{\prime}
, provides a continuous sesquilinear form with entries given by

\displaystyle\big(\langle P(x),Q(y)\rangle_{u}\big)_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=1}^{p}\big\langle(u_{x,y})_{k,l},P_{i,k}(x)\otimes Q_{j,l}(y)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=1}^{p}\big\langle\mathcal{L}_{u_{k,l}}(Q_{j,l}(y)),P_{i,k}(x)\big\rangle,

where
\mathcal{L}_{u_{k,l}}:\mathbb{C}[y]\to(\mathbb{C}[x])^{\prime}
is a continuous linear operator. This can be condensed into a matrix form. For
u_{x,y}\in(\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}[x,y])^{\prime}=(\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}[x,y])^{*}\cong\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}[\![x,y]\!]
, a sesquilinear form is given by

\displaystyle\langle P(x),Q(y)\rangle_{u}

\displaystyle=\langle u_{x,y},P(x)\otimes Q(y)\rangle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{u}(Q(y)),P(x)\rangle,

where
\mathcal{L}_{u}:\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}[y]\to(\mathbb{C}^{p\times p}[x])^{\prime}
is a continuous linear map.

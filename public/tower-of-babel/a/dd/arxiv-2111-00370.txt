On the tensor product of two oriented quantum algebras Thanks: * Corresponding author
Tianshui Ma *, Haiyan Yang, Tao Yang

Abstract
. In this paper, we give the oriented quantum algebra (abbr. OQA) structures on the tensor product of two different OQAs by using Chen’s weak ℜ \mathfrak{R} -matrix in [J. Algebra 204(1998):504-531]. As a special case, the OQA structures on the tensor product of an OQA with itself are provided, which are different from Radford’s results in [J. Knot Theory Ramifications 16(2007):929-957].

16T05

1.
Introduction

It is well known that the quantum Yang-Baxter equation has wide applications in mathematics and physics and an
\mathfrak{R}
-matrix gives rise to a solution for the quantum Yang-Baxter equation. Algebraic structures related to the quantum Yang-Baxter equation have been extensively studied in the literature recently, see Refs
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
5
,
7
]
. The notion of oriented quantum algebra (abbr. OQA) (this structure can provide the solution for the quantum Yang-Baxter equation) was introduced and motivated from a topological point of view. OQAs account for most known regular isotopy invariants of oriented links, thus OQAs are important for that reason. Quasitriangular Hopf algebras are the important examples of OQAs.

So it is important to construct OQAs for obtaining new invariants. This is the main motivation to write this paper.

In
[
8
]
, Radford gave the OQA structure on the tensor product of an OQA with itself, via an algebra isomorphism of the quantum double
D(A)
with
A\otimes A
under certain condition. In
[
5
]
, Ma and Wang got a generalization of Radford’s results just by observing and analyzing his construction. Let
H
and
H^{\prime}
be two quasitriangular Hopf algebras, Chen got the quasitriangular structures of bicrossed coproducts
H\bowtie^{r}H^{\prime}
by using a so-called weak
\mathfrak{R}
-matrix of
(H,H^{\prime})
(
[
1
]
). In this paper, we follow Chen’s idea about weak
\mathfrak{R}
-matrix and investigate a new approach to obtain some OQA structures.

In this paper, we present a class of OQA structures on the tensor product of two OQAs, which is a generalization of the Chen’s Theorem (see
[
1
, Theorem 2.2]
) and is different from the Radford’s Theorem (see
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
). In Section 3, the OQA structure on the tensor product of two OQAs is derived under a suitable condition (see Theorem
3.6
). Then we can obtain the OQA structure on the tensor product of an OQA with itself (see Theorem
3.7
), which is different from
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
(see Remarks
3.8
and
4.3
). And also, our result generalizes the OQA construction implicit in Chen’s theorem
[
1
, Theorem 2.2]
(see Theorem
3.10
). Finally, in Section 4, several nontrivial concrete examples are given.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, we follow the definitions and terminologies in
[
1
,
7
,
8
]
and all algebraic systems are over a field
K
. Let
C
be a coalgebra. Then we use the Heyneman-Sweedler’s notation for the comultiplication
\Delta(c)=c_{1}\otimes c_{2}
for any
c\in C
. Given a
K
-space
M
, we write
id_{M}
for the identity map on
M
. Let
(H,\mu,\eta)
be an algebra. The opposite algebra

(H,\mu^{op},\eta)
, denoted by
H^{op}
, has multiplication described in terms of that for
H
by
\mu^{op}(a\otimes b)=ba
for all
a,b\in H
.

Let
r=\sum r_{i}\otimes r^{i}\in A\otimes A
. We represent sums of tensors formally as a single tensor. For example we write
r=r_{i}\otimes r^{i}=r_{j}\otimes r^{j}=r_{k}\otimes r^{k}=\cdots
. When
r
is invertible, we will frequently write
r^{-1}=R=R_{l}\otimes R^{l}=R_{m}\otimes R^{m}=R_{n}\otimes R^{n}=\cdots
. We define
r_{12}=r_{i}\otimes r^{i}\otimes 1,r_{13}=r_{i}\otimes 1\otimes r^{i}
and
r_{23}=1\otimes r_{i}\otimes r^{i}
.

Next we recall
[
1
,
8
,
3
]
some basic definitions and results which will be used later.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose that
r=r_{i}\otimes r^{i}\in H\otimes H
is invertible and

D,U
are commuting algebra automorphisms of
H
. Then
(H,r,D,U)
is an
oriented quantum algebra
(abbr. OQA) if the following conditions hold:

\displaystyle(D\otimes I_{H})(r^{-1})\hbox{~and~}(I_{H}\otimes U)(r)\hbox{~are inverses in~}H\otimes H^{op};

(2.1)

\displaystyle(D\otimes D)(r)=r=(U\otimes U)(r);

(2.2)

\displaystyle r_{12}r_{13}r_{23}=r_{23}r_{13}r_{12}.

(2.3)

Formally writing
r=r_{i}\otimes r^{i}=r_{j}\otimes r^{j}=r_{k}\otimes r^{k}
and
r^{-1}=R=R_{l}\otimes R^{l}
, we can
reformulate the preceding axioms:

\displaystyle D(R_{l})r_{i}\otimes U(r^{i})R^{l}=1\otimes 1=r_{i}D(R_{l})\otimes R^{l}U(r^{i});

(2.4)

\displaystyle D(r_{i})\otimes D(r^{i})=r=U(r_{i})\otimes U(r^{i});

(2.5)

\displaystyle r_{i}r_{j}\otimes r^{i}r_{k}\otimes r^{j}r^{k}=r_{j}r_{i}\otimes r_{k}r^{i}\otimes r^{k}r^{j}.

(2.6)

The condition Eq.(
2.3
) has the following equivalent form:

r_{j}\otimes r_{i}\otimes r^{j}r^{i}=R_{l}r_{j}r_{i}\otimes R^{l}r_{k}r^{i}\otimes r^{k}r^{j}.

(2.7)

Remark 2.2
.

Suppose that
(H,r,D,U)
is an OQA. Then
U\otimes D
is an algebra automorphism of
H\otimes H^{op}
. Since
D(R_{l})\otimes R^{l}
and
r_{i}\otimes U(r^{i})
are inverses in
H\otimes H^{op}
, it follows that
(H,r,U,D)
is an OQA. Suppose that
(H^{\prime},r^{\prime},D^{\prime},U^{\prime})
is also an OQA and let
\tau_{H,H^{\prime}}:H\rightarrow H^{\prime}
be defined by
\tau_{H,H^{\prime}}(h\otimes h^{\prime})=h^{\prime}\otimes h
for all
h\in H,h^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}
. Then
(H\otimes H^{\prime},r^{\prime\prime},D\otimes D^{\prime},U\otimes U^{\prime})
is an OQA, called the tensor product of
(H,r,D,U)
and
(H^{\prime},r^{\prime},D^{\prime},U^{\prime})
, where write

r^{\prime\prime}=(id_{H}\otimes\tau_{H,H^{\prime}}\otimes id_{H^{\prime}})(r\otimes r^{\prime})
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose that
(H,r,D,U)
is an OQA and write
r=r_{i}\otimes r^{i}=r_{j}\otimes r^{j}
,
r^{-1}=R=R_{l}\otimes R^{l}=R_{m}\otimes R^{m}
. Then
(H\otimes H,\tilde{\alpha},\tilde{D},\tilde{U})
is an OQA,
where

\tilde{\alpha}=R^{l}r_{i}\otimes R^{m}r_{j}\otimes r^{i}r^{j}\otimes R_{l}R_{m},

(2.8)

\tilde{D}=D\otimes D
and
\tilde{U}=U\otimes U
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
quasitriangular Hopf algebra
is a pair
(H,r)
, where
H
is a Hopf algebra,
r
is an
element in
H\otimes H
satisfying the following conditions:

\displaystyle(\Delta\otimes id)(r)=r_{j}\otimes r_{i}\otimes r^{j}r^{i};

(2.9)

\displaystyle(id\otimes\Delta)(r)=r_{j}r_{i}\otimes r^{i}\otimes r^{j};

(2.10)

\displaystyle\Delta^{cop}(h)r=r\Delta(h);

(2.11)

\displaystyle\varepsilon(r_{i})r^{i}=r_{i}\varepsilon(r^{i})=1,

(2.12)

where
r=r_{i}\otimes r^{i}=r_{j}\otimes r^{j}
.

Remark 2.5
.

In this case,
r
is called a
universal
\mathfrak{R}
-matrix of
H
and
r
is invertible. If
(H,r)
is a quasitriangular Hopf algebra, then the antipode
S_{H}
is bijective and
(H,r,id_{H},S_{H}^{-2})
is an OQA.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
H,H^{\prime}
be bialgebras. An invertible element
r\in H\otimes H^{\prime}
is a
weak
\mathfrak{R}
-matrix of
(H,H^{\prime})
if the following conditions are satisfied:

\displaystyle(\Delta\otimes id)(r)=r_{i}\otimes r_{j}\otimes r^{i}r^{j};

(2.13)

\displaystyle(id\otimes\Delta)(r)=r_{i}r_{j}\otimes r^{j}\otimes r^{i},

(2.14)

where
r=r_{i}\otimes r^{i}=r_{j}\otimes r^{j}
.

Remark 2.7
.

If
H
and
H^{\prime}
are two Hopf algebras with bijective antipodes and
r=r_{i}\otimes r^{i}\in H\otimes H^{\prime}
is a weak
\mathfrak{R}
-matrix of
(H,H^{\prime})
, then

r^{-1}=S_{H}(r_{i})\otimes r^{i}=r_{i}\otimes S_{H^{\prime}}^{-1}(r^{i})

(2.15)

and

r=(S_{H}\otimes S_{H^{\prime}})(r).

(2.16)

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
H
and
H^{\prime}
be two Hopf algebras and
r=r_{i}\otimes r^{i}\in H\otimes H^{\prime}
a weak
\mathfrak{R}
-matrix of
(H,H^{\prime})
( write
r^{-1}=R=R_{l}\otimes R^{l}
), then
H\bowtie^{r}H^{\prime}
(
=H\otimes H^{\prime}
as an algebra) with the following coproduct
\bar{\Delta}
and antipode
\bar{S}
is a Hopf algebra, where

\bar{\Delta}(h\otimes h^{\prime})=h_{1}\otimes r^{i}h^{\prime}_{1}R^{l}\otimes r_{i}h_{2}R_{l}\otimes h^{\prime}_{2}

and

\bar{S}(h\otimes h^{\prime})=r^{-1}(S_{H}(h)\otimes S_{H^{\prime}}(h^{\prime}))r,

\forall h\in H,h^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}
. We call this Hopf algebra
bicrossed coproduct Hopf algebra
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Assume that
(H,p)
and
(H^{\prime},p^{\prime})
are quasitriangular Hopf algebras, where
p=p_{i}\otimes p^{i}\in H\otimes H,~p^{\prime}=p^{\prime}_{j}\otimes p^{\prime j}\in H^{\prime}\otimes H^{\prime}
. Let
r=r_{k}\otimes r^{k}\in H\otimes H^{\prime}
be a weak
\mathfrak{R}
-matrix of
(H,H^{\prime})
( write
r^{-1}=R=R_{l}\otimes R^{l}
). Set
[p,p^{\prime}]=r_{k}p_{i}\otimes p^{\prime}_{j}R^{l}\otimes p^{i}R_{l}\otimes r^{k}p^{\prime j}
, then
(H\bowtie^{r}H^{\prime},[p,p^{\prime}])
is a quasitriangular Hopf algebra.

3.
New oriented quantum algebra structures

In this section, we will give the OQA structure on the tensor product of two OQAs.

3.1.
OQA nonuple

Definition 3.1
.

Let
H
and
H^{\prime}
be algebras. Suppose that the elements
p\in H\otimes H,~p^{\prime}\in H^{\prime}\otimes H^{\prime}
and
r\in H\otimes H^{\prime}
are invertible. Let
D,U:H\rightarrow H
be commuting algebra automorphisms,
D^{\prime},U^{\prime}:H^{\prime}\rightarrow H^{\prime}
be commuting algebra automorphisms, and
p,p^{\prime},r,D,U,D^{\prime},U^{\prime}
satisfy the following conditions:

\displaystyle D(R_{l})r_{k}\otimes U^{\prime}(r^{k})R^{l}=1_{H}\otimes 1_{H^{\prime}}=r_{k}D(R_{l})\otimes R^{l}U^{\prime}(r^{k});

(3.1)

\displaystyle(D\otimes D^{\prime})(r)=r=(U\otimes U^{\prime})(r);

(3.2)

\displaystyle p_{i}r_{k}\otimes p^{i}r_{s}\otimes r^{k}r^{s}=r_{k}p_{i}\otimes r_{s}p^{i}\otimes r^{s}r^{k};

(3.3)

\displaystyle r_{k}r_{s}\otimes r^{k}p^{\prime}_{j}\otimes r^{s}p^{\prime j}=r_{s}r_{k}\otimes p^{\prime}_{j}r^{k}\otimes p^{\prime j}r^{s},

(3.4)

where
p=p_{i}\otimes p^{i},~p^{\prime}=p^{\prime}_{j}\otimes p^{\prime j}
,
r=r_{k}\otimes r^{k}=r_{s}\otimes r^{s}
and
r^{-1}=R=R_{l}\otimes R^{l}
. Then we call
(H,H^{\prime},p,p^{\prime},r,D,U,D^{\prime},U^{\prime})
an
OQA nonuple
.

.

(1) When
(H,p,D,U)
is an OQA,
(H,H,p,p,p,

D,U,D,U)
is an OQA nonuple.

D^{\prime})
is

and
D^{\prime},U^{\prime}
are commuting algebra automorphisms, respectively.

Remarks

(3.5)

\displaystyle r_{k}\otimes p^{\prime}_{j}\otimes r^{k}p^{\prime j}=R_{l}r_{s}r_{k}\otimes R^{l}p^{\prime}_{j}r^{k}\otimes p^{\prime j}r^{s};

Example 4.4
.

Let
H=K\langle g,x\rangle
be an algebra with multiplication
g^{2}=1,x^{2}=0,gx=-xg
. Then
D=id_{H}:H\rightarrow H
and
U:H^{\prime}\rightarrow H^{\prime}
such that
U(1)=1,U(g)=g,U(x)=-x,U(gx)=xg
are commuting algebra automorphisms of
H
and
(H,p,D,U)
is an OQA with

p=p^{1}\otimes p^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1\otimes 1+1\otimes g+g\otimes 1-g\otimes g)+\frac{\nu}{2}(x\otimes x+x\otimes gx+gx\otimes gx-gx\otimes x)

for
\forall~\nu\in K
.

Let
H^{\prime}=K\langle t\rangle
such that
t^{2}=1
, then
D^{\prime}=U^{\prime}=id_{H^{\prime}}
are commuting algebra automorphisms of
H^{\prime}
and
(H^{\prime},p^{\prime},D^{\prime},U^{\prime})
is
an OQA with

p^{\prime}=p^{\prime 1}\otimes p^{\prime 2}=\frac{1}{2}(1\otimes 1+1\otimes t+t\otimes 1-t\otimes t).

Assume that
r=r^{1}\otimes r^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(1\otimes 1+1\otimes t+g\otimes 1-g\otimes t)\in H\otimes H^{\prime}
, and a direct verification shows that
(H,H^{\prime},p,p^{\prime},r,D,U,D^{\prime},U^{\prime})
is an
OQA nonuple.

By a straightforward computation and Theorem
3.7
, we get

\displaystyle\tilde{\alpha}=\tilde{\alpha}^{1}\otimes\tilde{\alpha}^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(1\otimes 1\otimes 1\otimes 1+1\otimes 1\otimes g\otimes t+g\otimes t\otimes 1\otimes 1-g\otimes t\otimes g\otimes t)

\displaystyle+\frac{\nu}{2}(x\otimes t\otimes gx\otimes 1+x\otimes t\otimes x\otimes t+gx\otimes 1\otimes gx\otimes 1-gx\otimes 1\otimes x\otimes t),

for
\forall~\nu\in K
.

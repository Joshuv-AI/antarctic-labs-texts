Abstract
. In this note, we obtain a classification result for positive solutions to the critical p p -Laplace equation in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} with n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 and p > p n p>p_{n} for some number p n ∈ ( n 3 , n + 1 3 ) p_{n}\in\left(\frac{n}{3},\frac{n+1}{3}\right) such that p n ∼ n 3 + 1 n p_{n}\sim\frac{n}{3}+\frac{1}{n} , which improves upon a similar result obtained by Ou [ Ou ] under the condition p ≥ n + 1 3 p\geq\frac{n+1}{3} .



1.
Introduction and main result

We consider positive, weak solutions
u\in W^{1,p}_{\loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\cap L^{\infty}_{\loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
to the critical
p
-Laplace equation

-\Delta_{p}u=u^{p^{*}-1}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.1)

where
n\geq 2
,
1<p<n
,
\Delta_{p}:=\divergence\left(\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)
is the
p
-Laplace operator and
p^{*}:=np/\left(n-p\right)
is the critical Sobolev exponent.

Well-known solutions to (
1.1
) are the functions

u_{\mu,x_{0}}\left(x\right):=\left(\frac{n^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{n-p}{p-1}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\mu^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}{\mu^{\frac{p}{p-1}}+\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}}\right)^{\frac{n-p}{p}}\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.2)

where
\mu>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. As was shown by Rodemich
[
Rod
]
, Aubin
[
Aub
]
and Talenti
[
Tal
]
, these functions realize the equality in the optimal Sobolev inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Guedda and Véron
[
GueVer
]
obtained that the functions defined in (
1.2
) are the only positive, radially symmetric solutions to (
1.1
). In the case where
p=2
, Caffarelli, Gidas and Spruck
[
CafGidSpr
]
(see also Chen and Li
[
ChenLi
]
) used the moving plane method to obtain that these functions are in fact the only positive solutions of (
1.1
). This classification result was later extended by Damascelli and Ramaswamy
[
DamRam
]
to the case of solutions with sufficiently fast decay at infinity with
1<p<2
, and in a series of papers by Damascelli, Merchán, Montoro and Sciunzi
[
DamMerMonSci
]
, Vétois
[
Vet
]
and Sciunzi
[
Sci
]
to the case of solutions in
D^{1,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
for all
p\in\left(1,n\right)
. We mention in passing that a similar classification result was also obtained by Esposito
[
Esp
]
for solutions with finite mass of the critical
n
-Laplace equation, in which case the nonlinearity is of exponential type.

More recently, Ciraolo, Figalli and Roncoroni
[
CirFigRon
]
used a strategy based on integral estimates to extend the classification of positive
D^{1,p}
-solutions to a class of anisotropic
p
-Laplace-type equations in convex cones (see also the survey article by Roncoroni
[
Ron
]
on this topic). In the case where
p=2
, this type of approach can be traced back to the work of Obata
[
Oba
]
on the conformal transformations of the sphere. An approach of this type was then used by Catino, Monticelli and Roncoroni
[
CatMonRon
]
to obtain new classification results for positive, weak solutions to (
1.1
) which are not a priori in
D^{1,p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. In particular, Catino, Monticelli and Roncoroni
[
CatMonRon
]
managed to obtain the complete classification of positive, weak solutions to (
1.1
) in the case where
n=2
or [
n=3
and
3/2<p<2
]. The method was recently improved by Ou
[
Ou
]
who managed to extend this result to the case where
n\geq 3
and
p\geq\left(n+1\right)/3
.

In this note, we obtain the following extension of Catino, Monticelli and Roncoroni
[
CatMonRon
]
and Ou’s
[
Ou
]
results:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
n\geq 4
and
p_{n}<p<n
, where

p_{n}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{8}{5}&&\text{if }n=4\\
&\frac{4n+3-\sqrt{4n^{2}+12n-15}}{6}&&\text{if }n\geq 5.\end{aligned}\right.

Then every positive, weak solution
u\in W^{1,p}_{\loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\cap L^{\infty}_{\loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
to (
1.1
) is of the form (
1.2
), i.e.
u\equiv u_{\mu,x_{0}}
for some
\mu>0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

It is easy to see that

\frac{n}{3}<p_{n}<\frac{n+1}{3}\quad\forall n\geq 4

and

p_{n}\sim\frac{n}{3}+\frac{1}{n}\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

The main difficulty in our proof in the case where
p<\left(n+1\right)/3
is to obtain a priori integral estimates with an exponent on the gradient which is larger than
p
. This can be seen for example by looking at the formula (
2.24
) in our proof, where the exponent on the function g (defined in (
2.2
)) is less than
1
for small
\varepsilon>0
if and only if
p>\left(n+1\right)/3
. While the former case can be achieved by using some rather straightforward estimates (see Lemma
2.1
), the case where
p_{n}<p<\left(n+1\right)/3
requires a little more work. In this case, by using the integral identity in Lemma
2.3
, we manage to obtain the key estimate (
2.37
), which compares two integrals with different exponents on the gradient and from which we manage to derive our classification result. The case where
p\leq p_{n}
remains open. In this case, the exponent on the gradient in the right-hand side of (
2.37
) becomes too large for us to conclude. The situation appears to be even more problematic when
p<n/3
since the exponent on the gradient in the right-hand side of (
2.37
) then becomes greater than the exponent in the left-hand side.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
u\in W^{1,p}_{\loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\cap L^{\infty}_{\loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
be a positive, weak solution of (
1.1
). Results by DiBenedetto
[
DiB
]
and Tolksdorf
[
Tol
]
give that
u\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
for some
\alpha\in\left(0,1\right)
. Furthermore, as was shown by Antonini, Ciraolo and Farina
[
AntCirFar
]
(see also the references therein for previous results), the critical set
Z:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,\left|\nabla u\left(x\right)\right|=0\right\}
has measure zero,
u\in W^{2,2}_{\loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash Z\right)
,
\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla u\in W^{1,2}_{\loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and
\left|\nabla u\right|^{p-2}\nabla^{2}u\in L^{2}_{\loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

Following the approach developped by Catino, Monticelli and Roncoroni
[
CatMonRon
]
and Ou
[
Ou
]
(see also the previous work by Ciraolo, Figalli and Roncoroni
[
CirFigRon
]
), we define the function

v:=u^{-\frac{p}{n-p}}.

(2.1)

The equation (
1.1
) can then be rewritten as

\Delta_{p}v=g:=\frac{n\left(p-1\right)}{p}v^{-1}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p}+\left(\frac{p}{n-p}\right)^{p-1}v^{-1}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2.2)

Furthermore, it follows from the above-mentioned regularity properties of
u
that
v\in C^{1,\alpha}_{\loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\cap W^{2,2}_{\loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash Z\right)
,
\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\nabla v\in W^{1,2}_{\loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
and
\left|\nabla v\right|^{p-2}\nabla^{2}v\in L^{2}_{\loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
p\in\left(1,n\right)
,
r\in\left[0,p\right]
,
q<\left(np-n+p\right)/p
,

,
u\in W^{1,p}_{\loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\cap L^{\infty}_{\loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
be a positive, weak solution of (
1.1
) and
v
be the function defined in (
2.1

\int_{B_{R}\left(0\right)}v^{-q}\left|\nabla v\right|^{r}\leq C\left\{\begin{aligned} &R^{n-q}&&\text{if }r\leq q<\frac{np-n+p}{p}\\
&R^{n-\frac{pq-r}{p-1}}&&\text{if }q<r\end{aligned}\right.

(2.3)

for some constant
C=C\left(n,p,q,r\right)>0
.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Let
r\in\left[0,p\right]
and
q<\left(np-n+p\right)/p
. We refer to Ou
[
Ou
]
*Lemma 3.1 for the proof of (
2.3
) when [
q\geq 0
and
r=0
] or [
q\geq p
and
r=p
]. In the case where
q\geq r
and
0<r<p
, Hölder’s inequality gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{-q}\left|\nabla v\right|^{r}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{-q-\sigma\left(p-r\right)}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{r}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{-q+\sigma r}\right)^{\frac{p-r}{p}},

(2.4)

where

\sigma:=\max\left(\frac{p-q}{p-r},0\right),

so that

q+\sigma\left(p-r\right)=\max\left(p,q\right)\in\left[p,\frac{np-n+p}{p}\right)

(2.5)

and

q-\sigma r=\min\left(\frac{p\left(q-r\right)}{p-r},q\right)\in\left[0,\frac{np-n+p}{p}\right).

(2.6)

It follows from (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) (together with the above-mentioned proof by Ou
[
Ou
]
*Lemma 3.1) that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{-q-\sigma\left(p-r\right)}\left|\nabla v\right|^{p}\leq CR^{n-q-\sigma\left(p-r\right)}

(2.7)

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{-q+\sigma r}\leq CR^{n-q+\sigma r}

(2.8)

for some constant
C=C\left(n,p,q,r\right)>0
. By combining (
2.4
), (
2.7
) and (
2.8
), we then obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}v^{-q}\left|\nabla v\right|^{r}\leq C\left(R^{n-q-\sigma\left(p-r\right)}\right)^{\frac{r}{p}}\left(R^{n-q+\sigma r}\right)^{\frac{p-r}{p}}=CR^{n-q}

for some constant
C=C\left(n,p,q,r\right)>0
. We now consider the case where
q<r
and
0\leq r\leq p
. In this case, by observing that
\Delta_{p}u\leq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we obtain (see Serrin and Zou
[
SerZhou
]
*Lemma 2.3)

u\left(x\right)\geq C\left|x\right|^{-\frac{n-p}{p-1}},\text{ i.e. }v\left(x\right)\leq C^{-\frac{p}{n-p}}\left|x\right|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{1}\left(0\right)

(2.9)

for some constant
C=C\left(n,p\right)>0
. It follows from (
2.9
) that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\left(0\right)}v^{-q}\left|\nabla v\right|^{r}

\displaystyle\leq C^{-\frac{p\left(r-q\right)}{n-p}}R^{\frac{p\left(r-q\right)}{p-1}}\int_{B_{R}\left(0\right)}v^{-r}\left|\nabla v\right|^{r}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}R^{\frac{p\left(r-q\right)}{p-1}+n-r}

\displaystyle=C^{\prime}R^{n-\frac{pq-r}{p-1}},

for some constant
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}\left(n,p,q,r\right)>0
. This ends the proof of Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Let

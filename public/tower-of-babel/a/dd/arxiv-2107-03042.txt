Process-optimized phase covariant quantum cloning
Chloe Kim, Eric Chitambar

Abstract
After the appearance of the no-cloning theorem, approximate quantum cloning machines (QCMs) has become a well-studied subject in quantum information theory. Among several measures to quantify the performance of a QCM, single-qudit fidelity and global fidelity have been most widely used. In this paper we compute the optimal global fidelity for phase-covariant cloning machines via semi-definite programming optimization, thereby completing a remaining gap in the previous results on QCMs. We also consider optimal simulations of the cloning and transpose cloning map, both by a direct optimization and by a composition of component-wise optimal QCMs. For the cloning map the composition method is sub-optimal whereas for the transpose cloning map the method is asymptotically optimal.



I
Introduction

The famous no-cloning theorem tells us that
there exists no quantum operation that copies an arbitrary quantum state

Park1970
;
Dieks1962
;
Wootters1982
. Despite this impossibility, the notion of approximate quantum cloning machines was introduced in 1996
Buzek1996
, and the topic has received extensive development over the past twenty-five years. The inability to universally copy states is a fundamental signature of “quantumness” that has significant applications. In particular, the no-cloning theorem is essential for ensuring security in cryptographic tasks like quantum key distribution (QKD)
Bennett1984
. A general overview of quantum cloning and its relevance to quantum information processing can be found in review papers such as
Scarani2005
;
Fan2014
.

An ideal
N\to M
quantum cloner is a non-linear map

\rho^{\otimes N}\mapsto\rho^{\otimes M}

(1)

for any density matrix
\rho
of some
d
-dimensional system
\mathsf{S}
, and a quantum cloning machine (QCM) approximates this operation via a physically-realizable quantum
channel. That is, an
N\to M
QCM is a completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) map
\mathcal{E}
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\left(\rho^{\otimes N}\right)=\sigma^{\mathsf{S}_{1}\cdots\mathsf{S}_{M}}\approx\rho^{\otimes M}.

(2)

Different criteria are used to categorize QCMs. For instance, the QCM is called symmetric if
\sigma^{\mathsf{S}_{i}}:=\tr_{\overline{\mathsf{S}}_{i}}\sigma
is the same reduced state for all
\mathsf{S}_{i}
, where
\tr_{\overline{\mathsf{S}}_{i}}
indicates a partial trace over all subsystems but
\mathsf{S}_{i}
. An economic QCM is one that restricts to maps using no ancillary system other than the
\mathsf{S}_{i}

output systems
Buscemi2005
. Finally, universality of a QCM means that it clones all the input states equally well, and a QCM is optimal
if the cloning process is simulated optimally with respect to a given figure of merit
Scarani2005
.

The most commonly used figure of merit for cloning is the average single-qudit fidelity, which measures the distance between the input state
\rho
and the reduced state of each output
copy
\sigma^{\mathsf{S}_{i}}
. Another possible measure evaluates the average global fidelity between the QCM output state
\sigma^{\mathsf{S}_{1}\cdots\mathsf{S}_{M}}
and the ideal output state

\rho^{\otimes M}
. In
Koniorczyk2013a
, this global fidelity was called

process fidelity
, and the authors used the semidefinite programming (SDP)
techniques introduced in
Audenaert2002a
to optimize the process fidelity
for cloning one qubit (
d=2
). The goal of this paper is to extend this result by computing the optimal process fidelity for cloning qudits (
d>2
). In particular, we consider the cloning of maximally coherent pure states having the form

\left|\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{k=1}^{d}e^{i\theta_{k}}\left|k\right\rangle,

(3)

where
\theta
denotes
d
-tuple of angles
(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{d})
,
\theta_{i}\in\left[0,2\pi\right)
. For
d=2
, this represents the family of equatorial states on the Bloch sphere. Up to a unitary transformation, the four BB84 states
\{\left|0\right\rangle,\left|1\right\rangle,\left|\pm\right\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\left|0\right\rangle\pm\left|1\right\rangle)\}
belong to this family
Bennett1984
.

A (non-universal) QCM whose domain is restricted to the phase-encoded input states
\left|\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right\rangle
is called a phase-covariant quantum cloner.
Bruß et al. first studied the cloning of BB84 states
Bruss2000a
,
and they showed that the optimal map that clones BB84 states also optimally
clones any arbitrary phase-covariant states on the Bloch sphere.
They obtained an optimal single-qubit fidelity of

\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{8}}
, which is greater than that of the universal
cloner (
\frac{5}{6}
).
Subsequent work has been conducted on the phase-covariant cloning of

1\to M
qubits

,

1\to 2
qutrits
DAriano2003
;
DAriano2001

Cerf2002b

qudits

Buscemi2007

qubits

N\to M
qudits for
M=kd+N

Buscemi2007

optimizing over the single qudit fidelity (See
Buscemi-PhD

II
Notations and definitions

Let
B(\mathcal{H})
denote the set of bounded operators on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
Let
\mathcal{D}\subset B(\mathcal{H})
be the set of density operators, i.e. positive trace-one
elements,
\mathcal{D}_{\text{pure}}\subset\mathcal{D}
the set of pure states, i.e. rank-one density operators,
and
\mathcal{D}_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\subset\mathcal{D}_{\text{pure}}
the set of states
|\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\rangle\langle\text{\boldmath$\theta$}|
having the form of (
3
).
The fidelity
Jozsa-1994a
of any two
\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{D}
is given by

F(\rho,\sigma):=\left(\tr\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}\sigma\sqrt{\rho}}\right)^{2}.

(4)

Suppose
\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}:B(\mathcal{H})\to B(\mathcal{K})
is an “ideal” map that is not
necessarily a quantum channel (not even necessarily linear), i.e,
completely-positive and trace-preserving (CPTP). We are interested in
approximating
\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}
by a physically-realizable quantum channel
\mathcal{E}
,
or possibly approximating just the action of
\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}
on some restricted
set of inputs
S
. There are various approaches to quantifying how well
\mathcal{E}

approximates
\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}
, and here we consider the process fidelity.

Definition 1
.

Let
S\subset\mathcal{D}
be the set of states that are generated by the action of a compact group
G
,
and let
\mu
be the Haar measure induced by
G
.
The
process fidelity
between an arbitrary ideal map
\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}
and a CPTP map
\mathcal{E}
is defined as

F_{\text{proc}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}},\mathcal{E}|S):=\int_{S}F(\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}(\sigma),\mathcal{E}(\sigma))d\mu(\sigma).

(5)

If
\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}
maps pure state to pure states and
S\subset\mathcal{D}_{\text{pure}}
,
then the process fidelity is equivalent to

F_{\text{proc}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}},\mathcal{E}|S)=\int_{S}\tr\Big[\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}(\sigma)\mathcal{E}(\sigma)\Big]\;d\mu(\sigma).

(6)

A key feature of this expression is that the integrand is linear in
\mathcal{E}(\sigma)
. This will allow us to exploit symmetry properties of the Haar measure below. On the other hand, note that
\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}
need not be linear, such as with the cloning map
\rho\mapsto\rho\otimes\rho
.

Definition 2
.

Let
\text{CPTP}(\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K})
be the set of all quantum channels mapping
B(\mathcal{H})
to
B(\mathcal{K})
. The
optimal process fidelity
of a map
\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}:B(\mathcal{H})\to B(\mathcal{K})
is the maximum process fidelity
over all possible quantum channels, i.e.

F^{*}_{\text{proc}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}|S):=\max_{\mathcal{E}\in\text{CPTP}(\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K})}\;F_{\text{proc}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}},\mathcal{E}|S).

(7)

Given an orthonormal basis
\{\left|i\right\rangle\}_{i=1}^{d}
for
\mathcal{H}
, the Choi-Jamiołkowski isomorphism
Jamiolkowski-1972a
;
Choi-1975a
establishes an equivalence between every channel
\mathcal{E}\in\text{CPTP}(\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K})
and an operator
J(\mathcal{E})\in B(\mathcal{H})\otimes B(\mathcal{K})
,

\mathcal{E}\leftrightarrow J(\mathcal{E}):=\sum_{i,j}|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes\mathcal{E}(|i\rangle\langle j|).

(8)

The operator
J(\mathcal{E})
is called
the Choi matrix of
\mathcal{E}
and the action of
\mathcal{E}
can be directly expressed
in terms of
J(\mathcal{E})
as

\mathcal{E}(\rho)=\tr_{\mathcal{H}}(J(\mathcal{E})(\rho^{T}\otimes\mathds{1})),

(9)

where
\mathds{1}
is the identity on
\mathcal{K}
and
\rho^{T}
is the transpose of
\rho
in some fixed basis. When
S\subset\mathcal{D}_{\text{pure}}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}:\mathcal{D}_{\text{pure}}\to\mathcal{D}_{\text{pure}}
, the process fidelity can be expressed in terms of the Choi matrix as

\displaystyle F_{\text{proc}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}},\mathcal{E}|S)=\int_{S}\tr\Big[J(\mathcal{E})\rho^{T}\otimes\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}(\rho)\Big]\;d\mu(\rho).\hskip 20.00003pt

(10)

To compute
F_{\text{proc}}^{*}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}},\mathcal{E}|S)
, the problem then reduces to finding the Choi matrix
J(\mathcal{E})
that maximizes Eq. (
10
).

The

\mathcal{T}(X):=\int U(\text{\boldmath$\theta$})\otimes U(-\text{\boldmath$\theta$})^{\otimes 2}\left(X\right)U(-\text{\boldmath$\theta$})\otimes U(\text{\boldmath$\theta$})^{\otimes 2}\;d\mu(\text{\boldmath$\theta$}).

We exploit the symmetric properties of

(15)

\pi\in S_{d}

Next, let

For example,

V_{(23)}\left|\varphi_{1}\right\rangle\otimes\left|\varphi_{2}\right\rangle\otimes\left|\varphi_{3}\right\rangle=\left|\varphi_{1}\right\rangle\otimes\left|\varphi_{3}\right\rangle\otimes\left|\varphi_{2}\right\rangle.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{E}
is a quantum channel with Choi matrix

If we define the average Choi map for the channel

where we have used the facts that

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(X_{2}-X_{8}),\hskip 9.24994ptP_{E}=\frac{1}{2}(X_{6}-X_{9}).

(48)

When
d\geq 3
all the coefficients are positive, and when

d=2
it can be easily checked that
\hat{Z}\geq 0
. This shows that
Z
is also
positive, and therefore
\frac{2d-1}{d^{2}}
is indeed the optimal solution.
∎

Note that one can calculate the single-qudit fidelity using a map
\mathcal{E}
that is optimal for the process fidelity. Doing so leads to a single-qudit fidelity of

(d+1)/(2d-1)
, which is strictly smaller than the optimal value
FanEtAl2003
.
This means that the map yielding optimal process fidelity does not
give optimal single-qudit fidelity unlike the universal cloners
Werner1998
.

IV
Transposition cloning

IV.1
Optimal phase-covariant transposition

The transposition map is another famous non-CP map. The ideal operation
of transposition map is given by

\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}:|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\mapsto|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|^{T},\hskip 18.49988pt\forall\rho\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{pure}},

(49)

It has been shown that the optimal fidelity for approximate transposition
channel of arbitrary pure states is
\frac{2}{d+1}

Buscemi2003
. What happens if we
restrict the input states to phase covariant states? In this case, Eq. (
49
) takes the form

\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}:|\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\rangle\langle\text{\boldmath$\theta$}|\mapsto|\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\rangle\langle\text{\boldmath$\theta$}|^{T}=|-\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\rangle\langle-\text{\boldmath$\theta$}|,\hskip 18.49988pt\forall\rho\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}},

(50)

where
-\text{\boldmath$\theta$}=(-\theta_{1},\cdots,

-\theta_{d})
. To compute the optimal process fidelity in this restricted case, we
can use the same SDP analysis. The calculation yields to
F_{\text{proc}}=\frac{2}{d}
, and the Choi operator of an optimal map is given as

\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E})=\frac{1}{d-1}\sum_{i\neq j}(|ij\rangle\langle ij|+|ij\rangle\langle ji|).

(51)

Hence we see that restricting to maximally coherent states
\left|\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right\rangle
only yields an improved process fidelity of
\frac{2}{d}
versus
\frac{2}{d+1}
in the universal case. These have the same asymptotic scaling, yet in the qubit case perfect transposition can be achieved only for equatorial states since the map reduces to a
\pi
-rotation on the Bloch sphere.

IV.2
Process-optimized universal transposition cloning map

We can also define the ideal transposition cloning operation as the map that outputs
two copies of the transpose of the input state, i.e.

\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}:\rho\mapsto\rho^{T}\otimes\rho^{T},\hskip 18.49988pt\forall\rho\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{pure}}.

(52)

Here we compute the process fidelity of the
1\to 2
transposition
cloning map for an arbitraty pure state as its input. Let
\mu
be the induced Haar measure for the pure states
\mathcal{D}_{\text{pure}}
and
\sigma
be
the Haar measure on the set of unitary operators
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
.
Define the average Choi operator

\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E}):=\int_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})}U^{\dagger\otimes 3}J(\mathcal{E})U^{\otimes 3}\;d\sigma(U).

(53)

Notice that by the Haar invariance we get

\displaystyle F_{\text{proc}}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}},\mathcal{E}|\mathcal{D}_{\text{pure}})

(54)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{D}_{\text{pure}}}\tr\left[J(\mathcal{E})(\rho^{T})^{\otimes 3}\right]\;d\mu(\rho)

\displaystyle=\tr\left[\int_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})}J(\mathcal{E})(U|0\rangle\langle 0|U^{\dagger})^{\otimes 3}\;d\sigma(U)\right]

(55)

\displaystyle=\tr\left[\int_{\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})}U^{\dagger\otimes 3}J(\mathcal{E})U^{\otimes 3}\;d\sigma(U)\left|0\right\rangle\left\langle 0\right|^{\otimes 3}\right]

(56)

\displaystyle=\tr\left[\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E})\left|0\right\rangle\left\langle 0\right|^{\otimes 3}\right].

(57)

To find the optimal process fidelity, we exploit the fact that
\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E})

is
U^{\otimes 3}
-invariant. We then obtain the following proposition as a direct consequence
of the results from work by Eggeling and Werner
Eggeling2001
.

Proposition 1
.

The optimal process fidelity of the
1\to 2
universal transpose cloning map is
6/(d^{2}+3d+2)
.

Proof.

Eggeling and Werner’s have shown that any quantum state
\rho
satisfying
U^{\otimes 3}\rho U^{\dagger\otimes 3}=\rho
can be uniquely
expressed as

\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E})=\sum_{i\in\{+,-,0,1,2,3\}}c_{i}R_{i},\hskip 9.24994ptc_{i}\in\mathbb{R},

(58)

where

\displaystyle R_{+}=\frac{1}{6}(\mathds{1}+V_{(12)}+V_{(23)}+V_{(31)}+V_{(123)}+V_{(132)})

(59)

\displaystyle R_{-}=\frac{1}{6}(\mathds{1}-V_{(12)}-V_{(23)}-V_{(31)}+V_{(123)}+V_{(132)})

(60)

\displaystyle R_{0}=\frac{1}{3}(2\cdot\mathds{1}-V_{(123)}-V_{(321)}),

(61)

\displaystyle R_{1}=\frac{1}{3}(2\cdot\mathds{1}-V_{(31)}-V_{(12)}),

\displaystyle R_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(V_{(12)}-V_{(31)}),\;R_{3}=\frac{i}{\sqrt{3}}(V_{(123)}-V_{(321)}).

(62)

Furthermore, it satisfies the conditions (i)
c_{+},c_{-},c_{0}\geq 0
, (ii)
c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}+c_{3}^{2}\leq c_{0}^{2}
, and (iii)
\tr[\rho R_{+}]+\tr[\rho R_{-}]+\tr[\rho R_{-}]=1
. Let
\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E})
be the average Choi operator defined as (
53
).
Then
\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E})
is invariant under
U^{\otimes 3}
action, and
the preceding statement holds except replacing (iii) with

\tr_{23}[R_{+}\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E})+R_{-}\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E})+R_{0}\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E})]=\mathds{1}.

(63)

Suppose
\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E})=\sum c_{k}R_{k}
.
Then the objective function to maximize becomes

\displaystyle\tr\left[\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E})\left|0\right\rangle\left\langle 0\right|^{\otimes 3}\right]=c_{+},

(64)

while the constraints
\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E})\geq 0
is equivalent to
c_{+},c_{-},c_{0}\geq 0

and
c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}+c_{3}^{2}\leq c_{0}^{2}
and the trace condition
\tr\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E})=d

is equivalent to

\frac{d^{2}+3d+2}{6}c_{+}+\frac{d^{2}-3d+2}{6}c_{-}+\frac{2(d^{2}-1)}{3}c_{0}=1.

(65)

Solving this linear programming gives

c_{-}=c_{0}=c_{1}=c_{2}=c_{3}=0,\hskip 18.49988ptc_{+}=\frac{6}{d^{2}+3d+2}.

(66)

∎

Eggeling and Werner use Schur-Weyl duality to characterize

U^{\otimes 3}
-invariant positive matrices, and details
of the proof are in
Eggeling2003
and
Eggeling2001
.

IV.3
Process-optimized phase-covariant transposition cloning map

We next compare the previous result with the same type of cloner except with the domain further restricted to
\mathcal{D}_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}
. The ideal map for phase covariant
transposition cloner is given by

\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}:\left|\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right\rangle\left\langle\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right|\mapsto\left|-\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right\rangle\left\langle-\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right|^{\otimes 2}\hskip 18.49988pt\forall\left|\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right\rangle\left\langle\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right|\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}.

(67)

Different from the cloning case, the average Choi operator is defined as

\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E}):=\frac{1}{\left\lvert S_{d}\right\rvert\left\lvert S_{3}\right\rvert}\sum_{\pi\in S_{d}}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{3}}V_{\sigma}U_{\pi}^{\otimes 3}\mathcal{T}(J(\mathcal{E}))U_{\pi}^{\dagger\otimes 3}V_{\sigma}^{\dagger},

(68)

where the twirling operation is given by

\mathcal{T}(X):=\int U(-\text{\boldmath$\theta$})^{\otimes 3}\left(X\right)U(\text{\boldmath$\theta$})^{\otimes 3}\;d\mu(\text{\boldmath$\theta$}).

(69)

The positivity and trace condition of
\widetilde{J}
remains the same and its invariance under the
permutations of the basis vector also remains the same. The main difference is that

\widetilde{J}
is invariant under
U(\text{\boldmath$\theta$})^{\otimes 3}
instead of
U(-\text{\boldmath$\theta$})\otimes U(\text{\boldmath$\theta$})^{\otimes 2}
,
and it is also invariant under permuting any subsystems instead of just 2 and 3.
Despite the difference, how we solve the optimal process fidelity is
analogous to that of the cloning case. Here we just state the result as a theorem
and include the proof in the appendix for interested readers.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}
be given by Eq. (
67
).
Then, the optimal process fidelity of
\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}
is

F_{\text{proc}}^{*}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}|\mathcal{D}_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})=\begin{cases}3/4&d=2\\
6/d^{2}&d\geq 3.\end{cases}

(70)

IV.4
Phase-covariant hybrid transposition cloning

Now consider a hybrid transposition cloning map

\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}:\left|\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right\rangle\left\langle\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right|\mapsto\left|\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right\rangle\left\langle\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right|\otimes\left|-\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right\rangle\left\langle-\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right|\hskip 18.49988pt\forall\left|\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right\rangle\left\langle\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right|\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}.

(71)

It is easy to see that the symmetries of the process fidelity
between the optimal map and the above ideal map are identical to that of the
cloning case, except we permute the system 1 and 3.
In other words, we can reuse the constraints (i)-(iii) in Lemma
1
to
construct the average Choi map for the hybrid cloning
and make a modification for (iv) to be
\tr_{12}(\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{ideal}}))=\mathds{1}
,
instead of taking the partial trace over system 2 and 3. In fact,

U(-\text{\boldmath$\theta$})\otimes U(\text{\boldmath$\theta$})^{\otimes 2}
invariance guarantees the partial trace
over any two system of
\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E})
to be a scalar multiple of
\mathds{1}
.
Hence, we can conclude that the process fidelity of the hybrid map is
(2d-1)/d^{2}
. Furthermore,
if we let
\mathcal{E}_{\text{hybrid}}
denote an optimal approximation of the hybrid transposition cloner (phase-covariant) and
\mathcal{E}_{\text{cloner}}
an optimal approximation of the
1\to 2
cloner (phase-covariant), then

\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{hybrid}})=V_{(13)}\widetilde{J}(\mathcal{E}_{\text{clone}})V_{(13)}.

(72)

It is interesting that the two maps

\displaystyle\left|\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right\rangle\left\langle\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right|

\displaystyle\mapsto\left|\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right\rangle\left\langle\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right|\otimes\left|-\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right\rangle\left\langle-\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right|

\displaystyle\left|\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right\rangle\left\langle\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right|

\displaystyle\mapsto\left|\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right\rangle\left\langle\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right|\otimes\left|\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right\rangle\left\langle\text{\boldmath$\theta$}\right|

(73)

can be approximated with the same process fidelity. One might not expect this since the second can be obtained from the first by applying a transpose on the first system, and as computed above, the transposition itself has a process fidelity of
2/d
. Hence a composition of maps yields a highly non-optimal approximation of the
1\to 2
phase-covariant cloner. We make this comparison more explicitly in the next section.

Modular building of QCMs

In this section we consider a modular approach of simulating some non-physical process by breaking the latter down into smaller parts and then simulating each of those. We have just observed that a

V
Conclusion

We have shown that the optimal process fidelity of the
1\to 2

phase covariant-cloner is
\frac{2d-1}{d^{2}}
. The obtained channel yields its
single-qudit fidelity of
(d+1)/(2d-1)
, which is less than the previously known result
by
FanEtAl2003
. Conversely, the cloner by
FanEtAl2003
also does not yield the optimal process fidelity. This means that the optimal phase-covariant cloners do not coincide with two different
fidelity measures, as it is the case for the universal cloner.
We also defined a transpose cloning map, and showed that the optimal process fidelity
for transpose cloning of arbitrary pure states is
6/(d^{2}+3d+2)
using the results from

Eggeling2001
. In comparison, the optimal fidelity for transpose cloning of phase-covariant states is
6/d^{2}
.

Future work in this direction would include finding an explicit formula for
1\to M

phase-covariant cloners. This will require a representation-theoretic approach
involving representations of
S_{M}
with additional positivity and phase covariant
structure. In general, the
1\to M
phase covariant cloner is interesting for
applications in phase estimation. We expect that the modular approach presented in
Fig.
1
may also be helpful in simulating such a map and other
multi-system maps like it.

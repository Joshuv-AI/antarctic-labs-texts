Linear actions of ℤ / p × ℤ / p \mathbb{Z}/p\times\mathbb{Z}/p on S 2 ​ n − 1 × S 2 ​ n − 1 S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1}
Jim Fowler and Courtney Thatcher

Abstract
. For an odd prime p p , we consider free actions of ( ℤ / p ) 2 (\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2} on S 2 ​ n − 1 × S 2 ​ n − 1 S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1} given by linear actions of ( ℤ / p ) 2 (\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2} on ℝ 4 ​ n \mathbb{R}^{4n} . Simple examples include a lens space cross a lens space, but k k -invariant calculations show that other quotients exist. Using the tools of Postnikov towers and surgery theory, the quotients are classified up to homotopy by the k k -invariants and up to homeomorphism by the Pontrjagin classes. We will present these results and demonstrate how to calculate the k k -invariants and the Pontrjagin classes from the rotation numbers.



1.
Introduction

There is a free, linear
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
action on
S^{2n-1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
given by
\gamma(x_{1},\dots,x_{j})=(e^{2\pi ir_{1}/p}x_{1},\dots,e^{2\pi ir_{j}/p}x_{j})
. The resulting quotient space is a called a lens space
L=L(p;r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
and the
r_{i}
are called rotation numbers. These spaces are well-known and well-studied. More generally, one can define a free, linear action of
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
on a product of
two
spheres,
S^{2n-1}\times S^{2m-1}
, by assigning a set of rotation numbers for each sphere separately. The classification of the resulting quotient spaces can be found in
[
14
]
.

Work has also been done on which groups besides
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
can act freely on a product of spheres, but less is known about the classification of the quotient spaces associated to those groups known to act freely. In
[
6
]
, free actions of
(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2}
on
S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1}
are classified up to homotopy equivalence. In this paper, we consider free, linear, spherewise actions of
(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2}
on
S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1}
with
n>1
and with
p
an odd prime, and classify the resulting quotient spaces up to piecewise linear or topological homeomorphism. To be more precise about the “spherewise linear” actions under consideration, our classification follows a definition from Ray
[
11
]
in which
(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2}
acts linearly on each sphere separately with the action being free on at least one of the spheres. One example of such an action is that of a lens space cross a lens space,
L(p;r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\times L(p;r^{\prime}_{1},\dots,r^{\prime}_{n})
, where the first
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
acts on the first sphere and the second
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
factor acts on the second sphere.

There are, however, many more possibilities than lens space cross lens space. In Section
2
, we build those more general examples from the representation theory of
(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2}
. Then in Section
3
we recall the classification up to homotopy from
[
6
]
but recast for the specific case of linear spherewise actions, followed by the homeomorphism classification in Section
4
. The paper concludes with an application of the classification theorem presented in Section
5
.

2.
Constructions

Throughout this paper, we assume
n>1
.
The quotient space resulting from the free, linear
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
action on
S^{2n-1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
given by
\gamma(x_{1},\dots,x_{j})=(e^{2\pi ir_{1}/p}x_{1},\dots,e^{2\pi ir_{j}/p}x_{j})
is a called a generalized lens space, often denoted by
L=L(p;r_{1},\dots,r_{n})
. We often conflate a group action with its quotient. The
r_{i}
are called rotation numbers, and the homotopy, simple homotopy, and homeomorphism types of generalized lens spaces are determined by various combinations of products of rotation numbers. We now generalize this type of action to the case of
(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2}
acting on
S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1}
.

2.1.
Notation

Let
\Gamma
be the group
(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2}
, where
p\neq 2
is a
prime. The goal is to consider free actions of
\Gamma
on

S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1}
.

Let
R=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n},r^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,r^{\prime}_{n})
and

Q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n},q^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,q^{\prime}_{n})
be elements of
(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2n}
so
that the span of
R
and
Q
yield a
(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2}
inside
(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2n}
. The parameters
r_{1},\ldots r_{n}
and
r^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,r^{\prime}_{n}
and
q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}
and
q^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,q^{\prime}_{n}
are called “rotation
numbers” in analogy with the case of a lens space. To get an action on a product of spheres, note that the unit
(2n-1)
-sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}
gives rise to a product of spheres
S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1}
in the product
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and then
R
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n}
preserving these spheres via

\displaystyle R\cdot(z,z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(r,r^{\prime})\cdot(z,z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(r,r^{\prime})\cdot(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},z^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,z^{\prime}_{n})

\displaystyle=\left(e^{2\pi ir_{1}/p}z_{1},\ldots,e^{2\pi ir_{1}/p}z_{n},e^{2\pi ir^{\prime}_{1}/p}z^{\prime}_{1},\ldots e^{2\pi ir^{\prime}_{n}/p}z^{\prime}_{n}\right),

and similarly for
Q
. As a result, the group

(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2}\cong\langle R,Q\rangle
acts on
S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1}
. This action may not be free, but when the resulting action
is
free, we call these the

standard linear examples.
Classifying these examples is the goal of this paper; we will not consider other possibilities for
(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2}
actions on
S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1}
.

The tiniest bit of representation theory reveals that this
construction involving
4n
rotation numbers captures every free
linear action of
(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2}
on
S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

A representation of
(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\times\mathbb{R}^{2n}

preserving the decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{4n}
into

\mathbb{R}^{2n}\times\mathbb{R}^{2n}
is equivalent to a standard linear
example.

Proof.

Recall that “equivalent” for
(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2}
-representations
V
and
W
means there is an equivariant isomorphism of vector spaces
V\to W
.

By virtue of its being abelian,
\Gamma
’s irreducible complex
representations are one-dimensional. Let
\chi
be a character
of an irreducible complex representation. Since the map

x\mapsto x^{2}
is a bijection and since
p\neq 2
, the
Frobenius-Schur indicator of
\chi
is

\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\chi(\gamma^{2})=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\chi(\gamma)=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if $\chi$ is trivial,}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Consequently, a real irreducible representation of
\Gamma
is
either the trivial representation or a complex representation

[
12
, page 108]
. In other words, a nontrivial real
irreducible representation has character
\chi+\bar{\chi}
for
some complex irreducible representation with character
\chi
. Then
for
\Gamma=(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2}
generated by
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
, there are
integers
r
and
q
, not both zero, so that

\displaystyle\chi(1,0)

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi ri/p},

\displaystyle\chi(0,1)

\displaystyle=e^{2\pi qi/p},

and consequently
\chi+\bar{\chi}
is the character of the real
two-dimensional representation for which
(1,0)
acts on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by
rotating through the angle
2\pi r/p
and
(0,1)
by the angle

2\pi q/p
. Write
W_{r,q}
for this representation.

To finish, suppose
\Gamma:=\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
and
\Gamma
acts on

\mathbb{R}^{2n}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2n}
preserving the decomposition, so the representation decomposes as
V_{1}\oplus V_{2}

with
\dim V_{1}=\dim V_{2}=2n
. Then by Maschke, the
representation on
V_{i}
decomposes into a sum of irreducible real
representations. Each real representation is either trivial or

W_{r,q}
for some
r,q
. After gathering together any trivial
representations in pairs as
W_{0,0}
, we conclude that every linear
representation of
\Gamma
has the form

W_{r_{1},q_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus W_{r_{n},q_{n}}\oplus W_{r^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime}_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus W_{r^{\prime}_{n},q^{\prime}_{n}},

i.e., the form given at the beginning of
Section
2
.
∎

For convenience, define

W_{R,Q}:=W_{r_{1},q_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus W_{r_{n},q_{n}}\oplus W_{r^{\prime}_{1},q^{\prime}_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus W_{r^{\prime}_{n},q^{\prime}_{n}},

for the pair of
2n
-tuples
R=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n},r^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,r^{\prime}_{n})

and
Q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n},q^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,q^{\prime}_{n})
. Also note that
Proposition
2.1
generalizes to
describe linear representations of
(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{m}
on
(S^{2n-1})^{m}
.

As a warning, note again that the action given by these linear
representations need not be a free action. There is a
criterion, however, which characterizes when the action is free.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
B_{ij}
be the codimensional two plane in the
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
-vector
space
(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2n}
consisting of tuples

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n})
where
x_{i}=0
and
x^{\prime}_{j}=0
.
A standard linear example is free exactly when the two-dimensional
plane
\langle R,Q\rangle\subset(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2n}
only intersects any
of the
B_{ij}
at the origin.

Proof.

Let
W_{R,Q}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2n}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2n}
be a representation of

\Gamma
as above. The action on
S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1}

associated to
W_{R,Q}
is free exactly when, for all vectors

v=(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2n}
with both
v_{1}\neq 0

and
v_{2}\neq 0
, the vector
v
has trivial stabilizer. Stated the
other way, this means that for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the
restriction of
W_{R,Q}
from
\Gamma
to
\langle\gamma\rangle

decomposes as a sum of representations—but there is a trivial
representation in the decomposition of at most one term of

\mathbb{R}^{2n}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

Let
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\in(\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2}
. Then the
two-dimensional real representation
W_{r,q}
restricted to

\langle\gamma\rangle
is the representation on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by which

\gamma
acts by rotation through an angle of

2\pi(\gamma_{1}r+\gamma_{2}q)/p
. So the action is free
exactly when, for all nontrivial
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
, there are
some
i
’s so that

\gamma_{1}r_{i}+\gamma_{2}q_{i}\equiv 0\mod p,

or some
j
’s so that

\gamma_{1}r^{\prime}_{j}+\gamma_{2}q^{\prime}_{j}\equiv 0\mod p,

but no pair
(i,j)
which satisfies both conditions. This is
exactly the condition given in the lemma.
∎

In analogy with the lens space case, when the action given by these linear representations is free, we write the quotient as
L(p,p;R,Q)
.

3.
Homotopy classification

The homotopy type of a CW complex in which the action of
\pi_{1}
on all homotopy groups is trivial is determined by its homotopy groups and a sequence of cohomology classes called
k
-invariants. In the lens space case of free
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
actions on
S^{2n-1}
, all of the quotients for fixed
p
and
n
have the same homotopy groups, hence the homotopy type is determined by the
k
-invariants. It turns out that the first nontrivial
k
-invariant of a lens space is the product of its rotation numbers times a cohomology class in
H^{n+1}(\mathbb{Z}_{/p};\mathbb{Z})
, and it is the only
k
-invariant necessary to determine the homotopy type
[
5
]
. It is also the case (cf.
[
6
]
) that the first nontrivial
k
-invariant determines the homotopy type of the
L(p,p;R,Q)
.

In this section, we start with the cohomology ring for
K(\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p},1)
, then we describe the
k
-invariant to provide the homotopy classification. A torsion calculation reveals that the homotopy and simple homotopy classifications coincide.

3.1.
The first
k
-invariant and the homotopy type

The first nontrivial
k
-invariant for a
(2n-1)
-dimensional lens space with rotation numbers
r_{1},\dots,r_{n}
is

k(L)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(r_{i}a)\in H^{2n}(\mathbb{Z}_{/p};\mathbb{Z})

where
a\in H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{/p};\mathbb{Z})
is the generator
[
5
]
. We think of the
(r_{i}a)
as “rotation classes” and then the
k
-invariant is just the cup product of the rotation classes. The first nontrivial
k
-invariant in the case of linear
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
action on a product of equidimensional odd spheres can be defined similarly.

To begin, we provide the integral cohomology of the fundamental group, i.e., the cohomology of
K(\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p},1)
, as the generators are needed to describe the first nontrivial
k
-invariant. The ring structure is well known and can be found in
[
6
]
,
[
3
]
, and
[
9
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The integral cohomology ring of
K(\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p},1)
is

H^{\star}(K(\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p},1);\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}[a,b,c]/(pa,pb,pc,c^{2})

where
|a|=|b|=2
and
|c|=3
.

More specifically, the isomorphism in Proposition
3.1
is chosen so that
a
and
b
correspond to the standard basis
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
of
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
. There is a copy of
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\subset\mathbb{Z}[a,b]/(pa,pb)
contained in the cohomology ring, and an automorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\to\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
gives rise to an automorphism
H^{\star}(\varphi)
which, when restricted to the copy of
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
, can be identified with
\varphi
.

Just as the first
k
-invariant for a lens space is given by the product of the rotation numbers modulo
p
, the first nontrivial
k
-invariant in the case of
L(p,p;R,Q)
is the product of rotation classes in
H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p};\mathbb{Z})
.

Lemma 3.2

(Lemma 5.1 in
[
6
]
)
.

Let
L=L(p,p;R,Q)
and suppose
p>n
. Then
k(L)\in H^{2n}(\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p});\mathbb{Z}^{2})
is

\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}(r_{i}a+q_{i}b),\prod_{i=1}^{n}(r^{\prime}_{i}a+q^{\prime}_{i}b)\right),

where
a
and
b
are generators of
H^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p};\mathbb{Z})
as in Proposition
3.1
.

Combining this with Theorem 3.3 in
[
6
]
, we have the following result.

Proposition 3.3

(Theorem 3.3 in
[
6
]
)
.

Assume the prime
p>3
satisfies
p>n+1
. For free linear actions of
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
on

S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1}
with a specified identification of the quotient’s fundamental group with
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
, the quantity

k(L)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}(r_{i}a+q_{i}b),\prod_{i=1}^{n}(r^{\prime}_{i}a+q^{\prime}_{i}b)\right)\in H^{2n}(\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p};\mathbb{Z}^{2}),

modulo automorphisms of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}

Changing the specified identification of the fundamental group with
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
amounts to applying the same automorphism to
a
and
b
, as in the discussion following Proposition
3.1
.

We compare Proposition
3.3
to the
corresponding statement for
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
actions on
S^{n}
. In the lens
space case, the homotopy classification (with a specified generator of
\pi_{1}
) boils down to the
product of the rotation classes, i.e., rotation numbers, up to

\mathbb{Z}

(1)

t^{n}r_{1}\dots r_{n}\equiv\pm r^{\prime}_{1}\dots r^{\prime}_{n}\pmod{p},

where

\mathbb{Z}_{/p}

, and

and

are the rotation numbers corresponding to two lens spaces.
This “numeric” formula might be contrasted with the cohomological perspective presented in Proposition

. The rotation numbers are now described as rotation classes; the power

accounts for a change of identification of
\pi_{1}
with
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}

which would be played by replacing

and

\mathbb{Z}^{2}

3.2.
Simple homotopy classification

In the lens space case, the simple homotopy type is determined by the rotation numbers. Specifically,
L(p;r_{1},\dots,r_{n})\simeq_{s}L(p;r_{1}^{\prime}\dots r_{n}^{\prime})
if and only if for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
and permutation
\sigma
,
r_{i}\equiv kr^{\prime}_{\sigma(i)}
for all
i

[
10
]
.

In the case of
L(p,p;R,Q)
, because the quotients are even dimensional, the homotopy classification and the simple homotopy classification coincide.

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose
f:X\to Y
is a homotopy equivalence between two of
(S^{2n-1}\times S^{2m-1})/G
’s, where
n\leq m
, and
G
is a finite abelian group that acts freely on
S^{2n-1}\times S^{2m-1}
and trivially on the cohomology of
S^{2n-1}\times S^{2m-1}
. If
\Wh(G)
is torsion-free, then
f
is a simple homotopy equivalence.

The proof carries through almost exactly the same as the proof for Proposition 2 in
[
14
]
. We include a version here for completeness.

Proof.

The spaces
X
and
Y
are both simple Poincaré complexes since they are
both manifolds, hence they are finite, connected, CW
complexes with fundamental classes
[X]
and
[Y]
, respectively,
and chain homotopy equivalences
\phi_{X}:C^{2n+2m-2-*}(X)\to C_{*}(X)
and

\phi_{Y}:C^{2n+2m-2-*}(Y)\to C_{*}(Y)
satisfying
\tau(\phi_{X})=0
and

\tau(\phi_{Y})=0
. Here
\tau
, the torsion of the chain equivalence, vanishes by the fact these are
simple
Poincaré complexes.

Similar to the work in
[
4
]

with
\pi_{1}X\cong G
finite and acting trivially on the cohomology of the universal cover
\tilde{X}
,
we have the chain-homotopy commutative diagram

\textstyle{C^{2n+2m-2-*}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\hskip 24.0pt\phi_{X}}
\textstyle{C_{*}(X)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{*}}
\textstyle{C^{2n+2m-2-*}(Y)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\hskip 24.0pt\phi_{Y}}
\textstyle{C_{*}(Y)}

and so we have

\displaystyle 0=\tau(\phi_{Y})=\tau(f_{*}\circ\phi_{X}\circ f^{*})

\displaystyle=\tau(f_{*})+\tau(\phi_{X})+\tau(f^{*})

\displaystyle=\tau(f_{*})+\tau(f^{*})=\tau(f)+(-1)^{2n+2m-2}\overline{\tau(f)},

where
\overline{\tau(f)}
is the result of applying the involution on
\Wh(\pi_{1}Y)
to

Since

is even and the involution on

is trivial when

]
,

As

.
∎

For
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
actions on
S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1}
, provided

the action on cohomology is trivial, and so we immediately get the following result.

.

For
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
with

Proof.

Note
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
is a finite, torsion abelian group that acts trivially on cohomology of

. Since
\Wh(\Gamma)=\Wh(\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p})
is torsion-free
[
2
]
, the result follows.
∎

.

Suppose
X_{1}
and

4.
Homeomorphism classification

For
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
and
X
a quotient space obtained from a free, linear action of
\Gamma
on
S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1}
as described above, the simple structure set
S^{\Cat,s}(X)
, for
\Cat
being

or

, is determined by the
p
-localized Pontrjagin classes. In this section we calculate the Pontrjagin classes explicitly, determine the set of normal invariants, and them determine the full classification.

4.1.
Classification by characteristic classes

Again
\Gamma
acts on
S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1}
via a
decomposition-preserving orthogonal representation on
V\oplus V
.
Let
\alpha:\Gamma\to SO(V\oplus V)
be this representation. In
the special case
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}

representations of this abelian group with
\dim V=2n
refines the

\alpha
to

\Gamma\to SO(2)^{n}\times SO(2)^{n}\subset SO(4n).

This description can be related to the above description: inside each

SO(2)

\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
, so the map
\alpha
amounts to a
linearization of the above description in terms of
R
and
Q
,
which are both vectors of
2n
elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
.

By constructing

appropriately, regard

(S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1})/\Gamma

BG
, and
therefore build the map

B\alpha|_{(S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1})/\Gamma}:(S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1})/\Gamma\to BSO(4n)

The main theorem in
[
13
]
implies that

T(S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1}/\Gamma)\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2}

is a
(4n)
-dimensional vector bundle over

The total Pontrjagin class in

To understand the possible actions of

In general, for a group

4.3.
Normal invariants

We first need a couple of lemmas. We note that they are similar to lemmas in

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
and that
Y
is a

\Cat
-manifold for
\Cat=\PL
or
\Top
and with a free
\Gamma
-action such that
\Gamma
acts
trivially on
H^{\star}(Y;\mathbb{Z}[1/p])
. Then

[Y/\Gamma,G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat]\left[1/p\right]\cong[Y,G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat]\left[1/p\right].

Proof.

Let
\pi:Y\to Y/\Gamma
be the projection and let
\pi_{*}:H_{*}(Y;\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}])\to H_{*}(Y/\Gamma;\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}])
be the induced map on homology. The transfer gives a map going the other way:
tr:H_{*}(Y/\Gamma;\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}])\to H_{*}(Y;\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}])
.
Then
tr\circ\pi_{*}:H_{*}(Y;\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}])\to H_{*}(Y;\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}])
is multiplication by
|\Gamma|
because
\Gamma
acts trivially on
H_{\star}(Y;\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}])
. Consequently,
\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}tr\circ\pi_{*}=id
and we also have that
\pi_{*}\circ\frac{tr}{|\Gamma|}=id
. Since
Y
is finite dimensional we see that there is an isomorphism between
H_{\star}(Y;\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}])
and
H_{\star}(Y/\Gamma;\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}])
, and by Poincaré duality, we obtain an isomorphism between
H^{*}(Y;\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}])
and
H^{\star}(Y/\Gamma;\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}])
.

As
G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat
is a spectrum, maps into the spaces of
G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat
are naturally equivalent to a generalized cohomology theory, i.e.
E^{n}(X)\cong

[X,G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat_{n}]
. It follows from the Atiyah-Hirzebruch spectral sequence that
H^{*}(Y;E_{*}(*;\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}]))\cong H^{*}(Y/\Gamma;E_{*}(*;\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}]))
and
[Y,G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat[\frac{1}{p}]]\cong[Y/\Gamma,G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat[\frac{1}{p}]]
.

∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Suppose
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}_{/p}\times\mathbb{Z}_{/p}
acts freely on
S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1}
. Then for
\Cat
being
\Top
or
\PL
,

\left[(S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1})/\Gamma,G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat\right]\left[1/p\right]\cong\mathbb{Z}_{/2}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
,

\displaystyle\left[(S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1})/\Gamma,G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat\right]\left[1/p\right]

\displaystyle\cong\left[S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1},G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat\right]\left[1/p\right]

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{2n-1}\left(G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat\left[1/p\right]\right)

\displaystyle\oplus\pi_{2n-1}\left(G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat\left[1/p\right]\right)\oplus\pi_{4n-2}\left(G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat\left[1/p\right]\right).

To identify maps out of the product
S^{2n-1}\times S^{2n-1}
with the given sum of homotopy groups, we need to verify that the Whitehead products vanish; this follows from the fact that
G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat
is an path-connected H-space and that localizations of such spaces are path-connected H-spaces. The odd-dimensional homotopy groups of
G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat
are trivial, so the localized group
\pi_{2n-1}\left(G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat\left[1/p\right]\right)
also vanishes. Since
4n-2\equiv 2\pmod{4}
and since
\pi_{4n-2}\left(G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat\right)=\mathbb{Z}_{/2}
, we have
\pi_{4n-2}\left(G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat\left[1/p\right]\right)=\mathbb{Z}_{/2}
and the conclusion follows.
∎

Lemma 4.5

4.4.
The Structure Set

Recall that the surgery exact sequence for
X=L(p,p;Q,R)
becomes

0\to\mathcal{S}^{\Cat,s}(X)\to[X,G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat]\to\Sigma\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{/2}.

From Lemma
4.5
, we have that the right most map in the surgery exact sequence above is
[X,G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat]\cong\mathbb{Z}_{/2}\oplus H^{4i}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{/p})\to\Sigma\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{/2}
. Both
G/\hskip-1.5pt\Top
and
G/\hskip-1.5pt\PL
have H-space structures that make this map into a homomorphism. Since
\Sigma
is torsion-free, and
H^{4i}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{/p})
is trivial or
p
-torsion for all

, all of the
p
-torsion in

must come from the structure set.

Consider the map
X\to S^{4n-2}
given by sending everything but a disk to a point. This induces a commutative diagram

\textstyle{[S^{4n-2},G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{[X,G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

By the Kerviare-Arf invariant,
\pi_{4n-2}(G/\hskip-1.5pt\Cat)
maps isomorphically into the

-group of the trivial group, and we see that the
\mathbb{Z}_{/2}
in the set of normal invariants maps isomorphically onto the
\mathbb{Z}_{/2}
in

From this discussion, we conclude that
S^{\Cat,s}(X)=\bigoplus_{i}H^{4i}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{/p})
, and the classification is determined by the Pontrjagin classes. This yields Theorem

Theorem 4.6
.

Suppose

and
p>n+1
, and following Proposition
3.3

5.
Applications and Open Questions

Kwasik–Schultz
[
7
]
classified squares of 3-dimensional lens spaces up to diffeomorphism: for a prime
p>3
and rotation numbers
r
and
q
, there is a diffeomorphism

L(p;1,r)\times L(p;1,r)\cong L(p;1,q)\times L(p;1,q).

The machinery of this paper can be used to provide a homeomorphism in that case, and also extend the classification to
products of
different
lens spaces.

Theorem 5.1
.

For a prime
p>3
and rotation numbers
r_{1}
,
r_{2}
,
q_{1}
,
q_{2}
such that
\pm(r_{1}r_{2})/(q_{1}q_{2})
is a quadratic residue mod
p
, there
is a homeomorphism

L(p;1,r_{1})\times L(p;1,r_{2})\cong L(p;1,q_{1})\times L(p;1,q_{2})
.

Conversely, if there is such a homeomorphism, then the above condition on rotation numbers is satisfied.

Proof.

Let’s first show that they are homotopy equivalent; this was done with somewhat more machinery in
[
6
]
but for completeness, here we present a proof relying only on Proposition
3.3
. In the notation of Section
2
,

\displaystyle L(p;1,r_{1})\times L(p;1,r_{2})

\displaystyle=L(p,p;R_{1},R_{2}),

\displaystyle L(p;1,q_{1})\times L(p;1,q_{2})

\displaystyle=L(p,p;Q_{1},Q_{2}),

where

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=(1,r_{1},0,0),

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=(0,0,1,r_{2}),

\displaystyle Q_{1}

\displaystyle=(1,q_{1},0,0),

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle=(0,0,1,q_{2}).

Invoke Proposition
3.3
to
conclude
L(p,p;R_{1},R_{2})\simeq L(p,p;Q_{1},Q_{2})
.
The argument proceeds via
k
-invariants. Specifically,

\displaystyle k(L(p,p;R_{1},R_{2}))

\displaystyle=\left(r_{1}a^{2},r_{2}b^{2}\right)\in H^{4}((\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2};\mathbb{Z}^{2})

\displaystyle k(L(p,p;Q_{1},Q_{2}))

\displaystyle=\left(q_{1}a^{2},q_{2}b^{2}\right)\in H^{4}((\mathbb{Z}_{/p})^{2};\mathbb{Z}^{2})

By the hypothesis, choose
z
so that

r_{1}r_{2}z^{2}=\pm q_{1}q_{2}
. Set

\lambda:=q_{1}/(r_{1}z^{2})
and set

\mu:=q_{2}/r_{2}
. Then
\lambda\mu\equiv\pm 1\mod p
and since the map

\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\{M\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p):\det M=\pm 1\}
is surjective, there is an automorphism of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
which, when reduced modulo
p
, amounts to multiplication by
\lambda
and
\mu
on the two generators, respectively. Note that
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
consists of matrices with integer entries and determinant
\pm 1
, and the map
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\to\{M\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p):\det M=\pm 1\}
is reduction modulo
p
.

So the
k
-invariant
k(L(p,p;R_{1},R_{2}))=\left(r_{1}a^{2},r_{2}b^{2}\right)
and the
k
-invariant
\left(\lambda r_{1}a^{2},\mu r_{2}b^{2}\right)
correspond to homotopy equivalent spaces, but the latter
k
-invariant is
\left(q_{1}a^{2}/z^{2},q_{2}b^{2}\right)
, which, after changing the chosen generator of
\pi_{1}
, yields the
k
-invariant
k(L(p,p;Q_{1},Q_{2}))=\left(q_{1}a^{2},q_{2}b^{2}\right)
.

To finish the homeomorphism classification, we must compare Pontrjagin classes modulo
p
.
The total Pontrjagin class of
L(p,p;R_{1},R_{2})
is

(1+\bar{a}^{2})(1+{r_{1}}^{2}\bar{a}^{2})(1+\bar{b}^{2})(1+{r_{2}}^{2}\bar{b}^{2}),

which is concentrated in
H^{4}
since
L(p,p;R_{1},R_{2})

is 6-dimensional, so the Pontrjagin class is

\bar{a}^{2}+{r_{1}}^{2}\bar{a}^{2}+\bar{b}^{2}+{r_{2}}^{2}\bar{b}^{2}\in H^{4}(L),

but
\bar{a}^{2}
and
\bar{b}^{2}
both vanish, so this Pontrjagin class is zero. There are other ways to see that this vanishes: twice the Pontrjagin class of
L(p,p;R_{1},R_{2})
is twice the product of the Pontrjagin classes of the two lens spaces, but the Pontrjagin class of a 3-dimensional lens spaces is trivial.

For the converse direction, note that a homeomorphism necessarily results in a homotopy equivalence, and with the discussion of
k
-invariant in Section 6 of
[
6
]
, the condition on the rotation numbers must be satisfied.
∎

Theorem
5.1
yields a homeomorphism, so a natural question is whether one can produce a diffeomorphism. One could combine the proof of Theorem 1.1 of
[
7
]
with the simple homotopy equivalence provided by Propositions
3.3
and
3.4
to produce a diffeomorphism.

However, several questions remain unanswered and present opportunities for future work. For instance, theorem
5.1
examined the case of
S^{3}\times S^{3}
; a classification in the case of
S^{5}\times S^{5}
remains open. In that case, instead of studying pairs of quadratic forms, the homotopy classification involves cubic forms. And in addition to studying diffeomorphism, one could seek a classification up to
almost
diffeomorphism, meaning up to connected sums with exotic spheres. It would also be interesting to extend Proposition
4.1
to include not only information on the Pontrjagin class, but also information on the action of the group of homotopy self-equivalences on the Pontrjagin class.

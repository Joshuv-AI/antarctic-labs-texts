Stability of the Type IIA flow and its applications in symplectic geometry 1 1 1 Work supported in part by the National Science Foundation Grants DMS-1855947, DMS-1809582 and Simons Collaboration Grant 853806.


Abstract
In this paper the dynamical stability of the Type IIA flow with no source near its stationary points is established. These stationary points had been shown previously by the authors to be Ricci-flat Kähler metrics on Calabi-Yau 3-folds. The dynamical stability of the Type IIA flow is then applied to prove the stability under symplectic deformations of the Kähler property for Calabi-Yau 3-folds.



Teng Fei, Duong H. Phong, Sebastien Picard, and Xiangwen Zhang

Introduction

In 1985, Candelas, Horowitz, Strominger, and Witten
[
8
]
proposed Calabi-Yau 3-folds as solutions of the heterotic string. Since then, many solutions of unified string theories have emerged, all of which can be recognized as defining suitable notions of canonical metrics. Here the notion of canonical metric is taken in a broad sense, as consisting of a curvature condition combined with a cohomological constraint, and the original notion in Kähler geometry is only a special case. Geometric flows are particularly effective in the search for such metrics, because they can often be chosen to preserve the cohomological condition. The earliest flow introduced for this reason appears to be Bryant’s Laplacian flow for
G_{2}
holonomy
[
6
]
, and more recent ones motivated specifically by unified string theories include the Anomaly flow
[
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
, the Type IIB flow
[
14
]
, and the Type IIA flow
[
12
,
13
]
(see e.g.
[
24
]
for a survey). Of these, the Type IIA flow stands out as a flow in symplectic rather than Riemannian or complex geometry.

Geometric flows have had many spectacular applications in topology, Riemannian geometry, and complex geometry, but their success in symplectic geometry has been more limited so far, despite the appearance of many natural interesting flows
[
19
,
30
,
11
]
. The main goal of this paper is to show that the Type IIA flow may be particularly promising in this regard. Already in
[
12
]
, by considering specific models, evidence had been presented to suggest that the Type IIA flow on a
6
-dimensional compact symplectic manifold should lead to the optimal almost-complex structure compatible with the given symplectic structure. Here we shall show that the Type IIA flow can also be applied to establish a remarkable property of deformations of symplectic structures on Calabi-Yau 3-folds, namely that small enough symplectic deformations preserve the Kähler property. This will follow from the facts that the stationary points of the Type IIA flow are Calabi-Yau 3-folds, and the Type IIA flow is dynamically stable. Dynamical stability is a particularly important property of a flow, and in general it is the best possible analytic statement that one can make about its convergence without more detailed information about its global behavior. It is perhaps fitting that, just as in the past, a structure originating from theoretical physics turns out to be valuable from both the geometric and the analytic standpoints as well.

We state now more precisely our main results. Let
M
be an oriented
6
-dimensional manifold. In
[
16
]
, Hitchin had shown how to associate to a non-degenerate
3
-form
\varphi
an almost-complex structure
J_{\varphi}
. In presence of a a symplectic structure
\omega
on

, it makes sense to require that
\varphi
be primitive with respect to
\omega
, i.e,
\Lambda_{\omega}\varphi=0
, where
\Lambda_{\omega}:\Lambda^{k}(M)\to\Lambda^{k-2}(M)
is the Hodge contraction with respect to
\omega
. In
[
12
]
, it was shown that this condition is equivalent to the form
g_{\varphi}(X,Y)=\omega(X,J_{\varphi}Y)
being invariant under
J_{\varphi}
, and hence is an almost Hermitian metric when
g_{\varphi}>0
. The triple
(\omega,g_{\varphi},J_{\varphi})
defines then an almost-Kähler structure. As shown in
[
12

, when

is in addition a closed form, the almost-Kähler structure

acquires very special properties. We shall refer to such structures as

Our first result is the dynamic stability of the Type IIA flow, which can be stated as follows:

Theorem 1

Let
(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})
be a Ricci-flat Type IIA structure on a compact oriented 6-manifold
M
. Then there is an
\epsilon_{0}^{\prime}>0
so that
for any Type IIA structure
(\varphi_{0},\omega_{0})
satisfying

(\varphi_{0},\omega_{0})\in\mathcal{U}_{(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})}=\{(\varphi,\omega)\,:\,|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{W^{10,2}}+|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}|_{W^{10,2}}<\epsilon^{\prime}_{0}\},

the Type IIA flow (
1.1
) with initial value
(\varphi_{0},\omega_{0})
exists for all
t\in[0,\infty)
and converges smoothly to a Ricci-flat Type IIA structure
(\varphi_{\infty},\omega_{0})
.

Using this dynamical stability of the Type IIA flow, we can then establish the following theorem:

Theorem 2

Let
(M,\bar{\omega})
be a compact symplectic 6-manifold with
c_{1}(M,\bar{\omega})=0\in H^{2}(M;{\bf R})
. Suppose that
(M,\bar{\omega})
admits a compatible integrable complex structure. Then there exists
\epsilon(\bar{\omega})>0
, with the following property: for any symplectic form
\omega
with
|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{W^{10,2}}<\epsilon
, the symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
also admits a compatible and integrable complex structure.

To the best of our knowledge, there does not appear to have been in the literature similar statements about the stability of Kähler structures under symplectic deformations at the level of differential forms. The only relevant paper we can find is by de Bartolomeis
[
2
]
, which investigated this problem at the cohomology level, by considering equivalence classes modulo the diffeomorphism group action. In a private communication along this line, Professor Dietmar Salamon has provided us with an argument showing that, when
H_{\bar{J}}^{2,0}(M)=0
, where
\bar{J}
is the complex structure associated to the Kähler form
\bar{\omega}
, then every symplectic form close to
\bar{\omega}
admits a compatible complex structure which is diffeomorphic to
\bar{J}
. But the situation is more obscure when
H_{\bar{J}}^{2,0}(M)\not=0
.

It may be worth stressing that Theorem
2
is a statement purely about symplectic structures, where the Type IIA flow does not appear in any way. Rather the Type IIA flow is only used in the proof, which is a clear indication that it can be an effective tool in symplectic geometry.

We provide now an outline of the proof of Theorems
1
and
2
, and of the organization of the paper. The group of diffeomorphisms acts on the space of Type IIA structures, and a first step is to classify the steady Type IIA solitons. By definition (see Definition

Theorem 3

Let

be a steady Type IIA soliton on a compact manifold
M
. Then
(\varphi,\omega)
is a Ricci-flat Type IIA structure and

is a Killing vector field for

\mathcal{L}_{V}\varphi=\mathcal{L}_{V}\omega=0.

(1.2)

We return to the proof of Theorem
1
proper.
The Type IIA flow is only weakly parabolic, so following DeTurck’s strategy for the Ricci flow, we consider instead the Type IIA flow reparametrized by a specific time-dependent vector field

6

(A,B)={1\over k!}g^{IJ}A_{I}B_{J},\quad g^{IJ}=g^{i_{1}j_{1}}\cdots g^{i_{k}j_{k}}.

(2.4)

The adjoint differential is denoted

(d^{\star}A)_{I}=(-\star\!d\!\star A)_{I}=-\nabla^{p}A_{pI}.

(2.5)

For local computations, we will often use the normal form of

. Around each point, there exists an orthonormal frame
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{6}
with respect to

such that
J_{\varphi}e_{2k-1}=e_{2k}

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{12}+e^{34}+e^{56}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{|\varphi|\over 2}(e^{135}-e^{146}-e^{245}-e^{236})

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}

\displaystyle=

We also note the fundamental Type IIA variational formulas:

Steady Type IIA solitons

In the Introduction, we had defined the Type IIA flow, which is a flow of Type IIA structures on the
6
-dimensional manifold
M
. We would like to consider now the reparametrizations of the Type IIA flow by a possibly time-dependent vector field
V
.

3

3

Such reparametrizations were actually needed in the proof given in
[
12
]
for the short-time existence of the Type IIA flow. But here, we would like to examine the issue of reparametrizations in greater generality.

Let then
V
be such a vector field, and consider reparametrizations by
V
of the Type IIA flow of a type IIA structure
(\varphi,\omega)
. Even if
\omega_{0}
were fixed, under such a reparametrization, the evolving Type IIA structure becomes a time-dependent pair
(\varphi_{t},\omega_{t})
which obeys the equation

\begin{cases}&\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}=d\Lambda_{t}d(|\varphi_{t}|^{2}\hat{\varphi}_{t})+d\iota_{V_{t}}\varphi_{t}\\
&\partial_{t}\omega_{t}=d\iota_{V_{t}}\omega_{t}\end{cases}

(2.13)

with
\varphi_{t=0}=\varphi_{0}
and
\omega_{t=0}=\omega_{0}
. The notion of steady Type IIA soliton can then be defined as follows:

Definition 1

A steady Type IIA soliton on a compact manifold
M
is a triple
(\varphi,\omega,V)
, where
(\varphi,\omega)
is a Type IIA structure, and
V
a vector field satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&d\Lambda d(|\varphi|^{2}\hat{\varphi})+d\iota_{V}\varphi=0\\
&d\iota_{V}\omega=0\end{cases}

(2.14)

In the Introduction, we had formulated as Theorem
3
a classification of steady Type IIA solitons on compact manifolds. We can give now the proof of this theorem.

Proof of Theorem
3
.
The proof is actually parallel to that given in
[
12
, Theorem 8]
for the stationary points of the Type IIA flow. Assume that
(\varphi,\omega,V)
is a steady Type IIA soliton. Then by integration by part we know that

0=\int_{M}\left(d\Lambda d(|\varphi|^{2}\hat{\varphi})+d\iota_{V}\varphi\right)\wedge\hat{\varphi}=-\int_{M}\left(\partial_{-}(|\varphi|^{2}\hat{\varphi})+\iota_{V}\varphi\right)\wedge d\hat{\varphi}.

(2.15)

As argued in
[
12
]
,
d\hat{\varphi}
is of type
(2,2)
, and for any vector field
V
, we know that
\iota_{V}\varphi
is of type
(2,0)+(0,2)
. Therefore the second term above
\iota_{V}\varphi\wedge d\hat{\varphi}
is identically zero. So we are in the same case as in
[
12
]
and conclude that
d\hat{\varphi}=0
hence the associated complex structure
J_{\varphi}
is integrable and we have a Kähler Calabi-Yau manifold.

Using the normal form of Type IIA structures
[
12

, it is straightforward to establish the following identities for any vector field
V

\displaystyle\iota_{V}\varphi=\Lambda(V^{\sharp}\wedge\hat{\varphi})=\Lambda(JV^{\sharp}\wedge\varphi),

\displaystyle\iota_{V}\hat{\varphi}=-\Lambda(V^{\sharp}\wedge\varphi)=\Lambda(JV^{\sharp}\wedge\hat{\varphi}),

where
V^{\sharp}
is the 1-form dual to
V
via the metric
g(\varphi,\omega)
. As a result, we know

d\Lambda\left((d|\varphi|^{2}+V^{\sharp})\wedge\hat{\varphi}\right)=0.

(2.16)

Let
W
be the real vector field on
M
such that
W^{\sharp}=d|\varphi|^{2}+V^{\sharp}
. Then, (
2.16

d\Lambda(W^{\sharp}\wedge\hat{\varphi})=0
. By Hodge theory, we know there exists a real harmonic 2-form
H
and a 1-form

such that

\Lambda(W^{\sharp}\wedge\hat{\varphi})=H+da,

or equivalently

W^{\sharp}\wedge\hat{\varphi}=\omega\wedge(H+da).

Notice that
W^{\sharp}\wedge\hat{\varphi}
is a
(3,1)+(1,3)
-form, so we also know that
H+da
is a
(2,0)+(0,2)
-form. Write
a=\alpha+\bar{\alpha}
where
\alpha
is the
(1,0)
-part of

. The above analysis implies that

\bar{\partial}\alpha+\partial\bar{\alpha}+H^{1,1}=0,

(2.17)

where
H^{1,1}
is the
(1,1)
-part of
H
, which is also harmonic. From (
2.17
) we know that
\bar{\partial}\alpha
is
\bar{\partial}
-exact and
\partial
-closed, by the

-lemma, we know that there exists a complex-valued function

such that
\bar{\partial}\alpha=\bar{\partial}\partial s
. By Hodge theory again, we can find a harmonic
(1,0)
-form

such that
\alpha=\partial s+\mu
. It follows that

\partial\alpha=H^{1,1}=0\ \ \textit{\rm and }\ W^{\sharp}\wedge\hat{\varphi}=\omega\wedge H,

(2.18)

where
H
is a harmonic
(2,0)+(0,2)
-form. Let
H^{2,0}
be the
(2,0)
-part of

which is holomorphic. Since
\Omega=\varphi+i\hat{\varphi}
is a holomorphic volume form, we see that

\frac{i}{2}\Omega\wedge(W^{\sharp})^{0,1}=\omega\wedge H^{2,0}.

(2.19)

By non-degeneracy of

we know that there exists a holomorphic vector field

\iota_{Y}\Omega=H^{2,0}.

(2.20)

Using

(W^{\sharp})^{0,1}=-2i\iota_{Y}\omega.

It follows that

W^{\sharp}=2\iota_{i(\bar{Y}-Y)}\omega.

On the other hand, since

, namely
V
is a Hamiltonian vector field, by Hodge theory for Kähler manifolds, we know that there exists a real-valued function

and a harmonic 1-form

Let

which is a real holomorphic vector field, namely the real part of a holomorphic vector field. We see that

(2.21)

Now consider an arbitrary real holomorphic vector field

\Omega\wedge\bar{\Omega}=-2i|\varphi|^{2}\frac{\omega^{3}}{3!}.

(2.22)

Hence we conclude that

JX
for

\begin{cases}&\Delta|\varphi|^{4}+2(d|\varphi|^{2},h+Jdv)=0\\
&\Delta v-(d\,{\rm log}\,|\varphi|^{2},Jh-dv)=0\end{cases}

By the strong maximum principle, the first equation implies that

is a constant so we have a Ricci-flat Type IIA structure and the theorem follows. Q.E.D.

In fact, by a theorem of Bochner

3
The reparametrized Type IIA flow

Let
(M,\omega)
be a compact symplectic 6-manifold with trivial first Chern class. It has been shown in
[
12
]
that the Type IIA flow is only weakly parabolic, and even the proof of the short-time existence of the flow required a suitable adaptation of DeTurck’s arguments
[
10
]
for the proof of the short-time existence of the Ricci flow.
In particular, the Type IIA flow had to be reparametrized by a specific time-dependent vector field
V_{t}
, which is the analogue for the Type IIA flow of DeTurck’s vector field for the Ricci flow. To prove the desired dynamic stability Theorem
1

First, we recall the specific vector field
V_{t}
which we need for this reparametrization. It is a vector field depending on
(\varphi_{t},\omega_{t})
and a reference metric
g^{\prime}

V_{t}=V(\varphi_{t},\omega_{t},g^{\prime}).

(3.1)

For simplicity, we omit the subscript

and write
g=g_{t}=g(\varphi_{t},\omega_{t})
for the associated metric (
2.2
). The expression of

is then given by

V^{k}=|\varphi|^{2}g^{pq}(\Gamma^{k}_{pq}-(\Gamma^{\prime})^{k}_{pq})-g^{lk}\partial_{l}|\varphi|^{2},

(3.2)

where
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are the Christoffel symbol associated to the metric
g
and
g^{\prime}
respectively. The choice of reference metric
g^{\prime}
is arbitrary. In
[
12
]
, it is chosen to be the initial metric
g^{\prime}=g_{0}
. However, throughout this paper, we choose

to be the fixed Ricci-flat Kähler metric, which comes from a stationary point
(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})
of the Type IIA flow.

Recall that the equations of a reparametrized Type IIA flow were given in (
2.13

V
be given by (

). As a comparison, we recall that the vector field

used by DeTurck

(3.3)

Let

be the set of all stationary points of the reparametrized Type IIA flow (

\mathcal{M}=\{(\varphi,\omega)\textrm{ is a Type IIA structure}:\ d\Lambda_{\omega}d(|\varphi|^{2}\hat{\varphi})+d\iota_{V}\varphi=0,\ \ d\iota_{V}\omega=0\}

since
g(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})=\bar{g}
is the reference metric which is Ricci-flat, and

is a constant. Similarly we let
\mathcal{N}
be the set of stationary points of the DeTurck-Ricci flow,

\mathcal{N}=\{g:{\rm Ric}(g)+\mathcal{L}_{V^{\prime}}g=0\}

with
V^{\prime}=V^{\prime}(g,\bar{g})
from (

Proposition 1

There is a well-defined map
g:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
. That is, for any
(\varphi,\omega)\in\mathcal{M}
, we have
g(\varphi,\omega)\in\mathcal{N}
.

Proof
: For any
(\varphi,\omega)\in\mathcal{M}
, we know that by definition
(\varphi,\omega,V(\varphi,\omega,\bar{g}))
is a steady Type IIA soliton. Hence by Theorem
3

(\varphi,\omega)
is a Ricci-flat Type IIA structure and
V(\varphi,\omega,\bar{g})
is a Killing vector field of

|\varphi|^{2}
is a constant. Therefore the expression in (

V^{k}=|\varphi|^{2}g^{pq}(\Gamma^{k}_{pq}-\bar{\Gamma}^{k}_{pq})=|\varphi|^{2}(V^{\prime})^{k},

(3.4)

namely,
V^{\prime}
is a constant multiple of
V
. It follows that
g=g(\varphi,\omega)
satisfies
{\rm Ric}(g)=\mathcal{L}_{V^{\prime}}g=0
, hence
g\in\mathcal{N}
. Q.E.D.

Proposition 2

For any
g\in\mathcal{N}
, we have
V^{\prime}(g,\bar{g})=0
.

Proof
: By the well-known classification steady compact Ricci solitons, we know
g\in\mathcal{N}
implies that
g
is Ricci-flat and
V^{\prime}
is a Killing vector field to

Second, since both

In this case, due to the existence of parallel 1-forms, the condition (

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\bigg[O(\omega_{a,b},\varphi_{a,b})*(\nabla\omega_{a,b}+\nabla\varphi_{a,b})*((\varphi_{a}-\bar{\varphi})+(\omega_{a}-\bar{\omega}))*(\alpha+\beta)

(6.15)

\displaystyle+O(\omega_{a,b},\varphi_{a,b})*(\nabla(\omega_{a}-\bar{\omega})+\nabla(\varphi_{a}-\bar{\varphi}))*(\alpha+\beta)

\displaystyle+O(\omega_{a,b},\varphi_{a,b})*((\varphi_{a}-\bar{\varphi})+(\omega_{a}-\bar{\omega}))*(\nabla\alpha+\nabla\beta)\bigg]

The
O(\cdots)
terms also depend on the positivity of
\varphi_{a,b}
and
\omega_{a,b}>0
. By the estimate (
6.9
), these have uniformly bounded positivity, and the Lemma’s assumption
|\omega(t)|_{C^{1}},|\varphi(t)|_{C^{1}}\leq C
implies that the
O(\cdots)
terms are uniformed bounded in
C^{0}
. The smallness comes from the differences e.g.
\varphi_{a}-\bar{\varphi}
(
6.9
), and thus the
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
terms are of the form (
6.12
).

\bullet
Term
\mathcal{Q}_{2}
. If we write
\mathcal{Q}_{2}=d\mathcal{R}
, the error term
\mathcal{R}=O(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})*H
from Theorem
4
involves bounded terms contracted with

\nabla\star(\delta\omega\wedge\bar{\varphi}+\bar{\omega}\wedge\delta\varphi)

(6.16)

Here
\delta\omega=\alpha
and
\delta\varphi=\beta
, and so using
\varphi(t)\wedge\omega(t)=0
,
\bar{\varphi}\wedge\bar{\omega}=0
,
\tilde{\varphi}\wedge\tilde{\omega}=0
,

\delta\omega\wedge\bar{\varphi}+\bar{\omega}\wedge\delta\varphi=\alpha\wedge(\bar{\varphi}-\varphi)+(\bar{\omega}-\omega)\wedge\beta+\alpha\wedge\beta.

(6.17)

Since
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\bar{\varphi}-\varphi
,
\bar{\omega}-\omega
are small in the
C^{1}
norm, we see that the
\mathcal{Q}_{2}
terms are of the form (
6.12
). Q.E.D.

Let
\chi=(\varphi,\omega)
denote the evolving Type IIA structure. Then the remainder terms
{\cal E}_{i}
introduced in Lemma
2
can be written as

{\cal E}_{i}=O(\chi)(1+\nabla\chi),

(6.18)

where
O(\chi)
is an unspecified smooth function of
\chi
which is bounded if
|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{C^{0}}+|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}|_{C^{0}}\leq\epsilon
. Using this language, we have

Lemma 3

The higher derivatives of
{\cal E}_{i}
are of the form

\nabla^{j}{\cal E}_{i}=O(\chi)\sum_{j\!\leq\!i_{1}\!+\!\dots\!+\!i_{s}\!\leq\!j\!+\!1}\nabla^{i_{1}}\chi*\dots*\nabla^{i_{s}}\chi.

(6.19)

Proof:
This can be proved by making induction on
j
and using the fact that

\nabla(O(\chi))=O(\chi)\nabla\chi.

(6.20)

Q.E.D.

6.2
Decay estimates for
\alpha(t)
and
\beta(t)

The goal is to derive the decay estimates for
\alpha(t)
and
\beta(t)
in
C^{k}
-norms. To achieve that, we first establish the
W^{2,m}
estimates for
\alpha(t)
and
\beta(t)
, then apply the Sobolev embeddings to get the desired estimates. Similar techniques were also used in the proof of the stability of other flows, see for example, the Ricci flow
[
29
]
and
G_{2}
flow
[
22
]
.

From now on in this section, we use norms, covariant derivatives, adjoints, volume forms, etc, all with respect to the background
\bar{g}
. So for example, instead of

I_{0}(t):=\int_{M}(|\alpha(t)|_{\bar{g}}^{2}+|\beta(t)|_{\bar{g}}^{2})dV_{\bar{g}}

(6.21)

we write
I_{0}=\int_{M}|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}
.

Lemma 4

Suppose the flow is defined on an interval
[0,A]
. There exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that if
|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{C^{1}}(t)+|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}|_{C^{1}}(t)\leq\epsilon
with
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
for all
t\in[0,A]
, then
I_{0}
satisfies

{d\over dt}I_{0}\leq-{1\over 2}\int_{M}(|\nabla\alpha|^{2}+|\nabla\beta|^{2})dV_{\bar{g}}+CI_{0}(t)

(6.22)

for any
t\in[0,A]
. Moreover, there exists
0<\delta<1
depending on
(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})
such that

I_{0}(t)\leq e^{-\delta t}I_{0}(0).

(6.23)

Here
\alpha(t)=\omega(t)-\tilde{\omega}
and
\beta(t)=\varphi(t)-\tilde{\varphi}
.

Proof:
By Lemma
2
, we have

\displaystyle{d\over dt}I_{0}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\int_{M}\langle-\Box_{\bar{g}}\alpha,\alpha\rangle+\langle-\Box_{\bar{g}}\beta,\beta\rangle

(6.24)

\displaystyle+\int_{M}\langle\mathcal{E}_{1}*(\alpha+\nabla\alpha+\beta+\nabla\beta),d^{\dagger}\alpha\rangle+\langle\mathcal{E}_{2}*(\alpha+\nabla\alpha+\beta+\nabla\beta),d^{\dagger}\beta\rangle

Let
0<2\delta<1
be smaller than the smallest positive eigenvalue of
\Box_{\bar{g}}
on 2-forms and 3-forms. This is where we use the fact that
\alpha
,
\beta
are orthogonal to the kernel (Lemma
1
) to obtain a
\delta
decay gap.

\displaystyle{d\over dt}I_{0}(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-2\delta\int_{M}|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}-\int_{M}|d\alpha|^{2}+|d^{\star}\alpha|^{2}+|d\beta|^{2}+|d^{\star}\beta|^{2}

(6.25)

\displaystyle+C\epsilon\int_{M}|\alpha|^{2}+|\nabla\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}+|\nabla\beta|^{2}+|d^{\dagger}\alpha|^{2}+|d^{\dagger}\beta|^{2}.

By the Bochner-Kodaira formula

(dd^{\dagger}+d^{\dagger}d)\alpha=-\bar{g}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\alpha+Rm*\alpha,

(6.26)

we have

\int_{M}|d\alpha|^{2}+|d^{\star}\alpha|^{2}=\int_{M}|\nabla\alpha|^{2}+\int_{M}Rm*\alpha*\alpha.

(6.27)

Therefore the
C\epsilon
terms can all be absorbed for
\epsilon
small enough. Therefore

{d\over dt}I_{0}(t)\leq-\delta I_{0}(t)-{1\over 2}\int_{M}|d\alpha|^{2}+|d^{\star}\alpha|^{2}+|d\beta|^{2}+|d^{\star}\beta|^{2}

(6.28)

and the lemma follows. Q.E.D.

Next, we estimate
I_{1}(t):=\int_{M}|\nabla\alpha|^{2}+|\nabla\beta|^{2}
.

Lemma 5

Suppose the flow is defined on an interval
[0,A]
. There exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that if
|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{C^{1}}(t)+|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}|_{C^{1}}(t)\leq\epsilon
with
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
for all
t\in[0,A]
, then
I_{1}
satisfies

{d\over dt}I_{1}\leq-\int_{M}(|\nabla^{2}\alpha|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}\beta|^{2})dV_{\bar{g}}+C\left(I_{0}(t)+I_{1}(t)\right)

(6.29)

for all
t\in[0,A]
. Moreover, there is a uniform positive constant
C_{1}
such that

I_{1}(t)\leq(I_{1}(0)+C_{1}I_{0}(0))\,e^{-{\delta\over 2}t}

(6.30)

Here
\alpha(t)=\omega(t)-\tilde{\omega}
and
\beta(t)=\varphi(t)-\tilde{\varphi}
.

Proof:

Let
\Delta=\bar{g}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}
. We have

\partial_{t}\alpha=\Delta\alpha+Rm*\alpha+d\mathcal{F},

(6.31)

where
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{E}_{1}*(\alpha+\nabla\alpha+\beta+\nabla\beta)
is as in Lemma
2
. We start with

\displaystyle\partial_{t}|\nabla\alpha|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\langle\nabla\Delta\alpha,\nabla\alpha\rangle+2\langle\nabla d\mathcal{F},\nabla\alpha\rangle+\nabla Rm*\alpha*\nabla\alpha+Rm*\nabla\alpha*\nabla\alpha

(6.32)

and

\Delta|\nabla\alpha|^{2}=2\langle\nabla\Delta\alpha,\nabla\alpha\rangle+2|\nabla^{2}\alpha|^{2}+Rm*\nabla\alpha*\nabla\alpha+\nabla Rm*\alpha*\nabla\alpha.

(6.33)

Thus

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)|\nabla\alpha|^{2}=-2|\nabla^{2}\alpha|^{2}+2\langle\nabla d\mathcal{F},\nabla\alpha\rangle+\nabla Rm*\alpha*\nabla\alpha+Rm*\nabla\alpha*\nabla\alpha

(6.34)

Integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle{d\over dt}\int_{M}|\nabla\alpha|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{M}|\nabla^{2}\alpha|^{2}-2\int_{M}\langle d\mathcal{F},\Delta\alpha\rangle+\int_{M}Rm*\nabla\alpha*\nabla\alpha

(6.35)

\displaystyle+\int_{M}\nabla Rm*\alpha*\nabla\alpha

We can estimate

\displaystyle d{\cal F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla{\cal E}_{1}*(\alpha+\nabla\alpha+\beta+\nabla\beta)

(6.36)

\displaystyle+{\cal E}_{1}*(\nabla\alpha+\nabla^{2}\alpha+\nabla\beta+\nabla^{2}\beta)

under the condition
|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{C^{1}}(t)+|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}|_{C^{1}}(t)<\epsilon
.
The second term on the right hand side can be easily handled using Lemma
2

|\mathcal{E}_{1}*(\nabla\alpha+\nabla^{2}\alpha+\nabla\beta+\nabla^{2}\beta)|\leq C\epsilon(|\nabla\alpha|+|\nabla^{2}\alpha|+|\nabla\beta|+|\nabla^{2}\beta|)

(6.37)

For the first term, from Lemma
3
, we see that

\displaystyle|\nabla{\cal E}_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(|\nabla^{2}\omega|+|\nabla^{2}\varphi|+|\nabla\omega|^{2}+|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+1)

(6.38)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C(|\nabla^{2}\omega|+|\nabla^{2}\varphi|+|\nabla(\omega-\bar{\omega})|^{2}+|\nabla(\varphi-\bar{\varphi})|^{2}+1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(|\nabla^{2}\omega|+|\nabla^{2}\varphi|+1)

because
\nabla\bar{\omega}=\nabla\bar{\varphi}=0
and our
C^{1}
-closedness assumption. To further control it, we make use of
\omega=\alpha+\tilde{\omega}
,
\varphi=\beta+\tilde{\varphi}
. It follows that

\displaystyle|\nabla{\cal E}_{1}*(\alpha+\nabla\alpha+\beta+\nabla\beta)|

(6.39)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(|\nabla^{2}\alpha|+|\nabla^{2}\beta|+|\nabla^{2}\tilde{\omega}|+|\nabla^{2}\tilde{\varphi}|+1)(|\alpha|+|\nabla\alpha|+|\beta|+|\nabla\beta|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(|\nabla^{2}\alpha|+|\nabla^{2}\beta|)(|\alpha|+|\nabla\alpha|+|\beta|+|\nabla\beta|)+C(|\alpha|+|\nabla\alpha|+|\beta|+|\nabla\beta|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\epsilon(|\nabla^{2}\alpha|+|\nabla^{2}\beta|)+C(|\alpha|+|\nabla\alpha|+|\beta|+|\nabla\beta|)

where we use Lemma
1
and Proposition
4
to bound
|\nabla^{2}\tilde{\omega}|+|\nabla^{2}\tilde{\varphi}|
by some uniform constant
C
depending on
\bar{\omega}
and
\bar{\varphi}
. Putting those estimates together, we have obtained

\displaystyle|d\mathcal{F}|\leq C\epsilon(|\nabla^{2}\alpha|+|\nabla^{2}\beta|)+C(|\alpha|+|\nabla\alpha|+|\beta|+|\nabla\beta|)

(6.40)

under the assumption that
|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{C^{1}}(t)+|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}|_{C^{1}}(t)<\epsilon
.

It follows that

\int_{M}|d\mathcal{F}||\Delta\alpha|\leq C\epsilon\int_{M}\left(|\nabla^{2}\alpha|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}\alpha||\nabla^{2}\beta|\right)+C\int_{M}|\nabla^{2}\alpha|(|\alpha|+|\nabla\alpha|+|\beta|+|\nabla\beta|)

(6.41)

We take
\epsilon_{0}
small such that
C\epsilon_{0}={1\over 4}
. There, for any
0<\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
,we can use Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and obtain

{d\over dt}\int_{M}|\nabla\alpha|^{2}\leq-{3\over 2}\int_{M}|\nabla^{2}\alpha|^{2}+{1\over 4}\int_{M}|\nabla^{2}\alpha||\nabla^{2}\beta|+C\bigg[\int_{M}|\alpha|^{2}+|\nabla\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}+|\nabla\beta|^{2}\bigg]

(6.42)

We can use the evolution equation of
\beta
in Lemma
2
to perform the same calculation and obtain

{d\over dt}\int_{M}|\nabla\beta|^{2}\leq-{3\over 2}\int_{M}|\nabla^{2}\beta|^{2}+{1\over 4}\int_{M}|\nabla^{2}\alpha||\nabla^{2}\beta|+C\bigg[\int_{M}|\alpha|^{2}+|\nabla\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}+|\nabla\beta|^{2}\bigg]

(6.43)

Putting (
6.42
) and (
6.43
) together, we get (
6.29
).

Next, we use (
6.29
) to derive the estimate for
I_{1}(t)
. It follows from (
6.29
) that

{d\over dt}I_{1}(t)\leq C(I_{0}(t)+I_{1}(t)).

(6.44)

Recall (
6.22
)

{d\over dt}I_{0}(t)\leq-{1\over 2}I_{1}(t)+CI_{0}(t).

(6.45)

We can take
\eta>1
large enough and obtain

{d\over dt}\bigg[I_{1}(t)+\eta I_{0}(t)\bigg]\leq-I_{1}(t)+C(\eta)I_{0}(t)\leq C(\eta)I_{0}(t).

(6.46)

Indeed,
\eta
can be taken as
2(1+C)
and therefore it is also a uniform constant, as
C
, which only depends on
\bar{\omega}
and
\bar{\varphi}
. We note that
I_{0}(t)\leq e^{-\delta t}I_{0}(0)
. Taking integration
\int_{0}^{t}\cdot\,ds
on both sides of

{d\over ds}\bigg[I_{1}(s)+\eta I_{0}(s)\bigg]\leq Ce^{-\delta s},

(6.47)

we obtain

I_{1}(t)\leq I_{1}(0)+CI_{0}(0).

(6.48)

Indeed, we can play with (
6.46
) more carefully and obtain a decay estimate for
I_{1}(t)
.

Denote
h(t)=I_{1}(t)+\eta I_{0}(t)
. Then,

{d\over dt}h(t)\leq-h(t)+(\eta+C(\eta))I_{0}(t)\leq-h(t)+\tilde{C}(\eta)e^{-\delta t}

(6.49)

where
\tilde{C}(\eta)=I_{0}(0)(\eta+C(\eta))
is a uniform constant depending on
\eta
. Next, taking a constant
\delta_{1}>0
small such that
\delta/2\delta_{1}-\tilde{C}(\eta)\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle{d\over dt}\bigg[h(t)+{1\over\delta_{1}}e^{-\delta t}\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{d\over dt}h(t)-{\delta\over\delta_{1}}e^{-\delta t}\leq-h(t)-(\delta/\delta_{1}-\tilde{C}(\eta))e^{-\delta t}

(6.50)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-h(t)-{\delta\over 2\delta_{1}}e^{-\delta t}=\left(\delta/2-1\right)h(t)-{\delta\over 2}\left[h(t)+{1\over\delta_{1}}e^{-\delta t}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-{\delta\over 2}\left[h(t)+{1\over\delta_{1}}e^{-\delta t}\right]

since
0<\delta<1
. This will give us the estimate

h(t)+{1\over\delta_{1}}e^{-\delta t}\leq\bigg[h(0)+{1\over\delta_{1}}\bigg]e^{-{\delta\over 2}t}.

(6.51)

Therefore,

h(t)\leq h(0)e^{-{\delta\over 2}t}.

(6.52)

This implies the decay of
I_{1}(t)
claimed in (
6.30
). Q.E.D.

Next, we will use the idea of induction to derive the estimates for

I_{k}(t)=\int_{M}|\nabla^{k}\alpha|^{2}+|\nabla^{k}\beta|^{2}\,dV_{\bar{g}}

(6.53)

Lemma 6

Suppose the flow is defined on an interval
[0,A]
. There exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that if
|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{C^{1}}(t)+|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}|_{C^{1}}(t)\leq\epsilon
for all
t\in[0,A]
for with
\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_{0})
, then

|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{C^{[{k\over 2}]+1}}(t)+|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}|_{C^{[{k\over 2}]+1}}(t)\leq S
implies that
I_{k}
satisfies

{d\over dt}I_{k}(t)\leq-\int_{M}(|\nabla^{k+1}\alpha|^{2}+|\nabla^{k+1}\beta|^{2})dV_{\bar{g}}+C\sum_{j=0}^{k}I_{j}(t)

(6.54)

for any
t\in[0,A]
. Moreover, there is a positive constant
C
such that

I_{k}(t)\leq(I_{k}(0)+C\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}I_{j}(0))\,e^{-{\delta\over 2}t}.

(6.55)

Here the constant
C
in (
6.54
) and (
6.55
) depend on
k,S
and the background Ricci-flat Type IIA structure
(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})
.

Proof:
In this proof, we use
C_{0}
to denote constants that do not depend on
k
or
S
, and we use
C
to denote a constant that may depend on
k
and
S
. From Lemma
2
, we have

\partial_{t}\alpha=\Delta\alpha+Rm*\alpha+d\mathcal{F},

(6.56)

where
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{E}_{1}*(\alpha+\nabla\alpha+\beta+\nabla\beta)
with
\mathcal{E}_{1}=O(\omega(t),\nabla\omega(t),\varphi(t),\nabla\varphi(t))

satisfy the estimate
|\mathcal{E}_{i}|_{C^{0}}(t)\leq C_{0}\epsilon
. We compute

\partial_{t}|\nabla^{k}\alpha|^{2}=2\langle\nabla^{k}\Delta\alpha,\nabla^{k}\alpha\rangle+2\langle\nabla^{k}d\mathcal{F},\nabla^{k}\alpha\rangle+\sum_{j=0}^{k}\nabla^{j}Rm*\nabla^{k-j}\alpha*\nabla^{k}\alpha

(6.57)

and

\Delta|\nabla^{k}\alpha|^{2}=2\langle\nabla^{k}\Delta\alpha,\nabla^{k}\alpha\rangle+2|\nabla^{k+1}\alpha|^{2}+\sum_{j=0}^{k}\nabla^{j}Rm*\nabla^{k-j}\alpha*\nabla^{k}\alpha.

(6.58)

Thus

(\partial_{t}-\Delta)|\nabla^{k}\alpha|^{2}=-2|\nabla^{k+1}\alpha|^{2}+2\langle\nabla^{k}d\mathcal{F},\nabla^{k}\alpha\rangle+\sum_{j=0}^{k}\nabla^{j}Rm*\nabla^{k-j}\alpha*\nabla^{k}\alpha

(6.59)

Integrating both sides over
M
, we obtain

\displaystyle{d\over dt}\int_{M}|\nabla^{k}\alpha|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{M}|\nabla^{k+1}\alpha|^{2}+\int_{M}\nabla^{k-1}d\mathcal{F}*\nabla^{k+1}\alpha

(6.60)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{k}\int_{M}\nabla^{j}Rm*\nabla^{k-j}\alpha*\nabla^{k}\alpha

Recall that

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{E}_{1}*(\alpha+\nabla\alpha+\beta+\nabla\beta)

(6.61)

with
\mathcal{E}_{1}=O(\chi)(1+\nabla\chi)
. We can compute

\displaystyle\nabla^{k-1}d\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}*(\nabla^{k}\alpha+\nabla^{k+1}\alpha+\nabla^{k}\beta+\nabla^{k+1}\beta)

(6.62)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\nabla^{k-j}\mathcal{E}_{1}*(\nabla^{j}\alpha+\nabla^{j+1}\alpha+\nabla^{j}\beta+\nabla^{j+1}\beta)

\displaystyle+\nabla^{k}{\cal E}_{1}*(\alpha+\nabla\alpha+\beta+\nabla\beta).

By Lemma
3
, we can formally write

\nabla^{j}\mathcal{E}_{1}=O(\chi)\sum_{j\!\leq\!i_{1}\!+\!\dots\!+\!i_{s}\!\leq\!j\!+\!1}\nabla^{i_{1}}\chi*\dots*\nabla^{i_{s}}\chi.

(6.63)

We can deal with those terms in a similar way as the estimate for
I_{1}(t)
in previous lemma. Note that,
\omega=\alpha+\tilde{\omega}
and
\varphi=\beta+\tilde{\varphi}
, we can use the replacements
\nabla^{\ell}\omega=\nabla^{\ell}\alpha+\nabla^{\ell}\tilde{\omega}
and
\nabla^{\ell}\varphi=\nabla^{\ell}\beta+\nabla^{\ell}\tilde{\varphi}
for any

. Notice that terms like
\nabla^{\ell}\tilde{\omega}
and
\nabla^{\ell}\tilde{\varphi}
are all bounded by Proposition
4
, therefore,

\displaystyle|\nabla^{j}{\cal E}_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(1+\!\sum_{i_{1}\!+\!\dots\!+\!i_{s}\!\leq\!j\!+\!1}|\nabla^{i_{1}}(\alpha,\beta)|\dots|\nabla^{i_{s}}(\alpha,\beta)|\right),

(6.64)

where
i_{1},\dots,i_{s}
are all positive integers. It follows that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\nabla^{k-j}\mathcal{E}_{1}*(\nabla^{j}\alpha+\nabla^{j+1}\alpha+\nabla^{j}\beta+\nabla^{j+1}\beta)\right|

(6.65)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(1+\!\sum_{i_{1}\!+\!\dots\!+\!i_{s}\!+j\!\leq\!k\!+\!2}|\nabla^{i_{1}}(\alpha,\beta)|\dots|\nabla^{i_{s}}(\alpha,\beta)||\nabla^{j}(\alpha,\beta)|\right),

where

in the summation ranges from
2
to

are less or equal than

[{k\over 2}]+1
. Therefore by our assumption that
|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{C^{[{k\over 2}]+1}}(t)+|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}|_{C^{[{k\over 2}]+1}}(t)\leq S
and Proposition

, we can bound all terms like

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\nabla^{k-j}\mathcal{E}_{1}*(\nabla^{j}\alpha+\nabla^{j+1}\alpha+\nabla^{j}\beta+\nabla^{j+1}\beta)\right|

(6.66)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\nabla^{j}\alpha|+|\nabla^{j}\beta|\right).

For the other terms, the same trick gives

|\mathcal{E}_{1}*(\nabla^{k}\alpha+\nabla^{k+1}\alpha+\nabla^{k}\beta+\nabla^{k+1}\beta)|\leq C_{0}\epsilon(|\nabla^{k}\alpha|+|\nabla^{k+1}\alpha|+|\nabla^{k}\beta|+|\nabla^{k+1}\beta|),

(6.67)

and

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}\mathcal{E}_{1}*(\alpha+\nabla\alpha+\beta+\nabla\beta)|\leq C_{0}\epsilon|\nabla^{k}{\cal E}_{1}|

(6.68)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\epsilon(|\nabla^{k+1}\alpha|+|\nabla^{k+1}\beta|)+C\sum_{j=0}^{k}(|\nabla^{j}\alpha|+|\nabla^{j}\beta|).

Plugging (
6.66
), (
6.67
) and (
6.68
) into (
6.62

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k-1}d{\cal F}|\leq C_{0}\epsilon(|\nabla^{k+1}\alpha|+|\nabla^{k+1}\beta|)+C\sum_{j=1}^{k}|\nabla^{j}\alpha|+|\nabla^{j}\beta|.

(6.69)

On the other hand, the curvature term in (

) can also be estimated as

\sum_{j=0}^{k}\nabla^{j}Rm*\nabla^{k-j}\alpha*\nabla^{k}\alpha\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{k}|\nabla^{k-j}\alpha||\nabla^{k}\alpha|.

(6.70)

We take
\epsilon_{0}
small such that
C_{0}
in (
6.69
) satisfies

. Hence, for any

, we can use Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to obtain

\displaystyle{d\over dt}\int_{M}|\nabla^{k}\alpha|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-{3\over 2}\int_{M}|\nabla^{k+1}\alpha|^{2}+{1\over 2}\int_{M}|\nabla^{k+1}\alpha||\nabla^{k+1}\beta|

(6.71)

\displaystyle+C\sum_{j=0}^{k}\int_{M}|\nabla^{j}\alpha|^{2}+|\nabla^{j}\beta|^{2}

By the same calculation, we also have

\displaystyle{d\over dt}\int_{M}|\nabla^{k}\beta|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-{3\over 2}\int_{M}|\nabla^{k+1}\beta|^{2}+{1\over 2}\int_{M}|\nabla^{k+1}\alpha||\nabla^{k+1}\beta|

(6.72)

\displaystyle+C\sum_{j=0}^{k}\int_{M}|\nabla^{j}\alpha|^{2}+|\nabla^{j}\beta|^{2}

The above two inequalities imply (
6.54
).

Next, we use (
6.54
) to derive the estimate (
6.55
). Recall that, for all
\ell
,

{d\over dt}I_{\ell}(t)\leq-I_{\ell+1}+C\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}I_{j}(t).

(6.73)

Taking any positive numbers
\eta_{\ell},\ell=0,1,\cdots,k-1
and setting
\eta_{k}=1
, we compute

\partial_{t}\left[I_{k}(t)+\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\eta_{\ell}I_{\ell}(t)\right]\leq-\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\left(\eta_{\ell-1}-C\sum_{j=0}^{k-\ell}\eta_{k-j}\right)I_{\ell}+C\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\eta_{\ell}\right)I_{0}(t)

(6.74)

Now, we can choose
\eta_{\ell}>1,\ell=1,\cdots,k-1
satisfying

\eta_{\ell-1}-C\sum_{j=0}^{k-\ell}\eta_{k-j}\geq\eta_{\ell}.

(6.75)

Note that this can be done inductively. For example, let
\ell=k
, the above requirement is simply
\eta_{k-1}-C\geq\eta_{k}=1
, which will determine
\eta_{k-1}
; next, taking
\ell=k-1
and we can make the choice for
\eta_{k-2}
. Then, we have

\partial_{t}\left[I_{k}(t)+\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\eta_{\ell}I_{\ell}(t)\right]\leq-\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\eta_{\ell}\,I_{\ell}(t)+C\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\eta_{\ell}\right)I_{0}(t)\leq C(\eta_{0},\cdots,\eta_{k-1})I_{0}(t)

(6.76)

Now, using the decay estimate
I_{0}(t)\leq e^{-\delta t}I_{0}(0)
and taking integration
\int_{0}^{t}\cdot\,ds
on both sides of

{d\over ds}\bigg[I_{k}(s)+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\eta_{j}I_{j}(s)\bigg]\leq C(\eta_{0},\cdots,\eta_{k-1})I_{0}(0)e^{-\delta s}

(6.77)

we obtain

I_{k}(t)\leq I_{k}(0)+C\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}I_{j}(0).

(6.78)

Next, we use a similar argument as in Lemma
5
to derive the decay estimate (
6.55
).

Denote
h_{k}(t)=I_{k}(t)+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\eta_{j}I_{j}(t)
. Then,

{d\over dt}h_{k}(t)\leq-h_{k}(t)+(\eta_{0}+C(\eta_{0},\cdots,\eta_{k-1}))I_{0}(t)\leq-h_{k}(t)+\tilde{C}e^{-\delta t}

(6.79)

where
\tilde{C}=I_{0}(0)(\eta_{0}+C(\eta_{0},\cdots,\eta_{k-1}))
is a uniform constant. Next, taking a constant
\delta_{1}>0
small such that
\delta/2\delta_{1}-\tilde{C}(\eta)\geq 1/2
, we have

\displaystyle{d\over dt}\bigg[h_{k}(t)+{1\over\delta_{1}}e^{-\delta t}\bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{d\over dt}h_{k}(t)-{\delta\over\delta_{1}}e^{-\delta t}\leq-h_{k}(t)-(\delta/\delta_{1}-\tilde{C})e^{-\delta t}

(6.80)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-h_{k}(t)-{\delta\over 2\delta_{1}}e^{-\delta t}=\left(\delta/2-1\right)h_{k}(t)-{\delta\over 2}\left[h_{k}(t)+{1\over\delta_{1}}e^{-\delta t}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-{\delta\over 2}\left[h_{k}(t)+{1\over\delta_{1}}e^{-\delta t}\right]

since
0<\delta<1
. This will give us the estimate

h_{k}(t)+{1\over\delta_{1}}e^{-\delta t}\leq\bigg[h_{k}(0)+{1\over\delta_{1}}\bigg]e^{-{\delta\over 2}t}.

(6.81)

Therefore,

h_{k}(t)\leq h_{k}(0)e^{-{\delta\over 2}t}.

(6.82)

This implies the decay of
I_{1}(t)
claimed in (
6.55
). Q.E.D.

From the above lemmas, we have obtained the
W^{2,k}_{\bar{g}}
estimates for
\alpha
and
\beta
. Then, we can apply the Sobolev embedding theorem to conclude

Theorem 5

There exists
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})>0
and

such that, for any

, if the flow is defined on an interval

(6.83)

for any

, where

is a constant depending only on
\bar{\omega}
,
\bar{\varphi}
,

,

Proof:
Let
\epsilon_{0}
and

be the positive numbers appeared in Lemma

. Write

when

\displaystyle I_{k}(t)\leq(I_{k}(0)+C\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}I_{j}(0))e^{-\delta t/4}\leq I_{k}(0)+C\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}I_{j}(0),

where

\bar{\varphi}
,
\bar{\omega}
,

(6.85)

for

6.3
Long time existence and convergence of the reparametrized Type IIA flow

The estimates obtained in the above theorem allow us to prove the long time existence and exponential convergence for the perturbed initial data.

Theorem 6

Let
(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})
be a fixed Ricci-flat Type IIA structure on a 6-manifold. Then there exists a positive constant
\epsilon_{0}^{\prime}>0
such that for any initial Type IIA structure
(\varphi_{0},\omega_{0})
with

|\varphi_{0}-\bar{\varphi}|_{W^{10,2}}+|\omega_{0}-\bar{\omega}|_{W^{10,2}}<\epsilon_{0}^{\prime},

(6.86)

the solution
(\varphi(t),\omega(t))
of the reparametrized Type IIA flow exists for all time and converges exponentially to a Ricci-flat Type IIA structure
(\tilde{\varphi},\tilde{\omega})
in the
C^{k}
-norm for any
k
.

Proof:
Choose
\epsilon_{0}
and
\delta
as before. By Sobolev inequality and using (
6.85
) with
k=10
, we know there exists a constant
C_{1}(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})
such that

|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{C^{6}}(t)+|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}|_{C^{6}}(t)<\epsilon_{0}
for all
t\in[0,A]
implies

|\alpha(t)|_{C^{6,\gamma}}+|\beta(t)|_{C^{6,\gamma}}\leq C_{1}(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})(|\alpha(0)|_{W^{10,2}}+|\beta(0)|_{W^{10,2}}).

(6.87)

On the other hand, by Proposition (
4
) and Sobolev imbedding theorem, we know there exist constants
C_{2}(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})
and
C_{3}(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})
such that

\displaystyle|\tilde{\omega}-\bar{\omega}|_{C^{6,\gamma}}+|\tilde{\varphi}-\bar{\varphi}|_{C^{6,\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})(|\omega_{0}-\bar{\omega}|_{W^{10,2}}+|\varphi_{0}-\bar{\varphi}|_{W^{10,2}}),

(6.88)

\displaystyle|\alpha(0)|_{W^{10,2}}+|\beta(0)|_{W^{10,2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{3}(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})(|\omega_{0}-\bar{\omega}|_{W^{10,2}}+|\varphi_{0}-\bar{\varphi}|_{W^{10,2}}).

(6.89)

Combining (
6.87
), (
6.88
) and (
6.89
) we get

\displaystyle|\omega(t)-\bar{\omega}|_{C^{6,\gamma}}+|\varphi(t)-\bar{\varphi}|_{C^{6,\gamma}}

(6.90)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\alpha(t)|_{C^{6,\gamma}}+|\tilde{\omega}-\bar{\omega}|_{C^{6,\gamma}}+|\beta(t)|_{C^{6,\gamma}}+|\tilde{\varphi}-\bar{\varphi}|_{C^{6,\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})(|\alpha(0)|_{W^{10,2}}+|\beta(0)|_{W^{10,2}})+C_{2}(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})(|\omega_{0}-\bar{\omega}|_{W^{10,2}}+|\varphi_{0}-\bar{\varphi}|_{W^{10,2}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(C_{1}(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})C_{3}(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})+C_{2}(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega}))(|\omega_{0}-\bar{\omega}|_{W^{10,2}}+|\varphi_{0}-\bar{\varphi}|_{W^{10,2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M(|\omega_{0}-\bar{\omega}|_{W^{10,2}}+|\varphi_{0}-\bar{\varphi}|_{W^{10,2}}),

where
M=C_{1}C_{3}+C_{2}
depends only on
(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})
. Now choose
\epsilon_{0}^{\prime}=\epsilon_{0}/M
. We need to show that (
6.86
) implies the long-time existence and exponential convergence of the reparametrized Type IIA flow.

Let
A
be the supremum of time
T
such that on the interval
[0,T]
the following hold:

i).

the solution
(\varphi(t),\omega(t))
of the reparametrized Type IIA flow exists;

ii).

the solution satisfies
|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{C^{6}}(t)+|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}|_{C^{6}}(t)\leq\epsilon_{0}
.

Indeed, such an
A>0
must exist by the short time existence of the reparametrized Type IIA flow. If
A<\infty
, then at finite time
A
we must have

|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{C^{6}}(t)+|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}|_{C^{6}}(t)=\epsilon_{0}.

(6.91)

However, for our choice of
\epsilon_{0}^{\prime}
, the estimate (

\displaystyle|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{C^{6}}(t)+|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}|_{C^{6}}(t)

\displaystyle M(|\omega_{0}-\bar{\omega}|_{W^{10,2}}+|\varphi_{0}-\bar{\varphi}|_{W^{10,2}})

\displaystyle<

6.4
Stability of Type IIA flow

In this section, we will prove that a solution of the reparametrized Type IIA flow (
2.13
) converges exponentially fast to a Ricci-flat Type IIA structure (
\tilde{\varphi},\tilde{\omega}
) implies the corresponding solution of the Type IIA flow (
1.1
) converges exponentially to a (possibly distinct) Ricci-flat Type IIA structure.

Theorem 7

Let
(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})
be a Ricci-flat Type IIA structure on a 6-manifold. There is a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})
such that for any Type IIA structure
(\varphi_{0},\omega_{0})\in\mathcal{U}_{(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})}=\{(\varphi,\omega)\,:\,|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{W^{10,2}}+|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}|_{W^{10,2}}<\epsilon^{\prime}_{0}\}
, the Type IIA flow (
1.1
) with initial value
\varphi_{0}
and symplectic form
\omega_{0}
exists for all
t\in[0,\infty)
and converges smoothly to
\varphi_{\infty}
which gives a Ricci-flat Type IIA structure
(\varphi_{\infty},\omega_{0})
.

Proof
:
Theorem
6
shows that the solution
(\varphi(t),\omega(t))
of the reparametrized Type IIA flow (
2.13
) with initial data
(\varphi_{0},\omega_{0})
converges exponentially to a Ricci-flat Type IIA structure
(\tilde{\varphi},\tilde{\omega})
smoothly. Let
\phi(t)
be the solution of the Type IIA flow (
1.1
) with initial data
\varphi_{0}
. Then

\phi(t)=f_{t}^{*}\varphi(t),

(6.93)

and the associated metric
\textrm{g}(t)=f_{t}^{*}g(t)
, where
f_{t}
is a family of diffeomorphisms satisfying

\partial_{t}f_{t}(x)=-V(\varphi(t))|_{f_{t}(x)}

(6.94)

with
f_{0}=Id
, for all
x\in M
. Recall that
V(\varphi(t))
is the vector field defined in (
3.2
), which involves the first order covariant derivative of
\varphi(t)
. Since
\varphi(t)
exists for all
t\in[0,\infty)
and converges exponentially to
\tilde{\varphi}
smoothly, the vector field
V(\varphi(t))
also exists for all
t\in[0,\infty)
. Moreover, recall Proposition
2
, we conclude that it converges to
V(\tilde{\varphi})=0
exponentially.

Now, back to the ODE for the diffeomorphism (
6.94
). Using the exponential convergence
V(x,t)\to 0
, we know that the diffeomorphisms
f_{t}
exists for all
t\in[0,\infty)
(Lemma 3.15 in
[
9
]
) and converges to a limit map
f_{\infty}
smoothly. For our setting, it is easy to see that the limit map
f_{\infty}
is also a diffeomorphism. Indeed, by the evolution equation for
\omega(t)
in the reparametrized Type IIA flow (
2.13
), we know that
f_{t}^{*}\omega(t)=\omega_{0}
. It follows that
f_{\infty}^{*}\tilde{\omega}=\omega_{0}
. Therefore, the differential of
f_{\infty}
is nondegenerate as
\omega_{0}
is. The inverse function theorem then implies that
f_{\infty}
is a local diffeomorphism. Since
f_{0}=Id
the identity map and each
f_{t}
is a diffeomorphism which is isotopic to the identity map,
f_{\infty}
is a surjective local diffeomorphism homotopic to identity. It implies that
f_{\infty}
is a degree one covering map. Hence, it must be a diffeomorphism of
M
.

Summarize the above discussion,
\phi(t)=f_{t}^{*}\varphi(t)
is a long time solution of the Type IIA flow and converges to
\phi_{\infty}:=f_{\infty}^{*}\tilde{\varphi}
smoothly. Q.E.D.

Finally, we would like to mention that the dynamic stability of the Type IIB flow has been proved recently by Bedulli and Vezzoni
[
3
]
.

7
Applications in symplectic geometry

Definition 2

We say a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
is Kähler, if it admits a compatible and integrable complex structure.

Given
M
, clearly being Kähler or not is a property of the symplectic form
\omega
, and this property is invariant under the diffeomorphism group action. A fundamental question in symplectic geometry is whether the property of being Kähler is stable or not under small perturbations. More precisely, suppose we know that a compact symplectic manifold
(M,\bar{\omega})
is Kähler. Then for a symplectic form
\omega
sufficiently close to
\bar{\omega}
, can we always deduce that
(M,\omega)
is also Kähler?

When
\omega
has the same cohomology class as
\bar{\omega}
, the statement is true because of Moser’s trick
[
23
]
. Therefore it is essential to perturb the de Rham cohomology class of
\bar{\omega}
. A weaker version of such deformations was studied by de Bartolomeis
[
2
]
.

As an application of the stability of the Type IIA flow, we shall prove that the Kähler property is stable under perturbation if we assume that
(M,\bar{\omega})
is a Calabi-Yau 3-fold.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

•

Step 1: By our assumption, there exists an integrable complex structure compatible with
(M,\bar{\omega})
, making it a Kähler manifold. Since
c_{1}(M,\bar{\omega})=0
, using the Calabi-Yau theorem and the Moser’s trick, we can find a compatible and integrable complex structure
\bar{J}
, such that the corresponding metric is Ricci-flat. By passing to a finite cover if necessary, there exists a Type IIA structure
(M,\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})
such that the complex structure associated to
\bar{\varphi}
is exactly
\bar{J}
. By
[
12
, Theorem 9]
, we may further assume that
(M,\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})
is Ricci-flat, namely it is a stationary point of the Type IIA flow.

•

Step 2: We are given
\omega
, a small perturbation of
\bar{\omega}
, the goal is to upgrade it to a small perturbation
(\varphi,\omega)
, of the Type IIA structure
(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})
with desired estimates. In fact, we shall prove a slightly more general statement which does not require
\bar{g}
to be Ricci-flat.

Proposition 6

Let
(M,\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})
be a fixed Type IIA structure such that the associated almost complex structure
J_{\bar{\varphi}}
is integrable. Let
\bar{g}
be the Riemannian metric induced by
(\bar{\varphi},\bar{\omega})
. There exists positive constants
\epsilon
and
C
such that for any symplectic form
\omega
such that
|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}<\epsilon
, one can find a Type IIA structure
(\varphi,\omega)
with estimate

|\varphi-\bar{\varphi}|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}\leq C|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}.

(7.1)

Proof of Proposition
6
: Let
\omega_{s}=s\omega+(1-s)\bar{\omega}
for
0\leq s\leq 1
. The goal is to construct a smooth family
\varphi_{s}
for
0\leq s\leq 1
satisfying

\displaystyle\varphi_{s}\textrm{ is positive},

(7.2)

\displaystyle d\varphi_{s}=0,

(7.3)

\displaystyle\omega_{s}\wedge\varphi_{s}=0.

(7.4)

First let us assume we already have this family
\varphi_{s}
. By taking the
s
-derivative of (
7.4
), we get
\dot{\omega}_{s}\wedge\varphi_{s}+\omega_{s}\wedge\dot{\varphi}_{s}=0
. At the cohomology level, this is

[\omega_{s}]\wedge\dot{[\varphi_{s}]}=-\dot{[\omega_{s}]}\wedge[\varphi_{s}]\in H^{5}(M;{\bf R}).

(7.5)

Since
J_{\bar{\varphi}}
is integrable, we know
[\bar{\omega}]
satisfies the Hard Lefschetz theorem, hence that
[\bar{\omega}]^{2}\wedge\cdot:H^{1}(M;{\bf R})\to H^{5}(M;{\bf R})
is an isomorphism. As
[\omega_{s}]
is a small perturbation of
[\bar{\omega}]
, we can choose
\epsilon
very small so that
[\omega_{s}]^{2}\wedge:H^{1}(M;{\bf R})\to H^{5}(M;{\bf R})
is also an isomorphism for every
s
, therefore there exists a unique class
[\alpha_{s}]\in H^{1}(M;{\bf R})
such that

[\omega_{s}]^{2}\wedge[\alpha_{s}]=[\omega_{s}]\wedge\dot{[\varphi_{s}]}=-\dot{[\omega_{s}]}\wedge[\varphi_{s}],

(7.6)

hence

[\alpha_{s}]=-[\omega_{s}]^{-2}(\dot{[\omega_{s}]}\wedge[\varphi_{s}]).

(7.7)

Now let us construct the family
\varphi_{s}
. Consider the following linear ODE on the cohomology group
H^{3}(M;{\bf R})

\begin{cases}&\dfrac{d}{ds}[\varphi_{s}]=-[\omega_{s}]\wedge([\omega_{s}]^{-2}(\dot{[\omega_{s}]}\wedge[\varphi_{s}])),\\
&[\varphi_{s}]\bigg|_{s=0}=[\bar{\varphi}]\end{cases}

(7.8)

The unique solution
[\varphi_{s}]
of this ODE satisfies (
7.5
) by its construction. Now let
{\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]
be the harmonic representative of the class
[\varphi_{s}]
with respect to the fixed metric
\bar{g}
. In particular we know that
{\cal H}[\varphi_{0}]=\bar{\varphi}
as
J_{\bar{\varphi}}
is integrable. Moreover, we have the isomorphism
H^{3}(M;{\bf R})\cong\mathcal{H}^{3}(M)
, where
\mathcal{H}^{3}(M)
is the space of harmonic 3-forms with respect to the metric
\bar{g}
. Since any two norms on the finite-dimensional spaces
\mathcal{H}^{3}(M)
are equivalent, there exists a constant
C
, such that

\displaystyle|{\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]-\bar{\varphi}|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|[\varphi_{s}]-[\varphi_{0}]|_{C^{0}_{\bar{g}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{1}\left|[\omega_{s}]\wedge[\omega_{s}]^{-2}([\dot{\omega}_{s}]\wedge\cdot)\right|_{\bar{g}}\left|[\varphi_{s}]\right|_{C^{0}_{\bar{g}}}ds,

where
|\cdot|_{\bar{g}}
is the operator norm on
H^{*}(M;{\bf R})
induced by
\bar{g}
. Because
[\omega_{s}]\wedge\cdot
,
[\omega_{s}]^{-2}
are bounded linear operators on cohomology groups,
[\dot{\omega}_{s}]=[\omega-\bar{\omega}]
, and that
|[\varphi_{s}]|_{C^{0}_{\bar{g}}}
is bounded as well, we get

|{\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]-\bar{\varphi}|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}\leq C|[\omega-\bar{\omega}]|_{\bar{g}}\leq C|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}.

(7.9)

With this construction, we know that
{\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]
is closed and positive, because it is harmonic and
W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}
-close to
\bar{\varphi}
. The only problem is that
{\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]
is not necessarily primitive with respect to
\omega_{s}
in the pointwise manner.

However, by integrating (
7.5
) over
s
, we know that at the cohomology level
[{\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]\wedge\omega_{s}]=[0]
, therefore
{\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]\wedge\omega_{s}
is
d
-exact. Moreover,

\displaystyle|{\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]\wedge\omega_{s}|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]\wedge\omega_{s}-\bar{\varphi}\wedge\bar{\omega}|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}

(7.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\omega_{s}\wedge({\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]-\bar{\varphi})|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}+|\bar{\varphi}\wedge(\omega_{s}-\bar{\omega})|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(|{\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]-\bar{\varphi}|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}+|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}.

Let
\gamma_{s}=d^{\star}\Box_{\bar{g}}^{-1}({\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]\wedge\omega_{s})
, where
d^{\star}
and
\Box_{\bar{g}}
are the adjoint of
d
and the Hodge-Laplacian operator with respect to the metric
\bar{g}
. Since
{\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]\wedge\omega_{s}
is
d
-exact, we know
\Box_{\bar{g}}^{-1}({\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]\wedge\omega_{s})\in{\cal H}^{4}(M)^{\perp}
is defined, and
d^{\star}\Box^{-1}_{\bar{g}}
is the so-called Neumann operator. From its construction we know that
d\gamma_{s}={\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]\wedge\omega_{s}
. In addition, by standard theory of linear PDE, the following estimate holds

|\gamma_{s}|_{W^{k+1,2}_{\bar{g}}}\leq C|{\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]\wedge\omega_{s}|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}\leq C|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}.

(7.11)

As
\omega_{s}
is a small perturbation of
\bar{\omega}
, we know that
\omega_{s}\wedge\cdot:\Omega^{2}(M)\to\Omega^{4}(M)
is an isomorphism pointwise, therefore there exists a unique 2-form
\lambda_{s}
, such that
\gamma_{s}=\omega_{s}\wedge\lambda_{s}
. It follows that

|\lambda_{s}|_{W^{k+1,2}_{\bar{g}}}\leq C|\omega-\bar{\omega}|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}.

(7.12)

The above construction indicates that

{\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]\wedge\omega_{s}=d\gamma_{s}=d\lambda_{s}\wedge\omega_{s}.

(7.13)

Let
\varphi_{s}={\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]-d\lambda_{s}
, then
\varphi_{s}
is primitive with respect to
\omega_{s}
pointwise. Moreover,
[\varphi_{s}]=[{\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]]
and we have the estimate

\displaystyle|\varphi_{s}-\bar{\varphi}|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|{\cal H}[\varphi_{s}]-\bar{\varphi}|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}+|d\lambda_{s}|_{W^{k,2}_{\bar{g}}}

(7.14)

\displaystyle\leq

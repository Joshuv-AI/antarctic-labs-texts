On a conjecture about generalized integration operators on Hardy spaces Thanks: † \dagger Corresponding author.
Rong Yang and Songxiao Li †

Abstract
. A conjecture posed by Chalmoukis in 2020 states that if T g , a : H p → H q ​ ( 0 < q < p < ∞ ) T_{g,a}:H^{p}\to H^{q}(0<q<p<\infty) is bounded, then g g must be in H p ​ q p − q H^{\frac{pq}{p-q}} . In this article, we provide a positive answer to the aforementioned conjecture. We also consider the compactness of T g , a : H p → H q ​ ( 0 < q < p < ∞ ) T_{g,a}:H^{p}\to H^{q}(0<q<p<\infty) . Keywords : Integration operator, tent space, Hardy space.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the unit disc on the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
,
\mathbb{T}
its boundary, and
H(\mathbb{D})
be the set of
all analytic functions in
\mathbb{D}
. We denote by
{\mathbb{N}}
the set of positive integers.
The Hardy space
H^{p}(0<p<\infty)
consists of all
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
for which

\|f\|_{H^{p}}^{p}=\sup_{0\leq r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}<\infty.

Let
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
0\leq k<n
. The generalized integration operator
T_{g}^{n,k}
is defined by

T_{g}^{n,k}f(z)=I^{n}\left(f^{(k)}(z)g^{(n-k)}(z)\right),\quad f\in H(\mathbb{D}).

Here,
I^{n}
is the
n
-th iteration of the integration operator
If(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(t)dt
.
The operator
T_{g}^{n,k}
was first introduced by Chalmoukis
[
5
]
. In particular, when
n=1
and
k=0
, we have

T_{g}^{1,0}f(z)=\int_{0}^{z}f(\eta)g^{\prime}(\eta)d\eta=T_{g}f(z).

In 1977, the Volterra integration operator
T_{g}
was first introduced and stuided by Pommerenke in
[
13
]
. It was proved that
T_{g}
is bounded on
H^{2}
if and only if
g\in BMOA
. In 1995, Aleman and Siskakis proved that
T_{g}
is bounded on
H^{p}
(
p\geq 1
) if and only if
g\in BMOA
in
[
2
]
.
In
[
3
]
, they proved that
T_{g}
is bounded on
A^{p}
if and only if
g\in\mathcal{B}
, the Bloch space.
See
[
1
,
10
,
8
]
and the references therein for more study of the operator
T_{g}
.
In
[
5
]
, Chalmoukis studied the boundedness of the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}
between different Hardy spaces. For example, he showed that
T_{g}^{n,0}:H^{p}\to H^{p}
is bounded if and only if
g\in BMOA
,
T_{g}^{n,k}:H^{p}\to H^{p}
is bounded if and only if
g\in\mathcal{B}
when
k\geq 1
, while
T_{g}^{n,k}:H^{p}\to H^{q}
is bounded if and only if

\sup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}+n-k}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|<\infty
when
0<p<q<\infty
.
From the results just mentioned, we see that the operator
T_{g}^{n,k}
has a very different behavior from that of
T_{g}
.

Let
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
a=(a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
. The generalized integration operator
T_{g,a}
is defined by (see
[
5
]
)

\displaystyle T_{g,a}=T_{g}^{n,0}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}a_{k}T_{g}^{n,k}.

Chalmoukis proposed a conjecture in
[
5
]
.
Is it true that if
T_{g,a}:H^{p}\to H^{q}
,
0<q<p<\infty
is bounded, then
g
must be in
H^{s}
,
\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}?

Due to the relationship between
T_{g,a}
and
T_{g}^{n,k}
, to characterize the boundedness of
T_{g,a}
, it suffices to characterize the boundedness of
T_{g}^{n,k}
for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
such that
0\leq k<n
. In this paper, we give a positive answer to this conjecture. More specifically, the main result of this paper can be stated as follows.

Main Theorem.
Let
0<q<p<\infty
,
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
such that
0\leq k<n
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)
The operator
T_{g}^{n,k}:H^{p}\to H^{q}
is bounded.

(ii)
The operator
T_{g}^{n,k}:H^{p}\to H^{q}
is compact.

(iii)

g\in H^{s}
, where
\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we mainly state some definitions and preliminary results. In Section 3, we provide the proof of the main theorem.

In this paper, the symbol
A\lesssim B
means that there exists a positive finite constant
C
such that
A\leq CB
. The value of
C
may change from one occurrence to another. The expression
A\asymp B
means that both
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
hold.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we state some notations, lemmas and propositions, which will be used in the proof of main results in this paper.

Let
\beta(z,w)
be the hyperbolic metric on
\mathbb{D}
.
If
\beta(z,w)<r

1-|w|\asymp 1-|z|
.
Let

be the hyperbolic disc of radius

z\in\mathbb{D}
.
Let
\eta\in\mathbb{T}
and
\zeta>\frac{1}{2}
. The non-tangential region
\Gamma_{\zeta}(\eta)
is defined by

\Gamma(\eta)=\Gamma_{\zeta}(\eta)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z-\eta|<\zeta(1-|z|^{2})\}.

The following lemma is very important in this paper and will be used frequently.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
14
, Lemma 2.3]

Let
\zeta>1,r\geq 0
and
\eta\in\mathbb{T}
. If
\zeta_{+}=(\zeta+1)e^{2r}-1
, then

\displaystyle D(z,r)\subset\Gamma_{\zeta_{+}}(\eta)

(1)

for all
z\in\Gamma_{\zeta}(\eta)
.

In the rest of this paper, we will denote
\Gamma_{\zeta_{+}}(\eta)
by
\widetilde{\Gamma}(\eta)
in (
1
) when we need not mention the aperture
\zeta_{+}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
0<p,q<\infty
and
\alpha>-2
. The tent space
T_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
consists of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
with

\displaystyle\|f\|_{T_{p}^{q}(\alpha)}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty.

In particular, for
\alpha=0
, we write
T_{p}^{q}
instead of
T_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
.

For
p=\infty
and
0<q<\infty
, the tent space
T_{\infty}^{q}(\alpha)
consists of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{T_{\infty}^{q}(\alpha)}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\operatorname{esssup}_{z\in\Gamma(\eta)}|f(z)|\right)^{q}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty.

For
q=\infty
and
0<p<\infty
,
the tent space
T_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)
consists of all measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
for which

\|f\|_{T_{p}^{\infty}(\alpha)}=\operatorname{esssup}_{\eta\in\mathbb{T}}\left(\sup_{u\in\Gamma(\eta)}\frac{1}{1-|u|^{2}}\int_{S(u)}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha+1}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,

where

S(re^{i\theta})=\left\{\lambda e^{it}:|t-\theta|\leq\frac{1-r}{2},1-\lambda\leq 1-r\right\}

for
re^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{D}\backslash\{0\}
and
S(0)=\mathbb{D}
.

Tent spaces were initially introduced by Coifman, Meyer and Stein in
[
6
]
to study problems in harmonic analysis. They provided a general framework for questions regarding some important spaces such as Hardy spaces and Bergman spaces. The aperture
\zeta
of the non-tangential region
\Gamma_{\zeta}(\eta)
is suppressed in the above definition since any two apertures generate the same function space with equivalent quasinorms. Denote
T_{p}^{q}(\alpha)\cap H(\mathbb{D})
by
AT_{p}^{q}(\alpha)
.
Notice that the definition of
T^{q}_{\infty}(\alpha)=T^{q}_{\infty}
is independent of
\alpha
.
By the non-tangential maximal function characterization of the Hardy space, we see that
AT^{q}_{\infty}=H^{q}
(see
[
15
]
).
From
[
12
, Theorem G]
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
0<s<\infty
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
and
f(0)=0
, then

\|f\|_{H^{s}}^{s}\asymp\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)}|f^{(n)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}|d\eta|.

The sequence
Z=\{a_{j}\}
is a separated sequence if there is a constant
\tau>0
with
\beta(a_{j},a_{j})\geq\tau
for
k\neq j
.
For
r>\kappa>0
, the sequence
Z=\{a_{j}\}
is said to be an
(r,\kappa)
-lattice if

\mathbb{D}=\bigcup_{k}D(a_{j},r)
and
the sets
D(a_{j},\kappa)
are pairwise disjoint.
It is obvious that any
(r,\kappa)
-lattice is a separated sequence.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
0<p,q<\infty
and
Z=\{a_{j}\}
be an
(r,\kappa)
-lattice. The tent space
T_{p}^{q}(Z)
consists of all
\{x_{j}\}
such that

\displaystyle\|\{x_{j}\}\|_{T_{p}^{q}(Z)}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\sum_{a_{j}\in\Gamma(\eta)}|x_{j}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty.

For
p=\infty
and
0<q<\infty
, the tent space
T_{\infty}^{q}(Z)
consists of all
\{x_{j}\}
with

\|\{x_{j}\}\|_{T_{\infty}^{q}(Z)}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sup_{a_{j}\in\Gamma(\eta)}|x_{j}|^{q}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty.

For
q=\infty
and
0<p<\infty
, the tent space
T^{\infty}_{p}(Z)
consists of all
\{x_{j}\}
for which

\|\{x_{j}\}\|_{T_{p}^{\infty}(Z)}=\operatorname{esssup}_{\eta\in\mathbb{T}}\left(\sup_{u\in\Gamma(\eta)}\frac{1}{1-|u|^{2}}\sum_{a_{j}\in S(u)}|x_{j}|^{p}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

Analogously, the aperture
\zeta
of the non-tangential region
\Gamma_{\zeta}(\eta)
is suppressed in the above definition since any two apertures generate the tent spaces of sequences with equivalent quasinorms.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
10

Let
0<p<\infty
and
\alpha\geq 0
. There exists
r_{0}\in(0,1)
so that if

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sup_{z\in\Gamma(\eta)}|f(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}|d\eta|\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sup_{a_{j}\in\Gamma(\eta)}|f(a_{j})|^{p}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{\alpha}|d\eta|,

whenever

such that the left-hand-side is finite.

The following lemma is very important, and we can find it in
[

]
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let

,

, and

-lattice. Then the operator

We will use the following proposition, which is a factorization of tent spaces of sequences.

Proposition 2.7
.

[
1
, Proposition 2.16]

Let

and

be an
(r,\kappa)
-lattice. If

,

The following proposition play a key role in the proof of our main theorem.
From now on,

is the conjugate exponent of

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1

and

.

Proposition 2.8
.

[
4

Let

-lattice . Then
\left(T_{p}^{q}(Z)\right)^{*}\cong T_{p^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}}(Z)

\langle\{c_{j}\},\{d_{j}\}\rangle=\sum_{j}c_{j}d_{j}(1-|a_{j}|^{2}),\quad\{c_{j}\}\in T_{p}^{q}(Z).

In fact,

\|\{d_{j}\}\|_{T_{p^{\prime}}^{q^{\prime}}(Z)}\asymp\sup\left\{\left|\sum_{j}c_{j}d_{j}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})\right|:\|\{c_{j}\}\|_{T_{p}^{q}(Z)}=1\right\}.

.

[

3.
Proof of the main theorem

Proof.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i).
It is obvious.

(i)\Rightarrow(iii).

Let
Z=\{a_{j}\}
be an
(r,\kappa)
-lattice and
\{x_{j}\}\in T_{2}^{p}(Z)
. Let
r_{j}:[0,1]\rightarrow\{-1,1\}
be the Radermacher functions. For
L>\max\{1,\frac{2}{p}\}
, set

F_{u}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}x_{j}r_{j}(u)\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{L}}{(1-\overline{a_{j}}z)^{L}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Using Proposition
2.6
, we get
\|F_{u}\|_{H^{p}}\lesssim\|\{x_{j}\}\|_{T_{2}^{p}(Z)}.

By the assumption and Lemma
2.3
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)}|(T_{g}^{n,k}F_{u})^{(n)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}\|^{q}\|F_{u}\|_{H^{p}}^{q}\lesssim\|T_{g}^{n,k}\|^{q}\|\{x_{j}\}\|_{T_{2}^{p}(Z)}^{q}.

Integrating both sides with respect to
u
from
0
to
1
,
using Fubini’s theorem and Kahane’s inequality (see
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Gamma(\eta)}|F_{u}^{(k)}(z)|^{2}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2}dA(z)du\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)}|F_{u}^{(k)}(z)|^{2}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}du|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}\|^{q}\|\{x_{j}\}\|_{T_{2}^{p}(Z)}^{q}.

Applying Khinchine’s inequality (see
[
9
]
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)}\left(\sum_{j}|x_{j}|^{2}\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{2L}}{|1-\overline{a_{j}}z|^{2L+2k}}\right)|g^{n-k}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}\|^{q}\|\{x_{j}\}\|_{T_{2}^{p}(Z)}^{q}.

Let
w_{j}=|g^{(n-k)}(a_{j})|(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{n-k}
.
From Lemma
2.1
we get
\cup_{z\in\Gamma(\eta)}D(z,4r)\subset\widetilde{\Gamma}(\eta)
. Using Proposition
2.9
, Fubini’s theorem and Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\sum_{a_{j}\in\Gamma(\eta)}|x_{j}|^{2}|w_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\sum_{a_{j}\in\Gamma(\eta)}|x_{j}|^{2}|g^{(n-k)}(a_{j})|^{2}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{2n-2k}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\sum_{a_{j}\in\Gamma(\eta)}|x_{j}|^{2}\int_{D(a_{j},4r)}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{2}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2}(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{2L}}{|1-\overline{a_{j}}z|^{2L+2k}}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}(\eta)}\sum_{j}|x_{j}|^{2}\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{2L}}{|1-\overline{a_{j}}z|^{2L+2k}}\chi_{D(a_{j},4r)}(z)|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\widetilde{\Gamma}(\eta)}\left(\sum_{j}|x_{j}|^{2}\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{2L}}{|1-\overline{a_{j}}z|^{2L+2k}}\right)|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}\|^{q}\|\{x_{j}\}\|_{T_{2}^{p}(Z)}^{q}.

Let
b>\max\left\{\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{2}\right\}
. For any
\{l_{j}\}\in T_{\frac{2b}{2b-1}}^{\frac{bq}{bq-1}}(Z)
,
using Fubini’s theorem and Hölder’s inequality twice, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j}|l_{j}x_{j}^{\frac{1}{b}}w_{j}^{\frac{1}{b}}|(1-|a_{j}|^{2})

(2)

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\sum_{a_{j}\in\Gamma(\eta)}|l_{j}||x_{j}|^{\frac{1}{b}}|w_{j}|^{\frac{1}{b}}\right)|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\sum_{a_{j}\in\Gamma(\eta)}|x_{j}|^{2}|w_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2b}}\left(\sum_{a_{j}\in\Gamma(\eta)}|l_{j}|^{\frac{2b}{2b-1}}\right)^{\frac{2b-1}{2b}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\sum_{a_{j}\in\Gamma(\eta)}|x_{j}|^{2}|w_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{1}{bq}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\sum_{a_{j}\in\Gamma(\eta)}|l_{j}|^{\frac{2b}{2b-1}}\right)^{\frac{q(2b-1)}{2(bq-1)}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{bq-1}{bq}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}\|^{\frac{1}{b}}\|\{x_{j}\}\|_{T_{2}^{p}(Z)}^{\frac{1}{b}}\|\{l_{j}\}\|_{T_{\frac{2b}{2b-1}}^{\frac{bq}{bq-1}}(Z)}.

Applying Proposition
2.7
we get

\{l_{j}x_{j}^{\frac{1}{b}}\}\in T_{1}^{\frac{qbs}{pbq-p+q}}(Z)=T_{\frac{2b}{2b-1}}^{\frac{bq}{bq-1}}(Z)\cdot T_{2b}^{pb}(Z).

By Proposition
2.8
,
we obtain

\left(T_{1}^{\frac{pbq}{pbq-p+q}}(Z)\right)^{*}=T_{\infty}^{\frac{pbq}{p-q}}(Z).

By
(\ref{4.5})
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left\{|w_{j}|^{\frac{1}{b}}\right\}\right\|_{T_{\infty}^{\frac{pbq}{p-q}}(Z)}=\left\|\left\{|w_{j}|\right\}\right\|_{T_{\infty}^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}(Z)}^{\frac{1}{b}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\sup_{a_{j}\in\Gamma(\eta)}|g^{(n-k)}(a_{j})|(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{n-k}\right)^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{p-q}{pbq}}<\infty.

Thus, we have
|g^{(n-k)}(a_{j})|(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{n-k}\in T_{\infty}^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}(Z)=T^{s}_{\infty}(Z)
.
By Lemma
2.5
, we obtain

g^{(n-k)}(\cdot)(1-|\cdot|^{2})^{n-k}\in AT_{\infty}^{s}
, i.e.,
g\in H^{s}
.

(iii)\Rightarrow(ii).

Assume that

g\in H^{s}
. Then
g^{(n-k)}(\cdot)(1-|\cdot|^{2})^{n-k}\in AT_{\infty}^{s}=H^{s}
.
Using Lemma
2.3
and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}f\|_{H^{q}}^{q}

(3)

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)}|(T_{g}^{n,k}f)^{(n)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)}|f^{(k)}(z)|^{2}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\sup_{z\in\Gamma(\eta)}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2k}\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}\cdot\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)}|f^{(k)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2k-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\sup_{z\in\Gamma(\eta)}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{n-k}\right)^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{p-q}{p}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)}|f^{(k)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2k-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|g^{(n-k)}\|^{q}_{AT_{\infty}^{s}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)}|f^{(k)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2k-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|g^{(n-k)}\|^{q}_{H^{s}}\|f\|^{q}_{H^{p}}.

Thus,
T_{g}^{n,k}:H^{p}\to H^{q}
is bounded.

Consider the sequence
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset H^{p}
such that
\sup_{j}\|f_{j}\|_{H^{p}}<\infty
. This ensures that
\{f_{j}\}
is uniformly bounded on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
. By Montel’s theorem, it forms a normal family. Hence, we can extract a subsequence
\{f_{n_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
which converges uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
to
f\in H(\mathbb{D})
. According to Fatou’s Lemma,
f\in H^{p}
. Define
h_{j}=f_{n_{j}}-f
. Then
h_{j}\in H^{p}
. Now, we only need to prove that

\lim_{j\to\infty}\|T_{g}^{n,k}h_{j}\|_{H^{q}}=0,

which implies that
T_{g}^{n,k}:H^{p}\to H^{q}
is compact.
From the fact that

g^{(n-k)}(\cdot)(1-|\cdot|^{2})^{n-k}\in AT_{\infty}^{s}=H^{s}
,
for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
R_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\sup_{z\in\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R)}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{n-k}\right)^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{p-q}{pq}}<\epsilon

when
R\geq R_{0}
.
Choose
j_{0}
such that
\sup_{j\geq j_{0},|z|\leq R_{0}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|<\epsilon
. Using
(\ref{bb})
, we get

\displaystyle\|T_{g}^{n,k}h_{j}\|_{H^{q}}^{q}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\cap\{|z|\leq R_{0}\}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|^{2}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left(\int_{\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R_{0})}}|h_{j}^{(k)}(z)|^{2}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|^{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2n-2}dA(z)\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}|d\eta|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{q}+\|h_{j}\|_{H^{p}}^{q}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}\left(\sup_{z\in\Gamma(\eta)\backslash\overline{D(0,R_{0})}}|g^{(n-k)}(z)|(1-|z|^{2})^{n-k}\right)^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}|d\eta|\right)^{\frac{p-q}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{q}

when
j\geq j_{0}
.
Thus,
T_{g}^{n,k}:H^{p}\to H^{q}
is compact. The proof is complete.

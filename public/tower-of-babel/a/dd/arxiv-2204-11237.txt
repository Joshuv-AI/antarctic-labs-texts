Asymptotic Normality of the Coefficients of the Morgan-Voyce Polynomials
Moussa Benoumhani, Bernhard Heim, Markus Neuhauser

Abstract
. We study arithmetic and asymptotic properties of polynomials provided by Q n ​ ( x ) := x ​ ∑ k = 1 n k ​ Q n − k ​ ( x ) Q_{n}(x):=x\sum_{k=1}^{n}k\,Q_{n-k}(x) with initial value Q 0 ​ ( x ) = 1 Q_{0}(x)=1 . The coefficients satisfy a central limit theorem and a local limit theorem involving Fibonacci numbers. We apply methods of Berry and Esseen, Harper, Bender, and Canfield.

1.
Introduction and main results

Let
g
be a normalized arithmetic function. Let
G(t):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g(n)\,t^{n}
be
regular at
t=0
. We are interested in asymptotic properties of
the double sequence
a(n,k)
of coefficients of the polynomials
p_{n}(x)
defined by

(1.1)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{n}(x)\,t^{n}=\frac{1}{1-x\,G(t)}.

Let
g(n)=\frac{1}{n!}
. Then
the coefficients are asymptotically normal
[
Ha67
,
Be73
]
.
They calculate the number of partitions of an

n
-set having exactly
k
labeled blocks. Thus, are equal to
k!\,S_{n,k}
, where
S_{n,k}
are
the Stirling numbers of the second kind (
[
Be73
]
, section 3 applications).
It is obvious that the case

g(n)=1
leads to the Central Limit Theorem by de Moivre–Laplace,
since

a(n,k)=\binom{n-1}{k-1}

(cf.
[
HNT20
]
).

In this paper we study the case
g(n)=n
in detail and show that the
underlying expected values and variances involve Fibonacci numbers.

We denote the associated polynomials and their coefficients by

Q_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}A_{n,k}\,x^{k}.

Let
B_{n}(x)
denote the Morgan-Voyce polynomials (
[
Ko01
]
, Chapter 41). Then
Q_{n+1}(x)=x\,B_{n}(x)
.
In 1959, Morgan-Voyce
[
MV59
]
discovered importance of
B_{n}(x)
in the study of electric ladder networks of resistors.
Further, in 1967 and 1968,
several papers appeared by Swamy, Basin, Hoggatt, Jr., and Bicknell.
These polynomials are closely related to Fibonacci polynomials.
From
[
HNT20
]
, we know that

A_{n,k}:=\binom{n+k-1}{2k-1},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},\,0\leq k\leq n.

The definition (
1.1
) is equivalent to
Q_{n}(x)=x\sum_{k=1}^{n}kQ_{n-k}(x)
with initial value
Q_{0}(x)=1
.
This hereditary recurrence relation
can be reduced to a three term recurrence relation

(1.2)

Q_{n+2}(x)-(2+x)Q_{n+1}(x)+Q_{n}(x)=0,\qquad n\geq 0,

with initial values
Q_{1}(x)=x
and
Q_{2}=\left(x+2\right)x
.
The double sequence
A_{n,k}
(see Table

) is recorded in Sloane’s data base as A078812.

1.1.
Asymptotic normality by singularity analysis

Our first result, using techniques from singularity analysis
(see Canfield
[
Ca15
]
, Section 3.6), states
that the double sequence
A_{n,k}
is asymptotically
normal with asymptotic mean
a_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\,\,n
and asymptotic variance

b_{n}^{2}=\frac{2}{5}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\,\,n
.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist real sequences
(a_{n})_{n}
and
(b_{n})_{n}
with
b_{n}>0
, such that

(1.3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathop{\rm sup}_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|{\frac{1}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n,k}}\sum_{k\leq a_{n}+xb_{n}}A_{n,k}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}\,dt}\right|=0.

The sequences given by
a_{n}:=n\,a
and
b_{n}^{2}:=n\,b^{2}
satisfy (
1.3
)
with

a:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\text{ and }b^{2}:=\frac{2}{5}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}.

Obviously, the sequences are not unique.
In singularity analysis they are frequently called mean and variance (see Bender
[
Be73
]
) to
indicate the link to probability theory and
the classical Central Limit Theorem (cf.
[
Fi11
]
, section 1.1).

In this paper, we provide sequences
(\mu_{n})_{n}
and

\left(\sigma_{n}^{2}\right)_{n}
, the expected value and variance
of a suitable sequence of random variable
X_{n}
. We obtain a refined version of Theorem
1.1
.
We apply a method of Harper
[
Ha67
]
(see also
[
Ca15
]
section 3.4: the method of negative roots).
The values
\mu_{n}
and
\sigma_{n}^{2}
involve Fibonacci numbers
F_{n}
and the golden ratio
\varphi
.
They characterize the peaks and modes of the coefficients of the
generating series. These are real-rooted polynomials, which
are related to the Jonquière function (also
called polylogarithm
\mathop{\rm Li}_{-1}(t)
) given by

{G}(t):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\,t^{n}
. The radius of convergence is
R=1
.

1.2.
Probabilistic approach

Let
X
be a random variable. Then
X
is called normally distributed, if the probability

P(X\leq x)
is equal to the normal distribution

\Phi(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}}\,dt,

for all real
x
. The distribution
\Phi
is called normal or
Gauss distribution. Let further,
\phi(x)
be the underlying density function.

Definition
.

A sequence
(S_{n})_{n}
of random variables satisfies a Central Limit Theorem, if
there exist sequences
(a_{n})_{n}
and
(b_{n})_{n}
with
a_{n},b_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
and
b_{n}>0
, such that
the normalized random variables

S_{n}^{*}:=\frac{S_{n}-a_{n}}{b_{n}},

converge in distribution against the normal distribution

P(S_{n}^{*}\leq x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{D}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Phi(x).

Consider the sequence of random variables
X_{n}\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}

defined by:

(1.4)

P(X_{n}=k):=\frac{A_{n,k}}{\sum_{m=0}^{n}A_{n,m}},\text{ where }Q_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n,k}\,x^{k}.

Let
S_{n}:=\sum_{m=1}^{n}X_{m}
. Then

(1.5)

P(S_{n}^{*}\leq x)=\frac{1}{\sum_{m=0}^{n}A_{n,m}}\sum_{k\leq a_{n}+x\,b_{n}}A_{n,k},

leads to a probabilistic interpretation of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
S_{n}^{*}
be the normalized random variables as defined in (
1.5
)
with
a_{n}
and
b_{n}^{2}
provided by the expected value
\mathbb{E}(X_{n})
and variance

\mathbb{V}(X_{n})
of the random variable
X_{n}
,
associated with the double sequence
A_{n,k}
.
Then
P(S_{n}^{*}\leq x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{D}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Phi(x)
. Therefore,

(1.6)

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\left|\!\left|\frac{1}{\sum_{m=0}^{n}A_{n,m}}\sum_{k\leq\mathbb{E}(S_{n})+\sqrt{\mathbb{V}(S_{n})}\,x}A_{n,k}-\Phi(x)\right|\!\right|}_{\mathbb{R}}=0.

Here
{\left|\!\left|f(x)\right|\!\right|}_{M}
denotes the supremum norm of
f
on
M\subset\mathbb{R}
.
This theorem is obtained by a method introduced by Harper
[
Ha67
,
Ca15
]
and
a result by Pólya for continuous distribution functions.
We determine the explicit values of the mean and variance of
X_{n}
.
These are expressed in terms of Fibonacci numbers
F_{n}
and are
related to the peaks and plateaus of the unimodal sequence

A_{n,0}\leq A_{n,1}\leq\ldots\leq A_{n,m}\geq\ldots\geq A_{n,n}

by Darroch’s Theorem
[
Da64
]
.
The sequence is log-concave and therefore, unimodal, since the
polynomials
Q_{n}(x)
are orthogonal polynomials.
Indeed,
Q_{n}(x)=x\,U_{n-1}(x/2+1)
, where
U_{n}(x)
are the Chebyshev polynomials
of the second kind
[
HNT20
]
.
We first determine the normalizing factor, the average over all coefficients.

Lemma 1.3
.

The normalizing factor is related to Fibonacci numbers.
We have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}A_{n,k}=Q_{n}(1)=F_{2n}.

Proposition 1.4
.

The random variable
X_{n}
defined in (
1.4
)
has the expected value
\mu_{n}:=\mathbb{E}(X_{n})
and variance
\sigma_{n}^{2}:=\mathbb{V}(X_{n})

given by

\displaystyle\mu_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{5}\left(\frac{F_{2n+1}}{F_{2n}}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{n}\right)\,n,

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{25}\,\left(\frac{F_{2n+1}}{F_{2n}}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{n}{F_{2n}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2n}\right)\,n.

Remarks
.

a) Note that
\mu_{n}
is never an integer for

n\geq 2
. If
\mu_{n}
is an integer then

2\frac{F_{2n+1}}{F_{2n}}n
is also an integer. Since

F_{2n+1}
and
F_{2n}
are coprime,

\frac{2n}{F_{2n}}
is also an integer. Since

F_{2n}>2n
for
n\geq 3
and the only remaining case
is
n=1
.

b) The reciprocal polynomial of
Q_{n}(x)
has the coefficients
\binom{N-k-1}{k}
with
N=2n
.
These sequences had been investigated by Tanny and Zucker
[
TZ74
,
TZ78
]
and Benoumhani
[
Be03
]
.
It had been shown that the sequence is strictly log-concave, the smallest mode had been determined and
the indices, at which a double maximum occurs.

Darroch’s theorem
[
Da64
]
(see also Benoumhani
[
Be96
]
) leads to:

Corollary 1.5
.

The modes of
Q_{n}(x)
are located around
\mu_{n}
. Let
m_{n}
be a mode, then

0<\left|\frac{2}{5}\left(\frac{F_{2n+1}}{F_{2n}}-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{n}\right)\,n-m_{n}\right|<1.

Using elementary calculations and explicit solutions of
the Pell–Fermat equation leads to:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The polynomials
Q_{n}(x)
are unimodal and have at most two modes.
The smallest mode
m
is uniquely determined by

\frac{\sqrt{5n^{2}+1}-1}{5}\leq m<\frac{\sqrt{5n^{2}+1}+4}{5}.

The mode
is unique if

. We have two modes

and

, iff

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}5m_{k}+1\\
n_{k}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}161&360\\

.

If

has

A_{n,m}
at

and

.
For example, for

and

, we have

. The sequence of

The expected values

and variances

converge against

and

,
obtained in Theorem

by methods of singularity analysis.

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
X_{n}
be the random variable defined by (

) with expected value
\mu_{n}
and
variance
\sigma_{n}^{2}
. Let

and

be as in Theorem

. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mu_{n}}{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{5}\left(\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}=a,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sigma_{n}^{2}}{n}

\displaystyle=

where

This follows from Proposition

, since we know that

It is possible to obtain a rate of convergence of (

)

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
S_{n}^{*}
be the normalized random variables as defined in (
1.5
),
associated with

.
Then

|P(S_{n}^{*}\leq x)-\Phi(x)|\,\leq\,C\,\sigma_{n}^{-1}.

Here

.

The Central Limit Theorem, to quote Bender
[
Be73
]
and Canfield
[

]
, provides a certain

. Further information is provided by

[

]
, section 3.7).
Let

.
We consider subsets
K\subset\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
and
k\in K
with the asymptotic behavior

{A_{n,k}^{*}}\,\,\sim\,\,\left.\frac{\phi(x)}{\sigma_{n}}\right|_{x=(k-\mu_{n})/\sigma_{n}}.

More generally, we say the doubly indexed sequence
a(n,k)
satisfies a local limit theorem on a set
S
of real numbers
provided

\mathop{\rm sup}_{x\in S}\Big|\frac{\sigma_{n}\,a(n,\left\lfloor\mu_{n}+x\,\sigma_{n}\right\rfloor)}{\sum_{k}a(n,k)}-\phi(x)\Big|\longrightarrow 0,

where
\mu_{n}
and
\sigma_{n}
are the expected values and variances (
[

]
, definition 3.7.1).
Further, we quote the following result.

Theorem 1.9

(Bender,
[
Ca15
]
, Theorem 3.7.2)
.

Suppose that

are asymptotically normal and
\sigma_{n}^{2}\rightarrow\infty
. If for
each
n
the sequence
a(n,k)
is unimodal in
k
, then
a(n,k)
satisfies a local limit theorem on the set

\{x\,\,:|x|\geq\varepsilon\}
, for any
\varepsilon>0
. If for each
n
the sequence

is log-concave in
k
, the

a(n,k)
satisfies a local limit theorem on the set
\mathbb{R}
.

We have that
A(n,k)
are asymptotically normal by Theorem
1.2
and
\sigma_{n}^{2}\rightarrow\infty
.
Further, that
Q_{n}(x)
has real roots. This implies by Theorem
1.9
:

Corollary 1.10
.

\lim_{n\to\infty}{\left|\!\left|\sigma_{n}\,{A_{n,\left\lfloor\mu_{n}+x\,\sigma_{n}\right\rfloor}^{*}}-\phi(x)\right|\!\right|}_{\mathbb{R}}=0.

Corollary 1.11
.

Further, we have the asymptotic formula

A_{n,k}\,\,\sim\,\,F_{2n}\frac{\phi(x)}{\sigma_{n}}\text{ for }n\to\infty,

where

and

is bounded.

2.
Singularity analysis

Bender
[
Be73
]
provided a criterion for a double sequence
a\left({n,k}\right)
to be
asymptotically normal.
Typically the criterion applies, if
the generating function has only one singularity on the circle of convergence.
We recall the approach by Bender (see also
[
Ca15
]
, section 3.6 Method 4).

Theorem 2.1

(Bender)
.

Let
f(x,t)=\sum_{k,n}\,a(n,k)\,x^{k}\,t^{n}
, with
a(n,k)\geq 0
.
Suppose there exist

(i)

a function
A(s)
continuous and non-zero near
0
,

(ii)

a function
r(s)
with bounded third derivative near
0
,

(iii)

a non-negative integer
m
, and

(iv)

positive numbers
\varepsilon
and
\delta
such that

\left(1-\frac{t}{r(s)}\right)^{m}\,f\left(e^{s},t\right)-\frac{A(s)}{1-\frac{t}{r(s)}}

is analytic and bounded for
|s|<\varepsilon
,
|t|<r(0)+\delta
.

Put
a=-r^{\prime}(0)/r(0)
and
b^{2}=a^{2}-r^{\prime\prime}(0)/r(0)
. If
b^{2}\neq 0
, then the numbers
a(n,k)

are asymptotically normal with
a_{n}=n\,a
and
b_{n}^{2}=b^{2}\,n
.

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
a(n,k)=A_{n,k}
. Let
G(t)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\,t^{n}
. This power series
has radius of convergence
1
and has a pole at
t=1
.
The generating series of
A_{n,k}
(we refer to
[
HNT20
]
) is provided by

f(x,t)=\frac{1}{1-x\,G(t)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Q_{n}(x)\,t^{n}.

We have
G(t)=\frac{t}{(t-1)^{2}}
, thus

G(t)=1
has two real solutions.
The smallest in absolute value is given by

t_{1}=\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}
.

Suppose
x\neq 0
. If
G\left(t\right)=\frac{1}{x}
, then

xt=\left(1-t\right)^{2}
i. e.
t^{2}-\left(2+x\right)t+1=0
. Therefore,

t=1+\frac{x}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{x^{2}}{4}+x}
with the principle
branch of the square root for
\left|x-1\right|<1
.
Let

r\left(s\right)=1+\frac{e^{s}}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{e^{2s}}{4}+e^{s}}

for
\left|s\right|<\ln 2
.
Then the pole of
f\left(x,t\right)
closest to
0
is
located at
r\left(s\right)
. The other pole is located
at

1/r\left(s\right)=1+\frac{e^{s}}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{e^{2s}}{4}+e^{s}}
.

Since

\displaystyle f\left(e^{s},t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-e^{s}G\left(t\right)}=\frac{\left(1-t\right)^{2}}{1-\left(2+e^{s}\right)t+t^{2}}=1+\frac{e^{s}t}{\left(1-r\left(s\right)t\right)\left(1-t/r\left(s\right)\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{e^{s}}{2\sqrt{\frac{e^{2s}}{4}+e^{s}}}\left(\frac{1}{1-t/r\left(s\right)}-\frac{1}{1-r\left(s\right)t}\right),

we obtain

(2.1)

f\left(e^{s},t\right)-\frac{e^{s}}{2\sqrt{\frac{e^{2s}}{4}+e^{s}}}\frac{1}{1-t/r\left(s\right)}=1-\frac{e^{s}}{2\sqrt{\frac{e^{2s}}{4}+e^{s}}}\frac{1}{1-r\left(s\right)t}.

For
\left|s\right|<\ln 2
holds

\left|r\left(s\right)\right|<\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore,

\left|1/r\left(s\right)\right|>2
and
(
2.1
) is
analytic and bounded for
\left|t\right|<\frac{3}{2}
.

We obtain

r^{\prime}\left(s\right)=\frac{e^{s}}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{e^{2s}}{4}+e^{s}\right)^{-1/2}\left(\frac{e^{2s}}{2}+e^{s}\right)
and

r^{\prime\prime}\left(s\right)=\frac{e^{s}}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{e^{2s}}{4}+e^{s}\right)^{-3/2}\left(\frac{e^{2s}}{2}+e^{s}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{e^{2s}}{4}+e^{s}\right)^{-1/2}\left(e^{2s}+e^{s}\right).

Further,

\displaystyle r^{\prime\prime\prime}\left(s\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{s}}{2}-\frac{3}{8}\left(\frac{e^{2s}}{4}+e^{s}\right)^{-5/2}\left(\frac{e^{2s}}{2}+e^{s}\right)^{3}

\displaystyle{}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{e^{2s}}{4}+e^{s}\right)^{-3/2}\left(\frac{e^{2s}}{2}+e^{s}\right)\left(e^{2s}+e^{s}\right)

\displaystyle{}+\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{e^{2s}}{4}+e^{s}\right)^{-3/2}\left(e^{2s}+e^{s}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle{}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{e^{2s}}{4}+e^{s}\right)^{-1/2}\left(2e^{2s}+e^{s}\right),

which shows that the third derivative is bounded near
0
,

r^{\prime}\left(0\right)=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}\frac{3}{2}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{2\sqrt{5}}
, and

r^{\prime\prime}\left(0\right)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\frac{8}{5\sqrt{5}}\frac{9}{4}-\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{11}{10\sqrt{5}}
.
With

r\left(0\right)=\frac{3}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}=\left(\frac{3}{2\sqrt{5}}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\sqrt{5}
. This yields

a=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}
.
As
1/r\left(0\right)=\frac{3}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}
, we obtain

b=\frac{1}{5}-\left(\frac{3}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{11}{10\sqrt{5}}\right)=\frac{1}{5}-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{11}{20}-\frac{\sqrt{5}}{4}+\frac{33}{20\sqrt{5}}=\frac{2}{5\sqrt{5}}.

3.
The Berry–Esseen theorem

Let
X\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
be a random variable
with expected value
\mathbb{E}(X)
and variance
\mathbb{V}(X)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
P_{X}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}P(X=k)\,x^{k}
be the probability generating function.
A straightforward calculation leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{\prime}(1),

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}(X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{\prime\prime}(1)-\left(P^{\prime}(1)\right)^{2}+P^{\prime}(1).

There is a Central Limit Theorem for a sequence of independent, but not necessarily identically distributed
random variables. We work this out in the setting of a triangular array of Bernoulli random variables.
To control the rate of convergence,
we utilize a Berry–Esseen theorem (we refer to
[
Ca15
]
, section 3).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X_{n,k}
for
1\leq k\leq n
be independent random variables
with expected values
\mu_{n,k}
, variances
\sigma_{n,k}^{2}
and
absolute third central moment

\rho_{n,k}=\mathbb{E}(|X_{n,k}-\mu_{n,k}|^{3})<\infty.

Let

\mu_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu_{n,k}
,
\sigma_{n}^{2}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sigma_{n,k}^{2}
and
S_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{n,k}
.
Let
S_{n}^{*}=(S_{n}-\mu_{n})/\sigma_{n}
. Then

3.1.
Harper’s method

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
P_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{n,k}\,x^{k}

be a monomic polynomial of degree
n
,
a_{n,k}\geq 0
and
P_{n}(1)>0
.
Suppose the roots of
P_{n}(x)
are real and
P_{n}(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(x+r_{k}\right)
.
Harper
[

]
introduced a triangular array of Bernoulli random variables

with
distribution

P(X_{n,j}=0):=\frac{r_{j}}{1+r_{j}}\text{ and }P(X_{n,j}=1):=\frac{1}{1+r_{j}}.

Let
S_{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{n,j}
. Then
P(S_{n}=k)=\frac{a_{n,k}}{P_{n}(1)}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X_{n,j}
be given. Then

\mathbb{E}(X_{n,j})=\frac{r_{j}}{1+r_{j}},\,\,\,\mathbb{V}(X_{n,j})=\frac{r_{j}}{(1+r_{j})^{2}},\,\,\,\mathbb{E}(|X_{n,j}-\mathbb{E}(X_{n,j})|^{3})=\frac{r_{j}(1+r_{j}^{2})}{(1+r_{j})^{4}}.

This implies that
\mathbb{E}(|X_{n,j}-\mathbb{E}(X_{n,j})|^{3})<\mathbb{V}(X_{n,j})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Due to Lemma,
3.2
we obtain

\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\rho_{n,k}}{\sigma_{n}^{3}}\leq\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sigma_{n,k}^{2}}{\sigma_{n}^{3}}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}}.

Finally, Theorem
3.1
gives the result.

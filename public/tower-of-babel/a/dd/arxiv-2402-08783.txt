1.
Introduction and Statement of Results

In an important paper on integer partitions, MacMahon
[
16
]
introduced the family of
q
-series

(1.1)

\displaystyle A_{k}(q):=\sum_{0<s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots<s_{k}}\frac{q^{s_{1}+s_{2}+\cdots+s_{k}}}{(1-q^{s_{1}})^{2}(1-q^{s_{2}})^{2}\cdots(1-q^{s_{k}})^{2}}.

For positive integers
k,
we have that

(1.2)

A_{k}(q)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{m}(k;n)q^{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<s_{1}<s_{2}<\dots<s_{k}\\
(m_{1},\dots,m_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}\end{subarray}}m_{1}m_{2}\dots m_{k}q^{m_{1}s_{1}+m_{2}s_{2}+\dots+m_{k}s_{k}},

and so
A_{k}(q)
is a natural partition generating function. Indeed, we have that
\mathfrak{m}(k;n)
is the sum of the products of the
part multiplicities
for partitions of
n
with
k
distinct part sizes.

These series connect partitions to disparate areas of mathematics. In elementary number theory,
MacMahon realized some of the
A_{k}(q)
as generating functions for divisor sums.
For example, he found that

\begin{split}A_{1}(q)&=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{1}(n)q^{n}=1+3q^{2}+4q^{3}+7q^{4}+6q^{5}+\dots,\\
A_{2}(q)&=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left((-2n+1)\sigma_{1}(n)+\sigma_{3}(n)\right)=q^{3}+3q^{4}+9q^{5}+\dots,\end{split}

where
\sigma_{\nu}(n):=\sum_{d\mid n}d^{\nu}.

Extending beyond number theory, these series arise in the study of Hilbert schemes,
q
-multiple zeta-values, representation theory, and topological string theory (for example, see
[
6
,
11
,
13
,
14
]
).
Recent research has focused on the quasimodularity of the
A_{k}(q)

(for example, see
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
17
]
). Andrews and Rose

[
5
,
17
]
proved that
A_{k}(q)
is a linear combination of quasimodular forms on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with weights
\leq 2k.

In this note, we instead focus on the combinatorial properties of MacMahon’s series. We show that they satisfy infinitely many systematic identities, that, in turn, illustrate the ubiquity of the
\mathfrak{m}(k;n)
partition functions.
To set the stage, we offer some terms of
A_{0}(q),A_{1}(q),\dots,A_{5}(q):

\begin{split}A_{0}(q)&:=q^{0},\\
A_{1}(q)&=q+3q^{2}+4q^{3}+7q^{4}+6q^{5}+\dots,\\
A_{2}(q)&=q^{3}+3q^{4}+9q^{5}+15q^{6}+30q^{7}+\dots,\\
A_{3}(q)&=q^{6}+3q^{7}+9q^{8}+22q^{9}+42q^{10}+\dots,\\
A_{4}(q)&=q^{10}+3q^{11}+9q^{12}+22q^{13}+51q^{14}+\dots,\\
A_{5}(q)&=q^{15}+3q^{16}+9q^{17}+22q^{18}+51q^{19}+\dots\end{split}

As these examples suggest, the
A_{k}(q)
behave well as
k\rightarrow+\infty.
Indeed, in terms of the
q
-Pochhammer symbol

(a;q)_{\infty}:=(1-a)(1-aq)(1-aq^{2})\dots,

and Jacobi’s famous identity for
(q;q)_{\infty}^{3},

it is known that (see Theorem 1.1 of
[
1
]
)

(1.3)

\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{3}}=q^{-\frac{k^{2}+k}{2}}A_{k}(q)+O(q^{k+1}).

In a recent preprint, Bringmann, Craig, van Ittersum and Pandey
[
8
]
obtain further such results relating infinite products with MacMahon-type
q
-series.

It is natural to ask whether (
1.3
) is a glimpse of explicit identities, one for each non-negative integer
k
. We show that this is indeed the case, where
q^{-\frac{k^{2}+k}{2}}A_{k}(q)
is simply the first summand of a closed formula involving
A_{k}(q),A_{k+1}(q),A_{k+2}(q),\dots.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
k
is a non-negative integer, then we have

\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{3}}=q^{-\frac{k^{2}+k}{2}}\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}\binom{2m+1}{m+k+1}A_{m}(q).

To further appreciate these identities, we note, for positive
m
, that (
1.3
) implies

(1.4)

q^{-\frac{k^{2}+k}{2}}A_{k+m}(q)=q^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}+mk}+\dots.

The first terms of these
q
-series have exponents that grow quadratically
m.
Therefore, we can use Theorem
1.1
to compute initial segments of

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{3}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}p_{3}(n)q^{n},

the generating function for the 3-colored partition function
p_{3}(n),
using a “small number” of summands. In this way, we obtain a doubly infinite family of formulas relating the 3-colored partition function to MacMahon’s
\mathfrak{m}(k;n)
partition functions.

Corollary 1.2
.

If
k
and
j
are non-negative integers, then

\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{3}}=q^{-\frac{k^{2}+k}{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{j}\binom{2m+2k+1}{m+2k+1}A_{m+k}(q)+O\left(q^{\frac{(j+1)(j+2k+2)}{2}}\right).

In particular, if
n<(j+1)(j+2k+2)/2,
then we have

p_{3}(n)=\sum_{m=0}^{j}\binom{2m+2k+1}{m+2k+1}\mathfrak{m}\left(m+k;n+\frac{k^{2}+k}{2}\right).

Remark
.

Letting
j=1
in Corollary
1.2
gives Theorem 1.1 (ii) of
[
1
]
.

Example
.

If
k=100
and
j=2,
then Corollary
1.2
gives

\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{3}}=q^{-5050}\cdot(A_{100}(q)+203A_{101}(q)+20910A_{102}(q))+O(q^{306}).

Therefore, for
n<306,
we have

p_{3}(n)=\mathfrak{m}(100;n+5050)+203\mathfrak{m}(101;n+5050)+20910\mathfrak{m}(102;n+5050).

In addition to the
A_{k}(q),
MacMahon also introduced
[

the

(1.6)

\displaystyle C_{k}(q)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{m}_{{\text{\rm odd}}}(k;n)q^{n}:=\sum_{0<s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots<s_{k}}\frac{q^{2s_{1}+2s_{2}+\cdots+2s_{k}-k}}{(1-q^{2s_{1}-1})^{2}(1-q^{2s_{2}-1})^{2}\cdots(1-q^{2s_{k}-1})^{2}}.

The numbers
\mathfrak{m}_{{\text{\rm odd}}}(k;n)
have the same partition theoretic description as the
\mathfrak{m}(k;n)
, where here the parts are required to be odd.
Furthermore,
in analogy with the work of Andrews and Rose
[

17
]
, Bachmann
[

Here we show that the
C_{k}(q)
also enjoy properties that are analogous to those of
A_{k}(q)
described above.
Namely, we prove the following theorem, where
C_{0}(q):=1.

Theorem 1.3
.

The following are true.

(1) If
k
is a non-negative integer, then we have

q^{-k^{2}}C_{k}(q)=\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}+O(q^{2k+1}).

(2) If
k
is a non-negative integer, then we have

\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}=q^{-k^{2}}\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}\binom{2m}{m+k}C_{m}(q).

Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
establish infinitely many formulas, one for each integer
k
, between MacMahon’s two families of
q
-series and the reciprocals of the theta functions

\begin{split}(q;q)_{\infty}^{3}&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}(2n+1)q^{\frac{n^{2}+n}{2}}=1-3q+5q^{3}-7q^{6}+9q^{10}-\dots,\\
(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}&=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}}=1-2q+2q^{4}-2q^{9}+2q^{16}-2q^{25}+\dots.\end{split}

Corollary
1.2
, which relates the 3-colored partition function to MacMahon’s
\mathfrak{m}(k;n)
partition functions, relies on the fact that
1/(q;q)_{\infty}^{3}
is the generating function of
p_{3}(n)
. Rather nicely, it turns out that

(1.7)

\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{p}(n)q^{n}=1+2q+4q^{2}+8q^{3}+14q^{4}+24q^{5}+\dots,

where
\overline{p}(n)
denotes the number of
overpartitions
of size
n.
Recall that an overpartition of
n
is an ordered sequence of nonincreasing positive integers, where the first occurrence of each integer may be overlined
[
10
]
. Overpartitions have been the focus of intense research in recent years (for example, see
[
4
,

,

Therefore, in analogy with Corollary

, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 1.4
.

If
k
and
j
are non-negative integers, then

\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}=q^{-k^{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{j}\binom{2m+2k}{m+2k}C_{m+k}(q)+O\left(q^{(j+1)(j+2k+1)}\right).

In particular, if
n<(j+1)(j+2k+1),
then we have

\overline{p}(n)=\sum_{m=0}^{j}\binom{2m+2k}{m+2k}\mathfrak{m}_{{\text{\rm odd}}}\left(m+k;n+k^{2}\right).

Example
.

If
k=100
and
j=2,
then Corollary
1.4
gives

\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}=q^{-10000}\cdot(C_{100}(q)+202C_{101}(q)+20706C_{102}(q))+O(q^{609}).

Therefore, for
n<{609},
we have

\overline{p}(n)=\mathfrak{m}_{{\text{\rm odd}}}(100;n+10000)+202\mathfrak{m}_{{\text{\rm odd}}}(101;n+10000)+20706\mathfrak{m}_{{\text{\rm odd}}}(102;n+10000).

The proofs of our results are rather straightforward, and follow from the Jacobi triple product identity. Namely, we recognize the role of MacMahon’s
q
-series as coefficients of power series in
(z+z^{-1})^{2}
obtained from this well-known bivariate infinite product.

Remark
.

The proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
follow along similar lines. They differ in their choice of specialization (i.e. changes of variable) of the Jacobi triple product identity. It would be interesting to see if other natural partition generating functions emerge from further specializations, to supplement these results on
p_{3}(n)
and
\overline{p}(n).
Finally, we point out that it would be interesting to carry out a similar analysis for the quintuple and septuple infinite product identities.

Proof of Theorem

We recall the Jacobi triple product identity (see Theorem 2.8 of

(2.1)

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}z^{n}=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-q^{2n+2})(1+z^{-1}q^{2n+1})(1+zq^{2n+1}).

By factoring out

\begin{split}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}z^{2n}=\left(1+z^{-2}\right)(q;q)_{\infty}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+z^{-2}q^{n})(1+z^{2}q^{n}).\end{split}

After straightforward algebraic manipulation, we find

\displaystyle\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}z^{2n}=\left(1+z^{-2}\right)(q;q)_{\infty}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left((1-q^{n})^{2}+(z+z^{-1})^{2}q^{n}\right).

After factoring out
(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}
from the infinite product, we obtain

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}z^{2n}=\left(1+z^{-2}\right)(q;q)_{\infty}^{3}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{q^{n}}{(1-q^{n})^{2}}\cdot(z+z^{-1})^{2}\right).

Thanks to definition (
1.1
), we find that the infinite product on the right, as a power series in
(z+z^{-1})^{2},
is the generating function for MacMahon’s series.
Therefore, we find that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{3}}\cdot z^{2n}=\left(1+z^{-2}\right)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}(q)(z+z^{-1})^{2n}.

Proof of Theorem

We again use the Jacobi triple product identity (
2.1

After factoring out
(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}z^{2n}=(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+z^{-2}q^{2n-1})(1+z^{2}q^{2n-1}).

One easily checks that

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}z^{2n}=(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left((1-q^{2n-1})^{2}+(z+z^{-1})^{2}q^{2n-1}\right).

\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}}z^{2n}=(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{q^{2n-1}}{(1-q^{2n-1})^{2}}\cdot(z+z^{-1})^{2}\right).

1.6

\displaystyle\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\cdot z^{2n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}(q)\sum_{j=0}^{2n}\binom{2n}{j}z^{2j-2n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}(q)\sum_{j=-n}^{n}\binom{2n}{j+n}z^{2j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{n=|j|}^{\infty}\binom{2n}{j+n}C_{n}(q)z^{2j}

Spacelike hypersurfaces in standard static spacetimes
Giulio Colombo, José A. S. Pelegrín, Marco Rigoli

Abstract
. In this work we study spacelike hypersurfaces immersed in spatially open standard static spacetimes with complete spacelike slices. Under appropriate lower bounds on the Ricci curvature of the spacetime in directions tangent to the slices, we prove that every complete CMC hypersurface having either bounded hyperbolic angle or bounded height is maximal. Our conclusions follow from general mean curvature estimates for spacelike hypersurfaces. In case where the spacetime is a Lorentzian product with spatial factor of nonnegative Ricci curvature and sectional curvatures bounded below, we also show that a complete maximal hypersurface not intersecting a spacelike slice is itself a slice. This result is obtained from a gradient estimate for parametric maximal hypersurfaces.

1.
Introduction and main results

When searching for general solutions of Einstein’s field equations in a spacetime
\overline{M}
, it is customary to assume the a priori existence of an infinitesimal symmetry (see
[
15
]
,
[
17
]
). The symmetry often comes from a Killing or, more generally, a conformal vector field
X
on
\overline{M}
, see for instance
[
16
]
. In this case, the spacetime can be classified depending on the causal character of the symmetry. Thus, a Lorentzian manifold admitting a timelike Killing vector field
X
is called a stationary spacetime due to the fact that observers along the vector field
X
see a metric that does not change. Moreover, if the timelike Killing vector field is irrotational, that is, the distribution orthogonal to the field is involutive, then a local warped product structure appears and the spacetime is called static
[
7
]
. When this structure is global the spacetime is called a standard static spacetime.

More precisely, by a standard static spacetime
(\overline{M},\overline{g})
we mean a product
\overline{M}=\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{R}
, with
\mathbb{P}
a (connected) orientable manifold of dimension
m\geq 2
, endowed with the Lorentzian metric

(1.1)

\overline{g}=\pi_{\mathbb{P}}^{\ast}(\sigma)-(h\circ\pi_{\mathbb{P}})^{2}\pi_{\mathbb{R}}^{\ast}(\mathrm{d}t^{2}),

where
\pi_{\mathbb{P}}:\overline{M}\to\mathbb{P}
,
\pi_{\mathbb{R}}:\overline{M}\to\mathbb{R}
are the projections onto the factors of the product, and where
\sigma
and
h
are respectively a Riemannian metric and a smooth positive function on
\mathbb{P}
. Thus, the spacetime
\overline{M}
is a warped product in the sense of
[
23
]
, with base
(\mathbb{P},\sigma)
, fiber
(\mathbb{R},-\mathrm{d}t^{2})
and warping function
h
. Each tangent vector
X\in T_{p}\overline{M}
,
p=(x,t)\in\overline{M}
, can be decomposed as
X=\mathcal{H}X+\mathcal{V}X
with
\mathcal{H}X
tangent to the leaf
\mathbb{P}\times\{t\}
and
\mathcal{V}X
tangent to the fiber
\{x\}\times\mathbb{R}
, that is,
\mathcal{H}X\in\ker(\pi_{\mathbb{R}})_{\ast}
and
\mathcal{V}X\in\ker(\pi_{\mathbb{P}})_{\ast}
. Following
[
23
]
, vectors
X=\mathcal{H}X
tangent to leaves will be called horizontal and vectors
X=\mathcal{V}X
tangent to fibers will be called vertical. Note that a horizontal vector
X
is always spacelike so for such
X
we can set
|X|=\sqrt{\overline{g}(X,X)}\geq 0
.

When
h\equiv 1
the resulting standard static spacetime
(\overline{M},\overline{g})
is a semi-Riemannian product with factors
(\mathbb{P},\sigma)
and
(\mathbb{R},-\mathrm{d}t^{2})
; in the following, a manifold of this type will be called a Lorentzian product.

On a standard static spacetime, the role of the vector field
X
responsible of the infinitesimal symmetry is played by the global timelike Killing vector field
\partial_{t}
on
\overline{M}
. In
[
23
]
it is proved that any static spacetime is locally isometric to a standard static one. Moreover, in
[
3
]
and
[
31
]
sufficient conditions are given for a static spacetime to be standard. The importance of standard static spacetimes is due to the fact that they include some classical spacetimes like Lorentz-Minkowski spacetime
\mathbb{L}^{n}
, Einstein static universe as well as models that describe a universe with one spherically symmetric non-rotating mass, such as a star or a black hole, as it happens in the exterior Schwarzschild spacetime
[
29
]
.

In any standard static spacetime
\overline{M}=\mathbb{P}\times_{h}\mathbb{R}
there exists a distinguished foliation whose leaves are given by the totally geodesic level hypersurfaces of the function
\pi_{\mathbb{R}}
. They are known as the spacelike slices
\mathbb{P}\times\{t_{0}\}
,
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. We recall that in the spatially closed case, several uniqueness results have been obtained on the splitting of these spacetimes in terms of their usual orthogonal decomposition (see
[
3
]
and
[
32
]
). However, the problem of guaranteeing the uniqueness of the splitting for spatially open standard static spacetimes remains open, in fact there exist spacetimes with different splittings of type (
1.1
), for instance
\mathbb{L}^{n}
. Other nontrivial cases are considered in
[
36
]
and in
[
19
]
, where the authors describe the general structure of a standard static spacetime admitting more than one decomposition and give some uniqueness results under suitable curvature assumptions.

In this paper we focus our attention on spatially open standard static spacetimes. Their importance comes from the fact that, despite the historical relevance of spatially closed models, observations suggest that our physical universe is actually spatially open
[
14
]
. Moreover, spatially closed spacetimes lead to a violation of the holographic principle
[
6
]
, making spatially open models more suitable for a possible quantum theory of gravity
[
10
]
.

Given an
m
-dimensional manifold
M
, an immersion

\psi:M\to\overline{M}

in a standard static spacetime
\overline{M}=\mathbb{P}\times_{h}\mathbb{R}
is said to be spacelike if
g=\psi^{\ast}\overline{g}
is a Riemannian metric on
M
. In this case,
M
is called a spacelike hypersurface and
\psi:(M,g)\to(\overline{M},\overline{g})
is an isometric immersion. Roughly speaking, each spacelike hypersurface represents the physical space of some observer in a given instant of their time and their study has been crucial in General Relativity
[
22
]
. Among other reasons, their interest rely on the key role they play in the proof of the positivity of the gravitational mass
[
33
]
, their importance in the study of the structure of singularities in the space of solutions of Einstein’s equations
[
5
]
and the fact that the initial value problem for the Einstein’s field equation in General Relativity is formulated in terms of a spacelike hypersurface (see, for instance
[
27
]
and references therein). Furthermore, in Causality Theory, the existence of a certain spacelike hypersurface can determine the causal properties of the spacetime. For instance, a spacetime is globally hyperbolic if and only if it admits a Cauchy hypersurface
[
18
]
. Indeed, any globally hyperbolic spacetime is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}\times S
, being
S
a smooth spacelike Cauchy hypersurface, see Theorem 1 of
[
8
]
.

Let
\psi:M\to\overline{M}
be a spacelike hypersurface immersed in the standard static spacetime
\overline{M}=\mathbb{P}\times_{h}\mathbb{R}
. The map

\pi=\pi_{\psi}=\pi_{\mathbb{P}}\circ\psi:M\to\mathbb{P}

is an immersion and therefore a local diffeomorphism. The tensor field
\hat{\sigma}=\pi^{\ast}\sigma
is a Riemannian metric on
M
that satisfies
\hat{\sigma}\geq g
in the sense of quadratic forms, where
g=\psi^{\ast}\overline{g}
is the Riemannian metric induced by the immersion
\psi
.

If
\mathbb{P}
is noncompact then
M
itself is noncompact, since
\pi
is continuous and open. If
(\mathbb{P},\sigma)
is complete then
(M,\hat{\sigma})
is complete if and only if
\pi:M\to\mathbb{P}
is a (topological) covering map. Vice versa, if
(M,\hat{\sigma})
is complete, then
\pi:(M,\hat{\sigma})\to(\mathbb{P},\sigma)
is a Riemannian covering map and
(\mathbb{P},\sigma)
itself is complete. For a justification of these statements we refer the reader to Proposition 23 and Lemma 17 of
[
24
]
. We remark that completeness of
(M,g)
implies completeness of
(M,\hat{\sigma})
, since
\hat{\sigma}\geq g
. We also remark that if
\pi:M\to\mathbb{P}
is a covering map of degree 1, that is, a global diffeomorphism, then
\psi(M)\subseteq\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{R}
is the graph of a smooth function
u:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{R}
. Viceversa, the graph of a smooth function
u:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

h^{2}\sigma(Du,Du)<1\qquad\text{on }\mathbb{P}

is the image of the spacelike hypersurface
\psi_{u}:\mathbb{P}\to\overline{M}
defined by
\psi_{u}(x)=(x,u(x))
for each
x\in\mathbb{P}
, and the corresponding map
\pi_{u}=\pi_{\mathbb{P}}\circ\psi_{u}:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{P}
is the identity map.

The manifold
M
is orientable as a consequence of the time-orientability of
\overline{M}
ensured by the presence of the global timelike vector field
\partial_{t}
. In particular, there exists a unique unit timelike normal vector field
N\in\mathfrak{X}^{\bot}(M)
with the same time-orientation of
\partial_{t}
, that is, satisfying
\overline{g}(N,\partial_{t})<0
everywhere. The wrong way Cauchy inequality implies that

\overline{g}\left(N,\frac{1}{h\circ\pi}\,\partial_{t}\right)\leq-1.

This enables us to define the hyperbolic cosine of the hyperbolic angle
\theta
between
N
and
\partial_{t}
by setting

\cosh\theta=-\overline{g}\left(N,\frac{1}{h\circ\pi}\,\partial_{t}\right).

The mean curvature function of
\psi
in the direction of
N
will be denoted by
H
. Both
\cosh\theta
and
H
are smooth functions on
M
.

In Section 2 below we derive the geometric and analytical equations relevant for our pourposes and in Section 3 we obtain, as first result, some lower bounds on the hyperbolic angle of a spacelike hypersurface under different geometric assumptions. Our first result is the following

Theorem 1
.

Let
\overline{M}=\mathbb{P}\times_{h}\mathbb{R}
be a standard static spacetime with complete, noncompact base
(\mathbb{P},\sigma)
. Suppose that for some constant

it holds

(1.2)

\overline{\mathrm{Ric}}(X,X)\geq-mG_{0}|X|^{2}

\psi:M\to\overline{M}

\pi:M\to\mathbb{P}

(1.3)

\limsup_{M\ni x\to\infty}\cosh\theta(x)\geq\sqrt{1+\frac{H_{0}^{2}}{G_{0}}}.

As a consequence, but see also Corollary
2
below, we have

Corollary 1
.

Let
\overline{M}=\mathbb{P}\times_{h}\mathbb{R}
be a standard static spacetime with complete, noncompact base
(\mathbb{P},\sigma)
. Suppose that

\overline{\mathrm{Ric}}(X,X)\geq 0

Remark 1
.

As a consequence of Lemma
1
below we have that for each horizontal vector

it holds

\overline{\mathrm{Ric}}(X,X)=\mathrm{Ric}^{\mathbb{P}}(X_{0},X_{0})-\frac{\mathrm{Hess}^{\mathbb{P}}(h)(X_{0},X_{0})}{h},\quad|X|^{2}=\sigma(X_{0},X_{0})

with

. Therefore, assumptions (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) can be regarded as generalized curvature assumptions on the weighted manifold
(\mathbb{P},\sigma,h)
, since
\mathrm{Ric}^{\mathbb{P}}-\frac{\mathrm{Hess}^{\mathbb{P}}(h)}{h}
is the modified Bakry-Emery Ricci tensor

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ric}^{\mathbb{P}}_{m,m+1}(\mathrm{\Delta}^{\mathbb{P}}_{-\log h})=\mathrm{Ric}^{\mathbb{P}}+\mathrm{Hess}^{\mathbb{P}}(-\log h)+\mathrm{d}(-\log h)\otimes\mathrm{d}(-\log h)=\mathrm{Ric}^{\mathbb{P}}-\frac{\mathrm{Hess}^{\mathbb{P}}(h)}{h}

introduced by Qian,
[
25
]
, and ubiquitous in the study of generalized
k
-Einstein manifolds, Ricci solitons, etc. See for instance
[
21
]
,
[
12
]
and references therein. A lower bound of the form (
1.2
) implies an upper bound on the growth of the weighted volume of geodesic balls in
\mathbb{P}
, with weight function
h
, namely, inequality (
1.5
) below (cfr.
[

]
). Indeed, a bound of the form (

) can be obtained when (

) is directly replaced by a volume growth assumption on

, in the case where the covering map

has finite degree. This is the content of the next result. We recall that in the non-parametric case where
M=\mathbb{P}
,
\psi=\psi_{u}
and
\psi(M)=\psi_{u}(\mathbb{P})
is an entire graph over

, the map
\pi_{u}
has degree
1
.

We start this section with a preliminary result about the curvature tensors of a standard static spacetime.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\overline{M}^{m+1}=\mathbb{P}^{m}\times_{h}\mathbb{R}
be a standard static spacetime with warped metric

\overline{g}=-h(x)^{2}\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\langle,\rangle_{\mathbb{P}}

where
h\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})
,
h>0
and
t
is the standard coordinate on
\mathbb{R}
. Then

(a)

the Riemannian curvature
(0,4)
-tensor
\overline{\mathrm{Riem}}
of
\overline{M}
is given by

(2.1)

\overline{\mathrm{Riem}}=\mathrm{Riem}^{\mathbb{P}}+\left(h\,\mathrm{Hess}^{\mathbb{P}}(h)\right)\mathbin{\bigcirc\mspace{-15mu}\wedge\mspace{3mu}}(\mathrm{d}t\otimes\mathrm{d}t),

where
\mathrm{Riem}^{\mathbb{P}}
,
\mathrm{Hess}^{\mathbb{P}}
are the Riemannian curvature
(0,4)
-tensor and the Hessian of
\mathbb{P}
, respectively, and
~\mathbin{\bigcirc\mspace{-15mu}\wedge\mspace{3mu}}
denotes the Kulkarni-Nomizu product of symmetric
(0,2)
-tensors;

(b)

the Ricci tensor of
\overline{M}
is given by

(2.2)

\overline{\mathrm{Ric}}=\mathrm{Ric}^{\mathbb{P}}-\frac{\mathrm{Hess}^{\mathbb{P}}(h)}{h}+\left(h\,\mathrm{\Delta}^{\mathbb{P}}h\right)\mathrm{d}t\otimes\mathrm{d}t,

where
\mathrm{Ric}^{\mathbb{P}}
,
\mathrm{\Delta}^{\mathbb{P}}
are the Ricci tensor and the Laplace-Beltrami operator of

Remark 2
.

With a little abuse of notation, in the RHS’s of formulas (

) and (

) we are omitting writing the pullback
\pi_{\mathbb{P}}^{\ast}
for tensors and functions defined on

to avoid introducing an unnecessarily complicated notation, so that

\pi_{\mathbb{P}}^{\ast}(\mathrm{Riem}^{\mathbb{P}})
,
(h\cdot\mathrm{\Delta}^{\mathbb{P}}h)\circ\pi_{\mathbb{P}}
, etc. We also point out that we are adopting the definitions

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{Riem}}(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},X_{4})

\displaystyle=\overline{g}(\overline{R}(X_{3},X_{4})X_{2},X_{1}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Riem}^{\mathbb{P}}(Y_{1},Y_{2},Y_{3},Y_{4})

\displaystyle=\sigma(R^{\mathbb{P}}(Y_{3},Y_{4})Y_{2},Y_{1}),

for

,
Y_{i}\in T\mathbb{P}
,

, with
\overline{R}
the curvature operator of
\overline{M}
given by

(2.3)

\overline{R}(V,W)Z=\overline{\nabla}_{V}\overline{\nabla}_{W}Z-\overline{\nabla}_{W}\overline{\nabla}_{V}Z-\overline{\nabla}_{[V,W]}Z

for each

and with

the similarly defined curvature operator of

. Moreover, we recall that for a given real vector space

the Kulkarni-Nomizu product of two bilinear symmetric forms

is the
4

\alpha\mathbin{\bigcirc\mspace{-15mu}\wedge\mspace{3mu}}\beta:V\times V\times V\times V\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\displaystyle(\alpha\mathbin{\bigcirc\mspace{-15mu}\wedge\mspace{3mu}}\beta)(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},X_{4})

\displaystyle=\alpha(X_{1},X_{3})\beta(X_{2},X_{4})+\alpha(X_{2},X_{4})\beta(X_{1},X_{3})

\displaystyle\phantom{=\;}-\alpha(X_{1},X_{4})\beta(X_{2},X_{3})-\alpha(X_{2},X_{3})\beta(X_{1},X_{4})

Proof of Lemma

.

We apply Proposition 7.42 of
[

]
. Note that the definition of the Riemannian curvature operator

given by
[
23
]
, see Lemma 3.35 therein, differs from the above (

) by a minus sign, that is,
\overline{R}(V,W)Z=\overline{R}_{WV}Z=-\overline{R}_{VW}Z
, where
\overline{R}_{(\;\cdot\;,\;\cdot\;)}(\;\cdot\;)
is the notation adopted in
[
23
]
to denote the curvature operator.

Let

be a given point and let

be given tangent vectors at

, with

tangent to the leaf

and

tangent to the timelike curve

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{Riem}}(\;\cdot\;,Z,X,Y)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Riem}(\;\cdot\;,Z,X,Y),

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{Riem}}(\;\cdot\;,Z,V,X)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{Hess}^{\mathbb{P}}(h)(X,Z)}{h}\overline{g}(V,\;\cdot\;),

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{Riem}}(\;\cdot\;,W,X,Y)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{Riem}}(\;\cdot\;,W,X,V)

\displaystyle=-\overline{g}(V,W)\frac{\mathrm{Hess}^{\mathbb{P}}(h)(X,\;\cdot\;)}{h}.

By the symmetry properties of the curvature tensor, see Proposition 3.36 of

]
, we can rewrite the formulas above as

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{Riem}}(X,Y,Z,\;\cdot\;)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Riem}(X,Y,Z,\;\cdot\;),

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{Riem}}(X,Y,W,\;\cdot\;)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{Riem}}(X,V,Z,\;\cdot\;)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{Hess}^{\mathbb{P}}(h)(X,Z)}{h}\overline{g}(V,\;\cdot\;),

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{Hess}^{\mathbb{P}}(h)(X,\;\cdot\;)}{h}\overline{g}(V,W).

A direct computation shows that the RHS of (

. By the symmetry properties of

again, these identities uniquely determine its action on

∎

Now consider a spacelike hypersurface
\psi:M\to\overline{M}
immersed into a standard static spacetime
\overline{M}=\mathbb{P}\times_{h}\mathbb{R}
. In the Introduction we have defined the global unit normal vector field
N\in\mathfrak{X}^{\bot}(M)
with the same time-orientation of the Killing vector field
\partial_{t}\in\mathfrak{X}(\overline{M})
. The hyperbolic cosine of the hyperbolic angle
\theta
between
N
and
\partial_{t}
is the smooth function on
M
given by

\cosh\theta=-\overline{g}\left(N,\frac{1}{h\circ\pi}\,\partial_{t}\right).

The global shape operator
A:TM\to TM
of
\psi
in the direction of
N
is defined by

g(AX,Y)=\overline{g}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.0ptI}(X,Y),N)

for every
X,Y\in TM
, where
\mathrm{I\hskip-1.0ptI}:TM\times TM\to TM^{\bot}
is the second fundamental tensor of
\psi
and
g=\psi^{\ast}\overline{g}
is the Riemannian metric on
M
induced by the ambient manifold
(\overline{M},\overline{g})
. The mean curvature function
H\in C^{\infty}(M)
satisfying
\mathbf{H}=HN
, where
\mathbf{H}
is the mean curvature vector of
\psi
, is given by

H=-\frac{1}{m}\,\mathrm{trace}_{g}(A).

Each
p\in M
has an open neighbourhood
U\subseteq M
such that
\psi|_{U}
is an embedding. For any smooth vector field
\tilde{N}
extending
N
on a neighbourhood
V\subseteq\overline{M}
of
\psi(U)
we have

H=\frac{1}{m}\,\mathrm{div}_{\overline{g}}(\tilde{N})\circ\psi

on
U
, as a consequence of Weingarten’s equation

g(AX,Y)=-\overline{g}(\overline{\nabla}_{\psi_{\ast}X}\tilde{N},\psi_{\ast}Y).

Hereafter,
\overline{\nabla}
and
\mathrm{div}_{\overline{g}}
denote the Levi-Civita connection of
(\overline{M},\overline{g})
and the corresponding divergence operator, and
\mathrm{d}\psi=\psi_{\ast}:TM\to T\overline{M}
is the pushforward of tangent vectors induced by
\psi
, that is, the differential of
\psi
.

Let
t
be the standard coordinate on the factor
\mathbb{R}
of
\overline{M}
. The composition of
t
with the map
\pi_{\mathbb{R}}\circ\psi:M\to\mathbb{R}
defines the smooth vertical height function

\tau=t\circ\pi_{\mathbb{R}}\circ\psi

on
M
. We also set

\hat{h}=h\circ\pi=h\circ\pi_{\mathbb{P}}\circ\psi.

We first consider the manifold
M
with the induced Riemannian metric
g=\psi^{\ast}\overline{g}
. Let
\nabla
,
\mathrm{\Delta}
and
\mathrm{div}_{g}
be the Levi-Civita connection, the Laplace-Beltrami operator and the divergence operator on
(M,g)
, respectively. The gradient of the height function
\tau
satisfies

g(\nabla\tau,\;\cdot\;)=\mathrm{d}\tau=\psi^{\ast}\mathrm{d}t=-\frac{1}{\hat{h}^{2}}\,\overline{g}(\partial_{t},\psi_{\ast}\;\cdot\;)

and therefore is given by

\nabla\tau=-\frac{1}{\hat{h}^{2}}\,\partial_{t}^{\top},

where
\partial_{t}^{\top}\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
is the tangential part of
\partial_{t}
along
\psi
. In other words,
\partial_{t}^{\top}
is the unique vector field such that
\psi_{\ast}\partial_{t}^{\top}|_{p}
is the orthogonal projection of
\partial_{t}|_{\psi(p)}
onto
\psi_{\ast}T_{p}M
for each
p\in M
. Hence, we have

|\nabla\tau|^{2}_{g}=\frac{1}{\hat{h}^{4}}[\overline{g}(\partial_{t},\partial_{t})+\overline{g}(\partial_{t},N)^{2}]=\frac{1}{\hat{h}^{2}}(\cosh^{2}\theta-1)

or, equivalently,

(2.4)

\cosh\theta=\sqrt{1+\hat{h}^{2}|\nabla\tau|^{2}_{g}}.

The height function
\tau
satisfies the differential equation

(2.5)

mH\cosh\theta=\hat{h}\mathrm{\Delta}\tau+2g(\nabla\hat{h},\nabla\tau)

on
M
. Indeed, considering a local orthonormal reference frame
\{E_{1},\dots,E_{n}\}
on
(M,g)
we compute

\displaystyle\mathrm{\Delta}\tau

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}g\left(\nabla_{E_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{\hat{h}^{2}}\,\partial_{t}^{\top}\right),E_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}\left(\frac{1}{\hat{h}^{2}}\right)g(\partial_{t}^{\top},E_{i})-\frac{1}{\hat{h}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(\nabla_{E_{i}}\partial_{t}^{\top},E_{i})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\hat{h}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(E_{i},\nabla\hat{h})g(\nabla\tau,E_{i})+\frac{\cosh\theta}{\hat{h}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{g}(\overline{\nabla}_{E_{i}}N,E_{i})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\hat{h}}g(\nabla\hat{h},\nabla\tau)-\frac{\cosh\theta}{\hat{h}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g(AE_{i},E_{i}),

where in the third identity we have used the equality

\partial_{t}^{\top}=\partial_{t}+\overline{g}(N,\partial_{t})N=\partial_{t}-\hat{h}\cosh\theta N

and the fact that
\overline{g}(\overline{\nabla}_{E_{i}}\partial_{t},E_{i})=0
since
\partial_{t}
is Killing.

Equation (
2.5
) can be rewritten as

(2.6)

\frac{mH\cosh\theta}{\hat{h}}=\frac{1}{\hat{h}^{2}}\mathrm{div}_{g}(\hat{h}^{2}\nabla\tau)=\mathrm{\Delta}_{-2\log\hat{h}}\tau,

where the operator appearing in the RHS is the drifted Laplacian

\mathrm{\Delta}_{-2\log\hat{h}}=\mathrm{\Delta}-g(\nabla(-2\log\hat{h}),\nabla\;\cdot\;)=\mathrm{\Delta}+\frac{2}{\hat{h}}g\left(\nabla\hat{h},\nabla\;\cdot\;\right),

or even in the form

mH\cosh\theta=\frac{1}{\hat{h}}\mathrm{div}_{g}(\hat{h}^{2}\nabla\tau)=\mathrm{div}_{-\log\hat{h},g}(\hat{h}\nabla\tau)

where
\mathrm{div}_{-\log\hat{h},g}
is the weighted divergence operator

(2.7)

\mathrm{div}_{-\log\hat{h},g}=\mathrm{div}_{g}-g(\nabla(-\log\hat{h}),\;\cdot\;)=\mathrm{div}_{g}+\frac{1}{\hat{h}}g(\nabla\hat{h},\;\cdot\;).

Our aim is now to provide a further expression for
mH
in terms of
\tau
by considering a second metric on
M
different from the induced metric
g=\psi^{\ast}\overline{g}
. Towards this end we observe that the smooth map
\pi:M\to\mathbb{P}
is an immersion because
\psi
is spacelike. Since
\dim M=m=\dim\mathbb{P}
, we have that
\pi
is a local diffeomorphism. This allows us to define a second Riemannian metric

\hat{\sigma}=\pi^{\ast}\sigma

on
M
. Let
D
,
\mathrm{div}_{\hat{\sigma}}
be the Levi-Civita connection and the corresponding divergence operator on
(M,\hat{\sigma})
, respectively. From the definition of
\overline{g}
and
g
it follows that

(2.8)

\hat{\sigma}=g+\hat{h}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}.

Thus, the gradient
D\tau
of
\tau
satisfies

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}(D\tau,\;\cdot\;)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}\tau=g(\nabla\tau,\;\cdot\;)=\hat{\sigma}(\nabla\tau,\;\cdot\;)-\hat{h}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau(\nabla\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\hat{\sigma}(\nabla\tau,\;\cdot\;)-\hat{h}^{2}|\nabla\tau|^{2}_{g}\hat{\sigma}(D\tau,\;\cdot\;).

As a consequence using (
2.4
)

D\tau=\frac{\nabla\tau}{1+\hat{h}^{2}|\nabla\tau|^{2}_{g}}=\frac{\nabla\tau}{\cosh^{2}\theta}.

From (
2.8
) we obtain

|D\tau|^{2}_{\hat{\sigma}}=\frac{1}{\cosh^{4}\theta}\left(|\nabla\tau|^{2}_{g}+\hat{h}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau(\nabla\tau)^{2}\right)=\frac{|\nabla\tau|^{2}_{g}}{1+\hat{h}^{2}|\nabla\tau|^{2}_{g}}.

It follows that

h^{2}|D\tau|^{2}_{\hat{\sigma}}=\frac{\hat{h}^{2}|\nabla\tau|^{2}_{g}}{1+\hat{h}^{2}|\nabla\tau|^{2}_{g}}<1

so that the above can be equivalently written as

|\nabla\tau|^{2}_{g}=\frac{|D\tau|^{2}_{\hat{\sigma}}}{1-\hat{h}^{2}|D\tau|^{2}_{\hat{\sigma}}}.

From (
2.4
) we infer

(2.9)

\cosh\theta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\hat{h}^{2}|D\tau|^{2}_{\hat{\sigma}}}}.

The following proposition will be crucial for the results in the next sections.

Proposition 1
.

In the above notations we have

(2.10)

mH=\frac{1}{\hat{h}}\mathrm{div}_{\hat{\sigma}}\left(\frac{\hat{h}^{2}D\tau}{\sqrt{1-\hat{h}^{2}|D\tau|^{2}_{\hat{\sigma}}}}\right)=\mathrm{div}_{-\log\hat{h},\hat{\sigma}}\left(\frac{\hat{h}D\tau}{\sqrt{1-\hat{h}^{2}|D\tau|^{2}_{\hat{\sigma}}}}\right),

where the weighted divergence operator
\mathrm{div}_{-\log\hat{h},\hat{\sigma}}
is defined in way similar to that in (

Proof.

Let
p\in M
be a given point. Since
\pi
is a local isometry, there exists an open neighbourhood
U_{p}\subseteq M
of

such that the restriction
\psi|_{U_{p}}:U_{p}\to\overline{M}
is an embedding and
\pi|_{U_{p}}:U_{p}\to\mathbb{P}
is an isometric diffeomorphism onto the image

. We fix the index ranges

1\leq i,j,k,\dots\leq m,\quad 1\leq a,b,c,\dots\leq m+1.

Up to restricting
U_{p}
, we can assume that there exists a local orthonormal coframe
\{\theta^{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
for
(\mathbb{P},\sigma)
defined on

, with corresponding Levi-Civita connection forms
\{\theta^{i}_{j}\}_{i,j=1}^{m}
defined by the structural equations

(2.11)

\begin{split}\mathrm{d}\theta^{i}&=-\theta^{i}_{j}\wedge\theta^{j},\\
\theta^{i}_{j}+\theta^{j}_{i}&=0\qquad\qquad\text{for }1\leq i,j\leq m.\end{split}

We recall that orthonormality of the coframe
\{\theta^{i}\}
means that the metric
\sigma
is expressed as
\sigma=\delta_{ij}\theta^{i}\otimes\theta^{j}
, with

the Kronecker symbol, and we also recall that the Levi-Civita connection forms
\{\theta^{i}_{j}\}
are the unique

dual to

are given by

De_{j}=\theta^{i}_{j}\otimes e_{i}.

We can define a local Lorentz orthonormal coframe
\{\omega^{a}\}_{a=1}^{m+1}
on
U\times_{h}\mathbb{R}\subseteq\overline{M}
by setting

(2.12)

\omega^{i}=\theta^{i}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq m,\quad\omega^{m+1}=h\mathrm{d}t.

In this case, Lorentz orthonormality means that the metric
\overline{g}
is given by

\overline{g}=\delta_{ij}\omega^{i}\otimes\omega^{j}-\omega^{m+1}\otimes\omega^{m+1}.

The corresponding Levi-Civita connection forms
\{\omega^{a}_{b}\}_{a,b=1}^{m+1}
are defined by the structural equations

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\omega^{a}

\displaystyle=-\omega^{a}_{b}\wedge\omega^{b},

\displaystyle\omega^{i}_{j}+\omega^{j}_{i}=\omega^{i}_{m+1}-\omega^{m+1}_{i}

\displaystyle=\omega^{m+1}_{m+1}=0\quad\text{for }1\leq i,j\leq m.

A straightforward computation using (
2.11
) and (
2.12
) shows that

(2.13)

\omega^{i}_{j}=\theta^{i}_{j},\quad\omega^{m+1}_{i}=\frac{h_{i}}{h}\omega^{m+1},

where
\mathrm{d}h=h_{i}\theta^{i}
on
\mathbb{P}
.

Since
\pi|_{U_{p}}
is an isometry onto the image, we have that
\{\pi^{\ast}\theta^{i}\}
is a local orthonormal coframe on
U_{p}
and that
\{\pi^{\ast}\theta^{i}_{j}\}
are the corresponding Levi-Civita connection forms. From now on, we will omit writing the pullback
\pi^{\ast}
. We set
\alpha=\psi^{\ast}\omega^{m+1}
on
M
. Note that
\alpha=\hat{h}\,\mathrm{d}\tau
. Writing
\alpha=\alpha_{i}\theta^{i}
on
U_{p}
and letting
\{\overline{e}_{a}\}_{a=1}^{m+1}
be the local orthonormal frame for
(\overline{M},\overline{g})
dual to
\{\omega^{a}\}
we can easily verify that the local orthonormal frame
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
for
(M,\hat{\sigma})
dual to
\{\theta^{i}\}
must satisfy

(2.14)

(\mathrm{d}\psi)_{q}(e_{i})_{q}=(\bar{e}_{i})_{\psi(q)}+\alpha_{i}(q)(\bar{e}_{m+1})_{\psi(q)}

for each
q\in U_{p}
. From (
2.14
) it follows that for any smooth extension
\overline{N}=\overline{N}^{a}\overline{e}_{a}
of
N
on a neighbourhood of
\psi(U_{p})\subseteq\overline{M}
the functions
N^{a}:=\overline{N}^{a}\circ\psi\in C^{\infty}(U_{p})
,
1\leq a\leq m+1
, must satisfy

(2.15)

N^{m+1}=-\overline{g}(N,\overline{e}_{m+1})=\omega^{m+1}(N)=-\overline{g}\left(N,\frac{1}{h}\,\partial_{t}\right)=\cosh\theta

and

(2.16)

N^{i}=\alpha_{i}N^{m+1}=\alpha_{i}\cosh\theta

for
1\leq i\leq m
.

Let
\mathrm{I\hskip-1.0ptI}=\mathrm{I\hskip-1.0ptI}_{ij}\theta^{i}\otimes\theta^{j}\otimes N
be the second fundamental tensor of the immersion
\psi
on
U_{p}
. By Weingarten’s equation we have

\mathrm{I\hskip-1.0ptI}_{ij}=\overline{g}\left(\overline{\nabla}_{(\mathrm{d}\psi)e_{i}}\overline{N},(\mathrm{d}\psi)e_{j}\right)

for
1\leq i,j\leq m
. Writing
\overline{\nabla}\,\overline{N}=\overline{N}^{a}_{b}\omega^{b}\otimes\overline{e}_{a}
we have, by the properties of covariant differentiation,

(2.17)

\overline{N}^{a}_{b}\omega^{b}=\mathrm{d}\overline{N}^{a}+\overline{N}^{b}\omega^{a}_{b}

for
1\leq a\leq m+1
. From (
2.14
) and (
2.17
) we deduce

\displaystyle\mathrm{I\hskip-1.0ptI}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\overline{g}\left(\overline{N}^{a}_{b}\omega^{b}((\mathrm{d}\psi)e_{i})\overline{e}_{a},(\mathrm{d}\psi)e_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}(\overline{N}^{k}_{b}\omega^{b})((\mathrm{d}\psi)e_{i})-\alpha_{j}(\overline{N}^{m+1}_{b}\omega^{b})((\mathrm{d}\psi)e_{i})

\displaystyle=\left[\delta_{jk}(\mathrm{d}N^{k}+N^{a}\psi^{\ast}\omega^{k}_{a})-\alpha_{j}(\mathrm{d}N^{m+1}+N^{a}\psi^{\ast}\omega^{m+1}_{a})\right](e_{i})

and from (
2.16
) we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{I\hskip-1.0ptI}_{ij}\theta^{i}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{d}(\alpha_{j}\cosh\theta)-\alpha_{k}\cosh\theta\cdot\psi^{\ast}\omega^{k}_{j}+\cosh\theta\cdot\psi^{\ast}\omega^{m+1}_{j}

\displaystyle\phantom{=\;}-\alpha_{j}(\mathrm{d}\cosh\theta)-\alpha_{j}\alpha_{k}\cosh\theta\cdot\psi^{\ast}\omega^{k}_{m+1}

\displaystyle=\cosh\theta\left[\mathrm{d}\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k}\psi^{\ast}\omega^{k}_{j}+\psi^{\ast}\omega^{m+1}_{j}-\alpha_{j}\alpha_{k}\psi^{\ast}\omega^{k}_{m+1}\right].

Using (
2.13
) we can further write

(2.18)

\mathrm{I\hskip-1.0ptI}_{ij}\theta^{i}=\cosh\theta\left[\mathrm{d}\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k}\theta^{k}_{j}+\left(\hat{h}_{j}-\alpha_{j}\alpha_{k}\hat{h}^{k}\right)\frac{\alpha}{\hat{h}}\right],

where
\mathrm{d}\hat{h}=\hat{h}_{k}\theta^{k}
on
U_{p}
. Again, by the properties of covariant differentiation we have

(2.19)

(\mathrm{d}\alpha_{j}-\alpha_{k}\theta^{k}_{j})\otimes\theta^{j}=D\alpha.

Note that the metric
g=\psi^{\ast}\overline{g}
is given by
g=g_{ij}\theta^{i}\otimes\theta^{j}
with
g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}-\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}
and that, by (
2.9
),

\cosh^{2}\theta=\frac{1}{1-\delta^{ij}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}}.

A straightforward computation shows that the elements of the inverse matrix
(g^{ij})=(g_{ij})^{-1}
are

g^{ij}=\delta^{ij}+\cosh^{2}\theta\,\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}.

Therefore, the mean curvature function

H=-\frac{1}{m}\mathrm{trace}_{g}(A)=\frac{1}{m}\mathrm{trace}_{g}(\mathrm{I\hskip-1.0ptI}_{ij}\theta^{i}\otimes\theta^{j})

is given by

\displaystyle mH

\displaystyle=\cosh\theta\cdot\mathrm{trace}_{\hat{\sigma}}(D\alpha)+\cosh^{3}\theta\cdot D\alpha(\hat{h}D\tau,\hat{h}D\tau)

\displaystyle\phantom{=\;}+\cosh\theta\cdot\mathrm{d}\hat{h}(D\tau)-\cosh\theta\cdot\mathrm{d}\hat{h}(D\tau)\cdot\delta^{ij}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle\phantom{=\;}+\cosh^{3}\theta\cdot\mathrm{d}\hat{h}(D\tau)\cdot\delta^{ij}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}-\cosh^{3}\theta\cdot\mathrm{d}\hat{h}(D\tau)(\delta^{ij}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j})^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{trace}_{\hat{\sigma}}(D(\cosh\theta\cdot\alpha))+\cosh\theta\cdot\mathrm{d}\hat{h}(D\tau)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}_{\hat{\sigma}}\left(\frac{\hat{h}D\tau}{\sqrt{1-\hat{h}^{2}|D\tau|^{2}}}\right)+\frac{\hat{\sigma}(D\hat{h},D\tau)}{\sqrt{1-\hat{h}^{2}|D\tau|^{2}}}

that is, (
2.10
).
∎

Remark 3
.

We observe that, if the map
\pi:M\to\mathbb{P}
is injective,
\psi(M)\subseteq\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{R}
is the graph of a smooth function
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{P}
an open domain. In this case
\Omega=\pi(M)
,
u=\tau\circ\pi^{-1}
and
\pi:(M,\hat{\sigma})\to(\Omega,\sigma|_{\Omega})
is an isometry. Thus equation (
2.10
) reduces to the prescribed mean curvature equation

m\,(H\circ\pi^{-1})=\frac{1}{h}\,\mathrm{div}_{\sigma}\left(\frac{h^{2}Du}{\sqrt{1-h^{2}|Du|^{2}_{\sigma}}}\right),

with
Du
the gradient of
u
in
(\Omega,\sigma|_{\Omega})
.

3.
Hyperbolic angle estimates

Throughout this section we consider
M
furnished with the metric
\hat{\sigma}
and we denote by
H
and
\tau
the mean curvature function and the vertical height function, respectively, of the immersion
\psi:M\to\overline{M}
, as defined in the previous section; in particular
H
is the mean curvature function in the direction of the normal
N
to the immersion. Integration over domains
\Omega\subseteq M
will always be intended with respect to the volume element induced by
\hat{\sigma}
and integration over boundaries of sufficiently regular domains is intended with respect to the corresponding
(m-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. In particular, for each relatively compact domain
\Omega\subseteq M
we define the weighted volume

\mathrm{vol}_{-\log\hat{h}}(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}\hat{h}

and, if
\partial\Omega
is sufficiently regular, we also define

\mathrm{vol}_{-\log\hat{h}}(\partial\Omega)=\int_{\partial\Omega}\hat{h}.

This apparently cumbersome notation for the weighted volume is due to the standard notation

\mathrm{vol}_{f}(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}e^{-f}

for
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
. For each relatively compact domain
\Omega\subseteq M
we shall also denote the weighted integral mean of
H
over
\Omega
by

(3.1)

\widehat{H}(\Omega)=\frac{\int_{\Omega}H\hat{h}}{\mathrm{vol}_{-\log\hat{h}}(\Omega)}.

Our aim is to deduce two consequences of equation (
2.10
). The first is based on a clever idea of Salavessa,
[
30
]
. Suppose that
(M,\hat{\sigma})
is complete, noncompact. We introduce the weighted Cheeger constant
\mathfrak{C}_{-\log\hat{h}}
of the weighted manifold
(M,\hat{\sigma},\hat{h})
by setting

(3.2)

\begin{split}\mathfrak{C}_{-\log\hat{h}}&=\inf\left\{\frac{\int_{\partial\Omega}\hat{h}}{\int_{\Omega}\hat{h}}:\text{$\Omega\subseteq M$ relatively compact domain, $\partial\Omega$ smooth}\right\}\\
&=\inf\left\{\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{-\log\hat{h}}(\partial\Omega)}{\mathrm{vol}_{-\log\hat{h}}(\Omega)}:\text{$\Omega\subseteq M$ relatively compact domain, $\partial\Omega$ smooth}\right\}.\end{split}

Note also that the definition (
3.2
) of the Cheeger constant has to be changed in case
M
is compact.

Introducing the operator
\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta}}_{-\log\hat{h}}=\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta}}+\hat{\sigma}\left(\frac{D\hat{h}}{\hat{h}},D\;\cdot\;\right)
where
\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta}}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator of
(M,\hat{\sigma})
and indicating with

\lambda_{1}^{\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta}}_{-\log\hat{h}}}(M)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in C^{\infty}(M)\\
\varphi\not\equiv 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|D\varphi|^{2}\hat{h}}{\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{2}\hat{h}}

its spectral radius, following the original argument in Cheeger,
[
13
]
, it is not hard to show that

(3.3)

\lambda_{1}^{\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta}}_{-\log\hat{h}}}(M)\geq\frac{1}{4}\left(\mathfrak{C}_{-\log\hat{h}}\right)^{2}.

We are now ready to prove

Proposition 2
.

Let
\overline{M}^{m+1}=\mathbb{P}^{m}\times_{h}\mathbb{R}
be a standard static spacetime with complete, noncompact base
(\mathbb{P},\sigma)
and let
\psi:M\to\overline{M}
be a spacelike hypersurface such that
\pi:M\to\mathbb{P}
is a covering map. Let
\mathfrak{C}_{-\log\hat{h}}
be the weighted Cheeger constant of
(M,\hat{\sigma},\hat{h})
and let
\cosh\theta
be the hyperbolic cosine of the hyperbolic angle of the immersion
\psi
. Suppose that

(3.4)

\sup_{M}\cosh\theta=\cosh\theta^{\ast}<+\infty.

Then, the mean curvature
H
in the direction of
N
satisfies

(3.5)

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\subseteq M,\\
\overline{\Omega}\text{ compact}\end{subarray}}|\widehat{H}(\Omega)|\leq\frac{\sqrt{\cosh^{2}\theta^{\ast}-1}}{m}\,\mathfrak{C}_{-\log\hat{h}}.

In particular, if
H
is a constant,
\hat{h}\notin L^{1}(M,\hat{\sigma})
and the function
r\mapsto\mathrm{vol}_{-\log\hat{h}}(\widehat{B}_{r})
has subexponential growth for some (hence, any)
o\in M
, where
\widehat{B}_{r}=B^{\hat{\sigma}}_{r}(o)
, then
\psi:M\to\overline{M}
is maximal.

Proof.

Let
\hat{\sigma}=\pi^{\ast}\sigma
and
\Omega\subseteq M
a relatively compact domain with smooth boundary and outward unit normal vector
\nu
. We apply the divergence theorem to (
2.10
) to obtain

(3.6)

m|\widehat{H}(\Omega)|\mathrm{vol}_{-\log\hat{h}}(\Omega)=\left|\int_{\Omega}mH\hat{h}\right|=\left|\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\hat{h}^{2}\hat{\sigma}(D\tau,\nu)}{\sqrt{1-\hat{h}^{2}|D\tau|^{2}}}\right|\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\hat{h}^{2}|D\tau|}{\sqrt{1-\hat{h}^{2}|D\tau|^{2}}}

where
\tau=t\circ\pi_{\mathbb{P}}\circ\psi
and where the measure we are considering is with respect to
\hat{\sigma}
. Note that this latter is complete because
g=\psi^{\ast}\overline{g}
is so by assumption. As we have already observed we have the validity of (
2.9
). From (
3.4
) and (
3.6
) it follows that

m|\widehat{H}(\Omega)|\mathrm{vol}_{-\log\hat{h}}(\Omega)\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}\hat{h}\sqrt{\cosh^{2}\theta-1}\leq\sqrt{\cosh^{2}\theta^{\ast}-1}\int_{\partial\Omega}\hat{h}

or, in other words,

|\widehat{H}(\Omega)|\leq\frac{\sqrt{\cosh^{2}\theta^{\ast}-1}}{m}\frac{\mathrm{vol}_{-\log\hat{h}}(\partial\Omega)}{\mathrm{vol}_{-\log\hat{h}}(\Omega)}.

Letting
\Omega
run over all relatively compact domains in
M
with smooth boundary and taking the infimum, from the previous inequality we deduce the validity of (
3.5
).

To complete the proof we observe, see
[
9
]
, that if
\mathrm{vol}_{-\log\hat{h}}(M)=+\infty
and
\mathrm{vol}_{-\log\hat{h}}(B^{\hat{\sigma}}_{r})
has subexponential growth, then

\lambda_{1}^{\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta}}_{-\log\hat{h}}}(M)=0

and the final part of the Proposition follows from (
3.5
) and (
3.3
).
∎

Consider now the case where
M=\mathbb{P}
and
\psi:M\to\overline{M}=\mathbb{P}\times_{h}\mathbb{R}
is a graph given by the function
u:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{R}
, that is,
\psi(x)=\psi_{u}(x)=(x,u(x))
. In this case
\pi(x)=\pi_{\mathbb{P}}\circ\psi(x)=x
and therefore

\hat{\sigma}=\pi^{\ast}\sigma=(\pi_{\mathbb{P}}\circ\psi)^{\ast}(\sigma)=\sigma,\quad\hat{h}=h\circ\pi=h,\quad\tau=t\circ\pi=t.

It follows that (
2.10
) becomes exactly the mean curvature equation of the graph
\psi_{u}
, that is,

mH=\frac{1}{h}\,\mathrm{div}_{\sigma}\left(\frac{h^{2}Du}{\sqrt{1-h^{2}|Du|^{2}_{\sigma}}}\right).

As a consequence we have the following

Corollary 9
.

Let
\psi:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{P}^{m}\times_{h}\mathbb{R}
be a spacelike graph given by the function
u:\mathbb{P}\to\mathbb{R}
. Assume that
(\mathbb{P},\sigma)
is complete and let
\mathfrak{C}_{-\log h}^{\mathbb{P}}
be the weighted Cheeger constant of
(\mathbb{P},\sigma,h)
. Assume the hyperbolic cosine of the hyperbolic angle is bounded above by
\cosh\theta^{\ast}<+\infty
. Then the mean curvature
H
of the graph satisfies

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\subseteq M,\\
\overline{\Omega}\text{ compact}\end{subarray}}|\widehat{H}(\Omega)|\leq\frac{\sqrt{\cosh^{2}\theta^{\ast}-1}}{m}\,\mathfrak{C}_{-\log h}^{\mathbb{P}}.

In particular, if
H
is a constant,
h\notin L^{1}(\mathbb{P},\sigma)
and
\mathrm{vol}_{-\log h}(B^{\sigma}r)
has subexponential growth, then
\psi_{u}
is maximal.

Remark 4
.

Note that completeness of
g=\psi^{\ast}_{u}\overline{g}
implies that of
\hat{\sigma}=\sigma
in this case. However completeness of
\hat{\sigma}
is generally less stringent.

We shall now analyze more consequences of equation (
2.10
) that will lead to a proof of Theorems
1
and
2
. In particular, we develop the observations above restricting ourselves to consider the case where
\Omega
runs over all geodesic balls
\widehat{B}_{r}=B^{\hat{\sigma}}_{r}(o)
of
(M,\hat{\sigma})
centered at a fixed point
o\in M
. Towards this end, set
\widehat{\gamma}=d_{\hat{\sigma}}(o,\;\cdot\;)
, with
d_{\hat{\sigma}}
the distance on
M
induced by the metric
\hat{\sigma}
. When
\widehat{B}_{r}
is a relatively compact subset in
M
, we have the validity of the next two lemmas. The first, a weighted divergence theorem with low regularity assumptions, is well known in the non-weighted version. We report its proof here for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 2
.

Let
r>0
be such that
\widehat{B}_{r}
is relatively compact in
M
. Then

(3.7)

\int_{\partial\widehat{B}_{s}}\hat{h}\,\hat{\sigma}\left(\frac{\hat{h}D\tau}{\sqrt{1-\hat{h}^{2}|D\tau|^{2}}},D\widehat{\gamma}\right)=m\int_{\widehat{B}_{s}}\hat{h}H

for a.e.
s\in(0,r)
.

Proof.

The function
\widehat{\gamma}
is Lipschitz continuous and therefore the integral in the LHS of (
3.7
) is well defined for a.e.
s\in(0,r)
. Let
f:(0,r)\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

f(s)=\int_{\widehat{B}_{s}}\hat{h}\,\hat{\sigma}\left(\frac{\hat{h}D\tau}{\sqrt{1-\hat{h}^{2}|D\tau|^{2}}},D\widehat{\gamma}\right).

By the coarea formula (see
[
34
]
, p. 89) we have

(3.8)

\begin{split}f(s_{1})-f(s_{0})&=\int_{\widehat{B}_{s_{1}}\setminus\widehat{B}_{s_{0}}}\hat{h}\,\hat{\sigma}\left(\frac{\hat{h}D\tau}{\sqrt{1-\hat{h}^{2}|D\tau|^{2}}},D\widehat{\gamma}\right)\\
&=\int_{s_{0}}^{s_{1}}\left[\int_{\partial\widehat{B}_{s}}\hat{h}\,\hat{\sigma}\left(\frac{\hat{h}D\tau}{\sqrt{1-\hat{h}^{2}|D\tau|^{2}}},D\widehat{\gamma}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}s\end{split}

for each
0<s_{0}<s_{1}<r
. On the other hand, we also have
f\in C^{1}((0,r))
and

(3.9)

f^{\prime}(s)=m\int_{\widehat{B}_{s}}\hat{h}H

for each
s\in(0,r)
. Indeed, for every
0<t_{0}<t_{1}<r
let
\psi_{t_{0},t_{1}}\in\mathrm{Lip}_{c}(M)
be defined by

\psi_{t_{0},t_{1}}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x\in\widehat{B}_{t_{0}},\\
\dfrac{t_{1}-\widehat{\gamma}(x)}{t_{1}-t_{0}}&\text{if }x\in\widehat{B}_{t_{1}}\setminus\widehat{B}_{t_{0}},\\
0&\text{if }x\in M\setminus\widehat{B}_{t_{1}}.\end{cases}

We use (
2.10
) and we integrate by parts to get

(3.10)

m\int_{\widehat{B}_{t_{1}}}\hat{h}H\psi_{t_{1},t_{0}}=\int_{\widehat{B}_{t_{1}}\setminus\widehat{B}_{t_{0}}}\hat{h}\,\hat{\sigma}\left(\frac{\hat{h}D\tau}{\sqrt{1-\hat{h}^{2}|D\tau|^{2}}},D\psi_{t_{1},t_{0}}\right)=\frac{f(t_{1})-f(t_{0})}{t_{1}-t_{0}}.

For any given
s\in(0,r)
, identity (
3.9
) follows by fixing either
t_{0}=s
or
t_{1}=s
, letting
t_{1}-t_{0}\to 0^{+}
in (
3.10
) and applying the dominated convergence theorem. By (
3.8
) we get

\int_{s_{0}}^{s_{1}}\left[f^{\prime}(s)-\int_{\partial\widehat{B}_{s}}\hat{h}\,\hat{\sigma}\left(\frac{\hat{h}D\tau}{\sqrt{1-\hat{h}^{2}|D\tau|^{2}}},D\widehat{\gamma}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}s=0

for each
0<s_{0}<s_{1}<r
. Then (
3.7
) follows for a.e.
s\in(0,r)
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

Let
r>0
be such that
\widehat{B}_{r}
is relatively compact in
M
. Then

(3.11)

\log\int_{\widehat{B}_{r}}\hat{h}-\log\int_{\widehat{B}_{R}}\hat{h}=\int_{R}^{r}\left[\frac{\int_{\partial\widehat{B}_{s}}\hat{h}}{\int_{\widehat{B}_{s}}\hat{h}}\right]\mathrm{d}s

for each
R\in(0,r)
.

Proof.

Let
f:(0,r)\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

f(s)=\int_{\widehat{B}_{s}}\hat{h}.

By the coarea formula again, we have that

f(s_{1})-f(s_{0})=\int_{\widehat{B}_{s_{1}}\setminus\widehat{B}_{s_{0}}}\hat{h}=\int_{s_{0}}^{s_{1}}\left[\int_{\partial\widehat{B}_{s}}\hat{h}\right]\mathrm{d}s

for each
0<s_{0}<s_{1}<r
. The function
f
is absolutely continuous on
(0,r)
, hence it is a.e. differentiable on
(0,r)
and there exists
\varphi\in L^{1}((0,r))
such that

f(s_{1})-f(s_{0})=\int_{s_{0}}^{s_{1}}\varphi(s)\mathrm{d}s

for each
0<s_{0}<s_{1}<r
. Therefore,

\varphi(s)=\int_{\partial\widehat{B}_{s}}\hat{h}

for a.e.
s\in(0,r)
. For each
R\in(0,r)
, the function
\log f
is also absolutely continuous on
(R,r)
and its a.e. defined derivative is a.e. equal to
\varphi/f
. Hence, (
3.11
) follows.
∎

We will also need the following “long-range” version of the mean value theorem.

Lemma 4
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable, locally integrable function. Let
0<R<R_{1}
,
\varepsilon>0
be given. There exists
R_{2}>R_{1}
such that

(3.12)

\essinf_{(R_{1},r)}f-\varepsilon<\frac{1}{r-R}\int_{R}^{r}f(t)\mathrm{d}t<\esssup_{(R_{1},r)}f+\varepsilon

for each
r>R_{2}
.

Proof.

We only prove the existence of
R_{2}>R_{1}
such that the first inequality in (
3.12
) is satisfied for each
r>R_{2}
. The proof of the second one is analogous. Set

f_{\ast}(r)=\essinf_{(R_{1},r)}f

for each
r>R_{1}
. Suppose that
r>R_{1}+\varepsilon
is such that

\frac{1}{r-R}\int_{R}^{r}f(t)\mathrm{d}t\leq\essinf_{(R_{1},r)}f-\varepsilon=f_{\ast}(r)-\varepsilon.

Multiplying both sides by
r-R
and noting that
f_{\ast}
is nonincreasing, we get

\displaystyle-\varepsilon(r-R)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{R}^{r}[f(t)-f_{\ast}(t)]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{R}^{R_{1}+\varepsilon}[f(t)-f_{\ast}(t)]\mathrm{d}t+\int_{R_{1}+\varepsilon}^{r}[f(t)-f_{\ast}(t)]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\int_{R}^{R_{1}+\varepsilon}[f(t)-f_{\ast}(R_{1}+\varepsilon)]\mathrm{d}t+\int_{R_{1}+\varepsilon}^{r}[f(t)-f_{\ast}(t)]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\int_{R}^{R_{1}+\varepsilon}[f(t)-f_{\ast}(R_{1}+\varepsilon)]\mathrm{d}t.

Hence, it must be

(3.13)

r\leq R-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{R}^{R_{1}+\varepsilon}[f(t)-f_{\ast}(R_{1}+\varepsilon)]\mathrm{d}t.

Note that the RHS of (
3.13
) is different from
+\infty
. Setting

R_{2}:=\max\left\{R_{1}+\varepsilon,R-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{R}^{R_{1}+\varepsilon}[f(t)-f_{\ast}(R_{1}+\varepsilon)]\mathrm{d}t\right\}

we have that the first inequality in (
3.12
) holds for each
r>R_{2}
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 10
.

Consider the manifold
(M,\hat{\sigma})
. Then, with the notations introduced above, the following statements hold true.

(a)

If
r>0
is such that
\widehat{B}_{r}
is relatively compact in
M
, then

(3.14)

\max_{\partial\widehat{B}_{s}}\sqrt{\cosh^{2}\theta-1}\,\frac{\int_{\partial\widehat{B}_{s}}\hat{h}}{\int_{\widehat{B}_{s}}\hat{h}}\geq m|\widehat{H}(\widehat{B}_{s})|

for a.e.
s\in(0,r)

A half-space theorem

We reproduce some of the arguments of Section 3.3 of
[
4
]
, with due differences, to prove a comparison principle at infinity for the Lorentzian mean curvature operator

Lu=\frac{1}{q}\,\mathrm{div}\left(\frac{q^{2}Du}{\sqrt{1-q^{2}|Du|^{2}}}\right)

on every end of a complete, noncompact Riemannian manifold
(\widetilde{M},\langle,\rangle)
, where
q
is a sufficiently regular positive function on
\widetilde{M}
. We recall that an end of a noncompact manifold
\widetilde{M}
with respect to a compact subset
K\subseteq\widetilde{M}
is any unbounded connected component of
\widetilde{M}\setminus K
. We start by stating and proving some preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 6
.

Let
(V,\langle,\rangle)
be a real vector space with a given positive definite symmetric bilinear form. Let
X,Y\in V
be such that
|X|,|Y|<1
, where
|X|=\sqrt{\langle X,X\rangle}
,
|Y|=\sqrt{\langle Y,Y\rangle}
. Then

\left\langle\frac{X}{\sqrt{1-|X|^{2}}}-\frac{Y}{\sqrt{1-|Y|^{2}}},X-Y\right\rangle\geq 0

and the equality holds if and only if
X=Y
.

Proof.

If
|X|=|Y|
, then

\left\langle\frac{X}{\sqrt{1-|X|^{2}}}-\frac{Y}{\sqrt{1-|Y|^{2}}},X-Y\right\rangle=\frac{|X-Y|^{2}}{\sqrt{1-|X|^{2}}}

and the claim is proved. If
|Y|=t|X|
,
t\in[0,1)
, then

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{X}{\sqrt{1-|X|^{2}}}-\frac{Y}{\sqrt{1-|Y|^{2}}},X-Y\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle X,X-Y\rangle}{\sqrt{1-|X|^{2}}}-\frac{\langle Y,X-Y\rangle}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}|X|^{2}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1-t)|X|^{2}}{\sqrt{1-|X|^{2}}}-\frac{(1-t)t|X|^{2}}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}|X|^{2}}}

\displaystyle=(1-t)|X|\left(\frac{|X|}{\sqrt{1-|X|^{2}}}-\frac{t|X|}{\sqrt{1-t^{2}|X|^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 0,

where the last inequality is strict unless
|X|=0
.
∎

Lemma 7
.

Let
(\widetilde{M},\langle\;,\;\rangle)
be a Riemannian manifold and let
\Omega\subseteq\widetilde{M}
be a nonempty, connected, relatively compact open subset. Let
q
be a measurable function on
\Omega
such that
q>0
a. e. on
\Omega
and let
u,v\in\mathrm{Lip}(\overline{\Omega})
be such that

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\esssup_{\overline{\Omega}}q|Du|<1,\\
\esssup_{\overline{\Omega}}q|Dv|<1,\\[2.84544pt]
\mathrm{div}\left(\dfrac{q^{2}Du}{\sqrt{1-q^{2}|Du|^{2}}}\right)\leq\mathrm{div}\left(\dfrac{q^{2}Dv}{\sqrt{1-q^{2}|Dv|^{2}}}\right)&\text{weakly on }\Omega,\\
u\geq v&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Then,
u\geq v
on
\overline{\Omega}
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be given. Let
\alpha\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
be such that

\begin{cases}\alpha(t)=0&\text{for each }t\geq-\varepsilon,\\
\alpha^{\prime}(t)<0&\text{for each }t<-\varepsilon.\end{cases}

Consider a vector field
W
on
\Omega
such that

\begin{cases}W=[\alpha\circ(u-v)]\left(\dfrac{q^{2}Du}{\sqrt{1-q^{2}|Du|^{2}}}-\dfrac{q^{2}Dv}{\sqrt{1-q^{2}|Dv|^{2}}}\right)&\text{a.~e.~on }\Omega,\\
W\equiv 0&\text{where }u\geq v-\varepsilon.\end{cases}

Because of (
4.1
), the vector field
W
is compactly supported in
\Omega
and its weak divergence satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}W

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle\frac{q^{2}Du}{\sqrt{1-q^{2}|Du|^{2}}}-\frac{q^{2}Dv}{\sqrt{1-q^{2}|Dv|^{2}}},D[\alpha\circ(u-v)]\right\rangle

\displaystyle=[\alpha^{\prime}\circ(u-v)]\left\langle\frac{qDu}{\sqrt{1-q^{2}|Du|^{2}}}-\frac{qDv}{\sqrt{1-q^{2}|Dv|^{2}}},qDu-qDv\right\rangle\leq 0

weakly on
\Omega
, where the last inequality follows from Lemma
6
. By applying the divergence theorem to
W
on an open subset
\tilde{\Omega}\subseteq\Omega
with smooth boundary and such that
\supp W\subseteq\tilde{\Omega}
we get

0=\int_{\tilde{\Omega}}\mathrm{div}W=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}W.

It follows that

[\alpha^{\prime}\circ(u-v)]\left\langle\frac{qDu}{\sqrt{1-q^{2}|Du|^{2}}}-\frac{qDv}{\sqrt{1-q^{2}|Dv|^{2}}},qDu-qDv\right\rangle=0

a. e. on
\Omega
. From Lemma
6
again we deduce that
Du=Dv
a. e. where
u<v-\varepsilon
. Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrarily given, it follows that the Lipschitz function
(u-v)_{-}
has almost everywhere vanishing gradient on
\Omega
, and therefore it is constant on
\overline{\Omega}
. From (
4.1
) it follows that it is identically zero on
\overline{\Omega}
and the claim is proved.
∎

Lemma 8

Let

. Let

Then

Proof.

Note that

and

only differ by an additive constant, we also have

This implies that

\begin{cases}u-v>0&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{R},\\
u-v<0&\text{on a nonempty subset of }\partial\Omega_{r},\\

So, the subset

is nonempty and relatively compact in

on

By Lemma

Remark 5
.

A Riemannian manifold admitting only constant functions as upper bounded subharmonic functions is said to be parabolic. In
[
4
]
the definition of parabolicity is extended to a wide family of elliptic differential operators
L
, including the drifted Laplace-Beltrami operator. Such an operator
L
is said to be parabolic on
(M_{0},\langle,\rangle)
if each function
u\in C^{1}(M)
satisfying
u^{\ast}<+\infty
and
Lu\geq 0
on
M
(in the weak sense) is constant. Therefore, Proposition
4
gives a sufficient condition for the operator
\mathrm{\Delta}_{f}=\mathrm{\Delta}-\langle Df,D\;\cdot\;\rangle
to be parabolic on
(M_{0},\langle,\rangle)
. For more elaborated results on parabolicity of elliptic operators, we refer the interested reader to Chapter 4 of
[
4
]
.

A different way of ensuring that an upper bounded function
u\in C^{1}(M_{0})
satisfying
\mathrm{\Delta}_{f}u\geq 0
is constant is obtained by replacing assumption (
1.9
) in Theorem
7
by

(5.6)

\int_{R}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{\int_{\partial B^{0}_{r}}u^{p}e^{-f}}=+\infty,

provided that
u
is non-negative and
p>1
. The case
p=1
requires more care and an extra assumption on the behaviour of
u
(see for instance Theorem C of
[
26
]
). Note that
u\in L^{p}(M_{0},e^{-f})
implies (
5.6
). We can also modify assumption (
1.10
) in Theorem
8
in an analogous way to obtain our non-parametric uniqueness result.

In this last section of the paper we prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem

on

on

be given. We will prove that

The proof is divided into five steps.

Step 1

which will be used in the following steps, then we obtain the existence of a point

contained in the ball and satisfying a suitable inequality, namely (
6.5
) below. Denote by

the positive part of a real valued function (that is, set

for each

in the domain of

). Let
R>2u(o)
be given. On the geodesic ball
\widehat{B}_{R}=B^{\hat{\sigma}}_{R}(o)
consider the functions

(6.1)

\varphi:=\left(1-\frac{\widehat{\gamma}^{2}}{R^{2}}-Cu\right)_{+}

and

\eta:=e^{K\varphi}-1,

where

and where the constants

,

are to be chosen later. Note that

has compact support in

has the same support of

. Moreover, both functions do not vanish in a neighbourhood of

For the sake of brevity, we set

on

. Note that

on

. By the previous remarks we have that the function

&=\left(\frac{e^{K}-1}{e^{K}-e^{KCu(o)}}\right)^{m-1}e^{(m-1)KCu(o)}z(x_{0}).\end{split}

), that holds true for any

Lz=z^{3}\cdot(a^{ij}a^{kt}u_{ik}u_{jt}+u^{i}u^{j}R_{ij}).

Next,

(6.54)

\mathrm{d}\zeta=z^{\alpha}\left(\mathrm{d}\eta+\alpha\frac{\eta}{z}\mathrm{d}z\right)

and having assumed
d\zeta=0
at
x_{0}
, from (
6.54
) we deduce

(6.55)

\mathrm{d}\eta=-\alpha\frac{\eta}{z}\mathrm{d}z

at
x_{0}
. Since

\widehat{\mathrm{Hess}}(\zeta)=z^{\alpha}\widehat{\mathrm{Hess}}(\eta)+\mathrm{d}z^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{d}\eta+\mathrm{d}\eta\otimes\mathrm{d}z^{\alpha}+\eta\widehat{\mathrm{Hess}}(z^{\alpha}),

from (
6.55
) and
dz^{\alpha}=\alpha z^{\alpha-1}dz
we obtain

(6.56)

\widehat{\mathrm{Hess}}(\zeta)=z^{\alpha}\widehat{\mathrm{Hess}}(\eta)-2\alpha^{2}\eta z^{\alpha-2}\mathrm{d}z\otimes\mathrm{d}z+\eta\widehat{\mathrm{Hess}}(z^{\alpha}).

On the other hand,

\widehat{\mathrm{Hess}}(z^{\alpha})=\alpha z^{\alpha-1}\widehat{\mathrm{Hess}}(z)+\alpha(\alpha-1)z^{\alpha-2}\mathrm{d}z\otimes\mathrm{d}z

and inserting into (
6.56
) we obtain

(6.57)

\widehat{\mathrm{Hess}}(\zeta)=z^{\alpha}\cdot\left(\widehat{\mathrm{Hess}}(\eta)+\frac{\alpha\eta}{z}\widehat{\mathrm{Hess}}(z)-\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)\eta}{z^{2}}\mathrm{d}z\otimes\mathrm{d}z\right).

By the definition of
L
we get

(6.58)

L\zeta=z^{\alpha}\cdot\left[L\eta+\alpha\eta\left(\frac{Lz}{z}-\frac{\alpha+1}{z^{2}}a^{ij}z_{i}z_{j}\right)\right].

From (
6.45
) it follows that

(6.59)

a^{ij}z_{i}z_{j}=z^{6}a^{ij}u^{k}u^{t}u_{ik}u_{jt}.

Putting (
6.59
) and (
6.53
) into (
6.58
) we get

(6.60)

L\zeta=z^{\alpha}\cdot[L\eta+\alpha\eta(z^{2}a^{ij}a^{kt}u_{ik}u_{jt}-(\alpha+1)z^{4}a^{ij}u^{k}u^{t}u_{ik}u_{jt}+z^{2}u^{i}u^{j}R_{ij})]

Since
\alpha
,
\eta
and
z
are nonnegative, the claim of the Lemma will follow by showing that

a^{ij}a^{kt}u_{ik}u_{jt}-(\alpha+1)z^{2}a^{ij}u^{k}u^{t}u_{ik}u_{jt}

is nonnegative.

We define the tensor field
B=B^{i}_{j}\theta^{j}\otimes e_{i}
by setting

(6.61)

B^{i}_{j}:=a^{ik}u_{kj}

for
1\leq i,j\leq m
. We also define the tensor field
a_{ij}\theta^{i}\otimes\theta^{j}
by setting

(6.62)

a_{ij}:=\delta_{ij}-u_{i}u_{j}

for
1\leq i,j\leq m
. Observe that
a_{ij}\theta^{i}\otimes\theta^{j}=\psi^{\ast}\overline{g}
on
\Omega
, hence it is a Riemannian metric on
\Omega
and
(a_{ij})
is the inverse of the matrix
(a^{ij})
, that is,

(6.63)

a^{ij}a_{jk}=\delta^{i}_{k}.

Using (
6.61
) and (
6.63
), we have

(6.64)

\begin{split}a^{ij}a^{kt}u_{ik}u_{jt}-(\alpha+1)&z^{2}a^{ij}u^{k}u^{t}u_{ik}u_{jt}=\\
&=B^{j}_{k}B^{k}_{j}-(\alpha+1)z^{2}B^{j}_{k}u^{k}\delta^{l}_{j}u_{lt}u^{t}\\
&=B^{i}_{j}B^{j}_{i}-(\alpha+1)z^{2}B^{j}_{k}u^{k}a_{ij}a^{il}u_{lt}u^{t}\\
&=B^{i}_{j}B^{j}_{i}-(\alpha+1)z^{2}a_{ij}B^{j}_{k}u^{k}B^{i}_{t}u^{t}.\end{split}

The tensor field
B
induces, at each point
x\in\Omega
, a linear operator

\displaystyle B(x):T_{x}M

\displaystyle\to T_{x}M

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\mapsto B^{i}_{j}X^{j}e_{i}=:B(x)X\qquad\forall X=X^{j}e_{j}\in T_{x}M

which is self-adjoint with respect to the scalar product
a_{ij}(x)\theta^{i}|_{x}\otimes\theta^{j}|_{x}
. Indeed, recalling (
6.61
), (
6.63
) we have

\displaystyle(a_{ij}\theta^{i}\otimes\theta^{j})(BX,Y)

\displaystyle=a_{ij}B^{i}_{t}X^{t}Y^{j}=a_{ij}a^{ik}u_{kt}X^{t}Y^{j}

\displaystyle=a^{ki}a_{ij}u_{kt}X^{t}Y^{j}=\delta^{k}_{j}u_{kt}X^{t}Y^{j}=\widehat{\mathrm{Hess}}(u)(X,Y)

for each
X=X^{i}e_{i},Y=Y^{i}e_{i}\in T_{x}M
. We let
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}
be the eigenvalues of
B
.

We are now ready to show that

B^{i}_{j}B^{j}_{i}-(\alpha+1)z^{2}a_{ij}B^{j}_{k}u^{k}B^{i}_{t}u^{t}

is nonnegative and by (
6.64
) this will conclude the proof. Without loss of generality, we suppose that
(\lambda_{1})^{2}\geq(\lambda_{i})^{2}
for
2\leq i\leq m
. Let
B^{2}=B\circ B
. We have

(6.65)

B^{i}_{j}B^{j}_{i}=\mathrm{trace}(B^{2})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\lambda_{i})^{2}.

On the other hand, we have

a_{ij}B^{j}_{k}u^{k}B^{i}_{t}u^{t}=(a_{ij}\theta^{i}\otimes\theta^{j})(BDu,BDu)=(a_{ij}\theta^{i}\otimes\theta^{j})(B^{2}Du,Du).

Since
\{(\lambda_{i})^{2}\}_{1\leq i\leq m}
are the eigenvalues of
B^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle(a_{ij}\theta^{i}\otimes\theta^{j})(B^{2}Du,Du)

\displaystyle\leq(\lambda_{1})^{2}(a_{ij}\theta^{i}\otimes\theta^{j})(Du,Du)

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1})^{2}(u^{i}u_{i}-u_{i}u_{j}u^{i}u^{j})=(\lambda_{1})^{2}|Du|^{2}(1-|Du|^{2})

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1})^{2}\frac{|Du|^{2}}{z^{2}}

and therefore, using the fact that
|Du|<1
,

(6.66)

-(\alpha+1)z^{2}a_{ij}B^{j}_{k}u^{k}B^{i}_{t}u^{t}\geq-(\alpha+1)(\lambda_{1})^{2}|Du|^{2}\geq-(\alpha+1)(\lambda_{1})^{2}.

Now, recalling that
Lu=0
on
\Omega
we have

0=a^{ij}u_{ij}=\delta^{k}_{i}a^{ij}u_{jk}=\delta^{k}_{i}B^{i}_{k}=\mathrm{trace}(B)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}.

The triangular inequality then implies

|\lambda_{1}|=\left|0-\sum_{i=2}^{m}\lambda_{i}\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\lambda_{i}|

and from Newton’s inequality we get

(6.67)

(\lambda_{1})^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{i=2}^{m}|\lambda_{i}|\right)^{2}\leq(m-1)\sum_{i=2}^{m}(\lambda_{i})^{2}.

Recalling that by assumption
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{m-1}
, using (
6.65
), (
6.66
) and (
6.67
) we finally obtain

\displaystyle B^{i}_{j}B^{j}_{i}-(\alpha+1)z^{2}a_{ij}B^{j}_{k}u^{k}B^{i}_{t}u^{t}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\lambda_{i})^{2}-(\alpha+1)(\lambda_{1})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=2}^{m}(\lambda_{i})^{2}-\alpha(\lambda_{1})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=2}^{m}(\lambda_{i})^{2}-\frac{1}{m-1}(\lambda_{1})^{2}\geq 0

and the Lemma is proved.
∎

Korevaar-Schoen spaces on Sierpiński carpets
Meng Yang

Abstract
We prove that certain L p L^{p} -regularity functional inequality holds on generalized Sierpiński carpets . This gives an affirmative answer to an open question raised by Fabrice Baudoin. Our technique originates from an old idea of Alf Jonsson in 1996.

Introduction

As generalizations of the standard Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
p
-energy
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla f(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}x
, some developments were recently given about
p
-energy on fractal spaces and metric measure spaces, in particular, the spaces where upper gradients are not available. Certain
L^{p}
-regularity functional inequalities, such as the condition
\mathcal{P}(p,\delta)
which is given as follows, play an important role.

Let
(K,d,\mu)
be a doubling metric measure space. For
p\geq 1
,
\delta>0
,
f\in L^{p}(K;\mu)
,
r>0
, define

E_{p,\delta}(f,r)=\frac{1}{r^{p\delta}}\int_{K}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{B(x,r)}|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\right)\mathrm{d}\mu(x),

where
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{A}=\frac{1}{\mu(A)}\int_{A}
for a Borel measurable set
A
with
\mu(A)\in(0,+\infty)
. The Korevaar-Schoen space
\mathcal{KS}^{p,\delta}(K)
is defined as

\mathcal{KS}^{p,\delta}(K)=\left\{f\in L^{p}(K;\mu):\varlimsup_{r\downarrow 0}E_{p,\delta}(f,r)<+\infty\right\}.

We say that the condition
\mathcal{P}(p,\delta)
holds if there exists some positive constant
C
such that for any
f\in\mathcal{KS}^{p,\delta}(K)

\sup_{r>0}E_{p,\delta}(f,r)\leq C\varliminf_{r\downarrow 0}E_{p,\delta}(f,r).

We call the type of the condition, “
\sup\ldots\lesssim\varliminf\ldots
”, that the
global
supremum term can be bounded by the
local
limit infimum term, the
weak monotonicity type condition
(following
[
11
]
). The condition
\mathcal{P}(p,\delta)
was introduced in
[
5
, Definition 4.5]
. Another weak monotonicity type condition, denoted as
(P_{p,\theta})
, which involves the heat kernel and was introduced in
[
1
, Definition 6.7]
, is as follows. There exists some positive constant
C
such that for any
f
in the Besov type space
\mathbf{B}^{p,\theta}(K)

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\frac{1}{t^{p\theta}}\int_{K}\int_{K}|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}p_{t}(x,y)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\varliminf_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{t^{p\theta}}\int_{K}\int_{K}|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}p_{t}(x,y)\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

If the heat kernel satisfies two-sided Gaussian or sub-Gaussian estimate with walk dimension
d_{w}
, then it was proved in
[
11
, Theorem 1.7]
that
\mathcal{P}(p,\delta)
is equivalent to
(P_{p,\delta/{d_{w}}})
(
(NE)
and
(KE)
in their notation).

The weak monotonicity type conditions come naturally in the critical exponent phenomenon of Korevaar-Schoen spaces and Besov spaces, see
[
5
, Lemma 4.7]
.

The weak monotonicity type conditions have many significant applications. First, they can give fruitful Sobolev inequalities, see
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
with
(P_{p,\theta})
and
[
5
, Subsection 4.3]
with
\mathcal{P}(p,\delta)
. Second, they can give generalizations of the well-known Bourgain-Brezis-Mironescu (BBM) convergence, see
[
11
, Theorem 1.3]
with
(P_{p,\theta})
. Third, for
p=2
, they are related to the construction of local regular Dirichlet forms, see
[
22
, Assumptions 1 (A
2^{*}
)]
. Fourth, for
p=1
, they are related to BV functions, see
[
2
, Remark 4.5]
, which is
\mathcal{P}(1,1)
, for the Gaussian case, and
[
3
, Theorem 4.9]
, which is
\mathcal{P}(1,d_{W}-\kappa)
, for the sub-Gaussian case.

In this section, we introduce basic definitions to state our main result.

Let
(K,d)
be a metric space. Denote
B(x,r)=\{y\in K:d(x,y)<r\}
for
x\in K
,

\alpha\in(0,+\infty)

\mu
on

\alpha

such that

\frac{1}{C_{\mathrm{AR}}}r^{\alpha}\leq\mu(B(x,r))\leq C_{\mathrm{AR}}r^{\alpha}

for any

, where
\mathrm{diam}(K,d)=\sup\{d(x,y):x,y\in K\}
is the diameter of the metric space
(K,d)
. If such a Borel measure
\mu
exists, then by the well-known Frostman lemma (see
[
24
, Page 112]
and
[
10

), the Hausdorff dimension
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{H}}(K,d)
of the metric space
(K,d)
is
\alpha
, and we say that the metric
d
or the metric space
(K,d)
is (
\alpha
-)Ahlfors regular.

Ahlfors regular conformal dimension was introduced implicitly by Bourdon and Pajot
[

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(K_{1},d_{1})
,
(K_{2},d_{2})
be two metric spaces. We say that
(K_{1},d_{1})
is quasisymmetric to
(K_{2},d_{2})
if there exist a homeomorphism
f:K_{1}\to K_{2}
and a homeomorphism
\eta:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
such that

\frac{d_{2}(f(x),f(a))}{d_{2}(f(x),f(b))}\leq\eta\left(\frac{d_{1}(x,a)}{d_{1}(x,b)}\right)

for any
x,a,b\in K_{1}
with
x\neq b
. It is easy to verify that quasisymmetry is an equivalence relation. Let
(K,d)
be a metric space. Define the conformal gauge
\mathcal{G}(K,d)
as the set of all metric spaces
(K_{0},d_{0})
which are quasisymmetric to
(K,d)
. The Ahlfors regular conformal dimension
\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{ARC}}(K,d)
is defined as

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{ARC}}(K,d)=\inf\left\{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{H}}(K_{0},d_{0}):(K_{0},d_{0})\in\mathcal{G}(K,d)\text{ is Ahlfors regular}\right\}.

.

(1)

\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{T}}(K,d)=\inf\left\{\mathrm{dim}_{\mathrm{H}}(K_{0},d_{0}):K_{0}\text{ is homeomorphic to }K\right\}\in\{-1,0,1,\ldots\}.

and dividing by
\mu(K_{w})
,
\mu(K_{v})
, by the choice Equation (
2
) of the constant
c
, we have

\displaystyle|M_{n}f(w)-M_{n}f(v)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{K_{w}}|f-M_{n}f(w)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{K_{w}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{K_{v}}|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{K_{v}}|f-M_{n}f(v)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu\right)

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}\left(a^{\alpha n}\int_{K_{w}}|f-M_{n}f(w)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu+a^{2\alpha n}\int_{K_{w}}\int_{B(x,ca^{-n})}|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+a^{\alpha n}\int_{K_{v}}|f-M_{n}f(v)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu\right).

Taking summation with respect to
(w,v)\in E_{n}
, by the choice Equation (
1
) of the constant
L_{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{(w,v)\in E_{n}}|M_{n}f(w)-M_{n}f(v)|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}\left(2a^{\alpha n}\sum_{w\in W_{n}}\int_{K_{w}}|f-M_{n}f(w)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+L_{*}a^{2\alpha n}\int_{K}\int_{B(x,ca^{-n})}|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(y)\mathrm{d}\mu(x)\right)

\displaystyle=3^{p-1}\left(2a^{\alpha n}\sum_{w\in W_{n}}\int_{K_{w}}|f-M_{n}f(w)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu+L_{*}a^{2\alpha n}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}A_{n+j}(f)\right).

By Proposition
3.6
, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{n}}(M_{n}f)

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}\left(2a^{\beta_{p}n}\sum_{w\in W_{n}}\int_{K_{w}}|f-M_{n}f(w)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu+L_{*}a^{(\alpha+\beta_{p})n}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}A_{n+j}(f)\right)

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}\left(2C_{\mathrm{P2}}a^{(\alpha+\beta_{p})n}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{2\alpha}\right)^{j}A_{n+j}(f)+L_{*}a^{(\alpha+\beta_{p})n}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}A_{n+j}(f)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(3^{p-1}(2C_{\mathrm{P2}}+L_{*})\right)a^{(\alpha+\beta_{p})n}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{2\alpha}\right)^{j}A_{n+j}(f).

∎

With the help of
A_{n}
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{n}}(M_{n}\cdot)
can be “bounded” by
A^{(n)}_{p,\beta_{p}}
in the following result.

Proposition 3.8
.

There exists some positive constant
C_{\mathrm{P4}}
such that

\varliminf_{n\to+\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{n}}(M_{n}f)\leq C_{\mathrm{P4}}\varliminf_{n\to+\infty}A^{(n)}_{p,\beta_{p}}(f)

for any
f\in\mathcal{F}_{p}
.

Proof.

Let
J\geq 1
be an integer to be determined. For any
n\geq 1
, by Proposition
3.7
, we have

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{n}}(M_{n}f)\leq C_{\mathrm{P3}}a^{(\alpha+\beta_{p})n}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{J}+\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\right)\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{2\alpha}\right)^{j}A_{n+j}(f).

Recall that

A^{(n)}_{p,\beta_{p}}(f)=a^{(\alpha+\beta_{p})n}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}A_{n+j}(f)\geq a^{(\alpha+\beta_{p})n}A_{n}(f).

Hence

\displaystyle a^{(\alpha+\beta_{p})n}\sum_{j=0}^{J}\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{2\alpha}\right)^{j}A_{n+j}(f)\leq a^{(\alpha+\beta_{p})n}\sum_{j=0}^{J}\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{2\alpha}\right)^{J}A_{n+j}(f)

\displaystyle\leq\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{2\alpha}\right)^{J}a^{(\alpha+\beta_{p})n}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}A_{n+j}(f)=\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{2\alpha}\right)^{J}A^{(n)}_{p,\beta_{p}}(f),

and

\displaystyle a^{(\alpha+\beta_{p})n}\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{2\alpha}\right)^{j}A_{n+j}(f)

\displaystyle\leq a^{(\alpha+\beta_{p})n}\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{2\alpha}\right)^{j}a^{-(\alpha+\beta_{p})(n+j)}A^{(n+j)}_{p,\beta_{p}}(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{-(\beta_{p}-\alpha)}\right)^{j}A^{(n+j)}_{p,\beta_{p}}(f).

By the choice Equation (
4
) of the integer
k\geq 1
, we have
2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{-(\beta_{p}-\alpha)}\in(0,1)
. By Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
3.5
, we have

A^{(n+j)}_{p,\beta_{p}}(f)\leq C_{\mathrm{P1}}\sup_{l\geq n+j}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{l}}(M_{l}f)\leq C_{\mathrm{P1}}C_{\mathrm{WM}}\varliminf_{n\to+\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{n}}(M_{n}f).

Hence

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{n}}(M_{n}f)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{P3}}\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{2\alpha}\right)^{J}A^{(n)}_{p,\beta_{p}}(f)+C_{\mathrm{P3}}\sum_{j=J+1}^{\infty}\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{-(\beta_{p}-\alpha)}\right)^{j}C_{\mathrm{P1}}C_{\mathrm{WM}}\varliminf_{n\to+\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{n}}(M_{n}f)

\displaystyle=C_{\mathrm{P3}}\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{2\alpha}\right)^{J}A^{(n)}_{p,\beta_{p}}(f)+\left(C_{\mathrm{P1}}C_{\mathrm{P3}}C_{\mathrm{WM}}\frac{\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{-(\beta_{p}-\alpha)}\right)^{J+1}}{1-2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{-(\beta_{p}-\alpha)}}\right)\varliminf_{n\to+\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{n}}(M_{n}f).

(5)

Firstly, since
2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{-(\beta_{p}-\alpha)}\in(0,1)
, choosing
J\geq 1
sufficiently large, we have

C_{\mathrm{P1}}C_{\mathrm{P3}}C_{\mathrm{WM}}\frac{\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{-(\beta_{p}-\alpha)}\right)^{J+1}}{1-2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{-(\beta_{p}-\alpha)}}\leq\frac{1}{2},

then

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{n}}(M_{n}f)\leq C_{\mathrm{P3}}\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{2\alpha}\right)^{J}A^{(n)}_{p,\beta_{p}}(f)+\frac{1}{2}\varliminf_{n\to+\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{n}}(M_{n}f).

Secondly, letting
n\to+\infty
, we have

\varliminf_{n\to+\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{n}}(M_{n}f)\leq C_{\mathrm{P3}}\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{2\alpha}\right)^{J}\varliminf_{n\to+\infty}A^{(n)}_{p,\beta_{p}}(f)+\frac{1}{2}\varliminf_{n\to+\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{n}}(M_{n}f),

hence

\varliminf_{n\to+\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{n}}(M_{n}f)\leq\left(2C_{\mathrm{P3}}\left(2^{\frac{p-1}{k}}a^{2\alpha}\right)^{J}\right)\varliminf_{n\to+\infty}A^{(n)}_{p,\beta_{p}}(f).

∎

Remark 3.9
.

By Equation (
5
), we can indeed obtain that for any

C(\varepsilon)

\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{n}}(M_{n}f)\leq C(\varepsilon)A^{(n)}_{p,\beta_{p}}(f)+\varepsilon\varliminf_{n\to+\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{n}}(M_{n}f)

for any
n\geq 1
,

.

Now we give the proof of Theorem
2.8
as follows.

Proof of Theorem
2.8
.

For any
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle A^{(n)}_{p,\beta_{p}}(f)\leq C_{\mathrm{P1}}\sup_{l\geq n}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{l}}(M_{l}f)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{P1}}C_{\mathrm{WM}}\varliminf_{n\to+\infty}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{p}^{G_{n}}(M_{n}f)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{P1}}C_{\mathrm{WM}}C_{\mathrm{P4}}\varliminf_{n\to+\infty}A^{(n)}_{p,\beta_{p}}(f),

where we use Proposition

3.1

3.8

\sup_{n\geq 1}A^{(n)}_{p,\beta_{p}}(f)\leq\left(C_{\mathrm{P1}}C_{\mathrm{P4}}C_{\mathrm{WM}}\right)\varliminf_{n\to+\infty}A^{(n)}_{p,\beta_{p}}(f).

∎

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a general class of weighted spaces of holomorphic Dirichlet series (with real frequencies) analytic in some half-plane and study composition operators on these spaces. In the particular case when the symbol inducing the composition operator is an affine function, we give criteria for boundedness and compactness. We also study the cyclicity property and as a byproduct give a sufficient condition so that the direct sum of the identity plus a weighted forward shift operator on the Hardy space H 2 H^{2} is cyclic.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Dirichlet series

Let
\Lambda=(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a strictly increasing sequence of non-negative real numbers tending to
\infty
.
A
Dirichlet series of type
\Lambda
(Dirichlet series in short) is a series of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\,,

where
z
is a complex variable, and the coefficients of the series are given by a sequence
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of complex numbers.

If
\lambda_{n}=n
,
n\geq 1
, then (
1.1
) are power series in
\zeta=e^{-z}
. When
\lambda_{n}=\log{n}
,
n\geq 1
, we get the so-called classical Dirichlet series, which have many applications in analytic number theory (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
). Classical Dirichlet series also relate to several problems in functional analysis (see, e.g.,
[
15
]
and references therein). We refer the reader to the monograph
[
14
]
or
[
26
]
for more detailed information on Dirichlet series.

The properties of convergence of Dirichlet series depend on two specific quantities that we introduce now. Fix
\Lambda=(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
a strictly increasing sequence of non-negative real numbers tending to
\infty
, and define

(1.2)

L=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log n}{\lambda_{n}}.

Now to each Dirichlet series
f
of type
\Lambda
given by (
1.1
) we may associate the following quantity

(1.3)

D_{f}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log|a_{n}|}{\lambda_{n}}.

Note that the quantity
D_{f}
is specific to each Dirichlet series of type
\Lambda
with coefficients
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. Nevertheless, most of the time there is no confusion and we omit the reference to the associated Dirichlet series and write generally
D
.

It is known that if a Dirichlet series converges at some
w\in\mathbb{C}
, it converges for all
z
with
\Re(z)>\Re(w)
, and more precisely, for every fixed non-negative real number
M
, it converges uniformly in the region
\{\Re(z)\geq\Re(w),\,|z-w|\leq M(\Re(z)-\Re(w))\}
, see
[
26
, page 5]
. Let us denote by
\sigma_{c}
the abscissa of convergence of a Dirichlet series, which is defined as

\sigma_{c}=\inf\left\{r\in\mathbb{R}:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\ \text{converges on}\ \mathbb{C}_{r}\right\},

where
\mathbb{C}_{r}:=\{z:\Re(z)>r\}
. We also need the following extension of the definition of
\mathbb{C}_{r}
for
r=\pm\infty
. By convention
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}=\emptyset
and
\mathbb{C}_{-\infty}=\mathbb{C}
.

The abscissa
\sigma_{u}
of uniform convergence and
\sigma_{a}
of absolute convergence are defined in a similar way. So a Dirichlet series converges (resp. uniformly, absolutely) in the right half-plane
\mathbb{C}_{\sigma_{c}}
(resp.
\mathbb{C}_{\sigma_{u}}
,
\mathbb{C}_{\sigma_{a}}
) to a holomorphic function.

In the case
L<\infty
the three abscissae are related by the Valiron formula (see, e.g.,
[
28
]
)

(1.4)

D\leq\sigma_{c}\leq\sigma_{u}\leq\sigma_{a}\leq D+L.

In particular, the Dirichlet series (
1.1
) represents an entire function if and only if
D=-\infty
.

1.2.
Composition operators

Let
X
be a functional Banach space and suppose that all
f\in X
have a common domain
G\subseteq\mathbb{C}
. Let
\varphi
be an analytic self-map on
G
. Then
\varphi
induces the
composition operator

C_{\varphi}
on
X
defined by

\displaystyle C_{\varphi}(f)=f\circ\varphi,\qquad\forall f\in X.

The first natural and non trivial question is to know if
C_{\varphi}
maps
X
into itself, and if this is the case, what can be said about standard spectral properties of
C_{\varphi}
as an operator on
X
. There is a rich literature on this topic when
X
is the Hardy space, the Bergman space or the Dirichlet space (see for instance
[
4
,
25
]
). The situation of spaces of classical Dirichlet series received also much attention after the founding papers of Gordon–Hedenlmann
[
12
]
and Bayart
[
3
]
(see for instance
[
2
,
22
,
23
]
). The case of general Dirichlet series is less studied. Nevertheless, when
X
is some weighted space of
entire
Dirichlet series of type
\Lambda
satisfying a certain property, properties of composition operators acting on
X
are quite well understood (see
[
9
,
18
,
19
]
). In
[
8
]
, using Liouville’s theorem, it is proved that if
C_{\varphi}
defines a bounded composition operator on a weighted Hilbert space of entire Dirichlet series, then
\varphi
must be an affine function.

The aim of this paper is to focus on the situation when our space of Dirichlet series is formed by functions which are holomorphic in
some half-plane
but not necessarily the whole plane. In this context, we could not apply Liouville’s theorem. Nevertheless, we will still focus on the case when
\varphi
is a polynomial (and then necessarily
\varphi
must be affine), since, for general symbols, we could not really hope that
C_{\varphi}
maps a space of Dirichlet series into itself because the restrictions on the sequence
\Lambda
generating the space will be too severe.

When
\varphi(z)=az+b
, we will characterize boundedness (see Section 3) and compactness (see Section 4) of
C_{\varphi}
on a weighted Dirichlet space
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
(see next section for the definition). Then we will also study the dynamics properties (cyclicity and supercyclicity) in Section 5 of
C_{\varphi}
on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. In Section 6 we give some results about complex symmetry. In the final section we end with a note of how our results relate for weighted Hilbert spaces of entire Dirichlet series.

2.
Weighted spaces of holomorphic Dirichlet series

Fix
\Lambda=(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
a strictly increasing sequence of non-negative real numbers tending to
\infty
and satisfying
L<\infty
. To perform the main object of our investigation, we need the following result from
[
19
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

L<\infty
if and only if
\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-r\lambda_{n}}<\infty
for all
r>L
. Furthermore,
\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-r\lambda_{n}}=\infty
for any
r<L
.

Let
\beta=(\beta_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive real numbers. The sequence space

\ell_{\beta}^{2}=\left\{(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{C}:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\beta_{n}^{2}<\infty\right\}

is a Hilbert space with inner product defined for
a=(a_{n})_{n\geq 1},b=(b_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\ell_{\beta}^{2}
by

(2.1)

\langle a,b\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\overline{b}_{n}\beta_{n}^{2}.

We put

(2.2)

\beta_{*}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\beta_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}.

The following result serves as an important motivation of our investigation.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose
\beta_{*}\neq-\infty
. If
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}
is a Dirichlet series of type
\Lambda
whose coefficients
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\ell_{\beta}^{2}
, then
f
converges uniformly on every compact subset of
\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}
. In particular,
f
is holomorphic in
\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
. Fix a compact subset
K
of
\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}
. Then, there exists
\eta>\frac{L}{2}-\beta^{*}
such that for every
z\in K
, we have
\Re(z)\geq\eta
. Thus

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sup_{z\in K}\left|a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\right|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|e^{-\lambda_{n}\eta}.

Apply the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to get

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sup_{z\in K}\left|a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\right|\leq\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\beta_{n}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-2\lambda_{n}\eta}}{\beta_{n}^{2}}\right)^{1/2}.

Since
\eta>\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}
, we can take
0<\varepsilon<\delta:=\eta-\left(\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}\right)
. By definition of
\beta_{*}
, there exists
N
such that for all
n\geq N
,

\frac{1}{\beta_{n}^{2}}\leq e^{-2\lambda_{n}\left(\beta_{*}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)}.

Applying Lemma
2.1
with
r=L+\delta
, we obtain

\sum_{n\geq N}\frac{1}{\beta_{n}^{2}}e^{-2\lambda_{n}\eta}\leq\sum_{n\geq N}e^{-2\lambda_{n}\left(\beta_{*}-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)}e^{-2\lambda_{n}\eta}\leq\sum_{n\geq N}e^{-\lambda_{n}(L+\delta)}<\infty,

which proves that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sup_{z\in K}\left|a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\right|<\infty.

Thus,
f
converges uniformly on
K
. Since this is valid for every compact
K
in
\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}
, we deduce that
f
is analytic on
\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}
.

The case when
\beta_{*}=\infty
follows along the same lines with some tiny adjustments left to the reader.
∎

Proposition
2.2
leads us to the following definition of weighted space of holomorphic Dirichlet series in
\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}
:

\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)=\left\{f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}:(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\ell_{\beta}^{2}\right\}.

This is a Hilbert space with inner product inherited from (
2.1
). More precisely, the inner product on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
is defined as

(2.3)

\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\overline{b}_{n}\beta_{n}^{2},

for every
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z},g(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Note that the inner product (
2.3
) is well defined because of the uniqueness property on coefficients for Dirichlet series of type
\Lambda
, namely if
\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}=0
, then
a_{n}=0
for all
n\geq 1
, see
[
26
, page 8]
.

Remark 2.3
.

The assumption
\beta_{*}>-\infty
in Proposition
2.2
is important, otherwise
\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}=\emptyset
. It is also essential because we can prove that if
\beta_{*}=-\infty
then for every
z_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
there exists a Dirichlet series of type

\Lambda
,
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}
with
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\ell_{\beta}^{2}
, which does not converge at
z_{0}
. Indeed, there exists
(n_{p})_{p\geq 1}\uparrow\infty
large enough, such that

\beta_{n_{p}}^{2}<e^{-2\lambda_{n_{p}}\Re(z_{0})},\ \text{for all $p\geq 1$}.

Take
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
as follows

a_{n}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{p}e^{\lambda_{n_{p}}z_{0}},&\text{$n=n_{p}\ (p=1,2,\ldots)$,}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\beta_{n}^{2}\leq\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{2}}e^{2\lambda_{n_{p}}\Re(z_{0})}e^{-2\lambda_{n_{p}}\Re(z_{0})}=\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{2}}<\infty.

However, at
z_{0}
we have

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z_{0}}=\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p}e^{\lambda_{n_{p}}z_{0}}e^{-\lambda_{n_{p}}z_{0}}=\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p}=\infty.

Note that for the case
\beta_{*}=\infty
, we have entire Dirichlet series which have been studied quite well (see, e.g.,
[
8
]
and related references).
Therefore, in the sequel we assume that the condition
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
always holds.

In the rest of the paper, we adopt the following notation. For
n\geq 1
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
,

(2.4)

q_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{\beta_{n}}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}.

Proposition 2.4
.

The sequence
(q_{n})_{n\geq 1}
forms an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
.

Proof.

It is immediate from (
2.3
).
∎

Note that the proof of Proposition
2.2
shows that for every point
w\in\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}
, the evaluation functional
\delta_{w}
is continuous on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Furthermore, using Proposition
2.4
, we can compute the kernel
k_{w}
at point
w\in\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}
by

\displaystyle k_{w}(z)=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle k_{w},q_{n}\rangle q_{n}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{q_{n}(w)}q_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\beta_{n}^{2}}e^{-\lambda_{n}(z+\overline{w})}.

Thus we obtain the following result.

Proposition 2.5
.

The spaces
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
are all reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces with reproducing kernel

(2.5)

K(z,w)=k_{w}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\beta_{n}^{2}}e^{-\lambda_{n}(z+\overline{w})},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}.

In particular, we deduce the norm of the kernel

(2.6)

\|k_{w}\|^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\beta_{n}^{2}}e^{-2\lambda_{n}\Re(w)},\quad w\in\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}.

3.
Bounded composition operators induced by a polynomial

Since
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
is a functional Hilbert space (in which evaluations are continuous), it follows easily from the closed graph theorem that the space
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
is
invariant
under a composition operator
C_{\varphi}
, i.e.
C_{\varphi}\big(\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)\big)\subseteq\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
, if and only if
C_{\varphi}
is bounded on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
.

The study of boundedness is based on the following two simple lemmas. A version of the first one appears in
[
12
]
but for the completeness we give a proof.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f
be holomorphic in some half-plane
C_{\theta}=\{z:\Re(z)>\theta\}
(
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
) and suppose that

(3.1)

\lim_{\Re(z)\to\infty}e^{f(z)}=b,

for some
b\in\mathbb{C^{*}}:=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, there exists a branch of the logarithm
\log_{\alpha}
and there exists
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

(3.2)

\lim_{\Re(z)\to\infty}f(z)=\log_{\alpha}(b)+2i\pi\ell.

Here we denote
\log_{\alpha}(w)=\log|w|+i\arg_{(\alpha,\alpha+2\pi)}(w)
for all
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus L_{\alpha}
, and
L_{\alpha}=\{re^{i\alpha}:r\geq 0\}
.

Proof.

First note that since
b\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, there exists
\alpha\in[0,2\pi)
such that
b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus L_{\alpha}
, and since
\mathbb{C}\setminus L_{\alpha}
is open, there exists
\delta_{1}>0
such that the open disc
D(b,\delta_{1})
is contained in
\mathbb{C}\setminus L_{\alpha}
. Now using (
3.1
), we can find
R_{0}>\theta
such that

\Re(z)>R_{0}\Longrightarrow e^{f(z)}\in D(b,\delta_{1})\subset\mathbb{C}\setminus L_{\alpha}.

In particular,
\log_{\alpha}(e^{f(z)})
is well defined on
\mathbb{C}_{R_{0}}
and the function

z\longmapsto\frac{\log_{\alpha}(e^{f(z)})-f(z)}{2i\pi}

is holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}_{R_{0}}
and its values are contained in
\mathbb{Z}
. The open mapping theorem (for holomorphic functions) implies that there exists
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
such that for all
z\in\mathbb{C}_{R_{0}}
, we have
\log_{\alpha}(e^{f(z)})=f(z)-2i\pi\ell
.

Now let
\varepsilon>0
. Then (by continuity of
\log_{\alpha}
) we can find
\delta_{2}>0
such that

(3.3)

|w-b|<\delta_{2}\Longrightarrow|\log_{\alpha}(w)-\log_{\alpha}(b)|<\varepsilon.

Using (
3.1
) once more, we can find
R_{1}>\theta
such that

\Re(z)>R_{1}\Longrightarrow|e^{f(z)}-b|<\delta_{2},

and by (
3.3
), we get

\Re(z)>R_{1}\Longrightarrow|\log_{\alpha}(e^{f(z)})-\log_{\alpha}(b)|<\varepsilon.

We thus deduce that

\Re(z)>\max(R_{1},R_{0})\Longrightarrow|f(z)-2i\pi\ell-\log_{\alpha}(b)|<\varepsilon,

which gives the result.
∎

The second lemma is similar to one that appears in
[
19
]
for the case of entire Dirichlet series.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}
be a Dirichlet series with
L<\infty
and
D\neq\infty
. If
a_{m}
is the first non-zero coefficient, then

\lim_{\Re(z)\to\infty}e^{\lambda_{m}z}f(z)=a_{m}.

Proof.

Let
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}
be a Dirichlet series where
a_{m}\neq 0
. Then we have

(3.4)

e^{\lambda_{m}z}f(z)=\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}e^{-(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m})z}.

Notice that
\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}\geq 0
for all
n\geq m
, so that the right-hand side of (
3.4
) is a Dirichlet series of type
(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m})_{n\geq m}
. We claim that the associated abscissa of uniform convergence
\sigma_{u}^{\prime}
satisfies
\sigma_{u}^{\prime}\neq\infty
.

We show that the associated
L^{\prime}
and
D^{\prime}
to the Dirichlet series above satisfy
L^{\prime}=L
and
D^{\prime}=D
. Indeed, since
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}}=1
, it follows that

L^{\prime}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log n}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\frac{\log n}{\lambda_{n}}\cdot\frac{\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m}}\right)=L<\infty.

and similarly
D^{\prime}=D
.

Since
L<\infty
and
D\neq\infty
, It follows from (
1.4
) that
\sigma_{u}^{\prime}\neq\infty
, so that
e^{\lambda_{m}z}f(z)
is uniformly convergent in some half-plane. Therefore we may interchange limit and sum to obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\Re(z)\to\infty}e^{\lambda_{m}z}f(z)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\Re(z)\to\infty}\sum_{n\geq m}a_{n}e^{-(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m})z}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq m}\lim_{\Re(z)\to\infty}\left(a_{n}e^{-(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m})z}\right)=a_{m}.

∎

3.1.
Important necessary condition for bounded
C_{\varphi}
with general symbols

In this subsection, we obtain an important necessary condition which a general symbol
\varphi
inducing a bounded composition operator
C_{\varphi}
must satisfy. See
[
8
,
19
]
for analogous results in the entire case. First, note that in the case when
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
, all
f\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
converge on the proper right half-plane
\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}
and in particular, satisfy
D_{f}\neq\infty
(by Valiron’s formulae). Hence Lemma
3.2
can be applied to elements of
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
and
\varphi
be a holomorphic self-map on
\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}
. Suppose that the operator
C_{\varphi}
is bounded on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Then the following condition holds

(3.5)

\lim_{\Re(z)\to\infty}\frac{\varphi(z)}{z}=A,

where
A
is a real constant such that

(1)

if
\lambda_{1}>0
, then
A\geq 1
, while

(2)

if
\lambda_{1}=0
, then either
A=0
or
A\geq 1
.

Moreover, if for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we denote by
m_{k}
the index of the first non-zero term of
C_{\varphi}(e^{-\lambda_{k}z})=e^{-\lambda_{k}\varphi(z)}
in the Dirichlet series representation (
1.1
), then

(3.6)

\lambda_{m_{k}}=A\lambda_{k},

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

- Case 1:
\lambda_{1}>0
.
For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
C_{\varphi}(e^{-\lambda_{k}z})\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
, and hence we can represent it as

C_{\varphi}(e^{-\lambda_{k}z})=e^{-\lambda_{k}\varphi(z)}=\sum_{n\geq m_{k}}b_{n}^{(k)}e^{-\lambda_{n}z},

where, by assumption,
m_{k}
is the smallest integer such that
b_{m_{k}}^{(k)}\neq 0
, and
(b_{n}^{(k)})_{n}\in\ell_{\beta}^{2}
.

By Lemma
3.2
, we have

\lim_{\Re(z)\to\infty}{e^{\lambda_{m_{k}}z}e^{-\lambda_{k}\varphi(z)}}=b_{m_{k}}^{(k)}.

Applying Lemma
3.1
to
f(z)=\lambda_{m_{k}}z-\lambda_{k}\varphi(z)
, there exist a branch of the logarithm
\log_{\alpha}
and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, such that

\lim_{\Re(z)\to\infty}\big(\lambda_{m_{k}}z-\lambda_{k}\varphi(z)\big)=\log_{\alpha}\big(b_{m_{k}}^{(k)}\big)+2i\pi\ell.

Thus, we get

\lim_{\Re(z)\to\infty}\left(\frac{\lambda_{m_{k}}}{\lambda_{k}}-\frac{\varphi(z)}{z}\right)=0,

that is

\lim_{\Re(z)\to\infty}{\frac{\varphi(z)}{z}}=\frac{\lambda_{m_{k}}}{\lambda_{k}}\geq 0.

This shows that
\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{m_{k}}}{\lambda_{k}}
is independent of
k
, and hence we can denote this non-negative constant as
A
.

In particular, for
k=1
, we have
A=\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{m_{1}}}{\lambda_{1}}\geq 1
, because
m_{1}\geq 1
.

- Case 2:
\lambda_{1}=0
.
In this case, replacing
k\in\mathbb{N}
by
k\geq 2
in the proof for Case 1 above, we still have a non-negative constant
A=\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{m_{k}}}{\lambda_{k}}
, for all
k\geq 2
. In particular, for
k=2
, we have

A=\frac{\lambda_{m_{2}}}{\lambda_{2}}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if $m_{2}=1$ (because $\lambda_{1}=0$)},\\
\geq 1,&\text{if $m_{2}\geq 2$ (because $\lambda_{m_{2}}\geq\lambda_{2}$)}.\end{cases}

Finally, in that case, we have
m_{1}=1
and thus
\lambda_{m_{1}}=0=A\lambda_{1}
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

If
A\geq 1
, it follows immediately from (
3.6
) that the map
k\longmapsto m_{k}
is strictly increasing.

3.2.
Necessary conditions for boundedness of
C_{\varphi}
: a polynomial symbol

In case the symbol
\varphi
is a polynomial, we can get a crucial information:
\varphi
must be an affine function.

To study this case, for a composition operator to be well-defined, we first need the following result.

Lemma 3.5
.

An affine function
az+b
(
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
) is a self-map of the half-plane
\mathbb{C}_{\theta}
(
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
) if and only if

(i)

a\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\Re(b)\geq(1-a)\theta
, or

(ii)

a=0
and
\Re(b)>\theta
.

Proof.

Suppose
az+b
is a self-map on
\mathbb{C}_{\theta}
. If
a=0
, then it clear that
\Re(b)>\theta
, because
b\in\mathbb{C}_{\theta}
. So we get (ii).

In case
a\neq 0
, we show that
a\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Assume to the contrary that
a\not\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, which means that
a=|a|e^{i\theta_{1}}
, with
\theta_{1}\neq 0\mod(2\pi)
. For every
z=x+iy
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
\Re(az)=x|a|\cos(\theta_{1})-y|a|\sin(\theta_{1})
.

Case 1:
If
\theta_{1}=\pi\mod(2\pi)
, then
\Re(az)=-x|a|
, and so letting
\Re(z)=x\to\infty
, we get that
\Re(az+b)\to-\infty
, which contradicts that
az+b
is a self-map of
\mathbb{C}_{\theta}
.

Case 2:
If
\theta_{1}\neq\pi\mod(2\pi)
, then
\sin(\theta_{1})\neq 0
. Thus if we fix
x>\theta
and let
\Im(z)=y\to\infty
(if
\sin(\theta_{1})>0
) or
\Im(z)=y\to-\infty
(if
\sin(\theta_{1})<0
), we get that
\Re(az+b)\to-\infty
, which also contradicts the assumption.

Thus we have
a\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, a point
z\in\mathbb{C}_{\theta}
with
\Re(z)=\theta+\varepsilon
satisfies
\Re(az+b)=a\theta+a\varepsilon+\Re(b)>\theta
. This means
\Re(b)>(1-a)\theta-a\varepsilon
. Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain (i).

Conversely, if (ii) holds, i.e.
a=0
and
\Re(b)>\theta
, then we are obviously done. If (i) holds, i.e.
a\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\Re(b)\geq(1-a)\theta
, then for any
z\in\mathbb{C}_{\theta}
, we have
\Re(az+b)=a\Re(z)+\Re(b)>a\theta+\Re(b)\geq a\theta+(1-a)\theta=\theta
, which shows that
az+b\in\mathbb{C}_{\theta}
. Thus
az+b
is a self-map of
\mathbb{C}_{\theta}
.
∎

Now we have the following result.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
and
\varphi
be a self-map polynomial of
\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}
. Suppose that the operator
C_{\varphi}
is bounded on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Then the following assertions hold:

(1)

If
\lambda_{1}>0
, then
\varphi(z)=az+b,\ a\geq 1,\ b\in\mathbb{C}
.

(2)

If
\lambda_{1}=0
, then either
\varphi(z)=b,\ b\in\mathbb{C}
(that is
a=0
), or
\varphi(z)=az+b,\ a\geq 1,\ b\in\mathbb{C}
.

Moreover, in addition,
a
satisfies the condition

(3.7)

\lambda_{m_{k}}=a\lambda_{k},\quad\text{for all $k\in\mathbb{N}$},

where
m_{k}
is the index of the first non-zero term of
C_{az+b}(e^{-\lambda_{k}z})=e^{-\lambda_{k}(az+b)}
in the Dirichlet series representation (
1.1
), and
b
satisfies either of the following conditions:

(3.8)

\Re(b)>\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*},\quad a=0,

(3.9)

\Re(b)\geq(1-a)\Big(\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}\Big),\quad a\geq 1.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.3
, we have
\displaystyle\lim_{\Re(z)\to\infty}\frac{\varphi(z)}{z}
is a (real) constant, which implies that
\deg(\varphi)\leq 1
. So
\varphi(z)=az+b
, where
a
and
b
are complex numbers. Then again by Proposition
3.3
, we get
a\in\mathbb{R}
and moreover, if
\lambda_{1}>0
, then
a\geq 1
, while if
\lambda_{1}=0
, then either
a=0
or
a\geq 1
, and also
a
satisfies condition (
3.7
). Furthermore, (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) follow from Lemma
3.5
with
\theta=\displaystyle\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}
.
∎

From now on, an affine symbol
\varphi
stated in Theorem
3.6
is supposed to be given.

3.3.
Two trivial cases of the affine symbols (
a=0,1
)

In the following, we denote by
\|\cdot\|_{\rm op}
the operator norm.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
and
b\in\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}
. Then
C_{b}
is a bounded composition operator on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
if and only if
\lambda_{1}=0
. Moreover, in that case, we have
\|C_{b}\|_{\rm op}=\beta_{1}\|k_{b}\|
.

Proof.

Suppose that
C_{b}
is a bounded on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. By Theorem
3.6
,
\lambda_{1}
must necessarily be zero.

Conversely, suppose that
\lambda_{1}=0
. It implies that the function
\beta_{1}q_{1}(z)=e^{-\lambda_{1}z}=1
belongs to
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. (Recall that the functions
q_{k}
are given by (
2.4
).) Now, note that for all
f\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
, we have

(C_{b}f)(z)=f(b)=\langle f,k_{b}\rangle\beta_{1}q_{1}(z),

Hence
C_{b}=\beta_{1}q_{1}\otimes k_{b}
is a rank one operator. In particular, it is bounded and
\|C_{b}\|_{\text{op}}=\beta_{1}\|q_{1}\|\|k_{b}\|
. But
\|q_{1}\|=1
, which implies that
\|C_{b}\|_{\text{op}}=\beta_{1}\|k_{b}\|
.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
. Then
C_{z+b}
is a bounded composition operator on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
if and only if
\Re(b)\geq 0
. Moreover, in that case, we have
\|C_{z+b}\|_{\rm op}=e^{-\lambda_{1}\Re(b)}
.

Proof.

Suppose
C_{z+b}
is bounded. Then by (
3.9
), we get immediately that
\Re(b)\geq 0
. Conversely, suppose that
\Re(b)\geq 0
. Note that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
C_{z+b}q_{k}=e^{-\lambda_{k}b}q_{k}
. Thus
C_{z+b}
is a diagonal operator with a sequence of eigenvalues equal to
(e^{-\lambda_{k}b})_{k\geq 1}
. Using now that
\Re(b)\geq 0
, we see that this sequence is decreasing in modulus, and so it is well-known (and easy to see) that
C_{z+b}
is bounded and
\|C_{z+b}\|_{\rm op}=e^{-\lambda_{1}\Re(b)}
.
∎

3.4.
Characterization for boundedness of
C_{az+b}\ (a\neq 0,1)

Throughout this subsection, we always assume that an affine symbol
\varphi(z)=az+b
, with
a>1
and
b\in\mathbb{C}
, is given.

We need some supplementary notation.

Definition 3.9
.

For a given sequence of real frequencies
\Lambda=(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, define the set

\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)=\left\{r\in[1,\infty):\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\ \exists m=m_{n}\geq n,\ r\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{m}\right\}.

This set, by Theorem
3.6
, is precisely the set of all possible values of
a
for which a non-trivial symbol
\varphi(z)=az+b
induces a bounded composition operator
C_{\varphi}
on the space
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
.

Remark 3.10
.

Notice that we always have
1\in\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
. Furthermore, since
(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a strictly increasing sequence, for a given
r\in\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
, to each
n
there corresponds a unique
m_{n}\geq n
, such that
r\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{m_{n}}
.

We put
\mathcal{R}_{1}(\Lambda)=\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)\setminus\{1\}
. Depending on the given sequence
(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, it may happen that
\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)=\{1\}
, i.e.
\mathcal{R}_{1}(\Lambda)=\emptyset
, as well as
\mathcal{R}_{1}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. The following examples are taken from
[
18
]
.

Example 3.11
.

Let
\lambda_{n}=n!
. Then
\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
is the singleton
\{1\}
.

Example 3.12
.

(1) For
\lambda_{n}=\log n
(the classical Dirichlet series) or
\lambda_{n}=n
, every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
belongs to
\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
.

(2) Consider a geometric sequence
(\lambda_{n})
with the ratio
q>1
, given by
\lambda_{1}>0
and
\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{1}q^{n-1}
,
n\geq 1
. In this case any value
q^{\ell}\ (\ell\in\mathbb{N})
belongs to
\mathcal{R}(\Lambda)
.

Now let
a\in\mathcal{R}_{1}(\Lambda)
be given. In principle, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the index
m_{n}\geq n
for which
\lambda_{m_{n}}=a\lambda_{n}
, depends on
a
, i.e.
m_{n}=m_{n}^{(a)}
(note that by Propositions
3.3
, if
\lambda_{1}=0
, then
m_{1}=1
). To simplify the expositions, in what follows, we skip the superscript
(a)
whenever there is no confusion in context.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we also define the quantity

r_{n}(a,x)=r_{n}(\Lambda,\beta,a,x):=e^{-\lambda_{n}x}\frac{\beta_{m_{n}}}{\beta_{n}}.

Now we are able to state and prove the following boundedness criterion for the case
a>1
.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
,
a>1
and
b\in\mathbb{C}
. Then
C_{az+b}
is a bounded composition operator on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
if and only if the following conditions are satisfied

(1)

a\in\mathcal{R}_{1}(\Lambda)
,

(2)

\Re(b)\geq(1-a)\Big(\displaystyle\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}\Big)
,

(3)

the sequence
\Big(r_{n}(a,\Re(b))\Big)_{n\geq 1}
is bounded.

Moreover, in this case,

\|C_{az+b}\|_{{\rm op}}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}r_{n}(a,\Re(b)).

Proof.

Necessity
. Suppose
C_{az+b}
is a bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Then conditions (1) and (2) follow from Theorem
3.6
. It remains to show (3).

There is some constant
M>0
such that
\|C_{az+b}f\|\leq M\|f\|
for all
f\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. In particular, for probe functions
q_{n}(z)=\frac{1}{\beta_{n}}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}
, by (1) we have
C_{az+b}q_{n}=\frac{\beta_{m_{n}}}{\beta_{n}}e^{-\lambda_{n}b}q_{m_{n}}
, and thus

\|C_{az+b}q_{n}\|=e^{-\lambda_{n}\Re(b)}\cdot\frac{\beta_{m_{n}}}{\beta_{n}}\leq M\|q_{n}\|=M,\quad\text{for all $n\in\mathbb{N}$},

which gives (3).

Sufficiency
. Conversely, suppose all three conditions (1) – (3) are satisfied. Note that (2) guarantees, by Lemma
3.5
, that
z\mapsto az+b
is a self-map of
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}
. Also, (3) shows that there exists
M>0
such that
0<r_{n}(a,\Re(b))\leq M
, for all
n
. Let
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Hence, by (1),

\displaystyle\|C_{az+b}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}b}|^{2}\beta_{m_{n}}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\beta_{n}^{2}r_{n}(a,\Re(b))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\beta_{n}^{2}=M^{2}\|f\|^{2},

which shows that
C_{az+b}
is bounded on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
.

Moreover, from proofs of both necessity and sufficiency it follows that
\displaystyle\|C_{az+b}\|_{{\rm op}}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}r_{n}(a,\Re(b))
.
∎

Combining Propositions
3.7
,
3.8
and
3.13
, we obtain a characterization of boundedness for
C_{az+b}
on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
and
\varphi(z)=az+b
(
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
). Consider the following statements.

(1)

\varphi(z)=b
for some
b\in\mathbb{C}
with
\Re(b)>\displaystyle\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}
.

(2)

\varphi(z)=az+b
, where

\begin{cases}a\in\mathcal{R}(\Lambda),\\
\Re(b)\geq(1-a)\Big(\displaystyle\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}\Big),\\
\text{the sequence $\Big(r_{n}(a,\Re(b))\Big)_{n\geq 1}$ is bounded}.\end{cases}

The following are true for a composition operator
C_{\varphi}
acting on the space
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
.

(i)

If
\lambda_{1}=0
, then
C_{\varphi}
is bounded if and only if either (1) or (2) holds.

(ii)

If
\lambda_{1}>0
, then
C_{\varphi}
is bounded if and only if (2) holds.

4.
Essential norm, compactness, Schatten class and compact differences

4.1.
Essential norm and compactness

Compactness of a bounded composition operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
can be investigated in different ways. The first makes use of a compactness criterion which states that a bounded linear operator
T
on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
is compact if and only if for any sequence
(f_{n})
from
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
which is weakly convergent to
0
, the sequence
(Tf_{n})
converges strongly to
0
in
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. The other way is via the essential norm of
T
defined by

\displaystyle\|T\|_{e}=\inf\{\|T-K\|_{\text{op}}:K\ \text{is a compact operator on $\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)$}\}.

Clearly,
T
is compact if and only if
\|T\|_{e}=0
.

For a bounded composition operator
C_{az+b}
, in case
a=0
, by Proposition
3.7
, we have the following simple result.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
. For any
b\in\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}
, a composition operator
C_{b}
is always compact on the space
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
.

Proof.

As noted in Proposition
3.7
,
C_{b}
has a rank one and hence it is compact.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
and let
C_{z+b}
be a bounded composition operator on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Then

\|C_{z+b}\|_{e}=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{if }\Re(b)=0\\
0&\mbox{if }\Re(b)>0.\\
\end{cases}

In particular,
C_{z+b}
is a compact operator on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
if and only if
\Re(b)>0
.

Proof.

Proposition
3.8
shows that
\Re(b)\geq 0
. Furthermore, as already noticed,
C_{z+b}
is a diagonal operator with a sequence of eigenvalues equal to
(e^{–\lambda_{k}b})_{k\geq 1}
. But then it is well-known (see for instance
[
13
, Problem 171]
) that
\|C_{z+b}\|_{e}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}e^{-\lambda_{n}\Re(b)}
, which gives the conclusion.
∎

Proposition
4.2
can also be obtained from the following results for
a\geq 1
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
,
a\geq 1
and
C_{az+b}
be a bounded composition operator on the space
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Then
\displaystyle\|C_{az+b}\|_{e}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}r_{n}(a,\Re(b))
. In particular,
C_{az+b}
is compact if and only if
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}r_{n}(a,\Re(b))=0
.

Proof.

We follow the standard technique (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
).

Note that by Theorem
3.14
, the sequence
\Big(r_{n}(a,\Re(b))\Big)_{n\geq 1}
is bounded.

\bullet

Upper bound
. We use compact (finite rank) operators on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
defined by

K_{N}\colon f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\longmapsto\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\quad(N\in\mathbb{N}).

As the
C_{az+b}K_{N}
are also compact, we have

\|C_{az+b}\|_{e}\leq\inf_{p\geq 1}\|C_{az+b}-C_{az+b}K_{N}\|_{\text{op}}.

Observe that for an arbitrary
\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
we have

\displaystyle\|(C_{az+b}-C_{az+b}K_{N})f\|^{2}=\Big\|\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}(az+b)}\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}e^{-2\lambda_{n}\Re(b)}\beta_{m_{n}}^{2}\leq\sup_{n\geq p+1}\Big(e^{-\lambda_{n}\Re(b)}\frac{\beta_{m_{n}}}{\beta_{n}}\Big)^{2}\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\beta_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|^{2}\sup_{n\geq p+1}r_{n}(a,\Re(b)).

Thus

\|C_{az+b}\|_{e}\leq\sup_{n\geq N+1}r_{n}(a,\Re(b)),\quad\text{for every $N\geq 1$}.

Letting
N\to\infty
, we get

\|C_{az+b}\|_{e}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}r_{n}(a,\Re(b)).

\bullet

Lower bound
. Let
K
be an arbitrary compact operator on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Consider the sequence of probe functions
(q_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, whose norms are all
1
. Since it converges weakly to
0
(because it is an orthonormal basis),
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|Kq_{n}\|=0
. Hence

\|C_{az+b}-K\|_{\text{op}}\geq\|(C_{az+b}-K)q_{n}\|\geq\|C_{az+b}q_{n}\|-\|Kq_{n}\|\quad\text{($n\geq 1$)},

from which it follows that

\displaystyle\|C_{az+b}-K\|_{\text{op}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\|C_{az+b}q_{n}\|-\|Kq_{n}\|\right)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|C_{az+b}q_{n}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(e^{-\lambda_{n}\Re(b)}\frac{\beta_{m_{n}}}{\beta_{n}}\right)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}r_{n}(a,\Re(b)).

Taking the infimum over all compact operators
K
on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
, we obtain

\|C_{az+b}-K\|_{e}\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}r_{n}(a,\Re(b)).

∎

4.2.
Schatten class

Recall that a bounded linear operator
T
on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
is called a Hilbert–Schmidt operator if it has finite Hilbert–Schmidt norm
\|T\|_{\rm HS}
, which means that for some orthonormal basis
(e_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
, we have

\|T\|_{\rm HS}:=\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|Te_{n}\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}<+\infty.

It is well known that
\|T\|_{\rm HS}
does not depend on the choice of the orthonormal basis and that if
T
is Hilbert–Schmidt, then it is compact.

Furthermore, for
0<p<\infty
, the Schatten
p
-class consists of all bounded linear operators
T
on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
for which
(T^{*}T)^{p/4}
is a Hilbert–Schmidt operator (here
T^{*}
is the adjoint operator of
T
). The set of Schatten
p
-class operators forms an ideal in the algebra of all bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. If
T
is diagonal with respect to an orthonormal basis
(e_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, that is,
Te_{n}=a_{n}\,e_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
, then it is well known that
T
belongs to the Schatten
p
-class if and only if
\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{p}<\infty
. Some developments on operators in Schatten classes can be found in
[
21
]
.

We study a Schatten class membership of
C_{az+b}
. For a bounded operator
C_{az+b}
on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
, since
C_{az+b}q_{n}=\frac{\beta_{m_{n}}}{\beta_{n}}e^{-\lambda_{n}b}q_{m_{n}}
, therefore
\|C_{az+b}q_{n}\|=e^{-\lambda_{n}\Re(b)}\cdot\frac{\beta_{m_{n}}}{\beta_{n}}=r_{n}(a,\Re(b))
, and we get an immediate result about its Hilbert–Schmidt property.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
.
A bounded composition operator
C_{az+b}
on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
is a Hilbert–Schmidt operator if and only if

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{n}(a,\Re(b))^{2}<\infty.

Concerning the Schatten
p
-class membership, for two trivial cases
a=0
and
a=1
, some results can be obtained easily.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
0<p<\infty
. A bounded composition operator
C_{b}
on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
belongs to the Schatten
p
-class.

Proof.

Since
C_{b}
is of rank one, in particular it is of finite rank and so has finitely many non-zero singular values.
∎

Also for the case
a=1
, as noted in Proposition
3.8
,
C_{z+b}
is a diagonal operator with a sequence of eigenvalues
(e^{-\lambda_{n}b})_{n\geq 1}
corresponding to eigenvectors
(q_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, and thus we have the following result.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
. A bounded composition operator
C_{z+b}
on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
belongs to the Schatten
p
-class if and only if

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e^{-p\lambda_{n}\Re(b)}<\infty.

In particular,
C_{z+b}
belongs to the Schatten
p
-class if
p\Re(b)>L
, and does not belong if
p\Re(b)<L
, where
L
is defined in (
1.2
).

Note that the second statement of the proposition above follows from Lemma
2.1
.

To go further and get a similar result for
a>1
, we need to compute the adjoint of
C_{az+b}
, which can be done easily. Indeed, for any
\displaystyle g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
, we write
\displaystyle(C_{az+b}^{*}g)(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}
and have

(4.1)

d_{n}\beta_{n}=\langle C_{az+b}^{*}g,q_{n}\rangle=\langle g,C_{az+b}q_{n}\rangle=\Big\langle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}e^{-\lambda_{k}z},C_{az+b}q_{n}\Big\rangle.

The terms
\langle e^{-\lambda_{k}z},C_{az+b}q_{n}\rangle
are computed in two cases:

- Case 1:
a=0
. By Proposition
3.7
,
\lambda_{1}=0
and hence

\langle e^{-\lambda_{k}z},C_{b}q_{n}\rangle=\begin{cases}\frac{e^{-\lambda_{n}\overline{b}}}{\beta_{n}}\beta_{1}^{2},&k=1\\
0,&k>1.\end{cases}

- Case 2:
a\geq 1
. By Theorem
3.6
, we have

\langle e^{-\lambda_{k}z},C_{az+b}q_{n}\rangle=\begin{cases}\frac{e^{-\lambda_{n}\overline{b}}}{\beta_{n}}\beta_{m_{n}}^{2},&m_{n}=k\\
0,&m_{n}\neq k.\end{cases}

Substituting back these equations into (
4.1
) yields the following result.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
, and let
C_{az+b}
be a bounded composition operator on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. If
\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
, then

(4.2)

(C_{az+b}^{*}f)(z)=\begin{cases}a_{1}\beta_{1}^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\lambda_{n}\overline{b}}}{\beta_{n}^{2}}e^{-\lambda_{n}z},&a=0\\
\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{m_{n}}\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{m_{n}}^{2}e^{-\lambda_{n}\overline{b}}}{\beta_{n}^{2}}e^{-\lambda_{n}z},&a\geq 1.\end{cases}

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
a\geq 1
. The eigenvalues of
C_{az+b}^{*}C_{az+b}
are precisely
r_{k}(a,\Re(b))^{2}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

We appeal to Proposition
4.7
which provides an explicit formula for the adjoint. Let
g_{k}=\beta_{k}q_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We have
(C_{az+b}g_{k})(z)=e^{-\lambda_{k}b}e^{-\lambda_{m_{k}}z}
. It follows that

\displaystyle(C_{az+b}^{*}C_{az+b}g_{k})(z)

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda_{k}b}\frac{\beta_{m_{k}}^{2}}{\beta_{k}^{2}}e^{-\lambda_{k}\overline{b}}e^{-\lambda_{k}z}=e^{-2\lambda_{k}\Re(b)}\frac{\beta_{m_{k}}^{2}}{\beta_{k}^{2}}g_{k}(z)

\displaystyle=r_{k}(a,\Re(b))^{2}g_{k}(z).

Now, recall that
(g_{k})_{k\geq 1}
forms an orthogonal basis of
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. It follows that the only eigenvalues are precisely those corresponding to these vectors, i.e. precisely
r_{k}(a,\Re(b))^{2}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

As
r_{k}(a,\Re(b))>0
for every
k
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.9
.

The eigenvalues of
|C_{az+b}|
are precisely the values
r_{k}(a,\Re(b))
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

As a consequence by direct substitution, we thus have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and
a\geq 1
. Then,
C_{az+b}
is a
p
th Schatten class operator if and only if

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r_{k}(a,\Re(b))^{p}<\infty.

Remark 4.11
.

Note that the series

h_{p}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\beta_{m_{k}}^{p}}{\beta_{k}^{p}}e^{-p\lambda_{k}z},

is a Dirichlet series of type
(p\lambda_{n})
, which can be considered as a “complex version” of the series
\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r_{k}(a,x)^{p}
when we replace
x\in\mathbb{R}
by
z\in\mathbb{C}
. By
[
14
, Chapter II, Theorem 8]
the computation of
\sigma_{c}
is

\displaystyle\sigma_{c}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\left(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\beta_{m_{k}}^{p}}{\beta_{k}^{p}}\right)}{p\lambda_{n}}.

Thus, if
\Re(b)
is larger than this value then
h_{p}(\Re(b))
converges and
C_{az+b}
is Schatten
p
-class. If
\Re(b)
is smaller, then
C_{az+b}
is not Schatten
p
-class.

Remark 4.12
.

Note also that it is possible that the membership to the
p
-th Schatten class of
C_{az+b}
does not rely on
p
at all. Pick
a>1
and
\Lambda=(a^{k})_{k\geq 1}
. In this case,
L=0
. Then pick
\beta=\displaystyle\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}e^{a^{i}}\right)_{k\geq 1}
, for which
\beta_{*}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{a^{2}-a}
and
C_{az+b}
is a well-defined bounded composition operator on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. The real function
\displaystyle h_{p}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{pa^{k}}e^{-pa^{k}x}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e^{-pa^{k}(x-1)}
converges for all
x>1
and diverges everywhere else, regardless of the value of
p
. It follows that
C_{az+b}
is a Schatten
p
-class operator if and only if
\Re(b)>1
.

4.3.
Compact differences

In this section we determine when a difference of two bounded composition operators
C_{\varphi_{1}}-C_{\varphi_{2}}
is a compact operator. Recall that the set of compact operators form a vector space. Hence by Proposition
4.1
and Theorem
4.3
, it suffices to consider only the case when
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are non-constant and the associated sequences
(r_{n})
do not have limit
0
.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
,
a,a^{\prime}\geq 1
. Let
C_{az+b}
and
C_{a^{\prime}z+b^{\prime}}
be bounded composition operators on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Assume that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}r_{n}(a,\Re(b))>0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\limsup_{n\to\infty}r_{n}(a^{\prime},\Re(b^{\prime}))>0.

If
C_{az+b}-C_{a^{\prime}z+b^{\prime}}
is compact, then
a=a^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Consider the sequence of probe functions
(q_{k})_{k\geq 1}
. By Proposition
2.4
,
q_{k}\rightharpoonup 0
. Since
C_{az+b}-C_{a^{\prime}z+b^{\prime}}
is compact, therefore
\|(C_{az+b}-C_{a^{\prime}z+b^{\prime}})q_{k}\|\to 0
. Assume to the contrary that
a\neq a^{\prime}
. Now, since for each
k
we have
m_{k}^{(a)}\neq m_{k}^{(a^{\prime})}
, therefore we have

\displaystyle\|(C_{az+b}-C_{a^{\prime}z+b^{\prime}})q_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}e^{-\lambda_{k}b}e^{-\lambda_{m_{k}^{(a)}}z}-\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}e^{-\lambda_{k}b^{\prime}}e^{-\lambda_{m_{k}^{(a^{\prime})}}z}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta_{k}^{2}}e^{-2\lambda_{k}\Re(b)}\beta_{m_{k}^{(a)}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\beta_{k}^{2}}e^{-2\lambda_{k}\Re(b^{\prime})}\beta_{m_{k}^{(a^{\prime})}}^{2}

\displaystyle=r_{k}(a,\Re(b))^{2}+r_{k}(a^{\prime},\Re(b^{\prime}))^{2},

which gives the desired contradiction.
∎

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
,
a\geq 1
. Suppose that
C_{az+b}
and
C_{az+b^{\prime}}
are bounded composition operators on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Then

\|C_{az+b}-C_{az+b^{\prime}}\|_{e}=\limsup\limits_{k\to\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{m_{k}}}{\beta_{k}}\left|e^{-\lambda_{k}b}-e^{-\lambda_{k}b^{\prime}}\right|.

Proof.

As before we define the finite-rank (and compact) partial sum operator
K_{N}
(see the proof of Theorem
4.3
). Then
(C_{az+b}-C_{az+b^{\prime}})K_{N}
is compact and

\displaystyle\|C_{az+b}-C_{az+b^{\prime}}\|_{e}\leq\|(C_{az+b}-C_{az+b^{\prime}})(I-K_{N})\|_{\text{op}},

where
I
is the identity operator on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
.

Let
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. We then have

\displaystyle\|(C_{az+b}-C_{az+b^{\prime}})(I-K_{N})(f)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}b}e^{-\lambda_{m_{n}}z}-\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}b^{\prime}}e^{-\lambda_{m_{n}}z}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\left|e^{-\lambda_{n}b}-e^{-\lambda_{n}b^{\prime}}\right|^{2}\beta_{m_{n}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{n\geq N+1}\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{m_{n}}^{2}}{\beta_{n}^{2}}\left|e^{-\lambda_{n}b}-e^{-\lambda_{n}b^{\prime}}\right|^{2}\|f\|^{2}.

It therefore follows that
\|(C_{az+b}-C_{az+b^{\prime}})(I-K_{N})\|\leq\sup\limits_{n\geq N+1}\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{m_{n}}}{\beta_{n}}\left|e^{-\lambda_{n}b}-e^{-\lambda_{n}b^{\prime}}\right|
. Taking limits as
N\to\infty
gives
\|C_{az+b}-C_{az+b^{\prime}}\|_{e}\leq\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{m_{n}}}{\beta_{n}}\left|e^{-\lambda_{n}b}-e^{-\lambda_{n}b^{\prime}}\right|
.

On the other hand, consider the probe functions
q_{k}
. Let
K
be a compact operator on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. As before, we have
\|q_{k}\|=1
for all
k
and
\|Kq_{k}\|\to 0
. We have

\displaystyle\|(C_{az+b}-C_{az+b^{\prime}})-K\|

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\|(C_{az+b}-C_{az+b^{\prime}})q_{k}\|-\|Kq_{k}\|\right)

\displaystyle\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|(C_{az+b}-C_{az+b^{\prime}})q_{k}\|

\displaystyle=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{m_{k}}}{\beta_{k}}\left|e^{-\lambda_{k}b}-e^{-\lambda_{k}b^{\prime}}\right|.

Taking infimum over all compact operators
K
gives

\|C_{az+b}-C_{az+b^{\prime}}\|_{e}\geq\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{m_{n}}}{\beta_{n}}\left|e^{-\lambda_{n}b}-e^{-\lambda_{n}b^{\prime}}\right|.

∎

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
, and let
C_{\varphi_{1}}
and
C_{\varphi_{2}}
be bounded composition operators on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Then the difference
C_{\varphi_{1}}-C_{\varphi_{2}}
is compact if and only if

(1) both
C_{\varphi_{1}}
and
C_{\varphi_{2}}
are compact

or

(2)
a\geq 1
,
\varphi_{1}(z)=az+b
and
\varphi_{2}(z)=az+b^{\prime}
, where

(i)

we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}r_{n}(a,\Re(b))>0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\limsup_{n\to\infty}r_{n}(a,\Re(b^{\prime}))>0,

(ii)

and
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{m_{k}}}{\beta_{k}}\left|e^{-\lambda_{k}b}-e^{-\lambda_{k}b^{\prime}}\right|=0
.

Proof.

Assume first that
C_{\varphi_{1}}-C_{\varphi_{2}}
is compact but one of the operators
C_{\varphi_{1}}
and
C_{\varphi_{2}}
is not compact. Since the set of compact operators is a vector space, it implies that indeed both operators
C_{\varphi_{1}}
and
C_{\varphi_{2}}
are not compact. Now, according to Theorem
4.3
, it means that condition (i) is satisfied (with the second inequality in
a^{\prime}
instead of
a
). Then, we can apply Lemma
4.13
to get that
a=a^{\prime}
, i.e.
\varphi_{1}(z)=az+b
and
\varphi_{2}(z)=az+b^{\prime}
. Condition (ii) now follows immediately from Proposition
4.14
.

Conversely, if both operators
C_{\varphi_{1}}
and
C_{\varphi_{2}}
are compact, then their difference is compact. Suppose now that
\varphi_{1}(z)=az+b
and
\varphi_{2}(z)=az+b^{\prime}
, and (i) and (ii) are satisfied. Proposition
4.14
implies that
\|C_{\varphi_{1}}-C_{\varphi 2}\|_{e}=0
, which gives that
C_{\varphi_{1}}-C_{\varphi_{2}}
is compact.
∎

Corollary 4.16
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
, and let
C_{z+ci}
and
C_{z+c^{\prime}i}
(
c,c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
) be bounded composition operators on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. The operator
C_{z+ci}-C_{z+c^{\prime}i}
is compact if and only if
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\cos(\lambda_{k}(c-c^{\prime}))=1
.

Proof.

We see that
m_{k}=k
and so
\beta_{m_{k}}=\beta_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, since
\Re(ci)=\Re(c^{\prime}i)=0
, we have
r_{n}(a,\Re(ci))=r_{n}(a^{\prime},\Re(c^{\prime}i))=1
. Then, according to Proposition
4.2
and Theorem
4.15
, we see that
C_{z+ci}-C_{z+c^{\prime}i}
is compact if and only if

\lim_{k\to\infty}|e^{-\lambda_{k}ci}-e^{-\lambda_{k}c^{\prime}i}|=0.

An easy computation shows that

|e^{-\lambda_{k}ci}-e^{-\lambda_{k}c^{\prime}i}|^{2}=2-2\cos(\lambda_{k}(c-c^{\prime})),

which gives the result.
∎

Corollary
4.16
is an analogue of
[
19
, Theorem 4.12]
corresponding to the case when
\beta_{*}=\infty
.

5.
Closed range and cyclicity

In this section, we assume that
C_{az+b}
is a bounded composition operator on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
, which means that
a
and
b
satisfies conditions of Theorem
3.14
.

5.1.
Closed range

We denote by
R(C_{\varphi})
the range of
C_{\varphi}
, i.e.
C_{\varphi}(\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda))
. In this section we determine when
R(C_{\varphi})
is closed. Note that when
\varphi(z)=b
(and
\lambda_{1}=0
), then
R(C_{\varphi})
is a one dimensional space (generated by the constant function
1=e^{-\lambda_{1}z}
) and so it is closed.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
,
a\geq 1
. Then,
R(C_{az+b})
is closed if and only if
\inf\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}r_{n}(a,\Re(b))>0
.

Proof.

First note that it follows from the open mapping theorem and uniqueness principle for analytic functions that, since
\varphi
is a non-constant analytic function, then
C_{\varphi}
is injective. Now, suppose
B:=\inf\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}r_{n}(a,\Re(b))>0
. Let
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{\|C_{az+b}f\|^{2}}{\|f\|^{2}}=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\beta_{m_{n}}^{2}e^{-2\lambda_{n}\Re(b)}}{\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\beta_{n}^{2}}=\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\beta_{n}^{2}r_{n}(a,\Re(b))^{2}}{\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\beta_{n}^{2}}\geq B^{2}.

Thus
C_{az+b}
is bounded from below and
R(C_{az+b})
is hence closed.

On the other hand, suppose
B:=\inf\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}r_{n}(a,\Re(b))=0
. Let
(n_{k})
be a subsequence of
\mathbb{N}
such that
r_{n_{k}}(a,\Re(b))\to 0
. For each probe function
q_{n_{k}}
, we then have

\displaystyle\frac{\|C_{az+b}q_{n_{k}}\|^{2}}{\|q_{n_{k}}\|^{2}}=\frac{\frac{1}{\beta_{n_{k}}^{2}}\beta_{m_{n_{k}}}^{2}e^{-2\lambda_{n}\Re(b)}}{1}=r_{n_{k}}(a,\Re(b))^{2}\to 0.

It follows that
C_{az+b}
cannot be bounded from below, and so
R(C_{az+b})
is not closed.
∎

Proposition
5.1
is an analogue of
[
7
, Theorem 6]
.

Remark 5.2
.

In the non-constant case, a compact
C_{az+b}
cannot have closed range, and vice-versa if
C_{az+b}
has closed range, it is not compact.

5.2.
Cyclicity

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
T:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
be a bounded operator. We define the orbit of a vector
x\in\mathcal{H}
(w.r.t.
T
) as the set

\displaystyle\Orb(T,x)=\left\{T^{n}x:n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Furthermore, we recall that
T
is said to be

•

cyclic if there exists
x\in\mathcal{H}
such that

\overline{\Span(\Orb(T,x))}=\mathcal{H},

•

supercyclic if there exists
x\in\mathcal{H}
such that

\overline{\left\{\mu y:y\in\Orb(T,x),\mu\in\mathbb{C}\right\}}=\mathcal{H}.

Note that for a given operator on an Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, if
T
is supercyclic, then it is of course cyclic. We will discuss in this section the cyclicity and supercyclicity of the operators
C_{\varphi}
on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. As we will see,
C_{\varphi}
is never supercyclic but cyclicity will depend on the arithmetic properties of
(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
.

It is trivial that if
\lambda_{1}=0
, then of course the operators
C_{b}
and
C_{z}
are not cyclic. Indeed, in both cases the orbit of
f
(for every
f\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
) contains only one function and so the orbit cannot generate a dense subspace.

We now split our study in two cases, depending whether
a=1
or
a>1
.

- The case
a=1
.

To study this case, we need the following two general results. The first one is quite classical and can be found for instance in
[
13
, Chap. 18]
for the finite dimensional case and in
[
24
, Lemma 1]
for the general case. The proof of the second one can be found in
[
17
]
and uses the spectral mapping theorem.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
D
be a diagonal operator on an Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, given by
De_{n}=s_{n}e_{n}
,
n\geq 1
, where
(e_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
. Then
D
is cyclic if and only if
s_{n}\neq s_{m}
,
n\neq m
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
T
be a normal operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
of dimension greater than
1
. Then
T
is not supercyclic.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
,
b\neq 0
. Then,

(1)

C_{z+b}
is cyclic if and only if
(\lambda_{n}-\lambda_{m})b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
whenever
n\neq m
, and

(2)

C_{z+b}
is not supercyclic.

Proof.

(1) Recall that the set of probe functions
q_{k}(z)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}e^{-\lambda_{k}z}
form a basis for
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Note also that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle(C_{z+b}q_{k})(z)=e^{-\lambda_{k}b}q_{k}(z).

That means that
C_{z+b}
is a diagonal operator with eigenvalue corresponding to
e^{-\lambda_{k}b}
. Thus (1) follows immediately from Lemma
5.3
.

(2) Since
C_{z+b}
is diagonal, it is in particular normal. So (2) follows from Lemma
5.4
.
∎

- The case
a>1
.

The situation in this case is more interesting and the behavior of the iterates of
C_{az+b}
will depend on the following notion.

Given
a\in R(\Lambda)
,
\Lambda=(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, we define an
initial point with respect to
a
to be a term
\lambda_{k}
such that no
n<k
exists such that
a^{s}\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{k}
for some
s\in\mathbb{N}
. This is equivalent to say that, for
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

a^{s}\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{k}\Longrightarrow s=0\mbox{ and }n=k.

Note that if
\lambda_{1}=0
, then
\lambda_{2}
is necessarily an initial point. If
\lambda_{1}\neq 0
, then
\lambda_{1}
is necessarily an initial point. Thus, in all cases, there exists at least one non zero initial point. Furthermore,
(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
has only one non-zero initial point w.r.t.
a
if and only if non-zero terms of
(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
are in geometric progression with common ratio
a
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
,
a>1
, and suppose
(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
has precisely one initial point with respect to
a
. Then,
C_{az+b}
is cyclic but not supercyclic.

Proof.

Since
(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
has precisely one initial point, it must be the case that
\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{1}a^{n-1}
for every
n
, and
\lambda_{1}\neq 0
. Let
f(z)=e^{-\lambda_{1}z}
. By induction, we easily check that

\displaystyle(C_{az+b}^{k}f)(z)=\exp(-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\dots+\lambda_{k})b)e^{-\lambda_{k+1}z}.

In particular,
\Orb(C_{az+b},f)
contains the vectors of the basis
(q_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Therefore
C_{az+b}
is cyclic.

To show
C_{az+b}
is not supercyclic, we show that for every
f\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
we can find a
g
such that
\dist(g,\left\{\mu y:y\in\Orb(C_{az+b},f),\mu\in\mathbb{C}\right\})
is bounded below by some non-zero constant. There are two cases.

•

Case 1:
f(z)=a_{1}e^{-\lambda_{1}z}
for some
0\neq a_{1}\in\mathbb{C}
. In this case, consider the function
g(z)=e^{-\lambda_{1}z}+e^{-\lambda_{2}z}
. It is then easy to see that

\displaystyle\|\mu f-g\|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\mu a_{1}-1|^{2}\beta_{1}^{2}+\beta_{2}^{2}\geq\beta_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle\|\mu C_{az+b}f-g\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\beta_{1}^{2}+|\mu a_{1}e^{-\lambda_{1}b}-1|^{2}\beta_{2}^{2}\geq\beta_{1}^{2},

\displaystyle\|\mu C_{az+b}^{k}f-g\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\beta_{1}^{2}+\beta_{2}^{2},\qquad\forall k\geq 2.

Therefore
\dist(g,\left\{\mu y:y\in\Orb(C_{az+b},f),\mu\in\mathbb{C}\right\})\geq\min\left\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\right\}
.

•

Case 2:
f(z)\neq a_{1}e^{-\lambda_{1}z}
for any
a_{1}\in\mathbb{C}
.

Write
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}
. Choose
\kappa\neq 0,-a_{1}
and pick
g(z)=\kappa e^{-\lambda_{1}z}
. Note that for all
k\geq 1
we have that the coefficient of
e^{-\lambda_{1}z}
in the representation of
C_{az+b}^{k}f
is
0
, it follows then that for each
k\geq 1
, we have
\|\mu C_{az+b}^{k}f-g\|^{2}\geq|\kappa|^{2}\beta_{1}^{2}
.

It remains to consider
k=0
. We have

\displaystyle(\mu f-g)(z)

\displaystyle=\mu\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}-\kappa e^{-\lambda_{1}z}

\displaystyle=(\mu a_{1}-\kappa)e^{-\lambda_{1}z}+\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 2}\mu a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}.

Since
f(z)\neq a_{1}e^{-\lambda_{1}z}
for any
a_{1}
, there exists
k\geq 2
such that
a_{k}\neq 0
. We have

\displaystyle\|\mu f-g\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq|\mu a_{1}-\kappa|^{2}\beta_{1}^{2}+|\mu a_{k}|^{2}\beta_{k}^{2}.

By minimizing over
\Re(\mu)
and noting that the minimum remains unchanged if we replaced
\kappa
and
a_{1}
with their moduli, the RHS satisfies

\displaystyle|\mu a_{1}-\kappa|^{2}\beta_{1}^{2}+|\mu a_{k}|^{2}\beta_{k}^{2}\geq\displaystyle\frac{|\kappa a_{k}|^{2}\beta_{1}^{2}\beta_{k}^{2}}{|a_{1}|^{2}\beta_{1}^{2}+|a_{k}|^{2}\beta_{k}^{2}}=:D,

where the inequality follows because the expression
ax^{2}+b(x-c)^{2}
where
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
,
a,b>0
, has minimum
\frac{abc^{2}}{a+b}
as
x
ranges over
\mathbb{R}
.

Thus
\dist(g,\left\{\mu y:y\in\Orb(C_{\varphi},f),\mu\in\mathbb{C}\right\})\geq\min\{|\kappa|\beta_{1},\sqrt{D}\}
.

∎

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
,
a>1
, and suppose
(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
has at least two non-zero initial points with respect to
a
. Then,
C_{az+b}
is not cyclic.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{p},\lambda_{q}
denote two non-zero initial points. Suppose for the sake of contradiction that
C_{az+b}
is cyclic. Let
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}
be a cyclic vector for
C_{az+b}
.

We claim that
a_{p},a_{q}\neq 0
. Assume to the contrary that one of them is zero, say WLOG
a_{p}=0
. Recall that
\Orb(C_{az+b},f)=\left\{f,C_{az+b}f,C_{az+b}^{2}f,\dots\right\}
. Note that

\displaystyle(C_{az+b}^{k}f)(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\exp\left(-b\frac{1-a^{k}}{1-a}\right)\exp\left(-a^{k}\lambda_{n}z\right).

Since
\lambda_{p}
is a non-zero initial point,
a^{k}\lambda_{n}\neq\lambda_{p}
for all
k,n\geq 1
. It follows that the coefficient of
\lambda_{p}
in any function in
\Orb(C_{az+b},f)
is always
0
. Hence for any
f_{1}\in\Span(\Orb(C_{az+b},f))
, one has

\displaystyle\|f_{1}-q_{p}\|^{2}\geq 1.

Therefore
\Span(\Orb(C_{az+b},f))
cannot be dense in
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
, a contradiction, completing the proof of the claim.

Hence
a_{p},a_{q}\neq 0
. Since
a^{k}\lambda_{n}\neq\lambda_{p},\lambda_{q}
for all
k,n\geq 1
, the only function in
\Orb(C_{az+b},f)
with non-zero coefficients for
e^{-\lambda_{p}z}
and
e^{-\lambda_{q}z}
terms in
\Span(\Orb(C_{az+b},f))
is
f
itself.

Consider now the function
g(z)=a_{p}e^{-\lambda_{p}z}+2a_{q}e^{-\lambda_{q}z}\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Let
F\in\Span(\Orb(C_{az+b},f))
and fix a representation of
F
in elements of
\Orb(C_{az+b},f)
. Let
w
the coefficient of
f
in this representation. Then we have

\displaystyle\|F-g\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq|w-1|^{2}|a_{p}|^{2}\beta_{p}^{2}+|w-2|^{2}|a_{q}|^{2}\beta_{q}^{2}.

Let
\xi=|a_{p}|^{2}\beta_{p}^{2}
and
\eta=|a_{q}|^{2}\beta_{q}^{2}
. Write
w=w^{\prime}+1
. Then,

\displaystyle\|F-g\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\xi|w^{\prime}|^{2}+\eta|w^{\prime}-1|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\xi\Re(w^{\prime})^{2}+\eta(\Re(w^{\prime})-1)^{2}\geq\displaystyle\frac{\xi\eta}{\xi+\eta},

where the last inequality is minimized in the same manner as in Proposition
5.6
.

It follows that
\Span(\Orb(C_{az+b},f))
cannot be dense in
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Contradiction.
∎

It remains to study the case when
(\lambda_{n})_{n\geq 1}
has precisely one zero and one non-zero initial point with respect to
a
. In other words,
\lambda_{1}=0
and
\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{2}a^{n-2}
,
n\geq 2
. In that case, we will give a sufficient condition to ensure that
C_{az+b}
is cyclic. Since cyclicity is invariant under unitary transformation, we will work for convenience with a transcription of our problem in the Hardy space of the unit disc
H^{2}
. Recall that

\displaystyle H^{2}=\left\{f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}\in\Hol(\mathbb{D}):f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}|^{2}<\infty\right\}.

The Hardy space has the canonical orthonormal basis
\{1,z,z^{2},\dots\}
and for the remainder of this section we will denote by
e_{n}
the vectors of this basis, that is
e_{n}(z)=z^{n}
for all
n\geq 0
.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
,
a>1
, and suppose
(\lambda_{n})
has precisely one zero and one non-zero initial point with respect to
a
. Then the matrix
M
of
C_{az+b}
with respect to the orthonormal basis
(q_{k})
is

\displaystyle M=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0&0&\dots\\
0&0&0&\dots\\
0&r_{2}(a,b)&0&\dots\\
0&0&r_{3}(a,b)&\dots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{matrix}\right).

Now, it is clear that our problem is a particular case of the following more general problem.

Question 5.9
.

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{n})
be a bounded sequence of complex numbers and suppose that
T:H^{2}\to H^{2}
is the linear map whose matrix, with respect to the orthonormal basis
\{e_{0},e_{1},e_{2},\dots\}
, is given by

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}1&0&0&\dots\\
0&0&0&\dots\\
0&\alpha_{1}&0&\dots\\
0&0&\alpha_{2}&\dots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{matrix}\right)

Is
T
cyclic?

Note that since
(\alpha_{n})
is supposed to be bounded, it is easy to see that
T
defines a bounded operator on
H^{2}
. It turns out that
T
is linked with a weighted shift operator. In the following, we denote by
H_{0}^{2}=\overline{\Span\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots\}}
.

Definition 5.10
.

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a bounded sequence. We define the weighted forward shift operator
S_{\alpha}
on
H^{2}_{0}
by
S_{\alpha}e_{n}=\alpha_{n}e_{n+1}
,
n\geq 1
.

Note that we have
T=I\oplus S_{\alpha}:H^{2}=\mathbb{C}e_{0}\oplus H_{0}^{2}\to H^{2}=\mathbb{C}e_{0}\oplus H_{0}^{2}
. In other words,

T(\gamma e_{0}\oplus x)=\gamma e_{0}\oplus S_{\alpha}x\qquad(\gamma,x)\in\mathbb{C}\times H_{0}^{2}.

By induction and basic computation we have the following two lemmas.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{C}[X]
. Then,
(p(T))(\gamma e_{0}\oplus x)=\gamma p(1)e_{0}\oplus p(S_{\alpha})x
.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
p(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{d}a_{j}z^{j}
be a polynomial. Let
\omega_{j}:=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\dots\alpha_{j}
for all
j\geq 1
. Then,

\displaystyle p(S_{\alpha})e_{1}=a_{0}e_{1}+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{j}\omega_{j}e_{j+1}.

The next lemma provides an equivalence we use in our investigation.

Lemma 5.13
.

The following are equivalent.

(1)

T
is cyclic.

(2)

There exists
\kappa\in\mathbb{C}
and
x\in H_{0}^{2}
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
,
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
, and
y\in H_{0}^{2}
, there exists
p\in\mathbb{C}[X]
such that

\displaystyle|\kappa p(1)-\mu|<\varepsilon\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|p(S_{\alpha})x-y\|<\varepsilon.

Proof.

By definition,
T
is cyclic if and only if there exists
\kappa\in\mathbb{C}
and
x\in H_{0}^{2}
such that for all
\varepsilon>0
,
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
, and
y\in H_{0}^{2}
, there exists
p\in\mathbb{C}[X]
such that

\displaystyle\|p(T)(\kappa e_{0}\oplus x)-(\mu e_{0}\oplus y)\|^{2}<\varepsilon.

By Lemma
5.11
and by virtue of being direct sums, we have

\displaystyle\|p(T)(\kappa e_{0}\oplus x)-(\mu e_{0}\oplus y)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(\kappa p(1)e_{0}\oplus p(S_{\alpha})x)-(\mu e_{0}\oplus y)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|(\kappa p(1)-\mu)e_{0}\oplus(p(S_{\alpha})x-y)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\kappa p(1)-\mu|^{2}+\|p(S_{\alpha})x-y\|^{2}.

Hence the condition in the definition can be rewritten as

\displaystyle|\kappa p(1)-\mu|^{2}+\|p(S_{\alpha})x-y\|^{2}<\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
is an arbitrary variable, this completes the proof.
∎

With this lemma, the question becomes one of whether such a polynomial
p\in\mathbb{C}[X]
can always be chosen so that the second statement of Lemma
5.13
holds. To that end, we have the following result.

Proposition 5.14
.

Let
(\alpha_{j})
be a bounded sequence of complex numbers, let
\omega_{j}=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\dots\alpha_{j}
for each
j\geq 1
, and suppose that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|w_{j}|^{2}}=\infty.

Let
\nu\in\mathbb{C}
be given.
Then, there exists a sequence of non-constant polynomials
(u_{k})\in\mathbb{C}[X]
such that

\displaystyle u_{k}(1)=\nu\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|u_{k}(S_{\alpha})e_{1}\|\to 0.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\nu
is a non-negative real number.
For a general
u_{k}\in\mathbb{C}[X]
we write
u_{k}(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{d_{k}}b_{j,k}z^{j}
. The question is to define appropriate choices of
b_{j,k}
that fulfill the conditions of the lemma.

We find
u_{k}(z)
with the form
u_{k}(z)=\nu+(z-1)p_{k}(z)
, where the
p_{k}
’s are polynomials. Note that such a
u_{k}
will always have
u_{k}(1)=\nu
. Write
p_{k}(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{s_{k}}a_{j,k}z^{j}
. Then we have

\displaystyle u_{k}(z)

\displaystyle=\nu+\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{s_{k}}a_{j,k}z^{j+1}-\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{s_{k}}a_{j,k}z^{j}

\displaystyle=(\nu-a_{0,k})+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s_{k}}(a_{j-1,k}-a_{j,k})z^{j}+a_{s_{k},k}z^{s_{k}+1}.

This gives by Lemma
5.12

\displaystyle u_{k}(S_{\alpha})e_{1}=(\nu-a_{0,k})e_{1}+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s_{k}}(a_{j-1,k}-a_{j,k})\omega_{j}e_{j+1}+a_{s_{k},k}\omega_{s_{k}+1}e_{s_{k}+2},

and so

\displaystyle\|u_{k}(S_{\alpha})e_{1}\|^{2}=|\nu-a_{0,k}|^{2}+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s_{k}}|a_{j-1,k}-a_{j,k}|^{2}|\omega_{j}|^{2}+|a_{s_{k},k}|^{2}|\omega_{s_{k}+1}|^{2}.

Since
\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|w_{j}|^{-2}=\infty
, it is well known that we can find a sequence of positive real numbers
(h_{j})_{j\geq 1}
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{h_{j}|w_{j}|}=\infty\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{h_{j}^{2}}<\infty.

Assume
\nu>0
. Pick
a_{0,k}=\nu
. Define
\varepsilon_{k}>0
such that

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{k}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{1}|\omega_{1}|}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{2}|\omega_{2}|}+\dots+\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{s_{k}}|\omega_{s_{k}}|}\right)=\nu,

and define recursively

\displaystyle a_{j,k}=a_{j-1,k}-\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon_{k}}{h_{j}|\omega_{j}|},\qquad 1\leq k\leq s_{k}.

It is easily checked that
a_{s_{k},k}=0
and
|a_{j-1,k}-a_{j,k}|^{2}|\omega_{j}|^{2}=\displaystyle\frac{|\varepsilon_{k}|^{2}}{h_{j}^{2}}
for all
1\leq j\leq s_{k}
. Under this choice of coefficients, we therefore have

\displaystyle\|u_{k}(S_{\alpha})e_{1}\|^{2}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s_{k}}\frac{|\varepsilon_{k}|^{2}}{h_{j}^{2}}<|\varepsilon_{k}|^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{h_{j}^{2}}=:|\varepsilon_{k}|^{2}M.

Since
M
is finite, we are done if we can pick values of
s_{k}
so that
\varepsilon_{k}
goes to zero. But by assumption,
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s_{k}}\displaystyle\frac{1}{h_{j}|\omega_{j}|}
will grow arbitrarily large, so indeed
s_{k}
can be chosen to make
\varepsilon_{k}
arbitrarily small.

If
\nu=0
, we fix
\varepsilon_{k}>0
, pick
a_{0,k}=\varepsilon_{k}
, and define
a_{j,k}
recursively as before, and we then have

\displaystyle a_{s_{k},k}=\varepsilon_{k}\left(1-\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s_{k}}\frac{1}{h_{j}|\omega_{j}|}\right).

It then follows that

\displaystyle\|u_{k}(S_{\alpha})e_{1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=|0-a_{0,k}|^{2}+\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s_{k}}|a_{j-1,k}-a_{j,k}|^{2}|\omega_{j}|^{2}+|a_{s_{k},k}|^{2}|\omega_{s_{k}+1}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{k}^{2}C_{k},

where
C_{k}
is a constant value dependent only on the choice of value of
s_{k}
. It follows that one may simply pick
s_{k}
freely and choose
\varepsilon_{k}
arbitrarily small to make
\|u_{k}(S_{\alpha})e_{1}\|
arbitrarily small.
∎

We are now ready to prove the following.

Theorem 5.15
.

Suppose
(|\omega_{j}|^{-1})\notin\ell^{2}
. Then,
T
is cyclic.

Proof.

Pick
\kappa=1
and
x=e_{1}
in Statement (2) of Lemma
5.13
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
,
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
, and
y\in H_{0}^{2}
. We need to show that
p\in\mathbb{C}[X]
exists such that

\displaystyle|p(1)-\mu|<\varepsilon\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|p(S_{\alpha})e_{1}-y\|<\varepsilon.

Write
y=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}
. Since
y\in H_{0}^{2}
, there exists
N\geq 1
such that
\displaystyle\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|^{2}<\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. Fix this
N
.

We claim we can choose a polynomial
\widetilde{p}
such that
\|\widetilde{p}(S_{\alpha})e_{1}-y\|<\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
. Write
\widetilde{p}(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}c_{n}z^{n}
and let
y^{\prime}(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}b_{n}z^{n}
. The question is to show that there exists a choice of coefficients
c_{n}
. We have

\displaystyle(\widetilde{p}(S_{\alpha})e_{1}-y^{\prime})(z)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{N}c_{n}S_{\alpha}^{n}(z)-\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}b_{n}z^{n}

\displaystyle=c_{0}z+\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}c_{n}\omega_{n}z^{n+1}-\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}b_{n}z^{n}

\displaystyle=(c_{0}-b_{1})z+\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{N}(c_{n-1}\omega_{n-1}-b_{n})z^{n}.

Clearly then

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{p}(S_{\alpha})e_{1}-y^{\prime}\|^{2}=|c_{0}-b_{1}|^{2}+\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{N}|c_{n-1}\omega_{n-1}-b_{n}|^{2}.

Since the
\omega_{n}
’s and the
b_{n}
’s are known values with
\omega_{n}\neq 0
for all
n
, it follows that we can pick an appropriate choice of
c_{n}
’s in every instance such that
\|\widetilde{p}(S_{\alpha})e_{1}-y\|^{2}<\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
, for instance

\displaystyle c_{0}=\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}{2}+b_{1},\qquad\text{and}\qquad c_{n}=\frac{b_{n}}{\omega_{n-1}},\quad\forall n\geq 2.

Since
(|\omega_{j}|^{-1})\notin\ell^{2}
, by Proposition
5.14
there exists a sequence
(u_{k})\subset\mathbb{C}[X]
such that

\displaystyle u_{k}(1)=\mu-\widetilde{p}(1)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|u_{k}(S_{\alpha})e_{1}\|\to 0.

In particular there exists
k
large enough such that
\|u_{k}(S_{\alpha})e_{1}\|<\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
. Fix this
k
.

Now, set
p=\widetilde{p}+u_{k}
. Then we have by the triangle inequality that

\displaystyle\|p(S_{\alpha})e_{1}-y\|

\displaystyle=\|\widetilde{p}(S_{\alpha})e_{1}+u_{k}(S_{\alpha})e_{1}-(y-y^{\prime})-y^{\prime}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\widetilde{p}(S_{\alpha})e_{1}-y^{\prime}\|+\|y-y^{\prime}\|+\|u_{k}(S_{\alpha})e_{1}\|<\varepsilon.

Also, note that
p(1)=\widetilde{p}(1)+u_{k}(1)=\mu
, which gives

\displaystyle|p(1)-\mu|=0<\varepsilon.

Hence indeed we can find such a
p
as per Statement (2) of Lemma
5.13
. By this same lemma,
T
is cyclic. This proves the theorem.
∎

The above leads to a partial solution of the remaining case for cyclicity of
C_{az+b}
.

Corollary 5.16
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
,
a>1
, and suppose
(\lambda_{n})
has precisely one zero and one non-zero initial point with respect to
a
.
Let
w_{j}=\displaystyle\prod_{k=2}^{j+1}r_{k}(a,b)
. Assume that
(w_{j}^{-1})_{j}\not\in\ell^{2}
. Then
C_{az+b}
is cyclic.

Corollary 5.17
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
,
a>1
, and suppose
(\lambda_{n})
has precisely one zero and one non-zero initial point with respect to
a
. Let
b\in\mathbb{C}
and suppose that
\Re(b)>a\beta_{*}
and
\Re(b)\geq 0
. Then,
C_{az+b}
is cyclic.

Proof.

Assume for the sake of contradiction that
C_{az+b}
is not cyclic. Hence by Corollary
5.16
, we have
(|\omega_{j}|^{-1})\in\ell^{2}
. In particular,
|\omega_{j}|^{-1}\to 0
. But a direct computation shows that

\displaystyle|\omega_{j}|^{-1}=\frac{\beta_{2}}{\beta_{j+2}}e^{\lambda_{2}\Re(b)(1+a+\dots+a^{j-1})},

and since
\Re(b)\geq 0
, we get
\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta_{j+2}}e^{\lambda_{2}\Re(b)a^{j-1}}\to 0
.

Recall now that
\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\log\beta_{n}}{\lambda_{n}}=:\beta_{*}
is finite. Therefore for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a strictly increasing subsequence
(n_{p})
of the natural numbers such that
\displaystyle\frac{\log\beta_{n_{p}}}{\lambda_{n_{p}}}<\beta_{*}+\varepsilon
. In particular we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta_{n_{p}}}>e^{-\lambda_{n_{p}}(\beta_{*}+\varepsilon)}=e^{-\lambda_{2}a^{n_{p}-2}(\beta_{*}+\varepsilon)},\qquad\forall n_{p}\geq 2.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
small such that
\Re(b)>a(\beta_{*}+\varepsilon)
. Choose the subsequence
(n_{p})
as in the above, and write
j_{p}=n_{p}-2
for all
n_{p}>2
. Then in particular we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta_{j_{p}+2}}e^{\lambda_{2}\Re(b)a^{j_{p}-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta_{n_{p}}}e^{\lambda_{2}\Re(b)a^{n_{p}-3}}

\displaystyle>e^{-\lambda_{2}a^{n_{p}-2}(\beta_{*}+\varepsilon)}e^{\lambda_{2}\Re(b)a^{n_{p}-3}}

\displaystyle=e^{\lambda_{2}a^{n_{p}-3}(\Re(b)-a(\beta_{*}+\varepsilon))}.

By assumption,
\Re(b)>a(\beta_{*}+\varepsilon)
, i.e.
\Re(b)-a(\beta_{*}+\varepsilon)>0
. Hence the RHS has infinite limit. But this means there exists a subsequence
(j_{p})
such that
|\omega_{j_{p}}|^{-1}\to\infty
. Contradiction.
∎

We end this section with a result which completes Proposition
5.5
on supercyclicity. As the proof methodology is similar to arguments in Proposition
5.6
, we omit the full details and provide a sketch.

Proposition 5.18
.

Let
\beta_{*}=\pm\infty
,
a>1
, and suppose
(\lambda_{n})
has precisely one zero and one non-zero initial point with respect to
a
. Then,
C_{az+b}
is not supercyclic.

Proof (Outline).

The idea is for every
f\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
, we can find a function
g
and a constant
B>0
such that
\dist(g,\left\{\mu y:y\in\Orb(C_{az+b},f),\mu\in\mathbb{C}\right\})\geq B
. The proof is split into four cases.

- Case 1:

f(z)\equiv a_{1}

(a_{1}\in\mathbb{C})
. The result is obvious as
\Orb(C_{az+b},f)
has only one element, so
\left\{\mu y:y\in\Orb(C_{az+b},f),\mu\in\mathbb{C}\right\}
has dimension 1.

- Case 2:

f(z)=a_{2}e^{-\lambda_{2}z},a_{2}\neq 0
. Pick
g(z)\equiv b>0
. Since all
h\in\Orb(C_{az+b},f)
have non-zero constant part, it can be verified that taking
B=|b|\beta_{1}
works.

- Case 3:

f(z)=a_{1}+a_{2}e^{-\lambda_{2}z}

(a_{1},a_{2}\neq 0)
. Pick
g(z)=a_{1}+(a_{2}+1)e^{-\lambda_{2}z}
. Since
\lambda_{2}
is a non-zero initial point, following the proof of Proposition
5.6
(Case 2) yields
\|\mu C_{az+b}^{k}f-g\|^{2}\geq|a_{2}+1|^{2}\beta_{2}^{2}
for all
k\geq 1
. Furthermore the computation for
k=0
gives

\displaystyle\|\mu f-g\|^{2}\geq|\mu-1|^{2}|a_{1}|^{2}\beta_{1}^{2}+|(\mu-1)a_{2}+1|^{2}\beta_{2}^{2},

which is minimized in a similar manner as in Proposition
5.6
.

- Case 4:

\exists k\geq 3
such that
a_{k}\neq 0
. Pick
\kappa\neq 0,-a_{2}
and
g(z)=\kappa e^{-\lambda_{2}z}
. This is again similar to Proposition
5.6
.
∎

6.
Complex symmetry

In this section we investigate the complex symmetry property of
C_{\varphi}
. In this section we adopt the convention that

(6.1)

e_{n}(z)=\beta_{n}q_{n}(z)=e^{-\lambda_{n}z}.

6.1.
Composition conjugations

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
\mathbb{C}
-Hilbert space. A map
\mathcal{C}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
satisfying the conditions of

(1)

isometry:
\|\mathcal{C}x\|=\|x\|,\forall x\in\mathcal{H}
,

(2)

involutivity:
\mathcal{C}\mathcal{C}=I
, the identity map, and

(3)

anti-linearity:
\mathcal{C}(\lambda x+\mu y)=\overline{\lambda}\mathcal{C}x+\overline{\mu}\mathcal{C}y,\forall x,y\in\mathcal{H},\forall\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{C}
,

is called a conjugation on
\mathcal{H}
.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
\xi:\mathbb{C}_{L/2-\beta_{*}}\to\mathbb{C}_{L/2-\beta_{*}}
be an analytic function. Define

\displaystyle J_{\xi}f(z)=\overline{f(\overline{\xi(z)})},\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda).

If
J_{\xi}
satisfies the definition of a conjugation, then it is called a composition conjugation.

We will investigate composition conjugations induced by polynomial functions
\xi
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Assume that
\xi
is a polynomial. The following statements are equivalent.

(1)

J_{\xi}
is a conjugation on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
.

(2)

\xi(z)=z+ci,c\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

(1)\Longrightarrow(2)
: first let us note that since
J_{\xi}
is a conjugation on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
, then
\xi
induces a bounded composition operator on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Indeed, let
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Then the function

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{a_{n}}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}.

also belongs to
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
, and so
J_{\xi}\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Note now that

J_{\xi}\widetilde{f}(z)=\overline{\widetilde{f}(\overline{\xi(z)})}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}\xi(z)}=(C_{\xi}f)(z).

Therefore, we get
C_{\xi}f\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
as required. In particular, according to Theorem
3.14
,
\xi
should be of the form
\xi(z)=az+b
, where
a=0
and
\Re(b)>\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}
or
a\geq 1
and
\Re(b)\geq(1-a)(\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*})
.

First, we will show that
a\neq 0
. Argue by absurdity and assume that
a=0
. Then
\xi(z)=b
and
(J_{\xi}f)(z)=\overline{f(\overline{b})}
. Apply that equation to the vector of the orthonormal basis
q_{k}(z)=\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}e^{-\lambda_{k}z}
, which gives

(J_{\xi}q_{k})(z)=\frac{1}{\beta_{k}}e^{-\lambda_{k}z}.

Since
J_{\xi}
is an isometry, we get

\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{k}}e^{-\lambda_{k}\Re(b)}=1,

that is

-\Re(b)=\frac{\log(\beta_{k})}{\lambda_{k}}-\frac{\log(\beta_{1})}{\lambda_{k}},\qquad k\geq 2.

Taking the limit when
k\to\infty
, we get
\Re(b)=-\beta_{*}
. In particular,
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
and the condition
\Re(b)>\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}
gives the contradiction. Hence
a\neq 0
.

Let us show now that
a=1
and
b\in i\mathbb{R}
. Remind that
e_{n}(z)=e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Since
J_{\xi}
is conjugation on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
, we have
e_{n}=J_{\xi}^{2}e_{n}
, which gives

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda_{n}z}=

\displaystyle J_{\xi}(\overline{e_{n})(\overline{\xi(z)})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle J_{\xi}(e^{-\overline{\lambda_{n}}\xi(z)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda_{n}\overline{\xi(\overline{\xi(z)})}}.

By analyticity, we get that for every
n\geq 1
, there exists
k_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

-\lambda_{n}\overline{\xi(\overline{\xi(z)})}=-\lambda_{n}z+2i\pi k_{n}.

In particular, since
\lambda_{2}>\lambda_{1}\geq 0
, we deduce that

\overline{\xi(\overline{\xi(z)})}=z-\frac{2i\pi k_{2}}{\lambda_{2}}=z+ic_{1},

where
c_{1}=-2\pi\frac{k_{2}}{\lambda_{2}}\in\mathbb{R}
. Recall now that
\xi(z)=az+b
. An easy computation shows that
\overline{\xi(\overline{\xi(z)})}=a^{2}z+\bar{a}b+\bar{b}
. Hence, we see that
a^{2}=1
, that
a=1
(because
a\geq 0
) and
b+\bar{b}=ic_{1}
, that is
2\Re(b)=ic_{1}
. This implies that
\Re(b)=c_{1}=0
. Finally, we get that
\xi(z)=z+i\Im(b)=z+ic
, with
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

(2)\Longrightarrow(1)
: assume that
\xi(z)=z+ic
,
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
\Re(\xi(z))=\Re(z)
, it is clear that
\xi
maps
\mathbb{C}_{\frac{L}{2}-\beta_{*}}
into itself. Then
J_{\xi}
is well defined. Now, if
f(z)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
, then

(J_{\xi}f)(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{a_{n}}e^{-\lambda_{n}\xi(z)}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\overline{a_{n}}e^{-ic\lambda_{n}}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}.

Since
\left|\overline{a_{n}}e^{-ic\lambda_{n}}\right|=|a_{n}|
, we see that
J_{\xi}f\in\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Moreover,

\|J_{\xi}f\|^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\beta_{n}^{2}|a_{n}|^{2}=\|f\|^{2}.

Hence,
J_{\xi}
is an isometry from
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
into itself. It is of course antilinear. It remains to check that
J_{\xi}
is involutive. But, note that

(J_{\xi}e_{n})(z)=e^{-\lambda_{n}\xi(z)}=e^{-ic\lambda_{n}}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}=e^{-ic\lambda_{n}}e_{n}(z),

and so

J_{\xi}^{2}(e_{n})=J_{\xi}(e^{-ic\lambda_{n}}e_{n})=e^{ic\lambda_{n}}J_{\xi}(e_{n})=e^{ic\lambda_{n}}e^{-ic\lambda_{n}}e_{n}=e_{n}

By linearity and continuity of
J_{\xi}^{2}
, and density of
\bigvee(e_{n}:n\geq 1)
in
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
, we get that
J_{\xi}^{2}=I
. That proves that
J_{\xi}
is a conjugation on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
.
∎

6.2.
Complex symmetry

Having proven the form of composition conjugations
J_{\xi}
on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
, we can now consider the complex symmetry property of bounded composition operators on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
with respect to conjugations
J_{\xi}
.

Definition 6.4
.

Let
T
be a continuous linear operator mapping a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
to itself. Given that
\mathcal{C}
is a conjugation, we say that
T
is
\mathcal{C}
-symmetric
, or
complex symmetric with respect to
\mathcal{C}
, if

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}T\mathcal{C}=T^{*}.

If such a
\mathcal{C}
exists, we say that
T
is
complex symmetric
.

Recall that Proposition
4.7
provides explicitly the adjoint functions
C_{az+b}^{*}
. The following two results follow easily (cf.
[
9
]
).

Proposition 6.5
.

The following are true.

(1)

C_{z+b}^{*}=C_{z+\overline{b}}
.

(2)

If
a>1
, then
\ker C_{az+b}^{*}
is non-trivial.

Proof.

(1) follows directly from Proposition
4.7
(note that since
a=1
we have
m_{n}=n
). Let us now prove (2). If
\lambda_{1}\neq 0
, then
\lambda_{m_{1}}=a\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{1}
, and so
m_{1}>1
. It follows from (
4.2
) that since
j\mapsto m_{j}
is strictly increasing, we have
C_{az+b}^{*}e_{1}=0
, that is
e_{1}\in\ker C_{az+b}^{*}
. If
\lambda_{1}=0
, then
m_{1}=1
and arguing as before, we show that
e_{2}\in\ker C_{az+b}^{*}
.
∎

As already mentioned, composition operators associated to non-constant analytic symbols are injective. Thus we immediately get the following.

Corollary 6.6
.

Suppose
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
. Let
a>1
and suppose
C_{az+b}
is a bounded composition operator on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
. Then, no symbol
\psi
exists such that
C_{az+b}^{*}=C_{\psi}
(here,
\psi
need not be a polynomial).

Proof.

First note that since
a>1
, then there cannot exists a constant symbol
\psi
such that
C_{az+b}^{*}=C_{\psi}
. Then it remains to apply Proposition
6.5
(2) to get the result.
∎

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
\xi(z)=z+ci,c\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
b\in\mathbb{C}
and suppose that
\Re(b)\geq 0
. Then
C_{z+b}
is
J_{\xi}
-symmetric.

Proof.

Remind that
e_{n}(z)=e^{-\lambda_{n}z}
,
n\geq 1
. Since
a=1
,
m_{n}=n
,
n\geq 1
, and so, on one hand, we have

(C_{z+b}J_{\xi}e_{n})(z)=C_{z+b}\left(e^{-\lambda_{n}ci}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\right)=e^{-\lambda_{n}(b+ci)}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}.

On the other hand, by (
4.2
), we have

(J_{\xi}C_{z+b}^{*}e_{n})(z)=J_{\xi}\left(e^{-\lambda_{n}\overline{b}}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}\right)=e^{-\lambda_{n}(b+ci)}e^{-\lambda_{n}z}.

Thus
C_{z+b}J_{\xi}
and
J_{\xi}C_{z+b}^{*}
coincide on an orthogonal basis, whence
C_{z+b}J_{\xi}=J_{\xi}C_{z+b}^{*}
. Hence
C_{z+b}
is
J_{\xi}
-symmetric.
∎

The above proves that all bounded composition operators with symbol
C_{z+b}
is complex symmetric. To determine complex symmetry property of
C_{az+b}
when
a>1
, we recall the following result.

Lemma 6.8

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
T
be a complex symmetric operator. Then,
\dim\ker T^{*}=\dim\ker T
.

Now we may prove the following result.

Proposition 6.9
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
and
a>1
. Then,
C_{az+b}
is not complex symmetric on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
.

Proof.

It follows immediately from Lemma
6.8
and Proposition
6.5
and the fact that
C_{az+b}
is one to one.
∎

Finally we consider the constant case.

Lemma 6.10

(
[
11
]
)
.

Any rank one operator is complex symmetric.

We deduce immediately the following.

Corollary 6.11
.

Let
\lambda_{1}=0
and
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
. Then,
C_{b}
is complex symmetric on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
.

We can summarize all the previous results to obtain the following characterization of complex symmetric composition operators.

Theorem 6.12
.

Let
\beta_{*}\neq\pm\infty
and
C_{\varphi}
be a bounded composition operator on
\mathcal{H}(\beta,\Lambda)
.
Then:

(1)

If
\varphi(z)=z+b
, then
C_{\varphi}
is complex symmetric. More precisely,
C_{\varphi}
is
J_{\xi}
-symmetric for all composition conjugations
J_{\xi}
where
\xi(z)=z+ci,c\in\mathbb{R}
.

(2)

If
\varphi(z)=az+b
where
a\in R_{1}(\lambda_{n})
, then
C_{\varphi}
is never complex symmetric.

(3)

If
\lambda_{1}=0
, then
C_{b}
is complex symmetric.

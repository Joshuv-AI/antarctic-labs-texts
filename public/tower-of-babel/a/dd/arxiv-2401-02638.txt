Abstract
. Let Y Y be a random variable such that the moment generating function of Y Y exists in a neighborhood of the origin. The aim of this paper is to study probabilistic versions of the degenerate Fubini polynomials and the degenerate Fubini polynomials of order r r , namely the probabilisitc degenerate Fubini polynomials associated with Y Y and the probabilistic degenerate Fubini polynomials of order r r associated with Y Y . As special cases of Y Y , we treat the gamma random variable with parameters α , β > 0 \alpha,\beta>0 , the Poisson random variable with parameter α > 0 \alpha>0 , and the Bernoulli random variable with probability of success p p .

Introduction

In recent years, degenerate versions,
\lambda
-analogues and probabilistic versions of many special polynomials and numbers have been investigated by employing various methods such as generating functions, combinatorial methods, umbral calculus,
p
-adic analysis, differential equations, probability, special functions, analytic number theory and operator theory (see [11-16, 18-21] and the references therein).

Let
Y
be a random variable satisfying the moment condition (see (
17
)). The aim of this paper is to study probabilistic versions of the degenerate Fubini polynomials and the degenerate Fubini polynomials of order
r
, namely the probabilisitc degenerate Fubini polynomials associated with
Y
and the probabilistic degenerate Fubini polynomials of order
r
associated with
Y
. We derive some properties, explicit expressions, certain identities and recurrence relations for those polynomials and numbers. In addition, we consider the special cases that
Y
is the gamma random variable with parameters
\alpha,\beta>0
, the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha(>0)
, and the Bernoulli random variable with probability of success
p
.

{n\brace k}_{\lambda}

r

Y

Y
,
E[e^{tY}]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}E[Y^{n}],\quad(|t|<r)
, exists for some

. Let
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of mutually independent copies of the random variable
Y

S_{k}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k},\,\,(k\geq 1)
,   with
S_{0}=0

Y
and the probabilistic degenerate Bell polynomials associated with
Y
,
\phi_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
. Also, we remind the reader of the gamma random variable with parameters
\alpha,\beta>0
. Section 2 is the main result of this paper. Let
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1},\,\,S_{k},\,\,(k=0,1,\dots)
be as in the above. Then we first define the probabilistic degenerate Fubini polynomials associated with the random variable

p
. For the rest of this section, we recall the facts that are needed throughout this paper.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, the degenerate exponentials are defined by

(1)

e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x)_{k,\lambda}}{k!}t^{k},\quad e_{\lambda}(t)=e_{\lambda}^{1}(t),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [6-21]),

where

(2)

(x)_{0,\lambda}=1,\quad(x)_{n,.\lambda}=x(x-\lambda)\cdots\big(x-(n-1)\lambda\big),\quad(n\geq 1).

Note that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}e_{\lambda}^{x}(t)=e^{xt}.

The Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(3)

(x)_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{1}(n,k)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-3,5,24]),

where

(x)_{0}=1,\quad(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1),\quad(n\geq 1).

The Lah numbers are defined by

(4)

\langle x\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [5-24]),

where

\langle x\rangle_{0}=1,\quad\langle x\rangle_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1),\quad(n\geq 1).

By (
4
), we easily get

(5)

L(n,k)=\frac{n!}{k!}\binom{n-1}{k-1},\quad(n\geq k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [5-24]).

In [13], the degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(6)

(x)_{n,\lambda}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

It is well known that the degenerate Bell polynomials are defined by

(7)

e^{x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [12-14]).

Thus, by (
6
) and (
7
), we get

(8)

\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [12,17,21]).

The degenerate Fubini polynomials are defined by

(9)

F_{n,\lambda}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}k!x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [17-19,26]).

Thus, by (
9
), we get

(10)

\frac{1}{1-x(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}[4,19,21,26]).

From (
10
), we note that

(11)

\frac{1}{1-x}F_{n,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{x}{1-x}\bigg)=\bigg(x\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)_{n,\lambda}\frac{1}{1-x}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k)_{n,\lambda}x^{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [18]).

For
r\in\mathbb{N}
, the degenerate Fubini polynomials of order
r
are defined by

(12)

\bigg(\frac{1}{1-y(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}\bigg)^{r}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(y)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [8,9,16]).

Thus, by (
12
), we get

(13)

F_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{k+r-1}{k}y^{k}{n\brace k}_{\lambda}k!,\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [8,18,19,22,23]).

From (
12
), we have

(14)

\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x}\bigg)^{r+1}F_{n,\lambda}^{(r+1)}\bigg(\frac{x}{1-x}\bigg)=\bigg(x\frac{d}{dx}\bigg)_{n,\lambda}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x}\bigg)^{r+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{k+r}{r}(k)_{n,\lambda}x^{k},

where
n,r
are nonnegative integers.

For any integer
k\geq 0
, the partial Bell polynomials are given by

(15)

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n-k+1})\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [5])

where

(16)

\displaystyle B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n-k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}+l_{2}+\cdots+l_{n-k+1}=k\\
l_{1}+2l_{2}+\cdots+(n-k+1)l_{n-k+1}=n\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{l_{1}l_{2}!\cdots l_{n-k+1}!}\bigg(\frac{x_{1}}{1!}\bigg)^{l_{1}}\bigg(\frac{x_{2}}{2!}\bigg)^{l_{2}}\cdots\bigg(\frac{x_{n-k+1}}{(n-k+1)!}\bigg)^{l_{n-k+1}}.

Let
Y
be a random variable such that the moment generating function of
Y

(17)

E[e^{tY}]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E[Y^{n}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(|t|<r)\quad\textrm{exists for some $r>0$}.

Assume that
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1}
is a sequence of mutually independent copies of
Y
and
S_{k}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k},\ (k\geq 1)
with
S_{0}=0
.

The probabilistic degenerate Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with random variable
Y
are defined by

(18)

{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E\big[(S_{j})_{n,\lambda}\big],\quad(n\geq k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [15]).

By binomial inversion, the equation (
18
) is equivalent to

(19)

E\big[(S_{k})_{n,\lambda}\big]=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}j!{n\brace j}_{Y,\lambda},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [15]).

From (
18
), we note that

(20)

\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [15]).

In view of (
8
), the probabilistic degenerate Bell polynomials associated with
Y
are defined by

(21)

\phi_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [15,20]).

When
Y=1
, we have
\phi_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\phi_{n,\lambda}(x)
.

By (
21
), we get

(22)

e^{x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [15]).

We recall that
Y
is the gamma random variable with parameter
\alpha,\beta>0
if probability density function of
Y
is given by

f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{\beta}{\Gamma(\alpha)}e^{-\beta x}(\beta x)^{\alpha-1},&\textrm{if $x\geq 0$},\\
0,&\textrm{if $x<0$,}\end{array}\right.,\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [3,25,27]),

which is denoted by
Y\sim\Gamma(\alpha,\beta)
.

2.
Probabilistic degenerate Fubini polynomials associated with random variables

Let
(Y_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of mutually independent copies of random variable
Y
, and let

S_{0}=0,\quad S_{k}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k},\quad(k\in\mathbb{N}).

Now, we consider the
probabilistic degenerate Fubini polynomials associated with random variable
Y
which are given by

(23)

\frac{1}{1-x\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\big)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

For
Y=1
, we have
F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=F_{n,\lambda}(x),\ (n\geq 0)
. When
x=1
,
F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}=F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(1)
are called the
probabilistic degenerate Fubini numbers associated with random variable
Y
.

From (
23
), we note that

(24)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-x\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\big)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}\Big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}k!\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E\big[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)\big]-1\Big)^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}k!\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}k!{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
24
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}k!x^{k}.

By (
24
), we get

(25)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}=\frac{1}{1+x-xE[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1+x}\frac{1}{1-\frac{x}{1+x}E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]}=\frac{1}{1+x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+x}\bigg)^{k}\Big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1+x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+x}\bigg)^{k}E\big[(Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k})_{n,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1+x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+x}\bigg)^{k}E[(S_{k})_{n,\lambda}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
25
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\frac{1}{1+x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+x}\bigg)^{k}E[(S_{k})_{n,\lambda}].

In particular, for
Y=1
, we have

F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\frac{1}{1+x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{x}{1+x}\bigg)^{k}(k)_{n,\lambda}.

Let
Y\sim\Gamma(1,1)
. Then we have

(26)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}\Big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}e_{\lambda}^{y}(t)e^{-y}dy-1\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{y(\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\lambda t)-1)}dy-1\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k!x^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\frac{\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\lambda t)}{1-\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\lambda t)}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}k!x^{k}\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}L(l,k)\frac{1}{l!}\bigg(\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\lambda t)\bigg)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{l}k!x^{k}L(l,k)\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}\lambda^{n-l}S_{1}(n,l)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{l}k!x^{k}L(l,k)\lambda^{n-l}S_{1}(n,l)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
S_{1}(n,l)
are the Stirling numbers of the first kind. Here we should observe that, for all
t
with
|t|
small, we have

\big|\frac{1}{\lambda}\log(1+\lambda t)\big|<1,

since
|\frac{\log(1+x)}{x}|
is bounded on
(0,\infty)
.
Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
26
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Let
Y\sim\Gamma(1,1)
. Then we have

F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{l}k!\lambda^{n-l}L(l,k)S_{1}(n,l)x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

Now, we observe from (
21
) and Theorem 2.1 that

(27)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(xy)e^{-y}dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}x^{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{k}e^{-y}dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}x^{k}\Gamma(k+1)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}x^{k}k!

\displaystyle=F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x),\quad(n\geq 0).

Thus, from (
27
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(xy)e^{-y}dy=F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x).

From (
20
), we note that

(28)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\Big)^{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}E[(Y)_{i,\lambda}]\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}\Big(E[(Y)_{1,\lambda}],E[(Y)_{2,\lambda}],\cdots,E[(Y)_{n-k+1,\lambda}]\Big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
28
), we get

(29)

{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}=B_{n,k}\Big(E[(Y)_{1,\lambda}],E[(Y)_{2,\lambda}],\cdots,E[(Y)_{n-k+1,\lambda}]\Big),\quad(n\geq k\geq 0).

Hence

(30)

\phi_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}y^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}B_{n,k}\Big(E[(Y)_{1,\lambda}],E[(Y)_{2,\lambda}],\cdots,E[(Y)_{n-k+1,\lambda}]\Big)y^{k}.

By (
27
) and (
30
), we get

(31)

\displaystyle F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}B_{n,k}\Big(E[(Y)_{1,\lambda}],E[(Y)_{2,\lambda}],\cdots,E[(Y)_{n-k+1,\lambda}]\Big)\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{k}e^{-y}dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}k!B_{n,k}\Big(E[(Y)_{1,\lambda}],E[(Y)_{2,\lambda}],\cdots,E[(Y)_{n-k+1,\lambda}]\Big),\quad(n\geq 0).

Therefore, by (
31
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}k!B_{n,k}\Big(E[(Y)_{1,\lambda}],E[(Y)_{2,\lambda}],\cdots,E[(Y)_{n-k+1,\lambda}]\Big).

For
r\in\mathbb{N}
, the
probabilistic degenerate Fubini polynomials of order
r
associated with random variable
Y
are defined by

(32)

\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}\bigg)^{r}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
Y=1
, we have
F_{n,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}(x)=F_{n,\lambda}^{(r)}(x),\ (n\geq 0)
.

From (
20
) and (
32
), we note that

(33)

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}\bigg)^{r}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\binom{-r}{i}(-1)^{i}x^{i}\Big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\Big)^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\binom{r+i-1}{i}i!x^{i}\frac{1}{i!}\Big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\Big)^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\binom{r+i-1}{i}i!x^{i}\sum_{n=i}^{\infty}{n\brace i}_{Y,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{r+i-1}{i}i!x^{i}{n\brace i}_{Y,\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
32
) and (
33
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

F_{n,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{r+i-1}{i}i!{n\brace i}_{Y,\lambda}x^{i}.

By (
23
), we get

(34)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}-1=\frac{x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{xE[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}-\frac{x}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}

\displaystyle=x\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}E[(Y)_{k,\lambda}]\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}F_{l,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}-x\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

\displaystyle=x\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}E[(Y)_{k,\lambda}]\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}F_{l,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}E\big[(Y)_{k,\lambda}\big]F_{n-k,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
34
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 7
.

For
n\geq 1
, we have

F_{n,}^{Y}(x)=x\sum_{k=1}^{n}\binom{n}{k}E[(Y)_{k,\lambda}]F_{n-k,\lambda}^{Y}(x).

From (
23
), we note that

(35)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n+1,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{xE[Ye_{\lambda}^{Y-\lambda}(t)]}{(1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1))^{2}}=\frac{x}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}\frac{E[Ye_{\lambda}^{Y-\lambda}(t)]}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}

\displaystyle=x\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{i,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}F_{j,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}E[Y(Y-\lambda)_{m,\lambda}]\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=x\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}F_{i,\lambda}^{Y}(x)F_{k-i,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\bigg)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}E[(Y)_{m+1,\lambda}]\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}x\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\binom{n}{k}F_{i,\lambda}^{Y}(x)F_{k-i,\lambda}^{Y}(x)E[(Y)_{n-k+1,\lambda}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
26
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 8
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

F_{n+1,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=x\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom{k}{i}\binom{n}{k}F_{i,\lambda}^{Y}(x)F_{k-i,\lambda}^{Y}(x)E[(Y)_{n-k+1,\lambda}].

Now, we observe that

(36)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{d^{r}}{dx^{r}}F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{d^{r}}{dx^{r}}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}\bigg)=r!\frac{\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]\big)^{r}}{\big(1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)\big)^{r+1}}

\displaystyle=r!\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{i,\lambda}^{(r+1,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}E\big[(Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{r})_{k,\lambda}\big]\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(r!\sum_{i=0}^{n}F_{i,\lambda}^{(r+1,Y)}(x)E[(S_{r})_{n-i,\lambda}]\binom{n}{i}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
36
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 9
.

For
r,n\geq 0
, we have

\frac{d^{r}}{dx^{r}}F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=r!\sum_{i=0}^{n}F_{i,\lambda}^{(r+1,Y)}(x)E[(S_{r})_{n-i,\lambda}]\binom{n}{i}.

From (
19
), we note that

(37)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}E[(S_{i})_{n,\lambda}]x^{i}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x^{i}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E[(S_{i})_{n,\lambda}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x^{i}E[e_{\lambda}^{S_{i}}(t)]=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}x^{i}\Big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]\Big)^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-xE[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]}=\frac{1}{1-x}\frac{1}{1-\frac{x}{1-x}\big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1\big)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-x}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}\bigg(\frac{x}{1-x}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
37
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 10
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\frac{1}{1-x}F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}\bigg(\frac{x}{1-x}\bigg)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}E[(S_{i})_{n,\lambda}]x^{i}.

Taking
x=\frac{1}{2}
, we get

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}E[(S_{i})_{n,\lambda}]\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\bigg)^{i}=2F_{n,\lambda}^{Y},\quad(n\geq 0).

Let
Y
be the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha(>0)
. Then we have

(38)

E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e_{\lambda}^{n}(t)\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n!}e^{-\alpha}=e^{\alpha(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}.

From (
38
), we have

(39)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}=\frac{1}{1-x(e^{\alpha(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}-1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{i}(x)\alpha^{i}\frac{1}{i!}\big(e_{\lambda}(t)-1\big)^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{i}(x)\alpha^{i}\sum_{n=i}^{\infty}{n\brace i}_{\lambda}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{n}F_{i}(x){n\brace i}_{\lambda}\alpha^{i}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
F_{i}(x)
are the Fubini polynomials given by
\frac{1}{1-x(e^{t}-1)}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{i}(x)\frac{t^{i}}{i!}
.
Therefore, by (
39
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 11
.

Let
Y
be the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha(>0)
. Then we have

F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}F_{i}(x){n\brace i}_{\lambda}\alpha^{i},\quad(n\geq 0).

Let
Y
be the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha>0
. Then we have

(40)

\Big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]\Big)^{k}=e^{k\alpha(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}(k\alpha)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

and

(41)

\Big(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]\Big)^{k}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E[(S_{k})_{n,\lambda}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
40
) and (
41
), we get

(42)

E\big[(S_{k})_{n,\lambda}\big]=\phi_{n,\lambda}(k\alpha),\quad(n\geq 0).

From Theorem 2.10 and (
42
), we have

(43)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}(k\alpha)x^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}E\Big[(S_{k})_{n,\lambda}\Big]x^{k}=\frac{1}{1-x}F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}\bigg(\frac{x}{1-x}\bigg).

Therefore, by (
43
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 12
.

Let
Y
be the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha(>0)
. For
n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}(k\alpha)x^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}E\Big[(S_{k})_{n,\lambda}\Big]x^{k}=\frac{1}{1-x}F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}\bigg(\frac{x}{1-x}\bigg).

By using Theorem 2.6 and (
19
), we note that

(44)

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x}\bigg)^{r+1}F_{n,\lambda}^{(r+1,Y)}\bigg(\frac{x}{1-x}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x}\bigg)^{r+1}\sum_{l=0}^{n}{n\brace l}_{Y,\lambda}\binom{l+r}{l}l!\bigg(\frac{x}{1-x}\bigg)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n}{n\brace l}_{Y,\lambda}l!\binom{l+r}{l}x^{l}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x}\bigg)^{l+r+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{n\brace l}_{Y,\lambda}l!\binom{l+r}{l}\binom{k+l+r}{k}x^{k+l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{k=l}^{\infty}{n\brace l}_{Y,\lambda}l!\binom{l+r}{l}\binom{k+r}{k-l}\bigg)x^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{k+r}{k}x^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{k}{n\brace l}_{Y,\lambda}(k)_{l}+\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\binom{k+r}{k}x^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{n}{n\brace l}_{Y,\lambda}(k)_{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{k+r}{k}x^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{k}{n\brace l}_{Y,\lambda}(k)_{l}+\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\binom{k+r}{k}x^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{k}{n\brace l}_{Y,\lambda}(k)_{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{k+r}{k}x^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{k}{n\brace l}_{Y,\lambda}(k)_{l}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{k+r}{k}x^{k}E\Big[(S_{k})_{n,\lambda}\Big].

Therefore, by (
44
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 13
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x}\bigg)^{r+1}F_{n,\lambda}^{(r+1,Y)}\bigg(\frac{x}{1-x}\bigg)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{k+r}{k}x^{k}E\Big[(S_{k})_{n,\lambda}\Big].

When
Y=1
, we have

\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x}\bigg)^{r+1}F_{n,\lambda}^{(r+1)}\bigg(\frac{x}{1-x}\bigg)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{k+r}{k}x^{k}(k)_{n,\lambda}.

Now, we observe from (
21
) and Theorem 2.6 that

(45)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{r-1}\phi_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(xy)e^{-y}dy=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}x^{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{r+k-1}e^{-y}dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}x^{k}\Gamma(r+k)=\Gamma(r)\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{Y,\lambda}x^{k}\binom{r+k-1}{k}k!

\displaystyle=\Gamma(r)F_{n,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}(x),\quad(r\in\mathbb{N}).

Therefore, by (
45
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 14
.

For
n\geq 0
and
r\geq 1
, we have

F_{n,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(r)}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{r-1}\phi_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(xy)e^{-y}dy.

From (
32
) and Theorem 2.14, we have

(46)

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}\bigg)^{r}=\frac{1}{\Gamma(r)}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{r-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(xy)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}e^{-y}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(r)}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{r-1}e^{xy(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}e^{-y}dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(r)}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{r-1}e^{y(xE[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1-x)}dy.

By (
46
), we get

\frac{1}{\Gamma(r)}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{r-1}e^{y(xE[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1-x)}dy=\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}\bigg)^{r},

where
r
is a positive integer.

We observe that

(47)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n+1}^{(r,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(\frac{1}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}\bigg)^{r}=\frac{rxE[e_{\lambda}^{Y-\lambda}(t)Y]}{\big(1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)\big)^{r+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{rx}{\big(1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)\big)^{r}}\frac{1}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}E[e_{\lambda}^{Y-\lambda}(t)Y]

\displaystyle=rx\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}F_{l,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{i,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{i}}{i!}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}E[(Y)_{j+1,\lambda}]\frac{t^{j}}{j!}

\displaystyle=rx\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}F_{l,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}(x)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}F_{k-j,\lambda}^{Y}(x)E[(Y)_{j+1,\lambda}]\bigg)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}rx\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{n}{k}\binom{k}{j}F_{n-k,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}(x)F_{k-j,\lambda}^{Y}(x)E[(Y)_{j+1,\lambda}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
47
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 15
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

F_{n+1,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}(x)=rx\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{n}{k}\binom{k}{j}F_{n-k,\lambda}^{(r,Y)}(x)F_{k-j,\lambda}^{Y}(x)E[(Y)_{j+1,\lambda}].

Let
Y
be the Bernoulli random variable with probability of success
p
. Then we have

(48)

E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]=\sum_{k=0}^{1}e_{\lambda}^{k}(t)p(k)=1-p+pe_{\lambda}(t)=1+p(e_{\lambda}(t)-1).

By (
48
), we get

(49)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-x(E[e_{\lambda}^{Y}(t)]-1)}=\frac{1}{1-xp(e_{\lambda}(t)-1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n,\lambda}(xp)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
49
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 16
.

Let
Y
be the Bernoulli random variable with probability of success
p
. For
n\geq 0
, we have

F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=F_{n,\lambda}(xp).

3.
Conclusion

In this paper, we studied by using generating functions the probabilistic degenerate Fubini polynomials associated with
Y
and the probabilistic degenerate Fubini polynomials of order
r
associated with
Y
, as probabilistic versions of the degenerate Fubini polynomials and the degenerate Fubini polynomials of order
r
, respectively. Here
Y
is a random variable such that the moment generating function of
Y
exists in a neighborhood of the origin. In more detail, we derived several explicit expressions of
F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
(see Theorems 2.1, 2.2, 2.4) and those of
F_{n,\lambda}^{r,Y}(x)
(see Theorems 2.6, 2.14). We obtained a recurrence relations for
F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
(see Theorem 2.7), and another one (see Theorem 2.8) together with its generalization (see Theorem 2.15). We expressed the
r
th derivative of
F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
in terms of
F_{i,\lambda}^{(r+1,Y)}(x)
(see Theorem 2.9). We showed the identity
\frac{1}{1-x}F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}\bigg(\frac{x}{1-x}\bigg)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}E[(S_{i})_{n,\lambda}]x^{i}
(see Theorem 2.10) and its generalization (see Theorem 2.13). We deduced an explicit expression for
F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)
when
Y\sim\Gamma(1,1)
(see Theorem 2.3) and also that when
Y
is the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha
(see Theorem 2.11). We proved that
\frac{1}{1-x}F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}\bigg(\frac{x}{1-x}\bigg)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,\lambda}(k\alpha)x^{k}
when
Y
is the Poisson random variable with parameter
\alpha
(see Theorem 2.12). We showed
F_{n,\lambda}^{Y}(x)=F_{n,\lambda}(xp)
when

Y
be the Bernoulli random variable with probability of success
p
(see Theorem 2.16).

As one of our future projects, we would like to continue to study degenerate versions,
\lambda
-analogues and probabilistic versions of many special polynomials and numbers and to find their applications to physics, science and engineering as well as to mathematics.

On the capacity of a quantum perceptron for storing biased patterns
Fabio Benatti, Giovanni Gramegna, Stefano Mancini, Gibbs Nwemadji

Abstract
Although different architectures of quantum perceptrons have been recently put forward, the capabilities of such quantum devices versus their classical counterparts remain debated. Here, we consider random patterns and targets independently distributed with biased probabilities and investigate the storage capacity of a continuous quantum perceptron model that admits a classical limit, thus facilitating the comparison of performances. Such a more general context extends a previous study of the quantum storage capacity where using statistical mechanics techniques in the limit of a large number of inputs, it was proved that no quantum advantages are to be expected concerning the storage properties. This outcome is due to the fuzziness inevitably introduced by the intrinsic stochasticity of quantum devices. We strengthen such an indication by showing that the possibility of indefinitely enhancing the storage capacity for highly correlated patterns, as it occurs in a classical setting, is instead prevented at the quantum level.



Keywords: quantum perceptron, storage capacity, quantum neural networks

Introduction

Machine learning aims at building methods that are able to make predictions or decisions based on sample data, without being explicitly programmed to do so. Quantum information theory studies the storage and transmission of information encoded in quantum states. Nowadays these two disciplines are becoming intertwined giving rise to the field of quantum machine learning.

The flow of ideas runs both ways: on the one hand applications of machine learning techniques are envisaged to analyze quantum systems

Here, by referring to the continuous variable quantum perceptron model introduced in

classification are taken to be independent and identically distributed (
i.i.d.

-1\leq m_{\mathrm{in}}\leq 1
for the patterns and
-1\leq m_{\mathrm{out}}\leq 1
for their classification. Such a model
admits the classical perceptron as a classical limit, thus enabling a direct comparison of the storage performances in the two cases.
For classical perceptrons, simultaneously large biases for patterns and output classification allow to greatly enhance the storage capacity, which diverges when
m_{\mathrm{in}}=m_{\mathrm{out}}=m\to 1

We show that this possibility is prevented at the quantum level.
Moreover, we also find that, when the biases

However, also in the quantum setting, when the output correlations are maximal, that is when

m_{\mathrm{in}}
, exactly as in the classical case. The dependence of the quantum storage capacity on
m_{\mathrm{in}}

in such a case is through the velocity with which the limit behaviour is reached. Overall, the performances of the continuous quantum model remain below the classical ones.
These results thus corroborate those found in Ref.
[
19

with unbiased patterns. They confirm that, at the level of a
simple, that is one-layer, quantum perceptron, the uncertainties brought about by pattern encoding via Gaussian states and homodyne measurements cannot be counteracted by linear super-positions of pattern states.

2
A continuous quantum perceptron model

The continuous variable model of a quantum perceptron proposed in Ref.
[
20
]

is characterized by
N
bosonic input modes and one bosonic output mode. The components
x_{j}
of an input pattern
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
are encoded by states of the form

|\psi_{j}\rangle=\frac{1}{(2\pi\sigma^{2}_{j})^{1/4}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}q_{j}\ {\exp\left(-\frac{(q_{j}-x_{j})^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}_{j}}\right)}|q_{j}\rangle,

(1)

which are Gaussian weighted normalized super-positions of pseudo-eigenstates
|q_{j}\rangle
of position-like operators
q_{j}
, centered around the pattern components
x_{j}
with widths
\sigma_{j}
.
As a result, a pattern
\boldsymbol{x}
is encoded into

|\Psi\rangle=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{N}|\psi_{j}\rangle.

(2)

Such a state is then given as input to a quantum circuit which first operates with a series of independent squeezing
operators

S_{j}(r_{j})=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\,r_{j}\,(q_{j}p_{j}+p_{j}q_{j})}\ ,\quad r_{j}\in\mathbb{R}\ ,\quad{\rm e}^{-2r_{j}}=w_{j}\ ,

(3)

where
p_{j}
is a momentum-like operator conjugated to
q_{j}
(
[q_{j},p_{j}]=\mathrm{i}
) and
r_{j}
is the squeezing parameter implementing the weight
w_{j}
1
1

1

In order to implement negative weights, a phase shift gate
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{2}(q_{j}^{2}+p_{j}^{2})}
is operated after the squeezing.
. Notice that

S_{j}(r_{j})|q_{j}\rangle=\sqrt{w_{j}}\,|w_{j}q_{j}\rangle\ .

(4)

Then, the circuit consists of entangling Controlled Addition gates
\mathrm{CX}
on pairs of consecutive modes:

{\mathrm{CX}_{j,j+1}}:=\exp\left(-\mathrm{i}\,q_{j}\otimes p_{j+1}\right)\ ,\qquad{\mathrm{CX}_{j,j+1}}\,|q_{j},q_{j+1}\rangle=|q_{j},q_{j}+q_{j+1}\rangle\ .

(5)

Their combined action on the attenuated multi-mode position eigenstates gives

\begin{split}|w_{1}q_{1},w_{2}q_{2},\ldots,w_{N}q_{N}\rangle&\rightarrow|w_{1}q_{1},w_{1}q_{1}+w_{2}q_{2},\ldots,w_{N}q_{N}\rangle\rightarrow\ldots\\
&\ldots\rightarrow|w_{1}q_{1},w_{1}q_{1}+w_{2}q_{2},\ldots,\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{j}q_{j}\rangle\ .\end{split}

(6)

In such a way, the amplitude associated to the last mode position eigenstate has the form of a Gaussian distribution centered around
\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{j}x^{\mu}_{j}
:

\psi_{\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}}(s)=\frac{1}{(2\pi\|\boldsymbol{w}\|^{2}\,\sigma^{2})^{1/4}}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu})^{2}}{4\|\boldsymbol{w}\|^{2}\,\sigma^{2}}\right),\quad\|\boldsymbol{w}\|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}w^{2}_{j}\ ,

(7)

where, for sake of simplicity, we have set
\sigma_{j}^{2}=\sigma^{2}
for all
j
and thus encoded the input patterns by Gaussian states of the same width.
Finally, homodyne detection operated on the last mode position-like quadrature yields a value
s
with
probability density

P_{\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\sigma}(s)=|\psi_{\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}}(s)|^{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\|\boldsymbol{w}\|\sigma}\exp\left(-\frac{(s-\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu})^{2}}{2\|\boldsymbol{w}\|^{2}\sigma^{2}}\right).

(8)

Remark 1

With a slight modification of the above protocol, it is possible to obtain a description of such a continuous variable quantum perceptron as controlled unitary acting on the tensor product
\mathscr{H}=\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{in}}\otimes\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{out}}
where
\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{in}}
is the Hilbert space of the
N
bosonic modes encoding the input, while
\mathscr{H}_{\mathrm{out}}
is the Hilbert space of the additional ancilla mode storing the output. Then, the present model can be connected with other models investigated in the literature, in particular
[
24
]
, where it was pointed out that a perceptron acting as a controlled unitary has as particular cases also the models considered in
[
25
,
26
]
. Actually, the action of the continuous variable quantum
perceptron here investigated can be described with the unitary

U(\boldsymbol{w}):=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\mathrm{CX}_{j,\mathrm{out}}S_{j}(r_{j}),

(9)

with
S_{j}(r)
as in Eq. (
3
), while
\mathrm{CX}_{j,\mathrm{out}}=\exp\left(-\mathrm{i}\,q_{j}\otimes p_{\mathrm{out}}\right)
is the controlled addition gate involving the
j
-th bosonic mode of the input and the output mode.

Classically, the classification of a pattern
\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}
as
\pm 1
by a weight vector
\boldsymbol{w}
is obtained by checking
the sign of
\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}
. Then, a correct classification relative to a prescribed target

\xi^{\mu}=\pm 1
is obtained when
\xi^{\mu}\,\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}\geq\kappa\|\boldsymbol{w}\|
where
\kappa
is a stabilizing threshold. It renders the classification more robust against noise affecting the weights that, when
\kappa=0
, might make
\xi^{\mu}\,\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}
jump from positive to negative values and vice versa. In the case of the quantum perceptron model outlined above, a pattern
\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}
is classified as
\xi^{\mu}=+1
(resp.
\xi^{\mu}=-1
) if the measurement outcome is above the threshold
\kappa\|\boldsymbol{w}\|
(resp. below
-\kappa\|\boldsymbol{w}\|
), while the pattern is not classified when the measurement outcome is in between
(-\kappa\|\boldsymbol{w}\|,\kappa\|\boldsymbol{w}\|)
.
Therefore, even when classically
\mathrm{sign}(\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}-\kappa\|\boldsymbol{w}\|)=+1
, quantumly, the pattern is classified as
-1
if
s<-\kappa\,\|\boldsymbol{w}\|
and such errors occur with probability density

P_{\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\sigma}(s)
.

Consequently, the inherent randomness due to the quantum encoding of the patterns is such that the correct classification
of pattern
\mu
becomes a binary stochastic variable with probability distribution given by

{R_{\kappa,\sigma}(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu})}={\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}}\mathrm{d}s\ P_{\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\sigma}(s)\,\Theta\left(\xi^{\mu}\frac{s}{\|\boldsymbol{w}\|}-\kappa\right),

(10)

where
\Theta(\cdot)
denotes the Heaviside function. Finally, an ancilla mode is appended to the initialized
N
ones and its state changed according to the actual outcome
of a suitable homodyne measurement. The result of the measurement can then be used, for instance, to implement the non-linear
ReLu
activation function as in Ref.
[
20
]
.

One of the advantages of the continuous quantum model just presented is that it allows to recover the functioning of the classical perceptron when
\sigma\to 0
, i.e.
by encoding a pattern
\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}
into the position-like pseudo-autokets
|x^{\mu}_{1},x^{\mu}_{2},\ldots,x^{\mu}_{N}\rangle
.
Indeed, in this limit the Gaussian probability density in Eq. (
10
) becomes a Dirac delta centered around
\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}
.

3
Statistical Mechanics Derivation of the Storage Capacity

3.1
Gardner’s approach

According to Gardner’s statistical approach
[
13
]
, the optimal storage capacity of a simple perceptron can be obtained from the fraction of weights which correctly reproduces the desired input-output
relations normalized to the total volume of allowed vectors
\boldsymbol{w}
. Indeed, the storage capacity is defined as the

critical
value
\alpha_{c}
of the ratio

\alpha\equiv\frac{p}{N},

(11)

of the number of patterns
p
to the dimension of the input space
N
such that the storage condition

\xi^{\mu}\frac{\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}}{\|\boldsymbol{w}\|}\geq\kappa

(12)

cannot be satisfied anymore.

In fact, by increasing the number of patterns, the volume of
vectors
\boldsymbol{w}
realizing the condition (
12
) typically shrinks, and the relative volume of
such weights vanishes. Then, it is exactly the limit of vanishing relative volume
that leads to the storage capacity of the perceptron.

We shall consider weights for which
\|\boldsymbol{w}\|^{2}=N
so that their components are typically of order
1
.
Then, the fraction of weights
\boldsymbol{w}
of length
\sqrt{N}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
that classify
p
binary patterns
\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}\in\{+1,-1\}^{N}
, up to an error
\epsilon
, is given by:

V^{Q}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu},\kappa,\sigma,\epsilon):=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}^{N}\boldsymbol{w}\ \delta(||\boldsymbol{w}||^{2}-N)\prod_{\mu=1}^{p}\Theta\Big({R_{\kappa,\sigma}(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu})}-1+\epsilon\Big).

(13)

where the total volume of the space of the weights is

{Z_{N}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}^{N}\boldsymbol{w}\ \delta(||\boldsymbol{w}||^{2}-N)\mathrel{{\mathop{\simeq}\limits}}\sqrt{\frac{(2\pi e)^{N}}{4\pi N}}\ ,

(14)

namely the volume of the sphere of radius
\sqrt{N}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The relation to the classification of the pattern
\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}
is due to the fact that Eq.(
8
) represents the probability distribution of the measurement outcomes of the quantum perceptron encoding the patterns

\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}
into Gaussian states of variance
\sigma
.

Remark 2

The probability
{R_{\kappa,\sigma}(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu})}
depends on the pattern
\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}
, on the target classification
\xi^{\mu}
, on the weights
\boldsymbol{w}
, on the threshold parameter
\kappa
and on the Gaussian width
\sigma
.
Therefore, the fraction of volume
V_{N}^{Q}
depends on patterns, targets, threshold, width and also on the allowed statistical error
\epsilon
: when the width
\sigma
vanishes, from the distributional limit

\lim_{\sigma\to 0}P_{\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{{x}}^{\mu},\sigma}(s)=\delta(s-\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu})\ ,

(15)

one recovers the expression of the fraction of weights of the classical perceptron

{V^{C}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu},\kappa)}=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}^{N}\boldsymbol{w}\ \delta(\|\boldsymbol{w}\|^{2}-N)\prod_{\mu=1}^{p}\Theta\left(\xi^{\mu}\frac{\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}}{\|\boldsymbol{w}\|}-\kappa\right).

(16)

Notice that this expression does not depend on the statistical error
\epsilon
that needs to be introduced in the quantum setting. Indeed, in this latter case, the measured parameter
s
is statistically distributed around the classical scalar product
\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}/\|\boldsymbol{w}\|
. Therefore,
{R_{\kappa,\sigma}(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu})}
cannot be equal to
1
unless the Gaussian distribution becomes a Dirac delta peaked around it. Note that the statistical error
\epsilon
is an upper bound to the perceptron allowed errors. The value
\epsilon=1/2
for the bound to the errors is a particular one: in such a case weights can provide classifications with equal probability of being right or wrong. Therefore, at
\epsilon=1/2
, weights are selected without further constraints beside the classical ones. Then, as far as the storage capacity is concerned, the quantum perceptron is expected to behave classically
at
\epsilon=1/2
, in spite of the quantum pattern encoding.

In analogy with the partition function of statistical mechanics, we take
{\log V^{Q}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu},\kappa,\sigma,\epsilon)}
as the relevant quantity, since it has the important property of being
self-averaging
, i.e. its average
{\braket{\log V^{Q}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu},\kappa,\sigma,\epsilon)}}
is a good representative of its typical behaviour for random choices of input patterns and targets
[
14
,
15
,
16
]
. In particular, this average will be computed considering the components of the input patterns as well as targets, to be binary stochastic variables distributed according to

\Pr(x^{\mu}_{j}=\pm 1)=\frac{1\pm m_{\mathrm{in}}}{2}\ ,\qquad\Pr(\xi^{\mu}=\pm 1)=\frac{1\pm m_{\mathrm{out}}}{2}\ .

(17)

The parameters
-1\leq m_{\mathrm{in}},m_{\mathrm{out}}\leq 1
measure the bias between the binary values of patterns and targets,
respectively, and thus of their correlations. The smaller the bias is, the greater the independence of their two
possible values.

Following the classical approach by Gardner we will derive a critical value
\alpha_{c}^{Q}

such that for
\alpha<\alpha_{c}^{Q}
we obtain a finite value (potentially vanishing) for the limit

\lim_{\mathrel{{\mathop{{p/N=\alpha}}\limits}}}\frac{\langle\log V^{Q}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu},\kappa,\sigma,\epsilon))\rangle}{N}\ ,

(18)

while for
\alpha>\alpha_{c}^{Q}
:

\lim_{\mathrel{{\mathop{{p/N=\alpha}}\limits}}}\frac{\langle\log V^{Q}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu},\kappa,\sigma,\epsilon)\rangle}{N}=-\infty\ .

(19)

3.2
Replica method and saddle point equations

The quenched average appearing in the limit (
18
) can be computed by means of the replica-trick
[
13
,
27
,
28
]
:

\braket{\log{V^{Q}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu},\kappa,\sigma,\epsilon)}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow 0}\frac{\braket{{[V^{Q}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu},\kappa,\sigma,\epsilon)]}^{n}}-1}{n}=\lim_{n\rightarrow 0}\frac{\log\braket{{[V^{Q}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu},\kappa,\sigma,\epsilon)]}^{n}}}{n}.

(20)

The relevant quantity
\braket{{[V^{Q}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu},\kappa,\sigma,\epsilon)]}^{n}}
involves
n
replicas indexed by the subscript
\gamma
:

\langle{[V^{Q}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu},\kappa,\sigma,\epsilon)]}^{n}\rangle=\frac{1}{Z_{N}^{n}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{nN}}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}^{nN}\boldsymbol{W}~\delta_{N}^{(n)}(\|\boldsymbol{W}\|^{2})}\,\left\langle\prod_{\gamma=1}^{n}\prod_{\mu=1}^{p}\Theta(R^{\mu}_{\gamma}-1+\epsilon)\right\rangle_{\boldsymbol{x},\xi},

(21)

where, for the sake of compactness, we introduced the symbols
\boldsymbol{W}=(\boldsymbol{w}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{w}_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{nN}
and

\mathop{}\mathrm{d}^{nN}\boldsymbol{W}\equiv\prod_{\gamma=1}^{n}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}^{N}\boldsymbol{w}_{\gamma}\ ,\quad\delta_{N}^{(n)}(\|\boldsymbol{w}\|^{2})\equiv\prod_{\gamma=1}^{n}\delta(\|\boldsymbol{w}_{\gamma}\|^{2}-N)\ .

(22)

Moreover, in Eq.(
21
), it is made explicit that the mean value is computed with respect to the patterns

\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu}
and targets
\xi^{\mu}
,
\mu=1,\ldots,p
.

The lengthy calculations of the mean value in Eq.(
21
) by means of the replica-symmetric ansatz and of the saddle point approximation are reported in Appendix
A
.

The replica method introduces several order parameters, the most important one being the average overlap of two randomly chosen weights
\boldsymbol{w}_{\gamma}
and
\boldsymbol{w}_{\delta}
in different replicas:

q_{\gamma\delta}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}w_{j}^{\gamma}w_{j}^{\delta}\ .

(23)

In the replica symmetric ansatz it is assumed that for the solution of the saddle point equations, the average overlap is the same for each pair of replicas, i.e.
q_{\gamma\delta}=q
for all
\gamma\neq\delta
. Notice that by increasing the ratio
p/N
, the number of weights satisfying (
12
) diminishes, hence their average
overlap increases. The critical value of

\alpha_{c}
both in the classical and the quantum scenario is then obtained in the limit of maximal overlap
q\to 1
.

Eventually, one arrives at the following equation that must be satisfied by
the critical ratio
\alpha_{c}^{Q}
of number of patterns to weight dimension, which according to (
11
) defines the quantum storage capacity:

\alpha_{c}^{Q}\Bigg[\frac{1+m_{\mathrm{out}}}{2}\int_{a_{-}(M)}^{+\infty}\mathop{}\mathrm{D}x\,(x-a_{-}(M))^{2}\,+\,\frac{1-m_{\mathrm{out}}}{2}\int_{a_{+}(M)}^{+\infty}\mathop{}\mathrm{D}x\,(x-a_{+}(M))^{2}\Bigg]=1.

(24)

In the above expression,

a_{\pm}(M)\equiv-\frac{\tilde{\kappa}\pm m_{\mathrm{in}}M}{\sqrt{1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2}}}\ ,\quad\mathop{}\mathrm{D}x\equiv\frac{\mathop{}\mathrm{d}x}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-x^{2}/2}\ ,

(25)

where

\tilde{\kappa}:=\kappa+\sigma\Phi^{-1}(1-\epsilon),

(26)

and
\Phi^{-1}
is the inverse function of

\Phi(x)\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{x}\mathop{}\mathrm{D}u=\frac{1+{\rm erf}(x/\sqrt{2})}{2}\quad\hbox{with}\quad\hbox{ erf}(x):=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{x}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}u\,{\rm e}^{-u^{2}}\ ,

(27)

while the quantity
M
satisfies

(1+m_{\mathrm{out}})\int_{a_{-}(M)}^{+\infty}\mathop{}\mathrm{D}x\,(x-a_{-}(M))=(1-m_{\mathrm{out}})\int_{a_{+}(M)}^{+\infty}\mathop{}\mathrm{D}x\,(x-a_{+}(M))\ .

(28)

Thus, in order to compute
\alpha_{c}^{Q}
from (
24
), one
has to first solve (
28
) in terms of
M
.

Remark 3

Notice that when
m_{\mathrm{in}}=0
, that is when the patterns are unbiased, we have
a_{+}(M)=a_{-}(M)
so that Eq. (
28
) can be satisfied only for
m_{\mathrm{out}}=0
, and the storage capacity is fixed by (
24
) only, which coincides with the expression found in
[
19
]
. This is due to the fact that a perceptron cannot match unbiased patterns with biased classifications. In fact, considering the mapping realized by a perceptron with weights
\boldsymbol{w}
, one finds that for a random input
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with independent components distributed according to
\Pr(x_{j}=\pm 1)=1/2
for each
j=1,\dots,N
, the distribution of the output
\sigma
is given by:

\Pr(\sigma=+1)=\Pr(\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}\geqslant 0)=\Pr(\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}\leqslant 0)=\Pr(\sigma=-1),

(29)

which implies
\Pr(\sigma=\pm 1)=1/2
(note that
\Pr(\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{x}=0)
for all
\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
except for those belonging to a set with zero Lebesgue measure on the sphere with radius
\sqrt{N}
).

In practice, the combined effects of quantum pattern encodings and measurements is to replace the classical
stabilizing threshold
\kappa
in
\tilde{\kappa}
defined in (
26
). Then, the classical storage capacity obtains not only by eliminating the errors due to quantum pattern encoding, that is by letting
\sigma\rightarrow 0
, but also, confirming the argument in Remark
2
,
when
\sigma\neq 0
, so that the pattern encoding is not sharp and carries quantum fuzziness; however,
\epsilon=1/2
so that
\Phi^{-1}(1-\epsilon)=0
and
\tilde{\kappa}=\kappa
.

5
Discussion and conclusion

Summarizing, we studied the storage capacity of the continuous variable model of quantum perceptron presented in Ref.
[
20
]
in the presence of a bias in the distribution of the patterns and their corresponding classifications. Besides the advantage of allowing an almost entirely analytical study, such a model admits the classical perceptron as a classical limit, thus allowing for a direct comparison of the
storage performances in the two cases.
We found that the additional randomness introduced in the quantum model gives rise to an effective increment in the stability parameter used in Gardner’s statistical approach,
\kappa\rightarrow\tilde{\kappa}
, which gives rise to several peculiar features that are not observed in the classical case with zero stability.

For instance, the possibility of indefinitely enhancing the storage capacity by increasing the bias of the patterns and their classifications is prevented at the quantum level.
Moreover, in contrast to the classical case, when the bias of the patterns and the bias in their corresponding classifications are varied separately, a dependence of the storage capacity on the input patterns’ bias appears even when the stability parameter reduces to zero.
Overall, however, the performance of the quantum perceptron model remains below that of the classical one.
This is likely due to the fact that the considered quantum model introduces two sources of randomness: one due to the encoding
of patterns by means of non-zero width Gaussian states and another one due to the final measurement operation implementing
the classical non-linear activation function.
It is worth stressing that the modification in the effective threshold
\tilde{\kappa}-\kappa=\sigma\Phi^{-1}(1-\epsilon)
contains both the contribution of randomness coming from the width
\sigma
of the Gaussian encoding of the patterns, and the statistical error due to the quantum measurement
\epsilon
: as a consequence, the worsening of the quantum storage capacity with respect to the classical one cannot be ascribed to only one of them.
These results clearly point to the necessity of considering multi-layer quantum perceptrons to hope for quantum advantages of the sort coming from linear superpositions and entanglement.

and considering it as a vector in

\mathbb{C}^{n^{2}+2n}
, one expands
G(\mathbf{z})\equiv G(\mathbf{E},\mathbf{F},\mathbf{L},\mathbf{M},\mathbf{Q})
around the stationary point
\mathbf{z}^{0}=(\mathbf{E}^{0},\mathbf{F}^{0},\mathbf{L}^{0},\mathbf{M}^{0},\mathbf{Q}^{0})
such that
\displaystyle\frac{\partial G(\mathbf{z}^{0})}{\partial z_{k}}=0
:

G(\mathbf{z})\simeq G(\mathbf{z}^{0})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{n^{2}+2n}\frac{\partial^{2}G(\mathbf{z}^{0})}{\partial z_{j}\partial z_{k}}\,(z_{j}-z^{0}_{j})\,(z_{k}-z^{0}_{k})\ .

Let
G^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{z}^{0})\equiv\left[\frac{\partial^{2}G(\mathbf{z}^{0})}{\partial z_{j}\partial z_{k}}\right]
be the
Hessian
(n^{2}+2n)\times(n^{2}+2n)
matrix at the stationary point. If such a matrix is negative semi-definite, by suitably deforming the integration paths into the complex domain, one can perform
n^{2}+2n
Gaussian integrations by rescaling with
\sqrt{N}
the corresponding integration variables and approximate:

\langle[V^{Q}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu},\kappa,\sigma,\epsilon)]^{n}\rangle{\simeq}\frac{1}{Z_{N}^{n}}\,\frac{1}{N^{n}}\,\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{|\det G^{\prime\prime}(\mathbf{z}^{0})|}}\right)^{n^{2}+2n}\ \mathrm{e}^{NG(\mathbf{z}^{0})}\ .

(58)

From Eqs. (
20
) and (
18
), one needs to control the behaviour of the ratio
\displaystyle\frac{1}{nN}\log\langle[(V^{Q}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu},\kappa,\sigma,\epsilon)]^{n}\rangle
for
n\to 0^{+}
and
N\to+\infty
. From Eq. (
58
) and using Eq. (
14
) one gets:

\frac{1}{nN}\log\langle[V^{Q}_{N}(\boldsymbol{x}^{\mu},\xi^{\mu},\kappa,\sigma,\epsilon)]^{n}\rangle=\frac{1}{n}\,G(\mathbf{z}^{0})-\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi\mathrm{e})+O\left(\frac{\log N}{N}\right)\ .

(59)

A.2
Replica symmetric ansatz

Making use of the replica-symmetric
ansatz
which states that the stationary point
\mathbf{z}_{0}
is replica-insensitive, one seeks it setting

q_{\gamma\beta}=q,\quad F_{\gamma\beta}=F,\quad E_{\gamma}=E\quad M_{\gamma}=M\quad\text{and}\quad L_{\gamma}=L,

(60)

so that (

\displaystyle{K_{\xi}\left(\boldsymbol{\eta},\boldsymbol{\lambda},\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{\omega},q,\boldsymbol{M}\right)}

\displaystyle=i\sum_{\gamma=1}^{n}\left[y_{\gamma}\lambda_{\gamma}-y_{\gamma}\Phi(\eta_{\gamma})-\left(\frac{\kappa-\xi pM}{\sigma}+\eta_{\gamma}\right)\omega_{\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{(1-q)(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}{2\sigma^{2}}\sum_{\gamma=1}^{n}\omega_{\gamma}^{2}-\frac{q(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}{2\sigma^{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{\gamma=1}^{n}\omega_{\gamma}\Bigg)^{2}.

(61)

Notice that the argument of the logarithm in (

) is the average of the following quantity

\displaystyle{\Delta(n)}

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{{[1-\epsilon,+\infty)^{n}}}\Bigg(\prod_{\gamma=1}^{n}\frac{\mathop{}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{\gamma}}{2\pi}\Bigg)\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}}\Bigg(\prod_{\gamma=1}^{n}\frac{\mathop{}\mathrm{d}\eta_{\gamma}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}y_{\gamma}}{2\pi}\Bigg)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{\gamma=1}^{n}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}\omega_{\gamma}\ \exp\Bigg(\mathop{}\mathrm{i}\frac{\xi m_{\mathrm{in}}M}{\sigma}\sum_{\gamma=1}^{n}\omega_{\gamma}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\Bigg[\mathop{}\mathrm{i}\sum_{\gamma=1}^{n}\Big(y_{\gamma}\lambda_{\gamma}-y_{\gamma}\Phi(\eta_{\gamma})\Big)-\mathop{}\mathrm{i}\sum_{\gamma=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{\kappa}{\sigma}+\eta_{\gamma}\Big)\omega_{\gamma}-\frac{(1-q)(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}{2\sigma^{2}}\sum_{\gamma=1}^{n}\omega^{2}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{q(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}{2\sigma^{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{\gamma=1}^{n}\omega_{\gamma}\Bigg)^{2}\Bigg].

(62)

Such a quantity can then be manipulated as follows: using the Gaussian representation

\exp\Bigg(-\frac{q(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}{2\sigma^{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{\gamma=1}^{n}\omega_{\gamma}\Bigg)^{2}\Bigg)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathop{}\mathrm{D}t\exp\Bigg(-\frac{t^{2}}{2}-\mathop{}\mathrm{i}\,t\frac{\sqrt{q(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}}{\sigma}\sum_{\gamma=1}^{n}\omega_{\gamma}\Bigg)\ ,

via straightforward Gaussian integration over the variables
\omega_{\gamma}
, one writes

\displaystyle{\Delta(n)}

\displaystyle=\int_{{[1-\epsilon,+\infty)^{2}}}\Bigg(\prod_{\gamma=1}^{n}\frac{\mathop{}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{\gamma}}{2\pi}\Bigg)\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}}\left(\prod_{\gamma}^{~}\frac{\mathop{}\mathrm{d}\eta_{\gamma}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}y_{\gamma}}{2\pi}\right)\prod_{\gamma=1}^{n}\mathop{}\mathrm{e}^{\mathop{}\mathrm{i}(y_{\gamma}\lambda_{\gamma}-y_{\gamma}\Phi(\eta_{\gamma}))}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathop{}\mathrm{D}x\ \prod_{\gamma=1}^{n}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\sigma^{2}}{(1-q)(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}}\,\exp{\left(-\frac{\Big(\kappa-\xi m_{\mathrm{in}}M+\sigma\eta_{\gamma}+x\sqrt{q(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}\Big)^{2}}{2(1-q)(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}\right)}\ .

Notice that the argument of the logarithm in (
55
) amounts to

\langle\Delta(n)\rangle_{\xi}
. Then, one sees that
\Delta(n)
consists in
n
independent integrals with respect to
\mathop{}\mathrm{d}\eta_{\gamma}
,
\mathop{}\mathrm{d}\lambda_{\gamma}
and
\mathop{}\mathrm{d}y_{\gamma}

\displaystyle{\Delta(n)}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathop{}\mathrm{D}x\ \Bigg[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\sigma\,\mathop{}\mathrm{d}\eta}{\sqrt{2\pi(1-q)(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}}\int_{1-\epsilon}^{+\infty}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}\lambda\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\mathop{}\mathrm{d}y}{2\pi}\mathop{}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathop{}\mathrm{i}y(\lambda-\Phi(\eta/\sigma))}

\displaystyle\quad\left.\times\exp{\left(-\frac{\Big(\kappa-\xi m_{\mathrm{in}}M+\sigma\eta+x\sqrt{q(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}\Big)^{2}}{2(1-q)(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}\right)}\right]^{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathop{}\mathrm{D}x\ \Bigg[\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\sigma\,\mathop{}\mathrm{d}\eta}{\sqrt{2\pi(1-q)(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}}\Theta\left[\Phi\left(\frac{\eta}{\sigma}\right)-1+\epsilon\right]

\displaystyle\quad\left.\times\exp{\left(-\frac{\Big(\kappa-\xi m_{\mathrm{in}}M+\sigma\eta+x\sqrt{q(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}\Big)^{2}}{2(1-q)(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}\right)}\right]^{n}

Due to the monotonicity of the error function, the function

Then, by changing the integration variable
\eta
into
\lambda=\sigma\eta+\kappa
and using again (
27
), one gets

\displaystyle{\Delta(n)}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathop{}\mathrm{D}x\ \Bigg[\int_{\kappa+\sigma\Phi^{-1}(1-\epsilon)}^{+\infty}\frac{\mathop{}\mathrm{d}\lambda}{\sqrt{2\pi(1-q)(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}}\exp{\left(-\frac{\Big(\lambda-\xi m_{\mathrm{in}}M+x\sqrt{q(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}\Big)^{2}}{2(1-q)(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}\right)}\Bigg]^{n}

where
\tilde{\kappa}=\kappa+\sigma\Phi^{-1}(1-\epsilon)
. Since the replica trick lets
n
vanish as a continuous quantity, we can use the first order approximation
z^{n}\simeq 1+n\log z
, valid for
n\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle{\Delta(n)}

\displaystyle=1+n\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mathop{}\mathrm{D}x\ \log{\Bigg[1-\Phi\Bigg(\frac{\tilde{\kappa}-\xi m_{\mathrm{in}}M+\sqrt{q(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}x}{\sqrt{(1-q)(1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2})}}\Bigg)\Bigg]}.

(64)

Finally, we notice that the replica symmetric
ansatz
makes all matrix and vector entries equal.
Then, averaging over the target parameter
\xi

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1-m_{\mathrm{out}}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hskip-5.69046pt\mathop{}\mathrm{D}x\,\log\left[1-\Phi\left(\frac{x\sqrt{q}-a_{+}(M)}{\sqrt{1-q}}\right)\right]\ ,

Because of the replica symmetric

Then, after straightforward Gaussian manipulations and integration,

\displaystyle{G_{2}(F,E,L)}=\log\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathop{}\mathrm{D}x\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathop{}\mathrm{d}w\ \exp\left(-x\sqrt{\mathop{}\mathrm{i}\,F}\,w-\mathop{}\mathrm{i}\frac{F{-}E}{2}\,w^{2}\,+\,\mathop{}\mathrm{i}\,w\,L\right)\right)^{n}\right)

Appendix B
Large output bias limit

In order to extract the leading order behaviour of the quantum critical storage capacity when the target bias

m_{\mathrm{out}}\to 1^{-}
, we distinguish two possibilities.
Firstly, in this Appendix, we keep the input bias
m_{\mathrm{in}}
fixed and let
m_{\mathrm{out}}\to 1^{-}
; then, in the next one we treat the case
when
m_{\mathrm{in}}=m_{\mathrm{out}}=m\to 1^{-}
.
Consider the equation

(1+m_{\mathrm{out}})\int_{a_{-}(M)}^{+\infty}\mathop{}\mathrm{D}x\,(x-a_{-}(M))=(1-m_{\mathrm{out}})\int_{a_{+}(M)}^{+\infty}\mathop{}\mathrm{D}x\,(x-a_{+}(M))\ ,

with
a_{\pm}(M)
as in (
25
).
If
m_{\mathrm{out}}\to 1^{-}
and
m_{\mathrm{in}}
is kept fixed,
M
must diverge to
+\infty
. Otherwise, the left-hand side
cannot vanish, being the integral of a positive function. Then, in the following, we shall consider

m_{\mathrm{out}}
close to
1
so that
a_{+}(M)<0
,
a_{-}(M)>0
and

\mp a_{\pm}(M)=\frac{m_{\mathrm{in}}\,M}{\sqrt{1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2}}}\pm\frac{\tilde{\kappa}}{\sqrt{1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2}}}\gg 1\ ,

yielding

M\gg\frac{\sqrt{1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2}}}{m_{\mathrm{in}}}\,+\,\frac{\tilde{\kappa}}{m_{\mathrm{in}}}.

(82)

In the classical case
\tilde{\kappa}=\kappa
; moreover, the limit behaviour of the critical
classical storage capacity for
m_{\mathrm{out}}\to 1^{+}
,

\alpha^{C}_{c}\simeq-\frac{1}{(1-m_{\mathrm{out}})\,\log(1-m_{\mathrm{out}})}\ ,

is obtained for
\kappa=0
, namely for
\displaystyle M\gg\frac{\sqrt{1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2}}}{m_{\mathrm{in}}}
.

We proceed by recasting the two storage capacity defining equations (
24
) and (
28
) in terms of Gaussian and error functions:

\displaystyle\alpha_{c}^{Q}\Bigg[\frac{1+m_{\mathrm{out}}}{4}\Bigg((1+a_{-}^{2}(M))\,\left(1-{\rm erf}\left(\frac{a_{-}(M)}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right)-a_{-}(M)\,\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}{\rm e}^{-a_{-}^{2}(M)/2}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1-m_{\mathrm{out}}}{4}\Bigg((1+a_{+}^{2}(M))\,\left(1-{\rm erf}\left(\frac{a_{+}(M)}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right)-a_{+}(M)\,\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}{\rm e}^{-a_{+}^{2}(M)/2}\Bigg)\Bigg]=1\ ,

(83)

respectively

\frac{1-m_{\mathrm{out}}}{1+m_{\mathrm{out}}}=\frac{\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,{\rm e}^{-a_{-}^{2}(M)/2}-a_{-}(M)\left(1-\hbox{erf}\left(\frac{a_{-}(M)}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right)}{\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,{\rm e}^{-a_{+}^{2}(M)/2}-a_{+}(M)\left(1-\hbox{erf}\left(\frac{a_{+}(M)}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right)}\ .

(84)

Equation (
84
) can be satisfied in the limit
m_{\mathrm{out}}\rightarrow 1^{-}
only if the right-hand side vanishes, which can happen only for values of
M
such that
a_{\pm}(M)\to\mp\infty
. For small but finite values of
1-m_{\mathrm{out}}
, the solution of (
84
) is obtained for
\mp a_{\pm}(M)\gg 1
, which implies

M\gg\frac{\sqrt{1-m_{\mathrm{in}}^{2}}}{m_{\mathrm{in}}}\,+\,\frac{\tilde{\kappa}}{m_{\mathrm{in}}}.

(85)

Using the asymptotic behaviour in (
77
) with
\mp a_{\pm}(M)\gg 1
, one gets

\displaystyle 1-\hbox{erf}\left(\frac{a_{-}(M)}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\simeq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}{\rm e}^{-a_{-}^{2}(M)/2}\,\left(\frac{1}{a_{-}(M)}-\frac{1}{a_{-}^{3}(M)}\right)\ ,

(86)

\displaystyle 1-\hbox{erf}\left(\frac{a_{+}(M)}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\simeq 2+\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}{\rm e}^{-a_{+}^{2}(M)/2}\,\left(\frac{1}{a_{+}(M)}-\frac{1}{a_{+}^{3}(M)}\right){\simeq 2}\ .

(87)

Hence, for
m_{\mathrm{out}}\to 1^{-}
, at leading order, (
84
) and (
83
) read

{1-m_{\mathrm{out}}\simeq-\frac{{\rm e}^{-a_{-}^{2}(M)/2}}{\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}a_{-}^{2}(M)a_{+}(M)}}\ ,\quad{1\simeq\alpha_{c}^{Q}\,\frac{1-m_{\mathrm{out}}}{2}\,a_{+}^{2}(M)}\ .

(88)

Since
m_{\mathrm{out}}\to 1^{-}
implies
a_{-}(M)\simeq-\,a_{+}(M)
, the first asymptotic behaviour in (
88
) yields

{\log(1-m_{\mathrm{out}})\simeq-\frac{a_{-}^{2}(M)}{2}-\log\left(\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}a_{-}^{2}(M)(-a_{+}(M))\right)\simeq-\,\frac{a_{-}^{2}(M)}{2}\simeq-\,\frac{a_{+}^{2}(M)}{2}}\ .

(89)

from which the limit of strongly correlated targets,
m_{\mathrm{out}}\to 1^{-}
, is as in (
30
), for all pattern biases
0\leq m_{\mathrm{in}}\leq 1
.
However, it must be emphasized that, because of (
82
) the limit is reached with possibly quite different slopes depending on both the quantum parameter
\sigma\Phi^{-1}(1-\epsilon)
and on the degree of independence of the input patterns
m_{\mathrm{in}}
.

Appendix C
Simultaneously large input and output bias

Setting
m_{\mathrm{in}}=m_{\mathrm{out}}=m
, the quantity

In the limit

Conformal boundaries of Minkowski superspace and their super cuts
Nicolas Boulanger, Yannick Herfray, Noémie Parrini

Abstract
In this article we carry out a detailed investigation of the geometric nature of the points at infinity of Minkowski superspace. It turns out that there are several sets of points forming the superconformal boundary of Minkowski superspace: on top of a well-behaved super ℐ \mathscr{I} , we find other sets that we exhibit and study. We also study the intersection of these boundaries with super null cones and explicitly construct the corresponding space of super cuts.

\displaystyle\overline{Z}{}^{\bar{\hat{\alpha}}{a}}h_{\bar{\hat{\alpha}}{\hat{\beta}}}Z^{\hat{\beta}b}

\displaystyle=0\,,\qquad\forall a,b\;,

(2.29)

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}_{\hat{\alpha}}{}^{b}\,h^{\alpha\bar{\hat{\beta}}}\,\overline{\widetilde{Z}}_{\bar{\hat{\beta}}}{}^{a}

\displaystyle=0\;,\qquad\forall a,b\;.

(2.30)

With these definitions, points in (

3
Invariant subspaces for the super Poincaré group

In this section we break superconformal invariance and discuss
the points left at infinity of Minkowski superspace by the action
of the Poincaré supergroup.

3.1
Super Poincaré group

We will now break superconformal invariance down to the super Poincaré group by introducing infinity bi-supertwistors

\displaystyle I^{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon^{AB}&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\tilde{I}_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&\epsilon^{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(3.1)

These bi-supertwistors are simple,
I^{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}=I^{[\hat{\alpha}}_{1}I^{\hat{\beta}]}_{2}
,
I_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}=I_{[\hat{\alpha}}^{1}I_{\hat{\beta}]}^{2}
, and together define a point
(I^{\hat{\alpha}b},\tilde{I}_{\hat{\alpha}}{}^{b})\in\overline{M}
given by

\displaystyle I^{\hat{\alpha}b}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\delta^{Ab}\\
{0_{A^{\prime}}}^{b}\\
0^{Ib}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\tilde{I}_{\hat{\alpha}}{}^{b}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}{0_{A}}^{b}&\delta^{A^{\prime}b}&0^{Ib}\end{bmatrix}.

(3.2)

The subgroup of (
2.3
) stabilising (
3.1
) is parametrised by

\begin{pmatrix}{M^{B}}_{A}&iT^{BA^{\prime}}&{Q^{B}}_{I}\\
0&-{\widetilde{M}}_{B^{\prime}}{}^{A^{\prime}}&0\\
0&{\widetilde{Q}}^{JA^{\prime}}&R^{J}{}_{I}\end{pmatrix}\;,

(3.3)

where now
{M^{B}}_{A},\ {{\widetilde{M}}_{B^{\prime}}}{}^{A^{\prime}}\in\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{C}_{c})
and
R^{J}{}_{I}\in\text{SL}(\mathcal{N},\mathbb{C}_{c})
. This is what we will call
the complex super Poincaré group
{(\text{ISO}(1,3|\mathcal{N}))}_{\mathbb{C}}\,
,
where by an abuse of terminology we include the
R
-symmetry group

\text{SL}(\mathcal{N},\mathbb{C}_{c})\,
in it.
The complex super Poincaré group has the structure

(\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\times\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})\times\text{SL}(\mathcal{N}))\ltimes\mathcal{T}^{4|4\mathcal{N}}
, where
\mathcal{T}^{4|4\mathcal{N}}

is the non-Abelian group consisting of the matrices

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\delta^{B}{}_{A}&iT^{BA^{\prime}}&Q^{B}{}_{I}\\
0&\delta_{B^{\prime}}{}^{A^{\prime}}&0\\
0&\widetilde{Q}^{JA^{\prime}}&\delta^{J}{}_{I}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.4)

If we now impose that the group of matrices (
3.3
) should preserve
the hermitian form (
2.6
), i.e.,
that
P^{\hat{\alpha}}{}_{\hat{\beta}}
should satisfy
\overline{P}^{\overline{\hat{\gamma}}}{}_{\overline{\hat{\beta}}}{}\,h_{\overline{\hat{\gamma}}{\hat{\delta}}}\,P^{\hat{\delta}}{}_{\hat{\alpha}}=h_{\overline{\hat{\beta}}\hat{\alpha}}
,
we find a subgroup given by the set of matrices of the form

\text{P}^{\mathbb{R}}=\begin{pmatrix}{M^{B}}_{A}&iM^{B}{}_{C}t^{CA^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}Q^{B}{}_{I}\overline{Q}^{IC^{\prime}}\overline{M}_{C^{\prime}}{}^{A^{\prime}}&{Q^{B}}_{I}\\
0_{B^{\prime}A}&-\overline{M}_{B^{\prime}}{}^{A^{\prime}}&0_{B^{\prime}I}\\
0^{J}{}_{A}&{-R^{J}{}_{I}\overline{Q}^{IB^{\prime}}}{{\overline{M}}_{B^{\prime}}}{}^{A^{\prime}}&R^{J}{}_{I}\end{pmatrix}\;,

(3.5)

where
R^{I}{}_{J}
is unitary and
t_{CA^{\prime}}
is hermitian.
We call this subgroup the real super Poincaré group.

3.2
Minkowski superspace

A point of the superconformal compactification is
at infinity whenever
\det(\tilde{I}_{\hat{\alpha}}{}_{a}Z^{\hat{\alpha}b}={\pi_{a}}^{b})\neq 0
,
on the contrary points of Minkowski superspace are those satisfying

\det(\tilde{I}_{\hat{\alpha}}{}_{a}Z^{\hat{\alpha}b}={\pi_{a}}^{b})\neq 0\;.

(3.6)

3.2.1
Chiral superspaces

The chiral left Minkowski superspace

[

as the subset of points

such that

{\delta_{A^{\prime}}}^{b}\\

The second line allows to identify

\theta^{Ib}\end{bmatrix}&=\begin{bmatrix}{M^{B}}_{A}iX^{Ab}_{+}+iT^{Bb}+Q^{B}{}_{I}\theta^{Ib}\\

\widetilde{Q}^{Jb}+R^{J}{}_{I}\theta^{Ib}\end{bmatrix}\\
&\sim\begin{bmatrix}{(\widetilde{M})}^{b}{}_{c}\cdot({M^{B}}_{A}iX_{+}^{Ac}+iT^{Bc}+Q^{B}{}_{I}\theta^{Ic})\\
{\delta_{B^{\prime}}}^{b}\\
({\widetilde{M}})^{b}{}_{c}(\widetilde{Q}^{Jc}+R^{J}{}_{I}\theta^{Ic})\end{bmatrix}\in M_{\ell}\;.\end{split}

The action is transitive which makes

The stabiliser is obtained from (

and

For

,

Reality conditions.

If we restrict ourselves to the real case, i.e.,

(3.42)

\displaystyle=R^{1}{}_{1}+\mathfrak{M}^{1}\,a\;,

(3.43)

\displaystyle R^{J}{}_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{R}^{J}\,a\quad\qquad\text{ if }J>2\;,

(3.44)

where
\mathfrak{M}^{1}
and
\mathfrak{R}^{J}
are some unconstrained anti-commuting numbers. Let us now stick to
\mathcal{N}=1
in order to simplify the discussion: The parameters left unconstrained for the stabiliser are then
M^{1}{}_{2}
,

\widetilde{M}^{1}{}_{1}
,
\widetilde{M}^{1}{}_{2}
,
T^{11^{\prime}}
,
T^{12^{\prime}}
,
T^{21^{\prime}}
,
Q^{A}
and
\mathfrak{M}^{1}
. One might be tempted to conclude that this stabiliser is a supergroup of dimension
\mathbb{C}^{6|3}
and therefore that the orbits an homogeneous space of dimension
\mathbb{C}^{4|1}
.
(It might here be useful for the reader to compare with
\mathscr{I}_{l}
which is an homogeneous space (
3.24
) of dimension
\mathbb{C}^{3|\mathcal{N}}
.)
Strictly speaking this would however be an incorrect conclusion: the fact that the “coordinate”
\mathfrak{M}^{1}
here only appears as the product “
\mathfrak{M}^{1}\,a
” means that it is ambiguous and that, following DeWitt
[
35
]
, it does not define a superchart; the stabiliser is
in fact strictly speaking not a supermanifold. If the same analysis had been instead
performed at the level of the algebra one would have equivalently concluded that the
corresponding stabiliser is a sub supervector space
(over
\Lambda_{\infty}
)
of the super Poincaré algebra
which does not admit a basis
.

We here wish to comment on the peculiar features of supergeometries which are responsible for these phenomena.
If one follows DeWitt’s approach to supermanifolds
(this approach is equivalent to the algebro-geometric approach, see

[
36
]
for a proof), then the supervector spaces on which
manifolds are modelled are constructed over the supernumbers
\Lambda_{\infty}
,
which form a graded ring and a graded infinite dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{C}
.
Nevertheless, because theses supervector spaces of DeWitt are not over a field
but rather over the graded
ring

\Lambda_{\infty}
, they are by definition (graded)
modules.
In general, the analogy with vector space is dangerous because
not all modules
over an algebra
\mathcal{A}
admit a basis. In the
case where a (graded) module over
\mathcal{A}
admit a basis it is said to
be free and the parallel with vector spaces is possible: in
[
37
]
this analogy
between vector spaces and free modules (there called
\mathcal{A}\,
-vector spaces) has
been investigated with great care. This, nevertheless, has certain important limits.
The main limit, that turns out to be crucial for our analysis, is the notion of
subobject.
In fact, there is no natural way to induce a basis on a submodule,
once a basis for the free graded
\mathcal{A}\,
-module is given,
due to the counter-intuitive fact that submodules of
a free module do not have to be free.
One therefore
[
37
]
restricts the notion of a graded
subspace in this new category as being a
graded subspace in the usual sense, with the additional restriction
that there exists a homogeneous basis for the total space within
its equivalence class such that a subset of it
forms a basis for the graded subspace in question;
these are the sub-
\mathcal{A}
-vector spaces of
[
37
]
.
This being introduced, supermanifolds and sub-supermanifold are respectively
modelled on
\Lambda_{\infty}
-vector spaces and sub-
\Lambda_{\infty}
-vector spaces.
However, one should realise that, contrary to the ordinary case, one cannot
guarantee that through every point of a supermanifold passes a sub-supermanifold
of lower dimension
[
37
]
.

Note however that, despite the fact that these orbits are not a supermanifold, this is very easy to describe them explicitly as

Z^{\hat{\alpha}b}=\begin{bmatrix}\omega^{A}&\hat{\pi}^{A}\\
\pi_{A^{\prime}}&0\\
\theta^{I}&\alpha a\end{bmatrix}\;.

(3.45)

Here
a\in\mathbb{C}_{a}
is fixed and parametrises different orbits while

\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{c}
is an extra commuting “coordinate” parametrising the
would-be extra bosonic dimension of these orbits as compared to

\mathscr{I}_{\ell}
.

As we shall see reality conditions complicate further the situation since
neither the real orbits nor the real subspace of
\mathcal{H}
are
sub-supermanifolds. The non-chiral models have the same problem.

Reality conditions.

We are going to impose the following reality conditions:

\displaystyle{(Z^{1})}^{2}=0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\omega^{A}{\overline{\pi}}_{A}+\pi_{A^{\prime}}{\overline{\omega}}^{A^{\prime}}+\delta_{IJ}\theta^{I}\overline{\theta}^{{J}}=0\textit{ i.e. }2\,\text{Re}(\omega^{A}\overline{\pi}_{A})-\delta_{IJ}\overline{\theta}^{{J}}\theta^{I}=0\;,

(3.46a)

\displaystyle{(Z^{2})}^{2}=0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\delta_{IJ}\eta^{I}\overline{\eta}^{J}=0\;,

(3.46b)

\displaystyle{Z^{1}\cdot Z^{2}}=0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\overline{\pi}_{A}\hat{\pi}^{A}-\overline{\theta}_{J}\eta^{J}=0\;.

(3.46c)

The same type of technical problem we met in the context of
the stabiliser of
\cal H
shows up at this stage:
The second equation above does not define a sub-supermanifold
and therefore, here again, strictly speaking the real
supermanifold
\mathcal{H}
cannot be defined.

For
\mathcal{N}=1\,
,
we can solve the reality conditions and deduce a parametrisation of the real
\mathcal{H}\,
: First, equation (
3.46b
) becomes
\eta\bar{\eta}=0
and is solved as

\eta=e^{i\phi}\,a
,
with
a\in\mathbb{R}_{a}
a real anticommuting supernumber and
\phi\in\mathbb{R}_{c}
a
real commuting supernumber. Then, in order to solve equation (
3.46c
), we introduce the parametrisation
\eta=\eta_{A}\hat{\pi}^{A}
,
where the odd variables
\eta_{A}
is chosen to satisfy

\eta_{A}\bar{\eta}_{A^{\prime}}=0
(this ensures ensure that
(
3.46b
) still holds). Such a decomposition
of
\eta
is not unique but always exists.
In fact, because
\hat{\pi}^{A}
has non zero body, at least one of the body of the
components
in
\{\hat{\pi}^{0},\hat{\pi}^{1}\}
is non zero.
Assume for example that
\hat{\pi}^{0}
has non zero body;
then we can take
\eta_{A}=(\eta/\hat{\pi}^{0},0)
. With this choice of
parametrisation, the third reality condition (
3.46c
)
reads

(\bar{\pi}_{A}-\bar{\theta}\eta_{A})\hat{\pi}^{A}=0\;,

(3.47)

and, since
\hat{\pi}^{A}
has a non vanishing body, can be solved as

\hat{\pi}^{A}=\alpha(\bar{\pi}^{A}-\bar{\theta}\eta^{A})\;,\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\;.

(3.48)

Using complex projective invariance to rescale
Z^{\hat{\alpha}2}
,
one gets

Z^{\hat{\alpha}b}=\begin{bmatrix}\omega^{A}&\bar{\pi}^{A}-\bar{\theta}\eta^{A}\\
\pi_{A^{\prime}}&0\\
\theta&\eta{}_{A}(\bar{\pi}^{A}-\bar{\theta}\eta^{A})\end{bmatrix}.

(3.49)

Finally, in order to solve the first reality condition (
3.46a
) we introduce
\Theta:=\theta+\eta^{A}\omega_{A}
and
iu_{+}:=\omega^{A}(\bar{\pi}_{A}-\bar{\Theta}\eta_{A})
. We then have

Z^{\hat{\alpha}b}=\begin{bmatrix}\omega^{A}&\bar{\pi}^{A}-\bar{\Theta}\eta^{A}\\
\pi_{A^{\prime}}&0\\
\Theta+\omega^{A}\eta_{A}&\eta_{A}(\bar{\pi}^{A}-\bar{\Theta}\eta^{A})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\omega^{A}&\bar{\pi}^{A}-\bar{\Theta}\eta^{A}\\
\pi_{A^{\prime}}&0\\
\Theta+\omega^{A}\eta_{A}&e^{i\phi}a\end{bmatrix}\;,

(3.50)

and equation (
3.46a
) becomes

2\,Im(u_{+})=\Theta\bar{\Theta}\;.

(3.51)

Which is solved as

u_{+}=u_{\ell}+\frac{i}{2}\Theta\bar{\Theta}\;,\quad u_{\ell}\,\in\,\mathbb{R}_{c}\;.

(3.52)

We thus found a parametrisation of
\mathcal{H}\,
by

(u_{\ell},[\pi_{A^{\prime}}],e^{i\phi},\Theta,a)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{CP}^{1}\times S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}_{a}\times\mathbb{R}_{a}
.
Once again, since
e^{i\phi}
always appears in the form “
e^{i\phi}a
”,
it would strictly speaking be incorrect to interpret these as coordinates on a
supermanifold.

Non-chiral
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\times\mathcal{H}_{r}\,
.

In coordinates, the flag conditions on

\displaystyle(Z^{\hat{\alpha}a},\tilde{Z}^{a}{}_{\hat{\alpha}})=\left(\begin{bmatrix}\omega^{A}&\hat{\pi}^{A}\\
\pi_{A^{\prime}}&0_{A^{\prime}}\\
\theta^{I}&\eta^{I}\end{bmatrix}\;,\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{\pi}_{A}&\tilde{\omega}^{A^{\prime}}&-\tilde{\theta}_{I}\\
0_{A}&\tilde{\hat{\pi}}^{A^{\prime}}&-\tilde{\eta}_{I}\end{bmatrix}\right)\in\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\times\mathcal{H}_{r}

are realised by

\displaystyle\tilde{\eta}^{I}\,\eta_{I}

\displaystyle=0\;,

(3.54a)

\displaystyle\tilde{\hat{\pi}}^{A^{\prime}}\,\pi_{A^{\prime}}-\tilde{\eta}_{I}\theta^{I}

\displaystyle=0\;,

(3.54b)

\displaystyle\tilde{\pi}_{A}\,\omega^{A}+\tilde{\omega}^{A^{\prime}}\,\pi_{A^{\prime}}-\tilde{\theta}^{I}\,\theta_{I}

\displaystyle=0\;,

(3.54c)

\displaystyle\tilde{\pi}_{A}\,\hat{\pi}^{A}-\tilde{\theta}^{I}\,\eta_{I}

\displaystyle=0\;.

(3.54d)

The first condition can be solved by

\tilde{\eta}=\gamma\,\eta\,
,
where
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}\,
.
As in the real case, we use a decomposition
\eta=\eta_{A}\hat{\pi}^{A}
.
Similarly, we introduce a decomposition
\tilde{\eta}=\tilde{\hat{\pi}}^{A^{\prime}}\tilde{\eta}_{A^{\prime}}

and to keep satisfied the first reality condition we ask
\eta_{A}\tilde{\eta}_{A^{\prime}}=0\,

The equation (

) can therefore be written as

(\tilde{\pi}_{A}-\tilde{\theta}\eta_{A})\hat{\pi}^{A}=0

which can be solved, because
\hat{\pi}^{A}

\hat{\pi}^{A}=\alpha(\tilde{\pi}^{A}-\tilde{\theta}\eta^{A})\;,\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\;.

(3.56)

Similarly, the equation (
3.54b
) can be written

\tilde{\hat{\pi}}^{A^{\prime}}(\pi_{A^{\prime}}-\tilde{\eta}_{A^{\prime}}\theta)=0\;,

(3.57)

which gives

\tilde{\hat{\pi}}^{A^{\prime}}=\tilde{\alpha}(\pi^{A^{\prime}}-\tilde{\eta}^{A^{\prime}}\theta)\;,\quad\tilde{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}\;.

(3.58)

We have then

\displaystyle(Z^{\hat{\alpha}a},\tilde{Z}^{a}{}_{\hat{\alpha}})=\left(\begin{bmatrix}\omega^{A}&~\tilde{\pi}^{A}-\tilde{\theta}\eta^{A}\\
\pi_{A^{\prime}}&~0_{A^{\prime}}\\
\theta&~\eta_{A}(\tilde{\pi}^{A}-\tilde{\theta}\eta^{A})\end{bmatrix}\;,\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{\pi}_{A}&~~\tilde{\omega}^{A^{\prime}}&~~-\tilde{\theta}\\

\widetilde{\Theta}=\tilde{\theta}+\tilde{\eta}^{A^{\prime}}\tilde{\omega}_{A^{\prime}}

(Z^{\hat{\alpha}a},\tilde{Z}^{a}{}_{\hat{\alpha}})=\left(\begin{bmatrix}\omega^{A}&~\tilde{\pi}^{A}-\widetilde{\Theta}\eta^{A}\\

\Theta-\eta^{A}\omega_{A}&~\eta_{A}(\tilde{\pi}^{A}-\widetilde{\Theta}\eta^{A})\end{bmatrix}\;,\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{\pi}_{A}&~~\tilde{\omega}^{A^{\prime}}&~~-\widetilde{\Theta}+\tilde{\eta}^{A^{\prime}}\tilde{\omega}_{A^{\prime}}\\

(3.60)

The last condition to be solved, equation (
3.54c
), can now be expressed as

\displaystyle\tilde{\pi}_{A}\,\omega^{A}+\tilde{\omega}^{A^{\prime}}\,\pi_{A^{\prime}}-\widetilde{\Theta}\,\Theta+\widetilde{\Theta}\eta^{A}\omega_{A}+\tilde{\eta}^{A^{\prime}}\tilde{\omega}_{A^{\prime}}\Theta

\displaystyle=0\;

\displaystyle\iff(\tilde{\pi}_{A}\,-\widetilde{\Theta}\eta_{A})\omega^{A}+\tilde{\omega}^{A^{\prime}}(\pi_{A^{\prime}}-\tilde{\eta}_{A^{\prime}}\Theta)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\Theta}\,\Theta\;.

(3.61)

If we define the quantities

and

-iu_{-}:=\tilde{\omega}^{A^{\prime}}(\pi_{A^{\prime}}-\tilde{\eta}_{A^{\prime}}\Theta)
,
then (

(3.62)

Setting

\displaystyle u_{+}

\displaystyle=u_{l}-\frac{i}{2}\,\widetilde{\Theta}\Theta\;,

\displaystyle u_{-}

\displaystyle=u_{r}+\frac{i}{2}\,\widetilde{\Theta}\Theta\;,

(3.63)

one solves the condition (
3.62
) with
u_{l}=u_{r}=u\,
.
A parametrisation of
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\times\mathcal{H}_{r}
is then given by

(u,[\pi_{A^{\prime}}],[\tilde{\pi}_{A}],\gamma,\Theta,\widetilde{\Theta},\eta)

where

.

Reality conditions.

The real points are obtained
by imposing on the points

\{(Z^{\hat{\alpha}b},\widetilde{Z}_{\hat{\alpha}}{}^{b})~|~{\widetilde{Z}}_{\hat{\alpha}}{}^{b}Z^{\hat{\alpha}a}=0\}

of complex non-chiral
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\times\mathcal{H}_{r}
the reality conditions

\widetilde{Z}_{\hat{\alpha}}{}^{b}=h_{\overline{\hat{\alpha}}\hat{\alpha}}\overline{Z}^{\overline{\hat{\alpha}}b}
,
where the complex structure
h_{\overline{\hat{\alpha}}\beta}

is given in (
2.23
).
This gives

\tilde{\omega}^{A^{\prime}}=\overline{\omega}^{A^{\prime}}
,
\tilde{\pi}_{A}=\overline{\pi}_{A}\,
,
\tilde{\hat{\pi}}^{A^{\prime}}={\overline{\hat{\pi}}}^{A^{\prime}}\,
,
\tilde{\theta}^{I}=\overline{\theta}{}^{I}\,
,
\tilde{\eta}^{I}=\overline{\eta}{}^{I}

,

, and

.

3.4.2
Fermionic subspace
\mathfrak{I}

The last invariant subspace that we will consider are points of the form

Z^{\hat{\alpha}b}=\begin{bmatrix}\omega^{Ab}\\
{0_{A^{\prime}}}^{b}\\
\theta^{Ib}\neq 0\end{bmatrix}\;,

(3.64)

i.e. such that
\pi_{A^{\prime}}{}^{b}=0
but
\theta^{Ib}\neq 0
. These points, which we collectively refer to as
\mathfrak{I}
, therefore appear to be a sort of
fermionic extension of time/space-like infinity
\iota:=\left\{I^{\hat{\alpha}b}\right\}
– with this particular isolated point here given by
\theta^{Ib}=0^{Ib}
.

In order for (
3.64
) to define a plan
\omega^{Ab}
must be invertible, we can therefore always use the

\text{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
freedom to set
\omega^{Ab}=\delta^{Ab}
and we are left with the complex coordinates
\theta^{Ib}\in\mathbb{C}^{0|2\mathcal{N}}
.

The super Poincaré group does not act transitively on this set

\begin{pmatrix}{M^{B}}_{A}&iT^{BA^{\prime}}&{Q^{B}}_{I}\\
0&-{\widetilde{M}}_{B^{\prime}}{}^{A^{\prime}}&0\\
0&{\widetilde{Q}}^{JA^{\prime}}&R^{J}{}_{I}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}\delta^{Ab}\\
{0_{A^{\prime}}}^{b}\\
\theta^{Ib}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}{M^{B}}_{A}\delta^{Ab}+Q^{B}{}_{I}\theta^{Ib}\\
0_{B^{\prime}}{}^{b}\\
R^{J}{}_{I}\theta^{Ib}\end{bmatrix}\;.

(3.65)

However, it clearly stabilises it. There are two different case depending on whether
\theta^{I1}
and
\theta^{I2}
are linearly independent or not. Similarly to the case of
\mathcal{H}
, it is not an homogeneous space for the super Poincaré group and the orbits are not supermanifolds.

Reality condition.

Denoting
(\theta^{I}{}^{1},\theta^{I}{}^{2})=(\theta^{I},\eta^{I})
, we are going to impose the following reality conditions:

\displaystyle{(Z^{1})}^{2}=0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\overline{\theta}^{{I}}\delta_{IJ}\theta^{J}=0\;,

\displaystyle{(Z^{2})}^{2}=0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\overline{\eta}^{{I}}\delta_{IJ}\eta^{J}=0\;,

\displaystyle{Z^{1}\cdot Z^{2}}=0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\overline{\theta}_{J}\eta^{J}=0\;.

As explained for
\mathcal{H}
, these conditions do not define a submanifold.
If
\mathcal{N}=1
, the first reality conditions impose that
\theta
can be
rewritten as
\theta=e^{i\phi}a
, with
a\in\mathbb{R}_{a}
and
\phi\in\mathbb{R}_{a}
.
The remaining reality conditions then give that
\eta=e^{i\psi}a
with
\psi\in\mathbb{R}_{c}
.
A parametrisation of
\mathfrak{I}
is then given by
(a,e^{i\phi},e^{i\psi})\in\mathbb{R}_{a}\times S^{1}\times S^{1}
.

3.4.3
Non-chiral invariant subspaces at the boundary

As explained in section
2.2.1
, elements of
\overline{M}=F(2|0,2|\mathcal{N},\mathbb{C}^{4|\mathcal{N}})

are obtained as pairs
(Z^{\hat{\alpha}b},{\widetilde{Z}}_{\hat{\alpha}}{}^{c})

of bi-supertwistors and dual bi-supertwistors satisfying

{\widetilde{Z}}_{\hat{\alpha}}{}^{b}Z^{\hat{\alpha}c}=0\;.

(3.66)

Non-chiral orbits will therefore be pairs of chiral orbits satisfying the condition (

).

One can show that the only admissible orbits under this condition are :

\mathscr{I}_{\ell}\times\mathscr{I}_{r}
,
\mathcal{H}_{\ell}\times\mathcal{H}_{r}
,

\iota^{0|2\cal{N}}_{\ell}\times\iota^{0|2\cal{N}}_{r}
,
\{\iota_{\ell}\}\times\{\iota_{r}\}
and
\{\iota_{\ell}\}\times\iota^{0|2\cal{N}}_{r}
.
In the cases where it applies, the stabilisers can be derived by doing the
intersection of the chiral ones.

4.1.1
Null supergeodesics

In the reverse sense, a point of the superambitwistor space

\displaystyle(Z^{\hat{\alpha}},\widetilde{Z}_{\hat{\alpha}})

\displaystyle=(\begin{bmatrix}\omega^{A}\\
\pi_{A^{\prime}}\\
\theta^{I}\end{bmatrix},\bm{[}\;\tilde{\pi}_{A}\quad\tilde{\omega}^{A^{\prime}}\quad-\tilde{\theta}_{I}\;\bm{]})\quad\mbox{s.t.}\quad\widetilde{Z}_{\hat{\alpha}}Z^{\hat{\alpha}}=\tilde{\pi}_{A}\omega^{A}+\tilde{\omega}^{A^{\prime}}\pi_{A^{\prime}}-\tilde{\theta}_{I}\theta^{I}=0\;,

(4.1)

defines a null supergeodesic in the (conformal compactification of)
complexified Minkowski space. To see what these are, let us look what the
condition is for one point of Minkowski to be on this null supergeodesic.
For that, one works in the local chart defining Minkowski superspace:

(Z^{\hat{\alpha}}{}^{b},\widetilde{Z}_{\hat{\alpha}}{}^{b})=(\begin{bmatrix}iX_{+}^{AB^{\prime}}\\
\delta_{A^{\prime}}{}^{B^{\prime}}\\
\theta^{I}{}^{B^{\prime}}\end{bmatrix},\bm{[}\;\delta_{A}{}^{B}\quad-iX_{-}^{A^{\prime}B}\quad-\tilde{\theta}_{I}{}^{B}\;\bm{]})

(4.2)

with

\displaystyle X^{AB^{\prime}}_{+}

\displaystyle=x^{AB^{\prime}}-\frac{i}{2}\,\tilde{\theta}_{I}{}^{A}\theta^{IB^{\prime}},

\displaystyle X^{A^{\prime}B}_{-}

\displaystyle=x^{A^{\prime}B}+\frac{i}{2}\,\tilde{\theta}_{I}{}^{B}\theta^{IA^{\prime}}\,.

This spacetime point is part of the null geodesics defined by
the ambitwistor (

\displaystyle Z^{\hat{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=Z^{\hat{\alpha}}{}^{b}\pi_{b}\;,

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}_{\hat{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{Z}_{\hat{\alpha}}{}^{b}\tilde{\pi}_{b}\;,

(4.3)

i.e. if the point satisfies

\displaystyle\omega^{A}

\displaystyle=iX^{AB^{\prime}}_{+}\pi_{B^{\prime}}\;,

\displaystyle\theta^{I}

\displaystyle=\theta^{IB^{\prime}}\pi_{B}^{\prime}\;,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}^{B^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-iX_{-}^{AB^{\prime}}\tilde{\pi}_{A}\;,

\displaystyle\tilde{\theta}_{I}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\theta}_{I}{}^{B}\tilde{\pi}_{B}\;.

(4.5)

Solutions to these equations can be parametrised by
(\epsilon,\epsilon^{I},\tilde{\epsilon}^{I})\in\mathbb{C}_{c}\times\mathbb{C}_{a}^{\mathcal{N}}\times\mathbb{C}_{a}^{\mathcal{N}}

As we recall in section

and is isomorphic to the homogeneous
space (
2.22
) for the (complexified)
superconformal group
\text{SL}(4|\mathcal{N})
.
The Lorentzian reality condition is obtained by introducing a hermitian metric

h_{\bar{\hat{\alpha}}\hat{\beta}}
and restricting to totally null planes;
real Lorentzian conformally compact Minkowski superspace
\overline{M}^{4|4\mathcal{N}}

is then isomorphic to the homogeneous space
(
2.25

superconformal group
\text{SU}(2,2|\mathcal{N})
.

Complexified AdS superspace

AdS^{4|4\mathcal{N}}_{\mathbb{C}}\subset\overline{M}^{4|4\mathcal{N}}_{\mathbb{C}}

and its real Lorentzian counterpart

AdS^{4|4\mathcal{N}}\subset\overline{M}^{4|4\mathcal{N}}

are then obtained by reducing the superconformal group to the respective
group of isometries
\text{Sp}(4|\mathcal{N},\mathbb{C})
and
\text{OSp}(\mathcal{N}|4)

(see figure
1
).

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\text{SL}(4|\mathcal{N},\mathbb{C})&\xrightarrow{\text{reality condition}}&\text{SU}(2,2|\mathcal{N})\\[3.99994pt]

\Lambda<0\\

Summary of the group reductions, horizontal arrows correspond to imposing Lorentzian reality condition while vertical arrows correspond to breaking of superconformal invariance.

We here would like to briefly recall the corresponding realizations in terms
of Grasmmanians of
\mathbb{C}^{4|4\mathcal{N}}
: In order to break superconformal
invariance one introduces the infinity supertwistors (see e.g.

38

39
]

\displaystyle I^{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon^{AB}&0&0\\
0&\Lambda\epsilon_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}&0\\
0&0&\sqrt{|\Lambda|}\delta_{IJ}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle I_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda\epsilon_{AB}&0&0\\
0&\epsilon^{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}&0\\
0&0&\sqrt{|\Lambda|}\delta^{IJ}\end{pmatrix},

(C.1)

that satisfy

I^{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\gamma}}I_{\hat{\gamma}\hat{\beta}}=|\Lambda|\delta^{\hat{\alpha}}{}_{\hat{\beta}}

and coincide with (
3.1
) in the limit where
\Lambda
vanishes.
For
\Lambda\neq 0
,
these are skew-symmetric and invertible; as such these define a
symplectic structure on
\mathbb{C}^{4|4\mathcal{N}}
. The requirement to preserve this
symplectic form then realises the left hand side reduction of supergroup in Figure

1
. (Complexified) conformally compact Minkowski
superspace
\overline{M}^{4|4\mathcal{N}}_{\mathbb{C}}=F(2|0,2|\mathcal{N},\mathbb{C}^{4|\mathcal{N}})

then decompose according to the following:
if
(Z^{\hat{\alpha}a},\tilde{Z}_{\hat{\alpha}}{}^{a})

is a point of
\overline{M}^{4|4\mathcal{N}}_{\mathbb{C}}
one can restrict the symplectic form
(
C.1
) to the corresponding plane
of
F(2|0,2|\mathcal{N},\mathbb{C}^{4|\mathcal{N}})

\displaystyle\sigma\epsilon^{ab}

\displaystyle:=I_{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}Z^{\hat{\alpha}a}Z^{\hat{\beta}b}\;,

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}\epsilon_{ab}

\displaystyle:=I^{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\beta}}\tilde{Z}_{\hat{\alpha}}{}^{a}\tilde{Z}_{\hat{\beta}}{}^{b}\;.

Because of projective invariance the actual values of
\sigma
and
\tilde{\sigma}
are
irrelevant; the only alternative to consider is whether or not they vanish, i.e.,
whether or not the induced skew symmetric bitwistors are invertible.
Points of

AdS^{4|4\mathcal{N}}_{\mathbb{C}}
corresponds to the situation where both induced symplectic
structure are non degenerate while points of the superconformal boundary

\overline{M}^{3|3\mathcal{N}}

\displaystyle(Z^{\hat{\alpha}a},\widetilde{Z}_{\hat{\alpha}}{}^{a})

\displaystyle\in AdS^{4|4\mathcal{N}}_{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle\sigma,\tilde{\sigma}\in\text{GL}(1|0),

\displaystyle(Z^{\hat{\alpha}a},\widetilde{Z}_{\hat{\alpha}}{}^{a})

(C.3)

\widetilde{Z}_{\hat{\alpha}}{}^{a}=\overline{Z}^{\overline{\hat{\beta}}a}\,h_{\overline{\hat{\beta}}\hat{\alpha}}

It is here useful to introduce the map

J^{\hat{\alpha}}{}_{\bar{\hat{\beta}}}:=I^{\hat{\alpha}\hat{\gamma}}h_{\bar{\hat{\beta}}\hat{\gamma}}\,

It satisfies

J^{\hat{\alpha}}{}_{\bar{\hat{\gamma}}}\,\overline{J}^{\,\bar{\hat{\gamma}}}{}_{\hat{\beta}}=

and plays the role of a real structure on
\mathbb{C}^{4|4\mathcal{N}}

Real twistors then correspond to the eigenspace

\displaystyle Z^{\hat{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{R}^{4|4\mathcal{N}}

\mathbb{C}^{4|\mathcal{N}}

\overline{M}^{3|3\mathcal{N}}

One can prove that this action is transitive and that one recovers in this way

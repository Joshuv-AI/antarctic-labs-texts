Hermitian null loci
Quang-Tuan Dang

Abstract
. We establish a transcendental generalization of Nakamaye’s theorem to compact complex manifolds when the form is not assumed to be closed. We apply the recent analytic technique developed by Collins–Tosatti to show that the non-Hermitian locus of a nef and big ( 1 , 1 ) (1,1) -form, which is not necessarily closed, on a compact complex manifold equals the union of all positive-dimensional analytic subvarieties where the restriction of the form is not big (null locus). As an application, we can give an alternative proof of the Nakai–Moishezon criterion of Buchdahl and Lamari for complex surfaces and generalize this result in higher dimensions. Finally, we investigate finite time non-collapsing singularities of the Chern–Ricci flow, partially answering a question raised by Tosatti–Weinkove.

Introduction

Characterizing the augmented base locus of a nef and big line bundle on a smooth algebraic variety over
\mathbb{C}
was initially motivated by applications to the diophantine approximation problem
[
32
]
. Nakamaye showed in
[
31
]
that the augmented base locus of a nef and big line bundle on a projective manifold equal to its null locus (which is the union of all irreducible subvarieties where the restriction of the bundle is not big). There are numerous generalizations of the latter result in
\mathbb{R}
-divisors
[
21
]
, in possibly singular varieties
[
2
,
8
,
3
]
, and in positive characteristics
[
9
]
. We refer interested readers to
[
42
]
for a comprehensive overview.

Our primary goal in this paper is to extend Nakamaye’s theorem to the context of compact non-Kähler manifolds with non-closed differential forms.
Let
X
be a compact complex manifold of dimension
n
, equipped with a Hermitian metric
\omega_{X}
.
We denote by
{\rm d^{c}}=\frac{1}{2i}(\partial-\bar{\partial})
so that
\mathrm{dd^{c}}=i\partial\bar{\partial}
.
Let
\theta
be a smooth real (1,1) form and let
[\theta]_{\partial\bar{\partial}}
denote the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class of forms (resp. currents):
[\theta]_{\partial\bar{\partial}}=\{\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi:u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})\;(\text{resp.}\,u\in L^{1}(X))\}.
For simplicity, we omit the subscript
\partial\bar{\partial}
.
When
\theta
is closed, the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
coincides with its Bott–Chern cohomology class in
H^{1,1}_{\rm BC}(X,\mathbb{R})
.
A
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
is said to be
Hermitian
(or
positive
) if it contains at least a positive definite form, i.e.,
\exists\,\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})
such that
\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi
is a Hermitian metric. Although
\theta
is not closed in general, one can still meaningfully define the concept (in the analytic sense) of nef
\partial\bar{\partial}
-classes; cf. Def.
2.1
. Similarly, a
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
is said to be
big
if it contains a positive current that dominates a Hermitian metric.
It should be emphasized that the previous concepts can be defined when the underlying variety is possibly singular; cf. Sect.
2.3
.
By Demailly’s regularization theorem, the Hermitian current in a big class can be taken to have (almost) analytic singularities (in the sense of
[
17
, Theorem 3.2]
). In particular, the Hermitian current is smooth in the complement of a Zariski closed subset of
X
. The smallest possible Zariski closed subset of
X
that can be taken in this way is the non-Hermitian locus of a big
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
, denoted by
\NK(\theta)
; cf.
[
5
, Definition 3.16]
.

A nef and big
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
, in general, may not be positive, and another subset to measure its non-positivity is its null locus, denoted by
\Null(\theta)
, explicitly introduced by Nakamaye. The latter subset is defined as the union of all positive-dimensional irreducible analytic subvarieties
V\subset X
where the restriction
\theta|_{V}
is not big. Clearly, the null locus is a proper subset of
X
. Repeating line by line the proof of Collins–Tosatti
[
12
]
, one can prove that given a nef and big
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
, its non-Hermitian locus and its null locus coincide. The proof does not require the closedness of the representative form.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold.
Let
\theta
be a smooth real (1,1) form whose
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
is nef and big. Then

\NK(\theta)=\Null(\theta).

In the Kähler case (or the Fujiki class
\mathcal{C}
in general), the bigness of a closed nef form
\theta
is equivalent to the positivity property of its self-intersection number, i.e.,
\int_{X}\theta^{n}>0
. Unlike the Kähler case,
the difficulty we face is that the volume of two forms in the same
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class could be different in general. Let
X
be a compact complex manifold of dimension
n
, equipped with a Hermitian metric
\omega_{X}
.
A question raised by Guedj–Lu
[
27
]
is whether

v_{+}(X,\omega_{X})=\sup\left\{\int_{X}(\omega_{X}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi)^{n}:\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X),\omega_{X}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi>0\right\}<+\infty;

v_{-}(X,\omega_{X})=\inf\left\{\int_{X}(\omega_{X}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi)^{n}:\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X),\omega_{X}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi>0\right\}>0.

We refer to
[
1
]
for some classes of manifolds that satisfy the condition above.
We say that a Hermitian metric
\omega_{X}
satisfies the volume condition (

H
X

) if:

(
H_{X}
)

v_{+}(X,\omega_{X})<+\infty\quad\text{and}\;v_{-}(V,\omega_{X}|_{V})>0,

for all closed positive-dimensional submanifolds
V\subset X
.

Remark 1.2
.

It has been shown in
[
1
, Theorem A]
that for any closed submanifold
V\subset X
,

v_{+}(X,\omega_{X})<+\infty\Longrightarrow v_{+}(V,\omega_{X}|_{V})<+\infty.

It is important to emphasize that the assumption (

H
X

) occurs in some contexts of complex differential geometry. For instance, if
\omega_{X}
satisfies the following condition
\mathrm{dd^{c}}\omega_{X}=0
,
{\rm d}\omega_{X}\wedge{\rm d^{c}}\omega_{X}=0
then by
[
11
]
, it is equivalent to the volume with respect to a form belonging to the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\omega_{X}]
is unchanged. That is, for any closed submanifold
V\subset X
,

\int_{V}(\omega_{X}|_{V}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi)^{\dim V}=\int_{V}\omega_{X}|_{V}^{\dim V},\forall\;{\omega_{X}}|_{V}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi>0.

These metrics, introduced by Guan and Li
[
26
]
, are referred to as the Guan–Li metrics in this article (cf. also
[
23
]
).

Given
\theta
a smooth real (1,1) form on
X
, if
\theta
is nef (cf. Def.
2.1
), we can define

\widehat{v}_{-}(X,\theta):=\inf_{\varepsilon>0}v_{-}(X,(1-\varepsilon)\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}).

Although the form
(1-\varepsilon)\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}
is not positive, one could find, by definition, for any
\varepsilon>0
, a smooth function
\varphi_{\varepsilon}
such that
\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}\geq-\varepsilon\omega_{X}
so that

(1-\varepsilon)\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}(1-\varepsilon)\varphi_{\varepsilon}\geq\varepsilon^{2}\omega_{X}.

Hence, the quantity
v_{-}(X,(1-\varepsilon)\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X})
can be understood as
v_{-}(X,(1-\varepsilon)\theta+\varepsilon\omega_{X}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}(1-\varepsilon)\varphi_{\varepsilon})
. Our main theorem is the following.

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold equipped with a Hermitian metric
\omega_{X}
.
Assume that
\omega_{X}
satisfies the condition (

H
X

). Let
\theta
be a
smooth real (1,1) form whose
\partial\bar{\partial}
class is nef with
\widehat{v}_{-}(X,\theta)>0
.
Then
[\theta]
is big and

(1.1)

\NK(\theta)=\Null(\theta)=\bigcup_{\widehat{v}_{-}(V,\theta|_{V})=0}V,

where the union is taken over all purely positive-dimensional irreducible analytic subvarieties
V
of
X
.

In particular, when
\theta
is closed or merely satisfies the so-called Guan–Li condition:
\mathrm{dd^{c}}\theta=0
,
{\rm d}\theta\wedge{\rm d^{c}}\theta=0
(equivalent to
\mathrm{dd^{c}}\theta^{k}=0
for all
k=1,\dots,n-1
), then we obtain
\widehat{v}_{-}(V,\theta|_{V})=\int_{V}\theta^{\dim V}
by
[
27
, Lemma 4.4]
, provided that
v_{+}(X,\omega_{X})<\infty
. This directly implies the Collins–Tosatti result in the case of compact complex manifolds belonging to the Fujiki class
\mathcal{C}
.

A direct application of Theorem
A
is to establish the Nakai–Moishezon criterion for some compact non-Kähler manifolds, which generalizes the
fundamental result of Demailly and Păun
[
17
]
for compact Kähler manifolds, and Buchdahl
[
7
]
and Lamari
[
30
]
for compact non-Kähler surfaces. Our proof relies essentially on the mass concentration result of Demailly–Păun, whose proof was simplified by Chiose
[
10
]
and Popovici
[
35
]
. Using Theorem
A
allows one to avoid the inductive argument on dimension established in
[
17
]
.
Inspired by the works of Collins–Tosatti
[
13
]
and Das–Hacon–Păun
[
16
]
, we establish the Nakai–Moishezon criterion on a possibly singular compact analytic normal variety, but the proof relies on the induction on dimension.

Theorem B
.

Let
X
be a compact analytic normal variety of pure dimension
n
and
\omega_{X}
a Hermitian metric on
X
such that
\mathrm{dd^{c}}\omega_{X}^{k}=0
for all
k=1,\ldots,n-1
. Let
\theta
be a smooth real (1,1) form on
X
such that

(1)

\mathrm{dd^{c}}\theta^{k}=0
, for any
k=1,\dots,n-1
;

(2)

for all
k=1,\ldots,n
,

\int_{X}\theta^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}>0;

(3)

for every irreducible analytic
subvariety
V\subset X
with
\dim V>0
,

\int_{V}\theta^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{\dim V-k}>0,\,\;\forall\,k=1,\dots,\dim V.

Then the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
is Hermitian.

We note that condition (2) can be absorbed in (3) when
V=X
. In the case of complex smooth surfaces, thanks to
[
6
, Proposition 5]
, we can relax the condition
(3)
to require only that
\int_{V}\theta>0
for every irreducible curve
V\subset X
with strictly negative self-intersection
V\cdot V<0
.
We refer to Sect.
2.3
for the definition of differential forms, Hermitian metrics, and integrals on singular analytic varieties.

Finally, we study finite time singularities of the Chern–Ricci flow on compact complex manifolds, partially answering a question raised by Tosatti–Weinkove.
Let
\omega=\omega(t)
be a solution of the Chern–Ricci flow on
X
, for
t\in[0,T[
, starting with a Hermitian metric
\omega_{0}
,

(1.2)

\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial t}=-\ric(\omega),\quad\omega(0)=\omega_{0},

where
\ric(\omega)
is the Chern–Ricci form (also called the first Chern form) of the Hermitian metric
\omega
.
It is classical that solving the Chern–Ricci flow boils down to solving a nonlinear parabolic scalar equation in
\varphi=\varphi(t)
of the form

\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}=\log\frac{(\theta_{t}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi)^{n}}{\omega_{0}^{n}},\quad\theta_{t}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi>0,\;\varphi(0)=0,

where
\theta_{t}:=\omega_{0}-t\ric(\omega_{0})
.
Tosatti and Weinkove
[
39
]
characterized the maximal existence time
T
of the flow as

T=\sup\{t>0:\exists\,\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)\;\text{with}\;\theta_{t}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\psi>0\},

by this, we mean that
T
is such that for all
t<T
, the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta_{t}]
is Hermitian.
Suppose that the flow develops a finite time singularity
T<\infty
. We observe that the limiting
\partial\bar{\partial}

class
[\theta_{T}]
is nef but not Hermitian.We define the set of singularities of the flow, denoted by
\Z(\omega(t))
, to be the complement of the set of all points
x\in X
such that there is a neighborhood
U\ni x
in which
\omega(t)
converges smoothly to a Hermitian metric
\omega_{T}
as
t\to T^{-}
.
Furthermore, we can show that the singularity set of the Chern–Ricci flow is equal to the set on which the Chern scalar curvature blows up. The latter was studied by Gill–Smith
[
24
]
, generalizing Zhang’s analog result for Kähler–Ricci flows.

Tosatti and Weinkove
[
40
, Question 6.1]
posed a question of whether singularities of the Chern–Ricci flow develop precisely along analytic subvarieties of
X
?
In the Kähler case, this question was initially raised by Feldman, Ilmanen, and Knopf and later answered affirmatively by Collins and Tosatti
[
12
, Theorem 1.5]
. Gill and Smith
[
24
, Theorem 1.2]
gave an affirmative answer
for the case when
X
is a complex surface and
\omega_{0}
is Gauduchon. As observed in
[
40
, Secion 6]
, the answer is affirmative when the volume with respect to
\omega(t)
shrinks to zero as
t\to T
. We investigate non-collapsing the case when
\widehat{v}_{-}(X,\theta_{T})>0
and provide an answer to this question on some underlying manifolds.

Theorem C
.

Let
(X,\omega_{0})
be a compact Hermitian manifold of dimension
n
. Let
\omega(t)
be a solution of the Chern–Ricci flow starting at
\omega_{0}
, which develops
a singularity at finite time
T
. Assume that the limiting form
\theta_{T}=\omega_{0}-T\ric(\omega_{0})
satisfies
\widehat{v}_{-}(X,\theta_{T})>0
.
Then if either

•

n=2
and
\omega_{0}
is arbitrary,

•

or
n\geq 3
,
\mathrm{dd^{c}}\omega_{0}=0
,
{\rm d}\omega_{0}\wedge{\rm d^{c}}\omega_{0}=0
,

then the metrics
\omega(t)
develop singularities precisely along the analytic set
\Null(\theta_{T})
.

This theorem generalizes Gill–Smith’s result when the underlying manifold
X
is a surface, but the initial metric
\omega_{0}
is given to be arbitrary and also partially answers the question of Tosatti and Weinkove.
With assumptions in this theorem, we have that the limiting form
\theta_{T}
is big, so its null locus
\Null(\theta_{T})
is a proper analytic subset of
X
(since
[\theta_{T}]
is not Hermitian), and our theorem shows that no singularities develop on its complement. We have seen from Theorem
A
that
\Null(\theta_{T})
is precisely the union of all purely positive-dimensional irreducible subvarieties
V
of
X
with
v_{-}(V,\omega(t))
shrinking to zero as
t\to T^{-}
, so the singularities of the flow develop at least there. We will see in Theorem
4.1
that the scalar curvature of
\omega(t)
is locally uniformly bounded on compact sets outside singularities. Furthermore, it should be emphasized that in the setting of our theorem, as
t\to T^{-}
, the metrics
\omega(t)
weakly converge to a positive current
\omega_{T}
that is smooth on
X\backslash\Null(\theta_{T})
and has minimal singularities.

A crucial case of such finite time singularities occurs when the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta_{T}]
contains a pullback of a Hermitian metric via the blowdown of an exceptional divisor to a point. Interesting results were obtained by Tosatti and Weinkove
[
38
]
, Tô
[
37
]
, and Nie
[
33
]
, in which they proved that the Chern–Ricci flow performs a
canonical surgical contraction
.

This paper is structured as follows. In Sect.
2
, we introduce notation and definitions and briefly review fundamental results extended to the non-Kähler context. In Sect.
3
, we establish an extension-type theorem for Hermitian currents with analytic singularities, which enables us to prove Theorem
A
, and we also prove Theorem
B
there.
Finally, we investigate finite time singularities of the Chern–Ricci flow and prove Theorem
C
in Sect.
4
.

Let

2.1.
\partial\bar{\partial}
-classes

It is well-known that the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-lemma does not hold on an arbitrarily complex manifold, so it is better to work with the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class of differential forms. We denote by
\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C})
(resp.
\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{R})
) the space of smooth (resp. real) forms of bidgree
(p,q)
. We say that two forms
\theta,\eta\in\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{C})
are equivalent if there exists a smooth form
\psi\in\mathcal{A}^{p-1,q-1}(X,\mathbb{C})
such that

\theta-\eta=\mathrm{dd^{c}}\psi.

Since then, we let
[\theta]_{\partial\bar{\partial}}
denote the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class with smooth representative
\theta
. For simplicity, we skip the subscript
\partial\bar{\partial}
, and unless we emphasize the
\partial\bar{\partial}
class
[\theta]
is interpreted as the set of forms
\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\psi
for all
\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)
.

We refer the interested reader to
[
4
, Sect. 1]
for more details.

Definition 2.1
.

A smooth real (1,1) form
\theta
is called

if for any
\varepsilon>0

there exists a form
\theta_{\varepsilon}\in[\theta]
such that
\theta_{\varepsilon}\geq-\varepsilon\omega_{X}
, i.e.,

\exists\,\psi_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})
such that
\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\psi_{\varepsilon}\geq-\varepsilon\omega_{X}
. In this case, the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
is said to be nef.

If there is a Hermitian metric
\omega\in[\theta]
, then we say that the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
is
Hermitian
.

It should be emphasized that, unlike in the Kähler case, even if two Hermitian metrics
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
lie in the same
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class, i.e., there exists a function
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)
such that
\omega^{\prime}=\omega+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi
, the volumes with respect to
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
are generally not equal. Chiose
[
11
, Theorem 0.1]
showed that the preservation of Monge–Ampère masses is equivalent to the condition introduced by Guan–Li
[
26
]
: namely, that
\int_{X}\omega^{n}=\int_{X}\omega_{X}^{n}
for all
\omega\in[\omega_{X}]
Hermitian if and only if
\mathrm{dd^{c}}\omega_{X}=0
and
{\rm d}\omega_{X}\wedge{\rm d^{c}}\omega_{X}=0
. The latter condition is equivalent to
\mathrm{dd^{c}}\omega_{X}^{k}=0
for all

; see also
[
11
, Theorem 0.2]
for additional equivalent conditions.

We recall some quantities on Monge–Ampère volume bounds introduced by Guedj and Lu
[

]
.

.

We define

v_{+}(X,\omega_{X})=\sup\left\{\int_{X}\omega^{n}:\forall\,\omega\in[\omega_{X}],\,\omega\,\text{Hermitian}\right\},

and

v_{-}(X,\omega_{X})=\inf\left\{\int_{X}\omega^{n}:\forall\,\omega\in[\omega_{X}],\,\omega\,\text{Hermitian}\right\}.

A question raised by Guedj and Lu

Definition 2.4
.

If

Although the form

2.2.

The

Lemma 2.7

Let

\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi=\psi\}}(\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi)^{n}\geq\mathbf{1}_{\{\varphi=\psi\}}(\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\psi)^{n}.

Proof.

The proof directly follows Bedford–Taylor’s maximum principle
[

in the local context. See also

, Lemma 1.2]
for a proof in the global one.
∎

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{I}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
be an analytic coherent ideal sheaf and
c>0
.
A quasi-psh function
\varphi
on X is said to have
almost analytic singularities
of type
(\mathcal{I},c)
if the following are satisfied

(i)

for any
x\in X
, there exists a neighborhood
U
of
x
such that on
U
we have

\varphi=c\log\left(\sum_{j}|f_{j}|^{2}\right)+g,

where the
f_{j}
’s are local generators of a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{I}
, and
g
is a bounded function on
U
,

(ii)

there is a (global) proper modification
\pi:X^{\prime}\to X
of
X
, obtained as a finite composition of blow-ups with smooth
centers, and an effective divisor
\sum_{\ell}a_{\ell}D_{\ell}
with normal crossings
such that one can write
\varphi\circ\pi
locally as

\varphi\circ\pi=c\sum_{\ell}a_{\ell}\log|g_{\ell}|^{2}+h,

where
(g_{\ell}=0)
are local equations of the divisors
D_{\ell}
and

h
is a smooth function.

If a current
T=\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi
with
\varphi
a quasi-psh function having almost analytic singularities (along subscheme
V(\mathcal{I})
induced by
\mathcal{I}
), then one says that
T
has
almost analytic singularities
.

We emphasize that the definition above is a bit lengthy, but turns out to be the most
suitable one for this paper. We will see below that a
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class with appropriate positivity contains plenty of currents with such singularities.

Definition 2.9
.

We say that a real (1,1)-current
T
is a
Hermitian current
if there exists a Hermitian metric
\omega
such that
T\geq\omega
.

The
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
is said to be
big
if it contains a Hermitian current, i.e., there exists
\varphi\in\PSH(X,\theta)
such that
T=\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi\geq\omega
for some Hermitian form
\omega
. In this case, the smooth representative
\theta
is also called big.

We introduce the notion of psh envelopes understood as the
least singularity
function lying below a prescribed one.

Definition 2.10
.

Given a Borel function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
, we define the
\theta
-psh envelope
of
f
by

P_{\theta}(f):=(\sup\{u\in\PSH(X,\theta):u\leq f\})^{*},

where the
∗
means that we take the upper semi-continuous regularization.

The
\theta
-psh envelope
P_{\theta}(f)
might be
\equiv-\infty
when the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
contains no positive currents. When
\theta
is big, and
f
is bounded from below,
P_{\theta}(f)
is well defined as a
\theta
-psh function.

We recall the basic type of regularization due to Demailly; cf.
[
17
, Lemma 2.1, Theorem 3.2]
.

Theorem 2.11

(Demailly’s regularization theorem)
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold equipped with a Hermitian form
\omega
.
Let
T=\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi
be a real (1,1) current; i.e.,
\theta
is a smooth real (1,1) form and
\varphi
is quasi-psh.
Suppose that
T\geq\gamma
for some smooth (1,1) form
\gamma
. Then, there exists a decreasing sequence of quasi-psh functions
\varphi_{m}
such that

(1)

T_{m}=\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{m}
are currents with almost analytic singularities;

(2)

T_{m}\to T
weakly, and
\varphi_{m}
decreases to
\varphi
as
m\to\infty
;

(3)

T_{m}\geq\gamma-\varepsilon_{m}\omega
for some sequence
\varepsilon_{m}\searrow 0
.

By replacing
T
(resp.
\gamma
) with
T-\theta
(resp.
\gamma-\theta
) we can work with
T=\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi
a closed quasi-positive current.
The proof relies on the appropriate technique using an asymptotic Bergman kernel procedure as we describe what happens locally. If
\varphi
is a psh function on the ball
B\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
one considers for any
m\geq 1
the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}(B,m\varphi)
of
L^{2}
holomorphic function on
B
such that
\int_{B}|f|^{2}e^{-m\varphi}dV<\infty
. We choose a Hilbert basic
(f_{k}^{m})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathcal{H}(B,m\varphi)
and put
\varphi_{m}:=\frac{1}{m}\log\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|f_{k}^{m}|^{2}\right)
. One can check that
\varphi_{m}
is well defined, psh, with analytic singularities, and almost increasing. The difficult part then is to prove that
\varphi_{m}
converges to
\varphi
as
m
goes to infinity. To get the global approximations, one glues the local ones by using a partition of unity argument. In this gluing process, a small loss of positivity is established as a result of the change of holomorphic coordinates. This is where the
\varepsilon_{m}
’s come from. For each
m
, the corresponding modification
\mu_{m}
is then obtained by principalization of singularities of the multiplier ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}(m\varphi)
so that
\mu_{m}^{*}\mathcal{I}(m\varphi)
is the principal ideal sheaf associated with a normal crossing divisor, due to the fundamental result of Hironaka; cf.
[
43
]
.

From the above regularization,
a big
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
contains various Hermitian currents with almost analytic singularities.

We introduce the definition of the non-Hermitian locus of a big
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class, analogous to the one in
[
5
]
.

Definition 2.12

(non-Hermitian locus)
.

If
\theta
is a big (1,1) form on
X
, we define its
non-Hermitian locus
to be the set

\NK(\theta)=\bigcap_{T\in[\theta]}\Z(T),

where the intersection is taken over all Hermitian currents with almost analytic singularities in the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
.

We investigate the bigness in some particular context of a fixed Hermitian metric.

Theorem 2.13

(Guedj–Lu)
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold equipped with a Hermitian metric
\omega_{X}

satisfying
v_{+}(\omega_{X})<+\infty
. Let
\theta
be a nef (1,1) form with
\widehat{v}_{-}(X,\theta)>0
. Then the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
is big, i.e., there exists a Hermitian current
T
in
[\theta]
with almost analytic singularities.

Proof.

The proof was given in
[
27
, Theorem 4.6]
, which was inspired by Chiose
[
10
]
and Popovici
[
35
]

based on the characterization result of Lamari
[
30
]
.

We note that, without the nefness condition, Boucksom, Guedj and Lu
[
4
]
recently proved that the positivity of the lower volume of
\theta
implies the existence of Hermitian current.
∎

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
\theta
be a big (1,1)-form. Then there exists a Hermitian current
T_{0}
with almost analytic singularities such that
\NK(\theta)=\Z(T_{0})
.
In particular, the non-Hermitian locus of a big class
[\theta]
is an analytic subvariety of
X
.

Proof.

The proof is quite close to that of
[
5
, Theorem 3.17]
.
We denote by
\mathcal{S}
the set of all Hermitian currents with almost analytic singularities in the class
[\theta]
. Fix
T_{j}=\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{j}\in\mathcal{S}
for
j=1,2
. First, we claim that there exists a Hermitian current with almost analytic singularities
T
such that
\Z(T)\subset\Z(T_{1})\cap\Z(T_{2})
. Indeed, if we set
\varphi_{3}=\max(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
then
T_{3}=\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\phi_{3}
is a Hermitian current such that
(\varphi_{3}=-\infty)\subset\Z(T_{1})\cap\Z(T_{2})
. By Theorem
2.11
, we can find a Hermitian current
T=\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
with almost analytic singularities such that
\varphi\geq\varphi_{3}
so
\Z(T)\subset\Z(T_{1})\cap\Z(T_{2})
. This proves our claim.

Thanks to the claim above and Theorem
2.11
again, we can construct a sequence of currents
T_{m}=\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{m}\in\mathcal{S}
such that
\Z(T_{m})
is a decreasing sequence of analytic subsets. By the strong Noetherian property,
\Z(T_{m})
must be stationary for
m\geq m_{0}
. Therefore, we obtain
\NK(\theta)=\Z(T_{0})
for
T_{0}\coloneqq T_{m_{0}}
, as desired.

Moreover, if
\NK(\theta)=\varnothing
then
\Z(T_{0})=\varnothing
, which means that
T_{0}
is smooth on
X
, so
T_{0}
is in fact a Hermitian form.
∎

In summary, we have the following.

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold equipped with a positive form
\omega_{X}
satisfying
v_{+}(\omega_{X})<+\infty
. Let
\theta
be a nef (1,1) form with
\widehat{v}_{-}(X,\theta)>0
. Then there exists a Hermitian current
T
in the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
with almost analytic singularities such that

\Z(T)=\NK(\theta).

We recall the following lemma, which implies that the non-Hermitian locus of a big form has no isolated points.

Lemma 2.16
.

If
\theta
is a big (1,1)-form, then the analytic set
\nh(\theta)
has no isolated points.

Proof.

We refer to
[
12
, Lemma 3.1]
. The closedness is not involved.
∎

2.3.
Extension on complex analytic spaces

We give here a brief recap of the extension of pluripotential theory to compact complex spaces. We refer the interested readers to
[
18
]
for more details.

Let
X
be a reduced complex analytic space of pure dimension
n
. We will let
X_{\rm reg}
denote the locus of regular points of
X
, and
X_{\rm sing}:=X\backslash X_{\rm reg}
the locus of singular points. We see that
X_{\rm reg}
is a complex manifold of dimension
n
and
X_{\rm sing}
is an analytic subset of
X
of complex codimension
\geq
1. If
X=\bigcup_{\alpha}U_{\alpha}
is an open covering,
we consider local embeddings
j_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{N}
into analytic subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
for some
N\geq 1
. By these local embeddings, one can define the sheaves of smooth
(p,q)
-forms
\mathcal{A}^{p,q}
, and the space of currents is, by definition, the dual of the space of differential forms as in the smooth case. The differential operators
{\rm d}
,
\partial
,
\bar{\partial}
,
{\rm d^{c}}
, and
\mathrm{dd^{c}}
satisfy the usual rules and are then well defined by duality.
We can also define the concepts of Hermitian
\partial\bar{\partial}
-classes, nef
\partial\bar{\partial}
-classes, big
\partial\bar{\partial}
-classes, etc.

We next move on to the notion of psh functions. Similarly, by the local embeddings above, we say that a function
u:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is psh on
X
if it is locally the restriction of a psh function defined an open set
\Omega_{\alpha}
of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, which contains
j_{\alpha}(U_{\alpha})
.

Another way to define a psh function is introduced by Demailly: a function
u
is weakly psh on
X
if it is locally bounded from above on
X
and its restriction to the complex manifold
X_{\rm reg}
is psh. We can extend it to
X
by

u^{*}(x):=\limsup_{X_{\rm reg}\ni y\to x}u(y).

The function
u^{*}
is upper semi-continuous, locally integrable on
X
, and
\mathrm{dd^{c}}u^{*}
is a positive current. Since
X
is locally irreducible, thanks to
[
18
, Théorème 1.10]
,
u^{*}
is psh on
X
.
In the same way, one can define the notion of quasi-psh functions with almost analytic singularities, Hermitian currents, non-Hermitian loci, etc.

We end this section by discussing the integration over analytic (irreducible) subvarieties of a smooth complex manifold
X
. If
V
is an analytic subvariety of
X
of pure dimension
k>0
, for any smooth
(k,k)
form
\alpha
on
X
, then the integral

\int_{V}\alpha:=\int_{V_{\rm reg}}\alpha

is finite thanks to the fundamental result of Lelong; see, e.g.,
[
25
, page 32]
. In the same way, we can define the quantities
v_{-}(V,\omega_{V})
,
v_{+}(V,\omega_{V})
and
\widehat{v}_{-}(V,\theta)
.
Furthermore, for a smooth real
(k-1,k-1)
-form
\beta
, we have by
[
25
, page 33]
,

\int_{V}\mathrm{dd^{c}}\beta=0.

In particular, we can extend Lemma
2.5
to the singular context, namely, for any nef (1,1) form
\theta
with
\mathrm{dd^{c}}\theta^{\ell}=0
for all
1\leq\ell\leq k
, then

\widehat{v}_{-}(V,\theta|_{V})=\int_{V}\theta^{k}.

3.
Null loci and Nakai–Moishezon criterions

3.1.
Extension theorem

We define the null locus of a big form, following the algebraic version in
[
31
]
.

Definition 3.1

(Null locus)
.

Let
\theta
be a big (1,1) form. We define the
null locus
of
\theta
, denoted by
\Null(\theta)
, to be the set

\Null(\theta)=\bigcup_{\theta|_{V}\,\text{is not big}}V,

where the union is taken over all purely positive-dimensional irreducible analytic subvarieties
V\subset X
.

We describe the null locus with the following example.

Example 3.2
.

Let
Y
be a compact complex manifold of dimension
n
equipped with a positive (1,1) form
\omega_{Y}
. Let
\pi:X\to Y
be a blow-up of a point
y\in Y
with the exceptional divisor
E=\pi^{-1}(y)\simeq\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{X}(E)
denote the line bundle associated with
E
and
\Theta_{h}(E)
the curvature form associated with a Hermitian metric
h
on
\mathcal{O}_{X}(E)
.
The form
\theta=\pi^{*}\omega_{Y}
is semipositive, so it is nef. It was shown (from, e.g.,
[
17
, Lemma 3.5]
) that there exists a smooth Hermitian metric
h
on the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{X}(E)
with curvature
\Theta_{h}(E)
such that
\pi^{*}\omega_{Y}-\varepsilon\Theta_{h}(E)
is positive definite on
X
for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. In particular, let
s
be a section of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(E)
vanishing along
E
of order 1. Then for any
\varepsilon
small, we have that
\pi^{*}\omega_{Y}+\varepsilon\mathrm{dd^{c}}\log|s|^{2}_{h}
is a Hermitian current on
X
that belongs to the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
. Obviously, one sees that the latter current has almost analytic singularities precisely along
E
. Since
\theta
is positive on
X\backslash E
, we also observe that

E=\bigcup_{\theta|_{V}\;\text{is not big}}V,

where the union is taken over all positive-dimensional irreducible analytic subvarieties
V\subset X
.

The above example motivates us to prove our main theorem in this section.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold of dimension
n
. Let
\theta
be a nef and big (1,1) form. Then

\NK(\theta)=\Null(\theta).

The key ingredient in the proof of the theorem above is an extension-type result for a Hermitian current with almost analytic singularities in nef and big
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class on complex subvarieties, generalizing
[
12
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(X,\omega_{X})
be a compact complex manifold of dimension
n
. Let
\theta
be a smooth real (1,1) form on
X
, whose
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class is nef and big. Let
E=V\cup\cup_{i}Y_{i}
be an analytic closed subvariety of
X
of pure dimension
k
, where
V
and
Y_{i}
are irreducible components of
E
, and
V
a compact positive-dimensional complex submanifold of
X
.
Let
S=\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}F
be a Hermitian current with almost analytic singularities precisely along
E
.
Let
T=\theta|_{V}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi
be a Hermitian current in the class
[\theta|_{V}]
on
V
with almost analytic singularities. Then there exists a Hermitian current with almost analytic singularities
\widetilde{T}
on
X
in the
\partial\bar{\partial}
-class
[\theta]
such that

\widetilde{T}
is smooth in a neighborhood of the generic point of
V
.

When
X
belongs to the Fujiki class
\mathcal{C}
and
\theta
is closed, the result was proved by Collins and Tosatti
[
12
, Theorem 3.2]
, and later simplified by Darvas et al.
[
15
, Theorem 6.1]
. We adapt their arguments to our setting to prove the corresponding result.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

We divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1:

Lifting to the desingularization.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{T}\subset\mathcal{O}_{V}
be the coherent sheaf of ideals defining the singularities of
T
on
V
. It follows from Hironaka’s theorem that we can find a principlization of the sheaf of ideals
\mathcal{I}_{T}
(cf.
[
29
,
43
]
), that is, a finite sequence

p^{\prime}:V^{\prime}=V_{r}\xrightarrow{p_{r}}V_{r-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow V_{1}\xrightarrow{p_{1}}V_{0}=V

of blow-ups
p_{i}
with smooth centers
C_{i-1}\subset V_{i-1}
.
Next, regarding the first center
C_{0}
as a submanifold of
X
, we blow it up inside
X
and iterate
r
times to obtain a sequence

\pi:\widetilde{X}=X_{r}\xrightarrow{\pi_{r}}X_{r-1}\xrightarrow{\pi_{r-1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\pi_{2}}X_{1}\xrightarrow{\pi_{1}}X_{0}=X

of blow-ups
\pi_{i}:X_{i}\to X_{i-1}
with smooth centers
C_{i-1}\subset X_{i-1}
.
We observe that if
\widetilde{V}
is the strict transform of
V
under
\pi
, then
\widetilde{V}\to V^{\prime}

is a proper finite bimeromorphic morphism, so it is indeed an isomorphism since
V^{\prime}
is smooth (due to Zariski’s main theorem).
We also see that the exceptional divisor
E_{i}
of
\pi_{i}
has simple normal crossings with
\widetilde{V}_{i}
and
E_{i}\cap\widetilde{V}_{i}
coincides with the exceptional divisor of
p_{i}
. Finally, on
\widetilde{X}
, we get a divisor
D=\sum D_{\ell}
which has simple normal crossings with the strict transform
\widetilde{V}
of
V
via birational morphism
\pi
and

(\pi|_{\widetilde{V}})^{*}\mathcal{I}_{T}=\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{V}}\left(-\sum_{\ell}a_{\ell}D_{\ell}|_{\widetilde{V}}\right),\quad a_{\ell}>0.

Note that since the analytic subvariety cut out by
\mathcal{I}_{T}
has codimension at least 2 in
X
, it follows that all divisors
D_{\ell}
are
\pi
-exceptional divisors. Let
\widetilde{Y}_{i}
be the strict transform of
Y_{i}
in
\widetilde{X}
.

It follows from
[
17
, Lemma 3.5]
that there is a small
\delta>0
such that

\pi^{*}\omega_{X}+\delta\mathrm{dd^{c}}\log|s_{D}|^{2}_{h_{D}}\geq\widetilde{\omega}

for some Hermitian metric
\widetilde{\omega}
on
\widetilde{X}
, where
s_{D}
is a holomorphic section defining
D=\sum_{\ell}D_{\ell}
and
h_{D}
is a smooth Hermitian metric on the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}}(D)
.
Thanks to
[
17
, Lemma 2.1, Theorem 3.2]
, there exists a globally defined
C_{0}\widetilde{\omega}
-psh function
\psi
on
\widetilde{X}
for some
C_{0}>0
, with almost analytic singularities along
\cup_{i}\widetilde{Y}_{i}
.

By assumption, we assume
T=\theta|_{V}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi\geq 4\varepsilon_{0}\omega_{X}|_{V}
for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
.
If we set
\widetilde{\varphi}\coloneqq\varphi\circ\pi+\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{C_{0}}\psi+\delta\log|s_{D}|^{2}_{h_{D}}
, then
\widetilde{\varphi}
has analytic singularities along
\cup_{\ell}D_{\ell}\cup_{i}\widetilde{Y}_{i}\cup\widetilde{V}
and

\pi^{*}\theta|_{\widetilde{V}}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\widetilde{\varphi}\geq 3\varepsilon_{0}\widetilde{\omega}|_{\widetilde{V}}.

Similarly, the function
\widetilde{F}\coloneqq F+\delta\log|s_{D}|^{2}_{h_{D}}
has analytic singularities along
\cup_{\ell}D_{\ell}\cup_{i}\widetilde{Y}_{i}\cup\widetilde{V}
. Up to shrinking
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, we assume that

\pi^{*}\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\widetilde{F}\geq 3\varepsilon_{0}\widetilde{\omega}.

Since
\widetilde{V}
is a closed submanifold of
\widetilde{X}
and
\sum_{\ell}D_{\ell}|_{\widetilde{V}}
has simple normal crossing support, we can find a finite open covering
\{W_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
of
\widetilde{V}
such that on each local chart
W_{j}
there exist local coordinates
z_{j}=(z_{j}^{1},\ldots z_{j}^{n})
with
\widetilde{V}\cap W_{j}=\{z_{j}^{1}=\cdots=z_{j}^{n-k}=0\}
, where
k=\dim\widetilde{V}
, and with
\cup_{\ell}D_{\ell}\cap W_{j}=\{z^{i_{1}}_{j}\cdots z^{i_{p}}_{j}=0\}
,
n-k+1\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{p}\leq n
. On
W_{j}
, the functions
\widetilde{\varphi}
and

\widetilde{F}
can be written as

\widetilde{\varphi}=\frac{\alpha}{2}\log\sum_{p=1}^{n-k}|z_{j}^{p}|^{2a_{p}}+\mathcal{C}^{\infty},\quad\widetilde{F}=\frac{\beta}{2}\log\sum_{p=1}^{n-k}|z_{j}^{p}|^{2b_{p}}+\mathcal{C}^{\infty}.

Since
\pi^{*}\theta
is nef, for any small
\lambda\in(0,1)
there exists a smooth function
\rho_{\lambda}
such that

\pi^{*}\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\rho_{\lambda}\geq-3\lambda\varepsilon_{0}\widetilde{\omega}

holds on
\widetilde{X}
, and we can normalize
\rho_{\lambda}
so that it is strictly positive. Then we have

\pi^{*}\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}(\lambda\widetilde{F}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{\lambda})\geq 3\varepsilon_{0}\lambda^{2}\widetilde{\omega}.

We choose
\lambda
so small that
\lambda\beta b_{p}<\alpha a_{p}
for any
p=\overline{1,n-k}
. We stress that this is the only place where the nefness of
\theta
is used.

Set
F^{\prime}\coloneqq\lambda\widetilde{F}+(1-\lambda)\rho_{\lambda}
. We take
0<\varepsilon<\lambda^{2}\varepsilon_{0}
so that

\pi^{*}\theta|_{\widetilde{V}}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\widetilde{\varphi}\geq 3\varepsilon\widetilde{\omega}|_{\widetilde{V}},\;\text{and}\;\pi^{*}\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}F^{\prime}\geq 3\varepsilon\widetilde{\omega}.

Step 2:

Gluing technique of Richberg.
In this step, we follow the trick of
[
15
]
.

We denote by
z^{\prime}_{j}=(z^{1}_{j},\ldots,z^{n-k}_{j})
and
z^{\prime\prime}_{j}=(z^{n-k+1}_{j},\ldots,z^{n}_{j})
on
W_{j}
. We define a function
\varphi_{j}
on
W_{j}
by

\varphi_{j}(z_{j}^{\prime},z_{j}^{\prime\prime})=\log\Big[e^{\widetilde{\varphi}(0,z_{j}^{\prime\prime})}+e^{F^{\prime}(z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime})}\Big]+A\cdot\textrm{dist}((z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime}),\widetilde{V})^{2},

where
dist
denotes the Riemannian distance with respect to
\widetilde{\omega}
and
A>0
.
Up to shrinking the
W_{j}
’s if necessary, we can choose
A>0
so large that
\widetilde{\varphi}(0,z^{\prime\prime}_{j})+A\cdot\textrm{dist}((z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime}),V)^{2}

and
F^{\prime}(z_{j},z^{\prime\prime}_{j})+A\cdot\textrm{dist}((z^{\prime},z^{\prime\prime}),V)^{2}
are
(\pi^{*}\theta-2\varepsilon\widetilde{\omega})
-psh. Hence,

\pi^{*}\theta+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi_{j}\geq 2\varepsilon\widetilde{\omega}\;\text{on}\;W_{j}

holds for all
j
; see, e.g.,
[
19
, I.4.16]
.
By construction,
{\varphi_{j}}
is continuous on
W_{j}\setminus\widetilde{V}
and
\varphi_{j}
has almost analytic singularities on
W_{j}
.

Claim 3.5
.

For any point
x\in W_{j}\cap W_{\ell}\cap\widetilde{V}

Let

\NK(\theta)

Proof of Theorem

We first treat the case where

Suppose by contradiction that

Since

By Theorem

If

[

Induction.
Suppose the theorem holds for any compact normal analytic variety of pure dimension smaller than

[

[

Let

such that
\theta|_{W_{Y}}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\rho_{Y}\geq\varepsilon\omega_{X}|_{W_{Y}}
for some
\varepsilon>0
.

Finally, we apply the same gluing process for
\pi_{*}\psi
and
\rho_{X_{\rm sing}}
to conclude the proof.
∎

4.
Finite time singularities of the Chern–Ricci flow

Let
(X,\omega_{0})
be a compact Hermitian manifold of dimension
n
.
We consider a solution
\omega=\omega(t)
of the Chern–Ricci flow starting from
\omega_{0}
that is given by a smooth family of Hermitian metrics satisfying

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial\omega}{\partial t}=-\ric(\omega),\;0\leq t<T\\
\omega(0)=\omega_{0}.\end{cases}

One observes that, just as the Kähler–Ricci flow, the Chern–Ricci flow is equivalent to a parabolic equation for a scalar function. That is, solving a solution to the Chern–Ricci flow starting at
\omega_{0}
boils down to solving the parabolic complex Monge–Ampère equation for
\varphi=\varphi(t)
with
t\in[0,T[
,

(4.2)

\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}=\log\frac{(\theta_{t}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi)^{n}}{\omega_{0}^{n}},\quad\theta_{t}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\varphi>0,\quad\varphi(0)=0,

where
\theta_{t}\coloneqq\omega_{0}-t\ric(\omega_{0})
. Since
X
is compact, by the standard parabolic theory,
the Chern–Ricci flow always admits a unique solution in some time interval
[0,T[
for some
0<T\leq\infty
; see, e.g.,
[
41
, Theorem 3.5]
.
As follows from
[
39
]
, the maximal existence time
T
can be characterized by

T=\sup\{t>0:\exists\,\psi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(X)\,\text{with}\,\theta_{t}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\psi>0\}.

When
\omega_{0}
satisfies the Guan–Li condition
\mathrm{dd^{c}}\omega_{0}^{k}=0
for all
1\leq k\leq n-1
, we can check that this condition is preserved by the Chern–Ricci flow. Moreover,
\mathrm{dd^{c}}\theta_{t}^{k}=0
for all
1\leq k\leq n-1
.
Thanks to the Nakai–Moishezon criterion (Theorem
B
), we obtain a geometric characterization of the maximal existence time

\begin{split}T=\sup\bigg\{t>0:&\int_{V}\theta_{t}^{k}\wedge\omega_{0}^{\dim V-k}>0,\\
&\forall\,\text{irreducible subvarieties}\,V\subset X,k=\overline{1,\dim V}\bigg\}.\end{split}

This extends the result
[
39
, Theorem 1.3]
to higher dimensions.

In this section, we study finite time singularities of the Chern–Ricci flow, proving Theorem
C
.
Suppose that
T<\infty
, so that the flow develops singularities at a finite time.
Following
[
12
]
(see also
[
40
]
), the set on which the singularities of the Chern–Ricci flow develop on
X
(depending on the initial metric
\omega_{0}
) is defined by

(4.3)

\begin{split}\Z(\omega(t))=X\backslash\{x\in X|U\ni x,\exists\,\omega_{T}\,&\text{Hermitian metric on}\,U\\
&\text{s.t.}\,\omega(t)\xrightarrow{t\to T^{-}}\omega_{T}\,\text{in}\,\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\rm loc}(U)\}.\end{split}

Following Z. Zhang
[
44
]
, we
define the singularity formation set
\Sigma
of the flow (which
depends on the initial metric
\omega_{0}
) to be the complement of the set of points
x\in X
such that there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
x
and a constant
C>0
with
|\textrm{Rm}(t)|_{\omega(t)}\leq C
on
[0,T[\times U
, where
\textrm{Rm}(t)
denotes the curvature tensor of
\omega(t)
. Also, we define the set
\Sigma^{\prime}
as the complement of the set of points
x\in X
such that there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
x
a constant
C>0
with
|R(t)|\leq C
on
[0,T[\times U
, where
R(t)
is the scalar curvature of
\omega(t)
.

Gill and Smith
[
24
]
showed that the singularity set of the flow is equal to the region on which the Chern scalar curvature blows up. The latter set is the singularity formation locus of the flow due to
[
22
]
. We give two equivalent definitions of the singularity set
\Z(\omega(t))
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that the Chern–Ricci flow (
4.1
) exists on the maximal time interval
[0,T[
with
T<\infty
. Then

\Z(\omega(t))=\Sigma=\Sigma^{\prime}.

Proof.

This was proved by M. Gill and K. Smith. However, it is unclear how the derivative
\partial_{i}Q_{1}
in (3.9) of
[
24
]
vanishes at the maximum point of
Q_{2}
.
We give the proof here,
mimicking their estimates with minor modifications.

Since a uniform bound on the curvature tensor implies an upper bound on the scalar curvature, we have
\Sigma^{\prime}\subset\Sigma
.
If the metrics
\omega(t)
converges smoothly to a Hermitian metric
\omega_{U}
on
[0,T[\times U
for some open subset
U
, then we have
|\textrm{Rm}(t)|_{\omega(t)}\leq C
, hence the inclusion
\Z(\omega(t))\subset\Sigma^{\prime}
.
It remains to show that if for any
x\in X
with an open neighborhood
U
of
x
and
C>0
satisfying
|R(t)|\leq C
on
[0,T[\times U
, then on
a possibly smaller open neighborhood
U^{\prime}
of
x
we have that
\omega(t)
converges smoothly to a Hermitian metric
\omega_{U^{\prime}}
as
t\to T^{-}
.
We note that most of estimates are local.

Fixing
x\in K\subset\subset U
a compact subset, we will establish uniform estimates on
\omega(t)
in
K
. It follows from the flow equation that

\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}=\log\frac{\omega(t)^{n}}{\omega_{0}^{n}},\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}\right)=-R.

Since
|R(t)|\leq C
on
[0,T[\times U
, we have
|{\partial^{2}_{tt}\varphi}|\leq C
. Integrating in time implies that
|\partial_{t}\varphi|\leq C
and
|\varphi|\leq C
on
[0,T[\times U
. Therefore, the quantity
H\coloneqq t\partial_{t}\varphi-\varphi-nt
is uniformly bounded on
[0,T[\times U
, also is
\partial_{t}H=-tR-n
, and

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{\omega}\right)H=-\tr_{\omega}\omega_{0}.

We set on
[0,T[\times K

Q\coloneqq\log\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega+AH+e^{H}

for
A>0
to be determined hereafter. The quantity
Q
reaches its maximum at some point
(t_{0},x_{0})\in[0,T[\times K
.
It follows from
[
39
, Proposition 3.1]
(also
[
39
, (4.2)]
) that

(4.4)

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{\omega}\right)\log\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega\leq\frac{2}{(\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega)^{2}}\textrm{Re}(g^{\bar{q}k}(T_{0})^{p}_{kp}\partial_{\bar{q}}\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega)+C\tr_{\omega}\omega_{0},

where
(T_{0})^{p}_{kp}
denote the torsion terms corresponding to
\omega_{0}
. At the maximum point
(t_{0},x_{0})
of
Q
we have
\partial_{\bar{q}}Q=0
, hence

\frac{1}{\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega}\partial_{\bar{q}}\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega+A\partial_{\bar{q}}H+e^{H}\partial_{\bar{q}}H=0.

Therefore, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields

(4.5)

\begin{split}\left|\frac{2}{(\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega)^{2}}\textrm{Re}(g^{\bar{q}k}(T_{0})^{p}_{kp}\partial_{\bar{q}}\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega)\right|&\leq\left|\frac{2}{\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega}\textrm{Re}((A+e^{H})g^{\bar{q}k}(T_{0})^{p}_{kp}\partial_{\bar{q}}H\right|\\
&\leq e^{H}|\partial H|^{2}_{\omega}+C(A+1)^{2}e^{-H}\frac{\tr_{\omega}\omega_{0}}{(\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega)^{2}}\end{split}

for uniform
C>0
only depending on the torsion term. From (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we have at
(t_{0},x_{0})
,

(4.6)

\begin{split}0\leq\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{\omega}\right)Q&\leq C(A+1)^{2}e^{-H}\frac{\tr_{\omega}\omega_{0}}{(\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega)^{2}}+C\tr_{\omega}\omega_{0}-(A+e^{H})\tr_{\omega}\omega_{0}.\end{split}

If
(\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega)^{2}\leq C(A+1)^{2}e^{-H}
at
(t_{0},x_{0})
, then

Q\leq C-\frac{1}{2}H+AH+e^{H}\leq C_{1},

we are done. Otherwise,
we choose
A=C+1
, then it follows from (
4.6
) that at
(t_{0},x_{0})
,

\tr_{\omega}\omega_{0}\leq C.

On the other hand, since
\log(\omega^{n}/\omega_{0}^{n})=\partial_{t}\varphi\leq C
, hence

\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega\leq C(\tr_{\omega}\omega_{0})^{n-1}\frac{\omega^{n}}{\omega_{0}^{n}}\leq C.

This implies that
Q
, and thus
\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega
is bounded from above on
[0,T[\times K
. Finally, the local higher-order estimates for
\omega
on
[0,T[\times U^{\prime}
with
U^{\prime}\subset K

follow, thanks to Sherman–Weinkove’s estimates
[
36
]
. The proof is complete.
∎

Consequently, the scalar curvature blows up at a finite time singularity, generalizing Zhang’s analogous result
[
44
]
in the Kähler case.

Corollary 4.2

(
[
24
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

If the Chern–Ricci flow develops a finite time singularity, then
the singularity formation set
\Sigma
is nonempty, and furthermore,

\limsup_{t\to T}\left(\sup_{X}R(t)\right)=+\infty.

Proof.

If
\Sigma=\varnothing
, then by Theorem

, replacing

with

, we would see that
\omega(t)
converges in

to a Hermitian metric

on

, a contradiction. By
[

, Lemma 3.1]
and the maximum principle, the scalar curvature

is uniformly bounded from below.
The blow-up of the supremum of the scalar curvature again follows from Theorem

.
∎

Next, we compare the singularity formation set of the flow

.

If

.

Lemma 4.5

There is a constant

Proof.

The proof relies on the trick of Phong–Sturm

, we repeat the estimates derived in the proof of

with minor modifications. Set
\psi\coloneqq\varphi-\rho+C_{0}\geq 0

We compute the evolution of

for
A>0
to be determined hereafter.
It suffices to show that

on

At the maximum point

of

Therefore, the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality yields

(4.8)

\begin{split}\left|\frac{2}{(\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega)^{2}}\textrm{Re}(g^{\bar{q}k}(T_{0})^{p}_{kp}\partial_{\bar{q}}\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega)\right|&\leq\left|\frac{2}{\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega}\textrm{Re}((A+e^{-\psi})g^{\bar{q}k}(T_{0})^{p}_{kp}\partial_{\bar{q}}\psi\right|\\

for uniform

It thus follows from (

\begin{split}0\leq\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{\omega}\right)Q&\leq C(A+1)^{2}e^{\psi}\frac{\tr_{\omega}\omega_{0}}{(\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega)^{2}}+C\tr_{\omega}\omega_{0}\\

&\leq C(A+1)^{2}e^{\psi}\frac{\tr_{\omega}\omega_{0}}{(\tr_{\omega_{0}}\omega)^{2}}+(C-A\delta_{0})\tr_{\omega}\omega_{0}+(A+1)\log\frac{\omega_{0}^{n}}{\omega^{n}},\end{split}

Proof of Theorem
4.3

We apply Lemmas

.

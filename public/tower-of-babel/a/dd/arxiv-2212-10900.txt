Counter-examples to the Hasse Principle among the twists of the Klein Quartic
Elisa Lorenzo García, Michaël Vullers Dedicated to Jaap Top on the occasion of his 62th birthday.

Abstract
. In this paper we inspect from closer the local and global points of the twists of the Klein quartic. For the local ones we use geometric arguments, while for the global ones we strongly use the modular interpretation of the twists. The main result is providing families with (conjecturally infinitely many) twists of the Klein quartic that at counter-examples to the Hasse Principle.



1.
Introduction

The Klein quartic
\mathcal{C}
is the curve in the projective plane given by the equation

X^{3}Y+Y^{3}Z+Z^{3}X=0

which is isomorphic to the modular curve
X(7)
over the cyclotomic field
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7})
.
It has a number of interesting properties which are deeply studied in
[
2
]
. For instance it is a Hurwitz curve, meaning that it has the maximum number of automorphisms possible for its genus. In fact it is the smallest genus example of such a curve.

Given a curve
C
over a field
k
, we say that a curve
C^{\prime}/k
is a twist of
C
if over an algebraic closure
\overline{k}
of
k
the curves
C
and
C^{\prime}
are isomorphic. Geometrically speaking both curves are equal, however they may have very different arithmetic properties.
In this paper we focus our attention on the following question: Suppose that
C^{\prime}
is a twist of
\mathcal{C}
,
does
C^{\prime}
violate the Hasse principle?

That is, does
C^{\prime}
have a point over every completion of
\mathbb{Q}
but not over
\mathbb{Q}
itself?

In
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
a similar question was asked and answered for the modular curves
X_{0}(N)
. The modularity of these curves is strongly used there to prove the existence of local and global points. In this paper we start by using a classification of all the twists of the Klein quartic
[
8
]
, we use geometric arguments as well as Hensel’s Lemma to deal with the existence of local points and we only use modular reasonings to study rational points. Those differ by the ones used in the aforementioned papers. Ther are based on the central result in
[
3
]
.

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

There are (conjecturally infinetely many) twists of the Klein quartic yielding counterexamples to the Hasse principle.

2.
Non-Archimedean local points

In this section we study the existence of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-points for primes
p
not dividing the discriminant or the order of the automorphism group of the twists of the Klein quartic.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
p
be a prime different from
2
,
3
or
7
and let
C^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a twist of the Klein quartic such that
p
does not divide its discriminant. Then
C^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

If
p\geq 33
, the result follow immediately from the Hasse-Weil bound, so assume
p<33
.
Let
\pi
denote the Frobenius on
\mathrm{Jac}(C^{\prime})
. Since

\#C^{\prime}(\mathbb{F}_{p})=p+1-\mathrm{tr}(\pi),

it suffices to show that
\mathrm{tr}(\pi)\neq p+1
.
In their paper
[
9
]
Meagher and Top show that

\mathrm{tr}(\pi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\text{if }p\equiv 3,5,6\,\,\mathrm{mod}\,\,7\\
3\mathrm{tr}(\pi_{E}),0,-\mathrm{tr}(\pi_{E}),\mathrm{tr}(\alpha\pi_{E})\text{ or }\mathrm{tr}(\pi_{E})&\text{if }p\equiv 1,2,4\,\,\mathrm{mod}\,\,7\end{array}\right.

where
\pi_{E}
is the Frobenius of the elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{F}_{p}
given by
y^{2}+xy=x^{3}+5x^{2}+7x
and
\alpha\in\mathrm{End}(E)
satisfies
\alpha^{2}+\alpha+2=0
.

It follows immediately that if
p\equiv 3,5,6\,\,\mathrm{mod}\,\,7
then there is nothing to prove, so assume
p\equiv 1,2,4\,\,\mathrm{mod}\,\,7
. This gives us only the cases
p=11
,
23
or
29
. For which the elliptic curve
E
is ordinary and
\mathrm{End}(E)\cong\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]\subset\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})
after making
\alpha=\frac{-1+\sqrt{-7}}{2}
. Moreover, after this identification, we get
\pi_{E}=\pm 2\pm\sqrt{-7}
,
\pm 4\pm\sqrt{-7}
,
\pm 1\pm 2\sqrt{-7}
for
p=11
,
23
,
29
respectively.

Straightforward substitution now yields
\mathrm{tr}(\pi)\neq p+1
hence
C(\mathbb{F}_{p})\neq\emptyset
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
p\neq 2,3,7
be a prime. If
C^{\prime}
is a twist of the Klein quartic with
p
not dividing its discriminant, then
C^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
\overline{C^{\prime}}/\mathbb{F}_{p}
be the reduction of
C^{\prime}
, then
\overline{C^{\prime}}
is a twist of the Klein quartic over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, in particular it is a smooth curve.
From Proposition
2.1
one has
\overline{C^{\prime}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\neq\emptyset
hence by Hensel’s Lemma it follows that
C^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\neq\emptyset
.
∎

3.
Real points

In this section, and before proving that all twists
C^{\prime}
of the Klein quartic have real points, we introduce a special twist:

C_{0}:x^{4}+y^{4}+z^{4}+6(xy^{3}+yz^{3}+zx^{3})-3(x^{2}y^{2}+y^{2}z^{2}+z^{2}x^{2})+3xyz(x+y+z)=0.

We fix the isomorphism
\phi_{0}:\,C_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
as in
[
8
, Sec. 6]
. The special property of this twist is that among all twists of the Klein quartic, the Klein quartic itself included, it is the one with the smallest field of definition of its automorphism group, namely
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})
. This is why, in
[
8
]
instead of directly computing the twists of
\mathcal{C}
, the twists of
C_{0}
are computed. Of course, both sets are equal, but the algorithm developed in
[
7
]
runs faster while inputing
C_{0}
. Moreover,
C_{0}
is isomorphic over
\mathbb{Q}
to
X(7)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
C^{\prime}
be a twist of the Klein quartic
\mathcal{C}
. Then
C^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

By the classification of twists of the Klein quartic in
[
8
]
when
K=\mathbb{R}
we get that there are only two twists of the Klein quartic up to
\mathbb{R}
-isomorphism, the Klein quartic itself and the twist
C_{0}
. The Klein quartic has for instance the rational point
(1:0:0)
. Plugging
(x:1:1)
into
C_{0}
yields a degree
4
polynomial in
x
over
\mathbb{R}
which has real solutions, hence
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})\neq\emptyset
.
∎

4.
Rational points

In order to study the rational points of the twists of the Klein quartic, we start by recalling some definitions and rephrasing the main theorem of
[
3
]
.

Let
\chi_{p}
be the quadratic character of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}
obtained from the
p
-th cyclotomic character.
The Galois action on the
p
-torsion points of an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
produces a representation

\overline{\rho}_{E,p}:\,G_{k}\rightarrow\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
,
determined up to conjugation by the isomorphism class of
E
unless the
j
-invariant
of
E
is
0
or
1728
, and with determinant
\chi_{p}
. Going the other way, we say that a
representation
\rho:\,G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

is realized by an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
if
\overline{\rho}_{E,p}=\rho
, where this last equality is considered
up to conjugation inside
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

The automorphism group of
X(p)
is naturally (via the modular interpretation) isomorphic to
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
and it is defined over the only quadratic field
k_{p}
contained in the cyclotomic field
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
. Fix
V:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-v\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
for
v
a non-square in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
. Then, by
[
3
, Prop. 2.1]
we have that the action of the non-trivial element
\tau
of
\operatorname{Gal}(k_{p}/\mathbb{Q})
on an element
g\in\operatorname{Aut}(X(p))\simeq\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is given by
g\mapsto VgV
.

A cocycle
\xi\in\operatorname{H}^{1}(G_{\mathbb{Q}},\operatorname{Aut}(X(p)))
defining a twist of
X(p)
can be seen as a representation
\rho:\,G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(X(p))\rtimes\operatorname{Gal}(k_{p}/\mathbb{Q})\simeq\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}):\,\sigma\mapsto(\xi(\sigma),\overline{\sigma})
, where the last isomorphism is given by sending
(g,1)
to
g
and
(1,\tau)
to
V
. Going the other way around, starting by a representation
\rho:\,G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
whose determinant is the quadratic character
\chi_{p}
, we can define the cocycle
\xi
given by the first projection of
\rho
by using the previous isomorphism, i.e. setting
\xi(\sigma)=\pi_{1}(\rho(\sigma))
. This cocycle produces a twist
X_{\rho}(p)
. We can as well define a cocycle
\xi^{\prime}(\sigma)=\pi_{1}(\sigma^{-1})^{t}
defining a twist
X^{\prime}_{\rho}(p)
. These two twists, defined over the same field, are equivalent under the conditions in
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

(
[
3
, Thm. 2.1]
)
The map
X(p)\rightarrow X(1)
define a surjective map from the set of non-cuspidal rational points with
j\neq 0,1728
on the curves
X_{\rho}(p)
and
X^{\prime}_{\rho}(p)
to the set of isomorphism classes of elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
realizing
\rho
.

It is important to note that not every twist of
X(7)
arises as
X_{E}(7):=X_{\overline{\rho}_{E,7}}(7)
or
X_{E}^{-}(7):=X^{\prime}_{\overline{\rho}_{E,7}}(7)
for some elliptic curve
E
, in particular
\mathcal{C}
itself does not arise from an elliptic curve. This type of twists have brought the attention of the community: explicit equations for
X_{E}(7)
are given in
[
4
]
, and the study of their rational points for some particular examples are in
[
12
]
, which is used to solve a particularly difficult diophantine equation.

In the case in which the elliptic curve
E
does not have CM, we follow
[
14
, Thm. 1.5]
to determine the possible images of the representations
\overline{\rho}_{E,7}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. If
E
does not have CM, then the image of
\overline{\rho}_{E,7}
is isomorphic to
S_{3}
(then
j(E)=3^{3}\cdot 5\cdot 7^{5}/2^{7}
) or has cardinality
14
,
42
or
336
. If
E
has CM then
\overline{\rho}_{E,7}
has cardinality
7
or larger than
12
.

Proof.

The aforementioned reference classify the possibilities for
\rho_{E,7}:\,G_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{7})
when
E/\mathbb{Q}
does not have CM.

There are only a finite number of
E/\mathbb{Q}
with CM up to
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}-
isomorphism. When
7
does not divide the discriminant of the CM-field and up to quadratic twists, the corresponding representation is maximal according to the LMFDB
[
6
]
. When
7
divides the discriminant then the CM-field is
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})
and again according to the LMFDB, and up to quadratic twist,
\overline{\rho}_{E,7}
has cardinality
14
.
∎

Twists not arising from elliptic curves do not have rational points apart from eventually the cusps.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\phi\colon C^{\prime}\rightarrow C_{0}
be a twist defined over a field
L
. If
L\cap\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7})=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})
, then
C^{\prime}
has no rational cusps.

Proof.

The cusps of
C_{0}
are defined over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7})
but not over
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})
. An isomorphism
\phi\colon C^{\prime}\rightarrow C_{0}
maps
C^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})
to
C_{0}(L)
.
If
C_{0}
has no cusps over
L
, then
C^{\prime}
has no cusps over
\mathbb{Q}
.
∎

5.
Counter-examples to the Hasse Principle

In the classification of twists of the Klein quartic in
[
8
]
there are
11
different families (or cases) of twists accordingly to the isomorphism class of the Galois group of the field of definition of the twists. The families 6. and 10. do not appear when the Klein quartic is considered to be defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. And the family
11.
contains all the twists
X_{E}(7)
when
\overline{\rho}_{E,7}
is a big as possible which is the generic case: all those twists have then rational points according to Theorem
4.1
. We hence study the other cases in order to find counter-examples to the Hasse Principle.

Proposition 5.1
.

Twists from cases
1
. and
2
. satisfy
C^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{2})=\emptyset

Proof.

Case
1
. corresponds to the curve
C_{0}
. It is straightforward to check that
\overline{C_{0}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})=\emptyset
. For case
2
. we distinguish the cases
m
even or odd, where
m
is the parameter of the family given in
[
8
, Thm. 6.1]
. This time
\overline{{C}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\neq\emptyset
, but
\overline{{C}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})=\emptyset
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Twists in case
7
. satisfy
C^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{7})=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Independently of the valuations at
7
of the parameters defining this family we always obtain, after simplification, an equation of the form
Q^{2}+7G=0
with
Q,G\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y,z]
homogeneous of degree 2 and 4 respectively, and not divisible by
7
. This model of the twists clearly shows the hyperelliptic reduction behavior of the Klein quartic at
7
, see
[
5
, Prop. 1.2]
. While trying to construct
\mathbb{Q}_{7}
-points we find an obstruction. We do find points over
\mathbb{F}_{7}
in the conic, but they are double points of the quartic and they cannot even be lifted to
\mathbb{Z}/7^{2}\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

For infinitely many twists in cases
5
.,
9
. and
11
. we have
C^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

They are infinitely many non-CM elliptic curves giving Galois representations with
\overline{\rho}_{E,7}
isomorphic to the corresponding groups of order
14
,
42
and
336
respectively, see
[
14
, Thm. 1.5]
.
∎

We look next to families that do provide counter-examples to the Hasse Principle.

5.1.

Therefore if

From Proposition

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
f(x)=x^{3}+Bx+C
and
C^{\prime}
as above, if
2\nmid C
then
C^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}_{2})\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

If
2\nmid C
then
2\nmid\Delta

F
reduces modulo

\displaystyle x^{4}+x^{2}yz+xy^{3}+xz^{3}+y^{2}z^{2}

\displaystyle\text{if }2\mid B

\displaystyle x^{4}+x^{2}y^{2}+x^{2}yz+x^{2}z^{2}+xy^{3}+xyz^{2}+xz^{3}+y^{4}+y^{2}z^{2}+z^{4}

\displaystyle\text{if }2\nmid B

Note that if

(0:1:0)

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
f(x)=x^{3}+Bx+C
and

as above. If

If

∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
f(x)=x^{3}+Bx+C
and
C^{\prime}
as above. Assume

We check with Magma

.

Let us address now the question about rational points.

Proposition 5.8
.

In cases 4. and 8.
C^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})=\emptyset
.

Proof.

From Theorem

tells us that those twists have no non-cuspidal rational point. Finally, Proposition

Proposition 5.9
.

In case 3., if
C^{\prime}\not\simeq_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathcal{C}
and the field of definition is not the field

(with the
[

]

.

Proof.

The same proof than in previous proposition applies except that we have to remove the special case in which the elliptic curve has

, in this case the 7-torsion is defined over the field

As a consequence of the previous results, we conclude:

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
f(x)=x^{3}+Bx+C\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be an irreducible polynomial such that all of the following conditions hold

(i)

2\nmid C
,

(ii)

3\nmid B
,

(iii)

7\mid B
and
7\mid\mid C
,

(iv)

p\mid B
and
p\mid\mid C
for any prime
p\neq 2,3,7
dividing
-4B^{3}-27C^{2}
.

(v)

The splitting field
\mathbb{Q}_{f}\not\simeq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{7})
nor the field
6.0.214375.1
.

Then
f
gives a twist of the Klein quartic (constructed as in Subsection
5.1
) that is a counterexample to the Hasse principle.

Remark 5.11
.

Notice that the condition (iii) in Theorem
5.10
implies that
f
is irreducible because it is Eisenstein at
7
.

In order to get polynomials
f
satisfying all the conditions above we need
B=\pm 2^{a}7^{b}\prod p_{i}^{e_{i}}
and
C=\pm 3^{c}7\prod p_{i}
with
e_{i},b\geq 1
,

, the
p_{i}\neq 2,3,7
prime and such that the discriminant is only divisible by the primes
7
and
p_{i}
. This last condition yields an equality of the form:
3^{3+2c}\pm 1=2^{3a+2}7^{3b-2}p_{i}^{3e_{i}-2}
. In order to get the left hand size of this equality to be
0
modulo
4
we need the first sign to be positive, and in order to be
0
modulo
7
we need
3\mid c
. Actually, if
c=3c^{\prime}
with
c^{\prime}\neq 3\text{ mod }7
then
4\mid\mid 3^{3+6c^{\prime}}+1
and
7\mid\mid 3^{3+6c^{\prime}}+1
.

Conjecture 5.12
.

There are infinitely many values of
c^{\prime}\neq 3\text{ mod }7
such that all the primes different from
2
and
7
in the factorization of

Any such

giving

satisfying conditions

in Theorem

.

Example 5.13
.

After checking condition
(v)
in Theorem
5.10
, we find that the polynomials

,

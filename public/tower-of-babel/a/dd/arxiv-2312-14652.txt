Cauchy numbers in type B B


Abstract
In this paper, we will introduce the Cauchy numbers of both kinds in type B and produce their corresponding exponential generating functions. Then we will provide some identities involving Cauchy, Lah, and Stirling numbers in type B through combinatorial methods.

Cauchy numbers are defined as the integration of rising and falling factorials
[
3
]
. These numbers can be classified into two categories: Cauchy numbers of the first kind, denoted by
C_{n}
, and Cauchy numbers of the second kind, denoted as
c_{n}
. The first kind is defined by

C_{n}=\int_{0}^{1}(x)_{n}\,dx

where
(x)_{n}:=x(x-1)(x-2)\cdots(x-n+1)
represents the falling factorial. The second kind is defined to be

c_{n}=\int_{0}^{1}[x]_{n}\,dx

where
[x]_{n}:=x(x+1)(x+2)(x+3)\cdots(x+n-1)
represents the rising factorial
[
3
]
.

The ordinary generating function of any infinite sequence
(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is defined by
g(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}x^{k}
and shortly denoted by
\mathcal{G}(a_{k})=g(x)
. The corresponding exponential generating function to the sequence
(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
has the form
\hat{g}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{k!}x^{k}
and briefly denoted by
\mathcal{E}(a_{k})=\hat{g}(x)
. Due to
[
8
]
, the inverse operator of a formal power series
g(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{k}x^{k}
provides the coefficient of
x^{k}
such that
[x^{k}]g(x)=a_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Riordan array is actually a direct coefficient derivation method and it was first introduced by Shapiro in
[
11
]
. Thus, it is useful to derive the exponential generating function of Cauchy numbers. Riordan array
B=(b_{n,k})_{n,k\in\mathbb{N}}
, which is a lower triangular and infinite matrix, is identified with a pair of formal power series such that
B=\mathcal{R}(b_{n,k})=(b(x),c(x))
, where

b_{n,k}=[x^{n}]b(x)(xc(x))^{k}

(1)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
where
\mathbb{N}
denotes the set of natural numbers. One of the fundamental properties of Riordan array is the
summation property
which is given as follows:

\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{n,k}g_{k}=[x^{n}]b(x)g(xc(x))

(2)

where
\mathcal{R}(b_{n,k})=(b(x),c(x))
and
g(x)
is the ordinary generating function of the sequence
(g_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. We will mainly use the following expression instead of Eq. (
2
):

\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{n,k}g_{k}=[x^{n}]b(x)\left[g(y):y=xc(x)\right].

(3)

It is well-known from
[
7
]
that,

\mathcal{R}(\frac{k!}{n!}c(n,k))=\left(1,\frac{1}{x}ln\frac{1}{1-x}\right)~\textrm{and}~\mathcal{R}(\frac{k!}{n!}S(n,k))=\left(1,\frac{e^{x}-1}{x}\right).

where
c(n,k)
is the classical signless Stirling number of the first kind and
S(n,k)
is the classical Stirling numbers of the second kind.

According to
[
7
]
, the classical Cauchy numbers of the first kind have the exponential generating function which is given by:

\mathcal{E}(C_{n})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}C_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{x}{ln(1+x)}

and these numbers are related to the Stirling numbers of the first kind through the formula:

C_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{s(n,k)}{k+1}

where
s(n,k):=(-1)^{n-k}c(n,k)
is known as a Stirling number of the first kind. The exponential generating function of Cauchy numbers of the second kind has the following form (see
[
7
]
):

\mathcal{E}(c_{n})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{x}{(1+x)ln(1+x)}

and these numbers can be expressed in terms of signless Stirling numbers of the first kind as follows:

c_{n}=(-1)^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{c(n,k)}{k+1}.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows: In section 2, we recall the concept of Stirling numbers in type
B
. In section 3, we introduce the Cauchy numbers of both kinds and Lah numbers in type B. Furthermore, we drive the exponential generating functions of Cauchy numbers in type
B
with the help of the Riordan array. Finally, we generalize the Cauchy and Lah numbers into
G_{m,n}
type.

The following theorem, which is provided by
[
2
]
and
[
10
]
, expresses
x^{n}
as a sum of the terms involving both Stirling numbers of the second kind and falling factorial in type
B
. The falling factorial in type
B
is defined to be
(x)_{n}^{B}=(x-1)(x-3)(x-5)\cdots(x-2n+1)
with initial condition
(x)^{B}_{0}=1
(see
[
10
]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

For any integer
n\geq 0
, we have

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{B}(n,k)(x)_{k}^{B}.

Taking into account Corollary 2.7 in
[
10
]
, it is observed that the matrices
[s_{B}(n,k)]_{n,k\geq 0}
and
[S_{B}(n,k)]_{n,k\geq 0}
are inverse of each other. Therefore, it is easy to see that

(x)_{n}^{B}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}s_{B}(n,k)x^{k}.

(4)

The rising factorial in type
B
is defined as
[x]^{B}_{n}=(x+1)(x+3)(x+5)\cdots(x+2n-1)
with the initial condition
[x]^{B}_{0}=1
. It is well-known from part (c) of Theorem 2.1 in
[
10
]
that for any nonnegative integer n

[x]^{B}_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{B}(n,k)x^{k}.

(5)

Conversely, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
the ordering powers
x^{n}
can be easily expressed as a linear combination of rising factorials
[x]_{n}^{B}
as follows:

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{B}(n,k)(-1)^{n-k}[x]^{B}_{k}~~\textrm{for}~\textrm{all}~n\geq 0.

(6)

Proposition 2.2
.

The Cauchy numbers of the first kind hold for the following recurrence relation

C_{n+1}^{B}+(2n+1)C_{n}^{B}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{s_{B}(n,k)}{k+2}.

Proof.

Due to the definition of the falling factorial of type
B
, we can write the relation
x(x)_{n}^{B}=(x)_{n+1}^{B}+(2n+1)(x)_{n}^{B}
. Therefore, by Theorem
1.1
we get

\displaystyle C_{n+1}^{B}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}(x)_{n+1}^{B}\,dx=\int_{0}^{1}(x(x)_{n}^{B}-(2n+1)(x)_{n}^{B})\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}s_{B}(n,k)x^{k+1}\,dx-(2n+1)\int_{0}^{1}(x)_{n}^{B}\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{s_{B}(n,k)}{k+2}-(2n+1)C_{n}^{B},

as desired.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

We have the following recurrence relation for the Cauchy numbers of the second kind in type
B
:

\quad c_{n+1}^{B}-(2n+1)c_{n}^{B}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{c_{B}(n,k)}{k+2}.

Proof.

We can deduce the relation
[x]^{B}_{n+1}=x[x]^{B}_{n}+(2n+1)[x]^{B}_{n}
from the definition of the rising factorial of type
B
. Therefore, the desired result can be easily seen from Eq. (

).
∎

Theorem 2.5
.

For any positive integer
n
, we have the following formula

\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{B}(n,k)C_{k}^{B}=\frac{1}{n+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{B}(n,k)(-1)^{n-k}c_{k}^{B}.

Proof.

Considering Theorem
1.1
and the definition of the first kind of Cauchy numbers in type
B
, we have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{B}(n,k)C_{k}^{B}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{B}(n,k)\int_{0}^{1}(x)_{n}^{B}\,dx=\int_{0}^{1}x^{n}\,dx=\frac{1}{n+1}.

Using Eq. (
6
) and the definition of the second kind of Cauchy numbers of type B, then we get

\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{B}(n,k)(-1)^{n-k}c_{k}^{B}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{B}(n,k)(-1)^{n-k}\int_{0}^{1}[x]_{k}^{B}\,dx=\int_{0}^{1}x^{n}\,dx=\frac{1}{n+1}.

∎

2.1
The exponential generating functions of Cauchy numbers in type
B

In order to derive the exponential generating functions for Cauchy numbers of both kinds in type
B
, we will apply the Riordan array of the signless Stirling numbers of the first kind in type
B
. Before going into a further discussion of the exponential generating functions, we will give an important relationship between Cauchy numbers of both kinds and Stirling numbers of the first kind in type
B
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For all
n\geq 0
, we have the following relations:

1.

C_{n}^{B}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{s_{B}(n,k)}{k+1}
,

2.

c_{n}^{B}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{c_{B}(n,k)}{k+1}
.

Proof.

Considering Eq. (
4
), we then have

\displaystyle C_{n}^{B}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}(x)_{n}^{B}\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}s_{B}(n,k)\int_{0}^{1}x^{k}\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{s_{B}(n,k)}{k+1}.

From Eq. (
5
), we immediately obtain the second part as

c_{n}^{B}=\int_{0}^{1}[x]_{n}^{B}\,dx=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{B}(n,k)\int_{0}^{1}x^{k}\,dx=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{c_{B}(n,k)}{k+1}.

∎

We are now in a position to give the exponential generating functions of Cauchy numbers of both kinds in type
B
.

Theorem 2.7
.

For the Cauchy numbers of the second kind, we have

\mathcal{E}(c_{n}^{B})=\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{n}^{B}~\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-2x}}{(2x-1)ln\sqrt{1-2x}}.

Proof.

We can write
\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{B}(n,k)~\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!\sqrt{1-2x}}\left(ln\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2x}}\right)^{k}
from Theorem 4.1 (c) in
[
10
]
. Therefore, we can deduce from Eq. (
1
) that

\mathcal{R}(\frac{k!}{n!}c_{B}(n,k))=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2x}},\frac{1}{x}ln\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2x}}\right).

(7)

Using the second part of Lemma
2.6
, we can get
\frac{c_{n}^{B}}{n!}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{k!}{n!}c_{B}(n,k)\frac{1}{(k+1)!}
. Since the ordinary generating function of
\frac{1}{(k+1)!}
is equal to
\frac{e^{x}-1}{x}
and considering Eq. (
3
) and Eq. (
7
) , we then extract

\frac{c_{n}^{B}}{n!}=[x^{n}]\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2x}}\left[\frac{e^{y}-1}{y}:y=ln\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2x}}\right],

as desired.
∎

Theorem 2.8
.

For the Cauchy numbers of the first kind, we have

\mathcal{E}((-1)^{(n-1)}C_{n}^{B})=\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{(n-1)}C_{n}^{B}~\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-2x}}{\sqrt{1-2x}~~ln\sqrt{1-2x}}.

Proof.

Using the first part of Lemma
2.6
, we can write
\frac{C_{n}^{B}}{n!}=(-1)^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{k!}{n!}c_{B}(n,k)\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}
. Since the ordinary generating function of
\frac{(-1)^{k+1}}{(k+1)!}
is equal to
\frac{e^{-x}-1}{x}
and taking into consideration Eq. (
3
) and Eq. (
7
), we then derive the desired formula by means of the following relation:

\frac{C_{n}^{B}}{n!}=[x^{n}]\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2x}}\left[\frac{e^{-y}-1}{y}:y=ln\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-2x}}\right].

∎

2.2
Lah numbers in type
B

The classical Lah numbers, which were discovered by Ivo Lah in 1954 (see
[
5
]
), are defined by binomial coefficient as

L(n,k)=\frac{n!}{k!}\binom{n-1}{k-1},

and were also defined by means of the Stirling numbers as follows (see
[
6
]
):

L(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}c(n,j)S(j,k)

where
c(n,j)
and
S(j,k)
are the classical Stirling numbers of the first and second kind, respectively. The recurrence relation of
L(n,k)
is given by

L(n,k)=L(n-1,k-1)+(n-1+k)L(n-1,k)~~\textrm{for}~\textrm{all}~~n,k\in\mathbb{N}.

It is well-known from
[
6
]
that the exponential generating function of
L(n,k)
is stated as

\mathcal{E}(L(n,k))=\sum_{n\geq 0}L(n,k)~\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}(\frac{x}{1-x})^{k}.

Thus, we can conclude that Riordon array
\mathcal{R}(\frac{k!}{n!}L(n,k))=\left(1,\frac{1}{1-x}\right).

Definition 2.9
.

The Lah numbers in type
B
may be defined by

L_{B}(n,k)=\binom{n}{k}^{2}2^{n-k}(n-k)!

and the type
B
Lah numbers can be expressed as a linear combination of Stirling numbers:

L_{B}(n,k)=\sum_{j=k}^{n}c_{B}(n,j)S_{B}(j,k)~~\textrm{for}~\textrm{all}~~n,k\in\mathbb{N}.

The recurrence relation of
L_{B}(n,k)
is defined by

L_{B}(n,k)=L_{B}(n-1,k-1)+2(n+k)L_{B}(n-1,k)~~\textrm{for}~\textrm{all}~~n,k\in\mathbb{N},

(8)

with the initial conditions
L_{B}(n,0)=2^{n}n!
,
L_{B}(n,n)=1
and
L_{B}(n,k)=0
if
k<0
.

Theorem 2.10
.

The exponential generating function of
L_{B}(n,k)
is given by

\mathcal{E}(L_{B}(n,k))=\sum_{n\geq 0}L_{B}(n,k)~\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{x^{k}}{(1-2x)^{k+1}k!}.

(9)

Proof.

We argue by induction on
k
. If
k=0
, then we get

\sum_{n\geq 0}L_{B}(n,0)~\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n\geq 0}2^{n}n!~\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{1-2x}.

Now let
\mathcal{E}(L_{B}(n,k)):=f_{k}(x)
. Thus we can write
f_{k}(x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}L_{B}(n,k)~\frac{x^{n}}{n!}
. Considering Eq. (
8
), we conclude that

\displaystyle f_{k}(x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n\geq k}L_{B}(n,k)~\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq k}L_{B}(n-1,k-1)~\frac{x^{n}}{n!}+\sum_{n\geq k+1}2(n+k)L_{B}(n-1,k)~\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq k-1}L_{B}(n,k-1)~\frac{x^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}+\sum_{n\geq k}2(n+1+k)L_{B}(n,k)~\frac{x^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq k-1}L_{B}(n,k-1)~\frac{x^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}+2\sum_{n\geq k}L_{B}(n,k)~\frac{x^{n+1}}{n!}+2k\sum_{n\geq k}L_{B}(n,k)~\frac{x^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq k-1}L_{B}(n,k-1)~\frac{x^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}+2xf_{k}(x)+2k\sum_{n\geq k}L_{B}(n,k)~\frac{x^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}

hence

(1-2x)f_{k}(x)=\sum_{n\geq k-1}L_{B}(n,k-1)~\frac{x^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}+2k\sum_{n\geq k}L_{B}(n,k)~\frac{x^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}.

If we take the derivative of both sides of the previous equation with respect to
x
, then we get

(1-2x)f^{\prime}_{k}(x)-2f_{k}(x)=f_{k-1}(x)+2kf_{k}(x)

hence

(1-2x)f^{\prime}_{k}(x)-2(k+1)f_{k}(x)=f_{k-1}(x).

Multiplying both sides of the above equality by
(1-2x)^{k}
, then we obtain by the induction hypothesis for
f_{k-1}(x)
that

(1-2x)^{k+1}f^{\prime}_{k}(x)-2(k+1)(1-2x)^{k}f_{k}(x)=\frac{x^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}.

Thus we have

((1-2x)f_{k}(x))^{\prime}=\frac{x^{k-1}}{(k-1)!}.

If we solve the initial value problem
((1-2x)f_{k}(x))^{\prime}=\frac{x^{k-1}}{(k-1)!},~~f_{k}(0)=0
, we
then obtain

f_{k}(x)=\frac{x^{k}}{(1-2x)^{k+1}k!},

as desired.
∎

It is straightforward to check that Eq. (
9
) gives the Riordon array of
\frac{k!}{n!}L_{B}(n,k)
as

\mathcal{R}(\frac{k!}{n!}L_{B}(n,k))=\left(\frac{1}{1-2x},\frac{1}{1-2x}\right).

Proof.

It is clear from the definitions of the rising and the falling factorials of type

\displaystyle[x]^{B}_{n}

Remark 2.12
.

Lemma
2.11
has the following additional interpretation. The set
V=K[x]
be a vector space of all polynomials in the indeterminate
x
with coefficients in the field
K
. The sets
B_{1}=\{1,[x]_{1}^{B},[x]_{2}^{B},\cdots\}
and
B_{2}=\{1,(x)_{1}^{B},(x)_{2}^{B},\cdots\}
are both bases of
V
. Then the first part of Lemma
2.11
asserts that the infinite matrix
\textbf{L}=[L_{B}(n,k)]_{k,n\in\mathbb{N}}
is the transition matrix between the basis
B_{2}
and the basis
B_{1}
. Conversely, the second part of Lemma
2.11
asserts that the infinite matrix
\textbf{M}=[(-1)^{n-k}L_{B}(n,k)]_{k,n\in\mathbb{N}}
is the transition matrix between the basis
B_{1}
and the basis
B_{2}
. Therefore, the matrix
M
is the inverse to the matrix
L
.

The following theorem provides an important relationship between Cauchy numbers of both kinds in type
B

L_{B}(n,k)

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
n\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle c_{n}^{B}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L_{B}(n,k)C_{k}^{B}\quad and\quad C_{n}^{B}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}L_{B}(n,k)c_{k}^{B}

where
L_{B}(n,k)
is of type
B
Lah numbers.

Proof.

Consider the first part of Lemma
2.11
. We obtain by integrating of
[x]^{B}_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L_{B}(n,k)(x)_{k}^{B}
from
0
to
1
on both sides that
c_{n}^{B}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L_{B}(n,k)C_{k}^{B}
.
Using the second part of Lemma
2.11
, it can be easily proved that
C_{n}^{B}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{n-k}L_{B}(n,k)c_{k}^{B}
in a similar way.
∎

Two kinds of incomplete Cauchy numbers of type

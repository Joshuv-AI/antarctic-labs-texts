Rank Rigidity for CAT(0) spaces without 3-flats
Stephan Stadler

Abstract
. If a group Γ \Gamma acts geometrically on a CAT(0) space X X without 3-flats, then either X X contains a Γ \Gamma -periodic geodesic which does not bound a flat half-plane, or else X X is a rank 2 Riemannian symmetric space, a 2-dimensional Euclidean building or non-trivially splits as a metric product. Consequently all such groups satisfy a strong form of the Tits Alternative.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Main results

In
[
Gro93
]
Gromov proposed a rough classification of periodic CAT(0) spaces modulo hyperbolic ones.
It vaguely states that these spaces decompose into pieces such that every piece either displays
almost hyperbolic behavior or else has uniformly distributed flats. One may want to think of
a non-positively curved compact 3-manifold and its decomposition into Seifert and atoroidal pieces.
Each Seifert piece admits an
\mathbb{R}^{3}
- or

\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
-structure whereas the atoroidal pieces admit

\mathbb{H}^{3}
-structures.
Gromov’s expectation roots in the general principle that the failure of hyperbolic behavior is caused by (unbounded parts of) flats –
isometric embeddings of higher dimensional Euclidean spaces. For instance, an axial isometry exhibits north-south dynamics
at infinity unless one (and then every) of its axes bounds a flat half-plane.

One concrete interpretation of Gromov’s vision is provided by the following two conjectures due to Ballmann and Buyalo
[
BB08
]

which classify CAT(0) spaces by means of their
rank
.
A complete geodesic is said to have
rank 1
if it does not bound a flat half-plane.
Accordingly, a CAT(0) space is called
rank 1
if it contains a rank 1 geodesic,
otherwise we say it has
higher rank
.

Recall that if a group
\Gamma
acts on a CAT(0) space
X
, then its
limit set

\Lambda(\Gamma)
is the set of accumulation points
of a
\Gamma
-orbit in the ideal boundary
\partial_{\infty}X
.

Conjecture A

(Closing Lemma)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space which admits a properly discontinuous action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
with
\Lambda(\Gamma)=\partial_{\infty}X
.
If
X
contains a geodesic of rank 1, then it also contains a
\Gamma
-periodic
geodesic of rank 1.

Conjecture
A
is known to hold in each of the following settings.

•

If
\Gamma
satisfies the duality condition of Chen
and Eberlein, compare
[
Bal95
, Chapter III]
.

•

If
X
is a piecewise
smooth complex of dimension two and
\Gamma
acts
geometrically
– properly discontinuously with compact quotient, see
[
BB95
]
.

•

If
X
is a finite-dimensional CAT(0) cube complex and
\Gamma
acts geometrically
[
CS11
]
.

Conjecture B

(Diameter Rigidity)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact and geodesically complete CAT(0) space which admits a properly discontinuous action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
with
\Lambda(\Gamma)=\partial_{\infty}X
.
If
X
has higher rank, then
X
is a Riemannian symmetric space, a Euclidean building or non-trivially splits as a
metric product.

Recall that in a CAT(0) space
X
, every complete geodesic bounds a flat half-plane if and only if its Tits boundary
\partial_{T}X

has diameter equal to
\pi
, compare Section
2.6
. This explains the name of the conjecture.

The following special cases of Conjecture
B
are known to hold.

•

If
X
is a Hadamard manifold and
\Gamma
satisfies the duality condition
[
Bal85
,
BS87
,
EH90
]
; for a comprehensive proof, see Chapter IV in

[
Bal95
]
.

•

If
X
is a homogeneous Hadamard manifold
[
Heb93
]
.

•

If
X
is a piecewise
smooth complex of dimension two or a piecewise Euclidean complex of dimension 3 and

\Gamma
acts geometrically
[
BB95
,
BB00
]
.

•

If
X
is a finite-dimensional
CAT(0) cube complex and
\Gamma
acts geometrically
[
CS11
]
.

We confirm Conjectures
A
and
B
for CAT(0) spaces with geometric group actions and without 3-flats.
By
[
Kle99
, Theorem C]
, the absence of 3-flats is equivalent to
X
not containing 3-dimensional quasi-flats.

Theorem A
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group acting geometrically on a locally compact CAT(0) space without
3
-flats.
If
X
contains a geodesic of rank 1, then it also contains a

\Gamma
-periodic geodesic of rank 1.

Theorem B
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group acting geometrically on a locally compact and geodesically complete CAT(0) space without
3
-flats.
If
X
has higher rank,
then
X
is a Riemannian symmetric space, a Euclidean building or non-trivially splits as a metric product.

A consequence of Theorems
A
and
B
is that all such groups satisfy the Tits Alternative:

Corollary C
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group acting geometrically on a locally compact and geodesically complete CAT(0) space without
3
-flats.
Then either
\Gamma
contains a free non-abelian subgroup or else
X
is flat and
\Gamma
is a Bieberbach group.

Another application of Theorem
A
gives the following.

Corollary D
.

Let
X
and
X^{\prime}
be irreducible locally compact geodesically complete CAT(0) spaces. Suppose that
X
does not contain a 3-flat.
If the same group
\Gamma
acts geometrically on
X
and
X^{\prime}
,
then either
X
and
X^{\prime}
are isometric after possibly rescaling or there exists an element
\gamma\in\Gamma
with
\gamma

and

such that neither of them bounds a flat half-plane.

1.2.
Organization

In Section
2
, we introduce notation and recall background on the geometry of spaces with upper curvature bounds.
Apart from the standard material, we introduce the notion
essential Tits boundary
of a CAT(0) space without 3-flats.
In Section
3
we proof the Closing Lemma by showing that if a group
\Gamma
acts geometrically on a CAT(0) space without 3-flats
and preserves a proper closed subset at infinity, then the space has higher rank (Theorem
3.8
).
Section
4
builds up to a proof of Theorem
B
assuming our key technical result, the
Half-Plane Lemma
.
We proceed by first showing that the essential Tits boundary is a spherical building and then argue that the essential Tits boundary
already agrees with the ordinary Tits boundary.

Appenix
A
is devoted to geometric measure theory with the aim to provide a proof of the
Half-Plane Lemma
.
We also prove some folklore results, including monotonicity and volume rigidity of minimizing currents in CAT(0) spaces.
However, our approach is to find a streamlined path to the
Half-Plane Lemma
and we do not try to prove
required ingredients in their most general form.

2.1.
Metric spaces

Euclidean
n
-space with its flat metric will be denoted by
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The unit sphere
S^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
equipped with the induced metric will be referred to as a

round sphere
. Its intersection with a half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times[0,\infty)
is called a
round hemisphere
.
We denote the distance between two points
x
and
y
in a metric space
X
by
|x,y|
.
If
A\subset X
denotes a subset, then
|x,A|
refers to the greatest lower bound for distances from points in
A
to
x
.
For
x\in X
and
r>0
, we denote by
B_{r}(x)
and
\bar{B}_{r}(x)
the open and closed
r
-ball around
x
, respectively.
Similarly,
N_{r}(A)
and
\bar{N}_{r}(A)
denote the open and closed
r
-neighborhood of a subset
A\subset X
, respectively.
Moreover,
S_{r}(x)
denotes the
r
-sphere around
x
and by
\dot{B}_{r}(x)
we denote the punctured
r
-ball
B_{r}(x)\setminus\{x\}
.
A
geodesic

is an isometric embedding of an interval. It is called a
geodesic segment
, if it is compact.
The
endpoints
of a geodesic segment
c
are denoted by
\partial c
.
A geodesic segment
c

branches
at an endpoint
y\in\partial c=\{x,y\}
, if there are geodesics
c^{\pm}
starting in
x
which strictly contain
c

and such that
c^{-}\cap c^{+}=c
. The point
y
is then called a
branch point
.

A
triangle
is a union of three geodesics connecting three points.

X
is
a geodesic metric space
if
any pair of points of
X

is connected by a geodesic.
It is
geodesically complete
if every geodesic segment is contained in a complete local geodesic.

2.2.
Spaces with an upper curvature bound

For
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
, let
D_{\kappa}\in(0,\infty]
be the diameter of the complete, simply connected surface
M^{2}_{\kappa}

of constant curvature
\kappa
. A complete metric space is called a
CAT(
\kappa
) space

if any pair of its points with distance less than
D_{\kappa}
is connected by a geodesic and if
all triangles
with perimeter less than
2D_{\kappa}

are not thicker than
the
comparison triangle
in
M_{\kappa}^{2}
. In particular, geodesics between points of distance less than
D_{\kappa}

are unique. Hence,
X
is a CAT(
\kappa
) space, then we can define for every
p\in X
and every subset
A\subset B_{D_{\kappa}}(p)

the
geodesic cone
C_{p}(A)
.

2.3.
Directions and angles

For any CAT(
\kappa
) space
X
,
the angle between each pair of geodesics starting at the same point
is well defined.
Let
x,y,z
be three points at pairwise distance less than
D_{\kappa}
in a CAT(
\kappa
) space
X
.
Whenever
x\neq y
, the geodesic between
x
and
y
is unique and will be denoted
by
xy
. For
y,z\neq x
, the angle at
x
between
xy
and
xz

will be denoted by
\angle_{x}(y,z)
. It is defined by

\angle_{x}(y,z)=\lim\limits_{y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\to x}\tilde{\angle}_{x}(y^{\prime},z^{\prime})

where
\tilde{\angle}_{x}(y^{\prime},x^{\prime})
denotes the angle of the comparison triangle at the vertex corresponding to
x
;
and the points
y^{\prime},z^{\prime}
converge to
x
along the geodesic
xy
and
xz
, respectively.
A comparison argument shows that this is well defined as a monotonic limit.
The
space of directions
or
link

at a point
x\in X
is the completion of the space of equivalence classes of geodesic germs at
x
with respect to the angle metric.
The resulting space
(\Sigma_{x}X,\angle)

is a CAT(1) space. Its elements are called
directions (at
x
)
.

2.4.
Dimension

A natural notion of dimension
\dim(X)
for a CAT(
\kappa
) space

was
introduced by
Kleiner in
[

]
, originally referred to as

.
It vanishes precisely when the space is discrete.
In general, it is defined inductively:

\dim(X)=\sup_{x\in X}\{\dim(\Sigma_{x}X)+1\}.

For instance, in a 1-dimensional CAT(
\kappa
) space every link is discrete.
The geometric dimension coincides with the supremum of
topological dimensions
1
1

1

Here topological dimension corresponds to Lebesgue covering dimension.
of compact subsets in
X

[
Kle99
]
.
If the dimension of

is finite, then

agrees with the largest number

such that

admits a bilipschitz embedding of an open subset in
\mathbb{R}^{k}

[
Kle99
]
.
The dimension of a locally compact and geodesically complete space is finite and agrees with the topological dimension
as well as the Hausdorff dimension
[

,
LN19
]
.

2.5.
CAT(1) spaces

For two CAT(1) spaces
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
we denote by
Z_{1}\circ Z_{2}
their
spherical join
.
It is a CAT(1) space of diameter
\pi
.
Note that every round subsphere
\sigma^{\prime}
in a round sphere
\sigma
yields a join decomposition
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}\circ\sigma^{\prime\prime}

where
\dim(\sigma)=\dim(\sigma^{\prime})+\dim(\sigma^{\prime\prime})+1
. Also, every round hemisphere
\tau
decomposes as
\tau=\partial\tau\circ\zeta

where
\zeta
denotes the center of
\tau
. A CAT(1) space
Z
is called
irreducible
, if it does not admit a non-trivial
spherical join decomposition.

Recall that two points in a CAT(1) space
Z
are called
antipodes
, if their distance is at least
\pi
.
In case
C
consists of a pair of
antipodes

\xi^{\pm}
,
|\xi^{-},\xi^{+}|=\pi
, then the convex hull of

\{\xi^{-},\xi^{+}\}
and
\operatorname{pol}(\{\xi^{-},\xi^{+}\})
is isometric to a spherical suspension of
\operatorname{pol}(\{\xi^{-},\xi^{+}\})
.
For a subset
M\subset Z
we denote by
\operatorname{Ant}(M)\subset Z
the set of antipodes of
M
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
Z
be a CAT(1) space of dimension
k
. We call a point
\xi\in Z

regular
, if
it has a neighborhood homeomorphic to an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
BL06
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
Z
be a CAT(1) space of dimension
n
. If
\sigma\subset Z
is a round
n
-sphere, then every point
\xi\in Z
has an antipode in
\sigma
.

This implies the following well-known criterion for geodesic completeness.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
Sta22a
, Lemma 2.4]
)
.

Let
Z
be a CAT(1) space of dimension
n
. If every point
\xi\in Z
is contained in a round

-sphere in

,
then

is geodesically complete.

2.6.
CAT(0) spaces

The
ideal boundary
of a CAT(0) space
X
, equipped with the cone topology, is denoted by
\partial_{\infty}X
.
If
X
is locally compact, then
\partial_{\infty}X
is compact. If
X
is a Hadamard
n
-manifold, then
\partial_{\infty}X
is homeomorphic to an
(n-1)
-sphere.
The
Tits boundary
of
X
is denoted by
\partial_{T}X
, it is the ideal boundary equipped with
the Tits metric
|\cdot,\cdot|_{T}
. Recall that the Tits metric is the intrinsic metric associated to the
Tits angle
. For a point
p
in
X

and ideal points
\xi,\eta
in
\partial_{\infty}X
the Tits angle is defined by

\angle_{T}(\xi,\eta):=\lim\limits_{x\to\xi,y\to\eta}\tilde{\angle}_{p}(x,y).

The Tits boundary of a CAT(0) space is a CAT(1) space. It decodes the intersection patterns of asymptotically flat subspaces.
For instance, the Euclidean
n
-space has the round
(n-1)
-sphere as Tits boundary, while any hyperbolic space has a discrete Tits boundary.

A subset in a CAT(0) space is convex, if it contains the geodesic between any pair of its points.

If
C
is a closed convex subset, then it is CAT(0) with respect to the induced metric. In this case,
C
admits a 1-Lipschitz retraction
\pi_{C}:X\to C
.
If
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are closed convex subsets, then the distance function
d(\cdot,C_{1})|_{C_{2}}
is convex; and constant if
and only if
\pi_{C_{1}}
restricts to an isometric embedding on
C_{2}
. We call
C_{1}
and
C_{2}

parallel
,
C_{1}\|C_{2}
, if
and only if
d(\cdot,C_{1})|_{C_{2}}
and
d(\cdot,C_{2})|_{C_{1}}
are constant.
Let
Y\subset X
be a geodesically complete closed convex subset. Then we define the
parallel set

P(Y)

as the union of all closed convex subsets parallel to
Y
. The parallel set is closed, convex and splits canonically as a metric product

P(Y)\cong Y\times CS(Y)

where the
cross section

CS(Y)
is a closed convex subset.
The Tits boundary is given by

\partial_{T}P(Y)\cong\partial_{T}Y\circ\operatorname{pol}(\partial_{T}Y).

If
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are CAT(0) spaces, then their metric product
X_{1}\times X_{2}
is again a CAT(0) space.
We have
\partial_{T}(X_{1}\times X_{2})=\partial_{T}X_{1}\circ\partial_{T}X_{2}
and
\Sigma_{(x_{1},x_{2})}(X_{1}\times X_{2})=\Sigma_{x_{1}}X_{1}\circ\Sigma_{x_{2}}X_{2}
.
If
X
is a geodesically complete CAT(0) space, then any join decomposition of
\partial_{T}X
is induced by a metric product decomposition of
X

[
KL97
, Proposition 2.3.7]
.
A CAT(0) space
X
is called
irreducible
, if it does not admit a non-trivial splitting as a metric product.

The Tits boundary of a closed convex subset
Y\subset X
embeds canonically
\partial_{T}Y\subset\partial_{T}X
.
If two closed convex subsets
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
intersect in
X
, then

\partial_{T}(Y_{1}\cap Y_{2})=\partial_{T}Y_{1}\cap\partial_{T}Y_{2}.

Let
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
be CAT(0) spaces containing closed convex subsets
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, respectively.
If there is an isometry
f:C_{1}\to C_{2}
, then by
[
AKP19
, 8.9.1 Reshetnyak’s gluing theorem]
, the glued space

X_{1}\cup_{f}X_{2}:=(X_{1}\dot{\cup}X_{2})/x_{1}\sim f(x_{1})

is CAT(0) with respect to the induced length metric.
In particular, for every CAT(0) space
X
which contains a closed convex subset
C
we can construct a CAT(0) space
\hat{X}
, the
double

of
X
along
C
, by

\hat{X}=X^{-}\cup_{C}X^{+}

where
X^{\pm}
are two isometric copies of
X
. The Tits boundary of the double is given by

\partial_{T}\hat{X}=\partial_{T}X^{-}\cup_{\partial_{T}C}\partial_{T}X^{+}.

For points
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}X
and
x\in X
we denote the
Busemann function centered at
\xi
based at
x
by
b_{\xi,x}
.
If
\rho:[0,\infty)\to X
denotes the geodesic ray asymptotic to
\xi
and with
\rho(0)=x
, then

b_{\xi,x}(y)=\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}(|y,\rho(t)|-t).

It is a 1-Lipschitz convex function whose negative gradient at a point
y\in X
is given by
\log_{y}(\xi)
.
We denote the
horoball
centered at a point
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}X
and based at the point
x\in X
by

HB(\xi,x):=b^{-1}_{\xi,x}((-\infty,0]).

It is a closed convex subset with

\partial_{T}HB(\xi,x)=\bar{B}_{\frac{\pi}{2}}(\xi)\subset\partial_{T}X.

A
n
-flat

F
in a CAT(0) space
X
is a closed convex subset isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In particular,
\partial_{T}F\subset\partial_{T}X
is a round
(n-1)
-sphere.
On the other hand, if
X
is locally compact and
\sigma\subset\partial_{T}X
is a round
(n-1)
-sphere, then either there exists an

n
-flat
F\subset X
with
\partial_{T}F=\sigma
, or there exists a round
n
-hemisphere
\tau^{+}\subset\partial_{T}X

with
\sigma=\partial\tau^{+}

[
Lee00
, Proposition 2.1]
. Consequently, if
\partial_{T}X
is
(n-1)
-dimensional, then any round
(n-1)
-sphere in
\partial_{T}X

is the Tits boundary of some
n
-flat in
X
. Moreover, a round
n
-hemisphere
\tau^{+}\subset\partial_{T}X
bounds a flat
(n+1)
-half-space in
X
if and only if
its boundary
\partial\tau^{+}
bounds an
n
-flat in
X
. A
flat (
n
-dimensional) half-space

H\subset X
is a closed convex subset isometric to a Euclidean half-space
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
.
Its boundary
\partial H\subset X
is an
(n-1)
-flat and its Tits boundary is a round
(n-1)
-hemisphere
\partial_{T}H\subset\partial_{T}X
. Flat half-spaces will play a certain role in our arguments later, and we agree to denote them by
H

and their boundaries by
\partial H=h
.

We define the
parallel set
of a round sphere
\sigma\subset\partial_{T}X
as

P(\sigma)=P(F)

where
F
is a flat in
X
with
\partial_{T}F=\sigma
, if such a flat exists.

2.7.
The essential Tits boundary

Definition 2.4
.

For a CAT(0) space without 3-flats, we define its
essential Tits boundary

\partial^{ess}_{T}X
as the subset of
\partial_{T}X
given by the union of all simple closed local geodesics.

Note that if
X
is a CAT(0) space with isolated flats and 1-dimensional Tits boundary which supports a geometric group action, then
\partial^{ess}_{T}X
is dense in
\partial_{\infty}X
but not closed.
However, we do have the following.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
X
be a CAT(0) space with
1
-dimensional Tits boundary which contains a 2-flat. If the diameter of
\partial_{T}X
is equal to
\pi
, then

\partial^{ess}_{T}X\subset\partial_{T}X
is a convex subset of diameter
\pi
. In particular,
\partial^{ess}_{T}X
is a geodesically complete CAT(1) space.

Proof.

Since
X
contains a 2-flat, the essential Tits boundary is non-empty.
Let
\xi
and
\hat{\xi}
be points in
\partial^{ess}_{T}X
. Then there is a geodesic
\alpha
of length at most
\pi
between them in
\partial_{T}X
.
We will show that this geodesic is contained in
\partial^{ess}_{T}X
. Since
\partial^{ess}_{T}X
is geodesically complete, we may assume that
\alpha
has length
\pi

to begin with. Let
\eta
be a point on
\alpha
and let
\epsilon>0
be such that
\eta\notin N_{\epsilon}(\{\xi,\hat{\xi}\})
.
Now we extend
\alpha
beyond
\xi
to a point
\xi^{\prime}
by a geodesic
\alpha^{\prime}
of length
\epsilon
in
\partial^{ess}_{T}X
.
Let
\beta
be a geodesic in
\partial_{T}X
from
\xi^{\prime}
to
\hat{\xi}
.
Then
\xi\notin\beta
and since
\partial_{T}X
is CAT(1), we must have

\beta\cap(\alpha\setminus B_{\epsilon}(\hat{\xi}))=\emptyset
.
The union
\alpha\cup\alpha^{\prime}\cup\beta
contains a simple closed local geodesic
c
.
By definition,
c
lies in
\partial^{ess}_{T}X
, and by construction,
c
contains
\alpha\setminus B_{\epsilon}(\hat{\xi})
and therefore
\eta
.
Thus, all of
\alpha
is contained in
\partial^{ess}_{T}X
as required.

Now, the convexity implies that
\partial^{ess}_{T}X
is geodesic. Since
\partial^{ess}_{T}X

4.1.

Lemma 4.1

Let

|\gamma_{k}x_{k},(\gamma_{k}g\gamma_{k}^{-1})\gamma_{k}x_{k}|=|x_{k},gx_{k}|=a_{g}

Proposition 4.5
.

Let

Lemma 4.6

Let

\xi^{\pm}
the two endpoints of

Since the diameter of

\pi

5.
Applications

In this section we provide the proofs of Corollaries
C
and
D
.

Proof of Corollary
C
.

If the Tits diameter is strictly larger than
\pi
, then by Theorem
A
,
X
contains a
\Gamma
-periodic geodesic of rank one.
Thus
\Gamma
contains a non-abelian free subgroup
[
Bal95
, Theorem 3.5]
. On the other hand, if the Tits diameter is equal to
\pi
,
then
X
is either an irreducible symmetric space, an irreducible Euclidean building or splits metrically as a product.
The first case follows from Tits original theorem
[
Tit72
]
and the second case is covered by
[
BB95
, Theorem F]
.
We are left with the product case,
X\cong X_{1}\times X_{2}
. By assumption, the factors
X_{i}
do not contain
2
-flats.
After passing to an index two subgroup, we may assume that
\Gamma
preserves the factors.
We will argue that there exist two
\Gamma
-axes such that their projections to one of the factors
are not asymptotic to one another and therefore lead to a non-abelian free subgroup
[
Bal95
, Theorem 3.5]
.
Suppose for contradiction that any pair of
\Gamma
-axes project to (one-sided) asymptotic geodesics in both factors.
Then every axis has to be regular, i.e. not parallel to a factor.
Since
\Gamma
acts geometrically, this means that every axial element lies in a subgroup
\mathbb{Z}^{2}<\Gamma
.
Every such subgroup preserves a
2
-flat. Two different
\Gamma
-periodic
2
-flats cannot be asymptotic to the same Weyl chamber because

\Gamma
acts properly discontinuous. Thus, we either find the required
\Gamma
-axes and hence the non-abelian subgroup, or
\Gamma
is
a Bieberbach group.
∎

Proof of Corollary
D
.

Under the assumptions, we find a
\Gamma
-equivariant quasi-isometry
\Phi:X\to X^{\prime}
.
Hence for every axial element
\gamma\in\Gamma
a pair of
\gamma
-axes
c\subset X
and
c^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}

has to have the same rank. Indeed,
\Phi(c)
lies at finite Hausdorff distance from
c^{\prime}

and therefore
c^{\prime}
has quadratic growth if
c
does. Thus the claim follows from Theorems
A
and
B

together with
[
KL97
, Theorem 1.1.3]
.
∎

Appendix A
The relative asymptotic Plateau problem after Kleiner–Lang

The primary goal of this section is to provide a proof of the
Half-Plane Lemma
.
Our proof requires a solution of an asymptotic Plateau problem relative to a flat.
Since this has not been done in the literature, we need some preparation.
The (non-relative) asymptotic Plateau problem has recently been solved by Kleiner–Lang
[
KL20
]

and we use their work extensively throughout this section. The idea to use solutions to a relative Plateau problem originated from
[
HKS22
]
.

In Section
A.1
, we agree on notation and proof monotonicity and volume rigidity of minimizing currents in CAT(0) spaces,
a result which is folklore.
In Section
A.2
, we solve the asymptotic Plateau problem relative to a 2-flat.
We provide basic properties of relative minimizers which are required for our proof of the
Half-Plane Lemma
.
At last, in Section
A.3
, we present the proof of the
Half-Plane Lemma
.
We have refrained from any kind of general treatment, in particular we only discuss spaces of rank 2.
This is all we need here and a broader discussion would get out of hand.

The general reference for this section is
[
KL20
]
.
See also
[
AK00a
,
Lan11
]
for a thorough background on geometric measure theory in metric spaces.

A.1.
Currents in CAT(0) spaces

Let
X
be a locally compact metric space.
For every integer

denote the set of all

(n+1)
-tuples
(\pi_{0},\ldots,\pi_{n})

such

We denote by

On Euclidean space

The following monotonicity property of minimizers is certainly well-known. We include

Proposition A.1

(monotonicity)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space and
S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,c}(X)
an area minimizer.
Then for every point
p\in\operatorname{spt}(S)\setminus\operatorname{spt}(\partial S)
the
r
-density
\Theta_{p,r}(S)

is a non-decreasing function of
r
, as long as
r\leq|p,\operatorname{spt}(\partial S)|
, and bounded below by
\omega_{n}
,

\omega_{n}\leq\Theta_{p,r}(S)\nearrow.

Moreover, if
\Theta_{p,r_{0}}(S)=\omega_{n}
holds for some
r_{0}\leq|p,\operatorname{spt}(\partial S)|
, then
\operatorname{spt}(S)\cap\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p)
is
isometric to a Euclidean ball of radius
r_{0}
and
S
has constant multiplicity 1.

Proof.

Monotonicity follows from the standard cone comparison since CAT(0) spaces satisfy a Euclidean coning inequality.
More precisely, for almost all
r\leq|p,\operatorname{spt}(\partial S)|
holds

{\mathbf{M}}(S\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>B_{r}(p))\leq\frac{r}{n}\cdot{\mathbf{M}}(\partial(S\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>B_{r}(p))).

By the coarea inequality we have

\frac{d}{dr}{\mathbf{M}}(S\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>B_{r}(p))\geq{\mathbf{M}}(\partial(S\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>B_{r}(p)))

for almost all
r
, and monotonicity follows by integration.
Recall that
S
is concentrated on the countably
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-rectifiable characteristic set
\chi_{S}
which is given by
\chi_{S}:=\{x\in X|\ \Theta_{x}(S)>0\}
.
The isoperimetric inequality yields some positive lower density bound at all points
p\in\operatorname{spt}(S)\setminus\operatorname{spt}(\partial S)

[
KL20
, Lemma 3.3]
.
This implies
\chi_{S}\cap\bar{B}_{r}(p)=\operatorname{spt}(S)\cap\bar{B}_{r}(p)
for every
r<|p,\operatorname{spt}(\partial S)|
.
It follows from
[
AK00b
, Theorem 5.4]
that
\Theta_{x}(S)=\theta\cdot\omega_{n}
for almost all points
x\in\chi_{S}
where
\theta:\chi_{S}\to\mathbb{N}
denotes the multiplicity function of
S
.
The bound
\Theta_{x}(S)\geq\omega_{n}
extends to the set
\operatorname{spt}(S)\setminus\operatorname{spt}(\partial S)
by upper semi-continuity of
\Theta_{x}(S)
.

Now suppose
\Theta_{p,r_{0}}(S)=\omega_{n}
holds for some
r_{0}<|p,\operatorname{spt}(\partial S)|
.
Then the above inequalities become equalities and we have

{\mathbf{M}}(S\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>B_{r}(p))\equiv\omega_{n}\cdot r^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathbf{M}}(\partial(S\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>B_{r}(p)))\equiv n\cdot\omega_{n}\cdot r^{n-1}

for all
r\leq r_{0}
.

We set
S_{r}:=S\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>\bar{B}_{r}(p)
and
\chi_{r}:=\operatorname{spt}(S_{r})=\chi_{S}\cap\bar{B}_{r}(p)
.
For
\lambda\in[0,1]
denote by

the map which contracts

radial geodesics by the factor
\lambda
.
We claim that
(c_{\lambda})_{\#}S_{r}
is an area minimizer for every
r\leq r_{0}
. By slicing,

{\mathbf{M}}(C_{p}(\partial S_{r})\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>(B_{r}(p)\setminus B_{\lambda r}(p)))\leq(1-\lambda^{n})\cdot\frac{r}{n}\cdot{\mathbf{M}}(\partial S_{r})=(1-\lambda^{n})\cdot\omega_{n}\cdot r^{n}.

Since
c_{\lambda}
is
\lambda
-Lipschitz, we have

{\mathbf{M}}((c_{\lambda})_{\#}S_{r})\leq\lambda^{n}\cdot\omega_{n}\cdot r^{n}.

Because
C_{p}(\partial S_{r})\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>(B_{r}(p)\setminus B_{\lambda r}(p))+(c_{\lambda})_{\#}S_{r}
is a filling of
\partial S_{r}
,
both inequalities above have to be equalities and
(c_{\lambda})_{\#}S_{r}
is an area minimizer with Euclidean mass growth relative

.

Sublemma A.2
.

For every

Proof.

Define

Since

The Lytchak-Rademacher theorem

Moreover, since

A.2.
The asymptotic Plateau problem relative to a 2-flat

We fix the following setup for this section. Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space with
1
-dimensional Tits boundary which contains a round
1
-sphere

\sigma\subset\partial_{T}X
in its Tits boundary. We fix a 2-flat
F\subset X
with
\partial_{T}F=\sigma
and a base point
o\in F
.
Moreover,
\alpha^{+}\subset\partial_{T}X
denotes an embedded local geodesic with
\alpha^{+}\cap\sigma=\partial\alpha^{+}
.
Our goal is to solve an asymptotic Plateau problem relative
F
. Informally speaking, we search for a minimal surface
S\subset X

whose boundary lies in
F
and which is asymptotic to
\alpha^{+}
. If the length of
\alpha^{+}
is close to
\pi
, then we expect
S

to behave almost like a flat half-plane orthogonal to
F
.
In order to achieve this, we employ doubling to reduce the problem to the ordinary asymptotic Plateau problem treated in
[
KL20
]
.
So we consider the double of
X
along
F
:

\hat{X}=X^{-}\cup_{F}X^{+}

where
X^{\pm}
are two isometric copies of
X
. Note that
\partial_{T}\hat{X}=\partial_{T}X^{-}\cup_{\sigma}\partial_{T}X^{+}
.
In particular,
\dim(\partial_{T}\hat{X})=1
. Let us denote by
\iota
the natural isometry of
\hat{X}
which interchanges
X^{-}

and
X^{+}
. For subsets
A^{+}\subset X^{+}
we set
A^{-}:=\iota(A^{+})\subset X^{-}
. We call a current
S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{2,loc}(\hat{X})

symmetric
, if

S=S^{+}-\iota_{\#}(S^{+})

where
S^{+}:=S\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>X^{+}
. Finally, we also double our boundary data, and define
\alpha:=\alpha^{+}\cup\iota(\alpha^{+})\subset\partial_{T}X
.

Definition A.5
.

For
S\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,loc}(\hat{X})
set
S^{+}:=S\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>X^{+}
. Then
S^{+}
is a
relative minimizer
if

{\mathbf{M}}(S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>B^{+})\leq{\mathbf{M}}(W^{+})

whenever
B^{+}\subset X^{+}
is a Borel set such that
S\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>(B^{+}\cup B^{-})\in{\mathbf{I}}_{n,c}(\hat{X})
and

satisfies
\operatorname{spt}(\partial(S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>B^{+}-W^{+}))\subset F
.

Lemma A.6
.

Let
S^{+}\in{\mathscr{D}}_{2,loc}(X^{+})
be a current of locally finite mass and with
\operatorname{spt}(\partial S^{+})\subset F
.
Suppose that
\|S^{+}\|(F)=0
and that there is a sequence
t_{k}\to 0
, such that
S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>(X\setminus N_{t_{k}}(F))\in{\mathbf{I}}_{2,loc}(X^{+})
.
Then
\partial S^{+}
is a local flat chain in
F
,
\partial S^{+}\in{\mathscr{F}}_{1,loc}(F)
. Moreover, the double
S:=S^{+}-\iota_{\#}(S^{+})
is a locally integral cycle,

S\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{2,loc}(\hat{X})
.

Proof.

For a point
p\in X^{+}
we denote by
d_{p}
the distance function to
p
.
To see that
\partial S^{+}
is a local flat chain in
F
, it is enough to prove the following claim.
For any
p\in\operatorname{spt}(\partial S^{+})
there exists
r_{0}>0
such that
\langle S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>(X\setminus N_{t_{k}}(F)),d_{p},r_{0}\rangle\in{\mathbf{I}}_{1,c}(X^{+})
for every

k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p)
is a flat limit of integral chains
T_{k}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{2,c}(X^{+})
.

Indeed, if the claim holds, then, since the nearest point projection
\pi_{F}:X^{+}\to F
is Lipschitz,
(\pi_{F})_{\#}(S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p))\in{\mathscr{F}}_{2,c}(F)

and therefore
\partial(\pi_{F})_{\#}(S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p))=(\pi_{F})_{\#}\partial(S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p))\in{\mathscr{F}}_{1,c}(F)
.
Then, either
p\notin\operatorname{spt}(\partial S^{+}-(\pi_{F})_{\#}\partial(S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p)))
or, there exists a large
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

p\notin\operatorname{spt}(\partial S^{+}-(\pi_{F})_{\#}\langle S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>(X\setminus N_{t_{k}}(F)),d_{p},r_{0}\rangle
. In any case, we see that
\partial S^{+}
is locally flat near

.

To prove the claim, we set
T_{k}:=S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>(X\setminus N_{t_{k}}(F))\in{\mathbf{I}}_{2,loc}(X^{+})
. By continuity of
S^{+}
as an extended functional

[
Lan11
, Theorem 4.4]
, we have
T_{k}\to S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>(X^{+}\setminus F)
weakly.
Since
\|S^{+}\|(F)=0
, we have
S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>(X^{+}\setminus F)=S^{+}

[
Lan11
, Lemma 4.7]
.
For
r>0
we infer from
[
Lan11

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\langle T_{k},d_{p},s\rangle\|(B_{r}(p))\ ds\leq\|T_{k}\|(B_{r}(p))\leq\|S^{+}\|(B_{r}(p)).

By monotone convergence, we conclude

\int_{0}^{\infty}\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\|\langle T_{k},d_{p},s\rangle\|(B_{r}(p))\ ds\leq\|S^{+}\|(B_{r}(p)).

Now choose
r_{0}\in(0,r)
such that
\langle T_{k},d_{p},r_{0}\rangle\in{\mathbf{I}}_{1,c}(X^{+})
for every

and
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\|\langle T_{k},d_{p},r_{0}\rangle\|<\infty

Set

. Because

. Then

Hence
(T^{\prime}_{k})
is a Cauchy sequence with respect to flat distance.
Because flat convergence implies weak convergence, the limit coincides with
S^{+}\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>\bar{B}_{r_{0}}(p)
. This confirms the claim and therefore

.

By the deformation theorem
[
FF60
,
Whi99a
]

and a locally flat

such that

. Recall that since

is locally integral. Hence

\partial_{T}F
.
Choose
\epsilon\leq\frac{\varphi}{2}
and then choose
r_{\epsilon}>0
according to Lemma
A.10
.
By assumption,
C_{o}(\partial\alpha^{+})
intersects
g_{0}
transverse in exactly one point, say
p\in F
.
Now we can find
r\geq\max\{r_{\epsilon},2|o,p|\}
such that all points in
\operatorname{spt}(\langle W,d_{o},r\rangle)
have distance larger than
\epsilon\cdot r
from the geodesic
g_{0}
.
Since
\partial(W\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>B_{r}(o))=\langle W,d_{o},r\rangle+(\partial S^{+}-C_{o}(\alpha^{+}))\>\mbox{\rule{0.43057pt}{5.16663pt}\rule{4.73611pt}{0.43057pt}}\>B_{r}(o)
is a flat 1-cycle with compact support,

\operatorname{spt}(\partial S^{+})
has to intersect
g_{0}
in
B_{r}(o)
.
∎

A.3.
Producing flat half-planes as relative minimizers

Now we have all the necessary ingredients to prove:

Half-Plane Lemma.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space with
1
-dimensional Tits boundary. Let
F\subset X
be a 2-flat with
\partial_{\infty}F=\sigma
.
Further, let
g
be an axis of an axial isometry
\gamma
and assume
\partial_{\infty}g\subset\sigma
.
Let
\xi^{\pm}\in\sigma
be antipodes disjoint from
\partial_{\infty}g
.
Suppose that there is a sequence of local geodesics
\alpha^{+}_{k}\subset\partial_{T}X
such that

•

\partial\alpha^{+}_{k}=\{\xi^{-}_{k},\xi^{+}_{k}\}
and
\xi^{\pm}_{k}\to\xi^{\pm}
with respect to the Tits metric;

•

the length of
\alpha_{k}^{+}
converges to
\pi
as
k\to\infty
.

Then there exists a 2-flat
\tilde{F}\subset P(g)
, and a flat half-plane
\tilde{H}
with boundary
\tilde{h}:=\partial\tilde{H}\subset\tilde{F}
such that the following properties hold.

(1)

g\subset N_{a}(\tilde{F})
if
g\subset N_{a}(F)
for
a>0
;

(2)

|\partial_{\infty}\tilde{h},\partial_{\infty}g|=|\{\xi^{-},\xi^{+}\},\partial_{\infty}g|
;

(3)

\partial_{\infty}\tilde{H}\cap\partial_{\infty}\tilde{F}=\partial_{\infty}\tilde{h}
.

Proof.

Choose a base point
o\in F
.
As before, we consider the double of
X
along
F
,
\hat{X}=X^{-}\cup_{F}X^{+}

and put
\alpha_{k}:=\alpha_{k}^{+}\cup\iota(\alpha_{k}^{+})
.
By Lemma
A.8
, we find a symmetric minimizer
S_{k}\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{2,loc}(\hat{X})
with
F_{\infty}(S_{k}-C_{o}(\alpha_{k}))=0

and
\Theta_{\infty}(S_{k})=\frac{\operatorname{length}(\alpha_{k})}{2}
. Let
g_{0}
be a complete geodesic in
F
parallel to
g
. By Lemma
A.11
,
there exists a point
p_{k}\in\operatorname{spt}(S_{k})\cap g_{0}
.

Now let us choose numbers
m_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that the points
\gamma^{m_{k}}(p_{k})
stay in a fixed compact set. Put
\gamma_{k}:=\gamma^{m_{k}}
.
After passing to subsequences, we have convergences with respect to pointed Hausdorff topology

\gamma_{k}(C_{p_{k}}(\alpha^{+}_{k}),p_{k})\to(\tilde{H},p_{\infty})\quad\text{ and }\quad\gamma_{k}(F,p_{k})\to(\tilde{F},p_{\infty})

where
\tilde{H}
is a flat half-plane,
\tilde{F}
is a 2-flat, and
\tilde{h}:=\partial\tilde{H}\subset\tilde{F}
.
We will now show that
\tilde{F}
and
\tilde{H}
satisfy the three required properties.

(1) Since
\gamma
preserves
g
, we have
g\subset N_{a}(\tilde{F})
for
a>0
whenever
g\subset N_{a}(F)
.

(2) Let
\partial_{\infty}\tilde{h}=\{\tilde{\xi}^{-},\tilde{\xi}^{+}\}
.
Since
\xi^{\pm}_{k}\to\xi^{\pm}
with respect to the Tits metric, and since
\gamma
preserves
g
, we have

|g(\pm\infty),\gamma_{k}\xi_{k}^{+}|=|g(\pm\infty),\xi_{k}^{+}|
and
|g(\pm\infty),\gamma_{k}\xi_{k}^{-}|=|g(\pm\infty),\xi_{k}^{-}|
.
Hence the statement follows from lower semi-continuity of the Tits metric with respect to the cone topology

[
KL97
, Lemma 2.3.1]
.

(3) Note that if this fails, then we must have
\tilde{H}\subset\tilde{F}
since
\partial\tilde{H}\subset\tilde{F}
.

Denote by
\tilde{F}^{\pm}\subset\tilde{F}
the two half-planes determined by
\partial\tilde{H}
.
Further, denote by
\sigma_{k}^{\pm}\subset\sigma
the two closed arcs determined by
\{\xi_{k}^{-},\xi_{k}^{+}\}
.
Then, as currents, we can write
F=F_{k}^{+}-F_{k}^{-}
where
F_{k}^{\pm}=C_{p_{k}}(\sigma_{k}^{\pm})\in{\mathbf{I}}_{2,loc}(\hat{X})
.
From
[
KL20
, Proposition 4.5]
, we conclude that for every
\epsilon>0

there exists
a_{\epsilon}^{\prime}>0
such that
F_{p_{k},r}(S_{k}-C_{p_{k}}(\alpha_{k}))\leq\frac{\epsilon}{3}
for all
r\geq a_{\epsilon}^{\prime}
.
On the other hand, Lemma
A.9
implies
F_{p_{k},r}(S_{k}-F)\geq\frac{\pi}{3}
for all
r>0
.
Hence,
F_{p_{k},r}(F-C_{p_{k}}(\alpha_{k}))\geq\frac{\pi-\epsilon}{3}
and thus, by symmetry of
C_{p_{k}}(\alpha_{k})
, for
r\geq a_{\epsilon}^{\prime}
,

F_{p_{k},r}(F_{k}^{\pm}-C_{p_{k}}(\alpha^{+}_{k}))\geq\frac{\pi-\epsilon}{6}.

Since
\operatorname{length}(\alpha_{k}^{+})\to\pi
, we have the uniform mass bound

(\|C_{p_{k}}(\alpha^{+}_{k})\|+\|\partial C_{p_{k}}(\alpha^{+}_{k})\|)(B_{r}(p_{k}))\leq\pi r^{2}+2r.

Using
[
KL20
, Theorem 2.3]
, we may assume that
(\gamma_{k})_{\#}(C_{p_{k}}(\alpha^{+}_{k}))
converges to a limit
C_{\infty}\in{\mathbf{I}}_{2,loc}(X^{+})
with respect to local flat topology. By
[
Wen11
, Proposition 2.2]
, the support of
C_{\infty}
is contained in
\tilde{H}
.
Because
(\gamma_{k})_{\#}\partial(C_{p_{k}}(\alpha^{+}_{k}))\to\partial\tilde{H}
weakly, we have
\partial C_{\infty}=\partial\tilde{H}
. But
C_{\infty}

is a top-dimensional locally integral current in
\tilde{H}
, hence
C_{\infty}=\tilde{H}

[
Lan11
, Theorem 7.2]
.

Now suppose for contradiction, that
\tilde{H}=\tilde{F}^{+}
. Set
T_{k}=F_{k}^{+}-C_{p_{k}}(\alpha^{+}_{k})\in{\mathbf{Z}}_{2,loc}(X^{+})
. Because
(\gamma_{k})_{\#}F_{k}^{+}\to\tilde{F}^{+}
locally flat, we infer
(\gamma_{k})_{\#}(T_{k})\to 0
locally flat. Since
\partial T_{k}=0
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

[
LW11
, Proposition 3.2]
implies
F_{p_{\infty},r}((\gamma_{k})_{\#}(T_{k}))\to 0
for every
r>0
.
Since
\gamma_{k}(p_{k})\to p_{\infty}
, this contradicts (
\star
). Hence
\tilde{H}
cannot be contained in
\tilde{F}
and the proof is complete.
∎

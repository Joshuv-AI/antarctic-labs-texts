Let

]

where

We start with the metaplectic representation from

2

Constructing the Metaplectic Representation

We will use lower-case letters to denote elements in a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and the corresponding upper-case letter for the corresponding element in its associated uniform pro-
p
group
G\coloneqq\exp(p\mathfrak{g})
. It is a standard fact of Iwasawa algebras (see for example
[
Dix+03
, §7]
) that a general element
\zeta\in KG
can be uniquely written as

\zeta=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\lambda_{\alpha}(\mathbf{G}-1)^{\alpha},\qquad\qquad(\mathbf{G}-1)^{\alpha}\coloneqq(G_{1}-1)^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(G_{d}-1)^{\alpha_{d}},

where
\left(G_{1},\dots,G_{d}\right)
is a topological generating set for
G
and
\lambda_{\alpha}\in K
are uniformly bounded with respect to the
p
-adic valuation on
K
.

For fixed
n\geq 2
, let

\mathfrak{g}\coloneqq\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&-A^{T}\end{bmatrix}:A,B,C\in M_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}),B^{T}=B,C^{T}=C\right\}

with associated uniform pro-
p
group

G\coloneqq\exp(p\,\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}))=\SP_{2n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\cap(I+pM_{2n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})).

We will use the following map adapted from
[
Fol89
]
to construct an infinite dimensional
KG
-module.

Proposition
\thetheorem

(Metaplectic Representation)
—

There is a Lie algebra homomorphism
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to A_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
given by

\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&-A^{T}\end{bmatrix}\longmapsto-\frac{1}{2}\tr(A)-\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}{A_{ij}x_{j}\partial_{i}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}{B_{ij}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}{C_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}}.

Here
A_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is the
n^{th}
Weyl algebra on generators
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
and
\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n}
. We will denote
\textnormal{{x}}^{\alpha}\coloneqq x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
and
\partial^{\beta}\coloneqq\partial_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots\partial_{n}^{\beta_{n}}
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
.

We are interested in knowing if there are any non-zero prime ideals of
KG
of infinite codimension. To this end, we lift the above homomorphism to a map on
KG.
We tensor first with
R
to obtain a map of
R
-Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}_{R}\coloneqq\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(R)\to A_{n}(R)
and lift this to a map of associative algebras
U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})\to A_{n}(R),
where
U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})
is the universal enveloping algebra.

Now note that both
U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})
and
A_{n}(R)
inherit
p
-adic valuations from
R
. Explicitly, as
K
is a finite
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
extension, the valuation on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
can be extended to a valuation on
K,
which we denote by
v
. Let
d=2n^{2}+n
be the dimension of
\mathfrak{g}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and fix a basis
g_{1},\ldots,g_{d}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. We denote
\mathbf{g}^{\alpha}\coloneqq g_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots g_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, and write
\left\lvert\alpha\right\rvert=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}
. Then, for an element of
U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})
we define its valuation by

v_{p}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}c_{\alpha}\mathbf{g}^{\alpha}\right)\coloneqq\min_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}v(c_{\alpha}),

and similarly, for elements of
A_{n}(R),

v_{p}\left(\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha,\beta}\textnormal{{x}}^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}\right)\coloneqq\min_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}v(c_{\alpha,\beta}).

We then note the algebra homomorphism is continuous with respect to the induced topologies. Therefore, this extends to a map
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})}\to\widehat{A_{n}(R)}
of the
p
-adic completions.

Then

\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})}\coloneqq\varprojlim_{\lambda\geq 0}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})_{\lambda}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\textstyle{U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})_{\lambda}}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})_{\lambda}}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})_{\lambda}}$}}}=\left\{\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}c_{\alpha}\mathbf{g}^{\alpha}:c_{\alpha}\in R,c_{\alpha}\to 0\text{ as }|\alpha|\to\infty\right\}

with
U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})_{\lambda}\coloneqq\left\{x\in U(\mathfrak{g}_{R}):v_{p}(x)\geq\lambda\right\}
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, and similarly

\widehat{A_{n}(R)}\coloneqq\varprojlim_{\lambda\geq 0}{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{A_{n}(R)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{A_{n}(R)_{\lambda}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{A_{n}(R)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\textstyle{A_{n}(R)_{\lambda}}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{A_{n}(R)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{A_{n}(R)_{\lambda}}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{A_{n}(R)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{A_{n}(R)_{\lambda}}$}}}=\left\{\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha,\beta}\textnormal{{x}}^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}:c_{\alpha,\beta}\in R,c_{\alpha,\beta}\to 0\text{ as }|\alpha|+|\beta|\to\infty\right\},

with
A_{n}(R)_{\lambda}\coloneqq\left\{y\in A_{n}(R):v_{p}(y)\geq\lambda\right\}
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

Now, we tensor with
K
and obtain a map
\rho:\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}_{K}\to\widehat{A_{n}(R)}_{K}
, where

\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}_{K}\coloneqq\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})}\otimes_{R}K=\left\{\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha}\mathbf{g}^{\alpha}:c_{\alpha}\in K,c_{\alpha}\to 0\text{ as }|\alpha|\to\infty\right\}

and

\widehat{A_{n}(R)}_{K}\coloneqq\widehat{A_{n}(R)}\otimes_{R}K=\left\{\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha,\beta}\textnormal{{x}}^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}:c_{\alpha,\beta}\in K,c_{\alpha,\beta}\to 0\text{ as }|\alpha|+|\beta|\to\infty\right\}.

We also define
U(\mathfrak{g})_{K}\coloneqq U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})\otimes_{R}K\subseteq\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}_{K}
. Finally, as noted in
[
Man23
, Corollary 2.5.4]

embeds into
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}_{K}
via
g\mapsto e^{pg}
for
g\in G
, so we can restrict
\rho
along this embedding to obtain a map
\rho|_{KG}:KG\to\widehat{A_{n}(R)}_{K}
. The aim is to show that
\ker{\left.\kern-1.2pt\rho\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{KG}}=0
.

Let us also mention the valuations defined on
U(\mathfrak{g}_{R})
and
A_{n}(R)
extend in the natural way to valuations on
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}_{K},
and
\widehat{A_{n}(R)}_{K}
respectively. That these are indeed valuations follows from
[
AW14
, Lemma 5.2]
together with
[
ST02
, Remark 4.6]
for
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{g})}_{K}
and
[
Pan07
, Lemma 1.2.4]
for the Weyl algebra (and by continuity for the completed Weyl algebra as well).

From now on, we fix the following basis for
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
e_{ij}=[\delta_{iI}\delta_{jJ}]_{IJ}
for
1\leq i,j\leq 2n
denote the
2n\times 2n
unit matrices. Then, for
1\leq i,j\leq n
we let

\displaystyle a_{ij}

\displaystyle=e_{ij}-e_{j+n,i+n}

\displaystyle b_{ij}

\displaystyle=e_{i,j+n}+e_{j,i+n}

\displaystyle c_{ij}

\displaystyle=e_{i+n,j}+e_{j+n,i}

\displaystyle\rho(a_{ij})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ij}-x_{j}\partial_{i}

\displaystyle\rho(b_{ij})

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}\partial_{j}

\displaystyle\rho(c_{ij})

\displaystyle=-x_{i}x_{j}

so note that in particular

and

. We record here the commutation relations for later reference.

Lemma
\thetheorem
—

For all

[a_{ij},a_{kl}]=\delta_{jk}a_{il}-\delta_{il}a_{kj},\qquad[a_{ij},b_{kl}]=\delta_{jk}b_{il}+\delta_{jl}b_{ik},\qquad[a_{ij},c_{kl}]=-\delta_{il}c_{jk}-\delta_{ik}c_{jl},

Proof.

For all
1\leq i,j,k,l\leq 2n
we have

[e_{ij},e_{kl}]=\delta_{jk}e_{il}-\delta_{il}e_{kj}

which gives the relations above.
∎

The Gluing Lemma

Then

is a faithful

Proof.

Let

which does not depend on

and

is finitely generated over

such that

and

are

. Moreover, by taking common denominators for the coefficients of

Lemma
\thetheorem
—

. In particular,

and

are faithful
\widehat{A_{n}(R)}_{K}
-modules.

Proof.

Take

\zeta=\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}}\lambda_{\alpha,\beta}x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\partial_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots\partial_{n}^{\beta_{n}}\in\Ann_{\widehat{A_{n}(R)}_{K}}K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}].

\mathfrak{n}\oplus\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}
and
W_{1}
and
W_{X_{1}^{-1}}
are still
\widehat{U(\mathfrak{t})}_{K}
-submodules since the elements of
\mathfrak{b}
act by homogeneous operators of even non-positive degree. Then:

•

RH
acts locally finitely on

since for

RH\cdot f\subseteq\left\{g\in K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]:\deg g\leq\deg f,\,v_{p}(g)\geq v_{p}(f)\right\},

which is finitely generated as an

as

•

KH
acts faithfully on

since
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\rho\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{KH}}={\left.\kern-1.2pt\rho\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{KT}}
is injective, and
\rho(KH)
acts faithfully on

by

4
.

•

The multiplication map is injective by

4.1
.

So

is faithful as a

{\left.\kern-1.2pt\rho\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{KG}}

5
Abelian subalgebras

In this section, let
G
be any uniform pro-
p
group, and
H\leq G
a torsion-free abelian pro-
p
group. We give conditions for maps out of
KH
with infinite-dimensional image to be injective, first in terms of closed subgroups of
\Aut(H)
, and then in terms of the normaliser of
\mathfrak{h}
in
\mathfrak{g}
.

5.1
Invariant ideals in commutative algebras

Let
H
have topological generating set
H_{1},\ldots,H_{d}
. We equip
KH
with the
p
-adic filtration

w_{p}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\lambda_{\alpha}\left(\mathbf{H}-1\right)^{\alpha}\right)=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}v(\lambda_{\alpha})

where
(\mathbf{H}-1)^{\alpha}=(H_{1}-1)^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots(H_{d}-1)^{\alpha_{d}}
and the
\lambda_{\alpha}\in K
are uniformly bounded. Note that
w_{p}
is complete and separated with
\gr KH\cong kH[s,s^{-1}]
, where
s
is the image of the uniformiser of
K
.

We endow
\Aut(H)
with the congruence topology as defined in
[
Dix+03
, §5.2]
, and let
\Gamma\leq\Aut(H)
be a closed subgroup
such that

(i)

\mathcal{L}(H)
is an irreducible
\mathcal{L}(\Gamma)
-module;

(ii)

[\Gamma,H]\coloneqq\{\gamma(h)h^{-1}:\gamma\in\Gamma,\,h\in H\}\subseteq H^{p}
.

Here
\mathcal{L}
denotes the corresponding
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-Lie algebra as in
[
Dix+03
, §9.5]
. Note that (ii) forces
\Gamma
to be isomorphic to a closed subgroup of the first congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{1}\coloneqq 1+M_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
of
\GL_{d}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, which is uniform by
[
Dix+03
, Theorem 5.2]
. In particular then,
\Gamma
is a finitely generated pro-
p
group, and every
\Gamma^{l}\coloneqq\left\langle\gamma^{l}:\gamma\in\Gamma\right\rangle\leq\Aut(H)
is also a closed subgroup by
[
Mar94
]
.

We extend the action of
\Gamma
first to
RH
by linearity and then to
KH
, as the
\Gamma
-action does not affect the
w_{p}
-filtration. Similarly,
\Gamma
acts on the local ring
kH
, and is exactly chosen so that
[
Ard12
, Corollary 8.1]
gives

Lemma
\thetheorem
—

For
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the only
\Gamma^{l}
-invariant prime ideals of
kH
are 0 and the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}=(H_{1}-1,\ldots,H_{d}-1)
.

This uses the observation that
\mathcal{L}(\Gamma^{l})=\mathcal{L}(\Gamma)
, which follows directly from the definition of
\mathcal{L}
. This maximality condition lifts to a condition on
\Gamma
-invariant ideals in
KH
.

Proposition
\thetheorem
—

Let
I
be an non-zero,
\Gamma
-invariant ideal in
KH
. Then
I
has finite
K
-codimension.

Proof.

If
I=KH
this is trivial, so suppose not. Consider the
\gr K
-module
\gr KH
, and the non-zero, proper,
\Gamma
-invariant, graded submodule
\gr I
. Identifying
\gr KH\cong kH[s,s^{-1}]
, we see that
s
is a unit, and so each graded component of
\gr I
is equal. Thus we may write
\gr I=J[s,s^{-1}]
for some ideal
J\subseteq kH
.

As
kH
is noetherian,
\textnormal{rad}(J)=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\mathfrak{p}_{i}
for some non-zero prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{m}
. As
\textnormal{rad}(J)
is also
\Gamma
-invariant and
\Gamma
acts by automorphisms, each element of
\Gamma
permutes the
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
, so we can find an
l\geq 1
such that
\Gamma^{l}
fixes all of the
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. So by
Section

5.1
,
\textnormal{rad}(J)=\mathfrak{m}
.

Thus we can find an
r\geq 0
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{r}\subseteq J
, and since
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.77777pt}{$\displaystyle{kH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.86722pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}^{n}}$}}{\raisebox{2.77777pt}{$\textstyle{kH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.61722pt}{$\textstyle{\mathfrak{m}^{n}}$}}{\raisebox{1.94444pt}{$\scriptstyle{kH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.8772pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{m}^{n}}$}}{\raisebox{1.38889pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{kH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.43887pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathfrak{m}^{n}}$}}}
is finite-dimensional over
k
so is
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.77777pt}{$\displaystyle{kH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.73332pt}{$\displaystyle{J}$}}{\raisebox{2.77777pt}{$\textstyle{kH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.73332pt}{$\textstyle{J}$}}{\raisebox{1.94444pt}{$\scriptstyle{kH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.91333pt}{$\scriptstyle{J}$}}{\raisebox{1.38889pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{kH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.36665pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{J}$}}}
. Now note that

{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.73332pt}{$\displaystyle{\gr KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.73332pt}{$\displaystyle{\gr I}$}}{\raisebox{2.73332pt}{$\textstyle{\gr KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.73332pt}{$\textstyle{\gr I}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\scriptstyle{\gr KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\scriptstyle{\gr I}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\gr KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\gr I}$}}}\cong{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.46611pt}{$\displaystyle{kH[s,s^{-1}]}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.46611pt}{$\displaystyle{J[s,s^{-1}]}$}}{\raisebox{3.21611pt}{$\textstyle{kH[s,s^{-1}]}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.21611pt}{$\textstyle{J[s,s^{-1}]}$}}{\raisebox{2.30498pt}{$\scriptstyle{kH[s,s^{-1}]}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.30498pt}{$\scriptstyle{J[s,s^{-1}]}$}}{\raisebox{1.86665pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{kH[s,s^{-1}]}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.86665pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{J[s,s^{-1}]}$}}}\cong\left({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.77777pt}{$\displaystyle{kH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.73332pt}{$\displaystyle{J}$}}{\raisebox{2.77777pt}{$\textstyle{kH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.73332pt}{$\textstyle{J}$}}{\raisebox{1.94444pt}{$\scriptstyle{kH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.91333pt}{$\scriptstyle{J}$}}{\raisebox{1.38889pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{kH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.36665pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{J}$}}}\right)[s,s^{-1}]

so
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.73332pt}{$\displaystyle{\gr KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.73332pt}{$\displaystyle{\gr I}$}}{\raisebox{2.73332pt}{$\textstyle{\gr KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.73332pt}{$\textstyle{\gr I}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\scriptstyle{\gr KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\scriptstyle{\gr I}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\gr KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\gr I}$}}}

. By

, Theorem I.4.2.4]
we have
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.73332pt}{$\displaystyle{\gr KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.73332pt}{$\displaystyle{\gr I}$}}{\raisebox{2.73332pt}{$\textstyle{\gr KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.73332pt}{$\textstyle{\gr I}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\scriptstyle{\gr KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\scriptstyle{\gr I}$}}{\raisebox{2.15277pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\gr KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\gr I}$}}}\cong\gr\left({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.73332pt}{$\displaystyle{KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.73332pt}{$\displaystyle{I}$}}{\raisebox{2.73332pt}{$\textstyle{KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.73332pt}{$\textstyle{I}$}}{\raisebox{1.91333pt}{$\scriptstyle{KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.91333pt}{$\scriptstyle{I}$}}{\raisebox{1.36665pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.36665pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{I}$}}}\right)

\gr\left({\mathchoice{\raisebox{2.73332pt}{$\displaystyle{KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.73332pt}{$\displaystyle{I}$}}{\raisebox{2.73332pt}{$\textstyle{KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.73332pt}{$\textstyle{I}$}}{\raisebox{1.91333pt}{$\scriptstyle{KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.91333pt}{$\scriptstyle{I}$}}{\raisebox{1.36665pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{KH}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.36665pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{I}$}}}\right)

Corollary
\thetheorem
—

Let

be a filtered

-algebra, and let
\psi:KH\to T
a filtered
K
-algebra homomorphism such that
\psi(KH)
is not finite

-dimensional. If
\ker\psi
is
\Gamma
-invariant, then
\psi
is injective.

5.2
Abelian subalgebras

A key example of such automorphism groups
\Gamma
comes from conjugation. Suppose now that
H
is subgroup of a uniform pro-
p
group
G
, and let
\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}
be their associated
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Lie algebras.
Let
\mathfrak{n}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be a subalgebra contained in the normaliser
\{x\in\mathfrak{g}:[x,y]\in\mathfrak{h}\text{ for }y\in\mathfrak{h}\}
of
\mathfrak{h}
, and
N
its associated pro-
p
group. We assume that
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{n}
is torsion-free as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module so that by
[
Dix+03
, Proposition 7.15]
,
N\leq_{c}G
is a closed uniform subgroup.

Proposition
\thetheorem
—

Let
T
be a filtered
K
-algebra, and let
\psi:KG\to T
be a filtered
K
-algebra homomorphism such that
\psi(KH)
is not finite
K
-dimensional. If
\mathcal{L}(H)
is an irreducible
\mathcal{L}(N)
-module, then
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\psi\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{KH}}
is injective.

Proof.

We first show that the conjugation homomorphism
\varphi:N\to\Aut(H)
by
x\mapsto(\varphi_{x}:y\mapsto xyx^{-1})
is continuous. To this end, suppose a net
(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
converges to
1_{N}
in
N
. It suffices to prove
\varphi_{x_{\lambda}}
converges to
\id_{H}
in the congruence topology; that is, that for any open normal subgroup
H^{\prime}\lhd_{o}H
, there is a
\mu_{H^{\prime}}\in\Lambda
such that
[\varphi_{x_{\lambda}},H]\subseteq H^{\prime}
for
\lambda\geq\mu_{H^{\prime}}
.

Fix some
H^{\prime}\lhd_{o}H
. For any
y\in H
,
\varphi_{x_{\lambda}}(y)y^{-1}=[x_{\lambda},y]
converges to
1_{H}
in
H
, so there is a
\mu_{y}\in\Lambda
such that
[x_{\lambda},y]\in H^{\prime}
for
\lambda\geq\mu_{y}
. If
h\in H^{\prime}
, we also see that
[x_{\lambda},hy]\in H^{\prime}
by normality, so
[x_{\lambda},g]\in H^{\prime}
whenever
g\in H^{\prime}y
,
\lambda\geq\mu_{y}
. As
H
is profinite,
H^{\prime}
has finite index in
H
, so it is enough to take
\mu_{N^{\prime}}\geq\mu_{y_{i}}
,
1\leq i\leq m
, for some finite collection
y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}
of coset representatives of
H/H^{\prime}
.

We check that
\Gamma=\varphi(N)
satisfies conditions (i) and (ii).

(i)

\mathcal{L}(\varphi):\mathcal{L}(N)\to\mathcal{L}(\varphi(N))
is surjective by
[
Dix+03
, §9, Ex. 7]
, so
\mathcal{L}(H)
is irreducible as an
\mathcal{L}(\varphi(N))
-module.

(ii)

[\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{h}]\leq\mathfrak{h}\cap p\mathfrak{g}=p\mathfrak{h}
as
\mathfrak{g}
is a powerful Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
is torsion-free. Then the Campbell-Baker-Hausdorff formula guarantees that
[\varphi(N),H]=[N,H]\leq H^{p}
.

Finally,
\ker\psi
is a (two-sided) ideal of
KG
and the conjugation action of
N
on
G
fixes
H
, so both
\ker\psi
and
KH
are
N
-invariant. Thus
\ker{\left.\kern-1.2pt\psi\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{KH}}=KH\cap\ker\psi
is
N
-invariant, and we can apply
Section

5.1
with
\Gamma=\varphi(N)
.
∎

We apply
Section

5.2
with

,

,

and
\psi=\rho

2

Corollary
\thetheorem
—

{\left.\kern-1.2pt\rho\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{KC}}

KC

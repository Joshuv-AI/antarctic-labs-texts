Remarks on the rational SFT formalism
Janko Latschev

Abstract
Recently, rational symplectic field theory (SFT) has played a central role in the work of Siegel [ 7 ] and of Moreno and Zhou [ 6 ] , who defined (different) new invariants of contact manifolds using the theory. The goal of this note is to show how these are related to the original formulation of the theory by Eliashberg, Givental and Hofer [ 1 ] .



1
Introduction

Symplectic field theory (SFT) as described by Eliashberg, Givental and Hofer
[
1
]
gives a general framework for discussing holomorphic curve invariants of contact and symplectic manifolds. The theory comes in several levels of generality, ranging from full SFT, which takes into account curves with underlying domains of arbitrary genus, to (linearized) contact homology, which deals with a specific subcollection of curves defined on punctured spheres. The analytic foundations of the theory have still not been published completely, which has rather slowed down the development of the subject.

Recently, however, there have been some works that use the intermediate world of
rational
SFT, i.e. the part of the theory that deals with all curves of genus zero. In
[
7
]
, Siegel used rational SFT to define a large number of new symplectic capacities, and showed that they give interesting new embedding obstructions. In
[
6
]
, Moreno and Zhou describe and use new numerical invariants of contact manifolds which obstruct the existence of fillings or cobordisms, and which are also extracted from rational SFT. Both of these papers use a rather ad hoc description of the theory developed by Siegel, who implemented suggestions of Hutchings
[
3
]
.

The goal of this note is to show how the relevant parts of the theory can be formulated in the algebraic language of the original description of rational SFT by Eliashberg, Givental and Hofer, and in this way establish a clearer link between the two descriptions of the theory. At the same time, I believe that the algebraic formalism used here makes the symmetries and combinatorial factors in the various constructions a bit more transparent than the graphical approach chosen in
[
7
]
.

In the following section, which forms the core of this work, I describe the relevant aspects of the algebraic formalism. After a brief review of rational SFT in §
3
, I then explain how the algebraic aspects of the work of Siegel (in §
4
) and of Moreno-Zhou (in §
5
) are dealt with in this formalism. I refrain from discussing any of the geometric arguments given or consequences derived in these works, as at the moment I have nothing to add in this regard.

2
Algebra

Throughout this section, we fix a unital ring
\Lambda
containing
{\mathbb{Q}}
, and an integer
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. For a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded
\Lambda
-module
V=\oplus_{d\in{\mathbb{Z}}}V_{d}
, our convention is that the degree shifted module
V[n]
has graded components
V[n]_{d}=V_{d+n}
, so the degree of individual elements is lower by
n
after the shift.

2.1
Structures

Let
C
be a free graded module over the ring
\Lambda
, generated by symbols

q_{\gamma}
with
\gamma
in some indexing set
\Gamma
. Set
\mathcal{A}:=SC
,
which we will view as either an algebra or a coalgebra depending on the
context. A linear basis consists of all monomials in the
q_{\gamma}
.
Moreover, let
\mathcal{P}:=SC\otimes S(C[-2N])^{*}
be (a degree shifted version of)
the linear self-maps of
\mathcal{A}=SC
.
We identify these with power series in variables
p_{\gamma}
with polynomial coefficients in the
q_{\gamma}
. The degree shift means that

|p_{\gamma}|=2N-|q_{\gamma}|.

Thinking of
\mathcal{P}
as an algebra over
\Lambda
, we can endow it with a Poisson bracket of degree
-2N
, characterized by the properties that all
p_{\gamma}
(respectively all
q_{\gamma}
) pairwise commute, and

\{p_{\gamma_{1}},q_{\gamma_{2}}\}=\kappa_{\gamma_{1}}\delta_{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}},

where
\kappa:\Gamma\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is a choice of weights.

Remark
.

I have included the weights
\kappa_{\gamma}
for easier comparison with the formalism of SFT as described in
[
1
]
. One could avoid them by absorbing them into either the
p
- or the
q
-variables, for example redefining
p_{\gamma}
as
\frac{1}{\kappa_{\gamma}}p_{\gamma}
.

Any element
h\in\mathcal{P}

can be expanded as

h=\sum_{r,s\geq 0}h^{r}_{s},

where
h^{r}_{s}
is of degree
r
in
p
and of degree
s
in
q
.
Moreover, we have the ideals

\overline{\mathcal{P}}:=\{h\in\mathcal{P}\,:\,h^{0}_{s}=0\text{ for all }s\geq 0\}

and

\underline{\mathcal{P}}:=\{h\in\mathcal{P}\,:\,h^{r}_{0}=0\text{ for all }r\geq 0\},

Thinking in terms of power series,

h\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}\quad\iff\quad h|_{p=0}=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad h\in\underline{\mathcal{P}}\quad\iff\quad h|_{q=0}=0.

Elements of the intersection

\widehat{\mathcal{P}}:=\overline{\mathcal{P}}\cap\underline{\mathcal{P}}

correspond to multiplinear maps from
C
to
C
which are symmetric in inputs and outputs.

We now consider an element
h\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}
which is homogeneous of degree
d
. For any
r\geq 1
, we use
h^{r}=\sum_{s\geq 0}h^{r}_{s}
to define a map

\overrightarrow{h^{r}}:S^{r}\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}

of degree
d-2Nr
by using iterated brackets, i.e.

\overrightarrow{h^{r}}(w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{r})=\{\dots\{h^{r},w_{1}\},\dots\},w_{r}\},

where
w_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
. The fact that this operation is indeed symmetric in the
w_{i}
is a direct consequence of the Jacobi identity for the Poisson bracket and the fact that the
w_{i}
pairwise Poisson commute. It is convenient to shift degrees and consider the map of degree
d-2N

\overrightarrow{h}=\sum_{r\geq 1}\overrightarrow{h^{r}}:\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}[2N])\to\mathcal{A}[2N],

where we use the notation
\overline{S}V=\oplus_{r\geq 1}S^{r}V
. Viewing
\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}[2N])
as a coalgebra, this map admits a unique extension to a coderivation

D_{h}=\sum_{r=1}^{\infty}D^{r}_{h}:\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}[2N])\to\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}[2N])

of the same degree, where for
n\geq r
the map
D^{r}_{h}:S^{n}(\mathcal{A}[2N])\to S^{n-r+1}(\mathcal{A}[2N])
is given by

D^{r}_{h}(w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{n})=\hskip-14.22636pt\sum_{{i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{r}}\atop{i_{r+1}<i_{r+2}<\dots<i_{N}}}\hskip-14.22636pt(-1)^{\boxtimes}\,\,\overrightarrow{h^{r}}(w_{i_{1}}\odot\dots\odot w_{i_{r}})\odot w_{i_{r+1}}\odot\dots\odot w_{i_{n}}.

(1)

Here the sum is over all
(r,n-r)
-unshuffles, and
(-1)^{\boxtimes}
is the sign acquired from permuting the words
w_{i}
into the given order (the even shift in degrees is harmless here). By a routine computation, we get

Lemma 1
.

For homogeneous elements
h
and
g
of
\overline{\mathcal{P}}
we have

D_{h}\circ D_{g}-(-1)^{|h|\cdot|g|}D_{g}\circ D_{h}=D_{\{h,g\}}.

In particular, if
h\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}
has odd degree and
\{h,h\}=0
, then
D_{h}
is a codifferential.
blah

∎

Remark
.

By our assumption
h\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}
, the codifferential associated to an odd Poisson-self-commuting element
h
has the additional property
D_{h}({{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}})=0
, where
{{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}}\in\Lambda[2N]\subseteq\mathcal{A}[2N]\cong S^{1}(\mathcal{A}[2N])
is the unit of the algebra
\mathcal{A}
.

Suppose
{\mathbf{h}}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}
is of degree
2N-1
and satisfies
\{{\mathbf{h}},{\mathbf{h}}\}=0
, so that
D=D_{\mathbf{h}}
has degree
-1
. It is well-known (cf.
[
4
]
) that this means that the operations
\{\overrightarrow{{\mathbf{h}}^{r}}\}_{r\geq 1}
can be interpreted as an
L_{\infty}
-structure on
\mathcal{A}[2N+1]
. This is the main structure that we will study and refine in the following subsections. Note that, by construction, the individual operations
\overrightarrow{{\mathbf{h}}^{r}}
are derivations with respect to the algebra structure on
\mathcal{A}
in each entry.

Remark
.

It is worth pointing out that, at this point, we have the following invariants associated to an element
{\mathbf{h}}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}
of degree
2N-1
satisfying
\{{\mathbf{h}},{\mathbf{h}}\}=0
:

(i)

Taking a single Poisson bracket with
{\mathbf{h}}
defines a derivation
\mathbf{D}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{P}
of degree
-1
, and so there is an associated homology
H_{*}(\mathcal{P},\mathbf{D})
. This is the point of view taken in the description of rational SFT in
[
1
]
.

(ii)

We also have the homology
H_{*}(\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}[2N]),D_{\mathbf{h}})
of the symmetric coalgebra
\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}[2N])
with respect to the coderivation introduced above. Note that, by construction,
D_{\mathbf{h}}
preserves each of the truncations
S^{\leq k}(\mathcal{A}[2N])
, so we also have the corresponding homologies.

(iii)

The operator
D^{1}=\overrightarrow{{\mathbf{h}}^{1}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
induced from the part
{\mathbf{h}}^{1}
of
{\mathbf{h}}
linear in the
p
-variables is a differential of degree
-1
, and so one can consider its homology
H_{*}(\mathcal{A},D^{1})
. In the context of SFT, this is what is known as (full) contact homology.

If desired, one could use homotopy transfer to induce an
L_{\infty}
-structure on
H_{*}(\mathcal{A}[2N+1],D^{1})
from the one on
\mathcal{A}[2N+1]
.

2.2
Morphisms

Now suppose we have two graded
\Lambda
-modules
C^{+}
and
C^{-}
, together with the associated
\mathcal{A}^{\pm}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\pm}
. We introduce the module
\mathcal{L}:=SC^{-}\otimes S(C^{+}[-2N])^{*}
of power series in the
p^{+}
with polynomial coefficients in the
q^{-}
. As with
\mathcal{P}^{\pm}
above, we introduce the notation

\overline{\mathcal{L}}:=\{f\in\mathcal{L}\,:\,f|_{p^{+}=0}=0\},\quad\underline{\mathcal{L}}:=\{f\in\mathcal{L}\,:\,f|_{q^{-}=0}=0\}\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{\mathcal{L}}:=\overline{\mathcal{L}}\cap\underline{\mathcal{L}}.

To an element
g=g_{s}\in\mathcal{P}^{+}
which is of pure degree
s
in the
q
-variables, we can associate the map

\mathcal{L}\leftarrow S^{s}\mathcal{L}:\overleftarrow{g_{s}}

given by iterated brackets

(c_{1}\odot\dots\odot c_{s})\overleftarrow{g_{s}}=\{c_{1},\{\dots,\{c_{s},g_{s}\}\dots\}.

Remark
.

Note that
s=0
is allowed here, and gives the inclusion of a power series
g_{0}
in
p^{+}
independent of
q^{+}
into
S^{1}\mathcal{L}\cong\mathcal{L}
.

For
s\geq 1
, these maps are derivations in each entry. For all
s\geq 0
, they can be extended to coderivations

S\mathcal{L}\leftarrow S\mathcal{L}:\overleftarrow{D_{g_{s}}}

in the usual way (cf. (
1
)).

Similarly, given an element
g=g^{r}\in\mathcal{P}^{-}
which is of pure degree
r
in
p^{-}
, we associate to it the map

\overrightarrow{g^{r}}:S^{r}\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}

given by iterated brackets

\overrightarrow{g^{r}}(c_{1}\odot\dots\odot c_{r})=\{\dots\{g^{r},c_{1}\},\dots\},c_{r}\}.

These can also be extended to coderivations

\overrightarrow{D_{g^{r}}}:S\mathcal{L}\to S\mathcal{L}.

The analogue of Lemma
1
in this context is

Lemma 2
.

If
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are two homogeneous elements of
\mathcal{P}^{+}
with
\{g_{1},g_{2}\}=0
, then

\overleftarrow{D_{g_{1}}}\circ\overleftarrow{D_{g_{2}}}=(-1)^{|g_{1}||g_{2}|}\overleftarrow{D_{g_{2}}}\circ\overleftarrow{D_{g_{1}}}.

The corresponding result also holds for pairs of elements of
\mathcal{P}^{-}
.
∎

As a consequence of this observation, the action of
\mathcal{P}^{+}
on
S\mathcal{L}
defined above gives rise to an action of
\overline{S}\mathcal{A}^{+}
on
S\mathcal{L}
, given by

(c_{1}\odot\dots\odot c_{n})\overleftarrow{D}_{w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{k}}:=(c_{1}\odot\dots\odot c_{n})\overleftarrow{D_{w_{1}}}\circ\dots\circ\overleftarrow{D_{w_{k}}}.

Remark
.

The total number of brackets taken for
\overleftarrow{D}_{w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{k}}
corresponds to the total word length in the
q^{+}
-variables of the words
w_{i}
.

Given an element
{\mathbf{f}}\in\mathcal{L}
of degree
2N
, we now consider the expression

e^{\mathbf{f}}=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\underbrace{{\mathbf{f}}\odot\dots\odot{\mathbf{f}}}_{k\text{ times}},

where
1\in\Lambda\cong S^{0}\mathcal{L}
is the unit of our ground ring.

Remark
.

As an infinite sum, this is technically not an element of
S\mathcal{L}
, but rather
lives in a completion of
S\mathcal{L}
with respect to the word length filtration.
The coderivations
\overrightarrow{D_{g^{r}}}
and
\overleftarrow{D_{g_{s}}}
introduced above
still make sense on this completion. As we also use other completions later on, we choose not to overburden the notation and will write things like
e^{\mathbf{f}}\in S\mathcal{L}
without explicitly mentioning completions.

One easily verifies that for
g^{r}\in\mathcal{P}^{-}
of degree
r
in
p^{-}
an
g_{s}\in\mathcal{P}^{+}
of degree
s
in
q^{+}
we have

\overrightarrow{D_{g^{r}}}(e^{\mathbf{f}})=\overrightarrow{g^{r}}(\frac{1}{r!}{\mathbf{f}}^{\odot r})\odot e^{\mathbf{f}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad(e^{\mathbf{f}})\overleftarrow{D_{g_{s}}}=e^{\mathbf{f}}\odot(\frac{1}{s!}{\mathbf{f}}^{\odot s})\overleftarrow{g_{s}}.

Remark
.

As we assume that
{\mathbf{f}}
has even degree, the position of the
e^{\mathbf{f}}
-factor is rather cosmetic.

Note that we abuse notation slightly here, as both
\overrightarrow{D_{g^{r}}}(e^{\mathbf{f}})
and
(e^{\mathbf{f}})\overleftarrow{D_{g_{s}}}
are elements of
\overline{S}\mathcal{L}\subset S\mathcal{L}
, with leading terms
\overrightarrow{g^{r}}(\frac{1}{r!}{\mathbf{f}}^{\odot r})
and
(\frac{1}{s!}{\mathbf{f}}^{\odot s})\overleftarrow{g_{s}}
in
S^{1}\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}
, respectively.

Remark
.

The terms
\overrightarrow{g^{r}}(\frac{1}{r!}{\mathbf{f}}^{\odot r})
and
(\frac{1}{s!}{\mathbf{f}}^{\odot s})\overleftarrow{g_{s}}
have the following alternative description, which helps to establish the relationship to the SFT formalism as formulated in
[
1
]
.
Consider a summand
w\cdot w^{\prime}
in
g^{r}
, where
w
is a word in
q^{-}
and
w^{\prime}=p_{\gamma_{1}}^{-}\cdots p_{\gamma_{r}}^{-}
is a word of length
r
in
p^{-}
. Then

\displaystyle\overrightarrow{ww^{\prime}}(\frac{1}{r!}{\mathbf{f}}^{\odot r})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r!}w\cdot\{\dots\{w^{\prime},{\mathbf{f}}\},\dots\},{\mathbf{f}}\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r!}w\cdot\sum_{\sigma\in S_{r}}\kappa_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots\kappa_{\gamma_{r}}\frac{\partial{\mathbf{f}}}{\partial p_{\gamma_{1}}}\cdots\frac{\partial{\mathbf{f}}}{\partial p_{\gamma_{r}}}

\displaystyle=w\cdot\kappa_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots\kappa_{\gamma_{r}}\frac{\partial{\mathbf{f}}}{\partial p_{\gamma_{1}}}\cdots\frac{\partial{\mathbf{f}}}{\partial p_{\gamma_{r}}}.

So

\overrightarrow{g^{r}}(\frac{1}{r!}{\mathbf{f}}^{\odot r})=g^{r}|_{L_{\mathbf{f}}},

where
L_{\mathbf{f}}\subseteq T^{*}C^{-}\oplus T^{*}(C^{+})^{*}
is the “Lagrangian” graph of
d{\mathbf{f}}
, where
{\mathbf{f}}
is viewed as a function of the variables
p^{+}
and
q^{-}
. It is given by the equations

L_{\mathbf{f}}:=\left\{p^{-}_{\gamma}=\kappa_{\gamma}\frac{\partial{\mathbf{f}}}{\partial q^{-}_{\gamma}}\,,\,q^{+}_{\gamma}=\kappa_{\gamma}\frac{\partial{\mathbf{f}}}{\partial p^{+}_{\gamma}}\right\}.

Similarly, we have

(\frac{1}{s!}{\mathbf{f}}^{\odot s})\overleftarrow{g_{s}}=g_{s}|_{L_{\mathbf{f}}}.

Lemma 3
.

Any
{\mathbf{f}}\in\overline{\mathcal{L}}
of degree
2N
gives rise to a morphism of coalgebras
\Phi:\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{+}[2N])\to\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{-}[2N])
of degree
0
satisfying
\Phi({{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}})={{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}}
, given on homogeneous generators by the formula

\Phi(w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{r})=\left((e^{\mathbf{f}})\overleftarrow{D}_{w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{r}}\right)_{|p^{+}=0}.

Remarks on the proof.

In this setting,
\Phi
is a morphism of coalgebras if and only if it can be written as
\Phi=e^{\phi}
for a map
\phi:\overline{S}\mathcal{A}^{+}\to S^{1}\mathcal{A}^{-}
. We claim that the component
\phi^{r}:S^{r}\mathcal{A}^{+}\to\mathcal{A}^{-}
of
\phi
of arity
r
can be explicitly described: On input words
w_{1}
, …,
w_{r}
from
\mathcal{A}^{+}
of total word length
\tau
it is given by

\phi^{r}(w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{r})=\frac{1}{(\tau-r+1)!}\left({\mathbf{f}}^{\odot(\tau-r+1)}\overleftarrow{D}_{w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{r}}\right)_{|p^{+}=0}.

(2)

Indeed, consider a typical summand

\frac{1}{n!}\left({\mathbf{f}}^{\odot n}\overleftarrow{D}_{w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{r}}\right)_{|p^{+}=0}

in the definition of
\Phi
. This is itself a sum of various iterated bracket expressions. To each term contributing to such an iterated bracket we can associate a graph with
r
vertices labelled by the words
w_{i}
and
n
vertices labelled by
{\mathbf{f}}
, which describes the pattern in which the iterated brackets connect the pieces. We can think of this graph as being build up gradually, where each new bracket connects two previously unconnected components to each other by a single edge. In particular, all connected components stay trees at all times. As we are taking exactly
\tau
Poisson brackets in this situation, the final graph has Euler characteristic
r+n-\tau
. In order for the result to land in
\mathcal{A}^{-}
, this graph should be a single tree, so we need
n=\tau-r+1
.

From (
2
), we can now also conclude that
\phi^{r}:S^{r}\mathcal{A}^{+}\to S^{1}\mathcal{A}^{-}
is homogeneous of degree
(1-r)2N
, so it is of degree
0
after the shift.

Finally, as
{\mathbf{f}}\in\overline{\mathcal{L}}
, we have

\left((e^{\mathbf{f}})\overleftarrow{D}_{{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}}\right)_{|p^{+}=0}=\left(e^{\mathbf{f}}\odot{{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}}\right)_{|p^{+}=0}={{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}}.

∎

Remark
.

The relationship between iterated bracket expressions and graphs used in the above discussion is slightly more subtle than it may appear at first. Indeed, suppose
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
are two words of word length 2 and
w_{3}
is of word length 1 in
\mathcal{A}
. An expression like

\{{\mathbf{f}}^{1},\{\{{\mathbf{f}}^{2},\{\{{\mathbf{f}}^{2},w_{1}\},w_{2}\}\},w_{3}\}\}

which occurs as a summand in

is then made up of two contributions whose corresponding graphs differ. Indeed, in one summand

interacts with both

Example 4
.

For
\mathcal{A}^{+}=\mathcal{A}^{-}=\mathcal{A}
, we have an isomorphism of algebras
\mathcal{L}\cong\mathcal{P}
. Note however that the actions of elements from
\mathcal{P}
via (iterated) Poisson brackets on
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{L}
will differ. To keep this point clear, we will use variables
p^{+}
and
q^{-}
when thinking in terms of
\mathcal{L}
, and variables
p
and
q
with the same index set for
\mathcal{P}
. The identity morphism
\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}[2N])\to\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}[2N])
corresponds to the element

{\mathbf{i}}:=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{1}{\kappa_{\gamma}}q_{\gamma}^{-}p_{\gamma}^{+}\in\overline{\mathcal{L}}.

Now suppose we are given two elements
{\mathbf{h}}^{\pm}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}
or degree
2N-1
satisfying

\{{\mathbf{h}}^{\pm},{\mathbf{h}}^{\pm}\}=0.

As explained in the previous subsection, they give rise to codifferentials
D^{\pm}:\overline{S}\mathcal{A}^{\pm}[2N]\to\overline{S}\mathcal{A}^{\pm}[2N]
of degree
-1
.
They also give rise to codifferentials

\overrightarrow{D^{-}}:=\overrightarrow{D_{{\mathbf{h}}^{-}}}:S\mathcal{L}\to S\mathcal{L}\qquad\text{and}\qquad S\mathcal{L}\leftarrow S\mathcal{L}:\overleftarrow{D^{+}}:=\overleftarrow{D_{{\mathbf{h}}^{+}}},

extending

\overrightarrow{{\mathbf{h}}^{-}}:S\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{L}\leftarrow S\mathcal{L}:\overleftarrow{{\mathbf{h}}^{+}}.

Lemma 5
.

Suppose
w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\in\mathcal{A}^{+}
,
{\mathbf{h}}^{\pm}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}
are as above, and
{\mathbf{f}}\in\overline{\mathcal{L}}
is of degree
2N
. Then

\left(e^{\mathbf{f}}\overleftarrow{D}_{D^{+}(w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{k})}\right)|_{p^{+}=0}=\left(\left(e^{\mathbf{f}}\overleftarrow{D^{+}}\right)\overleftarrow{D}_{w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{k}}\right)|_{p^{+}=0}

and

D^{-}\left(e^{\mathbf{f}}\overleftarrow{D}_{w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{k}}|_{p^{+}=0}\right)=\left(\left(\overrightarrow{D^{-}}e^{\mathbf{f}}\right)\overleftarrow{D}_{w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{k}}\right)|_{p^{+}=0}.\hfill\qed

It follows that in order for the coalgebra map
\Phi:S(\mathcal{A}^{+}[2N])\to S(\mathcal{A}^{-}[2N])
associated to an element
{\mathbf{f}}\in\overline{\mathcal{L}}
of degree
2N
as above to commute with the codifferentials
D^{\pm}
determined by
{\mathbf{h}}^{\pm}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}
, we must have

\overrightarrow{D^{-}}e^{\mathbf{f}}=e^{\mathbf{f}}\overleftarrow{D^{+}}.

By an earlier remark, we have

\overrightarrow{D^{-}}e^{\mathbf{f}}={\mathbf{h}}^{-}|_{L_{\mathbf{f}}}\odot e^{\mathbf{f}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad e^{\mathbf{f}}\overleftarrow{D^{+}}=e^{\mathbf{f}}\odot{\mathbf{h}}^{+}|_{L_{\mathbf{f}}}.

So we find

Lemma 6
.

Suppose
{\mathbf{h}}^{\pm}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}^{\pm}
are Poisson self-commuting elements of degree
2N-1
, and
{\mathbf{f}}\in\overline{\mathcal{L}}
is an element of degree
2N
. Then the coalgebra map
\Phi:\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{+}[2N])\to\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{-}[2N])
associated to
{\mathbf{f}}
intertwines the codifferentials
D^{\pm}
induced by
{\mathbf{h}}^{\pm}
if and only if

{\mathbf{h}}^{-}|_{L_{\mathbf{f}}}={\mathbf{h}}^{+}|_{L_{\mathbf{f}}}.\hfill{\qed}

In particular, under the condition formulated in the lemma,
\Phi
can be interpreted as an
L_{\infty}
-morphism between the
L_{\infty}
algebra structures on
\mathcal{A}^{\pm}[2N+1]
induced by
D^{\pm}
.

2.3
Composition of morphisms

Suppose we have three structures
(\mathcal{A}^{+},{\mathbf{h}}^{+})
,
(\mathcal{A},{\mathbf{h}})
and
(\mathcal{A}^{-},{\mathbf{h}}^{-})
as above, and consider
\mathcal{L}^{+}:=SC\otimes S(C^{+}[-2N])^{*}
and
\mathcal{L}^{-}:=SC^{-}\otimes S(C[-2N])^{*}

, elements
{\mathbf{f}}^{+}\in\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{+}
and
{\mathbf{f}}^{-}\in\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{-}
define morphisms of coalgebras

\Phi^{+}:\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{+}[2N])\to\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}[2N])\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Phi^{-}:\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}[2N])\to\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{-}[2N]).

Our next lemma gives a description of the composition
\Phi:=\Phi^{-}\circ\Phi^{+}
. To this end, let
\mathcal{L}:=SC^{-}\otimes S(C^{+}[-2N])^{*}
, and denote by

the submanifold given by the equations

p_{\gamma}=\kappa_{\gamma}\frac{\partial{\mathbf{f}}^{+}}{\partial q_{\gamma}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad q_{\gamma}=\kappa_{\gamma}\frac{\partial{\mathbf{f}}^{-}}{\partial p_{\gamma}},

where
\gamma
runs over the index set
\Gamma
associated to

.

Given
{\mathbf{f}}^{+}\in\mathcal{L}^{+}
and
{\mathbf{f}}^{-}\in\mathcal{L}^{-}
, we now follow
[
1
]
and define
{\mathbf{f}}\in\mathcal{L}
as

{\mathbf{f}}(p^{+},q^{-}):=\left({\mathbf{f}}^{+}(p^{+},q)+{\mathbf{f}}^{-}(p,q^{-})-\sum_{\gamma}\frac{1}{\kappa_{\gamma}}q_{\gamma}p_{\gamma}\right)_{|L},

(3)

where
L
is as above.

For what follows, it is useful to understand the mechanics of this expression. We start by considering the first term. Restricting
{\mathbf{f}}^{+}
to
L
means replacing each occurence of a
q
-variable by a corresponding derivative of
{\mathbf{f}}^{-}
. As we have argued in the previous subsection in similar circumstances, this has the same effect as taking
r
brackets with
{\mathbf{f}}^{-}
. That in turn might introduce “free” instances of the variables
p
. Replacing these corresponds to taking new iterated brackets with
{\mathbf{f}}^{+}
again. This continues until no new
p
s or
qs
have been introduced. The combinatorics of these iterated replacements can be captured by a tree with vertices labelled by
{\mathbf{f}}^{+}
and
{\mathbf{f}}^{-}
, and an edge for each instance of a bracket “connecting” two such terms. If such a tree has
r
vertices labelled with
{\mathbf{f}}^{+}
, then it appears
r
times in
{\mathbf{f}}^{+}(p^{+},q)_{|L}
, as it can be built starting from any of these vertices. Similarly, if the tree has
n
vertices labelled
{\mathbf{f}}^{-}
, then it appears
n
times in
{\mathbf{f}}^{-}(p,q^{-})_{|L}
. Finally, the tree can also be built starting from each of its
\tau
edges, so it will appear exactly
\tau
times in the last term. In summary, each such tree
T
is counted in the expression (
3
) exactly
n+r-\tau=\chi(T)=1
time.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\Phi^{\pm}
be morphisms as above associated to elements
{\mathbf{f}}^{\pm}\in\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{\pm}
. Then the composition
\Phi:=\Phi^{-}\circ\Phi^{+}
is associated to the element
{\mathbf{f}}\in\overline{\mathcal{L}}
given by (
3
).

Sketch of proof.

From the definitions, we find that

\displaystyle\Phi^{-}(\Phi^{+}(w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{k}))

\displaystyle=\Phi^{-}\left(\left((e^{{\mathbf{f}}^{+}})\overleftarrow{D}_{w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{r}}\right)_{|p^{+}=0}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(e^{{\mathbf{f}}^{-}}\right)\overleftarrow{D}_{\left((e^{{\mathbf{f}}^{+}})\overleftarrow{D}_{w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{r}}\right)_{|p^{+}=0}}\right)_{|p=0}.

With an obvious extension of our previous notations, one checks that this can be rewritten as

\left(\left(\left(e^{{\mathbf{f}}^{-}}\right)\overleftarrow{D}_{e^{{\mathbf{f}}^{+}}}\right)_{|p=0}\overleftarrow{D}_{w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{r}}\right)_{|p^{+}=0}.

Hence it remains to understand the term
\left(\left(e^{{\mathbf{f}}^{-}}\right)\overleftarrow{D}_{e^{{\mathbf{f}}^{+}}}\right)_{|p=0}
. As in the outlined proof of Lemma
3
, a typical term

\left(\frac{1}{n!}({\mathbf{f}}^{-})^{\odot n}\right)\overleftarrow{D}_{\frac{1}{r!}({\mathbf{f}}^{+})^{\odot r}}

corresponds to iterated brackets whose combinatorics can be described by a union of trees with
n
vertices labelled by
{\mathbf{f}}^{-}
and
r
vertices labelled by
{\mathbf{f}}^{+}
, and whose
\tau
edges each correspond to a bracket where two of the terms interact. In order to find
{\mathbf{f}}
, we need to find the connected building blocks, i.e. those summands where the underlying graph is a tree. As we have argued just before the statement of the lemma, the expression (
3
) precisel captures the sum of all such trees (with varying
n
,
r
and
\tau

∎

We will now consider the special case

4

{\mathbf{f}}\in\overline{\mathcal{L}}_{0}\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{L}}
satisfying
{\mathbf{h}}^{+}|_{L_{\mathbf{f}}}=0

.

3
Relation to the SFT formalism from
[
1
]

Here we very briefly review the relevant formalism from the foundational paper by Eliashberg, Givental and Hofer
[
1
]
. We do assume that the reader is familiar with most of the content, and just highlight the relevant points for our discussion of
rational SFT
here.

Given a contact manifold
(V^{2n-1},\alpha)
with all periodic orbits
non-degenerate, the formalism uses the structures introduced in section
2
with the indexing set
\Gamma
consisting of all (good) Reeb orbits
\gamma
. Here the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading exists under favourable circumstances, and comes from the Conley-Zehnder indices of the Reeb orbits via

|q_{\gamma}|=n-3+CZ(\gamma).

The integer
N
introduced above equals
n-3
in this context, so that

|p_{\gamma}|=n-3-CZ(\gamma),

and
\kappa_{\gamma}
is the geometric multiplicity of the Reeb orbit.

There are several options for the coefficient ring:

•

In the simplest situations, one can choose
\Lambda={\mathbb{Q}}
. This works whenever one is interested in invariants of single contact manifolds, or when all considered cobordisms are exact, and when no additional constraints beyond the asymptotics at the punctures are implemented.

•

If one wants to include the discussion of non-exact cobordisms, one can instead use
\Lambda=\Lambda_{\mathrm{Nov}}
, the universal Novikov ring

\Lambda_{\mathrm{Nov}}:=\left\{\left.\sum_{i}c_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}\,\right|\,c_{i}\in{\mathbb{Q}},\,a_{i}\geq 0,\,\lim_{i\to\infty}a_{i}=\infty\right\}.

It is filtered by the minimal exponent of a term with non-zero coefficient.

•

In the most general setting, one can use for
\Lambda
a graded polynomial ring in (potentially infinitely many) variables
t_{i}
, which correspond to possible point constraints (and the degree of
t_{i}
corresponds to the codimension of the constraint) over the universal Novikov ring
\Lambda_{\mathrm{Nov}}
. This is not exactly what is done in
[
1
]
, where they use the Novikov completion of
{\mathbb{Z}}[H_{2}(V;{\mathbb{Z}})]
, but our version just amounts to a convenient repackaging of the information, and allows us to treat all contact manifolds and cobordisms over the same coefficients (for the constraints, “same” has to be suitably interpreted).

Remark
.

We use
point constraint
as a generic term for any constraint on a map which is imposed at a single point of the domain. This encompasses all of the different constraints Siegel
[
7
]
and Moreno and Zhou
[
6
]
use, if one interprets Siegel’s higher order self-contact constraints in terms of maps to blow ups of the target symplectic manifold as in
[
7
, § 5.3]
.

To a contact manifold as above,
rational SFT
associates an element

{\mathbf{h}}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}
of degree
2N-1
, where the coefficient of any monomial in the
q_{\gamma}
,
p_{\gamma}
and
t_{i}
is the count of rigid rational holomorphic curves of finite Hofer energy in
{\mathbb{R}}\times V
with respect to a cylindrical almost complex structure
J
and with positive asymptotics corresponding to the
p
-variables, negative asymptotics corresponding to the
q
-variables, and subject to constraints at (distinct) interior marked points corresponding to each of the
t_{i}
. This count will typically depend on the auxillary choices, such as the almost complex structure and the choice of virtual fundamental chain technique needed to deal with failure of regularity.
The compactness, transversality and gluing results give a description of the boundaries of moduli spaces rational holomorphic curves of expected dimension 1. The algebraic consequence of this description for the generating function
{\mathbf{h}}
has a very concise form:

Theorem 13
.

[
1
, Lemma 2.2.6]

The rational Hamiltonian
{\mathbf{h}}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}
satisfies

\{{\mathbf{h}},{\mathbf{h}}\}=0.

(9)

Remark
.

For later use, we point out that equation (
9
) can be expanded in the constraints, so that each monomial in the
t_{i}
yields a separate equation. Temporarily denote by
{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime}
the part of
{\mathbf{h}}
independent of
t
(i.e. without point constraints), and by
{\mathbf{h}}(t_{1})
the part of
{\mathbf{h}}

linear
in a single constraint

. Then we have

\{{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime},{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime}\}=0\quad\text{and}\quad 2\{{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime},{\mathbf{h}}(t_{1})\}=\{{\mathbf{h}}(t_{1}),{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime}\}+\{{\mathbf{h}}^{\prime},{\mathbf{h}}(t_{1})\}=0.

This will be used in §
5
below.

Next suppose
W
is a compact symplectic cobordism with positive end
(V^{+},\alpha^{+})
and negative end
(V^{-},\alpha^{-})
. Associated to the two ends we have
{\mathbf{h}}^{\pm}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}^{\pm}}
. The counts of rigid rational holomorphic curves associated to choices of almost-complex structure and perturbation data suitably consistent with the choices at the ends can be packaged into an element
{\mathbf{f}}\in\mathcal{L}
of degree
2N
. In this situation, the analysis of compactifications of moduli spaces of rational holomorphic curves in the completion of

of expected dimension 1 yields

Theorem 14
.

[
1

The rational potential
{\mathbf{f}}\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{comp}}
of a cobordism

and the rational Hamiltonians
{\mathbf{h}}^{\pm}
of the symplectizations of the two ends are related by

{\mathbf{h}}^{-}|_{L_{\mathbf{f}}}={\mathbf{h}}^{+}|_{L_{\mathbf{f}}}.

(10)

Remark
.

•

When the cobordism

is exact, one does not need to complete and we have

(since for energy reasons every holomorphic curve in

must have at least one positive end). In particular,

(the part of the potential without constraints) will give rise to a morphism

\Phi:(\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{+}[2N]),D^{+})\to(\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{-}[2N],D^{-})

as in §

•

When the cobordism

and interpreting

to construct the corresponding morphism

\Phi:(\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{+,\mathrm{comp}}[2N]),D^{+})\to(\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{-,\mathrm{comp}}[2N],D^{-,{\mathbf{a}}}).

We stress again that (

1

for some more details on this). Most relevant to us are two particular situations:

•

In §

with empty negative end. Algebraically, this has the effect that the left hand side of (

, but not in

. Expanding (

\left(e^{{\mathbf{f}}}(T)\right)\overleftarrow{D^{+}}=0

(11)

for each monomial

in the constraints

denotes the part of

the part of

corresponding to curves without constraints, and by

the part of

corresponding to a given monomial

\displaystyle e^{\mathbf{f}}(t_{1})

\displaystyle={\mathbf{f}}(t_{1})\odot e^{{\mathbf{f}}^{\prime}},

\displaystyle e^{\mathbf{f}}(t_{1}t_{2})

\displaystyle=\left({\mathbf{f}}(t_{1}t_{2})+{\mathbf{f}}(t_{1})\odot{\mathbf{f}}(t_{2})\right)\odot e^{{\mathbf{f}}^{\prime}}\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle e^{\mathbf{f}}(t_{1}^{2})

from which the general pattern should be clear. For each

is the product of

•

In §

4
The morphisms used by Siegel

With the language developed above, it is now fairly straightforward to describe the algebraic nature of the morphisms that Siegel uses to define his capacities.

Siegel works in the setting of a contact manifold
(V^{+},\alpha^{+})
with a symplectic filling
(W,\omega)
. As already mentioned in our summary of rational SFT, he considers holomorphic curves with (various types of) constraints at interior points of
W
, so formally we are in the setting of (
11
). One possible interpretation of this equation is that for each monomial
T
in the constraints we have a map

\Phi(T):\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{+}[2N])\to\overline{S}\Lambda,\qquad\Phi(T)(w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{r})=\left(\left(e^{\mathbf{f}}(T)\right)\overleftarrow{D}_{w_{1}\odot\dots\odot w_{r}}\right)|_{p=0}

whose degree depends on the degree of
T
, but which vanishes on the image of
D^{+}
. Note that
\Phi(T)
is
not
a morphism of coalgebras unless
T=0
, so for
T\neq 0
these maps are
not
augmentations. Nonetheless, as they vanish on
{\rm im\,}D^{+}\subseteq\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{+}[2N])
, they do descend to well-defined maps on
H_{*}(\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{+}[2N],D^{+})
.

In the restricted setting of studying a contact manifold
(V^{+},\alpha^{+})
as the boundary of a given compact symplectic domain
(W,\omega)
, the contact form
\alpha^{+}
will be well-defined up to addition of an exact 1-form
df
. This has the effect of giving the whole theory a well-defined filtration by “action”, which is essential in Siegel’s work. Many of his higher capacities are obtained by looking at specific monomials
T
in the constraints and specific elements
b\in S\Lambda
, and looking for the infimum of exponents
a
such that
\lambda^{a}\cdot b
is in the image of
\Phi(T)
.

Remark
.

The well-definedness of these capacity requires enough of the machinery needed to prove invariance of (rational) SFT to be able to conclude that these numbers are indeed independent of any auxillary choices.

An important property of a symplectic capacity is its monotonicity with respect to symplectic embeddings, meaning that whenever there is a symplectic embedding
(W^{-},\omega^{-})\subseteq(W^{+},\omega^{+})
, the inequality
c(W^{-},\omega^{-})\leq c(W^{+},\omega^{+})
should hold. In the situation of interest to us,
(W^{\pm},\omega^{\pm})
are compact Liouville domains with convex boundaries
V^{\pm}
, meaning that the Liouville forms
\lambda^{\pm}
on
W^{\pm}
restrict to (positive) contact forms
\alpha^{\pm}
on
V^{\pm}
. By shrinking
W^{-}
slightly if necessary, we can arrange that
W^{-}\subseteq\operatorname{int}(W^{+})
, so that we get a compact symplectic cobordism
W=W^{+}\setminus W^{-}
with positive end
(V^{+},\alpha^{+})
and negative end
(V^{-},\alpha^{-})
. We assume that the constraints that are used to define our capacity
c
are localized at points in
W^{-}\subseteq W^{+}
. By a neck stretching argument, we may decompose the relevant map
\Phi^{+}(T):\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{+}[2N])\to\overline{S}\Lambda[2N]
as the precomposition of
\Phi^{-}(T):\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{-}[2N])\to\overline{S}\Lambda[2N]
with the cobordism map.

Now there are two cases to consider, depending on whether or not the cobordism
W
is exact (equivalently, whether or not the embedding
W^{-}\hookrightarrow W^{+}
is exact). In the exact case, the potential
{\mathbf{f}}
of the cobordism
W
is an element of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
, and so by Lemma
3
it straightforwardly translates into a morphism
\Phi:\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{+}[2N])\to\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{-}[2N])
. We will then have
\Phi^{+}(T)=\Phi^{-}(T)\circ\Phi
, and so if
w\in\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{+}[2N])
gets mapped to
\lambda^{a}\cdot b\in\overline{S}\Lambda[2N]
by
\Phi^{+}(T)
, then
\Phi(w)
is mapped to the same element under
\Phi^{-}(T)
. This clearly proves monotonicity in this case.

When the cobordism
W
is not exact, we split the potential
{\mathbf{f}}
into

{\mathbf{f}}={\mathbf{f}}^{0}+{\mathbf{f}}^{\prime}
, where
{\mathbf{f}}^{0}
is the part which is independent of the
p^{+}
-variables, and
{\mathbf{f}}^{\prime}\in\overline{\mathcal{L}}
. As described in §
2.5.1
,
{\mathbf{f}}^{0}\in\mathcal{A}^{-,\mathrm{comp}}
will be a Maurer-Cartan element, and
{\mathbf{f}}^{\prime}
will define a morphism
\Phi^{\prime}:\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{+,\mathrm{comp}}[2N]\to\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{-,\mathrm{comp}})
which intertwines the codifferential
D^{+}
induced from
{\mathbf{h}}^{+}
with the codifferential
D^{-,{\mathbf{f}}^{0}}
induced from the twisted Hamiltonian
{\mathbf{h}}^{-,{\mathbf{f}}^{0}}\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}^{-,\mathrm{comp}}
. We then have

\Phi^{+}(T)=\Phi^{-}(T)\circ\Psi^{{\mathbf{f}}^{0}}\circ\Phi^{\prime},

where
\Psi^{{\mathbf{f}}^{0}}:\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{-,\mathrm{comp}}[2N]\to\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{-,\mathrm{comp}}[2N])
is the multiplication with
e^{{\mathbf{f}}^{0}}
already considered in §
2.4
. In particular, if
w\in\overline{S}(\mathcal{A}^{+}[2N])
gets mapped to
\lambda^{a}\cdot b\in\overline{S}\Lambda[2N]
by
\Phi^{+}(T)
, then
e^{{\mathbf{f}}^{0}}\odot\Phi^{\prime}(w)
is mapped to the same element under
\Phi^{-}(T)
. As
e^{{\mathbf{f}}^{0}}
only makes sense in the completed setting, this proves monotonicity for general symplectic embeddings under the assumption that one uses completed complexes in the definition of the capacities.

In the special case that one considers a single point constraint
T=t_{1}
, there is also a linearized version, which looks at the corresponding maps

\phi(t_{1}):\overline{S}C[2N]\to S^{1}\Lambda=\Lambda

on the linearized theory, whose defining components

\phi^{r}(t_{1}):S^{r}C[2N]\to\Lambda

for
r\geq 1
is given (in the notation of the previous section) by the part
{\mathbf{f}}^{r}(t_{1})
of
{\mathbf{f}}(t_{1})
which is of degree
r
in
p^{+}
. The invariance argument then requires the linearized structures mentioned in Lemma
11
.

Let
\mathcal{A}

Definition 15

and

{{\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}\subseteq\mathcal{A}[2N]\cong S^{1}(\mathcal{A}[2N])

.

•

Note that

Remark

One can also consider an alternative invariant defined as

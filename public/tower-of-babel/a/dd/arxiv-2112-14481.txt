Abstract
It was recently demonstrated that super–Schwarzian derivatives can be constructed from the Cartan forms of the super–conformal supergroups O ​ S ​ p ​ ( 1 | 2 ) , S ​ U ​ ( 1 , 1 | 1 ) , O ​ S ​ p ​ ( 3 | 2 ) , S ​ U ​ ( 1 , 1 | 2 ) OSp(1|2),SU(1,1|1),OSp(3|2),SU(1,1|2) . Roughly speaking, the super–Schwarzian is just the component of the corresponding Cartan forms with the lowest dimension. In this paper, we apply the same approach for superalgebra D ⁡ ( 1 , 2 , α ) D(1,2;\alpha) . The minimal set of constraints we used includes: a) introducing new superspace coordinates the Cartan forms depend on, which are completely invariant with respect to the corresponding group; b) nullifying the form for dilatation. In contrast to the S ​ U ​ ( 1 , 1 | 2 ) SU(1,1|2) case, the new super–Schwarzian appears to be a d ​ θ i ​ a d\theta^{ia} component of the form for s ​ u ​ ( 2 ) su(2) automorphism.

IV
{\cal N}=4
Schwarzian action

Like the previously considered cases

- invariant generalization of the bosonic Schwarzian action,

S=-\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau{\cal S}(t,\tau)=-\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau\left(\frac{\dddot{t}}{\dot{t}}-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{\ddot{t}}{\dot{t}}\right)^{2}\right),

could be constructed. As is shown in the Section
3

D(1,2;\alpha)
group transformations, are the super–Schwarzian
\sigma^{ia}
and its derivatives. Therefore, it would be natural to expect that the superfield action is some integral of
\sigma^{ia}
over the part of superspace. Indeed, let us show that the expression

S=-\frac{1}{72}\int d\tau D^{kc}D_{lc}D_{kb}\sigma^{lb}|_{\theta\rightarrow 0}

(4.27)

is invariant with respect to
{\cal N}=4
supersymmetry, realized on superspace coordinates
\tau
and
\theta_{ia}
as

\delta\tau=-{\rm i\,}\epsilon^{ia}\theta_{ia},\;\;\delta\theta_{ia}=\epsilon_{ia},\;\;\delta\triangle\tau=0,\;\;\delta d\theta_{ia}=0.

(4.28)

The active variation of any superfield
f
with respect to transformations (
4.28
) is given by the formula

\delta^{\star}f=-\delta\tau\frac{\partial f}{\partial\tau}-\delta\theta_{ia}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta_{ia}}\equiv-\epsilon_{ia}{\widehat{Q}}{}^{ia}f,\;\;{\widehat{Q}}{}^{ia}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{ia}}-{\rm i\,}\theta^{ia}\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}.

(4.29)

It can be straightforwardly shown that the differential operator
{\widehat{Q}}{}^{ia}
anticommutes, as expected, with the covariant derivative
D^{jb}
and differs from it by the sign of the
\theta\partial_{\tau}
-term. Therefore,

As after applying differential operators on

(4.31)

where the expression for

is a consequence of the constraint (
3.12
).

The supersymmetry invariant integral over
d\tau
can be presented as an integral over part of the superspace:

S=\frac{1}{72}\int d\tau d\theta^{kc}d\theta_{lc}d\theta_{kb}\sigma^{lb}.

(4.32)

(

\sigma^{ia}

\displaystyle S=-\frac{1}{72}\int d\tau D^{kc}D_{lc}D_{kb}\sigma^{lb}|_{\theta\rightarrow 0}=-\int d\tau\left[\frac{1}{2}\alpha(1+\alpha)\left(\frac{\partial_{\tau}\big(\ddot{t}-{\rm i\,}\ddot{\xi}_{ia}\xi^{ia}\big)}{\dot{t}-{\rm i\,}\dot{\xi}_{jb}\xi^{jb}}-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\big(\ddot{t}-{\rm i\,}\ddot{\xi}_{ia}\xi^{ia}\big)^{2}}{\big(\dot{t}-{\rm i\,}\dot{\xi}_{jb}\xi^{jb}\big)^{2}}\right)\right.+

\displaystyle+\left.{\rm i\,}\alpha(1+\alpha)\frac{\dot{\xi}_{ia}{\ddot{\xi}}{}^{ia}}{\dot{t}-{\rm i\,}\dot{\xi}_{jb}\xi^{jb}}-\frac{1}{2}\alpha\partial_{\tau}\big(e^{-v}\big)_{l}^{i}\partial_{\tau}\big(e^{v}\big)_{i}^{l}+\frac{1}{2}(1+\alpha)\partial_{\tau}\big(e^{-\phi}\big)_{d}^{a}\partial_{\tau}\big(e^{\phi}\big)_{a}^{d}\right.-

\displaystyle\left.-{\rm i\,}\alpha(1+\alpha)\frac{\big(e^{-v}\big)_{k}^{j}\partial_{\tau}\big(e^{v}\big)_{j}^{l}\dot{\xi}_{lb}\dot{\xi}{}^{kb}}{\dot{t}-{\rm i\,}\dot{\xi}_{ia}\xi^{ia}}-{\rm i\,}\alpha(1+\alpha)\frac{\big(e^{-\phi}\big)_{c}^{b}\partial_{\tau}\big(e^{\phi}\big)_{b}^{d}\dot{\xi}_{jd}\dot{\xi}{}^{jc}}{\dot{t}-{\rm i\,}\dot{\xi}_{ia}\xi^{ia}}-\frac{{\rm i\,}}{9}\dot{\sigma}{}^{kc}\sigma_{kc}-\frac{1}{9}F^{2}\right].

(4.33)

Here, we denote the first component of each superfield with the same letter, and

Baxter operators in Ruijsenaars hyperbolic system I. Commutativity of Q Q -operators


Abstract
We introduce Baxter Q Q -operators for the quantum Ruijsenaars hyperbolic system. We prove that they represent a commuting family of integral operators and also commute with Macdonald difference operators, which are gauge equivalent to the Ruijsenaars Hamiltonians of the quantum system. The proof of commutativity of the Baxter operators uses a hypergeometric identity on rational functions that generalize Ruijsenaars kernel identities.

Introduction

1.1
Ruijsenaars system

In this paper we develop the theory of Baxter operators for relativistic hyperbolic Ruijsenaars system
[
Ru1
]
. This model is parametrized by three positive constants

\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
(“periods”) and
g
(coupling constant), subject to the relation

0<g<\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}.

(1.1)

The dual coupling constant

g^{\ast}=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-g

(1.2)

is used as well everywhere.

The Ruijsenaars system is governed by commuting symmetric difference operators

H_{r}(\bm{x}_{n},g|\bm{\omega})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n]\\
|I|=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\sh^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}-\imath g\right)}{\sh^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)}\cdot T^{-\imath\omega_{1}}_{I,x}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\sh^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}+\imath g\right)}{\sh^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)}

(1.3)

acting on meromorphic functions of
n
complex variables analytic in the strip

|\operatorname{Im}x_{i}|<\omega_{1}+\varepsilon,\qquad\varepsilon>0.

Here and in what follows we denote tuples of
n
variables as

\bm{x}_{n}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}).

(1.4)

The sum in (
1.3
) is taken over all subsets

I\subset[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}

(1.5)

of cardinality
r
. By
T^{a}_{x_{i}}

we denote shift operators

T^{a}_{x_{i}}:=e^{a\partial_{x_{i}}},\qquad(T^{a}_{x_{i}}f)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i},\ldots,x_{n})=f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i}+a,\ldots,x_{n}),

(1.6)

with
T^{a}_{I,x}
being their product

\displaystyle T^{a}_{I,x}=\prod_{i\in I}e^{a\partial_{x_{i}}}

(1.7)

for any subset
I\subset[n]
.

The Ruijsenaars operators (
1.3
) are closely related to Macdonald operators

M_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g|\bm{\omega})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n]\\
|I|=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\
j\notin I\end{subarray}}\frac{\sh\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}-\imath g\right)}{\sh\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(x_{i}-x_{j}\right)}\cdot T^{-\imath\omega_{1}}_{I,x}.

(1.8)

Namely, denote by
\mu(z|\bm{\omega})
the function

\mu(z|\bm{\omega})=S_{2}(\imath z|\bm{\omega})S_{2}(-\imath z+g^{\ast}|\bm{\omega})

(1.9)

and by
\mu(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{\omega})

the product

\mu(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{\omega})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{n}\mu(x_{i}-x_{j}).

(1.10)

Here
S_{2}(z|\bm{\omega})
is the double sine function, its definition and key properties are given in Appendix
A
. Then

\sqrt{\mu(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{\omega})}\,M_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g|\bm{\omega})\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{\omega})}}=H_{r}(\bm{x}_{n},g|\bm{\omega}).

(1.11)

Note that the function
\mu(\bm{x}_{n}|\bm{\omega})
is non-negative (assuming real constants
g,\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
), since

\displaystyle\mu(x|\bm{\omega})\mu(-x|\bm{\omega})

\displaystyle=S_{2}(\imath x|\bm{\omega})S_{2}(-\imath x|\bm{\omega})S_{2}(\imath x+g^{*}|\bm{\omega})S_{2}(-\imath x+g^{*}|\bm{\omega})

(1.12)

\displaystyle=\bigl|S_{2}(\imath x|\bm{\omega})S_{2}(\imath x+g^{*}|\bm{\omega})\bigr|^{2}.

Ruijsenaars operators are symmetric with respect to the pairing

(\varphi,\psi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(\bm{x}_{n})\bar{\psi}(\bm{x}_{n})d\bm{x}_{n},

while the
Macdonald operators are symmetric with respect to the pairing

(\varphi,\psi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(\bm{x}_{n})\bar{\psi}(\bm{x}_{n})\mu(\bm{x}_{n})d\bm{x}_{n}.

(1.13)

1.2
Kernel function and kernel identities

Unlike the original Ruijseenars’ setting, we do not suppose that the periods
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
and the coupling constant
g
are real positive. Instead, we assume everywhere that all of them are complex numbers with positive real parts

\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{1}>0,\qquad\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{2}>0,\qquad\mathrm{Re}\,g>0.

(1.14)

We also require the condition

0<\mathrm{Re}\,g<\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{1}+\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{2}.

(1.15)

Further we usually fix the periods
\bm{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
and for brevity skip them in the notation, for example we use the symbol
S_{2}(z)
for the double sine function

S_{2}(z):=S_{2}(z|\bm{\omega}).

Denote by
{K}(z)
the following function of a complex variable

{K}(z)=S_{2}^{-1}\left(\imath z+\frac{g^{\ast}}{2}\right)S_{2}^{-1}\left(-\imath z+\frac{g^{\ast}}{2}\right).

(1.16)

In terms of the Ruijsenaars hyperbolic Gamma function

G(z)=S_{2}\left(\imath z+\frac{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}{2}\right)

(1.17)

using reflection formula for the double sine function (
A.6
) it can be written as

{K}(z)=G\left(z-\frac{\imath g}{2}\right)G^{-1}\left(z+\frac{\imath g}{2}\right).

(1.18)

Also let

\bm{z}_{n}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}),\qquad\bm{y}_{n}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}),\qquad z_{i},y_{i}\in\mathbb{C}

be two tuples of
n
complex variables. The Ruijsenaars
kernel function

{K}(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})
is
defined as a product

{K}(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})=\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}{K}(z_{i}-y_{j}).

(1.19)

The kernel function
K(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})
satisfies the relations

\left(M_{r}(\bm{z}_{n};g)-M_{r}(-\bm{y}_{n};g)\right)K(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})=0,\qquad r=1,\dots,n,

(1.20)

that is
K(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{y})
is a zero value eigenfunction for commuting difference operators

M_{r}(\bm{z}_{n};g)-M_{r}(-\bm{y}_{n};g),

see
[
Ru3
]
. The relations (
1.20
) are the corollary of the trigonometric version of
kernel function identity

[
Ru3
]
, valid for any tuples
\bm{z}_{n}
and
\bm{y}_{n}
of
n
complex variables and arbitrary parameter
\alpha
:

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n]\\
|I|=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{i\in I}\left(\prod_{j\in[n]\setminus I}\frac{\sin(z_{i}-z_{j}-\alpha)}{\sin(z_{i}-z_{j})}\prod_{a=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(z_{i}-y_{a}+\alpha)}{\sin(z_{i}-y_{a})}\right)=\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A\subset[n]\\
|A|=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{a\in A}\left(\prod_{b\in[n]\setminus A}\frac{\sin(y_{a}-y_{b}+\alpha)}{\sin(y_{a}-y_{b})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(z_{i}-y_{a}+\alpha)}{\sin(z_{i}-y_{a})}\right).\end{split}

(1.21)

In the following we also use the kernel function with the second argument being a tuple of
n-1
complex variables

{K}(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}{K}(z_{i}-y_{j}).

(1.22)

1.3
Baxter
Q
-operators

Let us introduce the family of Baxter
Q
-operators
Q_{n}(\lambda)
parameterized by
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
as the integral operators

\begin{split}&\left(Q_{n}(\lambda)f\right)(\bm{z}_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Q(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{y}_{n};\lambda)f(\bm{y}_{n})d\bm{y}_{n}\end{split}

(1.23)

with the kernel

Q(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{y}_{n};\lambda)=e^{2\pi\imath\lambda(\underline{\bm{z}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n})}{K}(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})\mu(\bm{y}_{n})\,,\qquad z_{j},y_{j}\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.24)

Here and in what follows we denote the sum of tuple components as

\underline{\bm{z}}_{n}=z_{1}+\ldots+z_{n}.

The
Q
-operator maps functions of
n
real variables to functions of
n
real variables.

The following theorem is a simple consequence of kernel function identities (
1.21
), its proof is given in Section
2
.

Theorem 1
.

Under the condition

0<\mathrm{Re}\,g<\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{2}

(1.25)

the operators
Q_{n}(\lambda)
commute with Macdonald operators
M_{r}(\bm{z}_{n};g|\bm{\omega})

M_{r}(\bm{z}_{n};g)\,Q_{n}(\lambda)=Q_{n}(\lambda)\,M_{r}(\bm{z}_{n};g),\qquad r=1,\ldots,n.

(1.26)

Assume in addition to (
1.14
), (
1.15
) that

\nu_{g}=\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{g}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}>0.

(1.27)

Due to the bounds for the functions
K(y)
and
\mu(y)
(
B.3
) proven in Appendix
B
, the product of two
Q
-operators

Q_{n}(\lambda)\,Q_{n}(\rho)

is a well defined integral operator with the kernel
Q_{n}(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{w}_{n};\lambda,\rho)
given by absolutely convergent integral

Q_{n}(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{w}_{n};\lambda,\rho)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Q(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{y}_{n};\lambda)Q(\bm{y}_{n},\bm{w}_{n};\rho)d\bm{y}_{n}

(1.28)

and the domain that consists of fast decreasing functions
f(\bm{w}_{n})
, see Proposition
5
and remark after it in Appendix
B
. The main result of this paper is the commutativity of Baxter
Q
-operators, its proof is given in Sections
3
and
4
.

Theorem 2
.

Under the conditions (
1.14
), (
1.15
), (
1.27
) Baxter operators commute

Q_{n}(\lambda)\,Q_{n}(\rho)=Q_{n}(\rho)\,Q_{n}(\lambda).

(1.29)

The kernels of the operators in both sides of (
1.29
) are analytic functions of
\lambda,\rho
in the strip

|\operatorname{Im}(\lambda-\rho)|<\mathrm{Re}\,\frac{g}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}.

(1.30)

Remark
. Both sides of the relation (
1.29
) depend analytically on all the parameters
\lambda,\rho,g,\bm{\omega}
in the region of absolute convergence of the integrals.

As it was observed for other integrable systems (see lectures
[
S
]
for review), the classical analog of the Baxter
Q
-operator is a special canonical transformation called Backlund transformation. In the paper
[
KS
]
V. Kuznetsov and E. Sklyanin proposed a general scheme that relates the kernel of a
Q
-operator and generating function of the corresponding Backlund transformation. In the work
[
HR1
]
M. Hallnäs and S. Ruijsenaars showed that in the certain classical limit the kernel and measure functions
K(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{y}_{n}),\mu(\bm{y}_{n})
contained in the
Q
-operator kernel (
1.24
) give rise to the Backlund transformation for the classical Ruijsenaars system. In this context the
Q
-operator we defined is a quantum counterpart of this transformation.

We also note that for the case of two particles
n=2
(and real constants
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},g
) such an operator first implicitly appeared in the work
[
HR5
]
. Moreover, the commutativity of
Q
-operators in this particular case also follows from the results of
[
HR5
]
.

1.4
Hypergeometric identities

The proof of Theorem
2
consists of residue calculation of the integrals (
1.28
). This includes the proof of cancellation of higher order poles and the equality of sums of ordinary poles. The latter is equivalent to certain identity for basic hypergeometric series, which resembles duality transformation theorem for multiple hypergeometric series by Y. Kajihara and M. Noumi, see
[
KN
]
.

Let
q
and
t
be formal variables. Denote by
(z;q)_{k}
the
q
-analog of the Pochhammer symbol,

(z;q)_{k}=(1-z)(1-qz)\cdots(1-q^{k-1}z).

(1.31)

Let
\bm{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})
and
\bm{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
be two tuples of
n
variables.

Theorem 3
.

For any integer
K
we have the following equality of rational functions

\begin{split}&\sum_{|\bm{k}|=K}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(qt;q)_{k_{i}}}{(q;q)_{k_{i}}}\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(t^{-1}q^{-k_{j}}u_{i}/u_{j};q)_{k_{i}}}{(q^{-k_{j}}u_{i}/u_{j};q)_{k_{i}}}\times\prod_{a,j=1}^{n}\frac{(tu_{j}/v_{a};q)_{k_{j}}}{(u_{j}/v_{a};q)_{k_{j}}}=\\
&\sum_{|\bm{k}|=K}\prod_{a=1}^{n}\frac{(qt;q)_{k_{a}}}{(q;q)_{k_{a}}}\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b=1\\
a\not=b\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(t^{-1}q^{-k_{a}}v_{a}/v_{b};q)_{k_{b}}}{(q^{-k_{a}}v_{a}/v_{b};q)_{k_{b}}}\times\prod_{a,j=1}^{n}\frac{(tu_{j}/v_{a};q)_{k_{a}}}{(u_{j}/v_{a};q)_{k_{a}}}.\end{split}

(1.32)

Here the sum on both sides of the equality is taken over
n
tuples of non-negative integers with total sum equal to
K

\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}),\qquad k_{i}\geq 0,\qquad k_{1}+\ldots+k_{n}=K.

(1.33)

Note that the kernel function identity (
1.21
) is a particular limit of the hypergeometric identity (
1.32
), see
[
BDKK2
]
for details.

After our work was completed, O. Warnaar and H. Rosengren communicated to us that an elliptic analog of this identity was proven by different methods in the papers
[
LSW
, Corollary 4.3]
,
[
HLNR
, eq. (6.7)]
.

Further results

Denote by
\Lambda_{n}(\lambda)
the integral operator

\displaystyle\left(\Lambda_{n}(\lambda)f\right)(\bm{x}_{n})

\displaystyle=d_{n-1}(g)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\Lambda(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1};\lambda)f(\bm{y}_{n-1})d\bm{y}_{n-1}

(1.34)

with the kernel

\Lambda(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1};\lambda)=e^{2\pi\imath\lambda(\underline{\bm{x}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n-1})}{K}(\bm{x}_{n},\bm{y}_{n-1})\mu(\bm{y}_{n-1})

(1.35)

and the constant

d_{n-1}(g)=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\left[\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}S_{2}(g)\right]^{-n+1}.

(1.36)

The operator
\Lambda_{n}(\lambda)
maps functions of
n-1

n
real variables. M. Hallnäs and S. Ruijsenaars
[

\bm{\omega}
under the condition

\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n};g|\bm{\omega})=\Lambda_{n}(\lambda_{n})\,\Lambda_{n-1}(\lambda_{n-1})\,\cdots\,\Lambda_{2}(\lambda_{2})\,e^{2\pi\imath\lambda_{1}x_{1}}

(1.38)

is given by absolutely convergent integral and represents the joint eigenfunction of Macdonald operators

M_{r}(\bm{x}_{n};g)\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n};g)=e_{r}\bigl(e^{{2\pi\lambda_{1}\omega_{1}}},\dots,e^{{2\pi\lambda_{n}\omega_{1}}}\bigr)\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n};g),\qquad r=1,\dots,n.

(1.39)

Here
e_{r}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
is
r
-th elementary symmetric function,

e_{r}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{r}\leq n}z_{i_{1}}\cdots z_{i_{r}}.

In the next paper
[

) can be obtained in the certain limit from Baxter

-operators, so that the commutativity of

-operators imply commutation relations between
\Lambda
-operators and
Q
-operators, and between

-operators themselves. These relations allow to derive important properties of the eigenfunction. In particular, we show that the eigenfunction (

\Psi_{{\bm{\lambda}}_{n}}(\bm{x}_{n};g|\bm{\omega})=\Psi_{\bm{x}_{n}}({\bm{\lambda}}_{n},\hat{g}^{*}|\hat{\bm{\omega}}),

(1.40)

and consequently admits another iterative integral representation given by Mellin-Barnes type of integrals over spectral parameters
\lambda_{j}
. Here we denoted

\hat{a}=\frac{a}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}

(1.41)

for any

, so that

(1.42)

2.

is symmetric function of the coordinates
x_{j}
, as well as of the spectral variables
\lambda_{j}
;

3.

is an eigenfunction of the Baxter

-operator with the eigenvalue

\prod_{j=1}^{n}\hat{K}(\lambda-\lambda_{j})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(\imath(\lambda-\lambda_{j})+\frac{\hat{g}}{2}\Big|\hat{\bm{\omega}}\Bigr)\,S_{2}^{-1}\Bigl(-\imath(\lambda-\lambda_{j})+\frac{\hat{g}}{2}\Big|\hat{\bm{\omega}}\Bigr);

(1.43)

is a solution of bispectral problem

and

2
Baxter and Macdonald operators commute

Theorem
1
follows from the kernel function identity and from the invariance of the measure
\mu(\bm{x}_{n})d\bm{x}_{n}
with respect to the Macdonald operators.

We present here two proofs of this theorem. Both work for complex periods
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
and coupling constant
g
. The first prove is direct analytical. The second one is its short algebraic reformulation. It exploits symmetry properties of the Macdonald operators with respect to symmetric bilinear pairing (
2.22
) in a way analogous to

[
M
, Chapter VI, §9]
. We describe here both proofs since the first one allows to visualize the appearing restriction on the coupling constant
g
, while the second outlines the responsible algebraic properties.

I. Direct proof
.
Let
\Phi(\bm{z}_{n})
be a function of
n
complex variables
\bm{z}_{n}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
analytic in a strip

\Pi_{\varepsilon}\colon\quad-\varepsilon-\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{1}<\operatorname{Im}z_{i}<\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{1}+\varepsilon.

(2.1)

We are going to prove the equality

M_{r}(\bm{z}_{n};g)\,Q_{n}(\lambda)\,\Phi(\bm{z}_{n})=Q_{n}(\lambda)\,M_{r}(\bm{z}_{n};g)\,\Phi(\bm{z}_{n}),\qquad r=1,\ldots,n.

(2.2)

Explicitly it looks as

\begin{split}M_{r}(\bm{z}_{n};g)\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mu(\bm{y}_{n})\,K(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})\,e^{2\pi\imath\lambda(\underline{\bm{z}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n})}\,\Phi(\bm{y}_{n})\,d\bm{y}_{n}=\\
\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mu(\bm{y}_{n})\,K(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})\,e^{2\pi\imath\lambda(\underline{\bm{z}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n})}\,M_{r}(\bm{y}_{n};g)\,\Phi(\bm{y}_{n})\,d\bm{y}_{n}.\end{split}

(2.3)

Here we assume convergence of the corresponding integrals and
\bm{z}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Note the important property of the integration contour
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in the integral (
2.3
):
it separates two series of poles of the kernel function

\imath y_{i}=\imath z_{j}+\frac{g^{\ast}}{2}+m\omega_{1}+k\omega_{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\imath y_{i}=\imath z_{k}-\frac{g^{\ast}}{2}-m\omega_{1}-k\omega_{2},\qquad\ m,k\geq 0,

(2.4)

and two series of poles of the measure function

\imath y_{i}=\imath y_{j}+g+m\omega_{1}+k\omega_{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\imath y_{i}=\imath y_{k}-g-m\omega_{1}-k\omega_{2},\qquad\ m,k\geq 0,

(2.5)

see (
A.11
), (
A.12
) for the poles and zeros of the double sine function.

The left hand side of (
2.2
) looks as

M_{r}(\bm{z}_{n};g)\,Q_{n}(\lambda)\,\Phi(\bm{z}_{n})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n]\\
|I|=r\end{subarray}}\;\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I\\[2.0pt]
j\in[n]\setminus I\end{subarray}}\frac{\sh\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(z_{i}-z_{j}-\imath g\right)}{\sh\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(z_{i}-z_{j}\right)}\cdot T^{-\imath\omega_{1}}_{I,z}Q_{n}(\lambda)\Phi(\bm{z}_{n}),

(2.6)

where the shift operator
T^{-\imath\omega_{1}}_{I,z}
is defined in (
1.7
).
Consider the summand corresponding to subset

I=\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{r}\}
. Denote this summand by
J_{I}
:

J_{I}=\frac{\sh\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(z_{i}-z_{j}-\imath g\right)}{\sh\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}\left(z_{i}-z_{j}\right)}\cdot T^{-\imath\omega_{1}}_{I,z}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mu(\bm{y}_{n}){K}(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})e^{2\pi\imath\lambda(\underline{\bm{z}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n})}\,\Phi(\bm{y}_{n})\,d\bm{y}_{n}.

(2.7)

Shifts act nontrivially on the kernel
K(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{y}_{n})
and
exponent
e^{2\pi\imath\lambda(\underline{\bm{z}}_{n}-\underline{\bm{y}}_{n})}
. By (
1.16
),
(

3
Commutativity of Baxter operators

The commutativity of
Q
-operators

Q_{n}(\rho)\,Q_{n}(\rho^{\prime})=Q_{n}(\rho^{\prime})\,Q_{n}(\rho)

(3.1)

follows from the equality of the kernels

Q_{n}(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{x}_{n};\rho,\rho^{\prime})=Q_{n}(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{x}_{n};\rho^{\prime},\rho)

(3.2)

of their products

Q_{n}(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{x}_{n};\rho,\rho^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}d\bm{y}_{n}\,Q(\bm{z}_{n},\bm{y}_{n};\rho)Q(\bm{y}_{n},\bm{x}_{n};\rho^{\prime}),\qquad z_{j},x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.3)

One can further note that the variables
\bm{z}_{n}
and
\bm{x}_{n}
enter the equality (
3.2
) in a similar way. Thus we combine them into a common
2n
array

\bm{z}_{2n}=\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}

and set

Q_{n}(\bm{z}_{2n};\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi\imath\lambda\underline{\bm{y}}_{n}}\prod_{a=1}^{2n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}K(z_{a}-y_{i})\mu(\bm{y}_{n})d\bm{y}_{n}.

(3.4)

Then the equality (
3.2
) takes the form of the following integral identity

Q_{n}(\bm{z}_{2n};\lambda)=e^{2\pi\imath\lambda\underline{\bm{z}}_{2n}}Q_{n}(\bm{z}_{2n};-\lambda),

(3.5)

where we put

\lambda=\rho^{\prime}-\rho.

Under the condition (
1.27
)
both integrals in (
3.5
) absolutely converge uniformely on compact subsets of the parameters, see Proposition
5
in Appendix
B
. Thus, both sides of the
equality (
3.5
) are analytic functions of all the parameters therein. Having in mind these analyticity properties we prove the equality (
3.5
) step by step following the plan below.

1.

We prove that for complex periods
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
with
\mathrm{Re}\,\omega_{i}>0
and
\omega_{1}/\omega_{2}\not\in\mathbb{R}
both integrals may be calculated by residues technique for big enough negative values of
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda
.
In other words, one can find a sequence of contours that in the limit encircle all poles in the corresponding half plane (for each integration variable) and such that the integrals over encircling contours tend to zero.

2.

Next we assume that the real parameters
z_{i}
are generic. Under this assumption we prove that the sum of residues over higher order poles vanishes. For this we accumulate the vanishing properties of the integration measure
\mu(\bm{y}_{n})
into Lemma
4
, which says that the sum of
4^{r}
integrand values over the points with interchanged coefficients at the periods gives zero of order
2r
. This lemma is used to describe the result of
k
successive integrations computed by residues and to show that after each integration higher order poles vanish.

3.

At this stage we are left with the sum of simple poles on both sides; each of them is a product of one-dimensional residues integrals over shifted parameters
z_{i}
. Their sums decompose into the sums of
\binom{2n}{n}
series, depending of which parameters
z_{i}
enter the residue calculations. The equalities of corresponding series reduce to certain identities on rational functions which we prove separately. The identities generalize Ruijsenaars’ kernel function identity and could be treated as certain duality transformations for multivalued basic hypergeometric series
[
KN
]
.

4.

Finally, due to analiticity of the statement (
3.5
) we conclude that it is valid first for all values of the parameters
z_{i}
,
\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda
and as well for
\omega_{1}/\omega_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
(in particular, for real values of the periods
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
).

Appendix A
Double Gamma and sine functions

The Barnes double Gamma function
\Gamma_{2}(z|\bm{\omega})

[
B1
]
is defined by the relation

\Gamma_{2}(z|\bm{\omega})=\exp{\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\zeta_{2}(s,z|\bm{\omega})\right)}\Big|_{s=0},

where
\zeta_{2}(s,z|\bm{\omega})
is the analytical continuation of the series

\zeta_{2}(s,z|\bm{\omega})=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\geq 0}(z+n_{1}\omega_{1}+n_{2}\omega_{2})^{-s},\qquad\mathrm{Re}\,s>2

which under assumptions (
1.14
) and
\mathrm{Re}\,z>0
can be presented by the integral

\zeta_{2}(s,z|\bm{\omega})=\Gamma(1-s)\int_{C}\frac{e^{-zt}(-t)^{s}}{\left(1-e^{-\omega_{1}t}\right)\left(1-e^{-\omega_{2}t}\right)}\frac{dt}{2\pi\imath t}

over the Hankel contour
C
enclosing the ray
\{t\geq 0\}
counterclockwise.
Under the same assumptions analogous integral presentation of
\ln\Gamma_{2}(z|\bm{\omega})
looks as follows

\ln\Gamma_{2}(z|\bm{\omega})=\frac{\gamma}{2}B_{2,2}(z|\bm{\omega})+\int_{C}\frac{e^{-zt}\ln(-t)}{\left(1-e^{-\omega_{1}t}\right)\left(1-e^{-\omega_{2}t}\right)}\frac{dt}{2\pi\imath t}.

(A.1)

Here

B_{2,2}(z|\bm{\omega})=\frac{z^{2}}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}-\frac{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\,z\,+\,\frac{\omega_{1}^{2}+3\omega_{1}\omega_{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}}{6\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}

(A.2)

is a particular multiple Bernoulli polynomial,
\gamma
is the Euler constant.

The double sine function
S_{2}(z):=S_{2}(z|\bm{\omega})
, see
[
Ku
]
and references therein, is then defined as

S_{2}(z|\bm{\omega})=\Gamma_{2}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-z|\bm{\omega})\Gamma_{2}^{-1}(z|\bm{\omega}).

(A.3)

It satisfies functional relations

\frac{S_{2}(z)}{S_{2}(z+\omega_{1})}=2\sin\frac{\pi z}{\omega_{2}},\qquad\frac{S_{2}(z)}{S_{2}(z+\omega_{2})}=2\sin\frac{\pi z}{\omega_{1}}

(A.4)

and inversion relation

S_{2}(z)S_{2}(-z)=-4\sin\frac{\pi z}{\omega_{1}}\sin\frac{\pi z}{\omega_{2}},

(A.5)

or equivalently

S_{2}(z)S_{2}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-z)=1.

(A.6)

The double sine function is a homogeneous function of all its argumets

S_{2}(\gamma z|\gamma\omega_{1},\gamma\omega_{2})=S_{2}(z|\omega_{1},\omega_{2}),\qquad\gamma\in(0,\infty)

(A.7)

and is invariant under permutation of periods

S_{2}(z|\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=S_{2}(z|\omega_{2},\omega_{1}).

(A.8)

The relation (
A.4
) has a useful corollary

\displaystyle\frac{S_{2}(z)}{S_{2}(z+m\omega_{1}+k\omega_{2})}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{mk}\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}2\sin\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}(z+j\omega_{1})\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}2\sin\frac{\pi}{\omega_{1}}(z+j\omega_{2}),

(A.9)

\displaystyle\frac{S_{2}(z-m\omega_{1}-k\omega_{2})}{S_{2}(z)}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{mk}\prod_{j=1}^{m}2\sin\frac{\pi}{\omega_{2}}(z-j\omega_{1})\prod_{j=1}^{k}2\sin\frac{\pi}{\omega_{1}}(z-j\omega_{2})

that holds for
m,k\geq 0
. The latter relations also imply the following factorization formula

{S_{2}(z)}{S_{2}(z+m\omega_{1}+k\omega_{2})}=(-1)^{mk}{S_{2}(z+m\omega_{1})}{S_{2}(z+k\omega_{2})}

(A.10)

for
m,k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
The function
S_{2}(z)
is a meromorphic function of
z
with poles at

z_{m,k}=m\omega_{1}+k\omega_{2},\qquad m,k\geq 1

(A.11)

and zeros at

z_{-m,-k}=-m\omega_{1}-k\omega_{2},\qquad m,k\geq 0.

(A.12)

For
\omega_{1}/\omega_{2}\not\in\mathbb{Q}
all poles and zeros are simple. The residues of
S_{2}(z)
and
S^{-1}_{2}(z)
at these points are

\displaystyle\underset{z=z_{m,k}}{\operatorname{Res}}\,S_{2}(z)=\frac{\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}}{2\pi}\frac{(-1)^{mk}}{\prod\limits_{s=1}^{m-1}2\sin\dfrac{\pi s\omega_{1}}{\omega_{2}}\prod\limits_{l=1}^{k-1}2\sin\dfrac{\pi l\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}}},

(A.13)

\displaystyle\underset{z=z_{-m,-k}}{\operatorname{Res}}\,S^{-1}_{2}(z)=\frac{\sqrt{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}}{2\pi}\frac{(-1)^{mk+m+k}}{\prod\limits_{s=1}^{m}2\sin\dfrac{\pi s\omega_{1}}{\omega_{2}}\prod\limits_{l=1}^{k}2\sin\dfrac{\pi l\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}}}.

(A.14)

The integral representation for the logarithm of double sine function

\ln S_{2}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{2t}\left(\frac{\sh\left[(2z-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})t\right]}{\sh(\omega_{1}t)\sh(\omega_{2}t)}-\frac{2z-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}t}\right)

(A.15)

holds true for
\mathrm{Re}\,z\in(0,\mathrm{Re}\,(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}))

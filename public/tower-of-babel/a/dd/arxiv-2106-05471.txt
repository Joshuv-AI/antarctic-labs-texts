Coxeter Pop-Tsack Torsing
Colin Defant, Nathan Williams

Abstract
. Given a finite irreducible Coxeter group W W with a fixed Coxeter element c c , we define the Coxeter pop-tsack torsing operator 𝖯𝗈𝗉 T : W → W \mathsf{Pop}_{T}:W\to W by 𝖯𝗈𝗉 T ​ ( w ) = w ⋅ π T ​ ( w ) − 1 \mathsf{Pop}_{T}(w)=w\cdot\pi_{T}(w)^{-1} , where π T ​ ( w ) \pi_{T}(w) is the join in the noncrossing partition lattice NC ⁡ ( w , c ) \mathrm{NC}(w,c) of the set of reflections lying weakly below w w in the absolute order. This definition serves as a “Bessis dual” version of the first author’s notion of a Coxeter pop-stack sorting operator, which, in turn, generalizes the pop-stack-sorting map on symmetric groups. We show that if W W is coincidental or of type D D , then the identity element of W W is the unique periodic point of 𝖯𝗈𝗉 T \mathsf{Pop}_{T} and the maximum size of a forward orbit of 𝖯𝗈𝗉 T \mathsf{Pop}_{T} is the Coxeter number h h of W W . In each of these types, we obtain a natural lift from W W to the dual braid monoid of W W . We also prove that W W is coincidental if and only if it has a unique forward orbit of size h h . For arbitrary W W , we show that the forward orbit of c − 1 c^{-1} under 𝖯𝗈𝗉 T \mathsf{Pop}_{T} has size h h and is isolated in the sense that none of the non-identity elements of the orbit have preimages lying outside of the orbit.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Pop-stack sorting

Suppose
X
is a set and
f:X\to X
is a function. Define the
forward orbit
of an element
x\in X
under the map
f
to be the set

O_{f}(x)=\{x,f(x),f^{2}(x),\ldots\},

where
f^{i}
denotes the
i^{\text{th}}
iterate of
f
. One of the primary aims of
dynamical combinatorics
is to understand the sizes of forward orbits of combinatorially-defined maps.

A rich source of combinatorial dynamical systems comes from the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, which is the group of permutations of the set
[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
. For example, the
pop-stack sorting map
is the function
\mathsf{Pop}:\mathfrak{S}_{n}\to\mathfrak{S}_{n}
that acts by reversing all of the descending runs of a permutation while keeping different descending runs in the same order relative to each other. This map, which originally appeared in an article by Ungar concerning directions determined by points in the plane
[
Ung82
]
, has gained interest in the past few years among enumerative combinatorialists
[
AV21
,
ABH21
,
ABB+19
,
EG21
,
CG19
,
CGP19
,
PS19
]
. See
[
Def21
]
and the references therein for several other dynamical systems arising from sorting operators on permutations.

It is not difficult to show that the identity permutation
e=12\cdots n
is the unique fixed point of
\mathsf{Pop}:\mathfrak{S}_{n}\to\mathfrak{S}_{n}
and that every forward orbit of this map contains
e
. However, it is surprisingly difficult to determine the maximum size of a forward orbit; this was done by Ungar, who proved that
\max\limits_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\lvert O_{\mathsf{Pop}}(w)\rvert=n
. This result was recently reproven by Albert and Vatter
[
AV21
]
.

1.2.
Coxeter pop-stack sorting

Let
W
be a finite Coxeter group. Let
S
be a set of simple reflections of
W
, and let
w_{\circ}
be the longest element of
W
. Let
\leq_{S}
denote the left weak order on
W
. For

, let

be the longest element of the parabolic subgroup of
W
generated by

. Define the
longest-element projection
from
W
to the set of parabolic long elements by

\displaystyle\pi_{S}:W

\displaystyle\to\big\{w_{\circ}(J):J\subseteq S\big\}

(1)

\displaystyle\pi_{S}(w)

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{s\leq_{S}w}^{\mathrm{Weak}}s.

That is,
\pi_{S}(w)
is the join in left weak order of the set of simple reflections lying weakly below
w
. Alternatively,
\pi_{S}(w)=w_{\circ}(\mathrm{Des}_{R}(w))
is the longest element of the parabolic subgroup generated by the right descent set
\mathrm{Des}_{R}(w)
of
w

A natural generalization of the pop-stack sorting map to Coxeter groups was introduced in
[
Def21
]
as

\displaystyle\mathsf{Pop}_{S}

\displaystyle:W\to W

(2)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Pop}_{S}(w)

\displaystyle=w\cdot\pi_{S}(w)^{-1}.

Indeed, as discussed in
[

]
, this operator agrees with the pop-stack sorting map when
W
is the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. Observe that
\mathsf{Pop}_{S}
fixes the identity element
e\in W
. On the other hand, if

is not the identity element, then
\mathsf{Pop}_{S}(w)
is strictly less than
w
in the right weak order. When

is replaced by its braid group, the elements of the forward orbit of

under

are the prefixes of the Brieskorn normal form of

,
BS72
]

One of the main results from

1.3.
Coxeter pop-tsack torsing

Let
T

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{t\leq_{T}w}^{\mathrm{NC}(W,c)}t.

1.4.
Normal forms

Because a presentation of the braid group of a finite Coxeter group is given by simply forgetting that simple reflections square to the identity, the Coxeter group embeds canonically into the positive braid monoid by simply lifting reduced expressions (in simple reflections). It has been an open problem to find a similar ‘‘canonical’’ embedding in the dual braid monoid.
2
2

2

We thank T. Gobet for bringing this problem to our attention.
The pop-tsack torsing operator gives a solution to this problem for those finite Coxeter groups in which the only periodic orbit under
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
is that of the identity—that is, for coincidental Coxeter groups and for type
D
.

Theorem
8.1
.

Let
W
be a finite irreducible Coxeter group whose only periodic orbit under
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
is that of the identity. For
w\in W
, write
w_{1}=w
and
w_{i}=\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(w_{i-1},c)
for
i\geq 2
. Let
k=\lvert O_{\mathsf{Pop}_{T}}(w)\rvert-1
so that (assuming
w\neq e
)
w_{k}\neq w_{k+1}=e
. Then the factorization

w=\pi_{T}(w_{k},c)\pi_{T}(w_{k-1},c)\cdots\pi_{T}(w_{1},c)

gives a lift from
W
to the dual braid monoid of
W
.

It would be interesting to study the properties of this lift or to extend it to complex reflection groups (one potential issue is that for such groups, some reflections may not be noncrossing partitions).

We conclude with a generalization of stabilized-interval free permutations (as in
[
Cal04
,
Bli
]
) to finite Coxeter groups using our noncrossing projection map.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout this article, we assume that
W
is a finite irreducible Coxeter group with a set
S
of simple reflections. The
rank
of
W
is
|S|
. Given
g,w\in W
, we write
w^{g}
for the conjugate
gwg^{-1}
of
w
by
g
. Let
T=\{s^{w}:s\in S,w\in W\}
denote the set of
reflections
of
W
. A
T
-word
for an element
w\in W
is a word
t_{1}\cdots t_{r}
over the alphabet
T
that is equal to
w
when considered as an element of
W
. The
reflection length
of
w
, denoted
\ell_{T}(w)
, is the minimum length of a
T
-word for
w
. The
absolute order
on
W
, denoted
\leq_{T}
, is defined by saying
v\leq_{T}w
if
\ell_{T}(wv^{-1})=\ell_{T}(w)-\ell_{T}(v)
. Since
T
is closed under conjugation by elements of
W
, we have
v\leq_{T}w
if and only if
\ell_{T}(v^{-1}w)=\ell_{T}(w)-\ell_{T}(v)
.

It is often useful to consider the
geometric representation
of
W
on a vector space
V\cong\mathbb{R}^{|S|}
. We refer the reader to
[
Arm09
,
BB05
]
for more thorough discussions of this representation; here, we simply mention the basic results that we will need. We denote the action of an element
w\in W
on a vector
\alpha\in V
by
w\cdot\alpha
. For every
t\in T
, there is a hyperplane
H_{t}\subseteq V
such that
t
acts via the linear transformation that reflects through
H_{t}
. Furthermore,
w\cdot H_{t}=H_{t^{w}}
for every
w\in W
and
t\in T
. Define the
moved space
of
w
to be
\mathrm{Mov}(w)=\mathrm{im}(w-1)
, the image of the linear transformation
w-1:V\to V
(
1
is the identity transformation). Brady and Watt
[
BW02
]
showed that if
t\in T
and
w\in W
, then

(4)

\mathrm{Mov}(t)\subseteq\mathrm{Mov}(w)\text{ if and only if }t\leq_{T}w.

The ring
\mathrm{Sym}(V_{\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{W}
of
W
-invariant polynomials is a polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}]
over a set of invariant polynomials
f_{i}
that may be chosen homogeneous and whose degrees
d_{1}\leq\cdots\leq d_{n}
are uniquely determined. We write
h=d_{n}
for the
Coxeter number
of
W
.

A
standard Coxeter element
of
W
is an element obtained by multiplying the simple reflections together in some order. A
regular element
of
W
is an element with an eigenvector that lies in the complement of the reflecting hyperplanes. Following
[
RRS17
]
, we define a
Coxeter element
of
W
to be a regular element of order
h
. For Weyl groups, Coxeter elements coincide with conjugates of standard Coxeter elements.
More generally, a
reflection automorphism
of
W
is a group automorphism that preserves the set of reflections of
W
. By
[
RRS17
, Proposition 1.4]
, the reflection automorphisms of
W
act transitively on its Coxeter elements—thus, all Coxeter elements have the same order
h
.

We assume throughout this article that we have fixed a Coxeter element
c
of
W
. A
noncrossing partition
is an element
v\in W
such that
v\leq_{T}c
. The interval
[e,c]
in the absolute order is called the
noncrossing partition lattice
and denoted
\mathrm{NC}(W,c)
; it is indeed a lattice. This is the only type
of lattice we will consider in the rest of the article, so all meets (denoted by
\bigwedge
) and joins (denoted by
\bigvee
) will be with respect to this lattice. Furthermore, the definitions of the noncrossing projection
\pi_{T}
and the Coxeter pop-tsack torsing operator
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
from (
1.3
) and
Definition

1.2
are made with respect to this
fixed Coxeter element. Since the reflection automorphisms of
W
act transitively on the Coxeter elements, different Coxeter elements give rise to isomorphic noncrossing partition lattices.

4.
General Properties of
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}

4.1.
Absolute monotonicity

Given
x\in W
, let
W_{x}
be the subgroup of
W
generated by the reflections lying weakly below
x
in the absolute order. The subgroups
W_{x}
for
x\in\mathrm{NC}(W,c)
are called the
noncrossing parabolic subgroups
of
W
. As discussed in
[
Rea10
]
, the noncrossing parabolic subgroups of
W
form a lattice under the containment order, and the map
x\mapsto W_{x}
gives an isomorphism from
\mathrm{NC}(W,c)
to this lattice.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
x\in W
, we have
\pi_{T}(\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(x))\leq_{T}\pi_{T}(x)
.

Proof.

The parabolic subgroup generated by the reflections lying weakly below
\pi_{T}(x)
in absolute order is
W_{\pi_{T}(x)}
. All of the reflections lying weakly below
x
also lie weakly below
\pi_{T}(x)
, so
x
is an element of
W_{\pi_{T}(x)}
. Of course,
\pi_{T}(x)
also belongs to
W_{\pi_{T}(x)}
. This means that
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(x)=x\pi_{T}(x)^{-1}
is in
W_{\pi_{T}(x)}
, so all of the reflections weakly below
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(x)
are in
W_{\pi_{T}(x)}
. It follows that

W_{\pi_{T}(\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(x))}
is contained in
W_{\pi_{T}(x)}
, and this proves the result.
∎

The following proposition gives a condition for elements to have an “isolated” forward orbit under
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
. It states that the only possible preimage under
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
of an element
w\in W
with
\pi_{T}(w)=c
is
wc
.

Proposition 4.2
.

If
x,w\in W
are such that
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(x)=w
and
\pi_{T}(w)=c
, then
x=wc
.

Proof.

Suppose
x
is such that
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(x)=w
. By
Lemma

4.1
,
c\leq_{T}\pi_{T}(x)
, so we must have
\pi_{T}(x)=c
. However, this implies that
x=\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(x)\pi_{T}(x)=wc
.
∎

4.2.
Equivariance under reflection automorphisms

In this section, we make a point of decorating
\pi_{T}
and
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
by the Coxeter element
c
in order to differentiate different choices of Coxeter elements. We show a sort of equivariance of
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
with respect to reflection automorphisms, which establishes that the orbit structure of
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
does not depend on the choice of Coxeter element
c
. Thus, the Coxeter pop-tsack torsing operators defined with respect to different Coxeter elements are dynamically equivalent to each other, so none of our results about
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
depend on the choice of
c
. This means that we have the freedom to choose any Coxeter element we want when we work with specific Coxeter groups, and it will often be helpful to use a specific choice that makes the resulting combinatorics easiest to describe.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\phi
be a reflection automorphism of
W
.
For
w\in W
, we have

•

\phi(\pi_{T}(w,\phi^{-1}(c)))=\pi_{T}(\phi(w),c)
, and

•

\phi(\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(w,\phi^{-1}(c)))=\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(\phi(w),c)
.

Proof.

For the first point, observe that the map
w\mapsto\phi(w)
is a lattice isomorphism from
\mathrm{NC}(W,c)
to
\mathrm{NC}(W,\phi(c))
. Furthermore, for each reflection
t
, we have
t\leq_{T}w
if and only if
\phi(t)\leq_{T}\phi(w)
. Therefore,

\phi\left(\pi_{T}(w,\phi^{-1}(c))\right)=\phi\left(\bigvee_{t\leq_{T}w}^{\mathrm{NC}(W,\phi^{-1}(c))}t\right)=\bigvee_{t\leq_{T}w}^{\mathrm{NC}(W,c)}\phi(t)=\bigvee_{t\leq_{T}\phi(w)}^{\mathrm{NC}(W,c)}t=\pi_{T}(\phi(w),c).

For the second point, we use the first point to check that

\displaystyle\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(\phi(w),c)

\displaystyle=\phi(w)\pi_{T}(\phi(w),c)^{-1}=\phi(w)\left(\phi\left(\pi_{T}(w,\phi^{-1}(c))\right)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\phi\left(w\pi_{T}(w,\phi^{-1}(c))^{-1}\right)=\phi\left(\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(w,\phi^{-1}(c))\right).\qed

As conjugation by
c
is a special kind of reflection automorphism that restricts to a lattice isomorphism of
\mathrm{NC}(W,c)
, we can specialize the preceding result to conclude the following.

Corollary 4.4
.

For
w\in W
, we have

•

\pi_{T}(w,c)^{c}=\pi_{T}(w^{c},c)
, and

•

\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(w,c)^{c}=\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(w^{c},c)
.

Since reflection automorphisms act transitively on the set of Coxeter elements, we further obtain:

Corollary 4.5
.

The orbit structure of
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
does not depend on the choice of Coxeter element.

Definition 4.6

Let

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
{W^{\prime}}
be a finite irreducible Coxeter group that unfolds into
W
. Let
c^{\prime}
be a bipartite Coxeter element of
{W^{\prime}}
, and let
c=\mathrm{unfold}(c^{\prime})
be the corresponding Coxeter element of
W
. Then
\mathrm{unfold}(\mathrm{NC}({W^{\prime}}),c^{\prime})
is a sublattice of
\mathrm{NC}(W,c)
.

Proof.

Suppose that
w^{\prime}\in W
and
w^{\prime}\leq_{T^{\prime}}c^{\prime}
so that
{w^{\prime}}
can be found as a prefix of
c^{\prime}
. Then
\mathrm{unfold}({w^{\prime}})
can be found as a prefix of
c
, so
\mathrm{unfold}({w^{\prime}})\leq_{T}c
. By the same reasoning (replacing now
c^{\prime}
by any element of
\mathrm{NC}({W^{\prime}},c^{\prime})
), we have that
\mathrm{unfold}(\mathrm{NC}({W^{\prime}},c^{\prime}))
is a subposet of
\mathrm{NC}(W,c)
.

We now check that
\mathrm{unfold}(\mathrm{NC}({W^{\prime}},c^{\prime}))
is a sublattice of
\mathrm{NC}(W,c)
(that is, that
\mathrm{unfold}(\mathrm{NC}({W^{\prime}},c^{\prime}))
is closed under the meet and join operations of
\mathrm{NC}(W,c)
). We know by
[
BW08
]
that a noncrossing partition
w
is characterized by the set of reflections that lie below it:
w=\bigvee_{t\leq_{T}w}t
. Since
w\wedge v=\bigvee_{t:t\leq_{T}w\text{ and }t\leq_{T}v}t
, it follows that
\mathrm{unfold}(\mathrm{NC}({W^{\prime}}))
is a meet-sublattice. Indeed, for fixed
t^{\prime}\in T^{\prime}
and
r\in\mathrm{fold}^{-1}(t^{\prime})
, we have
r\leq\mathrm{unfold}(w^{\prime})\wedge\mathrm{unfold}(v^{\prime})
if and only if
r\leq_{T}\mathrm{unfold}(w^{\prime})
and
r\leq_{T}\mathrm{unfold}(v^{\prime})
. This occurs if and only if
t^{\prime}\leq_{T^{\prime}}w^{\prime}
and
t^{\prime}\leq_{T^{\prime}}v^{\prime}
, which occurs if and only if
t^{\prime}\leq_{T^{\prime}}w^{\prime}\wedge v^{\prime}
, which occurs if and only if
r\leq_{T}\mathrm{unfold}(w^{\prime}\wedge v^{\prime})
.

The Kreweras complement maps
K:\mathrm{NC}(W,c)\to\mathrm{NC}(W,c)
and
K^{\prime}:\mathrm{NC}(W^{\prime},c^{\prime})\to\mathrm{NC}(W^{\prime},c^{\prime})
, defined by
K(w)=cw^{-1}
and
K^{\prime}(w^{\prime})=c^{\prime}(w^{\prime})^{-1}
, are lattice anti-isomorphisms
[
Arm09
]
. We have
K\circ\mathrm{unfold}=\mathrm{unfold}\circ K^{\prime}
because
\mathrm{unfold}
is a homomorphism that sends
c^{\prime}
to
c
. This shows that
K
preserves the set
\mathrm{unfold}(\mathrm{NC}({W^{\prime}},c^{\prime}))
, so
\mathrm{unfold}(\mathrm{NC}({W^{\prime}},c^{\prime}))
is also a join-sublattice of
\mathrm{NC}(W,c)
.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

If a finite irreducible Coxeter group
W^{\prime}
unfolds into
W
, then for
w^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}
,

\mathrm{unfold}(\mathsf{Pop}_{T^{\prime}}^{k}(w^{\prime}))=\mathsf{Pop}_{T}^{k}(\mathrm{unfold}(w^{\prime})).

Proof.

It suffices to prove the case when
k=1
since the general result then follows by induction. Choose
w^{\prime}\in{W^{\prime}}
. If
t^{\prime}\in T^{\prime}
is such that
t^{\prime}\leq_{T^{\prime}}w^{\prime}
, then
\mathrm{unfold}(t^{\prime})\leq_{T^{\prime}}\mathrm{unfold}(w^{\prime})
. By the lattice embedding of
Lemma

4.7
, we have
\pi_{T}({W^{\prime}})=\mathrm{unfold}(\bigvee_{t^{\prime}\leq_{W^{\prime}}{w^{\prime}}}t^{\prime})=\bigvee_{t^{\prime}\leq_{W^{\prime}}{w^{\prime}}}\mathrm{unfold}(t^{\prime})=\pi_{T}(\mathrm{unfold}({w^{\prime}}))
.
Because
\mathrm{unfold}
is a group homomorphism, we have

\mathrm{unfold}(\mathsf{Pop}_{T^{\prime}}({w^{\prime}}))=\mathrm{unfold}({w^{\prime}}\pi_{T^{\prime}}({w^{\prime}})^{-1})=\mathrm{unfold}({w^{\prime}})\mathrm{unfold}(\pi_{T^{\prime}}({w^{\prime}}))^{-1}=\mathrm{unfold}({w^{\prime}})\pi_{T}(\mathrm{unfold}({w^{\prime}}))^{-1}

=\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(\mathrm{unfold}({w^{\prime}})).\qed

We can now prove the part of
Theorem

4.10
stating that
c^{-1}
requires
h-1
iterations of
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
to reach the identity.

Corollary 4.9
.

We have
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}^{h-1}(c^{-1})=e
. If
W
is not the trivial group, then
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}^{h-2}(c^{-1})\neq e
.

Proof.

If
W
is trivial, then so is the result, so we may assume
|W|\geq 2
. Let
W^{\prime}=I_{2}(h)
, and let
c^{\prime}
be a Coxeter element of
W^{\prime}
. We have seen that
W
folds to
W^{\prime}
via the Coxeter plane and that
\mathrm{unfold}(c^{\prime})
is a Coxeter element of
W
. By
Proposition

4.3
, we may assume
c=\mathrm{unfold}(c^{\prime})
. As discussed in
Section

3
, we have
\mathsf{Pop}_{T^{\prime}}^{h-2}(c^{-1})\neq\mathsf{Pop}_{T^{\prime}}(c^{-1})=e
. The result now follows from
Theorem

4.8
and the fact that
\mathrm{unfold}:W^{\prime}\to W
is injective.
∎

4.4.
The inverse Coxeter element orbit

In this section, we prove the following theorem regarding the forward orbit of
c^{-1}
under
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(\cdot,c)
.

Theorem 4.10
.

The forward orbit of
c^{-1}
under
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(\cdot,c)
is

O_{\mathsf{Pop}_{T}}(c^{-1})=\{c^{-1},c^{-2},\ldots,c^{-(h-1)},e\}.

The element
c^{-1}
has no preimages under
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
. Moreover, if
2\leq i\leq h-1
, then the only preimage of
c^{-i}
under
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
is
c^{-(i-1)}
.

Using the fact that
W
folds to
I_{2}(h)
via the Coxeter plane, we proved in
Corollary

4.9
that
c^{-1}
requires
(h-1)
iterations of
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
to reach the identity. Let us provide an alternative proof of this fact. We require a lemma, which states that the join in the noncrossing partition lattice of any orbit of a reflection under the conjugation action of
c
is just
c
.

Lemma 4.11
.

If
t\in T
is a reflection, then
c=\bigvee\limits_{k=0}^{h-1}t^{c^{k}}
.

Proof.

Since
W
folds to
I_{2}(h)
via the Coxeter plane, we can consider the Coxeter element
\mathrm{unfold}(c^{\prime})
of
W
, where
c^{\prime}
is a Coxeter element of
I_{2}(h)
. Each Coxeter element of
W
can be obtained by applying a reflection automorphism to
\mathrm{unfold}(c^{\prime})
, so it suffices to prove the lemma when
c=\mathrm{unfold}(c^{\prime})
.

Conjugation by
c
induces an automorphism of
\mathrm{NC}(W,c)
; therefore, if a set
O\subseteq\mathrm{NC}(W,c)
is invariant under conjugation by
c
, then the element
x=\bigvee_{w\in O}w
is invariant under conjugation by
c
:

x^{c}=\left(\bigvee_{w\in O}w\right)^{c}=\bigvee_{w\in O}w^{c}=\bigvee_{w\in O}w=x.

It is a classical result that the centralizer of
c
is the cyclic group generated by
c
(see
[
Car72
, Proposition 30]
), so we must have
x=c^{i}
for some integer
i
. Therefore, we are left to prove that the only powers of
c
in
\mathrm{NC}(W,c)
are
c
and
e
. This will follow if we can show that the only powers of
c^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{NC}(I_{2}(h),c^{\prime})
are
c^{\prime}
and
e
. But this is clear since the absolute length of any power of
c^{\prime}
(a rotation) is
0
or
2
(see also
[
Bes15
, Lemma 12.2]
).∎

Proposition 4.12
.

For each integer
1\leq i\leq h-1
, we have
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(c^{-i})=c^{-(i+1)}
. Thus, the forward orbit of
c^{-1}
under
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
is
\{c^{-1},c^{-2},\ldots,c^{-(h-1)},e\}
, which has size
h
.

Proof.

Fix
1\leq i\leq h-1
. We claim that if
t\in T
is such that
t\leq_{T}c^{-i}
, then
t^{c}\leq_{T}c^{-i}
. According to (
4
), we have
\mathrm{Mov}(t)\subseteq\mathrm{Mov}(c^{-i})
. Take
\alpha_{t}
to be a vector orthogonal to the hyperplane
H_{t}
so that
\mathrm{Mov}(t)=\mathrm{span}\{\alpha_{t}\}
. Then
\alpha_{t}\in\mathrm{Mov}(c^{-i})
, so there exists a vector
\beta\in V
such that
(c^{-i}-1)\cdot\beta=\alpha_{t}
. Applying
c
to each side of this equation shows that

(c^{-i}-1)\cdot(c\cdot\beta)=c\cdot((c^{-i}-1)\cdot\beta)=c\cdot\alpha_{t}.

Thus,
\mathrm{span}\{c\cdot\alpha_{t}\}\subseteq\mathrm{Mov}(c^{-i})
. Since
c\cdot H_{t}=H_{t^{c}}
, we know that
c\cdot\alpha_{t}
is orthogonal to
H_{t^{c}}
. Thus,
\mathrm{Mov}(t^{c})=\mathrm{span}\{c\cdot\alpha_{t}\}
. Appealing to (
4
) again, we find that
t^{c}\leq_{T}c^{-i}
.

Note that there must exist some
t\in T
with
t\leq_{T}c^{-i}
since
c^{-i}\neq e
. It follows from the preceding paragraph that
t^{c^{k}}\leq_{T}c^{-i}
for all
0\leq k\leq h-1
. Therefore, we can use
Lemma

4.11
to see that

c=\bigvee\limits_{k=0}^{h-1}t^{c^{k}}\leq_{T}\bigvee_{t^{\prime}\leq_{T}c^{-i}}t^{\prime}=\pi_{T}(c^{-i}).

Consequently,
\pi_{T}(c^{-i})=c
, so
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(c^{-i})=c^{-i}c^{-1}=c^{-(i+1)}
.
∎

Corollary 4.13
.

The element
c^{-1}
has no preimages under
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
. Moreover, if
2\leq i\leq h-1
, then the only preimage of
c^{-1}
under
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
is

Proof.

By

and

, the only possible preimage of

∎

This completes the proof of

5.1.

Recall that the Coxeter group of type

is the symmetric group

, the group of permutations of

c=(12\cdots n)
.

There is a simple combinatorial way to describe the noncrossing partitions in
\mathrm{NC}(\mathfrak{S}_{n},c)
. Begin by labeling
n
equally-spaced points on a circle with the numbers
1,\ldots,n
in clockwise order. Now choose a set partition
\rho
of
[n]
. For each block of
\rho
, draw the convex hull of the points on the circle labeled with the elements of that block. We say
\rho
is a
noncrossing set partition
if none of the convex hulls of different blocks intersect each other. Suppose
\rho
is a noncrossing set partition. For each block of
\rho
, form a cycle by ordering the elements of the block cyclically in clockwise order around the circle. The product of these cycles is a noncrossing partition in
\mathrm{NC}(\mathfrak{S}_{n},c)
, and every noncrossing partition in
\mathrm{NC}(\mathfrak{S}_{n},c)
arises uniquely in this way. See
Figure

6
.

Figure 6.
A noncrossing set partition. The corresponding noncrossing partition in
\mathrm{NC}(\mathfrak{S}_{n},c)
is
(1\,\,3\,\,6\,\,7)(2)(4\,\,5)(8\,\,9\,\,10)
.

We will find it convenient to refer to a cycle of a permutation and to the set of entries in the cycle interchangeably. For example, we sometimes write
i\in\mathcal{C}
to mean that
i
is an entry in a cycle
\mathcal{C}
. Let
\mathrm{cyc}_{>1}(w)
denote the number of non-singleton cycles of a permutation
w
.

The partial order on
\mathrm{NC}(\mathfrak{S}_{n},c)
, which is the restriction of the absolute order on
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
to
[e,c]
, is easy to describe combinatorially because it corresponds to the reverse refinement order on noncrossing set partitions. In other words, if
u,v\in\mathrm{NC}(\mathfrak{S}_{n},c)
, then
u\leq_{T}v
if and only if every cycle in
u
is contained (as a set) in a cycle in
v
. The noncrossing projection of a permutation
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is the smallest noncrossing partition
v\in\mathrm{NC}(\mathfrak{S}_{n},c)
such that every cycle in
w
is contained in a cycle in
v
.

An
antiexceedance
of a permutation
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is an element
i\in[n]
such that
i<w^{-1}(i)
. Write
\mathrm{Aexc}(w)
for the set of antiexceedances of
w
, and let
\mathrm{aexc}=\lvert\mathrm{Aexc}(w)\rvert
.

Theorem 5.2
.

The following properties relating antiexceedances and
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
hold:

•

The element
c^{-1}
has
h-1=n-1
antiexceedances.

•

Every element of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
other than
c^{-1}
has at most
n-2
antiexceedances.

•

For every
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, we have
\mathrm{aexc}(\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(w))=\mathrm{aexc}(w)-\mathrm{cyc}_{>1}(\pi_{T}(w))
.

Proof.

The set of antiexceedances of a permutation
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is a subset of
[n-1]
. It is straightforward to see that this set of antiexceedances is equal to
[n-1]
if and only if
w=c^{-1}=(n\cdots 21)
. This proves the first two bulleted items. Now fix
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, and let
v=\pi_{T}(w)
so that
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(w)=wv^{-1}
.

Suppose
i\in[n]
is not an antiexceedance of
w
. This means that
i\geq w^{-1}(i)
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the cycle of
v
that contains
i
. Since every cycle in
w
is contained in a cycle in
v
, we know that
\mathcal{C}
also contains
w^{-1}(i)
. The cycle
\mathcal{C}
is cyclically ordered clockwise, so
v(w^{-1}(i))
, which is the entry immediately following
w^{-1}(i)
in
\mathcal{C}
, is less than or equal to
i
. Because
v(w^{-1}(i))=(\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(w))^{-1}(i)
, this shows that
i
is not an antiexceedance of
\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(w)
. As
i
was arbitrary, this proves that
\mathrm{Aexc}(\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(w))\subseteq\mathrm{Aexc}(w)
.

We claim
j\in\mathrm{Aexc}(w)\setminus\mathrm{Aexc}(\mathsf{Pop}_{T}(w))
if and only if
v^{-1}(j)
is the largest entry in a non-singleton cycle. Note that this claim immediately implies the third bulleted item in the statement of the theorem. To prove this claim, suppose first that

Type

In this section, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let

be a Coxeter group of type

. Then

•

\mathsf{Pop}_{T}^{2n-3}(w)=e
for all
w\in W
, and

•

\max\limits_{w\in W}\left|O_{\mathsf{Pop}_{T}}(w)\right|=2n-2
.

As in the previous section, let
\pm[n]=\{\overline{n},\ldots,\overline{1},1,\ldots,n\}
(with bars replacing negative signs). The Coxeter group
D_{n}
is the group of permutations
w:\pm[n]\to\pm[n]
such that
w(\overline{i})=\overline{w(i)}
for all
i\in\pm[n]
and such that
\lvert\{i\in[n]:w(i)<0\}\rvert
is even. The reflections in
D_{n}
are the elements of the form
(i\,\,j)(\overline{i}\,\,\overline{j})
, where
i,j\in\pm[n]
and
i\neq\overline{j}
. Throughout this section, we fix the Coxeter element

c=(\overline{1}\,\,\overline{2}\,\cdots\overline{n-1}\,\,1\,\,2\cdots n-1)(\overline{n}\,\,n)
. Given a cycle
\mathcal{C}=(i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{k})
in an element

, let
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
denote the cycle
(\overline{i_{1}}\,\,\overline{i_{2}}\cdots\overline{i_{k}})
, and note that
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
is also a cycle in

. We say the cycle
\mathcal{C}
is

if
\mathcal{C}=\overline{\mathcal{C}}
. It follows from the definition of

that

is balanced if and only if there exists an entry

such that

and
\overline{k}
are both in

. A cycle that is not balanced is

. Each element of

has an even number of balanced cycles.

Every element

can be written uniquely as

are unbalanced cycles,

are balanced cycles, and the cycles appearing in the factorization are pairwise disjoint. The reflection length of

can be computed as

\ell_{T}(w)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(k_{i}-1)+\sum_{j=1}^{s}m_{j},

where

is the number of entries in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
(and, therefore, also
\overline{\mathcal{C}_{i}}
) and

is the number of entries in
\mathcal{D}_{j}
(see
[
BW02
, Section 3]
). The following useful lemma is a consequence of the computations in
[
BW02
, Example 3.6]
(see also
[
AR04
, Section 3]
).

Lemma 6.2

(
[
BW02
]
)
.

Let
w\in D_{n}
, and consider

with

. We have
(i\,\,j)(\overline{i}\,\,\overline{j})\leq_{T}w
if and only if
i
and

are in the same cycle of
w
or
i
and
j
are in different balanced cycles of
w
.

Our combinatorial description of noncrossing partitions in type

is based on the work of Athanasiadis and Reiner
[
AR04
]
. Begin by labeling

equally-spaced points on a circle with the numbers
\overline{1},\overline{2},\ldots,

\overline{n-1},1,2,\ldots,n-1

\overline{n}

of

(2)

.

(3)

We associate each type-

with the diagram showing the labeled points on the circle and at the center of the circle together with the convex hulls of the blocks of
\rho
. A

of

. Let

Let

Lemma 6.4
.

Let

, and let

. Consider distinct

. If

and

.

Suppose first that

. It follows from

that

, so

. Since

is noncrossing, it has at most

. This means that

.

Now suppose

. This means

Our goal in the remainder of this section is to prove
Theorem

6.1
. The Coxeter number of

. The following lemma will do all of the heavy lifting. When we refer to “the circle,” we mean the circle that has

Lemma 6.5
.

Suppose

If

Suppose

be the cycle in

,

tells us that

. Because

that occurs right after

.

If there does not exist an integer

Let

v_{i-1}(u_{i-1}^{-1}(j))=u_{i}^{-1}(j)\in\{\overline{n},n\}

. In other words,

. This implies that

Athanasiadis and Reiner

(j\,\,u_{i}^{-1}(j))(\overline{j}\,\,\overline{u_{i}^{-1}(j)})\leq_{T}v_{i}

is among the largest

.

We have shown that if

is among the largest
n-2

. We assumed in the hypothesis of the lemma that

. It follows from these facts that

for every

. Therefore, the sequence

8.1.
Dual braid lifts

For this subsection, fix

\mathsf{Pop}_{T}

Recall that we introduced

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
W

\mathsf{Pop}_{T}
is that of the identity. For

and

. Let

w\neq e
)
w_{k}\neq w_{k+1}=e
. Then the factorization

w=\pi_{T}(w_{k},c)\pi_{T}(w_{k-1},c)\cdots\pi_{T}(w_{1},c)

gives a lift from
W
to the dual braid monoid of
W
.

\displaystyle\pi_{T}(w_{1})

\displaystyle=(12634),

\displaystyle=(12346)

On the operators of Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya type
Jianjun Jin

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce and study several new Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya-type operators. In particular, we study a Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya-type operator induced by a positive Borel measure on [ 0 , 1 ) [0,1) . We establish some sufficient and necessary conditions for the boundedness (compactness) of these operators. We also determine the exact values of the norms of the Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya-type operators for certain special cases.

1.
Introduction and main results

Throughout this paper, for two positive numbers
A,B
, we write
A\preceq B
, or
A\succeq B
, if there exists a positive constant
C
independent of the arguments such that
A\leq CB
, or
A\geq CB
, respectively. We will write
A\asymp B
if both
A\preceq B
and
A\succeq B
.

Let
p>1
. We denote the conjugate of
p
by
p^{\prime}
, i.e.,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
. Let
l^{p}
be the space of sequences of complex numbers, i.e.,

l^{p}:=\{a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}:\|a\|_{p}=(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}<+\infty\}.

For
a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, the Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya operator
\mathbf{H}
is defined as

\mathbf{H}(a)(m):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{\max\{m,n\}},\,m\in\mathbb{N}.

It is well known (see
[
13
, page 254]
) that

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p>1
. Then
\mathbf{H}
is bounded on
l^{p}
and the norm of
\mathbf{H}
is
p+p^{\prime}
.

Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya operator is related to some important topics in analysis and there have been many results about this operator and its analogous and generalizations. The classical results of this topic can be founded in the famous monograph
[
13
]
. In the past three decades, the so-called Hilbert-type operators, including Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya-type operators, have been extensively studied by Yang and his coauthors, see the survey
[
21
]
and Yang’s book
[
22
]
. For more recent results see for example
[
20
]
and
[
23
]
. Fu et al. have studied in
[
9
]
some
p
-adic Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya-type operators.Very recently, in the work
[
3
]
, Brevig established some norm estimates for certain Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya-type operators in terms of the Riemann zeta function. Some further results have been obtained in
[
4
]
.

In this paper, we first introduce and study the following operator of the Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya type,

\mathbf{H}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(a)(m):=m^{\frac{1}{p}[(\alpha-1)+\alpha\mu]}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}[(\beta-1)-(p^{\prime}-1)\beta\nu]}}{[\max\{m^{\alpha},n^{\beta}\}]^{\gamma}}a_{n},a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},m\geq 1,

where
\gamma>0
,
0<\alpha,\beta\leq 1
,
-1<\mu,\nu<p-1
.

The operator
\mathbf{H}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
reduces to the classical Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya operator
\mathbf{H}
when
\alpha=\beta=\gamma=1
,
\mu=\nu=0
. We first study the boundedness of
\mathbf{H}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
. We will provide a sufficient and necessary condition for the boundedness of
\mathbf{H}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
in terms of the parameters
\gamma,\mu,\nu
and prove that

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p>1
,
\gamma>0
,
0<\alpha,\beta\leq 1
and
-1<\mu,\nu<p-1
. Let
\mathbf{H}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
be defined as obove. Then
\mathbf{H}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
is bounded on
l^{p}
if and only if
p(\gamma-1)-(\mu-\nu)\geq 0.

When
p(\gamma-1)-(\mu-\nu)=0
, i.e.,
\gamma=1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}
, we use
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta}
to denote the operator
\mathbf{H}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
. That is to say,

\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta}(a)(m):=m^{\frac{1}{p}[(\alpha-1)+\alpha\mu]}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}[(\beta-1)-(p^{\prime}-1)\beta\nu]}}{[\max\{m^{\alpha},n^{\beta}\}]^{1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}}}a_{n},a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},m\geq 1.

We denote by
\|\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta}\|
the norm of
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta}
. We will show the following result, which is an extention of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p>1
,
0<\alpha,\beta\leq 1
and
-1<\mu,\nu<p-1
. Let
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta}
be defined as above. Then
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta}
is bounded on
l^{p}
and

(1.1)

\|\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta}\|=\frac{p}{{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}{\beta}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}\left(\frac{1}{1+\mu}+\frac{1}{p-1-\nu}\right).

When
\alpha=\beta=1
. From Theorem
1.2
, we know that the operator

\mathbf{H}^{\mu,\nu}_{1,1,\gamma}(a)(m)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\frac{\mu}{p}}n^{-\frac{\nu}{p}}}{[\max\{m,n\}]^{\gamma}}a_{n},a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},m\geq 1,

is not bounded on
l^{p}
when
\gamma<1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}
. On the one hand, we note that

\int_{[0,1)}t^{\max\{m,n\}-1}(1-t)^{\gamma-1}dt=B(\max\{m,n\},\gamma)=\frac{\Gamma(\max\{m,n\})\Gamma(\gamma)}{\Gamma(\gamma+\max\{m,n\})},

for
\gamma>0
,
m,n\geq 1
. Here
B(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Beta function, which is defined as

B(u,v):=\int_{0}^{1}t^{u-1}(1-t)^{v-1}\,dt,\>u>0,v>0.

The Gamma function
\Gamma(\cdot)
is defined as

\Gamma(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{x-1}\,dt,\>x>0.

It is known that

B(u,v)=\frac{\Gamma(u)\Gamma{(v)}}{\Gamma(u+v)}.

For more introductions to these special functions, see
[
1
]
. On the other hand, we see from

(1.2)

\Gamma(x)=\sqrt{2\pi}x^{x-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-x}[1+r(x)],\,|r(x)|\leq e^{\frac{1}{12x}}-1,\,x>0,

that

\frac{\Gamma(\max\{m,n\})\Gamma(\gamma)}{\Gamma(\gamma+\max\{m,n\})}\asymp\frac{1}{[\max\{m,n\}]^{\gamma}},\,\gamma>0,m,n\geq 1.

Hence, in order to make
\mathbf{H}^{\mu,\nu}_{1,1,\gamma}
to be bounded on
l^{p}
when
\gamma<1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}
, we let
\lambda
be a positive Borel measure in
[0,1)
, and consider the following operator

\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\gamma,\lambda}(a)(m):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}m^{\frac{\mu}{p}}n^{-\frac{\nu}{p}}\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}[m,n]a_{n},\,a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\,m\geq 1.

Where

(1.3)

\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}[m,n]=\int_{[0,1)}t^{\max\{m,n\}-1}(1-t)^{\gamma-1}d\lambda(t),\,m,n\geq 1.

We will characterize measures
\lambda
such that
\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\gamma,\lambda}
is bounded (compact) on
l^{p}
. To state our results, we introduce the notion of generalized Carleson measure on
[0,1)
. Let
s>0
, let
\lambda
be a positive Borel measure on
[0,1)
, we say
\lambda
is an
s
-Carleson measure if there is a constant
C>0
such that

\lambda([t,1))\leq C(1-t)^{s}

holds for all
t\in[0,1)
.
Moreover, an
s
-Carleson measure
\lambda
on
[0,1)
is said to be a vanishing
s
-Carleson measure, if it satisfies further that

\lim_{t\rightarrow 1^{-}}\frac{\lambda([t,1))}{(1-t)^{s}}=0.

We shall prove the following criterion for the boundedness of
\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\gamma,\lambda}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
p>1,\gamma>0
and
-1<\mu,\nu<p-1
. Let
\lambda
be a positive Borel measure on
[0,1)
such that
d\rho(t):=(1-t)^{\gamma-1}d\lambda(t)
is a finite measure on
[0,1)
, and
\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\gamma,\lambda}
be defined as above. Then
\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\gamma,\lambda}
is bounded on
l^{p}
if and only if

\rho
is a
[1+\frac{1}{p}(\mu-\nu)]
-Carleson measure on
[0,1)
.

For the compactness of
\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\gamma,\lambda}
, we shall show that

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
p>1,\gamma>0
and
-1<\mu,\nu<p-1
. Let
\lambda
be a positive Borel measure on
[0,1)
such that
d\rho(t):=(1-t)^{\gamma-1}d\lambda(t)
is a finite measure on
[0,1)
, and
\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\gamma,\lambda}
be defined as above. Then
\widehat{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\gamma,\lambda}
is compact on
l^{p}
if and only if

\rho
is a vanishing
[1+\frac{1}{p}(\mu-\nu)]
-Carleson measure on
[0,1)
.

The paper is organized as follows. Two lemmas will be given in the next section. We will first prove Theorem
1.3
in Section 3. The proof of Theorem
1.2
will be given in Section 4. We prove Theorem
1.4
and
1.5
in Section 5. Final remarks will be presented in Section 6.

2.
Two lemmas

We need the following lemmas in the proof of our main results of this paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p>1
,
0<\alpha,\beta\leq 1
and
-1<\mu,\nu<p-1
. We define

E(m):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{\beta-1}}{[\max\{m^{\alpha},n^{\beta}\}]^{1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}}}\cdot\frac{m^{\frac{\alpha(1+\mu)}{p}}}{n^{\frac{\beta(1+\nu)}{p}}},\,m\geq 1;

F(n):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{m^{\alpha-1}}{[\max\{m^{\alpha},n^{\beta}\}]^{1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}}}\cdot\frac{n^{\frac{\beta(p-1-\nu)}{p}}}{m^{\frac{\alpha(p-1-\mu)}{p}}},\,n\geq 1.

Then we have

(2.1)

E(m)\leq\frac{p}{\beta}\left(\frac{1}{1+\mu}+\frac{1}{p-1-\nu}\right),\,m\geq 1,

(2.2)

F(n)\leq\frac{p}{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{1+\mu}+\frac{1}{p-1-\nu}\right),\,n\geq 1.

Proof.

In view of the assumption, we see that, for
m\geq 1
,

E(m)\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\beta-1}}{[\max\{m^{\alpha},x^{\beta}\}]^{1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}}}\cdot\frac{m^{\frac{\alpha(1+\mu)}{p}}}{x^{\frac{\beta(1+\nu)}{p}}}\,dx.

Consequently, by the change of variables
s=x^{\beta}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle E(m)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{[\max\{m^{\alpha},s\}]^{1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}}}\cdot\frac{m^{\frac{\alpha(1+\mu)}{p}}}{s^{\frac{1+\nu}{p}}}\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{-\frac{1+\nu}{p}}}{[\max\{1,t\}]^{1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}}}\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{0}^{1}t^{-\frac{1+\nu}{p}}dt+\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{1+\mu}{p}-1}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{p}{\beta}\left(\frac{1}{1+\mu}+\frac{1}{p-1-\nu}\right).

This proves (
2.1
). By the similar way, we can obtain that (
2.2
) also holds. The lemma is proved.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\gamma>0,-1<\mu,\nu<p-1
. Let
\lambda
be a positive Borel measure on
[0,1)
and
\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}[m,n]
be defined as in (
1.3
) for
m,n\geq 1
. Set
d\rho(t)=(1-t)^{\gamma-1}d\lambda(t)
. If
\rho
is a
[1+\frac{1}{p}(\mu-\nu)]
-Carleson measure on
[0,1)
, then

(2.3)

\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}[m,n]\preceq\frac{1}{[\max\{m,n\}]^{1+\frac{1}{p}(\mu-\nu)}}

holds for all
m,n\geq 1
. Furthermore, if
\rho
is a vanishing
[1+\frac{1}{p}(\mu-\nu)]
-Carleson measure on
[0,1)
, then

(2.4)

\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}[m,n]=o\left(\frac{1}{[\max\{m,n\}]^{1+\frac{1}{p}(\mu-\nu)}}\right),\,\,\max\{m,n\}\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

When
m\geq 1,n\geq 2
, or
m\geq 2,n\geq 1
. We get from integration by parts that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}[m,n]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}t^{\max\{m,n\}-1}d\rho(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho([0,1))-(\max\{m,n\}-1)\int_{0}^{1}t^{\max\{m,n\}-2}\rho([0,t))dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\max\{m,n\}-1)\int_{0}^{1}t^{\max\{m,n\}-2}\rho([t,1))dt.

If
\rho
is a
[1+\frac{1}{p}(\mu-\nu)]
-Carleson measure on
[0,1)
, then we see that there is a constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\rho([t,1))\leq C_{1}(1-t)^{1+\frac{1}{p}(\mu-\nu)}

holds for all
t\in[0,1)
. It follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}[m,n]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}(\max\{m,n\}-1)\int_{0}^{1}t^{\max\{m,n\}-2}(1-t)^{1+\frac{1}{p}(\mu-\nu)}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1}\frac{(\max\{m,n\}-1)\Gamma(\max\{m,n\}-1)\Gamma(2+\frac{1}{p}(\mu-\nu))}{\Gamma(\max\{m,n\}+1+\frac{1}{p}(\mu-\nu))}.

By using (
1.2
) again, we obtain that

\frac{(\max\{m,n\}-1)\Gamma(\max\{m,n\}-1)\Gamma(2+\frac{1}{p}(\mu-\nu))}{\Gamma(\max\{m,n\}+1+\frac{1}{p}(\mu-\nu))}\asymp\frac{1}{\max\{m,n\}^{1+\frac{1}{p}(\mu-\nu)}}.

It follows that (
2.3
) holds for
m\geq 1,n\geq 2
or
m\geq 2,n\geq 1
.

Next we consider the case
m=n=1
, we see from the fact
\rho
is a finite measure on
[0,1)
that

\mathcal{I}_{\lambda}[1,1]=\int_{0}^{1}d\rho(t)=\rho([0,1))\preceq 1.

Then we get that (
2.3
) holds for all
m,n\geq 1
. Similarly, if
\rho
is a vanishing
[1+\frac{1}{p}(\mu-\nu)]
-Carleson measure on
[0,1)
, by minor modifications of above arguments, we can show that (
2.4
) holds. The lemma is proved.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

For
a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in l^{p},m\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle m^{\frac{1}{p}[(\alpha-1)+\alpha\mu]}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}[(\beta-1)-(p^{\prime}-1)\beta\nu]}}{[\max\{m^{\alpha},n^{\beta}\}]^{1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}}}a_{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\{[K(m,n)]^{\frac{1}{p}}O_{1}(m,n)\cdot[K(m,n)]^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}O_{2}(m,n)\right\}:=I(m).

Where

{K}(m,n)=\frac{1}{[\max\{m^{\alpha},n^{\beta}\}]^{1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}}},

{O}_{1}(m,n)=\frac{n^{\frac{\beta(1+\nu)}{pp^{\prime}}-\frac{\beta\nu}{p}}}{m^{\frac{\alpha(p-1-\mu)}{p^{2}}}}\cdot m^{\frac{1}{p}(\alpha-1)}\cdot|a_{n}|,

{O}_{2}(m,n)=\frac{m^{\frac{\alpha(p-1-\mu)}{p^{2}}+\frac{\alpha\mu}{p}}}{n^{\frac{\beta(1+\nu)}{pp^{\prime}}}}\cdot n^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}(\beta-1)}.

Applying the Hölder’s inequality on
I(m)
, we get from (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle I(m)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{K}(m,n)[{O}_{1}(m,n)]^{p}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{K}(m,n)[{O}_{2}(m,n)]^{p^{\prime}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[{E}(m)]^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{K}(m,n)[{O}_{1}(m,n)]^{p}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}.

It follows from (
2.2
) that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta}a\|_{p}=[\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}I^{p}(m)]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\beta}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}\left(\frac{p}{1+\mu}+\frac{p}{p-1-\nu}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}{K}(m,n)[{O}_{1}(m,n)]^{p}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\beta}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}\left(\frac{p}{1+\mu}+\frac{p}{p-1-\nu}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{F}(n)|a_{n}|^{p}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{p}{{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}{\beta}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}\left(\frac{1}{1+\mu}+\frac{1}{p-1-\nu}\right)\|a\|_{p}.

This means that
\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta}
is bounded on
l^{p}
and

(3.1)

\|\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta}\|\leq\frac{p}{{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}{\beta}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}\left(\frac{1}{1+\mu}+\frac{1}{p-1-\nu}\right).

For
\varepsilon>0
, we take
\widetilde{a}=\{\widetilde{a}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
with
\widetilde{a}_{n}=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}n^{-\frac{1+\beta\varepsilon}{p}}
. On the one hand, we have

\|\widetilde{a}\|_{p}^{p}=\varepsilon\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-1-\beta\varepsilon}\geq\varepsilon\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{-1-\beta\varepsilon}\,dx=\frac{1}{\beta}.

On the other hand, we have

\|\widetilde{a}\|_{p}^{p}=\varepsilon+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n^{-1-\beta\varepsilon}\leq\varepsilon+\varepsilon\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{-1-\beta\varepsilon}\,dx=\varepsilon+\frac{1}{\beta}.

Thus, we obtain that

(3.2)

\|\widetilde{a}\|_{p}^{p}=\frac{1}{\beta}(1+o(1)),\,\,\,\,\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}.

We write

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta}\widetilde{a}\|_{p}^{p}=\varepsilon\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{(\alpha-1)+\alpha\mu}\cdot[J(m)]^{p}.

Here

J(m):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}[(\beta-1)-(p^{\prime}-1)\beta\nu]}\cdot n^{-\frac{1+\beta\varepsilon}{p}}}{[\max\{m^{\alpha},n^{\beta}\}]^{1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}}}.

In view of the assumption
0<\beta\leq 1,-1<\nu<p-1
, we have
1+\beta\nu\geq 0
. Hence we get that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}[(\beta-1)-(p^{\prime}-1)\beta\nu]-\frac{1+\beta\varepsilon}{p}=\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}(\beta-1)-\frac{1+\beta\nu+\beta\varepsilon}{p}<0.

Consequently,

(3.4)

\displaystyle J(m)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}[(\beta-1)-(p^{\prime}-1)\beta\nu]}\cdot x^{-\frac{1+\beta\varepsilon}{p}}}{[\max\{m^{\alpha},x^{\beta}\}]^{1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}}}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{s^{-\frac{1+\nu+\varepsilon}{p}}}{[\max\{m^{\alpha},s\}]^{1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}}}\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}m^{-\frac{\alpha}{p}(1+\mu+\varepsilon)}\int_{\frac{1}{m^{\alpha}}}^{\infty}\frac{t^{-\frac{1+\nu+\varepsilon}{p}}}{[\max\{1,t\}]^{1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}}}\,dt.

Also, for
0<\varepsilon<p-1-\nu
, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{m^{\alpha}}}^{\infty}\frac{t^{-\frac{1+\nu+\varepsilon}{p}}}{[\max\{1,t\}]^{1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}}}\,dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{-\frac{1+\nu+\varepsilon}{p}}}{[\max\{1,t\}]^{1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}}}\,dt-\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{m^{\alpha}}}t^{-\frac{1+\nu+\varepsilon}{p}}\,dt

\displaystyle=p\left(\frac{1}{1+\mu}+\frac{1}{p-1-\nu-\varepsilon}\right)-\frac{p}{p-1-\nu-\varepsilon}m^{-\frac{\alpha(p-1-\nu-\varepsilon)}{p}}

\displaystyle:=L(\varepsilon)-Q(m).

Combining (
3.3
), (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we get that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta}\widetilde{a}\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{\beta^{p}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{-1-\alpha\varepsilon}\cdot[L(\varepsilon)-Q(m)]^{p},

for
0<\varepsilon<p-1-\nu
.
By using the Bernoulli’s inequality(see
[
17
]
), we obtain that

(3.7)

[L(\varepsilon)-Q(m)]^{p}\geq[L(\varepsilon)]^{p}\left[1-\frac{p^{2}}{L(\varepsilon)(p-1-\nu-\varepsilon)}m^{-\frac{\alpha(p-1-\nu-\varepsilon)}{p}}\right],

for
0<\varepsilon<p-1-\nu
.
From (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we obtain that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta}\widetilde{a}\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{\beta^{p}}[L(\varepsilon)]^{p}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{-1-\alpha\varepsilon}

\displaystyle-\frac{\varepsilon p^{2}}{\beta^{p}L(\varepsilon)(p-1-\nu-\varepsilon)}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{-1-\alpha\varepsilon-\frac{\alpha(p-1-\nu-\varepsilon)}{p}}.

We note that

(3.9)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{-1-\alpha\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\alpha}(1+o(1)),\,\,\,\,\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+},

and, for
0<\varepsilon<p-1-\nu
,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{-1-\alpha\varepsilon-\frac{\alpha(p-1-\nu-\varepsilon)}{p}}=O(1),\,\,\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}.

It follows from (
3.8
)-(
3.10
) that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta}\widetilde{a}\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha\beta^{p}}(1+o(1))\cdot[L(\varepsilon)]^{p}\cdot[1-\varepsilon O(1)].

Hence, by (
3.2
), we get that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta}\|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\|\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta}\widetilde{a}\|_{p}}{\|\widetilde{a}\|_{p}}\geq\frac{\frac{1}{\alpha^{\frac{1}{p}}\beta}(1+o(1))\cdot[L(\varepsilon)]\cdot[1-\varepsilon O(1)]^{\frac{1}{p}}}{\frac{1}{\beta^{\frac{1}{p}}}(1+o(1))}.

Take
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, we see that

(3.11)

\|\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta}\|\geq\frac{p}{{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{p}}{\beta}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}}\left(\frac{1}{1+\mu}+\frac{1}{p-1-\nu}\right).

Combining (
3.1
) and (
3.11
), we see that (
1.1
) is true and the proof of Theorem
1.3
is finished.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We first prove the if part. If
p(\gamma-1)-(\mu-\nu)\geq 0
, that is
\gamma\geq 1+\frac{\mu-\nu}{p}
, then, for
a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty},\,m\geq 1
, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{(\alpha-1)+\alpha\mu}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}[(\beta-1)-(p^{\prime}-1)\beta\nu]}\cdot n^{-\frac{1+\beta\varepsilon}{p}}}{[\max\{m^{\alpha},n^{\beta}\}]^{\gamma}}\right]^{p}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{s^{-\frac{1+\nu+\varepsilon}{p}}}{[\max\{m^{\alpha},s\}]^{\gamma}}\,ds

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{H}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\widetilde{a}\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle\frac{p^{p}}{[\beta(1+\mu+\varepsilon)]^{p}}\left[\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{\alpha[p(\gamma-1)+(\mu-\nu)-\varepsilon]-1}\right].

(4.5)

\displaystyle+\infty

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\frac{\|\mathbf{H}^{\mu,\nu}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\widetilde{a}\|_{p}^{p}}{\|\widetilde{a}\|_{p}^{p}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1+o(1))\frac{p^{p}}{[\beta(1+\mu+\varepsilon)]^{p}}\left[\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{\alpha[p(1-\gamma)+(\mu-\nu)-\varepsilon]-1}\right].

However, by

, if

Remark 6.1

We first point out that the assumptions

Proposition 6.2
.

\overline{\mathbf{H}}_{\delta}

Proof.

For
\varepsilon>0

\bar{a}_{n}=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}n^{-\frac{1+\varepsilon}{p}}

\|\bar{a}\|_{p}^{p}=\varepsilon+\varepsilon\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n^{-1-\varepsilon}\leq\varepsilon+1,

\|\overline{\mathbf{H}}_{\delta}\bar{a}\|_{p}^{p}=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{\delta}\left[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{-\frac{\delta+1+\varepsilon}{p}}}{\max\{m,n\}}\right]^{p}.

{\bf{I}}
) If
\delta<-1
, when
\varepsilon<-(\delta+1)
, we see from
\delta+1+\varepsilon<0

m\geq 1
, it holds that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{-\frac{\delta+1+\varepsilon}{p}}}{\max\{m,n\}}\geq\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}\frac{n^{-\frac{\delta+1+\varepsilon}{p}}}{n}\geq\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}n^{-1-\frac{\delta+1+\varepsilon}{p}}=+\infty.

This means that
\overline{\mathbf{H}}_{\delta}
is not bounded on
l^{p}
in this case.

(
{\bf{II}}
) If
\delta=-1
or
\delta\geq p-1
, for all
m\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\frac{n^{-\frac{\delta+1+\varepsilon}{p}}}{\max\{m,n\}}\geq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{-\frac{\delta+1+\varepsilon}{p}}}{\max\{m,x\}}\,dx$\hss}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m^{-\frac{\delta+1+\varepsilon}{p}}\int_{\frac{1}{m}}^{\infty}\frac{t^{-\frac{\delta+1+\varepsilon}{p}}}{\max\{1,t\}}\,dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle m^{-\frac{\delta+1+\varepsilon}{p}}\int_{1}^{\infty}t^{-1-\frac{\delta+1+\varepsilon}{p}}\,dt=\frac{p}{1+\delta+\varepsilon}m^{-\frac{\delta+1+\varepsilon}{p}}.

\displaystyle\|\overline{\mathbf{H}}_{\delta}\bar{a}\|_{p}^{p}\geq\left(\frac{p}{1+\delta+\varepsilon}\right)^{p}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(1+o(1)).

\widetilde{f}_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{a}_{n}z^{n}
with

\widetilde{a}_{n}=(\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon})^{\frac{1}{q}}(n+1)^{-\frac{\mathbb{I}_{X}+1+\varepsilon}{q}}.
It is easy to see that
\{\widetilde{a}_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a decreasing sequence and
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+1)^{\mathbb{I}_{X}}\widetilde{a}_{n}^{q}<\infty.
Hence
\widetilde{f}_{\varepsilon}\in X_{q}
. We set

b_{m}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n}}{[\max\{m+1,n+1\}]^{\gamma}},\,m\geq 0.

We suppose that
{\mathbb{H}}_{\gamma}:X_{q}\rightarrow X_{q}
is bounded. Then, by the fact that
\{b_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a decreasing sequence, we see that
g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}\in X_{q}
and hence

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)^{\mathbb{I}_{X}}b_{n}^{q}<+\infty.

That is

(6.1)

\displaystyle+\infty

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)^{\mathbb{I}_{X}}\left[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\widetilde{a}_{n}}{[\max\{m+1,n+1\}]^{\gamma}}\right]^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)^{\mathbb{I}_{X}}\left[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(n+1)^{-\frac{\mathbb{I}_{X}+1+\varepsilon}{q}}}{[\max\{m+1,n+1\}]^{\gamma}}\right]^{q}.

On the other hand, for any
m\geq 0
, we have

(6.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(n+1)^{-\frac{\mathbb{I}_{X}+1+\varepsilon}{q}}}{[\max\{m+1,n+1\}]^{\gamma}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{-\frac{\mathbb{I}_{X}+1+\varepsilon}{q}}}{[\max\{m+1,n\}]^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{-\frac{\mathbb{I}_{X}+1+\varepsilon}{q}}}{[\max\{m+1,x\}]^{\gamma}}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m+1)^{(1-\lambda)-\frac{\mathbb{I}_{X}+1+\varepsilon}{q}}\cdot\int_{\frac{1}{m+1}}^{\infty}\frac{y^{-\frac{\mathbb{I}_{X}+1+\varepsilon}{q}}}{[\max\{1,y\}]^{\gamma}}\,dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(m+1)^{(1-\gamma)-\frac{\mathbb{I}_{X}+1+\varepsilon}{q}}\int_{1}^{\infty}y^{-\gamma-\frac{\mathbb{I}_{X}+1+\varepsilon}{q}}\,dy

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(m+1)^{(1-\gamma)-\frac{\mathbb{I}_{X}+1+\varepsilon}{q}}E(\varepsilon).

Combining (
6.1
) and (
6.2
), we get that

(6.3)

\displaystyle+\infty

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}[E(\varepsilon)]^{q}\left[\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)^{q(1-\gamma)-1-\varepsilon}\right].

But, if
\gamma<1
, when
\varepsilon<q(1-\gamma)
, we have

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(m+1)^{q(1-\gamma)-1-\varepsilon}=+\infty.

Thus (
6.3
) is a contradiction since
E(\varepsilon)>0
. This means that
{\mathbb{H}}_{\gamma}:X_{q}\rightarrow X_{q}
can not be bounded when
\gamma<1
. The proposition is proved.
∎

For
\gamma>0
, let
\lambda
be a positive Borel measure in
[0,1)
, we define the operator

\mathbb{H}_{\gamma,\lambda}(f)(z):=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\Bigg[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{I}_{\gamma,\lambda}[m,n]a_{n}\Bigg]z^{m},\,f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}).

Here

(6.4)

\mathbf{I}_{\gamma,\lambda}[m,n]=\int_{[0,1)}t^{\max\{m,n\}}(1-t)^{\gamma-1}d\lambda(t),\,m,n\geq 0.

When
\lambda
in (
6.4
) is the Lesbegue measure in
[0,1)
, we see that

\mathbf{I}_{\gamma,\lambda}[m,n]\asymp\frac{1}{[\max\{m+1,n+1\}]^{\gamma}},m,n\geq 0.

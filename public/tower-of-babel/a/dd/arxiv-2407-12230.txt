Optimal Padded Decomposition For Bounded Treewidth Graphs


Abstract
A ( β , δ , Δ ) (\beta,\delta,\Delta) -padded decomposition of an edge-weighted graph G = ( V , E , w ) G=(V,E,w) is a stochastic decomposition into clusters of diameter at most Δ \Delta such that for every vertex v ∈ V v\in V , the probability that 𝐁 G ​ ( v , γ ​ Δ ) \mathbf{B}_{G}(v,\gamma\Delta) is entirely contained in the cluster containing v v is at least e − β ​ γ e^{-\beta\gamma} for every γ ∈ [ 0 , δ ] \gamma\in[0,\delta] . Padded decompositions have been studied for decades and have found numerous applications, including metric embedding, multicommodity flow-cut gap, multicut, and zero extension problems, to name a few. In these applications, parameter β \beta , called the padding parameter , is the most important parameter since it decides either the distortion or the approximation ratios. For general graphs with n n vertices, the padding parameter β \beta is known to be in Θ ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) \Theta(\log n) . Klein, Plotkin, and Rao [ 52 ] (KPR) showed that K r K_{r} -minor-free graphs have padding parameter β = O ⁡ ( r 3 ) \beta=O(r^{3}) , which is a significant improvement over general graphs when r r is a constant. However, when r = Ω ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) r=\Omega(\log n) , the padding parameter in KPR decomposition can be much worse than log ⁡ n \log n . A long-standing conjecture is that constructing a padded decomposition for K r K_{r} -minor-free graphs is possible with padding parameter β = O ⁡ ( log ⁡ r ) \beta=O(\log r) . Despite decades of research, the best-known result is β = O ⁡ ( r ) \beta=O(r) , even for graphs with treewidth at most r r . In this work, we make significant progress toward the aforementioned conjecture by showing that graphs with treewidth tw {\rm tw} admit a padded decomposition with padding parameter O ⁡ ( log ⁡ tw ) O(\log{\rm tw}) , which is tight. Our padding parameter is strictly better than O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) O(\log n) whenever tw = n o ⁡ ( 1 ) {\rm tw}=n^{o(1)} , and is never worse than what is known for general graphs. As corollaries, we obtain an exponential improvement in dependency on treewidth in a host of algorithmic applications: O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ⋅ log ⁡ ( tw ) ) O(\sqrt{\log n\cdot\log({\rm tw})}) flow-cut gap, the maxflow-min multicut ratio of O ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( tw ) ) O(\log({\rm tw})) , an O ⁡ ( log ⁡ ( tw ) ) O(\log({\rm tw})) approximation for the 0-extension problem, an ℓ ∞ O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) \ell^{O(\log n)}_{\infty} embedding with distortion O ⁡ ( log ⁡ tw ) O(\log{\rm tw}) , and an O ⁡ ( log ⁡ tw ) O(\log{\rm tw}) bound for integrality gap for the uniform sparsest cut.

Introduction

A basic primitive in designing divide-and-conquer graph algorithms is partitioning a graph into clusters such that there are only a few edges between clusters. This type of primitive has been extensively applied in the design of divide-and-conquer algorithms. Since guaranteeing a few edges crossing different clusters in the worst case could be very expensive, we often seek a good guarantee in a probabilistic sense: the probability that two vertices
u
and
v
are placed into two different clusters is proportional to
d_{G}(u,v)/\Delta
where
d_{G}(u,v)
is the distance between
u
and
v
in the input graph
G
and
\Delta
is the upper bound on the diameter of each cluster. A (stochastic) partition of
V(G)
with this property is called a
separating decomposition
of
G

[
32
]
.

In this work, we study a stronger notion of stochastic decomposition, called
padded decomposition
. More formally, given a weighted graph
G=(V,E,w)
, a partition is
\Delta
-
bounded
if the diameter of every cluster is at most
\Delta
. A distribution
\mathcal{D}
over partitions is called a
(\beta,\delta,\Delta)
-
padded decomposition
, if every partition in the support is
\Delta
-bounded, and for every vertex
v\in V
and
\gamma\in[0,\delta]
, we have:

\Pr[\mathbf{B}_{G}(v,\gamma\Delta)\subseteq P(v)]\geq e^{-\beta\gamma}\qquad\text{where $P(v)$ is the cluster containing $v$.}

(1)

That is, the probability that the entire ball
\mathbf{B}_{G}(v,\gamma\Delta)
of radius
\gamma\Delta
around
v
is clustered together, is at least
e^{-\beta\gamma}
. If
G
admits a
(\beta,\delta,\Delta)
-padded decomposition for every
\Delta>0
, we say that
G
admits
(\beta,\delta)
-padded decomposition scheme. The parameter
\beta
is usually referred to as a padding parameter.

In an influential work, Klein, Plotkin and Rao
[
52
]
showed that every
K_{r}
minor free graph admits a weak
\left(O(r^{3}),\Omega(1)\right)
-padded decomposition scheme; the padding parameter is
O(r^{3})
. This result has found numerous algorithmic applications for solving problems in
K_{r}
-minor-free graphs; a few examples are the flow-cut gap of
O(r^{3})
for uniform multicommodity flow
[
52
]
, extending Lipschitz functions with absolute extendability of
O(r^{3})

[
57
]
, the maxflow-min multicut ratio of
O(r^{3})
for the multicommodity flow with maximum total commodities
[
69
]
, an
O(r^{3}\log\log(n))
approximation for minimum linear arrangement, minimum containing interval graphs
[
66
]
, an
O(r^{3})
approximation for the 0-extension problem
[
15
]
, and an
O(r^{3}\log n)
-approximation for the minimum bisection problem
[
29
]
. An important takeaway is that key parameters quantifying the quality of these applications depend (linearly) on the padding parameter; any improvement to the padding parameter would imply the same improvement in the applications.

Fakcharoenphol and Talwar
[
28
]
improved the padding parameter of
K_{r}
minor free graphs to
O(r^{2})
. Abraham, Gavoille, Gupta, Neiman, and Talwar
[
3
]
(see also
[
32
]
) improved the padding parameter to
O(r)
. These improvements imply an
O(r)
dependency on the minor size of all aforementioned applications. The only lower bound is
\Omega(\log r)
coming from the fact that
r
-vertex expanders (trivially) exclude
K_{r}
as a minor while having padding parameter
\Omega(\log r)

[
8
]
. Closing the gap between the upper bound of
O(r)
and the lower bound

has been an outstanding problem asked by various authors
[
28
,
56
,
3
,
32
]
.

Conjecture 1.1
.

There exists a padded decomposition of any
K_{r}
-minor-free metric with padding parameter
\beta=O(\log r)
.

Progress on
Conjecture

1.1
has been made on very special classes of minor-free graphs. Specifically, graphs with
pathwidth

{\rm pw}
admit padding parameter
O(\log{\rm pw})

[
3
]
. This result implies that
n
-vertex graphs with treewidth
{\rm tw}
admit padding parameter
O(\log{\rm tw}+\log\log n)
, since the pathwidth of graphs of treewidth
{\rm tw}
is
O({\rm tw}\cdot\log(n))
(also see
[
49
]
). However, the padding parameter depends on

. Thus, a significant step towards
Conjecture

1.1
is to show that graphs of treewidth
{\rm tw}
admit a padded decomposition with padding parameter
O(\log{\rm tw})
. The best-known result (without the dependency on
n
) for small treewidth graphs is the same as minor-free graphs, implied by the fact that such graphs of treewidth
{\rm tw}
exclude
K_{{\rm tw}+2}
as a minor. Our first main result is to prove
Conjecture

1.1
for the special case of treewidth-
{\rm tw}
graphs:

Theorem 1.2
.

Every weighted graph
G
with treewidth
{\rm tw}
admits a
\left(O(\log{\rm tw}),\Omega(1)\right)
-padded decomposition scheme. Furthermore, such a partition can be sampled efficiently.

Our
Theorem

1.2
implies that we could replace

with
O(\log{\rm tw})
in the aforementioned problems when the input graphs have treewidth
{\rm tw}
:
O(\log({\rm tw}))
for uniform multicommodity flow-cut gap, extending Lipschitz functions with absolute extendability of
O(\log({\rm tw}))
, the maxflow-min multicut ratio of
O(\log({\rm tw}))
, an
O(\log({\rm tw})\log\log(n))
approximation for minimum linear arrangement, minimum containing interval graphs, an
O(\log({\rm tw}))
approximation for the 0-extension problem, and an
O(\log({\rm tw})\log n)
-approximation for the minimum bisection problem. Furthermore, we obtain the first

embedding of treewidth-
{\rm tw}
metrics with distortion
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\log{\rm tw}\cdot\log n})
, an
\ell^{O(\log n)}_{\infty}
embedding with distortion
O(\log{\rm tw})
, and
O(\log{\rm tw})
bound for integrality gap for the uniform sparsest cut. For several of these problems, for example, uniform multicommodity flow-cut gap, maxflow-min multicut ratio, and 0-extension, our results provide the state-of-the-art approximation ratio for an entire range of parameter
{\rm tw}
, even when

. We refer readers to
Section

Here we point out another connection to
Conjecture

1.1
. Filtser and Le
[

showed that one can embed any

-minor-free metric of diameter

into a graph with treewidth

. Even an embedding with a treewidth

Sparse Covers.

A related notion to padded decompositions is
sparse cover
.
A collection
\mathcal{C}
of clusters is a
(\beta,s,\Delta)
-sparse cover if it is
\Delta
-bounded, each ball of radius
\frac{\Delta}{\beta}
is contained in some cluster, and each vertex belongs to at most
s
different clusters.
A graph admits
(\beta,s)
-sparse cover scheme if it admits
(\beta,s,\Delta)
-sparse cover for every
\Delta>0
.

Sparse covers have been studied for various classes of graphs, such as general graphs
[
7
]
, planar graphs
[
14
]
, minor-free graphs
[
53
,
14
,
4
]
, and doubling metrics
[
32
]
.

By simply taking the union of many independently drawn copies of padded decomposition, one can construct a sparse cover. Indeed, given
(\beta,\delta,\Delta)
-padded decomposition, by taking the union of
O(e^{\beta\gamma}\log n)
partitions (for
\gamma\leq\delta
) one will obtain w.h.p. a
(\gamma,O(e^{\beta\gamma}\log n),\Delta)
-sparse cover. In particular, using
Theorem

1.2
one can construct
(O(1),O(e^{\log{\rm tw}}\log n))=(O(1),{\rm tw}^{O(1)}\log n))
-sparse cover scheme. The main question in this context is whether one can construct sparse covers for bounded treewidth graphs with constant cover parameter (
\beta
) and sparseness (
s
) independent from
n
. If one is willing to sacrifice a (quadratic) dependency on
{\rm tw}
on the cover parameter
\beta
, then a sparse cover with parameters independent of
n
is known
[
52
,
28
,
2
]
. However, in many applications, for example, constructing sparse spanners, it is desirable to have
\beta=O(1)
as it directly governs the stretch of the spanners.

Theorem 1.3
.

Every graph
G
with treewidth
{\rm tw}
admits a
\left(6,\mathrm{poly}({\rm tw})\right)
-sparse cover scheme.

It is sometimes useful to represent the sparse cover
\mathcal{C}
as a union of partitions, for example, in
metric embeddings
[
53
]
, and the construction of ultrametric covers
[
37
,
30
]
,
leading to the notion of
padded partition cover scheme
:

Definition 1.4

(Padded Partition Cover Scheme)
.

A collection of partitions
\mathcal{P}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{P}_{\tau}

is
(\beta,s,\Delta)
-padded partition cover if (a)
\tau\leq s
, (b) every partition
\mathcal{P}_{i}
is
\Delta
-bounded, and (c) for every point
x
, there is a cluster
C
in one of the partitions
\mathcal{P}_{i}
such that
B(x,\frac{\Delta}{\beta})\subseteq C
.

A space
(X,d_{X})
admits a
(\beta,s)
-
padded partition cover scheme
if for every
\Delta
, it admits a
(\beta,s,\Delta)
-padded partition cover.

While a padded partition cover implies a sparse cover with the same parameters, the reverse direction is not true. For example, graphs with pathwidth
{\rm pw}
admit
(10,5({\rm pw}+1))
-sparse cover scheme
[
33
]
, however, they are only known to admit
(O({\rm pw}^{2}),2^{{\rm pw}+1})
-padded partition cover scheme (this is due to
K_{r}
-minor free graphs
[
52
,
28
]
(see also
[

,

]
)).

Tree-Ordered Net.

A key new technical insight to all of our aforementioned results is the notion of
tree-ordered net
(
Definition

1.6
). A tree order net is analogous to the notion of
nets
.
Nets
were used extensively in designing algorithms for metric spaces. More formally, a
\Delta
-net is a set of points
N
such that every two net points are at a distance at least
\Delta
(i.e
\min_{x,y\in N}d_{X}(x,y)\geq\Delta
), and every point has a net point at a distance at most
\Delta
(i.e.
\max_{x\in V}\min_{y\in N}d_{X}(x,y)\leq\Delta
). Filtser
[
32
]
showed that if there is a
\Delta
-net such that every ball of radius
3\Delta
contains at most
\tau
net points, then the metric admits a
\left(O(\log\tau),\Omega(1),O(\Delta)\right)
-padded decomposition. This result implies that metrics of doubling dimension
d
have padding parameter
O(d)
since doubling metrics have sparse nets:
\tau=2^{O(d)}
. Unfortunately, graphs of small treewidth do not have sparse nets; this holds even in very simple graphs such as star graphs. Nonetheless, we show that small treewidth graphs possess a structure almost as good: a net that is sparse w.r.t. some partial order. We formalize this property via tree-ordered nets. As we will later show, a sparse tree-ordered net is enough to construct the padded decomposition scheme; see
Section

3
. We believe that the notion of a tree-ordered net is of independent interest.

Tree-Order.

Another important insight, is that of tree orders. A tree order
\preceq
of a set
V
is a partial order (i.e. transitive, reflexive, and antisymmetric) associated with a rooted tree
T
and a map
\varphi:V\rightarrow V(T)
such that
u\preceq v
iff
\varphi(v)
is an ancestor of
\varphi(u)
in
T
. Given a weighted graph
G=(V,E,w)
, a tree order
\preceq
w.r.t. tree
T
is a
valid order
of
G
if for every edge
\{u,v\}\in E
, it holds that
u\preceq v
or
v\preceq u
or both (i.e.
v
is an ancestor of
u
or
u
is an ancestor of
v
). A simple consequence of the validity is that every connected subset
C
in
G
must contain a maximum element w.r.t
\preceq
; see
Section

3
.
For a vertex
v\in V
, and subset
S\subseteq V
let
S_{v\preceq}=\{u\in S\mid v\preceq u\}
be the ancestors of
v
w.r.t.
T
in
S
. Similarly, let
S_{\preceq x}=\{u\in S\mid u\preceq x\}
be all the descendants of
x
in
S
.

Definition 1.6
.

Given a weighted graph
G=(V,E,w)
and parameters
\tau,\alpha,\Delta>0
, a
(\tau,\alpha,\Delta)
-tree-ordered net is a triple
(N,T,\varphi)
where
N\subseteq V
, and
T
and
\varphi
define a tree order
\preceq
of
V
such that for every
v\in V
:

•

Covering.
There is
x\in N_{v\preceq}
such that
d_{G[V_{\preceq x}]}(v,x)\leq\Delta
. That is, there exists an ancestor
x
of
v
in
N
such that the distance from
v
to
x
in the subgraph of
G
induced by descendants of
x
is at most
\Delta
.

•

Packing.
Denote by
N_{v\preceq}^{\alpha\Delta}=\left\{x\in N_{v\preceq}\mid d_{G[V_{\preceq x}]}(v,x)\leq\alpha\Delta\right\}
the set of ancestor centers of
v
at distance at most
\alpha\Delta
from
v
(w.r.t the subgraphs induced by descendants of the ancestors. Then
\left|N_{v\preceq}^{\alpha\Delta}\right|\leq\tau
.

While we state our main result in terms of treewidth, it will be more convenient to use the notion of
bounded tree-partition width

[
25
]
. We will show that graphs of bounded tree-partition width admit a small tree-ordered net.

Definition 1.7

(Tree Partition)
.

A
tree partition
of a graph
G=(V,E)
is a rooted tree
\mathcal{T}
whose vertices are bijectively associated with the sets of
partition

\mathcal{S}=\{S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{m}\}
of
V
, called
bags
, such that for each
(u,v)\in E
, there exists a
S_{i},S_{j}\in\mathcal{S}
such that
S_{j}
is the parent of
S_{i}
and
\{u,v\}\subseteq S_{i}\cup S_{j}
. The width of
\mathcal{T}
is
\max_{i\in m}\{|S_{i}|\}
.

Unlike a tree decomposition, bags of a tree partition are disjoint. Therefore, graphs of bounded-tree partition width have a more restricted structure than graphs of bounded treewidth. Indeed, one can show that any graphs of tree-partition width
k
have treewidth at most
2k-1
. However, from a metric point of view, graphs of bounded treewidth are the same as graphs of bounded tree-partition width: We could convert a tree decomposition into a tree partition by making copies of vertices; see
Lemma

2.2
. We will show in
Section

4
that:

Lemma 1.8
.

Every weighted graph
G=(V,E,w)
with a tree-partition width
{\rm tp}
admits a
(\mathrm{poly}({\rm tp}),3,\Delta)
-tree-ordered net, for every
\Delta>0
.

In
Section

3
, we show how to use the tree-ordered net in
Lemma

1.8
to obtain all results stated above.

Follow-up Work.

Recently, inspired by our technique,
[
44
]
constructed a tree cover with stretch
1+\varepsilon
and
2^{({\rm tw}/\varepsilon)^{O({\rm tw})}}
trees. This result has applications to constructing distance oracles and approximate labeling schemes for graphs of small treewidth. Filtser
[
31
]
showed recently that
K_{r}
-minor-free graphs admit a
(4+\varepsilon,O(1/\varepsilon)^{r})
-sparse cover scheme for every
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. While the stretch is an absolute constant, the dependency on the minor size is exponential and hence is incomparable to our
Theorem

1.3
.

In a subsequent work
[
22
]
, Conroy and Filtser answered the main open question left by this paper (see
Section

5.8
), and showed that every
K_{r}
-minor-free graph admits a padded decomposition with padding parameter
O(\log r)
. Conroy and Filtser have been inspired by the techniques developed in the current paper. Specifically, they use the buffered cup decomposition of
[
17
]
and, in the spirit of the current paper, recursively remove many supernodes at once to reduce the treewidth of each remaining component. Conroy and Filtser establish their result by showing the existence of good sparse covers for
K_{r}
-minor-free graphs and a general reduction from sparse cover to padded decomposition.

Preliminaries

Graphs.

We consider connected undirected graphs
G=(V,E)
with edge weights
w:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. We say that vertices
v
and
u
are neighbors if
\{v,u\}\in E
. Let
d_{G}
denote the shortest path metric in
G
.
\mathbf{B}_{G}(v,r)=\{u\in V\mid d_{G}(v,u)\leq r\}
is the ball of radius
r
around
v
. For a vertex
v\in V
and a subset
A\subseteq V
, let
d_{G(x,A)}:=\min_{a\in A}d_{G}(x,a)
, where
d_{G}(x,\emptyset)=\infty
. For a subset of vertices
A\subseteq V
, let
G[A]
denote the induced graph on
A
, and let
G\setminus A:=G[V\setminus A]
.

The
diameter
of a graph
G
is
\mathrm{diam}(G)=\max_{v,u\in V}d_{G}(v,u)
, i.e. the maximal distance between a pair of vertices.
Given a subset
A\subseteq V
, the
weak
-diameter of
A
is
\mathrm{diam}_{G}(A)=\max_{v,u\in A}d_{G}(v,u)
, i.e. the maximal distance between a pair of vertices in
A
, w.r.t. to original distances
d_{G}
. The
strong
-diameter of
A
is
\mathrm{diam}(G[A])
, the diameter of the graph induced by
A
.
A graph
H
is a
minor
of a graph
G
if we can obtain
H
from

G
by edge deletions/contractions, and isolated vertex deletions. A graph
family
\mathcal{G}
is
H
-minor-free
if no graph

G\in\mathcal{G}
has
H
as a minor. We will drop the prefix
H
in
H
-minor-free whenever
H
is not important or clear from the context.

Some examples of minor-free graphs are planar graphs (
K_{5}
- and
K_{3,3}
-minor-free), outer-planar graphs (
K_{4}
- and
K_{3,2}
-minor-free), series-parallel graphs (
K_{4}
-minor-free) and trees (
K_{3}
-minor-free).

Treewidth.

A
tree decomposition
of a graph
G=(V,E)
is a tree
\mathcal{T}
where each node
x\in\mathcal{T}
is associated with a subset
S_{x}
of
V
, called a
bag
, such that: (i)
\cup_{x\in V(\mathcal{T})}S_{x}=V
, (ii) for every edge
(u,v)\in E
, there exists a bag
S_{x}
for some
x\in V(\mathcal{T})
such that
\{u,v\}\subseteq S
, and (iii) for every
u\in V
, the bags containing
u
induces a connected subtree of
\mathcal{T}
. The

of

(Padded Decomposition)

From Tree Decomposition to Tree Partition

We first show that any graph of treewidth

Lemma 2.2
.

into a graph with tree partition of width

Proof 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a tree decomposition of width at most
r
of

. We create a graph

and its tree partition as follows (see
Figure

2
). For each
u\in V
, if

appears in

and replace

in bag

with its copies

. This defines the set of vertices

of

.

If

and

\Pr\left[\mathcal{F}_{i}\right]=\Pr\left[\delta_{i}\geq\rho\right]=\int_{\tilde{\rho}}^{\beta}\frac{\lambda\cdot e^{-\lambda y}}{e^{-\lambda}-e^{-\beta\lambda}}dy=\frac{e^{-\tilde{\rho}\cdot\lambda}-e^{-\beta\lambda}}{e^{-\lambda}-e^{-\beta\lambda}}~.

Let
v_{i}\in B
by the closest vertex to
x_{i}
w.r.t.
G[V_{\preceq x_{i}}]
. Note that
d_{G[V_{\preceq x_{i}}]}(x_{i},v_{i})=\rho\cdot\Delta
.
Then for every
u\in B
it holds that

d_{G[V_{\preceq x_{i}}]}(u,x_{i})\leq d_{G[V_{\preceq x_{i}}]}(v_{i},x_{i})+2\gamma\Delta=\left(\rho+2\gamma\right)\cdot\Delta~.

Therefore, if
\delta_{i}\geq\rho+2\gamma
, the entire ball
B
will be contained in
C_{i}
.
We conclude that:

\displaystyle\Pr\left[\mathcal{C}_{i}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\Pr\left[\rho\leq\delta_{i}<\rho+2\gamma\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{\rho}}^{\min\left\{\beta,\rho+2\gamma\right\}}\frac{\lambda\cdot e^{-\lambda y}}{e^{-\lambda}-e^{-\beta\lambda}}dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{-\tilde{\rho}\cdot\lambda}-e^{-\left(\tilde{\rho}+2\gamma\right)\cdot\lambda}}{e^{-\lambda}-e^{-\beta\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\left(1-e^{-2\gamma\cdot\lambda}\right)\cdot\frac{e^{-\tilde{\rho}\cdot\lambda}}{e^{-\lambda}-e^{-\beta\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\left(1-e^{-2\gamma\cdot\lambda}\right)\cdot\left(\Pr\left[\mathcal{F}_{i}\right]+\frac{e^{-\beta\lambda}}{e^{-\lambda}-e^{-\beta\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1-e^{-2\gamma\cdot\lambda}\right)\cdot\left(\Pr\left[\mathcal{F}_{i}\right]+\frac{1}{e^{(\beta-1)\cdot\lambda}-1}\right)~.

We now bound the probability that the ball
B
is cut. As the events
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\}_{x_{i}\in N}
are disjoint, we have that

\displaystyle\Pr\left[\cup_{i}\mathcal{C}_{i}\right]=\sum_{x_{i}\in N_{B}}\Pr\left[\mathcal{C}_{i}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-e^{-2\gamma\cdot\lambda}\right)\cdot\sum_{x_{i}\in N_{B}}\left(\Pr\left[\mathcal{F}_{i}\right]+\frac{1}{e^{(\beta-1)\cdot\lambda}-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-e^{-2\gamma\cdot\lambda}\right)\cdot\left(1+\frac{\tau}{e^{(\beta-1)\cdot\lambda}-1}\right)\qquad\mbox{(by \lx@cref{creftypecap~refnum}{clm:NB-size})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-e^{-2\gamma\cdot\lambda}\right)\cdot\left(1+e^{-2\gamma\cdot\lambda}\right)=1-e^{-4\gamma\cdot\lambda}~,

where the last inequality follows as

e^{-2\gamma\lambda}=\frac{e^{-2\gamma\lambda}\left(e^{(\beta-1)\cdot\lambda}-1\right)}{e^{(\beta-1)\cdot\lambda}-1}\overset{(\#)}{\geq}\frac{e^{-2\gamma\lambda}\cdot e^{(\beta-1)\cdot\lambda}\cdot\frac{1}{2}}{e^{(\beta-1)\cdot\lambda}-1}\overset{(*)}{\geq}\frac{\frac{1}{2}\cdot e^{\frac{\alpha-1}{4}\cdot\lambda}}{e^{(\beta-1)\cdot\lambda}-1}=\frac{\tau}{e^{(\beta-1)\cdot\lambda}-1}~.

The inequality
(#)
only holds if
(\beta-1)\lambda\geq\ln(2)
, which is the case since
(\beta-1)\alpha=\left(\frac{\alpha+1}{2}-1\right)\cdot\frac{4}{\alpha-1}\cdot\ln(2\tau)=2\ln(2\tau)\geq\ln(2)
.

The inequality
(∗)
holds as
\beta-1-2\gamma\geq\frac{\alpha+1}{2}-1-2\cdot\frac{\alpha-1}{8}=\frac{\alpha-1}{4}
.

To conclude, we obtain a partition where each cluster has diameter at most
2\beta\Delta\leq(\alpha+1)\cdot\Delta
, and for every
\gamma\leq\frac{\alpha-1}{8}
, every ball of radius

\gamma\cdot\Delta=\frac{\gamma}{\alpha+1}\cdot(\alpha+1)\cdot\Delta

is cut with probability at most
e^{-4\gamma\cdot\lambda}=e^{-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha+1}\cdot(\alpha+1)\cdot 4\cdot\lambda}
.
Thus the padding parameter is
(\alpha+1)\cdot 4\cdot\lambda=\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha-1}\cdot 16\cdot\ln(2\tau)
,
while the guarantee holds for balls of radius up to
\frac{1}{\alpha+1}\cdot\frac{\alpha-1}{8}
.
The lemma now follows.

\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
be a connected component of uncovered bags in a specific round. Let

be a set of vertices, called the

associated with
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. Note that

. The following lemma implies that clusters over different rounds form a hierarchy.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
be two different connected components of uncovered bags. If
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
belong to the same round, then

. If
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
belong to two consecutive rounds such that
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is a subtree of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, then
C_{\mathcal{T}_{2}}\subseteq C_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}
.

Proof 4.5
.

We prove the lemma by induction. The base case holds since in round 1, we only have a single tree
T
whose associated cluster
C_{\mathcal{T}}=V(G)
. Let
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
be a connected component of uncovered bags at round
i
, and
\mathcal{T}_{1},\mathcal{T}_{2}
be two different components of uncovered bags that are subtrees of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
in round

. It suffices to show that

for any

, and
C_{\mathcal{T}_{1}}\cap C_{\mathcal{T}_{2}}=\emptyset
.

Observe that in a round

, the attachment of a bag

either shrinks (due to the removal in

) or grows by the addition of the cores. As any core

created from

,

in both cases. This means all attachments of bags in
\mathcal{T}_{1}
are subsets of

, implying that

. The same argument gives

.

Let

and

be the root bag of
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
, respectively. Assume w.l.o.g that

is an ancestor of

. Let

that is the ancestor of

. By

. Suppose that there exists a vertex

. Let

be the child bag of

. Then we have that

, and

is the center bag of a core

. When

was created, all the uncovered vertices in

were marked covered. This means

. Thus,

in round

The Semi-tree-ordered Net.

We now construct a tree ordering of the vertices using
\mathcal{R}
. We define the rank of a core
R
, denoted by
\mathrm{rank}(R)
, to be the round number when
R
is constructed. That is,
\mathrm{rank}(R)=i
if

was constructed in the
i
-th round. Lastly, for a vertex

, we define

to be the center bag of the
smallest-rank core
containing
v
. We observe that:

{observation}

The core

covering

for the first time is the smallest-rank core containing

.

We now define the tree ordering of vertices, and the tree-ordered net as follows.

Definition 4.6

(Tree Ordering and Tree-Ordered Net)
.

Let

be the rooted tree that is
isomorphic
to the tree partition
\mathcal{T}
of
G
; each node

corresponds to a bag

in
\mathcal{T}
.

•

The map

maps each vertex

such that its corresponding bag

is exactly

. This map naturally induces a semi-tree ordering of vertices in

if and only if

is an ancestor of

in the tree partition
\mathcal{T}
.

•

The tree-ordered net

is the union of all the centers of the cores in
\mathcal{R}
. Recall that the centers of a core is the set of uncovered vertices of the center bag in
Algorithm

2
.

The semi-tree-ordered net for

is

.

The following lemma stems directly from the description of the algorithm and the discussion above.

Lemma 4.7
.

Given the tree partition
\mathcal{T}
of
G
, the semi-tree-ordered net
(N,T,\varphi)
can be constructed in polynomial time.

We can see that the tree ordered induced by

and

is a semi-tree-order since it does not have the antisymmetric property: there could be two different vertices

and

such that

and hence they will be mapped to the same vertex of

via

.

The packing property is substantially more difficult to prove. Our argument goes roughly as follows. First, we establish several structural properties of the cores. In particular, we show that for every bag
B
in
T
, the number of cores intersecting
B
is at most
O(\mathrm{tp}^{2})
, and that each vertex
v
belongs to at most
O(\mathrm{tp})
cores (
Corollary

4.14
). This allows us to bound
\left|N_{v\preceq}^{2\Delta}\right|
via bounding the number of cores that contain at least one vertex in
N_{v\preceq}^{2\Delta}
. This set of cores can be partitioned into two sets: those that contain
v
—there are only
O({\rm tp})
of them—and those that do not contain
v
. To bound the size of the latter set, we basically construct a sequence of cores of strictly increasing ranks
R_{1}^{*},R_{2}^{*},\ldots,R_{\ell}^{*}
for
\ell\leq{\rm tp}
, and for each
R_{j}^{*}
, show that there are only
O({\rm tp}^{2})
ancestral cores of
R_{j}^{*}
that are not ancestors of lower ranked cores in the sequence. This implies a bound of
O({\rm tp}^{3})
on the set of cores, giving the packing property.

We begin by analyzing several properties of the cores.

Lemma 4.10
.

The cores of the same rank are vertex-disjoint.

.

Let

and

. Let

Suppose for contradiction that there exists

. By

. As

is an ancestor of

In this section, we give a more detailed exposition of the applications of

5.1
Flow Sparsifier

Given an edge-capacitated graph
G=(V,E,c)
and a set
K\subseteq V
of terminals, a
K
-flow is a flow where all the endpoints are terminals. A flow-sparsifier with quality
\rho\geq 1
is another capacitated graph
H=(K,E_{H},c_{H})
such that (a) any feasible
K
-flow in
G
can be feasibly routed in
H
, and (b) any feasible
K
-flow in
H
can be routed in
G
with congestion
\rho
(see
[
26
]
for formal definitions).

Englert
et al.
[
26
]
showed that given a graph
G
which admits a
(\beta,\frac{1}{\beta})
-padded decomposition scheme, then for any subset
K
, one can efficiently compute a flow-sparsifier with
quality
O(\beta)
. Using their result, we obtain the following corollary of
Theorem

1.2
.

Corollary 5.1
.

Given an edge-capacitated graph
G=(V,E,c)
with treewidth
{\rm tw}
, and a subset of terminals
K\subseteq V
, one can efficiently compute a flow sparsifier with quality
O(\log{\rm tw})
.

The best previously known result had quality
O({\rm tw})
approximation
[
26
,
3
]
. Thus, our result improves the dependency on
{\rm tw}
exponentially. For further reading on flow sparsifiers, see
[
61
,
62
,
60
,
18
,
20
,
5
]
.

We note that, though the graph
G
has treewidth
{\rm tw}
, the flow-sparsifier
H
in
Corollary

5.1
can have arbitrarily large treewidth. Having low treewidth is a very desirable property of a graph, and naturally, we would like to have a sparsifier of small treewidth. A flow-sparsifier
H=(K,E_{H},c_{H})
of
G
is called
minor-based
if
H
is a minor of
G
. That is,
H
can be obtained from
G
by deleting/contracting edges, and deleting vertices. Englert
et al.
[
26
]
showed that given a graph
G
which admits a
(\beta,\frac{1}{\beta})
-padded decomposition scheme, then for any subset
K
, one can efficiently compute a minor-based flow-sparsifier with
quality
O(\beta\log\beta)
. By
Theorem

1.2
, we obtain:

Corollary 5.2
.

Given an edge-capacitated graph
G=(V,E,c)
with treewidth
{\rm tw}
, and a subset of terminals
K\subseteq V
, one can efficiently compute a minor-based flow-sparsifier
H=(K,E_{H},c_{H})
with quality
O(\log{\rm tw}\log\log{\rm tw})
. In particular,
H
also has treewidth at most
{\rm tw}
.

5.2
Sparse Partition

Given a metric space
(X,d_{X})
,
a
(\alpha,\tau,\Delta)
-sparse partition
is a partition
\mathcal{C}
of
X
such that:

•

Low Diameter:

\forall C\in\mathcal{C}
, the set
X
has diameter at most
\Delta
;

•

Sparsity:

\forall x\in X
, the ball
B_{X}(x,\frac{\Delta}{\alpha})
intersects at most
\tau
clusters from
\mathcal{C}
.

We say that the metric
(X,d_{X})
admits a
(\alpha,\tau)
-sparse partition scheme if for every
\Delta>0
,
X
admits a
(\alpha,\tau,\Delta)
-sparse partition.

Jia
et al.
[
48
]
implicitly proved (see
[
33
]
for an explicit proof) that if a space admits a
(\beta,s)
-sparse cover scheme, then it admits a
(\beta,s)
-sparse partition scheme. Therefore, by
Theorem

1.3
we obtain:

Corollary 5.3
.

Every graph
G
with treewidth
{\rm tw}
admits a
\left(O(1),\mathrm{poly}({\rm tw})\right)
-sparse partition scheme.

Previously, it was only known that graphs with treewidth
{\rm tw}
admit
\left(O({\rm tw}^{2}),2^{{\rm tw}}\right)
-sparse partition scheme
[
28
,
33
]
. Thus,
Corollary

5.3
implies an exponential improvement in the dependency on
{\rm tw}
. For further reading on sparse partitions, see
[
48
,
13
,
33
,
23
,
31
]
.

5.3
Universal Steiner Tree and Universal TSP

We consider the problem of designing a network that allows a server to broadcast a message to a single set of clients. If sending a message over a link incurs some cost, then designing the best broadcast network is classically modeled as the Steiner tree problem
[
47
]
. However, if the server has to solve this problem repeatedly with different client sets, it is desirable to construct a single network that will optimize the cost of the broadcast for every possible subset of clients. This setting motivates the
Universal Steiner Tree
(
UST
) problem.

Given a metric space
(X,d_{X})
and root
r\in X
, a
\rho
-approximate
UST
is a weighted tree
T
over
X
such that for every
S\subseteq X
containing
r
, we have

\displaystyle w(T\{S\})\leq\rho\cdot\text{OPT}_{S}

where
T\{S\}\subseteq T
is the minimal subtree of
T
connecting
S
, and
\text{OPT}_{S}
is the minimum weight Steiner tree connecting
S
in
X
.

A closely related problem to
UST
is the
Universal Traveling Salesman Problem
(
UTSP
).
Consider a postman providing post service for a set
X
of clients with
n
different locations (with distance measure
d_{X}
). Each morning, the postman receives a subset
S\subset X
of the required deliveries for the day. In order to minimize the total tour length, one solution may be to compute each morning an (approximation of an) Optimal
TSP
tour for the set
S
. An alternative solution will be to compute a
Universal
TSP

(
UTSP
) tour
R
containing all the points
X
. Given a subset
S
,
R\{S\}
is the tour visiting all the points in
S
w.r.t. the order induced by
R
.
Given a tour
T
, denote its length by
|T|
. The
stretch
of
R
is the maximum ratio among all subsets
S\subseteq X
between the length of
R\{S\}
and the length of the optimal
TSP
tour on
S
,
\max_{S\subseteq X}\frac{|R\{S\}|}{|\mbox{Opt}(S)|}
.

Jia
et al.
[
48
]
showed that for every
n
-point metric space that admits
(\sigma,\tau)
-sparse partition scheme, there is a polynomial time algorithm that given a root
\mbox{\rm rt}\in V
computes a
UST
with stretch
O(\tau\sigma^{2}\log_{\tau}n)
.
In addition, Jia
et al.
[
48
]
also showed that such a metric admit a
UTSP
with stretch
O(\tau\sigma^{2}\log_{\tau}n)
.
Using our
Corollary

5.3
, we conclude:

Corollary 5.4
.

Consider an
n
-point graph with treewidth
{\rm tw}
. Then its shortest path metric admits a solution to both universal Steiner tree and universal TSP with stretch
\mathrm{poly}({\rm tw})\cdot\log n
.

The best-known previous result for both problems had a stretch of
\exp({\rm tw})\cdot\log n

[
33
]
, which is exponentially larger than ours in terms of the
{\rm tw}
dependency. For further reading on the
UTSP
and
TSP
problems, see
[
64
,
9
,
48
,
42
,
45
,
68
,
40
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
33
,
31
]
. Interestingly, our solution to the
UST
problem in
Corollary

5.4
produces a solution which is not a subgraph of the low treewidth input graph
G
. If one requires that the
UST
will be a subgraph of
G
, the current state of the are is by Busch
et al.
[
12
]
who obtained stretch
O(\log^{7}n)
. To date, this is also the best known upper bound for graph with bounded treewidth.

5.4
Steiner Point Removal

Given a graph
G=(V,E,w)
, and a subset of terminals
K\subseteq V
, in the Steiner point removal problem, we are looking for a graph
H=(K,E_{H},w_{H})
, which is a minor of
G
. We say that
H
has stretch
t
, if for every
u,v\in K
,
d_{G}(u,v)\leq d_{H}(u,v)\leq t\cdot d_{G}(u,v)
.
Englert
et al.
[
26
]
showed that given a graph
G
which admits a
(\beta,\frac{1}{\beta})
-padded decomposition scheme, one can compute a distribution
\mathcal{D}
over minors
H=(K,E_{H},w_{H})
, such that for every
u,v\in K
, and
H\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{D})
,
d_{G}(u,v)\leq d_{H}(u,v)
, and
{\mathbb{E}}_{H\sim\mathcal{D}}[d_{H}(u,v)]\leq O(\beta\cdot\log\beta)\cdot d_{G}(u,v)
. That is, the minor has expected stretch
O(\beta\log(\beta))
. Thus, by
Theorem

1.2
, we obtain:

Corollary 5.5
.

Given a weighted graph
G=(V,E,w)
with treewidth
{\rm tw}
, and a subset of terminals
K\subseteq V
, one can efficiently sample a minor
H=(K,E_{H},c_{H})
of
G
such that for every
u,v\in K
,
d_{G}(u,v)\leq d_{H}(u,v)
, and
{\mathbb{E}}[d_{H}(u,v)]\leq O(\log{\rm tw}\log\log{\rm tw})\cdot d_{G}(u,v)
.

The best previously known result had expected stretch
O({\rm tw}\log{\rm tw})

[
26
,
3
]
.
For the classic Steiner point removal, where we are looking for a single minor, the stretch is
2^{O({\rm tw}\log{\rm tw})}

[
44
]
. For further reading on the Steiner point removal problem, see
[
41
,
16
,
50
,
19
,
34
,
35
,
33
,
46
,
44
]
.

5.5
Zero Extension

In the
0
-Extension
problem, the input is a set
X
, a terminal set
K\subseteq X
,
a metric
d_{K}
on
K
, and a cost function

c\colon\binom{X}{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

The goal is to find a
retraction

f:X\rightarrow K

that minimizes
\sum_{\{x,y\}\in\binom{X}{2}}c(x,y)\cdot d_{K}(f(x),f(y))
.

A retraction is a surjective function
f:X\rightarrow K
that satisfies
f(x)=x
for all
x\in K
. The
0
-Extension
problem, first proposed by Karzanov
[
51
]
,
generalizes the
Multiway Cut
problem
[
24
]

by allowing
d_{K}
to be any discrete metric (instead of a uniform metric).

Lee and Naor
[
57
]
(see also
[
6
,
15
]
) showed that for the case where the metric
(K,d_{K})
on the terminals admits a
(\beta,\frac{1}{\beta})
-padded-decomposition, there is an
O(\beta)
upper bound. By
Theorem

1.2
, we get:

Corollary 5.6
.

Consider an instance of the
0
-Extension
problem
\left(K\subseteq X,d,_{k},c:\binom{X}{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}\right)
, where the metric
(K,d_{K})
is a sub-metric of a shortest path metric of a graph with treewidth
{\rm tw}
. Then, one can efficiently find a solution with cost at most
O(\log{\rm tw})
times the cost of the optimal.

In particular, there is a
O(\log{\rm tw})
-approximation algorithm for the multiway cut problem for graphs of treewidth
{\rm tw}
.

The best previously known result was
O({\rm tw})
approximation
[
15
,
3
]
. For further reading on the
0
-Extension
problem, see
[
15
,
27
,
6
,
57
,
26
,
35
]
.

5.6
Lipschitz Extension

For a function
f:X\rightarrow Y
between metric spaces
(X,d_{X}),(Y,d_{Y})
, set
\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}=\sup_{x,y\in X}\frac{d_{Y}(f(x),f(y))}{d_{X}(x,y)}
to be the Lipschitz parameter of the function. In the Lipschitz extension problem, we are given a map
f:Z\rightarrow Y
from a subset
Z
of
X
. The goal is to extend
f
to a function
\tilde{f}
over the entire space
X
, while minimizing
\|\tilde{f}\|_{{\rm Lip}}
as a function of
\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}
.
Lee and Naor
[
57
]
, proved that if a space admits a
(\beta,\frac{1}{\beta})
-padded decomposition scheme, then given a function
f
from a subset
Z\subseteq X
into a closed convex set
C
in some Banach space, one can extend
f
into
\tilde{f}:X\rightarrow C

such that
\|\tilde{f}\|_{{\rm Lip}}\leq O(\beta)\cdot\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}
.
By
Theorem

1.2
, we obtain:

Corollary 5.7

(Lipschitz Extension)
.

Consider a graph
G=(V,E,w)
with treewidth
{\rm tw}
, and let
f:V^{\prime}\rightarrow C
be a map from a subset
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
into
C
, where
C
is a convex closed set of some Banach space.
Then there is an extension
\tilde{f}:X\rightarrow C
such that
\|\tilde{f}\|_{{\rm Lip}}\leq O(\log{\rm tw})\cdot\|f\|_{{\rm Lip}}
.

5.7
Embedding into
\ell_{p}
spaces

Metric embedding is a map between two metric spaces that preserves all pairwise distances up to a small stretch. We say that embedding
f:X\rightarrow Y
between
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
has distortion
t
if for every
x,y\in X
it holds that
d_{X}(x,y)\leq d_{Y}(f(x),f(y))\leq t\cdot d_{X}(x,y)
. Krauthgamer, Lee, Mendel and Naor
[
53
]
(improving over Rao
[
65
]
) showed that every
n
-point metric space that admits
(\beta,\frac{1}{\beta})
-padded decomposition scheme can be embedded into an
\ell_{p}
space with distortion
O(\beta^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\cdot(\log n)^{\frac{1}{p}})
.
Previously, it was known that the shortest path metric of an
n
point graph with treewidth
{\rm tw}
(or more generally
K_{{\rm tw}}
-minor free) embeds into
\ell_{p}
space with distortion
O(({\rm tw})^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\cdot(\log n)^{\frac{1}{p}})

[
53
,
3
]
.
By
Theorem

1.2
, we conclude:

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
G
be an
n
-point weighted graph with treewidth
{\rm tw}
. Then there exist embedding of
G
into
\ell_{p}
(
1\leq p\leq\infty
) with distortion
O((\log{\rm tw})^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\cdot(\log n)^{\frac{1}{p}})
.

Since every finite subset of the Euclidean space
\ell_{2}
embed isometrically into
\ell_{p}
(for
1\leq p\leq\infty
), it follows that for
1\leq p\leq\infty
such
G
can be embedded into
\ell_{p}
space (in particular
\ell_{1}
) with distortion
O(\sqrt{\log{\rm tw}\cdot\log n})
.

A norm space of special interest is
\ell_{\infty}
, as every finite metric space embeds into
\ell_{\infty}
isometrically (i.e. with distortion
1
, this is the so called Fréchet embedding). However, the dimension of such embedding is very large:
\Omega(n)
.
Krauthgamer
et al.
[
53
]
proved that every graph with treewidth
{\rm tw}
(or more generally
K_{{\rm tw}}
-minor free) embeds into
\ell^{d}_{\infty}
with dimension
d=\tilde{O}(3^{\rm tw})\cdot\log n
and distortion
O({\rm tw}^{2})
.
This was recently improved by Filtser
[
31
]
who showed that every graph with treewidth
{\rm tw}
(or more generally
K_{{\rm tw}}
-minor free) embeds into
\ell^{d}_{\infty}
with dimension
d=\tilde{O}({\rm tw}^{2})\cdot\log n
and distortion
O({\rm tw})
(alternatively distortion
3+\varepsilon
and dimension
d=\tilde{O}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})^{{\rm tw}+1}\cdot\log n
).
More generally, Filtser
[
31
]
proved that if a graph
G
admits a
(\beta,s)
-padded partition cover scheme, then
G
embeds into
\ell^{d}_{\infty}
with distortion

5.8
Stochastic decomposition for minor-free graphs - a reduction from additive stretch embeddings

A major open question is to determine the padding parameter of
K_{r}
-minor-free graphs. The current state of the art is
O(r)

[
3
]
(see also
[
32
]
), while the natural conjecture is exponentially smaller
O(\log r)
. A somewhat weaker guarantee which we call
(t,p,\Delta)
-
stochastic decomposition
, is a distribution over partitions with diameter
\Delta
such that every pair of vertices at distance at most
\frac{\Delta}{t}
is clustered together with probability at least
p
. Compared to padded decomposition, the guarantee here is over pairs (instead of balls), and there is a threshold distance for the guarantee (instead of a linear dependence as in padded decomposition). Nonetheless, for many applications, a stochastic decomposition is good enough. Moreover, in most cases, the parameters of padded and stochastic decompositions are the same. The only case that we are aware of where they are different is high-dimensional Euclidean spaces. In particular, for minor-free graphs, the best-known result is a
(O(r),\frac{1}{2},\Delta)
-stochastic decomposition
[
3
]
. Here, we argue that if the parameters of recently studied stochastic embeddings with additive distortion are improved, then our
Theorem

1.2
will imply much better stochastic decompositions for minor-free graphs.

Definition 5.10

(Stochastic embedding with additive distortion)
.

Consider an
n
-vertex weighted graph
G=(V,E,w)
with diameter
D
. A
stochastic additive embedding of
G
into a distribution over embeddings
f
into graphs
H
with treewidth
\tau
and expected additive distortion
\varepsilon\cdot D
, is a distribution
\mathcal{D}
over maps
f
from
G
into graphs
H
, such that

•

Small treewidth: Any graph
H
in the support has treewidth at most
\tau
.

•

Dominating: for every
u,v\in V
, and
(f,H)\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{D})
,
d_{G}(u,v)\leq d_{H}(f(u),f(v))
.

•

Expected additive distortion: for every
u,v\in V
,
{\mathbb{E}}_{(f,H)\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{D})}\left[d_{H}(f(u),f(v))\right]\leq d_{G}(u,v)+\varepsilon\cdot D
.

Recently, Filtser and Le
[
37
]
(see also
[
38
,
21
,
36
]
) constructed a stochastic embedding of every
n
-vertex
K_{r}
-minor free graph
G
with diameter
D
, into a distribution over graphs with treewidth
O(\frac{(\log\log n)^{2}}{\varepsilon^{2}})\cdot\chi(r)
and expected additive distortion
\varepsilon\cdot D
. Here
\chi(r)
is an extremely fast-growing function of
r
(an outcome of the minor-structure theorem
[
67
]
).
If one would construct such stochastic embedding into treewidth
\mathrm{poly}(\frac{r}{\varepsilon})
, then
Lemma

5.11
bellow will imply stochastic decomposition with parameter
O(\log r)
. Furthermore, even allowing treewidth
\mathrm{poly}(\frac{r\cdot\log n}{\varepsilon})
will imply stochastic decomposition with parameter
O(\log r+\log\log n)
which is yet unknown as well.

Lemma 5.11
.

Suppose that there is a coordinate monotone function
\pi(\varepsilon,r,n)
, such that every
n
-vertex
K_{r}
-minor free graph with diameter
D
admits a stochastic embedding into a distribution over graphs with treewidth
\pi(\varepsilon,r,n)
and expected additive distortion
\varepsilon\cdot D
.
Then for every
\Delta>0
, every
n
-vertex
K_{r}
-minor free admits a
(t,\frac{1}{2},\Delta)
-stochastic decomposition, for
t=O(\log\pi(\Omega(\frac{1}{r}),r,n))
.

Proof 5.12
.

Consider an
n
-vertex,
K_{r}
-minor-free graph
G=\left(V,E,w\right)
.
Fix
\Delta>0
. We will construct a distribution
\mathcal{D}
over partitions of
G
such that every
\mathcal{P}\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{D})
will be weakly
\Delta
-bounded and for every pair
u,v
at distance
\frac{\Delta}{t}
,
u,v
will be clustered together with probability at least
\frac{1}{2}
.

The partition will be constructed as follows:

1.

Use Filtser’s
[
32
]
strong padded decomposition scheme with diameter parameter
\Delta^{\prime}=O(\frac{r}{t})\cdot\Delta
to sample a partition
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of
V
. Note that every cluster
C\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is a
K_{r}
-minor free graph of diameter
\Delta^{\prime}
.

2.

Fix
\varepsilon=\frac{\Delta}{5\cdot t\cdot\Delta^{\prime}}=\Omega(\frac{1}{r})
. For every cluster
C\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, sample an embedding
f_{C}
of
G[C]
into a graph
H_{C}
with treewidth
\pi(\varepsilon,r,|C|)\leq\pi(\Omega(\frac{1}{r}),r,n)
and expected additive distortion
\varepsilon\cdot\Delta^{\prime}
. We will abuse notation and use
v\in C
to denote both
v
and
f_{C}(v)
.

3.

For every
C\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, use
Theorem

1.2
to sample a

\left(O(\log(\pi(\Omega(\frac{1}{r}),r,n))),\Omega(1),\Delta\right)
-padded decomposition
\mathcal{P}_{C}
of
H_{C}
.

The final partition we return is the union of all the created partitions
\mathcal{P}=\cup_{C\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{P}_{C}\cap V)
. We claim that
\mathcal{P}
has a weak diameter at most
\Delta
. Consider a cluster
P\in\mathcal{P}
and
u,v\in P
. There is some cluster
C\in\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
containing both
u
and
v
. Then, it holds that
d_{G}(u,v)\leq d_{G[C]}(u,v)\leq d_{H_{C}}(u,v)\leq\Delta
.

Consider a pair of vertices
u,v
at distance
\leq\frac{\Delta}{t}
. Denote by
\Psi_{1}
the event that the ball of radius
\frac{\Delta}{t}

around
v
belongs to a single cluster in
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
. Denote by
\Psi_{2}
the event that conditioned on
\Psi_{1}
occurring,
d_{H_{C}}(u,v)\leq d_{G}(u,v)+5\cdot\varepsilon\cdot\Delta^{\prime}
. Denote by
\Psi_{3}
the event that conditioned on
\Psi_{2}
occurring,
u
and
v
belong to the same cluster of
\mathcal{P}_{C}
.

Let
C
denote the cluster containing
v
in
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
. By choosing the constant in
\Delta^{\prime}
large enough, it holds that

\Pr\left[\Psi_{1}\right]=\Pr\left[\mathbf{B}_{G}(v,\frac{\Delta}{t})\subseteq C\right]=\Pr\left[\mathbf{B}_{G}(v,\frac{\Delta}{t\Delta^{\prime}}\cdot\Delta^{\prime})\subseteq C\right]\geq e^{-O(r)\cdot\frac{\Delta}{t\Delta^{\prime}}}\geq\frac{4}{5}~.

Note that if
\mathbf{B}_{G}(v,\frac{\Delta}{t})\subseteq C
, that the entire shortest path from
u
to
v
is contained in
C
, and hence
d_{G[C]}(u,v)=d_{G}(u,v)
.
Using Markov, the probability that the additive distortion by
f_{C}
is too large is bounded by:

\displaystyle\Pr\left[\overline{\Psi_{2}}\mid\Psi_{1}\right]

\displaystyle=\Pr\left[d_{H_{C}}(u,v)>d_{G[C]}(u,v)+5\cdot\epsilon\cdot\Delta^{\prime}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\mathbb{E}}\left[d_{H_{C}}(u,v)-d_{G[C]}(u,v)\right]}{5\cdot\epsilon\cdot\Delta^{\prime}}\leq\frac{1}{5}~.

We conclude
\Pr\left[\Psi_{2}\mid\Psi_{1}\right]\geq\frac{4}{5}
.
Note that if
\Psi_{2}
indeed occurred, then
d_{H_{C}}(u,v)\leq d_{G[C]}(u,v)+5\cdot\epsilon\cdot\Delta^{\prime}=d_{G[C]}(u,v)+\frac{\Delta}{t}\leq\frac{2\Delta}{t}
.
Finally in step 3 we sample a padded decomposition
C_{\mathcal{P}}
of
H_{C}
.
As
H_{C}
has treewidth
\pi(\Omega(\frac{1}{r}),r,n)
, the probability that
u
and
v
are clustered together is bounded by:

\Pr\left[\Psi_{3}\mid\Psi_{1}\wedge\Psi_{2}\right]\geq\Pr\left[\mathbf{B}_{H_{C}}(v,\frac{2\Delta}{t})\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{C}(c)\right]\geq e^{-O(\log\pi(\Omega(\frac{1}{r}),r,n))\cdot\frac{2}{t}}\geq\frac{4}{5}~,

where the last inequality holds for a large enough constant in the definition of
t
.
We conclude

\Pr\left[\mathcal{P}(v)=\mathcal{P}(u)\right]\geq\Pr\left[\Psi_{1}\wedge\Psi_{2}\wedge\Psi_{3}\right]\geq\left(\frac{4}{5}\right)^{3}>\frac{1}{2}~.

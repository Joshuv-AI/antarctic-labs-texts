Standing Waves for Schrödinger Equations with Kato-Rellich potentials
Aleksander Ćwiszewski, Piotr Kokocki

Abstract
. We show the existence of standing waves for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with Kato-Rellich type potential. We consider both resonant with the nonlinearity satisfying one of Landesman-Lazer type or sign conditions and non-resonant case where the linearization at infinity has zero kernel. The approach relies on the geometric and topological analysis of the parabolic semiflow associated to the involved elliptic problem. Tail estimates techniques and spectral theory of unbounded linear operators are used to exploit subtle compactness properties necessary for use of the Conley index theory due to Rybakowski. The obtained results extend those by Prizzi [ 17 ] for the non-resonant case, resolve the existence problem at resonance and complete those from [ 4 ] where the bifurcation of stationary solutions from infinity was studied.



1.
Introduction

In the time-dependent nonlinear Schrödinger type equation

i\psi_{t}=-\Delta\psi+V(x)\psi+g(x,|\psi|)\frac{\psi}{|\psi|},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\ t>0,

where
V:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is a potential and
g:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is a nonlinear map, searching for a standing wave solution having the form
\psi(x,t)=e^{-i\lambda t}u(x)
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, leads to the following elliptic problem

[formula]

-\Delta u+V(x)u-\lambda u=f(x,u(x)),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a nonlinear map.

Many authors have extensively studied the equation
(E)_{\lambda}
by the use of various methods.
In
[
17
]
the existence of solutions by Conley index was obtained in the non-resonant case with Kato-Rellich type
V
. Bifurcation theory involving topological degree methods was used in
[
7
]
for bounded
V
in order to obtain the existence of large solutions near eigenvalues, i.e. the so-called bifurcation from infinity. For the same bifurcation problems with bounded potential
V
variational methods were applied in
[
14
]
, whereas in
[
24
]
, an argument relying on topological degree were employed.
Conley index techniques were used in
[
4
]
to study bifurcations from infinity for
(E)_{\lambda}
with potentials of Kato-Rellich type, while in
[
2
]
, the same approach were utilized in the case of bounded potentials
V
.

Throughout the paper we assume that
V
is a Kato-Rellich type potential, that is,

[formula]

\begin{gathered}V=V_{\infty}+V_{0},\ \text{where}\ V_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\ \text{and}\ V_{0}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\ \text{with $p$ satisfying}\\
p\geqslant 2\ \text{ if }\ 1\leqslant N\leqslant 3\ \ \text{ and }\ \ p>N/2\ \text{ if }\ N\geqslant 4.\end{gathered}\right.

We also assume that the map
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the conditions:

(f1)

the map
f(\,\cdot\,,u)
is Lebesgue measurable, for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
, and
c(x):=f(x,0)
is an element of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

(f2)

there is a function
l:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

|f(x,u)-f(x,v)|\leqslant l(x)|u-v|\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\text{ and all }u,v\in\mathbb{R},

and the following (stronger Kato-Rellich type) conditions are satisfied

[formula]

\begin{gathered}l=l_{\infty}+l_{0},\ \text{where}\ l_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\ \text{and}\ l_{0}\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\ \text{with $r$ satisfying}\\
r\geqslant 2\ \ \text{if}\ \ N=1,\ \ r>2\ \ \text{if}\ \ N=2\ \ \text{and}\ \ r\geqslant N\ \text{if }\ N\geqslant 3.\end{gathered}\right.

In some cases we shall also assume that

(f3)

there exists
a:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the condition
(KR)
such that

u\cdot f(x,u)\leqslant a(x)|u|^{2}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\text{ and all }u\in\mathbb{R}.

Our study of the existence of standing waves and the related problem
(E)_{\lambda}
strongly relies on the fact that
V
is a Kato-Rellich type potential and its consequences for the Schrödinger operator
-\Delta+V
, including its spectral properties. In this paper we shall consider the realization of
-\Delta+V
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which appears to be a closed operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with the domain equal to
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover,
-\Delta+V
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is self-adjoint, its spectrum
\sigma(-\Delta+V)
is bounded from below and the essential spectrum
\sigma_{ess}(-\Delta+V)
is located in the interval
(\varrho(V_{\infty}),+\infty)
where the so-called
asymptotic bottom
is given by

\varrho(V_{\infty}):=\lim_{R\to+\infty}\essinf_{|x|>R}V_{\infty}(x).

The spectrum of
-\Delta+V
may have a nonempty part below the value
\varrho(V_{\infty})
, which then contains eigenvalues only. Therefore if
\varrho(V_{\infty})>\lambda
, then

\sigma(-\Delta+V)\cap(-\infty,\lambda)
consists of a finite number of eigenvalues (see Section
3
for a more detailed discussion).
This enables us to define the collective multiplicity of eigenvalues less than
\lambda
as

d^{-}(V,\lambda):=\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\mu\in\sigma(-\Delta+V)\cap(-\infty,\lambda)}\!\!\dim\mathrm{Ker}\,(-\Delta+V-\mu).

Let us start with the so-called non-resonant case, i.e. when the linearizations of
f
at zero and at infinity give potentials that do not resonate with the operator
-\Delta+V-\lambda
. We shall start with the following criterion on the existence of solutions of
(E)_{\lambda}
and connecting orbits for the parabolic equations associated with
(E)_{\lambda}

[formula]

u_{t}=\Delta u-V(x)u+\lambda u+f(x,u),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\ t>0.

.

satisfies

If

is such that

\lambda\not\in\sigma(-\Delta+V-\alpha)\cup\sigma(-\Delta+V-\omega)\ \ \text{ and }\ \ d^{-}(V-\alpha,\lambda)\neq d^{-}(V-\omega,\lambda),

In the resonant case, i.e. when

has nonempty kernel we shall assume that

satisfies
(f2)
and is "bounded" in the sense that there is

[formula]

|f(x,u)|\leqslant m(x)\quad\text{ for a.e. $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$ and all $u\in\mathbb{R}$}.

Here we shall impose additional assumptions on

at infinity, the so-called
Landesman-Lazer type
and
strong resonance
conditions. The
Landesman-Lazer type
conditions state that either

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\check{f}_{+}(x)\geqslant 0\ \mbox{ and }\ \hat{f}_{-}(x)\leqslant 0\ \mbox{ for a.e. }\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\mbox{there is a set of positive measure on which none of}\;\;\check{f}_{+}\mbox{ and }\hat{f}_{-}\mbox{vanishes},\end{array}\right.

or

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{f}_{+}(x)\leqslant 0\ \mbox{ and }\ \check{f}_{-}(x)\geqslant 0\ \mbox{ for a.e. }\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\mbox{there is a set of positive measure on which none of }\;\hat{f}_{+}\mbox{ and }\check{f}_{-}\mbox{ vanishes,}\end{array}\right.

where
\hat{f}_{\pm}(x):=\limsup_{s\to\pm\infty}f(x,s)
and
\check{f}_{\pm}(x):=\liminf_{s\to\pm\infty}f(x,s)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
We remark that the continuation property

8
]
for the eigenvalues of the Schrödinger operator implies that the conditions

represent a particular form of the original resonance assumptions introduced by Landesman and Lazer in
[

(see

for more details).

The so-called

or
strong resonance conditions
state

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}f(x,s)s\geqslant 0,\mbox{ for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mbox{ and all }s\in\mathbb{R},\\
\mbox{there is a set of positive measure on which }\check{k}_{+}\mbox{ and }\check{k}_{-}\mbox{ are positive,}\end{array}\right.

or

[formula]

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}f(x,s)s\leqslant 0,\mbox{ for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mbox{ and all }s\in\mathbb{R},\\
\mbox{there is a set of positive measure on which }\hat{k}_{+}\mbox{ and }\hat{k}_{-}\mbox{ are negative,}\end{array}\right.

where
\check{k}_{\pm}(x)=\liminf_{s\to\pm\infty}f(x,s)s
and
\hat{k}_{\pm}(x)=\limsup_{s\to\pm\infty}f(x,s)s
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

In the above setting we obtain the following existence result in the resonant case, i.e. when

is in the point spectrum of

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 provides basic facts on Conley-Rybakowski index theory. In Section 3 we recall basic properties of the operator

and derive an inequality that we shall use for tail estimates. Section 4 deals with the definition and properties of the semiflow

Section 5 is devoted to the proof of Theorem

, which is based on the mentioned Conley index formulas and the properties of irreducible sets. Finally in Section 6 we shall use the geometric implications of assumptions

and

in order to prove Theorems

and

as well as Corollary

2.
Preliminaries on Conley index due to Rybakowski

In this section we will briefly recall a version of Conley index for the non-compact metric spaces due to Rybakowski (see
[
18
]
or
[
19
]
). Let
\Phi\colon[0,+\infty)\times X\to X
be a semiflow on a complete metric space
X
.
A continuous map
u\colon J\to X
, where
J\subset\mathbb{R}
is an interval, is a
solution of
\Phi
if
u(t+s)=\Phi_{t}(u(s))
for all
t\geqslant 0
and
s\in J
such that
t+s\in J
.
Given
N\subset X
, we define the
invariant part
of the set
N
by

\mathrm{Inv}_{\Phi}(N):=\{x\in X\ |\ \text{there is a solution}\;u\colon\mathbb{R}\to N\;\text{such that}\;u(0)=x\}.

We say that a set
K\subset X
is a
\Phi
-invariant
or
invariant
(w.r.t.
\Phi
) if
\mathrm{Inv}_{\Phi}(K)=K
. If there exists an
isolating neighborhood
of
K
, i.e. the set
N\subset X
such that
K=\mathrm{Inv}_{\Phi}(N)\subset\mathrm{int}\,N
, then
K
is called an
isolated invariant
set.

A set
N\subset X
is
\Phi
-admissible
or
admissible
(w.r.t.
\Phi
) if, for any sequences
(t_{n})
in
[0,+\infty)
,
(x_{n})
in
X
such that
t_{n}\to+\infty
and
\{\Phi(t,x_{n})\ |\ t\in[0,t_{n}]\}\subset N
, the sequence of the end-points
\left(\Phi_{t_{n}}(x_{n})\right)
has a convergent subsequence.

Suppose that
\{\Phi^{(s)}\}_{s\in\Lambda}
, where
\Lambda
is a metric space, is a family of semiflows on
X
such that the map
[0,+\infty)\times X\times\Lambda\ni(t,x,s)\mapsto\Phi^{(s)}_{t}(x)
is continuous. A set
N\subset X
is

admissible
with respect to the family of semiflows
\{\Phi^{(s)}\}_{s\in\Lambda}
if, for any sequences
(t_{n})
in
[0,+\infty)
,
(x_{n})
in

and
(s_{n})
such that

,
s_{n}\to s_{0}
in
\Lambda
and
\{\Phi^{(s_{n})}(t,x_{n})\ |\ t\in[0,t_{n}]\}\subset N
, the sequence
(\Phi^{(s_{n})}_{t_{n}}(x_{n}))
has a convergent subsequence.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}(X)
be the family of all pairs
(\Phi,K)
such that
\Phi
is a semiflow on

and

is an isolated invariant set having an admissible isolating neighborhood w.r.t.
\Phi
. Given
(\Phi,K)\in{\mathcal{I}}(X)
, we can choose a pair of closed sets

, where

is an isolating block of

relative to
\Phi
and

is its exit set (see

18
]
). Then the Conley

h(\Phi,K)
of

relative to
\Phi
is defined as the homotopy type of the quotient space

h(\Phi,K):=[(B/B^{-},[B^{-}])],

provided

is a nonempty set. If

then we put

h(\Phi,K):=[(B\,\dot{\cup}\,\{a\},a)]
, where
\{a\}
is the one point space disjoint with

. In particular,

, where
\overline{0}:=[(\{a\},a)]
.

Let us enumerate several important properties of the homotopy index.

(H1)

For any
(\Phi,K)\in{\mathcal{I}}(X)
, if

, then

;

(H2)

if
(\Phi,K_{1}),(\Phi,K_{2})\in{\mathcal{I}}(X)
and

, then

(H3)

for any

and

x\in X
such that
\Phi_{t}(x)=x
for all
t\geqslant 0
(see
[
18
, Theorem 2.5.4]
).

3.
Properties of the Schrödinger operator

Let us consider an operator
A
on the space
X:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
given by the formula

(3.1)

Au:=-\Delta u+Vu-\lambda u,\quad\ u\in D(A):=H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
\lambda<\varrho(V_{\infty})
.
Clearly
A
is a perturbation of
A_{0}:H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, given by
A_{0}u:=-\Delta u
,
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, by the multiplication operator
V:H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. It is known that
A_{0}
is self-adjoint and
-A_{0}
generates an analytic
C_{0}
-semigroup of bound linear operators on
X
. Since the potential
V
is of Kato-Rellich type
we infer that
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
continuously embeds into
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
q=+\infty
if
N=1,2,3
, for arbitrary
q\geqslant 2
if
N=4
and
q=2N/(N-4)
if
N\geqslant 5
(see
[
1
, Corollary 9.13]
). This implies that the multiplication operator
V
is well-defined and
A_{0}
-bounded with zero
A_{0}
-bound, i.e., for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\|Vu\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\varepsilon\|A_{0}u\|_{L^{2}}+C_{\varepsilon}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\quad\text{for all }\ u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Therefore, by the Rellich-Kato theorem (see e.g.
[
11
, Th. 13.5]
), the perturbed operator
A=A_{0}+V-\lambda I
is self-adjoint and, by
[
6
, Th. 2.10]
, the operator
-A
generates an analytic
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{S(t)\}_{t\geqslant 0}
. Moreover, it appears that the multiplication operator
V_{0}:H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is
(A_{0}+V_{\infty})
-compact, which due to the Weyl theorem (see e.g.
[
11
, Th. 14.6]
) implies that the essential spectra of
A_{0}+V_{\infty}
and
A
are equal, i.e.

\sigma_{ess}(-\Delta+V-\lambda)=\sigma_{ess}(-\Delta+V_{\infty}-\lambda).

By the Persson formula (see e.g.
[
11
, Th. 14.11]
or
[
16
]
) for the bottom of essential spectrum we get

\displaystyle\inf\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(-\Delta+V_{\infty})=\sup_{R>0}\ \inf\left\{(-\Delta\phi+V_{\infty}\phi,\phi)_{L^{2}}\ |\ \phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus D(0,R)),\,\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}=1\right\},

where
D(0,R):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid|x|\leqslant R\}
.
Therefore the essential part of the spectrum of the operator
A
satisfies
\inf\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(A)\geq\varrho(V_{\infty})-\lambda
. Hence the part of the spectrum
\sigma(A)
contained in the interval
(-\infty,\varrho(V_{\infty})-\lambda)
consists of isolated eigenvalues of finite multiplicity, that in turn satisfy the estimate

(3.2)

\displaystyle|u(x)|\leqslant Ce^{-\delta|x|}\qquad\text{ for a.e. }\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
\delta=\delta(u)>0
and
C=C(u)>0
are constants – see
[
22
, Th. C.3.4]
.

Since
A
is self-adjoint, by the spectral theorem, there are closed mutually orthogonal subspaces
X_{-}
,
X_{0}
,
X_{+}
of
X=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
X=X_{-}\oplus X_{0}\oplus X_{+}
and

\sigma(A|X_{-})=\sigma(A)\cap(-\infty,0),\ \ \ X_{0}=\mathrm{Ker}\,\,A,\ \ \ \sigma(A|X_{+})=\sigma(A)\cap(0,+\infty).

It is known that
\dim\,X_{-}<\infty
and
\dim X_{0}<\infty
. We denote by
Q_{-},P,Q_{+}
, be the orthogonal projections onto
X_{-}
,
X_{0}
and
X_{+}
, respectively, and we write
Q:=Q_{+}+Q_{-}
. By the semigroup theory we know that

S(t)Q_{\pm}u=Q_{\pm}S(t)u,\quad u\in X,\ t\geq 0,

and there exist
K,\rho_{+}>0
such that

(3.3)

\|S(t)u\|_{H^{1}}\leqslant Kt^{-1/2}e^{-\rho_{+}t}\|u\|_{L^{2}}\ \ \mbox{ for all }\ \ u\in X_{+},\ t>0.

Obviously, the restricted semigroup
\{e^{-tA}|_{X_{-}}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
can be extended to a
C_{0}
-group on
X_{-}
and there exist
\rho_{-}>0
such that

(3.4)

\|S(t)u\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant e^{\rho_{-}t}\|u\|_{L^{2}},\quad u\in X_{-},\ t\leqslant 0.

The following theorem says that the spectral properties enable us to determine the Conley index of zero in the linear case.

Theorem 3.1
.

(see
[
18
, Ch. I, Th. 11.1]
)
The restriction of the semigroup
\{S(t)_{|Y}\}_{t\geq 0}
to
Y:=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap(X_{+}\oplus X_{-})
determines the semiflow on the space
Y
equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}}
(note that
Y=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
if
\lambda\not\in\sigma(-\Delta+V)
). Furthermore the set
K_{0}=\{0\}
is the maximal bounded invariant set of the semiflow,
(S(t)_{|Y},K_{0})\in{\mathcal{I}}(X)
and

h(S(t)_{|Y},K_{0})=\Sigma^{\dim X_{-}}.

Let us consider the semilinear differential equation

(3.5)

\dot{u}(t)=-Au(t)+g(t),\quad t>0,

where
g:[0,\infty)\to X
is a continuous function and define the cut-off map
\phi_{n}(x):=\phi(|x|^{2}/n^{2})
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where the profile
\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
is a smooth function such that
\phi(x)=0
for
x\in[0,1]
and
\phi(x)=1
for
|x|\geq 2
. The following version of the estimates for the solutions of (
3.5
) can be obtained along the lines of
[
4
, Lem. 3.6]
and
[
3
, Lem. 5.3]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

There exist a sequence
(\beta_{n})
with
\beta_{n}\to 0^{+}
as
n\to\infty
and
n_{0}\geqslant 1
, such that if
u\in C((t_{0},t_{1}),H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap C^{1}((t_{0},t_{1}),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
is a classical solution of the equation (
3.5
), then

(3.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{n}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx\leqslant-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(V_{\infty}(x)-\lambda)\phi_{n}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx+\gamma_{n}\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\langle\phi_{n}u(t),g(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}

for
t\in(t_{0},t_{1})
and
n\geq n_{0}
. The above sequence
(\beta_{n})
and integer
n_{0}\geq 1
depend only on the potential
V
.

Proof.

We begin with the observation that

(3.7)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\langle\phi_{n}u(t),u(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle\phi_{n}u(t),\dot{u}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle\phi_{n}u(t),-Au(t)+g(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle\phi_{n}u(t),\Delta u(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}\!+\!\langle\phi_{n}u(t),-Vu(t)\!+\!\lambda u(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}\!+\!\langle\phi_{n}u(t),g(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:I_{1}(t)+I_{2}(t)+I_{3}(t)

for
t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]
. Then we have the following estimates

(3.8)

\displaystyle I_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\langle\phi_{n}u(t),\Delta u(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}=-\langle\nabla(\phi_{n}u(t)),\nabla u(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{n}(x)|\nabla u(t)|^{2}\,dx-\frac{2}{n^{2}}\int_{\{n\leqslant|x|\leqslant n\sqrt{2}\}}\!u(t)\phi^{\prime}\left(\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{n^{2}}\right)\,x\cdot\nabla u(t)\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{n}L_{\phi}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{n}L_{\phi}\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

where
L_{\phi}:=\sup_{t\geq 0}|\phi^{\prime}(t)|
. On the other hand we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle I_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\langle\phi_{n}u(t),-Vu(t)+\lambda u(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(V_{\infty}(x)-\lambda)\phi_{n}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V_{0}(x)\phi_{n}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(V_{\infty}(x)-\lambda)\phi_{n}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx+\|\phi_{n}V_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2p/(p-1)}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(V_{\infty}(x)-\lambda)\phi_{n}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx+\|\phi_{n}V_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

where, in the last estimate, we used the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{2p/(p-1)}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which is valid for
p>2
satisfying the condition
(KR)
.
Therefore, combining (
3.7
), (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) gives (
3.6
) with

\displaystyle\beta_{n}:=\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{n}L_{\phi}+2\|\phi_{n}V_{0}\|_{L^{p}},\quad n\geq 1.

Since
V_{0}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have
\beta_{n}\to 0^{+}
as
n\to\infty
and the proof of the proposition is completed.
∎

4.
Semiflow and admissible homotopy

Consider the differential equation

(4.1)

\dot{u}(t)=-Au(t)+F(u(t)),\quad t>0,

where
A
is defined by (
3.1
) and the nonlinear map
F:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the Nemytskii operator determined by
f
, i.e. it is given by

(4.2)

[F(u)](x):=f(x,u(x))\quad\mbox{ for a.e. }\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\text{ and all }u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Remark 4.1
.

From the assumptions
(f1)
and
(f2)
it follows that
f
is a Carethéodory function. Therefore, for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the composition in (
4.2
) is a measurable function. On the other hand, by
(f2)
and the Hölder inequality, for any
u,v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|F(u)-F(v)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|(l_{\infty}+l_{0})(u-v)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|l_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u-v\|_{L^{2}}+\|l_{0}\|_{L^{r}}\|u-v\|_{L^{2r/(r-2)}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\|l_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|l_{0}\|_{L^{r}}\right)\|u-v\|_{H^{1}},

where we used the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{2r/(r-2)}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which holds true as
r\in(2,\infty)
is restricted in
(PT)
. This implies that
F
is a Lipschitz map. Furthermore, by
(f1)
and (
4.3
), we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|F(u)\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\left(\|l_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|l_{0}\|_{L^{r}}\right)\|u\|_{H^{1}}+\|c\|_{L^{2}}

for
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which gives the sublinear growth of
F
.
\square

Since the operator
A
is sectorial and
F
satisfies (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), it follows (see e.g.
[
10
, Theorem 3.3.3]
) that, for any
\bar{u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a unique continuous function
u=u(\,\cdot\,;\bar{u},t_{0}):[t_{0},\infty)\to H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
u(t_{0})=\bar{u}
,

u\in C((t_{0},+\infty),H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap C^{1}((t_{0},+\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
and (
4.1
) holds for all
t>t_{0}
. Consequently the equation (
4.1
) determines the semiflow
\{\Phi_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
on the space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, given by

\Phi_{t}(\bar{u}):=u(t;\bar{u},0),\quad\bar{u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ t\geq 0.

It appears that the semiflow
\Phi
is gradient-like.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathbb{R}
be given by

(4.5)

{\mathcal{E}}(v):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v(x)|^{2}+(V(x)-\lambda)|v(x)|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\mathcal{F}}(x,v(x))\,dx,\quad v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
{\mathcal{F}}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is given by
{\mathcal{F}}(x,v):=\int_{0}^{v}f(x,w)\,dw
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
v\in\mathbb{R}
.
The function
{\mathcal{E}}
is a well-defined continuous Lyaponov functional for the semiflow
\Phi
, i.e. if
u:[t_{0},t_{1})\to H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a solution of the semiflow
\Phi
, then

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}(u(t))=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\dot{u}(t)|^{2}\,dx\quad\text{for}\ \ t\in(t_{0},t_{1}).

Proof.

If
u:[t_{0},t_{1})\to H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a solution of (
4.1
), then both
\dot{u}
and
F\circ u
are continuous functions on
(t_{0},+\infty)
with values in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and consequently
A\circ u=-\dot{u}+F\circ u
is continuous, which implies that
u\in C((t_{0},t_{1}),H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
. Fix
t\in(t_{0},t_{1})
and take any small
h\neq 0
, then, using the fact that
u\in C((t_{0},t_{1}),H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}\big(\|\nabla u(t+h)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\quad=\langle\nabla u(t+h),\nabla\big(h^{-1}(u(t+h)-u(t))\big)\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle\nabla\big(h^{-1}(u(t+h)-u(t))\big),\nabla u(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad=-\langle\Delta u(t+h),h^{-1}(u(t+h)-u(t))\rangle_{L^{2}}-\langle h^{-1}(u(t+h)-u(t)),\Delta u(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\to 2\langle-\Delta u(t),\dot{u}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}\ \text{ as }h\to 0.

In a similar manner

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(V(x)-\lambda)|u(t)|^{2}=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(V(x)-\lambda)u(t)\dot{u}(t)\,dx.

By standard arguments

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\mathcal{F}(x,u(t+h)(x))-\mathcal{F}(x,u(t)(x))\,dx

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{h}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g_{n}(x)\big(u(t+h)(x)-u(t)(x)\big)\,dx\to\langle F(u(t)),\dot{u}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}\ \text{ as }h\to 0^{+},

where
g_{h}(x):=f\big(x,u(t)(x)+\theta_{x}(u(t+h)(x)-u(t)(x))\big)
, with
\theta_{x}\in(0,1)
from the Lagrange mean value theorem applied for fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and we use the fact that
g_{h}\to F(u(t))
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
h\to 0
. The latter follows from the following inequality implied by
(f2)

|g_{h}(x)-f(t,u(t)(x))|\leqslant l(x)|u(t+h)(x)-u(t)(x)|\ \text{ for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

which implies
\|g_{h}-F(u(t))\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant C(\|l_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|l_{0}\|_{L^{r}})\|u(t+h)-u(t)\|_{H^{1}}
as in (
4.3
).

Combining the above three equalities together we obtain

(\mathcal{E}\circ u)^{\prime}(t)=\langle-\Delta u(t)+(V-\lambda)u(t)-F(u(t)),\dot{u}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}=-\|\dot{u}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2},

which proves the assertion.
∎

Let us also consider a parameterized by
s\in[0,1]
family of differential equations

\dot{u}(t)=-Au(t)+G(u(t),s),\quad t>0,

(G1)

Given

The following assertions hold.

Proposition 4.4

By Proposition

with

(4.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{n}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx\!\leqslant\!-\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(V_{\infty}-\lambda)\phi_{n}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx+\beta_{n}\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\langle\phi_{n}(t),G(u(t),s)\rangle_{L^{2}}

and

. Since

On the other hand, by the Hölder inequality and Sobolev embedding, we have

(4.10)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{n}a_{0}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx\leqslant\|\phi_{n}a_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2p/(p-1)}}^{2}\leqslant\|\phi_{n}a_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{n}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{n}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx+(\beta_{n}+\|\phi_{n}a_{0}\|_{L^{p}})\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\phi_{n}b\|_{L^{2}}\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{n}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx+\varepsilon\gamma_{n}

for

and
n\geq n_{0}
, where we define
\gamma_{n}:=\varepsilon^{-1}R^{2}(\beta_{n}+\|\phi_{n}a_{0}\|_{L^{p}})+R\|\phi_{n}b\|_{L^{2}}
. Since
a_{0}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
b\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have
\gamma_{n}\to 0^{+}
as
n\to\infty
. Integrating the above inequality, for any
n\geq n_{0}
, gives

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{n}|u(t)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{-2\varepsilon(t-t_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{n}|u(t_{0})|^{2}\,dx+\gamma_{n}(1-e^{-2\varepsilon(t-t_{0})})

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{-2\varepsilon(t-t_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{n}|u(t_{0})|^{2}\,dx+\gamma_{n},

which completes the proof.
∎

As an immediate consequence we obtain the following admissibility result.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let the assumptions of Proposition

hold. If
\rho(V_{\infty}-a_{\infty})>\lambda
, then an arbitrary bounded set

is admissible with respect to the family of semiflows
\{\Psi^{(s)}\}_{s\in[0,1]}
.

Proof.

Let us assume that
(s_{m})
in
[0,1]
,
(t_{m})
in
[0,\infty)
and
(\bar{u}_{m})
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
are sequences such that
s_{m}\to s_{0}
and
t_{m}\to\infty
and
\{\Psi^{(s_{m})}_{t}(\bar{u}_{m})\ |\ t\in[0,t_{m}]\}\subset M
for
m\geq 1
. We show that the set
\{\Psi^{(s_{m})}_{t_{m}}(\bar{u}_{m})\}
is relatively compact in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Let us assume that
R>0
is such that

and let

be a solution of the semiflow
\Psi^{(s_{m})}
starting at

. In view of Proposition
4.3
(ii) it is enough to check that the set

is relatively compact in

. To this end we use Proposition
4.4
to obtain
\varepsilon>0
, a sequence

with
\gamma_{n}\to 0^{+}

, such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{n}|u_{m}(t_{m})|^{2}\,dx\leqslant e^{-2\varepsilon t_{m}}\|\bar{u}_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\gamma_{n}\leqslant e^{-2\varepsilon t_{m}}R^{2}+\gamma_{n}

in

∎

5.
The non-resonant case

In this section we shall prove Theorem
1.1
. The following extended versions of the Conley index formulae
[
17
, Th. 3.3 and 3.6]
will be crucial.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies the conditions
(f1)
,
(f2)
,
(f3)
and
\varrho(V_{\infty}-a_{\infty})>\lambda
.

(i)

If there there exists a potential
\alpha
satisfying
(PT)
such that

\lim_{u\to\,0}\frac{f(x,u)}{u}=\alpha(x)\ \mbox{ for a.e. }\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

\varrho(V_{\infty}-\alpha_{\infty})>\lambda

and
\lambda\not\in\sigma(-\Delta+V-\alpha)
,
then
K_{0}:=\{0\}
is an isolated invariant set with respect to the semiflow
\Phi
and

h(\Phi,K_{0})=\Sigma^{d^{-}(V-\alpha,\,\lambda)}.

(ii)

If there exists a potential
\omega
satisfying
(PT)
such that

\lim_{|u|\to\infty}\frac{f(x,u)}{u}=\omega(x)\ \mbox{ for a.e. }\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

\varrho(V_{\infty}-\omega_{\infty})>\lambda
and

\lambda\not\in\sigma(-\Delta+V-\omega)
,
then the set
K_{\infty}(\Phi)
, consisting of all
\bar{u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
u(0)=\bar{u}
for some bounded solution
u:\mathbb{R}\to H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of
\Phi
, is a compact isolated invariant set with respect to
\Phi
and

h(\Phi,K_{\infty}(\Phi))=\Sigma^{d^{-}(V-\omega,\,\lambda)}.

Proof.

(i) Consider the map
G:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times[0,1]\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
given, for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
s\in[0,1]
, by

[G(u,s)](x):=(1-s)F(u)(x)+s\alpha(x)u(x)\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
F
is given by (
4.2
). It can be easily seen that
G
satisfies conditions
(G1)
and
(G2)
from Section 4. Let
\{\Psi^{(s)}\}_{s\in[0,1]}
, be the family of semiflows generated by the equation (
4.6
). We claim that there exists
r_{0}>0
such that the set

N_{r}:=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\ \mid\ \|u\|_{H^{1}}\leqslant r\}
has the property

(5.1)

\mathrm{Inv}_{\Psi^{(s)}}\,N_{r}=\{0\}\ \text{ for all }r\in(0,r_{0}]\ \text{ and }s\in[0,1].

Indeed, if we suppose to the contrary, then we get a sequence of
(s_{m})
in
[0,1]
such that, for any
m\geqslant 1
, there exists a bounded nontrivial full solution
u_{m}
of the semiflow
\Psi^{(s_{m})}
such that

\|u_{m}(0)\|_{H^{1}}\geqslant(1-1/2m)\cdot\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|u_{m}(t)\|_{H^{1}}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{m}:=\|u_{m}(0)\|_{H^{1}}\to 0^{+}\quad\text{as}\ \ m\to\infty.

Note that, for each
m\geqslant 1
,
v_{m}:=\rho_{m}^{-1}u_{m}
solves the equation

\dot{v}(t)=-Av(t)+F_{m}(v(t)),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

with
F_{m}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
given, for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, by

[F_{m}(u)](x):=f_{m}(x,u(x))\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

where
f_{m}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

f_{m}(x,u):=(1-s_{m})\rho_{m}^{-1}f(x,\rho_{m}u)+s_{m}\alpha(x)u\ \mbox{ for a.e. }\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mbox{ and }u\in\mathbb{R}.

Clearly

(5.2)

\|v_{m}(0)\|_{H^{1}}=1\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|v_{m}(t)\|_{H^{1}}\leqslant(1-1/2m)^{-1}\leqslant 2\ \text{ for all }\ m\geqslant 1.

On the other hand, by the assumption
(f3)
, for all
m\geqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle uf_{m}(x,u)

\displaystyle=(1-s_{m})u\rho_{m}^{-1}f(x,\rho_{m}u)+s_{m}\alpha(x)|u|^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant(1-s_{m})a(x)|u|^{2}+s_{m}\alpha(x)|u|^{2}\leqslant\widetilde{a}(x)|u|^{2},

where
\widetilde{a}=\widetilde{a}_{\infty}+\widetilde{a}_{0}
with
\widetilde{a}_{\infty}:=\max\{a_{\infty},\alpha_{\infty}\}
and
\widetilde{a}_{0}:=a_{0}+\alpha_{0}
. Here
a_{0}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\alpha_{0}\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where the exponents
p=p(a_{0})>2
and
r=r(\alpha_{0})\geq 2
are restricted by the conditions
(KR)
and
(PT)
, respectively.
Since
\varrho(V_{\infty}-a_{\infty})>\lambda
and
\varrho(V_{\infty}-\alpha_{\infty})>\lambda
we infer that

\varrho\left(V_{\infty}-\widetilde{a}_{\infty}\right)>\lambda.

Hence, in view of Proposition
4.4
with
G(u,s)=F_{m}(u)
for
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
s\in[0,1]
, we see that there exist
\varepsilon>0
, sequence
(\gamma_{n})
with
\gamma_{n}\to 0^{+}
and
n_{0}\geqslant 1
(independent of
m
) such that, for all
m\geqslant 1
and
n\geqslant n_{0}
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,n)}|v_{m}(t)|^{2}\,dx\leqslant e^{-2\varepsilon(t-r)}\|v_{m}(r)\|^{2}+\gamma_{n}\leqslant 4e^{-2\varepsilon(t-r)}+\gamma_{n},\quad r<t,

where in the last inequality we used the estimate (
5.2
). Fixing
t\in\mathbb{R}
and passing with

, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B(0,n)}|v_{m}(t)|^{2}\,dx\leqslant\gamma_{n},\quad n\geq n_{0},\ m\geq 1.

Since the sequence
(v_{m}(t))
is bounded in

, by the Rellich-Kondrachov compact embedding theorem, we infer that the set of restricted functions
\{v_{m}(t)|_{B(0,n)}\ |\ m\geq 1\}
is relatively compact in

for any
n\geq 1
. Combining this with the fact that
\gamma_{n}\to 0^{+}
we see that, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the set
\{v_{m}(t)\}_{m\geqslant 1}
is relatively compact in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Take any
\tau>0
and let
\big(v_{m_{k}}(t-\tau)\big)
be a subsequence of
(v_{m}(t-\tau))
such that
v_{m_{k}}(t-\tau)\to\bar{v}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Using the weak compactness argument we can suppose that
\bar{v}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Since, for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
and a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

f_{m}(x,u)\to\alpha(x)u,\quad m\to\infty,

we can use
[
3

taking into consideration (
5.2
), to see that

v_{m_{k}}
converges in

, uniformly on compact subsets of

, to the solution

of

(5.3)

\dot{v}(t)=-Av(t)+m_{\alpha}v(t),\quad t>0,

where
m_{\alpha}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is given by
m_{\alpha}(u)(x):=\alpha(x)u(x)
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and all
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Since
\tau>0
is arbitrary, we can combine this with a diagonal type argument to obtain the existence of a bounded solution
v:\mathbb{R}\to H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of the equation (
5.3
) such that

\|v(0)\|_{H^{1}}=1
. If we consider the functional
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathbb{R}
, given by

\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(|\nabla u|^{2}+(V(x)-\alpha(x)-\lambda)|u|^{2}\big)\,\,dx,\quad u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

then, by Proposition
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(v(t))=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\dot{v}(t)|^{2}\,dx\ \text{ for all }\ t\in\mathbb{R},

i.e. the semiflow generated by (

) is gradient-like with respect to the Lyapunov functional
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}
. Hence, since
v
is nontrivial and bounded, there exists a nontrivial equilibrium point in the set
\alpha(u)\cup\omega(u)
. Then we have
\mathrm{Ker}\,(-A+m_{\alpha})=\mathrm{Ker}\,(-\Delta+V-\alpha-\lambda)\neq\{0\}
, which is a contradiction proving the existence of

such that
(
5.1
) holds.

Consequently from (
5.1
) it follows that
\mathrm{Inv}_{\Psi^{(s)}}\,N_{r}=\{0\}\subset\mathrm{int}\,N_{r}
for

and

and furthermore, in view of Proposition

is admissible with respect to the family of semiflows

. Hence, we can apply the homotopy invariance property (H4) to get

h(\Phi,K_{0})=h(\Psi^{(0)},K_{0})=h(\Psi^{(1)},K_{0}).

Since
\Psi^{(1)}
is the semiflow generated by (

, we infer from Theorem

, that

which together with (
5.4
) ends the proof of the assertion (i).

(ii) In this case we consider the family of semiflows
\{\Psi^{(s)}\}_{s\in[0,1]}
determined by the equation (
4.6
), where
the map
G
is given, for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
s\in[0,1]
, by the formula

[G(u,s)](x):=(1-s)F(u)(x)+s\omega(x)u(x)\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

We claim that there exists
R>0
such that if
u
is a bounded in

solution of the semiflow
\Psi^{(s)}
for some

, then
\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}<R
for all

. If we suppose to the contrary, then there is a sequence of
(s_{m})
in
[0,1]
such that, for any

, there exists a bounded solution
u_{m}
of the semiflow
\Psi^{(s_{m})}
such that

and
\|u_{m}(0)\|_{H^{1}}\geqslant\rho_{m}-1
. Taking

for

, we can show in a similar manner as in the proof of (i), that

, which is a contradiction.

Therefore, if we take

N_{R}:=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\,\mid\,\|u\|_{H^{1}}\leqslant R\}
, then we have

K_{\infty}(\Phi)\subset N_{R}\quad\text{and}\quad K_{s}:=\mathrm{Inv}_{\Psi^{(s)}}\,N_{R}\subset\mathrm{int}\,N_{R}\ \text{ for all }\ s\in[0,1].

Similarly as in the point
(i)
, Proposition

and Corollary
4.7
show that the set

is admissible with respect to the family of semiflows

. Hence, we can apply the homotopy property (H4) to get

(5.5)

h(\Phi,K_{\infty}(\Phi))=h(\Psi^{(0)},K_{0})=h(\Psi^{(1)},K_{1}).

Since
\Psi^{(1)}
is the semiflow generated by the equation (

) with
\alpha
replaced by
\omega
and
\mathrm{Ker}\,(-A+m_{\omega})=\{0\}
, we can again apply Theorem

to obtain

h(\Psi^{(1)},K_{1})=\Sigma^{d^{-}(V-\omega,\,\lambda)}.

Combining the above equation with (

) completes the proof of the assertion (ii).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

From Theorem
5.1
(ii) it follows that
K_{\infty}(\Phi)
is an isolated invariant set
with respect to
\Phi
and

h(\Phi,K_{\infty}(\Phi))=\Sigma^{d^{-}(V-\omega,\,\lambda)}.

On the other hand, by Theorem (
5.1
) (i),
K_{0}=\{0\}
is also an isolated
\Phi
-invariant set and

h(\Phi,K_{0})=\Sigma^{d^{-}(V-\alpha,\,\lambda)}.

Since, by assumption,

, we infer that

\overline{0}\neq\Sigma^{d^{-}(V-\alpha,\,\lambda)}=h(\Phi,K_{0})\neq h(\Phi,K_{\infty}(\Phi))=\Sigma^{d^{-}(V-\omega,\,\lambda)}\neq\overline{0}.

This together with Theorem

, implies the existence of a nontrivial full bounded solution

for the semiflow
\Phi

or

. Recall that, in view of Proposition

,

both
\alpha(u)
and
\omega(u)
consists of equilibria. Since
u
is nontrivial and bounded and hence either

or

contains a nonzero equilibrium point. Then the zero solution is in the remaining limit set of

and the proof is completed.
∎

The resonant case

Throughout the whole section we assume that at least the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
are satisfied, i.e.

f
satisfies
(f1)^{\prime}
and
(f2)
,

is a Kato-Rellich type potential,
\varrho(V_{\infty})>\lambda
and
\lambda\in\sigma(-\Delta+V)
. Let us define
G:H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times[0,1]\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by

(6.1)

G(u,s):=PF(Pu+sQu)+sQF(Pu+sQu)\quad\text{for}\ \ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ s\in[0,1],

where

and

Lemma 6.1
.

The mapping
G
satisfies conditions
(G1)
,
(G2)
and there exists

such that, for all

,

(6.2)

|G(u,s)(x)|\leqslant b(x)\ \text{ for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Proof.

Indeed, for
(G1)
note that, by (

), there is

such that, for all

and

\displaystyle\|G(u,s)-G(v,s)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\|F(Pu+sQu)-F(Pv+sQv)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2L\|P(u-v)+sQ(u-v)\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant 4L\|u-v\|_{L^{2}}.

Furthermore, by
(f1)^{\prime}
, we have

(6.3)

\displaystyle\|G(u,s)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\|F(Pu+sQu)\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant 2\|m\|_{L^{2}},\quad u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ s\in[0,1],

which implies

.

In order to verify the property (

and

G(u,s)=sF(Pu+sQu)+(1-s)PF(Pu+sQu)\quad\text{ almost everywhere on }\ \mathbb{R}^{N}.

If we take an orthogonal basis of

consisting of elements

\varphi_{k}
for

, then

(6.5)

\displaystyle PF(Pu+sQu)=\sum_{k=1}^{d}e_{k}(u,s)\varphi_{k},\quad u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ s\in[0,1],

\displaystyle e_{k}(u,s):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(Pu+sQu)\varphi_{k}\,dx,\quad 1\leqslant k\leqslant d.

Clearly, by
(f1)^{\prime}
, for all
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

,

\displaystyle|e_{k}(u,s)|\leqslant\|F(Pu+sQu)\|_{L^{2}}\|\varphi_{k}\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\|m\|_{L^{2}}\|\varphi_{k}\|_{L^{2}}.

and consequently, for a.e.

(6.6)

\displaystyle|PF(Pu+sQu)(x)|\leqslant\sum_{k=1}^{d}|e_{k}(u,s)||\varphi_{k}(x)|\leqslant\|m\|_{L^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|\varphi_{k}\|_{L^{2}}|\varphi_{k}(x)|.

Combining (

gives, for all
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and a.e.

\displaystyle|G(u,s)(x)|\leqslant|m(x)|+|PF(Pu+sQu)(x)|.

and hence, by the inequality (

), the condition (

) holds with

given by

for a.e.

.
∎

Let us consider the family of semiflows
\{\Psi^{(s)}\}_{s\in[0,1]}

.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
K_{\infty}(\Psi)
be the set of all
\bar{u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that there exist

and a bounded solution

of
\Psi^{(s)}
with

. Then the set
QK_{\infty}(\Psi)
(the projection of

onto

) is bounded in

and relatively compact in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

Proof.

Let
u:\mathbb{R}\to H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a bounded solution of
\Psi^{(s)}
for some

. Then
u
is also a mild solution of the equation (

(6.7)

u(t)=S(t-t^{\prime})u(t^{\prime})+\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}S(t-\tau)G(u(\tau),s)\,d\tau,\quad t^{\prime}<t.

By applying the projection operator
Q_{+}
we get

Q_{+}u(t)=S(t-t^{\prime})Q_{+}u(t^{\prime})+\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}S(t-\tau)Q_{+}G(u(\tau),s)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

In view of (
3.3
) and the boundedness of

, by passing to the limit with

, we get

Q_{+}u(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}S(t-\tau)Q_{+}G(u(\tau),s)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

where the integral is convergent in

. Therefore, by (

) and (

(6.8)

\displaystyle\|Q_{+}u(t)\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{-\infty}^{t}K(t-\tau)^{-1/2}e^{-\rho_{+}(t-\tau)}\|Q_{+}G(u(\tau),s)\|_{L^{2}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leqslant 2K\|m\|_{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}z^{-1/2}e^{-\rho_{+}z}\,\mathrm{d}z

for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Let us recall that the restriction of
\{S(t)\}_{t\geqslant 0}
to the space

can be extended to a

-group
\{S(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on this space. Hence, applying with the projection
Q_{-}

-group property, we get

Q_{-}u(t^{\prime})=S(t^{\prime}-t)Q_{-}u(t)-\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t}S(t^{\prime}-\tau)Q_{-}G(u(\tau),s)\,d\tau,\quad t^{\prime}<t.

Using (
3.4
) and passing to the limit with

we obtain

(6.9)

Q_{-}u(t^{\prime})=-\int_{t^{\prime}}^{+\infty}S(t^{\prime}-\tau)Q_{-}G(u(\tau),s)\,d\tau,\quad t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R},

where the integral is convergent in
X=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Applying (
3.4
) and (
6.3
) to the equation (
6.9
) yields

\|Q_{-}u(t^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\int_{t^{\prime}}^{+\infty}e^{(t^{\prime}-\tau)\rho_{-}}\|Q_{+}G(u(\tau),s)\|_{L^{2}}\,d\tau\leqslant 2\|m\|_{L^{2}}\int_{t^{\prime}}^{+\infty}e^{(t^{\prime}-\tau)\rho_{-}}\,d\tau\leqslant 2\|m\|_{L^{2}}/\rho_{-}.

This combined with (
6.8
) implies the existence of
R_{\infty}>0
such that

(6.10)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|Qu(t)\|_{H^{1}}<R_{\infty},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}

for any full bounded solution
u
of
\Psi^{(s)}
with
s\in[0,1]
.

To show the relative compactness, we act on (
4.6
) with the operator
Q
and we write
w(t):=Qu(t)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, to obtain the equation

\displaystyle\dot{w}(t)=-Aw(t)+QG(u(t),s),\quad t>0.

By Proposition
3.2
, there exist a sequence
(\beta_{n})
with
\beta_{n}\to 0^{+}
as
n\to\infty
and
n_{0}\geqslant 1
, such that if
u
is a full solution of the semiflow
\Psi^{(s)}
, where
s\in[0,1]
, then

(6.11)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\langle\phi_{n}w(t),w(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}\leqslant-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(V_{\infty}-\lambda)\phi_{n}|w(t)|^{2}\,dx+\beta_{n}\|w(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\langle w(t),\phi_{n}QG(u(t),s)\rangle_{L^{2}}

for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. From the assumption
\varrho(V_{\infty})>\lambda
, there are
\varepsilon>0
and
n_{0}\geq 1
such that, for all
n\geqslant n_{0}
,

(6.12)

\displaystyle\phi_{n}(x)(V_{\infty}(x)-\lambda)\geq\varepsilon\phi_{n}(x)\quad\text{for a.a. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

By (
6.1
) and
(f1)^{\prime}
, we have

(6.13)

\displaystyle\|\phi_{n}QG(u(t),s)\|_{L^{2}}=\|\phi_{n}QF(Pu(t)+sQu(t))\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\|\phi_{n}m\|_{L^{2}}+\|\phi_{n}PF(Pu(t)+sQu(t))\|_{L^{2}}.

Since
m\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we have
\delta^{1}_{n}:=\|\phi_{n}m\|_{L^{2}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. On the other hand, the image of the map
F
is contained in the ball
B_{L^{2}}(0,\|m\|_{L^{2}})

and hence its projection onto the finite dimensional space
X_{0}
is relatively compact in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Therefore, by the Kolmogorov-Riesz theorem (see
[
9
]
), there is a sequence
(\delta^{2}_{n})
, depending on
\|m\|_{L^{2}}
, such that
\delta^{2}_{n}\to 0^{+}
as
n\to\infty
and
\|\phi_{n}PF(Pu(t)+sQu(t))\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant\delta^{2}_{n}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
s\in[0,1]
. Consequently, we get
\delta_{n}:=\delta^{1}_{n}+\delta^{2}_{n}
that is independent of
s
,
u
and
t
such that

\displaystyle\langle w(t),\phi_{n}QG(u(t),s)\rangle_{L^{2}}\leqslant\delta_{n}\|w(t)\|_{H^{1}},\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

and
\delta_{n}\to 0^{+}
as
n\to\infty
. Now we combine this with (
6.11
) and (
6.12
) and use (
6.10
) to get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\langle\phi_{n}w(t),w(t)\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant-\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{n}|w(t)|^{2}\,dx+\beta_{n}R_{\infty}^{2}+\delta_{n}R_{\infty},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

By integrating the above inequality we obtain, for all
n\geqslant n_{0}
and
t,r\in\mathbb{R}
with
t>r
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{n}|w(t)|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{-2\varepsilon(t-r)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{n}|w(r)|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}(1-e^{-2\varepsilon(t-r)})(\beta_{n}R_{\infty}^{2}+\delta_{n}R_{\infty})

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{-2\varepsilon(t-r)}R^{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}(\beta_{n}R_{\infty}^{2}+\delta_{n}R_{\infty}).

Passing with
r\to-\infty
we see that, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\geqslant n_{0}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi_{n}|w(t)|^{2}\,dx\leqslant\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}(\beta_{n}R_{\infty}^{2}+\delta_{n}R_{\infty}),

which by the Rellich-Kondrachov theorem shows that
the set
QK_{\infty}(\Psi)
is relatively compact in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Thus the proof of the proposition is completed.
∎

From now on, we assume that
f
satisfies additionally one of the resonance conditions
(LL)_{\pm}
or

. Both Landesman-Lazer and sign conditions have geometric implications in the phase space of the parabolic semiflow
\Phi
.

Proposition 6.3
.

(see
[
4
, Lemma 5.2]
)
Let the map
F
be given by (
4.2
) and
M\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. If either

(i)
(LL)_{\pm}
holds and
M
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
; or

(ii)
(SR)_{\pm}
holds and
M
is relatively compact in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

then there exists
R_{0}>0
and
\alpha>0
such that, for all
v\in X_{0}
with
\|v\|_{L^{2}}\geqslant R_{0}
and
w\in M

\pm\langle v,F(v+w)\rangle_{L^{2}}>\alpha.

By Proposition
6.2
, there exists
R_{\infty}>0
such that for any bounded solution
u
of
\Psi^{(s)}
with
s\in[0,1]
,

(6.14)

Qu(t)\in B_{H^{1}}(0,R_{\infty}),\quad\text{ for all }\ t\in\mathbb{R},

and the set
QK_{\infty}(\Psi)
is relatively compact in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Therefore, by the use of Proposition
6.3
, we can choose
R_{0}>0
such that

(6.15)

\displaystyle\pm\langle v,F(v+sw)\rangle_{L^{2}}>\alpha\quad\text{for \ $s\in[0,1]$, $w\in QK_{\infty}(\Psi)$ \ and \ $v\in X_{0}$ \ with \ $\|v\|_{L^{2}}\geqslant R_{0}$}

(the sign in (
6.15
) is the same as in the Landesman-Lazer or sign condition that is satisfied by
f
).
Now let us define

(6.16)

N:=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\mid\|Qu\|_{H^{1}}\leqslant R_{\infty}\ \text{ and }\ \|Pu\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant R_{0}\}.

The set
N
appears to be an isolating neighborhood with respect to the family
\Psi
and plays a key rule in the Conley index formula derivation that is the main result of the section.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
\Phi
be the semiflow generated by (
4.1
) and
K_{\infty}(\Phi)
be the set of all
\bar{u}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, for which there exists a bounded solution
u:\mathbb{R}\to H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of
\Phi
such that
u(0)=\bar{u}
. Then
K_{\infty}(\Phi)
is an isolated invariant set such that

(6.17)

K_{\infty}(\Phi)=\mathrm{Inv}_{\Phi}(N)\subset\mathrm{int}\,N

where
N
is given by (
6.16
) and its Conley index with respect to
\Phi
is given by the formula

(6.18)

h(\Phi,K_{\infty}(\Phi))=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Sigma^{d}&&\text{ if $(LL)_{+}$ or $(SR)_{+}$ holds},\\
&\Sigma^{d^{-}}&&\text{ if $(LL)_{-}$ or $(SR)_{-}$ holds},\end{aligned}\right.

where
d^{-}:=d^{-}(V,\lambda)
and
d:=d^{-}+\dim\mathrm{Ker}\,(-\Delta+V-\lambda)
.

Proof.

Let
u:\mathbb{R}\to H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be an arbitrary bounded solution of of
\Psi^{(s)}
for some
s\in[0,1]
. We show that
u(R)\subset\mathrm{int}\,N
. Indeed, suppose to the contrary, that, for some
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, either
\|Qu(t_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\geqslant R_{\infty}
or
\|Pu(t_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\geqslant R_{0}
. From (
6.14
) it follows that
\|Pu(t_{0})\|_{L^{2}}=R_{0}
and, by the geometric condition (
6.15
)

\pm\left.\frac{d}{dt}\|Pu(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right|_{t=t_{0}}=\pm 2\langle Pu(t_{0}),PG(u(t_{0}),s)\rangle_{L^{2}}=\pm 2\langle Pu(t_{0}),F(Pu(t_{0})+sQu(t_{0}))\rangle_{L^{2}}>2\alpha.

This in turn implies that either
\|Pu(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geqslant R_{0}^{2}+2\alpha(t-t_{0})
for
t>t_{0}
(or
\|Pu(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geqslant R_{0}^{2}+2\alpha(t_{0}-t)
for
t<t_{0}
, respectively), which means that
u
would not be bounded. This gives a contradiction and proves the desired inclusion. Consequently (
6.17
) holds. Furthermore, by Corollary
4.5
,
N
is admissible with respect to
\{\Psi^{(s)}\}_{s\in[0,1]}
and, by the homotopy invariance of Conley index – see (H4) in Section 2, we get

h(\Phi,K_{\infty}(\Phi))=h(\Psi^{(1)},K_{1})=h(\Psi^{(0)},K_{0}).

Now we observe that
\Psi^{(0)}
is conjugate to the semiflow
\Phi_{P}\times\Phi_{Q}
,
where
\Phi_{P}:X_{0}\to X_{0}
is generated by
\dot{v}=PF(v)
, whereas the semiflow
\Phi_{Q}:Y\to Y
, where
Y=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap(X_{+}\oplus X_{-})
is the linear space defined in Theorem
3.1
, is generated by the linear equation
\dot{w}=-A_{|Y}w
for
t>0
. In particular

(6.19)

h(\Psi^{(0)},K_{0})=h(\Phi_{P}\times\Phi_{Q},\mathrm{Inv}_{\Phi_{P}}\,(N_{P})\times\mathrm{Inv}_{\Phi_{Q}}\,(N_{Q})),

where
N_{P}:=N\cap X_{0}=\{u\in X_{0}\mid\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leqslant R_{0}\}
and
N_{Q}:=N\cap Y=\{u\in Y\mid\|u\|_{H^{1}}\leqslant R_{\infty}\}
.

By use of Theorem
3.1
, we have
\mathrm{Inv}_{\Phi_{Q}}\,(N_{Q})=\{0\}

and

(6.20)

h(\Phi_{Q},\{0\})=\Sigma^{d_{-}}.

Clearly, if
u_{P}:[\delta_{1},\delta_{2})\to X_{0}
, where
\delta_{1}>0
and
\delta_{2}\geq 0
, is a solution of the flow
\Phi_{P}
and
u_{P}(0)\in\partial N_{P}
, then

\|u_{P}(0)\|_{L^{2}}=R_{0}
and, by the use of (
6.15
), we have

\pm\left.\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{P}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right|_{t=0}=\pm 2\langle u_{P}(0),PF(u_{P}(0))\rangle_{L^{2}}=\pm 2\langle u_{P}(0),F(Pu_{P}(0))\rangle_{L^{2}}>2\alpha.

This implies that the set
N_{P}
is an isolating block for
\Phi_{P}
with the exist set

(6.21)

N_{P}^{-}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\{u\in X_{0}\mid\|u\|_{L^{2}}=R_{1}\}&&\text{ if $(LL)_{+}$ or $(SR)_{+}$ holds},\\
&\emptyset&&\text{ if $(LL)_{-}$ or $(SR)_{-}$ holds}.\end{aligned}\right.

Therefore we have

(6.22)

h(\Phi_{P},\mathrm{Inv}_{\Phi_{P}}\,(N_{P}))=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Sigma^{\dim X_{0}}&&\text{ if $(LL)_{+}$ or $(SR)_{+}$ holds},\\
&\Sigma^{0}&&\text{ if $(LL)_{-}$ or $(SR)_{-}$ holds}.\end{aligned}\right.

By (
6.19
) and (
6.20
) and the multiplication property of the Conley index – see (H3) in Section 2, we have

\displaystyle h(\Psi^{(0)},K_{0})

\displaystyle=h(\Phi_{P}\times\Phi_{Q},\mathrm{Inv}_{\Phi_{P}}\,(N_{P})\times\{0\})

\displaystyle=h(\Phi_{P},\mathrm{Inv}_{\Phi_{P}}\,(N_{P}))\wedge h(\Phi_{Q},\{0\})

\displaystyle=h(\Phi_{P},\mathrm{Inv}_{\Phi_{P}}\,(N_{P}))\wedge\Sigma^{d^{-}},

which together with (
6.22
) yields the required index formula (
6.18
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Note that in view of Theorem
6.4
we have
h(\Phi,K_{\infty}(\Phi))=\Sigma^{k_{\infty}}
, where

(6.23)

k_{\infty}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &d^{-}(V,\lambda)+\dim\,X_{0}&&\text{ if $(LL)_{+}$ or $(SR)_{+}$ holds},\\
&d^{-}(V,\lambda)&&\text{ if $(LL)_{-}$ or $(SR)_{-}$ holds}.\end{aligned}\right.

This implies that the Conley index of

is nontrivial and, in view of the existence property of Conley index – see (H1), we infer that
K_{\infty}(\Phi)\neq\emptyset
. Hence there exists a full bounded solution for
\Phi
, which allows us to apply the argument with Lyapunov functional (
4.5
) from the proof of Theorem
1.1
, to get that the sum
\alpha(u)\cup\omega(u)
contains the desired stationary solution.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By Theorem
5.1
(i) we see that
K_{0}=\{0\}
is an isolated invariant set
with respect to
\Phi
and
h(\Phi,K_{0})=\Sigma^{d^{-}(V-\alpha,\,\lambda)}
. In view of Theorem

,
K_{\infty}(\Phi)
is also an isolated
\Phi
-invariant set and
h(\Phi,K_{\infty}(\Phi))=\Sigma^{k_{\infty}}
, where

is given by (
6.23
).
In both cases (i) and (ii) we have

\overline{0}\neq\Sigma^{d^{-}(V-\alpha,\,\lambda)}=h(\Phi,K_{0})\neq h(\Phi,K_{\infty}(\Phi))=\Sigma^{k_{\infty}}\neq\overline{0}.

Therefore, in virtue of Theorem
2.1
, there exists a full solution
u:\mathbb{R}\to H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
that is nontrivial and either
\alpha(u)\subset K_{0}
or
\omega(u)\subset K_{0}
.
Using the Lyapunov functional (
4.5
),
we derive that either
\alpha(u)
or
\omega(u)
contains a nonzero equilibrium point and the zero solution is the other side limit set of

. Thus the proof of the theorem is completed.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.4
.

If (i) holds, then we have
d^{-}(V-\alpha,\lambda)=d^{-}(V,\bar{\alpha}+\lambda)\leqslant d^{-}(V,\lambda)<k_{\infty}
, where
k_{\infty}
is given by (
6.23
). In case of (ii) we get
d^{-}(V-\alpha,\lambda)=d^{-}(V,\bar{\alpha}+\lambda)>d^{-}(V,\lambda)=k_{\infty}
, since
d^{-}(V,\bar{\alpha}+\lambda)-d^{-}(V,\lambda)\geqslant\dim\,X_{0}
due to the fact that
\bar{\alpha}>0
.
When (iii) holds, then obviously
k_{\infty}=d^{-}(V,\lambda)+\dim\,X_{0}<d^{-}(V,\bar{\alpha}+\lambda)=d^{-}(V-\alpha,\lambda)
, since, by assumption, there is at least one eigenvalue in the interval
(\lambda,\bar{\alpha}+\lambda)
.
Finally, if (iv) holds, then in a similar way, we obtain
k_{\infty}=d^{-}(V,\lambda)>d^{-}(V,\bar{\alpha}+\lambda)=d^{-}(V-\alpha,\lambda)
.
In all four cases we use Theorem
1.3
to derive the assertion.
∎

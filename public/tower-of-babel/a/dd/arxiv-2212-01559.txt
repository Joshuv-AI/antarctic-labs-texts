A Global Maximum Principle for Controlled Conditional Mean-field FBSDEs with Regime Switching
Tao Hao, Jiaqiang Wen, Jie Xiong

Abstract




Abstract.
This paper is devoted to a global stochastic maximum principle for conditional mean-field forward-backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs, for short) with regime switching.
The control domain is unnecessarily convex and the driver of backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs, for short) could depend on
Z
.
Different from the case of non-recursive utility, the first-order and second-order adjoint equations are both high-dimensional
linear BSDEs.
Based on the adjoint equations, we reveal the relations among the terms of the first- and second-order Taylor’s expansions.
A general maximum principle is proved, which develops the work of Nguyen, Yin, and Nguyen
[
22
]
to recursive utility.
As applications, the linear-quadratic problem is considered and a problem with state constraint is studied.

Key words:
Regime switching, conditional mean-field FBSDE, maximum principle, adjoint equation, variational equation.

AMS subject classifications.
60H10, 60H30.

Introduction

Let
T>0
and
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
be a filtered probability space satisfying the usual conditions, on which a
1
-dimensional standard
Brownian motions
W
and a continuous-time Markov chain
\alpha(\cdot)
with a finite state space
{\cal I}=\{1,2,\cdot\cdot\cdot,I\}
are defined. The generator of
Markov chain
\alpha(\cdot)
is denoted by
\Lambda=(\lambda_{ij})_{i,j\in{\cal I}}
, which satisfies
\lambda_{ij}\geqslant 0
, for
i\neq j\in{\cal I}
and

\sum\limits_{j\in{\cal I}}\lambda_{ij}=0
, for every
i\in{\cal I}
. For each
s>0
, we set

\mathscr{F}^{\alpha}_{s}=\sigma\{\alpha(r):0\leqslant r\leqslant s\}\vee\mathscr{N},\quad\mathscr{F}_{s}=\sigma\{W(r),\alpha(r):0\leqslant r\leqslant s\}\vee\mathscr{N},

where
\mathscr{N}
is the set of all
\mathbb{P}
-null subsets, and denote
\mathbb{F}^{\alpha}=(\mathscr{F}^{\alpha}_{s})_{s\in[0,T]},\ \mathbb{F}=(\mathscr{F}_{s})_{s\in[0,T]}
.

In this paper, we consider the following forward-backward control system:

\hskip-10.81218pt\left\{\begin{aligned} dX^{v}(t)&=b(t,X^{v}(t),\mathbb{E}[X^{v}(t)|\mathscr{F}^{\alpha}_{t-}],v(t),\alpha(t-))dt\\
&\quad+\sigma(t,X^{v}(t),\mathbb{E}[X^{v}(t)|\mathscr{F}^{\alpha}_{t-}],v(t),\alpha(t-))dW(t),\ t\in[0,T],\\
X^{v}(0)&=x,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

and

\hskip-10.81218pt\left\{\begin{aligned} -dY^{v}(t)&=f(t,X^{v}(t),\mathbb{E}[X^{v}(t)|\mathscr{F}^{\alpha}_{t-}],Y^{v}(t),Z^{v}(t),v(t),\alpha(t-))dt-Z^{v}(t)dW(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
Y^{v}(T)&=\Phi(X^{v}(T),\mathbb{E}[X^{v}(T)|\mathscr{F}^{\alpha}_{T-}],\alpha(T)).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

The precise assumptions on the coefficients
b,\sigma,f,\Phi
refer to Section 3. The cost functional is defined by

J(v(\cdot))=Y^{v}(0).

(1.3)

The purpose of present paper is to give a necessary condition to the optimal control
\bar{v}(\cdot)
, which minimizes the cost functional

J(v(\cdot))
over
{\cal V}_{0,T}
(see
2.1
).

Let us explain the motivation to investigate the equation (
1.1
) for the angle of particle system. Consider an interacting particle
system:

\hskip-10.81218pt\left\{\begin{aligned} dX^{i,N}(t)&=b(t,X^{i,N}(t),\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}X^{j,N}(t),v^{i}(t),\alpha(t-))dt\\
&\quad+\sigma(t,X^{i,N}(t),\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N}X^{j,N}(t),v^{i}(t),\alpha(t-))dW^{i}(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
X^{i,N}(0)&=x,\quad i=1,2,\cdot\cdot\cdot,N,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

where
\{W^{i};1\leqslant i\leqslant N\}
is
N
independent standard
1
-dimensional Brownian motions. In many applications, the state process
X^{i,N}

is observable while the Brownian motion
W^{i}
, which is used to model the distribution of
X^{i,N}
, may not be observable. Hence, the closed-loop form of the control
v^{i}
is usually used, i.e.,

v^{i}(t)=\psi(t,X^{i,N}(t),\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}X^{j,N}(t),\alpha(t-)).

Here
\psi
is a deterministic function depending on the time, the stage, the average state and the switching state. In order to reduce the
computation complexity of the above particle system, generally speaking, let
N\rightarrow\infty
and consider its limit. As stated in many
literatures, for example,
[
4
,
5
]
, according to the law of large numbers, one usually has

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}X^{j,N}(t)\rightarrow\mathbb{E}[X^{v}(t)].

But notice that the appearance of
\alpha(\cdot)
in all the dynamics of (
1.4
) can lead to the dynamic of each particle depending on
the history of this process. Consequently, both the mean-field term
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}X^{j,N}
and its limit (as
N\rightarrow\infty
) depend
on the history of
\alpha
. Thereby, as
N\rightarrow\infty
, we obtain the conditional mean-field SDE (
1.1
), see
[
20
]
for
more details.

Next, let us overview the history of general maximum principles. As we know, to derive maximum principles, namely, necessary conditions for optimality, is an important approach in solving optimal control problems. Its history can be traced back to the work by
Boltyanski-Gamkrelidze-Pontryagin
[
2
]
in 1956 on the Pontryagin’s maximum principle for deterministic control
systems. They introduced the spike variation, and considered the first-order term in a kind of Taylor expansion with respect to this perturbation.
However, if the diffusion term in stochastic control systems depends on control, the approach introduced by

[
2
]
does not work. The reason is that the order of
\int_{t}^{t+\varepsilon}\sigma(t)dW_{t}
is

\sqrt{\varepsilon}
but not
\varepsilon
. Later, Peng
[
17
]
considered the second-order term in the Taylor expansion of the variation to solve this
difficulty, and obtained the global maximum principle for classical stochastic optimal control problems.

In 1993, Peng
[
18
]
generalized the classical stochastic optimal control problem to one where the cost functional is
defined by
Y(0)
, where
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot))
is the solution to the following BSDE:

\hskip-10.81218pt\left\{\begin{aligned} -dY^{v}(t)&=f(t,X^{v}(t),Y^{v}(t),Z^{v}(t),v(t))dt-Z^{v}(t)dW(t),\quad t\in[0,T],\\
Y^{v}(T)&=\Phi(X^{v}(T)).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.5)

The notion of recursive utilities in continuous time is firstly proposed by Duffie, Epstein
[
10
]
. El Karoui, Peng and Quenez
[
12
]
developed this notion, and defined a more general class of stochastic recursive utilities in economic theory by solutions of BSDEs.

When the control domain is convex, one can use the technique of convex variation to obtain a local stochastic maximum principle,
see, for example, Dokuchaev and Zhou
[
8
]
, Ji and Zhou
[
16
]
,
Wu
[
25
]
, Xu
[
27
]
. However, if the control domain is nonconvex, an essential difficulty is how to construct the first-order and second-order expansions for the BSDE (
1.5
). It is proposed as an open problem in Peng
[
19
]
.

A method for solving this problem is to regard
Z(\cdot)
as a control process and the terminal condition
Y(T)=\Phi(X^{v}(T))
as a
constraint, and apply the Ekeland variational principle to obtain the maximum principle, see Wu
[
25
]
, Yong
[
28
]
. But the maximum principle obtained by this method contains unknown parameters. In 2017, Hu
[
13
]
firstly brought in a new second-order Taylor expansion for the BSDE (
1.5
), and solved the open problem proposed by Peng completely. Hu, Ji and Xue
[
14
]
generalized Hu’s work to the fully coupled forward-backward stochastic control systems. Hu, Ji and Xu
[
15
]

obtained a global stochastic maximum principle for forward-backward stochastic control systems with quadratic generators.

As for stochastic maximum principle for mean-field control systems, we refer to Andersson and Djehiche

[
1
]
for a local maximum principle for SDEs of mean-field type, to Buckdahn, Djehiche and Li
[
6
]
for a global stochastic maximum principle for SDEs of mean-field type, to Buckdahn, Li and Ma
[
7
]
for a global stochastic maximum principle for general mean-field systems.
Recently, Nguyen, Yin, Hoang
[
20
]
proved the laws of large numbers for systems with mean-field interactions and Markovian switching.
Making use of this result, Nguyen, Nguyen and Yin
[
21
]
obtained a local maximum principle for mean-field type control problems of switching diffusion.
Subsequently, Nguyen, Yin and Nguyen
[
22
]
relaxed the convexity assumption on control domain to the non-convex case.

In this paper, we develop the work of Nguyen, Yin and Nguyen
[
22
]
to recursive utilities.
It is nontrivial. This is reflected in the following three aspects.

(i)

For the non-recursive utilities case, the purpose of constructing first-order adjoint equation
is to use the following equality

\mathbb{E}[Y^{1,\varepsilon}(t)]=\mathbb{E}\big[p(t)X^{1,\varepsilon}(t)\big],

where
X^{1,\varepsilon}(\cdot)
and
Y^{1,\varepsilon}(\cdot)
are the solutions to the first-order variational equations of SDE (
1.1
) and BSDE (
1.2
);
p(\cdot)
is the solution to the
first-order adjoint equation. According to the fact

\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[X^{1,\varepsilon}(t)|\mathscr{F}^{\alpha}_{t-}]\Phi(t)]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[\Phi(t)|\mathscr{F}^{\alpha}_{t-}]X^{1,\varepsilon}(t)],

where
\Phi(t)
is an
\mathscr{F}_{t}
-measurable stochastic process, the first-order adjoint equation can be written as a linear conditional mean-field BSDE (see
[
22
]
for example). However, the above approach is not suitable for the
recursive utilities case, since
f
depends on
(y,z)
. In fact, we need the following slightly “stronger” relation,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.,
t\in[0,T]
,

Y^{1,\varepsilon}(t)=p_{0}(t)X^{1,\varepsilon}(t)+p_{1}(t)\mathbb{E}[X^{1,\varepsilon}(t)|\mathscr{F}^{\alpha}_{t-}].

(1.6)

From this, one can know that the first-order adjoint equation should be a high-dimensional BSDE (without conditional mean-field term)
(see (
2.3
)-(
2.4
)).

(ii)

Due to the appearance of
p_{1}(\cdot)
in our method, it comes naturally to deal with the term

p_{1}(t)\mathbb{E}[X^{2,\varepsilon}(t)|\mathscr{F}^{\alpha}_{t-}]
when deducing the second-order expansion of BSDEs. But the order of the term

\mathbb{E}[(X^{1,\varepsilon}(t))^{2}|\mathscr{F}^{\alpha}_{t-}]
appearing in
\mathbb{E}[X^{2,\varepsilon}(t)|\mathscr{F}^{\alpha}_{t-}]
is
O(\varepsilon)
, but not
o(\varepsilon)
.
This leads to the order of
p_{1}(t)\mathbb{E}[X^{2,\varepsilon}(t)|\mathscr{F}^{\alpha}_{t-}]
being
O(\varepsilon)
, but not
o(\varepsilon)
.
Based on the point above, our second-order adjoint equation is used to deal not only with the resulting impact of
(X^{1,\varepsilon}(t))^{2}
, but
also with that of
\mathbb{E}[(X^{1,\varepsilon}(t))^{2}|\mathscr{F}^{\alpha}_{t-}].

This means that in our case the second-order adjoint equation should also be a high-dimensional BSDE, see (
2.5
).
Note that the first component of (
2.5
) is just the second-order adjoint equation (4.2)
[
22
]
for the non-recursive utilities case.

(iii)

Since the diffusion term
\sigma
depends on control, the first- and second-order variational equations for the
BSDE (
1.2
) (see (
3.7
), (
4.2
)) involve the term
p^{0}(t)\delta\sigma(t,v(t))\mathbf{1}_{E_{\varepsilon}}(t)
in the variation of
z
.
But, since its order is
O(\varepsilon)
for any order expansion of
f
, we need to consider a new second-order Taylor expansion, and
introduce an auxiliary BSDE (
4.3
) to handle this obstacle.

Compared with the existing literatures, three points should be lighten.
First, since the coefficient
f
depends on
(y,z)
and the conditional mean-field term, we establish two pair of new equalities to reveal the
relations among those terms of the first- and second-order Taylor expansions of conditional mean-field FBSDEs, see (
3.8
), (
4.4
).
Second, different to the non-recursive utilities case, the first- and second-order adjoint equations in our case are two high-dimensional linear BSDEs (without conditional mean-field term) (see (
2.3
), (
2.5
)).
Third, a global maximum principle for conditional mean-field FBSDE (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) is proved, which extends the work of Nguyen et al.
[
21
]
from the non-recursive utilities case to the recursive utilities case (
Theorem 2.5
).

This paper is arranged as follows. In Section 2, the first- and second-order adjoint equations as well as the maximum principle are
shown.
The first-order Taylor expansion of the FBSDE (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) and some estimates are supplied in Section 3.
The Section 4 is devoted to the second-order Taylor expansion of the FBSDE (
1.1
)-(
1.2

We study the linear-quadratic case in Section 5.
A problem with state constrain is investigated in Section 6.
In Section 7, some concluding remarks are listed.

&\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\delta^{1}\widehat{X}(t)|^{2}\cdot\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\delta^{1}X(t)|^{2}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\delta^{1}\widehat{X}(t)|^{2}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\delta^{1}Y(t)|^{2}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+(\int_{0}^{T}|\delta^{2}Z(t)+Z^{1,\varepsilon}(t)-p^{0}(t)\delta\sigma(t,v(t))\mathbf{1}_{E_{\varepsilon}}(t)|dt)^{2}\Big)\bigg]\\
&\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\delta^{1}\widehat{X}(t)|^{2}\cdot\Big(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\delta^{1}X(t)|^{2}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\delta^{1}\widehat{X}(t)|^{2}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\delta^{1}Y(t)|^{2}\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{T}|\delta^{2}Z(t)|^{2}dt+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{1,\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}\cdot\int_{0}^{T}|p^{0}(t)\sigma_{x}(t)+q^{0}(t)|^{2}dt\\
&\quad+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\widehat{X}^{1,\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}\cdot\int_{0}^{T}|p^{0}(t)\sigma_{x^{\prime}}(t)+q^{1}(t)|^{2}dt\Big)\bigg]\leqslant\varepsilon^{3}.\end{aligned}

\mathrm{d)}
As for

, thanks to the boundness of the derivatives of

with respect to

,
3.2
,
3.3
,
3.4

we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|I_{2}(t)|dt\Big)^{2}\bigg]\\
&\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\Big|\Big(\delta^{2}X(t)+\delta^{2}Y(t)+\delta^{2}Z(t)\Big)\Big(\delta^{1}X(t)+\delta^{1}Y(t)+\delta^{1}Z(t)-p^{0}(t)\delta\sigma(t,v(t))\mathbf{1}_{E_{\varepsilon}}(t)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+X^{1,\varepsilon}(t)+Y^{1,\varepsilon}(t)+Z^{1,\varepsilon}(t)\Big)\Big|dt\Big)^{2}\bigg]\\
&\leqslant C\Bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\delta^{2}X(t)|^{4}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\delta^{2}Y(t)|^{4}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|Z(t)|^{2}dt\Big)^{2}\Big]\Bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\\
&\qquad\Bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\delta^{1}X(t)+X^{1,\varepsilon}(t)|^{4}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\delta^{1}Y(t)+Y^{1,\varepsilon}(t)|^{4}\\
&\qquad+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|\delta^{1}Z(t)|^{2}+|[p^{0}(t)\sigma_{x}(t)+q^{0}(t)]X^{1,\varepsilon}(t)+[p^{0}(t)\sigma_{x^{\prime}}(t)+q^{1}(t)]\widehat{X}^{1,\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}dt\Big)^{2}\Big]\Bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\

\mathrm{e)}
Next, we analyse

. Thanks to the boundness of
\sigma_{x},\sigma_{x^{\prime}}
and

, Hölder

3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|I_{3}(t)|dt\Big)^{2}\bigg]\\
&\leqslant C\mathbb{E}\bigg[\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\Big|X^{1,\varepsilon}(t)\widehat{X}^{1,\varepsilon}(t)\Big[(p_{0}(t)+1)(p_{0}(t)\sigma_{x^{\prime}}(t)+p_{1}(t)+q_{1}(t))+p_{0}(t)q_{1}(t)\sigma_{x}(t)\\
&\qquad+(p_{0}(t)\sigma_{x^{\prime}}(t)+p_{1}(t))(p_{0}(t)\sigma_{x}(t)+q_{0}(t))+q^{0}(t)q^{1}(t)\Big]\\
&\qquad+(\widehat{X}^{1,\varepsilon}(t))^{2}\Big[(p_{1}(t))^{2}+(p_{0}(t)\sigma_{x^{\prime}}(t)+p_{1}(t))(p_{0}(t)\sigma_{x^{\prime}}(t)+q_{1}(t))+p_{0}(t)q_{1}(t)\sigma_{x}(t)+(q^{1}(t))^{2}\Big]\Big|dt\bigg)^{2}\bigg].\end{aligned}

We just estimate the term
\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|X^{1,\varepsilon}(t)\widehat{X}^{1,\varepsilon}(t)q^{0}(t)q^{1}(t)dt\Big)^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|X^{1,\varepsilon}(t)\widehat{X}^{1,\varepsilon}(t)q^{0}(t)q^{1}(t)dt\Big)^{2}\bigg]\\
&\leqslant\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|\widehat{X}^{1,\varepsilon}(t)|^{8}\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{4}}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}|X^{1,\varepsilon}(t)|^{8}\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{4}}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|q^{0}(t)|^{2}dt\Big)^{4}\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{4}}\bigg\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|q^{1}(t)|^{2}dt\Big)^{4}\Big]\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{4}}\\
&\leqslant\varepsilon^{3}\end{aligned}

Consequently, one has
\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}|I_{3}(t)|dt\Big)^{2}\bigg]\leqslant\varepsilon^{3}.

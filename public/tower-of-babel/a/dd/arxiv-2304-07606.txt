Abstract
Let G G be graph with vertex set V V and order n = | V | n=|V| . A coalition in G G is a combination of two distinct sets, A ⊆ V A\subseteq V and B ⊆ V B\subseteq V , which are disjoint and are not dominating sets of G G , but their union A ∪ B A\cup B is a dominating set of G G . A coalition partition of G G is a partition 𝒫 = { S 1 , … , S k } \mathcal{P}=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}\} of its vertex set V V , where each set S i ∈ 𝒫 S_{i}\in\mathcal{P} is either a dominating set of G G with only one vertex, or it is not a dominating set but forms a coalition with some other set S j ∈ 𝒫 S_{j}\in\mathcal{P} . The coalition number C ⁡ ( G ) {C}(G) is the maximum cardinality of a coalition partition of G G . To represent a coalition partition 𝒫 \mathcal{P} of G G , a coalition graph CG ⁡ ( G , 𝒫 ) {\rm CG}(G,\mathcal{P}) is created, where each vertex of the graph corresponds to a member of 𝒫 \mathcal{P} and two vertices are adjacent if and only if their corresponding sets form a coalition in G G . A coalition partition 𝒫 \mathcal{P} of G G is a singleton coalition partition if every set in 𝒫 \mathcal{P} consists of a single vertex. If a graph G G has a singleton coalition partition, then G G is referred to as a singleton-partition graph. A graph H H is called a singleton coalition graph of a graph G G if there exists a singleton coalition partition 𝒫 \mathcal{P} of G G such that the coalition graph CG ⁡ ( G , 𝒫 ) {\rm CG}(G,\mathcal{P}) is isomorphic to H H . A singleton coalition graph chain with an initial graph G 1 G_{1} is defined as the sequence G 1 → G 2 → G 3 → ⋯ G_{1}\rightarrow G_{2}\rightarrow G_{3}\rightarrow\cdots where all graphs G i G_{i} are singleton-partition graphs, and CG ⁡ ( G i , Γ 1 ) = G i + 1 {\rm CG}(G_{i},\Gamma_{1})=G_{i+1} , where Γ 1 \Gamma_{1} represents a singleton coalition partition of G i G_{i} . In this paper, we address two open problems posed by Haynes et al. We characterize all graphs G G of order n n and minimum degree δ ⁡ ( G ) = 2 \delta(G)=2 such that C ⁡ ( G ) = n C(G)=n . Additionally, we investigate the singleton coalition graph chain starting with graphs G G where δ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ 2 \delta(G)\leq 2 .

4.1
G
-SC chain with
\delta(G)=0

We first consider the case when the SP-graph
G
contains an isolated vertex. We shall prove the following theorem.

Theorem 8

If
G
is an
SP-graph
of order
n
with
\delta(G)=0
, then the following properties hold.

(a)

If
n=1
, then
G=K_{1}
,
L_{\rm{SCC}}(G)=0
, and the
G
-SC chain is
K_{1}\rightarrow K_{1}\rightarrow K_{1}\rightarrow\cdots

(b)

If
n=2
, then
G=\overline{K}_{2}
,
L_{\rm{SCC}}(G)=\infty
, and the
G
-SC chain is
\overline{K}_{2}\rightarrow K_{2}\rightarrow\overline{K}_{2}\rightarrow K_{2}\rightarrow\cdots

(c)

If
n>3
, then
G\cong K_{1}\cup K_{n-1}
,
L_{\rm{SCC}}(G)=1
, and the
G
-SC chain is
G\rightarrow K_{1,n-1}
.

(d)

If
n=3
, then
G=K_{1}\cup K_{2}
,
L_{\rm{SCC}}(G)=\infty
, and the
G
-SC chain is

K_{1}\cup K_{2}\rightarrow P_{3}\rightarrow K_{1}\cup K_{2}\rightarrow P_{3}\rightarrow\cdots

Proof.

Let
G
be a SP-graph of order
n
with
\delta(G)=0
. If
n=1
, then
G=K_{1}
. In this case, the
K_{1}
-SC chain is given by the sequence

K_{1}\rightarrow K_{1}\rightarrow K_{1}\rightarrow\cdots
. This proves part (a).

(b) If
n=2
, then
G=\overline{K}_{2}
. In this case,
{\rm CG}(\overline{K}_{2},\Gamma_{1})=K_{2}
. Since
{\rm CG}(K_{2},\Gamma_{1})=\overline{K}_{2}
, the
\overline{K}_{2}
-SC chain is therefore given by the sequence

\overline{K}_{2}\rightarrow K_{2}\rightarrow\overline{K}_{2}\rightarrow K_{2}\rightarrow\cdots.

(c) and (d): Suppose that
n\geq 3
. By Theorem
1
,
G\cong K_{1}\cup K_{n-1}
. We now consider the graph
{\rm CG}(G,\Gamma_{1})
. Let
q
be the isolated vertex of
G
, and
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}\}
be the vertex set of
K_{n-1}
. Thus, the set
\{x_{i}\}
forms a coalition with
\{q\}
, and so
\tilde{x_{i}}
is adjacent to
\tilde{q}
in
{\rm CG}(G,\Gamma_{1})
for all
i\in[n-1]
. By Observation
1
, there is no coalition between the sets of
\{x_{i}\}
and
\{x_{j}\}
for

. Hence,
{\rm CG}(G,\Gamma_{1})
is the star
K_{1,n-1}
. We now consider the graph
K_{1,n-1}
, which has minimum degree
1
and contains exactly one full vertex. By Theorem
2
, the graph
K_{1,n-1}
is a SP-graph if and only if
n=3
. Hence if
n>3
, then the
G
-SC chain is given by the sequence
G\rightarrow K_{1,n-1}
, which proves part (c).
Suppose, finally, that
n=3
. In this case,
G=K_{1}\cup K_{2}
and
{\rm CG}(G,\Gamma_{1})=K_{1,2}\cong P_{3}
. Since
{\rm CG}(P_{3},\Gamma_{1})=K_{1}\cup K_{2}
, the
G
-SC chain for
G=K_{1}\cup K_{2}
is given by the sequence

K_{1}\cup K_{2}\rightarrow P_{3}\rightarrow K_{1}\cup K_{2}\rightarrow P_{3}\rightarrow\cdots

which completes the proof of part (d), and of Theorem
8
.
\Box

In this section, we consider the case when the SP-graph

(a)

that

We first next the case when the SP-graph

\overline{K}_{3}+\overline{K}_{2}
and
\left(\overline{K}_{2}+(K_{1}\cup K_{2})\right)
. Thus, in this case the
G
-SC chain is given by Part (d), (e), (f) or (g) in the statement of Lemma
3
.

Hence, we may assume that in this case
\delta(B)=2
, we have
|W^{\prime}|\geq 3
. Since
B
is an isolate-free SP-graph, by Theorem
5
we have
B\in{\cal F}_{2}
. Thus,
B\in{\cal F}_{2}^{1}\cup{\cal F}_{2}^{2}\cup{\cal F}_{2}^{3}
. We show that
B\notin{\cal F}_{2}^{3}
. Suppose, to the contrary, that
B\in{{\cal F}_{2}^{3}}
. Consider the vertices
x,y
and
z
, and the sets
W,R_{1},L_{1},R_{2}
and
L_{2}
used in the definition of
{\cal F}_{2}^{3}
(see Definition
3
).

Since
|W^{\prime}|\geq 3
, the vertex
x^{\prime}
has degree at least
3
in
B
. Since the vertex
x
has degree
2
in
{\cal F}_{2}^{3}
, the vertex
x^{\prime}\neq x
. If
x=z^{\prime}
and
y=y^{\prime}
, then
z\in W^{\prime}
. Hence,
W=\{x\}
and
W^{\prime}=X\cup L_{2}
. Since
W^{\prime}
is an independent set of
B
, there are no edges between the vertices of
X\cup L_{2}
, which contradicts the definition of the family
{\cal F}_{2}^{3}
. If
x=z^{\prime}
,
y\in W^{\prime}
and
z\in W^{\prime}
, then
y^{\prime},z^{\prime}\notin W
. Hence,
W\subseteq W^{\prime}
. If
|W\cap W^{\prime}|=1
, then it is not hard to see that
R_{2}=\emptyset
or
L_{1}=\emptyset
(see Definition
3
), which is a contradiction. If
|W\cap W^{\prime}|>1
, then since
W^{\prime}
is an independent set,
G[W]
is not a clique, which is a contradiction. The other cases for the selections of
x,y,
, and
z
analogously leads to a contradiction. Hence,
B\not\in{{\cal F}_{2}^{3}}
. Thus,
B\in{\cal F}_{2}^{1}\cup{\cal F}_{2}^{2}
. Hence by Theorem
7
,
{\rm CG}(B,\Gamma_{1})\in{\cal H}_{2}
and by Definition
4
, we infer that
{\rm CG}(B,\Gamma_{1})\in{\cal H}_{2}^{1}
or
{\rm CG}(B,\Gamma_{1})\in{\cal H}_{2}^{2}
. Hence, using Lemmas
1
and
2
, the
G
-SC chain is given by Part (l), (m), (n), (o), (p), (q), (r), (s), (t), or (u) in the statement of Lemma
3
.

Hence, we may assume that
\delta(B)=3
. If
y^{\prime}
is not adjacent to
z^{\prime}
, then
B\cong K_{3,n-3}
. In this case, the
G
-SC chain is given by Part (v) in the statement of Lemma
3
. If
y^{\prime}
is adjacent to
z^{\prime}
, then
B\cong(K_{1}\cup K_{2})+\overline{K}_{n-3}
. In this case, the
G
-SC chain is given by Part (w) in the statement of Lemma
3
. This completes the proof of Lemma
3
.

As an immediate consequence of Lemmas
1
,
2
, and
3
, we have the following result.

Theorem 12

If
G
is a
SP-graph
of order
n
with
\delta(G)=2
that contains no full vertex, then
L_{\rm{SCC}}(G)=\infty
or
L_{\rm{SCC}}(G)\leq 5
.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have addressed two open problems posed by Haynes et al (see
[
1
,
5
]
). The first problem was to characterize all graphs
G
of order
n
and minimum degree
\delta(G)=2
, such that
C(G)=n
. The second problem was to investigate singleton coalition graph chains. We showed that there exist singleton coalition graph chains with the infinite length. Moreover, we characterized the singleton coalition graph chains starting with the graphs
G
satisfying
\delta(G)\leq 2
. It would be interesting to characterize the singleton coalition graph chains starting with the graphs
G
satisfying
\delta(G)\geq 3
.

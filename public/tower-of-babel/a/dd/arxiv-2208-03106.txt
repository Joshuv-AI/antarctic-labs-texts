Scattering theory with both regular and singular perturbations
Andrea Mantile, Andrea Posilicano

Abstract
. We provide an asymptotic completeness criterion and a representation formula for the scattering matrix of the scattering couple ( A 𝖡 , A ) (A_{\mathsf{B}},A) , where both A A and A 𝖡 A_{\mathsf{B}} are self-adjoint operator and A 𝖡 A_{\mathsf{B}} formally corresponds to adding to A A two terms, one regular and the other singular. In particular, our abstract results apply to the couple ( Δ 𝖡 , Δ ) (\Delta_{\mathsf{B}},\Delta) , where Δ \Delta is the free self-adjoint Laplacian in L 2 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}) and Δ 𝖡 \Delta_{\mathsf{B}} is a self-adjoint operator in a class of Laplacians with both a regular perturbation, given by a short-range potential, and a singular one describing boundary conditions (like Dirichlet, Neumann and semi-transparent δ \delta and δ ′ \delta^{\prime} ones) at the boundary of a open, bounded Lipschitz domain. The results hinge upon a limiting absorption principle for A 𝖡 A_{\mathsf{B}} and a Kreĭn-like formula for the resolvent difference ( − A 𝖡 + z ) − 1 − ( − A + z ) − 1 (-A_{\mathsf{B}}+z)^{-1}-(-A+z)^{-1} which puts on an equal footing the regular (here, in the case of the Laplacian, a Kato-Rellich potential suffices) and the singular perturbations.



1.
Introduction

The mathematical scattering theory for short-range potential is a well developed subject; the existence and completeness of the wave operators can be obtained by two essentially different approaches: the trace-class method and the smooth method (see, e.g.,
[
22
]
). An important object defined in terms of the wave operators is the scattering operator and, even more important from the point of view of its physical applications, the scattering matrix, which is its reduction to a multiplication operator in the spectral representation of the self-adjoint free Laplacian.

The scattering problem for singular perturbations of self-adjoint operators, which is outside the original scope of these methods,
is connected with scattering from obstacles with impenetrable or semi-transparent boundary conditions (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
11
]
-
[
14
]
). On this side, a general scheme has been developed in
[
11
]
by combining the construction in
[
15
]
with an abstract version of the Limiting Absorption Principle (simply LAP in the following) due to W. Renger (see
[
18
]
) and a variant of the smooth method due to M. Schechter (see
[
19
]
). In particular, the results in
[
11
]
apply to obstacle scattering with a large class of interface conditions on Lipschitz hypersurfaces in any dimension. Let us recall that in
[
4
]
boundary triple theory and properties of the associated operator-valued Weyl functions were used to obtain a similar representation of the scattering matrix for singularly coupled self-adjoint extensions. It is worth to remark that, while the approach in
[
11
]
avoids any trace-class condition, these are needed in
[
4
]
and so the applications there are limited to the case of smooth obstacles in two dimensions.

The target of the present paper is to provide a general framework for the scattering with both potential type and singular perturbations. Since our concern is the scattering theory with respect to the free Laplacian, we regard the regular and the singular parts of the perturbation as a single object; this constitutes the main novelty of our approach. In particular, we give an abstract resolvent formula, generalizing the one in
[
15
]
, which puts on an equal footing the two components of the perturbation. Such a representation is a key ingredient in the derivation of LAP which leads then to the main results of the first part: the asymptotic completeness and an explicit formula for the scattering matrix. These results rely on a certain number of assumptions whose validity is carefully analyzed in the second part where we consider the specific case of a short range potential plus a distributional term, supported on a closed surface and describing self-adjoint interface conditions. In this way, we obtain new representation formulae for the scattering matrix which are expected to be relevant in different physical applications involving wave propagation in inhomogeneous media with impenetrable or semi-transparent obstacles.

Here, in more details, the contents of the paper.

\langle u,A_{\mathsf{B}}v\rangle_{\mathsf{H}}=\langle Au,v\rangle_{\mathsf{H}}+\langle\tau u,\rho_{\mathsf{B}}v\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{2},\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{2}^{*}}\,,\quad u\in\text{\rm dom}(A),\,v\in\text{\rm dom}(A_{\mathsf{B}})\,.

Proof.

Let
v=R_{z}^{\mathsf{B}}u=v_{z}+G_{z}\Lambda_{z}^{\!\mathsf{B}}\tau v_{z}
, where
v_{z}:=R_{z}u
(hence
\tau v_{z}=G_{\bar{z}}^{\ast}u
). Then

\displaystyle\langle u,A_{\mathsf{B}}v\rangle_{\mathsf{H}}-\langle Au,v\rangle_{\mathsf{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle u,(-A_{\mathsf{B}}+z)v\rangle_{\mathsf{H}}+\langle(-A+\bar{z})u,v\rangle_{\mathsf{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle u,(-A+z)v_{z}\rangle_{\mathsf{H}}+\langle(-A+\bar{z})u,v_{z}+G_{z}\Lambda_{z}^{\!\mathsf{B}}\tau v_{z}\rangle_{\mathsf{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(-A+\bar{z})u,G_{z}\Lambda_{z}^{\!\mathsf{B}}\tau v_{z}\rangle_{\mathsf{H}}=\langle\tau u,\Lambda_{z}^{\!\mathsf{B}}\tau v_{z}\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{2},\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{2}^{*}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(\pi_{1}\oplus 1)\tau u,\Lambda_{z}^{\!\mathsf{B}}\tau v_{z}\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{2},\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{2}^{*}}=\langle\tau u,(\pi_{1}^{\ast}\oplus 1)\Lambda_{z}^{\!\mathsf{B}}\tau v_{z}\rangle_{\mathfrak{h}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{2},\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{h}_{2}^{*}}\,.

Suppose now that
R_{z_{1}}^{\mathsf{B}}u_{1}=R_{z_{2}}^{\mathsf{B}}u_{2}
. Then, by the above
identities, one gets, for any
u\in\text{\rm dom}(A)
,

\langle\tau^{\ast}(\pi_{1}^{\ast}\oplus 1)(\Lambda_{z_{1}}^{\!\mathsf{B}}G_{\bar{z}_{1}}^{\ast}u_{1}-\Lambda_{z_{2}}^{\!\mathsf{B}}G_{\bar{z}_{2}}^{\ast}u_{2}),u\rangle_{\mathsf{H}_{A}^{*},\mathsf{H}_{A}}=0\,.

Hence
\tau^{\ast}((\pi_{1}^{\ast}\oplus 1)\Lambda_{z_{1}}^{\!\mathsf{B}}G_{\bar{z}_{1}}^{\ast}u_{1}-(\pi_{1}^{\ast}\oplus 1)\Lambda_{z_{2}}^{\!\mathsf{B}}G_{\bar{z}_{2}}^{\ast}u_{2})=0
. However,
\text{\rm ker}(\tau^{\ast})\cap\text{\rm ran}((\pi_{1}^{\ast}\oplus 1))=\{0\}
since
\pi_{1}^{\ast}\oplus 1
is the projector onto the subspace
orthogonal to
\text{\rm ker}(\tau^{\ast})
.
∎

The next Lemma provides a sort of abstract boundary conditions holding for the elements in
\text{\rm dom}(A_{\mathsf{B}})

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A_{\mathsf{B}}
be the self-adjoint operator in Theorem
2.1
. Then, for any
z\in\varrho(A_{\mathsf{B}})\cap\varrho(A)
, one has the representation

\text{\rm dom}(A_{\mathsf{B}})=\{u\in\mathsf{H}:u_{z}:=u-G_{z}\rho_{\mathsf{B}}u\in\text{\rm dom}(A)\}\,,

(-A_{\mathsf{B}}+z)u=(-A+z)u_{z}\,.

Moreover,

u\in\text{\rm dom}(A_{\mathsf{B}})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad(\pi_{1}^{*}B_{1}\oplus B_{2})\tau u=(1\oplus B_{0})\rho_{\mathsf{B}}u\,.

Proof.

Since
G_{z}=R_{z}\tau^{*}
(see (
2.12
) below) and
\pi_{1}^{*}\oplus 1
is the projection onto the orthogonal to
\text{\rm ker}(\tau^{*})
, one has
G_{z}=G_{z}(\pi_{1}^{*}\oplus 1)
.
Hence,
u\in\text{\rm dom}(A_{\mathsf{B}})
if and only if
u=R_{z}v+G_{z}(\pi_{1}^{*}\oplus 1)\Lambda_{z}^{\mathsf{B}}G^{*}_{\bar{z}}v=R_{z}v+G_{z}\rho_{\mathsf{B}}u
. Therefore,

\text{\rm dom}(A_{\mathsf{B}})=\{u\in\mathsf{H}:u=u_{z}+G_{z}\rho_{\mathsf{B}}u\,,\ u_{z}\in\text{\rm dom}(A)\}\,.

Moreover, given any
u\in\text{\rm dom}(A)
,
u=R^{\mathsf{B}}_{z}v
, one has

(-A+z)u_{z}=(-A+z)R_{z}v=(-A_{\mathsf{B}}+z)R^{\mathsf{B}}_{z}v=(-A_{\mathsf{B}}+z)u\,.

Finally, given
u=R^{\mathsf{B}}_{z}v\in\text{\rm dom}(A_{\mathsf{B}})
, one has

\displaystyle(\pi_{1}^{*}B_{1}\oplus B_{2})\tau u=(\pi_{1}^{*}\oplus 1)(B_{1}\oplus B_{2})\tau R^{\mathsf{B}}_{z}v

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pi_{1}^{*}\oplus 1)\big((B_{1}\oplus B_{2})G_{\bar{z}}v+(B_{1}\oplus B_{2})\tau G_{z}\big((1\oplus B_{0})-(B_{1}\oplus B_{2})\tau G_{z}\big)^{-1}(B_{1}\oplus B_{2})G_{\bar{z}}v\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\pi_{1}^{*}\oplus 1)(1\oplus B_{0})\Lambda^{\mathsf{B}}_{z}G_{\bar{z}}v=(1\oplus B_{0})(\pi_{1}^{*}\oplus 1)\Lambda^{\mathsf{B}}_{z}G_{\bar{z}}v=(1\oplus B_{0})\rho_{\mathsf{B}}u\,.

∎

3.
The Limiting Absorption Principle and the Scattering Matrix

Now, given the measure space

and

4.
Kato-Rellich perturbations and their layers potentials

4.1.
Potential perturbations

In this section we suppose that the real-valued potential
\mathsf{v}
is of Kato-Rellich type, i.e.,
\mathsf{v}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})+L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, equivalently,

(4.1)

\mathsf{v}=\mathsf{v}_{2}+\mathsf{v}_{\infty}\,,\qquad\mathsf{v}_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,,\qquad\mathsf{v}_{\infty}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,.

We use the same symbol
\mathsf{v}
to denote both the potential function and the corresponding multiplication operator
u\mapsto\mathsf{v}u
.

Given
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, open and bounded with a Lipschitz boundary
\Gamma
, we define
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma)\hookleftarrow H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
by

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma):=H^{s}(\Omega)\oplus H^{s}(\Omega_{\rm ex})\,,\qquad s\geq 0\,.

We refer to
[
16
, Chapter 3]
for the definition of the Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
H^{s}(\Omega)
and
H^{s}(\Gamma)
. One has

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma)=H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,,\qquad 0\leq s<1/2\,.

Since (see
[
16
, Theorems 3.29 and 3.30]
),

H^{s}(\mathcal{O})^{\ast}=H_{\overline{\mathcal{O}}}^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,,\qquad s\in\mathbb{R}\,,

H_{\overline{\mathcal{O}}}^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
denoting the set of distributions
H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with support in
{\overline{\mathcal{O}}}
,
one has

(4.2)

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma)^{\ast}=H^{s}(\Omega)^{\ast}\oplus H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\overline{\Omega})^{\ast}=H^{-s}_{\overline{\Omega}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\oplus H^{-s}_{\Omega^{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\,.

Let us notice that

(4.3)

\mathscr{B}(H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma),H^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma)^{\ast})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{B}(H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),H^{-t}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\,,\qquad s,t\geq 0\,,

and

(4.4)

\mathscr{B}(H^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),H^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\hookrightarrow\mathscr{B}(H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma)^{*},H^{t}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma))\,,\qquad s,t\geq 0\,.

Lemma 4.1
.

(4.5)

\mathsf{v}\in{\mathscr{B}}(H^{1+s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma),H^{1-s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma)^{*})\,,\qquad-1\leq s\leq 1\,.

Proof.

Given
u=u_{\rm in}\oplus u_{\rm ex}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma)
one has

\|\mathsf{v}_{\infty}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\|\mathsf{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\|\mathsf{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma)}\,.

and

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{v}_{2}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{v}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u_{\rm in}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\mathsf{v}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\overline{\Omega})}\|u_{\rm ex}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\overline{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{v}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u_{\rm in}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mathsf{v}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\overline{\Omega})}\|u_{\rm ex}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\overline{\Omega})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{v}_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma)}\,.

Hence
\mathsf{v}\in{\mathscr{B}}(H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
. Then, for any
u,v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma)
, one has

\displaystyle\big|\langle\mathsf{v}u,v\rangle_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma)^{*},H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma)}\big|=\big|\langle\mathsf{v}u,v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\langle u,\mathsf{v}v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\big|\leq\|\mathsf{v}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|v\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma)}

and so
u\mapsto\mathsf{v}u
extends to a map in
\mathscr{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma)^{*})
. The proof is then concluded by interpolation.
∎

In the following,
R_{z}
denotes the resolvent of the free Laplacian, i.e.,

Since

sufficiently far away from

and

(4.7)

(4.8)

(1-\mathsf{v}R_{z})^{-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\left(\mathsf{v}R_{z}\right)^{k}\in{\mathscr{B}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\,.

Remark 4.3
.

Let us notice that Theorem
4.2

and

Lemma 4.10

For any

The scaling limit of the weakly self-avoiding walk on a high-dimensional torus
Emmanuel Michta

Abstract
We prove that the scaling limit of the weakly self-avoiding walk on a d d -dimensional discrete torus is Brownian motion on the continuum torus if the length of the rescaled walk is o ⁡ ( V 1 / 2 ) o(V^{1/2}) where V V is the volume (number of points) of the torus and if d > 4 d>4 . We also prove that the diffusion constant of the resulting torus Brownian motion is the same as the diffusion constant of the scaling limit of the usual weakly self-avoiding walk on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} . This provides further manifestation of the fact that the weakly self-avoiding walk model on the torus does not feel that it is on the torus up until it reaches about V 1 / 2 V^{1/2} steps which we believe is sharp.

Introduction and results

1.1
Introduction

A self-avoiding walk (SAW) on any graph is a path on this
graph that does not visit any vertex more than once. The SAW-model consists in understanding properties of the SAW : How does the number of SAW of length
n
behaves as a function of
n
? How far does a uniformly chosen length-
n
SAW go? What is the scaling limit of such walks and how can it be proven to exist? etc. Historically and still today, most of the research on this topic
focuses on the case where the graph is a lattice and especially the euclidean lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
where the
dimension
d
plays a key role in the behaviour of this model. In dimension
d>4
the model is now very well understood following the introduction
of the
lace-expansion
by Brydges and Spencer
in 1985
[
5
]
in the context of the closely related
weakly self-avoiding walk
(WSAW). Hara and Slade developed the lace-expansion to prove that
the usual self-avoiding walk model
has
mean-field
behaviour in dimension
d\geq 5
in the early 90’s
[
9
,
11
,
10
]
which means in short that the SAW behaves asymptotically like the simple random walk. It was shown in this context
[
11
,
20
]
that the scaling limit of the SAW on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is Brownian
motion with some diffusion constant
D>1
. The scaling limit is also believed to be Brownian motion at the critical dimension
d_{c}=4
but with extra
\log
corrections to the rescaling factors
[
7
]
. Relatively recent rigorous results in that direction have been obtained through renormalization group arguments for the WSAW in
[
1
,
2
]
. In lower dimensions the picture is quite different. In two dimension, the scaling limit of the SAW is conjecturally the so-called Schramm-Loewner Evolution with parameter
\kappa=8/3
.
And in fact this is known to be the case under the widely open assumption that the scaling limit exists and is
conformally invariant following the breakthrough work of Lawler, Schramm and Werner
[
15
]
. In three dimensions there seems to be no good candidate as of today for the scaling limit and the model more generally remains poorly understood even from the physics standpoint.

Here we study the WSAW model where true SAW are assigned weight
1
and other walks get penalized for intersecting and have a small but non-zero weight. A more formal definition is given in the next subsection. We are interested in the model on a
d
-dimensional torus (a box with periodic boundary conditions) of sidelength
r\geq 3
where
d>4
. It was pointed out and partly proven in
[
17
]
that the WSAW on the torus behaves the same as its
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
counterpart provided that its length is less than
r^{d/2}
and that this should be sharp. In this article we study the scaling limit of the WSAW on the torus which exhibits again this interplay between
r
and
n
the length of the WSAW when both go to infinity. We show that the scaling limit of the WSAW is Brownian motion on the continuum torus if the walk that we rescale has length less than
r^{d/2}
and more than
r^{2}
, which is possible only if
d>4
. If the walk has length much less than
r^{2}
it is not possible to scale both the discrete torus into a continuum one and the walk into a continuous non-degenerate random curve and the model exhibits no interesting behaviour. Our main tool to prove the above is the lace expansion. Since its introduction in
[
5
]
, the lace expansion technology has been simplified and explained by several authors
[
16
,
21
,
4
]
(to which we refer for further background) as well as applied to a broad range of models (percolation, Ising,
\phi^{4}
, etc.) above their critical dimensions.

1.3
The weakly self-avoiding walk

An
n
-step walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is a function
\omega:\{0,1,\ldots,n\}\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
\|\omega(i)-\omega(i-1)\|_{1}=1
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
We let
\mathcal{W}_{n}
denote the set of
n
-step walks starting at
0
.
For an
n
-step walk
\omega
, and for
0\leq s<t\leq n
, we define

U_{st}(\omega)=\begin{cases}-1&(\omega(s)=\omega(t))\\
0&(\omega(s)\neq\omega(t))\end{cases}

(1.1)

and

K[0,n]=\prod_{0\leq s<t\leq n}(1+\beta U_{st}(\omega))

(1.2)

where we keep the dependency on
\omega
and
\beta
implicit to simplify the notation. Given
\beta\in[0,1]
, we define the

partition function

c_{n}=c_{n}(\beta)
by

c_{n}=\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal{W}_{n}}K[0,n].

(1.3)

The product in (
1.2
)
discounts
\omega
by a factor

1-\beta
for each pair
s,t
with an intersection
\omega(s)=\omega(t)
.
When
\beta=0
,
c_{n}
is simply the number of
n
-step walks and is thus equal to
(2d)^{n}
. For
\beta=1
,
c_{n}
is the number of
n
-step strictly self-avoiding walks.
The case
\beta\in(0,1)
is the
weakly self-avoiding walk
.
In particular, it is proved in
[
12
]

that for
d>4
with
\beta>0
sufficiently small

c_{n}=A\mu^{n}(1+O(\beta n^{-(d-4)/2})),

(1.4)

with
A=1+O(\beta)
and where
\mu=\mu(\beta,\mathbb{Z}^{d})
is the WSAW
connective constant of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. In addition, the
mean-square displacement
defined by the left-hand side of (
1.5
) satisfies

\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal{W}_{n}}|\omega(n)|^{2}K[0,n]=Dn(1+O(n^{-1/2}))

(1.5)

where
D=D(\beta)>1
is some constant called the
diffusion constant
of the WSAW. It is also known (see
[
16
, Theorem 6.1.8]
) that the scaling limit of the weakly self-avoiding walk is Brownian motion in dimension
d>4
and that the diffusivity of the limiting Brownian motion is equal to
D
.
It is widely acknowledged even though conjectural that the asymptotic behaviour of the WSAW and the SAW are the same in all dimensions and thus that both models lie in the same universality class.

There is a natural probability measure on
\mathcal{W}_{n}
denoted
\mathbb{P}_{\beta,n}
that comes with the WSAW model. If we let
\mathbb{E}_{\beta,n}
be its corresponding expectation, then it is defined by

\mathbb{E}_{\beta,n}\,f=\frac{1}{c_{n}}\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal{W}_{n}}f(\omega)K[0,n]

(1.6)

for any bounded function
f\colon\mathcal{W}_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
. We write
\omega\sim\beta
-WSAW
n
to mean that
\omega
has law
\mathbb{P}_{\beta,n}
.

We are mostly interested in walks on a discrete torus
\mathbb{T}_{r}^{d}=(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})^{d}
for an integer
r\geq 3
which will be taken to be large.
The
volume
of the torus is
V=r^{d}
. Torus walks are defined as on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
but with steps
\omega(i)-\omega(i-1)

computed using addition modulo
r
in each one of the
d
components. We let
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\mathbb{T}}
denote the set of
n
-steps torus walks starting at
0
. In the same way as on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we define torus quantities

\displaystyle c_{n}^{\mathbb{T}}=\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\mathbb{T}}}K[0,n],\qquad\mathbb{E}_{\beta,n,r}^{\mathbb{T}}\,g=\frac{1}{c_{n}^{\mathbb{T}}}\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\mathbb{T}}}g(\omega)K[0,n],

(1.7)

for any
g\colon\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\mathbb{T}}\to\mathbb{R}
. We write
\omega\sim\beta
-WSAW
{}^{\mathbb{T}}_{n,r}
to mean that
\omega
has law
\mathbb{P}^{\mathbb{T}}_{\beta,n,r}
.

1.4
Main result

We introduce some notations before rescaling the walk and stating our main result. Let
\mathbb{T}^{d}=(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^{d}
,
T>0
possibly infinite and
\mathcal{C}^{0}_{0}([0,T],\mathbb{T}^{d})
be the space of continuous functions from

to
\mathbb{T}^{d}

For a torus walk

where we see

We define the stopped process

So the diffusion constant of all the Brownian motions appearing in what follows is taken equal to

).
In the case

is simply

.
We defer the precise definition of convergence in law and continuity to Section

2.1
. Our main result is the following:

.

Let

,

1.5
Discussion

In fact we shall only prove the case
T=\infty
which is the most interesting and from which the proof can be easily adapted
to cover the easier case
T>0
and finite. We exclude the case
T=0
because then the rescaling in (
1.8
) is
degenerate in the sense that the torus is properly scaled whereas the rescaled walk shrinks to
0
as is noted in Remark

rmk:degener_lim

2.5
.

We refer to the regime of values of
n
for which the limiting behaviour (such as the scaling limit) of the torus WSAW is the same as on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as the subcritical regime or dilute
phase. It corresponds conjecturally to walks having length
o(V^{1/2})
. On the other hand it would be interesting to determine what the scaling limit of the walk is when its length is of order
V^{1/2}
(critical regime) or much larger
than
V^{1/2}
(supercritical regime or dense phase). In the critical case we expect the scaling limit to be Brownian motion with a different diffusion constant than on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. The above
conjectural distinction of regimes :
n\ll V^{1/2}
,
n\asymp V^{1/2}
and
n\gg V^{1/2}
has been first identified
for the simpler and solvable case of the SAW on the
complete graph in
[
23
]
(see also
[
17
,
24
]
for progress on the torus and the hypercube).
In order to establish a version of Theorem

thm:main

1.1
for the critical regime we would need at least to understand better how
c_{n}^{\mathbb{T}}
behaves when
n\asymp V^{1/2}
.
In this regime we do not expect
c_{n}^{\mathbb{T}}
to be equivalent to
c_{n}
when
n\to\infty
. This prevents the proof contained in this article to apply directly in this case and is a manifestation of the fact that new ideas are required to understand the critical regime as was already noted in
[
17
, Remark 5.3]
.

We end this subsection by discussing a closely related open problem. Recall that the torus and
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
WSAW two-point functions are given by

G^{*}_{z}(x)=\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal{W}^{*}(x)}z^{|\omega|}K[0,|\omega|]

(1.12)

where
``*"
is either replaced by
\mathbb{T}
for torus walks or nothing for
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
walks. The sum is over torus or
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
walks starting from
0
and ending at
x
. The torus and
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
susceptibilities are defined similarly by
\chi^{*}(x)=\sum_{x}G^{*}_{z}(x)
and the sum is either over
x
in
\mathbb{T}_{r}^{d}
or in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. The
critical window
corresponds to the
r
-dependent values of
z
for which
\chi^{\mathbb{T}}(z)\asymp V^{1/2}
. We let
z_{c}
denote the
critical point
for the
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-WSAW model i.e. the radius of convergence of
\chi(z)
. The proof of Theorem

thm:dilute

1.2
uses the key estimate of
[
22
, Theorem 1.2]
called the
plateau
of the torus two-point function critical window (see
[
23
,
17
,
22
,
8
,
6
]
for background on the plateau, the critical window and simulations in the case of SAW). The plateau phenomenon states that inside the critical window
G^{\mathbb{T}}_{z}(x)
decays like
G_{z_{c}}(x)\asymp|x|^{2-d}
(when
d>4
) for small values of

up until it reaches a constant value of order
V^{-1/2}
where it levels off for larger values of
x
.
Some limitations encountered in
[
17
]
come from the fact that the plateau is not known to hold inside the whole critical window.
It would thus be a natural first step to find a way to extend the plateau of
[
22
, Theorem 1.2]
to the entire critical window. This would lead to a better understanding of the critical regime for WSAW on the torus. We note that similar plateaux have been obtained for the simple random walk
[
22
,
26
,
25
]
when
d>2
, for percolation on a high-dimensional torus
[
13
]
and in part for Ising
[
18
,
26
,
25
]
when
d>4
.

2.1
Set-up

For any

, we recall that
\mathbb{T}_{r}^{d}
is the

(for

into
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\mathbb{T}^{d}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In the rest of the article we will mention convergence in law on several metric spaces that we now define. We let

•

\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=(\mathcal{C}^{0}_{0}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}^{d}),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})

•

\mathcal{C}^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{d})=(\mathcal{C}_{0}^{0}([0,\infty),\mathbb{T}^{d}),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})

Definition 2.1

(The

We define the

\displaystyle\hat{\ell}\colon\mathcal{W}_{n}^{\mathbb{T}}

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle\mapsto\hat{\ell}[\omega]

The proof consists in formalizing the obvious.
By construction we shall show that the lift of a torus process is well defined and is unique. We recall that

t_{n+1}=\inf\{t>t_{n},\;|(x(t)-x(t_{n}))_{1}|=1/8\}

The fact that the range of

follows from the fact that for any

which satisfies

\omega\in\mathcal{W}_{n}

|Y^{(n)}_{t}(\omega)-Y^{(n)}_{s}(\omega)|=(r^{2}t-r^{2}s)|Y^{(n)}_{t_{r}+r^{-2}}(\omega)-Y^{(n)}_{t_{r}}(\omega)|\leq r(t-s).

(2.45)

and since
t_{r}=t_{s}
implies
r\leq|t-s|^{-1/2}
the result follows with
A=1
by squaring the above equation and taking the expectation on both sides.

Now we suppose that
t_{r}\neq s_{r}
, then by (
2.27
)–(
2.29
) again, we can write

\displaystyle|Y^{(n)}_{t}(\omega)-Y^{(n)}_{s}(\omega)|

\displaystyle\leq|Y^{(n)}_{t_{r}}(\omega)-Y^{(n)}_{s_{r}}(\omega)|+|\varphi_{n,t,r}(\omega)|+|\varphi_{n,s,r}(\omega)|

\displaystyle\leq|Y^{(n)}_{t_{r}}(\omega)-Y^{(n)}_{s_{r}}(\omega)|+\frac{2}{r}.

(2.46)

Since
t_{r}\neq s_{r}
implies
r\geq|t-s|^{-1/2}
we further obtain using
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\beta,n}|Y^{(n)}_{t}(\omega)-Y^{(n)}_{s}(\omega)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}_{\beta,n}|Y^{(n)}_{t_{r}}(\omega)-Y^{(n)}_{s_{r}}(\omega)|^{2}+8|t-s|.

(2.47)

And thus to conclude with the proof of (
2.44
) we see that it is enough to show that

\mathbb{E}_{\beta,n}|Y^{(n)}_{t_{r}}(\omega)-Y^{(n)}_{s_{r}}(\omega)|^{2}\leq A|t-s|.

(2.48)

By definition the left-hand side of (
2.48
) is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{2}c_{n}}\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal{W}_{n}}|\omega(\lfloor r^{2}t\rfloor)-\omega(\lfloor r^{2}s\rfloor)|^{2}K[0,n].

(2.49)

Using the fact that
K[0,n]\leq K[0,\lfloor r^{2}s\rfloor]\,K[\lfloor r^{2}s\rfloor,\lfloor r^{2}t\rfloor]\,K[\lfloor r^{2}t\rfloor,n]
, decomposing
\omega
into three subwalks corresponding to these time intervals and using that (
A
may change from line to line)

c^{-1}_{n}\leq Ac^{-1}_{\lfloor r^{2}s\rfloor}c^{-1}_{\lfloor r^{2}t\rfloor-\lfloor r^{2}s\rfloor}c^{-1}_{n-\lfloor r^{2}t\rfloor}

(2.50)

from
[
16
, Theorem 6.1.1(a)]
we get that (
2.49
) satisfies

\frac{1}{r^{2}c_{n}}\sum_{\omega\in\mathcal{W}_{n}}|\omega(\lfloor r^{2}t\rfloor)-\omega(\lfloor r^{2}s\rfloor)|^{2}K[0,n]\leq\frac{A}{r^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\lfloor r^{2}t\rfloor-\lfloor r^{2}s\rfloor,\beta}|\omega(\lfloor r^{2}t\rfloor-\lfloor r^{2}s\rfloor)|^{2}.

(2.51)

which by the bound on the mean square displacement in (
1.5
) gets bounded further by
A|t-s|

and thus achieves the proof.

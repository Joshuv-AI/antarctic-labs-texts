A T-Duality of Non-Supersymmetric Heterotic Strings and an implication for Topological Modular Forms
Vivek Saxena

Abstract
Motivated by recent developments connecting non-supersymmetric heterotic string theory to the theory of Topological Modular Forms (TMF), we show that the worldsheet theory with central charge ( 17 , 3 2 ) (17,\frac{3}{2}) obtained by fibering the ( E 8 ) 1 × ( E 8 ) 1 (E_{8})_{1}\times(E_{8})_{1} current algebra over the 𝒩 = ( 0 , 1 ) \mathcal{N}=(0,1) sigma model on S 1 S^{1} with antiperiodic spin structure (such that the two E 8 E_{8} factors are exchanged as we go around the circle), is continuously connected to the ( E 8 ) 2 (E_{8})_{2} theory in the Gaiotto − - Johnson-Freyd − - Witten sense of going “up and down the RG trajectories”. Combined with the work of Tachikawa and Yamashita, this furnishes a physical derivation of the fact that the ( E 8 ) 2 (E_{8})_{2} theory corresponds to the unique nontrivial torsion element [ ( E 8 ) 2 ] [(E_{8})_{2}] of 𝖳𝖬𝖥 31 \mathsf{TMF}^{31} with zero mod-2 elliptic genus.

Conclusions and Future Directions

In this paper, we have established that the torsional class in
\mathsf{TMF}_{-31}
corresponding to the
(E_{8})_{1}\times(E_{8})_{1}
theory (which was discussed in
[
65
]
) admits another distinguished representative, namely the
(E_{8})_{2}
theory. Our conclusion relies on the fact that these two theories are continuously connected in the space of 2d
{\cal N}=(0,1)
SQFTs with anomaly coefficient
\nu=-31
(as shown in Section
5.2
), and that Topological Modular Forms are complete deformation invariants of 2d
{\cal N}=(0,1)
SQFTs under the assumption of the Segal-Stolz-Teichner conjecture.
33
33

33

The term ”complete deformation invariant” means the following: two 2d
{\cal N}=(0,1)
SQFTs (with the same anomaly coefficient
\nu
) are in the same deformation class or equivalently, are homotopic (and hence can be smoothly deformed into each other) if their images in
\mathsf{TMF}^{-\nu}
under the Segal-Stolz-Teichner isomorphism (
1
) coincide.
In particular, we have used physical arguments to give a homotopy between these theories
[
1
]
defined in terms of a relevant tachyon vertex deformation. This work identifies the TMF class of the
(E_{8})_{2}
theory with that of the
(E_{8})_{1}\times(E_{8})_{1}
theory. This is not a mere curiosity: at the time of writing this paper, an independent
mathematical
derivation of the
\mathsf{TMF}
class
[(E_{8})_{2}]
of the
(E_{8})_{2}
theory was not available, at least to the knowledge of the author.

Computations in TMF involve the intricate use of spectral sequences
[
8
,
7
]
, and physics-inspired reasoning based on dualities and RG flows can provide hints when conventional mathematical techniques prove to be intractable. To determine the TMF class of a given 2d
{\cal N}=(0,1)
SQFT, one can try to find another representative in its deformation class, one for which the TMF class is already known or is more easily computable. Indeed, this was a key motivation spelled out in the
Introduction
. The
philosophy of exploiting dualities and RG flows to connect field theories in the space of 2d
{\cal N}=(0,1)
SQFTs can naturally be applied to other classes of models. (See
(
30
, Sec. 5)
for some recent applications to ten-dimensional heterotic strings.) Another potential direction is the study of TMF classes of UV theories with multiple vacua splitting into distinct IR theories – along the lines of (
188
), but now with not all isomorphic summands (see footnote
30
). Finally, an investigation of TMF classes of internal CFTs that arise in certain heterotic string compactifications to lower dimensions is work in progress.

Appendix C
Factorizing internal SCFT partition functions

For a 2d CFT with central charge
(c_{L},c_{R})
, the Hilbert space decomposes in terms of Verma modules as

\displaystyle{\cal H}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\Delta,\overline{\Delta}}c_{\Delta,\overline{\Delta}}{\cal V}(c_{L},\Delta)\otimes{\cal V}(c_{R},\Delta)~,

(254)

where
\{c_{\Delta,\overline{\Delta}}\}
is a theory-dependent set of coefficients. The partition function has the schematic form

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Z}}(\tau,\overline{\tau})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\Delta,\overline{\Delta}}c_{\Delta,\overline{\Delta}}\chi_{\Delta}(q)\chi_{\overline{\Delta}}(\overline{q})~.

(255)

For unitary CFTs
c_{L}\geq 1
and
c_{R}\geq 1
(see
(
31
, Ch. 15)
and
(
79
, Ch. 7)
), and the only module inside
{\cal V}(c_{L},\Delta)\otimes{\cal V}(c_{R},\overline{\Delta})
that has a null state is the vacuum module with
\Delta=0
and
\overline{\Delta}=0
. So, all Verma modules other than the vacuum module are irreducible (that is, devoid of null submodules containing singular vectors or null states).
To summarize,

\displaystyle\chi_{\Delta}(q)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{q^{\Delta+(1-c_{L})/24}}{\eta(\tau)}&\text{ for a non-vacuum module ($\Delta\neq 0$)}~,\\
\frac{(1-q)q^{(1-c_{L})/24}}{\eta(\tau)}&\text{ for a vacuum module ($\Delta=0$)}~,\end{array}\right.

with a similar expression for
\chi_{\overline{\Delta}}(\overline{q})
with
c_{L}\to c_{R}
,
q\to\overline{q}
,
\tau\to\overline{\tau}
, and
\Delta\to\overline{\Delta}
. Thus (
255
) has the form

\displaystyle\hskip-15.89948pt{\mathsf{Z}}(\tau,\overline{\tau})

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{\frac{(1-c_{L})}{24}}\overline{q}^{\frac{(1-c_{R})}{24}}}{|\eta(\tau)|^{2}}\bigg(c_{0,0}(1-q)(1-\overline{q})+(1-q)\sum_{\overline{\Delta}\neq 0}c_{0,\overline{\Delta}}\overline{q}^{\overline{\Delta}}+(1-\overline{q})\sum_{\Delta\neq 0}c_{\Delta,0}q^{\Delta}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Delta\neq 0\\
\overline{\Delta}\neq 0\end{subarray}}c_{\Delta,\overline{\Delta}}q^{\Delta}\overline{q}^{\overline{\Delta}}\bigg)~.

(258)

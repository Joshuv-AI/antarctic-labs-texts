Introduction

This paper concerns problems about (finite) digraphs, for which we recall a few basic definitions. Given vertices
u,v
of a digraph
D
, we define
\mathrm{dist}(u,v)
to be the length of a shortest directed path from
u
to
v
, and for a set of vertices
S
, we define
\mathrm{dist}(S,v)=\min_{u\in S}\mathrm{dist}(u,v)
. We say a set of vertices
S
is
independent
if there exist no arcs between two vertices of
S
.

The following (less basic) objects will be the starting point for this paper.

Definition 1
.

A set of vertices
K
of a digraph
D
is said to be a
kernel
if (1)
K
is an independent set and (2)

\mathrm{dist}(K,x)\leq 1
for all
x\in V(D)
(that is, every vertex
x\in V(D)
is either contained in
K
or has a vertex of
K
as an in-neighbor).

Definition 2
.

A set of vertices
Q
of a digraph
D
is said to be a
quasikernel
if (1)
Q
is an independent set and (2)
\mathrm{dist}(Q,x)\leq 2
for all
x\in V(D)
(that is, every vertex
x\in V(D)
can be reached from
Q
by a directed path of length at most 2).

The concept of kernels was introduced by Von Neumann and Morgenstern
[
13
]
in relation to problems from game theory. Not every digraph contains a kernel (for example, a directed 3-cycle does not), but it turns out that every digraph has a quasikernel, as was originally shown by Chvátal and Lovász
[
3
]
.

Because every digraph contains a quasikernel, it is natural to ask extremal type problems about the set of quasikernels for a given digraph. One conjecture in this direction is the Small Quasikernel Conjecture due to P.L. Erdős and Székely
[
4
]
, where here we recall a digraph is
source-free
if it contains no vertices with in-degree 0.

Conjecture 1.1

(Small Quasikernel Conjecture)
.

If
D
is a source-free digraph, then
D
contains a quasikernel
Q
with
|Q|\leq\frac{1}{2}|V(D)|
.

The source-free condition is necessary for the conclusion
|Q|\leq\frac{1}{2}|V(D)|
to hold, as can be seen by considering, for example,
D
to be a digraph with no arcs. The bound
\frac{1}{2}|V(D)|
is best possible, as can be seen by considering (disjoint unions of) directed 2-cycles and 4-cycles.

The Small Quasikernel Conjecture seems difficult, with very little progress being made between its original statement in 1976 and 2020. However, since 2020, there have been a number of results solving the Small Quasikernel in a few special cases
[
1
,
9
,
10
,
12
]
, with this recent increase in popularity largely due to work of Kostochka, Luo, and Shan
[
7
]
who (in particular) showed that 4-chromatic digraphs satisfy the Small Quasikernel Conjecture.

We provide our own small step towards the Small Quasikernel Conjecture by proving what appears to be the first general bound for the size of the smallest quasikernel in source-free digraphs. Here and throughout our logarithms are written base 2.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
D
is a source-free digraph, then
D
contains a quasikernel with

|Q|\leq|V(D)|-\max\{\lfloor\sqrt{|V(D)|}\rfloor,\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{|V(D)|\log|V(D)|}\}.

Note that the bound of
|V(D)|-\lfloor\sqrt{|V(D)|}\rfloor
from
Theorem

1.2
is almost always weaker than the other bound
|V(D)|-\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{|V(D)|\log|V(D)|}
. We include this generally weaker bound both because it has a simple proof and because it is tight when
D
is a directed 2-cyles or 4-cycle.

Much more can be said about the Small Quasikernel Conjecture, and we refer the interested reader to the nice survey by P.L. Erdős, Győri, Mezei, Salia, and Tyomkyn
[
5
]
for a more thorough overview of this problem.

Given an integer

2.1
Disjoint
q
-kernels

Here we study the problem of finding
r
disjoint
q
-kernels in a digraph
D
. One immediate obstacle is the following structure, where here and throughout
we define the open in-neighborhood of a set
S
by

N^{-}_{D}(S):=\{u\in V(D)\setminus S:\exists v\in S,\ uv\in E(D)\}.

Definition 4
.

Given a digraph
D
, we say that a set of vertices
S\subseteq V(D)
is a
source set
if
N^{-}(S)=\emptyset
. We say that
S
is an

-source set
if

is non-empty and
|S|\leq s
.

For example, 1-source sets are just sources.

Observe that if
D
contains an
(r-1)
-source set

, then it does not contain
r
disjoint
q
-kernels for any

. Indeed, every

-kernel needs to use at least one vertex of

, and hence

can contain at most
|S|<r

-kernels.

It is not difficult to show that
(r-1)
-source sets are the only obstruction for

containing
r
disjoint
q
-kernels for some
q\leq|V(D)|
. With more work, one can significantly improve upon this trivial bound
q\leq|V(D)|
.

Theorem 2.2
.

If a digraph has no
(r-1)
-source sets, then it contains
r
disjoint
2^{r+1}
-kernels.

In other words, if a digraph contains
r
disjoint

-kernels for some

, then it in fact contains
r
disjoint
2^{r+1}
-kernels. We will ultimately prove a somewhat stronger result which guarantees disjoint sets

such that each

is a
q_{i}
-kernel with

; see

for more.

2.2
Smaller
q
-kernels

The examples showing sharpness of
Conjecture

1.1
and
Proposition

2.1
both involve disjoint unions of directed 2-cycles or 4-cycles. As such, it is reasonable to ask if these bounds can be improved if
D
avoids certain cycle lengths. A reasonable guess here would be the following.

Question 2.3
.

Let
q\geq 2
be an integer and let
D
be a source-free digraph with
L(D)
the set of
\ell
such that
D
contains a directed
\ell
-cycle. Is it true that
D
contains a
q
-kernel
Q
such that

|Q|\leq\max_{\ell\in L(D)}\frac{\lceil\ell/(q+1)\rceil}{\ell}\cdot|V(D)|?

Observe that this upper bound is tight if
D
is the disjoint union of directed
\ell
-cycles. Also observe that
D
being source-free implies
L(D)\neq\emptyset
, so this maximum is well defined.

We suspect
Question

2.3
is too ambitious to be true in general. We are, however, able to verify a few cases of
Question

2.3
when our digraph is bipartite, which is analogous to the work of
[
7
]
solving the Small Quasikernel Conjecture for digraphs of chromatic number at most 4. To this end, we say a partition
U,V
of
V(D)
is a
bipartition
of
D
if every arc has exactly one vertex in
U
and exactly one vertex in
V
, and we say that
D
is
bipartite
if it has a bipartition.

Note that for bipartite source-free digraphs, we trivially have quasikernels with
|Q|\leq\frac{1}{2}|V(D)|
by simply considering
Q
to be the smaller part of the bipartition, and this is tight whenever
D
is the disjoint union of directed 2-cycles and 4-cycles. We show that this is the unique extremal construction for bipartite digraphs.

Proposition 2.4
.

If
D
is a source-free bipartite digraph which is not the disjoint union of directed 2-cycles and 4-cycles, then
D
contains a quasikernel
Q
with
|Q|<\frac{1}{2}|V(D)|
.

We do not know how to improve the bound of
Proposition

2.4
if we impose the stronger condition that
D
contain no directed 2-cycles or 4-cycles, and in particular we are quite far from the optimal possible bound of
|Q|\leq\frac{2}{5}|V(D)|
predicted by

2.3
(due to
\ell=10
). However, if one shifts from quasikernels to the setting of
q
-kernels with

, then one can prove sharp results when the smallest cycle length is small relative to
q
. Moreover, we can do this in the more general setting of digraphs which have kernels (which in particular includes source-free bipartite digraphs by considering either part of the partition).

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
D
be a source-free digraph which has a kernel
K
. If
q,\ell\geq 3
are integers such that
\ell\leq(q+3)/2
and such that every directed cycle of
D
of even length has length at least
\ell

contains a
q
-kernel

of size at most
\frac{1}{\ell}|V(D)|
with

.

For example, taking

implies that every source-free digraph with a kernel and no directed 2-cycles contains a 3-kernel of size at most
\frac{1}{3}|V(D)|
, which is best possible by considering directed 6-cycles. More generally, the bound for

is best possible and matches what

2.3
predicts if

is the disjoint union of directed

is the disjoint union of directed

.

We suspect that one can extend our approach for

to verify

2.3
in full for bipartite digraphs if we continue imposing the additional condition

in

2.3
Large
q
-kernels

This subsection studies the problem of finding “large”
q
-kernels in digraphs. The most straightforward problem of trying to maximize
|Q|
amongst
q
-kernels
Q
is not interesting, as
|Q|
could be as small as 1 if
D
is a tournament. However, this problem becomes interesting if we change how we measure
Q
. To this end, we denote the closed out-neighborhood of a set
S\subseteq V(D)
by

N^{+}[S]=S\sqcup N^{+}(S)=S\cup\{v:\exists u\in S,\ uv\in E(D)\}.

Conjecture 2.6

(Large Quasikernel Conjecture)
.

Every digraph
D
contains a quasikernel
Q
with
|N^{+}[Q]|\geq\frac{1}{2}|V(D)|
.

This bound is asymptotically best possible by considering
Q
to be an Eulerian touranment. The main motivation for this conjecture is the following.

Proposition 2.7
.

The Small Quasikernel Conjecture implies the Large Quasikernel Conjecture.

In fact, we will prove a slightly stronger result
Proposition

4.1
which shows that proving any non-trivial linear upper bound on the size of a smallest quasikernel implies a non-trivial linear lower bound on
|N^{+}[Q]|
. With this, we see that being able to prove a linear lower bound for
|N^{+}[Q]|
is a barrier towards improving our
Theorem

1.2
into a linear upper bound.

We are able to take a modest step towards the Large Quasikernel Conjecture, with us making significantly more progress for 3-kernels.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
D
be a digraph.

(a)

There exists a quasikernel
Q
with
|N^{+}[Q]|\geq|V(D)|^{1/3}
.

(b)

There exists a 3-kernel
Q
with
|N^{+}[Q]|\geq\frac{1}{3}|V(D)|
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
: Small Quasikernels

We begin with a simple lemma that will be used in the next section as well.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every digraph
D
and
x\in V(D)
, there exists a quasikernel
Q
of
D
such that
Q\cap N^{+}(x)=\emptyset
and
Q\cap N^{-}[x]\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
D^{\prime}=D-N^{+}[x]
and let
Q^{\prime}
be any quasikernel of
D^{\prime}
, noting that
Q^{\prime}\cap N^{+}(x)=\emptyset
by construction. If
Q^{\prime}\cap N^{-}(x)\neq\emptyset
, then it is straightforward to show that
Q^{\prime}
is a quasikernel for
D
which intersects
N^{-}[x]
. If instead
Q^{\prime}\cap N^{-}(x)=\emptyset
, then
Q=Q^{\prime}\cup\{x\}
is an independent set, and it is not difficult to see that
Q
is a quasikernel which satisfies
Q\cap N^{+}(x)=\emptyset
and
Q\cap N^{-}[x]\neq\emptyset
, giving the result.
∎

We also need the following observation.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
D
is a digraph with minimum in-degree
\delta
and maximum out-degree
\Delta
, then every independent set
I
of
D
satisfies

|I|\leq|V(D)|-\frac{\delta}{\delta+\Delta}|V(D)|.

Proof.

Let
I
be an independent set and let
D^{\prime}\subseteq D
be the subdigraph consisting only of the arcs that go from
V(D)\setminus I
to
I
. Because
I
is independent, each
v\in I
has
\deg_{D^{\prime}}^{-}(v)=\deg_{D}^{-}(v)\geq\delta
. From this we conclude

\delta|I|\leq\sum_{v\in I}\deg_{D^{\prime}}^{-}(v)=\sum_{u\in V(D)\setminus I}\deg_{D^{\prime}}^{+}(u)\leq\Delta(|V(D)|-|I|).

Rearranging gives the result.
∎

These two results give our weaker bound on the size of small quasikernels.

Proposition 3.3
.

If
D
is a source-free digraph, then
D
contains a quasikernel
Q
with

|Q|\leq|V(D)|-\lfloor\sqrt{|V(D)|}\rfloor.

Proof.

First consider the case that
D
contains a vertex
x
with out-degree at least
\lfloor\sqrt{|V(D)|}\rfloor
. By
Lemma

3.1
, there exists a quasikernel
Q
disjoint from
N^{+}(x)
, so

|Q|\leq|V(D)|-|N^{+}(x)|\leq|V(D)|-\lfloor\sqrt{|V(D)|}\rfloor,

proving the bound.

We now assume
D
has maximum out-degree at most
\lfloor\sqrt{|V(D)|}\rfloor-1
, noting that the source-free condition is equivalent to saying that
D
has minimum in-degree at least 1. By
Lemma

3.2
, any independent set (and in particular, any quasikerenl) has size at most

|V(D)|-\frac{1}{\lfloor\sqrt{|V(D)|}\rfloor}|V(D)|\leq|V(D)|-\sqrt{|V(D)|},

proving the result.
∎

As an aside, this same approach can be used to show that digraphs with minimum in-degree
\delta
contain quasikernels of size at most
|V(D)|-\sqrt{\delta|V(D)|}+\delta
; see also the discuss around
Question

7.4
for problems related to this.

To improve
Proposition

3.3
, we extend the argument of
Lemma

3.1
by deleting a set
A
of vertices rather than just a single vertex. To this end, we say a set
A\subseteq V(D)
is
kernel-perfect
if for every
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
, the digraph
D[A^{\prime}]
contains a kernel.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
D
is a digraph and
A\subseteq V(D)
is kernel-perfect, then
D
contains a quasikernel which is disjoint from
N^{+}(A)
.

We note that this lemma follows from a more general recent result
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
, but for completeness we include the easy proof.

Proof.

Let
D^{\prime}=D-N^{+}[A]
and let
Q^{\prime}
be any quasikernel of
D^{\prime}
, noting that
Q^{\prime}\cap N^{+}(A)=\emptyset
by construction. Let
A^{\prime}=A\setminus N^{+}(Q^{\prime})
, and by hypothesis there exists some
K\subseteq A^{\prime}
which is a kernel of
D[A^{\prime}]
. Define
Q=Q^{\prime}\cup K
, noting that this is an independent set since
Q^{\prime}
is an independent set disjoint from
N^{+}[A]\supseteq N^{+}[K]
and
K\subseteq A^{\prime}
is an independent set disjoint from
N^{+}[Q^{\prime}]
. It is straightforward to show that every vertex can be reached from
Q
by a path of length at most 2, proving the result.
∎

Our goal will be to use
Lemma

3.4
to show that if
D
is a source-free digraph, then either every independent set has size at most
|V(D)|-m
or there exists a kernel-perfect set
A
such that
|N^{+}(A)|\geq m
for some
m\approx\sqrt{|V(D)|\log|V(D)|}
. This bound on
m
is the best one can obtain with this approach. Indeed, if one takes
T
to be a random tournament on
\sqrt{n\log n}
vertices and then forms a digraph
D
from
T
by adding
\sqrt{n/\log n}
leaves to each vertex of
T
, then
|V(D)|\approx n
, its largest independent set
D-T
is around size
|V(D)|-\sqrt{|V(D)|\log|V(D)|}
, and the best kernel-perfect sets
A
one can use are transitive subtournaments of
T
which all have size about
\log|V(D)|
.

Partially motivated by this extremal example for our approach, we observe the following.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
D
is a non-empty digraph, then there exists a set of vertices
A\subseteq V(D)
which is kernel-perfect for
D
with
|A|\geq\log|V(D)|
.

Proof.

First observe that if
D
contains a directed two-cycle
uv,vu
and if
A
is kernel-perfect for the digraph
D-uv
, then
A
is still kernel-perfect for
D
(since any kernel of
D^{\prime}[A^{\prime}]
is still independent and a kernel in
D[A^{\prime}]
). As such, it suffices to find a large kernel-perfect subset of the digraph
D^{\prime}\subseteq D
obtained by arbitrarily deleting one arc from each directed 2-cycle of
D
.

Let
D^{\prime\prime}\supseteq D^{\prime}
be an arbitrary tournament on the same vertex set of
D^{\prime}
obtained by arbitrarily adding arcs between vertices
u,v
which have no arcs between them (noting that this
D^{\prime\prime}
will be a tournament since
D
contains no directed 2-cycles). A well known result (see
[
11
, Exercise 10.44]
) implies there exists a set of vertices
A\subseteq V(D^{\prime\prime})
of size at least
1+\lfloor\log|V(D^{\prime\prime})|\rfloor\geq\log|V(D)|
such that
D^{\prime\prime}[A]
is a transitive tournament. This implies that for any
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
, the digraph
D^{\prime}[A^{\prime}]
contains no directed cycles, and it is known that such digraphs always contain (unique) kernels (see
[
11
, Exercise 8.5]
). We conclude that
A
is a kernel-perfect subset of
D^{\prime}
of the desired size, proving the result.
∎

We now have all we need to prove our main result about small quasikernels.

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

Recall that we wish to prove that every source-free digraph
D
has a quaikernel
Q
with

|Q|\leq|V(D)|-\max\{\lfloor\sqrt{|V(D)|}\rfloor,\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{|V(D)|\log|V(D)|}\}.

In view of
Proposition

3.3
, we only need to prove the bound
|Q|\leq|V(D)|-\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{|V(D)|\log|V(D)|}
, and for ease of notation we let
n=|V(D)|
and
m=\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{n\log n}
. If every independent set of
D
has size at most
n-m
then we are done, so we can assume there exists an independent set
I
of size at least
n-m
. Our aim now is to show the following.

Claim 3.6
.

There exists a kernel-perfect subset
A\subseteq V(D)
such that
|N^{+}(A)|\geq m
.

Proof.

The result is trivial if there exists a vertex
u
with
\deg^{+}(u)\geq m
, so we may assume this is not the case.

We will prove the claim by finding such an
A
which is disjoint from
I
with the additional property that
|N^{+}(A)\cap I|\geq m
. To this end, define the leftover vertices
L=V(D)\setminus I
. Because
D
is source-free and
I
is an independent set, there exists some function
f:I\to L
such that
f(v)\in N^{-}(v)
for every
v\in I
(e.g. by defining
f(v)
to be the smallest element of
N^{-}(v)
under some arbitrary ordering of
V(D)
). For each
u\in L
, define its weight
\phi(u)=|f^{-1}(u)|
, and for any given set
U\subseteq L
define
\phi(U)=\sum_{u\in U}\phi(u)
. Observe from the definitions that for any
A\subseteq L
we have

|N^{+}(A)\cap I|\geq\phi(A),

so it will suffice to find a kernel-perfect
A
with
\phi(A)\geq m
. Towards this end, we observe from the definitions that

\phi(L)=|I|\geq n-m

|L|=n-|I|\leq m.

Let
L^{\prime}\subseteq L
consist of the vertices
u
with
\phi(u)\leq\frac{1}{2}nm^{-1}
and observe that

\phi(L\setminus L^{\prime})=\phi(L)-\phi(L^{\prime})\geq n-m-|L|\cdot\frac{1}{2}nm^{-1}\geq\frac{1}{2}n-m.

For each
i\geq 0
define the set

L_{i}=\{u\in L:2^{i-1}nm^{-1}<\phi(u)\leq 2^{i}nm^{-1}\},

noting that these
L_{i}
sets partition the vertices of
L\setminus L^{\prime}
.

We claim that
L_{i}=\emptyset
for any
i
with
2^{i-1}\geq n^{-1}m^{2}
. Indeed, any
u
in such an
L_{i}
would have

|N^{+}(u)|\geq\phi(u)>2^{i-1}nm^{-1}\geq m,

a contradiction to our assumption at the start of the claim. We also claim that some
i\geq 0
has
\phi(L_{i})\geq 2^{-1-i}(\frac{1}{2}n-m)
, as otherwise we would have

\phi(L\setminus L^{\prime})=\sum_{i\geq 0}\phi(L_{i})<\left(\frac{1}{2}n-m\right)\sum_{i\geq 0}2^{-1-i}=\frac{1}{2}n-m,

contradicting our bound above.

In total, these subclaims imply the existence of some
i
such that
\phi(L_{i})\geq 2^{-1-i}(\frac{1}{2}n-m)
and such that
2^{i}<2n^{-1}m^{2}
. By definition of
L_{i}
this implies

2^{-1-i}\left(\frac{1}{2}n-m\right)\leq\phi(L_{i})\leq|L_{i}|\cdot 2^{i-1}nm^{-1},

and rearranging gives

|L_{i}|\geq 2^{-2i}\left(\frac{1}{2}m-n^{-1}m^{2}\right)>\frac{1}{8}n^{2}m^{-3}-\frac{1}{4}nm^{-2}\geq\frac{1}{16}n^{2}m^{-3}\geq n^{1/4},

where the second to last inequality used
n^{2}m^{-3}\geq 4nm^{-2}
(equivalently that
n\geq 4m
, i.e. that
n\geq\log n
), and the last inequality used
\frac{1}{16}n^{2}m^{-3}=4n^{1/2}(\log n)^{-3/2}
and
4n^{1/4}\geq(\log n)^{-3/2}
for
n\geq 2
(which must hold if
D
is source-free). By
Lemma

3.5
, there exists some
A\subseteq L_{i}
such that
A
is a kernel-perfect subset of
D[L_{i}]
(and hence of
D
) and such that
|A|\geq\log(|L_{i}|)\geq\frac{1}{4}\log(n)
. By definition of
A\subseteq L_{i}\subseteq L\setminus L^{\prime}
this implies

|N^{+}(A)|\geq\phi(A)\geq|A|\cdot\frac{1}{2}nm^{-1}\geq\frac{1}{8}n\log(n)m^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{n\log n}\geq m,

proving the claim.
∎

The result follows from this claim together with
Lemma

3.4
.
∎

4
Proof of
Theorem

2.8
: Large
q
-kernels

We begin by proving a slight strengthening of
Proposition

2.7
, namely that weak variants of the Small Quasikernel imply weak variants of the Large Quasikernel Conjecture.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
is a real number such that every source-free digraph
D^{\prime}
contains a quasikernel
Q^{\prime}
with
|Q^{\prime}|\leq(1-\varepsilon)|V(D^{\prime})|
, then every digraph
D
contains a quasikernel
Q
with
|N^{+}[Q]|\geq\varepsilon|V(D)|
.

Note that the case
\varepsilon=1/2
is exactly the statement of
Proposition

2.7
.

Proof of
Proposition

2.7
.

Let
D
be a minimal counterexample to the statement for some given value of
\varepsilon
. If
D
contains a source
v
, then
D
being a minimal counterexample implies we can find a quasikernel
Q^{\prime}
in
D^{\prime}=D-N^{+}_{D}[v]
with

|N^{+}_{D^{\prime}}[Q^{\prime}]|\geq\varepsilon(|V(D)|-|N^{+}_{D}[v]|).

One can then verify that
Q:=Q^{\prime}\cup\{v\}
is a quasikernel of
D
and that

|N^{+}_{D}[Q]|=|N^{+}_{D^{\prime}}[Q^{\prime}]|+|N^{+}_{D}[v]|\geq\varepsilon|V(D)|+(1-\varepsilon)|N^{+}_{D}[v]|\geq\varepsilon|V(D)|,

giving the result. Thus we may assume
D
is source-free.

Let
k
be a large integer to be determined later and define a digraph
D^{\prime}
by taking each vertex
v\in V(D)
and adding
k
new vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
together with the arcs
vv_{i}
for all
i
. Let
Q^{\prime}
be a smallest quasikernel of
D^{\prime}
, noting that
|Q^{\prime}|\leq(1-\varepsilon)|V(D^{\prime})|=(1-\varepsilon)(k+1)|V(D)|
by hypothesis.

It is not difficult to verify that
Q:=Q^{\prime}\cap V(D)
is a quasikernel of
D
and that
Q^{\prime}\setminus Q=Q^{\prime}\setminus V(D)
contains every vertex
v_{i}
with
v\in V(D)\setminus N^{+}_{D}[Q]
(as otherwise
Q^{\prime}
would not be a quasikernel for
D^{\prime}
). These observations imply

(1-\varepsilon)(k+1)|V(D)|\geq|Q^{\prime}|\geq|Q^{\prime}\setminus Q|\geq k(|V(D)|-|N^{+}_{D}[Q]|),

and rearranging gives

|N^{+}[Q]|\geq\varepsilon|V(D)|-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{k}|V(D)|.

Since
|N^{+}[Q]|
is an integer, this implies
|N^{+}[Q]|\geq\lceil\varepsilon|V(D)|-\frac{1-\varepsilon}{k}|V(D)|\rceil
for all
k\geq 1
. By taking
k
sufficiently large, we see that this ceiling is at least
\varepsilon|V(D)|
, proving the result.
∎

We now prove that digraphs have
q
-kernels with
|N^{+}[Q]|
relatively large. The following observations will be useful, where here we say an independent set
S
is
maximal
if
S\cup\{v\}
is not independent for all
v\notin S
and that it is
acyclic
if it contains no directed cycles.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
D
be a digraph.

(a)

If every vertex of
D
has out-degree at most
\Delta
, then there exists a set
A\subseteq V(D)
such that
D[A]
is acyclic with
|A|\geq\frac{|V(D)|}{\Delta+1}
.

(b)

There exists a maximal independent set
S
with
|N^{+}[S]|\geq\frac{1}{2}|V(D)|
.

As an aside,
Lemma

4.2
(b) can be viewed as a very weak version of the Large Quasikernel Conjecture (as the Large Quasikernel Conjecture requires finding such a set which is additionally a quasikernel).

Proof.

For (a), let
A
be a largest set such that
D[A]
is acyclic, noting that at least one such set exists by considering
A=\emptyset
. If
|A|<\frac{|V(D)|}{\Delta+1}
, then there exists some vertex
v\notin N^{+}[A]
(since
N^{+}[A]
has size at most
|A|(\Delta+1)<|V(D)|
). Observe that
D[A\cup\{v\}]
continues to be acyclic (since
v
can not be a vertex in any directed cycle with
A
by construction), contradicting the maximality of
|A|
and giving the result.

For (b), we introduce the notation
N[S]=N^{+}[S]\cup N^{-}(S)
, i.e. this is the set of vertices which are contained in some arc containing a vertex of
S
. Let
S
be a largest independent set satisfying

|N^{+}[S]|\geq\frac{1}{2}|N[S]|,

(1)

noting that at least one such independent set exists by considering
S=\emptyset
.

Let
T=V(D)\setminus N[S]
. If
T=\emptyset
(i.e. if
N[S]=V(D)
) then
S
is a maximal independent set with
|N^{+}[S]|\geq\frac{1}{2}|V(D)|
, so we may assume for contradiction that this is not the case. Let
D^{\prime}=D[T]
. Because
D^{\prime}
is a non-empty digraph, there exists some vertex
v\in T
with

|N^{+}_{D^{\prime}}[v]|\geq\frac{1}{2}|N_{D^{\prime}}[v]|,

(2)

namely this holds by taking any
v\in T
with at least as many out-neighbors as in-neighbors. In this case,
S^{\prime}=S\cup\{v\}
is a larger independent set satisfying

N_{D}[S^{\prime}]=N_{D}[S]\cup(N_{D}[v]\setminus N_{D}[S])=N_{D}[S]\cup N_{D^{\prime}}[v],

and

N_{D}^{+}[S^{\prime}]=N_{D}^{+}[S]\cup(N^{+}_{D}[v]\setminus N_{D}^{+}[S])\supseteq N_{D}^{+}[S]\cup(N_{D}^{+}[v]\setminus N_{D}[S])=N_{D}^{+}[S]\cup N_{D^{\prime}}^{+}[v].

Combining these with (
1
) and (
2
) gives

|N^{+}_{D}[S^{\prime}]|\geq|N^{+}_{D}[S]|+|N^{+}_{D^{\prime}}[v]|\geq\frac{1}{2}|N_{D}[S]|+\frac{1}{2}|N_{D^{\prime}}[v]|=\frac{1}{2}|N_{D}[S^{\prime}]|,

a contradiction to the maximality of
|S|
. We conclude that a maximal independent set with the desired properties must exist.
∎

We next show that if
D
contains a small quasikernel, then
D
contains a large 3-kernel.

Proposition 4.3
.

If
D
is a digraph and
Q\subseteq V(D)
is a quasikernel with
|N^{+}[Q]|=\varepsilon|V(D)|
for some
0<\varepsilon<1
, then there exists a 3-kernel
Q^{\prime}\subseteq V(D)
with
|N^{+}[Q^{\prime}]|\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}|V(D)|
.

Proof.

Let
T=V(D)\setminus N^{+}(Q)
, noting that
|T|\geq(1-\varepsilon)|V(D)|
and that
Q\subseteq T
. By
Lemma

4.2
(b), the digraph
D[T]
contains a maximal independent set
Q^{\prime}
with

|N^{+}_{D}[Q^{\prime}]|\geq|N^{+}_{D[T]}[Q^{\prime}]|\geq\frac{1}{2}|T|\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}|V(D)|.

It thus remains to show that
Q^{\prime}
is a 3-kernel. Note that
Q^{\prime}
is an independent set by construction. For any
w\in V(D)
, we know there exists some
v\in Q
such that
\mathrm{dist}(v,w)\leq 2
. Observe that
v\in Q
has
N^{+}(v)\subseteq N^{+}(Q)
since
Q
is an independent set, and hence
v
has no out-neighbors in
T=V(D)\setminus N^{+}(Q)
. As such, since
Q^{\prime}
is a maximal independent set of
D[T]
and
v\in Q\subseteq T
, either
v\in Q^{\prime}
or some in-neighbor of
v
lies in
Q^{\prime}
. In either case we find
\mathrm{dist}(Q^{\prime},w)\leq 1+\mathrm{dist}(v,w)\leq 3
. This shows
Q^{\prime}
is a 3-kernel, proving the result.
∎

Proposition

4.3
is essentially a refinement of
Theorem

2.8
(b), and in particular implies this result.

Proof of
Theorem

2.8
(b).

Recall that we aim to show every digraph
D
contains a 3-kernel with
|N^{+}[Q]|\geq\frac{1}{3}|V(D)|
. Let
Q\subseteq V(D)
be any quasikernel. If
|N^{+}[Q]|\geq\frac{1}{3}|V(D)|
then we are done, otherwise there exists a 3-kernel of the desired size by
Proposition

4.3
.
∎

It remains to show that digraphs always contain large quasikernels. For this we use the following.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
D
is a digraph and
A\subseteq V(D)
is such that
D[A]
is acyclic, then there exists a quasikernel
Q\subseteq V(D)
such that
A\subseteq N^{+}[Q]
.

Proof.

Let
S\supseteq A
be a maximal subset such that
D[S]
is acyclic. Observe that every
v\notin S
has an in-neighbor in
S
, as otherwise
S\cup\{v\}
would be a larger subset inducing an acyclic digraph (since
v
can not be a part of any directed cycle in
S\cup\{v\}
).

It is known that every acyclic digraph contains a (unique) kernel (see
[
11
, Exercise 8.5]
), and we let
Q
denote the kernel of
D[S]
. Observe that
A\subseteq S\subseteq N^{+}[Q]
by definition of
Q
being a kernel of
D[S]
. We also have
\mathrm{dist}(Q,v)\leq 2
for all
v\notin S
, since by the observation above,
v
has an in-neighbor in
S
which is at distance at most 1 from
Q
. Thus
Q
is a quasikernel with the desired properties, giving the result.
∎

We now have all we need to prove our main result for this section.

Proof of
Theorem

2.8
(a).

Recall that we aim to show every digraph contains a qusaikernel with
|N^{+}[Q]|\geq|V(D)|^{1/3}
. Let
X\subseteq V(D)
be the set of vertices
x
which are not contained in any quasikernel of
D
.

First consider the case
|X|\geq|V(D)|-|V(D)|^{2/3}
. By the second half of
Lemma

3.1
, we in particular see that for each
x\in X
, there exists some
y\in V(D)\setminus X
such that
yx\in E(D)
(namely, by considering the quasikernel
Q
which intersects
N^{+}[x]
after noting that
Q
is disjoint from
X
by definition of
X
). This implies that there are at least
|X|
arcs from
V(D)\setminus X
to
X
, and hence there must exist some
y\in V(D)\setminus X
with

\deg^{+}(y)\geq\frac{|X|}{|V(D)|-|X|}\geq|V(D)|^{1/3}-1.

Because
y\notin X
, there exists some quasikernel
Q
such that
y\in Q
, and hence
|N^{+}[Q]|\geq\deg^{+}(y)+1\geq|V(D)|^{1/3}
, proving the result.

Next consider
|X|\leq|V(D)|-|V(D)|^{2/3}
and let
D^{\prime}=D-X
, noting that
|V(D^{\prime})|\geq|V(D)|^{2/3}
. By the same argument as above, if there exists
y\in V(D^{\prime})
with
\deg^{+}_{D^{\prime}}(y)\geq|V(D)|^{1/3}-1
, then any quasikernel of
D
containing
y
gives the desired result, so we may assume
\deg^{+}_{D^{\prime}}(y)\leq|V(D)|^{1/3}-1:=\Delta
for all
y\in V(D^{\prime})
. By
Lemma

4.2
(a), there exists a subset
A\subseteq V(D^{\prime})
such that
D^{\prime}[A]=D[A]
is acyclic with
|A|\geq\frac{|V(D^{\prime})|}{\Delta+1}\geq|V(D)|^{1/3}
.
Lemma

4.4
then implies the existence of quasikernel with
|N^{+}[Q]|\geq|A|\geq|V(D)|^{1/3}
, proving the result.
∎

5
Proof of
Theorem

2.2
: Disjoint
q
-kernels

Recall that
S\subseteq V(D)
is an
(r-1)
-source set if
N^{-}(S)=\emptyset
and if
0<|S|\leq r-1
. In this subsection, we show that digraphs without
(r-1)
-source sets contain
r
disjoint
q
-kernels with

small. We will in fact prove something slightly stronger.

Definition 5
.

Given a vector
\beta
of length
r
and a digraph
D
, we say that a tuple
(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r})
of disjoint vertex subsets of
D
is a
\beta
-kernel
of
D
if
Q_{i}
is a
\beta_{i}
-kernel of
D
for all
i
.

For example, a (3,2)-kernel of a digraph is a pair of disjoint sets
Q_{1},Q_{2}
such that
Q_{1}
is a 3-kernel and
Q_{2}
is a 2-kernel.

Theorem 5.1
.

Define a sequence of vectors
\beta^{(r)}
of length
r
by taking
\beta^{(2)}=(3,2)
, and for
r>2
by setting

\beta_{i}^{(r)}=\begin{cases}2(\beta_{i}^{(r-1)}+r-2)&i<r,\\
2r-3&i=r.\end{cases}

If
D
contains no
(r-1)
-source sets for
r\geq 2
, then it contains a
\beta^{(r)}
-kernel.

It is not difficult to determine the exact values of
\beta^{(r)}
from
Theorem

5.1
.

Corollary 5.2
.

If
D
contains no
(r-1)
-source sets for
r\geq 2
, then it contains a
\beta^{(r)}
-kernel where

\beta_{i}^{(r)}=\begin{cases}7\cdot 2^{r-2}-2r&i=1,\\
3\cdot 2^{r-1}-2r&i=2,\\
(4i-3)2^{r-i}-2r&3\leq i\leq r.\end{cases}

Indeed, this follows by checking for each
i
that the sequence
\beta_{i}^{(r)}
in
Theorem

5.1
satisfies the same initial condition and recurrence relation as the sequence in
Corollary

5.2
. From this our main result for disjoint
q
-kernels is easy to derive.

Proof of
Theorem

2.2
assuming
Corollary

5.2
.

Recall that we wish to show that every digraph without
(r-1)
-sources contains
r
disjoint
2^{r+1}
-kernels. By
Corollary

5.2
we know there exist
r
disjoint sets which are each
\beta_{i}^{(r)}
-kernels. It is straightforward to show that
\beta_{i}^{(r)}\leq 2^{r+1}
for all
i
(for example, by noting that
4i-3\leq 2^{i+1}
for
i\geq 3
), giving the result.
∎

It remains to prove
Theorem

5.1
. We begin by proving a slight strengthening of the
r=2
case which will serve as a warmup for the general approach.

Lemma 5.3
.

If
D
is a source-free digraph, then for every independent set
Q\subseteq V(D)
, there exists a 3-kernel
Q^{\prime}
of
D
which is disjoint from
Q
.

Note that this together with the fact that every digraph contains a quasikernel implies that every source-free digraph contains a (3,2)-kernel. This implies the
r=2
case of
Theorem

5.1
as well as
Proposition

2.1
.

Proof.

Define
D^{\prime}=D-Q
and let
Q^{\prime}
be any 2-kernel of
D^{\prime}
. Because
D
is source-free and
Q
is independent, every vertex of
Q
has an in-neighbor in
V(D)\setminus Q
. Since
Q^{\prime}
can reach every vertex of
V(D)\setminus Q
with a path of length at most 2 by construction,
Q^{\prime}
can reach every vertex of
V(D)
with a path of length at most 3. Since
Q^{\prime}
is independent by construction, we conclude that
Q^{\prime}
is a 3-kernel, giving the result.
∎

We would like to adapt the approach above to find 3 disjoint
q
-kernels in digraphs which contain no 2-source sets. Specifically, we would like to prove that given any independent set
Q
, there always exist two disjoint
q
-kernels in
V(D)\setminus Q
.

Unfortunately this approach will not work. Indeed, consider the digraph
D_{s}
with vertex set
\{u,v_{1},\ldots,v_{s}\}
which consists of the arcs
uv_{i}
and
v_{i}u
for all
i
(equivalently,
D_{s}
is a star graph where each edge is replaced by a directed 2-cycle). It is not difficult to check that
D_{s}
contains no 2-source sets if
s\geq 2
, but there exists an independent set
Q=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{s}\}
such that
V(D_{s})\setminus Q=\{u\}
does not contain two disjoint
q
-kernels.

Roughly speaking, the issue with the previous example is that, although
D_{s}
contains no 2-source sets for
s\geq 2
, there exists an independent set
Q
such that
D_{s}-Q
contains sources, and hence there is no hope in finding two disjoint
q
-kernels in
D_{s}-Q
. More generally, one can show that the following condition is an obstruction to the approach of
Lemma

5.3
.

Definition 6
.

Given a digraph
D
, we say that a non-empty set of vertices
S
is a
pseduo-source set
if
N^{-}(S)
is an independent set and if
S\cup N^{-}(S)
is a source set (i.e.
N^{-}(S\cup N^{-}(S))=\emptyset
). Equivalently,
S
is a pseudo-source set if every
v\in N^{-}(S)
has
N^{-}(v)\subseteq S
. We say that such a set of vertices
S
is an
s
-pseudo-source set
if
S
is non-empty with
|S|\leq s
.

For example, every source set is a pseudo-source set (since in this case
N^{-}(S)=\emptyset
), and in particular digraphs without 1-pseudo-source sets are source-free. More generally, one can check that a digraph
D
contains a 1-pseudo-source set if and only if it contains an induced copy of
D_{s}
for some
s\geq 0
which has no incoming arcs from the rest of the digraph (noting in particular that the
s=0
case is equivalent to
D
containing a source).

It turns out that pseudo-source sets are the only obstruction to the approach of
Lemma

5.3
going through.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\beta
be a vector of length
r-1\geq 2
such that every digraph
D^{\prime}
without
(r-2)
-source sets contains a
\beta
-kernel. If
D
is a digraph which contains no
(r-2)
-pseudo-source sets, then for every independent set
Q\subseteq V(D)
, there exist disjoint sets
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r-1}
which are disjoint from
Q
such that each
Q_{i}
is a
(2\beta_{i}+1)
-kernel for
D
.

Proof.

Define a digraph
D^{\prime}
on
V(D)\setminus Q
by including the arc
uw
if either
uw\in E(D)
or if there exists a vertex
v\in Q
such that
uv,vw\in E(D)
.

Claim 5.5
.

D^{\prime}
contains no
(r-2)
-source sets.

Proof.

Assume for contradiction that there exists a non-empty set
S\subseteq V(D^{\prime})=V(D)\setminus Q
with
|S|\leq r-2
and
N^{-}_{D^{\prime}}(S)=\emptyset
. Our aim is to show that
S
is an
(r-2)
-pseudo-source set in
D
, contradicting our hypothesis on
D
. In particular, it suffices to show that each
v\in N^{-}_{D}(S)
has
N^{-}_{D}(v)\subseteq S
.

Consider an arbitrary
v\in N^{-}_{D}(S)
(if such a vertex exists), say with
vw\in E(D)
and
w\in S
. If
v\in V(D)\setminus Q
, then
vw\in E(D^{\prime})
by definition of
D^{\prime}
(since
w\in S\subseteq V(D)\setminus Q
), contradicting
N^{-}_{D^{\prime}}(S)=\emptyset
. Thus we can assume
v\in Q
. Note then that every
u\in N^{-}_{D}(v)
satisfies
u\notin Q
(since
v\in Q
and
Q
is independent) and
u\notin V(D^{\prime})\setminus S=V(D)\setminus(S\cup Q)
(since otherwise
v\in Q
would imply
uw\in E(D^{\prime})
by definition of
D^{\prime}
, contradicting
N_{D^{\prime}}^{-}(S)=\emptyset
), so we must have
u\in S
. Since
u\in N_{D}^{-}(v)
was arbitrary we conclude
N_{D}^{-}(v)\subseteq S
, completing our contradiction for
S
being an
(r-2)
-pseudo-source set.
∎

By hypothesis and the claim above, there exists a
\beta
-kernel
(Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r-1})
of
D^{\prime}
. We claim that each
Q_{i}
is a
(2\beta_{i}+1)
-kernel of
D
, which will complete the proof.

Indeed, fix some
i
and first consider an arbitrary vertex
w\in V(D^{\prime})
. By definition of
Q_{i}
, there exists some
u\in Q_{i}
and a directed walk from
u
to
w
of length at most
\beta_{i}
in
D^{\prime}
. By definition of
D^{\prime}
, each of these walks in
D^{\prime}
can be extended to a walk of length at most
2\beta_{i}
in
D
. This gives the right distance condition for vertices in
V(D^{\prime})=V(D)\setminus Q
, so it remains to show the result for
w\in Q
.

Because
D
is source-free (since it contains no
(r-1)
-pseudo-source sets) and
Q
is an independent set, every
w\in Q
has some in-neighbor
v\in V(D)\setminus Q=V(D^{\prime})
. By our previous analysis, we know
v
can be reached from some
u\in Q_{i}
by a walk of length at most
2\beta_{i}
, which means
w
can be reached by a walk of length at most
2\beta_{i}+1
. We conclude that
Q_{i}
is a
(2\beta_{i}+1)
-kernel, proving the result.
∎

In order to use
Proposition

5.4
towards proving
Theorem

5.1
, we must transform digraphs which avoid
(r-1)
-source sets into digraphs which avoid
(r-2)
-pseudo-source sets. This is accomplished through the following lemma, where here we say that a pseudo-source set
S
is
proper
if there does not exist a non-empty set
T\subsetneq S
which is a pseudo-source set.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
r\geq 3
be an integer and
D
a digraph without

Putting these last two claims together, we see that

To use

Lemma 5.10

If

We begin with the following.

Claim 5.11

For any two vertices

We next show that every

6
Proof of
Theorem

2.5
: Bipartite Digraphs

In this section we show that bipartite digraphs with high girth have small
q
-kernels. We begin by proving our result for quasikernels, for which the following will be useful.

Lemma 6.1
.

If
D
is a digraph such that for every vertex
u\in V(D)
there exists a vertex
v\in N^{+}(u)
which has in-degree 1, then
D
is a disjoint union of directed cycles.

Proof.

Since by hypothesis every vertex in
V(D)
has an out-neighbor with in-degree 1, and since by definition each vertex of in-degree 1 has only 1 in-neighbor, it follows that there are at least (and hence exactly)
|V(D)|
vertices with in-degree 1. It follows that every vertex also has out-degree 1 (using that every vertex has positive out-degree by hypothesis and that the sum of the in-degrees equals the sum of the out-degrees), from which we conclude that
D
is a disjoint union of directed cycles.
∎

With this we can prove our result for small quasikernels.

Proof of
Proposition

2.4
.

Recall that we wish to show that if
D
is a source-free digraph with bipartition
U\cup V
which is not the disjoint union of directed 2-cycles and 4-cycles, then there exists a quasikernel
Q
such that
|Q|<\frac{1}{2}|V(D)|
. If
|U|<\frac{1}{2}|V(D)|
, then taking
Q=U
is a quasikernel (since
D
is source-free and bipartite) which satisfies the result. A similar result holds if
|V|<\frac{1}{2}|V(D)|
, so we may assume
|U|=|V|=\frac{1}{2}|V(D)|
.

First consider the case that
D
is a directed cycle on
x_{1},\ldots,x_{2\ell}
for some
\ell\geq 3
. In this case the set
Q=\{x_{1}\}\cup\{x_{2i}:2\leq i\leq\ell-1\}
is a quasikernel with
|Q|=\ell-1<\frac{1}{2}|V(D)|
. A similar argument works if
D
is a disjoint union of directed cycles (which necessarily has at least one cycle of length at least 6 by hypothesis), so we may assume
D
is not of this form. The previous lemma thus implies that there exists a vertex
u\in V(D)
such that every vertex in
N^{+}(u)
has in-degree at least 2, and without loss of generality we may assume
u\in U
.

Consider
Q=U\setminus\{u\}
, and observe that
Q
is a quasikernel due to both our hypothesis on
u
(which ensures that every vertex of
V
is within distance 1 of
Q
) and our hypothesis that
D
is source-free (which ensures
u
is within distance 1 of some vertex in
V
). We also have
|Q|=\frac{1}{2}|V(D)|-1
by hypothesis on
U
, proving the result.
∎

We now prove our results about
q
-kernels. Here the key idea will be to reduce the problem to the following case.

Definition 7
.

We say that a finite digraph
D
is
unicyclic
if every vertex has in-degree 1 and if the underlying graph of
D
is connected.

The name “unicyclic” is motivated by the following result.

Lemma 6.2
.

If
D
is unicyclic, then (1)
D
contains exactly one directed cycle
C
, and (2) for every vertex
w\in V(D)
, there exists a unique directed path
P_{w}
whose first vertex is in
C
, last vertex is
w
, and which contains no other vertices of
C
.

Proof.

Let
w_{1}\in V(D)
be arbitrary and iteratively define
w_{i+1}
to be the unique vertex in
N^{-}(v_{i})
. Because
D
is finite, we must have
w_{i}=w_{j}
for some
i<j
. If one chooses this
i,j
so that
j-i
is as small as possible, then we see that
w_{j},w_{j-1},\ldots,w_{i+1}
are distinct vertices with
w_{k+1}w_{k}\in E(D)
for all
i<k<j
and with
w_{i+1}w_{j}\in E(D)
. We conclude that
D
contains at least one directed cycle. Moreover, the directed path
w_{i},w_{i-1}\ldots,w_{1}
shows that every vertex of
D
has a directed path to it from a directed cycle which intersects this cycle only at its endpoint. It thus remains to prove uniqueness of these cycles and paths.

Let
C=(v_{j},v_{j-1},\ldots v_{1})
be a directed cycle and assume for contradiction that there exists some other directed cycle
C^{\prime}
which contains
v_{1}
. Because
v_{1}
has a unique in-neighbor, namely
v_{2}
, we see that the in-neighbor of
v_{1}
in
C^{\prime}
must also be
v_{2}
, and continuing this we see that

, a contradiction. Thus if another directed cycle
C^{\prime}
exists it must be disjoint from

. Because

is unicyclic, there exists some shortest (undirected) path
u_{1}\cdots u_{t}
in the underlying graph of

with

and

. Because this is a shortest path, we must have
u_{2}\notin C
, and because the unique in-neighbor of

. Continuing this argument by induction, we conclude that
u_{i}u_{i+1}\in E(D)
for all

, but this implies

has an in-neighbor that is not in

, a contradiction to the in-degree 1 hypothesis. We conclude that

is the only directed cycle in

.

Finally, let
w\in V(D)
and suppose there exists two directed paths
u_{s}u_{s-1}\cdots u_{1}
and

with the properties as in the lemma, noting that this means
u_{1}=v_{1}=w
. Iterativelly given that
u_{i}=v_{i}
for some

, we must have that
u_{i+1}=v_{i+1}
since

has a unique in-neighbor. If, say,

, then this implies the path

contains two vertices of

, namely

and

(since this is the unique in-neighbor of

), a contradiction. Thus we must have

and that

for all

, proving these directed paths are unique.
∎

We now prove the following special case of

Let

Assume for contradiction that there exists a counterexample

as small as possible. Let

Claim 6.4

For this proof, we will make multiple uses of the fact that if

U^{\prime}\cup V^{\prime}
with e.g.
U^{\prime}=U\cap V(D^{\prime})
.

We thus see that
D^{\prime}
satisfies the conditions of the proposition. Because
D
was a minimal counterexample, there must exist a
q
-kernel
Q^{\prime}\subseteq U^{\prime}
of
D^{\prime}
satisfying

|Q^{\prime}|\leq\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D^{\prime})|=\frac{2}{q+3}(|V(D)|-|X|)\leq\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D)|-1,

with this last step using
|X|\geq(q+3)/2
(since
X
includes every vertex along the path from
w^{\prime}
to
w
).

Define
z
to be
w^{\prime}
if
w^{\prime}\in U
and
z
to be the unique vertex in
N^{-}_{D}(w^{\prime})
otherwise. Note that in either case
z\in U
. Define
Q=Q^{\prime}\cup\{z\}\subseteq U
, noting that this satisfies
|Q|\leq\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D)|
by the inequality above. We also have for
x\notin X
that

\mathrm{dist}_{D}(Q,x)\leq\mathrm{dist}_{D^{\prime}}(Q^{\prime},x)\leq q,

and for
x\in X
we have

\mathrm{dist}(Q,x)\leq\mathrm{dist}(z,x)\leq\mathrm{dist}(z,w^{\prime})+\mathrm{dist}(w^{\prime},x)\leq 1+(q+1)/2\leq q.

Thus
Q
is a
q
-kernel satisfying the conditions of the proposition, contradicting
D
being a counterexample. We conclude the claim. ∎

We can boost the previous claim by showing there are relatively few vertices outside the cycle. For this we introduce some new notation that will be vital for the rest of the proof.

Label the vertices of the cycle
C
by
(u_{1},v_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell},v_{\ell})
with
u_{j}\in U
for all
j
. We say that a vertex
w\in V(D)
has
type
j
if the path
P_{w}
begins with either
u_{j}
or
v_{j}
. Equivalently,
w
is of type
j
if
\mathrm{dist}(C,w)=\mathrm{dist}(\{u_{j},v_{j}\},w)
. We let
\mathrm{ty}(j)
denote the number of vertices of type
j
in
D
. A useful observation that we use throughout the proof is that if
w
is of type
j
, then

\mathrm{dist}(u_{j},w)\leq\mathrm{ty}(j)-1,

(3)

as the shortest path from
u_{j}
to
w
only uses vertices of type
j
(and the length of this path will be 1 less than the total number of vertices in the path). With this observation in mind, we see that the following significantly improves upon the previous claim.

Claim 6.5
.

We have
\mathrm{ty}(j)<(q+3)/2
for all
j
.

Proof.

Assume for contradiction that there exists some
j
with
\mathrm{ty}(j)\geq(q+3)/2
.

First consider the case
|V(D)|-\mathrm{ty}(j)\leq(q+1)/2
, and let
Q=\{u_{j}\}\subseteq U
. Observe that
|Q|\leq\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D)|
since we assume
|V(D)|\geq(q+3)/2
. Also observe that if
w\in V(D)
is of type
j^{\prime}\neq j
, then there exists a shortest path from
u_{j}
to
w
obtained by using the arc
u_{j}v_{j}
followed by some set of vertices not of type
j
, so

\mathrm{dist}(u_{j},w)\leq 1+(|V(D)|-\mathrm{ty}(j))\leq 1+(q+1)/2\leq q.

On the other hand, if
w
is of type
j
, then
Claim

6.4
implies

\mathrm{dist}(u_{j},w)\leq 1+(q-1)\leq q,

where the
1
comes from the fact that
P_{w}
may begin with
v_{j}
.
We conclude that
Q\subseteq U
is a
q
-kernel for
D
of size at most
\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D)|
, a contradiction to
D
being a counterexample to the proposition. Thus we may assume
|V(D)|-\mathrm{ty}(j)\geq(q+3)/2
.

Next consider the subcase
\ell=2
and define
Q=\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\subseteq U
. Observe that our hypothesis
\mathrm{ty}(j),|V(D)|-\mathrm{ty}(j)\geq(q+3)/2
imply
V(D)|\geq q+3
, so
|Q|\leq\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D)|
. Moreover, if
w\in V(D)
has type
j
, then again by using
Claim

6.4
we find

\mathrm{dist}(Q,w)\leq\mathrm{dist}(u_{j},w)\leq q,

so
Q
is a
q
-kernel, contradicting
D
being a counterexample.

With this we may assume
\ell>2
and
|V(D)|-\mathrm{ty}(j)\geq(q+3)/2
. Let
D^{\prime}
be the digraph obtained from
D
by deleting all vertices of type
j
and then adding the arc
v_{j-1}u_{j+1}
(with these indices written mod
\ell
). It is not difficult to see that
D^{\prime}
is a unicyclic digraph with cycle length
2(\ell-1)\geq 4
which has
|V(D^{\prime})|=|V(D)|-\mathrm{ty}(j)\geq(q+3)/2
and which has bipartition
U^{\prime}\cup V^{\prime}
defined by e.g.
U^{\prime}=U\cap V(D^{\prime})
.

Since
D^{\prime}
satisfies the conditions of the proposition, and since we choose
D
to be a minimal counterexample, there must exist some
q
-kernel
Q^{\prime}\subseteq U^{\prime}
for
D^{\prime}
of size at most

\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D^{\prime})|=\frac{2}{q+3}(|V(D)|-\mathrm{ty}(j))\leq\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D)|-1.

Let
Q:=Q^{\prime}\cup\{u_{j}\}\subseteq U
, noting that
|Q|\leq\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D)|
by the above. We claim that
Q
is a
q
-kernel for
D
.

Indeed, if
w\in V(D)
is of type
j
then
Claim

6.4
implies
\mathrm{dist}(u_{j},w)\leq q
. Otherwise
w\in V(D^{\prime})
, so there exists a directed path
P^{\prime}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{t})
from
Q^{\prime}
to
w
in
D^{\prime}
of length at most
q
. If the arc
v_{j-1}u_{j+1}
is not in
P^{\prime}
, then every arc of
P^{\prime}
is still in
D
, so
P^{\prime}\subseteq D
and the distance condition is satisfied. Thus we may assume
v_{j-1}u_{j+1}
is in
P^{\prime}
, say with
w_{s}=v_{j-1}
, noting that
s\geq 2
since any path from
Q^{\prime}\subseteq U
must start with a vertex in
U
. In this case we consider the path
P=(u_{j},v_{j},w_{s+1},\ldots,w_{t})
(i.e. we delete the beginning of
P^{\prime}
up to
u_{j+1}
and then append
u_{j},v_{j}
at the start). Note that

\mathrm{len}(P)=\mathrm{len}(P^{\prime})+2-s\leq\mathrm{len}(P^{\prime})\leq q,

so this path shows
\mathrm{dist}_{D}(Q,w)\leq q
.

In total we conclude that
Q\subseteq U
is a small
q
-kernel for
D
, a contradiction, giving the claim.
∎

Define
m=\lceil(q+3)/4\rceil
. Equivalently,
m\geq 2
is the integer such that either
q=-3+4m
or
q=-5+4m
. The idea here is that if we can find some
Q\subseteq U
which uses a
1/m
fraction of the
\ell
vertices
u_{j}
, then it turns out
Q
will have the desired size. This idea quickly gives the following.

Claim 6.6
.

We have
\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D)|<\lceil\ell/m\rceil
.

Proof.

Assume for contradiction that
\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D)|\geq\lceil\ell/m\rceil
. Define

Q=\{u_{1+mt}:0\leq t\leq\lceil\ell/m\rceil-1\}\subseteq U,

noting that
|Q|=\lceil\ell/m\rceil\leq\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D)|
.

We aim to show
Q
is a
q
-kernel. To this end, consider any
w\in V(D)
, say of type
j
. Observe that there exists some integer
0\leq t<\lceil\ell/m\rceil
such that
1+mt\leq j\leq m(t+1)
(namely
t=\lfloor(j-1)/m\rfloor
works via observing
j-m\leq mt\leq j-1
). This and (
3
) implies

\mathrm{dist}(Q,w)\leq\mathrm{dist}(u_{1+mt},u_{j})+\mathrm{dist}(u_{j},w)\leq 2(j-(1+mt))+\mathrm{ty}(j)-1\leq 2(m-1)+(q-1)/2,

with this last step using
j\leq m(t+1)
and
Claim

6.5
. Note that this quantity is at most
q
since
q\geq-5+4m
. Thus
Q\subseteq U
is a small
q
-kernel for
D
, a contradiction.
∎

We will use the bound above to prove the following set of inequalities, where here and throughout we let
0\leq a\leq m-1
be the integer such that
\ell\equiv a\mod m
.

Claim 6.7
.

For all
j
we have

\mathrm{ty}(j)\leq q-2m-2a+5,

and further

\min_{j}\mathrm{ty}(j)\leq q-2m-2a+3.

Proof.

We begin by making a few observations about
|V(D)|
. By
Claim

6.6
, we have

\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D)|<\lceil\ell/m\rceil\leq\lfloor\ell/m\rfloor+1=(\ell+m-a)/m.

Recall that
|V(D)|\geq(q+3)/2
by hypothesis of the proposition, so the inequality above combined with
\ell\equiv a\mod m
implies

\ell\geq m+a.

Using these two inequalities gives

|V(D)|<\frac{(\ell+m-a)(q+3)}{2m}=(2\ell+2m-2a)+\frac{(\ell+m-a)(q+3-4m)}{2m}\leq(2\ell+2m-2a)+(q+3-4m),

where this last step used
q+3-4m\leq 0
and
\ell\geq m+a
. In total, this implies

|V(D)|\leq 2\ell+q-2m-2a+2.

(4)

We also note that

q-2m-2a+2>0.

Indeed, recalling that either
q=-5+4m
or
q=-3+4m
, we see that this immediately holds if either
a\leq m-2
or if
a=m-1
and
q=-3+4m
, so the only potential issue is when
a=m-1
and
q=-5+4m
. In this case, (
4
) would imply
|V(D)|\leq 2\ell-1
, a contradiction to having
|V(D)|\geq 2\ell
by virtue of
D
containing a directed
2\ell
-cycle.

Returning to the proof of the claim; if there existed some
j
with
\mathrm{ty}(j)\geq q-2m-2a+6
, then this together with the trivial bound
\mathrm{ty}(j^{\prime})\geq 2
for all
j^{\prime}
(coming from
u_{j},v_{j}
) would imply

|V(D)|=\sum\mathrm{ty}(j^{\prime})\geq 2(\ell-1)+q-2m-2a+6=2\ell+q-2m-2a+4,

a contradiction to (
4
). This proves the first part of the claim.

Similarly, if we had
\mathrm{ty}(j)\geq q-2m-2a+4
for all
j
, then this would imply

\displaystyle|V(D)|

\displaystyle\geq\ell(q-2m-2a+4)=2\ell+\ell(q-2m-2a+2)

\displaystyle=2\ell+q-2m-2a+2+(\ell-1)(q-2m-2a+2)>2\ell+q-2m-2a+2,

where this last step used
q-2m-2a+2>0
and
\ell\geq 2
. This contradicts (
4
), so we conclude the result.
∎

With these inequalities and the definition of
a
in mind, we assume without loss of generality that
\mathrm{ty}(\ell)=\min_{j}\mathrm{ty}(j)
and define

Q=\{u_{1+mt}:0\leq t\leq\lfloor\ell/m\rfloor-1\}=\{u_{1+mt}:0\leq t\leq(\ell-a)/m-1\}\subseteq U.

Note that

|Q|=\lfloor\ell/m\rfloor\leq\frac{1}{2m}\cdot 2\ell\leq\frac{2}{q+3}\cdot 2\ell\leq\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D)|,

where this first inequality used that
\frac{2}{q+3}
either equals
\frac{1}{2m}
if
q=-3+4m
or
\frac{1}{2m-1}\geq\frac{1}{2m}
if
q=-5+4m
. Note that this inequality together with
Claim

6.6
implies
a\neq 0
(i.e. that
\lfloor\ell/m\rfloor\neq\lceil\ell/m\rceil
).

We aim to show that
Q
is a
q
-kernel. First, we claim that if
w\in V(D)
is of type
j<\ell
, then there exists some
0\leq t\leq(\ell-a)/m-1
such that
1+mt\leq j\leq a-1+m(t+1)
. Indeed, if
j\leq\ell-a
then one can take
t=\lfloor(j-1)/m\rfloor\leq(\ell-a)/m-1
, and as in the proof of
Claim

6.6
this satisfies

1+mt\leq j\leq m(t+1)\leq a-1+m(t+1).

If instead
\ell-a<j<\ell
then we take
t=(\ell-a)/m-1
, in which case

a-1+m(t+1)=\ell-1\geq j\geq 1+mt.

Using this together with (
3
) and
Claim

6.7
gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(Q,w)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{dist}(u_{1+mt},u_{j})+\mathrm{dist}(u_{j},w)

\displaystyle\leq 2(j-(1+mt))+\mathrm{ty}(j)-1

\displaystyle\leq 2(m+a-2)+(q-2m-2a+5)-1=q,

where this last inequality used
j\leq a-1+m(t+1)
. Similarly if
w
has type
\ell
then we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(Q,w)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{dist}(u_{1+\ell-a-m},u_{\ell})+\mathrm{dist}(u_{\ell},w)

\displaystyle\leq 2(\ell-(1+\ell-a-m))+\mathrm{ty}(\ell)-1

\displaystyle\leq 2(m+a-1)+(q-2m-2a+3)-1=q.

We conclude that
Q
is a small
q
-kernel for
D
, a contradiction. We conclude that no counterexample
D
exists, proving the result.
∎

With
Proposition

6.3
in hand we can prove
Theorem

2.5
in the special case when
q
is odd and
\ell=(q+3)/2
.

Theorem 6.8
.

Let
D
be a source-free digraph with kernel
K
. If
q\geq 3
is odd and every directed cycle of
D
of even length has length at least
(q+3)/2
, then
D
contains a
q
-kernel
Q
of size at most
\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D)|
with
Q\subseteq K
.

Proof.

Let
D,q
be as in the hypothesis of the theorem. Observe that if we can find some
Q\subseteq K
which is a
q
-kernel for some spanning subgraph
D^{\prime}\subseteq D
, then
Q
will still be a
q
-kernel of
D
since
K
is independent in
D
(and so will
Q
). With this in mind, we first define
\tilde{D}^{\prime}\subseteq D
by deleting every arc which does not use a vertex of
K
, noting that this digraph continues to be source-free since
K
is a kernel (which means every vertex outside of
K
still has an in-neighbor from
K
, and every vertex inside
K
still has its previous incoming arcs). We then define a subdigraph
D^{\prime}\subseteq\tilde{D}^{\prime}
by iteratively deleting arcs from
\tilde{D}^{\prime}
until every vertex has in-degree exactly 1 (and this can be done since
\tilde{D}^{\prime}
is source-free).

We can write
D^{\prime}
as the disjoint union of digraphs
D_{1}\cup\cdots\cup D_{t}
where the underlying graph of each
D_{i}
is connected, and we observe that each
D_{i}
is unicyclic and bipartite with bipartition
U_{i}\cup V_{i}
defined by
U_{i}=V(D_{i})\cap K
and
V_{i}=V(D_{i})\setminus K
. Moreover, since every directed cycle of
D
of even length
has length at least
(q+3)/2
, the (even lengthed) directed cycle in each
D_{i}
has length at least
(q+3)/2
. This implies
|V(D_{i})|\geq(q+3)/2
for all
i
, and that the length of the cycle in each
D_{i}
is at least 4 (since this cycle length is an even integer that is at least
(q+3)/2\geq 3
). We can thus apply
Proposition

6.3
to obtain
q
-kernels
Q_{i}
for each
D_{i}
with
|Q_{i}|\leq\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D_{i})|
and
Q_{i}\subseteq U_{i}
.

Let
Q=\bigcup Q_{i}
. Because
Q_{i}\subseteq U_{i}\subseteq K
for all
i
, we have
Q\subseteq K
(and hence is an independent set). We also have
|Q|\leq\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D)|
(since
|Q_{i}|\leq\frac{2}{q+3}|V(D_{i})|
for all
i
) and that
\mathrm{dist}_{D}(Q,x)\leq q
for all
x\in V(D)
(since
x\in V(D_{i})
for some
i
and
\mathrm{dist}_{D}(Q,x)\leq\mathrm{dist}_{D_{i}}(Q_{i},x)\leq q
). We conclude that
Q
is a
q
-kernel of the desired size of
D^{\prime}\subseteq D
and hence of
D
, completing the proof.
∎

We now use this to complete the proof of our main result.

Proof of
Theorem

2.5
.

Recall that we wish to show that if
D
is a source-free digraph which has a kernel
K
and if
q,\ell\geq 3
are integers such that
\ell\leq(q+3)/2
and such that every directed cycle of
D
of even length has length at least
\ell
, then
D
contains a
q
-kernel
Q
of size at most
\frac{1}{\ell}|V(D)|
with
Q\subseteq K
.

By the hypothesis of the theorem, we can apply
Theorem

6.8
with
q^{\prime}=2\ell-3\geq 3
to obtain a
q^{\prime}
-kernel
Q
of
D
with size at most
\frac{2}{q^{\prime}+3}|V(D)|=\frac{1}{\ell}|V(D)|
that lies in
K
. Note that
Q
is also a
q
-kernel since
q\geq q^{\prime}=2\ell-3
by hypothesis, proving the result.
∎

One can extend
Theorem

2.5
somewhat with some (non-trivial) extra work. To this end, given a digraph

, we say that a partition
V_{1}\cup\cdots V_{r}
of

is an

-cyclic partition
if every arc

has

and
v\in V_{i+1}
for some

(where here and throughout we write our indices mod

). By adapting our methods we claim without proof that we can establish the following generalization of

2.5
.

Theorem 6.9
.

Let
D
be a source-free bipartite digraph with
r
-cyclic partition
V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{r}
. If
q\geq r^{2}-1
and
\ell\geq(r^{2}+r)/2
are integers such that
\ell\leq(q+1+r)/2
,
\ell\equiv 0\mod r
, and such that every directed cycle of

has length at least
\ell
, then
D
contains a
q
-kernel
Q
of size at most

with

.

As
Theorem

6.9
is not our focus, we defer discussing the tightness of its parameters and the details of its proof to the Appendix.

7
Concluding Remarks

In this section we outline a number of remaining problems involving
q
-kernels.

7.1
Disjoint
q
-kernels

Recall that
Theorem

2.2
guarantees the existence of
r
disjoint
q
-kernels with
q\leq 2^{r+1}
provided
D
contains no
(r-1)
-source sets. The best construction we know of is
D=C_{2r-1}
, which requires
q\geq 2r-2
in order to have
r
-disjoint
q
-kernels. We suspect that this lower bound is much closer to the truth compared to the upper bound.

Conjecture 7.1
.

There exists
\varepsilon,r_{0}>0
such that if
D
contains no
(r-1)
-source sets with
r\geq r_{0}
, then
D
contains
r
-disjoint
q
-kernels with
q\leq(2-\varepsilon)^{r}
.

The main barrier for proving
Conjecture

7.1
is
Proposition

5.4
, which roughly involves replacing
D
by its “square” and is the primary source of the growth rate of
2^{r}
. As
Proposition

5.4
is the heart of our present argument, significant new ideas would be needed in order to prove
Conjecture

7.1
.

7.2
Small
q
-kernels

We next turn to questions involving small
q
-kernels in source-free digraphs with certain restrictions.

7.2.1
Restricted Cycle Lengths

Question

2.3
involving digraphs with restricted cycle lengths seems too difficult to solve in general, but many interesting problems remain even in the case when
D
is bipartite. For example,
Theorem

2.5
gives optimal bounds when
D
is a bipartite digraph of an appropriate girth. It would be interesting to extend this result to all bipartite
D
. One promising approach for odd
q
would be the following, where here we recall a digraph is unicyclic if every vertex has in-degree 1 and if the underlying graph is connected.

Question 7.2
.

Let
D
be a unicyclic digraph with directed cycle of length
2\ell
and bipartition
U\cup V
. If
q\geq 3
is odd, do there exist
q
-kernels
Q_{U}\subseteq U
and
Q_{V}\subseteq V
such that

|Q_{U}|+|Q_{V}|\leq 2\cdot\frac{\lceil\ell/(q+1)\rceil}{\ell}\cdot|V(D)|?

As mentioned in the introduction, it is not true in general that we can always guarantee e.g.
|Q_{U}|\leq\frac{\lceil\ell/(q+1)\rceil}{\ell}\cdot|V(D)|
, but
Question

7.2
suggests that on average at least one of the sets
Q_{U}
or
Q_{V}
will meet this bound if
q
is odd. If this were true, then one could use the same reduction from the proof of
Theorem

2.5
to give a positive answer to
Question

2.3
whenever
q
is odd and
D
is bipartite (with the additional conclusion that
Q
is contained in either
U
or
V
, though we can not specify which set it will lie in ahead of time).

The situation is more complex for bipartite
D
when
q
is even, as we can not in general find small
q
-kernels contained in either
U
or
V
. For
q=2
we managed to prove the weak bound
|Q|<\frac{1}{2}|V(D)|
whenever
D
(in particular) avoids directed 2-cycles and 4-cycles. We suspect this can be improved substantially.

Conjecture 7.3
.

There exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that if
D
is a source-free bipartite digraph with no directed 2-cycles or 4-cycles, then
D
contains a quasikernel
Q
with
|Q|\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon\right)|V(D)|
.

The optimal possible value here would be
\varepsilon=1/10
due to
C_{10}
. We note that
Question

2.3
predicts that
Conjecture

7.3
should hold even without the hypothesis that
Q
is bipartite, but this seems comparable in difficulty to the Small Quasikernel Conjecture itself.

7.2.2
Variants of Source-Free Digraphs

Source-free digraphs are exactly digraphs with minimum in-degree 1. As such, the following is a natural followup question to the Small Quasikernel Conjecture.

Question 7.4
.

For integers
\delta\geq 1
and
q\geq 2
, what is the smallest constant
c_{\delta,q}
such that every digraph
D
with minimum in-degree
\delta
contains a
q
-kernel
Q
with
|Q|\leq c_{\delta,q}|V(D)|
?

For example, the Small Quasikernel Conjecutre is equivalent to the statement
c_{1,2}=\frac{1}{2}
. The full extent of our knowledge around this question is as follows.

Proposition 7.5
.

Let
c_{\delta,q}
be defined as above.

(a)

For all
\delta\geq 1
and
q\geq 2
, we have
c_{\delta,q}\geq\frac{1}{\delta+1}
.

(b)

If
q\geq 2^{\delta+2}
, then
c_{\delta,q}=\frac{1}{\delta+1}
.

(c)

For all
\delta\geq 1
, we have
c_{\delta,2}\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

Part (a) follows by considering a bidirected
K_{\delta+1}
, which has minimum in-degree
\delta
and every non-empty independent set has size
\frac{1}{\delta+1}|V(D)|
.

For (b), observe that any digraph
D
with minimum in-degree
\delta
has no
\delta
-source sets. As such,
Theorem

2.2
implies that
D
contains
\delta+1
disjoint
q
-kernels for any
q\geq 2^{\delta+2}
, and in particular one of these has size at most
\frac{1}{\delta+1}|V(D)|
.

For (c), we will show that
c_{\delta,2}\geq\frac{\ell^{2}}{(\ell+1)(2\ell+1)}
for every integer
\ell\geq 1
, from which the result follows. Let
T
be an Eulerian tournament on
v_{1},\ldots,v_{2\ell+1}
and let
k=\delta\ell
(though any large integer will also work). We define a digraph
D
as follows. The vertices of
D
lie in disjoint vertex sets
V_{i},V_{i}^{\prime}
with
|V_{i}|=\delta
and
|V_{i}^{\prime}|=k
for all
1\leq i\leq 2\ell+1
. We add every arc from
V_{i}
to
V_{j}
if
v_{i}\to v_{j}
is an arc in
T
, and we add every arc from
V_{i}
to
V_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i
. In other words,
D
is formed by taking
T
, adding leaves
v^{\prime}_{i}
to each vertex, and then blowing up the
v_{i}
and
v_{i}^{\prime}
vertices into independent sets of size
\delta
and
k
, respectively.

Observe that
D
has minimum in-degree
\delta
. We claim that every quasikernel
Q
of
D
contains at least
k\ell
vertices. Indeed, because
Q
is independent, it can contain vertices from at most one
V_{i}
set. It follows that
Q
must contain every vertex of
V_{j}^{\prime}
for each
j
with
v_{j}\notin N^{+}_{T}[v_{i}]
(as these vertices will not be reached by a path of length at most 2 otherwise). There are exactly
\ell
such vertices
v_{j}
, from which the claim follows. Since
|V(D)|=(k+\delta)(2\ell+1)
, this claim implies that
c_{\delta,2}
must satisfy

c_{\delta,2}\geq\frac{k\ell}{(k+\delta)(2\ell+1)}=\frac{\ell^{2}}{(\ell+1)(2\ell+1)},

proving the result.
∎

The above suggests the following weak version of the Small Quasikernel Conjecture, which is asymptotically optimal in view of
Proposition

7.5
(c).

Conjecture 7.6
.

There exists some
\delta\geq 1
such that every digraph
D
of minimum in-degree at least
\delta
contains a quasikernel
Q
with
|Q|\leq\frac{1}{2}|V(D)|
.

The proof of
Proposition

4.1
can be adjusted to continue to hold if we replace the source-free hypothesis with in-degree at least
\delta
for any fixed
\delta
(which is in fact how we arrived at the construction for
Proposition

7.5
(c)), so weak versions of the Large Quasikernel Conjecture continue to be an obstacle for this conjecture as well. It is unclear what the true behavior of
c_{\delta,q}
should be in general, especially when
\delta
is large in terms of
q\geq 3
.

Another variant of being source-free is being strongly connected. In
[
5
, Example 17]
it is shown that there exist strongly connected digraphs with smallest quasikernels of size
(\frac{1}{2}-o(1))|V(D)|
. For
q\geq 3
we ask the following.

Question 7.7
.

If
D
is a strongly connected digraph and
q\geq 3
is an integer, does there exist a
q
-kernel
Q
of
D
such that
|Q|\leq\frac{|V(D)|}{q+1}+O_{q}(1)
?

This bound would be best possible by considering a collection of
C_{q+2}
’s sharing a common vertex. We have not spent much time thinking about this problem and it is likely that better constructions exist, especially in view of
[
5
, Example 17]
for quasikernels. Still, it would be interesting to see if any non-trivial bounds could be obtained under the assumption of being strongly connected. The best we can show is the existence of
q
-kernels of size at most roughly
\frac{|V(D)|}{\log q}
by using
Theorem

2.2
to show the existence of around
\log q
disjoint
q
-kernels.

7.3
Large Quasikernels

We asked in
Conjecture

2.6
whether every digraph contains a “large” quasikernel, namely one with
|N^{+}[Q]|\geq\frac{1}{2}|V(D)|
, and we made some modest progress towards this goal in
Theorem

2.8
by proving
|N^{+}[Q]|\geq V(D)|^{1/3}
. We believe that our bounds can be improved by proving the following result.

Conjecture 7.8
.

Every digraph
D
either contains a subset
A\subseteq V(D)
with
D[A]
acyclic and
|A|\geq|V(D)|^{1/2}
, or a vertex
y\in V(D)
which is contained in some quasikernel and which has
\deg^{+}(y)\geq|V(D)|^{1/2}-1
.

The bounds of
Conjecture

7.8
are best possible, as can be seen by considering the disjoint union of Eulerian tournaments of size
|V(D)|^{1/2}
. We note that our proof of
Theorem

2.8
(a) essentially reduced to showing the conjecture above holds with
1/2
replaced by
1/3
, and in particular
Conjecture

7.8
would improve
Theorem

2.8
(a) to the stronger bound
|N^{+}[Q]|\geq|V(D)|^{1/2}
, which is the natural barrier for our present approach.

There are various weakenings of the Large Quasikernel Conjecture that one could try to solve, such as by improving the bounds for
q
-kernels, or by replacing
|N^{+}[Q]|
with the larger quantity
\sum_{v\in Q}|N^{+}[v]|
. Another direction would be to look for stronger versions of
Conjecture

2.6
, such as the following.

Question 7.9
.

Does every digraph
D
contains a quasikernel
Q
with
|N^{+}(Q)|\geq\frac{1}{2}|V(D)\setminus Q|
? Equivalently, does there exist a quasikernel with
|N^{+}[Q]|\geq\frac{|V(D)|+|Q|}{2}
?

This result would be sharp by considering the disjoint union of Eulerian tournaments of arbitrary sizes (cf the Large Quasikernel Conjecture which is only asymptotically sharp). It would be interesting to know if, like the Large Quasikernel Conjecture,
Question

7.9
is a consequence of the Small Quasikernel Conjecture.

Update:
shortly after posting our paper, it was shown by Ai, Liu, and Peng
[
2
]
that
Question

7.9
is indeed a consequence of the Small Quasikernel Conjecture. Moreover, a partial converse to
Proposition

4.1
was established, showing that proving non-trivial linear bounds for the Large Quasikernel Conjecture is equivalent to proving non-trivial linear bounds for the Small Quasikernel Conjecture.

Rheomergy : Collective behavior mediated by active flow-based recruitment
S Ganga Prasath, L Mahadevan

Abstract
The physics of signal propagation in a collection of organisms that communicate with each other both enables and limits how active excitations at the individual level reach, recruit and lead to collective patterning. Inspired by the patterns in a planar swarm of bees that release pheromones, and use fanning flows to recruit additional bees, we develop a theoretical framework for patterning via active flow-based recruitment. Our model generalizes the well-known Patlak-Keller-Segel model of diffusion-dominated aggregation and leads to more complex phase space of patterns spanned by two dimensionless parameters that measure the scaled stimulus/activity and the scaled chemotactic response. Together these determine the efficacy of signal communication that leads to a variety of migration and aggregation patterns consistent with observations.



In groups of individual agents in both natural and artificial settings, communication is central to achieving a coordinated collective response. Indeed, the survival of the colony pivots around robust communication in the context of foraging
Couvillon 2012
;
Jackson and Ratnieks 2006
;
Hölldobler 1978
, protection against invasion by an intruder
Nouvian et al. 2016
;
Trhlin et al. 2011
, brood care in eusocial clusters
Gordon 1996
;
Hölldobler et al. 1990
and other tasks inside the nest
Greene and Gordon 2003
;
Free 1987
. The efficacy of communication is limited by the dynamics of signal propagation among agents through the environment
Wingreen and Levin 2006
;
Seeley 2009
;
Gernat et al. 2018
;
Prasath et al. 2021
. Over the last century, stigmergy has emerged as one of the most well studied mechanisms to achieve robust communication
Theraulaz and Bonabeau 1999
;
Perna and Theraulaz 2017
;
Camazine et al. 2003
: here animals modify the environment by using localized signals that serve as recruitment cues in the context of functional behavior. However, stigmergy is just one way of environmentally modulated communication. Organisms use slow diffusive signals
Patlak 1953
;
Keller and Segel 1970
;
Hillen and Painter 2009
that underlie the principle of quorum sensing
Waters and Bassler 2005
, passive and active fluid flows
Ocko and Mahadevan 2015
;
Jost et al. 2007
;
Mathijssen et al. 2019
, and even elastic deformations
Peleg et al. 2018
to transmit information via physical communication channels. Indeed, social insects such as honey bees (e.g. the species
apis mellifera
) use all these different modes of communication and actively modify their local mechanical, thermal and hydrodynamic environment to transmit information and coordinate their activities and functions.

A particularly striking example of active, long-range and fast communication is seen in bees that settle on a substrate and flap their wings to generate a fluid flow parallel to their body axis while releasing pheromones into it
Peters et al. 2019
;
Nguyen et al. 2021
;
Reinhard and Srinivasan 2009
;
Peters et al. 2017
;
Peters et al. 2017
;
Nguyen et al. 2021
;
Ranch 2013
. In Fig.

(a)
and SI Fig. S1, we show how fluid flow mediated communication leads to a characteristic triangular swarm shape that migrates towards the source of pheromones
Nguyen et al. 2021

To understand the nature and efficacy of flow-mediated patterning, we need to couple the three observable spatiotemporal fields that are shown schematically in Fig.
1
(b)
: the bee density
\varrho(\mathbf{x}_{||},t)
(and orientation
\hat{\mathbf{p}}(\mathbf{x}_{||},t)
denotes head forward) as a function of location on the plane
z=0
denoted by
\mathbf{x}_{||}=(x,y)
and time, the scalar pheromone concentration field
c(\mathbf{x},t)
and the active fluid flow generated by the bees
\mathbf{u}_{f}(\mathbf{x},t)
. Bees disperse pheromones from their Nasonov glands into the active flow that they generate by fanning. This leads to the active recruitment of bees from the environment and their migration which then leads to an enhanced rate of pheromone dispersal. The positive feedback leads to spatio-temporal patterns of growing, self-focusing and migrating swarms. While there are many qualitative observations of the process
Reinhard and Srinivasan 2009
;
Winston 1991
, the quantitative collective dynamics of recruitment, agent-environment feedback, and inter-agent interaction mediated by volatile pheromones is largely unexplored. Here, we develop a minimal model for fluid flow mediated communication and recruitment which we dub
rheomergy
. As we will see, our model broadens the scope of the classical Patlak-Keller-Segel (PKS) model
Patlak 1953
;
Keller and Segel 1970
;
Hillen and Painter 2009
for chemotaxis by accounting for dynamics of active fluid flow and the behavioral response of the agents to flow-mediated communication leading to a more complex phase space of patterns.

(

The agent density can exhibit two distinct behaviors, determined by the relative magnitude of chemotaxis and recruitment.

(IIa) Chemotaxis dominated regime
(
\mathsf{V}_{\varrho}\gg 1,\mathsf{R}_{\varrho}\ll 1
):
In this limit, Eq.
13
reduces to a nonlinear heat equation with a negative diffusivity. As the agents sense the pheromone generated by their immediate neighbors (as
\mathsf{V}_{\varrho}\gg 1
) they migrate towards each other which results in a pile-up of the density (see SI sec. IV A for details of the similarity solution associated with aggregation). This aggregation behavior is qualitatively similar to what is seen in the PKS model, however the dynamics follows a different scaling law.

(IIb) Recruitment dominated regime
(
\mathsf{R}_{\varrho}\gg 1,\mathsf{V}_{\varrho}\ll 1
): In this limit, Eq.
13
reduces to a linear homogeneous equation and the agent density grows exponentially as
\varrho\sim\exp(\mathsf{R}_{\varrho}\mathsf{R}_{c}t)
. This leads to homogeneous growth (see SI Fig. S2
(d)
) unlike the flow-dominated scenario where advection of the pheromone leads to polarized recruitment and anisotropic patterning.

Our 1D setting and analysis translates naturally to 2D scenarios. When recruitment is driven by fluid flow (
\mathsf{V}_{c}\sim\mathsf{R}_{c}
), we expect accumulation along the transverse direction (
x

\mathsf{R}_{\varrho}\gg 1
as shown in Fig.

y

Our study of the spatio-temporal organization of a collective of agents that actively generate and advect pheromones using autonomous flows to recruit additional agents generalizes the classic Patlak-Keller-Segel diffusive aggregation framework and shows the emergence of spatio-temporal patterns of agents that collectively communicate, migrate and aggregate. We delineate this in a phase-space defined by the ratio of recruitment strength to chemotaxis i.e.
\mathsf{R}_{\varrho}/\mathsf{V}_{\varrho}
and the ratio of shear strength to pheromone decay strength i.e.
\mathsf{V}_{c}/\mathsf{R}_{c}
. For large shear strength and recruitment strength (
\mathsf{V}_{c}/\mathsf{R}_{c},\mathsf{R}_{\varrho}/\mathsf{V}_{\varrho}\gg 1
) the agent density exhibits successful polarized recruitment driven by the autogenic fluid flow. On the other hand, when the chemotaxis strength dominates (
\mathsf{V}_{c}/\mathsf{R}_{c},\mathsf{V}_{\varrho}/\mathsf{R}_{\varrho}\gg 1
) the agents are able to collectively migrate along the direction of the fluid flow. When the pheromone decay effects dominate (
\mathsf{R}_{c}/\mathsf{V}_{c},\mathsf{V}_{\varrho}/\mathsf{R}_{\varrho}\gg 1
) agents collectively aggregate which eventually results in the formation of shocks. Lastly, when the chemotactic velocity is weak but the recruitment strength is large (
\mathsf{R}_{c}/\mathsf{V}_{c},\mathsf{R}_{\varrho}/\mathsf{V}_{\varrho}\gg 1
) the agents can perform isotropic recruitment. Generalizing our framework to include the orientation dynamics of agents while accounting for the role of noise in sensing and actuation are natural next steps in characterizing decision making and collective response in active, embodied agents that use rheomergy.

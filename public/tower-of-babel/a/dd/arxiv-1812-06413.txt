Derived equivalences for the flops of type C 2 C_{2} and A 4 G A^{G}_{4} via mutation of semiorthogonal decomposition
Hayato Morimura

Abstract
. We give a new proof of the derived equivalence of a pair of varieties connected by the flop of type C 2 C_{2} in the list of Kanemitsu [ 13 ] , which is originally due to Segal [ 22 ] . We also prove the derived equivalence of a pair of varieties connected by the flop of type A 4 G A^{G}_{4} in the same list. The latter proof follows that of the derived equivalence of Calabi–Yau 3-folds in Grassmannians Gr ​ ( 2 , 5 ) \text{Gr}(2,5) and Gr ​ ( 3 , 5 ) \text{Gr}(3,5) by Kapustka and Rampazzo [ 15 ] closely.

2.
flop of type
C_{2}

Let
P
and
Q
be the parabolic subgroups of
the simple Lie group
G
of type
C_{2}

associated with the crossed Dynkin diagrams

and

.
The corresponding homogeneous spaces are
the projective space

\mathbf{P}=\mathbb{P}(V),

the Lagrangian Grassmannian

\mathbf{Q}=\operatorname{LGr}(V),

and the isotropic flag variety

\mathbf{F}=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{P}}\left(\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{P}}^{\perp}/\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{P}}\right)=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Q}}\left(\mathscr{S}_{\mathbf{Q}}\right).

Here
V
is a
4
-dimensional symplectic vector space,

\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{P}}^{\perp}
is the rank 3 vector bundle
given as the symplectic orthogonal
to the tautological line bundle

\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{P}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(-h)
on
\mathbf{P}
,
and
\mathscr{S}_{\mathbf{Q}}
is the tautological rank 2 bundle on
\mathbf{Q}
.
Note that
\mathbf{Q}
is also a quadric hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
.
Tautological sequences on
\mathbf{Q}=\operatorname{LGr}(V)
and
\mathbf{F}\cong\mathbb{P}_{\mathbf{Q}}\left(\mathscr{S}_{\mathbf{Q}}\right)
give

(2.1)

\displaystyle 0\to\mathscr{S}_{\mathbf{Q}}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{Q}}\otimes V\to\mathscr{S}^{\vee}_{\mathbf{Q}}\to 0

and

(2.2)

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(-h+H)\to\mathscr{S}_{\mathbf{F}}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(h)\to 0,

where

\mathscr{S}_{\mathbf{F}}\coloneqq\varpi_{+}^{*}\mathscr{S}_{\mathbf{Q}}.

We have

\displaystyle(\varpi_{-})_{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(H)\right)\cong\left(\left(\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{P}}^{\perp}/\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{P}}\right)\otimes\mathscr{L}_{\mathbf{P}}\right)^{\vee}

and

\displaystyle(\varpi_{+})_{*}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(h)\right)\cong\mathscr{S}_{\mathbf{Q}}^{\vee},

whose determinants are given by

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(2h)
and

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{Q}}(H)
respectively.
Since

\omega_{\mathbf{P}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(-4h)
,

\omega_{\mathbf{Q}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{Q}}(-3H)
,
and

\omega_{\mathbf{F}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(-2h-2H)
,
we have

\omega_{\mathbf{V}_{-}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}_{-}}
,

\omega_{\mathbf{V}_{+}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}_{+}}
,
and

\omega_{\mathbf{V}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}}(-h-H)
.

Recall from
[
3
]
that

(2.3)

\displaystyle D^{b}(\mathbf{P})=\langle\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(-2h),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(-h),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(h)\rangle,

and from
[
17
]
(cf. also
[
14
]
) that

\displaystyle D^{b}(\mathbf{Q})=\langle\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{Q}}(-H),\mathscr{S}_{\mathbf{Q}}^{\vee}(-H),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{Q}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{Q}}(H)\rangle.

Since
\varphi_{\pm}
are blow-ups along the zero-sections,
it follows from
[
20
]
that

(2.4)

\displaystyle D^{b}(\mathbf{V})=\langle\iota_{*}\varpi_{-}^{*}D^{b}(\mathbf{P}),\Phi_{-}(D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{-}))\rangle

and

(2.5)

\displaystyle D^{b}(\mathbf{V})=\langle\iota_{*}\varpi_{+}^{*}D^{b}(\mathbf{Q}),\Phi_{+}(D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{+}))\rangle,

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{-}\coloneqq\left((-)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}}(H)\right)\circ\varphi_{-}^{*}\colon D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{-})\to D^{b}(\mathbf{V})

and

\displaystyle\Phi_{+}\coloneqq\left((-)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}}(h)\right)\circ\varphi_{+}^{*}\colon D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{+})\to D^{b}(\mathbf{V}).

By abuse of notation,
we use the same symbol for an object of
D^{b}(\mathbf{F})

and its image in
D^{b}(\mathbf{V})
by the push-forward
\iota_{*}
.
(
2.3
) and (
2.4
) give

\displaystyle D^{b}(\mathbf{V})=\langle\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(-2h),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(-h),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(h),\Phi_{-}(D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{-}))\rangle.

Since
\omega_{\mathbf{V}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}}(-h-H)
,
by mutating the first term to the far right,
and then

\Phi_{-}(D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{-}))
one step to the right,
we obtain

\displaystyle D^{b}(\mathbf{V})=\langle\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(-h),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(h),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(-h+H),\Phi_{1}(D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{-}))\rangle,

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}\coloneqq R_{\langle\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(-h+H)\rangle}\circ\Phi_{-}.

In the sequel,
we will use the following fact.

Lemma 2.1
.

Given two vector bundles
\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{F}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{F}}
on
\mathbf{F}
,
if

\mathop{\mathbf{h}}\nolimits\left(\mathcal{E}^{\vee}_{\mathbf{F}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{F}}(-h-H)\right)\simeq 0
,
then we have

\mathop{\mathbf{hom}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}}}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\mathbf{F}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{F}}\right)\simeq\mathop{\mathbf{h}}\nolimits\left(\mathcal{E}^{\vee}_{\mathbf{F}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\mathbf{F}}\right)
.

Proof.

We have

Mukai flop

For
n\geq 2
,
let

and

be the maximal parabolic subgroups
of the simple Lie group of type

associated with the crossed Dynkin diagrams

and

.

\mathbf{Q}=\mathbb{P}V^{\vee},

and the partial flag variety
\mathbf{F}=\operatorname{F}\left(1,n;V\right)
,
where
V
is an
(n+1)
-dimensional vector space.
Since

\omega_{\mathbf{P}}\cong\mathcal{O}(-(n+1)h),

\omega_{\mathbf{Q}}\cong\mathcal{O}(-(n+1)H),

and

\omega_{\mathbf{F}}\cong\mathcal{O}(-nh-nH),

we have

\omega_{\mathbf{V}_{-}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}_{-}},

\omega_{\mathbf{V}_{+}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}_{+}},

and

\omega_{\mathbf{V}}\cong\mathcal{O}(-(n-1)h-(n-1)H).

Lemma 4.1
.

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(-ih+jH)
and

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(-(i+1)h+(j-1)H)

are acyclic for
1\leq j\leq n-1
and
1\leq i\leq n-j
.

Proof.

Since

j-n\leq-i\leq-1

and

j-n-1\leq-i-1\leq-2
,
the derived push-foward of

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(-ih+jH)

and

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(-(i+1)h+(j-1)H)

vanish by
[
9
, Exercise III.8.4]

unless

i=n-1
and
j=1
,
in which case the acyclicity of

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(-nh)

is obvious.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

\mathop{\mathbf{hom}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(ih-jH),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}\right)\simeq 0

for
1\leq j\leq n-1
and
1\leq i\leq n-j
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbf{hom}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(ih-jH),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{h}\left(\left\{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(-ih+jH)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(-(i+1)h+(j-1)H)\right\}\right),

which vanishes
by Lemma
4.1
.

3
]

\displaystyle D^{b}(\mathbf{P})=\langle\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(h),\cdots,\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}}(nh)\rangle

The corresponding homogeneous spaces are the projective spaces

\mathbf{P}=\mathbb{P}V
,

\mathbf{Q}=\mathbb{P}V^{\vee},

and their product
\mathbf{F}=\mathbb{P}V\times\mathbb{P}V^{\vee}
.
Since

\omega_{\mathbf{P}}\cong\mathcal{O}(-(n+1)h)
,

\omega_{\mathbf{Q}}\cong\mathcal{O}(-(n+1)H)
,
and

\omega_{\mathbf{F}}\cong\mathcal{O}(-(n+1)h-(n+1)H)
,
we have

\omega_{\mathbf{V}_{-}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}_{-}}
,

\omega_{\mathbf{V}_{+}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}_{+}}
,
and

\omega_{\mathbf{V}}\cong\mathcal{O}(-nh-nH)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

\mathop{\mathbf{hom}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(ih-jH),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}\right)\simeq 0

for
1\leq j\leq n-1
and
1\leq i\leq n-j
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathbf{hom}}\nolimits_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(ih-jH),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{h}\left(\left\{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(-ih+jH)\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(-(i+1)h+(j-1)H)\right\}\right),

which vanishes
for
1\leq i\leq n-j\leq n-1
.
∎

It follows from
[
20
]
that

(5.1)

\displaystyle D^{b}(\mathbf{V})=\langle\iota_{*}\varpi_{-}^{*}D^{b}(\mathbf{P}),\cdots,\iota_{*}\varpi_{-}^{*}D^{b}(\mathbf{P})\otimes\mathcal{O}((n-1)(h+H)),\Phi_{-}(D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{-}))\rangle

and

(5.2)

\displaystyle D^{b}(\mathbf{V})=\langle\iota_{*}\varpi_{+}^{*}D^{b}(\mathbf{Q}),\cdots,\iota_{*}\varpi_{+}^{*}D^{b}(\mathbf{Q})\otimes\mathcal{O}((n-1)(h+H)),\Phi_{+}(D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{+}))\rangle,

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{-}\coloneqq(-)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}}(n(h+H))\circ\varphi_{-}^{*}\colon D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{-})\to D^{b}(\mathbf{V})

and

\displaystyle\Phi_{+}\coloneqq(-)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}}(n(h+H))\circ\varphi_{+}^{*}\colon D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{+})\to D^{b}(\mathbf{V}).

We write
\mathcal{O}_{i,j}\coloneqq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{F}}(ih+jH)
.
(
4.1
) and (
5.1
) give
a semiorthogonal decomposition of the form

\displaystyle D^{b}(\mathbf{V})=\langle\mathcal{A}_{0},\Phi_{-}(D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{-}))\rangle

where
\mathcal{A}_{0}
is given by

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\mathcal{O}_{0,0}&\mathcal{O}_{1,0}&\cdots&\mathcal{O}_{n-2,0}&\mathcal{O}_{n-1,0}&\mathcal{O}_{n,0}\\
&\mathcal{O}_{1,1}&\cdots&\mathcal{O}_{n-2,1}&\mathcal{O}_{n-1,1}&\mathcal{O}_{n,1}&\mathcal{O}_{n+1,1}\\
&&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
&&&\mathcal{O}_{n-2,n-2}&\mathcal{O}_{n-1,n-2}&\mathcal{O}_{n,n-2}&\mathcal{O}_{n+1,n-2}&\cdots&\mathcal{O}_{2n-2,n-2}\\
&&&&\mathcal{O}_{n-1,n-1}&\mathcal{O}_{n,n-1}&\mathcal{O}_{n+1,n-1}&\cdots&\mathcal{O}_{2n-2,n-1}&\mathcal{O}_{2n-1,n-1}.\end{matrix}

Note from Lemma
5.1
that
there are no morphisms from right to left in (
5.3
).
Since
\omega_{\mathbf{V}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{V}}(-nh-nH)
, by mutating first

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\mathcal{O}_{0,0}&\mathcal{O}_{1,0}&\cdots&\mathcal{O}_{n-2,0}\\
&\mathcal{O}_{1,1}&\cdots&\mathcal{O}_{n-2,1}\\
&&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&&\mathcal{O}_{n-2,n-2}\end{matrix}

to the far right, and then

\Phi_{-}(D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{-}))

to the far right, we obtain

\displaystyle D^{b}(\mathbf{V})=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\Phi_{1}(D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{-}))\rangle,

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{-}))\coloneqq R_{\langle\mathcal{O}_{n,n},\cdots,\mathcal{O}_{2n-2,2n-2}\rangle}\circ\Phi_{-}

and
\mathcal{A}_{1}
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\mathcal{O}_{n-1,0}&\mathcal{O}_{n,0}\\
\mathcal{O}_{n-1,1}&\mathcal{O}_{n,1}&\mathcal{O}_{n+1,1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\mathcal{O}_{n-1,n-1}&\mathcal{O}_{n,n-1}&\mathcal{O}_{n+1,n-1}&\cdots&\mathcal{O}_{2n-2,n-1}&\mathcal{O}_{2n-1,n-1}\\
&\mathcal{O}_{n,n}&\mathcal{O}_{n+1,n}&\cdots&\mathcal{O}_{2n-2,n}\\
&&\mathcal{O}_{n+1,n+1}&\cdots&\mathcal{O}_{2n-2,n+1}\\
&&&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&&&\mathcal{O}_{2n-2,2n-2}.\end{matrix}

By mutating
\Phi_{1}(D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{-}))
one step to the left,
and then
\mathcal{O}_{2n-1,n-1}
to the far left, we obtain

(5.4)

\displaystyle D^{b}(\mathbf{V})=\langle\mathcal{A}_{2},\Phi_{2}(D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{-}))\rangle,

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}(D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{-}))\coloneqq L_{\mathcal{O}_{2n-1,n-1}}\circ\Phi_{1}

and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
is given by

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\mathcal{O}_{n-1,-1}\\
\mathcal{O}_{n-1,0}&\mathcal{O}_{n,0}\\
\mathcal{O}_{n-1,1}&\mathcal{O}_{n,1}&\mathcal{O}_{n+1,1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
\mathcal{O}_{n-1,n-1}&\mathcal{O}_{n,n-1}&\mathcal{O}_{n+1,n-1}&\cdots&\mathcal{O}_{2n-2,n-1}\\
&\mathcal{O}_{n,n}&\mathcal{O}_{n+1,n}&\cdots&\mathcal{O}_{2n-2,n}\\
&&\mathcal{O}_{n+1,n+1}&\cdots&\mathcal{O}_{2n-2,n+1}\\
&&&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&&&\mathcal{O}_{2n-2,2n-2}.\end{matrix}

By comparing (
5.4
)
with (
4.2
) and (
5.2
),
we obtain a derived equivalence

\displaystyle\Phi\coloneqq(\varphi_{+})_{*}\circ((-)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{-(2n-1),0})\circ\Phi_{2}\colon D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{-})\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{b}(\mathbf{V}_{+}).

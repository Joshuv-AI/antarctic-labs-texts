Interplay between the weak-coupling results and the lattice data in dense QCD
Yuki Fujimoto

Abstract
We discuss the interplay between two first-principles calculations of QCD at high density: perturbative results in the weak-coupling regime and the recent lattice-QCD result at finite isospin density. By comparing these two results, we verify empirically that the weak-coupling calculations of the bulk thermodynamics and the gap parameter for Cooper pairing between quarks can be applicable down to the quark chemical potential μ ∼ 1 ​ GeV \mu\sim 1\,\text{GeV} . Having verified the validity of the weak-coupling results in QCD at finite isospin density, we discuss possible effects on QCD at finite baryon density, which is relevant for the application to realistic environments such as neutron stars, by using the fact that QCD at finite baryon and isospin density have the common weak-coupling expansions. First, we show the size of the color-superconducting gap at finite baryon density is as small as a few MeV at μ = 1 ​ GeV \mu=1\,\text{GeV} , which implies that the color-flavor locked phase may be unstable against unpairing up to μ ∼ 1.4 ​ GeV \mu\sim 1.4\,\text{GeV} even in the weak-coupling regime. We also introduce a prescription to reduce the ambiguity arising from the undetermined renormalization scale in the weak-coupling calculation by matching with the lattice-QCD data. We demonstrate the effect of such reduction on neutron-star phenomenology by performing the Bayesian analysis.

II
Weak-coupling results in dense QCD

Here, we summarize weak-coupling formulas obtained in pQCD at high
\mu
.
We work in
N_{c}=3
unless otherwise noted.
We write the vector of chemical potential in the flavor basis as
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu_{u},\mu_{d},\mu_{s})
.
We use the value
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu,-\mu,0)
for QCD
I
to compare with the lattice data, and
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu,\mu,\mu)
for QCD
B
.
The number of flavors in the matter that have nonzero chemical potential is denoted as
N_{f}^{\mathrm{M}}
.
The number of flavors in the vacuum is
N_{f}^{\mathrm{V}}=3

The perturbative expansion of pressure

\displaystyle\quad+18-\frac{22}{9}N_{f}^{\mathrm{V}}+0.22429N_{f}^{\mathrm{M}}\Big]\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\right)^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})\,,

(1)

The pressure of the ideal quark gas

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{\Delta}{\mu}\right)=

\displaystyle-\frac{\sqrt{3}\pi}{2\sqrt{c_{R}}}{\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\right)}^{\!\!-\frac{1}{2}}\!\!\!-\frac{5}{2}\ln\bigg[N_{f}^{\mathrm{M}}\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi}\bigg]

\displaystyle\quad+\ln\frac{2^{\frac{13}{2}}}{\pi}-\frac{\pi^{2}+4}{12c_{R}}-\zeta+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{\frac{1}{2}})\,,

where

The color factor is

As is classified in Appendix

For the running coupling constant

90

Below, we do not consider the uncertainty of

.

The important property we exploit in this work is that these weak-coupling formulas are universal for QCD
B
and QCD
I
.
It is shown that the expression of

is the same for QCD

and QCD

Below, we use the LQCD

IV
New prescription for setting
\bar{\Lambda}

The only ambiguity in the weak-coupling formulas (
1
-
3
) is in the choice of
\bar{\Lambda}
.
As explained above, this uncertainty is conventionally estimated by varying
\bar{\Lambda}
around a central value
2\mu
by a factor of two.
The range and the central value are chosen based on historical practice, so this ad-hoc scale-variation procedure may not quantify the actual size of the error.
Recently, several authors have attempted to properly characterize the missing higher-order uncertainty in perturbative calculations and distinguish it from the ad-hoc scale variation error using statistical methods
[
98
,
99
,
100
,
101
,
102
,
37
]
(see also Ref.
[
103
]
and reference therein).

Here, we discuss an alternative prescription for the choice of
\bar{\Lambda}
using the non-perturbative physical quantities obtained from the LQCD
I
computation.
Since a perturbative series is at best asymptotic series, we can view it as a workable approximation of the physical quantities nonperturbatively defined on the lattice.
Then, we determine the range of
X=\bar{\Lambda}/(2\mu)
so that the weak-coupling results
P_{\rm pQCD}+\delta P
(
1
,
3
) is consistent with the uncertainty band of the LQCD
I
computation.
As shown in Fig.
1
, the majority of the blue band with the conventional scale setting
X\in[0.5,2]
is inconsistent with the LQCD data.
By performing a nonlinear least-squares fit of
P_{\rm pQCD}+\delta P
to the upper bound of the LQCD data, we exclude the inconsistent part of the blue band and obtain the optimal range
X\in[1.22,2]
.
The fit is performed in the intermediate window
\mu\in[1.0,1.4]\,\text{GeV}
since the weak-coupling and the LQCD
I
results become more unreliable as
\mu
become smaller and larger, respectively.
We note that we keep the upper bound of
X
the same since it is unlikely on the physical ground that the interaction scale is even larger than
4\mu
.

Because
\alpha_{s}
is the same for QCD
I
and QCD
B
, the range of
X
obtained here applies to both theories.
One can use the same lattice-informed value of
X
in QCD
B
as long as the formulas (
1
-
3
) at this specific order of expansion are concerned, and thus the uncertainty of
P_{\rm pQCD}
,
\Delta
, and
\delta P
in QCD
B
shrinks.

In Fig.

While

This can be understood from the difference in the strength of the one-gluon exchange attraction

The size of the CFL gap in the weak-coupling regime at

This is consistent with the bound

The contribution of
\delta P
from the weak-coupling

to the bulk EoS is suppressed by the factor
\delta P/P_{\mathrm{id}}\sim(\Delta_{\rm CFL}/\mu)^{2}
relative to

(

it is less than
\sim 0.01\%

\mu=0.8\,\text{GeV}
.
Therefore, in the weak-coupling regime, the color-superconductivity effect on the bulk EoS is totally negligible.
This justifies the pQCD input used in the NS phenomenology, in which the contribution of the color-superconducting gap is usually neglected.

The weak-coupling calculation of
\Delta_{\rm CFL}
may also modify the QCD phase diagram in the high-
\mu

In strange quark matter, the strange quark mass
m_{s}
stresses the CFL pairing by splitting the Fermi momenta of different quark flavors apart
[
111
,
112
]
.
The CFL vacuum remains favored over the unpaired vacuum if
\Delta_{\rm CFL}
is larger than the stress induced by

, i.e.,
\Delta_{\rm CFL}\gtrsim m_{s}^{2}/(4\mu)

[
113
,
114
]
.
At
\mu=0.8\,\text{GeV}
, the condition
\Delta_{\rm CFL}\gtrsim m_{s}^{2}/(4\mu)
is not satisfied for the weak-coupling
\Delta_{\rm CFL}
.
The size of the CFL gap becomes comparable with the stress
m_{s}^{2}/(4\mu)
only at
\mu\gtrsim 1.4
–
2.4\,\text{GeV}
, where the range corresponds to the range of

We took into account the running of

m_{s}(2\,\text{GeV})=93.4\,\text{MeV}

[

]
.
This suggests that even in the weak-coupling regime, the CFL phase may not be the ground state below
\mu_{B}\sim 4\mbox{--}7\,\text{GeV}
.

We stress that this does not exclude the possibility of large
\Delta
and the existence of the CFL phase in the core of NSs, inside which
\mu_{B}
ranges only up to
\mu_{B}\simeq 1.7\,\text{GeV}

This was indeed the original intention of the color superconductivity, namely, a small gap in the weakly-coupled regime is enhanced by instanton-induced interactions
[
17
,
18

See also Ref.
[

for the instanton contribution in the dilute gas approximation.
Because the NS mass-radius constraint can only be sensitive to the large value of
\Delta
, whether
\Delta
is large or not inside NSs has to be verified by other means more sensitive to
\Delta
, e.g., the transport properties.

VI
Impact on EoS of NS matter

Figure 3:
The posterior distribution of the Bayesian inference of NS EoS incorporating the weak-coupling inputs, i.e., requiring every EoS to connect to the weak-coupling values at
\mu_{B}=\mu_{\rm pQCD}
.
The blue and orange lines correspond to the conventional and LQCD-improved weak-coupling inputs, respectively.

The fitting prescription for
X
above shrinks the relative range of
P/P_{\rm id}
from
\pm 31\%
(blue shaded band in Fig.

) to
\pm 4.2\%
(orange shaded band in Fig.

Also, from the discussion above, we slightly extend the applicable limit of the pQCD input down to
\mu_{B}=2.4\,\text{GeV}
from the customary choice
\mu_{B}=2.6\,\text{GeV}
.
Here, we discuss its impact on the NS EoS.
To this end, we perform the Bayesian inference incorporating the weak-coupling QCD inputs.
As discussed above, we can safely neglect the contribution of
\delta P
.
We follow the constant-likelihood approach developed in Refs.
[

,

]

In Fig.
3

The conventional
X
is marked by thin blue curves and the fitted
X
is marked by thick orange curves.
One can see that the effect of the reduction in

already appears around
\mu_{\rm c,\max}

at the center of the maximum-mass NSs.
Limiting the range of
X
excludes the region of the EoS with smaller values of
P/P_{\rm id}
around
\mu_{\rm c,\max}
; this implies that the softer EoS is favored over the stiffer EoS at higher density.

To quantitatively discuss the stiffness of the EoS, we consider the behavior of the trace anomaly
\Theta\equiv\varepsilon-3P

[
12
]
.
When
\Theta
is positive, the softer EoS is favored at high density.
One can compute the Bayes factor
\mathcal{B}_{H_{0}}^{H_{1}}
, which quantifies the extent to which the observed data
D
support some given hypothesis
H_{1}
over a hypothesis
H_{0}
:

\mathcal{B}_{H_{0}}^{H_{1}}=\frac{\Pr(D|H_{1})}{\Pr(D|H_{0})}=\frac{\Pr(H_{1}|D)/\Pr(H_{0}|D)}{\Pr(H_{1})/\Pr(H_{0})}\,.

(4)

For the conventional
X
,
\mathcal{B}^{\Theta\geq 0}_{\Theta<0}=3.53
, and for the fitted
X
, it is increased to
20.3
; it gives strong evidence for the EoS to have the positive
\Theta
at high density
[
117
]
.
This strongly implies the EoS in the NS core is soft.

We note that what we have shown here is the most optimistic scenario in which the pQCD input affects the inferred result as large as possible
[
118
]
.
This treatment suffices here as the purpose of this Bayesian analysis is to show the maximum impact of the new prescription for fixing
\bar{\Lambda}
.

VII
Conclusions and outlook

We considered synergy between first-principles calculations of QCD, namely, perturbation theory and lattice simulation.
LQCD information can be synthesized into the weak-coupling calculation by tightening the range of renormalization scale
\bar{\Lambda}
; this can further constrain the NS EoS.
We also point out that the CFL phase may not be the ground state up to
\mu_{B}\sim 4\,\text{GeV}
in the weak-coupling regime.
Perturbation theory will allow a precise determination of the QCD phase diagram down to
\mu_{B}\sim 2.4\,\text{GeV}
.

Appendix A
Classification of various corrections to the weak-coupling formula

Here, we argue that
\delta P
is the only large correction in the weak-coupling regime of QCD
I
.
One can classify possible corrections to
P_{\rm pQCD}\sim\mu^{4}
by expanding
P
in terms of
\mu

[
6
]

P=P_{\rm pQCD}+a_{2}\mu^{2}+a_{0}\,,

(5)

where the odd powers of
\mu
are absent because of
C
-symmetry in QCD.
The coefficients
a_{2}
and
a_{0}
are treated approximately as constants since their
\mu
-dependences are weak.
Aside from
\delta P
, the corrections of the quadratic order are a current mass of flavor-
f
quarks
a_{2}\propto m_{f}^{2}
, and the temperature correction
a_{2}\propto T^{2}
as the lattice data is measured at
T\sim 20\,\text{MeV}
.
Both of these corrections are less than
1\%
relative to
P_{\rm pQCD}
at
\mu=0.8\,\text{GeV}
.
The constant correction term
a_{0}
may originate from the bag constant
a_{0}=-B
and instantons
[
125
,
*Shuryak:1978ew
,
*Shuryak:1982hk
,
128
,
129
,
*Abrikosov:1981qb
,
*Abrikosov:1982mi
,
*Abrikosov:1983hxi
,
133
,
134
,
135
]
.
The bag constant of a typical size
B^{1/4}\sim 150
–
200\,\text{MeV}
amounts to
\sim 2
–7% correction at
\mu=0.8~\text{GeV}
and its contribution decreases by a factor
\mu^{-4}
with increasing
\mu
.
The instanton contribution is small as it is suppressed by quark masses
\propto\prod_{f}(m_{f}/\Lambda_{\overline{\rm MS}})
.
From the above consideration, we conclude that
\delta P
is the only largest correction to
P_{\rm pQCD}
.

Appendix B
Details of the Bayesian analysis

Here, we outline the method of our Bayesian analysis.
We follow the constant-likelihood approach developed in Refs.
[
31
,
35
]
, which can be regarded as a Bayesian extension of Ref.
[
11
]
.

For the prior distribution, we randomly sample EoSs by modeling them with the piecewise sound-speed parametrization introduced in Ref.
[
11
]
.
We use the crust EoS at low
\mu_{B}

[
136
]
, and use the EoS from the
\chi
EFT
[
29
]
up to
\mu_{B}=\mu_{0}
, where
\mu_{0}\simeq 1.0\,\text{GeV}
.
The density and pressure at
\mu_{0}
are
n_{0}\simeq 0.24\,\text{fm}^{-3}
and
P_{0}\simeq 8.8_{-1.5}^{+1.4}\,\text{MeV}/\text{fm}^{3}
, respectively.
The error corresponds to
\pm 1\sigma
credible region presented in Ref.
[
29
]
, and we sample equal numbers of the EoS at average and at
\pm 1\sigma
credible interval (CI) in the prior distribution.
We randomly sample parameters
\mu_{i}
and
v_{s,i}^{2}
(
i=1,\ldots,N
) from the uniform distributions
U(\mu_{0},\mu_{\rm pQCD})
and
U(0,1)
, respectively.
We choose the number of segments as
N=5
, and
\mu_{\mathrm{pQCD}}=2.6\,\text{GeV}
for the conventional X range
[0.5,2]
and
\mu_{\mathrm{pQCD}}=2.4\,\text{GeV}
for the fitted X range
[1.2,2]
.
Then, we linearly interpolate between each segment boundary
(\mu_{i},v_{s,i}^{2})
, and from this linearly-interpolated
v_{s}^{2}(\mu)
, one can construct the complete thermodynamic function
P(\mu)
through the integration:

P(\mu)=P_{0}+n_{0}\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu}d\mu^{\prime}\exp\left[\int_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu^{\prime}}\frac{d\mu^{\prime\prime}}{\mu^{\prime\prime}v_{s}^{2}(\mu^{\prime\prime})}\right]\,.

(6)

For the Bayesian update step, we incorporate the pQCD input as a likelihood, i.e., we require an EoS to be
P_{\min}<P(\mu_{\rm pQCD})<P_{\max}
, where
P_{\min}
and
P_{\max}
are
P_{\rm pQCD}
evaluated for the minimum and maximum
X
, respectively.
We also use the observations of
2M_{\odot}
radio pulsars
[
137
,
138
,
139
]
,
R
measurements from NICER
[
140
,
141
,
142
,
143
]
, and the binary tidal deformability
\tilde{\Lambda}
measurements from the GW170817 event
[
144
,
145
]
.
Namely, following the procedure in Refs.
[
31
,
35
]
, we impose the following conditions on an EoS:

M_{\rm max}>2~M_{\odot}
,

R(M=2.0~M_{\odot})>10.75~\text{km}
,

R(M=1.1~M_{\odot})>10.80~\text{km}
, and
\tilde{\Lambda}<720
at a chirp mass
\mathcal{M}_{\rm chirp}=(M_{1}M_{2})^{3/5}(M_{1}+M_{2})^{-1/5}=1.186M_{\odot}
for any choice of mass in the binary
M_{1,2}
with a mass ratio
M_{2}/M_{1}>0.73

The probability density shown in Fig.
3

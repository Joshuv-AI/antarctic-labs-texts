Limiting Behaviour of Poisson-Dirichlet and Generalised Poisson-Dirichlet Distributions
Ross Maller a and Soudabeh Shemehsavar c

Abstract
We derive large-sample and other limiting distributions of the “frequency of frequencies” vector, 𝐌 n {\bf M}_{n} , together with the number of species, K n K_{n} , in a Poisson-Dirichlet or generalised Poisson-Dirichlet gene or species sampling model. Models analysed include those constructed from gamma and α \alpha -stable subordinators by Kingman, the two parameter extension by Pitman and Yor, and another two parameter version constructed by omitting large jumps from an α \alpha -stable subordinator. In the Poisson-Dirichlet case 𝐌 n {\bf M}_{n} and K n K_{n} turn out to be asymptotically independent, and notable, especially for statistical applications, is that in other cases the conditional limiting distribution of 𝐌 n {\bf M}_{n} , given K n K_{n} , is normal, after certain centering and norming.

Introduction

Kingman 1975
suggested a way of constructing random distributions on the infinite unit simplex
by ranking the jumps of a subordinator up to a specified
time,
then normalising them by the value of the subordinator at that time.
Taking the subordinator to be a driftless gamma subordinator
generates his well known Poisson-Dirichlet distribution
\textbf{\rm PD}(\theta)
,
for
\theta>0
,
which was later shown to be intimately connected with the famous Ewens sampling formula in genetics.
Another of Kingman’s distributions, denoted by
\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}
, arises when a driftless
\alpha
-stable subordinator with parameter
\alpha\in(0,1)
is used instead of the gamma subordinator. Later again, an encompassing two parameter Poisson-Dirichlet distribution
\textbf{\rm PD}(\alpha,\theta)
was constructed by
Pitman & Yor 1997
.

These distributions and the methodologies associated with them have subsequently had a big impact in many applications areas, especially in population genetics, but also in the excursion theory of stochastic processes, the theory of random partitions, random graphs and networks, probabilistic number theory,
machine learning, Bayesian statistics, and a variety of others.
They have also given rise to a number of generalisations and a large literature analysing the various versions.
Among these, generalising Kingman’s
\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}
class,
is the two parameter
\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}^{(r)}
class of

Ipsen & Maller 2017
, defined for
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
r>0
.

We consider a sample of size
n\in\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
from
\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}
,
\textbf{\rm PD}(\theta)
,
\textbf{\rm PD}(\alpha,\theta)

or
\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}^{(r)}
,
and form the “frequency of frequencies” random vector,
{\bf M}_{n}
, together with the number of species, random variable
K_{n}
.
Thus we have
{\bf M}_{n}=(M_{1n},M_{2n},\ldots,M_{nn})
, where
M_{jn}
is the number of alleles or species represented
j
times in the sample, and

K_{n}=M_{1n}+M_{2n}+\cdots+M_{nn}
is the total number of alleles or species in the sample.
Specialising our general notation, when the model under consideration depends on parameters, these are made explicit in the particular

{\bf M}_{n}
and
K_{n}
analysed; thus, from
\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}
we have
({\bf M}_{n}(\alpha),K_{n}(\alpha))
,
from
\textbf{\rm PD}(\theta)
we have
({\bf M}_{n}(\theta),K_{n}(\theta))
, etc.

The paper is organised as follows. We start in the next section with

\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}^{(r)}
, finding the limiting distribution of

({\bf M}_{n}(\alpha,r),\,K_{n}(\alpha,r))

as
n\to\infty
. In Section
3
we apply the methods thus developed to

\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}
, while Section
4
has
\textbf{\rm PD}(\theta)
and
\textbf{\rm PD}(\alpha,\theta)
.
Section
5
gives other related limiting results;
for example, the limit as
r\downarrow 0
of

\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}^{(r)}
is
\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}
.
Special cases of some of the results are known of course but for each we add some extra information.
In particular, we obtain the joint limiting distribution of the appropriate
({\bf M}_{n},K_{n})
, in each case.
For
\textbf{\rm PD}(\theta)
, it turns out that

{\bf M}_{n}(\theta)
and
K_{n}(\theta)
are asymptotically independent, while for
\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}^{(r)}
,
\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}
and
\textbf{\rm PD}(\alpha,\theta)
,
the conditional limiting distribution of
{\bf M}_{n}
given
K_{n}
is normal, after certain centering and norming –
a useful fact for statistical applications.
Further discussion of these results and more references are in the concluding Section
6
.

Kingman’s

6
Discussion

The methods developed in Sections
2
–
5
offer a unified approach with nice interpretations to limit theorems of various sorts for Poisson-Dirichlet models, and we expect
can be applied in other situations too.
For example,
Grote & Speed 2002
analyse a “general selection model” based on the infinite alleles mutation scheme.
Their formula (1.13)
gives a version of the distribution of
({\bf M}_{n},K_{n})
amenable to analysis by our methods.

Ruggiero, Walker & Favaro 2013
(see their Eqs. (3.1), (3.2))
analyse a species sampling model based on normalized inverse-Gaussian diffusions as a generalisation of
\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}
; an extra parameter
\beta
is introduced to the
\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}
model, somehat analogous to our
\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}^{(r)}
.
See also
Lijoi, Mena & Prunster2005
and
Favaro & Feng 2014
.

Formula (
2.1
) can be compared with an analogous formula, Eq. (4.14), p.81, of
Pitman 2006
, in which the component

\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Psi^{(n_{i})}(\lambda)

can be replaced by
\prod_{j=1}^{n}\big(\Psi^{(j)}(\lambda)\big)^{m_{j}}
using
the identity

m_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}1_{\{j=n_{i}\}}
.
See also
Pitman 1997

and the Gibbs partitions in
Pitman 2006
, pp.25-26, Eq. (1.52).

Hansen 1994
gave a general treatment of decomposable combinatorial structures having a Poisson-Dirichlet limit.

There are many other limiting results in the literature.

Joyce, Krone, & Kurtz2002
prove a variety of
Gaussian limit theorems
as the mutation rate
\theta\to\infty
in the
\textbf{\rm PD}(\theta)
model;
see also
Griffiths 1979
,
Handa 2009
.

Feng 2007
;
Feng 2010
gives large deviation results as
\theta\to\infty
.

James2008
gives results like this related to
\textbf{\rm PD}(\alpha,\theta)
in a Bayesian setup.

Dolera & Favaro2020
investigate
\alpha
-diversity in
\textbf{\rm PD}(\alpha,\theta)
.
When
r\to\infty
, size-biased samples from

\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}^{(r)}

tend to those of
\textbf{\rm PD}(1-\alpha)
, i.e., to
\textbf{\rm PD}(\theta)
with
\theta=1-\alpha
.
Together with Part (i) of Theorem
5.1
this suggests that
\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}^{(r)}
is intermediate in a sense between
\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}
and
\textbf{\rm PD}(\theta)
.

Chegini & Zarepour 2023
show that
\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}^{(r)}
can alternatively be obtained as the ordered normalised jumps of
a negative binomial process (
Gregoire 1984
).
When
r\to\infty
,

\alpha\to 0
such that
r\alpha\to a>0
, a limit involving the Dickman subordinator
is obtained from
\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}^{(r)}

(
Maller & Shemehsavar 2023
).

The appearance of the normal as the conditional limiting distribution of
{\bf M}_{n}
given
K_{n}
, after centering and norming, for

\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}^{(r)}
,
\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}
and
\textbf{\rm PD}(\alpha,\theta)
,
is useful for statistical applications.
In
Ipsen, Shemehsavar & Maller 2018
we used a least squares method to fit

\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}^{(r)}
to a variety
of data sets, some quite small. Even so we observed by simulations that the finite sample distributions of the estimates of
\alpha
and
r
were quite closely approximated by the normal.
The asymptotic normality derived in the present paper provides a rationale for using least squares and helps explain the approximate normality of
the parameter estimates. Similar ideas may be useful for

\textbf{\rm PD}_{\alpha}
and
\textbf{\rm PD}(\alpha,\theta)
.
Functionals of
{\bf M}_{n}

such as
\sum_{j=1}^{n}(M_{jn}-EM_{jn})^{2}/EM_{jn}

are important in many aspects of population genetics; see for example the discussion of measures of sample diversity in Section 4 of

Watterson 1977
.

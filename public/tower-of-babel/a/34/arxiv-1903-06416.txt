Characteristic (Fedosov-)class of a twist constructed by Drinfel’d
Jonas Schnitzer

Abstract
In a seminal paper Drinfel’d explained how to associate to every classical r-matrix for a Lie algebra 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} a twisting element based on 𝒰 ​ ( 𝔤 ) ​ [ [ ℏ ] ] \mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})[[\hbar]] , or equivalently a left invariant star product of the corresponding symplectic structure ω \omega on the 1-connected Lie group G of g. In a recent paper, the authors solve the same problem by means of Fedosov quantization. In this short note we provide a connection between the two constructions by computing the characteristic (Fedosov) class of the twist constructed by Drinfel’d and proving that it is the trivial class given by [ ω ] ℏ \frac{[\omega]}{\hbar} .



Introduction

The notion of Drinfel’d twists goes back to the 80’s in the context of
Quantum Groups
, see e.g. the textbooks

[
7
]
,
[
15
]
and
[
3
]
.
It is still an active field of research, since it is a powerful (functorial) tool used in Quantum field theory to quantize
classical field
theories, see e.g.
[
18
]
, and references therein. This application is due to the fact that a Drinfel’d twist together
with a suitable Hopf algebra action always induces a deformation quantization. To be more precise, if a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
acts on an associative
\mathscr{A}
by derivations via
\phi\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathscr{A}
,
then a
formal Drinfel’d twist

\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\mathbin{\otimes}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})[[\hbar]]
induces a deformation of the associative product
\mu\colon\mathscr{A}\times\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}
by

\displaystyle a\star_{\mathcal{F}}b=\mu(\Phi\mathbin{\otimes}\Phi(F)(a\mathbin{\otimes}b))

for
a,b\in\mathscr{A}[[\hbar]]
and the extended action
\Phi\colon\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\to\Diffop(\mathcal{A})
. The properties of the twist (see Definition
1
) ensure that this product is associative again and that it is a formal deformation in the sense of Gerstenhaber
[
9
]
.

The existence of a Drinfel’d twist was first shown by
Drinfel’d himself in
[
6
]
using the fact that a Drinfel’d twist is equivalent to a left-invariant star product and
the construction of the canonical star product on the dual of a Lie algebra,
which is nowadays called Gutt-star product, since in
[
12
]
it is given a construction of it.
There are just very few explicit examples known, see e.g.
[
10
]
, besides the
Weyl-Moyal twist
,
even though they are
completely classified via there infinitesimal counter parts, the
r
-matrices, in
[
14
]
via Formality.
This approach even provides
a full classifiaction of Drinfel’d twists in terms of their infintesimal counterparts, the so-called
r-matrices
.
Recall that two twists
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\mathbin{\otimes}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})[[\hbar]]
are said to be
equivalent, if there exists a
S\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})[[\hbar]]
with
S=\id+\mathcal{O}(\hbar)
and
\varepsilon(S)=1
,
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\Delta(S)^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{F}\cdot(S\mathbin{\otimes}S),

which induces an equivalence, i.e. an ismorphism

, of the induced deformations
\star_{\mathcal{F}}
and
\star_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}
(see
[

In
[

, a construction of
Drinfel’d twists and their classification

]
, for symplectic Lie algebras.
In fact, if the given

[

Additionally, the (invariant) classification result boils down to the one obtained in

\displaystyle\frac{[\omega]}{\hbar}+H^{2}_{\text{dR}}(G)^{G}[[\hbar]],

where the reference point
\omega

Moreover,
H_{\text{dR}}(G)^{G}
is the cohomology of the left-invariant differential forms.
The purpose of this note is now straight forward: computing the characteristic class in the sense of
[
2
]
of the
Drinfel’d twist obtained in

First, we recall the original construction of Drinfel’d and of the Gutt-star product and use the equivalence of Drinfel’d

\displaystyle\text{ Drinfel'd's Twist }\sim\text{ invariant Kontsevich star product }\sim\text{ invariant Fedosov star product },

the class of the

1
The Drinfel’d twist construction and the Gutt star product

Drinfel’d twists can be defined on arbitrary Hopf algebras (in fact even just bialgebras) (see e.g.
[
11
]
), but throughout this note we use the following

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra. A (formal) Drinfel’d twist is an element

\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\mathbin{\otimes}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})[[\hbar]]
, such that

i.)

(\Delta\mathbin{\otimes}\id)(\mathcal{F})\cdot(\mathcal{F}\mathbin{\otimes}1)=(\id\mathbin{\otimes}\Delta)(\mathcal{F})\cdot(1\mathbin{\otimes}\mathcal{F})

ii.)

(\varepsilon\mathbin{\otimes}\id)(F)=(\id\mathbin{\otimes}\varepsilon)(\mathcal{F})=1

iii.)

\mathcal{F}=1\mathbin{\otimes}1+\mathcal{O}(\hbar^{2})

for the usual Hopf algebra structures
(\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g}),\cdot,i,\Delta,\varepsilon,S)

A Drinfel’d twist has a classical limit, which can be seen as the equivalent to Poisson structures in deformation
quantization.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and let
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\mathbin{\otimes}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})[[\hbar]]
be a
Drinfel’d twist, then

\displaystyle r:=F_{1}-F_{1}^{\mathrm{opp}}\in\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\mathbin{\otimes}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})

is a
r
-matrix, i.e.
[\![r,r]\!]=0
.

Let us now state the theorem of Drinfel’d connecting
r
-matrices with Drinfel’d twists.

Theorem 1.3
(Drinfel’d
[
6
]
).

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a real finite dimensional Lie algebra and let
r
be a
r
-matrix. Then there exists a Drinfel’d twist

\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\mathbin{\otimes}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})[[\hbar]]
, such that

\displaystyle r=F_{1}-F_{1}^{\mathrm{opp}}.

Note that Drinfel’d twists have a deep connection to deformation quantization, i.e. they induce a deformation quantization on
any algebra the underlying Lie algebra acts on.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra acting as derivations on an algebra
\mathscr{A}
by
\phi\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\operatorname{\mathsf{Der}}(\mathscr{A})

and let
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\mathbin{\otimes}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})[[\hbar]]
be a Drinfel’d twist. Then the extended
action induced by
\phi
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Diffop(\mathcal{A})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}

induces a formal deformation of the product of
\mathscr{A}
by

\displaystyle a\star_{\mathcal{F}}b:=\mu_{\mathscr{A}}(\Phi\mathbin{\otimes}\Phi(\mathcal{F})(a\mathbin{\otimes}b))

for
a,b\in\mathscr{A}[[\hbar]]
.

From now on, we focus on real Lie algebras where we can use the following

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a real Lie algebra, let
G
be a Lie group with
\mathsf{Lie}(G)=\mathfrak{g}
, then the following are equivalent:

i.)

a Drinfel’d twist
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})\mathbin{\otimes}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{g})[[\hbar]]
with corresponding

r
-matrix
r

ii.)

a left invariant star product
\star\colon\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)[[\hbar]]^{\mathbin{\otimes}2}\to\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(G)[[\hbar]]

with left-invariant Poisson structure
\pi\in\Gamma^{\infty}(\Lambda^{2}TG)
, such that
\pi(e)=r
.

Moreover, two Drinfel’d twists are equivalent if and only if the left-invariant star products induced by them are invariantly
equivalent.

This allows us to forget about twists for the moment and take care about left-invariant star products on Lie groups and their
equivalences. In the
following we recall the construction of Drinfel’d
[
6
]
, which is based on the fact that there is a canonical star
product on the dual of a Lie algebra from
[
12
]
, which we in return also recall briefly.
Let therefore
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])

be a finite dimensional real
Lie algebra, then there is a star product on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
induced by the Poincaré-Birkoff-Witt isomorphism

\displaystyle P_{\hbar}\colon\mathrm{S}^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}[[\hbar]]\to\mathcal{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g}),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})=\frac{T^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})[[\hbar]]}{\langle x\mathbin{\otimes}y-y\mathbin{\otimes}x-\hbar[x,y]\rangle}.

and the Poincaré-Birkoff-Witt isomorphism is given by

\displaystyle P_{\hbar}(X_{1}\vee\dots\vee X_{k})=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}X_{\sigma(1)}\bullet_{\hbar}\dots\bullet_{\hbar}X_{\sigma(k)}.

Here
\bullet_{\hbar}
denotes the associative product in
\mathcal{U}_{\hbar}(\mathfrak{g})
.
We interpret
\mathrm{S}^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}
as the polynomials on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and define a product

\displaystyle f\star_{G}g=P_{\hbar}^{-1}(P_{\hbar}(f)\bullet_{\hbar}P_{\hbar}(g)).

It is easy to see that since the
product is defined by differential operators, it can be extended to
\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
and that this product is

\Ad
-invariant with respect to the 1-connected Lie group
G
integrating
\mathfrak{g}
. We refer to this star product as the
Gutt-star product
. Note that the semi-classical limit of this star product is the canonical (linear) Poisson structure on the dual of the Lie algebra.

Let us now assume symplectic Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-],\omega)
, i.e. a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[-,-])
together with a non-
degenerate 2-form
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, such that
\delta_{\text{\tiny{CE}}}\omega=0
. We consider the central extension adapted to

\omega
which is given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathbb{R}\text{ with }[(X,x),(Y,y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}=([X,Y],-\omega(X,Y))

and the canonical projection
p\colon\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
, which is a lie algebra map.

A short computation shows that that the Jacobi identity of
[-,-]_{\mathfrak{h}}
is equivalent to
\delta_{\text{\tiny{CE}}}\omega=0
.
Note that in
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, we have the distinguished element

\displaystyle C\colon\mathfrak{h}\ni(X,x)\to x\in\mathbb{R},

where we one can show that
\delta_{\text{\tiny{CE}}}C=-p^{*}\omega
. Note that
(\mathfrak{h},C)
is a contact Lie algebra and is the
contactification
of the symplectic Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
, see
[
16
]
.
Let us prove the following

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
G
be a Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Then

\displaystyle\dim(\Ad_{G}^{*}(\alpha))=\rank(\delta_{\text{\tiny{CE}}}\alpha^{\sharp}).

Proof
:

It is enough to prove that the tangent space of the coadjoint orbit at
\alpha
has dimension
\rank(\delta_{\text{\tiny{CE}}}\alpha^{\sharp})
.
But since it is an orbit of a Lie group action it is spanned by the fundamental vector fields. But they are given by

\displaystyle X_{\mathfrak{g}}(\alpha)=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\Ad^{*}_{\exp(tX)}\alpha=\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\exp(t\ad^{*}_{X})\alpha.

which is
0
if and only if
\ad_{X}^{*}\alpha=0
. Moreover, we can write
\ad_{X}^{*}\alpha=-\delta_{\text{\tiny{CE}}}\alpha^{\sharp}(X)
and the claim is
proven.

\Box

We have additionally a Lie algebra action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
by

\displaystyle\mathbin{\triangleright}\colon\mathfrak{g}\ni X\to(X,0)_{\mathfrak{h}^{*}}\in\Gamma^{\infty}(T\mathfrak{h}^{*})

where
(X,0)_{\mathfrak{h}^{*}}
is the fundamental vector field of the coadjoint representation of
H
(the 1-connected Lie group

\mathfrak{h}
). It is easy to check that

of

by the Theorem
of Palais. Note that, we can lift every map in order to obtain the following commutative diagram

The orbit
\Phi_{G}(C)
for the previously defined

Using Lemma

Remark 1.8
.

The previous Lemma shows that
\Phi_{C}\colon G\ni g\mapsto\Phi_{g}(C)\in\Phi_{G}(C)
is a local diffeomorphism.
Moreover, we have that
\Phi_{C}\circ\ell_{g}=\Phi_{g}\circ\Phi_{C}
. Using the fact that
\Phi_{G}(C)
is a coadjoint orbit of the

and hence

is a left-invariant symplectic structure on

. A tiny

\Phi_{C}^{*}\Theta(e)=\omega
and hence

Let us now apply these considerations to the construction of the Gutt-star product. First of all we have

Proposition 1.9
.

The Gutt-star product is invariant with respect to the action
\Phi\colon G\times\mathfrak{h}^{*}\to\mathfrak{h}^{*}
.

Moreover, we have

Lemma 1.10
.

The Gutt-star product of
\mathfrak{h}
is tangential to
\Phi_{G}(C)
.

Proof
:

We have to check that for functions
f,g\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}(\mathfrak{h}^{*})
, such that
f\big|_{\Phi_{G}(C)}=0
we have

f\star_{G}g\big|_{\Phi_{G}(C)}=0
. But we actually show a bit more: we want to show that the Gutt-star product is tangential to
the submanifold

\displaystyle D=\{\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\ |\ \alpha((0,1))-1=0\}\supseteq_{\mathrm{open}}\Phi_{G}(C),

where the inclusion of
\Phi_{G}(C)
in

follows from the fact that
\Ad_{h}^{*}C(0,1)=C(\Ad_{h}(0,1))=C(0,1)=1
and the fact that it is open follows from dimensional reasons (see Lemma

).
Note that this is the zero set of the polynomial
p=(0,1)-1\in\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathbb{R}\in\mathrm{S}^{\bullet}\mathfrak{h}
and hence every

vanishing on

is given by

. Moreover, by the definition of the Gutt-star
product we have that

\displaystyle Y\star_{G}f=Y\cdot f

for a central element

seen as a polynomial. Hence, we have

\displaystyle(pf)\star_{G}g=((0,1)f)\star_{G}g-f\star_{G}g=p(f\star_{G}g)

and hence
(pf)\star_{G}g\big|_{D}=0
, since

is central. And the claim follows.

\Box

2
Kontsevich star product on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}

The dual of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
can be alternatively seen as a Poisson manifold and apply the the Kontsevich
map to obtain a star product. More, precisely we apply the Kontsevich map to
\hbar\pi
, seen as a Maurer-Cartan element,
which is invariant with respect to the adjoint representation of
H
to obtain a
H
-invariant star product
\star_{K}
. In

[
4
]
it was shown that the two star product are invariantly equivalent, i.e. there is a series of invariant
differential operators
S=\id+\mathcal{O}(\hbar)\in\Diffop(\mathfrak{h}^{*})^{H}
, such that

\displaystyle S(f\star_{G}h)=(Sf)\star_{K}(Sh),

which basically follows by the universal property of the universal enveloping algebra.
Moreover, we have

Lemma 2.1
.

The Kontsevich star product obtained on the dual Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is tangential to
\Phi_{G}(C)
and so is
S
.

Proof
:

The proof of both statements works exactly the same. It is based on the following observation: we can choose coordinates

\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n},u\}
, where
\{x_{i}\}
are coordinates in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
u
is the remaining coordinate, when we identify

\mathfrak{h}^{*}=\mathfrak{g}^{*}\oplus\mathbb{R}
. In this coordinates we have that the canonical Poisson structure
\pi
is given by

\displaystyle\pi=\frac{1}{2}x_{k}\cdot C^{k}_{ij}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}-\frac{1}{2}u\cdot\omega_{ij}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\wedge\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}},

where
C^{k}_{ij}
are the structure constants of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\omega_{ij}
are the components of the symplectic form

\omega
.
Moreover we have for the hyperplane
D=\{u=1\}
. A tangential differential operator has to be of the form

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}=\sum_{(I,k)}D_{(I,k)}(x,u)\frac{\partial^{|I|}}{\partial x^{I}}\bigg(\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\bigg)^{k}

such that
D_{(I,k)}(x,1)=0
if
k\neq 0
. It is easy to see that for our specific bivector field
\pi
the Bidifferential
operators
B_{k}(\pi)
constructed from Kontsevich’s formality map do not contain any derivatives in
u
-direction, since the
Poisson bivector does not possess any and hence they are canonically tangential.
Let us now turn towards the equivalence
S
, due to
[
4
, Thm 3]
this eqivalence is build up out of differential
operators of the form (adapted to our coordinates)

\displaystyle D_{r}=\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{r}=1}^{\dim\mathfrak{g}+1}\mathrm{Tr}(\ad_{e_{i_{1}}}\dots\ad_{e_{i_{r}}})\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i_{1}}}\cdots\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i_{r}}}

where

for

and

. and and the

\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Note that
\ad_{e_{\dim\mathfrak{g}+1}}=0
and hence also in these differential operators no derivatives in

appear. This concludes the proof.

If a star product is tangential to a submanifold it can be shrinked to it and one can obtain again a star product on it. But
we have moreover that in our case:

Corollary 2.2
.

The star product obtained by shrinking the Kontsevich star product to
\Phi_{G}(C)
is the same as the Kontsevich star product
constructed by the shrinked Poisson bivector
\pi\big|_{\Phi_{G}(C)}
directly on
\Phi_{G}(C)
.

Since the Gutt-star product, the Kontsevich-star product and the equivalence bewtween them are invariant under the action of

G
, we can pull the back via the local diffeomorphism in order to obtain two left-invariant star products on
G
and a left-
invariant equivalence between them.

Moreover, one can show that pull-back of the Kontsevich star product is oobtained by Dolgushev globalization of the
Kontsevich formality with taking pull-back the canonical flat connection on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
shrinked to
D
, which is invariant
under the
group action of
H
, since it acts by linear transformations. Moreover, since everything is left-invariant we see that the
invariant Kontsevich class of the Kontsevich star product, and hence the Gutt–star product, is

[\hbar\Pi]_{G}
. The aim is now to compute the invariant Fedosov class of this star product, which is
an element in
\frac{[\Phi_{C}^{*}\Theta]}{\hbar}+H_{dR}^{2}(G)^{G}[[\hbar]]=\frac{[\omega]}{\hbar}+H_{\mathrm{CE}}^{2}(\mathfrak{g})[[\hbar]]
, where we identify the left-invariant de Rham cohomology with the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology and the fact that the invariant symplectic structure
\Phi_{C}^{*}\Theta
is just
\omega
under this identification by Remark

.

3
The Fedosov-class of the Drinfel’d twist

A Drinfel’d Twist obtained by a symplectic structure
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is a left-invariant star product on a Lie
group integrating the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. The original Drinfel’d construction of a twist, is basically described above: the
Drinfel’d twist is
the "pulled-back" Gutt-star product.
In
[
2
]
it is shown that it is possible to modify the Fedosov-construction in
order to get a full classification of symplectic Drinfel’d twists and the moduli space of symplectic Drinfel’d twists with

r
-matrix
r=\omega^{-1}
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{[\omega]}{\hbar}+H_{CE}^{2}(\mathfrak{g})[[\hbar]].

Moreover, it is easy to see that the left invariant star product
\star
induced by a twist
\mathcal{F}
from Corollary
1
of characteristic class
\frac{[\omega]}{\hbar}+\Omega
has invariant characteristic class

I(\frac{[\omega]}{\hbar}+\Omega)
, where
I\colon\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\Gamma^{\infty}(\Lambda^{\bullet}T^{*}G)^{G}
is the canonical isomorphism of the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex to the left-invaraint de Rham complex.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\omega)
be a symplectic Lie algebra and let
\mathcal{F}
be the Drinfel’d twist constructed above. Then its characteristic class is

\frac{[\omega]}{\hbar}
.

Proof
:

From the discussion in Section
2
, we know that the invariant Kontsevich class of the of the star product induced by Drinfel’d’s twist
is given by
[\hbar\Pi]_{G}
. To compute its invariant Fedosov class, we observe that in

[
13
]
, the authors construct an explicit equivalence between the Kontsevich-star product obtained by the
globalization procedure from
[
5
]
and a Poisson structure
\pi
and the Fedosov star product obtained by the
original Fedosov construction
[
8
]
with the symplectic structure
\pi^{-1}
.
If additionally a Lie group
G
acts on the manifold acts on the manifold, it can be shown that this equivalence can be
choosen to be
G
-invariant, if one can find a invariant connection, which is a straightforward computation.
Note that a Lie group acts always properly from the left
on itself and hence it is possible to find an invariant connection, and with the above discussion, it is clear that the
Fedosov star product constructed from
\Pi^{-1}
is equivalent to the Drinfel’d twist. By the
definition of the (invariant) Fedosov classes, the class of the Fedosov star product constructed by
\Pi^{-1}
has Fedosov class
\frac{[\omega]}{\hbar}
and so has hence the Drinfel’d twist.

\Box

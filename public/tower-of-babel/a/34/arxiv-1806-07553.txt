Coadjoint Orbits of Lie Algebras and Cartan Class
Michel GOZE † and Elisabeth REMM ‡

Abstract
We study the coadjoint orbits of a Lie algebra in terms of Cartan class. In fact, the tangent space to a coadjoint orbit 𝒪 ⁡ ( α ) \mathcal{O}(\alpha) at the point α \alpha corresponds to the characteristic space associated to the left invariant form α \alpha and its dimension is the even part of the Cartan class of α \alpha . We apply this remark to determine Lie algebras such that all the nontrivial orbits (nonreduced to a point) have the same dimension, in particular when this dimension is 2 2 or 4 4 . We determine also the Lie algebras of dimension 2 ​ n 2n or 2 ​ n + 1 2n+1 having an orbit of dimension 2 ​ n 2n .

Introduction

Let
G
be a connected Lie group,
\mathfrak{g}
its Lie algebra and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
the dual vector space of
\mathfrak{g}
. We identify
\mathfrak{g}
with the Lie algebra of left invariant vector fields on
G
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
with the vector space of left invariant Pfaffian forms on
G
. The Lie group
G
acts on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
by the coadjoint action. If
\alpha
belongs to
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, its coadjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}(\alpha)
associated with this action is reduced to a point if
\alpha
is closed for the adjoint cohomology of
\mathfrak{g}
. If not, the coadjoint orbit is an even-dimensional manifold provided with a symplectic structure. From the Kirillov theory, if
G
is a connected and simply connected nilpotent Lie group, there exists a canonical bijection from the set of coadjoint orbits onto the set of equivalence classes of irreducible unitary representations of this Lie group. In this work, we establish a link between the dimension of the coadjoint orbit of the form
\alpha
and
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)
its class in Elie Cartan’s sense. More precisely
\dim\mathcal{O}(\alpha)=2\big[\frac{\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)}{2}\big]
. Recall that the Cartan class of
\alpha
corresponds to the number of independent Pfaffian forms needed to define
\alpha
and its differential
{\rm d}\alpha
and it is equal to the codimension of the characteristic space
[
4
,
13
,
19
]
. The dimension of
\mathcal{O}(\alpha)
results in a natural relation between this characteristic space and the tangent space at the point
\alpha
to the orbit
\mathcal{O}(\alpha)
.

As applications, we describe classes of Lie algebras with additional properties related to its coadjoint orbits. For example, we determine all Lie algebras whose nontrivial orbits are all of dimension
2
or
4
and also the Lie algebras of dimension
2p
or
2p+1
admitting a maximal orbit of dimension
2p
that is admitting
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
such that
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)\geq 2p
.
Notice that the nilpotent Lie algebras classified in Sections
3
and
4
play a role in connection with a so-called inverse problem in representation theory of nilpotent Lie groups (see
[
6
]
and
[
7
, Section 5.1]
).

2
Dimension of coadjoint orbits and Cartan class

2.1
Cartan class of a Pfaffian form

Let
M
be a
n
-dimensional differentiable manifold and
\alpha
a Pfaffian form on
M
, that is a differential form of degree
1
. The

characteristic space of
\alpha
at a point
x\in M
is the linear subspace
\mathcal{C}_{x}(\alpha)
of the tangent space
T_{x}(M)
of
M
at the point
x
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{x}(\alpha)=A(\alpha(x))\cap A({\rm d}\alpha(x)),

where

\displaystyle A(\alpha(x))=\{X_{x}\in T_{x}(M),\,\alpha(x)(X_{x})=0\}

is the
associated subspace of
\alpha(x)
,

\displaystyle A({\rm d}\alpha(x))=\{X_{x}\in T_{x}(M),\,{\rm i}(X_{x}){\rm d}\alpha(x)=0\}

is the
associated subspace of
{\rm d}\alpha(x)
and
{\rm i}(X_{x}){\rm d}\alpha(x)(Y_{x})={\rm d}\alpha(x)(X_{x},Y_{x})
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\alpha
be a Pfaffian form on the differential manifold
M
. The
Cartan class of
\alpha
at the point
x\in M

[
9
]
is the codimension of the characteristic space
\mathcal{C}_{x}(\alpha)
in the tangent space
T_{x}(M)
to
M
at the point
x
. We denote it by
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)(x)
.

The function
x\rightarrow\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)(x)
is with positive integer values and is lower semi-continuous, that is, for every
x\in M
there exists a suitable neighborhood
V
such that for every
x_{1}\in V
one has
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)(x_{1})\geq\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)(x)
.

The
characteristic system of
\alpha
at the point
x
of
M
is the subspace
\mathcal{C}_{x}^{*}(\alpha)
of the dual
T_{x}^{*}(M)
of
T_{x}(M)
orthogonal to
\mathcal{C}_{x}(\alpha)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{x}^{*}(\alpha)=\{\omega(x)\in T_{x}^{*}(M),\,\omega(x)(X_{x})=0,\,\forall\,X_{x}\in\mathcal{C}_{x}(\alpha)\}.

Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)(x)=\dim\mathcal{C}_{x}^{*}(\alpha).

Proposition 2.2
.

If
\alpha
is a Pfaffian form on
M
, then

•

\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)(x)=2p+1
if
(\alpha\wedge({\rm d}\alpha)^{p})(x)\neq 0
and
({\rm d}\alpha)^{p+1}(x)=0
,

•

\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)(x)=2p
if
({\rm d}\alpha)^{p}(x)\neq 0
and
(\alpha\wedge({\rm d}\alpha)^{p})(x)=0
.

In the first case, there exists a basis
\{\omega_{1}(x)=\alpha(x),\omega_{2}(x),\dots,\omega_{n}(x)\}
of
T_{x}^{*}(M)
such that

\displaystyle{\rm d}\alpha(x)=\omega_{2}(x)\wedge\omega_{3}(x)+\dots+\omega_{2p}(x)\wedge\omega_{2p+1}(x)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{x}^{*}(\alpha)=\mathbb{R}\{\alpha(x)\}+A^{*}({\rm d}\alpha(x)).

In the second case, there exists a basis
\{\omega_{1}(x)=\alpha(x),\omega_{2}(x),\dots,\omega_{n}(x)\}
of
T_{x}^{*}(M)
such that

\displaystyle{\rm d}\alpha(x)=\alpha(x)\wedge\omega_{2}(x)+\dots+\omega_{2p-1}(x)\wedge\omega_{2p}(x)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{x}^{*}(\alpha)=A^{*}({\rm d}\alpha(x)).

If the function
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)(x)
is constant, that is,
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)(x)=\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)(y)
for any
x,y\in M
, we say that the Pfaffian form
\alpha
is of constant class and we denote by
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)
this constant. The distribution

\displaystyle x\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{x}(\alpha)

is then regular and it is an integrable distribution of dimension
n-\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)
, called the characteristic distribution of
\alpha
. It is equivalent to say that the Pfaffian system

\displaystyle x\rightarrow\mathcal{C}^{*}_{x}(\alpha)

is integrable and of dimension
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)
.

If
M=G
is a connected Lie group, we identify its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with the space of left invariant vector fields and its dual
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
with the space of left invariant Pfaffian forms. Then if
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, the differential
{\rm d}\alpha
is the
2
-differential left invariant form belonging to
\Lambda^{2}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
and defined by

\displaystyle{\rm d}\alpha(X,Y)=-\alpha[X,Y]

for any
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
. It is obvious that any left invariant form
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is of constant class and we will speak on the
Cartan class
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)
of a linear form
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. We have

•

\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)=2p+1
if and only if
\alpha\wedge({\rm d}\alpha)^{p}\neq 0
and
({\rm d}\alpha)^{p+1}=0
,

•

\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)=2p
if and only if
({\rm d}\alpha)^{p}\neq 0
and
\alpha\wedge({\rm d}\alpha)^{p}=0
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
n
-dimensional Lie algebra.

•

It is called
contact Lie algebra
if
n=2p+1
and if there exists a contact linear form, that is, a linear form of Cartan class equal to
2p+1
.

•

It is called
Frobenius Lie algebra
if
n=2p
and if there exists a Frobenius linear form, that is, a linear form of Cartan class equal to
2p
.

If
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is neither a contact nor a Frobenius form, the characteristic space
\mathcal{C}(\alpha)=\mathcal{C}_{e}(\alpha)
at the unit
e
of
G
is not trivial and the characteristic distribution on
G
given by
\mathcal{C}_{x}(\alpha)
with
x\in G
has a constant non-zero dimension. As it is integrable, the subspace
\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be a linear form of maximal class, that is

\displaystyle\forall\,\beta\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},\qquad\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)\geq\operatorname{cl}(\beta).

Then
\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
is an abelian subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\alpha
is a linear form of maximal class. If
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)=2p+1
, there exists a basis
\{\omega_{1}=\alpha,\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{n}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
such that
{\rm d}\alpha=\omega_{2}\wedge\omega_{3}+\dots+\omega_{2p}\wedge\omega_{2p+1}
and
\mathcal{C}^{*}(\alpha)
is generated by
\{\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{2p+1}\}
. If the subalgebra
\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
is not abelian, there exists
j
,
2p+2\leq j\leq n
such that

\displaystyle{\rm d}\omega_{j}\wedge\omega_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\omega_{2p+1}\neq 0.

Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{cl}(\alpha+t\omega_{j})>\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)

for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
. But
\alpha
is of maximal class. Then
\mathcal{C}(\alpha)
is abelian. The proof when
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)=2p
is similar.
∎

Note that this result can alternatively derived from the result of M. Duflo and M. Vergne
[
12
]
.

Recall some properties of the class of a linear form on a Lie algebra. The proofs of these statements are given in
[
14
,
15
,
19
]
.

•

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra, then the class of any non-zero
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is always odd.

•

A real or complex finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie algebra is never a Frobenius Lie algebra. More generally, an unimodular Lie algebra is non-Frobenius
[
11
,
24
]
.

•

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a real compact Lie algebra. Any nontrivial
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
has an odd Cartan class.

•

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex semisimple Lie algebra of rank
r
. Then any
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
satisfies
\ \operatorname{cl}(\alpha)\leq n-r+1

[
15
]
. In particular, a semisimple Lie algebra is never a Frobenius algebra. A semisimple Lie algebra is a contact Lie algebra if its rank is
1
, that is,
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
. In particular, if
\mathfrak{g}
is a contact real simple Lie algebra, then
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
or
\mathfrak{so}(3)
.

•

The Cartan class of any linear nontrivial form on a simple nonexceptional complex Lie algebra of rank
r
satisfies
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)\geq 2r

[
15
]
. Moreover, if
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic of type
A_{r}
, there exists a linear form of class
2r
which reaches the lower bound.

•

Any
(2p+1)
-dimensional contact real Lie algebra such that any nontrivial linear form is a contact form is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{so}(3)
.

2.2
Cartan class and the index of a Lie algebra

For any
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, we consider the stabilizer
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=\{X\in\mathfrak{g},\,\alpha\circ\operatorname{ad}X=0\}
and
d
the minimal dimension of
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
when
\alpha
lies in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. It is an invariant of
\mathfrak{g}
which is called the index of
\mathfrak{g}
. If
\alpha
satisfies
\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=d
then, from Proposition
2.4
,
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
is an abelian subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Considering the Cartan class of
\alpha
,
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
is the associated subspace of
{\rm d}\alpha
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=A({\rm d}\alpha),

so the minimality is realized by a form of maximal class and we have
d=n-\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)+1
if the Cartan class
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)
is odd or
d=n-\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)
if
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)
is even. In particular,

1)

if
\mathfrak{g}
is a
2p
-dimensional Frobenius Lie algebra, then the maximal class is
2p
and
d=0
,

2)

if
\mathfrak{g}
is
(2p+1)
-dimensional contact Lie algebra, then
d=n-n+1=1
.

This relation between index and Cartan class is useful to compute sometimes this index. For example we have

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
L_{n}
or
Q_{2p}
be the naturally graded filiform Lie algebras. Their index satisfy

1)

d(L_{n})=n-2
,

2)

d(Q_{2p})=2
.

In fact
L_{n}
is defined in a basis
\{e_{0},\dots,e_{n-1}\}
by
[e_{0},e_{i}]=e_{i+1}
for
i=1,\dots,n-2
and we have
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)\in\{1,3\}
and
d=n-2
for any
\alpha\in L_{n}^{*}
. The second algebra
Q_{2p}
is defined in the basis
\{e_{0},\dots,e_{2p-1}\}
by
[e_{0},e_{i}]=e_{i+1}
for
i=1,\dots,2p-3
,
[e_{i},e_{2p-1-i}]=(-1)^{i-1}e_{2p-1}
for
i=1,\dots,p-1
. In this case we have
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)\in\{1,3,2p-1\}
for any
\alpha\in Q_{2p}^{*}
and
d=2
. Let us note that a direct computation of these indexes are given in
[
1
]
.

2.3
The coadjoint representation

Let
G
be a connected Lie group and
\mathfrak{g}
its (real) Lie algebra. The adjoint representation of
G
on
\mathfrak{g}
is the homomorphism of groups:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ad}\colon\ G\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})

defined as follows. For every
x\in G
, let
A(x)
be the automorphism of
G
given by
A(x)(y)=xyx^{-1}
. This map is differentiable and the tangent map to the identity
e
of
G
is an automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
. We denote it by
\operatorname{Ad}(x)
.

Definition 2.6
.

The coadjoint representation of
G
on the dual
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is the homomorphism of groups:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ad}^{*}\colon\ G\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})

defined by

\displaystyle\big\langle\operatorname{Ad}^{*}(x)\alpha,X\big\rangle=\big\langle\alpha,\operatorname{Ad}\big(x^{-1}\big)X\big\rangle

for any
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
X\in\mathfrak{g}
.

The coadjoint representation is sometimes called the
K
-representation. For
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, we denote by
\mathcal{O}(\alpha)
its orbit, called the coadjoint orbit, for the coadjoint representation. The following result is classical
[
23
]
: any coadjoint orbit is an even-dimensional differentiable manifold endowed with a symplectic form.

Let us compute the tangent space to this manifold
\mathcal{O}(\alpha)
at the point
\alpha
. Any
\beta\in\mathcal{O}(\alpha)
is written
\beta=\alpha\circ\operatorname{Ad}\big(x^{-1}\big)
for some
x\in G
. The map
\rho\colon G\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{*}
defined by
\rho(x)=\alpha\circ\operatorname{Ad}(x)
is differentiable and its tangent map at the identity of
G
is given by
\rho^{T}_{e}(X)={\rm i}(X){\rm d}\alpha
for any
X\in\mathfrak{g}
with
{\rm i}(X){\rm d}\alpha(Y)=-\alpha[X,Y]
. Then the tangent space to
\mathcal{O}(\alpha)
at the point
\alpha
corresponds to
A^{*}({\rm d}\alpha)=\{\omega\in\mathfrak{g}^{*},\,\omega(X)=0,\,\forall\,X\in A({\rm d}\alpha)\}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Consider a non-zero
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. The tangent space to
\mathcal{O}(\alpha)
at the point
\alpha
is isomorphic to the dual space
A^{*}({\rm d}\alpha)
of the associated space
A({\rm d}\alpha)
of
{\rm d}\alpha
.

Corollary 2.8
.

Consider a non-zero
\alpha
in
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Then
\dim\mathcal{O}(\alpha)=2p
if and only if
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)=2p
or
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)=2p+1
.

An immediate application is

Proposition 2.9
.

Any
(2p+1)
-dimensional Lie algebra with
\dim\mathcal{O}(\alpha)=2p
for all
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{so}(3)
or
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
and then of dimension
3
.

Proof.

From
[
15
,
19
]
any contact Lie algebra such that any nontrivial linear form is a contact form is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{so}(3)
. Assume now that any nontrivial form on
\mathfrak{g}
is of Cartan class equal to
2p
or
2p+1
. With similar arguments developed in
[
15
,
19
]
, we prove that such a Lie algebra is semisimple. But in this case, we have seen that
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
or
\mathfrak{so}(3)
.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

Assume that
\dim\mathfrak{g}=2p
and all the nontrivial coadjoint orbits are also of dimension
2p
. Then for any nontrivial
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
,
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)=2p
. If
I
is a nontrivial abelian ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
, there exists
\omega\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
,
\omega\neq 0
such that
\omega(X)=0
for any
X\in I
. The Cartan class of this form
\omega
is smaller than
2p
. Then
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple. But the behavior of the Cartan class on simple Lie algebra leads to a contradiction.

We deduce also from the previous corollary:

Proposition 2.11
.

1.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to the
(2p+1)
-dimensional Heisenberg algebra, then any nontrivial coadjoint orbit is of dimension
2p
.

2.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to the graded filiform algebra
L_{n}
, then any nontrivial coadjoint orbit is of dimension
2
.

3.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to the graded filiform algebra
Q_{n}
, then any nontrivial coadjoint orbit is of dimension
2
or
n-2
.

4.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a
(2p+1)
-dimensional
2
-step nilpotent Lie algebra with a coadjoint orbit of dimension
2p
, then
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to the
(2p+1)
-dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra
(
see
[
19
]
)
.

5.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a
n
-dimensional complex classical simple Lie algebra of rank
r
, then the maximal dimension of the coadjoint orbits is equal to
n-r
if this number is even, if not to
n-r-1

(
see
[
15
]
)
.

3
Lie algebras whose coadjoint orbits are of dimension 2 or 0

In this section, we determine all Lie algebras whose coadjoint orbits are of dimension
2
or
0
. This problem was initiated in
[
3
,
5
]
. This is equivalent to say that the Cartan class of any linear form is smaller or equal to
3
. If
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra having this property, any direct product
\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\mathfrak{g}\bigoplus J
of
\mathfrak{g}
by an abelian ideal
J
satisfies also this property. We shall describe these Lie algebras up to an abelian direct factor, that is indecomposable Lie algebras. It is obvious that for any Lie algebra of dimension
2
or
3
, the dimensions of the coadjoint orbits are equal to
2
or
0
. We have also seen:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a simple Lie algebra of rank
1
. Then for any
\alpha\neq 0\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
,
\dim\mathcal{O}(\alpha)=2
. Conversely, if
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra such that
\dim\mathcal{O}(\alpha)=2
for any
\alpha\neq 0\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
then
\mathfrak{g}
is simple of rank
1
.

Recall that the rank of a real semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is the dimension of any Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. This is well defined since
\mathfrak{h}
is a Cartan subalgebra if and only if
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}
is Cartan in the complexified simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
. Now we examine the general case. Assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra of dimension greater or equal to
4
such that for any nonzero
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
we have
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)=3,2
or
1
.

Assume in a first step that
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)=2
for any nonclosed
\alpha\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Let
\alpha
be a non-zero
1
-form and
\operatorname{cl}(\alpha)=2
. Then there exists a basis
\{\alpha=\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
such that
{\rm d}\alpha={\rm d}\omega_{1}=\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{2}
. This implies
{\rm d}({\rm d}\omega_{1})=0=-\omega_{1}\wedge{\rm d}\omega_{2}
. Therefore
{\rm d}\omega_{2}=\omega_{1}\wedge\omega
with
\omega\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. As
\operatorname{cl}(\omega_{2})\leq 2
,
\omega_{2}\wedge\omega=0
. If
\{X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}\}
is the dual basis of
\{\alpha=\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n}\}
, then
A({\rm d}\omega_{1})=\mathbb{K}\{X_{3},\dots,X_{n}\}
is an abelian subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Suppose
[X_{1},X_{2}]=X_{1}+aX_{2}+U
where
U,X_{1},X_{2}
are linearly independent. The dual form of
U
would be of class 3 so
U=0
. Under the assumption of change of basis if
a\neq 0
we can assume that
[X_{1},X_{2}]=X_{1}
. So
{\rm d}\omega_{2}=\omega_{1}\wedge\omega
and
\omega\wedge\omega_{2}=0
imply that
{\rm d}\omega_{2}=0
. This implies that
A({\rm d}\omega_{1})
is an abelian ideal of codimension
2
. Let
\beta
be in
A({\rm d}\omega_{1})^{*}
, with
{\rm d}\beta\neq 0
. If such a form doesn’t exist then
\mathbb{K}X_{1}\oplus A({\rm d}\omega_{1})
is an abelian ideal of codimension
1
. Otherwise
{\rm d}\beta=\omega_{1}\wedge\beta_{1}+\omega_{2}\wedge\beta_{2}
with
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in A({\rm d}\omega_{1})
. As
\beta\wedge{\rm d}\beta={\rm d}\beta^{2}=0
,
\beta_{1}\wedge\beta_{2}=0
which implies
{\rm d}\beta=(a\omega_{1}+b\omega_{2})\wedge\beta
. But
{\rm d}({\rm d}\beta)=0
implies
a{\rm d}\omega_{1}\wedge\beta=0=a\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{2}\wedge\beta
thus
a=0
and
{\rm d}\beta=b\omega_{2}\wedge\beta
. We conclude that
[X_{1},A({\rm d}\omega_{1})]=0
and
\mathbb{K}\{X_{1}\}\oplus A({\rm d}\omega_{1})
is an abelian ideal of codimension 1.

Assume now that there exists

\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
or
\mathfrak{so}(3)
. From Proposition
2.9
these algebras are the only Lie algebras of dimension
2p
or
2p+1
whose orbits
\mathcal{O}(\alpha)
with
\alpha\neq 0
are of dimension
2p
.

5.2
\boldsymbol{2p}
-dimensional Lie algebras with a
\boldsymbol{2p}
-dimensional coadjoint orbit

Such Lie algebra is Frobenius (see
[
24
]
). Since the Cartan class of a linear form on a nilpotent Lie algebra is always odd, this Lie algebra is not nilpotent. In the contact case, we have seen that any contact Lie algebra is a deformation of the Heisenberg algebra. On other words, any contact Lie algebra can be contracted on the Heisenberg algebra. In the Frobenius case, we have a similar but more complicated situation. We have to determine an irreducible family of Frobenius Lie algebras with the property that any Frobenius Lie algebra can be contracted on a Lie algebra of this family.

In a first step, we recall the notion of contraction of Lie algebras. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
be a
n
-dimensional Lie algebra whose Lie bracket is denoted by
\mu_{0}
. We consider
\{f_{t}\}_{t\in]0,1]}
a sequence of isomorphisms in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
with
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. Any Lie bracket

\displaystyle\mu_{t}=f_{t}^{-1}\circ\mu_{0}(f_{t}\times f_{t})

corresponds to a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{t}
which is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. If the limit
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}\mu_{t}
exists (this limit is computed in the finite-dimensional vector space of bilinear maps in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
), it defines a Lie bracket
\mu
of a
n
-dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
called a contraction of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
.

Remark 5.10
.

Let
\mathfrak{L}^{n}
be the variety of Lie algebra laws over
\mathbb{C}^{n}
provided with its Zariski topology. The algebraic structure of this variety is defined by the Jacobi polynomial equations on the structure constants. The linear group
{\rm GL}(n,\mathbb{C})
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
by changes of basis. A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is contracted to the
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
if the Lie bracket of
\mathfrak{g}
is in the closure of the orbit of the Lie bracket of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
by the group action (for more details see
[
8
,
14
]
).

5.2.1
Classification of complex Frobenius Lie algebras up to a contraction

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a
2p
-dimensional Frobenius Lie algebra. There exists a basis
\{X_{1},\dots,X_{2p}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that the dual basis
\{\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{2p}\}
is adapted to the Frobenius structure, that is,

\displaystyle{\rm d}\omega_{1}=\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}\wedge\omega_{4}+\dots+\omega_{2p-1}\wedge\omega_{2p}.

In the following, we define Lie algebras, not with its brackets, but with its Maurer–Cartan equations. We assume here that
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{a_{1},\dots,a_{p-1}}
,
a_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
be the Lie algebras defined by

\displaystyle{\rm d}\omega_{1}=\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\omega_{2k+1}\wedge\omega_{2k+2},\qquad{\rm d}\omega_{2}=0,

\displaystyle{\rm d}\omega_{2k+1}=a_{k}\omega_{2}\wedge\omega_{2k+1},\qquad 1\leq k\leq p-1,

\displaystyle{\rm d}\omega_{2k+2}=-(1+a_{k})\omega_{2}\wedge\omega_{2k+2},\qquad 1\leq k\leq p-1.

Then any
2p
-dimensional Frobenius Lie algebra can be contracted in an element of the family
\mathcal{F}=\{\mathfrak{g}_{a_{1},\dots,a_{p-1}}\}_{a_{i}\in\mathbb{C}}
. Moreover, any element of
\mathcal{F}
cannot be contracted in another element of this family.

Proof.

See
[
16
]
and
[
19
]
.∎

Remark 5.12
.

The notion of principal element of a Frobenius Lie algebra is defined in
[
24
]
. In the basis
\{X_{1},\dots,X_{2p}\}
for which
{\rm d}\omega_{1}=\omega_{1}\wedge\omega_{2}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{p-1}\omega_{2k+1}\wedge\omega_{2k+2}
, the principal element is
X_{2}
.

Proposition 5.13
.

The parameters
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{p-1}\}
which are the invariants of Frobenius Lie algebras up to contraction are the eigenvalues of the principal element of
\mathfrak{g}_{a_{1},\dots,a_{p-1}}
.

5.2.2
Classification of real Frobenius Lie algebras up to contraction

We have seen that, in the complex case, the classification up to contraction of
2p
-dimensional Lie algebras is in correspondence with the reduced matrix of the principal element. We deduce directly the classification in the real case:

Theorem 5.14
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{a_{1},\dots,a_{s},b_{1},\dots,b_{2p-s-1}}
,
a_{i},b_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
be the
2p
-dimensional Lie algebras given by

\displaystyle[X_{1},X_{2}]=[X_{2k-1},X_{2k}]=X_{1},\qquad k=2,\dots,p,

\displaystyle[X_{2},X_{4k-1}]=a_{k}X_{4k-1}+b_{k}X_{4k+1},\qquad[X_{2},X_{4k}]=(-1-a_{k})X_{4k}-b_{k}X_{4k+2},\qquad k\leq s,

\displaystyle[X_{2},X_{4k+1}]=-b_{k}X_{4k-1}+a_{k}X_{4k+1},

\displaystyle[X_{2},X_{4k+2}]=b_{k}X_{4k}+(-1-a_{k})b_{k}X_{4k+2},\qquad k\leq s,

\displaystyle[X_{2},X_{4s+2k-1}]=-\tfrac{1}{2}X_{4s+2k-1}+b_{k}X_{4k+2k},\qquad 2\leq k\leq p-2s,

\displaystyle[X_{2},X_{4s+2k}]=-b_{k}X_{4s+2k-1}-\tfrac{1}{2}X_{4s+2k},\qquad 2\leq k\leq p-2s.

Then any
2p
-dimensional Frobenius real Lie algebra is contracted in an element of the family
\mathcal{F}=\{\mathfrak{g}_{a_{1},\dots,a_{s},b_{1},\dots,b_{2p-s-1}}\}_{a_{i},b_{j}\in\mathbb{R}}
. Moreover, any element of
\mathcal{F}
cannot be contracted in other different element of this family.

Corollary 5.15
.

Any
2p
-dimensional Lie algebras described in (
5.11
) in the complex case and in (
5.14
) in the real case have an open coadjoint orbit of dimension
2p
. Moreover any
2p
-dimensional Lie algebras with an open coadjoint orbit of dimension
2p
is a deformation of a Lie algebra of these families.
